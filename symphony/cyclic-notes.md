# Cyclic notes for movements II and III (from movement-one critics)

Collected from critics 10 and 20 (mvt1/critics/reports/c010.md, c020.md).

- The second-theme head is the motto's rhythm with a rising sixth. When later movements quote it, use only the anacrusis and two-bar head, in this rhythm, changing only mode, key and scoring:
  - major: `g4 | e'2.~ e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2 g'4` (mvt1 bars 138–140)
  - minor: `g4 | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4 aes'2 g'4` (this anacrusis form appears in mvt1 only in B minor at bars 97–99; its first bar is identical to the motto with its pickup (Vln I 9–10, cor anglais 167–168), and the two part ways only in the second bar; the coda at 167 starts the minor theme without the pickup)
- Movement III's brass chorale should use the major form above; it is then both the motto and the second theme. Movement I plants the sound: pp three-part trombone chorale on the augmented motto at bars 171–175.
- Movement II (A♭): do not open with a lyrical motto in A♭; the exposition's second theme (bar 47) already is that. Options: start from the E♮→E♭ of movement I's last chord (E♭ = fifth of A♭); build the melody from the motto's chromatic tail (E♭–D–D♭–C) or the inverted sixth; quote the second-theme head only in II's middle section, in a remote key.
- Two slow forms of the motto exist in mvt I: G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C (bars 1–4, 167–177) and G–E♭–D–D♭–C (109–113). Use the intro form for the chorale.
- (Critic 30) The trumpets' last note in movement I is an accented, unresolved B♮ at bar 166. In movement III's C-major chorale, let the trumpets enter on B and resolve up to C on the chorale's arrival, so the finale answers that exact sound.
- (Critic 30) Proportions: movement I runs about 7:02 (v59). Plan movement III at 6 minutes or more so the redemption is not front-weighted (Tchaikovsky 5 model).
- (Critic 40) The pp trombone chorale over tam-tam at 171–175 is the device of Tchaikovsky 6/iv 137–146 (funeral music). Movement III's chorale should keep its voicing and pitches but be in major, at f/ff, with horns and trumpets added and no tam-tam. Movement I never plays the falling major motto (G–E–D–C) in full brass at forte; that is what III can offer as new.
- (Critic 40) The B♮ is left unresolved in four places, not only 166: Hn 1 at 14; Tpt and Hn at 166; Vc at 170; Cl 1 at 171–173 (recalls the intro horn call E♭–C–B). For the chorale's tail use C–D♭–C in its first statement and C–B–C (the first theme's tail) in its last.
- (Critic 40) A♭ is the pitch of all three climaxes (82, 114, 156), and the motto's D♭ is the fourth degree of A♭: movement II can harmonise the motto's tail as a plagal figure.
- (Critic 40) Movement I uses the motto throughout (unlike Tchaikovsky 5/i), so movement III needs genuinely new material besides the motto's return. III should not begin with a soft strings-only C-major chord before its chorale; the viola's E♭→E♮ at 178 leaves that question open.
- (Critic 54) Movement I never gives the bass clarinet a line of its own and the bassoon only short solos; give both an exposed moment in movement II or III.
- (Critic 60) D♭ is deliberately left unresolved as a key in movement I (passing note in the head, sf peak at 20, ff at 132, IV in A♭ at 48/52, Neapolitan at 169, C–D♭–C at 173–177; absent from 139–162). Movement II: make D♭ major the key of the middle section or the climax, built from the motto's tail; end with a plagal D♭–A♭ cadence with D♭–C in the top voice; quote the second-theme head in a more remote key. Movement III: a direct shift from forte D♭ major to C major ff before the final chorale (cf. Eroica i 557–561).
- (Critic 62) Movement I's bass is in root position on 71% of beats (Tchaikovsky 6/i 40%, Dvořák 9/i 59%, Beethoven 9/i 42%) and never has a countermelody under a theme. In II and III use more inversions and give the bass at least one melodic counterline.
- (Critic 70) Movement I combines the first- and second-theme heads only once, at f/p in the development (v90 bars 103–110). A fast, full-brass combination of the two heads in major is reserved for movement III.
- (Critic 74) In movement I the harp, timpani, a soft trumpet, horn without string doubling and the bass clarinet never carry a melody, and strings sound in every bar. Give some of these melodic moments in II/III, and include string-free passages.
- (Critic 77) Movement I's exposition groups have almost no exchange between instrument groups (11% of bars). In II/III build more of the texture from call and response and imitation between winds and strings (Dvořák 9/i 24–39, Tchaikovsky 6/i).
- (Critic 80, proposed in /tmp/critic-080/work; applies only if the composer accepts the cut of v99 bars 156–159, after which later bars are −4) The recapitulation now withholds the violins' singing statement of the falling C-major head (`g'4 | e''2.~ e''8 d''8 | c''4 a''2-- g''4 | f''4.( e''8 d''4 f''8 a''8 | c'''2 b''2)`) and its chromatic counter (`r4 c''4( b'4 bes'4 | a'2) c''2~( | c''4 bes'4 a'2 | g'2 f'2)`) over a C tonic pedal. Movement I keeps the falling major head only at pp in Hn/Va/Vc (147–150). Give this statement to movement III's violins, mf–f molto cantabile, after the brass chorale has arrived in C major; keep movement II to the minor/anacrusis form and the motto's tail.

3. The trumpets' B♮ resolving to C (trumpets last play B♮ at 170).
4. A fast, ff, full-brass combination of the two heads in C major. Movement I has the combination at f in A♭ (69–72; brass: Tpt 1 on S, horns on P, trombones held, mf) and at f/p in minor keys (103–110).
5. D♭ as a key, and a plagal D♭–C close.
6. Melodic lines for harp, timpani, soft trumpet, unsupported horn and bass clarinet; string-free passages; a bass countermelody; call and response between wind and string groups.
7. The hammered tutti layout in major. In v109 all four forte tuttis with a repeated-eighth pulse are in C minor and carry the P head (27–32, 131–134, 167–170); critic 90 proposed giving the codetta 69–71 the S group's broken-chord eighths instead, so this layout stays the first theme's sound (see c090.md). ### 3. Plan for movements II and III

**Movement II (Adagio, A♭ major, ABA').**
- Opening: begin from the last sound of movement I, E♮ in the violas: let E♮ fall to E♭ as the fifth of A♭ (cello or viola solo, p), not a lyrical motto statement (the S theme at 47 already is that).
- A theme: build from the motto's tail E♭–D–D♭–C read in A♭ (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂) or from the inverted sixth (falling E♭–G), in a new rhythm; do not use the S-head rhythm `2.~ 8 8 | 4 2 4` here.
- B section: D♭ major as the key of the middle section or its climax. Quote the S head in minor with its pickup (`g4 | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4 aes'2 g'4`, transposed to a remote key such as E minor or F♯ minor, as at 97–99), once, in a solo woodwind or the bass clarinet.
- Use the instruments movement I kept from melody: bass clarinet solo, unsupported horn solo, harp line; at least one string-free passage.
- Close with a plagal D♭–A♭ cadence with D♭–C in the top voice (the motto's last step).
- No tam-tam, no trombone chorale, no C-major material.

**Movement III (Allegro con fuoco → Maestoso, C minor → C major, at least 6 minutes).**
- New fast main material, with the P head used only as a link (movement I already saturates it).
- Development or approach to the coda: forte D♭ major shifting directly to C major ff (cf. Eroica i 557–561).
- The last C-minor tutti: reuse the P tutti layout of 27–32/167–170 (head in octaves, Cb repeated eighths, held brass), then turn it to C major in the same layout, so the return of that sound is the point.
- Chorale: the intro form of the motto in major, `g1 | e'1 | d'1 | c'2 des'2 | c'1` for the first statement and `... c'2 b2 | c'1` for the last, in the trombone voicing of 175–179 (Tbn 1 / Tbn 2 / B.tbn), f to ff, with horns and trumpets added, no tam-tam. Trumpets enter on B♮ and resolve to C on the arrival.
- After the chorale: the violins' withheld C-major S head with its counter (item 1 above), mf–f molto cantabile, over a C tonic pedal.
- Final page: the fast full-brass combination of the two heads in C major (item 4), the falling major motto in full brass (item 2).
- More inverted bass positions and one melodic bass counterline (movement I's bass is in root position on about 71% of beats).
