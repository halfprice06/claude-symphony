(182 bars, 4/4, percussion) and movement II `mvt2/v30` (92 bars, 3/4 + 4/4, no percussion). Before = r02 on the same

**Verdict.** Movement I: **better** (LRA 22.2 -> 19.0 LU, Spearman 0.74 -> 0.83, bars printed <= mf louder than the

## 8. Research (with sources)

1. **Melody against accompaniment.** Rimsky-Korsakov: the melody "should always stand out in relief from the
   accompaniment", by dynamics, timbre, doubling or register. In piano all winds are "of fairly equal balance", and in
   forte "1 Horn = 2 Clarinets = 2 Oboes = 2 Flutes = 2 Bassoons" and "1 Trumpet = 1 Trombone = 2 Horns"
   (https://gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). Consequence: the printed dynamics already express the
   intended balance. A render should mark the melody by one step of relief (about 3-4 dB), and must not undo a printed
   ff tutti for an espr. line written into it.
2. **Faders, CC7/CC11, velocity.** Mock-up practice: velocity for attack and layer of short notes, CC1 for dynamic
   layers of long notes, CC11 for shaping, a static fader per track
   (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/poll-how-do-you-use-dynamics-and-expression.101377/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/rant-about-cc1-cc11-and-velocity.168479/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/questions-about-prepping-ahead-for-dynamics-and-volume-for-orchestral-mock-ups.159951/).
   Riding a fader is a slow, phrase-length move. Deliberate offsets beat random humanisation
   (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/232667 ; https://vi-control.net/community/threads/timing-of-brass-strings.121576/).
3. **Timing and attack compensation.** Shorts need the least compensation, longs more, legato the most. Offsets must
   be measured per articulation, and maxed-out random delay makes attacks sloppy
   (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/quantising-slow-attack-strings.155143/ ;
   https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/325250).
4. **Short notes and releases.** Recorded short articulations carry their own natural decay and "play themselves out"
   (https://online.berklee.edu/courses/orchestral-mockups-in-film-scoring); reverb is used as a tail where samples lack
   one (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/92604). This supports VPO staccato samples for wind staccato rather than shortened
   FluidR3 sustains.
5. **Mixing and staging.** Depth through early reflections per section group, with one tail on the orchestra bus
   (pre-delay about 40-80 ms for the tail, or a few ms for near instruments)
   (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/151501 ; https://vi-control.net/community/threads/how-do-you-add-reverb.7399/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mixing-dry-recorded-orchestral-instruments-with-virtual-orchestration.147277/).
   Low-mid buildup from stacked sample ambience. Cut about 200-350 Hz on most instruments, gently
   (https://old.cinematiccomposing.com/blog/composing-part-iv-eq-reverb ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/first-time-mixing-orchestral-piece.105397/ ;
   https://mastering.com/muddy-mixes/). Seating is a convention, not a law; directivity matters for the room send
   (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/196805).
6. **Free samples to replace FluidR3 (checked in the archive listing).** The full VPO 3.2 wave archive
   (https://archive.org/download/virtual-playing-orchestra-3-2-wave-files/Virtual-Playing-Orchestra3-2-wave-files.zip/,
   zip index read here) contains `libs/VSCO2-CE/Strings/Harp/` (**18 harp samples, KSHarp_*_mf, CC0**),
   `libs/VSCO2-CE/Woodwinds/Flute/` (sustain-vibrato and staccato with round robins), Mattias Westlund horns
   (CC BY-SA 3.0), No Budget Orchestra flute, clarinet and bassoon sections and trumpet/trombone, SSO woodwinds and brass
   [measured: listing]. VPO licence: free for any music, redistribution with credit, not for sale; it recommends adding
   reverb to merge the sources into one room. It has no legato articulation; the author found overlapping sustains as
   good (https://www.virtualplaying.com/virtual-playing-orchestra/). VSCO 2 CE is CC0
   (https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/; SFZ and WAV on GitHub, which this container cannot reach). The
   Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra harp is CC Sampling Plus 1.0 (https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=30767).
7. **Impulse responses.** OpenAIR (University of York) holds CC-licensed hall IRs, e.g. the Jack Lyons Concert Hall,
   CC BY 4.0 (https://www.openair.hosted.york.ac.uk/?p=571). The Detmold set in use is adequate; no change proposed.
8. **Loudness.** LRA = 10th-95th percentile of gated short-term loudness. Short loud events barely move it, which is
   why m2's LRA fell while its max rose (https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/tech3342). Classical recordings are not
   normalised to broadcast targets; normalise a whole work as one unit
   (https://iconnectivity.com/blog/2017/6/10/ebu-r128-the-important-audio-breakthrough-youve-never-heard-of). The house
   target of -20 LUFS with a true peak of -1 dBTP remains appropriate.
9. **Timpani rolls** (critic 2's sources, still valid): a roll is a sustained single-stroke tremolo whose speed and
   weight follow the dynamic; it begins with a defined first stroke
   (https://www.percussionclinic.com/art_timptech.htm ; https://theinstrumentalist.com/march-2009/percussion-rolls/).

## 9. Ranked plan for actor 4

Measure every item on both test inputs with `critics/c03/scripts/meas.sh`, plus `duckaudit.py`, `rollband.py` and a
`--no-balance` A/B where the balance stage is involved.

1. **Make the balance stage obey the printed dynamics and preserve bar loudness** (volume; fixes all three m2
   regressions). (a) Never cut a stem whose printed dynamic in that bar is higher than the melody's. Cut a stem printed
   at the same dynamic by at most 4 dB (`duck_max_dB` 8 -> 4); stems printed softer by at most 6 dB. (b) End a melody
   phrase where the melody staff's printed dynamic rises to f or louder and most sounding staves are printed f or
   louder (a tutti). Do not duck in such bars at all. (c) Never cut a marked melody: concurrent melodies may only be
   lifted, within `lift_max_dB`. (d) After computing the gains, scale each bar so that the summed loudness of all stems
   stays within ±1 dB of the no-balance bar loudness (relief between staves, not a quieter bar). (e) Smooth over
   1.0-1.5 s and start the ramp one beat before the phrase (fader slope <= 6 dB/s; now up to 20). Source 8.1, 8.2.
   Targets: m2 LRA 18.5-20.5 (now 17.7; no-bal 20.1), m2 inversions <= 25 (46), m2 short-term max <= +9 LU and
   p95 <= +6 (10.6 / 7.2), 0 stem-bars cut where printed above the melody (m2 has 2), stem-bars cut >= 6 dB <= 30 per
   movement (m1 171). Keep m1 at melodies >= -6 dB vs sum >= 17/20, >= 0 dB vs loudest other >= 10/20, Spearman
   >= 0.83, LRA 18-20.5.
2. **Timpani rolls audible again, in printed order** (Dan). Reduce the total settle to about -6 dB (`timp_roll_long_dB`
   -7 -> 0 to -2; keep the 1.5 s tau). Make the first stroke an accent at the printed velocity or higher (now -14).
   Exempt the roll note from `fff_velocity_cut`, or map roll velocity per printed dynamic, so that start levels rise
   with the printed dynamic. Keep the roll rule as the safety net (3 dB under the loudest other). Source: Dan
   (BRIEF.md), 8.9. Targets: start level (0.4-0.8 s) monotonic with the printed dynamic by >= 2 dB per step (now f
   -31.5 > ff -33.5 > fff -35.1); roll body 4-9 dB under the loudest other stem (now 12-23); in band >= -8 dB for f-fff
   rolls (now -12 to -15); timpani loudest 0 bars; settle 5-8 dB by the end of the roll; stroke autocorrelation < 0.25
   (0.29 / 0.27).
3. **Wind staccato from recorded staccato samples** (articulations). Activate `artic_channels` for flutes, oboes,
   clarinets and bassoons with VPO/VSCO2-CE staccato SFZ regions only (sustains stay FluidR3). Calibrate their level per
   printed dynamic against the FluidR3 sustain of the same staff (one-note test as `tools/sfcal.py`). Add the files to
   assets/vpo3 with the licence line. Source 8.4, 8.6. Targets: flute staccato audible ratio < 1.3 (now 2.07); wind
   staccato at +250 ms >= 8 dB below same-length detached notes; staccato peak within ±2 dB of detached neighbours; no
   wind stem spread vs string median worse than now.
4. **Spectral blend EQ** (blend). A gentle -3 dB bell at 250-300 Hz (Q about 1) on the stems that crowd it (violas,
   cellos, horns, bassoons, FluidR3 winds), or on the mix bus. Relax the string `air` shelf from -14 dB above 9 kHz to
   about -6 dB above 11 kHz. Give clarinets and bass clarinet +4 to +6 dB at 3-8 kHz, or raise their brightness floor.
   Source 8.5. Targets: 250-315 Hz within ±1.5 dB of mean(Brahms 1, 4) (now +3.1 / +4.0); 6.3-8 kHz within ±2 dB (now
   -4.0 / -5.6); < 100 Hz -9 to -11 dB in both (now -11.6 / -12.2); clarinets' > 6 kHz share above -60 dB (now -69.5);
