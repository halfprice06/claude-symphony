## 8. Research (with sources)

1. **Accents and dynamics in mock-ups.**
   - Common practice keeps velocity for the attack of a note and CC1/CC11 for its shape. High CC1 or velocity values
     select layers with "harsh attacks"; for a gentle front, play with a lower velocity and use the controller for the
     swell (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/changing-lass-i-attacks.30193/ ;
     https://vi-control.net/community/threads/questions-about-prepping-ahead-for-dynamics-and-volume-for-orchestral-mock-ups.159951/ ;
     https://www.garritan.com/?p=8025 ; https://forums.steinberg.net/t/help-to-export-midi-with-accents-converted-to-velocity-not-cc/1035550).
   - Consequence: an accent is a moderate velocity rise (keep the dynamic's own layer, not the ff layer) plus a CC11
     "weight" bump of 2-4 dB that rises over 30-60 ms and returns over 200-400 ms. Not a hard-layer spike followed by a
     dip. Musical dynamics are carried by power and timbre together (Weinzierl et al., JASA 2018,
     https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/items/b919b35f-45ca-4a73-9536-77570c603359/full), so a small timbre change (a
     partial layer blend) is enough.
2. **Legato with detached samples.**
   - SFZ practice: notes played over a sounding note (`trigger=legato`) skip the sample's attack with `offset` and
     crossfade from the old note (`off_mode`, `ampeg_release` about 0.1-0.3 s for solo lines; up to about 1 s for
     slow ensembles) (https://sfzformat.com/tutorials/legato ; https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/trigger ;
     https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/sw_previous).
   - Sampled legato often has a level dip at the transition. Library makers and users fix it with the crossfade shape
     or a CC11 compensation (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/legato-and-legato-connections-do-any-of-them-really-work.39336/ ;
     https://vi-control.net/community/threads/legato-and-future-woodwind-and-brass-libraries.28443/).
   - An overlap is needed so that the notes crossfade
     (https://musescore.org/en/user/527826/blog/2016/05/29/weekly-status-update-may-23rd-may-29th).
3. **Section balance.**
   - Mock-up practitioners: woodwinds usually sit under strings and brass in loud passages, and sampled winds tend to
     end up "too hot" because live winds blend unless they have a solo. Watch the mf→fff trend: winds that keep pace
     with brass are rising too fast. Balance per section bus
     (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/balancing-instruments-across-dynamics.51339/ ;
     https://vi-control.net/community/threads/volume-balancing-an-orchestral-template.109030/page-2 ;
     https://old.cinematiccomposing.com/blog/composing-part-iii-balance-panning).
   - Rimsky-Korsakov's comparison of resonance between groups (https://gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm):
     in forte, strings and brass carry, and the winds need doubling to match.
4. **Free samples.**
   - University of Iowa MIS: the brass sets (horn, B-flat trumpet, tenor and bass trombone, tuba) are at pp/mf/ff
     (pre-2011, chromatic runs), "may be downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions"
     (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS.html).
   - **The newer per-pitch sets avoid segmentation:** for example the bassoon page offers one stereo AIFF per pitch,
     Bb1-D5, ff only (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS-Pitches-2012/MISBassoon2012.html; files under "sound
     files/MIS Pitches - 2014/Woodwinds/Bassoon/").
   - The newer sets are recorded with a Decca tree, so the L/R pair gives real stereo, which also helps the mono-wind
     problem (MIS.html).
   - Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra brass: CC Sampling Plus 1.0, sustains without loop points
     (https://os.zynthian.org/factory_sfz/Other/sonatina/Sonatina%20Symphonic%20Orchestra/Readme.html ;
     https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2011/01/31/free-creative-commons-orchestral-sample-library-sonatina-symphonic-orchestra/).
5. **Staging and depth.** Early reflections per section move groups back (strings closest, then woodwinds, then
   brass/percussion). Pre-delay alone is a disputed depth cue: a nearer source has the longer gap before its first
   reflection (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143166 ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143312 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/early-reflections-positioning-and-panning.148432/). The Detmold
   loudspeaker-orchestra IRs already encode seat positions. For the near-mono Iowa winds, convolving player 1 and
   player 2 with two different stage-position IRs (S5/S6 or S6/S7) is the physically right way to widen them.
6. **Timpani rolls.** Libraries crossfade dynamic layers for swells, and players often accent the concluding stroke
   slightly. A roll ending can be shaped by a quick level drop before the reverb
   (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/288710 ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/250901 ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143809).
   Critic 4's teaching sources stand: a defined first stroke, then a sustained body
   (https://freepercussionlessons.com/how-to-play-timpani-rolls-from-soft-to-loud-and-back-to-soft).
7. **Mastering loudness.**
   - Classical masters commonly sit at -16 to -18 LUFS with true peak <= -1 dBTP. Platforms normalise to about -14
     (Apple -16) and do not raise quiet masters much. A limiter taking more than 1-2 dB on the loudest passage is
     compressing (https://violetrecording.com/how-to-master-classical-music/ ;
     https://www.roexaudio.com/blog/what-lufs-should-i-master-at ; https://orphiq.com/resources/mastering-for-streaming).
   - LRA has no prescribed maximum; it is the 10th-95th percentile spread of short-term loudness
     (https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/tech3342).
   - Consequence: keep -20 LUFS, linear gain and the peak-only limiter. Fix m2's range in the dynamics curve, not at
     the master.
