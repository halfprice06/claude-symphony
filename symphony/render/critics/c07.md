## 8. Research (with sources)

1. **Dynamics: layers and crossfades.** In sample libraries the dynamic controller (CC1) crossfades between recorded
   dynamic layers so timbre and level move together; CC11 is a level trim on top and CC7 balances tracks; relying on a
   level control alone makes soft samples sound loud and loud ones soft, and layer switches are audible in exposed
   lines (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/poll-how-do-you-use-dynamics-and-expression.101377/ ;
   https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/239314 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/questions-about-prepping-ahead-for-dynamics-and-volume-for-orchestral-mock-ups.159951/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/bbcso-expression-vs-dynamics-vs-velocity.103333/). SFZ has velocity
   crossfades for this (https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/xfin_lovel/ ; https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/xfout_lovel/), and
   r07/sfzplay.py implements them. Consequence: overlap the pp/mf/ff regions of the brass maps (plan 2).
2. **Timing and legato.** Real sections are slightly out of sync; libraries recorded to a click lose that; useful
   amounts are small (VSL's humanize / legato blur around 30 %); random velocity rarely helps; legato needs overlaps
   (https://forums.steinberg.net/t/help-with-realistic-mock-ups/157194 ;
   https://www.vsl.info/tutorials/guides/orchestration-recipes/recipe27 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/how-do-you-make-vsts-more-expressive-and-realistic.113056/page-2 ;
   https://forums.taxi.com/post569952.html). Critic 6's fixed per-player offsets fit this.
3. **Distance for close-miked brass.** Mock-up makers put an air-absorption EQ on close-miked brass and percussion that
   sound "too there" (about 0.2 dB per 100 ft at 1 kHz rising to about 9 dB at 20 kHz), set by the instrument's
   distance, then early reflections and room; reverb alone does not remove the close-mic character
   (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/air-absorption-plot-can-i-get-an-audio-numbers-geek-to-crosscheck-my-math.34539/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/how-important-is-it-really-to-simulate-distance-with-delay-eq.35853/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mimic-mic-positions-for-close-samples.140537/ ;
   https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/164634).
4. **Depth by section and seating.** Per-section early reflections / pre-delay steps (one recipe: about 10 ms strings,
   30 ms woodwinds, 50 ms brass and percussion; the direction of the effect is disputed), IRs chosen by how far they
   place a source, a shared tail with per-section sends (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143512 ;
   https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/246855 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/good-ol-reverb-setup-question.122508/page-2 ;
   https://www.waves.com/get-more-from-reverb). Pan by seating with narrowed section widths and the basses near the
   centre (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/panning-the-orchestra.132058/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/achieving-a-balanced-stereo-mix-after-panning-an-orchestra.148034/).
5. **Timpani rolls.** Roll speed rises with the dynamic and falls when soft; a roll is normally on one drum; the end
   is a single, often slightly accented stroke; fast controller crescendos behave non-linearly; notation programs
   often ignore hairpins on rolls (https://freepercussionlessons.com/how-to-play-timpani-rolls-from-soft-to-loud-and-back-to-soft ;
   https://forums.musicplayer.com/topic/53957-question-about-timpani-rolls ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/250901 ;
   https://forum.vsl.co.at/topic/53560/best-practices-to-get-dynamic-timpani-rolls-in-sychron-sound-nice ;
   https://forums.steinberg.net/t/timpani-roll-dynamic/107904 ; https://musescore.org/en/node/302263). Consequence:
   a drum change keeps the roll's level (plan 1).
6. **Why the trombone samples "click".** At ff the wave in a trombone's cylindrical bore steepens into a shock front;
   the bell radiates it as a sharp spike once per period: the "brassy" timbre (Hirschberg et al., JASA 99, 1996,
   https://doi.org/10.1121/1.414698 ;
   https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/dm_campbell_why_do_brass_instruments_sound_brassy.pdf ;
   https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/s_stevenson_dm_campbell_effects_of_non-linear_propagation_on_timbre_of_brass_instruments.pdf ;
   https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/watch-a-trombones-shock-wave-180274743/).
7. **Free samples and IRs.** VSCO 2 Community Edition (CC0) has F horn (sustain, staccato, muted), tenor trombone,
   trumpet, tuba, and bassoon / clarinet / flute / oboe / piccolo (https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE ;
   https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/): a source of **brass staccato** and a second horn set. Sonatina
   Symphonic Orchestra (CC Sampling Plus 1.0): 12 brass instruments, minor-third sampling, no loop points
   (https://os.zynthian.org/factory_sfz/Other/sonatina/Sonatina%20Symphonic%20Orchestra/Readme.html ;
   https://forum.renoise.com/t/free-orchestral-sample-library/32787). Philharmonia samples: free to use in works, not to
   be sold or offered "as is" (https://philharmonia.co.uk/audio-sound-samples/). OpenAIR has CC BY concert-hall IRs
   (e.g. Jack Lyons Concert Hall, a computer model: https://www.openair.hosted.york.ac.uk/?p=571); the Detmold set in
   use is measured, CC BY 4.0 (https://zenodo.org/records/4116247).
8. **Mastering loudness.** Classical masters sit around -18 to -23 LUFS integrated; LRA 10-20 LU is typical for
   classical, a Mahler 1 at 23.5 LU was called the wide end; keep a limiter to 1-2 dB on the peaks
   (https://violetrecording.com/how-to-master-classical-music/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/what-db-is-your-masters-peak-and-also-low.156562/page-2 ;
   https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/loudness-standards-lufs-peaks-and-streaming-limits ;
   https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/tech3342). m1 (17.7 LU) is inside that; m2 (20.6) is at the wide end, and its
   slow-movement references are 15-17.
