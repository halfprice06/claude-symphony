## 2. Dan's priority 1: articulations

Table: `critics/c01/artic_table.txt` (r00 / r01 wet / r01 dry for every stem).

### What improved [measured]
- **Slurred against detached.** Boundary dip (wet), slurred / detached: Violins I -2.2 / -5.7, Violins II -1.2 / -5.3, violas -0.1 / -5.4, cellos +0.2 / -7.4, basses -0.3 / -8.1, flutes -1.1 / -7.4, oboes -1.4 / -7.9, clarinets -0.5 / -8.4, bassoons -0.7 / -8.0, trumpets -0.6 / -6.2, trombones -1.8 / -6.8 dB. Dry, the detached gaps are true silence (-17 to -102 dB); the room fills them to -5 to -8 dB.
- **String staccato** level at 75 % of the notated length: Violins I -14.0, Violins II -13.6, violas -14.2, cellos -16.5 dB (r00: -2.9, -4.8, -14.6, -10.9). Target (below -12 dB) met.
- **Timpani rolls:** level at 75 % of the roll -1.2 dB (r00 -34.1), envelope 6.3 peaks per second wet and 12.5 dry, standard deviation 1.5 dB. Rolls are now sustained and follow the hairpins.
- **Accents:** sustain after the accent falls back (Violins I -5.0 dB at 75 %, horns 1-2 -5.1, trumpets -4.3; r00 -1.5, -1.7, -1.4), so an accent is now an attack, not a louder whole note.
- **Pizzicato** (GM 45 on its own channel): basses audible 0.62 s wet / 0.53 s dry, 1.17 / 1.06 times the notated length (r00 0.67 s / 1.30); level at 75 % -12.7 dB (r00 +1.2).
- **Hairpins on held notes:** of 108 notes of 1 s or longer under a crescendo, the CC11 change over the note follows the printed change (median +2.5 dB realised for +2.1 printed); of 65 under a diminuendo, -4.0 for -3.8. In r00 none of them moved.

### What is wrong or got worse
1. **Staccato is shortened twice (bug).** [measured] LilyPond's MIDI already plays staccato at 50 % length (c00). `articulate.py` takes the note length from the MIDI note-off (`L = t1 - t0`), not from the notated length, and then applies its own 50 % (winds) or 35 % (strings). Realised sounding/notated length: strings 0.18, winds 0.25 (`notes_r01.csv`, e.g. cello quavers at bar 85 sound 42 ms out of 238 ms, flute quavers at bar 94 sound 60 ms). The string sample needs 70-100 ms to reach its peak, so string staccato notes are cut before the attack ends: **their peaks are 3.7 to 6.3 dB below unmarked notes at the same dynamic within two bars** (dry stems: Violins I -6.3, Violins II -3.7, violas -4.4, cellos -5.3 dB; cor anglais -1.6). [inferred] Staccato in the score means short and separated, not softer; the strings' staccato quavers now sound like soft clicks.
2. **"Tremolo strings" GM 44 is not a tremolo in FluidR3.** [measured from the SoundFont] Preset 44 "Tremolo" uses exactly the same 34 samples as preset 48 "Strings" ("Strings A#2L" etc.); a rendered test note differs from preset 48 by -20 dB of the signal and has the same envelope modulation. In the stems, tremolo notes show 2.2 (violas), 3.3 (Violins II) and 5.0 (Violins I) envelope peaks per second, the same as sustained notes. The 93 :32 tremolo notes therefore still sound as sustained notes; for violas (solo viola sample, GM 41) the switch to GM 44 also changes the timbre to the ensemble sample. The target from c00 ("GM 44 or more than 10 peaks/s") was met only on paper. Note: an envelope-peak count also cannot detect section tremolo well (players are not in phase); see the modulation measure in section 7.
3. **Woodwind staccato and short notes are lengthened by the room.** [measured] Staccato level at 75 %, dry / wet: flutes -44 / -9.2, cor anglais -78 / -11.4, bassoons -81 / -13.6 dB; audible length flutes 0.12 / 0.37 s, cor anglais 0.17 / 0.43 s. Detached woodwind notes are audible for 1.6 (flutes) and 1.7 (oboes) times their notated length wet, against 0.98 dry and 1.13-1.23 for strings and brass wet. The woodwinds have a reverb send of -4 dB against -7 dB for strings, and the IR scale is set so that send 0 dB equals the dry energy (250-2000 Hz). [inferred] The amount of room on the whole mix is right (cut-offs fall 20 dB in 0.51 s against 0.49 s in Brahms 1), but the woodwinds carry about 3 dB more room than the strings, which is what blurs their staccato. The actor's statement that the hall adds the same tail in reality is true for the room tail, not for its level relative to the direct sound.
4. **Accent boost is small for some stems.** [measured, wet / dry] oboes 1.4 / 2.3 dB (n = 56), cor anglais 1.9 / 4.0 (n = 2, unreliable), trombones 2.8 / 3.9 (n = 14), timpani 2.0 / 3.0 (n = 7), Violins I 2.7 / 2.9 (n = 49), Violins II 2.8 / 2.9, cellos 2.7 / 2.1; other stems 3.2-7.4. The attack time of accented notes equals that of unaccented notes in every stem (same sample), so the accent is only the level step. [inferred] Below about 3 dB the accent is hard to distinguish from note-to-note variation.
5. **CC11 is per staff, not per voice.** [measured] In two-voice staves (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons) the accent dip of one voice and the reset of the hold term at another voice's onset move the held note of the other voice: 51 steps of 3-12 dB within one 20 ms control step on a held note (flutes 13, oboes 19, clarinets 12, bassoons 5, trumpets 1, Violins II 1). Example: flutes at 91.3, 93.6, 95.1 s, +5.1 dB steps. Also 11 of 108 crescendos on held notes hit CC11 = 127 and stop rising (shortfall 1-9 dB: bass clarinet bar 3, printed +13.8 dB, realised +4.5; cor anglais bar 7, +9.4 / +4.7; trombones, bass trombone and tuba bar 159, +5.8 / 0.0), because CC11 can only lower the level and is already near 127 at f/ff.
6. **Tenuto** is not distinguishable from detached notes in the audio (level at 75 % within ±2 dB of detached in every stem; n = 31). [measured] Expected, since only length and +5 velocity differ. Low priority.
7. **Short detached notes are near-staccato.** [measured] Unslurred notes under 0.2 s sound 0.75 of their length (148 notes), 0.2-0.4 s notes 0.83 (601 notes); the minimum gap of 40 ms dominates at fast tempi. Staccato notes sound 0.17-0.25 (item 1). [inferred] The order is right (staccato much shorter than detached), but the detached quaver and semiquaver runs are close to the length a player would give a light staccato; a minimum gap of 25-30 ms at fast tempi would keep them clearly longer than staccato.

## 6. Accepted, rejected, open

Accepted (keep in r02): one stem per staff mixed in float; plain gain plus peak limiter instead of loudnorm; one measured hall (Detmold) with sources by seating; seating pans; legato overlap and detached gaps; CC11 dynamics with ramps on held notes; accent shape (attack then return); timpani rolls as repeated strokes; pizz on GM 45; release shortening by SoundFont NRPN for string staccato.

Open: melody balance; tam-tam and cymbal roll missing; dB per printed step 1.5-3.8 (target 4-5); high-band correlation 0.29; fff peaks +10 LU; accent boost under 3 dB for oboes, trombones, timpani, violins, cellos; CC11 per staff instead of per voice; no timing variation; real articulation samples not yet used.

## 7. Research: better samples and tools (checked from this container on 2026-10-09)

**sfizz (SFZ player with a command-line renderer) is installable here.** GitHub is blocked, and there is no sfizz in apt or pip, but the openSUSE Build Service publishes Ubuntu 24.04 packages: `https://download.opensuse.org/repositories/home:/sfztools:/sfizz/xUbuntu_24.04/amd64/sfizz_1.2.3-0_amd64.deb` and `libsfizz1_1.2.3-0_amd64.deb` (HTTP 200, 3.4 MB and 0.8 MB). Unpacked with `dpkg-deb -x` (no root install needed), `sfizz_render --sfz X.sfz --midi in.mid --wav out.wav -s 48000 -q 3` runs with `LD_LIBRARY_PATH=<dir>/usr/lib/x86_64-linux-gnu`. Usage: https://man.archlinux.org/man/sfizz_render.1.en ; project: https://sfz.tools/sfizz/ (sfizz library BSD-2-Clause; the VST3 part of the package is GPL-3). Rendering 9 test files of 20 s took 5 s.

Unpacked: 1.3 GB. **Timing.** Rasch (1979, Acustica 43, 121-131) measured onset asynchrony in trios with a standard deviation of 30-50 ms; Aiba et al. (2011) cite a mean of about 36 ms and note that asynchrony helps listeners hear the instruments as separate (https://ist.ksc.kwansei.ac.jp/~nagata/data/Aiba_2011_JACIII.pdf ; summary of Rasch: https://blog.zhdk.ch/zmoduletelematic/slides/). r01 has 0 ms between all stems.

**Reverb on short notes.** Orchestral mock-up practice keeps the send low on sections whose short notes must stay clear, and balances early reflections against the tail rather than adding more tail (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/gotta-catch-em-all-them-feedback.118960/ ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/120273 ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143489). These are practitioner opinions, not measurements.
