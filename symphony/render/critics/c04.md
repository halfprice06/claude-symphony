### 5.2 Findings
1. **Audibility and printed order of the starts are fixed** [measured]:
   - start levels f (117) -30.3, ff (41) -28.8, fff (122) -25.9 LUFS (r03 -31.5 / -33.5 / -35.1);
   - in band the roll bodies are -3 to -9 dB where the score has them under f/ff textures (r03 -12 to -15);
   - the attack stroke is now 3.5-10 dB over the body, a defined first stroke as percussion teaching describes
     (section 8.9);
   - no roll is the loudest stem.
   Critic 3's item 2 is done.
2. **New: dynamics and hairpins printed inside a tied roll are lost** [measured from `notes.csv` + `events.tsv` +
   audio]:
   - `score_events.py` merges tied notes into one row and keeps only the first note's dynamic and hairpin (`notes.csv`
     row for bar 167: dyn f, hairpin empty, 4608 ticks; `events.tsv`: `cresc-1` at bar 169 and `dyn:ff` at 170);
   - `articulate.py` builds the roll's CC11 curve from that one row;
   - so every printed change after the first note is ignored: the ff > mf > pp of 41-43, the fff > pp < mf f < of
     122-130, and the crescendos at the ends of 117-121 and 167-169;
   - the climax roll 122-130 is held flat at 3 dB under the loudest other stem by the balance safety net, through a
     printed pp.
   This breaks Dan's rule twice: a printed crescendo does not make the roll grow, and printed diminuendos are not
   followed. It also breaks the brief's "a rendering must not contradict the printed dynamics". The defect has been
   present since r02; no earlier critic caught it.
3. **The safety net flattens exposed soft rolls** [measured]: the roll rule cuts only bars where the timpani is less
   than 3 dB under the loudest other stem. On an exposed opening (m1 bar 1, cut 5.0 dB; m2 bar 2, cut 2.9 dB) it cuts

### 5.3 How much should a long roll fade? (open question; target set here)

The evidence:
- Dan asks that long rolls "fade softer unless explicit crescendo" and "not drown other things", and that they stay
  support (BRIEF.md).
- Timpani teaching: a roll is a sustained single-stroke tremolo. It begins with a defined, slightly louder first
  stroke; after that the speed and weight are set so that the tone sounds sustained at the printed dynamic. Without
  the difference at the start it "sounds like a series of attacks" (8.9).
- So a real roll at a constant printed dynamic does not keep decaying. It settles from the attack to a steady body.
- r03 (total settle -13 dB) made bodies 12-23 dB under the loudest stem and masked in band; Dan's "support" was gone.
- r04 (-6 dB settle plus -2.5 dB for long rolls; measured settles of -4.2 to -7.2 dB) keeps every unmarked roll
  audible in band, except where the safety net flattened it (5.2.3).

**Target (both movements, every roll longer than one bar):**
- **(a) Inside a printed dynamic, no hairpin (body):**
  - first stroke 3-6 dB over the body (now +3.5 to +10.1: soften the large ones);
  - the body settles 3 dB within about 2 s;
  - after that it sinks at most about 0.5 dB per bar, to a total of **-4 to -6 dB at the end for rolls >= 3.5 s**
    (-2 to -4 dB for shorter ones), never below -6 dB under its start window;
  - the body stays **3 to 9 dB under the loudest other stem**;
  - it stays **>= -10 dB in its own band in >= 90 % of 1 s windows**;
  - exception: a roll printed two or more steps under every sounding stem may go under -9 dB against the loudest
    stem, but not below -10 dB in band.
- **(b) Printed dynamic changes inside the roll:** each new printed dynamic is reached at the printed point, about
  3-4 dB per step (the house step target, item 2). A diminuendo hairpin runs to the next printed dynamic. The settle
  is suspended in those segments.
- **(c) Printed crescendo:** the level rises from the printed start, by at least 3 dB per step, to the next printed
  dynamic. Nothing is subtracted during the crescendo (no settle, no safety-net cut beyond keeping the timpani under
  the loudest stem).
- **(d) Safety net:** one offset per roll, or per printed segment, so that the shape is kept.

## 8. Research (with sources)

1. **Dynamics: velocity, CC1, CC11, CC7.** Mock-up practice:
   - velocity sets the attack and layer of short notes, CC1 crossfades the dynamic layers of long notes, CC11 extends
     the range, and CC7 or the fader is a static balance (https://vsl.co.at/tutorials/guides/orchestration-recipes/recipe27 ;
     https://beatkitchen.io/guides/orchestral/06-programming-strings/ ;
     https://vi-control.net/community/threads/questions-about-prepping-ahead-for-dynamics-and-volume-for-orchestral-mock-ups.159951/);
   - a crossfaded timpani roll swell does not respond linearly to a fast CC1 sweep, so crescendos must be drawn and
     checked by measurement (https://forum.vsl.co.at/topic/53560/best-practices-to-get-dynamic-timpani-rolls-in-sychron-sound-nice).
   Musical dynamics are carried by sound power **and** timbre, measured on 40 instruments at pp and ff (Weinzierl et
   al., JASA 144 (2018) 1347, https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/items/b919b35f-45ca-4a73-9536-77570c603359/full).
   Consequence: a step in printed dynamic must give a clear level step, together with brightness, on every stem; GM
   samples that change little with velocity need a larger CC11 share (item 2).
2. **Note length and legato.**
   - Overlap legato notes slightly (about 10-20 ticks), keep shorts tight, and quantise shorts more carefully than
     legato lines (https://beatkitchen.io/guides/orchestral/06-programming-strings/).
   - Recorded shorts "play themselves out", and reverb supplies a tail where a sample lacks one
     (https://online.berklee.edu/courses/orchestral-mockups-in-film-scoring ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/92604).
   - This supports natural-release samples for wind shorts over truncated sustains (item 3).
3. **Humanisation and timing.** Library humanise controls offset sections slightly, for example VSL's Humanize Delay
   Scale (CC25) of about 25-30 (https://vsl.co.at/tutorials/guides/orchestration-recipes/recipe27). Deliberate,
   per-articulation offsets beat random jitter (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/232667 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/quantising-slow-attack-strings.155143/). r04's onset test confirms that
   blind offsets do not help, so timing stays low priority.
4. **Free samples to replace FluidR3 winds and brass** (checked here):
   - **University of Iowa MIS**: oboe, B-flat and E-flat clarinet, bass clarinet, bassoon, flute, horn, B-flat
     trumpet, tenor and bass trombone, tuba (also gongs/tam-tams and cymbals). The pre-2011 sets are at pp, mf and ff.
     "May be downloaded and used for any projects, without restrictions" (https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/MIS.html).
   - The 2012 sets come as per-pitch files (`MIS-Pitches-2012/MISOboe2012.html` etc.). The pre-2011 sets come as
     chromatic AIFF runs per octave and dynamic (`MISoboe.html`).
   - I downloaded one file here (Oboe.mf.C5B5.aiff: 44.1 kHz, 45.7 s, 12 notes about 3.8 s each, readable by
     soundfile), so the host is reachable from this container [measured]. Iowa samples are also in the VPO archive
     (bassoon, horn, trombone and trumpet only: zip index read [measured]).
   - Iowa notes are single, detached notes with natural attack and release, recorded dry [inferred from the site
     description]. That suits the Detmold convolution and gives three dynamic layers, which the VPO winds lack (actor
     3's "irregular dynamic layers").
   - **Philharmonia Orchestra samples**: all instruments, with articulations and lengths
     (https://philharmonia.co.uk/audio-sound-samples/). The terms are "free to use ... must not be sold or made
     available 'as is'"; a mirror labels them CC BY-SA 3.0 (https://github.com/skratchdot/philharmonia-samples/). This
     is not an open data licence, and storing them in assets/ arguably makes them "available as is", so **do not
     use**.
   - **VSCO 2 CE** (CC0, https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/; GitHub not reachable here).
   - **Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra**: CC Sampling Plus 1.0; oboes, clarinets and bassoons have sustain only, and the
     sustains reportedly lack loop points (https://os.zynthian.org/factory_sfz/Other/sonatina/Sonatina%20Symphonic%20Orchestra/Readme.html ;
     https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2011/01/31/free-creative-commons-orchestral-sample-library-sonatina-symphonic-orchestra/).
   - VPO 3 archive contents: https://archive.org/download/virtual-playing-orchestra-3-2-wave-files/Virtual-Playing-Orchestra3-2-wave-files.zip/
5. **Staging and reverb.**
   - Distance comes from the balance of early reflections against the tail, and from wet level; near sources get a
     longer pre-delay than far ones. One practitioner uses about 80 ms for strings, 50 ms for winds and brass, 20 ms for
     percussion (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/using-reverb-predelay-to-position-sections.10754/ ;
     https://vi-control.net/community/threads/pre-delay.31822/ ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/120273 ;
     https://manual.yamaha.com/pa/software/vstrack/pr/en/11_reverb_plugins_en.html).
   - The Detmold loudspeaker-orchestra IRs already encode source position. No change proposed; the stereo and decay
     measures are in band.
6. **Mixing and EQ.** Cutting about 200-350 Hz against stacked-ambience mud is standard
   (https://old.cinematiccomposing.com/blog/composing-part-iv-eq-reverb ; https://mastering.com/muddy-mixes/). r04
   did this; now trim the presence side effect (item 6).
7. **Mastering loudness.**
   - Streaming services normalise whole albums as a unit, so "quiet passages stay quiet as intended"
     (https://artists.spotify.com/help/article/loudness-normalization). True peak below -1 dBTP is advised
     (https://blog.insideblackbird.com/getting-your-levels-right-for-spotify-apple-music-etc).
   - LRA is the 10th-95th percentile of gated short-term loudness (https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/tech3342).
   - Consequence: keep linear gain to -20 LUFS with no compression. Fix m2's top-heavy range in the dynamics curve,
     where the contrast is made, not at the master.
8. **Melody relief.** Rimsky-Korsakov: the melody must stand out in relief from the accompaniment, and in piano the
   winds are of fairly equal balance (https://gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). A marked line at the
   same printed dynamic as its accompaniment deserves about one step of relief; a line printed softer does not.
9. **Timpani rolls.**
   - A roll begins with an attack slightly louder than the following strokes; without it the roll sounds like a series
     of attacks (https://freepercussionlessons.com/how-to-play-timpani-rolls-from-soft-to-loud-and-back-to-soft ;
     https://www.percussionclinic.com/art_timptech.htm).
   - Roll speed rises with loudness and falls for soft rolls, so that the tone stays sustained
     (https://theinstrumentalist.com/?p=2525).
   - Practitioners end a roll with a slightly louder final stroke, and drive the crossfade (CC1) for swells while the
     first hit rings (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/288710 ; https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/250901).
   - Consequence: a steady body after the attack, printed dynamics followed inside the roll (5.3).
