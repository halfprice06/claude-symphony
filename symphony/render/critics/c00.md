# Critic 0: baseline review of r00 and brief for actor 1

Date: 2026-10-09. Subject: `r00/score.mp3` (FluidR3_GM.sf2 through FluidSynth 2.3.4, gain 0.6, single-pass `loudnorm=I=-20:TP=-2`, 192 kb/s), rendered from `r00/score.midi` (identical to `mvt1/v120/score.midi`).

Nobody can hear the audio. Every statement below is marked **[measured]** (a number from the tools in `tools/`, files in `critics/c00/`) or **[inferred]** (a conclusion drawn from measurements, from the MIDI, or from the sources cited). Dan's priorities (BRIEF.md, 2026-10-09) are articulations, the volume of individual tracks, and blend; the plan in section 6 puts those three first.

## 1. What was built (toolkit, `tools/README.md`)

- `measure.py`: loudness (EBU R128 via ffmpeg plus our own BS.1770 series; the two agree to 0.0 LU on r00), short-term percentiles, peaks and clipping, noise floor, 1/3-octave long-term spectrum, brightness against loudness, stereo correlation and pan spread, decay after sharp cut-offs (works on recordings too), and with `--midi`: loudness per bar against the printed dynamic, decay after the final note and after the five general silences in the score, onset lag. Plots: spectrogram, loudness per bar with velocities, spectrum, final decay.
- `compare.py`: one table and overlay plot for any set of measured files.
- `score_events.py` with `ly/eventlog.ily`: compiles a scratch copy of v120 with a logging engraver and lists every printed note with its printed dynamic, hairpin, articulations, slur position and technique, matched to the MIDI note it became (5121 of 5121 notes matched). Output for r00: `critics/c00/notes_r00.csv`, summary `articulation_midi_r00.json`.
- `stems.py`: renders one stem per staff (22 stems) with any render command, and measures per stem: level while sounding, level per printed dynamic, pan, colour, articulation audio metrics (attack, level at 75 % of the notated length, audible length, tremolo modulation, accent boost, dip at slurred against detached note boundaries), and blend measures across stems (loudest-minus-second, balance inversions, family levels per dynamic). Output: `critics/c00/stems_r00/`.
- `sfcal.py`: level, level range and brightness of each GM program by velocity for any soundfont. Output: `critics/c00/sfcal_FluidR3/`, `critics/c00/sfcal_MuseScore_General/`.

## 4. Main problems

1. **Dynamics flattened by the mastering step.** [measured] Single-pass `loudnorm` runs in dynamic mode: LRA fell from 19.4 LU (FluidSynth output) to 12.1 LU, the softest 10 % rose from -14.6 to -8.3 LU below I, and the pp bars now sit only 8 dB under the mean (15 dB before). The Lento introduction (bars 1-14, pp to mf) plays at about the level of the ff Allegro (loudness plot `critics/c00/r00/loudness.png`). Brahms 1, the closest reference, has LRA 18.5 LU and p10 at -15.3 LU. Before loudnorm r00 is close to Brahms 1 on these two numbers. [inferred] This is the largest single deviation from the references, and it is cheap to remove. ffmpeg documents that single-pass loudnorm is dynamic and that linear mode needs the measured values of a first pass (sources 1, 2).
2. **Clipping in the FluidSynth output.** [measured] The 16-bit WAV reaches full scale on 2743 samples and +0.1 dBTP at gain 0.6. loudnorm hides it in the MP3 (-2.3 dBTP), but the clipped waveform is what was normalised.
3. **Articulations not rendered** (Dan's priority 1). [measured] Slurred and unslurred notes are identical in MIDI (full length) and in audio (same boundary dip in every stem). Tenuto is not distinguished. Tremolo (120 notes) is one sustained note at a lower velocity; string stems show no modulation and the 26 timpani rolls die away as one stroke. Pizzicato (35 notes) plays the arco sample. Staccato is realised only as half length: it works for winds (-15 to -23 dB at 75 % of the notated length) but not for violins (-3 to -5 dB, because the string-ensemble sample has a long release). Accents (+20 velocity) give +2 to +7 dB, which works. [inferred] FluidR3 has one sample set per instrument, so attack shape and length are the only articulation means available in r00; the attack time of each stem is the same for every articulation [measured: e.g. Violins I 71-99 ms for accent, detached, legato].
4. **Instrument levels do not follow orchestral balance** (Dan's priority 2). [measured] At the same printed f/ff, the trombone pair is 6 dB above the trumpet pair and 7 dB above the horns; violas, cellos and basses are 5-8 dB below Violins I; the contrabassoon is 13 dB and the bassoons 8 dB below Violins I; timpani 14 dB below. Per-instrument dynamic range is 1.3-3.5 dB per printed dynamic step. [inferred, using Rimsky-Korsakov's ratios (source 3): in forte 1 trumpet = 1 trombone = 1 tuba = 2 horns, 1 horn = 2 woodwinds, a whole first-violin section = 2 woodwinds; in piano all winds about equal and one string section = one wind; reading "twice as strong" as +3 dB] trumpets should equal trombones, horns should sit 3 dB under them, and the string sections should be roughly equal to each other. The levels above are sample levels of FluidR3, not choices.
5. **No common stage and a narrow image** (Dan's priority 3). [measured] All stems are within ±1.6 dB of centre; pan spread 0.27 against 0.32-0.35 in the references; correlation above 4 kHz is -0.02 against 0.38-0.53. Cut-offs decay with an RT60 estimate of 0.9 s against 1.3 s in the Brahms 1 recording; Beranek's best-rated halls are 1.8-2.0 s occupied (source 4). [inferred] The image is a centred cluster with decorrelated highs from FluidSynth's chorus/reverb, not an orchestra on a stage; the short decay makes the five general pauses (bars 14, 26, 32, 138, 170) fall 20 dB in 0.21-0.26 s.
6. **Tone colour does not change with dynamics.** [measured] Loud passages gain +4 to +5 dB at 5-10 kHz relative to soft ones in r00, against +6 to +10 dB in the references; sfcal shows FluidR3 samples do not brighten with velocity. [inferred] ff brass and strings sound like louder pp; the brightening r00 does show comes from orchestration (more and higher instruments), not from the samples.
7. **Hairpins on held notes, mechanical timing.** [measured] 242 notes of 1 s or longer under hairpins do not change level; every onset is on the grid. [inferred] Swells are heard as steps at the next note; lower priority than items 1-6.

What is already acceptable [measured]: overall spectral slope and balance between bass and treble (-3.2 dB/oct vs -2.7 to -2.9; share below 100 Hz -9.5 dB vs -8.8 to -8.9), noise floor (digital silence), tempo map and fermatas (from LilyPond), accent levels, wind staccato.

Techniques found (practitioner sources, sources 6-9): give each section its own track so it can be panned and gain-staged; shape dynamics phrase by phrase with CC11 (and CC7 for the static balance) instead of fixed values, especially on held strings and brass; keep timing and velocity variation small (velocity within 5-10 %, timing offsets of 10-30 ms) and phrase-related rather than random; pan moderately by seating (first violins left, cellos and basses right, horns left, trumpets and trombones centre-right) and send all sections to one stereo reverb, with more send for sections further back and percussion kept drier; legato by overlapping sustained notes when no legato samples exist (VPO author). FluidSynth: `-R 0 -C 0` turns its reverb and chorus off so that one external room can be used; `interp 7` (shell command) selects 7th-order interpolation; `-o synth.polyphony=` raises the voice limit (source 10).

## 6. Ranked plan for actor 1

Ranking follows Dan's priorities (articulations, per-track volume, blend), then the expected size of the measured difference. Each item names its target measure so critic 1 can check it with the same tools. The architecture in item 0 is needed by items 1-3.

**0. Architecture: one stem per staff, mixed with ffmpeg.** Write a script in r01 that reads v120's MIDI plus `notes.csv` (run `tools/score_events.py`), writes one MIDI per staff (and per technique where the program changes, e.g. pizz.), renders each with FluidSynth with internal reverb and chorus off (`-R 0 -C 0`), a fixed gain low enough not to clip, and a float or 24-bit WAV, then mixes the stems with ffmpeg (per-stem gain, pan, reverb send). This removes the shared-channel problem (Violins I/II etc.), lets every stem have its own level and position, and makes `tools/stems.py` measure exactly what the mix uses. Target: stems sum to the mix (re-measure with `measure.py`), no sample at full scale before mastering.

**1. Articulations (Dan's priority 1).** Rules applied to the MIDI from the printed marks in `notes.csv`, for every staff:
- slurred notes (slur start/in): extend to overlap the next note by 30-50 ms; unslurred unmarked notes: shorten to about 85-90 % of the notated length (gap 60-120 ms, larger at slower tempi); slur end notes: 90 %. Target: in each sustained-instrument stem, the detached boundary dip at least 4 dB deeper than the slurred dip (now equal within 1 dB).
- staccato: keep 50 % for winds and brass; strings 35-40 % of the notated length (the string-ensemble sample has a long release). Target: violin staccato level at 75 % of notated length below -12 dB (now -3 to -5 dB).
- tenuto: 100 % length, +5 velocity. accent: keep +20 velocity (measured +2 to +7 dB); for strings and brass add a shorter note-on with a short CC11 dip after the attack (about -4 dB after 150 ms) if the stem allows it.
- tremolo: strings to GM program 44 (tremolo strings) on their own stem, gain-matched with `sfcal` (+0 to +2 dB vs program 48); timpani rolls as repeated strokes at 12-16 per second with velocity varying ±8 around the printed level and slightly lower base velocity. Targets: timpani roll level at 75 % within 6 dB of its start (now -34 dB); string tremolo stems either program 44 or more than 10 envelope peaks/s.
- pizz.: 35 notes (basses 31, cellos 4) to GM program 45 on a separate stem, gain +11 dB relative to arco (sfcal). Target: audible length below 0.6 s and below 1.0 x notated length (now 1.3-1.44 x).
- later cycle (try in r01 only if time allows): VPO samples converted with Polyphone, one SF2 per articulation (staccato, pizzicato, tremolo, accent, sustain), to get real articulation samples instead of length tricks.

**2. Volume of individual tracks (Dan's priority 2).** Static gain per stem (CC7 or ffmpeg gain) derived from `stems_r00`, so that at the same printed dynamic the stems follow the orchestral ratios, then dynamics as continuous expression:
- first-estimate corrections (dB, relative to Violins I, from section 3 against Rimsky-Korsakov's ratios read as +3 dB per "twice"): Violins II +1.7, Violas +7, Cellos +5, Basses +4.5, Flutes +1, Oboes +2.5, Clarinets +2, Bass clarinet +5, Bassoons +7, Contrabassoon +9, Cor anglais 0, Horns +7, Trumpets 1-2 +9, Trumpet 3 +11.6, Trombones 1-2 +3, Bass trombone +4.5, Tuba +6.5, Timpani about +10 after the roll fix, Harp +3. Apply about half of each in r01 and re-measure (the mapping from "twice as strong" to dB is an assumption, and K-weighting lowers the reading of low instruments by a few dB). Then normalise the mix.
- printed dynamics as CC11 (expression) per staff, with hairpins as continuous ramps between the start and target dynamic (242 long notes under hairpins now static), keeping velocity for the attack. Aim for 4-5 dB per printed step per instrument (now 1.3-3.5 dB) [inferred target; FluidR3's own velocity response gives about 2.5 dB per step].
- Targets (`stems.py`): balance inversions well under 793; Trombones 1-2 no longer louder than Trumpets 1-2 at the same dynamic; strings within ±3 dB of each other at f/ff; Spearman bar loudness vs printed dynamic above 0.85 in the mix. Melody against accompaniment is not measured automatically yet: critic 1 should check the stems in the bars where `notes.csv` shows "espr.", "dolce", "molto cantabile" or solo voices.

**3. Blend (Dan's priority 3).** One stage and one room for every stem:
- pan by seating, moderate widths: Violins I -40 %, Violins II -15 %, Violas +15 %, Cellos +35 %, Basses +45 %; flutes/oboes centre ±10 %, clarinets/bassoons ±10 % behind them; horns -30 %, trumpets +10 %, trombones/tuba +30-40 %; timpani centre, harp -50 %. Use mono or narrowed stems before panning (the string and some wind samples are wide stereo, the brass mono). Target: pan spread 0.32-0.35 and pan histogram like the references (`stereo.pan_energy_hist_L_to_R`).
- one convolution reverb for all stems (Detmold Konzerthaus IR, CC BY 4.0, or Voxengo as fallback; credit in assets/README.md), with smaller send for strings (front) and larger for winds, brass and percussion (back), a 10-20 ms pre-delay difference between front and back. Target: cut-off RT60 estimate 1.3-2.0 s (now 0.9 s), high-band L/R correlation 0.35-0.55 (now -0.02), general pauses not falling 20 dB in under 0.4 s.
- tone colour with dynamics: a gentle low-pass on stems in p/pp passages (or per-note filter via soft velocity layers when VPO is used). Target: loud-minus-soft gain at 5-10 kHz of +6 to +10 dB like the references (now +4 to +5).

**4. Mastering: stop flattening the dynamics.** Replace single-pass `loudnorm` with either two-pass linear loudnorm (pass the measured_I/LRA/TP/thresh values, `linear=true`) or a plain gain to about -20 LUFS integrated plus a true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP that touches only the loudest peaks (sources 1, 2). Target: LRA 17-20 LU, short-term p10 about -15 LU below I, pp bars 13-16 dB below I, true peak -1 dBTP, 192 kb/s or higher. This is the cheapest large change and should be done in r01 regardless of the other items.

**5. Small timing and velocity variation (later cycle).** Phrase-related tempo (already in the MIDI tempo map) is the main part; add only small per-section offsets (5-15 ms, strings slightly late on soft entries) and ±3-5 velocity variation within chords; check with `measure.py` onsets that the lag stays near 20 ms and that the onset correlation does not drop.
