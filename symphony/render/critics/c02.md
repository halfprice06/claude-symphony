1. **The fade rule does not reach the rolls (bug)** [measured + code]: no roll without a crescendo falls; the level at
   the end of the roll minus the level at 0.4-0.8 s after its start is -0.9 to +1.7 dB (median +0.6) in r02, the same as

## 8. Research (with sources)

1. **Dynamics controllers.** Practitioner consensus: velocity selects attack/dynamic layer of short notes; CC1 moves
   long notes through recorded dynamic layers (timbre + level); CC11 is a secondary fader for shaping inside one layer;
   CC7 or a per-track gain is a static fader set once per track so that the curves stay intact
   (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/poll-how-do-you-use-dynamics-and-expression.101377/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/rant-about-cc1-cc11-and-velocity.168479/ ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/questions-about-prepping-ahead-for-dynamics-and-volume-for-orchestral-mock-ups.159951/).
   Consequence here: the melody lift should be a balance between staves (static/phrase fader on the accompaniment as
   well as the melody), not an extra CC11 rise that lifts the whole bar's loudness.
2. **Dynamic crossfades in SFZ.** `xfin_loccN`/`xfout_loccN` crossfade quiet and loud samples by a controller (equal
   power by default, `xf_cccurve`); `amp_veltrack=0` when a CC should own the dynamics
   (https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/xfin_loccN ; https://sfzformat.com/opcodes/xf_cccurve ;
   https://sfzformat.com/tutorials/volume). VPO's Standard scripts use velocity for volume, its Performance ("PERF") scripts
   CC1, and DXF (CC1 dynamic crossfade) files exist for trumpet, horn and trombone; woodwinds and brass have sustain,
   staccato and accent sets (https://www.virtualplaying.com/virtual-playing-orchestra/). The author dropped a legato
   articulation because overlapping sustains sounded the same (same page), which supports r02's overlap legato.
3. **Free samples to replace FluidR3 winds, brass, harp.** VPO 3 (sources SSO CC Sampling+, VSCO 2 CE CC0, No Budget
   Orchestra CC BY-SA 4.0, Westlund CC BY-SA 3.0, Iowa MIS; same page); VSCO 2 CE itself is CC0
   (https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/; SFZ release on GitHub, blocked here). The Philharmonia sound samples
   are free for use in music but may not be redistributed as-is and are single MP3 notes
   (https://philharmonia.co.uk/audio-sound-samples/) — usable only as a last resort and not to be stored as a sample
   pack. VPO is already partly in `assets/vpo3`; the full archive (616 MB, archive.org mirror in assets/README.md)
   holds the woodwind and brass files.
4. **Humanised timing.** Plain random onset jitter sounds like poor ensemble; mock-up writers keep variation within
   about ±10 ms, move sections together (a section pushes or lags as a unit) and treat legato strings as more forgiving
   than percussion (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/how-precise-in-terms-of-milliseconds-and-db-are-professional-musicians-vs-the-score.90243/post-4513796 ;
   https://musescore.org/en/node/376281). Ensemble asynchrony in performance: 30-50 ms SD in trios (Rasch 1979, via
   critic 1's sources).
5. **Depth and room.** Depth comes from the direct-to-reverberant balance and early-reflection density; a common recipe
   is a shared tail plus mostly early reflections on rear sections, narrowing woodwinds before the room; pre-delay as a
   depth cue is disputed (https://forum.vsl.co.at/post/143489 ;
   https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mixing-dry-recorded-orchestral-instruments-with-virtual-orchestration.147277/).
   Stage acoustics: reflections of brass and percussion from the back wall can mask the strings' direct sound
   (https://www.esm.rochester.edu/iml/prjc/poly/?p=3114). Orchestral directivity and seating: Meyer, Acoustics and the
   Performance of Music (https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/J%20MEYER%20ACOUSTICS%20AND%20THE%20PERFORMANCE%20OF%20MUSIC.pdf).
6. **Timpani rolls.** Timpani rolls are single strokes; speed depends on dynamic, pitch, drum size and mallet: slower on
   low and soft notes, faster for loud; too fast chokes the sound, too slow becomes a rhythm
   (https://adamholmesmusic.com/blog-notating-rolls/ ; https://www.percussionclinic.com/art_timptech.htm ;
   https://freepercussionlessons.com/how-to-properly-perform-a-timpani-roll/). A roll begins with an accented first
   stroke (https://theinstrumentalist.com/march-2009/percussion-rolls/, cited by actor 2). For a looped recorded roll,
   the periodicity to avoid is the loop length, not only the stroke rate (my measurement above).
7. **Loudness.** LRA is the 10th-95th percentile spread of gated short-term loudness (EBU Tech 3342,
   https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/tech/tech3342.pdf). Streaming practice for classical: integrated around -16 LUFS or quieter,
   true peak <= -1 dBTP (AES TD1008 as summarised in https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/aes-update-loudness-recommendations-for-audio-only-streaming ;
   https://www.roexaudio.com/blog/what-lufs-should-i-master-at). The -20 LUFS house target is fine for comparison with
   the references; LRA should come from the references (Brahms 1 18.5, Dvorak 9 21.8), not be pushed beyond them.
8. **Air absorption** at 20 m removes several dB above 8 kHz (https://sengpielaudio.com/calculator-air.htm), which the
   references show (12.7 kHz band 23-31 dB below r02).
