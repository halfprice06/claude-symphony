# Top books on orchestral composing, arranging, engraving, composition and mockups

Compiled 2026-10-09. Ranking is by how often and how strongly composers, orchestration teachers and composer forums recommend each book (sources at the end). The order inside each list is a judgment from those sources, not a published poll. Years are first publication; later editions are noted where they matter.

"Neo-Romantic fit" marks books whose examples and methods center on the 19th- and early 20th-century orchestra (Brahms through Strauss, Tchaikovsky, Rachmaninoff, Elgar), which is the sound this symphony aims at.

## Orchestral composing (writing for the orchestra)

| # | Book | Author | Year | Why it matters | Neo-Romantic fit |
|---|------|--------|------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 | *The Study of Orchestration* | Samuel Adler | 1982 (4th ed. 2016) | The standard US university text for decades; full instrument-by-instrument coverage plus orchestration chapters and Eastman recordings of every example. | Good. Many Romantic examples; weaker on detail than Piston. |
| 2 | *Orchestration* | Walter Piston | 1955 | More detailed than Adler on instrument technique, with an often-praised final part on texture and counterpoint across the whole orchestra. | Strong. Its texture chapters analyze Brahms, Tchaikovsky and Strauss. |
| 3 | *Principles of Orchestration* | Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov | 1913 (Eng. 1922; Dover reprint) | Written by one of the great orchestrators, organized by melody, harmony and balance rather than by instrument; Belkin calls it as valuable today as when it was published. | Very strong. All examples are from his own late-Romantic scores. |
| 4 | *Treatise on Instrumentation*, rev. Richard Strauss | Hector Berlioz / Richard Strauss | 1844 / 1905 | The founding orchestration treatise; Strauss's revision adds Wagner examples and his own notes on how the modern orchestra should sound. Free on IMSLP. | Very strong. It is the Romantic orchestra described by two of its main builders. |
| 5 | *The Technique of Orchestration* | Kent Kennan & Donald Grantham | 1952 (6th ed. 2002) | Compact, clearly organized course text with exercises; often used alongside Adler in teaching. | Good. Traditional repertoire, practical scoring problems. |
| 6 | *Traité de l'orchestration* (4 vols.) | Charles Koechlin | 1954–59 | The most comprehensive orchestration treatise ever written; Belkin calls it "in a class apart". French only, hard to obtain. | Strong for color and balance in late-Romantic and Impressionist writing. |
| 7 | *Orchestration* | Cecil Forsyth | 1914 (2nd ed. 1935) | Readable and witty, with excellent string chapters; frequently recommended as the most enjoyable of the classics. Brass and saxophone chapters are dated. | Strong. It describes the Elgar-era orchestra as it was being used. |
| 8 | *Artistic Orchestration* | Alan Belkin | online, 2000s | Free online book on how orchestration expresses form: building climaxes, contrast, continuity. Fills the gap the instrument manuals leave. | Strong for a symphony: it addresses large-scale pacing and climax directly. |
| 9 | *Anatomy of the Orchestra* | Norman Del Mar | 1981 | A conductor's survey of how each section actually works in the standard repertoire; not a method, but full of practical detail on balance, divisi and seating. | Very strong. Its examples are mostly Romantic and early 20th-century repertoire. |
| 10 | *The Cambridge Guide to Orchestration* | Ertuğrul Sevsay | 2013 | The most thorough modern text, with deep sections on combining instruments and on orchestrating from piano. | Good, though it covers contemporary techniques too. |

Close calls left off: Alfred Blatter, *Instrumentation and Orchestration* (1980; strong on ranges and extended techniques, thinner on orchestration); Gardner Read, *Style and Orchestration* (1979). For form rather than orchestration, Schoenberg's *Fundamentals of Musical Composition* (1967) is the usual companion book.

## Orchestral arranging (scoring existing music, pops, studio and commercial work)

| # | Book | Author | Year | Why it matters | Neo-Romantic fit |
|---|------|--------|------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 | *The Contemporary Arranger* | Don Sebesky | 1975 (definitive ed. 1994) | The standard arranging text for orchestra plus rhythm section, by an arranger with a long film and record career; known for its point about economy in scoring. | Moderate. Commercial idiom, but the string and woodwind chapters transfer. |
| 2 | *Orchestral Technique* | Gordon Jacob | 1931 | Teaches orchestration by arranging piano music for orchestra, which is exactly the short-score-to-full-score step. | Strong. British late-Romantic idiom. |
| 3 | *Sounds and Scores* | Henry Mancini | 1962 | A working arranger's guide to voicing and color, with recorded examples; still cited by film and studio orchestrators. | Moderate. Lush string and woodwind writing translates well. |
| 4 | *Arranged by Nelson Riddle* | Nelson Riddle | 1985 | Riddle's own account of how he scored for strings and brass behind singers; valued for string voicing. | Moderate. Useful for warm string textures. |
| 5 | *Acoustic and MIDI Orchestration for the Contemporary Composer* | Andrea Pejrolo & Richard DeRosa | 2007 (2nd ed. 2017) | Pairs each orchestration topic with how to realize it in samples; the standard text where live and mock-up work meet. | Moderate. Most relevant to this project's MP3 renders. |
| 6 | *The Professional Arranger Composer* | Russell Garcia | 1954 | A classic of Hollywood-era arranging pedagogy; short, practical, widely used by film and TV arrangers. | Moderate. |
| 7 | *Orchestration: A Practical Handbook* | Joseph Wagner | 1959 | Organized around scoring problems (doublings, tutti balance, transcribing from piano), which makes it an arranger's book. | Good. Traditional orchestral idiom. |
| 8 | *Thesaurus of Orchestral Devices* | Gardner Read | 1953 | A catalog of orchestral effects with repertoire citations; arrangers use it to find how a given sound was scored before. | Good. Citations are heavy on Romantic and early modern scores. |
| 9 | *Inside the Score* | Rayburn Wright | 1982 | Detailed analyses of large-ensemble scores by Thad Jones, Sammy Nestico and Bob Brookmeyer; the model for score-study books. | Weak. Big band idiom; useful for method only. |
| 10 | *100 Orchestration Tips* | Thomas Goss | 2017 | Short practical tips from an orchestrator and teacher with a large online following; aimed at people scoring for both live players and samples. | Moderate. |

Close calls left off: Ken Pullig & Richard Lowell, *Arranging for Large Jazz Ensemble* (Berklee, jazz only); Fred Karlin & Rayburn Wright, *On the Track* (1990; film scoring more than arranging).

## Orchestral engraving, editing and publishing

| # | Book | Author | Year | Why it matters | Use for this symphony |
|---|------|--------|------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 | *Behind Bars: The Definitive Guide to Music Notation* | Elaine Gould | 2011 | Written by Faber Music's senior editor; the current standard reference, with more detail than any other notation book and a full section on score and part layout. | Essential. Settles nearly every notation and layout question for score and parts. |
| 2 | *Music Notation in the Twentieth Century* | Kurt Stone | 1980 | Concise and easy to look things up in; Gould cites it as a model for its brevity and layout. | Quick lookups. |
| 3 | *The Art of Music Engraving and Processing* | Ted Ross | 1970 | The engraver's book, written from plate-engraving practice: spacing, beaming, stem and slur placement. Out of print; listed with Gould, Read and Stone as one of the "big four". | Fine detail of spacing and placement. |
| 4 | *Music Notation: A Manual of Modern Practice* | Gardner Read | 1964 (2nd ed. 1969) | A standard work on notation conventions, cited in the LilyPond bibliography; thorough on traditional practice. | Background and conventions. |
| 5 | *Guidelines for Music Preparation* | Major Orchestra Librarians' Association (MOLA) | free PDF, revised periodically | What professional orchestra librarians expect from scores and parts: paper size, page turns, cues, rehearsal marks, binding. Free. | Essential for parts that an orchestra would accept. |
| 6 | *Music Engraving Today* | Steven Powell | 2002 (2nd ed. 2007) | Covers engraving with notation software; recommended on MuseScore and notation forums. | Practical computer engraving. |
| 7 | *The Art of Music Copying* | Clinton Roemer | 1973 | The standard handbook of the professional part copyist: part extraction, cues, page turns. | Orchestral parts. |
| 8 | *Essential Dictionary of Music Notation* | Tom Gerou & Linda Lusk | 1996 | A short, inexpensive alphabetical reference used by copyists and students. | Quick lookups. |
| 9 | *The Critical Editing of Music* | James Grier | 1996 | The first full introduction to music editing: sources, transcription, editorial method (Cambridge UP). The book for the editorial side of publishing. | Editorial principles if the score is ever edited for publication. |
| 10 | *Musiknotation: Von der Syntax des Notenstichs zum EDV-gesteuerten Notensatz* | Helene Wanske | 1988 | The German engraving tradition (Schott, Bärenreiter) set down in detail; cited in the LilyPond literature list. German only. | Detailed engraving rules, mainly through LilyPond's defaults. |

Close calls left off: Herbert Chlapik, *Die Praxis des Notengraphikers* (1987, German); the Music Publishers Association booklet *Standard Music Notation Practice* (free PDF); the LilyPond documentation essay on automated engraving (free, relevant because this project engraves in LilyPond).

Engraving sources: [Behind Bars (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behind_Bars_(book)), [Scoring Notes interview with Elaine Gould](https://www.scoringnotes.com/people/interview-elaine-gould-part-2-behind-bars/), [Symposium review](https://symposium.music.org/51/item/479-behind-bars-the-definitive-guide-to-music-notation.html), [notat.io forum](https://notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=4362), [MuseScore forum](https://musescore.org/comment/61980), [LilyPond short literature list](https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/essay/short-literature-list), [MOLA guidelines (SMU copy)](https://s2.smu.edu/robfrank/MOLA.pdf), [Western University guide to preparing scores and parts](https://guides.lib.uwo.ca/musictheoryandcomposition/preparingscoresandparts), [Grier at Cambridge UP](https://www.cambridge.org/9780521558631). Dates for Ross, Roemer, Gerou & Lusk, Powell and Wanske are from general knowledge.

## Composition in general (form, harmony, counterpoint, craft)

| # | Book | Author | Year | Why it matters | Neo-Romantic fit |
|---|------|--------|------|----------------|------------------|
| 1 | *Fundamentals of Musical Composition* | Arnold Schoenberg | 1967 | The distillation of forty years of Schoenberg's teaching: how phrases, sentences, periods and whole forms are built from motives, with examples from Beethoven and Brahms. The most widely assigned composition text. | Very strong. Its developing-variation method is how Brahms worked. |
| 2 | *Gradus ad Parnassum* (Eng. *The Study of Counterpoint*, trans. Alfred Mann) | Johann Joseph Fux | 1725 (Eng. 1943) | The species-counterpoint method that Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven and Brahms studied; still the standard starting point for voice-leading discipline. | Strong. Clean inner voices are what Romantic textures depend on. |
| 3 | *Harmony and Voice Leading* | Edward Aldwell & Carl Schachter (later with Allen Cadwallader) | 1978 (5th ed. 2018) | The harmony text most respected by composition and theory teachers, treating chords as products of voice leading rather than as isolated labels. | Strong. Covers chromatic harmony through the late 19th century. |
| 4 | *The Classical Style* | Charles Rosen | 1971 (exp. ed. 1997) | National Book Award winner; explains how Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven made large forms dramatic. Composers read it for how form and tonality create tension. Pair with his *Sonata Forms* (1980). | Strong for symphonic structure; the Romantics built on this model. |
| 5 | *Classical Form* | William Caplin | 1998 (textbook *Analyzing Classical Form*, 2013) | Extends Schoenberg's theory into a precise system of themes, transitions and cadences; the standard modern form text. | Good. Classical repertoire, but the sentence and period types carry into Brahms and Dvořák. |
| 6 | *Structural Functions of Harmony* | Arnold Schoenberg | 1954 | Explains tonal regions and how far-flung modulations still serve one key, with examples from Wagner, Brahms, Bruckner and Mahler. | Very strong. Written about the exact harmonic language a neo-Romantic symphony uses. |
| 7 | *Harmony* | Walter Piston | 1941 (5th ed. rev. DeVoto, 1987) | The long-standing American harmony textbook by a composer, built on repertoire examples rather than rules. | Good. Strong chapters on chromatic and late-Romantic harmony. |
| 8 | *Twentieth-Century Harmony* | Vincent Persichetti | 1961 | A composer's catalog of harmonic resources beyond common practice (quartal, polychordal, modal); praised for giving tools rather than models. | Moderate. Useful for widening the harmonic palette, which critic 99 named as a gap in movement I. |
| 9 | *The Craft of Musical Composition* | Paul Hindemith | 1937 (Eng. 1941) | A complete theory of harmonic tension and melodic line from one of the century's leading composer-teachers. Idiosyncratic but influential. | Moderate. More neo-Classical than Romantic. |
| 10 | *Musical Composition: Craft and Art* | Alan Belkin | 2018 | A composition teacher's account of the general principles of large-scale form: continuity, contrast, climax, pacing. Short and practical. | Strong. Written for tonal composers thinking about long movements. |

Close calls left off: James Hepokoski & Warren Darcy, *Elements of Sonata Theory* (2006; detailed but very academic); Robert Gjerdingen, *Music in the Galant Style* (2007); Charles Rosen, *Sonata Forms* (1980); Schoenberg, *Theory of Harmony* (1911); Arnold Schoenberg, *Models for Beginners in Composition* (1942).

General composition sources: [Art of Composing reading list](https://artofcomposing.com/books-on-music-composition-composers-reading-list), [Young Composers thread](https://www.youngcomposers.com/t33489/what-books-do-you-recommend-to-learn-music-composition/), [Dummies: 10 recommended books for composers](https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/music/music-composition/10-recommended-books-for-music-composers-282803/). Dates and the details on Fux, Aldwell & Schachter, Rosen, Structural Functions, Piston *Harmony*, Hindemith and Belkin's 2018 book are from general knowledge, not checked against a source in this search.

## Orchestral mockups with sample libraries (recent)

This field has few books. Most current teaching is in paid courses and on forums (VI-Control, Berklee Online's *Orchestral Mockups in Film Scoring*), because sample libraries change faster than books are revised. These are the books worth having, newest first within reputation:

| # | Book | Author | Year | Why it matters |
|---|------|--------|------|----------------|
| 1 | *Acoustic and MIDI Orchestration for the Contemporary Composer* | Andrea Pejrolo & Richard DeRosa | 2nd ed. 2017 | The standard text: each orchestral section is covered twice, once for live players and once for sequencing with samples (CC1/CC11 dynamics, key switches, layering, panning, reverb, mixing). Used as the recommended text in Berklee mockup courses. |
| 2 | *The Art of Digital Orchestration* | Sam McGuire & Zbyněk Matějů | 2020/21 (Focal Press) | The most recent full practical book: orchestration, performance tools, MIDI programming, mixing, with online video demonstrations. |
| 3 | *Mixing Modern Orchestral Music* | Joël Dollié | 2018 (e-book) | Short book on mixing orchestral music made from sample libraries: EQ, reverb, depth and loudness. The author teaches a well-reviewed mixing course on the same subject. Aimed at film and trailer sound. |
| 4 | *The Guide to MIDI Orchestration* | Paul Gilreath & Jim Aikin | 4th ed. 2010 | The original book on the subject; very detailed section by section. The software and library chapters are out of date, but the programming principles still apply. |
| 5 | *The Hyperorchestra: Screen Music and Virtual Musical Ensembles* | Sergi Casanelles | 2025 (Palgrave Macmillan) | The newest title; an academic study of how virtual orchestras differ from real ones, by an NYU teacher of MIDI orchestration. Theory, not a how-to. |

A second search (2026-10-09) of publisher catalogs and VI-Control found no practical mockup book newer than McGuire & Matějů (2020/21); Casanelles (2025) is the only newer title. Current technique is taught in courses instead: [Berklee Online, Orchestral Mockups in Film Scoring](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/orchestral-mockups-in-film-scoring), [Guy Michelmore, Sampled Orchestration (Thinkspace)](https://thinkspace.ac.uk/courses/sampled-orchestration/), [Master The Score, The Virtual Orchestra](https://www.masterthescore.com/course/the-virtual-orchestra), Beat Kaufmann's orchestral mixing tutorial, and Dollié's *Mixing Cinematic Music* course.

Not orchestral-specific but often recommended alongside these: Mike Senior, *Mixing Secrets for the Small Studio* (2nd ed. 2018), for general mixing.

**For this project's renders:** our pipeline renders from LilyPond MIDI through soundfonts, not a DAW with commercial libraries, so the parts of these books that transfer are the programming principles in Pejrolo & DeRosa and Gilreath (dynamics through continuous controllers rather than velocity alone, articulation switching, note-length and timing adjustments, orchestral seating and panning) and Dollié's mixing chapters (reverb depth, section balance, EQ).

Mockup sources: [Sample Library Review on Gilreath](https://www.samplelibraryreview.com/the-reviews/the-guide-to-midi-orchestration/), [Sound on Sound review of Gilreath](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/guide-midi-orchestration), [Berklee Online mockup course](https://online.berklee.edu/courses/orchestral-mockups-in-film-scoring), [Routledge: The Art of Digital Orchestration](https://www.routledge.com/The-Art-of-Digital-Orchestration/McGuire-Mateju/p/book/9780367362744), [Goodreads: Dollié](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/40540588-mixing-modern-orchestral-music), [VI-Control thread on Dollié](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/mixing-modern-orchestral-music-ebook-for-sample-library-composers.72533/), [Epicomposer review (Dollié 2018 date)](https://epicomposer.com/trailer-music-mixing-review/), [Alibris: The Hyperorchestra](https://origin-www.alibris.com/search/books/isbn/9783031751929?qsort=p&svs=1), [VI-Control thread on The Hyperorchestra](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/new-book-on-the-theory-and-practice-of-composing-with-vis.159459/), [VI-Control: books on mixing MIDI orchestra](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/books-on-mixing-midi-orchestra.29423/). Mike Senior's details are from general knowledge.

## For this symphony

If only three books are bought: Rimsky-Korsakov (free on IMSLP in Russian, cheap in Dover English), Berlioz–Strauss (free on IMSLP), and Belkin (free online). Add Piston for texture and Del Mar for balance and practical detail.
