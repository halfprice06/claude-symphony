# Critique of v1 (short score)

Based on reading the engraved score, the MIDI analysis in `analysis.txt`, and the rendered length (`mvt1.mp3`, 7:04). (I cannot hear the audio; the MP3 is for Dan.)

## What works
- The overall shape is sound: slow introduction, sonata form, A♭ major second group, development climax on a dominant pedal, C-major recapitulation of the second theme, dark coda.
- The rising sixth as a unifying interval is a good idea, and the lydian G over D♭ in the second theme (bar 50) is the one genuinely haunting moment.

## What is weak (in order of importance)
1. **The themes are not memorable.** The motto (G–E♭–D–C–A♭–G) is a stock minor-key descent with no rhythmic identity; it could be by anyone. The first theme has no hook: its only feature is a dotted quarter. The second theme is pretty but its continuation (bars 51–56) wanders, and its rhythm is uniform.
2. **Literal repetition everywhere.** First theme bars 19–22 are bars 15–18 transposed up a fourth note for note. The transition repeats one three-note cell sixteen times. The development's first section is the first theme head stated three times in three keys unchanged. Both recapitulated themes are exact transpositions. A master never repeats this mechanically; repetition is always varied.
3. **Accompaniment formula.** Off-beat eighth-note chords over a repeated-eighth bass pedal are used for 18 bars in the exposition and again in the development and recapitulation. It is the most generic "agitato" texture there is.
4. **Uniform harmonic rhythm.** Almost every bar has exactly one chord, and almost every phrase is four bars. Nothing breathes, nothing hurries.
5. **The development is not development.** The fugato has no real countersubject, and the second-theme-in-minor is a transposition, not a transformation.
6. **Proportions.** 7:04 against a 6:00–6:30 target; the climax at bars 113–120 sits on a 12-bar G pedal with little harmonic motion.

## Plan for v2
Rewrite the thematic material first, because everything else is built from it.
- New motto with a chromatic tail: G↑E♭–D–D♭–C (rising minor sixth, then a three-semitone sigh). The D♭ gives the symphony a Neapolitan/Phrygian colour that can return in every movement.
- New first theme built from the motto in diminution, with a hemiola continuation (three-beat groups across the barline) instead of a literal sequence.
- New second theme: the rising sixth becomes major and recurs three times (E♭→C, A♭→F, D♭→A♭), each long note sinking by a semitone (the motto's sigh, now consoling). Nine-bar antecedent, twelve-bar consequent that climaxes on C6 after a minor-iv chord.
- Rebuild the development from the new material: motto-head dialogue by falling major thirds, a real fugato with countersubject, the second theme transformed into B♭ minor, and a rising wedge into the dominant pedal; the pedal collapses to pianissimo before the recapitulation instead of crashing into it.
- Vary the accompaniment: syncopated tied chords, hemiola quarter pulse, sustained chords with breathing harp arpeggios.
