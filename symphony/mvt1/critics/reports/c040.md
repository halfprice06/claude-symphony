## Findings

### 1. The tempo arc still reads after cycle 39. Keep it and do not add marks (no change)
The nominal timings of v59 (MIDI 414 s, MP3 7:02):
- intro 0–67 s;
- exposition Allegro from 67 s;
- development climax at 114 ≈ 261 s (63%);
- ♭VI climax at 156 ≈ 345 s (83%);
- denied tutti at 166 ≈ 381 s;
- slow close 167–178 ≈ 46 s.

Every remaining slowing now belongs to one of two ideas:
- the second theme (hope): poco rit. 45–46 and meno mosso 47; poco rit. 137–138 and meno mosso 139; largamente 156;
- the motto (fate): allargando and sostenuto 113–118; the fermata at 166; Più lento → rit. → Tempo I at 167–178.

Before cycle 39, the word at 73 was the only slowing tied to neither, and the A♭-minor motto at 73–76 is better heard inside the Allegro's time: it is the motto entering the Allegro.

The coda's graded descent (126 → fermata → 72 → rit. → 50) matches the models' use of an intermediate coda tempo: Brahms 1/i Meno allegro, Tchaikovsky 6/i Andante mosso.

**Fix:** none. Do not restore "poco più tranquillo" at 73, and do not make 167 "Tempo I": at ♩=50 the close would last 58 s and lose its graded descent.

### 2. The motto's journey is clear, and each slow statement changes meaning (no change)
Each statement has its own colour and dynamic, so the meaning changes without any new tempo:

| Bars | Statement | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | Bass clarinet, Vc, Cb pp over a timpani C roll | question |
| 5–8 | Cor anglais on D | lament, ending over the C-major chord with the first E♮ |
| 73–76 | Flute in F minor over an A♭ pedal | shadow on the second key |
| 109–113 | Brass ff, augmented, the form without the C | fate asserted |
| 119–122 | Stretto from pp | fate gathering |
| 138–140, 147–149 | Major form | hope |
| 167–170 | Cor anglais at the tonic | the intro's answer has become the statement |
| 171–175 | Trombone chorale with tam-tam | ritual |
| 173–177 | Violins | acceptance |

Tchaikovsky 5 keeps the motto out of movement I's Allegro and coda and so saves it for later movements. This movement uses it throughout instead. That is legitimate, but it means movement III cannot rely on the motto's return alone. It needs a "new element" in Franck's sense (findings 3 and 4).

### 3. cyclic-notes.md: the chorale with tam-tam is a funeral sound, so movement III must change the colour (correction)
cyclic-notes says movement I "plants the sound" of the chorale at 171–175. That sound is the pp trombone chorale over a pp tam-tam, which is Tchaikovsky 6/iv bars 137–146, the Pathétique's death music.

In movement I the motto's descending major form (G–E–D–C) is heard only at p to mf: horn 138–140 and violins 147–149. At ff (bar 154) the brass take the sixth G–E and then turn upward to A♭. What movement I never sounds is the major descending motto in full brass at forte.

**Correction to the notes:**
- Movement III's chorale should keep 171–175's three-part voicing and pitches, in major, at f/ff.
- It should add the horns and trumpets.
- It must not use the tam-tam.

The chorale then recalls movement I's chorale as transformed, not repeated.

### 4. cyclic-notes.md: the unresolved B♮ is a four-point thread, not one note (correction)
The leading tone is left hanging in the voice that sings it at four junctures:
- Hn1 at 14 (E♭–C–B, then rests at the Allegro);
- Tpt1·2 and Hn1·2 at 166;
- Vc at 170 (`c''2 b'2`, then the cello drops to G);
- Cl1 at 171–173 (`es'4 | c''1~ | c''2 b'2`).

The clarinet line is the intro horn call an octave up. At 173 its B completes the 166 chord (B–D–F–A♭) at pp.

**Correction:** the notes should list all four. Movement III's resolution B→C (critic 30) then answers a thread through the whole movement. For the chorale's tail, movement I offers two forms:
- the intro form C–D♭–C, which in major gives the Neapolitan and keeps the fate inflection;
- the first-theme form C–B–C (Vln I 15–16, `c''4 b'8 c''8`).

Use the first form for the chorale's first statement and C–B–C for its last.

### 5. cyclic-notes.md: A♭ and D♭ for movement II (addition)
A♭ is the pitch of each climax: the A♭ bass crash at 82, the top a♭''' over G at 114, and the ♭VI chord at 156. Movement II's tonic is therefore the pitch that fate struck on in movement I.

The motto's D♭ becomes IV in A♭. Movement II can harmonise the motto's tail as a plagal, consonant figure (E♭–D–D♭–C over IV–I in A♭), so the "fate note" is heard as warmth.

### 6. cyclic-notes.md: citation and fact fixes
1. The minor anacrusis head in B minor sits at bars 97–99 (anacrusis at 97, head at 98–99), not 97–98. This matches how the major form is cited (138–140).
2. The first bar of the minor head (`g4 | es'2.~ es'8 d'8`) is the same as the first bar of the motto with its pickup:
   - Vln I 9–10 begins this way;
   - the cor anglais at 167–168 plays it complete.

   The two diverge only in their second bar: C–D♭–C for the motto, C–A♭–G for the theme. Later movements can start one and continue with the other, as 167–169 already does between cello and cor anglais.
3. The duration is now about 7:02 (MP3) or 6:54 (MIDI), not 7:13. Movement III at 6 minutes or more still holds.

### 7. The ending leaves the right question (no change)
- Tchaikovsky 5/i ends in plain minor to keep the narrative open.
- Brahms 1/i ends in plain C major.
- Elgar 1/i's closing motto is "indecisive".

v59 sits between these. Bar 177 is C minor. At 178 only the violas move E♭→E♮, at ppp, morendo, under fermatas. The question is "is that major real?", which is the one movement III answers and the one movement II can open from (E♮→E♭).

Keep it as it is. Movement III must not give a soft, strings-only C-major tonic chord before its chorale, or it will repeat this question instead of answering it.
