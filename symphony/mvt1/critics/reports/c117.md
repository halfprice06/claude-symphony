## Findings

### 1. Bars 103–106: the second-theme head has lost its cello colour (applied)

Problem. In the combination at 103–106 the first-theme head is in Ob 2 + Cl 1·2 (woodwind only) and the second-theme head is in Hn 1 (mf) with Bn 1 an octave lower. No string plays either element. The second theme was exposed as horn + cello (47–55) and returns as horn + viola + cello (147–155). At 103 the listener has to find it by its pitches alone, inside a wind-only texture right after the ff tutti at 97–102.

Evidence. Adler's *Meistersinger* analysis (PDF 177–178) gives two reasons the three elements stay clear: each was exposed separately before the combination, and each keeps "a very distinctive color combination". His first observation (PDF 178) is that "the color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument". The second theme there is Vln I + Cl 1, with the cellos an octave lower and Horn I doubling the cellos. That is almost exactly the colour this theme already has at 47. On PDF 179 he adds that the big nineteenth-century sound, forte or piano, needs mixed-colour doubling. The Mozart analysis (PDF 176) makes the same point: every statement of the cantus firmus is "supported by one member of the string choir".

Answer to the earlier decision. Cycle 74 made 103–106 "winds and horns only" as a colour plan: rest the strings after the ff string tutti 97–102, and keep the string entries at 107 (violas) and 111 (Vln I) fresh. This fix keeps that plan for the upper strings. Vln I, Vln II and violas still rest at 103–106. Only the cellos join, and they move from the bass function they had at 97–102 to the tenor tune. That is a change of role, not more string weight. The violas still take the head over at 107, so the theme passes from cello to viola, which is a better handover than wind to viola. Notes, rhythms and the f/p contrast of 103–110 are unchanged.

Fix (dev.ily, `devVc`, replacing `R1*4` at 103–106; the line doubles Bn 1, an octave under Hn 1):

The cellos go from F♯2 at 102 to F3 at 103, a small shift in bass clef. The Cb still rests.

### 2. Bars 117–121: every layer is ff, and the timpani roll starts ff (applied)

Problem. At G (117) there are three layers, as in Holst's "Jupiter" (PDF 180–181): the foreground is the augmented motto in Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and Bn 1, together with the first-theme head fragments in Fl/Ob/Vln; the middleground is the Cl 1·2 + viola triplets; the background is the G pedal. All of them start ff, including the timpani roll and the cellos' measured sixteenths. The triplets lie at B3–G4, the same register as the motto's E♭4–C4 in horns and trombones, and on the downbeat of 118 they touch the motto's own E♭4. The motto in augmentation is the cyclic statement of the development, so it is the line that must not be covered.

Evidence. Adler PDF 184 describes Schoenberg's practice: principal gestures are marked p, and subsidiary ideas "if they lie in any kind of piercing register" are marked pp, one level lower. On the same page, about Bloch's build to m. 243: "The composer wisely marks both percussion instruments piano and then crescendo, because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra and completely change the emphasis of this passage, which is meant to develop the themes and combine them for the sake of musical tension, not merely to build a climax." This score already follows that at 126–130 (timp pp → mf → f under the motto stretto) and at 159 (suspended cymbal pp <). The roll at 117 is the exception.

Fix (dev.ily). The thematic layers and the bass stay ff, and fff stays only at 122.
- `devClOne`, `devClTwo`, `devVla`: first triplet of 117 `\tuplet 3/2 { b8\f d' f' }`; first triplet of 121 `\tuplet 3/2 { c'8\< es' g' }` into the existing fff at 122. Both clarinets get the same marks, so the shared staff is consistent.
- `devTimp`: `g,1:32\f~` at 117. The existing `\<` at 121 leads to fff at 122.
- `devVc`: `g,1:16\f` at 117 and `g,1:16\<` at 121.

This changes the arrival level, not the crescendo chain 110→117 itself. The 111 mp cresc. now reaches f in these parts and ff in the thematic parts. HISTORY protects 117–122 (cycle 104, for the octave fill under the top line) and the G pedal. Neither the notes nor the fill are touched.

### 3. Bars 56–59: the accompaniment is as loud as the countermelody, in the same register (applied)

Problem. Under the violins' tune (p, molto cantabile), the counterline in Cl 1 + Hn 2 (p espr., E♭4–B♭4) shares its register with two background layers that are also marked p. One is the violas' divided off-beat dyads (C4–F4), the other the Hn 3·4 pad (A♭3–D♭4). There are three p layers in one octave, and the two soloists on the counterline have the same marking as a divided viola section and two horns.

Evidence. Adler PDF 184: subsidiary material in a competing register is marked one level below the principal ideas. PDF 179 (*Scheherazade*): the background sustained harmony is a separate, lower layer under the theme and the middleground figure. In the exposition's first half (47–55) these violas are already pp.

Fix (tTwo.ily): `tTwoVla` 56 `r4 <c' es'>4\pp^"div." <c' e'>2`; `tTwoHnThree` 56 `c'1\pp`; `tTwoHnFour` 56 `aes1\pp`. The two horns share a staff and both get pp. The existing crescendos at 60–61 and the sub. p at 62 stay as they are.

### 4. Bars 94–95: last fugato entry with static inner voices (not applied, observation)

At the fourth entry (Fl 1 + Vln I, with Cl 1 + Vln II on the countersubject), the free voices stop moving. The violas hold F4 for five beats, and the cellos and Bn 1 hold B♭ for six. Adler's Mozart analysis (PDF 176) shows the combination at its densest with every strand moving and keeping its colour. Here, though, the held notes act as a background layer under two active lines, which Adler's three-layer model also allows. The crescendo into 97 works. I would leave it. If the composer wants more motion, the place for it is the viola, in quarter notes (a rhythm neither subject nor countersubject uses), within the B♭ minor / E♭ minor–F harmony of 94–95. The subject, entries and countersubject stay protected.
