## Findings

### 1. 13–14: the cyclic horn call is doubled in unison by the viola and starts under Hn 3 (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 1's call E♭3–C4–B3 (13.2–14) is one of the movement's cyclic seeds. Its B♮ is the first of the "four unresolved B♮s" in cyclic-notes. Cl 1 recalls the call at 175–177, and movement III is planned to answer it with the trumpets' B→C.
- From 13.3 the viola's lower voice (`<c' d'>2 | <c' d'>4 <b d'>2`) plays the same C4 and then the same B3 as the horn, in the same rhythm. vl.py flags this as `bar 14 beat 2.00 P8 hnOne:C4->B3 vla.lo:C4->B3`.
- Hn 3's G3 also holds through beat 2 of 13 (`g2`), a third above the horn's entering E♭3. The solo horn therefore enters under another horn.
- So the call that the coda recalls as a clean solo line (Cl 1, an octave higher, above the pad) is a horn-plus-viola unison in its first statement.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: keep the accompanying forces "registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible". A unison doubling is no distance at all.
- PDF 283 (Mahler): the voice is "helped in pitch, yet not doubled throughout".
- PDF 264 (Mozart Horn Concerto No. 2, Ex. XVII-9): Mozart "never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn is playing … so as not to interfere with the solo sound".
- PDF 275: lead the player to the first pitch. The viola's C4 at 13.1 already does this, so it can stop once the horn has the C.
- Corpus: Dvořák 9/ii 7–10. The muted violas stay below the cor anglais and never take its pitches.

**Answer to c122.** Cycle 122 put the viola's C at 13–14 to make 13.1 a complete V6/4. It accepted that the horn's C "arrives on a note that is already sounding". My fix keeps the viola's C on 13.1, so the 6/4 is still complete, and keeps the viola's 6–5 E♭–D on beat 3 (cycle 42). Only the 4–3 is left to the horn alone, which is the horn's own line. The horn's notes, the Ger6–V at 12–13 and the silence on 14.4 are unchanged.

**Fix (intro.ily).**
```
introVla, 13–14:     <c' es'>2\p\>( d'2) | d'2.\pp r4 |      % was <c' es'>2\p\>( <c' d'>2) | <c' d'>4\pp <b d'>2 r4 |
introHnThree, 13:    g4\p r4 r2 |                            % was g2\p r2 |
```

Hn 3's F♯→G resolution stays, on beat 1. Ob 2 and Vln II also resolve F♯ to G. At 14 the chord is G–B–D–F with the B in Hn 1 only. The viola now plays single notes from 13.3, which is easier as well.

### 2. 174: cor anglais and cello meet on C5 (no change)

**What happens.** The C.a.'s motto ends on C5, entered at 173.4 and held through 174 beats 1–2. The cello's minor second-theme head rises F4–A♭4 into C5 at 174. The two solo lines merge on one pitch, and the cello's peak arrives on a sound that is already there. Read against Adler PDF 273–274, which says instruments blend when they share a register, this is a candidate fault.

**Why I recommend no change.** The rest of 171–174 follows row 3 exactly: the C.a. holds E♭5 while the cello moves (172), and moves in quarters while the cello holds a dotted figure (173). At 174 the motto's last note and the second theme's peak become the same C. That is the movement's last joining of its two cyclic shapes before the chorale, and the cello then leaves alone on the unresolved B. The duet's pitches are protected (cycles 107, 125, 157, 170). The reading gives no reason strong enough to reopen them.

### 3. 148 and 150: the cor anglais's answers land on the Vln II tremolo's lower note (no change; optional)

**What happens.** The C.a.'s answers to the C-major second-theme head (the motto's major form) end on C5 at 148 and D5 at 150. Vln II's div. sul tasto tremolo is already holding those pitches (`<c'' a''>`, `<d'' f''>`). At 153 the C.a.'s C5 sits a second under the tremolo's D5. By Adler PDF 274 the answers' goal notes partly blend into the halo.

**Why no change.** The tremolo is pp sul tasto and the C.a. p, and the rhythm separates them, which Adler allows ("rhythmic contrast lessens the importance"). The answers and the accompaniment design 147–155 are protected (cycles 114, 157, 158).

**Optional, if the composer hears the answers swallowed.** At 150 only, Vln II `<f'' a''>1:32` (D5 out; D is still in the cellos and harp). Bar 148's C–C♯ line (cycle 82) should not be touched. Not applied.

### 4. Notes for movements II and III (cyclic; for the coordinator, not for cyclic-notes.md)

1. **Fresh colour after an attacca (Adler PDF 263).** Movement II already opens its solos after a wind passage (its c090). Movement III should follow the same rule. Movement II ends with Cl 1's tail, Vln I's C5 and muted horns 3·4, so III's first theme should not start in the clarinet or in solo violin. Strings with brass, or a woodwind choir, keeps III's first colour fresh.
2. **Owed soft trumpet melody (Adler PDF 264, 267).** Mozart keeps the orchestral horns silent under a solo horn and uses no orchestral flutes in a flute concerto. When III's trumpet finally plays its soft melody, keep the other trumpets and the horns out of its accompaniment and register. Its first entry should be undoubled; the octave doubling is for its climax (Bizet, PDF 279–281).
3. **Timpani as a link (Adler PDF 273).** Movement I's last timpani pulse (179–180) is the first group's rhythm `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4`. In III the timpani can use this rhythm between phrases of the major chorale, as Beethoven's timpani join the two halves of the violin concerto's theme. Movement II's tuning has no D, so stay on C and G.
4. **The horn call's return (Haydn re-doubling, PDF 288).** The call E♭–C–B has had Hn 1 (13–14, now undoubled) and Cl 1 an octave up (175–177). In III, give its major form (E–C–B→C) to the trumpets, with the B rising to C. Double it in a new way: for example horns two octaves below, not in unison.
5. **Canon (Fidelio, PDF 283).** Movement I ends with the motto in canon at pp (chorale bass, then Vln I two bars later). III's forte chorale can reuse the canon at f with the violins entering two bars after the brass. Keep the texture light around the canon, and give the chorale an independent bass (cycle 160 note).
6. **Mutes (Bizet "senza sordini", Mahler "con sord.", PDF 281–282).** Horns 3·4 end movement II con sord. Movement III's first Hn 3·4 entry needs "senza sord." (repeats movement II's relay).
