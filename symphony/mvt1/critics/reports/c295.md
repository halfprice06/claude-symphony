## Findings

### 1. Exposed entries after long rests have no cue (bars 94, 115, 181, 182)
**What is wrong.** MOLA PDF 5 says cues are "expected during long periods of rest" and "must be audible and obvious"; Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows the form: a small-note cue, labelled, in the bars just before the entry. c265 added nine cues, but several of the most exposed entries still come after long rests with nothing to hear:
- Vln I 94 and Fl 1 94: the fourth fugato entry, f, alone on its own line, after 84–93 (10 bars). This is the most common place for a cue in an orchestral part.
- Ob 1 115 (after 103–114) and timpani 115 (after 98–114, with the A♭ retuning at 111), in the wedge.
- Hn 3 and Hn 4 181: a ppp entry on the last chord after 171–180, under a tempo change at 179. Hn 3 then ties into movement II.
- Harp 182: the ppp last chord after 167–181.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, parts-gen.py only):**
```
V2 = ("cueVlnTwo", "Vln. II");  TIH = ("cueTimpHigh", "Timp.")
Fl 1:  V2+("DOWN",92,2)        Vln I: V2+("DOWN",92,2)
Ob 1:  VI+("DOWN",113,2)       Timp:  VC+("UP",113,2)
Hn 3, Hn 4: TIH+("DOWN",179,2) (the 179–180 pulse, an octave up so it sits in the stave)
Harp RH: ("cueVlnOne" "Vln. I" ,UP 179 2)
\addQuote "cueVlnTwo" { \vlnTwo }   \addQuote "cueTimpHigh" { \transpose c c' \timp }
```
All 30 parts keep their page counts. Every part that crosses into movement II still ends on an even page (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc 2 pp; Cb 4; Hn 3, Hn 4, timp 2 with cover). Three page turns moved, all into longer rests: Vln I from 73–78 to 84–91 (the c201 plan named 84–93), Fl 1 from 111–114 to 103–108, Ob 1 from 162–166 to 147–155.
Not cued, with reasons: tutti entries on a rehearsal letter or tempo change (23, 26, 82, 96–97, 158, 167). Percussion 159 and 175 are not cued either. A pitched cue cannot go on the one-line staff, and both entries come one bar after a letter (K, M).

### 2. Contrabassoon cue at 67–68 is on five ledger lines
**What is wrong.** The contrabassoon part is read an octave above sounding, so the cello cue quoted into it rises to C5 in the bass clef at 68 (audit: "5 ledger lines"). MOLA PDF 5 asks for cues in the reading key that the player can take in at a glance. A cue an octave higher than the cellos' own written notes, on five ledger lines, does not do that. Its whole-bar rest also crossed the cue beam.
**Fix (applied):** `\addQuote "cueVcLow" { \transpose c c, \vc }` and the Cbsn cue `VCL+("UP",67,2)`. The cue now prints E♭2–C4, at the cellos' written pitch, and the part's rest sits clear below it.

### 3. Rest ranges are not counted in vertical spacing, so markings land on them
**What is wrong.** In the percussion part, "largamente" (160) is printed on top of the range "128–130" of the system above. MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) name exactly this: anchored markings that collide after extraction. The cause is general. `mmr-with-range` (c265) draws the range under the rest, but the rest's height comes from LilyPond's own callback, which ignores it. The skyline that spaces the systems therefore does not see the range, and any part can hit this when a tall marking falls below such a rest.
**Fix (applied, parts-gen.py \layout):** `\override MultiMeasureRest.Y-extent = #ly:grob::stencil-height`. Page counts and page turns are unchanged in all 30 parts, and the percussion collision is gone.

### 4. Viola 117: the f dynamic touches the triplet 3 (score and part)
**What is wrong.** At G (117) the viola's first triplet number is printed below the notes, against its own f. This is the same kind of collision as in Ex. 7, PDF 12, and it appears in both the score (page 11) and the part.
**Fix (applied, sections/dev.ily, devVla):** `\tupletUp` before the first triplet of 117 and `\tupletNeutral` after the `\undo \omit TupletNumber` of 121. The numbers now sit above the group, clear of the dynamic. No pitch or rhythm changes, and vl.txt is identical.

### 5. Fl 1 rest 42–44 printed as 42–43 | 44
**What is wrong.** MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 3–4 (PDF 10) show one numbered rest per silence, split only at rehearsal letters and tempo or metre changes. The Fl 1 part prints "2 (42–43)", then a one-bar rest at 44, then "2 (45–46)". The split at 45 (poco rit.) is right; the split at 44 is not. The score's `\partCombineAutomatic` sits between the rests in transFluteOne, and parts-lib.ily keeps it, so `merge-mmrs!` cannot join them.
**Fix (applied, parts-lib.ily):** a `part-combine-force?` test in `drop-for-part?` drops the partCombineForced property events (bare, or wrapped in ContextSpeccedMusic) from the parts. I compared every range in all 30 parts before and after: this rest is the only change (42–44, then 45–46). An automatic check of all adjacent ranges found no other unjustified split.
