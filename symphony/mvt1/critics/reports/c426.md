## Findings

### 1. Double basses 5-12: the bows run against the crescendos and against the other strings. Applied.

Bars 5-14 are one bow per whole note in every string part. The cellos enter at 6. Their bows run like this: 6 down, 7 up with the crescendo, 8 down with the diminuendo, 9 up with the crescendo, 10 down, 11 up, and 12 down at f. Violas and Vln II do the same, using the double up-bow at 11.3 (c316, c406). The basses enter one bar earlier, alone on C2 at 5. A player restriking after the long tie of 3-4 starts a new note down-bow by habit. Counted from there, the basses play 6 up, 7 **down** with the crescendo, 8 **up** with the diminuendo, 9 **down** with the crescendo, 10 up and 11 down. They then need a retake to reach the marked down-bow at 12. So for six bars the bass section bows opposite to the rest of the strings, and each hairpin falls on the wrong stroke.

Adler (PDF 38) says crescendo comes naturally on an up-bow because the hand gains leverage toward the frog. Diminuendo comes naturally on a down-bow. The composer marks bowings exactly where the players' habit has to be overruled. He also reminds the reader (PDF 39) that the bass bow is shorter than the others, so a heavy stroke is harder to save on it. A single up-bow at the soft entry of bar 5 sets the habit right. From 6 the basses then move with the cellos: 7 and 9 up, 8 down, and the f at 12 on a natural down-bow with no retake. Starting a pp entry at the tip, after a tie, also helps the soft attack.

c406 saw this count and kept it. Its reason was that a retake before a strong attack is the Coriolanus pattern (Ex. II-20) and that a Lento leaves time for it. That reason covers the retake at 11→12. It does not cover the crescendos at 7 and 9 and the diminuendo at 8, which go against the bow in the basses only. Those are the bars that Adler's PDF 38 rule is about, and one mark fixes all of them. The marks c316 added at 12 (down, then up on the tie) stay. They now confirm the natural stroke.

Fix (intro.ily, introCb bar 5):
```
c,1\pp\upbow | g,1 | f,1\< | c,1\p\> |
```
This changes marks only. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The up-bow prints cleanly under the pp in the score (PDF page 2) and in the bass part.

### 2. Checked and kept: nothing else in the strings conflicts with the reading.

- **Vln I 39, accented D6 on an up-bow.** The slurs at 37-40 give 39.1-2 down and the d'''2-> up, then the run at 40 up into the down-bow sf at 41. The crescendo runs through 37-41. An up-bow suits it (PDF 38), and the down-bow sf at 41 comes out naturally. It should stay as it is.
- **Fff A♭6 at 160 on an up-bow** (Vln I, II and Vc, after the marked down-bow C at 160.1). The note carries a tenuto, not an accent, and the up-bow follows the crescendo habit. The 160.4→161 retake is protected (c316). It should stay as it is.
- **Vla 158-161, repeated quaver double stops at ff-fff** (C4/G4, B3/F4, C4/E♭4, D4/F4). Each pair lies on the G and D strings in first position. These are détaché double stops with no shifts (PDF 29, 41). They should stay as they are.
- **c411's sul D G4→E♭5 at 9.4-10.** This is one slurred shift on the D string at mp, the same as the protected 177-178. It is playable, and the portamento it implies is the intended colour (PDF 33: portamento is the natural way to join distant melody notes).
- **Part layout.** Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc parts have two facing pages and no turn. The bass part turns at 171-174 inside a rest (protected, c416). In the parts the 8va at 121-123 is written out at pitch, by design (c245). Every part that crosses into movement II still ends on an even page.
