## Findings

### 1. 68/69: four parameters change at once with nothing carried across (APPLIED)
**Problem.** Bar 68 is a rit. down to MIDI ♩≈88 on a p deceptive F-minor chord. At 69, four things change on the same downbeat:
- tempo, to 126 (+43%);
- dynamic, p to f;
- texture, from 11 voices to 27;
- harmony, vi to I.

Nothing overlaps the barline: no crescendo, no anacrusis, no held note. The other tempo snaps in the movement are each prepared or separated. 14/15 has a full beat of silence. 166/167 has the p crescendo, E♮→E♭ and the timpani roll. 170/171 has a fermata. 68/69 is the only join that is neither prepared nor separated, so it sounds pasted. The codetta then opens I–vi (bass A♭–F), so the F minor just heard comes straight back, which makes the downbeat sound like a restart rather than an arrival.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 302–305: the decay into "Andante come prima" is bridged by a rising arco bass scale F♯–G♯–A–A♯–B (305), pp, under the theme.
- Dvořák 9/i 22–24: Vln I hold B5 across the change from Adagio to Allegro molto.
- In both corpus movements the only unprepared loud attack after a slowing is Tchaikovsky's 160/161 (ppppp → ff, Allegro vivo), used once, to open the development.
- Rimsky-Korsakov p. 112: "Short crescendi … are generally produced by natural dynamic means", and a note is emphasised by "a chain of … notes in the form of a scale … an upward glide".
- A deceptive cadence followed by a stronger restatement is Schmalfeldt's "one more time" pattern. The restatement should sound driven by the evasion, not stuck on after it.

**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily, bar 68 only, no notes changed in 69):**
```
Vln I:  aes'2\p aes'16(\< c'' des'' es'' f'' g'' aes'' bes'') |   % glide into c''' at 69
Vln II: \after 2 \< aes1\p |      Vla: \after 2 \< <c' f'>1\p |
Vc:     f,8(\p c f aes c'2\<) |   Cb:  \after 2 \< f,1\p |
Cl 1, Hn 2: \after 2 \< c'1\p |
```
- The hairpins close on the existing f at 69. The rit. and its \mt steps are unchanged.
- The run uses the 16th run of bar 40, the only other 16th run into an arrival, so it is motivic, not new.
- Answer to cycles 11/19 ("endings 64–68 do-not-touch"): the cadential figures of 64–68 and the beat-1 deceptive chord are untouched. Only beats 3–4 of 68 change, after the cadence has landed.
- 68/69 now rhymes with 166/167 (rit. + crescendo + Tempo dell'Allegro). That fits the rotation described in coda.ily, where 167–170 stand where 69–72 stood. The two joins still differ in material: a glide here, the E♮→E♭ turn and timpani roll there.

### 2. 32/33: the elision chord cut off from its dominant by two beats of silence (APPLIED)
**Problem.** The Allegro's first 18 bars stop dead three times (end of 14, 26, 32). At 32 the stop is the longest: two hammered V chords, a two-beat rest, then an isolated ff eighth chord on i, then the bassoon at mf. With the chord preceded by silence and followed by mf, it reads as a separate stroke rather than the end of the V. The recapitulation already fills this bar: 142 holds c'''2 b''2 straight into the 143 chord.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 39–42: the first group's ff climax decays through staggered descending scales (Vln I 39, Vln II 40, Vla 41, Vc 42; ff→f→mf→p→pp). There is no tutti silence before the pp saltando transition.
- Dvořák 9/i 181: the exposition's last G-major chord is struck ff, and the development's bass figure G–B–D♯ starts in the same bar with no gap.
- Rimsky-Korsakov p. 112: a short upward scale slurred into the main note stresses the arrival chord.

**Fix (applied, tOne.ily bar 32, violins only):**
```
Vln I:  c'''4-> b''4-> r4 f''16(\< g'' aes'' b'') |
Vln II: c''4-> b'4-> r4 f'16(\< g' aes' b') |
```
- Beat 3 stays silent, so the hammer-and-breath gesture survives (the MIDI silence drops from 2 beats to 1).
- F–G–A♭–B spells the V7♭9 already sounding, and B resolves to the C of the chord.
- 27–32 layout, chromatic bass, brass grading and the hemiola are unchanged. The coda's 170 stop (protected) remains the only fermata stop.

### 3. Hammered stops as phrase endings: six times (no action, note for II/III)
Accented tutti chords followed by a rest or a stop end phrases at 26, 32, 72, 83, 138 and 170. Five of these are protected (cycle 90: 26/138/170; the codetta 69–72; the dev 82–83 tutti). With Finding 2 the 32 stop is now softened. Movements II and III should end phrases by overlap or decay more often (cf. Tchaikovsky 6/i 39–42).

### 4. 102/103: ff tutti cut to f winds with no decay (proposal, not applied; protected join)
The full B-minor block ends at ff with no hairpin in any part, and seven winds and horns start the theme combination at f. The harmonic link (G♯→A, F♯→D) is good and protected by cycle 72, and the f/p plan of 103–110 is protected by cycles 74/84. A minimal answer that changes no note in 103–110 would put a `\>` on beats 3–4 of 102 in strings and brass, so the drop from ff is heard as a decay. Untested; leave it to the composer.

### 5. 14/15 caesura plus f entry (no action, protected by cycle 50)
For the record: Tchaikovsky 6/i 18/19 also uses a caesura into the Allegro but keeps the dynamic level (pp → p, violas). Dvořák 9/i holds a note across. Our join combines silence with a dynamic jump. It is acceptable as the movement's single "out of silence" start.

### 6. The pedal links are at model level
- 76/77 (A♭ held from the codetta into the development).
- 130/131 (G pedal with measured accelerando).
- 174/175 (timpani G carried into the augmented motto).

These match Dvořák 9/i 87–91, where a pp C♯/D pedal under ostinato carries the music into the second theme. Keep them.
