# Critic 494 — v494, lens: orchestration

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 5 "Scoring for Strings" (adler1 PDF 125–167), third pass. I read every text page in the range. I rendered every page at 110 dpi and looked at each example: V-1 to V-6 (Haydn op. 76/3, PDF 126–131), V-7 to V-11 (Brahms 3/iii and the four rescorings, PDF 132–134), V-12/13 (Mozart, PDF 136–137), V-14 (Leonore 3, PDF 137), V-15 (Beethoven PC 4/ii, PDF 138), V-16/17 (Haydn 103, Hindemith, PDF 139–140), V-18/19 (Schumann 2/iii, Shostakovich 6/iii, PDF 141), V-20 (Beethoven 7/ii, PDF 142–143), V-21 to V-24 (Vivaldi, Bach, Weber, Bartók, PDF 144–149), V-25 to V-28 (overtone spacing, inversions, ways to strengthen a melody, PDF 150–152), V-29 (Tchaikovsky Serenade, PDF 153–154), V-30 (Grieg, PDF 155), V-31 (Penderecki, PDF 156–157), V-32 to V-38 (accompaniment formulas, PDF 158–162), V-39 to V-43 (piano to strings, PDF 163–166) and the listening list (PDF 166–167).

Method. I dumped every string note of v494's full.midi bar by bar (847 lines) and read it through, and I looked at score pages 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 15 and 16. I wrote three scans in /tmp/critic-494/scripts: `dump.py` (notes per bar and instrument), `unis.py` (every unison between two string sections that lasts at least an eighth, with or without the same rhythm) and `space.py` (every string sonority of four or more notes on a beat whose gaps widen upward). Critics 294 and 384 made the first two passes over this chapter. Their fixes (anacrusis clearances at 19, 37, 143; the Vln II halo at 154) are in the score, and I do not repeat them.

## Findings

None to apply. Every principle in the table is followed, or departs from the reading only where an earlier critic protected the passage for a reason I cannot answer. Below are the candidates I tested against the reading for this pass, and why each stays.

### Checked and kept

**60–61, the violas' rising arpeggio crosses above Vln II (PDF 125, 135).** At 60.3 the violas reach D♭5 while Vln II holds B♭4; at 61.3 they reach D♭5 again over Vln II's G♭4. Vln II is the lower octave of the tune here, and the unison scan shows the arpeggio passing through its pitch (B♭4 at 60.2.5, A♭4 at 61.2.5). Adler warns against background in the melody's register when the colours are alike (PDF 135). But the top of the tune is Vln I at B♭5–A♭5, an octave clear. The arpeggio is an eighth-note figure against the tune's held notes, so it differs in rhythm, which is Adler's main remedy (PDF 144, item 4). Adler also lists voice crossing as a resource of the quartet style (PDF 125, item 2). The figure is c277's "restatement grows by addition". A fix (dropping the last arpeggio note to B♭3/A♭3) would leave the swell to mf without its peak. Keep.

**67.3, D♭4 in four parts at once (PDF 152, 154).** On beats 3–4 of 67, Vln II, the violas' lower note, the cello's held tenor note and Hn 3 all sound D♭4, the seventh of the V7, under Vln I's D♭5. Tchaikovsky doubles the melody notes (PDF 152–154), and D♭5 is the melody note. The cello's D♭4 is the high point of its own tenor line (A♭3, E♭3, D♭4, C4 on beats 3–4 of 65–68), The violas, cellos and Hn 3 resolve D♭4 to C4 at 68, and the two violins follow the tune down through B♭. The chord is p diminuendo into the deceptive cadence, where the thickness does not tip the balance. Keep.

**A♭6 as the violin peak at 31, 72, 122 and 160 (PDF 129, 133).** Haydn's lesson is to save the top for the last build-up. The final climax at 160 does not reach higher than the exposition. It gets its greater weight from fff, largamente, and the tune in three string octaves with the violas hammering (PDF 137, 155). Bar 31 is quoted by the finale. c294 showed that lowering 72 puts the tune in unison with Vln II's counter. The climax order is protected (c244, c318, c384, c484). A higher peak, B♭6 or C7, would not fit the harmony at 160 and would be a fourth A♭-goal climax in a new key. Keep.

**15–18 and 177–182, Vln I alone in G4–E♭5 (PDF 133, 141).** This is Adler's "weak" register, and Shostakovich doubles the low violins with the cellos. c294 kept the thin start of P because the build to 27 depends on it, and because doubling would take the cellos off the bass. The coda is protected, and its last chord and five tied parts are quoted by movement II. I have nothing new against these reasons. Keep.

**47–54, the Vln II/viola pad shares chord tones with the cello tune (PDF 135).** The unison scan lists C4, F4, D♭4, B♭3 and E♭4 as held pad notes that the tune touches. They are chord tones held in a sustained pp pad under a tune that moves. The tune is in Vc + Hn 1, a different colour from the upper-string pad. This is Brahms's own layout (V-7: the background encloses the cello). The pad is protected (c447, c474). Keep.

**98–102, viola arpeggio inside the lower octave of the ff tune (PDF 135, 144).** The unison scan again shows the viola touching D4, G4, B4 and B♭4 with Vln II sul G and the cellos. c294's reasons still hold: the top octave is in Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1; the viola's eighths differ in rhythm; the brass counter fills every other register (protected c128, c186, c231). Keep.

**Where the strings could be more vivid.** I asked where a master would score the strings differently. The two places that come closest are the bare Vln I start of P (15) and the repeated A♭6 peak. Both are protected for reasons tied to the form and to the other movements, as above. Elsewhere the reading's devices are already used where they belong: Brahms's layering of the two S statements, the cello in its intense register at 52, the viola above the violins at 107–110, Grieg's 8' + 4' at 60–63, the Leonore octave build at 80–82, the Hindemith unison before the 122 chord, and Tchaikovsky's diminuendo by scoring at 123–127. I found no change that the reading supports and that would make the music better rather than only different.

No scratch build was needed, because no file changed. The v494 build as published: build-warnings.txt is empty, score.pdf has 18 pages, and analysis.txt lists only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1".
