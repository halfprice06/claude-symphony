## Findings

No score change. The timpani and percussion writing is the same as at v476 and still meets every rule in the assigned reading. The harmony check gives the same list of attacks and "not in chord" cases as c476, so the other parts' changes since v476 have not put any drum note against its harmony. As the task asks, I also looked within this lens for places where the drums fall short of a master's use. I tested three candidates and rejected all three, for the reasons below.

### Checks re-run on v566

- **Harmony check (PDF 58, 79).** 60 attack groups. The five not-in-chord cases are those in rule 2, and all are deliberate dominant or root strokes inside passages the finale quotes (27-32) or that are protected (138, 161; c476). No drum note is below the bass. The A♭2 roll at 140 lies over the D♭1 bass as the fifth of the Neapolitan chord. The G2 roll at 174 sits on the bass G2 of the cadential 6/4.
- **Bar 41 (PDF 55).** This was the only place where an ff drum meets a melodic brass entry. The roll is ff only on the downbeat and is marked > to mf at 42, while the four horns play ff with "molto espr." from 41.4. The drum is on E♭, the pitch the horns start on, so it reinforces their note. No change.
- **Climax page 16 (158-162).** G roll ff at 158, crescendo at 159, A♭ roll fff at 160 with the cymbal stroke, then a G roll with diminuendo ending on a tied crotchet at 161. Rests from 162, where the harp and low brass carry the decay. The notation is exact (rule 15) and the drum moves only between adjacent drums.
- **Parts and page turns.** I built the parts in the scratch copy. Timpani: cover page plus one page of music, so no turns. The mutas print at 34, 44, 84 and 98 (B♮ shown with its natural), the stick words at 1, 15 and 175, and the Vc. cues at 47-48 and 113-114. Percussion: one page, with "take ..." texts before 122, 159 and 175. Every part that crosses into movement II ends on an even page (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc 2 pp; Hn 3 2 pp).

### Considered and kept (no change)

1. **Hard sticks for the ff pulse at 27-32 and 131-134 (PDF 80, 88).** Hard sticks would articulate the dotted figure more sharply. They would also need two more stick changes per passage, because the stop strokes at 26 and 138 and the rolls at 40-43 and 140 want the fuller sound of medium sticks. The trumpets double the figure note for note (the timpani-trumpet pairing is protected, c460), and they already give it its edge. Brahms, Dvořák and Tchaikovsky leave stick choice to the player at such places. The gain is too small to be worth the extra texts. Medium sticks stay.
2. **Medium sticks instead of soft for the pp pulse at 179-180 (PDF 80).** At Lento crotchet = 50 the quaver G2 lasts 0.6 s, which is long enough for soft sticks to articulate it at pp. Adler ties soft sticks to soft playing, and this link has to sit under the pp chorale without making the drums a separate voice. Keep (the notes are protected by c410 and c458).
3. **Playing nearer the rim on the ppp rolls 1-4, 76, 181-182 (PDF 80).** Same verdict as c366 and c476. At 1-4 and 181-182 the drum's C3 gives fundamental to the lowest C. A rim stroke would thin it.

Within this lens the timpani writing does not fall short of the models. The drums take the classical roles Adler lists on PDF 79 (tonic and dominant pedals, tutti strokes, cadence strokes, soft atmosphere at 1-4 and 76). They have a solo identity of their own (the pulse, the B♮ stroke at 97, the 179-180 link), as in Ex. XII-52, and every retuning is marked and given time, as in Ex. XII-51. I found nothing a change would improve.
