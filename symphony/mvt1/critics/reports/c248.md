## Findings

### 1. Bars 97–102: the ff statement of the second theme has no phrasing dynamics (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 97–102 the second theme returns in B minor at ff in Fl 1, Ob 1, C.a., Vln I, Vln II (sul G) and Vc. It is the theme's only fortissimo statement before 158. Every other statement of the theme is shaped bar by bar: at 47–52 (Hn 1, Vc) and 147–152 (Hn 1, Vla, Vc) the long note swells and falls (`c'2.~\< c'8 bes8\>`), the minim on beat 2 has its own `--\<`, the eighth-note pickup to the minim peak carries `\<`, and the peak minim falls `\>`. At 97–102 the same line is printed flat: six bars of ff with tenuto dashes and nothing else. At ff the players will give each bar equal weight, the held D5/B5 at 98 will not grow and the 6/4 peak at 101 will not lead anywhere. The marks the cellos had in the soft exposition are missing exactly where the theme carries the most weight.

**Evidence.** Adler's climax example from Franck (adler2 PDF 38, Ex. XI-20, viewed as an image) shows the lyrical theme at its f climax with "molto sostenuto" and a per-bar `< >` in every part that carries the tune: flutes, oboes, cor anglais, clarinets, horns, violins, violas, cellos and basses all swell identically in each bar. The same example's soft statement before it has the same swells at p. A loud statement keeps its shaping, and every doubling part shows it. Adler's Berlioz example (PDF 188, Ex. XV-33) shows the same with a solo: the tune's hairpins sit on its long notes and belong to the line whatever the level. The brass counter at 98–102 was already lowered to f (c128) so that the tune can carry. Hairpins in the tune parts finish that work: the counter, the viola arpeggio and the bass stay level, and the tune moves against them.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/dev.ily).** The hairpins of 47–52 are copied onto the six tune parts at 98–102. Pitches, slurs, tenutos, the sul G span and the levels are unchanged. Vln I, for example:

```
r2. f'4(\ff | des''2.~\< des''8 c''8\>) |
bes'4(\! ges''2--\< f''4\!) | es''4.( des''8 c''4 es''8\< ges''8) | bes''2--(\> a''2\!) | f''4( des'''2--\< c'''8\! g''8) }
```

The same marks are added to devFluteOne, devOboeOne, devCorAnglais (the line in the C.a. block), devVlnTwo and devVc, with a c248 comment above devVlnOne. Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest at 97–102, so the shared staves print the marks once under "1." with no part-combine conflict (checked on PDF page 9). The last hairpin closes on the C at 102, as at 51 (`c''2--\< bes'4\!`), so the ff line ends level and hands over to the f woodwinds at 103 without a seam (Adler PDF 192).

**Earlier fixes.** This undoes nothing. It does not touch c231's protected items (brass counter, viola arpeggio, Vln II sul G, B.tbn F♯3), c128's brass f, or c186's sul G range.

### Checked and kept (no finding)

- **Climax 122 and 160 (reading 1, 3).** Every part restates fff at 122, the timpani roll is fff, and the trombones carry the motto beneath, as in Tchaikovsky 4/i (PDF 39–40). At 160 the held brass harmony is ff under the fff tune. That is c244's deliberate grading, and it differs from Tchaikovsky's all-fff page. I keep it: the tune at 160 is in the violins and woodwinds, while Tchaikovsky's tune is the low brass itself.
- **Sforzando and subito scoring (reading 6, 7).** Bar 41 is the textbook case of PDF 189–190 item 2: the brass have a short sf quaver over long sf > mf > p notes in winds and strings, and the horn call enters ff as the chord recedes. Bar 33 matches Ex. XV-36: the tutti ff stroke is short and the basses alone hold fp under it. Both stay.
- **sub. p at 62 and 84.** The drop happens in the same instruments, not through a second choir as Adler prefers (PDF 190). At 62 the second theme continues through the drop and the timpani's pp roll enters under it. At 84 a crotchet rest stands before the cellos' sub. p. Both are normal Brahms/Dvořák practice. Rescoring them as overlaps would add instruments to protected passages (56–68; 77–84), so I leave them.
- **Tempo words.** Every printed tempo word has its `\mt` step. "largamente" at 160 carries through 163–164 until the rit. at 165–166, which is consistent. "sostenuto" 123–126 and "a tempo" 127 match the collapse and the retransition.
