# Critic 038, lens 8 (expression), on v57

## What I studied
I read v57's sections, global.ily and the PDF (pages 2, 3, 9, 15 and 16 at 90–110 dpi), and checked HISTORY entries 29–37. In the corpus I pulled per-bar dynamics, words and articulation from the MusicXML files with a small script:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`), bars 49–60, 84–92 and 119–126.
- Dvořák 9/i (`Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`), bars 405–452.
- The `Fespr`/`MFespr`/`MPespr` dynamic-plus-word macros in Dvořák 7 (`mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../Common/Declarations.ly`, lines 34–116), which show that Dvořák always pairs "espr." with a dynamic.

On the web I read Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. I (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). Searches for the text of Gould's *Behind Bars* on restating dynamics and of Adler on accompaniment levels found no primary text, only forum summaries (e.g. https://www.youngcomposers.com/t13230/a-million-notation-questions/). I do not rely on those.

All findings below are applied in `/tmp/critic-038/work`. The build is clean: lilypond rc=0, 0 warnings, 17 pages, and the parallels check shows only the known "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1".

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 19–22: the new viola counterline has no dynamic or shape of its own (applied)
**Problem.** The violas carry "espr." but no dynamic, so the mf from bar 15 still applies. They also have no hairpins, while Vln I swells from f to ff at 20 and back to mf at 22. Under them, Vc and Cb are still f from bar 15, although Bsn 1 doubles the same bass at mf. The Vln II syncopations (c'–d', below the viola) are mf. As written, an inner line at mf sits between an f bass and an mf pulse, under an ff tune. It will not be heard.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i, bar 121: the horn counter-voice is "mf espress." at the strings' own level.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i, bars 51–54: the sustained horn counterline is "mp espressivo" with a cresc./dim. per two-bar phrase.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i, bar 86: viola "pp espress." with its own hairpins.
- Dvořák 7 never writes "espr." without a dynamic (`MFespr`, `Pespr` macros).

**Fix (tOne.ily).**
- Vla: `aes'2.~\mf\<(^\markup \italic "espr." aes'8 g'8 | f'2\f aes'2 | aes'4\> g'4 f'2~ | f'2\mf es'2) |`
- Vln II, bar 19: `r8 c'8~\mp …`
- Vc, bar 19: `f2\mf`. Cb, bar 19: `f,,4\mf`.
- Bars 23, Vc and Cb: remove the now-redundant mf and keep only `\<`.

The tune, the notes and cycle 18's "Vc/Cb mf at 23" level are unchanged; that level now simply starts four bars earlier.

### 2. Bars 163–166: the brass head is marked at the same level as everything that sustains around it (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and Vla play the head at ff. Woodwinds (Cl at es'–g'', in the trumpets' octave), violin tremolo chords and the cello tenor line are also ff. Because everything is ff, the ff accented chord at 166 is only a restatement.

**Evidence.**
- Dvořák 9/i, bars 416–423: the trumpets carry the theme with "cresc." while the trombones sustain at f, one level below.
- Rimsky-Korsakov, ch. I: woodwinds in forte are "twice as weak as the horns", and dynamics must be graded by group for the melody to be "detached by means of strongly accentuated dynamic shades".

**Fix (coda.ily).**
- Fl, Ob, Cl, B.cl, Bsn, Cbsn, Vln I/II tremolo and Vc go to `\f` at 163, with `\<` in 165 into the existing ff at 166. Paired staves are kept equal.
- Hn 3·4, Tpt 3, Tbn and B.tbn/Tuba (already f) also get `\<` in 165.
- The restated `\ff` at 166 is removed from the head parts (Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2, Vla), which are already ff.
- Cb eighths stay ff as the pulse.

No notes or scoring change, so the protected "brass head 163–166" stands. This refines cycle 29, which set no dynamic levels for the sustaining parts.

### 3. Bar 162: violins and basses do not take part in the crescendo into the coda (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 1·2, Vla, Cl 1, Vc and timpani crescendo on beats 3–4 (cycle 10's E♮→E♭ turn). Vln I and II hold `c''1\mf` / `c'1\mf` and then enter ff tremolo at 163. Cb holds `c,1\mp` and then plays ff eighths.

**Fix (recTwo.ily).** Vln I `c''2\mf~ c''2\<`, Vln II `c'2\mf~ c'2\<`, Cb `c,2\mp~ c,2\<`. The note values and cycle 36's breaths are kept.

### 4. Bar 33: the double basses start an mf whole note under the ff tutti chord (applied)
**Problem.** The tutti eighth chord is ff, and the contrabassoon rests. The Cb attack is therefore the chord's lowest string, but it is marked mf. It then continues as a pedal under the cellos' mf restatement of the theme.

**Fix (trans.ily).** `transCb`, bar 33: `c,1\fp`. The bass joins the ff chord, then sits below the cello theme. The crescendo at 37 already leads it back up.

At 135 the Vc/Cb downbeat at f under the winds' ff chord is acceptable, because the strings continue into the bridge at f. Leave it.

### 5. Bars 103–108: the violas' new eighths have no phrasing (applied)
**Problem.** The turn figure `c'8 c'8 b8 c'8` is unslurred and unarticulated. In the theme (15–16) the turn is `c''4) b'8( c''8)`, with the lower neighbour slurred to its resolution.

**Fix (dev.ily).** Slur each neighbour pair, for example `c'8\mf\cresc c'8 b8( c'8) c'8 c'8 b8( c'8)`, and do the same on A♭ (`aes8 aes8 g8( aes8)`). This keeps the motive recognisable inside the detached crescendo. Notes and dynamics are unchanged.

### 6. Bars 127–130: should the recapitulation echo the 19–22 viola counterline? (no change recommended)
No.
- The counterline belongs to the theme's second phrase (19–22), and 127–130 corresponds to the hemiola (23–26), which never had one.
- In the recapitulation the second phrase is 131–134. That is an ff tutti rising to a♭''', where a sustained mid-register viola line would be covered. The violas there already have cycle 17's turn figure, and 123–134 is protected (cycles 19 and 37).

If the composer still wants the echo heard, the only audible place is a quiet one. It would have to come after the ff, for example the cor anglais or cello lead-in at 136–138, marked `p espr.`. I would not add it: the dolce oboe sixth at 137–138 already fills that space.

### 7. Minor, not applied: oboe and violin levels at 19–22
Ob 1 doubles Vln I's peak at f (Vln ff) and its cadence at mp (Vln mf). Wind doublings one level under strings are common, so I left this alone. If the composer wants a single level, mark Ob 1 `\ff` at 20 and `\mf` at 22.
