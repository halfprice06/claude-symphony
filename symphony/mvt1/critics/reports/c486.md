# Critic 486: playability, instrument by instrument (v486)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, Appendix A (adler2 PDF 302-312), third pass. I read every text page in the range. The OCR of PDF 302-307 is unusable because those pages are range charts, so I rendered all eleven pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one. PDF 302 (strings, printed sideways) and PDF 304-305 (woodwinds, horn, trumpets) were also rendered at 200-220 dpi, rotated or cropped, and read again to confirm individual ledger-line pitches. PDF 308-312 are word lists: instrument names in four languages and common orchestral terms.

Method: I built v486 unchanged in /tmp/critic-486/work and measured each player's lowest two and highest three sounding pitches, with their bars, from `full.midi` (one track per player, script /tmp/critic-486/scripts/ranges.py). Then I checked each extreme against the section files for its dynamic and context. I converted Adler's written ranges to sounding pitch for transposing instruments (C4 = middle C).

## Findings

This third pass agrees with cycles 286 and 376: every note in v486 is inside Adler's professional ranges. The extreme notes per player are the same as in v376, so the cycles since then have not moved any part toward an edge. I report no fix. The points below record what I checked beyond the earlier passes, and why I decided against the changes I considered.

### 1. Range compliance: no change
I checked every player's two lowest and three highest pitches against the charts as images, not the OCR. The notes nearest a limit are Ob 2 B3 at 117, Bsn 2 B♭1 at 92-105, B.cl B♭1 at 105, Hn 1 written B5 at 106, harp C♯7 at 57-59 and the flutes' A♭6. Each sits at a dynamic that suits its register. The low oboe and bassoon notes are f or ff, not soft. The flute and violin A♭6 come only at the f-fff climaxes, and the harp's top notes have their damping plan (c246, c336). Adler's footnote on PDF 302 says the professional top notes are "those usually called for in orchestral playing". None of these notes exceeds them. All are protected by c146, c185, c256, c286, c306 and c376. Applied: no.

### 2. Amateur column: not applicable, recorded for the parts
Against Adler's nonprofessional column (PDF 302-306), the score goes past the amateur limits in four places: Vln I A♭6 (amateur top about D6), Hn 1 written B5 (amateur top written G5), the bass clarinet's low extension, and the cor anglais itself, which Adler calls "very seldom available" to amateurs. The commission is for a professional orchestra, so none of these is a defect. The one note an orchestra might struggle with, the horn's B5, already has a part ossia. Applied: no.

### 3. Timpani on the standard four drums: no change
Adler's chart (PDF 307) fixes the assignment: G2 on the 30", A♭2 on the 28", C3 on the 25", E♭3 on the 23". One way to give the E♭ more resonance would be to move it off the 23" drum, where it is a semitone above the bottom. That cannot be done: G2 and A♭2 need the 30" and the 28", and C3 cannot also sit on the 25" with E♭3. Only the A♭ drum is ever retuned (to B♭2 and B2, both within the 28" drum's F2-C3), so the player never pedals a drum that is still sounding. Every tuning change in the part has time to be made. Protected by c276, c366 and c476. Applied: no.

### 4. Is there a master-level gain inside this lens? I tested two ideas and rejected both
(a) The double basses never go below F1 and never use the low C extension. The low C1 at 167-170 is in the contrabassoon alone. Brahms and Tchaikovsky do call for the extension. But Adler (PDF 302) marks it "not found on every instrument", and the bars it would affect (167-182) are among the movement's protected, quoted bars (the coda and last chord, the 167-170 tremolo). Adding a bass C1 at 167-170 would also thicken a stop chord whose layout the finale answers. Rejected.
(b) The bass trombone never rises above F♯3, so it never plays in the strong middle register Adler's chart allows (up to about B♭4). That keeps it in its normal role as the bass of the trombone choir under the tenor trombones (G2-F4). Moving it higher would cross the tenors in the 175-179 chorale and in the 27-32 tutti, and the finale quotes 27-32 at pitch. Rejected.
Neither change would make a phrase sing better or a climax land harder enough to justify touching protected bars. Applied: no.

### Parts check (page turns)
v486's parts run (parts-run.txt) gives rc 0 and 0 warnings. The five parts that tie into movement II (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3) have 2 pages each, so each ends on an even page. The double bass part (4 pages) turns only in the rests at 171-174. The harp part (3 pages) turns in the rests at 145-146. No change in this cycle touches the parts.
