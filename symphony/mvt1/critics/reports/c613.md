# Critic 613: Form, pacing and proportion (v613)

Reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 3 "Rhythm and Meter" (aldwell-schachter PDF 47–57, printed pp. 36–46). Under the form lens this replaces a fifth pass of Unit 26 (c433). Cycles 268, 301, 368, 391, 468, 501, 568 and 591 read Unit 3 under the expression and melody lenses. No earlier form critic has used it, so this pass reads the unit as a theory of phrase rhythm and of where arrivals fall: hypermeter, phrase length, and the placement of the movement's arrivals and climaxes.

## Findings

No score change. The score follows every principle in the table. In this lens I found no change that would be a clear improvement and could be supported from the reading or the corpus. Findings 1–4 record what the reading shows about the form. Finding 5 answers the one open form item.

### 1. Arrivals and hypermeter: the climax order matches the strength of the hyperbeats. Keep.

A&S (PDF 53) say that the first and third bars of a four-bar group are strong. On PDF 54 they say a phrase's tonal goal comes at its end, as in Ex. 3-13, where the tonic falls in the eighth bar. Those are two different ways for an arrival to carry weight. I mapped every structural arrival in the movement to its place in its bar group:

| Arrival | Group | Position | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 (P), 27 (tutti), 47 (S), 64 (S peak), 69 (codetta), 77 (development), 97 (B minor), 103, 117 (G, a tempo), 131 (recapitulation), 147 (S in C), 158 (K, cadential 6/4), 167, 171, 179 | new group | bar 1 | hypermetric downbeat |
| 26, 138, 170 (stop chords) | 23–26, 135–138, 167–170 | bar 4 | phrase goal |
| 82 (ff, first A♭ climax) | 77–84; entries at 77, 79, 80, 81 shorten the units to one bar | bar 6 | phrase goal of a stretto |
| 122 (fff, second A♭ climax) | 117–122: trombone motto 117–121, then C rises a minor sixth to A♭ | bar 6 | phrase goal |
| 160 (fff, ♭VI, third A♭ climax) | 158–161 | bar 3 | secondary strong bar with a new harmony |

The first two A♭ climaxes are goals at the end of their phrases (PDF 54). The third, the biggest, is the only one that falls on a strong bar of a hypermeasure (PDF 53). It also changes harmony there, and it has the extra accents of length, register and chromatic colour that PDF 50 lists (A♭6, a ♭6 bass, largamente). So the order of the three climaxes (ff, fff, fff with ♭VI) runs parallel to the strength of their metrical positions. The timings agree. In the MIDI, 122 falls at 276 s of 430 (0.64) and 160 at 354 s (0.82). The arrival of the dominant pedal at 117 falls at 0.62. Keep.

### 2. Asymmetric phrases sit where the form needs them to. Keep.

PDF 54 allows asymmetric phrases (Ex. 3-14, Schubert's five bars). The score uses them only at joints:
- 47–55 is 8 + 1. The one-bar suffix at 55 delays the cadence, and the violins' pickup at 55.4 starts a regular 8 at 56–63.
- 64–68 is 5: a peak, a descent, a rit., and the deceptive cadence at 68.
- 44–46 and 144–146 are 3-bar lead-ins. PDF 53 cites Ex. 3-12 for three-bar groups.
- 162 is a one-bar suffix after 158–161.

Everything else falls in 4- and 8-bar groups. The irregular bars are the hinges, and the theme statements stay regular. That is the balance A&S describe when they call eight- and four-bar phrases "very common" and list the others as exceptions. The suffixes 55, 68 and 162 are protected (c033). Keep.

### 3. Three-bar fugato units, and the hemiolas that rejoin the metre on the stop chords. Keep.

The fugato entries at 85, 88, 91 and 94 make four three-bar hypermeasures, the case shown in Ex. 3-12 (PDF 53). Ex. 3-12 also shows why the unit works there. Each three-bar group is complete in itself and repeats as a unit, so the ear accepts a new length. The return to duple grouping at 97 then marks the B-minor S as an arrival. The hemiolas at 23–26 and 135–138 are the larger-scale form of Ex. 3-7 and 3-8 (PDF 52): four three-beat cells over 4/4. The metre comes back on the downbeat of the stop chord (26, 138), and the next tutti begins on a hypermetric downbeat (27, 139). A&S's description fits: a consistent shifted pattern that is heard as a temporary change of metre. Both are protected (3-bar fugato spacing c033; hemiola c007, c057). Keep.

### 4. Tempo and harmonic rhythm: the written-out acceleration at 127–130 follows PDF 49. Keep.

PDF 49 says the sense of tempo comes from the beat and the rate of chord change, not from the quickest notes. The retransition uses this. The tempo is a tempo (126) from 127 and the harmony stays V over the G pedal, but the cellos' pulse moves from crotchets (127) to quavers (128), triplets (129) and semiquavers (130). The surface speeds up while the tempo and the harmony stay put. That is the opposite case to A&S's Adagio full of 64th notes, and it is just as clear. The two Poco meno mosso statements of S (47–55, 147–155) slow the harmonic rhythm to one chord a bar, so their tempo sounds slower than the printed change from 126 to 112 alone would make it. Keep.

### 5. The open item 156–157, read as a hypermetric upbeat. Keep (protected; the reading supports the design).

Form critics since c443 call the two-bar climb from the C-major S (pp to 155) to the ff at 158 the movement's one weakness. Lengthening it is blocked: bars 160–182 are quoted by bar number in movements II and IV, c083 forbids raising the level or density of 147–157, and the section lengths are protected (c043, c243, c433). The no-bar-count alternatives have been tested and rejected: the timpani roll at 157 (c533), the iv6 bass at 157 (c553), and a crescendo from 155 (c593).

Unit 3 gives a new reason to keep it. A&S (PDF 52, Ex. 3-9b) say a rhythmic group may begin on an unaccented beat and belong to the downbeat that follows. On PDF 53 they extend metre to groups of bars. Read that way, 156–157 is not a short phrase. It is a two-bar upbeat to the hypermeasure 158–161, which Ex. 3-13 (PDF 54) would call bar 1 of the closing phrase. The one-bar suffix at 155 makes 156 a fresh start, so 158 falls as a hypermetric downbeat after a 1 + 2 group. Two features support this reading:
- The tune in 156–157 descends (D6, D♭6, C6, B♭5, A♭5, G5: the motto's D–D♭–C) under a crescendo. A falling line read as an upbeat is weak in register (PDF 50), so the accent of the ff downbeat at 158 is reserved for the downbeat itself.
- The exposition has the same two-bar unit at 62–63 (poco animando into the peak at 64). The recapitulation keeps that unit's length and removes the 6-bar build at 56–61 before it.

In the recapitulation the S group is shortened and goes straight to the arrival. That is the case A&S describe as a group that belongs to the following downbeat. Tchaikovsky 6/i gives a corpus parallel. In the MCTL annotation, the exposition's 2nd-A phrases last about 12 bars each (beats 350–398.5 and 514–563). The recapitulation's 2nd-A runs 12.5, 4, 4 and 9.4 bars (beats 1214–1334), so two short units come before its last phrase. I found no answer to the stated protections, and I do not reopen the item.

### Considered and not applied

- The D♭6 peak at 20 falls on bar 6, a weak bar, of the P sentence 15–22. It is long, high and chromatic (PDF 50), so it acts as a hypermetric syncopation, the large-scale form of the sf in Ex. 3-6 (PDF 51). This is expressive and intended. The notes of 15–22 are protected (c011, c041, c049). Keep.
- The first two A♭ climaxes (82, 122) fall on weak bars. Moving either to a downbeat would mean adding or removing a bar in the development, whose length is protected (c043, c243). It would also take away 160's position as the only climax on a strong hyperbeat (finding 1). Rejected.
