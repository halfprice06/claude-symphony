**Evidence.** A&S PDF 290–291 present the 6/3 as the chord of transition and the 5/3 as the goal. PDF 291 says the middle chords of a 6/3 passage lose their identity, so the passage moves toward its end. In this movement the share of first-inversion chords is highest in the last six bars before the dominant pedal. The goals (15, 27, 47, 69, 117, 131, 147, 167, 182) are root-position chords or pedals. The corpus has the same shape. By the MCTL annotations, Tchaikovsky 6/i's development runs 0% (section "C", onsets 638–677), 15% ("1st'"), 13% ("D"), then 68% ("1st''", onsets 910–966) before its retransition. Beethoven 9/i has 24% in the main part of its development and 50% at the D-major recapitulation over F♯ (onsets 628–676), where the 6/3 keeps the return unstable. Movement I's second theme at 47–68 is at 25%. This is higher than Tchaikovsky's 2nd-A (0%), but it comes from passing 6/5s inside the S tune's harmony (51, 53, 58, 60–61, 63), not from the cadences. **Fix:** none. The pacing of stability already follows the reading.

### 2. The development's seams are root-position shifts, not 6/3 links (keep)

**Bars.** 76→77, 84→85, 96→97, 102→103, 110→111. Each new block of the development starts on a root-position chord or a bare octave: A♭ minor at 77, the G octave at 84 (the cellos' subject begins alone at 85), B minor at 97, D minor at 103, F minor at 111. Between them, 77–84 and 97–102 contain no first-inversion chord at all.

**Evidence and judgement.** The reading would join stable points with a 6/3 bridge (PDF 290–291). A critic of form has to ask whether the development sounds like a set of blocks. I measured the block durations: 15.2, 22.9, 11.4, 15.2 and 11.4 s, then a 29.2 s pedal. That is not a stop-start succession of equal units. The longest block (the fugato) comes first, the two shortest come just before the pedal, and each block's own bass already moves (the fugato's G–C–F–B♭ chain by its own dominants, c142). Tchaikovsky 6/i also opens its development with a 0% block (above) and saves its 6/3 chain for the build. The seams carry material the other movements use: the semitone shift 96→97, the A♭–E–C bass at 77–83 (the key cycle in small), and the G octave at 84 heard as a root (PDF 298: a tone alone in a low register is heard as a tonic root, which suits the fugato's start). They are protected (c309, c323, c345, c360) for those reasons, and the reading gives no reason to override them. **Fix:** none.

### 3. The wedge 111–116 uses the reading's chromatic voice-leading chords correctly (keep)

**Bars.** Bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C. The harmony runs i (111), V6/5 of F (112), C minor 6/3 (113, i6 of C), a voice-leading chord over D (114), D♭ 5/3 (115), Fr6 over C (116), then V at 117.

**Evidence.** PDF 293 allows a 6/3 passage to contain one or more 5/3 chords ("irregularities"). The D♭ triad at 115 is that kind of variant, not a goal. The winds stay inside the crescendo, and the ff arrives at 116–117 (c143, c298). PDF 301 says a leading tone that falls is taken natural: E♮ at 112 is a chromatic passing tone in the bass, and it falls to E♭ at 113 without being doubled (only bass octaves, checked at 112.1 and 112.3). I considered making 115 a 6/3 (D♭–F–B♭, iv6 of F, the Phrygian IV6 of PDF 297) so that the middle of the series loses its identity completely. I reject it for three reasons. Vln I and Fl 1 sit on D♭6 over the D♭ bass. A&S PDF 484 (c180) lets the top line leave the ♭II upward through E♭, and the motto's E♭–D needs the D♭ chord under it. The harmony is protected (c319, c352). **Fix:** none.

### 4. The Phrygian descending fourth at 155–158 (keep the reason of c303)

**Bars.** 155 C (I, pp), 156 B♭ (♭VII), 157 F minor with B♭ added, 158 cadential 6/4 of C over G. The bass is C–B♭–F–G.

**Evidence.** PDF 297–298 (Ex. 18-12) is the textbook model for this approach: I – v6 (minor dominant over ♭7̂) – iv6 (over ♭6̂) – V, with a stepwise bass 1̂–♭7̂–♭6̂–5̂. Bar 156 already has the ♭7̂ in the bass, and 157 has iv. Only the A♭ bass at 157 is missing. Critic 303 rejected an A♭ at 157 because it would anticipate the fff ♭VI at 160. I tested that reason against the reading, and it holds. The reading's iv6 is a goal chord, the last before V, and its ♭6̂→5̂ is the cadential motion (PDF 297). With A♭→G at 157→158, the deceptive G→A♭ at 159→160 would reverse a motion the listener heard two bars earlier, and 160 would lose its surprise. The present leap F→G keeps ♭6̂ in the bass for 160 only. **Fix:** none. Do not reopen.

### 5. No 7–6 into the last pre-dominant 6/3 (keep)

**Bars.** N6 at 21 (beats 1–2), 141 (beats 1–2), 173 (beats 3–4); iv6 over F♭ at 61.

**Evidence.** Handel lengthens the last 6/3 before V7 and adds a 7–6 suspension into its 6th (PDF 294–295, Ex. 18-8a). PDF 297 says the suspension is "almost invariably" present with the Phrygian iv6. Here all four chords have the length, and none has the suspension. At 21 and 141 the N6 grows out of a root-position ♭II (20, 140) by voice exchange (Ex. 18-19e). That gives the same emphasis by other means, and a 7 (E♭ over F) would weaken the D♭6 top note the P sentence climbs to. At 173 the C.a. moves D5 (on 173.1) to D♭5, a 6–♭6 over the held F, which is the Romantic replacement for the 7–6 (Ex. 18-19j, 6 displacing 5). At 61 the only line that could hold E♭ into the bar is the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline, which moves E♭4→F♭4 (60.4→61.1). It is protected, and c273 declined the same change. **Fix:** none.

### 6. Closing tonic expansion and the coda's passing 6/3 (keep)

**Bars.** 69 (A♭: I–I6 | ii7–vii°7/V | V7 | I) and 176–178 (i6/4 – i6 – V4/3 – i under the chorale).

**Evidence.** Bar 69 is Ex. 18-19e, a 5/3 expanded by its 6/3, and it gives the codetta a moving bass under the held P head. At 176–178 the passing chord inverts Ex. 18-10 (I6–VII6–I instead of I–VII6–I6). The motto's bass E♭–D–C carries it, so the chorale moves contrapuntally, not harmonically (PDF 291), and its only root-position goal is 179. Both suit the reading's division between moving and arriving chords. Both are protected (69–72; coda notes). **Fix:** none.
