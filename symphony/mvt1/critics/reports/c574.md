## Findings

No change. The score follows every rule in the reading, and none of the places I tested would become better music with a change that the reading or the masters support. I rank below the places that come nearest to a master's standard question within this lens, with what I tested and why each stays.

### 1. The three A♭ climaxes as a series (keep)

82, 122 and 160 each add what the earlier one lacked, which is Adler's "holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event" (PDF 37) and his warning that constant brass "robs the climactic passages of their full power" (PDF 17). Check (c) puts numbers on it: the trumpets come fresh to each climax (9, 5 and 15 bars of rest). 82 is the first full brass with trumpets after the hammered tutti, held f under the ff head, strokes ff at 83. 122 is the Tchaikovsky device of PDF 38–41: the brass-only motto in long notes over the timpani roll, then fff with tam-tam. 160 is the Bartók layout of PDF 44–47 with the suspended cymbal. Rimsky-Korsakov's order for a long crescendo, "strings, wood-wind, brass" (rimsky-principles PDF 130), is how 155–160 is built: strings with C.a., Bsn 2 and Hn 1 at 155; harp, Cl 1, both bassoons and all four horns join at 156–157; the full woodwinds, trumpets, trombones, tuba and timpani enter at 158. A master would not reorder this. The one weakness other critics name near 160, the two-bar climb at 156–157, is a form question protected since c083 and c243, and the brass part of it (full brass entering at 158 after 15 bars' rest) is right by PDF 37.

### 2. Bar 122: the one loud chord that does not put the trumpets on top (keep; protected c244, c374, c484, c544)

Ex. XI-1 puts the trumpets at the top of every loud brass chord (PDF 15–16), and PDF 15 says a low trumpet does not count as two horns. At 122 the top brass note is A♭4 (Tpt 1 and Hn 1), Hn 2 F4 is above Tpt 2 D4, and Tpt 3 has B3. I checked whether the reading gives a new reason to raise Tpt 1 to A♭5. It does not: PDF 37 argues for keeping A♭5 back for 160, which is what the low layout does, and every pitch class of the V♭9 at 122 is doubled at the same or a higher octave by woodwinds and strings, so nothing is lost from the harmony. Keep.

### 3. Tpt 1's A♭5 at 72 before 160 (keep; decided c198)

By PDF 37 the trumpet's top note could be saved for 160. c198 closed this: at 72 it is an accented f crotchet inside the codetta tune; at 160 it is a fff note held for one and a half beats with the cymbal and largamente, so dynamic and length already separate the events, and removing it would take the brass out of the tune's peak. That reason still holds under the reading. Keep.

### 4. The Schubert pairing of soft trombone entries (tested in thought, rejected)

Adler's Schubert example (PDF 37, Ex. XI-19) uses the trombones' new soft colour twice, at the end of the exposition and again before the coda. This movement uses it once, at 175. The matching place would be 73–76 (the flute motto over the A♭ pedal, end of the exposition). I did not test it in the score: c103 protects "no trombone rhyme at 73–76" for "saved colours", which is PDF 37's own principle, and 73–76 is the chord movement II scores for winds at its 3–6 (coherence/mvt1.md; c557). A pp trombone chord at 73 would spend the colour that makes 175 a new sound. Keep.

### 5. The Tchaikovsky model at 123–134 (keep)

PDF 38–41: the climax breaks off, strings dwindle to cellos and basses, then a tutti with timpani roll and a brass cantus firmus. 123–126 collapse to the G pedal, 127–130 thin to cellos, basses, timpani roll, Cl 1, Hn 3·4 pp and then violas, and 131 is the tutti recapitulation with timpani and the P head in Tbn 1·2 with bassoons and cellos. The trombones are f, not ff, inside that unison (c144, Adler PDF 147: trombones marked below the others in a unison). The difference from Tchaikovsky is that his cantus firmus is new material and fff; here it is the returning theme, which needs the blend rather than trombone dominance. Keep.

### 6. Horn doubling at forte (keep)

Check (a) found one beat, 82.1, where a horn (Hn 1 A♭4) sits above all sounding trumpets (Tpt 3 E♭4), for a quaver before Tpt 1 enters with the head. This is not the "weaker" chord of Ex. XI-1 (PDF 16), which is a sustained mf layout. Every loud horn line is a2 or doubled by a trombone (PDF 15, 44; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41, "two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone"). Keep.

### 7. Special effects (not used; keep)

Mutes, stopped horn and the jazz effects of PDF 50–54 do not appear. They are listed as unused in coherence/mvt1.md and protected (c408). Brahms, Dvořák and Elgar rarely use them in a sonata allegro, and none of them would make an existing passage clearer. Keep.

### Where the movement still falls short of a master, within this lens

I looked for a phrase the brass could make sing more, a colour that could be more vivid, or a landing that could be harder. The brass writing does not have such a gap: the trumpets are fresh at every arrival, the trombones have three distinct roles (motto, low theme, chorale), the horns carry the movement's lyrical solos and the climax counter. The remaining distances from a master's first movement that critics keep naming (the 156–157 approach, the loud span 113–143, the lack of a soft distant episode in the development) are form questions, each protected with a stated reason, and a brass change would not answer them.
