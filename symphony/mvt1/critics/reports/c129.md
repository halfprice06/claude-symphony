## Holistic verdict

**Against Dvořák 8/i.** Dvořák's movement shows two habits that this score shares.

1. **The opening idea changes character by scoring alone.** Dvořák opens with a G-minor chorale in cellos, clarinets, bassoons and horn. In the recapitulation the same chorale comes back ff in the trumpets over the full orchestra. This score does the same with its motto: B.cl, Vc and Cb pp at 1–4; augmented ff in flutes, oboes and violins over the trombones' head at 131; a pp trombone chorale at 175–178. That is Rimsky's principle 15 carried out on the largest scale.
2. **Each group gets its own sound.** Dvořák gives the woodwind, brass and string groups their own material rather than doubling everything. This score does this at 117–121 (woodwind tune, brass counterline, viola triplets) and at 47–55 (horn and cello over harp and divided violas).

Dvořák differs on two points:
- **Recapitulation scoring.** Dvořák re-scores the transition music in the recapitulation so that it is lighter where the music is lighter.
- **Solo colours.** Dvořák rarely gives two structurally parallel solos to the same instrument.

Both points lead to the two findings below. Neither is a sign of an amateur. A conductor reading v148 would take it for professional late-Romantic writing. The places a conductor might query are small, and the two findings deal with them.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 135–137: trombones and tuba hold mp under the bridge. Exposition 23–25 has no low brass (applied)

**Problem.**
- At 131–134 the trombones play the head ff, with bass trombone and tuba ff.
- At 135 they carry straight on as sustained, accented half notes at mp. Tbn 1 doubles Hn 3 (C4–B3–C4–A♭3). Tbn 2 holds G3–F3. B.tbn and tuba double the bassoon, cello and bass hemiola.
- In the exposition (23–25) the same bars have no trumpets, trombones or tuba. Four horns, clarinets, bassoons and strings carry them, and the brass enters at 26.

So the recapitulation is scored heavier at the point where it should drop: four low brass parts cross the ff→mf change without a break. This is the problem cycle 128 left open as "sub. mf at 135". That cycle could not fix it without a markup dynamic and a MIDI rule.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 113: strings and brass are seldom set side by side. The exceptions are string tremolo under sustained brass, short sf string chords, and horns doubled by violas or cellos. Bars 135–137 are none of these: held trombones and tuba sit under the strings' quarter-note pulse and the violins' mf tune.
- PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords.
- Ex. 169 (PDF 339–340) and Ex. 158 (PDF 331): Rimsky reduces a big chord by marking horn IV and the trombones "mor." and letting them go. He does not keep them at a lower level.
- The recurrence examples (Ex. 178–181, 192–195) score the softer statement of an idea with fewer groups, not with the same groups marked down.

**Fix (applied, recOne.ily).** Tbn 1, Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba rest at 135–137 and re-enter at 138 f, as they do at 26:
```
recOneTbnOne  = { ... | R1*3 | aes2.->\f r4 | ... }
recOneTbnTwo  = { ... | R1*3 | f2.->\f r4 | ... }
recOneBassTbn = { ... | R1*3 | d2.->\f r4 | ... }
recOneTuba    = { ... | R1*3 | d,2.->\f r4 | ... }
```

**Why no chord tone is lost:**
- C4/B3/A♭3 stays in Hn 3.
- G3/F3 is still in the chord through Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II at G4/F4, and through Bsn 1 and Vc at F3 in 137.
- The hemiola bass stays in Bsn 1·2, Cbn, Vc and Cb.

The ff→mf drop now happens through the scoring at the barline, which answers cycle 128's open point without a new markup.

**Protection.** HISTORY protects "123–138"/"first group 123–135" (cycle 60/63; 131–146 and 131–143 in today's numbers). The stated reason for that protection is the D♭ key plan and the recapitulation's harmony. No note or harmony changes here, only doublings. Cycle 89's "held chords under the hemiola 135–138" remain in horns, clarinets and bassoons.

### 2. Bars 77–78: the head's solo opens the development on the same bassoon that opened the transition at 33 (applied)

**Problem.**
- At 33 Bsn 1 has "1. Solo, espr." with the head (G3–E♭4, D–D♭–C).
- At 77, after the codetta's pp A♭ pedal, Bsn 1 again has "1. Solo, espr." with the same head a third lower (E♭3–C♭4).

The two solos are structurally parallel: each starts a new section out of silence. Both use the same colour.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 117–118: an idea that recurs should be given new colouring; key and dynamics are only two of the means.
- Ex. 175a/b (PDF 345) sets the same recitative twice in different groups.
- Ex. 190–191 (PDF 357) gives a solo dolce first to the clarinet and then to the bass clarinet, over sustained horns. The bass clarinet's upper-middle register (here E♭3–C♭4, concert) is the register Rimsky uses for it there.
- The bass clarinet plays the motto at 1–4 and then nothing of its own until 107. Giving it 77 joins the start of the development to the opening of the movement. Dvořák 8/i does the same with its opening chorale's own colour (cellos and low winds).
- The bass clarinet is free at 77–90 (R1*14 in v148).

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).**
```
devBassCl = { r8 es8\mf^\markup \bold "Solo"_\markup \italic "espr."( ces'4.->) bes8( beses8 aes8 | aes2) r2 | R1*12 | f,1\f | ... }
devBsnOne = { R1*3 | \partCombineApart r8\f g,8 es4-> r2 | ... }
```
The bassoon keeps its entries from 80 onwards. The viola's E♭3 at 77 (cycle 127) still lies under the solo.

**Cost.** Cl 1's canonic answer at 77–78 is now the same family as the leader. The bassoon gave more contrast there. If the composer prefers that contrast, the alternative is to keep the bassoon at 77 and give the 33 solo to the bass clarinet. However, 33 follows a ff tutti stop and needs the bassoon's f attack, so I chose 77.

**Protection.** HISTORY protects the "5–6 shift at 77–78" (harmony), which is unchanged.

## Not raised

**Muted or stopped brass (principle 7).** Rimsky's examples in this range use con sord. brass as a main pp resource (Ex. 132, 138, 150, 167, 174). The places in this movement where it would fit are all protected for stated reasons: 124–126, 145, 162–166 and the trombone chorale at 175–179. Brahms and Dvořák also rarely mute in a first movement, so I did not raise it. It is noted for movements II and III.
