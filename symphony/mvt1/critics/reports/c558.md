## Findings

### 1. No change to the score

Every principle in the table is followed, and I found no expressive change that clears the bar of a real improvement. Below are the checks I made and the candidates I tested and rejected, so the next pass does not repeat them.

**Scan 1: entries without a dynamic.** I wrote a script that walks every instrument variable in sections/*.ily, finds each entry after two or more bars' rest, and checks that the entry bar carries a dynamic or a dynamic-plus-word mark. Result: no entry without a dynamic. This is the reading's PDF 152 point in its plainest form (the player needs the starting level written).

**Scan 2: crescendos without a destination.** The same script lists every `\<` whose next dynamic event is another hairpin or lies more than two bars away. Every hit is one of three kinds: (a) the paired `\< \>` swells of the S statements (principle 13, intended); (b) crescendos at the end of the development into the ff/f marks at 131 in the next section file (they do arrive); (c) the C.a., Fl 1, Ob 1 and violin swell `des2--\< c8\! g8` at 102, which ends the B-minor S statement on its half cadence and is cut off by the f/mf texture at 103 (the 102–103 seam is protected, c463/c470). Nothing to fix.

**Candidate A: "espr." on the S head in Hn 1, Bsn 1 and cellos at 103–106 (rejected).** Every other soft or medium S statement carries a word (dolce 46/147, molto cantabile 56, espr. 107 and 171), and Casanelles PDF 155–156 and 160 say that "lyrical" legato is a separate playing style that has to be asked for. But at 103–106 the P head in Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 at f is the primary line and the S head at mf is secondary. The S head already has the lyrical sign the reading names: a swell on each arrival (`<>` in Hn 1 and Vc at 103–106, page 10). A word would invite the three players to push up to the P head's level and blur the f-over-mf layering that principle 17 (PDF 290) describes. Keep.

**Candidate B: let the viola drop below the new fugato entry at 91 (rejected).** At 91 the Vln II + C.a. subject enters f and the violas, whose entry was at 88, are also f. In a textbook fugato the earlier voice would yield. But here the entry is the top voice, doubled by the cor anglais, and the cellos and basses crescendo into 91 (90, mf<f), so 91 is the first step of the crescendo that the fugato is built on (85 mf, 91 f, 94 f, 96 ff). That crescendo is protected (c453; checked again by c497), and a sub. mf in the violas would break the build. Keep.

**Candidate C: a tempo word (tranquillo or poco sostenuto) for the flute motto 73–76 (rejected).** It would make the moment more inward, but the motto is already in half and whole notes at p espr. → pp over a held A♭ pedal (a written-out broadening). A new word needs an `\mt` step and changes the duration. c458 names the tempo plan as master-level, and c409 protects it. The reading has nothing on tempo. Keep.

**Candidate D: hairpins on the viola quavers under the B-minor S statement 97–102 (rejected).** The violas play six bars of f quavers with no hairpin, while the tune swells in every bar. That is principle 13 itself: a sustained accompaniment wants transparent legato and the swell belongs to the line (PDF 155). The quavers change pitch on every beat, so principle 7 (the machine-gun effect) does not apply. Keep.

**Earlier rejections rechecked.** I checked these again, and the reasons for keeping them still hold. The timpani pulse 179–180 stays pp in both bars: it is quoted and protected (c458), and two pulses are not a run. The Vln I E♭5 at 178 stays without a swell, because it is in canon with the pp trombones (c258, c358, c458). The ff B-minor S at 97 stays without a word: ff, sul G and the swells already say what the word would say (c458). The eight dynamic levels stay: they are used one or two steps apart in neighbouring parts, and fff only at 122 and 160 (c098).

**Where the expression could still fall short of a master's.** I asked this at the three moments a listener most needs to remember: the horn call 13–14, the S theme 47–68, and the coda 171–182. In each the marks already do what Casanelles says only a player can supply and only a composer can request (PDF 149–152, 155). The call is a single horn, mp espr., fading to p. The S theme has dolce and molto cantabile, a swell on each appoggiatura, sub. p with poco animando at 62 and the peak at 64. The coda has espr. on each soft line, morendo, and the violas' ppp E♮. I found no mark whose change would make these more vivid without undoing a protected decision. The remaining distance to a master lies elsewhere: in the 156–157 climb and the proportions, which other lenses have noted and which are protected.
