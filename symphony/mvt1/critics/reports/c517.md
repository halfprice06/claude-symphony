## Findings

I found no change that meets the bar. I tested one candidate in a scratch copy and do not recommend it. Findings 2–4 record why three places that the reading and the corpus point to should stay as they are. The scratch copy at /tmp/critic-517/work is identical to v517 and builds with 0 warnings, 18 PDF pages and only the known "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1" in analysis.txt. The rejected test is kept separately in /tmp/critic-517/work-test129.

### 1. 127–130: no brass voice in the retransition stretto. Tested; keep as it is.

**What the reading suggests.** Adler calls Bartók's stretto (mm. 363–386) "especially thrilling" (PDF 44). The images on PDF 47 show it carried by the brass, with entries a bar apart, each one marked. At 127–130 the half-augmented motto enters in Cl 1 (127, p → mf at 129), the violas (129.3, mf) and Vln II + Bsn 1 (130, f). The only brass are Hn 3·4 on the G pedal and Hn 2's F4 seventh on 130.3. Hn 1 rests from 124 to 130.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-517/work-test129/sections/dev.ily, Hn 1 doubles the viola entry:
`\partCombineAutomatic R1*5 | r2 g4\mf\< es'4~ | es'4 d'4 des'4 c'4\! |` (was `R1*7`).
It builds with 0 warnings and 18 pages, analysis.txt is unchanged, and the vl.txt diff against v517 is empty (no new parallels).

**Why it should not be applied.**
- Adler's balance rule (PDF 44) says two horns equal one trombone. By that rule, one horn at mf in G3–E♭4 weighs more than Cl 1's chalumeau at mf in the same octave. Cl 1 states the same pitches only two beats earlier (G3 on 129.1, E♭4 on 129.2), so the horn would cover the entry that begins the stretto. Critic 498 already found this clarinet line in a weak register (adler1 PDF 185, 210).
- Bartók's stretto is a brass stretto because his fugato is a brass fugato (PDF 44–47). Ours is a woodwind-and-string stretto over a brass pedal. Its brass moment is already 117–121, and a second brass motto six bars later repeats that gesture at lower strength. Critic 479 made the same point against a louder 127–130.
- Hn 1's f entry at 131 is fresh after its rest from 124. The test spends it.
- On the shared Hn 1·2 stave, the part-combiner needs "1." and "2." labels and two dynamics inside one bar at 130. The page gets busier for a doubling the listener would not hear as new.
- The stretto is protected (c117, c437, c467, c479) for reasons that still hold.

### 2. 85–96: the brass take no part in the fugato. Keep as it is.

**What the corpus and the reading suggest.** In the string fugato of Tchaikovsky 6/i (corpus MusicXML, measures 171–189 as numbered in that file), trumpets and trombones punctuate the ends of the fugato statements with single short notes (measures 174, 178, 182). Then the trumpets lay a broad descending line in octaves over the string figuration (measures 190–197). This is Hindemith's punctuation (Adler PDF 42, 44) and the cantus firmus of Ex. XI-21 (PDF 38, 40–41). In v517 no brass player sounds in bars 84–95. The first brass notes after the strokes at 83 are the bass trombone's B♭2 on 96.1 (with the low winds and Cb) and the brass answer on 96.2.5.

**Why it should stay.** The reading itself gives the reason. In Ex. XI-24 Bartók keeps the horns silent through the whole fugato and brings them in only for the inversion, a2 and "f, ben marc." (PDF 44; 46, m. 342). Their entry is heard as new because they were held back. In v517 the brass answer at 96 (Hn 1·2 a2, Tbn 1, accent on D♭4) works the same way. It is the stretto's last voice, the first brass line since the strokes at 83, and it leads straight into the brass counter at 97–102. Brass punctuation at 88, 91 or 94 would take that away. A brass cantus firmus over the fugato would anticipate the motto cantus firmus at 117–121, which is this movement's version of Ex. XI-21. In Tchaikovsky's own example, too, the cantus enters at the tutti, not inside the fugato (PDF 38). The fugato's entries and dynamics are also protected (c142, c227, c427, c437, c497).

### 3. 97–102: the four-player counter under the B-minor S. Keep as it is.

The counter (B3–D4 region, half note plus a three-quaver pickup in each half bar) is played in unison by Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Bsn 1 at f. The tune is ff in Fl, Ob, C.a., Vln I·II and Vc, two octaves and more above it. By Adler's rule of two horns equalling one trombone (PDF 44), the counter has about the weight of two trombones plus a bassoon. That is enough to be heard as a line under a ff string-and-wind tune without covering it, and the register is the best middle register of both horn and trombone (PDF 42). Like Bartók's trombones after their entry (PDF 45–46), the voice keeps moving in its own rhythm instead of holding. Hn 1 drops out at 102.3 so that Hn 2 alone takes the pickup into 103 ("2." in the score), which thins the line before the mf at 103. Protected since the early cycles ("Hn/Bsn/Tbn counter at 97–102"; "brass fills 97–102"). No change.

### 4. 117–121 and 131–134: the reading's two main brass textures are already here. Keep (record).

- **117–121 is Ex. XI-21's layout.** On PDF 40–41 Tchaikovsky's trombones and tuba hold long notes in octaves. Above them the woodwinds and horns play the theme in short figures, and below and around them the strings run in semiquavers, all over a timpani roll. At 117–121 the motto moves in semibreves and minims in Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn. The P head is imitated at half-bar distance in Fl/Ob/Vln I·II, the clarinets and violas run in triplets, the tuba and Cb hold the G pedal, and the timpani roll. In both, the long-note brass line is the newest and strongest element of the climax (Adler PDF 38: "a powerful new thematic element (cantus firmus) against other previously heard contrapuntal fragments").
- **131–134 is Hindemith's punctuation.** Trumpets play the timpani rhythm `c4 r8 c8 c4 r8 c8` while the low P head is in Tbn 1·2 a2, Bsn, Cl and Vc (PDF 42, 44, mm. 189–196). The theme is in the trombones' best register, G3–E♭4 (PDF 42).

Both are protected (117–121 by c186, c317, c491; 131–134 layout by c361, c427). No change.
