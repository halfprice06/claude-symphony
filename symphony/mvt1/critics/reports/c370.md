### 2. The handovers at 9 and 182 follow the transfer rule, and movement II repeats them at its own end; keep

Rimsky-Korsakov's rule for passing a phrase on is that "the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one" (PDF 125–126; his example is Snegourotchka 191, solo violin to solo cello). Critic 270 checked the coda. A second place in the introduction follows the same rule. The cor anglais holds the motto's last G4 from 7.4 to 9.3, and Vln I enters on that G4 at 9.4 to begin the rising-sixth chain. The introduction's line passes from wind to violins at one pitch, and the violins then climb from there (protected 9–13).

The same device frames the attacca. Vln I's last motto ends on C5 at 181–182, and movement II holds that C5 through its bars 1–3. Movement II ends by handing Cl 1's C5 to Vln I's C5 at its 89 (mvt2.md, Ending). So movement I's transfers are the model for movement II's last bars. The device runs across the join, not only inside movement I.

Fix: none. Keep the cor anglais G4 tied to 9.3 and Vln I's entry on G4 at 9.4.

### 3. The law of register in the development dialogue 77–81 supports the A♭–E–C seed; keep

PDF 128: "When a phrase is imitated in the upper register it should be given to an instrument of higher range and vice versa." At 77–81 every answer of the head goes higher and to a higher instrument:

- B.cl at 77.1.5 (E♭2–B2) is answered by Cl 1 at 77.3.5 (E♭5–B5).
- Hn 1·2 and Vc at 78.3.5 (B3–G4, G4–F♯4–F4–E4) are answered by Ob 1 and Vln I an octave higher at 79.1.5 (B4–G5–F♯5–F5–E5). This is the scherzo's theme A bar 2 at its own pitch.
- Bsn 1, Hn 3·4 and the trombones at 80.1.5 (G2/G3–E♭3/E♭4) are answered by Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I at 80.3.5 (G4–E♭5), then by Fl 1 and Vln I at 81.3.5 (G5–E♭6).

The bass under this dialogue is A♭–E–C (77–80), the symphony's key cycle in small (mvt1.md seeds). Because the answers climb by register and by instrument, the scherzo's figure at 79 arrives in the oboe and first violins, where the scherzo itself puts theme A (Vln I, Ob 1). The scoring at 79 therefore matches the later movement's colour as well as its pitch (PDF 20, row 4).

Fix: none.

### 4. Movement I keeps back the colours the reading names for echo and for the brass, and the finale uses them; keep

The echo paragraph names "an echo given to muted brass following the same phrase not muted" and "muted trumpets are eminently suited to echo a theme in the oboes" (PDF 128). Ex. 252 (PDF 417, Sadko) shows the oboe solo answered by an off-stage trumpet. The preface counts Beethoven's trumpets "standing out above the rest of the orchestra" as a fault for the student (PDF 22). Movement I uses none of these colours:

- it has no muted or stopped brass;
- it has no trumpet melody;
- its trumpets end on the unresolved B4 at 170.

Movement II has trumpets tacet. The scherzo uses them only in tutti rhythm. The finale's second theme is then a soft trumpet solo (IV 51), and its recapitulation pairs muted trumpet with the cor anglais (IV 171), close to the oboe-and-trumpet pairing of Ex. 252. Tpt 1 resolves movement I's B4 to C5 only at IV 222→223. Movement I's restraint is what makes the finale's trumpet colours new. Adding a trumpet line or a muted-brass echo anywhere in movement I would spend them early.

Fix: none. Do not add muted brass or a trumpet melody to movement I.

### 5. The counter at 167–170 is buried, as in the author's own fault, but it must stay; keep

PDF 18 records Rimsky-Korsakov's own fault in The Golden Cockerel 233: a viola/cello counter-melody doubled by woodwind "will hardly be heard" if the brass keep their marks. At 167–170, Vc and Bsn 1 play C4–E♭4 | E♭4–F4 | E♭4–C4 | D4 under the ff brass head. At the same time Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 hold E♭4 and Hn 3 holds C4. The counter is therefore at the pitches of three held brass notes and will not be heard as a line. The reading would call this a fault.

Three things outweigh it here:

- The layout of 167–170 copies 27–32 on purpose (c083, c254, c279, c299; the HISTORY.md protection reads "alike on purpose").
- The finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part at its 199–204, including this tenor line (finale.md item 1: "Vc + Bsn 1 tenor line"). A change at 27–32 would break that quotation. A change at 167–170 alone would break the rhyme.
- At 167–170 the tenor line works as harmonic motion inside the hammered block, not as a melody.

The finding concerns a protected passage. I record it so a later critic does not re-raise it without this context.

Fix: none.
