## Findings

### 1. 167–169: Tpt 3 in the first group's pulse rhythm under the brass head. Tested; do not apply.

**What the reading suggests.** Hindemith's brass punctuate a theme that another group carries, "a most common use of the brasses" (adler2 PDF 42; image PDF 44, mm. 189–196). The movement already does this at 27–30 and 131–134, where Tpt 3 plays `es'4 r8 es'8 es'4 r8 es'8` under the wind and string tune. At 167–169 Tpt 3 holds the same pitches (E♭4, E♭4, F4) as plain semibreves. Giving it the pulse would make 167–169 match 27–30 in rhythm as well as in layout, and the pp timpani pulse at 179–180 would then echo a forte pulse heard twelve bars earlier.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-617/test/sections/coda.ily, codaTptThree:
`es'4\f r8 es'8 es'4 r8 es'8 | es'4 r8 es'8 es'4 r8 es'8 | f'4\< r8 f'8 f'4 r8 f'8 | f'2.->\ff r4\fermata |` (was `es'1\f | es'1 | f'1\< | f'2.->\ff r4\fermata |`).
It builds with 0 warnings and 18 pages. analysis.txt is unchanged and the vl.txt diff against v617 is empty.

**Why not.**
- At 27–30 the tune is in the winds and violins, two octaves above Tpt 3. At 167–169 the tune is in the brass: Tpt 1·2 a2 on G4–E♭5 and Hn 1·2 a2 an octave lower on G3–E♭4, with the violas. Tpt 3's E♭4 is the horns' E♭4 at 167.2, and its F4 at 169 is the horns' F4 at 169.2. The pulse would re-strike the horns' melody notes in a brighter colour. By Adler's balance rule (PDF 44: two horns equal one trumpet), one trumpet at f equals the whole horn octave of the tune, so its re-attacks would cover the lower octave of the head, not punctuate it.
- In Hindemith's passage the punctuation comes after the brass fugato, when the timpani take the theme (PDF 42, "ends with the brass punctuating the theme as presented by the timpani"). The punctuating brass are not playing the theme at the same time. At 167 the brass are the theme.
- The bars already have a quaver pulse (Cb on C) and a timpani roll rising to the fff stop chord at 170. A second repeated-note layer adds rhythm without adding a line.
- 167–170 is protected (c267, c299, c417). My change agreed with c299's stated reason ("layout as 27–32"), but the masking above is a reason of its own to leave it.

### 2. 113–116: Tpt 3 doubles Hn 3 at the unison (open since c157). Keep.

Tpt 3 enters pp at 113 on E♭4–D4–D♭4–D4, the same notes as Hn 3, while Tpt 1 has its own line (C5–B4–C5–B4–F4–A♭4–C5–D♭5–C5–D♭5–E♭5–D5) and Tpt 2 has G4–F4–F4–F♯4. Critic 157 suggested a rest. The reading supports keeping it. The trumpets enter one level below the horns and get louder faster than the strings (c143, c186). The unison gives the chromatic inner line E♭–D–D♭ trumpet colour as it rises to ff at 116, and that line becomes the motto's E♭–D–D♭–C in the brass at 118–120. Tpt 3 then rests from 117 to 121 and comes in fresh for the fff B3 at 122. Removing 113–116 would leave Tpt 1 and 2 as the only new colour in the wedge and thin the line that leads into the motto. This closes the c157 open item: keep.

### 3. 77–81: the brass in the head dialogue. Keep.

Adler says contrapuntal brass writing means imitation and free counterpoint rather than antiphonal exchange (PDF 41). At 80–81 Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 (with Bsn) answer the rising head with two-note fragments (Hn 3 `r8 g8 es'4->`, G3–E♭4) between the woodwind and violin statements. Taken alone that is an antiphonal gesture. But it sits at the end of an imitative chain that starts at 77 (B.cl solo 77, Cl 1 78, Ob 1/Vln I and Hn 1·2/Vc at 79), and the fragments shrink the head to its rising sixth, one step in the motivic compression that ends in the ff A♭ tutti at 82, which is followed by the short tutti strokes at 83 that frame the fugato, as Bartók's chord blocks at 342–349 do (PDF 46). A full brass statement at 80–81 would anticipate the tutti at 82 and the brass answer at 96. The layout of 77–83 is protected (c133, c167, c533). No change.

### Where the lens still finds the music short of a master's

I looked for a passage where brass counterpoint would make a moment more memorable. 117–121 already follows Ex. XI-21 (motto as cantus firmus under the P-head imitation). The fugato holds the brass back as Bartók does. 97–102, 103–106 and 139–142 give brass voices their own counterlines. The two places with only held brass under a moving tune, 158–161 and 167–169, are where the movement wants mass: the third A♭ climax and the last forte statement before the soft coda. I did not find a contrapuntal addition that the reading supports and that would not mask the tune or take away a fresh entry.
