## Findings

### 1. Two uncued string entries after long rests (Vln II 91, Vln I 155) — applied in scratch

**What is wrong.** MOLA PDF 5 says "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest" and "Cues must be audible and obvious to the musician reading the part". Example 4 (PDF 10) shows a cue right before the entry after a multi-bar rest. The parts follow this for every wind and brass entry after a long Allegro rest, and Vln I already has a cue (Vln II, 92–93) for its 84–91 rest. Two string entries are left without one:

- **Vln II, 91.** It rests 84–90 (seven bars at crotchet = 126, about 13 s) and then makes the third fugato entry, f, on C. An entry that is late or early here breaks the fugato. The bass clarinet, which enters with it, got a viola cue at 89–90 in c385 for the same reason. The Vln II part has none.
- **Vln I, 155.** It rests 147–154 at Poco meno mosso (crotchet = 112), plus two beats, about 18 s, which is longer in time than the 84–91 rest that is cued. It then enters on the mf pickup F5 at 155.3 that leads the violins into K. The key changes at 147, so the player also has a new signature to read.

**Fix (parts-gen.py only; the score does not change):**
- Vln I cue list: `[VC+("DOWN",53,2), V2+("DOWN",92,2), VA+("DOWN",153,2), CL+("DOWN",175,2)]`. The cue is the violas' S phrase at 153–154: F5 E5 D5 C5 A4 | C5 B4 D5 F4, in the treble staff with no ledger lines.
- Vln II cue list: `[VA+("DOWN",89,2)]`. The cue is the violas' fugato subject at 89–90: C4 B3 C4 A♭3 G3 | C4 D4 E♭4 E4, the same quote the bass clarinet uses.

**Test.** Parts build with lilypond rc=0 and 0 warnings. Vln I and Vln II stay at 2 pages. Vln I page 1 and Vln II page 2 render identically to v495. The turns stay at 84–91 (Vln I) and 103–106 (Vln II). Vla, Vc and Hn 3 are still 2 pages, so every part that crosses into movement II still ends on an even page. In the Vln II part the rest now reads "5 (84–88)" followed by the two cue bars. I viewed both cues at 100 dpi and found no collisions.

I did not cue Vln I at 111 (rest 103–110). That entry is the mp downbeat of the whole string section at the start of the wedge, after a steady eight bars that begin with the f entry of the theme combination at 103. The only material at 109–110 is the soft divided Vln II pad, which would not make an "obvious" cue (MOLA PDF 5). c465 also left it uncued.

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **Double-bass part, 4 pages, page 4 holds 175–182 only.** MOLA PDF 7 asks for an even amount of music per system and page. The c201 plan places the cover and the turn at 171–174 so that the part ends on an even page for the attacca. Without the cover, the only turn would be the two-bar rest at 88–89 (about 4 s). That reason still holds, so I kept it.
- **Harp part ends on page 3.** The harp does not cross into movement II (it has six bars' rest there), so an odd last page does no harm.
- **Hn 4 "a2 with 3." at 158.** This is a part-only tag. It tells the player which part he is doubling, and Hn 3 is his neighbour. I kept it.
- **Vln II 55 text stack** ("tutti, div." / "unis." / "molto cantabile" / "sul D"). The four texts read top to bottom in time order. c329 and c395 set this deliberately, and it is crowded but legible at 7.2 mm.
- **The audit's "entry without a dynamic" lines** (e.g. Fl 1 62, Vln I 53, Tpt 1 67) are the cue bars, not real entries. Every real entry after a long rest I checked has its own dynamic.

### Beyond compliance

My lens covers the parts. Measured against a master's published set (Breitkopf or Simrock material for Brahms or Dvořák), these parts are now cleaner than most: they have cues in the reader's key, bar ranges under rests, tempo words carried through rests, and turns placed in rests. The two cues above were the only gap I could find where a player is more likely to miscount than the conductor is to cover it. I found no change in the engraving of the score that would make a phrase sing more or a climax land harder. Earlier passes of this lens already settled the score's dynamic, hairpin and slur notation at the climaxes (114/122, 158–162) and the coda, and I found nothing new to change there.
