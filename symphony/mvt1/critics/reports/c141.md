Cycle 126 marked row 4/6 (the one-string colours) "not applicable (no one-string effect is intended)". That was a playability reading. Read for melody, Adler's own examples of these colours are theme statements, and two of them are restatements of a theme first heard in another voice (Ex. III-5, III-7). ## Findings

### 1. Bars 57–59: the violins' p restatement of the second theme crosses to the E string. Mark *sul A*. (Applied)

**Problem.** At 47 the horn and cellos state the second theme. At 56 both violin sections restate it in unison, p *molto cantabile*. That statement is the most memorable melody of the exposition. In normal first or third position the expressive peak F5 (57, tenuto), the A♭5 (59) and the G5 (59) fall on the E string, while the rest of the phrase lies on the A string. The quiet statement therefore changes colour in the middle of the head. The violins only need the E string's brilliance from 60, where the line turns mf and climbs to the D♭6 peak at 63.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75: "In soft, lyrical, expressive passages, the composer will often prefer the higher pitches to be performed on the A-string rather than have the player cross to the E-string, where these upper notes take on a different, more brilliant timbre." His example, Ex. III-7 (Brahms 3/iii, bars 13–24), is the same situation. The violins restate, *sul A*, p espress., a theme the cellos have just stated on their upper strings. Adler ch. 5, PDF 133, uses that same Brahms theme as the model for a cello statement, and this score's 47–55 follows that model. PDF 76 says to mark *sul A* when a passage should stay on one string. Ex. III-5 and III-7 show the mark with a dashed extension line.

**Fix.** The mark starts at 57 beat 2, because the A♭4 on 57 beat 1 lies below the open A and cannot be played on that string. It ends at the end of 59. The notes, slurs and dynamics are unchanged, and the mf at 60 brings the E string back. Same edit in `tTwoVlnOne` and `tTwoVlnTwo`:

```
| c''2.~ c''8 bes'8 | aes'4 \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul A" f''2--^\startTextSpan es''4) |
des''4.\<( c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''2 g''2)\stopTextSpan |
```

Highest note on the A string is A♭5, about sixth position, which is normal for a section. This touches no protected item: the notes, rhythm and scoring of the violins at 56–63 are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 98–102: Vln II carries the ff second-theme head an octave under Vln I in the G-string register. Mark *sul G*. (Applied)

**Problem.** In the B-minor block, Vln II plays the head an octave below Vln I and with the cor anglais, at ff. The line spans B3–D5. As written it will be fingered across the G, D and A strings, and the held D4 at 98 (seven beats, ff) may be taken on the open D string. The movement never uses the violins' G-string sound for a tune. This block is the development's one ff statement of the lyrical theme in a dark key. It is where Tchaikovsky and Elgar would give the lower violin octave the G-string colour.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 74: the G string is "the thickest and most sonorous of the four strings. As one goes into its higher positions, the sound becomes very intense." Ex. III-5 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, bars 111–119) shows a lyrical theme marked *sul G* with sf and mf swells. Adler PDF 134 (ch. 5, Ex. V-9): "If the second violin were to play the theme on the G-string, the result would be intense, but of a much darker hue than the upper strings of the cello." On open strings at sustained notes, see PDF 73–74. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/iv, bars 147–149, Vln I and II *sul G*, f, at the return of the theme (`Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt4.ly` line 3465); Tchaikovsky 6/iii, bars 96–102, violins *sul G*, ff (`Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt3.ly` line 11499).

**Fix.** In `devVlnTwo`, inside the existing `\transpose c cis`:

```
\transpose c cis { r1 | \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul G" des'2.~(\ff^\startTextSpan des'8 c'8) |
  bes4( ges'2-- f'4) | es'4.( des'8 c'4 es'8 ges'8) | bes'2( a'2) | f'4( des''2-- c''8 g'8)\stopTextSpan } |
```

The top note is D5 at 102, about eighth position on the G string. That is within normal section use; Tchaikovsky's sul-G passages above go as high. Notes, dynamics and scoring of the protected block 97–102 are unchanged. Only the string is specified.

### 3. Bars 64–67: Vln II's lower octave of the tune's f close. Optional *sul G*. (Not applied)

**Problem.** From 60, Vln II plays the tune an octave under Vln I. At 64–67 (f, A♭4 down to B♭3) Vln I is on the A and E strings. Vln II's line lies entirely in the low positions of the G string. A *sul G* there would give the close of the exposition's main tune the darker octave that Adler describes on PDF 134.

**Why not at 60–63.** Bar 62 has the rising sixth E♭4→C5 under a subito p. On one string that is a slurred shift of nine semitones. Adler PDF 82–83 lists wide skips on one string as a section risk ("difficulties in simply hitting the correct pitch"). From 64 the line moves by step and by thirds only.

**Fix (if wanted).** In `tTwoVlnTwo`, bar 64: `\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul G" aes'2.~(\f^\startTextSpan aes'8 g'8)`, and close with `bes4)\stopTextSpan` at the end of 67. I rank this below F1 and F2 because Vln II is the lower octave here, not the melody. Two one-string marks within eight bars (*sul A* 57–59, *sul G* 64–67) is also the most this page should carry. I would leave it out if F1 is accepted.
