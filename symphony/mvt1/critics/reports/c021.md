## Overall judgment
I went through the places this cycle asked about. Most of them are functional in the way the models are functional, so they should stay:
- **Transition 33–46.** It imitates the T1 head, sequences it (37–38), fragments it (39–40), runs up and holds g'''. Tchaikovsky 6/i 55–80 (one sixteenth-note cell sequenced, then a held, dying unison) and Dvořák 9/i 63–90 (the T1 head sequenced downward, then a three-note fragment repeated four times) work the same way. Neither puts a new lyrical idea in its transition, so I do not ask for one here.
- **Development wedge 103–108.** The units shorten from one bar to half a bar to a quarter. Tchaikovsky 6/i 259–266 accelerates the same way.
- **Climax line 113–115.** It is a scale to a♭''' and then a held g'''. This is right: Tchaikovsky's post-climax melody (6/i 283–297) is also a scale.

I found three spots where the top line is only filler and a master would place a motive.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 137–138: the recapitulation's bridge into T2 is a bare held G
**Problem.** After the T1 head (135–136), the top line is Cl 1 and Vln I holding g' for six beats over G7. Then the T2 pickup arrives. In the exposition the same spot (43–46) carries the planted sixth: Hn 1 `es'4 c''4~ | c''2 bes'2`, a 13th falling to the 5th over the dominant, with an oboe line. HISTORY protects that planting (cycles 13 and 20). The recapitulation drops it, so the C-major T2 arrives unprepared. The motto's minor sixth (G–E♭, heard at 135) also never turns major before the theme that answers it.

**Evidence.** In a standing on the dominant, the melodic line thins to one voice and uses "motives from the upcoming section" (Caplin summary, above). The exposition 43–44 already does this, and the recapitulation should rhyme with it. Tchaikovsky 6/i 89–100 likewise prepares each phrase of its second theme with a short anticipating figure (horns 90, 93: C♯–C, G–F♯).

**Fix.** `recOneOboeOne`, section bars 15–16 (movement 137–138). Replace `R1*2 |` with:
`r2 g'4(^\markup \italic "dolce" e''4~ | e''2\> d''2)\! |`

The oboe has just played the T1 head at 135–136, so its G–E♭ sixth becomes G–E♮ two bars later. Over G7 (Vln II f', Vla d', Hn 3·4 b/g, Vc b,), the e'' is the 13th and resolves to d'', which is the exposition's c''→b♭' on the same scale degrees. Ob 2 rests, so there is no conflict on the shared staff. The cor anglais takes the line at 139 (r8 g' a' b'), which joins the two oboe-family colours. The oboe is the only part that changes; no part doubles it. The slur opens at 137 and closes at the end of 138. This does not affect the protected range 123–134.

### 2. Bar 106: the development never touches T2's most distinctive cell
**Problem.** The development works the T1 head (77–96, 109–112), the T2 head (97–102 and the wedge's `2.~ 8 8` units) and the motto. It never uses the dotted step followed by the rising arpeggio (49: `des'4. c'8 bes4 des'8 f'8`). Critic 1 created that cell and critic 11 judged it the theme's second impulse. Bar 106 is now a neutral neighbour figure, `c'''4. b''8 d'''4. c'''8`.

**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/i builds its development (flute 197–212) from the *continuation* figures of its closing theme (E5 dotted, C♯5–B4, the rising E–G♯–B arpeggio at 199, the A–C♯–E arpeggio repeated at 206–212), not only from the theme's head.

**Fix.** Change the second half of 106 to the cell's leap and rising arpeggio. This is section bar 30 of `dev`.
- `devFluteOne` and `devVlnOne`: `c'''4. b''8 f''4 aes''8 c'''8 |`
- `devFluteTwo`, `devOboeOne` and `devTptOne` (an octave lower): `c''4. b'8 f'4 aes'8 c''8 |`

Those five parts are every doubling of this line. On beats 3–4 the harmony is B°7 over D (Vln II f'/b', Vla d', Ob 2/Cl 1/Hn 1 b'), so f'' and a♭'' are chord tones and c''' passes to the d♭''' of 107. The outer voices move in contrary motion: c'''→d♭''' above, D→D♭ below. Do not end the bar on d''': I tried it, and it makes outer-voice octaves D–D♭ with the bass. The wedge's crescendo and its unit lengths do not change.

### 3. Bars 116–118: the post-climax collapse is a plain scale
**Problem.** After g''' at 115, Vln I, Ob 1 and Fl 1 drop to `g''2 f''2 | es''2 d''2 | d''1` in even half notes. This is the first lyrical moment after the movement's climax, and it carries no motive.

**Evidence.** After the climax of Tchaikovsky 6/i, the strings descend by step (Vln I 283–297). Each step is answered by the trombones' sigh `C♯4 dotted–B3 sixteenth–B3 half` (285–297), so the scale carries a recognisable rhythm. Here the wedge (103–105) has already made the T2 head rhythm `2.~ 8 8` the development's sound, so the collapse should speak it as it dies away.

**Fix.** These are section bars 40–42 of `dev`.
- `devVlnOne`: replace `g''2\> f''2 | es''2 d''2 | d''1\p |` with `g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8 | es''4. d''8 c''2) | d''1\p |`
- `devOboeOne`: the same notes.
- `devFluteOne`, bar 116 only: `g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8)\! |` (the flute rests from 117).

The harmony is G7 at 116, a C minor 6/4 over G at 117 (Vla c', Vln II e♭') and G at 118. So e♭'' and c'' are chord tones, d'' is a passing note, and the line closes on scale degree 2 over V. The retransition motto stretto (121–122) is unchanged and is not anticipated, because the motto's sixth is absent here.

## Countermelodies
- **56–68 and 148–162 (Cl 1 with Hn 2).** These use one two-bar module, `x2 r8 y z w | v2 u2`, four or five times. It fills the melody's held notes correctly. Tchaikovsky's counter-voices at 6/i 89–100 do the same: held chromatic horns and three-note viola echoes (F♯3–G–A, 93 and 97). This is serviceable, not a fault.
- **123–126 counterline and 154–157 brass.** Both are good. I leave them alone, as HISTORY asks.
