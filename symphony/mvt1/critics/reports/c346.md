# Critic 346: Playability, instrument by instrument (v346)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 6 The Woodwind Choir and ch. 7 Individual Woodwinds (adler1 PDF 168-232), second pass. Cycle 256 read the same chapters. I read every text page in the range in full and rendered every page from 172 to 232 at 110 dpi as an image, so I took the register charts and the examples from the images, not the OCR. The charts are flute Ex. VII-2 (PDF 185), oboe VII-32 (198), cor anglais VII-47 (205), clarinet VII-56 (210), bass clarinet VII-73 (216), bassoon VII-89 and VII-90 (226-227) and contrabassoon VII-97 (230). The examples I looked at closely are Brahms 1/iv (VII-9, PDF 188), Brahms *Haydn Variations* (VII-99, PDF 231) and Tchaikovsky 6/i (VII-93, PDF 227).

Method. I parsed full.midi with a script and listed, for each of the eleven woodwind parts, its range, every stretch played with no gap of 0.2 s or more (a quaver rest at crotchet = 126 is 0.24 s), the bars without a full bar's rest, and every note in a register Adler marks as weak, hard or soft-restricted, with its velocity and its unison doublings. \breathe marks do not show in the MIDI, so I read every long stretch in the .ily files. I also checked bassoon slurs for downward leaps, every wind trill and tremolo, the transposition labels in the score and parts, and the page counts of the parts.

## Findings

### 1. Bassoon 2 plays 156-166 without a breath, about 25 s from mp to fff in its low register (applied)

Bars 156-166, recTwo.ily, `recTwoBsnTwo`:
```
f,1\mp\< | f,1 | g,1\ff | g,1\< | aes,1\fff | d2 g,2 | e,1\ff\> | f,1 | d,2\mp g,2~ | g,1\> | c,2.\p r4 |
```
Bassoon 2 plays the bass of the recapitulation's climax: F2 F2 G2 G2 A♭2 D3-G2 E2 F2 D2-G2 C2. None of the notes is slurred, but there is no rest from 156.1 to 166.3. At this passage's tempi (poco animando 115-120, largamente 100, rit. to 81) that is 24.8 s, ff and fff for five bars of it, on F2-A♭2, a register that takes a lot of air. After 166 the player has one crotchet rest and then holds C2 f-ff through 167-170. Every other wind and brass part in these bars has a breath. Fl 1·2, Ob 2, Hn 1·2, the three trumpets, Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone breathe after 159. The tuba breathes after 158 and 160. Bsn 1 rests on beat 4 of 161 and again on beat 4 of 162. Cycle 146 already called this "Bsn 2's long 156-174 stretch" and gave the player a rest before it, but put no breath inside it.

Evidence from the reading:
- Adler PDF 175: "careful consideration should be given to the limitations placed on the player in certain registers of an instrument."
- PDF 172 and Ex. VII-9 (PDF 188): "give the [player] time to rest after especially difficult or sustained passages". Brahms dovetails the two flutes so that one can breathe "without diminishing the intensity of the passage".
- PDF 230: the low double reed "takes a considerable amount of breath. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests."
- PDF 212: multiple bassoons "relieve one another" in sustained passages. Here Bsn 1 breathes and Bsn 2 does not.
- Score precedent: cycle 256 gave the contrabassoon a breath for 14.8 s of low crotchets at 131-138, and cycle 186 gave the flutes theirs at 159.

Fix. Two breath marks, with no note or rhythm change:
```
  g,1\< \breathe | aes,1\fff | d2 g,2 | e,1\ff\> | % c314: ...
  f,1 \breathe | d,2\mp g,2~ | g,1\> | c,2.\p r4 |
```
The first mark is after 159, with the rest of the winds and brass, before the fff at 160. The second is after 163, at the end of the diminuendo and before the mp at 164. Bsn 1 shares the staff: it plays G3 an octave above at 159 and is in unison at 163, so it gets the same marks (`g1\< \breathe`, `\partCombineUnisono f,1 \breathe`) and the score shows one comma per place. The longest stretches are now 8.1 s (156-159), 9.6 s (160-163) and 7.0 s (164-166.3). The bass goes on through both breaths: the Cbsn and Cb hold G across 159→160, and the Vc, Cb and harp hold F across 163→164. A comment in the file gives the reason and the source.

- **Cl 1 dolce link 45-46** on the throat tones. Adler calls them "rather pale" but gives the clarinet a full dynamic range everywhere (PDF 210). A pale p link into the S theme suits the passage, and the notes are protected (c161, c274).
- **C.a. at 69-72** (to written C6, c331). Adler says the top register "sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality" (PDF 205). Here the instrument is one of five doubling the P head's upper octave at f, so its personality does not matter. Ob 2 is busy with the harmony. Keep.
