## Findings

### F1. 179–181: the held tonic in Vln II, violas and cellos is struck again on every bar line (applied)

**Bars and what is wrong.** From 179 (Tempo I) to the end the harmony is one C-minor chord over C. Only the last motto moves: Vln I's E♭5 tied over from 178, D5 on 179.4, C5–D♭5–C5 at 180–181. The basses hold C2 tied through 179–182 ("change bow at will" at 179). Vln II G4, violas E♭4 and cellos C3 are written as three untied whole notes, `g'1 | g'1 | g'1~`, `es'1 | es'1 | es'1`, `c1 | c1 | c1~`. So three string parts re-attack the same pitch at 180.1 and 181.1. The records call these notes held: c160 ("inner voices held"), mvt1.md ("Vln II and violas hold the inner parts") and finale.md item 5 ("over the same held C in the bass").

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 49 (Ex. 3-5): ties combine notes across the bar line and make durations no single note value can. PDF 51: "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation". A note struck on each downbeat is a metrical accent, and here it falls in three parts at once on 180.1 and 181.1. The motto's own accents are elsewhere: the syncopated E♭5 tied across 178→179 (PDF 51) and the chromatic D♭5 on the weak third beat of 180 (PDF 50, built-in accent). PDF 47–48: the beat is still heard when no part sounds it, and the timpani pulse already sounds it at 179–180. The re-strikes put downbeat weight under a line whose expressive points are off the downbeat. The basses are already tied.

At 175–176 the violas' C4 is also struck twice. I keep it there because the chorale bass moves G→E♭ at 176 and all the chorale parts change together. At 179–181 nothing changes.

**Fix (coda.ily), applied in the scratch copy:**
```
codaVlnTwo 179–182:  g'1~^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | g'1~ | g'1^~\> | g'1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer |
codaVla    179–182:  es'1~^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | es'1~ | es'1\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | e'1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer |
codaVc     179–182:  c1~^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | c1~ | c1~\> | c1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer |
```
"change bow at will" moves from 181 to 179, where the long note now begins, as in the basses. "morendo" stays at 181. Pitches, levels, hairpins, the last chord and the five parts tied into movement II are unchanged. Six note-ons go from full.midi. Tbn 1's protected restrike at 180 stays.

This undoes no fix. c438 judged these bows on the assumption that the notes are held.

### F2. 178–181: the motto's last neighbour D♭ is cut off from its resolution by a slur end (applied)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 54–55: in Ex. 3-15 each accented dissonance is slurred to its resolution. PDF 52 (Ex. 3-9 to 3-11): slurs show the rhythmic groups. The script listed every note on beat 1 or 3 where a slur ends and the next note follows at once a step lower. There are 55 such notes (v568).

**Found.** 53 are not dissonances cut off from a resolution:
- 38 are the P head's second-bar figure, where a chord tone is followed by the turn or the chromatic fall.
- 9 are cadence strokes and chord changes (12.1 to an anticipation tied into 13, 63.3, 141–142, 161.1).
- 5 are the chorale's E♭→D at 176→177, both chord tones.
- 1 is a divisi artifact (Vln II at 110).

The other 2 are real, and both are the motto's last neighbour figure:
- **180.3–181, Vln I:** `d''4( | c''2 des''2) | c''1~`. The slur ends on the D♭5 neighbour, and the C5 that resolves it starts a new bow on the downbeat of 181.
- **178.3–179, cellos:** `c2( des2) | c1`. The slur ends on D♭3, and C3 at 179 is a new bow. The bass clarinet, Tbn 1 and bass trombone, which play the same motto bass, slur through to the C at 179.

In the opening motto the same figure is one group: the cellos slur C–D♭–C at 3.1–3.4, and the bass clarinet slurs it to 4.1. The D♭ is a chromatic dissonance with a built-in accent (PDF 50). Its resolution is the weaker note and belongs to the same group (PDF 54–55). In the last statement the bow change falls exactly on the resolution, so the end of the movement's main idea is articulated against its own grouping.

**Fix (coda.ily), applied in the scratch copy:**
```
codaVlnOne 179.4–182:  es''2. d''4( | c''2) des''2( | c''1)~\>^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | c''1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer ...
codaVc     178–179:    c2( des2\stopTextSpan | c1)~^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | ...
```
For Vln I the bow change moves from 181.1 to 180.3. The D♭ starts its own bow, which suits its built-in accent (PDF 50), and D♭–C share one bow of about 7 s, as in bar 3. This is within c438's accepted 7.8 s. For the cellos the slur now ends on the C at 179 with the other motto-bass instruments, and the F1 tie continues from it. No note, level, hairpin, "sul D"/"sul G" line or timing changes. full.midi note-ons are unaffected by the slurs.

### F3. Weak-beat dynamics, soft accents, unshaped runs (kept)

- **Soft accents.** I listed 53 accents and tenutos under ppp–mp. The tenutos are S syncopations and appoggiaturas (Ex. 3-12 type).
