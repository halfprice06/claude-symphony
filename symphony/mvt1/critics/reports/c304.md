## Findings

### 1. Bar 82, the first A♭ climax: the held brass are ff, level with the head they accompany (APPLIED)

**Problem.** At 82 the P head is stated ff in three octaves: Fl 1·2 and Vln I at E♭6; Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1 and Vln II at E♭5; violas alone at E♭4. Ten brass hold the A♭ chord under it at the same ff: Hn 1 A♭4, Hn 2 E♭4, Hn 3·4 C4, Tpt 2 A♭4, Tpt 3 E♭4, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 A♭3, B.tbn A♭2 and tuba A♭1. The held E♭4, C4 and A♭4 lie on the notes the head's two lower octaves pass through (E♭–D♭–C). Casanelles says that loud brass have the densest spectrum and come forward (PDF 289), that sounds in one band mask each other (PDF 287), and that a dynamic should state the level that results, as Mahler marks his trumpets below the woodwinds (PDF 271–272, Fig. 6.1). At 82 the sustained harmony, not the head, will be in front, and the violas' lower octave will be covered. c264 called that octave "barely heard" and kept it. Every other melodic tutti in the score already grades the held brass one level below the tune: 27–32, 97–102, 131, 140, 160 (c244) and 167–170. c244 recorded 82 as the only ungraded one and did not reopen it because it was "protected".

**Answer to the protection.** The do-not-touch items on 82 are its layout (c164, c254), the three-climax order (c243, c260, c264, c294) and its harmony. None of them is about the held brass being level with the tune. This change keeps every note, the layout, the octave plan, the timpani and the bass. It lowers 82 slightly against 122 and 160 (both fff), so the climax order becomes clearer, not weaker.

**Fix (sections/dev.ily, bar 82 = dev bar 6):** the held whole notes go from ff to f, and the strokes at 83 get ff back, as at 27/32.
- devHnOne `aes'1\f | aes'4->\ff …`, devHnTwo `es'1\f | f'4->\ff …`
- devHnThree `c'1\f | d'4->\ff …`, devHnFour `c'1\f | b4->\ff …`
- devTptThree `es'1\f | b4->\ff …`
- devTbnOne `c'1\f | aes4->\ff …`, devTbnTwo `aes1\f | f4->\ff …`
- devBassTbn `aes,1\f | g,4->\ff …`, devTuba `aes,,1\f | g,,4->\ff …`

Tpt 2 stays ff because it shares a staff with Tpt 1, which has the head (shared staves need equal dynamics). That leaves one trumpet on the root, as at 160. The woodwinds, strings and timpani stay ff, following Rimsky-Korsakov's woodwind-a-degree-above-brass rule (rimsky-principles PDF 112, cited in c234). The score page shows f on Hn 1·2, Hn 3·4, Tpt 3, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and tuba at 82, and ff on their strokes at 83.

### 2. Bars 23.4–24.2: Hn 4 G2 makes a major third in the second octave (APPLIED)

**Problem.** In the first-theme hemiola, the third cell (23.4–24.2) is V6/5: Cb B1, Bsn 1·2 and Vc B2, with Hn 4 on G2 between them. G2–B2 is a major third at 98/123 Hz, and B1–G2 a minor sixth below it. In the harmonic series the bottom is octave, fifth and fourth, and thirds first appear between partials 4 and 5 (Casanelles PDF 274, and instrumental synthesis PDF 259). A third this low beats, and in a mp< crescendo of horn, bassoons and cellos it will thicken the cell. c259 fixed the same fault one bar later (Hn 4 F2 over D2 at 25; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 124). The recapitulation (135.4–136.2) has no Hn 4 at all.

**Answer to the protection** ("low horns 23–26 accents", c106/c156/c259; "Hn 3·4 at 23–26", c297). The horn rhythm, ties and every accent are unchanged. Hn 4 keeps the root G, so the chord stays V6/5 and does not turn into vii°. It drops back to G2 on the accented 24.3, where G2 is the fifth over the C2 bass and fits the series. G3 is written D4 for the horn, still the low middle register, a fourth under Hn 3's D4.

**Fix (sections/tOne.ily, tOneHnFour):** `g2 g2 | c2.\mp\<-> g4->~ | g2 g,2->~ | g,4 f,2-> r4 |` (was `g,4->~ | g,2 g,2->~`).

### 4. Bars 97–102, 117–121 and 131–134: lines inside held brass (no change)
