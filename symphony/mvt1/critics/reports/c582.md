## Findings

No finding changes a note. I list the six candidates I tested hardest, ranked by how much each bears on the gap to a master's score.

### Candidate 1. Bars 145–147: V7 with ♯5 into the C-major S (keep)

**Question.** PDF 542 and 549 (point 5) say a V7 "frequently" takes a raised 5th in the soprano that leads to the 3rd of a major tonic (Ex. 30-17: Chopin Op. 35, Franck). The V7 at 145–146 goes to the movement's first plain C major at 147. Ob 1 holds D5 on 146.3–4 and Vln II enters on E5/G5 at 147. A D♯5 on 146.4 would make the textbook V7♯5 → I, with the D♯ handed to the violins' E5.

**Why it stays.**
- Ob 1's G4–E5–D5 at 145–146 is recorded as a cross-movement seed (c580: the rising sixth of the S head and of the finale chorale's top line). The "soprano 13th at 145–146" and the 143–150 lead-in are protected (c239).
- On 146.4 the S pickup G3 rises a sixth to E4 in Hn 1, violas and cellos. A D♯5 at the same moment would put a chromatic soprano note against the pickup, so the soft entry of the theme would lose its focus.
- PDF 532 says mixture and chromatic colour stand out more in major. The score keeps 147–155 diatonic and spends its colour at 156–166 (c492). A ♯5 at 146 would spend some of that colour early.

### Candidate 2. Bar 151: V7♯5 applied to IV (keep; not possible)

**Question.** PDF 542 (Ex. 30-17c, Beethoven Op. 120) calls the augmented V7 applied to IV as frequent as the plain augmented triad. Bar 151 is C7/E → F at 152.

**Why it stays.** The only G at 151 is the tune's own note, the S head's pickup G3 in Hn 1, violas and cellos. It cannot be raised. Bar 148 already has the ♯5 colour (F–A–C♯ → D minor, PDF 538–539), and a second one four bars later would be repetition, not colour.

### Candidate 3. Bar 157: ♮4 °4/3 before the cadential 6/4 (keep)

**Question.** PDF 561 (Ex. 31-14) gives the °4/3 on ♮4, coming from IV or II6, as the most frequent rising °7 before V. Over the F bass of 157 that chord is F–A♭–B–D.

**Why it stays.**
- The tune at 157.3 is B♭5 (C6–B♭5–A♭5–G5). B♮ under it would clash on the beat.
- The chord is the stop chord B–D–F–A♭, which the score saves for 26, 138, 161 and 170. The finale resolves it.
- c542 tested the ♯4 form (vii°7 of V) and rejected it. c553 tested A♭ in the bass and the m7–M10 exchange and rejected them. The 156–157 climb is protected. I found no route in this reading that the earlier passes had not already tried.

### Candidate 4. Bars 123–124 and 145: CT°7 embellishing V7 (keep)

**Question.** PDF 546 (Ex. 30-23a) shows a CT°7 on G (G–A♯–C♯–E) rising by semitones into V7. The score holds V7 for two bars twice, at 123–124 and at 145–146, and never embellishes it this way.

**Why it stays.**
- 123–124 is the collapse after the fff, protected for its exit order and levels (c143, c289, c581).
- At 145 the V7 sounds pp under Bsn 1's answer and the oboe seed (candidate 1). Its job there is to stand still before C major.
- A CT°7 is a chord of motion. Either place would gain activity at the point where the form needs a held dominant.

### Candidate 6. Preparation of the remote chords (§5, PDF 536–537): survey. Follows; record.

I checked each chord that the reading would call remote for one of the three preparations PDF 536–537 names:
- **G♭ (♭VII in A♭) at 60.** G♭ is first heard at 51 as the seventh of V6/5 of IV (A♭–C–E♭–G♭), its simple, normal use. This is the "prior statement of the same scale degree used in a simpler, more normal fashion".
- **F♭ at 61.** It is a step of the chromatic bass G♭–F♭–E♭ ("part of a chromatic melodic progression").
- **E minor at 78.** It comes inside the sequence A♭–E–C ("chords that belong to a repeated pattern tend to stand out less").
- **A♭ (♭VI) at 160.** A♭ is already in ♭VII7 at 156.4 and in iv at 157.
- **D♭ at 115.** It comes from the motto's D♭ and the lament bass.
- **B minor at 97.** This is the one unprepared shift (B♭ minor → B minor). PDF 537 says the most drastic alterations "function more effectively within a large structure than as details". The shift places the station B of the B–D–F–A♭ plan (c553).
