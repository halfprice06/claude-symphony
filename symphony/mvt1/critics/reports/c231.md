## Findings

### 1. The B-minor second theme at 97–102 has its lower octave buried, and it lacks the cellos the theme was born with (applied)

**Bars.** 97 (beat 4) to 102, the ff statement of S in B minor at letter F.

**What is wrong.** This is the only ff statement of the second theme before the recapitulation, and the development's main melodic event. The tune is laid out as Fl 1 (D6), Ob 1 + Vln I (D5), and C.a. + Vln II sul G (D4). So the strings carry it in only two octaves. The lower octave, D4–D5 (Vln II and the cor anglais), lies in exactly the register of the f counter in Hn 1·2 (B3–D4, the eighth-note figures F♯3–D4–C♯4) and Tbn 1 (B3–D4). Meanwhile the cellos play ff whole notes B2–B2–E3–C♯3–F♯2–F♯2. Those notes are already sounding in the bass clarinet, Bsn 2 and bass trombone (B2), with the double basses and contrabassoon an octave lower. The cellos are the fourth instrument on a bass that needs no more weight.

The reading gives three reasons this falls short of what the movement does elsewhere:
- Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 54) says a melody in the middle of the range "is not so prominent" and has to be reinforced by doubling or by timbre. The lower octave here gets neither: it is one violin section and one cor anglais inside a brass counter.
- For a "full cantabile melody, extremely tense in character, and in forte passages for choice", the treatise's layout is double octaves: Vln I / Vln II / violas or cellos (PDF 62 a; Ex. 32 *Antar*, PDF 210). The movement already does this at its other two big melodic peaks: Vln I/II/violas at 27–32, and Vln I/II/cellos at 158–161, where the cellos play the tune in tenor clef. At 97 it does not.
- The cello is the instrument for "tense passionate cantabile" on the A string (PDF 56). Violins and cellos in octaves, with the cellos on the A or D string, is the method for "very expressive passages" and gives "a more resonant tone" (PDF 60–61 e). Ex. 30 (*Sheherazade* III, PDF 207) shows exactly Vln I in octaves over Vln II + cellos, with the winds holding.

There is a smaller fault too. The tune's pickup F♯4 at 97 is in Vln I and Ob 1, but the lower octave has no pickup, because F♯3 is below the violins' open G. Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 60 c) names this case: when the lower octave goes below the G string, another string section takes it. The cellos can.

**Why it matters for the melody.** S was introduced by the horn and cellos at 47. In B minor, ff, it is heard without the cellos, as a violin-and-wind tune. Putting the cellos on its lower octave does three things. It gives the theme its own colour at its loudest development statement. It adds a whole section to the octave that is now covered. And it makes 97–102 match 158–161, so the listener hears the two ff statements of S as the same tune at two pitches.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/dev.ily, devVc):**

```lilypond
\transpose c cis { bes,2.\ff f4( | \clef tenor des'2.~ des'8 c'8) | bes4( ges'2-- f'4) | es'4.( des'8 c'4 es'8 ges'8) |
bes'2--( a'2) | f'4( des''2-- c''8 g'8) } | \clef bass
```

In sounding pitches:
- 97: the cellos still strike the B2 arrival ff with the basses, then take the pickup F♯3 on beat 4.
- 98–102: they play Vln II's line at the unison (D4 … D5 at 102, A and D strings, tenor clef), and return to bass clef for 103.

The bass at 98–102 is still Cb + Cbsn (B1, E2, C♯2, F♯1) and B.cl + Bsn 2 + B.tbn (B2, E3, C♯3, F♯2/F♯3), so Rimsky-Korsakov's rule that the basses never carry a line without cellos or bassoons (PDF 56) is kept: Bsn 2 is there.

### Points examined and kept

- **15–22, P exposition.** Vln I alone, f, is the top voice, and the syncopation in Vln II and violas is marked mf. Rimsky-Korsakov's "artificial" means, dynamics, is used (PDF 54). The D♭6 peak at 20 is doubled an octave below by Vln II (PDF 59; Note I, PDF 62). Keep.
- **56–63, S in the violins.** Unison p sul A, then octaves from 60 as the line rises to D♭6. This is the treatise's own sequence (PDF 57, then 58–59). Keep.
- **107–110, S head in the violas, p.** Four bars, the top voice, with Fl 1 joining at 109. This is a "short characteristic phrase" doubled by wind (PDF 55). Keep; it is also protected.
- **147–155, S in C.** Violas and cellos in octaves with Hn 1, while the violins play sul tasto tremolo. This is PDF 60 d), "when the violins are otherwise employed". Keep.
- **177–182, last motto in Vln I, pp.** It lies in the middle register but is the top voice, so it stands out by position (PDF 54). Keep.
