## Findings

### 1. Hn 4 part, bars 11–12: a stale bass clef for two treble-range notes (applied)

**Problem.** The Hn 4 part switches to the bass clef at bar 11 under the text "bass clef in new notation: sounds a 5th lower". It switches back to treble at bar 13. In between there are only two whole notes:

- bar 11: written A3 (concert D3), p<
- bar 12: written C♯4 (concert F♯3), f

Both notes sit on the treble staff with one ledger line or none. This clef dates from before c284, when bar 12 was concert A♭2 (written E♭3). That note did need the bass clef. c284 moved bar 12 up to F♯3 and left the clef plan alone.

The player now reads two clef changes and a notation warning, all in the dark Lento, for two notes they could read without either change. The clef plan's own comment says the bass clef should only cover low notes. The other two bass clef spans, 23–25 and 135–137, do follow that rule: they reach written D3 and C3.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 32 says to use the bass clef for horn only for notes below written G3, and to change clef only when the passage is long enough to justify it. Neither note at 11–12 is below G3, and two bars is not long enough.

**Fix.** In `parts-clefs.ily`, drop the first bass clef and the treble return. Move the new-notation text to the first real bass clef at bar 23:

```lilypond
hnFourClefs = {
  s1*22 \clef bass % c396: 11-12 (written A3, C#4) stay in treble
  s1*0_\markup \small \italic "bass clef in new notation: sounds a 5th lower"
  s1*3 \clef treble % back to treble at 26: ...
  s1*109 \clef bass s1*3 \clef treble % c393: 135-137 as 23-25
}
```

Bar arithmetic: before the change, 10 + 2 + 10 = 22 bars led to the clef at 23. The bass clef now falls at the same bar, and every later offset is unchanged.

**Applied in scratch.** I rendered the new Hn 4 page. Bars 11–12 now read as an A3 and a C♯4 in treble. The cautionary bass clef now ends the line at 22, with the text under 23. Hn 4 is still 2 pp., the even count it needs to run into movement II. No other part changed.
