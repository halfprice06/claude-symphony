## How a score reader would see it
An experienced reader who does not know the composer would find v108 competent and idiomatic. Its themes have a clear identity, and the cyclic plan and the large climaxes are well placed. The signs that a master did not write it are local. These are the places where every part does the obvious thing at the same moment, and the places where phrases close the same way.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 23–26 and 135–138: the whole orchestra plays repeated quarter notes. APPLIED.
- **Problem:** Above the melody, about fifteen staves (eighteen in the recapitulation) repeat chord tones in quarter notes, for example `g'4 g'4 g'4 f'4`. The hemiola appears only as accents on repeated notes. On page 2 and page 12 this is the plainest texture in the score, and it reads as a pad.
- **Evidence:**
  - Dvořák 9/i bars 39–46: the strings play the line in octaves with long–short syncopation, while winds and brass hold each chord for a bar and then cut it off (`B4 2 | B4 0.5 r`). The melody, the held harmony and the bass pulse each have their own rhythm.
  - Rimsky-Korsakov, as quoted above: when strings carry the line, the wind should sustain the harmony.
  - The harmony here already changes every three beats (Cm, B°, Cm, Fm). Only the note values hide it.
- **Fix:**
  - Winds, horns, trombones, tuba and cellos now hold each three-beat chord as a dotted half, tied across the barline. Same pitches, same accent positions, same dynamics.
  - Vln II, viola, Cb and Cbsn keep the quarter-note pulse, and the timpani keep their hemiola strokes at 135–137.
  - Example (Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1, from `g'4\mf\<-> g'4 g'4 f'4-> | f'4 f'4 g'4-> g'4 | g'4 f'4-> f'4 f'4`): `g'2.\mf\<-> f'4->~ | f'2 g'2->~ | g'4 f'2.->`
  - Example (Bsn 2, B.tbn and Tuba in the recapitulation): `c2.-> b,4->~ | b,2 c2->~ | c4 f,2-> aes,4`
- **Protection:** the hemiola is protected (cycles 7, 17, 28, 57). The hemiola is kept and made clearer, and the bass accents survive in Cb and Cbsn.
- **Files:** tOne.ily (Ob 2, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Hn 1–4, Vc) and recOne.ily (Ob 2, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Hn 1–3, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, Tuba, Vc).

### 2. Bars 55, 68 and 72: three root-position V–I cadences in A♭ in 18 bars. Applied at 68.
- **Problem:**
  - 54–55: E♭ goes to A♭, with the cello closing on C.
  - 66–68: the dominant lasts six beats, then a rit., then a whole-bar A♭ tonic at p in every part.
  - 71–72: the codetta repeats the same bass, E♭ then A♭, hammered.
  - The second group therefore closes completely and is then closed again. A reader notices that each paragraph ends the same way.
- **Evidence:**
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i (cadence.csv, offsets converted to bars): the second group cadences are AC at 95, AC at 107, AC at 115, HC at 123, AC at 137, then a plagal cadence at 145 as the closing section begins. Both the type of cadence and the way it is approached change.
  - Dvořák 9/i: the closing-theme cadences at 156 and 162 are elided. At 156 the first violins run up in sixteenths into the restatement, and 163–166 repeat a cadential tag in eighths. No bar holds the tonic still.
  - Schmalfeldt's "one more time" technique: an evaded cadence forces the cadential approach to be repeated before a real close. The codetta at 69–72 already does that repeating, so it only needs an evaded cadence before it.
- **Fix at 68:** V7 now resolves deceptively to vi (F minor). The melody `g4 f8 es8 des4 bes4 | aes1` is unchanged, and so are the 4–3 at 66, the six-beat dominant, the rit. and the one-bar suffix.
  - Vc: `f,8(\p c f aes c'2)`
  - Cb: `f,1\p`
  - Bsn 2: `f2. r4`
  - Vla: `<c' f'>1\p`
  - Harp RH: `r2 <f' aes' c''>2\arpeggio`
  - Harp LH: `<f,, f,>2\p r2` (F♮ pedal is already set)
  - The bass rises E♭ to F, the seventh D♭ falls to C, and the third of vi (A♭) is doubled, which is correct for V–vi.
  - The exposition's structural cadence moves to the codetta (71–72). The recapitulation ending (162–166, the E♮→E♭ turn) also avoids a plain close, so the two now correspond.
- **Protection:** this touches "endings 64–68" (cycles 11 and 19) and "one-bar cadence suffixes" (cycle 33). The stated reasons were that the line is built from the theme's own figures and that the suffix bar exists. Both still hold. It keeps the cycle-52 rule: the change is diatonic, with no Neapolitan and no B–D–F–A♭.
- **File:** tTwo.ily.

### 3. Bars 27–32, 69–72, 131–134 and 167–170: the same tutti recipe four times. Not applied.
- **Problem:** each time, the tune is in octaves, the winds and brass hold whole notes, and the Cb plays repeated eighths. Cycle 27 already counted this. Dvořák 9/i varies its tuttis between unison lines, wind chords that are cut off, and string figures (39–52).
- **Fix:** at 69–72 only, the Cb could play `aes,,4 r8 aes,,8` (the bar-15 rhythm) instead of half notes. Alternatively, the violas could change from repeated eighths to the turn figure of 103–108.
- **Status:** not applied, because 69–72 is protected (cycles 11 and 51).

### 4. Bars 47–55 and 147–155: one root-position chord per bar. Not applied.
- **Problem:** the harp and Cb play a root on every downbeat. This has been open since cycle 22. A reader takes it as the clearest "student" sign in the movement's best tune.
- **Fix:** only for the protected passages, if Dan wants it. At 49 (exposition) the bass could take `des,` (first-inversion iii) instead of `bes,,`. At 51 the bass could take `ges,2 c,2`.
- **Status:** not applied (protected).

### 5. Four-bar grid in the second group and the theme combination. Not applied.
- **Problem:** 47–50, 51–54, 56–59, 60–63, 69–72, 73–76, 103–106 and 107–110 are all four bars. Only 27–32 (six bars) and the three-bar fugato break the grid.
- **Evidence:** Tchaikovsky's Andante theme is also square. He breaks the grid with the Moderato mosso at 101 rather than within the phrases.
- **Fix:** none needed in movement I beyond finding 2. Noted for movements II and III.

### 6. Every first-group phrase starts with the same upbeat rhythm. Not applied.
- **Problem:** `r8 X8 Y4.->` opens 15, 17, 19, 27, 29, 33–40, 77–83, 85–96, 131 and 167. This is deliberate motivic saturation and is mostly acceptable, but the motto head is almost never heard in diminution or augmentation except at 117–122 and 171–175.
- **Fix:** none in movement I.

## v98 compared with v108 (cycles 79–88)
- **Section diffs:** intro, tOne and coda are unchanged in notes. Note changes are in dev, recOne and recTwo. The other changes are slurs, dynamics, breath rests and the MIDI tempo map.
- **Improvements:**
  - The recapitulation cut (cycle 80, now 182 bars) answers a real proportion problem: the second group was too complete, compared with Tchaikovsky's roughly 42%.
  - Harmonic-rhythm details: 148–149 and 155.
  - Brass f at 131–134.
  - Breath rests, now staggered (cycle 87).
- **Nothing got worse.**
- **Limit of cycles 79–88:** none of the ten cycles touched the problems in findings 1–4, so the movement improved only slightly. Most remaining "not by a master" signs sit inside passages that later critics protected.
