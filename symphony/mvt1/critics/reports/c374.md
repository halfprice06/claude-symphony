## Findings

### 1. Bar 159, beats 3–4: the dominant seventh's leading tone has only a low trumpet in the brass (applied)

**Bars and problem.** Bar 159 is the V7 between the cadential 6/4 at 158 and the fff ♭VI at 160. It crescendos from ff into fff. On beats 1–2 the leading tone B is strong: Ob 1 and Cl 1 B5, Hn 3·4 a2 B4, Tpt 3 B3, violas B3. On beat 3 the counter in Ob 1, Cl 1 and Hn 3·4 moves B→F, and for the loudest two beats before the climax B is left to Tpt 3's B3 and the violas' lower divisi B3. In the brass the weights on beats 3–4 are G 6 (Tpt 1 G5, Tpt 2 G4, Hn 1·2 G4, Tbn 2 G3, B.tbn G2, tuba G1), D 1 (Tbn 1 D4), F 1 (Hn 3·4 F4), B 0.5 (Tpt 3 B3). Counting the whole orchestra, G sounds in eighteen parts (with the timpani) and B in two. The note that makes 160 a deceptive cadence (B→C under A♭) is the weakest note in the chord.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: one horn is worth half a trumpet above mf, and the rule "does not hold true if the trumpet is in a low register". Tpt 3's B3 is in that register, so it does not carry as a full brass part at fff. In Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) every ff chord has its third in a trumpet or a horn pair and doubles the root, not the fifth, at the bottom; at 159 the brass hold six G's. In the Tchaikovsky climax (Ex. XI-21, PDF 39–41) the trombones hold the chord tones that the upper parts leave.

**Why not undo c169.** c169 moved Tbn 1 at 159 from B3 to D4 so that the V7 had its fifth in the brass and the leading tone was not doubled at pitch with Tpt 3 for the whole bar. On beats 1–2 that reasoning still holds, because B is in five other parts. On beats 3–4 B is in two. Splitting the bar keeps c169's D for the first half and puts a full brass part on B only where the counter leaves it.

**Fix.** sections/recTwo.ily, `recTwoTbnOne`:
```
R1*11 | c'1\ff | d'1 \breathe | c'1\ff |   →   R1*11 | c'1\ff | d'2 b2 \breathe | c'1\ff |
```
Tbn 1 now resolves B3→C4 into 160, in unison with the violas. The trumpets' B→C stays reserved for the finale: Tpt 3 still goes B3→E♭4 and no trumpet changes. Dynamics, breaths, the 158 and 160 layouts and the fff marks at 122 and 160 do not change. The part-combined Tbn 1·2 staff prints D4 and B3 half notes above Tbn 2's G3.

**Checks.** vl.txt diff against v374: removed `bar 160 P8 clTwo:D5->C5 tbnOne:D4->C4` (an octave pair); added `bar 160 P8 tbnOne:B3->C4 vla.lo:B3->C4` (a unison doubling). No new parallel fifths or octaves.

### Considered and kept (no change)

- **Low trumpets at 122 (c244, c264, c284).** Adler's low-trumpet remark (PDF 15) applies: at fff all ten brass at 122 lie between G2 and A♭4, and the trumpets are at A♭4, D4 and B3. c284 already gave the B a horn. Raising Tpt 1 to A♭5 would give 122 the same trumpet register as 160 and flatten the climax order that Adler's hold-back principle supports (PDF 17, 37). Keep.
- **Hammered tutti 27–32 and stop chords 26/138/170.** At 26 the D has only Tpt 3 D4 and Hn 3 D4, and at 27 the E♭ has only Tpt 3 E♭4 and Hn 2, both under Adler's weight. These layouts are quoted at pitch by the finale and protected; the strings and woodwinds carry D and E♭ in strength (vla, Vln II, Ob, Cl). Not reopened.
- **Single horns at f in background chords 23–25 and 69–72.** Adler's horn rule (PDF 15) is about the brass alone. Here the horn chord is a background pad: at 69–72 Hn 3·4 are doubled by bassoons, bass clarinet and violas, and at 23–25 the horns sit over the bassoons, cellos and basses, which carry the hemiola at the same level. Hn 4's G2 and F2 at 24.3–25.3 are low for f (the G2 is the fifth over the C bass, the F2 doubles Bsn 2), but they are protected (c259, c304) and add weight to a crescendo, not a chord tone the brass would otherwise lack. Keep.
- **103–106 horns each on a separate pitch.** They are mf/mp, which Adler (PDF 15) allows. Hn 1 undoubled on the S head is protected. Keep.
- **The three A♭ climaxes as a series.** 82: the first full brass with trumpets after a nine-bar trumpet rest (73–81), held brass f under the ff head (c305). 122: brass-only motto in long notes over the pedal (the Tchaikovsky model, PDF 38–41), then the trumpets' fff entry with tam-tam. 160: the trumpet tune at A♭5 with horn pairs on tune and counter (the Bartók layout, PDF 44–47) and the suspended cymbal. Each adds a colour that the one before did not have (PDF 17, 37). Finding 1 changes one inner note on the way into 160 and nothing at the climaxes themselves.
