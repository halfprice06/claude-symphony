## Findings

### 1. Violas 107–110: write the S phrase in treble clef, changing in the rests before 107 and back to alto at 111. Applied.

**What is wrong.**
- The violas rest at 103–106 and enter at 107 with the S head (`g'2.~( … c''4)` to 110, one slur), doubled by Fl 1 at 109–110.
- In alto clef 9 of the 13 notes need ledger lines. The climb at 109.4–110.3, F5–A♭5–G5, needs three, four and three ledger lines. That is the top of the phrase, the place where the contour matters most. A♭5 at 110 is the only note in any string part that needs more than three ledger lines in its clef (probe).
- In treble clef the whole phrase lies in the stave apart from A♭5 on one ledger line. The flute and the violas, in unison at 109–110, then also look the same on the page.

**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 21: stay in one clef "using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches". Here the alto clef goes past that limit (four lines), and a change would not be "frequent": the violas are otherwise in alto for all 182 bars.
- Gould PDF 23, "after periods of rests": "Where there needs to be a change from the more standard clef, retain this clef until the barline before the next entry." The example on that page (image) is a viola going from alto to treble at the barline before its entry after rests. Bars 103–106 are those rests.
- Gould PDF 22, "between phrases": the slur closes at 110.4, and 111 starts the repeated-quaver figure. The return to alto at 111 falls between the two phrases. Bar 111 also starts a system and the Viola part's next line, so LilyPond prints the warning alto clef at the end of 110 (PDF 21, "at the beginning of the system").
- Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 3 (adler1 PDF 85): "Viola music is usually notated in the alto clef, but to avoid ledger lines, the upper notes are sometimes written in treble clef."
- Corpus: Dvořák, Symphony No. 7 (Mutopia), viola part, mvt II line 216 and mvt IV lines 423–426 (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/DvorakSYMPH7/DvorakSYMPH7-lys/DvorakSYMPH7M{2,4}/…_musique-Viola.ly). Both put two-bar viola phrases around C♯5–F5 into treble clef and return to alto straight after.

**Earlier decisions.**
- c115 removed an alto–treble–alto change at 109–111 and kept the violas in alto "so that 107–110 read in one clef". c315 kept that, calling A♭5 "a single peak inside a phrase".
- The change c115 removed sat at 109, inside the phrase. This one sits in the rests before the phrase and between phrases after it. So 107–110 still read in one clef, and no clef falls inside the slur. That keeps c115's reason.
- On c315's reason: the probe shows three notes in a row on three or more ledger lines, not one, and nine of thirteen notes on ledger lines. PDF 23's viola example covers this case directly.
- The notes, slurs, dynamics and the Fl 1 doubling (c167, c178, c348) do not change.

### Beyond compliance

I asked where, within this reading, the page falls short of a master's score. The clef, stem and notehead work is now at the level of a good modern edition. The viola phrase at 107–110 was the one place where the notation hid the shape of a melody: the S head's climb to A♭5, which is its expressive point, sat on a stack of ledger lines. Finding 1 fixes that. I found no other passage where a change of clef, stem or notehead would make the music clearer to a player or conductor. I make no other proposal.
