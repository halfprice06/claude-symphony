## Findings

All findings are "keep". The harmony and durations are identical to v473, which c473 checked against the same reading, so I looked for what a fourth pass can add: one new corpus check, one spelling check the earlier passes did not record, and one test of whether the exposition transition should use the reading's technique. None of them justifies a change.

### 1. The 6/3 runs sit where Tchaikovsky puts his: in transitions, not in thematic areas (keep)

**Bars.** Wedge 111–116; links 44–45, 6–7.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 290–291 calls the 6/3 series "particularly well suited to passages of a transitional nature" and says its chords "merge into a continuous linear flow". In the Tchaikovsky 6/i annotation, every run of three or more first-inversion chords falls inside a section labelled Transition in form_analysis.csv: iii6–ii6–I6 in A (onset 183.5 crotchets), vi6–I6–I6 in F (227.5), and two runs at 254–257 (vii°6/vi–vi6–vii°6/vi–vi6, then V6–IV6–V6). None falls in the first or second theme. This score does the same thing: its longest 6/3 passage is the development's retransition wedge, and the themes rest on root-position or pedal basses (15, 47, 131, 147). Tchaikovsky's runs last 3–5 chords; the wedge's six chords (111–116, 11.4 s) are in the same range. **Fix:** none.

### 2. Bar 116 follows the reading's applied-chord model and its spelling rule (keep; new record)

**Bars.** 115–117.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 298 (Ex. 18-11b): the applied chord before a tonicised V is lengthened so that V falls on a strong beat. Bar 116 holds the French sixth over C (C–D–F♯–A♭, with B in the violas' figure) for the whole bar at ff, and V arrives on 117.1. The F–F♯–G line that the reading's suspension would supply is already there, stepwise, in Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 2, Hn 4, Tbn 2 and cellos (inst.py dump). PDF 302 gives the spelling rule: a 7̂ that rises is raised. I checked the source: every part that moves to G at 117 writes fis at 116 (devClTwo, devBsnOne, devHnTwo, devHnFour, devTptTwo, devTbnTwo, devVc). The ges spellings in those variables lie elsewhere, in the E♭-minor and D♭ areas. No earlier pass recorded this. A suspension (G held from 115 into 116 and falling to F♯) is not available: no wedge voice has G at 115 (the chord is D♭–F–A♭–B♭). Adding one would change the protected wedge (c463, c473). **Fix:** none.

### 3. The exposition transition 33–46 uses root-position steps, not a 6/3 series (keep; tested on paper)

**Bars.** 34–41: bass A♭–B♭–E♭–E♭–F–B♭–B♭–E♭.

**Evidence.** By principle 1 (PDF 290–291) and the Tchaikovsky practice in Finding 1, a transition is the natural home for a 6/3 series. I asked whether 34–40 should become one, for example a descending chain from the C of 33 toward the V of A♭. I reject this for three reasons. (a) The passage is a rising sequence of the P head (Vln I 35–38, Ob 1/Cl 1/Vla answers), and each stage needs a root to tonicise (E♭ minor at 36–37, B♭ at 39–40). A 6/3 chain would blur exactly the key steps that carry the music from C minor to the V of A♭ at 41. A&S PDF 291–292 say the chords of a 6/3 series do not stand out, which is wrong for a modulating sequence. (b) The reading's own example of a transition (Mendelssohn, Ex. 18-3a) ends on a stressed cadential chord. Here that is the four-horn call over E♭ at 41–43, followed by V4/2–I6 at 44–45: a short 6/3 link of the kind the reading describes, already in place. (c) The development's wedge uses the 6/3 chain. Using it in the exposition transition as well would make the retransition a repeat rather than an intensification. The reading keeps the device for one main job per passage. **Fix:** none.

### 5. Within the lens, no new master-level gap that this reading can support

I looked for a place where the pacing falls short of a master and where 6/3 technique would help. The only one recent critics name is the two-bar climb at 156–157 (c523, c533, c541–c544, c553). It is protected, and every 6/3-based remedy (an ascending 6/3 chain, PDF 293 Ex. 18-6f–j, or a passing v6 to a Phrygian iv6) needs ♭6̂ or a longer bar count before 158. The first spends the A♭ that 160 depends on. The second would change the bar numbers that movements II and IV cite. I do not answer the protection's reason, so I do not reopen it.
