### 3. The C-major-to-minor turn at 166 stays an inner-voice event (candidate considered and rejected)

The reading's strongest statement on two forms of one degree is "dramatic juxtaposition and, even, conflict" (PDF 387). Its sharpest example is Brahms 3/i, where the conflict sits in the outer voices (Ex. 23-13, PDF 395). In movement I the ♮3→♭3 turn at 166 is the last place the movement states its own major/minor conflict before the coda. It is played at p, in the middle register, in four doubled inner voices under Vln I's held C5 (score page 17). I asked whether a master would put it on top, for example Vln I E5→E♭5 over 166, which would make it the Schubert-like ♮3–♭3 line of Ex. 23-4 (PDF 389).

I rejected it without building it, for three reasons:

- **It would break the cadence.** Vln I's D5 (165.4) → C5 (166) is the 2–1 close of the second group's cadence. An E5 on top would turn that perfect cadence into an imperfect one, and it would leave the D5 unresolved in its register.
- **It would spend the finale's moment.** A top-voice E♮→E♭ at 166 would state the thread in the outer voice before the finale closes it in the violas at IV 219–220. The reserve list keeps "closing the E♭/E♮ thread" for that place.
- **The inner-voice form already does the job.** At this p level the four-voice inner unison is already the most audible line under a held top note. The crescendo into 167 f makes the minor arrival audible without a top-voice cross relation.

Keep 166 as it is.

### 4. 162–163 vi–IV after the ♭VI climax: keep the ♮6 release (candidate considered and rejected)

Ex. 23-5 (PDF 390) shows ♭VI opening a minor passage that closes plagally with IV♭–I. Ex. 23-6 (PDF 390–391) has ♭VI lead to a borrowed II6/5 and then to V. In this score, 160 ♭VI → 161 vii°7/V–V7 is followed by vi (A minor, 162) and IV (F major, 163), both with ♮6. Only then does ♭6 come back in the iiø7 at 164. I considered turning 163 into iv (A♭), so that 160–166 would be one unbroken minor-coloured passage, as in Brahms.

I rejected it:

- **The order is already the good one.** 163–165 gives ♮6 (Vln I A5 at 163.4, inner A4) → ♭6 (164) → 5 (164.3 and 165). That is the order the reading calls good (PDF 388, 395). Exercise 3 (PDF 396) asks for exactly this ♮6/♭6 contrast.
- **The change would rewrite the tune.** iv at 163 would change Vln I's A5 at 163.4 and the falling-thirds release 162–166, which is protected (c442 "158–166 cadence chain", c443 "162–166 release", c452 "162–166 falling thirds").
- **The bright vi–IV belongs to the arc.** The fff ♭VI at 160 is a deception, the third A♭ climax. Giving way to diatonic major colour before the soft C-major cadence is the release, and darkening it would blur the p C-major close at 166, which then turns to minor.

### Arc summary for this lens

The mixture plan works at the symphony scale. ♭VI (A♭) is the goal of all three climaxes and the key of movement II. III♯ (E) appears only as a two-beat glimpse at 78.3, for the scherzo. C major is always soft. The minor-to-major resolution, the E♭/E♮ thread's closing and the B→C trumpet resolution of the stop chord are left to the finale. I found nothing in v590 that spends a finale reserve. I also found nothing whose change would make the cyclic design clearer without undoing a protected passage.
