## The three A♭ climaxes

c264 described the three climaxes; I re-checked them against rows 1, 7, 10, 11, 22 and 23.

- **Bar 82.** The P head is ff in three octaves. Since c344 the lowest octave is violas + Bsn 1. The held brass are f, the strokes at 83 ff.
- **Bar 122.** All motion stops on the fff V♭9 over G. The upper octaves are filled to A♭6 and the bass is only G1/G2, Adler's Mahler 1 and Holst models (PDF 164, 181). The fade from 123 goes to violins, oboe and clarinets, which can control it (PDF 164–165).
- **Bar 160.** The tune is in three octaves, each mixing string, woodwind and brass. The countermelody has its own rhythm, the violas have their quavers, and the held brass are ff under the fff tune (PDF 178–179).

The order 82 < 122 < 160 holds, and neither change below touches these bars.

## Findings

### 1. 139–141: the held horns and trumpets are as loud as the tune (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 139 the recapitulation turns to F minor. The tune climbs to A♭5 at 140 in Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I·II, with Fl 1 an octave above (A♭6). The cellos, Bsn 1 and Tbn 2 have a tenor line, and the bass falls F–A♭–D♭. Above the tenor line Hn 1·2 hold A♭4/F4, and Tpt 1–3 hold the close chord A♭4–F4–D♭4 (c324). All five crescendo from f at 139 to ff at 140, the same level as the tune. Tpt 1's A♭4 sits one octave under the tune's goal note. Three trumpets in close position at ff are the strongest sound in the middle of the orchestra, so the oboe and clarinet half of the tune is covered.

c317 found that this held ff chord also covered the tenor line, and fixed that by doubling the line in Tbn 2 instead of softening the chord. Every other tutti in the movement grades the held brass a level below the tune: 27–32, 82 (c304), 131 (c144, c264, c308) and 160 (c244, ff under fff). Bar 140 is the only exception.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 159, rule 1: "the melody notes should be more prominent than the harmony notes." PDF 184 (Schoenberg): principal gestures are marked louder than subordinate ones. PDF 169 (Schubert 8/i): the ear separates foreground from background when they differ in colour and register. Here the held chord lies in the register of the tune's lower octave and of the tenor line, so the dynamic has to make the difference. PDF 164 (Brahms 3/i): heavy wind and brass chords that "neither overwhelm nor overshadow" the violins' tune.

**Earlier decisions.** c144 protected "brass grading 27–32, 139, 167". The grading at 139 is Hn 3·4, trombones and tuba f, with the upper brass rising to ff. The change keeps a graded layout: all held brass now stay f. It extends c144's own rule (Tbn 1·2 f under the theme at 131) to the bar where c144 left an exception. It also answers c283's "dense 139–141 below the 158 arrival": the recapitulation's F-minor turn now stays further below the brass arrival at 158 and the fff at 160. The accents on C–B at 142 (c288) and the ff strokes at 143 are unchanged. No pitches change.

### 2. 80–81: the timpani roll starts f under the head entries (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 80–81 the P head passes in stretto through Bsn, Hn 3·4, Tbn and Vc (80), and then Ob 1, Cl 1, Fl 1 and Vln I (81). Each entry is f and begins after a quaver rest. The background is Vln II and violas, tremolo p with a crescendo, and the timpani roll on C3. The roll starts f on the downbeat of 80 and crescendos into the ff A♭ quavers at 82. So the roll is the only background part already at f, and it sounds before each entry's first note.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184, on Bloch's *Schelomo*: the crescendos are "greatly aided by a timpani roll", and "the composer wisely marks both percussion instruments piano and then crescendo, because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra and completely change the emphasis of this passage, which is meant to develop the themes and combine them for the sake of musical tension". Bars 80–81 are exactly such a passage: developmental imitation building to the first A♭ climax. The movement already follows this rule at its other building rolls (39–40 pp<, 115 mf<, 127–130 pp<, 159 cymbal pp<).

**Fix (applied, `sections/dev.ily`, `devTimp`).** `R1*3 | c1:32\p\< | c1:32 | aes,8\ff …` (was `c1:32\f\<`). The roll now grows with the string tremolo and still reaches the ff at 82. This is not part of the protected "G-pedal timpani roll 117–130", and it does not touch the 77–83 notes or the key plan.

## Considered and kept

- **Tpt 2 fff on the held A♭4 at 160, with a crescendo at 159.** c244 kept the other held brass ff, but Tpt 2 shares Tpt 1's staff and takes its fff. I keep it. On beats 2–4 the A♭4 is the tune's own note in the Hn 1·2 + Vc octave, so it reinforces that octave instead of covering it, which is the *Meistersinger* trumpet "in a register where it cannot disturb" (PDF 172). A part-only ff would also make the part and the score disagree.
- **Counter ff above the low P theme at 131–134** (Fl 1 and Vln I E♭6, Ob 1 and Vln II E♭5). Row 25 would lower it. However, its accented notes are the theme's own long notes E♭–D♭–C–B, two octaves up and syncopated (E♭6 enters with the theme's E♭4 on beat 2, D♭6 with its D♭ on beat 4). It works as the theme's upper octave in another rhythm, not as a separate idea. c264 has already lowered the accompaniment in the theme's register.
- **Held brass ff at 158–159, equal to the ff tune.** The full-brass arrival on the cadential 6/4 is the event of the bar: a Tchaikovsky-type "punctuation" doubling for power (PDF 166–167), protected by c283. The tune's upper octaves are clear above it.
- **Pedal ff at 117–121** (B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, tuba, Cb, Hn 3·4). Adler's Holst passage asks for heavy bass notes to steady the build (PDF 181). The Hn 3·4 octave pedal is protected.
