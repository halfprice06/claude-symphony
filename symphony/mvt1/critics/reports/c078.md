# Critic 078, lens 8 (expression), applied to tempo: v97

## What I studied
Corpus: the printed tempo words in `musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`, extracted bar by bar with a script, and the tempo/fermata layer of the Mutopia Dvořák 9/i (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/*notesA.ily`, `percussion.notesA.ily`). The corpus has no Brahms 1. The `sound tempo` values in the MuseScore files and the `4 = 63 / 136` values in the Mutopia file are editors' playback settings, not printed marks, so I did not use them as metronome evidence. Web: Wikipedia, Symphony No. 6 (Tchaikovsky), which gives the Allegro non troppo as ♩ = 116 and the Andante as ♩ = 69 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._6_(Tchaikovsky)). Kenneth Woods on Brahms 1/i tempi (https://kennethwoods.net/blog1/?p=2908): Un poco sostenuto at about 88–92 and Allegro at ♩. = 80–84, one basic tempo held through the movement with small unmarked expansions; and Brahms's remark, passed on through Primrose, that "dolce implied slower". I could not find Dvořák's own metronome mark for 9/i on the web (a search turned up only reviews and catalogue pages). The v97 score, its MIDI tempo events (read with mido), and pages 4–6, 10–17 of the PDF.

## Findings

### 1. The MIDI realises none of the internal tempo marks or fermatas (bars 45–46, 66–68, 121–126, 145–146, 164–170, 174, 181–182, 186)
Problem: `score.midi` has tempo events only at 1, 15, 47, 69, 147, 171, 175, 183. Text-only `\tempo \markup` produces no MIDI tempo, and LilyPond 2.24 does not lengthen fermatas. So the mp3 plays the post-climax "sostenuto" 123–126 at full Allegro (126), the fff largamente at 164 at 112, every rit. in strict time, and the denied-tutti fermata at 174 as a 0.48-second quarter rest. The audio file is the only rendering Dan hears, and it contradicts the printed plan at the two climaxes and at the coda's main gesture.
Fix (applied): a MIDI-only helper in global.ily that prints nothing:
```
mt = #(define-music-function (bpm) (integer?) #{ \set Score.tempoWholesPerMinute = #(ly:make-moment bpm 4) #})
```
with half-bar steps inside each rit.: 45–46 `\mt 120 s2 \mt 116 s2 \mt 110 s2 \mt 104 s2`; 66–68 110 → 80; allargando 121 `\mt 120 s2 \mt 114 s2`, 122 `\mt 106`; sostenuto 123–126 `\mt 100`; a tempo `\mt 126`; 145–146 as 45–46; largamente 164–168 `\mt 100`; 169–170 94 → 76; 174 beat 4 `s1*3 s2. \mt 40 s4` (rest lasts 1.5 s); 181–182 66 → 54; 186 `\mt 30` (final bar doubled). Total length 429 s → 441 s. No printed output changes; the Tempo_performer reads the property, the Metronome_mark_engraver does not.

### 2. The second-theme groups have no forward motion before their rit./largamente (bars 62–65, 160–163)
Problem: from 47 to 68 (22 bars) and from 147 to 170 (24 bars) the only tempo instruction is the slower tempo itself, followed by a slowing (rit. 66, largamente 164). With ♩ = 112 held through the subito-p crescendo 62–64 and through the crescendo 160–163 into the fff, a conductor has nothing to pull back from, and both groups risk sagging into the slowings. The movement already has 17 printed tempo indications, so this is the only place I would add any.
Evidence: Tchaikovsky 6/i does exactly this inside its second theme: Andante 89, incalzando 93, ritenuto 95, come prima 97 (`Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`), and before the exposition climax: Un poco animando 67, Un poco più animato 73, ritardando molto 84. Dvořák 9/i and (by Woods's account) Brahms 1/i leave such pushes unmarked, so the evidence is split. That is why I propose it at the two places where the next tempo word (rit., largamente) already marks the end, and nowhere else.
Fix (applied): `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco animando"` at 62 (above the subito p, Fl 1 entry) and at 160 (the mf crescendo into K). No "a tempo" is needed: the rit. at 66 and largamente at 164 end it. MIDI 115/118, 120 at 162–163. This answers cycle 39 ("too many tempo changes") and cycle 48 ("tranquillo acts as a tempo word"): those removed slowings and section words; these are two short pushes, each ended by an existing mark, with no new tempo section and no new slow bars. If the composer accepts only one, keep 160 (push into the ♭VI climax, then largamente: Tchaikovsky 67–84).

### 3. Metronome values: consistent and realistic, no change
- Lento ♩ = 50 against Tchaikovsky's Adagio ♩ = 54: same order. Tempo I (Lento) at 183 has the same value as bar 1, which is correct.
- Allegro appassionato ♩ = 126 gives eighths at 252. Woods takes Brahms 1/i at ♩. = 80–84, eighths at 240–252; Tchaikovsky's Allegro non troppo is ♩ = 116. "Appassionato" justifies the slightly faster value.
- Poco meno mosso 112 is 0.89 × 126, a "poco" easing of the kind Woods describes for Brahms's dolce passages. It is not a Tchaikovsky-style new Andante (69/116 = 0.59), which is right for a "poco".
- Più lento ♩ = 72 is 0.57 × 126, almost exactly Tchaikovsky's Andante/Allegro ratio (0.59).
- The three "Tempo dell'Allegro (♩ = 126)" marks repeat the number, which helps the conductor; keep them.

### 4. Every slowing ends clearly, no change
poco rit. 45 → 47 Meno mosso; rit. 66 → 69 Tempo dell'Allegro; allargando 121 → 123 sostenuto → 127 a tempo; poco rit. 145 → 147; largamente 164 → rit. 169 → 171; fermata 174 → 175 Più lento; rit. 181 → 183 Tempo I. No return is ambiguous. "sostenuto" after "allargando" holds the broadened tempo; "a tempo" at 127 can only mean 126 (the last numbered Allegro mark). Tchaikovsky uses the same ritenuto → come prima chain (95–97, 99–101).

### 5. Climax approach 111–122: no stringendo, no change
I tested whether the wedge 111–116 or the G pedal 117–120 needs "poco a poco animando" or "stringendo". The models write none before their development climaxes: Tchaikovsky 6/i has no tempo word between Allegro vivo (161) and the fermata at 304 before "Andante come prima" 305 (the "più" at 209 in the file is "più f", a dynamic), and the Dvořák 9/i Allegro molto has none anywhere in the movement (both corpus files). The pacing is in the notes (wedge, pedal, timpani swell), and the allargando at 121 is the one nuance a conductor needs. Adding a push would also make the arrival at 122 ambiguous (stringendo then allargando within six bars).

### 6. Coda 171–186: no change
Four tempo events in 16 bars (171, 174 fermata, 175, 181/183) is dense, but each has a reason in the table and the descent is protected (cycle 40). The rit. 181–182 falls inside the augmented bass motto, so the motto's last C lands on Tempo I at 183, which is the right place. Do not restore the allargando at 174 (cycle 30: the fermata is enough).

### 7. Bar 14: measured silence, no fermata, no change
Tchaikovsky closes his introduction with ritenuto 17 and a fermata at 18. Here the beat of silence at ♩ = 50 (1.2 s) does the same job, measured, and it was a deliberate cycle-50 choice. A fermata would make the Allegro's attack depend on the conductor; leave it.
