## Findings (ranked)

### 1. P and S pitches: no change is justified. Not applied.
- **What the features show:**
  - All three themes have the most common contour type, the arch (I < M > F).
  - Each theme opens with a minor-sixth leap that is then filled in stepwise. Against a corpus of tunes that move mostly by step, this is an uncommon gradient.
  - That is the combination Jakubowski et al. found in earworm tunes: a common global shape with unusual local gradients.
- **Peaks:**
  - P peaks once, on D♭6 sf at 20, at 0.69 of the phrase. This placement is close to T6 (0.50) and D9/i (0.55).
  - The violins' S peaks once, on D♭6 at 63, at 0.57.
  - Both peaks come after a sequence that raises the expectation. In P, the basic idea is repeated a step lower at 17 and then rises at 19. In S, the head is sequenced up a third at 60 before 62–63, which is the same procedure Tchaikovsky uses at 93 after 89–92.
- **Rhythmic repetition:** P repeats its `r8 X8 Y4.` cell at 15, 17 and 19. This is the within-theme repetition that Müllensiefen and Halpern associate with correct recognition.
- **Any substitute would be worse:**
  - Changing one P pitch would break about 30 statements, including the fugato subject at 85–96 and the coda brass at 167–170.
  - Changing one pitch in the S head would break nine statements: 47, 56, 69, 97, 103, 107, 147, 156 and 171.
- **Conclusion:** neither theme has a measurable defect that a one-note change would fix.

### 2. The first S statement (46–55) has little rhythmic repetition inside the theme. This is offset by what follows. Not applied.
- **The figures:**
  - 1 of 10 bars repeats an earlier bar's rhythm (51 repeats 48). The models have 0.33–0.50.
  - Its range of 26 semitones is the widest of any theme measured.
- **Why this is acceptable:**
  - The head (`2.~ 8 8 | 4 2 4`) is the part a listener retains, and it returns in nine pitched statements plus the wedge at 111.
  - D9/i has 3 such statements, and T6/i has 3 (89, 130, and the recapitulation).
  - The order is also the models' order: first a dolce statement in a middle register, then at once the violins an octave higher. Compare D9/i, where the flute plays 149–156 and the violins take it an octave and more higher at 157–164, and T6/i 89 then 130.
  - The movement's risk is saturation (cycle 31 counted 45 rising sixths in Vln I), not under-exposure.
- **Note for movements II and III:** new themes should repeat their opening cell within two bars, as D9/ii does at 7 and 9 and T6/i at 89 and 91.

### 3. Scoring of the first clear statements: keep. Not applied.
- **S at 47:** Hn 1 (mp, dolce) and Vc (mf, dolce) in unison, over harp p and strings pp.
- **Comparable models:**
  - T6/i 89 doubles Vln I and Vc in octaves over the horns.
  - D9/i 149 gives the theme to a solo flute over held strings.
  - Brahms 3/iii opens with the cellos in the tenor register.
  - Rachmaninoff 2/iii opens with a clarinet solo.
- In each model, a single blended colour sits in the middle register, the accompaniment is held, and the theme is not doubled at the octave until the restatement. 47–55 already does this.
- **P at 15:** played by the strings alone at f, with the accompaniment at mf. This is the clearest first hearing in the movement.
- **Motto at 1–4:** BCl and Vc at pp in a low register. This matches the low pp bassoon at the opening of T6/i, and cycle 50 protects it.

### 4. Bass under 47–55: keep it plain and reject critic 89's alternatives. Not applied.
- **Bar 49:**
  - Critic 89 proposed `des,` for 49, which makes the chord ii6, not iii.
  - Bar 48 already has D♭ in the bass (IV), so the bass would stand on D♭ for two bars (48–49) and lose the falling third D♭–B♭ that leads to E♭ at 50.
  - The cello melody also begins 49 on `des'`, so the melody's downbeat would double the bass at the moment the tune needs the bass to move away from it.
- **Bar 51:**
  - The bass is already inverted: Cb `c,` and harp `c,1` under `c8 ges aes c' es'`, which is A♭7 in first inversion (V7/IV) resolving to D♭ at 52.
  - The proposed `ges,2 c,2` would turn a 4/2 chord back into a 6/5 chord instead of resolving the G♭ down to F. It would also add a second pizzicato attack to a pattern that is otherwise one note per bar.
- **Memorability reason for keeping it plain:** a listener learns a tune first against a predictable bass. The recapitulation's changed harmony (148–149, C–C♯–D, cycle 82) can only be heard as a variant because the exposition stayed plain. Cycle 82's reasoning stands.

### 5. A pickup into the codetta's S head at 68 beat 4: considered and not recommended. Not applied.
- **The idea:** the codetta (69) is the loudest S statement in the exposition but starts on `c'''` with no rising sixth. Five of the nine statements have the E♭–C pickup.
- **Against it:**
  - Adding `es''4` on beat 4 of 68 in Vln I and Fl 1 would put the anacrusis at the slowest point of the rit. (♩≈88, global.ily line 24), right before the jump to ♩=126.
  - It would change the one-bar suffix and the deceptive cadence protected by cycles 33 and 89.
  - The sixth is still heard in bar 2 of the codetta (`aes''4 f'''2`).
