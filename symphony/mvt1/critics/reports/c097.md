# Critic 097 — lens 7 (texture): the frame, introduction 1–14 and coda 167–182

## What I studied
Score v116 (sections/intro.ily, sections/coda.ily, score.pdf pp. 1 and 17, analysis.txt). I listed every sounding pitch in bars 1–14 and 167–182 and checked the gap between each pair of adjacent voices.

I could not reach a text of Adler's chapter on spacing, so I have not cited it.

The rules applied below are Rimsky-Korsakov's:
- "The bass should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it."
- The natural harmonic series "may serve as a guide to the orchestral arrangement of chords", with "the widely-spaced intervals [lying] in the lower part".
- Wide gaps are tolerated only in *piano* passages, and even then rarely.

## The frame as a pair (no change needed)
The two ends rhyme in register.
- Bars 1–4 put the motto in octaves at G1/G2 (Cb, Vc, B.cl) under a timpani C3 roll. That is one real line plus a pedal.
- Bars 175–179 restate the motto in augmentation at exactly the same G1/G2 octave (Cb, Vc, B.cl, B.tbn). The trombones' chorale and the strings sit above it in close spacing between E♭3 and C5, which makes about 6 real lines.

The intro thickens from 3 parts (1–4) to 5 (6–8) and 12 (11–12), then thins to 6 (13–14). The coda does the reverse: 20 parts (167–170), then 6 (171–174), 9 (175–179), 5 (180) and 7 (181–182).

The coda's top voice never goes above E♭5 after 175, and its last sonority lies between C2 and C5. This matches the models:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 347–354 ends between B1 and D♯4 (Hn, Tbn, Tuba, Timp: B1/B2–F♯3–B3–D♯4).
- Coriolan 303–314 ends with nothing above C4.
- Dvořák 7/i 311–314 ends between D2 and D4.

The movement's shape is right. The faults are local spacing holes, all in places where an instrument stops or is missing.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 180–182: a tenth gap when the chorale stops, and no fifth above the bass octave in the final chord (applied)
**Problem.** At 179 the trombones and bass clarinet stop. On beats 1–2 of 180 the voices are C2 (Cb), C3 (Vc, timp), then nothing until E♭4 (Vla), G4 and C5. That is a gap of a tenth in the tenor register.

In 181–182 the horns enter at C4 and G4. The G4 doubles Vln II at the unison, and the octave C3–C4 stays empty: the final chord is C2–C3–C4–E4–G4–C5. The harp's G2 is plucked and dies away.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov p. 67 (above): no part should lie more than an octave from the part directly above it.
- The harmonic series puts the fifth (partial 3) directly above the bass octave.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 351–354 ends B1/B2–F♯3–B3–D♯4, with the fifth sustained in the bass trombone right above the octave.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 327–334 holds B2–D♯3–F♯3–B3–D♯4–F♯4.
- Dvořák 9/i 450–452 is laid out E2–E3–G3–B3–E4.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
- Horn 3 enters one bar earlier, on C4, and holds to the end.
- Horn 4 moves from C4 to G3.

```
codaHnThree: ... R1*5 | c'1\ppp~^\markup \small "3." | c'1~ | c'1\fermata |
codaHnFour:  ... R1*6 | g1\ppp~ | g1\fermata |
```

**Result.**
- Bar 180 has C4 between C3 and the viola. On beat 3 the C4 is the tonic pedal under D♭/C.
- 181 is C2–C3–G3–C4–E♭4–G4–C5, and 182 is C2–(G2)–C3–G3–C4–E4–G4–C5. Both are laid out in harmonic-series order.
- The violas' E♮ now has one voice above it (Vln II G4) instead of a horn and violins on the same G4, so the turn to major is less covered.
- No pitch class changes. The horns still enter ppp, the tempo map and length are unchanged, and the viola E♭→E♮ is untouched.

**Protection.** This touches the "coda 171–182" list. The reasons recorded for that protection are the motto, the chorale, the viola E♮ and the tempo descent, and this change leaves all of them alone. It moves only filler notes.

### 2. Bar 174: a gap of an octave and a sixth under the cadential 6/4 (applied)
**Problem.** The double basses rest in 171–174, so the bass is Bsn 2 (G2) with the timpani roll on G2. The next voice up is the violas at E♭4/D4, an octave and a sixth higher. Only at 175 do the bass clarinet, the trombones and the motto fill this space.

**Evidence.** Same Rimsky rule. Tchaikovsky 6/i 4–6 (B1–A2–B2–D♯3–G3) shows that even at pp the next voice stays within an octave of the bass.

**Fix (applied).** Bsn 1 plays G3 for one bar. The G3 passes straight to Tbn 1's G3 at 175, so the dominant pedal is bound into 174/175 (a protected join, which this strengthens).

```
codaBsnOne: ... | R1 | R1 | R1 | g1\pp |
```

Bsn 2 is already at pp, so the dynamics on the shared staff agree.

### 3. Bars 6–7 (introduction): the double basses lie two octaves below the next voice (applied)
**Problem.** The basses are on G1 and F1. The next voice up is the cellos on G3 and A♭3. The low G1 and F1 sound undoubled in the instrument's bottom fourth, and the space between is empty.

The coda's version of this register (175) has the same G1 doubled at G2 by three instruments. The intro's version is thinner than its rhyme.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov p. 64: extra parts are octave doublings, "the bass being doubled in the lower octave only".
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 1–3 opens with divided basses in E2/B2, in close spacing.

**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** Divide the basses in octaves for two bars.

```
introCb: c,1\pp | <g,, g,>1^"div." | <f,, f,>1\< | c,1\p\>^"unis." |
```

The cellos' tenor line and the cor anglais (protected 5–8) are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 171–173 (verified, no change)
**What I checked.** The cello melody sits above the violas, with only Bsn 2 below it.

**Result.** The spacing is C3 / C4–B3 / E♭4 / G4, so every gap is an octave or less. It is correct.

### 5. Bars 175–179 doubling (verified, no change)
**What I checked.** In soft chords Rimsky-Korsakov prefers doubling the root and bass. The motto doubles the bass in four instruments at G1/G2, E♭2/E♭3 and so on. The thirds (E♭3 Tbn 2 at 175 and 179, F3 at 177) are single.

**Result.** The doubled E♭ at 176 is the bass of a first-inversion minor chord, which is acceptable. No change.
