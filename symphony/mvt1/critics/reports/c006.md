# Critic 6 (lens 6: playability), v25

## What I studied
I compiled a copy of v25 with one MIDI track per player and no part-combining (in /tmp/critic-006). From it I extracted every note with bar, beat, sounding duration in seconds (using the tempo map) and velocity, for all 30 parts. Scripts measured ranges, the longest stretches without any rest, string double stops, slur lengths, harp pitch classes per half bar (for pedal changes) and timpani tunings. I read the source .ily files and rendered PDF pages 5–6.

Corpus comparisons used music21 on `musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1`: the longest runs without a rest per part, the highest horn notes, and Tchaikovsky's tied tuba and bassoon pedals.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Double basses stay pizzicato from bar 85 to the end of the development (85–122)
- **Problem:** `devCb` has `d,4\mf^"pizz."` at bar 85, and no "arco" follows anywhere in the movement. Everything after it would be plucked: the half notes from 90, the ff whole notes at 97–102, the chromatic descent at 103–108 and the ten-bar tied G pedal at 109–122 (ff–fff–pp). This is the most serious error in the score, because a section that follows the part plays nothing audible under the climax. The PDF shows "pizz." at bar 85 (page 5) and no "arco" on page 6 or later.
- **Fix** (bar 90 of `devCb`):
```
aes,2\mf^"arco" g,2 | f,1 | ...
```

### 2. Single wind and brass players hold 40–65 seconds with no rest
These parts never rest, not even for an eighth:

| Part | Bars | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|

| Bassoon 2 | 139–166 | about 65 s | through the fff at 156 |

| Bass clarinet, contrabassoon, bassoon 2 | 97–118 | about 42 s | ending in a ten-bar tied G at ff–fff |

| Clarinet 1 | 96–118 | about 44 s | triplet arpeggios, then f'' held ff |
| Horns 3 and 4 | 56–73 | about 39 s and 48 s | |

- **Corpus comparison:**
  - In Tchaikovsky 6/i the longest horn run is 54 quarters (Corni III–IV, 287–300) and the longest tuba run is 32 quarters (189–197). His tuba changes pitch at 194 and stops at 197.
  - In Dvořák 9/i no horn part goes more than 25 quarters without a rest (396–408).
  - Tchaikovsky's long bassoon F♯ (284–297) is written for two players in unison, so they can stagger breaths. Here each line belongs to one player, so nobody can cover for anyone.

- **Fix:** stagger written breaths so the pedal never empties.
  - Dominant pedal, 109–118 (bass clarinet and bassoon 2 follow the same pattern, breathing at other bars):
```
devTuba:    g,,1\ff~ | g,,1~ | g,,2. r4 | g,,1~ | g,,1\fff~ | g,,2. r4 | g,,1\>~ | g,,1~ | g,,2. r4 | g,,1\pp

devHnFour:  breathe at the end of 104, 107, 111 and 115 (g2. r4); rest in 127–130 (the low brass have the line)
```

### 3. Horn 1 reaches written high C (concert f'') twice in an exposed lyrical line (52–53 and 144–145)
- **Problem:** `tTwoHnOne` leaps an octave, f'4 to f''4, ties the high note over the bar line at mp dolce, and doubles only the cellos. The recapitulation repeats it at mf. Concert F5 is written c''' for horn in F, the practical ceiling of the first horn.
- **Evidence:** Tchaikovsky 6/i horns never sound above C5 (bar 73); Dvořák 9/i tops out at C♯5 (bar 224). Timbre & Orchestration: the repertoire "rarely exceeds high C", and the note should not be sustained.
- **Fix:** horn 1 leaves before the peak and clarinet 1 takes the high phrase with the cellos.
```
tTwoHnOne bar 52–53:  aes'4.( g'8 f'2) | R1 |
tTwoClOne bar 52–54:  r2. f''4~(\mp | f''4 es''8 des''8 c''4. bes'8 | aes'4 g'4 bes'4 des'4) |
```
  Do the same at 144–145 in C major (horn `c''4.( b'8 a'2) | R1`).

### 4. Harp pedal changes at 56–67: dense, unmarked, and two of them on a string that is still ringing
- **Problem:** at ♩=100 the left-hand arpeggios need eight pedal changes in twelve bars:

| Bar | Change |
|---|---|
| 60 | G♭ |
| 61 | F♭ |
| 63 (downbeat) | G♮ |
| 63, beat 3 | E♭→E♮ |
| 64 | F♮ |
| 64, beat 3 | D♭→D♮ |
| 65, downbeat (1.2 s later) | D♮→D♭ |
| 67 | E♭ |

  At 56 beat 3 and 63 beat 3 the E♭ plucked two beats earlier is still sounding when the E pedal moves, which produces an audible buzz. No pedal settings are printed.
- **Evidence:** Gramophone allows at least one second per change and warns of the "clack" when changes are rushed. Timbre & Orchestration calls frequent pedal changes "the number one harp pitfall".
- **Fix:**
  - Respell bar 63, second half, using the F♭ already set at 61: `fes,8( bes, des g)`.
  - Leave the D♮ to the cellos in bar 64: `f,8( c f aes) f,8( aes, c f)`.
  - Print the initial setting at 47 and at 148 with `\markup \harp-pedal "^-v-|vv--"` (D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭), and in C major at 148.
  - The recapitulation (148–162) is fine. It has four bars of rest (154–157) for B♭→B and A♭→A.

### 5. Sustained string double stops with no divisi (47–54, 103–108, 116–118)
- **Problem:** violas hold pp whole-note thirds for eight bars at 47–54. B♭3+D♭4 (49, 50, 53), A♭3+D♭4 (52) and G3+B♭3 (54) need the C string in fourth position under the G string. That stretch is awkward to tune at pp. Violins II hold thirds and fourths at 103–108 and cellos at 116–118. The score has no "div." or "unis." anywhere.
- **Evidence:** Hugill, *Orchestra Manual*: a section will normally divide chords, and awkward double stops are played divisi. Divisi is the norm for soft sustained chords.
- **Fix:** add `^"div."` at 47 (violas), 103 (violins II) and 116 (cellos), and `^"unis."` at 55, 109 and 119. The quick repeated thirds at 69–72, 123–126 and 131–134 can stay as double stops.

### 6. Slurs that one bow cannot play (55–68, 173–178, 1–4)
- **Problem:**
  - `tTwoVlnOne` and `tTwoVlnTwo` put one slur over fourteen bars (55–68, about 34 s).
  - `codaVlnOne` slurs 173 to the fermata in 178: pp, high on the E string, about 25 s plus the fermata.
  - The intro motto in cellos and basses (1–4) is one slur of 19 s at pp.
- **Evidence:** in string parts a slur is read as a bowing (Gould, *Behind Bars*, string chapter). Corpus string slurs in Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i run one or two bars each.
- **Fix:** bowing slurs of one or two bars, with an optional outer `\(` … `\)` phrasing slur. For example:
```
c''2.~(\p c''8 bes'8 | aes'4 f''2 es''4) | des''4.( c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''2 g''2) | ...
```
  Coda: `g'4( | es''1~ | es''2.) d''4( | c''2 des''2) | c''1~( | c''1\fermata)`.

### 7. Timpani and percussion: no tuning indications, an undeclared fifth tuning, and one player asked to do two things at once (97, 114)
- **Timpani:** the part has no opening tuning and no change marks. The B♮ at 97 is outside the declared set (G, A♭, B♭/C, E♭). The retuning times themselves are fine:

| Change | Between | Time available |
|---|---|---|
| C→B♭ | 33 and 40 | 13 s |
| B♭→C | 41 and 80 | ample |
| C→B | 84 and 97 | 23 s |
| B→C | 97 and 123 | ample |

  Drum sizes fit: G on the 32", A♭ on the 29", B♭/B/C on the 26", E♭ on the 23".
- **Fix:** at bar 1 add `^\markup "G, A♭, C, E♭"`; at 34 `^"muta C in B♭"`; at 42 `^"B♭ in C"`; at 85 `^"C in B"`; at 98 `^"B in C"`.
- **Percussion:** at 114, `<cymc tt>` asks for clash cymbals and tam-tam at the same moment. Clash cymbals need both hands, so this takes two players, which the plan provides. Rename the staff "Percussion (2 players)" and mark "Piatti" and "Tam-tam" on the stroke.

Take `bes,,` an octave higher (`bes,`); contrabassoon and bassoon 2 already double the low octave. All other ranges are within practical orchestral limits.
