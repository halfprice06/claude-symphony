## Findings

The score follows Units 16 and 19 nearly everywhere. Every 6/4 chord in the movement has a function the unit names. My main finding answers the open question with no change. The other findings settle two existing questions from the reading and propose no edits.

### F1. Development (77–130): do not add a held key area. Rank 1. No edit.

**The question.** c113 found that no surface key lasts longer than 6 bars. In the corpus, one key holds for 20–45 bars.

**What the corpus actually holds.** I read the Roman numerals inside Tchaikovsky 6/i's 45-bar "D minor" (160.5–205.5):
- It tonicizes v (174), ii (178) and VI (168), and returns to i in between (170, 201–202).
- It closes with an expanded i6/4 (193.5–196.5) and V7 (204.5).

That is a key held by Unit 16's means: one governing chord expanded by its upper 5th and upper 3rd and by neighbour chords (PDF 253–254, 257–258). The key is held because the stations are degrees of one tonic, framed by its return.

**What this development holds.** By the same reading, the development holds one harmony across its stations:
- **Large scale.** The movement's bass is C (15) – A♭ (47–82, prepared by its V from 41) – F (111) – G (117–130) – C (131). This is the I–VI–IV pattern of descending 3rds, which "arpeggiates the goal chord" (PDF 251), followed by V. A♭ is held for 36 bars: S, the codetta, the flute motto over the A♭ pedal, and the A♭ bass at 77–78 and 82.
- **The stations.** The fugato's G–C–F–B♭ are v–i–iv–VII of C minor in a descending-5ths chain, which is "strongly goal-oriented" (PDF 249–250). B minor (97–102) is the upper 3rd of V (PDF 253–254: "VII as upper 3rd of V"). D–E–F–G (103–117) is a rising stepwise line, "an expanded passing motion" whose inner chords are tonics "in name only" (PDF 250–251, 256–257).
- **The held dominant.** V is reached at 83 and held on the surface for 14 bars (117–130, 29.2 s) by neighbouring and accented 6/4s (F3).

So the development already has a plateau: the dominant pedal, with its own "cadence" at 131. It has the 12–20-bar size c113 asked for (14 bars). The difference from Tchaikovsky is that the plateau is a prolonged V, not a key.

**Why a 12–20-bar key would hurt here.**
1. **Proportion (c073, c083).** In the v142 tempo map the development is 105.3 s and the exposition 123.8 s (ratio 0.85). Adding 12 bars at ♩=126 adds 22.9 s: ratio 1.04, development share 28.3%. Adding 20 bars adds 38.1 s: ratio 1.16, share 30.6%. Tchaikovsky's ratio is 0.58 and Dvořák's 0.63; only Brahms 1/i reaches about 1.0 (c083 table). The development climax (122) would move from 0.643 to 0.66–0.67 of the total. c073's reason ("any further bars push the development past the Romantic models") still holds, and nothing in the reading weakens it.
2. **Where it could go.** Each candidate place gives a station a structural weight Unit 16 says it should not carry:
   - **B minor after 102, with a cadence.** This turns a chromatic upper 3rd of V into a pillar. It also replaces the evaded cadence 102→103 (protected, cycles 70/72/113), the one sharp seam the development has.
   - **One key through 103–110.** This breaks the rising passing line D–E–F–G that leads to V (protected, cycle 72). PDF 257 says a long-range passing motion needs subordinate chords between its main ones, not a halt.
   - **The fugato in one key.** This removes the descending-5ths chain, the development's most goal-directed span (PDF 249–250), and undoes the protected 3-bar entries.
   - **A G-minor plateau** (minor v as a pillar, PDF 259 §12). It would come right before the G dominant pedal, giving two G areas back to back. The only way to separate them would mean cutting 117–130.
3. **Surface stability already arrives on schedule.** The unstable stretch 85–116 lasts 60.9 s. The fugato chain (22.9 s) and the rising line (26.6 s) are directed motions, not drift. The pedal follows at the place c073 and c083 judged right.

**Verdict.** I reject the addition for movement I. I keep c113's note for later movements and sharpen it with PDF 259 §12: a sonata movement II or III should make minor v (or another diatonic degree) a held key area of 12–20 bars, framed by its own tonic and closed by an expanded cadential 6/4. That is the method of Tchaikovsky 160–205, which this symphony has not yet used.

### F2. Bars 101–102: the 6/4 after V7 is a neighbouring 6/4 over a sustained dominant. Closes c122 F5. No edit.

c122 read 102.1 as a cadential 6/4 approached from V (Unit 10 rules). Unit 19 classifies it differently. The beat-by-beat content over the F♯ pedal is:
- 101: V7, with 4–3 (B→A♯);
- 102 beats 1–2: B–D–F♯ (6/4);
- 102 beats 3–4: V7 again, with A♯ (Bsn 1 `fis ais cis'` after transposition) and E;
- 103: D minor, where E moves on.

The upper voices make 3–4–3 (A♯–B–A♯), 5–6–5 (C♯–D–C♯, the tune's D–C♯ on beat 4) and 7–6–7 (E–D–E). This is the neighbouring 6/4 of PDF 308: two voices in parallel move above a stationary bass, and the 6/4 sits on the weaker bar after the 5/3 it decorates. It also matches PDF 311–313 (Ex. 19-14, Mozart's *Mi tradì*: V7–6/4–V over a held dominant, with the 7th resolving after the dominant). c122's rule ("do not approach the cadential 6/4 from V") does not apply, because this 6/4 does not stand for a cadence. The protected brass counter 97–102 is correct as written.

### F3. The dominant pedal 117–130 is a textbook Unit 19 prolongation. No edit.

The pedal alternates the two dissonant 6/4 types:
- **Neighbouring 6/4 on a weak bar** at 118, after V9 at 117 (PDF 308).
- **Accented 6/4 on the first bar of the allargando group** at 121, delaying the fff V9 at 122. The book says an accented 6/4 exists "to delay the arrival of an expected… harmonic event" (PDF 306). The same pattern recurs at 125→126.

Together these give the 8-7-6-5 over 6-5-4-3 motion of Points 8 (PDF 323).

At 130 the motto lands on C and E♭ over G. Its bass arrives at 131. This is the "retrospectively stabilized" 6/4 of PDF 319–320 (Ex. 19-26), a 5/3 "whose bass is delayed". It gives a second, independent reason for c113's decision not to add a seventh or B♮ at 129–130. Unit 16's other option, III as a divider before the reprise (PDF 252), is not needed.

The 6/4 count for the whole movement backs the book's closing note (PDF 323). Every 6/4 has a function:
- **neighbouring:** 57, 76, 102, 118, 168–169;
- **accented or cadential:** 13, 62, 110, 121, 125, 144, 154, 158, 174;
- **passing:** 95, 108;
- **consonant or retrospective:** 72, 130, 175.

### F4. The ♭VI upper-neighbour thread and the minor-mode degrees. No edit.

- **VI over V.** Unit 16 names VI as "the most important" long-span upper neighbour to V (PDF 258, Points 10). The score uses it three times at structural points: 82→83 (the development's i–VI–V7♭9), A♭ over the G pedal at 122, and 159→160 (V7–♭VI fff). All three are protected, and the reading supports each one.
- **Major V and the augmented 2nd.** Major V is used at every half and deceptive cadence (PDF 259 §11). There is no stepwise 8–7–6 with B♮–A♭, so PDF 259 §12's augmented-2nd warning is met.
