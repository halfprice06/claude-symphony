## Findings

### 1. Bars 173–174: the coda's Neapolitan resolves its ♭2̂ before V. Hold the cor anglais's D♭5 for two beats (applied in scratch)

**What the score does.** 171–174 is the coda's last cadence: i – iv – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V, with the cor anglais's motto (G4 | E♭5 … D5 | C5 D♭5 C5 | C5) on top of the solo cello's minor S head. At 173.3 the N6 is complete (Bsn 2 F3, Vla A♭3, Vc D♭4, C.a. D♭5). On 173.4 the C.a. already falls to C5 while the cello goes F4–A♭4 over the same F: so 173.4 is iv again, and the N6 is a one-beat neighbour inside iv. ♭2̂ reaches 1̂ over iv, a beat before the cadential 6/4.

**What the reading says.** ♭2̂ "cannot", in normal circumstances, move to 1̂ as a goal before V; a 1̂ that comes over a cadential 6/4 is a passing tone, and the delay is the source of the chord's intensity (PDF 483). The standard cadential shape is Ex. 28-2c: ♭II6 – V6/4–5/3 with ♭2̂–1̂–♯7̂ on top (PDF 484; Points for Review 2–3, PDF 499). Ex. 28-1 (Mozart) and Ex. 28-8 (the Requiem's ♭II6 – "I" – V6/4–5/3, PDF 486) show the same thing: ♭2̂ is held on the chord, and the C comes on the 6/4. Bar 173 is the only one of movement I's three structural N6 cadences that does not do this: 21 has the direct diminished third (Ex. 28-2a) and 141–142 the filled one through an applied chord and 6/4 (Ex. 28-2e).

**Fix (applied in scratch, sections/coda.ily, `codaCorAnglais`):** `d''4 c''4 des''4 c''4~ | c''2) r2` → `d''4 c''4 des''2 | c''2) r2`. No other part changes. Result:
- 173.3–4 is N6 for two beats, ♭2̂ on top, and the cello's F4–A♭4 now arpeggiates the N6 under it (the figuration "touches on all three chord tones", PDF 483).
- D♭5 falls to C5 on the downbeat of 174, over the cadential 6/4. The solo cello arrives on the same C5 and takes it to B4 at 174.3, so D♭5–C5–B4 is the filled diminished third of Ex. 28-2c, in one register.
- The motto's D♭ is again a minim falling to C on a downbeat. That is how it goes at 3 (`c2 des2 | c1`), in the bass at 178, and in Vln I at 180. Bar 173 was the only statement in the movement where the D♭ fell to C in mid-bar.

**Protection.** The coda's notes and timing are protected (c228, c249, c251), 171–174 in particular (c337 duet, c358 C.a.). c300 kept this bar because the C.a. keeps "the motto's fixed rhythm (D♭ on beat 3, C on beat 4), as at 3 and 178". That is not what the score has: at 3, 178 and 180 the D♭ lasts two beats and C comes on the next downbeat. The fix brings 173 into line with the reason c300 gave. Bar length, tempo, dynamics, the cello solo, and the unresolved B4 at 174 (c270) are unchanged. c337 and c358 protected texture and dynamics, not this note value.

**Cyclic effect.** The finale's thread says it completes "the coda's N6 – cadential 6/4 – V" (finale.md, ♭II thread), and the scherzo's 331 is recorded as "movement I reaches V through its N6 at I 173". With the fix the coda's N6 is a real chord with its ♭2̂ held into the 6/4. Before, it was a one-beat inflection of iv. So the progression the finale completes in major is now stated in full in minor.
