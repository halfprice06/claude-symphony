## Findings

### F1. The low motto's last C is cut off, not faded, at 4 and 179 (intro, coda). Applied.

**What is wrong.** The motto in the bass clarinet and cellos (with the basses an octave lower) ends on a held C3: tied from beat 4 of 3 through bar 4, and in the augmented chorale form a whole note at 179. Both times the bass clarinet stops at the barline at pp with no shading.
- Bar 4: the B.cl and cello hairpin `des4\> c4~ | c1\!` closes on the downbeat of 4, so the C is held flat for a whole Lento bar (4.8 s at crotchet = 50) and released. The timpani's ppp C3 roll stops at the same barline. At 5 only the basses' C2 remains. The C3 vanishes in one step, two instruments at once.
- Bar 179: B.cl (C3) and bass trombone (C3) end the chorale's motto bass `c1)` at pp and stop, as the timpani pulse begins and the cellos and basses hold C on. Nothing tells them how to leave.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Clarinets specialise in diminuendo effects and are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)." The same page: a diminuendo is made by eliminating instruments, brass and wood-wind before strings, "combined with" natural dynamic means. Both places already follow the order (wind leave, low strings stay); they lack the natural means. The score does this correctly elsewhere: Cl 1's unresolved B4 at 177 fades (c228), Cl 1 at 45–46 (c298), and the strings' last bars are > ppp. Support: Tchaikovsky 6/i, the slow introduction (corpus `musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly`, Fagotti bars 5–6, 11–12, 15–16; Basso bars 5–6, 11–12, 17): every held note that ends a phrase of the low motto carries a diminuendo to its release.

**Fix** (hairpins only; notes, slurs, the cello "change bow at will" and all levels unchanged):
- intro.ily, introBassCl: `c2(\< des4\> c4~ | c1) |` then `R1\! | R1*9 |` (was `c1\!) | R1*10 |`).
- intro.ily, introVc: `des4\>\stopTextSpan c4)~ | c1 |` then `R1\! | g1\pp |` (was `c1\! | R1 |`). The basses keep their hairpin ending on 4 and hold C2 at 5, so the tonic passes down to them.
- coda.ily, codaBassCl and codaBassTbn: `c1\>) | R1\! | R1 |` (was `c1) | R1*2 |`).
- The trombones 1·2 share a staff and Tbn 1 restrikes at 180, so Tbn 2 is left as it is (a hairpin on one player only would break the shared-staff rule).

Protection: the coda's "notes, levels and timing" are protected (c228, c249, c258, c278). This change keeps every note, the pp level and the timing and only shapes two releases, as c228 did for Cl 1 at 177 under the same protection. The intro's "scoring of 1–4" (c250) is unchanged.

### F2. The fff suspended-cymbal stroke at 160 rings unshaded through the diminuendo of 162–163 (recTwo). Applied.

**What is wrong.** At 160 the percussionist strikes the suspended cymbal fff with soft sticks, tied as a written note through 163 and damped at 164 (c251, c276). The only mark is fff. Around it the orchestra falls away: the brass go ff > mp at 162, then rest at 163; strings and woodwind go from ff at 162 to mp at 164. At largamente (MIDI crotchet = 100) the stroke is 7–10 s old by 163–164. A soft-stick fff stroke on a large suspended cymbal blooms after the attack and is still loud after that time. So the cymbal's tail sits at its own level under the diminuendo, then stops at the damp.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 135: cymbals and gong "played fortissimo" are "sufficient to overpower any orchestral tutti"; PDF 130: a prolonged diminuendo is made with natural shading as well as elimination. Here every part except the cymbal is shaded. The score already treats the other long metal stroke this way: the fff tam-tam at 122 has "damp gradually" and > to p before its damp (c228, Rimsky PDF 130, 135). Adler (adler2 PDF 70: suspended cymbal with soft mallets; PDF 71: "one must be careful ... to specify exactly how long the sound should last") supports writing how the sound ends, not only where. c276 wrote the length "as the tam-tam at 122–127"; this finishes that parallel.

**Fix** (recTwo.ily, recTwoPerc; stroke, beater, written length and the damp at 164 unchanged):
```
cymr1->\fff~ | cymr1~ | cymr1~\>_\markup \small \italic "damp gradually" | cymr1\p | R1^\markup \small \italic "damp" |
```
The fade starts at 162, with the brass's ff >, and reaches p at 163 as the strings pass f, so the cymbal is under the strings when they reach mp. The protected climax scoring 158–166 (c381, c389, c407) keeps its instruments; only the shading of one tie is added.
