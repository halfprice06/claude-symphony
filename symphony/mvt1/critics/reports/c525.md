# Critic 525: Engraving and notation (Gould ch. 1: beams, PDF 31-41, fourth pass)

Version reviewed: v525. Scratch copy: /tmp/critic-525/work.

Method. I read PDF 31-41 of *Behind Bars* in full and rendered every page as an image (110 dpi), because the OCR loses all the examples. I then added a probe to a second scratch copy (/tmp/critic-525/probe). It overrides `Beam.stencil` in the score's Voice context and logs every one of the 502 beams in the score after line breaking: the source line, bar and beat, the final beam positions, the beam direction, each stem's direction, the staff position of every notehead, the stem x positions and the number of beams. Each Gould rule below was checked against that log by script, and the cases the scripts flagged were checked by eye on 300 dpi crops of the PDF. This is the fourth pass of this reading. The beam-angle callback in global.ily (c235, c325, c415) already covers most of it, so I gave most of my attention to the rules on PDF 38-40 (stem direction, visual considerations, divided beams, kneed beams), which earlier passes had checked only for the cellos at 56-68.

## Findings

### 1. Violas 27-30, 88-89, 139, 167-169: beamed groups in the P head flip stem direction from beat to beat (applied)

**Bars.** In 27-30 the violas play the P head (the hammered tutti the finale quotes) in rhythmic unison with Vln II, a sixth or an octave lower. Vla 27.3 D4-D♭4-C4 has down-stems. The pair B3-C4 at 28.2 has up-stems. 29.3 E♭4-D4-C4 has down-stems again, and the staccato group B♭3-C4-D♭4-B♭3 at 30.1 has up-stems. So the slur on the pair at 28 hangs under the notes while the up-bow sign is above them, and the staccato dots at 30 go below the stave. In Vln II, the same figures one stave higher (B4-C5 at 28, B♭4-C5-D♭5-B♭4 at 30) have down-stems, with the slur, bow mark and dots above. The coda repeats 27-29 at 167-169 with the same flips. The fugato's viola entry at 88-89 has the up-stem pair at 89. At 139 the first group, C4-C4-B3-C4, has up-stems, and the seven groups after it (139.3-142) all have down-stems.

**Evidence.** Each flipped group follows the bare count rule on Gould PDF 38: three of its notes are on or below the middle line. But every group in these passages lies within a third of the middle line (staff positions -1 to +2), and together they span the middle of the stave. Gould PDF 39 ("visual considerations") covers exactly this case: "When groups within a passage together roughly span the width of the stave, visual considerations may suggest maintaining the same stem direction for the whole passage. In the following example, this allows all slurs and harmonic indications to remain next to their noteheads." The same page adds that "the context may determine the direction. For example, notes adjacent to the group in question may take stems in one particular direction," and the example there turns a group's stems to match its neighbours. Here the neighbouring groups (27.3, 29.3, 139.3-142) and the matching violin line all go down. In a ff tutti read at crotchet = 126, the violas at 28 and 30 also see their slur and dots on the opposite side from the violins they bow with, which works against MOLA's aim of parts that read alike across a section.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy).** `\stemDown ... \stemNeutral` around the five groups. No pitch, rhythm or slur changes.
- tOne.ily, tOneVla: `c'4--) \stemDown b8\upbow( c'8) \stemNeutral aes4.-> g8` (28) and `\stemDown bes8-. c'8-. des'8-. bes8-. \stemNeutral g'4.-> f'8` (30), with a c525 comment.
- coda.ily, codaVla: `c'4--) \stemDown b8\upbow( c'8) \stemNeutral aes4.-> g8` (168).
- dev.ily, devVla: `c'4 \stemDown b8( c'8) \stemNeutral aes4.-> g8-.` (89).
- recOne.ily, recOneVla: `\stemDown c'8\f\< c'8 b8 c'8 \stemNeutral f'8 ...` (139).

I first tried `\once \override Beam.direction = #DOWN`. With the c225 middle-line engraver it causes a cyclic-dependency error in Stem.direction, so the stem commands are needed. The comments go on their own lines, because a `%` in the middle of recOne.ily's one-line bar group comments out 140-142. That happened in my first test build. It still gave 0 warnings, and only the vl.txt diff and tools/lencheck.py caught it, so lencheck should be wired into build.sh (c419/c520 open item).

After the change, the probe shows each new group meeting the beam rules. 28.2 and 89.2 slope ½ space upward with the outer interval and lie below the stave (stems 2.9 spaces, minimum 2½ outside, PDF 35). 30.1 is horizontal because it starts and ends on B♭3 (PDF 36a). 139.1 is horizontal (two pitches, odd note inside, PDF 37), with stems of 3.06 spaces. The viola's slurs, bow marks and dots at 28, 30, 89 and 168 are now above the notes, as in Vln II (checked on a 300 dpi crop of page 3 and in the viola part, page 1).

Not changed, for stated reasons:
- The harp RH groups at 47-55 and 162-166 alternate direction from bar to bar. Each spans more than an octave and a half and lies on one side of the stave, so PDF 38 governs; c125 also protects them.
- Vc 56-68 (c415 checked it under PDF 39), Vc 61.1 (protected).
- Vln II and Ob 2 beat-4 pairs at 156-163. Each is a single pair between held notes in a different bar, not a run of groups, so PDF 39 does not apply.

## Within the lens: where the score still falls short of a master's

I looked for any beaming that obscures the music's shape, such as a group divided against the beat, a beam that hides a phrase grouping, or a slur forced to the wrong side. Apart from Finding 1 I found none. The quaver accompaniment groups by half-bar (Gould's metrical grouping) and the semiquaver runs (40, 68) by beat. The two places where beaming could mark phrasing are the P-head pickups at 27/29 (quaver G unbeamed before the dotted crotchet) and the S-head pickups at 47 and 147. Both are printed as an unbeamed quaver anacrusis followed by a beat that starts with the downbeat note. That shows the pickup as a pickup, so I made no change. The beams themselves are now at the standard of a careful hand-engraved edition. Four passes of this reading have brought them into line with every rule on PDF 31-40 that LilyPond can express.
