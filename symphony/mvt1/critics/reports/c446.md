## Findings

### 1. Bar 56→57: the harp's F♭6 rings into the E→F resolution and under its own F♮ pedal change (applied)

At 56 the RH plays C6-F♭6-A♭6-C7 on beats 3-4 (Ab augmented triad; the orchestra spells the same note E♮: Cl 1, Hn 2 E4 at 56.3½, Vc E4 half). At 57.1 the clarinets and horn resolve E4→F4 and the violins reach F5 on beat 2, but the harp's F♭6 (= E6) has not been damped: the harp rests on 57 beats 1-2 and the string keeps sounding a semitone against the F's of the resolution. At 57.3 the F♮ pedal moves that same string while it still vibrates, so its remaining sound slides up to F before the RH plucks F6 a half-beat later.

Evidence: Adler PDF 111-113 (one pedal per pitch class; change just before the note; the composer must make sure the change is possible), PDF 117 (sons étouffés), and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46 (at a change of harmony the player stops the strings, otherwise one chord mixes with the next). The score already marks this exact case at 62, 68 (c246) and 61.4, 63.4 (c336); 57 is the one bar of 56-68 where a chromatic resolution happens against a still-ringing harp string and no mark is given. The 6th octave sustains less than the middle (Adler PDF 114), but at p and 1.3 seconds after the pluck the E6 is still audible, and it sits exactly a minor ninth above the resolving F5.

### 2. Bar 165→166: the harp's V7 rings into the C-major arrival (applied)
