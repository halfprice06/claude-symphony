## Overall verdict on bars 1–14
The motto arrives within four seconds, which matches the models: Tchaikovsky 6/i has the bassoon in bar 1 and Dvořák 9/i has the cellos in bar 1. The introduction then states the motto three times in rising registers in 40 seconds:
- bass clarinet, cellos and basses, G1–E♭3, pp (1–4);
- cor anglais, D4–B♭4 (5–8);
- Vln I from G4 (9), opening the sixth ladder.

This is clearer than Tchaikovsky's plan. He repeats his bassoon phrase at the same pitch in 1–6 and 7–12. The harmonic plan of 6–9 (E♭/G, D♭/F, C major, C minor) is distinctive, and the bars 1–4 sound returns literally at 171–175.

Two things fall short of the models:
- the boundary into the Allegro (finding 1);
- the absence of any dynamic event in the first 40 seconds (finding 2).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 13–15: the boundary into the Allegro is a ramp; the models make it a contrast. (Applied.)
**Problem.** In v69, bar 13 drops to p on V. Bar 14 then holds a full G pedal (Bsn 2, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb), and the timpani roll crescendos p→mf straight into the f Allegro. Because the dynamic rises continuously, the Allegro's first bar arrives already expected.

**Evidence.** Every model separates the two tempos with a break in sound or a contrast of dynamic:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: the introduction ends with F♯/A♯ in the low strings, pp, at bar 18 beat 2. The rest of the bar is a fermata on a rest in all parts (corpus). The Allegro starts p in the violas at 19.
- Dvořák 9/i: the ff dominant at 22 decays to a p timpani B at 23 beat 1. The rest of 23 is silent ("attacca"), and the Allegro enters pp at 24 (corpus).
- Beethoven 7/i: the end of the introduction is a single E passed between flute, oboe and strings with rests between the notes (flauti.ly, the bars before `\time 6/8`). The Vivace begins "sempre p".
- Brahms 1/i: the introduction ends "with a plucked G note in the cellos", and the exposition "begins abruptly … with an orchestral exclamation" (Wikipedia).

Brahms is the only model with a forte Allegro start, and he gets there through near-silence, not a crescendo.

**Cyclic gain.** The horn's unresolved B at 14 is one of the four hanging B♮s listed in cyclic-notes.md. If bar 14 ends with that B followed by silence, the denied tutti at 166 becomes a recall of it: there the horns and trumpets end on B (`b2.-> r4\fermata`). The same gesture then appears twice: at 14 it opens the Allegro, and at 166 it opens the Lento coda. The retransition at 119–122 keeps the timpani G-roll crescendo, so the recapitulation's entrance is prepared while the exposition's is not. Leave 119–122 as it is.

**Fix (no added bars, no tempo change, notes of 12–13 and the horn call unchanged):**
```
introBsnTwo: ... | g,1\p~\> | g,2.\pp r4 |
introHnOne:  ... | r4 es4(\mp c'2~ | c'4\> b2)\p r4 |
introTimp:   ... | g,1:32\p~ | << g,2.:32 { s2\> s4\pp } >> r4 |
introVlnTwo: ... | g'1\p\> | f'2.\pp r4 |
introVla:    ... | es'2\p\>( d'2) | <g d'>2.\pp r4 |
introVc:     ... | g1\p\> | g2.\pp r4 |
introCb:     ... | g,1\p~\> | g,2.\pp r4 |
```
Bar 14 beat 4 is now a full rest (1.2 s at ♩=50). The Allegro's cello C2 f on the downbeat of 15 is the first sound after it.

### 2. Bars 6–8: 38 seconds without a dynamic event; the strings under the cor anglais are flat pp. (Applied.)
**Problem.** Between bar 1 and bar 10 nothing rises above p, and the strings have no shading. Yet bar 7 (D♭/F under the cor anglais's G, the motto's Neapolitan, harmonised) moving to C major at 8 is the most striking event in the first half of the introduction.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i marks "cresc." in the basses at bars 2 and 8, leading to sf at 5 and 11 inside the pp opening (corpus).
- Dvořák 9/i places fz in the horns at 4 and in the flutes at 7 before the ff at 10 (corpus).

**Fix.** Give the strings a swell that matches the cor anglais's own hairpin. Dynamics only:
```
introVlnTwo: R1*5 | g'1\pp | f'1\< | e'1\p\> | es'1\pp\< | ...
introVla:    R1*5 | es'1\pp | des'1\< | c'1\p\> | c'1\pp\< | ...
introVc:     R1 | g1\pp | aes1\< | g1\p\> | g1\pp\< | ...
introCb:     c,1\pp | g,,1 | f,,1\< | c,1\p\> | c,1\pp\< | ...
```
The notes of the E♭/E♮ pivot at 8–9 are untouched.

### 3. Motto recognition at 73, 109, 138 and 167. (Checked; no change.)
- 73: flute `c''4 | aes''2.~ aes''8 g''8 | ges''2 f''4`. This is the intro's rhythm exactly, transposed.
- 109–113: trombones in augmentation. This is the development form, without the return to C.
- 137–138: oboe G–E–D, the major form.
- 167: cor anglais at the original pitch of bar 1.

All four keep the pickup, the long sixth and the eighth-note slip of bars 1–2, so they will be recognised. The sixth ladder of Vln I at 9–11 now returns only at 111–113 (cycle 41). That makes 111–113 a unique recall of the introduction, and so cycle 41 strengthened the link.

### 4. Bar 168: the cor anglais tail rhythm was changed in cycle 42. (Checked; no change.)
In v69 the line reads `es''8 d''8~ | d''4 c''4 des''4 c''4~`, where bar 3 reads `c2 des4 c4~`. The D♭ is still on beat 3 and the C is still tied over the barline, so the tail is still recognisable. Restoring `c''2` would bring back the octaves with the cello (es''–d''–c'' over es'–d'–c') that cycle 42 removed. Keep it.
