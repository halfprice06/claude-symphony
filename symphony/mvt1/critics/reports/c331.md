## Findings

### 1. Bars 69–72: the P head's upper octave has only two clarinets above two horns at f. Applied.

**What is wrong.** The codetta is the first place where the two heads sound together: the S head on top (Fl 1·2 + Vln I at C6, Ob 1 + Tpt 1 at C5) and the P head below. The finale builds its apotheosis on this combination (IV 247–254), so it should register on first hearing. The P head is laid out as Hn 1·2 a2 at E♭3–D♭4 with Cl 1·2 a2 and Vln II an octave higher at E♭4–F5. (c307's table row 5 recorded the clarinets as "at the unison" with the horns and called the rule "not applicable". The source has `closClOne` at `es'8 c''4.` and `closHnOne` at `es8 c'4.`, and the MIDI scan gives a 20-onset octave run, so the clarinets are an octave above.)

Rimsky-Korsakov is specific about this layout. Over one horn he puts two clarinets, two oboes or two flutes. "But if there are two horns playing the lower octave in unison, three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages" (PDF 75), with the formulas 1 Ob + 1 Cl over 1 Horn and 2 Fl + 2 Cl over 2 Horns. His Ex. 90 (*Scheherazade* IV, PDF 265) is the model. The clarinets' octave also lies across their throat tones: concert E♭4, G4 and A♭4 are written F4, A4 and B♭4, and they make up eight of the fifteen notes of the line. PDF 66 calls the clarinet "weak in the middle compass". The result is that the two f horns outweigh their upper octave. The P head is heard as a low horn counter rather than as the theme in octaves, and the combination of the heads is harder to hear.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-331/work/sections/clos.ily).** The cor anglais, silent from 10 to 90, joins Cl 1·2 and Vln II on the upper octave with the same notes and articulation as Cl 2:
```lilypond
closCorAnglais = { r8 es'8(\f c''4.->) bes'8( aes'8 g'8 | aes'8 bes'8 c''8 des''8) f''4.-> es''8 | des''8( c''8 bes'8 aes'8) g'4.-> bes'8 | aes'4-> c''4-> aes'2-> | R1*4 | }
```
- This gives three winds above the two horns, which meets PDF 75's count. Written B♭4–C6 is the cor anglais's strongest band, so it carries the throat-tone notes of the clarinets.
- With the violas' and horns' register it makes Rimsky-Korsakov's natural three-group unison "Violas (or 'Cellos) + Cl. (Eng. horn) + Horn" (PDF 79).
- No harmony note is lost. The alternative, Ob 2, would drop its C♭5 at 70, the seventh of vii°7/V, which otherwise sounds only in the violas' quavers.
- Parts only: parts-gen.py gives the cor anglais a Cl. 1 cue at 67–68 before the f entry, as Ob 1, Ob 2 and Cl 2 already have.

**Protections.** 69–72 has been protected as the codetta tune and texture (c253 "69–76", c249 "grading at 69–72", c279 "two flutes 69–72", c291 "Tpt doubling Ob 69–72", c327 "counters 69–72"). None of those reasons concerns the number of winds on the P head's upper octave. The notes, rhythms, dynamics, grading, flutes and trumpet stay as they are. The cor anglais's fugato entry at 91 (c270 "fugato wind colours 88/91/94") is still its first exposed solo since the introduction: at 69–72 it is one voice inside an f tutti doubling.

### 2. Bars 158–161: brass on the passionate S climax (PDF 73). Keep.

PDF 73 says phrases charged with passion "become sickly and insipid when confided to the brass". At 158–161 Tpt 1 (fff, middle octave) and Hn 1·2 (low octave) are in the S tune. But they only double it. The top octave is Fl 1·2 + Vln I, the middle is Vln II + Ob 2 (Rimsky's Vln + Ob + Tpt, PDF 79), and the low is Vc. The horns hold the tune's long notes, as in Ex. 96 (PDF 273, "melody simplified in the horns"). This is Rimsky-Korsakov's own climax practice (PDF 76, *Scheherazade* IV: 2 Fl + 2 Ob over 2 Trumpets). The layout is protected (c241, c291, c304). No change.

### 3. Bars 13–14, 41–44: the horn call follows the brass-melody rules. Keep.

E♭3–C4–B3 (written B♭3–G4–F♯4) is a fanfare-shaped call in the horn's middle register (PDF 71–72). It is soft and solo, the "genial and poetic" horn of Ex. 73 (*Antar*, PDF 245). The four-horn ff unison at 41–42 is the "extreme power and resonance" of PDF 73. Hn 1's p restatement E♭4–C5–B♭4 at 43–44 then leads to S. The call's chromatic B is the kind PDF 73 allows in a fanfare. Nothing to change. The notes are protected and quoted by the scherzo and the finale.

### 4. Bars 129–130: the half-augmented motto in Cl 1's low register. Keep.

Cl 1 states G3–E♭4–D4–C♯4–C4 mf, partly on throat tones (PDF 66). Here that is the right colour. The motto is minor and "dark and gloomy" (PDF 72). It begins a stretto in which the violas (129.3), Vln II and Bsn 1 (130) enter in turn, so each voice is heard by its entry, not by its weight. Raising it an octave would put it above the violas' entry and break the stretto's register order. No change.

### 5. The themes judged against the lens. No change.

- **The motto.** Six notes with one leap and one chromatic return, given a new single colour at each return (B.cl + Vc, C.a., Fl, brass, Cl, Vln I). This is the treatment PDF 64–67 asks for in expressive soft melody.
- **S.** The tune most likely to be hummed. It is given first to the horn-and-cello blend that PDF 79 praises.
- **P.** A driving sentence suited to an Allegro.
- **The horn call.** The finale completes it.

The reading concerns presentation, and every theme's presentation follows it except at 69–72, now fixed. I found no reason to change a theme. A change would have to go to three other movements, and nothing in the reading points to a gain that would justify that.
