## Overall verdict against Dvořák 9/i
Section by section, the score now holds up well:
- **Introduction:** it prefigures the first theme as Dvořák's does. The violin leaps at 9–12 (G–E♭, A♭–F, B–A♭) are the pitch skeleton of 15–19, just as Dvořák's horns preview the arpeggio before bar 24.
- **Second theme:** it is better integrated than either of Dvořák's second-group tunes. They are new melodies; ours grows from the motto's sixth.
- **Development:** it has a clearer plan than Dvořák's (dialogue, fugato, B-minor theme with the T1 tail combined, wedge, pedal climax). The augmented trombone motto at 109–113 is the kind of move a conductor credits to a master.
- **Recapitulation:** it reshapes its material as Dvořák does. Dvořák moves the second group up a semitone; this score adds the ♭VI climax at 156.

The one place where Dvořák is plainly ahead is how the **last forte statement of the main theme sounds**. At 396–407 Dvořák gives the head to the trumpets while the strings hold fff tremolo chords (corpus p. 44), so the final appearance sounds new. Here, 163–166 is the fifth statement of the same sound: theme in three octaves of winds and strings, brass on whole notes, basses on repeated eighths (27, 69, 123, 131, 163; cycle 27 noted this). Findings 1 and 2 address that. The rest are small.

## v48 against v38 (cycles 19–28)
**Better:**
- the measured accelerando in the retransition (119–122);
- the wedge figure at 106 and the sigh at 115–117;
- the oboe's dolce G–E at 137, the best new idea of the ten cycles: it turns C minor toward C major without a transition;
- the cello tenor line at 163–166;
- the chromatic recapitulation counter at 148–151;
- the three-part trombone chorale and the pp tam-tam at 171;
- one articulation for the motto head;
- the deceptive cadence at 157–158 (V to vi) after the fff.

**Worse:** nothing in pitch or form got worse. Markings have grown: recTwo hairpins went from 65 to 93, dev tenutos from 0 to 20, slurs in dev from 42 to 88. I checked the rendered pages. Most of this is the consistency that was asked for, not clutter, and it does not cost spontaneity. The one spot that reads as over-edited is 107, with four tenutos in each of five doubling parts under a "cresc." (finding 4). Doublings are not excessive anywhere except where intended (154–157). A carried-over gap: at 163, Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 have no dynamic of their own. They inherit mf, mf and mp< from 162, so the MIDI and any extracted parts have them soft under an ff tutti. Finding 1 fixes this; if it is rejected, add `\ff` to the first note of `codaFluteOne`, `codaOboeOne` and `codaClOne`.

## Findings

### 1. Coda 163–166: the last forte T1 statement repeats the exposition's tutti sound (coda section bars 1–4)
- **What is wrong:** the coda opens with the exposition's own tutti sound. The motto head appears for the last time at forte with no new colour.
- **Evidence:** Dvořák 9/i, 396–407: trumpets carry the main-theme head, strings hold fff tremolo chords, and the woodwinds sustain or answer. Rimsky-Korsakov treats a fanfare over string tremolo as the standard way to score this.
- **Fix:**
  - Trumpets 1·2 and horns 1·2 take the head a2, an octave apart. The violas stay on it with the horns.
  - Violins move to high tremolo chords.
  - Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 move from the head to the sustained chord.
  - Hn 3·4, Tpt 3 and the trombones keep their f pads, so the grading still holds: the melody is now in the brass, and the pads stay a level below it.
  - Harmony, the tonic pedal and the cello tenor line are unchanged.
  - This revises the "graded brass 163" do-not-touch item only for Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2. It is not the rejected "motto rhythm in trumpets under the tutti": here the trumpets replace the doubling instead of adding to it.

### 2. Exposition tutti 27–31: brass on whole notes give the theme no rhythmic drive (tOne section bars 13–17)
- **What is wrong:** the brass sit on whole notes under the tutti, so nothing in the brass drives the rhythm.
- **Evidence:** at rehearsal 1 (bar 59), Dvořák's horns turn from long notes to `ef4 r8 d | ef4 r8 d` (brass.notesA.ily, hornImA). The brass punctuate the bass rhythm without doubling the tune.
- **Fix:** give the trumpets the timpani's existing rhythm in 27, 28 and 30. This is the bass's rhythm, not the melody's, so it does not reopen the v17/v18 rejection. Dynamics stay f, as the grading requires.

tOneTptOne = { R1*11 | b'2.->\ff r4 | c''4\f r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8 | g'4 r4 g'4 r4 |

### 3. The fff ♭VI chord at 156 has almost no fifth (recTwo section bar 18)
- **What is wrong:** at the movement's main climax, E♭ sounds only as one quarter note in the counterline. Every other part has A♭ or C (Tbn 1 c', Tbn 2 aes, Tpt 3 c', Cl 2 c', Hn 4 aes, tune c'''–aes'''), so the fff chord is hollow.
- **Evidence:** Dvořák's fff chords at 400ff are complete in the brass, with the fifth in the trombones (corpus p. 44).
- **Fix:** Tbn 2 moves G–E♭–F instead of G–A♭–F. A♭ remains in the bass trombone, tuba, Cb and bassoons, and the move makes no parallels (tested).

```
recTwoTbnTwo = {
  R1*15 | g1\ff | g1\< | es1\fff |
  f2 f2 | e2\>~ e2\mp | R1*4 |
}
```

### 4. Bar 107: one articulation too many (dev section bar 31)
- **What is wrong:** `des'''4-- c'''4-- es'''4-- des'''4--` is marked in Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Tpt 1 and Vln I (20 tenutos in one bar), under "cresc.". This is the only place where the cycle-28 articulation pass adds ink without adding information: the crescendo and the arrival on the whole note at 108 already broaden the line.
- **Fix:** remove the tenutos from 107 in all five parts, for example in `devVlnOne`: `des'''4 c'''4 es'''4 des'''4 |`. Keep the cycle-28 slurs and tenutos at 103–106.

### 5. No other change needed for over-editing
I checked 47–55 and 139–147, which have hairpin pairs, a tenuto on the leap note and espr./dolce. This matches Tchaikovsky's practice for second themes and stays within one pair per bar. I leave these bars as they are and do not re-raise them.
