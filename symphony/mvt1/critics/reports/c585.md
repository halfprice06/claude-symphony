## Findings

### 1. Hairpins crushed between two dynamics in four parts (Perc 159, Tpt 1 and Tpt 2 162, Vc 41-42). Applied.

What is wrong. In a part, a whole-note or half-note bar is given little width, and when a hairpin runs from one dynamic to the next, the two dynamic letters take up almost the whole bar. A probe that measured every hairpin stencil in all 30 parts found these, in staff spaces:

- Percussion 159, suspended-cymbal roll pp < fff: 0.35. It prints as "pp ‹ fff", which looks like a stray mark. In the score the same hairpin spans the whole bar (score p. 16).
- Trumpet 1 162, ff > mp: 0.70. Trumpet 2 162: 0.82. Both print "ff › mp", which reads as an accent between two dynamics. The score's hairpin runs across the bar.
- Violoncello 41 sf > and 42 mf > pp: 1.41 and 1.02. These also read as accents.

All other hairpins in the parts measure 1.7 or more.

Why it matters. MOLA PDF 8 warns that parts extracted from a score are not error-free by themselves, because anchored markings can collide or float (Ex. 7, PDF 12, shows dynamics, hairpins and tempo words running into each other). PDF 7 asks the layout to "avoid over-crowding to provide clarity within each measure". These are not minor places. The cymbal roll is the one-bar crescendo into the largamente fff at 160, the third A♭ climax. The trumpets' ff > mp at 162 is the planned exit from that climax (c244 graded brass). The cello diminuendos at 41-42 shape the medial caesura. In each case the player's part loses the gesture the score shows.

Fix (part-only, through `\tag #'part`, so the score is unchanged):

```
% recTwo.ily, recTwoPerc, bar 159
\tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #12 } cymr1:32\pp\< ...
% recTwo.ily, recTwoTptOne / recTwoTptTwo, bar 162
\tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #9 } c''2\ff\>~ c''2\mp
\tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #9 } e'2\ff\>~ e'2\mp
% trans.ily, transVc, bars 41-42
\tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #8 } g1\sf\> | \tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #8 } g1\mf\> | g1\pp | g1 |
```

The override must name `Staff`, because parts.ly moves the Dynamic_engraver to the Staff context. Tpt 1 and Vc were not yet passed through `\removeWithTag #'part` in score.ly, so I added it for both (the comment at score.ly line 67 lists them now):

```
<< \global \pc \removeWithTag #'part \tptOne \removeWithTag #'part \tptTwo >>
<< \clef bass \global \removeWithTag #'part \vc >>
```

After the change the probe measures the cymbal hairpin at 7.4 spaces, the trumpets' at 4.7 and the cellos' at 3.3 and 3.0. No hairpin in any part is now under 1.6 spaces, and these read as hairpins. Page counts are unchanged in every part, page turns are in the same bars (Vc still turns between 109 and 110), and all 18 score pages are pixel-identical to v585 at 50 dpi.

I left the borderline cases (Hn 3-4 162-163, Tbn 2 and B.tbn 40, Vla 41-42, all 1.7-2.0 spaces) alone. They read as hairpins, and a global change of `Hairpin.minimum-length` would re-space every part and put the attacca page plan at risk (viola spacing 1.42 is already near the edge, c275).

### 2. Bass clarinet: 24 bars' rest, 143-166, with no cue before the f entry at L (167). Applied.

What is wrong. The parts audit lists every entry after eight or more bars' rest. Apart from the protected uncued tutti entries (26, 69, 82, 158; c465), the longest uncued wind rest is the bass clarinet's: 143-166, 24 bars, through poco rit., Poco meno mosso (crotchet = 112), poco animando, K, largamente and rit., about 45 seconds. It then enters f at L with the bright first theme. The C.a. (171), Hn 3 and Hn 4 (179), Vln I (153, 175) and the harp (154, 179) all have cues before comparable rests. Earlier passes gave the bass clarinet cues at 67, 89 and 137 for shorter rests (c385).

Evidence. MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest, the cues being transposed to the reading key of the instrument. Cues must be audible and obvious to the musician reading the part." Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows the model: a named instrument's notes in small type in the last bars of a long rest, with the tempo words printed above the rests.

Fix. In parts-gen.py, the bass-clarinet cue list gains `VIL2+("UP",165,2)`: the Violin I line at 165-166 (the rit. descent C6-B5-A5-G5-F5-D5 to the held C5), two octaves down so that in the B♭ bass-clarinet transposition it prints inside the stave (written D5-C♯5-B4-A4-G4-E4, D4). The violins carry the tune here, so it is the most audible line. I tried the Vc arpeggio first; its C2 at 166 printed four ledger lines below the treble stave, which is harder to read. The part stays on one page.
