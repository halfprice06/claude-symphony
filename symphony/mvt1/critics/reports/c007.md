# Critic 7 (lens 7: texture and counterpoint) on v26

## What I studied
I read every section file of v26 and checked bar numbers against score.pdf (page 6, bars 89–99). From the corpus I extracted, with music21, Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 161–200 (the development fugato) and bars 130–140 (the tutti restatement of the second theme), Beethoven 9/i bars 217–232, and Dvořák 9/i bars 149–156 (accompaniment of the second theme). The corpus has no Brahms or Schumann. Web sources: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Steinberg's preface (Project Gutenberg, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33900); the Kiel Brahms edition page for Symphony No. 2, which quotes Walter Frisch on Brahms's revision of the inner string parts (https://www.brahmsausgabe.uni-kiel.de/erschienene-baende/symphonie-nr-2-d-dur-op-73/). Searches for published analyses of the Pathétique fugato returned nothing usable.

## Findings

### 1. The fugato never gets beyond two real voices (bars 85–96)
The four entries are the cello at 85, viola and bassoon 1 at 88, viola and cor anglais at 91, and violin I at 94. The viola takes two entries in a row (88 and 91). The cello plays the countersubject at both 88 and 91. So at 91 there is still only subject, countersubject and a harmonic bass. The voice that finished the previous entry never continues as a free part, and no new instrument joins. At 94 the countersubject is tripled in unison (Vla, Vln II, Cl 1) instead of being a third independent line. The density profile confirms this: an average of 1.8 voices at 85–88 and 5.0 at 89–92, most of them doublings.

Evidence: in Tchaikovsky 6/i the fugato (bars 171–187) runs the violas from 171, adds the second violins at 175 with the violas entering again one beat later (a stretto at the fourth), and keeps a third, independent figure going throughout (Vln I with the head of the main theme at 171–174, the cellos' turn figure at 175–178). It then converges into rhythmic unison at 186–187. Voices accumulate, enter in stretto and climb in register.

Fix (bars 91–95): each new entry goes to a new instrument, the viola moves on to the countersubject, and the cello becomes a third, sustained voice. Every interval between subject and countersubject stays as it is now.
- `devVlnTwo`: replace the 10 bars of rest (84–93) with `R1*7 | r8 c''8\f aes''4.-> g''8 ges''8 f''8 | f''4 e''8( f''8) des''4.-> c''8 | f''4 g''4 aes''4 a''4 |`. Then raise bars 94–95 an octave: `bes'4. c''8 des''8( es''8) f''4 | des''4 c''8( bes'8) es''4. des''8 |`.
- `devVla` 91–93: `f'4.\f g'8 aes'8( bes'8) c''4 | aes'4 g'8( f'8) bes'4. aes'8 | des''2 c''2 |` (the countersubject, an octave above Bsn 1).
- `devVc` 91–93: `c'1 | des'2 c'2 | f2 g2 |` (this removes the cello's present doubling of Bsn 1 and its octaves with the bass, des–c, at 93).
- `devCorAnglais` and `devBsnOne`: unchanged. The cor anglais now doubles Vln II an octave lower and stays in its best register.

### 2. At the ♭VI climax the only countermelody is in clarinet 1 alone (bars 154–157)
`recTwoCounter` (`g'2 c''2 | b'2 f'2 | c''2 r8 c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2) f'2`) is the only independent line at 154–157. Everyone else plays the melody (Fl, Ob 1, Vln I/II, Vla, Vc, Tpt 1) or holds whole-note chords at fff (Hn 1–4, Tpt 2–3, Trombones). A single middle-register clarinet will not be heard, so the climax sounds as melody plus pads.

Evidence: Steinberg's preface records Rimsky-Korsakov's own correction of *The Golden Cockerel* [233]: "the counter-melody on the violas and violoncellos doubled by the wood-wind will hardly be heard" against brass. In Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 134–140 the trumpets and trombones play a rising counter-figure (G♯–A–B, F♯–G♯–A) against the descending violin and flute line, instead of holding chords.

Fix: horns 1 and 2 play the countermelody in unison with Cl 1, keeping equal dynamics because they share a staff. In `recTwoHnOne` and `recTwoHnTwo`, bars 154–157 become `g'2\ff c''2 | b'2 f'2 | c''2\fff r8 c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2) f'2 |`. The chord tones they give up are still held by Tpt 2–3, Tbn 1–2, Hn 3–4, Ob 2 and Cl 2.

### 3. The second theme's countermelody stops moving at the climax (exposition 62–63 and 66–67; recap 160–161)
The Cl 1 countermelody at 56–68 moves well while the melody holds (56, 58, 60, 64). At 62–63 and 66–67, where the violins hold their longest notes, it falls back to `c'2 es'2 | des'2 des'2` and `des'2 des'2 | des'2 des'2`, which are chord tones only. The recap does the same at 160–161 (`f'2 f'2 | f'2 d'2`).

Evidence: Frisch, quoted by the Kiel edition, describes how Brahms revised the "simple homorhythmic string accompaniment" under the horn solo at 2/i bar 455 so that the inner voices became "thematically integral". Dvořák 9/i bars 149–156 shows that pads are fine under a *first* statement, which is why I leave 47–55 alone.

Fix (`tTwoClOne`):
- 62–63: `c'4 es'8( f'8 g'4 aes'4~ | aes'4 g'8( f'8) g'2 |` (a 4–3 suspension over E♭7, with no seconds against Vln II).
- 66–67: `des'4 f'8( g'8 aes'4 bes'4 | bes'2( g'4 des'4) |`

Recap (`recTwoCounter`, bars 13–14 = 160–161): `f'4 a'8( b'8) c''4 d''4 | d''2( b'4 f'4) |`

### 4. A chromatic clash in the recap counterline (bar 123, beat 3; also Fl 2 in bar 124)
The counterline `r4 es'''2-> d'''4~` (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, and Vln II an octave lower) sounds d against the theme's des' (Vc, Bsn, Cl, Tbn) on the same eighth. That is an augmented octave between two prominent ff lines.

Fix: let the counterline follow the motto's own D♭. In `recOneFluteOne` and `recOneVlnOne`, bars 123–124 become `r4 es'''2-> des'''4~ | des'''4 c'''2-> b''4`. In `recOneOboeOne` and `recOneVlnTwo` they become `r4 es''2-> des''4~ | des''4 c''2-> b'4`. In `recOneFluteTwo`, bar 124 becomes `c''2 d''2`, so it does not sit against des'''. The des''' over C minor is then a Phrygian appoggiatura that resolves to c''' on beat 2.

### 5. The coda's combination of the two themes ends in octave doublings (bars 169–170)
The cello's C-minor second theme and the horn's motto (167–170) are good counterpoint until 169, beat 3, where both land on des'. In 170, Vc `c''2 b'2`, Hn 1 `c'2 b2` and Vla `c'2 b2` all play the same 4–3 resolution, so the leading tone is tripled and the two themes merge. Earlier cycles fixed this kind of doubled resolution (cycle 2).

Fix: in `codaVla` bar 170, `es'2 d'2`. This gives a 6–5 against the 4–3 in the cello and horn (a minor cadential 6/4 to 5/3). It also lets the violas move es'–d'–c' into 171.
