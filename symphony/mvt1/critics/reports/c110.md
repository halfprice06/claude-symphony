## Findings

Measured against this reading, movement I has no fault that stands between it and a master's work. Its key plan follows Unit 32 almost point by point. The findings below therefore do two things. They record what the reading shows the movement already does, so that later cycles do not undo it. And they turn the reading into concrete instructions for movements II and III. I made no change to the score.

**1. Movement II should open by a common-tone modulation from the last chord of I (182 → II/1–2).**
- Problem: cyclic-notes §3 says II should "begin from the last sound of movement I, E♮ … fall to E♭". That is half of the textbook device.
- Evidence: A&S PDF 587 and 603 (review point 2). Common-tone modulations are "particularly effective when the chord progression is from a major triad down a 3rd to another major triad … (example: C major–A♭ major)". The common tone sits "in an exposed position, usually in the soprano". With two major triads, the move "will also contain a chromatic inflection". Widmung (Ex. 32-3, PDF 586) has no pivot chord but does not sound abrupt, because the melody tone A♭/G♯ is held above the change of chord.
- Movement I already supplies both elements. The top voice of 182 is C5 (Vln I `c''1`), the common tone with A♭ major. The only E♮ is the viola's E4 (`e'1`), which falls to E♭, the fifth of A♭.
- Fix (for II, not I): keep the C on top, put the inflection in the same instrument, and put A♭ under it. II, bars 1–2: Vln I `c''1~\pp | c''1`; Vla `e'1\pp | es'1`; Vc `c1 | aes,1`; Vln II `g'1 | aes'1`; Hn 3 `c'1~ | c'1`. A shorter version starts II directly on `<aes, es' aes' c''>` with Vln I's C5 alone on top.
- Do not add a pivot chord. A&S's point is that the held tone makes a pivot unnecessary.

**2. The V7/Ger6 pivot for movement III's D♭ episode is already planted at bars 12 and 75.**
- Problem: cyclic-notes §3 asks III for "forte D♭ major shifting directly to C major ff" but gives no route into D♭.
- Evidence: A&S PDF 591–592 and Ex. 32-9. Beethoven's Op. 18/2 turns an It6 in G into V7 of A♭, a tonicization of ♭II "in place of an expected tonic", and returns through an enharmonic °7. Ex. 32-10b: "German 6/5 becomes V7 … produces a modulation up a half step".
- Movement I sounds this chord in both spellings. At 12 it is the Ger6 of C (Vln II `fis'1`, Hn 3 `fis1`, bass A♭), resolving to V at 13. At 75 it is V7 of D♭ over the A♭ pedal (Vln II `ges'1`, Fl 1 `ges''2`, Vla `c'1`). This is a true enharmonic in A&S's sense (PDF 588).
- Fix (for III):
  - First use it as in I: `<aes, es c' fis'>1\ff | <g, d b g'>1`.
  - Later respell it and go up a half step to ♭II: `<aes, es c' ges'>1 | <des, aes, f' des''>1`.
  - Return to C either directly, as the cyclic notes propose (Eroica), or through the movement's own °7 stop chord, which A&S's Beethoven uses for the return: `<des f aes des'>2 <b, f aes d'>2 | <c g e' c''>1`.
- This links the D♭ episode to two sounds the listener has heard in movement I.

**3. The development's key plan is two equal divisions of the octave; keep it intact (77–82, 97–122).**
- Evidence 1: A&S PDF 599–600 and Ex. 32-19. The Appassionata's development moves A♭ minor – E – E minor – C minor – A♭ major in major thirds. That motion "expands the A♭ chord".
- Score 1: bars 77–82 do the same. Cb `aes,,1 | aes,,2 e,2 | e,1 | c,1 | c,1 | aes,,8…`; Vla `ces'1 | ces'2 b2 | b1 | c'1` (C♭ = B is the common tone, spelled as a true enharmonic); Vln II `es'1 | es'2 e'2 | e'1 | es'1`. The cycle arrives at A♭ major ff at 82. As A&S's ♭VI rule asks (PDF 593), that A♭ goes by half step to G at 83.
- Evidence 2: Tchaikovsky 4/i (Ex. 32-20, PDF 600) builds its plan from minor thirds.
- Score 2: the development's second half rests on B minor (97), D minor (103), F minor (111) and A♭6 over the G pedal (122). B–D–F–A♭ over G is V♭9 of C. That is the same sound as the vii°7 stops at 26, 138 and 170, laid out over 26 bars.
- Under A&S's PDF 600–601, this is the strongest large-scale feature of the movement.
- Fix: none. Add to the do-not-touch list: the bass of 77–82; the key arrivals at 97, 103 and 111; A♭ over G at 122. Any edit that moves one of these keys breaks both cycles.

**4. The motto's tail is enlarged in the bass at 111–116; D♭ as a key stays withheld (111–117, 156–157, 175–181).**
- Evidence: A&S PDF 600 says the Appassionata's D♭ goal "results from an enlargement" of C–D♭–C. PDF 601 (Chopin) says the large-scale bass D♮–C♯ and the motive D♯–C♯ "are not unrelated".
- Score: in 111–116 the contrabassoon and Cb play one note per bar, `f,1 | e,1 | es,1 | d,1 | des,1 | c,1`, before G at 117. That is the E♮–E♭ thread followed by the motto's own D–D♭–C. The tonal plan C → A♭ (15 → 47) is the motto's rising minor sixth (G→E♭ transposed to C→A♭).
- What the movement does not do is make D♭ a key. Critic 60 withheld it for movement II. A&S supports that choice only if II does it: PDF 603 says remote chromaticism is integrated "by relating it to the motivic design". Spread across the symphony, the motto's tail becomes I (dev on D: 85, 103) → II (D♭ middle section) → III (C).
- Fix: none in I. For II: keep the D♭-major middle section and the plagal D♭–A♭ close with D♭–C on top.

**5. Movement III should approach its final C major through the intro's own progression (♭VI → Ger6 → V), enlarged.**
- Evidence: A&S PDF 593: "The usual goal of ♭VI is a cadential V …; the half-step progression in the bass helps to intensify the V. A most natural way of leading ♭VI to V is to transform it into an augmented 6th chord" (Chopin Op. 36, Ex. 32-11). PDF 594–595: Beethoven's large I–VI–IV in minor.
- The symphony's own key plan is C – A♭ – C. Movement I's introduction states its local form in bars 10–13: Cb `f,1 | d,1 | aes,1 | g,1`, iv – ii° – Ger6 – V.
- Fix (for III): before the C-major chorale, after II's A♭ has been answered, let A♭ major become the Ger6 and resolve to V. `<aes, es c' aes'>1\ff | <aes, es c' fis'>1 | <g, d b g'>1 | <g, d b g'>1`, with F♯ in the trumpets. The trumpets can then rise B→C at the chorale's arrival, as the cyclic notes already ask. The chorale's first chord then answers bar 13 of movement I.

**6. The E♭/E♮ inflections follow A&S's Haydn and Waldstein cases; keep them soft and in one voice (8–9, 145–147, 166, 181–182).**
- Evidence: A&S PDF 594 says the Waldstein exposition "resolves" its chromatic E major into E minor. PDF 597, in the footnote to the Creation example, places C minor's E♭ against C major's E♮. PDF 585 says a falling inflection is gentle.
- Score: three of the four inflections are direct, in one voice: Vln II `e'1 | es'1` at 8–9; Vla, Hn 1 and Cl 1 at 166; Vla at 181–182. The fourth passes between voices: Vla E♭ (`es'2 d'2`, 144) gives way to the solo oboe's E♮ over V (`g'4( e''4~ | e''2 d''2)`, 145–146). That suits a gentle change of mode on the dominant. In 147–166, C major is resolved into C minor exactly as A&S describe.
- Fix: none. One point for the composer: dev.ily line 257 still says the violins re-enter "with Tpt 1 at 111". Since cycle 108, Tpt 1 enters at 113. That comment should be corrected.
