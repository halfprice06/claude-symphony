## Findings

On this second pass, v367's combined-group writing follows Rimsky-Korsakov's chapter almost everywhere. The cycles since c267 (c304, c314, c324, c334, c357) removed most of the small departures it had. I found nothing that, if changed, would bring the score closer to a master's work without breaking a protected passage or making the part-writing worse. Below are the departures I checked, ranked by how much they would matter, with the reason each one stays. **No change was applied.**

### 1. Bars 181–182: Horns 3·4 held inside the strings in the last chord (kept)

What the score does: the last chord is Vc C3, Hn 4 G3, Hn 3 C4, Vla E4, Vln II G4, Vln I C5 (with Cb C2 and harp), ppp under a fermata. The two horns lie between the cellos and the violas, so the strings enclose the brass in a sustained chord.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 113 says strings and brass are "seldom employed in juxtaposition, crossing, or enclosure", because the two groups have no affinity of tone. The horns here are not doubled by divided violas or cellos (his exception), and the strings are not tremolo.

Why it stays:
- The horns fill the empty octave C3–C4 between the cellos and violas. Without them the chord would have a hole in the tenor, against PDF 114's demand for balance in a long held chord.
- At ppp the lack of affinity matters least. The horns enter a bar before the strings' last change (181), so they sound as the continuation of the trombone chorale's C4, which Tbn 1 hands to Hn 3 at 181 (c140). That is the reading's "bassoon and horn reconcile the groups" idea (PDF 108), applied across the change from brass to strings.
- The chord is protected (the last chord and the five tied parts; Hn 3 crosses into movement II).

### 2. Bars 164–165: the seventh of V7 is in Hn 1 while both bassoons double the root (kept)

What the score does: at 164.3–165, Bsn 1 and Bsn 2 both hold G2 in unison, Hn 3 goes G3→B3, Hn 1 holds F4 (the seventh), and Cl 1 plays D5→B4. It is p, in the dissolution after the climax.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 108: in soft horn–bassoon harmony give "the concords to the horns, the discords to the bassoons". Here the discord is in a horn, and one bassoon is spent on a unison.

I tested the obvious alternative on paper: Bsn 1 on F3 at 164.3–165, resolving to E3 at 166.
- The seventh would be doubled (F3 and F4), and both copies would resolve to E in octaves with Hn 1.
- It would add a second E♮ (E3) one beat before the protected E♮→E♭ turn at 166.3. That turn is meant to sound at one pitch (violas, Cl 1, Hn 1 on E4), and the cellos take E♭3 on the same beat.

Rimsky PDF 112 settles this case: "in a succession of chords where the melodic position is constantly changing, distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts." The present layout has a single seventh that resolves correctly. The pitches of 156–166 are also protected (c346). Keep.

### 3. Bar 162: only Cl 1 lies above the trumpets in the ff A-minor chord (kept)

What the score does: on the deceptive-cadence chord, Tpt 1 has C5 and Tpt 2 E4. Ob 2's A4 sits between them, and Cl 1's E5 is the only woodwind above Tpt 1; Cl 1 leaves for its counterline on beat 4. The flutes and Ob 1 rest.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 110–111 says that with trumpets and trombones in a chord, the flutes, oboes and clarinets "are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets". PDF 110 also lists "oboes and clarinets within trumpets" (Ex. 163, Kitesh) as a good enclosure, and Ob 2 here is exactly that.

Why it stays: the top of the chord is in Vln I (A5) and the harp (A5–C6–E6). The flutes and Ob 1 leave after the fff 160–161 on purpose, so that the brass can fade in stages (protected: climax scoring 158–166, c381 and c407; doubled third at 162, c324). Bringing the flutes back for one bar would undo the thinning that leads to the soft C-major cadence at 165–166.

### 4. Bars 117–120: Cl 2's triplets inside the Hn 3·4 octave G3–G4 (kept, as c267)

Rimsky PDF 108 says clarinets should rarely be set inside the horns. Cl 2 doubles the violas' triplet figure at pitch (B3–D4–F4). It is figuration, not a held chord note, so it does not change the horn chord's colour the way a sustained clarinet note would. The layering 117–121 is protected (c147, c267, c292). Keep.

### 5. Bars 167–169: moving brass over held woodwinds (kept, as c267)

This is the reverse of PDF 108's rule that stationary parts go to the brass and moving parts to the woodwind. It is protected because it rhymes with 27–32 and the finale quotes that layout (c254, c267, c343). Keep.

### 6. Bar 65: Hn 3 F4 above the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline's D♭4 (no change)

On beat 1 the pad horn lies a third above the counterline for half a bar, before the counterline leaps to A♭4. The reading says nothing about a pad crossing a melodic counterline; PDF 112's rule (parts move correctly) is met. The bar is inside the protected 56–68 counterline (c327, c267). No change.
