## Findings

### 1. Bar 154: the Vln II halo doubles the violas' tune at the unison for three beats (applied)

In the recapitulated S (147–155) the violas carry the tune, with the cellos and Hn 1 an octave lower. Vln II plays a pp, divided, sul tasto tremolo above them. Up to bar 153 the halo stays above the tune or crosses it only on a passing eighth. At 154 it drops into the tune's own notes:

- tune (violas): C5 – B4 – D5 – F4
- Vln II v384: C5/E5 on beat 1, then B4/D5 on beats 2–4

So the halo's lower note follows the tune's C5→B4 at the unison, and its D5 is the tune's D5 on beat 3. The scan of string unisons finds no other place in the movement where a background string part shadows a string tune note for note. vl.txt shows the result as five octave lines at 154.2: Vln II's C5→B4 moving with the violas, the cellos and Hn 1.

The reading:
- Adler PDF 135 says to keep the background out of the melody's register "especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color". Brahms gets away with a background in the cello's register only because the background is in its palest register and its figures are rhythmically different. They lie under the tune's long notes, "leaving the [anacrusis] figure undisturbed". At 154 the background moves with the tune's quarters instead.
- Clarity comes from "separating theme and countertheme registerally" and from rhythmic difference, and dynamics are the weaker remedy (PDF 144, items 3–4). The halo is already marked a level softer (pp under p), so only the weaker remedy is in use.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 54: "melody should always stand out in relief from the accompaniment… In the middle of the orchestral range it is not so prominent". The tune at 154 sits at C5–D5, the middle of the range.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139: sustained harmony does not cover a voice when it lies outside the voice's range.

Bar 154 is also the cadence of the phrase (cadential 6/4 to V over G), where the tune's C5–B4 should be clearest.

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoVlnTwo, bar 154; the rhythm, division and dynamics stay the same):

```
% v384
<d'' a''>1:32 | <c'' e''>4:32 <b' d''>2.:32 |
% c384
<d'' a''>1:32 | <e'' g''>4:32 <d'' g''>2.:32 |
```

- The halo now plays E5/G5 over the tune's C5 (C–E–G of the 6/4), then D5/G5 over B4 (G–B–D of the 5/3). It stays above the tune, as it does in 147–153.
- Its top is G5, below the C6 it reaches at 152, so the violins' high colour is still saved for 155–160 (Adler PDF 135).
- The halo's lines run A5→G5 and D5→E5→D5→C5 into 155. The only unison left is the tune's D5 on beat 3, against a held note.
- On beats 2–4 Vln II now holds the chord's root and fifth (G5, D5) and leaves the leading tone B to the tune alone. Before, it played the B at the unison with the tune, the doubling that c312 removed at 151.

This answers c294's "Kept: Vln II pad 147–154". The kept reasons were that the pad is pp, sul tasto, above the tune and below the violins' high colour. The fix makes all of them true at 154, where the second one was not.

## Checked and kept

**A♭6 at 31 and 72, G6 at 41 (Adler PDF 129, 133–135).** Haydn's lesson is to save the top octave. The violins use the A♭6 peak four times. c294 tested Vln I down an octave at 69–72 and found that it puts the tune in unison with Vln II's P-head counter (PDF 144, item 3). Bar 31 is quoted by the finale. The G6 at 41 is a held sf that fades over the four-horn call. Its Vln II octave G5 is needed, and moving Vln I down would put it in unison with Vln II, Ob 1 and Fl 2. The climax order is protected (c244, c318).

**167–170, Vln I and Vln II both divided (PDF 152–154).** Four half-sections play three pitches, with G5/A♭5 doubled. Adler notes that such double stops "are certainly playable non divisi" and that divisi weakens Vln I. But the violins here are a tremolo halo under the ff brass head, not the melody. The 168 double stop A♭5/E♭6 would be a fifth across strings. The layout repeats 27–32 for the finale (c279, c299). Keep.

**122, cellos on a sustained A♭3 while the upper strings play tremolo (PDF 137, 154).** The cellos sing the ♭9–8 sigh A♭3→G3 (122→123) with Bsn 1 and Tbn 1, and the G pedal stays in Cb, Cbsn, tuba, B.tbn and timpani. A held line against tremolo is Bizet's layout in Ex. V-37 (PDF 161) turned upside down. Keep.

**151 and 153, the Vln II halo around the tune (PDF 135).** At 151 the tune's E5 lies between the halo's C5 and B♭5. At 153 the tune passes the halo's D5 on one eighth. Neither is a unison on a beat, and both are in Brahms's own layout (background enclosing a stronger-register tune, Ex. V-7). The 151 voicing is c312's (root of V6/5 of IV). Keep.

**34, the double basses alone on A♭1 while the cellos rest (PDF 130, 134, 151).** Here the double basses carry the bass alone in a light texture, against Adler's usual pairing, and the result is a dark, thin colour after the ff stroke at 33. c359 protects the 33–36 string break, and the bass C–A♭–B♭ is protected (c102, c252). Keep.

**158–161, violas' repeated dyads inside the cellos' octave of the tune (PDF 135, 165).** c294 kept this: the cello octave is a doubling (the tune is in Vln I, Vln II and the winds), and repeated chords are the right idiom at speed (Ex. V-42/43). Nothing new against it.

**Three A♭ climaxes (PDF 137, 155).**

- 122: a tremolo chord with Vln I alone on A♭6 and Vln II A♭5 below it.
- 160: the tune in three octaves (Vln I, Vln II, cellos) with hammered violas and the Cb on A♭1.
