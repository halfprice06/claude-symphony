## Findings

The movement's combined-group writing follows this chapter closely; most of the chapter's rules were already applied by critics 124, 234 and others. I found one place that still breaks the soft bassoon–horn rule, and I checked the movement's other departures and give reasons to keep them.

### 1. Bar 37: Bsn 1 doubles Hn 4 and the soft wind chord has no fifth (applied)

Bars and parts. Bar 37 (transition, under the imitative answers of violas, Vln I, Cl 1 and Ob 1), E♭ minor seventh over the double basses' E♭2. The two bassoons and Horns 3·4 make the only wind harmony: Bsn 2 E♭3, Bsn 1 G♭3, Hn 4 G♭3, Hn 3 D♭4. The cellos also hold G♭3. So G♭3 sounds three times in one unison and the winds have no B♭; the fifth is only in Vln II's tremolo B♭4. From 38 to 40 Bsn 1 then holds A♭3 for three bars while the horns move (Hn 3 D♭4–C4–D4–D4, Hn 4 G♭3–A♭3–F3–D4).

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 108: "Four-part harmony given to two bassoons and two horns, especially in soft passages, yields a finely-balanced tone ... In the former case crossing of parts is to be recommended for the purposes of blend". The diagram (viewed at 300 dpi) interleaves the two pairs, horn–bassoon–horn–bassoon; it does not put a bassoon in unison with a horn. The same page: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind"; at 37–40 the bassoon is the static part. PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords, and bar 37 is a held p< semibreve. Critic 124 applied the same crossing at 39 (Bsn 1 on the seventh A♭3 inside the horns' D4/F3); critic 234 listed bar 37 as "a small exception" and left it. Bar 37 is the first chord of the four-bar crescendo into the sf at 41, so it is the bar where the soft blend matters most.

Fix (trans.ily, transBsnOne, bar 37):
```
\soloStemsOff ges1\mf\< | aes1 | aes1 | aes1\f |   % before
\soloStemsOff bes1\mf\< | aes1 | aes1 | aes1\f |   % after
```
The winds at 37 now read, bottom to top, Bsn 2 E♭3, Hn 4 G♭3, Bsn 1 B♭3, Hn 3 D♭4: four real parts, crossed as in Rimsky's diagram, with the complete E♭m7. Bsn 1 moves B♭3–A♭3 into 38 (it was G♭3–A♭3), so the bassoon line has a step at the chord change and then holds the seventh at 39–40 as critic 124 arranged. The horns' lines, Bsn 2, the dynamics (bassoons mf, horns p: woodwind one degree louder, PDF 112) and the imitation above are unchanged. The discord D♭ stays in Hn 3; moving it to a bassoon would need Bsn 1 above the horns (horns inside bassoons, which PDF 108 rejects) or Hn 3 on G♭4, which would double the violas' accented G♭4 answer at 37.4–38.1.

Voice leading: vl.txt loses five entries (bar 37, Bsn 1 E♭3→F♯3 in octaves with Vc and violas; bar 38, Bsn 1 F♯3→A♭3 in unison with Hn 4) and gains two (bar 38, Bsn 1 B♭3→A♭3 in octaves with Vln II's B♭4→A♭4, a doubling). No new fifths.

### Kept after checking against the reading

- 175–178, violas and Vln II inside and above the pp trombone chorale (PDF 113: strings and brass are seldom crossed or enclosed). At 176 the violas' C4 lies between Tbn 2 G3 and Tbn 1 E♭4, at 177 the violas' A♭3–G3 between Tbn 2 F3 and Tbn 1 D4. This is the movement's one departure from PDF 113. Critic 234 kept it because the chorale is protected and the obvious cure (horns or tuba) would anticipate the finale's major brass chorale. I considered the cure the reading itself offers: a bassoon on the viola line, since PDF 108 makes the bassoon the reconciler of wind and brass and Rimsky's crossing list includes three trombones with two bassoons and contrabassoon (Golden Cockerel 220, PDF 109; Brahms 1/iv bar 47 has the same chorale scoring). I do not recommend it. The violas and Vln II at 175–178 are the start of the lines that carry the E♭→E♮ thread (violas 179–182) and the tied G4 into movement II (Vln II); taking them out for four bars and bringing them back at 179 breaks the only continuous inner voice of the coda. Doubling them with Bsn 1 adds a seventh colour to a passage that already has trombones, bass clarinet, Cl 1, tam-tam and three string groups at pp. The chorale is pp, sustained and in close position, so the lack of "affinity" Rimsky describes costs less than either change. Keep.
- 117–120, Cl 2's triplets B3–D4–F4 inside Horns 3·4's G3/G4 octave (PDF 108: clarinets rarely inside horns). The clarinet doubles the violas' figure at the same pitch; it is figuration, not a held chord note, and the layering 117–121 is protected through many cycles (c147 already moved Cl 1 out). Keep.
- 167–169, brass on the moving head over held woodwinds (the reverse of PDF 108's stationary-brass rule). The layout rhymes on purpose with 27–32, the stop chord at 170 is quoted by the finale, and c254 kept the brass head here as the finale's reserve. Keep.
- 40, Hn 3·4 a2 on the leading tone D4 (c234), with the seventh A♭3 only in Bsn 1, Vc and Vln II at f. PDF 108 says horns overwhelm bassoons in forte, which argues for a horn on the seventh; but PDF 102 (c234) asks for the horns in pairs beside trombones, the seventh is in two string parts as well, and Hn 4 A♭3 would leave D4 to one horn against three trombones again. The two rules pull opposite ways and the gain either way is small. Keep c234's layout.
- 43, the p crotchet chord with the seventh D♭4 in Hn 3 rather than a bassoon (PDF 108). It is a single short chord after the sf, where PDF 114 says balance matters less. Keep.
