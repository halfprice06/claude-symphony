## What I studied

- **v168:** all section files, global.ily and analysis.txt. I looked at score pages 1–3, 5 and 6 at 85 dpi.
- **v158 against v168:** a full diff. It shows cycles 139–148: the Vln II double stop at 33, Hn 4 releases at 28/132, the coda Tbn 1/Hn 3 overlap, sul A/sul G, bar 93 and Vla 35, the timpani rest at 135–137, the wedge brass entering p, Tbn f at 131, Fl 2 rests, bassoon sharing, Cl 1 at 117–121, and the motto swells. **Nothing got worse.** None of these changes thickens the texture or moves a peak, and the build is still clean on 17 pages.
- **Per-bar activity maps** from score.midi (my scripts are in /tmp/critic-149/scripts/), used to locate brass re-entries and count low-brass bars.
- **Master: Brahms, Symphony No. 4/i.** No earlier holistic critic used it. I checked its facts on Wikipedia, "Symphony No. 4 (Brahms)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Brahms)):
  - trombones play only in the finale, the contrabassoon only in movements III–IV, the triangle only in III;
  - the second theme starts at bar 57 in the cellos (with horns) and then passes to the violins;
  - the recapitulation at bar 247 brings the primary theme back slowly, and the coda starts at 394.

**Verdict.** The plan of this movement stands up to the comparison. Both movements have:
- a second theme in cellos and horns that is then given to the violins;
- a recapitulation that changes the theme's scoring, not only its key;
- a coda built on the first theme.

What separates them is economy. In Brahms's first movement every brass sound comes from four horns and two trumpets. Here trombones and tuba sound in 64 of 182 bars, entering 14 separate times. Most of those entries are justified. Two are the kind Rimsky calls colourless (F1, F3).

## Findings

### F1 (applied). 69–71: low brass re-enter mf after 27 bars of rest, under the trumpet tune

**Problem.**
- Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and tuba last sound at 41.
- They return at 69 at mf, with sustained half notes, and go to f only at 72.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 136 says a re-entry of horns, trombones and tuba after a long rest should coincide with pp or ff, and that a mf re-entry produces "a colourless and common-place effect".
- PDF 138–139 adds that sustained harmony in the melody's register covers it. At 69–70 the tune is in Tpt 1 + Ob 1 at C5–F5, while Tbn 1 holds E♭4/F4 and Tbn 2 holds C4/A♭3. That is a fourth sustaining brass pad directly under the tune.
- The score already brings low brass in on stop chords and nowhere else in comparable places (26, 138; protected by c144 as "low brass entering on the stop chords").
- Brahms 4/i makes its tuttis without any trombones at all.

**Fix (applied in clos.ily):**
```
closTbnOne = { R1*3 | c'4->\f c'4-> c'2-> | R1*4 | }
closTbnTwo = { R1*3 | aes4->\f aes4-> aes2-> | R1*4 | }
closBassTbn = { R1*3 | aes,4->\f es4-> aes,2-> | R1*4 | }
closTuba = { R1*3 | aes,,4->\f es,4-> aes,,2-> | R1*4 | }
```

**What the codetta keeps.**
- The bass at 69–71 is still B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, Vc and Cb at f.
- The chord is still complete: Hn 3·4, Tpt 2, Ob 2 and Bsn 1.
- The tune, its bass A♭–C–B♭–D–E♭, the horn counter and the f dynamic are unchanged.

**Protection.** The codetta is protected for its notes, bass and combination of themes (c102, c103, c117, c142). The low-brass pad was never among the stated reasons. Cycle 4 marked these parts mf so they would not cover the trumpet. Resting them serves that same aim better.

### F2 (applied). 62–63: Fl 1 doubles the violins' soft D♭6 peak

**Problem.** At "poco animando", sub. p <, the flute enters in unison with Vln I+II on the tune's highest note, D♭6, and stays with it through 65.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 142, with Ex. 282 (PDF 450): a culminating point that is soft "is better left unsupported". In Ex. 282 the high pp note has only held woodwind chords under it, with no doubling.
- PDF 140–141: doubling belongs to "a few special phrases" and otherwise replaces the line's timbre with a mixed one.
- The forte answer at 64 is that kind of special phrase.

**Fix (applied in tTwo.ily):** Fl 1 `R1*17 | \partCombineSoloI aes''2.~(\f^\markup \bold "1." aes''8 g''8 | f''4.\> es''8 des''2) | \partCombineAutomatic R1\! | R1 |`. The flute now enters at 64 f.

**Protection.** The sub. p at 62 and the D♭6 peak (c118) are untouched; only the doubling goes.

### F3 (applied). 156: horns 2–4 re-enter mp< after 13–24 bars of rest

**Problem.** Hn 2 returns after 24 bars and Hn 3·4 after 13, all at mp<.

**Evidence.**
- This is the same PDF 136 rule. Cycle 143 applied it to the wedge brass at 113 (mp → p).
- Cycle 108 set mp so that the horns would be weaker than the violins' mf (PDF 130). p< is weaker still, so both reasons are met.

**Fix (applied in recTwo.ily):**
- Hn 1 `f'1\<` (continuing from its p at 155).
- Hn 2 `d'2.~\p\<`.
- Hn 3 `bes1\p\<`.
- Hn 4 `f1\p\<`.

**Unchanged:** the crescendo to ff at 158 and all notes.

### F4 (proposal, not applied: protected by c126/c127). 56–61: four sources on the bass under the p violin tune

**Problem.** Under the violin tune the bass comes from four sources:
- Cb arco, sustained whole notes;
- Bsn 2 at pp, the same notes;
- harp LH octaves on beat 1;
- the cello arpeggio starting on the bass note.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 139 warns against long sustained double-bass notes under the melody, and against strings doubled by woodwind.
- In every aria example in the range (Ex. 277, 279, 280, 284, 285), the bass under a cantabile is pizzicato or a single line.

**Possible fix.** Bsn 2 rests 56–59 and enters at 60 with the mf growth: `R1*13 | ges2 es2 | ...`. The line keeps sounding in the Cb, which satisfies cycle 87's breath rule.

**Status.** Cycles 126 and 127 protect the accompaniment design here, so I leave this to the composer.
