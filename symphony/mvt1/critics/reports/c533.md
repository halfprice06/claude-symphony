## Findings

No finding asks for a note to change. I applied nothing in the scratch copy. Section times from v533/full.midi are the same as in c443: intro 67.2 s, P 34.3, transition 26.9, S 47.1, codetta 15.2, development 105.3, recapitulation first group 30.7, second group 44.8, coda 58.9 (total 430.5 s). Inside the development: 77–84 15.2 s, fugato 85–96 22.9, B minor 97–102 11.4, 103–110 15.2, wedge 111–116 11.4, pedal 117–130 29.2. This pass adds the three points below.

### 1. The opening of the development matches the Appassionata's station rhythm. Its goal, A♭ at 82, is not held as Beethoven holds his. Keep.

Ex. 32-19 (PDF 600, image) gives the bar numbers of the Appassionata's first development half: A♭ minor from 67, then stations at 79, 81, 83, 85, 87 and 89, two bars each, then the goal, A♭ as V7 of D♭, held for 18 bars (91–108). PDF 599 says "each intermediate chord is expanded". In Beethoven's own plan that means two bars per station.

The score at 73–84:
- A♭ minor 73–77: the flute motto over the A♭ pedal, then the head at 77.
- E 78–79 and C minor 80–81: two bars each, the same rate as Beethoven.
- A♭ at 82, ff, then the V♭9 strokes at 83 and the sub. p at 84.

So c443's description of the stations as "compressed" is wrong. They run at Beethoven's rate, so I am correcting that record line. The real difference is the goal. Beethoven holds his arrival for 18 bars. The score leaves A♭ after one bar and moves to the fugato on D at 85.

I checked whether that is a shortfall. Beethoven's held A♭ is V7 of D♭, a dominant that has to wait for its resolution. The score's A♭ is ♭VI of C, and its real goal is G: the half step A♭→G, stated at 82→83 and enlarged at 122 over the pedal. The score does give a long hold, but for that later goal: the 14-bar dominant pedal (117–130, 29.2 s), which is the same order of length as Beethoven's 18 bars. Holding A♭ at 82 would spend that hold too early and add bars to a section whose length is protected (c243, c413, c433). Fix: none.

### 2. The stop chord takes four resolutions, and the recapitulation's "wrong turn" is put right at 161. Record only.

PDF 589 lists the °7's functions: vii°7 of a tonic, an applied chord whose resolution "need not be to a tonic", an embellishment of V7 (the Mozart Requiem, Ex. 32-5) and a common-tone chord. PDF 598–599 (Haydn Op. 54/1, Ex. 32-18, image) shows a °7 that takes "the wrong turn" and is put right when it comes back. Earlier passes (c443 row 6) listed three treatments of the stop chord B–D–F–A♭. There are four, and their order is a form plan:

- 26→27: → i. The exposition's normal resolution.
- 138→139 (bass D): → iv, F minor. The recapitulation takes the turn away from the tonic (the late iv of c223 and c433).
- 161, beats 1–2 (bass D): the same pitch classes, with A♭ in Hn 3·4, Ob 1 and Cl 1 falling to G on beat 3 over the G bass (MIDI). This is the °7 as an embellishment of V7, the Requiem function. The chord that went wrong at 138 finds V here, at the climax, as Haydn's chord does on its return.
- 170: over the C pedal, a common-tone °7 under the fermata, left open for the finale's B→C.

This is the reading's "enharmonic versatility" (PDF 589) used without enharmonic spelling, and it is laid out across the recapitulation and coda. It supports the protections of 138 (c154, c173), 160–161 (c172, c189) and 170 (Do-not-reuse list). Fix: none. Record line for HISTORY.md: "161: stop chord as °7 embellishing V7 (A&S PDF 589), the return that puts right 138's turn to iv (PDF 598–599)."

### 3. Considered and rejected: turn the ♭VI at 160 minor, as K. 542 does.

PDF 601 (Ex. 32-21, image) gives the step that c443 did not test. In K. 542 the deceptive G major (♭VI) "is inflected to become G minor (♭VI♭)". The B–B♭ that results is the line that enlarges the opening motive. The score has a ready-made voice for this. Tbn 1 holds C4 through 160 and moves to B3 at 161. Inflecting it to C♭4 at 160.3, with the violas' C4 quavers, would give A♭ minor for two beats and make C♭/B a common tone into the °7 at 161. That is K. 542's enharmonic link at the same point in the form.

I reject it for three reasons:
- The A♭6 at 160 is the top of the movement's three-step climax ladder (82 < 122 < 160), and it is an A♭-major sound. A minor third under the fff peak takes the brightness away at the one moment the ladder is built for.
- c530 recorded that A♭ major must stay plain at 160 because movement II's key is A♭ and the finale's first chord reuses it.
- K. 542 needs the B–B♭ step to enlarge its own motive. This movement's motive is E♭–D–D♭–C, and it is already enlarged here by Tpt 3 and the violas, E♭ (160) – D (161) – C (162) in whole bars (c530 Finding 3). A C–C♭ adds nothing the motive asks for.

The other K. 542 point, a slow relaxation followed by a strong authentic cadence, was answered in c443 Finding 3: the strong cadence in C is withheld for the finale. Fix: none.

### Where the movement still falls short

Within this lens, the one shortfall later critics keep naming is the two-bar climb at 156–157 (c523). The reading adds nothing to it: Unit 32 is about keys and enharmonic links, and the climb is a single ♭VII–IV step inside C major. I did not reopen it, because c523's reasons (protected four times, the phrase is continuous from 156, the cost in bars) still hold. On the evidence of this reading I found no change that clearly improves the score.
