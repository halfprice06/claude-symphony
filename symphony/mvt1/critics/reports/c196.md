## Sources

The main source is the Sebesky chapter above. I quote three passages:
- p24: a slur means a "smooth connected line, usually in one breath".
- p71: the oboe's "lowest part of the range (F down to Bb)... These notes are also the most difficult to control, and unless the reed player is an accomplished oboist, it would be wise to avoid writing them".
- p64, Ex. 72: the bass clarinet chart, written in treble clef down to the low-C extension.

**Corpus.** I ran a music21 scan of wind stretches without a rest in Tchaikovsky 6/i (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml). The combined "Clarinetti in A" staff has a tongued sixteenth run of about 45 quarter beats starting at bar 187. Long fast runs are normal in this style, so the Cl 2 finding below is about breathing before a loud climax, not about stamina in general.

**Web.**
- Orchestration Online (https://orchestrationonline.com/oboe-lowest-octave/): the oboe "gets louder and coarser" as it descends, and a soft low B♭ is "a hallmark of inexperienced scoring".
- Vienna Symphonic Library (https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/woodwinds/bass-clarinet): "Nowadays music for the bass clarinet in Bb is written in treble clef". It describes the German bass clef combined with the French treble clef (Rimsky-Korsakov) as a historical mixed system.
- Steinberg forum thread (https://forums.steinberg.net/t/bass-clarinet-notation/1033274?page=2): players and editors agree that new parts use French treble notation.

**Score and parts.** I checked HISTORY.md through cycle 195. I viewed all 18 score pages. I also viewed these parts: Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Cl 2, B.cl, Bsn 1, Hn 1, Hn 3, Hn 4, Tpt 1 and Tbn 1.

## Findings

**1. Bass clarinet part, 91-106: mixed German/French notation (applied, parts only).**
- **Problem.** The part is in French treble clef (sounds a 9th lower) everywhere except 91-106. There it switches to bass clef in German notation (sounds a 2nd lower), with a text label. The player has to change transposition system mid-movement for 16 bars and then change back. This is the open c185 item "B.cl clef choice at 91-106".
- **Evidence.**
  - Sebesky p64 Ex. 72 writes the whole bass-clarinet range in treble clef, the low extension included.
  - VSL and the Steinberg thread (URLs above) say modern parts use French treble notation only, and the mixed German-bass/French-treble system is historical.
  - The part already reads 83-86 in treble down to the low-C notes, so 91-106 in bass clef is also inconsistent within the part.
  - The cost is 4-5 ledger lines on the written C3-D3 notes at 104-106, the same as at 83-86.
- **Fix.** In parts-clefs.ily: `bclClefs = { \clef treble }` (the score staff is concert pitch and is not affected). The definition of `bclBassClefMtwo` is left in place and unused.
- **Note.** This reverses c185's choice. The new evidence is the Sebesky example and the two web sources, and c185 itself left the item open.

**2. Cl 2, 117-123: about 13 s of tongued triplets, f to fff, with no rest before the climax (applied).**
- **Problem.**
  - Cl 2 plays continuous triplet eighths from 117 to 121, f with a cresc. at 121. It then holds B4 fff at 122 and 123, with the first rest on beat 4 of 123.
  - At q=126 that is 6.75 bars, about 12.9 s, with nowhere to breathe. The player arrives at the fff without a fresh breath.
  - c186's staggered breaths at 117-123 covered the brass only.
- **Evidence.**
  - Sebesky p24 treats a phrase as a breath unit.
  - Sebesky p42-43: an interrupted doubling is acceptable when an equal instrument keeps the line. Here the violas play the same triplets in unison.
  - Sebesky p44 on resting players before the important entry.
  - The Tchaikovsky run above shows the length alone is not unusual. The breath matters because it comes right before the crescendo to fff.
- **Fix.** dev.ily, `devClTwo`, bar 120:
  - Was `\repeat unfold 4 { \tuplet 3/2 { bes8 des' f' } }`.
  - Now `\repeat unfold 3 { \tuplet 3/2 { bes8 des' f' } } r4`.
  - This gives a quarter rest before the cresc. at 121. The run is now 4 s from 121 to 123, and 117-120 is 7.4 s. The violas keep the full figure.
- **Protected material.** No triplet numbers are added. The motto, the ladder and the brass layout are unchanged. The HISTORY do-not-touch list for 117-122 allows breaths (c186).

**3. Ob 1, 46, and Ob 2, 43: soft low F4/E♭4 at phrase ends (not applied; recommendation).**
- **Problem.**
  - Ob 1's link at 45-46 (`bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4)`) ends exposed, at p, on F4-E♭4. The c161 dotted cadence figure leads into the Poco meno mosso.
  - Ob 2 ends the 41-43 diminuendo on E♭4 at p. That case is milder, because Ob 1 doubles it an octave above.
- **Evidence.** Sebesky p71 (quoted above) and Orchestration Online (URL above): the oboe gets louder and coarser as it descends, and its soft low notes are hard to control.
- **Fix options.**
  - (a) Give 45-46 to the cor anglais, which rests here. Sebesky p72: the CA "does not become coarse or nasal in the bottom of its range". In trans.ily, add `transCorAnglais = { R1*12 | bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4) r4 | }` and replace Ob 1's 45-46 with `R1*2`.
  - (b) Keep the oboe and mark 46 `\pp` with no diminuendo, relying on the player.
- **Why not applied.** The phrase is accepted material (c161; HISTORY: "Ob 1 link at 45–46 uses the second theme's dotted cadence figure"), and the colour choice belongs to Dan. I recommend (a).

**4. Cor anglais, 5-9: one slur of about 19 s at q=50 (not applied).**
- **Problem.** The slur runs from D4 on beat 4 of 5 to the end of the tied G4 in 9 (16 beats). Under Sebesky p24 that is a single breath. At p/pp on a double reed the issue is stale air more than running out of air.
- **Why not applied.**
  - The only musical breath point is after A4 in 6, which would split the a'-g' resolution.
  - The tie into 9 is c150's overlap with Vln I.
  - The CA can take a quick breath before the tied G4 in 8 without restriking. This is accepted practice.
- **Proposed fix.** No score change. If Dan wants it marked, add a part-only marking at 8: `\tag #'part { s1*0^\markup \small \italic "breathe ad lib." }`.

**5. Hn 4 bass clef at 11-12 only two bars (no change).**
Sebesky p32 says to change clef only when a passage is long enough. The written notes at 11-12 are below written G3, which is Sebesky's own threshold. The clef changes fall in rests (c185). It follows the reading.

**6. Bassoon high notes in bass clef (no change).**
Bsn 1's highest note is F4 (28, 70, 133, 168). Sebesky p72 allows the bass clef "up to the F or G above the staff" and p73 (Ex. 92) warns against alternating clefs. The parts are correct as they are.

**7. Oboe unisons and Ob 2 above Fl 2 (no change).**
Sebesky p71 and p79-80 advise against oboe unisons and against the oboe above the flute. The oboe unisons occur only at f/ff tuttis (27, 69-72, 82, 133, 139-142, 158), where intonation conflicts are masked. Ob 2 above Fl 2 occurs at 82 (protected) and at 132 at ff. I do not propose a change.

**Checked with nothing to report.**
- Ranges (rng.py, all winds and brass) are within Sebesky's normal ranges, apart from Hn 1 at 106, which already has its ossia.
- There are no fast far-position trombone jumps (slide.py) and no horn sixteenths.
- Page turns in the parts I viewed fall in rests.
