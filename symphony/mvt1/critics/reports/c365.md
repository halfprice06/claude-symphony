## Findings

### 1. No cue before the entries at 96 in six parts (applied)

**Where.** The Cl 1, Hn 1, Hn 2, Tbn 1, B.tbn and Cbsn parts.

**Problem.** These six players rest 84–95, 12 bars at crotchet = 126, about 23 s. That is the whole fugato. They come back together at 96, f/ff, three of them on an off-beat (Cl 1 r8, Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1 r4.). There is no rehearsal letter between E (77) and F (97), so the count runs 20 bars without a landmark. Nothing in their parts shows them where they are. The audit lists this group as the only off-letter re-entry after more than 10 bars that has neither a letter nor a cue.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 299, item 6: "Cues are often necessary to facilitate re-entry after a long period of rest. Both the instrument playing that particular cue and the place where the cue begins should be clearly indicated … the cue should be transposed to fit the part into which it is written."
- Ex. XVIII-7 (PDF 300): a two-bar cue in small notes, labelled with its instrument, right before the entry.
- Adler PDF 299, item 4: "every player has detailed information about what is happening every moment".
- MOLA PDF 5: cues "audible and obvious".

**Fix.** The cue is the fourth fugato entry, Vln I at 94–95, the most audible line there.
- parts-gen.py, three octave-shifted quotes so each cue sits on or near the staff:
  ```
  \addQuote "cueVlnOneLow" { \transpose c c, \vlnOne }
  \addQuote "cueVlnOneLowII" { \transpose c c,, \vlnOne }
  \addQuote "cueVlnOneLowIII" { \transpose c c,,, \vlnOne }
  ```
- Table P entries:
  - Cl 1: `VI+("DOWN",94,2)`
  - Hn 1 and Hn 2: `VIL+("DOWN",94,2)`
  - Tbn 1 and B.tbn: `VIL2+("UP",94,2)`
  - Cbsn: `VIL3+("UP",94,2)`

**Result.** Each part reads "10 (84–93)", then "Vln. I" in small notes at 94–95, then the entry. Page counts and turns are unchanged; the score is unaffected.

### 2. "medium sticks" never appears in the score (applied)

**Where.** Timpani, bar 15 (tOne.ily).

**Problem.** The stick change from soft to medium sits on the whole-bar rest at 15. The timpani staff is empty in the 15–22 system, so RemoveEmptyStaves hides it, and the instruction is printed nowhere in the score. The conductor cannot see that the stop chord at 26 and the hammered strokes from 27 are played with different sticks from the opening roll. The part shows it correctly. Every other text on a rest (mutas, soft sticks at 176, damp at 127 and 164, harp marks) falls in a system where its staff is shown.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 291, item 3: "the score must contain every detail for every instrument, including special instructions".
- Adler PDF 291, item 1: the score must be clear, so that the conductor reads it as self-evident.

**Fix (tOne.ily).** The score prints it over bar 25, the last rest before the entry at 26, where the staff is visible. The part keeps it at 15, so the player has the whole 16–25 rest to change sticks:
```
\tag #'part { R1^\markup \small "medium sticks" | R1*10 } \tag #'score { R1*10 | R1^\markup \small "medium sticks" } | g,2.->\ff r4 | …
```
score.ly now passes the timpani through `\removeWithTag #'part`, like the other tagged variables (`<< \clef bass \global \removeWithTag #'part \timp >>`). full.ly already does this. The timpani part is unchanged.

### 3. Cl 1·2 at 126: natural sign under the slur (not applied; keep as is)

**Where.** dev.ily: Cl 1 `d'1-\tweak X-offset #-2 \p`, Cl 2 `\once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #1.7 c'4-\tweak X-offset #-2 \p( b2.)`.

**Problem.** Cl 2's C4 is offset to the right of Cl 1's semibreve D4, as Gould (PDF 66) prescribes for a second between parts. But the B♮3 natural touches the end of Cl 2's slur, and the p stands just before the bar's first note.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 291, item 2: alignment and legibility.

**What I tested.** Removing the shift and the tweaks lets LilyPond's default put the crotchet head into the semibreve, which is worse. I reverted that change in the scratch copy.

**Recommendation.** Leave bar 126 as it is. The mark is legible and the rhythm is clear.
