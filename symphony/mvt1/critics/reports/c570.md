### 2. The law of register in imitation holds at every answer in the movement (PDF 128, Ex. 251)

Ex. 251 shows the rule. The flutes' phrase is repeated an octave higher by the piccolo, not by a lower instrument straining upward. I checked every place in v570's full.midi where a phrase is answered or restated within a few bars:

- 1→5: B.cl/Vc G2 motto, then C.a. from D4, a fifth higher, in a higher instrument.
- 47→56: S head in Hn 1 + Vc from C4, then Vln I·II from C5.
- Fugato: Vc D3 (85), Vla + Bsn 1 G3 (88), Vln II C5 with C.a. an octave below (91), Vln I + Fl 1 F5 (94). Each higher entry goes to a higher instrument.
- 103–106 ↔ 107–110: in the invertible pair the S head moves from the tenor (Hn 1, Bsn 1, Vc) to the top (violas, then Fl 1), and the P head moves from Ob 2/Cl to the B.cl in the bass.
- 13–14 → 175–177: the horn call E♭3–C4–B3 (Hn 1) comes back as E♭4–C5–B4 in Cl 1.
- 175 → 177.4: the trombone motto from G3, then Vln I from G4.

None of these breaks the rule. The rule also explains why the cyclic answers in the later movements work: II 32–34 keeps the B.cl at movement I's pitch, and IV 243 puts Hn 2 at movement I's pitch with Hn 1 and Tpt 2 an octave higher. Fix: none.

### 3. Considered and rejected: Hn 1 doubling Cl 1 on the coda horn call (175–177)

This is the one place where this reading's two principles pull apart, and it is the strongest candidate I found for a cyclic improvement. The preface says an idea's colour is "native to it from the hour of its birth" (PDF 20). The horn call was born in a solo horn (13–14), the scherzo quotes it in Hn 1 (III 3–6), and the finale answers it in Hn 2 at pitch, with Hn 1 an octave higher (IV 243). Movement I's last statement is the only one without a horn. If Hn 1 doubled Cl 1 at the unison, pp (E♭4–C5–B4, written B♭4–G5–F♯5), the horn colour would return at the octave the finale's Hn 1 later uses.

I reject it for three reasons:

- An earlier critic rejected a horn on this line because horn and trombones have affinity and merge (Adler adler2 PDF 188–189). Rimsky-Korsakov's own recorded fault is a line that is doubled and still lost under brass of the same weight (PDF 18, Golden Cockerel 233). In a unison with the clarinet the horn would pull the line toward the pp trombone chord that sits just below it (Tbn 1 E♭4–D4 at 176–177). The line would lose the one feature that makes it heard, a reed colour standing apart from the brass. Nothing in this reading answers that reason.
- The law of register (PDF 128) is already satisfied by the clarinet. A higher answer is given to a higher instrument.
- The finale's horn call at 243 is the arrival, in major, with the B resolved. Keeping the horn out of movement I's last statement makes the horn colour at IV 243 fresher, in the same way the reading keeps the echo and muted brass for the finale (row 22).

Applied in the scratch copy: no.

### 5. Where the movement could still fall short, and why no change meets the bar

I asked where a listener might lose the thread across the four movements. The weakest link is the horn call. It is heard once in solo horn (13–14), once in four low horns (41–42), and once pp in the clarinet inside the coda (175–177). Of the movement I ideas the later movements answer, it has the fewest clear statements. A fourth statement would have to go into the development, whose scoring is protected (77–130), or into the climb at 156–157, where c544 found every addition blocked by protections and c563 left the climb protected. Finding 3 rejects the colour change. I found no addition that the reading supports and that does not cost a reserve or a protected passage. So it stays.
