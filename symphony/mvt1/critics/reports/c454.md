## Findings

This pass finds no change that meets the bar. Each item below is something I tested against the reading and the masters. In each case the score's present solution is the right one, for the reason given.

### 1. Bar 82, the first A♭ climax, is lower and lighter than the exposition tutti at 31 (checked, keep)

**Question.** At 82 the top is E♭6 (Fl 1·2, Vln I) with 25 players. At 31 the P tutti already reaches A♭6 (Fl 1 + Vln I) with 25 players, and at 72 the codetta reaches A♭6 with 27. Measured by register and players, the first of the "three A♭ climaxes" is not above the exposition tuttis. Would 82 land harder if it went to A♭6?

**Assessment.** No. Adler PDF 150 names the purpose of a multi-octave unison like this one: to "state a major melody or gesture before it is contrapuntally developed", and his example is Mozart 40/iv, a partial tutti in octaves that leads directly into the fugal development. That is exactly the job of 82: one bar of the head in three octaves, the 83 strokes, sub. p at 84 and the fugato at 85. Mozart's passage is not a climax in register either; it is a statement. Raising 82 would turn a statement into a peak one bar before the music drops to a single cello line, and it would remove the step between 82 (ff, no percussion) and 122 (fff, tam-tam, A♭6), which the movement's climax order needs. The octave weights at 82 also follow PDF 151 now (c254, c344): reed and string colour in every octave, with Tpt 1 in the middle. Keep.

### 2. Bars 97–100: Fl 1 alone above Ob 1 + Vln I on the ff B-minor S (checked, keep)

**Question.** PDF 151 says the flute should not be "left alone in its upper reaches", and PDF 152 shows Beethoven putting the top octave in two flutes. At 97.4–100 Fl 1 alone plays D6–G6 above Ob 1 and Vln I (D5–G5), with C.a., Vln II sul G and Vc a further octave down. Fl 2 rests.

**Assessment.** Adler's sentence about Mozart is about a flute left without support on top of a passage it would then have to abandon. Here Fl 1 plays the whole phrase to its end, and PDF 152 says the high flute octave "is so piercing it need not be doubled by any other instrument to cut through". The tune's weight is meant to sit in the two lower octaves (PDF 151): Ob 1 + Vln I and C.a. + Vln II + Vc, with the brass counter (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1, Bsn 1, f) in between. A second flute on the top octave would make the high octave sound like the theme, which is the effect of 158–161, the S climax. 97–102 should stay one level below it. c289, c424 and c431 reached the same conclusion from other readings. Keep.

### 3. Bars 117–121: Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2 ff in unison on the motto (checked, keep)

d'Indy marks the trombones ppp in a unison with horns (PDF 147–148). At 117–121 both pairs are ff. Adler's case is a whole-orchestra unison in which the trombones only colour the line. Here the motto is the foreground and must carry over ff P calls in winds and violins (Fl, Ob 1/2, Vln I/II) and the triplet undercurrent (Cl 2, Vla). The trombones are the motto's carriers in this movement (175–179 chorale). The d'Indy texture of mixed articulation (PDF 148) is present: held brass, slurred triplets, measured cello semiquavers. c344 and c424 kept this. Keep.

### 4. Bars 131–134 and 167–169: brass on the quick notes of the P head (checked, keep)

Smetana (PDF 154, Ex. XV-5) keeps the brass on sustained chords so the quick figure stays light. At 131–134 Tbn 1·2 play the head (with Bsn a2 and Vc), and at 167–169 Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2 play it a2. Both are deliberate. At 131 the recapitulation states P in the low octave as a weight against the high descant (Fl, Ob, Vln I). The trombones are marked f under ff (c144), as d'Indy marks them (PDF 147). The quick-note brass are also not alone: Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2 and B.tbn/Tba hold the harmony, which is Smetana's cohesion. 167–169 is the summing-up (PDF 150) and should be heavy. It is also the material the finale answers, so a change there would reach other movements. Keep both.

### 5. Bars 47 and 147: S enters together with its accompaniment (checked, keep)

Weber (PDF 156, Ex. XV-7) starts the strings before the horns so that the horn entry sounds fresh. At 47 harp, cellos and Hn 1 start together on the pickup, and at 147 Vln II sul tasto, Hn 1, Vla and Vc start together. In both places a different device does the same work. At 47 the poco rit. and the bar of string-only V at 45–46 serve as the "rest before a colour" (PDF 156). At 147 the soft C-major tremolo is new colour in itself after the p rit. of 144–146. In both bars, holding back the melody by a bar would push the S head off its downbeat and change the theme's phrase rhythm, which other movements quote. The Weber principle is used where it fits: at 13 (string chord, then the horn call) and 73 (strings and Hn 4 pedal, then Fl 1 at 73.4). Keep.

### 6. Bar 160: the low octave of the tune is heavier than the middle (checked, keep)

At 160.1 the tune's three octaves are C6 (Fl 1·2, Vln I), C5 (Vln II, Ob 2, Tpt 1) and C4 (Hn 1·2, Vla, Vc), with Tbn 1 also on C4. By Mozart's rule (PDF 151) the heaviest octave tells the listener which octave is the theme. Counting by Rimsky-Korsakov's ff equivalences (rimsky-principles PDF 51: one trumpet = two horns), the middle octave (Tpt 1 = 2 horns, plus Ob 2 and Vln II) and the low octave (Hn 1·2 plus Vla and Vc) are close to equal. The A♭6 peak on beat 2 lies in the top octave, which two flutes and Vln I carry as Beethoven does (PDF 152). The result is a double-octave climax in which no octave is missing weight. This passage is protected (c344, c431, c449). Keep.

### What I would have changed, and why I did not

The one shortfall I can still hear in the score within this lens is that the first group's tutti at 27–32 and the recapitulation tutti at 131–142 use the same graded brass layout. Adler's Barber example (PDF 150) varies the doubling a little each time a germ motive returns. But c254 through c444 have shaped 131–142 as a separate event: low P head at 131, the descant on top, the hemiola at 135–137, the stop chord at 138, and the D♭ bass and Fl 1 A♭6 at 140. The finale also quotes 27–32 at pitch. I found no change to 131–142 that is clearly better and also keeps those protections, so I propose none.
