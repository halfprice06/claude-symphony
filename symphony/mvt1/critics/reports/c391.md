## Findings

Under this reading nothing in the motto, P, S or the horn call stands between this score and a master's work. I made no change to the score. Finding 1 is a correction to the records. Findings 2–5 are second-pass checks; each says what I tested, what I found, and why the passage should stay.

### 1. Bars 23–26 and 135–138 are not a hemiola in the book's sense (records only; not applied)

**What is wrong.** HISTORY.md, mvt1.md and several section-file comments call 23–26 and 135–138 a "hemiola" (cycles 7, 17, 89, 238, 268, 297). A&S PDF 51 restricts hemiola to triple and compound metres: "two groups of three beats" become "three groups of two beats", so two bars of 3/4 sound like one bar of 3/2 (Ex. 3-8, Mozart K. 283, bars 8–9). Bars 23–26 do something else. Four slurred groups of three crotchets (E♭5–D5–C5 | F5–E♭5–D5 | G5–F5–E♭5 | A♭5–G5–F5) run across three bars of 4/4, so a 3/4 pattern is heard inside the 4/4 until the B5 on the downbeat of 26 restores the metre. That is the device of Ex. 3-7 (Brahms, Violin Concerto, a 5/4 pattern inside 3/4), which the book describes as "a temporary change of meter".

**Why it matters.** It does not matter for the sound, and the passage works. Each group starts a step higher (E♭–F–G–A♭), so the group's first note carries the step-wise rise to the leading tone. The slurs mark the groups, the bass keeps the bar in minims, and the stop chord on a downbeat ends the displacement, as the book says the metre must return. It matters for the records, because the scherzo's second idea (three minims over two bars of 3/4, coherence/scherzo.md line 31) is a true hemiola. If both are called hemiola, a later critic may "match" them, and they are different devices.

**Fix.** No score change. In mvt1.md and in future HISTORY lines, read "3/4 grouping over 4/4 (A&S Ex. 3-7 type)" for 23–26 and 135–138. Keep the protection on these bars (cycles 7 and 17).

### 2. Every accented dissonance in the theme lines is a suspension or an appoggiatura (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 54: dissonances are normally short and on weak beats. On strong beats only accented passing notes, accented neighbours, appoggiaturas (PDF 54–55, Ex. 3-15) and suspensions (PDF 55, Ex. 3-16 to 3-18) are normal. I went through the melody of every statement of the motto, P, S and the horn call against its bass.

**Found.**
- S, 47–50, over I–IV–ii–V (Cb pizz. A♭, D♭, B♭, E♭): the only dissonance on a strong beat is the A♭4 at 50.1 over E♭. It comes by leap from F4 and falls to G4 on beat 3, an appoggiatura of the Ex. 3-15c kind. It is a minim, so it is long, which the book allows after 1600 (PDF 54). B♭3 at 47.4.5 and E♭4 at 48.4 are passing notes on weak parts of the bar.
- S restatement and climax: B♭♭5 at 60.4.5 and D♭6 at 156.4.5 are chromatic passing notes on the last eighth. The 4–3s at 66→67 and 164→165 are tied over the bar line (c232), the Ex. 3-17a form.
- Horn call 13–14: the C4 is tied from beat 3 into 14 and resolves to B3 on beat 2, a suspension from syncopation (Ex. 3-16).
- Coda duet, 172–173: the C.a.'s motto ties D5 across the bar line while the solo cello moves to C4 on the downbeat of 173. The D5 becomes a 9–8 suspension and resolves to C5 on beat 2. This is the book's Ex. 3-17a exactly: one tone takes over part of the next tone's time. It turns the motto's plain D–C into a suspension against the second theme. The two themes are bound together at the moment they meet.

**Keep.** The coda is protected (c228, c249, c328), and this check gives one more reason.

### 3. Tempo: S is broader than its metronome change (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 49: the sense of tempo comes from the pace of the beats, "the frequency with which chords change", and "the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody".

**Found.** The metronome step at 47 is small (crotchet 126 to 112, 11%). The other two measures in the book's sentence widen it. Under P the bass and harmony change every minim (15–18: C–D | E♭–D | F–A♭ | G–G). Under S they change every bar (47–50). The main tones of P move in crotchets and dotted crotchets. Those of S move in dotted minims and minims. So S's harmony is half as fast, and S sounds much slower than the metronome alone would make it. That is how Dvořák (Symphony 9/i, the G-major flute theme) and Tchaikovsky (Symphony 6/i, the D-major theme) get their contrast without a large tempo change. No change is needed: a larger metronome step would make 56–68 drag, because the restatement already slows through 'rit.' at 66–68.

### 4. The introduction's hypermeter begins after the bar line (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 53: in a group of four bars the first and third are strong. The introduction opens with a bar that holds only the timpani roll and the motto's pickup.

**Found.** The groups run 2–5, 6–9 and 10–13, each preceded by a bar that holds only a pickup (bar 1: G2 on beat 4; bar 5: the C.a.'s D4 on beat 4; bar 9: Vln I's G4 on beat 4). The strong bars are 2, 6 and 10, where the top of the rising sixth lands. The horn call at 13–14 then overlaps the last group, like Ex. 3-11. The listener therefore hears the sixth three times on a hypermetric downbeat before the Allegro (A&S PDF 51, the "simplest and most natural" placement). This explains why the sixth is the most memorable interval of the movement. **Keep** (protected 1–4, 5–8, 9–13).

### 5. The pitch peak of P is the syncopation the book describes (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 50–51: a high note carries a built-in accent, and a note held from a weak beat through a strong one "conflicts—sometimes very strongly—with the meter". P's climax is the D♭6 at 20.

**Found.** The D♭6 enters on beat 3 of 20, sf, and is tied into the downbeat of 21, the hypermetric strong bar of the consequent (19–22). Three accents come together in this note: register, length and the chromatic ♭II. It then falls by step to the cadence at 22.3, so the period ends with the head's widening leaps answered (minor sixth 15, minor seventh 17, octave 19, then the D♭6). Tchaikovsky's Symphony 5/i first theme has the same plan: three rising heads, then a syncopated peak. **Keep** (15–22 protected since cycle 11; P head rhythm, c301).
