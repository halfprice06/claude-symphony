# Critic 356: playability, instrument by instrument (brass, second pass)

Version reviewed: v356. Lens 6 (playability). Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 9 "Introduction to Brass Instruments" and ch. 10 "Individual Brass Instruments" (adler1 PDF 282–329, adler2 PDF 1–13), second pass after c266. I read every text page in the range. I rendered every page of music examples at 110 dpi and looked at each one (adler1 PDF 284–292, 296–297, 301–311, 313–329; adler2 PDF 1–5, 7–12). I used the images, not the OCR, for every range chart and register chart (X-2, X-4, X-5, X-45, X-46, X-68, X-69, X-70, X-71, X-72, X-73, X-74, X-85, X-86) and for the examples I cite (IX-9, X-24, X-80, X-87).

Method. I extracted every brass note from v356's full.midi (one staff per player) and timed it in seconds against the tempo map, including every `\mt` step. For each player I listed: (a) every unbroken span of playing of 7 s or more, (b) every stretch with no rest of a full bar, with the share of time spent playing, (c) soft entries and sustained notes above written F5 for the horns, (d) trumpet notes below middle C and above G5, (e) leaps of a minor seventh or more with no rest, and (f) short notes at the bottom of the tuba and bass trombone. Then I read each flagged place in the section files for slurs, ties, `\breathe` marks and dynamics, and checked the parts (build-parts.sh) for layout and page turns.

## Findings

The brass writing of v356 is close to faultless against this chapter. Earlier cycles (c087, c156, c176, c186, c266) fixed the long spans in the climaxes, so this pass found one small gap and confirmed the rest. The findings are ranked. Only Finding 1 changes the score.

### 1. Hn 3·4 at 19–26 and 37–42: no breath in the exposition where the recapitulation has one (applied)

**Bars and problem.** Hn 3 plays from 19.1 to 26.3 with no rest: p semibreves at 19–22 (C4, D♭4, D♭4–B3, B3–D4–C4), then the mp< hemiola at 23–25 into the ff stop chord at 26. That is 14.8 s, ending ff. Hn 4 plays 19.1–25.3 (12.9 s) and has had a quarter rest at 25.4 since c259. In the recapitulation the same Hn 3 line (131–138) has a printed breath at the end of 134 (c176), at the seam before the hemiola. Bar 22 has no breath mark in the exposition. In the transition, Hn 3·4 play 37.1–43.2 with no rest. They hold p semibreves rising to f at 40 and ff into the slurred four-horn call at 41–42. That is 11.9 s from p to ff. Hn 1·2 enter the call fresh on 41.4.

**Evidence.** Adler: brass "require a great deal more wind than the woodwinds... one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath" (adler1 PDF 292). On the same page: "it does take more breath to play loudly than softly." Both spans end at ff. PDF 303: "periodic rests are most welcome." Ex. IX-9 (PDF 290) shows each loud brass attack struck after a release.

**Fix.** These are unslurred notes, so a player can take a breath by clipping a note. The mark puts the breath at the musical seam instead of leaving it to chance, as c176 did at 134. tOne.ily:
```
tOneHnThree: b4 d'4 c'2 \breathe | \clef bass c'2.\mp\<-> ...
tOneHnFour:  g2 g2 \breathe | c2.\mp\<-> g4->~ ...
```
(the breath falls on the repeated note C4–C4 in Hn 3 and G3→C3 in Hn 4, before the hemiola; 7.6 s before it, 7.1 s after it). trans.ily:
```
transHnThree: R1*4 | des'1\p\< | c'1 | d'1 \breathe |
transHnFour:  R1*4 | ges1\p\< | aes1 | f1 \breathe |
```
This is at the end of 39, the repeated D4 in Hn 3, 5.7 s before it and 6.2 s after it. The breath comes before the f bar, so the horns go into the ff call with fresh air. No note, tie, dynamic or hairpin changes. The hemiola 23–26 (protected) keeps all its accented units; only the p minim before it is clipped. The stop chord at 26 is unchanged. Each staff shows one comma (checked on score pages 3 and 5), and both parts print it (Hn 3 part page 2).

### 2. Hn 1, 146–170: 52.5 s of playing with rests only at 153 and 163 (keep)

Hn 1 plays the S head (146.4–152), 154–157, the climax line 158–162 (ff–fff, breath at 159.4), 164–166 and the coda P head at 167–170. It plays 89 % of the time. Adler warns against "the higher register all the time" (PDF 303) and asks for lip rests (PDF 292). But the line lies mostly at written G4–E♭5, in the "bright and heroic" register of Ex. X-5. Only one note reaches written G5 (150, inside the S head's swell). The two full-bar rests and the breath at 159 fall at phrase ends. A first horn carries this load in the Romantic repertoire Adler quotes: Ex. X-24, Tchaikovsky 5/ii, is a long solo of similar register. The passage is protected (147–155, 147–162 layout, climax scoring 158–166), and the reading gives no reason to reopen it. Keep.

### 3. Hn 1·2, 119.4–123: one breath from the motto's D through the fff A♭ (keep)

Since c186 the horns breathe before the D at 119.4, and the trombones breathe at the end of 121 (staggered on purpose, so the motto line never stops in all brass at once). From the breath to the release at 124 the horns play four notes in 9.1 s (allargando, ff< fff >). Adler's limit in PDF 292 is "too many notes on one breath in a slow tempo". Four notes in 9 s is not too many, and the line lies at written G4–E♭5. Adding a horn breath at 121.4.5 would silence C4 in every brass part on the same eighth. Keep.

### 4. Trombone chorale slur 177–179: 12.7 s at pp (keep)

Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn hold one slur from 177 through 179, across the rit. and Tempo I: 3.6 s + 4.3 s + 4.8 s. Adler: a slurred phrase is one breath (PDF 309; adler2 PDF 3). But soft playing takes less air than loud (PDF 292), and Brahms writes his trombone chorale p dolce in long slurs (Ex. X-80, adler2 PDF 4). The players breathe at the slur break 176/177 and after 179 (c156). c266 also kept it. Keep.

### 5. Hn 4 at written G5 (158, 160) and its drop to written D4 at 161.3; Hn 1 written F♯5→D4 at 150→151 (keep)

Hn 4 (a low horn) doubles Hn 3 a2 on the counter-line to written G5 at fff. That is the top of the cor basso range in Ex. X-2 (adler1 PDF 301). At 161.3 Hn 4 leaves written E♭5 for written D4, a minor ninth down, while Hn 3 takes G4. Adler warns about wide leaps (PDF 304). But a falling leap at ff to the bottom of the "deep and solid" register is the easy direction to find. The G5s come after rests at 147–155. At 150→151 Hn 1 falls a tenth at a phrase seam, at mp, from the end of a 9.1 s slur onto a new phrase doubling the cellos. The seam is the breathing place, and the lower note is in the horn's most secure register (Ex. X-5). The climax layout was decided in c181 and kept by c266. Keep.
