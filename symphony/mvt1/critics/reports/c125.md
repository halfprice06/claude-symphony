# Critic 125: engraving and notation (Gould, *Behind Bars*, ch. 1 "Beams", PDF 31–41)

Version reviewed: v144. Scratch copy with fixes: /tmp/critic-125/work (build clean: lilypond rc=0, no warnings, 17 pages, parallels check shows only the known "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1").

## What I studied

I read Gould PDF 31–41 in full (Beams: design, placing within the stave, stem length, angled beams, direction of beam angle, stem direction on beamed groups, dividing beams, beams with opposite stems; Ledger lines PDF 40–41) and viewed every page as an image at 110 dpi. I then looked at all 17 pages of the score at 100 dpi and zoomed to 220–300 dpi on every passage with beamed groups. I also read the part-combiner source of LilyPond 2.24.3 (`scm/lily/part-combiner.scm`, `ly/music-functions-init.ly`) to find out why some solos print with up-stems, and tested each fix in small files and then in the full build. Supporting reference: Gould's double-stemmed writing (PDF 66–67, ch. 2), which is outside the assigned range. I used no web sources. The corpus was not needed for these notation points.

## Findings

**F1 (applied). Bars 33–40 and 77–78: solo lines on shared staves print with up-stems, so beams and stems stick out above the staff.** In LilyPond 2.24 the part-combiner marks a passage as solo only when no hairpin is active (`try-solo` in part-combiner.scm). Each of these solos carries a hairpin, so it stays in the "apart" voice "one", which has `\voiceOne` and therefore up-stems. Examples: Fl 1 at 40 has sixteenths B♭5–F♯6 on two to three ledger lines with their beam above them. Ob 1 has the same at 39–40. Bn 1 at 33–36 and 77–78 is in the upper half of the bass staff with up-stems throughout. Gould PDF 38 says the note farthest from the middle line sets the stem direction, and that short stems are better than many long ones. PDF 33 and 35 put beams for ledger-line notes close to the notes. A single line on a shared staff takes normal stem directions (Gould PDF 66–67, double-stemmed writing applies only while both parts sound).
Fix, in global.ily:
```
soloStemsOn = \context Voice = "one" \oneVoice
soloStemsOff = \context Voice = "one" \voiceOne
```
`\soloStemsOn` goes at the start of each solo and `\soloStemsOff` where the second player comes back in. In trans.ily: Fl 1 (`\partCombineSoloI \soloStemsOn r2^…` … `\partCombineAutomatic \soloStemsOff g'''1`), Ob 1 37–40, Cl 1 37–38, Bn 1 33–36 (off before `ges1` at 37). In dev.ily: Bn 1 77–78 (off before `R1` at 79). In the build, stems now point down, beams sit at the staff, and two-voice writing comes back correctly at 41 and 80.

**F2 (not applied; open). Bars 158–162: Flutes a2 (and Fl 1 at 162) have up-stems on notes four to five ledger lines above the staff.** The stems add 3½ spaces above C7, G♭7 and A♭7, and the beam of `aes'''8 g'''8` at 160 sits far from the staff. This is the worst instance of the PDF 38 problem, and the cramped top of page 15 shows it. When I compiled the flute parts of recTwo.ily alone, LilyPond printed these bars with down-stems in the "shared" voice. In the full score they come out up. `\context Voice = "shared" \oneVoice` at 158 changed nothing, so I reverted it. The likely cause is a direction set earlier in the movement on the same staff. Suggested next step for the composer: put `\stemNeutral` in *both* `recTwoFluteOne` and `recTwoFluteTwo` at 158, or test `\partCombineUnisono` replaced by `\partCombineApart` with Fl 2 using `\voiceOne`-free hidden notes. Check page 15 after either change. This does not touch the protected climax scoring 158–166, because no note changes.

**F3 (not applied; open). Bars 88–90: Bn 1 fugato entry prints with up-stems** (E♭4, D4, D♭4 in the bass clef, beams above the staff). Here the part-combiner does detect a solo, and debugging showed the notes are in the voice "solo". Even so, `\context Voice = "solo" \stemNeutral` left the stems up. The cause is the same as in F2 and needs one fix in score.ly. Gould PDF 38: most of these notes are above the middle line, so the stems should point down. The fugato notes are protected and are not affected.

**F4 (applied). Bar 139, Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2: the label "2." is printed where both players play.** Player 1 starts with an eighth rest and player 2 holds a whole note, so for one eighth the combiner sees "player 2 alone" and prints "2.". The rest of the bar is in two voices, so a reader takes "2." to mean player 1 is silent. This is a part-combining label error (lens item; no Gould page in my range covers labels). Fix in recOne.ily: `\partCombineApart` at 139 in `recOneFluteOne`, `recOneOboeOne` and `recOneClOne`, and `\partCombineAutomatic` again at 143. The Cl 1 `\partCombineAutomatic` that ended 138 is moved to 143, so Apart covers 135–142. The correct "2." for Ob 2 alone at 143 is kept.

**F5 (applied). Harp RH 47–63: four-note broken-chord groups have beams slanting about two spaces, and at 47, 50 and 54 the beam cuts across the stave lines below the notes.** Gould PDF 33: "the wider the interval, the more flattened the beam angle". PDF 34: "the beam of shorter groups should not cross more than one stave-space", and "avoid steep angles through stave-lines" (lattice). Fix in tTwo.ily: `\override Beam.damping = #3` at the start of `tTwoHarpR`, `\revert Beam.damping` after bar 63. The slopes are now about one space. Notes, slurs and layout are unchanged, and the page count is still 17. This does not touch the protected harp voicings or the 47–55 scoring.

**Checked and found correct (Gould PDF 31–41):** metric beaming in every 4/4 bar. Horizontal beams on repeated notes (Cb 27–31, 82, 131–134, 167–169; Vc 127–130; Timp 82, 134). Concave figures (Vla 97–102). Triplet groupings at 117–121 and 129 (numbers given once, as cycle 55 decided). Sixteenth and thirty-second beams at 39–40, 111–116, 130 and 152. No beam runs through a clef change (the cello clefs at 46, 146, 157 and 161 stand before the group, per cycle 115). No kneed beams. Ledger lines in overlapping parts at 41–42 and 160 do not cut stems.
