## Findings

No score change. The timpani and percussion writing meets every rule in the assigned reading. On this third pass I also looked for places where, within this lens, the timpani or percussion fall short of a master's use. I did not find one that a change would improve. Below are the checks behind that verdict and the three changes I considered and rejected.

### Checks that found nothing to change

- **Harmony check (Adler PDF 58, 79).** I re-ran it on full.midi built from v476. It gives the same result as c366, with the c394 (141 ends in one stroke) and c419 (roll fades through 4) changes included. The five "not in chord" attacks are the dominant G under vii°7 at 26, 28.3, 138 and 161, and the C root at 30. These are deliberate dominant and tonic pedal strokes, not the out-of-tune near-misses PDF 58 describes. The ones at 26–32 are also quoted at pitch by the finale (IV 199–204). The timpani are never below the bass. At 11 the A♭2 roll sits over the D2 bass of iiø7 and becomes the bass only at the German sixth on 12, where the bass instruments also move to A♭.
- **Drum moves.** 40→41 (B♭ roll on the 28" to E♭ roll on the 23") and 71–72 (E♭–A♭–E♭–A♭ crotchets at crotchet = 126) cross the C drum. Both are at ordinary speeds. 159–161 (G–A♭–G rolls) are on adjacent drums. All other moves are over one drum or between adjacent drums.
- **Retunings (PDF 79, 81).** A♭→B♭ (whole tone) in 6 bars at crotchet = 126, about 11 s. B♭→A♭ with 25 bars free. A♭→B♮ in 13 bars. B♮→A♭ in 17 bars, including the soft bars 103–110 where the player can check the pitch quietly. All four are on the 28" drum and inside F2–C3. The score (page 9) and the part print "muta B♮ in A♭" correctly at 98, with the natural sign.
- **Attacca.** The timpani end on a ppp C3 roll under the fermata and release with the basses, harp and tam-tam. Movement II's A♭2 roll at its bar 2 is on the 28" drum, which has been at A♭ since 98.
- **Parts and page turns.** I built the parts in the scratch copy. Timpani: cover page plus one page of music, no turns. Mutas are at 34, 44, 84 and 98, stick texts at 1, 15 and 175, and cues at 47–48 and 113–114 (Vc.). Percussion: one page. Every part that crosses into movement II ends on an even page (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc 2 pp; Hn 3 2 pp).

### Considered and kept (no change)

1. **The single B2 stroke at 97, which takes two retunings (84, 98).** Each retune is safe in time (rule 12). The stroke reinforces the low ff arrival in B minor (Cb, Cbsn, B.tbn on B1/B2), and that arrival is protected ("low arrival at 97", c134). I considered dropping the stroke to save two retunings. The arrival would then lose the only percussive weight in the development's key chain, so that is worse. I also considered a roll through 97–100. The block ends on a half cadence on F♯ (101–102), which the drums cannot play, so a roll would leave the drums sitting on the tonic under the dominant. The single stroke is the right use. It should stay as it is.
2. **Coda tam-tam (175) written as an l.v. tie with "damp" at the release of 182, while 122–127 and 160–164 use tied written values.** PDF 71 asks only that the length of the sound be fixed, and "damp" at the release of 182 does that. A semibreve tied across eight bars and into the fermata would show the same length with more ink, and the fermata-bar "damp" would still be needed. This is not worth changing.
3. **Playing near the rim for the ppp rolls 1–4 and 181–182 (PDF 80).** The rim spot thins the fundamental. At 1–4 the timpani C3 is the only C below the motto in the B.cl and cellos, and at 181–182 it doubles the cellos and harp in the final C-major chord. A thinner pitch would weaken both. Soft sticks at the normal spot give the soft colour without that loss. Same verdict as c366, item 14.
