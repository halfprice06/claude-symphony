## Findings

Under this reading I found nothing in the motto, P, S or the horn call that stands between this score and a master's work, and I made no change to the score. F1–F3 are new whole-score checks. F4 lists the improvements I weighed in answer to the "master" question and why I did not propose them.

### F1. No note starts off the beat and crosses the half-bar (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 49, Ex. 3-5c: in 4/4, crotchet–quaver–dotted crotchet–crotchet (the dotted crotchet starting on 2-and and crossing beat 3) is "less clear" than the same rhythm with a tie at beat 3. The earlier passes checked only the theme lines. I scanned every note in all 30 staves of v501 for a note that begins on a fraction of a beat and is still sounding at beat 3 without a tie at that point.

**Found.** None. The P head's dotted crotchet always begins on beat 2 (15, 17, 19, 27, 82, 85, 117, 131, 167), which is a beat, so the half-bar is not hidden. Every off-beat entry that crosses beat 3 is written with a tie at the beat (Vln II and violas at 15, 17, 19, `r4 r8 g'8~ g'2`). The notation shows the metre wherever the rhythm contradicts it, as the book asks.

**Keep.**

### F2. Bar 85: the only bar of the Allegro in which no part sounds beat 1 (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 51: a syncopation is "a rhythmic accent at a metrically weak place", and it conflicts with the metre. PDF 51–52 (Ex. 3-7): if the contradicting accents form a consistent pattern, the listener may hear a temporary new metre. The P head puts its long, accented note on beat 2 and leaves beat 1 empty. In every other statement another part marks beat 1 (the basses' quavers at 15–22, the minims at 27–32, and so on). I counted the attacks on each beat of every bar from 15 to 182.

**Found.** Bar 85 is the only bar from 15 to 182 with no attack on beat 1 in any part. (Bar 4, the held motto C in the Lento, is the only other bar.) At 85 the cellos play the fugato subject alone: `r8 d8 bes4.-> a8 aes8 g8 | g4 fis8 g8 es4.-> d8 | g4 a4 bes4 b4`. Heard without a metre, the B♭3 on beat 2 could be taken as a downbeat, and 85–87 would then sound displaced by one beat until the countersubject's C4 on the downbeat of 88.

**Why it stays.** Four reasons, the first from the reading.
- The metre is established when the subject enters. Bar 83 has the full orchestra on beats 1 and 3, ff. Bar 84 has the bare G octave, sub. p, starting on beat 1, and the cello releases it on the downbeat of 85. A syncopation needs a metre to conflict with (PDF 51), and that metre has just been sounded. In Ex. 3-6 the bass chords keep beat 1. Here the strokes of 83 and the held bar of 84 have already set the count, so one bar without a downbeat does not overturn it.
- The listener already knows where this rhythm's long note falls. The head has been heard with its accompaniment at 15–22, 27–30, 33–39, 69–71 and 77–82, always with the long note on beat 2.
- The cello marks beat 1 itself at 86 (G3) and at 87 (G3–A3–B♭3–B3 in crotchets), so any doubt lasts about one bar.
- Cycle 227 removed the double basses' pizzicato notes under 85–87 so that the subject is heard alone (Adler, adler1 PDF 145; PDF 144, Ex. V-21; PDF 147, Ex. V-23). A soft Cb pizz. G1 on 85.1, the only fix the reading would suggest, would partly undo that. It would also give the subject a bass note that the other three entries (88, 91, 94) do not have. I do not think the risk is large enough to reverse c227.

**Keep.** Fugato 85–96 is protected (c107, c142, c227).

### F3. Every theme's pitch peak is on a strong bar or tied into one (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 50: very high notes carry a built-in accent. PDF 51: the simplest rhythms put accents where the metre has them. PDF 53: in a four-bar group, bars 1 and 3 are strong. A peak that falls on a weak bar without a reason sounds unplanned. I placed the highest note of each theme statement in its group.

**Found.**
- P, 15–22: D♭6 enters at 20.3 and is tied into 21, bar 3 of 19–22.
- S, 47–55: F5 enters at 52.4 and is tied into 53, bar 3 of 51–54.
- S restatement, 56–68: the long C6 at 62 falls on bar 3 of 60–63, and the apex B♭5 at 66.1 on bar 3 of 64–67. The passing D♭6 at 63 is on a weak bar; c301 kept it so that S does not copy P's syncopated D♭6 peak.
- S in B minor, 97–102: the peak D6 is on 102, bar 5 counting from 98, a strong bar.
- Wedge, 111–116: D6 at 116 is the tonal goal at the end of the group, the Ex. 3-13 case (PDF 54).
- Retransition: A♭6 fff at 122 is bar 1 of 122–126.
- Recapitulation climax: G5–E6 at 158 and C6–A♭6 at 160, with 160 bar 1 of 160–163.
- Coda mottos: E♭5 at 172 and 178 on downbeats.
- The A♭6 at 140 (Fl 1) is on bar 2 of 139–142. That is where the P head's own syncopation puts the high note of its second bar, as in 16 and 28 (c468).

**Keep.**

### F4. Improvements weighed and rejected

The task asks where, within this lens, the music falls short of a master's work. These are the candidates I weighed. None meets the bar.

- **A soft bass pizzicato on 85.1** (F2). Rejected for the reasons given there.
- **Strict augmentation of the last Vln I motto, 177.4–182.** In the original motto (1–4) the D♭ falls on beat 3: `g4 | es2.~ es8 d8 | c2 des4 c4~ | c1`. The last statement doubles only the first half (E♭ for seven beats, D a crotchet at 179.4) and keeps the second half at its original metric place: `c''2 des''2 | c''1~ | c''1`. Strict doubling would move the D♭ to the downbeat of 181 and push the final C past the fermata bar. As written, the chromatic D♭ lands on beat 3 of 180, the secondary strong beat (PDF 50), as it does at 3 and at 173 (C.a.). That keeps the motto's metric shape, so a listener knows it at once. 177–182 is protected (c228, c251, c328). Keep.
- **A longer or downbeat D♭6 at 63.** Rejected by c301, and I agree. A&S PDF 50–51 would give it more accent, but then the two themes would share their climax pitch and rhythm (P at 20–21).
- **Triplet figures in a theme for rhythmic contrast between P and S** (PDF 49, Ex. 3-4). Rejected. P and S already contrast in the rate of their main tones and chords (crotchet and dotted-crotchet tones over a minim bass in P; minims and dotted minims over one chord a bar in S; PDF 49). They share only the dotted-crotchet-and-quaver cell, and that link helps the themes sound like one movement. Brahms 3/i ties its themes together the same way, through the F–A♭–F cell.
