## Findings

Under this reading no passage of v403 stands between the score and a master's work, so I made no edit. Since v314 the harmonic reduction differs in only five bars (44, 61, 136–137, 154, 173). Every key boundary, section length and cadence type is the same as when c313 judged it. The findings below are the questions this reading still raises. They are ranked by how much a change at that place would matter, and each gives the reason to keep the passage.

### 1. Retransition 117–130: keep the prolonged, untonicized V. Mozart 41/i shows the other route the reading allows

The reading gives two ways to handle the dominant before the return. In the first, V is held as a key and then turned back by its 7th (PDF 219–220 §10). In the second, a middle phrase "prolongs (but does not tonicize) V" (PDF 224, Ex. 14-12).

Mozart 41/i, the reading's own kind of movement, takes the first route exactly. The MCTL annotation marks G as a key from onset 712 (bar 179): I–V4/2–I–V7–I and vii°7–I over eight bars. The 7th F arrives at onset 740 (bar 186), and C follows at 744 with IV7–V7–I into the recapitulation at 752 (bar 189).

The score takes the second route. Over the G pedal, V♭9 and V7 alternate with i6/4. No chord applied to V sounds: there is no F♯ between 117 and 130 (census below), so G is never heard as 1̂. The 7th F is restated at 130.3 in Hn 2 (protected, c120, c353) and resolves to E♭ at 131, the "neutralizing" degree of PDF 220.

Tonicizing G in Mozart's way would need F♯ over the pedal. That would rewrite the protected chain 117–130 (c239, c249, c303, c393) and weaken the minor-ninth A♭ that carries the fff climax at 122. In a minor-key movement whose dominant pedal is approached by a chromatic bass wedge (111–116), the prolonged V is the stronger choice. Keep.

### 2. The approach to A♭, 39–47, matches Mozart's approach to G: keep, including c371's change at 44

In Mozart 41/i, onsets 192–212 (bars 49–53) stand on D, V of the new key, alternating V and I6/4 of G. The second theme follows in G at onset 220 (bar 56).

The score does the same one key lower in the hierarchy:
- 39–40: V/V of A♭, with D♮ = ♯4̂ (PDF 214 §5).
- 41–43: E♭, with D♭ added at 43 (PDF 219–220 §10).
- 44–45: V4/2–I6 of A♭, as in Schubert's bar 6 (Ex. 14-1, PDF 211, labelled "V4/2" under the example).
- 46–47: ii6/5–V7–I with the S head.

The only change since v314 is the viola at 44 (c371). The B♭3 now enters on beat 3 with Hn 1's 7–6, so beats 1–2 of 44 are D♭–E♭–G–C (V4/2 with the suspended C over D♭) and no B♭ sounds there. The chord is still a V4/2 to the ear, because its root E♭, its third G and its seventh D♭ (in the bass) are all present, and the fifth is the member a seventh chord can most easily lack. Nothing the reading asks for at this point (PDF 211, 219 §9) is lost. Keep.

c313 listed "37–40" but treated only the G♭ of 37–38. Bar 40 holds D♮ in five parts across three octaves. This is the V/V that makes the medial caesura at 41 (PDF 215, Ex. 14-4).

I traced each D into 41:
- The violas carry it as a real voice, D4→E♭4.
- Hn 3 and Hn 4 leave it downward to B♭3 and G3. These are inner doublings that take chord tones of the E♭ triad, as an inner leading tone may.
- Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I pass through D inside the rising runs 40.3–40.4 and arrive on G.

So the leading tone has one real resolution, and the doubled forms are either colour or passing tones. The note in PDF 214 is a four-part rule about real voices; it does not forbid octave doubling of a scale run. The horn pair on D4 is protected by c234 for balance against three trombones, and c252 and c272 kept it. Keep.

### 4. Changes since c313 checked against the reading: keep all

- **137.4, bass D (c402), as at 25.4 (c242).** The bar before the stop chord is now iv – iiø7 in both the exposition and the recapitulation. PDF 217 says the chord "just before the first dominant" is the likely pivot or pre-dominant. With the bass D, iiø7 leads by step to the vii°7 stop chord at 138 (bass D held), and both statements are the same. This follows.
- **154–155, Vln II B4→C5 (c402).** The soft C-major cadence now has 7̂–1̂ in an inner voice, while the tune (violas and cellos) ends on 3̂ (E4) at 155. That keeps 155 an imperfect cadence and saves the perfect one for 166, where Vln I moves D6–C6 to C5 at p. This is the order of Ex. 14-1 (PDF 219 §9): first the key is touched, then confirmed. It also holds the protected rule of no forte C major before the finale (c240, c293). This follows.
- **68, rit. ending on beat 2 and Tempo dell'Allegro from beat 3 (c368).** The deceptive cadence on F minor at 68 still sits inside the rit. The a tempo arrives before the closing PAC at 69–72, so the cadence that confirms A♭ (PDF 216 §6, 219 §9) gets the faster tempo. This moved the development's start by 0.3 s (191.0 s to 190.7 s). This follows.
- **61 (c401), 136 (c393), 173 (c400).** These are inner-voice changes inside one harmony. They do not touch a key boundary.

### 6. The A♭ area is turned back by its 7th, then by the major-third cycle, 73–80: keep

PDF 219–220 §10: after a long stay in a key (A♭ lasts 62 s, 128.4–190.7 s), the temporary tonic should lose its stability by gaining its minor 7th. Bar 75 adds G♭ to A♭ (A♭7). Bar 76 is D♭ minor 6/4 over the A♭ pedal, and the development then opens in A♭ minor (77) and moves A♭–E–C (77–80). The G♭ is the degree that A♭ major lacks, so 75 does the work that §10 describes. The return to C at 80 then needs "no special preparation" (PDF 219), because C is the main tonic and the stay away from it in E (78–79) is short. Keep (c223, c230, c333 protect 73–83).
