## Findings

I found nothing in v542 that the reading marks as wrong, and no change in this lens that I could support as a clear improvement. The score is unchanged in my scratch copy. The findings below record the new checks and the one candidate I weighed and rejected.

### F1. Melodic augmented seconds, checked by spelling across every line. Clean.

PDF 267 says the melodic augmented second "should be avoided in four-part vocal writing", and Ex. 17-26c (PDF 284) marks the 6̂–♯7̂ step "wrong" in a minor-key 5-6 series into V. Earlier passes checked this by eye at the cadences. I scanned every variable in every section file for two consecutive notes a letter-step apart and three semitones apart, so A♭–B counts and G♯–B does not.

The only hit is the Vln I and Vln II semiquaver run F–G–A♭–B at 32.4 (tOne.ily, `f''16 g'' aes'' b''`), which runs up the harmonic minor over V7 into C at 33. This is a fast instrumental scale, not a part-writing step, and c252 and c342 accepted it. The other hits were false readings of `\transpose c cis` and `\clef bass`. Cl 1 at 164 still goes A♭4–G4 (c252), not A♭4–B4. No change.

### F2. Strong-beat fifths and octaves since v452. Clean.

PDF 266 says the weak-beat chord in a sequence "prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". I ran c452's all-pairs downbeat scan on v542 and diffed it against v452. The only difference is one line that has gone: the octaves Tbn 2 G3–F3 / Vln II G4–F4 from 176 to 177, which c512 removed with Vln II A♭4–F4 at 177. The fifths list is the same eight places c452 examined (28, 45, 92, 98, 114, 139, 141, 153). Each still has a voice-leading chord or a moving voice between the downbeats. The changes since v452 at 157 (c502), 158 (c514) and 159 (c522) add no strong-beat parallels. vl.txt is identical to v542's. No change.

### F3. Bars 156–158 as an ascending-fifths fragment. Considered a ♯4 approach; rejected.

Later critics (c523, c533, c541) name the two-bar climb at 156–157 as the score's one remaining weakness. They treat it as a matter of form, and it is protected (c083, c143, c243, c448). I asked whether harmony alone could make the arrival at 158 land harder without adding bars.

Read with this unit, 156–158 is ascending fifths: B♭ (♭VII) – F (iv) – C (the cadential 6/4 over G). Vln I falls by step over the pairs, D6 | C6–B♭5–A♭5 | G5. That is the soprano that PDF 270 describes for this type ("each pair of chords often supports a soprano line that descends by step"). The pattern is sound. It is short, not faulty.

PDF 281 (§18) says a 5-6 series leading to a tonicized V brings in ♯4 early. The obvious way to intensify 157 is a ♯4 on 157.3, for example a bass F1–F♯1–G1 with vii°7 of V. I rejected it for four reasons:
- The tune at 157.4 is A♭5, the S head's 6̂–5̂ into G5 at 158. A vii°7 of V needs A♮ and would sound against it. Without the A♮, F♯ under A♭ makes an augmented-sixth chord with its diminished third in the bass. The reading does not support that sonority, and it is harsh at mf.
- Hn 1 (F4), Cl 1 (F5) and the violas (F4) sound F on 157.3. A bass F♯ would clash with them unless the inner parts were rewritten, which goes beyond a harmonic touch.
- The other route to ♯4, a German sixth over A♭ at 157.3, would put A♭ in the bass two bars early. It would take the surprise from the climax move G→A♭ (V–♭VI) at 159→160, which is protected.
- The movement already uses the German sixth into V at its structural point (12–13, the finale's first chord). The recapitulation's C-major colour is kept diatonic up to 155 and modal at 156–157 on purpose (c170: the G–B♭–D♭ colour leads into iv).

I did not apply it. The weakness, if it is one, is one of length. The reading's remedy would be more statements of the sequence, which means more bars, and c523 has already given the reasons against that.

### F4. Falling thirds 64–66 and 162–164 against Ex. 17-16. Kept.

Ex. 17-16 (PDF 277) gives three top voices for the descending 5-6: 10-10, 10-6 and 5-6.
- **64–66.** The bass falls F–D–D♭–B♭, then goes to E♭ (V7) and a deceptive cadence to F minor at 68. Vln I sounds A♭5 (64.1), A♭5 (64.3), F5 (65.1) and B♭5 (66.1) against it: 10, dim 5 (on the passing iiø7), 10, 8. The diminished chord is inside the series, not at its ends (PDF 268).
- **162–164.** The outer voices run 8–10–8 (A5/A1, A5/F1, D6/D2), and the series breaks off on iiø7 into V–I (PDF 282: "VII6 or II7 is used instead").

Both follow the reading and both are protected. No change.

### F5. The development's two-statement sequence 103–106. Kept.

The head is stated in D minor at 103–104. It is answered over B♭ (G minor 6) at 105, and C7 at 105.3 tilts it to V of E at 106. This is two statements plus the start of a third, the norm on PDF 264. C♯ at 104.4 (A7, V of d) and E♮ at 105.3 (C7) give the "♯4 early" turn to the new goal that PDF 281 describes. The augmented triad B–D♯–G on 106.3 sharpens the arrival in E minor. No change.

### Where the music could come nearer a master

Apart from the protected length of 156–157, I found no passage in this lens where a listener would lose the thread or where a harmonic colour could be made more vivid without spending one of the reserves the finale needs: C major at forte, the trumpets' B→C, and ♭VI moving to ♭II. Unit 17's techniques are all present where the form needs them: rising sequences in the introduction and transition, descending fifths in the fugato, falling thirds before the cadences, and a 6-5 chromatic wedge into the dominant pedal. The voice-leading details that the unit warns about are all in order: fifths on strong beats, a doubled leading tone before a goal, diminished chords at a sequence's ends, and augmented seconds.
