## Findings

### 1. Bar 159, beat 1: the violas strike the 7th F4 against the tune's held 6th E4 (applied in scratch)

**What happens.** Bar 158 is the ff cadential 6/4 of C over the G pedal. On the downbeat of 159 the 4th C falls to B as it should, in Tpt 3, Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Hn 3·4 and the violas. The 6th E does not fall. The tune holds it through beat 1 in every octave: Fl 1·2 and Vln I E6, Ob 2, Tpt 1 and Vln II E5, Hn 1·2 and cellos E4. It then rises to F on beat 2 and to G on beat 3. This is the 6–7 motion of Ex. 10-4b (PDF 154), delayed by one beat. The violas' repeated quavers, however, go straight from C4/E4 (158) to B3/F4 on the downbeat of 159. So for the whole first beat, F4 in the violas sounds a semitone above E4 in Hn 1·2 and the cellos, at ff. The horns and cellos then move onto the F4 that the violas are already playing. The sonority on 159.1 is G–B–D–E–F; without the violas' F it is G–B–D–E.

**Why it is wrong.**
- **The 6th's own voice should bring the 7th.** In Ex. 10-4b (PDF 154), when the 6th of the cadential 6/4 goes to the 7th of V7, the 7th arrives in that voice. No other part sounds it first. Unit 9 gives the same rule for any 7th of V7 (§6, PDF 139–140, Ex. 9-8, 9-9): it enters prepared in one voice or, if it enters by leap, with the preparation in another voice. In neither case does it sound against the note it grows out of, a semitone away in the same octave.
- **The held E acts as a suspension.** PDF 153 §3 says the 6/4 functions as a dissonant chord that grows out of a suspension, and that suspensions resolve by step. Here the held E is a rising suspension (retardation) whose resolution note F is struck at the same moment in the same register. That makes the resolution redundant before it happens.
- **The horns hear it most.** The rub falls on the melody's lower octave, E4 in Hn 1·2 and the cellos. That is the octave c229 and c174 doubled so the climax line would carry. A minor second in that register on a ff strong beat sounds like a wrong note, not like an appoggiatura.

**The precedent.** c502 changed the violas at 157 for the same reason (A&S PDF 469–470): the violas struck Vln II's resolution note in the same register while the dissonance sounded. My new scan found no other place in the movement where a part strikes the resolution a semitone from a held note on a beat; the hits at 114.2 and 132 are the violas' lower-neighbour ostinato and the P head's C–D♭–C–B, both motivic.

**The fix.** The violas keep E4 for beat 1 and rise to F4 with the tune on beat 2. In sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoSync, bar 159:

```
old: <b f'>8[\< <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] <b f'>[ <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] |
new: <b e'>8[\< <b e'> <b f'> <b f'>] <b f'>[ <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] |
```

The viola's top line stays stepwise, E–(E)–F–E♭ into 160, as c514 intended, and its lower note keeps C–B–C. Beat 1 is now V with a suspended 6th (G–B–D–E). The 7th F then enters on beat 2 in every part that carries it at once, which makes the V7 arrival before the ♭VI at 160 sharper. I added a "% c522" comment above the line in the scratch file.
