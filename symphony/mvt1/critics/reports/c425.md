## Findings

### 1. Horns 3·4, 162–171: the score goes back to treble at 167 in the middle of a held C4/G3, a bar where the bass clef serves better. Applied.

**What is wrong.**
- Bar 162 puts the Hn 3·4 staff into bass clef for Hn 4's E3–D3 (four ledger lines in treble). The bar before the change, 166, holds Hn 3 C4 and Hn 4 G3 under a breath mark.
- `codaHnThree` then opens 167 with `\clef treble`. The same C4/G3 that sat at the top of the bass stave in 166 now sits under it, and Hn 4's G3 and A♭3 need two ledger lines through 170.
- In bass clef, 167–170 (C4, D4 over G3, A♭3) need one ledger line at most: C4 and D4 on and above the first ledger line, G3 and A♭3 inside the stave.
- After the fermata at 170 the staff rests for ten bars and enters again at 181 with C4/G3.

**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable … This shows the contour of the pitches, which a change of clef would obscure."
- Here the clef change made a held unison look like a leap of a tenth. A change that buys nothing cannot be the practicable choice.
- Gould PDF 23 ("after periods of rests"): the return to the commonest clef belongs "at the end of the system after the player has finished … better than an immediate clef change" elsewhere. Bar 170 ends a system (the break before 171 in global.ily), and 171–180 are rests.
- The change at 167 does follow PDF 22 ("between phrases": the breath at 166). This is why earlier passes left it. It is still a change that the next bars do not need, and PDF 21 asks for fewer such changes.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/coda.ily, `codaHnThree`:
```
c'1\f | c'1 | c'1\< | d'2.->\ff r4\fermata | \clef treble R1 | R1 | R1 | R1 |
```
(was `\clef treble c'1\f | … | R1 | …`). LilyPond prints the warning treble clef at the end of 170 and the treble clef at the start of the 171 system, so 181–182 read in treble as before. Pitches and parts are unchanged: the horn parts drop the score's Hn 3·4 clefs through `\partCleanNoClefs`.

**Earlier decisions.** c225 protected the "clef plan" on the grounds that it followed Gould PDF 21–23. This change follows the same pages more closely. It does not touch c405's protected plan for 135–158 or the notes of 167–170 (protected by c343, c349 and c367).

### 2. Horns 3·4, 19–23: the bass clef should come in at 19, in the rests before the entry, not at 23 between two C4s. Applied.

**What is wrong.**
- The Hn 3·4 staff rests 15–18, then plays 19–22 in treble: Hn 4 on F3 (three ledger lines) for four bars, Hn 3 from C4 to E♭4.
- The staff changes to bass clef at the system start 23, for Hn 4's G2/F2 at 24–25.
- Across that change Hn 3 plays C4 at 22.3 and C4 again at 23.1. The first C4 hangs under the treble stave, the second sits above the bass stave.
- In bass clef, 19–22 need one ledger line at most (C4, D4), against three in treble.

**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 23: "Where there needs to be a change from the more standard clef, retain this clef until the barline before the next entry. This placing alerts the performer to the change at the relevant point (i.e. at the entry)." The viola example on that page places the new clef straight before the bar of the entry, after the rests. The barline before 19 is that place.
- Gould PDF 21: the same contour argument as in finding 1. The current change splits a repeated note between two clefs. The new one falls where nothing sounds.
- The number of clef changes stays at two (bass at 19, treble at 27). Bar 27 already follows PDF 22 (after the crotchet rest on 26.4).

**Fix (applied).** In sections/tOne.ily, `tOneHnThree`:
```
R1*4 | \clef bass c'1\p | des'1 | des'2 b2 |
b4 d'4 c'2 \breathe | c'2.\mp\<-> d'4->~ | …
```
(the `\clef bass` moves from 23 to 19). The recapitulation keeps treble at 131–134 and bass from 135 (c393, c405). There the horns play without a break from 117, and 120–121 (G4) rule out an earlier bass clef. So the two places differ for a reason the reading gives, not by oversight.
