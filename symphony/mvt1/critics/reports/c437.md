## Findings

I found nothing in v437 that the assigned reading shows to be wrong and that an earlier cycle has not already settled. I recommend no change. The checks below are the places where the reading came nearest to asking for one, and the reason for keeping each.

### Checked and kept

1. **Fugato 85–96, each entry's texture (Adler PDF 175–176, 178).** The four entries rise D–G–C–F, and each has a new string carrier and a wind partner (PDF 176: "each rendition of the first theme is colored somewhat differently and supported by one member of the string choir").
   - The colour pairs stay fixed for as long as their lines run. Bsn 1 stays with the violas from 88 to 95, and the C.a. stays with Vln II from 91 to 95 (c237, c264). This is what Mozart does at 389–400.
   - Each voice runs subject, then countersubject, then free voice. The free voice is in half notes, the countersubject is slurred with a held note and a turn (c107), and the subject is accented and detached. So the three moving parts have three rhythms and three articulations (PDF 178, 179).
   - The basses rest until 90 and then have their own line (PDF 176). The cellos' Bb3, held across 94–95, is the only static part, and it sits under the three moving voices.

   Nothing in the reading asks for more.

2. **103–110, the two heads combined (PDF 177–178, 181, 184).** This is the movement's clearest case of what Adler calls the Wagner model: two themes already heard separately, combined in two colours.
   - **103–106.** The P head has woodwind only (Ob 2 + Cl 1·2), is accented, and is marked f. The S head has Hn 1 + Bsn 1 + Vc, is slurred with swells, and is marked mf. The strings rest above them, as in Bloch's separation of choirs (PDF 184). The articulation, dynamic and colour all differ, as in Ex. XV-26.
   - **107–110.** The themes are inverted: the S head is on top in the violas (p espr.), and Fl 1 joins it at 109. The P head is in the bass clarinet alone, below them, in a register clear of the violas: B2–G3 under G4–C5. Vln II divisi pp is the background.

   The one gap against PDF 178 is that the 103–106 P head has no string. Critic 237 kept that on this same reading, and it is the reason the passage sounds different from the ff B minor block just before it. Keep it.

3. **69–72, the codetta (PDF 176, 178, 179).** It has four layers:
   - the S head in Fl 1 + Vln I;
   - a held counter in Ob 1 + Tpt 1·2;
   - the P head in C.a., Cl 1·2, Hn 1·2 and Vln II, which is string, woodwind and brass together, as PDF 178 asks;
   - the viola quaver figure over a half-note bass.

   The P head and the viola figure are both slurred quavers, which is the one point where PDF 178 (its own articulation for each element) is only partly met. They differ in span and contour, and both c237 and c337 kept them on this reading. Keep it.

4. **158–161, the climax counter without a string (PDF 178).** The counter is Ob 1 + Cl 1·2 + Hn 3·4. The violas, the only free strings, play the repeated-quaver middleground, which is the *Scheherazade* layout (PDF 179): staccato figuration under the themes. Moving them to the counter would remove the middleground and the climax's pulse, and 158–161 is protected (c283, c337, c408). Keep it.

5. **107–109, the bass clarinet alone on the P head (PDF 184: give each fragment to an instrument that will be heard).** I checked whether it is covered. It plays p below Vln II's divisi pp and the violas' p, and the only other sound in its register is the double basses' pizzicato on single beats. It is heard. Keep it (c094).

6. **127–130, the stretto (PDF 184, Bloch's rolls).** The timpani roll goes pp, then mf at 129, then f at 130, under the entering motto voices. This is the dynamic shape PDF 184 asks for: a roll that grows without covering the themes. Keep it (protected 117–130).

7. **The bass line.** It has five kinds of writing: Cb quaver and crotchet pulse under P (15–22, 27–32, 167–169); pizzicato on the beat under S (47–55); held pedals (73–76, 117–130); an independent contrapuntal line in the fugato (90–96); and the motto itself in the coda (175–179). The cellos leave the bass when they have a line of their own, at 47–55, 85–96, 98–102, 103–106 and 171–174, and join it on strokes and pedals. That is the independent, part-writing bass that PDF 176 credits to Mozart. Nothing to change.

8. **33–40, the imitation in the transition (PDF 179, 184).** The P head enters in Bsn 1 at 33, the violas at 34, Vln I at 35, Ob 1 at 37, Vln I at 37 and Cl 1 at 38, over a whole-note bass. The later entries are marked to sit under the leading voice: the violas p under Vln I at 37. This follows the Schoenberg rule on principal and subsidiary voices (PDF 184). Keep it.
