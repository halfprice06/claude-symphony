### 4. The theme record reports a false change in the horn call since v309 (record tool; fix in coherence/lyparse.py)

`themecheck.py themes-mvt1.ly v320 --beats 4` gives "34/35 unchanged, 1 CHANGED: mvtOneHornCall HnOne 13". It gives the same for v310 and v319, and 35/35 for v221 and v300. The printed record and version are identical (E♭3, C4, B3). The cause is c308's `\set Staff.soloText = "1. Solo"` and `= "1."` inside `introHnOne`. `lyparse.strip()` removes the quoted string, so the tokenizer then reads "soloText" as a note named "solo" and "Text" as "ext". Each becomes a pitchless event with the previous duration, and the call is shifted off its beat.

This matters for the merge, because finale.md records "movement I record 35/35" against v309 and the tool now contradicts that.

Fix (tested in /tmp/critic-320/tools/lyparse.py): add one line in `strip()` before `text = re.sub(r"#'\w+", ' ', text)`:

```python
    text = re.sub(r'\\(set|unset)\s+[\w.]+(\s*=\s*(#\S+|"[^"]*"))?', ' ', text)
```

With it, v320 and v221 both give 35/35. The score does not change. Applied in the scratch copy of the tool only, because I may not write under /mnt/project-files. The composer should apply it to /mnt/project-files/symphony/coherence/lyparse.py and note "record 35/35 (parser fix for \set)" in mvt1.md.

### 6. The arc across four movements needs no change (judgement; keep)

- **The key cycle is the motto enlarged.** The motto's D♭–C and the P head's A♭–G (♭6–5) become the three A♭ climaxes (82 ff, 122 fff A♭ over G, 160 fff ♭VI). Movement II is in A♭, so its key continues that line. This is exactly A&S PDF 600–601: Chopin chose D major so that he could enlarge D–C♯, and the *Appassionata*'s D♭ goal enlarges C–D♭–C. The A♭–E–C bass at 77–83 is the cycle of major thirds of the *Appassionata*'s development (Ex. 32-19, image), at the pitch the symphony uses across its movements.
- **The E♭/E♮/D♯ thread is Haydn's integration device.** In *The Creation* (A&S PDF 597–598, 601, Ex. 32-16/17), D♯ is first heard as a leading tone and later becomes E♭. Here movement I plants D♯5 (106) and D♯4 (108) as leading tones of E. The scherzo is built on that E. The finale's violas take D♯4→E4 into C major (IV 219–220). The cross relations at 8→9 and 181–182 present the major/minor clash openly, as Brahms 3's motto does (A&S PDF 394–395, Ex. 23-13, image).
- **The reserve is held.** Movement I ends ppp on C major (violas' lone E4), like Brahms 1/i. Everything the finale needs is left out: forte C major, C7, clashed cymbals, a soft trumpet tune, the major brass chorale and the B→C resolution. This follows Casanelles PDF 227 and A&S PDF 386–387.
- **Proportions.** I 7:11, II 5:45, III 4:36, IV 7:02 (about 24½ minutes). The outer movements carry equal weight and the inner ones are shorter, which suits a cyclic C minor → C major plan (Sebesky PDF 240).

The finale's first theme already grows from movement I's material: the Ger6 at 12 is its first chord, and E♭ minor is first heard at 37. Dropping a theme hint into a frozen movement I would also leave the finale unable to answer it.
