# Critic 552: Harmony and voice leading (v552)

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 22 Leading-Tone Seventh Chords and Unit 23 Mixture (aldwell-schachter PDF 373–397), fourth pass. The earlier passes were c262, c352 and c462. I read every text page in the range in full and rendered every page at 110 dpi and looked at it (PDF 379 is scanned upside down; I rotated it). As the earlier critics noted, the scan skips printed pp. 393 and 399, so Ex. 23-2h, 23-3, 23-10 and 23-11 are cut off; their points survive in the prose on PDF 388 and in the Points for Review on PDF 395.

Method. I wrote five scratch scripts in /tmp/critic-552/scripts that read v552's full.midi: red.py (every sounding pitch with its instruments, per beat or half-beat), dim7.py (every full diminished or half-diminished seventh and its bass), tri.py (every tritone between two parts that moves in similar motion to a perfect fifth, the Ex. 22-3c case), mix.py (where each ♭6 goes next, in each part, in the A♭-major and C-major passages) and xrel.py (chromatic successions split between the outer voices). I traced each result into the source files to check spelling, because MIDI cannot tell A♭ from G♯. Changes since c462 (v463) are few: violas 157 (c502), violas 159 (c522), Vln II 177 (c512). I checked each against the reading.

## Findings

All four findings recommend keeping the score as it is. None is applied in the scratch copy. They are ranked by how audible the point is.

### 1. Bars 30→31.1: two irregular resolutions of the vii°7 of iv in the ff tutti (keep)

At 30 the chord is E–G–B♭–D♭ over E2, the vii°7 of F minor, going to iv at 31. tri.py finds two departures from the rules on PDF 375 and PDF 381:

- Hn 2 G4→F4 against Tpt 1 D♭5→C5. G–D♭ is the upper-voice d5 between 2 and ♭6 of F minor, and it moves in similar motion to the P5 F–C. This is the voice leading Ex. 22-3c marks "avoid" (PDF 375). c352 and c462 recorded it.
- Fl 2 B♭5→C6 against the bass E→F. This was not recorded before. B♭ is a d5 above the bass E, and the box on PDF 381 says that a dissonance involving the bass must resolve, here inward to a third (B♭→A♭). Fl 2 instead rises to a fifth above the bass F.

Both are covered by Ex. 22-5 (PDF 376): the resolution is transferred to other voices. B♭ falls to A♭ in Hn 4 (B♭3→A♭3), Tpt 2 (B♭4→A♭4) and Bsn 1/Vc (B♭3→A♭3). Of the four parts on G4 at 30, three (Hn 1, Ob 2, Tpt 3) rise to A♭4 as the rule asks. Hn 2's F4 is the only root in the middle register at 31.1 (the other Fs are F2/F3 in the bass), so its descent has a purpose. With three other parts on Hn 2's starting pitch and an ff tutti around it, the hidden fifths are far less exposed than in the four-voice setting the book describes.

I considered Hn 2 A♭4 at 31.1 (the regular resolution, Ex. 22-3a). It puts Hn 1 and Hn 2 in unison, removes the middle-register root, and changes a bar that the finale quotes at pitch in every part at its 203. That is a cross-movement change for a gain no listener would notice. Keep. The reason c352 gave (27–32 quoted at pitch) still stands.

### 2. Bar 162.4.5: the A–G♯–A neighbour over vi is spelled correctly; it is not ♭6→♮6 (keep; record)

mix.py reports Vln I, Vln II and Ob 2 moving "A♭→A" at 162.4.5→163.1, which would be the ♭6–♮6 order that PDF 388 and Point 3 on PDF 395 call poor. recTwo.ily spells the note `gis'` (`a'2.~( a'8 gis'8 | a'4.`). With the violas' B3 and Cl 1's F4 over the A bass, it is the vii°7 of vi (G♯–B–D–F, without D) over a vi pedal, the Ex. 22-10 type on PDF 379. G♯ is a lower neighbour to A, not mixture. ♭6 (A♭) is used at 160–161 and reaches G at 161.3, and the next A♭, at 164, reaches G at 164.3. Keep. I record this so that later MIDI scans do not report it as a fault.

### 3. Bar 56.3: harp F♭6 against E♮4 in Cl 1, Hn 2 and Vc (keep)

At 56 the line E♭–E♮–F (Cl 1/Hn 2 `es'2 r8 e'8( f'8 g'8`, Vc `e'2`) is a rising chromatic passing tone. Over the A♭ pedal it makes I–I+–IV6/4 (A♭ at 56, D♭ over A♭ at 57). PDF 387–388 says that ♭6 is active toward 5. A tone that rises to F is therefore ♯5, and the winds and cellos spell it correctly. The harp's broken chord spells the same pitch F♭6 (`c'''8( fes''' aes''' c'''')`). The sound is the same. The spelling follows the harp pedalling and damping plan of c446, which belongs to the playability lens. Harp parts are routinely respelled for the pedals. Keep. No harmonic change follows from it.

### 4. Changes since the third pass (157, 159, 177) conform to Units 22–23 (keep)

- Violas 157, F3–A♭3–C4–F4 (c502): the A♭ in iv with mixture goes to G at 157.4.5 and 158 in Vln I and II, so ♭6→5 is kept (PDF 388).
- Violas 159, E4→F4 (c522): the cadential 6 rises to the 7th of V7 with the tune. The doublings that vl.py lists at 159.2 are octave doublings of the tune.
- Vln II 177, A♭4→F4 (c512): A♭ is the 7th of the iiø7 at 177.1. At 177.3 it is the ♭6 of the vii°6/5 (Tbn 2 F3, Vln II F4, Cl 1 B4 over D), and it goes on to G in the violas (G3 at 177.4) before 178. The °6/5 goes through V4/3 to I, as Ex. 22-4 allows (PDF 376).

## Where the score stands against a master, within this lens

The movement already uses the reading's best effects. They are the common-tone °7 stop chords at 26, 138 and 170, which the other movements quote, and the °7 over the pedal under the flute motto at 73 (Ex. 22-10). There is the ♭VI deceptive cadence at 160, set against the exposition's vi at 68 (Ex. 23-6). The ♭VI at 160 opens a short minor passage, and the ♮6–♭6–5 close follows at 163–164.3 (Ex. 23-5, PDF 395 Point 3). The exposition darkens to A♭ minor at 73–76 (§5), and the movement ends on a soft Picardy third at 182 (§4).

I looked for places where a technique from the reading could make a colour more vivid:

- Ex. 22-7h, an accented ♮7 against ♯7 over °7 (PDF 377–378), is not used. Every °7 in the score is at a protected place (11, 26, 28, 63, 70, 114, 138, 161, 170, 177), and most of them are quoted or answered by other movements.
- Extending a stop chord by voice exchange (§12, Ex. 22-11) would lengthen the protected stop chords at 26, 138 and 170.
- A louder Picardy third at 182 (Ex. 23-7) would break the attacca join and the E♭/E♮ thread that movement II continues.

None of these meets the bar of a real improvement against its cost. As c462 recorded, almost all the movement's diminished sevenths are the one set B–D–F–A♭. I agree with keeping that: it is the harmony of the quoted stop chords, and the development key plan B–D–F–A♭ composes it out. No change.
