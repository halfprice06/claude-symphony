## Findings (ranked)

**1. 77–83: do not add a long-breathed countermelody. Keep the exchanges as they are (no change).**
- **Problem as posed:** cycle 79 called the passage stop-start and asked whether a continuous line in one section would join the bars.
- **Why there is no room for a line:**
  - In 77–79 the motto relay already overlaps. Bsn 77, then Cl 1 from 77 beat 4, then Hn 1·2 + Vc from 78 beat 4, then Ob 1 + Vln I in 79. Some motto note sounds on every half-bar, in registers from E♭3 to C♭6.
  - The motto tails take the chromatic pitches: C♭–B♭–B♭♭–A♭ in 77–78 and G–F♯–F–E in 78–79.
  - A sustained countermelody would therefore have to avoid those pitches, which leaves only chord tones. Those chord tones are what Vln II and Vla already play as a tremolo: E♭–E–E♭ and C♭–B–C. That tremolo is already a slow semitone line.
  - Any genuine tune in that space would double or rub against the tails and blur the motto, which is the subject of this section.
- **Corpus evidence:**
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 161–170 does not use a countermelody. It joins its hammered first-theme fragments, which have rests between them, with a sustained bass that falls by semitone: Vc/Cb/Bsn hold C for 161–164, B for 165–168 and B♭ at 169. It also uses upbeat flourishes in the gaps, at 164 and 168 (E5–G5–G5–E♭6).
  - Dvořák 9/i 179–200 does the same with held notes. Vln I holds D♯6 at 184–188 and G♯5 at 193–200, and Vc holds D♯4 at 186–192, while horns and flute exchange the closing-theme fragment over moving inner strings.
- **Comparison with this score:** 77–81 already has the equivalent glue (Vln II/Vla tremolo, Cb A♭–E–C, timpani roll at 80–81).
- **Answer to cycle 79:** the fragmentation at 80–81 (two-note heads only, rising in register) is Schoenberg's liquidation before a new section. It is a valid process, not a defect. The only real break in continuity is at 83 (finding 2).

**2. Bar 83: the bass stops with everything else, so 82→83→84 sounds like three separate blocks (applied).**
- **Problem:**
  - At 83 every part, including Vc, Cb, Cbsn and tuba, plays two accented quarters with quarter rests.
  - The bare G octave at 84 then starts as a new event.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 161–164 does hammer quarter-note chords with rests (Ob/Vln I "D4 r C4 r"), but over Vc/Cb whole notes that do not stop. Rimsky-Korsakov describes isolated sforzando chords set against a line that continues, and names pedal notes as the double bass's independent role.
- **Fix:** Cb only. The hammer stays on beats 1 and 3, the G sustains through 83 and is re-bowed sub. p at 84. In dev.ily, devCb:
  `g,,4-> r4 g,,4-> r4 | g,,1\subP |` → `g,,2-> g,,2-> | g,,1\subP |`
- **Protection:** this touches the protected "motto exchanges 77–83" (cycle 77) and "bass 73–84" (cycle 62). Both were protected because each gesture is motivic and the bass pitches are right. No gesture, pitch or exchange changes: Cb still plays G on beats 1 and 3, and the subito p at 84 remains a fresh bow. The G pedal now runs from 83 into the fugato's pizzicato at 85, so 82–85 sounds as one approach to the fugato.

**3. 156–157 (cycle 80's new violin entry): it continues the second theme naturally and leads well into K. Keep it (no change).**
- **Why it reads as the second theme:**
  - The pickup f''→d''' is a major sixth with the head's rhythm (quarter, then dotted half tied to an eighth), so the ear hears the head (compare 55–56: es'4 | c''2.~ c''8 bes'8).
  - Its continuation is a close rhyme of the exposition's second phrase, 60–61: bes''2.~ bes''8 a''8 | aes''2 ges''4 fes''8 es''8. The recap version is d'''2.~ d'''8 des'''8 | c'''2 bes''4 aes''8 g''8, with the same rhythm and the same three-note chromatic start.
  - The listener therefore hears the familiar continuation, and D–D♭–C is also the motto's tail.
- **Why it leads well into K:**
  - The fall to G places the line exactly on the climax head's first note (g''4 → e''' at 158), approached by step from A♭.
  - The peaks rise across 156–160: D6, E6, G6, A♭6.
  - The bes'' on beat 3 of 157 over F minor is a stressed dissonance resolving to aes''. That gives the bar expressive weight.
- **The repeated g''8 | g''4 across the bar line:** this is the anticipation the exposition already has at 61→62 (es''8 | es''4). Under ff and a new down-bow it re-strikes the climax note rather than weakening it. I would not change it.
- **Protection:** cycle 80's do-not-touch on 156–157 is justified.

**4. Bar 156, beat 4½: Hn 2 holds D4 while both violin octaves move to D♭ (applied).**
- **Problem:**
  - On beat 4½ the sounding chord is B♭ bass, F (Hn 1/Hn 4/Bsn), A4 (Cl 1 counter), G3 (Vc tail), D♭5/D♭6 (violins) and D4 (Hn 2, a whole note).
  - Without the D, the violins' D♭ and the clarinet's A form a clean F-augmented passing chord into F minor at 157.
  - The held D4 against D♭ makes the motto's D♭ sound like a wrong note just where it should be heard as the motto tail.
  - The exposition model avoids the same problem: at 60, Hn 3 releases des' before beat 4 (`des'2.\< r4`), and no held note contradicts the violins' a''.
- **Fix:** Hn 2 doubles the motto tail in the tenor. In recTwo.ily, recTwoHnTwo bar 10:
  `d'1\mf\<` → `d'2.~\mf\< d'8 des'8`
  Bar 157 already begins c'2., so the horn line becomes D–D♭–C under the violins. Hn 1 keeps f'1 with an equal dynamic on the shared staff (mf<), which is consistent with the part-combine rule.
- **Protection:** this does not touch the climax scoring at 158–166.
