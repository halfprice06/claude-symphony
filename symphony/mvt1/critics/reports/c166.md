## What I studied

I studied the assigned Adler chapter and checked it against the corpus. Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly`, timpani voice) ends nearly every roll on a short release note, for example `a4:32 a8 r8` at bars 71–75, `d2:32 d8 r8 r4` at 136 and 154–160, and `c2:32\ff\> c8\pp r8 r4` at 162. Tchaikovsky 6/iv (`Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt4.ly`, tam-tam stave) writes the tam-tam p as a note tied through 137–141 and ending on an eighth at 142, so the written length tells the player where to damp. Movement II of this symphony (mvt2/v61 `intro.ily`) already follows Adler PDF 80–81 and 88: it names the sticks, writes its tuning, and ends rolls on a tied stroke. Movement I does not yet do this. Web sources: the Indiana ISFEE tam-tam page (https://isfee.music.indiana.edu/tamtamGongs) says a long-ringing tam-tam usually needs a damping sign and that tam-tam beaters are heavy beaters built for the instrument. The VSL tam-tam page (https://www.vsl.co.at/academy/percussion/tam-tam) and a percussion lesson on tied and separated rolls (https://freepercussionlessons.com/how-to-play-tied-and-separated-timpani-rolls) describe the same practice. I ran a MIDI check of every timpani attack against the pitches sounding at that moment. All timpani notes belong to the harmony (Reading note 2).

## Findings

### F1. Rolls that stop inside a bar have no release stroke (12, 14, 63, 141, 146, 161). Applied.
Problem: six rolls simply stop before a rest, as in `aes,2.:32\f r4` at 12 and `g,2:32\> r2\!` at 161. The player cannot tell whether the roll should end with a stroke or be lifted off, so it may end on a dead, unarticulated decay. This matters most at 14, where the silence before the Allegro must be clean.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80, Ex. XII-47 and XII-48. Both of the notations Adler allows tie the roll into a short note. Tchaikovsky 6/i writes rolls this way throughout. Movement II, cycle 56, applied the same rule.
Fix (keeps each roll's sounding length; the release stroke falls on the roll's last beat):
- 12 `aes,2:32\f~ aes,4 r4`; 14 `<< { g,2:32~ g,4 } { s2\> s4\pp } >> r4`
- 63 `es4:32\>~ es4\! r2`; 141 `aes,4:32\ff~ aes,4 r2`; 146 `g,4:32~ g,4 r2`; 161 `g,4:32\>~ g,4\! r2`

Rolls that run to a barline and are followed by new material (4, 43, 76, 174, 182) stay as they are.

### F2. Tam-tam at 122: wrong beater, and no point where the sound stops. Applied.
Problem: "hard beater, l.v." at fff. A hard beater gives a clangy attack without the tam-tam's bloom. The l.v. with no end leaves a fff tam-tam ringing for 20 s or more, through the diminuendo of 123–125, the p arrival at 126 and the pp a tempo stretto at 127 (cellos and horns pp, timpani pp roll).
Evidence: Adler PDF 72 (the tam-tam is struck with "an especially large beater"), PDF 88 (beater list: "Heavy beater (for tamtam etc.)"), PDF 71 ("specify exactly how long the sound should last") and Ex. XII-29, PDF 73, where the tied tam-tam note ends on a beat. Tchaikovsky 6/iv 137–142 writes the tam-tam the same way. ISFEE says a damping sign is usually needed.
Fix (`devPerc`):
`tt1\fff~^\markup { "1: Tam-tam" \italic "heavy beater" } | tt1~ | tt1~ | tt1~ | tt1 | R1^\markup \small \italic "damp" | R1*3 |`
It rings through 126 and is damped at the a tempo (127). The protected placement (tam-tam alone at 122 and 175) is unchanged.

### F3. Timpani sticks are never named. Applied (three markups).
Problem: the part goes from a ppp roll under the motto (1–4) to ff hammer strokes doubled by the trumpets (26–33), then to pp/ppp strokes and rolls at the close (179–182). Movement II opens its timpani part with "soft sticks", and that part follows directly on movement I's last bar (attacca).
Evidence: Adler PDF 80 (hard, medium and soft mallets; tone depends on mallet and striking spot) and PDF 88 (name the beaters). Movement II, mvt2 `intro.ily`, c056.
Fix: bar 1 markup `\column { "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭" "soft sticks" }`; bar 15 `R1^\markup \small "medium sticks"` (11 bars of rest before 26); bar 175 `R1^\markup \small "soft sticks"`. This also means the player already holds the right sticks for movement II.

### F4. The stop chord at 170 gives the timpani a quarter while the orchestra holds a dotted half. Applied.
Problem: at 170 every other part plays a dotted half ff with an accent, then a quarter rest with a fermata. The timpani has `c4 r4 r4 r4\fermata`, so it either damps on beat 2 while the chord sounds or rings into the fermata silence; the score does not say which. The parallel stop chords at 26 and 138 already give the timpani `g,2. r4`.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80 ("the exact duration of each note… how long a note may ring"). Cycle 65 fixed only the rest grouping here, not the note length.
Fix: `c2.->\ff r4\fermata`. The C pedal is in the chord throughout beats 1–3 (MIDI check), and the timpani now stops with the orchestra.

### F5. The crescendo roll at 115 breaks off at the barline. Applied.
Problem: `aes,1:32\mf\< | R1\!`. The timpani grows through 115 and then stops dead at 116, while the whole orchestra keeps its cresc. into the ff at 117. The hairpin ends on a rest.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80 (show where a roll ends and its dynamics) and Ex. XII-47. A♭ is still sounding on beats 1–2 of 116 (C2 A♭2 C3 G♭3 D4; A♮ only arrives on beat 3), so a release stroke on A♭ is in the harmony.
Fix: `aes,1:32\mf\<~ | aes,4\f r4 r2 |`

### F6. B♭ at 40 sits at the bottom of the 25" drum. Note only, not applied.
"muta C in B♭" at 34 lowers the 25" drum to its lowest note (Adler Ex. XII-46: 25" B♭–f). The pp< roll at 40 would speak better on the 28" drum (F–c), which is free from 33 to 68: A♭ is not needed again until 69. The alternative is "muta A♭ in B♭" at 34 and "muta B♭ in A♭" at 44, both with ample time. Timpanists often choose drums themselves, so this is optional. The front-page tuning line would change to "G, A♭/B♭, C/B♮, E♭".
