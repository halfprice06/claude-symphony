## Did the earlier form changes work?
- **A♭ at 82 (cycle 13): yes.** It is now a deceptive crash (i–VI–V7♭9), not a tonic feint. The tonic is withheld until 123, apart from the mf viola fugato entry on C at 88, which is harmless.
- **127–130 from mf (cycle 13): yes.** Loudness drops from about 7400 (123–126) to about 6200 (127–129), and the hemiola again builds into 131–134, as at 23–26. The recapitulated first group now has two waves instead of a 12-bar ff block.
- **Sostenuto 115 / a tempo 119 (cycle 13), plus the later sigh 116–118 and accelerando 119–122: yes.** From climax to return the music runs 4 bars of release (fff held, then the sigh, sostenuto) and 4 bars of rebuild. The rebuild is a measured cello accelerando (quarters, eighths, triplets, sixteenths) with timpani crescendo, motto heads in Vla 121 and Vln II 122, and the T1 head in Cl 1 at 119. Nine bars from the 114 climax to the 123 return is shorter than Dvořák 9/i (261–277, 16 bars), but the written-out acceleration makes the arrival sound earned.
- **Ob 1 bridge 137–138: it works** as a lead-in. With the poco rit. it plays the same role as the fill at 43–46.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 66–67 and 160–161: both second-theme groups approach their final cadence with the same one-bar ii–V
**What is wrong.**
- **Length is not the problem.** T2 plus closing takes 53% of the exposition's time (69 of 130 s). Dvořák 9/i's S-zone (91–176) takes 56% (76 of 135 s), and Tchaikovsky 6/i's (89–160) takes 64% (264 of 410 s). The recapitulation's T2 (57.6 s) is shorter than Dvořák's (312–400, about 79 s). Do not cut bars.
- **Regularity is the problem.** Every phrase in both groups is four bars long, with one root-position chord per bar, ending ii–V–I on the next downbeat: 47–50, 51–54, 56–59, 64–67, 139–142, 143–146, 148–151, 158–161. That makes eight cadential approaches with the same harmonic rhythm. The two structural cadences, the EEC at 68 and the ESC at 162, are approached exactly like the internal ones, so the listener gets no signal that the theme is closing. Caplin puts the subordinate theme among the "loose" regions, and its standard means of loosening the final cadence is to expand it (the ECP). Rothstein calls rigid four-bar hypermeter in this repertoire the "great nineteenth-century rhythm problem".
- **Corpus.** Tchaikovsky 6/i's second group never repeats a bass formula: it has an A pedal (90–101), a stepwise or chromatic bass (102–116, 122–126) and D pedals (134–138, 150–160). Dvořák 9/i holds the dominant D for two bars (175–176) before its closing G at 177.
- **Critic 22's idea does not work as stated.** Critic 22's untested idea, a G pedal from the downbeat of 160, creates outer-voice parallel fifths (c'''/F at the end of 159 to d'''/G at 160). The same is true of an E♭ pedal at the downbeat of 66 (a♭''/D♭ to b♭''/E♭).

**Fix.** The bass moves to the dominant on beat 3 instead. This keeps ii on beats 1–2. The melody's 7th (c''' at 160, a♭'' at 66) becomes a 4–3 suspension over V that resolves on the next downbeat, and the dominant lasts six beats. The bass moves up a fourth while the melody falls, which is contrary motion. Only bass and inner parts change; melodies are untouched (the cycle-19 do-not-touch on the endings 64–68 and 158–161 covers the melodic rebuild, which stays).

Exposition, tTwo bar 20 (= 66):

### 2. Bar 69: "a tempo" is the wrong word after the rit. in a Poco meno mosso section
**What is wrong.** By convention "a tempo" restores the tempo in force before the rit., which here is ♩ = 100. The codetta is meant to return to the Allegro. Bar 163, which does the same thing, is marked "Tempo dell'Allegro". The metronome mark prevents an error, but the wording blurs one of the exposition's main arrival points.

**Fix (global.ily, closing line).** Change `\tempo "a tempo" 4 = 126` (the one followed by `s1*4` and "Poco più tranquillo") to `\tempo "Tempo dell'Allegro" 4 = 126`. Tested: it prints "Tempo dell'Allegro (♩ = 126)" at 69. Leave the "a tempo" at 77 as it is.

### 3. Introduction 1–14 (67 s): right length; do not lengthen or cut
- **Share of the movement.** It takes 15.8% of the movement. Dvořák 9/i's introduction (23 bars, 88 s) takes 19%, and Tchaikovsky 6/i's (18 bars, 81 s) takes 9% of a much longer movement. Ours is shorter in absolute time than either.
- **Density.** The sparseness of bars 1–8 (about 5 attacks per bar) matches Tchaikovsky 6/i 1–16 (4–8 per bar).
- **The empty bar 5.** The near-empty bar 5 (Cb only plus the CA upbeat) has a direct model in Dvořák 9/i bar 5 (0 attacks) and Tchaikovsky 6/i bar 6.
- **Shape.** It is one wave peaking at 12 (Ger6 into V). Dvořák uses two (10–12, then 15–22 with rising attack density). One wave suits a 14-bar introduction going into a forte Allegro. A second wave would need about 6 more bars (about 30 s) and would weaken the f entry at 15. No change.

### 4. Judgements, no change requested
- **Development quarters (8/12/12/14).** They are well proportioned, and the dip to 1–2 voices at 85–90 is the right reset after the 82 crash.
- **Recapitulation link 135–138.** It gives the C-major T2 the same space as the exposition's fill gave the A♭ T2: a medial caesura of about 8 s.
- **Coda.** At 12.5% the coda is above the corpus's 9–10%. That is justified because 167–178 is an epilogue for the whole symphony.
- **Minor: "Poco più tranquillo ♩ = 108" at 73.** It gives four tempo changes in 12 bars (66 rit., 69, 73, 77), and a 14% slowing over four bars of whole notes is barely audible. Making it an expressive word only (`\tempo \markup \medium \italic "tranquillo"`, without the 108) would be cleaner. This is optional.
- **Outside my lens.** A♭6 is the top note seven times (25, 31, 72, 114, 129, 132–133, 156), so the apotheosis at 156 has no registral exclusivity. The repeats at 129–133 sit in the protected 123–134, so I only note it.
