## What I studied

I studied the whole of Unit 5, text and images. I also used two pages of Unit 23 (the Neapolitan) for doubling in the N6. I wrote one-track-per-instrument MIDI from the score variables and ran five checks over all 182 bars: outer voices on an eighth grid (parallels, hidden intervals, d5→P5, contrary perfect intervals); all instrument pairs with critic 2's vl2.py; all pairs on a beat grid, which catches fifths broken by a passing tone (vl2 and analyze.py miss these); melodic leaps per instrument; and spacing and completeness per beat. Most hits were octave doublings, which §14 allows. The five places below are the ones that remain under the reading. I used no corpus files or web sources this cycle. The reading was enough for each point.

## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Bars 60–61: consecutive fifths in the outer voices, hidden by a passing tone, ending d5→P5 (applied)
**Problem.** Vln I and II hold B♭ (B♭5/B♭4) from 60.1. The bass reaches E♭2 at 60.3 (Cb, Vc, Bsn 2, harp), so the outer voices form a fifth for six eighths. On 60.4.5 the violins pass through B♭♭, a d5 over E♭. On 61.1 they reach A♭ over D♭2, another fifth. The underlying motion is B♭/E♭ → A♭/D♭, parallel fifths between melody and bass on two strong beats, at forte, with no flutes above. Ex. 5-19 (PDF 83) marks this "wrong". The passing tone adds a d5→P5 step, which Ex. 5-20b (PDF 84) calls "less good" because "the dissonant interval does not resolve". The beat-grid check finds it; the eighth-note checks do not, because the passing tone hides it.
**Fix.** Leave the protected violins (56–63) and Tpt 1 (60–63) unchanged. Put the bass on F♭ for all of bar 61, so D♭ minor stands in first inversion and moves into the F♭ chord already on beat 3. The d5 E♭–B♭♭ then resolves inward to the third F♭–A♭, as Ex. 5-20 requires. F♭→E♭ into the subito-p dominant at 62 is kept.
- `tTwoCb` bar 61: `fes,1 |` (was `des,2 fes,2`)
- `tTwoBsnTwo` bar 61: `fes1 |`
- `tTwoVc` bar 61: `fes,8( des fes aes) fes,8( aes, des fes) |`
- `tTwoHarpL` bar 61: `<fes, fes>2 <fes, fes>2 |` (the F♭ pedal is already set at 60)

Cl 1/Tpt 1 now meet the bass on F♭ by a hidden octave in an inner voice. Rule 5 on PDF 87 calls this the least noticeable case.

### F2. Bars 6–7: parallel octaves between Vln II and the bass in the introduction's string chorale (applied)
**Problem.** Bars 5–8 are a four-part pp string setting (Cb, Vc, Vla, Vln II) under the cor anglais. This is the texture Unit 5 is about (PDF 74). Vln II G4→F4 moves in double octaves with the bass G→F (Cb divisi G1/G2 → F1/F2). Ex. 5-18 (PDF 83) marks this "wrong". In bar 6, G also appears three times while E♭ and B♭ appear once each.
**Fix.** Revoice the entry chord. Both parts enter in bar 6, so nothing before it changes.
- `introVlnTwo`: `R1*5 | es'1\pp | f'1\< | e'1\p\> |`
- `introVla`: `R1*5 | bes1\pp | des'1\< | c'1\p\> |`

Bar 6 is now a complete E♭/G chord (G G B♭ E♭ + CA B♭), with no upper-voice gap over an octave (§5, PDF 77). Vln II moves E♭–F–E♮ against the bass G–F–C in contrary motion. The protected E♮ at bar 8, the CA motto and the swell are unchanged.

### F3. Bar 22: doubled leading tone resolving in octaves at the first theme's cadence (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 3 holds B3 through the V of 21.3–22.2, and the Vln I tune passes through B4 (22.2.5). Both go to C on 22.3, so two independent lines resolve the leading tone in octaves. PDF 76: the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V… One rule must be regarded as virtually absolute" (Ex. 5-4, "wrong (doubled leading tone)"). Strings and horns alone give this cadence and it is exposed. The horn is a pad, not a doubling of the tune, so the §14 exemption does not apply.
**Fix.** `tOneHnThree` bar 22: `b4 d'4 c'2 |` (was `b2 c'2`). On beat 2 the horn takes the fifth D. Vln I's C5–B4 then sounds as a 4–3 over a complete V, and Hn 3 falls D→C by step. The protected tune is unchanged.

### F4. Bar 173: cor anglais and violas in octaves C→D♭, with ♭2̂ tripled in the Neapolitan (applied)
**Problem.** The viola holds C4 from 171 and moves to D♭4 on 173.3 together with the CA motto C5→D♭5. Two real voices move in octaves (Ex. 5-18). The N6 then has D♭ in CA, Vla and Vc, and its bass tone F only in Bsn 2. The viola's D♭ then rises to E♭ at 174, so ♭2̂ resolves up in one voice and down in the other (CA D♭→C). PDF 76: double the stable tones. Unit 23 adds that in the N6 the bass (4̂) is normally doubled.
**Answer to c012.** Critic 12 moved the viola's D♭ to beat 3 to avoid C4 against D♭4 on beats 1–2. That reason still holds and this change keeps it: beats 1–2 stay C4.
**Fix.** `codaVla` bar 173: `c'2 f'2 |` (was `c'2 des'2`). The viola doubles the bass F, and F→E♭ at 174 is a step down into the 6/4's sixth. The iv–N6–i6/4–V harmony and the cello/CA duet are unchanged.

### F5. Bar 66: counterline and bass in fifths across an eighth passing note (applied; lowest rank)
**Problem.** The Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline (D♭4, F4, G4, B♭4) has F4 over B♭1 on beat 2 and B♭4 over E♭2 on beat 3. The fifths fall on two consecutive beats, separated only by the eighth G4 (§13, Ex. 5-19).
**Fix.** `tTwoClOne` and `tTwoHnTwo` bar 66: `des'4\> aes'8( g'8 bes'2~ |`. The A♭ is the seventh of ii7, and it resolves at once to G (PDF 76 on chord sevenths). The rising contour into B♭ is kept. This undoes nothing from cycle 51, which re-pitched notes at 65 and 66 to avoid unisons with the tune; I checked the result for that too.
