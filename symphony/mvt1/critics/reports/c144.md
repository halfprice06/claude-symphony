## Findings

### 1. Bars 131–134: two ff trombones take over the reprise of the first theme (APPLIED)

**Problem.** The recapitulation states the first theme low, in one octave (G3–F4), in Cl a2, Bsn a2, Tbn 1·2 and Vc, all ff. Two trombones at ff in their strongest register outweigh two clarinets in the chalumeau, two bassoons and the cellos together. What sounds is a trombone solo with the other instruments inaudible inside it. The clarinets add nothing, which is the case Adler gives for leaving an instrument out (principle 7).

**Evidence.** Adler, adler2 PDF 147, on *Istar* (Ex. XV-1, PDF 148): "Notice that the trombones are marked ppp so as not to dominate the entire orchestra." On the image, the cor anglais, clarinets, bass clarinet, bassoons and horns are f sempre and the two trombones ppp, all in unison. The same chapter says the most heavily scored octave tells the listener where the theme is (Mozart, PDF 151). Here that octave stays the heaviest with the trombones at f. The score already grades brass this way in its other tuttis: brass one level below the strings and woodwinds at 27–32, 139 and 167. The trombones were the one exception, because they carry the tune here.

**Fix (applied, recOne.ily).** Tbn 1·2 `r8 g8\f es'4.->` at 131 (was `\ff`). Every other part keeps its dynamic. Cl a2, Bsn a2 and Vc stay ff, so the theme is heard as a blend of woodwind, brass and cello. The trombones are still one forte level above Adler's ppp because this is the reprise. Both trombones share a staff and get the same dynamic. Their f at 138 is unchanged. No notes change, so the "notes of 131–134" protection (cycle 104) is not touched. The cycle-129 design is also kept: the low brass still leave at 135, now from f instead of ff.

### 2. Bars 23–25 and 135–137: Flute 2 doubles Flute 1, Oboe 1 and Violins I in unison and is wasted before the stop chord (APPLIED)

**Problem.** In the hemiola build, Fl 1 and Fl 2 both play the E♭5–A♭5 line, in unison with Ob 1 and the first violins (four lines at mf<). Two flutes in their middle register add almost nothing to an oboe and a violin section on the same notes. Both flutes are also already playing when the music reaches ff at 26/138, so the arrival gains no new instrument from the woodwinds.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151 (Mozart 40/iv, Ex. XV-3): if Mozart "had kept the unison of the flute and the violins … it would have added nothing to the heavy string scoring". Mozart places each wind where it "fill[s] out the space much better". PDF 150 (Barber, Ex. XV-2): the crescendo is built "by adding the brass instruments", and the doubling is enlarged "slightly in every measure". PDF 156: "An anacrusis or a rest before a certain color enters are two excellent ways of introducing new elements and making sure they are clearly perceived."

**Fix (applied, tOne.ily and recOne.ily).**
- `tOneFluteTwo`: `R1*3 | aes''2.->\ff r4 |` for 23–26.
- `recOneFluteTwo`: `R1*3 | aes''2.->\f r4 |` for 135–138.
- Fl 1 gets `\partCombineSoloI` with a bold "1." at 23 and `\partCombineAutomatic` at 26. At 135, Fl 1's `\partCombineUnisono` becomes `\partCombineSoloI`.

Fl 2 now enters on the stop chord, at the dynamic Fl 1 has there. Cycle 61's decision stands: Fl 1 stays in unison, so A♭6 is still first heard at 31/140. The pitches and rhythm of the hemiola are unchanged. Ob 2 already rests in these bars (cycle 119). The woodwind entry order is now: Fl 1, Ob 1, clarinets and bassoons at 23, then Fl 2 at 26, then the trombones, tuba and timpani at 26.

### 3. Bars 131–134: the theme is stated in a single octave (checked, no change)

Adler lists the multi-octave unison as the usual way to "summarize an old" idea (PDF 150). At 131 the theme has no octave doubling of its own. The octave spread comes from the counterline instead: Fl 1/Vln I at E♭6 and Ob 1/Vln II at E♭5, with the bass C in Cb, Cbsn and tuba. This is the protected "low theme with counterline above" (cycles 9, 17, 27). Mozart's rule (PDF 151) asks for the heaviest scoring in the theme's octave. After Finding 1, that octave still has Cl a2, Bsn a2, Vc and Tbn 1·2. I recommend no change.

### 4. Bars 158–161 and 82: octave weighting at the climaxes (checked, no change)

At 158–161 the tune has four lines in the top octave (Fl a2, Vln I, Vln II), two in the middle (Ob 1, Tpt 1 fff) and four in the lower one (Hn a2, Vla, Vc). The trumpet makes the middle octave the strongest in sound. The brass that are not on the tune hold chords (Smetana principle, PDF 154). The flutes, trumpets and trombones enter after rests (PDF 156). At 82, the motto head has five lines in the middle octave (Fl 2, Ob 1, Cl 1, Tpt 1, Vln II), one string section above with Fl 1, and violas below: this is Mozart's own layout (PDF 151). Both passages follow the reading and are protected.
