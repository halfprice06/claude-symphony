## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Below are the places that come closest to what the reading warns against, ranked by how much each could stand between this score and a master's work, with the reason each should stay.

### 1. Bars 67–68: five parts in unison on D♭4, then on C4, at p — keep

**What the scan shows.** At 67.4 Cl 1, Hn 2, Hn 3, the violas and the cellos all sound D♭4. At 68 Cl 1, Hn 2, Hn 3 and the violas hold C4, and the cello arpeggio adds a fifth C4 on beat 3. Hn 4 and Vln II are on A♭3 and the violas on F4, so at 68 the space A♭3–F4 holds eight parts at p under the rit. This is the thickest soft lower-middle register in the movement, the situation of Ex. 1 (Sebesky PDF 12–13).

**Why it stays.** Ex. 1's problem was an independent moving line (F♯–G–G♯–G♮) that added a rhythm and a line of its own to the lower-middle register. At 67–68 every part is sustained, and all of them carry out the same voice-leading step: the seventh D♭ of the V7 of A♭ resolves to C on the deceptive F-minor chord. In this bar that unison makes a difference to the result (Sebesky PDF 12):
- It ends the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline (D♭4→C4).
- It resolves Hn 3's seventh.
- It is the chord the violas lead into from their protected figure at 60–67.

The primary element is above all of it: Vln I lands on A♭4 and starts the sixteenth-note scale into the f codetta at 68.3. Nothing below competes with that scale rhythmically. Each possible cut makes something worse:
- Cutting Hn 3 leaves its D♭4 unresolved in that voice.
- Cutting the violas' C4 thins the one soft bar that cushions the jump to f at 69.
- Changing the cello's top note to B♭3 or A♭3 only moves the unison onto Hn 4 or Vln II.

Not applied.

### 2. Bars 47–55: the S tune inside its own pad — keep (protected)

At 47 the tune (Hn 1 + Vc) is on C4, which is also the lower note of the violas' div. pad (C4/E♭4), and Vln II's held A♭4 lies above it. At 50 the tune's A♭4–G4 sits a step under Vln II's held B♭4, and at 52 four parts are on F4. Read literally, the focus rule (Sebesky PDF 13) would move the pad out of the tune's register.

Adler gives the case for keeping it (adler1 PDF 135, on Brahms 2/ii). Background in the melody's register works when the melody is in the best register of its instrument and the background is in the "palest or most nondescript" registers and is rhythmically unlike the melody. That is the case here:
- The pad is a half section, pp, in whole notes.
- The tune is horn plus cellos in the cellos' tenor register, mf dolce, with a swell on every bar.
- The harp arpeggios move only on beats 1–2, while the tune holds its long first notes.

Focus comes from colour and dynamic, not from register. The passage is protected (47–55 in many cycles; c339 compares it with Dvořák 6/i at letter B). Not applied.

### 3. Bars 56–67: rhythmic interlock of tune, counterline and accompaniment — follows Ex. 2A; keep

This passage is the movement's clearest use of Ex. 2A's method (Sebesky PDF 17–18):
- **64:** the Cl 1/Hn 2 counter moves (C4–D4–E♭4 eighths) only on beats 3–4, while the violins hold A♭5.
- **65:** the counter holds D♭4–A♭4 while the tune moves.
- **66:** the counter's eighths fall on beat 2, inside the tune's held B♭5.
- **67:** the tune's falling eighths sound over the counter's held B♭4.
- **60–61:** the cello and viola arpeggios fill the held B♭5.
- **62–63:** the counter rises through the tune's tied C6.

So the secondary line never diverts the ear from the primary one (PDF 13). The harp's half-bar chords at 64–67 sit between the tune's two octaves (A♭4–C5–F5 at 64). They are plucked, they decay, and they are mf under f strings. They also strike the half-bar harmonic rhythm, which no sustained part attacks, so they make the "legitimate difference" (PDF 12). Not applied.

### 4. Bars 69–72: viola eighths between the two octaves of the P head — keep (protected)

In the codetta the P head is in two octaves: C.a., Cl 1·2 and Vln II at E♭4–C5, and Hn 1·2 at E♭3–C4. The violas' arpeggio eighths (A♭3–E♭4) run between and through them, and the S head is on top. This is the lower-middle register Ex. 1 says to lighten. It stays for these reasons:
- The violas give the only continuous eighth-note motion under two themes moving in longer values, so they hold the f tutti together.
- The P head's horn octave is a doubling, not a separate line that the violas could hide.
- Bar 69–72's harmony, dynamics and viola articulation are protected (c331, c337, c347).

Not applied.

### 5. Bars 147–150: the same bass and harmony as 47–50 — keep (confirms c277's Finding 3)

The bass C–F–D–G is the exposition's A♭–D♭–B♭–E♭ moved to C (I–IV–ii–V both times). This is against the letter of Ex. 3/3A (PDF 19). Sebesky's aim, to avoid repetition that loses the listener (PDF 20), is met in two other ways:
- The S head is reharmonised at 56, 97, 103, 107 and 171, including a stepwise bass of the Ex. 3A kind at 103–106 (D–G–A–B♭–C–B).
- At 147 the colour is new: Hn 1 + violas + cellos over the Vln II sul tasto tremolo, with cor anglais fills.

The plain C-major statement is protected (c311). Not applied.

### 6. Bars 38–40 and 9–11: bassoon doubling the cellos at pitch — keep

At 38–40 Bsn 1 and the cellos both hold A♭3 (with Hn 4 at 38). At 9–11 Bsn 1 doubles the cellos (G3–A♭3–A♭3) and Bsn 2 doubles the basses an octave up. Both are sustained, soft, under a crescendo. They add a reed edge, not a line, and both feed the tutti that follows (the four-horn call at 41; the f German sixth at 12). So the economy test (PDF 12) is met. Bar 37's four-part E♭-minor chord is a protected seed. Not applied.
