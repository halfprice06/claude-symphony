## Findings

The harp part is already in good order. Cycle 136 worked through this same chapter and its fixes (59–60, 62, 53, 57, 164) hold. I found nothing that a harpist could not play. The three findings below are small: one gap in the damping scheme and two unnecessary pedal changes. All three are applied in my scratch copy.

### 1. Bar 68: no damping at the deceptive cadence (applied)
At 67 the right hand plays the V7 arpeggio E♭5–G5–B♭5–D♭6 on beats 3–4, and the left hand has struck E♭2–E♭3 on beat 1. At 68 the left hand moves to F1–F2 (the deceptive cadence to F minor, under the rit.) and the right hand rests for two beats before its rolled F-minor chord. Nothing in the harp stops the dominant. The strings ring, so G5 and D♭6 sound over the F-minor chord, and the low E♭ octave rings a whole step under the new F octave. E♭ and F are neighbouring strings, so placing the finger on F does not damp the E♭.

Evidence: Adler PDF 113–114: the lower two octaves are "somber, dark, and sonorous" and the middle two "rich and warm", so these strings sustain for several seconds. PDF 117: damping (sons étouffés) needs time for the hand to stop the strings. The right hand's two beats of rest on 68 give that time. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46 adds that harp strings ring on through a change of harmony. The score already damps in the same situation, where a harp chord would ring into a new harmony during a rest: at 62 (sub. p after the F♭ chord), 163 and 164 (before the A♭ change and the dominant). Bar 68 is the one place where this is missing. The goal chord of the exposition's second group is the one that gets blurred.

Fix (tTwo.ily, tTwoHarpR, last bar):
`r2^\markup \small \italic "étouffez" <f' aes' c''>2\arpeggio |`
No notes change. The harp part keeps the word, because partCleanHarp removes only pedal letters.

### 2. Bar 158: the B♮ pedal change is not needed, and the A♮ can be marked later (applied; score only)
At 158 the left-hand staff reads "B♮ A♮". The harp plays no B of any kind from 156 to 182 (162 A–C–E, 163 A–C–F, 164 A♭–D–F, 165 G–D–F, 166 C–G–C, 182 C–G–C). The B♮ only adds work. The pedal must also go back: movement II's harp needs B♭ for its first entry (diagram D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭). Leaving B on B♭ saves two left-foot moves, one at 158 and one during the attacca. At the join the harpist then has to change only D (left foot) and E (right foot), and the two can move together (Adler PDF 112).

The A♮ is not needed until 162. Marked at 158 beat 1, it moves the pedal one bar after the f A♭5 of 157 was struck. Cycle 136 accepted this because the ff tutti covers the slide. Adler PDF 113 says the change is made "just a split second before playing the note", and that "the experienced harpist usually will make the pedal change even later than it is marked". Marking it at 161 gives the A♭ string three more bars to decay and still leaves a full bar before 162.

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoHarpL): `R1_\markup \small "B♮ A♮" | R1*3 |` becomes `R1*3 | R1_\markup \small "A♮" |`.
The harp part does not print pedal letters (MOLA PDF 5), so this changes the score only. It still corrects what the score tells the harpist and the conductor.

### 3. Bar 47: the opening diagram sets G♮, but the first G is G♭ (applied; score only)
The 47 diagram (`pedAflat`, D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G♮ A♭) is followed by a "G♭" change on beat 4 of 50. The first G played is G♭4 at 51, and G♮ is not used until 54 (the "G♮" mark is at 53 beat 3). No G is played in 47–50. If the diagram sets G♭, the change at 50 disappears and the G pedal moves only once, at 53. Adler PDF 112 asks for the setting "at the beginning" so that the harpist is ready for the first notes. A setting that has to change before its first G is used gives the player an extra move for no reason. It also makes cycle 171's note about the G♭ change under a ringing G5 at 50 irrelevant.

Fix (global.ily): `pedAflat = \markup \harp-pedal #"^-^|^-^^"`. In tTwo.ily, tTwoHarpL bar 50 becomes `es,,1 |` (the "G♭" markup is removed). The 155 diagram and every other mark are unchanged. This touches cycle 136's "pedal diagrams at 47, 155" protection. That cycle protected the diagrams because they were correct. They stay as diagrams, and the 47 diagram is still correct for every note. It now matches the first G.

### Checked and not proposed
- 51→52: C2 (whole note) rings into the D♭2 of 52 (neighbouring strings, a minor second in the sonorous low register). This is ordinary Romantic harp accompaniment. A harpist damps a stepwise bass by habit when the hand is placed on the next string, and Adler's own Brahms example (Ex. IV-7) has no damping marks in a similar line. Unlike 68, there is no rest here, and the harmony moves on in every bar. No mark is needed.
- 56–59, right hand C6–D♭7 at p under 8va: this is the "light and clear" top register (PDF 114) above the violins' tune. It was placed there deliberately by c157, and the 8va is kept in the part (c245). It is playable.
- 56 F♭ against the violas' E♮: the spelling is correct by Ex. IV-3 (E♭ still rings from 55 and is needed again at 59).
- 162 f under ff: the harp is covered (PDF 114; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47). This item was closed as no change in c197 and I do not reopen it.
- 182 final chord C2–G2–C3 ppp, solid, damped with "étouffez": a correct register and notation for a low, soft close (PDF 113–115). The C and G strings are not affected by the pedals movement II needs, so the harpist can reset D and E during 167–181.
- Harmonics (PDF 116) are not used in movement I. Movements II, III and IV use them. I do not propose adding any: the harp's tacets 69–155 and 167–181 are protected (HISTORY: "held-back harp and percussion"; c136: "harp tacets"). Harmonics in movement I would also use up the colour that movement II introduces at its 83.
