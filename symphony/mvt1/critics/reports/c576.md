# Critic 576: playability, instrument by instrument (v576)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, Appendix A (adler2 PDF 302-312), fourth pass. I read the text of all eleven pages. PDF 302-307 are range charts and their OCR is unusable, so I rendered all of them at 110 dpi and looked at each. I rotated PDF 302 (printed sideways) and read it again, and I re-rendered the timpani chart on PDF 307 at 250 dpi to read the drum brackets. PDF 308-311 list instrument names in four languages, and PDF 312 lists common orchestral terms. I rendered those pages too and checked the OCR against the images.

Method: I built v576 unchanged in /tmp/critic-576/work. Then I took every player's two lowest and three highest sounding pitches, with their bars, from `full.midi` (script /tmp/critic-576/scripts/ranges.py). A second script (/tmp/critic-576/scripts/edges.py) listed every note in each part's outer fifth of its range, with its MIDI velocity, so I could check the dynamic of each extreme note as well as its pitch. Written ranges were converted to sounding pitch for the transposing instruments (C4 = middle C).

## Findings

This fourth pass agrees with cycles 286, 376 and 486. Every note in v576 is inside Adler's professional ranges. The extremes for every player are the same as in v486, so the ninety cycles since then have moved no part closer to an edge. I recommend no change. The points below record what this pass checked beyond the earlier ones, and the two improvements I considered and rejected.

### 1. Extreme notes against their dynamics: no change
Earlier passes checked pitch only. This pass also checked the dynamic of every note in the outer fifth of each part's range. The high notes all come at a loudness that suits them: flutes and Vln I F6-A♭6 at f-fff (velocities 95-127), Tpt 1 F5-A♭5 at f-fff, Ob 1 D6 at f, Hn 3·4 sounding C5 at ff, Cl 1·2 B♭5-C6 at mf-fff. The low notes are mostly forte, where the low register speaks: Ob 2 B3 at ff (117), Bsn 2 B♭1 at f (92-105), Tpt 3 B3 at f-ff, tuba F1-G1 at f-ff, and the cellos and violas on C at ff at 33. The soft extremes are where Adler's chart and the instrument chapters expect them: Fl 1 B♭4-C5 at p at 73 and 109 (low flute, soft accompaniment), viola A♭5 at p at 110 in treble clef, Tbn 1 E♭4 at pp in the 176 chorale (well below the tenor trombone's D5 top), Hn 1 written G5 at p in the horn call at 43 (at the amateur limit but standard for a professional first horn; the call is protected). None of these notes is a hazard. Applied: no.

### 2. Timpani assignment against the drum chart: no change
The chart on PDF 307 (read at 250 dpi) gives the five drums overlapping ranges. The score's tuning, "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭" (intro), puts each note well inside one drum: G2 on the largest drum, A♭2 on the 28" (F2-C3), C3 on the 25" (B♭2-F3), E♭3 on the 23" (D3-A3). The only drum that is ever retuned is the 28", to B♭2 (muta at 34, B♭ roll at 40, muta back at 44) and to B2 (muta at 84, stroke at 97, back at 98, A♭ needed at 115). Both new pitches are inside F2-C3, and every change has at least four bars of rest. Protected by c276, c366, c476 and c566. Applied: no.

### 3. Parts: page turns and transposed ranges hold
I ran `build-parts.sh` on the unchanged copy: rc 0, 0 warnings, 30 parts. The five parts that tie into movement II (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3) have two pages each and end on an even page. The double-bass part (4 pages) turns in the rests at 171-174, and the harp part (3 pages) in the rests at 145-146. The written ranges in the parts match Adler's written columns: bass clarinet in treble clef down to written C3 (low-C instrument, as Adler's parenthesised extension allows), Hn 4 written C3 in new-notation bass clef, cor anglais written up to C6. Applied: no.

### 4. Could the ranges serve the music better? Two ideas tested and rejected
(a) The flutes never use their low register. Fl 1's lowest note is B♭4 and Fl 2's is B4, so the octave C4-B4 that Adler's chart (PDF 303) gives the flute is unused, and the flute is always above the violins' middle register or doubling the top of a tutti. The soft, dark low-flute colour that late-Romantic scores use in quiet passages is missing. I looked for a bar where it would add something. The soft passages where it could sound (1-14, 47-55, 73-80, 125-126, 145-157, 171-182) are either protected (intro, S at 47-55, Vln II 73-80, the soft horn chords, recap S, coda) or already coloured by clarinets, cor anglais and bass clarinet in that octave, which would cover a low flute. At 73 Fl 1 already reaches C5 at p in its swell to A♭5. A new low-flute line would mean breaking protected bars for a colour that the clarinets and violas would mostly hide. Rejected.
(b) The double basses never go below F1, so C minor's lowest tonic in the strings is C2 (cellos, 33 and 166). As c486 found, the low C1 is in the contrabassoon alone at 167-170. Adler marks the bass extension "not found on every instrument" (PDF 302), and 167-170 is a protected, quoted stop-chord passage. Rejected for the same reasons as in c486.
Neither idea meets the bar of a real improvement that is worth breaking a protection. Applied: no.
