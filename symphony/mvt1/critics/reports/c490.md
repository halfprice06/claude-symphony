### 2. Mixture and cross relations across the whole movement: clean (no change)

The reading (PDF 388, 395) gives the order rule (♭6 goes to 5 and is not replaced by ♮6 first). PDF 394 gives the cross-relation rule, with its doubling exemption in Ex. 23-12b. This pass I checked both mechanically over all 182 bars, not only at the known spots.

**Order rule.** The per-voice scan finds one ♭6–♮6–5 pattern by pitch: Vln I·II at 162.4.5–163. The source spells it G♯ (`a''8 gis''8 | a''4.`). It is a lower neighbour to A, so A♭ is not cancelled before G. A♭ itself resolved to G at 161.3, before the A♮ chords of 162–163. Then 164 brings ♭6 back (iiø7), and it goes to G at 164.3. This is ♮6–♭6–5, the good order.

**Cross relations.** The outer-voice scan looked for any semitone pair split between top and bass on adjacent eighths where no single voice states the succession. It finds nine:

- 70.4.5 (D/D♭, vii°7/V → V7 in A♭). This was kept in series 3 ("normal vii°7/V → V7; bass protected c103", HISTORY line 890).
- 96.3.5 and 96.4.5 (B♭/B, the fugato's B♭ minor shifted up to B minor at 97). This is a protected seam.
- 100.4.5 and 102.4.5 (G/F♯ and F♯/F inside the B-minor block, and the swerve to D minor at 103). Both are protected.
- 107.3.5, 107.4, 108.4.5 and 109.3.5. These are the c262 seeds, including Vln II D♯4→F4 at 108→109, which the finale's violas answer at IV 219–220.

None of them is new, and each was kept with a stated reason. Ex. 23-13 (PDF 395) allows such outer-voice conflicts where they carry the movement's chromatic or modal argument. Here they mark the development's semitone key shifts (B♭→B, B→D, E→B♭), which is that argument. No change.

### 3. The last bars keep A♭ out on purpose; do not add a plagal iv♭ at 179–182 (candidate considered and rejected)

The reading's most "characteristic use of mixture" is Ex. 23-5 (Brahms 3/ii, PDF 390): ♭VI opens a short minor passage that ends in a plagal IV♭–I, and Brahms puts it in a soft coda. This movement's coda has no iv after 178. The last subdominant colour is the D♭ neighbour chord at 178.3 (A♭3 in the violas, F4 in Vln II). From 179 to 182 no part plays A♭: the MIDI shows only C, E♭/E, G and Vln I's D♭5 at 180.3.

I considered restoring a iv colour at 180.3 (violas `es'2 aes2`, Vln II `g'2 f'2`) so that ♭6 falls to 5 just before the E♮. I rejected it for three reasons.

- **c160 already removed exactly this.** The comments are in coda.ily lines 76 and 89. Its reason was to keep Vln I's D♭5 a bare neighbour over C minor, as at bar 3, rather than a third D♭/iv sonority after 173 and 178. Restoring the iv would undo that fix without answering its reason.
- **The cycle needs A♭ fresh at the join.** Movement I's last chord, C major, is III♯ of A♭. Movement II's bar 2 then takes the violas E4→E♭4 in one voice and puts A♭ in the bass. The reading's I–♭VI relation (PDF 390–391) is thus spread across the attacca: movement II itself is the ♭VI that follows movement I's major tonic. An A♭ at 180 would anticipate the bass of movement II's bar 2 by two bars and weaken that arrival.
- **The A♭ colour is already used heavily.** It belongs to the movement's three climaxes (82 ff, 122 fff, 160 fff), all on A♭. The quiet close is the one place where it is absent, and that absence is what makes movement II's A♭ new.

Keep 179–182 as they are.

### 4. A♭ and E in movement I: weighting matches the later movements (keep)

The unit's subject is mixture as a long-range device. In this symphony the cycle of keys C–A♭–E–C is itself made of two mixture relations to C: ♭VI (A♭, from minor) and III♯ (E, secondary mixture; PDF 393, 395). Movement I weights them in the same order and proportion as the later movements:

- **A♭.** It carries the exposition's whole second group (47–76) and all three climaxes. 160 is the textbook ♭VI as the goal of a deceptive cadence (Ex. 23-6, PDF 390–391), and it is reached by a leap to A♭6 (compare Ex. 23-2g, PDF 388). The Adagio follows directly in A♭.
- **E.** It is a two-beat glimpse at 78.3–78.4, a G♯ over the E bass turned at once to E minor (79). That is the Brahms 3/i kind of deliberate conflict (Ex. 23-13, PDF 395), and it is the scherzo's own opening move (G♯ in Hn 1 at III 1–2, E minor at III 7–8). The scherzo is two movements away and needs its E major to sound new.

I considered whether 78.3 is "a moment a listener would not remember" and should be lengthened into a full bar of E major. I rejected it. A longer E-major chord would weaken the scherzo's opening, and it would also stretch the protected 77–83 key circle (c230, c262), whose bass A♭–E–C is the symphony's key cycle in small. That circle works because each station is brief. Keep.

### 5. The soft Picardy thirds at 8 and 182 remain correct for the cycle (keep)

The reading says the Picardy third "can create effects of startling power" (PDF 391), and Ex. 23-7 shows it ff. Movement I uses ♯3 only at pp (8, Vln II E4) and ppp (182, violas' lone E4). Ex. 23-1b (PDF 386) shows why: the delayed mode should come back late, ff, and in full orchestra. That place is IV 222→223, where Tpt 1 resolves B4→C5 (movement I's last trumpet pitch is B4 at 170), the violas take E4 at movement I's pitch (IV 219–223) and the cymbals crash for the first time in the symphony. A louder or more exposed E♮ at 182 would spend that power early and would break the attacca, since movement II turns the E back to E♭ at its bar 2. This agrees with c290 and c390. No change.
