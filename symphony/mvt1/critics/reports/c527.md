### 1. 171–174: the duet's background, and the basses held back until 175 (keep)

The score. One solo cello plays the minor S (E♭4–D4 | C4–A♭4–G4 | F4–E♭4–D♭4–F4–A♭4 | C5–B4), and the cor anglais plays the motto above it (G4–E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5). Under them are only the violas, pp (C4–B3 | C4 | C4–A♭3 | E♭4–D4), and Bsn 2, pp (C3 | F3 | F3 | G2), with Bsn 1 on G3 at 174. The other cellos and the double basses are silent. The basses and the tutti cellos come in at 175 with the trombone chorale.

I checked this against three passages of the reading:
- **PDF 162 (V-38, Fauré).** "To produce a somber background, the lower strings only may be used." The background here is violas and one bassoon. That is darker than Fauré's divided violas and cellos, but it keeps the cellos' colour for the soloist alone.
- **PDF 139 (V-16, Haydn).** "The simple addition of the cellos and double basses … sets off the first and the second statement." Because the basses rest through 171–174, their pp G1 at 175 marks the chorale as a new stage. Adding Cb pizz. under the duet (the Brahms V-7 bass) would spend that entry four bars early. I considered this and rejected it.
- **PDF 135 (register).** The scan shows two overlaps. At 172.1 the solo cello's C4 meets the violas' C4 for one beat. At 174.1–2 the solo's C5 meets the cor anglais' C5. At 172 the two lines converge on C4 in contrary motion (viola B3→C4, cello D4→C4), and the cello leaves on beat 2. At 174 the duet's two voices meet on C, the fourth above the bass G in the cadential 6/4, on the strong beat of their last bar. Both are the voice-leading of two real melodies, not a background that sits on the tune. Cycle 247 also recorded the 172 overlap as kept.

Keep 171–174 as written. Cycle 477 protects it ("171–174 solo cello and C.a.").

### 2. Spacing scan, whole movement (follows)

Adler PDF 151 (V-26) says one "usually leaves greater space between the lower than between the upper instruments, just as … between the more sonorous lower partials." I scanned every quaver of full.midi, leaving out the timpani and harp. The test flags a lowest interval smaller than a fourth when the second-lowest pitch is below E3. There are three hits:
- 60.3.5: G♭2–B♭2 (Cb pedal, Vc arpeggio)
- 61.3.5: F♭2–A♭2 (same layout)
- 147.4.5: C3–D3 (Bsn 1 pedal, Vc tune passing tone)

Each lasts one quaver and is a moving arpeggio or passing note against a held bass. None is a sustained close chord in the low register. The tutti chords (26, 27–32, 82, 122, 138, 158–160, 170, 182) all have open spacing at the bottom. No change.

### 4. 47–55: the viola pad and the tune share pitches (keep; not reopened)

My unison scan finds the same contacts that c247, c257, c447 and c494 recorded. The divided viola pad holds C4, F4, D♭4/B♭3, E♭4 and C4 while the Hn 1+Vc tune passes through those pitches (47, 48.2–3, 49, 51.1, 52.3, 55). Adler's advice to keep the background out of the melody's register is conditional (PDF 135): "in some instances, especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color." He then accepts Brahms's layout, where the background lies around the cello tune, for two reasons. The foreground is in its best register, and the background is in a pale register with a radically different rhythm. Both hold here:
- The tune is cello and horn together, mf/mp dolce, in the cello's A-string register.
- The pad is a pp half-section of violas in whole notes on the G and D strings, a different rhythm from the moving tune.

The passage is protected as "neutral accompaniment 47–55" and "47–55 viola pad" (c447). I have no new argument against that reason, so I do not reopen it.

### 5. 69.3: the I6 of A♭ with its third in four octaves (follows, protected)

The PDF 152 scan flags one major first-inversion chord with the bass doubled above. At 69.3 the bass moves A♭→C (I–I6), and C sounds in C2/C3 (bass), C4 (Hn 1·2), C5 (Ob 1, Tpt 1, Cl 1·2, C.a., Vln II) and C6 (Fl 1·2, Vln I). The root is in Ob 2/Tpt 2 (A♭4) and Hn 3 (A♭3). The fifth is in Bsn 1/Vla (E♭4) and Hn 4 (E♭3).

Every upper C belongs to one of the two heads: the S head's held top note and the P head's C on that beat. The doubling therefore comes from the melodies, not from the harmony. The I6 is a passing chord on beat 3, and the P head moves to A♭ on beat 4, which restores the root. Adler's rule is about chord distribution, and he puts melodic considerations first (PDF 150: "spacing, register, and melodic considerations"). 69–72 is protected (c147, c187, c337, c464). The N6 chords at 21.1 and 141.1, also flagged, double the fourth scale degree in the bass, which PDF 152 allows. Keep.

### 6. Diminuendo by thinning, and the bass on the tonic (follows)

Adler PDF 154 says Tchaikovsky's diminuendo comes from thinning and from "the drop-out of the bass each time it reaches the tonic." The score thins out the same way:
- **163–166.** The ff texture of 162 is reduced to Vc arpeggios, the Cb line, the harp and the soft horns, and the bass reaches C2 at 166, p.
- **179–182.** B.cl, Tbn 2 and the bass trombone drop out at the end of 179, Tbn 1 at 181.1, and the timpani change from the pulse to a ppp roll.

The bass does not drop out on the final tonic because the movement does not end there. The Vc C3 crosses into movement II, and the Cb releases with the timpani and the harp at the end of 182, so the bass's departure is the last event before the attacca. This is the PDF 154 point in its own form: the bass's last note is the one the ear follows out. Keep.
