# Critic 571: Melody and memorability (v571)

Lens: the motto, P, S and the horn call: their contour, rhythm, climaxes and development.
Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations, pedal point (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371), fourth pass. Book page = PDF page + 2.

Method. I read all 26 text pages in full, including the Points for Review (PDF 369) and the exercises (PDF 370–371). I rendered every page at 110 dpi (/tmp/critic-571/pg-346-346.png to pg-371-371.png) and looked at each one, because the OCR loses the notes and the labels (for example "good / sometimes / avoid" under Ex. 21-9, "poor / good" under Ex. 21-12, the "avoid A4" bracket in Ex. 21-16d and the figures under the Strauss waltz, Ex. 21-15f). I read c481 (this lens and this reading, third pass) so as not to repeat it. I then diffed v481 against v571 to find what changed since that pass: c498 (129), c502 (157), c508 (163), c512 (177), c514 and c522 (158–159), c518 (108) and c568 (178–181). I built v571 unchanged in /tmp/critic-571/work, rebuilt full.midi and wrote two scripts. /tmp/critic-571/scripts/susp.py finds every note that is held from one beat into the next, is dissonant against the lowest sounding note there, and then falls by step in the same part, and it lists any other part (not the bass pitch class) that sounds the tone of resolution meanwhile. Its output is /tmp/critic-571/susp.txt. /tmp/critic-571/scripts/dump.py prints the sounding pitches part by part on each beat or half beat. I used it at 23–24, 47–55, 108, 113–114, 143–147, 157–160, 168 and 176–182.

## Findings

No change is recommended. The changes made since the third pass (c481) all agree with the reading, and the scan turns up no early resolution of the kind the reading calls "poor". I looked again for a place where a theme could sing more or a climax land harder, within what this reading supports. I found none that improves on the present notes without breaking a protection whose reason the reading does not answer. The details follow, ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. Re-scan of the score after the series 4 changes (no change)

susp.py finds 10 held, falling dissonances with the tone of resolution sounding elsewhere. None is a case of the rule (PDF 352 §12):

- 23.4–24.1 and 135.4–136.1: Cl 1 F4 over B is the seventh of V6/5 (G–B–D–F), a chord tone. It is not held into a new harmony. The E♭5 above it belongs to the three-beat groups of the tune.
- 114.1–114.2 (six parts): the tutti C over D falls to B at 114.3, inside vii°7. The violas' B3 at 114.2 is the quaver lower neighbour of their wedge figure (c'8 c'8 b8( c'8)). It is a quick neighbour against held winds and brass, which is the reading's exception for "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre" (PDF 352).
- 168.3 (Bsn 1, Vc F4): a chord tone of iv over the C pedal. Over a pedal, dissonance against the bass is free (PDF 368).

Then the places changed since c481, beat by beat from full.midi:

- 108 (c518): Vla C5 at 108.3 over B1/B2, falling to B4 on 108.4. No upper part sounds B (Vln II has A3 and D♯4). This is the 9–8 with its resolution only in the bass, the one case the reading calls "good" (Ex. 21-12d). The swell now peaks on the dissonance, as §4 asks (PDF 348).
- 157.3 (c502): Vln II's accented B♭4 has no A♭ under it any more. Its resolution A♭4 arrives only at 157.4.
- 158–159 (c514, c522): the melody's E over V at 159.1 is a consonant 6 and rises to F. That is allowed, because "a consonant suspension does not need to resolve; it can move up or down" (PDF 348). The violas now rise E–F with it instead of striking F against the held E.
- 177 (c512): Cl 1 C5, prepared at 176 as a 6 over E♭, is the 7 over D and falls to B4 at 177.3. Vln II's A♭4 (the 5th) leaves for F4, the 10th above the bass, at that moment. That is Ex. 21-9c on the image, the "sometimes" case. The violas keep A♭3 for one more beat, so the release is vii°6/5 before V4/3 at 177.4. Ex. 21-9d warns against a 6/5 "unless the figures specify it". Here the 6/5 is the planned dominant-function seventh chord, so the warning does not apply.
- 178–181 (c568): Vln I's E♭5 is held over the D♭ bass from 178.3 and becomes the 10th when the bass falls to C at 179. This is the 9–(10) with moving bass (Ex. 21-14b). From 179 the C-minor chord is tied over the C pedal (Vln II G4, Vla E♭4, Vc C3, Cb C2), and only the motto moves. The ending pedal (§28, PDF 367) now holds, and the motto's D♭5 at 180.3 is a dissonance against the bass over a pedal, which the reading allows (PDF 368).

### 2. The themes against the reading's literature examples (no change)

c481 set out where each theme's memorable moment comes from in this reading's terms. I checked every one of those points on v571's notes, and they still hold. I add two points that c481 did not make:

- **S's half close at 50 is an indirect suspension.** Vln II holds A♭4 through 49. At 50.1 Hn 1 and the cellos take A♭4 over the E♭ bass, and Vln II moves to B♭4. The tune's A♭–G at 50 is therefore a 4–3 over V. Its dissonance was prepared in another instrument, which the reading calls typical of "instrumental rather than vocal style" (PDF 357 §17, Ex. 21-22). This is why the tune's leap D♭4–A♭4 into the dissonance does not sound unprepared. The parallel place at 54 (Cl 1/Vc A♭4 after C5) is a plain appoggiatura with no preparation. It is the weaker of the two, but it is the second phrase of a period and comes in the protected accompaniment 47–55. Keep it.
- **The Strauss figure and the Scarlatti scale define S's two peaks.** On the image of Ex. 21-15f, the 7/6 is held across the bar before it resolves into V7. Vln I's C6 at 62–63 does the same thing. On the image of Ex. 21-27a, Scarlatti brings in the 3rd of a delayed 4–3 as part of a fast descending scale. The S theme's last climax does that at 164–165: C6 is held over G and resolves to B5 inside the scale B5–A5–G5–F5–D5. The movement's lyric high points are the reading's two best-described suspension types. No other theme statement needs one.

### 3. Where the music still falls short within this lens, and why no change meets the bar

I looked for a phrase that does not sing or a climax that does not land. The candidates were:

- the P sentence's close at 21–22 (protected, c421 and c561: the plain scale fall contrasts with the suspended climaxes at 31 and 141–142);
- the horn call's small number of statements (c570 open item: the places where a statement could be added are protected or held in reserve);
- the plain appoggiatura at 54 (point 2 above).

The reading could support one more device in each place: a cadential 4–3 at 22, an anticipation before 22.3, or an indirect preparation at 53. Each would add decoration to a moment that the movement keeps plain on purpose, or would touch a protected passage. Brahms and Dvořák also keep their first statements plain and save the held dissonance for the peak, and this movement already does so (62–63, 164–165). The rest of the gap to a master's movement lies in material held back for the finale: S at forte in C major, the B→C arrival on a C-major chord, and the trumpet melody. Those are reserves, not faults. I tested nothing in the score, because no candidate passed the bar.
