# Critic 074: v93, lens 4 (orchestration). The colour plan over time

## What I studied
- **v93 score:** I built a MIDI with one track per player (all 30 parts, no part-combining) and grouped the lines that double each other in every bar. From that and the score I wrote down, for each of the 186 bars, which instruments carry the melody. Scripts and tables are in /tmp/critic-074/ (groups.txt, scripts/colour.py).
- **Corpus:** I ran the same grouping on the MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/`) and checked passages by pitch:
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 19–22: divided violas alone.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 125–129: woodwinds alone in octaves.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 153–160: clarinet falling to D3, with the bassoon finishing the phrase.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 201–204: trombone chorale.
  - Dvořák 9/i 24–27: horns 3·4.
  - Dvořák 9/i 91–94: flute 1 and oboes in unison on G4–C5.
  - Dvořák 9/i 149–156: flute 1 solo on G4–D5, down to D4.
- **Rimsky-Korsakov,** *Principles of Orchestration*, https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/33900/pg33900.txt:
  - the table of extreme registers (flute low register "dull, cold");
  - the violas, which carry melody "in the alto-tenor register… generally doubled by other strings or by the wood-wind";
  - "Oboe + Clarinet. A fuller quality than that of either instrument";
  - the balance rules: in f, Violins I = 2 wind; in p, 1 wind; in f, horns are about half as strong as the other brass.
- **Adler:** no free text online, so I did not use it.

## The colour sequence of v93 (melody carriers)
1. **Introduction:** low strings + B.cl (1–4) → CA (5–8) → Vln I (+Fl 1) (9–12) → Hn 1 (13–14).
2. **First theme and transition:** strings alone, Vln I (15–22) → Fl/Ob + Vln I (23–25) → tutti in octaves, with Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I/II and Va on the tune (27–32) → Bsn 1 solo (33–34) → Vln I (35–38) → Fl/Ob + Vln I (39–43) → Hn 1 / Ob 1 (44–46).
3. **Second theme, codetta and D1:** Hn 1 + Vc over harp (47–55) → Vln I+II, with a Cl/Hn/Tpt counterline (56–67) → codetta, Tpt 1 + Fl/Ob + Vln I (69–72) → Fl 1 solo (73–76) → D1 relay: Bsn, Cl, Ob, Fl and low brass (77–83).
4. **Fugato and B minor:** Vc (85–87) → Bsn 1 + Va (88–90) → CA + Vln II (91–93) → Fl 1 + Vln I (94–96) → Fl/Ob/CA + Vln I/II (97–102).
5. **Combination passage and wedge:** **Ob 1 + Vln I/II (103–106) → Cl 1 + Vln I (107–110) → Tpt 1 + Vln I (111–116)** → Fl/Ob + Vln I/II over the trombone motto (117–126).
6. **Retransition and recapitulation:** Cl 1 chalumeau (127–129) → low winds, trombones and Vc (131–134) → tutti (135–142) → Vln I (143–144) → Ob 1 (145–146) → Hn 1 + Va + Vc (147–155) → Vln I/II (156–161) → tutti (162–165) → Vln I/II (166–169).
7. **Coda:** brass head (171–174) → CA + Vc (175–178) → B.cl, trombones and low strings chorale (179–182) → Vln I (183–184).

**Counts:**
- Vln I carries the melody in 111 of 186 bars.
- Fl 1 + Vln I share it in 54 bars, and Ob 1 + Vln I in 51.
- A wind–string mixture carries it in 118 bars, woodwind alone in 18, brass alone in 3.
- Strings sound in all 186 bars. With no strings at all, Tchaikovsky 6/i has 20 bars (14, 24–26, 68, 127–129, 326–334, 352–354) and Dvořák 9/i has 7 (5–8, 264–268).

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 103–106: the combination passage uses the same colour as 97–102 (applied).**
- **Problem:** the top line is Ob 1 + Vln I + Vln II in unison, and the tenor is Hn 1 + Va + Bsn 1. That is the 97–102 sound with the flute, CA and clarinets taken out. The new key and the two combined heads arrive without a change of colour, and Vln I stays on the melody from 94 to 126 (33 bars, the longest run in the movement).
- **Evidence:** both models mark development sections with string-less bars (Dvořák 9/i 264–268; Tchaikovsky 6/i 127–129, which is exposition, and 326–334). v93 has none anywhere.
- **Fix:** winds and horns alone at 103–106. Notes are unchanged; cycle 71/72/73 notes and dynamics are kept.
  - Vln I, Vln II, Va, Vc and Cb: `R1*4`.
  - Cl 2 doubles Ob 1: `r8 a'8(\f f''4.->) e''8( es''8 d''8 | d''4) cis''8( d''8) g''4.-> f''8 | r8 d''8( bes''4.->) a''8( aes''8 g''8 | b''4) ais''8( b''8) g''4.->\> fis''8 | R1*4\! |`. Cl 1 rests, so the shared staff shows "2." with no conflicting dynamics.
  - Hn 1 takes `\mf` instead of `\f` on the tenor head. Ob + Cl at f equal two wind (Rimsky-Korsakov), and Hn 1 at mf plus Bsn 1 at f matches them, so neither head covers the other.
  - B.cl doubles Bsn 2 on the bass: `d,1\f | d,4 g,2 a,4 | bes,,2 c,2 | b,,2.\> r4\! | R1*4 |`. The B♭1 needs the low-C instrument already specified at 92. The Vc/Cb bass was at D2, so the octave does not change.
  - Hn 3·4 stay mp.
- **Result:** the movement's first string-less bars come at f. The strings then re-enter p at 107, so the f→p step at 107 (cycle 73) also becomes a change of colour.

**2. Bars 107–110: violas and low flute on the second-theme head; Vln I rests until 111 (applied).**
- **Problem:** Cl 1 + Vln I carry the head at G4–A♭5. The clarinet starts on concert G4, which is written A4, a throat note and weak when sustained. Vln I then goes straight on into 111. No section of the orchestra ever has a melody of its own here, and the flute never plays below B4 (v93 range B4–A♭6).
- **Evidence:**
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 19–22 gives the main theme to violas alone (B4–G5).
  - Dvořák 9/i 91–94 (Fl 1 + Ob, G4–C5) and 149–156 (Fl 1 solo, G4–D5 down to D4) use the flute's low register for a tune over soft strings.
  - Rimsky-Korsakov: viola melodies are "generally doubled… by the wood-wind", and in p a string section equals one wind instrument, so violas p with Fl 1 mp is balanced.
- **Fix:**
  - Va: `g'2.~(\p^\markup \italic "espr." g'8 fis'8 | e'4 c''2 b'4) | \clef treble des''4.( c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''2\< g''4 c''4) | \clef alto`.
  - Fl 1 (solo on the shared staff): `g'2.~(\mp^\markup \italic "espr." g'8 fis'8 | e'4 c''2 b'4) | des''4.( c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''2\< g''4 c''4\!) |`.
  - Cl 1 and Vln I rest from 103 to 110 (`R1*8`).
  - Vln II divides and takes the violas' pp sustains: `<< { e'1\pp^"div." | e'2( dis'2) | f'1( | f'2\< e'2) } \\ { b1 | g4( a2.) | s1 | c'1 } >>`. The two halves share the F4 at 109.
  - Vc/Bsn 1 head, Cb pizz. and Hn 3 are unchanged.
- **Result:** the colour sequence becomes:
  - 97–102: full wind and strings;
  - 103–106: winds and horns;
  - 107–110: violas and low flute;
  - 111: Vln I returns with Tpt 1 after 8 bars' rest.

  Vln I's melody bars fall from 111 to 103. Its longest run drops from 33 bars to 16 (111–126).
- **Earlier fixes:** this sets aside cycle 71's "Vln I loco" only because Vln I no longer plays in 103–110. The oboe line keeps the loco register, so the register reason for that fix still holds.

**3. Structural points (no further change).**
- Every section start before 97 brings a new solo colour: Bsn 33, harp with Hn + Vc 47, Tpt 69, D1 relay 77, Vc 85.
- The recapitulation's second theme at 147 repeats the exposition's horn + cello and adds violas. This is protected, and it is a normal way to recapitulate.
- The weak points were 103 and 107 (findings 1 and 2).

**4. Overused combinations (note, no change).**
- Fl 1 or Ob 1 doubling Vln I accounts for 105 bar-instances. Almost all of them are in protected tuttis (23–32, 117–126, 135–142, 162–165).
- Cycles 44 and 54 have already thinned this where it was allowed. I found no further unprotected place.

**5. Colours never used for a melody (for movements II/III).**
- **Harp:** never melodic. Use harmonics doubling a flute or solo violin.
- **Timpani:** never states a motive. The motto's G–E♭ fits the existing tuning (G, A♭, B♭/C, E♭). Every candidate bar here is protected (1–4, 84, 127–130, 179–186), so I propose nothing in this movement.
- **Trumpet:** has no soft melody of its own (60–63 is a counterline).
- **Horns:** never carry a theme without string doubling, apart from 13–14 and 44. Compare Dvořák 9/i 24–27.
- **Bass clarinet:** only doubles (cycle 54 note).
- **Chalumeau clarinet and viola melody:** the chalumeau is used at 127–129. Viola melody is now used at 107–110.

**6. Tchaikovsky and Dvořák compared.** In both models a theme's first wind statement is in a colour different from the one before it, and the strings take the theme on the second statement:
- Dvořák 9/i: Hn 24 → Cl + Bsn 28; Fl/Ob 91; Fl 149.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: Va 19 → Fl 23; Cl 153 → Bsn 160.

v93 does this at 33, 73 and 77 but nowhere between 94 and 126. Findings 1 and 2 restore it there.
