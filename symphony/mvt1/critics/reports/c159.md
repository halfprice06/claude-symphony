## Holistic comparison with Schubert 8/i

The movement follows Schubert's model closely, and in most places deliberately:

- **Opening and coda.** Schubert opens with a soft low-string idea that returns to drive the development and the coda. Here the motto in B.cl/Vc/Cb at 1–4 drives 77–84 and 109–122, and returns at 175–179.
- **Second theme.** Schubert gives it to the cellos and then to the violins. This score uses horn + cello at 47 and violins at 56, and changes the setting at each later return (97, 147, 158), which is Adler's point 20 (PDF 238).
- **Contrast.** Schubert breaks his tuttis with silences. This score has the stop chords at 26, 138 and 170 and the rest at 14.
- **Where this score goes further.** It has more counterpoint (fugato, theme combination 103–110) and a real orchestral climax on ♭VI. Its density of detail is closer to Tchaikovsky than to Schubert, and that suits the stated models.

Against all 35 principles in the reading, I found one clear violation. It is small, and it sits at the most exposed join in the exposition. My verdict: a conductor would take this for professional, late-Romantic work. The orchestration does not give it away.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 55: the violins' anacrusis into the second statement is buried (applied)

- **Problem.** At 55 beat 4, Vln I + II enter p "molto cantabile" on E♭4. At that moment Hn 1, the cellos and the violas all sustain C4 at p, a third below, and the harp's E♭5 is still ringing. The pickup of the theme's most important restatement starts inside a held C4 cluster.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 245: after the first statement, Strauss "suspends the pedal for a moment so that the anacrusis to the second statement in the first violins is clearly heard; thus, this repetition is separated from the initial exposition. This device also provides a natural breathing space."
  - Adler PDF 139 (Haydn 103) makes the same point about setting off the first and second statements.
  - Beethoven 7/ii bar 50 (corpus; Adler Ex. V-20, PDF 143) does the same thing: the lower strings end on a quarter note and rest before the restatement.
  - In the recapitulation (155 → 156) the pickup is F5, above everything, so it is already clear there. Only the exposition needs the fix.
- **Fix** (tTwo.ily). Hn 1, Vc and Vla release on beat 4. Pitches, scoring and dynamics are unchanged, and the harp and Vln II (already resting from beat 3) are untouched.
  - Hn 1: `... | R1*2 | c'2.\p r4 |`
  - Vc: `c'2.\p r4 | \clef bass aes,8\p( ...`
  - Vla: `c'2.^"unis." r4 | r4 <c' es'>4\pp^"div." ...`

  This touches the last note of the protected "horn + cello 47–55". The reason for that protection (the horn + cello scoring) is kept. Only the length of the cadence note changes.
- **Status.** Applied in /tmp/critic-159/work.

### 2. Bars 97–102: clarinets a2 doubling the violas' arpeggio (open item from c157) — keep, and close the item

- **Problem as raised in c157.** The clarinets duplicate the violas at unison.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 238 says this is Schubert's own device in this very movement: "first the clarinets combine with the violas … to assume the duties of accompanists." Ex. VIII-6 (PDF 238–239) shows clarinets and violas on the same syncopated accompaniment.
  - Adler PDF 249–250 objects to like winds a2 only in solo passages, "not tutti sections where simple volume is required". 97–102 is ff with the tune in three octaves.
- **Fix.** None. This answers c157's proposal (clarinets rest): the book's own Schubert example supports the doubling. I recommend recording the item as closed.

### 3. Bars 113–116: Tpt 3 in unison with Hn 3 (optional item from c157) — keep, and close the item

- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 257: unison overlapping "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". In a crescendo wedge a stronger pitch is the purpose.
  - Adler PDF 268 (Tchaikovsky 6/i 237–244, the model for this kind of wedge) asks that the brass be kept down early. Here the brass already enter p at 113 (c143).
- **Fix.** None.
