## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 69, Clarinets 1·2: the unison is printed as two parts (applied)
Problem: both clarinets play `r8 es'8( c''4.->) bes'8...` in unison, but the part combiner prints beats 1–2 as two voices: E♭4 and C5 with stems up and down, two slurs (above and below), two accents, and the "a2" label only at beat 3. The *f* touches the down-stem of the E♭. The horns one system lower, with the same line, print correctly because `closHnOne` starts with `\partCombineUnisono`.
Evidence: Gould PDF 65 keeps a shared notehead with two stems for two separate parts, not one line played by two players; PDF 55 says no characters should collide; Intro PDF 13 asks for a consistent approach (the horns already have the right form). Dvořák 9/i bar 64 (flutes) prints "a 2" on the first note and writes the line once.
Fix (applied, clos.ily):
`closClOne = { \partCombineUnisono r8 es'8(\f c''4.->) ... aes'4-> c''4-> aes'2-> \partCombineAutomatic | R1*4 | }`
Result: one stem per note, one slur, one accent, "a2" at the first note, as in the horns.

### 2. Bar 116, Flutes 1·2: two semibreves side by side after "a2" (applied)
Problem: at 115 the flutes are labelled "a2"; at 116 both hold `d'''1\<`, but the combiner prints two D6 semibreves next to each other. That is the notation for two separate parts and contradicts the a2 label one bar earlier.
Evidence: Gould PDF 65 (stemless unisons take a separate head only when written as two parts) and Intro PDF 13 (consistency). The LilyPond manual cited above: an a2 unison is printed once.
Fix (applied, dev.ily, devFluteOne): `\partCombineUnisono des'''4\f c'''4 es'''4 des'''4 | d'''1\< |` before the existing `\partCombineApart` at 117. 116 now shows one semibreve; 117 is unchanged.

### 3. Bar 124, Violins I: *loco* missing after the 8va (applied)
Problem: the 8va bracket (121–123, protected) ends after the G6 at 123, written as G5. Bar 124 starts on written G5 at actual pitch. The player sees the same written note and has only the end of the bracket to tell them it is an octave lower.
Evidence: Gould PDF 48: *loco* "is also used when the same written (or a neighbouring) pitch is immediately repeated after the octave transposition". The example on PDF 48 shows exactly this case. Dvořák 9/ii also marks "loco". This does not change the protected 8va layout.
Fix (applied, dev.ily, devVlnOne): `g''2.~\>(^\markup \italic "loco" g''8 f''8 |`.

### 4. Bar 146, Violas and Cellos: "dolce" touches the double barline (applied)
Problem: the "dolce" above the beat-4 pickup in viola and cello is left-aligned on the note, so it runs to the double barline before the key change at 147, touching it.
Evidence: Gould PDF 55 (no characters should collide, at least half a stave-space between them) and PDF 56–57 (leave a stave-space on either side of a barline).
Fix (applied, recOne.ily): `\once \override TextScript.self-alignment-X = #RIGHT` before the pickup in `recOneVla` (`g4(\mf^\markup \italic "dolce"`) and in `recOneVc` (after `\clef tenor`). The word now ends at the note, over the rest, clear of the barline. The protected clef placement at 146 is unchanged.
