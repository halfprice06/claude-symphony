## Findings

### 1. Bar 157 beat 3: the violas strike A♭4 a second under Vln II's accented B♭4, the note it resolves to (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 156–157 the violins come down from the climax approach: D6 (156), D♭6, C6 (157.1), then B♭5 on beat 3, A♭5 on beat 4 and G5 into 158. Vln II plays the same line an octave lower (c229). Over the F-minor chord of 157, the B♭ on beat 3 is an accented passing tone (a 4th over F) that resolves to A♭ on beat 4. The violas' arpeggio (`r8 aes8( c'8 f'8 aes'4) r4`) reaches A♭4 on beat 3, the same moment Vln II strikes B♭4. So the viola section sounds Vln II's note of resolution a major second below the dissonance, in the same register and the same timbre. Vln II then "resolves" to a pitch the violas have just played. Hn 3's held A♭3 also contains the resolution pitch, but it is an octave and a seventh lower and in another colour.

**Evidence from the reading.** On PDF 469–470 (§7, Ex. 27-10), A&S say that when a dissonance sounds together with its note of resolution in a voice other than the bass, "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution of the dissonance". Only the "unusually wide space" between the parts saves the Chopin example. That section points to Unit 21 §12 (PDF 352, Ex. 21-12). There, anticipating the note of resolution "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The exception is a texture with "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics", or a resolution tone an octave lower in an inner voice. At 157.3 none of these holds: violas against second violins, the same crescendo, a second apart.

The score follows this rule in the matching places. c302 cleared Tpt 2's G4 under the A♭4 at 28.3, and c392 cleared the cellos' G3 under Tbn 1's A♭3 at 83. Where the score allows a resolution tone to sound, at 117 and 122, it is a held inner pedal (PDF 471). The viola A♭4 at 157 is not a pedal. It is the last note of a rising arpeggio.

**Why it matters musically.** 156–158 carries the motto's chromatic descent D–D♭–C–B♭–A♭–G into the C-major climax at 158. The B♭→A♭ step is the one dissonant step of that descent. With A♭4 already sounding beside it, that step comes through as a smudged second in the middle strings instead of a falling sigh. A master's inner voices make way for the line at that moment. Brahms's accompanying arpeggios under an appoggiatura usually stop on the 5th or the root, not on the tone of resolution.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoSync, bar 157:
`r8 aes8( c'8 f'8 aes'4) r4` → `r8 f8( aes8 c'8 f'4) r4`

- The arpeggio is now F3–A♭3–C4–F4, the same root-position shape as the B♭3–D4–F4–B♭4 at 156, a step lower. That gives an exact sequence.
- On beat 3 the violas hold F4, a 4th under Vln II's B♭4. Vln II's A♭4 on beat 4 is now new in the strings.
- c227's reasons still hold. The violas stay under the tune's lower octave. Against the cello's F2–C3–F3–A♭3–C4 they move in 4ths and 3rds (F3/C3, A♭3/F3, C4/A♭3, F4/C4), with no octaves and no fifths. The chord pitches are unchanged (c182).
- The F3 start is well inside the viola's range.
