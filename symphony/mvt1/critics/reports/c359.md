## Findings

### 1. The cor anglais staff name does not match the front page and the other transposing instruments (applied)

c355 changed the front page's instrument list to "Cor anglais in F". The staff names in score.ly still read "Cor anglais" / "C.a.", while every other transposing instrument in this C score has its key on the staff name: "Clarinets 1·2 in B♭", "Bass clarinet in B♭", "Horns 1·2 in F", "Trumpets 1·2 in C". The part is already headed "Cor anglais in F" (parts-gen.py line 24). So score, front page and part now disagree. This is the only regression I found between v349 and v359, and it is small. The Saint-Saëns score names every transposing instrument with its key in the staff label ("1 Cor Anglais", "2 Clarinettes en Si♭", "1er et 2e Cors en UT", PDF 3), so the convention is the one the score already follows elsewhere.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-359/work/score.ly line 113:

```
\new Staff = "Cor anglais" \with { instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Cor anglais" "in F" } shortInstrumentName = \markup \center-column { "C.a." "(F)" } midiInstrument = "english horn" }
```

Notation only. The page breaks stay the same (the last bars of pages 2–18 are unchanged: 14 30 38 46 63 72 84 102 110 120 130 138 146 157 162 170 182). The parts are not affected.

### 3. Overall comparison with Saint-Saëns 3/i (no change)

What a conductor who knows Op. 78 would recognise here:

- **A slow introduction with a sighing string figure and one woodwind solo, then a nervous Allegro.** Saint-Saëns opens Adagio with pp violins and violas in swells and an oboe solo (PDF 3). This score opens with the motto low and bare, then the cor anglais solo (1–8). The two openings have the same economy: strings alone for several bars, one solo wind.
- **A cyclic motto carried through.** Saint-Saëns's Allegro theme returns in every movement. This movement's motto returns at 5, 73, 117 and 175–182, and the other movements quote it.
- **A first movement that does not end but leads into a slow movement in a flat key.** Saint-Saëns's Allegro dies away pp in pizzicato strings, soft timpani and isolated wind notes, and runs into the D♭ Poco adagio (PDF 56–57). This score ends ppp on C major, timpani pulse, tam-tam, and runs attacca into A♭. The soft attacca ending is the same idea, and it should stay.
- **Returning material scored differently** (reading row 1). Saint-Saëns hands his semiquaver theme from the strings to the full woodwind at letter L while the strings strike short chords (PDF 40), which is Rimsky-Korsakov's alternation and transfer (PDF 126–127). This score varies each return in a comparable way: P at 15 (strings), 27 (hammered tutti), 69 (horns with three winds above), 82 (three octaves over held brass), 131 (low, in the tenor).

There is one real difference. Saint-Saëns's Allegro is driven by an unbroken semiquaver motor in the strings, and his transitions keep it going (PDF 5, 8, 14). In this score the texture breaks after each tutti: 33–36 is a bassoon solo with violas and soft low strings, and 123–126 is a collapse. That is the Brahms and Tchaikovsky way of building a movement, the models named in the brief. It is protected for good reasons (c239, c243, c283: 33–46 route and stages; collapse 123–126), and its thin moments follow the reading (row 13: the violas answer the bassoon a fourth higher, PDF 128). It should stay.

The scale is also different. Op. 78's first part runs about 18 minutes with its Poco adagio; this movement alone lasts about 7 minutes 11 seconds. The proportions were measured at c243 and are protected. Brahms 3/i and Tchaikovsky 4/i work on this scale, so it should stay.

### 4. Two brass re-entries at mf, checked against PDF 136 (no change)

Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 136) says brass returning after a long rest should come in pp or ff, and that mf/mp re-entries sound colourless. I scanned every brass part for entries after six or more bars of rest. All are pp, p or ff except two. One is the trombones mf at 12 (Ger6), kept by c228 and c308 as part of the graded brass of the introduction. The other is Hn 1·2 mf at 79, inside the protected motto exchanges 77–83 (c369), where the horns answer Ob 1 and Vln I at the same mf as a dialogue, not as an arrival. Both have stated reasons that I accept. I do not reopen them.
