## Findings

The string writing of v236 is close to faultless against this chapter. Ranges, clefs, double stops, divisi marks, sul-string marks and the cello and viola couplings all match what Adler teaches, and earlier playability cycles (c226 Adler ch. 2, c186, c196) have already removed the problems this chapter would have caught. One small marking is missing; the other items below were checked and should stay as they are, with the reason given so later critics need not reopen them.

### F1. Double basses 3–4: a 9.6-second bow with a swell and no "change bow at will" (applied)

Bars 3–4 (intro.ily, `introCb`): `c,2(\< des,4\> c,4)~ | c,1\!`. The slur and tie make one bow of eight beats at crotchet = 50, 9.6 s, with a swell in the middle of it, in the exposed opening motto (Cb an octave under B.cl and Vc). Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." The score already does this for every other long bass note: 13–14 (8.4 s, marked), 72–76 (8.6 s), 117–130 and 179–182. Bars 3–4 are the longest unmarked bow and the only one with a swell inside it, so they are the one place where a section would either bow it short or change together and leave a hole in the motto's C.

c226 kept "long pp slurs 1–3 (one bow at pp)" from Adler ch. 2, which treats the strings together. This chapter's bass-specific rule (PDF 108) answers that reason for the basses only; the cellos' slur stays one bow, since their bow is longer and 9.6 s at pp is within it.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-236/work/sections/intro.ily):

```
r2. g,,4(\pp | es,2.~\< es,8\> d,8) | c,2(\<^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" des,4\> c,4)~ | c,1\! |
```

No pitch, slur or dynamic changes; the slur stays as the phrasing. The markup sits above the Cb staff at bar 3 in the score and appears in the double bass part.

### F2. Double basses 46→47: arco to pizz. with no rest (stays)

Bar 46 ends `des,2 es,2\upbow` and 47 starts `aes,4\pp^"pizz."`. c195 added a rest at 102→103 for the same change. Here it should stay: the up-bow (already marked) ends at the frog, where a bass player plucks with the bow held, at pp in a poco rit. (crotchet about 114). The c195 case was an ff down-bow ending at the point. Adding a rest would cut the dominant root E♭ under the protected lead-in 43–47. Adler PDF 105 (slow speech of the bass) matters for fast arco passages, not for a single soft pizz.

### F3. Violins II 122–123: fff tremolo double stops without "div." (stays)

`<d'' aes''>1:32\fff | <d'' g''>1:32` follow "unis." at 117; "div." comes only at 124. Adler PDF 76 allows easy double stops for "especially sonorous passages" and sends only difficult ones to divisi. D5–A♭5 is a 3–2 shape on the A and E strings and D5–G5 a 3–2 fourth, both in first position, measured tremolo. Divisi would halve the A♭5 that doubles Vln I's A♭6 an octave below at the climax (c229's octave rule), so the double stop is the stronger choice.

### F4. Violas 158–161: repeated double stops in eighths, ff–fff (stays)

`recTwoSync` repeats C4–G4, B3–F4, C4–E♭4, D4–F4 in eighths. Adler's Lohengrin example (PDF 90, Ex. III-37) writes such repeated chords "div." in all three upper sections. Here the shapes are first-position fifths, a tritone and thirds, each held for a whole bar under one hand position, so there is no re-fingering per eighth; Adler PDF 76 and 88 accept easy double stops for sonorous passages, and every viola playing both notes is louder than half the section on each. The scoring of 158–166 is protected (c169, c229) and nothing in this chapter answers that protection.

### F5. Violas 151–153: up to three ledger lines in alto clef (stays)

The S restatement reaches E5 and F5 (two and three ledger lines) eight times in 151–153. Adler PDF 85 (Ex. III-28) says the viola's upper notes are "sometimes" written in treble clef to avoid ledger lines, which makes the clef optional, and Adler PDF 104 sets the bass threshold for a clef change at three ledger lines; 151–153 touch three only on F5 and fall back to A4–C5 in every bar. Two clef changes inside one legato phrase would cost the player more than the ledger lines. Keep alto here and at 110 (c115).
