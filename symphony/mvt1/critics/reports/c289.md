## Borodin 2/i in brief, and what the comparison shows

Borodin's first movement (B minor, Allegro, 2/2, about the same length as this one) is built on a terse unison motto that returns throughout in sequence, diminution and augmentation, often in heavy low brass, with abrupt tempo changes. Maes calls it "a montage of contrasts rather than ... a developing sonata form". The lyrical second subject is first in the cellos in D major and returns in E♭, not in the tonic. The motto closes the movement unaltered. Sources: Wikipedia, "Symphony No. 2 (Borodin)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._2_(Borodin)), which cites Maes and Brown; fugueforthought.de (https://fugueforthought.de/2015/10/29/borodin-symphony-no-2-in-b-minor/); score at IMSLP (https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.2_(Borodin,_Aleksandr)).

Set against that model, v289 holds up in the points that make Borodin's movement memorable:
- One motto runs through the whole movement and changes colour at each return: low B.cl/Vc at 1–4, C.a. at 5–8, flute in A♭ minor at 73–76, development exchanges at 77–83, horns and trombones ff over the G pedal at 117–121, stretto at 127–130, trombone chorale at 175–179, Vln I at 177–182. This matches Adler's Mahler 4/iv point (PDF 169–171).
- The second theme starts in a low singing voice: horn and cello over the harp at 47, like Borodin's cello theme.
- Borodin augments the motto in the low brass. This score does the same at 117–121. The recapitulation follows Tchaikovsky 4/i closely: collapse, cellos and basses alone, then a ff tutti with the theme low (Adler PDF 38–41).
- Both movements cut sharply between tempos and characters. Here: Lento, Allegro, Poco meno mosso, allargando–sostenuto, Più lento.

There are two real differences, and neither is a weakness. Borodin states his motto in a full ff unison. Here the motto is never given to the whole orchestra in octaves, and the movement ends ppp attacca rather than with the motto at full force. Both choices follow from the cycle: the finale holds the apotheosis, and the movement runs straight on into the Adagio.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 100: Fl 1 drops out of its octave halfway through the climb to the phrase's peak (applied)

**Bars:** 97–102, the ff B-minor S statement. Fl 1 plays the octave above Vln I and Ob 1 from 97.4 to 100 beat 2 (D6, B5–G6–F♯6, E6–D6–C♯6). On beat 3 of 100 it drops a major sixth, C♯6 to E5, and plays in unison with the violins to 102. The flute part is the only voice above the violins (Fl 2 rests), so the top of the texture falls from C♯6 to E5 at the exact moment the tune starts its climb C♯5–E5–G5 | B5–B♭5 | F♯5–D6. The listener hears a high line that peaks at G6 at 99 and then vanishes in the middle of a gesture. The tune's real peak (D6 at 102) arrives after the top has already dropped.

**Evidence (reading first):**
- Adler PDF 147–148, d'Indy, Ex. XV-1: the composer "wisely omits the upper woodwinds because their ranges do not extend down far enough to play the entire passage". An instrument is either in for the whole gesture or out of it.
- PDF 150, Barber, Ex. XV-2: the flutes are cut "before the glissando so they won't be forced to drop out before the last notes".
- PDF 151, Mozart 40/iv, Ex. XV-3: had the flute kept the violins' octave it "would have had to drop the flute out for the final note". Mozart therefore places every instrument where it can play every note of the gesture. Adler concludes that "the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument that he uses".
- The upper octave cannot continue: it would reach B6–D7, and cycle 9 lowered 100–101 so there is no B6 peak outside the climaxes. The fault is only where the switch falls.

**Fix:** move the switch to the downbeat of 100, the start of the second two-bar unit (98–99 | 100–101 | 102). On the downbeat the tune steps down (F♯5–E5), so the flute's move into the lower octave follows the tune's direction, and the climb from 100.3 to 102 is then heard in one octave from start to finish. ### 2. A Borodin-style ff unison tutti of the motto: considered, recommend no change

The test of a "unison-octave tutti" (Adler PDF 150: used "to introduce a new idea, summarize an old one, or state a major melody") applied to Borodin's signature gesture points at one spot: the motto could be stated in full octaves, for example at 167–170 or 117–121. I recommend keeping things as they are, for two reasons:
- movement I deliberately holds back its loudest combined statements for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md "Do not reuse"; Adler PDF 37, holding a colour back for the decisive event);
- the 117–121 horn/trombone unison against figuration is already the "dark" brass-unison model Adler praises (PDF 19–20).

An extra full unison would spend the finale's effect and add nothing this movement lacks. Not applied.

### 3. High horn unison 158–161: not reopened

Adler PDF 26 (Schuller) bears on Hn 3·4 a2 at C5 (written G5). Cycle 181 examined this against the same page and the corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i bar 73 has all four horns on written G5, and Dvořák 9/i has Hn I·II on G5. I agree with its verdict and add nothing.
