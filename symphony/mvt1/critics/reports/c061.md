- **33–41:** the transition imitation climbs from bassoon (G3) to viola (C4) to Violin I (F4, B♭4, C5), then to D5–G6.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 23–26 and 127–130: the flute octave reverses the hemiola's contour and pre-empts A♭6 (APPLIED)
**Problem.** In the hemiola the melody is in Violin I, Oboe 1 and Flute 2 at E♭5–A♭5. It rises to the accented B5 at 26, the vii°7 that rhymes with 130 and 166. Flute 1 doubles it an octave higher (es''' … aes'''), and then at 26 it drops back to the violin octave (b''2.), because cycle 1 removed B6. As a result the top line runs F6 → G6 → **A♭6 (25)** → B5 (26) → E♭6 (27).

The listener's top line therefore falls a minor seventh into the ff goal bar. The tutti at 27 also arrives *below* what came before, so it opens no new register. The movement's ceiling is first heard at 25, as a single flute at mf in a crescendo, six bars before the theme's ff peak at 31.

The recapitulation repeats this exactly. A♭6 sounds at 129 (mf<), three bars before the ff peak at 132–133.

**Evidence.**
- **Tchaikovsky 6/i 66–70.** The flutes' statements climb D♯6 (66) → F♯6 (68) → A6 (70). The violins stay at G♯4–B4 (67–69) and leap to A6/C7 only at the arrival in 70, which is also the movement's highest Violin I note. The registral staircase ends on the arrival.
- **Dvořák 9/i 47–57.** The top line is the melody itself, rising C5 → E5 → C6/E6 → F♯6 → G6 → B6. Doubling never inverts its contour.
- **Agawu and the GfM study.** Listeners hear the highest pitch as the climax. Placing the movement's ceiling pitch on a flute-only mf doubling at 25 and 129 gives it to the wrong moment.

After the change the top line is E♭5–A♭5 (23–25) → B5 (26) → E♭6 (27) → A♭6 first at 31. In the recapitulation it is A♭5 (129) → B5 (130) → F6 (131) → A♭6 at 132. The hemiola's notes, rhythm, harmony and dynamics are unchanged. Only Flute 1's octave moves, so I do not think this touches the reason the hemiola is protected (cycles 7, 17, 33). In 127–130 it does not affect the stated reasons for protecting 123–138 (the two-wave design and the D♭ question). Cycle 1 already treated the flute octave at 26 as adjustable.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-061/work):**
- tOne.ily, `tOneFluteOne` bars 23–25: `es''4(\mf\< d''4 c''4) f''4( | es''4 d''4) g''4( f''4 | es''4) aes''4( g''4 f''4) |`. This is unison with Flute 2, and the part-combiner prints "a2".
- recOne.ily, `recOneFluteOne` bars 127–130: `\partCombineUnisono es''4(\mf\< d''4 c''4) f''4( | es''4 d''4) g''4( f''4 | es''4) aes''4( g''4 f''4) | \partCombineAutomatic b''2.-> r4 |`. The forced-apart mode now ends at 126, which keeps cycle 25's explicit mode for 123–126, and "a2" prints at 127.

Flute 2's dynamics already match on the shared staff, there is no ottava, and slurs are unchanged. Range: F5 and A♭5 lie in the flute's middle register, below the oboe's A♭5, so nothing is exposed.

### 2. A♭6 count after the fix: six bars (31, 72, 114, 132, 133, 156). Leave it.
Bars at G6 or above fall from 18 to 14 (7.9% of bars). The corpus uses the top three semitones of Violin I for about 4% of bars (Tchaikovsky 6/i: C7 at 70–73, B6 at 213–214, B♭6 at 195, A6 in eight bars; Dvořák 9/i: C7 at 346, 412, 444, B6 at 22, 57 and 428–448). The remaining density is within reason, because each remaining A♭6 is a goal note in the violins.

The codetta's A♭6 at 72 is the one non-climactic case. The corpus does not settle it:
- Dvořák keeps C7 out of his exposition (exposition ceiling B6 at 22 and 57).
- Tchaikovsky puts his absolute top, C7, in the exposition (70–73), and his development climax sits lower (A6–B6).

Since 69–72 is protected and the weight argument from cycle 24 holds, I recommend no change.

### 3. Peak lengths: 156 holds A♭6 for the shortest time. Observation only.
A♭6 lasts 1.5 beats at 31, 4 beats at 114 (tremolo), 3.5 beats at 132–133 and 2.5 beats at 156. The main climax relies on harmony (V–♭VI), the cymbal and full weight rather than duration. The notes of 154–157 are protected, and lengthening would change the second theme's rhythm, so I propose nothing. Do not shorten 156 any further, and do not lengthen 132–133.

### 4. Bar 100: the flute folds down by a ninth in the B-minor statement. Leave it.
Flute 1 drops from C♯6 to E5 on beat 4 of 100, where cycle 9 removed B6. Violin I and the oboe carry the rising arpeggio at the same pitch, so the line continues audibly in the same instruments, and nothing is lost. Protected (97–102). No change.

### 5. Seams where the top line drops more than an octave: all intended.
- 32–33: C6 to the bassoon solo on G3
- 43: E♭6 to the horn on E♭4, the medial-caesura fill
- 68–69: A♭4 p to C6 f
- 83–84: bare G2
- 118–119: D5 to clarinet G3
- 166–167: fermata

Each falls at a section seam or follows a rest or fermata, so the drop is heard as a fresh start, not a lost thread. Within phrases, the only contour break I found was the one in finding 1.
