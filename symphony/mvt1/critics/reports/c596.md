Lens: playability, instrument by instrument. Assigned reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 2, Wind instruments (sebesky PDF 21-110), fourth pass. Earlier passes over this chapter were c196, c306, c396 and c506. - I rendered all 90 pages at 110 dpi and looked at them in groups of six. I enlarged the range charts and read them from the images, not the OCR: trumpet Ex. 4-5 (PDF 21), trombone and bass trombone Ex. 23-31 (PDF 27-29), horn Ex. 35-36 (PDF 32-33), tuba Ex. 44 (PDF 39), clarinet Ex. 64-66 (PDF 60), bass clarinet Ex. 72 (PDF 64), flute Ex. 74-76 (PDF 65), oboe Ex. 87-88 (PDF 70-71), cor anglais and bassoon Ex. 90-94 (PDF 72-74), and the woodwind intensity pairs Ex. 104-107 (PDF 79-80). - I diffed v506 against v596 section by section. Changes since c506 that touch winds or brass: Hn 1's horn-call recall at 125-126 (c581), Hn 3·4 mf restrike at 163 (c508), the Tbn 1 part-only breath at 119.4 and its score mark (c556, c565), the C.a. "(breathe ad lib.)" now also in the score (c565), and a rest position in Ob 1 at 160 (c535). Everything else in the winds and brass is as c506 checked it.
- I wrote scripts (in /tmp/critic-596/scripts) over v596's full.midi that list each wind and brass part's range and where its extremes fall; continuous spans over 12 s, timed with the MIDI tempo map; brass notes shorter than a quaver; horn notes at or above concert A4; oboe notes at or below F4; soft clarinet notes in the throat register; seconds between unlike woodwinds below forte; a flute sounding under an oboe below forte; and every place where a wind doubling of a melodic line (8 or more notes) stops while the line goes on. ## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. I made no edits in the scratch copy. Below is what I checked, and the three improvements I considered and rejected.

### 1. Wind and brass spans over 12 s (Sebesky PDF 24, 30, 70). Keep.

The MIDI lists 33 spans over 12 s. MIDI does not shorten a note for a breath mark, so the list over-reports. In the source, every span is broken:
- by a slur end: B.cl 1-4 after bar 2; the chorale 175-179 at 176/177; Hn 1 S 46-52 and 146-152 every two bars; Ob 1 121-126 at 125 (slur split, c198);
- by a \breathe: Bsn 1 10, 22, 36, 120, 159, 163; Bsn 2 10, 159, 163; Hn 1·2 119; Hn 3·4 22, 39, 64, 134; Hn 4 58; Tbn 1 119.4 (part, c556) and 179;
- by a rest or a part licence: C.a. 5-9 (now also printed in the score, c565).

The tightest one left is the B.cl at 114.1-120.3: about 13 s from mf crescendo to ff, with a fresh rest at 113.4. A note change and a new ff attack fall at 116→117 (C3 to G2), where the player can clip the C and breathe. Bsn 2, Cbsn, tuba and Cb hold the same bass there, so the gap cannot be heard. Sebesky PDF 70 asks for breathing space in large bores, and the space is already there. No mark is needed.

Hn 3's C4 at 181-182 (ppp, fermata) ties into movement II. That is one of the five protected tied parts.

### 2. Seconds between unlike woodwinds and flute-oboe order (Sebesky PDF 79, 81). Keep.

Below forte there are only two seconds between unlike woodwinds in the movement:
- 145.3: Ob 1 G4 against Cl 1 F4, for one beat. Ob 1 is passing on its way up to E5, and Vln I holds the same G4.
- 153.2.5: Cl 1 D5 against C.a. C5, for a quaver. Both are passing notes in the doubled viola/cello line.

Neither chord is sustained, and Ex. 108-109 concern held voicings. No flute sounds under an oboe below forte; the one case at 132.1 is inside the ff tutti (c506).

### 3. Changes since c506 checked against the reading. Keep.

- **Hn 1 recall at 125-126 (c581).** The call is written B♭3-G4-F♯4, mp. It comes after a rest from 124.1 to 125.2, about 3 s at sostenuto crotchet = 100, following fff at 122-123. Sebesky PDF 44 asks that the lead player be rested. Hn 2 carries the same load through 123, and Hn 3·4 play on through 124-125, so no horn is fresher. From the C4 on, the line is doubled at pitch by Hn 3, Cl 2 and violas, so only two notes are exposed. c586 reached the same verdict against Rimsky-Korsakov; I find nothing in Sebesky against it.
- **Hn 3·4 mf restrike at 163 (c508).** It is a middle-register restrike inside a diminuendo, and it gives a breath (PDF 24). Follows.
- **Tbn 1 breath at 119.4 (c556, c565).** It is staggered with Tbn 2 holding the same E♭4, which matches Sebesky's rule that an equal instrument continues the line (PDF 43). Follows.

### 4. Octave-doubling breaks (Sebesky PDF 42-43, Ex. 49). Keep.

The scan found 80 places where a wind stops doubling a melodic line of 8 or more notes while the line continues. Nearly all fall at phrase ends or climax releases (32.4, 122, 125, 138, 143, 162, 170). The two that fall inside a phrase show the handling Sebesky asks for:
- at 52.4 the cello leaps to F5 and Hn 1 cannot follow; Cl 1 takes the line on the same beat;
- at 152.4 the same thing happens in the recapitulation; Cl 1 again takes over from Hn 1 on F5.

### 5. Ranges and registers. Keep.

Every part is within Sebesky's normal or best range. There are three exceptions, all known and justified:
- Hn 1 written B5 at 106 is extended range, ff and doubled, with a part ossia (c185);
- Ob 1 D6 at 102 is at the top of the hard register, ff, doubled and reached by step;
- Bsn 2 and B.cl B♭1 at 92-106 are at the bottom, with the B.cl marked for low-C instrument (c146).

No brass note is shorter than a quaver, so Sebesky's late-speaking-horn point (PDF 46) has nothing to act on. The parts as last built have every wind and brass part on 1 or 2 pages, so none has a page turn. The five parts that cross into movement II end on page 2.

### Where the music could go further, and why I did not change it

- **A. C.a. in unison with Cl 1 at 175-177** (Sebesky PDF 63: clarinet and English horn in unison, "especially beautiful 'woody' sound"; PDF 106: C.a. "best used in unison with a clarinet as a lead line over soft brass"). The trombone chorale under it is exactly Sebesky's "soft brass". But the C.a. has just finished its single-colour duet with the cello at 174. The point of 175 is the change of colour to Cl 1 (horn call, protected c581), before Vln I takes the motto at 177.4. Doubling would blur that change and thicken a pp line that hands off at the unison. Rejected.
- **B. Stopped or muted horn for the 125-126 recall** (PDF 37: stopped horn "vague and distant" when blown normally). It would mark the recall as an echo of 13-14. But the call starts on written B♭3, below written middle C, where Sebesky says stopped notes are unreliable and the straight mute is preferable (PDF 37). A mute needs time the 3 s rest after fff does not give, and the movement has no mutes by decision (c76). Rejected.
- **C. Low flute colour** (PDF 65: the low register "dark and rich"; c576 open item: flutes never go below B♭4). The places where a low flute would be heard are soft and protected: 73-76 flute motto (c456), 171-174 duet, 175-179 chorale. In tuttis, a low flute is covered (PDF 79). Rejected.
