## Findings

The movement's counterpoint is already at the standard this chapter sets: imitation in the transition, a four-entry fugato with an honest countersubject and free voice, countermelodies in their own rhythm, pedals that are given rhythm, and accompaniments that never copy the tune's rhythm. What is left is a small family of entries whose first note is buried. A held note in another part already sounds that pitch when the entry begins. Earlier critics fixed this at 15, 17, 19, 37, 91, 143, 144 and 145–146. The scan finds three more places where the masking note is unthematic and can move without touching any protected line.

### 1. Bar 41: the four-horn call's pickup E♭3 is struck inside Bsn 2's held E♭3. Applied.

All four horns enter ff on 41.4 with the call E♭3–C4–B♭3. Bsn 2 has held E♭3 sf> from the downbeat of 41, the same pitch. The call therefore starts inside a sounding unison. Cycle 164 lifted Cl 2's B♭3 off the call's second note for the same reason ("the call lands on its B♭3 alone"), but the first note was missed.

Adler explains why this matters. Dvořák's delayed oboe entrance "clarifies the tune much better" (PDF 213). Ravel hands the tune over "without overlap and a fresh articulation" (PDF 217). Like sounds at the same pitch "would swallow each other up" (PDF 229).

Fix (trans.ily, transBsnTwo): Bsn 2 keeps the B♭2 it already holds at 40 through 43.
```
bes,1\f | bes,1\sf\> | bes,1\mf\> | bes,4\p r4 r2 |     % was: bes,1\f | es1\sf\> | es1\mf\> | es4\p r4 r2 |
```
The line now has no leap. The root E♭ stays in Cb and Cbsn (E♭2), the violas (E♭4) and Cl 1 (E♭5), and the chord at 43 is still the full V7 of A♭. Cycle 306's note "the E♭ stays in Bsn 2, Cbsn, Cb" at 43 now reads "Cbsn, Cb". The vl.txt diff shows only the bass doubling Bsn 2/Cb at 38→39 (notes unchanged) and loses the old Bsn 2/B.tbn octave at 40→41. The protected horn call 41–42 is unchanged.

### 2. Bar 35: the clarinet pad starts on the downbeat, on the pitch of Vln I's pickup F4. Applied.

In the head imitation of the transition, Vln I enters at 35.1.5 on F4. Cl 1·2 strike their p pad B♭4/F4 on the downbeat, so Cl 2 already sounds that F4. Two bars later, cycle 294 moved Vln II's tremolo to beat 2 at 37 for exactly this reason, Vln I's pickup B♭4. Brahms waits for the first offbeat before sounding the whole harmony "so as not to thicken the downbeat" (PDF 205). PDF 213 gives the clarity reason.

Fix (trans.ily, transClOne and transClTwo): `r4 bes'2.\p( | g'1)` and `r4 f'2.\p( | es'1)`; the old notes were `bes'1\p(` and `f'1\p(`. Only the rhythm changes. The violas' tenuto F4 on 35.1 stays: it is the end of their own answer, the same dovetail as Bsn 1 → violas on C4 at 34 that cycle 151 kept.

### 3. Bar 69: the codetta's P head begins in Hn 1·2 on E♭3 while Hn 4 already holds E♭3. Applied.

At the codetta, Hn 1·2 a2 take the lower octave of the P head with the pickup E♭3 on 69.1.5. Hn 4 strikes E♭3 f on the downbeat. That is a horn unison under a horn entry, the most complete masking possible: same colour, same pitch (PDF 229). The full harmony also sounds before the head's offbeat start (PDF 205).

Fix (clos.ily, closHnFour): `r4 es4\f es2 |`; the old bar was `es2\f es2 |`. Hn 4 now enters on beat 2, after the pickup. The downbeat keeps A♭ and C (bass, Bsn 1, Hn 3, Tpt 1), and the E♭ arrives with the head at 1.5 in six parts. Harmony, doublings and dynamics of 69–72 are unchanged. The protections of 69–72 (c249, c279, c291, c327, c331 "harmony and dynamics", c347) cover the tune, the doublings and the grading, not the onset of the Hn 4 pad, and cycle 331 reopened 69–72 on a similar point. On the shared Hn 3·4 staff the combiner prints "3." with f on beat 1 and Hn 4's f on beat 2. Each player sees its own entry dynamic, and both are f.

### 4. Bars 27, 131 and 167 show the same pattern. They should stay as written.

The scan finds the same layout at the three hammered tuttis: a chord held from the downbeat, with the head's pickup on 1.5 at a pitch the chord already holds. At 27 the masked line is only the violas' lower doubling (G3 under Hn 4/Tbn 2), and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part. At 131 the low theme has six instruments at ff against one held horn at f. At 167, Hn 4's G3 is tied over from 166 (c234), so it is a sustained voice, not a fresh attack on the head's note. HISTORY protects all three layouts as alike on purpose. Bar 69 is different: there a fresh horn attack sits under a horn entry, and only one other instrument holds the note.

### 5. Bars 139–142: Vln I and Vln II in unison on the ff tune. It should stay as written.

Schoenberg prefers octaves to at-pitch doubling of like colours (PDF 229). Here Vln II doubles Vln I at pitch, with Ob 1 and Cl 1, and Fl 1 is alone an octave above (A♭6 at 140, protected). An octave below would put Vln II at G3–A♭4, right on the violas' neighbour quavers (C4–A♭4) and the Vc/Bsn 1/Tbn 2 tenor counterline (F3–C4). That would hide the two inner lines the passage depends on. Adler's priority of the layers (PDF 203) favours the present unison.
