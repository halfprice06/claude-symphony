### 2. The solo cello at 171–174 strengthens the finale's minor-then-major second theme (keep; record it)

The theme record lists 171–174 (mvtOneThemeSCodaMinor) as the model for the finale's second theme, "minor first, major on the repeat". In the finale the minor statements are a single wind voice: an undoubled trumpet solo at its 51 and, in its recapitulation at its 171, muted trumpet with the cor anglais (finale.md, F2). Since v270, movement I's last statement of S is also a single soloist, the cello, in minor, against the cor anglais's motto. So both movements put the minor form of their second theme in a single voice beside the cor anglais. The finale then turns that single-voice minor statement into a full major one (violins at 67, A♭ at 187), so movement I's soloist sets up the change the finale makes. This follows PDF 20 (colour belongs to the idea: the cello was S's first colour at 47, with the horn) and PDF 19–20 (the melody is set apart from a pp harmonic background). Movement I keeps its S in minor at the end, so it does not use up the finale's turn to major.

### 4. Add bar 37 to the seeds list (records)

### 5. The record's "high horn unison" should read "high four-horn unison" (records)

coherence/mvt1.md ("Not used anywhere in movement I") and its "Do not reuse" list say movement I has no "high horn unison". At the climax it does have pairs in unison: Hn 3·4 on the counterline up to C5, fff, at 158–161 (since c174; at 160 C5 for two beats), and Hn 1·2 on A♭4 at 160. cyclic-notes.md (line 216) defines the finale's reserve as "a high four-horn unison". Movement I's only four-horn unison is the low call E♭3–C4–B♭3 at 41–42. The score keeps the reserve. The record's shorter wording does not match the score, and a reader of mvt1.md could take it to mean that horn pairs are never doubled at the unison high in movement I. The pairs at 158–161 follow Rimsky-Korsakov's balance rule (two horns to one trumpet, cited by c174), so they stay.

### 6. The solo cello's B4 at 174 goes on to Cl 1's C5 at 176 at the same pitch (keep)

The question here is whether the solo's unresolved leading tone at 174 breaks the transfer rule (PDF 125–126) or uses up the finale's B→C. It does neither. The cor anglais and the solo cello meet on C5 at 174 beat 1, which is the rule's common note. The cello's B4 then passes, at the same pitch, to Cl 1's C5 at 176, the second note of its recall of the horn call. Cl 1's own B4 at 177 is left open, as Hn 1's B3 is at 14 and Tpt 1·2's B4 at 170. Movement I hands its leading tone from player to player and never resolves it loudly in the trumpets, so the trumpets' B→C stays the finale's (Do not reuse, item 1). The coda's notes are protected (c228, c249, c251, c262, c269), and this reading supports keeping them.
