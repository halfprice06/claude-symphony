## Findings

### 1. Bars 129–130: the dominant reaches the reprise without a 7th or a leading tone (applied)

**Problem.** The G pedal (117–130) carries B♮ and F through 117–123. After 123 no part plays F. The leading tone is last heard in the violas (B held to beat 3 of 127) and as a passing eighth in Cl 1 at 128. In 129–130 the motto stretto (Cl 1 `g4 es'2 d'4 | des'4 c'2.`, Vla `es'4 d'4 des'4 c'4`, Vln II and Bsn 1 `g es d`) sinks onto C over the pedal. The final sonority before 131 is G–C–D: a bare suspended fourth whose C anticipates the tonic. The bass move G→C at 131 carries the whole arrival.

**Evidence.** PDF 449: "The minor V, of course, does not lead forcefully to I; before the recapitulation, therefore, the leading tone must appear." PDF 456: "When the V comes back, it is often preceded by a II or IV and often supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly to I." PDF 445: approach the new tonic "with a melodic line that gravitates to its 1̂ (or possibly 3̂) and reaches it with a sense of rhythmic resolution." Here the motto reaches 1̂ a bar early, on beat 2 of 130. In Beethoven 9/i, the movement the book cites for this plan, bars 296–300 hold vii°7 of D (leading tone and seventh) until the reprise at 301 (harmony.csv, onsets 591–600).

**Fix.** Add one note in a resting player. The protected stretto (126–130, cycle 117) and the motto pitches stay as they are. Horn 2 sounds the seventh F4 on beats 3–4 of 130 and resolves it to its existing E♭4 at 131 (`es'1\f`), the chord third of the reprise. dev.ily, `devHnTwo`, last line:

```
  c'1 | f'1\fff | f'1 | R1*6 |
  r2 f'2\mf\<^\markup \bold "2." |
```

Beat 4 of 130 now sounds G–C–D–F (V7 with suspended fourth). F4 lies a fourth above the clarinet's and viola's C4 and a sixth below Vln II's D5, so it rubs against nothing. I did not add B♮: with the motto's C4 held in Cl 1 and Vla, a B3 would form a minor second at f. Hn 1 rests in 130, so the shared staff has a single dynamic.

### 2. The symphony's key plan: after movement II's VI, movement III must pass through IV–V (no score change)

**Problem.** Movement I realizes I–VI | IV–V | I (row 16). The symphony's movement keys, C minor – A♭ – C, put movement II on VI. cyclic-notes.md fixes III's final approach (bars 10–13 enlarged, trumpets B→C) and critic 113 proposes G as a held key in III's development. Nothing yet says how III gets from II's A♭ to its dominant.

**Evidence.** PDF 452–454: tonicized VI "lead[s] down in 3rds from I to IV", and "the expanded and tonicized IV moves to V" (Ex. 26-14). PDF 454 states the sonata form of that motion. PDF 456 §16 gives the alternative: VI may return to I "without an intervening dominant". PDF 443: a key area on V in minor "is, in principle, minor". PDF 449: before the return, "the leading tone must appear", in Mozart "prominently in the bass, supporting V6/5".

**Fix (for movement III).** Repeat movement I's large plan at symphony level. In III's development, tonicize F minor (IV) after A♭/D♭ material, then hold G minor as the 12–20-bar key area critic 113 asked for. Turn it to G major with B♮ in the bass (V6/5) before the reprise, as at K. 457 bar 93–99 (Ex. 26-8). Movement I's wedge (F at 111, bass falling F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C into G at 117) can be quoted as the link from IV to V, so that it returns in the same tonal function.

### 3. Movement II: D♭ (IV of A♭) belongs in the middle section only, entered without transition (no score change)

**Problem.** cyclic-notes.md makes D♭ the key of movement II's middle section and asks for a plagal D♭–A♭ close. II is planned to begin from movement I's last E♮ falling to E♭, a pitch heard throughout movement I as part of the C/A♭ complex. An early emphasis on D♭ would make II's tonic A♭ sound like V of D♭.

**Evidence.** PDF 451: "If it occurs early in the piece, a modulation to IV in major can disorient the listener … too much emphasis on IV as a 'key' can upset our sense of tonality, making us hear IV as I and I as V." PDF 451–452: IV is unproblematic "in the middle section of a three-part form … it frequently — and typically — enters right at the beginning of the new section without a transition (phrase modulation)", and then "usually moves on to V of the home key" (Ex. 26-11, K. 377).

**Fix (for movement II).** Keep D♭ harmony to passing IV chords in the A section. Start the B section directly in D♭ on its first downbeat, with no modulating bridge. Return through E♭7 (V of A♭) before A′. Keep the plagal D♭–A♭ only for the final cadence (PDF 456 §16 allows the contrapuntal return there).

### 4. Movement I confirms C minor only contrapuntally after bar 135; the finale owes the authentic cadence (no score change)

**Problem.** The last root-position V–i in C minor is 134→135. The ESC at 164–166 is V–I in C major and turns minor at 166. In the coda the dominant of 174 passes to i6/4 over the same G (175). The final closes are by step: vii°6/5–i at 177–178 (Cl 1 C–B over D–F–A♭) and N–i at 178–179. This is deliberate. It is protected (cadence 172–174, chorale 175–179, the unresolved B♮s) and it is how this coda ends unconsoled. In the book's terms it is a contrapuntal expansion of the tonic, not a cadential confirmation (PDF 456 §16; PDF 449: tonal plans "normally function within a larger framework of tonic-dominant").

**Fix (for movement III).** Write III's final cadence as the symphony's first root-position V7–I in C since bar 135 of movement I. Put the leading tone in the trumpets, B4 to C5, as in cyclic-notes.md item 3, approached through IV or II (PDF 443). Then the C-major ending answers the cadence movement I withheld, in the voice that left B♮ unresolved at 170.
