# Critic 15 (lens 5: engraving), review of v34

## What I studied
I rendered all 11 pages of `v34/score.pdf` at 80 and 200 dpi, plus page 1 of v24, v25 and v28 to date one regression. I read `score.ly`, `global.ily` and the woodwind, horn, harp and timpani variables. Corpus: `pdf/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.pdf` p. 3, which has dynamics under each staff, clear of the notes, and a "I." label on a clarinet entry after rests, and `pdf/DvorakA_O70__DvorakSYMPH7_conductor.pdf` p. 3, whose editorial note says the rehearsal mark "I" is "usually omitted in other scores". Web: the LilyPond 2.24 Internals page for Dynamic_align_engraver (https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/internals/dynamic_005falign_005fengraver), which shows that it creates DynamicLineSpanner and lives in Voice and DrumVoice. For harp pedal practice I found only secondary sources: Scoring Notes on Gould's advice about diagrams (https://www.scoringnotes.com/reviews/harp-font-makes-easy-work-of-creating-diagrams/) and Harp Column on marking pedal changes below the staff (https://harpcolumn.com/forums/topic/pedal-diagrams/). I could not reach Gould's text itself, so I state her rules from my own knowledge. **I tested every fix below together in `/tmp/critic-015/vA/`, which holds copies of `tools/` and v34 (build: `bash tools/build.sh vA score.ly`). The result has 0 warnings and 17 pages.**

## Findings

### 1. Since v25, every dynamic and hairpin prints inside the staff, on top of the noteheads (whole score)
Cycle 5 moved `Dynamic_engraver` to Staff but left `Dynamic_align_engraver` in Voice. The dynamics are therefore created outside any DynamicLineSpanner and get no side-positioning. In v34, ff, p and pp print on the noteheads: bass clarinet in bar 1, cor anglais in 5, bassoon chords in 9, flutes and clarinets in 11–12, every tutti chord at 26–32 and 43, the harp in 47, clarinets in 119, and so on. Hairpins run through the notes, for example flute 11, oboes 39–40 and flutes and oboes 150–151. v24 does not have this problem and v25 does, so cycle 5's finding 3 caused it. Gould and the Tchaikovsky engraving put dynamics below the staff, clear of the notes. A conductor cannot read the score as it stands. This is the most serious problem in the PDF.

Cycle 5 reported 280 programming errors when this engraver was moved. I reproduced them. They all come from the two percussion notes (dev.ily:211, recTwo.ily:115). `DrumVoice` keeps its own dynamic engravers, so adding them to `DrumStaff` as well creates a cyclic chain. **Fix (score.ly `\layout`):**
```
\context { \Voice \remove "Dynamic_engraver" \remove "Dynamic_align_engraver" }
\context { \Staff \consists "Dynamic_engraver" \consists "Dynamic_align_engraver" }
```
Delete the line `\context { \DrumStaff \consists "Dynamic_engraver" }`. Tested: 0 warnings, dynamics sit below the staff, shared staves still print one dynamic, and the score grows to 14 pages. This keeps cycle 5's one-dynamic-per-staff setup.

### 2. The harp has no pedal changes in the second theme (51–65), and bar 56 cannot be played
The only marking is the A♭ diagram at 47. Later bars need G♭ (RH 51, LH 60), G♮ (54, 63), E♮ (56), F♭ (61–64), and D♮ followed by D♭ (65–66). Bar 56 asks for E♭2 and E♮3 in the same arpeggio, which one E string cannot give. Gould's rule is to mark every pedal change below the bass staff where it happens. recTwo already does this correctly (B♭, A♭, "B♮ A♮"). **Fix (`tTwoHarpL`, bars 51–52 and 55–66):**
```
c,1_\markup \small "G♭" | des,1_\markup \small "G♮" | bes,,1 | es,1 |
aes,,1 | aes,,8\p(_\markup \small "F♭" es, aes, c fes2) | aes,,8(_\markup \small "F♮" des, f, aes, des2) | bes,,8( f, bes, des f2) |
es,8( bes, des g bes2) | ges,,8(_\markup \small "G♭" des, ges, bes,) es,8( bes, es ges) | des,8(_\markup \small "F♭" aes, des fes) fes,8( aes, des fes) | es,8( aes, c es aes2) |
es,8(_\markup \small "G♮" bes, des g) fes,8( bes, des g) | f,8(_\markup \small "F♮" c f aes) d,8(_\markup \small "D♮" aes, c f) | des,8(_\markup \small "D♭" aes, des f aes2) | bes,,8( f, bes, des f2) |
```
In bar 56 the E♮ is respelled as F♭.

### 3. The ottava on the shared flute staff is back, with flute 2 under it (41–43, 71–72, 113–115)
Cycle 5's finding 8 removed it, but `transFluteOne`, `closFluteOne` and `devFluteOne` add `\ottava #1` again. The ottava applies to the whole staff, so flute 2's g''1 at 41 prints as g', and its d''1 at 114–115 prints as d' below the staff under an "8va" bracket. This is a regression. **Fix:** delete the ottava pairs in these three places and write the notes with ledger lines. For example, `transFluteOne` bars 41–43 become `g'''1\ff~ | g'''2 f'''2\> | es'''4\p r4 r2 |`. Do the same for `closFluteOne` bars 71–72 and `devFluteOne` bars 113–115. The ottavas in 98–100, where flute 2 rests, can stay. Tested.

### 4. Duplicate dynamics an eighth apart, and a wrong "2." label (27, 69, 109, 131, 163)
Part 1 enters after `r8` with the same dynamic that part 2 has on beat 1, so the staff prints "ff ff" side by side. This happens in flutes, oboes and clarinets at 27 and 163, in flutes, oboes and clarinets at 131 (f), and in flutes at 109. At 69 the clarinets also get a "2." label while both players are playing. **Fix:** remove the part-1 dynamic. In `tOneFluteOne` write `r8 g''8 es'''4.->`, and do the same in `tOneOboeOne`, `tOneClOne`, the three `coda…One` parts, `recOneFluteOne`/`OboeOne`/`ClOne` (`r8 aes''8`, `r8 aes'8`) and `devFluteOne` (`r8 b'8`). In `closClOne` write `\partCombineApart r8 es'8 c''4.-> … aes'2-> \partCombineAutomatic | R1*4`, with no `\f`, because cl. 2's `\f` covers the staff. Tested.

### 5. Entries on shared staves with no player label (oboe 148; horn 47)
Oboe 1 enters at 148 after 12 bars' rest with no "1.". It could be read as a2, because the combiner prints a label only when its state changes. At 47 the horn tune begins a new system, a new section and a new key with no label. Tchaikovsky 6/i p. 3 labels the clarinet's entry after rests "I." **Fix:** in `recTwoOboeOne` write `e''2.~\mf^\markup \bold "1." …`. In `tTwoHnOne` bar 47 write `c'2.~\<^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "dolce" }`. Check the other entries after long rests for the same problem.

### 6. Expressive words are set in roman type
espr. (5, 56, 73, 167), dolce (47, 138), largamente (156) and morendo (177) print upright, the same as div., pizz. and muta. Gould sets expressive words in italic and technical instructions in roman. **Fix:** in all section files, change `^"espr."` to `^\markup \italic "espr."`, and do the same for `dolce`, `largamente` and `morendo`. A single sed command does it. Leave div., unis., pizz., arco, sul tasto, ord. and the timpani muta texts in roman.

### 7. Page and system breaks: the Allegro starts on the last bar of page 1, and the Lento coda is squeezed (15, 167–178)
Bar 15 (A, Allegro appassionato) is the last bar of page 1, so the new tempo arrives right before a page turn. Bars 163–178 share one system, which squeezes the cor anglais motto and the timpani pulse of 167–178 into half the width. **Fix (global.ily):** put `\break` after the first `s1*14`, before `\mark \default` at 15, and put `\break` after `s1*3 \tempo … "allargando" s1`, before 167. Tested: page 1 holds bars 1–14, and bars 167–178 fill a full system on the last page. The total rises to 17 pages, which is normal for an A3 full score.

### 8. Smaller details
(a) Rehearsal letter "I" (139) is easily mistaken for a numeral, and the Dvořák 7 note above confirms that editions omit it. Change to `rehearsalMarkFormatter = #format-mark-box-letters`, which skips I. The marks then run …H, J, K, L, M. (b) Bar numbers on every bar collide with tempo words, for example "166allargando". Either show them only at system starts (`barNumberVisibility = #all-bar-numbers-visible` with `\override BarNumber.break-visibility = ##(#f #f #t)`), or leave them every bar and add `\override BarNumber.font-size = #-1` and `\override BarNumber.padding = #2`. (c) The harp's final chord has no fermata: in `codaHarpL` write `<c, g, c>1\pp\laissezVibrer\fermata`, and add `\fermata` to the matching RH bar if the RH plays there.
