## Findings

No score change. In this third pass every Neapolitan and augmented-sixth setting in the movement still meets Units 28–29. I looked for places where a master would make the colour stronger, and I list them below in order of how much each could matter, each with the reading behind it and the reason I recommend keeping the current text. Two items (1 and 2) were not discussed in earlier reports. None reached the bar of a real improvement, so nothing was changed in the scratch copy. As a baseline I rebuilt the unchanged copy: 0 warnings, 18 PDF pages, only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1" in analysis.txt, and vl.txt identical to v482.

### 1. Bars 105.3–106: Italian-sixth sound whose ♯4 falls (keep)

Considered: at 105.3 the bass is C2, with E (Bsn 1 E3, Hn 1 E4) and B♭5 in Ob 2/Cl 1·2. Heard against the B at 106 (V of E minor), this is an Italian sixth of E minor, C–E–A♯, with the 3rd doubled as A&S PDF 505 (Ex. 29-7) prescribes. But the winds write B♭ and take it down, B♭5–A5–A♭5–G5, and only then leap to B5 at 106. The augmented sixth therefore does not open to the octave B (PDF 503, Ex. 29-2). One possible fix is to respell B♭ as A♯ and have it rise to B.

Why keep it: the wind line is the P head on D (D5–B♭5–A–A♭–G), the same G–E♭–D–D♭–C motif the whole movement is built on. Its chromatic fall is written in lowered notes descending, which is the correct spelling (PDF 497, Ex. 28-23). Respelling would cut the motif. In the reading the sound is "aug6 → III♯", a passing deception that fits development sections (PDF 523, Ex. 29-38). At 105.3 the ear follows the falling motif in the top voice, not an unresolved A♯, and the bass C–B already gives the ♭6–5 pull. Bars 96–108 ("passing keys") are protected (c413). Recommendation: keep.

### 2. Bars 111–117: lament bass that stops on C under the French sixth (keep)

Considered: the bass falls one semitone a bar, F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C, from 111 to 116. This is the descending chromatic bass that A&S PDF 510–511 (Ex. 29-18, Don Giovanni) names as the classic path to an augmented sixth, and it carries the end-of-development return of Ex. 29-28 (PDF 516–517). At 116 the line stops on C, a third above A♭, under D–F♯–A♭, so the aug6 is in an "other position" (PDF 520 §20: weak pull, no stepwise bass motion to root-position V). A master might have added A♭ at 116.3 so that the bass reaches ♭6 and the A♭–F♯ sixth opens onto the G pedal.

Why keep it: PDF 521 adds that the pull is strongest when the bass and an upper voice form the aug6. Here A♭ is present in Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1, Tbn 1 and Vln II, and F♯ in Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 2·4, Tpt 2, Tbn 2, Vln II and Vc, so the aug6 interval and its expansion to G (117) are still heard, only not with the bass. The C bass at 116 also gives the bypass for a motivic reason that PDF 516 (Ex. 29-27, K. 491) allows. The 117 G pedal then arrives as a fifth-leap, not a semitone, and that keeps it apart from the 12–13 Ger6 approach. Adding A♭ would also put a second ♭6–5 bass into the motto's register just before the trombone motto at 117. The wedge 111–116 and its bass are protected (c382, c452), and the reason given there still holds. Recommendation: keep.

### 3. Bars 160–163: no augmented sixth after the deceptive ♭VI (keep)

A&S PDF 513 (Ex. 29-21, 29-22): an aug6 often follows a deceptive cadence to ♭VI, and all it needs is ♯4 added to ♭VI. At 160 the fff A♭ chord goes to vii°7/D at 161 and to V at 161.3. Adding F♯ at 160.4 (Ob 1/Cl 1 B♭5 is a passing note there) would turn the end of 160 into a Ger6. Why keep it: c282 and c372 tested and rejected this. A German sixth here would repeat the 12–13 approach at the climax and weaken the second deception (162, vi) and the cadence at 166. "158–166 cadence chain" is protected. Recommendation: keep.

### 4. Bar 178.3: root-position ♭II as a bass neighbour without ♮7 (keep)

A&S PDF 522 (Ex. 29-36, Brahms Haydn Var. IV): over a bass ♭2 that is a neighbour of a final tonic (here minor, as in Brahms), adding ♮7 above makes an aug6 with a "Neapolitan" flavour. At 178.3 the bass is C–D♭–C under the chorale. Adding B♮ would follow Brahms closely. Why keep it: B→C over a C chord at the end is held back for the finale (do-not-reuse list; c282). The bare ♭II as a plagal-like neighbour inside I is the other form the reading gives for a coda (PDF 495, Ex. 28-20). Recommendation: keep.

### 5. Bar 61: Phrygian iv6/5 without ♯4 (keep)

A&S PDF 504 (Ex. 29-3) shows the Phrygian iv6–V as the origin of the aug6, and PDF 507–508 the doubly augmented 4th for a major 6/4. A D♮ at 61.3 would make bar 61 a German sixth in A♭. As c372 found, the plain Phrygian stage is valid in its own right. The G♭–F♭–E♭ bass is a seed for movement II, and bars 56–68 are protected. I also checked the outer voices at 61.4–62.1: Vln I goes F♭5–D♭5–E♭5 over the held F♭2, so there are no octaves. Recommendation: keep.

### 6. Bars 12–13: German sixth with anticipation of 5̂ (keep)

The Ger6 (A♭ bass, C, E♭, F♯) goes to the cadential 6/4 with C and E♭ held as common tones (PDF 505–506, Ex. 29-8). Fl 1 and Vln I anticipate G5 at 12.4. PDF 507 (Ex. 29-11) shows anticipating 2̂ to make a Fr6, but anticipating 5̂ against held ♭6 in the bass is a plain anticipation and does not produce fifths. The approach from iiø7 over D (bass D→A♭) is Haydn's (Ex. 29-15, PDF 509). The doubled ♯4 (F♯3/F♯4) is orchestral octave doubling, which c372 already examined. These bars are protected and quoted by the finale. Recommendation: keep.
