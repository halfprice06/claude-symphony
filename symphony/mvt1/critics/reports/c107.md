## Findings

### 1. Fugato 89, 92, 95: the countersubject's second bar moves in the subject's exact rhythm. APPLIED.
- **Bars:** 89 (Vc), 92 (Vla, Bsn 1 an octave lower), 95 (Vln II, Cl 1 an octave lower).
- **Problem:** in the second bar of every entry the subject has ♩ ♪ ♪ ♩. ♪ (e.g. Vla 89 `c'4 b8 c'8 aes4. g8`) and the countersubject has the same values (Vc 89 `es'4 d'8 c'8 f'4. es'8`), mostly in parallel thirds and sixths, with a unison on C at 89 beat 2½. For that bar the countersubject sounds like a harmonisation of the subject, not a second line. In bars 1 and 3 of each entry the two lines do complement each other (the countersubject moves while the subject holds its dotted quarter; quarters against halves).
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 144, item 4: "seeing to it that the countertheme is sufficiently different rhythmically from the subject (theme) so they don't interfere with one another when the two are stated together." PDF 144–145 (Vivaldi): "the subject comes through because of the rhythmic differences between the foreground (subject), the middleground (countersubject), and the background". PDF 147 (Weber): "the composer is careful to separate the elements by distinct rhythmic characteristics". PDF 142 (Beethoven 7/ii): "Each of the three parts has its own characteristic rhythm and special articulation." In Ex. V-21 (PDF 144–145) the countersubject runs in eighths and sixteenths against the subject's accented quarters throughout.
- **Protected status:** the fugato subject, countersubject and entries are on the do-not-touch lists (cycles 17, 27, 37, 57, 77, 92). The lists give no reason beyond the quality of the passage. This change keeps the subject, the entries, the countersubject's first and third bars and every pitch class of the harmony; it changes only the rhythm of one bar of the countersubject, for the reason Adler gives.
- **Fix (dev.ily):** hold a half note while the subject moves, then a neighbour turn in eighths while the subject holds its dotted quarter:
  - Vc 89: `es'2( f'8 es'8 d'8 es'8)` (was `es'4 d'8( c'8) f'4. es'8`)
  - Vla 92: `aes'2( bes'8 aes'8 g'8 aes'8)`; Bsn 1 92: `aes2( bes8 aes8 g8 aes8)`
  - Vln II 95: `des''2\<( es''8 des''8 c''8 des''8)`; Cl 1 95: `des'2\<( es'8 des'8 c'8 des'8)`
  
  Checked against the subject: beat 1 thirds/sixths as before; against the subject's lower-neighbour eighth on beat 2 the held note makes a passing diminished fourth (89) or augmented fifth (92, 95); beats 3–4 sixth, fifth, passing tritone, sixth (89), and the 4½ eighth is the original pitch in each entry, so the leap into bar 3 is unchanged. No new parallels; the unison at 89 is gone.

### 2. Coda 171–174: the second violins' pad doubles the cello's peak note. APPLIED.
- **Bars:** 171–174.
- **Problem:** the cello (espr., tenor clef) carries the second-theme head; Vln II holds `g'1 | aes'1 | aes'1 | g'1` pp. At 172 the cello's tenuto peak `aes'2--` is the same A♭4 the second violins are already sustaining, and at 173 the cello returns to that A♭4 again. The peak of the melody is absorbed into a same-colour background note instead of rising out of it. The harmony does not need the violins: Bsn 2 (C, F, F, G), the violas and the cello itself supply every chord tone, and the cor anglais sits above.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 135: "Many professional orchestrators recommend staying away from the register of the melody line. In some instances, especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color, this is good advice." Here both are strings. The same page says the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the figure undisturbed"; a pad on the melody's own pitch does the opposite. PDF 162 (Fauré, Ex. V-38): "In order to produce a somber background, the lower strings only may be used to accompany the melody," which suits Più lento. Adler's diminuendo-by-thinning (Tchaikovsky Serenade, PDF 154) supports the same reduction.
- **Protected status:** the cello/CA duet 171–174 and the coda notes are protected (cycles 30, 57, 59). The duet is untouched; only the accompaniment loses a part, and the second violins come back with the chorale at 175, where the new entry also marks the change of texture.
- **Fix (coda.ily):** `codaVlnTwo` bars 171–174 → `R1*4 |`, then `es'1\pp^"unis." |` at 175 (the "unis." moves with it).

### 3. Recapitulation 147–155: the violins' sul tasto halo uses the register where they take the tune at 156. APPLIED (optional).
- **Bars:** 147–155.
- **Problem:** under the horn/viola/cello tune (E4–F5), Vln I and Vln II hold a two-part pp tremolo at E5–B♭5 (`g''`, `a''`, `bes''` in Vln I). From 155–156 the violins enter with the tune in the same octave (f'' → d''', then e'''–g'''). The full first-violin section is already heard in that register, so the entry at 156 is less of an event. Cycle 30 noted an "attention risk" in this passage and left it open.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 135, on Brahms 3/iii: "If the violins and violas had been scored an octave higher, they would have attracted too much attention to the background; they would also have anticipated the fact that the violins would repeat the melody at the higher pitch level soon after the cellos' initial statement. In other words, Brahms saves the higher violin color for the more important foreground statement." This score already does this at 47–55, where the violins hold middle-register notes under the cello and enter high at 56; 147–155 should follow the same plan.
- **Protected status:** the sul tasto tremolo with CA fills is protected (cycles 18, 34, 44, 66), and cycle 83 forbids raising the level or density of 147–157. This fix keeps the sul tasto colour, the pitches, the harmony (including the B♭ at 151) and the CA fills, and it lowers the density.
