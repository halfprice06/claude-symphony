# Critic 256: Playability, instrument by instrument (v256)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 6 The Woodwind Choir and ch. 7 Individual Woodwinds (adler1 PDF 168-232). I read every text page in the range and rendered every page of examples at 110 dpi (Ex. VI-1 to VI-18, VII-1 to VII-101). I took the register charts from the images, not the OCR: flute VII-2 (PDF 185), oboe VII-32 (PDF 198), cor anglais VII-47 (PDF 205), clarinet VII-56 (PDF 210), bass clarinet VII-73 (PDF 216), bassoon VII-89 (PDF 226), bassoon trills VII-90 (PDF 227) and contrabassoon VII-97 (PDF 230).

Method. I checked the score against each principle bar by bar. I used the section files, the clarinet, bass clarinet and contrabassoon parts (rebuilt in the scratch copy), and a script over v256/full.midi. For each woodwind the script lists sounding range, every note in the instrument's weak or soft-restricted register with its MIDI velocity, every stretch played without a break of 0.2 s and of 2.5 s, repeated-note runs, slurred leaps of an octave or more, and the timpani pitches by bar. Every hit was then checked in the source. \breathe marks do not show in the MIDI, so I read each long stretch the script found in the .ily files.

## Findings

### 1. Contrabassoon has no breath in the recapitulation tutti, 131-138 (applied)

Bars 131-138.3. The contrabassoon plays crotchets C2 ff from 131 (`c,4\ff c,4 c,4 c,4 | … | g,,4 g,,4 g,,4 g,,4 |`), goes straight into the mf < f hemiola at 135-137, and stops only on beat 4 of 138. That is 7¾ bars at crotchet = 126, about 14.8 s, in the instrument's lowest octave at ff and f. It is the contrabassoon's longest unbroken stretch in the movement. In the exposition at 27-32 it plays six bars and rests at 26.4. In the development it has a breath every three bars at 117-126 (c146).

Evidence. Adler (adler1 PDF 230): in the contrabassoon's lower range "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests so that the player can replenish his breath more easily." The general rule on PDF 175 says the same: careful consideration of "the limitations placed on the player in certain registers". The fix follows the score's own rule from cycle 87: when adding a breath rest, check that the line keeps sounding in another player.

Fix. A crotchet rest on beat 4 of 134, at the end of the ff block and before the mf hemiola entry:
```
  c,4\ff c,4 c,4 c,4 | c,4 c,4 g,,4 g,,4 | aes,,4 aes,,4 aes,,4 aes,,4 | g,,4 g,,4 g,,4 r4 |
```
At 134 the tuba (G1, `g,,1`), bass trombone (G2) and double basses (repeated G1 quavers) hold the bass, so the 16-foot G is still in the tuba and basses. The contrabassoon then re-enters on the mf < accented C at 135, which is cleaner after a breath. No other note changes.

### 2. No other woodwind finding; items checked and kept, with reasons

I checked every woodwind passage that comes near one of Adler's limits. Each one meets the reading, so I recommend no change to the following:

- **Bass clarinet low C at 92-93 and 105-106** (sounding B♭1-C2). Adler (PDF 216) calls low C "still risky" because in 1989 not all orchestras had extended instruments. The score marks it in three places: the "(low C instr.)" label, the part title "with low C", and the treble-clef French notation Adler recommends (PDF 217). Low-C bass clarinets are standard in professional orchestras now, and the passage is protected (c146). Keep.
- **Oboe link F4-E♭4 at p, 45-46, and Ob 2 E♭4 p at 43.** Adler's limit (PDF 198) is pp in the lowest fifth. These are p, at the end of a diminuendo, and the link was decided at c196. Keep.
- **Long single-breath woodwind stretches.** Cor anglais 5-9 (part has "breathe ad lib.", c196). B.cl 177-179, one pp slur of about 13 s in the chalumeau through the rit.; a pp chalumeau note needs little air, the line is doubled by the bass trombone and cellos, and the coda is protected (c228, c249, c251). Ob 1 120.4-126, about 14.6 s; Adler (PDF 175) singles out the oboe as the wind that can play longest on one breath, and the player rests 127-130 afterwards. Fl 1 97.4-102 has slur breaks at 100 and 102 under doubling by Ob 1, C.a. and Vln I. All kept.
- **Bassoon lowest fifth.** Nothing under F2 is marked pp. The softest is Bsn 2 C2 p < at 110 and Bsn 1 C2 p at 166, which Adler (PDF 226) allows ("a firmer attack").
- **Staggered breaths on held pedals.** At 117-126 Bsn 2, B.cl and Cbsn never breathe in the same bar. This is Adler's Brahms principle (PDF 188, Ex. VII-9) applied to the bass, and it is already right.
- **Timpani tunings** (lens item). The MIDI pitches agree with the tuning table in coherence/mvt1.md: B♭2 only at 40 (after "muta A♭ in B♭" at 34), B2 only at 97 (after 84), and A♭2 from 115 (after 111). There are no pitches outside the drums' current tuning.
