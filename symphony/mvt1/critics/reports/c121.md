# Critic 121 — lens: melody and memorability. Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II "Melody", strings (PDF 54–63) and Examples 1–34

## What I studied

I read every text page of PDF 54–63 in full, plus the end of the section on PDF 64. I also looked at the pages as images, because the distribution diagrams on PDF 60–63 (the braces showing which strings play which octave) do not survive OCR. I rendered and viewed all forty example pages the section cites, Examples 1–34 (PDF 173–212). Then I checked v140 (sections/*.ily, score.pdf) against each principle. To do that I extracted the notes per track from v140/score.midi and listed, for every bar, whether the first violins' notes above G5 have a unison or octave-below doubling in any other part. That listing is the evidence for the finding below. I did not need the corpus or the web. The book's own examples gave the comparisons.

## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bars 20–21: the first theme's D♭6 peak is the only unsupported high violin melody in the movement

**Problem.** In the second phrase of the exposition's first theme, Vln I climbs to the sf D♭6 at 20 and falls back through c'''–f'' in 21. Strings play alone here (Hn 3·4 and Bsn 1 hold a pp pad). Vln II and the violas hold their F4–A♭4 accompaniment and counterline, so the peak stands more than an octave above the next string.

**Evidence.**
- The per-bar MIDI listing found 84 bars in which Vln I plays above G5. Bars 20 and 21 are the only two with **no** unison and **no** octave-below doubling of those notes, 0% of their duration in either. Every other high violin line is doubled at the octave below:
  - by Vln II at 60–63, 97–102, 117–123, 139–142, 156–157;
  - by Ob 1 + Tpt 1 at 69–72 and 158–161.
- The same D♭6 peak at 63 (second theme) is set as Rimsky prescribes, with Vln I and Vln II in octaves. So is the recapitulation's version of this very phrase, which peaks on A♭6 at 140 (Vln II a♭''). Only the exposition statement, the first time the listener hears this peak, is left bare.
- Rimsky, PDF 58–59: "melodic figures in the very high register of the violins become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves. Such doubling secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre." PDF 62, Note I: melodies above the middle of the 5th octave "are generally doubled an octave below".
- Ex. 22 (Tsar's Bride, PDF 198) is a quiet cantabile that rises past the staff with Vn I and Vn II in octaves the whole way. Ex. 26 (PDF 202) gives the lower octave more players than the upper one.

**Fix.** Vln II leaves its syncopation for one and a half bars and doubles the peak and its descent an octave below, with the same slur, sf and hairpin as Vln I. It then picks up the syncopation again on beat 3 of 21.
- It does not double beats 1–2 of bar 20. There the line runs D♭4–A♭4, in the viola's register, and would cover the viola counterline that cycles 37–38 protect. From beat 3 of 20 on, Vln II stays above the viola and does not cross it.
- The D♭ that the syncopation drops at 20.3–21.2 is still sounding: in Hn 3, Bsn 1 and the cellos at 20, and in Hn 3 (d♭'2) in the first half of 21. No chord tone is lost.
- tOne.ily, `tOneVlnTwo`, bars 20–21:

```
r8 des'8~ des'4 des''2~\sf | des''8\>( c''8 bes'8 aes'8) r8 d'8~\mp d'4 |
```

(was `r8 des'8~ des'4 r8 des'8~ des'4 | r8 des'8~ des'4 r8 d'8~ d'4 |`)

**History.** The fix changes no notes of the theme, no viola counterline note, no Vln II note at 15–19 or 22, and adds no wind. It keeps cycle 44's "strings alone 15–22", cycle 91's theme pitches and rhythms, and cycle 118's D♭6 peaks. It undoes no earlier fix. It also follows the cycle 88 rule: the doubling part has the same slur and sf as Vln I.

### Points checked and left as they are (no finding)

- **158–161.** No string plays the octave between the violins' unison (E6–A♭6) and the violas and cellos (G3–A♭4). Rimsky's double-octave layouts (PDF 62, Ex. 32) fill that octave with strings. Here, though, Tpt 1 at fff and Ob 1 hold it. That meets his condition for wide string spacing, "only when supported by wind instruments" (PDF 63). Cycle 24 chose the unison for weight on the climax note, and cycles 7 and 14 warned against flipping this scoring without new evidence. The book gives none, so I do not reopen it.
- **107–108.** The violas carry the second-theme head alone for two bars. PDF 55 says viola melodies are "generally" doubled. Ex. 5 and Ex. 6 (PDF 179) show undoubled viola phrases, and the flute joins at 109 (cycle 109). This follows the book.
- **56–59.** Violins in unison with no wind doubling. Rimsky's "usually" (PDF 57) is not a rule, and his own Ex. 12 (PDF 186) is strings alone. Cycles 44 and 54 removed the oboe and flute here on purpose.
- **69–72 (codetta).** Vln I alone among the strings plays C6–A♭6, but Ob 1 and Tpt 1 double it an octave below (100% of the high notes). That satisfies Note I.
