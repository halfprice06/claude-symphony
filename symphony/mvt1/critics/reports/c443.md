## Findings

No finding asks for a note to change. I applied nothing in the scratch copy. The proportions have not changed since c243: v443 section timings from full.midi are intro 67.2 s, P 34.3, transition 26.9, S 47.1, codetta 15.2, development 105.4, recapitulation first group 30.7, second group 44.8, coda 58.9 (total 430.5 s). Below are the points this pass adds and the changes I considered and rejected.

### 1. Every ♭VI in the movement reaches V by a half-step bass or a °7, as PDF 593 asks. Keep.

PDF 593: "The usual goal of ♭VI is a cadential V … the half-step progression in the bass helps to intensify the V. A most natural way of leading ♭VI to V is to transform it into an augmented 6th chord … not the only way." I traced every ♭VI, including one earlier passes did not list under this rule:

- **10–12→13** (C minor): A♭ triad → German sixth on A♭ (Hn 3·4 F♯3 at 12) → V. The textbook route.
- **60–62** (A♭ major, local ♭VI = F♭): bass G♭–F♭–E♭ by whole bars, iv6 over F♭ at 61, into the cadential 6/4 at 62. This is the Chopin Op. 36 shape in Ex. 32-11 exactly: an expanded chord a half step above the dominant, falling to it, with the arrival marked by the cadential 6/4 and "poco animando" at 62–63. Earlier reports recorded 60–62 only as movement II's bass; under this unit it is also the model case of ♭VI→V inside the second key.
- **82→83**: A♭ ff → G (V♭9 strokes), half step in the bass.
- **122**: A♭6 over the G pedal, the half step stated as a neighbour on top of V.
- **160→161**: ♭VI fff → °7 over D → V7. This is the "not the only way" route, and the °7 is the K. 542 device (PDF 601). Protected since c172/c190 ("♭VI–°7–V climax at 160–161").

So the movement uses both routes the reading names, and each ♭VI is placed where it intensifies a dominant. Fix: none.

### 2. The compressed recapitulation of S stands up against the reading's Tchaikovsky plan. Keep the section lengths.

Ex. 32-20 (PDF 600, image) gives bar numbers for Tchaikovsky 4/i. In the plan's numbering the second and third themes occupy 104–192 in the exposition and 282–311 in the recapitulation: about 89 bars reduced to about 30. This movement reduces S + codetta from 26 bars (47–72) to 20 (147–166), and the first group from 32 (15–46) to 16 (131–146), with the first group's soft sentence 15–22 dropped and the modulating transition replaced by the ff C-minor cadence at 143 (c433 Finding 4). Recapitulation/exposition time is 75.5/123.7 s = 0.61. The master who compressed most in the reading's own examples went much further; Dvořák 7/i (c433) is at 0.47. The score is inside the range the models show. This supports the protected section lengths (c215, c243, c323, c413, c433) and gives no reason to lengthen the recapitulation. Fix: none.

### 3. After the ♭VI at 160 the score relaxes faster than K. 542, and it should. Keep.

PDF 601: in K. 542 the deceptive ♭VI "is not followed by a quick relaxation of tension"; a transitional passage leads to V "and a strong authentic cadence". The score (page 16–17 images, full.midi): 160 fff; 161 °7 over D → V7, still fff; 162 vi ff with diminuendo to mp in the brass; 163–165 p–mp, harp and strings; 166 C major p, rit. That is five bars and about 12 s from the fff to the soft resolution, followed by the forte coda head at 167.

The reading's model would ask for a longer high plateau and then a strong V–I in C. Both are deliberately withheld: movement I never confirms C major at forte, so the finale's 222→223 can be the first (mvt1.md, Do-not-reuse; protected c243 F3, c330, c440). The relaxation is also not abrupt: 162 keeps ff on its downbeat, and the bass then runs a full cadential circle A–F–D–G–C (vi–IV–ii–V–I, 162–166) instead of dropping straight to the tonic. The forte that K. 542 spends on a cadence is spent here on 167–170, which ends on the °7 over C with a fermata, a common-tone °7 (PDF 589, Ex. 32-7). The tension is therefore not lost; it is held over a chord that does not resolve, which is what the finale needs. Fix: none.

### 4. Considered and rejected

- **Expand the stations of 77–82 (PDF 599: "each intermediate chord is expanded").** Each station would need more bars. That changes protected section lengths and renumbers every bar the other movements cite (c243, c333, c413). The compressed form is the Appassionata plan at 1–2 bars per station, and the motivic content (row 17) is complete. Rejected.
- **Make 160 a German sixth so the bass falls A♭→G (PDF 593, "most natural way").** It would replace the °7 route that the reading also allows and that the finale's quotations of the stop chord depend on. The reason for protecting 160–161 (c172, c190) stands. Rejected.
- **Keep the top line on beat 1 of 97 (violins tie B♭5→B5) so the inflection is in one voice.** PDF 585 says Beethoven keeps such a join abrupt when the contrast is motivic. Here the C–B in the horns, trombone, bassoon and cellos at 96.4.5–97.1 is the horn call at pitch, and the violins' re-entry with the S pickup at 97.4 is part of the protected dialogue (c309, c323). Rejected.
- **A longer plateau after 160 (K. 542).** See Finding 3. Rejected.
