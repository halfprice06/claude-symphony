## What I studied

I read every OCR page from PDF 163 to 210 in full. I rendered all 48 pages at 110 dpi and looked at every example (Ex. 11-1 to 11-14, 12-1 to 12-22, 13-1 to 13-20, the exercise pages and the figured-bass chart). I took the chord labels from the images because the OCR labels are unreliable. Then I checked v151 bar by bar against what the reading teaches, using the score PDF and three scripts run on score.midi:
- a beat-by-beat reduction giving the bass, the pitch-class set and the top note;
- an all-voice parallel check, adapted from tools/critic-scripts/vl.py so that it takes the track names from the MIDI file;
- a scan for melodic augmented seconds.

The scan found no parallel fifths except the known clarinet figuration at 99. All the octave pairs it found are orchestral doublings. I did not use the corpus or the web; the assigned reading was enough for these findings.

## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Bar 11: bare root-position diminished II in the introduction (applied)
**Problem.** On beats 1, 2 and 4 of bar 11 the chord is only D–F–A♭ over D. D sounds in five parts: Cb, Bsn 2, Hn 4, Vla, and Cl 2 with Hn 2. The B appears only on beat 3, in Fl and Vln I. The result is a root-position diminished supertonic in C minor, exposed during the p< swell before the German sixth.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 165: "II5/3 in minor is a problematic diminished triad".
- PDF 172, Points 3: "In minor avoid II5/3".
- PDF 179 (Ex. 12-4b) and PDF 191, Points 4: "In minor, the 7th of II7 improves the root-position sonority of II."
- PDF 177: the 7th "enters as a common tone held over … from the preceding chord" and resolves "downward and by step".
- PDF 186 (Ex. 12-14): II6/5 to V4/2 over a common bass.

**Fix.** In intro.ily, Vla bar 11 becomes `c'2 b2` (was `d'1`). The violas' C, held through 9 and 10, becomes the prepared 7th of iiø7. On beat 3 it falls to B over the same D (iiø7 to vii°6/5), and the B rises to C in the German sixth at 12. This also gives the B a stepwise resolution that Vln I's protected motto leap (B4–A♭5) does not. D stays in Cb, Bsn 2, Hn 4, Cl 2 and Hn 2. The change also removes two octave pairs into 12 (Vla with Cl 2/Hn 2, D–C). The protected violin leaps 9–12 and the German sixth at 12–13 are unchanged.

### F2. Bars 82–83: augmented second in Hn 4, VI to V♭9 (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 4 holds A♭3 for the ff A♭ chord at 82 and then moves to B3 at 83. That is a melodic augmented second, played forte.

**Evidence.** PDF 166: "in minor, the 3rd of VI must be doubled to avoid a melodic augmented 2nd". Ex. 11-5 marks the A♭–B motion "not / A2", and Ex. 11-6b marks it "wrong". PDF 202–203 (Ex. 13-12) repeats the rule. The scan found no other A♭–B step between chords.

**Fix.** In dev.ily, devHnFour bar 82 becomes `c'1\ff` (was `aes1\ff`). Hn 3 already plays `c'1\ff`, so the dynamics on the shared staff are equal and the part-combiner prints the bar a2. Hn 4 then moves C4–B3 by step. A♭ remains in Hn 1, Tpt 2, Tbn 2, Bsn 2, the bass trombone, the tuba and the basses. Cycle 33's A♭ (Hn 1, Tpt 2, Vla, chosen to avoid an A♭maj7) is untouched.

### F3. Bar 46: II6 under 6̂ before the second theme; make it II6/5 (applied)
**Problem.** At 46 the strings play D♭–F–B♭ with F doubled; the oboe has F on top, which is 6̂ in A♭. The chord then goes to V7 on beat 3. The cello falls from A♭3 (bar 45) to F3, so the 1̂ that could become the chord's 7th is dropped, and the leading tone G3 on beat 3 is reached from below.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 178: II6/5 "derives a much richer sonority from the added dissonant tone; its progression to V highlights the leading tone by resolving into it from a dissonance". Also: "II6/5 supports 6̂ in the soprano much more easily; the repetition of 1̂ (coming from a tonic) removes the danger of parallel 5ths" (Ex. 12-3d; PDF 191, Points 3).
- PDF 177: the 7th prepared as a common tone.
- PDF 183, Ex. 12-8c–e: I6–II6/5–V.

**Fix.** In trans.ily, Vc bar 46 becomes `aes2 g4 \clef tenor es4(\mf` (was `f2 g4 …`). A♭3 is held from 45, becomes the 7th of ii6/5 (D♭–F–A♭–B♭: Cb, Ob, Vc, Vla, Vln II), and falls to G3 on beat 3. No parallels result; this was checked against every part. This bar lies in the protected fill 43–46 (cycle 13) and the route 36–47 (cycle 120). Both were protected for length, key route and pacing. This change alters one inner note and leaves the length, keys, dynamics, the oboe line and the cello's E♭ pickup as they were.

### F4. Bars 64 and 68: two deceptive arrivals on the same vi; V7–IV6 at 68 (proposal, not applied)
**Problem.** The exposition's closing section reaches F minor at 64 (V7 at 63, then vii°7/vi, then vi) and again at 68 (V7, then vi, the p deceptive cadence). That is the same chord as the goal twice in four bars. The recapitulation varies its two deferrals: V–♭VI at 160 and V–vi at 162.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 204–205: "IV6 will sometimes appear instead of VI at a deceptive cadence, where it has the effect of temporarily turning the 7th of V7 into a consonance" (Ex. 13-15; Ex. 13-20i "deceptive cadence (to IV6) major or minor"; PDF 209, Points 6a).
- Cycle 103 already removed a third F-minor arrival from the codetta for this reason.

**Fix (tTwo.ily, bar 68 only).**
- Vc: `f,8(\p des f aes des'2\<)`
- Vla: `\after 2 \< <des' f'>1\p`
- Hn 3: `des'2.\p r4`
- Cl 1 and Hn 2: `\after 2 \< des'1\p`
- harp RH: `r2 <f' aes' des''>2\arpeggio`

The bass F, the bassoon, the violins' A♭ and their run are unchanged. The leading tone still rises (G–A♭), the soprano still goes 2̂–1̂, and the D♭ 7th resolves to C at 69.

I did not apply this. It changes the last note of the protected counterline (cycle 131) and the harmony under the protected ending 64–68 (cycles 11 and 19). Both of those protections cover melody, and no melody pitch changes here. It is offered for the composer's judgment.
