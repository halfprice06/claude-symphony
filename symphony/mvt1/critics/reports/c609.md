# Critic 609: holistic comparison with Tchaikovsky, Piano Concerto No. 1/i, and with v599

Version reviewed: v609. Lens 9. Master movement: Tchaikovsky, Piano Concerto No. 1 in B♭ minor, Op. 23, i (Andante non troppo e molto maestoso – Allegro con spirito).

**Source for the master.** The local corpus has no score of Op. 23. archive.org has the Breitkopf & Härtel two-piano edition (London, 1890, V.A. 4076), item `concertono1bmino00tcha` (https://archive.org/details/concertono1bmino00tcha). ## Findings

### 1. Second group: the same plan as Tchaikovsky's. Keep.

Tchaikovsky's second group (PDF 18, letter E) is marked "Poco meno mosso", the same word this score uses at 47 and 147. The tune is given first to the orchestra, marked "espress.", p. The harmony mostly changes once or twice a bar under a stepwise tune. The soloist then restates it "p dolce e molto espress." with fuller chromatic inner lines and a "marcato" lower voice. After a "poco riten." comes the "a tempo tranquillo" idea in the violins, pp.

This movement uses the same order:
- At 47–55, horn and cello play S over a plain I–IV–ii–V frame, with the harp under them.
- At 56–63, the violins take it an octave higher, "molto cantabile". The chromatic colour comes in here: the bass falls G♭–F♭–E♭ at 60–62 and a mixture iv6 sits at 61.

A&S PDF 552 explains why this order works. A chromatic passage is heard against the diatonic frame it decorates, and 47–55 states that frame first. The protection of the plain 47–55 (c389, c429, c489) is therefore the same plan Tchaikovsky follows, not a weakness. No change.

### 2. Where the master is ahead: a second lyrical idea in the second group. Not applied.

Tchaikovsky gives his second group a second tune (the "a tempo tranquillo" violins, PDF 18) and returns to it later. This movement's second group has one tune, S. It goes on to the codetta (69–72, on the P head) and the flute motto (73–76).

A second lyrical idea would make the group more varied. It would also cost at least 8 bars. That changes the bar numbers that movements II–IV cite, puts the 17-page layout at risk, and alters protected proportions (c215, c1070: section lengths). It would also take some of movement II's role as the lyrical centre, since movement II's theme A answers the Hn + Vc colour of 47 (coherence/mvt1.md, seeds 46–52). I do not recommend it.

### 3. The introduction: a cyclic motto instead of a detachable tune. Keep.

Tchaikovsky opens with the horns ff and full chords, then gives a broad tune to violins and cellos, mf (PDF 6). The introduction ends pp and "morendo" over held chords, with a fermata (PDF 12). Its material does not come back in the first movement. That last point is from published descriptions; I checked only these pages.

This introduction is a 14-bar motto, low and bare, and every later theme grows from it: the rising sixth opens P, S and the horn call (c550). In that respect this movement is more tightly built than the master. Its quiet close is the solo horn call over V7 into the silence at 14 (protected: c265, c583). That does the same job as Tchaikovsky's "morendo" fermata. A&S PDF 483 (the ♭II6 before rhetorical silences) and the German sixth before the structural point at 12–13 (PDF 503) are both used here as the reading describes. No change.

### 4. Comparison with v599: nothing got worse. Keep.

Four changes were made between v599 and v609:
- **c599, 113–116.** "poco a poco stringendo" (MIDI 129–138) and "a tempo" at 117. The score page (PDF 11) shows the words clear of the notes, and the stringendo lies under the brass pp cresc. Tchaikovsky's approaches are written the same way, with the tempo word in the score (PDF 12 "rallent.", "meno mosso"; PDF 18 "poco riten."). Better.
- **c604, 161.** Violas B3/F4 on beats 1–2 (was D4/F4). The vii°6/5 now has its leading tone in the strings, and the bass D is no longer quadrupled (A&S PDF 374–375, Ex. 22-1/22-2). The viola B3 doubles Tbn 1 B3 in unison. B is the key's own leading tone, and Tbn 1 resolves it to C4 at 162, so the Unit 25 rule on applied leading tones (PDF 419) does not apply. Better.
- **c605.** The Hn 3·4 staff is in bass clef 19–157. This is notation only.
- **c607, 160–161.** Cl 2 stays on the counter and Bsn 1 takes E♭4 at 160. The vl.txt entries it added are doublings (Bsn 1 with the bass, Bsn 1 with Tpt 3). The d5→P5 scan is identical for v599 and v609 (49 and 49 instances, none in 158–162).

The page count, the warnings and the outer-voice parallel are unchanged.

### 5. Record correction: a paper d5→P5 at 26→27. Keep, with the reason recorded.

c409 and c509 recorded "no d5→P5 pair at 26→27". The scan finds two pairs:
- Hn 1 A♭4→G4 against Hn 3 D4→C4.
- Fl 2 A♭5→G5 against Bsn 2 D3→C3.

Each pair forms D–A♭ in the stop chord and C–G in the tonic at 27, and the parts move in similar motion. This is the voice leading Ex. 22-3c marks "avoid" (A&S PDF 375). Two facts make it inaudible as hidden fifths. First, the whole orchestra is silent on 26.4 (son.py shows no sounding note there), so no line connects the two chords. Second, 27 is a fresh ff attack with C and G doubled in many octaves. The layouts of 26 and 27 are quoted by the finale at its 199–204 (BRIEF4, the cross-movement list). No change. The record should say the pair exists on paper and is broken by the rest.

The other hits do not come from a vii°7 chord resolving:
- 70–72 are arpeggios inside V7 or ii7. These are chordal skips (PDF 329).
- 151.4 is passing motion in the C.a. answer against the tune over V6/5 of IV.
- 157 is the protected 156–157 bass.

### 6. Where the master is ahead: weight at the main climax. Not reopened.

Tchaikovsky's climaxes rest on long dominant preparations. This movement's main climax (158–161) is reached in two bars (156–157). Ten critics have flagged this, and it stays protected (c533, c553: the ♭6 bass at 157 and the ♭VII7–V7 exchange were tested and rejected). I have no new answer to the stated reasons, which are bar counts, the quoted 167–170, and parallels in the tested versions. I do not reopen it. What reaches the listener at 158–161 is at a master's level in itself. The cadential 6/4 is ff over the G pedal, the counter is Ob 1 + Cl 1·2 over Hn 3·4 in octaves, and ♭VI at 160 is fff with the cymbal (A&S PDF 532, mixture as a special device in major).

### 7. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

On the evidence of these pages, mostly yes. Next to Op. 23/i the score shows the same craft in three ways:
- tempo words placed where the music changes speed;
- a tune given to winds and then to strings, with a fuller setting the second time;
- climaxes coloured by mixture rather than by new keys.

Two things set it apart from Tchaikovsky's first movement:
- **The second group's range.** It has one tune where his has two (Finding 2). This is structural and costs bars.
- **The surface.** Tchaikovsky's surface is more extravagant (a soloist, and a big detachable tune). That belongs to a concerto, and this symphony's cyclic plan should not copy it.

Neither is a fault the assigned reading can name. Every change I considered would touch a protected or quoted passage without answering the stated reason, so the result is no change.
