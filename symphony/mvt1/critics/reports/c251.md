## Findings

The themes themselves stand up under this lens. The motto (rising minor sixth, then D–D♭–C) is short, built on one interval and one chromatic fall, and is heard in four colours (low cellos and bass clarinet 1–4, cor anglais 5–8, cor anglais 171–173, Vln I 177–182), so it will be remembered in the way Tchaikovsky's fate motif in No. 5 is. P (15–22) carries the motto in Allegro rhythm and climbs to D♭6 at 20 before it falls back by step. S (47–55) is the one long-breathed tune, a dolce cello and horn melody in A♭ of the Dvořák 8/i kind, and its consequent rises to F5 at 52. I propose no change to any theme's notes. The findings concern how the strings sound the motto at its two softest and most exposed moments, which is the subject of Adler's chapter.

### 1. Cellos sul G for the motto, 1–3 and 175–178 (applied)

Bars 1–3: the cellos play the motto G2–E♭3–D3–C3–D♭3–C3 pp at Lento with the bass clarinet. With ordinary fingering the E♭3 falls under the first finger on the D string and the D3 quaver is taken as the open D string. The rising sixth G2→E♭3 then crosses strings, and the next note is unvibrated. Bars 175–178: the cellos play the same pitches as the chorale bass in whole notes, so D3 at 177 is held for a whole bar (4.8 s at crotchet = 50), and a player using ordinary fingering would take it open.

Adler says that "in slow, expressive passages, one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture", and that the open string, without "the controlling influence provided by the finger which oscillates a stopped note", "stands out peculiarly" (adler1 PDF 73–74). He allows a string or fingering to be specified when "the desired timbre" requires it (PDF 73) and gives the marking to use (PDF 76). His Tchaikovsky 5/ii example (PDF 74, Ex. III-5) keeps a sung line on the G string so that it has one colour, with the leaps taken as shifts.

On the cello's G string the whole motto lies in first to fourth position. The G2 pickup is the open string and cannot be avoided. The sixth becomes one shift to E♭3, and D3, C3 and D♭3 are stopped and vibrated. The G string is the cello's weakest in carrying power (PDF 99), but here the line is pp and is doubled by the bass clarinet, with the basses an octave below, so it does not need to carry.

### 2. Vln I sul D for the last motto, 177–182 (applied)

Bars 177–182: Vln I plays the last motto pp to ppp: G4 | E♭5 | (E♭5) D5 | C5 D♭5 | C5 | C5, with the C5 tied into movement II. With ordinary fingering the G4 is on the D string and the line crosses to the A string on the rising sixth, so the defining interval is split between two strings with different colours. Adler describes the D string as "warm and rather subdued", and it "mellows even more in its higher positions" (PDF 75, Ex. III-6). For soft, lyrical high notes he recommends staying on the lower string rather than crossing to a brighter one (PDF 75, Ex. III-7, the reason c141 marked sul A at 57–59). He asks for equal, controlled tone in slow expressive lines (PDF 73–74).

Sul D puts E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5 in fifth position, a standard section position. The sixth becomes one slow shift at pp, under the rit. of 177–178. Adler lists same-string skips as a hazard only in fast passages (PDF 82–83), which this is not. The last motto then has the covered sound of the opening's cellos (Finding 1) and not the brighter A string. That suits the morendo and the ending's dynamic plan (pp, then ppp under a fermata), which c249 protects. I considered and rejected a solo violin here (PDF 81–82): the C5 crosses into movement II as a section note (mvt2.md bar 1), so a solo would need a "tutti" re-entry inside a tie.

### 3. Sub. p peak C6–D♭6 at 62–63 stays on the E string (no change)

The violins' soft peak at 62–63 (E♭5 up to C6, then D♭6, sub. p) could follow Adler's sul A advice (PDF 75). But C6–D♭6 on the A string needs seventh to eighth position, and Adler warns that control becomes "more and more tenuous" above seventh position, where the thumb loses its hold (PDF 72). c141 deliberately ended sul A at 59, and c149 left this peak unsupported by winds. Played softly on the E string the peak is "clear and luminous" (PDF 75). It should stay as it is.

### 4. The S head in the coda cellos, 171–174, stays on the A string (no change)

The mp espressivo S head in C minor (E♭4–C5, tenor clef) lies on the cello's A string, which Adler calls "brilliant and piercing" (PDF 99). Moving it to the warmer D string would need thumb position up to C5. Moving it down an octave would put it under the violas' C4/B3 and the cor anglais's motto, so it would no longer be the foreground voice. At mp in a Più lento farewell the A string gives the line the carrying power it needs over Bsn 2 and the violas. It should stay as it is.
