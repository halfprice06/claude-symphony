## Findings

### 1. Bars 77–78: the bass clarinet's solo statement of the head lies above its chalumeau and slurs down across the break (applied)

**Problem.** The development opens with the bass clarinet alone on the first-theme head: `es8( ces'4.) bes8 beses8 aes8 | aes2`, E♭3–C♭4, which is written F4–D♭5. The whole phrase lies above the chalumeau, which ends at written G♯4. The slurred descent C5–A4–B♭4 (written) crosses the break twice. The movement's most recognisable sound is the bass clarinet's dark chalumeau motto at bars 1–4 (G2–E♭3, written A3–F4). When this instrument returns as a soloist to open the development, it should bring that register back. At present it plays an octave higher, where it sounds like a weaker clarinet. The first note E♭3 also doubles the violas' tremolo E♭3. Cycle 127 moved the violas to E♭3 so that they would not double the solo's accented C♭4, but that was when the solo was on the bassoon, before cycle 129.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 217: the bass clarinet's chalumeau is "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective sound ... mysterious and shadowy and can be quite sinister, but as it goes up, it loses some of this quality." PDF 184: give each instrument the part that sounds best on it and that no other instrument could play. PDF 210: the break between written B♭4 and B4 needs care. PDF 217, Ex. VII-74 (Wagner, *Götterdämmerung*): clarinet and bass clarinet pass a line between them, and each stays in its own register. The book's two models for a dark C-minor-type opening, Tchaikovsky 5/i (Ex. VII-59, low clarinets) and 6/i (Ex. VII-93, low bassoon), both keep the solo in the instrument's bottom register over almost nothing.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).**
```
devBassCl = {
  r8 es,8\mf^\markup \bold "Solo"_\markup \italic "espr."( ces4.->) bes,8( beses,8 aes,8 | aes,2) r2 | ...
```
The solo now sounds E♭2–C♭3 (written F3–D♭4), entirely in the chalumeau, with no break crossing. It lies between the Cb A♭1 and the viola's E♭3 and stays clear of both. The answering voices stay as they are. Cl 1's answer at beat 3 (E♭5) is now three octaves above the solo instead of two, so the line moves from the darkest woodwind colour to the brightest: chalumeau, then clarino, then oboe at 79, then cellos and horns at 78–79. The pitch classes, rhythm, dynamics and texture are unchanged. Cycle 147 protected "solo winds over tremolo strings 77–81", and cycle 129 protected the choice of the bass clarinet; both still hold. This changes only the octave. Cycle 129 cited Rimsky-Korsakov Ex. 191, a dolce B.cl solo in the upper-middle register. Adler's chapter on the instrument itself ranks the chalumeau first, and in this movement the chalumeau also ties the passage back to bar 1.

### 2. Bar 34: the violas enter on the bassoon solo's held C4 at the same dynamic (applied)

**Problem.** At 33 the bassoon has the first-theme head alone (f, espr.) and diminuendos to mf at 34 for its tail `c'4 bes8 aes8 g4 f4`. On the off-beat of 34 the violas enter at mf with the head on C4, the same pitch the bassoon is sounding, and rise through the bassoon's register (C4–A♭4) while the bassoon falls below them. The bassoon is in its "sweet, more subdued" band and is at the same dynamic as a viola section, so its tail, which is still melody, disappears into the viola entry.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 226: the bassoon "is superb in all its registers, but when accompanied, it has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments"; when a bassoon solo is accompanied, "care must be taken to mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist." On the same chart the G3–E♭4 range is labelled "sweet, more subdued".

**Fix (applied, trans.ily).** `transVla`, bar 34: `r8 c'8\mp( aes'4.->) g'8( ges'8 f'8 |` (was `\mf`). The bassoon finishes its phrase over the stretto, and the violas are still clearly the next voice. Vln I and Vc enter at 35 in other registers and stay mf. The viola is a full section, so mp keeps it audible. The stretto design and the slurred runs into 33 are unchanged.

### 3. (No change) Passages checked against the reading that need nothing

- CA 171–174 lies at written D5–B♭5. At 300 dpi the chart puts the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band at written A4–C6, so cycle 146's do-not-touch stands.
- Cl 1 52–68 sits mostly on the throat tones. For a background counterline under the violins' tune, a "rather pale" colour (PDF 210) is the right choice.
- Cl 1 at 77 reaches written D♭6 at mp. Adler (PDF 210) gives the clarinet the full dynamic range up to its top, so this stays.
