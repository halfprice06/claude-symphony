## Findings

### 1. Cello recapitulation S phrase 146–155 is in tenor clef for a G2–F4 line. Applied.

**What is wrong.** At 146.4 the cellos change from bass to tenor clef for the pickup G2. They stay in tenor through 155 and return to bass at 156. In the exposition, 46–50, this phrase lies a sixth higher (E♭3–A♭4), and tenor clef suits it. In the recapitulation it lies an octave lower: G2 (146), C3 (148), up to F4 (152–153). In tenor clef the pickup G2 sits on the second ledger line *below* the staff, and C3 at 148 needs a ledger line below. A clef chosen for high notes is here carrying notes beneath its own staff. In bass clef the whole of 146–155 fits with at most two ledger lines above (E4/F4 at 151–153), and there is no clef change at all from 139 to 157.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21: cello "use[s] the tenor clef" when needed. Also on PDF 21: "For performance material, stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches, which a change of clef would obscure." The example on that page marks a phrase that changes clef to avoid a few ledger lines "but not". Here two clef changes (146, 156) are made to avoid two ledger lines, and the tenor clef adds ledger lines below. PDF 23: a non-standard clef should be kept for passages that need it.

**Protection answered.** HISTORY c115/c125 protect the "cello clef plan at 46/146/157", meaning the clef is *placed* before the pickup so the phrase reads in one clef (Gould PDF 22). Bass clef throughout serves the same purpose better: the phrase from the pickup to 155 is in one clef, and no clef is needed at 146 or 156. The clefs at 46 (exposition), 97 and 158 are unchanged.

**Fix (applied).**
- recOne.ily, recOneVc, bar 146: `r2. \clef tenor \once \override …` becomes `r2. \once \override …`
- recTwo.ily, recTwoVc: delete `\clef tenor` before `e2.~\<` (147), and `\clef bass` before `\recTwoArp` (156).
- `recTwoArp` keeps its own tenor clef at 158 (system start, warning clef at the end of 157) and bass at 162.

Checked on the rebuilt page 15: 147–155 read inside the bass staff, with the semiquaver run at 152 on two ledger lines. Stem directions follow (Gould PDF 27). The cello part changes the same way and stays at 2 pages.

### 2. Middle-line note against its own half-bar at Vln II 157.3 and Cl 1 165.3. Applied.

**What is wrong.** The c225 engraver turns a middle-line note up only when *all* its neighbours in the bar go up. When the neighbours disagree, LilyPond's default down-stem stays. Two bars have that case:
- Vln II at 157: C5 crotchet (down) | **B4 minim on beat 3 (down)** | then A4–G4 quavers (up) on beat 4.
- Cl 1 at 165: C5 minim (down) | **B4 crotchet on beat 3 (down)** | F♯4 crotchet (up) on beat 4.

In both, the middle-line note belongs to the second half-bar with the up-stemmed note, but it is stemmed with the first half-bar.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28: "When the stem direction varies within a bar, maintain the stem direction of the notes that are part of the same beat or half-bar." The 4/2 example on that page (image checked) shows C5 down, B4 down | A4 up, B4 up: each middle-line B goes with its own half-bar. Down is the default only "when there is no clear-cut case".

**Fix (applied).** global.ily, `gould-middle-line-engraver`: the engraver now also stores each stem's `measurePosition`. When the two neighbours disagree, the middle-line note takes the direction of the neighbour in its own half-bar (floor of 2 × position), if exactly one neighbour is there and that neighbour goes up. Otherwise the default down stays. A probe of the rebuilt score shows that exactly these two stems changed, Vln II 157.3 and Cl 1 165.3, and nothing else apart from the cello clef bars of finding 1. parts-gen.py takes the engraver from global.ily, so the parts get the same rule.

### 3. Isolated middle-line notes across barlines. Not changed; correct as they are.

The probe found 13 single middle-line notes with no other stemmed note in their bar, for example the contrabassoon and bass trombone D3 at the stop chord 138 (137 and 139 go up) and Cb D3 minims at 48, 52, 53, 103 and 149. Gould's examples on PDF 27–28 look at neighbours within the bar. A note alone in its bar after rests has no "clear-cut case", and PDF 28 gives down as the convention. Leave them.

### 4. Ledger-line limits elsewhere. Keep.

The probe lists four ledger lines on Vla A♭5 at 110 (alto kept by c115 so that 107–110 read in one clef) and on Hn 3·4 D3 at 141 (a single note, kept by c245). Bassoons, trombones and cellos never need more than three ledger lines above the bass staff. Both four-ledger-line cases are single peaks inside phrases. PDF 21 prefers ledger lines to clef changes there. Keep.

### 5. Stems and tails. Correct.

The probe found no stem on a note beyond one ledger line that stops short of the middle line (PDF 28). It found 209 stems shortened in two-voice staves, all at about 2½ spaces and none shorter (PDF 28–29), and no flagged note on a 2½-space stem (PDF 30). No change.
