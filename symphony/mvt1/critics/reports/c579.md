## Findings

### 1. Bruckner 9/i: source and comparison (no change)

Source. I could not reach a full score of Bruckner 9. The local corpus has no Bruckner (reference-corpus/README.md). archive.org has two items for No. 9, and both are piano duets: `imslp-no9-in-d-minor-wab-109-bruckner-anton` (Peters, file PMLP07928-Bruckner_Sinfonie_Nr.9_Singer_4hands.pdf, 64 pages) and `ixieneuntesympho00bruc` (Schalk and Löwe). The IMSLP full scores (Peters 1929, Orel 1932, Nowak, Cohrs; https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.9_in_D_minor,_WAB_109/143_(Bruckner,_Anton)) answer a direct request with the disclaimer page, and I did not get around it. I therefore used the Peters four-hand reduction, pages 155-156 (PDF 2-3) read at full size and pages 155-177 as thumbnails, for the notes, dynamics and pacing, together with general published descriptions of the movement. I make no claim that needs Bruckner's exact scoring.

What the reduction shows, with what our score does at the same points:
- Preparation and release. Bruckner opens ppp on a D pedal, with the horns' p theme in short entries and rests. He rises in terraced waves (mf cresc. to f at A; p at B with a chromatic violin line; cresc. sempre) to the ff unison "Tempo I, sehr breit" (V. Orch.), and right after it drops to pp tremolo. This is the Leonore and Hindemith principle (adler1 PDF 137, 139-140): add octaves, then a unison that prepares chords. Our movement does the same work in a shorter span. It moves from pp at 1-8 through f at 12 to the ff hammered tutti at 27, and the first ff block falls away at 33 to Bsn 1 alone over held strings. The plan is Brahmsian, not Brucknerian, and proportioned for a 7½-minute movement, not a 25-minute one.
- Block structure and silence. Bruckner separates his groups with full rests. Our 41-46 caesura, the silence after the 170 fermata (c460) and the 122-126 collapse do the same job.
- Final pages. Bruckner 9/i ends fff on a bare D. Ours ends ppp, attacca, which the cyclic plan requires: the finale's first strong C-major cadence is at its 222→223, and movement I has to stay unconfirmed. This is a planned difference, not a fault.

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? For the craft, yes. On every reading principle above, the score follows the reading. The one place where a Bruckner conductor would feel the scale is short is the recapitulation climb at 156-157 (Finding 2).

### 2. Bars 156-157: two-bar climb into 158 (considered, keep)

Bruckner's culminations come after long pedal waves, and Adler's Leonore example (PDF 137) adds a new octave in every system. At 156-157 the density stays at 14 players, and only the dynamics rise (p and mp crescendo to ff at 158). The point has been open since c523, and protected since c083, c143 and c243 for phrase length, with every orchestral addition at 157 rejected for a stated reason (c534 timpani roll; c511 Vln I 157.4; c502 viola arpeggio; c553 bass; c544 Finding 3: the trumpets' 15-bar rest that makes 158 new, which is Adler adler2 PDF 156). Bruckner's remedy is a longer build, which means adding bars. That would shift every bar number the other movements cite from 158 on (the finale quotes 175-182), and the 147-155 phrase cannot be shortened to make room (c083). The ch. 5 and 8 reading gives no answer to the stated reason that costs nothing at 158. I keep it.

### 3. Bar 69.3: I6 with the third on top (considered, keep)

A scan of every beat with 12 or more players found six first-inversion triads (69.3, 69.4, 113.1, 113.3, 139.3, 141.1). Only 69 has the bass C also on top, in 16 of 22 parts at 69.3. That C is the S tune held in Tpt 1, Ob 1, Fl 1·2 and Vln I, the bass moves A♭→C in passing (I–I6 in one bar, c103), and the horn and clarinet Cs belong to the P head counter. Adler's rule (PDF 152, 257) is about harmonic doubling of an inversion that stays put. Here the doubling is melodic, and it sits on a passing chord for half a bar. No change.

### 4. Motto colour against one-colour characterisation (considered, keep)

Adler PDF 245-246 (Debussy) advises one colour for a recurring idea. Our motto changes colour on purpose (B.cl + Vc, C.a., Fl 1, brass, trombones, Vln I), and the cor anglais frames it at 5 and 171. PDF 238 (Schubert: score each main element differently) and PDF 239 (change something the third time) support the plan. The plan is protected (c470). Keep it.

### 5. v569 against v579 (nothing worse)

The section files differ only in two "change bow at will" marks, now `\small` (c575, dev 117 and coda 179). The header stamp also differs. No pitch or rhythm changed, and vl.txt is identical. Nothing got worse.

### No change proposed

After four passes of this reading, every principle in it is met. The two gaps that a Bruckner comparison exposes (the climb at 156-157 and the lack of a long soft development episode, c459, c559, c569) are about proportion. Both are protected for reasons this reading does not answer. I did not find a change that is a real improvement without spending something the 158 arrival or the cyclic plan needs.
