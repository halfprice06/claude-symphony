## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Bars 175–179: Trombone 2 has no slurs in the chorale (applied)
**Problem.** Tbn 1 and B.tbn are slurred `g1( | es'1) | d'1( | c'2 des'2 | c'1)`, but Tbn 2 has `es1\pp | g1 | f1 | g2 f2 | es1` with no slurs. The full score hides this because the part-combiner prints the pair as chords under Tbn 1's slur. The Trombone 2 part, which is built from the single-player variable, shows separate notes, so the second player will tongue each note of the pp chorale while the players on either side play legato.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 and 321: notes under a slur are played on one breath, and separate notes are tongued. The cycle-88 rule says that parts in one statement carry the same slurs. Adler also says each player of a shared staff needs a separate part (archive copy above), and the notat.io thread says each voice keeps its own slurs. In Tchaikovsky 6/i, bars 336–339, both trombone voices carry the phrase marks.
**Protection.** Cycle 140 protects the chorale's "notes and slurs". This fix changes neither in the printed score; it only puts into the Tbn 2 part the slurs the score already shows.
**Fix.** In `coda.ily`, Tbn 2: `es1(\pp | g1) | f1( | g2 f2 | es1) |`.

### F2. Bar 156: Horn 2's D–D♭ is tongued while Violin II plays it slurred (applied)
**Problem.** Vln II plays `d''2.~(\< d''8 des''8) | c''2(`, and Hn 2 doubles the same motto tail an octave lower as `d'2.~\p\< d'8 des'8 | c'2.` with no slur. The tongued D♭ eighth after a held note stresses the chromatic step that the strings play as one bow.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 (slur = legato, separate notes tongued) and the cycle-88 rule on doublings.
**Fix.** In `recTwo.ily`, Hn 2: `d'2.~(\p\< d'8 des'8) | c'2. r4`.

### F3. Bars 103–106 and 164: soft horn inner lines are not marked legato (applied)
**Problem.** Under the second-theme head (Hn 1, Bsn 1, Vc, mf, slurred), Hn 3·4 move in a mp chorale, `d'1 | d'4 d'2 cis'4 | d'2 e'2 | fis'2 b2` and `a1 | f4 g2 e4 | d2 c2 | b,1`, with no slurs. Players will read this as ordinary tonguing. At 164, Hn 3's ♭6→5 sigh `aes2\p g2` is also unslurred.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 309–310 separates soft or legato tonguing from hard tonguing, and the examples always show the soft kind with slurs (Ex. X-24, X-9). In the Humperdinck four-horn chorale (Ex. X-16, PDF 306–307) every voice is slurred.
**Fix.** In `dev.ily`: Hn 3 `d'1\mp | d'4 d'2( cis'4 | d'2 e'2 | fis'2\> b2)`; Hn 4 `a1\mp | f4( g2 e4 | d2 c2 | b,1\>)`. In `recTwo.ily`: Hn 3 `aes2\p( g2)`. No notes or dynamics change, so the protected "notes and f/p contrast of 103–110" and the dip at 163–166 are untouched.

### F4. Bar 175: the trombone chorale has a dynamic but no word for its tone (applied)
**Problem.** The augmented low motto at 175 is the foreground: Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn at pp, doubled by B.cl, Vc and Cb. Unlike every other foreground solo in the movement (C.a. "espr." at 171, Vc "espr.", Hn "dolce" at 47 and 146), it has no expressive word. A brass entry at pp after 4 bars' rest gets a tongued attack unless it is told otherwise.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 306–307, Ex. X-16: Humperdinck marks his p horn chorale "sehr weich" in each part. Tchaikovsky 6/i, bars 336–339, marks the trombone chorale "cantabile" at p.
**Fix.** Add `_\markup \italic "dolce"` to Tbn 1 and B.tbn at 175, and to Tbn 2 as a part-only tag (`-\tag #'part _\markup \italic "dolce"`). Tbn 2 is now passed through `\removeWithTag #'part` in `score.ly`, so the shared staff prints "dolce" once. No dynamics, slurs or notes in the coda change.

### F5. Bar 162: Horn 1's ff hairpin ends in a rest without a level (note only, not applied)
**Problem.** Hn 1 has `e'1\ff\> | R1`, so in the Horn 1 part the diminuendo has no stated end. Hn 2 continues to p at 164 on the shared staff, but Hn 1's part does not show how far to fall.
**Evidence.** In Adler's examples, brass diminuendi end on a printed level (Ex. X-37 ">pp", X-40 "> p", X-13 "fff p"; PDF 299, 300, 305).
**Fix if wanted.** Part-only: `e'1\ff\> | R1-\tag #'part \!` with `-\tag #'part \mp` on beat 3 of 162 (or `\>` ending at mp). Not applied, because the exit order at 162–166 is protected (cycle 143) and the full score is correct.

Everything else in the brass checked against the reading is in order: trumpet soft dynamics stay in the middle register, the horn solos carry expressive words and phrase swells, no brass slur is longer than a breath, and no mutes or effects are used outside this idiom.
