## Findings

### 1. Comparison with Brahms, Violin Concerto/i: no change needed

I compared the two scores at the points where they do comparable work. In each case the v499 practice matches Brahms's, or differs for a reason the history records.

- **Opening (Brahms PDF 7, mm. 1–9).** Brahms states his main theme mp in a low unison of violas, cellos and bassoons, with the horns joining only from m. 3 and the basses later. The full harmony comes in by stages. That is the rule of adler2 PDF 205 (no thick downbeat) applied to a whole opening. Our 1–4 does the same with less: bass clarinet and cellos in unison, double basses an octave lower, timpani ppp, with the strings' chords only from 6. Brahms's answering oboe, p dolce over a moving violin figure (m. 8), corresponds to our cor anglais at 5–8 over held strings. The accompaniment is held, not moving, because our tempo is Lento at crotchet = 50. That is the right choice there.
- **First tutti (Brahms PDF 8, m. 17).** The theme is in octaves across winds and strings, and horns and trumpets sustain. This is adler2 PDF 209 (the most doubling on the melodic figure). Our 27–32 does the same, and it is protected as the finale's quotation.
- **Soft colour after the tutti (Brahms PDF 10, at A).** The whole orchestra cuts to fp, and a lyrical idea goes to winds and Hn 1 over p strings. Our 33 puts the bassoon solo over a Cb fp, with the violins dropped. That is Adler's "strong, but soft color" and "dropout of the strings" (PDF 209, 213). Our solo starts f espr. rather than p. I tested the idea against the music: the bassoon carries the transition's thematic line in its middle register, and it falls to mf within a bar. c457 and c692 protect the entry. I leave it.
- **Coda (Brahms PDF 60–66).** After the cadenza, Brahms writes tranquillo pp. The soloist sings the main theme high over pp held strings with small swells, Bsn 1 pp, and the horns in fifths as a pedal (PDF 61–62). Then comes "poco a poco cresc." and "string. poco a poco" (PDF 63), an animato with a p pulse in horns, trumpets and timpani (PDF 64–65), and short f chords with a fermata (PDF 66). Our coda follows the first half of this plan: the soft restatement at 171–174 (solo cello and cor anglais over Bsn 2 and the violas, pp) and the pp chorale at 175–179. Our timpani pulse at 179–180 is the same device as Brahms's p pulse, Adler's "rhythmatized" pedal (PDF 213). It then fades to ppp instead of building to f, because movement I ends attacca into movement II and the forte C-major close is reserved for the finale ("Do not reuse" list; c339, c379, c439). Brahms's concerto has no following movement to set up, so the difference is not a weakness here.
- **I considered and rejected two changes.** The first was a pp horn pedal under 171–174, after Brahms's PDF 61. It would anticipate the Hn 3·4 ppp entry at 181. That entry is the delayed-entry plan protected by c736 and c876, and the coda notes and dynamics 171–182 are protected repeatedly. The second was small swells on the held Vln II/viola chords at 175–179, after Brahms's PDF 61. c328 kept the pp chorale without swells, so that the trombones' motto stays the only moving voice, and that reason still holds. Neither change would make the score more like a master's. Each would only make it more like this particular master.
- **Where the distance remains.** Brahms's themes span longer arcs, and his harmony stays diatonic over long pedals. Our language is more chromatic, nearer Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff, who are among Dan's named models. Our proportions are protected (c215, c329, c493). Brahms uses no percussion beyond timpani, while we use a suspended cymbal at 159–163 and a tam-tam at 122 and 175. A conductor would accept these in a late-Romantic symphony, and Adler PDF 224 (special effect used once, recalled at the end) describes our tam-tam plan exactly.

## Cross-movement effects

None. Nothing changed. For the record only: the Brahms comparison confirms the reasons for the soft attacca ending and for the forte C-major close kept for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse").

## At a master's level; do not touch

These stand comparison with Brahms's own practice in Op. 77/i and should stay as they are:
- the bare low motto 1–4 with the staged entry of the harmony;
- the soft-colour drop at 33 after the hammered tutti;
- the horn and cello S over harp at 47–55;
- the coda's soft restatement, chorale and timpani pulse 171–182.
