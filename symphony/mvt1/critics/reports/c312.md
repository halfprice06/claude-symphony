## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 54→55, the second subject's cadence in A♭: the dominant seventh is doubled and the leading tone goes nowhere (applied)

**What is wrong.** Bar 54 is V7 of A♭ over the E♭ bass. On beat 3 Vln II moves E♭4→D♭4. On beat 4 the tune (Vc and Cl 1) also takes D♭4, so the chord seventh sounds twice in unison. The tune's copy falls to C4 at 55, but the Vln II copy rises to E♭4/A♭4. Meanwhile the leading tone G3 (Va, lower div.) leaps a fourth up to C4 at 55 instead of rising to A♭. As a result, the only real resolution of the V7 at the exposition's main cadence is in the tune itself. The two inner voices that fill out the chord contradict it.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "The chord 7th forms a dissonance, so it must never be doubled." On the same page, the leading tone has "its active tendency toward 1". PDF 80: the inner voices' "main function, however, is to complete the tones of the chord framed by the bass and soprano", and smooth connections "make likely a preponderance of conjunct motion". The points for review on PDF 89 repeat both rules. The recapitulation's matching cadence, 154→155, is already correct: Vln II B4→C5, the seventh F single in CA, Va and Hn 1, falling to E. Only the exposition is wrong.

**Fix** (tTwo.ily):
- Vln II, bar 54: `es'2 des'2` → `es'1`. E♭4 is held as a common tone into 55's `<es' aes'>`.
- Va, bar 55: `c'2.^"unis." r4` → `<aes c'>2. r4`. The div. continues from 54's `<g bes>`, so G3→A♭3 and B♭3→C4 move by step. The "unis." mark at the violas' next entry (60) already exists.

**Protection.** The 47–55 protections (theme, swells, harp, horn and cello scoring, and c067's "neutral accompaniment 47–55") are unchanged. Only two pp inner notes move. Both stay in the same instrument and register, and the accompaniment stays sustained and neutral. c114's metà balance (one undivided Vln II note against the violas' pair) is kept.

**Applied in the scratch copy:** yes.

### 2. Bar 151, the recapitulation's V6/5 of IV: the chord has no root (applied)

**What is wrong.** Bar 151 should be V6/5 of IV in C: E in the bass (Cb pizz. E2, Bsn 2 E3), then F at 152. It sounds only E–G–B♭: Vln II div. G5/B♭5, the tune G→E (Va, Vc and Hn 1), and CA B♭4 late in the bar. No C sounds anywhere in the bar (the harp rests). The leading tone E is the most heavily represented pitch, in four octaves (E2, E3, E4, E5). This is the exact counterpart of the exposition's bar 51, where c272 gave V6/5 of IV its root with Va A♭3. The recap never received the same correction, so the chord reads as a diminished triad on E rather than as C7 in first inversion.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects", only the 5th may be dropped, and "Inversions of seventh chords are usually complete, with exceptions occurring even less often than with inversions of triads." A&S PDF 76 also allows the root of a root-position seventh chord to be doubled and the 5th omitted. So the member that can go is G, never C.

**Fix** (recTwo.ily, Vln II): bar 151, `<g'' bes''>1:32` → `<c'' bes''>1:32`.
- The lower divisi moves F5→C5→F5 (152 `<f'' a''>`), which supplies the root.
- The upper line still resolves the seventh B♭5→A5.
- The 5th G remains on beat 1 in Va, Vc and Hn 1. After that the chord is E–C–B♭, which is complete except for the 5th.
- Spacing: C5–B♭5 is under an octave, and Va E5 sits between them from beat 2 (PDF 77).
- The bass E, the CA answer and Bsn 2 are untouched. This matters because a C in the bass would turn the chord into root-position C7 once the pizz. dies.

**Applied in the scratch copy:** yes.
