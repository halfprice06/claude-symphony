## Proportion and tension curve: no change

I count a bar as full tutti when at least 80% of the parts are active, and as thin when 35% or fewer are.

| Movement | Full tutti | Thin | Tutti plateaus of 2+ bars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beethoven 9/i | 48% | 5% | 23 |
| Dvořák 9/i | 28% | 28% | 13 |
| Tchaikovsky 6/i | 6% | 35% | 5 |
| This movement | 18% | 46% | 8 |

The thin share counts the slow introduction and coda. This movement sits inside the corpus range, so it is neither saturated with tutti nor short of it.

The plateaus are 26–32, 82–83, 115–116, 122–123, 131–134, 138–142, 158–161 and 167–170. Each one is separated from the next by a thinner passage, which is the foreground/background alternation Adler's ch. 17 describes (PDF 257, 262). The timings are exposition about 127 s, development 110 s, recapitulation 76 s and coda 60 s. I see no reason to reopen the proportions, which are protected since cycle 13 and confirmed at cycles 43 and 83.

## Findings

**1. Bars 72→73: the f cadence chord falls to the pp pedal with no overlap (applied).**
- **Bars:** 72–73.
- **Problem:** Every part plays the A♭ cadence chord f with accents to the barline of 72. At 73, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb, the same players, must start the pp pedal and cycle 172's common-tone diminished seventh on the downbeat. They change from an accented f down-bow to pp with no time between. The pp downbeat, which is the only bar where the CT°7 sounds, falls into the decay of the full f chord. This is the movement's one loud-to-soft join that has neither a rest (as at 83→84 and 170→171) nor a graded exit.
- **Evidence:**
  - Adler PDF 190 and Ex. XV-36: the effective way to get a sudden soft chord is for another group to enter at the soft dynamic "before that loud sound ends" and sustain, "since the chord is already there at that dynamic. It is essential that the two chords overlap."
  - Adler PDF 267: when a soft soloist follows a tutti, "the orchestra is reduced drastically", and a sustained tonic in the basses marks the arrival.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 76–79 (corpus MusicXML): trumpets and timpani stop at mid-76. Clarinets, bassoons, violas, cellos and basses go on through f, mf and mp to p, so the soft group is already sounding when the loud one stops.
- **Fix (applied, clos.ily):**
  - Vc 72: `aes4->\downbow es4->\downbow aes2\pp~ | aes1~ |` (beat 3 moves from A♭2 to A♭3 and is tied into the pedal; the duplicate `\pp` at 73 is removed).
  - Cb 72: `aes,,4->\downbow es,4->\downbow aes,,2\pp~ | aes,,1~ |`
  - Vln II 72: `aes'4->\downbow c''4->\downbow aes'4->\downbow r4 |`
  - Vla 72: `<c' es'>4->\downbow <c' es'>4->\downbow <c' es'>4->\downbow r4 |`
  - Winds, brass, timpani and Vln I are unchanged, so the cadence chord keeps its full f weight. Its low A♭ stays in Bsn 2, B.cl, Cbsn, B.tbn and tuba, and the A♭/C/E♭ that Vln II and Vla drop on beat 4 stay in Hn 3·4, Tpt 2, Ob 2, Bsn 1 and the trombones.
  - The pp pedal now sounds from beat 3 of 72. Vln II and Vla get one beat to lift and place the pp CT°7.
- **Protection:** This touches the protected codetta 69–72. The protection covers its theme combination, cadence route and tutti scoring, and none of those changes: there are no pitch changes apart from the Vc octave on the last half note, and no changes in winds or brass. The 73–76 pedal, the flute motto and the 76/77 join are untouched.

**2. Bars 107–108: the Vln II pp chord sits inside the viola tune's register (not applied; protected notes).**
- **Bars:** 107–108.
- **Problem:** The violas' second-theme head starts G4–E4–C5. Vln II div. holds E4/B3 and then E4–D♯4 over G3–A3. At 107 beat 4 and 108 beat 2 the viola tune passes through E4 while Vln II sustains E4.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible". He adds that rhythmic contrast lessens the problem, and here the accompaniment is sustained against a moving tune at pp against p. That contrast is why I rank this low and do not apply it.
- **Fix (optional):** Replace the two Vln II voices for 107–108 with `<g b>1\pp^"div." | <g b>4( <a dis'>2.)`, keeping the E-minor chord and then B7 but below the tune's E4. Bars 109–110 are unchanged. It needs a fresh full voice-leading check before anyone adopts it.
- **Protection:** Cycles 71, 74 and 84 protect the notes of 103–110. Their reasons concern the theme heads and the f/p contrast, not Vln II's held chord. I leave the decision to the composer.
