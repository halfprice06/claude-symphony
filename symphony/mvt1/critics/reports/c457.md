## Findings

The texture and counterpoint already meet the standard this chapter sets. The transition imitates. The fugato has four entries, each in a new colour pair, with a real countersubject and a bass that waits. Countermelodies have their own rhythm (56-68, 97-102, 131-134, 158-161). Pedals are given rhythm (127-130, 179-180). Each return of S has a new background. In this third pass I looked for places where the music falls short of a master within my lens, beyond whether it follows the rules. I tested five candidates against the reading. None is a real improvement that can be made without undoing a protected or quoted passage, so I recommend no change. The findings below record why each candidate stays as written, so later critics need not repeat the work.

### 1. Bars 135-137 (and 23-25): the accompaniment moves in crotchets under a crotchet tune. Keep as written.

The woodwinds and Vln I carry the tune in crotchets. Vln II and the violas repeat crotchets underneath (Vln II G4-G4-G4-F4 | F4-F4-E♭4-E♭4 | E♭4-F4-F4-F4). Ravel (PDF 217) separates a tune and an accompaniment that share note values by "highly contrasting colours", and here Vln I and Vln II are the same colour. But the two layers do not compete. The accents in Vln II and the violas fall on every third crotchet, so the inner parts carry the three-beat grouping against the bar while the tune keeps its own phrasing. The inner parts also sit a sixth to an octave and a half below the tune. The tune is doubled by Fl, Ob and Cl, so its colour is wind-led, not violin-led, which is the contrast Ravel asks for. Syncopating or thinning the inner crotchets would hide the grouping. The grouping and top line are protected (c391, c422, c438), and c393 added Hn 4's G3 here. No change.

### 2. Bars 97-102: semibreve bass under the ff S in B minor. Keep as written.

All five bass instruments (B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, Cb) hold B1-B1-E2-C♯2-F♯1-F♯1 in semibreves under a tune with quaver brass pickups and a viola quaver arpeggio. In Dvořák's ff tutti (XVI-10, PDF 210-211) the basses move in crotchets with the dance. That model fits a dance strain, not a cantabile theme. For a soft or broad tune Adler's rule is the other one: the most doubling on the melody and sustained "harmonic counterpoint" below it (PDF 209). The motion here is already in the middle (violas, brass counter), so a moving bass would add a fourth rhythmic layer under ff and blur the brass counter's pickups. c433 recorded the harmony (iv-ii7-V7-cad6/4), and 97-102 is protected (c147, c187, c454). No change.

### 3. Bars 39-40 and 68.3: the rising runs are doubled, not dovetailed. Keep as written.

Bizet's triplet run passes "from the bottom to the top" through several instruments (PDF 213), and on its repeat it dovetails through the woodwinds for a colour change. Here the run into 41 sounds in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I together, and the run at 68.3 in Vln I and the flutes (c137). Both runs cover about an octave inside one register. Neither crosses from low to high instruments, which is the situation Bizet's dovetail solves. A dovetail of Cl to Ob to Fl over one octave would weaken the push into the four-horn call at 41 and the codetta at 69 without gaining smoothness. No change.

### 4. Bar 33: the bassoon solo starts right after the ff tutti cut-off. Keep as written.

Bsn 1 enters f espr. on 33.1.5, one quaver after the whole orchestra's ff stroke. Stravinsky lightens the texture before the oboe and bassoon solos "so that ... their entrance is very fresh" (PDF 228). The cut at 33 does exactly that: everything stops except the Cb fp, which is the lightest possible texture. Moving the solo later would break the P-head rhythm the transition imitates (34 violas, 35 Vln I). The bassoon solo notes and slurs are protected (c147, c138). No change.

### 5. Bars 56-59 and 139-142: Vln I and Vln II in unison at pitch. Keep as written.

My scan finds only two long like-colour unisons between melodic parts, both in the violins. Schoenberg prefers octaves where like colours "would swallow each other up" (PDF 229). His case is different: he replaces two distinct colours (piano and cello) with one family, so the unison adds nothing. At 56-59 the at-pitch violins sul D/sul A are the theme's single warm colour over the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline and the cello arpeggio, and the violins split into octaves at 60, where the phrase climbs. That is a planned growth by octave, the procedure of Dvořák's repeat (PDF 209). At 139-142 c357 already showed that a lower octave for Vln II would land on the violas' neighbour quavers and the tenor counterline. Both are protected (c187: violins at 56; c357: 139-142). No change.

### What would still separate this from a master

Within texture and counterpoint, I found no place where the reading or the corpus points to a better solution than the one on the page. The places where a master might do more are the protected ones: the first S statement 47-55 with Hn 1 + Vc in unison (kept for movement II's colour, c439), and the hammered tuttis 27-32 / 131-134 / 167-170 (quoted by the finale). Changing those is a decision for the cyclic lens, not for the texture lens.
