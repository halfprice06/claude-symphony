# Critic 073: v92, lens 3 (form, pacing and proportion). The development after the 103–110 insertion

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY, c053, c070, c072, critique.md, global.ily and dev.ily, and pages 8–11 and 15 of the PDF. I reused c053's timeline scripts (copied to `/tmp/critic-073/s`, with the tempo modifiers and fermatas moved +8 bars). I built a per-player MIDI (`all.ly`) and computed four curves per bar in seconds: dynamic level (1 = ppp … 8 = fff), density (share of 31 players), register span, and dissonance index. I ran the same curves on Tchaikovsky 6/i, Dvořák 9/i and Beethoven 9/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`, via c053's JSON extracts). For Beethoven 9/i I use only proportions in bars, because the tempo parse of that file gives an implausible total time. Web sources:
- Brahms 1/i outline (P. Saelzer), https://fmmc.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Brahms-1.1-Pablos-Sketch.pdf: dominant pedal 273–289, then a drift to B minor, the "Beethovenian swell" from 293, climax 321, recapitulation 343, last bar 511.
- Farbood & Upham (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3874841: loudness drives tension almost instantly (about 2 s window), harmony over about 22 s.
- I found no published study placing Romantic first-movement climaxes at the golden section. The nearest is Lendvai-style work on other repertoire (https://www.mi.sanu.ac.rs/vismath/lend/b5.htm). I treat golden section as a reference point only.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 103–110: the lull is useful and matches Tchaikovsky 6/i almost exactly. (No change)
- **Problem examined:** whether the f-then-p passage breaks the momentum of the fugato and the B-minor block.
- **Evidence:**
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 214–244: ff with density 0.76 (214–218), then f at 0.41 (219–223), p (224–228), and pp at 0.15–0.25 for 12 s (229–236). Density then rises first (238–240, 0.46→0.82 at mp) and loudness follows, reaching fff at 244.
  - v92 97–122 has the same steps: ff at 0.64, f at 0.35 (103), p at 0.20 (107), then the rebuild to fff at 122.
  - The trough's share of the development is the same in both: Tchaikovsky 15.4 s of 222 s (7%), v92 7.6 s of 105 s (7%). Its position is also the same: 0.45–0.52 of the development in Tchaikovsky, 0.51–0.58 here.
  - Brahms 1/i (outline above) also places a soft drift after a pedal before the final swell to its development climax.
  - Beethoven 9/i puts its trough later (pp from 256 to 295 after the f fugato), with a short swell into the recapitulation at 301.
- **Verdict:** a two-wave development with a trough at about its midpoint is the norm in these models. The fugato and B-minor wave (85–102: dynamic 5→7, density 0.05→0.65) is complete before the insertion begins, so the lull ends a wave instead of interrupting one. It also adds dissonance where c053 asked for it: 103–109 measures 1.45–1.88, against a development mean of 1.10.

### 2. 110→111: the trough is left too abruptly, and 111 sounds like an arrival. (Applied)
- **Problem:**
  - At 110 the strings, Bsn and Cl1 go p< to **mf** at 111, where 12 more parts enter. Density jumps 0.29→0.65 and the level rises two steps within one bar (MIDI level 4→5).
  - The rest of the crescendo then rises only mf→ff over six bars. The steepest part of the build falls in the cadence bar.
  - This makes 111 a fourth "crescendo into a downbeat on V–i" seam (with 96→97, 130→131 and 170→171) and anticipates the recapitulation's arrival 20 bars early.
- **Evidence:** Tchaikovsky 6/i 237–243 adds its players at mp and only then gets loud (above). Farbood & Upham: loudness is the fastest-acting tension cue, so a one-bar p→mf with a doubled orchestra is heard as an arrival.
- **Fix (dynamics only, dev.ily). Every part is one level lower at its wedge entry, so the graded brass of cycle 68 is kept:**
  - 111, strings and woodwinds: `\mp\cresc` instead of `\mf\cresc`. Parts: Vln I `aes''2.~(\mp\cresc`, Vln II `<f' aes'>1:32\mp\cresc`, Vla `c'8\mp\cresc`, Vc `f1\mp\cresc`, Cb `f,1\mp\cresc`, Ob2, Cl1·2, B.cl, Bsn1·2, Cbsn.
  - 111, Hn 1–4, Tpt 1–3 and Tuba: `\p\cresc` instead of `\mp\cresc`.
  - Tbn 1·2 at 113 and B.tbn at 114: `\mp\cresc` instead of `\mf\cresc`.
  - The 110 hairpins, timpani mf at 115, Fl/Ob f at 115 and ff at 117 are unchanged.
- **Result:** MIDI levels for 110–115 go from 4,5,5,6,6,6 to 3,4,4,5,5,6. Density still rises at 111, but loudness now follows over six bars, and the trough's loudness extends to about 11 s.

### 3. 107–109: the trough stops at p. (Applied)
- **Problem:** the sustained Vln II and Vla are marked p, the same level as the espr. tune and the cello's P head. The trough is therefore one level shallower than the troughs in the models.
- **Evidence:** Tchaikovsky 6/i 229–236 is pp, and Dvořák 9/i 190–199 is p/pp (density 0.17–0.26).
- **Fix:** Vln II 107 `e'1\pp`; Vla 107 `b1\pp`. The tune (Vln I/Cl1), the cello/Bsn1 P head and the Cb pizz. stay p. The existing 110 hairpins now lead pp→mp.

### 4. Climax at 122: well placed. (No change)
- **Position in v92:**
  - 278 s of 439 (0.633) in the whole movement;
  - 0.655 of the fast music (15–174);
  - 0.82 of the development.
- **Golden section:** 0.618 of the total falls at bar 118, on the G pedal, 4 bars (7 s) before the climax.
- **Models:**
  - Dvořák 9/i: development peak 245 at 0.61 of the movement.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i: development peak 269 at 0.72 and global peak 313 at 0.81. v92's second peak, 165, is at 0.83, so the two-peak layout matches.
  - Brahms 1/i: climax 321 of 511 bars (0.63), and 0.86 of its development (189–342).
- The insertion moved the climax from about 0.62 to 0.633. That remains inside the models' range.

### 5. Proportions: development to exposition. (No change; do not grow further)
- **Development to exposition:** 105 s against 125 s, or 0.84 in time. Tchaikovsky 6/i is 0.54, Dvořák 9/i 0.61, and Beethoven 9/i about 0.99 in bars.
- **Development share of the movement:** 24%, the same as Tchaikovsky's 24% (Dvořák 18%).
- **Loud and quiet time in the development:** 28% loud, 9% quiet. Tchaikovsky is 39% / 6%, Dvořák 35% / 21%.
- **Static stretches:** no window of 20 s or more is static in both density and span anywhere in the Allegro.
- **Rule:** any further bars in the development would push it past the Romantic models toward Beethoven's ratio. Add none.

### 6. Seam 102→103: a terraced drop, not a repeated device. (No change)
- **The change at the barline:** ff → f, density 0.63→0.35, span 56→39, with semitone voice leading (c072).
- **Model:** Tchaikovsky 6/i 218→219 makes the same move: ff → f, density 0.76→0.41, span 46→39.
- **Other cuts in the movement are 4–5-level drops:** 72→73 (ff → pp pedal) and 83→84 (sub. p on a bare G). The one-level terrace at 103 is a different device.
- **Deliberate rhyme:** the major-third bass drop F♯→D repeats 77–80 (c072).

### 7. Seams 106→107 and 110→111: devices in the development's second half. (Covered by finding 2)
- **106→107:** diminuendo into a new p unit, with the voices inverted. This device occurs nowhere else in the movement.
- **Crescendo into a downbeat** is now used at 96→97, 110→111, 116→117, 121→122, 130→131 and 170→171. Within a build that is normal. After finding 2, 111 is a soft waypoint, so the build has one arrival at 117 (G, ff) instead of two, and 131 keeps its weight as the only V–i arrival at ff.

### 8. Unit lengths 97–116 are regular. (Report only)
- **The chain:** four units of 6+4+4+6 bars (8–12 s each). Each begins with the S head at a rising pitch level: B 97, D 103 (in the tenor), E 107, F 111.
- **Why it is acceptable:** in the models' developments a regular sequence of 4-bar units is common (Tchaikovsky 6/i 206–211), and the dynamic shape differs from unit to unit (ff, f, p, crescendo).
- **No fix:** the heads are protected (c071). If a later cycle wants less regularity, the place to do it is the scoring of 111 (density), not the bar lengths.
