## Master score

The master score I used is Mendelssohn, Symphony No. 3, Op. 56, in the Cranz full score (c. 1910). It is a Sibley Library scan, mirrored on archive.org as item imslp-no3-op56-mendelssohn-felix. Movement I runs from PDF 3 to 46. I looked at PDF 3, 4, 6, 7, 14, 15, 45 and 46 as images.

- **Opening (PDF 3).** The Andante begins with oboes, clarinets, bassoons, horns and divided violas at p, with no violins.
- **End of the introduction (PDF 6-7).** A ff string tremolo is followed by the wind choir.
- **PDF 14.** A lyrical idea in flute and clarinet in unison, marked "espress.", sits over pp off-beat strings.
- **End of the movement (PDF 45-46).** The storm coda breaks off ff and the winds thin out to one instrument. "Andante come I" then brings back the opening wind and viola colour at p, and pizzicato strings lead attacca into the Scherzo.

This score works in the same way. Its introduction is dark and low (B.cl and Vc sul G at 1-4). Its first big tutti is a sudden, single-layer event (27-32). Its coda goes back to the opening colour: cor anglais at 171 and the low B.cl/Vc/Cb motto at 175-178. Mendelssohn holds that colour back until the end (PDF 46), and so does this score. That is also Adler's point at PDF 261.

Read against the Mendelssohn, the movement holds up. A conductor would see:

- tuttis spaced like Mendelssohn's, with a wide gap at the bottom and close position above (adler1 PDF 151);
- background figures that change from section to section (PDF 158-159);
- wind colours kept back for the places where the form turns (PDF 238).

Nothing in the score marks it as student work. Mendelssohn does one thing this score does not do: he lets a single wind colour, the clarinet, run as a thread through the whole movement. Here the colour changes at every return. Adler, at PDF 238, prefers the second approach for "clarity of form", so I am not asking for a change.

## Findings

I checked every principle in the table bar by bar. None of them gives a reason to change a note. Below, ranked, are the points where the score departs from what the reading or the Mendelssohn would suggest. For each one I give the reason it should stay. None was applied, so the scratch copy is v279 unchanged.

**1. Bars 27-32, the A-flat 6 ceiling in the first tutti.**

- *What is wrong:* Adler PDF 238 rejects Schubert's alternative scorings because they "anticipate the expansion of registers". This score reaches its top note, A-flat 6, at 31, in the first tutti. It reaches the same note again at 72, 122 and 160. By that rule the climaxes at 122 and 160 should go higher than 31, or 31 should stay lower.
- *Evidence:* adler1 PDF 238. Mendelssohn does the opposite of Adler's rule here: on PDF 15 his first tutti already uses his highest flute range.
- *Verdict:* keep, and change nothing.
  - The finale quotes 27-32 at pitch, so a change would break a cross-movement link.
  - Cycles c194, c264 and c271 kept A-flat 6 on purpose.
  - 122 and 160 are already louder than 31 by scoring. They add the trombones and a held bass, so the climaxes grow without a higher note.

**2. Bars 167-170 repeat the layout of 27-32.**

- *What is wrong:* Adler PDF 255 says masters change something the third time the same scoring would recur.
- *Evidence:* adler1 PDF 255. 131-138 is re-scored, so 167 is only the second identical use, not the third.
- *Verdict:* keep. HISTORY.md protects the matching layouts as a deliberate return. The rule is about a third recurrence, and this is a second.

**3. Bars 103-106, oboe and clarinet in unison on the P head.**

- *What is wrong:* Textbooks warn against an Ob+Cl unison (adler1 PDF 235). Adler also says a unison of two like winds muddies the tone (PDF 249-250), and Cl 1-2 play a2 here.
- *Evidence:* Adler himself shows at PDF 235-236 that the Ob+Cl unison works in Schubert 8. Mendelssohn uses flute+clarinet in unison at PDF 14.
- *Verdict:* keep. The line is a forte contrapuntal entry against a horn countersubject, not a solo that needs rubato, so the PDF 249-250 warning does not apply. The passage is protected (c274).

**4. Bars 69-72, Fl 1-2 a2 on the codetta tune.**

- *What is wrong:* At first sight this is the "two like winds in unison" case of adler1 PDF 249-250.
- *Evidence:* Adler's exception covers tutti (PDF 250), and 69-72 is a forte tutti with Tpt 1 and Vln I on the same line.
- *Verdict:* keep. Without the second flute, the octave above Tpt 1 and Ob 1 is too weak against two horns and two clarinets on the counter-line (c131).

**5. Bar 103, Cb pizzicato marked f under B.cl mf.**

- *What is wrong:* The dynamics do not match across the bass line.
- *Evidence:* A pizzicato note decays at once. adler1 PDF 158-159 shows a plucked bass under held winds, with the pluck marked a level above.
- *Verdict:* keep. The f gives the pizzicato the same weight as the mf held bass clarinet.

**6. Bars 146-154, Hn 1 one level below the strings.**

- *What is wrong:* The dynamics within one chord are unequal.
- *Evidence:* adler1 PDF 267-268 (Tchaikovsky 6/i): keep the brass down so strings and winds come through.
- *Verdict:* keep. This is the reading's own technique, and it is protected (c278).
