## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. The findings are ranked by how much each would matter if it were wrong. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. The transition's III stopover is right for the exposition, and its absence in the recapitulation is also right. Keep.

**Exposition.** A&S say minor "tends to gravitate to III", which is "frequently expanded into a key area" that "functions as a stopover on the way to V" (PDF 232). They add that "intervening chords usually lead from I to III" (PDF 231). The transition does this:
- The bass runs C (33) – A♭ (34) – B♭ (35) – E♭ (36), which is i–VI–VII–III with natural VII as V of III (Ex. 15-1, PDF 230).
- E♭ is darkened to E♭ minor at 37 and approached again through F (38) and B♭7 (39–40).
- It is confirmed ff at 41 with the four-horn call.
- It is then reinterpreted as V of A♭ at 43–46.

E♭ is therefore heard twice, the second time as a goal, which is the "confirmed by a strong cadence" of Ex. 15-3.

**Corpus comparison.** Beethoven 9/i, which also puts its second group in VI, goes straight from D minor to B♭ through V/V in about eight bars and has no III at all (MCTL harmony.csv, onsets 140–152 = bars 71–77). This movement's 14-bar route through III is longer, and more typical of a minor-key movement in A&S's sense. It gives the A♭ arrival at 47 a prepared dominant (E♭) rather than a sudden one. The route is protected (c303, c371).

**Recapitulation.** I asked whether a master would bring back the III stopover or the horn-call caesura before the C-major second group. I do not think so. In A&S's account, III is a stopover between I and V when the music is leaving the tonic. In the recapitulation, S returns in I, so there is nothing to stop over on the way to. Bars 139–143 instead use the other route the unit names from the tonic area to V: iv – ♭II – iiø – V with a top line falling by step, A♭6–G–F–D–C–B (Ex. 15-2e/f, PDF 232). A III at 139–142 would suggest E♭ a second time just before C major and weaken the tonic return. Keep.

### 2. Root-position diminished triads: none, apart from an articulation gap at 16.4. Keep.

dim.py tested every half-beat from 1 to 182 for a sounding diminished triad over its root. The only hit is 16.4.0: D–F–A♭ over D3 for one quaver, while the low strings' G1 rests between quavers. The G sounds at 16.3 and again at 16.4.5. This is the case of Ex. 15-19 (PDF 244): "Because it grows out of the strong V at the beginning of the bar, the diminished chord would be heard as representing a V6/5". Since c393, 23–24 and 135–136 are V6/5 with Hn 4 G3, as Points for Review 10 asks (PDF 245). Nothing to change.

### 3. Rising-third root motion across barlines: one real case, 68→69, and it is strong-to-strong. Keep.

A&S: a rising-third root progression "tends to sound less like a progression of two chords than like an expansion" of the first chord, and composers "avoid [it] when moving from a weak to a strong beat" (PDF 231; restated on PDF 240). r3.py flagged seven barlines. Five are detection artefacts:
- 16→17 is V♭9 to iiø.
- 118→119 is a neighbour 6/4 to V over the pedal.
- 149→150 is ii to V9.
- 158→159 is the cadential 6/4 to V7.
- 96→97 is a chromatic upbeat into B minor.

59→60 is V7 of A♭ to the G♭ bass of the descending bass G♭–F♭–E♭, protected since c303.

The one real case is 68→69: F minor (vi, the deceptive cadence) to A♭ (I, the f codetta). The vi is struck on 68.1 and held through the bar, so the change falls from one strong beat to the next. It is not the weak-to-strong placement the rule targets. The quasi-repetition that A&S describe is also what the passage needs here: after the deceptive cadence the codetta restarts from the tonic it was denied, and does not cadence into it a second time. The codetta 69–72 is protected (c007, c034). Keep.

### 4. Cross-relations: no new soprano/bass split. Keep.

PDF 236: a cross-relation is "unduly harsh" between soprano and bass and causes "no problem" when one of the two is an inner voice, and there is none when the chromatic step stays in one voice. xrel.py lists:
- **79→80, 96→97, 102→103, 108→109:** c393 dealt with these, and nothing has changed in them.
- **114→116:** the bass itself steps D–D♭–C, so no split.
- **70→71:** bass D♮ (vii°7/V) and then soprano D♭6 (V7). Kept by c193 as the normal vii°7/V→V7 of the closing theme, with the bass protected (c103).
- **107.4 (new to the record):** the bass clarinet, lowest voice of a p three-part texture, runs F♯3–F3–E3 in quavers, and the violas' S head touches F♯4 one quaver after the F3. The bass clarinet itself had F♯ one quaver before, so this is a chromatic passing line against a returning F♯, not a chord-to-chord cross-relation of the kind in Ex. 15-8c. At p, in the S head's turn, it is not heard as harsh. 107–110 is protected (c094, c107, c142).

Keep all.

### 5. ♭VII used for drama: 96→97 and 156–159. Keep.

PDF 241: natural VII moving to V7 "can sound both unexpected and, in retrospect, logical. And the chromatic inflection of 7̂ can produce a particularly expressive effect." The movement uses this twice at large scale:
- **96→97:** the fugato's B♭ minor shifts up to B minor for the ff S statement, the bass stepping B♭1→B1.
- **156–159:** ♭VII–iv–6/4–V7, the shape of Ex. 15-15, with 4̂ (F6) in the soprano at 156 and again as the 7th of V7 at 159 (PDF 242).

The first is the development's second peak and the second leads to the movement's last fff. Both are protected (c233, c292, c323; c363 for the 157 bass). As c303 and c393 said, the stepwise bass of Ex. 15-15 (A♭ at 157) would anticipate the fff ♭VI at 160. Keep.

### 6. Where the form is smaller than a master's: the return of the tonic. No change.

The one measurable way this movement is smaller than its models is still the tonic return: recapitulation 0.61 of the exposition and coda 13.7 %, against 0.79 and 22 % in Beethoven 9/i (c493). Unit 15 does not support lengthening it. In A&S's large-scale view, III and VI belong to the departure and V to the return. The returning tonic needs only the cadential route the recapitulation already has (iv–♭II–ii–V, then the expanded cadence 156–166 through ♭VII and ♭VI). Bars 144–146 also show the point on PDF 234: after a PAC, V does not need extra length to work.

Three further reasons stand against adding material:
- What a longer return would need is reserved for the finale ("Do not reuse": C-major brass chorale, ff C-major arrival, the violins' C-major S).
- Bars 138, 170 and 175–182 are quoted by bar number elsewhere in the symphony.
- Proportions are protected (c013, c043, c243, c433, c493).

Keep.
