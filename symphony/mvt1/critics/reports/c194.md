## Findings

### 1. 139–142: Vln I's A♭6 comes before the 160 climax and is held longer there (APPLIED)

**Problem.** At 140–141, Fl 1 and Vln I reach A♭6 by a rising scale and hold it 3.5 beats at ff (`aes'''2->~ | aes'''4.`). At 160, the movement's last climax, the same pitch is held 2.5 beats (c191, c193). Vln I is the only string on the E-string A♭6 at 140, and that is the colour 160 depends on. The result is that 160 offers more doublings and a cymbal, but no register the ear has not just heard.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 257: orchestrating includes "planning how to use the instrumental forces to create a musical structure for the piece".
- Casanelles PDF 277–278: a flute added to violins on the same pitch is not heard as a separate note. At 140 the A♭6 is therefore heard as the violins' note.
- Casanelles PDF 287: the fewer instruments in a range, the more present they are. If Fl 1 is alone at A♭6, that octave is light at 140 and full at 160 (Fl 1·2 + Vln I·II).
- With Vln I down an octave, Fl 1's A♭6 sits on the second partial of a strong A♭5 unison (Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II). It adds brightness to that octave (PDF 259, instrumental synthesis) rather than standing as a peak in its own right.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 78 (printed 60) gives this layout as the norm for a soprano melody: "it is better to allow the wood-wind to progress in octaves, the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups". His formula is Fl. / Ob. (Cl.) + Vns, and Tsar Saltan [102] has 2 Fl. + Picc. / Vns I + II + Ob. Project Gutenberg text, same wording.
- Adler PDF 129: "too often the extremes in the range are wasted too early in a work, and the final buildup is, as a result, anticlimactic." PDF 133 makes the same point about Brahms 3/iii.
- Corpus: in Dvořák 9/i (2/4), Vln I touches its B6/C7 ceiling only in passing before the coda: 1/8 beat at 22, 1/4 at 57, 1/4 at 346, 1/2 at 412 and 428. Its only full-beat C7 is at 444 in the coda.

**History.** Cycles 24 and 61 ruled "156 [now 160] peak by weight" and "keep A♭6 to its current six bars". This fix keeps both: A♭6 still sounds at 140 (Fl 1), and nothing at 160 changes. The answer to the "weight" reason comes from the reading: weight added to the same register does not create a new register (Casanelles 277–278, 287; Adler 129). c167's protection of the lower-octave doubling at 139–142 is kept, since Vln II stays and Vln I joins it. c105's "no 8va at 139–141" is moot because no ottava is needed now.

**Balance check.** After the fix, the octaves at 140 are:
- A♭6: Fl 1, with Fl 2 on F6 at beats 3–4;
- A♭5: Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II;
- lower octave: the Bsn 1/Vc tenor line;
- below that: brass chord tones topped by Tpt 1/Hn 1 on A♭4.

The tune stays a full octave above the ff trumpets, so they do not mask it (Casanelles 287, 289). Two string sections with two woodwinds in unison keep the strings on top (Rimsky PDF 76: "the strings will predominate" when the powers are equal). A flute at A♭6 ff is in its strongest register and will be heard.

**Fix (c184's tested version), in `recOne.ily`, `recOneVlnOne` bars 139–142:**
```
r8 g'8\downbow\f\<( f''4.->) es''8( d''8 c''8 | des''8\ff es''8 f''8 g''8) aes''2->~ | aes''4. g''8 f''4 d''4 | c''2->\downbow b'2->\downbow |
```
Fl 1 is unchanged. Recommendation: accept, and record in HISTORY that the c24/c61 rule now means "A♭6 in the violins only at 160 in the recapitulation".

### 2. Bar-28 fix (Hn 3/Tbn 1 `c'1\f~ | c'4 b2.`) at 140 and 168: does not apply (no change)

**Problem checked.** At 28 the leading tone B3 sounded under the tune's C on the downbeat, so the resolution was anticipated. I checked the corresponding bars note by note in full.midi:

- **140** (bar-number parallel of 28) sets the Neapolitan rise D♭–E♭–F–G–A♭ over D♭ in the bass. The tune has no C→B, and Hn 3/Tbn 1 hold D♭4, a chord tone. The 28 situation does not arise.
- **132** (the material parallel of 28: the low theme `c'4 b8 c' aes4. g8`). Hn 3 already holds `c'2.` and moves to B only on beat 4, after the tune has left C. Tbn 1·2 play the tune itself, and no part has B on beat 1.
- **142** (the C→B in the tune): Hn 3, Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 play `c'2 b2` in time with the tune's `c'''2 b''2`. This is already the bar-28 solution.
- **168**: tonic pedal (plagal coda opening, protected). Hn 3 holds C4 for the whole bar and Tbn 1 holds E♭4. The only B is the tune's own neighbour note in Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and Vla. There is no dominant, so there is no leading tone to anticipate.

**Decision.** No change. This confirms c193's closure of the item. Making 140 or 168 "match" 28 would put a B where the harmony has none. Casanelles PDF 271–273 (Mahler) adds no reason to change it: the brass grading at these bars already gives the intended result.

### 3. 150: the cor anglais's answer lands in unison with Vln II's tremolo (APPLIED; optional)

**Problem.** The C.a. answer reaches D5 at 150 and holds it two beats. Vln II div. sul tasto is already holding `<d'' f''>`, so the C.a.'s goal note is doubled at the same pitch by a string section.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 277–278: an instrument added at a section's pitch adds only upper frequencies and is not heard as a note of its own. PDF 287: sounds in the same range mask each other. c190 left this open because "the rhythm separates them". That holds for the eighth-note pickups, but not for the two-beat goal note, which is the part that sustains on the identical pitch. Rimsky PDF 76 says the same: in a wind/string unison "the strings will predominate".

**Fix (c190's option),** in `recTwo.ily`, `recTwoVlnTwo`, bar 150: `<f'' a''>1:32` (was `<d'' f''>1:32`).
- A is held over from 149, and the line goes on to `<g'' bes''>` at 151 in parallel thirds.
- D is still present in the C.a.
- No B is added, so the Hn 1/Vla/Vc C→B suspension is not anticipated.
- 148, the protected C–C♯ line, is untouched.

This is a small change to a protected halo and can be dropped on its own if the composer prefers.

### 4. 82: Fl 2 on G4–E♭5 in unison with Tpt 1 at ff (checked, no change)

Fl 2 doubles Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II and Tpt 1 on the motto head (G4, then E♭5–D–C♯–C). This is the only flute note below A4 that sounds with loud brass anywhere in the movement. Casanelles PDF 275 (weak low flute) and PDF 287 (brass masking a flute in the same range) say Fl 2 adds almost nothing here. However, c144 and c164 kept the heavy middle octave on purpose, after Mozart's layout (Adler PDF 151). Moving Fl 2 would change only colour, not balance. I leave it alone. If the composer wants it anyway, the option is Fl 2 in unison with Fl 1 (`g''8 es'''4. …`), but I do not recommend reopening it.

### 5. 122: A♭6 held four beats at fff in the development (checked, no change)

Fl 1 and Vln I hold A♭6 for a whole bar at 122. That is longer than at 160. But 122 is the development's own climax (♭9 over the G pedal, protected chain 117–130), with a different harmony, a different section and 38 bars of distance. Its role is to prepare a return, while 160 is an arrival. Adler's warning (PDF 129) concerns the final build, and after Finding 1 the recapitulation reserves Vln I's A♭6 for 160. No change.
