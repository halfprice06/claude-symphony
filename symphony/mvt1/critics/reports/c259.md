## Findings

### 1. Bar 25, beat 4: low minor third D2–F2 under the crescendo into the stop chord (applied)

Since c242 (v243), the double basses and Bsn 2 move to D on beat 4 of 25, so the bar now goes iv – iiø7 – vii°7. Horn 4 still holds the F2 it took on beat 2 (`g,4 f,2.->`). On beat 4 the bottom of the chord is D2 (Cb), F2 (Hn 4), D3 (Bsn 2), F3 (Bsn 1, Vc). That is a minor third at the very bottom of the orchestra, played by a horn in its weakest register, during the crescendo to the ff stop chord.

- Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 124: writing in the lowest octaves sounds bad "where the proximity of harmonic intervals creates a bad effect".
- PDF 85: a chord should follow the harmonic series, wide at the bottom and close at the top.
- Before c242 the bass on this beat was A♭1, a sixth below the horn's F2. The third is a by-product of a later harmony fix, not something anyone chose.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-259/work/sections/tOne.ily (tOneHnFour):

```
  g2 g2 | c2.\mp\<-> g,4->~ | g,2 g,2->~ | g,4 f,2-> r4 |
```

Horn 4 drops the last beat, breathes, and attacks its F3 ff on the stop chord at 26. Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 136) recommends ff for a brass re-entry. The iiø7 stays complete: D (Cb, Bsn 2), F (Bsn 1, Vc), A♭ (Hn 3), C (Cl 2, Hn 2, Vla). The bar-26 stop chord is unchanged. On the do-not-touch "low horns 23–26" (c106, c156): the horns stay low, the hemiola accents at 23.1, 23.4, 24.3 and 25.2 are unchanged, and the only change is one unaccented beat that the protection did not foresee, because it predates c242. At 137 the bass on beat 4 is A♭1 and Hn 4 rests, so the recapitulation needs no change. The rendered page shows Hn 3·4 split cleanly for that beat.

### 3. Overall comparison with Rachmaninoff 2/i (no change)

A conductor who knows Op. 27 would recognise its outline here:

- The motto first sounds pp in the low strings (bass clarinet, cellos and basses, 1–4).
- The cor anglais has a solo role at the end of the slow music (5–8 here; in Rachmaninoff it leads into the Allegro).
- The first subject is the motto in diminution: the P head G–E♭–D–D♭–C at 15.
- The development works the motto and the heads.
- A long dominant pedal with the motto in the brass (117–130) prepares the recapitulation.
- The second subject returns in the tonic major (C major at 147, like Rachmaninoff's E major).

The scoring follows the same Russian school as the reading. Every row of the table except one is "follows": varied returns, stepwise crescendos by adding groups, percussion tied to brass chords, imitation that rises through the registers.

There are two real differences.

- **Scale.** Rachmaninoff's paragraphs run to thirty or forty bars, and the movement lasts about twenty minutes. Here P is 12 bars (15–26), the S paragraph is 22 bars (47–68), and the movement lasts about 7 minutes 11 seconds. This should stay. The movement opens a four-movement cycle that runs attacca into an Adagio, and other movements quote its bar numbers. c243 measured the proportions and rejected longer approaches with reasons that still hold. Brahms 3/i and Tchaikovsky 4/i are on the same scale.
- **The recapitulation's join.** Rachmaninoff brings back the first subject inside the climax. This score lets the climax fall away (123–126) and then builds a new stretto into 131. Both are standard. The collapse and the stretto are protected (c239, c243), and the measured speeding-up of the cellos' G (crotchets, quavers, triplets, semiquavers at 127–130) is the same device Rachmaninoff uses for his dominant pedal. It should stay.

The movement ends pp on C major and goes attacca into movement II, where Rachmaninoff ends with a fast coda. That difference is deliberate, recorded in the "Do not reuse" list, and should stay.

### 4. Bar 160, the passing G against held A♭ (no change)

At 160 beat 4.5, the tune's quaver G5 (Ob 2, Tpt 1, Vln II) and G4 (Hn 1·2, Vc) sounds against held A♭5 (Cl 1, Ob 1) and A♭4 (Hn 3·4, Tpt 2) at fff. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132 says such clashes matter less the shorter the note and the more the colours differ. Here the note is one quaver at largamente, and the countermelody resolves to G on the next beat. It should stay. Moving the counter would undo c241 and c244.
