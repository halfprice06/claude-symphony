## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 68–69: the codetta's f arrival in the first violins and cellos comes on an up-bow. Applied.

**Problem.** The Vln I run into the codetta (68.3, p with crescendo, protected slur c108) is the third of the score's three runs into a strong downbeat. At 33 and 41 the arrival is marked ⊓ so it falls on a down-bow. At 69 nothing is marked. The bows run:
- 66–67: one down-bow (4.3 s, f with diminuendo);
- 68.1: A-flat4 p, up;
- 68.3: the rising crescendo run, down, so the crescendo is drawn toward the tip;
- 69: C6 f, the start of the codetta tune with Trumpet 1, up-bow.

The cellos do the same. Their 68 arpeggio carries the crescendo on a down-bow, and their f half note at 69 (A-flat2, the bass of the new section) is up-bow. The double basses play the same A-flat at 69 down-bow, so the two bass sections bow the tutti arrival in opposite directions.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog... The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural... the composer should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit".
- PDF 38 and Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*, viewed): two strong attacks in a row get a double down-bow or a bow change on the long note "so that the following attack will be solid".
- In this score: 32.4 V run → 33 ⊓ and 40 run → 41 ⊓ (c108, c238).

**Fix (tTwo.ily, clos.ily).**
- Vln I 68: `aes'2\p aes'16(\upbow\< c'' des'' es'' f'' g'' aes'' bes'')`. The run continues the up-bow of the half note after a short stop, as the hooked figure at 16, so the crescendo is drawn toward the frog.
- Vln I 69: `c'''2.~(\downbow\f c'''8 bes''8 | aes''4)`.
- Vc 69: `aes2\f\downbow c2`. The cellos lift after the crescendo and take the f with the basses, as the strings do at 33.

No notes, slurs or dynamics change. Rehearsal letter D and the "Tempo dell'Allegro" sit above, and the marks print clear of them (page 7).

### 2. 19–22: the violas' "espr." counter-line crescendos to f on a down-bow and fades on an up-bow. Applied.

**Problem.** Under the first theme's climb to the D-flat6 peak at 20, the violas have the only sustained counter-line of the passage: `aes'2.~(\mf\< aes'8 g'8 | f'2\f aes'2)` then `aes'4\>( g'4 f'2~ | f'2\mf es'2)`. Each pair of bars is one 3.8 s bow. The syncopated entries of 15–18 end with an up-bow (b8~b4 at 18.3.5), so:
- 19–20 (mf to f): down-bow, so the crescendo is drawn toward the tip;
- 21–22 (diminuendo to mf): up-bow, so it is drawn toward the frog.

Both are against the habit. At mf to f with the bow already 3.8 s long, the f at 20 arrives near the tip, where the arm has least weight.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: the crescendo goes naturally with the up-bow and the diminuendo with the down-bow; mark the bowing where the habit must be countered. PDF 39: "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow". A 3.8 s crescendo needs the frog at its end.

**Fix (tOne.ily).** `aes'2.~\mf\<(\upbow^\markup \italic "espr." aes'8 g'8 | f'2\f aes'2)`. The players take the short syncopation before it (18.3.5) down-bow. Then 19–20 are up-bow and 21–22 down-bow, by alternation. This is one mark, and nothing else changes. Page 3: the V prints between "espr." and the note.

### 3. 171–174: the coda cello solo bows the second-theme head the other way round from every other statement. Applied.

**Problem.** The solo cello plays the S head (E-flat4 held, C-A-flat-G, F-E-flat-D-flat-F-A-flat, C5-B4) after the ff fermata, starting on the downbeat after a rest. It is down-bow by habit, and the one-bar slurs give:
- 171 (swell): down;
- 172: up;
- 173 (crescendo into A-flat4, the phrase's highest pressure over the Neapolitan): down, toward the tip;
- 174 (C5 with diminuendo, the peak and its fall): up.

In the exposition and recapitulation (c338 restored them), the up-bow pickup puts the crescendo on the held note on an up-bow and the diminuendo from the peak at 50 and 150 on a down-bow. In the coda the solo arrives on its peak with the bow at the tip and fades toward the frog.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: the anacrusis habit; the crescendo/diminuendo tendencies; mark direction to counter the habit. PDF 37–38 (Ex. II-19): the upbeat figure sets the bowing of the bars that follow. Here there is no upbeat, so the bowing must be marked.

**Fix (coda.ily).** `es'2.~(\upbow\mp\< ... es'8 d'8)\>`. Then 171 is up (the swell starts at the point, as at 47 and 147), 172 down, 173 up into A-flat4, and 174 down from C5 with the diminuendo, as at 50 and 150. The tutti cellos enter at 175 after rests, so the bowing of 175–182 is unchanged for them. This is one mark. The notes, levels and timing of the protected coda are untouched. Page 18: the V prints under "Solo espr.".
