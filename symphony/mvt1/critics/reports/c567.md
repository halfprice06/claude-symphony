## Findings

**No change applied.** The four bars that changed since c467 (violas 157, 158, 159.1; Vln II 177) keep the chapter's distribution rules. None of them is a wind–brass layout. The scan found no soft chord where bassoons enclose horns, and no clarinet line strictly inside soft horns other than the unisons PDF 106 allows. Within this lens I looked for places where the texture falls short of a master's work and tested three candidates against the reading and the protections. None meets the bar.

### 1. Bars 37, 67, 164.1: the chord's seventh is in a horn in soft horn–bassoon harmony (kept, as c467)

At 37 (p) the parts are Bsn 2 E♭3, Hn 4 G♭3, Bsn 1 B♭3 and Hn 3 D♭4. The seventh, D♭4, is in Hn 3, which takes it down to C4 at 38. PDF 108 asks for the discords in the bassoons. Moving the D♭ to Bsn 1 would put Bsn 1 above both horns and leave the horns enclosed by the bassoons, which the next diagram on the same page advises against. PDF 112 puts correct part-writing ahead of distribution. Bar 37 is also a recorded seed of the finale's E♭-minor theme, with these four pitches in these instruments. At 67 the bassoon doubles the bass; it is not part of a quartet, and 56–68 is protected. At 164.1 the pitches of 156–166 are protected (c346; the bassoon alternative was tested and rejected by c367). Keep.

### 2. Bars 69–72 and 167–169: horns lie between Bsn 2 and Bsn 1 at forte (kept)

This pass's scan flags these beats under the PDF 108 rule that horns should not be enclosed by bassoons. The rule is given for soft four-part writing ("especially in soft passages"). Here the music is f–ff. Hn 1·2 play the P head in unison (with the violas at 167–169), Hn 3·4 hold the middle, Bsn 2 doubles the bass, and Bsn 1 holds upper chord tones above the horns (C4–E♭4–F4 at 69–70 and 167–169), doubled in the clarinets and strings. The horns are not a two-part pad framed by bassoon colour, which is what PDF 108 warns against. At this level the bassoons add weight but no separate colour (PDF 108: "in forte passages the horns overwhelm the bassoons"). Ex. 157 (*Antar*, PDF 330) places bassoons on both sides of the horns in the same way on its crescendo, and adds the trumpets at the arrival. Both passages are protected (69–72 harmony and dynamics; 167–169, c254 and later). Keep.

### 3. Bars 145–146: the soft wind V7 with all parts at pp (candidate rejected)

The chord is Hn 4 G3, Hn 3 and Bsn 1 B3, Cl 2 D4, Cl 1 F4, all pp: four horns completed by clarinets above, with the bassoon in the chord (PDF 108, third diagram). PDF 112 suggests marking the woodwind one degree louder when the balance is hard. Cl 1's sounding F4 is its written G4, the open throat note, which is weaker than the horns' G3 and B3. I considered marking Cl 1·2 p. I rejected it: at 145.1–146.2 Vln I and Vln II hold G4 and F4 at pp, so the seventh is already doubled in the strings, and the balance is not the hard case PDF 112 describes. The 143–150 lead-in is also protected (c239). Keep.

### 4. Bars 124–126: a moving woodwind voice out of the held chord, as in Ex. 155 (candidate rejected)

Ex. 155 (*Servilia* [73], PDF 329), viewed for the first time this pass, holds a woodwind-and-horn chord f dim. to p. Then a single bassoon moves pp inside the held chord while the strings play pizzicato. The score's collapse at 124–126 is a similar moment: a held wind chord over the G pedal with fading dynamics. It already has the moving line, in Ob 1 with Vln I (G5–F5–E♭5–D5–C5–D5), and the inner parts move by step at 126 (Hn 3, Cl 2 and violas C4→B3). That is the PDF 108 division, with stationary brass and moving woodwind. A further bassoon line would cover Cl 1's stretto entry on G3 at 127, which starts in the same register. Exit order 123–127 and collapse length 122–126 are protected. Keep.

### 5. Bars 175–178 and 181–182: strings among soft brass (kept, as c267, c367, c467)

PDF 113 says strings and brass are seldom crossed or enclosed. In the chorale, the violas (C4) and Vln II (G4) sit at and above Tbn 1 G3 / Tbn 2 E♭3. At 181–182, Hn 3·4 sit between the cellos and the violas. Both passages fill held chords at ppp–pp (PDF 114: balance matters most in long sustained chords), and both are protected (chorale 175–179; the last chord and the five tied parts into movement II). Keep.

## What this pass adds

- Ex. 154 (*Sadko* [338], PDF 329): a mf woodwind chord laid over four horns, with the horns carrying a chromatic inner line. Rimsky lets the brass move when the woodwind is the held "roof". At 64–67 the score does the reverse: Hn 3·4 hold while Cl 1 and Hn 2 carry the counterline in unison. This is the PDF 108 order, so the score does not need the Ex. 154 exception.
- Ex. 146 (*Kitesh* [10], PDF 319): the cor anglais and clarinets play legato while four horns play non legato, under quintuplet figuration in bass clarinet and strings. The movement's nearest texture is 47–55 (harp figuration under Hn 1 and the cellos). It is protected and has no woodwind under S (c147 and later).
- Ex. 167 (*Christmas Night* [205], PDF 336): pp muted brass and woodwind pad with one florid violin line. This is a muted-brass colour, which movement I reserves.
- Ex. 173 (*Sadko*, PDF 343): short sf tutti chords of all three groups, then ff sustained brass under woodwind and string scales. This is the layout of 26–27 and 138–139 (stroke, then hammered tutti). It follows PDF 113 C and PDF 114 (resonance matters less in short chords).
- Ex. 166 (*Snegourotchka* [198], PDF 335): a brass chorale doubled by full woodwind at ff. Movement I keeps its chorale pp and in minor (175–179). The major f–ff brass chorale is on the finale's reserve list, so this example confirms the reserve rather than suggesting a change.
