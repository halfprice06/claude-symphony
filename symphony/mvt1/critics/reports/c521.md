## Findings

This pass applies no change. I re-ran the reading's checks on v521 rather than relying on c431, because ten cycles of changes (c502, c512, c514, c518, c519 among them) have landed since. All of them still hold. The two findings below answer the task's further question, where the melodies fall short of a master's within this lens, and say why I do not change them.

### Finding 1. The checks on v521: the changes since v431 keep the reading's rules. No change.

- Note II (PDF 62). The new viola double stops are thirds: C4/E4 at 158 (c514) and the thirds at 166. A third in one divided section is what PDF 63 asks for ("the same quality of tone in both parts"). No octave double stop exists in the violas, cellos or basses.
- Note I (PDF 62) and PDF 58–59. Every sustained violin note at A5 or above has its octave below at the onset, apart from four places that are not cantabile melody: the accented E♭6 at 81.4 (one beat of the head dialogue, doubled by Fl 1; the octave layout arrives with the ff at 82), the fugato entry at 94–96 (kept by c431 F3), the stop chord F6 at 169–170 and Vln II's sul tasto halo at 148–153 (background). The four A♭6 peaks (31, 72, 122, 160) all have their octave.
- PDF 56. The basses never carry a phrase alone. Every bass note without cello or bassoon support is a pedal, a held tone or a pizzicato bass.
- c512's Vln II A♭4–F4 at 177 sits under the Vln I pickup G4 at 177.4 in the normal register order (PDF 63–64), so the last motto is still on top and alone, as in Ex. 2.

### Finding 2. Melody and memorability: the themes against the masters and the new examples. No change.

I compared each theme with the examples rendered on this pass and through my lens, looking for a place where a phrase could sing more or a climax could land harder.

- **Motto (1–4, 5–8, 73–76, 117–121, 177–182).** Ex. 21 (PDF 197) gives a "sinister" pp phrase to cellos and basses at the bottom with contrabassoon. The motto at 1–4 already uses this colour family (B.cl + Vc sul G, Cb an octave below, timpani ppp), and it changes colour at every return, as Tchaikovsky's motto does in his Fifth. It is the movement's most memorable idea. Making it louder or doubling it more at 1–4 would spend the effect the finale reserves (no ff unison tutti of the motto). Keep it.
- **P (15–22).** P is the least singable theme because its rhythm is the busiest. Its head is the motto, though, so the listener already knows it, and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part. A change here would affect IV 199–204. Nothing in the reading argues for it: PDF 54–55 asks only for relief by position and dynamics, and 15–22 has both. Keep it.
- **Horn call (13–14).** E♭3–C4–B3, three notes with the B unresolved. Movements II and III answer it at pitch. Keep it.
- **S (46.4–68 and its returns).** This is where I looked hardest. Ex. 25 (PDF 201) shows a long-note cantabile in violins in octaves, dolce, pp, over harp arpeggios. That is the same kind of tune as S (long notes on strong beats, one sixth per phrase) and the same kind of setting as 56–63. Ex. 5 (PDF 179) shows that a viola cantabile can stand alone at p, which supports the undoubled viola line at 151–155 even before the cello octave below is counted. The S chain 47 (Hn + Vc) → 56 (violins) → 97 (ff, B minor) → 147 (Vla + Hn, C major) → 158 (double octaves, fff) gives every statement a different layout from the chapter. Each return is a new colour and the last is the fullest. That is the plan of Dvořák 8/i and Tchaikovsky 5/i for their second themes. I considered two possible improvements and rejected both on the evidence, without building them:
  1. Doubling the exposition cello line with Vln II or violas at 52–53, where Hn 1 leaves and the cello climbs alone to F5. Cl 1 already joins the cello on F5 at 52.4 (PDF 55: doubled by wind). PDF 56 also values the cello's high A string for "tense passionate cantabile". A string doubling would cover that colour and take away from the violins' entry at 55.4. Not an improvement.
  2. Adding Fl 1 an octave above the violas at 151–155, on the model of Ex. 25 (flute over the tune). It would bring the flute into the tune before 158, where both flutes arrive with Vln I as the climax colour (c431's protected chain), and the fff A♭6 at 160 would lose some of its novelty. The halo already gives 147–155 a high register. Not an improvement.
- **Climax 158–161.** This is the double-octave layout of PDF 62 (Ex. 32) with the three-octave wind support of PDF 63. The rising sixths G5–E6 and C6–A♭6 are the motto's leap in major. It lands as hard as the reserves allow: C major is never confirmed at forte, and the finale keeps the trumpets' B→C. Keep it.

Every other move I considered would either undo a protected setting (c431, c451, c471, c491 do-not-touch lists) or spend a resource that the finale reserves. "No change" is the supported result.
