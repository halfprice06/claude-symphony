# Critic 352: Harmony and voice leading (v352)

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 22 Leading-Tone Seventh Chords and Unit 23 Mixture (aldwell-schachter PDF 373–397), second pass (the first pass was c262). I read every text page in the range and looked at every page as an image at 110 dpi (PDF 373–397; PDF 379 is scanned upside down and was rotated). As c262 noted, the scan skips printed pp. 393 and 399, so Ex. 23-2h, 23-3, 23-10 and 23-11 are cut off. Their point is stated in the prose on PDF 388 and in the Points for Review on PDF 395.

Method. I used scratch scripts in /tmp/critic-352/scripts on v352's full.midi. They list every diminished and half-diminished seventh at quaver resolution (about 70 places). For each one I traced every voice into the next chord. I searched every pair of parts for a d5 moving to a P5 in similar motion (Ex. 22-3c), listed outer-voice chromatic successions split between voices (Ex. 23-12a), and parsed the .ily files for written melodic A2, d4, A4 and d7 intervals (§11). I also followed every ♭6 in the major-key passages (47–76 in A♭; 147–166 in C) to see whether it reaches 5 before any ♮6 sounds.

## Findings

On a second pass the score follows Units 22–23 closely. Every diminished seventh is approached and resolved as the reading teaches, or it embellishes V7 over a pedal. Every ♭6 in the major-key passages reaches 5 before any ♮6. c262's fix at 177 (viola A♭3–G3, so 176–178 is i6 – V4/3 – i) is still in place. I found nothing that needs a change. Below are the four departures from the letter of the reading that are left, ranked by audibility, with the reason each should stay. None is applied.

### 1. Bar 31.1: the vii°7/iv → iv resolution has an upper-voice d5 → P5 in similar motion, and Fl 2 moves against the bass (keep)

At 30 the chord is E–G–B♭–D♭ over the bass E, vii°7 of F minor. Moving to 31 (F minor), Hn 2 goes G4→F4 while Tpt 1 goes D♭5→C5. G–D♭ is a d5, and the two parts fall in similar motion to the P5 F–C. This is the voice leading that Ex. 22-3c marks "avoid" (PDF 375 §4: "obtrusive set of hidden 5ths"). In the same beat Fl 2 rises B♭5→C6 against the bass E2→F2, so a d5 with the bass ends on a P5 instead of the inward third the box on PDF 381 asks for.

Keep both. The reading's own remedy is Ex. 22-3a: send 2̂ up to 3̂, so Hn 2 would go G4→A♭4 in unison with Hn 1. But Hn 2 holds the only F4 at 31.1. Without it the brass and woodwind chord above F3 would be A♭ and C only, the root left to B.tbn, tuba and the low strings. The ♭6 itself resolves correctly in every leading part (Tpt 1 D♭5–C5, Hn 3, Tbn 1, Cl 2 D♭4–C4). The B♭ falls to A♭ in Hn 4, Bsn 1, Vc and Tpt 2, so Fl 2's C6 is a doubling that keeps the top of the chord full. Bars 27–32 are quoted at pitch in every part by the finale at its 199–204 (protected: c224, c302, c329). A change would cost a cross-movement correction for a hidden fifth between a horn and a trumpet inside the ff tutti.

### 2. Bar 28: the diminished seventh over D is a °6/5. Its 7th is held into iv4/2 at 29 (keep; correct the records)

From 28.2 the chord is B–D–F–A♭ over the bass D. With D, the chord's third, in the bass this is VII°6/5 (Points for Review 1, PDF 383), not the "vii°4/3" named in c302's history line and in mvt1.md's v303 entry ("vii°4/3, as at 132"). Bar 132.3–4 is a different chord: the same pitches over the G pedal, an embellished V (Ex. 22-9, PDF 378 §9).

The bass at 28→29 rises D→E♭, the normal path of a °6/5 to I6 (Ex. 22-4a, PDF 376), and Hn 3 and Tbn 1 take B3→C4. But bar 29 is not i6. It is F–A♭–C over E♭ (iv4/2), with the 7th A♭ held as a common tone (Hn 1, Ob 2, Tpt 2 to A♭4, and the strings' line F6–D6) instead of falling to G (PDF 375 §3). No G sounds in bar 29.

Keep it. The held A♭ is what lets the chromatic bass D–E♭–E–F of 28–31 carry one chord of F-minor colour after another (iv4/2, vii°7/iv, iv). That chain is protected since c012 ("chromatic bass 27–32") and quoted by the finale. A falling A♭→G would put i6 on the downbeat of 29 and break the rise to the A♭6 peak at 31.

Records: in the coherence file mvt1.md (v303 line), "vii°4/3, as at 132" should read "vii°6/5 over D; at 132 the same pitches embellish V over G". I have not written to /mnt/project-files. The composer should pass this to whoever keeps mvt1.md.

### 3. Bars 114→115: B→A♭ augmented second in five inner parts leaving vii°6/5 for the D♭ triad (keep)

At 114.3 the wedge has vii°6/5 over D (B–D–F–A♭). At 115 a ff root-position D♭ triad follows over the chromatic bass D–D♭. Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II move B4→A♭4, and Tbn 1 moves B3→A♭3: a descending melodic A2. §11 (PDF 380) accepts a melodic A2 when it arpeggiates the °7, or when it comes from II, IV or VI into the °7. This one leaves the °7 for a chord outside it, so the reading does not cover it.

Keep it, as c252 did, and for a reason the reading supplies. The leading tone has nowhere to go in the D♭ chord: C is not in it, and D♭ would be a d3. The other upper voices keep their common tones (F, A♭) or move by semitone (D→D♭). So the A2 is the only path that does not double a tone at the unison or open a gap in the chord. It lies under the top line F5–A♭5–D♭6 and inside the bass rising F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C, protected since c319, where it is heard as part of the chromatic slide, not as a line. Respelling B as C♭ would make the leap a minor third on paper. But it would misname the leading tone of C at 114, where the B arrives from C5 as the leading tone. So the spelling should stay as it is.

### 4. Bars 163–165: A–A♭–G, then a passing A♮ at 165.2 (follows; keep)

The final C-major cadence has ♮6 at 163 (IV), ♭6 at 164 (iiø7 with A♭ in Hn 3, Cl 1 and the harp), and 5 at 164.3 (Hn 3 A♭3→G3, Cl 1 A♭4→G4, harp A♭5→G5 at 165.1). This is the "good" order ♮6–♭6–5 of PDF 395 Point 3. At 165.2 the violins' descent C6–B5–A5–G5–F5–D5 over the G pedal passes through A♮. That does not break the rule on PDF 388: the ♭6 has already reached 5, and the descent from B to G cannot use A♭ without a melodic A2 (§11). Keep. This is the passage c262 named as at a master's level, and the second pass confirms it.
