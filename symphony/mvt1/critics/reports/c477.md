### 1. Bars 143–146: the approach to the recapitulated S — keep

What is there (from full.midi): Vln I states the P head at 143 (f); Bsn 1 answers it a bar later at 144 (G3–E♭4–D4–C4 to B3), the same Bsn → Vla → Vln I chain as 33–35 compressed to two entries. Then a V7 of C is held pp at 145–146: Cb G1, Vc D3, Vla G3 + Hn 4 G3, Bsn 1 B3 + Hn 3 B3, Cl 2 D4, Cl 1 F4 + Vln II F4, Vln I G4, and Ob 1 enters p dolce on G4 (beat 3) and rises to E5–D5.

Against the reading:
- Economy (PDF 12). Three of the nine parts are unisons (Vla/Hn 4, Bsn 1/Hn 3, Vln II/Cl 1). I tested whether any can go. Bsn 1's B3 is the end of its own imitative entry (D–C–B); cutting it leaves the answer unfinished. Hn 3's B3 resolves to C4 at 147 (c153). Cl 1–2 make the V7 complete in the winds (c224). The string unisons give the chord its bowed core under a rit. None is a "moving line in the lower-middle register" of the Ex. 1 kind: all are held.
- Focus (PDF 13–14). Ob 1 starts from Vln I's held G4 and leaves it upward. This is Ex. 2A's handoff: the new colour emerges from a held note and takes the lead without a gap.

Not applied.

### 2. Bars 163–166: the dissolution after the climax — keep

What is there (full.midi and PDF page 17): Vln I and Vln II carry the falling line in octaves (163 A5–G5–F5–A5–C6, 164 D6–C6 held, 165 B5–A5–G5–F5–D5, 166 C5), mp to p. Under it three parts move: the cellos arpeggiate in eighths on beats 1–2.5 of every bar (F2–C3–F3–A3, D2–A♭2–D3–F3, G2–D3–F3–B3, C2–G2–C3–E3), the violas attack on beat 2 and move in eighths on 3.5–4.5 (163 C4…A3–C4–F4, 164 F4…E4–D4–G3, 165 G3…B3–D4–B3), and Cl 1 has a counter fragment (164 F4–A♭4–G4–D5, 165 B4–F4). Hn 1–4, bassoons, double basses and the harp hold or strike the chords. This is the busiest soft passage in the second half.

Against the reading:
- Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) asks whether an inner line hides the outer voices' motion. None of the three moving parts is a line in Ex. 1's sense: the cellos and violas are arpeggiated chord tones, not a chromatic voice like Sebesky's F♯–G–G♯–G♮, and the outer voices (violin line against the bass F–D/G–G–C) stay audible because they lie two to three octaves apart with nothing moving between D5 and F6 except the tune.
- Ex. 2A (PDF 17–18) and focus (PDF 13). The interlock is strict only at 164, where the tune holds D6–C6 and the cellos, violas and clarinet move in turn. At 163 and 165 the cellos' eighths coincide with the tune's moving notes on beats 1–2.5, and at 163.4–4.5 the violas' eighths coincide with the tune's A5–C6. These coincidences are in a different register (C3–F4 under A5–C6) and in a different figure (arpeggio against scale). The combined effect is a continuous eighth-note motion handed from cellos (first half-bar) to violas (second half-bar), which is the dovetailing Adler describes in the Brahms example V-7: a simple, repetitious background "dovetailed to create extreme smoothness without any pauses in the texture" (adler1 PDF 135).
- I tested cutting the viola eighths and the cello arpeggios in turn (on paper; see the dependencies). The violas carry the protected E♮→E♭ thread into 166 (c391, c430), and their eighths lead into it. Without the cellos, 163–165 lose the bass motion F–D–G that carries the harmony toward 167, since the double basses and bassoons only hold.

Not applied.

### 3. Bars 15–22: the accompaniment to P — keep

Vln II and violas play off-beat tied notes (eighth-note syncopations) in two parts; the cellos play a half-note tenor line (C3–D3–E♭3–D3–F3–A♭3–G3–G2) that moves against the double basses' repeated-eighth pulse on C2–C2–G1–A♭1–G1. Bsn 1 and Hn 3–4 join at 19.

The question was whether the syncopated inner parts are too static for a master's first theme (a master's accompaniment often gives one inner part a line of its own). The reading argues against adding one: the P tune moves in eighths and sixteenths, and Sebesky's focus rule (PDF 13) and the Ex. 2A practice (fills only in held notes) leave no room for a further moving line between E♭4 and G4 under a tune that rarely holds. The independent line is already there, in the right place: the cellos' half notes against the tune's eighths, in contrary motion at the half-bar of 15 (tune D5→C5, cellos C3→D3) and 17 (tune E♭5→C5, cellos F3→A♭3), and the double basses' pulse is the rhythmic layer. This is Adler's rule for background figures: rhythmically unlike the melody and in a less prominent register (adler1 PDF 135, on the Brahms cello theme of Ex. V-7). Not applied.

### 4. Bars 171–174: theme combination in the coda — already at the standard

The solo cello (A string, mp) has the S head E♭4–D4–C4–A♭4–G4–F4…; from 171.4 the cor anglais has the motto G4–E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5 above it; violas and Bsn 2 hold i–iv–N6–V. The two lines move alternately: the C.a. holds E♭5 while the cello moves at 172.2–4, the cello holds F4 while the C.a. moves at 173.2–3, and at 174 the cello's C5–B4 sits under the C.a.'s held C5. This is the interlock Sebesky shows in Ex. 2A (PDF 17–18) and the "few melodic ideas" principle (PDF 11): the coda's counterpoint is made only from material already heard. Nothing to change.
