Web sources:

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Recap 158–161: no level after the fff, and the harp entry is buried (applied)
**Problem.** After fff at 156, the parts that continue get no new level at 158:
- Fl1, Ob1, Cl1, Vln I/II, Vla, Vc, Cb, bassoons, horns, Tpt2/3, trombones and tuba.

Only Tpt1 has ff. So the strings and woodwinds read fff through 158 and then diminuendo to mf at 162 without any level in between. That gives three bars of fff, although "only one fff, at 156" was the cycle-3 decision. The harp enters at 158 at mf, two levels below the fff tune, in the same register (a''–f''').

**Evidence.** The exposition has the same shape. At 62–68 the violins are f and the harp mf (one level lower), then a hairpin at 66 leads to p at 68. Tchaikovsky 6/i re-marks every part after its fff peaks, for example "dim. un poco" in 15 parts at bar 216. Rimsky-Korsakov says the harp "is never a very powerful instrument".

**Fix.** At 158, ff on every sounding part, with the existing hairpins kept. For example:
- Vln I `a''2.~(\ff a''8 gis''8)`
- Vla `r4 e'4~\ff\> …`
- Vc `a,8(\ff\> e a c' e'2)`
- Tpt2 `e'2\ff\>~ e'2\mp`

Harp 158 goes from mf to `\f`. The 162 levels and the E♮→E♭ crescendo are unchanged.

### 2. Harp 148–153 too soft for the violins, and an open hairpin at 152 (applied)
**Problem.** At 148 the harp is p under violins at mf ("ord.") and a mp counterline. In the exposition, harp and violins share the level (p at 56). The hairpin at 152 runs into the rest at 154 and has no target.

**Fix.** Harp L.H. 148 `<c, c>2\mf_\pedNat`. At 152 the hairpin stays, and at 153 the bass becomes `<f,, f,>2\f`. Remove `\!` from `R1*4`.

### 3. Harp 160: the R.H. chord rings into the 4–3 suspension (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 47 kept beat 3 of 160 free so that the tune's c'''–b'' suspension is not doubled. But the R.H. chord a'' d''' f''' from beat 1 keeps sounding over the G bass on beat 3, and d''' then sits a second above the suspended c'''. Harp notes ring unless the score marks damping (Hugill). Players damp at a rest only by habit (Harp Column).

**Fix.** On the R.H. rest at 160, add `r2^\markup \small \italic "étouffez"`. No l.v. is needed elsewhere: the other chords change harmony at the bar line, and the harpist will damp there anyway.

### 4. Harp 56–68: dynamics printed twice, and no crescendo or diminuendo with the orchestra (applied)
**Problem.**
- The harp gets two copies of the same mark, one on each staff: at 56 and 62 (both hands), and at 68 and 162 (once on beat 1, once on beat 3). Harp dynamics belong once, between the staves (Dorico, MuseScore).
- At 58–61 the whole orchestra crescendos to f at 62, but the harp stays p and then jumps to mf.
- At 66–67 the orchestra diminuendos, but the harp stays at mf until 68.

**Fix.**
- In score.ly, add to the Harp LH staff `\override DynamicText.direction = #UP \override DynamicLineSpanner.direction = #UP`.
- Remove the R.H. dynamics at 47, 56, 62, 68, 148, 152, 158 and 162, and give 47's `\p` to the L.H.
- L.H. 59 `<es, es>2\< r2`, which runs to the mf at 62. L.H. 66 `<bes,, bes,>2\> <es, es>2`, which runs to the p at 68.

### 5. Shared staves: player 1's dynamic prints for player 2 (applied)
The probe found these places where a shared staff prints a dynamic or hairpin that belongs to one player only:
- **Bassoons 152:** Bsn1 is `\mf\<` and Bsn2 has only `\<` from mp. Fix: Bsn2 `bes,1\mf\<`.
- **Bassoons 162:** Bsn1 is `\mp`. Bsn2 has no level, and its last mark is the fff. Fix: Bsn2 `c2.\mp r4`.

- **Clarinets 11:** Cl2 has no first dynamic. Fix: `d'1\p\<`.
- **Horns 3·4, 130:** Hn4 is mf, Hn3 has no mark. Fix: Hn3 `d'2.->\mf`.

### 6. Vocabulary: one word, "espr.", used for every lyrical entry (partly applied)
**Count.** The movement has 12 "espr.", 3 "dolce", 1 "morendo" and nothing else.

**Corpus.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i uses many character words, each placed at a structural point:
  - "teneramente, molto cantabile, con espansione" (strings, 89, the second theme)
  - "con dolcezza" (305, its reprise)
  - "dolcissimo, ma espressivo" (329)
  - "marcato" (134–139)
  - "pesante" (130, 267)
  - "feroce" (174–175)
  - "sempre" 24 times, "dim." 30 times
- Dvořák 9/i is the opposite: almost no words ("legato" 3, "leggiero" 1). Its expression is in fz (378) and staccato (1316).

This score sits between the two in density. Its fault is uniformity: at 47 the horn and the cello double one tune, but one is marked "dolce" and the other "espr.".

**Applied.**
- Vc 47 "dolce", to match Hn1.
- Vln I/II 55 "molto cantabile" in place of "espr." (the violins' first statement). The counterline (Cl1/Hn2) keeps "espr.", so the tune and the counterline are told apart.

**Suggested, not applied.** "con dolcezza" or "dolce" for Vla/Vc at 138, where they now carry "mf espr." This would follow Tchaikovsky's change of word at the reprise.

### 7. Articulation palette: no staccato and no sf/fz in 178 bars (not applied)
**Count.** 632 accents and 18 tenutos, but no staccato, sf, sfz or fz, and no text "dim.".

**Corpus.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i has 2295 staccatos and 91 sf. In the Allegro, bars 19–70, almost every accompaniment figure is dotted. Bar 70 has "détaché", and 163–168 have sf on successive notes.
- Dvořák 9/i has 1316 staccatos and 378 fz. At 28–30 and 36–38 the woodwind answer carries three dots and an accent per bar; at 34 the oboes have fz.

In this score, short notes are left to the player. Concrete candidates:
- Cb 15–22: the eighth of `c,4 r8 c,8`, written `c,8-.`.
- Tpt/Timp 27, 28, 30 and 163: `c''4-> r8 c''8-.`.
- Violas 69–71: the repeated eighths marked "marcato". The double basses 27–31 likewise.
- Fugato quarters 87, 89 …: `g4-- a4-- bes4-- b4--`, so every entry is played the same way.
- "sf" instead of `->` on the syncopated peak at 20 (`des'''2\sf~`). This is a matter of taste.

I did not apply these. They are many small edits and the composer should choose among them.
