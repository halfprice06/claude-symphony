Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III "Harmony", brass and combined groups; overlaying, crossing, enclosure (rimsky-principles PDF 100–114). This is the fifth critic on this range (c234, c324, c414, c434 before me). I read every text page from 100 to 114 in full. I rendered at 110 dpi and looked at every page with notation: PDF 100, 101, 102, 103, 104, 106, 108, 109, 110, 111. PDF 105, 107, 112, 113 and 114 contain text and lists of examples only.

Method. I read c434's report in full, and every HISTORY.md entry from cycle 371 on, so that I would not repeat earlier points. First I compared the full.midi note lists of v434 and v524. Since c434 the only pitch changes are in the strings: violas at 157–159 (c502, c514, c522) and Vln II at 177 (c512). Every wind, brass, timpani and harp note is the same as in v434. So c434's eight scans of the brass and wind layouts are still valid, and I did not repeat them. Instead I did three things:

1. I checked the four changed string bars against the strings-with-brass rules (PDF 113).
2. I re-read the three A♭ climaxes (82, 122, 160) and the brass-heavy passages (12, 26–27, 41, 69–72, 97–102, 111–117, 131–143, 156–170, 175–182) against the diagrams, beat by beat, from a fresh dump of full.midi and from score pages 8, 12, 13, 15, 16 and 17 at 100 dpi.
3. The task also asks where the music falls short of a master's, so I looked for places where a layout the reading praises could make a passage more vivid, and I built and tested the strongest candidate.

## Findings

No score change. The brass and wind layouts are identical to v434 note for note, and c434 found no breach of PDF 100–114 in them. The four string changes since then (157–159 violas, 177 Vln II) follow PDF 113: at 158–159 the violas' repeated E4/F4 quavers sit with Hn 1·2's held E4–F4 (horns doubled by violas), and at 177 the Vln II line sits above a pp trombone chorale whose layout is a double-choir chorale (PDF 103). I looked for places where the music falls short of a master's within this lens. I found two candidates that the reading could support. I tested one and rejected both, for the reasons below. A forced change next to passages the finale quotes would add risk without a clear gain.

### Tested and kept: bar 82, doubled third in the held brass

At the first A♭ climax (82, ff head over held brass f), the held brass are: tuba A♭1 and B.tbn A♭2 (bass in octaves), Tbn 2 A♭3 with Hn 1 A♭4, Tbn 1 C4 with Hn 3·4 a2 on C4, and Tpt 3 E♭4 with Hn 2 E♭4. Counting brass weight (one trumpet = one trombone = two horns, PDF 51), the third C has as much weight as the fifth E♭ or more. That is a heavy third in a major chord. I tested the alternative: Hn 4 E♭4 at 82 (`es'1\f` for `c'1\f` in devHnFour, bar 82), which goes E♭4→B3 into the stroke at 83. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 18 pages, only the known bar-96 line in analysis.txt. In the vl.txt diff, three doublings of Hn 4's C4→B3 (with Bsn 1, Vla, Vln I, Ob 2) are replaced by one doubling with Tpt 3 E♭4→B3. I reject it. The current layout is the diagram on PDF 101 note for note: Tbn 3 and tuba on the bass in octaves, and the upper trombone parts "reinforced by a trumpet or two horns in unison". Tbn 1 + Hn 3·4 a2 is the "two horns in unison" form, and Tpt 3 on E♭ doubles the upper part. Splitting Hn 3·4 breaks that pair and leaves single horns beside the trombones, which PDF 102–103 says should then be marked a degree louder, and that cannot be done under a ff head without upsetting the climax ladder (82 f brass < 122 fff < 160). c132 chose C4 to avoid the augmented second A♭3–B3. Keep. The test copy is /tmp/critic-524/t82 and is not part of the recommendation.

### Considered and kept: bar 157, Cl 1 alone above four horns in the crescendo

At 156 the soft four-horn chord with Cl 1 above and the bassoons and basses below is PDF 108's diagram exactly. PDF 108–109 adds that in forte the horns outweigh the woodwind, so the upper parts should be doubled. In 157 the horns crescendo from p towards the ff at 158 while Cl 1's counter-line (G5–A♭5–F5) is still single. Ob 1 could join Cl 1 on beat 1 of 157. I did not apply it. The crescendo lasts one bar at poco animando, about two seconds. PDF 114 says balance matters most in long sustained chords, and this is a moving line inside a short crescendo. Cl 1's A♭5 is in its clear upper register, and the violins carry the tune above it. The fresh entry of Ob 1 with Cl 1·2 on the counter at 158 (c337, c407) is part of what makes the ff arrival land, and an early oboe would weaken it. c523 records the two-bar approach at 156–157 as protected.

### Record notes (no change)

- Bar 131: the low P head in Tbn 1·2 + Bsn 1·2 + Cl 1·2 + Vc is a mixed unison of the kind PDF 106 calls "very rare" for three trombones with three bassoons or clarinets. Here there are two of each, the woodwind are marked ff against the trombones' f (PDF 112's dynamic grading), and the cellos carry the line. It is a deliberate dark colour for the reprise and is protected (c431, c491). Keep.
- Bar 167: Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 in unison on E♭4 (PDF 104, "not so common"). PDF 101's diagram shows a trumpet reinforcing one trombone part, so this is within the reading. Bars 167–170 are protected because the finale quotes them. Keep.
- Bar 72: Hn 1–3 on A♭3 at the codetta's I is where Hn 1·2's P head ends, and Hn 3 joins the same note. It is melodic, not a filler doubling. Keep (c364).
