## Findings

### 1. Beam angles: steep arpeggio beams and sloped concave groups (applied)

**Bars:** Vc 56–61, 64–68, 157, 162–166; Hp LH 62–63, Hp RH 65; Vla 60–61, 69–71, 92, 97–102, 117–121, 156–157, 163–165; Cl 2 95, 117–121; Vln II 95; Vln I 20, 68; Tbn and Bn 98–102; Fl 29, 139, 140. 94 of the 500 beams changed.

**What is wrong.** LilyPond's beam angles break three of Gould's rules in this score.

(a) The four-note cello arpeggios of the S accompaniment (56–68) and its recapitulation (157–166) slope 2.0–2.65 stave-spaces. Gould PDF 33: beams "usually ... cross no more than one stave-line. Thus the wider the interval, the more flattened the beam angle". PDF 34: "the beam of shorter groups should not cross more than one stave-space", with an "and not" example at two spaces. In the same system on page 6 the harp RH, which c125 already flattened with `Beam.damping = #3` under the same rule, has flat beams, while the cellos below it and the harp LH at 62–63 are steep. The page therefore uses two beam styles for the same figure.

(b) The viola figure at 97–102 (A4–E4–C♯4–E4 and similar, twelve groups at ff) has its inner note closest to the down-beam, and the pattern repeats. Gould PDF 36 makes the beam horizontal in both cases: "(b) there is a repeated pattern of pitches" and "(c) an inner note is closer to the beam than either of the outer notes". PDF 37 adds that a group with both shapes is also horizontal. LilyPond sloped these groups by 0.35–0.82 spaces, so the beams alternately tilt across a passage that should read as an even ostinato. The same happens at Vla 69–71, 163, 165.

(c) Flute groups that lie wholly above the first ledger line slope 0.68 (29, 139) and 1.0 (140). Gould PDF 35: "When all the notes of a beam fall outside the first ledger line, the beam takes only a slight slope ... all wider intervals take a slope of ½ stave-space."

**Fix (applied).** I did not add bar-by-bar `\override Beam.positions` calls (about 90 of them). Instead, one callback in global.ily, `gould-beam-positions`, wraps LilyPond's own quanted positions (`beam::place-broken-parts-individually`) and changes them only in these three cases:
- concave group (an inner note nearer the beam than both outer notes) → horizontal at the end farther from the noteheads (PDF 36 (c));
- slope capped at 1 space for groups of up to four notes and at 2 for longer groups (PDF 33–34);
- slope capped at ½ space when every note lies beyond the first ledger line (PDF 35).

The beam moves only away from the noteheads, so stems lengthen and never fall below Gould's minimum (PDF 35). The far end keeps LilyPond's sit/hang/straddle quant, and a 1-space cap keeps the other end on a line too (PDF 32, 34). Kneed beams and beams broken across a system are left alone. The callback is switched on next to the c225 middle-line engraver:

```
\context { \Voice \remove "Dynamic_engraver" \remove "Dynamic_align_engraver" \consists #gould-middle-line-engraver \override Beam.positions = #gould-beam-positions }
```

in score.ly (line 49) and parts-gen.py (line 146), so the score and the parts match. After the fix, the probe found no group above the slope limits and no sloped concave group. All 67 remaining flags concern direction only, and each is a forced stem direction on a shared two-voice staff or an equidistant group.

This does not undo c125. The harp damping at 47–63 stays and gives the same result. c125's "do not touch: beaming and metric grouping" protects grouping, which is unchanged; only angles change. No pitch, rhythm or grouping changed.

### 2. Two clarinet voices interlock at 117–121 (not applied; keep)
Cl 2's triplet arpeggio (down-stems) rises into Cl 1's off-beat eighths (up-stems), and at 117 beat 3 the beams cross Cl 1's noteheads. Gould PDF 41 deals only with ledger lines in overlapping parts, and they are drawn correctly here. Splitting the clarinets onto two staves would add a stave to a full page 11. The overlap lasts five bars, Cl 1 is labelled "1." and the stem directions are clear, so I recommend keeping it as it is.

### 3. Stem direction of Vc 61, first group (not applied; keep)
F♭2–D♭3–F♭3–A♭3 has two notes above the middle line and one below, so the majority rule (PDF 38) gives down-stems. LilyPond sets it up. The next group is up, and the passage alternates by register so that slurs stay at the noteheads, which PDF 39 ("context may determine the direction"; "visual considerations") allows. Keep.
