What the images show beyond the text (this pass):
- Ex. XV-27 (PDF 178-179): the "background" is not only held notes. The violas and bassoons play an off-beat figure (rest, then quaver-tied-to-crotchet) under the oboes' and Hn 3-4's held chords, and only the double basses hold plain dotted minims. A background can carry its own rhythm, provided it differs from the foreground and middleground rhythms.
- Ex. XV-24, mm. 418-424 (PDF 175): when Mozart's combination of elements ends, the closing bars turn into a homophonic block. The upper wind and violins hold or repeat chord notes, the inner strings repeat crotchets and quavers, and the bass moves in crotchets. Contrapuntal combination gives way to rhythmic unison at the cadence.
- Ex. XV-26 (PDF 177): each element has its own printed character word as well as a dynamic: "sehr markiert", "aber sehr ausdrucksvoll", "scherzando" with "stacc.".

## Findings

I found nothing in v537 that the assigned reading shows to be wrong, and no texture change that would clearly improve the music and is not already settled by an earlier cycle. I recommend no change. Below are the places I examined most closely on this pass, with the reason for keeping each. Items 1-3 are places not discussed in c437.

### Checked and kept

1. **139-142, the recapitulation tutti in F minor (PDF 177-179).** This is the movement's best case of Adler's three-element model, and it is easy to miss because it is loud.
   - The tune is in Fl 1-2, Ob 1-2, Cl 1-2 and Vln I-II, at the unison and octave (PDF 184).
   - Under it, a slow counter-line rises and falls in minims and crotchets (C5-B♭4, A♭3-F3, F3-G3-A♭3-C4, C4-B3) in Vc + Bsn 1 + Tbn 1. That is one string, one woodwind and one brass, exactly the rule on PDF 178. Its rhythm (long notes) is unlike the tune's dotted quaver turns.
   - The violas play repeated quavers as the middleground, and the double basses play quavers as the "heavy bass notes" that steady the rhythm (PDF 181).
   Nothing to add.

2. **97-102, S in B minor, ff (PDF 179, 181).** The tune is in six instruments over three octaves. Below it the violas play broken-chord quavers (middleground). Bsn 1, Hn 1-2 and Tbn 1 answer in the tune's held notes with a rising quaver figure, f under the ff tune (c128). That is the Holst device on PDF 181: a subsidiary figure repeated bar by bar in another group. The counter has a woodwind and brass but no string. Giving it a string would mean taking the violas off the middleground or the cellos off the tune (c231). Keep it.

3. **23-25 and 135-137, the hemiola build into the stop chords (PDF 175, image).** Every part moves in crotchets with an accent every three beats, and the inner strings repeat notes. I asked whether a master would give the inner parts more line here. The *Jupiter* close at mm. 418-424 (PDF 175) does the same thing: once the build to a structural cadence starts, Mozart puts the whole orchestra into one rhythm. Here the hemiola is the point, and an independent inner line would blur it. The recapitulation already varies the scoring at 136.3 (c297). Keep it.

4. **Fugato 85-96 (PDF 175-176).** I checked again that each voice runs subject, countersubject, then free voice, that each keeps its wind partner, and that the basses rest until 90. At 94-95 the cellos hold B♭3 while three voices move above them. I considered giving them a moving line or a stretto head. Either would make five active parts in the middle register under f, and that would go against the reading's own point about clarity (PDF 176: the listener "must not be overburdened"). The fugato is also protected (c142, c337, c437). Keep it.

5. **103-110, the two heads combined (PDF 177-178, 184).** The colour, articulation and dynamic of the two elements differ, and the strings rest at 103-106 as in Bloch's separate choirs. The P head at 103-106 still has no string. This was kept on c237 and c437 for the contrast with the ff block before it. Keep it.

6. **147-155, S recapitulated (PDF 176, 179).** The tune stays in Hn 1 + violas + cellos for the whole phrase (PDF 176: keep an established colour). The C.a. fills the held notes, and Vln II divisi sul tasto gives a background that moves only where the tune holds. The exposition's harp middleground is left out on purpose (do-not-touch). Keep it.

7. **The bass line overall (PDF 176, 181).** It has five kinds of writing:
   - a quaver or crotchet pulse under P (15-22, 27-32, 140-142, 167-169);
   - pizzicato under S (47-55, 147-155);
   - pedals (73-76, 117-130);
   - an independent contrapuntal line in the fugato (90-96);
   - the motto itself in the chorale (175-179).
   The cellos leave the bass whenever they have a line (47-55, 85-96, 98-102, 103-106, 139-142, 171-174). Nothing to change.

8. **69-72, the codetta (PDF 178).** The P head and the viola figure are both slurred quavers. This is the one partial point against PDF 178's rule of a separate articulation for each element, and it was kept by c237, c337 and c437. I see no new reason to reopen it.
