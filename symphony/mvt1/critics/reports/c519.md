## Findings

### 1. The harp under the first S statement is written a level too soft for a decaying sound (applied)

**Bars:** 47-55, harp (one dynamic for both hands, printed on the LH staff).

**What is wrong.** At 47 the S tune is in Hn 1 (mp from the 46.4 pickup) and the cellos (mf from 46.4, "dolce"). Under it are pads, Vln II metà and divided violas at pp, pizz. basses pp, and the harp's broken chords marked p. The harp RH climbs from A-flat4 to E-flat6 in eighths and holds the top note as a minim; the LH holds a low octave. A harp note has attack and decay but no sustain. A p written for it names only the attack, and by beat 2 it is well under p. Its lower notes, A-flat4 to C5, lie in the same band as the Vln II and viola pads. So the written p gives a sound that falls under the pp pads within each bar. That works against what c358 set up: the accompaniment one level under the tune. Here the decaying harp ends up two or three levels under the tune.

**Evidence.** Casanelles: the written dynamic names the resulting loudness, not the player's effort (PDF 271-272, Fig. 6.1). The envelope of a sound, "the appearance, disappearance, and changing relative amplitudes" of its partials, is part of its timbre (PDF 276). ADSR is the model: a plucked note has no sustain stage (PDF 279). Instruments in the same frequency band mask each other (PDF 277-278). **Master:** Kalinnikov does exactly this when his S returns (rehearsal 22, K PDF 70, printed p. 58). The tune is in Vln I, Vln II, violas and cellos in unison and octaves, "mp dolce". Hn 1 holds a pedal mf, flutes and clarinets play p repeated chords, and the harp's sextuplet arpeggios are marked **f**. The harp is written two levels above the tune it accompanies because its notes die away. Rimsky-Korsakov says the harp "is never a very powerful instrument, and the orchestrator should treat it with respect", and that it is "more an instrument of colour than expression" (rimsky-principles ch. I, Harp, PDF 47). An instrument of colour that is not heard adds no colour.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-519/work/sections/tTwo.ily, `tTwoHarpL`, bar 47):**
```
  aes,,1\mp_\pedAflat | des,1 | bes,,1 | es,,1 |
```
(was `aes,,1\p_\pedAflat`). The harp is now level with Hn 1 and a level under the cellos. Its attacks sit just above the pp pads, as Kalinnikov's do. The p at 56 stays. There the tune moves to the violins at p, and the harp RH moves above them (c157), out of the pads' band. Notes, voicings, pedals and étouffez marks do not change (c136, c246, c446). c358's rule (accompaniment a level under the tune) holds for the sustained parts. This answers it for the one part whose sound does not sustain.

**Test.** 0 warnings, 18 pages, analysis.txt unchanged, vl.txt identical (no pitch change). Parts: 0 warnings, the harp part 3 pages as before, every other part's page count unchanged. Score page 6 checked at 130 dpi: "mp" sits where "p" was, with no collision with the pedal diagram.

**Weight.** This is a small change. I rank it first because it is the only place where the reading and the master both point at the same mark, and because 47 is the first time the listener hears S.

### 3. Holistic judgment against Kalinnikov 1/i (no change)

I compared the score with Kalinnikov on the points where the two movements do the same job.

- **Opening.** Kalinnikov opens with the bare main theme, strings in octaves p, with no harmony. Horns answer it p with chromatic chords and accents (K PDF 13-14). A 3/2 bar stretches the phrase. Our motto at 1-4 is also bare (B.cl, Vc, Cb pp). It is answered by the cor anglais and string chords at 5-8, and the horn call follows at 13-14. The method is the same: a bare line, then the colour answers it. Our introduction is slower and darker. That fits a C-minor Lento, and it is protected (c409, c429).
- **Second theme, re-scored at every return.** This is Kalinnikov's strongest skill. S first comes in cellos and violas dolce mp over pizzicato violins, a held horn and high woodwind repeated chords (rehearsal 3, K PDF 21-22). Hn 1 then takes it. At rehearsal 4 the violins have it in octaves, mf, over a clarinet triplet arpeggio marked f and horn off-beat chords (K PDF 24). In the recapitulation the harp is added (K PDF 70). The score does the same: Hn 1 + Vc over harp at 47; violins sul D/A with the cello arpeggio and the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline at 56; B minor ff in Fl/Ob/C.a. with violins sul G at 97; violas espr. at 107; Hn 1 + violas over the sul tasto pad at 147; tutti fff at 158. Each return has its own accompaniment figure. This is at the master's level.
- **Harp only in the recapitulation (Kalinnikov) against harp in the exposition (here).** I considered moving the harp to 147-155 to follow the model. I reject that for the reasons c470 gave and I do not answer them: the harp would then sit under both S statements (rimsky-principles PDF 22, the student's "passion for the harp, using it in every possible chord"), and movement II's theme A is Hn 1 over harp (II 7-14), about three minutes after 147.
- **Development and recapitulation.** Kalinnikov's development is about as long as his exposition. It has a pp imitative passage for strings (rehearsal 14, K PDF 50) and dolce lyrical episodes (from rehearsal 11, K PDF 43-45). Then come a poco ritard. and a soft recapitulation (rehearsal 20, K PDF 64). The score has the same parts in a shorter frame: fugato 85-96, S in B minor 97-102 and the viola S 107-110, then the G pedal 117-130 to a ff recapitulation. The proportions are protected (c215, c243, c283, c493). Our recapitulation arrives loud where Kalinnikov's arrives soft. Ours follows the line of a tragic C-minor movement, and the soft C-major S at 147 gives the contrast he gets from his soft return.
- **Ending.** Kalinnikov's coda drops to a pp string pulse with a soft woodwind solo (K PDF 90), then ends ff with tutti chords and string scales (K PDF 91-92). The score drops in the same way (162-166), and the bright P head at 167-170 is our ff stop. Then it ends ppp and attacca. That ending belongs to the four-movement plan (the finale has the loud C major) and is protected.

A conductor who knows Kalinnikov would see the same craft in the treatment of S. The score is denser in harmony than Kalinnikov (Neapolitan and augmented-sixth colour, the lament bass at 111-117), and that is the late-Romantic idiom Dan asked for. Where Kalinnikov does more, the passages are protected for reasons I do not answer: the long, relaxed development and the exposition repeat (Kalinnikov repeats his exposition, K PDF 32-33; a repeat here would change every bar number the other movements cite).
