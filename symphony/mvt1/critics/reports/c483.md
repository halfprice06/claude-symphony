## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. The items are ranked by how much they bear on the form, and each ends with a recommendation.

### 1. The sudden drops follow Ex. XV-36 or are deliberate caesuras, including the five c373 did not list: keep

Adler (PDF 190) says the soft choir should already be sounding when the loud chord stops. I checked the five drops c373 did not examine:
- **41→42.** The brass play an sf quaver and stop. Woodwinds and strings hold the chord sf>mf and continue at mf. This is Ex. XV-35's second layout (a short trumpet sf over long strings) followed by an overlap.
- **62.** The whole orchestra drops to sub. p together. This is not a cut-off: every part continues, so Ex. XV-36 does not apply, and the drop is the player's own. It is protected ("sub. p at 62", c268, c273).
- **102→103.** The ff B-minor S ends with the bar. At 103 a new, lighter texture starts at f (winds, Hn 1, Vc, Cb pizz.). This is a change of section, not a subito.
- **122→123.** The tam-tam and the pedal parts carry over the bar line while the upper parts fade after two beats. The soft layer is already there, as in Ex. XV-36.
- **162→163.** The brass make ff>mp inside 162 under the strings, which carry on into 163. The overlap is written in.

Recommendation: keep all of them.

### 2. Every arrival is prepared by rest and contrast, as chapter 17 asks: keep

Adler gives two rules for arrivals. A solo colour is fresh when a contrasting choir comes before it (PDF 263, Ex. XVII-7/8). A soloist introduced by the tutti is then given a drastically reduced orchestra (PDF 267, Ex. XVII-10). The re-entry counts show the score plans this at every large arrival:
- At 158, fourteen players return after 11–15 bars, the longest rests before any tutti.
- At 147 the C.a. returns after 44 bars, introduced by Ob 1 at 145.
- At 156 the harp returns after 87 bars.
- After each tutti the orchestra is cut at once: Bsn 1 alone at 33 and 144, Fl 1 at 73, two players at 84, the solo cello and C.a. at 171.

Mozart's sustained tonic in the horns and basses under the reduced orchestra (PDF 267, point 3) is matched at 147 (Bsn 1 C3, Hn 3 C4, Cb C2 pizz.) and at 171 (Bsn 2). Recommendation: keep.

### 3. Role exchange between the two S statements (Ex. XVII-5/6): keep

In the Tchaikovsky concerto opening (PDF 261–262) the theme moves to the strings while the soloist turns to chords. This score does the same between its two statements of S:
- **47.** Hn 1 and the cellos carry S. The harp's arpeggios and the violas' pp pad form the background.
- **147.** The violas, cellos and Hn 1 carry S. Vln II plays a sul tasto tremolo halo, and the harp is held back until 156.

The recapitulation is therefore heard as the same theme in a new setting, not a copy. That is the formal clarity the dialogue principle claims (PDF 258). Recommendation: keep. The 147–155 layout is protected (c384, c402, c427).

### 4. The silence after 170 and the pp entry at 171: keep

Walton's recitative (Ex. XVII-18, PDF 277) breaks off, holds a silence, and continues "lugubre" ppp on a low chord with soft cymbal, timpani and gong. This score's 170→175 follows the same order:
- 170: a ff stop chord under a fermata.
- 171: silence, then Più lento, with the solo cello mp and violas pp.
- 175: the trombone chorale pp with the tam-tam pp, soft stick.

The interruption at 170 is one of the three stop chords. They are the movement's version of the "orchestral interruptions" that "heighten the drama" (PDF 276). The coda's extra time comes from its two slower tempi, not from extra bars. Recommendation: keep. The silence after 170 is protected (c460), and so are the coda notes and tempi.

### 5. Where the music could still fall short of a master: the recapitulated first group, 131–146

I asked where, within this lens, a listener might not remember a moment. The candidate is 131–146: 30.7 s, the shortest main section. After the fff climax at 122 and the stretto at 127–130, a first-time listener may hear the ff return at 131 as a continuation of the climax rather than as a new formal event.

The reading answers the doubt three ways:
- The dialogue at 131→135 (ff tutti, then the mf hemiola in woodwinds and strings) repeats the first group in a "radically different colour" (PDF 257).
- The ff N6 at 140 is new to the recapitulation.
- The drop to Bsn 1 alone at 144 is the drastic reduction of Ex. XVII-10.

Tchaikovsky 6/i (corpus annotation above) shows a master willing to fold the first-group return into the development's climax. This score is more explicit than that.

Lengthening 131–146 is not a fix. It would break the protected proportions (c243, c393, c433, c473) and the 122 < 160 climax order (c283, c373). Recommendation: keep. I record the question so that a later form pass does not have to reopen it without new evidence.
