# Critic 462: Harmony and voice leading (v462)

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 22 Leading-Tone Seventh Chords and Unit 23 Mixture (aldwell-schachter PDF 373–397), third pass. The earlier passes were c262 and c352. I read every text page in the range and looked at every page as an image at 110 dpi (PDF 379 is scanned upside down and was rotated). As c262 and c352 noted, the scan skips printed pp. 393 and 399, so Ex. 23-2h, 23-3, 23-10 and 23-11 are cut off. Their points are stated in the prose on PDF 388 and in the Points for Review on PDF 395.

Method. I wrote two scratch scripts (/tmp/critic-462/scripts/red.py and dim7.py) that read v462's full.midi. red.py prints every sounding pitch, with its instruments, at each beat or half-beat. dim7.py lists every beat where a complete diminished seventh sounds, with its bass. I traced each one into the next chord. I also reduced 33–47, 56–76, 77–84, 97–116, 139–170 and 171–182 beat by beat and checked the ♭6 and ♮6 paths in the major-key passages.

## Findings

This third pass confirms what c262 and c352 found: every diminished seventh in the movement is approached and resolved as Unit 22 teaches, or it embellishes V over a pedal. Every ♭6 in the major-key passages reaches 5 before any ♮6 sounds. I also looked for places where the harmony falls short of a master's within this lens. I found one candidate, at bar 108 (Finding 1). I built it and tested it, and it fails, so I recommend no change. The findings below are ranked by how audible they are. None is applied.

### 1. Bars 108.3→109: the move from E minor to B♭ minor is an enharmonic diminished seventh. Completing the chord creates parallel fifths with the bass (keep)

At 108.3 the chord over the bass B (B.cl B2, Cb pizz. B1) is D♯4 (Vln II upper), A3 (Vln II lower) and C5 (violas), with B4 in the violas on beat 4. This is an incomplete V♭9 of E minor with no F♯. At 109 it moves straight to B♭ minor. Read as V7 of E, it breaks §3 (PDF 375): the 7th, A3, rises to B♭3. But the three upper notes D♯–A–C are three notes of the diminished seventh F♯–A–C–D♯. Spelled A–C–E♭–G♭, that chord is VII°7 of B♭ minor, the kind of reinterpretation shown in Ex. 22-13 (PDF 381, §13). Heard that way, the A3 rises as the leading tone of B♭, C goes to D♭ (2→3), E♭ goes to F, and the bass slides B→B♭. The modulation by a tritone therefore works the way the reading describes, and it is the only place in the movement where this third diminished-seventh set sounds even in part (see Finding 2).

I considered completing the diminished seventh so the listener hears it whole. The reading suggests G♭→F (♭6→5 in B♭, §3 and Unit 23 PDF 388). I tested this in a copy (/tmp/critic-462/test): cellos `r2 fis2(\pp | f2) r2` at 108–109, where the cellos rest. vl.py then reports two new fifths: "bar 109 P5 vc:F#3->F3 BASS:B2->B♭1". With the bass sliding B→B♭, the fifth of the B chord cannot move to the fifth of B♭ without parallel fifths. A held G♭ as a 6–5 suspension would only delay the same fifths. The composer's voicing omits F♯ for this reason. Leaving out the fifth is the standard incomplete dominant ninth, so the present voicing is the correct one. Keep. The written diminished thirds D♯4→F4 (Vln II) and B4→D♭5 (violas) also stay: the sharp spelling names the dominant of E that the ear hears first. The bars are also inside the protected 103–110 combined-heads passage (c437) and the 96–108 passing keys (c413).

### 2. The movement's diminished sevenths are almost all one pitch-class set (keep; record)

dim7.py finds 17 complete diminished sevenths. Fourteen use the pitch classes B–D–F–A♭: at 11, 26, 28, 35, 70 (as vii°7/V in A♭), 73, 83, 114, 122, 132–133, 138, 161, 170 and 177. Two use D♭–E–G–B♭ (30 and 63). The third set (C–E♭–F♯–A) never sounds complete; only part of it appears at 108 (Finding 1). §13 (PDF 381) presents the diminished seventh's ambiguity as a means of modulation. This score instead uses one diminished seventh as a fixed colour, and its development key plan B–D–F–A♭ (protected since c233 and c430) composes out that same chord. This is a design choice and it serves the movement's unity: the vii°7 of C is the harmony of all three stop chords that the other movements quote. Keep. I record it so that later critics do not propose a second diminished seventh "for variety" without answering this reason.

### 3. Bar 164.2–165.1: Cl 1 leaves ♭6 by a leap, and other voices resolve it (keep)

In the C-major cadence, iiø7 at 164 has A♭ in Hn 3 (A♭3), Cl 1 (A♭4 on 164.2) and the harp (A♭5). Hn 3 falls to G3 at 164.3 and the harp to G5 at 165.1. Cl 1, however, leaps A♭4→D5, a tritone up. PDF 388 says ♭6 tends to move to 5. Ex. 22-5 (PDF 376) shows that a dissonance's resolution may be transferred to another voice. Here the horn, in the same register, carries A♭3→G3 and the harp carries A♭5→G5. The order ♮6 (163) – ♭6 (164) – 5 (164.3) is the "good" order of PDF 395 Point 3. Keep. This passage is protected (c262, c352, c442).

### 4. Bar 148.3–4: Vln II D♭5 over F major is a chromatic passing note, not mixture (keep)

In the C-major second theme the Vln II halo moves C5–D♭5–D5 over IV (148) into ii (149). D♭ is not ♭6 of C, so Unit 23 §2 does not apply. It is a chromatic passing note in one voice, and Ex. 23-12b (PDF 394) shows that such a succession is not a cross relation. It also does not anticipate the A♭ mixture held back for 156–166. Keep.

### 5. Bars 31.1 and 114→115 (keep, as c352)

The hidden fifths at 31.1 (Hn 2 G4→F4 against Tpt 1 D♭5→C5, Ex. 22-3c, PDF 375) and the A2 B→A♭ in five parts at 114→115 (§11, PDF 380) are the only departures from the letter of Unit 22. c352 gave the reasons to keep both: 27–32 is quoted at pitch by the finale, and the A2 is the only path that does not double a tone. I re-checked both and agree.

### Where the score stands against a master, within this lens

The reading's best effects are already in the score. They include the °7 over the tonic pedal under the flute motto at 73 (Ex. 22-10), the ♭VI deceptive cadence at 160 against the exposition's vi at 68 (Ex. 23-6), the ♮6–♭6–5 close at 163–165, and the Picardy E♮ at 182 after the violas' E♭ (Ex. 23-7). The single-colour diminished seventh (Finding 2) limits the harmonic range, but it is the movement's structural chord, and widening it would weaken the stop chords that the other movements quote. I found no change that meets the bar of a real improvement.
