## What I studied

Books: Sebesky as above. For supporting evidence I also used MOLA guidelines PDF 5 on horn key signatures and the movement's own HISTORY and cyclic-notes.md, including the movement II relays for cycles 41–50. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i coda, `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly`, bars 335–354. Over the pizzicato descending scale, its trombone cadence is stated twice with the same harmony: Tbn `e2 e4. e8 | fis2.~ fis8` at 337–338, then `e2 e4. e8` again at 339. Literal repetition over a closing pedal is therefore a legitimate model. Web: Wikipedia "Neapolitan chord" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neapolitan_chord) notes the root-position ♭II in the next-to-last bar of Chopin's C-minor Prelude Op. 28 No. 20, which is the device at our bar 178. Wikipedia "Cyclic form" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_form) notes that cyclic returns are usually transformed rather than literal (Berlioz, "a different guise in every part"; Schubert D 959 "in reverse"; Brahms Op. 115 "in the subdominant").

## Findings

### 1. Bar 180: the motto's last D♭ is the third D♭-major triad in eight bars, with the same inner voices as at 178 (applied in scratch)

Problem. The coda harmonizes the motto's D♭ as a D♭-major triad at 173 (N6 under the cor anglais), at 178 (root position, chorale) and at 180 (D♭/F over the C pedal, Vln I's last statement). At 178 and 180, Vln II plays `g'2 f'2` and Vla plays `es'2 aes2` in both bars, so the canonic echo at 180 also repeats the 178 harmony note for note in the inner voices. The very first D♭ of the movement (bar 3, `c2 des4 c4`) is heard bare over the timpani's C roll.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 19: "I usually try to avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice, whenever possible". Ex. 3/3A reach the same resting chord by different routes. Sebesky PDF 12–13, Ex. 1: removing a moving inner line in the lower-middle register made the remaining voices clearer and let the primary element stand out. At 180 the primary element is the single violin D♭. This also follows HISTORY cycle 52's rule ("no more Neapolitan … cadences"). The counter-evidence is Tchaikovsky 6/i 337–340, where a closing cadence repeats on the same harmony. But his repetition is a two-bar ostinato over a scale pedal. Ours is a canonic echo two bars later, which the cyclic-form sources say should be transformed.

Fix (coda.ily, bar 180 only):
- `codaVlnTwo`: `g'1 | g'2 f'2 | g'1~\>` → `g'1 | g'1 | g'1~\>`
- `codaVla`: `es'1 | es'2 aes2 | es'1\>` → `es'1 | es'1 | es'1\>`

Beats 3–4 of 180 become C–E♭–G with D♭ above as a bare upper neighbour, at pp. The movement's last D♭ then rhymes with its first one (bar 3, over C), and the coda's three D♭s are set three ways: N6 at 173, root-position ♭II at 178, and a neighbour over the tonic at 180.

Protection. This touches coda notes protected by cycles 130, 140 and 150 (coda notes 171–182). Their stated reasons were the closing design: the Vln I motto 177–182, the viola E♭→E♮ at 181–182, the five tied parts, "attacca" and the hand-offs. None of these changes. Vln I is untouched, the viola still goes E♭ (181) → E♮ (182), and the outgoing ties and dynamics are as before. This is optional if the composer prefers Tchaikovsky's literal-repetition model, but the book evidence favours the change.

### 2. Notes for movement III: do not reuse the 175–179 "chorale" voicing as written (no change in movement I)

Problem. The coda's "three-part trombone chorale" puts the motto in both outer voices: Tbn 1 `g1 | es'1 | d'1 | c'2 des'2 | c'1` and B.tbn `g,1 | es1 | d1 | c2 des2 | c1`. These are octaves throughout, with only Tbn 2 and the violins/viola filling between. In movement I that is correct, because it is the low unison motto of 1–4 returning (protected, cycle 130). cyclic-notes.md §3, however, tells movement III to use "the trombone voicing of 175–179 (Tbn 1 / Tbn 2 / B.tbn), f to ff". A forte chorale whose bass doubles its melody in octaves would be harmonized exactly like the opening unison and would have no harmonic motion of its own.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 19–20 (reharmonize to the same resting place; this is "harmonic variety"). Sebesky PDF 241, keep notes of what worked and reuse the principle rather than the copy. The cyclic-form sources favour transformed returns.

Fix for movement III (for the composer's cyclic notes): keep the trombone colour and the top line `g1 | e'1 | d'1 | c'2 des'2 | c'1` (major), but give the bass trombone an independent bass, for example `c1 | c1 | b,1 | a,2 bes,2 | c1` (I–I6/4 colour–V–vi–♭VII/N–I). Alternatively, use Sebesky's Ex. 3A route for the last statement: `... c'2 b2 | c'1` over `aes,2 g,2 | c1` (the trumpets' B→C already planned).

### 3. Bar 177: focus at Vln I's last motto entry (checked, no change)

Vln I's pickup `g'4\pp` on beat 4 of 177 enters under Cl 1's `b'2` and inside the chorale's held D/F/A♭, all pp. Sebesky PDF 13–14 asks that the primary element not be confused by the background. I judge this sufficient as written. The pickup is 16 violins against one clarinet, and from 178 Vln I's E♭5 is the highest sound in the texture by a fourth. The dynamics are protected (cycles 150, 158). No change.

### 4. Motto timbre and harmonic plan (verified)

Ten motto statements use nine different colour combinations (Reading notes, row 6). The two cor anglais statements (5–8, 171–174) are a deliberate rhyme, and they are harmonized differently: i7–♭II6–I (major) at 5–8, and i–iv–N6–cad. 6/4–V at 171–174. This meets Sebesky PDF 15–20 on both counts.

### Notes for movements II and III (not written into cyclic-notes.md)

- Chorale: see Finding 2. Movement III's chorale needs an independent bass. Movement I's octave-doubled version is the frame return of bars 1–4, not a model.
- If Finding 1 is accepted, movement I's last chord change is C minor → C major over a held pedal, with no D♭ chord after 178. Movement II's plagal D♭–A♭ close and movement III's D♭ episode keep the D♭ triad as the later movements' colour.
- Brass key signatures (relay, mvt II cycles 41–49): MOLA PDF 5 says that "traditionally, horns, tuned percussion, and timpani use any required accidentals rather than a key signature". That applies to parts. A concert-pitch score may keep the key signatures, as movement I does. Recommend one convention for both movements' scores: either both with signatures or both without. In all parts, horns go without key signatures (cycle 185 parts stage).
- Sebesky PDF 11 (delay a colour for surprise): movement I has spent the tam-tam twice and the trombone chorale once at pp. Movement III's chorale arrival should be the first time trumpets and full brass sustain a major chord at forte, with no tam-tam (as cyclic-notes already says).
