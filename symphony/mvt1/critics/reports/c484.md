## Findings

No change. The score obeys every rule in the reading, and I found no change within this lens that would make the music better and that the reading or the masters support. Below are the places I tested against the question "where does this fall short of a master?" and why each stays.

### 1. The three A♭ climaxes as a series (kept)

82, 122 and 160 each add a colour the previous one lacked, which is Adler's hold-back principle (PDF 17, 37). 82 is the first full brass with trumpets after a nine-bar trumpet rest, with the held brass f under the ff head and ff strokes at 83. 122 follows the Tchaikovsky model (PDF 38–41): the brass-only motto in long notes over the timpani pedal, then fff with tam-tam. 160 follows the Bartók layout (PDF 44–47): a single trumpet on the tune with horn pairs on tune and counter, the whole orchestra doubling, and the suspended cymbal. My weight check finds every chord tone of the 82, 158 and 160 chords at 2.5 or more in the brass. A master would not change this order. I considered whether 160 needs a further colour, for example the four horns in unison on the tune. That is on the "Do not reuse" list for the finale (a high four-horn unison) and would weaken the finale's arrival. Keep.

### 2. Bar 122, low trumpets at fff (kept, protected c244, c264, c284, c374)

At 122 the highest brass note is A♭4 (Tpt 1 and Hn 1), and Tpt 2 D4 and Tpt 3 B3 are in the register where Adler says the trumpet is weak (PDF 15). The weight check gives D only 0.5 in the brass at 122–123. c374 kept this because raising Tpt 1 to A♭5 would give 122 the same trumpet register as 160 and flatten the climax order. That reason still holds under the reading (PDF 17, 37). D is carried by five other parts. I do not reopen it.

### 3. Bar 161 beat 4, the fifth of V7 only in Tpt 3 D4 (kept)

The check flags D at 161.4 at 0.5 in the brass (Tpt 3 D4, low register). The beat is the V7 between ♭VI and vi, with the protected anticipation of C in Tpt 1 (c481). The fifth of a dominant seventh can be dropped in four-part writing, the leading tone (Tbn 1 B3) and the seventh (Tpt 2 F4, Tbn 2 F3) are present, and the beat lasts a crotchet at largamente. Adding a second D would make the chord thicker at a point where the brass already start their diminuendo into 162. Keep.

### 4. Single horns at f in background chords: 23–25, 135–137, 69–72, 104–106 (kept)

Adler's horn rule (PDF 15) is about balancing the brass among themselves. In all four places the single horns are part of a mixed chord. At 23–25 and 135–137 they sit over bassoons, cellos and basses in a crescendo (protected c259, c304, c404). At 69–72 the woodwinds, Tpt 1·2 and the violas carry the same notes. At 104–106 the horns rise to about f in a crescendo with each horn doubling a bassoon or cello line (protected c397, 103–106 unison). None of these is a chord tone that only a single horn carries. Keep.

### 5. Brass motto at 117–121 (kept, protected)

The weight check lists B, A♭, D and F at 0 in the brass at 117–121. This is correct for the passage: the brass play the motto in unison and octaves (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn) over the G pedal (Hn 3·4, tuba), and the woodwinds and strings carry the harmony. This is the Tchaikovsky cantus-firmus layout of Ex. XI-21 (PDF 39–41) and the Handel homophonic choir of Ex. XI-3 (PDF 18) reduced to one line. Keep.

### 6. Where the movement could still gain, and why I do not propose it here

The one brass resource the movement does not use is a special effect (PDF 16, use 7; PDF 50–54): muted or stopped horns, for example in the collapse at 123–126 or the coda. coherence/mvt1.md lists muted and stopped brass among the things movement I does not use, and c408 protected "no mutes/stopped horn". The late-Romantic models (Brahms, Dvořák, Elgar's first movements) rarely use them in a sonata allegro. A stopped-horn colour would be new and would not make an existing passage clearer, so it does not meet the bar for a change.
