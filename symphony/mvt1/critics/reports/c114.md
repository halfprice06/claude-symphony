# Critic 114, lens 4 (orchestration), v133. Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov ch. I "Comparison of resonance" (PDF 51–53) and ch. III "Harmony" (PDF 81–99)

## What I studied
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration* ("principles of form.pdf"). I read every text page from PDF 51 to 53 and from 81 to 99. I rendered the music-example pages (PDF 82–86 and 88–97) at 110 dpi and read them as images. The OCR loses most of the diagrams, and on PDF 92 it also drops the paragraph on three-part woodwind harmony, which I took from the image. PDF 98–99 are lists of examples and contain no music.
- The score: v133 sections, the PDF (pages 3–5 after my build), and a MIDI with one staff per player. I made it by un-part-combining a copy of score.ly (/tmp/critic-114/split/split2.ly); the dump and check scripts are in /tmp/critic-114/split/. From it I measured:
  - intervals between paired players;
  - doubling of the bass of inverted seventh chords;
  - bass-to-tenor distance;
  - interior voids in tutti chords;
  - woodwind register order;
  - wind doubling of Violin I.
- I used no corpus file or web source this cycle. The assigned reading answered every question I tested.

## Findings

**1. Bars 41–42: the four horns' call is covered by seven brass parts held at f. APPLIED.**
- *Problem.* The horns play ff in unison: E♭3 on beat 4 of 41, then C4–B♭3. Under them, Tpt 1–3, Tbn 1–2, B.tbn and tuba hold the E♭7 chord at a steady f through both bars. The call starts on E♭3, the same pitch as the B.tbn, and lies between Tbn 2 (G3), Tbn 1 (B♭3) and Tpt 3 (D♭4). The strings and woodwinds already go sf > mf here (c108). The brass does not.
- *Evidence.* RK PDF 51: "In loud passages the horns are only one-half as strong, Trumpet = Trombone = Tuba = 2 Horns." By that table, four horns on one line weigh two trumpets. The accompaniment weighs seven, one dynamic step lower. One dynamic step does not make up a factor of three and a half, all the more when the call is enclosed inside the brass chord (RK PDF 91–92: an enclosed part needs different colours on both sides). Cycle 98 set brass f here, citing RK as "horns one degree stronger than the other brass". The table on PDF 51 says the opposite for forte. It gives horns half the strength of the other brass, so grading the horns one step up does not balance seven parts against one line.
- *Fix (trans.ily):*
  - Tpt 1 `bes'1\sf\> | bes'2\mf aes'2\>`; Tpt 2 `g'1\sf\> | g'2\mf f'2\>`; Tpt 3 `des'1\sf\> | des'1\mf\>`.
  - Tbn 1 `bes1\f | bes1\sf\> | bes1\mf\>`; Tbn 2 `f1\f | g1\sf\> | g1\mf\>`.
  - B.tbn `es1\sf\>~ | es1\mf\>`; tuba `es,1\sf\>~ | es,1\mf\>`.
  - The brass still attacks with the tutti at 41 and still supports the call (cycle 98's purpose). It drops to mf by the time the horns arrive, as every other section already does. Pairs on shared staves keep equal dynamics.

**2. Bars 41–43: the two oboes hold a tritone, G5 over D♭5. APPLIED.**
- *Problem.* Ob 1 holds G5 and Ob 2 holds D♭5 for six beats at sf > mf, then F5/B♭4 (a fourth) and E♭5/B♭4 (a fourth at 43).
- *Evidence.* RK PDF 90, woodwind rule 4: "Concords (octaves, thirds and sixths) and not discords (fifths, fourths, seconds and sevenths), should be given to instruments of the same kind or colour … This rule should be specially observed in writing for the oboe." The example on PDF 90 (image) marks two oboes on a discord as "to be avoided".
- *Fix (trans.ily):* Ob 2 and Cl 1 exchange their lines for 41–43.
  - Ob 2: `g'1\sf\>~ | g'2\mf f'2\> | es'4\p`.
  - Cl 1: `des''1\sf\> | des''2\mf bes'2\> | bes'4\p`.
  - The oboes now sound octaves (G, F, E♭). The D♭ sits in Cl 1 against Cl 2's B♭3, a minor tenth.
  - The order becomes Ob 1, Cl 1, Ob 2, Cl 2. That is crossing, which RK allows (PDF 91, "Crossing").
  - The pitches and rhythm of the chord do not change.

**3. Bars 56–68: the horn 3·4 pad has a unison at 57 and an open fourth at 65. APPLIED.**
- *Problem.* The violins carry the tune and the cellos, basses and harp carry the bass. Hn 3·4 hold the middle harmony, which is RK's "harmonic basis" case. At 57 both horns sit on A♭3, so the pad has one pitch. At 65 they hold D♭4/A♭3, an open fourth. Every other bar has a third or a sixth.
- *Evidence.* RK PDF 97, remark 4: when the upper part is in the strings and the bass is treated separately, "there remains nothing for the wind except the sustained harmony in the two middle parts … it is desirable to provide the intermediate parts with a moderately full tone, choosing no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths", and "intervals of open fourths and fifths will be obviated."
- *Fix (tTwo.ily):*
  - Hn 4 bar 57 `f1` (was `aes1`), giving A♭3/F3.
  - Hn 3 bar 65 `f'1` (was `des'1`), giving F4/A♭3.
  - F is a chord tone in both bars (D♭/A♭ at 57; D♭ major at 65).
  - My first version put Hn 4 on F3 at 65. That moved A♭→F in octaves with the violins' tune (RK PDF 83: no consecutive octaves between upper parts), so I moved Hn 3 instead. It moves C4 → F4 → D♭4 against the tune's A♭5 → F5, in contrary motion.
  - A parallel check of the changed voices against all parts (bars 40–44 and 55–66) shows no new fifths or octaves. Hn 4's change also removes the octaves A♭→B♭ with the bass at 57–58.

**4. Bars 47–54: an undivided Violin II note against a divided viola pair. APPLIED (text only).**
- *Problem.* The pad under the horn/cello tune is Vln II on one note (A♭4, B♭4, G♭4 …) with the violas divided on two notes. The top note of the pad therefore has twice the players of each lower note. It also lies in the tune's own register (the cello reaches A♭4 at 50).
- *Evidence.* RK PDF 88: "The method of dividing strings … should be avoided in such cases, as certain parts of the chord will be divided and others will not". If the composer keeps the division, the undivided parts' "tone must therefore be eased, either by marking the parts down or reducing the number of players." Viola double stops, RK's first choice, are not practical here: B♭3/D♭4 and G3/B♭3 lie on one string.
- *Fix (tTwo.ily):* `aes'1\pp^\markup \italic "metà"` at 47, and `<es' aes'>2^\markup \italic "tutti"` at 55. The notes and dynamics are unchanged. This does not touch the protected soft scoring of 47–55; it balances it.

## Notes (checked, not proposed)
- **26 and 138 (RK PDF 83–84).** The vii°4/3 stop chord doubles its bass D in Ob 2, Hn 3, Tpt 3 and Vln II, which RK marks "Bad". The rule exists to protect the resolution, and here a quarter rest separates the chord from bar 27/139, so no octave progression follows. These stops are protected (cycles 30, 90). I leave them.
- **12 (RK PDF 90 rule 4).** In the German sixth the oboes hold C5/F♯4, a tritone. Ob 2 carries the F♯→G resolution into 13, so moving the F♯ to a clarinet would cut the line. Correct part movement comes first in RK's own order (PDF 81).
- **62–65 (RK PDF 52).** Fl 1 in unison with Vln I only thickens the line, as RK says. It enters with the new phrase at sub. p and thickens the climax of the phrase, which is a deliberate use. Kept.
- **167–170 (RK PDF 88).** Both violin sections are divided in tremolo while the violas and cellos are not. Under ff brass this is a texture, not a sustained balance problem, and the layout is protected (cycle 90).
