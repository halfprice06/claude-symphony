## Findings

There is no finding to apply. The score obeys every principle in the table. Below I list what I tested beyond compliance and why each candidate fails the bar the brief sets: a real improvement that the reading or the masters support.

### Checked against the reading and kept

**66.3–67.2, Vln I A♭5 over V7 of A♭ leaps to F5.** Over E♭–D♭–F–B♭, the violins' A♭5 is a 4th above the bass, in the top voice and above the 7th, as PDF 475 requires for an unresolved 4th. It does not fall to G5. Instead the line runs down the chord (F5–D♭5–B♭4) into the deceptive cadence at 68. The G arrives in Vln II and Cl 1 (G4) on 67.2–3. This is the texture of Ex. 27-6a (PDF 466–467): the added tone appears "in the course of a florid melodic line" and its resolution "is transferred into the accompaniment". The A♭ also returns as the 3rd of F minor at 68, so it works as an anticipation, as in Ex. 27-17 (PDF 474). The 67–68 D♭ fall and the 68 voicing are protected (c421, c422). Keep.

**151.4 (and 51.4), violas D5 a step above Vln II's held C5 over C7/E (V6/5 of IV).** This is the only inverted ninth chord with the resolution tone close by that the scan finds in the second group. A&S's warning on PDF 469 is about an accented suspension that sounds together with its resolution ("a dissonant suspension in the soprano and the tone to which it will resolve"). The viola's D5 is an unaccented passing crotchet in E5–D5–C5, inside the chord and not on top, and Vln II's C5 is the chord root, held, not an anticipation. Ex. 27-4d/e (PDF 465) allows the passing 9th. S at 147–155 is protected. c502 judged the same shape at 51.4 the same way. Keep.

**130.3, violas D♭4 against Cl 1's C4 over the G pedal.** This is the stretto of the half-augmented motto. Cl 1 has just reached C4 in D–D♭–C, and the violas' D♭ is the next canonic entry. A&S PDF 468–469 (Ex. 27-9) accepts a clash that "arises because the winds play a melodic figure that requires" it, and Ex. 27-21 (PDF 478) shows voices that arrive late over a dominant pedal. 127–130 is protected. Keep.

**131.4 and 139.4, 141.4: passing quavers in the hammered tutti and the F-minor return.** At 131.4 the D♭4s (bassoons, clarinets, trombones, cellos) sound a minor 2nd above Hn 3's held C4. At 139.4 and 141.4 the violas' E4 sits under F4 in Hn 2 and the trumpets. All are weak-quaver chromatic passing or neighbour notes inside the motto and P figures, not added chord tones. Unit 27 does not cover them, and c502 kept 142.4 (C♯4 under D4) on the same ground. 131–134 belongs to the hammered layout the finale answers, and 139–142 is a protected brass block. Keep.

**168.3–169.2, F4 falls to E♭4 in Bsn 1/Vc and in the horns and violas while E♭4 is held.** Over the C pedal this is iv7 (F–A♭–C–E♭). F is the chord root, not a dissonance, so this is a chord tone moving within the chord over a pedal (PDF 471). Keep.

### Candidates beyond compliance, tested in thought and rejected

**A. Ex. 27-18 at 145–147: an unresolved soprano 6th floating into the C-major second group.** Chopin's Ballade holds the 6th A over the alternating V and I, as "a wonderful dramatic preparation" with "a common element that connects these two contrasting sections" (PDF 474–475). At 145–146 Ob 1's E5 over V7 is the same tone that becomes the 3rd of C major at 147. Holding it instead of falling to D5 at 146.3 would join the sections in that way. I reject it for three reasons:
- At 146.3 the 7th has already stopped (Vln II and Cl 1 F4 end on 146.2.5). A held E5 over only G and B would sound as iii6, not as a 6th over V7. PDF 475 says an unresolved 6th must be heard clearly as one, or "it will create a mess".
- Ob 1's G4–E5–D5 is a recorded cross-movement seed, and the soprano 13th at 145–146 is protected (c239, c580).
- c582 rejected the related ♯5 at 146.4 because the S pickup G3–E4 rises underneath at that moment. A held E5 would double the pickup's goal note an octave higher, before the pickup reaches it.

**B. Ex. 27-13 at 167–168: the tonic over the coda's C pedal turned into V♭9 of iv (C–E–B♭–D♭) before iv7 at 168.3.** Schumann shows how this colours a tonic pedal (PDF 471). Here it would need E♮ two bars after the violas turn E♮ to E♭ at 166. That turn is the point where the movement goes back to C minor for good, and it is protected. It would also put a major C chord at forte in the coda, which mvt1.md's "Do not reuse" list keeps for the finale ("Movement I has C major only softly ..., never confirmed at forte"). Rejected.

**C. A ♭9 on the introduction's V7 at 13–14, or on the coda's V at 174.** c502 rejected 13–14: the score keeps V♭9 for 83, 117 and 122, and PDF 466 says the 9th's job is to intensify the dominant, so each extra one weakens those three. 174 is inside the protected coda harmony 171–182, and c590 protected "no A♭ in 179–182". A ♭9 at 174 would put the movement's A♭ colour back four bars before the line that is meant to end without it. Rejected.

**D. Resolve Vln I's A♭5 to G5 in register at 67.2.** This would satisfy the "resolve by step" norm literally, but it would break the protected D♭ fall at 67–68. It would also replace an Ex. 27-6a shape that the reading endorses with a plainer one. Rejected.

None of these meets the bar. The way the movement uses added dissonance follows the reading's main point (PDF 466, 475): the added tones come from lines and are saved for the dominant at its strongest moments. I found nothing in my lens where a change supported by this reading would make the music more vivid without spending a reserve or breaking a protected passage.
