# Critic 071: v90, lens 1 (melody and memorability). The theme combination 103–110

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY, c070, critique.md, and all of dev.ily, plus the theme sources in tOne.ily (15–16) and tTwo.ily (47–50, Vln I 55–58). I looked at page 9 of score.pdf and the v90 analysis.txt.

Corpus (extracted with music21):
- **Mozart 41/iv coda** (`musicxml-converted/xml/Mozart_Sym41_mvt4.musicxml`, MusicXML bars 372–380). The cello cantus moves in whole notes, the viola in eighths and Vln II in halves. Each theme has its own rhythmic layer and its own register, and the downbeats are consonant.
- **Dvořák 9/iv** (`Dvorak_Sym9_mvt4.musicxml`, 333–336). The trombones state the first-movement head (E–G♯–B–E) in bars 333 and 335 and alternate with held wind chords. It is a dialogue, not a superimposition, like the 9/i 217–228 case c070 cited.

Web sources:
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration* (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): "Wood-wind instruments, in forte passages, are twice as weak as the horns"; strings at f equal two winds; "melody should always stand out in relief from the accompaniment."
- On the Meistersinger prelude: The Listeners' Club (https://thelistenersclub.com/2025/06/09/wagners-die-meistersinger-overture-giuseppe-sinopoli-and-the-staatskapelle-dresden/) and the WETA listening guide (https://weta.org/fm/classical-score/listening-guide-wagners-die-meistersinger). Both say three themes are combined and compare it with the Jupiter coda. Neither gives bar numbers.
- On the Fantastique finale: Berliner Philharmoniker programme notes (https://www.berliner-philharmoniker.de/en/programme-notes/hector-berlioz-symphonie-fantastique-op-14) name the "Dies irae et Ronde du Sabbat ensemble" section; Musikforschung (https://mf.journals.qucosa.de/mf/article/download/1940/1061/1075) places the Ronde at bars 241–495. I found no bar-by-bar web analysis.
- The register layout I give for Meistersinger (~158ff) and the Fantastique ensemble (~414ff) is from my own memory of the scores, not verified online. The Prize Song is on top in long values, with the march in the bass and the Banner theme in the middle; in Berlioz, the Dies irae is in long notes in the winds against running string eighths.

## Overall verdict on 103–110
The design is sound and matches the models:
- The P head is active (eighths) and the S head is long (dotted half and quarters), so each theme has its own rhythmic layer, as in Mozart 372ff and Meistersinger.
- Both heads are recognisable. Bar 1 of each is quoted exactly (P: minor sixth up, then chromatic descent; S: 3̂–2̂–1̂–♭6̂–5̂).
- Bar 2 of P is varied at 104 (D–C♯–D–G–F instead of D–C♯–D–B♭–A) and at 108 (E–G–F♯–B). The reason is real: in its second bar, each theme falls ♭6̂–5̂ on the same beats, so a literal P would move in octaves with S. I would keep both variants.

The weaknesses are in register, in one suspension, and in the scoring of the accompaniment.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bar 105: Vln I 8va reaches B♭6, the highest note in the movement (applied).**
- **Problem:** Vln I plays `bes'''4.` (B♭6), then A6 and an extra A♭6. analysis.txt gives Violins I a range of E♭4–B♭6, and bars 105–108 top out at B♭6. No other part of the movement goes above A♭6.
- **Rules it breaks:** the register plan (c009: no high peaks outside the climaxes; c023/c061: A♭6 reserved for 31, 72, 122, 140–141, 164). A passing f bar in the development now sounds above the 122 climax.
- **Why it happens:** a P head sequenced up a fourth from an 8va start (A5→F6) has to reach B♭6.
- **Fix:** remove the ottava and play Vln I at the Vln II/Ob 1 octave, in unison:
  - `r8 a'8(\f f''4.->) e''8( es''8 d''8 | d''4) cis''8( d''8) g''4.-> f''8 | r8 d''8( bes''4.->) a''8( aes''8 g''8 | …`
- **Result:** the top line now rises steadily, with bar peaks F5, G5, B♭5 and then B5 (106). The 111–122 build starts from below its goal again. Violins I now span E♭4–A♭6.

**2. Bar 106: the suspension, voice crossing and the P head's second bar (applied).**
The tenor's 4–3 (`e''2 dis''2`, Hn 1/Vla) is badly accompanied:
- Vln I sounds D♯ (`dis'''8`) on beat 2 against the suspended E. Cycle 12's rule bans sounding the resolution note against a suspension.
- Vln II and Ob 1 (`e''2`) double the suspended E at the unison.
- Vln II and Ob 1 then drop to `c''4.` below the tenor's D♯5, so the tenor briefly becomes the top voice.
- The P head's bar 2 starts on E after bar 1 ended on G. In every other statement, bar 2 starts on the note that ended bar 1.

**Fix**, for Vln I, Vln II and Ob 1: `b''4) ais''8( b''8) g''4.->\> fis''8`. This is P's bar-2 shape with its exact intervals (−1, +1, −4, −1 semitones). The G is a ♭13 appoggiatura over B7 resolving to F♯. The line stays above the tenor throughout, and D♯ is sounded only by the tenor.

Horn 3 held F♯4 under that G (clash.py flagged it), so Hn 3 becomes `fis'2\> b2`. Horn 4's A3 stays as the seventh.

**3. Bars 103–106: the Hn 3·4 fill covers the tenor horn (applied).**
- **Answer to the balance question:** the 8va violins were not the main threat to the S head. Hn 1 at f in concert F4–E5 equals about two woodwinds (Rimsky-Korsakov), and with the violas it carries against two violin sections.
- **The real masking:** Hn 3·4 at mf play in the same colour and register. Hn 3 meets the tenor at the unison on 104 beat 1 (D4) and 105 beat 3 (E4), so at those points the tenor's line merges into the fill.
- **Fix:** Hn 3·4 mp instead of mf. Both players change together (`d'1\mp`, `a1\mp`), so the shared-staff dynamic rule holds.

**4. Bar 104, beat 1: hollow D (applied).**
Top, tenor and bass all have D, and Hn 3·4 have D/A, so the strong beat has no third. Fix: Hn 4 `f4 g2 e4` instead of `a4 g2 e4`. The step F3→G3 against the bass D→G is an inner-voice approach by step, and no parallels result.

**5. Bars 110–111: the link into the wedge repeats A♭–G (applied).**
- **Problem:** the top line goes `aes''2 g''2 | aes''2.~ aes''8 g''8`, so the wedge's opening A♭–G is heard twice and its arrival is pre-empted.
- **Comparison:** in the exposition (Vln I 55–58), `aes''2 g''2` is followed by B♭, not A♭.
- **Fix** in Vln I and Cl 1, bar 110: `aes''2\< g''4 c''4)`. The C→A♭ leap into 111 is the S head's own minor sixth (C–A♭ at 48), so the wedge's first note now arrives by the theme's leap, under the crescendo. C is a chord tone of the V chord on beat 4.

**6. Bars 103 and 104: octave meetings between the heads (not changed).**
- At 103 the P head's sixth lands on F, the note the S head is holding, so the two voices are in octaves on beats 2–3.
- At 104 beat 1 they meet on D again. The approach is 9–8 (tenor E over top D), which is correct.
- **Cause:** both heads begin on ♭3̂, so these meetings are built into the material. A master would accept them for one beat, as Mozart does at the unison entries of 372ff. With findings 2 and 4 applied, the other strong beats are real two-voice intervals (B/E at 106, B♭/G at 105).
- I leave this alone. Rewriting it would mean changing the S head's starting degree, which would cost recognisability.

**7. Bar 108: the cello's P bar 2 (not changed).**
- **Problem:** `e4 g8( fis8) b,2` gives up the C–B–C neighbour. On beat 2.5 the cello's F♯3 sits a semitone under the violas' held G3.
- **Reason it stands:** a literal `e4 dis8( e8) c4. b,8` would shadow the top's C–B in octaves an eighth apart.
- **Optional:** violas `g4 e4 a2` in 108 would remove the semitone rub. I did not test it, because the violas' 107–110 fill was checked for inner fifths in cycle 70.
