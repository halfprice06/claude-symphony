## Findings

### 1. Bars 73–74, Fl 1, the A♭-minor motto: put the swell on the held A♭5 (applied)

**Problem.**
- The flute plays the motto p espr.: C5 pickup, A♭5 dotted minim tied to a quaver.
- The diminuendo starts on the attack of A♭5, so the top of the rising sixth is the loudest moment and decays at once.
- The motto's other soft woodwind statements swell into the sixth:
  - B.cl at 2: `es2.~\< es8\> d8`.
  - C.a. at 5–6: `d'4(\p\< | bes'2.\>`.
- The flute statement has the same rhythm as bar 2, but it is the only one without the swell.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 185 and Ex. VII-2: the pickup C5 lies in the flute's "sweet, little carrying power" register, where "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume". A crescendo on the pickup (the C.a. model) would not sound.
- A♭5 is in the "clear" register, where the flute can swell. So the swell belongs on the held note (the B.cl model).
- Every flute melody in the chapter is shaped by a crescendo and diminuendo, not by a diminuendo from the attack: Ex. VII-4 (Brahms 4/iv, PDF 186), Ex. VII-5 (Tchaikovsky, PDF 186), Ex. VII-6 and VII-8 (PDF 187–188), and Ex. VII-9 (Brahms 1/iv <f> on the long notes, PDF 188).

**Protection.**
- c199 protects this statement "in tempo". The tempo is unchanged.
- Notes, the p, "espr." and the pp ending are unchanged.

**Fix** (clos.ily, closFluteOne):

```
r2. c''4(\p_\markup \italic "espr." | aes''2.~\< aes''8\> g''8 | ges''2 f''4 es''8 fes''8)\pp | R1 |
```

**Applied.** Page 8 renders a < under the A♭5 and the > from the tied quaver to the pp, as at bar 2.

### 2. Bars 153–155, cor anglais: slur the last phrase (applied)

**Problem.**
- C.a. 147–152 plays three slurred "dolce" fills with swells (c378).
- Its last phrase, C5 (whole bar) – G4 F4 – E4, has no slur. In the score these are three separately tongued notes, in a p dolce line moving in thirds with the violas' and cellos' E4–D4 at the cadence 154→155.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 187: "all separately notated pitches are tongued, while slurred ones are played legato (on one breath)".
- The soft, sustained cor anglais lines in the chapter are slurred through the phrase: Rossini "dolce" (Ex. VII-7, PDF 187) and Tristan (Ex. VII-49, PDF 206). Separate notes appear only in Berlioz's rhythmic mf phrase (Ex. VII-48).
- No other soft woodwind phrase in this score is left unslurred.

**Fix** (recTwo.ily, recTwoCorAnglais):

```
r2 r8 bes'8(\< a'8 g'8 | a'2\>) r2\! | c''1( | g'2 f'2 | e'2) r2 |
```

The level stays at p: the tune reaches p at 154, so the two lines meet at one level. **Applied.** Page 15 renders the slur from 153 to 155.

### 3. Bar 77, Cl 1 answer at mp on written D♭6 (no change)

**What I checked.**
- Cl 1 answers the bass clarinet's mf head three octaves up: E♭5–C♭6, written F5–D♭6, accented, at mp.
- Ex. VII-56 (PDF 210) calls written C♯6 and up "piercing, shrill", so the accented note is the first note of that register.

**Why it stays.**
- PDF 210: "a good clarinettist can play the entire dynamic spectrum ... extending to the highest range". mp is playable there.
- The mp is the point of the passage: the answer is lighter than the statement, then Hn 1·2 and Ob 1/Vln I come in mf at 78–79. The notes and the G♯5 spelling at 78 are protected (c231).

### 4. Bars 103–106, Ob 2 on the P head with Cl 1·2 at f (no change)

**What I checked.** PDF 201 warns that the oboe is a doubler that "can overshadow" a featured line. Here Ob 2 adds its edge to the P head while Hn 1, Bsn 1 and Vc carry the S head at mf with swells.

**Why it stays.**
- c357 protects the notes and the f/p levels of 103–110, and c397 protects the unison.
- The S head has three instruments in two families, with per-bar swells (c348, c388), against one oboe in its middle register (F4–C6, "warm, prominent", PDF 198).

### 5. Bars 33–36 and 144, Bsn 1 solos (no change)

**What I checked.** PDF 226 says an accompanied bassoon is easily swallowed, so the accompaniment must be marked lower.

**Why it stays.**
- 33–36: the solo is f "espr." falling to mf. The violas enter mp at 34 and the cellos p at 35.
- 144: Bsn 1 mf> pp over strings mp→pp (c262).
- Both are graded the way the reading asks, and the solo lies in the register Ex. VII-89 calls "sweet, more subdued but expressive".

### 6. Bars 175–179, bass clarinet without "dolce" (no change)

**What I checked.** The trombones and bass trombone are "pp dolce". The bass clarinet, doubling the bass trombone, has pp only.

**Why it stays.**
- PDF 217 describes the B.cl chalumeau as "mysterious and shadowy". The bare pp suits the chorale bass, and Adler's B.cl examples carry no word at pp (Ex. VII-74, VII-77).
- The cellos and basses doubling the same line also have no word.
- The coda's levels and text are protected (c228, c278).
