# Critic 459: holistic comparison with Elgar, Symphony No. 2 in E-flat, Op. 63/i (v459; also v449)

Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, examples (rimsky-principles PDF 376-440), third pass. I rendered every page from PDF 376 to 440 at 110 dpi and looked at each one. The OCR on these pages is captions and notation noise. The printed numbers run from the end of Ex. 209 (PDF 376) to Ex. 274 (PDF 440), so the range is Ex. 210-274, a little past the "201-260" in the task. Those examples belong to the text of ch. IV from "Tutti in the wind" to "Tone quality as a harmonic force" (PDF 120-134). I read those text pages in full, and I checked each principle below against the pictures.

Master: Elgar, Symphony No. 2, first movement, Allegro vivace e nobilmente. There is no copy in the local corpus (reference-corpus/README.md, line 64: "no Mahler, Elgar ..."). The facts about the master come from what I know of the score, checked against https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._2_(Elgar). That page confirms the following. The movement opens at once with the "Spirit of Delight" theme. Elgar wanted the second subject played very softly at its first entry, and it then rises by sequence from pp to ff. A soft cello song follows at fig. 11. The development opens with an eerie, ambiguous passage at fig. 24 (the "ghost" motive in the violins). A break after the first bar of fig. 42 comes before the recapitulation. The coda (fig. 61) starts calm and ends brilliantly. Because I had no score to look at, I make no claim below that depends on bar-level detail of the Elgar.

## The master comparison

1. **Opening.** Elgar starts at full speed with the main theme and has no introduction. v459 has a 14-bar Lento with the motto, as in Tchaikovsky 5/i or Elgar's own No. 1, not No. 2. This is a difference of plan, not a fault. No change.
2. **Second subject.** Elgar asks for the wistful second subject very soft at first and builds it by sequence from pp to ff. v459 does the same: horn + cello mp over harp and pad (47-55), the violins from 56, the peak at 64 and the rit. at 66-68. On this point the plan matches a master's.
3. **Cello song.** Elgar's slow cello song at fig. 11 has a counterpart in v459's Hn 1 + Vc S head (47-52) and the solo cello in the coda (171-174, R-K principle 4).
4. **Development.** Elgar opens the development with an eerie, ambiguous, soft passage, and that "ghost" passage is the part listeners remember. v459's development opens with soft solos over tremolo strings (77-81), but it reaches ff at 82 within five bars, and its quiet spots after that are short (84, 107-110). This is the one place where v459 is plainly less memorable than Elgar. See Finding 2 for why I do not change it.
5. **Into the recapitulation.** Elgar has six bars of falling leaps and a break before the recapitulation. v459 has the collapse at 123-126 and the stretto at 127-130 into 131. Both prepare the return with a drop and a fresh build. Equal in effect.
6. **Coda.** Elgar's coda starts calm and then ends brilliantly. v459 does the opposite: the forte coda 167-170, then a soft close at 171-182 and attacca. The symphony's plan needs this, because the finale owns the bright C-major ending (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). No change.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** On craft, yes. Every principle from this reading holds bar by bar, and the scoring of 27-32, 82-83, 103-106, 117-130, 158-162 and 171-182 matches Rimsky's examples. Next to Elgar No. 2, two things are thinner: the number of themes (Elgar gives four themes in the first sixteen bars), and the lack of one long, soft, strange episode in the development. The first is a matter of style. Brahmsian economy is one of the brief's models. The second is discussed below.

## Findings

No score change is proposed. Nothing was applied in a scratch copy, so no build was needed. I checked v459's own build: build-warnings.txt is empty, score.pdf has 18 pages, analysis.txt lists only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1", and v449/vl.txt and v459/vl.txt are identical.

### 1. v449 → v459: nothing got worse

The changes since v449 are the following:
- c455: lone whole-bar notes at 4, 76, 176 and 182 placed nearer the centre (score spacing only); Hn 1 and Hn 2 part tags end the 162 diminuendo before the rest at 163.
- c458: Cb crescendo at 169 into the ff stop chord at 170.
- c449-c457: no pitch changes. vl.txt is unchanged.

The c458 crescendo matches R-K PDF 130 (a crescendo carried by every sustaining group) and the treatment at 26 and 138. The c455 tags give the parts a defined end level, as R-K's examples mark one ("dim." to a level in Ex. 245 and 248, PDF 411 and 413). All are improvements or neutral. Keep.

### 2. 117-121: the development has no long soft episode like Elgar's "ghost" passage. Keep.

Elgar holds a soft, strange colour in the development long enough for it to become the movement's most haunting moment. v459's G pedal at 117 starts f (timpani f, trombone motto ff). Its soft colours are short: 77-81, 84 and 107-110.

I considered starting 117-121 at pp, with the motto in soft trombones over the timpani G and growing into 122. R-K's text would allow it: a stationary basis carrying contrasting chords (PDF 133, Ex. 267-271, PDF 433-438), and a crescendo by groups (PDF 130). I reject it for three reasons.
- (a) The f start at 117 is protected (c163 "G pedal with motto 117-130", c408 "117-121 brass motto", c448 "117 timpani f"). Moving it to pp would put the movement's softest brass passage straight before the fff at 122, so the climax would come from a four-bar crescendo instead of the c229 brass build.
- (b) The pp trombone colour belongs to the coda chorale at 175-179 (protected c149/c190), and using it at 117 would weaken that return.
- (c) Adding a soft episode of its own would change protected proportions (c433).

The soft centre that v459 has, 107-110 p with the S head in Vla + Fl 1 low over the B.cl's P head, is the right place for it, and it is already scored by R-K's rules (principles 4 and 14). No change.

### 3. 156-158: the heavy brass enters directly ff at 158 with no swell. Keep.

R-K PDF 127 (amplification) says a second group joins a crescendo from p and crescendos faster. Ex. 211 (PDF 378-379) shows brass entering mf cresc. two bars before a sf tutti. At 156-157 the strings, horns, harp and Cl 1 crescendo, and the trumpets, trombones, tuba, timpani, flutes and oboes all enter on the 6/4 at 158. A Tbn 1-2 p< swell at 157 would follow the letter of the rule. But "brass attack at 158" is protected (c149), and so is the "156-157 horn approach" (c448). The direct entry is also R-K's other model, the tutti stroke that takes over a crescendo (PDF 127, item 2, Ex. 244, PDF 410: brass p cresc. molto into a sf tutti). The horns are the second group here, and they already carry it. No change.

### 4. 47-55: two octaves open below the S tune. Keep.

R-K PDF 125 warns against outer parts far apart without filling. At 47.25-47.75, 50.25 and 55.75 the harp's A-flat1 or E-flat1 lies more than two octaves under the C4 tune. These are plucked bass touches under a held pad (Vla/Vln II), not two held outer parts. R-K's own Ex. 228 (PDF 396-397) and Ex. 229 (PDF 399) put harp bass notes the same distance under soft woodwind and string lines. Harp 47-55 is also protected (c397, c446). Follows. No change.

### 5. Imitation and echo by register (77-81, 175-177). Keep.

R-K PDF 128-129 says an answer higher up goes to a higher instrument, and woodwind does not echo strings.
- At 77-81 the dialogue rises in register and in instrument: B.cl E-flat3 → Cl 1 E-flat5, then Hn 1-2 B3 → Ob 1 B4, then the upper woodwind.
- At 175-177 Cl 1 takes the horn call an octave above Hn 1's 13-14.
- The C.a. phrases at 147-153 are separate fills, not echoes of the strings.

All follow. No change.

## Cross-movement effects

None.

## Already at a master's level

The pp-to-ff growth of the second subject (47-64), the wind tutti with pizzicato bass at 103-106, the wedge 111-116, and the coda from the stop chord at 170 to the five tied parts at 182 match Elgar's planning and R-K's examples. Do not touch them.
