## What I studied

Books: the assigned App. A in full (PDF 302–312, all pages as images). Supporting pages: Adler ch. 12 timpani (adler2 PDF 79–81); Adler ch. 9 brass breathing (adler1 PDF 292); Adler ch. 4 harp, "sons étouffés" (adler1 text line 5547, about PDF 117); and critic 156's Adler brass notes (c156.md). Corpus: I parsed the horn, trumpet, trombone and timpani parts of `musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml` with music21, at sounding pitch. Web: freepercussionlessons.com/timpani-ranges (drum by drum ranges and tone); a search across drumspy.com/timpani-ranges and bandworld.org for the same point. Score: I measured every part's range from a freshly built full.midi (one track per player), with bar numbers; measured the longest unbroken wind/brass stretches in seconds with the tempo map; listed every string double stop; and looked at PDF pages 5, 10 and 13.

Result of the range check: every part is inside Adler's professional chart. The score never goes below E1 in the basses or B♭0 in the contrabassoon. It uses the bass clarinet's low C extension and labels it. No D or E♭ clarinet or B♭ trumpet transposition problems. Only two places sit badly against the chart, and both are about how a note is placed, not whether it exists.

## Findings

**Finding 1 (applied). Bars 34–40 and 84–97, timpani: both retunings lower the C drum to its bottom or below it. The A♭ drum is free in both places and should take them.**
Problem: the tuning is G, A♭, C, E♭ (bar 1). At 34 "muta C in B♭" puts B♭2 on the C drum (25"/26"), and the drum rolls on it pp< at 40. At 84 "muta C in B♮" puts B2 on the same drum for the ff stroke at 97. Adler's chart (adler2 PDF 307) gives the 25" drum B♭2–F3, so B♭2 is its lowest pitch. The 28" drum (F2–C3) has both B♭2 and B2 inside its range. The web source agrees: "while the 26" drum is capable of playing a Bb, it really doesn't sound great. So if you have an option you should play that Bb on your 29" drum", and the 26" "doesn't sound very resonant below a C" (freepercussionlessons.com/timpani-ranges). The A♭ drum is idle in both places. A♭2 is last struck at 12 and next at 69, and last at 82 and next at 115. Cycle 166 left this open as an optional item ("B♭ at 40 on the 28" drum"). With this change the C drum stays at C for the whole movement, and the pp roll at 40 gets a resonant B♭.
Fix (applied, text only, no pitch changes):
- trans.ily: `R1^\markup \small "muta A♭ in B♭"` at 34; `R1^\markup \small "muta B♭ in A♭"` at 46.
- dev.ily: `R1^\markup \small "muta A♭ in B♮"` at 84; `R1^\markup \small "muta B♮ in A♭"` at 111 (A♭ is next needed at 115).
Adler ch. 12 (PDF 81) asks for tuning changes to be marked, and they still are.

**Finding 2 (not applied; reopens c156 F6 because its premise was wrong). Bar 106, Hn 1: written B5 (sounding E5) is exposed. No woodwind doubles it.**
Problem: Hn 1 doubles Bsn 1 and the cellos an octave higher at 103–106 and ends on `e''2\> dis''2`, which is written B5–A♯5 at mf with a diminuendo. On Adler's chart (PDF 305) this is one semitone under the professional top (written C6) and a major third above the amateur top (written G5). Cycle 156 kept it partly because "Cl 1 doubles the line (cycle 84), so a cracked note would not destroy the phrase". That is not what the score does. Cycle 84 put Cl 1 on the first-theme head with Cl 2 and Ob 2 (`r8^"a2" a'8(\f f''4.->)…`, dev.ily devClOne), and that is a different line. Nobody plays E5–D♯5 with the horn. Corpus: in the model first movements the horns stop lower. Tchaikovsky 6/i has its horns' top at sounding C5 (bars 73–75, 97, 134; written G5). Dvořák 9/i Hn 1·2 top out at sounding B4 (bars 57–58, 171–196), with one C♯5 at 224. Both stay at or under Adler's amateur ceiling, and this bar goes a major third above it, on a soft diminuendo, where control is hardest (Adler adler1 PDF 284, cited by c156).
Fix options, for the composer, because 103–110 are protected (cycles 74, 84, 173):
(a) Keep the note and add a safety doubling: Ob 1 is resting 103–110, so it can play `r2 r4 r8 b'8( | e''2\> dis''2)` at 105–106, at the horn's pitch and p, joining under the horn.
(b) Keep the scoring and write the part-only ossia `\tag #'part` "ossia 8va bassa" for bar 106 in Hn 1's part.
I recommend (a). It changes no pitch and no harmony, only adds one doubling for one and a half bars.

**Finding 3 (applied). Bars 131–142, Hn 1·2 and Hn 3: twelve bars at f–ff with one quarter rest.**
Problem: in the exposition Hn 1·2 rest until 23. In the recap they also play 131–134, so from 131 to 142 they hold f/ff notes with only the quarter rest at 138. My MIDI measurement gives 14.8 s unbroken for Hn 1 at 131–138 and 15.7 s for Hn 2 at 130–138, followed straight away by 139–142 ff. Adler (adler1 PDF 292, the basis of c156's brass breaths): brass need frequent rests, and loud playing must not put too many notes on one breath. The natural place is the end of 134. At that point the dynamic drops from f to mp, the hemiola starts with accents, and every horn re-attacks at 135.
Fix (applied, recOne.ily): `g'1 \breathe` (Hn 1), `f'1 \breathe` (Hn 2), `d'1 \breathe` (Hn 3) at the end of 134. Hn 4 already rests 135–137. Strings and woodwinds keep sounding, so the chord has no hole (c87 rule). On page 13 the commas print once per staff.

**Finding 4 (not applied; closes c168's open wording item if accepted). Bar 182, harp: "étouffez with Cb." mixes French and English in one instruction.**
Adler's term table (PDF 312) gives each instruction in one language per column. The harp's other damping marks are French: "étouffez" at 62 and 163, and "étouffez (m.d.)" at 164. Adler's harp chapter uses the French "sons étouffés", so the French belongs to the harp. The tam-tam's "damp" at 127 is English, which is normal for percussion, so that one can stay. Only the hybrid at 182 needs changing. Fix in coda.ily: replace `"étouffez with Cb."` with `"étouffez"`, keeping its right alignment at the end of the fermata. The harp then stops with the conductor's release, like the basses, and the "with Cb." is not needed. If the composer wants to keep the cue, use `"étouffez (avec les Cb.)"`.
