### 2. The silence after the stop chord at 170 should stay; Adler's overlapped forte-subito-piano does not belong there (keep)

Bars: 170–171.

Adler's sub. p device (adler2 PDF 190, Ex. XV-36) has a soft choir enter under the ff chord and stay, so that the piano is already sounding when the loud chord stops; "it is essential that the two chords overlap". c360 tested it against 61→62 and 83→84. The other strong drop in the movement is 170→171, the ff stop chord over C followed by the Più lento coda. Measured: on 170.4 nothing sounds (the fermata is on a rest), and 171.1 opens with Bsn 2 C3, violas C4 and the solo cello's E♭4, i of C minor, p/pp/mp.

An overlap here would be easy (Bsn 2 and violas entering pp on 170.3 under the ff chord). I recommend against it for two reasons. First, Adler's device keeps one harmony under a dynamic change; at 170 the harmony changes (vii°7 over C to i) and so do the tempo, the colour and the mood. Adler's own Monteverdi rule (adler1 PDF 20) says a change of mood is a change of combination, and the silence is what lets 171 be heard as a new section rather than the stop chord's quiet resolution. Second, the cyclic plan: in movement I the stop chord resolves directly only into minor (26→27, 138→139); at 170 it is left in silence, and the soft i at 171 arrives after the break, in another tempo. The finale's 222→223 is the one place where the same chord goes straight, ff and at pitch, to C major (Tpt 1 B4→C5, timpani G→C). Joining 170 to 171 without a break would give movement I a seamless resolution of the chord into C at its own end, which takes some force from the finale's arrival.

Fix: none.

### 3. The timpani–trumpet pair is separated only in the last bars, and the finale rejoins it (verified; keep)

Bars: 26–33, 82–83, 122, 138–141, 160, 167–170, 179–182.

Adler notes that in the classical orchestra the timpani "invariably played together with the trumpets" (adler1 PDF 21). Movement I follows that rule at every tutti arrival: timpani strokes or rolls with the trumpets at 26, the hammered `c4 r8 c8` with trumpets at 27–32, 82–83, 122, 138, 160 and 170. The trumpets' last note is the unresolved B4 at 170; from 171 they are silent, and the timpani alone carry the pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` at 179–180 and the C3 roll at 181–182. So the pair splits only in the coda, at the point where the leading tone is left hanging. The finale puts them back together exactly where that leading tone resolves: Tpt 1 B→C with timpani G→C at IV 222→223 and 262→263. This is a clear cyclic use of Adler's historical pairing and should stay. The held-note colouring of Ex. XV-37 (adler2 PDF 191) and the dovetail rule of Ex. XV-40 (PDF 192) are also met at the end: the snapshot at 181.1 has Hn 3's C4 entering while Tbn 1's C4 still sounds, so the C passes from trombone to horn without a seam, and from Hn 3 to movement II's Hn 4 across the attacca.

Fix: none.

### 4. The sforzandi at 41 use Adler's methods and set up the finale's E♭ minor (verified; keep)

Bars: 40–43.

Adler lists ways to make a sforzando more than an accent: a short sf trumpet note over long string notes, a short sf oboe note over long strings, pizzicato with a long wind note (adler2 PDF 189–190, Ex. XV-35, which I checked as an image). At 41 the trumpets, horns and trombones strike sf quavers while the flutes, oboes, clarinets and string tremolo hold sf> and the four horns begin the call. That is methods 2 and 3 combined. The bar's E♭ (bass E♭2 under Bsn 2's held B♭2) is the passing key that the finale turns into its real E♭ minor for F2 (IV 51), and the seed is recorded. Nothing to change.

Fix: none.

### 5. Where this movement could still fall short in the cyclic lens: my assessment (no change)

I looked for a place where a listener would miss a connection the later movements need. I considered three:

- **Foreshadowing the finale's F1/F2.** Not reopened. c260 and c330 rejected it with the Tchaikovsky 4 / Brahms 1 precedent (finale themes arrive new; the cyclic material is the motto and the stop chord), and nothing in v460 answers that reason differently. Adler's list of works to study names Tchaikovsky 4's finale (adler2 PDF 195) for exactly that kind of return of first-movement material, not for a preview of the finale's own tunes.
- **Bar 12 as the finale's opening chord.** The snapshot at 12.1 is a full f chord (A♭2 bass in Cb, Bsn 2, B.tbn, timpani; F♯3 Hn 3·4; E♭4 violas and Hn 1; A♭5 Fl 1/Vln I). The finale opens ff on the same German sixth, with Hn 4 F♯3 and violas E♭4 at the same pitches. A listener can hear the link without it being made louder in movement I; making 12 ff would bring the introduction's peak forward of the Allegro and spoil its pp start.
- **The motto's colours across the movement.** Adler's register-and-intent rule (adler2 PDF 188, Ex. XV-33, the Berlioz cor anglais solo) is met: no two statements share colour and register, and the one colour held back is bright full brass at forte in major, which is the finale's chorale (IV 224–228, 255–264). The C.a. statement at 5–8 is the instrument Adler's example uses for this register and mood.

None of these meets the bar of a real improvement. The movement's arc in this lens (A♭ reached three times at 82, 122 and 160 and never resolved to C major at forte; the stop chord left in silence at 170; the leading tone and the timpani–trumpet pair resolved only in the finale; a ppp C-major chord with the violas' E♮ handed into movement II's A♭) is consistent and complete.

Fix: none.
