# Critic 421: melody and memorability (series 4, cycle 421), v421

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 20 "Melodic Figuration", PDF 327–345 (third pass; c221, c311 and c401 read the same unit). I read every text page from PDF 327 to 345 in full and rendered all 19 pages at 110 dpi. I looked at every example as an image: Ex. 20-1 to 20-27, the Points for Review (PDF 343) and Exercises 1–4 (PDF 343–345).

The earlier passes checked the themes' figuration types (c221), chromatic spelling, the turn and the sevenths (c311), and octaves caused by figuration (c401, which led to the D♭ skip at 61). This pass therefore covered three things they did not check bar by bar:
1. Polyphonic melody (section 3, PDF 330, Ex. 20-3): where the tunes leave out a tone of an implied voice, does another part supply it?
2. Point 9a (PDF 343): fifths caused by *consonant* passing or neighbour tones. c401 counted these together with the dissonant ones.
3. PDF 336: dissonances approached or left by leap belong in the soprano. Do any inner parts have them?

For items 2 and 3 I wrote two scripts that read full.midi: /tmp/critic-421/scripts/beat58.py (beat-to-beat fifths and octaves with figuration between the beats, sorted by whether the figuration tone is consonant or dissonant) and /tmp/critic-421/scripts/freediss.py (every note that is dissonant against the sounding bass and is both approached and left by a leap). Their output is in /tmp/critic-421/beat58.txt and /tmp/critic-421/freediss.txt.

## Findings

The movement follows every principle in the unit that applies to it. All five findings below conclude with no change. They are ranked by how close each comes to being a real fault.

### F1. Bars 20.3–22: P's stepwise descent D♭6–C5 (keep; earlier reason confirmed with a new argument)

This is the one passage that all three earlier readers called the weakest stretch of P. The line falls ten notes by step: D♭6 C B♭ A♭ | G (dotted) F | E♭ D C B | C. Ex. 20-2d (PDF 329) says chordal skips "improve melodic lines by varying a prevailing stepwise movement", and that is a reason one could give for adding a skip here. c221, c311 and c401 kept the descent because of Ex. 20-10 (PDF 333): two passing tones fill a fourth, and an extended passing motion fills a larger span, provided some of its tones belong to the chord. Here D♭ and A♭ belong to the N6 at 21.1–2, G and F to V7 at 21.3–4, and B and D to V at 22, so the condition holds.

The new point is that the movement already writes the varied form of this descent. At 31 (hammered tutti) and 141–142 (recapitulation) the same descent becomes A♭–G–F–**D**–C–B, with the F–D chordal skip that Ex. 20-2d describes. This is the procedure of Exercise 4 (PDF 345): first the plain theme, then variations with other figuration. If a skip were added at 21, the exposition would sound the same as its own variants, and the listener would lose the change at 31. 31–32 are quoted at pitch in the finale (199–204), so the skip form has to stay there. Keep 20–22 as written.

### F2. Bars 67→68: the tune leaves the 7th of V7 by a skip (keep)

At 67 Vln I and Vln II play A♭5 (tied) G F E♭ D♭5 B♭4, and reach A♭4 at 68 over the F-minor chord of the deceptive cadence. The 7th of V7 (D♭5) falls a third to B♭4 instead of a step to C. Ex. 20-18 (PDF 339) is the case where "the 7th of V7 does not resolve". The large-scale line shows that it does resolve. Cl 1 and Hn 2 play D♭4 on 67.4 and go to C4 at 68 (`bes'2 g'4 des'4 | c'1`), and the violas hold C4 at 68. The tune's D♭5 doubles that resolving voice an octave higher, and leaves it in the way Ex. 20-3b (PDF 332) allows for an implied tone the context supplies. The tune's 4̂–2̂–1̂ (D♭–B♭–A♭) puts A♭ on top of the F-minor chord, the third of vi, which is the colour of the deceptive cadence. Making it D♭–C would put the tune in octaves with the Cl 1/Hn 2 resolution and land on 5̂ of F minor, which is weaker. Keep.

### F3. Bars 23–26 and 135–138: the implied upper line E♭–F–G–A♭ → B (keep)

The groups of three crotchets (E♭ D C | F E♭ D | G F E♭ | A♭ G F) form a polyphonic melody (PDF 330). The tops rise E♭5–F5–G5–A♭5 and continue to B5 at the stop chord, so the implied upper voice has an augmented second, A♭→B. Section 15 (PDF 340) says that the ascending line in minor normally uses raised 6̂, but "the larger harmonic context sometimes leads to the use of lowered 6̂ and 7̂ ascending". Here the harmony is iv – iiø7 – vii°7, and every one of these chords contains A♭. A♭5 does not move to B. Vln II takes over A♭5 at 26 at the same pitch (`<d'' aes''>2.`), and B5 enters in Vln I as a new voice of the vii°7. So the augmented second never sounds inside one voice. The same is true at 137–138. Keep.

### F4. Bar 18: C5 over V7 left by leap to G4 (keep)

In P's second bar pair (`b'4 d''8 c''8 g'2`), C5 on 18.2-and is a fourth over the dominant (bass G, Vln II F4, violas D4) and falls a fourth to G4. Read literally, that is a dissonance left by leap without resolution. c311 read B–D–C as a double neighbour of C (Ex. 20-12, PDF 334). The reading on PDF 330 gives the fuller explanation. The C goes to B, and the violas supply that B on 18.3-and (`r8 b8~ b4`), the same procedure as Schubert's omitted B♭ in Ex. 20-3b. The leap to G4 then gives the tune its low register for the pickup into 19. This bar also carries the rhythm of the scherzo's motive x (mvt1.md seeds), so it should not change. Keep.

### F5. Inner parts: no free dissonance by leap and no consonant-figuration fifths (keep)

PDF 336 says that dissonances approached or left by leap draw attention, so they belong in the soprano. freediss.py found 84 notes that are dissonant against the bass and are both approached and left by a leap. All of them are chord sevenths or tones of augmented-sixth chords inside arpeggios:
- 59, 64, 67 and 165: cello arpeggios
- 70.3–70.4: viola C♭5 inside D–F–A♭–C♭
- 71.4: oboe and trumpet D♭5–F5 inside V7
- 101: violas, B minor
- 117–120: Cl 2 and viola triplets on the G pedal
- 105.4: Hn 1, Bsn 1 and cellos arpeggiate through B (G–B–E)

None is a non-chord tone. Point 9a (PDF 343) says fifths caused by a consonant passing or neighbour tone are almost always bad. beat58.py found five beat-to-beat fifths with figuration between the beats. Three are quick and weak in position (71.4 Vla/Vc; 120.4 Fl 2/Vla, twice). At 165.4 Bsn 1 and the violas move G2/D4 → C3/G4, and the violas' quaver B3 between them is a chordal skip, which can break fifths (Point 10). None comes from a consonant passing or neighbour tone. The 263 octave cases are all doublings in which one part figurates (PDF 344, footnote), as c401 found. Keep.

### Would a listener hum them afterwards?

Yes, for three of the four ideas.
- **Motto.** It is a rising minor sixth and a chromatic fall, the C–D♭–C neighbour enlarged as in Ex. 20-11. It is short and appears in at least eight colours.
- **P.** Its leaps widen bar by bar, sixth to seventh to octave, and break through to D♭6 at 20. It has the head-and-sequence shape of Tchaikovsky's Fifth and Brahms's First, and its neighbour cells at 16 and 18 make each second bar easy to recall.
- **S.** It grows from the motto's sixth turned major (E♭–C at 46–47, A♭–F at 48, E♭–C at 63) in the Dvořák manner. Its later forms add exactly the figuration Exercise 4 asks for: the turn at 56, the run at 152, and the C–A♭ minor sixth of the climax at 160.
- **Horn call.** It is the least singable of the four, a two-note sixth with an unresolved B. It is meant as a signal, not a tune, and it is protected because movement II and the scherzo fill its sixth.

Nothing in the reading points to rewriting a theme.
