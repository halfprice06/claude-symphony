## Verdict
A conductor would accept the architecture. Its large design is Tchaikovsky 5's:
- the exposition closes by turning its major key to minor (73–76, like T5 218–219);
- the recapitulation's second group turns to the tonic minor before the coda (162, like T5 475–476);
- the coda stays in the tonic minor and ends ppp, low.

The movement also has an Eroica-type "problem note":
- the D♭ peak at 20 (sf), whose consequences are 132, 169 and 176;
- the E♭/E♮ pivot (8–9), whose consequences are 147–148, 162 and 178.

Its surprises are comparable in number to the Eroica's: the A♭ crash at 82, the semitone lift at 97, the fff tam-tam at 114, the major motto at 147–148, V–♭VI at 154–156, the denied tutti at 166 and the viola's E♮ at 178. The tell that remains is rhetorical, not structural: the music never contradicts what it has prepared. Findings 1 and 3 address that.

## Findings

### 1. Every crescendo arrives (applied: 62–64)
**Problem.** In v78, 0 of 213 crescendo marks resolve to a softer dynamic. Only 31% of rises to f/ff/fff are subito; the rest are approached by a hairpin.

**Evidence.** Counts are from the source files with the same script for each work:

| | Crescendos landing softer (strict) | Crescendos landing same or softer | Rises to f/ff/fff that are subito |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eroica/i, all parts | 12 of 308 | 76 of 308 | 57% |
| Dvořák 7/i (string, wind, timp. part files) | 49 of 563 | not counted | 53% |
| v78 | 0 of 213 | 4 of 213 | 31% |

Concrete Eroica cases (Violin1MvtI.lyi, confirmed from MIDI velocities):
- the crescendo of 13–14 lands p at 15;
- sf with diminuendo at 10, then p at 11;
- the coda's crescendo 627–630 opens p at 631 for the final statements of the theme.

A score in which every build pays off sounds predictable even when each passage is well made.

**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily).** The crescendo 58–61 now resolves to a subito p on the return of the theme's head at the octave (62). The f is reached at 64, where cycle 58 already put the 64–65 slur at f. The recapitulation's 154 then becomes the first ff statement of the head at the top, which strengthens the protected climax.
- At the top of tTwo.ily: `subP = #(make-dynamic-script (markup #:line (#:normal-text #:italic "sub." #:dynamic "p")))`.
- Vln I: `es''4\subP( c'''2.~ | c'''4)\< des'''4( bes''2) | aes''2.~(\f aes''8 g''8) |`. Vln II likewise, an octave lower.
- Fl1: `\partCombineSoloI es''4\p(^\markup \bold "1." c'''2.~ | c'''4\< ... | aes''2.~(\f ...`. The explicit "1." was needed because the label was lost.
- Changes at 62, 63 and 64:

### 2. The recapitulation's problem note gives colour but no change of course (not applied)
**Problem.** At 132, D♭ is one ff bar between F minor and V.

**Evidence.** In both models the recapitulated first group is where the opening's foreign note redirects the music:
- Eroica: C♯ at 402, then F major at 408 and D♭ at 416 (8 bars of new keys);
- Tchaikovsky 5: F♯ minor at 364–372.

This is the largest remaining gap from the models.

**Possible change.** Hold D♭ for two inserted bars after 132, re-using 20's figure: Vln I `des'''8 es''' f''' ges''' aes'''2~ | aes'''4. ges'''8 f'''4 des'''4` over `des,1 | des,1`. Then continue as at 133.

Not applied: it changes the protected two-wave 123–134, adds bars (proportions, rule from cycle 43) and moves page breaks. It is listed for the composer's judgment only.

### 3. One hinge, four times: G octave + timpani roll diminuendo (applied at 84)
**Problem.** 13–14, 84, 115–118 and 136–138 all join sections with the same scoring: a held G, horns, a timpani roll on G and a diminuendo to pp. (119–122 is the same roll in crescendo.)

**Evidence.** Beethoven's hinges differ every time:
- strings alone on B7, diminuendo, at 280–283;
- the horn's early tonic at 394–395 followed by ff;
- block key shifts at 557–561.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily, bar 84; unprotected).** The ff hammer at 83 is now followed by a bare G octave subito p:
- Vc `g,1\p`, Cb `g,,1\p`;
- Hn3·4 rest at 84 (`R1*2` for 84–85);
- Timp rests, with "muta C in B♮" moved to 84;
- Vc 85 loses its `\!`.

The other three hinges stay. They are protected and are the intended rhymes.

### 4. Coda: combines and transforms, but only with devices the development used (no change)
- The minor second theme (167) was heard in B minor at 97.
- The trombone augmentation (171) was used at 109–113.
- The brass head (163) was heard at 27 and 82.

New are the denied tutti, the simultaneous second theme and motto, and the pp chorale colour. The Eroica's coda adds new key moves (557–561). Tchaikovsky 5's does not: its coda re-states P in the tonic minor and fades. With T5's arc (major only in the finale), the restraint is correct.

### 5. The Eroica horn entry already has an analogue (no change)
At 119–120, Cl1 plays the T1 head pp over the G pedal. Its accented E♭ sounds against the violas' held B♮ (from cycle 57). That is tonic material over the dominant, as at Eroica 394–395, at a lower volume. Do not add a horn there. The texture is right.

### 6. Bassoon solo on the head after a loud close, twice (not applied)
33 and 77 are both "1. Solo espr." mf bassoon heads, 44 bars apart. Option: Hn3 at 33–34 (`r8 g8\mf( es'4.->) d'8( des'8 c'8 | c'4) bes8( aes8) g4 f4`), Bsn1 from 35. Low priority.

### 7. Cadence predictability (no change)
In the exposition's second group every cadence is root-position ii–V(4–3)–I with a one-bar suffix (54–55, 66–68); the recapitulation evades it (V–♭VI 155–156, vi 158). Fine.
