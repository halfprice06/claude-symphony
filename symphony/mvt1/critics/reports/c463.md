## Findings

The first two passes checked every 6/4 and the main arrivals. This pass asked a different question, as the task requires: where, within form and pacing, does the movement fall short of a master's, and does the reading support a change there? I tested the places where the reading's two strongest form tools (expanding a cadential 6/4, and interpolating between a 6/4 and its resolution) could make a cadence land harder, and I checked the edits made since v353 that touch a 6/4. I recommend no change to the score. Each finding states why, so later cycles do not reopen the point without answering it.

### 1. The exposition keeps its cadential 6/4 short and the recapitulation expands it; this contrast should stay (no change)

What the reading says. PDF 317: "The tension created by the cadential 6/4 can be enhanced by expanding it" (Ex. 19-21, a bass arpeggio under the 6/4 with a crescendo). PDF 318–319: material interpolated between the 6/4 and its resolution is the basis of the cadenza (Ex. 19-23, 19-24).

What the score does. The second group's first cadence in the exposition is a plain one-bar cadential 6/4 of A♭ at 62 (bass E♭2, sub. p, poco animando), V7 at 63, then deceptive vi at 64; a second, metrically weak 6/4 at 66.3 leads through V7 at 67 to another deceptive vi at 68, and the full cadence comes only in the codetta at 72. In the recapitulation the same place becomes the climax: 158 is a whole-bar cadential 6/4 of C with the full brass, 159 V7, and then the reading's interpolation, ♭VI (160, fff) – vii°7/V–V (161) – vi (162) – IV (163) – ii (164) – a second, weak 6/4 (164.3) – V7 (165) – I (166). The exposition's two deceptive cadences return as the recapitulation's ♭VI and vi, and the 6/4's resolution, which the exposition reaches in eleven bars by way of a new section, is reached in eight bars inside one span.

The candidate change I tested in thought and rejected: expanding the exposition's 6/4 at 62 by a bass arpeggio (Ex. 19-21) so that the second group's first cadence "lands harder". It would add a bar (sections.txt, every later bar number, and the other movements' citations would change), it would spend at 62 the device the score saves for 158, and it would weaken the contrast between the two cadences that makes 158–166 sound like the goal of the movement. In Ex. 19-24 Beethoven also states the plain cadence first and keeps the expanded 6/4 for the end of the movement. Keep 62–68 and 158–166 as they are.

### 2. The coda's cadential 6/4 at 174 is already expanded across the bar line (no change)

What the reading says. PDF 323 (Ex. 19-30b, Schumann "Eusebius"): a 6/4 after the cadential V can stand for I. PDF 320–321 (Ex. 19-26): a 6/4 may come first and be stabilized afterwards by its 5/3. PDF 317–318 (Ex. 19-22, the end of Brahms's Requiem): the bass of a final 6/4 can be prolonged.

What the score does. At 174 the cadential 6/4 lasts two beats (Vc C5→B4, violas E♭4→D4, timpani and Bsn 2 G2) and V two beats; the cor anglais's C5 doubles the cello's 4th in unison and stops, so only one voice resolves (PDF 317: no octaves arise). At 175 the bass stays on G (Cb G1, bass trombone, bass clarinet, cellos G2) while the upper voices take C–E♭–G: a tonic 6/4 over the dominant bass, exactly the Eusebius chord. The chorale bass then falls G–E♭–D–C (the motto) and the root-position tonic arrives at 178–179, which stabilizes the 6/4 of 175 retrospectively, as in Ex. 19-26. So the last cadence of the movement is expanded, not by more bars on 174, but by keeping G in the bass from 174 to 175 and delaying the root-position tonic three bars.

I considered prolonging 174 itself (Ex. 19-22) and rejected it: the coda notes 171–182 are quoted by the other movements and protected, and the expansion the reading asks for is already there in another form. Keep 174–178.

### 3. Two seams in the development were checked again against this reading; both stay (no change)

Bars 101–102. The B-minor second theme ends with V7 of B (101, E in Hn 3), a B-minor 6/4 over the same F♯ bass (102.1–2), and V7 again (102.3–4). This is Mozart's "Mi tradì" pattern on PDF 312–313 (figures 7 – 6/4 – 7): a passing 6/4 over a held dominant that does not resolve the 7th. The swerve to D minor at 103 then leaves E unresolved on purpose (c123 and c322 closed this; "Vc 101–102" and the 102→103 seam are protected). The reading supports the 6/4 as written; the 7th's non-resolution belongs to the protected swerve, not to the 6/4.

The station plan 97–122. PDF 251 says rising 3rds other than I–III–V usually need applied dominants between. The stations B (97) – D (103) – F (111) – A♭ over G (122) rise in minor 3rds. D→F has its applied dominant (C major at 110 as V of F minor; cadential 6/4 of F at 110.1–2). B→D has none: V of B moves straight to D minor. That is a chromatic third relation, a 19th-century colour the reading does not forbid (PDF 254 and 258 note other 19th-century third progressions), and it sits on the protected 102→103 seam. F→A♭/G goes through the stepwise wedge 111–116, an expanded passing motion (PDF 257). The plan spells the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ onto V (critic 430's record). Keep it.

### 4. The 6/4s touched by edits since v353 all keep a clear function (no change)

What the reading says. PDF 324: be ready to explain the function of every 6/4.

What the score does. Since v353 these edits touched a 6/4: the coda cor anglais holds D♭5 two beats at 173 and C5 on the 6/4 at 174 (c400; Finding 2); Vln II B4→C5 at 154–155 completes V7 after a one-beat cadential 6/4 of C (c402: 154.1 C–E–G over G2, 154.2 V7, 155 I with E4 on top, an imperfect cadence at pp, so the movement's first C-major cadence stays soft, as the cyclic plan requires); Hn 3 B3–C4–B3 at 124–126 inside the i6/4 of 125 (neighbouring 6/4, PDF 308); the cellos' tremolo A♭3–G3 at 122–123 (c444) is the VI-over-V neighbour of PDF 258, not a 6/4. The beat reduction finds no 6/4 that was not already accounted for by c263 and c353.

### 5. Proportion check against the corpus (no change)

The reading's claim that a cadential 6/4 is a delay of the expected event (PDF 307), and that a 6/4 over a held dominant can be elaborated at length (PDF 313–315), gives a way to compare pacing: how long a master keeps tonic notes over a dominant bass before a structural arrival. In the MCTL annotation of Tchaikovsky 6/i (harmony.csv) the D-major close of the exposition holds i6/4 over A for 8 + 2 + 20 crotchets (onsets 770–802, with one bar of v between) before the tonic. Mozart 41/i states its cadential I6/4 of C five times between onsets 144 and 182 in its closing area. Here the G pedal 125–130 alternates tonic notes over G (i6/4 at 125, C/E♭ over G at 127–130 in the motto stretto) with V for six bars (24 crotchets) before the recapitulation at 131, where Hn 2's F4 resolves to E♭4 (c353 Finding 2). The delay is of the same order as Tchaikovsky's and is placed at the movement's main structural arrival. Nothing in the proportions calls for a change; section lengths are protected (c243 and later).
