## What the series changed, and whether the movement reads as a master's work
**v20 (before the series):** 180 bars and 15 pages. The material was all there: motto, first theme, second theme rising by sixths, codetta, development with fugato and G pedal, recap, and a coda ending on the viola E♮. v20/critique.md listed its own weaknesses: long off-beat viola chords, brass that mostly sustain, one-cell monotony, a 14-bar pedal, and a coda that was a loud repeat of the head followed by the cello theme and the motto.

**v20 → v98 (cycles 1–78):** this is where the real changes were made.
- **Voice leading:** cleaned bar by bar. The analyser now finds no outer-voice parallels except the known doubling at 96, and inner fifths were removed at about 20 places.
- **Motivic and cyclic design:**
  - the E♭/E♮ and ♭VI threads;
  - the cor anglais motto in intro and coda;
  - the augmented trombone chorale;
  - the theme combination 103–110.
- **Expression and orchestration:**
  - graded brass;
  - the swells in the second theme;
  - the subito p at 62 and 84;
  - the fermata on the denied tutti at 170;
  - the tam-tam and cymbal plan;
  - oboe and flute doubling of Vln I cut from about 74% of bars to about 50%.
- **Engraving and playability:** brought to a professional standard (part-combine, dynamics engravers, breaths, harp pedals, divisi).

**v98 → v118 (cycles 79–98):** mostly refinement.
- The one structural change was the 4-bar cut in the recap second group (cycle 80; 186 → 182 bars), which removed a literal repeat of the violins' head.
- Everything else was small: breaths, labels, part cues, final-chord spacing, Cl 1 breath at 167, bass clarinet solo at 107–109, and dynamic grading at 41–42 and 96.
- I compared every v98→v118 diff in the coda, development and recap and found nothing that got worse.

**Comparison with Coriolan:** v118's large-scale design matches Beethoven's C-minor plan closely.
- **Loud stop:** Coriolan's coda breaks off its fortissimo with general pauses (Vln I 276–289: unison C held, chord, bar of rest, repeated). v118 does the same at 167–170 with the fermata rest.
- **Dissolving the head:** Coriolan then dissolves the head in augmentation, Vln I 296–309 `c1~ | c2 b2 | c1~ | c2 b2 …` sempre più piano. v118's chorale 175–179 and violins 177–182 state the motto in augmentation in the same way.
- **Ending:** Coriolan ends on three pp pizzicato Cs (310–314). v118 ends on a ppp C with the viola E♮.
- **Second theme in the coda:** Coriolan's second theme comes back in the coda before the final collapse (Vln I 243–246 `e2.( f4 | e d b g)`). v118 does this at 171–174, where the cello carries the second theme in C minor against the cor anglais.

**Verdict:** a conductor reading v118 would accept it as the work of a skilled, well-schooled late-Romantic composer.
- **Professional level:** form, proportions, cyclic thinking, part-writing and scoring.
- **What separates it from a master's first movement:** three things, all already recorded in HISTORY.md as settled or held over for movements II/III:
  - block-chord tuttis with one chord per bar over a root-position bass (27, 69, 131, 167);
  - the rising sixth everywhere;
  - a harmonic language that stays inside textbook progressions.
- **What I found to fix:** after reading every section against the do-not-touch lists, I found no musical weakness in movement I that is both important and fixable without reopening a settled decision. The weakness I can fix is in how the score presents its two most important arrival points to the conductor.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 122–126 and 73–76: the climax and the flute motto print as slivers. APPLIED in /tmp/critic-099/work (global.ily only).**
- *Problem:*
  - The forced breaks give page 11 a 12-bar system (119–130), and the first three of those bars are full of triplet eighths. I measured bar widths from the bar-number positions on the A3 page. Bar 119 is 115 pt wide, but the fff A♭6 climax with the tam-tam (122) is 29 pt, and the collapse bars 123–126 are 31–44 pt.
  - Page 6 is a 13-bar system (64–76). The flute's A♭-minor motto (73–76, protected cyclic material) gets 28–46 pt per bar, against 101 pt for bar 64.
- *Evidence:*
  - In Gould's proportions (whole note 7 spaces, eighth 2½), a whole-note bar should get about a quarter of the width of a bar of twelve triplet eighths. That is close to what LilyPond gives here, which shows the cause is not the spacing rule.
  - The systems are overfull, so every column is pressed to its minimum. In that case changing `spacing-increment` barely helps. I tested `\newSpacingSection` with increment 1.6–2.6 on the old breaks: bar 122 only went from 29 to 33 pt.
  - Larger increments also made LilyPond reflow pages 2–4 (15–46) and warn "too few pages: 17 (should have at least 19)".
- *Fix (global.ily):*
  - Move the break before 119 to before 121, so pages 10 and 11 hold 111–120 and 121–130.
  - Move the break before 77 to before 73, so page 6 holds 64–72 and page 7 holds 73–84.
  - Code: `s1*2 s1*2 \break \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" …`, and in the closing `s1*4 \break s1*4 \bar "||" \key c \minor`.
- *Result:*
  - Bar 122 is now 44 pt and 123–126 are 45–57 pt; bar 121 is 131 pt.
  - Bars 73–75 are 69, 41 and 56 pt.
  - Pages 2–5, 8–9 and 12–17 are unchanged.
  - The allargando, climax, collapse and a tempo now sit on one page, and the conductor's page turns fall at 120/121 (ff, as before at 118/119) and 72/73 (after the codetta's final chord).
  - score.midi is byte-identical to v118's. This reverses part of cycle 85's break list (the break before 119); that break was not on any do-not-touch list.

**2. Optional, tested, not applied: spacing sections on top of finding 1.** On the new breaks, adding `\newSpacingSection \override Score.SpacingSpanner.spacing-increment = #2.4` at 122 and at 73, each with a `\revert` in a new section at 127 and 77, widens 122 to 51 pt and 73–75 to 87, 54 and 71 pt. The build stays clean and there is no reflow. I left it out because the gain is small and the setting is fragile (see 3).

**3. Bars 15–46: the layout depends on other systems. Advice only.** In four of my test builds, changes on pages 6 and 11 alone reflowed 15–46 from 8+8 / 8 / 8 bars per system to 10 / 11 / 11. That put the 27–32 tutti into an 11-bar system. Any later layout edit should check pages 2–4. Adding `\break` before 23, 31 and 39 would fix the present layout in place (untested).

**4. Musical findings: none of first rank.** I checked 47–68, 97–130, 131–166 and 167–182 against Coriolan and against the HISTORY.md rules. What remains open (tutti layout, root-position bass, rising-sixth saturation, thin-texture share) is recorded there as settled or held over for movements II/III, and I have no new evidence to reopen it.
