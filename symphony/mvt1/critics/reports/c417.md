## Findings

All six findings are "keep". After 400 cycles the contrapuntal places that this chapter addresses already follow it. Changes that look possible conflict with protected passages for reasons that still hold. I made no change to the score. The scratch copy (/tmp/critic-417/work) builds unchanged: 0 warnings, 18 PDF pages, analysis.txt shows only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1".

### 1. 139–142: the held brass block could punctuate instead of holding. Keep as it is.

**What the reading suggests.** Hindemith's brass punctuate with short chords and rests while the theme is elsewhere (PDF 42, 44, mm. 189–196). Adler calls this "a most common use of the brasses". At 139–142 twelve brass players hold whole-note chords at f, then ff at 140. Hn 1 doubles Tpt 1 (A♭4, G4), Hn 2 doubles Tpt 2 (F4, D4), and Hn 3 doubles Tbn 1 and, from 141, Tpt 3. The tune (Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I·II), the tenor line (Vc, Bsn 1, Tbn 2) and the violas' neighbour figure C–B–C / F–E–F all move inside or next to that block. The punctuation model is right there in the exposition: the trumpets play `c4 r8 c8 c4 r8 c8` with the timpani at 27–30 and 131–134.

**Why it should stay.** Hindemith's brass punctuate a theme that the timpani present. At 139–142 the timpani do not present a theme: they roll at 140, strike at 141 and play G2 strokes at 142. So the 27-rhythm would be a new figure, not a rhyme. The broad held chord on F minor – D♭ (N6) – V is the recapitulation's change from the exposition's hammering. c283 kept it as "dense 139–141 below the 158 arrival", c354 kept the held brass at f, and c364 protected 139–142. The line that was really buried, the tenor line, got its trombone at c317 (Adler PDF 42, 44). The violas' figure is an accompaniment figure, not a line that has to be heard. Not applied.

### 2. 167–169: the Bsn 1/Vc counter lies in the same octave as the horns' and violas' head. Keep as it is.

At 167–169 the P head is in Tpt 1·2 a2 (G4–E♭5), with Hn 1·2 a2 and the violas an octave lower (G3–E♭4). Bsn 1 and the cellos play C4–E♭4 | E♭4–F4 | E♭4–C4 in the same octave as Hn 1·2 and the violas, and they cross the head throughout. Tbn 1 (E♭4/C4), Hn 3 (C4) and Cl 2 (E♭4) hold notes in the same octave. By the "best register" rule (PDF 42) the counter will not be heard as a line. But the head is carried clearly by the trumpets an octave above, so the subject still "comes through clearly". In this octave the counter works as a moving part of the chord. It also rhymes with the Bsn 1/Vc line at 27–30. The passage is protected repeatedly (c254, c279, c337, c349, c367, c370), and the stop chord at 170 is quoted by the finale. Not applied.

### 3. Trombones mostly double the horns in the tuttis. Keep as it is.

Schoenberg's trombones are independent of the trumpets and winds (PDF 44). In v417, Tbn 1 doubles Hn 3 at 27–31 and 139–142. The only independent trombone lines are Tbn 2 at 139–141, Tbn 1 at 159 and Tbn 2 at 178. My doubling test gives the same picture as c317's. The brass texture is Dvořák's (9/i: trombones 69 bars with two or more attacks, 4–6 of them independent), not Tchaikovsky's. That is a valid neo-Romantic choice. The bars where independence would matter are protected: 27–32 is quoted at pitch by the finale at its 199–204, and 117–121 is a cantus firmus in unison by design. Splitting the trombones elsewhere would only produce more held chord notes, not lines. Not applied.

### 4. 88–96: the fugato's layering. Keep as it is.

Hindemith marks the entering voice mf and drops the others to p, with rests (PDF 43, mm. 165–181). The fugato does this for the first two entries: Vc mf at 85; at 88 Vla + Bsn 1 mf, with the cello's countersubject mp. At 91–96 every voice is f (kept by c237). The reading's purpose is still met, because the third and fourth entries (Vln II + C.a. at 91, Vln I + Fl 1 at 94) are each the highest sounding voice. A top voice in its best register does not need the others to drop.

The cello's countersubject at 88–90 lies above the violas' subject (C4–G4 against G3–E♭4). By the "best register" rule this is the one place where the subject could be covered. It should stay: Bsn 1 doubles the subject at pitch, the cello is a level softer (mp against mf), and the cello's B3→C4 across 87–88 joins its own subject to its countersubject (c227, c327). Moving the countersubject down an octave breaks that join and puts it below the subject in the cello's dull C3 region. Not applied.

The cello's held B♭3–D♭3 at 94–95, with the Bsn 2/B.cl/Cb bass under a crescendo, matches Bartók's held, crescendoing lower voices under the stretto (PDF 47, mm. 378–386). It is not a dead voice. Not applied.

### 5. The bass instruments never take the subject. Keep as it is.

The tuba carries the fugue subject in Hindemith (PDF 43, m. 168ff), and Trb III takes it in Bartók's stretto (PDF 47, m. 376). Adler praises Schoenberg for giving "very significant parts" to the tuba, double bass and contrabassoon (PDF 44). In v417 the tuba plays only the bass, and the fugato's bass voice joins only as harmony from 90. Two items on the "Do not reuse" list in coherence/mvt1.md reserve this for the finale: a tuba melody and a full low-brass unison in the bass. The low instruments do get thematic work in movement I: the motto in B.cl, Vc and Cb at 1–4; B.cl on the P head at 107–109; the bass motto tail by whole bars at 111–116; B.tbn in the brass motto at 117–121. A bass fugato entry would change the protected entry plan D–G–C–F (c142, c143). Not applied.

### 6. Places that follow the reading closely (for the record)

- **80–82.** The motto's rising sixth G–E♭ passes from Bsn 1, Vc and the low brass (G2, then G3) to Ob, Cl and Vln I (G4), then Fl, Vln I (G5), each a half bar later, into the ff tutti over A♭ at 82. This is imitation rising by register into the tutti, as in Bartók's stretto (PDF 44, 47). Adler sets plain antiphony aside (PDF 41), and this is not antiphony.
- **117–121.** The augmented motto in Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn is a cantus firmus. The P head is imitated above it at half-bar distance in flutes, oboes and violins, with the clarinet/viola triplets as a third layer. It is the Stravinsky arrangement turned upside down (PDF 42): the long-note voice in the brass, the florid voices around it.
- **127–130.** The half-augmented motto in stretto (Cl 1, Vla at 129.3, Vln II + Bsn 1 at 130) over the cellos' G2 speeding up from crotchets to semiquavers, into the tutti at 131. Entries tighten and the tutti takes over, as at Bartók mm. 363–386 (PDF 47).
