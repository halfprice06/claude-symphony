The brass texture is close to Dvořák's: the brass double the woodwinds and strings, punctuate, and hold chords. It is not like Tchaikovsky's, whose trombones carry their own lines through his development (bars 196–205, 236–269). That is a valid neo-Romantic choice, and much of it is protected: the unison motto 117–121, the chorale 175–179, and the layouts 27–32 and 167–170. I do not ask for a Tchaikovsky-style brass development. I found one place where a line that already exists is buried inside the held brass, and a trombone that only doubles can carry it.

## Findings

### 1. 139–142: the tenor counterline is inside the held brass chord. Give it to Tbn 2. (Applied in the scratch copy.)

**Bars.** 139–142, recapitulation, P continuation in F minor, f< to ff.

**What is wrong.** c157 added a tenor line in contrary motion to the tune for Vc and Bsn 1: C4–B♭3 | A♭3–F3 | (F3) G3 A♭3 C4 | C4–B3. It lies between F3 and C4. In the same octave, the brass hold the chord at f–ff: Tbn 1 C4 / D♭4 / D♭4–C4 / C4–B3, Hn 3 the same four notes, Tpt 3 F4 / F4 / D♭4–C4 / C4–B3, Tbn 2 A♭3, Hn 4 F3. All five held brass notes are within a sixth of the moving line, and two of them, C4 and A♭3, are its own pitches. One bassoon and a cello section moving inside eight held brass parts at ff will not carry. The line is there on paper but is not heard, which is the opposite of Adler's Hindemith rule, "all the instruments are in their very best registers, so that the subject comes through clearly every time it is played" (PDF 42). Tbn 1 adds nothing to the chord, because Hn 3 already plays every one of its notes (and Tpt 3 joins them from 141). Adler's comment on Schoenberg, "the wonderful independence of the trombones against the trumpets and winds" (PDF 44), describes what this bar is missing: the movement already has a trombone line that fits there.

**Fix (applied).** Tbn 2 doubles the Vc/Bsn 1 line at pitch, at f as before (the graded brass at 139 are unchanged), with the cello's slurs, and keeps the existing accents on the 4–3 at 142:

```
% recOne.ily, recOneTbnTwo, bars 139–142 (was: aes1\f | aes1 | aes2 aes2 | g1 |)
c'2.\f( bes4) | aes2( f2~ | f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2-> b2->) | g8->\ff r8 r4 r2 | R1*3 |
```

I used Tbn 2, not Tbn 1. With Tbn 1 the line crossed under Tbn 2's A♭3 at 140.3–141.3, and the shared staff became hard to read; I built that version and looked at it. Tbn 2 stays at or below Tbn 1 throughout, so the staff prints cleanly (checked at 110 dpi on PDF page 14). What moves: Tbn 2's held A♭3 and its G3 at 142. A♭ is still in Hn 1, Tpt 2, Cl 2 and Fl 2, and G is in Hn 4, Tpt 1, B.tbn and the tuba. The line itself passes through A♭3. No pitch class of any chord changes, so c223's protection of 139–146 (the recapitulation's turn to iv at 139–143) is kept. The tenor line now has a trombone in its best register, F3–C4, with the bassoon and cellos, and it is the same weight as the horn pair. This follows Adler's equation (PDF 44): one trombone equals two horns, against Hn 1·2 on A♭4/F4.

### 2. 27–30: the same tenor line in the exposition stays as it is.

At 27–30, Bsn 1 and Vc play the model of this line (C4–E♭4 | D4–F4 | E♭4–C4 | E4–B♭3), and Tbn 1 again holds the same notes as Hn 3. It should stay. The finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part at its 199–204, and the reason given for protecting 27–32 (c224, c270) is the bass, the tune and the layout. The exposition line is also less covered than at 139. It sits mostly above Tbn 1 and Hn 3 (C4/B3), between them and Tpt 3, and the trumpets there punctuate in the timpani rhythm instead of holding. Adler's punctuation model (PDF 42, Ex. XI-23 at *) leaves gaps that a held chord does not. Not applied.

### 3. 117–121: horns and trombones in unison on the motto stay as they are.

Adler admires the trombones' independence (PDF 44). Here Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone play the augmented motto in unison and octaves. That is a cantus firmus against the P-head imitation in the flutes, oboes and violins and the triplets in clarinets and violas. It is real counterpoint between groups, and its weight follows Adler's two-horns-equal-one-trombone (PDF 44). It is protected (c174, c241, c308), and splitting it would weaken the line that leads to the fff at 122. Not applied.

### 4. 96: the brass answer at f under the ff head stays as it is.

The stretto entry of Hn 1·2 + Tbn 1 + Bsn 1 + Vc on F3 at 96.2.5 is marked f, accented, against the ff upper entry a beat earlier. Bartók marks each brass entry on its own (PDF 45–47), and this entry has its own accent. The answer also comes in while the upper voices hold B♭5/D♭6, so it is heard. The brass then step up to ff at 97, and that step is part of the protected crescendo entry order 85–97 (c143). Not applied.

### 5. 175–179: the chorale's outer voices in octaves stay as they are.

Tbn 1 and the bass trombone play the motto in octaves, so the chorale has two real brass parts. Ex. XI-22 (PDF 42) shows the other way: the chorale doubled in the low strings and bassoons, with the brass free for counterpoint. The "Do not reuse" list in coherence/mvt1.md keeps "the brass chorale in major … with an independent bass" for the finale. This pp minor chorale with an octave bass is the deliberate contrast to it, and the coda's pitches are protected. Not applied.
