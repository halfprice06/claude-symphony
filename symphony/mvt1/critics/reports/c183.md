## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–42: the medial caesura sounds as V7, not V (applied)
**Problem.** The transition's goal is the sf chord at 41, held through 42, with the four-horn call. The strings sound a plain E♭ triad (Vln II G5, Vla E♭4/G4, Vc G3, Cb E♭2). The winds add the 7th:
- Cl 1 holds D♭5 for six beats.
- Hn 3 holds D♭4.
- Tpt 3 plays D♭4 in its sf eighth.

So the caesura that marks the exposition's main division sounds as V7, while the top line comes to rest on G6, which is 7̂ of A♭. The fill at 43–46 then repeats the same V7 at p (D♭ in Vln II and Hn 3 at 43, in the bass at 44). As a result, the arrival at 41 and the lead-in 43–46 are the same chord, and the lead-in adds no new intensity.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 99 §10: "If the melodic line comes to rest on 7̂, 2̂, or 5̂, V will generally produce a better effect than V7, which conveys an inappropriate feeling of activity. For this reason, V7 is rarely used in a semicadence." Points for Review (PDF 103) no. 8: "V7 is not normally the goal of a semicadence."
- A&S PDF 101 (Ex. 6-18): V is extended and intensified by moving from 5/3 to 7.
- A&S PDF 117 (Ex. 8-8): V7 → V4/2 → I6 carries the 7th into the bass, which is exactly what bars 43–45 already do.
- The score's own recapitulation does this. At 145–146 the HC before the second group drops the F, so 146 is a V triad (Hn 3 B3 resolving at 147, from c153).
- Mozart 41/i bars 50–53 (MCTL harmony.csv) has a triad MC. Open Music Theory gives the HC dominant as "almost invariably a triad".
- Against: Beethoven 9/i bars 72–76 uses V7. That dominant keeps moving into I without a caesura fill, so it is a different case. Rothfarb also allows V7.

**Fix.** Change only the three wind 7ths at 41–42 to the root. The 7th then enters at 43 with the p chord, where Vln II and Hn 3 already take D♭.
- `sections/trans.ily`, transClOne: `des''1\sf\> | des''2\mf bes'2\>` → `es''1\sf\> | es''2\mf bes'2\>`
- transHnThree: `des'2.\ff es4(` → `bes2.\ff es4(`. The B♭3 goes with Hn 4's G3. E♭4 would make a doubled D4→E♭4 unison with the violas, which the all-pairs check flagged, so I used B♭3.
- transTptThree: `des'8->\sf` → `es'8->\sf`

Result: the caesura is a V triad (sf, horn call), and 43–45 become V8–7 → V4/2 → I6. Nothing changes in the brass scoring, rhythms, dynamics or the horn call (cycle 128 rule: only pitches change, and the new source is A&S). The protected MC and fill (c13, c23, c163) are kept. Only the chord quality of the arrival changes, which those entries did not discuss.
