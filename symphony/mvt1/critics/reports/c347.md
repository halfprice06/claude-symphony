# Critic 347: texture and counterpoint (v347)

Lens: texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 17 "The Orchestra as Accompanist" (adler2 PDF 256–290), second pass. I read every text page in full and rendered every page of the range at 110 dpi and looked at each example (Ex. XVII-1 to XVII-25). The landscape pages (Bizet XVII-20, Fidelio XVII-22, Bach XVII-23, Orff XVII-25) are too small to read note by note at that size; for them I rely on the prose and on what the layout shows (which staves move, which hold).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 104–105: Hn 3 strikes two of Hn 1's S-head notes at the same pitch and moment (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 103–106 the S head is in the tenor: Hn 1 (F4–E4 | D4–B♭4–A4 | G4–F4–E4–G4–B4 | E5–D♯5) with Bsn 1 and the cellos an octave lower, mf, under the P head in Ob 2 and Cl 1·2. Hn 3·4 play an inner legato line, mp (c188). Two of Hn 3's attacks are Hn 1's attacks:
- 104.1: Hn 3 re-strikes D4 (`d'4`) together with Hn 1's D4.
- 105.3: Hn 3 moves to E4 together with Hn 1's E4.

The `coattack.py` scan over 23 tune spans found no other accompanying part that strikes the tune's pitch at the same moment in the same family. The exceptions are deliberate doublings and three overlaps that earlier critics kept. In bar 104 the `rhythm.py` scan also flags Hn 3·4 moving crotchet–minim–crotchet with Hn 1. That is the only bar of the 14 lyrical spans where an accompanying part copies the tune's rhythm with different pitches. So at those two beats the solo horn's line becomes the top of a three-horn chord and loses its identity in its own colour. HISTORY (cycle 177) protects this passage with "Hn 1 undoubled", but Hn 3 doubles it on these two beats.

**Evidence.**
- Adler adler2 PDF 264: Mozart "never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn is playing ... so as not to interfere with the solo sound".
- PDF 274: keep the accompanying forces registrally apart from the soloist; rhythmic contrast lessens the need, but here there is none.
- PDF 259–260: the accompaniment "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part".
- PDF 283: the line is "helped in pitch, yet not doubled".
- adler1 PDF 257: a unison doubling robs both timbres.

**Fix (dev.ily, `devHnThree`).**
```
\clef bass d'1\mp | r4 d'2( cis'4 | d'2) r4 e'4( | fis'2\> b2) |
```
It was `d'1 | d'4 d'2( cis'4 | d'2 e'2 | fis'2\> b2)`. Hn 3 keeps every pitch of its line. It rests on 104.1 and enters D4 on beat 2, under Hn 1's B♭4. It rests on 105.3 and takes E4 on beat 4, under Hn 1's G4. Each change removes an attack and adds nothing new.

The chords stay as they were:
- 104.1 was D–F with no fifth (Hn 4 F3, the bass, Bsn 1/Vc D3, top D5) and stays so.
- 105.3 is C–E–B♭ (bass and Hn 4 C, Hn 1 E4, Ob/Cl B♭5). G arrives at 105.4 in Hn 1 and Hn 3's E4.

I tried A3 at 104.1, which would add the fifth, but it makes parallel twelfths with the bass D2→G2 under Hn 3 A3→D4. C4 at 105.3 gives parallel octaves with Hn 4 (D3/D4 → C3/C4). So the rests are the clean solution.

**Earlier fixes.** c188's legato stays: the slurs are kept, split at the new rest. c225's bass-clef plan for Hn 3·4 at 103–106 is unchanged.

### 2. Bar 145: Hn 3 enters on the bassoon's arriving B3 (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 144–145 Bsn 1 answers Vln I's P head (G3–E♭4–D4–C4 | B3, mf diminuendo to pp). Its last note, B3 at 145.1, is struck at the same instant and pitch by Hn 3 (`b1\pp~ | b1`), which then holds B3 through 146 into C4 at 147. c247 moved the violas off this answer for the same reason (Adler PDF 257–258, 274). The horn on the bassoon's arrival note is the last remaining doubling. Here the line's goal disappears into a horn, an instrument that carries more than the bassoon at pp.

**Evidence.**

### 3. Bars 69–72: the two heads share one octave (no change)

At 69–72 the P head (Cl 1·2, C.a., Hn 1·2, Vln II, E♭4–F5) and the lower octave of the S head (Ob 1, Tpt 1, C5–F5) lie in the same octave. They meet at the unison at 69.2 (C5), 70.1 (A♭4), 70.3 (F5), 70.4.5 (E♭5) and 71.1 (D♭5). By PDF 274 two foreground lines want registral room. It should stay as written:
- The shared notes are the chord tones of I–I6–ii7, struck by two lines in opposed rhythms: the S head holds minims while the P head moves in quavers. Rhythmic contrast is the condition under which Adler relaxes the registral rule (PDF 274; PDF 259–260).
- The S head is carried by its upper octave, Fl 1·2 and Vln I at C6–A♭6, an octave clear of the P head.
- Raising Ob 1 and Tpt 1 an octave would put Tpt 1 on A♭6 at 72. Lowering the P head would put Vln II and the C.a. below their ranges.
- The passage is protected (cycles 106 and 187 "codetta doublings 69–72", c271 "70–71 brief octave contact", c331), and coherence/mvt1.md lists Tpt 1's S head at 69–72.

### 4. Fugato 88.3 and 91.3: the countersubject's scale passes through the subject's held note (no change)

The countersubject's first bar (Vc at 88, Vla at 91) is a rising scale that reaches E♭4 at 88.3 and A♭4 at 91.3. In each case that is the pitch the subject's new entry (Vla + Bsn 1 at 88, C.a. + Vln II at 91) has held since beat 2. The contact is one quaver on a passing note inside a held one. Chapter 17 is about accompanying a soloist. A fugato's equal voices may cross through each other, and PDF 286–287 (Bach) asks only for clear harmonic support, which 90–96 have. The fugato is protected (c142, c237, c257, c337).

### 5. Bar 34: the viola's answering pickup starts inside the bassoon's tenuto C4 (no change)

Vla's C4 pickup at 34.1.5 sounds while Bsn 1 still holds the tenuto C4 of its head. Vln I's F4 pickup at 35.1.5 does the same over the viola's F4. This is the transition's stretto hand-off: each answer takes the previous voice's repeated note as its pickup, so the chain C–F–B♭ is audible as one line. The overlap is a quaver and the colours change (bassoon, then strings). The bassoon's notes and slurs at 33–36 are protected (cycle 151).
