## Findings

### F1. Bar 164: Cl 1 leaps an augmented second, A♭4–B4, into V. Applied.

**What is wrong.** Bars 162–166 are the recapitulation's falling-thirds approach: A minor (vi) at 162, F at 163, Dø7 at 164 (D–F–A♭–C, with the A♭ borrowed from minor), then G at 164 beat 3 and C at 166. Cl 1 plays alone, mp, at the top of the woodwinds: F4, A♭4–B4 (quavers, slurred), D5. The A♭4 is the borrowed ♭6̂ and the B4 is the leading tone, so the line steps 6̂–♯7̂ by an augmented second just before V. The B4 is also an anticipation: it enters on beat 2½, over the iiø7 and against Hn 2's C4, before the G chord.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 268: the melodic augmented 2nd "should be avoided".
- PDF 284, Ex. 17-26: in a minor-key series to V, the soprano that moves 6̂–♯7̂ by A2 is the one example labelled "wrong". The two that avoid it are labelled "good".
- PDF 283: in a falling-3rds approach in minor, II is reached through II7. The score already does this with the iiø7, so only the A2 in the line is left.
- The score has its own rule here. Cycle 152 set Hn 3 A♭3→G3 at 164 because "once introduced, flat 6 goes to 5" (comment in recTwo.ily). Cycle 170 used the same rule at 161 (A♭–G–F–E, recTwo.ily comment). Cl 1 is the only part at 164 that still takes the borrowed A♭ upward.
- The exposition's counterpart at 66 (Cl 1 D♭4–A♭4–B♭4, ii–V in A♭) has no augmented second.

**Fix (sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoClOne, bar 164):**
`f'4\mp aes'8( b'8) d''2~` → `f'4\mp aes'8( g'8) d''2~`

- The A♭ now falls to G, as it does in Hn 3 half a beat later. The G4 anticipates the root of V.
- The line then rises a fifth to D5 on beat 3, which is unchanged.
- The B4 of bar 165 (`d''2( b'4 f'4)`) is untouched, so the leading tone still sounds inside V.

I made the same change in the unused copy `recTwoCounter` so the two stay identical.

**Check.** At 164 beat 2½ the sounding notes are D2, D3, F3, A♭3, C4, F4, G4 (Cl 1), D5, A♭5, D6 and F6, so the G4 does not collide with any other part. Cl 1's G4 (beat 2½) and Hn 3's G3 (beat 3) do not move together, so there are no octaves. The vl.txt diff is empty.

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-252/work. This is the only change.

### C1. Bars 33–36: the motto sequence in c, f and b♭ over the bass C–A♭–B♭–E♭. Checked, kept.

The bassoon (33), violas (34) and Vln I (35) state the motto in C minor, F minor and B♭ minor: a real sequence a fourth higher each time. The exercise note (PDF 286) suggests that such a melody wants a descending-5ths bass, C–F–B♭–E♭.

I keep the A♭ that cycle 102 put at 34, for two reasons:
- It is Ex. 17-4's 5/3–6/3 alternation in C minor (PDF 269), and it gives one stepwise line A♭–B♭ under the statements.
- Over the A♭ the upper parts hold C and A♭ on the strong beats and reach F only on beat 4. That is a 5-6 over a held bass (Ex. 17-9, PDF 274), so the F-minor statement still ends on its own iv6.

Changing it would undo cycle 102's fix without a better reason from the reading.

### C2. Bars 40→41: a doubled leading tone before the goal tonic. Checked, kept.

At 40 (B♭7), D sounds in Va, Hn 3 and Hn 4 (D4 unison) and in Ob 1 (D5) and Fl 1 (D6). The goal E♭ arrives at 41. PDF 267 allows a doubled leading tone in a sequence "as long as it does not appear before a goal tonic".

Here the D4 is one line played by three instruments, and it resolves in the violas (D4→E♭4). The horns split to B♭3 and G3 at the arrival chord. The D5 and D6 are not left hanging: they run up through E♭ to G in the quaver-semiquaver scales. Cycle 234 made the horn pair deliberately (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 102). No change.

### C3. Bar 38, beat 2: F♭ against F in the stretto entry. Checked, kept.

Ob 1 and the violas end the E♭-minor motto (G♭–F–F♭–E♭) half a bar late, over 38's F-minor bass. For one quaver, F♭4/F♭5 sound against F2/F3 in the bass. PDF 266 says melodic intervals change to fit the key. But this tail is the motto's chromatic descent, which is the identity of the theme.

The case differs from bar 35, which c142 fixed:
- At 35 the clash was an accented, held D♭5.
- Here it is a passing quaver on the off-beat that resolves at once to E♭, the seventh of F minor.

The only alternative is to delay the bass to the half bar, and that would break the bar-by-bar bass of 36–40, which follows the Vln I statements. No change.

### C4. Bars 114→115: B4–A♭4 in Cl 1, Hn 1, Ob 2 and Vln II. Checked, kept.

This is an augmented second in the wedge (protected, c142): vii°7 over D, then D♭ major. B is the leading tone of C. It cannot rise to C, because Ob 1 and Tpt 1 have D♭5 on the downbeat of 115 and C5 would make a minor second against it. A♭ is the nearest tone of the next chord. Unlike bar 164 this is a forte, fully scored tutti chord change, not a solo line approaching V. No change.
