## Findings

### 1. Bar 83: the cellos strike G3 a semitone under Tbn 1's A♭3 in the ff V♭9 (applied)

**What is wrong.** Bar 83 strikes the V♭9 of C minor twice, ff, on beats 1 and 3 (G1 Cb/Cbsn/tuba, G2 B.tbn/Bsn 2, F3 Tbn 2, A♭3 Tbn 1, B3 Bsn 1/Hn 4/Tpt 3/Vla, D4, F4, A♭4, B4, D5, F5, A♭5). The cellos (`g4->\downbow r4 g4->\downbow r4`) strike G3 directly under Tbn 1's A♭3. That puts the 9th's note of resolution in an upper voice a minor second below the 9th, at the same time as the 9th, in the tenor register where a semitone at ff in cellos and trombone sounds thick. The root is then present in G1, G2 and G3, so the chord has a tripled root plus the 9th.

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 463 (Ex. 27-1, 27-2): in V9 the incomplete V7 is "accompanied not by 5̂, forming an octave, but by 6̂, forming a 9th". The 9th takes the place of the upper octave of the root, and the root stays in the bass. All three voicings of Ex. 27-2 have G only in the bass. PDF 465 says the 9th resolves "to a tone already present in a lower voice and register", meaning the bass, a ninth or more below. PDF 469 (with Unit 21 §12) gives the reason: a dissonance is weakened when its note of resolution sounds at the same time in a voice other than the bass, and the result is "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". The score already follows this rule in the same kind of chord elsewhere. At 28 beat 3, c302 removed Tpt 2's G4 a semitone under the tune's A♭4 for this reason, and c139 had taken Hn 4's G3 off the same beat. At 122 the G3 under A♭3 is allowed because Hn 4 has held it as an inner pedal since 117 (PDF 471, ninths above a pedal point; c302 kept it on that ground). At 83 there is no pedal. The cellos leap up a ninth from A♭2 (82) to strike G3 and drop back to G2 at 84.

There is a second reason. c302 kept 83's unresolved A♭ because "Tbn 1's A♭3 is answered at the same pitch by the fugato subject's A♭3–G3 at 85" (A&S PDF 467: an emphasised unresolved 9th can be compensated by a later stepwise descent from the same pitch). That answer only works if the G3 is new when the subject reaches it. In v392 the cellos have already struck G3 twice under the A♭3 at 83, so the subject's A♭3→G3 at 85 lands on a pitch the listener has just heard.

**Fix (applied).** sections/dev.ily, devVc, bar 83: `g4->\downbow r4 g4->\downbow r4` → `g,4->\downbow r4 g,4->\downbow r4`. The cellos now play A♭2 (82) – G2 (83) – G2 (84, sub. p) with B.tbn and Bsn 2. That is the bass's own ♭6–5 by step, in place of a leap up a ninth and back. The chord keeps every pitch class, the A♭ on top in Fl 1/Vln I, Cl 1/Hn 1/Tpt 2 and Tbn 1, and the common-tone A♭ 82→83 in Tbn 1 (c192). Above the bass, G appears only when the fugato reaches G3 at 85. I added a one-line comment above the next line of devVc.

**Protections.** c192 protected "♭9-on-top dominants at 83, 117, 122" and "common-tone A♭ 82→83 with the fugato's A♭–G answer at 85". The ♭9 stays on top, the common-tone A♭ is unchanged, and the change makes the A♭–G answer at 85 clearer, so it serves the reason for the protection. The dynamics, accents, bowing and held brass of 82–83 (c244, c304, c308) are unchanged. This does not undo an earlier fix. I found no cycle that chose the cellos' G3 at 83.

### Checked against the reading and kept

- **117.2–117.3, Hn 3 G4 under the A♭4 of Cl 1, Ob 1 and Vln II.** This is the same semitone as at 83, but Hn 3 enters on beat 1, before the A♭, and holds G4 through 121 as part of the inner G pedal. A&S PDF 471 (§9) covers 9ths above a pedal point. c192 also protected it.
- **35.4, Vln I B♮4 against Cl 1's held B♭4 over V7 of E♭.** This is a passing eighth in the motto contour F–D♭–C–B–B♭. Ex. 27-9 (Dvořák, PDF 468–469) shows A&S accepting this kind of simultaneous chromatic conflict when "a melodic figure ... requires" it. The motto statements at 33–36 are protected (c102, c252).
- **165.2, Vln I A5 over the harp's G5.** The A is a passing 9th in a falling line, B5–A5–G5–F5–D5, which is the passing function on PDF 464–465, not a suspension. Ex. 27-10's warning concerns accented dissonance. The harp chord G5–D6–F6 (c232) leaves out B on purpose, so the violins' 4–3 C6→B5 is not anticipated.
- **132.3–132.4, the 9th A♭3 in the low P theme, below the 3rd B3.** A&S say the 9th lies "mostly" in the soprano (PDF 463). Here it is the theme's own A♭–G, resolving 9–8 inside V on the next eighth (PDF 465–466). The low theme with the counter above is protected (c84, c242).
- **83→84, the 7th F left without a stepwise resolution.** A&S PDF 466 calls unresolved 7ths rare. Here the dominant becomes the key of the fugato, which enters in G minor at 85, so F becomes 7̂ of G (with F♯ at 86) instead of a dissonant 7th that needs to fall. c302 kept it for the same reason, and the reading's rule covers a 7th that keeps its function.
- **97.1, brass C4 over Hn 4's B3 in B minor.** This is the motto's ♭2–1 on the B-minor tonic, not an added dissonance over V, so Unit 27 does not apply. The brass counter at 97–102 is protected.
- **159.1, E6/E5/E4 over V7 with D in Tbn 1 and Cl 2.** The soprano 6th lies above the 7th, rising E–F–G (PDF 475). It is protected and quoted by the finale (c101, c281, c302).
