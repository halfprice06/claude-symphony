## Findings

### 1. Bar 144: the bassoon's answer to the P head is covered by strings that are still at f (applied)

**Bars.** 143–145, the last statement of the first-theme head before the second group.

**What is wrong.** At 143 Vln I play the P head f. At 144 Bsn 1 answers it a bar later, mf diminuendo, `r8 g8( es'4.->) d'4( c'8 | b1\pp)`, G3–E♭4–D4–C4–B3. This is the only exposed woodwind statement of P in the recapitulation, and it repeats the imitation the transition set up at 33–35 (bassoon, then violas, then violins). In v261 the whole string body starts 144 at the f of 143 and only then diminuendos: violas hold G3 (the bassoon's first note, at the unison), cellos hold E♭3–D3 (an octave under the bassoon's E♭4–D4), Vln II G4–F4, basses G1. The bassoon enters at mf into a string chord that is louder than it and partly on its own pitches. Its line lies in the "sweet, more subdued" band.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 226: the bassoon "when accompanied ... has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments", and when a bassoon solo is accompanied "care must be taken to mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist." PDF 185: a register the player cannot make louder must not be covered. Critic 151 applied the same rule to the bassoon solo at 34 (violas mp under it). The case at 144 is the same, with a unison on the entry note.

For memorability, the point is that the P head is the movement's most repeated figure, and this answer is its last appearance before the music turns to C major at 145–147. If it is heard, the first group ends with the head passing from violins to bassoon and fading. If it is covered, the listener hears only a string diminuendo.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-261/work/sections/recOne.ily).** The accompaniment starts 144 at mp and diminuendos to pp at 145, the same level as the timpani roll that already starts mp at 144. Vln I keep their f> tail as the top line.
```
recOneVlnTwo, 144:  g'2\mp\> f'2 |          (was g'2\> f'2)
recOneVla,    144:  g1\mp\> |               (was g1\>)
recOneVc,     144:  es2.\mp\> d4~ |         (was es2.\> d4~)
recOneCb,     144:  g,,1\mp\> |             (was g,,1\>)
```
No notes change. The protected items near here are the "oboe G–E pivot 137/145", "dominant 136–138", "sul tasto 147" and the c222 cello D3 at 145. None of them is touched. The first-group notes protected by c84/c242 are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 107–109: bass clarinet P head peaks above its chalumeau (no change)

The bass clarinet's G3 at 107 (written A4) is above the chalumeau Adler prefers (PDF 217). Critic 151 kept it because the only lower octave needs the low-C extension under the bass line. That reason still holds, so I keep it.

### 3. Bars 147–153 and 171–174: cor anglais in its mellow band (no change)

The cor anglais answers (written D5–A5) and the coda motto (written D5–B♭5) lie inside the band Adler labels "mellow, reedy, sonorous" (written A4–C6, PDF 205). They do not reach the "thin, pinched" top, where the instrument "loses its personality." The cor anglais' "richly beautiful" lowest sixth (concert E3–C4) is not used, but the motto's pitches at 5 and 171 do not allow it. No change.

### 4. Bars 73–76: flute motto in the sweet band over pp strings (no change)

C5–A♭5 lies in the "sweet, little carrying power" band (PDF 185). Under it there are only the pp A♭ pedal and inner strings, the condition Adler shows in Dvořák 9/i and Brahms 4/iv (PDF 186). It stays.

### 5. The themes judged against the lens (no change)

- **Motto (G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C).** The minor sixth, the stepwise fall and the D♭ neighbour make one gesture. It is short enough to recognise in any colour, and the score gives it a new woodwind colour at each return: bass clarinet chalumeau (1), cor anglais (5), flute (73), bass clarinet again (77), cor anglais (171), then clarinet for the horn call (175). Tchaikovsky 5 does the same with its clarinet motto (Ex. VII-59, PDF 211). This is at master level.
- **P.** The pickup sixth G–E♭ and the syncopated fall are memorable, and the head is passed through bassoon, bass clarinet, oboe and clarinets. Finding 1 restores the last of these handoffs.
- **S.** It begins in horn and cellos with the clarinet joining for the high consequent (52–55, again at 153). That passes the tune from horn to clarinet the way Wagner passes a line between clarinets (Ex. VII-74, PDF 217). The tune's rising sixths answer the motto's. I found nothing in this reading that improves its notes, and its pitches are protected in every statement.
- **Horn call (E♭–C–B).** The clarinet recall at 175–177 (written F4–D5–C♯5, crossing the break upward at pp) is easy on a clarinet, because Adler gives the clarinet pp in every register (PDF 210). It stays.
