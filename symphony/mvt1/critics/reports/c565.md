## Findings

### 1. Two breaths are printed only in the parts (C.a. bar 8, Tbn 1 bar 119.4). Applied.

Adler PDF 291, item 3: "the score must contain every detail for every instrument, including special instructions concerning bowing, tonguing, or articulations." A breath is a tonguing detail. It decides where a held line breaks, and the conductor needs to see it to balance the other players around it. Item 2 on the same page adds that the conductor "has to read all the lines simultaneously". He can only do that with what the score shows.

Of the fifteen part-only items in the sources, thirteen are already in the score in another form. The stick changes are printed at the last visible rest. "a2 with 3." at 158 is the a2 on the Hn 3·4 staff. The Bsn 1 swells at 103–105 are reading aids for a doubling the score shows. Hn 4's mp at 67 is Hn 3's hairpin level on the shared staff. Two are not shown in the score at all:

- C.a. bar 8 (intro.ily line 95). c198 added a part-only "(breathe ad lib.)" inside the 16-beat G4 from 5.4 to 9.3 (about 19 s at crotchet = 50). The score showed one unbroken slurred G. A conductor reading it would expect no break.
- Tbn 1 bar 119.4 (dev.ily). c556 added a part-only `\breathe` before Tbn 1's D4, so that Tbn 1 breathes with Hn 1·2 while Tbn 2 holds (the staggered-breath rule of c087). The score's Tbn 1·2 staff is in forced unison from 117 and showed no breath. The comma over Hn 1·2 at the same point made it look as if only the horns breathe.

Fix, as applied:

```lilypond
% intro.ily 95 (C.a.): the markup is no longer part-only
g'1~\pp^\markup \small \italic "(breathe ad lib.)" | g'2.) r4 | R1*5 |

% dev.ily, devTbnTwo bar 119: a score-only "1." comma. Under \partCombineUnisono the a2
% staff prints this voice's events; Tbn 1's own \breathe or markup does not appear.
es'2. d'4-\tag #'score ^\markup \translate #'(-2.2 . 1.2) \concat { \fontsize #-2 \bold "1." \hspace #0.3 \musicglyph "scripts.rcomma" } |
```

The label "1." follows Adler's own score example for a shared staff (Ex. XVIII-10a, PDF 301), where the part number stands over the passage that belongs to one player only. A comma without a label above an a2 staff would tell both trombones to breathe, which would undo c556's staggering. I first tried a `\breathe` and then a markup in the Tbn 1 variable. The part combiner dropped both in forced unison, so the mark sits on the Tbn 2 variable with a score tag and a comment that explains why. At 160 dpi the "1.," sits clear above and to the left of the D4 head, at the same height as the horns' comma two staves up. The C.a. text sits over the slur in the empty space under the oboes. It runs a little past the barline into bar 9, as "molto espr." does at 41–42 (c475 kept that), and it touches nothing.

This keeps both earlier decisions (c198, c556). It only makes the score show them. It does not undo any fix. Only pages 2 and 11 changed (40 dpi image comparison; the other 15 music pages are identical). The parts are unchanged: the C.a. part image is identical, and the Tbn 2 part does not contain the "1." markup.

### 2. Parts audit "entries without a dynamic" (no change)

PARTS_AUDIT lists 56 entries "after a long rest without a dynamic". I checked each bar against the cue table in parts-gen.py. Every one is the first note of a cue (small notes, instrument named). The audit counts a cue as an entry. The player's own entries after these cues all carry a level: for example Tpt 1 has f at 69 and pp cresc. at 113, and Harp has mf at 156, f at 162 and ppp at 182. This follows Adler PDF 299 item 6 and Ex. XVIII-7. The audit could skip quoted music, but that is a tooling matter and changes nothing a player sees.

### 3. String and harp page turns (no change)

Adler PDF 299 item 5 also covers string parts: half the section drops out to turn, so a turn must not fall in an important passage. Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc are two pages with no turn. The harp part is a title page plus facing pages 2–3, with no turn. The Cb part turns from page 3 to 4 in the four-bar rest 171–174 (protected). No string or harp turn falls inside a passage.

### 4. Where the music could be better within this lens (no change)

I looked for a mark that would make a phrase sing more or a climax land harder on the page. The candidates were the Tpt 1·2 ff at 32 against f in the other brass, the unclosed hairpin in Hn 2/4 at 162, and the rehearsal-letter gaps 77–97 and 97–117. All three are protected with stated reasons: "Tpt1·2 ... ff at 32" and "brass grading 27–32" (early cycles; HISTORY lines 75, 660), c188, and c155/c165. I found nothing in Adler's chapter that answers those reasons. The tempo words at the three climaxes (122–127, 158–160) and in the coda are complete and placed as in the Mahler example. No other change meets the bar.
