## Findings

**1. Harp 59–60: the G♭ pedal moves while the G♮ plucked half a beat earlier is still ringing. (Applied.)**
In 59 the RH plays `es''8( g'' bes'' des''')`, so g'' sounds on beat 3½. The G pedal has to reach G♭ before 60 beat 1 (`<bes' des'' ges''>`, LH `ges,,`). The only time left for the change is beats 3½–4 of 59, about 0.8 s at ♩=112, and during all of it the G5 string is sounding inside a crescendo. Moving the pedal then slides the string audibly from G to G♭, or makes it buzz. Adler (PDF 113) asks the orchestrator to check "that the tempo will permit time for the changes"; Harp Column says the affected strings must be muted. Cycle 56 (c056) proposed removing the g'' but did not apply it because "the strings hold the G". That reason supports the change: Vln I play `aes''2 g''2` in 59, the same pitch, so the harp's G adds nothing.
Fix (tTwo.ily): `r2 bes'8( es'' bes'' des''') |`. The figure still climbs to D♭6, the hand spans a tenth (Adler PDF 114: tenths are easy), and the RH-on-beats-3–4 pattern of 56–68 is unchanged, so the protected cello/harp accompaniment design is kept.

**2. Harp 61→62: the sub. p comes while the forte F♭ chord is still ringing. (Applied.)**
The harp crescendos from 59 to 61. At 61 beat 3 it strikes `<fes, fes>` and `<fes' aes' des''>`; F♭2 and F♭3 are in the "sonorous" low register (Adler PDF 113). At 62 everyone, harp included, drops to sub. p on an A♭-major arpeggio. A harp cannot get softer by itself: unless the strings are damped, the F♭ chord rings for seconds over the p arpeggio and clashes F♭ against E♭. The F pedal also has to return to F♮ at 63 beat 3 under those F♭ strings. The score already uses "étouffez" on a rest for exactly this purpose at 163 (cycle 56).
Fix (tTwo.ily, RH bar 62): `r2^\markup \small \italic "étouffez" aes8( c' es' aes')`. The RH rests on beats 1–2, so it is free to damp.

**3. Harp pedal marks at 52 and 57 come earlier than needed and fall on ringing strings. (Applied.)**
- 52: "G♮" is marked on beat 1. The G♭4 from 51 beat 1½ (middle register, slurred to ring) is still sounding, so it would slide to G♮ under the D♭ chord. G♮ is not needed until the g'' on 54 beat 2½. I moved the mark to 53 beat 3: `<< bes,,1 { s2 s2_\markup \small "G♮" } >>`.
- 57: "F♮" is marked on beat 1, 1½ beats after the F♭5 of 56 (beat 3½). F♮ is not needed until f'' on 57 beat 3½. I moved the mark to the LH rest: `<aes,, aes,>2 r2_\markup \small "F♮"`.
Adler PDF 113: the change is made "just a split second before playing the note", and experienced harpists change "even later than it is marked". The later marks give the strings longer to decay. No notes change in protected 47–55.

**4. Harp 164–165: low octaves left at f while the orchestra is at mp. (Applied.)**
The harp's last dynamic is f at 162. Through 163–165 the LH strikes `<f,, f,>`, `<d, d>`, `<g,, g,>` and `<g,, g,>`, and none of these has a new dynamic. Meanwhile the strings, clarinet, bassoons and horns reach mp at 164 and p at 166. For the RH top chords (F5–F6) the f is correct, because Adler (PDF 114) says ff at the top sounds like mf in the middle. The LH octaves are a different case: they sit in the "somber, dark, and sonorous" bottom two octaves (PDF 113), and at f they would stand out above the mp texture. The harp has one dynamic line, under the LH (cycle 48), which also controls the RH in the MIDI.
Fix (recTwo.ily): `<d, d>2\mp <g,, g,>2 |`. 166 keeps its p.

**5. No further change: pedalling elsewhere checked against Adler.**
I traced every pedal from the 47 diagram (D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭) through 68, the 155 diagram (D C B♭ | E F G A♭), the B♮/A♮ change at 158 (one per foot; the ringing B♭1 from 156 is covered by the ff tutti), the A♭ change at 163 (damped), and the final C–G–C at 182. No two pedals on the same side change together. Every note matches the pedals in force, and each change has at least half a beat of free time. The enharmonic F♭ at 56 and 60–61 follows Adler's Ex. IV-3 rule, because E♭ is needed alongside it. Note for the composer, not proposed: at 162 the f rolled chord A5–C6–E6 is covered by the ff tutti (Adler PDF 114). It does no harm, and the voicings 147–166 are protected.
