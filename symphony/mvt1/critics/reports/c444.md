## Findings

### 1. Bars 122–123: the cellos stop their tremolo at the fff chord while the other strings start theirs (applied)

What is in v444. From 117 to 121 the cellos play G2 in measured semiquaver tremolo (`g,1:16`), with a crescendo into 122. At the fff arrival on 122 Vln I (A♭6), Vln II (D5/A♭5 double stop, "non div."), and the violas (B3/D4) all change to unmeasured tremolo (`:32`) for 122–123. The cellos at that moment drop their tremolo and hold a plain A♭3 (122) and G3 with diminuendo (123). So the one string group that had been trembling for five bars goes still at the exact point where the rest of the strings begin to tremble. On the page the cello staff at 122 is the only plain string semibreve between tremolo staves above it.

Evidence from the reading.
- Appendix PDF 505, viewed at 220 dpi: in chords 1, 2 and 4 (fff), and 6 and 7 (f, ff), the tremolo strokes stand on all five string groups, Vc and Cb included. Chord 4 (*The Tsar's Bride*, p. 301) is the model c334 matched to 122: fff in every group, timpani roll, strings tremolo in every part. Chord 5, the other pure chord climax, has no tremolo in any string group. The appendix offers "all strings tremolo" and "no strings tremolo", never the split that 122 has. PDF 506 chords 9–12 follow the same rule (Vc and Cb with strokes where the violins have them).
- Ex. 212 (*Ivan the Terrible*, PDF 380), the example ch. IV cites for string figuration under a sustained wind and brass tutti: Vln I·II, violas and cellos all carry the measured triplets; only the basses take plain crotchets. The cellos are part of the trembling mass, not set apart from it.
- Text PDF 116: the harmonic layer should differ from the tune "in fullness and intensity". At 122 there is no tune; the whole string body is the intensity layer of a chord climax. A plain cello A♭3 at fff under heavy brass adds no intensity of its own, because Bsn 1 and Tbn 1 already sound the same A♭3 at fff.

What it costs and what is kept. The cello pitches, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged: A♭3 fff at 122, G3 with diminuendo at 123, then the divided G3/B3 and G3/C4 at 124–125 that carry the collapse (c287 kept that line; it is untouched). c338 kept the cellos' bowing 122→123 (A♭3 down-bow, G3 up-bow); the bow direction for a tremolo note is the same, and no bow marks are printed there. The double basses stay on the tied G1 pedal (117–130 is protected as one held pedal, and the timpani G2 roll already gives the bottom its trembling). Nothing a later movement quotes is in these bars.

### 3. Considered and rejected

- **Bar 122 bass spacing (G1–G2, then G2–F3, a seventh).** A D3 in Bsn 2 would give the octave–fifth–third order of chords 1–5. But Bsn 2 on G2 with B.tbn is the appendix's own "bassoons on the bass" layout (chords 1, 3, 4), the tenor at 122 is protected by c404 with a stated reason (only Tbn 2's F3 could move), and the bar is protected by c254, c264, c294, c349 and c374. The reading does not answer those reasons. Keep.
- **Harp at the fff climaxes (chords 17–18, Ex. 209).** At 160 the c269 reason (harp pedalling that hands B♭ to movement II) still holds. At 122 a glissando on the diminished-seventh setting is possible, but the tam-tam fff stroke already marks the arrival, and the harp's first loud entry is its f chord at 162; spending the harp at 122 would weaken that. Keep.
- **Cb tremolo at 122 (chord 4).** The basses hold G1 tied from 117 to 130 as one pedal (protected); retaking it as a tremolo would break the tie, and the timpani G2 roll supplies the trembling bass. Keep.
