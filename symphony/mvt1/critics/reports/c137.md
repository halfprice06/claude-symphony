## What I studied

This review rests on the reading above. I checked each principle against the v156 section files and against the PDF (all 17 pages at 70 dpi; pages 3, 5, 6 and 14 at 110 dpi). I read HISTORY.md for the protected passages and report c104 for an earlier open texture point. I did not use the corpus or the web this cycle: the two findings are local, and the chapter's examples answer them directly.

The general result is that the texture already does nearly everything chapter 16 teaches. Crescendos and diminuendos are orchestrated. Colours change at each entrance. Pedals are given rhythm. The fugato entries each have their own colour. The coda brings back the opening colours. Most bars are protected, and I found no reason in the reading to reopen any of them. I found two small places where a solo or a run does not get the treatment the chapter's models give it. Both are outside the protected lists.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 35–36: the cellos absorb the bassoon solo's last two bars at unison (APPLIED)
- **Problem.** Since cycle 54, Bsn 1 has the transition's solo head at 33 ("1. Solo", f espr.), with the cellos resting at 33–34. At 35 the cellos come in mf on exactly the bassoon's pitches, `f2 aes2 | g2 es2`. So the second half of the solo phrase (33–36) is played by a cello section at unison with one bassoon. The colour changes from bassoon to cello in the middle of the phrase, and the solo ends inside a string line in the same register.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 228: Stravinsky changes colour "as smoothly and gracefully as possible, never breaking up a phrase needlessly". He thins the texture so that "when the oboe and bassoon play the solos at m. 7, their entrance is very fresh" (Ex. XVI-13b, PDF 226).
  - Adler PDF 229: Schoenberg does not double at pitch where the colours are similar, because "doubling at pitch, the violins and cellos would swallow each other up". A section of cellos doubling a solo bassoon at unison is the extreme case.
  - Adler PDF 217: in Ravel's Petit Poucet the melody passes "without overlap and a fresh articulation". The background may overlap; the solo line does not.
- **Fix (applied, trans.ily `transVc`).** The cellos double the bass an octave above the Cb for these two bars and leave the bassoon's line to the bassoon:
  ```
  R1 |
  bes,1\mf | es1 |
  ges1\< | ...
  ```
  - The harmony is unchanged: B♭7 at 35 (Cb B♭1, Vc B♭2, Bsn 1 F3–A♭3, Vla, Cl) and E♭ at 36.
  - The cello B♭2 also fills the space between Cb B♭1 and Bsn 1 F3.
  - From 37 the cellos and Bsn 1 share the held G♭–A♭ as before. That is accompaniment, not the solo.
  - The cycle-21 imitation chain (Vla 34–36, winds 37–40) is not touched.

### 2. Bar 68: the run into the codetta is one string line and does not reach the woodwinds that take the tune at 69 (APPLIED)
- **Problem.** On beats 3–4 of 68, Vln I alone runs A♭4–B♭5 in sixteenths (cycle 93) while every other part holds a p chord under a hairpin. At 69, two flutes enter cold, f, on C6 with the tune, together with Ob 1, Tpt 1 and Vln I.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 213 on Bizet's "La Toupie": the triplet run is "an excellent example of dovetailing for absolute smoothness from the bottom to the top". On the repeat, "the triplet run this time dovetails through the woodwinds for a real color change".
  - Ex. XVI-11, PDF 216, m. 31: the run starts in the cellos and is passed up through the violas to the violins.
  - Adler PDF 224 on Ravel: an orchestrated crescendo gains "the effect of fullness" through "the intense register of the instruments and the at-pitch doublings".
- **Fix (applied, tTwo.ily).** Flutes a2 take the top half of the run at pitch, so the woodwind that leads the codetta tune is already moving when the tune starts:
  ```
  % tTwoFluteOne, 66-68
  \partCombineAutomatic R1\! | R1 |
  r2. f''16(\p\< g''16 aes''16 bes''16) |
  % new variable tTwoFluteTwo (was all rests by default)
  tTwoFluteTwo = { R1*21 | r2. f''16(\p\< g''16 aes''16 bes''16) | }
  ```
  - Both flutes have the same dynamic at the same moment, so the part-combiner prints "a2" with one p<, and there is no conflict warning.
  - The hairpin ends on the existing \f at 69.
  - Vln I's run, the cycle-93 crescendo and all other parts are unchanged.
  - This adds one beat of flute doubling, which does not affect the flute-doubling statistics noted at cycle 44.

### Checked and not changed (protected; the reading gives no reason to reopen them)
- **27–32 and 167–170: the "block" held winds (c104 F3, cycle 90).** Dvořák's repeated strain (PDF 209, 213) supports varying the distribution of a repeated tutti. But 167–170 already differs from 27–32 (brass on the head, tremolo strings). Inside the tutti, rhythmic life is already in the trumpets (27, 28, 30), timpani and Cb. I do not reopen cycle 90.
- **43–46 and 73–76: soft sustained chords.** These are what Dvořák's "harmonic counterpoint" describes (PDF 209): the violas and cellos move at 45–46, Vln II at 73–76, and the timpani roll at 76. Bizet's added motion (PDF 213) is already present in kind.
- **58–59: the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline's D♭4.** It coincides with Hn 3 and the lower viola for two beats (four parts on D♭4). The pad is pp under a p espressivo line, so the line is still heard. The change would sit inside the cycle-127 accompaniment design, and it is too small to justify reopening it.
