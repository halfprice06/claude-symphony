## Findings

### 1. Bar 82: the development's first tutti lands on the tonic with the T1 head, which pre-empts the recapitulation
**What is wrong.**
- At 80–82 the major-third sequence (A♭–E–C) arrives on C minor. Bar 82 is a full ff tutti with the T1 head in flute, oboe, clarinet, trumpet and violin I. Under it the timpani and basses hammer C in eighths.
- This sound appears at 27, 123 and 163. Here it comes 6 bars after the exposition ended without a repeat.
- The fugato then enters in C at 88 as well. The tonic is therefore heard twice early in the development, and the double return at 123 loses its weight.

**Evidence.**
- Hepokoski and Darcy (p. 207, quoted by Taylor) allow a passing tonic early in a development. They warn that, after an unrepeated exposition, a tonic P at the head of the development is read as an "expositional-repeat feint" (p. 351, Brahms 4/i). A full-orchestra statement in the original tutti voicing is exactly what makes that reading likely.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i opens its development with an ff tutti at bar 161 on D minor (harmony.csv: key d at 160.5), not the tonic. The tonic B minor returns only at 242, as the recapitulation's substitute.
- Beethoven 9/i's development (bars 160–300) moves through g, c, B♭, a and F, and touches D minor only at 295.

**Fix (development bar 6 = bar 82; upper parts unchanged).** Put the bass on A♭, so that 81–83 runs C–A♭–G (i–VI–V7♭9). The arrival becomes a deceptive crash instead of a tonic.
- `devBsnTwo`: `R1*3 | c1\f | c1 | aes,8\ff aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 |`
- `devCbsn`: `R1*5 | aes,,1\ff | g,,4-> r4 g,,4-> r4 | R1*12 |`
- `devHnFour`: `R1*3 | r8 g8\f es'4-> r2 | r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | aes1\ff |`
- `devTbnTwo`: `R1*3 | r8 g,8\f es4-> r2 | r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | aes1\ff |`
- `devBassTbn`: `R1*5 | aes,1\ff | …`
- `devTuba`: `R1*5 | aes,,1\ff | …`
- `devTimp`: `R1*3 | c1:32\f\< | c1:32 | aes,8\ff aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 aes,8 |`. The drums are on G, A♭, C, E♭ here, so A♭ is available.
- `devVc`: `… r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | aes,2\ff aes,2 | …`
- `devCb`: `… c,1 | aes,,8\ff aes,,8 aes,,8 aes,,8 aes,,8 aes,,8 aes,,8 aes,,8 | …`
- **Housekeeping.** With the new layout, Hn 3·4 bar 84 raises the "decrescendo too small" warning. Replace it in both horns:
  - `devHnThree`: `<< g'1 { s4\p\> s2 s4\pp } >> | R1*12 |` → `g'1\p\> | R1\! | R1*11 |`
  - `devHnFour`: `<< g1 { s4\p s2 s4\pp } >> | R1*12 |` → `g1\p\> | R1\! | R1*11 |`

  With both changes the build has 0 warnings.

### 2. Bars 123–130: twelve bars of unbroken ff in the recapitulated first group outweigh the T2 climax
**What is wrong.**
- In the exposition, T1 dips to mf at 22 and builds mf< over the hemiola (23–26).
- In the recapitulation, the hemiola (127–130) stays ff, so 123–134 is a block of ff at 1700–2300 per bar.
- The T2 apotheosis gets 5 bars (154–158), and its peak bar is quieter than 130 or 134.

**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/i recapitulates at 277 with the horn mf over pp strings. Its first ff comes at 292, 15 bars later, and the tutti ends by 303. Its own fff is held back for the coda (400).

**Fix (recOne bar 5 = 127).** Restore the exposition's build:
- strings and woodwinds `\mf\<` on the first note of bar 127, brass `\mp\<`;
- the hairpins end at the `\f` already written at 131;
- timpani at 127 `c4->\mf r2.`, and at 130 `g,2.->\f r4`;
- trumpets at 130 `b'2.->\mf r4` / `f'2.->\mf r4` / `d'2.->\mf r4`.

Strings: Vln I `es''4(\mf\< d''4 c''4) f''4(`; Vln II and Ob 2 `g'4->\mf\< g'4 g'4 f'4->`; Vla `es'4->\mf\< …`; Vc and Bsn 1·2 `c4\mf\< c4 c4 b,4`; Cb `c,4\mf\< …`; Cbsn `c,4\mf\< …`.

Winds and brass: Fl 1 `es'''4(\mf\<`; Fl 2 and Ob 1 `es''4(\mf\<`; Cl 1 `g'4->\mf\<`; Cl 2 `es'4->\mf\<`; Hn 1 `g'4->\mp\<`; Hn 2 `es'4->\mp\<`; Hn 3 and Tbn 1 `c'4->\mp\<`; Hn 4 `c4->\mp\<`; Tbn 2 `g4->\mp\<`; B.tbn `c4->\mp\<`; Tuba `c,4->\mp\<`.

In the test build, 127–129 fall to about 1600, and 123–126 stay ff. This continues critic 3's finding 1 and does not touch the counterline from cycle 12.

**Part (b), optional.** Separate the two climaxes by colour. At 114 use the tam-tam alone (`devPerc = \drummode { R1*37 | tt1\fff^\markup \italic "l.v." | R1*8 | }`) and keep the cymbal only for 156. The development climax then reads as catastrophe and 156 as arrival.

### 3. Bar 97: "largamente" on the development's B-minor T2 anticipates the recapitulation's "largamente" at 156
**What is wrong.** Cycle 8 gave both T2 climaxes the same broadening word. The development statement (97–102, 6 bars, ff) is longer than the recapitulation's (154–157). The listener hears the T2 apotheosis gesture first in the wrong key and section.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i keeps T2 out of the development entirely; it returns only at 305.
- Dvořák 9/i develops its closing theme at full tempo.

**Fix.** In `devFluteOne`, `devOboeOne` and `devVlnOne`, delete `^"largamente"` from `f''4(\ff`, `f'4(\ff` and `f'4(\ff`. Keep the ff and keep 154 as it is. This partly undoes a cycle-8 marking, deliberately.

### 4. Bars 115–122: "a tempo" falls on the collapse, so the retransition has no tempo signal
**What is wrong.**
- "a tempo" is printed at 115, where every part holds fff whole notes and starts to die away. A return to 126 there cannot be heard.
- The first moving material is the clarinet's T1 head at 119. That is where the return of the Allegro pulse would tell the listener that the recapitulation is coming.

**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/i, 260–276: after the ff, the pp chord (265–270) is unmeasured in feel. Motion resumes only with the p/f wind figures at 273–275 before the return at 277.

**Fix (`global.ily`, development line).** Replace
`s1*4 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" s1*2 \tempo "a tempo" s1*8`
with
`s1*4 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" s1*2 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "sostenuto" s1*4 \tempo "a tempo" s1*4`

The bar count is unchanged and sections.txt is unchanged.
