## Findings

No finding asks for a change to the notes. Movements II, III and IV are all running again (mvt2 v310, scherzo v311, finale v291), so the other movements could follow a change to quoted material. That removes one of c320's reasons for keeping the score as it is, and I re-judged the arc with that in mind. Findings 1–2 are judgements that keep the score as it is. Findings 3–6 are verifications and record corrections.

### 1. The wedge bass 111–116 is the motto tail enlarged, and the theme record misses it. Keep the score; correct the record.

At 111–117 the bass falls F2–E2–E♭2–D2–D♭2–C2 by whole bars, then G1 (Cb, Bsn 2, Cbsn, tuba, B.tbn from 114). The bass clarinet doubles it an octave higher: F3–E3–E♭3–D3–C♯3–C3 (D♭3 written), then G2 (full.midi). Bars 113–116 are the motto's tail E♭–D–D♭–C, one note per bar, and they lead into the V pedal on which the trombones play the motto's second form (117–121). This is what A&S describe on PDF 600–601: the Appassionata's large-scale motion enlarges its C–D♭–C figure, and Chopin chose a key area to enlarge D–C♯. The tail also comes back at the motto's own pitch in the instrument that first played it: the B.cl plays E♭3–D3–C3–D♭3–C3 at 2–3. It is also a chromatic sequence connecting two areas, F minor and V (review point 3, PDF 603). It is protected (c319) and should stay.

The record is wrong in two places:
- themes-mvt1.md, difference 1, says: "The bare E♭–D–D♭–C occurs in movement I only in the motto's second form (118–121) and as D6–D♭6–C6 in the violins at 156–157." It also occurs at 113–116 in the bass, with the B.cl at E♭3.
- The record's "use" note for mvtOneMottoTail says "not a third time at E♭3 in the B.cl.". Counting I 113–116, movement II's B.cl tail at II 32–34 is already the third B.cl E♭3 tail in the symphony (I 2–3, I 113–116, II 32–34). Nothing has to change in II, but the finale should not add a fourth.

Fix: none in the score. Add the wedge bass to themes-mvt1.md difference 1 and to the seeds list in mvt1.md: "111–116: bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C → G by whole bars, the motto tail enlarged; B.cl E♭3–D3–D♭3–C3 at 113–116". Not applied (records are outside my write area).

### 2. ♭VI at 160 and the deferred C-major cadence follow the reading's model of a delayed tonic. Keep.

At 159→160, V7 goes to ♭VI fff and cuts off the S theme's C-major resolution. This is the situation of K. 542 (Ex. 32-21, image; PDF 601): "a deceptive cadence on ♭VI (G major) prevents the expected resolution of the second theme". Mozart darkens ♭VI to minor and then reaches "a strong authentic cadence". Movement I does not darken it. It goes ♭VI – vii°6/5 over D (the stop chord's 138 form, unaccented) – V7 – vi (162, a second deceptive cadence) – IV – ii – V – I, and the cadence lands at p (165→166). The bass G–A♭–G at 158–161 is the P head's ♭6–5 enlarged, which is the motivic link that PDF 602 requires.

I considered making 162–166 a full forte C-major cadence, as in K. 542. I reject it for three reasons:
- The reading itself gives the alternative. In Schubert 9/iv the remote stage is "one phase of an elaborate progression … to a delayed C major much later" (PDF 587), and here the delay spans the symphony (IV 222→223).
- The finale's "No C major before the finale's arrival" rule and mvt1.md's "never confirms C major at forte" depend on it.
- A♭ major also has to stay plain at 160, because II's key is A♭ and the finale's first chord turns that A♭ into a Ger6.

The development's long run of minor areas (85–116, relieved only by V chords at 112 and 116) sits uneasily with A&S PDF 596 ("avoid an unrelieved succession of minor chords"). But each minor area is reached as PDF 596–598 allow. A♭ and E sound in major first and are then darkened (73–77, 78), and B minor at 97 is a direct confrontation tied to the movement's B♭/B♮ motive (13–14 against 41–42). The relief comes in the C-major second group (147), and the finale owns the bright major. No change.

### 3. New seed: the common-tone °7 at 73 is movement II's bar-33 chord, with the same spelling and the same C♭4. Record only.

At 73 the pp A♭ pedal carries Vln II D4/F4, violas C♭4 and cellos A♭3 over Cb A♭1: D–F–A♭–C♭, the stop chord as a common-tone °7 of A♭ (A&S PDF 589: a °7 "can act as a common-tone embellishment"). It goes to C4 (the viola's C♭→C) on beat 3. Bar 70 has the same chord as vii°7/V of A♭ (violas C♭5 at 70.4). Movement II's transition has the complete stop chord at 33 beat 2, "spelled D–F–A♭–C♭, over the A♭ pedal" (B.cl D3, Bsn 1 F3, C.a. C♭4, Cbsn A♭1; mvt2.md line 158). The C♭4 and the A♭1 are at movement I's pitch. So movement I already gives, in A♭, the reading of the stop chord that movement II uses. This is the enharmonic versatility of Ex. 32-7 spread across movements: C minor (I 26), A♭ (I 70/73, II 33), C major (IV 223). The only difference is direction: I 73 takes C♭ up to C, and II always lets it fall. That is not a conflict. The reserved item is the trumpets' B→C on the C-major arrival, not any rising B.

Fix: add to the seeds list in mvt1.md: "73 (and 70): stop chord respelled D–F–A♭–C♭ as a common-tone °7 over the A♭ pedal, violas C♭4 = movement II 33 (C.a. C♭4, Cbsn A♭1)."
