## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bar 106, beat 2: augmented octave A3/A♯5 (applied).**
- **Problem:** c071's new upper line `b''4) ais''8( b''8)` (Vln I, Vln II, Ob 1, at f) sounds A♯5 against Horn 4's held `a1` (A3). This is a simultaneous false relation between the top line and the chord's seventh, inside a suspension bar.
- **Why c071 missed it:** clash.py tests only intervals of 1, 11, 13 and 23 semitones, so it does not catch 25.
- **Fix** (Hn4, 106): `b,1\>` instead of `a1\>`. Horn 4 steps C3→B2 with the bass, and the chord becomes B sus4 → B with no seventh.
- **Why this fix:**
  - The seventh is not needed before a p tonic. c071 already removed the ♭9, so 106 is a plain V with a 4–3, which resolves more simply.
  - `fis2 a2` would need a C3–F♯3 tritone leap.
  - Moving the A to beats 3–4 alone (`r2 a2`) would need a new dynamic inside the shared-staff hairpin.
- vl2.py lists Hn4 C3→B2 as an octave with Vc and Cb. This is bass doubling of the same kind as Hn4's C3 at 105.3, not an independent voice.

**2. Bar 108, beat 2: the predominant is not what c070 describes (applied; this is c071's optional change, refined).**
- **Problem:** c070 labels 108 "Em–F♯ø–B7♭9", but the sounding pitches on beat 2 are C–E–G with the cello's F♯3 passing a semitone under the viola's G3. The cello's G3 is also swallowed at the unison with the viola.
- **Fix** (Vla 108): `g4( a2.)` instead of `g2( a2)`. Beat 2 becomes Am7 (iv7), and on the cello's F♯ it becomes F♯ø7 (iiø7), then B7♭9 follows on beat 3.
  - The viola's A is the seventh it already held on beats 3–4. It just enters one beat earlier, by step.
  - The cello's G becomes an appoggiatura to F♯ against A, a third.
- **Why not c071's version:** `g4 e4 a2` would put the viola below the cello on beat 2.
- **Check:** vl2.py reports no new parallels from this change.

**3. The route is logical and directed. Keep it.**
- **Shape:** after the fugato's falling fifths (g–c–f–b♭) and the semitone lift to B (97), the tonal centres rise by step: D at 103, E at 107, F at 111, G at 117 (4 + 4 + 6 bars).
- **Each unit ends on the dominant of the next key:**
  - 106 is V of E;
  - 110 is V of F;
  - 116 is V/V into the G pedal.
- **The two units are parallel.** Each reaches a B♭ sonority in its third bar (105 deceptive B♭; 109 B♭m) and the dominant through C.
- **Return to the fugato's keys:** the B♭m–F at 109–111 retraces the fugato's last two keys in the opposite direction, B♭ → F → (C) → G. The insertion therefore closes the fugato's fifth chain instead of digressing from it.
- **Comparison:**
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 206–211 drives with a symmetrical sequence of keys (minor thirds, one or two bars each).
  - Beethoven 9/i moves by fifths and holds one region (a, 242–280) before the dominant.
  - Ours combines a stepwise sequence with a long dominant (117–130), closer to Beethoven in its final approach.
- **The one weak link is E minor.** It gets one tonic bar (107) between two dominants (106, 108), and the second dominant is abandoned. Dvořák 9/i 269–273 (B♭ → B → e) shows that a semitone slide next to a structural arrival is idiomatic.
- The B7♭9 → B♭m move can be heard through the diminished seventh D♯–F♯–A–C, which is A°7, the leading-tone chord of B♭. No change needed.

**4. The 102→103 join (F♯7 → D minor) is convincing. Keep it.**
- **Hexatonic poles:** F♯ major and D minor are hexatonic poles. Every upper voice moves by semitone:
  - Hn4 A♯3→A3;
  - Vla E4→F4;
  - Hn3 C♯4→D4;
  - Vln II and Vln I G♯→A (Vln I down an octave).
  The bass falls a major third (F♯→D).
- The D natural was already sounding at 102 beat 3 (D6 in Fl/Ob/Vln I, a ♭13 over F♯).
- The major-third root motion matches the development's opening (A♭–E–C, 77–80). It is the "smooth but non-diatonic" relation described in the MTO and arXiv sources above.

**5. The 110→111 join is a perfect cadence in F minor with correct voice leading. Keep it.**
- **Correction to the task wording:** the wedge begins on an F-minor triad. A♭ is its top note, not its harmony.
- **Voice leading:**
  - cadential 6/4 (root doubled) → V → i;
  - Vln II E4→F4 (leading tone resolved);
  - Vc and Bsn 1 G3→F3;
  - Hn3 and Vla C4 held as a common tone;
  - bass C→F.
- The C5→A♭5 leap in Vln I and Cl 1 is the S head's minor sixth (c071).
- The cycle-57 rule still holds: no A♭ in the bass before 122.

**6. Harmonic rhythm. Report only, no change.**
Chords per bar:
- 85–96: about 2;
- 97–102: 1.3;
- 103–106 and 107–110: 1.75 each (pattern 1–3–2–1 / 1–3–1–2);
- 111–116: 1.5;
- 117–130: 1.

So 103–110 is not slower than its neighbours. Its contrast is textural (eighths against long notes, f → p) and in register.

Tchaikovsky 6/i contrasts much more sharply: 0.2–0.5 chords per bar in static blocks (161–178, 227–238) and 2.7–4 in sequences (209–226). Slowing 107–110 would mean freezing the cello's P head, which c071 protects. The steady rate across 97–116 suits a passage whose drive comes from the rising tonal centres. I do not recommend a change.

**7. Passing clashes between the two themes, accepted.**
- **105 beat 4:** tenor G4 and top A♭5, a struck minor ninth for one eighth. Both are thematic: the S continuation and the P head's chromatic descent.
- **106→107:** the leading tone D♯5 (Hn 1/Vla) leaves its register. It resolves an octave lower in Vln II E4 as the texture drops to p.

Both are normal in combination counterpoint (cf. c071 on the Jupiter coda). Fixing them would alter the themes.
