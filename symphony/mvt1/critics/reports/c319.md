## Findings

### 1. Bars 113–116: the second trombone lies under the bass's upper octave. Applied.

What is wrong. In the wedge 111–116 the bass descends E♭–D–D♭–C in the tuba, B.tbn, Cbsn, Bsn 2 and Cb, and the bass clarinet doubles it an octave higher (E♭3, D3, D♭3, C3). Tbn 2's line (C3 | C3–B2 | A♭2 | A♭2) lies below that doubling, inside the octave above the bass. The held chords therefore read, from the bottom:
- 114 beat 3: D2 – B2 (Tbn 2) – D3 (B.cl) – F3: a minor third B2–D3 one octave above the bass.
- 116 (the augmented sixth, crescendo to the ff at 117): C2 – A♭2 (Tbn 2) – C3 (B.cl) – F♯3: a major third A♭2–C3, with nothing else between C2 and F♯3.

I scanned every beat of full.midi for intervals of a third or less with the lower note below C3. Outside the protected stop chord at 26 and the B.cl solo line at 77, these two bars are the only places in the movement where this happens. Casanelles PDF 274: the bottom of the harmonic series holds only perfect intervals and the thirds come higher; over C2 nothing sounds between C2 and C3, and the first third is two octaves up. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85 gives the same model ("the widely-spaced intervals lie in the lower part of the scale"). Casanelles PDF 277–278: in one frequency band the ear can separate only so much; at 116 the A♭2 sits in the band already filled by five bass instruments and the B.cl, at near-f. There is also a voice-leading cost: the A♭ in Tbn 2 (held through 116 since c272) leaps up a major seventh to G3 at 117 instead of falling to it, while Tbn 1's F♯3 rises to G3.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-319/work/sections/dev.ily). The two tenor trombones exchange lines, and Tbn 2's line goes up an octave:
- devTbnOne 113–116: `c'1\pp\cresc | c'2 b2 | aes2. r4 | aes1 |` (was `g1~(\pp\cresc | g4 f2.) | f1 | fis2. r4 |`)
- devTbnTwo 113–116: `g1~(\pp\cresc | g4 f2.) | f1 | fis2. r4 |` (was `c1\pp\cresc | c2 b,2 | aes,2. r4 | aes,1 |`)

The chords become E♭2–E♭3–G3–C4 (113), D2–D3–F3–A♭3–B3 (114.3), D♭2–D♭3–F3–A♭3 (115), C2–C3–F♯3–A♭3 (116). Every beat keeps the same pitch classes; the bass, the B.cl doubling, the top line and the B4–A♭4 at 114→115 do not change. A♭3 now falls a step to G3 at 117 and F♯3 rises to it, the normal resolution of the augmented sixth into the unison motto. Tbn 1 stays above Tbn 2 on the shared staff; both have the same dynamics. The c087 breaths stay staggered: one trombone breathes on beat 4 of 115, the other with the B.tbn on beat 4 of 116, the tuba on beat 4 of 114. The c272 rule (A♭ held through 116, no A♮) holds: Tbn 1 holds A♭3.

Protection. "Wedge 111–116" (c070, c252) and "bass 111–116" protect the wedge's bass, top line and harmony (c252's reason: the B4–A♭4 at 114→115). None of these changes. Only the inner trombone pair is re-voiced.

### 2. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Yes. No change.

Manfred i is not a sonata movement: it alternates a Manfred block in B minor (Lento lugubre, PDF 3–29), the Astarte theme in D major (Andante, muted strings alone, PDF 30) and a return that turns the Manfred theme into a fff apotheosis (Andante con duolo, PDF 46; Andante non tanto with tam-tam fff, PDF 59), ending on hammered B-minor chords (PDF 63). Set beside it, v319 holds up in the points a conductor would check first:
- Low reed colour for the opening idea. Tchaikovsky gives the Manfred theme to the bass clarinet and three bassoons in unison, ff, with the strings pulsing below (PDF 3). Movement I gives the motto to the bass clarinet and cellos at pp (1–4). The colour is the same family; the level suits a Lento introduction that must leave room for 15–32.
- Tam-tam at the main climax. Manfred PDF 59: tam-tam fff with the theme "con tutta forza e molto marcato". Here: tam-tam fff (heavy beater) at 122 under the A♭ over the G pedal, and since c318 the upper parts fade from 123, so the tam-tam's decay is heard as the orchestra thins (Casanelles PDF 275: the inharmonic spectrum has its own shape and is recognised as such).
- A static background with a foreground placed on it. Manfred PDF 43: held woodwind and string chords f under harp arpeggios. Here: the G pedal 117–130, the A♭ pedal 73–76, the sul tasto pad 147–155 (Casanelles PDF 272, 280).
- String colour by string and contact point. Manfred PDF 46: "senza sordini, sul G" for the return. Here: sul G, sul A, sul D and sul tasto at 1–3, 57–59, 98, 147, 175, 177.

The differences are choices, not weaknesses. Manfred ends ff; this movement ends ppp because it runs attacca into the Adagio. Tchaikovsky's lyrical theme gets its own tempo and seven tempo words in about twenty bars (PDF 30: Andante, riten., Largo, un poco stringendo, molto stringendo, molto riten., Andante come sopra); here the second theme has Poco meno mosso, poco animando and rit. (47, 62–63, 66–68), which is enough for a theme inside a sonata Allegro. Tchaikovsky uses muted strings for Astarte; c076's reason for no mutes (no time to fit them at 147–155 and in the coda) still holds, and the sul tasto at 147 does the same filtering job (Casanelles PDF 272–273: distance and walls cut the upper partials).
