Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 15 "Scoring for Orchestra", homophonic and polyphonic texture (adler2 PDF 159–186, printed pp. 472–499). I read every text page in the range and rendered every page (PDF 159–186 at 110 dpi) and looked at each example: Beethoven *Missa Solemnis* (XV-9), Weber *Freischütz* (XV-10), Schumann 1/i (XV-11/12), Brahms 3/i final chord and mm. 183–87 (XV-13/14), Mahler 1/iv (XV-15), Debussy *La Mer* (XV-16), Mahler 4/i (XV-17), Tchaikovsky *Romeo and Juliet* (XV-18), Schubert 8/i (XV-19), Mahler 4/iv (XV-20–22), Wagner *Meistersinger* (XV-23, XV-26), Mozart 41/iv (XV-24/25), Rimsky-Korsakov *Scheherazade* (XV-27), Holst "Jupiter" (XV-28), Bloch *Schelomo* (XV-29/30), Schoenberg op. 16/5 (XV-31). The landscape full scores at PDF 173–174, 180, 182–183 and 185–186 are too small at 110 dpi to read note by note; I used them for layout and relied on Adler's prose for their content. Supporting: Adler ch. 7 on the oboe's low register (adler1 PDF 198); earlier reports c117, c124, c134 and c144, which used parts of this chapter or the brass chapters.

Method: I compiled `full.ly` (one MIDI track per player) and scripted three checks: pitch-class counts and spacing gaps on every beat where 17 or more players sound, at-pitch unisons held for 4 bars or more between two players, and register against dynamic for oboes, flutes, horns and trumpets.

## Findings

### F1. Bar 160: the fff ♭VI chord has no fifth above E♭3, and Tpt 3 sits on Tbn 1's pitch for five bars (APPLIED)

**Problem.** At the movement's second fff (160, A♭ major under the tune's held A♭6), beats 2–4 sound A♭ in 19 players and C in 3–5, but E♭ in one: Tbn 2's E♭3. No woodwind, no upper brass and no string plays E♭ (the cycle-12 E♭ in Ob 2/Cl 1/Hn 3 is on beat 1 only; the counterline then rises to C). Above E♭3 the chord is a bare A♭–C dyad. At the same time Tpt 3 doubles Tbn 1 note for note at pitch through 158–162 (C4, B3, C4, B3, C4).

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 164 on Brahms 3/i mm. 183–87: brilliant scoring that does not cover the violins' tune, because "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave." PDF 160 (Beethoven, m. 21): the big chord is "a full chord in which all the sections play all the notes." PDF 164 (Mahler 1/iv): "all the upper octaves are completely filled in; only the lowest octave … is left bare."
- PDF 165: "doublings at pitch usually result in the loss of the individual color characteristics of both instruments. Further, sustained doublings tire the ear." PDF 161: Weber leaves instruments that sit well undoubled at pitch.
- In the scan, the other sustained f/ff tutti chords (26, 33, 83, 122, 138, 170) each have every chord tone in at least two octaves and in more than one choir. 160 is the only held climax chord with a tone missing from the winds and the upper octaves.

**Fix (applied).** recTwo.ily:
```
recTwoTptThree: R1*11 | c'1\ff | b1\< | es'1\fff | b1 | ...      % was c'1\fff
recTwoClTwo:    R1*11 | e''1\ff | d''1\< | c''4\fff es''2 c''4 | d''1 | ...   % was c''1\fff
```
E♭ now sounds in three octaves on beats 2–3 (Tbn 2 E♭3, Tpt 3 E♭4, Cl 2 E♭5) and two on beat 4. Cl 2 returns to C5 on beat 4, where Ob 2, Hn 3 and Cl 1 pass through B♭, so the third does not thin out. Voice-leading: Tpt 3 B3–E♭4–B3 and Cl 2 D5–C5–E♭5–C5–D5 make no parallels with the bass (G–A♭–D) or the tune; the bass-to-Cl 2 motion D/G → E♭/A♭ that a straight D5–E♭5 would make (parallel fifths) is avoided by the C5 on beat 1. Tpt 3 now has its own note (Adler's affinity rule is kept: trumpet E♭4 over trombone E♭3, an octave apart). Tune, counterline, Hn 1·2, strings, dynamics, cymbal and the A♭6 are untouched.

**Protection.** 158–166 are protected (cycles 80, 86, 104). The stated reasons were the tune's register, the cymbal and the harmony of the peak (cycle 31) and the horn question at 154–157 (cycles 7/14). This change adds the missing chord tone in two inner parts and does not affect any of those reasons. It does not touch the cycle-12 E♭ on beat 1.

### F2. Bar 122: trumpets in the low-middle register under Hn 1 (observation, not applied)

**Problem.** At the development's fff arrival the trumpets re-enter on A♭4, D4 and B3, each in unison with a horn or viola, below Hn 1. Adler's Mahler 1 chord (PDF 164) puts the four trumpets at the top of the brass, with violins and upper winds an octave above them, and PDF 159 asks for registers that can deliver the marked dynamic.

**Why I did not apply it.** Rimsky-Korsakov's tutti chords (c134, PDF 505–507) put trumpets in the middle register with woodwinds above them, and c124 accepted the enclosure. Two of Dan's books disagree here, and the current voicing keeps 122 below 160 in brilliance, which the climax plan needs. If the composer wants a brighter 122 later: `devTptOne … aes''1\fff | g''1`, `devTptTwo … d''1\fff | d''1`, `devTptThree … b'1\fff | b'1` (trumpets over the horns, doubling Ob 1/Ob 2/Cl 2). I do not recommend it now.

### F3. 158–161: four-way unison on the tune's top octave (checked, no change)

Fl a2 + Vln I + Vln II play the top octave in unison. Adler PDF 165 says unison of like instruments gives "flatness" and that octave doublings are clearer for power. But PDF 166 also shows composers seeking this weight on purpose (Mahler's four flutes), and Brahms 3 mm. 183–87 (PDF 163) puts both violin sections on the tune. Cycle 24 chose this layout for weight at the peak. No change.

### F4. 181–182: the final chord avoids Adler's "dangerous" soft chord (checked, keep)

Adler PDF 162 warns that a soft final chord with high flutes "always sounds louder than it should." Movement I ends with no flutes; the top C5 is in Vln I with Hn 3 on C4 below, which is the layout Adler says would have been easier to control. Keep.
