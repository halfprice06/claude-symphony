## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 69–71: the codetta is the one tutti that should not share the P-tutti layout. APPLIED.
- **Problem:** Critic 89 grouped 27–32, 69–72, 131–134 and 167–170. From the cyclic side they do different jobs.
  - **27–32 and 167–170: keep alike.** Both are the P head in octaves over a C-minor bass in Cb eighths, and both end on the vii°7 stop (26, 170; also 138). The coda is the return of the exposition's climax sound, and the return is what the coda is for. They already differ where they should: at 167 the tune is in the brass, under violin tremolo, over a C pedal, while 27 has a chromatic ascent. Dvořák 9/i does the same: 440–443 copy 59–62 and change only the chord on top.
  - **131–134: already different.** The theme is in the low register under a falling line above. It is protected (cycles 7, 17, 37, 57) and needs nothing.
  - **69–72: should differ.** It is the only forte tutti in a major key, and it is movement I's first combination of the two heads, which is the seed of movement III's goal. In v109 the violas hammer repeated eighth chords (`<c' es'>8` ×8, `<des' f'>8` ×8, `<des' g'>8` ×8), so the codetta borrows the first-theme tutti sound. Then the hammered layout no longer belongs only to the C-minor first theme, and movement III cannot turn that layout to major as something new.
- **Evidence:** Dvořák 9/i separates the two sounds: the minor theme's tutti at 59 and 440, and the major closing tutti at 163–170 with neighbour turns and broken-chord bass. Rimsky-Korsakov: phrases of different character get scoring "most suitable to each". The broken-chord figure is already in this movement's second group: Vc 64–68 (`f,8( c f aes c'2)` at 68) and harp 47–55. On page 6 the viola now continues straight on from the cello's figure at 68.
- **Fix (clos.ily, closVla 69–71; 72 and 73–76 unchanged):**
  `c'8\f( aes8 c'8 es'8) c'8( aes8 f8 aes8) | f'8( des'8 f'8 aes'8) f'8( des'8 bes8 des'8) | g8( bes8 des'8 es'8) g'8( es'8 des'8 bes8) | <c' es'>4-> <c' es'>4-> <c' es'>2-> |`
  - Each half-bar starts on the chord's third or fifth, so the viola does not double the bass root at each change.
  - The harmony is unchanged: A♭ | Fm, D♭ | B♭m, E♭7.
  - The register stays between the horns' P head and Vln II's.
- **Protection:** 69–72 is protected (cycles 7, 11, 19, 51, 54, 67) for its tune, its P-head counter, Tpt 1 on the tune, and the counters. None of those lines changes. Only the viola's accompaniment changes, and no entry protected it.
- **Critic 89's options, rejected from the cyclic side:**
  - Cb `aes,,4 r8 aes,,8`: this is the first theme's own bass rhythm (bar 15), so it would tie the codetta more closely to P.
  - The viola turn figure: it belongs to the P-derived passages (111–116, 139–142).
