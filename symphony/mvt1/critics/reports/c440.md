## Findings

Under this lens and this reading I found nothing in v440 that stands between the score and a master's work, and I propose no score change. This is the third pass of Unit 26 under the cyclic lens (c340, and c433 under the form lens, also proposed none). I did not repeat their points (c340: Beethoven plan, mixture join, leading tone before 131, 110 as V of iv). The findings below are new checks, ranked by weight, and two record suggestions.

### 1. The transition 33–47 is the reading's own C minor → A♭ model, and it passes through the finale's S key (keep)

Bars 33–47. A&S PDF 445–446 describe the route from C minor to A♭ and print it as Ex. 26-4b: read the C-minor tonic as III of A♭, then go by descending fifths III–VI–II–V, then reach the new tonic with a line that goes to 1̂ or 3̂ with a sense of rhythmic resolution. v440 does exactly this, one bar per step: 33 C minor (the ff stroke, III of A♭), 34 A♭ (VI of C, I of A♭; Cb A♭1), 35 B♭ (II of A♭, with D♭ on beats 2–3 (Vln I D♭5, Vla D♭4) turned to D♮ on beat 4 in the violas, so II becomes the applied V7 of E♭, the "applied chord intensifies the modulation" of PDF 444), 36 E♭ (V). Then the passage does what PDF 447–448 call a transient modulation: E♭ is held as a key (E♭ minor at 37, the B♭ pedal 39–40, E♭ major at 41–43 with the four-horn call) and is then reinterpreted as V of A♭ (V4/2 at 44, I6 at 45, ii6/5 and V at 46). The S head lands on C4, 3̂, on the downbeat of 47 over A♭1, as PDF 445 asks.

For the cycle this matters because the transient key is E♭, the key of the finale's second theme, and its two colours (E♭ minor at 37, the B♭ pedal into E♭ at 39–41) are the finale's transition route in small (mvt1.md seed lines for 37 and 37–44). Movement I passes through the finale's S key on the way to its own S key, which is movement II's key. The plan is sound and should stay. Fix: none.

### 2. The outer movements use the two minor-mode plans the reading names; keep them different (keep; record suggestion)

A&S PDF 449: "sonata-form movements in minor normally move to III for the second key area"; PDF 454: Beethoven, in later works, used I – VI ‖ IV–V – I instead (Op. 111, the Ninth Symphony). Movement I takes the Beethoven plan: A♭ (VI) at 47, IV (F minor) as the development's goal at 111, V at 117, I at 131. The finale takes the normal plan: C minor, E♭ (III) for its second theme (finale.md key plan, 51–98), and its development G minor → G major, which is Points for review 8, I – v – V – I (PDF 457). The corpus file for the Ninth (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/ly/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.ly) has the same first-movement plan, with the second group in B♭ (VI).

This division is good cyclic design: the two C-minor movements are told apart by their second keys, and each second key names another place in the symphony (A♭ is movement II, E♭ is the finale's own second theme). It is not recorded in coherence/mvt1.md. Record suggestion for the composer (seed list): "Movement I's plan is I – VI ‖ IV – V – I (A&S PDF 454); the finale's is I – III, development v – V (PDF 449, 457). Keep them different: movement I should not gain an E♭ second-key area, and its G stays a major pedal (117–130), never a G-minor-to-G-major key area." Fix in the score: none.

### 3. Movement I uses minor v only as a fugato entry, and keeps the dominant as a key for the finale (keep)

Bars 85–96 and 117–130. A&S PDF 443: "As a key area, V in minor is, in principle, minor"; PDF 450: minor V arises naturally in fugal expositions. v440's fugato starts with the cellos' entry in G minor (85–86, the P head on D: D3–B♭3–A3–A♭3–G3, alone), then C minor (88), F minor (91), B♭ minor (94), by descending fifths, which is the sequence-as-modulation of Ex. 26-1 (PDF 445): no pivot, the sequence is the transition. The finale's development makes G a real key area, first minor then major (finale.md, 99–134). Movement I touches G minor for three bars at a piano fugato entry and otherwise treats G only as a dominant pedal, so the finale's G area is not anticipated. The reading's PDF 449 point that the leading tone must appear before the recapitulation is met inside that pedal (B in 117–128). Fix: none.

### 4. The development's transient keys foreshadow the scherzo at the reading's small scale (keep)

Bars 77–83, 103–111. PDF 447–448 (Ex. 26-7, Mozart K. 498) shows a transient key as a springboard, and notes that a small figure (V6/5–VI, B♮–C in the melody) foreshadows the larger tonicization. v440 does this twice at the scale of the symphony. At 77–83 the bass A♭–E–C is the four-movement key cycle in small, a sequence of tonicized chords falling by thirds (PDF 447, Ex. 26-6), with the E-major chord carrying Cl 1's G♯5 and the scherzo's theme-A bar at 79. At 103–109 D minor, E minor and B♭ minor are passing keys that lead to the development's goal, F minor at 111, confirmed by a short iv6/4–V–i (110–111; PDF 446 "any progression that expands a tonic can be used to tonicize"). The E minor at 107 is the scherzo's key, reached and left as a passing station, which is the right weight for a foreshadowing in movement I. All are recorded seeds. Fix: none.

### 5. The coda closes contrapuntally and keeps the root-position V–I for the finale (keep)

Bars 166–182. A&S PDF 456 (§16): a section may return to the tonic without an intervening dominant, expanding contrapuntal progressions. v440's last structural cadence is 164–166 (ii7 – V – I in C major, p, then E♮→E♭ at 166); the forte coda 167–170 holds a C pedal under the stop chord; 171–174 are i – iv – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V, and V (174) moves to i6/4 over the same G (175), then i6 – vii°4/3 – i over the motto bass (176–178). No root-position V–I ends the movement. The finale's 259–263 is its first root-position V–I in C (finale.md), so the reading's contrapuntal close is what lets the finale own the strong cadence. The C-major S recapitulation (147–166) turning back to minor at 166 is the same order as Beethoven's Ninth, first movement, whose recapitulation also brings its second group partly in major before minor returns. Fix: none. Keep 147–166 soft and the coda contrapuntal.
