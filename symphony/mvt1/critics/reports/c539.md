## Findings

### 1. The movement's design against Dvořák 5/i: keep (no change)

What Dvořák does. The Allegro ma non troppo opens with two clarinets, solo, p, over Hn 3-4 pp, a held cello pp and a pp viola semiquaver figure (p. 4, PDF 11). The theme then passes to the flute with fz-pp swells in the winds and a pp timpani roll, and only after that to a mf/fz tutti (p. 5, PDF 12). The recapitulation brings the clarinet theme back pp over the same horn pedal and viola figure after the development has died down (p. 20, PDF 27). The coda thins out (dim., morendo), goes Poco meno mosso, gives the horn a last solo on the head and ends ritard. on a pp chord (p. 27, PDF 34).

Our movement. The principle that matters in the comparison is that the end returns to the colour and register of the opening. That is here: the motto in B.cl and Vc at 1-4 comes back as the pp trombone chorale over B.cl/Vc/Cb at 175-179, Vln I sul D takes the last motto at 177-182, the tempo slows (Più lento 171, Tempo I 179), and the movement ends ppp. Dvořák lets one solo wind or horn speak alone over a bare pedal, and our score does the same at 5-9 (C.a.), 13-14 (Hn 1), 73-76 (Fl 1), 77 (B.cl) and 171-174 (solo Vc and C.a.). Dvořák's recapitulation starts softly, but the A♭ climax plan and the ff recapitulation at 131 belong to a C-minor appassionato (the Tchaikovsky/Brahms model in BRIEF4). The do-not-touch lists protect them: "117-130", "arrivals 47, 131, 147", "recap first group 131-146 length". Dvořák is a pastoral F-major movement, so it does not answer those reasons. A conductor who knows Op. 76 would find the same craft in our scoring: winds and strings doubled only by matching register (rows 8, 12), brass held to fanfare and power, never to tenderness (rows 2-6), and stop chords voiced as Rimsky's short-chord examples (row 15).

Fix: none. Not applied.

### 2. Discords inside one woodwind colour (Rimsky PDF 90, rule 4): five places, all kept

I scanned full.midi for every Fl, Ob, Cl and Bsn pair that holds a second, seventh, fourth, fifth or tritone for half a beat or more. Most hits are quaver passing notes in the ff P-head tuttis (27-33, 131-142), where Rimsky's own tutti examples (Ex. 97, PDF 274; Ex. 98, PDF 275-276) do the same. Five held discords remain:
- 12: Ob C5/F♯4 tritone (German sixth). Protected (c404 "12 oboe tritone"), and the finale opens on this chord.
- 39-40: Bsn A♭3/B♭2 seventh, 8 beats. This was deliberate in c124: the horns take the concords and Bsn 1 takes the seventh, doubled by the cellos' A♭3. Bsn 2's B♭2 is the recorded B♭-pedal seed (coherence/mvt1.md, 37-44). Rule 4 allows a discord in a colour that is masked by a stronger unison (Vc), and the pedal is quoted, so keep.
- 116: Ob D5/A♭4 and Cl A♭4/F♯4, the French sixth. Rimsky's exception applies: here the discord is the point, and it opens outward to G at 117.
- 122: every wind pair on the tritone of V♭9 at fff. The climax is the emphasised discord, and its layout is protected (c444, c484).
- 170: Ob A♭5/D5 tritone, 3 beats, ff, on the diminished-seventh stop chord. At 26 and 138 the oboes have D5/F5 (a third). I tested on paper whether 170 should match. Ob 1 holds A♭5 as a common tone from 168.3, and Ob 2 moves C5→D5 by step. PDF 97 (rule: correct progression of parts outweighs a brief imbalance) supports keeping the line. Every pair of notes in a diminished seventh is a third or a tritone, so a swap would change one oboe interval inside a full ff chord, where Vln I/II, Cl 1 and the trumpets cover both pitches, and a listener would not hear it. The 170 layout is also on the cross-movement list. Keep.

Fix: none. Not applied.

### 3. Where it still falls short of a master: the same two protected places

Within this lens, the distance from Dvořák is not in the scoring the reading covers. It is in two places earlier critics found and protected:
- the two-bar climb 156-157 into the recapitulation climax (c523, c533: "the one weakness");
- the loud span 113-144, about 70 s (c513).

Dvořák's own climaxes in Op. 76 are prepared at greater length and released into long soft stretches. Matching that would mean changing section lengths, which are protected (c403, c413, c493, c503: proportions). The comparison does not answer those reasons, so I do not reopen them. I looked for a smaller change that would help either place without touching the proportions. One candidate was a pp timpani roll under 156, but c534 already tested that and rejected it. Another was adding winds to the 156 bassoon layout, but c424 protects 156 at mp. Nothing new meets the bar.

Fix: none. Not applied.
