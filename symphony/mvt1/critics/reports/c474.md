## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. I give the three questions I tested hardest, each with the reading first.

### 1. Bars 47–55: no woodwind accompanies the first statement of S (no change)

**Question.** Adler PDF 238 (Ex. VIII-6) points out that Schubert gives the introduction to his second theme a woodwind-and-string accompaniment, clarinets first with the violas and then with the bassoons. Here the first S statement has no woodwind at all until Cl 1 joins the cello line at 52.4. Would a soft clarinet pair doubling the divided violas make the S colour more vivid?

**Assessment.** No. The same page says the point of Schubert's choice is that "each major thematic element" is scored differently. In this movement S at 47 is horn + cello over harp and pp strings, a colour movement II answers (c439 do-not-touch: "46-52 Hn 1+Vc unison (mvt II colour)"). A clarinet pad doubling the divided violas at 47–51 would sit at A♭3–F4, inside the range of the horn and cello tune (B♭3–C5). Adler PDF 241–243 says sustained winds help an active theme only when they lie "well away" from it. Here that would mean going above the harp's broken chords, which reach E♭5–B♭5. Cl 1 entering at 52.4 is also the "new wind colour" of PDF 259–260, and an earlier clarinet would use it up. Keep.

### 2. Bars 145–146: Ob 1 dolce in the low register just before S (no change)

**Question.** c274 moved the exposition link at 45–46 from Ob 1 to Cl 1 because the oboe went down to its "honking" low F4/E♭4 at p (Adler PDF 270–271, Copland). The recapitulation's link at 145–146 gives Ob 1 a p dolce figure starting on G4. Is this the same fault?

**Assessment.** No. Copland drops the oboe because the line descends into the oboe's lowest fifth (B♭3–F4) during a diminuendo. At 145 Ob 1 starts on G4, above that fifth, and rises a sixth to E5, then falls only to D5. The highest note is in the oboe's best register. The figure is the motto's rising sixth in the major, which needs a reed to stand out over the Cl 1·2 + Hn 3·4 pp chord (PDF 255–256: in homophonic wind writing "the melody must come through"). The exposition has Cl 1 at the same point, so the recapitulation already changes the colour (PDF 255). Keep.

### 3. Flutes under oboes or clarinets (no change)

**Question.** Adler PDF 256 (Ex. VIII-22) marks a flute placed under an oboe as "bad, especially if the dynamic is pp", and a clarinet under a flute as only "weaker".

**Test.** I scanned full.midi of v474 for every onset where Fl 1 or Fl 2 sounds below Ob 1, Ob 2, Cl 1 or Cl 2. It happens at 117–119 (flute pickups under Ob 1's held F5–G5, f to ff, protected by c429 and c424) and on one beat at 132 (Fl 2 C5 under Ob 1 D♭5, ff tutti). There is no case at p or pp. The rule is met.

### Where the music falls short of a master's, within this lens

I looked for a colour a listener would not remember. Of the reading's devices, the score already uses the woodwind pedal (73–76, 127–130), relief colour at the end (175–182), passing short gestures between choirs (33–40, 77–82), separate-then-together choirs (103–111), a single colour for the motto at each return, and a different scoring for each S statement. The one device it does not use is Brahms's single bassoon in octaves under a soft violin tune (PDF 267). I checked the soft violin tunes at 56–63, 107–110 and 177–182. At 56–63 Vln II already gives the lower octave, and the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline occupies the bassoon's octave. 107–110 is a viola tune and 177–182 is an exposed ppp motto. A bassoon would cover the counterline or the solo colour in each case. The distance that remains from Dvořák or Tchaikovsky in this lens lies in the length of the themes, which the proportion critics protect, not in the scoring.
