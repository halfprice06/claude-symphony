## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 75→76: cross relation F5 (Fl 1) against F♭4 (Vln II) at the entry of the mixture chord. Applied.

**Problem.** Bar 75 is A♭7 over the A♭ pedal (Vln II G♭4, Vla C4). It goes to D♭ minor 6/4 at 76 (Vln II F♭4, Vla D♭4), the first A♭-minor mixture chord of the closing. Fl 1 ends its pp motto tail on F5 (`es''8 f''8`), the last eighth of 75. The next sound, a different instrument an eighth later, is F♭4. This makes an exposed cross relation at the very point where the mixture colour should enter smoothly. The flute is the only moving voice, so the F♮ is the most audible note in the bar.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 536–537 list the ways to prepare a chromatic chord: its own dominant, association, sequence, or a chromatic line. They prefer the chromatic line when the chord is a colour event rather than a goal. PDF 537 also says a mixture chord at a focal point should not be weakened. Here the score already uses a chromatic line in Vln II (G♭→F♭). The flute's F♮ contradicts that line in another voice instead of joining it.

**Fix (applied, clos.ily, closFluteOne).**
`ges''2 f''4 es''8 f''8)\pp` → `ges''2 f''4 es''8 fes''8)\pp`

The flute's tail becomes F–E♭–F♭. The F♭5 anticipates the F♭ of 76 an octave above Vln II, so the mixture is approached by step from inside the line. The motto proper (A♭–G–G♭–F) is unchanged. A comment line cites the source.

### 2. Bar 120 (protected 117–121): B♭ minor between B° and C minor over the G pedal. No change.

**Problem considered.** The alternative is V7♭5 (G–B♮–D♭–F), which A&S treat on PDF 543. It would keep the leading tone through the triplet chords.

**Verdict.** The B♭-minor chord is prepared by association with the fugato's B♭ minor and by the chromatic motto line (A&S PDF 536–537). Its D♭ belongs to the chromatic descent. A&S call V7♭5 rare outside the 4/3 (Fr6) position (PDF 543). This leaves no reason under the reading to override the protection.

**Fix (not applied):** none.

### 3. Bars 127–130 (stretto, protected): no B♮ in the last two bars before the reprise. No change.

**Problem considered.** The leading tone last sounds at 128.2. The last two bars of the dominant pedal use E♭, D, D♭, C, F over G. At 130 the D♭ produces a V7♭5 sound in root position.

**Verdict.** The D♭ is a passing tone in the motto line E♭–D–D♭–C. A&S treat such chromatic tones over a held V as voice-leading events, not as chords (PDF 542–543, 552). HISTORY protects the stretto 126–130 and the accepted Hn 2 F4 at 130.

**Fix (not applied).** If Dan ever wants more pull into 131, the smallest change would be Hn 3 `g'1` at 130 → `b1`. I do not recommend it.

### 4. Bars 77–83: M3 cycle with minor triads. No change.

**Problem considered.** The cycle is A♭m–E(F♭)m–Cm–A♭. A&S describe the M3 division as usually built of major triads (PDF 572–573).

**Verdict.**
- The d4 is notated ♭6→3, A♭→E. The bass ♭6 (A♭) resolves as a neighbour to 5 (G) at 83, exactly as on PDF 573.
- Minor triads keep the tonic C minor audible inside the cycle, which answers A&S's warning that equal division weakens the tonic (PDF 571–572).
- Kopp's circles of thirds include minor-mode cycles.
- The bass 77–82 and the 5–6 shift 77–78 are protected.

**Fix (not applied):** none.

### 5. Bars 111–116 (wedge, protected): 6–10 exchange variant without the passing 6/4. No change.

**Verdict.**
- The outer voices expand by chromatic contrary motion and reach the octave on a root-position Neapolitan at 115, where A&S put a passing 6/4 (PDF 563–566).
- The descending bass has no 7–6 suspensions, so PDF 552–553 do not apply.
- The C (7th of D7/C at 116.3) leaps to G in the bass. Its resolution to B is transferred to Vla and Cl 2 at 117, which A&S allow in a voice exchange that is read beyond chord-to-chord (PDF 566).

**Fix (not applied):** none; protected.

### 6. Bar 73: CT°7 spelling with C♭. Confirmed.

**Verdict.**
- A♭–C♭–D–F resolves with C♭→C, D→E♭ and F→E♭ over the held A♭.
- This is A&S's chromatic neighbour pattern (PDF 543–545). The C♭ follows their Brahms minor-3rd notation for a context where mixture matters (A♭ minor at 76–77).
- Open Music Theory and the Kent State page give the same resolution.

**Fix:** none.
