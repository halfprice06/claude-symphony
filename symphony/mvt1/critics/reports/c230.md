## Findings

### 1. Bar 78: the E-major moment of the development is blurred, and it is the movement's miniature of the II→III join. Applied.

What is there. 77–83 move A♭ minor (77) → E (78–79) → C minor (80–81) → A♭ major (82) → V (83). This is the Appassionata's major-third division (A&S PDF 599–600, Ex. 32-19: "the A♭ changes to G♯ and moves (common-tone progression) to E major. The E major turns to minor; then the motion continues in major 3rds to C minor and, again, to A♭"), and it is the symphony's own key cycle C – A♭ – E – C. At 78 beat 3 the bass moves A♭1→E2, Vln II inflects E♭4→E4, the violas hold B3 (C♭), and Cl 1 re-strikes A♭5 on top: E major, with A♭/G♯ as the exposed common tone (PDF 586–587, Widmung). This is the same link as II → III, where the scherzo's Hn 1 takes movement II's A♭4 as G♯4 over E.

What is wrong. Cl 1's A♭5 is held through beat 4 while Hn 1·2 enter with the head B3–G4. So G♯5 and G4 sound together, and the E-major beat is heard as a clash, not as a major chord turning minor. A&S PDF 596 says composers in minor avoid an unrelieved succession of minor chords; Beethoven's E major turns to minor in succession, not simultaneously. As things stand, 77–81 are heard as three minor areas in a row. The re-struck note on beat 3 is also spelled A♭ over an E-major chord. A&S PDF 588 allows notation for reading convenience, and Schumann spells the held tone G♯ once the E section begins (Ex. 32-3).

Fix (dev.ily, devClOne, bar 78):
```
old: ces'''8) bes''8( beses''8 aes''8 aes''2) |
new: ces'''8) bes''8( beses''8 aes''8 gis''4) r4 |
```
E major sounds on beat 3, and the horns' G4 turns it to E minor on beat 4. The rest also clears the oboe's and Vln I's G5 entry at 79. I kept the colour and order of the motto entries (protected c179, c182) and changed only the length and spelling of one held note.

### 2. Should movement I foreshadow the scherzo's or the finale's themes? Decision: add nothing. The scherzo's seeds are already here; the finale's themes should stay new. No change.

The reading's test for a remote area or a cross-reference is organic connection through the motive (PDF 600–602: in K. 542 "without the motivic implication, the G minor would make little sense"). Movement I already contains these links, and none of them is a pasted quotation:

- **Scherzo theme A, second bar, at pitch.** At 79 Ob 1 and Vln I play B4 | G5–F♯5–F5–E5 in E minor, and at 78–79 Hn 1·2 play G4–F♯4–F4–E4. The scherzo's theme A bar 2 is G5 (minim)–F♯5–F5 | E5 (themes-scherzo.ly, Vln I 13–15): the same pitches in the same instrument.
- **Motive x.** The P head's second bar (`4 8 8 4.`, C–B–C–A♭, 16) gives the scherzo's motive x and theme A head rhythm (`4 8 8 4`).
- **Hn 1's unresolved D♯.** At 106 Hn 1 falls E5→D♯5 over V of E minor and stops, with no E in its own voice. This is the only D♯ movement I gives a horn. It anticipates the scherzo's held, unresolved D♯4 (III 5–6) and the finale's Hn 3 D♯4→E4 (IV 219–220).
- **D♯/E♭ as a true enharmonic pivot.** At 108→109 B7 (V7 of E) is left as the German sixth of E♭ minor (PDF 589–592, Ex. 32-10a). Vln II's D♯4 becomes E♭ and goes to F4. In A&S's terms (PDF 598–599, Ex. 32-18) this is the wrong turn. The finale puts it right: its violas take the Ger6's E♭4 as D♯4 to E4 at the same pitch (IV 219–220). Haydn prepares the same E♭/D♯ link (PDF 597–598, and the footnote that ties C minor/E♭ to C major/E♮). This is the symphony's arc.
- **The finale's first chord.** The Ger6 at 12 (A♭2 bass, violas C4–E♭4, F♯ in Vln II, Ob 2, Hn 3) is the finale's bar-1 chord, and the violas hold E♭4 in both. A&S PDF 593: the Ger6 is "the most natural way" from ♭VI to V. The finale opens with it after the A♭ Adagio.

The finale's own themes F1 and F2 should not be hinted. They are its fresh material, and finale.md and themes-mvt1.md treat movement I as the source that the finale quotes, not the reverse. A pre-echo would also have to go into passages that are protected for stated reasons: the development dialogue 77–83 (c179/c182), the fugato, 117–130, and the coda 171–182 (c229). It would add a fifth idea to a development that A&S's model (Ex. 32-19) builds from one motive. A literal statement of trio tune T is not needed either: its head is the horn call (3–1–7), which movement I states at 13–14 and 175–177.

The composer should add the four facts above to coherence/mvt1.md under a short "Seeds of later movements" note. The scherzo thread should know that changing theme A's bar 2 (G–F♯–F–E) breaks the link at I 79.
