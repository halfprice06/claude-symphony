## Findings

### 1. Timpani 85: "muta C in B" is not printed, so the B at 97 has no tuning instruction (applied)
- **Problem.** The markup sits on a full-bar rest at bar 85. RemoveEmptyStaves hides the timpani staff for the whole system 85–92, and the markup is hidden with it. Page 8 has no tuning change at all, but at 97 the player strikes B2.
- **Evidence.** The text extracted from the v55 PDF contains three "muta" lines. "muta C in B" is not one of them.
- **Fix.** Attach the instruction to the last note before the rest, which is also where orchestrationonline says to mark a change.
- `devTimp` bar 84:
```
<< g,1:32 { s2.\> s4\pp_\markup \small "muta C in B" } >> | R1 | R1*11 |
```
- In the test build it prints under bar 84 on page 7.

### 2. Flute 1, oboe 1 and bassoon 1 have no breath before the ff tutti at 163 (applied)
- **Problem.** Fl1 plays 152–166 with no rest at all, about 37 s: E6–A♭6 at fff, then c''1 at 162, then es'''1 ff at 163. Ob1 plays 154–166 (32 s) and Bsn1 plays 152–166 (37 s), also straight into the 163 ff.
- **Evidence.** The total lengths are within the corpus (Tchaikovsky's Flauti run 56 quarters, this one 59). The problem is where the stretch ends. A wind player needs a real inhalation before a new ff attack, and flutes have the least breath capacity of the woodwinds (orchestrationonline).
- **What is lost.** At 162 Vln I holds the same c'' for the whole bar. The E♮→E♭ turn, which is protected, is in Cl1, Hn1, Vln II/Vla, the cello arpeggio and the timpani, and none of those change. Fl2 and Ob2 rest here, so nothing on the shared staves conflicts. Bsn1 now matches Bsn2's `c2. r4`.
- **Fix:**
```
recTwoFluteOne, recTwoOboeOne, bar 162:  c''2\mf r2 |
recTwoBsnOne, bar 162:                   c,2.\mp r4 |
```

### 3. Horns 3·4 at 23–26: the 4th horn's low notes are on up to eight ledger lines in treble clef (applied)
- **Problem.** Hn4 plays C3, G2, F2 and B2 (F2 at 25 is written C3 for the player). In treble clef these sit five to eight ledger lines below the staff. This is the lowest horn passage in the movement.
- **Evidence.** Cycle 5 already put bars 11–12 in bass clef for the same reason. The horn guides cited above write low horn notes in bass clef.
- **Fix** (`tOneHnThree`, which governs the shared staff):
```
b2 c'2 | \clef bass c'4\mp\<-> c'4 c'4 b4-> | ... | d'2.->\ff r4 | \clef treble c'1\f |
```
- The same treatment would suit 130, 133 and 160–162 if the composer wants consistency, but those are single notes on four to five ledger lines.

### 4. Bass clarinet 1–4: the opening motto is one 15.6 s slur at pp (applied)
- **Problem.** `introBassCl` slurs from g,4 in bar 1 through c1 in bar 4. That is 13 beats at ♩=50, at pp, in the low register, which uses a lot of air, and it is the first sound of the symphony.
- **Evidence.** The cello and double bass in unison split the same line after d8 in bar 2. Cycle 16 split the coda's bass clarinet and cello slurs at 171–175 for the same reason.
- **Fix:**
```
r2. g,4(\pp | es2.~\< es8\> d8) | c2(\! des4\< c4~\> | c1\!) |
```

### 5. Optional, not applied: the 4th horn plays 116–126 without a rest (about 23 s)
- **Problem.** The G is re-struck at 116–118, then tied pp→f from 118 to 122, then played ff at 123 on the downbeat with no gap.
- **Evidence.** This is within the corpus, which is why it is unapplied.
- **Possible fix.** If a player complains, change `devHnFour` bar 122 to `g2.\f\< r4`. The timpani, cellos, double basses and contrabassoon keep the G pedal.

None of these findings undoes an earlier fix. Finding 2 changes only parts outside the cycle-10 turn.
