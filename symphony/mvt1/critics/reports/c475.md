## Findings

On the third pass I found no violation of the reading that needs a change. The places I checked most closely are below, each with its evidence. I tried to find a change within this lens that would make the music itself better: a mark that would make a phrase sing more, or let a climax or transition read better from the page. I found none that meets the bar. The tempo, dynamic and expression marks at the three climaxes (122, 131, 158–160) and in the coda are complete. They are placed where the conductor reads them, at the top and over the strings, as in Mahler's page (Ex. XVIII-3, PDF 296).

### 1. Ob 1·2 at 160: seconds between the voices (no change)

Ob 1 has E♭5, C6, then r8 C6–B♭5–A♭5. Ob 2 has C5, then A♭5 held as a half and tied to an eighth, then G5. Beats 4 and 4.5 are seconds between the parts. The lower part's head is set to the right, as Gould prescribes, and the stems go opposite ways. Adler PDF 291 item 2 asks for exact vertical alignment so that notes and beats coincide, and they do. At 600 dpi every head and accidental is separate. The alternative is to split the oboes onto two staves for one bar on the fff page, which would cost a stave on the fullest page and break the c105 plan of 17 pages. No change.

### 2. Horns at 41: "molto espr." runs across the barline into 42 (no change)

In both horn staves the barline at 42 falls in the gap between "molto" and "espr.". Adler PDF 291 item 1 asks for a score that is clear and easy to read. The words do not touch the barline, and the text belongs to the phrase that starts on 41.4. Moving it would put it under the ff or away from its note. No change.

### 3. Rehearsal-letter gaps 77–97 and 97–117 (no change; earlier reason stands)

Adler's Ex. XVIII-5 (PDF 299) assumes rehearsal numbers about every ten bars. The fugato (85), the theme combination (103) and the wedge (111) start without a letter. c155 and c165 kept the letters because gaps of 20–22 bars are normal in the models, and every bar is numbered under the basses. c365 added cues before 96 for the six parts that rest 84–95, which was the practical risk Adler's item 4 warns about. I found no new reason, so the decision stands.

### 4. Transposition repeated in the short names (no change)

Adler PDF 294 says the transposition "is not necessary" after the first page. The short names "Hn. 1·2 in F", "Tpt. 1·2 in C", "C.a. (F)" and "Cl. 1·2 (B♭)" repeat it on every system. "Not necessary" is not a rule against it. c359 deliberately made all the transposing staves consistent, following the Saint-Saëns score, and removing the keys would undo that and gain nothing. No change.

### 5. Hn 3·4 at 69–72 in treble clef with three ledger lines below (no change)

Adler PDF 300 (8a) sets the limit at four ledger lines. A♭3, E♭3 and G3 need two or three. A bass clef for four bars would add two clef changes inside the f codetta, and Hn 4 holds A♭3 into 73–76 in the same clef. The clef plan was settled at c405/c425. No change.
