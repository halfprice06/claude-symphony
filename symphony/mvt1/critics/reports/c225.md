## Findings

### 1. Middle-line notes are turned down even when every note around them is stemmed up (48 places). Applied.

Gould PDF 27: "When notes are on the centre stave-line, the stems may go in either direction. The direction is determined by context. Continue the stem direction of surrounding stems that are in one direction only." The example on that page (images checked) shows a middle-line B in an up-stemmed line marked "and not" when it is stemmed down. PDF 28 adds that within a bar the stem direction of the same beat or half-bar is kept, and that down is the default only "when there is no clear-cut case". LilyPond always turns a middle-line note down (`Stem.neutral-direction`), so the score has the "and not" case wherever a middle-line note sits between up-stemmed notes.

I listed every unbeamed single note on the middle line whose neighbours in the same bar all go up, from a probe of the compiled score. Before the fix there were 48:
- motto bass: B.cl, Vc and Cb at 3 (D♭3 between C3 and C3), and B.cl, Vc, Cb and B.tbn at 178 (the same motto in the coda);
- C.a. B♭4 at 6, the first note of its motto statement (followed by the up-stemmed B♭–A quavers);
- Ob 1 B♭4 at 45; Hn 1 B♭4 at 104; Cl 1 / Hn 1 at 54, 57, 66, 67; Bsn at 143;
- B.cl 70, 109; B.tbn 120, 161; Cbn 70, 161; Cb 58, 70, 93, 161; C.a. 101; Hn 3·4 159;
- Vla 11, 28, 78, 89, 126, 168, 171, 173; Vln I 36; Vln II 66, 101, 165; Vc 15, 54, 70, 143, 148.

The motto at 1–4 and 175–178 is the most exposed: three staves show C3 up, D♭3 down, C3 up, which reads as a change of voice in a one-line melody.

Fix (applied): a Voice-level engraver in global.ily, `gould-middle-line-engraver`, consisted in the Voice context of score.ly's `\layout` and of the parts' `\layout` in parts-gen.py. For each unbeamed single note on staff position 0 whose direction is not set explicitly, it looks at the previous and next stemmed notes of the same voice in the same bar; if all of them go up (a beamed neighbour counts by its beam's direction), the stem is turned up. Explicit directions (`\voiceOne`, `\stemDown`, part-combined apart voices) are left alone. After the fix the probe finds 0 such notes. No pitch, rhythm, line break or page changes.

### 2. Single-line solos on shared staves stemmed up above the middle line (Fl 1 64–65, Ob 1 121, Hn 1 106, Bsn 1 130). Applied.

Gould PDF 27: "Notes above the centre stave-line take down-stems, notes below the centre stave-line take up-stems." When a solo on a part-combined staff carries a hairpin, the part-combiner keeps it in the "apart" voice with `\voiceOne`, so every stem goes up, as global.ily's own c125 comment says. The c125 helpers `\soloStemsOn/\soloStemsOff` were applied only at 33–40 and 77–78. A probe of voice "one"/"two" stems with no note sounding in the other part found four remaining solo phrases:
- Fl 1 64–65 (A♭5–G5–F5–E♭5–D♭5, all above the stave or on its top line, every stem up, with the "1." pushed high);
- Ob 1 121 (E♭5–F5–G5 up-stemmed, while Ob 1·2 at 122–125 are down-stemmed);
- Hn 1 106 (C5 and C♯5 minims up, after down-stemmed notes at 103–105 in the same phrase);
- Bsn 1 130 (E♭3 minim up).

Fix (applied), in the sections:
- tTwo.ily: `\partCombineSoloI \soloStemsOn aes''2.~(…` and `\partCombineAutomatic \soloStemsOff R1\!`
- dev.ily (devOboeOne): `\soloStemsOn es''4.\< f''8 g''2 | \soloStemsOff aes''1\fff |`
- dev.ily (devHnOne): `\tag #'score { \soloStemsOn e''2\> dis''2) \soloStemsOff }`
- dev.ily (devBsnOne): `\partCombineSoloI \soloStemsOn g,4(\f\<… es2 d4) \soloStemsOff |`

Checked on the rebuilt PDF (pages 5, 7, 10, 12): the stems now follow position, and the Hn 1 phrase 103–106 is consistent. The parts ignore these helpers (parts.ly sets them to `{}`).

### 3. Forced directions inside two-voice bars. Not changed; correct as they are.

The probe also lists isolated apart-voice stems where the other player has a rest only for a beat or half a bar: Bsn 1 at 96 and 119, Fl 1 at 119, Cl 1 at 82, Hn 2 at 130, Hn 3 at 164. In each of these bars both players write separate rhythms, and Gould PDF 28 says that when direction varies within a bar the direction of the notes in the same beat or half-bar is kept; with two players on one staff that means voice directions. Turning these single notes would make the two parts cross visually. Leave them.

### 4. Stem thickness. Keep.

Gould PDF 27 asks for stems "thinner than the stave-line, but not so thin as to reproduce too faintly". LilyPond's default stem is 1.3 × the staff-line thickness. At global staff size 14 the lines are already about 0.12 mm; a thinner stem on an A3 score reduced to a conductor's copy would reproduce faintly, which the same sentence warns against. The current proportion is that of the plate-engraved editions LilyPond copies. No change.

### 5. Clef plan. Correct; no change.

I checked every clef change in the sections (19 in all) against PDF 21–23.
- **Positions.** Every change at a bar start is printed before the barline (PDF 22). The warning clefs at system ends (Hn 3·4 after 14, Vc after 170) sit before the final barline (PDF 21). The mid-bar cello clefs at 46 and 146 fall between phrases (PDF 22). No change falls inside a tie (PDF 23).
- **Number of clefs.** Bassoons, trombones and cellos stay in bass up to two ledger lines (B4 with three, Vc 88, once). That is what PDF 21 asks for: "up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently".
- **The Hn 3·4 bass clef at 103–106.** I considered treble here, since Hn 3's D4–F♯4 would need fewer ledger lines. But Hn 4 descends to C3 and B2 in the same bars, five ledger lines below the treble stave, so the shared staff needs the bass clef. It returns to treble at 110, before Hn 3's entry in the same system (PDF 23). The parts take the horns' clefs from parts-clefs.ily, so the horn players read treble there anyway.

### 6. Noteheads. Correct.

The cymbal semibreves at 159–160 print as a cross inside a circle, as PDF 26 prescribes for white crossed notes. The tam-tam keeps an ordinary head, which distinguishes the two instruments on the one-line stave (PDF 26, uses). No harmonic or triangular heads occur.
