## Findings

### 1. Bar 163: Ob 2 goes down into its lowest fifth during the diminuendo after the climax (APPLIED)

**What is wrong.** At 162–163 Ob 2 doubles Vln II on the lower octave of the tune: `a'2.~(\ff a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\>( g'8 f'2)\!`. The ff passage after the ♭VI climax is dying away here. By 164 the violins are at mp, and the harp, the clarinet counterline and the bassoons take over. Ob 2 is the last wind on the tune. It ends on a held F4 (two beats, beats 3–4 of 163), alone among the winds on that line, during a diminuendo from ff. F4 is the top of the oboe's lowest fifth (B♭3–F4). There the tone is thick and hard to make soft. Every other oboe note at F4 or lower in the movement sits inside a ff tutti (28, 31–32, 117–120, 132–133); this is the only one in a diminuendo. The F4 lands at the softest point of the oboe's line.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Adler PDF 270–271, Ex. VIII-41 (Copland, *Appalachian Spring*): Copland "orchestrates a diminuendo by cutting out the first oboe in the fifth measure, for it honks too much in that extreme low register". The string doubling goes on without it. Bar 163 is the same case: a string line descending in a diminuendo, doubled by an oboe that is heading for its low register.
- Adler PDF 257: a unison doubling "usually only results in a stronger pitch". Strength is what 162 needs, at ff. It is the opposite of what the end of 163 needs.
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II (rimsky-principles PDF 78): when only one of two octave parts is doubled, double the lower one. c187 and c197 put the oboe on Vln II for this reason. The fix keeps that doubling where it matters, at the ff of 162 and the A4 resolution on the downbeat of 163.
- Adler PDF 233: the woodwinds have specific jobs and do not play continuously. Once 163 is past its downbeat, a fourth doubling of a soft descending string line is not one of those jobs.

**The protection, and my answer to it.** The scoring of 158–166 is protected (cycle 179, kept by c236 and c346). The reason given was a diminuendo by drop-out (Adler PDF 202, 215), with one wind leaving at 162 and one at 164. This fix follows the same principle: Ob 2 drops out one and a half beats into 163 instead of at the end of the bar. The tune, the harmony, the Vln II line, the ff at 162 and the climax 158–161 do not change. c346's "156–166 pitches" protection is about bassoon breathing, and no pitch is altered here; two notes of a doubling are removed.

**Fix (recTwo.ily, `recTwoOboeTwo`).** Old: `a'2.~(\ff a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\>( g'8 f'2)\! | R1*3 |`. New: `a'2.~(\ff a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\> r8\! r2 | R1*3 |`, with a c364 comment. The A4 keeps its original length (a dotted crotchet), so the oboe still resolves G♯4→A4 with Vln II. Then it stops, and Vln II carries G4–F4–A4–C5 into 164 by itself. Ob 1 rests at 162–166, so the shared staff has no dynamic conflict.

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **The A♭6 at 31 and 72, ahead of the climaxes at 122 and 160 (Adler PDF 238).** Schubert keeps his top register back until the climax. This movement reaches A♭6 in the first tutti (31) and in the codetta (72). The finale quotes 31 at pitch in every part, so 31 cannot move without a cross-movement change. Moving only 72 down would not make 122 and 160 the only A♭6s. The climaxes are set apart in other ways: fff only at 122 and 160, the G pedal and tam-tam at 122, the ♭VI bass and suspended cymbal at 160, and a different scoring each time (Adler PDF 255). Keep, as c194, c264 and c294 decided.
- **No flute on the violins' rising peak C6–D♭6 at 62–63 (Adler PDF 266).** Adler says a flute on the first violins helps "especially as the line goes up", and the flute currently comes in only on the descent at 64. But c149 took the flute out of 62–63 on purpose, citing Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 142: a soft culminating point is better left undoubled. Adler's Berlioz example is a long pp melody, not a sub. p culmination, so it does not answer c149's reason. Keep.
- **Clarinets resting through the ff B-minor S at 97–102 (Adler PDF 238–241).** The held harmony under the tutti is in Hn 3·4, B.cl, Bsn 2 and Cbsn, which meets the rule. c157 and c184 removed the clarinets because they doubled the viola arpeggio in the register of the tune's lower octave. A clarinet pad would sit in one of the two tune octaves. Keep.
- **The unisons under Fl 1 at 139–142 (Adler PDF 250).** Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I and Vln II play in unison, with Fl 1 alone an octave above. The organ analogy would prefer more weight on the 4′ line, but c194 keeps the violins' A♭6 for 160 and c357 protects the violin unison. Keep.
- **Cl 1·2 doubling Hn 1·2 in unison at 11 (Adler PDF 257).** This overlap gives the stronger pitch that the crescendo into the f German sixth at 12 needs. The notes of 9–13 are protected. Keep.
