## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. The items are ranked by how much they bear on the form.

### 1. Bars 156–157: the two-bar climb, checked against Bizet's crescendo plan. Keep.

Earlier critics have named this as the movement's one weakness (c523, c533, c541, c542, c544, c563, c569). It is protected: "do not raise level or density of 147–157" (c083), the 158 arrival with fourteen players returning (c283), and no added bars (c073, c243).

This reading's one direct model for an approach to a climax is Bizet's Ex. XVII-20 (PDF 279–281). Over about four bars he writes "poco cresc." in each wind and string part, and he adds the Vln I octave only on the climax word. That plan builds by dynamics while the scoring stays the same, and then adds one new colour at the arrival. Bars 156–157 follow it:
- The players stay at 14 in both bars (prof.txt).
- Horns, violins and Cl 1 rise from p/mp to mf.
- The new colour comes at 158: the brass and timpani after 15 bars' rest, and Vln I on top.

Bizet's crescendo is longer, but it carries a whole sentence of a slow aria. Here the climb ends a phrase that began pp at 147, so its real length is 147–158, eleven bars at a rising tempo (poco animando, 112 to 118).

The reading gives no new answer to the protected reasons, and every orchestral addition has already been tested and rejected for stated reasons (timpani c534; woodwinds and brass c544; bass c553). Recommendation: keep. I record that the Bizet model supports the present plan, so a later form pass need not reopen this item from ch. 17.

### 2. Bars 113–143: the long loud span, checked against Ex. XVII-10. Keep.

c513 noted about 70 s of loud music from 113 to 143. The profile shows that the span is not a plateau. Adler's Mozart example says that after a tutti the orchestra should be "reduced drastically" (PDF 267), and the span contains two such reductions:
- **123–130:** the players fall from 27 to 5 (128). The mean velocity falls from 106 to 76 (127). The tempo slows to crotchet = 100 (sostenuto) before the a tempo at 127.
- **135–137:** the players fall from 26 to 14–16 for the hemiola at mf. This is the radically different colour of Adler's dialogue principle (PDF 257).

The ff blocks are 113–122, 131–134 and 138–143, at 13, 5 and 6 bars. None of them is longer than the hammered tutti 27–32 in the exposition. Recommendation: keep.

### 3. The Ex. XV-36 overlaps at the cut-offs, including its first layout. Keep.

In the first layout of Ex. XV-36, the soft group enters pp under the ff chord and is left alone when the chord stops. I checked each cut-off from a loud tutti:
- **122→123.** Tam-tam and pedal parts carry over the bar line.
- **134→135.** The mf woodwinds and strings start on the beat as the ff brass leave.
- **143→144.** Bsn 1 and the strings carry on at mp. This is Ex. XVII-10's reduction.
- **162→163.** The brass make ff>mp inside 162 under the continuing strings.

The two subito drops at 62 and 84 involve the whole orchestra, so Ex. XV-36 does not apply to them, and both are protected (c268, c448). Recommendation: keep.

### 4. Background figures and phrase joins: the Bartók and Beethoven timpani. Keep.

Ex. XVII-12 (PDF 270–272) shows quietly pulsing timpani as part of a background whose vague rhythm sets off the melody. Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273) shows the timpani playing the motive that joins two phrases. The score uses the timpani this way once, at 179–180: the P accompaniment rhythm `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` pp under the last motto. It is protected as a quoted passage (c410, c460).

Adding the device elsewhere would make it a habit. c534 makes the same point about the roll into 167. The single use at the end gives the pulse its weight. Recommendation: keep.

### 5. The coda proportion. Keep.

The coda takes 13.7 % of the time and 8.8 % of the bars. The extra time comes from the two slower tempi at 171 and 179.

Adler's Walton example (PDF 277) supports the order 170→171→175: an interruption, a silence, then a low soft colour with percussion. Beethoven 9/i (corpus, beats 1024–1092) has a coda of 68 beats against a recapitulation of 396, about 15 % of the second half. This movement's coda is 58.9 s against a recapitulation of 75.5 s. That is larger because the coda leads attacca into movement II and holds the cyclic motto's last statement.

The coda is quoted by the other movements and is protected. Recommendation: keep.
