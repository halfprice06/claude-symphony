## Findings

### 1. Bar 40: trombones enter f< under a crescendo that is already f — applied

What is wrong. In 37–40 the strings, Hn 3·4 and bassoons build from p to f; the woodwind answers join at mf. At 40 Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone enter `f\<` after six bars' rest, at the level the strings have already reached. They add mass but no audible change of colour, and the last bar before the sf at 41 is flat.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 127 (viewed): when a second group joins a crescendo it "enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes." PDF 130: the brass is the group that best turns "crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes", and the brass are added last. PDF 136: a brass re-entry at f is "less successful"; it should have a marked intensity. The timpani in the same bar already do what the reading asks (B♭ roll `pp\<` into ff at 41).

Fix (trans.ily): `transTbnOne` `bes1\p\<`, `transTbnTwo` `f1\p\<`, `transBassTbn` `bes,1\p\<` (each was `\f\<`), followed by the existing sf eighth at 41. Tbn 1·2 share a staff and change together. No notes change. Applied in the scratch copy.

Protection. 33–46 is protected for its route, proportions, medial caesura and fill (cycles 13, 23 and 51, and a later "transition route 33–46" entry); c228 added the crescendo at 40 and c234 the Hn 4 D4. This change keeps all of that and only changes the trombones' starting level.

### 2. Transition 33–46: proportion and set-up — keep as it is

Measured. P 15–32 = 18 bars (about 34 s), transition 33–46 = 14 bars (about 27 s), S 47–68 = 22 bars (about 48 s), closing 69–76 = 8 bars (about 15 s). The transition is 23% of the exposition proper. In the corpus form annotations (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations, beats): Tchaikovsky 6/i transition 164.5–350 of an exposition 72–638 (33%); Beethoven 9/i transition 146–160 of 31.875–374 (4%). 23% sits inside that range.

Shape. 33 ff hit; 33–36 solo bassoon motto with Vla/Vln I P-head entries (density 7.4 in analysis.txt); 37–40 a rising sequence with crescendo (10.4); 41–42 the four-horn call over the sf>mf chord, the medial caesura; 43–46 four pp bars on V of A♭ (V4/2–I6–ii6/5–V7) with Ob 1's anticipation of S's cadence figure and the Hn 1/Vc pickup. Each step follows the chapter: contrast after the 27–32 tutti by dynamic change (PDF 118, operation e), crescendo by adding groups (PDF 130), a partial tutti of four horns as the local peak (PDF 119), diminuendo by removing brass, then woodwind, leaving strings (PDF 130). The four-bar fill is long enough to let the dominant settle and short enough not to become a second introduction; S enters on a prepared V7 with its own pickup. Nothing to change beyond Finding 1.

### 3. Closing 69–76: proportion and set-up — keep; trumpet entry at f noted

Measured. 8 bars, 12–13% of the exposition proper; Tchaikovsky 6/i's closing part 562–638 is 13% of its exposition and Beethoven 9/i's 326–374 is 14%. The proportion matches both.

Function. 69–72 gives the exposition its real perfect cadence (V7 at 71 → I at 72) after the deceptive cadence at 68, with both theme heads combined at f; 73–76 drops to pp, the flute's motto darkens A♭ major to A♭ minor (F♭ at 75–76), and the development opens in A♭ minor at 77. The density falls from 19.4 (69–72) to 5.8 (73–76) and 4.8 (77–80) before the first development tutti at 82 (ff). The closing therefore states the key, closes it, and turns its mode toward the development within eight bars. Elimination at 72–73 (brass and winds stop, strings already pp under them, Hn 4 re-enters pp on beat 2 of 73) is the PDF 130 diminuendo and the PDF 136 pp re-entry.

The reading's one reservation: the trumpets return at 69 after 27 bars at f, and the trombones and tuba at 72 at f (PDF 136: pp or ff is better). I recommend keeping f. ff here would equal the 27–32 tutti and pre-empt the development's ff at 82, the first step of the climax plan (82 ff, 122 fff, 160 fff); c098 set the 72 cadence f in every part for that reason. The trumpets' entry is already marked by other means: it coincides with the return of the tempo, of A♭ and of the S head on a held C5, and the low brass enter with accents on the cadence chord only (c149).

## Other checks (no change)

- 37–39 imitations follow the law of register (PDF 128): each answer rises, and the highest goes to the flute.
- 41: the horn call's harmony is in strings and woodwind, a different colour from the call (PDF 116); the trumpets, trombones and tuba strike only the sf eighth so the call is heard alone (c128).
- 69–72: Tpt 2's held A♭4–G4 is in the tune's colour (PDF 133 prefers the harmony an octave away and softer). It holds common tones and never crosses Tpt 1, and the trumpets share a staff, so they cannot have different dynamics. Kept.
