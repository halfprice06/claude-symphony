# Critic 084, lens 4 (orchestration): balance of melody against accompaniment, v103

## What I studied
I took a census of every passage in v103 marked f or louder: which parts carry the melody, which accompany, their registers and their dynamics. The passages were 11–12, 27–32, 39–42, 64, 69–72, 82, 91–96, 97–102, 103–106, 117–122, 131–134, 139–143, 158–162 and 167–170. I worked from the score PDF, the `.ily` sources and a per-bar dump of `score.midi` (my script in /tmp/critic-084/scripts/bars.py). I weighed parts with Rimsky-Korsakov's balance rules from *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. I, "Comparison of resonance": "1 Trumpet = 1 Trombone = 1 Tuba = 2 Horns" and "1 Horn = 2 Clarinets = 2 Oboes = 2 Flutes = 2 Bassoons" in forte. Other points I used from him: horn marks should be "one degree stronger than in the rest of the brass"; a melody in the middle of the range "is not so prominent", so it needs doubling or dynamic grading; "4 horns in unison… produces extreme power and resonance" (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). I could not reach Adler's text online. A search found only catalogue pages and the table-of-contents heading "Foreground–Middleground–Background" (https://personal.stevens.edu/~abrick/orchestration2/files/HMU398_notes_04.html). I scanned the corpus with music21 for forte bars where only winds play and for bars where trombones carry a line. The bar numbers below are MusicXML measure numbers:
- Beethoven 9/i m. 146 (`musicxml-converted/xml/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`): winds alone at f, all eight woodwinds plus two horns, with both flutes in unison on top.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i mm. 127–129: woodwinds alone at ff, with every pair doubled.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i mm. 218–222: Tbn 1·2 at ff in A♭3–F♯4, with horns, clarinets and bass trombone graded to f.
- Dvořák 9/i mm. 438–439: trumpets at ff, trombones at f.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 103–106: the first-theme head is the weaker of the two lines (APPLIED).**
- The top line is two single reeds, Ob 2 and Cl 2 in unison, A4–B5, at f. By Rimsky-Korsakov's rule that equals one horn.
- The tenor line is Hn 1 at mf (D4–E5) plus Bsn 1 at f an octave lower. The pad is Hn 3·4 at mp; the bass is B.cl and Bsn 2 at f.
- Hn 1 overlaps the reeds' register in 104 and 106: B♭4 against C♯5–G5, and E5 against B5–F♯5. Horn plus bassoon outweighs the top line, and the whole group follows a ff tutti.
- Beethoven 9/i m. 146 and Tchaikovsky 6/i 127–129 both use all their woodwinds for a winds-only forte, with the top line doubled.
- Fix: Cl 1 joins Cl 2 (a2). Cl 1 was free, because its 102 arpeggio ends on an eighth rest. The top line becomes three reeds, about one and a half horns, which is equal to or above Hn 1 + Bsn 1. The notes, the f/p contrast, the winds-only colour, the range (nothing above B5) and Hn 1 are all unchanged. In `devClOne`, replace the `R1*8 |` after the B-minor block with:
  `r8 a'8(\f f''4.->) e''8( es''8 d''8 | d''4) cis''8( d''8) g''4.-> f''8 | r8 d''8( bes''4.->) a''8( aes''8 g''8 | b''4) ais''8( b''8) g''4.^>\> fis''8 | R1\! | R1*3 |`
- Not applied, and I would not do it: Fl 2 in unison would add more weight, but it would take away the freshness of Fl 1's low entry at 107 (cycle 74). The upper octave would put B6 at 106, against the cycle 9 rule.

**2. Bars 131–134: the low theme is masked by sustained brass in its own octave (APPLIED).**
- The theme (G3–F4) is Tbn 1·2, Bsn a2, Cl a2 in the chalumeau, and Vc, all at ff in one octave.
- Sustained at ff inside the same range: Hn 4 G3, Hn 3 C4, Hn 2 and Tpt 3 E♭4. Just above it: Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1 on G4.
- In Rimsky-Korsakov's units, the sustained brass in the theme's octave equal the two trombones. At bar 27 your own convention grades the sustaining brass to f under a ff tune. Tchaikovsky 6/i 218–222 does the same thing for a trombone line.
- Fix, dynamics only (the notes and the "low theme, counter above" layout stay protected):
  - `recOne`: Hn 1·2 `\f`; Tpt 1·2 `\f`; Tpt 3 `\f`.
  - Hn 3 `c'1\ff\> | c'2.\f b4` and Hn 4 `g1\ff\> | g1\f`. This keeps the goal of the protected Hn 4/timpani swell at 127–130 and then gets out of the theme's way.
  - B.tbn and Tuba stay at ff.

**3. Bars 41–42: the E♭→C horn planting is buried (APPLIED).**
- Hn 1·2 a2 play E♭3–C4–B♭3 at ff. This is half a trombone's weight, inside a held ff chord in the same octave: Tbn B♭3/G3, B.tbn E♭3, Hn 3·4 D♭4/G3, Tpt 3 D♭4.
- Rimsky-Korsakov's remedy is four horns in unison.
- Fix in `trans.ily`:
  - Hn 3: `des'2.\ff es4( | c'4 bes2.)\>`
  - Hn 4: `g2.\ff es4( | c'4 bes2.)\>`
- The chord stays complete: D♭ is in Tpt 3, G in Tbn 2. The register and pitches of the planting are unchanged (cycle 20). The planting is now about two trumpets' weight.

**4. Bars 97–102: verified, no change.** The tune is in Fl 1 (8va), Ob 1 and Vln I, with CA and Vln II at the lower octave, all at ff. The arpeggios (Cl, Vla) are at f. The brass counter enters only in the tune's rests.

**5. Bars 158–161: verified, no change.** The tune is in three octaves: Fl a2 + Vln I/II (to A♭6), Ob 1 + Tpt 1, and Hn 1·2 + Vla/Vc. The sustaining brass are below it.

**6. Bars 167–170: verified, no change.** The trumpets a2 at ff carry the head, with Hn 1·2 and Vla, over winds and string tremolo at f. This is the strongest-balanced forte in the movement.

**7. Bars 69–72 and 27–32: verified, no change.** At 69–72, Tpt 1 (f) on the tune against Hn 1·2 a2 (f) on the counter is an even pairing (two horns = one trumpet), and the tune also has Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I. At 27–32 the tune is in three octaves at ff, with the brass graded to f.

**8. Bars 131–134, optional and not applied.** The Cl a2 chalumeau doubling adds little at ff. Another option is to move it up an octave (`r8 g'8( es''4.->)…`), which gives the theme an upper edge. It would partly change the protected "low theme" idea, so I leave it to the composer.
