## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 45–46: the oboe's link into the second theme is a plain scale; give it the theme's own dotted cadence cell (applied)

**Problem.** Bars 43–46 are the slowest-moving bars of the Allegro: after the call at 41–42, every onset falls on beat 1 or beat 3. The only lead in 45–46 is Ob 1 `bes'2 aes'4 g'4 | f'2 es'4`, a descending scale in even values. It carries no figure from the theme it introduces. Everything else in 41–47 is motivic: the horn call and Hn 1's echo plant E♭–C–B♭ (protected, cycle 31).

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 206: material before the tune "may be derived from a portion of the song itself". PDF 207: "sometimes an idea will come from the end of a phrase which will later suggest an introduction". PDF 210 (Ex. 277 vs 278): in a soft, slow passage the lead line "should have some character". The plain stepwise version is the one he calls "much less interesting". Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 86–88: just before the second theme (89), the viola solo `A3 D4 F#4 A4(dotted quarter) B4(eighth) | B4 D5(dotted quarter) F#5 | F#5` anticipates the theme's dotted-quarter–eighth cell (theme bar 90: `D5:1.5 B4:0.5`). Dvořák 9/i 145–148 shows the plain alternative: B–C repeated, then B–A–G into the theme's first note. So a plain link is also master practice. The dotted version costs nothing, though, and ties the link to the tune.

**Fix (trans.ily, transOboeOne, bar 45):** `bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4) r4 |` (was `aes'4 g'4`). `aes'4. g'8` → `f'2` is the theme's own cadence figure from bar 52 (`aes'4. g'8 f'2`, Hn 1/Vc), at the same pitches. The oboe's E♭4 on beat 3 of 46 still cues Hn 1's E♭3 pickup (Sebesky PDF 222). Pitches, dynamics, the poco rit. and the 43–46 dynamic grading (cycle 158) are unchanged. Ob 2 rests at 45–46, so the shared staff is unaffected. The G4 eighth passes between the A♭/C chord and ii6/5. The outer-voice check shows no new parallels.

### 2. Bar 150: the recapitulation theme's fourth bar has no fill (note only, not applied)

**Problem.** At 150, Hn 1/Vla/Vc hold `c''2 b'2` and the cor anglais holds the D5 its answer reached at 149. Onsets fall only on beats 1 and 3, so the "slack" of Sebesky PDF 222 (Ex. 286 bar 13) is not taken up. In the exposition (bar 50) the harp's arpeggio fills the same moment.

**Why I do not change it.** The CA answers (147–153), the harp tacet and the sul tasto pad are protected. The recapitulation is meant to be plainer than the exposition (cycle 82). Sebesky also warns that inner motion "must not be overdone" (PDF 208). One still bar inside an otherwise answered phrase is acceptable. I record it so a later texture critic does not have to re-measure it.

### Checked and confirmed (no change)

- **E♭–C plantings at 13, 41–44, 46–47, and Ob 1 G–E–D at 145–146 before the C-major head.** Both second-theme entries are prepared the same way. This is Sebesky's "intro from the tune's initial two-note motive" (PDF 206), done consistently. Protected since cycle 31.
- **Shadowing (Ex. 279).** The counterlines move against the tune: Vln I vs Cl 1/Hn 2 at 56–68 has 25 oblique, 12 contrary, 9 similar and 2 parallel moves; Cl 1 vs Vln I at 156–166 has 22 oblique, 7 contrary, 8 similar and 2 parallel. This is Ex. 280's model.
- **Colour on held notes (PDF 223).** Tam-tam 122 and 175, and the crash cymbal at 160, all fall on held notes. Nothing falls on a quick note.
- **Climax register.** A♭6 appears only at 31, 72, 122, 140 and 160, and G6 only at the main peaks.
