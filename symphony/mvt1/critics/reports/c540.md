## Findings

This is the fourth pass of Unit 26 under this lens. c340 and c440 recorded the Beethoven plan, the C minor → A♭ route of 33–47, minor v kept for the finale, the K. 457 reading of 110 and the contrapuntal coda. I did not repeat them. I looked for places where movement I falls short of a master's in setting up the later movements, not only for rule breaks. I found none that clears the bar without spending a finale reserve or reopening a protected passage. The result is no score change. Findings 1–3 are new checks, ranked by weight. Finding 4 lists what I considered and rejected.

### 1. The dominant of C is withheld at every join until the finale's bar 2 (keep)

A&S PDF 448 (§7) says a tonal plan is "no more haphazard than is the succession of chords in a single well-composed phrase", and PDF 456 (§16) allows a section to return to its tonic "without an intervening dominant", expanding contrapuntal progressions instead. Applied to the four joins of the symphony:

- Movement I itself ends contrapuntally. In the MIDI, 174 is the cadential 6/4 (solo Vc C5, Vla E♭4 over Bsn G2/G3 and timp G2), which resolves to V on beat 3 (Vc B4, Vla D4). Then 175.1 is i6/4 over the same G (Cb G1, Vc G2, Tbn 1 G3, Tbn 2 E♭3, Vla C4). At 177 the bass moves to D under vii°4/3 (Vc D3, Cb D2, Vln II A♭4, Vla A♭3, Tbn 2 F3). The close at 179–182 is i over C, and only the timpani pulse sounds G. There is no root-position V–I.
- I → II goes by common tone: C major at 182 (Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3, Hn 3 C4 tied) becomes A♭ with the violas' E4→E♭4. A♭'s dominant does not come first.
- II → III goes by enharmonic common tone: A♭ is taken as G♯ in Hn 1 (scherzo.md, Opening). E's dominant does not come first.
- III → IV is the only join with a dominant. The finale's bar 1 German sixth on A♭ (= I 12) goes to V of C at 2 (finale.md, Opening).

So the symphony's one dominant preparation at a join belongs to the movement that must make C major sound final. Movement I's coda is what makes this work: it reaches V at 174 and then stays on G as a 6/4 bass, so the listener hears the dominant but never its resolution. This is the reading's distinction between the basic I–V–I plan (PDF 449: "the importance of I–V–I as the basic harmonic progression") and the contrapuntal alternative (§16), spread over four movements. Nothing in v540 weakens it. Fix: none. Keep 174–175 as V → i6/4 over the same bass, and keep the coda free of a root-position V–I.

### 2. Major-key arrivals in movement I land on 3̂; the arrival on 1̂ by a loud leading tone is kept for the finale (keep)

A&S PDF 445: for a strong arrival, approach the new tonic "with a melodic line that gravitates to its 1̂ (or possibly 3̂) and reaches it with a sense of rhythmic resolution" (Ex. 26-4, where both versions put 3̂ or 1̂ on the downbeat). I checked the melody note on each major-key arrival in v540's full.midi:

- 47.1, A♭ major, S head: Hn 1 and Vc C4, which is 3̂, over A♭1/A♭2.
- 147.1, C major, S head: Hn 1 and Vla E4, which is 3̂ (Vln II E5/G5 above it as a halo).
- 155.1, C-major cadence, pp: the tune is on E4 (3̂). The leading tone B4→C5 is in Vln II, an inner voice.
- 166.1, C-major cadence, p: the tune is on E4 (3̂) in Cl 1 and Hn 1. The leading tone B3→C4 is in Hn 3, an inner voice.
- Movement II bar 1: Vln I C5 tied from I 182, which is 3̂ of A♭.

Every major arrival in movement I, and the first one in movement II, puts 3̂ on top. The only top-voice 7̂→1̂ resolutions in movement I go into minor or into non-tonic chords: Tpt 1 at 26→27, 28→29, 138→139, and 161→162 onto vi. The finale's 222→223 (Tpt 1 B4→C5 on the C-major arrival, finale.md item 3) is then the first loud major arrival on 1̂ by its leading tone, as the "Do not reuse" list intends. The reading names both 1̂ and 3̂ as good arrival tones, so movement I is not weaker for using 3̂. The choice keeps the stronger tone for the finale. Fix: none. If a later revision rewrites the S head in C major (147) or the cadences at 155 and 166, keep 3̂ on top and the leading tone inside.

### 4. Considered and rejected

- **Seeding the finale's F1 head (C–G–A♭–B♭–A♭–G–F–E♭) or its E♭-minor F2 in movement I.** This would be a cyclic hint in the manner of Ex. 26-7's foreshadowing figure (PDF 448). Rejected: it is protected ("no seed of the finale's F1/F2", c389 and later), and the stated reason still holds. F1's opening chord and F2's key already come from movement I's 12 (German sixth) and 37 (E♭ minor). A melodic preview would also take from the finale the surprise that Tchaikovsky 4/iv and Brahms 1/iv get from new finale themes.
- **Giving the B-minor S at 97–102 a cyclic role by turning it toward E (the scherzo's key).** PDF 443 calls B minor remote from C minor. In this score it is reached by a semitone shift and spells the first station of the stop chord (B – D – F – A♭, c430). Making it act as v of E would add a fourth E-key seed beside 78–79, 107 and the A♭–E–C bass at 77–83. That would weaken the scherzo's arrival, and it would change the protected fugato/B-minor route. The present weight is right.
- **Giving movement I's G an I – v – V shape before the reprise (Points 8, PDF 457).** Rejected as in c440: it would change the protected G pedal 117–130 and anticipate the finale's development plan.
- **An E♭-major or E♭-minor key area in movement I (the I – III plan, PDF 449).** Rejected: the outer movements are told apart by their second keys (A♭ here, E♭ in the finale), as c440 recorded.
