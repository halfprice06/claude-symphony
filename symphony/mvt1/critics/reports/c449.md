# Critic 449: holistic comparison with Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 2 in C minor, Op. 17/i (v449; also v439)

Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, examples (rimsky-principles PDF 305-375), third pass. I rendered every page from PDF 305 to 375 at 110 dpi and looked at each one. The OCR on these pages is captions and notation noise. The printed numbers on PDF 305-375 run from Ex. 130 (Sadko, PDF 305) to Ex. 209 (Sheherazade, PDF 375). ## The master comparison

What Tchaikovsky does in 2/i, and how v449 compares:

1. **Frame.** Tchaikovsky opens with one f chord and a solo horn folk tune (score p. 3, PDF 15). He ends the movement with the same tune at the same Andante sostenuto, first on the horn (f>p) over pizzicato cellos and basses, then on the bassoon, morendo, over pizzicato chords at pp-ppp (score p. 57, PDF 69). v449 frames the movement the same way. The motto opens in B.cl + Vc (1-4) and comes back in the coda in trombones with B.cl + Vc (175-179), then in Vln I (177-182), at Tempo I. That is R-K's rule 22 applied to a frame, and it is Tchaikovsky's plan. Follows.
2. **Introduction into Allegro.** Tchaikovsky ends the introduction with the horn pp, riten., and starts the Allegro vivo with woodwind pp poco cresc. over a held horn (score pp. 18-19, PDF 30-31). v449 ends its introduction with the horn call over V (13-14) and starts P in the strings alone at mf (15). Both hand over from a soft horn to a new group. v449's introduction is far shorter (14 bars against Tchaikovsky's 80 or so), but its proportion is protected (c429, c433). No change.
3. **Second theme.** Tchaikovsky sets his A♭ theme as soft woodwind chords over pizzicato cellos, then gives the violins espressivo lines over mf woodwind (score pp. 23-24, PDF 35-36). v449 gives S to horn + cello over harp and a string pad (47-55), then to the violins with clarinet and horn counterlines (56-63). This is R-K principle 16 (horns doubled by cellos) and principle 15, and it is at least as clear in colour as Tchaikovsky. No change.
4. **Development.** Tchaikovsky combines the folk tune with the Allegro figures. v449 combines the P and S heads (103-110) and sets the motto in the brass against the P calls in woodwind and violins (117-121). That is the same kind of combination, with R-K's division of stationary brass and moving woodwind (principle 11).
5. **Coda.** Tchaikovsky hammers ff tutti chords with rests, cuts to pizzicato, then plays the slow tune (score pp. 56-57, PDF 68-69). v449 has its forte coda at 167-170, a fermata stop chord, and a Più lento cello solo (171). That is R-K Ex. 209 and Tchaikovsky's plan.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** On scoring, yes. Every rule from this reading holds, bar by bar, and the protected layouts (27, 122, 160, 175-182) are textbook R-K. Two things still stand between this movement and Tchaikovsky's, and neither is something this reading can fix. (a) Tchaikovsky's folk tune comes back whole, many times, so the listener learns it as a song. v449's most memorable material is the four-note motto, and its themes return mostly as heads. (b) Tchaikovsky's Allegro keeps a steady quaver drive under its syncopations for pages at a time. v449's P section moves in shorter blocks. Fixing either would mean recomposing protected themes and proportions (c411, c421, c433). The reading on orchestration gives no basis for that, so I make no proposal.

## Findings

No score change is proposed in this cycle. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy, so no build was needed. I checked v449's own build: build-warnings.txt is empty, score.pdf has 18 pages, and analysis.txt lists only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1". The items below are places where the reading or the master raised a real question. Each is answered with a reason to keep the passage.

### 1. v439 → v449: nothing got worse

The changes are c444 (Vc tremolo on A♭3/G3 at 122-123), c445 (Vln I 8va removed at 121-123; rest positions at 62.4, 102, 126.4, 134.4), c446 (harp étouffez at 57.1 and 165.3) and the c447/c448 no-change cycles. v439/vl.txt and v449/vl.txt are identical. The cello tremolo matches R-K principle 16 (strings join brass in tremolo; Ex. 174, PDF 344) and the appendix tutti chords. The Vln I A♭6 now written at pitch keeps the octave drop at 123-124 visible. That matters for a conductor reading the climax, and the page layout did not change. The harp damping keeps an F♭6 and a V7 from ringing into their resolutions. That is R-K principle 18 (balance matters most in held sounds) applied to l.v. strings. All are improvements. Keep.

### 2. 158-159: heavy brass ff under the ff tune. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 112 and Ex. 137 (PDF 312-313) put the harmony brass at f under a ff woodwind and string melody. At 158-159 Tpt 3, the trombones, B.tbn and the tuba hold ff chords under the S tune. The tune is not left to the woodwind, though. It is doubled at ff by Hn 1-2 a2 and Tpt 1-2, which is R-K's own fix at PDF 108-109 (double the upper parts when the brass is strong). The heavy brass also stays ff into the fff at 160, and the c244 note in recTwo.ily says that is on purpose. Lowering it to f would make 158 weaker than the B-minor S tutti at 97. No change.

### 3. 175-179: soft trombones under strings, with tam-tam. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 105, Ex. 140 (PDF 315: three trombones ppp under muted strings) and Ex. 156 (PDF 330: three flutes and three trombones ppp over divided low strings). The v449 chorale is the same texture: Tbn 1-2 and B.tbn pp dolce, B.cl and Vc doubling the bass, strings pp. Tchaikovsky's coda goes lighter (pizzicato and bassoon, PDF 69). The brass chorale is the darker way to end and still meets R-K's tutti rule (PDF 120: tutti seldom p), because 175-179 is a partial group, not a tutti. The coda is protected and quoted by movement II. No change.

### 4. 162-163: Hn 2 and Hn 4 hold through 163 after "ff >" with no end level. Keep in this cycle.

Reading: R-K Ex. 143 (PDF 317) and Ex. 168-169 (PDF 338-340) mark each group's diminuendo to a level ("dim. poco a poco", "f dim." to p). At 162 the trumpets and trombones go ff > mp, but Hn 2 (c'1\ff\> | c'1 | c'2\p) and Hn 4 (e1\ff\> | f1 | d2\p) reach p only at 164. This was raised in c188 and kept as part of the protected exit order. The sound is the same either way, because the hairpin ends inside the held note. Changing it is a dynamics-marking question for the expression or engraving lens, not a change in sound. No change in this cycle.

### 5. Introduction length against Tchaikovsky. Keep.

Tchaikovsky's introduction states its tune three times (horn, bassoon, then woodwind over string figures) before the Allegro. v449 states the motto twice (B.cl + Vc, then C.a.) and adds the rising-sixth chain. R-K PDF 116 says the choice depends on whether a passage complements or contrasts with what follows. v449's short Lento makes a contrast, and the long return in the coda makes up for it. Proportions are protected (c429: "intro length and bar 12"). No change.
