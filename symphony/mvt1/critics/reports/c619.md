## Findings

### 1. Nothing got worse between v609 and v619 (no change)

Only three cycles in v610-v619 changed the score:
- **c614, bar 158.** Tbn 1 E4 (was C4). The MIDI at 158.1 shows G1 (Cb, Cbsn, tuba), G2 (B.tbn, Bsn 2, timpani), G3 (Hn 1-2, Tbn 2, Bsn 1, Vc), C4 (Tpt 3, Vla), E4 (Tbn 1, Vla), G4 and G5 above. Before c614, the brass had only the bare fourth G-C on the downbeat, and the cadential 6/4's sixth came with the tune on beat 2. Now the brass hold a complete 6/4 from the downbeat, laid out as Casanelles describes the series (PDF 274): octaves and fifths at the bottom and the sixth higher up. The 4-3 is still in Tpt 3 (C4-B3) and the violas. vl.txt for v609 and v619 is identical, so the change added no parallels. On page 16 the trombone dyad prints as one stem and needs no label. Better, not worse.
- **c615, beams.** Four beams (harp RH 56.3 and 62.3, violas 115.1 and 115.3) now sit just outside the stave line. Page 6 and page 11 print cleanly.
- **c609-c618 other cycles.** No change, apart from record lines in coherence/mvt1.md.

### 2. Holistic judgement against Rachmaninoff 2/i (no change)

I compared the two movements where they do the same job.

- **Main theme.** Rachmaninoff gives his first theme to Vln I, Vln II and violas in unison, ff "con passione", doubled by Cl 1, over pizz. basses and the piano's arpeggios (R PDF 2). The tune is long and moves slowly (minims and semibreves) against fast accompaniment. Our P at 15-26 also moves against a running bass and hammered chords, and its head (G-E♭ sixth, falling chromatic close) is as easy to remember as his. His tune is wider in breath: eight-bar phrases over a static chord. Ours turns faster, which suits an Allegro appassionato rather than a Moderato. The difference is one of tempo, not of quality.
- **Second theme and its counter-lines.** Rachmaninoff's second theme (Un poco più mosso, R PDF 8) is in the piano. Under it, desk-divided Vln I hold pp chords, the cellos give sf off-beat strokes, and Hn 1 plays pp a slow chromatic line in long notes. That is the orchestra as a pad plus one soft wind line, as in principles 11 and 13. Our S at 47-55 has the pad (Vln II metà, Vla div. pp) and the harp, but no soft wind line. The wind counter-line comes at the second phrase, 56-63, in Cl 1 + Hn 2. I considered adding a pp horn line under 47-55. I reject it. The "S at 46.4-55" and "47-55 colour" are protected (c351, c479), the harp fill at 50.3-4 was rejected (c601), and Hn 1 already carries the tune there. A second horn line in the same band (principle 11) would blur the one horn the listener should follow. Our counter-line comes later than Rachmaninoff's, but it does the same work.
- **Recapitulation as a climax.** Rachmaninoff's recapitulation (Maestoso, alla marcia, R PDF 32) brings back the main theme ff in the strings. The trumpets, trombones and timpani play the march rhythm mf under it, two levels below the tune. The dynamic marks give the balance he wants to hear, as in principle 1. At our 131 (page 13) the P head is low (Cl a2 ff, Bsn a2 ff, Tbn a2 f, Vc ff), the violins have the ff descant, the off-beat viola dyads are f (c264), and the held horns, trumpets and B.tbn are f. The accompaniment is a level under the tune and the trombones, which carry more weight, are written a level under the woodwinds and cellos on the same line. This is the same grading Rachmaninoff uses, and the recapitulation arrives as the climax goes on, not after it has dissolved. Master's level.
- **Second theme in the recapitulation.** Rachmaninoff gives it to solo Hn 1, p dolce and undoubled, over pp repeated string chords, with the clarinets and bassoons answering pp (reh. 13, R PDF 38). At our 147, Hn 1 plays the tune in unison with the violas, mf dolce, with the cellos an octave lower, over the Vln II sul tasto pad pp. The C.a. answers p in the gaps. The texture is the same and the grading is the same (tune two levels over the pad). The only difference is the horn's doubling. I keep it. 47/147 together is protected (c454). Casanelles's point about unison doubling (PDF 277-278) cuts both ways: horn and violas in unison make one fused sound, warmer than either alone, which suits a tonic return in C major. A bare solo horn over a pad and harp is movement II's theme A (Hn 1 over harp, II 7-14). Here, three minutes before it, a bare horn solo would take that colour away from movement II.
- **Development peak.** Rachmaninoff builds his peak (Allegro, R PDF 28-30) on held f horns and woodwinds over a moving bass, with the trumpets entering mf marc. on a dotted figure and the timpani rolling. Our 117-122 does the same over the G pedal, with the trombone motto as the moving line, to fff at 122 with the tam-tam. Both pile up layers before the peak instead of only getting louder. Master's level.
- **Coda.** Rachmaninoff's coda drops to pizz. strings pp with horn pairs holding chromatic sighs (G-F#, D♭-C) pp (R PDF 44). Then come a poco a poco accelerando and a ff ending. Our coda has the same drop (162-166, then 171-174). The horn sighs have their counterpart in our trombone chorale with its motto D-D♭-C (175-179). Our soft attacca ending is protected (finale reserve, c409, c599) and I do not answer its reason: movement IV needs the loud C major.

Would a conductor who knows Rachmaninoff 2/i believe a master wrote this? On orchestral grading, the layering of climaxes and the treatment of the recapitulation, yes. Where Rachmaninoff does more, it comes from the solo piano (constant figuration, a second melodic voice over the orchestra), which a symphony does not have, or from passages that are protected for reasons I do not answer: a longer development and a fast loud ending.

### 3. Things I looked for and did not find (no change)

- **Resulting loudness of decaying sounds (principles 1 and 12).** The harp is marked mp at 47 (c519), mf and f at 156-157 under a mp-mf texture, f at 162 under ff, and mp/p at 163-166 with étouffez marks. In every case the decaying sound is written at or above the level of the sustained parts around it. No harp mark is too soft.
- **Low thirds (principle 5).** The two hits at 60.3 and 61.3 are passing eighths of the cello arpeggio and last half a beat. No held chord has a third below C3.
- **Weak registers at soft dynamics (principle 6).** No flute note below A4. No soft melody in the clarinet throat (c337 moved 156-157). Loud brass close seconds only where earlier critics chose them (83, 122-123, 170).
- **Masking of a soft solo (principle 11).** The solo lines with soft accompaniment are the horn call 13-14 (alone), C.a. 5-9 (string pp), Hn 1/Vc S 47-55 (pads pp), Cl 1 p dolce 44-46 (Vla/Vc pp), C.a. p counter 147-155 (between tune phrases), solo cello mp at 171-174 (violas pp, bassoons pp, C.a. p in a different register) and Vln I pp espr. 177-182 (above the pp trombones). In each, the solo is either alone in its band or a level above what shares its band.

I found no place where the reading or the master supports a change that the history has not already made or protected. The score is complete by this lens, and I recommend no change.
