## Findings

The reading gives one test that matters for form: is the succession of keys a purposeful plan, with each key introduced by a pivot or sequence and confirmed by a cadence with II or IV and V? I checked every key area of the movement against it. Every one passes. Movement I follows the I–VI ‖ IV–V–I plan that A&S give for Beethoven's Op. 111 and Ninth Symphony (PDF 454), and its development fills the dominant region with the VI-above, IV-below neighbours of PDF 456. I found no change that the reading supports. Below are the five places where I looked hardest, each with the reason to keep it. None of them is a score change.

### 1. The large tonal plan (whole movement): keep

Bass reduction from the MIDI dump: C (15–32) – A♭ (47–76, confirmed at 55 and 72) ‖ A♭ (77, 82) – G (83–87, then the fugato in G minor) – descending fifths to B♭ minor (96) – B minor (97–102) – D minor – E minor – F minor (103–111) – G (117–130) – C (131). This is A&S's I–VI ‖ IV–V–I plan (PDF 454). Inside the development the bass outlines VI (82) – V (83) … IV (111) – V (117), which is the VI–V–IV–V shape of Ex. 26-17 (PDF 456). Corpus check: in Beethoven 9/i the MCTL harmony file (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1/form/harmony.csv) gives d (1) – B♭ (from about bar 49 on the file's onsets, ÷2 for 2/4) ‖ g (160) – c – g – B♭ – a – F – d (295) – D (301). Beethoven puts his iv at the start of the development; this score puts it at the end, which is the Mozart K. 488 order (Ex. 26-14, IV → aug6 → V). Both orders are in the reading. No change.

### 2. The transition's cadence in III at 41 (33–46): keep

At 39–40 B♭7 resolves to E♭ at 41 with sf brass and timpani, so for a moment the music sounds like it is cadencing in III, the usual minor-mode second key (PDF 449). D♭ enters at 43 and the bass falls to D♭ at 44, which makes E♭ the V of A♭. This is the "transient modulation" of PDF 447–448 (Ex. 26-7: an apparent key becomes a pivot in a larger modulation). It is also Ex. 26-4's route from C to A♭ (PDF 446). After the pivot the new key gets a full cadential progression with II: V4/2–I6–ii6/5–V7–I at 44–47 (PDF 443). The top line steps G4–A♭4 into 47, and the S head starts on 3̂ on the downbeat (PDF 445). Protected since c183 (plain V at 41–42); the reading supports it. No change.

### 3. The leading tone before the recapitulation (127–130): keep

From beat 2 of 128 to the downbeat of 131 no B♮ sounds. The motto stretto (Cl 1 127, Vla 129, Bsn 1 and Vln II 130) puts G, E♭ and C over the G pedal, and the last beat of 130 is G–D–F with C (Cl 1 and Vla anticipate the tonic). PDF 449 says that before the recapitulation "the leading tone must appear", and PDF 456 says the returning V "often supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly to I". I tested both against the score. The rule on PDF 449 is about a minor V. Here the V is major from 117: B♮ sounds at 117–119 and 122–124, and the 4–3 at 125–126 resolves onto it (Hn 3, Cl 2, Vla, Vc B3 at 126). The 7th is present (F at 117–124 and Hn 2 F4 at 130). I considered adding Hn 3 B3 at 129–130 (it rests 127–130 and plays C4 at 131, so B3→C4 would resolve within the part). I rejected it. At beats 2–4 of 130 it would sound a minor second against the C4 anticipations in Cl 1 and Vla, in the same register, at f. It would also add a leading tone that the ear has heard through most of the ten preceding bars. The passage is protected (117–130, c142/c182). The reading does not give a reason to reopen it. No change.

### 4. Development keys entered without a dominant (97, 103): keep

B minor at 97 follows B♭ minor by a semitone shift, and D minor at 103 follows B minor's V7 with no dominant of D. PDF 443 places modulations to remote keys outside diatonic modulation, so this unit does not judge them. Under PDF 447–448 both keys are transient: B minor has its ii°7–V7 (100–102) but no tonic cadence, and D minor turns at once to V of E minor (106). The keys that the plan rests on are each confirmed by a dominant, as PDF 443/445 require: E minor by B7 (106–107), F minor by cad. 6/4–V (110–111), and the fugato's B♭ minor by iv–V–i (95–96). The arrivals at 97, 103 and 111 have been protected since c096/c116. No change.

### 5. Recapitulation: IV to V before the second group, and its confirmation (139–166): keep

PDF 451–452: a tonicized IV late in the piece moves to V. The recapitulation does this at 139 (iv) – 140–141 (N) – 142 (V/V) – 143 (i) – 144–146 (V9 with E♮, which announces the major). The C-major second group is confirmed twice: by V7–I at 154–155 (bass G2–C2; the tune ends on E4) and by IV–ii°7–V7–I at 163–166 (PDF 443). The climax at 156–160 uses ♭VII as a link to IV and V (PDF 455, Ex. 26-16), followed by two deceptive moves (159→160 ♭VI, 161→162 vi). PDF 456 describes this kind of extension of the dominant. No change.
