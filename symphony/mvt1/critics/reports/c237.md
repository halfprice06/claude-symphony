## Findings

### 1. Fugato, bars 94–95: the fourth entry has fewer moving parts than the third, and the viola–bassoon pairing breaks (applied)

**Bars and what is wrong.** The fugato (85–96) sets up a clear rotation. Each voice states the subject, then the countersubject, then a free voice:
- Cellos: subject 85–87, countersubject 88–90, free voice 91–93 (`c'2. es'4~ | es'4 des'4 c'2~ | c'4 bes4 f2`).
- Violas with Bsn 1 an octave below: subject 88–90, countersubject 91–93.
- Vln II with the cor anglais: subject 91–93, countersubject 94–95.

At the fourth entry (Vln I + Fl 1 on F, bar 94) the violas should take the free voice. Instead they hold F4 for five beats and then drop to B♭3 (`f'1~ | f'4 ges'4 bes2`). Bsn 1 leaves the violas and doubles the cellos' held B♭3. Bars 91–93 have three moving parts over the bass (subject, countersubject, free voice). Bars 94–95 have two (subject, countersubject) over three held notes. The web thins exactly where the entries pile up and the crescendo to the ff B-minor statement at 97 begins.

**Evidence.**
- Adler's account of the Jupiter finale (adler2 PDF 176): "as the texture thickens" Mozart strengthens the lines, and from m. 389 every element keeps moving, with its colour held constant.
- On PDF 175–176 each element is recognizable by "its own characteristic rhythmic makeup". A held semibreve has none.
- Adler also says to "keep an established color intact long enough to carry out its assignment" (PDF 176). The viola + Bsn 1 pairing has run for six bars (88–93) and is dropped for no textural reason at 94.
- Cycle 142 already made every voice follow the sequential bass (G–C–F–B♭; A&S Unit 17). 94–95 is the one place where a voice does not follow it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-237/work/sections/dev.ily).** Transpose the cellos' 91–93 free voice up a fourth into the violas, so it follows the F entry as the cellos' line followed the C entry. Bsn 1 stays with the violas an octave below, as at 88–93.

```
devVla, 94–95:    f'2.( aes'4~ | aes'4\< ges'4 f'2) |      % was f'1~( | f'4\< ges'4) bes2 |
devBsnOne, 94–95: f2.( aes4~ | aes4\< ges4 f2) |          % was bes2 bes2~ | bes2\< des2 |
```

The A♭4 is prepared on beat 4 of 94 and resolves 4–3 to G♭4 over the E♭ bass at 95. This is the same suspension the cellos have at 92 (E♭4 to D♭4 over B♭). Beats 3–4 of 95 go to F4, which leads to the violas' D♭4 at 96. The cellos keep their held B♭3–D♭3 as a fifth, background part, and the double basses keep their bass line. Hairpins stay where they were (95 beat 1).

**Protection.** Series-3 protection covers the fugato subject, countersubject and entries (c227, c245, c433). This change touches none of them: it changes only the free voice and its doubling. The older "fugato 85–96" items from series 1 kept the entries and colours, and both are unchanged. This does not undo c142: it carries c142's sequence into the last entry.

## Checked and kept (no change recommended)

- **103–106, P head in woodwinds only (Ob 2 + Cl 1·2).** This breaks Adler's Meistersinger rule (string + woodwind + brass, PDF 178), but keep it. The strings rest on purpose after the ff tutti at 97–102 (c74/c76), and the f/p contrast of 103–110 is protected (c357, c398). Adler frames the rule as the way to get the "big" sound (PDF 179), and this passage is meant to be lighter. The S head beneath it has all three families (Hn 1, Bsn 1, Vc).
- **117–121, motto without a string.** Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and Bsn 1 in unison at ff. Keep it. Adler's Holst example gives the middleground to brass alone (two trombones, then six horns in unison; PDF 181). All the strings are needed for the foreground head and the triplet and pedal background.
- **167–169, P head without a woodwind (Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2, Vla).** I tried moving Bsn 1 from the cellos' tenor line onto the head. That would undo c150, which put Bsn 1 on the cellos' line because the line was covered at f under the ff brass (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 18). The head is already carried by four brass and a string section, so c150's reason holds. Keep it.
- **91–96 all voices at f.** Adler's Schoenberg rule (PDF 184: subsidiary voices softer) does not apply here. This is a crescendo to the ff at 97, and the elements are told apart by rhythm and colour, as in Mozart's "each theme must be heard at good strength" (PDF 176). The softer cello under the entry at 88 (c197) is the right place for the Schoenberg principle, and it is already in the score.
