### 7. The arc needs no musical change (judgement; keep)

I asked where movement I, seen from the whole symphony, falls short of a master's work. I tested three candidates against the reading.

- **A hint of the finale's F1 or F2, or of the scherzo's trio tune.** Tchaikovsky 5 and Dvořák 9 plant later themes early. This movement already holds the seeds that the later movements answer: the German sixth at 12, the E♭-minor sound at 37, the B♭ pedal at 39–44, 78–79 at the scherzo's pitch, the D♯ at 106 and 108, and the timpani pulse at 179–180. A new seed now would be answered nowhere, because the other three movements are closed. c260, c330 and c420 rejected this for the same reason. Rejected.
- **A firmer close.** For example, a forte C-major cadence before the soft coda, or a held tonic in place of the stepwise ending. A&S PDF 386–387 and 391 support keeping the major back, so that the finale's 223 is the first strong C major. The ppp Picardy chord at 182 already carries the "startling power" of the soft form and hands its C5 to movement II. Rejected.
- **c519's harp at 47 against movement II's colour.** The S head with Hn 1 over the harp at 46–52 is the colour of movement II's theme A (Hn 1 over the harp, II 7–14). At II 7–14 the harp plays pp to p under Hn 1's p. At I 47 the harp is now mp, level with Hn 1. The relation still holds: in both movements the harp sits at or just under the horn. Movement II keeps its solo fresh by dropping the cellos, not by changing the harp level. Keep.

The emotional curve is sound:
- The soft low motto opens the movement.
- Three A♭ climaxes rise ff, fff, fff (82, 122, 160), and each is broken off as Adler2 PDF 38 describes.
- The C-major recapitulation stays soft, and the coda dies away to a ppp C major. That leaves the Adagio a soft start and the finale its brass, its C7 and its forte major.
