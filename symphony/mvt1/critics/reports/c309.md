## Findings

### 1. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Yes, in the way Sibelius 2/i is built. Keep.

Sibelius frames his first movement with an accompaniment idea: the strings' repeated-note pulse of the opening (printed p. 161–162) returns at the very end under "diminuendo al pp, poco rallent." (p. 195), with the woodwind theme reduced to fragments. This score does the same with its own material: the first group's bass pulse (Cb `c4 r8 g,8`, 15–22) comes back at the recapitulation's cadence (Cb 143) and in the timpani at 179–180 under the last motto, and the motto's low colour of 1–4 returns at 175–179. Sibelius's development peak at letter M (p. 181) is three bars of "cresc. possibile" over a string ostinato, an fff brass chord, and an immediate "dim. molto" with the strings continuing; bars 111–126 here follow the same plan (six-bar "mp cresc." wedge 111–116, brass motto, fff at 122 over the G pedal, collapse 123–126 with tam-tam). Sibelius places the movement's last loud point (letter S, "Tenuto", p. 194) a few bars before the end and then, after short wind and brass interjections, lets the texture thin to strings alone (p. 195); here the ff stop chord at 170 and the Più lento coda do the same. The harmonic surface also passes the assigned reading at every point I checked by script and by eye (table above): every V7 resolves its leading tone and seventh, the V9 at 122 resolves 9–8 as a neighbour (A&S PDF 463–464), the climactic ♭II at 140 is in sudden ff with ♭2 falling to 1̂ at 141.3 (A&S PDF 482–483), ♭6 always reaches 5 before ♮6 (A&S PDF 388), and both augmented sixths stand before structural dominants (A&S PDF 503). Where Sibelius is more original is in phrase rhythm (irregular groups and pauses); this score's regular four-bar hypermeter is the norm of the named models (Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák), and the proportions are protected (c215, c243, c283). No change.

(b) 28 beat 3 Tpt 2 F4 (c302): beat 3 is now a clean vii°6/5 (B–D–F–A♭) passing to iv4/2 at 29, which is the passing function A&S give this position (PDF 375). ### 3. Coda 177→178: Tbn 2 doubles the seventh of V4/3 and takes it up. Keep, with reason.

At 177 beat 4 the chord is V4/3 (D2/D3 bass, F3 Tbn 2, G3 viola, D4 Tbn 1, F4 Vln II, B4 Cl 1). At 178 Vln II resolves F4→E♭4, but Tbn 2 takes its F3 up to G3. A&S state that the 7th resolves down in every seventh chord (PDF 375, 399), so the doubled 7th rising is against the letter of the reading. I tried the two possible repairs on paper. Tbn 2 F3→E♭3 at 178 makes octaves E♭3–F3 / E♭4–F4 with Vln II into the D♭ chord on beat 3 of 178; holding E♭3 through 178 puts a major second D♭3–E♭3 low in the pp trombones. Both are worse than the present line, the seventh is resolved in Vln II in the next octave, and the trombone trio's open C–G–C at 178 is the archaic chorale sound that the coda is built on. The chorale pitches are protected (c433, c455) and quoted in coherence/mvt1.md. Keep.

### 4. Bars 59→60: outer-voice cross relation G5 / G♭2. Keep (already answered).

A&S PDF 420 rule 1 (keep cross relations off the top voice unless it moves by step) is broken here: Vln I G5 on beats 3–4 of 59, bass G♭2 on the downbeat of 60, while the top leaps G5→B♭5. c303 kept it because the cello's arpeggio puts G3 and G♭ in one inner line first, and every repair changes the S restatement or the bass G♭–F♭–E♭ that movement II quotes. A&S PDF 420 also allows the cross relation "between an inner voice and the bass" when the bass carries an active chromatic tone, which is the case here (G♭ is the bass of a descending chromatic line, principle 21). I agree with keeping it.

### 5. Seams at 96→97 and 102→103 (B♭ minor → B minor; F♯ → D minor). Keep.

Both are outer-voice chromatic successions at key seams. A&S PDF 584–585 explain that modulation by chromatic inflection is abrupt by design and is right when the inflection belongs to the work's motivic plan; here the semitone shift is the motto's own semitone and the joins are protected (c349). No change.
