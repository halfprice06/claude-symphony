## Findings

On divisi, bowing and pizzicato, the string notation is at a professional standard. Every *div.* has its *unis.*, *metà* has its *tutti*, *Solo* has its *Tutti*, *sul tasto* has its *ord.*, and every pizz./arco change has time or an up-bow before it. I found two real gaps. Both are about long held notes in the low strings, where the reading asks for an instruction the score gives elsewhere in the same situation.

### 1. Cb 41–43: a 12-beat tie from sf without "change bow at will" (applied)

**Bars:** Cb 41–43, `es,1\sf\>~ | es,1\mf\>~ | es,1\pp`, Allegro, crotchet = 126.

**What is wrong:** The basses hold E♭1 under one tie for 12 beats, about 5.7 s. It starts with an sf, which uses a lot of bow at once, and then fades to pp. As written, the tie asks for one bow. Adler PDF 38–39 says the number of notes on one bow depends on tempo and dynamic: fewer at forte (Ex. II-22, six notes at f), and the cello and bass bows are shorter. PDF 108 names exactly this case for the bass, a lengthy tenuto, and gives the remedy: "ask the players to change bow at will". Adler PDF 40 (Ex. II-24/25) shows the same staggered-bow solution for a long line. The score already prints this instruction at every other long Cb tie: 3, 13, 72, 117 and 179. Only 41–43 lacks it. The cellos at the same place strike their notes again at 42 (`g1\sf\> | g1\mf\>`), so the basses are the only string part asked to hold one bow through the sf.

**Fix (applied, trans.ily):**
```
es,1\sf\>~^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" | es,1\mf\>~ | es,1\pp | des,1 |
```
The tie stays, so the sound has no audible break. The text sits above the Cb staff at 41 in the score and in the bass part, as it does at 72 and 117. The ∨ at 46 before the pizz. at 47 is unaffected.

### 2. Vc 3–4: the motto's 8-beat bow at Lento without the instruction the basses have (applied)

**Bars:** Vc 3–4, `c2(\< des4\> c4)~ | c1\!`, Lento, crotchet = 50, pp, sul G.

**What is wrong:** The slur runs to the C3 on beat 4 of bar 3, and the tie carries it through bar 4. That is one bow of 8 beats (9.6 s) with a crescendo and a diminuendo inside it. The double basses play the same line in octaves, and c236 gave them "change bow at will" at 3 for exactly this reason (comment in intro.ily: "one bow for 3–4 is 8 beats (9.6 s at q = 50) with a swell"). The cellos were left out on the grounds that the bass bow is shorter than the cello's. The assigned reading does not support that difference here. Adler PDF 39 groups the two: "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow… Remember also that the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter than those of the violin and viola." A swell at pp over 9.6 s on a cello bow is at the edge of one stroke. The section can sustain it without audible changes only if the bowing is staggered (PDF 40, Ex. II-25). This is the score's first exposed string line, and the cellos carry the motto: they are the melody, not the support. Two adjacent staves with the same notes and different bowing instructions would also prompt a question at the first rehearsal.

**Answer to c226** ("Kept: long pp slurs 1–3 … one bow at pp"). The slur is kept, so the phrase still reads as one bow. The text only lets players change inaudibly where they need to, which c236 accepted for the basses. No notes, slurs, dynamics, the sul G spanner or the motto change.

**Fix (applied, intro.ily):**
```
... | c2(\<^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will" des4\>\stopTextSpan c4)~ | c1\! |
```
The text prints above the sul G line, level with the Cb text below. It does not collide with anything in the score or the cello part (checked at 150 dpi and 110 dpi).

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **Vc 117–121, two-slash tremolo.** These are measured semiquavers (Adler PDF 50: measured tremolo is shorthand for repeated détaché notes), set against the three-slash unmeasured tremolo in Vln II. The difference is intended (accepted in the earlier series: "Vc measured sixteenths 117–121"). The written stretto at 127–130 makes the measured reading clear.
- **Vc 61→62 and Cb 46→47, arco to pizz. with no rest.** Adler PDF 54 allows this when an up-bow brings the hand to the frog, and both have the ∨. Removing the cello's last quaver at 61 would touch the protected accompaniment 47–68.
- **Vc 140–142, ff slurs of 6–7 beats (about 3 s).** This is within normal orchestral practice at Allegro. Bowing was settled by c238.
- **Vla "unis." at 60 after four bars' rest.** It repeats the *unis.* at 55. As a reminder after rests it helps in the part, so keep it.
- **Vln I ⊓ on the tied G6 at 13.** It is the retake Adler shows in Ex. II-20 (PDF 38). Protected since c226.
