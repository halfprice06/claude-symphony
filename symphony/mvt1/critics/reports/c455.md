## Findings

Ranked by how much each stands between this page and a professionally engraved score. Both are engraving only: no pitch, rhythm or printed dynamic in the score changes, and full.midi is unchanged (vl.txt identical).

### 1. Bars 4, 76, 176, 182: the only note in each bar sits at the start of the bar — applied

**What is wrong.** In these four bars every part holds a single semibreve (or rests): 4 (B.cl, Vc, Cb, timpani roll at the end of the motto), 76 (Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb, Hn 4, timpani at the end of the flute's motto), 176 (the chorale's held chord), 182 (the last chord). LilyPond prints the notes about 1½ spaces after the barline and leaves the rest of the bar empty. At 300 dpi in v455, bar 76 had its notes 1.3 spaces from the left barline and about 7 spaces from the right one; 176 and 182 were the same.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 55, "Notes of bar-length duration": "when a bar contains a single note and there are no other durations in any part, the note is placed just left of the centre of the bar. Such bars, by definition, are usually fairly narrow. This spacing creates a better balance than a single note positioned at the beginning of the bar." The example on that page shows the semibreve in mid-bar, "rather than" at the barline. Only "in widely spaced bars" may the note sit nearer the barline; these four bars are the narrowest on their systems.

**Answer to the stated reason for keeping it (c255, c345).** c255 kept 76 and 176 because the difference was under 1 mm and because a per-bar offset on about 20 staves would break after a later layout change. In v455 the difference is 3–4 mm at 76, 176 and 182. The fix is one Score-level line per bar in global.ily, not an offset on each staff, and it does not depend on where the systems break: if a later change adds a note to one of these bars, the extra space only widens that bar a little.

**Fix (applied).** global.ily: before bars 4, 76, 176 and 182, `\tag #'score { \once \override Score.BarLine.extra-spacing-width = #'(0 . 2.5) }` (the skips are split as `s1*3 … s1 s1*10`, `s1*3 … s1`, `s1*5 … s1`, `s1*3 … \mt 30 s1`). The tag keeps it out of the parts, where it had split multi-bar rests (Horn 3 printed 1–3 | 4–10 and 171–175 | 176 | 177–178 and went to 3 pages). Result at 300 dpi: in 76, 176 and 182 the note now stands just left of the centre; in 4 it moves 2 spaces to the right and is still left of the centre. Bars 74–75 lose about 2 % of their width; the c145 spacing section 73–76 and every system and page break are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 162–163, Horn 1 and Horn 3 parts: the diminuendo runs under a whole-bar rest — applied

**What is wrong.** Hn 1 (`e'1\ff\> | R1 | f'1\p`) and Hn 3 (`a1\ff\> | R1 | aes2\p`) end their notes at the end of 162 and rest in 163. Their hairpins are closed only by the p at 164, so in the Horn 1 and Horn 3 parts the hairpin is drawn the full length of the rest bar 163. A player cannot get softer during a rest. This is the c449 open item ("Hn 2/4 162–163 no end level after ff>"). Hn 2 and Hn 4 are correct: they hold through 163 and reach p at 164. The problem was in Hn 1 and Hn 3, and only in their parts.

**Evidence.** The assigned reading does not cover hairpins (Gould's dynamics chapter is not in the local copy). The rule used is the one this score already follows elsewhere: a hairpin ends on its last note or on the following rest, e.g. Hn 2 `R1\!` at 45 (c265) and the other `R1\!` endings on 23 lines of the section files. A script over every section file found these two as the only hairpins that cross a whole-bar rest into a later dynamic.

**Fix (applied).** recTwo.ily: Hn 1 `e'1\ff\> | \tag #'score R1 \tag #'part { R1\! } |`; Hn 3 the same after `a1\ff\>`. score.ly: `\removeWithTag #'part \hnThree` (as Hn 1, 2 and 4 already are; Hn 3's only other tag is a 'score tag at 145). A first test without tags ended the shared hairpin at 163 on the score staves and hid Hn 2/4's diminuendo through 163, so the score keeps the v455 notation. Horn 1 and Horn 3 parts now show `ff >` within 162 and a clean rest at 163.

### 3. No further change

I looked for places in this lens where the page holds the music back: the bar-length bars above were the one thing a reader would see. The 147–162 system plan (11 bars next to 5) breaks PDF 54, but it is protected for the page count and the fff climax, and the wide 158–162 bars carry the most text in the score. Fl 1·2 at 117–120 breaks PDF 67, but c345 tested the fix and found three new collisions. I agree with both earlier decisions and do not reopen them.
