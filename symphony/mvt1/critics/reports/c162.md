## What I studied

Books: Aldwell & Schachter PDF 398-441 in full, plus PDF 83, 89, 122 and 375 on unequal fifths and on resolving the seventh of vii°7. Corpus: the MCTL harmony annotations in /mnt/project-files/reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/. In Dvorak 9/iii (harmony.csv), two successive phrases end on the same deceptive goal, vi, and only the approach changes (onsets 816 and 840: V-vi, then V/flat VII-flat VII-vi). Applied chords make up 20% of chord labels in the Tchaikovsky 6/i annotation (108 of 532), 13% in Beethoven 9/i and 5% in Dvorak 9/iii. Web: Wikipedia, "Consecutive fifths" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecutive_fifths), notes that teachers differ on unequal fifths and most object when the bass or the leading tone is involved. Fiveable, "Unequal fifths" (https://fiveable.me/ap-music-theory/key-terms/unequal-fifths.md), says d5 to P5 ignores the tendency tones, while P5 to d5 is more often allowed. The College Board AP Music Theory 2012 scoring guidelines (https://secure-media.collegeboard.org/apc/ap12_music_theory_q6.pdf) accept unequal fifths between upper voices only when the voices descend. I checked the score with MIDI scripts, run on score.midi with mido (scripts in /tmp/critic-162/scripts): per-beat chords, leading-tone and seventh resolution, doubled leading tones, outer-voice cross relations, and an all-pairs scan for parallel P5 and d5 to P5. I checked every hit by hand, voice by voice.

## Book passages relied on

1. PDF 406-407 (Ex. 24-11): when a seventh does not resolve in its own voice, the resolution is "most successful" when the next lower voice in the same register, or the bass, supplies the note of resolution. This is why I accept the rising F at 24.3 and the other transferred resolutions in the table.
2. PDF 409 (Ex. 24-17): in V7-IV6-V6/5, "in both cases, the 7th eventually resolves down by step." An extended seventh has to resolve down eventually.
3. PDF 425 (Ex. 25-14): vii°7/V to V is shown with a d5 to P5 marked wrong. PDF 83 says the same in general terms: "Motion from a diminished 5th to a perfect 5th is normally avoided since the dissonant interval does not resolve."
4. PDF 420-421 (Ex. 25-3, 25-6): outer-voice cross relations should be avoided unless the soprano moves by step. They are softened when an inner voice carries the chromatic step.

## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Bars 23.4-24 and 135.4-136: d5 to P5 against the leading tone in the low horns. Applied.

Problem: at V6/5 to i, Hn 3 holds B3 (23.4-24.2) under Hn 1/Cl 1 F4. The two rise together to C4-G4. This is a d5 to P5 in which the leading tone is part of the interval. A&S PDF 122 says the d5 formed with the leading tone in V6/5 "must resolve according to rule," and PDF 425 marks the same motion wrong in vii°7/V-V. The F's rise is a valid transferred resolution (PDF 406-407). The fault is the B3 under it, which turns that rise into a d5-P5 pair. Bar 136 in the recapitulation repeats the same voicing (Hn 3 against Hn 1).

Fix, sections/tOne.ily (tOneHnThree), was `\clef bass c'2.\mp\<-> b4->~ | b2 c'2->~` and is now:
```
\clef bass c'2.\mp\<-> d'4->~ | d'2 c'2->~ |
```
sections/recOne.ily (recOneHnThree), was `\partCombineSoloI c'2.->\mp\< b4->~ | b2 c'2->~` and is now:
```
\partCombineSoloI c'2.->\mp\< d'4->~ | d'2 c'2->~ |
```
D4 is the fifth of V and falls by step to C, so Hn 3 still moves on the same rhythm in the same register. The "low horns 23-26" protection covers the colour and register, and both are kept. I rejected G3 because it makes parallel twelfths with the melody's D5-G5. A compound d5-P5 remains in Vln II/Cl 1 against the bass (B2/B1 to C, more than an octave apart). I left it, since A&S PDF 406 allows more freedom in a complex instrumental texture and the leading tone resolves in the bass.

### F2. Bars 30-31: Cl 2 makes a d5 to P5 against the bass trombone. Applied.

Problem: under vii°7/iv (E in the bass) Cl 2 holds B-flat3 over B.tbn E3. Both move to C4 over F3 at 31, a d5 to P5 with the temporary leading tone. Cycle 152 removed this same fault from Tpt 1 and Tbn 1 (A&S PDF 375). Cl 2 was missed.

Fix, sections/tOne.ily (tOneClTwo), was `es'1\ff | d'1 | c'1 | bes1 | c'2 d'2 |` and is now:
```
es'1\ff | d'1 | c'1 | des'1 | c'2 d'2 |
```
D-flat4 is the seventh of vii°7/iv and falls to C4, which is the resolution PDF 425 and 375 require. Cl 2 now forms a C-D-flat-C neighbour figure. A compound B-flat5 to C6 remains in Fl 2 against the horns. I left it, because D-flat6 would cross Fl 1 on the shared staff, and cycle 104 asked for C6 at 31.

### F3. Bars 31.3-32 and 141.3-142: Fl 2 makes a d5 to P5 above the melody. Applied.

Problem: Fl 2 A-flat5 over the D5 of Ob 1/Cl 1/Vln II falls to G5 over C5. This is a d5 to P5 in two upper voices that do not descend together in the way the AP guideline accepts, and it adds a second, unresolved A-flat over the leading-tone chord. The recapitulation at 141-142 repeats it.

Fix, sections/tOne.ily (tOneFluteTwo), `c'''2 aes''2` becomes:
```
c'''2 f''2 | g''4-> g''4-> r2 |
```
sections/recOne.ily (recOneFluteTwo), `aes''2 aes''2 | g''1` becomes:
```
aes''2 f''2 | g''1 |
```
F5 is the octave under the F6 melody note. This keeps cycle 104's principle that Fl 2 fills the octave, and it rises by step to G5.

### F4. Bar 68, HISTORY Open item (cycle 132 F4, IV6 for vi): recommend closing it with vi kept. Not applied; no change.

Evidence against the proposal:
1. IV (D-flat) already fills all of bar 65 at forte. IV6 at 68 would replace a repeated vi with a repeated IV.
2. V7-IV6 is an extended seventh only if the D-flat eventually resolves down (PDF 409, Ex. 24-17). In the c132 voicing it does not. Cl 1/Hn 2 go D-flat to E-flat at 69, and the Vc line goes to A-flat.
3. The model in Unit 25 for a second deceptive cadence goes to a chord applied to V and then to V again (PDF 427). Here the codetta begins on I at 69. The score already restates vii°7/V at 70 before the authentic cadence at 72, so the pattern the reading describes is there one bar later.
4. The two approaches to vi already differ: 64 comes through vii°7/vi with E-natural in the bass (a back-relating applied chord, PDF 438), and 68 comes straight from V7. Dvorak 9/iii keeps the same deceptive goal in successive phrases and varies only the approach, as this score does.

### F5. Bars 59-60: outer-voice cross relation. Note only; no change.

The violins' G5 (leaping to B-flat5) stands against G-flat2 in the bass. Ex. 25-3b (PDF 420) puts this in the "avoid" group. Vla carries G4-D-flat-G-flat4, which keeps the chromatic step in one inner voice, and PDF 421 (Ex. 25-6) accepts that softening. It sounds intended. Leave it.

### F6. Bar 64.3: viiø7/V spelling resolving to IV. Note only; no change.

D-F-A-flat-C goes to IV at 65, so sharp-4 falls to D-flat instead of rising. Over the falling-thirds bass this works as a chromatic neighbour, an apparent applied chord (PDF 430). Cycle 42 added the D in Cl 1/Hn 2 on purpose, and cycle 142 protects 64-68. No change.
