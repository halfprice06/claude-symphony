## Findings

I made no edit. I looked for the place where this reading and this lens point at a weakness, and I did not find one that would bring the score closer to a master's work. Findings 1–3 record what the reading confirms, with the bars, so the next pass does not redo the count. Finding 4 is the honest answer to "where does it still fall short", and why I do not touch it.

### 1. The movement shortens the unit as it nears each goal, as Bach's sequence C does. Keep.

A&S PDF 264–265 show the decisive pacing device in Ex. 17-1: the same ascent is done once with one-bar units and then again with one-beat units, and the shorter unit carries the music into the goal. The score uses this at every structural approach in the movement, in four different forms:

- **9–13 (introduction).** Three rising sixths in Vln I: G4–E♭5 (9.4, four beats), A♭4–F5 (10.4, three beats), B4–A♭5 (11.3, two beats, tied into 12). Each unit is a beat shorter. The goal (German sixth at 12, V at 13) arrives after three statements, the reading's norm (PDF 265).
- **35–40 (transition).** P head in Vln I as a two-bar unit (35–36), then one-bar units on G♭5 (37) and A♭5 (38), then runs (39–40) into the sf at 41.
- **77–82 (development opening).** Head statements in A♭ minor (77), E minor (78–79) and C minor (80–81), three keys of the major-third cycle. At 80–81 the full head is cut to its first two notes, alternating every two beats in winds, horns and cellos under a crescendo, into the ff at 82.
- **117–122 (dominant pedal).** P calls one per bar at 117 (B4–A♭5) and 118 (E♭5–C6), two per bar at 119 (D5–B5, F5–D6) and 120 (F5–D♭6, B♭5–F6), then the broadened E♭6–F6–G6 at 121 into A♭6 at 122. Two statements at one unit length, two at half length, then a change of rhythm before the goal: PDF 265 and PDF 269 (Ex. 17-4, the goal set off by rhythm and register).
- **127–130 (retransition).** Over the same pedal the cellos move from crotchets (127) to quavers (128) to triplets (129, mf) to semiquavers (130, f), while the motto entries come every two bars (Cl 1 127), then every bar (Vla 129), then on the beat (Vln II, Bsn 1 130, Hn 2 130.4).

The same device is in the corpus. In Tchaikovsky 6/i (MCTL harmony.csv) the exposition's rising sequence in minor thirds (B–D–F–G♯–B) has eight-beat units at onsets 117 and 125, four-beat units at 133 and 137, and reaches B minor at 141 (bars 30–36). The development's sequence at bars 205–213 (F–A♭–B–D–E) goes from two-bar to one-bar to half-bar units. The movement does what the master does. Nothing to change.

### 2. The two series longer than three statements meet the reading's exception. Keep.

PDF 265 allows more than three statements only when the unit is "very short and simple". Two passages run longer:

- **Wedge 111–116.** Six one-bar units. The bass falls F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C by whole bars, Vln I rises A♭5–B♭5–C6–C6–D♭6–D6, and the crescendo runs mp to ff. The unit is one bar of held notes over a repeated viola figure, as short and simple as Bach's one-beat sequence C, and it is the second, shorter stage after the two four-bar units of 103–110 (finding 3). The rising top over a scalewise bass is the "prominent scalewise line" that PDF 267 says carries a sequence from one stable point to the next. Protected (c142, c382, c482); the reading supports the protection.
- **Fugato 85–96.** Four entries, three bars apart, descending by fifths (D, G, C, F). This is not a plain fourfold repetition: each entry thickens the texture (by the reduction 1 sounding part at 85, 3 at 88, 8 at 91, 10 at 94), and the dynamic grows mf–f–ff. At 96 the chain breaks into one-beat head fragments (15 sounding parts at 96.3), the "very short" unit of PDF 265, and the key change (B♭ minor → B minor) comes only at 97, one bar later, as PDF 280 (Ex. 17-20) asks: the change of pattern and the change of rhythm do not coincide. Protected (c142, c227, c437). Keep.

### 3. 103–117 follows the shape of Bach's sequences B and C, including the drop at 107. Keep.

Earlier passes treated the f/p contrast at 103/107 as a texture choice. The reading gives it a structural reason. PDF 264: sequence B "moves up a step with each bar until the D is reached in bar 5, where the sequence breaks off and the bass returns to the tonic and to a low register. The stepwise ascent begins once more in sequence C, but with a shorter pattern." The score does the same at a larger scale:

- 103–106: four-bar unit, f, D minor to V of E minor.
- 107–110: four-bar unit, the heads inverted, dropped to p, E minor to V of F minor. This is the break-off and drop back.
- 111–116: the ascent starts again with one-bar units, mp cresc., into G at 117.

The keys rise D–E–F–G, one step per stage, which is PDF 267's scalewise component at the level of keys. The goal of each stage is the first chord of the next (106→107, 110→111, 116→117), the overlap of PDF 265. Section times from the MIDI: 103–110 15.2 s, 111–116 11.4 s, then the pedal 29.2 s; each stage is shorter than the one before until the goal is held. Protected (c142, c382, c413). Keep.

### 4. Where the movement still falls short, and why I leave it.

Read for pacing, the one approach that does not use the device of finding 1 is the recapitulation climax: from the pp cadence at 155 the music reaches the ff cadential 6/4 at 158 in two bars (156 ♭VII, 157 IV, mp cresc., poco animando). Chopin's Ex. 17-8 (PDF 273) and Tchaikovsky's sequences reach their ff after three or more statements with shrinking units. Bars 156–157 are two statements of a rising step, not a series. A master could have built a four- to six-bar sequence here.

I do not propose it, for three reasons.
- The approach is protected four times (c083, c143, c243, c448), and the reason given there still holds: the violins enter at 156 and carry the S phrase into K at 158 without a break; a built-up approach would make 158 a second arrival after 147 instead of the continuation of the same phrase.
- A longer approach adds bars to recTwo (recap ratio 0.61 already noted and protected, c493, c503) and moves the protected 17-page layout and the bar numbers the other movements cite.
- The climb that the reading asks for is already present one level up: the S theme rises from p (147) through mf (156) to ff (158) and fff (160) across one phrase, and the horns, harp chords and the Cl 1 octave at 156–157 (c337) add weight inside the two bars, with the cymbal roll at 159.

So the shortfall is real but small, and the cost of fixing it is larger than the gain. Keep.
