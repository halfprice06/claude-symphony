## Findings

### 1. Bar 177: the viola's A♭3 is the seventh of vii°6/5 and leaps away from it (applied)

At 177 the chorale has the motto bass on D (Cb D2, Vc/B.cl/B.tbn D3), Tbn 2 F3, viola A♭3, Tbn 1 D4, Vln II F4 and Cl 1 C5 falling to B4 on beat 3. From beat 3 the chord is B–D–F–A♭ over D: vii°6/5. At 178 the bass falls to C and the viola leaps from A♭3 up to C4. Nothing resolves A♭ to G, and B4 is left hanging on purpose (protected). So the arrival of the tonic at 178 resolves neither tendency tone that the reading names as the chord's identity (♯7 and ♭6 "active in the direction of 1 and 5", PDF 374).

The reading's rules here are plain. The 7th of VII°7 resolves by stepwise descent (PDF 375 §3). "All dissonances involving the bass must resolve" (box, PDF 377): the viola's A♭3 is a diminished fifth above the bass D, and it goes to an octave C–C. And VII°6/5 to I5/3 over the bass 2–1 is the "poor" progression of Ex. 22-4b (PDF 376), the same reason c152 moved the bass at 26.

Critic 192 left the A♭ unresolved as "the 9th, left unresolved as the 7th resolves (A&S PDF 466)". That passage is about the ninth of a root-position V9. At 177 no G sounds in the bass; the only G is Vln I's G4 pickup on beat 4. The same page says inverted ninth chords are infrequent. Under a D bass the A♭ is a chord seventh, and PDF 378 §9 gives the treatment: a diminished seventh made by a suspension that resolves to the root of V7 is an embellishment of V7.

Fix (coda.ily, codaVla, bar 177): `aes1` → `aes2. g4`. The viola keeps A♭3 under Cl 1's B4 on beat 3, so the diminished seventh still sounds. On beat 4 it falls to G3 as the violins' G4 enters. Beat 4 becomes V4/3 (G–B–D–F over D), and 176–178 is i6 – V4/3 – i over the motto bass E♭–D–C, a standard passing progression. Bar 178 is unchanged, and the viola still goes to C4 there, so c192's doubled root at 178 is kept. Rhythm, levels, timing, the motto, the chorale's brass and the E♭/E♮ thread are untouched. Applied in the scratch copy.

### 2. Bars 26→27: an inner d5 moves to a P5 in similar motion (keep)

Hn 3 D4 and Hn 1 A♭4 (a d5) go to C4 and G4. This is the hidden-fifth motion that Ex. 22-3c marks "avoid" (PDF 375). Tbn 1 and the cellos' A♭3 also rise to C4. Keep it. The chord is cut off on beat 4, so the "resolution" comes after a rest and is heard as a new attack. The tendency tones resolve audibly in the leading parts (Tpt 1 B4–C5, Hn 1 A♭4–G4, Fl 2 A♭5–G5, PDF 375 §3). The layout of 27 is quoted at pitch, in every part, by the finale at its 199–204, so any change to Hn 3 at 27 is a cross-movement change for a nominal gain.

### 3. Bars 23–24 and 135–136: the seventh of V6/5 rises in a doubled inner voice (keep)

Over B1–C2, Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II take F4–G4 while the bass rises B–C: a d5 with the bass going to a P5 (box, PDF 377; Ex. 22-4b, PDF 376). Keep it. The same seventh is resolved in the top line F5–E♭5–D5 inside the dominant (23.4–24.2). The F–G alternation is the pitch pattern of the protected hemiola 23–26 and 135–138, whose accents fall on these changes. At forte, in a doubled middle voice, the effect is the "hardly noticeable" improper resolution that PDF 375 §4 accepts.

### 4. Outer-voice cross relations at 79→80 and 108→109 (keep)

At 108.4 the violas' S tune ends on B4 and at 109 the double basses enter on B♭1. At 79→80 the bass E2 goes to the top E♭4. Both are cross relations between the outer voices (Ex. 23-12a, PDF 394). Both mark key shifts by tritone and major third (E minor → B♭ minor; E → C minor in the A♭–E–C circle). This is the kind of "compelling reason" for which the reading cites Brahms 3/i (Ex. 23-13, PDF 394–395). Keep both. They are protected (77–83 key circle; notes of 103–110) and carry the scherzo and finale seeds listed in mvt1.md.
