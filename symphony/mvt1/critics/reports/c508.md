## Findings

I have one finding. It is small, and it closes an item left open by c449 for this lens. I looked for a larger change and did not find one I could support from the reading (see "Considered and kept").

### 1. Re-attacks without a level inside the dissolution at 163 (recTwo)

**What is wrong.** At 162 all four horns play ff with a diminuendo that runs to p at 164. Hn 1 and Hn 3 rest at 163 (c455 made their parts end the hairpin before the rest). Hn 2 and Hn 4 do not rest: Hn 2 restrikes its C4 at 163 (it is not tied from 162) and Hn 4 moves from E3 to F3. Both attacks come halfway through a two-bar hairpin, and the last printed level is ff. The trumpets and trombones have already reached mp on beat 3 of 162 and stopped, the strings reach mp at 164 and the woodwinds are fading, so these two horn attacks are the only fresh brass sounds in the bar. A player who re-attacks with no level written will judge it from the last mark, ff. That puts a brass bump into the first bar of the dissolution 162–166, which c188 and c483 protect. c449 listed this as "Hn 2/4 162-163 no end level after ff> ... for the expression/engraving lens". c455 fixed only Hn 1 and Hn 3, which rest, so it did not close the point for the two horns that keep playing.

**Evidence.** In every brass example in the reading, the composer writes where a held or restruck note sits in its decay: Siegfried's fermatas with the next phrase at p (PDF 310, X-25), Mahler 4 ff>p and f>>p (PDF 308, X-21), Tchaikovsky's held trumpets ending >p (PDF 316, X-40), Mahler 2 f<ff>p (PDF 317, X-44), sff>p<ff (PDF 323, X-54), Mahler 4's gestopft horns with a new level on each re-attack (PDF 307, X-18). This is the same problem c408 fixed at 124 (Hn 3, Hn 4 and Tbn 1 re-attacking inside the fff collapse, now "f>"). Bar 163 is the second climax's matching place.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-508/work).** sections/recTwo.ily:
```
recTwoHnTwo:  ... | c'1\ff\> |
              c'1\mf\> | c'2\p r2 | R1 | c'2\p~ c'2\< |        % was c'1 | c'2\p ...
recTwoHnFour: ... | e1\ff\> | f1\mf\> | d2\p r2 | ...              % was e1\ff\> | f1 | d2\p ...
```
I chose mf because it is the midpoint of a two-bar ff→p hairpin and lies between the trumpets' mp end level (162.3) and the strings' mp target (164). It keeps the horns slightly above the fading strings until the C-major chord thins to p at 164. Hn 1 and Hn 3 rest at 163, so each shared staff has only one player there and the equal-dynamics rule is met. Nothing about the protected exit order changes: no notes, rests, breaths or hairpin end points move. I checked the score page (p. 17): "mf >" prints at 163 on both horn staves, with no collision. I checked the Hn 4 part, which reads ff> | mf> | p at 162–164, and the Hn 2 part (pdftotext shows the new mf).

## Considered and kept (no change)

- **Stopped horn, third look.** c408 cited the corpus (Tchaikovsky 6/i, Dvořák 9/i) as evidence that the models do not use it. That evidence is now weaker. Tchaikovsky 6/iv marks both horn pairs "gestopft" at ff in bar 126 (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt4.musicxml, parts P8 and P9). So a named model does use stopped horn in a symphony, but loud and at a climax, not in a soft passage. Adler gives three uses: single notes, soft passages and sf>p (PDF 308). I checked each candidate in movement I. At 14 the horn call's unresolved B is the cyclic note other movements answer, and a nasal colour would weaken it. At 124–126 the collapse is protected, and its horn line (Hn 3's leading tone B3 resolving to C4) needs to be heard as a line. The pads and the chorale need the open middle-register "accompaniment choir" (PDF 311–312). None of these is an improvement I can defend, so the c76/c308/c408 decision stands. The new corpus fact is recorded here for later critics.
- **Brass counter-line at 97–102 (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1, Bsn 1) has no hairpins under the strings' swelling S.** Don Juan (X-12) shows hairpins inside a loud horn line. But this line is accompaniment, f under an ff tune, and its rising quaver pickups already lead into each half note. Adding swells would make it compete with the tune's swells, which happen at the same moments. The block is protected (c7, c404, c424). Keep.
- **Trumpets ff on the French sixth at 116, a bar before the other brass.** This looks like an early arrival, but c143 wrote it on purpose (the second group reaches its crescendo faster, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127), and c355 protects it ("116 cl/tpt ff"). Keep.
- **Strokes at 32: trumpets ff, trombones, tuba and Hn 3·4 f with accents.** These follow the grading of the held brass at 27–31 (rule 19). The finale quotes 27–32 at pitch, and c308 protects the trumpets' ff. Keep.
- **Trumpets' rhythm 27–31 and 131–134 has no articulation marks.** Beethoven (X-38) and Mendelssohn (X-41) mark fanfare figures only where the articulation differs from plain tonguing. The rhythm is identical to the timpani's, and the finale quotes it. Keep.
- **Mechanical scan.** I ran a script over every brass part in all nine section files to find entries after a full bar's rest with no dynamic. It found none apart from two false alarms from `\tag` blocks. Bar 163 is a re-attack with no rest before it, which is why I found it on the page and not with the script.
