## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Every passage where the chapter could argue for a change is either already consistent with it or protected in HISTORY.md for a reason the chapter does not answer. Below are the new checks, ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. Ex. 1 scan: no soft chromatic inner line under a lead (all bars) - keep

Method: a script (/tmp/critic-577/scripts/chrom.py) reads full.midi and, bar by bar, lists every part that has three or more onsets inside C3-C5, lies strictly between the bar's highest and lowest sounding notes, and moves by semitone at least twice. This is Sebesky's cut trombone line (PDF 12-13) in score terms. The MIDI velocity gives the dynamic level.

Result: 60 hits. 55 fall in bars whose loudest note has MIDI velocity 99 or more (mf to fff): the P head's own chromatic descent G-E-flat-D-D-flat-C doubled in inner parts in the tuttis (27, 82, 131-132, 167-168); the head entries of the transition (33-39) and of the development dialogue (79); the fugato voices (91-96); the violas' C-B-C turns (116, 139-142); the S tune's lower octave in the cellos at 152; and the motto stretto at 130. These are theme statements, melody doublings or the fugato's own voices, not background lines. The five soft hits (velocity 80-94) are 56 (Cl 1 and Hn 2 on the counterline E-flat-E-F-G, protected by cycles 377 and 567; it is Ex. 2A's kind of fill, since it moves while the violins hold C5) and 111, 113, 114 (the violas' C-B-C turn in the wedge, its rhythmic motor, protected as 'wedge 111-116' by cycles 70 and 142). None hides the outer voices. The score follows PDF 12-13.

### 2. Bars 94-96: the last fugato entry - keep

The question (focus, PDF 13; "less is more", PDF 13) was whether the fourth entry (Vln I + Fl 1 on F, 94) is buried or under-supported. From full.midi: subject in Vln I/Fl 1 (D-flat6-B5-B-flat5-A5-F#5), countersubject in Vln II/C.a. an octave and more below (B-flat4-D-flat5-F5 | D-flat5-E-flat5-C5), a third voice in Vla/Bsn 1 in halves (F4-A-flat4 | F#4-F4), Vc B-flat3 then D-flat3, bass B-flat1-E-flat2-F2. So the entry has two moving lines under it, both slower than the subject and two octaves or more lower, and the bass moves once per half-bar. That is the order Sebesky asks for: one primary line, a secondary line, and a slow tertiary layer (PDF 14 chart). Giving the cellos a moving line here (tested on paper: a quarter-note line B-flat3-C4-D-flat4-E-flat4 against the subject's B5-B-flat5) would put a third moving voice in the band of the countersubject and make the busiest bar of the fugato before its own ff at 96. Not applied; the fugato 85-96 is also protected (cycles 437 and 142).

### 3. Bars 175-179: five parts on the motto bass at pp - keep

Economy (PDF 12) asks whether every doubling earns its place. The motto bass G-E-flat-D-C-D-flat-C sounds in the bass trombone, bass clarinet and cellos at pitch, the double basses an octave lower and Tbn 1 an octave higher. At pp this is a colour blend (the chorale's reed and bowed edge on the trombone tone), not loudness, and the low register needs it to speak through the tam-tam. The passage is protected (cycle 144 "pp trombone/B.cl/Vc unison 175-179", cycle 140 "at-pitch bass tripling 175-179"). The chapter's rule is about notes that make no difference; these change the colour of the motto's last low statement. Not applied.

### 4. Variety of accompaniment figures (whole movement) - follows

Sebesky's variety list (PDF 15-16) is about combinations; I applied it to the accompaniment layers, which earlier passes did not list. The movement uses: off-beat syncopated chords (15-22), quarter pulse (23-25, 135-137), hammered eighths (27-32, 131-134), held pads with tremolo (37-40, 77-81, 147-155), harp arpeggios (47-55), rising cello arpeggios (56-68, 163-165), the viola eighth arpeggio (97-102), the viola C-B-C turn (111-116, 139-142), pizzicato bass (47-55, 147-155), a timpani-roll pedal (117-130) and the timpani pulse (179-180). No figure returns in a section where it would be heard as a repeat of the previous one: the two pizzicato-bass passages are the exposition and recapitulation of the same theme, the condition c311 protects. The bass line (lowest sounding pitch, per beat) is also varied: chromatic rising (27-31), stepwise falling wedge (111-116, Ex. 3A's kind), pedal (73-77, 117-130, 167-170), and moving pizzicato roots (47-55, 147-155). Nothing to change.

### 5. Where the movement could still fall short of a master's, within this lens

I looked for a passage a listener would not remember or a line that could sing more. The one place where the texture is plainer than in Brahms or Dvořák is the transition 33-46, where the cellos and basses hold one note a bar for fourteen bars under the imitative head entries. A walking bass there would add motion, but the imitation above already carries three entries (Bsn 1 33, Vla 34, Vln I 35, Ob 1/Cl 1 37-39), and focus (PDF 13) argues against a fourth moving layer under them. The route 33-46 and its hand-offs are protected (cycles 163, 177 and 371). I found no change that meets the bar of a real improvement.
