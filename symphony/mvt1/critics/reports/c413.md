## Findings

I checked every row of the table bar by bar. I found no place where the score breaks a principle of the reading, and I am not proposing a change. The points below are the places where a form critic could want one, with the evidence for keeping each. Nothing was applied in the scratch copy.

1. **The key plan is the Beethoven plan of PDF 454, and the corpus confirms it (bars 15–131).** A&S PDF 454 gives I – VI ‖ IV–V – I for late Beethoven, naming the Ninth Symphony. The MCTL harmony file for Beethoven 9/i (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1/form/harmony.csv, onsets in crotchets) has d 0–96, B♭ (VI) from 141 to 318, then g (iv) 318–401, c 401–444, B♭ 456–484, a 484–560, F 560–588, d from 588. This movement does the same with C, A♭ 47–82 and iv at 109–116. The difference is the order inside the development: Beethoven reaches iv first, this movement last, directly before V. That order is the one PDF 451–452 describes (tonicized IV moves on to V of the home key to prepare the return) and the one Ex. 26-11 sketches (IV, transition, V, reprise). Keep.

2. **The development as a prolonged dominant (77–130).** Ex. 26-17 (PDF 456) shows V extended by VI as upper neighbour and IV as lower neighbour, then V7 into I, and the text says development sections do this "on a much larger scale". Here V is first touched at 83–84 (A♭ at 82 as the upper neighbour), the passage then moves away through the fugato and the passing keys, comes to iv at 109–116, and returns to V7 at 117 with A♭ over G again at 122. The seventh F is present when V comes back (117–121, and Hn 2 F4 at 130.3 into 131), as PDF 456 asks. Tchaikovsky 6/i gives its retransition a similar share: the B-minor dominant region 1062–1214 is about 26 % of its development (Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1/form/harmony.csv and form_analysis.csv); the G pedal 117–130 is 26 % of this development's bars and 28 % of its time. Keep (117–130 is protected; the reading supports it).

3. **Remote passing keys 96–108.** PDF 443 says keys that last should be closely related. B minor (6 bars, ff), D minor (4), E minor (2) and B♭ minor (2) are remote, but none is confirmed by a cadence with II or IV and V (row 3), and each joins the next by phrase modulation (PDF 452) or through its own dominant (B major at 106 for E minor; C at 110 for F minor). Under PDF 447–448 they are passing keys between VI and iv. The only strong event among them, the ff B-minor S at 97–102, is a semitone above the fugato's B♭ minor and falls back by step through D, E, F to G (protected c393 key rise and seams). Keep.

4. **E♭ at 41 as a passing key, not the second key.** PDF 449 says III is the normal second key in minor, so a listener could take the sf E♭ with four horns at 41–42 for the second group. The D♭ seventh at 43, V4/2 at 44 and IV–V–I at 46–47 turn it into V of A♭, as Ex. 26-4 turns C minor's tonic into III of A♭ (PDF 446) and as Ex. 26-7 turns a C-minor "tonic" into a springboard (PDF 448). The arrival on A♭ at 47 has the melodic approach PDF 445 asks for (S head on 3̂ with a long downbeat). Keep (route 33–46 protected).

5. **Leading tone before the return (117–130).** PDF 449 and Ex. 26-10 (bar 12) require the leading tone before the tonic returns when V has been minor. V here has been major since 117 (row 11). The two bars without B♮ at 129–130 (the motto stretto) come after fourteen bars of major V, so the rule's concern, a minor V that does not lead to I, does not arise. c233 and c263 kept them for the same reason. Keep.

6. **The short recapitulation (0.61 of the exposition).** The recapitulation has no transition because S returns in the tonic. The iv–N6–V turn at 139–143 takes the place of the old modulation, a late IV that PDF 451–452 allows. In the corpus, Beethoven 9/i's recapitulation is about as long as its exposition (form_analysis.csv: exposition to 374, recapitulation 628–1024), but Tchaikovsky 6/i recapitulates only its second group, in the tonic major (2nd-A in B major from 1214), which is the model for this movement's C-major S at 147. The tonic region from 131 lasts 134 s against 202 s for the departure 33–130, so the return balances it. Section lengths are protected (c243, c323) and the reading gives no reason to reopen them. Keep.

7. **Cadential confirmation of every key, including the coda.** Every key that lasts more than a phrase is confirmed by IV or II and V (row 3). The coda's i–iv–N6–I6/4–V at 171–174 confirms C minor without a root-position V–i into 175. PDF 456 (section 16) accepts tonic returns by contrapuntal motion, and the soft ending serves the attacca and keeps a strong C cadence back for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md, "Movement I never confirms C major at forte"). Keep (coda protected).
