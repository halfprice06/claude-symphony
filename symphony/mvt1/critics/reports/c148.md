# Critic 148 — v167, lens: expression (accent and hypermeter)

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, 3rd ed., Unit 3 "Rhythm and Meter", PDF 47–57 (printed pp. 36–46). I read every text page in the range and viewed every page with music examples as an image at 110 dpi (PDF 47, 48, 49, 51, 52, 53, 54, 55, 56, 57; PDF 50 is text only). The examples I compared against this score: Ex. 3-1 (Mozart K. 467/ii, long notes on downbeats), 3-6 (Beethoven op. 69, *sf* on a tied syncopation), 3-7 (Brahms violin concerto, 5/4 grouping inside 3/4, with hairpins), 3-8 (Mozart K. 283, hemiola with *fp*/*f*), 3-9 to 3-11 (rhythmic groups and overlap), 3-12 (Beethoven op. 126/6, three-bar hypermeasures, *ten.* on weak beats), 3-13/3-14 (eight- and seven-bar phrases with slurs), 3-15 to 3-20 (accented dissonances, suspensions, anticipations).

Method: I compiled the v167 sections with LilyPond's event listener (one file per instrument) and listed, by bar and beat, every accent, tenuto, dynamic, hairpin start and end, and slur. I then checked where each accent, *sf* and hairpin peak falls relative to the beat and to the two-bar and four-bar groups. No corpus file or web source was needed for these findings. The three fixes below are applied in /tmp/critic-148/work.

## Findings

### F1. Bar 3 (B.cl, Vc, Cb): the motto's swell peaks on the anticipation, not on the D♭ — APPLIED
**Problem.** In bar 3 the motto goes C (half note) – D♭ (beat 3) – C (beat 4, tied into bar 4). The hairpins were `c2(\! des4\< c4~\>`: the crescendo covers only the D♭ and turns at the C on beat 4, so the C on beat 4 was marked as the loudest note of the bar. That C is an anticipation of bar 4's C.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 56 (Ex. 3-19/3-20): an anticipation enters before the strong beat and is unaccented. PDF 50: chromatic tones, such as this D♭, carry a built-in accent. The cor anglais' answer in bar 7 already does what the reading describes: `g'2\< aes'4\> g'4~`, with the swell turning on the chromatic A♭ and the anticipated G inside the diminuendo. Bar 3 contradicted bar 7 and the reading.
**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** B.cl `c2(\< des4\> c4~ | c1\!)`; Vc `c2(\< des4\> c4)~ | c1\!`; Cb `c,2(\< des,4\> c,4)~ | c,1\!`. Same notes and slurs. The pp level and the shorter bows from cycle 116 are kept. Checked on page 1 of the scratch PDF: the hairpins now match the cor anglais in bar 7.

### F2. Bar 66: the diminuendo begins on the consonant ii chord, so the cadential 4–3 is not stressed; the recap at 164 does it correctly — APPLIED
**Problem.** At 66 the bass moves B♭ (ii) to E♭ (V) on beat 3. Over it the violins play B♭5 (beat 1) and A♭5 (beat 3, the 4 above E♭), which resolves to G5 on 67.1. The `\>` started on the B♭, so the A♭ dissonance sat partway down a diminuendo and was quieter than the consonant note before it. The recapitulation's version of this bar (164: `d'''2\mp( c'''2\>` over D–G) holds the level through ii and starts the diminuendo on the 4. The exposition contradicted both the recap and the reading.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 55: a suspension stands in an accented position and resolves to a weaker one (Ex. 3-16 to 3-18). PDF 50: dissonant elements carry a built-in accent. The bass's arrival on V at 66.3 (cycle 23's "beat-3 dominant with 4–3") is the harmonic accent in the bar. The dynamic should arrive there too and then fall away.
**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily).** Vln I `bes''2( aes''2\> | g''4 ...`, Vln II `bes'2( aes'2\> |`, Vc `bes,8( f bes des') es2\> |`, Cb `bes,,2 es,2~\> |`. The diminuendo still reaches *p* at 68, now over six beats instead of eight. The inner voices (Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline, Hn 3, violas) still begin their diminuendo at 66.1. That is deliberate: the accompaniment recedes while melody and bass carry the 4–3. Notes and the rit. at 66 are unchanged.

### F3. Bar 101 (and 59): the 4–3 appoggiatura in the head's fourth bar has no stress in the B-minor statement — 101 APPLIED, 59 proposed
**Problem.** The fourth bar of the second-theme head always has the 4–3 over V on the downbeat. At 50 and 150 (horn/cello/viola) it is the peak of a swell with a diminuendo into the resolution, and at 174 (coda cello) it carries `\>`. At 101 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II at *ff*: B over the F♯ bass, resolving to A♯) it had no mark. The surrounding statement marks only the beat-2 leap notes (tenutos at 99 and 102), so the one accented dissonance in the phrase was the only long note left unmarked.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 54–55, Ex. 3-15c: the appoggiatura is an accented dissonance; suspensions and appoggiaturas fall on the strong beat and the resolution is weaker. PDF 51, Ex. 3-6: the composer marks the emphasis he wants when the texture does not supply it. Here the texture is a flat *ff* tutti with no hairpins. Cycle 88's rule (doubling parts carry identical marks) applies.
**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** Tenuto on the appoggiatura in all four doubling parts: Fl 1 and Ob 1 `bes''2-- a''2)`, Vln I `bes''2--( a''2)`, Vln II `bes'2--( a'2)` (inside the `\transpose c cis` block, sounding B). I used a tenuto rather than a hairpin so the *ff* level into 102 is not lowered and the cycle-141 sul G and bowings are not affected.
**Proposal for 59 (not applied).** At 59 (Vln I and II, A♭5 over E♭) the 4–3 sits inside the crescendo from 58 to the *mf* at 60, so the resolution G5 is louder than the dissonance. A tenuto, `aes''2--` / `aes'2--`, would give the appoggiatura its weight and leave the crescendo in place. I did not apply it because 56–63 is close to the protected head phrasing (47–58). The composer should decide.

### F4. Checked and consistent with the reading (no change)
- **Theme-head accents (15, 17, 27, 29, 131, 139, 167).** The accent falls on the beat-2 dotted quarter, a syncopation held through beat 3. This is the case of Ex. 3-6 (PDF 51). Keep.
- **D♭6 peak at 20.** The *sf* falls on beat 3 of the sixth bar of the sentence, and the note is held across into the strong seventh bar: a syncopation at the level of the hypermeasure (PDF 51, 53), marked at its attack. Keep.
- **Recap 139–141.** The *ff* arrives on 140.1 with the D♭ bass, and the A♭6 peak on beat 3 carries an accent. In the exposition the hairpin runs on to the peak instead. Both follow PDF 51: the recap puts the dynamic target on the harmonic arrival and the downbeat. The timpani's *ff* at 141 marks the strong third bar under the held A♭6. Keep.
- **3-beat groups at 23–25 and 135–137.** The accents and slurs start each group consistently, and a downbeat stop chord restores the meter (PDF 51–52, Ex. 3-7/3-8). Keep (protected).
- **Weak-beat accents at 32.2, 72.2, 80–81, 119–120.** These are cadential hammer strokes, motto exchanges and the compressed ladder. None of them is an unmotivated weak-beat accent, and 119–120 resolves into the strong 121–122. Keep.
- **Climax 158–161.** The crescendo of 159 peaks at 160.1 with the ♭VI bass, the cymbal and the third bar of the group; the A♭6 enters on beat 2 as a syncopation (PDF 51, 53). Keep.
