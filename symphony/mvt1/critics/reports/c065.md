# Critic 065: engraving and notation (lens 5), v84

## What I studied
I checked the spelling of every accidental with a script rather than by eye. I added a dumping engraver to a scratch copy of score.ly. It logs every notehead: staff, bar, beat, spelled pitch, whether an accidental is printed, its staff position, and every rest. The scripts are /tmp/critic-065/scripts/acc.py and overlap.py, and the dump step is in /tmp/critic-065/dump/score.ly. The checks were:
- simultaneous enharmonic clashes between any parts, using overlapping durations;
- melodic steps in each staff that form augmented or diminished intervals;
- notes that need a cautionary accidental after a barline;
- rests that cross the middle of the bar;
- passages with three or more ledger lines.

I read pages 3–5, 7–9 and 15 of the PDF at 80–250 dpi.

Corpus sources:
- Mutopia Dvořák 9: `mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/header.ily` line 28 sets `\accidentalStyle Score.modern`.
- Mutopia Eroica: `BeethovenLv_O55/.../ScoreMvtI.ly` lines 39–84 set `\accidentalStyle modern` on every staff.
- Mutopia Dvořák 7: `DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Violino_I.ly` lines 246–248 put `\bar "||"` before `\key a \minor`. `..._Fagotto_I_II.ly` puts the bassoons in tenor clef for high passages.

Web sources:

## Findings, ranked

### 1. No cautionary accidentals anywhere (whole score). Applied.
The score uses LilyPond's default accidental style, which follows 18th-century practice and never prints a reminder in the bar after an alteration. The script found 83 places where the same note in the same octave was altered in the previous bar and now appears without any sign. Examples:
- 65: Cb, Vc, Hn 2 and Cl show a plain D (a D♭ in A♭ major) straight after the D♮ in 64.
- 31: Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I/II and Vla show a plain D after the D♭ in 30.
- 108: Fl, Ob, Tpt and Hn 3 show a plain D after the D♭ in 107.
- 133: the low strings and brass show a plain D after the D♭ in 132.
- 159: Fl and Vln show a plain G after the G♯ in 158.
- 62: Cl and Tpt show a plain C and F after the C♭ and F♭ in 61.

Common practice, and Gould as Dorico implements her, puts a cautionary on the first such note in the next bar. The hand-engraved Mutopia scores of Dvořák 9 and the Eroica both set `modern` style.

Fix, at the top of `global` in global.ily:
```
global = {
  \accidentalStyle Score.modern
  \key c \minor \time 4/4
```
This adds 81 accidentals across the score (556 to 637). Page count, system breaks and the K page break are unchanged; only page 4/5 moved one bar (54 now ends page 4).

### 2. Key changes have a single barline (47, 77, 97, 103, 139, 163). Applied.
All six key changes fall at section boundaries. Five of them are rehearsal letters; the change back to C minor at 103 is the B-minor block's end. Gould (pp. 92–93), Dorico's default and the Dvořák 7 Mutopia source all mark such a change with a double barline. Bar 103 on page 9 shows the problem most clearly: naturals cancel two sharps and three flats appear, with nothing marking the new section.

Fix in global.ily: put `\bar "||"` before each of the six `\key` changes after the first, for example `\bar "||" \key b \minor \mark \default`.

### 3. 154–157: cellos play the climax tune in bass clef up to A♭4. Applied.
In 154–157 the cellos double the tune, and recTwoArp reaches G4 and A♭4 at fff for four bars. That is three ledger lines throughout. Every other statement of this tune is in tenor clef: 47–55, 139–147 and 167–170. Tenor clef keeps it within one ledger line.

Fix in recTwoArp:
- `\clef tenor g4\ff e'2.~` at 154;
- `\clef bass a,8(\ff\> e a c' e'2)` at 158, where the arpeggios return to A2.

### 4. 159 and 161: bassoon unisons print as two whole notes side by side. Applied.
In 159 (F2) and 161 (G2, tied from 160) both bassoons have the same whole note. The part combiner stays in apart mode there, and LilyPond does not merge stemless unisons between voices. As a result the staff shows two noteheads next to each other, which looks like a second (see page 15, Bn staff). This passage sits next to the cycle-64 a2 change at 157.

Fix in recTwoBsnOne:
```
e,1\ff\> | \partCombineUnisono f,1 | \partCombineAutomatic d,2\mp \partCombineUnisono g,2~ | g,1\> | \partCombineAutomatic c,2.\p r4 |
```
The unisono switch is placed on the half note so that the tie does not cross a mode change. A first attempt that switched mode across the tie gave "unterminated tie". "a2" now prints at 159 and on beat 3 of 160. Dynamics remain equal.

### 5. 158–162: Hn 3·4 low in treble clef. Applied.
- Hn 4 plays E3, F3, D3, G3 and C3; the C3 at 162 needs four ledger lines.
- Hn 3 plays A3–B3.

Earlier cycles put this pair in bass clef at 11–12 and 23–26 for the same register.

Fix:
- `\clef bass a1\ff\>` at 158 in recTwoHnThree;
- `\clef treble c'1\f` at the start of codaHnThree. Bar 163 starts a system, so the courtesy clef falls at the line end.

### 6. 166: the timpani half rest straddles the middle of the bar. Applied.
`c4->\ff r2 r4\fermata` puts a half rest on beat 2 that crosses the half-bar. That breaks the rule that rests in 4/4 must show the middle of the bar (Open University; Gould's grouping rules).

Fix: `c4->\ff r4 r4 r4\fermata`. The fermata stays on beat 4, matching the other parts.

### 7. Spelling audit: no errors found. No change.
- **Simultaneous clashes:** the only enharmonic clash between parts is bar 56, harp F♭5 against E♮ in Cl, Hn and strings. The harp needs F♭ for its pedal setting, so this is correct harp practice.
- **B-minor block (97–102, `\transpose c cis`):** spells cleanly. It uses B, C♯, D, E, F♯, G, A♯ and G♯ as the raised 6th, plus three C♮ Neapolitans. There are no E♯, B♯ or double sharps.
- **Chord functions:**
  - German sixth at 12: F♯ (Vln II, Hn 3·4 A♭→F♯).
  - French sixth at 108: A♭–C–D–F♯.
  - Neapolitans: D♭ throughout.
  - Motto in A♭ minor at 77–78: E♭–C♭–B♭–B𝄫–A♭. The B𝄫 is correct, since it is the ♭2 and keeps the letters stepping.
- **Bar 78:**
  - The violas re-spell C♭ as B when the chord moves from A♭ minor to E.
  - Cl 1 keeps A♭ on its held note over the E chord. That is correct: a tied or held note keeps its spelling.
- **Chromatic lines** are spelled by direction: B♭–A–A♭, D–D♭–C, F♯–F–E at 79.
- **Bar 152:** the cycle-63 D♭ against the clarinet's A♮ is two descending chromatic passing notes and is correct.
- **102→103:** G♯ moves to A♭ across the key change. Keep the G♯: it lies inside an F♯ chord.

### 8. Checked and left alone (not applied)
- **Motto rhythm:** a dotted quarter on beat 2 crossing the half-bar (`r8 g8 es4. d8`, 75 occurrences). The rhythm is clear, it is the protected motto rhythm, and it is normal Romantic practice. Rewriting it with ties would hurt readability.
- **Dotted-half rests in 4/4:** `r2.` appears at 1, 5, 9, 41, 46, 52, 55, 73, 97, 138, 144, 167, 171 and 173. Some style guides prefer `r2 r4`, but sources disagree, so I give this low priority. It is optional.
- **Clefs that should stay as they are:**
  - Bassoon solo 33–34 and 77 reaches E♭4 and C♭4 at most, two ledger lines. A tenor clef for two bars would cost more than it saves.
  - Viola reaches F5 only briefly in the 144 scale, so no treble clef is needed.
  - Hn 4 F3 at 26 is in bass clef; at 130 it is a single note.
- **Other items that need no change:**
  - Vln I/II A♭6 at 155–156 is written loco. That is acceptable for violins at the climax, and the flutes are a2 and cannot take an ottava.
  - Beaming of eighths follows the half-bar.
  - Triplet numbers 109–113 follow cycle 55.
  - Subito p at 62 is correct.
  - The bare G octave at 84 is correct.
  - Harp pedal marks in rests (154 B♮ A♮) are correct.
  - Cl 1 F5 at 116 is correct.
