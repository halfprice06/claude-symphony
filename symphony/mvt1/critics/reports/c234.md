## Findings

### 1. Bar 72: the trombones double the root and leave the fifth to Hn 4 alone (applied)

What is wrong. Bar 72 is the f cadence chord of the exposition (A♭ major), held as a half note on beats 3–4 after the tune's A♭–E♭–C. c149 brought the trombones and tuba in on this chord only. The trombones play Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 A♭3, B.tbn A♭2, tuba A♭1. With trumpets and horns the brass weights (1 trumpet = 1 trombone = 2 horns) on beats 3–4 are A♭ 11, C 4, E♭ 1. The only E♭ in the brass is Hn 4's E♭3. Above the trumpets the woodwinds hold only C (Fl a2 C6, Ob 1·2 C5) and the clarinets A♭4; on beat 4 Hn 4 is the only E♭ in the orchestra (violas and Vln II stop on beat 3).

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 101: "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts are generally allotted to the two remaining trombones reinforced by a trumpet or two horns in unison, so as to obtain a perfect balance of tone." The bass here is right (B.tbn A♭2 / tuba A♭1), but the two upper trombones spend themselves on root and third, so the third upper part falls to one horn below them. PDF 100: brass part-writing "of the close order with no empty spaces". PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords. PDF 110–111: when trumpets and trombones are in the chord, the woodwind above should complete the harmony; here it cannot, because the woodwinds are on the tune and counter.

Fix (clos.ily). Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 take the fifth and the third; with Tpt 2 they make the close triad C4–E♭4–A♭4 that the diagram on PDF 101 shows (two trombones plus one trumpet over trombone 3 and tuba in octaves).
```
closTbnOne: R1*3 | c'4->\f c'4-> c'2->  |   % before
            R1*3 | es'4->\f es'4-> es'2-> |   % after
closTbnTwo: R1*3 | aes4->\f aes4-> aes2-> |   % before
            R1*3 | c'4->\f c'4-> c'2->    |   % after
```
Brass weights on beats 3–4 become A♭ 9, C 4, E♭ 3, with the E♭ in the middle register (E♭4, with the violas' E♭4 on beat 3). On beat 2 (bass E♭) the trombones hold C4/E♭4 over B.tbn E♭3, a plain 6/4 inversion as before. Tune, counter, bass, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. The protected items for 69–72 (tune, counter, bass A♭–C–B♭–D–E♭, no trombones before 72) are untouched; c149, which added these two trombone notes, gave no reason for the doubled root.

### 2. Bar 166: Hn 4 doubles the bass and the horn chord has no fifth (applied)

What is wrong. At 166 (mp, the tonic on which E♮ turns to E♭ before the coda) the horns hold Hn 4 C3, Hn 2 C4, Hn 3 C4, Hn 1 E4→E♭4. Three of the four horns are on C, and Hn 4 doubles the bass C (Bsn 1·2 C2/C3, Vc C2, Cb C2, harp). The fifth G is only in the violas' G4 and the harp's G5. Hn 4 then leaps up to G3 for 167.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 100: "the quartet of horns presents every facility for four-part harmony, perfectly balanced in tone, without doubling the bass in octaves" (the diagrams on PDF 100–101 show the four horns as a complete chord above the bass). At 166 the low strings and bassoons already have the bass, so Hn 4's C3 adds weight to the root and takes the fifth away from the horn chord. My scan of every soft horn chord (c above) found this the only one in the movement where the horns double the bass and also leave out a chord note.

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoHnFour, last bar):
```
... | d2\p r2 | R1 | c1\mp | }   % before
... | d2\p r2 | R1 | g1\mp | }   % after
```
The horns now hold G3–C4–C4–E4/E♭4, a complete triad without the bass, and Hn 4's G3 is the note it plays at 167, so the leap disappears. Hn 3's B3→C4 (c222) and the E♮→E♭ in Hn 1, Cl 1 and the violas are unchanged. HISTORY protects "climax scoring 158–166" and "139–166" (form, c233); this change keeps the instruments, levels, harmony and form of 166 and only moves an inner horn from a bass doubling to the missing fifth, as c222 did for Hn 3 in the same bars.

### 3. Bar 40: the leading tone of the dominant seventh is one horn against three trombones (applied)

What is wrong. At 40 (f<, into the sf chord at 41) the brass chord over the B♭ bass is Tbn 1 B♭3, Tbn 2 F3, B.tbn B♭2, Hn 4 F3, Hn 3 D4. The seventh A♭ is in Bsn 1 and the cellos, as c124 arranged. The leading tone D has one horn in the brass, worth half a trombone, while Hn 4 doubles Tbn 2's F3 at the unison. All the brass are marked the same f<.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 102, three-part writing: "If the instruments are mixed the number of horns should be doubled"; the diagrams on PDF 102–103 always put the horns in pairs beside trombones and trumpets. PDF 103 gives the alternative only for orchestras where pairs are impossible: mark the horns one degree louder. Adler (adler2 PDF 44): composers "equate the strength of two horns in unison with that of one trumpet or one trombone". Bar 39, where the horns are alone with the bassoons, stays as c124 left it (Hn 4 F3, so the fifth is not missing there).

Fix (trans.ily, transHnFour, bar 40):
```
R1*4 | ges1\p\< | aes1 | f1 | f1\f\<  | g2.\ff es4( | ...   % before
R1*4 | ges1\p\< | aes1 | f1 | d'1\f\< | g2.\ff es4( | ...   % after
```
Hn 3·4 now play D4 a2 (same dynamic, so the shared staff prints "a2"); F3 stays in Tbn 2 and the violas' tremolo. Hn 4 goes F3–D4–G3 into the four-horn call at 41, whose notes are unchanged. Two vl.txt entries leave (Hn 4 F3→G3 in octaves with Tbn 2 and the violas into 41). This is the smallest of the three findings; the violas' f tremolo on D4 already gives the leading tone some weight, so the gain is in brass balance, not in harmony.
