## Findings

The brass presentation of the movement's melodies matches this reading almost point by point. Each theme gets a brass colour from Adler's list, and each colour is used once, at a structural joint:
- the motto: dark horn/trombone unison at 117–121;
- the second theme: horn + cello at 47, trumpet + oboe at 69 and 158;
- the first theme: trombones + bassoons + cellos at 131, horns + trumpets in octaves at 167.

None of the three findings below justifies a change in movement I. I applied nothing.

### 1. Bars 158–161: Hn 3·4 unison on the counterline at fff (checked against Schuller; keep)

**Question.** Since c174, Hn 3 and Hn 4 play the counterline in unison at ff–fff: G4–C5 | B4–F4 | E♭4–C5 … A♭4 | A♭4–G4, which is written D5–G5 | F♯5–C5 | B♭4–G5. Adler quotes Schuller (adler2 PDF 26): "When more than one player attempts a high passage at a loud dynamic level … it becomes virtually impossible for any one of the players to sustain his note … advisable to orchestrate such passages … for first and third horn alone, or to reinforce the two horns with one trumpet." That reads as an argument against c174.

**Evidence.**
- Schuller's warning is about the high register. The top note here is written G5, reached three times on strong beats. That is the top of the normal tutti range, not the high register.
- The corpus treats written G5 as ordinary for unison horns: Tchaikovsky 6/i bar 73 has all four horns on written G5, and Dvořák 9/i has Hn I·II on written G5 in about 30 places, plus A5 at 224.
- Schuller's other remedy (horns plus a trumpet) would take Tpt 3 off the E♭4 that c154 added at 160, which is protected.
- Removing Hn 4 would bring back the imbalance c174 fixed, on the authority of adler2 PDF 15 and Rimsky-Korsakov.

**Fix.** None. At the parts stage (cycle 185) the Hn 4 part can carry a small "a2 with 3." note at 158 so the player knows the line is doubled and need not force. This needs no score change.

### 2. Bars 131–134: does the tenor theme dominate? (checked; keep)

**Question.** Adler (PDF 23) says to know which melody notes must dominate and how. In *1812* (Ex. XI-13, PDF 29–30, checked on the zoomed image) the horns play the anthem with the trombones, bassoons and low strings, against the counterpoint above. At 131 our Hn 3·4 instead hold C4/G3, then B3/D4, at ff>f, inside the octave of the theme (G3–E♭4). The theme's D4 and D♭4 rub a second against Hn 3's sustained C4.

**Evidence and reasons to keep.**
- The horns' ff at 131 is where the G-pedal crescendo (Hn 4 at 127–130) arrives. They reach f by 132, and the theme enters an eighth later.
- The theme is already in seven wind players plus the cellos: Tbn 1·2, Bsn a2, Cl a2, Vc.
- c144 lowered the trombones to f here because brass were outweighing the blend (Adler PDF 147–148, *Istar*). Adding Hn 3·4 to the theme (the *1812* layout) would undo that balance.
- c174 looked at this passage, moved the trumpets into the timpani rhythm and kept the horns sustained on purpose.
- Passing seconds against a held chord tone in the same octave are normal in Tchaikovsky's tuttis. In 6/i bars 214–217 the horns' line crosses the trombones' chromatic descent.

**Fix.** None. Do not apply the *1812* layout here. If a later cycle finds the D–D♭–C descent unclear at 131 and 133, the smallest remedy is Hn 3·4 at `\f` (not `\ff\>`) at 131. Graded brass at 131 is protected, so that change would need its own case.

### 3. Colours Adler lists that movement I does not use: a note for movements II and III (no change)

The reading names three melodic brass colours that this movement leaves out:
- the tuba as a melodic voice, either doubling a low theme with bass clarinet and bassoons (Wagner, PDF 33) or saved to give the theme "fresh color … two octaves lower" (Hindemith, PDF 20);
- the high four-horn unison, "a brilliant, high concept" (PDF 26);
- the full low-brass bass unison of tuba, trombones, bassoons, cellos and basses (Liszt, PDF 33–34).

Holding these back is correct in movement I, where the tuba only supports the bass and the four-horn call at 41–42 is low and dark on purpose. They are the obvious unused resources for the finale's apotheosis. Suggested layout for movement III's motto apotheosis: horns in pairs in octaves, or Hn 1 + Hn 3 on the top octave reinforced by one trumpet (Schuller's layout, PDF 26), rather than four horns in unison above written G5. I recommend adding this to cyclic-notes.md. Nothing to change in movement I.
