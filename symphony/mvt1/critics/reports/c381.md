## Findings

This is a second pass with the same reading. Every principle in the chapter that applies to this movement is already followed, and I found no bar where the reading supports a change. The findings below record what I checked and why each passage should stay as it is. I applied nothing.

### 1. Bars 117–121 and 131 / 167: one head, three registers. Keep.

**Check.** Adler (PDF 20) says register decides whether a brass unison sounds dark or bright: Hindemith "could have transposed it up a third or a fourth" for brilliance.

The score uses this on its two cyclic heads:
- **Motto at 117–121:** dark. Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2 on G3–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4, ff, with B.tbn an octave below and no trumpets.
- **P head at 131:** dark again. It is in the tenor (Tbn 1·2, Bsn a2, Cl a2, Vc), at the same G3–E♭4 pitch level.
- **P head at 167:** bright. Tpt 1·2 a2 at G4–E♭5, ff, with Hn 1·2 an octave below.

The listener therefore hears the same rising sixth G–E♭ first as a dark brass unison, then as the bright trumpet line at the coda.

Holding the trumpets back from the head until 167 is what makes 167 sound like a summary. It follows Adler's Beethoven principle (PDF 17).

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bars 46–52, 103–106 and 146–150: the horn-and-strings S. Keep.

**Check.** Adler item 1 (PDF 27) is the horn doubling a long sustained string melody. In each of these statements the S head is in Hn 1 with the cellos, at a different level and with a different partner:

| Bars | Key | Doubling |
|---|---|---|
| 46–52 | A♭ | Hn 1 + Vc |
| 103–106 | D minor | Hn 1, with Bsn 1 + Vc an octave below |
| 146–150 | C major | Hn 1 + Vla, with Vc an octave below |

This fits Adler PDF 20: repetitions get new colours, but the lead colour stays the same. That lead colour is the one movement II takes up for its theme A (mvt1.md, Seeds, 46–52).

**The high notes.** The doubling also protects the exposed horn notes:
- C5 at 51 (written G5), approached by a sixth at p dolce, is in unison with the cellos.
- E5 at 106 (written B5) is the one note not covered: there Hn 1 is an octave above Bsn 1 and the cellos, alone on top, at mf>. It is a single horn, and the part already has an ossia an octave lower (E4–D♯4, in unison with the cellos), so no change is needed.

A split note would not cost the tune. Schuller's warning (PDF 26) concerns loud unisons of two horns, not one horn with strings.

**Corpus.** Dvořák 9/i gives its Allegro theme to the horns, mf< f (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesA.ily, `hornIIImA`, first bars of the Allegro molto). That is the same idea in a louder form: a theme made memorable by its horn colour.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 158–161: S climax doubled in three octaves with trumpet and oboe. Keep.

**Check.** The Elgar (Ex. XI-8, PDF 24) builds a massive sound from octave and double-octave doublings. The Weinberger (Ex. XI-11, PDF 28) shows a trumpet and oboe together cutting through a full texture. At 158–161 the tune is laid out as follows:

| Octave | Instruments |
|---|---|
| E6 / A♭6 | Vln I, Fl |
| E5 | Tpt 1, Ob 2, Vln II |
| E4 | Hn 1·2, Vc |

Adler (PDF 23) asks which melody notes must be stressed, and how. Here they are the two rising-sixth goals, E (158) and A♭ (160). Each is the longest note in its bar, carries a tenuto in every part with the tune, and falls on a downbeat under "largamente".

Schuller's high-unison point (PDF 26) applies to Hn 3·4 at 158–161 and was answered at c174, c181, c266 and c291. I found no new reason to reopen it.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 13–14 and 41–44: the horn call. Keep.

**Check.** Adler (PDF 26) calls the four-horn unison "a brilliant, high concept". The score uses the four-horn unison only low:
- At 41–42 Hn 1–4 play E♭3–C4–B♭3 ff.
- At 43–44 Hn 1 alone answers an octave higher, E♭4–C5–B♭4 p.
- At 13–14 Hn 1 plays the call solo, mp: E♭3–C4–B3, with B♮ unresolved.

Each statement is in a different register and at a different level, and the call has the motto's shape (a rising sixth, then a semitone down). That is what makes it memorable. Its last form, Cl 1 at 175–177, keeps the shape in a new colour.

The high, loud four-horn unison and the major form of the call in the trumpets are reserved for the finale (mvt1.md "Do not reuse"). Raising 41–42 by an octave would spend the first of these.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bars 175–181: trombone chorale and the motto canon. Keep.

**Check.**
- **The last phrase must not be muddy** (Adler PDF 24). The three trombones are pp in open position, and the lowest third (D♭3–F3 at 178) is above the low-interval limit.
- **A canon works by colour contrast** (Berlioz, PDF 30–32: p trombones answering a bassoon over an ostinato). Tbn 1's motto at 175 is answered at the octave by Vln I at 177.4, brass against strings. The timpani pulse at 179–180 plays the part of the ostinato.
- **Ending a brass passage** (*Lohengrin*, PDF 35–36: the brass fade to pp under a continuing melody). Here Tbn 2, the bass trombone and the bass clarinet leave after 179. Tbn 1 restrikes C4 at 180 and fades to ppp at 181, while Vln I continues the motto.

These coda bars are protected by c228, c249, c267 and c278.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Melody overall: would a listener hum the themes?

The reading is about presentation, so this verdict comes from the lens.

- **Motto** (G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C). It is short, has a rising minor sixth and a chromatic return, and is heard in at least six colours. It is as easy to keep in mind as the motto of Tchaikovsky 5.
- **S** (E♭ | C–B♭ | A♭–F–E♭ | D♭–C–B♭–D♭–F | A♭–G). This is the hummable tune: a rising sixth and then a long stepwise descent. Its climax at 160 is the rising sixth again, a minor sixth C6–A♭6 at fff. That is the kind of arrival Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff build their second groups towards.
- **P.** A driving figure rather than a song, with the motto's sixth and D♭ in its head. Its memorability comes from repetition and the hammered tutti, which is right for an Allegro appassionato.
- **The horn call.** Two bars, completed in the finale.

No theme should change. Every change to a quoted theme would have to go to three other movements, and the reading gives no gain to set against that.
