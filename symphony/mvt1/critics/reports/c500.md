## Findings

### 1. The long-range ♭II plan across the four movements holds in v500 (no change)

Unit 28 gives ♭II two kinds of use. The local uses are a chord leading to V (§1–8) and a neighbour inside I (§15). The long-range uses are tonicizing ♭II, restating a theme on ♭II, ♭VI→♭II in major, and ♭II as a pivot (§9, §11, §14). Movement I keeps the local uses and leaves the long-range uses to the later movements. I checked this against v500 bar by bar.

- **Three structural N6 cadences, each with a different one of the Ex. 28-2 voice leadings** (PDF 484):
  - 20–22 is the direct diminished third, D♭4–B3 in Hn 3 (28-2a).
  - 140–143 fills it through iiø6/5 and a 6/4, D♭4–C4–B3 in Hn 3/Tbn 1/Tpt 3 (28-2e). The soprano D6 sits on top of the applied chord (Ex. 28-6).
  - 173–174 fills it through the 6/4, C.a. D♭5–C5 and solo Vc C5–B4 (28-2c).
  This is what a master does with a recurring cadence: the same function each time, with a different surface.
- **Neighbour "♭II" inside the tonic** (Ex. 28-20): at 3 (motto), 7→8, 178.3 (full root-position D♭ chord over the motto bass) and 180.3 (Vln I D♭5 alone over the C pedal). The intro and the coda frame the movement with the same plagal ♭II–I colour. This is the one long-range use movement I does make, and it is the right one for a coda (PDF 495).
- **Root-position ♭II at 115**, ff, over the wedge bass. A&S require a motivic reason for ♭II5/3 (PDF 487). Here the reason is the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C, enlarged in the bass at 113–116. It leads through 116 into the G pedal at 117, so the development reaches its dominant through ♭II on the largest scale in the movement.
- **Reserves, measured in full.midi:**
  - The highest violin and flute note is A♭6 (MIDI 92).
  - Trumpet B→C lands at 27 and 168 (C minor), 29 (over E♭, with F and A♭), 114, 139 (F minor) and 161.88 (inside V). None lands on a C-major chord.
  - There is no D♭ key area, no ♭VI→♭II in C, and no theme on ♭II.
  The finale's D♭ tonic at 211–212, the theme on ♭II (§11, Ex. 28-17) and the ♭VI–♭II close (Ex. 28-14) all remain unspent.

**Fix:** none.

### 2. The coda's N6 (173) is the last ♭II cadence before the attacca. It does what the later movements cite (no change)

The scherzo record says its Neapolitan 6/4 at 331 restates "♭II before its last dominant, as movement I reaches V through its N6 at I 173". The finale's ♭II thread says it completes "the coda's N6 – cadential 6/4 – V at 171–175". Movement II's 87 reinterprets D♭/F as IV6 of A♭ (A&S §14, PDF 494). In v500, 173.3–4 is a full two-beat N6 with ♭2̂ on top. Its ♭2̂ reaches 1̂ only on the 6/4 at 174 (PDF 483), and V at 174.3 is left open on B4 (protected c270). So the three later movements answer a real, complete progression, which ends on V and is not resolved in this movement. That is the arc a cyclic finale needs.

I considered adding a soft horn or harp to the N6 at 173 to give the last Neapolitan more colour (A&S PDF 483: ♭II6 "at climactic moments, at fermatas, before rhetorical silences"). I rejected it, for three reasons:
- The solo cello and cor anglais duet (c270, c337, c477) is what movement II's solo violin and Ob 1 answer at II 15–22.
- The rhetorical weight belongs to the stop chord and silence at 170, just before.
- A&S's own coda example of ♭II's inward colour is a thinning, not a reinforcement: Verdi's ♭VI–♭II at ppp, morendo (Ex. 28-14, PDF 490).

**Fix:** none.

### 3. The soprano at 21: keep the arpeggiated fall rather than the diminished third (no change)

A&S say the diminished third ♭2̂–♯7̂ "often makes for a peculiarly intense melodic line, especially when it occurs in the soprano" (PDF 484). At 21 the top line is D♭6–B♭5–G5. The diminished third is in Hn 3, an inner voice. Rewriting the violins as D♭6–B5 would make P's first cadence more intense. I am not proposing it, for two reasons:
- P's stepwise fall 20.3–22 is protected (c421), and the stated reason is the contrast with 31 and 141–142.
- The recapitulation (141–142) is where the soprano gets the filled chromatic descent, over iiø6/5 and the 6/4 (Ex. 28-2e). If the exposition took the diminished third in the soprano, the recapitulation's version would no longer be the stronger one.

This matches what Mozart does in Ex. 28-1. The intense ♭2̂ figuration is saved for the climax (bar 52), not spent at the first cadence.

**Fix:** none.

### 4. Would a theme on ♭II make movement I more memorable? No: it belongs to the later movements (no change)

The strongest long-range ♭II device in the unit is the Appassionata design (Ex. 28-17, PDF 492–493): a theme on I in minor, restated a half step higher on ♭II, then V. Movement I could state P's head on D♭ in the development (for example in place of the B♭-minor fugato end at 94–96, or at 115). The design is memorable, and it would tie the motto's D♭ to a key.

I reject it on cyclic grounds:
- The finale makes D♭ its one tonicized ♭II (205–212: A♭7 → D♭, timpani retuned C→D♭ at 206 for it).
- The finale's own c383 declined an earlier D♭-major statement to keep D♭ for its coda.
- Movement II spends ♭II as A minor at 63–64 with P's head in Tbn 1.
- coherence/mvt1.md lists "D♭ as a key in C … and a theme restated on ♭II" under Do not reuse.

If movement I states a theme on ♭II, the finale's arrival loses its one new harmonic event. The 115 ♭II chord already plants the sound at ff for one bar, which is the right amount of foreshadowing.

**Fix:** none.

### 5. Chromatic notation is consistent with A&S §17–21 (no change)

The spelling scan of all parts found 17 three-note chromatic runs that do not follow the plain "raise going up, lower going down" pattern. Every one is covered by the unit:
- 90 (D–E♭–E) and 93 (G–A♭–A) are fugato lines whose middle notes are diatonic in the local key, rising to its leading tone. The scale degrees of the key stay represented (PDF 498–499).
- 96 and 114–115 (B♭–A–A♭, C–B–B♭) use the raised 7̂ of the local minor in both directions (§21).
- 7 (F–E–E♭) and 165 (F–E–E♭) show tonal direction. E♮ is the third of the C-major chord at 8 and 165, then turns to E♭ as mixture (Ex. 28-25).
- 35 (Vla D♭4–D4) is ♭7̂ of E♭ rising, which §20 allows (Ex. 28-24b).
- The one augmented-unison neighbour is D–D♭–D at 114–116 in Hn 3/Tpt 3. There the D♭ is the root of the ♭II chord at 115. Writing it C♯ would hide the Neapolitan (§9, "be guided by the sound"), so keep it.

**Fix:** none.
