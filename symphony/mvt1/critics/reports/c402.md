## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 154→155, the recapitulation's main cadence in C: no voice takes the leading tone to C, and the V7 has no third on beats 3–4 (applied)

**What is wrong.** Bar 154 is the cadential 6/4 and V7 that closes the C-major second theme. The 155 tonic is the recapitulation's first full cadence. Here is what sounds now:

| Beat | Pitches | Chord |
|---|---|---|
| 154.1 | G2 (Cb), G3 (Bn 2), C4 (Hn 1, Vc), G4 (C.a.), C5 (Va), E5/G5 (Vln II) | 6/4 |
| 154.2 | B3 (Hn 1, Vc) and B4 (Va), the tune | 5/3 |
| 154.3 | G2, G3, D4 (Hn 1, Vc), F4 (C.a.), D5 (Va and Vln II), G5 | G–D–F, no B |
| 154.4 | F3 (Vc), G3, F4 (C.a., Hn 1, Va), D5, G5 | G–D–F, no B |
| 155 | C2, C3, E3, E4, C5/E5 (Vln II) | I |

The tune C–B–D–F–E leaves the leading tone by a leap to D on beat 3. No other part holds B, so for two beats the dominant has no third. At 155 the C arrives only in Vln II, from D5.

This is a regression. c312 found the cadence correct at v312 because Vln II had B4→C5. c384 then moved Vln II's B4 up to D5/G5 so that the halo would not shadow the tune at the unison. Since then nothing has taken the B on to C. The exposition's matching cadence (54→55) has the leading tone in the violas, G3→A♭3 (c312).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 76: the leading tone has "its active tendency toward 1". The third of a triad "is never omitted except for special effects". Inversions of seventh chords "are usually complete", and root-position seventh chords may drop only the 5th.
- PDF 80: the inner voices' "main function ... is to complete the tones of the chord framed by the bass and soprano", by smooth, mostly conjunct connections.
- PDF 89, points 3–4, restate both rules.
- Here the third is missing on two of the four beats of the dominant, at the movement's structural cadence in C.

**Fix** (recTwo.ily, recTwoVlnTwo, bar 154). Rhythm, divisi, tremolo and dynamics stay as they are; only the lower note of beats 3–4 changes.
```
% v402
<d'' a''>1:32 | <e'' g''>4:32 <d'' g''>2.:32 |
% c402
<d'' a''>1:32 | <e'' g''>4:32 <d'' g''>4:32 <b' g''>2:32 |
```
- Vln II's lower note now runs E5–D5–B4–C5. It takes the B on beat 3, after the tune has left it, and rises to C5 at 155, where the part already has C5.
- Beats 3–4 are now a complete V7: G (bass), B4, D, F. The leading tone sounds in only one part at a time, never doubled.
- B4–F4 (Va, Hn 1, C.a.) is an augmented fourth. It opens to a sixth, E4–C5, at 155, the normal resolution of the tritone (PDF 83: d5→P5 is what to avoid, not this).
- c384's point holds. Vln II never plays the tune's notes at the unison: the tune is on D5 and F4 while Vln II holds B4.
- Vln II's lower note lies a third under the tune's D5 for one beat. That is an inner-voice crossing of one beat at pp sul tasto, the case PDF 88 calls least problematic.
- This changes c384's fix on beats 3–4 only. It does not undo c384's reason.

**Applied in the scratch copy:** yes.
- vl.txt diff: no added lines.
- d5→P5 scan: nothing new.
- The page image (score p. 15) shows a crotchet, a crotchet and a minim tremolo in Vln II at 154.

### 2. Bars 137→138: the bass leaps A♭→D, a tritone, into the recapitulated stop chord, in octaves with Horn 3 (applied)

**What is wrong.** In the recapitulated first group, bar 137 ends its three-crotchet groups on A♭ in the bass: Cb and Cbsn A♭1, Bn 2 A♭2. The stop chord at 138 stands on D. So the bass leaps A♭→D, an augmented fourth, at the ff arrival.

Hn 3 makes the same leap, A♭3→D4, in the same direction. The v402 vl.txt lists it four times among the doublings:
```
bar 138 beat 1.00 P8 bsnTwo:Ab2->D3 hnThree:Ab3->D4
bar 138 beat 1.00 P8 cbsn:Ab1->D2 hnThree:Ab3->D4
bar 138 beat 1.00 P8 hnThree:Ab3->D4 BASS:Ab1->D2
bar 138 beat 1.00 P8 hnThree:Ab3->D4 cb:Ab1->D2
```
Hn 3 is not a bass doubling. It is the inner pad voice that holds A♭3 through 137 and takes D4 in the stop chord. So these are real parallel octaves between the bass and an inner voice, reached by a dissonant leap in both parts.

The exposition never had this. At 25.4 c242 replaced the same A♭ with D: "iv – iiø7 – vii°7 by falling thirds, no augmented second A♭–B in the bass". That makes the bass F–D–B into 26, and Hn 3 A♭3→D4 moves against a falling bass. The recapitulation kept the A♭ while its stop chord moved to D.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 83: parallel octaves "provide some contrast but not enough to give the feeling of two individual voices". They are forbidden "within the same pair of voices". PDF 84 exempts only doublings, and Hn 3 here is not doubling the bass.
- PDF 82: dissonant leaps are "excluded entirely from the simplest vocal styles". They become logical only inside chords like the dominant seventh. The A♭ at 137.4 belongs to iv6 (F–A♭–C), not to the arriving diminished seventh.
- PDF 80: the bass must "make explicit the harmonic meaning of the soprano" and regulates the chord succession. c242 already applied the same reading at 25 (A&S Ex. 11-14b, PDF 171).

**Fix** (recOne.ily; rhythm, accents and dynamics unchanged): bar 137 beat 4 takes D, as at 25.4.
```
recOneBsnTwo:  c4 f,2-> aes,4 | d2.->\f r4 |   →   c4 f,2-> d4 | d2.->\f r4 |
recOneCbsn:    c,4 f,,4-> f,,4 aes,,4 |         →   c,4 f,,4-> f,,4 d,4 |
recOneCb:      c,4 f,,4-> f,,4 aes,,4 |         →   c,4 f,,4-> f,,4 d,4 |
```
- 137.4 becomes iiø7: D2/D3, F3, A♭3, C4, F4, F5. That is the exposition's chord at 25.4 note for note.
- The bass then holds D into the stop chord, where C4 (Cl 2, Hn 2, Va) moves to B. Bn 2 already repeats D3 into the stop chord at 25→26.
- Hn 3's A♭3→D4 now moves over a held bass (oblique motion), so its octaves with the bass are gone.
- The repeated D does not weaken the stop chord. Its accent comes from the fresh ff entry of the brass, the upper winds and the strings at 138.1, which this fix leaves as it is.

**Protections answered.** The stop-chord layout at 138 is unchanged in every part (c222, c224, c244; finale IV 222 matches it). So are the three-crotchet grouping, rhythm and dynamics of 135–137 (c158, c238, c393), and Hn 4's G3 at 135–137 (c393). Bar 137 is not quoted by any other movement (checked in finale.md, mvt2.md and scherzo.md).
