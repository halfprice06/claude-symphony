## Decision
**Do not make D♭ a key in this movement's recapitulation.** Keep 123–138 as written, with no added bars and no re-harmonisation. The D♭ should become a key in movement II and be resolved as a key in movement III (findings 3 and 4). Nothing was applied, so the bar count is unchanged at 178.

## Findings

### 1. Neither model gives grounds for a D♭ excursion at 131–134 (no change)
**Eroica.** The excursion sits in a soft recapitulation that has not yet arrived.
- The recapitulation begins p in the cellos at 398.
- At 402 the C♯ gets a new harmony: under E–G–B♭, where in bar 7 it was under G–B♭ (MIDI pitch-class extraction).
- The music then goes to F major. That passage is a horn solo marked dolce: Horn1MvtI.lyi line 248, `\transposition f c2(_\mDolce`, at 408.
- D♭ major follows in the flute at 416, then B♭7 at 424.
- The ff tutti arrives only at 430.
- So the excursion happens before the arrival and is quiet. The Eroica's P-zone (398–430) is about as long as its exposition's.

In v79 the recapitulation arrives ff at 123 after a crescendo retransition. Its P-zone is half the exposition's, which is the cycle-43 ratio, the same as Beethoven 9's. There is no soft pre-arrival space where a dolce excursion could happen.

**Tchaikovsky 5.** The F♯-minor turn at 364–372 is not the return of a problem note. Oh (pp. 2–3) explains it as a transposition plan: the recapitulation's TR is "an exact transposition of Exposition's TR by a whole-step" (c♯ minor), so S returns in E major. v79's S already returns in the tonic, C major, at 139, so it has no such need. Cycle 59 cited both models as making the excursion "structural". That holds for the Eroica, not for Tchaikovsky 5.

**Compressed recapitulations in the corpus do not make the detour:**
- Beethoven 9/i (harmony.csv, onsets 628–676): the first group tonicises only iv (g minor, onsets 645–651), then cadences in D.
- Dvořák 9/i: the first group (277–291) stays in E minor (277–288 E/C/G/B chords; 289–291 G/D). The key shift to G♯ minor comes only with S (316 onward).

### 2. Inside this score, the insertion works against the reasons 123–134 was protected (no change)
**Why 123–134 is protected.**
- Cycle 19 protected it for the low theme with the syncopated counterline and the Neapolitan a♭''' peak.
- Cycle 23 protected it for the two-wave shape.

**What two inserted D♭ bars after 132 would do** (cycle 59's sketch: `des'''8 es''' f''' ges''' aes'''2~ | aes'''4. ges'''8 f'''4 des'''4`):
- They would extend the second wave's ff peak from 2 bars to 4, with A♭6 in four consecutive bars. In v79 the Vln I strikes A♭6 only at 31, 72, 114, 132 and 156 (score.midi), and cycles 23–24 worked to give 156 that register by weight.
- They would lengthen the ff block 123–135 that cycle 43 warned against following the 97–116 plateau.

**Re-harmonising without adding bars is not possible.**
- In 131, the melody on beat 4 is the transposed head, `es'''8 d'''8 c'''8`, and the violas have `e'8`. An A♭7 (G♭) to tonicise D♭ would clash with the D♮ and the E♮.
- In 133 the melody `aes'''4. g'''8` excludes staying in D♭.

Any D♭ key would therefore need new melody notes in protected bars.

**The arc.** The recapitulation's departures are already chosen: the major motto at 147–148, V–♭VI (A♭) at 154–156, and E♮→E♭ at 162. A D♭-major area 22 bars before 156 would make that A♭ sound like D♭'s dominant, an area already visited, instead of a fresh ♭VI.

### 3. In movement I, the D♭ already does enough. Leave it unresolved as a key (no change)
- The D♭ is the passing note in the head (15, 123).
- It is the sf peak at 20 and returns ff over a D♭ bass at 132.
- It is IV in the A♭ second group: Cb D♭ at 48 and 52 (tTwo.ily).
- It is the N6 at 169 and the motto's C–D♭–C at 173–177.
- It never appears in 139–162: recTwo.ily has no D♭ at all, so the C-major recapitulation is free of it.

This is Tchaikovsky 5's arc (Oh: the first movement and coda end in minor; the major key and resolution come in the finale). The first movement states the problem and does not solve it.

### 4. Proposed text for cyclic-notes.md (I did not edit that file)
> - (Critic 60) D♭ is the motto's problem note. Movement I never makes it a key: it is peak colour (20, 132), IV in A♭ (48, 52), N6 (169) and C–D♭–C (173–177). A recapitulation excursion was tested and rejected (c060). The later movements must give D♭ its key and then resolve it:
>   - **Movement II (A♭):** make D♭ major (IV of A♭, ♭II of the symphony's C) the key of the middle section or of II's climax. Derive it from the motto's tail E♭–D–D♭–C (in A♭: 5–♯4–4–3). End II with a plagal IV–I whose top voice is D♭–C. The motto's last two notes then become a cadence that resolves for the first time. Quote the second-theme head elsewhere, in a key remote from A♭ (for example E major), so the D♭ section keeps its own material.
>   - **Movement III (C major):** before the final chorale, a block shift from a forte D♭-major chord or phrase straight to C major ff. This is the Eroica's 557 (D♭, f) → 561 (C, ff), here doing the symphony's own D♭→C as keys. It fits the existing notes: the trumpets' B→C and the chorale tail C–D♭–C in its first statement, C–B–C in its last.

### 5. When the question may be re-raised
Re-open the excursion only if the recapitulation's start is re-composed to arrive softly, as in Eroica 398. A soft start would create the pre-arrival space where an Eroica-type detour belongs. As long as 123 arrives ff, the answer above stands.
