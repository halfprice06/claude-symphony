# Critic 407: texture and counterpoint (v407)

Lens: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II, "Melody in different groups of instruments combined together" (rimsky-principles PDF 74–80, book pages 56–62), second pass. I read all seven text pages in full and rendered each at 110 dpi. The music in this range is the bracketed doubling formulas (PDF 74–80) and the two trumpet/flute staff examples on PDF 75. ## Findings

The doubling plan follows the chapter almost everywhere. Finding 1 is the one clear breach of a rule the chapter states as a requirement, and I applied it. Findings 2 and 3 depart from the letter of the reading, and I recommend keeping both, for the reasons given.

### 1. Climax counterline 158–160: two winds over two horns at fff (applied)

**Reading.** On PDF 75 Rimsky-Korsakov writes: "If a single horn is used, the upper part is allotted to 2 clarinets, 2 oboes, or 2 flutes. But if there are two horns playing the lower octave in unison, three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages." The image of the page shows the two formulas exactly: 1 Ob + 1 Cl over **1** Horn, and 2 Fl + 2 Cl over **2** Horns. Ch. I gives the reason (PDF 41): "in forte passages two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone". So a pair of horns weighs as much as a trumpet, and a trumpet in the lower octave needs three or four winds above it (PDF 75, the next rule).

**Score.** Since c174, the counterline at 158–161 (G4–C5 | B4–F4 | E♭4–C5 …) is in Hn 3 and Hn 4 a2, ff< to fff. Above it, at the octave, there are only Ob 1 and Cl 1 (c241). That is the one-horn formula over two horns, at the movement's loudest point, with the full brass at ff around the line. The c241 comment in recTwo.ily quotes both formulas and then settles for the smaller one. Meanwhile Cl 2 holds E5 | D5 | C5. At 158 its E5 is at pitch with the tune's middle octave (Ob 2, Tpt 1, Vln II, E5 from beat 2). At 159 its D5 is a fifth that Tbn 1 already holds. So Cl 2 is the only wind in the passage that is not carrying something on its own. Every other wind is taken: Fl 1·2 double the tune's top octave above Tpt 1 (rule 6), Ob 2 is on the tune's middle octave, and the C.a. and B.cl are kept silent for 171 and 175 (c334).

**Fix (applied).** Cl 2 joins Cl 1 and Ob 1 on the counter for 158–159 and the downbeat of 160. That gives three winds over the two horns, the minimum the reading asks for. On beats 2–3 of 160 Cl 2 keeps c154's E♭5 (the woodwinds' fifth of the A♭ chord), C5 on beat 4, and D5 at 161 as before. At 161 Hn 4 leaves the counter on beat 3, so one horn stays under two winds there, which is the one-horn formula. One `a2` prints on beat 1 of 158.

### 2. Fugato third and fourth entries, 91–95: violas with Bsn 1 an octave below (keep)

**Reading.** The editor's note on PDF 78 says a string part and a wind part alone in octaves is "not to be recommended, as the tone quality of the two groups is so widely different". The same note says this doubling was "often used by the classics to obtain balance of tone". On PDF 76, Violas + Fag in unison is one of the natural pairs.

**Score.** At 88–90 Bsn 1 doubles the violas' entry at pitch. From 91 it drops an octave and doubles the violas' countersubject (F4–C5 / F3–C4), and at 94–95 their free voice (c237). Together with Vln II over the C.a. at 91–93 (c307 Finding 1), every line of the third entry is now a string–wind octave pair. My half-bar scan found this. c307 did not list it.

**Why it should stay.** Moving Bsn 1 back to the unison would put it at F4–C5. That is the bassoon's highest, thinnest register, and it would lie in the same octave as the C.a.'s C4–A4 entry, so the countersubject would weigh more against the subject's lower octave. Bsn 1 at F3–C4 fills the octave between the bass (B.cl, Bsn 2, Cb on F2) and the cellos' free voice on C4–E♭4, which would otherwise be empty. A fugato also wants its lines to differ in colour, and a string over a reed an octave apart is a line that keeps its own colour. The reading gives the classics' use as the reason for the practice, and this score's models are classics. Adler2 PDF 175–176 (keep a fugato's established colours) is the basis of c237's protection. No change.

### 3. 23–25 and 135–137: Fl 1 and Ob 1 both in unison with Vln I (keep)

**Reading.** PDF 76: "If several wind instruments play in unison with one group of strings, the latter will be overpowered."

**Score.** In the 3/4 grouping before each stop chord, Fl 1 and Ob 1 join Vln I at the unison, mf<, so the colour of the line changes from string to woodwind.

**Why it should stay.** The passage is the second step of the graded build 15–27 (P on Vln I alone 15–22, then winds, then the tutti at 27; protected c287). The change of colour is the purpose of the step. Rimsky-Korsakov describes the overpowering as an effect, not a fault. The clarinets' thirds underneath (rule 13) and the 3/4 grouping (c391) are also protected. No change.
