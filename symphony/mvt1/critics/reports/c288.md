## Reading notes

I read every text page of the MOLA guidelines in full (PDF 3–8, 12, 13, 16). PDF 2, 14 and 15 are blank or carry only a page number. I rendered and viewed every example page at 110 dpi: PDF 9 (Ex. 1–2, score order and names), PDF 10 (Ex. 3 cautionary signs with dynamics and hairpins, Ex. 4 cues and tempo words over multi-rests, Ex. 5 doubling change), PDF 11 (Ex. 6, Beethoven 9/iv from bar 851, Presto) and PDF 12 (Ex. 7, collisions of "cresc. molto", "rit.", "a tempo", mf and accents in an extracted part). The prose says little about expression itself. What it teaches on expression comes from three things: the rule that each player has a separate part (PDF 7), the examples, and the proofreading rules (PDF 8).

## Method

I wanted to test the score the way MOLA says parts are used, one player at a time (PDF 7). I reused the per-player engravers from v288/check-log.ly (written by an earlier critic), with the log pointed at /tmp/critic-288/notes.log, and ran them on all 30 single-player variables. I wrote three scripts in /tmp/critic-288/scripts: hp.py (hairpins without a target), dbl.py (articulation, hairpins and dynamics in every unison or octave doubling of four or more notes) and pair.py (dynamics and hairpins of the two players on each shared staff). I built the 30 parts with PARTS_AUDIT=1 and viewed the Vln I and Vc parts at 80 and 200 dpi. I also viewed the score pages around the changes. Cycle 168 used the same lens and reading and applied seven fixes, so this pass mainly checks what has changed since v188.

General verdict: by MOLA's standard the expression marking is in good order. Entries, hairpins, tempo words and bowings all hold up player by player. There are two small faults, both applied, and four points I checked and recommend leaving as they are.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bar 142: Hn 3, Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 play the hammered C–B without the accents of every other doubling.** Applied.
At 142, Fl 1, Ob 1, Ob 2, Cl 1, Vln I and Vln II play C–B in minims, all with accents. Cycle 278 added Ob 2's accents for the same reason. Hn 3 (C4–B3), Tpt 3 (C4–B3) and Tbn 1 (C4–B3) double the same notes an octave lower with no accent. In the exposition's counterpart at 32, every part on the C–B has the accent, Tbn 1 included (`c'4-> b4->`). MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11) shows the rule: in Beethoven's tutti the editor even adds bracketed [sf] to the winds so that every doubling has the same stress. In the brass parts read alone (PDF 7), 142 now looks like a sustained pad and not the cadential hammer stroke it is. Bsn 1 keeps its unaccented C–B, because it ends its slurred tenor line there (c157). The held G whole notes (Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2, Tbn 2, B.tbn) stay as they are.
Fix in recOne.ily: Hn 3 `c'2-> b2->`, Tpt 3 `c'2-> b2->`, Tbn 1 `c'2-> b2->` in bar 142. The shared staves combine correctly (Hn 4 and Tbn 2 hold G, so the voices are already apart). Checked on score page 14.

**2. Bars 124–125: the Bsn 2 and Cbsn diminuendo starts on a rest, a bar after Bsn 1's.** Applied.
Bsn 2 reads `g,2. r4\> | g,1~ | g,1\p` and the Cbsn reads `g,,2. r4\> | g,,1~ | g,,1\pp`. The hairpin starts on the breath rest on beat 4 of 124. Bsn 1, on the same staff, starts its hairpin on the note at 124.1 (`g2.\> r4`). MOLA Ex. 3 and Ex. 7 (PDF 10, 12) put each mark under the note it applies to. In the extracted Bsn 2 and Cbsn parts a hairpin under a rest is unclear: does it start before the breath or after it? The intended start is the restrike at 125, so the hairpin goes there.
Fix in dev.ily: Bsn 2 `g,2. r4 | g,1\>~ | g,1\p |`, Cbsn `g,,2. r4 | g,,1\>~ | g,,1\pp |`. Levels and notes are unchanged.

**3. "damp" (percussion) and "étouffez" (harp): open since cycle 168. Close it with no change.**
Cycle 168 left open the wish to put the damping instructions in one language (MOLA PDF 4). The phrase that mixed two languages ("étouffez with Cb." at 182) no longer exists: the harp now has a plain "étouffez" on beat 4 of 182. MOLA PDF 4 asks for "a conventional language such as English, Italian, German, or French" for each instruction. It does not ask for one language across instruments. "étouffez" is the term harpists read, and "damp" is the usual word in English percussion parts. Each part has only its own instruction, so no player sees both. I recommend it stays as it is and the open item is closed.

**4. Bar 116: Ob 2 crescendos into a rest at 117.** Keep.
In Ob 2's part, `aes'1\< | R1\!` swells to nothing. MOLA Ex. 3 has hairpins that end on a written level. Here, though, Ob 2 shares the staff with Ob 1, whose identical hairpin leads into its ff at 117. The score rule for shared staves (equal simultaneous dynamics) needs the two hairpins to match. A target dynamic on Ob 2's rest would be a mark under a rest, which is worse (principle 11). Removing Ob 2's hairpin would leave its part without the crescendo the whole wind section is making. The swell into the ff downbeat where the other winds arrive is ordinary Romantic practice. Keep.

**5. Vln I part, bars 179–182: "Tempo I (Lento)" and "change bow at will" nearly touch.** Keep.
At 200 dpi, the tempo word's "(♩ = 50)" ends about 1 mm before "change bow at will", with "attacca" above both. They do not overlap and both are readable, so this is not the collision MOLA Ex. 7 warns about. Moving the bowing text below the staff would put it on the hairpin and the ppp, and the coda's marks are protected (c228, c249). Keep, and look again if any mark is added in 177–182.

**6. Bars 139–142: Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 hold f while the rest of the tutti crescendos to ff.** Keep.
pair.py and dbl.py show Tbn 2's A♭3 doubling Cl 2's A♭4 (f< ff) at a steady f, and Hn 3·4 at f under Hn 1·2's f< ff. MOLA Ex. 6 shows doubled parts with the same dynamics, so this needed a decision. Cycle 283 kept "dense 139–141 below the 158 arrival", and cycle 264 graded the inner brass under the low theme at 131. Holding the inner brass at f keeps the A♭6 at 140 below the fff climax at 160. That is a balance decision, not an oversight, and MOLA's examples do not cover balance. Keep.
