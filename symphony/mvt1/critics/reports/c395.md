## Findings

The string notation in the score follows Adler ch. 2 throughout. I checked every chord against Ex. II-8/9/10, every div. against its unis., and every special effect, pizz. and arco against its return. I found no fault in the score itself. The two faults I found are in the instrumental parts. There the tighter part spacing turns two correct score markings into misleading ones. Adler PDF 30 says div. and unis. "should appear on the part", and PDF 54 says the same of pizz. and arco. So the reading treats the part as the document the player reads, not the score. Both fixes are text placement only.

### 1. Vln II part, bar 55: "unis." printed above "tutti, div." (applied)

**Bars:** Vln II 55. The half section (*metà*, 47–54) becomes tutti and divides on the beat-1 chord E♭4/A♭4. It returns to unison on the beat-4 pickup E♭4, which starts the S statement sul D.

**What is wrong:** In the score (c329), "tutti, div." sits over the chord, and "unis." and "sul D" sit over the pickup, at different heights, in time order. In the part, the bar is about 115 px wide at 220 dpi, and "tutti, div." is about 140 px. The two texts overlap horizontally, so LilyPond stacks them, and it puts "unis." on top. Reading down the stack, the player meets "unis." first, then "tutti, div.", then "sul D", over a chord that is to be divided. Adler PDF 30 makes the div./unis. pair the whole instruction: "If the word divisi does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop. When divisi is no longer in order … unis. should appear on the part." PDF 32 asks the same for metà/tutti. A part that reads "unis." over the divided chord fails that rule in the one place where the section changes size and division at the same moment. It also contradicts the score.

**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily 117–118):** the pickup's "unis." gets an outside-staff priority between the spanner (350) and the default text (450), so it always sits below "tutti, div." and above "sul D":
```
<es' aes'>2^\markup \italic "tutti, div." r4 ... es'4(\p^\tweak self-alignment-X #CENTER ^\tweak outside-staff-priority #400 ^\markup "unis."^\startTextSpan
```
Part, top to bottom: "tutti, div." / "unis. molto cantabile" / "sul D". The score is unchanged in effect: the same three levels, already in that order. Checked at 220 dpi in both. First I tried a two-line `\column { "tutti," "div." }`, but it put "tutti," and "unis." on one line, which read as "tutti, unis.", so I rejected it.

### 2. Vln I part, bar 182: the sul D extension line runs into "attacca" (applied; closes c385's open item)

**Bars:** Vln I 177.4–182, the last motto, sul D (c251), ending on the tied C5 that crosses into movement II.

**What is wrong:** In the part, the dashed sul D line ends exactly where the italic "attacca" end mark begins, on the same baseline: "- - - - attacca". c385 listed this as open ("Vln I sul D dashes touch 'attacca' at 182"), and it is still there in v395. Adler PDF 35 (Ex. II-16, Mahler "Sul G" with its line) and PDF 53 treat a string indication as running to a definite end, after which normal playing resumes. A line that runs into a word reads as if the word belonged to it ("sul D … attacca") and hides where the indication stops. MOLA PDF 8 asks for no collisions in parts.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily 60):** the spanner keeps its end on the last note (the indication still covers the tied C5, as mvt1.md records), but its dashes stop 3 staff spaces short:
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul D" \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.right.padding = #3 g'4(\ppEspr^"unis."\startTextSpan
```
Result: in the part, the line stops before the fermata, and "attacca" moves above "change bow at will", clear of everything. In the score, the line also stops before the fermata of 182. Checked at 200–220 dpi.
