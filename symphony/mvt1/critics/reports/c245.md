## Findings

### F1. Fifteen rests sit half a stave-space off the line grid (applied)

**Bars and rests** (offset from the centre line, in stave-spaces, measured by the probe):
- Hn 4 minim rest at 28 beat 3: −5.5.
- Hn 1·2, Hn 2 minim rest at 164 beat 3: −3.5.
- Hn 4 crotchet rests at 115, 123: −5.5; at 126: −6.5.
- Fl 1 quaver rests at 29: 5.5; at 117, 119 (two), 120 (two): 4.5; at 139: 5.5.
- Fl 1 crotchet rests at 131: 4.5; at 134: 5.5.
- Ob 1 quaver rest at 160 beat 3: 5.5.

**What is wrong.** On the shared staves, LilyPond's rest-collision code moves the second player's rest out of the way by whatever distance clears the other player. It does not round that distance to whole spaces. At 28 the result is visible at 500 dpi. Hn 4's minim rest stands on its own short ledger stroke halfway between the third and fourth ledger lines below the stave, so it lines up with neither the stave nor Hn 4's G3. The crotchet and quaver rests lose their fixed relation to the lines in the same way: their hooks fall on lines where they should fall in spaces.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 49: "From the centre of the stave, rests move an exact number of stave-spaces up or down, so that they remain correctly positioned in relation to the stave-lines". The "and not" example on that page shows exactly half-space positions. PDF 51: "Minim and semibreve rests are placed on a ledger line." The 15 cases are the only ones out of the 785 rests in the score.

**Fix (applied).** I added one callback to global.ily rather than 15 bar-by-bar overrides, which a later layout change would leave wrong:

```
#(define (gould-rest-snap grob)
  (let ((ss (ly:grob-object grob 'staff-symbol)))
    (if (ly:grob? ss)
        (let* ((common (ly:grob-common-refpoint grob ss Y))
               (y (- (ly:grob-relative-coordinate grob common Y)
                     (ly:grob-relative-coordinate ss common Y)))
               (frac (- y (round y))))
          (if (> (abs frac) 0.01)
              (let ((target (if (>= y 0) (ceiling y) (floor y))))
                (ly:grob-translate-axis! grob (- target y) Y)))))))
```

It is switched on next to the c225/c235 callbacks in score.ly line 49 and parts-gen.py line 146: `\override Rest.after-line-breaking = #gould-rest-snap`.

Only a rest that is off the grid moves, and it moves a further half space away from the centre, so it cannot come nearer the other player. After the fix the probe finds no rest off the grid. The 770 rests that were already correct are unchanged, including c135's overrides at 157, 161 and 162. The minim rests at 28 and 164 now stand on real ledger-line positions. No system or page break changed.

### F2. The Violin I part prints the score's octave signs (applied)

**Bars:** the Violin I part at 31, 71–72 and 121–123, plus the "loco" at 124.

**What is wrong.** parts-lib.ily strips score-only material from the parts: breaks, combine labels, rest overrides and `\tag #'score`. It does not strip `\ottava`, so the part prints all three 8va brackets I checked in the v245 build. The part is also inconsistent: it writes G6 and A♭6 at pitch at 41–42 and 160, but puts the A♭6 at 31 and 122 under 8va.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 47: "A full score that is not used to play from may replace ledger lines with the octave … sign as necessary, to save space between staves … These octave signs should not be transferred to a playing part, except where absolutely necessary." The same page says: "In performance material, always use ledger lines in preference to octave signs", and woodwind and strings "can use up to five ledger lines". The highest note under these signs is A♭6, which needs four ledger lines. Cycle 135 (reading row 15) noted the intent "8va in the score, not in the parts". The parts generator never carried it out.

**Fix (applied).**
- parts-lib.ily: new predicate `ottava-event?`. `partClean` and `partCleanNoClefs` now drop `OttavaEvent` (`(part-clean! … ottava-event?)` and `(lambda (x) (or (clef-change? x) (ottava-event? x)))`). `partCleanHarp` is unchanged. The harp keeps its 8va at 56–59, which saves six ledger lines and is allowed for harp (PDF 47).
- dev.ily line 311: the "loco" at 124 becomes score-only, `-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "loco"`. Without the 8va it would be a wrong instruction in the part.

The score is unchanged. I built the parts and viewed the new Violin I part: 31, 71–72 and 121–123 are at pitch, with four ledger lines at most, and 124 has no "loco". The page counts of all 30 parts are identical to the v245 build. Violin I, Violin II, viola, cello and Horn 3, which cross into movement II, each still have 2 pages and end on an even page.

### F3. Octave signs in the score at 31, 71–72, 121–123 against A♭6 at pitch at 140 and 160 (not applied; keep)

The score shows the movement's A♭6 peaks in two ways: under 8va at 31 and 122 and at pitch at 160. Gould PDF 47 allows both in a full score and allows the octave sign only "to save space". Cycles 105, 135, 175 and 193 protect this layout because of page fit (17 pages, K page break). The flute staff already shows the same pitches at pitch above the violins in every case. Each sign covers a whole phrase or held notes (PDF 47–48), and each ends correctly (PDF 44). With F2 the players never see them. Keep.

### F4. Horn ledger lines in treble clef at 13 and 141 (not applied; keep)

Hn 1's call at 13 goes down to E♭3, three ledger lines below the treble stave. Hn 3·4 at 141 reaches D3, four lines below. PDF 46 prefers a clef change to an octave sign, and PDF 47 says players read four ledger lines at a glance. The bass clef in the horn parts is handled by the part's own clef plan (`hnFourClefs`), and the score's clef plan is protected (c135, c175, c225). Four lines is within Gould's limit. Keep.
