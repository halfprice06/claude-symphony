## Findings

### 1. Vln II sul G reaches D5 at 101–102 (applied)
Bars 98–102. The sul G marking (cycle 141) runs to the end of 102, where Vln II plays B4, A♯4, F♯4 and D5 on the G string at ff in tempo 126. D5 is a twelfth above the open G; the leap F♯4→D5 at 102 has to be taken in seventh position.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26: "A limit should be set to the use of a high note on any one of the three lower strings ... This note should be the one in the fourth position, either the octave note or the ninth of the open string." He also says skips to high notes should be avoided. The corpus agrees in practice: Dvořák 9/i stops sul G before the high note (Vln II 213–217); Tchaikovsky's furthest example (6/iv m. 147, B4) is muted, p and slow, not an ff leap. Bars 98–100 already peak at G4, the octave, so the G-string colour that cycle 141 wanted is kept where the line lies low.
Fix (dev.ily, devVlnTwo): end the spanner on the last note of 100: `es'4.( des'8 c'4 es'8 ges'8)\stopTextSpan | bes'2--( a'2) | f'4( des''2-- c''8 g'8) } |` (inside the existing `\transpose c cis`). This does not touch the c141 reason (no open D on the held D4 at 98). Notes unchanged.

### 2. Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1 hold the augmented motto 117–123 without a breath (applied)
Bars 117–123: Hn 1·2 (a2) play G4–E♭5–D–D♭–C–A♭/F–G as tied whole notes, ff rising to fff, about 14 s with no rest; Bsn 1 does the same from 117 to 124 (about 16 s). The trombones on the same line breathe at the end of 121 (cycle 87), Hn 3·4 at 120; Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1 have nothing.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37 ("Wood-wind players cannot manage extremely long sustained passages, as they are compelled to take breath; care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time") and PDF 44 (the same "apply with equal force to the brass"); Adler adler1 PDF 303 (high horn playing is taxing; periodic rests). 
Fix (dev.ily): Hn 1 and Hn 2 `es'1~ | es'2. \breathe d'4 |` at 119; Bsn 1 `des'2. \breathe c'4~ |` at 120. The breaths are staggered against the trombones' breath at 121, so the line always sounds in at least two groups (cycle 87 rule). No note or dynamic changed.

### 3. Flutes and Ob 1 have no breath in the climax 158–163 (applied)
Bars 158–161: Fl 1·2 (a2, E6–A♭6) and Ob 1 play the tune ff–fff in two two-bar slurs with no rest; Fl 1 then continues into its 162–163 phrase without a gap (about 14 s in all, largamente). Cycle 156 gave the brass breaths at the end of 159; the woodwinds doubling the same tune were left out.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37 (as above); high fff flute playing uses the most air of any woodwind writing. The violins hold the tune in unison throughout, so a short breath costs nothing.
Fix (recTwo.ily): `g'''2) \breathe |` at the end of 159 in Fl 1 and Fl 2; `g''2) \breathe |` in Ob 1; `b''8 c'''8) \breathe |` at the end of 161 in Fl 1 before its new phrase at 162. This only adds the woodwinds to cycle 156's breath plan; the 158–166 scoring is untouched.

### 4. Hn 1 written A5 at mp at 154 (applied; pitch change)
Bar 154: after one bar's rest Hn 1 re-enters mp on concert C5–B4–D5–F4 (written G5–F♯5–A5–C5), doubling the violas at the unison. The written A5 is above the tenth partial, the top of the register Rimsky-Korsakov's Table C marks "very frequent" (PDF 43, image; written G5 and C6 are "little used"). A soft high entry is the hardest kind of horn note, and Hn 1 plays almost without stop from 147 to 170 (rests only at 153 and 163), including the fff climax 158–161 and the coda head 167–170.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 43 (Table C) and PDF 42 (the horn produces its tone "in a languid and lazy manner", little mobility); Adler adler1 PDF 303 (Ex. X-5 puts written G5–C6 in the "brilliant and loud" register; periodic rests welcome).
Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoHnOne): `R1 | c'4(\mp\> b4 d'4 f'4) |` (was `c''4 b'4 d''4 f'4`). The horn now doubles the cellos' octave for three notes and meets the violas on F4, then continues as before (`e'2 d'2` at 155). The violas keep the upper octave, so the horn + viola setting of 147–155 (cycle 131) is kept in substance. full.midi rebuilt; the full all-pairs voice-leading run (tools/critic-scripts/vl.py) differs from v205 in one line only, at 154 beat 2, an octave doubling of the same line (Hn 1 with Vln II lower divisi), not a new parallel.

### 5. Horns marked at the same level as the other brass in the wedge 111–116 (applied)
Bars 111–116: horns enter p cresc. at 111 with the tuba at p; trumpets, trombones and bass trombone enter p cresc. at 113 (cycle 143). MIDI velocities show the horns equal to the other brass in 111–114, the only such bars in the movement.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41: "To obtain an equal balance, therefore, the marks of expression in the horns should be one degree stronger than in the rest of the brass; if the trumpets and trombones play pp, the horns should be marked p."
Fix (dev.ily): `\pp\cresc` instead of `\p\cresc` at the entries of Tpt 1, 2, 3, Tbn 1, 2, B.tbn (113) and tuba (111). Horns stay p. This keeps cycle 143's entry order and its source (PDF 127: the second group enters p or pp and crescendos faster); the shared trumpet and trombone staves have equal dynamics.

### 6. Harp f at 162 under the ff tutti (not applied)
Bar 162: the harp's rolled A-minor chord, f, sounds on the same downbeat as the ff brass and woodwinds. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47: "At least three, if not four harps in unison are necessary, if they are to be heard against a full orchestra playing forte." The chord will not be heard until the brass have faded. The harp voicings 147–166 are protected (cycles 85, 136), so I only note it. If the composer wants the chord heard, the change is a delayed entry, `r4 <a'' c''' e'''>2.\arpeggio\mf` with the LH octave moved to beat 2.
