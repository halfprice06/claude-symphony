## Comparison with Glazunov 5/i

What Glazunov does, and how this movement compares:

1. Introduction. Glazunov opens Moderato maestoso with the main theme in a low wind and string unison, f, answered softly by the woodwind. The Allegro (page 6) is the same theme in diminution, ff. This movement opens pp with the motto in a low unison and links the motto to P through the rising sixth, not through diminution. Both are workable. This movement's plan is quieter and suits a four-movement cyclic work that ends attacca.
2. Second theme. At reh. 11-13 Glazunov gives the tune to solo flute and clarinet (dolce), then solo oboe, then violins cantabile. The harp's broken chords run through all three, with pizz. strings and sustained bassoons and horns below the tune (Rimsky principles 6 and 8). This movement's 47-63 follows the same method (Hn 1 + Vc over harp and pizz. basses, then violins an octave up, with the harmony below). The accompaniment here is as transparent as Glazunov's.
3. Development. At reh. 18 (page 27) Glazunov has a long soft passage: divided string tremolo p, trumpets and trombones "soli, p dolce" on the theme. This movement's development has no soft distant episode. Critics 459 and 559 raised this, and it was kept because the f start at 117 and the proportions are protected. I do not answer that reason, so I do not reopen it.
4. Climaxes. Glazunov builds each climax over eight to sixteen bars (reh. 33 Poco più mosso through reh. 37, pages 52-56: the theme in whole-bar brass notes under string figuration, in steps of p<f, f, ff). This movement reaches its recapitulation climax in two bars (156-157). That point has been open and protected since c523 (c533, c541-544, c553, c563). The Rimsky reading supports the arrival itself (principle 11: brass attack at 158). Nothing in the reading answers the protection's reason, so I leave it.
5. Ending. Glazunov ends ff with detached tutti chords (reh. 39, page 58). This movement ends ppp attacca. That ending is quoted by movement II and protected. It is the right ending for this symphony.

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? For the second-group scoring, the coda 171-182 and the climax layouts, yes. They are as clean as Glazunov's and more economical. A conductor would notice the two places noted above, the short climb at 156-157 and the lack of a soft development episode. Both are known, protected, and justified by the proportions.

## Findings

No change. I examined these candidates and rejected them:

1. **47-55, Vln II metà A-flat4 above the Hn 1/Vc tune.** I checked this against PDF 139, which says harmony in the solo's own register hurts it. Here the held A-flat4 lies above the tune's C4-B-flat3-A-flat3 at 47-48, not inside its sustained register. PDF 139 lists harmony "duplicated in the higher register" as one of the safe layouts, and the pad is pp metà under an mf/mp tune. Glazunov's reh. 13 also keeps the violins' sustained notes above the cello line. Keep.
2. **Unison pairs in soft accompaniments (PDF 139).** I scanned full.midi for two like instruments (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons, any two horns) holding the same pitch at a soft level for two beats or more. The only hits are single notes where the Hn 2 counterline meets the Hn 3 pad (59 D-flat4, 68 C4, 166). These are melodic meetings, not a doubled harmonic part. Keep.
3. **A rush of strings into 158 (PDF 141, Ex. 281, PDF 449).** This would strengthen the culmination as the reading allows. But Vln I's 157.4 (c511), the 157 viola arpeggio (c502) and the 156-157 bass (c553) are protected, and the arrival already has the brass attack that the same passage names. Not proposed.
4. **Soft brass episode in the development, after Glazunov reh. 18.** Blocked by the protected 117 f start and proportions (c459, c559), and by "127-130 no soft trumpets" (c467). Not proposed.
