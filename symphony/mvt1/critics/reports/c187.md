## What I studied

**Books.** Rimsky-Korsakov as above. Aldwell & Schachter Unit 25, PDF 420 (cross relations: keep them out of the top voice; harshest between outer voices).

**Corpus.** I compiled Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly`) to MIDI and ran the same doubling script I used on this score. It has 22 string–wind unison runs. It also has 13 octave runs in which neither part has a unison partner, and these are almost all sixteenth-note figuration (flute over Vln II in the development) or bassoon over the basses, which is Rimsky's low-register rule. Melodic lines get a unison partner. Rimsky's own Ex. 85 (*Ivan the Terrible*) shows the full layout: each octave of the line has a string group plus a wind.

**Web.** Project Gutenberg text of Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles* (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). It confirms the wording of the "only one is doubled" paragraph and of the register-matching list. The page was too long for the fetch tool to reach the editor's footnote, so I quote that note from the book scan.

**Tools.** A pairwise doubling script on full.midi (lists every run of five or more common onsets at a fixed interval, for all part pairs), and `vl.py` and `vl2.py` before and after my changes.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 94–95: the fugato countersubject is a string and a single clarinet an octave apart (applied)
- **Problem.** At the fourth entry the subject is in Fl 1 + Vln I in unison (F5), the register-matched pair of PDF 76. The countersubject is Vln II at B♭4 with Cl 1 alone an octave lower (`bes4. c'8 des'8 es'8 f'4 | des'2 ...`). The clarinet line starts on the B♭3 already held by Bsn 1 and Vc, and it ends on the viola's held F4. It doubles two held notes at the unison and adds only an inner shadow to the countersubject. It then leaps D♭4 → D♭5 into 96.
- **Evidence.** PDF 76–77: the best string–wind combinations are unisons of matching registers, Vns + Cl among them, and the wind "increases the resonance of the latter and amplifies its tone". PDF 78, editor's note: a string and a wind in octaves, neither doubled, "is not to be recommended, as the tone quality of the two groups is so widely different". Ex. 85 puts a wind on every octave of the line. In Tchaikovsky 6/i, isolated wind–string octaves appear only in figuration and bass doublings, not on thematic lines.
- **Fix (dev.ily, `devClOne`):** `bes'4.\f c''8 des''8( es''8) f''4 | des''2\<( es''8 des''8 c''8 des''8) |`. Cl 1 is now in unison with Vln II, so both fugato lines at 94 are string + wind unisons. Bar 96's `r8 des''8 bes''4.->` follows without the octave leap. Entry colours, countersubject notes and rhythm are unchanged. Cycle 107 named the countersubject colour at 95 as "Vln II + Cl 1", and it stays so.

### 2. Bar 162, beat 4½: Cl 1 G♮4 sounds against the tune's G♯ (applied)
- **Problem.** On the last eighth of 162 Cl 1's counterline `r8 e'8( f'8 g'8` sounds G♮4 while Vln II plays G♯4 and Vln I and Fl 1 play G♯5. G♮ and G♯ sound together in the same octave, and G♯ is in the top voice. The counterline is the exposition's (64: `r8 c'8( d'8 es'8`) moved up a third. In 64 the E♭ lies a third under the tune's G. In the recapitulation the tune's neighbour became G♯, but the counterline kept G♮.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 81: faulty part movement spoils resonance whatever the instruments. Aldwell & Schachter PDF 420: a chromatic conflict between voices should not involve the top voice. Here it is simultaneous, which is harsher than a cross relation. No script flags it because the scripts check parallels, not spelling.
- **Fix (recTwo.ily, `recTwoClOne`):** `e''2\ff\> r8 e'8( f'4~ | f'2) c''2 |` (was `r8 e'8( f'8 g'8 | f'2)`). The F arrives on beat 4 and is tied into 163's F major. Against E (Vc, Hn 1, Tpt 2), G♯ (violins) and B (Vla), the beat becomes a V♭9 colour of A minor. I tried E4 first, but it made new octaves with Bsn 1 and Hn 4 (E→F) into 163. The tied F adds no parallels.

### 3. Bars 162–163: the single wind doubles the upper octave of the tune, not the lower (tested; not applied in the main scratch copy)
- **Problem.** After the climax the tune runs in octaves: Vln I at A5, Vln II at A4. Fl 1 doubles Vln I, and the lower octave (Vln II) stands alone among the E4s of Hn 1, Tpt 2, Vla and Vc and Cl 1's counterline.
- **Evidence.** PDF 78: "It is necessary to pay more attention to cases where, of the two parts in octaves, only one is doubled. When this method is applied to a melody in the soprano register it is better to allow the wood-wind to progress in octaves, the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups." Ex. 90 (Scheherazade IV) reinforces the lower octave (cellos + 2 horns under 2 clarinets). PDF 76: Vns + Ob is a register-matched unison. Ob 1 is already on the lower octave at 158–161 and stops at 162 for no reason of its own.
- **Fix (recTwo.ily):** Fl 1 `f'''2 d'''4 b''8 c'''8) | \partCombineAutomatic R1*5 |`. Ob 1 `f''2 d''4 b'8 c''8) \breathe | a'2.~(\ff a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\>( g'8 f'2)\! | R1*3 |`. This also ends Fl 1's long span from 158 that cycle 186 had to break with a breath.
- **Protection.** The scoring of 158–166 is protected (cycle 179). The reason given there was diminuendo by drop-out (Adler 202, 215), and this change keeps it: one wind leaves at 162 and one at 164, as before. Tested in `/tmp/critic-187/work-f3`: 0 warnings, 18 pages, vl2 identical. It is optional and left to the composer.

### 4. No melody anywhere is set in thirds or sixths across strings and winds (no change here)
The doubling script finds only three runs of parallel thirds or sixths in the movement, all harmonic: Vln II/Vla at 16–18 and the trumpets at 28–32. Rimsky gives combined thirds and sixths as a standard resource (PDF 78, Ex. 88–89: Ob + Vln I a third over C.a. + Vln II in *Servilia*). Almost every melodic passage in movement I is protected, so I do not propose one here. It is a note for movements II/III (cyclic-notes.md): the Brahms/Dvořák thirds-and-sixths duet colour is so far unused.

### 5. Other wind–string octave pairs (not proposed)
Ob 1 and Cl 1 answer an octave above the violas at 37–39. At 91–93, C.a. lies an octave under Vln II's subject and Bsn 1 an octave under the viola's countersubject. These pairs fall under the PDF 78 editor's note. The note itself says the classics used such octaves "to obtain balance of tone". The C.a. cannot take Vln II's octave (A5). A bassoon in unison with the violas would have to go to C5 at f. The passages are protected as "transition hand-offs 34–41" and "fugato entry colours 85–96". I leave them as they are.
