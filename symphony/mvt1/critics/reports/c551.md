## Findings

### 1. The cor anglais at 171–174 sits in the thin band, not the mellow one (record correction; no change)

c461's table (its principle 13 and Finding 4) puts all three cor anglais solos in Adler's "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band. The scan shows that 171–174 is not in that band:

| Bar.beat | Sounding | Written | Beats | Band (Ex. VII-47) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 171.4 | G4 | D5 | 1 | mellow |
| 172.1 | E♭5 | B♭5 | 3.5 | thin, pinched |
| 172.4.5 | D5 | A5 | 1.5 | thin, pinched |
| 173.2 | C5 | G5 | 1 | top of mellow |
| 173.3 | D♭5 | A♭5 | 2 | edge of thin |
| 174.1 | C5 | G5 | 2 | top of mellow |

The longest note, the B♭5 written, is in the band where Adler says the cor anglais "sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality" (PDF 205). At 5–8 the same motto lies in written A4–F5, the band Adler praises. So the movement's last double-reed solo is less characteristic than its first one.

I looked for a fix:
- **The C.a. an octave lower** (sounding G3–C4, written D4–B♭4, the "deep, rich, intense" band of PDF 205). This puts the motto under the solo cello's S head (E♭4–C5). It crosses the cello at 172.1, meets it in unison on D♭4 at 173.3, and lies under the violas' E♭4–D4 at 174. Adler's first rule for a soloist is not to cover it (PDF 185, 226), and the motto would become an inner voice. It would also take the motto's low register one bar before the chorale's own low motto at 175–179. This is worse than what is there now.
- **Ob 1** (the same sounding pitches, in the oboe's best range F4–C6, PDF 198). This would give the oboe its first theme in the symphony. That is reserved for movement II (c461, Finding 3).
- **Cl 1** (written A4–F5, clarino). This breaks the motto colour plan (protected): the C.a. frames the movement at 5–8 and 171–174, and Cl 1 has its own return at 175.4.

**Recommendation: keep it as it is.** At p over a solo cello, two violas and one bassoon, the thin band carries, and the motto needs to be the top line. The record should be corrected, though. 171–174 lies in written D5–B♭5, and its long notes are on A5–B♭5 (Ex. VII-47's thin band), not in the mellow band. The protection of the C.a. octave at 171–174 stands for the covering reason above, not because of its register.

### 2. Cl 1's dolce link at 45–46 lies on the throat tones (no change)

Cl 1 plays B♭4–A♭4–G4–F4–E♭4 sounding (written C5–B♭4–A4–G4–F4), p dolce with a diminuendo, into the S pickup at 46.4. Three of its five notes are throat tones, which Ex. VII-56 calls "rather pale" (PDF 210). I asked whether the link would sing more an octave higher (written C6–F5, in the clarino).

No. This link lets the transition fade before Hn 1 and the cellos bring in S. A pale colour falling away suits that job. An octave higher would sound "bright, incisive" right before a mp dolce theme. It would also take away the effect of Cl 1's first clarino entry, which arrives on the S peak F5 at 52 (c461's master-level item). Adler also calls the clarinet the most homogeneous woodwind (PDF 210), so the throat notes are a shade of colour, not a weakness. The lead-in 43–47 is protected. Keep.

### 3. Peaks and dynamic limits (no change)

These are new scan results. c461's scan gave ranges; this one also gives the approach to each peak and its dynamic.
- **Flute.** A♭6 occurs at 31, 72, 122, 140 and 160, always f–fff and always doubled by Vln I or by the other flute. It is approached from F6 at 31 and 72, by scale at 122 and 140, and by the S head's rising sixth C6–A♭6 at 160. Nothing goes above A♭6, so the reading's caution about notes above A6 (PDF 186) is not reached.
- **Oboe.** Notes below F4 occur only at 117–120 (B3–E♭4), at f inside the trombone motto. Nothing is soft in the lowest fifth (PDF 198).
- **Bassoon.** All 35 notes in B♭1–F2 are p or louder: the softest are C2 at 110 and 166, at p. This follows PDF 226.

### 4. Where the music falls short of a master's, within this lens

On a fourth pass the question is whether the woodwinds help a listener remember the themes as much as they do in the reading's models. In Dvořák 9/i (Ex. VII-3) and Brahms 4/iv (Ex. VII-4) one woodwind takes a whole theme alone and makes it its own. In this movement the woodwinds own the motto (B.cl, C.a., flute, clarinet): five colours for one idea, which is the strongest woodwind writing in the score. They do not own P or S. P goes to Bsn 1 for four bars, and S goes to a woodwind only in fragments (Cl 1 at 52, Fl 1 at 109–110, C.a. answers at 147–153).

I checked whether S could take a full woodwind statement anywhere:
- At 47–55 and 56–63 the scoring of both settings is protected, and the cello+horn colour is the point of the first statement.
- At 147–155 the horn and violas carry S with the C.a. answering, and that plan is protected.
- At 107–110 the violas and Fl 1 already split it.

No place is left that is not protected for reasons this reading does not answer. Keeping the oboe solo for movement II is also a sound cyclic reason for restraint here. So the gap stays as c461 described it: S is nine bars and P eight, shorter than the second themes of Tchaikovsky 5/i or Dvořák 8/i. Proportion critics have protected that length repeatedly. This reading gives no grounds to reopen it.

**Result of this pass: no change.** No proposal met the bar of a tested, supportable improvement.
