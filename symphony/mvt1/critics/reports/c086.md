# Critic 086 — lens 6, playability (each wind, brass, timpani and harp part), v105

## What I studied
I built a per-player copy of v105 with no part-combining (one staff and MIDI track per player, same `global.ily` tempo map with the cycle-78 `\mt` steps). From it I measured each player's playing time, every stretch without a rest (in seconds at the MIDI tempi), and the highest and lowest notes. I also ran LilyPond's `event-listener.ly` to time every string slur. I compared the results with the MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i in the corpus (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`, `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`), using music21. Web sources: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. I (Gutenberg, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): "Wood-wind players cannot manage extremely long sustained passages … periodic rests" (p. 19), and the same "applies with equal force to the brass" (pp. 25–26). Also orchestrationonline.com, "Woodwinds: note durations and breathing" (https://orchestrationonline.com/woodwinds-note-durations-and-breathing/), which says the flute has the least capacity for long tones and that bass clarinet and contrabassoon use breath faster, and faster still when loud. Practitioner threads say tuba and bass trombone need the most frequent breaths (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/long-brass-sustains-and-how-to-break-them-up.80158/).

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 113–124, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, Tuba: 22–24 s of ff–fff with no rest (applied).** The augmented motto runs from the mp crescendo at 113 straight into the fff ♭VI at 122. It is tied across 118–119 and 120–121, so the only places to breathe are inside the motto line. Evidence: Tchaikovsky 6/i 285–297 (trombones `r2 r4` before each ff unit), 228–237 (Tbn 1 and 2 alternate a held B♭ so each one rests a bar), and Rimsky-Korsakov pp. 25–26. In this score Bsn 1, Hn 3 and Cl 2 already rest on beat 4 of 116. Fix (motto pitches and rhythm unchanged; this answers the cycle-13/66 protection on breath grounds only): Tbn1 `fis2. r4` (116) and `c'2. r4` (121); Tbn2 `aes,2 a,4 r4` (116) and `c'2. r4` (121); B.tbn `c,2. r4` (116) and `c2. r4` (121); Tuba `c,2. r4` (116). The strings and the Cb/Cbsn bass carry the line through both gaps. Longest stretch is now about 10 s.

**2. Bars 156–158, Hn 1, 3, 4: no breath before the K climax (applied).** Hn1 holds f' for 156–157 and then attacks the ff tune `g4 e'2.~` at 158. Hn3 jumps from A♭3 to G4 at ff on the downbeat. All three have no rest, while Hn2 already has `c'2. r4` at 157 (cycle 81). Fix: Hn1 `f'2. r4`, Hn3 `aes2. r4`, Hn4 `f2. r4` at 157. This lowers density and does not raise it (cycle 83 rule), matches Hn2's rhythm on the shared staff, and leaves the violins' approach 156–157 untouched. The hairpins still end at the ff of 158.

**3. Bars 61–76, Hn 3·4: the codetta forte at 69 starts with no breath (applied).** Hn3 plays 61–72 without a rest (25 s) and Hn4 plays 67–76 (20 s, ending in the pp A♭ pedal under the flute motto). Both go from the p chord of 68 straight into a f downbeat at 69, while Hn1·2 have an eighth rest there. Fix: `c'2.\p r4` (Hn3) and `aes2.\p r4` (Hn4) at 68, during the rit.

**4. Bars 97–106, Hn 4: ten bars without a rest; the shared staff has unequal rhythms at 102 (applied).** Hn3 has `des'2 c'4 r4` at 102 and Hn4 has `bes2 a2`. Fix: Hn4 `bes2 a4 r4` (inside the `\transpose c cis` block). This gives Hn4 a breath before the new mp entry at 103 and equal rhythm with Hn3.

**5. Bars 97–106, B.cl: 19 s of ff–f low register with no rest (applied).** In 97–102 the bass clarinet doubles Bsn 2 at the unison, and Bsn 2 already breathes at 100 (`c2. r4`). Bass clarinet uses breath fastest (orchestrationonline). Fix: B.cl `c2. r4` at 100, the same as Bsn 2.

**6. Bars 115–123, Fl 2: nine whole notes f→fff, 18 s with no rest (applied).** The flute has the least breath capacity (orchestrationonline; Rimsky-Korsakov p. 19). Fl 1 has eighth rests in every bar from 117. Fix: Fl2 `f''2. r4` at 119. The other half of the stretch, 120–123, is about 9 s.

**7. Bars 109–119, Bsn 2: 20 s in the low register, ff from 117 (applied).** The bass clarinet, which plays the same chromatic line an octave higher, already rests at 113. Fix: Bsn2 `es,2. r4` at 113. The Cbsn and Cb keep the bass line going.

**8. Bars 162–170, Cl 1: 18 s with no rest, from the counterline into the f wind chord at 167 (not applied).** The only place to rest is the E♭ of 166, which is protected (E♮→E♭ turn). Suggestion for the composer: Cl1 `es'4\< r4` at 166 beat 3, or let Cl1 enter at 167 on `r8 g''8~ g''2.`. It is low priority, because 163–166 are soft and a clarinet can sneak a breath.
