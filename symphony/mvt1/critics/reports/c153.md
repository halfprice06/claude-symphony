## What I studied

Besides the reading, I studied the v172 recapitulation and coda (131–182) in the sources and on PDF pages 12–17, and compared them with the exposition (15–76) bar by bar. For the corpus I used Beethoven 9/i, the work A&S cite on PDF 454 for this movement's own I–VI ‖ IV–V | I plan. I read the MCTL annotations in `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1/form/` (form_analysis.csv, harmony.csv, cadence.csv; onsets in quarter notes in 2/4, so bar = onset/2 + 1) and looked at bars 326–331 in `pdf/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.pdf` (page 58). I did not use the web.

Overall: measured against the reading, the recapitulation's tonal plan and proportions are correct. In row 22 the book says the return to the tonic belongs to the development, so the recapitulation has no modulating bridge. That is why 143–146 can be four bars where the exposition's 33–46 needed fourteen. Beethoven 9/i does the same thing. Its recapitulation first group and transition (MCTL onsets 628–688) are about 30 bars against roughly 80 in the exposition. Its second group returns in the tonic major (onset 688, bar 345, "D: I") before turning to minor (bar 351). Earlier form critics protected the proportions, and the reading gives no reason to reopen them.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 138: the recapitulation's stop chord should keep its D bass (no change; answers the question left open by cycle 152)

**Question.** Cycle 152 changed the bass of the stop chord at 26 from D to B♮, so that vii°7 rises B→C into the tonic at 27. It left 138 on D. Should 138 now match?

**Answer: no.** The two stop chords lead to different places, and each bass should resolve into what follows it.

- At 26→27 the stop chord goes to the tonic tutti (i). A bass leading tone that rises to C is right there.
- At 138→139 the stop chord goes to iv: the bass is F at 139, then D♭ at 140, F–D at 141 and G at 142, and the cadence comes at 143. In the recapitulation the tonic tutti has already come at 131, so 139–142 is the predominant drive to the cadence. The book describes exactly this route: the V that leads to I "is often preceded by a II or IV" (PDF 456 §15). The recapitulation's cadence must include "some form of II or IV as well as V" (PDF 456, Point 2). A B♮ bass at 138 would have to move B→F, a tritone after the rest, and would leave the leading tone unresolved. With D, the bass moves D→F (a third, inside iv's approach) and then on to G at 142.
- The approach is also sound. The bass at 137 beat 4 is A♭, and A♭→D lies inside the diminished-seventh arpeggio. That is where the book allows augmented intervals (PDF 380 §11, the cycle-152 citation).
- Form: A&S PDF 220 §11 says form comes from "the relationship of part to part". The recapitulation turns the same gesture (hemiola, then stop chord) toward the subdominant instead of the tonic. That is a recapitulation change, not an inconsistency. Beethoven 9/i does the same in its reprise. In the MCTL harmony.csv, onsets 638–664 (bars 320–333) read V/iv, then vii°7 (bar 327), then iv (bar 328): a diminished seventh moving to iv in the recapitulation's first group.

The three stop chords now differ, and each bass fits what comes next. At 26 the bass is B, which goes to C (i). At 138 it is D, which goes to F (iv). At 170 the chord sits over the C pedal and goes to iv7 at 171. **Recommendation:** record in HISTORY that 138 keeps D on purpose, so that a later critic does not "match" it to 26. The cycle-134 protection of "stop chords and silences at 26, 138, 170" still holds (same rhythm, silence and scoring, different bass).

### 2. Bars 145–147: the leading tone drops out one bar before the C-major arrival (applied)

**Problem.** 144 is a cadential 6/4 over G, and 145 is V7 (G–B–D–F) at pp. The third, B, is in Vc (B2), Bsn 1 and Hn 3 (B3), and all three stop at the end of 145. Bar 146 therefore sounds G–D–F, with the oboe's dolce E–D on top. This is a dominant seventh without its leading tone, and no part takes B to C at 147. At 147 the C-major chord has C only in the bass (Bsn 1, Cb pizz.); above it are the horn/viola/cello E and Vln II E5–G5.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 219–220 §10: when V returns to its "permanent and basic function", the V7 "has so strong a drive toward I that the listener is prepared for the return of the tonic". PDF 456 §15: the returning V "supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly to I". Here the 7th (Vln II F4) is kept through 146, but the leading tone, which carries most of that drive, is gone. The arrival at 147 is a phrase-modulation-like change of mode and texture (PDF 223), which makes the 7̂–1̂ link more necessary, not less. The exposition's equivalent seam (45–47) is a different, protected progression, so the recapitulation cannot borrow its solution.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-153/work).** Hn 3 holds B3 through 146 and resolves it to C4, pp, at 147. Hn 4 rests in 146–147, so the shared staff has one player and one dynamic.
```
recOne.ily, recOneHnThree bars 143–146:   R1*2 | b1\pp~ | b1 |        (was R1*2 | b1\pp | R1 |)
recTwo.ily, recTwoHnThree bars 147–155:   c'2 r2 | R1*8 |             (was R1*9 |)
```
This adds a soft middle C to the C-major downbeat and leaves the oboe's major-sixth motto, the horn/viola/cello theme, and the Vln II sul tasto chord untouched. The only cost is a pp horn note under the oboe solo in 146. Horn 3 rests at 143–144 and again from 148 to 155, so endurance is not affected.

## Checked and left alone (reading verdicts, no action)

- 117–130: the prolonged dominant with ♭VI as upper neighbour (122) and the 7th at 130 match PDF 456 §15 exactly.
- 139–143: iv–N–V–i is the cadence "with II or IV as well as V" (PDF 456).
- 147–166: the tonic-major second group is a 20-bar key area. Its two deceptive cadences (159→160, 161→162) extend the phrase before the PAC 165→166 with melody D–C. This is the Schubert procedure of PDF 219 §9 and the 2̂–1̂ cadence of PDF 226.
- 167–182: plagal expansion I–IV–I (PDF 456 §16). 171–174 restates the second-theme cadence in minor, which is the "varied repetition of the final cadence" that PDF 220 §11 assigns to a coda.
- Proportions: the exposition is 62 bars, the recapitulation 36 and the coda 16. The reading (PDF 225–226) and Beethoven 9/i both support a recapitulation without a modulating bridge. Earlier form critics (cycles 13, 23) protected the proportions, and I found no reason to reopen them.
