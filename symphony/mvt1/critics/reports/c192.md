## What I studied

- **Books.** A&S Unit 27 (above). I also used A&S Unit 21 §12, which Unit 27 cites on PDF 469: a suspension is weaker when its resolution tone is anticipated. HISTORY shows the composer already applied this at bar 32 in cycle 122 ("suspensions not doubled by their resolution at 32, 141–142 and 54"), and at bar 28 in cycle 139 for the violas' A♭ appoggiatura.
- **Score.** I rebuilt full.midi and wrote detectors for every V7 with an added 9th, every V7 with a 4th or 6th (and whether it lies above or below the 7th), and every suspension of at least half a beat whose resolution pitch already sounds in another upper part. I checked each hit bar by bar (scripts in /tmp/critic-192/scripts).
- **Corpus.** I counted 4–3 suspensions of at least one beat over a held bass in the MusicXML scores, and checked whether the 3rd already sounds in another upper part. Beethoven 9/i: 0 of 6. Dvořák 9/i: 0 of 1. Tchaikovsky 6/i: 10 of 49, mostly in triplet figuration (offsets 1330.67, 1350.67).
- **Web.** Andy Brick, theory notes (https://personal.stevens.edu/~abrick/theory3/theory3_notes_03.html) says it is "usually best to avoid having the tone of resolution in another voice before the suspension resolves". Iowa State, *Comprehensive Musicianship* 10.3 (https://iastate.pressbooks.pub/comprehensivemusicianship/?p=381) says doubling the resolution "muddies the sound of the suspension". Robert Kelley, *Dissonance Treatment in Three or More Voices* (https://robertkelleyphd.com/home/diss34vc.htm).

## Findings

### 1. Bar 28: the leading tone sounds under the tune's suspended C (applied)

**Problem.** On the downbeat of 28 the tutti tune strikes C in four octaves (Fl 1/Vln I C6; Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II C5; Vla C4). The C is a 4th over the dominant and resolves to B on beat 2. Hn 3 and Tbn 1 already hold B3 on the downbeat, a semitone under the violas' C4, at ff. The result is an inverted V(♭9) (D bass, A♭ in Fl 2) with the suspension and its resolution sounding together.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 469: inverted ninth formations "are not very frequent, owing to the simultaneous presence of a dissonant suspension in the soprano and the tone to which it will resolve"; "suspensions are most effective if the tone of resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass". The Chopin example (27-10) works only because of very wide spacing. Bar 28 has the narrowest spacing possible, a minor second (C4/B3).
- A&S PDF 471: "the 5th, 7th, and 9th above V seem to form a II chord, especially when the leading tone ... does not sound" (Ex. 27-14, 27-21). With B removed from beat 1, the downbeat is iiø7 (D F A♭ C) over the dominant's G, and it moves into V(♭9) on beat 2.
- The score's own bar 32 already does this: Hn 3 and Tbn 1 move C4→B3 with the tune (cycle 122). Bar 28 is the one place in the tutti where this was not done.
- Corpus: no Beethoven 9/i or Dvořák 9/i 4–3 of a beat or more has the 3rd sounding in an upper part.

**Fix (tOne.ily, applied).**
- Hn 3: `\clef treble c'1\f~ | c'4 b2. |` (was `c'1\f | b1 |`).
- Tbn 1: `c'1\f~ | c'4 b2. |` (was `c'1\f | b1 |`).

The C is now prepared in bar 27 and resolves with the tune, as at 32. The all-pairs check shows only octave doublings of the tune's C→B at 28.2, the same kind already listed at 32.2. There are no new 5ths. The engraving is clean: Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 split into two voices for the bar.

**Protection.** The protections on 27–32 cover the bass line, the tutti layout and the brass dynamic grading. None of these changes. Cycle 139 already edited inner notes at 28 for the same reason (Hn 4).

### 2. Bars 177–178: the 7th of the final dominant rises in both voices that hold it (applied; reopens a protected passage)

**Problem.** At 177 the chorale reaches iiø7 and then vii°7 over D (Cl 1 C→B). On beat 4 Vln I adds G, so the sound is an inverted V♭9. Going into the tonic at 178:
- the 7th F rises to G in both Vln II (F4→G4) and Tbn 2 (F3→G3);
- the 9th A♭ leaps up to E♭ in the violas;
- the B♮ in Cl 1 stops without resolving (on purpose; cycle 120).

The only voice that resolves is the bass. The E♭ of 178 arrives only by leap (Vln I's motto G4→E♭5 and the violas). The cadential tonic before the Neapolitan therefore sounds as an open fifth C–G–C in the trombones, without a real resolution.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 466: "if the 9th accompanies a 7th that does resolve, composers will frequently omit the resolution of the 9th. The converse ... is not true; unresolved 7ths occur most infrequently, whether or not they accompany 9ths."
- A&S PDF 467: a 9th can be left in the air because its tone of resolution is a common tone. A 7th has no such cover.
- Ex. 27-6a shows the accepted way out: the 7th is moved to another voice where it resolves. Nothing does that at 178.

**Fix (coda.ily, applied).**
- Vln II at 178: `es'2 f'2` (was `g'2 f'2`). The F falls to E♭ in parallel 10ths with the bass D→C, which is the resolution on PDF 463.
- Vla at 178: `c'2 aes2` (was `es'2 aes2`). This avoids a tripled 3rd: C is doubled, E♭ is in the two violins, G is in Tbn 2. The A♭ is still left unresolved, which A&S allows once the 7th resolves.

Moving the viola to G3 would resolve the 9th as well, but it makes a d5→P5 against the bass (D–A♭ to C–G), the motion removed elsewhere in cycle 162. I did not do that.

Both lines stay stepwise or near it: Vln II E♭–G–F–E♭–F–G, Vla C–C–A♭–C–A♭–E♭. The motto, the bass, the B♮, the trombones, the dynamics and the slurs are unchanged, and so is bar 178 beats 3–4 (the N6). The all-pairs check shows no new parallels.

**Protection.** "Chorale 175–179 notes" is protected (cycles 120, 140, 188) as master-level material, with no specific reason given for these two inner notes. The reading gives the reason to change them. If the composer prefers the bare fifth at 178.1 on purpose, the smaller alternative is the Vln II change alone (`es'2 f'2`). The viola change only adjusts doubling.

### Checked against the reading, no change

- **83 and 85.** V♭9 with the A♭ on top, approached as a common tone from ♭VI (PDF 465, Ex. 27-4c), then left over a bare G. The fugato subject at 85 then descends B♭–A–A♭–G, which is the later stepwise answer A&S describes on PDF 467. Keep.
- **122.** B°7 over the G pedal at fff, with A♭ on top resolving to G at 123. This is the textbook case on PDF 470–471 ("adding V in the bass below a leading-tone seventh chord", Schumann Ex. 27-12). Keep.
- **145–146.** E5 on top of G7 (Ob 1), a 13th that resolves 6–5 (Ex. 27-16b) in the soprano. The 5th sounds below it, as in Chopin Ex. 27-18. Keep.
- **59, 63, 101, 159, 164.** Every 4th or 6th over V7 that is held for a beat or more sits above the 7th, in the top voice, and resolves (PDF 475). Keep.
- **14, 50, 150.** The inner 4ths below the 7th are prepared 4–3 suspensions that resolve. Their resolution is not anticipated, so the "mess" warning on PDF 475, which concerns unresolved tones, does not apply. Keep.
- **The development.** It has no descending-5ths chain of applied V9s (PDF 468–469). I do not propose adding one: it would add development material, which the cycle 73/83 rules forbid.
