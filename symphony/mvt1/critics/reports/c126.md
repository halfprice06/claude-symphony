## Findings

### 1. Double basses 117–130 and 179–182: long ties with no bow-change instruction (applied)

**Bars:** 117–130 (dev.ily `devCb`), 179–182 (coda.ily `codaCb`).

**Problem:** The basses hold G1 tied through 14 bars, 117 to 130, about 30 seconds at the tempo map's 126–100–126. The note passes ff, a crescendo, fff at 122, a diminuendo to pp at 126, then mf and f. In the coda they hold C2 tied through four bars at Tempo I (crotchet 50, with the final bar at 30 in the MIDI map) and then a fermata, which is 25 seconds or more at pp to ppp. Nothing in the score tells the players how to sustain these notes. The cellos above have measured sixteenths or tremolo at 117–121 and separate bows in 179–180, so the basses are the only strings with a written-out sustain of this length.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 108 (ch. 3, "Bowing"): the bass bow "is thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." Both passages are exactly this case. At 117–125 the bass is the ff/fff floor under the tam-tam stroke, so audible section-wide bow changes would leave gaps in the pedal. At 179–182 an audible change in the bottom voice at ppp is just as exposed.

**Fix (applied):**
```
% dev.ily, devCb bar 117
g,,1\ff~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" |
% coda.ily, codaCb bar 179
c,1~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" |
```
Optional and not applied: the same words on Vc, Vln I and Vln II at 181, where those parts tie into the fermata. A cello or violin bow can hold about 15 seconds at ppp, so this is less urgent than for the basses.

### 2. Arco→pizzicato with no time: Cb 46→47 and Vc 61→62 (applied)

**Bars:** Cb 46 (trans.ily `transCb`) into the pizz. at 47; Vc 61 (tTwo.ily `tTwoVc`) into the sub. p pizz. at 62.

**Problem:** At 46 the basses play arco half notes D♭2–E♭2 up to the barline and must pluck on the downbeat of 47 (pp, start of the second theme). At 61 the cellos play two slurred eighth-note groups up to the barline and must pluck sub. p on the downbeat of 62. Neither change has a rest. As the slurs fall from 56 on (one bow per slur), the cellos' last group in 61 comes on a down-bow, which leaves the hand at the tip just before the pizz. Cycle 116 fixed the same problem for the basses at 84 by adding a rest.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54 (ch. 2, support): "for the normal pizzicato, some short time must be allowed ... There are instances ... when no time is provided ... but these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible ... it is much easier to change from arco to pizzicato, if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog." Adler PDF 86 (ch. 3) confirms that the same pizzicato practice applies to all the bowed strings. Adler's remedy changes no notes, which matters here because 56–68 is protected accompaniment.

**Fix (applied):**
```
% trans.ily, transCb bar 46
c,1 | des,2 es,2\upbow |
% tTwo.ily, tTwoVc bar 61
fes,8( des fes aes) fes,8(\upbow aes, des fes) | es,4\subP^"pizz." ...
```
If the composer prefers Adler's first remedy (time), the alternative is `fes,8(\upbow aes, des) r8` at 61. That drops the F♭3 that closes the arpeggio, so I did not apply it.

### 3. Violins II bar 55: "tutti" entry on a double stop (applied)

**Bars:** 55 (tTwo.ily `tTwoVlnTwo`).

**Problem:** Half the section (metà) plays 47–54. At 55 the whole section is marked "tutti" on the dyad E♭4/A♭4 for a half note, then takes the p pickup into the violins' tune. As written, every player, including the half that has been silent for eight bars, enters on a double-stopped fourth (E♭4 stopped high on the G string, A♭4 on the D string) at pp and then has to play the cantabile pickup in tune. The two notes are plainly two parts of the chord the violas and harp are sustaining.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 76: "in orchestral writing, only the most easily accessible double-stops are usually used, particularly for secco chordal effects or especially sonorous passages. Since there are at least sixteen first violins and fourteen second violins ... passages that would be quite difficult for one performer are quite simple played divisi." This dyad is neither secco nor sonorous; it is a soft chord tone, the case where Adler expects divisi. The violas' identical situation in 47–68 is already marked div./unis. (cycle 116).

**Fix (applied):**
```
<es' aes'>2^\markup \italic "tutti, div." r4
\once \override TextScript.self-alignment-X = #RIGHT
es'4(\p^\markup \right-column { \italic "molto cantabile" "unis." } | ...
```
It prints cleanly above the staff, with no collision (checked on PDF page 5).

### Checked and found sound (no change)

- Ranges (Adler PDF 72, 85, 96, 104): every string part lies inside Adler's practical orchestral range. The violins' A♭6 stays below the E7 limit and at the Classical A6 limit. No bass note lies below E1, so no C extension is needed.
- Double stops (PDF 76, 86, 97, 107): the ff stop chords at 26, 83 and 138 are fifths, sixths and tritones on adjacent strings. The violas' repeated thirds at 131–134 use standard 3-1 and 4-2 fingerings. All other dyads are marked div., and every div. has a matching unis.
- Skips (PDF 82–83): the slurred high violin leaps on the head (27, 29, 139) and the cello's octave at 52 are single shifts of a sixth to an octave, each doubled (Vln II, Cl 1). That is ordinary section technique, not the "extreme range" skips Adler warns about.
- Bass articulation (PDF 105): the score already keeps the basses on sustained or repeated notes under the cellos' arpeggios at 56–64 and 158–166. This is Adler's Beethoven 4 remedy (Ex. III-69), so no change is needed.
- Viola alto clef at 109–110 and 151–153 (PDF 85, "sometimes" treble clef): at most four ledger lines, for a few notes. Cycle 115 decided this and I do not reopen it.
- For movements II/III, not this movement: the viola C string, which Adler (PDF 86) calls the instrument's most characteristic string, is never used (the violas' lowest note is G3). A dark viola pedal or solo on the C string is an unused colour.
