## Findings

This lens gives the form critic the motivic view of key areas (rows 12, 15, 16, 17) and three concrete models of large plans: the Appassionata exposition and development (PDF 596, 599–600), Tchaikovsky 4/i (PDF 600) and K. 542 (PDF 601–602). I measured every section against them and against Tchaikovsky 6/i. The result is that I recommend no larger structural change. The findings below give the evidence for each section, ranked by how close it comes to needing one.

### 1. Section lengths and the tension curve: no section is too long or too short for its material (no change)

- **Exposition.** The two halves balance: P + transition take 61.2 s, and S + codetta take 62.6 s.
- **Development.** At 0.85 of the exposition's time, it is close to Tchaikovsky 6/i. MCTL gives his development, including the P return, 638–1214 crotchets against an exposition of 72–638, a ratio of 1.02.
- **Recapitulation.** 0.61 of the exposition's time. The second group is 0.67 of 47–76 in bars. Tchaikovsky keeps only his S (1214–1334 = 30 bars) against an S group of 350–638 (72 bars), a ratio of 0.42. Dvořák and Brahms recapitulate more (c083). The score lies inside that range.
- **Tension curve.** The per-bar data show one wave per section. In the development there are three waves: 82, then 96–102, then 117–122. This is the same number of waves as Beethoven 5/i, whose development also ends in a soft dissolution before a sudden ff return (c239).

I checked each section for padding and for material cut short:
- **Transition 33–46.** It has a solo restart, an imitative build and a medial caesura with a fill. All 14 bars are used.
- **Codetta 69–76.** It has four f bars, then four bars of the flute motto over A♭. A&S PDF 596 (Ex. 32-15) is the reason for those last four bars: the major key is inflected to minor at the end of the exposition so that its expansion continues into the development. Without 73–76 the A♭ minor at 77 would be a new key, not a continuation.
- **103–110.** This is the softest stretch of the development: three keys in four bars at p (107–110). It has been protected (c074, c084) and the joins verified (c072). It is the valley that gives the wedge 111–116 a level to climb from, which is the function c093 measured.

None of the nine sections is longer than its material carries or shorter than its function needs.

### 2. The development's opening cycle 77–83 is a compressed Appassionata plan, and the compression is right (no change)

The score's development opens exactly as A&S Ex. 32-19 (PDF 599–600) does: the exposition's A♭, now minor, becomes G♯ under E (77→78, the common tone G♯5 in Cl 1), E goes to C minor, and the major thirds return to A♭ major (82). Beethoven takes 42 bars for this cycle (67–109), and the book says that in such passages "each intermediate chord is expanded". The score takes six bars, one or two per area.

I asked whether 77–83 is too short for that material. It is not.
- Beethoven's cycle is the body of his development and leads to its first main goal (D♭ = the enlarged 5–♭6–5 motive, PDF 600). In the score the cycle is an introduction. Its goal is the first A♭ climax at 82, and that climax enlarges the same 5–♭6–5 motive (A♭→G at 82→83, row 16).
- The body of the score's development is the fugato and what follows it. The cycle takes 15.3 s of the development's 105 s (15 %).
- Expanding each area would mean adding development bars. That breaks c073's rule, and the reading gives no reason to answer it: the plan's direction already reads, because each area is marked by a new instrument entering with the head.

The protection of 77–83 (c179, c182) stands.

### 3. The recapitulation's changes match the reading's models; the ♭VI at 160 is shorter than K. 542's but correct for this movement (no change)

The recapitulation makes three structural changes, and each follows a principle of this unit.

- **138→139: the stop chord resolves to iv.** The stop chord B–D–F–A♭ (bass D) goes to F minor, not to the tonic tutti of 27. A&S PDF 589: a °7 used as an applied chord "need not" resolve to a tonic. The same chord thus opens the subdominant region of the recapitulation (139 iv – 140 Neapolitan over D♭ – 142 V/V – 143 i – 144–146 V9 with E♮). This is the strongest change in the recapitulation, and it is made from the movement's own sonority, which is what PDF 600–602 asks for.
- **147: S in the tonic major.** A&S PDF 596: in minor, the major tonic is the normal expansion. It is confirmed only softly (154→155, 165→166), and that is protected for the finale (c240).
- **159→160: the deceptive cadence on ♭VI.** This is the K. 542 move (PDF 601–602, Ex. 32-21): ♭VI stops the second theme's resolution to I. Mozart then keeps the tension. He inflects ♭VI to minor, writes a transition, and ends with a strong authentic cadence. The score keeps the tension for two bars (161 fff, 162 ff on vi, a second deceptive move), then dissolves at 163–166 and cadences at p.

I weighed whether the K. 542 plan argues for a longer ♭VI region or a forte cadence. It does not.
- A forte C-major cadence is held back for the finale's 222→223 (coherence/mvt1.md, c240). A&S PDF 592 says the conflicts of a chromatic area "must somehow" be worked out in the composition. In this symphony the place where they are worked out is movement IV.
- A longer ♭VI would make 160 a key area, as at 47–76. That would repeat the exposition's key instead of recalling it as a climax chord.

The three A♭ climaxes (82, 122, 160) each enlarge G–A♭–G and each return to G. That is the Appassionata's 5–♭6–5 principle on the scale of the whole movement (PDF 600).

### 4. The development's climax plan 111–131 is the Schubert 9/iv approach in the tonic (no change)

The development's climax is 122: A♭ fff over the G pedal, with tam-tam. The pedal then thins: the collapse is 123–126, and at 127–130 the cellos and basses repeat G while the motto (G–E♭–C) enters in stretto. Then 131 is ff tutti. A&S PDF 587 (Ex. 32-4a) describes this texture in the Schubert: the V pedal "thins down to an octave G, against which a passing F moves to an E♭", and then "the full orchestra enters fortissimo".

The score keeps Schubert's preparation and resolves to the expected tonic. The ear has heard the motto's E♭ over G since bar 2, so the E♭ in 127–130 is a motivic common tone, not passing figuration. One could ask whether the development's peak should be the recapitulation itself, as in Brahms 1/i. I found the plan sound because:
- the cellos' written accelerando (crotchets, quavers, triplets, semiquavers, 127–130) carries the four-bar rebuild;
- the recapitulation enters with the P head at ff, not at the development's fff, so the climax hierarchy is not disturbed;
- 117–130 is protected (c142, c182, c239).

### 5. The single larger change I would make, if any: none. What I considered and why I rejected each

The focus asked for at most one larger structural change, tested. I considered four and rejected all four, so there was nothing to build beyond the unchanged scratch copy.

- **(a) Lengthening the two-bar approach 156–157 to the main climax.**
  - Answered twice (c083 finding 1, c143). The exposition's own peak (62–63 → 64) uses the same two-bar "poco animando". The reading's K. 542 model is about what follows the ♭VI, not about the approach to it.
  - Adding bars would renumber 158–182. 35 lines of the other movements' coherence files carry numbers between 110 and 189 (26 in finale.md, 8 in scherzo.md, 1 in mvt2.md), and every one that cites a movement I bar above 157 would have to be checked and changed and risks an 18th PDF page (c105).
- **(b) Cutting 103–110.** Without it, the B-minor ff block (97–102) would run straight into the wedge at mp. The trough would only move. The cut would also remove the movement's one combination of the two heads in minor (cycle 70, kept for movement III's major version), and renumber 111–182.
- **(c) Running the development's climax straight into the recapitulation (no collapse at 123–126).** Finding 4 and Ex. 32-4 support the present plan. Beethoven 5/i (c239) uses the same climax–dissolution–sudden-return order.
- **(d) Lengthening the forte coda 167–170.** Its four bars are an interruption, not a section. The soft epilogue that follows already makes the coda 13.6 % of the time, the one proportion above the models (c083). Adding forte bars would add time in the wrong place and weaken the 160 climax.

Of these, (a) is the only one I think a conductor might notice. The evidence for changing it is a matter of taste, not of the reading or the models.
