## Findings

The brass and combined-group writing follows this chapter closely; three passes have already fixed the clear breaches. Two places do not, and both were made by recent revisions that fixed a different point: c317 (Tbn 2 doubles the tenor line, 139–142) and c374 (Tbn 1 takes the leading tone at 159). Neither change removes what those cycles wanted.

### 1. Bar 142: both upper trombones play the same C4–B3 (applied)

What is wrong. Bar 142 is the accented C–B 4–3 over G, the hammer stroke that rhymes with 32. In the brass, C4–B3 sounds in Tbn 1, Tbn 2, Tpt 3 and Hn 3 together: two trombones, a trumpet and a horn on one line. The fifth D sounds in the brass only in Tpt 2 and Hn 2 (D4). The trombones are on G2 (bass trombone) and two unison C4s, so the trombone section holds two notes, not three. At the rhyme bar 32 the layout is Tbn 1 C4–B3 over Tbn 2 G3, with Tpt 3 on D4 (tOne.ily, tOneTbnTwo `g4-> g4->`). The unison came in with c317, which put Tbn 2 on the cello/Bsn 1 tenor line for 139–142. That line ends C4–B3 at 142, which Tbn 1 already played.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 101 (diagram): "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts are generally allotted to the two remaining trombones reinforced by a trumpet or two horns in unison, so as to obtain a perfect balance of tone". The two upper trombones take two of the three upper parts, not one. PDF 104: trumpets and trombones in unison are "not so common, as this unites the two most powerful agents"; here three of them share the note. PDF 100: no wasted doubling in close order. PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords and chords of rhythmic design, and 142 is two accented half notes at f into the ff stroke.

Fix (recOne.ily, recOneTbnTwo, bars 141–142):
```
c'2.\f( bes4) | aes2( f2~ | f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2-> b2->) | g8->\ff ...   % before
c'2.\f( bes4) | aes2( f2~ | f4) g4( aes2) | g2-> g2-> | g8->\ff ...          % after
```
Tbn 2 keeps the tenor line through 141.3 (C4–B♭3–A♭3–F3–G3–A♭3). It holds A♭3 on 141.4 and falls to G3 under the C–B strokes, which is its own pre-c317 note and the layout of bar 32. The line's last two notes, C4–B3, are still played by a trombone at the same pitch (Tbn 1), so c317's aim, a trombone carrying the line in its best register (Adler adler2 PDF 42, 44), still holds; Bsn 1 and the cellos double it too. Pitch classes, harmony, accents and dynamics are unchanged. The protections that touch this bar are kept: c118/c288/c388's accents on every C–B doubling (Tbn 2 is no longer a doubling of C–B; its G3 strokes carry accents with the others); c317's Tbn 2 line (it ends one beat earlier); c354's held brass at f. vl.txt: the new lines are octave doublings of the A♭→G line (Tbn 2 with Hn 1, Tpt 1, Cl 2 at 142.1) and of the tenor line at 141.3. Twelve octave-doubling lines at 142.3 drop out. No fifths. Rank: the more audible of the two. 142 is a held ff-level hammer bar in which the trombone section loses a part.

### 2. Bar 159, beats 3–4: Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 double the leading tone B3 in unison (applied)

What is wrong. At 159 the brass hold V7 of C under the climbing tune into the fff ♭VI at 160. Since c374, Tbn 1 plays D4 on beats 1–2 and B3 on beats 3–4, resolving to C4 at 160. Tpt 3 holds B3 for the whole bar. So on 159.3–4 the leading tone sounds at the same pitch in a trumpet and a trombone at ff: the two strongest brass in unison on the one note that may not be doubled. c169 had removed exactly this unison ("Tbn 1 D4 at 159 ... the leading tone is no longer doubled at pitch"). c374 brought it back by giving Tbn 1 B3 on beats 3–4, because once Ob 1, Cl 1 and Hn 3·4 leave B for F, the only B in the brass was Tpt 3's weak low B3. Tpt 3 then leaps a diminished fourth B3→E♭4 into 160, the doubled leading tone resolving outward (c332 kept that leap only as a doubled leading tone "resolved elsewhere").

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 104: trumpet–trombone unison "unites the two most powerful agents in the group". PDF 101: with three trumpets each trumpet takes its own part. PDF 102: in five- to seven-part harmony, notes are left out rather than doubled thinly. PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords. A&S (cited by c169 and c332) on not doubling the leading tone in a V7.

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoTptThree, bar 159):
```
R1*11 | c'1\ff | b1 \breathe | es'1\ff |    % before
R1*11 | c'1\ff | b2 r2 | es'1\ff |          % after
```
Tpt 3 leaves B3 on beat 3, at the moment Tbn 1 takes the same B3, so the leading tone passes from trumpet to trombone at pitch and never sounds in both. Tpt 3 then breathes (the old breath mark falls inside the rest and is dropped) and strikes E♭4 fresh at the fff 160. That is the trumpet's own entry on the climax chord, and the diminished-fourth leap disappears. This keeps c374's aim: the B on 159.3–4 is in Tbn 1, which is stronger in this register than a low trumpet (c374's own reason, Adler adler2 PDF 15). It also restores c169's aim (no leading tone doubled at pitch) and keeps Tbn 1's B3→C4 into 160 that mvt1.md records.

Two alternatives were tested and rejected. Tpt 3 D4 on 159.3–4 makes parallel fifths D→E♭ against the bass G→A♭ (Bsn 1, B.tbn, tuba, Cb). Tpt 3 F4 on 159.3–4 needs a tritone leap B3–F4 at ff and adds a third brass to the seventh. The rest is the smallest change that removes the unison. vl.txt: no change. Rank: smaller than finding 1 (two beats), but it sits on the approach to the movement's main climax, where the brass balance matters most.
