# Critic 053: v72, lens 3 (form, pacing and proportion), time as experienced

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY, c043 and c052, and the v72 section files. I wrote scripts (in /tmp/critic-053/s) that turn the per-instrument MIDI (`all.midi`, one track per player) and the tempo map of `score.midi` into a per-bar timeline in seconds. Rit., allargando and sostenuto bars were scaled by 0.85–0.9, and the fermatas at 166 and 178 were given extra time. The timeline has four curves:
- **Dynamic level:** 1 = ppp to 8 = fff, read from note-on velocities with accented notes left out.
- **Density:** the share of the 31 players sounding, averaged per beat.
- **Register span:** in semitones.
- **Harmonic tension:** a per-beat dissonance index, with interval-class weights m2 1.0, tritone 0.8, M2 0.45, m3 0.15, M3 0.1, P4 0.05.

The same curves were computed from the MusicXML of **Tchaikovsky 6/i** and **Dvořák 9/i** (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`), with transposing instruments corrected. Tempi come from the files' `<sound tempo>` marks, and bar numbers are as in those files. I also compared seams in **Beethoven 9/i**.

Web sources:
- Farbood (2012), "A parametric, temporal model of musical tension": https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/wp.nyu.edu/dist/f/11865/files/2020/08/Farbood_2012_Musical_Tension.pdf
- Farbood & Upham (2013), *Frontiers in Psychology*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3874841. Listeners integrate harmony over windows of about 22 s and loudness over about 2 s ("loudness induces the most instantaneous tension response"). This supports the 20-second threshold used below.
- Open Music Theory, "Sonata Form" (Caplin's cores and retransition): https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/sonata-form/
- Hepokoski & Darcy's *Elements of Sonata Theory*: I could not get a usable text online, so I do not cite it for specifics.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 158–163: the fff climax and the coda's tutti are separated by one bar of mp. (Applied)
**Problem.**
- The curves show 154–158 at fff/ff with density 0.84. Then 159–162 (9.4 s) has every string and wind line under a single four-bar hairpin from ff that reaches mf/mp only at 162.
- After that, 163–166 is the densest block in the movement (27 parts, C1–F6).
- The trough between the movement's climax and the coda's tutti is one bar, and it never gets below mp. This is the movement's clearest case of two climaxes with too little contrast.

**Evidence.**
- Dvořák 9/i: the recapitulation's ff at 346–350 falls to p (351–369) and then to pp for 370–391, about 19 s, before the ff coda at 400.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: the fff at 313–317 falls to mf (318–325) and then to pp (326–335) before the coda.

**Fix** (dynamics only; recTwo.ily). The four-bar hairpin is shortened so that mp arrives at 160. The melody and bass then fall to p at 162. The E♮→E♭ inner voices (Hn, Cl1, Vla) stay at mp, so the turn protected since cycle 10 sounds more clearly than before. Its notes and its crescendo on beats 3–4 are unchanged.
- `recTwoMelodyB` (Vln I), Vln II copy, Fl1, Ob1:
  - 160 `d'''2\mp( c'''2)`
  - 161 `b''4\>( a''8 g''8 f''4 d''4)`
  - 162 `c''2\p~ c''2\<` (Fl1/Ob1 `c''2\p r2`)
- `recTwoArp`: 160 `d,8(\mp`, 161 `g,8(\>`
- `recTwoCb`: `d,2\mp g,2~ | g,1\> | c,2\p~ c,2\<`
- Bsn 1·2: 160 `\mp` on both, 161 `\>` on both, 162 `\p` on both (equal on the shared staff)
- `recTwoSync`: 160 `r4 f'4~\mp`
- `recTwoCounter`: 160 `f'4\mp`, with the redundant `\mp` at 162 removed

**Result.** The new MIDI gives levels of 5/4/4/4 for 159–162 (was 6/6/5/4), so the coda's f/ff now enters after about 7 s at mp–p. This cannot reach the models' 19 s without adding bars, which the coda-length and 162 rules forbid.

### 2. Bars 73–78: the same flute states the same A♭-minor motto twice across the exposition–development seam. (Applied)
**Problem.**
- Fl1 plays the motto pp over the A♭ pedal at 73–76. At 77 Fl1 (with Cl1) plays it again, mp, over the same pedal and in the same register (C♭6).
- The curves change by one dynamic level and nothing else (density 0.17–0.19 against 0.15–0.23).
- dev.ily's own plan says "a new colour on every entry", but across the seam the colour does not change.

**Evidence.** Every model marks this seam with a change:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 160→161: a held bassoon at pppp, then 15 parts at ff.
- Dvořák 9/i 180→181: violins, then 22 parts.
- Beethoven 9/i 159→160: f to pp, with a return of the introduction.

**Fix** (dev.ily, `devFluteOne`): `R1*4 | \partCombineSoloI r2 r8 g''8\f^\markup \bold "1." es'''4-> |`. Cl1 keeps the 77–78 answer alone, in its clarion register. The flute's next entry (81, f) is then new. No melody note changes; this answers the protection of 77–83 (cycles 21 and 51).

### 3. Development 85–110: harmonically the least dissonant Allegro section. (Not applied: protected)
- Mean dissonance is 1.04 in the development, against 1.30 in the exposition. In both models the development is the most dissonant section: Tchaikovsky 2.00 against 1.68 (peak 2.81 at 265–272), and Dvořák 1.27 against 0.96 (peak 2.35 at 204–215).
- In v72 the development's weight comes from loudness and density. Its 4-bar dissonance peaks are only 1.27–1.29 (91–102), plus 1.75 at 111–114. Both are below the transition (2.15 at 39–42) and the first-theme tutti (2.00 at 27–30).
- The fugato, the B-minor statement and the wedge are all protected, so I propose nothing. Rule for any future edit in 91–108: add dissonance (suspensions or appoggiaturas on strong beats), not consonance.

### 4. Static stretches: none in the Allegro. (No change)
- No window of 20 s or more between 15 and 166 has three of the four curves static (dynamic level constant, density within ±0.1, span within ±7 semitones, dissonance range ≤0.9).
- The static stretches are the Lento frames: 1–7 (34 s at pp, although span grows from 13 to 39 at bar 6) and 170–178 (40 s at pp, density 0.26–0.31). Tchaikovsky ends the same way: 347–353, 29 s, pp and static. His Andante 90–100 holds density and span for 43 s.
- The nearest case in the Allegro is 137–148 (density 0.17–0.37, span 41–46 for 24 s), where only harmony and swells move. Cycle 30 noted this risk, and the passage is protected. The 33% quiet share comes almost entirely from the frames, which cycle 43 already accounted for.

### 5. Bars 97–136: one long loud block, broken only by 117–121. (No change)
- Density stays at 0.5 or above for 78 s, except 117–122.
- The trough falls to pp for about 4.5 s (118–119) between the fff at 114 and the ff at 123.
- Tchaikovsky's development holds its loudest block 281–300 for 31 s, and Beethoven 9/i reaches its recapitulation with no trough at all. So this is within the models' range.
- The internal relief at 103 (mf restart) and 127–129 (mf) is enough. Do not add any ff bars here (c043).

### 7. Checks on cycles 50 and 52: both work as intended
- **Bar 14 (cycle 50):** nothing sounds on beat 4. The Allegro starts after 1.2 s plus the eighth rest at 15, which is clearly audible.
- **Bars 136–138 (cycle 52):** they hold G for 6.3 s, against 12.6 s of E♭ at 41–46. That ratio (0.50) matches the recapitulation's compression ratio for the P zone (c043).
- The 135–138 dissonance (1.71) is now the highest 4-bar chunk outside the tuttis, so the link reads as a dominant, not a tonic.
