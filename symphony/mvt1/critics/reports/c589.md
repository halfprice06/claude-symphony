## Schumann 1/i and this movement

What I saw in the Breitkopf score:

- **Introduction.**
  - PDF 3–4: the brass fanfare is answered by the ff tutti chords that Adler reduces in Ex. XV-11.
  - PDF 6: soft woodwind writing ends with a flute solo, "un poco ritard.", and a pizzicato close.
  - PDF 7–8: "Più vivace e poco a poco accelerando". A timpani trill on B♭ runs under string semiquaver figures and sustained wind chords that crescendo bar by bar into the Allegro. The tempo change happens inside the music, not at a double bar.
- **Retransition (PDF 30).** Another long timpani trill runs under string figuration "p poco a poco cresc.".
- **Coda (PDF 41, 43).**
  - Sf chords drop to fp at "Animato. Poco a poco stringendo".
  - The last page is a fast f tutti of detached chords over string tremolo.

These are the parallels with this movement:
- **Intro chord voicing.** Bar 12 is voiced on Schumann's principles (reading notes 32–33). Every pitch is doubled, and the augmented-sixth tones resolve by the book. The trombones enter mf so that their first ff, at 26, is heavier.
- **Retransition.** The timpani roll on the dominant under a crescendo, 126–130, is Schumann's PDF 30 technique (and Adler's Bloch rule, PDF 184).
- **Coloured motto returns.** The motto returns with new colours at 1, 5, 73, 117, 175 and 177, as Schumann's fanfare returns in the Allegro (Adler PDF 169–171).

There are two real differences:

1. **Introduction to Allegro.** Schumann's join is a written-out accelerando and crescendo of about 15 bars. Here the Lento ends p on V with the horn call (13–14), and the Allegro starts mf at 15 with no transition.
2. **Ending.** Schumann's coda ends loud and fast. This one ends ppp and attacca.

A conductor who knows Schumann would notice the first difference. Both are planned choices, and earlier cycles protected both (see findings 1 and 3).

A conductor would take this movement for the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer. Its tuttis are graded more carefully than Schumann's (82 ff, 122 fff open, 160 fff full). Schumann's brass, valveless and in B♭, is restricted to "horn fifths" (Adler PDF 161), and this score has no such restriction. Where Schumann is stronger is momentum: his tempo transitions run on in the music. This score marks its changes at section boundaries, at 15, 47, 147, 167 and 171. Within the protections that is a matter of style, not a fault.

## Findings

No change is recommended. These are the points the comparison and the reading raised, ranked by how much each stands between this score and Schumann's level. I give my verdict on each.

### 1. Introduction to Allegro without a written-out accelerando (keep)
**Bars 13–15.** Schumann (PDF 7–8) moves from the slow introduction to the Allegro with "Più vivace e poco a poco accelerando", a timpani trill and a long crescendo. Here the Lento stops on V, p, and the Allegro starts at 15.

I considered a two-bar "poco accelerando" at 13–14 with a crescendo. I rejected it for three reasons:
- "13–14 V7 with no rit." and the horn call's p > level are protected (c583; horn call contour c411). The horn call is quoted by movement II (Hn 1 E♭3–C4 span) and by the scherzo trio.
- The strings-only, mf entry of P at 15–22 is protected (c149). An accelerando and crescendo would make the Allegro start loud and spend the effect that 26→27 now carries.
- Adler PDF 162 (Ex. XV-12) gives the Schumann principle that matters here: hold back weight so the next phrase is heavier. The present plan follows it. The intro peaks f at 12, the Allegro starts mf, and the first ff comes at 26–27.

Not applied.

### 2. The recalled horn call at 125–126 is a unison doubling (keep)
**Bars 125–126**, added by c581. From its C4 the call sits at unison with Cl 2, Hn 3, the violas and the cellos (full.midi; vl.txt 126.2). Adler PDF 165 says unison doubling loses the instrument's own colour. So only the E♭3 pickup and the rising sixth are new.

That is why the recall works as a recall and not as a second solo. The first statement at 13–14 stays the only undoubled horn melody, as coherence/mvt1.md requires. Adler PDF 37 (save a colour for its event) supports keeping 13–14 unique.

Making Hn 3 rest at 125.3–126.1 would expose the call more. It would also take away the held C4 that ties 124–126 together, which is Smetana's "adhesive" (PDF 154), and it would undo part of c581's design. Not applied.

### 3. Loud fast ending in Schumann against the ppp attacca here (keep)
**Bars 167–182.** Schumann ends on a fast f–ff tutti (PDF 43). This movement has its loud close at 167–170, ff with a fermata on the stop chord, and then the soft coda. The soft coda is protected (c130, c140, c150, c160) and is the start of movement II. The loud C-major close belongs to the finale ("Do not reuse"). Adler PDF 162–163 (Ex. XV-13) supports the soft high-string final chord without flutes at 182. Not applied.

### 4. The 26→27 stop-chord resolution (keep)
**Bars 26–27.** Schumann's chords resolve every seventh down (Adler PDF 162). At 26→27 the A♭ falls in Fl 2, Hn 1 and Vln II. Tbn 1 (A♭3→C4), Cl 2 (A♭4→E♭4) and Vc (A♭3→C4) leave it by leap. The beat-4 rest separates the chords, so this is a stop chord followed by a new tutti, not a connected progression; Adler's Holst rule (PDF 181) applies. The layout of 26 and 27–32 is also quoted at pitch by the finale (IV 199–204). Not applied.

### 5. Divisi chords at 167–170 instead of Schumann's non-divisi double stops (keep)
**Bars 167–170.** Schumann and Wagner (Adler PDF 161, 172) use non-divisi violin double stops for ringing loud chords. Here Vln I and II play divisi at 167–170, with C6 on top. Three-note stops at that height are not practical, and c384 protects the divisi. The ff at 170 is already full in the winds and brass. Not applied.
