## Findings

Ranked by weight. Six findings are verdicts to keep the score as it is, each with the reason from the reading. The seventh is the record update the other movements need.

### 1. Withheld finale material and reserved colours: keep as is (whole movement)

Adler's flute-concerto lesson (PDF 267: "Mozart uses no orchestral flutes in this work, so that the solo flute will not encounter any color competition") and his slow-movement lesson (PDF 263: the solo colour "is very fresh, even after the first movement has been heard") apply across the symphony. Movement I should hold back the colours and ideas that the later movements present as new. I checked v410 against that:

- Finale themes. A scan of every track in full.midi finds no statement of F1's head (C–G–A♭–B♭–A♭–G, intervals −5 +1 +2 −2 −1 −2 −2) or of F2 (B♭–G♭–F–E♭–G♭–B♭) at any transposition. c260 and c330 rejected hinting at them; Adler PDF 263 and 267 give a further reason to keep it that way. F2 is a soft, undoubled trumpet solo at IV 51 and depends on being new. Movement I has no trumpet melody (mvt1.md "no trumpet solo and no soft trumpet melody"; the pp trumpets at 113–114 are inner parts of the wedge crescendo, doubled).
- Colours later movements feature as solos: no solo violin (II 17), no harp melody (III 73–80), no timpani alone (III 9), no clashed cymbal (IV 223), no muted or stopped brass (IV 167–171). The violins' highest note is A♭6 (bars 31, 72, 122, 160; MIDI checked). C7 appears only in the harp at 56 (D♭7 at 57), which the finale's rule allows.
- Do-not-reuse list (mvt1.md): unchanged since c320's release check. No v402–v410 change touches the trumpets, the stop-chord pitches, the coda notes or the timpani pulse.

Fix: none. Do not add finale or scherzo hints. The scherzo seeds at 79 and 106 and in the rhythm of P's second bar are enough, and they sit inside development material, not in a theme.

### 2. The horn as soloist, here and in movement II: keep (13–14, 46–55, 146–154)

Adler PDF 264: Mozart "never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn is playing ... so as not to interfere with the solo sound." Movement I meets this at every solo horn passage. At 13 Hn 3 releases its G3 (the F♯3→G3 resolution the finale quotes at IV 1–2) before Hn 1's call enters on beat 2. At 46–55 Hn 2–4 rest for the whole S statement and enter at 56 under the violins. At 147 Hn 3's C4 lasts two beats and resolves its B3 (c153, kept by c247). Movement II's theme A (Hn 1 solo over harp, II 7) is therefore a colour that movement I has used in only two places, both more than five minutes earlier: 46–52, where it is shared with the cellos, and 146–154, shared with the violas. Movement II opens with a wind chord before the solo, as Adler's Dvořák and Tchaikovsky examples do (PDF 263–264).

Fix: none.

### 3. The timpani pulse at 179–180 as accompaniment: keep, including the eighth rest

Adler PDF 270 credits the "quietly pulsating timpani" and sustained chord in Bartók's PC 3 with leaving the soloist "free rein". PDF 273 shows Beethoven's timpani playing "the motive that joins the two phrases". At 179–180 the pp pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` sits under the held strings and Vln I's last motto, as in Bartók's example. The finale moves the same figure between chorale phrases (IV 229–230), as in Beethoven's. Between them sit II 84–85 (timpani answer) and III 9 (motive x alone): background in I, foreground in III, a link in IV. This is Adler's exchange of foreground and background roles (PDF 262) applied across movements.

Fix: none. MIDI of 179–180 is identical to v401; themecheck `mvtOneTimpaniPulse` ok.

### 4. Coda dialogue and canon 171–182: keep (seeds for II 15–22 and IV 224–230)

171–174 is a duet of two single players, the solo cello and the cor anglais, over half-note violas and Bsn 2. That is Adler's rhythmic separation (PDF 259–260, 274): long C.a. notes against the cello's dotted S rhythm. It is answered in movement II by the solo violin and Ob 1 at 15–22 (mvt1.md seed, c310). 175–182 is a Fidelio-type canon (PDF 283): Tbn 1 G3–E♭4–D4–C4–D♭4–C4, Vln I the same line about two bars later and an octave higher, the bass doubled in two octaves (B.tbn, B.cl and cellos G2; basses G1). The finale takes this canon over in major at IV 224–230. The solo entrances are prepared by thin textures (PDF 268–270): a fermata rest before 171, and held pp strings before 177.4.

Fix: none. Bars 170–182 are identical in MIDI to v401, and v401 was checked against movement II at c400.

### 5. Introduction: C.a. answer and low motto: keep (1–9)

PDF 274 asks to "keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible ... especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". At 6–8 the C.a. (B♭4–A4, G4–A♭4–G4, G4) lies only a third to a fifth above Vln II (E♭4, F4, E4). The same page says "rhythmic contrast lessens the importance of this consideration": the strings hold whole notes pp while the C.a. moves, and the C.a. is always the top voice. Moving the strings down would break Vln II's E♭4/E4 thread at one pitch (8–9, mvt1.md) and the protected bars 6–8 (c229, c402). The low motto at 1–4 is in the bass clarinet's and cellos' lowest fifth. Adler (PDF 275) says such a register "must be lightly accompanied", and here only a ppp timpani roll accompanies it.

Fix: none.

### 6. The attacca handover: verified unchanged (182 → II 1)

Adler PDF 275: "it is essential to lead a singer to his first pitch". Movement I gives movement II its first chord in five tied parts: Vln I C5, Vln II G4, violas E4, cellos C3, Hn 3 C4. The basses, Hn 4, timpani, harp and tam-tam release at the end of 182. MIDI 170–182 is identical to v401, and themecheck gives 35/35 against v410.

Fix: none.
