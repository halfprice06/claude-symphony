## Findings

### F1. Bars 158 and 160: the climax statement of the second theme leaves its two syncopated long notes unmarked (applied)

**What is wrong.** Every statement of the second-theme family in this movement rises by a leap onto a long note that starts on beat 2 and is held through beat 3. In every one of them that note carries a tenuto: 48 (F4), 51 (C5), 57 (F5), 70 (F6), 99 (G), 102 (D6), 104 (B♭), 148 (A4), 151 (E5), 172 (A♭4). The climax at K breaks this. At 158 the tune (Vln I·II, Vc, Fl 1·2, Ob 2, Hn 1·2, Tpt 1) leaps G5–E6 with E6 on beat 2, held for seven beats. At 160 it leaps C6–A♭6, the motto's rising sixth, with A♭6 on beat 2, the highest note of the movement over the ♭VI bass. Neither long note has a mark. The *ff* and *fff* are written on the beat-1 pickup notes G5 and C6, and in the strings the long notes fall on up-bows after the marked down-bow pickups (c088/c238 scheme, kept). As written, the players are asked to attack the pickup and let the long note sound as its continuation. That is the reverse of what the rest of the theme does and of what the reading describes.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 51: a note begun on a weak beat and held through a strong one is a syncopation, "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation", and in Ex. 3-6 Beethoven writes *sf* on exactly such a note to make the conflict audible. PDF 50: very high notes and long notes carry a built-in accent. Both apply to A♭6 at 160. PDF 53: 158 and 160 are bars 1 and 3 of the four-bar hypermeasure 158–161, the strong bars, so these are the two notes the phrase should lean on. The score's own S-head mark for this situation is a tenuto, not an accent, so I used a tenuto. It gives weight and full length on the up-bow without turning the melody into hammer strokes.

**Fix (applied, recTwo.ily).** Tenuto on E6/E5/E4 at 158.2 and on A♭6/A♭5/A♭4 at 160.2 in every tune part: Vln I `e'''2.--~` and `aes'''2--~`; Vln II `e''2.--~`, `aes''2--~`; Vc (recTwoArp) `e'2.--~`, `aes'2--~`; Fl 1 and Fl 2 `e'''2.--~`, `aes'''2--~`; Ob 2 `e''2.--~`, `aes''2--~`; Tpt 1 `e''2.--~`, `aes''2--~`; Hn 1 and Hn 2 `e'2.--~`, `aes'2--~`. Notes, slurs, bowings, breaths and dynamics are unchanged. The *fff* stays on the downbeat of 160 (c148's do-not-touch). c148 listed 158/160 under "follows" for syncopation without discussing the missing mark; my answer is the internal evidence above: these are the only two held beat-2 leap notes of the theme, apart from 62 (F2), that have no tenuto. Checked on page 16 of the scratch PDF: the tenutos print in every staff and nothing collides.

### F2. Bar 62: the violins' sub. p rising sixth E♭5–C6 has no tenuto on its held beat-2 C (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 62 Vln I and II play `es''4\subP( c'''2.~ | c'''4)`: the S-head rising sixth, with C6 (C5 in Vln II) entering on beat 2 and held through beat 3 and across the bar line to 63.1. This is the same figure as 48, 51, 57 and 70, all of which carry a tenuto on the held note. Here it is the longest syncopation in the violins' statement and the highest note before the D♭6 at 63. Without a mark, after the *sub. p* on E♭ and with *poco animando* starting in the same bar, players will tend to let the long note fade.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 51 (syncopation; Ex. 3-6 marks it) and PDF 50 (long and high notes carry accent). PDF 53: 62 is bar 3 of the hypermeasure 60–63, a strong bar, and the crescendo that follows (63.1 to *f* at 64) needs a sustained note to grow from. A tenuto at *p* keeps the subito level (c068's protected *sub. p*) and adds only weight and length.

**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily).** Vln I `es''4\subP( c'''2.--~ | c'''4)\<`, Vln II `es'4\subP( c''2.--~ | c''4)\<`. No other change. 63–64 (c221), the E string at 62–63 (c141) and the protected *sub. p* are untouched. Checked on page 6 of the scratch PDF.

### Considered and kept (no change)

- **142, accents and down-bows on both C and B of the 4–3 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I·II).** The reading puts the weight on the suspension and less on its resolution (A&S PDF 55). But these are the protected down-bow hammer strokes of cycle 118 (with 32, 72, 83) at the end of the recapitulation's tutti. The B falls on beat 3, the secondary metrical accent (PDF 50), and closes the phrase at the half cadence. The music then drops to *f* at 143 by a new entry, not by a decay. Keep.
- **180, Vln I's last D♭.** In bar 3 the motto's swell turns on D♭ (c148). Here the D♭ is the only moving note over a held C-minor chord, so its chromatic accent is built in (PDF 50), and the coda's flat *pp* and its timing are protected (c228, c249, c258). Keep.
- **131–134, unaccented D♭6 and F5 tied over the bar line in the upper counter.** The accents sit on the beat-2 halves, aligned with the low P head's own beat-2 accent. The tied beat-4 notes are links, and the bowing scheme 131–142 is protected (c195). Keep.

The rit. at 66–68, 165–166 and 177–178 falls on phrase ends (PDF 54). The written-out acceleration of 127–130 changes the subdivision, not the beat (PDF 49). Keep, including the metronome values (c098).
- **Hemiola 23–25 and 135–137; weak-beat accents 32, 72, 80–81, 119–120; *sf* at 20.** These follow Ex. 3-6 to 3-8, as c148 found. Keep.
