## Findings

### 1. Bars 139–141 against 158–161: the recapitulation's first A♭6 is longer, and has the stronger approach, than the main climax's (not applied; evidence for cycle 194)
**Problem.** From the tension-curve side, here is how the two top-register peaks compare, measured from v212/full.midi:
- **140–141 (ff):** Fl 1 + Vln I reach A♭6 by a rising scale (D♭–E♭–F–G) and hold it 3.5 beats (`aes'''2->~ | aes'''4.`), MIDI velocity 121.
- **160 (fff, cymbal):** Fl 1·2 + Vln I·II reach A♭6 by a leap from C6 and hold it 2.5 beats (`c'''4 aes'''2~ aes'''8`), velocity 107.

The other A♭6s are 1.5 beats at 31, 1 beat at 72 and 4 beats at 122. At 160 the climax has more doublings, a louder dynamic and the cymbal. In duration and in the drive of the approach, 140 is the bigger gesture. The listener hears the pitch goal of the ♭VI climax 20 bars earlier, at a higher speed and for longer.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 232 and 234 describe large-scale arrivals that are prepared by intervening harmony and not anticipated. The Open Music Theory sources treat the recapitulation's first group as a restatement, not a culmination. Critics 184 and 191 raised the same point on register grounds. HISTORY assigns the decision to cycle 194 (orchestration).

**Fix.** Leave the decision to cycle 194. From the form side I support critic 184's tested fix: Vln I 8vb at 139–142, with Fl 1 alone keeping A♭6 (`recOneVlnOne` bars 139–142 down an octave). That lowers the weight of 140 and leaves its pitch and length alone. It does not touch the cycle-24/61 rule for 160.

### 2. Large-scale use of III: withheld on purpose; no change
**Observation.** The exposition reaches III twice by ♮VII → III (35→36, 39–40→41; A&S PDF 231, 233) and tonicizes it with I–i7–ii7–V7–I (36–41). Then it reinterprets the E♭ chord as V of A♭ by adding D♭ at 43. This is the two-stage hearing A&S describe for Handel Op. 3/1 (Ex. 15-16, PDF 242): a chord first heard as the goal is then heard as a dominant.

The development's fugato chain G–C–F–B♭ (85–96) is a descending-fifths chain (Ex. 15-9, PDF 236–237). Its next link would be E♭. Instead, the chain stops on B♭ minor and B minor follows by semitone at 97, which is protected since cycle 2. The movement therefore never states III as a key, although A&S PDF 232 calls it minor's natural secondary key.

**Judgement.** This is a coherent design, not a flaw. It differs from Beethoven 9/i: there, III appears only as a dominant in the exposition (bars 73–77) and becomes a key in the development (from bar 281). Here III is the other way round: tonicized in the exposition, absent from the development. The cost is small, because A♭ (VI) and the C-major recapitulation supply the major-mode contrast that A&S assign to III (PDF 232).

**Fix.** None in movement I. Note for cyclic-notes.md: E♭ major has not been a key in movement I, and movement II only uses it as E♭7. Movement III (C minor → C major) can take E♭ as the key of its second group. That would be the minor mode's normal choice (A&S PDF 232; Open Music Theory), and in this symphony it would also be new.

### 3. c192's open item: does the bar-28 fix also apply at 132, 142 and 168? Checked; already consistent

- **142:** Hn 3, Tpt 3, Tbn 1, Bsn 1, Vc, Ob 2, Cl and Vln II all move `c2 b2` with the tune.
- **168:** this is a tonic pedal and no part holds B.

No change is needed, and the c192 item can be closed.

### 4. Bars 70→71: outer-voice cross-relation D♮ (bass) → D♭ (soprano); acceptable, no change
**Observation.** In the codetta, the bass D♮ on 70.3–4 (vii°7/V; B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, Vc, Cb at f) is followed on 71.1 by D♭6 in Fl, Ob 1, Tpt 1 and Vln I, the seventh of V7. A&S PDF 236 (Ex. 15-8c) and review point 5 (PDF 241) call a cross-relation between bass and soprano "unduly harsh". My scan found no other outer-voice case outside the protected 96→97 semitone shift.

**Judgement.** A&S's example puts the key's natural 7̂ in the soprano against its raised 7̂ in the bass. That contradicts the leading tone. Here D♮ is an applied leading tone that resolves up to E♭ while the soprano comes down E♭→D♭ in contrary motion: the usual vii°7/V–V7 succession. The codetta bass A♭–C–B♭–D♮–E♭ is protected (c103). I would not change it on this evidence. If a later critic hears the clash as harsh, the least invasive option is for the bass to hold B♭ through 70 (V♭9/V in root position) with D♮ in an inner voice. That needs an answer to c103's reason and a full voice-leading check.
