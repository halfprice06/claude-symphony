## Findings

### 1. Bars 28–31: the third trumpet doubles the chromatic bass two octaves up (applied)

What is wrong. In the hammered tutti 27–32 the bass rises C–D–E♭–E–F (Cb, Cbsn, tuba, B.tbn, Bsn 2). Tpt 3 plays the same line two octaves higher: D4 (28), E♭4 (29), E4 (30, repeated in the timpani rhythm), F4–D4 (31). Hn 2 adds E4 at 30 and moves E4→F4 with the bass into 31. The chords over that bass are V4/3 with a 4–3 suspension (28, bass D), iv4/2 (29, bass E♭, the chord seventh), and the diminished seventh of F (30, bass E, the leading tone). vl.txt lists the result as octaves between Tpt 3 and the bass at 30 and 31 and between Hn 2 and the bass at 31.

Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 82: extra parts "are nothing more than the duplication in the adjacent upper octave of one or more of the three upper parts ... the bass being doubled in the lower octave only", and the note on the same page: "doubling the bass part creates an effect of heaviness. The bass part should never mix with the others." PDF 83–84: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts", with the examples 6/5, 4/3 and 2 marked "Bad", and "This applies also to other chords of the seventh and diminished seventh" (PDF 84, examples with diminished sevenths). PDF 51 makes it worse here: one trumpet equals two horns in loud passages, so the strongest single part in the middle of the chord is a copy of the bass. At 30 the leading tone E is the most doubled note of the diminished seventh (bass group, Tpt 3, Hn 2, Bsn 1, Vc), while B♭ has only Vla/Hn 4.

This is not covered by the protections. "Chromatic bass 27–32" protects the bass line, which is unchanged. "Graded brass 27–32" (dynamics) and "trumpets' timpani rhythm 27–30" (rhythm) are kept. "Tutti layout at 27–32 and 167–170 (alike on purpose)" concerns the theme and string layout; the trumpets at 167–170 do not double the bass (it is a C pedal), so the two passages were not alike in this respect. Cycle 222 kept tutti doublings of tendency tones at 12–13, 26–27 and 142–143 on A&S grounds (PDF 84–87); 28–31 was not in that list, and the point here is Rimsky's separate rule about the bass of inversions, which A&S does not address.

Fix (tOne.ily). Tpt 3 takes upper chord tones; Tpt 2 moves to keep the trumpets in normal order (Rimsky PDF 90 rule 2); Hn 2 leaves the bass E. Rhythms and dynamics unchanged.
```
% Tpt 2, bars 28 and 30
f'4 r4 f'4 r4 | ... | g'4 r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8      % before
g'4 r4 g'4 r4 | ... | bes'4 r8 bes'8 bes'4 r8 bes'8   % after
% Tpt 3, bars 28-31
d'4 r4 d'4 r4 | es'1 | e'4 r8 e'8 e'4 r8 e'8 | f'2 d'2       % before
f'4 r4 f'4 r4 | f'1  | g'4 r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8 | aes'2 f'2     % after
% Hn 2, bar 30
e'1   % before
g'1   % after
```
Result: trumpets G4/F4 under Tpt 1's G4–B4 (28, full V7 with Tbn 1's C–B), F4 held into the iv4/2 (29), D♭5/B♭4/G4 on the diminished seventh (30, the upper three notes in close position), A♭4 then F4 (31). Tpt 3 reaches A♭4 at 31 instead of F4 so that it does not go G4–D♭5 (d5) → F4–C5 (P5) with Tpt 1, the motion cycle 152 removed. All new entries in the vl.txt diff are doublings of one line (Hn 2/Tpt 3 in unison, Fl 2/Tpt 2/Hn 4 in octaves on A♭–B♭–A♭); 34 entries leave vl.txt, among them every octave between Tpt 3 or Hn 2 and the bass.

### 2. Bars 145–146: the clarinets hold an open fifth under the strings (applied)

What is wrong. At 145–146 (pp, the V7 before the C-major second group) the wind chord is Hn 4 G3, Hn 3 and Bsn 1 B3, Cl 2 D4, Cl 1 G4. The two clarinets are a fifth apart, Cl 1 is in unison with Vln I's held G4, and the seventh F is only in Vln II. So the wind group, which is meant to complete the horn chord ("clarinet-completed p horn chords at 145", protected), sounds G–B–D–G with an open fifth on top.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 90, rule 4: "Concords (octaves, thirds and sixths) and not discords (fifths, fourths, seconds and sevenths), should be given to instruments of the same kind or colour, except when discords are to be emphasised." PDF 97, remark 4: when the strings take the upper part, "it is desirable to provide the intermediate parts with a moderately full tone, choosing no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths", and when the bass is separate "the three-part harmony in the wood-wind should form an independent whole ... in this manner intervals of open fourths and fifths will be obviated". PDF 52: one wind in unison with a string section only thickens it, so Cl 1's G4 adds nothing that Vln I does not have.

Fix (recOne.ily, recOneClOne, bars 145–146):
```
R1 | g'1\pp~ | g'2 r2 |    % before
R1 | f'1\pp~ | f'2 r2 |    % after
```
The clarinets become D4/F4, a third; horns and clarinets together now give the whole V7 (G–B–D–F) in one wind colour; Cl 1 doubles Vln II's F4 instead of Vln I's G4. The oboe's dolce G4 on beat 3 of 145 is a second above Cl 1, which is between different timbres and allowed by the same rule. The seventh F4 stops at 146.2 in Cl 1 as it already does in Vln II; the viola's E4 at 147 takes the resolution. This keeps the protected idea (clarinets complete the horns) and improves it. No voice-leading change in vl.txt.
