## Findings

### 1. Bar 160-161, Vln I, Vln II, Vc: the down-bow at 161 needs a hooked up-bow on the G before it (applied)

What is wrong. At 160 the climax melody is bowed `c4\fff\downbow aes2--~ aes8 g8 | f2\downbow`. Counting strokes: C down, the tied A♭ up, the separate G eighth down, and then the marked down-bow on F at 161. The F can only be down-bow if the player retakes after a 0.3 s eighth at fff largamente, which puts an unwanted accent on the passing G and a gap before the F. The other loud places in the movement where a downbeat must follow an up-bow already carry the second up-bow mark (16, 28, 36, 132, 144, 168); 160 is the only one without it. My bow count found no other unmarked retake after a short note.

Evidence. Adler PDF 38 (observation 2 on Ex. II-19, Schubert 5/ii, PDF 37): "two up-bows are called for in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat of the next measure", the player stopping the bow briefly between them. PDF 38 also: mark bow direction where you counteract the normal habit; the composer has already done that with the down-bow at 161, but only half of the instruction is there. In the exposition (64) the same A♭-G is slurred, so it never arose.

Fix (applied in recTwo.ily, recTwoMelodyB for Vln I, recTwoVlnTwo, recTwoArp for Vc):
`c'''4\fff\downbow aes'''2--~ aes'''8 g'''8\upbow | f'''2\downbow` (and the same at the Vln II and Vc octaves). The tenuto on the A♭ already gives the separation Adler's dash asks for. Winds and brass are untouched.

### 2. Bar 26, Vla and Vc: the ff stop chord falls on an up-bow (applied)

What is wrong. The violas' up-bow at 19 (crescendo, correct by Adler PDF 38) and the cellos' half notes from 15 lead both parts, by plain alternation, to the vii°7 stop chord at 26 on an up-bow: Vla 23-25 twelve détaché quarters, Vc `c2.-> b,4~ | b,2 c2~ | c4 f2.->`. Violins I and II and the basses reach 26 on a down-bow, and in the recapitulation all five parts reach the same chord at 138 on a down-bow (131-142 scheme, c538). So the exposition's stop chord is the only one of the three (26, 138, 170) where two string parts attack ff from the tip.

Evidence. Adler PDF 38: the player is "able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; PDF 38 Ex. II-20 (Beethoven, *Coriolanus*): the bowing is arranged so the ff triple-stop is a down-bow; PDF 42: heavy strokes are down-bows at the frog. The composer accepted the same kind of fix for the f at 12 (c405 Vla, c425 Cb).

Fix (applied in tOne.ily): `<f' b'>2.->\ff\downbow r4` (Vla) and `<b, aes>2.->\ff\downbow r4` (Vc). The Vla retakes after a quarter, the Vc after the accented dotted half; both are ordinary retakes at ff. Pitches and layout of the stop chord are unchanged (protected c395), and the beat's rest before 27 lets the players start the hammered tutti as they wish (Vla 27 is already marked down-bow).

### No further change

I looked for a place where better string notation would make the music itself stronger. The candidates were a *sul ponticello* or *col legno* colour for the soft development tremolos at 77-81 (Adler PDF 52), and splitting the 8-beat S-theme slurs at 46.4-48.4 and 146.4-148.4. I rejected the first: it changes the orchestration of a protected passage (77-130 scoring), and the development's soft episode is protected (c459). I rejected the second: 4.3 s at mf is well inside a cello bow, and the slur is the bowing for the rising sixth into the turn, as Adler's Ex. II-21/22 allow at that speed. The pp slur at Vc 178-179 (about 8 s across the rit.) is long but stays as it is, because "change bow at will" starts on its last note, and c568 placed it to join D♭ to C as at bar 3. Every other rule in the table is followed. The parts carry every technique word the score has (counts checked for all five string parts).
