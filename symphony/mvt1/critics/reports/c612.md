1. a six-four finder (4th and 6th above the lowest sounding note, no 3rd or 5th);
2. a new scan for this pass: every dominant-seventh sonority whose 7th is attacked when no voice sounded that pitch class on the previous half-beat (an unprepared 7th, against Unit 9 §6);

Earlier passes (c232, c322, c412, c432, c522) ran a retrogression scan, a weak-to-strong bass scan, an eighth-level 4th-resolution check and an early-resolution scan. c522 made the one change this reading produced in four passes (violas at 159.1).    - Most of the rest are the P head's D♭ neighbour (15.4, 27.4, 130.1, 131.4, 167.4, 180.3), which makes a passing E♭7 sonority, not a V7 function.
   - Remaining hits: 23.4/135.4 (the P restatement leaps C5–F5 into V6/5 straight from i. Unit 9 §6 governs 7ths reached from IV or II, and this one comes from the tonic. It resolves down by step to E♭5 on 24.1/136.1. P head protected, c414); 83.1/83.3 (the ff V♭9 strokes after the A♭ chord, E♭→F rising, protected "three ♭9s saved for 83/117/122", c592); 117.1 and 122.1 (arrival and climax on the G pedal, protected 117–130); 112.1, 116.1, 139.4 (augmented-sixth and applied chords in the wedge and recapitulation, governed by Units 28–29, protected).
   - Verdict: keep.

2. **21.3–22.3: the P consequent's V7 crosses the barline, with E♭5 (3̂) on the downbeat of 22 over V7.**
   - What happens: N6 at 21.1, V7 from 21.3 to 22.2, tonic on 22.3. The tune falls E♭5–D5–C5–B4–C5 over the held V7. On 22.1 the E♭5 is a 6th against the bass G, without the 4th; the 4th C5 comes on 22.2.
   - Why it might be flagged: PDF 156 §8 and the note to exercise 5 (PDF 162) say that a melodic 3̂–2̂–1̂–7̂–1̂ at a cadence may require the cadential 6/4, with 3̂ and 1̂ accented. Here V7 is already sounding and repeats from beat 3 of 21 into the downbeat of 22.
   - Why it stays: a cadential 6/4 on 22.1 would follow the V7 of 21.3, which review 7 (PDF 160) forbids, and replacing 21.3 with an intermediate chord would put the tune's G5 against IV or II as an unprepared dissonance. The current setting (an accented E♭ appoggiatura over V7, the 6th and 4th arriving one beat apart) gives the minor-mode ♭6 colour on the strongest beat of the phrase. The P sentence 15–22 and its stepwise fall are protected (c414, c471, c561).
   - Verdict: keep.

3. **154.4→155: the V7 sounds over F3 for one beat.** The double basses play pizzicato on beats 1 and 3 (G2 at 154.3) and rest on beat 4, so the lowest arco note at 154.4 is the cellos' F3, which carries the S tune with Hn 1 and the violas. The heard bass therefore moves F3→E3 under the pizzicato C2 at 155: V4/2 to I6 over the root, which keeps syntax rule 1 (PDF 148). Protected S (c489, c492). Keep.

4. **101–102: V, then a 6/4 over the same F♯ in the development S (B minor, ff).**
   - What happens: iiø7 at 100 leads to a 5/4 suspension on V at 101.1 (the strong bar), V7 at 101.3, then B–D over F♯ at 102.1 (the weak bar), V7 at 102.3, and the phrase breaks off into D minor at 103.
   - Why it might be flagged: review 7 (PDF 160) says a cadential 6/4 should not be approached from V, and Ex. 9-24a (PDF 149) warns against a weak-to-strong bass repetition under a change of chord.
   - Why it stays: 102 is not the cadential 6/4. The cadential gesture is on the strong bar 101 (the 5/4 of Ex. 10-2a). 102 stands where the exposition has its tonic (55): it is an evaded cadence. The dominant is re-decorated by an appoggiatura 6/4, which review 8 allows ("a 6/4 on the dominant is not restricted to cadences"), and the dissonant chord on the repeated bass is the exception Ex. 9-24b describes. The D-minor turn at 103 is protected (c604, c391).
   - Verdict: keep.
