## Changes made in cycles 25–33
- **163–166, brass head over violin tremolo.** Sound. This is Rimsky-Korsakov's own case for strings with brass: harmony in string tremolo, brass on the line. Tpt 1·2 a2 carry the line in the trumpet's strongest octave (G4–F5) and will be heard. Two qualifications:
  - The viola and cello lines here mostly duplicate Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 in the G3–F4 octave, so they add weight but are not heard as lines.
  - The violin double stops include perfect fifths (Finding 3).
- **Trumpets in timpani rhythm, 27–30.** Fine; the octave motion at 29→30 is a doubling, not a parallel.
- **Hn 3 on the counterline, 154–157.** Idiomatic: c'' is a normal high-horn note at fff. History already records this point as argued both ways, so I do not reopen it.
- **The ff chord at 135.** It is voiced well, but it hides the first note of the head (Finding 2).
- **The Vla/Vc scale at 144.** Idiomatic. Hn 1 holds the lower octave while the strings run up, and Cl 1 takes over the f''.
- **Hn 4/Tbn 2 A♭ at 166.** Fine.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 94–113: Fl 1 and Ob 1 double V1 for 20 bars without a break, so the 114 climax has no fresh colour
**What is wrong.** From 94 to 135 the top line is some form of Fl+Ob+V1 in 37 of 42 bars. As a result, the flutes' and oboes' arrival at the fff A♭6 at 114 is not an event: they have been on that line since 94.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i clears the high woodwinds before the climax. After the tutti at 267–269, flutes and oboes drop out at 277–280, leaving strings, horns and low brass. The oboes return at 281 and the flutes at 284, one bar before the fff at 285.
- Rimsky-Korsakov: in string–woodwind unison "all combinations refine the characteristics of each instrument… the wood-wind losing more than the strings". Constant doubling therefore removes both the pure string colour and the pure woodwind colour.

**Fix (tested).** Melody notes, the fugato spacing, the B-minor statement and the 109–113 layering are unchanged.
- **(a) Ob 1 rests in the fugato's 4th entry, 94–96.** Each entry is then one string section plus one woodwind (Vc; Va+Bsn 1; Vln II+CA; Vln I+Fl 1), and the oboe enters fresh with the B-minor theme at 97. `devOboeOne`: replace
  `| R1*10 | r8 f'8\f des''4.-> c''8 ces''8 bes'8 | bes'4\< a'8( bes'8) ges'4.-> f'8 | r8 f'8 des''4.-> c''8 ces''8 bes'8 | \transpose c cis {`
  with
  `| R1*13 | \transpose c cis {`
- **(b) The flutes rest at the wedge's mf restart, 103–106, and return f at 107** (dev section bars 27–30, re-entry at 31).
  - `devFluteOne`, bars 27–31: `R1*4 | des'''4\f c'''4 es'''4 des'''4 |`
  - `devFluteTwo`, bars 27–31: `R1*4 | des''4\f c''4 es''4 des''4 |`
  - Both flutes have equal dynamics on the shared staff. V1 carries 103–106 alone on top, with Ob 1·2 and Tpt 1 an octave below. This matches the cycle-8 "restart" dynamics.

Removing doublings cannot create parallels, and `vl3.py` confirms there are none.

### 2. Bars 33 and 135: the ff quarter-note chord covers the head's first note
**What is wrong.** The rising sixth G–E♭ starts on the second eighth of the bar:
- at 33 in Bsn 1 and Vc (mf);
- at 135 in Vln I (f).

That G sounds in unison with the chord's own ff G (at 135: Ob 2, Hn 1, Tpt 2; at 33: Tbn 2), and the chord releases just as the E♭ arrives. The listener hears the head begin on E♭, so the motto's interval is lost both times.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov's balance table: in forte, one string section equals two woodwinds and a horn equals two woodwinds. A single section at f cannot sound through eight or more brass and woodwind parts at ff on the same pitch class.

**Fix (tested).** Shorten the chord to an accented eighth, so that the G is attacked exactly as the chord stops. The weight of the elision stays.
- **Trans bar 1 (= 33).** Change `X4->\ff r2.` to `X8->\ff r8 r2.` in transFluteOne, transFluteTwo, transOboeOne, transOboeTwo, transClOne, transClTwo, transHnOne, transHnTwo, transTptOne, transTptTwo, transTbnOne, transTbnTwo, transBassTbn, transTuba, transTimp, transVlnOne and transVlnTwo. For example, transFluteOne becomes `c'''8->\ff r8 r2. |`.
- **RecOne bar 13 (= 135).** Make the same change in recOneFluteOne/Two, OboeOne/Two, ClOne/Two, Cbsn, HnOne/Two, TptOne/Two, TbnOne/Two, BassTbn, Tuba and Timp. For example: `c'''8->\ff r8 r2. |`, `es''8->\ff r8 r2. |`, `c,8->\ff r8 r2. |`.

Shared-staff dynamics remain equal (ff/ff). This keeps cycle 33's chord and changes only its length.

### 3. Bars 163–164: one-finger perfect-fifth double stops in high-position tremolo
**What is wrong.**
- Vln II plays `<c'' g''>` for all of 163.
- Vln I plays `<aes'' es'''>` on beats 3–4 of 164, which is a fifth in seventh position.

Both are one-finger fifths. Section players will either divide them anyway or play them out of tune.

**Evidence.** These sources call the perfect fifth the hardest double stop to play in tune, and the difficulty grows with position. They also note that section players divide two-note chords by default:
- https://robin-hoffmann.com/?p=2564
- https://orchestrationonline.com/?p=4999

**Fix (tested).** Mark the passage divisi and restore unison afterwards.
- `codaVlnOne`: `<g'' es'''>1:32\ff^"div."`, and at 173 `r2. g'4(\pp^"unis."`.
- `codaVlnTwo`: `<c'' g''>1:32\ff^"div."`, and at 167 `g'1\pp^"unis."`.

With this, each violin section is split in two, never more.

### 4. Bars 163–166: the coda's ff C-minor pedal has nothing below C2
**What is wrong.** Cb, Cbn and Tuba all sound C2, the same note. At 154–156, critic 24 gave the climax G1/A♭1 for weight. The tonic arrival at 163 is the one place where a 16-foot C1 belongs, and only the contrabassoon can supply it.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov lists "D. basses + C-fag." among the natural doublings.
- The usual practice is the contrabassoon an octave below the basses. C1 is inside the instrument's range, whose bottom is B♭0.

**Fix (tested).** `codaCbsn` bars 1–4: `c,,1\ff | c,,1 | c,,1 | c,,2.->\ff r4\fermata |`.
