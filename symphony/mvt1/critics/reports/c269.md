## Elgar 1/i and this movement

Elgar's movement is longer and slower to unfold: a long A♭ nobilmente motto (PDF 5, crotchet = 72, the tune in violas, flutes, clarinets and bassoons over a pp walking bass and a timpani roll), then an Allegro in a remote key. This movement does the same thing in small: a bare motto in the low strings over a timpani roll, then an Allegro on the motto's notes. In plan and harmony the two are comparable, and in compression this one is closer to Brahms. A conductor would accept the form, the key plan and the climaxes as professional work.

The difference that shows on the page is in the soft string writing. In the last four pages of Elgar's movement (PDF 62–65), the strings are subdivided again and again. The violas play "2 SOLI ponticello div." against "TUTTI" and later "1 SOLO naturale". The cellos alternate "SOLO" and "TUTTI", and then "2 SOLI". At figure 54 all strings go "con sordini". A solo cello takes the last phrase pizz. over the section. These are Rimsky-Korsakov's PDF 122–123 and Ex. 284. In this score every soft string passage uses whole sections or plain divisi, and no string player is ever alone. The one place where the music asks for a single player is the duet at 171–174.

## Findings

### 1. 171–174: the cellos' half of the C.a. duet should be one player (applied)

At 171–174 the cor anglais (p, espr.) plays the motto, G4–E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5, against the cellos' C minor S head, mp espr. in tenor clef. The only accompaniment is violas and Bsn 2, pp. The score treats the passage as a duet of two soloists: the harmony is i – iv – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V, the two lines cross and meet in unison on C5 at 174, and c127 and c247 moved the violas below the "cello solo" (coda.ily comments, lines 68 and 76). But the cellos play it as a section. Against one cor anglais, eight or ten cellos in their tenor register at mp give an unequal pair. The section sound also matches the full-section tutti at 167–170 (ff) and the full-section motto bass at 175. So the duet never sounds different from what is around it.

- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 142, Ex. 284 (PDF 457, The Tsar's Bride [108]): an oboe solo over Vln II and violas con sord. pp, with "V-c. solo (senza sord.) dolce" against "altri V-c. con sord." That is the same layout as here: a woodwind solo, a single cello as its partner, and the rest of the strings as a light background.
- PDF 122–123: solo strings are used "simply in order to obtain that singular difference in colour which exists between a solo stringed instrument and strings in unison", and "the comparatively weak tone of the solo instrument necessitates light, transparent accompaniment". Violas and one bassoon at pp are that kind of accompaniment (PDF 138–139).
- Ex. 285 (PDF 458–459, The Golden Cockerel [135]): a cor anglais and bass clarinet line over harp and pizzicato, with the cellos entering "dolcissimo" as a single counter-line.
- Elgar 1/i, PDF 62–65: "SOLO" and "TUTTI" in the cellos, inside the coda.

The change also helps the large plan. 175 is now heard as the section returning, Tutti and sul G, for the motto in augmentation with the trombones and tam-tam. That is a real change of colour on the downbeat where the chorale begins.

The tutti re-enters after four bars of rest. The cello part shows "Solo espr." at 171 and "Tutti sul G" at 175 on its last system (checked in partsout/parts-29-violoncello.pdf, 2 pages).

### 2. v259 → v269: nothing got worse

I diffed every section file, global.ily and score.ly between v259 and v269 and checked each change against the score pages:

- 25 (c259): Hn 4 breathes on beat 4. Better: the low D2–F2 third is gone.
- 37 (c267): Bsn 1 B♭3. The vl.txt diff shows the old Bsn 1 / Vc / Vla F♯ octaves at 37 are gone. The new 37→38 octave Bsn 1 / Vln II B♭–A♭ is a doubling inside an inner line, at mf. Acceptable.
- 94–96 (c264): C.a. with Vln II, and Cl 1 joins at 96. The colour is now kept through the countersubject. Better.
- 121–123 (c266): tuba and Hn 3 breaths. Better: the Cb and Cbsn keep the G1 through the tuba's eighth rest.
- 131 (c264): violas and Hn 3·4 at f under the low P. Better.
- 144 (c261): accompaniment mp under the bassoon's answer. Better.
- 158, 160, 62 (c268): tenutos on the syncopated long notes. Consistent with 48–151.
- 177 (c262): viola A♭3–G3. Better voice leading, and the vl.txt is unchanged at 177.

I found no regression. The other v259 → v269 differences in vl.txt are limited to bar 37–38.

### 3. Considered and not raised

- **Harp silent at the fff climax 158–161.** Rimsky's Ex. 275 (PDF 441–442) puts harp glissandi ff through a tutti, and PDF 134 says long glissandi are more brilliant than short ones. But the harp plan for 147–166 is protected (c189, c246: pedals set so that movement II inherits B♭). A G7♭9 glissando at 159 would need C♭, E♯ and A♭ pedals and a change back for the A♭ chord at 160, under the cymbal roll. The gain is small against that risk. Keep as is.
- **Mutes for the last motto (177–182).** Elgar mutes all strings at figure 54. c076's reason still holds: Vln I alone muted over unmuted Vln II would put the tune under its accompaniment, and the tied C5 crosses into movement II (sul D, c251). Keep.
- **Two soli in the violas at 175–182.** Ex. 299 (PDF 484) uses "4 V-le" and "4 V-c". But the violas carry the E♭→E♮ thread into movement II, which the other movements rely on. Changing their numbers is not worth a cross-movement note. Keep.
