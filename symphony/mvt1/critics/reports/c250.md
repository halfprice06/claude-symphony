## Findings

### 1. The crash cymbals at 160 spend the finale's reward sound (applied)

**Bars:** 159–160 (recTwoPerc).

**What is wrong.** Movement I's only cymbal crash is fff at 160 (crash cymbals l.v., after a suspended-cymbal roll at 159). Movements II and III have no cymbals: II has no percussion at all, and III keeps "no ... cymbals" as one of its rules for the finale. The finale's C-major arrival at IV 223 is fff with crash cymbals and bass drum. It is the event the symphony is built toward: the stop chord resolved, the trumpets' B→C, the first forte C major, the violins' C7. So the most distinctive sound of IV 223 has already been heard, at the same dynamic and in the same role, at the top of movement I's last climax. Movement I's own record is confused on this point. Its "Do not reuse" list includes "the first cymbal crash of the finale" as kept back, and the same line says "Its only cymbal crash is at 160."

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 227: in Man of Steel the drum ensemble "modestly appears" early, is absent from the first part of the three-part climax, and the score "rewards" the hero with it only in the last part.
- PDF 235, 237–238: Annihilation keeps its synthetic sound for the one reveal, so that the reveal "became clearly distinct".
- PDF 204: register and timbre are musical elements in their own right. Two fff crashes on two climaxes are the same sonic event, whatever the harmony under them.
- Dvořák 9, the corpus model nearest this cyclic plan, has one cymbal part in the whole symphony: a single stroke, mf, in the finale (percussion.notesF.ily, `R1*60 … cymc2\mf`, bar 64).

**Earlier protections.** The roll into 160 is protected as "sparing percussion" (cycle 108: "tam-tam 122, 175; cymbal roll into 160"). The crash came in at cycle 24 ("susp. cymbal roll 155 into crash 156", old numbering), with no cyclic reason given. c228 kept the "crash l.v. at 160", also without a reason. I keep the roll, so the protection stands. I change only the clash. That makes the percussion sparing across the symphony and not only inside movement I. Climax 3 keeps a cymbal colour that 82 and 122 do not have, so the movement's own percussion order (none – tam-tam – cymbal) is unchanged. In Casanelles's terms this is the "modest" early appearance.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy).**
```
recTwoPerc = \drummode { R1*12 | cymr1:32\pp\<^\markup { "Susp. cym." \italic "soft sticks" } | cymr4->\fff\laissezVibrer^\markup \italic "l.v." r2. | R1*6 | }
```
The roll swells into one soft-stick stroke on the suspended cymbal on the downbeat of 160, left to ring. The clashed pair is gone from movement I, so the percussion needs one player (tam-tam 122–127 and 175, suspended cymbal 159–160, more than 30 bars apart).
- The labels "1:"/"2:" are removed from dev.ily and coda.ily.
- score.ly front page reads "Percussion, 1 player: tam-tam, suspended cymbal", and the staff name is "Susp. cymbal / Tam-tam".
- The parts-gen.py header reads "Percussion: tam-tam, suspended cymbal".

### 2. Movement I's bar-138 stop chord is recorded wrongly in two coherence files (record only)

**Bars:** 138 (recOne).

**What is wrong.** coherence/mvt1.md ("Seeds", last-but-three line) says the 138 chord has "Tpt 1 A♭4; Tpt 2 F4". finale.md item 3 copies "Tpt 1 A♭4". In v250, and unchanged since v221, 138 has:

- Vln II D5/A♭5, violas F4/B4, Vc D3/A♭3, bass D.

c230's own report says "Tpt 1 B4 … match", so the A♭4 crept in when the record was written up (HISTORY cycle 230 has the same slip). **Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 221–222: shared, versioned material is what makes a team's score cohere, and a wrong shared record breaks that.

**Fix.** Do not change the score. The composer corrects mvt1.md to "Tpt 1 B4, Tpt 2 F4, Hn 1 A♭4" and tells the finale thread to correct item 3.

### 4. Movement II's quotation of the P head no longer has movement I's articulation (request to movement II)

**Bars:** I 15–16 (and 27, 131, 167) against II 63–64 (bSec, Trombone 1).

**What is wrong.** Since v239 every ff statement of the head in movement I slurs the chromatic descent into the repeated note and gives that note a portato dash:

`r8 g8( es'4.->) d'8( des'8 c'8 | c'4--) b8( c'8) aes4.-> g8`

The finale copied this at IV 200 and 248 (its v216). Movement II's quotation reads:

`r8 e8( c'4.->) b8 bes8 a8 | a4 gis8( a8) f4.-> e8`

It has no slur on B–B♭–A–A and no portato. Pitches and rhythm match (themecheck 35/35 and II's own table). II's comment c148 says its slurs follow "every ff statement of the P head in movement I", which was true before v239.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 200: a quotation works through what the listener already knows. The head's slurred chromatic fall into the repeated C is part of how it sounds in all of movement I's ff statements.

**Fix (movement II).**

`r8 e8( c'4.->) b8( bes8 a8 | a4--) gis8( a8) f4.->\> e8\p`

Movement I does not change. Movement II's cor anglais quotation of the S head (II 47–50) has no swell where movement I has one on the long note. It is a p solo marked "espr.", and the swells are dynamics, which the invariant leaves free, so it should stay as it is.
