## Findings

### 1. Climax 160: the held brass harmony is as loud as the tune (applied)

Bars 159–162. At 158 everything is ff. In 159 Tpt 3, Tbn 1·2, bass trombone and tuba crescendo with the tune instruments, and at 160 all of them are marked fff. On beat 2 of 160 the tune is A♭6 (Fl 1·2, Vln I), A♭5 (Ob 2, Tpt 1, Vln II) and A♭4 (Hn 1·2, Vc); the countermelody is C6 (Ob 1, Cl 1) and C5 (Hn 3·4). Under them Tpt 3 E♭4, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 E♭3, bass trombone A♭2 and tuba A♭1 hold whole notes at the same fff for two bars.

Evidence:
- Rimsky-Korsakov's appendix marks the brass a degree below the woodwind in the chords where the woodwind carries the top (chords 2 and 3, PDF 505: woodwinds fff, horns, trumpets, trombones and tuba ff; timpani ff).
- Ex. 206 (Sadko 177, PDF 369) is the louder restatement of Ex. 205. The woodwind and strings go to fff and the brass stay at ff, including the trumpets when they join the tune.
- Ex. 209 (Scheherazade III, PDF 375): over a tune in oboes, cor anglais and violins at ff, the trumpets, trombones, tuba, timpani and cymbal are marked f, and the heavy brass leave after one bar.
- PDF 41: at forte a horn is about half as strong as a trumpet or trombone, so the horns should be marked a degree higher. At 160 the tune's lower octave is in Hn 1·2 and the countermelody's lower octave in Hn 3·4. Both are at fff, the same level as trombones and tuba holding harmony.
- Adler (adler1 PDF 132) puts foreground, middleground and background in separate dynamic levels when the scoring alone does not balance them.
- The score already follows this rule in every other melodic tutti: f brass under ff tune at 27–32, 97–102, 131 (trumpets), 140 (trombones, tuba, Hn 3·4) and 167–170. Bar 160 is the only melodic tutti where it does not. (Bar 82 is also ungraded, but its grading is protected by an earlier cycle and I do not reopen it.)

### 2. Bar 170 stop chord: D4 in six instruments (no change)

The C-pedal vii°7 at 170 has D4 in Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 3, Tbn 1, viola and cello, and no F5. The appendix chords (PDF 505–507) spread doublings more evenly, and Adler (adler2 PDF 165) notes that unisons at pitch lose the individual colours. The D4s come from the parallel C→D motion of the tutti's inner parts at 169→170. That layout is the same at 27–32 on purpose (c090). The stop chords 26/138/170 are also on the cross-movement list. Moving Cl 2 to F4 would leave five D4s and gain little. It should stay as it is.

### 3. Climax 122: trumpets in the low middle register (no change)

At 122 the trumpets are A♭4, D4 and B3, below the trumpet register most of the appendix chords use (written G4–D5). They should stay. In the appendix the brass chord sits in the middle of the staff, and at 122 the brass chord is complete and close (G3–A♭4) under a woodwind stack that reaches A♭6, which matches chords 1–5. Raising the trumpets would give the dissonant 122 the brilliance that is now kept for 160, where Tpt 1 has A♭5 on the tune. That brilliance is one of the few ways 160 sounds above 122.
