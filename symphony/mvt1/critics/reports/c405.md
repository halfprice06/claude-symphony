## Findings

### 1. Horns 3·4 return to treble clef in the middle of the 137→138 gesture, which leaves the recapitulation on three and four ledger lines through 157. Applied.

**What is wrong.**
- **Exposition, page 3.** The Hn 3·4 staff goes into bass clef at 23 for Hn 4's low G2/F2. It keeps the bass clef through the stop chord at 26 and returns to treble at 27, after the crotchet rest on 26.4.
- **Recapitulation, page 13.** c393 copied the bass clef at 135 and meant to follow the exposition ("as at 23 and 26", c393 report). But it put `\clef treble` before 138, the stop chord itself. Hn 3 has no rest there: its A♭3 accent on 137 runs straight into the D4 of 138 under one crescendo. So the clef falls inside the gesture, not after it.
- **Ledger lines.** The change puts the stop chord's F3 on the third ledger line below the treble. All of page 14 then follows in treble:
  - Hn 4: F3 at 139–141 (three ledger lines), D3 at 141 (four), G3 at 142 and 145 (two).
  - Hn 3: C4/D♭4 (one) and B3 at 145–146 (one).
  - Page 15: B♭3/A♭3 over F3 at 156–157 (one to three).
- **In bass clef**, every one of these notes lies inside the stave or on the first ledger line above it.
- **Clefs that follow.** The next clef that is really needed is treble at 158, for the a2 G4–C5. Bar 158 starts page 16, so the change can fall on a system break with a warning clef.

**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 22: "Between beats, change the clef after rests" (the example shows the clef after a rest, "and not" before it). Also PDF 22: "In a passage of continuous playing, place a new clef between phrases … It enables the reader to see the pitch contour of the phrase intact."
- Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches." The present layout breaks the 135–138 phrase with a clef. It then uses up to four ledger lines for 20 bars, where the clef already in use would need at most one.
- Gould PDF 23: return to the commonest clef after rests longer than a system "provided this is suitable for the following entry". Hn 3·4 rests 148–155 inside one system (page 15), and the 156 entry (B♭3/F3) suits the bass clef. So the bass clef stays until the system break at 158, and Gould PDF 21 gives the warning clef at the end of 157.

**Protections answered.**
- c245 and c315 kept "four ledger lines at Hn 3·4 141" because a single low note did not justify an extra clef change (PDF 21). That reasoning came before c393 added the bass clef at 135. This fix adds no clef: the Hn 3·4 staff still has four clef changes in 135–167 (135 bass, 158 treble, 161 bass, 167 treble). It moves one of them from inside a phrase to a system break.
- c393's own aim, following the exposition, is kept: the stop chord 138 is now in bass clef like 26.
- The protected cello clefs at 46, 97 and 158, and the Hn 3·4 bass clef at 103–106, are not touched.

**Fix (applied).**
- sections/recOne.ily, recOneHnThree, bar 138: `c'4 aes2.-> | \clef treble d'2.->\f r4 |` becomes `c'4 aes2.-> | d'2.->\f r4 |`
