### 1. The coda gives the motto back its first colour, as the preface asks; keep (175–179)

Rimsky-Korsakov writes that "certain tone-colours" are "inseparable" from a work's ideas "and native to it from the hour of its birth" (PDF 20). The motto is born at 1–4 in bass clarinet and cellos with the basses an octave lower (B.cl/Vc G2–E♭3–D3–C3–D♭3–C3, Cb an octave down). At 175–179 the chorale's bass is the same three instruments on the same pitches (B.cl G2–E♭3–D3–C3/D♭3–C3, Vc sul G as at 1–3, Cb sul A as at 2–3), now under the trombones. Movement II then gives the motto's tail to the bass clarinet with the cor anglais at its own 32–34. The colour travels with the idea across the first two movements, and the cellos' sul G and the basses' sul A mark the identity in the parts. This is the cyclic link a listener hears without being told. Fix: none.

### 2. The editor's two recorded faults (PDF 18) were checked at three brass passages; one is present and protected

Steinberg reports two faults the author himself pointed out: a brass theme "not sufficiently prominent, the trombones being tacet" (Saltan 220), and a viola/cello counter doubled by woodwind that "will hardly be heard" when the brass keep their marks (Cockerel 233). I checked the brass theme statements of movement I against both:

- 69–72 (f codetta): the P head is in Hn 1·2 (E♭3–C4–B♭3…) with Cl 1·2, the cor anglais and Vln II at E♭4–C5, so the trombones' absence until 72 does not leave it weak; the S head above is Tpt 1 with Ob 1, Fl 1·2 and Vln I. Follows.
- 117–121: the motto is in the trombones themselves, ff, with Hn 1·2. Follows.
- 167–170: the Vc + Bsn 1 tenor line C4–E♭4 | E♭4–F4 | E♭4–C4 | D4 lies on the held E♭4 of Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 and on Hn 3's C4. This is the Cockerel 233 fault. It is protected (167–170 copies 27–32, which the finale quotes at pitch, part by part, at IV 199–204), and c370 already recorded it so it is not raised again without that context. No new evidence answers the protection. Fix: none.

### 3. The transfer rule holds at every join that crosses a section or a movement (PDF 125–126, Ex. 237)

Ex. 237 shows the rule exactly: each new part enters on the note the previous one ends on. Movement I's structural joins:

- 4→5: B.cl and cellos fade on C3; the basses carry C2 into 5 (same pitch class, the octave below), and the cor anglais starts the next motto statement from it.
- 7.4–9.3 → 9.4: C.a. G4 → Vln I G4 (same pitch).
- 174→175: Bsn 1 G3 → Tbn 1 G3 (same pitch), so the chorale grows out of the cadential 6/4.
- 180→181: Tbn 1 C4 → Hn 3 C4 (same pitch, overlapping one beat).
- 182 → II 1: Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3, Hn 3 C4 tied across; movement II ends by passing Cl 1's C5 to Vln I's C5 at its 89 in the same way.

The device is used for the movement's three thresholds (introduction to Allegro line, coda to chorale, movement I to II). Fix: none.

### 4. Movement I still keeps back what the finale needs; checked against v470 full.midi

The preface counts trumpets that stand out "above the rest of the orchestra" as a student's stumbling block (PDF 22), and the echo paragraph names muted brass as the echo colour (PDF 128). Movement I spends neither; the finale uses both (trumpet solo at IV 51, muted trumpet with cor anglais at IV 171). The reserve limits hold in v470: Vln I and flutes top at A♭6 (MIDI 92), Vln II at C6 (84), tuba at F2 (41); Tpt 1·2's last note is B4 at 170; no clashed cymbal; no root-position C-major chord in full brass at forte; harp absent from the C-major recapitulation of S at 147–155. Theme record 35/35. Fix: none.

### 5. Considered and rejected: harp under the C-major second theme at 147–155

The cyclic question: the exposition's S (47–55) has Hn 1 and cellos over harp; the recapitulation's S in C major (147–155) has Hn 1 and violas with no harp, which joins only at 156. Restoring the harp at 147 would make the rhyme exact. I reject it for two reasons from the reading and the cycle:

- The preface lists "a passion for the harp, using it in every possible chord" as the student's second phase (PDF 21–22). The harp now marks the exposition's S, the climax's approach (156–157), the dissolution (162–166) and the last chord, and nothing else. Adding 147–155 would put it under both statements of S.
- Movement II's theme A is Hn 1 over harp (II 7–14), and mvt1.md (cycle 310) asks movement I not to repeat that colour close to the attacca. A harp-accompanied horn S at 147 would be heard about three minutes before II 7 and weaken it.

Applied in scratch copy: no.
