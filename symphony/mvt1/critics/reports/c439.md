## Findings

v439 differs from v429 only in five bow marks (68.3 Vln I up-bow, 69 Vln I and Vc down-bow, 19 Vla up-bow, 171 solo Vc up-bow) and the stamp. I checked each in the PDF (score pages 7 and 18): they print cleanly, sit where the bow naturally falls, and change no note, dynamic or slur. Nothing got worse between v429 and v439.

The score therefore stands or falls on the same comparison every earlier holistic critic has made, now against a different master. I found no change worth making. The findings below are the points of comparison with Dvořák and the reading, each with a recommendation to keep, and the reason.

### 1. Exposition build (15-27): keep

Dvořák opens with the first theme pp in two clarinets over low strings (score page 1, PDF 11) and builds by adding instruments to the ff tutti (Grandioso, page 3, PDF 13). He keeps the solo colour for the intimate statement and the doublings for the loud one, which is Rimsky's rule (PDF 66-67: "doubling and mixed timbres is naturally more frequent in loud passages than in soft ones"). v439 does the same thing: Vln I alone at 15-18, the build 19-26, the hammered tutti at 27 (protected as the "graded build 15-27"). The short accented tutti chords at 27-32 under the melody are Rimsky's Ex. 99 type (PDF 88, viewed PDF 277: Snegourotchka, every group on accented quavers). A conductor who knows the Dvořák would find this pacing familiar. No change.

### 2. Second theme: horn + cellos at 46-52 against Dvořák's bare horn solo: keep

Dvořák's second theme is one horn, "1. Solo pp molto espressivo, Un poco sostenuto", over ppp strings with no wind or string doubling (bar 57, score page 6, PDF 16). It is one of the best-known horn solos in the repertoire because nothing doubles it. Rimsky gives the reason (PDF 66): "Phrases or melodies demanding diversity of expression alone should be entrusted to solo instruments of simple timbres." v439 doubles Hn 1 with the cellos at 46-52, Rimsky's best-blended soft pairing (PDF 79), which is warmer but less individual.

I would recommend the bare horn if movement I stood alone. It does not. coherence/mvt1.md (seeds, bars 46-52) records that movement II's theme A is Hn 1 over harp, kept fresh by dropping the cellos; a bare horn S here would take that colour first. c429 protected the 47 horn + cello unison for this reason, and I cannot answer it. Keep.

### 3. Recapitulation and coda: Dvořák's major apotheosis versus the soft attacca: keep

Dvořák recapitulates the second theme first, ff in B major, full tutti, and ends the movement ff with the brass. v439 recapitulates S softly in C major (147-166), never confirms C major at forte, and ends ppp attacca. This is the largest audible difference from the master. It is deliberate, and earlier cycles have kept it for stated reasons: the finale's C-major arrival at its 222-223 and the "Do not reuse" list (major brass chorale, C-major S with counter, no forte C-major cadence; coherence/mvt1.md). Rimsky's Ex. 97 (PDF 274: brass p< sf on a fermata chord) and Ex. 120 (PDF 292-295: wind harmonic basis growing from pp to sf) show the forte gestures the finale needs; using them here would take them from the finale. Keep.

### 4. Same-colour seconds in the oboes (134.1, 141.4, 159.3-4) and the bassoon seventh (39-40): keep

Rimsky PDF 90 rule 4 asks for concords between instruments of one kind, "specially ... for the oboe", "except when discords are to be emphasised". A scan of full.midi for seconds and sevenths sustained a beat or more between paired winds found:
- Ob 1 F4 / Ob 2 G4 at 134.1 (one beat, ff).
- Ob 1 D5 / Ob 2 C5 at 141.4 (one beat; both are chord tones of iiø7 over D).
- Ob 1 F5 / Ob 2 G5 at 159.3-4 (two beats, ff, climax approach).
- Bsn 1 A♭3 / Bsn 2 B♭2 at 39-40 (a seventh, p< f, V7 of E♭ over the B♭ pedal).

The first two are one-beat passing moments inside a doubled tutti line, where Rimsky says progression attracts less attention (PDF 98). The 159 second is the stressed discord his exception allows, and 156-166 pitches are protected. The bassoon seventh is a wide seventh, not a second, on the pedal recorded as a seed of the finale's transition (coherence/mvt1.md, bars 37-44). None is worth a change.

### 5. Development and climaxes against Dvořák's: keep

Dvořák's development is short and lyrical, turning the first theme into a slow A-flat minor song for the soloist, with one tutti outburst. v439's development (77-130) is denser: a head dialogue, the fugato, S in B minor, both heads combined, and the dominant pedal with the fff 122 climax. It is closer to a symphonic development than to Dvořák's concerto one, which is correct for a symphony. The brass writing at 117-121 (trombone and horn unison, ff, chromatic motto) matches Rimsky's Ex. 74 and 77 (PDF 245, 248-249), and the wind harmonic basis under the 122 climax follows Ex. 75 (PDF 246: wood-wind a3 with trumpets on repeated notes over the trombones). No change.
