# Critic 466: playability, instrument by instrument (brass, third pass)

Version reviewed: v466. Lens 6 (playability). Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 9 "Introduction to Brass Instruments" and ch. 10 "Individual Brass Instruments" (adler1 PDF 282–329, adler2 PDF 1–13). This is the third pass, after c266 and c356.

I read every text page in the range. I rendered every page with music examples or charts at 110 dpi (adler1 PDF 284–292, 296–297, 301–311, 313–329; adler2 PDF 1–12) and looked at each one. I re-rendered the horn range and register charts (Ex. X-4, X-5) and the tuba charts (Ex. X-85, X-86) at 250 dpi, because the OCR loses all the notes. Pages 293–295, 298–300 and 312 have only text or drawings of mutes and instruments.

Method. I rebuilt v466 in /tmp/critic-466/work and read every brass note from full.midi (one staff per player), timed against the tempo map. For each player I listed: (a) range; (b) every span of sound of 8 s or more; (c) every stretch with no rest of a full bar, with its share of sounding time; (d) every entry after a bar or more of rest, with written pitch and level. I then read each flagged place in the section files (slurs, ties, `\breathe`, dynamics) and in the 11 brass parts from build-parts.sh (rendered and read).

## Findings

v466's brass writing obeys every rule in this reading. c266 and c356 already added the breaths the chapter asks for. This pass looked for anything those passes did not measure, including changes made since v357. It also asked whether a master's brass writing would do something here that this score does not. I found three places worth recording and no change that meets the bar. All three findings are "keep".

### 1. Hn 4, 111–142: 32 bars with no full bar's rest (keep)

**Bars and problem.** Hn 4 is the busiest brass player in the movement (209 s of sound; Hn 3 206 s, Hn 1 174 s). From 111 to 142 it never has a full bar's rest: 32 bars, about 66 s, sounding 93 % of the time. The stretch covers the wedge (p cresc.), the G pedal at ff–fff (117–124), the retransition pedal (126–130, pp< to mf<), the low P theme at f (131–134), the hemiola mp< (135–137), the stop chord (138) and the f block (139–142). No other brass part has a stretch over 17 bars. c393 created it: before v394, Hn 4 rested 135–137 and the stretch was split into 111–134 and 138–142.

**Evidence.** Adler asks for "frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath" (adler1 PDF 292). He also says playing loud takes more breath than playing soft (same page). On PDF 303 the warning is narrower: "most taxing to play in the higher register all the time, so periodic rests are most welcome".

**Why keep.** The stretch lies at written C3–D4 apart from the a2 notes at 158 (not in this span). In Adler's terms (Ex. X-5) that is the "deep and solid" register, not the high register PDF 303 warns about. The part has a release every two to four bars: quarter rests at 112.4, 115.4, 119.4, 123.4, 125.4, 137.4 and 138.4, a half rest at 132.3, and printed breaths after 127 (c156) and 134 (c393). The longest unbroken blowing is about 8 s (120–123.3 at ff–fff, and 128–132.2 at mf–f). PDF 292's breath rule is met. The loud part, 117–124, is eight bars with two rests. Adler also calls four horns a "wonderful accompaniment choir" (adler1 PDF 312), and a low horn holding a pedal through a long Romantic crescendo is normal practice for that role.

The only places where a full bar's rest could go are protected. 127–130 is the retransition pedal and crescendo (c156, c388, c368 do-not-touch). 131–134 is the low P theme layout (c361, c427). 135–137 has c393's V6/5 G3: removing it brings back the bare VII5/3 that c393 fixed (A&S PDF 243–245), and the hemiola accents are protected (c438). Taking Hn 4 off F3/G3 at 133–134 would leave the f horn chord under the low theme with three horns. That breaks the c174/c264 layout and gains a rest that the breath at 134 already provides. Keep. I record the figure so a later change in 111–142 does not lengthen it further.

### 2. Hn 1, 111–123: written E♭5–G5 soft, then the motto ff–fff (keep)

Hn 1 enters at 111 on written E♭5, p, after four bars' rest. It rises to written G5 at 113–114 (sounding C5) in a crescendo, falls to written E♭5 at 116, then rests a quarter. It drops a minor ninth to written D4 for the brass motto at 117 (ff, breath before 119.4, c186) and reaches written E♭5 fff at 122. The whole stretch is 13 bars, 98 % sounding.

Adler warns about soft high attacks (adler1 PDF 284), the high register held for long (PDF 303) and wide leaps (PDF 304). But the soft entry is written E♭5, inside the "bright and heroic" band of Ex. X-5, not the "brilliant and loud" top. Only two bars touch written G5, the band's upper edge. The minor-ninth drop comes after a rest, onto a low, secure note, and falling leaps are the easy direction. Hn 1 then rests 124–130. c356 checked the same notes (its row 14) and kept them. No change.

### 3. Trombones, 80–81: low G2 pickup and the motto head (keep)

At 80 both tenor trombones play the head G2–E♭3 (f, eighth pickup). At 81 they play it again an octave higher. Adler says the trombone's low register articulates "a bit sluggish and a bit heavy" (adler2 PDF 3) and calls the register below about C3 "dark and rather nondescript" (Ex. X-69, adler1 PDF 329). The eighth lasts 0.24 s at crotchet 126. G2 is fourth position and E♭3 third, a one-position move. Ex. X-71's problem is the VII–I alternation, and that does not happen here. The darker first statement and brighter answer suit the head dialogue of 77–83 (horns, then trombones low, then trombones high, then the ff head at 82). No change.

### The master question

I also asked whether a master would make the brass more vivid here. The chapter's colour devices are sff>pp< attacks (Ex. IX-9, X-87), stopped horn sf>p (PDF 308), mutes (IX-15), bells up and cuivré (PDF 308), and glissandi. In this score each one either falls on a protected passage (117 f start, 122 fff, the 26/138/170 stop chords, "no mutes or stopped horn" c408) or belongs to a later style than the models (bells up, horn glissando: Mahler, Strauss, Barber in Adler's examples). The places where this reading counts most already match the masters it quotes:

- the soft unmuted trombone chorale (Ex. X-80, Brahms 1/iv, p dolce) at 175–179;
- the four-horn unison call ff molto espr. (Ex. X-12, Don Juan) at 41;
- the horn solo with soft tonguing (Ex. X-24) at 13–14 and 47–52.

I found no change I could support from the reading as a real improvement. No change.
