## Findings

### 1. Cellos leap to D5 at ff in tempo at 101–102 (applied)

Bars 101–102 (development, the B-minor second theme, ♩ = 126). Since cycle 231 the cellos double Vln II and the cor anglais at pitch on the ff statement 97.4–102. In bars 97–100 the line lies between F♯3 and G4, on the D and A strings, which is what cycle 231 wanted. In 101–102 it goes above the cello's practical limit: B4 and A♯4 at 101, then F♯4 to D5 by a minor sixth at 102, a half note, then C♯5 and G♯4, before the line falls a tenth to F3 at 103 with a change from tenor to bass clef.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26 fixes the cello's practical top at A4 (the image shows A above the third ledger line in bass clef) and says higher notes "should only be used with caution, that is to say when they are of long value, in tremolando, slow, flowing melodies, in not too rapid sequence of scales, and in passages of repeated notes. Skips should always be avoided." Bar 102 is the case the passage rules out: a leap into the high register, ff, in a fast tempo, for a full section. Cycle 186 accepted the theme's leaps at 51–54 only because they are "slow cantabile at p/mp", Rimsky-Korsakov's own exception; that exception does not cover 102. The cellos add nothing at 101–102 that Vln II and the cor anglais do not already give at that pitch, so the intonation risk buys no extra sound.

Fix (dev.ily, devVc, inside the existing `\transpose c cis`): `bes2--(\> a2\!) | f4( des'2--\< c'8\! g8) } | \clef bass` (was `bes'2--(\> a'2\!) | f'4( des''2--\< c''8\! g'8) }`). The cellos stay on the tune for its last phrase, an octave under Vln II and the cor anglais, so the statement ends in three octaves (Vln I, Vln II + C.a., Vc), the violins-and-cellos-in-octaves layout that cycle 231 cited (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 60–61). The rising sixth is kept (F♯3–D4). The slur boundary at 100→101 is where the octave changes, and the line now falls a minor third into 103's F3 instead of a tenth. Notes, slurs and dynamics of every other part are unchanged.

This partly undoes cycle 231 (protected "97–102 brass counter, viola arpeggio, Vln II sul G, B.tbn F♯3"; none of these is touched). Cycle 231's reasons hold for 97–100, where the cellos keep the at-pitch doubling and the A-string intensity it cited (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 56). Its reasons do not require the cellos to go above A4: the octave layout with the violins that it cited from PDF 60–61 is still there in 101–102.

Check: full.midi rebuilt; the all-pairs voice-leading list changes in one place only, bar 101 beat 3: Hn 4 B3→A♯3 against the cellos is now a unison instead of an octave. It is the same doubling (Hn 4 and the tune share B3–A♯3), not a new parallel.

### 2. Timpani retuning B♮→A♭ printed at 111, four bars before the A♭ roll (applied)

Bars 97–115. The A♭ drum is tuned to B♮ for one stroke at 97 and then is not used until the A♭ roll at 115 (mf<). The instruction "muta B♮ in A♭" is printed at 111, which leaves four bars of Allegro, about 7.6 s, during the wedge crescendo (mp to ff). The other three retunings (34, 44, 84) are printed in the bar after the drum's last stroke.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 48: "a good timpanist, having three kettle-drums at his command, will be able to tune at least one of them during a pause of some length." The pause is 13 bars long (98–110), and its soft second half (103–110, f then p) is the best place to tune a minor third down and check the pitch quietly. Printed at 111, the instruction asks for the retune just as the orchestra starts the long crescendo, which makes the check harder; a player on hand-tuned or older drums would not finish in four bars. It also matches the other three mutas in this part, each printed in the bar after the drum's last stroke.

Fix (dev.ily, devTimp): `\transpose c cis { bes,4\ff r4 r2 } | R1^\markup \small "muta B♮ in A♭" | R1*13 |` (was `\transpose c cis { bes,4\ff r4 r2 | R1*4 | R1 } | R1*8 | R1^\markup \small "muta B♮ in A♭" |`). Same bar count. In the timpani part the instruction now sits over bar 98, followed by the rests 99–102, 103–110, 111–112 and the Vc cue at 113–114 (checked in partsout/parts-23-timpani.pdf, still 2 pages).
