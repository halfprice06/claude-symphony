### 2. The Picardy endings at 8 and 182 should stay soft (keep; no change)

Ex. 23-7 (PDF 392) shows the Picardy third at its strongest: Don Giovanni's D major arriving ff. Ex. 23-1b (PDF 386) shows the delayed major returning ff in full orchestra. Movement I uses ♯3 only softly and only at section ends: Vln II E♮4 at 8 (pp), and the violas' lone E♮4 at 182 (ppp, the only E♮ in the last chord). That is the right choice for the cycle. The ff major arrival belongs to the finale's 223, where the trumpets' B4→C5 (IV 222→223) resolves movement I's last trumpet pitch (B4 at 170) and the violas take E4 at movement I's pitch (IV 219–223). A forte Picardy at 182 would use that up and would also break the attacca into movement II.

The reading also explains why the attacca join works. In A♭, movement I's last chord is C major, which is III♯ of A♭ (secondary mixture, PDF 393). Movement II's bar 2 leaves it by ♯5→♮5 (violas E4→E♭4), stated in one voice, which is the acceptable form (Ex. 23-12b, PDF 394; Points 6–7, PDF 395). Keep the five tied parts and the violas' E4.

### 4. The cross relation G♯5 → G4 at 78.3–78.4 is deliberate (keep)

On beat 3 of 78, Cl 1 G♯5 is the top voice over the E2 bass (E major). On beat 4, Hn 1·2 enter on G4 (Vc G3), and at 79 Ob 1 and Vln I take G5–F♯5–F5–E5. The chromatic succession G♯→G♮ is split between voices and passes through the top part, which is the "avoid" pattern of Ex. 23-12a (PDF 394). The reading allows it where it carries the movement's major/minor conflict (Brahms 3/i, Ex. 23-13, PDF 394–395). Here it carries the symphony's: E major turned to E minor is exactly the scherzo's opening (Hn 1 G♯4 at III 1–2, Cl 1·2 E minor with G♮ at III 7–8; scherzo.md), and the G5–F♯5–F5–E5 that follows is the scherzo's theme A bar 2 at pitch. The G♯ itself is reached as a common tone from A♭5, so III♯ is not approached by a direct ♮5–♯5 (Points 6, PDF 395). This is protected by c230/c262 and recorded as a seed. Keep.

### 5. Bar 115→116 after c382: the D♭/D♮ succession is in one voice (keep)

c382 changed the inner voices at 115. The bass falls D♭2→C2 at 115→116 while the top line reaches D6 at 116 (Fl 1·2, Vln I, Ob 1 D5, Tpt 1 D5). Taken alone, D♭ in the bass against D♮ on top is an outer-voice cross relation. However, Hn 3 and Tpt 3 move D♭4→D4 inside their own lines, and the top line itself runs D♭6–C6–D♭6–E♭6–D6. So this is the doubling case of Ex. 23-12b (PDF 394), which the book calls unobjectionable. The French sixth over C at 116 (A♭–C–D–F♯) then goes to the G pedal at 117: Tbn 1 A♭3→G3 (♭6→5) and F♯3→G3 in Bsn 1, Hn 4 and the cellos. No change.
