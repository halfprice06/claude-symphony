## Findings

### F1. Bars 15 and 17: the accompaniment attacks together with the motto's pickup (applied)

**What is wrong.** The first theme opens with the motto: G4 rising a sixth to E♭5 (15), then G4 to F5 (17). Vln II and violas play a syncopated pattern, `r8 x8~ x4`, which attacks on the second quaver of the bar. That is the same quaver as Vln I's pickup. At 15, Vln II plays the pickup G4 at the unison, and the violas' E♭4 sounds with it, so the sixth's goal is already in the chord before the melody reaches it. At 17, Vln II's A♭4 sounds a semitone above the pickup G4. In 16 and 18 the same figure works: it attacks only where the tune holds (c''4 at 16, b'4 at 18).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135, on Brahms 3/iii (Ex. V-7): the accompaniment "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the [anacrusis] figure undisturbed", so the conductor can take "soloistic rubato" on the anacrusis. PDF 132 and 142 make the same point: foreground and background are told apart by rhythm. Corpus: Dvořák 7/i, bars 1–8 (`reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Viola.ly`, `-Contrabasso.ly`). There the viola and cello theme enters with its semiquaver anacrusis over a sustained bass tremolo, and Vln II's tremolo starts only at bar 8. Nothing attacks with the pickup.

**Fix (applied).** In bars 15 and 17, Vln II and violas wait until beat 2-and. They then attack under the tune's held E♭5 or F5 and hold to the barline. Bars 16 and 18 are unchanged.
```
tOneVlnTwo: r4 r8 g'8~\mf g'2 | (16 unchanged) | r4 r8 aes'8~ aes'2 | (18 unchanged)
tOneVla:    r4 r8 es'8~\mf es'2 | (16 unchanged) | r4 r8 <d' f'>8~ <d' f'>2 | (18 unchanged)
```
The cello's half notes and the double basses' pulse keep the texture going. The theme stays strings-only (protected c149), and the beat-2 accents on the head (c148) are untouched. I kept the viola's held A♭4 at 19. It is the counterline that marks the consequent (row 7), and moving it would put its first attack on the tune's accented A♭5.

### F2. Bars 85–87: the fugato subject is not heard alone (applied)

**What is wrong.** The cello's subject (85–87) is accompanied by pizzicato double-bass roots: D2 on the downbeat of 85, before the subject begins on the second quaver, then G–E♭ and G–B♭. So the exposition starts with two voices, and the pizzicato is the first sound of the fugato.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 145: "In a fugal exposition the subject heard alone at the beginning carries added weight and makes the listener constantly aware of it." In Adler's examples the bass never adds a voice of its own under the first entry. Vivaldi, Ex. V-21 (PDF 144): the continuo plays the solo cello's line. Weber, Ex. V-23 (PDF 147): cellos and basses play the subject in octaves while the other strings rest. Bartók, Ex. V-24 (PDF 148): the violas begin alone. The subject's line already implies its harmony (D–B♭–A–A♭–G, G–F♯–G–E♭–D, G–A–B♭–B♮), and its anacrusis opening (PDF 145) carries it.

**Fix (applied).** In `devCb`, 85–89 become `R1*5`, and "arco" is removed at 90 because the basses were last arco at 84. The basses come back at 90 (`aes,2\mf\< g,2`) exactly as before. No entry, subject or countersubject note changes (c142/c177/c184 protections kept). The parts keep the same page counts, and the double-bass part still has cover + 3 pages, ending on an even page.

### F3. Bars 156–157: the viola arpeggio crosses the tune's lower octave (applied)

**What is wrong.** In the poco animando before the climax, the tune is in Vln I (D6–C6) and an octave lower in Vln II (D5, then C5–B♭4–A♭4–G4). The violas' rising arpeggio `r8 d' f' bes' d''4` reaches D5 at 156, in unison with Vln II. At 157, `r8 c' f' aes' c''4` reaches C5 on beat 3, a step above Vln II's B♭4. In 157 it also doubles the cello arpeggio exactly an octave higher (C3–F3–A♭3–C4 under C4–F4–A♭4–C5). So the background is above one melody voice and in octaves with another.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135: when foreground and background are "instruments of similar color", keep the background out of the melody's register. If Brahms's violins and violas "had been scored an octave higher, they would have attracted too much attention to the background". PDF 144, item 3: separate theme and accompaniment by register. PDF 136 on coupling: a background voice moving in thirds or sixths with another is the standard alternative to doubling it.

**Fix (applied).** The arpeggio starts a third lower and peaks under Vln II.
```
recTwoSync bars 1-2:  r8 bes8(\mp\< d'8 f'8 bes'4) r4 | r8 aes8( c'8 f'8 aes'4) r4 |
```
The chords are the same (B♭ at 156, F minor at 157), so the ♭VII→v chain that c182 protected for its harmony is unchanged. The violas now move in sixths with the cello (A♭3/C3, C4/F3, F4/A♭3, A♭4/C4) and sit under Vln II's B♭4. The vl.txt diff is empty.

I did not apply the same change at 60–61, where the violas reach D♭5 over Vln II's B♭4 and G♭4. There the viola does not double another line in octaves and does not land in unison with the tune. The bar is also part of c182's protected "♭VII–v7 join at 60" and of c167's note on 56–68. The recapitulation case is worse because of the unison at 156 and the octave doubling at 157.

### F4. Bars 88–90: the countersubject lies above the second entry (keep as it is)

The cello's countersubject (C4–G4) lies above the violas' and Bsn 1's subject (G3–E♭4). Adler PDF 144 prefers registral separation to "marking different dynamics", which is how c197 balanced it (cellos mp, entry mf). The registral fix, cellos an octave lower at 88–89, would make the subject's tail B3 leap a major seventh down to C3. That breaks the cello's B♮→C resolution into its own countersubject. The subject is also doubled by Bsn 1, the lower voice is the subject, and from 91 the voices are in Adler's low-to-high order. Keep the bars as they are.

### F5. Bars 97–102: viola arpeggio in the register of the tune's lower octave (keep as it is)

The violas' quaver arpeggio (B3–F♯4) runs through Vln II sul G and the cor anglais (B3–G4). Adler PDF 135 would place it elsewhere, but there is no other place for it. One octave down is below the viola's range at B2. One octave up runs into Vln I and Fl 1 (D5–B5). c184 already removed the clarinets that thickened it. This is the cleanest available layout.
