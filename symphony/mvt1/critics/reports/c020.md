## Overall verdict
The cyclic design is at a master's level. The planting is denser than critic 10 recorded:
- The second theme's opening interval (E♭ up to C, then falling) is planted three times before the theme arrives: the horn call at 13 (`es4( c'2~ | c'4 b2.)`), the horns at 41–42 (`es4( | c'4 bes2.)`) and at 43–44 (`es'4( c''4~ | c''2 bes'2)`).
- The theme then enters at 46–47 with the motto's exact rhythm: a quarter pickup, then a dotted half tied to an eighth and an eighth.
- In minor the head becomes the motto itself: at 97–98 F♯↑D–C♯–B, and at 167–168 G↑E♭–D–C.
- In the recapitulation the pickup g→e' at 138–139 (and violins at 147–148) is the motto in major, which is the form the finale's chorale needs.
- A♭ (♭VI, the key of movement II) is the goal of every crisis: 47, 82, 114 (a♭'''), 132, 156.

## Findings

### 1. Is the second theme memorable enough to carry the cyclic return? Yes. Do not rewrite 47–55; fix what later movements quote.
Critic 19 is right that 49–54 changes rhythm in almost every bar: seven different bar-rhythms in nine bars. Tchaikovsky 6/i 89–100 states its two-bar head four times in twelve bars (89–90, 91–92, 95–96, 99–100). Dvořák 9/i 149–156 repeats its long–short cell every two bars.

But memory works on the head, and this score repeats its head as often as the models do:
- the head rhythm (`2.~ 8 8`) at 47, 56, 60, 64;
- ff in the codetta at 69;
- in the development at 98 and 103–105, where it is sequenced up three times;
- at 139 and 148;
- in the coda at 167.

That is ten statements, plus the three plantings above. Jakubowski et al. found that tunes that stick have a common global contour combined with something distinctive. The head has both: a stepwise fall with one leap of a sixth. The bars that are hard to retain (49–54) never need to return.

**Fix:** none in this score. Cycles 11, 14 and 18 protect 47–55, and nothing here gives new evidence against that. For movements II and III, quote only the anacrusis and two bars, always in this rhythm, changing only mode, key and scoring, as Tchaikovsky 5 does with its motto:
- major: `g4 | e'2.~ e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2 g'4` (as at 138–140);
- minor: `g4 | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4 aes'2 g'4` (as at 166–168).

The movement III chorale should be the major form. It is then both the motto and the second theme, so one return pays off both.

### 2. Bars 171–175 (coda bars 9–13): plant the brass chorale that movement III will bring back
**What is wrong.** The plan's main cyclic payoff is "the motto in C major as the brass chorale climax of III". In movement I the brass never sound the motto as a harmonised chorale. At 109–113 it is a unison and octave line under the climax, and at 171–175 Tbn 1 and B.tbn play it in bare octaves. A finale chorale can then only recall a melody, not a sound.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 335–339: the coda gives the trombones, bass trombone and horns a soft four-part chorale in half and dotted-half notes. That sonority is what the listener carries out of the movement.
- In Rachmaninoff 2/ii the motto returns near the end as a quiet brass "chant", so the brass colour is already associated with the motto before the finale.
- Brahms 1/iv plants its trombone chorale pp in the introduction before the ff coda (not in the corpus).

**Fix (tested).** Give Tbn 2 an inner voice between the existing outer lines. Tbn 1 (`g1 | es'1 | d'1 | c'2 des'2 | c'1`) and B.tbn are unchanged. The result is a three-part pp chorale: i6/4 – i6 – ii° – i – N – i.

`codaTbnTwo`, replacing its `R1*12 |`:
```
R1*4 | es1\pp | g1 | f1 |
g2 f2 | es1 | R1*3 |
```
Voice leading:
- Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 are in sixths at 172–173 and at 174 (beat 3) into 175. Tbn 2 and B.tbn are in thirds.
- 173→174 moves in contrary motion, and there are no fifths.
- Tbn 2 doubles chord tones the strings already have (Vla aes at 173, Vln II f' at 174 beat 3).

Tbn 1 and 2 share a staff, and both are marked `\pp` at 171, so the dynamics are equal. Nothing else doubles the new line. The motto canon, the augmented line and the viola ending are untouched. The fix adds harmony and does not undo any earlier fix.

### 3. Critic 10's changes: both worked; keep them
- **The E♮→E♭ turn at 162.** The C-major cadence 159–162 now turns into C minor through the chromatic voice that frames the movement: E♮ at 8, E♭ in 162, E♮ again at 178. The coda's ff is caused, not spliced on. It is the same agent Dvořák 9/i uses (chromatic voice leading before the minor return), done in two beats.
- **The cor anglais motto at 167–170.** It brings back the intro's answering colour (5–8) and separates the motto from the cello's minor second theme. At 170 the CA's c'' meets the cello's c'' in unison. I accept that: the two themes converge on one note before the cello goes on to b'.

### 4. Cycles 11–19 changed the arc for the better; there are no regressions
- **A♭ at 82 (cycle 13).** The development now opens on the exposition's last key, and the ♭VI thread (82, 114, 132, 156) leads straight to movement II.
- **Retransition 119–122 (cycle 19).** The cello's measured accelerando on G gives the recapitulation an audible cause. The motto entries above it (Cl 1 120–121, Vla 121–122, Vln II and Bsn 1 at 122) use the second theme's quarter–half–quarter rhythm, which fuses the two ideas just before the return.
- **Pizzicato under 47–55 and 139–147.** This makes the C-major recapitulation enter as a remembered sound (sul tasto, plucked bass) that grows to the fff at 156. The order of hope, peak on ♭VI, and reversal is clear.
- **Coda tenor line 163–166.** It is neutral for the arc and harmless.

### 5. Does the listener leave with the motto in their ear? Yes
The last twelve bars state it three times:
- the CA at 167–170, in the original rhythm;
- the low strings, bass clarinet and trombones at 171–175, augmented;
- the violins at 173–177, augmented, ending C–D♭–C.

The ff T1 head at 163–165 is the motto in diminution. No change is needed.

### 6. Warning for movement II (no change here)
The plan says movement II, in A♭, transforms the motto "into a lyrical line". The second theme already is that transformation, in the same key. If II opens with a lyrical motto in A♭ it will sound like a reprise of 47. Better options:
- start II from the E♮→E♭ link (critic 10's note);
- build its melody from the motto's chromatic tail (E♭–D–D♭–C at 109–113, transposed) or from the inverted sixth;
- quote the second-theme head only in II's middle section, in a remote key.

### 7. Nothing severe outside the lens
Slurs that open in one section close in the next (138→139, 46→47) and are valid.

## Do not touch
These are at a master's level:
- the planting of the E♭→C sixth at 13, 41–44 and 46–47;
- the second theme's head rhythm, identical to the motto, in every appearance (47, 98, 139, 148, 167);
- the minor-sixth form of the head in minor (97–98, 167–168);
- the E♮/E♭ frame at 8, 162 and 178;
- the cor anglais motto at 167–170;
- the ♭VI thread at 82, 114 and 156;
- 47–55, as written.
