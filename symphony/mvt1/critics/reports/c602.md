### Kept after checking (not findings)

1. **121.3–4, bare C–G 6/4 before the fff at 122.** The beat reduction shows only C and G on beats 3–4, with every upper part on G (Eb6 → G6 → Ab6 in the top). The violas and Cl 2 play triplets C4–E♭4–G4 under it (dev.ily, devVla, bar 121), so the 3rd sounds on each triplet's second note. PDF 76 is met. I list it so that a later pass using an on-beat reduction does not report it.

2. **72.1, viola D♭4 to E♭4 (d5 → P5 with Hn 3 G3 → A♭3).** The violas play a double stop D♭4 → C4/E♭4. The 7th D♭ falls to C4 in the violas and in Bsn 1, and the leading tone G3 rises to A♭3 in Hn 3. The E♭4 is the chord 5th, entering in the same double stop. The 7th is resolved and not doubled (PDF 76). Keep.

3. **151.4, cor anglais B♭4 → A4 against the S tune E4 → D4 (Hn 1, Vc; d5 → P5).** The d5 E–B♭ belongs to V6/5 of IV. B♭ resolves to A as it should. The tune's E passes down to C, and F arrives in Vln II at 152.1. The P5 D–A lasts one quaver on beat 4, inside the cor anglais's turn B♭–A–G–A. This is the passing case that PDF 83 does not cover ("normally avoided"), in a free texture with five real lines. The tune is the protected S chain, and c378 shaped the cor anglais fills. Changing the turn would cost more than it gains. Keep.

4. **135.1, Cl 1 G3 → G4 against Hn 3 D4 → C4 (P5 → P5 by contrary motion).** At 134.3–4 the clarinets double the G of the bass with bassoons, trombones and cellos. At 135 Cl 1 leaves that doubling and begins the pad of the 3/4 group, which at 23.1 enters fresh. This is the change of the number of parts that PDF 84 allows in instrumental music, not two independent voices moving in fifths. The pad and its top line are protected (23–26/135–138, c421, c422). Keep.

5. **146.3, the V7 thins to B3 (Hn 3) and D5 (Ob 1) for one beat.** Root and 7th drop out under the poco rit. before the C-major S head, and the 7th F4 (Cl 1, Vln II) stops without resolving in register. The resolution comes from Hn 1 and the violas, which take E4 at 147 after the G3 pickup. A one-beat caesura before a new theme is the "special effect" PDF 76 allows, and the F→E resolution is heard from G3–E4 one octave below the stopped F4. c222, c312 and c582 kept 145–147. Keep.

6. **Octave to unison by contrary motion at 40.1 (Hn 4 F3 → D4 into the violas' D4), 43.1 (Hn 2 joins Cl 2 on G4), 134.2 (Ob 1 joins the tune's D5) and 154.4 (the violas' upper octave of the tune joins Hn 1 on F4).** In each one a doubling part joins a line it then doubles. The first three are inside one harmony (PDF 87, point 6). 154.4 is the protected recapitulation cadence 154→155 (c312, c402). Keep.

7. **The other d5 → P5 hits.** 27.1: the stop chord's resolution at 26→27, a fifteen-part ff tutti (PDF 87, points 2–3), quoted by the finale at pitch. 31.1: recorded by c552 (Fl 2 B♭5 → C6, resolution taken by other voices). 70.4.5 and 71.4.5: arpeggio fillers inside one chord (PDF 85, Ex. 5-26). 71.1: Ob 2 C♭5 → B♭4 and Hn 4 F3 → E♭3, inner voices two octaves apart, each moving by step to its own tone of V7. 139.1: across the silence after the 138 stop chord, so it is a new start, not voice leading. 144.4.5: Bsn 1's passing C4 against Vln I's G4 for one quaver over the G pedal. 157.1: the cello's bar-start bass note in its arpeggio. Keep all.

### Where the music still falls short of a master's, within this lens

I looked for a doubling, spacing or soprano position that would make a chord sound fuller or an arrival land harder. The places where Unit 5 could change what a listener hears are:
- the soprano position of the C-major chords at 8 and 182;
- the bare fifths of the coda duet at 172–173;
- the thin chord at 146.3.

Bars 8 and 182 are quoted. The others are protected for reasons Unit 5 does not answer (c422, c512; recap transition 144–146). On the level of chord construction and voice independence the movement is clean in its fourth pass of this unit:
- every leading tone and chord 7th is single, or doubled only as a line in octaves;
- every incomplete chord is short and has a stated reason;
- the recapitulation's soft C-major cadence at 155 has the 3rd on top, which PDF 78 calls the fullest sonority;
- the two closing arrivals (166, 182) have the root on top, as points of articulation.

The larger harmonic weaknesses earlier critics have listed as open lie outside this reading: no augmented sixth in the second half (c572), the raised fifth on V7 at 145–147 (c582), the 156–157 climb (protected). Unit 5 gives me no grounds to reopen them. So I propose nothing.
