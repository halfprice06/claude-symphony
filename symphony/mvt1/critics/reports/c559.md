# Critic 559: holistic comparison with Mahler, Symphony No. 6 in A minor/i, and with v549

Lens 9 (holistic comparison). Version reviewed: v559. Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, examples No. 201-260 (rimsky-principles PDF 376-440), fourth pass.

How I read it. The text layer of PDF 376-440 holds only captions and notation noise. I read every one of those pages, then rendered all 65 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one. The printed numbers run from the end of Ex. 209 (PDF 376) to Ex. 274 (PDF 440), so the range covers Ex. 210-274. To know what each example is meant to show, I read in full the text pages that cite them: ch. IV, PDF 120-134 (tutti, tutti in the wind, tutti pizzicato, tutti in one to three parts, string soli, limits of range, transfer of phrases, chords alternated between groups, amplification, imitation and echo, sf-p, emphasis, crescendo and diminuendo, diverging and converging progressions, harmonic basis, artificial effects). The OCR of PDF 127, which has the amplification text, is cut short. For that principle I relied on Ex. 245-250 (PDF 411-416).

Master score. There is no Mahler in the local corpus (reference-corpus/README.md, line 64). archive.org has no full score of No. 6. The item `imslp-no6-mahler-gustav` holds only an Andante arrangement. The IMSLP page lists the Kahnt 1906 full score (#514284), but the download sits behind a disclaimer that I was not permitted to accept. So the comparison below claims only what published descriptions support. My source is https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._6_(Mahler), which describes the movement as mostly a march. Its motto is an A-major triad that turns to A minor over a fixed timpani rhythm. Trumpets are loudest in the first chord and oboes in the second. There is a soaring "Alma" theme, whose restatement at the end of the movement is the happiest point of the symphony. Cowbells play offstage in movements I and IV. I make no claim that needs bar-level detail of the Mahler.

## Findings

### 1. The movement against Mahler 6/i: keep (no change)

Where the two match.
- **A motto as a recurring signal.** Mahler's motto is a harmonic event, major turning to minor. It is tied to one rhythm, and its two chords go to two colours (trumpets loudest, then oboes). That is Rimsky's "chords of different tone quality used alternately" (PDF 126) joined with sf-p by scoring (PDF 129). Our motto is melodic: G-E♭-D-C-D♭-C. Each return takes a new colour: B.cl/Vc/Cb at 1-4, C.a. at 5-9, Fl 1 at 73-76, trombones at 117-121, trombone chorale and Vln I at 175-182. Rimsky's law of register and his echo rule hold at each hand-over (principles 8 and 11). Both composers make the motto the thing a listener carries out of the movement.
- **Major and minor as one gesture.** Mahler's motto turns major to minor at forte. Ours does the same thing in a whisper at 8→9 (Vln II E♮4 to E♭4, C major to C minor, pp) and reverses it at 182 (violas' E♮4 under a ppp fermata). Our score keeps the forte form of this turn out of movement I on purpose: the finale owns the C-major arrival (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). The idea is the same and the scale is reversed. A conductor would read this as a choice.
- **A march pulse under the first theme.** Mahler's movement is mostly a march. Our P has a quaver pulse in the basses at 15-22, hammered at 27-32 with the timpani `c4 r8 c8`. Rimsky's tutti in one to three parts with doubling describes both (principle 4).
- **The lyrical theme grows across the movement.** Mahler's Alma theme comes back at the end of the movement as its happiest point. Our S starts mp dolce in Hn 1 and cellos (47), is ff in B minor at 97-102, and is fff over ♭VI at 160. We put the S peak in the recapitulation, not the coda, because the coda must stay soft to lead attacca into movement II. The arc of growth is the same.

Where they differ, and why I keep ours.
- **No slow introduction in Mahler.** Ours has 14 bars of Lento. This is a plan difference, and the introduction is protected (c409, c429).
- **The far-away episode.** Mahler's movement calls for offstage cowbells. They are a colour of distance that no other part of the movement has. Rimsky gives the means for such distance: echo by a weaker related instrument, or by muted brass after the same phrase open (PDF 128, Ex. 252: oboe solo echoed by offstage muted trumpets). In our score that is not available. Muted and stopped brass are reserved for the other movements (coherence/mvt1.md, "Not used anywhere in movement I"). The development's soft spots (77-81, 84, 107-110) are short. c459 raised the same point against Elgar 2 and kept the score, citing the protected f start at 117 (c163, c408, c448) and the proportions (c433). This comparison does not answer those reasons, so I do not reopen it. The nearest thing to distance we have, the flute motto over the pp horn A♭ at 73-76 before the development, already follows Rimsky's harmonic-basis rule (principle 16: melody and basis in different colours, basis softer).
- **The bright ending.** Mahler's first movement ends in major and at full strength. Ours ends ppp, attacca. The finale reserve list requires this, and the soft attacca ending is protected (c409, c479).

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? On craft, yes. Every principle in the reading holds bar by bar (table above), and two fresh scans of full.midi agree: the register-gap scan (principle 7) and the group-entry scan (principle 14). Next to Mahler 6, our movement has a smaller orchestra and less extreme contrast. That is a matter of style: the brief's models are Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák, Rachmaninoff and Elgar, not Mahler. The one plain shortfall is the lack of a long soft, distant episode in the development. It stays for the protected reasons above.

Fix: none. Not applied.

### 2. Could a single change make the motto land harder? Tested in thought and rejected

The one thing I could take from Mahler is his habit of fixing a rhythm to the motto, so that every return is recognised at once. Ours already has a fixed signal that does this job: the timpani pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` at 179-180, and its hammered form at 27, 30 and 131. Adding the pulse to the motto returns at 73-76 or 117-121 would bring the 179-180 figure in early. It would also clash with the reserve record: the scherzo and finale quote the pulse (coherence/mvt1.md, seed line for 179-180), and the timpani part at 117 is protected (c448). Rimsky's own advice for a recurring idea is to change its colour each time, and the score already does that (principle 11; ch. IV PDF 118, cited by c549). No change.

### 3. v549 → v559: nothing got worse

The section files are identical apart from dev.ily. The only change there is c556's part-only breath for Tbn 1 at 119.4 (`\tag #'part \breathe`). score.ly passes Tbn 1 through `\removeWithTag #'part`, so the score page is unchanged. The other changes are the "draft v559" stamp and a comment. full.midi is byte-identical to v549's, and vl.txt is identical. The breath is staggered against Tbn 2, which holds the same E♭4 at that point. That matches the c087 rule and Adler's breathing guidance (adler1 PDF 292). It is an improvement for the player and costs nothing. Nothing got worse.

## At a master's level, do not touch

The motto's change of colour at every return (1-4, 5-9, 73-76, 117-121, 175-182), each hand-over made by register and on a common note as Rimsky teaches (PDF 125-129). Like Mahler's motto in No. 6, it is the thread a listener remembers.
