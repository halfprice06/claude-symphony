## The three A♭ climaxes

- **82 (ff).** The P head is a unison-octave statement in three octaves (Fl 1·2 + Vln I; Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1, Vln II; Bsn 1 + violas) over an A♭ bass in quaver repetition (Bsn 2, timpani, Cb) and held brass at f. The upper octaves are not filled with chord tones. That is correct for a unison statement (c454 protects 82 as such). The full chord arrives on the ff strokes at 83, where every choir has the whole V♭9 (row 14).
- **122 (fff).** The Mahler 1 and Holst models (rows 10, 23): upper octaves filled to A♭6, the bass only G1/G2, all motion stops. The fade from 123 goes to the instruments that can control it (row 11).
- **160 (fff).** The Brahms 3 and *Scheherazade* models (rows 9, 22): the tune in three octaves, each with a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument; a countermelody with its own rhythm; viola quavers; held brass ff under the fff tune.

The order 82 < 122 < 160 holds in density, top note and dynamic. Nothing in this report changes these bars.

## Findings

### Considered and kept

1. **23–25 and 135–137: the accompaniment is accented and the tune is not.** In full.midi the tune's notes in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I (E♭5–D5–C5–F5) have velocity 86–90, while the accented dotted minims in Cl, Bsn, Hn 3·4, Vln II, Vla and Vc have velocity 106–110. Row 1 says that the melody notes must be more prominent. The accents mark the 3/4 grouping against the 4/4 bar. The grouping is protected (c391, c438, c457, and the "23-26/135-138 grouping and top line" items of c421–c422). The tune also lies an octave or more above every accented part, in three colours at pitch. Adler's Schubert example (PDF 169) says that register and colour separation is what lets the ear hear melody and harmony apart, and the separation is complete here. The MIDI velocity exaggerates the accent; the printed accents are on mf. Keep.

2. **158–159: the counter Ob 1 + Cl 1·2 (G5–C6, B5–F5) is ff, the same level as the tune.** Row 25 (Schoenberg, PDF 184) says that a subordinate line in a piercing register should be marked softer. But the counter was built up on purpose over five cycles (c104, c174, c241, c337, c407). Its oboe and clarinets lie between the tune's top octave (Fl 1·2 + Vln I, E6–G6) and its middle octave (Tpt 1 + Ob 2 + Vln II), not above the tune. This is the *Scheherazade* layout (PDF 179): a middleground in another rhythm, coloured differently, placed between the tune's octaves. Marking it f would also break the shared Ob 1·2 staff, which needs one dynamic. It is protected (c437 "158-161", c444 "158-162"). Keep.

3. **167–169: the coda's P head has no woodwind.** The head is in Hn 1·2 a2, Tpt 1·2 a2 and violas, ff. The woodwinds hold the chord at f. Row 21 (PDF 178) says that each theme in a combined texture mixes a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument, and the *Meistersinger* opening (PDF 171–172) doubles the trumpets with oboes and clarinets. But 167–169 is not a combined texture. It is one theme over held chords, the homophonic case of PDF 171–172, and Wagner's point there is a heavy brass colour. Taking Ob 2 or Cl 1 off the held chord would leave holes in the Weber-type chord above (Fl E♭6/C6, Ob G5/E♭5, Cl G5). A reed at pitch with two trumpets at ff would also not be heard (PDF 165: at-pitch doubling loses both colours). The brass head over winds is protected (c267, c389, c417). Keep.

4. **117–121: the timpani roll on G is f under a passage that develops themes.** Row 24 (Bloch, PDF 184) prefers p< for such a roll. Here the roll is not a crescendo device. It starts the dominant pedal, together with the ff pedal in B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, tuba, Cb and Hn 3·4. It is the "heavy bass notes" of the Holst model (PDF 181), which steady the rhythm before the climax. The themes above it are ff in trombones, flutes, oboe, clarinets and violins, so an f roll does not cover them. Protected (c448, c459). Keep.

5. **82: the upper octaves are not filled.** Row 10 (Mahler 1, PDF 164) fills every upper octave. 82 deliberately does not: it is a unison statement, and filling it would take away 122's difference. That difference is the reason the climaxes rise. Keep.

### Bar-by-bar checks with no fault

- Row 3: the only places where a trumpet doubles a wind at pitch on a tune are 69–71 (oboe, Weber's preferred pairing), 82 (oboe and clarinets together, the *Meistersinger* "affinity instruments", PDF 172) and 160 (Ob 2). None is clarinet alone with trumpet.
- Row 8: the last chord 182 has no wind above C4 except Hn 3·4 ppp, so Brahms's "dangerous" high soft chord does not arise.
- Row 11: at 162 the brass hold A minor at ff>mp for one bar and stop; Hn 2·4 continue at 163 under a strings-and-harp diminuendo. The instruments that carry the fade to pp are the ones Adler names (strings; PDF 164).
- Row 18: the trumpets' harmony notes after the head (Tpt 1 D5 at 83, A♭5 at 72, B4 at 170) are their own head pitches or lie under the flutes and violins, so they do not disturb the colour.
- Row 24: every crescendo roll under a developing passage starts at p or pp, except 115 (mf<, two bars before the f pedal), which grows out of the mp cresc. of the whole orchestra at 111.
