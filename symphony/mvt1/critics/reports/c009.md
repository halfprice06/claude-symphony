# Critic 9 (lens 9: holistic comparison with Tchaikovsky 6/i, and v18 → v28)

## What I studied
I compared v28 (all section files, score.pdf pages 1, 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, and analysis.txt) with Tchaikovsky's Symphony No. 6, first movement. I used the corpus files `musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly` and `xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`, plus the MCTL annotations `annotations/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1/form/{form_analysis,harmony}.csv`. I chose this work because it is the plan's own first model: a dark slow introduction, a lyrical second theme, a development fugato and a quiet coda. With music21 I extracted Tchaikovsky's tempo marks, dynamics, ffff bars, registral peaks and coda scoring. I diffed every v18 and v28 section file and compared the two density profiles.

## Section by section against Tchaikovsky 6/i
- **Proportions.** Tchaikovsky's movement has 354 bars:
  - introduction 1–18 (5%);
  - exposition 19–160 (40%);
  - development 161–304 (41%), with the first-theme recapitulation absorbed into it;
  - recapitulation of the second theme only, 305–334 (8%);
  - coda 335–354 (6%).

  v28 has 178 bars and lasts about 7:07: introduction 8%, exposition 35%, development 26%, recapitulation 22%, coda 9%. The development is shorter than Tchaikovsky's but in line with the plan. The fuller recapitulation is a Brahms/Dvořák choice, not a weakness.
- **How the themes are introduced.** Both movements preview the Allegro theme in the introduction: Tchaikovsky in the bassoon, this score through the motto, which is the head of the first theme. Both give the second theme a slower tempo and dense half-bar hairpins. Tchaikovsky 89–100 marks a swell every two beats, and so does v28 at 47–52.
- **Where the climaxes fall.** Tchaikovsky's are at 245–246 (fff, 69%) and 299 (ffff, 84%), just before the lyrical recapitulation. v28's are at 114 (fff, 64%) and 156 (fff, 88%). The overall curve matches the model closely.
- **How the recapitulation differs.** Tchaikovsky drops the first theme and recapitulates the second in the tonic major, peaking fff at 313. v28 keeps a recomposed first theme (low instruments, a Neapolitan peak at 131–132) and brings the second theme in C major to a deceptive V–♭VI at 156. This is at the model's level.
- **How the coda summarizes.**
  - Harmony: Tchaikovsky's coda (harmony.csv, onsets 1338–1410) is plagal over a tonic pedal, I–IV–I–IV. v28's 163–166 does the same thing over a C pedal (i–VI6/4–iv6/4–vii°7). That is sound.
  - Colour: Tchaikovsky's coda is a soft brass chorale (horns, trumpets, trombones, bass trombone at p–mp with swells) over pizzicato scales in the strings. v28's final 12 bars use no trombones at all. Finding 3 addresses this.

## v18 → v28: what improved and what got worse
Improved:
- Second theme bars 3–4 (49–50, 59–60, 141–142, 150–151) now rise (d4 f8 a8 | c2 b2) instead of v18's static e8 f8 | g1.
- The ♭VI climax at 153–156 rises e'''–f'''–g''' to a♭'''. In v18 it sagged (e'''–es'''–d''', topping out at e♭''').
- The codetta at 69–72 combines both themes.
- The fugato at 85–96 has real entries: Vla 88, Vln II and cor anglais 91, Fl/Ob/Vln I 95.
- v18 had fff at 132, 156 and 166. Now only 156 is fff after the development.
- The coda went from 18 to 16 bars, with a violin motto canon at 173–177 and a timpani pulse at 175–176.
- Tempo words and phrasing are now in place throughout.

Got worse:
- Bar 101: the flute now reaches B6/A♯6, the highest note of the movement (Finding 1).
- Bar 28: trumpet 1 sits a semitone under horn 1 at ff (Finding 2).
- The exit from B minor at 102→103 is now an unresolved F♯ arpeggio sliding to F. It is minor and is not given a fix here.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 101 (dev bar 25): the movement's highest note is in the wrong place
This is a regression. Moving D3 to B minor (v22), on top of the rising-arpeggio rewrite (v21), sends flute 1 to b'''2 ais'''2 (B6/A♯6) at ff, alone, an octave above violin 1. The density profile confirms it: the top pitch for 101–104 is B6, against A♭6 at the development climax (113–116) and at the main climax (153–156). In v18 the top of D3 was F6. Rimsky-Korsakov writes that the flute "in the very high register… becomes somewhat piercing". A shrill peak 13 bars before the development's real climax (114) flattens the arc.

**Fix.** In `devFluteOne`, inside the `\transpose c cis` block (dev bars 24–25), replace

`es'''4. des'''8 c'''4 es'''8 ges'''8 | bes'''2 a'''2) \ottava #0 |`

with

`es'''4. des'''8 c'''4 \ottava #0 es''8 ges''8 | bes''2 a''2) |`

Flute 1 then joins oboe 1 and violin 1 on b''/ais'' at 101, and bar 102 (fis'' d''' cis''') follows smoothly.

This partly undoes v18's three-octave layout of D3, but only for one and a half bars. The top octave is kept through bar 100.

### 2. Bar 28 (tOne bar 14): trumpet 1 g' against horn 1 a♭' at ff
This is a regression from v22. To avoid a doubled leading tone, trumpet 1 was changed from b' to g'. The chord is V♭9 over D (G B D F A♭). Trumpet 1 (g') now holds a minor second under horn 1 (aes'), in the same octave, at ff. Common practice places the minor ninth at least a ninth above the root (Piston, *Harmony*, on the dominant ninth; cited from my own knowledge, not fetched). The second flute already supplies a♭'' a ninth above.

**Fix.** In `tOneHnOne`, tOne bar 14, change `aes'1` to `f'1`. Horns 1 and 2 are then a2 on f', the horn 1 line moves g'–f'–a♭', and trumpet 1 stays on g'. The leading tone is not doubled again.

### 3. Coda 171–175: the motto's last augmentation lacks the brass colour that both Tchaikovsky and this movement set up
The trombones carried the augmented motto at the development climax (109–113). Tchaikovsky's coda (335–346) ends with a soft brass chorale. In v28, the closing motto (171–175) is in bass clarinet, cellos and basses only, and the trombones are silent from 167. Bringing them back pp for the last augmentation turns the coda into a real cyclic summary: the development's climactic voice returns transformed.

**Fix.** Slurs are in two-bar spans, for breath at ♩ = 72.
- `codaTbnOne`: `es'1\f | es'1 | c'1 | d'2.->\ff r4 | R1*4 | g1(\pp | es'1) | d'1( | c'2 des'2 | c'1) | R1*3 |`
- `codaBassTbn`: `c1\f | c1 | c1 | c2.->\ff r4 | R1*4 | g,1(\pp | es1) | d1( | c2 des2 | c1) | R1*3 |`

This doubles the cellos at the unison and the octave. It adds no new voice-leading, and it is clear of the violin's es'' at 174 and of clarinet 1's b' at 173. Leave trombone 2 tacet, so that the part-combined staff shows "1.".
