# Critic 002: v21, lens 2 (harmony and voice leading)

## What I studied
I read all nine section files of v21, `analysis.txt`, `critique.md`, the plan, HISTORY and c001. `analysis.txt` checks only the top line against the bass, so I rebuilt the score's MIDI with one track per instrument (no part-combining) and ran two scripts over it (scratch in /tmp/critic-002/scripts). The first checks every pair of voices, inner ones included, for parallel fifths and octaves, and filters out sustained doublings. The second lists every sustained minor 2nd, major 7th or minor 9th. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 157: A♮ against A♭ in the same octave, one bar after the fff climax
**Problem.** Bar 157 is beats 1–2 over bass D. Cellos and harp arpeggiate D–A♭3–B–D–F, which is B°7/D. Horn 3, trumpet 3 and trombone 1 hold A♮3 (`a2 b2`), and clarinet 1 holds a♭'. A♮3 and A♭3 sound together in the same register at fff. Simultaneous false relations are treated as an error in common-practice writing (Wikipedia, "False relation"; mymusictheory). After the ♭VI, A♭ is the note that carries the mixture colour, so the A♮ is the wrong one.
**Fix.** In horn 3, trombone 1 and trumpet 3, change bar 157 from `a2 b2` to `b1`. The chord is then vii°4/3 moving to V7, the same as in the cellos.

### 2. Suspensions and appoggiaturas sounding against their own resolution note
**Problem.** The same fault occurs throughout: a sustained inner voice already holds the note that the melody's suspension resolves to. Theory texts say not to double the resolution note in an upper part while the suspension sounds; only the bass is excepted (mymusictheory; Rush). Bar 50 was created by c001's new 4–3 suspension, and the accompaniment was not adjusted.
- **Bar 50.** Horn 1 and cellos play a♭'2 g'2, while violin II holds g'1. That is a minor 2nd at the same pitch level. Fix: violin II `bes'1`, which gives a sus4 sonority.
- **Bar 59.** Violins I/II play a♭''2 g''2 while the violas enter with g' on beat 2 and horn 4 holds g. Fix: violas `r4 <bes des'>2 <des' g'>4`, horn 4 `bes1`.
- **Bar 106.** Violins I, flutes, oboe 1 and trumpet 1 play c'''/c'' over B4 held in oboe 2, clarinet 1, horn 1 and violin II, at ff. Fix: oboe 2, clarinet 1 and horn 1 `c''2 b'2`; violin II `<f' c''>2:32 <f' b'>2:32`.
- **Bar 124.** The low unison melody plays a♭ (bassoons, clarinets, trombones, cellos) under g' in horn 1, oboe 2 and trumpet 2. Fix: those three play `g'2 f'2`.
- **Bar 28.** Melody c''/c''' over trumpet 1 b'1, a semitone below. Fix: trumpet 1 `g'1`. Horn 3 and trombone 1 still supply the B.
- **Bar 14.** Horn 1 resolves c'→b while the violas already hold b. Fix: violas `<g d'>1`.
- **Bar 170.** Horn 1 c'2 b2 and cellos c''2 b'2, with the violas on b1. Fix: violas `c'2 b2`, so the Neapolitan resolves D♭–C–B.

### 3. Bars 77–108: the development stays within the flat-side keys closely related to C minor
**Problem.** Key-window analysis of v21 gives these regions: A♭ minor and E minor (77–80, two bars each), then C minor, G minor, F minor and B♭ minor (81–104), then G. After bar 80 every region is v, i, iv or ♭vii of C minor. The exposition (A♭), the recapitulation climax (♭VI) and the coda (Neapolitan D♭) are also flat-side, so the movement never sets a remote key against the tonic. The plan asked for "E minor, G minor, B major/minor". The models range much wider:
- Dvořák 9/i development: C minor, E major, A minor, F, F♯ minor, B major, E♭ minor, F♯ minor.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i development: E minor, E♭, D minor, F♯ minor, C♯, F♯.

A development that reaches a key a tritone or chromatic third from the tonic, and returns from it, is the most audible sign of a master's harmonic plan (Open Music Theory on chromatic mediants).
**Fix.** Transpose D3 (bars 97–102, all parts) up a semitone into B minor. The bass becomes `b,,1 | b,,1 | e,1 | cis,1 | fis,,1 | fis,,1`. Violin I opens `r2. fis'4( | d''2.~ d''8 cis''8 | b'4 g''2 fis''4 |`. Bar 102's F♯ then falls a semitone to the F at bar 103, so the chromatic wedge F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C–G stays exactly as it is. In bar 96, make beats 3–4 an F♯7 (bass `fis,,4`, winds `<fis ais cis' e'>`) so that B minor arrives as a tonic.

### 4. Parallel fifths in exposed places
- **Bars 177–178.** In the pp coda, violin I d''→c'' moves against violin II g'→f'. These are the last parallels in the piece and the most exposed, and they came in with v21's reordering of the motto (c001 finding 1). The fix keeps that reordering. Change bar 178 to violin II `aes'1` and violas `f'1`, so the Neapolitan is complete (C pedal, D♭, F, A♭). Bar 179 then moves violas f'→es' and violin II a♭'→g' in thirds.
- **Bars 155–156.** At V–♭VI, horn 2 moves d'→es' against the bass G→A♭, at fff. This is the textbook deceptive-cadence fault: the fifth of V should fall. Fix: horn 2 bar 156 `c'1`.
- **Bars 82–83 and 113–114.** The violas move G→D (`<es' g'>` to `<b d'>`) against the bass C→G. At bar 114, flute 2 and horn 3 move G→D against the bass trombone c→g,. Fix for 114: bass trombone `b,1\fff | b,1 |`, so C falls to B. Fix for 83: violas `<b f'>4-> r4 <b f'>4-> r4`.
- **Bar 165.** Horn 4 moves a♭→f against trombone 1, clarinet 2 and oboe 2 moving e♭→c. Fix: horn 4 `aes1`.

### 5. Bars 7–8: parallel octaves between basses and violin II, with the chromatic E♮ doubled
**Problem.** The basses move f,,→e,, while violin II moves f'→e'. This puts the raised third of C/E in two octaves, approached in parallel. The plan's motto harmonisation intended a C-minor chord "whose inner voice slides E♭–E♮–F" over a stable bass.
**Fix.** Basses bar 8 `c,1`. Bars 8–9 then become C major → C minor over a held C, while violin II slides e'→es'.

### 6. Bars 42 and 65: sevenths placed a semitone above the dissonance they belong with
**Problem.** At bar 42, over E♭7, horns 1·2 hold c' (the 13th) for four beats, a semitone below d♭' in horn 3 and trumpet 3. In a thirteenth chord the 13th belongs above the 7th, a major 7th apart, not below it as a minor 2nd (Wikipedia, "Thirteenth"). At bar 65, horn 3 holds c' against d♭' in the violas over the D♭ chord, then resolves upward into a unison.
**Fix.** Bar 42, horns 1·2: `c'2( bes2)\>`, so the 13th falls 6–5 together with oboe 2's d♭''→b♭'. Bar 65, horn 3: `des'1`.

### 7. Bars 47–59 (and 139–151): the second theme's inner voices are diatonic and do not change on restatement
**Problem.** The bass runs A♭–D♭–B♭–E♭ at 47–50, 52–54 and 56–59, and at 69–72 as A♭–F–D♭–B♭–E♭–A♭. The violin restatement (56–59) repeats the cello statement's harmony unchanged. Its only chromatic pitch before bar 60 is the g♭' at bar 51. Tchaikovsky 6/i holds a D pedal under his second theme. At 89–100, and again at 130–141, each bar has a chromatic inner tone (D♯, G♯, B♭, E♯; e.g. bars 94 and 135, D2 | B♭ C♯ E G). The plan promised "chromatic voice leading in inner parts". Dvořák's diatonic theme at 9/i 149–156 does vary its restatement, ending 163–166 on a deceptive B–A–B.
**Fix (bars 56–57 only, so the cello statement stays plain).**
- Basses and harp: hold `aes,,1 | aes,,1`.
- Violas: `r4 <c' es'>2 <c' e'>4 | r4 <des' f'>2 <des' fes'>4`, giving I–I+–IV6/4–iv6/4 over the pedal.
- Clarinet 1, bar 56: `es'2 r8 e'8( f'8 g'8`.
- Cellos/harp, bar 56: end on `e'2`.
