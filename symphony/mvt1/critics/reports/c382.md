## Findings

### 1. Bars 114→115: augmented second B→A♭ in five inner parts (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 114.3 the chord over D is D–F–A♭–B (vii°7 of C in third inversion, with the viola already holding A♭3). At 115.1 the bass falls to D♭ and Cl 1, Ob 2, Hn 1, the upper note of Vln II (all B4) and Tbn 1 (B3) leap down an augmented second to A♭. That A♭ is already sounding in the viola, so the leap only doubles a note that is there. In every other bar of the wedge the inner line moves by step: A♭–B♭–C–C–B. The leap breaks the line at the point where the bass makes its last chromatic step before the Fr6.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 552 (31-1): chromatic parallel 6/3 chords over a chromatic bass. The upper voices move with the bass, by semitone, in 6ths.
- A&S PDF 552–553 (31-2): the chromaticized 7–6 chain over a bass descending chromatically from 1 to 5. The 7th becomes a 6th and then moves down by step with the bass.

  The wedge is this model. The bass runs F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C, the 7/4 at 114.1 resolves to 6/3 at 114.3, and the next link is the 6th over D♭, which is B♭. With B♭ the inner line runs C–B–B♭–A♭ in sixths with the bass E♭–D–D♭–C. That is the motto's chromatic descent in an inner voice.
- A&S PDF 558–560: a °7 moving to the next chord is spelled toward its goal. The B at 114.3 only makes sense as a leading tone if it moves by step. An augmented second leaves it unresolved and makes the voice leading follow neither spelling.
- History: c252 kept this with the note "B4–A♭4 at 114→115 (protected wedge)". c320 extended the leap to Tbn 1 when it swapped the trombone lines.

  The stated reasons for protecting the wedge are:
  - its bass and top line (HISTORY lines 389, 433);
  - "no A♭ in the bass before 122" (line 616);
  - its scoring (line 817);
  - its pp crescendo layering (line 854).

  The fix changes none of these. The bass, the top line, the instrumentation and the dynamics stay as they are. From 115.3 the harmony is the same plain D♭ triad as before (reading principle 4, PDF 537). Only the first half of 115 changes, and only in the moving inner parts. It does not undo c320's line swap: Tbn 1 keeps the upper line, one octave below the woodwinds, and still falls A♭3→G3 at 117.

**Fix.** In sections/dev.ily, bar 115:
- Cl 1: `aes'1` → `bes'2 aes'2`
- Ob 2: `\partCombineAutomatic aes'1\f` → `\partCombineAutomatic bes'2\f aes'2`
- Hn 1: `aes'1` → `bes'2 aes'2`
- Vln II: `<f' aes'>1:32` → `<f' bes'>2:32 <f' aes'>2:32`
- Tbn 1: `aes2. ... r4` → `bes2 aes4 ... r4` (the c335 rest override is kept)

The B♭ on 115.1 is a major 6th over D♭, a 6–5 into the triad at 115.3. The viola's own figure in 115 (A♭–A♭–B♭–A♭) already uses the B♭, so no new pitch is added to the bar.

Voice-leading check:
- B→B♭ against D→D♭ is M6→M6, a chromatic parallel 6th, as in 31-1.
- Against the held F3 and F4 the motion is oblique, so no fifths.
- vl.py shows no change.

**Applied in the scratch copy.** Yes. A comment has been added to dev.ily above the Cl 1 line.
