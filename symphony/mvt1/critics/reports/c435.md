# Critic 435: Engraving and notation, beams (Gould, *Behind Bars* ch. 1, PDF 31–41, third pass)

Version reviewed: v435. Scratch copy: /tmp/critic-435/work.

Method. I read every text page from PDF 31 to 41 in full. I rendered all eleven pages at 110 dpi, and PDF 33 and 37 again at 250–300 dpi, because the rules on ledger-line clearance and on mixed-shape groups depend on the examples. c235, c325 and c415 covered these pages at v235, v325 and v415, and the `gould-beam-positions` callback in global.ily now enforces most of the angle rules. This pass therefore did three things:
1. It re-measured the whole score after the 19 cycles of changes since v416.
2. It measured the instrumental parts for the first time. The parts use a 7.2 mm stave and different spacing and line breaks, so the callback's "close spacing" and "broken beam" decisions come out differently there.
3. It tested three rules that the earlier probes did not script: the clear stave-line between the innermost beam and the first ledger line (PDF 33), the outer-beam hang/sit rule for two beams (PDF 32), and stem direction in the parts (PDF 38–39).

The probe (/tmp/critic-435/scripts/probe.ily, probe-parts.ily) wraps `Beam.stencil` and logs, for each beamed group after line breaking, the staff, bar, beat, direction, final end positions, and each stem's direction, duration, x position and noteheads. It logged 504 groups in the score and 549 in the 30 parts. The scripts an.py, dir.py, ledg.py and long.py in /tmp/critic-435/scripts test the logs. I also looked at pages 6, 11 and 16 at 220 dpi (56–63, 111–120, 158–162), where the beaming is densest.

## Findings

No finding requires a score change. Three checks are recorded below so the next engraving critic does not repeat them.

### 1. The parts follow every beam rule in the range (no change)

The parts had never been measured. Their stave size, horizontal spacing and system breaks differ from the score, so the callback's spacing-dependent case (PDF 34, notes closer than three spaces) and its exclusion of beams broken across a line could have produced different results. The probe found no broken beams in any part. It found no group steeper than its cap, no in-stave end in mid-space, no short stem, and no two-beam group with a wrongly placed outer beam (PDF 32). In single-player parts, every stem direction follows PDF 38. The only exceptions are equidistant groups, where Gould allows either direction (PDF 38–39: "either stem direction is valid"; the convention is down-stems), and Vc 61.3. Keep as it is.

### 2. Ledger-line clearance (PDF 33) holds everywhere (no change)

The rule "There must be one clear stave-line between the innermost beam and the first ledger line" was never scripted before. The PDF 33 example shows that the beam may hang from the fourth line but must not touch the outer line. In every group with notes on ledger lines and the beam inside the stave, the edge of the innermost beam lies at most 1.05 spaces from the centre (Fl/Vln I 40.3–41.1, Vla/Vc 152.3, Cl 1 part 39). The outer line, at 2.0 spaces, stays clear. Keep as it is.

### 3. Ob 1·2 160.3 and the up-stem a2 / two-voice groups (not applied; keep)

Ob 160.3 (C6–B♭5–A♭5, up-stems, beam above the stave) slopes 0.68 of a space. Its last note, A5, lies on the first ledger line, not beyond it, so the ½-space rule of PDF 35 does not apply. Its stems are 5.6 spaces apart, so the close-spacing rule does not apply either. PDF 35 asks beams outside the stave to match in-stave angles: under one space for a three-note group. 0.68 meets that. Flute groups at 27–30 and 139–140 take up-stems above the stave because Fl 1 shares the staff with Fl 2's held notes (two-voice writing, not a2), and their angles meet PDF 35. Keep as they are. They do not reopen c125's a2 stem item.
