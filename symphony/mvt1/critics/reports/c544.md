## Findings

This pass finds no change that meets the bar. The reading's rules are all followed (the two places where the letter of PDF 151 is not followed are deliberate and were justified by c344 and c454). Below are the places where I asked whether the music could land harder within this lens. In each case I give the reason the present scoring stays.

### 1. Bar 122, the second A♭ climax: no trumpet above A♭4 (considered, keep)

**Question.** At 122 (fff, 27 players, tam-tam) the trumpets sound A♭4 (Tpt 1), D4 (Tpt 2) and B3 (Tpt 3). The climax note A♭ is placed at A♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I), A♭5 (Ob 1, Vln II upper), A♭4 (Hn 1, Tpt 1) and A♭3 (Bsn 1, Tbn 1, Vc). Adler PDF 146 asks for knowledge of the strength of each segment of the range, and PDF 151 says the heaviest octave tells the ear where the line is. With Tpt 1 on A♭5 the A♭5 octave would gain the brightest brass colour and the climax would gain brilliance.

**Assessment.** Keep. (a) At 122 the A♭ is the continuation of the brass motto of 117–121: Hn 1 rises C4→A♭4, a minor sixth, the motto's own interval, and Tpt 1 enters in unison with it. The weight belongs with the horns' motto line, which is the foreground of 117–122 (c454 Finding 3). That follows PDF 151: the heaviest octave carries the line the listener should follow. (b) Tpt 1 on the tune's A♭5 at 160.2, in the middle of three octaves, is what lifts the third climax above the second. Giving it to 122 would take that step away and flatten the order 82 < 122 < 160. (c) 122 is protected (c254, c399, c445). coherence/mvt1.md also records 122 as being separate from the finale's 223 in part because the trumpets do not move B→C. A high trumpet line at 122 would bring the two closer together.

### 2. Bars 131–134: the head's low octave carries 9–11 instruments (checked, keep)

The same-pitch scan finds the heaviest stacks in the movement here: E♭4 at 131.3 (9 instruments), F4 at 133.3 (11). That octave is the theme: the P head is in Bsn a2, Cl a2, Tbn a2 and Vc, with Hn 2, Tpt 2 and Tpt 3 on chord tones of the same pitch. The top line (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I·II) is a descant. This is Mozart's distribution as Adler reads it at PDF 151: the heavy octave tells the listener where the theme is, and the higher instruments add "sheen". The trombones stay f under ff, as d'Indy marks them (PDF 147). The finale quotes 27–32, not 131–134, so the two first-group tuttis already differ, which Barber's varied doubling recommends (PDF 150). Keep.

### 3. Bars 156–157: two bars at the same density before the 158 arrival (considered, keep)

Barber enlarges the doubling "slightly in every measure" (PDF 150). At 156 and 157 the players per bar stay at 14. Only the dynamics rise: Hn 1–4 p to mp cresc., Vln I·II mf, Cl 1 in the clarino register (c337). Then 26 players arrive at 158. Measured by Barber, the climb is one step, not two.

I looked for a single addition at 157 that would make it two steps. Every candidate fails a stated reason that this reading does not answer. A timpani roll was rejected by c534: the drums are part of the 158 return, and the same roll already leads into 167. Ob 1 or Fl 1 on the violins' line at 157 would put a woodwind on the tune's last bar before 158, where Ob 1 is meant to step F5→G5 into the counter (c337; c511 for 157.4). A held trumpet or trombone at 157 would break the 15-bar trumpet rest that makes the 158 entry new (PDF 156: a rest before a colour makes sure it is heard). Cycle 83's rule "do not raise level or density of 147–157" stands for the same reason. This is the open item "156–157 climb (protected)". The orchestration cannot fix it without spending one of those. Keep.

### 4. Bar 99.1: the brass counter and the tune's low octave meet on B3 (checked, keep)

At 99.1 eight instruments sound B3: the tune's lowest octave (C.a., Vln II sul G, Vc) and the counter (Bsn 1, Hn 1·2, Hn 4, Tbn 1). By PDF 151 this could blur which line is which. The overlap lasts one beat on the tonic root of B minor. The tune's other two octaves (Fl 1 B5; Ob 1 + Vln I B4) are clear of it, and the counter moves away on the next quaver figure. The passage is protected (97–102 brass counter-line, c424). Keep.

### 5. Bars 85–96: the fugato as Barber's enlarging doubling (checked; at a master's level)

Each fugato entry adds one colour to the one before: Vc alone at 85 (Cb rest, c227); Vla with Bsn 1 at 88; Vln II with C.a. an octave lower at 91; Vln I with Fl 1 at 94; Cl 1 with Vln II and Vla on the ff run into 96. Barber at PDF 150 does the same thing in a crescendo: a different doubling each time the germ motive returns. It also follows PDF 156 (each new colour enters after a rest). No change.

### Where the movement still falls short, within this lens

After four passes of this reading, I find no orchestral shortfall that a change could fix without breaking a protected reason. The one weakness that critics keep naming, the two-bar climb at 156–157, is a question of phrase length (c083, c143, c243, c534). Finding 3 shows that this reading has no orchestral answer to it that does not spend something the 158 arrival needs.
