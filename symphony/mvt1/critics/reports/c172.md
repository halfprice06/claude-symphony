Corpus: in Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`), bars 90 and 154 of the D-major second theme have a common-tone °7 over the tonic pedal: D–F–G♯–B, with horns on F (a minor 3rd written for E♯), bassoon and clarinet G♯→A, resolving to D major on the next downbeat. The viva.pressbooks Open Music Theory page returned 403.

Overall: the movement already uses the Unit 30 devices the way the book teaches. Augmented triads are always passing or neighbour sonorities and resolve as the book says. Mixture in the major-key groups reverts before cadences. The remote keys are prepared by chromatic lines or by association. I found one real voice-leading fault, which cycle 170 introduced, and one missing device that the reading and the models both point to.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 161→162: parallel fifths between Cl 1 and cellos into the deceptive cadence (applied)
- **Problem:** Cycle 170 changed Cl 1 at 161 from A♭5–F5 to A♭5–G5 (♭6→5). Now Cl 1 G5 over the cellos' C4 (161 beat 4½) moves to E5 over A2 (162), which is a fifth to a fifth in similar motion. vl2.py reports it: "bar 162 P5 clOne G5->E5 vc C4->A2". It was not in v189. The V7's seventh is now also gone from the counterline, so the line jumps G→E instead of resolving F→E.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 202 (Ex. 13-12): in V7–VI the voice leading is chosen to prevent parallel 5ths. PDF 83: parallel 5ths are excluded. PDF 537 §6: a mixture seventh chord (here the borrowed iiø7 with A♭) directed to its goal intensifies the motion, so the line should run A♭–G–F–E through iiø7 → V7 → vi.
- **Fix (recTwo.ily, `recTwoClOne`):** `| aes''2) g''4 f''4 |` (was `g''2`). This keeps cycle 170's ♭6→5 on beat 3 and lets the seventh resolve F5→E5 at 162. The F5–E5 octave with Tbn 2 and Tpt 2 is the same orchestral doubling v189 had; cycle 112 allowed it at 161–162 (A&S PDF 84–85). The crossing "fifth" with Vln II (C6→A4 octave drop) is also the v189 state.

### 2. Bar 73: a common-tone diminished seventh before the flute's motto (applied; optional)
- **Problem:** Bars 73–74 hold a bare A♭ triad over the pedal for eight beats before the chromatic chords of 75–76. A&S call the CT°7 on I "one of the most frequently used chromatic embellishments of major triads" (PDF 544) and show it as a 4/2 over a sustained bass (Ex. 30-19, PDF 543). The movement never uses it. Tchaikovsky 6/i uses it in the same place: a soft second group over a tonic pedal (bars 90, 154).
- **Notation (A&S PDF 544–545, §13):** use the minor 3rd C♭ instead of B♮ "when mixture is an important feature". Here A♭ major turns to A♭ minor (C♭) at 77, so C♭–C♮ at 73 plants the ♭3/♮3 pair that 77 will use. Brahms Op. 87 (Ex. 30-21) does the same thing to carry the minor colour across a movement boundary.
- **Fix (clos.ily):** `closVlnTwo` bar 73 `<d' f'>2(\pp es'2)` (was `es'1\pp`); `closVla` bar 73 `ces'2(\pp c'2)` (was `c'1\pp`). Beats 1–2 are A♭ (Vc, Cb, Hn 4)–C♭–D–F; on beat 3 C♭→C (♯2/♭3→3), D→E♭ (♯4→5) and F→E♭ (6→5), which doubles the fifth as the book's Ex. 30-20 does. The violin double stop D4 (G string) with F4 (D string) is a plain minor third; it closes to the single E♭.
- **Protection:** Cycle 51 protected 69–76 as melodic material, and cycle 40 the flute motto in tempo. No melodic line, tempo or dynamic changes, and the flute enters on beat 4 over the resolved tonic. Cycle 52's rule says "no more … B–D–F–A♭ cadences". This chord has the same pitch classes but is not a cadence: it is a neighbour chord over a tonic pedal with no root motion. If the composer thinks a fifth appearance of that sonority dilutes the stop chords (26, 138, 170), reject this finding and keep Finding 1.

### 3. Bars 160→161: bass leaps A♭–D, a melodic tritone, at fff (note only)
- Bassoons, contrabassoon, bass trombone, tuba and basses leap A♭→D from ♭VI to the D–F–A♭–B chord. A&S PDF 537 asks that a focal mixture chord move clearly to its goal, and the usual path for ♭6 in the bass is down to 5. A♭ held under B–D–F (vii°4/2) moving to G at 161 beat 3 would be smoother. However, this changes the protected "V–♭VI–ii–V–vi" (cycles 29, 82, 170), and the tritone gives the climax a hard edge that the models also use. I do not propose a change.

### 4. Bars 177–179: viola A♭3 leaps to E♭4 twice (checked, keep)
- The viola's A♭ is the seventh of vii°7 at 177 and the 5th of the Neapolitan at 178, and both times it leaps up to E♭ instead of falling to G. Over the bass D→C, A♭→G would make d5→P5, which A&S PDF 83 says is "normally avoided". The G is present in Vln II and Tbn 2. The composer's choice is correct, and the coda is protected.

## Checked, no change
- Every °7 (11, 26, 28, 30, 63, 70, 83, 114, 117, 122, 132, 138, 161, 170, 177) resolves its seventh and leading tone correctly, or holds them into the next dominant-function chord.
- Augmented sonorities: all are passing or neighbour sonorities (principle 9). The spellings follow the book: C♯ at 148, D♯ and A♯ at 106, E♮ at 56.
- A V7♯5 at 165 (D–D♯–E into 166) was tried on paper and rejected: D♯ in the viola would clash with Vln II D4.
