## Findings

All findings are confirmations or record corrections. I found nothing in the score that this reading shows to be wrong, so the scratch copy is unchanged.

### 1. Movement I's ♭II plan is the first stage of a four-movement ♭II design. Keep it (no change)

**What the score does.** Movement I uses ♭II only as a chord and never as a key. Each structural ♭II reaches V and then i in C minor:
- 20–22: ♭II, then N6, V7 and i, with the direct diminished third D♭4–B3 in Hn 3.
- 140–143: ♭II, then N6, iiø6/5 and V, ff, into i at 143. The diminished third is filled (D♭–C–B).
- 172–174: iv, then N6, cadential 6/4 and V.

Its other D♭s are neighbours inside the tonic (3, 7→8, 27/143/167, 178, 180). These are Unit 28's two families: the chord leading to V (PDF 483–484) and the "♭II" inside I (PDF 495, Ex. 28-20).

**How the later movements continue it.**
- Movement II states movement I's P head in A minor at 63–64. That is ♭II of A♭ written enharmonically, which A&S PDF 490 allows for keys with four or more flats. Its plagal close at 87 has D♭ over F: the N6 of C minor heard as IV6 of A♭ (PDF 494, §14).
- The scherzo has ♭II of E in all its forms: F in the theme-A head, the F-major harp episode, and the Neapolitan 6/4 C–F–A at 331. That 6/4 is exactly the Liszt case on PDF 490–491, with the bass C as upper neighbour to V (B).
- The finale tonicizes D♭ from its own dominant (A♭7, 205–210, then D♭ at 211). It then respells that chord as the German sixth at 219 and goes to V and I. This is the Verdi model of PDF 489–490 (♭VI acting as dominant of ♭II, then V7–I) and the tonicization of PDF 491.

So the symphony presents ♭II in root position and 6/3 (I), in enharmonic and tonicized form (II), in 6/4 (III), and tonicized and then used as a pivot (IV). Movement I keeps to the plain, local uses and leaves the long-range ones to the later movements.

**Support.** Tchaikovsky 6 is in the corpus (musicxml-converted/annotations, harmony.csv). Its first movement has one ♭II6 (Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1, onset 749, bar 188) and two V7/♭ii (onsets 1272 and 1288, bars 319 and 323). Its finale tonicizes ♭II: Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt4, onsets 297 and 411–415, bars 100 and 138–139, and ♭ii in 4/2 position at 447 and 465. The first movement keeps ♭II local and the finale makes it a key, which is the same distribution as here.

**Fix.** None. A tonicized D♭, or a theme on ♭II, in movement I would take what movements II and IV build on (Finding 3).

### 2. Seeds list: bars 12 and 75 are the finale's 219 and 209–210 (record only)

**What the score does.**
- Bar 75 (clos): A♭–C–E♭–G♭ over the A♭ pedal (Vln II G♭4, Fl 1 G♭5, Vla C4). This is V7 of D♭. At 76 it goes to D♭ minor as a 6/4 over the same pedal (iv6/4 of A♭ with F♭).
- Bar 12 (intro): the same sound spelled A♭–C–E♭–F♯, a German sixth going to V at 13.

The finale uses the same pair in the same order. Its 209–210 are A♭7, V7 of D♭, and resolve to D♭ at 211. Its 219 is the same chord respelled with Hn 2 G♭4 = F♯4, and it goes to V. A&S PDF 490 says to be guided by the sound, not the notation. Here one sound has two meanings, and movement I's two spellings are already the finale's two meanings: 75 points toward D♭ and 12 toward V.

mvt1.md lists bar 12 as a seed (the finale's first chord) and bars 73–76 as movement II's 3–6. It does not list 75 as the seed of the finale's A♭7 → D♭.

**Fix.** No score change. In the "Seeds of later movements" section of coherence/mvt1.md, add: "Bar 75: A♭7 spelled with G♭ (V7 of D♭) moving to D♭ minor 6/4 over the A♭ pedal at 76. It is the chord the finale tonicizes at 209–211, and bar 12 spells the same sound as the German sixth the finale reaches at 219. Keep the G♭ spelling at 75 and the F♯ spelling at 12."

### 3. Add three items to the "Do not reuse" list (record only)

None of these is in movement I (checked in the MIDI and the section files). Each is something the later movements already do. Unit 28 calls them the long-range uses of ♭II.

1. **D♭ as a key area in C, and ♭II reached from its own dominant.** The finale has A♭7 → D♭ at 205–211 (PDF 491, Ex. 28-16). In movement I, bar 75's V7 of D♭ resolves only to a 6/4 over the A♭ pedal, inside A♭ major.
2. **♭VI moving to ♭II in C** (Verdi Kyrie, PDF 489–490). Movement I's three ♭VI climaxes go elsewhere: 82 to V♭9 at 83, 122 as A♭ over the G pedal, 160 to vii°7 of V.
3. **A theme stated on ♭II** (Appassionata, PDF 492). Movement II does this with movement I's P head at its 63–64.

The bar-115 chord is not a pre-emption. It is a one-bar root-position ♭II, ff, passing in the chromatic wedge bass. A&S PDF 487 gives a motivic bass as the reason for root-position ♭II.

**Fix.** No score change. Add the three items to "Do not reuse" in coherence/mvt1.md. Note there that I 115 has a one-bar, ff, root-position D♭ chord over the passing bass E♭–D–D♭–C, so the finale thread knows the sound has been heard once without a key.

### 4. Bar 173: the coda's N6 lasts one beat and ♭2̂ returns to C over iv. Keep it (no change)

**What the score does.** At 173.3 the C.a. (D♭5), the solo cello (D♭4), the violas (A♭3) and Bsn 2 (F3) make a complete N6. On 173.4 the C.a. falls to C5 and the cello leaps to F4 over the same F. So the C, which A&S say should be withheld until V (PDF 483), arrives over iv, one beat before the cadential 6/4 at 174.

**Why it stays.** Three reasons:
- The C.a. line is the motto (G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C), in the motto's fixed rhythm (D♭ on beat 3, C on beat 4), as at 3 and 178.
- The coda uses ♭II as a neighbour, the use of PDF 495. Read that way, 173 is the "apparent Neapolitan" of PDF 495–496 (Ex. 28-21): iv with ♭2̂ as a chromatic neighbour.
- The tension the reading describes is still there in the large-scale plan. The V at 174 never reaches root-position i: it goes to the i6/4 at 175. The finale's thread says it completes movement I's N6–6/4–V. Coda notes, dynamics and timing are protected (c116, c126, c130, c140, c150).
