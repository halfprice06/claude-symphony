# Critic 307: texture and counterpoint (v307)

Lens: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II, "Melody in different groups of instruments combined together" (rimsky-principles PDF 74–80; book pages 56–62). I read all seven text pages in full and rendered each one at 110 dpi. The music on these pages is the inline doubling diagrams (PDF 74, 75, 76, 78) and the two-note trumpet/flute staff examples on PDF 75. The OCR garbles every bracketed doubling formula, so I took the formulas from the images. The chapter does not reprint full examples in this range. It cites Nos. 78–96 by number, and I took those citations from the text.

Rimsky-Korsakov writes on PDF 76 that his rules for combined groups "apply equally to melody, harmony, counterpoint and polyphonic writing". I used them for that reason: under this lens they decide how each counter-melody, fugato entry and bass line is doubled.

## Findings

I checked every passage the lens covers against the table: the accompaniment figures (15–22, 47–68, 97–102, 111–116, 147–155), the counter-melodies (19–22, 56–67, 97–102, 131–134, 139–142, 158–163), the fugato (85–96), the imitative passages (33–40, 77–83, 127–130, 175–182) and the bass line throughout. Almost every doubling here is one of Rimsky-Korsakov's "natural" pairs or groups, often exactly. The all-pairs check in v307/vl.txt has one perfect fifth between real parts, at bar 162 (Tbn 2 F3→E3 against Vc C4→A2, contrary motion, kept by c222). It has no parallel octaves between independent lines that are not doublings. I found no fault that would separate this score from a master's work. The four findings below are places where the score departs from the letter of the reading. In each case I recommend keeping it as written, and I give the reason. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. Fugato, third entry, 91–93: Vln II above the cor anglais in octaves (keep)

**What the reading says.** On PDF 78 Rimsky-Korsakov says that when only one of two octave parts is doubled in the soprano register, it is "better to allow the wood-wind to progress in octaves, the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups". The editor's note on the same page goes further: a string and a wind alone in octaves "is not to be recommended, as the tone quality of the two groups is so widely different".

**What the score does.** Bars 91–93 do the reverse. Vln II has the subject on C at C5–A5, and the C.a. is an octave below at C4–A4 (dev.ily, `devCorAnglais` and `devVlnTwo`). At 94 the two meet in unison on the countersubject (B♭4). To get there, Vln II drops a ninth from A5 to B♭4, so its subject's rising A♮ does not resolve in its own octave. In the earlier entries this resolution happens in place: Vla E4→F4 at 90→91 and Vc B3→C4 at 87→88.

**Why it should stay.** The obvious fix puts Vln II an octave lower, in unison with the C.a. That would place the subject at C4–A4, inside the violas' countersubject (F4–C5) and the cellos' free voice (C4–E♭4: `r4 c'2 es'4~ | es'4 des'4 c'2~`). At 92 Vln II's D♭4–C4 would collide with the cellos' C4, and the third entry would be buried. The opposite fix, keeping Vln II at B♭5 for the countersubject at 94, would put it above Vln I's fourth entry (F5–D♭6) and up to F6. Rimsky-Korsakov's rule 9 (PDF 76) settles the balance question: the string predominates over one wind of equal power, so the C.a. colours Vln II's entry without covering it, and the entry stays on top and clear. The register move at 93→94 is a normal fugal one: the C.a. carries the A4→B♭4 resolution, and Vln II joins it there. The fugato's notes are also protected (c237, c245, c284). Recommendation: no change.

### 2. Transition, bar 38: Cl 1 above the violas in octaves (keep)

**What the reading says.** The same editor's note (PDF 78) applies.

**What the score does.** At 38, Cl 1's entry (C5–A♭5, `r8 c''8 aes''4->~ | aes''8 g''16 ges''16 f''4`) has the violas an octave below and no other doubling. The entry one bar before follows Rimsky-Korsakov's own rule (PDF 78): Ob 1 and Vln I in unison, with the violas an octave lower.

**Why it should stay.** The clarinet's entry is one bar long and comes in stretto against Vln I's G5–A♭5 entry. The violas' lower octave ties it to bar 37, where they doubled the oboe in the same way, so the viola line runs continuously through both entries. Breaking that line to obey the editor's note would weaken the imitation, which is protected ("transition imitation 35–40", "transition hand-offs 34–41", c177). The editor also says (PDF 78) that this doubling had "recently come into fashion again", so it is a matter of taste, not a rule against it. Recommendation: no change.

### 3. B-minor S, 97–102: brass counter with Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1 in unison (keep)

**What the reading says.** On PDF 74 Rimsky-Korsakov calls horns with trombones "not so reliable", and says they should go "exclusively in octaves" (horns above).

**What the score does.** In the counter at 97–102 (`bes2 r8 f8 des'8 c'8` transposed up a semitone), Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Bsn 1 play in unison.

**Why it should stay.** Rimsky-Korsakov's octave rule is about two-part brass melody. This counter is a mid-register filler line under an ff tune that is doubled in two octaves. A unison of horns and trombone gives it one solid pitch, and PDF 75 explains why the bassoon belongs in it: a wind added to brass gives "a finer legato". An octave split (horns above) would push the horns to D♭5–E5 at f, into the octave the tune's lower doubling already holds (C.a., Vln II, Vc), and the counter would compete with the tune. The passage is protected (c128, c186, c231, c248). Recommendation: no change.

### 4. 109–110: Fl 1 with the violas (keep)

**What the reading says.** Rimsky-Korsakov's natural partners for the violas are the oboe, cor anglais, clarinet and bassoon (PDF 76). The flute's natural partner is the violins.

**What the score does.** At 109–110, Fl 1 (mp, D♭5–A♭5) doubles the violas' S head in unison. This is the flute's weak lower-middle register.

**Why it should stay.** The pitches lie on the violas' A string, which is a violin register, so the flute's register does correspond to the line, as the rule requires. The flute enters only for the last two bars of the phrase, to lift the line into the wedge. Critic 241 read these same pages and kept it. Recommendation: no change.

## What follows the reading exactly (for the record)

- **The two motto frames, 1–4 and 175–179.** The low motto's doubling is Rimsky-Korsakov's rule for a soft low melody (PDF 78–79): cellos doubled by a reed, basses an octave below and undoubled.
- **The S statement at 47–52.** Horn and cellos in unison is the combination he singles out for "a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone" (PDF 79).
- **The codetta at 69–72.** It uses two of his three natural three-group unisons at once (PDF 79): Vln I + Fl + Ob 1 + Tpt 1 on S, and Vla + Vln II + Cl 1·2 + Hn 1·2 on P.
- **The fugato at 85–96.** It adds one wind to each string entry from the second entry on (PDF 76), and the subject alone in the cellos at 85–87 keeps the opening exact.
- **The bass line.** It is doubled by Rimsky-Korsakov's register pairs throughout: Cb + Cbsn, Vc + Bsn, Vc + B.cl, and B.tbn + Tuba (PDF 76–77, 79).
