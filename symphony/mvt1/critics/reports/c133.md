## Findings

The reading treats chromatic sequences and equal divisions as the tools of developments and transitions. I checked each one against the development (77–130), the transition (33–46) and the recapitulation changes. Every technique the score uses is used the way the book describes, and the techniques it leaves out are ones the book calls rare or secondary. I propose no note changes. The findings are ranked by how much each passage carries the movement's form, and each one says what must stay.

### F1. The development's key plan uses both of the book's main equal divisions and binds them with fifth-based motion (77–130). Keep. No edit.

- Reading: PDF 572 says equal divisions should be "integrated … into compositions mainly based on very different procedures", and that a "predominance of equal division would weaken the gravitational attraction of the tonic". PDF 575: the minor-third division "often works more effectively within the expansion of a seventh chord than within a triad".
- Score: 77–82 divides the octave into descending major thirds around A♭: Cb `aes,,1 | aes,,2 e,2 | e,1 | c,1 | c,1 | aes,,8…`. This has the three features of Ex. 31-27 (PDF 573). The ♭6 of A♭ (F♭, written E♮) sits in an upper voice as a neighbour to E♭: Vln II `es'1 | es'2 e'2 | e'1 | es'1`. The viola respells C♭ as B (`ces'2:32 b2:32`) exactly where the bass makes the diminished fourth A♭→E. Critic 110 found this plan through Unit 32; Unit 31 confirms it detail by detail.
- The second half (97–122) arrives on B minor, D minor, F minor and A♭ over G. That outlines B–D–F–A♭, the V♭9 of C, so the minor-third division expands a seventh chord rather than the tonic, which is the use PDF 575–576 recommends. Between the two equal-division plans, the fugato enters on D, G, C and F (85, 88, 91, 94), a cycle of fifths with three bars per entry. PDF 578 says fifths "add an element of direction" that symmetric motion lacks. The tonic comes back through a 14-bar dominant pedal (117–130). The equal divisions are therefore framed and do not predominate.
- Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i also builds its development from a minor-third chain, but runs it fast. MCTL harmony.csv, quarter-note onsets 818–841 (about bars 205–211): F – A♭ – B – D, each key given one bar or less as I6/4 – vii°7 – vii°7/V – V. Our chain runs at structural pace (6, 8 and 11 bars per station) and gives each station different material: the B-minor second theme, the combination of both themes, the wedge, the climax. PDF 574 warns that a sequential minor-third chain "can sound excessive". Ours does not repeat a pattern, so the warning does not apply.
- Fix: none. Keep the key arrivals at 97, 103, 111 and 122 and the bass of 77–82, as cycles 110 and 130 already ruled.

### F2. The wedge (111–116) is a contrary-motion chromatic expansion of the predominant that ends on an augmented sixth. Keep it as written, and do not move A♭ into its bass. No edit.

- Reading: PDF 563 (Ex. 31-17) expands IV through contrary chromatic outer voices. PDF 564: "the inner voices remain stationary". PDF 557 (Ex. 31-9): augmented sixths over a descending chromatic bass give a "cumulative effect" that "adds to the power of the final resolution … to a dominant". PDF 563: a seventh that arises from voice leading "has no harmonic function whatever".
- Score: the bass falls `f,1 | e,1 | es,1 | d,1 | des,1 | c,1` while Vln I rises A♭5–B♭5–C6–C6–D♭6–D6 (contrary motion). The viola repeats C4 through 111–114 and again at 116, so the middle stays put, as in Ex. 31-19b. The framework is iv (111) – i6 (113) – iiø7 (114) – ♭II (115) – augmented sixth (116) – V (117), the predominant expansion the book describes.
- Bar 112 (C7 over E): Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II move `aes'1 | bes'1 | c''1`, so their B♭ rises to C instead of falling as a chord seventh would. Under PDF 563 this B♭ is a passing tone in the rising line A♭–B♭–C, and Vln I gives the descending resolution (`bes''2.~ bes''8 a''8`). It is correct.
- Bar 116: the sonority is C–D–F♯–A♭. Tbn 2 has A♭2 under F♯3 in Vc, Hn 4 and Bsn 1, so a real augmented sixth is present, and it resolves into the V♭9 at 117. The obvious textbook option would be to move the bass from C to A♭ on beat 3, so that the dominant arrives by half step, as in Ex. 31-9 and at 12–13. I reject it. The cycle-57 rule ("no A♭ in the bass before 122") saves that half step for the climax, where Vc A♭3 at 122 falls to G at 123. PDF 557's "cumulative effect" supports holding the strongest resolution back for the last statement.
- Fix: none.

### F3. The join at 108→109 (E minor to B♭ minor) is grammatical: a seventh chord reinterpreted as an augmented sixth. Keep. No edit.

- Reading: PDF 576: if a minor-third cycle "breaks off before the octave is reached, or if the final seventh chord is reinterpreted as an augmented sixth, the passage can lead to a new and possibly unexpected key area".
- Score: 108 ends on B7 (Cb `e,4 r4 b,,4 r4`, Vln II lower `g4( a2.)`, upper `e'2( dis'2)`), which is V7 of E minor. At 109 the bass falls a semitone to B♭ (`bes,,4`), Vln II lower moves A→B♭ and upper D♯→F. Spelled C♭–E♭–G♭–A, the B7 is a German sixth that resolves outward to B♭ (bass ♭6→5, A→B♭). B♭ minor then works as iv of F minor, and C (110) and F (111) follow. This is the only abrupt-sounding join in 103–111, and the reading accounts for it.
- Notation: the D♯→F step in Vln II reads as a diminished third. PDF 554 says the enharmonic relation "would still be inherent … though not expressed in its notation", and D♯ is the right spelling for what is heard first, the dominant of E. Keep D♯.

### F4. The retransition (117–130) prolongs one dissonant dominant. The reading supports its length. No edit.

- Reading: PDF 575–576 (Ex. 31-30d): a V7 expanded through changes of position of its °7, where "the fleeting °7's … form the connective threads". PDF 577–578 (Ex. 31-31b): C.P.E. Bach stretches one diminished-seventh bar into thirteen bars before I returns.
- Score: over the G pedal, the violas and clarinets alternate vii°7 and minor 6/4 triplets (117 `b d' f'`, 118 `c' es' g'`, 119 `b d' f'`, 120 `bes des' f'`, 121 `c' es' g'`). The A♭ ninth arrives at 122, and the dominant then decays and rebuilds through 130. Over a pedal the 6/4s are dissonant, so the tension does not let up every other bar; it accumulates until the A♭ at 122. These 14 bars of V do the job of the C.P.E. Bach expansion, and the whole V♭9 is spread over 26 bars (97–122).
- Proportion: this is 26% of the development. Tchaikovsky 6/i's retransition (MCTL form_analysis.csv, "Transition", onsets 1062–1214, about 38 bars) is longer in absolute terms. Cycle 43's rule (do not extend the G pedal) stands, and nothing in the reading argues for shortening it.

### F5. The recapitulation changes (131–166). The reading gives no ground to alter them. No edit.

- Reading: PDF 566–567 and 573 (Schubert D. 887). A ♭6 bass that did not go to V early in the movement does go to V "in a culminating moment near the very end", and that is the long-range use of a chromatic bass.
- Score: the bass half step A♭→G is heard at 12–13 (Ger6–V), at 82–83, and at 122–123 as the climax neighbour. In the recapitulation, the ♭VI at 160 goes A♭–D–G (♭VI–ii–V) into the deceptive cadence at 162. The movement's culminating A♭→G is therefore the development's at 122. With the coda's N6–cadential 6/4 at 172–174 (D♭→C over the motto), the final approach to C uses ♭2̂ rather than ♭6̂. This is a consistent division of labour: A♭ belongs to the development and D♭ to the coda (cycle 60's D♭ plan). Making A♭→G the bass of 160–161 would touch the protected climax harmony 158–166, and the reading gives no reason that outweighs that protection.
- Transitions: the exposition transition (33–46) is diatonic and fifth-based (bass C–A♭–B♭–E♭–E♭–F–B♭–B♭–E♭). PDF 567 names chains of voice exchanges as one possible transitional element, not a required one. A short diatonic transition before a long chromatic development is the usual Romantic balance. The recapitulation's 4-bar bridge on the G pedal (143–146) is correct for a C-major second theme.

### F6. Unit 31 techniques that movement I leaves unused, for movements II and III (note only)

These are deliberate absences in movement I, not faults. They are available to the later movements, and the composer may add them to cyclic-notes.md:
- The descending chromatic "lament" bass harmonized with 7–6 suspensions, for grief (PDF 552–553, Ex. 31-2). It suits movement II, for example a bass descending from the tonic to the dominant under the D♭ material.
- Descending °7 chains notated °7/°4/3 (PDF 558–560, Ex. 31-10 to 31-12), as a transitional device with more tension than parallel 6/3s.
- Ascending chromatic 5–6 with augmented triads on the weak beats (PDF 554, Ex. 31-3c/d; PDF 562, Ex. 31-16), for a build in movement III that rises instead of using a pedal.
