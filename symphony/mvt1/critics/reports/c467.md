## Findings

On this third pass the combined-group writing in v467 still follows Rimsky-Korsakov's chapter. The series-4 changes since c367 add no new departure. I also looked for places where, within this lens, the texture falls short of a master's work, and tested one candidate in principle (finding 4). None of the departures below would improve the score if changed without breaking either a protected passage or the part-writing that PDF 112 puts first. **No change was applied.**

### 1. Bars 37, 67, 164.1: the chord's seventh is in a horn in soft horn–bassoon harmony (kept)

What the score does:
- At 37 (p) the four real parts are Bsn 2 E♭3, Hn 4 G♭3, Bsn 1 B♭3 and Hn 3 D♭4, an E♭-minor seventh. The seventh, D♭4, is in Hn 3, which takes it down to C4 at 38.
- At 67 (p, V7 of A♭) Bsn 2 has E♭3, Hn 4 B♭3 and Hn 3 D♭4. Hn 3 moves to C4 at 68. Bsn 1 rests from 64 to 68.
- At 164.1 (mp, iiø7 of C) Bsn 2 has D2, Bsn 1 D3 (in unison with Hn 4), Hn 3 A♭3 and Hn 2 C4.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 108 says that in soft four-part harmony for two bassoons and two horns, "the concords [are] given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons." At 39–40 the score does this: the seventh A♭3 of the B♭7 chord is in Bsn 1, inside the horns.

Why each one stays:
- At 37, giving the D♭ to Bsn 1 would put Bsn 1 above both horns (Bsn 2–Hn 4–Hn 3–Bsn 1). The two horns would then sit between the bassoons, which the second diagram on PDF 108 advises against. The present layout interleaves the instruments, which the same page recommends for blend, and the seventh resolves down by step in the voice that holds it. PDF 112 settles the conflict: when the parts move, "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts." Bar 37 is also recorded as a seed of the finale's E♭-minor theme, with these four pitches in these instruments (coherence/mvt1.md).
- At 67 the soft chord is not a horn–bassoon quartet. The bassoon doubles the bass an octave up under a string tune and the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline, and Bsn 1 has a rest there that the 69 tutti needs (it enters f at 69). Moving the seventh to a bassoon would need Bsn 1 to enter for two beats, and Hn 3 would have to find another note. G4 doubles the counterline. B♭4 is the third doubling of the same pitch. Neither improves the chord. Bars 56–68 are protected (c327, c441).
- At 164.1 the A♭3 is the line that falls to G3 at 164.3, and the pitches of 156–166 are protected (c346; c367 finding 2 tested the bassoon alternative at 164.3 and rejected it).

### 2. Bars 113–114 and 162: woodwind doubles Tpt 1 rather than lying above the trumpets (kept)

What the score does: at 113–114 (pp cresc.) Tpt 1 C5 is doubled at the unison by Ob 2, Cl 1 and Hn 1. Only Vln I (C6) is higher, and the flutes and Ob 1 enter at 115. At 162 (ff, A minor) Cl 1's E5 is the only woodwind above Tpt 1's C5.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 110–111: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets." All three diagram sets on PDF 111 put 2 Fl + 2 Ob (or 3 Fl + 3 Ob) above the trumpets.

Why it stays:
- At 113–114 the trumpets are pp and the chord builds up in stages. The flutes and Ob 1 arriving at 115 on the ff D♭ chord are the last stage of a protected entry order (crescendo chain 110→117; entry orders 9–12, 19–26, 85–97, 110–117, c147). Starting the flutes two bars earlier would make 115 land weaker. The unison of Tpt 1 with Ob 2 and Cl 1 is the softening of brass by woodwind that PDF 106 describes.
- At 162 the flutes and Ob 1 have just left the fff peak (160–161) so that the brass can fade in stages (brass-first fade 158–166, c381, c407). Ob 2's A4 between the trumpets is the PDF 110 enclosure (Ex. 163). c367 finding 3 reached the same result.

### 3. Bars 117–121: Bsn 1 in unison with Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2 on the motto (kept)

PDF 106 calls trombone–bassoon unisons "very rare". Here one bassoon joins two horns and two trombones on the motto's second form at f. At that level the bassoon adds little colour, but it costs nothing either. The bassoon cannot be heard as a separate colour under five brass, so this is not the soft-colour pairing that PDF 106 warns about, and the rule's concern (a weak, mixed timbre) does not arise. The layering 117–121 is protected (c147, c267, c466). Keep.

### 4. Bars 127–130: a soft trumpet octave with flute thirds over the G pedal (considered, not proposed)

This is the one place where the reading suggests a colour the movement does not use. PDF 108 says that "thirds played in the low register of the flutes, sometimes combined with clarinets, produce a beautiful mysterious effect between trumpets in octaves." Ex. 159 (*Snegourotchka*, PDF 332) does this over a pp timpani roll, which is the same situation as 127–130: a G pedal, a timpani roll from pp to f, and the stretto of the half-augmented motto rising to the recapitulation at 131.

I did not propose it, for these reasons:
- The trumpets' entry at 131 (f, on the P rhythm with the timpani) is a fresh entry after their rest from 124. Holding G4/G5 in the trumpets from 127 would spend that entry. The movement's brass plan (coherence/mvt1.md, "Brass use") has the trumpets play only at the tuttis and climaxes.
- The stretto at 127–130 works by adding one imitative voice at a time (Cl 1, violas, Vln II, Bsn 1, Hn 2) over the bare pedal (protected: stretto with timpani roll 126–130, c117; c437). A sustained trumpet octave and flute thirds would cover the clarinet's entry in the same register.
- The movement has no soft trumpet colour anywhere. Bringing one in for four bars of retransition would make it the most unusual sound in the movement at a moment whose job is to build up to the recapitulation.

The texture here already meets the bar a master would set. No change.

### 5. Bars 175–178 and 181–182: strings among soft brass (kept, as c267 and c367)

PDF 113 says strings and brass are seldom crossed or enclosed. In the chorale the violas and Vln II hold inner parts between the trombones, and at 181–182 Hn 3·4 sit between the cellos and the violas. Both passages fill a held chord that would otherwise have a hole (PDF 114: balance matters most in long sustained chords), both are ppp–pp, and both are protected (chorale 175–179; the last chord and the five tied parts into movement II). Keep.
