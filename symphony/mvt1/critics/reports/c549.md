## Findings

### 1. The movement against Tchaikovsky 1/i: keep (no change)

What Tchaikovsky does (Muzyka 1982 score).
- Opening, bars 1-24 (PDF 9-10). Divided violins murmur pp in semiquavers. Flute and bassoon give the theme p, two octaves apart. Violas, then the pizzicato cellos and basses, enter only after about 18 bars. The colour is hollow and soft, and the first f comes late.
- Retransition, bars about 395-455 (PDF 42-46, printed pages 36-40, bar numbers 400, 410, 420, 440, 450 printed). The horns hold pp chords over a cello and bass line, with a dotted triplet figure on the horn chords. They go "poco a poco cresc." for about 30 bars. The full winds then hammer repeated chords ff (about 430-445), and the passage dies away again: decresc., p at 450, Hn 3·4 holding p over the strings. So the recapitulation is prepared by a long soft build and a fall, not by an arrival at full force.
- Ending, bars about 690-724 (PDF 67-69, printed 61-63; 690, 700, 710 and 720 printed). The flute and bassoon theme returns pp, two octaves apart, over the same violin murmur. Basses and cellos thin to pp, mf-sf. The last chord is ppp in the woodwind, horns and trumpets, with pizzicato ppp strings, under a fermata.

Our movement against this.
- The frame works the same way. The low, bare motto at 1-4 (bass clarinet and cellos) comes back at 175-179 in its own register as a pp chorale. Vln I sul D takes the last motto, and the movement ends ppp under a fermata. Tchaikovsky's ending applies Rimsky's operation (e) in reverse: the opening idea comes back unchanged in colour and softer. That shows a return to the first colour can be enough to close a movement, and ours does it.
- Solo winds over a bare string basis are used in both: Tchaikovsky's flute and bassoon; our cor anglais (5-9), Hn 1 (13-14), Fl 1 (73-76) and solo cello with cor anglais (171-174).
- Where they differ. Tchaikovsky's retransition is long, mostly soft, and falls away before the recapitulation. Ours climbs from 111 to fff at 122, collapses at 123-126, and the stretto at 127-130 crescendos into a ff recapitulation at 131. A conductor would read the difference as one of character (tranquillo against appassionato), not of craft. Our dominant pedal is shorter (14 bars against about 60), but it is protected: "117-130", "proportions", "arrivals 47, 131, 147" and "recap first group 131-146 length" (c403, c413, c483, c493). Tchaikovsky 1/i is a quiet, pastoral sonata movement, so it does not answer the reason for those protections, which is the C-minor appassionato plan named in BRIEF4. I do not reopen them.

Fix: none. Not applied.

### 2. Seconds inside one brass colour (Rimsky PDF 102): three places, all kept

A scan of full.midi for held seconds and sevenths inside one brass group, 1.5 beats or longer, finds close seconds in three places:
- 123: Hn 1 G4 / Hn 2 F4 and Tbn 1 G3 / Tbn 2 F3, four beats, after the fff. The second comes from voice leading. Hn 1 and Tbn 1 resolve the minor ninth A♭→G (9-8) while Hn 2 and Tbn 2 hold the seventh F. To put the seventh in another colour, Hn 2 would have to leave F for D4 and Tbn 2 F3 for D3. That breaks two held lines across the barline, adds two more D's (Tpt 2, Ob 2 and Fl 2 already have D) and leaves the seventh only in one instrument. Rimsky himself puts correct progression of parts ahead of distribution (PDF 112: "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts"). The 117-123 brass layout and the 123 downbeat are protected (c399, c445). Keep.
- 134.1: Hn 1 G4 / Hn 2 F4 inside the f P tutti. This layout matches 27-32 and 167-170 on purpose (c090, c139). Keep.
- 159.3: Hn 1·2 G4 (the tune) against Hn 3·4 F4 (the counter). These are two melodic lines, each doubled in woodwind and strings, not a chord. Rimsky's rule is about distributing a chord. Keep.

Fix: none. Not applied. No other brass group holds a close second.

### 3. Same layout for the three P tuttis (Rimsky PDF 118): keep

Rimsky's chief advice for recurring material is to change it each time: other octaves, another key, a wider range, changed details, or a forte phrase repeated piano (PDF 118). Ex. 178-181 (Tsar's Bride overture, PDF 348-350) and Ex. 192-195 (Sheherazade, PDF 358-360) show one theme rescored four ways. The score does this for the motto, S and P, but not for the hammered tutti. 27-32, 131-134 and 167-170 share one layout. That was decided deliberately (c090: "alike on purpose"; c139), and the finale quotes 27-32 at pitch in every part at its 199-204. The three statements already differ in length, key, the instrument on top and their surroundings (131 has the trombones' low P head, 167 the brass head over the C pedal), so they meet Rimsky's operations (d) and (e) in part. Keep.

Fix: none. Not applied.

### 4. Where the score still falls short of a master in this lens

The weaknesses are the ones earlier critics found and protected: the two-bar climb at 156-157 into the recapitulation climax (c523, c533, c541-c544), and the loud span 113-144 (c513). Tchaikovsky's soft, long retransition shows the other way, but taking it would change section lengths and the ff recapitulation, which are protected for reasons this comparison does not answer. I looked for something smaller within the reading that would help either place. Nothing new passed: the 156 additions were tested and blocked before (c534, c544), and a quieter 131 would undo the protected arrival. No change.
