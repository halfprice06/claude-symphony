## Findings

**1. Bars 98–100, Fl 1: an 8va on the shared flute staff. Applied.**
`devFluteOne` has `\ottava #1` from 98 to the middle of 100, inside the `\transpose c cis` block. The highest note is G6, four ledger lines. Gould (ch. 1, PDF 47): "Music for woodwind and strings can use up to five ledger lines before moving into an octave transposition. Octave transpositions are unhelpful, as a player identifies a written pitch with a given fingering." Cycle 5 also set the rule "no ottava on the shared flute staff". Fl 2 is silent here, but the rule was made so that the shared staff is read the same way everywhere. In the Dvořák 7 Mutopia set, the flute files contain no `\ottava` at all; only the Vln I file uses one, for long runs up to c'''' (lines 366, 412). Fix (in dev.ily): delete `\ottava #1` before `des'''2.~` and `\ottava #0` before `es''8 ges''8`. The sounding pitches do not change. On the rebuilt page 8 the notes clear Fl/Ob spacing with no collision.

**2. Vln I 8va at 31, 40–43, 71–72 and 121–123: keep them in the score, but do not carry them into the Vln I part. Not applied.**
Gould (PDF 47): a full score "may replace ledger lines with the octave ... sign as necessary, to save space between staves: These octave signs should not be transferred to a playing part, except where absolutely necessary." MOLA (PDF 5): "Care should be taken with the use of the abbreviations 8va and 8va basso, avoiding their use if possible." None of these passages goes above A♭6. Fix for when parts are made: write each one as `\tag #'score \ottava #1 ... \tag #'score \ottava #0` and build the parts with `\removeWithTag #'score`. The score does not change, so cycle 105's protection of these 8va marks is respected. I did not apply this because no parts are built yet and the tags would have no effect now.

**3. Page turns in the string parts: plan two forced breaks. Not applied (parts only).**
MOLA (PDF 7): "The page layout should allow each player ample time to turn the page ... It is preferable for a multi-measure rest to be at the end of a page." In my trial parts, every wind, brass and timpani part fits on two pages, which can be printed as a facing spread with no turn. The string parts at realistic spacing run 3–5 pages. Vln I, Vln II and Cb have rests of 4 s or more in several places. The viola has rests of 3 s or more only at 32.5–34, 84–88, 103–107 and 128–129. The cello has them only at 32.5–35, 77–78 and 103–110. Fix for the part files: in the Vla and Vc parts, page 1 ends after bar 33 and page 3 ends after bar 103 (`\tag #'part \pageBreak`). Pages 2–3 then hold 34–103 and pages 4–5 hold 104–182, and both turns fall in rests.

**4. Harp pedal letters in the harp part. Optional, not applied.**
MOLA (PDF 5): "Harp pedaling should be left to the performer and not printed." Adler (PDF 112) asks for the starting setting, and says the player "usually will make the pedal change even later than it is marked" (PDF 113). The Berklee guide and Harp Column replies agree that harpists prefer to choose their own changes. For the harp part, keep the diagrams at 47 and 155 and drop the single-letter changes (50, 52, 56–63, 158, 164) by tagging them `\tag #'score`. The score keeps them for the conductor and for checking. This reverses nothing: cycles 6 and 56 placed the letters in the score.

Nothing else I checked would cause trouble in a first rehearsal.
