## Findings

### 1. Bar 46→47: the arrival of the second theme. Doubled seventh, missing third, hidden octave with a leap in the top voice (applied)

What is wrong. At 46.3 the V7 of A♭ is E♭2 (Cb), G3 (Vc), D♭4 (Vla **and** Vln II), E♭4 (Ob 1). On beat 4 the oboe and the cello's G have gone and only E♭2–E♭3 and D♭4 remain, so the dominant has neither its third nor its fifth. Then Vln II, now the top voice, leaps D♭4→A♭4 while the bass goes E♭2→A♭2. A&S says the chord seventh "must never be doubled" (PDF 76), the third is "never omitted except for special effects" (PDF 76), and hidden octaves in the outer voices are avoided "except where the soprano moves by step" (PDF 88, Ex. 5-29b, marked "poor", is this V–I motion). This is the movement's most important join after the first theme: the A♭ arrival under the horn and cello tune.

Fix (trans.ily, transVlnTwo, last bar):
```
des'1\pp | es'1 | es'1 | des'2 des'2 |    % before
des'1\pp | es'1 | es'1 | des'2. g'4 |     % after
```
Vln II keeps the unison with the viola on beat 3, then takes G4 on beat 4 and steps G4→A♭4 into 47. Beat 4 now has its third, the viola alone carries the seventh to C4, and the top voice reaches the octave with the bass by step (Ex. 5-29a, "good"). The leap D♭–G lies inside the dominant seventh, which PDF 82 says makes a dissonant leap "logical". I tested two other versions first. B♭4 on beat 3 made a hidden fifth F4→B♭4 over D♭→E♭ and covered the oboe's last E♭4. E♭4–G4 made parallel octaves D♭→E♭ with the bass. Both are rejected. The oboe link 45–46, the horn and cello pickup, the bass and bar 47 are unchanged, so the protected medial caesura and fill (41–46) and 47–55 keep their design. Applied.

### 2. Bar 145: doubled leading tone in a pp dominant (applied)

What is wrong. At 145 the V7 is G1 (Cb), **B2 (Vc)**, G3 (Hn 4), **B3 (Bsn 1, Hn 3)**, D4, F4, G4. The leading tone sounds in two octaves for a whole pp bar, in the thin texture just before the recapitulation's second group. Neither doubling resolves: the cello stops and Bsn 1 stops. Hn 3 alone carries B3 on to C4 at 147 (c153). A&S calls the rule against doubling the leading tone "virtually absolute" (PDF 76). Inner voices should move mostly by step or common tone (PDF 80), but the cello falls a fourth, D3→B2, only to stop.

Fix (recOne.ily, recOneVc):
```
c2\f d2 | es2.\> d4 | b,1\pp |      % before
c2\f d2 | es2.\> d4~ | d1\pp |      % after
```
The cello keeps D3 as a common tone, the octave below the violas' D4, which it already doubles at 144. The chord stays complete with a single B, and Hn 3's protected hold and resolution are untouched. Applied.

### 3. Bar 165→166: the leading tone does not resolve at the last V7–I of the recapitulation (applied)

What is wrong. At 165 the V7 (G2 bass) has B3 in Hn 3. At 166 Hn 3 drops to G3, and no voice moves B→C: Vln I's B5 is a passing note in the scale B–A–G–F–D–C, and the viola's B3 on 165.4.5 moves to E4/G4. Against the cello's F3→C2 this also makes a d5→P5 (B3–F3 to G3–C2), which A&S marks "less good" because the tritone does not resolve (PDF 83, Ex. 5-20b). The tendency of the leading tone towards 1 is the reason for the doubling rule on PDF 76. Hn 3's line, B3–G3 followed by C4 at 167, is two leaps where a step is available (PDF 80).

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoHnThree, bass clef):
```
R1 | aes2\p( g2) | b1 | g1\mp |      % before
R1 | aes2\p( g2) | b1 | c'1\mp |     % after
```
Hn 3 now plays B3→C4 at 166 and holds C4 into the coda's C4 at 167. The C-major chord at 166 stays complete (G in the violas), Hn 2 and Hn 3 share C4 at mp, and the E♮→E♭ turn (Cl 1, Hn 1, Vla) is untouched. Applied.

### 4. Bar 16, beats 3–4: dominant without its third (keep)

At 16.3–16.4 the chord is G1, D3, D4, F4 with the tune's A♭4→G4. B is missing, against PDF 76. Every way of adding B to the accompaniment needs a dissonant leap: Vla E♭4→B3 is a diminished fourth, and Vc E♭3→B2→F3 is a diminished fourth followed by a diminished fifth (PDF 82). The tune sounds B4 on beat 2, and the gap lasts one second at crotchet = 126. The notes of 15–22 are protected. Keep as written.

### 5. Bars 12–13 and 26–27: leading tones doubled in tutti (keep)

At 12 the Ger6's F♯ is in Hn 3 (F♯3) and in Ob 2 and Vln II (F♯4), and both move to G (octaves in vl.txt). At 26→27 the stop chord's B is in the bass and in Tpt 1, and both rise to C. Under PDF 76 both are doubled tendency tones. PDF 84–85 exempts doublings in a free orchestral texture made "for emphasis", and PDF 87 (points 2–3) says dense, dissonant textures soften such motions. Both are f–fff tuttis. The horn spelling of the Ger6 ("graded brass 12") and the trumpet B→C (a cyclic thread) are protected for stated reasons, and the reading gives no grounds against them. Keep. For the same reason I keep Tpt 1/Cl 2 G4→C5 over the bass G→C at 142→143, the eighth-note tutti stroke.

### 6. Bar 162: the two fifths in vl.txt (keep)

Cl 1 F5→E5 against Vln II C6→A4, and Tbn 2 F3→E3 against Vc C4→A2. In both pairs one string part changes its doubling (Vln II leaves the top line, Vc leaves the horns' line for the bass), so these are the fifths "through doubling" that PDF 85 says "do not count". The four real lines (top C6→A5, horns, F→E, bass G→A) contain no fifths. Keep.
