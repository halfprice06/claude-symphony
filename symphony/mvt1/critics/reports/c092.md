# Critic 092: harmony lens, inner-voice voice-leading (v111)

## What I studied
I built a MIDI with one track per instrument (no part-combining) from the v111 section files, and wrote three scripts (in /tmp/critic-092/scripts). `allvl.py` splits each instrument's chords into ranked voices and checks every pair of voices for parallel fifths, octaves and unisons. It counts octaves and unisons only when both voices move independently before and after the motion, and skips pairs inside one instrument and leaps of an octave or more. All fifths are counted. It also checks root-position V(7)→I for doubled leading tones and for how each leading tone and seventh moves. `diss.py` lists inner-voice notes that are dissonant with the bass (2nd, 7th, 9th or tritone) and leave by leap. `xml2json.py` runs the same checks on the corpus MusicXML. I also re-ran the cycle-2 checker (`tools/critic-scripts/vl2.py`).

Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`), fugato 171–188 and recapitulation second theme 305–320. Dvořák 9/i (`Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`), development 177–272 and recapitulation 300–360.

Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III "Number of harmonic parts: duplication" (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). He says consecutive octaves between upper parts are not allowed, but extra parts that duplicate the real harmony in octaves are fine. He also says the bass of a dominant inversion "should never be doubled in any of the upper parts", and the same for seventh and diminished-seventh chords. M. DeVoto, Tufts orchestration handout (https://sites.tufts.edu/markdevoto/files/2015/10/FIFTHS.pdf): parallel octaves from doubling run all through orchestral texture. Aarden and von Hippel, MTO 10.2 (https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.04.10.2/mto.04.10.2.aarden_hippel.php): the doubling rules exist to prevent parallel octaves, so check the voice-leading, not the doubling as such.

I checked the corpus hits by hand. Tchaikovsky 183–184 has fifths between the strings' 16th-note scale (F♯–G) and the bass B–C. Dvořák 205–211 has viola/Vln II tremolo figures in fifths against the cello's A–F♯. Dvořák 254–257 is block-chord planing. So the masters allow fifths in fast figuration and in planing, and octaves where tutti doublings meet. The inner parts of v111 are already stricter than both models. I found no doubled leading tone that resolves in octaves anywhere in the focus passages. The faults below are few and local.

## Findings

**1. Bars 110–111, Violins II divisi: octaves with the bass, and the leading tone leaps (applied).**
At 110 the lower half holds C4 (the doubled bass of the cadential 6/4) and goes to F4 at 111 while the bass leaps C→F. That gives octaves on the 5→1 cadence. The upper half's leading tone E4 leaps up a diminished fourth to A♭4, and no voice in that register takes F. My script missed the octaves in v111 because the lower half rested at 109. The cycle-2 and cycle-12 critics fixed this kind of fault elsewhere (doubled resolutions, cadence octaves). Fix (dev.ily, Vln II 111): `<c' f'>1:32\mp\cresc` instead of `<f' aes'>1:32\mp\cresc`. E resolves to F, C is held as a common tone, and A♭ is still in Ob2, Cl1, Hn1, Tpt1 and Vln I.

**2. Bars 108–109, Violins II lower half: the seventh of B7 is dropped (applied).**
The lower half plays `g4( a2.)` and then rests at 109. A3 is the seventh of the B7♭9 (cycle 72) that slides to B♭ minor. At p the texture is only flute plus viola, Vln II and the bass, so a seventh that stops is heard as a gap. Read in B♭ minor, A works as a leading tone. Fix: `b1\pp | g4( a2.) | bes2( des'2 | c'1\<) }` (lower voice). A goes up to B♭, then D♭ goes down to C at 110. I chose D♭ on beat 3 because holding B♭ to 110 gives octaves with the cello's B♭2→C3. This changes protected notes in 103–110 (cycles 74/84). Their reason was the f/p colour plan, and this change keeps it: same instrument, same dynamic, same divisi. It only gives the dropped seventh a resolution.

**3. Bar 112, Hn 3 and Tpt 3: the leading tone in the bass of V6/5 is doubled (applied).**
Bar 112 is C7/E (V6/5 of F). Hn 3 and Tpt 3 have E4 above the bass E2 and E3, against Rimsky-Korsakov's rule for dominant inversions. Fix (dev.ily, Hn 3 and Tpt 3): `c'1\p\cresc | c'1 | es'1 |` instead of `… | e'1 | …`. They hold the root C, which merges with the viola's C pedal. The bass, top line, harmony and dynamics of the wedge are unchanged (protected "wedge bass 111–116"). The mid-register chromatic doubling E♭–D–D♭ at 113–115 remains. I left 114 (D in the bass of G7/D) alone because there D is the fifth, not the leading tone. If the composer wants it strict there too: Hn 3 and Tpt 3 at 114 `c'2 b2`.

**4. Bars 94–95, fugato: the cello free voice moves in octaves with the bass (applied).**
Vc and Bsn 1 play B♭3–E♭3 against the bass B♭–E♭ (BCl, Bsn 2, Cb). That is an octave pair on the V–I bass leap, in a passage where the cello is meant to be a free third voice (cycles 7 and 17). Fix (Vc and Bsn 1): `bes2\f bes2~ | bes2\< des2` (Bsn 1 without `\f`). B♭ becomes a held common tone. I rejected `ges2`: it would double the viola's F4→G♭4 resolution on beat 2, a fault that cycles 2 and 12 removed elsewhere. Subject, countersubject and entries are untouched, which is what the "fugato notes" protection guards.

**5. Bars 177–178, coda chorale viola: the seventh leaps (not applied; leave).**
In the vii°7 at 177, viola A♭3 goes to E♭4 instead of G. The textbook fix (`g2 aes2` at 178) makes parallel fifths G3→A♭3 over C→D♭ on beat 3. I tested it in the scratch copy and reverted it. The existing leap is the smaller fault, and the A♭/E♭ alternation is the viola's own figure (178–181). No change.

**6. Bars 158–159, climax: octaves in the tutti (not applied; leave).**
Cl 1 goes F4→G4 with the bass F→G into 158. The counterline (Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 3) goes C5→B4 with the Tpt 3/Tbn 1 pad C4→B3 into 159. The second case is the cadential 6/4's 4–3 doubled at the octave at fff, and the first joins the tune's first note. Both are tutti doublings of the kind Rimsky-Korsakov and DeVoto accept. The passage is also protected (climax scoring 158–166).

**7. Bars 32–33: the seventh rises across the rest (not applied; optional).**
Ob 2 and Tpt 2 go F4→G4 after the G7, while Hn 2 takes F→E♭. Cycle 42 fixed the parallel recap chord (134–135) and passed this one because of the two-beat rest. If wanted: Ob 2 and Tpt 2 at 33 `es'8`. This is outside the focus passages.

Also checked and fine: 103–106 (D♯ doubled only as the octave-doubled S head in Hn 1/Bsn 1); 147–157 (sustained violin sevenths resolve, CA fills); 165–166 (the seventh resolves in Cl 1 and Hn 1; the violins' F–D–C is a passing line); the 175–179 chorale (Tbn 1 doubles the bass at the octave, Tbn 2 and Vln II double each other); the Cl 1 B♮ at 177 is one of the planned unresolved B♮s (cyclic-notes.md).
