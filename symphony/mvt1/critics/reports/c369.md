## Findings

### 1. Nothing has got worse between v359 and v369 (no change)

I diffed the section files, global.ily and score.ly of v359 and v369, and rebuilt v369 in /tmp/critic-369/work. Each change at c360–c368:

- c364: Ob 2 stops after A4 at 163 (`a'4.\> r8\! r2`). This thins the winds one bar after the ff release, which is the Ex. 286 pattern (row 17, PDF 460–461): winds and brass leave first, strings carry the diminuendo. Better.
- c365: "medium sticks" printed over the timpani at 25. Notation only. Better.
- c366: part-only "take … beater" texts, removed from the score with `\removeWithTag #'part`. The score shows nothing new. Neutral.
- c368: the violins swell from 58.4 and fade through 59 (`des''8\< f''8 | aes''2--\> g''2)`), and the rit. now ends at 68.2 with Tempo dell'Allegro at 68.3. The high A♭5 is reached by a rising third after a step (row 21, PDF 155), and the fade gives the line a breath before 60. The new tempo starts on the half-bar (beat 3 of 68), so the conductor beats the change on a strong beat. Better.
- c359's cor anglais "in F" staff names are still in place.

The build is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty, score.pdf has 18 pages, analysis.txt lists only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1", and the vl.txt of the rebuilt full.midi does not differ from v369/vl.txt.

### 2. The mf at 60 after the c368 fade (no change)

The violins now fade at 59 and restart `\mf\<` at 60. That could read as a jump in volume, which Rimsky-Korsakov warns against when accompanied and orchestral passages meet (PDF 137–138: avoid a large gap between them). Here it is not a gap. The accompaniment at 60 is p< under the line, so the melody sits one level above a rising bed. The restart marks the phrase entry after the breath. The MIDI velocities step from the end of the p fade (about 70) to mf (about 86), the same step the S takes at 56. I leave it.

### 3. The release after the climax, 162–166 (no change)

Ex. 286 (PDF 460–461) is the clearest model in this reading for what this score does after its last climax. Rimsky-Korsakov lets the winds and brass release within one bar on a hairpin while strings, clarinets and bassoon carry a dim. with ritenuto to ppp, and only then lets the solo voice in p. This score does the same: the tutti ends at 161–162, the winds leave at 162–163 (Ob 2 now out after 163, c364), the strings carry the diminuendo through 166, and the C.a. enters p at 167.4 with the solo cello at 171. The voices sounding per four bars drop from 21.4 (157–160) to 6.6 (173–176). Nothing to change.

### 4. The pp roll and low pedal under a repeated string figure, 1–4 and 111–117 (no change)

Ex. 298 (PDF 482–483) puts a pp timpani roll and a low horn pedal under a repeated quaver figure in the strings and builds by adding parts with poco cresc. and accel. The wedge at 111–117 follows this: strings and winds mp cresc. at 111, trumpets and trombones pp cresc. from 113–114, so the attack count rises from 88 (109–112) to 158 (113–116) and the voices from 12.8 to 25.1. This is the order Rimsky-Korsakov gives for a long crescendo (strings, woodwind, brass, PDF 130), and it is protected (c239, c243). Nothing to change.

### 5. Two brass re-entries at mf, checked again against PDF 136 (no change)

I rescanned full.midi for brass entries after six bars of rest or more (/tmp/critic-369/scripts/reentry.py). All are pp, p or ff except the trombones mf at 12 and Hn 1·2 mf at 78–79. Both were checked by c359 and are protected: 12 by c228 and c308 (graded brass of the introduction), 78–79 by the motto exchanges 77–83. This reading adds support for the first. In Ex. 306 (PDF 497) Rimsky-Korsakov writes the trombones p inside an f chorus tutti, so the brass levels inside one build are graded, not uniform. I do not reopen either. The horn mf entries at 130.5 and the timpani mf at 115 are inside a crescendo already under way, so PDF 136 does not apply to them.

### 6. Overall comparison with Brahms PC1/i (no change)

What a conductor who knows Op. 15 would recognise here:

- **A pedal under the opening theme.** Brahms starts Maestoso, ff, with a timpani roll and horns on a low D pedal while the strings state a theme that implies B♭ over it. The clash between pedal and harmony is the point. This score opens on a low pedal with the motto (1–4) and uses the same device at length at 117–130, where the G pedal carries changing chords. That is row 4 (PDF 133, Ex. 267–271).
- **Trills as a motive.** Brahms makes the trill part of the theme. This score does not use trills as a motive. That is a difference of material, not of scoring, and nothing in the reading asks for it.
- **A lyrical second subject in solo colours, reharmonized on return.** Brahms gives his F major theme to the piano alone, quietly, and recolours it later. This score gives S to horn and cello over harp at 47–55 and recolours it at 97, 147 and 158 (row 26, PDF 165).
- **Weight from the bass in octaves at the returns.** Brahms's ff returns double the bass in octaves. This score does the same at 26–32 and 139–141 (row 24, PDF 160).

There are two real differences. Brahms's movement runs about 22 minutes and this one about 7 minutes 10 seconds. The proportions were measured at c243 and are protected; the movement is a symphonic first movement in a cyclic plan, not a concerto exposition with a double exposition, so its length should stay. Brahms ends his first movement ff in D minor. This one ends ppp on C major, with the timpani pulse and tam-tam, and runs attacca into A♭. That ending is required by the cyclic plan and protected, and it follows PDF 136 (do not end mf or mp). It should stay.

### 7. Ideas from this reading that c269 rejected, checked again (no change)

c269 considered a harp glissando at 158–161 (Ex. 275, PDF 441), mutes at 177–182 and viola soli. I checked them against the examples again. A glissando at 158–161 would put a harp sweep on top of the S climax, where Rimsky-Korsakov's own lyrical climaxes are carried by strings and brass in the melody's register (Ex. 280–281, PDF 448–451); the harp at 162 is already f and protected. Mutes at 177–182 would change the colour of the protected last chord. Viola soli would add a fourth solo colour to a coda that already has C.a., solo cello and Cl 1. I agree with c269 and do not reopen them.
