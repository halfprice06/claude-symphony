# Critic 325: Engraving and notation, beams (Gould, *Behind Bars* ch. 1, PDF 31–41, second pass)

Version reviewed: v325. Scratch copy: /tmp/critic-325/work.

Method. I read every text page from PDF 31 to 41 in full and rendered every page as an image at 110 dpi. I looked at PDF 36 and 37 again at 250 dpi, because the rules on concave, repeated and two-pitch groups depend on the examples. Next I built a probe copy (/tmp/critic-325/probe) that logs all 500 beamed groups in the full score: staff, bar, beat, voice, direction, LilyPond's own positions, the positions after the c235 callback, notehead positions, stem directions, durations and stem x positions. Two scripts (/tmp/critic-325/scripts/an.py, an2.py) test the log against each rule below. I looked at the flagged bars in the PDF at 220–300 dpi. c235 covered the same pages at v235, so this pass concentrates on rules c235 did not enforce and on material changed since v235.

## Findings

### 1. Closely spaced groups slope too steeply (applied)

**Bars:** 85 groups. The most visible are the viola and Cl 2 triplet ostinato 117–122 (32 groups at slope 1.0, notes 1.7–2.9 spaces apart), Vln I 33, 67–69, Fl 69, Hp RH 66 and 68 (0.82–1.0), the Vc arpeggios 60–68 (1.0), and the two-note pickups at Vln I/II, Fl, Ob, C.a., Vla, Vc 59, 66, 72, 101 and 150 (0.64).

**What is wrong.** Gould PDF 34: "Notes spaced very close together horizontally (closer than three spaces) take only a slight angle (¼ or ½ space) regardless of the interval." The example puts the same four notes under a compressed beam (slight slope) and a widely spaced one (steeper). c235 did not enforce this rule. It gave the reason that "a ½-space slope inside the stave conflicts with the rule on attaching both ends to lines (PDF 34–35) for 16ths". That reason does not hold. Both ends can stay attached to lines if the beam slopes 0.18 or 0.35 of a space between the sit, straddle and hang positions on one line. LilyPond itself uses these slopes elsewhere in this score, e.g. Ob 28.3 (2.82 → 3.18). For two-beam groups the slight slope keeps the outer beam hanging (up-stems) or sitting (down-stems), as PDF 32 requires. At 117–122 the viola triplets lie 1.7–2.9 spaces apart and each one rises a full space. On page 11 the result is a row of short steep wedges under the trumpets' ff motto. This is the lattice effect PDF 34 warns of ("Avoid steep angles through stave-lines").

**Fix (applied in global.ily).** I added a fifth case to `gould-beam-positions`. When any two adjacent stems of a group are less than 3 spaces apart and the slope is more than ½ space, a new helper `gould-close-pair` picks new end positions. It keeps the original direction and a slope between 0.15 and 0.5 space. Each end stays on a line quant inside the stave, or at least ½ space clear of it outside. Neither end moves nearer the noteheads, so stems only lengthen. Among the valid pairs it takes the one with the least movement. Kneed beams, beams broken by a line break and groups of three or more beams are left alone. I also tested the rule on the average spacing. That version left the first triplet of 117, whose third note is pushed right by spacing, at 1.0 while the rest of the bar went to 0.5. The minimum gap treats the whole ostinato alike. The Vc arpeggios 56–58 keep slope 1.0, because their notes are 3.0–3.1 spaces apart. 60–68 are closer and now slope ½. This follows the rule's own threshold. At 300 dpi the difference between the two systems is small.

### 2. Two-pitch groups with the odd note farthest from the beam are sloped (applied)

**Bars:** Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 28.1; Cl 1 and Vla 39.2; B.cl 78.1, 110.1; Ob 83.1; C.a. 92.1; Cl 128.1, 132.1; Hn 168.1 (12 groups, slopes 0.18–0.32).

**What is wrong.** Gould PDF 37: "Where there is only one different note at the beginning or end of the group and this note is furthest away from the beam, the beam is horizontal." The groups B.cl 78 (up-stems, positions −2, −2, −3) and C.a. 92 are the textbook case. The repeated note is nearest the beam, and the last note lies farther away. LilyPond slopes them toward the last note. The c235 callback does not catch them, because the repeated note equals the outer note and is not strictly nearer the beam.

**Fix (applied in global.ily).** Case (4) in `gould-beam-positions`: when exactly two pitches are present, one of them occurs once at the start or end, and that note lies farther from the beam, the beam is set horizontal at its end farther from the noteheads. The stem of the odd note lengthens by 0.18–0.32 of a space.

### 3. Three-beam groups at 152.3 are angled into mid-space (applied)

**Bars:** Vla and Vc 152 beat 3: A–B–C–D–E (an eighth beamed with four 32nds), down-stems.

**What is wrong.** Gould PDF 32 (three beams): "Down-stems: the outer beam sits on the stave-line. Any other outer beam position results in a beam ending up in the middle of a space." PDF 35: "With the addition of a third beam, the beams must slant a whole stave-space (a). Any other angle will result in one of the beams beginning or ending in the middle of a stave-space (b)." LilyPond gave the viola a straddle-to-sit slope of 0.18 and the cello a hang-to-sit slope of 0.35. At 300 dpi the inner beams of both groups end in spaces, and the white between beam and line closes up (page 15, bottom). These are the only three-beam groups in the movement.

**Fix (applied in sections/recTwo.ily).** In both voices, `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(-1.824 . -0.824)` goes before the eighth (`a'8` in recTwoVla, `a8` in recTwoVc). The outer beam now sits on the bottom line at the left and on the second line at the right, a whole-space slant as in Gould's (a). The stems lengthen and no pitch or rhythm changes. The parts pick this up from the same section file.

### 4. Ledger-line groups on the middle line stay horizontal (not applied; keep)

Vln I and Fl 28.1, 83.1, 95.1, 97.1 (A♭6–A♭6–G6 and similar, down-stems): only two pitches, and the odd note is nearest the beam. Gould PDF 37 says such a beam slopes. These notes lie on ledger lines, so LilyPond extends their stems to the middle line, and the beam straddles it horizontally. A 0.18 slope would be possible. However, the same figure in the c235-capped flute groups at 27–30 is horizontal or near it, and the passage is a hammered unison in which Vln I and Fl should look identical. The change is below what a reader notices. Keep as it is.

- The Vc 61.3 stem direction stays up, by context (PDF 39).
