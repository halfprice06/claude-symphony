## Findings

### 1. Bars 158–161: the counterline has the clarinet over the oboe in octaves, with one wind above two horns. Applied.

**What is wrong.** At the fff recapitulation climax the S tune is laid out in three octaves (Fl 1·2 + Vln I; Ob 1 + Tpt 1 + Vln II; Hn 1·2 + Vc). The counterline G–C | B–F | E♭–C–B♭–A♭ | A♭–G (recTwoCounter, the countermelody under the climax) was in Cl 1 at G5–C6, with Ob 2 and Hn 3·4 an octave below at G4–C5. That is the layout Rimsky-Korsakov names as wrong: "the clarinet above the oboe ... creates an unnatural resonance occasioned by the confusion of registers" (PDF 67). It also left one wind over two horns in the strongest passage of the movement. He asks for "three or four wind instruments ... above, especially in forte passages", with the formulas 1 Ob + 1 Cl over 1 Horn and 2 Fl + 2 Cl over 2 Horns (PDF 75). His Ex. 90 (*Scheherazade* IV, PDF 265) shows the pattern: two clarinets a2 sit over two horns a2 and the cellos. The same bars also had Cl 2's held E5/D5 above Ob 2's counter notes in 6ths and 3rds (158–160), the inverted pairing PDF 70 calls "strained and forced". For a listener this matters because the counterline is the second thing heard at the climax. With a single clarinet on top of a doubled horn octave, the top of the counterline is weak and its colour is muddy.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-241/work/sections/recTwo.ily).** The two oboes exchange lines. The notes are unchanged except that the counterline moves up an octave in Ob 1.

- **The counterline.** It is now Ob 1 + Cl 1 in unison at G5–C6 over Hn 3·4 at G4–C5. Ob 1 follows Cl 1's rhythm at 161 (A♭5 G5 F5), so the F resolves to Cl 1's E5 at 162.
- **The tune's middle octave.** It is now Vln II + Ob 2 + Tpt 1. That is Rimsky-Korsakov's "most natural" three-group unison, Vln + Ob + Trumpet (PDF 79).
- **Bars 162–163.** Ob 2 keeps c197's continuation, which is the same notes with Vln II, so the tune stays in one player.
- **Order of the oboes.** Ob 1 stays above Ob 2 except for two beats at 159.3–4 (F5 against G5). The part-combiner prints this cleanly.
- **Inner voices.** Cl 2's held E5/D5 now sit inside the oboes and no longer move against Ob 2 in inverted 6ths.

### 2. Bars 107–110: Fl 1 doubles the violas on the S head. Not applied: keep as it is.

The flute is not on Rimsky-Korsakov's list of register-matched partners for violas, which are Ob/C.a., Cl and Bsn (PDF 76). His preferred colour here would be a clarinet or the cor anglais. I do not recommend the change. The bass clarinet carries the P head in the bass at 107–109, its only line of its own (c094). A clarinet on the top voice would put one colour family on both themes, so they would no longer be told apart. The cor anglais would have to play A♭5, at the top of its range, at p. The flute enters only at 109 (D♭5–A♭5, c109/c178), where the violas are on the A string and the flute has some body. The passage is protected (c141, c231). Keep it.

### 3. Bars 117–121: Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2 play the motto in unison. Not applied: keep as it is.

Rimsky-Korsakov's recommended brass unisons are of one kind (4 Hn, 3 Tbn, PDF 73). He places horns with trombones "exclusively in octaves", horns above, and even that is "not so reliable" (PDF 74). A strict reading would put Hn 1·2 an octave above the trombones (G4–E♭5). That would cross the Ob/Cl/Vln II figure at G4–G5 and take the motto out of the dark tenor register. PDF 72 asks for a brass melody in minor to sound "dark and gloomy", and the tenor register is what gives it that sound. Late-Romantic practice uses horn-and-trombone unisons for this weight (Tchaikovsky 4/i). The motto pitches and layout are protected (c186, "brass/horn layout 117–123"). Keep it.

### Themes: judged against the lens

- **The motto.** G–E♭ (a rising minor sixth), then D–D♭–C, at Lento. It has one interval and one chromatic fall, the same kind of hook as the fate motif in Tchaikovsky 5. It returns in a new colour at each statement: B.cl+Vc, C.a., Fl, brass, Vln I. Each choice follows rows 1, 4 and 19 of the table.
- **P (15–22).** It sets the motto in dotted rhythm with a portato on the repeated C. It is an eight-bar sentence that peaks on D♭6 at 20. Its hook is rhythmic, which suits an Allegro appassionato. It stays strings-only until the tutti, as Rimsky-Korsakov's rule 4 advises.
- **S (47–55).**
  - Its leap A♭→F at 48 repeats the motto's rising sixth in major, and its peak C5 at 51 falls to the cadence. It is a long-breathed tune of the Dvořák and Tchaikovsky kind.
  - It is set horn + cello p dolce, the combination Rimsky-Korsakov singles out as "beautifully blended, soft" (PDF 79).
  - Its climax form at 158–161 (G→E, then C→A♭ on ♭VI) brings back the motto's minor sixth at fff. It is the most memorable moment in the movement.
- **Horn call (13–14).** E♭–C–B on a lone horn is a natural-register call for a poetic, soft horn (PDF 71–72). The four-horn version at 41–42 has the power of a horn unison (PDF 73).

None of these themes needs to change.
