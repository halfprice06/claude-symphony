Version reviewed: v445. Scratch copy with fixes: /tmp/critic-445/work.

## Method

I read Gould PDF 41–52 in full and rendered every page at 110 dpi to read the examples (PDF 41 overlapping ledger lines; 43 octave line placement; 44 system breaks and end corners; 45 rests under the line, single notes; 46 clefs before octave signs; 47 score vs part, "recommended / acceptable / incorrect" octave changes; 48 loco; 49–52 rest design and placement).

For the bar-by-bar check I built a probe copy (/tmp/critic-445/probe). A staff engraver tags every notehead and rest with staff, bar and beat, and an after-line-breaking callback logs its final vertical position: 5,400 noteheads and 802 rests. From this I listed every note with three or more ledger lines, and every rest more than two spaces from the centre line together with the notes of both players in that bar. I then looked at each flagged bar at 250 dpi (score pages 3, 6, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14) and at pages 3 and 12 whole.

This reading was assigned in cycles 135, 245 and 335. Their fixes still hold: the c245 rest snapping (all rests on whole stave-spaces), no octave signs in the Violin I part, the c335 removal of the 8va at 31 and 71–72, and the c335 rests at Cl 2 112 and Tbn 1 115. The findings below are new.

## Findings

### F1. Vln I 8va at 121–123 is not needed and hides the octave drop at 124 (applied)

**Bars:** 121–124, Vln I.

**What is wrong.** It is the score's only octave sign on a string staff. It covers E♭6–F6–G6 (three to four ledger lines), the fff A♭6 tremolo at 122 and the G6 tremolo at 123, all single notes. Three things follow from it:
1. At 121 Vln I is written e♭''–f''–g'', identical on the page to Vln II directly below, which plays those notes an octave lower. A conductor reading the two staves sees a unison where there is an octave.
2. The A♭6 at 122 is written at pitch in the flute staff on the same page, and in this same Vln I staff at 31, 41, 72, 140 and 160. The climax at 122 is the only one of the three A♭ climaxes where the violins' top note is written an octave down.
3. The octave drop G6 → G5 from 123 to 124 (protected by c399: "123 A-flat6-G6 downbeat; 124 octave drop") is written G5 → G5 with a "loco". The interval the music makes is invisible.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 47: "A full score that is not used to play from may replace ledger lines with the octave ... sign as necessary, to save space between staves." The same page: "Music for woodwind and strings can use up to five ledger lines before moving into an octave transposition." It also says: "A written change of octave tends to obscure the contour of a line ... Change octave between phrases", and the "incorrect" example on PDF 47 hides a leap inside a figure. PDF 48: loco cancels a transposition when "the same written ... pitch is immediately repeated". That is the case here, and it shows the sign hides a real leap. PDF 46: "Use up to at least three ledger lines before transposing". No note here needs more than four.

**Earlier decisions.** c135, c245 and c335 kept this sign. c335's reasons were: it starts at a system start (PDF 44), covers one gesture, ends with a correct corner and loco, and "spares the divisi tremolo chords at 122–123 four ledger lines in each voice". The last reason is mistaken. Vln I plays single notes at 122–123 (`aes'''1:32`, `g'''1:32`). The divided chords are in Vln II, which has no octave sign. The other reasons show the sign is drawn correctly, not that it is needed. Removing it keeps every page break (14, 30, 38, 46, 63, 72, 84, 102, 110, 120, 130, 138, 146, 157, 162, 170, 182). At 250 dpi the A♭6 and G6 tremolos at 122–123 stand clear of "allargando", "sostenuto" and the tam-tam staff above.

**Fix (applied).** sections/dev.ily, devVlnOne, with a c445 comment:
`| es'''4.\< f'''8 g'''2 | aes'''1:32\fff |` and `g'''1:32\> | g''2.~( g''8 f''8) |` (the `\ottava #1`, `\ottava #0` and the score-only "loco" markup are removed). The parts are unchanged: the Violin I part was already at pitch (c245).

### F2. Two upper-part rests outside the stave with nothing to avoid (applied)

**Bars:** Hn 1·2 102 beat 3 (Hn 1); Fl 1·2 134 beat 4 (Fl 1).

**What is wrong.**
- **102, Hn 1.** Hn 1 has a minim rest while Hn 2 plays the quaver figure F♯3–A♯3–C♯4, labelled "2.". c155 set the rest as a pitched rest `a''2\rest`, on a ledger line above the stave. Hn 2's notes and beam lie below the middle line, and Hn 1's own notes at 101–103 lie on or below the bottom of the stave. The rest is outside the stave on the side away from both players.
- **134, Fl 1.** Fl 1 plays F5–D5 and rests on beat 4. Fl 2 holds a whole-note G5 from beat 1. LilyPond lifted Fl 1's rest to staff position +12, four spaces above the stave, away from Fl 2's held note. That note has no head at beat 4. In the oboe staff directly below, with the same rhythm and the same pairing (Ob 1 F4–D5, Ob 2 G4 held), the rest sits at +4 inside the stave. This is the same LilyPond behaviour that c335 F3 corrected at 112 and 115.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 50: "upper-part rests are usually placed above the centre stave-line", and "A rest moves further from the centre of the stave or to outside the stave should parts otherwise collide". PDF 50: "When one part lies outside the stave (on ledger lines) ... rests for the other part may move back to the centre." PDF 51: "The space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches."

**Fix (applied).**
- sections/dev.ily, devHnOne (bar 102): `d''2\rest` (was `a''2\rest`). The minim rest now sits on the fourth line, above the centre line as PDF 50 requires for an upper part. The c165 offset of the "2." label still clears it (checked at 250 dpi).
- sections/recOne.ily, recOneFluteOne (bar 134): `d''2-> \once \override Rest.staff-position = #4 r4`, level with the oboe rest below.

### F3. Two Horn 4 rests below their own notes (applied)

**Bars:** Hn 3·4 62 beat 4 and 126 beat 4 (Hn 4 rests; Hn 3 holds).

**What is wrong.** Hn 4's crotchet rest sits at position −12 at 62, where Hn 4's own note is A♭3 at −8. At 126 it sits at −14, where Hn 4's own note is G3 at −9. In both bars LilyPond pushed the rest away from Hn 3's held note (C4 at 62; B3 tied from 126.2), which has no head at beat 4. The other Hn 4 beat-4 rests at 112, 115 and 119 sit within one space of Hn 4's notes. c335 accepted those, and I leave them.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 51: rests outside the stave sit "on the same level as surrounding pitches"; "when a note is on a ledger line, place the rests ... below the bottom stave-space". PDF 50: a rest moves further out only to avoid a collision.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tTwo.ily, tTwoHnFour: `aes2.\subP \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-8 r4`. sections/dev.ily, devHnFour: `g2.\p \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-10 r4`. Both were checked at 250 dpi: no contact with the "sub. p" or "p" below, or with Hn 3's notes. Rest overrides are stripped from the parts (parts-lib.ily `shared-staff-grob?`).

### Where the music could go further (within this lens)

I looked for places where the notation hides what the music does. F1 is the one I found: the octave drop at 124 is a structural gesture (c399), and the score now shows it. I found nothing else in the notation of ledger lines, octave signs or rests that blurs a melody's contour or a doubling. The harp 8va at 56–59 keeps each arpeggio at one octave (PDF 48), and the harp may use octave signs (PDF 47). I propose no change of pitch, rhythm or level.
