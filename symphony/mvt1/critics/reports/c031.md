# Critic 31 (lens 1: melody), version v50

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, HISTORY, LENSES, cyclic-notes.md, the v50 critique, report c030, and all nine section files of v50. I sang through every line that carries the tune from bar 1 to 178 by reading the section files, and I checked page 14 of score.pdf (139–153). I built a MIDI with one track per instrument (30 tracks, no part-combining) and ran `vl2.py` with the fifth-run filter switched off, plus `clash.py`, on v50 and on my scratch version. I also counted the rising sixths in the top lines.

Corpus (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`, MCTL numbering, read with music21). I compared the second theme's three appearances:
- **89–100:** Vln I and Vc in octaves. The peak is reached by a plain leap (93: A4 half, then A5 G5 F♯5 in even values).
- **130–141:** the same tune at full orchestra. The leap into the peak is now filled with a rushing scale: bar 134 `A5 | A5 B5 C♯6 D6 E6 F♯6 G6 → A6`, and again at 138.
- **305–320 (recapitulation):** the leap is plain again at 309, but now in dotted rhythm (F♯6 E6. D♯6). The scale-run into the peak returns at 313, in Fl, Vln I, Vln II and Vla together. Bars 317–320 add a new chromatic continuation.

So Tchaikovsky keeps the head intact and refreshes later hearings by ornamenting the approach to the peak.

Web sources:

Coverage at https://gold.ac.uk/news/scientists-find-key-to-writing-catchy-pop-hits and https://www.newswise.com/articles/psychologists-identify-key-characteristics-of-earworms (press reports; I did not reach the paper itself).

With repeated exposure, listeners' attention shifts to longer spans, and short repeated units are detected less well. A third literal hearing of a 9-bar phrase therefore gets less local attention unless something inside it changes.

## Findings

### 1. 139–147 (recTwo section bars 1–9): a Tchaikovsky-style scale-run into the phrase peak at 144 refreshes the third hearing without touching the head
**Problem.** The phrase repeats 47–55 note for note (up a major third) for five bars. The only melodic change is the cycle-15 minor sixth a'→f'' at 144–145. The listener knows the phrase by now (critic 30's drift point). Its one high point, the f'' at 144 beat 4, is approached exactly as in the exposition: a bare leap from beat 3. That is where Tchaikovsky changes his later hearings (134, 138, 313).

**Cyclic rules.** cyclic-notes.md fixes only the anacrusis and two-bar head (138–140). The "head rhythm in every appearance" protection (cycle 20) is not affected. Bar 144 is the phrase's continuation, not its head.

**Earlier fixes.** Cycle 15's minor sixth is kept as the frame of the gesture (it starts on a' and lands on f''). The sul tasto/CA texture (cycles 18 and 27), the horn's octave (cycle 16) and Cl 1's f'' entry are unchanged. The exposition (52) keeps its plain leap, so the ornament is new at 144 and nowhere else, as in Tchaikovsky.

**Fix (tested).** In recTwo.ily, section bar 6 = movement bar 144. Beat 3 becomes an eighth plus four 32nds, inside the existing slur, so no new slur is opened.
- recTwoVla: `c''4.(\> b'8 a'4\! f''4~ |` becomes `c''4.(\> b'8 a'8\! b'32 c''32 d''32 e''32 f''4~ |`
- recTwoVc: `c'4.(\> b8 a4\! f'4~ |` becomes `c'4.(\> b8 a8\! b32 c'32 d'32 e'32 f'4~ |`

Parts left as they are:
- recTwoClOne still enters on `f''4~` at beat 4, so it doubles the arrival note.
- recTwoHnOne (`c'4.(\> b8 a2\!)`) does not go to the peak.
- No other part doubles this line in 143–146.

**Harmony and part-writing.** The bar is F major (Bsn 2 f, Cb f, Vln I a'' and Vln II f'' tremolo). In the run, b′, d″ and e″ are passing notes and c″ is a chord tone. The run lands in unison with Vln II's f''. Vla and Vc move in octaves, which is a doubling.

**Test.** Scratch build at /tmp/critic-031/v50: `tools/build.sh /tmp/critic-031/v50 score.ly` gives rc=0 and 0 warnings. Page 14 engraves cleanly. vl2 with the fifth-run filter off gives output identical to v50 (no new parallels), and clash.py reports nothing at 143–146.

**Playability.** At ♩=100 the four 32nds take one eighth note. This is a single-bow slurred flourish, standard in violas and cellos (Tchaikovsky gives the same run to the violas at 313).

**Optional.** If the composer wants the event stronger, Cl 1 can join the run: `r2 r8 b'32( c''32 d''32 e''32 f''4~` with the slur moved, replacing `r2. f''4~(`. I did not test this, and I recommend strings alone, which keeps the clarinet's entry soft.

### 2. Listener's sing-through 1–178: where the tune is unclear
The tune is handed on with overlap almost everywhere:
- Vln I to Hn 1 at 13;
- Hn 1 to Cl 1 at 52;
- Vc/Hn to violins at 55 and 147 (held note under the pickup);
- Cl 1 to Vln I at 173.

Bars where a first-time listener may not know which line is the tune:
- **97, beats 1–3:** the tune rests. Only the T1 tail sounds (Bsn/Hn/Tbn `c'8 b8`) and the arpeggios start. This is a deliberate breath before the Vln I pickup. Fine.
- **109–113:** two simultaneous melodic layers, the eighth-note head imitations on top and the augmented brass motto. Protected (cycles 7, 14 and 17). It is deliberately ambiguous and works because both layers are the motto.
- **123–126:** the T1 head is in the tenor (Cl, Bsn, Tbn, Vc) under the ff counterline in Fl/Ob/Vln. Protected (cycles 17, 19 and 27). It is heard because the trombones carry it at ff.
- **167:** the cello's minor second theme starts on the downbeat after the fermata with no anacrusis. The rising sixth comes only in the CA one bar later. Protected (cycles 20, 29 and 30). The listener recognises the theme at 168 (c'4 a♭'2), which is enough.

No bar outside these has a buried or unaccompanied hand-off. **No change recommended.**

### 3. cyclic-notes.md cites a passage that does not exist (document only)
The note gives the minor head form `g4 | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4 aes'2 g'4` as "mvt1 bars 166–168". But:
- at 166, Vc plays `d'2.-> r4\fermata`;
- the cello's 167–168 has no G anacrusis;
- the CA's G–E♭ at 167–168 continues with the motto (c''2 des''4 c''4), not the theme.

The minor head with its anacrusis exists in movement I only in B minor (97–98). Change the reference to "the cello at 167–168 without its anacrusis; with anacrusis, transposed, at 97–98". Movement II/III composers will otherwise look for a C-minor model that is not in the score.

### 4. Motivic saturation of the rising sixth: noted, no change
- Vln I leaps up a sixth 45 times, in 41 different bars (Ob 1: 31; Fl 1: 27).
- Every theme opens with it: motto, T1, T2, codetta, the fugato subject and the coda.

This is the Brahms 1/i or Beethoven 5/i kind of saturation and serves the cyclic plan, so I do not argue against it. One consequence for the composer: the ♭VI peak at 156 (c'''→a♭''') comes after about 40 earlier such leaps in Vln I. It is marked as the peak by register, the cymbal and the harmony, not by the interval. That is correct, and it means no later revision should take register or weight away from 156.
