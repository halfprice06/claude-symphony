## Findings

### 1. 97–102: the background figure sits in the register of the melody's lower octave (APPLIED in scratch)

**Problem.** In the B-minor block the theme is in three octaves: Fl 1 (octave 6), Vln I + Ob 1 (octave 5), and Vln II sul G + C.a. (B3–D5). Its lower octave shares the register D4–B4 with:
- the eighth-note arpeggio figure, played by the violas and both clarinets a2 (the clarinets play the same arpeggio inverted);
- the brass/bassoon counterline (Hn 1, Hn 2, Tbn 1, Bsn 1, F♯3–E4);
- the held Hn 3·4.

At 98 beat 1 the melody's D4 (Vln II, C.a.) is in unison with Hn 3, Cl 1 and Cl 2, directly above a B3 in five parts (Hn 1, 2, 4, Tbn 1, Bsn 1). The clarinets then move away in eighths, so the line is blurred instead of reinforced. Vln II is marked ff sul G precisely so this octave carries (c141), but it is covered.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 135: background should stay out of the melody's register when foreground and background have similar colours (here, strings with strings, plus clarinets that blend with both).
- Adler PDF 144: clarity in counterpoint comes from "thinning the counterpoint to let the main theme break through" and from "separating theme and countertheme registerally"; dynamic marks alone are "not always as effective".
- Adler PDF 142: the three layers must reach the listener "in the exact order of priority".
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 54: a melody in the middle of the orchestral range is "not so prominent" and needs help.
- Grieg, *Åse's Death* 19–24 (Adler PDF 155): the second violins reinforce the melody's lower octave instead of adding more parts around it.

**Answer to the cycle-159 closure** ("Cl a2 with Vla is normal accompaniment doubling"). The doubling as such is not the problem. The problem is where it sits: in the same octave and in a similar colour as a foreground line the score has deliberately strengthened with sul G. The violas alone still supply the figure, so cycle 17's viola figure is kept. The parallel fifths of the clarinet arpeggio at 99 (closed earlier as acceptable figuration) disappear as a side effect.

**Fix (applied).** In `dev.ily`, `devClOne` and `devClTwo` replace the `\transpose c cis { des'8\f f' bes' f' … }` block for bars 97–102 with `R1*6 |`. Nothing else changes; the clarinets re-enter at 103 as before. If the composer wants the clarinets' colour in this block, the alternative is for Cl 1 to double Vln II/C.a. (foreground) instead of the violas (background). I did not test that alternative, because the line crosses the clarinet break around written B♭4–C♯5.

**Note.** 117–121 has a similar layout (Vla + Cl 2 triplets under Vln II's lower octave). I checked it: Vln II's line there rises to C5–A♭5 above the held E♭4 brass after each pickup, so it is not covered. No change.

### 2. 140–141: the violins' A♭6 anticipates the final climax at 160 (NOT applied; reopens a settled point)

**Problem.** Vln I's ceiling, A♭6, is reached at 31 (ff), 72 (f), 122 (fff), 140–141 (ff) and 160 (fff). The last occurrence before the recapitulation climax comes 19 bars earlier in the same section, with the same instrument, register and string (E string).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 129, on Haydn's last variation: "too often the extremes in the range are wasted too early in a work, and the final buildup is, as a result, anticlimactic." Haydn kept the octave transposition back until the final statement. Adler PDF 133 makes the same point about Brahms 3/iii: a higher first statement would make later statements "anticlimactic and subsequent restatements anticipated".

**History.** Cycle 23 raised the A♭6 count. Cycle 24 closed it ("156 peak by weight"), and cycle 61 set the rule "keep A♭6 to its current six bars". The settings 140–142 are also protected (Neapolitan settings, lower-octave doubling). My answer to the "weight" reason: Adler's point is about register, not mass. Adding flutes and unison violins at 160 makes the peak louder, but it does not give the ear a note it has not just heard at 140.

**Fix (tested in /tmp/critic-184/work2, not in work).** In `recOne.ily` (`recOneVlnOne`, bars 139–142), Vln I plays an octave lower, in unison with Vln II, as the violins do at 56–59:

```
r8 g'8\downbow\f\<( f''4.->) es''8( d''8 c''8 | des''8\ff es''8 f''8 g''8) aes''2->~ | aes''4. g''8 f''4 d''4 | c''2->\downbow b'2->\downbow |
```

Fl 1 keeps A♭6, so the pitch peak of 140 survives in a different colour, and the violins' E-string A♭6 is next heard at 160.
- Test result: clean build, 18 PDF pages. The all-pairs voice-leading output differs from work only by octave doublings that shift register; there are no new fifths or octaves between independent voices.
- I left it out of the main scratch copy because it reverses c24 and c61 and touches protected bars. The composer should decide.
- A wider version would also treat 72 (the codetta's A♭6 at only f). That would change the theme combination's shape in a protected cadence, and I do not recommend it.
