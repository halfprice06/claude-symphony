# Critic 387: v387, lens: texture and counterpoint

Reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 3, "Voicing the strings" and "Combining strings with winds" (sebesky PDF 133–173), second pass. The first pass was c287 (no change).

I read every text page in the range (PDF 133–173). I rendered every page in the range at 110 dpi and looked at every page that has music: Ex. 187–216 on PDF 133, 135, 137, 139–148, 150–163, 165, 167, 169–172. I read the register line of Ex. 190 from the image: it runs from A3 in the bass clef to E5 in the treble clef. Ex. 199 (PDF 147) and Ex. 207 (PDF 159) show two principles c287 did not use. Ex. 199: do not let a long run of four-note chords fall back to a bare triad or open a seventh between the cellos at the arrival. Ex. 207: when the cellos divide, the doubled players go on the top line unless an inner line carries an important melodic passing figure.

I checked v387's full.midi with my own scripts (in /tmp/critic-387/scripts):
- seconds between string notes with the lower note at F5 or above;
- string parts holding three or more notes at once;
- gaps of more than an octave between adjacent sounding pitches, in the full ensemble and in Vln II–Vla–Vc alone;
- beats where the whole ensemble has only one or two pitch classes (no third);
- unison doublings of each wind with each string section;
- bars where the lowest cello note is in octaves or unison with the double basses for at least three quarters of the bar;
- inner-string leaps larger than an octave.

I also looked at all 17 pages of music in score.pdf at 80 dpi.

## Findings

Every principle in the chapter is met in v387, so I recommend no change. My checks found nothing c287 did not see. The one string second above E5 is a passing quaver at 96. No string section divides into more than two lines. Every gap of more than an octave inside the lower strings is filled by winds. The only beats without a third are the bare C–G at 121.3–4 (protected motto end) and single-line or two-line passages (35, 80–81, 90, 127–129).

Below are the four places where this reading comes closest to asking for something else, ranked by weight, with the reason each stays. None of them repeats c287's five items. I made no change in the scratch copy.

### 1. 56–68: the cello arpeggios start in unison with the basses (keep)

The reading: rule 7 says not to double the bass with the lowest cello by habit (PDF 140). In Ex. 194 the bottom cello takes the root only "for temporary richness and depth ... but not to reinforce the bass" (PDF 146).

The score: from 56 to 68 the cellos play quaver arpeggios, and each half-bar group begins on the bass note at the double basses' pitch, not an octave above them. The basses are in their upper octave here (sounding E♭2–F2–G♭2). For example 64.1 F2/F2, 64.3 D2/D2, 65.1 D♭2/D♭2, 67.1 E♭2/E♭2, 68.1 F2/F2.

Why it stays: what Sebesky warns against is a held doubling that grounds the voicing and clouds the bass line. Here the shared note lasts one quaver per half-bar. The cello leaves it at once, and its arpeggio spans up to an octave and a sixth (A♭2–E4 at 56, F2–D♭4 at 67). The sustained bass is the double basses alone, which is what Sebesky praises at the end of Ex. 194: "The arco bass, supplying the chordal roots all by himself, is now part of the string ensemble" (PDF 146). Two other fixes would be worse:
- Moving the basses down an octave (to E♭1–F1) would make the bottom heavier in a p–mf passage that Sebesky would keep "floating" (PDF 140). It would also put movement II's climax bass G♭–F♭–E♭ (at II 62–65, an octave lower) into movement I.
- Starting the arpeggios a beat later would weaken the harmonic rhythm that the violins' octave tune needs.

The violas 60–67 and the Vc/Cb pp at 56–61 are also protected (c277, c358).

### 2. 147–155: the pad sits above the tune ("left hand, right hand" reversed) (keep)

The reading: Sebesky's left-hand/right-hand layout puts the violins high on a unison line and the lower strings on a chordal pad, with the third present somewhere (PDF 144).

The score: in the recapitulated C-major S, the tune is in the violas and cellos in octaves with Hn 1. The pad is Vln II, divided, sul tasto tremolo, above them (E5/G5, C5/A5, D5/A5, ...). The double basses play pizzicato roots and the cor anglais adds fills.

Why it stays: Sebesky's own examples use the reverse layout when the colour needs it. In Ex. 202 the lower strings carry the line under the violins (PDF 151). In Ex. 203 the violas carry the lead an octave down and keep it "in order to maintain the same string ensemble color throughout the passage" (PDF 153). The third is always in the pad (E5 at 147, C♯/D♭5 at 148, A5 at 152), as PDF 144 requires. The reversal is the planned contrast with the exposition's Hn 1 + Vc statement over harp at 47–55 (both protected). The sul tasto → ord. change at 155–156 then gives the Ex. 202 increase in body at the approach to K (PDF 151).

### 3. 164–165: two-octave gap between the cellos' low notes and the violas (keep)

The reading: in Ex. 199 Sebesky has the second cello play double stops at the climactic cadence. Without them, "the gap of a seventh between the cellos ... would have diluted the sonority" (PDF 147).

The score: at 164.1–165.2 the cellos are on D2/G2 and the violas are on F4/D4 or resting, so the strings alone leave a gap of up to two octaves and a minor third.

Why it stays: Sebesky's case is strings alone. Here the gap is filled by Bsn 1·2 (D3/G2), Hn 3 (A♭3, G3, B3), Hn 1 F4, Hn 2 C4, Cl 1 and the harp chords. That is the mixed-ensemble internal resonance of PDF 168, where each group contributes its own complete part to the overall chord. The bars are the protected dissolution 162–166, with the cadential 4–3 and Hn 3's B3→C4.

### 4. Horn and bassoon on the cello line (keep)

The reading: a horn on a unison cello line "generates a good deal of emotional intensity and should be used sparingly". A bassoon or bass clarinet strengthens a low cello line (PDF 166).

The score: Hn 1 doubles the cellos on the S head at 46–52, 103–106 and 146–150 (there with the violas), and Hn 1·2 join them on the tune at 157–162. Bsn 1 doubles the cellos in 13 spans, most of them tutti strokes or the tenor line of the loud tuttis (27–32, 139–143, 162–170).

Why it stays: the horn doubling appears only on statements of the S head, the theme it belongs to. That is four places in 182 bars, and the fourth is the fff climax, where Sebesky's intensity is the point. The bassoon doubling is the use he recommends without limit. Removing Bsn 1 from 139–142 would undo c317's Tbn 2 doubling of the same tenor line, and removing it from 27–32 would change the passage the finale quotes at pitch.
