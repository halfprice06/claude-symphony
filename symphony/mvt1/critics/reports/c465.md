## Findings

No change. I found no place where the score or the parts break a rule from this reading, and no engraving change that would bring the page closer to a master's edition.

I also asked whether something inside this lens holds the music back: a dynamic, hairpin or slur that hides a phrase, or a layout that makes a climax hard to read. I checked the three places where the page carries the most weight.

1. **The climax 158–162 (score p. 16).** The fff at 160 is printed in every part that has the tune or the counterline, with the held low brass at ff under it (in place since v240–v251; protected by c449). The diminuendo at 162 ends at mp in the trumpets and trombones and runs through 163 to p at 164 in the horns. The largamente is over the top staff and Vln I. A conductor can read the level of every group at a glance. This is what MOLA PDF 8 asks of the vertical line.
2. **The four-horn call at 41–42 (score p. 5).** "a2" and "molto espr." are on both horn staves, and ff is under the first note. The call reads as one gesture of four players, which is its point.
3. **The coda 171–182 (score p. 18; Vln I, Hn 3, timpani and C.a. parts).** Every tempo step (Più lento, rit., Tempo I) is in every part, also during rests (MOLA PDF 5, Ex. 4). The Vln I part has the Cl. 1 cue before its pp entry (c355). The Hn 3 and Hn 4 parts have the timpani pulse as a cue before the ppp entry at 181. "attacca" closes every part. The tied parts end on even pages, so nobody turns into movement II.

I did not find an engraving change that would make any of these pages clearer.

## Checked and kept

- **Multi-bar rests broken at 103 and 111 in the timpani, percussion and horn parts.** These parts have no key signature, so the double bar at a key change shows them nothing new. MOLA Ex. 4 breaks rests at letters and tempo changes. It neither asks for nor forbids a break at a key change. The double bars at 103 and 111 are the section joins the conductor beats (theme combination, wedge), the ranges "103–110" and "111–116" let the player check the count, and merging them would put the parts out of line with the score. No change.
- **Composer name on the cover and the part fronts** (MOLA PDF 6). Closed at c200; I do not reopen it.
- **"draft v465" in the score subtitle** (MOLA PDF 6: the title as in a concert programme). It stays until the release checks at cycles 520 and 620 (c429).
- **Tutti entries at 26, 69, 82 and 158 without cues** (MOLA PDF 5). c265 and c295 left them uncued because the whole orchestra enters on the downbeat with the conductor's cue. They are on no letter or tempo change, but the reason still holds.
- **Twelve staves on Hn 3 p. 2, Hn 4 p. 2 and the viola p. 2** (MOLA PDF 7: "up to twelve … as long as symbols are not crowded"). At 110 dpi none of these pages has a collision. Fewer systems would push the tied parts onto an odd page.
- **One-line percussion staff** (MOLA PDF 4, high to low). The cymbal and tam-tam never play together and are named at each entry (c135).
