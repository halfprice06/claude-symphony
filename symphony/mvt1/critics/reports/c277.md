## Findings

### 1. Bars 56–59: the violas' off-beat dyads duplicate three other layers in the lower-middle register (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 56 the violins (I and II in unison, p, molto cantabile) restate S. Under them the score has six other layers:
1. the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline, p espr.;
2. Hn 3·4, a pp pad;
3. the cellos' eighth-note arpeggio on beats 1–2;
4. the harp's broken chord on beats 3–4, above the tune;
5. Cb arco and harp LH roots;
6. the violas, divided pp, playing off-beat dyads `r4 <c' es'>4 <c' e'>2 | r4 <des' f'>2 <des' f'>4 | r4 <des' f'>2 <des' f'>4 | r4 <bes des'>2 <des' g'>4`.

Every viola pitch is already sounding at the same octave in another layer:
- **Bar 56:** the violas' E4 on beats 3–4 is the top of the cello arpeggio (`e'2`) and the note on which the counterline moves (`r8 e'8 f'8 g'8`). Their C4 is Hn 3's whole note.
- **Bar 57:** the violas' F4 is the counterline's F4 (beats 1–2). Their D♭4 is the cello arpeggio's top D♭4.
- **Bar 58:** the violas' D♭4/F4 sits on Hn 3's D♭4 and the cello's D♭4. The counter repeats F4 and descends F–E♭–D♭ through the violas' held F4 and D♭4.
- **Bar 59:** the violas' D♭4 is the counterline's D♭4 and Hn 3's D♭4, three instruments in unison on beats 2–3.

This is the case of Sebesky's Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13). An inner line in the lower-middle register adds no pitch and no rhythm that is not already there. It blurs the one moving secondary line (the counter), and the tune is only p in a single octave. Sebesky's rule is direct: a note that does not make "a legitimate difference to the score as a whole … should be erased" (PDF 12). Adler gives the same reason for at-pitch doublings of similar colours: they "swallow each other up" (adler2 PDF 229). Principle 3 (PDF 11) also applies. The violas had the pad at 47–55 and now simply carry on, so their figure at 60 (`r8 des'8( ges'8 bes'8 des''4)`, unis., with the crescendo) does not register as a new entrance.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/tTwo.ily, tTwoVla):**
```
  c'2.^"unis." r4 |
  R1*4 | r8 des'8(\p\<^"unis." ges'8 bes'8 des''4) r4 | ...
```
The violas rest 56–59 and enter p< at 60 with their own figure. This is where Bsn 2 also enters (c167, for the same reason). From 56 the restatement now has five layers (tune, counter, horn pad, cello and harp arpeggios, bass). At 60 it grows by addition into the mf. No chord tone is lost:
- 56 (A♭+): the cello and the counter have E, and Hn 3 has C.
- 57–58: Hn 3·4, the cello and the counter cover D♭ and F.
- 59: the cello has G3, and the tune has G5 on beats 3–4.

The protected "violas 60–67" (c327) are untouched, and this does not undo an earlier fix.

### 2. Bars 64–67: the violas also double the counterline's long notes — keep

At 64–66 the viola dyads again sound C4, D♭4 and D♭4 with the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counter (`c'2 r8 c'8 d'8 es'8 | des'2 aes'2 | des'4 …`) and with Hn 3. This should stay. Here the tune is f, in two octaves, with Fl 1 doubling Vln I. The counter at mf needs the extra weight, so the doubling makes "a legitimate difference" (Sebesky PDF 12), which it does not at the p of 56–59. The passage is also protected (c327, "violas 60–67").

### 3. Bars 147–150: the recapitulated S head has the same harmony as 47–50 — keep

The bass at 147–150 (Bsn 1, Cb: C–F–D–G) is the exposition's A♭–D♭–B♭–E♭ moved to C: I–IV–ii–V both times. This is against the letter of Sebesky PDF 19 (Ex. 3/3A). It stays, because Sebesky's aim, which is to avoid repetition that loses the listener (PDF 20), is met in other ways:
- The theme is reharmonised at its other returns: an A♭ pedal with A♭+ and D♭6/4 at 56–57, B minor at 97, and C minor i–iv–iv–V at 171–174.
- At 147 the colour is new: Hn 1 + violas + cellos over Vln II's sul tasto tremolo, with cor anglais fills in the tune's held notes. That is Ex. 2A's method (PDF 18).
- A recapitulation's job is recognition. The passage is also protected (47–55 and 147–155; c327, c357).

### 4. Bars 162–165: dense middle register in the dissolution — keep

At 164 F4 sounds in three parts: Hn 1 (whole note), Cl 1 (beat 1 of the counter) and the violas (beats 2–3 of their falling fill). The cellos' D2–A♭2–D3–F3 arpeggio sits under them. This looks like Ex. 1, but the result differs. The violas' fill falls F4–E4–D4–G3 while Cl 1 rises A♭4–G4–D5, and that contrary motion is what Sebesky's cut was meant to expose (PDF 13), so here it can already be heard. Hn 1's F4 is the held common tone into V7 at 165. The scoring of 158–166 is protected (c236, c252). No change.
