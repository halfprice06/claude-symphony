# Critic 057: v76, lens 7 (texture and counterpoint): suspensions in the development

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY and c053, and all of v76's section files. I rebuilt v76 and `all.ly` (one MIDI track per player) in /tmp/critic-057/base. Then I measured three things:
- **c053's per-beat dissonance index**, using the same weights and code (/tmp/critic-053/s/curves.py), so the numbers are comparable with c053.
- **A count of suspensions.** A suspension here is a held note that becomes a 2nd, 7th or tritone against a new attack and then falls by step. Script: /tmp/critic-057/s/susp.py.
- **How much of the index comes from notes shorter than a beat** (passing tones and figuration). Script: /tmp/critic-057/s/decomp.py.

Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i and Beethoven 9/i (MusicXML in `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`, read through c053's JSON exports; bar = MusicXML index + 1, as in c053).

Web:
- Open Music Theory 2e, "Fourth-Species Counterpoint": chains of 7–6 and 4–3; 7–6 and 4–3 are preferred because they resolve to imperfect consonances; no more than three of the same type in a row. https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Open_Music_Theory_2e_(Gotham_et_al.)/02%3A_Counterpoint_and_Galant_Schemas/2.05%3A_Fourth-Species_Counterpoint
- CMU species handout (the same three-in-a-row limit): https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/johnito/music_theory/CTP/species/4thSpecies.pdf
- mymusictheory, "Suspensions": the resolution note must not sound in another upper voice while the suspension is held. https://mymusictheory.com/?p=35592
- Essays on Music, "What is a suspension?": a 4–3 may be prepared by a dissonance; a 9–8 needs a consonant preparation. https://essaysonmusic.com/what-is-a-suspension/
- The Open Music Theory "embellishing tones" page on viva.pressbooks returned 403.

## Measurements

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Share from notes shorter than a beat | 76% | **91%** | 68% | 58% | 25% | 48% |
| Distinct suspensions per bar | 0.38 | 0.18 | 0.21 | 0.21 | 0.58 | 0.41 |

v76 already has more suspensions per bar than either model. The models' developments are dissonant mainly because of short notes: chromatic passing tones and scale figuration (Tch 6/i 182–189 strings), and chromatic contrary motion over a moving bass (Tch 205–222). When the models do use suspensions, all the doubling voices suspend together:
- Tch 6/i 207 and 209: bassoons, trumpets and trombones together (F>E, A♭>G);
- Tch 6/i 210–214: Vln I, Vln II and Va together;
- Beethoven 9/i 242–251: a wind chain of suspensions almost every bar in Ob/Bsn, while the strings carry the subject.

The index also scores a 4–3 (sus4) as less dissonant than the minor-seventh chord it delays. Suspensions therefore add tension in time (delay and expectation) more than they raise this number.

**Dissonance index for the scratch copy (after the changes below):** development 77–122 **1.037 → 1.062**; dev core 85–108 1.089 → 1.115; bars 91–93 0.29/1.20/0.98 → 0.29/1.35/1.32; 117–120 0.22 → 0.36; coda 0.966 → 1.005. Exposition (1.303) and recap first group (1.422) are unchanged.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Fugato 91–95: the free voices hold whole and half notes while the bass changes. (Applied)
**Problem.**
- At 91 the Vc holds c'1, and at 94 the Va holds f'1. Their next notes arrive with the bass, so the downbeats of 92, 93 and 95 are plain chords (index 0.95, 0.10 and 0.95).
- Beethoven 9/i 242–251 is the model. While the strings carry the subject, the winds suspend across almost every barline.

**Fix.** Subject and countersubject are unchanged.
- **Vc 91–93:** `c'2. es'4~ | es'4 des'4 c'2~ | c'4( bes4) g2 |`
  - A 4–3 over B♭ at 92. E♭ is prepared as the 7th of F, which is allowed for a 4–3.
  - A 7–6 over D♭ at 93, prepared as the octave of C.
- **Bsn1 93:** `f2 g2` (was `des'2 c'2`). This removes a D♭ that would have doubled the bass a minor 2nd above the suspended C. It also removes the old Bsn1–bass octaves at 93 beat 3.
- **Va 94–95:** `f'1~ | f'4\< ges'4 bes2 |`. This is a 9–♭10 retardation over E♭, rising by a semitone. It keeps cycle 17's line e'–f'–g♭', one beat later.

No other voice sounds a resolution note (D♭ at 92, B♭ at 93, G♭ at 95) while its suspension is held.

### 2. Wedge 105–106: the G→F step is the only downward step common to the inner parts, and all of them take it on the barline. (Applied)
**Problem.**
- 106 is the only change of chord in 103–108 where the inner voices fall by step: G becomes F over the bass E♭→D.
- Eight parts make this move together on the downbeat.

**Fix.** Tie G over the barline in every part that carries it, so that 106 starts with a 4–3 over D:
- Cl2, Hn2, Tpt2: `g'1~ | g'4 f'2.`
- Hn4, Bsn1, Vc: `g1~ | g4 f2.`
- Tbn1: `g1~\mf\cresc | g4 f2.`
- Vln II: `<g' c''>4:32 <f' c''>4:32 <f' b'>2:32`

No F sounds on beat 1. The model is Tch 6/i 207/209, where the suspension is in every doubling. Notes in 103–105 and 107–108 are unchanged (cycle 21 left 106 open).

### 3. Dominant pedal 118–120: a resolved 6/4 and then two bars of a single clarinet over G (index 0.03 at 120). (Applied)
**Fix.**
- **Bar 118:** delay the 6/4's C→B by one beat in every part that carries it:
  - Cl2 and Hn3: `c'4\p b2.`
  - Va: `c'4\p b2.~`
  - Vc: `<g c'>4\p <g b>2.`
- **Bars 119–120:** the violas keep the leading tone under the Cl1 motto: `b1~ | b2. r4`. The motto's D–D♭–C and the C–A♭ in 119–120 then pass against a held B.

The motto stretto, timpani, pedal and entries at 121–122 are untouched. Shared staves keep equal dynamics (Cl1/Cl2 and Hn3/Hn4 are both p at 118).

### 4. Coda 167: four parts on a static C-minor triad for 3½ beats under the cello's held E♭. (Applied)
**Problem.** The rest of 167–170 moves:
- the cor anglais suspends D across 168–169;
- the viola moves in half notes at 169–170.

Bar 167 is the only static bar.

**Fix.** Va 167: `c'2\pp b2` (lower neighbour, back to C at 168). It forms the major 7th over C on beats 3–4 and anticipates the B of 170. It is a step-wise neighbour, so it needs no preparation. The iv–N6–i6/4–V of 168–170 is untouched.

### 5. Recapitulation 123–138: checked, no change recommended.
- **123–126 and 131–134:** sustained wind and brass pads under ff tuttis. These already have an offbeat viola figure, an eighth-note viola turn and a moving bass. Added motion would blur the low theme and the counterline above it (protected).
- **127–130:** block chords on purpose, to articulate the hemiola, as at 23–26.
- **135–138:** the protected dominant link under the oboe's appoggiatura.

### 6. Development 77–84 and 97–100: checked, no change.
- **80–81** are C minor, and the i–VI at 82 is intentional (cycle 13).
- **97–100:** a viola lower-neighbour figure would add short-note dissonance in Tchaikovsky's manner, but it would undo cycle 17's inverted arpeggio for little audible gain.

### 7. Not recommended: a G–A♭ triplet neighbour in the cellos at 109–113 (as in Tch 6/i 205–222).
It would raise the index the most of anything I tried, but it states A♭ in the bass before the A♭ crash at 114 and weakens that arrival.

### 8. Note for the rule from cycle 53.
- Suspensions alone cannot bring the development above the exposition (1.30) on this index. The models get there through figuration in shorter notes (91% at Tch 161–244).
- Any further rise would have to come from chromatic passing tones in the protected fugato or B-minor lines.
- I suggest restating the rule as "add suspensions and chromatic passing tones in non-thematic voices", without a numeric target.
