## Findings

### 1. Bars 103–108: the second-theme head has no phrasing dynamics in the theme combination (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 103–106 the second-theme (S) head is in Hn 1, with Bsn 1 and the cellos an octave below, at mf. Against it Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 play the first-theme head at f with accents. At 107–110 the violas take the S head at p espr., and the bass clarinet answers with the P head. Every other statement of this tune is shaped bar by bar:
- At 47–50 and 147–150 the held first note swells and falls (`c'2.~\< c'8 bes8\>`).
- The tenuto minim of the second bar has its own `--\<`.
- The eighth-note pickup to the peak has a `\<`.
- The peak minim falls `\>`.

The violins at 56–57, the B-minor ff statement at 97–102 (c248) and the solo cello at 171–174 have the same marks. At 103–105 and 107–108 the line is printed flat. The only marks are the `\>` at 106 and the `\<` at 110, which leads into the wedge.

This passage is the one place where the two heads sound together. The P head is f and accented. The S head is a level lower and moves in long notes. With no swells, the players will hold the long F4/F3 at 103 and the G4 at 107 level, and the S line will be heard as a held chord under the P head rather than as a second tune. The layering of eighths against long notes is protected, and it works only if the long notes move.

**Evidence.**
- Adler's Franck example (adler2 PDF 38, Ex. XI-20, viewed as an image) gives the theme the same per-bar `< >` in every part that carries it. This holds in its soft statement ("p molto espress." in cor anglais, clarinet, bass clarinet and violins) and in its f climax ("f molto sosten."). The cellos and basses that double it carry the same marks.
- The Berlioz example (PDF 188, Ex. XV-33) puts the tune's hairpins on its long notes. Adler's point on the same page is that the dynamic belongs to the line and to the instrument carrying it.
- The score already follows this rule everywhere else, and c248 made it explicit at 97–102. Bars 103–108 are the gap.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/dev.ily).** I copied the hairpins of 47–49 onto the tune parts. Pitches, slurs, tenutos and the mf/p levels are unchanged, so the protection on "notes and f/p contrast of 103–110" holds.


- **Bar 106 and bars 109–110.** The existing `\>` at 106 now ends the pickup crescendo, as at 50. Bars 109–110 are unchanged. Fl 1 joins there, its dynamic is marked "settled" (c167, c178), and the `\<` at 110 leads into 111.
- **Bsn 1.** Bsn 1 shares its staff with Bsn 2, which plays the bass D2–A2–B♭1–B1 here. The score rule asks for equal simultaneous dynamics on shared staves, so Bsn 1 stays unmarked. It doubles the cellos in unison, and the cellos carry the shape.

**Earlier fixes.** This undoes nothing. It extends c248's rule (97–102) to the next two statements. The notes of the theme combination, the f/p contrast and the first bars of both heads (c103, c111) are untouched.

### 2. Bars 145–146: Hn 3 takes Bsn 1's B3 at the barline with no overlap (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 144 Bsn 1 answers the violins' P head and arrives on B3 at 145, pp, held for the whole bar. Since c347, Hn 3 enters on the same B3 on the downbeat of 146, at the moment the bassoon stops, and holds it into C4 at 147. Nothing else sounds B3 at 146: the strings and clarinets hold G, D and F, and Hn 4 rests. So the leading tone changes colour exactly at the barline: one instrument releases and the other attacks the same pitch at once. In a poco rit. at pp, the horn's new attack will be heard as a bump on the most exposed note of the bar.

**Evidence.**
- Adler's example of colouring a held note (adler2 PDF 191, Ex. XV-37, viewed as an image) passes one C from oboe to flute to bassoon to clarinet to cor anglais. Every entry is at the same level (pp), and each new instrument enters while the previous one still holds.
- The dovetailing rule on the next page (PDF 192, Ex. XV-40) says that at a colour change "the seams don't show" unless an accent is wanted. Its first example overlaps, and its second, unoverlapped example is the one that gives the accent.
- The same overlap is used for subito piano on PDF 190 ("It is essential that the two chords overlap").
- The score does this at 180–181, where Tbn 1's C4 overlaps Hn 3's entry (c140), and at 72–73.

**Fix (applied, sections/recOne.ily).** Hn 3 enters pp on beat 3 of 145, after the bassoon's B3 has sounded alone for two beats. It holds the note while the bassoon finishes the bar, and it continues alone at 146:

```
recOneHnThree 143–146:  R1*2 | \partCombineApart r2 b2\pp~-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "3." \partCombineAutomatic | b1 |
```

On the shared Hn 3·4 staff, the part-combiner had printed the tied B3 under Hn 4's "4." label. The two voices are now kept apart from 145.3, and a score-only "3." marks the entry. PDF page 14 shows a half rest, then "3." and B3, with Hn 4's G3 below it. The Hn 3 part shows `r2 F♯4~ | F♯4` (written) at pp.

**Earlier fixes.** This partly reverses c347, which moved the entry from 145.1 to 146. c347's reason was that the horn should not strike the bassoon's arriving note at the same instant. That reason still holds: the arrival at 145.1 is heard alone, and the horn joins two beats later. The wind V7 at 145 (Hn 4 G3, Bsn 1 B3, Cl 2 D4, Cl 1 F4) and the B3→C4 resolution into 147 are unchanged.
