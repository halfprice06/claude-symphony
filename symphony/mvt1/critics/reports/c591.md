## Findings

### F1. The horn call: five statements, three metric forms, each one a treatment the reading names (kept)

What this shows. The interval content never changes (rising minor sixth, falling semitone or tone), so a listener knows the call each time. Its rhythm shifts in the bar, which PDF 52–53 (Ex. 3-9a/b) gives as normal for a rhythmic group. Each shift is a treatment the reading names: suspension (13, 125), appoggiatura (42), syncopation across the bar (43–44) and the plain metric form (176). The c581 recall at 125–126 copies 13–14 exactly, so the minor call returns unaltered before the recapitulation. That is the right choice for recognition: the four-horn form at 41–42 has a different rhythm, and only an exact repeat of 13–14 lets the listener connect the two soft solo statements. The B3 is left unresolved both times, which keeps the finale's B→C in reserve.

Hypermetric position (PDF 53): the two full statements (13–14, 41–42) begin on a strong bar of their group. The two soft echoes (43–44, the third and fourth bars of 41–44; 125–126, the fourth and fifth bars of 122–126) sit on weak bars. This is consistent: statement on a strong bar, echo on a weak one. The c581 addition fits the scheme the movement already had at 43–44. **Keep.**

### F2. The motto's D♭: on beat 3 in seven statements, on a downbeat once, at fff (kept)

PDF 49–50: chromatic notes carry a built-in accent. PDF 50: beat 3 is the secondary accent in 4/4. The motto's chromatic neighbour D♭ falls as follows: 3.3 (B.cl, Vc), 7.3 (C.a., A♭ in the fifth-higher form), 75.3 (Fl 1, F5 in the A♭ form), 173.3 (C.a.), 178.3 (Tbn 1 chorale) and 180.3 (Vln I, last motto). In the trombones' second form at 117–121 it falls on the downbeat of 120, held three beats, ff into the fff at 122. In the P head (15.4, 27.4, 131.4, 167.4) it is a passing quaver inside the beat.

So the chromatic accent and the metric accent coincide fully only once, at the loudest motto of the movement, where the dominant pedal builds to the climax at 122. In every soft statement the D♭ has the lesser accent of beat 3. That is a graded use of what PDF 50 describes, and it means the downbeat D♭ at 120 sounds heavier than any other statement. **Keep** (final motto 177–182 protected: c228, c251, c328, c501).

### F3. The S head keeps one rhythm in all five statements; the climax uses its second bar (kept)

PDF 52: rhythmic groups are "an important element in the design of a composition". A listener recognises a tune by its rhythm at least as much as by its pitches. The extracted rhythm of the S head (four bars) is identical at 47–50 (Hn 1, Vc), 56–59 (violins), 98–101 (B minor, ff), 147–150 (C major, Hn 1, violas) and 171–174 (solo Vc, minor): dotted minim plus quaver and quaver | crotchet, minim, crotchet | dotted crotchet, quaver, crotchet, two quavers | minim, minim. The one variant is the violins' semiquaver turn at 56.4 (D♭–C–B♭ replacing the single B♭ quaver). That is a figuration inside the beat (PDF 49, Ex. 3-4 level) and leaves the rhythm on the beat unchanged. Mode, key, register and tempo change across these statements; the rhythm does not. That fixed rhythm is what makes S identifiable in the development and the coda.

The second bar of S (crotchet, minim on beat 2, crotchet: a minim begun on beat 2 and held across beat 3, PDF 51) is the theme's distinctive syncopation. The climax at 158–161 is built on it: G5 on 158.1, then E6 from beat 2, and C6 on 160.1, then A♭6 fff from beat 2. The movement's highest note therefore enters with S's own syncopation rather than a plain downbeat. A&S PDF 50 gives the high register a built-in accent, so the peak keeps its weight on beat 2. Because the rhythm is S's, the climax sounds like the theme reaching its peak, not like a separate event. **Keep** (158–161 layout protected: c431, c491).

### F5. Improvements weighed and rejected

- **Put the 125–126 recall on strong bars (124–125).** At 124 the fff collapse is still fading (tam-tam, upper parts). The horn would enter inside the decay of the climax, and the call would land on the stretto's a tempo at 127 instead of finishing before it. The weak-bar echo position also matches 43–44 (F1). Rejected.
- **A downbeat D♭ in the coda motto (180) for more weight.** This would take the one downbeat D♭ away from the fff second form (F2) and change the protected final motto (c228, c251, c328, c501). Rejected.
- **A♭6 on the downbeat of 160.** This would remove the S syncopation that ties the climax to the theme (F3) and alter the protected climax layout and its tenutos (c262). Rejected.
- **Restore 13–14's rhythm in the four-horn call at 41–42 (long C).** A longer C was tested by c581 and gave parallel fifths. F1 also shows that the short-C appoggiatura form gives variety the reading supports. Not reopened.

No candidate met the bar of a real improvement, so the scratch copy is unchanged.
