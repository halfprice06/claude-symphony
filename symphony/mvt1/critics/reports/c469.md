## Holistic comparison with Bruckner 4/i

These points come from the published score (IMSLP, https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.4_(Bruckner,_Anton)), not from the local corpus, which has no Bruckner.

- **Opening.** Bruckner opens with a horn call over pp string tremolo. A long single crescendo wave, built by adding instruments, then reaches the first ff unison tutti. v469 opens with the motto low and bare (B.cl, cellos, basses, timpani roll ppp). The cor anglais answers it a fifth higher. The rising-sixth chain crescendos 9–12 to the German sixth, and Hn 1 plays the call at 13–14. The order matches Rimsky's crescendo by addition (PDF 130: strings, then woodwind, then brass) and principle 1. A conductor would accept this as a master's slow opening on a smaller scale. Bruckner's first wave is about fifty bars; this movement's whole introduction is fourteen. That fits a 182-bar movement of c. 7½ minutes.
- **Song group.** Bruckner's second group is a polyphonic string texture: a viola melody with a violin counter-figure. Here, the horn + cello S over harp and pad (47–55) and the violins with the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline (56–63) are the same kind of setting: melody, independent counterline, and a held harmonic basis in a different timbre (principles 3, 10, 16).
- **Brass as structure.** Bruckner puts loud brass at the main joints and a brass chorale in the development. v469 keeps the trumpets back and uses the brass at the joints: stop chords 26/138/170, the trombone motto ff 117–121, the trumpets' fff entry at 122, and the brass attack at 158. Its chorale is pp in minor at 175–179. Principle 14 says a culminating point should coincide with a brass attack, and v469 places all three: 122, 158, 160.
- **Ending.** Bruckner ends 4/i with the horn call grown to ff in the tonic. v469 ends ppp and goes attacca into movement II. The loud major apotheosis is held back on purpose for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"), so this is the cyclic plan, not a weakness of this movement.
- **Where the gap to Bruckner remains.** Bruckner sustains long crescendo waves over pedal points. v469's longest build is 111–122 (twelve bars). Lengthening any of them would change the protected proportions and section lengths (c413, c433, c463). The reading gives no support for that; principle 1 is about order, not length. No change.

## Findings

### 1. Trombones' first entry at mf, bar 12 — considered, not applied
**Bars:** 12, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 E♭3, B.tbn A♭2, all mf (intro.ily `introTbnOne`, `introTbnTwo`, `introBassTbn`), under winds, horns and strings at f.

**Reading:** Rimsky, PDF 136: after a long rest, horns, trombones and tuba should return at pp or ff, because an mf or mp entry "produces a colourless and common-place effect". This is the trombones' first note in the symphony, and it comes at mf.

**Why it stays:** the mf is a balance grading, protected since c124 ("brass doublings and dynamic grading at 12"). It follows Rimsky's own balance rule in ch. III, where one trombone weighs about as much as two horns. At 12 the horns hold the augmented-sixth F♯3 as a pair (Hn 3·4, c284) and Hn 1 and Hn 2 each play alone. Three trombones at f would cover the F♯ that defines the chord. A pp entry would need the trombones in bar 11, which means new notes in the iiø7. Rimsky's PDF 136 rule is about how an entry sounds; here the chord balance matters more. **Not applied.**
