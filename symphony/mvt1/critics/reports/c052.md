# Critic 052: harmony and voice leading (lens 2), version v71. Focus: large-scale harmonic design

## What I studied
I rebuilt the one-track-per-instrument MIDI from the v71 sources (`all.ly`) and labelled the chord on every beat (bass plus the best-fitting triad or seventh chord, scripts in /tmp/critic-052/s). I ran the same labeller on the corpus MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i, Dvořák 9/i and Beethoven 9/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`), so the harmonic-rhythm figures compare like with like. I checked the results against MCTL's hand analyses (`annotations/{Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1,Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1}/form/harmony.csv` and `cadence.csv`), with bar numbers as given in the MCTL MusicXML. I re-ran critic 42's `clash.py` and `son.py`, and `tools/critic-scripts/vl2.py`.

Web sources: Wikipedia, "Borrowed chord" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borrowed_chord), which lists iiø7 and ♭VI as the usual minor-mode chords borrowed into major. Hutchinson, *Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom*, ch. 19 "Mode Mixture" (https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Music_Theory_for_the_21st-Century_Classroom_(Hutchinson)/19%3A_Mode_Mixture/19.01%3A_Mode_Mixture). Aziz's review of Caplin, *Analyzing Classical Form* (https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.14.20.1/mto.14.20.1.aziz.html), on standing on the dominant before a thematic arrival. Wikipedia, "Augmented sixth chord" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augmented_sixth_chord).

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 135–139: the dominant before the recapitulation's second theme is too short and arrives too late. (Applied)**
In the exposition the arrival at 47 has V/V at 39–40 and V7 of A♭ for six bars (41–46). Before 139 the bass stays on C until 136.2, so G sounds for only 2½ bars (136.3–138), at pp under a held chord. The models give the recapitulation's dominant at least the weight of the exposition's:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i (MCTL harmony.csv) stands on V of B from 290 to 304 before the B-major theme at 305: 15 bars, against 4 before the exposition's 89.
- Beethoven 9/i prepares the D-major second group at 345 with 335–344 (V4/2/V, ii4/2, V7, I, V7), 10 bars of 2/4, the same length as its exposition approach (71–80).

The fix needs one note. At 136 the upper strings already move as a cadential 6/4: Vla `es'2( d'2)`, Vc and Bsn 1 `es2 d2`, and Vln I's C resolving to B over A♭–G. Only the double bass's C on beats 1–2 turns this into a root-position tonic.
recOne.ily, `recOneCb`, bars 136–137: `g,,1\> | g,,1\pp~` (was `c,2\> g,,2 | g,,1\pp~`).
`recOneTimp`, bar 136: `g,1:32\mp\>~` (was `R1`), tied into the existing pp roll at 137.
Result: i6/4 → V7♭9 → V7 over three full bars of G (136–138). The elision chord at 135, the oboe G–E pivot at 137–138 and the "poco rit." are unchanged.

**2. Bar 160: all five perfect cadences of the second group use the same diatonic approach. The structural cadence closing the recapitulation should be coloured. (Applied)**
The cadences at 54–55, 66–68, 70–72, 146–147 and 160–162 are all approached by a root-position ii(7) → V(4–3) → V7 → I. None uses a chromatic pre-dominant. The models colour exactly this cadence:
- Tchaikovsky's recapitulation second group closes on vii°7/V – I6/4 – V7 – I at 313 and 333 (MCTL cadence.csv).
- Beethoven 9/i approaches the cadence at 369 through ♭II.

Here the melody's D on 160.1 rules out a German sixth or vii°7/V without changing the tune. The borrowed iiø7 (D–F–A♭–C) keeps every note of the tune, the D–G bass and the protected 4–3 at 160.3. Its A♭ recalls the ♭VI climax at 156, and it prepares the E♮→E♭ turn at 162, so the minor coda is foreshadowed one bar earlier. Mode-mixture sources list iiø7 as the standard borrowed pre-dominant in major (Wikipedia; Hutchinson ch. 19).
recTwo.ily, bar 160:
- `recTwoArp`: `d,8( aes, d f) g,2`
- `recTwoHarpR`: `<aes'' d''' f'''>2\arpeggio`; `recTwoHarpL`: `<d, d>2_\markup \small "A♭" <g,, g,>2` (pedal change; there is no A in the harp chords at 161–162 or in the coda)
- `recTwoHnThree`: `aes2\p a2` (a chromatic rise A♭–A–B into 161, which avoids a minor 2nd against Hn 4's G at 160.3)
- `recTwoCounter`: `f'4 aes'8( b'8) d''2~` (a diminished-seventh arpeggio; the old A♮ would have made a false relation)

The étouffez stops the harp's A♭ before Hn 3's A♮. I do not recommend the same change at the EEC (66): keeping that cadence diatonic leaves the darkening for the end of the movement.

**3. Colour chords: inventory. The Neapolitan and C's own vii°7 carry nearly every C-minor approach; vii°7/V and the German sixth are missing from the Allegro. (Not applied; most bars are protected)**
- **Neapolitan:** pre-dominant at 7, 20–21, 132–133, 169, 174 and 176. That is six cadential uses in 178 bars. Beethoven 9/i has about nine ♭II labels in 547 bars; Tchaikovsky 6/i has one.
- **B–D–F–A♭ (vii°7 of C):** at 11, 26, 106, 130, 133, 157, 166 and 173. With D in the bass at 26, 106, 130, 157 and 173. The same set appears as V7♭9 at 28, 83, 114 and 124.
- **vii°7/V (F♯–A–C–E♭) in a C-minor context:** none. Tchaikovsky 6/i uses vii°7/V 22 times; it is its most frequent chromatic chord.
- **Augmented sixths:** only 12 (Ger6, Lento) and 108 (Fr6 → V at 109).
- **♭VI** (82, 156) and the **chromatic mediants** (A♭m–E–Em–C at 77–80; ♭VII–iv at 60–61 and 152–153; the B-minor shift at 97) are well placed and not overused.

The Neapolitan density comes from the motto's D♭. Its chromatic descent D–D♭–C also rules out vii°7/V or a Ger6 wherever the motto sounds (133, 135–136, 169, 173 were checked). So this is a consequence of the cyclic design, not a fault to fix bar by bar. The rule for later revisions: do not add another Neapolitan or B–D–F–A♭ cadence. Use vii°7/V or a German sixth where the motto is absent.

**4. Harmonic rhythm: matches the models in rate, but chord changes fall on strong beats more often. (No change)**
Measured with the same labeller on all four works: chord changes per 4 quarters, converted to changes per second at the marked or MusicXML tempi.

| Section | This score | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| First theme | 1.89 per 4q (0.99/s) | Tchaikovsky 3.47 (1.68/s); Dvořák 2.87 (1.63/s); Beethoven 2.45 (0.90/s) |
| Second theme | about 1.6 (0.75/s) | Tchaikovsky's Andante 2.92 (0.73/s) |
| Development | 2.00 (1.05/s) | Beethoven 2.81 (1.03/s) |

- The overall pace is Beethoven's, not Tchaikovsky's or Dvořák's. It is not slow.
- What is uniform is placement. 74% of changes land on beats 1 or 3, against 54% in Tchaikovsky. 37% of exposition bars have a single chord change, on the downbeat, against 4% in Tchaikovsky.
- The runs of one chord per bar are 47–55, 97–105, 139–147 and 171–178. All four are protected. 97–122 (one chord per bar, then the pedal) has the same shape as Tchaikovsky's 267–304 (0.47 chords per bar, mostly pedal).
- The one place where a faster or stronger approach was missing was the dominant before 139 (finding 1).

**5. Key plan and arrivals: sound apart from 139.**
- 47: six bars of V7 with an MC; longer than Dvořák 9/i's approach to S2 at 149 (twelve 2/4 bars of D pedal).
- 77: enters through mixture (A♭ minor motto 73–76). Like the models' development openings, it needs no dominant.
- 123: fourteen bars of G pedal (109–122), with the dominant reduced to the motto line at 119–122. Beethoven 9/i arrives at its recapitulation with no dominant at all.
- 163: follows the ESC (G7 at 161, C at 162), as Tchaikovsky's coda follows his PAC at 333.

**6. Bars 56–68 and 148–162 after cycles 47–51: no new vertical faults. (Minor items only)**
- Every harp chord in 56–68 and 148–162 matches the harmony, including the F♭ and G♭ spellings and pedals.
- Viola 158–161 and the re-pitched counter notes (57, 65, 66, 159, 160) clash only as passing eighths: 158.4.5 viola B3 under brass C4; 150.2 counter B♭4 against the harp's decaying A5; 160.3.5 viola E4 against Hn 1 F4.

- One older item remains: at 56.4 Cl 1 and Hn 2 sound F4 while Vla and Vc still hold the augmented fifth E♮4, so the resolution arrives before the chromatic note leaves. An optional fix in tTwo.ily, `tTwoVla` bar 56: `r4 <c' es'>4 <c' e'>4 <c' f'>4`. Not applied: cycle 42 judged bar 56 acceptable.
