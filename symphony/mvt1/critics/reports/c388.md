## Method

I reused the per-player logging engraver in v388/check-log.ly (log pointed at /tmp/critic-388/notes.log) on all 30 single-player variables, with the score's tags (part-only marks removed). Scripts in /tmp/critic-388/scripts: dbl.py (articulation, dynamics and hairpins in every unison/octave doubling of four or more notes), hp.py (how each hairpin ends), hpdir.py (hairpins whose target level is not above/below the starting one) and pair.py (simultaneous dynamics on the eight shared staves). I viewed all 17 music pages at 90 dpi and the changed passages at 200 dpi, and built and viewed the Bsn 1 and Hn 4 parts.

Cycle 288 used the same reading and found the expression marks in good order. This pass checks mainly what changed in the 100 cycles since: c298, c318, c328, c338, c348, c358, c368, c378 and the texture/orchestration changes that put new instruments on existing lines (c317, c344, c347). The three faults below all come from such later changes: each added marks or an instrument to a line without carrying the marks to every part of it.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 103–105: Bsn 1 doubles the cellos' S head in unison without the swells the cellos and Hn 1 have.** Applied.
Cycle 348 gave the second-theme head at 103–106 the per-bar swells of 47–50 in Hn 1, Vc and Vla. Bsn 1 plays the same line at the same pitch as the cellos (F3–E3 | D3–B♭3–A3 | G3–F3–E3–G3–B3 | E4–D♯4, same slurs, same mf), a doubling set up in c117/c168, but it was left out. In the Bsn 1 part the phrase now reads flat mf while the cellos beside it and the horn an octave above swell on every long note. MOLA PDF 7 is the reason this matters: the bassoonist reads only his own part and has no way to know that his line is shaped. MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11) shows the rule applied by an editor: Beethoven's winds get bracketed [sf] so that every part on the line carries the strings' stress. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130 adds the balance reason: short crescendi and diminuendi are made "by natural dynamic means", which only works if every player on the line makes them.
The marks cannot go on the shared Bsn staff in the score, because Bsn 2 holds the plain bass there (D–G–A–B♭–C–B) and the score rule asks for equal simultaneous dynamics on a shared staff. They go in the part only, by the same means as Tpt 2's ff at 83 and Hn 4's mp at 67.
Fix in dev.ily, devBsnOne, bars 103–105:
`f2.~(\mf -\tag #'part \< f8 e8 -\tag #'part \> | d4 -\tag #'part \! bes2-- -\tag #'part \< a4 -\tag #'part \!) | g4.( f8 e4 g8 -\tag #'part \< b8 | e'2\> dis'2) |`
The score is unchanged at 103–106; the Bsn 1 part shows the swells exactly as the cello part does (checked in parts-09-bassoon-1.pdf).

**2. Bar 142: the cellos and Bsn 1 play the hammered C4–B3 without accents; the four brass instruments on the same notes have them.** Applied.
At 142, C4–B3 in minims sounds in six parts: Hn 3, Tpt 3, Tbn 1, Tbn 2 (all accented) and Vc, Bsn 1 (not accented); the upper octaves (Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I·II) are all accented. Cycle 288 left Bsn 1 unaccented because the note ends its slurred tenor line, and at that time no other part had that line. Cycle 317 then put Tbn 2 on the same tenor line (C4–B♭3–A♭3–F3–G3–A♭3–C4–B3) with accents on 142. Since then the same line is accented in the trombone and not in the cellos and bassoon, so c288's reason no longer holds. In the exposition's counterpart at 32 every part on the C–B is accented. This is the case MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11) covers: one stress for one line, written in every part. The accents go inside the existing slur (pressure accents in one bow for the cellos), so no bowing or slur changes; the violins' retaken down-bows are not copied.
Fix in recOne.ily, bar 142: Bsn 1 `c'2-> b2->)`, Vc `c'2-> b2->)`. Checked on score page 14: the accents print above the Bsn staff and the cello staff with no collision.

**3. Bar 130: Hn 4 has "f <" on a tied note and arrives at f at 131.** Applied.
Hn 4 reads `g1\mf~ | g1\f\< | ` and the recapitulation then gives it f at 131 (Hn 3·4 graded f under the ff tutti, c264). Two faults in one bar, both visible in the Hn 4 part. A crescendo that arrives at its own starting level contradicts itself: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10) shows each hairpin ending on a different written level ("> p", "pp < > mp"). And the f stands on a tied note, so the player cannot make it except as a jump inside a held sound. The intended effect is plain from the context (timpani and low strings step mf → f at 130 and crescendo to ff, Hn 2 enters mf < at 130.3 to reach f at 131): a crescendo from the tied mf of 129 to the horn's f at 131. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: the brass are the group that best makes such crescendo shades.
Fix in dev.ily, devHnFour, bar 130: `g1\< |` (was `g1\f\< |`). The protected timpani/Hn 4 swell into the reprise keeps its shape; only the contradictory level goes. Score page 12 shows mf at 129 and the hairpin over 130 to the f at 131.

**4. Bars 32–33: Vln I·II "ff < ff".** Keep.
hpdir.py flags the violins' semiquaver run at 32.4 crescendoing inside ff to the ff stroke at 33. Against principle 11 this is a hairpin to its own level. It is not the Hn 4 case: the ff at 33 is a new attack (the stroke that opens B, with fp in the basses) after a run, and the restated level marks the arrival as Beethoven's restated sf do in Ex. 6. Bars 27–32 are quoted by the finale. Keep.

**5. Bar 144: Vln I's A♭4 on beat 3 has no accent, unlike 16, 28 and 132.** Keep.
A horizontal check of the P head (MOLA PDF 8) across 15, 27, 131, 143 shows one difference: at 144 the accent on the A♭ is missing. At 144 the violin is in a diminuendo f → pp and hands the head to Bsn 1's answer (mf >, accented E♭4). An accent inside the fade would pull against both. The difference follows from the dynamic, so it stays.

**6. Bar 65: Fl 1 fades while the violins it doubles swell into 66.** Keep.
Fl 1 doubles Vln I at 64–65 at mf (one level under the violins, c167) and leaves the line on beat 3 of 65, where c378's swell starts. Its diminuendo is the taper of an instrument that stops, not a contrary marking on a shared note: from 65.3 the flute holds D♭6 and the violins move. Keep.

**7. Bars 149 and 151: the cor anglais fills enter with a swell and no level.** Keep.
The entry engraver flags them (principle 12). The rests before them are about one and a half beats, and the C.a. part has "p dolce" at 147. Writing p at each two-beat fill would clutter the part for no gain. Keep.
