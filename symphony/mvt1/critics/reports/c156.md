## Findings

**F1 (applied). Bars 158–162: about 11 s of ff–fff brass with no breath anywhere, and no staggering.** Tpt 1–3, Tbn 1–2, B.tbn, tuba and Hn 4 sustain from the K downbeat through the fff at 160 into the diminuendo at 162. There are no rests or breath marks; the tempo is 120 for two bars, then largamente 100. In the low brass this is the loudest and most air-hungry writing in the movement. Adler PDF 292 says to allow frequent breaths and not to phrase too many notes on one breath when loud and slow. Adler adler2 PDF 8 says the tuba "takes a great deal of breath" and needs frequent rests. Every player will breathe somewhere. If the score does not say where, they may all breathe together on the bar line before the fff. Fix: staggered breath commas, with no change to notes or dynamics.
- Sustained Gs breathe after 158: Hn 4, Tpt 2, Tbn 2 and tuba (`g,,1\ff \breathe | g,,1\<`). Each repeats the same pitch at 159.
- The tune and the moving parts breathe after 159: Hn 1·2, Tpt 1 (`e''4\< f''4 g''2 \breathe`), Tpt 3, Tbn 1 and B.tbn (`g,1\< \breathe | aes,1\fff`).
- The tuba breathes again after 160 (`aes,,1\fff \breathe`).
- On shared staves, where the lower player breathes, the comma is printed below (`\once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN`).

This does not touch the protected brass attack at 158, the 160 scoring or the clarinet counterline.

**F2 (applied). Bars 177–181, Tbn 1: one breath for about 19 s, pp to ppp.** The slur `d'1( | c'2 des'2 | c'1)` (12.7 s at the rit. tempi) is followed by a tie through 180 into 181 beat 1. That makes about 19 s of sound with nowhere to breathe, ending in a diminuendo to ppp. That is where an empty breath would show. Adler PDF 292 and 309 say all slurred notes go on one breath; adler2 PDF 3 adds that trombone legato is soft-tongued. Fix in coda.ily: `c'2 des'2 | c'1) \breathe | c'1~\> | c'4\ppp r4 r2`. The slur and all pitches stay; only the tie 179→180 becomes a breath and a soft restrike. Cycle 140's reason (the C overlaps Hn 3's entry at 181) still holds, because the restruck C continues into 181. The part combiner now prints "1." at 180, which is correct.

**F3 (applied). Bars 72–73, Hn 4: from f straight into a pp pedal with no gap.** Hn 4 plays the codetta f (`es4-> es4-> es2->`) and enters the A♭ pedal pp on the next downbeat, tied for four bars (15 s with no gap). A soft attack straight after loud playing with no breath is the risk Adler describes at PDF 284 and 292. Fix in clos.ily: `r4 aes2.\pp~ | aes1~ | aes1~ | aes1`. The cellos and basses already take the A♭ on the downbeat of 73. The codetta 69–72 is unchanged.

**F4 (applied). Bars 127–131, Hn 4: G3 tied 127–130 with a crescendo into the ff at 131, and no gap.** From 111 to 134, Hn 4 has only quarter rests (48 s of near-continuous playing; the loads at 117–125 were relieved in cycle 66). The last stretch has no breath before the ff of the reprise. Adler PDF 292 and 303 say to give periodic rests. Fix in dev.ily: `g1\pp\<~ | g1 \breathe | g1\mf~ | g1\f\<`. The restrike falls on the mf that is already printed at 129. The pedal join 130/131 is untouched.

**F5 (applied). Bars 61–68, Hn 3: a soft pad of about 17 s with no gap.** Hn 3 has the only part in the 3·4 pad without a rest after 60 (Hn 4 rests at 66). Fix in tTwo.ily: `c'1\mp \breathe | f'1`, a breath before the new chord at 65 (Adler PDF 292).

**F6 (not applied, for the composer's judgement). Bar 106, Hn 1: written B5 (sounding E5), mf with a diminuendo, reached by a leap.** This is inside Adler's range (top written C6, PDF 303), but it is in the register he labels "brilliant and loud". He calls soft, controlled playing at the top "fiendishly difficult" (PDF 284). Cl 1 doubles the line (cycle 84), so a cracked note would not destroy the phrase. The notes of 103–110 are protected, so I propose only an option: write "(Cl 1 may cover)" in the parts, or let Hn 3 double 106 an octave lower. No change is needed for a professional first horn.

**F7 (parts stage, cycle 185).** Adler PDF 304: in the horn parts, bass-clef passages (23–26, 103–106, 162–166) must use modern notation (a fifth down, not a fourth up). Some players expect the old notation, so add a line on the part's first page. Adler PDF 297–298: horn and trumpet parts without key signatures. This agrees with the item cycle 155 already deferred.

**F8 (applied; relay from movement II). Harp retuning cue at 176.** Movement II needs the harp's lowest D string at D♭ (D♭1 at its bar 10). The harp rests 167–181. Its last notes before that (up to 166) do not use the D1 string, and neither does its final chord at 182 (C2–G2–C3). I added, under the left-hand staff at 176: "Retune lowest D string (D1) / to D♭ for movement II" (`R1 | R1_\markup \small \italic \column {...} | R1*5`). It does not overlap "étouffez with Cb." at 182; I checked the rendered page.
