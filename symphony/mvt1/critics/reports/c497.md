## Findings

No score change. I checked every principle in the table bar by bar and found no place where the texture breaks one without a reason that still holds. I also looked for places where the texture is weaker than a master's, beyond what the rules cover, and tested the three strongest candidates against the reading. None of them would be a real improvement. The findings record why, ranked by how much each would matter if I were wrong.

### 1. Fugato 88–96: continuing voices stay at the level of the new entry (no change)

**What I checked.** In Britten's fugue (Ex. VIII-43, PDF 272–273) the image shows each continuing voice dropping to pp as the next entry comes in: the piccolo goes to pp at the flutes' entry, and the flutes go to pp at the oboes' mp entry. Here c197 applied the same idea at 88 (Vc countersubject mp under the violas' mf entry). At 91 and 94 the continuing voices stay at the entry's level. At 91 Vln II + C.a. enter f, and the Vla/Bsn 1 countersubject (F4–C5), the Vc free voice (C4–E♭4) and the Cb are also marked f. At 94 Vln I + Fl 1 enter f, and Vln II continues at f.

**Verdict.** Keep. Britten's entries go downward, so each new subject sits under the voices already playing, and those voices must give way. Here the entries go upward (Vc 85, Vla 88, Vln II 91, Vln I 94), so each new entry is the top voice and has the most players (a string section plus one wind, PDF 272: "two like instruments in unison"). The subject at 91 (C5–A♭5) lies above the countersubject, and at 94 (F5–D♭6) above everything. The step to mp at 88 was needed because the cellos' countersubject there lies above the violas' entry (c197). That condition does not recur at 91 or 94. Marking the continuing voices mf at 91 would also flatten the protected crescendo order 85–97 (c143, c163). The rhythmic-layer count rises steadily, 1 (85–87), 2 (88–90), 4 (91–93), 5 (94–95), the thickening that Adler's Mozart and Britten examples show.

### 2. Bar 176: the only bar in the chorale with no motion inside it (no change)

**What I checked.** In Copland's summation (Ex. VIII-41, PDF 270) the image shows Bsn 1 with a moving quaver line under the held hymn, so the quiet chordal passage is never fully static. The script finds that in 176 all eight sounding parts attack on beat 1 and hold for the whole bar (trombone chorale, B.cl/Vc/Cb bass, Cl 1 C5, Vln II and violas). I considered a moving inner line for 176, for example Bsn 1 pp under the chorale.

**Verdict.** Keep. The motion Copland gets from one line is spread across the passage here. Cl 1 moves E♭4 (175.4) → C5 (176) → B4 (177). The chorale itself moves at 175, 177 and 178. Vln I's last motto enters at 177.4, so 176 is the one held breath before the canon. At crotchet = 72 the bar lasts 3.3 s, pp, with the tam-tam ringing from 175. A moving bassoon line would compete with the trombone chorale in the same register (G2–E♭4) and blur the motto bass that B.cl, Vc and Cb double. The chorale's notes, Cl 1 at 175–177 and the at-pitch bass tripling are protected (c140, c187). Adler's own point on PDF 255–256 applies: in a chordal passage where the progression is the point, the choice is made by shade and dynamic, not by adding a line.

### 3. Bars 147–155: what fills the holds of the recapitulated second theme (no change)

**What I checked.** Berlioz (Ex. VIII-37, PDF 266) fills each rest and held note of the tune with a short string figure, and on the repeat adds an answering solo clarinet. In the exposition (47–55) the harp's four-quaver figure fills every bar. In the recapitulation the harp rests until 156, and the Vln II tremolo pad is static, so the holds are filled only by the cor anglais. The onset list shows the C.a. answering at 147, 149, 151 and 154. In 150 and 155 nothing moves after beat 3.

**Verdict.** Keep. The exposition and recapitulation settings differ on purpose: the third setting of the tune changes its accompaniment (PDF 255). The C.a. answer is the Berlioz solo-wind answer, and it keeps the motto's colour (row 15). Bar 150 was raised by c161 and closed by c487 (keep). Bar 155 is the phrase end before the poco animando at 156, where the violins and harp come back. A harp or pizzicato fill at 150 and 155 would bring back the exposition's figure and take away the difference the recapitulation has. The pad is protected (c387, c427).
