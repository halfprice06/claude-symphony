Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. IV "Composition of the orchestra" (rimsky-principles PDF 115–136, book pp. 97–118), second pass. I read all 22 text pages in full. I rendered and viewed the pages with music or layout the OCR loses: PDF 119 (the partial-tutti bracket), 127 (chords passed between groups; amplification and elimination of tone qualities, mostly lost in the OCR), 128 (imitation and echo) and 132 (the "melodic design / fundamental notes" example). The chapter's numbered examples (Nos. 175–276) are in the examples volume, outside this range.

The first pass (c253) covered the transition 33–46 and the closing section 69–76. This pass covers the second half of the form: the development from the wedge on (111–130), the recapitulation (131–166) and the coda (167–182). The tests are the chapter's rules on prolonged crescendo and diminuendo, brass re-entries, economy, and the place of each passage in the form. I also re-measured the proportions against two corpus first movements.

## Findings

All five findings recommend keeping the score as it is. I found no place in 111–182 where the score breaks a rule of this chapter without a reason that the chapter itself supports. They are ranked by how close each one comes to a real weakness.

### 1. Proportions and tension curve: keep

Measured from v343's full.midi with its tempo map: introduction 1–14 = 67.2 s (16%), exposition 15–76 = 123.8 s (29%), development 77–130 = 105.4 s (24%), recapitulation 131–166 = 75.5 s (18%), coda 167–182 = 58.8 s (14%). Total 430.8 s, the same as at v323 and v333.

Corpus, from the MCTL form annotations in reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations, in beats:
- Beethoven 9/i: exposition 0–374, development 374–628, recapitulation 628–1024, coda 1024–1092.5. Development/exposition = 0.68; recapitulation/exposition = 1.06.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: exposition 72–638, development 638–1214, recapitulation (second group only) 1214–1334, coda 1334–1414. Development/exposition = 1.02; recapitulation/exposition = 0.21.

Movement I has development/exposition = 0.85 and recapitulation/exposition = 0.61. Both lie between the two models. Like Tchaikovsky's, its recapitulation is short because it drops the transition and compresses the second group. Like Beethoven's, it restates the first group in full tutti.

The tension curve has three peaks of rising height: 82 ff (1 bar), 113–122 (a 10-bar build to fff), and 156–160 (a 2-bar build to fff after 11 soft bars). Each one is followed by a measured relaxation: 84–85, 123–130 and 162–166. PDF 116 asks whether each passage is a climax or a step. The score answers that question every time: 138 (f) is a step to 140 (ff), 158 (ff) is a step to 160 (fff), and 167–170 (f to ff) is a closing gesture, not a fourth climax. Section lengths are protected (c243, c329, c339), and this chapter gives no reason to change them.

### 2. Retransition 127–131: brass enter at f on the reprise: keep

What the reading asks. For a prolonged crescendo, strings come first, then woodwind, then brass (PDF 130). The second group should enter p or pp and crescendo faster (PDF 127). Brass returning after a rest should enter pp or ff (PDF 136).

What the score does. The 127–130 crescendo, pp to f in 7.6 s, is carried by the cellos and basses, Cl 1, the violas (129), Vln II and Bsn 1 (130), Hn 3·4 (pp → mf → f) and the timpani (pp → mf → f). Hn 2 adds the dominant seventh mf< on beat 3 of 130. The trumpets, trombones and tuba, silent since 123–126, enter on the downbeat of 131 at f, under strings and woodwind at ff.

Why it stays. At 131 the trombones carry the P head in the tenor with the cellos and bassoons, and the trumpets play the timpani rhythm. Both act as a harmonic or doubling layer under the ff strings. PDF 133 asks that such a layer in brass "be of inferior dynamic power", and that request outweighs PDF 136 here. The rest is short, about 7 bars (16 s), not the "long rest" PDF 136 describes. The brass already took part in the crescendo at 113–122 in exactly the PDF 127 manner (pp cresc. at 113–114). If they also swelled through 130, both arrivals would be built the same way, and the motto stretto, the one line moving in 129–130 (protected, c233, c263), would be covered. c328 kept the f entry at 131 for the same balance reason. I add only that PDF 133 supports it.

### 3. Approach to the last climax 156–158 and the dissolution 162–166: keep

At 156–157 (poco animando) the crescendo uses strings, Cl 1, bassoons, the four horns (p) and the harp. At 158 fourteen players return together at ff: flutes, oboes, trumpets, trombones, tuba and timpani. The trumpets have been silent since 143 and the trombones and tuba since 143 (15 bars, about 31 s). This is the PDF 136 case exactly: a group silent for some time gains fresh interest, and it returns ff. The climax arrival is protected (c283). This chapter confirms it rather than questioning it.

The dissolution follows PDF 130's elimination order. The flutes leave after 161. All the brass except the horns diminuendo ff>mp at 162 and leave at 163. The woodwind thin out over 163–166 (oboes to 163, Cl 1 and bassoons to 166). The strings remain. The horns stay into 163–166 as the converging progression's stationary middle (PDF 131: "the group in the middle region which remains stationary is the group to be retained"). Hn 1·2 hold C4, and Hn 3·4 take the B3→C4 resolution at 165–166 (c223).

### 4. Forte coda 167–170: the full brass return after four bars: keep

What the reading asks. Economy (PDF 136): the ear cannot stand the full orchestra for long, and a group gains interest by silence.

What the score does. The trumpets, trombones and tuba stop at 162 and return at 167, so 4 bars (about 9 s with the rit.) separate two full-brass passages. Tpt 3, the trombones and the tuba re-enter at f.

Why it stays.
- 167 is a new section (L, Tempo dell'Allegro, a new key signature) after a p rit., so the return is heard as a contrast of tempo and mode (PDF 116, "contrast with what goes before"), not as a continuation of 158–162.
- The low brass at 167–169 hold the tonic pedal under the horns' and trumpets' ff head, which is PDF 133's softer harmonic basis again.
- The passage is the rotation of the codetta 69–72 (coda.ily header), so its brass weight answers 69–72.
- The coda is protected (167–169: c253, c263; graded brass at 167). The four-bar gap is the only point in 111–182 where the chapter's economy rule is close to strained, but changing it would break the codetta rotation and the protected brass grading for a gain the reading does not require.

### 5. Coda 171–182: keep

- The solo cello at 171–174 has the light accompaniment PDF 123 asks for: C.a. p, violas pp, Bsn 2 pp, basses silent until 175.
- The trombones re-enter pp dolce at 175 with the tam-tam pp. That is PDF 136's pp re-entry and PDF 135's "gong with chords on trombones".
- The diminuendo removes the brass (Tbn 2, B.tbn, B.cl at 179; Tbn 1 on beat 1 of 181) and leaves strings. Cl 1's B4 fades at 177, the clarinet morendo of PDF 130.
- Hn 3·4 enter ppp at 181 as a pp re-entry (PDF 136), not as an added crescendo group.
- The last chord fills every octave from C2 to C5 (PDF 125).
- The movement begins pp and ends ppp, never mf or mp (PDF 136).

## Other checks (no change)

- 111–116, the wedge: this is the reading's model crescendo. Strings and woodwind begin mp cresc., the horns p cresc., the trumpets and trombones pp cresc. at 113–114, the timpani mf< at 115 and the flutes at 115. The bass falls while Vln I rises, with the violas' held C in the middle (PDF 127, 130, 131). Protected (c319, c333).
- 122–126, the collapse: it follows the converging-progression rule. Trumpets and Hn 1·2 leave after 123, Tbn 2 at 124 (c324), Tbn 1 and B.tbn after 124. The tuba, Hn 3·4 and the pedal strings keep the G. The tam-tam fades with the strings (c229).
- 131–146: the recapitulation varies the exposition's scoring as PDF 118 recommends. The P head moves to the tenor (operation a), 138 is graded f (operation e, c108), and 143–146 removes brass, then woodwind, leaving the strings and Bsn 1 (PDF 130).
- c253's first-pass fix holds in MIDI: the trombones enter p (velocity 69) at 40.
- The MIDI scan of entries after rests of 20 beats or more finds no horn, trumpet, trombone or tuba entry at mp or mf after a long rest. The only mf case is Hn 2 at 130 after 26 beats, inside a written crescendo and protected with the 129–130 seventh (c120, c233).

## Leave alone

The crescendo and diminuendo plans of the second half (111–117, 122–131, 143–146, 156–166, 175–182) are at a master's level and should not be touched. They add and remove groups in Rimsky-Korsakov's order and hold the trumpets back for fresh ff returns.
