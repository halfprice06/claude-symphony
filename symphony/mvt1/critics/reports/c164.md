## What I studied

- **Corpus:** with music21 I measured the interval between the two top flute notes at every held-note onset (half note or longer). - **Web:** Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Project Gutenberg text (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm), section "Wood-wind harmony": "Close writing is the more frequent form in all harmony devoted to the wood-wind, forte or piano", and "Modern orchestrators do not allow any void in the intermediate parts … These empty spaces create a bad effect especially in forte passages."

## Findings

### 1. Bars 167–170: Fl 2 makes a three-way unison and leaves an empty sixth under Fl 1 (applied)

**Problem.** In the coda's forte opening the woodwinds hold the background chord under the brass head. Fl 1 sits on E♭6/F6. Fl 2 is a sixth lower, on G5 (167) and A♭5 (168.3–170), and so are Ob 1 and Cl 1 (from beat 2 of 167). That puts three winds on one pitch. Between E♭6/F6 and G5/A♭5 no wind and no string sounds: Vln I div. also has G5/E♭6. The chord tone C6 (D6 at 170) is missing at the top of the texture.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 257: "it is usual to voice the upper woodwinds or the upper notes of the chord in close position"; unison overlapping "usually only results in a stronger pitch".
- Ex. VIII-26/27 (PDF 258) shows the Beethoven and 19th-century spacings with the flute pair close.
- Rimsky-Korsakov (web, above): no void in intermediate parts, "especially in forte passages".
- Corpus: Dvořák 9/i keeps held flute pairs within a sixth in 57 of 58 cases, and Tchaikovsky 6/i has no flute sixths at all. Here the pair is 8–9 semitones apart for four bars.

**Fix (coda.ily, `codaFluteTwo`).** Only Fl 2's pitches change:
```
codaFluteTwo = {
  c'''1\f | c'''1 | c'''1\< | d'''2.->\ff r4\fermata | ...
```
Fl 1 and Fl 2 now move E♭6/C6, E♭6/C6, F6/C6, F6/D6, a third or fourth apart. G5/A♭5 stays in Ob 1 and Cl 1. The vii°7 at 170 keeps B in the brass and gains D6 at the top.

**Protection.** "Tutti layout 27–32 and 167–170 (alike on purpose)" (cycles 90, 139) refers to the layout: brass head, sustained winds, string tremolo chords and the C pedal. That layout is unchanged. At 27–32 Fl 1 carries the moving tune, so the two passages never had the same flute spacing. The brass grading (cycle 144) is untouched.

### 2. Bars 41–43: Cl 2 holds B♭3 under the horn call, which ends on B♭3 (applied; closes c124 F5)

**Problem.** The four horns' ff call (E♭3–C4–B♭3) lands on B♭3, and Cl 2 has been holding that exact pitch sf>mf since beat 1 of 41, inside the horn chord. The call's arrival note therefore sounds in a unison already in progress, not on its own.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12: Strauss stops the held wind sound so the entering line "is clearly heard".
- PDF 257: a unison doubling robs both timbres.
- c124 F5 found the same fault from Rimsky-Korsakov (clarinets should not sit inside the horns) and left it open. Ch. 8 is the new source that the cycle-128 rule asks for.
- The fix touches no brass note or dynamic, so the rule on brass under the call does not apply.

**Fix (trans.ily, `transClTwo`).**
```
bes'1\sf\> | \after 2 \> bes'1\mf | g'4\p r4 r2 | R1*3 |
```
B♭4 fills the space between Ob 2's G4 and Cl 1's D♭5, which closes the upper winds as in principle 18. Cl 2 joins Cl 1's B♭4 at 42.3 and takes G4 in the p chord at 43 (Hn 4 keeps G3). The chord's fifth is still in the woodwinds.

## Checked, no change (notes)

- **A. 82–83:** the middle octave is the heaviest, with Fl 2 at G4. Adler PDF 256 calls the flute's low register weak, but c144 made the middle-heavy layout deliberate (Adler ch. 15, Mozart). I leave it alone.
- **B. 103–106:** Ob 2 + Cl a2 in unison on the first-theme head at f. Adler PDF 235 warns against oboe/clarinet unisons, but praises Schubert's use of one. An octave alternative does not work: Cl 2 an octave down collides with Hn 1's F4–E5 theme, and Fl or Ob an octave up reaches B6, above the movement's A♭6 ceiling. The c084 balance reason stands. Keep.
- **C. 145–146:** Ob 1's dolce G4 starts on the G4 held by Cl 1 and Vln I. This is a common-tone hand-off, not a covered entry, and the leap to E5 is exposed. Keep (protected, c21/c114/c147).
- **D. 163–166:** Bsn a2 in unison on the soft bass. Adler's PDF 249–250 objection is to solos, and this is an accompanying bass. Optional only: Bsn 2 could rest 163–165 to ease its 156–174 stretch. Not recommended in this cycle.
- **E.** The woodwind ranges and solo registers are in line with the chapter. The register expansion is saved for 158–161 (PDF 238), and each statement of the second-theme head has its own colour (PDF 238).
