## Findings

### Finding 1. Bars 9.4–10: mark the violins' first rising sixth "sul D" (applied)

**What is wrong.** The first time the violins play a theme is the rising sixth G4–E♭5 at 9.4–10, Lento, mp, slurred. Those are exactly the pitches of the last motto in Vln I at 177–178, and that one is marked sul D (c251). The movement marks its other exposed string statements of the rising sixth too: the cello motto at 1–3 is sul G (c251), and the S pickup at 55.4 is sul D (c321). Bar 9 is the only one left unmarked. c321 did not notice this; it called 55.4 "the only exposed string statement of a rising-sixth theme head with no string marked".

Without a mark, most players will take G4 on the D string and E♭5 on the A string. The slurred sixth then becomes a string crossing at Lento, and the opening of the movement does not have the covered D-string colour that its ending has. The rising sixth is the motto's head, and its colour is part of what the listener is meant to recognise. The violins' sixth at 9 should therefore sound the way it sounds at 177.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 57, Ex. 12 (*Sheherazade* III, rendered at PDF 186): a unison violin cantabile in the middle register, marked sul D over its opening phrase and sul G later. The string is named in the score because it decides the colour.
- PDF 56: the cello melody "usually laid out for the top string (A)" for its quality, and the same melody "on the D string" in the *Antar* example. The string is a choice the composer makes and writes down.
- PDF 54: a melody stands out by the choice and contrast of timbre. The string is part of the timbre.
- The fix is the one c321 applied at 55.4, with the same reading and the same reasoning. G4–E♭5 on the D string is a single shift, the same one the violins already make, pp, at 177.

**Fix.** The fix is a string mark only. Notes, slurs, bowing and dynamics do not change. From A♭4 at 10.4 the player chooses the string, as before. The second sixth, A♭4–F5, would lie around the ninth position on the D string, so I do not mark it.

## Melody and memorability: what I checked and keep

I tested each theme against the question in my lens: would a listener hum it afterwards?

- **Motto (1–4, 177–182).** Yes. It has six notes with one leap and a chromatic fall, and it returns five times in five different colours. It sits in the same family as Tchaikovsky 5's motto, short and harmonically loaded. Keep.
- **P (15–22).** It is a sentence: a two-bar idea, its sequence (G–E♭ sixth, then G–F seventh), and a continuation that climbs to the Neapolitan D♭6 at 20 and falls to the cadence at 22. It is the least singable of the themes because its rhythm is busy. But its head is the motto, so the listener already knows it, and the finale quotes it at 199–204. Vln I plays it alone at f over mf syncopations. That follows PDF 54–55 (relief by position and dynamics), and c287 protects it for the graded build to 27. Keep.
- **Horn call (13–14).** Yes. It is three notes, E♭3–C4–B3, with the B left hanging. Keep.
- **S (46.4–68).** Yes. It is a period: antecedent 47–50 to the leading tone, consequent 51–55 to the mediant. It has long notes on the strong beats and one leap of a sixth per phrase, which is the Dvořák and Brahms 2 kind of tune. Its four settings follow the reading: Hn 1 + cellos on the A string at 47 (PDF 56); violins an octave higher at 56 (Ex. 7); B minor with Vln I over Vln II + cellos at 97 (PDF 60–61, Ex. 29–30); and C major in violas and cellos in octaves at 147 (PDF 60). The climax at 158–161 is in double octaves (PDF 62, Ex. 32), with two rising sixths, G5–E6 and C6–A♭6, that complete the motto's leap in major. That is the movement's most memorable moment. Keep.
- **Development of the themes.** The P head in dialogue at 77–83, the fugato at 85–96, S in B minor at 97–102, the heads combined at 103–110, the S-head rhythm in the wedge at 111–116, and the motto in augmentation at 117–130 all keep the themes' contours recognisable. Keep.

I did not consider any theme change. Every melodic place where the reading could apply already follows it (rows 1–8 and 10–30). The heads are quoted by movements II and IV, and a change would cost more than the reading justifies.
