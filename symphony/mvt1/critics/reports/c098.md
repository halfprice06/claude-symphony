# Critic 098 — lens 8 (expression): the dynamic arc of the whole movement (v117)

## What I studied
- v117: all section files, global.ily, score.ly, the PDF (pages 4, 6, 8–10, 12, 13 inspected), score.midi.
- Method. I extracted every dynamic and hairpin with its time and staff, using a small Voice engraver added to a scratch copy of score.ly (1,375 events, 878 absolute dynamics). I computed two curves per bar. The first takes MIDI note velocities. The second takes MIDI note timing with each staff's written dynamic carried forward, 5 dB per dynamic step, power-summed over sounding notes and sampled every eighth. The written-dynamic method is the one I could also apply to the corpus. For the corpus I used music21 on `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml` (MCTL/MuseScore editions; hairpins ignored, exposition repeat not unfolded). I also cross-checked fff in the Mutopia Dvořák 9/i (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/*.notesA.ily`).
- Web sources: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Gutenberg text (https://gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): "the marks of expression in the horns should be one degree stronger than in the rest of the brass"; "horns, in forte passages, are about one half as strong". On Gould's *Behind Bars* hairpin practice I could reach only second-hand reports (https://forums.steinberg.net/t/broken-hairpins-for-interim-dynamics-a-la-gould-s-recommendation/1034557, https://notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=10113), so I cite Gould only for the general rule that a hairpin leads to a stated dynamic.
- Caveat on score.midi. Velocities are not a clean measure of the written dynamics. LilyPond's default instrument equalizer caps the winds (an ff flute plays at velocity 62–67 at 27, while ff violins play at 91–111), and accents add velocity. As a result, the MIDI curve makes the hemiolas 23–25 and 135–137 (written mf with a crescendo) as loud as the ff tutti 27–31, and puts bar 72 (f, accented) within 2 dB of the fff climaxes. My conclusions therefore use the written-dynamic curve. Do not judge the hierarchy from the MP3 (as cycle 30 also said).

The proportions of levels are close to Tchaikovsky 6/i. The difference is in the peaks: the models stay near their maximum for long stretches (Tchaikovsky 6/i 248–300, ff/fff almost throughout, ffff 299–307), while this score's peaks are short (fff 122–125 and 160–161). That suits a 182-bar movement and the cycle-83 rule, so I propose no increase.

**Static stretches:** the longest stretches at one level are the soft second-group passages, 47–63 (17 bars at p, with protected swells inside) and 147–155 (protected by cycle 83). No loud plateau lasts longer than 6 bars, against 35–53 bars in the models. No change is needed.

**pp moments:** every soft point drops 15–25 dB from the bar before (43, 73, 84, 107, 126, 145, 171). The final ppp (180–182) is about as soft as 73–76 and 84 on paper. Tchaikovsky's softest moment (pppppp, bar 160) is also at the end of his exposition, not at the end of the movement, so this is acceptable.

**Crescendos:** all structural crescendos reach a higher dynamic, except 95–97 (Finding 2). The crescendos into sub. p at 62 are intentional (cycles 59, 68).

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 41–42: full brass at ff covers the horns' low call. Applied.**
Problem: all 24 staves are ff. Trombones, bass trombone, tuba and trumpets hold B♭/G/E♭ chord tones at ff in the same register as the four horns' a2 call (page 4). Bar 41 was the loudest bar of the exposition on paper (48.8), louder than the first-theme tutti 27–31 (47.7) and equal to the climax approach 158–159 (48.7).
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov's rule quoted above. This score's own graded brass at 27–32, 131 and 167 (protected) follows the same rule.
Fix (trans.ily): Tpt 1·2 `bes'1\f`/`g'1\f`, Tpt 3 `des'1\f`, Tuba `es,1\f~`. Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn keep the f they already have at 40 (the `\ff` at 41 is removed). Horns stay ff, and all hairpins and the p at 43 are unchanged. Bar 41 is now 47.8, level with 27–31 and 117–121 and below 158–159. This does not reverse cycle 84, which added horns and set no brass dynamic.

**2. Bars 95–97: crescendos that arrive nowhere. Applied.**
Problem: Cl 1, Vln II and Vla start a two-bar crescendo from f at 95 and land on f at 97 (Vln II on a rest). Only the basses, Fl/Ob/Vln I and the brass reach ff.
Evidence: a hairpin should lead to a stated dynamic (Gould, as reported above). This was the only structural crescendo in the movement without a higher arrival.
Fix (dev.ily): `bes''4.->\ff` (Cl 1) and `bes'4.->\ff` (Vla) on the accented note of 96, and `bes''4.->\ff` in Vln II 96 (with the stray `\!` at 97 removed). The existing f at 97 in Cl/Vla now reads as the accompaniment dropping under the B-minor tune. No notes changed. Cl 2 rests in 96, so the shared clarinet staff has no conflict.

**3. Bar 72: a lone timpani ff under an f cadence. Applied.**
Problem: the timpani roll (71) crescendos to ff at 72, while every other part closes the codetta at f with accents. This was the only ff in the second group and closing section. Neither model marks ff in the second group of its exposition (Tchaikovsky 6/i 89–160 stays p–pppppp apart from the 123–138 surge; Dvořák's closing tutti is all at one level).
Fix (clos.ily): `es1:32\mf\< | aes,4->\f es4-> aes,2->`.

**4. Bars 98–102: six bars of uniform ff on a lyrical theme. Not applied.**
Problem: the B-minor second-theme statement has no dynamic shape. In the exposition, this phrase breathes with swells (47–55, protected), but in 98–102 every staff holds ff.
Proposal: tune only (Fl, Ob, CA, Vln I/II): `\<` across 100 into 101, then `\>` on beat 3 of 101 back to ff. The rest of the orchestra is unchanged, and there is no diminuendo at 102.
Note: cycle 94 rejected a diminuendo at 102. I could not read c094 for the reason because the project mount failed during my run. This proposal stays within the bars before 102 and keeps 102 as written.

**5. MIDI is not a reliable meter of dynamics. Information only; no score change.**
Because of the default instrument equalizer and accent velocities, the audio overstates 23–25, 135–137 and 72, and understates winds and horns in every tutti. If future critics are to use score.midi for loudness, an untested option is `instrumentEqualizer = #(lambda (s) '(0 . 1))` in the `\midi` Score context.
