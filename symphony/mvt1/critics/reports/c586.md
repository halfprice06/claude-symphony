## Findings

### 1. Double basses 164–166: the G1 drop added at 165.3 (c583) leaves two down-bows in a row into the p cadence at 166 (applied)

Bars 164–166, recTwoCb. Before c583 the basses played D2 (164.1) and G2 (164.3), with the G2 tied through 165. Counted from the down-bow D2 at 164 that is down, then one up-bow from 164.3 to the end of 165, so the C2 at 166 comes down-bow. c338 marked that down-bow, with an up-bow on the tied C at 166.3, so the f quavers at 167 and the ff stop chord at 170 come down-bow with the other strings. c583 then split 165 into G2 (tied from 164) and a new G1 on beat 3, with no slur. A separately bowed G1 takes a new bow. Counted from 164 that gives down (164.1), up (164.3–165.2), down (G1, 165.3) and then the marked down-bow at 166.1, so two down-bows come in a row. The player has to lift and retake the bow between 165.4 and 166.1. That falls inside a diminuendo to p, under a rit., at the cadence that closes the second group. The alternative is to ignore one of the printed marks, and then the c338 scheme from 166 to 170 comes out backwards. The bass part prints the problem plainly: the down-bow sign at 166 directly follows a separately bowed G1 (part page 3, last system).

Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26 lists the down-bow and the up-bow, and attack at the nut and at the point, among the means that "belong to the natural realm of the string quartet". He leaves the technique of bowing to other books (footnote 1, PDF 26), so the bowing count itself rests on Adler (adler1 PDF 38): bow direction alternates unless the notes are slurred, and a long note is bowed so that the next attack falls on the bow it needs. c338 and c516 applied that same count at 166 and at 110. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27 also says the double bass mostly doubles the cellos except on pedals, and the G1 does that: it is the cellos' G an octave lower, so c583's note stays.

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoCb, bars 164–165), with a comment line for c586:
`f,,1 | d,2\mp g,2~( | g,2\> g,,2) | c,2\p~\downbow c,2\<\upbow |`
(was `... d,2\mp g,2~ | g,2\> g,,2 | ...`). The slur starts on the tied G2 and ends on the G1, so 164.3 to 165.4 is one up-bow of the same length as before c583. The octave drop is a left-hand shift inside the bow, and 166 is down-bow again as marked. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The score (page 17) and the bass part (page 3) both print the slur cleanly. The cellos already slur their whole bar at 165 (`g,8(\> d f b f2)`), so the two bass lines now bow alike.

Applied in the scratch copy.
