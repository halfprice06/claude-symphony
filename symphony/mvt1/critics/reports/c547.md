# Critic 547: texture and counterpoint (v547)

Lens: texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 17 "The Orchestra as Accompanist" (adler2 PDF 256-290), fourth pass.

Method:
- I read the text of every page from PDF 256 to 290. The OCR drops some lines on pages that also hold examples, so I read those passages from the page images.
- I rendered PDF 256-290 at 110 dpi and looked at every example, XVII-1 to XVII-25. The landscape pages (Bizet XVII-20, Fidelio XVII-22, Bach XVII-23, Orff XVII-25) are too small at that size to read note by note. For them I rely on the prose and on what the layout shows: which staves move, which hold, and where the figuration thickens.
- I looked at all 17 music pages of v547 at 80 dpi.
- In /tmp/critic-547/work I rebuilt the score and full.midi. On full.midi I ran c447's scans and one new scan; the scripts are in /tmp/critic-547/scripts:
  - `coattack.py`: accompanying parts that strike a tune's pitch at the same instant (41 tune spans);
  - `rhythm.py`: accompanying parts that copy the tune's rhythm with other pitches;
  - `proximity.py` (new): for 14 wind and horn solos, every half beat at which another part sounds within a whole tone of the solo or above it. This tests the register rule on PDF 274, which the earlier passes checked only by eye.

Earlier uses of this chapter: c247, c347 and c447 used it for this lens, and c373, c410 and c510 for other lenses. I do not repeat their points. The `coattack.py` and `rhythm.py` results on v547 are the same as on v447 (47 co-attack lines, all of them doublings, cadence strokes or contacts already kept; rhythm hits only at 72 and 104), so nothing that changed between v448 and v547 broke what this chapter asks for.

## Findings

I found nothing in v547 that the reading shows to be wrong and that earlier cycles have not settled. I recommend no change. Below are the places where this pass came closest to a change, ranked by how much each could matter, with the reason for keeping each.

### 1. Fugato 85-96: the support builds entry by entry, as in Fidelio (keep)

The task asks where the texture falls short of a master. The most likely place in this lens is the fugato, the movement's only real imitative passage. Adler's Fidelio canon (PDF 283-286) adds orchestral figuration only as the voices pile up. Running string semiquavers first appear on PDF 286, the last page of the canon, when all four voices are in. The Bach chorus (PDF 286-287) puts clear harmony under the counterpoint.

The fugato does both:
- 85-87: the subject alone in the cellos, mf, with no bass (Cb rests 85-89, c228).
- 88-90: the viola entry, doubled by Bsn 1, with the cellos' counter. The Cb comes in only at 90, mf, to prepare the third entry.
- 91-93: Vln II + C.a. on C. The bass is now full (B.cl low C, Bsn 2, Cb), and the violas and cellos carry two free voices.
- 94-96: Vln I + Fl 1 on F. Cl 1, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Cbsn come in at 96, f to ff, into the B-minor ff at 97.

Each entry adds one layer, and the doublings change colour each time: Vla with Bsn, Vln II with C.a., Vln I with Fl. That is Haydn's varied doubling (PDF 288). I looked for one extra element a master might add, for example an off-beat pizzicato bass at 88-89 like Mozart's light bass. It would weaken the arrival of the bass at 90, which is the turning point of the passage, and the fugato is protected (HISTORY "Do-not-touch: Fugato 85-96"; c437). Keep.

### 2. New register scan of the wind and horn solos (PDF 274): all contacts are sustained or deliberate (keep)

`proximity.py` lists every half beat at which another part sounds within a whole tone of a wind or horn solo, or above it. The rule on PDF 274 matters most for woodwind solos. The only "near" contacts are these:
- **Bar 7:** Vln II F4 under the C.a.'s G4 (pp, Neapolitan sixth). It is a held string note against a moving solo, which is the rhythmic contrast PDF 274 says lessens the concern. The intro strings 6-8 are protected.
- **Bar 128.3-4:** Hn 4 G3 under Cl 1's A♭3 in the stretto. It is the dominant pedal. The minor ninth A♭ over G is the point of the passage, and 127-130 is protected.
- **Bar 150.1-2:** Hn 1 and the violas on C5 under the C.a.'s D5. This is the tune itself (the C.a. fill overlaps the tune's peak by two beats). The C.a. is the accompanying part here, so the rule does not apply.

The "above" contacts are held string pads over low solos: Vln II over the B.cl at 77-78 and 107-110, and the violins over Bsn 1 at 144-145. This is the layout of Beethoven's Ex. XVII-15 (PDF 274), where simple string harmony sits above and around the middle-register bassoon. No solo is crossed by a moving part of its own family. Keep.

### 3. Bars 144-145: Bsn 1's answer starts under the tail of Vln I's phrase (keep, protected)

Vln I is still finishing its head at 144 (C5–B4–C5–A♭4–G4) when Bsn 1 enters with G3–E♭4–D4–C4–B3. For one bar the two lines overlap, a tenth and more apart, in different rhythms. Adler's dialogue examples (PDF 257-258) separate the solo statement from the answer. Mozart's horn concerto (PDF 265-266), though, has the strings enter on the solo's last note, which is what happens here. The overlap also keeps the pulse going into the poco rit. at 145. c247 already moved the violas off the answer, and 143-146 is protected (c477). Keep.

### 4. Bars 98-102: the brass and Bsn 1 fill lands on the tune's passing note (keep)

At the end of each held tune note, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Bsn 1 play a three-quaver fill (F♯3–D4–C♯4 at 98.3.5-98.4.5; the same shape at 99, 100 and 102). At 98.4.5 and 102.4.5 the fill's last note is the tune's own passing note, at the octave of Vln II and the cellos. This is the Mozart pattern (PDF 265-266, Ex. XVII-9 bars 52-60): the accompaniment fills the solo's long note with an answering figure and joins the line at the next note. It is not a duplicated rhythm, because the tune holds while the fill moves. Adler's warning against unison doubling (adler1 PDF 257) concerns whole lines, and this is one quaver in an ff tutti, where the same page says the practice "is still common". Keep.

### 5. Holistic question: is any accompaniment in the movement forgettable? (keep)

The backgrounds use eight different patterns, and no two neighbouring sections share one:
- off-beat quavers (15-22);
- the hammered quaver bass (27-32);
- the harp arpeggio with a viola pad (47-55);
- the cello arpeggio with the obbligato (56-63);
- tremolo chords with interjections (77-84);
- the viola quaver ostinato (97-102);
- the viola semiquaver wedge (111-116);
- the sul tasto halo (147-155).

Tchaikovsky's PC1 opening (PDF 261) is the model for 97-102, and Sibelius (PDF 269) is the model for 147-155. Neither is applied mechanically: 97-102 keeps the wind fills, and 147-155 adds the C.a. answers. I found no bar where a change of accompaniment would give the listener something more memorable without undoing a contrast that the chapter itself asks for. "No change" is the result of this pass.
