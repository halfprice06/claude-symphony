# Critic 481: Melody and memorability (v481)

Lens: the motto, P, S and the horn call, their contour, rhythm, climaxes and development.
Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations, pedal point (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371), third pass. Book page = PDF page + 2.

Method. I read all 26 text pages in full, including the Points for Review (PDF 369) and the exercises (PDF 370–371). I rendered every page at 110 dpi (/tmp/critic-481/pg-346.png to pg-371.png) and looked at each one, because the OCR loses the notes and the figures (for example Ex. 21-9's "good / sometimes / avoid" labels, Ex. 21-12's "poor / good", and the figures under Ex. 21-15f). I read c371 (this lens, same reading, second pass) and c418 (expression, same reading) so as not to repeat them. I then built v481 unchanged in /tmp/critic-481/work, rebuilt full.midi, and wrote a new scanner (/tmp/critic-481/scripts/susp.py). For 15 melodic and inner parts it finds every note that is dissonant against the lowest sounding note on a beat and then falls by a step in the same part, and it lists any other part, other than a doubling of the bass pitch class, that sounds the tone of resolution while the dissonance lasts. Output: /tmp/critic-481/susp.txt. I also printed the sounding pitches at the movement's main suspensions beat by beat. The aim was to check the places that changed since c371 (24–25 and 136–137 at c404 and c422, 61 at c401, 68 at c422, 154 at c402, 159–160 at c374 and c414, 166 at c418) and to look again at whether any theme could sing more.

## Findings

No change is recommended. The score already follows every principle in the reading. The two places the scanner flags that are not covered by the reading's exceptions (Findings 3 and 4) are, on inspection, outside the scope of the rule. I tried to find a melodic improvement the reading supports and found none that is better than what is there. The details follow, ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. Re-scan after the series 3 and series 4 changes: no new early resolutions (no change)

Since c371, inner parts changed at 24–25 and 136–137 (Hn 1 A♭4, Cl 1/Vln II E♭4), 61 (Vln I D♭5 skip), 68 (Vla A♭4), 154 (Vln II B4–C5), 159–160 (Tbn 1 B3–C4, Tpt 3) and 166 (horn levels). Any of these could have put a tone of resolution under a suspension (PDF 352, §12, Ex. 21-12a–c "poor"). The scanner found 24 notes of a beat or longer with the resolution sounding elsewhere. None of them is the reading's case:

- 117–130, 122–123 and 168–169: the resolution is a tone of the G or C pedal. Over a pedal, upper-part dissonances against the bass are free; only dissonances among the upper parts must be prepared and resolved (PDF 368). The 9–8 over the bass is the reading's own exception (Ex. 21-12d).
- 24.4–25.3 and 136.4–137.3: F5 and G5 in the tune are chord tones or passing tones inside the three-beat groups. The harmony changes under them (iv – iiø7 – vii°7, c240). They are not held into a new harmony.
- 51.4, 151.4: B♭4 and D4 are passing tones on beat 4 of the S head, not suspensions.
- 63.1: Hn 4 B♭3 under Vln I's C6, two octaves and a step below in another choir. c371 kept it under the contrast exception (PDF 352), and I agree.
- 114.2, 130.3, 157.3: passing tones in the wedge, in the stretto and in the violins' approach to the climax.

The main suspensions of the themes are clean at beat level (pitch lists from full.midi):
- 13.3–14.3: Hn 1 holds C4 over G; no B sounds anywhere until the horn's B3 on 14.3.
- 44.1: C5 over D♭ with no B♭ (c371's fix holds); B♭3/B♭4 together at 44.3.
- 66.3–67.1½: Vln I/II A♭5/A♭4 over E♭; no G until 67.1½.
- 164.3–165.1½: C6 over G; no B until 165.1½.
- 178.3–179: E♭5 over D♭, released by the bass (9–10, PDF 353, Ex. 21-14b).

### 2. Where the themes' memorability comes from, judged against the reading (no change)

The reading says that with a suspension, "rhythm rather than melody assumes paramount importance" (PDF 346), and that the suspension brings its voice into prominence (PDF 348 §4). Each of the four themes has one held, displaced tone of this kind. That held tone is what a listener keeps:

- **Motto (1–4).** The long E♭ after the leap of a sixth, then the chromatic sigh D–D♭–C. In the coda the sigh's D♭ becomes the bass under the held E♭ (9–10 at 178–179), which is the reading's suspension with a moving bass.
- **Horn call (13–14).** C4 is held through the 6–5 of the strings and falls to B3 two beats late. This is the reading's delayed resolution (PDF 359–360) followed by a transferred resolution into the bass C at 15 (PDF 360–361).
- **P (15–22).** The accented E♭5 on 1-and is a long high note on a metrically weak beat. That is the Mozart K. 378 pattern the reading calls "rhythmically strong, though metrically weak" (PDF 363, Ex. 21-32). The quaver C before each bar line is an anticipation (PDF 363).
- **S (47–68).** Its peak is a real suspension: C6 prepared as the 6 of the cadential 6/4, held over V7 as the 7/6, then C6–D♭6–B♭5. This is the Josef Strauss figure the reading calls "a beautiful suspension resolving into V7" (PDF 353–354, Ex. 21-15f; on the image, the 7/6 is held two bars before it resolves, as the C6 is held into 63 here). Then the tied 4–3 at 66→67 is decorated by the descending scale A♭–G–F–E♭–D♭–B♭, which is the reading's Ex. 21-26f.

I compared these with the reading's literature examples. The S peak is longer and more prepared than the Strauss example, and the shared 7–6 line at 44–45 (horn to clarinet) works like Mozart's piano-to-violin line in Ex. 21-17 (PDF 355). I found no point where a further suspension or anticipation would make a theme easier to remember without making it more decorated. Brahms's and Dvořák's themes stay simple at their first statement for the same reason. The themes and their growth are protected (c411, c431, c471) and quoted in movements II and IV. The reading gives no reason to reopen them.

### 3. Bar 53.3: the accented C5 over ii6 sounds against the violas' B♭3 (no change)

At 53.3, Vc and Cl 1 play C5 for a dotted crotchet over the D♭ bass (ii6 of A♭). It is a major seventh above the bass and falls to B♭4 on 53.4½. The violas hold B♭3 (`<bes f'>1`, c163) through the whole bar. On paper this is the 7–6 shape of Ex. 21-12c. It is not a suspension, though. The C5 is approached by step from D♭5 in the run F5–E♭5–D♭5–C5–B♭4–A♭4, so it is an accented passing tone, and Unit 21's rule concerns tones held over from the preceding harmony. The B♭ also sounds a ninth lower in a sustained pad of another register, against a moving solo line in horn and cello. That is the contrast of rhythm and register in which the reading calls early resolution "much less problematic" (PDF 352). Taking the B♭ out of the violas would leave ii6 with no root above the bass for a beat and a half. c163 fixed the violas' notes here to avoid octaves with the bass. Keep it.

### 4. Bars 161.4–162: B5–C6 before the deceptive arrival on A minor (no change)

At 161.4 the violins and flutes play B5–C6 (quavers) over V7, and at 162 the line drops to A5 over A (vi), ff. The C6 arrives on the weak half of beat 4. It is the reading's upward resolution of the leading tone into VI (PDF 358 §19, "or, sometimes, the VI chord"), made early as an anticipation (PDF 363 §25: it "can create a slight surprise"). The leading tone resolves in the melody, but onto the third of the wrong chord. This is how the climax turns away from C major while the finale's B→C arrival on a C-major chord stays in reserve (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). The trumpets' B4–C5 at 161–162 is protected (c472). No change.

### 5. Did any phrase fall short of a master's? (no change)

I looked for the weakest point of the lens again: a phrase that does not sing, or a climax that does not land. The candidates are the transition's woodwind fragments at 37–40 and the development's head dialogue at 77–81. Both are deliberately fragments, and both lead to a held, suspended arrival: the G6 at 41 and the four-horn call, and the ff head over A♭ at 82. The reading's devices are already at work there: the sf G6 is held two bars and resolves down by step (41–43), and the four-horn call's C4 over E♭ is a consonant 6–5 (PDF 348). The remaining distance to a master's movement lies in material that is withheld on purpose: the S theme in C major at forte, and a confirmed C-major cadence, both kept for the finale. That is not a fault to fix in movement I.
