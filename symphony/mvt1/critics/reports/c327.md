## Findings

### 1. Bar 144: the violas strike the pitch of Bsn 1's answering pickup (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 144 Bsn 1 answers the P head alone, mf: `r8 g8 es'4. d'4 c'8 | b1`. Its pickup G3 comes on beat 1-and. Since c247 the violas hold G3 from the downbeat of 144 (`g1\mp\>`), so the bassoon's pickup sounds in unison with a string note struck half a beat earlier. G3 is the viola's open G string, and the bassoon's G3 is in its middle register, which is not strong. The answer is the only melodic event in the bar: Vln I is fading out of the head. My scan found this to be the only soft solo pickup in the movement that is masked this way and is not in a protected passage.

**Evidence.** Adler's analysis of Brahms 3/iii says the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the [anacrusis] figure undisturbed" (adler1 PDF 135). He calls for a "radical rhythmic and expressive difference between the foreground and the background" (PDF 135; also PDF 142, Ex. V-20). On contrapuntal clarity he puts rhythm and register first and says marking different dynamics "is not always as effective as the other ways" (PDF 144). Cycle 261 dealt with 144 by dynamics only (strings mp to pp). The reading asks for the rhythmic remedy, and cycles 227 and 294 applied that remedy to the violin pickups at 15, 17, 19, 37 and 143. Cycle 247 moved the violas to G3 so that "the bassoon's answer is heard alone in its register". This change finishes that aim; it does not undo it.

**Fix (applied).** In `sections/recOne.ily`, `recOneVla`, bar 144:
`g1\mp\>` → `r4 r8 g8~\mp\> g2`.
The G3 now enters on 2-and, under the bassoon's held E♭4, as at 15, 17 and 143. Bars 145–146 are unchanged. The G of the i6/4 on beat 1 is still in the double basses (G1) and Vln II (G4). The cellos' E♭3 is unchanged. Bsn 1's answer, the rit. and the ff stroke at 143 (c239's "lead-in events") are untouched. This is a rhythm change only: no pitch class is added or lost.
