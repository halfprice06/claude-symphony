## Findings

### 1. The themes under this reading: no change

The string tunes Adler chooses (Brahms 1/iv, Ex. III-4; Tchaikovsky 5/ii, Ex. III-5; Brahms 3/iii, Ex. III-7; Tristan, Ex. III-59) share three traits:
- one arch with a single high point, reached by one or two characteristic leaps and left by step;
- a repeated short rhythmic cell, so the second phrase is heard as an answer;
- a return of the tune in a new string colour rather than with new notes.

In v541:
- **Motto (1–4).** Rising minor sixth and chromatic fall. Each restatement has a new colour: B.cl/Vc sul G, C.a., Fl 1, horns/trombones, the chorale, Vln I sul D.
- **P (15–22).** The motto's sixth, rising thirds to D♭6 at 20 (sf, Vln II an octave below), a stepwise fall to C5. The repeated cell is the dotted crotchet with three quavers.
- **S (46.4–68).** The sixth again as the pickup, a tenuto peak every second bar, and the cell D♭ C B♭ D♭ F | A♭ G, heard six times by 68.
- **Horn call (13–14).** E♭3–C4–B3, the sixth, left open.

The string returns follow Adler's single-string practice: sul D then sul A at 55.4–59 (Ex. III-6, III-7), sul G at 98–100 (Ex. III-5), sul D for the last motto at 177–182. The climax 158–161 takes the S head's sixth up to C6–A♭6 on the E string at fff with every note doubled at the octave (PDF 75, Ex. III-8). This is the means Tchaikovsky uses in his Fifth: one interval present in every theme, and returns that change colour. I found no phrase where a change of notes would sing more without touching material the finale quotes (P head, S head, 27–32, coda). No change.

### 2. New check: held open strings in string melody (no change)

Adler: "in slow, expressive passages, one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture", and the open string "stands out peculiarly" (PDF 73–74). Earlier passes checked this bar by bar in known places. I scanned the whole of full.midi instead: every note of two beats or more at an open-string pitch that is the top note of its string part (script /tmp/critic-541/openscan.py). It lists 44 notes. Nearly all are pads, pedals or bass notes: the G pedal 117–121, Cb 6 and 11, Vc 15–18, the chorale bass 175–177, and so on. Adler's rule does not concern these, since they are chord tones in held textures. Four are melody notes:
- **148.2, Vla A4 and Vc A3**: the tenuto peak of the C-major S. c341 Finding 2 considered this note. No single string carries the whole phrase, and a held expressive A is taken with the fourth finger as a matter of course (PDF 73: fingering "best left to the performer"). I cannot answer that reason better, so it stays.
- **158.2, Vln II E5** (tenuto, four beats) and **162.1, Vln II A4** (three and a half beats): the tune's lower octave at ff. Adler's rule is for slow, soft playing. At ff the open string's "greater vibrating potential" (PDF 73) helps, and players stop these notes by habit if they want vibrato. No mark is needed.

The violin, viola and cello melodies at p and pp in slow tempi (9–13, 47–68, 171–182) contain no other held open-string note. No change.

### 3. The sul G limit at 98–100, checked against the example (no change)

At 98 Vln II takes the B-minor S sul G and returns to the D string at 101 (c186, from Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26: the top of a lower string is its octave or ninth). I checked whether Adler's own example justifies extending the G string to 102 for more intensity at ff (PDF 74: higher positions "very intense and stringent"). At 220 dpi, Ex. III-5 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, mm. 111–119) runs from A3 to A4. That is the ninth of the open G, and it is never higher. Bars 101–102 go to B4–D5, a third and a fifth above that. Adler's example and Rimsky's rule agree, and c186's end point holds. No change.

### 4. Where the music still falls short of a master's in this lens

I looked for a phrase that could sing more, and tested two candidates against the reading:

- **Violas and cellos in unison at 147–150** (Beethoven 5/ii, Ex. III-39, "very successful coupling", p dolce). The cellos would take E4 with the violas and Hn 1, which is the register of the exposition's cello S at 47. I rejected it. The recapitulation's lower cello octave (E3) is what makes 147 sound different from 47, and the exposition already has the unison horn-and-cello colour. Bars 147–155 are protected as horn + violas + cellos (c227, c341, c451), and the reading gives no reason that outweighs that.
- **The coda solo cello sul D at 171–174.** c451 Finding 2 rejected this for carrying power and high positions. I agree.

The remaining distance from a master in my lens is the two-bar climb into the recapitulation climax at 156–157. The form critics (c523, c533) named it, and it is protected. Changing it would mean adding bars, and that belongs to the form lens. Within melody and string writing, I found nothing that meets the bar for a change.
