## Findings

No score change. The texture already does what the chapter teaches, and the places where its letter is not followed have reasons that hold. The findings below record what I checked in the unprotected bars and why each should stay as it is, ranked by how much each would matter if it were wrong.

### 1. Bars 98–102 and 67–68: violas above the second violins (no change)

**What I checked.** Adler PDF 270 (Copland, Ex. VIII-41): when the violas lie above the second violins, "the second violins therefore need the heavy doubling of the second clarinet and the muted horn". The crossing script finds the violas above Vln II for whole stretches only at 19–22 (the violas' counter-melody over Vln II's mp syncopations), 98–102, 107–110 (violas on the S head over the Vln II pad) and 67–68.

**Verdict.**
- At 98–102 the violas' quaver arpeggio rises to B4 above Vln II's lower octave of the B-minor S (B3–D5, sul G). This is Copland's case exactly, and the score already has Copland's remedy: Vln II's line is doubled at pitch by the cor anglais and the cellos (c231), so it does not depend on Vln II's single section. Follows.
- At 107–110 the violas carry the tune and Vln II is a pp pad; at 19–22 Vln II is filler under the viola counter-melody. The crossing serves the line on top in both. Follows.
- At 67.3–68 Vln II's lower octave of the S tail (G4–F4–E♭4–D♭4–B♭3 | A♭3) sinks under the violas' syncopated dyads (D♭4/G4, C4/F4) and is not doubled. Vln I carries the same line an octave higher, the bar is a p diminuendo into the deceptive cadence, and the violas' figure at 60–67 is protected (c277). Taking Vln II up would make a unison with Vln I; taking the violas out would leave the cadence without its middle. It stays.

### 2. Bars 19–22: Bsn 1 doubles the cellos at pitch (no change)

**What I checked.** Bsn 1 plays `f2 aes,2 | des1 | f2 g2 | g2 c2`, the cellos' line note for note, at mf. Adler PDF 257 says a unison overlap "usually only results in a stronger pitch", and PDF 265–266 that at-pitch doublings thicken a line. PDF 267 (Brahms 1/ii, Ex. VIII-38) suggests the other use for a single bassoon: an octave under a melody that needs bringing out. I considered moving Bsn 1 to the violas' counter-melody an octave down (A♭3, F3–A♭3, A♭3–G3–F3, F3–E♭3).

**Verdict.** Stays. An octave under the violas the bassoon would run through the cellos' F3 and G3 at 19 and 21 and Hn 4's F3/G3 pad, crossing both and making seconds with the cello at 21. The counter-melody is mf espr. in one section against f in Vln I only, and it is audible without help. The bassoon at 19–22 is also the first wind colour after 15–18 (strings alone), and so a step in the graded build to 27 that c287 protects. A stronger bass for the climb to the sf D♭6 at 20 is what the at-pitch doubling is for.

### 3. Bars 33–40: the passed gesture and its accompaniment (no change)

**What I checked.** The transition follows the Eroica pattern of PDF 253–254: the P-head fragment goes Bsn 1 (33) → violas (34) → Vln I (35) → Vln I and violas half a bar apart (37–38), with Ob 1 joining the violas at 37.3 and Cl 1 at 38.3, then the rising scales (39–40), and the summation at 41 with the four horns' call over the E♭ pedal. In Beethoven's example (PDF 254) the second violins and violas keep a running quaver accompaniment under the passed gesture; here 33–36 has held notes under it.

**Verdict.** Stays. The motion is supplied in another way. Bsn 1 does not stop after its solo but continues as a slow counter-line (F3–A♭3 | G3–E♭3 at 35–36) under Vln I's entry at 35. Vln II's tremolo enters at 37.2 (c294), when the gesture starts to come every half bar. The pad is the clarinets' from 35.2. A running figure at 33–36 would cover the bassoon solo, which Adler PDF 226 (cited by c151) says is easily swallowed. The third statement differs from the first two (the scale at 39 instead of the head), as PDF 255 asks.

### 4. Bars 47–55: the harp figure and the third-time rule (no change)

**What I checked.** The harp plays the same rhythm (four rising quavers and a minim) in each of nine bars. PDF 255: "the third time it must be a bit different."

**Verdict.** Stays. The rule is met at the level of the phrase: the S tune is Hn 1 with the cellos for two two-bar units (47–50, 50–52), and at the third unit (52.4) Hn 1 leaves and Cl 1 takes the line with the cellos (F5–E♭5–D♭5–C5–B♭4), a change of colour on the third statement. The harp's chord changes every bar. The figure is protected as a neutral accompaniment (c279 notes, c327), and a pattern change there would draw attention away from the tune.

### 5. Bars 103–106: Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 in unison on the P head (no change)

**What I checked.** This is a three-instrument unison overlap at f outside a tutti (PDF 257).

**Verdict.** Stays, for the reason c274, c279 and c299 gave: it balances Hn 1 + Bsn 1 + cellos on the S head below, after the ff tutti. Splitting it into octaves would put Cl 1 above A5–B6 (shrill at f) or below into the horn register where the S head is. Mozart's example (PDF 271–272) also has its winds on a pizzicato string pulse, as Cb pizz. here (c134).

### 6. Bars 23–25 and 135–137: the hemiola cells (no change; c297 still holds)

Since c297 the horns enter on the third cell (24.3) in the exposition, as at 136.3, so the third statement changes (PDF 255) and the choirs come in one after the other (PDF 253). Vln II's repeated crotchets under the clarinets' held notes are the "pulsating" doubling of PDF 262–264 (*La Mer*: one part re-articulates what another sustains, like Vln I arco and Vln II pizz.). Nothing to change.

### 7. Fugato 85–96 and the development imitation (no change)

The fugato follows Britten (PDF 272–273): the subject is heard alone in the cellos (basses wait until 90, c227), and each later entry has its own wind partner a register apart (Bsn 1 an octave under the violas at 88, C.a. with Vln II at 91, Fl 1 with Vln I at 94). The countersubject has its own rhythm (c107), and by 94 there are three moving parts plus the bass. The rhythmic-layer count from full.midi gives 4–5 distinct rhythms at 91–96 against 1 at 85–87, a steady thickening as in Mozart's and Britten's examples. The head dialogue 77–81 (B.cl, Cl 1, Hn 1·2 + Vc, Ob 1 + Vln I) is the Mozart 38/iii idea (PDF 271): imitation between distinct colours. Both are protected and should stay.
