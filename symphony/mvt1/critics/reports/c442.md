## Findings

I checked every progression in 1–182 against the table. No progression breaks the reading in a way that a listener would hear and that lies outside a protected passage. This is a third pass, so I also asked where the harmony in my lens falls short of a master's. The four items below are the places where that question has real substance. In each one the decision is to keep the score as it is, and I give the reason from the reading. No change is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. Bar 149 (recap S, C major): plain ii where the exposition (49) has ii7. Keep

What I checked: at 49 the exposition harmonises the S head with ii7 (B♭–D♭–A♭). Vln II's A♭4 is held as a common tone from 47 and passes to the melody's 4th over V at 50. A&S call this the normal treatment (PDF 177: the 7th "enters as a common tone held over"; PDF 182, Ex. 12-8: the 7th becomes the 4th over V). At 149 the recap has a bare D–F–A, and the melody's C5 at 150 arrives by leap, unprepared. I tested in my head whether Vln II's lower halo note should hold C5 through 148.3–149 (making ii7 and preparing the C5).

Why keep: A&S PDF 178 says that II6/5 gives "a much richer sonority", but that II6 "is often to be preferred if a light texture is appropriate". The recap S is pp dolce, in a soft C major that the finale must outshine (coherence/mvt1.md: "Movement I never confirms C major at forte"). The difference from 49 is the right one: the exposition's darker 7th and the recap's lighter triad. Holding C would also cost the chromatic C♯5 at 148.3 (F–A–C♯, the halo's own colour) and change the protected Vln II tremolo 147–155 (c427). The C5 at 150 is a free appoggiatura in the melody, which the free-texture allowance on PDF 177 covers.

### 2. Bar 53 (and 153): melody C5 over ii6 (D♭–F–B♭), left by a downward skip. Keep

What I checked: on 53.3–4 the melody (Hn 1/Vc, Cl 1) holds C5 for two beats over D♭2, B♭3, F4. That is a 9th above the root B♭, or IV with an added 6th and major 7th. The melody leaves it by a skip to A♭4 (the 4th over V at 54), not by step to B♭.

Why keep: A&S PDF 188 (Ex. 12-17, 12-18) treat 3̂ in the soprano over subdominant harmony as one of the commonest dispositions: "3̂ occurs very frequently in the soprano". Its resolution to 2̂ need not be immediate. Here the B♭ comes in the melody's own register at 54.3 (C5 … A♭4–G4 … B♭4), a delayed step that A&S accept in Unit 20 figuration. The IV–ii6 motion over the held D♭ is the 5–6 technique of PDF 168 (A♭3→B♭3 in the violas). c432 protected 52–53/152–153, and the reading gives no reason to reopen it.

### 3. Bar 61 (iv6 over F♭ in A♭, the Phrygian approach to the cad. 6/4): bass doubled, root thin. Keep

What I checked: A&S PDF 169 (Ex. 11-10) say the usual doubling in IV6–V is the 3rd above the bass, and call a doubled bass "poor" when it makes octaves or an A2. At 61.1–2 the orchestra doubles F♭ (Cb, Vc, Bn 2, Hn 4, Hn 2, Cl 1), and the root D♭ sounds only in the harp until the violas' D♭5 at 61.3.

Why keep: none of the doubled F♭s moves in octaves with the bass to E♭. Hn 2/Cl 1 go F♭4–C♭4–C4, Hn 4 leaps F♭3→A♭3, and only the octave doublings of the bass line itself (Bn 2) fall to E♭. No voice moves F♭→G, so there is no A2. The thick F♭ is the point of the passage: it states the F♭→E♭ thread that movement II takes up (coherence/mvt1.md seed for 60–62; II 5, 21, 78, 88). The melody's A♭5 over F♭ and D♭ is the 3rd above the bass, so the doubling A&S recommend is present in the line that matters. The c401 change at 61.4 already removed the one octave problem here.

### 4. Bars 158–166: the reading's devices at full strength. No change

What I checked: this passage uses almost everything in Units 11–13. The cadential 6/4 is approached from iv with the 6th (C) doubled (PDF 167, Ex. 11-7b). ♭VI replaces I after V7 (PDF 201–203), with the leading tone resolving in Tbn 1 B3→C4 and the strings. A second deceptive move V7→vi (161.3→162) has Tpt 1 B4→C5 and the 3rd of vi doubled (Ex. 13-12). Then comes a descending-3rds chain vi–IV–iiø7 (A–F–D, Ex. 11-2 and 12-9). The ii7's 7th C is held into the cadential 6/4 and falls to B (Ex. 12-8). The authentic cadence at 165→166 follows, soft, as the repetition after a deceptive cadence should be (PDF 203–204, Ex. 13-14). I found no voice-leading fault. The one P5 entry in vl.txt (162.1, Tbn 2 F3→E3 against Vc C4→A2) is a voice crossing between two lines, not parallel fifths in one register. It is unchanged from earlier versions.

A master would not do more here. Beethoven's "Waldstein" Ex. 13-11b uses VI the first time to prevent "a cadential effect too early" and I the second time (PDF 201). Bars 160 and 162 do the same thing twice, and the I is kept back to 166. The passage is protected (158–166 climax harmony, c430).
