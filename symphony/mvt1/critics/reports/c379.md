## Findings

No change is recommended. Each item below was checked against the reading and against the protected passages, and the reason for keeping it is given. Nothing was edited in the scratch copy.

### 1. Fugato second entry already coloured by Bassoon 1 (88–93)
**Bars:** 88.3–93 (Bsn 1 with the violas from E♭4 at 88.3; F3 under the violas at 91).
**Problem considered:** Adler PDF 143 shows a fugato "exposed in the strings" before wind colour is added. Here the second entry gets Bsn 1 at once.
**Evidence:** In Adler's example (PDF 143) only the opening of the exposition is strings alone. That is the point: the subject must first be heard in one plain colour. Our first entry (Vc, 85–87) is strings alone, and the Cb rests until 90 (c182; PDF 145, "a subject heard alone carries"). After that, PDF 144's own clarity rules ask for colour and register separation between entries. A soft bassoon an octave under or in unison with the violas gives exactly that. Mahler does the same thing in 2/i: low strings play alone first, and the bassoons join only at bar 10.
**Fix:** keep as is (`devBsnOne` 88–95 unchanged). Removing Bsn 1 would undo c142 and c237 ("fugato 2nd entry to violas + bsn 1"; "keep an established colour", adler2 PDF 176). HISTORY protects the fugato wind colours at 88/91/94 (do-not-touch, line 270).
**Applied:** no.

### 2. A♭ I6 at 69.3–69.4: the bass third is doubled in most voices
**Bars:** 69.3–69.4 (C in 16 of 22 sounding voices, including the top C6).
**Problem considered:** PDF 152 (Ex. V-27) and PDF 257 say to double the third of a first-inversion chord near the bottom of the chord. Doubling it high and many times can sound hollow.
**Evidence:** The doubling comes from the melody: the C is the codetta's melodic goal, and PDF 152 lists octave doubling of the melody as the first way to strengthen it. The low C (C3/C4) is present, as Ex. V-27 asks, and the root A♭ and the fifth E♭ are both held in the horns and the lower strings. The chord lasts two beats in a mf diminuendo, so it does not sound hollow.
**Fix:** keep. The harmony of 69–72 is protected (c331, do-not-touch "codetta 69–72").
**Applied:** no.

### 3. Final chord: the third is only in the violas (182)
**Bars:** 181–182 (E♮ in the violas; Hn 3·4 ppp on the root and fifth).
**Problem considered:** PDF 270–271 (Copland) moves a horn onto the third of the last chord so it can be heard.
**Evidence:** In Copland's example the third is the new colour of an ending that is meant to resolve. Here the E♮ is a thread that is meant to stay faint: E♭→E♮ is the motto's unanswered question, and it is answered in the finale. Mahler 2/i does the reverse: the trumpets play E→E♭ ppp, major to minor, before the last descent. A horn on E♮ would state the major outright and take away what the finale is reserving. PDF 252 (Brahms 2/iii: a soft wind chord colours the final string chord) is already met by Hn 3·4.
**Fix:** keep. The last chord, its five tied parts and the E♭/E♮ thread are protected and on the "Do not reuse" list.
**Applied:** no.

### 4. Ending soft and attacca where Mahler ends with a ff collapse
**Bars:** 175–182.
**Problem considered:** Mahler 2/i ends with a sudden ff chromatic descent in the whole orchestra (441) and a pizz. pp C. A conductor coming from Mahler might expect a final stroke.
**Evidence:** Ours uses the same devices Mahler uses just before that stroke: a pp timpani pulse on the P rhythm (179–180; Mahler 431–440), a tam-tam stroke, and the faint major/minor thread. Adler PDF 154 (diminuendo by scoring: the bass drops out; a single tonic in the basses; a last bowed note) describes our ending exactly. The coherence file reserves the large cadential gesture for the finale, and movement I runs attacca into II. A ff stroke here would cut across both.
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

### 5. Low Oboe 1 B3 at 117
**Bars:** 117.
**Problem considered:** PDF 270 removes a low oboe that "honks" in a diminuendo.
**Evidence:** Here the note is ff, inside the tutti arrival on the G pedal, where the low oboe's edge is wanted. There is no diminuendo. No other oboe note below E4 is marked soft.
**Fix:** keep (protected, c286).
**Applied:** no.

## Mahler 2/i: would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

On orchestration, yes. The movement shares Mahler's main habits.
- **Opening.** It starts in low strings alone and lets the winds in only after the bass figure has been heard.
- **Second theme.** It keeps the first theme's motion going softly under the second theme. Mahler's triplet bass continues pp under the "lang gezogen" violins at bar 48. Our cellos and harp continue under S at 56–68.
- **Climaxes.** It holds brass chords in tutti while the strings carry the line (PDF 240–245; Mahler 39 ff., violins sul G under held brass).
- **Close.** It ends with a pulse, a tam-tam and a faint major/minor thread instead of a cadence.

The difference is scale. Mahler's movement is 443 bars and about 22 minutes, ours 182 bars and about 7. Mahler has room for long episodes, such as the pastoral E major at reh. 4 and the long development march. Ours does not have them and does not attempt them. That is a choice about form, outside this lens's orchestration reading, and it was set by the brief. Within its length, the scoring is consistent, every entry is audible, and every principle in the table that applies is followed.

## Do not touch

The fugato 85–96 (strings alone first, then entry colours), the layering 111–117, the 147–155 tremolo halo with the C.a. fills, and the coda 175–182 with its E♭/E♮ thread are at a master's level and should not be touched.
