## Findings

No change is recommended. I looked for places where, measured against the Rhenish and the reading, the music falls short, and tested each one against the reading and the protected passages. None met the bar for a change. Nothing was edited in the scratch copy; I built an unchanged copy of v479 to confirm the baseline: 0 warnings, 18 PDF pages, only the known bar-96 octaves in analysis.txt, vl.txt identical to v479's.

### 1. Retransition 127–130 is thin beside Schumann's preparation of the reprise
**Bars:** 127–131.
**Problem considered:** In the Rhenish the return of the main theme is prepared over a long dominant and arrives with the four horns stating the theme ff. That is the moment most listeners remember from the movement. Our retransition is four bars that start pp with Cl 1 alone on the motto over Hn 3·4 and the cello triplets, and the thick sound arrives only at 131.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 137 builds a crescendo by adding octaves until the top. 127–130 does exactly that: Cl 1 at 127, violas at 129, then Vln II, Bsn 1 and Hn 2 at 130, timpani pp → mf → f, and the tutti at 131. Adler PDF 245 (Strauss, *Fledermaus*) suspends the pedal so that the anacrusis of the next statement is heard. The thin texture of 130 does the same for the P pickup into 131. Our "Schumann horn moment" is already in the movement: 117–121, with the brass motto ff over the G pedal and the fff peak at 122. A second loud brass statement at 127–130 would weaken 122 and 131 and repeat Schumann's gesture without his length (his dominant preparation is several times longer than ours). HISTORY protects 127–131 (crescendo, c428), "no soft trumpets 127–130" (c467) and the timpani (c437).
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

### 2. The A♭6 peak is reached at bar 31, before the three climaxes
**Bars:** 31, 72, 122, 140, 160.
**Problem considered:** Adler PDF 238 says not to anticipate a register expansion the music is heading for. Schumann holds back his top register in the Rhenish until the late tuttis. Here Vln I and Fl 1 reach A♭6 already in the first ff tutti.
**Evidence:** The three A♭ climaxes (82, 122, 160) are graded by harmony (A♭ bass, V♭9 over G, ♭VI), by dynamics (ff, fff, fff), by percussion (none, tam-tam, suspended cymbal) and by scoring weight, not by register. Adler's Schubert case (PDF 238) is about a woodwind colour that was going to expand. Our movement expands in harmony and mass. Most importantly, bars 27–32 are quoted at pitch in every part by the finale at its 199–204. Lowering A♭6 at 31 would change a quoted passage, and HISTORY keeps it ("Kept: A♭6 at 31 (finale quotes 27–32)").
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

### 3. Bassoons a2 on the bass at 157 and 163–164
**Bars:** 157 (F2, mp crescendo), 163 (F2), 164 (G2).
**Problem considered:** Adler PDF 249–250 says that a unison of two like winds thickens the tone and blocks rubato, and that octave doubling is better.
**Evidence:** Adler limits this to "ordinary solo passages, not tutti sections" (PDF 250). Here the bassoons are not a solo line. They double the double basses' bass line, as Mozart's bassoons do in *Figaro* (PDF 241–243), and the cellos, harp and double basses carry the bass through Bsn 2's breaths (c346). At 158–161 they are already split in octaves (c334). The rit. at 165–166 comes after the unison.
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

### 4. The second theme has no woodwind colour at its first entry (47–55)
**Bars:** 47–55.
**Problem considered:** Schumann gives the Rhenish second theme, in G minor, to oboes and clarinets. Ours enters on Hn 1 and the cellos with harp arpeggios and a viola pad.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 238 (Schubert) asks for each main thematic element to be scored differently. P is in the violins over a string accompaniment. S in horn and cello with harp is a fresh colour, and the woodwind colour arrives at 56 (Cl 1 and Hn 2 counterline). HISTORY keeps "47–55 no woodwind under S" (c474) and the horn and cello unison as a colour for movement II (c439). Adding Schumann's woodwind colour here would take S's identity away.
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

### 5. Soft attacca ending where the Rhenish ends brilliantly
**Bars:** 171–182.
**Problem considered:** The Rhenish first movement ends with a fast, loud, major-key coda. A conductor who knows it might find our ending inconclusive.
**Evidence:** Our movement is in minor and runs attacca into an A♭ Adagio. The coherence file reserves the large cadential gesture and the C-major arrival for the finale. Adler PDF 252 (a soft wind chord colouring the last string chord) and PDF 154 (diminuendo by thinning and dropping out the bass) describe our ending. The coda 171–182 is quoted by other movements and protected.
**Fix:** keep.
**Applied:** no.

## Schumann "Rhenish"/i: would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

Within its scale, yes. The movement shares the habits Adler attributes to Schumann and the ones the Rhenish shows:
- **Octave doubling.** The tuttis are heavy in octave doublings (27–32, 82, 158–161), which Adler PDF 248 names as Schumann's own manner.
- **Violas on a wind line.** The violas double a wind line at the octave as a bridge into the full strings (147–152; Adler PDF 259, Schumann 2/iv).
- **Choirs, then tutti.** The choirs are used separately and then together (77–82; Adler PDF 253, Schumann 1/iii).
- **Brass for the big moments.** The horns and trombones carry the cyclic material at the large moments (13–14, 41–42, 117–121, 175–179), as the Rhenish horns do.

The Rhenish has two things ours does not: momentum and a grand major-key arrival. The Rhenish's momentum comes from a 3/4 hemiola theme that never stops. Ours starts with a 14-bar Lento and breaks off at the stop chords. The Rhenish's major-key arrival is not ours to have, because this movement is in minor, runs attacca, and saves its C-major arrival for the finale. Neither difference is a fault of the orchestration that the reading would correct. Both come from the brief and the cyclic plan.

## Do not touch

The 117–122 brass motto and fff peak, the 127–131 build into the reprise, the 147–155 C.a./viola octave over the tremolo halo and the 175–182 chorale and ending are at a master's level and should not be touched.
