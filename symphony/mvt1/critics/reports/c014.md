## Findings

### 1. At the ♭VI climax the horns' counterline covers the tune's bottom octave (bars 154–157)
**What is wrong.** Since cycle 7, horns 1·2 a2 play the counterline at ff/fff with clarinet 1 (`g'2 c''2 | b'2 f'2 | es'4 c''4 r8 c''8 bes' aes' | aes'2 f'2`). The tune's lowest octave (violas and cellos, added in cycle 4) lies in the same span, g–a♭'. At 156 the horns cross above it (horns c'' bes' a♭' against the tune's a♭' g').
- On Rimsky-Korsakov's scale, two horns at fff equal one trumpet, about twice a viola-plus-cello unison.
- The counterline is heard as noise in the tune's register, and the tune loses its third octave.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 283–285, the fff climax: the tune is in violins I, violins II, violas and cellos in octaves. All four horns hold sustained chord tones (C♯4–B4), and no brass plays a moving second line in the tune's register.
- Rimsky-Korsakov (Ch. II): a melody in the middle of the orchestral range “is not so prominent”.
- Rimsky-Korsakov also cites *Ivan the Terrible* for reinforcing exactly this case: “Violas + 'Cellos + … 4 Horns”.

**Fix.** This reverses cycle 7's horn doubling. Cycle 7 wanted inner-voice interest. The counterline is kept, but in oboe 2 and clarinet 1, where it does not cover the tune.
- `recTwoHnOne` and `recTwoHnTwo`, section bars 16–19 (154–157):
  `g4\ff e'2.~ | e'4 f'4 g'2 | c'4\fff aes'2~ aes'8 g'8 | f'2 d'4 b8 c'8 |`
- `recTwoOboeTwo`, same bars (its whole notes duplicated trumpet 2):
  `recTwoOboeTwo = { R1*15 | g'2\ff c''2 | b'2 f'2 | es'4\fff c''4 r8 c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2) f'2 | R1*5 | }`

The cycle-12 resolution `es'4 c''4` at 156 is kept in the oboe and clarinet.

### 2. The second-theme counterline is in the clarinet's throat register, inside the viola chords (bars 56–68, 148–151)
**What is wrong.** Clarinet 1's espressivo counterline (cycle 7) lies at concert c'–b♭' (written d'–c''). That is the throat register and the break, in unison or a step away from the violas' syncopated chords (57: clarinet f' against violas' d♭' f'). The recapitulation version (148–151, concert g'–c'') has the same problem.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov says the clarinet is “weak in the middle compass” and “will not stand out prominently”.
- He also says a middle-register melody needs “contrast of timbres” or doubling.
- Dvořák 9/i bars 149–152: the lyrical theme is in two flutes in unison over ppp strings. It is doubled, and the accompaniment is two levels softer.

**Fix.** Horn 1 is free in both places, so it doubles the clarinet. Horn and clarinet blend, and the horn's timbre separates the line from the violas.
- `tTwoHnOne`: replace the final `R1*13` with
  `es'2\p r8 e'8( f'8 g'8 | aes'2) f'2 | f'2 r8 f'8( es'8 des'8 | des'2) es'2 | f'2 r8 f'8( es'8 des'8 | fes'2) ces'2 | c'4 es'8( f'8 g'4 aes'4~ | aes'4 g'8 f'8) g'2 | c'2 r8 c'8( des'8 es'8 | f'2) aes'2 | des'4 f'8( g'8 aes'4 bes'4~ | bes'2 g'4 des'4) | c'1\p |`
- `recTwoHnOne`: replace `e'1\p | R1*4 |` with
  `e'1\p | g'2\mp r8 g'8( a'8 b'8 | c''2) a'2 | a'2 r8 a'8( g'8 f'8 | f'2) b'2 |`

### 3. The coda's cor anglais motto disappears into the violas and cellos (bars 167–170)
**What is wrong.**
- The cor anglais (p) lies at G3–E♭4, below the cellos' melody (E♭4–C5, p).
- At 169 (`c'2 des'4 c'4`) it is in unison with the violas (`c'2 des'2`).
- At 170 it moves in octaves with the cellos (`c'2 b2` against `c''2 b'2`).

So its answer is absorbed into the accompaniment just where it should be heard.

**Evidence.**
- Dvořák 9/ii bars 7–10: the cor anglais solo (sounding F4–B♭4) is the top voice. The muted strings lie below it.
- Rimsky-Korsakov: melody stands out “from the very fact of position”.

**Fix.** Up an octave, into the register of its intro statement (bars 5–8, D4–B♭4). The cor anglais ends on the cellos' c'' rather than doubling their b′.
`codaCorAnglais = { R1*4 | r2. g'4(\p^"espr." | es''2.~ es''8 d''8 | c''2 des''4 c''4~ | c''2) r2 | R1*8 | }`
This keeps cycle 10's cor anglais answer.

### 4. The development's first tutti has the theme in only two octaves (bars 82–83)
**What is wrong.** At 82 the T1 head is:
- above: flute 1 and violins I;
- below: flute 2, oboe 1, clarinet 1 and trumpet 1.

Four horns, trumpets 2·3 and the trombones are ff on the A♭ chord. Violins II and violas play off-beat chords that only double the brass. Critic 4's viola third octave was applied at 27 and 163 but not at this third statement.

**Fix.** (Dev bar 6.)
- `devVlnTwo`: `r8 g'8\ff es''4.-> d''8 des''8 c''8 |`
- `devVla`: `r8 g8\ff es'4.-> d'8 des'8 c'8 |`

Bar 83 is unchanged.

### 5. Trumpets 1·2 hold ff whole notes inside the tune's lower octave (bars 27–31, 163–165)
**What is wrong.** Trumpet 1 holds c''/g'/c''/b♭' at ff, at the top of the octave where oboe 1, clarinet 1 and violins II carry the tune. On Rimsky-Korsakov's scale that one trumpet weighs as much as the whole woodwind-and-violin octave. Critic 4 moved trumpet 3 and horns 3·4 to f, but trumpets 1·2 had to stay ff because they share a staff.

**Evidence.** Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 130–133: the strings carry the tune in three octaves at mf, while winds and horns repeat the harmony at p.

**Fix.** Mark both trumpets f and keep ff on the accented chords.
- `tOneTptOne` / `tOneTptTwo`: `c''1\f` and `g'1\f` at 27, with `\ff` added at 32 (`g'4->\ff`, `f'4->\ff`).
- `codaTptOne` / `codaTptTwo`: `c''1\f` and `g'1\f` at 163; 166 stays `\ff`.

The two parts stay at the same dynamic, so the shared-staff rule holds.

### 6. A lower-priority check: the final E♮ (bar 178)
The violas' E♮ is the only moving voice, and every part is ppp. It is surrounded by horns g′/c′ and violins I/II c″/g′, and it will be heard. Tchaikovsky 6/i 350–354 puts the final major third in the trombones, but that would undo the cycle-10 design, so I do not recommend it.
