# Critic 134: orchestration, tutti chords (v153)

Lens 4: orchestration. Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, appendix "Single tutti chords" (PDF 505–510) and the tutti examples cited in ch. IV (text PDF 119–129; Examples 203–221, PDF 366–391).

## What I studied

I read the text pages PDF 505–510 in full. 505–507 are the three plates of 20 tutti chords; 508 is blank; 509 is a 1933 Kalmus catalogue; 510 is blank. I read the ch. IV text on full tutti, tutti in the wind, tutti pizzicato, tutti in one to three parts, limits of range, transfer of phrases, alternating chords, imitation and echo, and sf-p chords (PDF 119–129). I rendered and looked at all three appendix plates at 110 and 220–300 dpi, because the OCR of those pages is useless, and at every example page from Ex. 203 to Ex. 222 (PDF 366–391). As a supporting source I used ch. III on duplication (PDF 82–83). For the score, I used the v153 PDF (pages 2, 3, 6–11) and a script that lists every sounding pitch per instrument at a given bar and beat from score.midi. I also scanned every f/ff full-tutti beat for three things: each group's harmonic completeness, gaps wider than a fifth above G3, and doubling. No corpus files or web sources were needed. The appendix plates were enough to check chord layout.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 33: the ff tutti stroke has no violas or cellos, and the violins play single notes (applied)

Problem: bar 33 closes the first group with an eighth-note ff stroke on C minor in every wind and brass part (Fl to tuba, with timpani). In the strings, Vln I plays C6 and Vln II plays C5, both single notes. Violas and cellos are silent, and the Cb holds C2 fp. The string group therefore gives only one pitch class and leaves C3–C4 empty, which is the register where the strings sound most strongly.

Evidence: in every ff/fff chord of the appendix (PDF 505 chords 1–5; 506 chords 8–12; 507 chords 17–20) all five string sections play, and in the short-stroke chords they play multiple stops. Chord 3 (*Tsar's Bride*, eighth-note chord) has four-note stops in both violin parts and chords in Vla and Vc. Chord 8 (*Ivan the Terrible*) has every string part on a short sff stroke. The recapitulation's equivalent bar, 143, already has all strings sounding.

Fix (trans.ily), using open strings and first-position stops:
```
transVlnTwo: <g es' c''>8->\downbow\ff r8 r4 r2 | ...      (was c''8)
transVla:    <c g es'>8->\downbow\ff r8 r4 r2 | ...        (was R1)
transVc:     <c, g, es>8->\downbow\ff r8 r4 r2 | R1 | ...  (was R1*2)
```
The bassoon solo's off-beat entry on G3 is unaffected: it already enters against the full brass stroke.

### 2. Bars 103–106: the f wind/horn tutti has no pizzicato bass (applied)

Problem: at 103–106 the first-theme head is at f in Ob 2 and Clarinets a2, over the second-theme head in Hn 1, Bsn 1 and Vc, Hn 3·4 chords, and a bass in B.cl and Bsn 2. This comes straight after the ff B-minor block (97–102). Cycle 79 recorded "103–106 light after ff" as open. Cycles 84 and 117 added Cl 1 and the cellos, but the bass is still woodwind only.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 121 says that "tutti passages in wood-wind and horns do not produce any great amount of power in forte". It also says that "violoncellos and double basses playing more or less important pizz. notes are often added to wood-wind instruments (tutti) ... this process renders the sustained notes in the wood-wind more distinct." Ex. 210 (PDF 377) shows this: V-c e C-b pizz. f under the woodwind tutti.

Fix (dev.ily, devCb; was R1*4):
```
d,4\f^"pizz." r4 r2 | d,4 g,4 r4 a,4 | bes,,4 r4 c,4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r2 |
```
These are the bassoon/B.cl notes on their attacks, at their sounding octave. The "pizz." at 107 is then redundant and is removed, so 107 reads `e,4\p r4 r2`. The basses now stay pizzicato from 103 to 109, and the f/p contrast of 103/107 is kept. No notes in 103–110 change, which the cycle 74/84 protection covers. Only the doubling of the bass changes, and cycle 117 already broke the "winds and horns only" colour.

### 3. Bar 26: the ff stop chord has no timpani, unlike its recapitulation twin at 138 (applied)

Problem: 26 and 138 carry the same diminished-seventh stop chord (D F A♭ B over D) at the end of the hemiola. At 138 the timpani play `g,2.->\f`. At 26 they are silent, from 15 to 26.

Evidence: the timpani take part in every f/ff full-tutti chord of the appendix (chords 1–5, 9–12, 17–20; PDF 505–507). They are left out only in chord 7 (no woodwind) and in soft chords. The G (dominant root, timpani tuned G, A♭, C, E♭) makes the sonority V♭9 exactly as it does at 138.

Fix (tOne.ily, tOneTimp): `R1*11 | g,2.->\ff r4 | c4\ff ...` (was `R1*12 | c4\ff`). The stop after 26 is unchanged.

### 4. Bars 122–123: at the fff climax the bass trombone doubles the leading tone instead of forming the brass octave on G (applied; touches the protected 117–122)

Problem: at 122 the bass trombone moves C3 → B2, so the brass bass is G1 (tuba), then a tenth up to B2. B is then doubled in four octaves: B.tbn B2, Tpt 3 B3, Vla B3, Cl B4. Inside the brass choir, the movement's loudest chord has no root above G1 except Hn 4 at G3.

Evidence: the appendix's fff chords put Tbn III and tuba in octaves on the bass note (505 chords 1–5; 506 chords 9, 11, 12; 507 chords 17–19). Ch. III (PDF 82) says the bass is "doubled in the lower octave only". The score already follows this at 27, 31, 32 and 160 (B.tbn an octave above the tuba).

Fix (dev.ily, devBassTbn): `b,1\fff | b,1 |` → `g,1\fff | g,1 |` (with 124 `g,1\>` as before). The B is still in Tpt 3, Vla and Cl.

Answer to the protection: 117–122 was protected for the ladder, the augmented trombone motto (G–E♭–D–D♭–C, ending on C at 121), the A♭ over G at 122, and the Hn 4/timpani swell. None of these changes. The motto still ends on C at 121. If the composer prefers to keep the C3→B2 resolution in the bass trombone, this finding can be dropped without affecting 1–3.
