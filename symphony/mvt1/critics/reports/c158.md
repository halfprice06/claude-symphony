## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 35–36: the cellos at mf cover the end of the bassoon solo (applied)
**Problem.** The Bsn 1 solo (33–36) falls to mf at 34 and ends on E♭3 at 36. Since cycle 137 the cellos double the bass an octave above the double basses (`bes,1 | es1`), but they kept the mf they had when they doubled the solo. They are now a sustained background at the solo's own level, and at 36 they hold E♭3, the solo's last note, in unison. The other accompaniment is softer: the double basses are p after the fp at 33, and the viola answer is mp (cycle 151, for the same reason: a bassoon with accompaniment is easily swallowed).
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the arranger must not let the ear be "diverted or confused by an overbusy background". PDF 211: the background must not "interfere in any way with the melodic motion of the solo line". Ex. 2A (PDF 18) marks the background two levels below the solo. Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 1–6: the bassoon solo grows from pp to mp while the divided basses hold pp underneath. Rimsky-Korsakov ch. II: "melody should always stand out in relief from the accompaniment".
**Fix (trans.ily, transVc):** `bes,1\p | es1 |` (was `bes,1\mf`). At 37 the cello crescendo now starts at p, together with Vln II, Vla and Cb.

### 2. Bars 131–134: the trombones play the head unslurred while the parts they double are slurred (applied)
**Problem.** At the start of the recapitulation, Tbn 1·2 double the first-theme head with Cl a2, Bsn a2 and the cellos. Those parts are slurred: `r8 g( es'4.->) d'8( des' c' | c'4) ...`. The trombones have only the two small slurs `b8( c'8)` and `d'8( c'8)`. The head itself is tongued, so each eighth is articulated against legato woodwinds and cellos. The part is also inconsistent within itself.
**Answer to the earlier decision.** Cycle 8 applied its slurs "not trombones" and gave no reason. Cycle 88 then set the rule that doubling parts within one statement carry identical slurs and accents, and this passage was not brought into line with it. Adler (adler2 PDF 3) says trombonists combine soft tonguing with the slide change "to give an almost perfect impression of legato playing", so a slur here is idiomatic.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215): trumpets and trombones carry the same slurs and accents as the saxes. Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 245–246: the trombones' `d4( -> cis8)` has the same slur and accent as the oboes.
**Fix (recOne.ily, recOneTbnOne and recOneTbnTwo):** `r8 g8\f( es'4.->) d'8( des'8 c'8 | c'4) b8( c'8) aes4.-> g8 | r8 g8( f'4.->) es'8( d'8 c'8 | b4) d'8( c'8) g2 |`. The f dynamic is unchanged (cycle 144).

### 3. Bars 127–128: the clarinet's head is at the same pp as the G pedal (applied)
**Problem.** At the a tempo, Cl 1 has the only melodic line, the first-theme head that starts the retransition stretto. It is marked pp<, the same level as Hn 4 (G3, which is also the clarinet's first note), the cello G quarters, the double basses and the timpani roll. All of them crescendo together to mf at 129. The clarinet is in its weak low register (G3–E♭4), so nothing in the marking puts it in front. The later entries are already graded (Vla mf at 129, Vln II/Bsn 1 f at 130); only the first entry is not separated from its pedal.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13–14 (primary focus level above the secondary and tertiary), Ex. 2A (PDF 18; the solo one or two levels above the background). Rimsky-Korsakov ch. II: the melody "is detached by means of strongly accentuated dynamic shades".
**Fix (dev.ily, devClOne, bar 127):** `r8 g8\p\<( es'4.->) d'8( des'8 c'8 |` (was `\pp\<`). Cl 2 rests here, so the shared staff has no dynamic conflict. The stretto, the pedal and the timpani roll (protected) are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 103–106: Bsn 1 at f doubles the cellos at mf (not applied; note only)
Bsn 1 plays the second-theme head in unison with the cellos (mf) and an octave below Hn 1 (mf), but it is marked f because it shares a staff with Bsn 2, whose bass line is f with B.cl and Cb. Sebesky's rule for identical marks within a statement (Ex. 281) points to mf. This should change only if Bsn 2's bass can drop to mf as well, which is a balance question for the bass line. I mention it so it is not mistaken for intent.

### Tested and withdrawn: swells on the cor anglais answers 147–152
Following Ex. 2A (the flutes' mp<mf>mp answers), I added `\< ... \>` to the three cor anglais pickup figures. In the engraving the hairpins were too short to read and collided with the slurs. Under Sebesky's economy rule (PDF 12: a mark that makes no audible difference should be erased), I reverted them. The answers already work at p as the counter-melody that moves under the theme's held notes, which is what Ex. 269 describes (PDF 207).
