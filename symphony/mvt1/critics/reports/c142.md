## Findings

### F1. Bar 93, beats 3–4: the fugato's link to the fourth entry contradicts its own sequence. Applied.

**Problem.** The fugato entries form a descending-5th chain of tonics: G minor (85), C minor (88), F minor (91), B♭ minor (94). The subject's third bar always rises chromatically to the next tonic: 87 G–A–B♭–B♮→C, 90 C–D–E♭–E♮→F, 93 F–G–A♭–A♮→B♭. At 87 and 90 the parts below support this. At 90 the bass falls A♭–G–F (3̂–2̂–1̂ of the coming F minor), with C minor/G turning to C major/G on beat 4. That is a passing V6/4 of the new key.

At 93 the bass does the same thing for B♭ minor: D♭–C–B♭. But the parts above it do not follow. Viola E4 (with Vc G3 and Bsn 1 G3) turns beats 3–4 into C major, with the subject's A♮ as an added sixth. So the bar sounds as V of F, the key just left. The fourth entry then arrives by C major → B♭ minor (V–iv of F) instead of by its own dominant.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 267: "Normally ... all the voices above a sequential bass are themselves sequential."
- PDF 268: in descending 5ths "each repetition of the two-chord pattern is one step lower", and the bass forms two stepwise lines. Here the bass and subject are sequential, but the inner voices repeat bar 90's function instead of transposing it.
- PDF 266: in a sequence the qualities change "as needed to conform with the key". The key at 93 b3 is B♭ minor, and E♮ is foreign to it.

**Fix (sections/dev.ily).** Make 93 b3–4 F/C (F minor/C turning to F major/C as the subject goes A♭→A♮), exactly as bar 90 is C/G:
- Vla bar 93: `f'2 e'2` → `f'1`
- Bsn 1 bar 93: `f2 g2` → `f1`
- Vc bar 93: `c'4 bes4) g2` → `c'4 bes4) f2`

Result at 93 b4: C2 (Cb, Bsn 2, B.cl), F3 (Vc, Bsn 1), F4 (Vla), A4/A5 (CA, Vln II). It goes to 94 with C→B♭ in the bass, F→B♭ in Vc/Bsn 1, F held in Vla, and A→B♭ in Vln II. There are no new parallels.

**Protected material, answered.**
- Cycle 17 kept "fugato 85–93 with free cello" and the viola free voice e′–f′–g♭′ at 93–95. Cycle 107 protected the subject, the entries and countersubject bars 1 and 3.
- The subject, the entries and the countersubject are unchanged. Countersubject bar 3 at 93 was already not the countersubject (its transposition would be D♭–C, in octaves with the bass). The viola's F–E there is free.
- The viola keeps its free line (F4 held, then G♭4 at 95), and the cello keeps its contour (C–B♭, then down a fourth instead of a third). What goes is one E♮ and two G3s, which belonged to F minor.

### F2. Bar 35, beat 3: in the real transposition of the transition motive, Vln I's D♭5 sounds against the viola's D♮4. Applied.

**Problem.** At 34 the viola plays the motive C–A♭–G–G♭–F over A♭. At 35 Vln I answers it a 4th higher, F–D♭–C–C♭–B♭, over B♭. That is a real transposition that keeps the minor sixth. At the same moment the viola's tail (F–E–F–D) puts D♮ on beat 3 to make B♭ the dominant of E♭.

On beat 3 the D♭5 (accented, held from beat 2) therefore sounds with D♮4 a minor ninth below, a simultaneous false relation. The rest of the chord is B♭2, A♭3, F4, B♭4. After the D♭ moves to C on beat 3½ the line is clean: C♭5 over D♮ on beat 4 is the ordinary passing minor ninth of B♭7.

**Evidence.** Aldwell & Schachter PDF 266: "When the pattern repeats in a diatonic sequence, the qualities of chords and melodic intervals will change as needed to conform with the key." Here the melody keeps its quality (minor sixth, the motto interval, which must not change per cycle 101). So the harmony must give way while the D♭ sounds: B♭ minor first, B♭ major after.

The fix keeps the melody unchanged and has the D♮ arrive one beat later, after the D♭ has moved on.

**Fix (sections/trans.ily).** Vla bar 35: `f'4) e'8( f'8) d'2` → `f'4) e'8( f'8) des'4 d'4`.
- Beat 3 is now B♭m7 (D♭ in octaves with Vln I).
- Beat 4 is B♭7 with C♭ as a passing ninth, then E♭ at 36.
- The viola's tail still lands on beat 3 as Vln I's tail does at 36, so the imitation protected at 35–40 (cycle 21) keeps its rhythm. No spelling in 35–40 changes.

### Checked and left as they are

Each entry has its own colour and countersubject, and after F1 every entry is reached by its own dominant. That keeps the chain moving forward rather than looping. The semitone lift at 97 breaks it off before a fifth link.
