### 4. Foreshadowing stays at its present strength; the motto's colours do the cyclic work (keep)

The seeds of the scherzo (79, P bar 2's motive-x rhythm, 106) and of movement II (13 horn-call span, 45–47, 46–52, 60–62, 73–76) are derivations inside movement I's own material, not quotations; c260 and c330 rejected seeds of the finale's F1/F2 with the Tchaikovsky 4 / Brahms 1 precedent, and I see nothing in v360 that answers differently. What the reading adds on the second pass: Adler's point that the instrument follows the tune's register and emotional intent (adler2 PDF 188, Ex. XV-33, the Berlioz cor anglais solo) is how the motto is carried across the symphony. In movement I it never appears twice in the same colour and register: B.cl + cellos G2 (1–4, bare), cor anglais a fifth higher (5–8, the Berlioz instrument, espressivo), flute over the A♭ pedal (73–76, darkened), horns and trombones ff (117–121), Cl 1 half-augmented (129–130), the pp trombone chorale with tam-tam (175–179), Vln I sul D (177–182). The one colour it withholds is the bright full brass at forte in major, which is the finale's chorale (IV 224–228, 255–264). The cor anglais's role at 5–8 and 171–173 is answered by movement II's theme B in the same instrument; the Vln I sul D C5 is continued in movement II. This plan should stay as it is.

Fix: none.

### 5. The breaks at 83→84 and 61→62 should stay as breaks, not Adler's overlapped forte-subito-piano (keep)

Adler's sub. p device (adler2 PDF 190, Ex. XV-36: a soft choir enters under the loud chord and stays when it cuts off) would be the obvious way to soften the two hard drops after the movement's early peaks. At 83 the whole orchestra strikes the dominant ninth over G ff on beats 1 and 3; at 84 only Vc G2 and Cb G1 continue, sub. p, and the fugato subject enters in the cellos at 85. At 61→62 the bass G♭–F♭–E♭ drops to sub. p on the cadential 6/4. I recommend keeping both. At 84 a held soft wind or horn chord over the G would sit on the register where the cello subject enters at 85 and would undo the "strings alone first" fugato that earlier critics protected (c227, c279). Emotionally, the break also matters for the arc: the first A♭ climax (82) is cut off, not resolved, as the second (122) collapses and the third (160) is denied, so A♭ is reached three times and C major is never confirmed at forte. Adler's device belongs where the same chord continues; in both places here the music turns to new material.

Fix: none.
