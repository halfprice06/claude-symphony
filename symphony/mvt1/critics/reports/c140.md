## Findings

### 1. Bars 179–182: Hn 3's C4 enters on a seam and is held too long alone (APPLIED)

**Problem.** The trombone chorale's top voice ends on C4 at 179 (Tbn 1 `c'1)`). Hn 3 re-attacks the same C4 ppp on the downbeat of 180 with no overlap, then holds it through 181 and the fermata at 182 and across the attacca. Timing from global.ily: 180 and 181 are at ♩ = 50 (4.8 s each), 182 is played at ♩ = 30 with the fermata (8 s, longer if the conductor holds it). Hn 3 is alone on that pitch for about 17.6 s in movement I, then about 1.2 s more in movement II until Hn 4 takes the C on bar 1 beat 2, and it keeps playing to the end of movement II bar 1: about 21 s in one breath, all at ppp, at the moment the whole movement is listening to it.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 191, Ex. XV-37 shows exactly this situation solved by colouring a held C: each new instrument enters on the held C before the previous one leaves, all pp. PDF 192, Ex. XV-40: when a line changes instrument, "make certain that the seams don't show", by overlapping. v159 does neither: the C passes from trombone to horn with a gap and a new attack, and the horn is then left alone. PDF 187 (XV-32) describes continuity instruments that bridge a hand-over; Tbn 1, already on the C4, is the natural continuity instrument. adler1 22: the choice of who sounds the C is part of the composition, so the colour change should be placed where it helps (at 181, together with Hn 4's entry, when the final chord forms) rather than at 180.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-140/work/sections/coda.ily).** Tbn 1 ties its C4 over into 180, dies away, and overlaps the horns' entry by one beat. Hn 3 enters at 181 together with Hn 4 (both ppp, so the shared staff carries one dynamic).

```
codaTbnOne (175–182):  g1(\pp | es'1) | d'1( | c'2 des'2 | c'1)~ | c'1~\> | c'4\ppp r4 r2 | R1\fermata |
codaHnThree (175–182): R1*6 | c'1\ppp~ | c'1\fermata\laissezVibrer |
```

Hn 4 is unchanged (`g1\ppp~ | g1\fermata` at 181–182). The five tied-out parts (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3), the "attacca", the "||", the fermatas and the tempo stay as they are. Pitch content is unchanged: the C4 still sounds in every bar from 175 to the end. Hn 3's solo hold until Hn 4's relief in movement II drops from about 19 s to about 14 s (4.8 + 8 + 1.2), which is a normal ppp hold for a professional horn in this register (written G4).

This changes cycle 97's entry bar (Hn 3 from 180), and touches the coda, which cycle 130 protected for notes. The reason given in cycle 97 was to fill the C3–E♭4 gap when the chorale stops; Tbn 1 now fills it at 180, so the reason is still met. The chorale itself (175–179) keeps its notes and slurs; only the last note is prolonged.

### 2. Movement II bar 1 (relay, not in movement I's files)

In mvt2 v33 sections/intro.ily, Hn 3 keeps `c'2.\repeatTie` to the end of bar 1 although Hn 4 has taken the C on beat 2. By Adler PDF 192 (XV-40) one beat of overlap is enough. Suggest for movement II: `introHnThree = { c'2\repeatTie\ppp r4 | ...` (Hn 3 releases on beat 3). With finding 1 this gives Hn 3 about 15 s in total. The coordinator should pass this to the movement II thread, and close the open item recorded at cycle 137.

### 3. Bars 175–177: clarinet, not horn, recalls the intro horn call (checked, no change)

The horn call E♭–C–B of 13–14 (Hn 1) returns at 175–177 in Cl 1 an octave higher, pp. A cyclic recall would usually keep the colour (adler1 19). But Adler PDF 188 says the instrument must be chosen for the register and dynamic of the new statement, and PDF 188–189 lists horn and trombone as instruments with affinity that merge when combined. A horn at pp over the pp trombone chorale would be absorbed into it; the clarinet is the one colour that stays distinct over the brass. Keep Cl 1.

### 4. Bars 12–13: forte to piano in the introduction (checked, no change)

The f German sixth at 12 drops to p on the dominant at 13. Adler PDF 190 (XV-36) recommends overlapping a new soft group under the loud chord. Here Ob 1, the clarinets, Bsn 1, Hn 2, Hn 4, Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn stop at the end of 12 (Fl 1 dies away on its tied G5), and the p at 13 is carried by a smaller group (strings, Ob 2, Bsn 2, Hn 3, timpani) that was already sounding; Hn 1's call enters after it on beat 2 at mp. This gets most of the effect Adler describes, because the loud colours leave and the remaining ones were already there. The strings must still drop from f to p themselves; this is a normal string skill at Lento. No change.

### What the reading confirms in the cyclic design

- The motto's colours are consistent and therefore recognisable (adler1 19): low B.cl./Vc/Cb at 1–4 and 175–179, cor anglais as the answering voice at 5–8 and 171–174, trombones for the motto at 117–121 (ff) and 175–179 (pp).
- The at-pitch tripling of the bass motto at 175–179 is used once, for the movement's special moment (adler2 189).
- The last chord is coloured by the harp entering l.v. at 182 over the held strings and horns, which is the XV-37 technique applied to a whole chord.
