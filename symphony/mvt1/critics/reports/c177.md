## Findings

**1. Bar 106, Hn 1 sounding E5 (written B5), undoubled: do not add Ob 1. Close the cycle-176 item. (Decision; nothing applied.)**

- Bars: 105.4–106. Proposed in cycle 176: Ob 1 `r2 r4 r8 b'8( | e''2\> dis''2)` at p.
- The facts. At 103–106 the texture is two theme heads in two fixed colours. The first-theme head is on top in Ob 2 + Cl 1·2, at f. The second-theme head is in the tenor: Hn 1 doubles Bsn 1 and Vc an octave higher, at mf. Under them are a Hn 3·4 dyad and a B.cl/Bsn 2/Cb pizz. bass. Ob 1 rests from 103 to 114 and re-enters on the top of the wedge at 115, f. The E5 is the highest horn note in the movement; the next highest is D5. Hn 1 approaches it from B4 (an eighth note) inside a slurred line and attacks it at mf with a diminuendo. It is not a soft entry from silence.
- Why the oboe would change the texture:
  - (a) Adler PDF 261: one colour tied to one gesture keeps the form clear. Ob 1 would put a double reed on both lines for the last six beats. At 106 beats 3–4 the two oboes would sound G5 over D♯5 and then F♯5 over D♯5 on the same staff, a pair moving in thirds. The ear would follow an oboe duet there, and the tenor line's arrival would lose its separate identity at the one moment the two heads come close in register.
  - (b) Adler PDF 257: a unison overlap "usually only results in a stronger pitch". At p under a mf horn the oboe adds neither a colour nor cover. If Hn 1 cracks, the crack is still heard over a p oboe, so the doubling does not do the job it was proposed for.
  - (c) The score's own rule: the oboes share a part-combined staff, so simultaneous dynamics must match. Ob 2 is at f with a diminuendo, and a p Ob 1 there breaks the rule. Marking Ob 1 f instead turns it into a real second oboe line (point a).
  - (d) Adler PDF 260 (Ex. VIII-32): a colour held back keeps its freshness. Ob 1 has rested since 102, and its entry at 115 is the planned new colour of cycle 76.
- On safety. The web sources (orchestrationonline.com: control decreases as the horn rises; hornmatters.com) put the risk in soft, exposed high writing. Here the note sits mid-phrase at mf, with the same line an octave below in Vc and Bsn 1 and three winds a fifth to a sixth above. The corpus does the same. Dvořák 9/iv bar 274 (MusicXML, horns in E) takes Hn 1·2 up an arpeggio to sounding E5 (written C6 in E), supported only by Hn 3 an octave below. At bar 268 one oboe doubles it, inside a forte tutti. Tchaikovsky 6/i–ii keep the horns at or below sounding C5, so the note is higher than his habit, but it is within master practice.
- Fix: none in the score. If the composer still wants a safety net, use cycle 176's option (b) at the parts stage (cycle 185): a `\tag #'part` ossia 8va bassa for Hn 1 at 106 only. It does not touch the score's texture.

**2. Bars 135–137: the recapitulation repeats 23–25's scoring exactly, and Cl 1·2 double Hn 1·2 at the unison for all four sequential cells. (Applied.)**

- Bars: 135–137 (recap of 23–25).
- Problem: the rising three-beat sequence (E♭–D–C, F–E♭–D, G–F–E♭, A♭–G–F) is scored the same way in all four cells. The 135–137 statement is also identical to 23–25: Fl 1 + Ob 1 + Vln I on the tune, Cl 1 = Hn 1 and Cl 2 = Hn 2 at the unison on the pad G4/E♭4–F4/D4, Hn 3 and the strings as before. The only growth is the crescendo.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 255: in repeated and sequential material "the third time it must be a bit different, in order to renew interest". Adler PDF 257: unison overlap of pairs gives only a stronger pitch outside tutti. Adler PDF 268–269 (Ex. VIII-40, Tchaikovsky 6/i 237–244): a rising sequence gains colour by adding instruments as it climbs.
- Fix (applied in recOne.ily): Hn 1·2 rest at 135 and enter on the third cell (136 beat 3) with the clarinets' existing notes.
  - Hn 1: `R1 | r2 g'2->\mp\< ~ | g'4 f'2.-> | aes'2.->\f r4 |`
  - Hn 2: `R1 | r2 es'2->\mp\< ~ | es'4 c'2.-> | f'2.->\f r4 |`
  
  For 135–136.2 the clarinets hold the pad alone. The horn colour then joins on the third cell and swells into the stop chord at 138. The exposition (23–25) is unchanged, so the recapitulation now differs. The c176 breath marks for Hn 1·2 at the end of 134 are dropped because a rest follows; Hn 3 keeps its breath. No pitch changed, and no meter accent of the protected three-beat groups moved. This also gives Hn 1·2 six beats of rest in a stretch that was continuous from 131 to 142 (Adler PDF 292, cited by c176).
- Check: full.midi rebuilt from v196 and from the scratch copy, and the all-pairs voice-leading script (vl.py) run on both. The only differences are two disappeared doubling-octave entries at 135.4 (Hn 2 with Cl 2 and with the viola). No new parallels.
