## Findings

Within texture and counterpoint I found nothing that falls short of the reading and can be improved without undoing a protected passage or something another movement quotes. I recommend no change. Three earlier passes read this chapter and found the same, so this pass added two measurements they did not make (Findings 1 and 2) and checked two more passages against points of the reading not cited before (Findings 3 and 4). Each finding says why the passage stays as written.

### 1. The strings never drop out. Keep as written; the one natural place is reserved for movement II.

Measurement (`scan.py`): at least one string part sounds in all 182 bars, and at least two in 178. The four exceptions are 5 (Cb alone under the cor anglais pickup) and 85-87 (the fugato subject in the cellos alone). No bar is for winds and brass alone.

The reading values a string dropout. Dvořák's strings, "which have been playing continuously since the beginning of the piece", stop at m. 33, which is "a great relief" and "a bridge color to the next section" (PDF 213). Milhaud's orchestral version alternates choirs where the band holds one colour (PDF 234). Schoenberg drops the brass, all but the horns, for a few bars so the tutti re-entry sounds fresh (PDF 245). Brahms, Tchaikovsky and Dvořák all give short passages to winds alone in their first movements.

I looked for a place to do this. The one place where it fits is 73-76: the flute motto over the A♭ pedal, with the strings holding a pp pad. That pad could pass to Cl 1-2, Bsn 2 and Hn 4 (Cl 1 F4-E♭4 | E♭4 | G♭4 | F♭4 for the Vln II line, Cl 2 C♭4-C4 | C4 | C4 | D♭4 for the violas, Bsn 2 A♭2 tied with Hn 4's A♭3). The strings would then come back as the tremolo at 77, a new colour for the development. I did not apply it, for three reasons:
- Movement II opens (its bars 3-6) with the same chord, A♭ pedal with the F♭ mixture at its bar 5, scored for winds alone: Hn 3, Cl 2, Hn 4 and B.cl. coherence/mvt1.md records that movement II "keeps its solo fresh by ... putting its wind chord at 3-6 first", and mvt2.md says its ties are written "like movement I's same chord at its 73-76". If movement I already gave this chord to the winds, movement II's opening would lose its new colour.
- c447 checked 73-76 against Adler's Mozart model for a soft wind solo, which has a sustained tonic in the horns and low strings (adler2 PDF 267). The current scoring follows that model.
- The strings already change their role often enough to give the relief the reading asks for: pizzicato basses 47-55, 103-106, 147-155; tremolo 37-42, 77-81, 147-155; one solo cello 171-174; the cellos alone 85-87.

No change.

### 2. Accompaniment in the tune's rhythm: only two passages, both justified. Keep as written.

Ravel separates a tune and an accompaniment that move in the same note values by "highly contrasting colors" (PDF 217). `rhy.py` lists every bar from 9 to 182 where Vln II, the violas or the cellos have exactly Vln I's onsets on other pitch classes, with at least three onsets. It finds only 23-25, 135-137 and 72. c457 (Finding 1) explained 23-25/135-137: the tune there is led by the winds (Fl, Ob, Cl), and the inner accents mark the three-beat grouping. Bar 72 is the codetta's last three hammered chords, a homophonic cadence. Schoenberg's secco chords on each downbeat of the big tutti (PDF 230) are the same thing. Nowhere else does an accompaniment shadow the tune's rhythm in the same family. No change.

### 3. The fugato and the Schoenberg fugue model (PDF 245). Keep as written.

Schoenberg's orchestral fugue begins with one string section, gives the second statement a new colour, softens the stretto entrance, and keeps only the horns in the brass before the tutti comes back. The fugato here does all four:
- the cellos state the subject alone at 85-87 (the basses rest, since v228);
- violas + Bsn 1 answer at 88, Vln II + C.a. enter at 91, Vln I + Fl 1 at 94;
- no brass plays until the f pickup at 96, into the ff at 97.

I also checked the bass through the fugato. After the subject the cellos are free (protected since cycle 17) and the basses come in at 90, one bar before the third entry. That is the "small bass counterpoint" Stravinsky adds without "interfering with the melody" (PDF 228). The only remaining question is whether the 97 tutti should be held back by horns alone for one more bar. 95-96 already crescendo through B♭ minor, and the S statement at 97 is ff by design (c453 protects the 97-102 ff step). No change.

### 4. 107-110, the softest texture in the development. Keep as written.

107-110 is the thinnest passage in the development: B.cl, Vln II divided, violas and the basses, p (`who.py`). I asked whether a listener would remember it, or whether it is too grey after the f combined heads at 103-106. The reading supports it in two places:
- Stravinsky lightens the texture of the second phrase so that the next entrance is fresh (PDF 228). Here the lightening prepares the wedge at 111.
- Ravel's thin harmony "in an intense register" sounds full (PDF 224). Here the violas reach C5 at 108 and C♯5-F5 at 109 with Fl 1.

The passage also carries two seeds: Vln II's D♯4 rising to F4 at 108-109 (quoted by the finale), and the B.cl's G3-F♯3-F3-E3 at 107. The pads at 107-110 and the Vln II divisi are protected (c447, c487). No change.
