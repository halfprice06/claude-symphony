Lens: dynamics, articulation, phrasing slurs, tempo changes, rubato and expressive text.
Assigned reading (second pass): Aldwell & Schachter, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations and pedal points (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371). Critic 318 reviewed v318 with the same lens and reading (changes at 122–124 and 45 stand), and critics 281 and 371 used it for melody (changes at 45 and 44 stand). I did not repeat their points.

Method: I wrote two scripts over v418's full.midi (in /tmp/critic-418/scripts/). susp.py lists every note that is held or restruck into a beat where it is a 2nd, 4th, 7th or 9th above the lowest sounding part and then moves down by step to a consonance; it found 168 tied cases in 54 places. For each I compared the MIDI velocity of the dissonance and of its resolution, and then read the marks in the section files and on the score pages. antic.py lists weak-beat anticipations (11 places). I read all 17 music pages at 110 dpi and looked separately at the pedal points 1–4, 39–40, 73–76, 117–130, 167–170 and 179–182.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 165–166: the horns resolve the V7 louder than they held it (applied)

What is wrong. At 164–165 Hn 1 holds the seventh of V7, F4, at p (`f'1\p | f'1`, restruck at 165), and Hn 3 moves to the leading tone B3 at p. At 166, the soft C-major cadence, both resolve (F4→E4, B3→C4), but all four horns are marked mp there (Hn 1 `e'2\mp`, Hn 2 `c'2\mp~`, Hn 3 `c'1\mp`, Hn 4 `g1\mp`). So the resolution is a level louder than the dissonance in the instruments that carry the dissonance. MIDI shows Hn 1 at velocity 69 on the seventh and 77 on its resolution. The strings, bassoons, harp and timpani are p at 166. In addition, Hn 3·4 have no crescendo into the f of 167, while Hn 1·2, violins, double basses, Cl 1 and the timpani all crescendo from beat 3 of 166.

Evidence. A dissonance resolves as "a relaxation, a decrease in intensity", and "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer" (A&S PDF 348, secs. 3–4). The seventh of a V7 is a suspension-type dissonance: "the 7ths of seventh chords often result from suspensions" (PDF 362), and the seventh held over from the dominant is the dissonant preparation of Ex. 21-23a (PDF 358). Here it is held and restruck, as a suspension is (PDF 347, Ex. 21-2: restruck suspensions behave as tied ones). The cadential 4–3 three beats earlier (164→165) already follows the rule with a diminuendo and rit.; the horns at 166 contradict it. Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365) shows the order the reading expects: the resolution softer, and any crescendo afterwards.

Fix (scratch copy, sections/recTwo.ily). Bar 166 only:
- Hn 1 `e'2\p es'2\<`; Hn 2 `c'2\p~ c'2\<` (shared staff, equal dynamics).
- Hn 3 `c'2\p~ c'2\< \breathe`; Hn 4 `g2\p~ g2\<` (shared staff, equal dynamics).
Notes, ties, the c386 breath and the f at 167 are unchanged. The crescendo now starts in all four horns on beat 3, where the E♭ of C minor arrives and where every other part starts its crescendo. Page 17 checked at 130 dpi: the Hn 3·4 chord stays combined, the hairpins do not collide.

Protection. c240 and c330 protect 165–166 as a soft C-major cadence (no forte C major before the finale's 222→223). The change makes the cadence softer, not louder, so it serves that reason. c222's Hn 3 C4 and c234's Hn 4 G3 are unchanged in pitch.

### 2. Bar 110: crescendo through the cadential 6/4–5/3 of F minor (no change)

At 110 Fl 1 and violas (A♭5→G5) and Vln II (F4→E4) resolve the cadential 6/4 over C to V with a crescendo that runs through the resolution into the mp cresc. of 111. Read alone, PDF 348 would put the crescendo after the resolution. I recommend keeping it. The dissonance already has its tenuto (Fl 1, Vla `aes''2--`), and the hairpin is the start of the wedge 111–116, which is one long crescendo to ff at 117. This is the same case as 62–63 and 159, where c318 kept phrase-length crescendos over a resolution in a secondary line. c348 also left 109–110 as they are.

### 3. Bar 126: the restruck 4–3 over the G pedal at the end of the collapse (no change)

At 126 Cl 2, Hn 3, violas and cellos restrike C4 over the G pedal and resolve to B3 on beat 2, all at p, after the fade from 123 (c318). The resolution is not louder (velocity 69 both), and the bass pedal parts drop to pp below it. The reading's demand is met, and 122–127 is protected for its exit order and hairpins (c318, c324). No mark is needed.

### 4. Bar 142 and bars 40→41: resolutions accented (no change)

At 142 C–B over G is accented in every part, and at 41 the cellos' A♭3 (seventh over B♭) resolves to G3 sf. Both are struck arrivals, not singing suspensions: 142 rhymes with the hammered 32 that the finale quotes, and 41 is the sf arrival of the transition (c228, c253). c318 answered 142 with the reading's own allowance for a strong rhythmic accent (PDF 362–363); the same applies to 41.

### 5. Coda 177–182: the motto's D♭5 over the C pedal (no change)

The last motto's D♭5 at 180 sits over the tonic pedal without a hairpin of its own, while the opening motto has swells at 2–3. The pedal and the level (pp, > to ppp at 181) follow PDF 367–368. The coda's notes, levels and timing are protected (c228, c249, c278, c358), and a swell would make the ending less still; I leave it.
