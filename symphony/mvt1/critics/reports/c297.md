## Findings

### 1. Bars 23–24: Hn 1·2 double Cl 1·2 at the unison through all four hemiola cells; let them enter on the third cell, as in the recapitulation (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 23–25 the inner hemiola line G4–F4 / E♭4–D4–C4 is played at the same pitch by Cl 1·2, Hn 1·2, Vln II and violas (tOne.ily: `tOneClOne` g'2.-> f'4~ | f'2 g'2~ | g'4 f'2. = `tOneHnOne` exactly; `tOneClTwo` = `tOneHnTwo`). All four cells (23.1, 23.4, 24.3, 25.2) are scored identically, and the horns enter together with everything else at 23. In the recapitulation the same passage (135–137) has had Hn 1·2 enter only at the third cell (136.3) since c177, a change made on the same Adler page. The exposition was never brought into line, so the first statement is the plainer one and the horn/clarinet unison runs three bars at mf.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 257 (pair voicing d.): overlapping instruments at the unison "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch"; it is a tutti habit. Bars 23–25 are not a tutti (no trumpets, trombones, tuba or timpani until 26), so the four-way unison of two clarinets, two horns and two string sections gives only volume.
- Adler PDF 253 and 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration. This is often true of any sequential material." The tune at 23–25 is a rising sequence of three-beat cells (E♭–D–C | F–E♭–D | G–F–E♭ | A♭–G–F), and nothing in the scoring changes at the third cell.
- Adler PDF 253 (Schumann 1/iii, Ex. VIII-19): when choirs are first heard apart and then together, "a most satisfying sense of completion or resolution results". With the horns held back, the line is heard in clarinets and strings first and the horn colour arrives on the third cell, then the brass at 26.
- Supporting: in Adler's Beethoven 3/i example (PDF 253–254, Ex. VIII-20) the horns and trumpets join only at the summation, with the dominant–tonic pedal, after the gesture has passed through the woodwinds and Vln I.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/tOne.ily).**

```lilypond
tOneHnOne = {
  R1*8 | R1 | r2 g'2->\mp\< ~ | g'4 f'2.-> |
  aes'2.->\ff r4 | ...
}
tOneHnTwo = {
  R1*8 |
  R1 | r2 es'2->\mp\< ~ | es'4 c'2.-> | f'2.->\ff r4 |
  ...
}
```

This is the recapitulation's figure at 136–137 note for note. Cl 1·2, Vln II and violas keep the line from 23, so nothing is lost from the harmony; Hn 3·4 (the protected low horns and their accents at 23–26) are untouched; the hemiola rhythm and every pitch of 23–26 are unchanged. The horns now rest 15–24.2, which also gives them the entry that 136.3 has.

### 2. Bars 117–120: the P-head "ladder" stays in one combination (no change)

Adler's Eroica example (PDF 253–254) passes a gesture from instrument to instrument before a summing tutti, and the ladder at 117–120 keeps the same combination (Fl 1·2/Vln I on top, Ob 1/Cl 1/Vln II an octave lower) for four bars. I considered splitting the two calls of 119 and 120 between woodwinds and violins. It should stay. The ladder already changes on its third bar (from 119 the call is halved and comes twice per bar, rising), which is the alteration PDF 255 asks for; and Adler's passing of the ball is a soft contrast after a tutti (PDF 253), whereas 117–121 sets the calls against the ff horn/trombone motto, where halving each call's instruments would let the brass cover it (PDF 268–269: keep the brass down so the strings and winds come through). The protected ladder (c474) and brass motto (c289) stand.

### 3. Bars 56–59: Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline at the unison (no change)

PDF 257 would object to a unison overlap, but there it concerns pairs voicing a chord. At 56–59 the clarinet and second horn share one counter-melody at p under the violins' S, the standard Brahms/Tchaikovsky blend of clarinet and horn on one line, and the violas were already taken off it (c277). The line needs both instruments to balance two violin sections in unison. It stays (protected by c513 and c589).

### 4. Fugato 85–96 and the bass line (no change)

The fugato follows PDF 271–273: the subject alone in the cellos, then each entry with its own wind partner (Bsn 1 an octave below the violas, C.a. with Vln II, Fl 1 with Vln I), five independent parts by 94, and the basses entering only at 90. The bass line elsewhere is a real counter-voice (Vc C–D–E♭–D against the Cb pulse C–C–G at 15–16; Bsn 1/Vc tenor line in contrary motion at 139–142), and the wind doublings of the bass follow Adler's Figaro and Dvořák remarks (PDF 267–268). Nothing to change.
