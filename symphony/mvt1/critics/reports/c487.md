## Findings

- No inner string sounds above Vln I.
- The only inner-string note held for four bars or more is the cello's A♭2 pedal at 73–76.
- The only full-bar unisons between adjacent string sections are deliberate: Vln I·II at 56–59, 139–142 and 167–170; Vla/Vc sharing one note of a held chord at 12 (C4, German sixth), 55 (C4) and 124–126 (G3), and on the stop chord at 170; one shared pad note in Vln II/Vla at 52–53 (pp, rule 1 economy).

### 1. 107–108: Vln II's lower divisi touches G3 under the viola tune (keep)

The reading: a soft background avoids the extreme low register and uses open or semi-open spacing. The violins are usually in thirds (Ex. 200, PDF 148–149). HISTORY has an optional open item from c173, "Vln II chord register at 107–108".

The score: Vln II div. pp holds E4/B3 at 107, then E4–D♯4 over G3–A3 at 108, F4 over B♭3–D♭4 at 109, and F4–E4 over C4 at 110. The viola tune (S head, p espr.) runs G4–C5–B4 above. The lower line touches G3, the violin's lowest note, for one crotchet at 108.1.

Why it stays:
- The pad is not the bottom of the texture. The bass clarinet's P head (B2–G3) lies under it, and the double basses are pizz. on E2/B1. So Vln II is an inner pad, not a low foundation, and Sebesky's warning is about the bottom of the voicing.
- The spacing is the semi-open kind he asks for: a fifth, then a sixth, a sixth and a fourth.
- G3 moves to A3, the seventh of B7 on beat 3. Raising the lower line an octave would put G4–A4 above the viola's E4 at 108.1, crossing the tune.
- The passage is protected (violas + Fl 1 107–110, c161/c241; 103–110 scoring, c197). The open item can be closed as "keep".

### 2. 150: nothing moves in the second half of the half-cadence bar (keep)

The reading: in Ex. 188 and Ex. 214 the attention passes from line to line, and a counterline enters where the lead holds (PDF 136, 168). c161 left an open note: "no moving fill under held notes at 150".

The score: in the recapitulated S (147–155), the cor anglais answers in two-bar units. A fill on beats 3.5–4 of 147, 149 and 151 leads into a held half note at 148, 150 and 152, and the second half of those bars is silent. At 150 the violas, cellos and Hn 1 hold C5–B4 (4–3 over V), Vln II holds its sul tasto tremolo, and Bsn 1 holds G2.

Why it stays: the silence on beats 3–4 of 150 is the same as at 148 and 152. It is part of the cor anglais's two-bar pattern, not a gap. A fill there would break the pattern and cover the 4–3 suspension that the tune holds. That suspension is the point of the bar, as at 50, where the harp alone moves. Sebesky's Ex. 214 adds the counterline when the lead changes registers or instruments, and here it does not. The passage is protected (c157, c378: C.a. answers 147–153, "dolce" and small swells).

### 3. No "soaring" six-note string chords anywhere (keep)

The reading: with sixteen or more strings, a six-note chord in the high register is "full-bodied and vibrant". Ex. 206 is a "soaring" passage for an emotional lift (PDF 158–159), and Ex. 208–209 divide the section into seven lines (PDF 160).

The score: no string section divides into more than two lines. At the emotional lifts (56–63, 97–102, 156–161) the strings play the tune in unison and octaves, and the harmony comes from the winds, the horns and the cello and viola arpeggios.

Why it stays: Sebesky himself puts rule 1 above this: "Having additional players at one's disposal should not automatically suggest the necessity for dividing" (PDF 147), and "dividing the section into many parts necessitates a loss in overall sonority" (PDF 161). His soaring chords belong to a studio string section without winds. In a full orchestra the late-Romantic lift is the octave-doubled tune, which is rule 6 (PDF 140) and Ex. 202, and which the score already uses. Dividing the violins at 156–161 would weaken the climax line that c184, c194 and c283 protect.

### 4. 110 and 144: open gaps of more than an octave inside the lower strings (keep)

The reading: in Ex. 199 a seventh-wide gap between the cellos at an arrival "would have diluted the sonority" (PDF 147).

The score: my scan finds only two downbeats where Vln II, viola and cello leave more than an octave that nothing fills.
- 110.1: Vln II's pad (C4/F4) and the viola's A♭5 (with Fl 1) are a minor tenth apart.
- 144.1: the cello's E♭3 and Vln II's G4 are a tenth apart. The chord is complete because Vln I has C5 on top.

Why it stays:
- At 110 the texture is Sebesky's own left-hand/right-hand layout (PDF 144): the tune alone above, the pad below, and the third (E4) in the pad on beat 3. It is not a dense four-note chord that thins out.
- At 144 the gap lasts one quaver. Bsn 1 enters on G3 at 144.1.5 and the violas on G3 at 144.2.5, inside the diminuendo after the f stroke at 143. The lead-in 143–146 is protected (c239, c327).

### 5. 167–170: violas and cellos inside the brass register while the brass have the P head (keep)

The reading: when the brass come forward, Sebesky gives up the string chords for a held high line and rests the cellos "in order to avoid clutter" (Ex. 216, PDF 171–173).

The score: at 167–170 the violins follow Ex. 216. They play high two-note tremolo chords (G5/E♭6, C5/G5). The violas play the P head ff in unison with Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2. The cellos play a counter in halves (C4–E♭4 | E♭4–F4 | E♭4–C4 | D4 under the fermata).

Why it stays:
- Sebesky's reason is rhythmic: a second moving part blurs the trombones' loose jazz phrasing. Here the violas double the brass line exactly, which is Sebesky's own rule 6 for a strong line, and the cellos move in half notes under quavers, so nothing blurs.
- The layout matches 27–32 on purpose (c090, c110), and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part.
- c287 Finding 1 answered the same principle for 117–121 and 131–134. This is the third tutti of the same kind, and the same answer holds.
