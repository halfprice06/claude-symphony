## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 23–25 and 135–137: Oboe 2 forms sevenths and ninths with Oboe 1 (applied)
**Problem.** In the hemiola crescendo, Ob 1 doubles the melody (E♭5–D5–C5–F5…). Ob 2 holds the syncopated G4–F4 chord line. The two oboes meet at E♭5/F4 (24 beat 1), F5/G4 (24 beat 4) and G5/F4 (25 beat 3). The same three clashes return at 136–137. The scan finds no other held discord between the oboes outside protected bars (12, 116, 131–134, 159).
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov's wind-harmony rule 4 (PDF 90): give concords to instruments of the same kind, "specially … for the oboe with its penetrating quality of tone". Rule 1 on the same page and PDF 91 ask for one timbre on the moving part and another on the stationary part. Ob 2 is the fourth doubling of the chord line, which is already in Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II. Cycle 114 applied the same rule to the oboes at 41–43.
**Fix.** Ob 2 rests and enters on the downbeat chord. Ob 1 gets the "1." label:
```
tOneOboeOne:  R1*8 | \partCombineSoloI es''4(\mf\< d''4 c''4) f''4( | … | \partCombineAutomatic f''2.->\ff r4 |
tOneOboeTwo:  R1*8 | R1*3 | d''2.->\ff r4 |
recOneOboeOne (135): \partCombineSoloI es''4(\mf\< … | \partCombineAutomatic f''2.->\f r4 |
recOneOboeTwo (135–138): R1*3 | d''2.->\f r4 |
```

### 2. Bars 63 and 67: diminished fifth between Horns 3 and 4 under the violins' tune (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 114 rewrote the horn pad so that it keeps thirds and sixths, but two places remain. At 63 (beats 3–4) and 67 the horns hold G3 under D♭4, a held diminished fifth at p/mp under the violins' tune.
**Evidence.** Rule 4 (PDF 90) asks for concords between instruments of one kind. Remark 4 (PDF 97) gives the intervals for two middle wind parts under a string melody as seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths. Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 89–92 keep the basis under the second theme in octaves and thirds (Hn I·II C♯4/C♯5, Hn III·IV A3/A4).
**Fix.** Hn 4 holds B♭3, which is a chord tone of both E♭7 (63 beats 1–2, 67) and E°7 (63 beats 3–4). The pair then moves in thirds, D♭/B♭ to C/A♭:
```
tTwoHnFour, 63–67: bes1\< | aes1\mp | aes1 | r1 | bes1 |
```

### 3. Bars 101–102: an empty eleventh above the bass in the ff cadence of the B-minor block (applied; protected block)
**Problem.** At the ff 6/4–V that ends the block, Cb and Cbsn hold F♯1, and Vc, Bsn 2, B.cl and B.tbn double it at F♯2. The next part up is at B3/A♯3, so nothing sounds between F♯2 and B3. Every other forte gap in the movement is under an octave. Tbn 2 rests here.
**Evidence.** PDF 85: "The bass should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it". PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages". The brass should be in "close order with no empty spaces" (PDF 100).
**Fix.** B.tbn moves up an octave, to the root inside the brass chord (F♯3 under Tbn 1/Hn C♯4–A♯3 and Hn 3·4 E4/B3):
```
devBassTbn: … \transpose c cis { bes,1\ff | bes,1 | es1 | c1 | f1 | f1 } |
```
**Answer to the protection.** Cycles 84 and 117 protect the scoring of 97–102 for its balance and its brass counter. This change does not touch the tune, the counter, the dynamics or any other part. It moves one of five bass doublings into the hole, so the five-fold F♯2 doubling becomes four-fold. No new parallels: B.tbn moves C♯3→F♯3 while the bass falls a fifth.

### 4. Woodwinds as an independent choir: note for movements II and III (no change in I)
In movement I the woodwinds double strings, carry solos (CA 5–8, Fl 73–76, B.cl 107–109, Ob 145–146) or hold doubled chord tones in tuttis. They never form the harmonic basis on their own, and the wind choir never alternates with the brass. Rimsky-Korsakov gives this a whole section (PDF 89–99) and 24 examples (Nos. 105–128, PDF 282–303). Examples: Ex. 112 (PDF 286–287), Sadko, three-part chords in 3 Fl / Ob / 3 Cl / Bsn over horns. Ex. 115 (PDF 289), wind and brass alternately. Ex. 120 (PDF 292–295), a wind basis under muted string tremolo, into which the brass enter p cresc. molto. Tchaikovsky does this at the end of 6/i: the brass chorale at 336–339 is answered by the whole woodwind choir in octave-doubled close chords at 340–342. In movement I the places for it are all protected and the movement works without it. For movement II, write at least one wind-choir basis under a string or solo melody, using rules 1–4 (PDF 90) and remark 4 (PDF 97). For movement III, write one brass–wind antiphony.

### 5. Comparison with v128
The changes in v129–v138 are: Fl 1 at 109; B♭♭ spelling; F♭ bass at 61; intro bar 6 revoiced; brass sf–mf and oboe octaves at 41–43; horn pad 57/65; Vln II metà; clefs and the percussion staff; bowings (cycles 116 and 118); cellos on the second-theme head at 103–106; graded dynamics at 117–122; mf at 60. None of them makes the music worse. Cycle 117's cellos at 103–106 go against cycle 74's plan of "winds and horns only" before the violas enter at 107. They do follow principle 12 (horn + cello, PDF 79) and principle 9 (Bsn + Vc, PDF 76), so I would keep them. The bar-6 revoicing doubles B♭ (Vla B♭3, CA B♭4). The CA moves on at once, so the doubling is harmless. Nothing from v128 needs restoring.

## Holistic verdict against Tchaikovsky 6/i

Measured by the reading, the orchestration follows the methods a conductor trained in this repertoire expects.
- **Climax.** 158–161 is scored like Rimsky's Ex. 87 (Kashchei, con tutta forza, PDF 261): the tune in woodwinds and strings in octaves, the brass sustaining, and a timpani roll.
- **B-minor block.** 97–102 uses the doubling order Rimsky recommends (row 10).
- **Second theme.** 56–63 uses Tchaikovsky's 6/i method: a horn and bassoon basis under the violins.
- **Final chord.** It is spaced as the harmonic series (row 18).

Before this cycle, the places that would make an attentive conductor stop were local. Two same-kind pairs clashed in the middle register (Findings 1 and 2), and one ff cadence chord had a hollow tenor register (Finding 3). All three are fixed in the scratch copy. The remaining difference from Tchaikovsky is the narrow role of the woodwind choir (Finding 4). That is a matter for the plan of movements II and III, not a fault in this movement.
