# Critic 535: engraving and notation (Gould, *Behind Bars*, ch. 1: ledger lines, octave signs, rests, PDF 41–52, fourth pass)

Version reviewed: v535. Scratch copy with fixes: /tmp/critic-535/work.

## Method

I read Gould PDF 41–52 in full and rendered all twelve pages at 110 dpi to read the examples. The examples are: PDF 41, ledger lines for adjacent and overlapping notes; 43, placing the line around slurs and tuplets; 44, system breaks and the end corner; 45, carrying the line across rests and the single-note sign; 46, clefs used before octave signs; 47, score versus part and the recommended/acceptable/incorrect octave changes; 48, *loco*; 49–52, rest design and placement.

For the bar-by-bar check I built a probe copy (/tmp/critic-535/probe). A Staff engraver tags every notehead and rest with its staff, voice (one/two/shared/solo), bar and beat. A wrapper on the stencil logs each grob's final staff position after line breaking. The log holds 6,209 grobs. From it I listed:
- every note with three or more ledger lines, by staff;
- every voiced rest outside the stave, with the pitches of both players in that bar;
- every voiced rest that sits three or more staff positions beyond its own part's outermost note;
- every pair of simultaneous rests of the two players that were not merged.

I looked at each flagged bar at 250 dpi (score pages 3, 6 and 16) and at pages 3 and 6 whole. I also ran the parts build with `PARTS_AUDIT=1`, which logs every note with four or more ledger lines in the 30 parts.

This reading was assigned in cycles 135, 245, 335 and 445, and their fixes still hold. The rests snap to whole stave-spaces (c245). The score has no string octave signs at 31, 71–72 or 121–123 (c335, c445). The rests set by c335 and c445 at Cl 2 112, Tbn 1 115, Hn 1 102, Fl 1 134 and Hn 4 62/126 are still level with their parts. The harp 8va at 56–59 is the score's only octave sign.

## Findings

### F1. Oboe 1 quaver rest at 160.3 floats two spaces above its own notes (applied)

**Bars:** 160, Ob 1·2 staff, beat 3.

**What is wrong.** Bar 160 is the third A♭ climax, fff. Ob 1 plays E♭5 C6 | rest C6 B♭5 A♭5. Ob 2 holds A♭5 tied from beat 2, so it has no notehead on beat 3. LilyPond put Ob 1's quaver rest at staff position 12, two spaces above the C6 on either side of it. The rest collides with nothing there. The clarinet staff directly below has the same rhythm and the same pitches in Cl 1 (E♭5 C6 | rest C6 B♭5 A♭5), and its rest sits at position 8, level with the C6. So on the same page two identical figures have their rests at different heights. This is the same LilyPond behaviour that c335 corrected at 112/115 and c445 at Fl 1 134 (there, too, the oboe rest below gave the comparison).

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 50: "A rest moves further from the centre of the stave or to outside the stave should parts otherwise collide." Nothing collides here. PDF 51: "The space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches." PDF 50 adds that upper-part rests go above the centre line, and position 8 satisfies that.

**Fix (applied).** sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoOboeOne:
`es''4\fff c'''4 \once \override Rest.staff-position = #8 r8 c'''8( bes''8 aes''8 |`
At 250 dpi the rest now sits level with both C6s and the Cl 1 rest below. It clears the Ob 2 tie and the slur that starts on the next C6. Rest overrides are removed in the parts, so the Oboe 1 part renders identical to before.

### F2. Horn 4 minim rest at 28 sits a space below its own part (applied)

**Bars:** 28, Hn 3·4 staff (treble clef 27–28), beats 3–4.

**What is wrong.** Hn 4 plays G3 (two ledger lines below) on beats 1–2, then rests. Its next note, A♭3 at 29, is also on two ledger lines. Hn 3 holds B3 (one ledger line) from beat 2. The minim rest sits on the fourth ledger line below the stave (position −12), so it is lower than any Hn 4 note around it. On the third line (−10) it is level with Hn 4's G3 and still a full space clear of Hn 3's B3. c445 F3 corrected the same fault for Hn 4 at 62 and 126. Bar 28 was listed there only under the minim-on-a-ledger-line rule, which it does follow; it was not checked for level.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 51: "Minim and semibreve rests are placed on a ledger line. The ledger line moves far enough away from the stave to be outside the other part." The other part here is Hn 3's B3 at −7, so −10 is already outside it. The same page: the rest's "distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches", and "When a note is on a ledger line, place the rests ... below the bottom stave-space."

**Fix (applied).** sections/tOne.ily, tOneHnFour:
`f2.->\ff r4 | g1\f | g2 \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-10 r2 | aes1 |`
At 250 dpi there is no contact with Hn 3's B3 or with the trumpet staff below. The Horn 4 part renders identical to before.

### Where the music could go further (within this lens)

I looked for places where ledger lines, octave signs or rests hide what the music does: an octave written in a way that obscures a leap or a doubling, or a rest that breaks a line on the page. Since c445 the violin climaxes at 31, 72, 122, 140 and 160 are all written at pitch, so a reader sees the A♭6 peaks and the octave drop at 123–124 directly. The harp sign at 56–59 keeps each arpeggio at one octave. No note in the score or the parts needs more than four ledger lines. I found no change of pitch, register or rhythm that this reading supports, so I propose none. F1 and F2 are visual only, but F1 is on the movement's climax bar, where a conductor's eye reads the woodwind staves together.
