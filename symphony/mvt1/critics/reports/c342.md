## Findings

I found nothing in v342 that the reading marks as wrong. The score is unchanged in my scratch copy. The items below are the places where the reading bears most directly, ranked by how close they come to a fault. Each says why it stays.

### F1. Bars 9–12: the rising-sixth chain over C–F–D. Checked, kept.

**What is there.** Vln I states the rising sixth three times: G4–E♭5 (9), A♭4–F5 (10–11), B4–A♭5 (11–12). The bass moves C–F–D, up a fourth and down a third. This is the pattern of Ex. 17-20 (PDF 279–280): the principal chords are i and ii, and iv is the voice-leading chord between them. The expected continuation is G. The composer breaks it off on A♭ (Ger6, 12) and reaches G at 13, which delays V by one bar.

**Against the reading.**
- PDF 283: rising sequences from I in minor are hard because of II°. Here II° appears once, at the break-off, with C held from 10 as a prepared seventh (c132). It is a iiø7 pre-dominant, which PDF 283 calls "good" as part of II–V–I.
- PDF 267: at 11.3 the leading tone sounds in Vln I (B4) and the violas (B3). A doubled leading tone is acceptable inside a sequence "as long as it does not appear before a goal tonic". The next chord is the Ger6, not a tonic. The violas take B3 to C4, and Vln I leaps to A♭5 as the motto requires.
- PDF 266: the sixths change size (minor, major, diminished seventh) to fit the key, as the reading asks of a diatonic sequence.

The bars are protected (notes 9–13; c250, c252). No change.

### F2. The S theme's harmonic frame at 47–54, 97–100 and 147–152 is Ex. 17-20. Checked, kept.

**What is there.** In all three statements the bass moves up a fourth and down a third with root-position triads:
- A♭–D♭–B♭–E♭–C (47–51), I–IV–ii–V–V6/5 of IV.
- B–E–C♯–F♯ (97–100), i–iv–iiø–V.
- C–F–D–G–E (147–151), I–IV–ii–V–V6/5 of IV.

The principal chords fall on alternate bars and outline a rising scale: A♭–B♭–C–D♭–E♭ at 47–54. This is exactly what PDF 279 describes: the first chord of each pair is principal, and the second is a voice-leading corrective. The B-minor statement replaces ii with iiø, which PDF 283 recommends in minor.

**Against the reading.**
- PDF 267 says the voices above a sequential bass are "normally" sequential. At 147–153 they are not: Vln II holds A5 for six bars (G5, A5, A5, A5, B♭5, A5) over a tune in the violas, cellos and Hn 1. The A5 is a common-tone pad. It is the ninth over G at 150 and the third of F at 152, and it leaves at B♭5 over the V6/5 of IV at 151. Because the sequence is carried in the tenor and the bass, the pad cannot form parallels with it. The all-pairs check shows no fifths or octaves here except doublings.
- The pad is protected (Vln II pad 147–154, c294; 147–155, c357), and the plain harmonisation of 47–55 is on the do-not-touch list.

No change.

### F3. Bars 85–96: the fugato as a descending-fifths chain. Checked, kept.

**What is there.** The entries are on D (85), G (88), C (91) and F (94). The local tonics fall by fifths and end on B♭ minor at 96. Entries 3 and 4 are harmonised chord for chord at the fourth:
- 91–92: F minor – B♭ minor 7 (iv7) – C7♭9 without its third.
- 94–95: B♭ minor – E♭ minor 7 (iv7, with a 4–3) – F7♭9 without its third.

The fourth entry's continuation (G♭–F at 96) is cut off by the ff B♭-minor restatement. That is the reading's "two plus the beginning of a third" (PDF 265), and the last three chords make the ii–V–i that PDF 268 names as the usual close.

**Against the reading.** The dominants at 92.3–4 (C–D♭–B♭–G) and 95.3–4 (F–G♭–E♭–D♭) have no third. PDF 267 regulates the doubling of the leading tone in a sequence, not its absence. These are linear dominants made by the subject and countersubject, and the leading tone A sounds as a passing note at 95.2 (Fl 1, Vln I A5). The fugato's notes, entries and colours are protected (c142, c237, c264). No change.

### F4. Downbeat fifths at 45→46, 69→70 and 139→140. Checked, kept.

**What is there.** My strong-beat scan found three places where the bass and an upper part form perfect fifths on two successive downbeats:
- 45→46: C2/G3 → D♭2/A♭3, cellos.
- 69→70: A♭1/E♭3 → B♭1/F3, Hn 4.
- 139→140: F1/C4 → D♭1/A♭3, Vc, Bsn 1 and Tbn 2; also F1/C5 → D♭1/A♭4 in Tpt 1.

**Against the reading.** PDF 267 says the voice-leading chord on the weak beat "prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". All three bars have one:
- At 45.3 the cellos move G3–A♭3 over C, a 5-6 in the sense of Ex. 17-10 (PDF 274). The A♭ becomes the fifth of D♭ only after it has been a sixth.
- At 69.3 the bass moves to C2, an I6.
- At 139.3 the bass moves to A♭1, an F-minor 6/3, the 5/3–6/3 alternation of Ex. 17-4 (PDF 269). The upper C holds while the bass moves, so it is a sixth over A♭ before it reaches the D♭ chord.

These are the procedures the reading teaches for exactly this risk. The scan found no other downbeat fifths or octaves outside doublings. No change.
