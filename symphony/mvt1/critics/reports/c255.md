## Findings

Ranked by how visible each is to a player or conductor. LilyPond already handles almost all of Gould ch. 2 correctly by default (I tested each case); what remains comes from the part-combiner, which decides stem layouts from the musical content and sometimes changes its mind inside a bar.

### 1. Hn 3·4 at 158 (K): one unison printed two ways in the same bar — applied
**What is wrong.** Hn 3 and Hn 4 play the same notes, G4 half then C5 half, both ff. The score prints beat 1 as a shared head with two stems and beat 3 as a single stem with 'a2'. The reader sees two layouts for one unison in one bar, at the fff climax's first bar, where the horn players are counting the tune entries.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 61: "The aim is to achieve a consistent stem direction within a phrase, bar or half-bar." PDF 65 allows either layout for unisons of equal value (shared head with two stems), but not both in one bar.
**Fix (applied).** recTwo.ily, `recTwoHnThree`: `\partCombineUnisono g'2\ff c''2 | \partCombineAutomatic`. The bar now reads 'a2' from beat 1 with one stem. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes; the parts are unaffected (one player per part).

### 2. Cl 1·2 at 43: equal crotchets on two stems — applied
**What is wrong.** At the p chord after the medial caesura, Cl 1 B♭4 and Cl 2 G4 are both crotchets followed by the same rests. The combiner leaves them in two voices (stem up and stem down a third apart), because bar 42's rhythms differ. Ob 1·2 and Fl directly above print the same kind of chord on one stem, so the woodwind block looks inconsistent.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 60: "Two or more notes of the same duration are combined onto one stem to form a chord"; only notes of different duration take separate stems.
**Fix (applied).** trans.ily, `transClOne`: `\partCombineChords bes'4\p r4 r2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1*3 |`.

### 3. Ob 1·2 at 42: two stems on beat 1, one stem on beat 3 — applied
**What is wrong.** Both oboes tie a whole note from 41 into a half at 42 (G5/G4), then play F5/F4 halves. Beat 1 is printed with two stems, beat 3 as one chord. The change is caused by the tie: the combiner cannot treat the tied continuation as a new chord.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 61 (consistent stem direction within a bar or half-bar); PDF 52/65 (double-stemmed writing is legitimate when used throughout).
**Fix (applied).** trans.ily, `transOboeOne`: start the oboes' bars 41–42 with `\partCombineApart` instead of `\partCombineAutomatic`, and return to `\partCombineAutomatic` at 43. Both halves of 42 now take two stems; 41 (stemless semibreves) and 43 look as before. I first tried switching state at beat 3 of 42 and at 42 itself; both broke the tie ("unterminated tie" warnings), so the state has to be set where the tie starts.
Related, not changed: Fl 1·2 41–43 keeps Fl 1's whole-bar rests printed above Fl 2's notes (partCombineApart from c179). Moving Fl 2 to a labelled solo at 42 also breaks Fl 2's tie from 41, and a centred bar rest above the staff is the placement Gould PDF 55 gives for a bar-length value, so it stays.

### 4. Bars 76 and 176: bar-length notes at the left of narrow bars — keep as is
In both bars every sounding part has a single semibreve (76: Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb, Hn 4, timpani roll; 176: Cl 1, chorale, strings, B.cl). Gould PDF 55 says such a note goes "just left of the centre of the bar" in traditional engraving. LilyPond places it at the left. I recommend leaving it: the bars are narrow (about 30 pt), so the visible difference is under 1 mm, and the fix would be a per-bar `NoteColumn.X-offset` on some 20 staves in each bar, which a later layout change would silently make wrong. Gould's own next sentence allows the note near the barline in wider bars, and the rule does not apply in the parts, where the other durations around these bars are rests.

### 5. Bar 134 Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2: overlapping semibreve under a tie — keep as is
Fl 1 (and Ob 1) tie F from 133 into 134 while Fl 2 (Ob 2) holds a semibreve G a tone above it. With the existing `force-hshift = #1.7` in recOne.ily, the semibreve sits to the left and Fl 1's F to its right, as Gould PDF 66 prescribes for overlapping parts ("place a down-stemmed part to the left"). Gould PDF 67 adds that overlapping semibreves should be split into stemmed notes to show part progression. I tested `g''2~ g''2` in Fl 2 with and without the shift: it adds a second tie that crosses the first and makes the bar harder to read, not easier. The present layout is the clearer one; keep it.

### 6. Rhythmic spacing — no change needed
I measured note-value widths on the systems with tempo changes (64–72, 73–84, 171–182). Equal values get equal space within each system, as Gould PDF 54 requires; the c145 spacing section on 73–76 does its job (eighths about 21–25 px at 150 dpi in 74 and 78), and adjacent systems carry similar bar counts. The partly empty lower half of page 3 (31–38, one system) is a consequence of the fixed breaks and the 17-page count; with eight bars it matches its neighbours, so it is not a spacing fault under this reading.
