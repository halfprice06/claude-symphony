# Critic 506: playability, instrument by instrument (v506)

Lens: playability, instrument by instrument. Assigned reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 2, Wind instruments (sebesky PDF 21–110), third pass. Critics 196, 306 and 396 covered this range before. Their changes were: B.cl part in treble clef (c196), Ob 2 resting from 42.3 (c306), and the stale Hn 4 bass clef at 11–12 removed (c396).

Method:
- I read the OCR text of every page from PDF 21 to 110.
- I rendered all 90 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one, in pairs or groups of four. I read the range charts from the images (Ex. 4, 23–24, 27–31, 35–36, 41, 44, 64, 66, 72, 74, 76, 87–94, 104–107, 125–128), not from the OCR.
- In /tmp/critic-506/scripts I wrote scripts over v506 full.midi that list:
  - each wind and brass part's range and where its extremes fall;
  - continuous playing spans over 11 s, timed with the MIDI tempo map;
  - notes shorter than a crotchet in the brass, to check slide positions and horn pickups;
  - flute–oboe/clarinet crossings;
  - the lowest horn against the overall bass.
- I then checked every long span against the source's slurs, `\breathe` marks and rests. MIDI does not shorten a note for a breath mark, so the span list over-reports.

## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. I made no edits in the scratch copy. What follows records what I tested and why each item stays.

### Checked and kept (no change)

1. **Wind spans over 11 s.** The MIDI lists 46 spans over 11 s. Every one is broken by one of these:
   - a slur break that gives a breath (Sebesky PDF 24): B.cl 1–4 after bar 2; trombone chorale at 176/177; Hn 1 S theme every two bars at 46–52 and 146–152;
   - a `\breathe` mark (PDF 30): Bsn 1 22 and 36; Bsn 1·2 at 10, 159 and 163; Hn 3·4 at 22 and 39; Hn 3 at 64 and 134; Hn 4 at 58; Hn 1·2 at 119; Tbn 1 at 179;
   - a rest: Ob 1 119.4 and 120.4; Cl 2 120.4;
   - a part-only licence: C.a. 5–9.

   The longest unbroken stretches left are Bsn 2 171–174 (pp, about 13 s, C3–F2–F2–G2, with a repeated F where the player can re-tongue) and Bsn 1 110–116 (mp cresc., repeated G and F). Both are well inside a bassoonist's normal breath at those dynamics. Nothing to change.

2. **Bass clarinet motto 1–4.** The tool reports 15.6 s, but the slur ends at 2.4 and a new slur starts at 3. The B.cl therefore plays the motto in two breaths of 6 s and 9.6 s, and the last C fades under the cellos (c328). This answers Sebesky PDF 64 and 24. Bars 1–4 are protected anyway.

3. **Fl 2 C5 under Ob 1 D♭5 at 132.1** (Sebesky PDF 79, Ex. 104: flute above oboe). Ex. 104 is about a soft mf third, where a low oboe covers the flute. At 132 both instruments are inside the ff hammered tutti. Fl 2's C5 is a held chord tone (pad), and Ob 1 is on the theme. Raising Fl 2 would put it on the theme's octave or an exposed seventh. Lowering it is impossible (below the flute's good register). It is inaudible as a separate colour at ff, and Sebesky's harshness argument concerns a soft exposed pair. Keep.

4. **Trombone slide positions** (PDF 28, Ex. 27). The fastest moves are quavers at 98–102 (F♯3→D4→C♯4: positions 5→1→2) and 131–134 (G3–D4–C♯4–C4–B3: 4–1–2–3–4). None is a 1/2↔6/7 jump in the low register, which is the only case Ex. 27 marks as awkward. Tbn 1·2 G2 at 80 is a single quaver after rests (protected, c466).

5. **Bassoon B♭1 at 92–96** (Ex. 91: bottom of the range). It is played f, in unison with the low-C bass clarinet. It is the written bottom, not beyond it, and Sebesky PDF 73 supports the B.cl carrying the low line, which it does. Keep.

6. **Horn pickups** (PDF 46, Ex. 51). Sebesky drops horn semiquaver pickups in a swing tutti because the horns cannot land them with the trumpets. Here the horn quavers are the P head's own legato line (r8 g8 es'4.), played a2 at 126. This is standard symphonic horn writing (Brahms 2/i and Tchaikovsky 5/i horn tutti figures). The trumpets do not double the figure at the same moment, so there is nothing for the horns to arrive late against. Keep.

7. **Parts and page turns.** No change was made, so I did not rebuild the parts. The v506 parts run lists two pages for every part except the harp (3) and double bass (4). Their inner turns fall in rests protected by c336 (harp 145–146) and c416/c436 (Cb 171–174). Every part that crosses into movement II still ends on an even page.

### Where the music could go further, within this lens, and why I did not change it

- **A high horn unison as a climax colour** (PDF 33: horns in unison "very effective and dramatic … f or ff in the high register"). The obvious place is the forte coda 167–170. A high four-horn unison is on the finale's reserve list (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). The pairs at 158–161 already give movement I its high unison colour. Rejected.
- **B.cl instead of bassoon for the soft bass under the coda duet 171–174** (PDF 73: B.cl is clearer for low woodwind notes). The B.cl is kept silent so its entry with the chorale at 175 is fresh (c-history: B.cl and C.a. silent 158–161 so 171 and 175 are fresh). Moving the bass at 171–174 into it would spend that colour four bars early. Rejected.
- **Clarinet with C.a. in unison** (PDF 63, 106: C.a. "best used in unison with a clarinet as a lead line over soft brass"). The C.a. solos at 5–9 and 171–174 are deliberately single colours, and both are protected. Rejected.

None of these would make the movement more playable. Each would cost something the score has kept on purpose, so "no change" stands.
