## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. They are ranked by how much they bear on the cyclic design.

### 1. The D♭ thread grows across the symphony as Unit 28 grows it, and movement I keeps the first, smallest stage (no change)

Unit 28 moves from local uses of ♭II to long-range ones. The local uses are a chord leading to V (§1–8, PDF 482–488) and a neighbour inside I (§15, PDF 495). The long-range uses are tonicizing ♭II and restating a theme on it (§11, PDF 491–493). The four movements follow that order:
- Movement I: ♭II is only ever a chord.
- Movement II: D♭ becomes a key area, as IV of A♭ (B section, 37–60).
- Movement III: F major, ♭II of E, gets a harp episode, and there is a Neapolitan 6/4 at 331.
- Movement IV: D♭ is tonicized ff from its own dominant (A♭7 → D♭, 205–218) and left through the German sixth to C major at 223.

This is the long-range use the unit describes, spread over a symphony.

Inside movement I the D♭ statements also form one dynamic arch:
- pp at 7 (N6 in the introduction);
- f and sf at 20 (P's peak D♭6);
- ff at 115 (root-position ♭II over the wedge bass) and 140 (Fl 1 A♭6 over D♭1);
- p at 173 (coda N6, two beats);
- pp at 178.3 and 180.3 (neighbour D♭s over the C pedal).

The movement opens and closes with the plagal neighbour form (Ex. 28-20) and saves the climactic form of Ex. 28-1 (Mozart's sudden forte) for P and the recapitulation. Nothing is spent that the later movements need. A&S PDF 492–493 names the Appassionata design (theme on I, then on ♭II, then V) as the most memorable long-range form, and the reserve list in coherence/mvt1.md ("D♭ as a key in C … a theme restated on ♭II") keeps it for the finale. c500 rejected it here for that reason, and I agree.

**Fix:** none.

### 2. The C-major recapitulation does not use ♭VI→♭II. This is the right device to withhold (no change)

The strongest master device in the unit that movement I could still use is Verdi's (Ex. 28-14, PDF 489–490): ♭VI acting as the dominant of ♭II at the end of a major movement, which gives "a peculiarly inward quality" to the close. Movement I's C-major recapitulation already reaches ♭VI fff at 160. A ♭II after it (for example D♭/F at 163–164, inside the dissolution, before iiø7–V7 at 164–165) would sound like the Kyrie. I considered it for 163–164, where the dissolution is soft and the texture is thin, because it would make the arrival of C minor at 167 darker and more memorable.

I reject it for three reasons:
- coherence/mvt1.md lists "♭VI moving to ♭II in C" under Do not reuse. The finale's 205–218 is built on it, A♭ to D♭.
- 158–166 is protected as a cadence chain (c430, c442, c443). Its 164 bass D–G carries the delayed 4–3 of 164–165.
- PDF 488 says ♭II in major is "more difficult to use convincingly". The finale has to make it convincing as its one new harmonic event. If movement I heard it first, the finale's 211 would be a repeat.

Withholding it is what keeps the finale's arrival strong, so the omission serves the four-movement arc.

**Fix:** none.

### 3. The attacca join works as A&S §14's pivot spread over two movements (no change)

A&S PDF 494 calls this "probably the most frequent use of ♭II as pivot": the N6 of a minor key becomes IV6 of the major key a third lower. C minor to A♭ major is exactly that relation. Movement I's last ♭2̂ is Vln I's D♭5 over the C pedal at 180.3, resolving to C5 at 181 (a neighbour, PDF 495). Movement II takes the same C5 in Vln I across the tie (II 1–3). At II 87 it states D♭/F as IV6 of A♭, with Cl 1 D♭5→C5 at the same pitch (mvt2.md). So the pivot chord of Ex. 28-19 is heard in its minor meaning at the end of movement I and in its major meaning at the end of movement II. This is a cross-movement link a listener can follow, and it costs movement I nothing. Changing 180.3 (for example removing the D♭ or moving it to another voice) would break it. The coda notes are protected anyway (c400, c410).

**Fix:** none.

### 4. New scan: every ♭2̂ in the C-minor sections moves down or stays inside the ♭II chord (no change)

PDF 484 and Points 3 (PDF 499) say ♭2̂ tends to move down. The scan of all 30 parts in 1–32, 131–146 and 167–182 found 17 places where a D♭/C♯ is followed by a higher note. All of them are covered by the reading:
- 20.1 (Vc, Bsn 1, Vln I, Vln II), 140.1 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, tuba, Vln I, Vln II, Cb 140.4.5) and Vc 173.3 rise from D♭ to E♭, to F or to the octave. These moves stay inside the ♭II chord: the bass arpeggiates root to sixth (D♭ to F), and the tonic-scale E♭ is used in the figuration (§12, PDF 493). The structural ♭2̂ in each of these bars still falls to C or B in the next bar.
- Vla 142.4 C♯4→D4 is a chromatic lower neighbour to D over V. It is spelled as a minor second, as Ex. 28-22 requires (PDF 497), and it is not ♭2̂.

So the voice leading is right in every part, not only in the outer voices that c500 and c572 checked.

**Fix:** none.

### 5. 174.3 (♮2̂ on top of V after the coda N6): c572's open item, kept for a cyclic reason as well

c572 left open whether the C.a. should rise C5–D5 at 174.3 to put ♮2̂ on top of V (Ex. 28-6, PDF 485). It kept the C.a. line because the line is the motto at the fifth and ends on C (theme record mvtOneMottoCodaCA). I add a cyclic reason:
- Every later movement's version of the motto tail ends on its tonic, without ♮2̂ after ♭2̂: II 85–89 E♭–D–D♭–C, III 357–360 B–A♯–A–G♯, and IV 275–276 E–D–D♭–C.
- The finale's ♭II thread also keeps ♭2̂→♮2̂ out of its top line (finale.md).

A D5 at 174.3 would give movement I a form of the tail that no later movement answers. The ♮2̂ is already present at 174.3 in the violas (D4), the inner-voice placement PDF 484 calls normal.

**Fix:** none. The open item is closed.
