## Findings

### F1. The percussion part announces each beater change only at the entry. Applied (part only).

Bars 117–121, 156–158 and 171–174 (`devPerc`, `recTwoPerc`, `codaPerc`). The single percussionist changes beaters three times: heavy tam-tam beater for 122, soft sticks on the suspended cymbal for 159, and a soft tam-tam stick for 175. Each time the part prints the instruction only over the note itself ("Tam-tam *heavy beater*" over the fff at 122). Before that come 45, 31 and 10 bars of multi-bar rests with nothing in them. So the player learns what to hold only when the entry arrives.

Evidence: in Adler's Ex. XII-22 (PDF 70) the suspended-cymbal line writes "take brushes" and "take bow" in the rests before the passage that needs them, not at the passage. PDF 59 frames the whole question of percussion playing as having "enough time" to change, and the reading of the time comes from the part. Ex. XII-2's footnote (PDF 61) makes the same point for mallet changes. The timpani part already follows the rule: "muta" is printed at the start of each retuning rest (34, 44, 84, 98), and "soft sticks" sits four bars before 179.

Fix (part only, through `\tag #'part`; the score's percussion staff is unchanged and score.ly now passes `\removeWithTag #'part \perc`):
```
devPerc   = \drummode { R1*40 | \tag #'part { R1*4^\markup \small \italic "take heavy beater" } \tag #'score { R1*4 } | R1 | tt1\fff~ ...
recTwoPerc = \drummode { R1*9 | \tag #'part { R1*2^\markup \small \italic "take soft sticks" } \tag #'score { R1*2 } | R1 | cymr1:32\pp\< ...
codaPerc  = \drummode { R1*3 | r2. r4\fermata | \tag #'part { R1*4^\markup \small \italic "take soft stick" } \tag #'score { R1*4 } | tt1\pp ...
```
The texts fall on the multi-bar rests that the rehearsal marks already split off (G 117–120, 156–157 under "poco animando", M 171–174), so the part keeps its rest layout and stays at one page. The entry texts stay as they are, so that each entry still names its instrument (PDF 59).

### F2. Timpani G2 on beat 3 of 28 under vii°6/5 over D. Kept; no change.

Since v303 (Tpt 2 F4 on beat 3), beat 3 of 28 sounds D–F–A♭–B with the bass on D, and the timpani G2 is not among those pitch classes. The same is true of the stop chords at 26, 138 and 161. Adler PDF 58 rejects the old practice of playing a chord over a timpani note "a second away" from the right one because the drum could not be retuned. That is not what happens here. G is the root of the dominant whose ninth chord the vii°7 is, and the timpani hold the dominant against it on purpose, as at 26 (accepted by c166 and c276). Changing the stroke would also touch the hammered tutti 27–32, which the finale quotes at pitch in every part, and the timpani rhythm there is protected. It should stay as it is.

### F3. The fff suspended-cymbal stroke at 160 is played with soft sticks. Kept; no change.

Adler PDF 70 gives the wooden stick "a well-articulated stroke" and yarn or timpani mallets "softer effects". Read literally, an fff single stroke with soft sticks goes against this. In practice, though, the stroke at 160 is the end of the soft-stick roll that has swelled from pp through 159, and the roll is what brings the cymbal up to fff. A wooden-stick stroke would add a hard attack close to a crash, and the symphony keeps that sound for the finale's crash at IV 223 (c250; coherence "Do not reuse"). Changing sticks between the roll and the stroke would also leave no time for the change. The notation is clear (roll to an accented tied note, "damp gradually", "damp" at 164). It should stay as it is.
