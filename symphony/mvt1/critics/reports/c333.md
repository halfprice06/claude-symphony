### 1. The development has no expanded key area before V. This is the Waldstein's plan, not the Ninth's or the Appassionata's, and it is right for this movement. Keep.

**What the score does.** The development, 77–130 (105 s), passes through about ten stations:

| Bars | Station | Bars long | Seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 77–83 | major-third cycle A♭m – E – Cm – A♭ → V | 7 | 13.4 |
| 85–96 | fugato on D, G, C, F, ending in B♭ minor | 3 per entry | — |
| 97–102 | B minor (S at ff) | 6 | 11.5 |
| 103–106 | D minor → V of E | 4 | — |
| 107–110 | E minor → B♭ minor → V of F | 4 | — |
| 111 | F minor | 1 | — |
| 112–116 | wedge to V | 5 | — |
| 117–130 | V pedal | 14 | 29.2 |

Only V is expanded. At 111, F minor lasts one bar: 112 is V6/5 of F, 113 is already C minor 6 over E♭ (Cb E♭2, Vln I C6), 115 is the Neapolitan, 116 the augmented sixth (full.midi).

**The three models in the reading.**
- The Appassionata. Its major-third cycle leads to a first main goal, D♭, which is held for bars 91–108 (Ex. 32-19, PDF 600). The book says that in such passages "each intermediate chord is expanded" (PDF 599).
- The Ninth. A&S cite it for large-scale I–VI–IV in minor (PDF 595). In the MCTL harmony annotation of Beethoven 9/i, the development opens with IV (G minor) for bars 160–201, 41.5 bars or about 57 s at ♩ = 88. Later it has A minor for bars 243–281 (38 bars, about 52 s).
- Tchaikovsky 6/i. MCTL labels its development's first area, D minor, from crotchet 638 to 818: 180 crotchets, close to this score's whole 216-crotchet development. MCTL's key labels are coarse and treat the fugato as one area, so I take this only as a sign that his development also opens with one long area.

**Why the score's plan still holds.** The reading gives the plan the score uses, and three facts support it:
- (a) A&S PDF 594: III♯ "moves on to V, usually through a passing IV", and the Waldstein's development reaches V "after a long transition". The score takes this route: VI at 82, a passing iv at 111, and V as the only long area, 28 % of the development's time.
- (b) Ex. 32-8 (K. 421, PDF 589) gives a second model: many stations inside one simple motion (III→V there, VI→V here), where the point is "a vast expanse of musical space" rather than one long goal. Each of the score's stations is marked by a new combination of material (cycle, fugato, ff S, combined heads, wedge), so the plan does not read as wandering.
- (c) A plateau of the size of the Ninth's G minor would add about 30–50 s to a 105 s development. The development would go from 0.85 to about 1.2 of the exposition's time. That breaks the protected section lengths (c215, c243, c329) and renumbers every later bar the other movements cite.

**Two corrections to the record of this plan:**
- c330's reading-notes row 11 says the plan is "the I–VI–IV plan A&S cite for the Ninth Symphony". The F minor at 111 is a passing IV, the Waldstein's kind, not the Ninth's expanded IV. The difference matters if a later critic proposes making 111 a goal. It should stay passing, because the wedge (protected c319, c341) depends on the bass leaving F at once.
- mvt1.md's key-plan row for 111–116 ("F minor at 111 ... into the dominant pedal") is accurate and needs no change.

Fix: none. Not applied (nothing to apply).

### 2. 73–80 carries out the Appassionata's motivic plan exactly: E♭–F♭–E♭, then E♭–E♮–E♭, in Vln II at one pitch. Keep; add it to the record.

A&S PDF 600–601 describe the Appassionata in two steps:
- The turn to A♭ minor at the end of the exposition "permits a transposition of the basic motive [5–♭6–5] to E♭–F♭–E♭".
- The major-third motion at the start of the development "contains the transposed motive enharmonically reinterpreted (E♭–E♮–E♭)".

The score does both, in one voice at one pitch (v333/sections/clos.ily, closVlnTwo, and dev.ily, devVlnTwo; confirmed in full.midi):
- **73–77.** Vln II plays E♭4 (73.3–74) – G♭4 (75, inside the A♭7 spelled with G♭) – F♭4 (76, the mixture iv6/4 D♭–F♭–A♭ over the A♭ pedal) – E♭4 (77). This is the movement's ♭6–5 motive, the P head's A♭–G and the three A♭→G climaxes, transposed to A♭ minor. Fl 1 leads into it with its last eighth F♭5 at 75 (c182).
- **77–80.** Vln II holds E♭4, takes E♮4 on beat 3 of 78 under the E-major chord of the A♭–E–C cycle, and returns to E♭4 at 80 under C minor. This is the same motive respelled inside the major-third cycle, as in Ex. 32-19.

c243 recorded the Appassionata key plan (73–83), and c330 recorded the E♭/E♮ thread at 77–80. Neither recorded that the F♭ at 76 completes the reading's first step. That F♭ is what makes 73–80 the motive enlarged and not only a key plan: without it, the E♭–E♮–E♭ would have no ♭6–5 model in the same voice, and the remote E at 78 would lack the "organic connection" A&S ask for (PDF 602).

The same F♭→E♭ at the same pitch class is movement II's thread (mvt1.md seeds: II 5, 21, 78, 88; I 60–62 bass G♭–F♭–E♭). So 76→77 is a further anticipation of movement II.

Fix: no score change. Record line for mvt1.md, under Seeds:

> 73–80: Vln II E♭4–G♭4–F♭4–E♭4 (73–77), then E♭4–E♮4–E♭4 (77–80), all at one pitch: the ♭6–5 motive transposed to A♭ minor and then respelled in the A♭–E–C cycle (the Appassionata's plan, A&S PDF 600–601). The F♭4 at 76 is movement II's F♭→E♭.

Not applied: records are outside my write area. Protect the Vln II notes 73–80 and Fl 1's F♭5 at 75.

### 3. The 96→97 confrontation is tied the way the reading requires. Verification; keep.

A&S PDF 596–598 allow a remote area to be entered abruptly if a top-voice or enharmonic link binds it. At 96 the top line (Fl 1, Vln I) falls C♯6–C6–C♭6–B♭5 in eighths: the motto tail's chromatic descent. At 96.4.5–97.1.5, Hn 1 plays C4→B3, which is the end of the horn call (13–14) in the same player at the same pitch.

So B minor enters on the horn call's unresolved B, prepared since bar 14, as Haydn's E♭ is prepared by D♯–E (Ex. 32-17b). The join is protected (c309, c323). c243 and c330 mention B♮ in general terms; this pins it to Hn 1 at pitch.

Fix: none.
