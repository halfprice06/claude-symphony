## Findings

No finding below calls for a note change. I tested none in the scratch copy beyond a clean build of v427 itself (0 warnings, 18 pages, only the known bar-96 entry in analysis.txt).

### 1. Fugato 85–96: meets each of Adler's clarity conditions (keep)

The score: subject D (Vc alone, 85), G (Vla + Bsn 1, 88), C (Vln II + C.a., 91), F (Vln I + Fl 1, 94). The countersubject opens with a dotted crotchet and quavers (88–89 Vc: C4. D4 E♭4 F4 G4 | E♭4 half, F–E♭–D–E♭ quavers). The subject opens with a quaver pickup and a dotted crotchet with an accent. The bass (Cb, B.cl, Bsn 2 from 90–91; Vc from 94) moves in minims and semibreves.

Against the reading:
- Item 1 of PDF 144: each subject entry sits in a good register for its instrument. The cello's D3–B♭3 is its singing middle, the violas' G3–E♭4 the C/G strings with Bsn 1 an octave lower, and Vln I's F5–D♭6 the E string.
- Item 4 of PDF 144 and PDF 145 ("the subject comes through because of the rhythmic differences between the foreground (subject), the middleground (countersubject), and the background (continuo)"): the three layers differ in rhythm in every bar from 91 to 95. The subject has its quaver pickup, the countersubject a dotted crotchet then a half note, the bass minims.
- PDF 144 on the order of entries: D–G–C–F runs bottom to top, which is Vivaldi's "traditional sequence" (Ex. V-21).
- PDF 145: the subject is heard alone at 85 (Cb rest since c227), and its anacrusis (r8 d8) gives it the carrying power Adler describes.
- Item 3 of PDF 144 (register separation) is met from 91 onward. At 88–90 the countersubject lies above the subject, but c227 kept that for the B♮→C resolution, and it is still right.

The one departure from the letter of PDF 143, where the strings play the whole exposition before the winds join, is the wind doubling from the second entry. That doubling is protected (c142 colours, c307), and Adler calls the strings-first exposition one effective device, not a rule. Keep 85–96 as it is.

### 2. Remaining S pickups against "leave the anacrusis undisturbed" (keep)

Adler PDF 135: the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the [anacrusis] figure undisturbed." Earlier cycles applied this to the P pickups. I checked every S pickup:
- 46.4 (Hn 1 + Vc E♭3): Cb E♭2 sounds from 46.3, an octave below, not at pitch. Vln II's G4 starts with the pickup on purpose (c223). Clear.
- 55.4 (violins E♭4): Hn 1, Vla and Vc release on beat 4 (c159). Only the harp's E♭4 from 55.1.5 still rings, and the harp is protected. Clear in the strings.
- 146.4 (Vla, Vc, Hn 1 G): the Cb G1, timpani G2 roll, Vln I G4 and Vln II F4 all stop by beat 3. Hn 3's B3 is a different pitch. Clear.
- 155.4 (Vln I F5): nothing sounds at F5 or F4 except Vln II, which joins the pickup itself. Clear.
- 97.4 (F♯4 in Vln I and Ob 1, F♯5 in Fl 1, F♯3 in Vc, ff): the violas' B-minor arpeggio strikes F♯4 on 97.3.5 and 97.4.5, on each side of the pickup. F♯ is a tone of the chord the arpeggio spells, the pickup is in three octaves at ff, and the arpeggio is protected (c017, c227). Keep.

So the anacrusis principle now holds at every P and S entry in the movement.

### 3. 147–155: Vln II tremolo above the tune (keep, with the reason stated)

The score: violas, cellos and Hn 1 carry S at E3/E4 to F5. Vln II div., sul tasto, pp tremolo, sits above it at E5–A5, with C6 at 152. Vln I enters at 155.4 and takes the tune to D6–E6 at 156–158.

Against the reading: in his Brahms analysis Adler says that violins and violas scored an octave higher "would have attracted too much attention to the background" and "would also have anticipated" the violins' higher restatement (PDF 135). That reads directly onto a violin background above a viola/cello tune, followed by a violin restatement. But PDF 161 (Ex. V-37, Bizet, Prelude to *Carmen*) shows the other accepted pattern. The cellos carry the tune and the upper strings play tremolo chords above it, and the tremolo is what makes it work. Here the tremolo is sul tasto and pp, so the violins lose the bright attacked tone that Adler's warning is about. The restatement at 156 also changes more than register: the violins go from tremolo to legato bowing and from pp to mf. The halo at 152 and 154 was set by c257 and c384, and "147–155 pad" is protected (c387). I find no case strong enough to reopen it. Keep.

### 4. Codetta 69–71: viola eighths touch the P head's register (keep)

The score: the S head is on top (Fl, Ob 1, Tpt 1, Vln I). The P head is in the middle (Cl 1·2, C.a., Hn 1·2, Vln II, E♭4–F5). The violas play quaver arpeggios. At 70 the violas run F4–D♭4–B♭3–D♭4–F4–A♭4–C♭5–A♭4 while the P head plays A♭4–B♭4–C5–D♭5 quavers and then a dotted F5.

Against the reading (PDF 144, items 3 and 4): on beats 1–2 of 70 the background and middleground both move in quavers, and on beats 3–4 the violas reach A♭4–C♭5 just under the head's held F5. This is the only place in the movement where a background figure shares both rhythm and register with a counter-line. It lasts two beats. The P head has five instruments against one viola section, and the viola's C♭5 is the seed movement II quotes (coherence/mvt1.md, bars 70 and 73). Bars 69–72 are protected (c147, c187, c337, c347). Keep.

### 5. 131–134: low P under the ff violin counter (keep, protected)

Adler's reading of Tchaikovsky's Serenade (PDF 154) says a bass statement needs a thin texture with "all violins and violas in a nonbrilliant register." At 131–134 the theme is low (Vc, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, G3–F4, f/ff), and violins and flutes play the augmented counter at E♭6 down to D5, ff. The counter is middleground, not background, so Adler's rule about background register applies only in part. The layout of 131–134 is protected as a deliberate rhyme with 27–32 and 167–170 (c089, c097, c264). Keep.

### 6. Accompaniment vocabulary (follows)

PDF 158–162 lists the standard string accompaniments. The movement uses five of the six, each in one section:
- on/off-beat pulse: Cb, 15–22
- sustained notes with agitated tremolo: 77–81, 111–116
- sustained notes with arpeggios: 56–68, 158–166
- upper-string tremolo under a low tune: 147–155
- lower strings alone for a sombre background: 171–179

The one it does not use is a characteristic dance rhythm in the cellos (Ex. V-36, Habanera), and that pattern does not suit this movement. The movement also has pizzicato bass under winds (103–106), from the chapter's list of pizzicato uses (PDF 167). No pattern is used in two sections in a row. Nothing to change.
