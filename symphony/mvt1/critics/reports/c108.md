## What I studied

I studied the text and the cited examples above. To check the score I ran LilyPond's event-listener on a scratch copy of v127, one staff per player, so that I could list every dynamic, hairpin and re-entry by bar and beat. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 111–112: trumpets enter the wedge with the strings (applied)
**Problem.** The wedge is the movement's longest orchestral crescendo, from mp/p cresc. at 111 to ff at 117 and fff at 122. In v127 Tpt 1–3 and tuba enter at 111 together with all the strings and woodwinds. Only the trombones (113), bass trombone (114) and Fl/Ob (115) are added later. Trumpet 1 doubles Vln I an octave lower, and Tpt 2–3 double horn chord tones, so their entry at 111 adds no notes and spends the brightest brass colour at the start.
**Evidence.** PDF 130: "Prolonged orchestral crescendi are obtained by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass." PDF 131: in a diverging progression "the intermediate intervals are filled up by the introduction of fresh parts as the distance widens". The wedge is exactly that kind of progression (the top rises while the bass falls F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C).
**Fix (dev.ily).** Tpt 1: `R1*2 | c''2.~(\mp\cresc c''8 b'8) | c''4.( b'8 f'4 aes'8 c''8) |`. Tpt 2: `R1*2 | g'1~(\mp\cresc | g'4 f'2.) |`. Tpt 3: `R1*2 | es'1\mp\cresc | d'1 |`. The trumpets now enter with the trombones at 113. The tuba stays at 111 because it doubles the bass line. The top line, the bass and the entry level of 111 (cycle 73) are unchanged, so this sharpens the graded crescendo that cycles 70–73 protected.

### 2. Bars 41–42: the held tutti chord covers the four-horn call (applied)
**Problem.** At 41 the woodwinds and strings arrive ff and hold the chord ff for six beats. The horns' call (E♭3–C4–B♭3, from beat 4) sits in the same register as the viola tremolo, the cello G3 and Cl 2's B♭3, all at ff. The call was strengthened in cycles 84 and 98 (four horns, other brass at f), but the strings and woodwinds still compete with it.
**Evidence.** PDF 129: "At the moment when the wood-wind begins a piano chord, the strings attack it sforzando … The first method is also employed for a sf-dim." In Ex. 253 (PDF 418) the strings strike an sf chord and the winds carry on underneath. The score uses sf only once (Vln I, bar 20) and fp only once (Cb, bar 33), so it never uses this device where it is most needed.
**Fix (trans.ily).** Strings, flutes, oboes, clarinets, Bsn 2 and Cbn: `\sf\>` on the 41 chord, `\mf` at 42, then the existing `\>` to p/pp at 43. Example: Vln I `g'''1\sf\>~ | g'''2\mf f'''2\> |`; Vla `<es' g'>1:32\sf\> | <es' g'>1:32\mf\> |`. Timpani: `\ff\>` at 41 and `\mf` at 42. Shared staves (Fl, Ob, Cl) carry identical marks. The c098 brass grading (f under the horns' ff) is kept.

### 3. Bar 43: the brass replay the closing p chord instead of dropping out first (applied)
**Problem.** After the diminuendo in 42, Tpt 1–3, Tbn 1–2, B.tbn and tuba all re-attack a p quarter at 43 together with the woodwinds and horns. As a result all the groups fade together; none drops out early, as Rimsky-Korsakov prescribes. The heavy brass also has to make an exact p attack straight after ff/f.
**Evidence.** PDF 130: "Diminuendo effects are accomplished by the elimination of the instruments in the reverse order (brass, wood-wind, strings)." The score already does this well at 162–163 (trumpets and trombones out, then horns and clarinet, then strings), so 43 is inconsistent with the movement's own best passage.
**Fix (trans.ily).** In those seven brass parts, replace the 43 quarter with `R1\!` and end the 42 hairpin at the barline. The woodwinds and horns keep the p quarter, and the strings keep their pp sustain. The chord at 43 stays complete (E♭–G–B♭ in the winds, D♭ in Vln II).

### 4. Bar 138: the recapitulation hemiola crescendo has no goal, and the trumpets enter mf (applied)
**Problem.** At 135 every part starts mf/mp with a crescendo, and no dynamic follows until 139. The stop chord at 138 therefore has no marked arrival. The only marks at 138 are mf for trumpets, Hn 3·4 and timpani: the trumpets re-enter after 4 bars' rest at a level below the crescendo, and Hn 3 is told to drop back to mf in the middle of its crescendo. In the exposition the same stop (26) is marked ff in every part.
**Evidence.** PDF 130: brass "glorifying crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". PDF 136: brass re-entries at mf/mp produce "a colourless and common-place effect".
**Fix (recOne.ily).** Add `\f` to the 138 chord in every part, which ends the 135 hairpins, and change the trumpets, Hn 3·4 and timpani from `\mf` to `\f`, e.g. Tpt 1 `R1*3 | b'2.->\f r4 |`. I chose f rather than ff so that ff stays reserved for 140 (the A♭6 peak), and 139 keeps its f to ff.

### 5. Bar 156: horns and bassoons start the K crescendo louder than the strings and the melody (applied)
**Problem.** At 156 Hn 1–4 and Bsn 1–2 enter mf with a crescendo. Hn 2–4 have rested 10–13 bars. The violins carry the tune at mf (from 155 beat 4), and violas, cellos, basses and Cl 1 are at mp. The horns are a chord basis here, but they start at the melody's level and above the strings that should lead the crescendo into K.
**Evidence.** PDF 133: the wind or brass harmonic basis "should be of inferior dynamic power". PDF 130: a crescendo is built strings first, brass last. PDF 136: mf brass re-entries are "colourless".
**Fix (recTwo.ily).** Hn 1–4 and Bsn 1–2 at 156 now read `\mp\<` instead of `\mf\<`, e.g. Hn 3 `R1*9 | bes1\mp\< |`. The pitches (including Hn 2 D–D♭, cycle 81) and the ff at 158 are unchanged.

### Considered and not changed
- Bars 12 and 69: the trombones enter mf after a long rest, which PDF 136 dislikes. But both entries sit inside a tutti f as a harmonic basis, where PDF 133 asks the basis to be weaker than the melody, and in 69 they reach f at the 72 cadence. The two principles conflict here, and the existing balance is defensible, so I left both.
- Bar 126: the tuba re-attacks G1 pp after the brass have gone. A pp re-entry is what PDF 136 asks for, and the note is the bass of the cadence into the 127 retransition (the breath is from cycle 86), so I kept it.
