## Findings

### 1. Bars 65–66: the apex of the exposition's second theme has no crescendo (applied)

**What is wrong.** The second theme reaches its highest point in the exposition at 66.1: Vln I B♭5 (Vln II B♭4), under the "rit." This comes after the f at 64 and the climb D♭–F–A♭ at 65.3–4. The phrase member that ends this climb (quaver pickup rising into a long note, then a fall) appears seven other times in the movement:
- 49–50
- 58–59
- 99–100
- 105–106
- 149–150
- 173–174
- and here at 65–66.

Each of the other six marks the rising quavers with `\<` and the arrival with `\>`. At 65–66 the violins have no hairpin until beat 3 of 66, so the apex note sits at a flat f between the arrival of 64 and the diminuendo. The tune does not grow into its highest note, at the one point where the conductor most needs a mark.

**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 269 (PDF 205, discussed on PDF 207): the lead's long notes are "modified by the dynamics". The violins crescendo into the arrival and fall away after it. Ex. 280 (PDF 212) does the same with the violins' high note. Ex. 2A (PDF 18) gives each phrase of the lead its own swell. Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215) asks that the same figure carry the same marks. The focus principle (PDF 13) is already satisfied in the background: Cl 1/Hn 2 start their diminuendo on 66.1 (`des'4\>`), so with the fix the counterline yields while the tune rises.

**Fix (tTwo.ily), Vln I and Vln II.**
- Vln I, 65–66: `f''4.( es''8 des''4\< f''8 aes''8) | bes''2\!( aes''2~\> |`
- Vln II, 65–66: `f'4.( es'8 des'4\< f'8 aes'8) | bes'2\!( aes'2~\> |`

The diminuendo still starts on beat 3 of 66, and the p at 68 and the rit. are unchanged. Fl 1 already fades at 65 and rests from 66, so no other part doubles the climb. This adds marks only; no notes change.

### 2. Bars 147–152: the cor anglais fills inside the recapitulated second theme have no character or shape (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 147–152 the cor anglais plays three short fills (G–A–B → C, A–B–C → D, B♭–A–G → A) in the gaps of the C-major S tune (Hn 1 mp, violas and cellos mf, "dolce", with a swell on each bar). Critic 377 counted these fills among the passages at a master's level for texture. On the page, though, they are a bare p with slurs: no word, no hairpin. Every other line of this passage has a shape, but the fills have none, so a player gets no indication of what they should be.

**Evidence.** This is the situation of Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 17–18). The flutes' echo inside the cor anglais's held note is marked "(SWEETLY)" with its own small swell, mp < mf > mp, one level under the solo. Ex. 280 (PDF 212) gives the background line its own hairpins. PDF 207 says a background should project "enough character to carry one bar into the next" without drawing attention from the lead. A small swell at p does that, and the level stays below the mf tune.

**Fix (recTwo.ily), `recTwoCorAnglais`, 147–152.**
```
r2 r8 g'8(\p\<_\markup \italic "dolce" a'8 b'8 | c''2\>) r2\! | r2 r8 a'8(\< b'8 c''8 | d''2\>) r2\! |
r2 r8 bes'8(\< a'8 g'8 | a'2\>) r2\! |
```
Each swell peaks on the half note at the start of 148, 150 and 152. At those points the tune is at the end of a swell (148, `c'4\!`) or starting a diminuendo after its arrival (150 `c''2\>`, 152 `c''4.(\>` in the violas), so the fill moves while the tune relaxes, as in Ex. 2A. The held C5–G4–F4–E4 at 153–155 stays unmarked under Cl 1's mp line. No notes change.
