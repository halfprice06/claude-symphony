## Findings

### 1. Bars 59–62: the approach to the S restatement's cadential 6/4 re-strikes the dominant before it arrives (applied)

**Bars and problem.** In the violins' restatement of S (56–63), the bass under 59–62 is E♭ (V7, 59) – G♭ (60, beats 1–2) – E♭ (60, beats 3–4) – F♭ (61, iv6 with mixture) – E♭ (62, cadential 6/4, sub. p). The dominant bass E♭ sounds at 59, again at 60.3 and again at 62–63. The bass approaches the cadential 6/4 at 62 by the neighbour F♭–E♭ from an E♭ two beats earlier. It does not descend to it. The arrival at 62 is the hinge of the S group's tension curve: sub. p, then the climb to D♭6 at 63 and the forte at 64. Because the dominant root was sounded half a bar earlier, 62 does not arrive. The tension curve sags for half a bar just where it should tighten.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 297–298 (Ex. 18-12, Handel): "A passing minor dominant in 6/3 position can lead from I to the IV6 of a Phrygian cadence, thus producing a completely stepwise bass motion through the descending 4th." Bars 60–62 already contain both chords of that pattern: the minor dominant (E♭ minor: harp RH B♭–E♭–G♭, cellos E♭–B♭–E♭–G♭ on 60.3–4) and the Phrygian iv6 (F♭–A♭–D♭ at 61). The minor dominant, however, stands in root position. In 6/3 position over G♭ the bass becomes G♭–F♭–E♭ (♭7̂–♭6̂–5̂), stepwise into the cadential 6/4, as the reading describes. PDF 290–291: within such a passage the middle chords should lose their identity and only the goal should be emphasised. A root-position minor v on 60.3 gives the dominant a separate weight of its own. Corpus support: Tchaikovsky 6/i uses the minor v6 as a passing chord in a descending bass (MCTL harmony.csv, `Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1/form/harmony.csv`, onsets 84 and 167.5, bars 22 and 42: v6 between V7/V or i and the next chord), never as a root-position re-strike before the dominant.

- Bsn 2 60: `ges2\p\<^\markup \bold "2." es2` → `ges1\p\<^\markup \bold "2."`
- Vc 60, second half: `es,8( bes, es ges)` → `ges,8( bes, ges es)`. The arpeggio now spells E♭ minor over G♭ and ends on E♭3, so the cello does not move G♭3→F♭2 in octaves with the bass.

### 2. Wedge 111–116: no parallel-6/3 setting (keep as is)

The chromatic descending bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C is the classic place for a descending parallel-6/3 series (PDF 290–292, 293 Ex. 18-6a–e). I considered it and reject it. A&S's series needs outer voices in parallel 6ths (PDF 291–292). The wedge's point is contrary motion, with Vln I rising to D6 at 116 while the bass falls. That is a wedge of voice-leading chords (PDF 302, Ex. 18-19h–i), not a 6/3 chain. It also meets the reading's rule for passages of this kind: the ends are the emphasised chords (F minor after the V of F at 110; Fr6 held a full bar, then V on the downbeat of 117, PDF 291 and 297). The middle chords pass under one crescendo. The wedge is protected (c223, c252; earlier "bass 111–116"), and nothing in the reading answers the stated reason. Keep it.

### 3. No extended 6/3 transition anywhere in the movement (keep as is)

The reading presents parallel 6/3s as a transition device (PDF 290). Movement I has none longer than two chords (6–7). Its transitions work differently. 33–46 is a root-position modulating chain over pedals (C–A♭–B♭–E♭–E♭–F–B♭–E♭). It enters the new key through a 6/3 tonic: V4/2 (44) – I6 (45) – ii6/5–V7 (46) – I (47). That is the reading's principle 1 in small: the 6/3 at 45 keeps the arrival fluid, and the 5/3 at 47 is the goal. The retransition is a 14-bar pedal decorated by 6-for-5 over V (Finding 4). A 6/3 chain would add a Baroque fauxbourdon colour that none of the models uses at sonata-form joints. The route 33–47 is protected (c253, c272). No change.

### 4. Dominant prolongations 117–130, 143–146 and the V of E minor at 106 use the "augmented III6" correctly (keep)

At 127 (G–B–E♭), 130 (G–C–D–E♭, E♭ to D on beat 4) and 144 (G–B–E♭, E♭ to D on beat 4), the 6th over the dominant decorates the 5th and resolves to it, as in Bach's Air (PDF 300–301, Ex. 18-18). At 106, B–D♯–G in E minor substitutes the 6th for the 5th and goes straight to i at 107. These prolong V without weakening it, so the retransition keeps its tension to the downbeat of 131. In pacing terms, the 14-bar pedal (117–130, about 31 s, 7% of the movement) is long enough to carry the fff at 122 and the collapse 123–126, and the 6-for-5 colour stops it from standing still. Protected (c233, c239, c249). Keep.
