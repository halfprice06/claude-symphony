# Critic 615: Engraving and notation (Gould ch. 1: beams, PDF 31-41, fourth pass)

Version reviewed: v615. Scratch copy: /tmp/critic-615/work.

Method. I read every text page of *Behind Bars* PDF 31-41 in full and rendered each page at 110 dpi. I also rendered the PDF 35 ledger-line examples at 300 dpi, because the OCR loses the notation. I then added a probe (/tmp/critic-615/tools/probe.ily, included only in a copy of score.ly) that logs all 504 beams after line breaking: staff, voice, bar and beat, source line, final beam positions, direction, and for each stem its direction, notehead staff positions, x position and duration. A script (/tmp/critic-615/tools/check.py) tests the log against each rule below. I ran the same probe on v526, the first version after the last beam pass (c525), and compared the two logs. The comparison shows which beams the ninety cycles since then have changed. Flagged cases were checked by eye on 300 dpi crops of the score PDF.

## What changed since the last beam pass

Between the v526 and v615 logs only six beams differ. All six follow the rules.
- Cl 1-2 at 160.3 (C6-B♭5-A♭5). Since c607 both clarinets play it a2, so the part-combiner prints it as a shared voice with down-stems. Before, Cl 1 alone had up-stems. Most of the notes are far above the middle line, so down-stems are correct (rule 19), and the beam lies on the middle line with one clear line below the first ledger line (rule 6).
- Vla 161 (now <b f'>, c604). The group is horizontal because it repeats one chord (rule 14), and the beam is outside the stave at -3.5.
- Fl 1, Vln I and Ob 2 at 117.3 and Ob 2 at 118.3 moved 0.18 space after c599's tempo words changed the spacing. Both ends still sit on a line, and the slope is one space (rule 7).
- c605 moved the Hn 3-4 staff to bass clef for 19-157. That changed none of its beams, because its few beamed groups (160.3) are in the treble section.

## Findings

### 1. Harp RH 56.3 and 62.3, violas 115.1 and 115.3: first stem 2.74 spaces with the beam centred on an outer stave line (applied)

**Bars.** At 56.3 the harp RH arpeggio (C5 upward, down-stems) has its beam centred on the bottom line of the stave. The first stem, from C5, measures 2.74 spaces to the outer edge of the beam. At 62.3 the rising arpeggio with up-stems ends with its beam centred on the top line, so the last stem is 2.74 spaces. At 115 the violas' two groups G-G-A♭-G (up-stems, the S-head rhythm in the wedge) have a horizontal beam centred on the top line, so the stem of the A♭ is 2.74 spaces. Every other stem in the score passes the test.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 35 ("angled beams modifying stem length"): "This stem may be shortened to 3 stave-spaces (a seventh) when inside the stave and to 2½ stave-spaces (a sixth) when outside the stave." PDF 32-33 gives the normal length as 3½ spaces, or 3¼ for a note on a line. PDF 31 counts a beam centred on a line as inside the stave ("attached to the top or bottom of a stave-line or else be centred on the line"), so a beam that straddles the top or bottom line is inside. The c415 minimum-stem code in global.ily (gould-min-stem) treats a beam end as inside only when `(abs y)` is below 2.0. A beam centred exactly on line ±2 falls through that test and is held to the 2½-space minimum for outside beams. These four groups are the only beams in the score with an end at exactly ±2.0 and a stem under 3 spaces. On the 300 dpi crops the short stem is visible at 56.3: the first note's stem is plainly shorter than the others on the page.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy).** One comparison in global.ily, with a c615 comment:

```
(need (+ (if (< (abs y) 2.05) 3.0 2.5) (if (= nb 2) 0.25 0)))) ; c615: a beam centred on an outer stave-line is inside the stave (was < 2.0)
```

The existing search then moves each beam 0.18 space away from the noteheads, to the next position that keeps both ends on a line. Harp 56.3 changes from (-2.00, -1.82) to (-2.18, -2.00), harp 62.3 from (1.82, 2.00) to (1.82, 2.18), and Vla 115.1 and 115.3 from (2.00, 2.00) to (2.18, 2.18). Every stem is now at least 2.92 spaces. The probe confirms that no other beam moved. An image diff of the 300 dpi crops shows that only those beam pixels changed. The parts use the same code (parts.ly includes global.ily and the same Voice override), so the harp and viola parts get the same correction.

**Considered and kept.** Groups that touch the first ledger line (Cl 78, Bsn/Tbn/Vc 133.3, Cl 160.3, Vln I 114.3 and 163.3, Vc 102.3) slope 0.18 space over a third or more, where PDF 35 would give ½ space. PDF 35's rule is for groups whose notes all "fall outside the first ledger line". The PDF 35 examples (viewed at 300 dpi) begin beyond that line. Each of these groups has a note on it, so the general inside-stave rules apply: both ends are attached to the middle line (PDF 34), and the slope goes in the direction of the interval (PDF 36). c415's predicate (`> 6`) is therefore a correct reading, and I left it unchanged.

## Within the lens: where the music still falls short

I looked for any place where the beaming hides the musical shape: a beat split against the metre, a phrase grouping obscured, or marks pushed to the wrong side of a line the section plays together. I found none. The quaver accompaniments are beamed by half-bar. The semiquaver runs (40, 68) and the 152 demisemiquaver turn are beamed by beat. The P-head and S-head pickups stand unbeamed before their downbeats, which shows them as upbeats. c525's viola stem plan keeps the viola marks on the same side as Vln II's in 27-30, 88-89, 139 and 167-169. In this lens the notation now presents the music as a careful engraver would. No change to the music itself meets the bar of a real improvement, so I propose none.
