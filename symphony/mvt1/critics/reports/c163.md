## Findings

### 1. Bars 53 and 153: the second theme's cadential phrase reaches V from a root-position ii. Use II6 (applied)

**Problem.** The answering phrase of the second theme (51–55, A♭ major) has the bass C–D♭–B♭–E♭–A♭: I6(7)–IV–ii7–V7–I, one pizzicato bass note and one harp root per bar. The recapitulation's statement (151–155, C major) has E–F–D–G–C. The question phrase before it (47–50, A♭–D♭–B♭–E♭) leaps by the same pattern, so the two phrases have the same bass rhythm and the same leap down a third onto ii. Cycle 22 first raised this one-root-position-chord-per-bar bass, cycle 62 measured 71% root position against 40–59% in the models, and cycle 89 put 49 and 51 on the open list. Bar 51 has since moved to C (V6/5 of IV). Bar 53 has not.

**Evidence from the reading.** The book's model for a phrase that moves to a cadence is Mendelssohn's Op. 53/3, reduced in Ex. 18-3a (PDF 291): I6–(VI6)–(IV6)–**II6**–V7–I. "The I6 that begins the series is important as an expansion of the initial tonic; the II6 at the end is important because of its cadential function." Bar 51 already begins this phrase on I6. Ending its predominant on II6 completes the book's pattern and turns the bass into a stepwise rise C–D♭–(D♭)–E♭ to the cadence: 3̂–4̂–5̂–1̂. Ex. 18-19l (PDF 301) shows the same progression: IV with 5–6 above a held bass becomes II6, then V–I. The score already uses this 5–6 move in the violins' statement at 58 (bass B♭–D♭) and in the codetta (I–I6–ii7), so this is the score's own idiom applied to the phrase that needs it most. On form: Unit 18's 5/3s "sound like goals" and its 6/3s give motion (PDF 290–291). With II6 at 53, the half-cadence phrase (47–50, root-position leaps) and the authentic-cadence phrase (51–55, a stepwise bass from I6) are paced differently, and the cadence at 55 is approached as a goal rather than as one more root in a row.

**Answer to the earlier closure.** Cycle 132 closed the "I–IV–ii–V chain" item because Units 11–13 call IV–II–V correct syntax. That is still true, and the fix keeps the chain: IV–II–V–I remains, with II in first inversion. The question raised here is bass motion and phrase pacing (Unit 18), not syntax. The protections on 47–55 and 147–155 cover the theme, its horn/cello and viola/cello scoring, the harp's design and pedalling, and the div./metà scheme. None of those changes: only the bass pitch changes, D♭ needs no harp pedal change, and the harp arpeggio keeps its shape. Bar 49 stays root position on purpose, so that the two phrases differ.

**Corpus.** Beethoven 9/i's second group reaches its cadences through inverted chords (bars 101–106: V6/5–I6–IV–I6/4–V7–I6; 81% inverted in the cadential phrase). Tchaikovsky 6/i's theme is mostly root position only because it sits on a pedal; this score's theme has no pedal at 51–55, so its bass is heard as a line.

At 54 it is E♭2, B♭3/E♭4, E♭4 and A♭4: the upper parts move against the bass and there are no new octaves or fifths. ### 2. Bars 43–47: the tonic of A♭ is held back in 6/3 position until the second theme (no change)

The lead-in runs V7 (43) – V4/2 (44, bass D♭) – I6 (45, bass C) – ii6/5 (46) – V (46.3) – I (47). The only A♭ chord before 47 is a 6/3, which the reading treats as the less stable, non-goal form (PDF 290) and as a neighbouring or passing chord between dominant forms (Ex. 18-19a, b; PDF 301). The first root-position tonic of the new key therefore comes with the theme at 47. This is the right pacing for a medial-caesura fill; leave it as it is (it is already protected as "MC and fill 41–46").

### 3. Bars 117–130: the motto's E♭ over the G pedal is the "III6 that stands for V" (no change; supports the protection)

At 119 (G–B–D–E♭–F), 127 (G–B–E♭) and 129 (G–D–E♭) the sixth E♭ decorates or replaces the fifth D over the dominant pedal. This is exactly the apparent augmented III6 in minor that the book says "are not III chords at all, but V's" (PDF 300, Ex. 18-18; Ex. 18-19k "6 displacing 5", PDF 301). The retransition is therefore one long dominant, with ♭VI at 122 as its upper neighbour (cycles 123 and 153). The reading gives a further reason to keep the G pedal and the motto statements on it as they are.

### 4. Bars 1–4: a lone low tonic with the 6th above (no change)

The book's Brahms Op. 15 example (Ex. 18-15, PDF 299) shows that a tone sounding alone in a low register at the start of a movement is heard as the tonic root, and a 6th above it as a decoration of the 5th. The timpani's ppp C under the motto's G→E♭ works the same way: the motto's first leap is heard as 5̂–3̂ of C minor, and the minor sixth reads as the motto's interval, not as a chord change. Keep it.

### 5. Bars 33–40: an interior goal at 36 inside the transition (checked, no change)

Unit 18 says a transition should articulate only its ends (PDF 291). At 35–36 the bass B♭–E♭ in root position could sound like a cadence on III halfway through the transition. It does not close. The top voice ends on 5̂ and 3̂ (Vln I B♭–G–F, Bsn G–E♭), the bass E♭ is held into 37, where the chord turns minor (G♭), and the fifths sequence e♭7–f–B♭7 continues to the V7 of A♭ at 41. The articulated ends are 33 (the C-minor stop) and 41–43 (the medial caesura). The cycle-120 protection of the route 36–47 stands.
