## A. First impressions (written before reading HISTORY/LENSES; unchanged)

Strong:
- The motto (G–E♭–D–D♭–C) is audible throughout and transforms well: low unison in the intro (1–4); rising-sixth sequence in the violins (9–13); eighth-note diminution as the P head (15); augmentation in trombones and horns over the G pedal (110–114); syncopated half-note version at the recap (123–126); pp trombone chorale and the violas' turn to major at the end (171–178).
- The second theme (47–68) is a real tune: a long held C, the sixth leap A♭–F onto an appoggiatura, a sequence, the chromatic sink at 60–61. Horn and cellos dolce over harp and pizzicato basses is idiomatic.
- The recap of S in C major with the ♭VI (A♭) arrival at 156 (fff, crash) is the right big moment. The coda's return to minor and its quiet close are well judged.

Weak or doubtful:
1. Scale. 178 bars, about 7 minutes (MIDI 414 s). P group 18 bars, transition 14, S 22, closing 8, recap P 16. Every idea is stated once and left. It reads like a condensed version of a movement, not a symphonic first movement of the models' size (Tchaikovsky 4/i and Brahms 1/i run 15–20 minutes).
2. The P group's continuation (23–26: es d c f | es d g f | es aes g f | b) is a stock rising scale-sequence over repeated-quarter chords. These are the least distinctive bars in the theme, and they return unchanged at 127–130.
3. Tutti scoring formula: the tune in 4–6 octaves (Fl/Ob/Cl/Vln I/Vln II/Vla) and the other winds and brass in whole-note pads (27–32, 109–114, 154–157, 163–166). The brass seldom have their own rhythmic line.
4. The closing section is 8 bars: 4 forte bars repeating the S head, then a 4-bar flute motto. There is no closing theme and no cadential drive, so the exposition stops rather than ends.
5. The development is four blocks (8+12+12+14). D3 (97–102) is the S theme transposed whole (`\transpose c cis`), not developed, and the joins between blocks are abrupt.
6. Static harmonic rhythm in the transition (37–40: whole notes under the line).
7. Engraving: the "(low C instr.)" text in the bass clarinet at bar 92 reads like a working note.

What would make me doubt a master wrote it: mostly points 1, 3 and 4. The material is good, but the movement does not take time to develop it, and the full-orchestra passages use one doubling recipe.

- A5 (D3 transposed): decided. The B-minor block is protected (cycles 2, 29, 31, 57). Not re-raised.
- A6 (37–40): not confirmed. The bass moves E♭–F–B♭ under G♭–A♭ (a B♭7 approach), and cycle 21 judged the transition fine.
- A7 ("low C instr."): added on purpose in cycle 16 because C2 at 92 needs a low-C bass clarinet. Not re-raised.
- Considered and rejected: the fast music uses one rhythmic cell. The cell `r8 x8 y4.->` occurs in about 33 Allegro bars, in every section. Tchaikovsky 4 sets two rhythmic characters against each other (polonaise fanfare against 9/8 waltz; Wikipedia). But Beethoven 5/i saturates a single cell and is a master's work, so this is a design choice, not a fault.

### 1. Absolute scale of the Allegro (whole movement) — re-raised, not applied
Problem: proportions are fine (cycle 43), but absolute time per idea is short. The S theme has 22 bars (about 50 s at ♩=112) and its recap 24 bars. Tchaikovsky's first movement runs about 20 minutes (Wikipedia), with an exposition long enough to hold three key areas and a separate closing theme. This is the main thing a conductor would notice first: the craft is a master's, the scale is a sketch's.
Answer to the recorded reasons: cycle 43 judged ratios, not durations. Dan's decision (cycle 40) concerned the repeat and says "length is not a constraint", so lengthening is allowed. I am not asking for a repeat or for slow music.
Fix (for the composer and Dan, not testable here): if the movement grows, it should grow in fast music only. One choice: an 8-bar counterstatement of P in the winds after 32, before the transition. Another: an 8–12-bar second development core that combines the P head with the S head before 109. Protected bars would stay untouched.

### 2. Flute 2 buried at the development climax, bars 114–115 — applied
Problem: at the fff B°7-over-G climax, Fl1 is on A♭6 and Fl2 on D5, more than two octaves lower and inaudible in fff tutti. Between A♭6 (Fl1, Vln I) and A♭5 (Ob1, Vln II) no instrument sounds, so the top of the climax chord is a bare octave.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov (ch. 3, tutti) keeps both flutes in their upper octave in fortissimo and fills the chord just under the top line. At the other climax (156) the flutes play a2 on A♭6 (cycle 24).
Fix (dev.ily, devFluteTwo): `g''1\< | d'''1\fff | d'''1 | R1*7 |` (was d''). D6 is a chord tone at both 114 and 115. G5–D6 is an easy fifth, and the shared flute staff has no ottava there.

### 3. Double basses ff against f basses at the coda opening, bar 163 — applied
Problem: cycle 38 set the parts around the brass head at f so that the ff head (Hn1·2, Tpt1·2, Vla) stands out. The basses' repeated C eighths were left at ff, while Vc, Cbsn, Tuba and B.tbn on the same bass are at f. The tutti then arrives at ff at 166.
Fix (coda.ily, codaCb): `c,8\f \repeat unfold 7 { c,8 } | …` (166 keeps `\ff`).
