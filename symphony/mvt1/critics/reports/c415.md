# Critic 415: Engraving and notation, beams (Gould, *Behind Bars* ch. 1, PDF 31–41, second pass)

Version reviewed: v415. Scratch copy: /tmp/critic-415/work.

Method. I read every text page from PDF 31 to 41 in full and rendered all eleven pages at 110 dpi. The examples on PDF 32, 35, 36 and 37 decide the cases below, so I worked from the images and not the OCR. c235 and c325 already covered these pages, so I measured the score instead of repeating their checks by eye. I built a probe copy (/tmp/critic-415/probe) that logs all 504 beamed groups of the full score after the `gould-beam-positions` callback: staff, bar, beat, direction, final end positions, and for each stem the notehead positions, duration and x position. Two scripts (/tmp/critic-415/scripts/an2.py, anA.py) test the log against each rule below. Stem length is measured as Gould measures it (PDF 32: "including the width of the outer beam"), from the notehead nearest the beam to the far edge of the outer beam. I checked the flagged bars in the PDF at 300 dpi.

## Findings

### 1. Ledger-line groups whose stems reach the middle line are horizontal (applied)

**Bars (15 groups):** Vln I 27.3& (D6–D♭6–C6) and 29.3& (E♭6–D6–C6); Fl 1·2 and Vln I 40.3 (sixteenths B♭5–C6–D6–E♭6); Hn 1·2 69.3& (B3–A♭3–G3, written low) and 99.3&; Fl 1·2 and Vln I 71.4 (a rising third), 82.3&, 94.3&, 96.3&; Vla 109.4 (a rising third above the alto stave).

**What is wrong.** Gould PDF 35: "When all the notes of a beam fall outside the first ledger line, the beam takes only a slight slope, regardless of the pitches. Intervals of a second take a slope of ¼ stave-space; all wider intervals take a slope of ½ stave-space." The right-hand example on PDF 35 shows such a group with its beam inside the stave, still sloping. In these groups LilyPond extends every stem to the middle line, so the beam lies flat on it whatever the notes do. At 29 the same figure is sloped in the flutes (beam above the stave, 7.5 → 7.0) and flat in Vln I directly below, so one passage is drawn two ways on the same page. The thirds at 71.4 and 109.4 get only ¼ space, where the rule asks for ½. The D–D♭–C groups at 27, 82, 94 and 96 (down-stems) also break PDF 37: the single different notehead is the one nearest the beam, and Gould says "the beam slopes".

**Earlier decision.** c325 (Finding 4) kept the four down-stem groups at 27/82/94/96 horizontal. It gave two reasons: Vln I and Fl should look identical, and the change is too small to notice. On the first: both staves go through the same callback, so at 82, 94 and 96 they change together and stay identical. At 27 and 29 they already differed in v415, because the flute beam lies above the stave and is sloped. On the second: at 29 and 40 the flat beam on a falling or rising run of three or four ledger-line notes is easy to see at 300 dpi. It is the most visible of these cases, and c325 did not consider it.

**Fix (applied in global.ily).** New case (6) in `gould-beam-positions`, after the concave and two-pitch cases. It applies when every note lies beyond the first ledger line, LilyPond has put both ends on the middle line (|y| < 0.2), the outer notes differ, and the group has one or two beams. A second gets a slope of 0.18 space: the end at the note nearer the beam moves 0.18 away from the noteheads. A wider interval gets 0.35 space (sit at one end, hang at the other on the middle line). That is the nearest slope to ½ that keeps both ends on a line, as PDF 34 requires. Two-beam groups (only Fl/Vln I 40.3) are limited to 0.18 so that the outer beam still sits or straddles (PDF 32). Every stem stays at least 3½ spaces long, and the beam stays more than a clear line away from the ledger lines (PDF 33).

### 2. Six groups have a stem shorter than three spaces inside the stave (applied)

**Bars:** Hp RH 62.3 (last stem 2.56 spaces), Hp RH 67.3 (2.74), Vla 70.3 (2.74), Hn 1·2 98.3& (2.74), Tbn 1·2 100.3& (2.74), Vla 142.1 (inner note of a concave group, 2.74).

**What is wrong.** Gould PDF 35: the shortest stem "may be shortened to 3 stave-spaces (a seventh) when inside the stave and to 2½ stave-spaces (a sixth) when outside the stave". PDF 33 sets 2½ as the absolute floor, and that floor applies only to the semiquaver beam. LilyPond's own floor (free stem length about 1.8 spaces plus the beam) is lower than Gould's. In these six groups it chose a quant that leaves the short stem a sixth or less inside the stave. At 62 the harp's arpeggio A♭3–C4–E♭4–A♭4 ends on a stem that barely clears the beam, next to the full-length stems of the left hand in 63.

**Fix (applied in global.ily).** New helper `gould-min-stem` runs on the result of every case. If a stem of a one- or two-beam group is more than 0.1 space short of 3 spaces (2½ where the beam lies outside the stave; ¼ more with a second beam), it moves the beam away from the noteheads to the nearest pair of positions that meets the floor. Both ends stay on a line quant inside the stave, the slope keeps its direction, and the beam gets no steeper. In practice each of the six moves by 0.18 space (Hp 62: 1.18/1.82 → 1.82/2.00). The concave group at 142 stays horizontal (PDF 36 (c)). Groups of three beams are left alone.

### 3. D–D♭–C groups in the upper woodwinds stay horizontal (not applied; keep)

Ob/Cl 27.3& and Ob 82.3& (up-stems; D5–D♭5–C5): three pitches, but two of them share a notehead position, so c325's case 4 treats them as a two-pitch group and makes the beam horizontal. Gould's rule (PDF 37) concerns what the eye sees on the stave: the odd notehead, C5, lies farther from the beam, so horizontal is right. Keep as it is.

### 4. Vc 61.1 stem direction (not applied; keep)

The probe found one beamed group in a single-player staff where the stems go against the majority: Vc 61.1, notes at −5, 0, 2, 4, stems up. Gould PDF 38 allows the minority to decide when it is "much further from the middle stave-line"; the low note is five steps out against four. c235 kept it for the context of the cello passage (PDF 39). Keep as it is.
