# Critic 456: Playability, instrument by instrument (v456)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 6 The Woodwind Choir and ch. 7 Individual Woodwinds (adler1 PDF 168-232), third pass. Cycles 256 and 346 read the same chapters. I read every text page from 168 to 232 in full and rendered every page from 172 to 232 at 110 dpi and looked at each one. I re-rendered the cor anglais register chart (Ex. VII-47, PDF 205) at 500 dpi because its bracket limits decide one of the questions below. (Book page = PDF page − 16.)

Method. I parsed full.midi with a script. For each of the eleven woodwind parts it lists the range, the four longest stretches without a gap of 0.2 s or more, and every note in a register Adler marks as weak, thick, thin or pinched, with its velocity. The MIDI does not carry `\breathe`, so I checked every long stretch against the .ily source. I also searched the wind parts for trills, tremolos and "div.", and checked the page counts of the wind parts in v456/parts-run.txt. The changes made since cycle 346 that touch the winds got particular attention: Ob 1/Ob 2/Fl 2 at 117-121 (c424), Bsn 1·2 at 162 (c424), Cl 2 on the climax counter 158-160 (c408), the coda C.a. D♭5 at 173 (c400), the Fl 1 swell at 73-74 and the C.a. slur at 153-155 (c398).

## Findings

### 1. No woodwind change is needed. The score meets the reading in every bar I checked.

Nothing in the woodwinds is unplayable, unsafe or likely to go wrong under a professional orchestra. Since cycle 346 the changes that touch the winds are the five listed at the top. Each still meets the reading:

- **Ob 2 at 117-120 (c424).** It plays the P phrase's lower octave at ff, B3-F5, with several notes in the lowest fifth (B3, D4, E♭4, F4). Adler forbids only pp there (PDF 174-175, 198), and he calls the oboe agile unless the skips are "extraordinarily large" (PDF 200). The leaps here are a sixth and a seventh, tongued, at crotchet = 126. Cl 1 and Vln II double it, so the oboe adds its edge to a line that the brass motto covers in any case (PDF 201). Keep.
- **Cl 1·2 at 158-160 (c408).** They reach written D6 at ff-fff, doubled by Ob 1. Adler's chart (Ex. VII-56, PDF 210) puts written D6 at the edge of the "piercing, shrill" register, which suits a fff climax counter. Keep.
- **Bsn 1 E3 and Bsn 2 A2 at 162 (c424).** Neither is in the lowest fifth, and they are ff. Keep.
- **Fl 1 at 72.4-75 (c398).** It enters on the low C5 at p with only a pp string pad under it, as in Adler's low-flute examples (Ex. VII-3 and VII-4, PDF 186: "doing little with the other instruments"). It swells only on A♭5, where the flute can add volume (PDF 185). Keep.
- **C.a. slurred at 153-155 (c398)** and **held D♭5 at 173 (c400).** Both are in one breath at p, and the C.a. rests from 155 to 170 and from 175 to the end. Keep.

### 2. Changes I considered and rejected

The reading also asks where a master would have done better. I tested four ideas from it against the score and the masters. None was a real improvement.

- **Move the coda cor anglais (171-174) down an octave into its "deep, rich, intense" register.** At 500 dpi the chart (Ex. VII-47, PDF 205) puts "thin, pinched" from about written A5, and the coda line reaches written B♭5 and A5 at 172-173. Adler praises the lowest fifth or sixth for its "tremendous expressive carrying power" (PDF 205). Against this, his own solo examples go just as high: Berlioz's *Roman Carnival* solo (Ex. VII-48, PDF 205) reaches the first ledger line at sf, and Sibelius's *Swan of Tuonela* (Ex. VII-50, PDF 206) starts there at ff. An octave lower, the line would sit under the solo cello's A-string S theme and cross it, so the duet would lose its upper voice. The line is also protected (c358, c400; the coda C.a. D♭5 is in themes-mvt1.ly). Keep.
- **Dovetail the C.a.'s held G4 at 7-9 into a second wind, as Brahms dovetails the flutes (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188).** Brahms does that because the flute "requires a great deal of breath". The cor anglais is the opposite case: Adler says oboe-family players can "play for longer stretches than any other wind player" (PDF 175, 198). The phrase is 18-19 s at pp, which professionals play in one breath, and the part already allows a breath. A second wind on G4 would also weaken the hand-over to Vln I at 9.4 (c150). Keep.
- **Mark Cl 1's pickup into the chorale at 175.4 "dal niente" (PDF 211).** The E♭4 is a pickup inside a pp trombone chorale and has to be placed in time, so it needs a soft attack, not a niente entry. The written F4→D5 slur across the break at pp is routine for a professional (PDF 210: the break "presents no difficulties whatsoever" to an experienced player). Keep.
- **Give the low flute a soloistic moment (PDF 186).** The flute's low register is used once, for the flute motto pickup at 72.4, in exactly the conditions Adler describes. A further low-flute solo would mean recomposing a protected passage (73-76 flute motto, c333/c351) for a colour the movement does not need. Keep.
