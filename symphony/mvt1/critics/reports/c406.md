## Findings

### 1. Violas reach the f at bar 12 on an up-bow (bars 11-13). Applied.

The intro strings play one bow per whole note from bar 6. The violas are the only part with two halves at 11 (C4 then B3, c132), so they gain a bow. Counted from 6: B♭ down, D♭ up, C down, C up, C down, C (11.1) up, B (11.3) down, and the f double stop C4/E♭4 at 12 **up**. Then 12.4 is down and the slurred diminuendo <c' es'>2( d'2) at 13 is up. At 12 Vln I, Vln II, cellos and basses all arrive down-bow: Vln I and Vln II marked so by c226 and c316, cellos by the natural count, basses marked. c316 put a double up-bow into Vln II at 11.3 "as the other strings since c226", but the violas were left out.

Adler (PDF 38) says crescendo comes naturally on an up-bow and diminuendo on a down-bow, and that the composer marks bowings exactly where the habit has to be overruled. He also gives the hook of two up-bows that puts the accented beat on a down-bow (Schubert 5/ii, Ex. II-19, PDF 37-38). As written, the violas play the f double stop near the tip and the p diminuendo moving toward the frog, against the other four sections.

Fix (intro.ily, introVla), as Vln II at the same beat:
```
c'1\pp\< | c'1 | c'2 b2\upbow | <c' es'>2.\f\downbow <c' es'>4 |
```
Marks only. The section reaches the f together, and the diminuendo at 13 falls on a down-bow. Bars 9-13 are protected as notes (c226), and no note changes.

### 2. Vln II double stops at 122-123 need "non div." (bars 107-125). Applied.

Vln II is marked div. at 107 and keeps the divisi through the tremolo chords 111-116. "unis." comes at 117. At 122-123 it plays the fff tremolo double stops D5/A♭5 and D5/G5, which c236 kept on purpose as undivided double stops. At 124 it divides again for D4/F4 and returns to "unis." at 125. A player who has just played ten bars divided and is about to divide again cannot tell from the part which way 122-123 go. Adler (PDF 30) says unmarked double notes are double stops, but that when every player is meant to play them "it is safer to write non div. so that no division between the two players will occur". In the Vln II part this spot sits on one line (116-126), between "unis." and "div.". That is where the warning is needed.

Fix (dev.ily, devVlnTwo bar 122):
```

```
No notes change. The build places the text cleanly above the A♭5, and the part still has 2 pages.

### 3. Nothing else in the strings is unplayable against this reading.

I checked all 27 principles bar by bar. The other candidates do not need a change:
- **Open strings held softly** (Cb G2 at 6 and 13-14, Vla D4 at 13-14, Vla G3 and Vc D3 at 144-146). Adler (PDF 33) gives the player two ways to warm an open string: sympathetic fingering an octave higher, or the stopped unison on the next string down. These are chord tones in a pp pad. Unlike the motto lines that c251 and c341 marked sul G/sul A, they are not melodic. Marking them would overmark the parts, which Adler (PDF 38) warns against. They should stay as they are.
- **Basses 5-12** start one bar before the other strings, so their bow count runs opposite: crescendo at 7 down-bow, retake at 11→12 before the marked down-bow at f. A retake before a strong attack is the Coriolanus pattern (PDF 38, Ex. II-20), and at Lento there is time for it. It should stay as it is.
- **Cb 102→103 arco to pizz.** with one crotchet rest (c195). The separate bows 97-102 end naturally on an up-bow at the frog, which is the position Adler (PDF 54) recommends. No mark is needed.
- **Long slurs**: the S statements are at most 9 beats at Allegro. The coda Vln I slur 177.4-179.3 is about 8.5 s at pp, and Vc 175-176 is 6.7 s at pp. All are inside Adler's limits for soft slow bows (PDF 39). The ff cello slurs at 140-142 are protected (c305, c316).
