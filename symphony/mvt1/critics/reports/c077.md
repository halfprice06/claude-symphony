# Critic 077 — lens 7, texture and counterpoint: dialogue and imitation between groups (v96)

## What I studied
All nine section files of v96 and the PDF (pages 3–4, 12–13). Corpus: MCTL MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`). I read them bar by bar with a dump script and ran a detector on both and on v96 (`perinst.midi`). The detector flags a motive of 4–5 notes, with the same intervals and rhythm, that one group (woodwind, brass or strings) answers in another group up to three bars later. It misses free call-and-response, so I also counted those by hand. Web sources:
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. IV, "Transference of passages" and "Repetition of phrases, imitation, echo" (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). Its points: an upward imitation goes to a higher instrument, and an echo stays in the same family.
- Two descriptions of Dvořák 9/i as a horn call answered by the woodwinds: https://arts.ucdavis.edu/post/dvorak-symphony-no-9-new-world and https://benjaminpesetsky.com/antonin-dvorak-symphony-no-9-in-e-minor-op-9-from-the-new-world/

## Inventory of exchanges in v96
**Group to group (imitation or call-and-response):**
- 1–8: low unison motto, answered by the cor anglais 4 bars later.
- 33–35: Bsn 1 head, then Vla one bar later a 4th up, then Vln I one bar later another 4th up.
- 37–38: Vln I sequence, with the viola answer half a bar later (within the strings only).
- 43–46: Hn 1, then Ob 1, then Vc/Hn upbeat, in 2-bar alternation.
- 77–83: the motto head passes Bsn → Cl (half a bar, two octaves up) → Hn/Vc → Ob/Vln I → Hn 3·4/Tbn → Cl/Ob/Vln I → Hn/Tbn → Fl/Vln I. These are half-bar alternations of brass against winds and strings, the best antiphony in the score.
- 85–96: fugato with entries 3 bars apart, each a 4th up, each in a new string+wind colour.
- 97–102: brass/bassoon fills answer the held notes of the theme.
- 103–110: invertible exchange 4 bars apart (winds/horns, then violas/flute/cellos).
- 127–130: Cl 1 head, then a motto stretto Cl 1 → Vla (half a bar) → Vln II + Bsn 1.
- 147–153: the cor anglais answers the held notes of the horn/viola tune.

**One group carrying the music, or everything in homorhythm:**
- 15–22: Vln I with strings, 8 bars.
- 23–32: hemiola, then octave tutti, 10 bars.
- 47–68: 22 bars of tune plus accompaniment. The counterline at 56–68 fills the gaps but imitates nothing.
- 69–72: codetta, homorhythmic.
- 111–126: the wedge, the layered climax and the unison sigh, 16 bars.
- 131–142: theme low against a counterline high. This is real two-group counterpoint, but simultaneous.
- 143–146: the Vln I head stated once, with no answer.
- 156–170, 171–174.

Proportion: inter-group exchange occurs in about 55 of 186 bars (30%). But 41 of those 55 are in the development and retransition (77–130). The exposition has 7 of 62 bars (11%) and the recapitulation 7 of 40 (18%). The detector finds literal inter-group imitation in 6% of v96's bars (34–35, 78–79, 129; the hit at 97–101 is an interlocked ostinato). Tchaikovsky 6/i scores 13% (5 notes) to 18% (4 notes), spread through every section: 55–69, 134–141, 189–222, 248–275, 301–354.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Transition 37–40: the stretto stays inside the strings, and then the winds simply double Vln I (APPLIED)
**Problem.** At 33–35 the head passes from woodwind to strings. From 37 on, the half-bar answers are viola only, under Vln I. At 39–40, Fl 1 and Ob 1 play the Vln I line in unison. So the build into the medial caesura has no exchange between groups. It also adds two more bars of the flute and oboe doubling Vln I, which cycles 44 and 54 worked to reduce.

**Evidence.** In Tchaikovsky 6/i 55–58, the first-theme figure alternates every half bar between the strings (beats 1–2) and the clarinets and oboes (beats 3–4). The detector finds a 2-quarter lag both ways at 55, 56, 57, 63 and 65. Dvořák 9/i 24–39 builds its first group from 4-bar exchanges: horns 24–27, clarinets/bassoons + strings 28–31, oboes 32–35, flutes/bassoons + strings 36–39. Rimsky-Korsakov says an imitation rising into the upper register belongs to a higher instrument. Ob 1 and Cl 1 therefore take the viola answer at the violin octave; the viola keeps it below.

**Fix (in `trans.ily`):** a continuous half-bar chain: Vln I (37.1) → Ob 1 + Vla (37.3) → Vln I (38.1) → Cl 1 + Vla (38.3) → Vln I (39.1) → Fl 1 + Ob 1 (39.3) → Vln I (40.1) → all (40.3). The Vln I and viola notes are unchanged.
```

transOboeOne:  … R1*3 | \partCombineSoloI r2 r8 bes'8\mf( ges''4->~ | ges''8 f''8 fes''8 es''8) r2 |
               r2 d''8\mf\<( es''8 f''8 bes''8) | d'''2-> r16 c''16( d'' es'' f'' es'' f'' fis'') |
transClOne:    … g'1) | R1 | \partCombineSoloI r2 r8 c''8\mf( aes''4->~ | aes''8) g''16( ges''16 f''4) r2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1 |
```
**Checks.** In bar 40 the winds hold d''' over the violins' f''–c''' (a passing 9th over B♭7). In bars 37–38 the only new rubs are passing seconds (ges''/fes'', aes''/ges'') that the viola already makes an octave lower. Ob 2 and Cl 2 rest, so the shared staves are fine. No ottava. Ranges and breathing are fine.

**On protection.** Cycles 21 and 51 protect 33–46 and "the imitation 35–40" for their melody. No Vln I or viola note changes here; the imitation is extended, not altered. Cycle 44 removed Ob 1 from 35–38 because it doubled Vln I; here Ob 1 plays the answer, not the violin line.

### 2. Recap 143–146: the transition head returns with no answer (APPLIED)
**Problem.** At 143 Vln I states the head once and fades to the dominant. In the exposition, the same formal point (33–35) was an imitation chain. The recap loses the one exposition gesture built from exchange.

**Evidence.** The composer's v17 note explains why a one-bar canon on this head fails: the head against its own second bar gives parallel fifths (A♭/D♭ → G/C). Tchaikovsky 6/i 248–275 restores the exposition's exchanges in the recapitulation (detector hits at 248–257 and 268–275).

**Fix (in `recOne.ily`, `recOneBsnOne`).** Bsn 1, which only doubles the cello's bass at 144, answers one bar later at the lower octave. The tail is D–C–B instead of D–D♭–C. This avoids the fifths and lands on the existing bass B at 145, so it recalls the horn call E♭–C–B of 13–14.
```
c2\f d2 | r8 g8\mf\>( es'4.->) d'4( c'8 | b1\pp) | R1 |
```
**Checks.** The bass stays in Vc/Cb. The vl2 check loses two old Bsn/Vla octave entries at 144 and gains none. Bsn 2 rests. The Ob 1 G–E pivot at 145 and the timpani/Cb dominant are untouched. Cycle 60's protection of 131–146 concerned a D♭ excursion; this adds no D♭.

### 3. The exposition's first and second groups contain no exchange (no change proposed)
**Problem.** Bars 15–32 and 47–76 never pass material between groups. In Dvořák 9/i, the first group itself is a call and response (24–39). In Tchaikovsky 6/i 134–140, trumpets and trombones answer each held note of the D-major theme with a rising three-note figure.

**Why no change.** Every candidate bar is protected for reasons this lens cannot override: 15–22 (strings alone, cycle 44), the hemiola 23–26, 27–32, 47–55 and 69–76. At 56–68 the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline already fills the held notes. This is the reason I spent both applied changes on 33–40 and 143–146, the exposition's and recap's only free texture. **For movements II/III:** build at least one theme as a call and response between groups.

### 4. Release 123–130 (no change)
The non-pedal voices already have the movement's second-best stretto (127–130). I tested an answer to the Cl 1 head at 128 in Bsn 1. It gives fifths on beats 4–4.5 (A♭/D♭ → G/C), the same fault as the coda canon, and violas 127–128 are protected. A half-bar imitation of the unison sigh at 124–126 puts B against the C of the cadential 6/4 at 125. Leave it.

### 5. Coda 171–174 (no change)
A string answer to the brass head would compete with the denied tutti that cycles 29, 30 and 38 built: the brass must stand out over f surroundings. A one-bar canon has the v17 fifths. This passage should stay a single concentrated statement.
