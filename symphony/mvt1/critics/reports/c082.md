## Findings (ranked)

**1. 147–153: seven bars of one consonant, root-position chord per downbeat (C–F–d–G–C7/E–F–d). Applied.**
Problem: the cycle-80 cut removed the A♭-pedal restatement with its chromatic inner voices (56–59 in the exposition). The only recap statement now repeats the exposition's plainest harmony bar for bar. This is the open cycle-22 item ("one-chord-per-bar root-position I–IV–ii–V").
Evidence: in T6 the second theme, both in the exposition (annotation, 92–100) and in the recap (309–316), gives every bar two harmonies. Beat 1 is a diminished-seventh appoggiatura chord (vii°7, vii°7/ii, vii°7/V) and beat 3 resolves it. In the human annotation, T6 305–334 averages 1.67 chords per bar at ♩=62.
Fix (recap only; texture, dynamics and melody unchanged; Vln II tremolo only): bar 148 `c''2:32 cis''2:32`, bar 149 `d''1:32` (was `f''1:32 | f''1:32`). This gives IV → IV⁺ (F–A–C♯) → ii, with the chromatic line C–C♯–D in a non-thematic voice. The CA's `c''2` doubles the C until C♯ arrives, so there is no simultaneous cross-relation.
Answer to the protection of 147–155 (cycles 27/66/67/80: "neutral accompaniment", "notes of 139–147 old"): the sul tasto pp tremolo texture stays the same. Only one inner pitch moves on a half-bar, and the 144 32nd-note scale (cycle 31) already set a precedent for varying the third hearing. The exposition 47–55 stays plain.

**2. Join 155→156: E–F semitone on beat 4 of 155. Applied.**
Problem: Vla e', Hn1 e' and Vc e hold the tonic third through the bar while Vln II sounds the pickup `f'` at the same octave (Vln I `f''` a ninth above). In the exposition (55) the pickup was E♭ over A♭ (a chord fifth). Cycle 80 lowered the head a step onto ♭VII, which made the held E dissonant. It is a by-product of the cut, not a chosen appoggiatura: the E does not resolve, it is simply abandoned at 156.
Fix: bar 155 Vla `e'2\p d'2`, Vc `e2\p d2`, Hn1 `e'2\p d'2`. The pickup F now sits over D–F, the upper third of the coming B♭ chord. The inner line E–D then joins the motto descent D–D♭–C of 156–157. The violins' approach is unchanged (protected).

**3. 156–166 (the rest of the recap second group): sound; no change.**
156 B♭ with D–D♭ on beat 4 (Vc A–A♭–G under it) → 157 iv → 158 I⁶₄ → 159 V7 → 160 ♭VI fff → 161 vii°⁶₅–V7 → 162 vi → 163 IV → 164 iiø7–V → 165 V7 → 166 I/i. **4. 47–55: the same nine-bar uniform run in the exposition. Not applied.**
Keep it. A plain first statement is what lets the 148 variant register. The models make the same distinction: D9 91–107 is plain i–V, while T6 colours its theme at once. Name it only so a later critic does not "fix" both.

**5. Development 117–121: one chord per bar over the G pedal straight into the fff at 122; no beat-level surge. Not applied.**
T6's development speeds up to the beat before its peaks (annotation 209–224: 3.9 chords per bar; 241–256: 4.9 chords per bar at ♩=156). v101's only fast stretch is the contrapuntal fugato. The layering 117–121, the augmented trombone motto and the "no lengthening of the G pedal" rule are protected and coherent. I record this as a stylistic difference (Brahms/Dvořák pedal climaxes rather than Tchaikovsky's), not a defect.

This is within the models (T6 82–88 holds 7 bars; D9 45–54 holds 10). It is the protected medial caesura and fill.

T6 changes harmony on almost every beat (vii°7/V–V–i). The sentence 15–22 and the chromatic bass 27–32 are protected and work in their own terms. Note for movements II/III, not a change here.
