## Findings

No change. I checked the four places where, within this lens, a master's score might do more, and in each the present marking holds.

**1. Bars 160–164: "largamente" without a closing word.** Keep.
The word at 160 is not followed by "a tempo" before the rit. at 165. MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 4 (PDF 10) treat a tempo word as standing until the next one, and the score does exactly that: global.ily keeps \mt 100 for 160–164 and begins the rit. at 165 (98–92–87–81) into Tempo dell'Allegro at 167. A conductor reading 163–164 at the broad tempo gets the dissolution the brass diminuendo (ff to mp at 162) and the p/mp strings at 163–164 ask for. Adding "a tempo" at 163 would put a speeding-up between the climax and the rit., which the music does not want. The only fault is in the record: coherence/mvt1.md lists "largamente 160–162"; the composer should correct it to 160–164 at the next mvt1.md update. That is a record change, not a score change.

**2. Bars 102→103: cello crescendo inside ff, then mf at 103.** Keep.
hp.py flags the cellos' hairpin on 102 leading to a lower written level. The crescendo is on the last ff group of the B-minor block; 103 is a new section (A minor key signature, both heads combined, f/p contrast protected in HISTORY.md: "notes and f/p contrast of 103–110"). The mf at 103 is a subito level at a section join, the same device as sub. p at 62 and 84. MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10) concerns a hairpin within one phrase; it does not forbid a terraced drop at a new section. A written "sub. mf" would add text to a join that already reads clearly from the new key signature and the fresh slur.

**3. Bars 171–174: the coda cor anglais has no hairpins while the solo cello beside it has swells.** Keep (protected, c478).
MOLA principle 12 (Ex. 6) would apply if the two instruments played one line. They do not: the cello has the minor S theme, the cor anglais the motto in long values. c478's reason (the motto keeps its plain shape against the shaped theme) is not answered by anything in this reading.

**4. Bars 47–55 and 147–155: Hn 1 mp against cellos mf (47) and violas mf (146) on the same tune.** Keep (protected, c244/c278).
dyn.py lists both. They are graded on purpose. Ex. 6's rule is about stress on one line; Beethoven's levels in that example are the same in every staff because the scoring is a tutti, not a horn-and-strings blend. Rimsky-Korsakov (rimsky-principles PDF 51) gives the balance: in piano passages one whole string section equals one wind instrument, and all winds are about equal. A single horn at the cello section's level would therefore match it; marked one degree lower, it colours the cellos' line without taking the lead, which is the sound earlier critics chose (horn plus cello as the S colour, protected). The horn part still carries its own complete marks ("1. dolce", swells, p at 55), which is what MOLA PDF 7 requires.

Beyond these four, I looked for a phrase whose marking could make it sing more and found none worth a change. The P sentence 15–22 already has the sf on the D♭6 peak at 20, the "espr. mp" counter in Vln II at 19 and the mf close at 22; the S theme has "molto cantabile" at 56, swells onto each appoggiatura, the sub. p at 62 and the poco animando into the 64 peak; the climax 158–162 has the tenutos on E6 and A♭6 in every part with the tune and staggered brass exits; the coda descends pp – ppp with "morendo" only in the violas, whose E♮ is the last event. Each was weighed by earlier expression critics (c378, c458, c468, c478) and nothing in MOLA bears against them.
