## Did cycles 59–78 improve the movement?
**v70 → v88 (cycles 51–68).** These cycles changed details only:
- dynamics audits and notation;
- breaths and rests;
- countermelody re-pitchings at 57, 65 and 66;
- Fl 1 in unison at 23–25 and at the recap equivalent;
- the cello motto in diminution at 58 and 152;
- subito p at 62 and 84.

Of these, a listener would notice only the Fl 1 change, which keeps A♭6 fresh for 31, and the subito p marks. **The subito p marks were never heard in the audio** (finding 1).

**v88 → v98 (cycles 70–78).** This period made the only structural change of the last 20 cycles, the theme combination at 103–110. It is a net gain:
- the development gains its one contrapuntal idea;
- it gains its first string-less bars;
- it gains a real D–E–F–G key ascent toward the G pedal.

It also added the wind answers at 37–40 (a small gain), the Bsn 1 answer at 144–145 (neutral) and the MIDI tempo map (a gain, with proportion faults, finding 2).

**Nothing from cycles 61–78 made the written music worse, so I recommend no reversion.** Two faults in how the score is realised in the audio date from cycle 59 (MIDI subito p, carried through cycles 68 and 78) and from cycle 78 (ritardando shape). I fixed both in the scratch copy rather than reverting anything.

## Findings

**1. The audio ignores every "sub. p", every sf and fp, and all harp right-hand dynamics (applied).**
- **Bars:**
  - 62: all parts;
  - 84: Vc and Cb;
  - 20: Vln I sf;
  - 33: Cb fp;
  - harp RH: 47–68, 156–161 and 166–170.
- **Problem:** the `dynamicAbsoluteVolumeFunction` in score.ly maps only ppp–fff. `\subP` is a markup dynamic, and sf and fp are unmapped, so all three fall back to LilyPond's default volume of 0.71. In this score's scale that is above ff (0.72 × 127 ≈ 91 against 90). Measured in v98 score.midi:
  - Vln I/II at 62 jump from velocity 88 to 90, so the subito p sounds like the top of the crescendo;
  - Cb at 33–39 plays at velocity 79 under a printed fp;
  - the harp RH has no dynamics of its own (they print under the LH), so all 134 RH notes play at velocity 90 under the pp/p second theme at 47–55, against LH 53.

  Dan judges the music partly by score.mp3, so the most important tune in the movement has been heard with a loud harp, and the cycle-59 subito p has never sounded.
- **Fix (score.ly, \midi block only; the PDF is unchanged):**
  ```
  dynamicAbsoluteVolumeFunction = #(lambda (d) (if (string? d)
    (assoc-get d '(("ppp" . 0.25) ("pp" . 0.33) ("p" . 0.42) ("mp" . 0.5) ("mf" . 0.58)
                   ("f" . 0.66) ("ff" . 0.72) ("fff" . 1.0) ("sf" . 0.66) ("fp" . 0.42)) #f)
    0.42)) }
  \context { \PianoStaff \consists "Dynamic_performer" }
  ```
  Also, dev.ily line 260: the Vln II lower divisi voice (107–110) had no dynamic and played at 90 against the upper voice's 41. Change it to `{ b1\pp | g4( a2.) | … | c'1\< }`. The pp prints once, as before.
- **Result:** a note-by-note comparison shows changes only at 19–22, 33–39, 58–63, 84, 107–110 and the harp. Harp RH now follows the LH (53 at 47). Vln I at 62 plays 53.

**2. The MIDI ritardandi undershoot the following tempo and slow down too early (applied, global.ily).**
- **Problem:**
  - Bars 45–46 and 145–146: "poco rit." drops 126 → 104, but the next tempo is Poco meno mosso 112. The audio therefore speeds up by 8% on the downbeat of a meno mosso (47 and 147).
  - Bars 66–68: "rit." loses 7% on its first half-bar and ends at 80, 29% below the 112 base. That is deeper than the largamente/rit. at 164–170.
  - All the ritardandi use equal bpm steps, so most of the slowing comes at the start.
- **Evidence:**
  - Friberg & Sundberg: performed and listener-preferred ritardandi keep the tempo nearly steady at first and slow mostly at the end (q ≈ 2–3). Their measured end points (0.32–0.51) apply to final ritardandi only. Internal ones are shallower.
  - Tchaikovsky 6/i 94–97 and 134–141 pairs a short ritenuto with an immediate return, not a deep fall.
- **Fix:** steps from v(x) with q = 2. Each poco rit. now ends just above the meno mosso tempo.
  - 45–46 and 145–146: `\mt 124 s2 \mt 121 s2 \mt 118 s2 \mt 114 s2`
  - 66–68: `\mt 116 s2 \mt 111 s2 \mt 105 s2 \mt 100 s2 \mt 94 s2 \mt 88 s2`
  - 169–170: `\mt 98 s2 \mt 92 s2 \mt 87 s2 \mt 81 s2`
  - 181–182: `\mt 70 s2 \mt 65 s2 \mt 59 s2 \mt 53 s2`
- **Unchanged:**
  - allargando and sostenuto at 121–126 (120/114/106 → 100: in proportion to the fff tam-tam bar);
  - largamente at 164 (100);
  - the fermata rest at 174 (≈1.5 s, three times the beat);
  - the last bar at 30 (60% of Lento, within the final-ritardando range).

  No printed tempo word changes. Duration goes from 446.6 s to 445.1 s.

**3. Weakest passage 1 (what a listener notices most): the second theme is heard twice in full in the recapitulation (147–161, protected; no fix).**
- **Problem:** the recap's second group runs 24 bars against the exposition's 22. The head is heard at 147 (pp, sul tasto) and again at 156 within about 30 seconds, then the climax continues the same tune.
- **Comparison:** in Tchaikovsky 6/i (form_analysis.csv), the exposition's second group spans onsets 350–638 (bars 89–160, 72 bars). The recap keeps only 2nd-A, onsets 1214–1334 (bars 304–334, 30 bars, 42%). The tune returns once, scored at its fullest.
- **Why no fix:** 147–155 has been protected since cycle 17. Cycle 30 already logged the attention risk. I have no new evidence beyond this proportion, so I note it and do not re-raise it.

**4. Weakest passage 2: the development opening is stop-start (77–84, protected; no fix).**
- **Problem:** the bars run as follows:
  - solo motto relay (77–79);
  - isolated accented single notes (80–81);
  - one ff bar (82);
  - two chords (83);
  - a bare subito-p octave (84).

  That is four separate gestures in eight bars before the fugato. With finding 1 applied, the sub. p at 84 now sounds, which makes the break clearer and, I think, better.
- **Comparison:** Tchaikovsky 6/i starts its development (bar 161) with a single ff blow and then continuous fugal motion.
- **Why no fix:** cycles 21, 62 and 77 protect it, and each new gesture is motivic.

**5. Weakest passage 3: the f wind statement at 103–106 is light after the ff tutti (notes and f/p contrast protected; no fix).**
- **Problem:** the first-theme head is carried by Ob 2 + Cl 2 in unison at A4–B5 against Hn 1 at mf. Coming straight after 97–102 at ff with full strings, it reads as a gap rather than a change of colour.
- **Comparison:** Tchaikovsky 6/i 125–129 (woodwinds alone) uses several octaves.
- **Why no fix:**
  - A flute octave above would reach B6, which is reserved for the climaxes (cycle 9).
  - A lower octave would cross Hn 1's tenor line.
  - I recommend leaving it as it stands. The contrast is cycle 74's deliberate design.

**6. Stale header comment in dev.ily, lines 4–6 (applied).** The comment still names "Vln, Ob1", "Hn1, Vla, Bsn1" and "Vln I, Cl1" for 103–110. I changed it to the actual scoring: Ob 2 + Cl 2; Hn 1, Bsn 1; Fl 1 low + Va; Vc, Bsn 2.
