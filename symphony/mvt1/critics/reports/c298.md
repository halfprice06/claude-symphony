## Method

- I dumped every note, dynamic, hairpin and text event of full.ly with LilyPond's event listener and built a per-note table of pitch and current dynamic.
- I scanned the table against principles 1, 2, 4, 8, 13, 14 and 15:
  - oboe pp in B♭3–F4
  - oboe at C6 or above below f
  - flute at A6 or above below f
  - bassoon and contrabassoon in their lowest fifth below p
  - entries after rests that have no dynamic
- None of these is breached. Bsn 1's C2 at 166 is at p, which the book allows with a firm attack.
- I then read the score page by page at 100 dpi to judge the expressive text and shaping, which a scan cannot check.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 45–46, Cl 1 link into the second theme: add "dolce" and a diminuendo into the hand-off (applied)

**Problem.**
- c274 moved this link from Ob 1 to Cl 1. It now lies in B♭4–A♭4–G4–F4–E♭4, which is concert A♭4 down to F4, written B♭4 to G4: the clarinet's throat register.
- It carries only p and the general "poco rit.". It is the only soloist in these bars, over strings at pp, and it hands its last note to Hn 1's mp E♭3 pickup on beat 4 of 46.
- With no expressive word and no shaping, the player has no instruction except "soft and slowing". The last E♭4 lands at the same level as the start of the phrase, just as the horn enters.

**Evidence.**
- Adler says the throat tones are "rather pale" (PDF 210). The weak spot needs a word telling the player how to colour it, not more volume.
- His soft clarinet examples on PDF 211–212 (Ex. VII-63, among others) give a soft line "dolce" with a hairpin that tapers the phrase end.
- PDF 187: a slurred phrase is one breath unit. A diminuendo over the last slurred notes is how the breath unit closes.
- Elsewhere this score marks its other soft woodwind solos: Bsn 1 "espr." at 33, C.a. "espr." at 171, trombones "dolce" at 175. The link is the exception.

**Fix.** Marks only. Notes and the p level, protected by c161 and c274, are unchanged.

```
\partCombineSoloI \soloStemsOn bes'2(\p_\markup \italic "dolce" aes'4. g'8 | f'2\> es'4)\! r4 \soloStemsOff \partCombineAutomatic |
```

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-298/work/sections/trans.ily. I checked the render: "dolce" sits under the p, and the hairpin ends on E♭4 before Hn 1's entry.

### 2. Bars 175–177, Cl 1 coda line: add "espr." (applied)

**Problem.**
- At 175–177 Cl 1 has the only moving line: E♭4 up to C5, then C5 to B4. Under it are the bass clarinet, trombones and strings, all at pp.
- The trombones are marked "dolce" and the bass trombone "dolce". At 177 Vln I takes the line as "pp espr." (c278).
- The clarinet is also at pp but has no word. In print it reads as one more voice of the chord, not as the line the violins continue.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 212: the clarinet keeps its full dynamic range down to a niente attack, so pp is right and stays. What tells a soft wind solo apart from soft accompaniment in his examples is the expressive word: the cor anglais "mf espressivo" on PDF 205–206, and "pp tranquillo assai" and "dolce" for the clarinet on PDF 211–212.
- PDF 201: when two lines meet at one dynamic, the line must be marked so it is not taken for a doubling.

**Protection answered.** HISTORY protects the coda's notes and dynamics, including "Cl 1 at 175–177". This fix changes neither. It adds the same word that c278 gave Vln I at 177, so the hand-off reads as one line in two instruments.

**Fix.**

```
R1*4 | r2. es'4(\pp_\markup \italic "espr." | c''1~ | c''2 b'2\>) |
```

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-298/work/sections/coda.ily. Rendered page 18: "pp espr." sits under the E♭4 and lines up with the trombones' "pp dolce".

### 3. Bars 115–116, flutes and oboe enter at f inside the strings' "mp cresc." (no change; it stays)

**What I checked.**
- Fl 1·2 enter at f on C6–E♭6, doubling Vln I's line, which is at mp<. Ob 1 joins at f an octave lower.
- At 116 the clarinets and trumpets are at ff while the strings and horns reach ff only at 117. This looks like uneven dynamics.

**Why it stays.**
- Adler PDF 185 places C6–E♭6 in the flute's clear, brilliant middle-upper range. It carries well at f. A soft flute entry there would be heard as a separate colour sitting on top, not as a reinforcement of the line.
- The oboe at C5–E♭5 is in its best range (PDF 198).
- The strings are at mp only on paper: they are mid-crescendo toward ff at 117. A wind entry that already carries the target level is how the crescendo chain 110→117 was designed (c143, protected).
- Lowering the flutes to mp would also need a second hairpin inside the protected wedge 111–116.

**Verdict.** Follows the reading. No change.

### 4. Bars 171–174, cor anglais p against the solo cello's mp swells (no change; it stays)

**What I checked.** The cor anglais answers at plain p with "espr.", while the solo cello has mp with swells.

**Why it stays.**
- Adler PDF 205: the cor anglais's low and middle registers carry with expressive power at a lower marked level. C5 here is mid-register.
- HISTORY protects "C.a. answers at plain p" and the solo/background grading at 171–174. The reason is that the cello leads the duet. The reading supports that reason rather than answering it.

**Verdict.** No change.
