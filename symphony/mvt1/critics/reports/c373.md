## Findings

No finding justifies a change. The items below are ranked by how much they bear on the movement's form, and each ends with one recommendation.

### 1. Climax hierarchy 122 < 160 and the recapitulation's first group 131–143: better than at v283; keep

At v283 the concern (c283 Finding 1) was that 139–141 were denser than 160–161. Three changes since then answer it in the printed dynamics. c318: the upper parts fade from 123, so 122's fff plateau lasts one bar. c354: the held horns and trumpets stay f at 139–141 under the ff tune. c337: Cl 1 moves to the clarion at 156–157, so the climax counter is heard before 158. By mass 139–142 (2446–2810) and 158–161 (2727–2806) are still alike, because both are full tuttis. The difference lies where Adler puts it. A colour is strong when it is fresh (PDF 263–264), and the orchestra is held back before the climax (PDF 267). At 158, fourteen players come back after 11–15 bars' rest, the longest rests before any tutti in the movement. At 131 seventeen come back after only 4–7 bars, and 135–137 thin the recapitulation out again. Only 160 has the violins' octave doubling at A♭6 (Bizet, PDF 279). Recommendation: keep 131–166 as written. Do not undo c318, c354 or c337. They are the reason the hierarchy now holds in the printed levels.

### 2. The forte-subito-piano seams follow Ex. XV-36; keep

Adler's rule (PDF 190) is that the soft group must already be sounding when the loud chord stops. I checked every sudden drop:
- 72→73: Vc A♭3 and Cb drop to pp on beat 3 of 72, and Vln II/Vla move on beat 4 (c173 cites the same example).
- 134→135: trombones, tuba and timpani stop, and the woodwinds, horns and strings carry on at mf (c129, c143).
- 143→144: the winds' ff eighth stops, and the strings carry on f, then mp under Bsn 1.
- 32→33: the Cb fp is held under the tutti eighth (the sforzando device of PDF 189–190).

The two drops that do not overlap, 83→84 and 170→171, are separated by rests or a fermata. They are the "orchestral interruptions" of the accompanied recitative (PDF 276, Ex. XVII-17, where Mendelssohn's chords stop dead before the voice). Recommendation: keep all six.

### 3. Stop chord 138 at f, where 26 and 170 are ff: keep

The three stop chords rhyme in layout (protected; the finale quotes them). Only 138 is marked f. It looks like a weaker "interruption" (PDF 276). It is right where it is. The hemiola at 135–137 rises from mf, and 139 must start at f so that it can grow to the ff N6 at 140. A ff stop chord at 138 would turn the move to iv at 139 into a drop. That would weaken the Neapolitan turn that c223, c233 and c311 protect, and it would raise the first group further toward 158 (Finding 1). Recommendation: keep c108's f at 138.

### 4. Development pacing 77–130: keep

The development has three loud peaks, at 82, 97–102 and 117–123. After each one the texture drops at once, to two players at 84, 26 pairs at 103 and 10 at 127. Each drop is followed by a build: the fugato 85–96, the wedge 111–116, and the stretto 127–130 with its rhythmic acceleration in the cellos (crotchets, quavers, triplets, semiquavers). The single cello line at 85–87 is the movement's thinnest texture. Ex. XV-42 and its text (PDF 195) explain why the sparse dialogue at 77–81 still sounds full. 85–87 is a different case: a fugato subject alone, which needs no colour change. The Mozart excerpt (PDF 267) keeps the accompaniment sparse so that the soloist has freedom. Recommendation: keep. The fugato and the Cb rest at 85–89 are protected (c227, c257).

### 5. Registral distance from woodwind and solo lines at 73–76 and 172: keep

PDF 274 asks for the accompaniment to stand clear of a woodwind solo. At 73–76 Fl 1 plays the motto between C5 and A♭5 (C5 pickup at 73.4, A♭5 at 74, down to E♭5–F♭5 at 75). Vln II holds E♭4, G♭4 and F♭4 in whole notes, a sixth or more below, with Hn 4 A♭3 and the low strings under them. The flute is clear in register and in rhythm. At 172 the viola's B3→C4 meets the solo cello's C4 for one beat, as c283 noted. It is the leading tone resolving, and the cello leaves at once. Both passages are protected (flute motto 73–76; coda notes 167–182). Neither touches the form. Recommendation: keep both.

### 6. Fresh solo colours after tuttis (PDF 263–264, 267): keep

Every soft solo that follows a tutti is reduced at once, as in Ex. XVII-10: Bsn 1 at 33 and 144, Fl 1 at 73, B.cl at 77, the cellos at 85, the solo cello and C.a. at 171. Every solo that opens a section comes in after a contrasting colour, as in Ex. XVII-7/8: Hn 1 at 46.4 after Cl 1, the C.a. at 147 after 44 bars' rest, Vln I at 177 after six bars' rest. One case runs against the letter of Mozart's flute-concerto remark ("no orchestral flutes ... no colour competition"). Fl 1 plays the codetta's top line at 69–72 and then the motto at 73. But the contrast there is the strongest the flute can give: from f in the third octave (A♭6) to p on C5, an octave and a sixth lower, after three beats' rest. The 69–72 doublings are protected (c131, c279, c331, c347). Recommendation: keep.

### 7. Coda 167–182: keep

The coda follows chapter 17 throughout:
- The forte interlude 167–170 belongs to the tutti (PDF 267).
- The fermata stop is an interruption (PDF 276).
- At 171 the orchestra is cut to the solo cello, the C.a., Bsn 2 and the violas.
- The chorale canon is lightly scored with octave doublings (Fidelio, PDF 283).
- The timpani pulse at 179–180 joins the chorale to the last motto (Ex. XVII-13, PDF 273).

Its 13.7 % of the time against 8.8 % of the bars is explained by the Più lento and the Lento, and it leads attacca into movement II. Recommendation: keep. Coda notes, tempi and length are protected (c228, c239, c313).
