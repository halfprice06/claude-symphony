## Findings

No change to the score is needed. The findings below record what this reading shows about the cyclic design, which coherence facts are missing or out of date, and which passages should be protected for cyclic reasons. Ranked by importance to the symphony as a whole.

### 1. The finale reuses movement I's passing E♭ as its second-theme key, and the two movements together set out both of the unit's plans. Keep 37–44 as it is and record it as a seed. Not a score change.

Movement I follows Beethoven's late sonata plan, I – VI ‖ IV–V – I (A&S PDF 454): C minor (15), A♭ (47), the development closing in F minor (111), the G pedal (117), C (131). The finale follows the standard minor-key plan of Ex. 26-8, I – III – passing IV – V (PDF 449): C minor, E♭ for its second theme (51), F minor (109, and the development's opening at 99), G (to 134), C. finale.md already cites K. 457, the piece behind Ex. 26-8. The symphony therefore states the unit's two main tonal plans for minor once each, the first in the first movement and the second in the last.

The link is in movement I's transition. Over a B♭ pedal (Cb B♭1 at 39–40, timpani B♭2 roll at 40) the music arrives at E♭ at 41 with the four-horn call and the E♭ timpani roll, then adds D♭ at 44 (V4/2 of A♭) so that E♭ becomes the dominant of the real goal. This is A&S's transient modulation (PDF 447–448, Ex. 26-7): the C-minor "tonic" in the Mozart trio becomes a pivot to the real goal, and the progression that foreshadows it comes first. The finale's transition (v217, 43–50) holds the same B♭1 in the basses (B♭2 in the cellos) and this time takes it to E♭ minor at 51. A goal that was passing in movement I becomes a real key in the finale. This is the reading's foreshadowing principle (PDF 448) applied across movements.

Also at symphony scale (PDF 456, section 16), the movement keys C – A♭ – E – C return to the tonic without a dominant between III and IV. The finale supplies one with its first chord: the German sixth on A♭ resolving to V (finale 1–2). It is movement I's bar 12 (Ger6 → V at 12–13), with A♭5 on top in Vln I and E♭4 in the violas in both. So the finale begins by going through VI to V, the end of movement I's own plan.

Recommendation: add "37–44" to the seeds list in coherence/mvt1.md (text in Cross-movement effects). Treat the B♭1 pedal, the E♭ arrival at 41 and the D♭ at 44 as protected for this reason. The transition route is already protected (c120: route 36–47; c163: 33–46; c233: 41–47).

### 2. Movement I introduces C major but never confirms it at forte, which leaves the finale's arrival as the first strong confirmation. Keep.

A&S say a key is introduced by a pivot and confirmed by a cadential progression with II or IV and V (PDF 443). In movement I's recapitulation, C major (147) is confirmed only softly: V–I at 154→155 with pizzicato basses at pp, and V–I at 165→166 at p, where Hn 3 takes B3 to C4 and the violas immediately turn E4 to E♭4. The loud approach goes elsewhere: V → ♭VI at 159→160 (fff) and V → vi at 161→162. The stop chord B–D–F–A♭ appears three times and never resolves to C major: to i at 26→27, to iv at 138→139, and held over a C pedal under a fermata at 170. Tpt 1·2 end on that B4. In A&S's terms this is a small progression stated several times that foreshadows a larger event (PDF 448). The larger event is the finale's 222→223, where the same chord resolves to C major with Tpt 1 B4→C5 at pitch. The design follows the reading and holds back what the finale needs.

A check of v240 confirms that nothing in movement I uses the reserved items. Vln I's highest note is A♭6 (at 31, 72, 122, 160) and Vln II's is B♭5, so no C7. Tuba's highest note is F2 and its last is C2 at 170. The trumpets end on B4/F4 at 170. Trumpet B→C occurs only into C minor (26→27), into F minor (138→139), inside the P head (28, 161, 168) and over a C-minor 6/3 at 113→114, never on a C-major arrival. The cymbal is used only at 159–160, the tam-tam only at 122 and 175, and there is no muted or stopped brass. The protections on the climax harmony 158–166 and on "do not restore a C-major codetta after 162" (c063) should stay for this cyclic reason as well.

Movement II's series-3 changes (v202–v228) do not touch bars 1–6. Its v224 change is in its own ending.

This is the relation A&S name in PDF 443: A♭ is VI of C minor and relates to C major through mixture. The join works because the violas' E♮ is turned straight back to E♭. Ex. 26-4 (PDF 446) shows the same move inside one key, with the C tonic reread as III of A♭. Movement I's last bars already contain movement II's first move: the violas go C4→A♭3 at 178 (movement II's cellos go C3→A♭2), and the violas hold E♭4 at 179–181 before the E4 at 182. The new key's tonic is approached with 3̂ (C5) on top, which Ex. 26-4 describes as one of the two strong melodic arrivals. Nothing should change here.
