## What I studied

**Books.** Besides the assigned unit: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, PDF 198 (the oboe's lowest fifth "tends to 'honk'… one should never require a pianissimo dynamic in this register"; oboists need "frequent rest periods"), PDF 205 (English horn registers) and PDF 39 (fewer notes per bow at forte, cited by c195). MOLA *Guidelines*, PDF 6 (title and composer on the cover; title, composer and instrument on each part).

**Corpus.** Grieg, *Peer Gynt* "Åse's Death" (`reference-corpus/mutopia/GriegE_O46/02-lamortdase-strings/…/violiniI.ly`, `viole.ly`). In bars 27–28, 31–32, 35–36 and 39–40 the first violins tie a note over the barline into a suspension: `a2)\! ~ | a4\decr^\downbow ( g fis4.)`. The slur ends at the barline, a new down-bow starts on the suspended note, and the diminuendo starts there, so the resolution is softer. The violas do the same at bar 30 (`b2\decr ( ais4.)`). This is A&S's rule at PDF 348 written as bowing and dynamics, and it is the model for Findings 1 and 2. Mozart Jupiter IV (`musicxml-converted/ly/Mozart_Sym41_mvt4.ly`) is A&S's Ex. 21-1. I compared it with 131–134: tied half notes against the subject, with no accents on the tied parts.

**Web.** C.P.E. Bach, *Versuch*, I.2.6 §10 (https://versuch.cpebach.org/v-html/part-I/chapter-2/section-6/I-2-6-p10.html): "the dotted note is always played strongly, the next one and the main note are struck softly." Leopold Mozart's slur rule, as summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_(musical_ornament) and https://blog.oup.com/?p=126239: the first note of a slurred group is accented and held, and the rest are slurred on to it with a diminuendo. The 18th-century performance treatises therefore state the same rule as A&S PDF 348.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 124–125: one bow from fff to p across the cadential 6/4 (applied; closes c195)
**Problem.** Ob 1 and Vln I have one slur, `g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8 | es''4. d''8 c''2)`. It lasts 4.8 s at sostenuto and starts from the fff of 122–123. At 125 the upper parts reach the cadential 6/4 over the G pedal (Vln II E♭, Vla/Vc C over G). That 6/4 is a double suspension, whose 4 is delayed to 126.2 (Vla, Vc, Cl 2, Hn 3 `c'4( b2.)`). The phrase's dissonant point is therefore 125.1, but the bow there is running out, so 125 is the weakest part of the stroke.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348 §4: the dissonance takes the stress and the resolution is softer. Delayed resolution builds tension: PDF 359 §21, the resolution here falls at 126. Grieg "Åse's Death" bars 27–28: a new down-bow on the suspended note, with the diminuendo starting there. Adler PDF 39 (c195): fewer notes per bow at forte. Fl 1, the third doubling, already ends its slur at the end of 124. Splitting both Ob 1 and Vln I keeps cycle 88's rule (identical slurs in doubled parts).
**Fix (dev.ily, devOboeOne and devVlnOne):** `g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8) | es''4.( d''8 c''2) | d''1\p |`. The hairpin still runs from 124 to the p at 126. No pitch or rhythm changes.

### 2. Bar 44: Hn 1's 7–6 suspension has no shaping (applied)
**Problem.** The horn-call echo `r2 es'4(\p c''4~ | c''2 bes'2)` ties C5 into 44, where the bass moves to D♭ (V4/2 of A♭: Cb D♭, Vc G, Vla B♭, Vln II/Hn 2 E♭). C over D♭ is a prepared 7–6, which is the 19th-century 7/6 delay of the fifth over V7 (A&S PDF 353–354 §15, J. Strauss Ex. 21-15f). At plain p the dissonance and its resolution are level. The viola's pp B♭3 also anticipates the resolution an octave lower. A&S PDF 352 §12 allows this when timbre and dynamics contrast, and the contrast is stronger if the horn falls away into its B♭.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348 §4; C.P.E. Bach §10; Grieg bars 27–28. The call itself at 42 already ends `c'4 bes2.)\>`.
**Fix (trans.ily):** Hn 1 `c''2\> bes'2) | R1\! |`; Hn 2 (shared staff, equal dynamics) `g'1\p( | es'1\>) | R1\! | R1 |`. The fill 43–46 keeps its notes and length. Only the dynamic shape inside the protected MC/fill changes.

### 3. C.a. 5–9: "breathe ad lib." in the part (applied, part only; closes c196)
The cor anglais holds one slur from 5.4 to 9.3: 16 beats, about 19 s at q = 50, p→pp, ending in a held G that is handed to Vln I at 9.4 (c150, protected). Adler PDF 198 says oboists need breathing points. The breath belongs after the resolution: the motto's A♭→G at 7.3–7.4 is the dissonance and its release (A&S PDF 348), so it must stay in one breath, and a breath inside the held G in bar 8 is covered by the cellos' G3. **Fix (intro.ily):** `g'1~\pp-\tag #'part ^\markup \small \italic "(breathe ad lib.)" | g'2.) r4 |`. The score is unchanged (`\removeWithTag #'part` is already applied to the C.a.), and the text prints in parts-05-cor-anglais.pdf only.

### 4. Ob 1 link 45–46: keep on the oboe (decided, no change; closes c196)
`bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4)` touches F4 and E♭4. Adler PDF 198 forbids pianissimo in the oboe's lowest fifth, and this is marked p, not pp. Only the last two notes lie at or below F4, and they end the phrase on its resolution, where A&S (PDF 348) wants the tone softest. The oboe's natural weight on E♭4 is therefore a small cost. With Finding 2 the horns now fall away into 45, so the oboe enters a thinner texture. Moving the link to the cor anglais would put the motto's colour (1–8, 171–174, answers 147–153, protected by c160/c190) on a non-motto link. That would weaken the identification that the movement's cyclic design depends on, and it would undo c161's oboe choice.

### 5. Vln II 150 `<f'' a''>`: keep (decided, no change; closes c194)
The change avoids a unison with the C.a.'s D5, the goal note of its answer, so that note is heard in its own colour (Adler PDF 283, cited by c190). The added F is the 7th of V under the melody's 4–3 (C→B). It is not left unresolved: its resolution is transferred to the melody's E at 151, which A&S treat as normal in free textures (PDF 360–361 §22, Ex. 21-28). The pp sul tasto dynamic keeps it as background, as in the Pergolesi example (PDF 357).

### 6. Tpt 1 A♭5 at 72: keep (decided, no change; closes c174)
The A♭5 is the peak of the codetta tune, which Tpt 1 doubles (protected in cycles 14, 24, 103 and 174). Here it is an accented f quarter inside a 4-bar f statement. At 160 it is a fff note sustained for 1½ beats with the crash cymbal, largamente and the violins' first A♭6. Dynamic and duration already separate the two events, so the climax hierarchy holds. c174's fix (`es''4->`) would leave Ob 1 alone on the tune's peak and take the brass colour out of the tune at its top note, against c88's rule for doubled statements.

### 7. Composer name on the title page (decided: none printed; closes c155/c185)
MOLA PDF 6 asks for the composer's name on the cover and on the parts. No project file (plan, BRIEF, LOOP-METHOD, mvt II headers) names an author, and mvt II prints none either. Printing a presumed name would be a false attribution, so the score and parts keep title, movement, duration and instrumentation and leave the composer field unset in both movements. This is a decision, not a deferral. No edit.

### 8. Note only, no change: equal accents on the 4–3 at 32 and 142, and on 131–134
At 32 and 142 both the C and the B carry accents and down-bows. A&S PDF 348 would soften the B. These are tutti hammer-stroke cadences after ff, not lyrical suspensions, and the bowing scheme 15–32/131–142 is protected (c195). At 131–134 the accents mark the syncopes of the Jupiter-type counterline (Ex. 21-1, Ex. 21-32) rather than the resolutions as such. The violin C at 132.2 is itself dissonant against the trombones' B, so it is a new stress, not a release. Both are kept.
