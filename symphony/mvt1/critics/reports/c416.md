# Critic 416: Playability, instrument by instrument (v416)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 3 "Individual Bowed String Instruments" (adler1 PDF 71–109), second pass. I read every text page from PDF 71 to 109 in full. I rendered all 39 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one. That covers the tuning and range examples (III-1, III-2, III-27 to III-29, III-52 to III-54, III-65 to III-67), the fingering charts (III-3, PDF 86, PDF 99, III-70), the double-, triple- and quadruple-stop tables (III-9 to III-12, III-33 to III-35, III-56 to III-58), the harmonics tables (III-13 to III-16, III-61, III-72) and every literature example. Among those examples are Brahms 1/iv (the open G), Tchaikovsky 5/ii sul G, Scheherazade sul D, Brahms 3/iii sul A, Meistersinger, the Bartók and Berg skips, Lohengrin, Beethoven 5/ii, Tristan, William Tell, and Beethoven 4/iv with Adler's simplified bass (Ex. III-68/69).

Method. c236 and c326 already checked this chapter against the score, and c326 did it systematically (146 string chords, 32 leaps, every bow unit over 5 s). I did not repeat its prose. I re-ran the checks on v416's full.midi with my own scratch scripts (in /tmp/critic-416/s). They list the range of each string part, every simultaneous string chord, every leap of an octave or more with less than a dotted-quaver gap, every bar of quavers in the basses together with its cello doubling, and every held note over 5.5 s, measured against the MIDI tempo map. I then read the section files at each place the scripts flagged. I looked hardest at the string changes made after c326: c338 bowings, c341 Cb sul A, c358/c368/c378 dynamics, c371 viola at 44, c384/c402 Vln II at 154–155, c392 Vc G2 at 83, c401 Vln at 61, c402 Cb D at 137.4, c406 Vla up-bow at 11 and Vln II "non div." at 122, and c411 Vln I sul D at 9–10. I also built the parts and looked at the string part pages.

## Findings

I found no place in v416 where the string writing goes against this chapter. I propose no score change. Below are the checks made since c326 and the places a critic might be tempted to change, each with the reason it should stay as it is.

### 1. Held notes and bow lengths (PDF 108; also PDF 73–74): no change

The tempo-mapped scan finds 16 string notes longer than 5.5 s. Every double-bass note in that list has "change bow at will" (3.4, 13.1, 41.1, 72.3, 117.1, 179.1). So do the cello A♭3 at 72.3–76 (c375 moved the mark to 73) and the four upper strings' last notes at 181–182 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc; about 12.8 s with the fermata). Those last notes tie into movement II, and movement II continues "change bow at will" on Vln I's C5 (coherence/mvt2.md, bar 1). Three units over 5.5 s remain unmarked: Vln I E♭5 178–179.3 (7.9 s, pp, sul D), Vln II G4 175–176 (6.7 s, pp) and Vln I 12.4 (6.0 s, which has its marked bow change from c226). They are within one violin bow at pp. Adler gives the change-bow rule for the shorter, heavier bass bow (PDF 108), so they should stay unmarked.

### 2. Changes since c326 checked against the reading: no change

- **Vln I sul D at 9.4–10 (c411).** G4→E♭5 on the D string is a slurred shift of a minor sixth (D string +5 → +13) at mp in the Lento. Adler's problem skips (PDF 82–83, Ex. III-22 to III-24) are leaps of an octave or more taken fast, between extremes, or across strings in legato. A one-string sixth at crotchet = 50 is the expressive shift his D-string and G-string examples rely on (PDF 74–75, Ex. III-5, III-6). The mark is kept, and it matches 55.4 and 177.
- **Cb sul A at 2–3 and 176–178 (c341).** On the A string E♭2, D2, D♭2 and C2 lie at +6, +5, +4 and +3 semitones, which is half to third position on the Ex. III-70 chart (PDF 106). That is an easy stopped line, and it avoids the open D string under a pp motto (PDF 73–74). Kept.
- **Vc G2 on the ff strokes at 83 (c392).** This is the open G string, struck and short at ff. Adler's warning about open strings concerns slow, soft lines (PDF 73). An accented open string at ff only adds resonance. Kept.
- **Vln II at 154–155 (c384, c402).** E5/G5, D5/G5, B4/G5 tremolo, then C5/E5. It falls inside the "div. sul tasto" span that starts at 147, so each player has one note (PDF 76). Kept.
- **Vln II "non div." fff at 122–123 (c406).** D5/A♭5 is a tritone on the A and E strings (fingers 4–3, first position) and D5/G5 a fourth (4–2). Both are secco-or-sonorous double stops of the kind PDF 76 allows, and dividing them would halve the top of the fff chord. Kept.
- **Vla double up-bow at 11 (c406), Vla B♭ at 44.3 (c371), bowings at 45, 54, 143–144, 166.3 (c338).** Bowing marks only. They fit the bow-direction plan that c226/c316 set from Adler ch. 2. Kept.

### 3. Double stops not under div. (PDF 76, 85, 88, 97, 107): no change

I fitted each remaining chord to its strings. Vln I B4/A♭5 at 83 is a sixth shape, fingers 1 and 3 on the A and E strings. Vln II D5/F5 at 83 is 4–1 across the A and E strings. Vla C4/E♭4 at 12–13 and D4/F4 at 17, 32, 40 and 170 are first-position thirds on the G and D strings. Vla F4/B4 at 26 and 138 is a 2–1 tritone. Vla B3/D4 at 122–123 uses the open D. Vla E♭4/G4 at 41–42 and 131–132 and C4/G4 at 158 need second or third position, but they are loud tremolo or repeated-quaver accompaniment held in one hand position, the sonorous use PDF 76 allows (kept by c236 and c326). Vc B2/A♭3 at 26 is a major-sixth hand shape, so it is not wider than the sixth Adler warns about in the low register (PDF 97). The basses have no double stops (PDF 107). Every chord that would need the 4th position or higher is marked div. (Vln I 167–170, Vln II 107–116 and 147–155, Vla 47–55, 62–68 and 166, Vc 124–126).

### 4. Cello octave F4→F5 inside the S head at 52.4 (PDF 82–83, 97): no change

This is the only legato octave leap in an exposed soft string line. Adler lists legato wide skips among the hard cases for a section. But the leap also belongs to the protected S theme ("cello high notes and theme leaps 47–55", c186/c296), and Adler's own cello text answers the problem. In thumb position the cellist "can stretch to octave double-stops on adjacent strings with relative ease" (PDF 97). The A♭4–G4–F4 before the leap can be taken in that position, so F4 (D string) and F5 (A string) lie under one hand. Horn 1 doubles the line. Keep it.

### 5. Basses doubling fast cello lines (PDF 105, Ex. III-68/69): no change

Adler's Beethoven 4 example shows that basses doubling a fast cello figure blur it, and his fix gives the basses a thinned version. In v416 every bar of bass quavers is a repeated pitch (27–31, 82, 131–134, 140–142, 167–169), and the bass sustains G1 under the cellos' semiquaver figure 117–130. Adler's fix is already in place throughout.

### 6. Parts for the strings: no change

I rebuilt the parts. Violin 1, Violin 2, Viola, Violoncello and Horn 3 are two pages each, a facing spread with no turn, ending on an even page for the attacca. The double-bass part is four pages: a title page, then 2–3 as a spread, then a turn into page 4 during the four bars' rest at 171–174 (about 13 s at crotchet = 72), with page 4 holding 175–182. That turn is safe and should stay.
