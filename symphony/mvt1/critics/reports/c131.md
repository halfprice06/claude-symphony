## Overall verdict on the melody

The motto (G rising a minor sixth to E♭, then D–D♭–C), the first-theme sentence 15–22 and the second-theme head are fixed in pitch and rhythm. They have been confirmed many times (cycles 11, 31, 91, 101). This reading gives no reason to change a pitch. It is about how a tune is presented so that it is heard as the tune. On that point the score is close to Rimsky-Korsakov's rules almost everywhere. Three places are not: the trumpet counterline at 60–63, the codetta 69–72 (two rules on one page, PDF 75), and the wedge 115–116. The fixes below change doublings only. No pitch of any theme, counter or bass changes.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 60–63: a chromatic counterline at p is in the trumpet instead of the horn (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 2 carries the Cl 1 counterline at 56–59 and 64–68. At 60–63 it rests, and Tpt 1 doubles the clarinet instead: F4–E♭–D♭–F♭4–C♭4–C4, with an enharmonic F♭/C♭ turn, marked p and *espressivo*, under the violins' molto cantabile tune. In a wind + brass unison "the tone of the brass predominates" (PDF 74). So for four bars the counterline sounds as a trumpet line.

**Evidence.** PDF 73: "Melodies involving chromatic or enharmonic writing are much less suitable to the character of brass instruments ... brass instruments lack the capacity to express passion or geniality. Phrases charged with these sentiments become sickly and insipid when confided to the brass." PDF 72: the horn's tone "in piano passages affords greater scope." The cited trumpet melodies, Ex. 71 and 72 (PDF 244), are diatonic. Ex. 94 (PDF 270) puts a soft trumpet on a diatonic tune, in unison with the violins and a clarinet; it does not put one on a clarinet's chromatic inner line.

**Answer to the protection.** Cycle 54 added this, and cycle 56 protected it as playable. Its first piece of evidence was Tchaikovsky 6/i 134–141. In the corpus file the trumpets there play three-note rising diatonic scale figures (written G♯–A–B, then F♯–G♯–A), together with the trombones. They do not double a sustained chromatic counterline. Its second piece of evidence was Rimsky's "finer legato" sentence (PDF 75). That sentence is about a wind added to a brass melody, so it supports the brass leading, which is the problem here.

**Fix (applied).**
- `tTwoTptOne = { R1*22 | }`
- In `tTwoHnTwo`, replace `R1*4 | c'2\mp^\markup \bold "2." r8 c'8( d'8 es'8 |` with
  `f'2\< r8 f'8( es'8 des'8 | fes'2) ces'2 | c'4\subP es'8( f'8 g'4 aes'4~ | aes'4\< g'8 f'8) g'2 | c'2\mp r8 c'8( d'8 es'8 |`

Hn 2 now plays 56–68 as one line, which was the layout from cycle 16 to cycle 53. There are eighth rests in bars 56, 58 and 60, the range is F3–A♭4, and the dynamic is p. Tpt 1 now enters first at 69, f, on the codetta tune, so the trumpet colour is fresh there.

### 2. Bars 69–72: the horns' counter has only one woodwind in the octave above (applied)

**Problem.** In the forte codetta, Hn 1·2 in unison carry the first-theme head as the counter (E♭3–F4). In the octave above there is only Cl 1, plus Vln II. Cl 2 holds A♭4 and G4, the same notes as Tpt 2.

**Evidence.** PDF 75 (diagram checked on the image): "if there are two horns playing the lower octave in unison, three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages." The formulas given there are "1 Ob + 1 Cl / 1 Horn" and "2 Fl + 2 Cl / 2 Horns."

**Fix (applied).** Cl 2 doubles Cl 1 in unison, so two clarinets plus Vln II sit above two horns. Cl 2's old A♭/G stay in Tpt 2. In `closClOne`, remove `\partCombineApart`/`\partCombineAutomatic` so the staff prints "a2". Then:

### 3. Bars 69–72: one flute above a trumpet melody (applied)

**Problem.** The tune (the second-theme head) is in Tpt 1 + Ob 1 at C5–A♭5 and in Fl 1 + Vln I at C6–A♭6. Fl 2 fills the inner harmony with E♭5–F5–G5–A♭5. Those notes double Tbn 1 (E♭4, F4) an octave above, and they also sit in Cl 2 and Tpt 2.

**Evidence.** PDF 75: "To double a trumpet in the upper octave three or four wind instruments are required, but in the top register two flutes will suffice." The diagram shows "2 Fl." over "Trumpet." On the same page: "Wood-wind instruments should not be used to double a trombone in the octave above." This is the first and only forte major-key statement of the second theme in the exposition, so it should be heard in the upper octave, as at 56 and 158. It should not become a trumpet tune with a high echo.

**Answer to the protection.** The codetta's tune, counter, bass and Tpt 1 are protected (cycles 11, 54, 103, 106, 117). None of them changes. Only Fl 2 and Cl 2 move between existing lines.

**Fix (applied).**
`closFluteTwo = { c'''2.~(\f c'''8 bes''8 | aes''4 f'''2-- es'''4) | des'''4.( c'''8 bes''4 des'''8 f'''8) | aes'''4-> es'''4-> c'''2-> | R1*4 | }` (prints "a2").

### 4. Bars 115–116: the same rule at the top of the wedge (applied)

**Problem.** Tpt 1 joins the wedge's top line at 113. At 115–116 (D♭5–C–E♭–D♭ | D5 ff) it is in unison with Ob 1 and Fl 2. Only Fl 1 and Vln I are in the octave above.

**Evidence.** PDF 75, as in finding 3.

**Fix (applied).** Fl 2 moves up an octave to join Fl 1 (a2). In `devFluteTwo`, replace `des''4\f c''4 es''4 des''4 | d''1\< |` with `des'''4\f c'''4 es'''4 des'''4 | d'''1\< |`. Ob 1 stays in unison with the trumpet. The pitches of the top line (protected by cycles 21, 62 and 92) are unchanged. Fl 2 drops to F5 at 117 as before.

### Checked and not raised

- 27–32: the bottom octave of the tune is violas only (principle 6). Moving Cl 1 down would put it in its weak throat register under f brass (PDF 66, "the clarinet, weak in the middle compass"). The layout is protected (cycles 14, 90, 121). Leave it.
- 158–161: three octaves with Tpt 1 in the middle (principle 16). The top has two flutes and all the violins, which satisfies PDF 75. Protected.
- 117–122: the augmented motto in horns and trombones is chromatic, but slow and plain. PDF 73 asks brass for "energetic power ... simplicity and eloquence", and Ex. 74 (PDF 245) shows a chromatic brass phrase con forza. This follows the reading.
- 139–142: Ob 2 doubles Tbn 1's held harmony an octave above. This is an inner voice, not melody, so ch. II's rule does not reach it.
