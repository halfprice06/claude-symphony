## Findings

### 1. Horn 4 holds the pp pad 56–62 for about 14 s without a marked breath (applied)

Bars 56–62 (second theme restatement in the violins, ♩ = 112, then poco animando). Horn 4 holds A♭3 | F3 | B♭3 | B♭3 | B♭3< | F♭3 | A♭3, pp to p, from 56.1 to 62.3 with no rest. full.midi gives 14.4 s. Horn 3 on the same staff rests on 60.4, so one of the pair already breathes inside the phrase. Horn 4 does not, and nothing in the score shows where it should.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37: wind players "cannot manage extremely long sustained passages, as they are compelled to take breath; care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time". PDF 44 says this applies "with equal force to the brass". A soft held low horn note uses more air than its dynamic suggests. Cycle 356 added the same kind of mark for Horns 3·4 at 22 and 39 ("at the repeated note"), and the composer accepted it. Here the B♭3 is struck again at 59 and at 60, so a breath costs no note.

Fix (tTwo.ily, tTwoHnFour): `R1*9 | aes1\pp | f1 | bes1 \breathe |` (was `R1*9 | aes1\pp | f1 | bes1 |`). Horn 4 breathes at the end of 58 and Horn 3 at 60.4, so the pad never loses both horns at once. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The mark prints on the Hn 3·4 staff at the end of 58 (score page 6) and in the Horn 4 part.

### 2. Horn 3 plays 164–170 for about 15 s, across the rit. and the f re-entry at 167, with no marked breath (applied)

Bars 164–170. Horn 3 plays A♭3–G3 (164), G3–B3 (165) and C4 mp (166) under the rit., then C4 f at 167–169 in Tempo dell'Allegro with a crescendo to the ff D4 at 170. There is no rest from 164.1 to 170.3 (14.9 s in full.midi). Horn 1·2 have a quaver rest at 167.1, and Horn 4 rests at 164.3–165. Horn 3 is the only horn with no gap, and it has to start the forte section and crescendo to ff at the end of that span.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37 and 44, as in Finding 1. PDF 41 says brass "possess a remarkable capacity for swelling from pianissimo to fortissimo", but a swell needs a full breath, and the crescendo 169–170 comes at the end of the span. The C4 is struck again at 167, so the breath belongs at the end of 166, where the rit. ends and the new tempo begins. This is the same place where Cl 1 already breathes (c096: Cl 1 rests on beat 1 of 167).

Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoHnThree): `R1 | aes2\p( g2~ | g4 b2.) | c'1\mp \breathe |` (was `... c'1\mp |`). No pitch changes. The protected Hn 3 B3→C4 at 165–166 (c222), the 156–166 pitches (c346) and the viola E♮→E♭ join at 166/167 (c437) are untouched. The mark prints on the Hn 3·4 staff before the clef change at the end of 166 (score page 17).

## Checked and kept

- **Cellos above A4 with a skip at 101–102.** F♯4–D5 at ff in tempo breaks the letter of PDF 26 ("skips should always be avoided"). Cycle 296 asked for 8vb, and the composer rejected it because professional cellos have these notes (Adler App. A). I do not reopen it, for three reasons from the same page. The D5 is a half note (one of Rimsky-Korsakov's "long value" cases). The leap is a single sixth onto a held note, not a series of skips. Vln II and the cor anglais double the line at pitch, so an uneven attack is covered.
- **Wind spans over 11 s** that already have a breath mark, a slur break, an eighth rest or a restruck note: C.a. 5–9 ("breathe ad lib." in the part), Bsn 1·2 9–13 (\breathe at 10), Bsn 1 117–124 (\breathe at 120), Ob 1 121–126 (slur break at 124/125, c198), Bsn 2 156–163 (\breathe at 159, 163), Hn 3 61–68 (\breathe at 64), Hn 1·2 117–123 (\breathe at 119).
