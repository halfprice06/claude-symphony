# Critic 076 (lens 6: playability) on v95

## What I studied
I read v95 `sections/dev.ily` bar by bar for 97–116, every pizz./arco change in `tTwo.ily`, `dev.ily` and `recTwo.ily`, the string parts of `recTwo.ily` (147–155) and `coda.ily`, and pages 8–9 and 15 of the PDF. I also grepped every bass clarinet note in the movement. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly`, lines 5603–5606 and 5721–5722). Vln I rests for bars 81–88 and then plays the Andante second theme at 89 "con sordini". Later "alzate sordini" is written over three bars' rest (about 128–130) before "senza sordini". Web sources:
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration* (Gutenberg, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). He calls the flute's low register "dull, cold". He says one wind instrument added in unison to a string section "thickens" the strings, "the wood-wind timbre being lost". He says muted strings "do not combine so well with wood-wind, as the two tone qualities remain distinct".
- cellofun.eu on arco/pizz. changes (https://cellofun.eu/home/cello-blog-encyclopaedia/mechanics-technique/right-hand-string-players/pizzicato/changing-between-arco-and-pizzicato/): an up-bow before a pizz. brings the hand to the string, and players pluck while still holding the bow.
- A MuseScore thread quoting the standard reference (https://musescore.org/node/213391): student bass clarinets go down to written E♭, and professional instruments are built to low C.

I could not reach Adler's text online. Nothing below depends on it.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. 97–106, oboe 1: ten bars at ff/f with a single eighth rest. Applied.**
Oboe 1 plays the B-minor tune at ff from 97 to 102, up to d''' in 102. It then plays the first-theme head at f in 103–106. The only gap is the r8 at the start of 103, about 0.24 s at ♩=126. An oboist plays this from bar 98 to 106, about 17 s, without being able to exhale stale air. Oboe 2 has rested from 84 to 110 and the range a'–b'' suits a second player. Fix: oboe 1 gets `R1*4 | R1*4 |` for 103–110. Oboe 2 gets `R1*19 | \partCombineSoloII r8 a'8(\f f''4.->) e''8( es''8 d''8 | … g''4.->\> fis''8 | R1\! | R1*3 |`. No pitch, octave or dynamic changes, and "2." prints at 103. Oboe 2 still has four bars' rest before its entry at 111.

**2. 102→103, clarinet 2: six bars of continuous eighths, then the f doubling after an eighth-note gap. Applied.**
Clarinet 2 plays arpeggio eighths from 97 through the last eighth of 102. It then enters at 103 after an r8, doubling the line up to b''. Clarinet 1 already rests on that last eighth. Fix: in the `\transpose c cis` block, 102 becomes `des' f' bes' f' c' es' a' r`, which gives a quarter-note breath. The violas sound the same E (written es') on that eighth, so the harmony is unchanged.

**3. 102–106, horn 1: ff counterline straight into the mf solo that peaks on concert e'' (written B5) under a diminuendo. Applied.**
Horn 1 plays the protected brass counter at ff from 97 to 102. Its only break is a mid-bar r8 in 102. It then begins the solo on the downbeat of 103 and reaches concert e''2 dis''2 with a diminuendo, written b''–a♯''. That is playable for a principal. A high diminuendo after six bars at ff is still the most exposed horn moment of the development. Cycle 6 took concert f'' away from horn 1 for the same reason. Horn 2, bassoon 1 and trombone 1 play horn 1's 102 notes in unison. Fix: horn 1 102 `bes2 r2` inside the transposed block. This gives two beats' rest before the solo, and no sounding note changes because horn 2 keeps `r8 f8 a8 c'8`. The part-combiner prints "2." there and "1." at 103. Protected "notes of 97–102" are respected: the sound is identical. I did not lower the solo's peak, because the leap b'→e'' is the second-theme head's own contour.

**4. 103–116, bassoon 1: fourteen bars with only two eighth rests, straight after the 96–102 counter. Applied.**
Bassoon 1 goes from the f tenor line (103–106) to the p bass head (107–109). It then plays the crescendo sustains 110–116 and the G pedal at 117. Bassoon 2 rests at 107–109. Fix: hand 107–109 to bassoon 2 at the same octave (`r8\p b,8( g4.->) fis8( f8 e8 | e4) g8( fis8) b,2 | r8 f,8( des4.->) c8( ces8 bes,8) | c,1\p\< |`). Bassoon 1 gets `R1\! | R1*2 | c2\p\< g2 |`. The cellos double the line, so the colour does not change.

**5. 107–110, low flute against violas. No change.**
The flute plays g'–fis'–e' (below G4) for only a bar and a half, at mp, in unison with the violas at p, over a pp accompaniment. Rimsky-Korsakov says that in this register and this combination the flute will not be heard as a flute. It thickens and softens the violas, and it becomes audible only from 109, where it rises to a♭''. That fits a veiled p restatement, and cycle 74 chose it on colour grounds. For playability it is easy: two-bar slurs, low register at mp, and four bars' rest on each side. Keep mp over the violas' p as the balance correction. If the composer wants the flute heard as a line in 107–108, the only real fix is the upper octave, which would cut across the cycle-74 plan, so I do not recommend it.

**6. 103–106, bass clarinet low C. No change, note only.**
Only four bars of the movement need the low-C instrument: c, at 92–93, and bes,, c, b,, at 105–106. Everything else lies at or above d♭, (D♭2, written E♭3). The part is labelled "Bass clarinet (low C)" on page 1, and professional bass clarinettists own low-C instruments, so there is no instrument change. A player with only a low-E♭ instrument could not play 105–106 in any case. Moving 105–106 up an octave would leave bassoon 2 alone at the bottom and cross horn 4 (d c | b,). The present unison with bassoon 2 is the better octave.

**7. 111, violin I re-entry after eight bars tacet (also violas at 107 and cellos at 107). Note for part extraction.**
The count is clear. The tacet runs from one double bar and key change (103, A minor) to the next (111, C minor), so the parts show one 8-bar rest between two key signatures. Violin I re-enters exposed on a♭'' at mp, though, and there is no rehearsal letter at 103 or 111. When parts are extracted, add a cue of the viola line in 109–110 to the violin I part (`\cueDuring "vla" #UP { R1*2 }`). Nothing needs to change in the score.

**8. Pizz./arco changes and mutes across the movement. No change.**
- 61→62 cellos: arco eighths run into a pizz. on the downbeat (sub. p) at ♩=112. The bar's second slur ends on an up-bow, which brings the hand to the string (cellofun). This works at this tempo.
- 63→64 cellos: pizz. to arco with one quarter rest (0.54 s). Adequate.
- 84→85 basses: an arco whole note into a pizz. on the downbeat at ♩=126. The player releases the whole note slightly early, which is normal. Acceptable.
- Basses at 55/56, 89/90, 106/107, 109/110 and 155/156: one to three beats or more for each change. All fine.

Mutes: Tchaikovsky gives his violins eight bars of rest to put mutes on (6/i, 81–88) and three bars to take them off (about 128–130).
- 147–155: the violins play sul tasto tremolo up to "ord." mf with only two beats' rest at 155. Mutes could not come off in time. Rimsky-Korsakov also says muted strings separate from the woodwind, and the cor anglais fills here need to blend. Sul tasto is the right choice.
- Coda: only violin I has time to mute (tacet 175–180, about 20 s). Violin II and the violas play throughout. Violin I alone con sord. on the final motto (181–186), over unmuted pp violin II, would put the tune below its accompaniment. I would not add mutes anywhere.
