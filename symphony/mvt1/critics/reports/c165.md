## Findings

### 1. A choir cut down to one staff loses its bracket (applied)

**Bars.**
- 15–22: Bn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 each stand alone.
- 47–55: Cl 1·2 and Hn 1·2.
- 56–63: Timp.
- Every system where the timpani or a single wind staff is the only staff of its choir: 85–102, 111–130, 147–157, 163–170 and others.

**Problem.** LilyPond hides a StaffGroup bracket whose height is at or below `collapse-height` (default 5 staff spaces). A one-staff choir therefore prints with no bracket. On printed page 2, system 1, Bn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 look like one unmarked block above the strings. On printed page 5, system 1, Cl 1·2 and Hn 1·2 look the same. The conductor cannot see at a glance where the woodwinds end and the brass begin.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 294: "heavy brackets set off the choirs".
- Adler PDF 291: "the separation of choirs must be immediately discernable, for a conductor has to read all the lines simultaneously".
- Adler PDF 295, on reduced scores: "all the instruments must be clearly marked in all systems … the order of choirs remains the same".
- Gould PDF 52: in a full score a single staff keeps its systemic barline and bracket ("see Square brackets").
- Ex. XVIII-2 (Stravinsky cutout, PDF 295) and Ex. XVIII-3b (Mahler reduced page, PDF 296) bracket every choir, including one-staff choirs.

**Fix (score.ly, \layout):**
```
\context { \StaffGroup \override SystemStartBracket.collapse-height = #1 }
```
Nothing else moves; breaks and page count are unchanged.

### 2. Timpani and percussion are not grouped as a choir (applied, bracket only)

**Bars.** Every system where both staves appear: page 1, 120–130, 158–162, 171–182.

**Problem.** The timpani and the one-line cymbal/tam-tam staff hang between the brass and harp brackets with no bracket of their own.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 292 lists timpani and percussion as one family.
- Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) brackets "6 Timpani, Xylophone, Triangle/Snare Drum" as one choir.
- MOLA PDF 4 asks for the percussion staves to be kept together.

**Counter-evidence.** The Mutopia Dvořák 7 and 9 scores leave a lone timpani staff unbracketed. That case is covered by Finding 1: a lone timpani staff now gets the standard one-staff bracket. Cycle 155 judged the separate barlines "follows" against MOLA Ex. 6, which also has timpani alone. I keep those separate barlines.

**Fix (score.ly):** wrap the Timpani staff and the DrumStaff in
```
\new StaffGroup \with { \remove "Span_bar_engraver" } << … >>
```
I first tried the bracket with Adler's barlines through the choir. The connecting barline then ran through the percussion texts "1: Tam-tam *soft stick, l.v.*" (175) and "*hard beater, l.v.*" (122), which are wider than their bars. So the span bars are removed and the bracket alone marks the choir. No other layout change.

### 3. Bar 102: Hn 2's "2." label touches Hn 1's half rest (applied)

**Bars.** 102, Hn 1·2, beats 3–4.

**Problem.** Since cycle 155, Hn 1's half rest sits on a ledger line above the staff (`a''2\rest`), which is correct by Gould PDF 51. The part-combiner's "2." for Hn 2's entry is printed at the same height directly beside it. At score size it reads as one symbol, "▬2.".

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 301: proofread "so as to make certain you have not overlooked even the smallest detail".
- Ex. XVIII-10a (PDF 301) keeps the "1." label clear of every note and rest on the shared oboe staff.

**Fix (dev.ily, devHnOne, bar 102):**
```
bes2 \override Staff.CombineTextScript.extra-offset = #'(0.8 . 1.6) a''2\rest } | \revert Staff.CombineTextScript.extra-offset
```
A `\once \override` placed in either part had no effect, because the part-combiner creates the label in another voice. The override is therefore set for this one beat and reverted at 103. The rest keeps its cycle-155 position.

## Checked, no change

- **Tempo and rehearsal marks between choirs** (Adler PDF 294 and Ex. XVIII-1, marks also above the horns and timpani). MOLA PDF 4 and the MuseScore handbook ask only for the top staff and Vln I, which the score does. Repeating the marks above Hn 1·2 would put them three times into the short reduced systems at 47, 56 and 147, where Hn 1·2 is the second to fourth staff. Do not add.
- **Rehearsal-letter spacing** (Adler PDF 299). The gaps of 20–22 bars at 47–69, 77–97 and 97–117 are normal for the models, and every bar is numbered. Cycle 155's decision stands.
- **Trumpet and trombone staff pairing** (Adler PDF 294). Pairing 1·2 with 3 alone is the other standard layout. Changing it would reopen every Tpt 2 dynamic (cycle 4).
- **Ranges** (App. A): all within the professional columns.
- **Terms** (App. A PDF 312): Italian terms are used correctly. The few English instructions ("change bow at will", the harp retune, "étouffez with Cb.") are allowed by MOLA PDF 4.

**Note A (for the parts stage, cycle 185).** Ex. XVIII-10 (PDF 301) prints different dynamics for two players on one staff, player 1 above and player 2 below. The parts must then show each player's own dynamic. This score's one-dynamic-per-staff setup (cycles 5, 15) cannot print that. It is fine for the score as long as pairs keep equal dynamics, which the 0-warning build confirms. It is the reason for c158's open note on Bn 1 at 103–106. When the parts are made, extract them from the single-player variables (bsnOne, bsnTwo …), not from the combined staves. Then a later difference between players only needs a `\partCombineApart` passage in the score.
