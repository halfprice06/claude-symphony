### Considered and kept

**1. Timpani A♭ roll entering mf< at 115 (dev, `devTimp`).** This is the only timpani entry after a long rest (17¾ bars) at mf. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136 calls mf re-entries "colourless" and says the remark "is capable of wider application". c354 changed the roll at 80 to p< because of Adler (adler2 PDF 184, printed p. 497): Bloch marks his timpani roll "piano and then crescendo, because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra". I tested whether 115 should match. Keep mf<, for three reasons:
- At 115 the wedge's crescendo has already reached f: Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2 are marked f there. An mf roll enters a level below the texture, which is what both rules ask for (Rimsky PDF 133: the supporting part quieter; Adler PDF 184: no roll loud enough to cover).
- The roll lasts one bar at crotchet = 126. From p it would have to climb three levels in under two seconds to reach the f stroke at 116.1, and then go on to the f roll at 117, which is protected (c448, c464). The swell would sound uneven.
- At 80 the roll was the first sound under two f head entries. At 115 it only reinforces a crescendo that strings, wood-wind and horns already carry. Its job is to add the A♭ body, not to stand out as a new colour.

**2. Bar 13, the dominant after the f German sixth at 12.** Rimsky's method (a) on PDF 129 would let the strings attack 13 sf while the wind drops to p, which gives a sharper sf-dim. The score instead carries the f across the tie into 13. Fl 1 and Vln I make a diminuendo on beats 1–2 and reach p where Hn 1's call enters mp (c258). An sf string attack on 13 would add a second accent on the V. It would fall on the beat where the horn call has to emerge alone, and the notes of 9–14 and the horn call are protected (c050, c226, c308). Keep.

**3. Bar 143: wind ff stroke while the strings go on at f.** This looks like the weaker layout of PDF 129 (b): short wind chords over sustained strings. It is not one. The strings are not holding a tenuto chord. Vln I plays the P head and Vln II, Vla and Cb play its off-beat answer. Only Vc holds C3, and Cb, Bsn 1 and the head move around it. The ff wind stroke closes the 139–142 tutti, and the strings' f begins the lead-in to J, which is protected (c239). Keep.

**4. Bar 177, Cl 1 B4 with a hairpin to silence.** PDF 130 gives the clarinet the morendo. The hairpin into the rest already writes that, and the viola's printed "morendo" at 181 marks the last fade of the movement. A second "morendo" four bars earlier would weaken the viola's. Keep.

**5. The open c093 item: a diminuendo on beats 3–4 of 102 before the f wind entry at 103.** PDF 130's exit order (brass, then wood-wind, then strings) would support a staged diminuendo here. But the drop from the B-minor ff block to the f wind statement of the combined heads is a deliberate seam. It is protected by c072, c074, c084 and c323 ("seams 96→97, 102→103"; "notes and f/p contrast of 103–110"), and nothing in the reading answers their reason, which is that the new section enters by contrast, not by a fade. Keep.

### Why nothing else meets the bar

Three passes of this chapter have already put its devices into the score:
- run slurs into 33, 41 and 69 (c108);
- brass swells into 41 and 122;
- clarinet and bass-clarinet fades (c328);
- MIDI levels for sf-p and sub. p (c428);
- tam-tam and cymbal fades with "damp gradually".

The places where expression shapes the music most are the swells of 47–55 and 147–155, the sub. p at 62 and 84, the fff only at 122 and 160, and the coda's pp–ppp chain. These are protected by many cycles (c038, c068, c098, c118, c168), and I found no reading-based case against them. Further marks would only add notation where players already know what to do.
