## What I checked this pass

1. **Ex. 284, the shape of each approach to a forte (row 27).** Earlier passes looked at the drop after a loud attack. I also checked the approach: does each arrival come from a crescendo that spans the approach, as Ex. 284's two-bar p crescendo does, rather than from a step? All eight do. The longest are 111–117 (mp/pp cresc. over six bars) and 127–131 (pp to ff over four bars, with the cellos' note values shortening). The only arrivals without a crescendo are the subito restarts at 60, 62 and 84, and those are intended (60 by c368, 84 by c448).
2. **Ex. 2A and Ex. 269, the pads.** Every example in the reading that has a sustained string pad gives the pad its own hairpins: Ex. 2A's "poco" swells, the cellos' chord in Ex. 269, and the violins in Ex. 273. Ex. 277 is the exception: its flugelhorn and trombone pad is plain mp, and its character comes from the lead line's notes. I compared both of this movement's S pads with this (Finding 1).
3. **Ex. 272 and 276, rit. at a close.** I listed every tempo word against the section ends. Every close has a rit. or poco rit. in its last bars except the end of the Lento introduction at 14 (Finding 2).
4. **Focus between two espressivo lines (PDF 13).** At 107–110 the bass clarinet (P head) and the violas (S head) are both p espr. They alternate: the violas hold the long notes while the bass clarinet moves, and the reverse. That is the complementary motion of Ex. 280 (PDF 212), and neither line covers the other. This is the protected "103-110 combined heads".

## Findings

None of the points below meets the bar for a real improvement. I list them, with the reasons, so the next critic does not need to test them again.

### 1. Bars 47–55 and 147–155: the S pads are flat pp with no phrase-level swell (no change)

**What I considered.** Under the S tune (Hn 1 mp and Vc mf at 47; Hn 1 mp, Vla and Vc mf at 147), the pads hold one level for nine bars: Vln II metà and Vla div. pp at 47–55, and Vln II div. sul tasto tremolo pp at 147–155. Sebesky's string pads in Ex. 2A (PDF 18), Ex. 269 (PDF 205) and Ex. 273 (PDF 208) all carry small hairpins. Dvořák 7/i at reh. D (bar 73) has a strings-pp accompaniment under the p dolce flute tune, with "cresc." in bar 3 and fp in bar 4, where the tune rises (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Viola.ly lines 228–231; Violino_II lines 161–164). A "poco <" in the pads over 51–52 and ">" over 53–54 would follow the consequent phrase's climb to its F5 at 52.4.

**Why it stays.**
- This score already builds the consequent's climb by addition. Cl 1 enters mp on the F5 at 52.4 (152.4), and the harp (mp at 47) restrikes a new chord every bar, so the accompaniment already moves with each bar. Sebesky makes instrumentation a means of formal growth (PDF 11), and c478 accepted the Cl 1 entry as the apex marker on the same ground.
- The tune itself swells in every bar (protected "S swells onto each appoggiatura"). Pad hairpins over the same bars would duplicate the lead's dynamic shape in the background. The focus rule (PDF 13) argues against this: the pad is the floor, and it is half a section (metà, c114) so that it stays below the tune.
- Ex. 277 (PDF 210) shows that a soft long-note background can stay at one level when its line has character. The Vln II line at 47–55 (A♭–B♭–G♭–F–E♭) and the 147–155 halo do.
- Dvořák's pad at reh. D is a moving semiquaver figure under a solo flute at p, so a crescendo changes the balance there. Here the tune is mf and the pad is two levels below it.
- The passage is protected ("47-63 second-theme texture" c569; "47–55 viola pad"; "147-155 pad" c387, c427). The reading does not answer those reasons.

### 2. Bars 13–14: the Lento introduction ends without a rit. before the Allegro (no change)

**What I considered.** Ex. 272 (PDF 207) and Ex. 276 (PDF 209) put "RIT." in the last bar before a close, and every later close in this movement has one (66–68, 145, 165–166, 177–178). Tchaikovsky 6/i marks "ritenuto" over the last two bars of its Adagio introduction, bars 17–18, before the double bar and the Allegro non troppo at 19 (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/ly/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.ly, line 47). A "poco rit." over bar 14 would make the horn call's leading tone B3 at 14.2 arrive late.

**Why it stays.**
- By bar 14 the motion has already stopped: the horn holds B3, the timpani roll fades out, and beat 4 is a rest. Tchaikovsky's ritenuto slows a line that is still moving. Here a rit. would only lengthen a held note and a rest that already last 4.8 s at crotchet 50.
- The call ends on the leading tone, which is open, and the Allegro's quaver pickup at 15 answers it. A slowing would make bar 14 sound like a close, which it is not.
- Ex. 276's RIT. comes before a fermata and FINE, at a real ending.
- Two standing reasons apply as well, and the case is not strong enough to answer them: c043's rule of no new ritardandos (slow music is already about 29% of the time), and c420's rule that the MIDI of the quoted bars 1–14 stays as at v321.

### 3. Bars 107–110: two espressivo lines at one level (no change)

**What I considered.** By the focus chart (PDF 14), one of the two lines should rank below the other.

**Why it stays.** The two lines take turns (row 23), so the primary element changes every half bar. This is the shifting focus that PDF 14 describes. Fl 1 enters mp espr. at 109 above both lines, as the third level. The passage is protected ("103-110 combined heads", c437).
