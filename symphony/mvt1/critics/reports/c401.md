# Critic 401: melody and memorability (series 3, cycle 401), v401

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 20 "Melodic Figuration", PDF 327–345 (second pass of this lens; c221 and c311 read the same unit). I read every text page from PDF 327 to 345 in full. I rendered all 19 pages at 110 dpi and looked at every example as an image: Ex. 20-1 to 20-27, the Points for Review (PDF 343) and the exercises (PDF 343–345).

c221 and c311 had already checked the themes' figuration, the spelling of chromatic passing tones, the turn and the sevenths. So in this pass I went after the one part of the unit that no earlier report checked bar by bar, sections 16–17 (PDF 340–344). These sections cover parallels caused by figuration and parallels that figuration does or does not avert. The movement's checker, vl.py, compares only adjacent sounding states. It therefore cannot see a fifth or octave on one beat that moves to another on the next beat with a passing tone, neighbour, anticipation or skip in between, which is exactly the case of Ex. 20-26. I wrote /tmp/critic-401/scripts/beatpar.py to sample every pair of parts on each crotchet beat and to list the beat-to-beat fifths and octaves that have figuration between them. It found 156 cases. 153 of them are octave doublings, where one doubling part is figurated and the other is not (the footnote to Ex. 20-27, PDF 344). I also wrote two spelling scripts: aug2.py finds melodic augmented seconds (PDF 340, section 15) and augprime.py finds chromatic neighbours spelled as augmented primes (PDF 341, Point 5).

## Findings

### F1. Bar 61→62: the violins and the bass move F♭→E♭ in octaves (applied)

The S restatement descends B♭5–B♭♭5–A♭5–G♭5–F♭5–E♭5 over the bass G♭–F♭–E♭ (60–62), into the cadential 6/4 at 62. In v401 Vln I plays `aes''2 ges''4 fes''8 es''8)` at 61, and Vln II plays the same an octave lower. On beat 4 of 61 the tune's F♭5 doubles the bass F♭2 (Cb, Bsn 2, harp, the cello's last quaver). The tune then takes E♭5 as an anticipation on the second quaver and strikes E♭5 again at 62, where the bass reaches E♭2. In the outer voices that is F♭/F♭ followed by E♭/E♭: parallel octaves on ♭6̂→5̂, the strongest tendency step in the passage. They come at the end of the 59–61 crescendo, just before the subito p.

The reading rules this out. "Unisons and octaves caused by figuration are more problematic than 5ths ... you should avoid them" (PDF 341). "Among tones of figuration, only the chordal skip is, in principle, strong enough to avert octaves" (PDF 341; Points 10–11, PDF 343). Unit 21 allows anticipations to break fifths only (A&S PDF 365–366). The passage is also the Phrygian iv6 (F♭ bass) → V situation, where A&S say to double the 3rd above the bass and not the bass, "to reduce the danger of parallel octaves" (Unit 11, PDF 169, Ex. 11-10). Earlier reports missed this: c273 described the outer voices as "10ths ... then the E♭ octave at 62", and c332 marked 61→62 "follows", but both read only the crotchet framework. vl.py and analysis.txt cannot see it because the two voices move a quaver apart.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-401/work/sections/tTwo.ily:
- Vln I 61: `aes''2 ges''4 fes''8 es''8)` → `aes''2 ges''4 fes''8 des''8)`
- Vln II 61: `aes'2 ges'4 fes'8 es'8)` → `aes'2 ges'4 fes'8 des'8)`

D♭ is the root of the iv6 chord, so the violins now leave F♭ by a chordal skip, which is the one figure the reading accepts as averting octaves. Bach does the same with an eighth-note skip in a crotchet texture (Ex. 20-4, PDF 331). The violins now come into 62 from below (D♭→E♭) while the bass falls F♭→E♭, so the motion into the 6/4 is contrary. Melodically, nothing that carries the line is lost. The F♭ mixture note stays in the tune on the same beat. The descent still ends on E♭5 at 62, where c273 wanted the "linear pull" over the F♭. The goal E♭ is now framed by both of its neighbours, F♭ above and D♭ below, which is the double incomplete neighbour of Ex. 20-13c (PDF 335). It is a figure the movement already uses at 15 (E♭–D–D♭–C). The change also does away with the re-struck E♭ across the barline: before, the phrase ended on an anticipated E♭ and the new phrase began on the same E♭ subito p. Now the subito p falls on a new note, as in the other S restarts (46.4, 55.4).

This reopens two protections: c273's "violins 56–63" and c322's "phrasing 47–63". c273's reason was the descent onto E♭ over the bass G♭–F♭–E♭, and that is kept. Slurs, dynamics, bowing and rhythm are unchanged. The bass that movement II quotes (G♭–F♭–E♭ by whole bars) is untouched, and so is its F♭→E♭ thread in the bass and the harp.

### F2. Bar 64→65: Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline and bass, D→D♭ (keep)

The scan flags Cl 1 + Hn 2 `r8 c'8( d'8 es'8 | des'2)` against the bass F2–D2 | D♭2. That gives D4/D2 on beat 4 of 64 and D♭4/D♭2 at 65, with the counterline's E♭4 in between. By the letter of PDF 341–342 an incomplete neighbour cannot break octaves. But the E♭ here is not a decoration of D♭. It is the resolution of the counterline's D♮, the leading tone of the applied viiø7/V at 64.3, which rises to E♭ as it should. Reduce the figure as Ex. 20-1 does: the counterline goes C4 → D4 → E♭4 (the leading tone resolving) → D♭4, and the bass goes D → D♭. The step E♭→D♭ against D→D♭ is a direct octave in an inner part, which is allowed, and the beat-level "octave" exists only at the surface. The counterline is protected (c067, c273, c377), and its C–D–E♭ answers the rising E♭–E–F–G at 56. Keep.

### F3. The other beat-level parallels through figuration (keep)

Of the remaining cases, 153 are octave doublings in which one part figurates the line its partner holds. Examples are Vln I's semiquaver run against the violas' F4–G4 at 40→41, the climax doublings at 141 and 161, and the wedge at 112–115. PDF 344 (footnote to Ex. 20-27) calls these "octaves by doubling", not parallels. The rest are covered by the reading's exceptions. At 39→40 (Fl 1 against Hn 4) and 57.2 (Vln I against the cello arpeggio) a chordal skip of a quaver breaks the octave (Ex. 20-2c, 20-4). At 71→72, 121 and 165→166 the fifths are between a quick inner arpeggio in a weak position and a held part (Ex. 20-6). None needs a change.

### F4. Wedge 114–116: D4–D♭4–D4 in Hn 3 and Tpt 3 (keep)

The only chromatic neighbour spelled as an augmented prime is Hn 3 and Tpt 3 at 114–116. Point 5 (PDF 343) asks for minor seconds. But these are whole-bar chord tones, not figuration. D♭ is the third of the root-position D♭ chord at 115, and D♮ is in the French sixth over C at 116. The harmony dictates the spelling. Ex. 20-23 concerns neighbours within one harmony. Keep.

### F5. Would a listener hum them afterwards? (no change)

Reduced in the stages of Ex. 20-1, the four ideas keep their identity because the figuration carries it (PDF 328). P's head widens its leap from a sixth to a seventh to an octave (G4–E♭5 at 15, G4–F5 at 17, A♭4–A♭5 at 19) and only then breaks through to D♭6. Tchaikovsky's Fifth and Brahms's First, first movements, build their first themes the same way. Its neighbour cells (C–B–C at 16, the double neighbour at 18) make the second and fourth bars easy to recall. S grows from rising sixths (E♭–C at 46–47, A♭–F at 48, E♭–C at 51), which are the motto's minor sixth turned major. That is the Dvořák kind of tune that one hears once and keeps. The motto and the horn call are short enough to hold after one hearing, and the movement states the motto in at least eight colours. Nothing in the reading points to rewriting a theme. The weakest stretch is still the plain scale descent at 21–22, which c221 kept for its function (PDF 333, Ex. 20-10). I agree and leave it.
