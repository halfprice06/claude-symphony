### F1. 117–121: the theme's lower octave is buried inside the brass, and both clarinets duplicate the violas inside the horns (applied)

**Problem.** Over the G pedal, Fl 1 and Vln I carry the theme on top. Ob 1 and Vln II carry it an octave lower, from B3 (`r8 b8 aes'4.->`) up to G5 at 121. That lower octave runs straight through the held line of Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and Bsn 1 (E♭4–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4, ff) and through the horns' G3/G4 octave. At 119.1.5 Ob 1's D4 sits a semitone under four brass on E♭4. At 117.1.5 and 117.4.5 its B3 and D4 sit under or inside them. Meanwhile Cl 1, Cl 2 and violas all play the same triplet arpeggio (B3–D4–F4, C4–E♭4–G4) in unison, also inside the horns. The scan flags clarinets inside the horns in every bar from 117 to 121. Three layers share the octave B3–G4, and the only reed on the theme there is one oboe in its lowest fifth.

**Evidence.** PDF 108–109: "Played forte, the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind; balance may be established by doubling the upper harmonic parts". The PDF 109 diagram doubles each upper part with two woodwinds over four horns at f. PDF 108: "Clarinets ... should rarely be set inside the horns." PDF 114: balance matters most "with long sustained chords or those of rhythmic design", which describes this passage exactly. PDF 91 and 108 assign the moving figure to woodwind and the stationary parts to brass, and one clarinet plus the violas keeps that layer.

**Fix (applied).** Cl 1 leaves the triplet unison and doubles Ob 1 and Vln II on the lower octave of the theme. Cl 2 and violas keep the figure.
```
% devClOne, bars 117–121 (was the triplet unison with Cl 2)
\partCombineApart r8\f b8 aes'4.-> g'8 f'8 d'8 | r8 es'8 c''4.-> b'8 c''8 g'8 |
r8 d'8 b'4-> r8 f'8 d''4-> | r8 f'8 des''4-> r8 bes'8 f''4-> | es''4.\< f''8 g''2 | \partCombineAutomatic
```
Cl 1 then goes from G5 to its existing F5 fff at 122. The marking is f, the same as Cl 2 on the shared staff, so the shared-staff dynamics stay equal and c117's layered f→fff dynamics are unchanged. `\partCombineApart` keeps the two rhythms in separate stemmed voices, with no repeated "2." labels. I checked the engraving on page 10.

**Protection.** "G pedal and rising figure 117–130" (c142), "alternating chords over the G pedal 117–121" (c133), "Hn 3·4 octave pedal 117–121" (c124) and the c117 layered dynamics are all protected. My answer: no pitch, rhythm or dynamic of any line changes. The figure still sounds in Cl 2 and violas, and the brass are untouched. One clarinet moves from a doubling the book advises against to the doubling it prescribes for forte. The clarinet's breath at 120 beat 4 is lost, but each bar of the new part starts with an eighth rest.

### F2. 97–102: two crossing eighth-note figures enclose the melody's lowest octave (recorded departure, no change)

At 98–101 the melody's lowest octave (cor anglais plus Vln II sul G, D4–G4) lies inside four layers. Cl 1·2 a2 play D4–F♯4–B4–F♯4, violas play the mirror figure B4–F♯4–D4–F♯4 and meet them on F♯4 every half beat, Hn 3·4 hold D4/B3, and Hn 1·2 + Tbn 1 + Bsn 1 have the counter on B3–D4. The clarinets also sit inside the horns at 99–101. By PDF 90 (normal order of register) and PDF 93 (crossing must follow the progression of the parts), this is crossing rather than overlaying.

I recommend no change. The melody is clear two octaves higher (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I), and PDF 112 says that when the melodic position keeps changing, "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts", and the octaves support each other. The passage is protected (c117, c133, c139, c141, c146). Neither figure can drop an octave: the clarinet figure goes down to C♯4 and the viola figure to B3, so the lower octave would be below the B♭ clarinet's D3 and the viola's C3.

### F3. 131–134: clarinets in unison with trombones and bassoons on the low theme, inside the horns (proposal, not applied)

Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 a2 and the cellos play the recapitulated theme in unison at G3–F4. The clarinets lie inside Hn 1·2/3·4 (G3–G4), and the scan flags all four bars. PDF 106 calls trombones with clarinets or bassoons in unison "very rare", and PDF 108 advises against clarinets inside the horns. Under ff trombones the chalumeau doubling adds little.

Do not move the clarinets up an octave. The theme would then cross the Vln II answer line (E♭5–D♭5–C5) in the same register with a different rhythm, which PDF 93 warns against. If a reading session finds the low theme thick, the clean fix is `recOneClOne`/`recOneClTwo` = `R1*4` at 131–134, entering at 135 as now. The passage is protected (c104, c139), so I leave it as a proposal.

### F4. 69–71: in the codetta, Bsn 1 holds Tbn 1's notes above the horns' moving counter (proposal, not applied)

Bsn 1 holds C4–E♭4 | F4 | D♭4, doubling Tbn 1 on E♭4/F4 (PDF 106: a "very rare" combination). Below it, Hn 1·2 a2 play the moving first-theme counter (E♭3–F4), and the violas' broken-chord eighths cross the horns on almost every half beat, in unison at 69.2, 69.4 and 70.3. That gives three departures in one place: horns inside the bassoons (PDF 108, "the inverse process is not to be recommended"), the held part in woodwind and the moving part in brass (the reverse of PDF 108's chain-of-chords rule), and strings crossing brass (PDF 113).

Possible fix: Bsn 1 joins the horns in unison at 69–70 (2 Hn + Bsn, the PDF 106 pairing), `r8 es8( c'4.->) bes8( aes8 g8 | aes8 bes8 c'8 des'8) f'4.-> es'8 |`, and keeps its held `des'1` at 71, which is the only sustained seventh of the V7. I did not apply it. The codetta appears on HISTORY's do-not-touch lists many times, most recently in c102, c106, c117 and c142, and the viola figure is a deliberate cycle-90 choice.
