## Findings

### 1. Bar 82, first A♭ climax: Fl 2 plays in its weak octave and Cl 2 is inaudible inside the brass (APPLIED)

**Problem.** At 82 the first-theme head is stated ff in three octaves over the A♭ bass. Before my change the octaves were scored like this:
- Top octave (G5–E♭6): Fl 1 and Vln I.
- Middle octave (G4–E♭5): Fl 2, Ob 1, Cl 1, Tpt 1 and Vln II.
- Lowest octave (G3–E♭4): the violas.

Fl 2 played G4–E♭5, the bottom of the flute's range, at pitch with Tpt 1 at ff. My scan of `full.midi` shows this is the only flute note below A4 in the movement that sounds in a ff bar. Cl 2 had off-beat E♭4 quavers that sounded entirely inside the held ff E♭4 of Hn 2 and Tpt 3. The listener cannot hear either part. The top octave, which carries the line to E♭6, had only one wind.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151, on Mozart 40/iv: "the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument that he uses", and the flute is placed where it fills the space, "not left alone in its upper reaches". Adler PDF 146: the timbre and strength "of every segment of each instrument's range" comes before range limits. Adler PDF 147, on *Istar*: an instrument that can play the notes but "would not contribute significantly to the sound" is left out. Adler PDF 152 (Ex. XV-4, which I checked on the image): Beethoven puts the top octave of the ff unison in the two flutes alone, because "this high octave in the flute is so piercing it need not be doubled by any other instrument to cut through."

**Earlier decisions.** c144 and c194 kept the layout at 82. Their reason was the heavy middle octave, after Mozart's layout (c194 Finding 4 named Fl 2 in unison with Fl 1 as the option). The fix below keeps that weight. Cl 2 takes Fl 2's place in the middle octave, so that octave still has five lines (Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1, Vln II), with a clarinet in its strong clarion register in place of a flute in its weakest. The top octave gains the second flute, as in Beethoven. No pitch class, rhythm or dynamic of the tutti changes.

### 2. Bars 158–161 and 122: the other two A♭ climaxes against the octave-weight rule (checked, no change)

**At 160:**
- Top octave: Fl 1·2 and Vln I.
- Middle octave: Ob 2, Tpt 1 (fff) and Vln II.
- Low octave: Hn 1·2 and Vc.
- Counterline: Ob 1 and Cl 1 above, Hn 3·4 below.
- The heavy brass hold the harmony ff (c244).

The trumpet makes the middle octave the strongest, as in Mozart's layout (Adler PDF 151). The brass that are not on the tune sustain chords (PDF 154). Trumpets, trombones and flutes enter after rests (PDF 156). Hn 3·4 cross above Hn 1·2 at 158–161. They are inner voices of one colour, and the tune is fixed by the trumpet and the violins, so I leave the crossing (scoring of 158–166 is protected, c179).

**At 122:** the arrival is a held fff chord, not a melodic unison. The trombones' fff is justified because they bring the motto into its A♭.

This gives a clear order across the three climaxes. 82 has a ff head in three octaves topped at E♭6. 122 and 160 reach A♭6, with tam-tam at 122 and the suspended cymbal at 160. After Finding 1, the 82 statement has its top octave doubled the way the other two are, but it keeps its lower top note (E♭6) and its ff level. The hierarchy is unchanged.

### 3. Bars 167–169: the head is in the brass, and the agile instruments sustain (checked, no change)

Adler's Smetana example (PDF 154) gives the fast figure to the woodwinds and strings and the sustained chords to the brass, and warns that brass on the quick notes add weight. At 167–169 this is reversed. Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and the violas play the head ff, and the flutes, oboes, clarinets and tremolo violins hold the chord at f. This passage is a summing-up, which is one of the uses Adler gives for the octave unison (PDF 150). The weight the brass add is the point here, and the quavers are short and slurred in pairs. A wind or violin doubling of the head would also give the coda the full multi-octave brass-and-strings statement that the finale keeps for itself (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). Keep.
