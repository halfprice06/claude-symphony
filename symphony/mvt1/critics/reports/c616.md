## Findings

I found no place in v616 where the string writing goes against this chapter. I also found no string change that would make the music clearly better. I propose no score change. Below are the checks on the string changes since v526, the parts, and two possible improvements that I tested against the reading and rejected.

### 1. String changes since v526: keep

- **Coda ties 179–181 (c568).** Vln II G4, violas E♭4 and cellos C3 are now tied through 179–181 at crotchet = 50: 14.4 s, then the fermata bar. Each tie carries "change bow at will" at 179, which is Adler's remedy for a long tenuto (PDF 108). The violas' "morendo" and E♭4→E♮4 change at 182 fall on a bow change the section staggers anyway. Vln I's new slur break before the D♭ at 180 (d''4( | c''2) des''2( | c''1)) gives two bows of about 5 s each at pp sul D, well within a section's soft bow. Keep.
- **Cb 164.3–165 (c583, c586).** The tied G2 and the drop to G1 sit under one up-bow (about 4 s at the rit.), so 166 lands down-bow as marked. G2→G1 is an octave down from the open G string to the E string, the easy direction (PDF 106 warns only about approaching the high range). Keep.
- **Down-bows at 26 and the hooked up-bow at 160 (c595).** Violas and cellos take the ff stop chord 26 down-bow with the other strings. The violas' F4/B4 (first position, D and A strings) and the cellos' B2/A♭3 are the easy stops of Ex. III-33 and III-56. The hooked up-bow at 160.4.5 in Vln I lets 161 start down-bow without a retake. Keep.
- **Violas B3/F4 at 161 (c604).** This is the same diminished fifth on the G and D strings as at 83, in first position, under ff repeated quavers. It is the kind of loud repeated double stop that PDF 76 allows. Keep.
- **Vln II up-bow at 98 and violas down-bow at 131 (c538).** I counted both through to their arrivals, and each does what its comment says. Keep.

### 3. Considered and rejected: marking the soft open-string notes as stopped (PDF 73–74)

Adler says an open string in a slow, soft, expressive passage "stands out peculiarly" and lacks vibrato. The scan finds soft held notes on open-string pitches at Cb G2 (6, 4.8 s, pp; 13–14, 8.4 s, p > pp), Cb D2 (11, 177), Vla D4 (13–14) and Vc G2 (175). None of them is a melody note. Each is the bass or an inner note of a chord, and each is doubled: at 6 by the cellos' G3, at 13 by the cellos, Bsn 2 and the timpani roll on G, at 177 by the bass trombone and bass clarinet. Adler's own advice on the same page is to leave fingering to the player unless timbre requires it (PDF 73). A bass player stops a soft held G2 on the D string as a matter of course when the colour calls for it. A "IIIe corde" or "stopped" mark would add ink to the part and give no audible gain. Rejected.

### 4. Considered and rejected: using the viola C string (PDF 86, Ex. III-30)

Adler calls the C string the viola's most characteristic one, and this movement hardly uses it (33, 77, 157). In a C-minor movement this is the one string colour of the chapter that stays unused, so I looked for a place where it would add something a listener would remember. The best candidate is 73–76, where the flute plays the darkened motto over the A♭ pedal and the violas hold C♭4–C4–C4–D♭4 pp. Taking the violas down an octave (C♭3–C3–C3–D♭3) would put them under the cellos' A♭3. The viola's C-string mixture C♭ would then sound below the cellos' root in the tenor, a minor tenth above the basses' A♭1. That thickens the low middle at pp, the muddy zone of PDF 107–108. Adler also says the heavier C string "speaks reluctantly" (PDF 86), which works against a soft sustained inner part. The C♭ spelling at 73 and the 73–84 layout are also protected. The other dark places (the 111–116 wedge, the 117–130 G pedal, the coda chorale 175–179, the introduction 6–8) are protected for reasons this reading does not answer. The colour is better kept for movements II–IV than forced in here. Rejected.
