## Findings

### F1. Bars 58–59: the violins crescendo through the half cadence of their S statement; every other statement fades there (applied)

**What is wrong.** The S head is a four-bar group that ends on V7 with an appoggiatura. At the half cadence the melody leaps a sixth up to the appoggiatura and resolves it down a step. In the first statement (Hn 1 and cellos, 49–50) the swell starts on beat 4 of 49, peaks on the appoggiatura B♭ at 50.1 and fades through its resolution. The same shape (crescendo from beat 4 of the third bar, diminuendo in the fourth) is printed in every later statement: 100–101 (ff, B minor), 149–150 (recapitulation), 173–174 (solo cello). The violins' restatement at 56–59 breaks this. At 58 a single crescendo starts on beat 1 and runs through 59 to the mf at 60. So the appoggiatura A♭5 at 59.1 and its resolution G5 at 59.3, over the E♭7 at the half cadence, get louder through the resolution, and the end of the hypermeasure 56–59 runs straight into 60. The cellos beneath already swell and return to pp inside 58 (c358), so the violins are also out of step with their own bass.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 53: in a four-bar hypermeasure bars 1 and 3 are strong and bars 2 and 4 weak. Bar 59 is bar 4 of 56–59. PDF 54: "Very often the end of a group of measures coincides with a goal of tonal motion"; here that goal is the half cadence on V7 at 59. PDF 54–55 and Ex. 3-15c: an appoggiatura is an accented dissonance and resolves to a weaker consonance. PDF 50: dissonant notes carry a built-in accent, so the A♭5 needs no extra crescendo after it to be heard. The movement's own pattern at 49–50, 100–101, 149–150 and 173–174 is the evidence that the composer reads these bars this way. 56–59 is the only exception.

**Fix (applied, sections/tTwo.ily, Vln I and Vln II, which play 58–59 in unison).**
```
des''4.( c''8 bes'4 des''8\< f''8 | aes''2--\> g''2)\stopTextSpan | bes''2.~(\mf\< ...   % Vln I
des''4.( c''8 bes'4 des''8\< f''8 | aes''2--\> g''2)\stopTextSpan | bes'2.~(\mf\< ...    % Vln II
```
Was `des''4.\<( ... | aes''2-- g''2)`. The crescendo now starts on beat 4 of 58, as at 49.4, and the diminuendo runs through 59 to the barline. The second hypermeasure then starts at mf on its strong first bar (60), where the cellos, basses, harp and horns begin their own crescendo toward the sub. p at 62 and the f at 64. The slurs, tenuto, sul D/sul A spanners and all pitches are unchanged. History: c088's protection of "phrasing 47–58" and c321/c338's "phrasing 47–63" concern slurs and bowing, and those are untouched. The change applies c088's own rule, that the S statements match their phrasing, to the one bar that did not match. It undoes no earlier fix: c258 shaped 56–57, and c358 shaped the lower strings, and neither touched 58–59. Checked on page 6 of the scratch PDF: the hairpins print cleanly under both violin staves.

### F2. Bars 68–69: the rit. runs through the codetta's upbeat run, and the new tempo arrives only after it (applied)

**What is wrong.** The S group ends with the deceptive cadence on F minor at 68.1 (Vln I A♭4 p, the whole orchestra p). On beats 3–4 of 68, Vln I and the flutes play a sixteenth-note run A♭4–B♭5 with a crescendo. It is the upbeat into the f codetta at 69, as the run at 32.4 is the upbeat into 33. The rit. printed at 66 has no end, and global.ily keeps slowing through the run, to crotchet = 88 at 68.3–4. "Tempo dell'Allegro" (126) is printed only at 69. As written, the players slow down most on the run and then jump 43% faster on the downbeat it leads to. The run's group, which begins on a weak beat and belongs to the bar it leads into, is split between two tempos.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 52, Ex. 3-9b and 3-11: rhythmic groups often begin on an unaccented beat and run into the next strong one. This run is such a group, and its goal is 69.1. PDF 49: the sense of tempo comes from the pace of the beats and the harmony. The harmonic goal of the rit. is the deceptive cadence at 68.1 (PDF 54: the phrase ends at the tonal goal), not the run after it. A tempo change belongs at the start of the group it governs. The score already does this elsewhere. At 32.4 the run into 33 is in tempo. At 46.4 and 146.4 the S pickups fall at the end of a poco rit. that only eases into a tempo 2 points slower (114 to 112), so there is no jump.

**Fix (applied, global.ily).** The rit. now spans 66.1–68.2 (MIDI steps 116, 111, 104, 98, 90, ending on the cadence), and `\tempo "Tempo dell'Allegro" 4 = 126` is moved from 69 to beat 3 of 68:
```
\tempo \markup \medium \italic "rit." \mt 116 s2 \mt 111 s2 \mt 104 s2 \mt 98 s2 \mt 90 s2
\tempo "Tempo dell'Allegro" 4 = 126 s2
\once \override Score.BarLine.extra-spacing-width = #'(-0.6 . 0)
\mark \default
s1*4
```
Rehearsal mark D stays on 69. Checked on page 7 of the scratch PDF: the tempo mark starts over the run at 68.3, above the flutes and above Vln I, and ends over D. Nothing collides, and the system and page breaks are unchanged. The parts take the mark from global, so it moves there too. The change is printed text and MIDI only, with no note changes.
