## Findings

### 1. Horns 3·4 score staff: stay in bass clef from 19 to 157. Applied.

**What is wrong.** The score is in C, so Hn 3·4 are written at sounding pitch. Between 19 and 157 their highest notes are G4 (99–100, 117–121) and F4 (65). Everything else lies from E♭4 down to D3. The staff still changes to treble at 27 and 110 (and back to bass at 103 and 135), so for most of the Allegro the two horns sit under the treble stave:

| Bars | Heads | On ledger lines, treble | On ledger lines, bass | Most ledger lines, treble / bass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27–32 | 17 | 16 | 7 | 2 / 1 |
| 37–43 | 14 | 10 | 6 | 3 / 1 |
| 56–76 | 45 | 38 | 11 | 3 / 2 |
| 80–83 | 9 | 5 | 5 | 2 / 2 |
| 97–102 | 15 | 9 | 7 | 2 / 3 |
| 110–134 | 48 | 37 | 18 | 3 / 3 |

Across 19–157 the current plan puts 144 heads on ledger lines (234 lines in all, 17 heads on three). With bass throughout, 83 heads are on ledger lines (104 lines, 7 heads on three: Hn 3's G4 at 99–100 and 117–121). In treble the two voices of the staff are both below the stave at once (61, 69–72, 111–116, 122–134). The upper voice's stems then run to the middle line (Gould PDF 28) and cross the empty stave, and the pitch shape of the inner horn lines is hard to see. In bass the same notes sit in the stave next to the trombones, which they often double (27–30, 97–102), so the conductor sees the doubling at a glance.

**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches." Bass needs at most three ledger lines anywhere in 19–157. Using it removes four clef changes (27, 103, 110, 135). The staff now changes clef six times instead of ten.
- Gould PDF 23 ("after periods of rests"): a return to the commonest clef after long rests applies "provided this is suitable for the following entry". The rests 44–55 and 84–96 are each longer than a system. Treble does not suit the entries at 56 (38 of 45 heads on ledger lines) or 97–102 (no better than bass), so the staff stays in bass. Where the treble clef is needed, at 158–161 (G4–C5 a2) and at 171–182, the existing changes stay (c405, c425).
- Gould PDF 28: stems of notes on two or more ledger lines reach the middle line. Under the old plan that applies to 73 Hn 3·4 heads in 19–157. With bass throughout it applies to 14 (E4–G4 above the stave at 65, 80–81, 99–101, 105–106, 113, 117–121).
- Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 10 (adler1 PDF 304): "In all new scores … the horn in F always transposes a perfect fifth down, whether the part is notated in the treble or the bass clef." Bass clef is a normal horn clef. Here it is a score staff at concert pitch, and the parts keep their own clef plan.
- Adler ch. 18 (adler2 PDF 300, 8a) limits ledger lines to four. That is a limit, not a target. It does not argue for treble where bass holds the notes in the stave.

### Beyond compliance

I asked where, within this reading, the page falls short of a master's score. The clefs and stems were correct note by note, but one staff was planned bar by bar instead of as a whole. Hn 3·4 carry the inner harmony of most of the Allegro: the pad under S (56–68), the codetta (69–76) and the G pedal (117–130). Those lines were written below the stave they sat on. Finding 1 fixes that, and it is the only proposal I make. I found no place where a change of notehead, stem or tail would make the music clearer to a player or conductor.
