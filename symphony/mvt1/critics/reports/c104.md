## Measurement
I counted the harmonised tutti beats: 70% or more of the parts sounding, three or more pitch classes, and the brass playing. On each beat, the test is whether the octave under the highest doubled note (the top line of the tune) holds any other chord tone.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: empty on 9% of tutti beats.
- Dvořák 9/i: empty on 1%.
- v123: empty on 31%. The bars are 28, 30–31, 41, 70, 72, 82, 101, 115–117, 131, 135–137, 140–141 and every beat of 158–161.

Dvořák's tuttis put the oboes, clarinets or Violin II inside that octave every time. At 9/i m. 442, for example, the tune is on E6 in Fl 2 and Vln I, with G5 in Ob/Cl and C6 in Vln II below it.

Spacing is otherwise sound, which confirms cycle 64. The bass is doubled in octaves, the lower intervals are wide and the upper ones close (RK's "normal order"), and the brass alone holds the complete chord.

## Findings

**1. Bars 158–161, the fff climax: the octave under the tune's top line is empty on every beat (APPLIED).**
- *Problem:* The tune is in three octaves: Fl a2 + Vln I/II, Ob 1 + Tpt 1, and Hn 1·2 + Vla/Vc. The harmony stops at the octave-4 counterline and sustains. So the gap between the top line and the next chord tone below is 1–2 octaves:
  - 158: E6 over C5;
  - 159: G6 over F4;
  - 160: A♭6 over C5, and on beat 3 over C4;
  - 161: F6 over A♭4.
- *Evidence:*
  - Rimsky-Korsakov, ch. III, PDF 85: "It is necessary to make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts." PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages."
  - Every chord in the appendix (PDF 505–506) has woodwind chord tones in close position directly under the top note.
  - Adler PDF 164, on Ex. XV-14 (Brahms 3/i 183–87): "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave". On Mahler 1/iv: "all the upper octaves are completely filled in; only the lowest octave … is left bare."
  - The exposition tutti already does this: Fl 2 holds A♭5 under C6 at 28.
- *Fix (recTwo.ily, `recTwoClOne`):* Cl 1 plays the counterline an octave higher at 158–162. Ob 2 and Hn 3 keep it at pitch.
  ```
  g''2\ff c'''2 | b''2\< f''2 | es''4\fff c'''4 r8 c'''8( bes''8 aes''8 | aes''2) f''2 | e''2\ff\> r8 e'8( f'8 g'8 | f'2) c''2 |
  ```
  The top octave now holds G5/C6, B5, E♭5/C6 and A♭5/F5 under the tune. Cl 1's highest note is C6 concert (written D6).
- *What it does not change:* the tune doublings, Hn 1·2 on the tune (cycles 14 and 24), the counterline pitches and the cycle-12 E♭ at 160, the dynamics and the instrument count. It is a register change in one doubling inside the protected 158–166, made for the reason above. It does not flip the horn question from cycle 14.

**2. Bars 31 and 140, the A♭6 peaks of the first theme: the same gap (APPLIED).**
- *Problem:* At 31 beats 1–2, A♭6 sits over A♭5 (Fl 2 doubles the tune), and the next different note is C5. At 140 beats 3–4, A♭6 is over D♭5.
- *Evidence:* the same as finding 1 (RK PDF 85).
- *Fix:*
  - tOne.ily, `tOneFluteTwo` bar 31: `c'''2 aes''2` (C6 is the fifth of iv).
  - recOne.ily, `recOneFluteTwo` bar 140: `aes''2\ff f'''2` (F6 under the held A♭6 of the N6).
- These are single-note changes. The A♭6 count (cycle 61) and the tutti layout (cycle 90) are unchanged.

**3. Bars 27–32: is it a "block-chord" tutti? A check against RK ch. III–IV and the appendix (not applied).**
- *Spacing and doubling:* these follow the rules. The brass chord is complete, the woodwinds are juxtaposed with the brass, the bass is doubled at the octave, and there are no voids except as in finding 2.
- *Where the "block" effect comes from:* rhythm, not spacing. Twelve wind and brass parts hold a whole note for each bar. Ob 2 and Cl 2 triple notes the horns and trumpets already hold (27: Ob 2 = Hn 1 = Tpt 2 on G4; Cl 2 = Hn 2 = Tpt 3 on E♭4). The only moving inner voice, the Vc/Bsn 1 tenor line, lies under five held brass notes in its own octave.
- *Sources:*
  - RK PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind."
  - Adler PDF 165: "sustained doublings tire the ear".
  - Adler's Brahms 3/i example (PDF 163) keeps the winds sustained while the inner strings move in eighths.
- *Proposal for the composer:* a cheap step, if the cycle-90 layout can be opened. Cl 2 doubles the tenor line instead of tripling Hn 2:
  ```
  c'2\ff es'2 | d'2 f'2 | es'2 c'2 | e'2 bes2 | aes2 f2 | g4-> g4-> r2
  ```
  This needs an answer to cycle 90 (27–32 and 167–170 are alike on purpose), so I have not applied it.

**4. Other places with an empty top octave (not applied; lower priority):** 41 (G6 over D♭5), 70 and 72 (F6/A♭6 over B4–D♭5), 82, 101, 115–117, 131, 135–137. Most of them are inside protected passages. The one cheap candidate is 72, where Fl 2 could take C6 under A♭6.

**5. Verified, no change.**
- 117–122: every octave is filled at 121–122. The top-octave gaps are at 115–117, where Fl 2 holds F5–G5 and the flutes rest at the start of the bar.
- 96: an octave bar, kept on purpose.
- 131–134: the theme is low and the brass is at f (cycle 84).
- Soft scoring: horn + cello over harp at 47–55, the sul tasto tremolo at 147–155, the low flute at 73–76 and the pp chorale at 175–179 are balanced. Each tune is either at least a dynamic level above its accompaniment or alone in its register.
