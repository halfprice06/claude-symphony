## Findings

### The themes under this lens

The motto, P, S and the horn call stand, and I propose no change to their notes. The reading's string tunes share a few traits. Brahms 1/iv, Tchaikovsky 5/ii, Brahms 3/iii, Tristan and Beethoven 5/ii each have one clear contour with a single high point. They rise by one or two characteristic leaps and fall back by step. The return of the tune is given to a single chosen string for colour (PDF 74–75, Ex. III-4 to III-7; PDF 99, Ex. III-59).

The score's themes have the same traits:
- **Motto.** A rising sixth and a chromatic fall, in four colours.
- **P (15–22).** It climbs by the motto's sixth and by thirds to D♭6 at 20, then falls by step to C5.
- **S (47–55).** It has one rising sixth in the pickup and one tenuto peak per two bars, and it reaches F5 at 52. Its climactic return (158–160) rises G5→E6 and then C6→A♭6, the head's sixth sequenced up a fourth.
- **Restatements.** They are already placed on single strings for colour: sul D and sul A at 55–59, sul G at 98–100, sul D at 177–182.

A listener will carry away the motto's sixth and fall, and the S head. That is the same kind of memorability as the fate motif and the Andante tune of Tchaikovsky 5.

### 1. Double basses sul A for the motto, 2–3 and 176–178 (applied)

At 1–3 the basses play the motto an octave below the cellos and bass clarinet: G1 | E♭2 (dotted minim tied to a quaver), D2 | C2, D♭2, C2. At 175–178 they play the chorale's motto bass under the cellos: G1 | E♭2 | D2 (a whole bar) | C2, D♭2. Both passages are pp, at Lento and at Più lento with the rit. of 177–178. c251 marked the cellos sul G in both places so that their D3 is stopped and vibrated, not the open D string (adler1 PDF 73–74). It left the basses unmarked, and on the bass D2 is the open D string. So at 177 the section most likely to ring an open string sounds an unvibrated D2 for a whole slow bar, an octave under the cellos' stopped D3. That is the effect Adler describes: the open string "has a characteristic sound which lends itself to great exploitation separately but stands out peculiarly in other situations". His rule covers "the open string on all string instruments" (PDF 73–74). He gives the remedy, a string marking when timbre requires it (PDF 73, 76). His bass fingering chart labels its passages "Sul E, Sul A, Sul D, Sul G" in exactly this way (PDF 106, Ex. III-70).

On the bass's A string, E♭2, D2, C2 and D♭2 lie between half position and about fourth position. That is low, comfortable and in one hand frame, so the motto has one colour and no string crossing. The G1 pickup stays on the E string, as the cellos' open G2 pickup is unavoidable (PDF 74). Fix, notation only:

I left the basses' held G2 at 6 and D2 at 11 unmarked. They are chord tones inside hairpins, not the motto line, and the cellos there are unmarked too, so the choice stays with the players (PDF 73).

### 2. The C-major S in violas and cellos, 147–154, uses open strings (no change)

In A♭ (47–55) every note of S is stopped. Transposed to C major at 147, the violas meet the open A4 and D4, and the cellos an octave below meet the open A3 and D3. These include the tenuto A at 148 (the tune's first held peak, where 48 has F4) and the D and A at 147.4, 149.3, 149.4, 152.3 and 153.4. By the letter of PDF 73–74 this is the same issue as Finding 1. But no single-string marking fits the phrase:
- The phrase goes down to C4 (violas) and C3 (cellos), below the D string.
- Sul G would put the violas' A4 in sixth position, the "very intense and stringent" upper G string (PDF 74), which is wrong for a dolce mf line.
- On the cello it would use the G string, which "carries less well than the others" (PDF 99).

What remains is fingering numbers, which Adler says are "best left to the performer" (PDF 73). A section plays a held expressive A with the fourth finger as a matter of course. Hn 1 also doubles the violas at the unison throughout. It should stay as it is.

### 3. The climactic skip C6→A♭6 at 160 stays (no change)

At 160 the first violins leap a minor sixth from C6 to A♭6 at fff, largamente, on the E string, a shift of about five positions. Adler lists wide skips on one string and into the extreme register as hard for a section (PDF 82–83, Ex. III-22, III-23), and puts A♭6 at the top of the reliable range (PDF 72). Here the skip is a crotchet at largamente, not a fast figure. Fl 1·2 double the A♭6, and Vln II and Ob 2 play it an octave below (c229, c241), so the pitch is secured even if some players slide. The leap is also the S head's sixth, which gives the climax its shape, and the finale cites the tenutos at 158/160 (c268). It should stay as it is.
