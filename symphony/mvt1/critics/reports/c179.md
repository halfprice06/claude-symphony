## Findings

### 1. Bars 41-43: Fl 1 holds G6 through a diminuendo to mf and p (applied)

Problem: after the sf attack at 41, Fl 1 holds G6 for six beats with a diminuendo to mf, then F6, then E♭6 at p, in unison with Vln I. Adler's register chart puts G6 in the flute's "a bit shrill" register and says that in such registers "in most cases the performer can only play extremely loud" (adler1 PDF 185). The written diminuendo cannot happen in the flute, and the flute's G6 stays on top of the four horns' call, which starts on beat 4 of 41. This is the only place in the movement where a flute holds a note at or above E6 below f (I checked full.midi velocities for both flutes).

Evidence from the reading: Dvořák's Slavonic Dance No. 8, m. 33 (adler2 PDF 213, score on PDF 211-212) is offered as "a model of exactly how one accomplishes a diminuendo from fortissimo to piano": the piccolo is rewritten an octave lower "to enable the player to accomplish the diminuendo", "all other instruments are also in controllable ranges", and part of the diminuendo is done by drop-out. Bizet does the same in "La Toupie" (PDF 202, 215: the violins stop after the attack, the violas continue). At 122-124 this score already follows the rule: Fl 1 drops from G6 to G5 at 124 before its diminuendo.

Fix (applied in trans.ily, `transFluteOne`): Fl 1 plays only the sf attack and leaves the diminuendo to Fl 2 (G5), the oboes and Vln I. Vln I keeps G6-F6-E♭6, which a violin can diminish.

The brass under the horn call is untouched (cycle-128 rule), and so is the horn call itself.

### 3. Holistic verdict against Brahms-Schoenberg Op. 25/i

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? On the points where the reading lets me compare directly, yes:
- **Opening.** Brahms opens with a bare unison cell, and Schoenberg gives it to a single wind colour (three clarinets), then ends the phrase in bassoon and horn (Ex. XVI-14, PDF 229). This score's motto 1-4 is set the same way: bass clarinet with low strings, the cor anglais at 5-8, and the colour changes only at the phrase joint.
- **Fragmented statements.** Schoenberg switches choirs to show the theme's 2 + 1 + 2 fragmentation (PDF 230). The development's motto dialogue 77-83 does the same, with one colour per fragment.
- **Restraint.** Schoenberg keeps the middle parts undoubled while the solo cello plays (PDF 230). The horn-and-cello second theme at 47-55 keeps the harp and strings as an undoubled pad.
- **Return.** Brahms's development begins by restating the opening bars. Here the coda brings back the opening colours: the low motto at 175-179, and the tam-tam once more, as Ravel recalls his single interlude colour (PDF 224).

The weakness I found is local and orchestral (Finding 1), not structural. The reading gives no basis for reopening any protected passage. I checked the obvious candidates: the unison doublings at 47-55 and 147-155 are different colours (PDF 229 concerns two like colours). The timpani-crescendo option before K at 157 (PDF 230) would conflict with cycle 83's rule against raising the density of 147-157, and the reading gives no reason strong enough to override it.
