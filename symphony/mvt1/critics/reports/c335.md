## Method

I read Gould PDF 40–52 in full (PDF 40 for the start of the ledger-line section) and rendered every page as an image at 110 dpi. I zoomed to 400 dpi on the PDF 50 example of rests in two-part writing to measure how far Gould moves a lower-part crotchet rest (one stave-space).

I viewed score pages 4, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12 and 16 at 110 dpi and zoomed to 300–600 dpi on Vln I at 31–32, 71–72 and 121–124, Vc/Cb at 72, Cl 1·2 at 112 and Tbn 1·2 at 115–116. I checked the bars on every page with pdftotext before and after my changes.

For a bar-by-bar rest check I compiled a probe copy (/tmp/critic-335/probe). It logs every rest and notehead with its section-file line, bar, beat, duration and staff position: 788 rests and 5397 noteheads. For each rest more than two spaces from the centre on a shared staff, a script (/tmp/critic-335/rc.py) reports the partner player's note at that moment and the rest's own player's notes before and after it.

This reading was assigned in cycles 135 and 245. Their fixes still hold: no 8va at 40–43, the c135 rest overrides, c245's gould-rest-snap (the probe finds no rest off the line grid), and no octave signs in the Violin I part. The findings below are new.

## Findings

### F1. Vln I 8va at 31 breaks the hammered descent at both ends and saves no ledger lines (applied)

**Bars:** 30–32, Vln I.

**What is wrong.** Bar 30 ends with G6 (four ledger lines) at pitch. The 8va then starts on the A♭6 of 31, which uses the same four lines, and stops before the C6 of 32. So the one line F6–A♭6–G6–F6–D6–C6–B5 changes written octave twice in two bars. Written F6 goes down a sixth to A♭5 for a rising third, and written D5 goes up a seventh to C6 for a falling step. The flute staff above shows the same notes at pitch.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 47: "A written change of octave tends to obscure the contour of a line and so, wherever possible, keep a phrase intact at the chosen octave. Change octave between phrases." The "incorrect" example on that page is a change inside a figure. PDF 46: "Use up to at least three ledger lines before transposing", and PDF 47: woodwind and strings "can use up to five ledger lines", and "a full score ... may replace ledger lines with the octave ... sign as necessary, to save space between staves". Here no space is saved, because bar 30 already needs four lines above the Vln I staff. The finale writes its Vln I A♭6/C7 "at pitch with ledger lines, no 8va sign, since v116" (coherence/finale.md), and movement I does the same at 41, 140 and 160.

**Earlier decisions.** Cycles 105, 135, 175 and 245 protect "8va at 31, 71–72, 121–123" for page fit (17 pages, K page break). c245 F3 kept it because "each sign covers a whole phrase". I answer both reasons. In the rebuilt score every page ends on the same bar as in v335 (14, 30, 38, 46, 63, 72, 84, 102, 110, 120, 130, 138, 146, 157, 162, 170, 182). Page 4 has no collision (checked at 300 dpi). Bar 31 is the middle of a phrase that starts at 29 and ends on B5 at 32, so it is not a whole phrase.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tOne.ily, tOneVlnOne:
`aes'''4.-> g'''8 f'''4 d'''4 | c'''4->\downbow ...` (the `\ottava #1` and `\ottava #0` are removed; a c335 comment is added above the line). Notation only. The part was already at pitch (c245), so the parts do not change.

### F2. Vln I 8va at 71–72 begins a step below an at-pitch note inside the same line (applied)

**Bars:** 69–72, Vln I (codetta counterline over the horns' P head).

**What is wrong.** Bar 70 has F6 and E♭6 at pitch (three lines). The 8va then starts on the D♭6 of 71, the next step down, which needs only two lines. Written E♭6 drops a ninth to D♭5. The line 69–72 is one four-bar phrase under the P head in the horns (the slur break at 70/71 is a bow change, not a phrase end). The sign only serves the A♭6 at 72, which needs four ledger lines, the same as Fl 1 directly above it, which plays the same notes at pitch.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 46 (three ledger lines at least before an octave sign), PDF 47 (keep the phrase intact; five ledger lines are allowed for strings; a score uses octave signs only to save space), PDF 47–48 (a change in the wrong place hides the intervals). Page 7 has room above the Vln I staff: "Tempo dell'Allegro" at 69 already sits higher than the A♭6 at 72.

**Earlier decisions.** The same protection as F1. Its reason is answered the same way: the page breaks are unchanged and nothing collides (page 7 viewed at 110 and 300 dpi).

**Fix (applied).** sections/clos.ily, closVlnOne:
`| des'''4.( c'''8 bes''4 des'''8 f'''8) | aes'''4->\downbow es'''4->\downbow c'''2->\downbow |`, with a c335 comment.

I keep the 8va at 121–123. It starts at a system start (PDF 44), covers one whole gesture (E♭6 to the fff A♭6 and its G6), ends with a corner just after the tremolo semibreve (PDF 44), and is followed by the correct *loco* (PDF 48). It also spares the divisi tremolo chords at 122–123 four ledger lines in each voice.

### F3. Two voiced rests pushed out of the stave with nothing to avoid (applied)

**Bars:** Cl 1·2 at 112 beat 4 (Cl 2's rest) and Tbn 1·2 at 115 beat 4 (Tbn 1's rest).

**What is wrong.** LilyPond moves a rest away from a note that the other player is holding even when that note began earlier and has no notehead at the rest's beat. At 112, Cl 1 holds B♭4 from beat 1 and Cl 2 rests on beat 4. Cl 2's crotchet rest is at staff position −6, below the stave, two spaces under Cl 2's own G4. At 115, Tbn 2 holds F3 and Tbn 1 rests on beat 4. Tbn 1's rest is at +8, a space above the stave, while Tbn 1's own A♭3 sits on the top line. One bar later (116) the same pair has the mirror case, and Tbn 2's rest is at −4. The other 23 voiced crotchet rests in the score also sit at ±4.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 50: "upper-part rests are usually placed above the centre stave-line, lower-part rests below the centre line". A rest "moves further from the centre of the stave or to outside the stave should parts otherwise collide". In the example on that page the lower part's crotchet rests are one space below centre, inside the stave (measured at 400 dpi). PDF 51: "The space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches."

**Fix (applied).** sections/dev.ily:
- line 112, devClTwo: `g'2. \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-4 r4 |`
- line 266, devTbnOne: `aes2. \once \override Rest.staff-position = #4 r4 |`

Both rests now sit at the house position (±4), level with their own notes, and mirror the Tbn 2 rest at 116 (checked at 600 dpi). Rest overrides are stripped from the parts (parts-lib.ily `shared-staff-grob?`). The Clarinet 2 and Trombone 1 part pages render byte-identical to v335.

### F4. Oboe 1 pickup below Oboe 2 at 82 and 139 (not applied; keep)

Ob 1's P-head pickup G4 starts under Ob 2's held C5, so Ob 1's quaver rest is above the stave and its note is below the other player. PDF 51 suggests separate staves "when the notation becomes this complicated". Here it is one quaver rest and one crossing note per bar, the stems separate the players, and two oboe staves would cost a staff on two pages with fixed breaks. Keep.
