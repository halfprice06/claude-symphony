## Findings

### 1. Bar 82, first A♭ climax: the head's lowest octave is the violas alone, and Bsn 1 adds a fourth C4 (APPLIED)

**What is wrong.** At 82 the P head is stated ff in three octaves over the A♭ bass:
- Top (G5–E♭6): Fl 1·2, Vln I.
- Middle (G4–E♭5): Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1, Vln II.
- Lowest (G3–E♭4): the violas alone.

Since c304 the brass hold the A♭ chord at f under the head: Hn 2 and Tpt 3 on E♭4, Hn 3, Hn 4 and Tbn 1 on C4, Tbn 2 on A♭3. The violas' G3–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 runs through exactly those pitches. c264 wrote that the violas "will barely be heard" there and kept them only because they "do no harm". Bsn 1 meanwhile holds C4 for the whole bar (`c'2 c'2`), the fourth instrument on that pitch. In bar 81 Bsn 1 plays the head fragment G3–E♭4 with the cellos, so at 82 it stops the line it has just played in that octave and becomes a filler.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151, on Mozart 40/iv: Mozart scores the octave below the tune with violas and clarinets, the next one lower with cellos and both bassoons, and "the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument that he uses." Adler PDF 147, on *Istar*: an instrument that "would not contribute significantly to the sound" is left out of that job. Adler PDF 154 (Smetana): the agile woodwinds take the figure and the brass sustain. Bsn 1's C4 contributes nothing beside three brass on C4. On the head, in its strong upper-middle register, it gives the lowest octave a reed edge that the violas do not have against f brass. The cellos cannot take this octave because they hold the A♭2 bass.

**Earlier decisions.** c254 settled the top and middle octaves, and c304/c308 settled the brass levels. This change keeps all of that, every pitch class, the held-brass chord (C4 stays in Hn 3, Hn 4 and Tbn 1) and the climax order 82 < 122 < 160. It answers c264's "violas alone … barely heard" with the solution that reason itself points to. Bar 82 is not quoted by any other movement.

**Fix (applied, `sections/dev.ily`, `devBsnOne`, dev bar 6):**
```
r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | r8\ff g8 es'4.-> d'8 des'8 c'8 | \partCombineAutomatic b4-> r4 b4-> r4 |
```
(was `r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | \partCombineAutomatic c'2\ff c'2 | b4-> …`). The ff sits on the rest so the shared bassoon staff prints one ff, the way Ob 1 does at 82. The stave stays in apart mode through 82, and Bsn 2's A♭2 quavers are unchanged. The C4→B3 into the 83 stroke is the violas' own step. A comment cites Adler PDF 147 and 151.

**Result.** The vl.txt diff adds 10 octave doublings of the head's last step D♭→C at 82.4.5 (Bsn 1 against Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1, Vln I·II, Vla). It removes 2 octave lines Bsn 1 C4→B3 into 83, which are now part of the line. There are no new fifths and no non-doubling octaves. I checked page 8 at 110–130 dpi: one ff, no collisions, same system breaks.

### 2. Bars 139–142: Fl 1 alone on the top octave to A♭6 (checked, keep)

Adler PDF 151 says the flute should "not [be] left alone in its upper reaches". Beethoven's top octave (PDF 152) has both flutes. At 139–142 the tune is in Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I·II at G4–A♭5, and Fl 1 alone doubles it at G5–A♭6. Fl 2 moves below Fl 1 in thirds and sixths (F6 under A♭6 at 140.3). I considered putting Fl 2 in unison with Fl 1, as c254 did at 82. I reject it. c194 moved the violins down an octave here so that A♭6 at 140 is a single sheen note and not a fourth A♭6 climax. Two flutes on A♭6 at 140 would match the top of 122 (Fl 1 + Vln I tremolo, fff) and blur the hierarchy 82 < 122 < 160. Fl 2's thirds also mean Fl 1 is not quite "alone" at the moments that matter (140.3–141). Keep.

### 3. Bars 117–121: Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2 ff in unison on the motto (checked, keep)

d'Indy marks the trombones ppp in a horn–trombone unison so they do not dominate (PDF 147–148). At 117–121 both pairs are ff, so the trombones will set the colour. Here, though, the motto is the foreground and has to cut through the rising P calls ff in winds and violins. The trombones are the motto's established carriers (chorale 175–179; protected at c241, c289, c291). Lowering them would weaken the line that leads into the fff at 122. Adler's case is a whole-orchestra unison, where the trombones only colour the line, so it does not apply. Keep.

### 4. Bars 167–169: the head in brass while the winds sustain (checked, keep)

This is the reverse of Smetana (PDF 154). c254 Finding 3 and c343 kept it as a summing-up (PDF 150) that should be heavy, and so the finale keeps the full multi-octave brass-and-strings statement. Nothing has changed since. Keep.

### 5. Bars 158–161 and 82/122: octave weight at the three climaxes (checked, no change)

At 160 the tune has three octaves, each with strings, woodwind and brass. Top: Fl 1·2 + Vln I. Middle: Ob 2 + Tpt 1 fff + Vln II. Lowest: Hn 1·2 + Vc. The trumpet makes the middle octave the strongest (PDF 151). The rest of the brass sustain ff (PDF 154). The trumpets enter after 15 bars' rest (PDF 156). At 122 the arrival is a held V♭9, not a unison. After Finding 1, 82 has a reed in each of its three octaves. The order 82 (ff, no percussion) < 122 (fff, tam-tam) < 160 (fff, cymbal, tune) is unchanged.

### 6. Bar 13: the horn call enters inside the string chord (checked, keep)

Hn 1's E♭3–C4–B3 lies under Vln II G4 and Vla C4/E♭4→D4. It is heard because it enters on beat 2 after the f tutti releases and the top line has faded to p (PDF 156, a rest before a colour). This bar is protected (horn call 13–14, c221 and later). Keep.
