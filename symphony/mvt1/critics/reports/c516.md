# Critic 516: Playability, instrument by instrument (v516)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 2 Bowed String Instruments: divisi, bowing, harmonics (adler1 PDF 24-70), third pass. I read every text page from PDF 24 to 70 in full and rendered every page from PDF 27 to 70 at 110 dpi and looked at each one (Ex. II-1 to II-77). Earlier passes on this reading were c226, c316, c406 and c426, and c438 used the same chapter for bowing as expression. I have not repeated their points. The chapter covers only the strings, so this report checks the five string parts in the score and in the parts that build-parts.sh produces.

Method. I dumped every string event of v516 with the LilyPond event listener that is already in the version folder (ev.ly, ev/el.ly) and wrote a bow simulator (/tmp/critic-516/scripts/bows.py). It follows Adler's rules: an unslurred note is a new bow (PDF 37), a slur is one bow and a tie continues it (PDF 37), an entry after a rest is down-bow on the beat and up-bow off it (PDF 38), and printed ⊓/V marks are obeyed. Otherwise the bows alternate. It gives each bow its length in seconds from the printed tempi, which slightly understates the length under a rit. It flags long bows, more than six notes on a bow at f, hairpins against the stroke, and f/ff attacks that come on an up-bow.

## Findings

### 1. Double basses 110-117: the ff dominant pedal at letter G is attacked on an up-bow. Applied.

**Problem.** The basses return to arco at 110 after the pizzicato of 103-109 (three beats' rest at 109). From there they play eight separate whole notes: C2 p< at 110, then F2-E2-E♭2-D2-D♭2-C2 under "cresc." (111-116), then G1 ff at 117, which they hold for fourteen bars to 130 under "change bow at will". Nothing between 110 and 117 is slurred, tied or rested, so the bows alternate. The arco entry on the downbeat is taken down-bow by habit (PDF 38). The count is then 110 down, 111 up, 112 down, 113 up, 114 down, 115 up, 116 down, and **117 up**. The bass section reaches the ff arrival of the dominant pedal, the start of letter G with the timpani and the trombone motto, at the point of the bow. The same count puts the crescendo of the 110 whole note on a down-bow. The cellos play tremolo from 117, so their bow direction does not matter there. "Change bow at will" lets each player change during the hold. It does not decide the direction of the attack, which the section plays together.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog of the bow than toward the tip ... The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural"; the composer should "indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". On the same page (Coriolanus, Ex. II-20) the bowing is marked so that "the following attack will be solid". PDF 39 adds that the bass bow is shorter, so a heavy stroke from the point is harder for the basses than for the violins. The other ff bass arrivals of the movement come down-bow: 26, 27, 82, 97, 158, 160, 167 and 170 by count, and 32 and 83 by mark. 117 is the one exception my simulation found. c438 ran a similar simulation but did not report this bar.

**Fix (dev.ily, devCb bar 110).**
```
c,1\p\<\upbow^"arco" |
```
This is one mark. The soft arco entry now starts at the point and its crescendo moves toward the frog. The seven bows that follow bring G1 at 117 onto a down-bow without a retake. Taking an up-bow after pizzicato means placing the bow at the point. The three beats' rest at 109 (1.4 s) is enough, and a p entry at the point is the usual soft attack (c426 made the same choice for the bass entry at 5). No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or slur changes. The V prints above the note under "arco" in the score (page 10) and in the bass part (page 3) with no collision.

Side effects. The direction at 131 is the players' choice either way, because the 117-130 tie carries "change bow at will". Nothing else changes.

### 2. The S head on a downbeat (Vla 107-110, Vc 103-106) starts down-bow. Checked and kept.

Every other statement of the S head puts its opening swell on an up-bow: the up-bow pickups at 46.4, 55.4, 97.4 and 146.4 (c338) and the mark at 171 (c438). Two statements begin on a downbeat with no mark. At 107-108 the viola starts after four bars' rest, so its swells on G4 and on the C5 tenuto fall on a down-bow. At 103-104 the cellos follow the up-bow end of the B-minor phrase, so their swells are also on a down-bow. Both go against Adler's habit (PDF 38). I tried an up-bow at the viola's 107 in the scratch copy and removed it again, for two reasons:
- With the up-bow, the second bow of the phrase (109-110) becomes a down-bow, and the crescendo at 110 into the mp cresc. at 111 then runs toward the point. That crescendo is the viola's link into the wedge.
- The 111-116 figure has six bows to a bar, so the flip carries through to 117, and the f triplets at letter G would start up-bow. Avoiding that takes a second mark at 111, which is overmarking (PDF 38).

As written, the swells at 107-108 are small hairpins at p inside a 3.8 s bow. Players shape them without strain, while the crescendo into 111 and the attack at 117 fall naturally. The cellos at 103 cannot change direction without a retake at the 102-103 seam. That would clip the last quaver (F♯4) of the ff B-minor phrase, which is protected (c483: sudden drop 102-103). Horn 1 and Bassoon 1 double the cellos at 103-106 and carry the swell. Both passages should stay as they are.

### 3. Bow loads at forte. Checked and kept.

The only bows with more than six notes at f are the slurred quaver climbs at 19-20, 69-70 and 139-140 (seven notes, 1.7 s, with a crescendo on the up-bow toward the frog) and the S cadence at 66-67. That cadence is eight notes in one down-bow at f, diminuendo from 66.3, about 4.3 s at crotchet = 112 and longer under the rit. Mendelssohn's six notes at f (PDF 39, Ex. II-22) are a guide for fast notes, where the bow speed sets the limit. At 66-67 the notes are slow, the bow runs toward the point with the diminuendo, and the violins have the whole bow. It is inside the limit for violins. The S chain is protected (c431, c471). It should stay as it is.

### 4. Colour devices from the chapter. No change.

The chapter's colours are already used where the models would use them: the "div. sul tasto" tremolo halo at 147-155 (PDF 51, Debussy Ex. II-51), sul G/D/A for single-string lines (PDF 33-35, Mahler Ex. II-16), unmeasured tremolo for the climaxes, measured tremolo written out for the 127-130 stretto (PDF 50, Ex. II-47), and pizzicato basses under the S theme. I looked for a place where sul ponticello, harmonics, mutes or a fingered tremolo would make a passage more vivid in the manner of the models. I did not find one I could support. The eerie passages (77-81, 111-116, 123-126) are marked p or crescendo and lead into the development's climaxes. Sul ponticello there (PDF 52, Strauss Ex. II-52) would sound like Mahler or Strauss, not like Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák or Elgar in a first movement. No natural or artificial harmonic gives the C5, G4 or E4 that cross into movement II (PDF 62-67). Mutes were rejected earlier (c076). "No change" is the right result for this item.
