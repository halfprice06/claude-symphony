## Findings

No finding leads to a change. They are ranked by how close each comes to a real fault or a real gain.

### F1. Bars 125–126: the soft horn-call recall (new since the last Unit 20 pass; keep)

c581 added Hn 1 E♭3 (125.2), C4 (125.3–126.1), B3 (126.2–3), mp espr. > p, over the G roll. at.py gives the harmony: 125 is the i6/4 over the G pedal (G–C–E♭), and 126.2 is V (G–B–D). In the reading's terms:
- E♭3–C4 is a chordal skip inside one chord (section 2, Ex. 20-2). Both notes are chord tones.
- C4–B3 is the 6–5 of the cadential 6/4. It is a consonant resolution, not a figuration tone.
- So the recall adds no dissonance approached or left by leap (PDF 336).

The one point to check is 125.3. Vln I and Ob 1 move E♭5→C5 on the same beat as Hn 1 leaps E♭3→C4. The two parts go from a double octave to an octave by contrary motion. Section 16 (PDF 341) warns against octaves caused by figuration, and a chordal skip is exactly the kind of figuration that causes them (Point 8). But these are not parallels, and both E♭s double the 6/4's third, which Cl 1 and Vln II also hold at E♭4. The reading excludes "doublings in free texture" from the rule (PDF 341, parenthesis; footnote to Ex. 20-27). vl.txt lists 124–127 only as unison doublings (Hn 1 with Cl 2, Hn 3, violas and cellos on C4→B3 at 126.2). Nothing needs fixing.

Memorability. A listener recognises the call by its rising sixth and the B left open. At 125.2 Hn 1's E♭3 is the only E♭ below E♭4 in the texture, so the leap can be heard. The doubling from the C4 on makes the B3 sound as a voice of the chord rather than a solo, and c581 intended exactly that: 13–14 stays the only undoubled horn melody. Exercise 4 (PDF 345) asks for one theme varied in many ways. The call now has four forms:
- 13–14: solo, unresolved.
- 41–42: four horns.
- 125–126: soft, resolved into V.
- 175–177: Cl 1, in the coda.

Keep.

### F2. Bar 159: violas E4→F4 with the tune (c522; keep)

At 159.1 the tune and all its doublings (Fl 1·2, Vln I, Vln II, Ob 2, Tpt 1, Hn 1·2, violas, cellos) are on E; at 159.2 on F; at 159.3 on G, over V7 on the G pedal. F is an unaccented passing tone filling the third E–G (Ex. 20-7a, PDF 332), and the violas now take it with the other doublings. Before c522 the violas held E against the tune's F, a second between doublings of the same line. This is now correct. The doubled passing motion is octaves by doubling (PDF 342, footnote). Keep.

### F3. Complementary rhythm under the themes' long notes (keep)

holds.py lists 58 theme notes of two beats or longer. Under every long note of S at 56–68, 97–102 and 151–166, some other part strikes a note at least every half beat. The cello arpeggios, the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline and the violas' quavers do this, as in the reading's Beethoven example: a long note over steady motion in another voice (Ex. 20-10, PDF 333). At 147–152 the cor anglais fills on the third beat of 147, 149 and 151, and Vln II's sul tasto tremolo keeps the sound alive.

The texture is still for two beats or more only in these places:
- 46.1, the poco rit. into S.
- 50.3–4 and 150, S's half cadence, in both exposition and recapitulation.
- 172–174, the solo cello in the coda, Più lento.
- 5–8 and 178–182, the Lento frame.

Each one is a phrase end or a Lento passage. At 50.3–4 I considered asking the harp to keep its arpeggio through beats 3–4. I rejected it:
- Section 9 says complementary rhythms are good "as long as they are not overdone".
- The harp figure has the same shape in every bar of 47–55 (beats 1–2, then a hold), so the hold at 50 belongs to the pattern.
- The recapitulation (150) leaves the half cadence open in the same way, so the two statements match.

Keep.

### F4. Whole-step lower neighbours in the melodies (keep)

lnscan.py found eight a–(a−2)–a figures in Vln I, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vc and C.a.:
- 20.1 Vln I E♭5–D♭5–E♭5: the D♭ is the root of the N6 chord (bass D♭).
- 40.3 and 40.4.25: Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1 inside the run to G6; B♭ and E♭ belong to the E♭ chord.
- 63.2.5 Cl 1 G4–F4–G4, with Hn 2 on F4.
- 151.4.5 C.a. A4–G4–A4.
- 152.3 Vc B3–A3–B3, with Hn 1, Vln II and violas on A.

In each case the lower note belongs to the harmony or doubles another part:
- At 20.1, 40.3 and 40.4.25 the lower note is a chord tone that other parts also sound.
- At 63.2.5 Cl 1's F4 is the counterline, doubled by Hn 2.
- At 151.4.5 the C.a.'s G4 is the fifth of the C7 chord (E3–D4–B♭5 with Vln II's C5).
- At 152.3 the cellos' A3 is the third of F, also in Hn 1, Vln II and the violas.

Ex. 20-23 (PDF 340) raises a lower neighbour so that it leans into the main note. Here the raised note would clash with the harmony. At 20.1, D♮ would sound against the cellos' and basses' D♭. At 151.4.5, G♯ would replace the chord's fifth. The chromatic lower neighbours that are real (16 and 168 C–B–C; 36 B♭–A–B♭; the fugato's G–F♯–G at 86) are already written as minor seconds (Point 5). Keep.

### F5. Repeated notes in the melodies (keep)

reps.py found 21 immediate repetitions in the melodic parts:
- 15.4.5, 27.4.5 and 143.4.5 C (with Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 at 27): the portato C of the P head. It is quoted by the finale at its 199–204, and the repeated C is the head's identity.
- 157.4.5 G5: the motto's own pickup into 158. c511 tested an F5 here and rejected it because it causes unisons with Cl 1.
- 160.4.5 Ab5 in Ob 1 and Cl 1: the counterline restriking its A♭ under the tune's held notes (protected c407/c408).
- 35.4.5 B♭4: the transition's P head at the lower fifth.
- The rest are in inner horn and clarinet parts (78, 79, 96, 103, 127, 128, 131, 167) or Tpt 1's G4 on the stroke at 32.

The reading's "ugly repetitions" (PDF 339) are diatonic neighbour figures that sound static. None of these is a neighbour figure. Keep.

### Would a listener hum them afterwards?

The motto, P and S meet the test, for the reasons c421 gave:
- The motto is the C–D♭–C neighbour enlarged (Ex. 20-11).
- P has the widening head with an arpeggiated breakthrough to D♭6 (section 2).
- S has a long note followed by figuration (Ex. 20-10).

Two places could sing more, and I tested both against the reading:
- The half cadence at 50 (F3). Filling it would break the harp's pattern, and the recapitulation leaves 150 open in the same way.
- 125.3 (F1). Making the call a true solo would undo c581's choice to keep 13–14 the only undoubled horn melody.

Neither change clears the bar the task sets, so I propose none. This agrees with c421 and c511, now with three more scans and the post-v511 material checked.
