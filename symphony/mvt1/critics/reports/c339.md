## Findings

### 1. Second theme 47–55 against Dvořák's second group. Keep.

Dvořák's second group (letter B, bars about 107–118, printed p. 12) gives the lyrical line to Horns III·IV and divided cellos, both "p espress.", under first and second violins playing "pp leggiero" semiquavers, with pizzicato basses. This score's 47–55 gives the S head to Hn 1 and the cellos (dolce) over harp arpeggios, Vln II metà, violas div. and Cb pizz. pp. Rimsky-Korsakov singles out exactly this pairing: "The combination of horns and 'cellos, frequently employed, produces a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone" (PDF 79). The string pad also follows his rule for sustained string chords: a held chord should not have some parts divided and others not (PDF 88). Here Vln II plays half the section (metà) on one note beside the violas divided in two, so each of the three pad lines is half a section, which gives the even balance he asks for. Dvořák's figuration is in the violins; this score's is in the harp. A conductor who knows Op. 60 would recognise the texture and find nothing out of style. No change.

### 2. Soft brass as colour, and the reserve of the full brass. Keep.

Dvořák opens his development (printed pp. 21–22, bars about 195–211) with trombones and tuba holding ppp chords over a ppp timpani roll while the violins hold long notes, and he keeps the trumpets for a few tutti strokes. That is Rimsky-Korsakov's "harmony in the brass" at its quietest (PDF 100–101: trombones and tuba with the bass in octaves, close order; Ex. 96, PDF 273: brass pp at the cadence; Ex. 115, PDF 289: wind fading as the brass enter p). This score does the same in its coda: the trombone chorale pp dolce with tam-tam 175–179 over the motto bass, and the brass fade at 162–166 before the harp. It keeps trumpets out of melody except the f codetta statement at 69–72, which Rimsky-Korsakov's rule for doubling a trumpet in the octave above (three or four winds, PDF 75) supports: Fl 1·2 and Ob 1 lie above Tpt 1 there. No change.

### 3. Where Dvořák 6/i differs, and why the score should stay as it is.

(a) Exposition repeat and length. Dvořák's movement is 562 bars in 3/4 with a repeated exposition (repeat at the end of the exposition, about bar 178); this one is 182 bars in common time without a repeat. A repeat would add about two minutes to a 7½-minute movement whose section proportions were measured and protected (c215, c243, c283, c333). Keep.

(b) First tutti in the major. Dvořák's first big tutti ("Tempo I. Grandioso", letter A, bar 51) puts the main theme in full orchestra ff in D major with trumpets and trombones on the harmony. This score's first tutti (27–32) is the hammered C-minor P with the bass climbing chromatically; the major full-brass statement is on the "Do not reuse" list for the finale. Keep.

(c) Coda. Dvořák builds a long ff coda (bars about 509–539, "pesante", "ff marcato sempre"), then drops to p, "poco sostenuto" with horns pp and strings ppp (about 540–555), and closes with five bars ff "in tempo". This score has its forte coda at 167–170, then the soft coda 171–182 and attacca into movement II. The quiet reflection before the end is the same gesture; Dvořák's final ff bars would undo the attacca and anticipate the finale's C-major close (protected: c239, c329, coda length). Keep.

### 4. Discords inside one instrument pair (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 90 rule 4, PDF 102). Keep.

Rule 4 asks for concords in instruments of one kind and discords (seconds, sevenths) in different colours "except when discords are to be emphasised"; PDF 102 repeats it for brass. pairs.py finds held seconds or sevenths inside one pair, a beat or longer, at only these places:

- 116: Cl 1·2, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 each hold A♭/G♭ (the augmented-sixth sonority over the wedge). The dissonance is the point of the bar and is protected (c272, c319, wedge 111–116). Rimsky-Korsakov's exception applies.
- 123: Hn 1·2 G4/F4 and Tbn 1·2 G3/F3 for a bar after the fff. Protected exit order (c318, c324); the V7 there is meant to grind against the pedal.
- 134: Hn 1·2 G4/F4 for a bar under Tpt 1·2 B4/G4. The seventh F4 sits in Hn 2 alone; moving it to another horn would cross Hn 2 and Hn 3 on separate staves for one bar and change the tutti layout that HISTORY.md protects as "tutti layout at 27–32 and 167–170 (alike on purpose); 131–134". The second is in the middle of a full ff chord, where Rimsky-Korsakov's own tutti appendix (PDF 505) has horn seconds. Keep.
- 39–40 and 71: Bsn 1·2 hold a minor seventh over the bass (A♭3/B♭2; D♭4/E♭3). These are V7 chords where Bsn 2 doubles the string bass and Bsn 1 the seventh; at a compound distance a seventh is not the close discord the rule warns about. 71 is protected (c331).

### 5. Open fifths in wind pads (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 97). Keep.

PDF 97 asks that a wind middle harmony over a string bass be complete in itself, without open fourths and fifths. pairs.py finds bare fifths or fourths held two beats or more inside a pair almost only in tutti, where other instruments supply the third. The exposed cases check out: bar 9 Bsn 1·2 G3/C3 double the cellos and basses at pitch (they are a doubling, not a pad, PDF 78); 103 Hn 3·4 D4/A3 have F in Hn 1, Bsn 1 and the cellos; 156 Bsn and Hn 3·4 in fifths have D4 in Hn 2 and D5/D6 in the violins. The c224 repair at 145–146 (clarinets in a third, not a fifth) is still in place. No change.

### 6. Space between melody and chord at 25 and 137 (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85–86, 96). Keep.

gaps.py finds one recurring hole wider than an octave in a loud sonority: at 25.2–25.3 and 137.2–137.3, between the chord's top (F4 in Vln II, Cl 1, Hn 1) and the tune A♭5–G5 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I in unison). PDF 86 and 96 condemn empty space between the parts of a harmony, especially forte. Here the upper note is not a harmony part but the melody, which Rimsky-Korsakov says stands out by its position at the top (PDF 54); the chord below it is close and complete, and the passage is a crescendo from mf into the stop chord. The scoring is protected (HISTORY.md: "Fl1 in unison (not octave above) at 23–25", "three-beat groups 23–25, 135–137"; c274 kept Fl 1/Ob 1/Vln I 23–25, 135–137). No change.
