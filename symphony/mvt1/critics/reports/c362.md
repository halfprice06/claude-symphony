## Findings

None of the points in this reading calls for a change to v362. All the score's applied chords resolve in the way Unit 25 prescribes. I checked them all: vii°7/iv at 30, V7 of III at 39–40, V4/2–I6 of A♭ at 44–45, V6/5 of IV at 51 and 151, vii°7/vi at 63, vii°7/V at 70, V♭9 of E at 108, the German sixth at 12 and the augmented sixth at 116. Each has its temporary leading tone in one upper voice or in the bass, and the only exception is bar 40, which is protected. Each 7th resolves down. Where a 7th does not resolve in its own voice, it moves into the bass, as at 25.4→26, or is delayed through a figure that ends on the note of resolution, as at 71→72 and 164→165 (PDF 406–409). Every spelling I checked matches the function:

- C♯ at 148
- C♭ at 35 and 70
- G♭ and F♭ at 37–38 and 61
- E♮ at 30 and 63
- F♯ at 12 and 116

This pass adds nothing to c272's two fixes (116 and 51). Both still hold.

The changes since c272 do not introduce new problems under this reading. I checked them against it:

- v274: bass G♭ through bar 60.
- v283: tonic held under the passing D♭ at 143.
- v313: Vln II E♭5 held through 54; violas A♭3/C4 at 55; V6/5 of IV complete at 151.

## Checked and kept (with the reason the reading does not overturn)

1. **Bar 114→115: B4 to A♭4 in Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II, and B3 to A♭3 in Tbn 1.** Here the leading tone of the diminished 7th (B–D–F–A♭ over D) falls by an augmented second to A♭ in the ♭II chord. Unit 25 allows melodic augmented seconds only in rising or falling third-sequences (PDF 438, 439 point 8). By that rule this is outside the norm. I keep it for three reasons. First, the line C5–B4–A♭4–A♭4 (113–116) is the descending harmonic-minor tetrachord. It carries the A♭ that c272 tied into the ♭9 at 117. Second, A&S's own exception for cross relations softened by a common tone and a held line (PDF 421) covers the A♭, which is already sounding in Vln I and Tpt 1 on beat 4 of 114. Third, c252, c319 and c352 protect the wedge's harmony. Moving B to C would turn 115 into D♭maj7 and change protected harmony. That trade is worse.

2. **Bar 25.4→26: the 7th C4 of iiø7 (Cl 2, Hn 2, Va) leaps up into the stop chord.** The B that resolves it appears only in the bass (B1, B2) and an octave above (B4). This is the transferred resolution into the bass of Ex. 24-12 (PDF 406–407), which the reading accepts. The stop chord's layout is on the cross-movement list, because the finale uses the same trumpet layout at its 138. Keep.

3. **Bar 40: doubled temporary leading tone D (Hn 3·4, Va, Ob 1, Fl 1) and tripled 7th A♭ in V7 of E♭.** This violates PDF 419 and 439. It is kept for the reason c234 gave (the horn pair) and c252 and c272 confirmed. Every D goes to E♭, A♭3 goes to G3, and Vln II's A♭4 is answered by G4 in the violas and oboe in the same register (PDF 406–407). The protection's reason holds.

4. **Bar 156: B♭ major (♭VII) to F minor (iv), not to a position of V7 as in Ex. 24-18 (PDF 410–411).** The cello's A♭3 on beat 4 anticipates Hn 3's A♭3 at 157, so the bar is not a ♭VII7 whose 7th fails to resolve. The idea Handel's example teaches, using ♭VII to make the approach to V less predictable, is present: ♭VII–iv–6/4–V7, cut off by ♭VI at 160. Keep (protected by c346, "156–166 pitches").

5. **Bar 63.3: vii°7/vi with Vln I's D♭6 as an échappée, and the resolution transferred two octaves down.** This is the weaker transfer in PDF 407. It is protected (c221), and the bass E→F carries the progression. Keep.
