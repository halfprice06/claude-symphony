## Overall verdict
The architecture of v58 now matches Brahms 1/i point by point:
- a slow introduction that plants both themes (motto 1–4, second-theme leaps 9–12, horn E♭→C at 13);
- a second key whose closing turns to its minor mode (A♭ codetta 69–72, then A♭ minor colour 73–76; Brahms goes from E♭ major to E♭ minor);
- a development that reaches a remote key (B minor at 97) and builds a dominant pedal (109–118);
- a compressed recapitulated first group (123–135);
- a second theme recapitulated in the tonic major (139–158);
- a coda that brings back the introduction's motto softly (171–178).

The climax at 114 falls at 61% of the timed length, about where Brahms and Beethoven place theirs. A conductor would not find a structural error. What still marks the score as not a master's is mainly scale and tempo handling, not the notes.

## Findings

### 1. Tempo fragmentation in the Allegro (global.ily) — partly applied
**Problem.** v58 has 19 tempo indications in 178 bars. Inside the Allegro (15–166) the plain tempo of 126 holds in only 92 of 152 bars (60%). The other bars are under poco rit., meno mosso, tranquillo, allargando, sostenuto, largamente or rit. Four indications fall in 12 bars (66 rit., 69, 73, 77). Critic 23 had already flagged this.

**Evidence.**
- Beethoven 9/i keeps one tempo for 547 bars (corpus file; the only exceptions are two ritard./a tempo pairs at 506–511).
- Dvořák 9/i has no tempo word between the Allegro molto and the end (Mutopia conductor track and parts).
- Brahms 1/i has three tempo marks: Un poco sostenuto, Allegro, Meno allegro (Wikipedia).

In these models the second theme is in tempo, and its broadening is written into the note values. That is already true here: the second theme moves in half notes. Many tempo words read as Tchaikovsky's practice, not Brahms's.

**Fix (applied):**
- delete `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco più tranquillo"` (73) and the `\tempo "a tempo" 4 = 126` (77) it required (bars 73–76 are already pp whole notes under a solo flute);
- change both `\tempo "Poco meno mosso" 4 = 100` (47, 139) to `4 = 112`.

This leaves 17 indications. Removing the word at 73 reverses the cycle-23 change (v43). I do this on critic 23's own evidence of four changes in 12 bars.

**Further step (not applied):** drop "poco rit." at 137, since 137–138 is already a pp pedal before the meno mosso.

### 2. Absolute scale: no exposition repeat (plan-level; tested separately, not applied)
**Problem.** The movement lasts 7:13 (MP3). Brahms 1/i lasts about 13–17 minutes, with its exposition repeated (Wikipedia), and Dvořák 9/i also repeats its exposition (corpus). The proportions are sound (critics 13 and 23), so the gap is absolute size. In v58 a listener hears the 62-bar exposition only once before the development takes it apart.

**Fix.** Add repeat signs around the exposition. No first-time bar is needed:
- bar 76 ends on an A♭ pedal under D♭-minor colour, which goes naturally to the motto's G upbeat over C at 15 (a ♭VI→i move like Brahms's return from E♭ minor);
- the `\key c \minor` at 77 prints after the repeat barline, as in Dvořák 9's first-time bar.

In global.ily:
- after the intro's `s1*14 \break`, insert `\bar ".|:"`;
- after the `s1*4` of 73–76, add `\bar ":|."`.

I built this in /tmp/critic-039/repeat-test: no warnings, layout unchanged. It takes the movement to about 9:20, which conflicts with the plan's "6 to 6.5 minutes" (movement-1-plan.md, line 55). This is Dan's decision, so I did not put it in the work copy.

### 4. Comparison with v48: nothing got worse in the notes; one note on 134–135
Ten cycles brought these changes:
- the viola counterline at 19–22;
- trumpets in the timpani rhythm at 27–31;
- eighth-note chords at 33 and 135;
- violas in eighths at 103–108 (this removed v48's octaves with the chromatic bass);
- the brass head at 163–166;
- the contrabassoon C1 at 163–166;
- dynamic grading.

None of them adds a parallel or a build warning, and the density profile is unchanged except for the intended thinning (flutes rest 103–106).

One effect to know about: since v53, bars 134–135 are identical in pitch and scoring to 32–33 (V half-notes, then the same c'''8 ff tutti chord, then rests). Brahms rewrites his recapitulated transition (Wikipedia: the recapitulation "stays in the tonic rather than following the exposition's tonal path"). Here, what follows 135 differs (bass c–d–es–d with the violins' quiet motto, recalling 15–16), so the literal match lasts only one beat. No change requested. The ff at 135 itself stays settled (cycle 35).

## Judgements, no change requested
- **Introduction.** At 14 bars and 15% of the time, it is proportionally close to Brahms's 37 bars of 6/8. Its job (planting the motto, the second-theme sixth and the E♭/E♮ pivot) is done. The timpani roll on C at 1–4 comes back at 177–178, so the frame exists without Brahms's pulse.
- **Motto saturation.** The head appears in its original rhythm in every section, and also transformed: augmentation (109–113, 171–175), syncopated augmentation (123–126), quarter–half displacement (121–122), the major form (147–148), and the inversion of its interval as the second theme's rising sixth. This matches Brahms's practice. More literal statements would hurt.
- **Climaxes.** 114 (fff, tam-tam) and 156 (fff, cymbal) are separated by the ff recapitulation, followed by the denied tutti at 166. The order resembles Brahms (the loudest music is inside the form, and the coda subsides), not Beethoven 9, whose coda builds the 513–530 ostinato to a final ff unison. For a symphony whose finale must redeem the opening, the Brahms model is the right one.
- **Coda.** Augmented motto, chorale and E♮ viola ending (171–178) do what Brahms's Meno allegro does with less material. Keep it.
