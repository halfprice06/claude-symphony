## Findings

### 1. First theme, 19–22: no inner voice moves; Vln II holds F4 for four bars (applied)
At 15–22, Vln II and Vla play off-beat syncopations, `r8 X8~ X4`, on fixed pitches.
- Vln II plays only f' from 19 to 22 (seven half-bars), and g' or f' from 18 to 25.
- Bsn 1 doubles the cello bass in unison.
- The harmony is thin: on the downbeats of 19 and 20 there is no A♭ (`chords.py`: "F1|C F" and "C#2|C# F").

The theme's continuation, rising to the Neapolitan d♭''', therefore runs over a frozen middle. Tchaikovsky 6/i 30–34 accompanies the same kind of statement with a figure taken from the theme. There is also an outer-pair fault: the Vc/Bsn E♭3→D♭3 (19 beat 3 → 20) moves in octaves with the tune's e♭''→d♭''.

Fix:
- The violas get a sustained espressivo counterline in contrary motion to the tune. It plants the second theme's head rhythm (`X2.~ X8 Y8`) in the first group.
- Vln II takes over the violas' syncopated chord tones.
- The bass's third beat in 19 moves to A♭.

```
tOneVla, bars 5–8:     aes'2.~(^\markup \italic "espr." aes'8 g'8 | f'2 aes'2 | aes'4 g'4 f'2~ | f'2 es'2) |
tOneVlnTwo, bars 5–8:  r8 c'8~ c'4 r8 c'8~ c'4 | r8 des'8~ des'4 r8 des'8~ des'4 | r8 des'8~ des'4 r8 d'8~ d'4 | r8 d'8~ d'4 r8 g'8~ g'4 |
tOneVc, bar 5:         f2 aes,2      tOneBsnOne, bar 5:  f2\mf aes,2
```

Every chord is now complete:
- 19: F minor;
- 20: D♭;
- 21: G7, with f' as the seventh;
- 22: f' resolves to e♭' on the C-minor beat.

Not one note of the tune or its doublings changes, so the protection of the T1 sentence (cycles 11 and 31, melody lens) is respected. Optional: if the viola is covered in the crescendo to ff at 20, mark it `\mf\<` … `\f` together with Vln I.

### 2. Clashes between the counterline and the accompaniment, 56–57 and 63 (applied)
The exposition counterline (Cl 1 + Hn 2) is good, but three accompaniment notes contradict it:
- **56:** the cello arpeggio reaches e' (A♭+) on beat 3 while the violas still hold e♭'. That is E♭ against E♮ in the same octave for one beat. The harp (f♭) and Cl 1 (e') agree with the cello; the viola is a beat late.
- **57 beat 4:** the viola's f♭' sounds against the counter's f' (a minor second) and the tune's e♭''. No other part has F♭ there: the harp and cello play F♮.
- **63 beat 1:** Hn 4 holds g (the third of E♭7) under the counter's suspended a♭' (Cl 1 + Hn 2), so the resolution sounds against the suspension. Cycles 12 and 32 removed the same fault at 123–124 and 166.

Fix:
```
tTwoVla, bars 10–11:   r4 <c' es'>4\p <c' e'>2 | r4 <des' f'>2 <des' f'>4 |
tTwoHnFour, bar 17:    bes2\> g2 |
```
Hn 4 now enters g only after the resolution, and it moves g→a♭ into 64, in contrary motion to the tune. A whole-bar b♭ made new octaves with the tune's B♭→A♭ at 64, so I rejected it. Hn 3·4 dynamics remain equal on the shared staff. The counterline itself is unchanged (critic 27 asked for that).

### 3. Wedge, 103–108: every inner part holds whole notes, and the viola doubles the bass (applied)
In 103–108 only the tune moves. Every other part holds whole notes or tremolo chords.
- The violas (with Hn 3, Tpt 3 and B.cl) play `c' e' es' d' des' d'`, which is the chromatic bass an octave up. `vl2` flags this as octaves at 105, 107 and 108, and at 108 the viola also moves in octaves with the tune's D♭→D.
- The tremolo also blurs the rhythmic plan. It is unmeasured, so it is faster than the measured triplets at 109 and the cello's measured accelerando at 119–122.

Tchaikovsky 6/i 224–228 sets the same contrary-motion wedge with measured motor figures in every layer.

Fix: the violas play measured eighths built on the first theme's turn (b8 c'8 from bar 16, the figure the violas already use at 131–134). The line holds c', descends to a♭, and returns to c', which leads by a semitone to the b of the 109 triplets.
```
devVla, bars 27–32:
c'8\mf\cresc c'8 b8 c'8 c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 |
c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 | c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 | c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 aes8 aes8 g8 aes8 | aes8 aes8 g8 aes8 aes8 aes8 g8 aes8 |
c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 c'8 c'8 b8 c'8 |
```
Effects:
- The violas continue their 97–102 eighths, and the passage now accelerates measurably: eighths at 103, triplets at 109, sixteenths at 122.
- The tune, its long notes, the dynamics and the 106 figure are unchanged. Cycle 21 protected the top line at 103–108 and cycle 18 the crescendo; both stay intact.
- Vln II keeps its tremolo, so the shimmer remains.
- A first try with a♮ at 108 beat 3 made octaves with Cl 1, Hn 1, Ob 2 and Vln II, so 108 returns to c'.
