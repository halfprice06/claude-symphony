## Findings

### 1. The tutti layouts and the three A♭ climaxes meet every rule of the reading (no change)

I checked each tutti beat by sounding pitch against the appendix.

- 26 and 138: bass B1 or D2 in Cbsn, tuba and Cb; B.tbn and cellos an octave up; bassoons on D3 and F3 in the tenor; a complete B–D–F–A♭ chord in horns and trumpets; Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2 and Cl 1·2 stacked in close position B4–B5 with the violins; timpani G2 under the diminished seventh. This is chord 1 and chord 3 of PDF 505.
- 82: A♭1–A♭2 in octaves (Cbsn, tuba, Cb; Bsn 2, B.tbn, Vc, timpani), held brass A♭–C–E♭ at f, head ff in three octaves (Bsn 1 and violas E♭4; Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1, Vln II E♭5; Fl 1·2, Vln I E♭6). This is the grading of chord 2 and Ex. 206.
- 122: fff in every group, all upper strings and cellos in tremolo, timpani G2 roll, tam-tam. This is chord 4 (*The Tsar's Bride*, p. 301).
- 160: bass A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–C4 (octave, fifth, fourth, third), tune in three octaves (A♭6 Fl 1·2 and Vln I; A♭5 Ob 2, Tpt 1, Vln II; A♭4 Hn 1·2, Tpt 2, Vc), Tpt 3, trombones and tuba ff under the fff tune, violas in repeated quavers as in Ex. 205–206, cymbal added on the restatement as in Ex. 206.
- 170: C1–C2–C3 bass, split bassoons C3/D4, complete brass chord, interlocked top with Fl 1 F6, Fl 2 D6.

The three climaxes are scored in three different ways (statement, pure chord, tune), as text PDF 117–118 asks of a returning idea, and each is one step louder and wider than the one before. The scan found no close intervals in the bass in any loud texture. These results match c444; the fourth reading gives no new reason to change them.

### 2. Bars 156–157: a timpani crescendo roll into 158 (considered, not applied)

The one passage later critics call weak is the two-bar climb from the pp cadence at 155 to the ff cadential 6/4 at 158 (c523, c533). Its length is protected for a stated reason (the phrase runs on from 156 without a break; c083, c143, c243, c448). I looked for an orchestral way to make it land harder without adding bars.

The reading has a direct model. In Ex. 211 (*Snegourotchka*, PDF 379), a two-bar allargando approach is carried by horns and trumpets holding mf cresc., strings in triplets mf cresc. and a timpani roll mf cresc. into the sf arrival. At 156–157 the score already has the four horns on held chords with a crescendo, the strings with a crescendo, the harp chords and Cl 1, but no timpani. The drum on C3 is a chord tone of the iv at 157 and could roll from 157 beat 3, p<, into the G2 roll at 158:

```
% recTwoTimp, bar 157 (was R1):
r2 c2:32\p\<
```

I did not apply it, for three reasons. (a) Rimsky-Korsakov excludes the timpani from the question of tutti scoring (text PDF 119: "the inclusion of kettle-drums ... does not come under discussion"), so the example supports the device, not this bar. (b) The timpani are one of the fourteen players who return together at 158 (c283, protected); a roll from 157 takes away that return. (c) The same figure, `r2 c2:32\p\<`, already leads into the P statement at 167, ten bars later, and the suspended-cymbal roll at 159 already builds into 160. Using the device at 157 as well would make it a habit. The climb stays as it is.

### 3. Bar 122: the top octave A♭5–A♭6 holds only Fl 2's D6 (considered, not applied)

In appendix chords 1, 3 and 4 (PDF 505) the top octave has two or three notes in close position. At 122 the top octave holds only D6 (Fl 2), a diminished fifth from A♭6 above and A♭5 below. Two ways to fill it are possible. Vln I divided A♭6/D6 would halve the violins on the climax's top note, which is the movement's peak and protected (c399, c445). Ob 1 on B5 would break its line E♭5–F5–G5–A♭5 at 121–122 and the A♭5–G5 resolution at 123, and the oboe's A♭5 is the ninth of the chord. The current layout also has an appendix model: in chord 2 (*The Tsar's Bride*, p. 246) the two flutes play one high note a2 above the oboes' close chord, so the top of the chord is not filled in thirds there either. Keep.

### 4. Bar 160 beat 3: the top octave is empty for a quaver (considered, not applied)

At 160.3 Ob 1 and Cl 1 rest for a quaver in the counter, so nothing sounds between A♭5 and A♭6 for that quaver. In Ex. 206 (PDF 369), oboes and clarinets hold chord tones under the tune in that octave. Here the octave is filled by the counter's C6 on beat 2 and B♭5 on beat 4, and the quaver rest is part of the counter's rhythm (c407 added Cl 2; c444 noted the same rest). Filling it would turn the counter into a held pad and weaken the line that answers the tune. Keep.

### 5. Low-register and middle-gap scan (no change)

The scan over every beat found one place with an interval smaller than a fifth below C3: 156, F2 (Bsn 2) and B♭2 (Bsn 1, Vc) over B♭1 (Cb, harp), mp. That is an octave split into fifth and fourth, the layout of the harmonic series and of appendix chords 1–5, not the close thirds Rimsky-Korsakov warns against (text PDF 124); the 156 bassoon layout is protected (c424). Of the gaps wider than a sixth in loud textures, almost all are a tune doubled at the octave on top (for example 72, 82, 115–116, 141), which Rimsky-Korsakov treats as normal melodic doubling (Ex. 204, 206, 209). The two others are passing: 120.4 (C4–F5 on the last quaver of the woodwind and violin P figure over the brass motto) and 124.4 (Fl 1's octave drop to G5, protected c399). No change.

### Where the movement still falls short, within this lens

On this reading, nothing in the orchestration does. The tutti chords are laid out as in the appendix, the brass are graded under the tunes as in Ex. 206 and 209, and each restatement is rescored as text PDF 117–118 asks. The one remaining weakness that critics keep naming, the two-bar climb at 156–157, is a matter of phrase length. Orchestration alone cannot fix it without spending something (Finding 2). The other open item from this lens, the harp's f chord at 162 under the ff tutti (c186), remains protected. This reading gives no new answer to the stated reason.
