## Findings

### 1. Bars 119–120: the ♭6 sounds against the 5th of V in the inner voices (applied)

**Problem.** From 117 to 121 the G pedal carries alternating triplet chords in Vla and Cl 2: 117 B–D–F, 118 C–E♭–G, 119 B–D–F, 120 B♭–D♭–F, 121 C–E♭–G. Above them the motto (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, Bsn 1 on E♭4; B.tbn on E♭3) holds E♭ from 118.1 for seven beats. It falls to D at 119.4, to D♭ at 120.1, and to C at 120.4. So for beats 1–3 of bar 119 the violas and Cl 2 repeat D4 in triplets, a minor second below a ff E♭4 in five brass and bassoon parts. At 120.4 the viola repeats D♭4 against the motto's C4. The middle voice of the triplets moves one beat ahead of the motto it should be following, so the harmony of 119.1–3 sounds as G–B–D–E♭–F, a V9 with an added ♭6. It is not the augmented-dominant colour that the motto implies.

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 537 describes ♭6 in minor as a tone that *displaces* the 5th of V: the result is an augmented-triad sound, with ♭6 moving to 5 later. The model voicings on PDF 301 (Ex. 18-19j/k, "6 displacing 5") omit the 5th while the 6th sounds. PDF 541 gives the same rule for the 6–♯5–6 and 5–6–5 neighbour figures: the neighbour stands for the main note. It does not sound alongside it. Elsewhere this score follows the rule: at 106, 127 and 144 the ♭6 never sounds with its own 5th in the same octave (checked note by note with a MIDI script). Bar 119 is the only exception, and it falls at the top of the development's dominant preparation, where the G pedal should sound most clearly.

**Fix.** The triplet middle note now follows the motto: E♭ for 119.1–3, D at 119.4, D♭ for 120.1–3, C at 120.4.

Cl 2 (sections/dev.ily line 114), bar 119:
```
\repeat unfold 3 { \tuplet 3/2 { b8 es' f' } } \tuplet 3/2 { b8 d' f' } |
```
Vla (sections/dev.ily lines 363–364), bars 119–120:
```
\repeat unfold 3 { \tuplet 3/2 { b8 es' f' } } \tuplet 3/2 { b8 d' f' } |
\repeat unfold 3 { \tuplet 3/2 { bes8 des' f' } } \tuplet 3/2 { bes8 c' f' } |
```
Cl 2 already rests on 120.4, so only the viola changes in bar 120.

**Protection.** Cycle 133 protected "the alternating chords over the G pedal 117–121" and "the motto pitches and ties 117–124". Its stated reason is the pull between the pedal and the alternating chords as the development reaches the retransition. The fix keeps the alternation: bar 119 still differs from 118 (B, not C, in the bass of the triplet) and from 120. The motto pitches, rhythm and ties do not change. The triplet middle voice now doubles the motto an octave or a unison lower rather than rubbing against it, so the alternation contrasts more clearly with the pedal. The eighth-note D at 119.1.5 in Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II is a passing note in the protected rising figure. It lasts one eighth and resolves, so I left it.

**Applied:** yes. Build: 0 warnings, 18 pages, analysis.txt shows only the known bar 96 octaves, and the vl.txt diff is empty. Parts were rebuilt with 0 warnings. The parts that cross into movement II (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3) each end on page 2, which is even.

### 2. Bars 138 → 139: a °7 over a held D resolving to iv (keep)

**Problem.** The stop chord at 138 (B°7 over D) resolves to F minor at 139. Two of its tones are common to the goal chord, so it behaves like a common-tone °7 decorating a minor triad. A&S PDF 545 (§15) says this is rare, because the common tones weaken the contrast between the embellishing chord and its goal.

**Why it stays.** This is the recapitulation's turn to iv, and HISTORY protects it. The finale also quotes the 138 layout (coherence/mvt1.md), so any change would affect another movement. The weaker contrast is also useful here: the music turns toward the subdominant as a softening, not a cadence. The reading does not ban the progression; it calls it uncommon. No change.

### 3. Bars 77–83: the major-third cycle with minor triads (keep)

**Problem considered.** The bass descends A♭–E–C–A♭. A&S PDF 572–573 says M3 divisions usually descend and usually use major triads. Here some stations are minor.

**Why it stays.** The cycle descends, the d4 E→A♭ is spelled as ♭6→3 in the seed F♯/A♭ notation, and at 83 the A♭ resolves to G as a neighbour to the 5th. That is exactly the ending A&S give for Schubert D.887 (Ex. 31-28, PDF 573). "Usually major" is a description, not a rule. The minor colour keeps the passage in the movement's minor-mode world. The passage is protected and follows the reading. No change.

### 4. Bars 97–122: the development key plan composes out the stop chord (strength; no change)

The development visits B minor (97), D minor (103) and F minor (111), and arrives on A♭ over the G pedal at 122. The roots B–D–F–A♭ spell vii°7 of C: the sonority of the movement's stop chord, spread across the development. A&S PDF 574–577 say that minor-third divisions are weak as tonic expansions but work well inside a °7 or V7 expansion. That is how they are used here: the cycle prolongs a dominant-function °7 and empties onto the G pedal. (A&S continue this point on PDF 586 §17, just past the assigned range.) This is the strongest large-scale harmonic decision in the movement.
