## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 67.4→68.1, the deceptive cadence that ends the second theme: the only leading tone falls (applied)

**What is wrong.** Bar 67 beat 4 is V7 of A♭ (E♭2, E♭3 / B♭3 / D♭4 / G4 / B♭4). The leading tone G4 sounds only in the upper violas. At 68 the harmony goes to vi (F minor), the deceptive cadence named in mvt1.md ("Deceptive cadence to F minor at 68"). The violas take G4 down to F4. No other part has G, so no voice resolves the leading tone, and the A♭ of 68 comes only from above (B♭→A♭ in Vln I, Vln II and Hn 4).

Of every V or V7 (any inversion) moving to I or vi in the movement, this is the only one where no voice takes the leading tone up a semitone. The passage is soft and thin (p, rit.), so the inner voices are heard.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 76: the leading tone has "its active tendency toward 1". PDF 80: inner voices complete the chord by smooth, mostly stepwise connection.
- A&S Unit 13, PDF 202 (Ex. 13-11, 13-12; Beethoven "Waldstein" I and II): in V(7)–VI "the 3rd of VI is doubled. This is usual ... because the doubled 3rd allows 7 to move to 8 and prevents parallel 5ths".
- PDF 83, Ex. 5-20: a tritone should resolve. The violas' D♭4/G4 is an augmented fourth. It now goes to C4/F4, a fourth, so the tritone does not resolve. With the fix it opens to the sixth C4/A♭4.

**Fix** (tTwo.ily, tTwoVla, bar 68). Only the upper note of the divisi changes. Rhythm, dynamics and the hairpin stay as they are.
```
% v422
r4 <des' g'>2 <des' g'>4 | \after 2 \< <c' f'>1\p |
% c422
r4 <des' g'>2 <des' g'>4 | \after 2 \< <c' aes'>1\p |
```
- At 68 the third A♭ is doubled (A♭3 in Vln II and Hn 4, A♭4 in Vln I and violas), as Ex. 13-12 asks. The root F stays in the bass (Cb, Vc, Bn 2, harp), and C is in four parts.
- The violas meet Vln I's A♭4 by contrary motion, a unison doubling (PDF 85).
- At 69 the violas leave A♭4 by a falling sixth to C4, at the new phrase and tempo mark.
- The c421 protection ("67–68 D♭ fall", the violins' tune) and c377's "67–68 unison" are untouched.

**Applied in the scratch copy:** yes. The vl.txt diff shows no change at 68.

### 2. Bars 23.4–25.1 and 135.4–137.1: the seventh of V6/5 rises to G in the inner pad, against the bass B→C (applied)

**What is wrong.** In the three-crotchet groups before the stop chord, Cl 1 and Vln II rock G4–F4–G4–F4, one note per group:
- 23.4–24.2 is V6/5: B1/B2 in the bass, G3 (Hn 4), D4 (Cl 2, Hn 3, violas), F4 (Cl 1, Vln II), and the tune F5–E♭5–D5.
- 24.3 is i: C2/C3, and Cl 1 and Vln II go F4→G4.

This makes three faults at once:
- The 7th is doubled: the tune's F5 and the inner F4. The tune resolves its F at once (F5–E♭5–D5). The inner F4 holds for three beats and then rises.
- The inner F4 over the bass B2 is a diminished fifth (a twelfth), and it moves to the perfect fifth C3–G4. That is A&S Ex. 5-20b, marked "less good".
- The inner F4→G4 moves by step into G4 while the tune leaps D5→G5. That is a hidden octave between an inner voice and the soprano, with the soprano leaping (PDF 87, point 7).

There is also a fourth fault, which the vl.txt lines show. At 25.2 (and 137.2) Cl 1 and Vln II go G4→F4 while Hn 4 goes G2→F2. Hn 4 is the separate low pad voice, not a doubling of the clarinet, so these are real octaves:
```
bar 25 beat 2.00 P8 clOne:G4->F4 hnFour:G2->F2
bar 25 beat 2.00 P8 hnFour:G2->F2 vlnTwo:G4->F4
```
The isolated-octave scan flags both (and the same two at 137).

**The earlier verdicts, answered.**
- c222 (row 5) called the doubled 7th at 23.4 a "melody doubling in octaves". The notes do not bear that out. Vln II and Cl 1 hold F4 for three beats while the tune moves F5–E♭5–D5, so they are an independent voice, not the tune's octave.
- c402 kept the d5→P5 at 136.3 as "protected". The protections that cover these bars are the following, and the fix keeps every one of them:
  - the three-beat grouping and its accents (c391);
  - the top line, Fl/Ob/Vln I (c421, c407);
  - Hn 4 G3 at 23.4–24.2 and 135.4–136.2 (c305, c393);
  - Hn 1·2 entering at the third cell, 24.3 and 136.3 (c177, c300);
  - Hn 1 A♭4 at 25.2–4 and 137.2–4 (c404).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 76: "The chord 7th forms a dissonance, so it must never be doubled."
- PDF 83, Ex. 5-20b: d5→P5 is "normally avoided since the dissonant interval does not resolve".
- PDF 87–88: hidden octaves are worst when both voices skip and in the outer voices. Here only the soprano skips, but the arrival is an exposed octave G4/G5 in the woodwind and violins.
- PDF 83, Ex. 5-18: octaves between two independent voices.
- Support: A&S Unit 8, PDF 121–123. Inversions of V7 used as neighbours of I (the bass C–B–C here) "require the normal descending resolution". Only a V4/3 passing from I to I6 lets the 7th rise.

**Fix** (tOne.ily and recOne.ily). Cl 1 and Vln II take E♭4 instead of G4 for the third cell (24.3–25.1 and 136.3–137.1). Accents, rhythm, slurs and dynamics are unchanged.
```
tOneClOne:    g'2.\mf\<-> f'4->~ | f'2 g'2->~ | g'4 f'2.-> |
          →   g'2.\mf\<-> f'4->~ | f'2 es'2->~ | es'4 f'2.-> |
tOneVlnTwo:   g'4\mf\<-> g'4 g'4 f'4-> | f'4 f'4 g'4-> g'4 | g'4 f'4-> f'4 f'4 |
          →   g'4\mf\<-> g'4 g'4 f'4-> | f'4 f'4 es'4-> es'4 | es'4 f'4-> f'4 f'4 |
recOneClOne:  \partCombineApart g'2.->\mf\< f'4->~ | f'2 g'2->~ | g'4 f'2.-> |
          →   \partCombineApart g'2.->\mf\< f'4->~ | f'2 es'2->~ | es'4 f'2.-> |
recOneVlnTwo: g'4->\mf\< g'4 g'4 f'4-> | f'4 f'4 g'4-> g'4 | g'4 f'4-> f'4 f'4 |
          →   g'4->\mf\< g'4 g'4 f'4-> | f'4 f'4 es'4-> es'4 | es'4 f'4-> f'4 f'4 |
```
What the fix does:
- The 7th F4 now resolves down to E♭4, in contrary motion to the tune's leap to G5. The d5→P5 and the inner hidden octave are gone.
- At 25.2/137.2 the line goes E♭4→F4 against Hn 4's G2→F2. That is contrary motion, so the octaves with Hn 4 are gone too.
- The pad line is now G–F–E♭–F. Each three-beat group still begins with a new accented note, so the grouping stays audible.

What the fix costs:
- At 24.3 E♭4 is in Cl 1, Cl 2, Hn 2, Vln II and violas. These are unison doublings, which PDF 85 accepts in orchestral writing.
- The fifth G is in Hn 1 (G4), Hn 4 (G2) and the tune (G5 in Fl, Ob, Vln I), so the chord stays complete.
- Cl 1·2 print "a2" on the E♭ at 24.3 and split at 25.2; at 136 they are already apart.

**Applied in the scratch copy:** yes. The vl.txt diff removes four real octave lines (Hn 4 against Cl 1 and Vln II at 25.2 and 137.2). The Cl 1/Vln II lines at those beats now read E♭4→F4, unison doublings. No line is added.
