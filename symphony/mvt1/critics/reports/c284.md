## Findings

### 1. Climax 122–123: the leading tone has no horn and only a low trumpet (applied)

**Bars and problem.** Bar 122 is the second A♭ climax, a sustained V♭9 over the G pedal at fff. Counting all parts that sound at 122: A♭ is in nine (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Bsn 1, Vc, Hn 1, Tpt 1, Tbn 1), D in six, G in seven or more, F in three, and the leading tone B in three: Cl 2 B4, the violas' lower note B3 and Tpt 3 B3. In the brass, B is only Tpt 3's B3. Hn 3 plays D4, which Tpt 2 already has in unison, and then moves D4→B3 at 124.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: above mf the horns should be doubled because one horn is half a trumpet, and that rule "does not hold true if the trumpet is in a low register". Tpt 3's B3 is in that low register. In Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) every ff chord has its third in at least one trumpet or one horn pair. In the Tchaikovsky climax (Ex. XI-21, PDF 39–41) the horns hold the chord tones a2. At 122 the tone that makes the chord a dominant is the weakest in the brass and the weakest in the orchestra.

**Fix.** dev.ily, `devHnThree`:
```
d'1\fff | d'2. r4 | b1\> | c'1 |   →   b1\fff | b2. r4 | b1\> | c'1 |
```
Hn 3 now holds B3 from 122 through 124 (with its existing breath on beat 4 of 123) and resolves it to C4 at 125, which it already does. B gets Tpt 3 plus a horn. D keeps Tpt 2, Fl 2, Ob 2, Vln II and the violas. The trumpets stay low, so c244's reason (brilliance kept for 160) still holds. The A♭6 top, the tam-tam and the dynamics do not change. The vl.txt diff shows one new unison of Hn 3 with the violas' lower note, G4→B3 (a doubling), and drops the Hn 3–Vln II and Hn 3–viola octaves on G→D.

### 2. Bar 12: the German sixth's F♯ is in one horn at f (applied)

**Bars and problem.** The horns play f and the trombones mf at 12. Hn 4 plays A♭2, the bass, which Bsn 2, the bass trombone, the double basses and the timpani already play in the same octave. F♯, the note that makes the chord an augmented sixth, is in Ob 2, Vln II and one horn (Hn 3 F♯3). C is in seven parts (Ob 1, Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 2, Tbn 1, violas, cellos). In the brass, F♯ has a weight of 0.5 and each other note has 2 or more.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: above mf, two horns per pitch. Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) uses "2 Hn." and "4 Hn." on one note in every loud chord and leaves the bass to trombone and tuba. It puts low horns only in the soft "dark" chords. Hn 4's A♭2 (written E♭3) is also a weak, low horn note at f.

**Fix.** intro.ily: `introHnFour = { R1*10 | d1\p\< | fis1\f | R1*2 | }` (was `aes,1\f`). Hn 3·4 now play F♯3 a2, written C♯4. Hn 4 rests at 13 as before and Hn 3 still takes F♯→G. The harmony, the dynamics ("graded brass at 12") and the Ger6–V progression are unchanged, so the protections of c222 and earlier hold. c222's "tutti doublings of tendency tones at 12–13" is kept: the change adds a unison, not a new octave doubling, and the vl.txt diff shows only one doubling pair removed (Bsn 2/Hn 4 D3→A♭2).

### 3. Bar 33: the closing ff chord has its third only in Hn 2, and Tpt 2 takes the seventh up (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 32 the V7 has F4 in Tpt 2 and Hn 2. At 33, the ff stroke on C minor that ends the hammered tutti, Hn 2 resolves F4→E♭4 while Tpt 2 rises F4→G4. The brass chord at 33 is C ×4 (Tpt 1, Tbn 1, bass trombone, tuba), G ×2.5 (Tpt 2, Tbn 2, Hn 1) and E♭ only in Hn 2. The recapitulation's matching stroke at 143 already has Tpt 2 on E♭4.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 15 (horn weight) and Ex. XI-1 (third present in loud chords). PDF 17: trumpets "get stuck on the tonic and dominant". At 33 Tpt 2 leaves the seventh to land on the dominant note, which Hn 1 and Tbn 2 already have.

**Fix.** trans.ily, `transTptTwo` bar 1: `es'8->\ff` (was `g'8->\ff`). The layout is now the same as 143. No new parallels (vl.txt diff empty at 32–33).

### Considered and kept (no change)

- **Low trumpets at 122 (c244, c264, c266).** Adler's low-trumpet remark (PDF 15) shows that they are weak, which is why Finding 1 adds a horn. It does not argue for raising them: 160 still needs the brighter trumpet (Tpt 1 A♭5 on the tune), and that follows principle 11.
- **Tpt 1·2 hammering G4 at 131–132 (PDF 17).** This is four bars, as at 27–30. The low P head is in the trombones, clarinets, bassoons and cellos, and c174 put the trumpets in the timpani rhythm so they do not hold the theme's pitches. Keep.
- **Single horns at ff at 26, 27–32, 82, 138.** Each horn is in unison with a trumpet or trombone (for example 26: Hn 2 = Tpt 2 F4, Hn 3 = Tpt 3 D4, Hn 4 = Tbn 2 F3), so no chord tone depends on one horn. 26, 27–32 and 138 are also quoted by the finale. Keep.
- **The three A♭ climaxes as a series.** Each one gets a colour the previous one did not have (principle 11). 82: first full brass with the P head after a 10-bar trumpet rest. 122: trumpets enter only at the arrival, after the brass-only motto (the Tchaikovsky model, PDF 38–41), with the tam-tam. 160: trumpet melody at A♭5, horn pairs in two lines, the cymbal and the violins in octaves. With Finding 1 the brass chord at 122 is balanced, and the order of the three is unchanged.
