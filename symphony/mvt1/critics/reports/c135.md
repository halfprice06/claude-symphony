## Findings

**F1 (applied). Bars 40–43, Vln I: remove the 8va bracket.**
Problem: `\ottava #1` starts on the second half of bar 40, in the middle of one rising gesture: the eighths F5–G5–A♭5–C6 lead straight into the sixteenths B♭5–F♯6. On the page the line reads C6 and then B♭4, a written drop of a ninth where the music moves down one step and keeps rising. The highest note under the bracket is G6 at 41–42, on four ledger lines.
Evidence: Gould PDF 47: "Music for woodwind and strings can use up to five ledger lines before moving into an octave transposition." Same page: "A written change of octave tends to obscure the contour of a line ... wherever possible, keep a phrase intact at the chosen octave." Gould's "incorrect" example on PDF 47 is this case: the octave change falls inside a figure. The flute and oboe staves above play the same run at 40 at pitch, so the score now shows identical music the same way. This matches cycle 105 (no 8va at 139–141, A♭6) and cycle 106 (no 8va for Fl 1 at 98–100).
Earlier decisions: this partly reverses cycle 35's "one 8va bracket Vln I 40–41". Its reason was to save vertical space. In the rebuilt score, page 4 still holds 39–46 with no collision, and the score is still 17 pages. Bar 40 is not on cycle 105's protected 8va list (31, 71–72, 121–123). Cycle 105 left it alone only because "cycle 35 chose one bracket".
Fix (trans.ily, `transVlnOne`): `f''8( g''8 aes''8 c'''8) bes''16( c''' d''' es''' f''' es''' f''' fis''') | g'''1\sf\>~ | g'''2\mf f'''2\> | es'''4\p r4 r2 |`, with no `\ottava`. The sounding pitches and the MIDI are unchanged.

**F2 (applied). Rests pushed outside the stave without a collision: Bn 161, Bn 162, Hn 3·4 157.**
Problem:
- At 161, Bn 1's beat-4 quarter rest prints on top of the stave, although Bn 1's notes (D3, G2) are in the lower half.
- At 162 the parts cross: Bn 1 holds E2 and Bn 2 plays A2. Bn 2's beat-4 rest is therefore pushed under Bn 1's E2, below the stave, where it touches the *ff* diminuendo hairpin. The probe gives −6.0 spaces.
- At 157, Hn 4's beat-4 rest is at −7.0 spaces, two spaces below Hn 4's own F3 and just above the crescendo hairpin.

Nothing sounds in any of these beat-4 columns except a held note from an earlier beat.
Evidence: Gould PDF 50: "upper-part rests are usually placed above the centre stave-line, lower-part rests below the centre line". A rest moves out of the stave only "should parts otherwise collide". PDF 51: "The space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches". PDF 43 (the general rule) says signs must not cut through other symbols.
Fix (recTwo.ily):
- `recTwoBsnOne`: `\partCombineAutomatic d2 g,4 \once \override Rest.staff-position = #2 r4 |`
- `recTwoBsnTwo`: `a,2.\ff\> \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-2 r4 |`
- `recTwoHnFour`: `f2. \once \override Rest.staff-position = #-10 r4 |`

In the rebuild, the Bn rests sit inside the stave above and below the centre line, and the Hn 4 rest sits level with its F3, clear of the hairpin. No notes or dynamics changed.

**F3 (closed; no change needed). Cycle 125's open items: "up-stems" on the Fl a2 at 158–162 and Bn 1 at 88–90.**
Both already have down-stems in v154. The Stem probe reports direction −1 (down) for every flute stem at 158–163 (G5, E6, C6, A♭6, the beamed `aes'''8 g'''8`, and Fl 1's A5 at 162). It reports the same for every Bn 1 stem at 88–90 (G3, E♭4, D4, D♭4, C4). At 220–300 dpi the stems of these ledger-line notes run down to the middle line, as Gould requires (ch. 1, stems: a stem extends to the middle line). That long stem probably made them look like up-stems at low resolution. Minimal tests confirm that in LilyPond 2.24 the part-combiner's "shared" and "solo" voices have neutral stems. Only the "apart" voice ("one") is `\voiceOne`, and cycle 125's `\soloStemsOn/Off` already handles that. Please record both items as closed.
