## Findings

### 1. Bar 156 has no more weight in the top octave than 114 or 132; the second flute and the basses are in the wrong octave (bars 153–157)
**What is wrong.** At 114, 132–133 and 156, the A♭6 is played by the same two instruments, flute 1 and violins I. On Rimsky-Korsakov's scale (one violin section = 2 woodwinds) that is 3 units each time.
- At 114 and 132 the A♭6 is held longer (4 beats and 3½ beats, against 2½ at 156).
- At 156 the octave below holds flute 2, oboe 1, violins II and trumpet 1 at fff, about 8 units. The ear therefore takes A♭5 as the melody and the A♭6 as a light doubling.
- Flute 2's line starts on g' and peaks at a♭'' (154–157). That is the register Rimsky-Korsakov calls "dull, cold", and it cannot be heard under fff brass.
- The double basses play A♭2 (`aes,1`), an octave above the tuba and contrabassoon. At 114 the basses are on G1 with the tuba and contrabassoon. So the apotheosis has a thinner sub-bass than the development climax.

**Evidence.**
- The models do not keep the top note for the final climax. In Tchaikovsky 6/i, violins I reach C7 at 70–73 in the exposition, and the fff climax at 265–267 tops at A6. In Dvořák 9/i, violins I reach C7 at 346, 412 and 444 and B6 as early as bar 22. What marks their climaxes is weight in the top octave and a deep bass.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 265–267: the flutes double violins I on A6, and trumpets hold chords, not the tune.
- Rimsky-Korsakov: the top melody goes "to the 1st Violins, sometimes to the 2nd Violins or to both in unison, a process which produces fuller resonance without impairing quality of tone".

**Fix.** These changes alter only doublings: the melody, the horns (cycle 14's note) and the counterline are untouched. Section bars 16–19 = 154–157.
- `recTwoFluteTwo`:
  `R1*13 | d''2.~(\mf\< d''8 cis''8 | c''2 bes'4 aes'8 g'8) | g''4\ff( e'''2.~ | e'''4 f'''4 g'''2) | c'''4(\fff aes'''2~ aes'''8 g'''8 | f'''2 d'''4 b''8 c'''8) | R1*5 |`
  - The flutes are a2. Both flutes carry the same dynamics.
- `recTwoVlnTwo`: violins II join violins I for 154–157 and return to the lower octave at 158:
  `... c''2:32 r4 g'4(\mf^"ord." | \recTwoMelodyA d''2.~( d''8 cis''8 | c''2 bes'4 aes'8 g'8) | g''4\ff( e'''2.~ | e'''4 f'''4 g'''2) | c'''4(\fff^\markup \italic "largamente" aes'''2~ aes'''8 g'''8 | f'''2 d'''4 b''8 c'''8) | a'2.~( a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\>( g'8 f'4 a'8 c''8) | d''2( c''2) | b'4( a'8 g'8 f'4 d'4) | c'1\mf | }`
- `recTwoCb`, section bars 15–18:
  `f,,1 | g,,1\ff | g,,1 | aes,,1\fff |`
  - The basses then descend B♭1–F1–G1–A♭1 with the tuba and contrabassoon.

The result: the top octave goes from 3 to 6 units, and the middle octave keeps oboe 1 and trumpet 1 (5 units). The A♭ spans five octaves (A♭1 to A♭6) with weight at both ends. That happens nowhere else in the movement.

### 2. The bassoon never has a solo moment; give it the motto at the development's start (bar 77)
**Colour check.** I looked for a memorable solo moment for each woodwind:
- Flute: 73–76.
- Cor anglais: 5–8, 139–147 and 167–170.
- Clarinet: the low motto at 120–122 and the counterline.
- Oboe: 58–61 and 137–138 (short, but clear).
- Bassoon: none. Every bassoon entry is doubled. At 77, "a new colour on every entry" according to the section header, bassoon 1 plays in unison with the cellos.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 1–5: the motto is given to a solo bassoon (E2–B2) over divided basses. The cellos rest.
- Rimsky-Korsakov describes the bassoon in the minor as having "a sad, ailing quality", which suits the chromatic descent C♭–B♭–B♭♭–A♭.

**Fix.**
- `devBsnOne` bar 1:
  `r8 es8\mf^\markup \italic "solo, espr." ces'4.-> bes8 beses8 aes8 |`
- `devVc` bars 1–2:
  `R1 | r2 r8 b,8\mf g4->~ |`

The cellos still enter with the horns at 78. The bassoon is one level above the p tremolo.

### 3. The percussion is well judged; add one tam-tam stroke at the trombone chorale (bar 171), and optionally a cymbal roll into 156
**Judgement.** One tam-tam stroke (114) and one cymbal crash (156) is the right economy. Tchaikovsky 6/i uses no cymbals or tam-tam at all, and Dvořák 9/i uses none.
- The tam-tam at 114, over trombones and the low G pedal, is what Rimsky-Korsakov prescribes ("the gong with chords on trombones or low sustained notes on 'cellos and double basses").
- The timpani play 40% of the bars, against 32% in Tchaikovsky 6/i and 25% in Dvořák 9/i. That is on the busy side, but each roll has a job: the pedals at 1–4 and 109–122, the colour swells at 62 and 137, and the ending.

I recommend no timpani cuts.

**Missed opportunity.** The pp three-part trombone chorale on the augmented motto (171–175) is the Pathétique finale's sound: Tchaikovsky 6/iv bar 137 has a p tam-tam stroke under the trombone and tuba chorale (corpus, part "Tamtam", 137–141). One soft stroke here links the motto's catastrophe colour (114) to its last statement. It does this without a dynamic peak.
- New `codaPerc`:
  `codaPerc = \drummode { R1*8 | tt1\pp^\markup \italic "soft stick, l.v." | R1*7 | }`

**Optional (also tested).** A suspended-cymbal roll by the tam-tam player into the crash. Rimsky-Korsakov pairs "cymbals struck with drum sticks" with sustained trumpets and horns, which is what 155 has.
- `recTwoPerc`:
  `R1*16 | cymr1:32\pp\<^\markup \italic "2nd player: susp. cym., soft sticks" | cymc1\fff^\markup \italic "1st player: a2, l.v." | R1*6 |`

### 4. The double basses never rest (bars 167–170)
**What is wrong.** The double basses play in 176 of 178 bars (99%), and the cellos in 177. Tchaikovsky 6/i's basses play in 79% of bars and Dvořák 9/i's in 80%. With no bass rests, the low entries lose their effect, above all the chorale's low G at 171.

**Fix.** The basses rest under the cello's espressivo phrase and the cor anglais motto. Bassoon 2 already carries the same bass (c f f g,) at pp, which gives a lighter, woodwind-based texture. The basses re-enter with the chorale and the tam-tam (finding 3).

### 5. Not worth changing
- The harp plays only in the second-theme areas (47–68, 148–162) and on the final chord. That is consistent: its colour is attached to the second theme. A harp part in the intro would dilute it.

## Do not touch
These are at a master's level:
- the tam-tam alone at 114;
- the horn-and-cello second theme over the harp (47–55);
- the cor anglais answers at 139–147;
- trumpet 1 on the codetta tune (69–72);
- the trombone and bass-clarinet augmented motto at 171–175;
- the horns 1·2 tune doubling at 154–157.
