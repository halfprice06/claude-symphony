Note on 17: Rimsky's appendix (PDF 505) rolls the timpani under sustained tutti chords, and Fig. 6.6 argues for sustain. I considered `g,2.:32` at 26 and 138 and rejected it. G is not in the vii°7 chord over D at those bars (B–D–F–A♭). A single stroke adds the root as an attack layer (principle 1). A roll would hold G under the whole chord and turn it into a sustained V♭9. At 170, a roll that ends in a short stroke is the standard idiom.

## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bars 34–36, Bassoon 1 solo: the espressivo tail is unslurred

**Problem.** The solo is marked "1. Solo", f, espr. It is slurred only through bar 34 beat 2: `c'4)\mf bes8( aes8) g4 f4 | f2 aes2 | g2 es2 \breathe`. From g4 on, every note is tongued. Bars 35–36 are the phrase's falling close (F–A♭–G–E♭ under the violas' and violins' imitation), and they come out as separate notes. All the other wind statements of this head-and-tail are slurred through the tail: Cl 1 at 129–130 `g4\mf( es'2 d'4 | des'4 c'2.)`, Bsn 1 at 144 `d'4( c'8 | b1\pp)`, and the BCl at 77–78 and 107–108. The other solos in the movement (CA 5–8, Fl 73–76, Ob 137–138) are also phrased in breath-length slurs.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 151: legato "emphasizes the line and the musical gesture and tries to hide the note", and the slur is "the arc as breath". PDF 152: notation must spell out the connection between notes, because a performer cannot read it from the pitches. The existing `\breathe` at the end of 36 already marks this as one breath unit; the slur should cover it.

**Fix** (trans.ily, `transBsnOne`). Two arcs: the first ends on F, the second starts on the repeated F. A wind player re-articulates the repeated note softly inside the new slur.
```
\set Staff.soloText = "1." c'4)\mf bes8( aes8 g4 f4) | f2( aes2 | g2 es2) \breathe |
```
Applied in /tmp/critic-138/work/sections/trans.ily with a comment citing PDF 151–152. The other player on the staff rests at 33–36, so the shared staff is unaffected.
