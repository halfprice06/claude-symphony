## Findings

The reading finds nothing in movement I that harms the later movements, and nothing they need is used up. I made no change to the score. The findings below are checks and record notes, ranked by how much each matters for the four-movement design.

### 1. The A♭ horn-over-harp colour of the second theme is movement II's opening solo colour. Keep it as a deliberate link (no change)

Bars I 46.4–52: Hn 1 (dolce) and cellos in unison play S in A♭ over the harp's broken chords, the other horns silent. Bars II 6.3–14: Hn 1 alone ("1. solo, dolce espr.") plays theme A in A♭ over arpeggiated harp chords, with no strings. Same key, same instrument, same pickup pitch E♭3.

Adler PDF 263 warns that a slow movement's solo colour must be fresh "even after the first movement has been heard", and his models open the slow movement with a contrasting choir. Dvořák 9 shows the extreme case: the cor anglais appears only in the Largo (corpus: reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys, "anglais" occurs only in Mvt2_Largo.ly and woods.notesL.ily).

Movement II meets this test. It separates the colour three ways. The cellos that double the horn in I are gone. Bars II 3–6 put the wind choir (B.cl, Cl 2, Hn 3·4) between the strings of the attacca and the solo, as on PDF 263. About five minutes separate I 52 from II 7, and I's later horn statements of S (103–106, 146–150) have other partners (Bsn 1 and cellos, violas). What remains is a family resemblance that the cyclic plan wants, since mvt1.md already lists 45–47 as the model for II's entry.

Fix: none. Record line for mvt1.md "Seeds": "Bars 46–52: Hn 1 + Vc over harp broken chords in A♭ is the colour of II 7–14 (Hn 1 alone over harp in A♭); II removes the cellos and puts the wind choir before it (Adler PDF 263–264). Keep Hn 1 + harp at 47."

### 2. The horn call's E♭3–C4 sixth is the pitch origin of movement II's theme A (record only)

Hn 1's call at 13 is E♭3–C4–B3, and the S head at 46.4–47 is Hn 1 E♭3–C4–B♭3. Movement II's theme A opens in Hn 1 with E♭3 F3 | A♭3 C4 E♭4: the same sixth E♭3–C4, at the same pitch, in the same player, filled with F3 and A♭3. The scherzo's trio tune T fills the sixth of the call in E in the same way (G♯–A–B–E; themes-scherzo.md). So the horn call is the source of the one rising-sixth idea the later movements may use, and II and III use it filled, not bare, which keeps mvt1.md's rule that new themes do not open with a bare rising sixth.

This is Adler's dialogue principle stretched across movements (PDF 257–258): the timbre is implanted at 13 (horn alone, marked "1. Solo" since v309) and returns transformed. The finale then gives the call itself, resolved, to trumpets and horns (IV 243–246).

Fix: none. Record line for mvt1.md "Seeds": "Bar 13: Hn 1 E♭3–C4 is the span II's theme A fills at the same pitch in Hn 1 (E♭3 F3 A♭3 C4), and the trio tune fills in E; keep the call's pitch and player."

### 3. The timpani carry one rhythm through all four movements (record only)

Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13): the timpani can carry the motive that joins two phrases. At 179–180 the timpani alone play `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4`, the first group's pulse (Cb 15–22, Tpt and timpani 27–30). That rhythm is the scherzo's motive x (crotchet, two quavers, crotchet) with the first quaver left silent. The chain is:
- I 179–180: `4 r8 8 4` on C–G–C.
- II 84–85: `4. 8 4` on E♭–C–A♭ (its last timpani sound).
- III 9–12 and 363–371: `4 8 8 4` on B–B–B–G | E and B–B–B–E.
- IV 9 and 143–145: `4 8 8 4` on G; IV 229–230 and 265–271: movement I's pulse at pitch.

Each movement's timpani hands the figure on, and the finale states both forms. mvt1.md records only P's bar 2 as motive x. Movement I should keep the rest in the pulse: filling it would anticipate the scherzo's form.

Fix: none. Record line for mvt1.md "Seeds": "179–180: the timpani pulse is motive x with its first quaver silent; II 84–85, III 9 and IV 9/229 continue the figure. Keep the eighth rest."

### 6. The pairing of a solo string with a double reed passes from I's coda to II's B section (record only)

At 171–174 a solo cello (S head, minor) and the cor anglais (motto) play a duet. Movement II's B section (15–22) pairs Ob 1 with a solo violin. The pairing is the same: a solo string and a double reed, a register higher and in major. Under Adler PDF 257–258 and 263, both are fresh. Movement I has no solo violin, and the oboe has no solo in I's coda. mvt1.md links 171–174 only to the finale's single-voice minor statements of its second theme.

Fix: none. Record line: "171–174 solo Vc + C.a. duet; II 15–22 answers it with solo violin + Ob 1."

### 7. The coda's three motto statements and the arc into movement II (considered, kept)

The coda states the motto three times: C.a. 171.4 at G4–E♭5, trombone chorale 175 at G2/G3, and Vln I 177.4 at G4–E♭5, the C.a.'s pitch. Is that too many for the bars that hand the symphony to the Adagio? Adler's dialogue principle (PDF 257–258) gives the test: each restatement must come in a radically different colour. Reed, then brass with tam-tam (Walton's "lugubre" ppp chord colour, Ex. XVII-18, PDF 277), then one violin section sul D are three distinct colours. The pitch repeat of C.a. and Vln I is joined by the trombone canon between them. The motto's tail is also not the pure chromatic E♭–D–D♭–C. It is the intro form E♭–D–C–D♭–C, ending on 1̂. Movements II, III and IV each close with the pure 5–♯4–4–3 descent, so they transform it and do not repeat it. The protections from c228, c249, c270 and c309 stand, and nothing in the reading answers them.

Fix: none.
