## Findings

I found no place in v526 where the string writing goes against this chapter, and no string change that would make the music clearly better. I propose no score change. Below are the checks on the changes since c436, and two possible improvements I considered and rejected, with the reasons.

### 1. String changes since v436: keep

- **Violas 158–159 (c514, c522).** The ff repeated quavers C4/E4, then B3/E4 for one beat and B3/F4, lie on the G and D strings in first position (fingers 3–1, 2–1, 2–2 low). Those are the easiest first-position stops in Adler's viola table (Ex. III-33, PDF 88), and repeated loud double stops of this kind are the use he allows (PDF 76). The change from E4 to F4 on beat 2 of 159 moves one finger by a semitone on the D string without changing the bow. Keep.
- **Viola 157 arpeggio F3–A♭3–C4–F4 (c502).** It crosses from the C string to the G and D strings in first position. That is the viola's accompaniment register (PDF 87). Keep.
- **Viola 107–110 in treble clef (c515).** Adler (PDF 85, Ex. III-28) permits treble for high viola notes. The part (page 2, first system) shows the clef at the start of 107 and alto again at 111, at a system break, with no clef inside the phrase. Keep.
- **Vln II 177 A♭4–F4 (c512).** Both notes are in first position on the D string at pp, inside the sul D colour of Vln I. Keep.
- **Vc tremolo A♭3–G3 at 122–123 (c445).** These are single notes, fingered tremolo-free on the G/D strings, under the fff. The basses hold G1 without tremolo, which matches Adler's point that the bass speaks slowly (PDF 105). Keep.
- **Bow marks (19, 68–69, 72, 110, 141–142, 171).** I counted each through to its target (f or ff arrivals on down-bows, crescendo entries on up-bows), and each one does what its comment says. Keep.

### 2. Parts (lens: page turns): keep

The parts build with rc 0 and no warnings. Violin I, Violin II, Viola and Violoncello each have two pages, a facing spread with no turn. The double-bass part has a title page, then the music on pages 2–3 (a spread) and page 4. The only turn, 3→4, comes during the four bars' rest at 171–174 (about 13 s at crotchet = 72). Each string part ends on an even page for the attacca. The "sul A" span at 2–3 begins on E♭2, not on the pickup G1, as PDF 76/106 require (G1 is on the E string). Every "div.", "unis.", "non div." and "change bow at will" from rows 8 and 30 is printed. Keep.

### 3. Considered and rejected: cello divisi in the coda (PDF 101–102, Ex. III-62)

Adler's William Tell and Barber examples show divided cellos as a rich colour, and this movement uses cello divisi only at 124–126. I looked for a soft place where a divided cello chord could deepen the colour. The obvious one is the chorale at 175–179, but there the cellos double the bass trombone's motto, and the motto statement order, its low register and the five-part ending are protected (c-list "motto colours and order"; "low motto return 175–179"). Splitting the cellos there would thin the motto line for a colour the trombones already give. Rejected.

### 4. Considered and rejected: sul G for Vln I on the P head at 15–18 (PDF 74, Ex. III-5)

Tchaikovsky 5/ii puts a lyrical line sul G for intensity. P's head at 15 (G4–E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5) could be taken sul G. But the line climbs to D♭6 at 20. Sul G would force high positions and a shift at mf in a fast tempo, then a string change anyway. It would also darken the strings-only first theme, whose notes and scoring are protected. The plain fingering (D and A strings) already gives a clear mf. Rejected.
