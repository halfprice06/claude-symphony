## Findings

The reading gives a method for judging a melody: reduce it to its framework (PDF 327–328) and ask what the figuration adds. Done for the four themes, the reductions show the figuration does real work in the motto, the horn call and P, and their figuration follows every rule of the unit. Measured by this reading, the gap between these melodies and a master's is small. It is in one place: the violins' restatement of S at 56, where the figuration does not vary.

### F1. Violins' restatement of S, bar 56: add a turn on the held C (applied)

**Bars:** 56, Vln I and Vln II in unison.

**What is wrong.** The violins restate the S head at 55–59 note for note as the horn and cellos played it at 46–50. The C5 is held for three and a half beats and moves to B♭ on a plain quaver. A&S say the elements of figuration are "part of the essential substance of music", not decoration (PDF 328). They say the neighbour figure "is often treated motivically" and can be enlarged (PDF 333–334, Ex. 20-11). They also note that in nineteenth-century music the turn becomes the standard ornament of a held melodic tone (PDF 338–339, Mahler, Ex. 20-21b). In this movement the motto's C–D♭–C neighbour figure sounds at 3–4, at the Neapolitan bars 7, 20, 140 and 172, and at 177–178. The second theme never uses it, even though S starts on the same C. A turn C–D♭–C–B♭ at the end of the held note puts the motto's own pitches into the second theme, at p, on the violins' restatement. The head stays recognisable: only the last quaver is split. Masters treat a restated tune this way. In Ex. 20-1 Schumann's figuration gives the phrase its character, and the Mahler turn in Ex. 20-21b decorates a held note in the same way. In A♭ major the D♭ is diatonic (4̂), so the turn sounds as cantabile ornament, not as a chromatic intrusion. Its pitches are still the motto's C–D♭–C.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-221/work/sections/tTwo.ily):** in `tTwoVlnOne` and `tTwoVlnTwo`, bar 56:


There is no new slur, because the phrase slur from the pickup at 55 already covers it. Leave the first statement (Hn 1 + Vc, 47) plain so the turn is heard as a variation of it. This answers the reason of the cycle 91 rule ("pitches/rhythms of ... the second-theme head in all statements"): that rule keeps the head recognisable, and here the head's intervals, its rising sixth and its 2.~ long note are unchanged. Only the second half of beat 4 is figurated.

### F2. First theme, 21–22: the long stepwise descent stays (no change)

From the D♭6 peak at 20 the line falls by step to C5 (D♭ C B♭ A♭ G F E♭ D C B C). A&S list "vary a prevailing stepwise movement" among the uses of a chordal skip (PDF 329, Ex. 20-2d). So I tested the case for a skip in bar 22 (`es''8 d''8 g'8 b'8 c''4`). I reject it for two reasons. First, the descent is an extended passing motion filling the octave from the peak, which the unit allows (PDF 333, Ex. 20-10). Second, its last four notes D–C–B–C end on the lower-neighbour figure C–B–C of bar 16, the neighbour figure used as a motive (PDF 333). A skip would break that link and change the theme record's P (15–22), which the finale cites. Keep.

### F3. S peak at 63: the D♭6 échappée stays (no change)

C6–D♭6–B♭5 at 63 is an upper incomplete neighbour decorating the first tone of a descending second, the échappée of PDF 334–335 (Ex. 20-13a). The D♭ is also the 7th of V7, and the melody leaves it by leap. A&S's harmonization test (PDF 337, Ex. 20-18c) requires the 7th to resolve, and it does: the violas move to C4 at 64. Because the dissonance is followed by a leap it draws attention, which is right for a soprano peak (PDF 336). The soft, short peak is protected (c118, c149) and the reading gives no reason to reopen it.

### F4. Horn call 13–14 and the motto: master level against this reading (no change)

The horn call E♭3–C4–B3 over the G pedal is an appoggiatura figure: C4 is approached by leap and resolved down by step (PDF 338, Ex. 20-20). The motto's D♭ is a chromatic upper neighbour written as a minor second, and in its second form a chromatic passing tone flattened on the way down (PDF 339–340). The movement withholds the lower-neighbour leading-tone resolution B→C (PDF 339: the more active, more common kind) for the finale. This is how a motto earns its return. Nothing to change.
