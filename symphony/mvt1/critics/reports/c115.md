## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Vc 46→47 and 146→147: the tenor clef splits the pickup from its downbeat (applied)
**Problem.** The cellos' second-theme pickup (E♭3 at 46 beat 4; G2 at 146 beat 4) is slurred into the downbeat of 47/147. In v134 the `\clef tenor` comes at the start of the new section file, so it prints between the pickup and its downbeat, inside the slur. The opening interval of the theme (E♭3→C4 at 46-47, G2→E3 at 146-147) is printed half in bass clef and half in tenor clef, and both changes fall at a page turn.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 22, "between phrases": "In a passage of continuous playing, place a new clef between phrases, wherever this occurs in the bar. This placing overrules the above considerations. It enables the reader to see the pitch contour of the phrase intact." Gould's example on that page puts the clef before the pickup, not after it. PDF 21: a clef change "obscures" the contour.
**Fix.** trans.ily `transVc`: `f2 g4 \clef tenor es4(\mf |`. recOne.ily `recOneVc`: `r2. \clef tenor g,4(\mf^\markup \italic "dolce" |`. E♭3 sits on a tenor-clef line. G2 needs two ledger lines, within Gould's three. The `\clef tenor` at the start of `tTwoVc`/`recTwoVc` now does nothing and does not print again.

### F2. Hn 3·4 bar 13: treble clef placed right before the rests (applied)
**Problem.** After the low chord at 11-13 (bass clef), the staff returns to treble mid-bar 13, right after Hn 3's half note. Horns 3·4 then rest until bar 19, more than a system. The small clef sits at an arbitrary point in bar 13 of page 1.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 23, "after periods of rests": "Where an instrument rests for more than a system, it is usual to return it to its commonest clef … Place the clef change at the end of the system after the player has finished … It is better than an immediate clef change before rests, which draws unwanted attention to the new clef at an arbitrary point." The example on that page shows the same "and not" case.
**Fix.** intro.ily `introHnThree`: `… | g2\p r2 |` then `R1 \clef treble |`. The clef now prints at the end of the page-1 system (bar 14), and page 2 opens in treble.

### F3. Cymbals/tam-tam on a five-line staff (applied)
**Problem.** The shared percussion staff (three strokes in the whole movement: tam-tam 122 and 175, suspended cymbal 159, crash 160) is a five-line DrumStaff. LilyPond's default positions put the cymbals on or above the top line, and the suspended-cymbal note needed a ledger line. Five lines suggest pitch where there is none.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 19, "The single-line stave, uses: to notate instruments of indefinite pitch". PDF 20: percussion clef placement, optional on a stave of fewer than five lines. PDF 26: crossed noteheads "in percussion writing, to distinguish certain instruments", with the circled cross for minims and semibreves. Cycle 5's decision (one named percussion staff, not split in two) is kept.
**Fix.** score.ly, DrumStaff `\with`: `\override StaffSymbol.line-count = #1 \override Stem.direction = #UP \override LaissezVibrerTie.direction = #UP drumStyleTable = #(alist->hash-table '((crashcymbal cross #f 0) (ridecymbal cross #f 0) (tamtam default #f 0)))`. All three instruments sit on the line. The cymbals have circled-cross heads and the tam-tam an ordinary head, so the "1:"/"2:" labels and the heads both identify the instrument. The stem/tremolo and l.v. overrides move the roll slashes and the l.v. ties above the line, so they no longer touch the pp/fff below (checked at 159-160).

### F4. Viola 109-110: two-bar treble excursion inside an alto passage (applied)
**Problem.** The violas switch alto → treble at 109 and back to alto at 111 for two bars, D♭5-A♭5. In alto clef only the A♭5 at 110 needs more than three ledger lines (it sits on the fourth). D♭5, F5 and G5 need two or three.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches, which a change of clef would obscure." The example there prefers one clef with ledger lines to a change in and out.
**Fix.** dev.ily `devVla`: delete `\clef treble` before `des''4.(` and `\clef alto` after `c''4) |`. The line 107-110 now reads in one clef, so its rise to A♭5 is visible as one contour.

### Checked and left alone (Gould-compliant)
- Vc 47-55: tenor, then treble at 51, then bass at 56. Both changes fall at phrase ends (slur closes at 50 and at 55). In tenor clef, F5 at 52-53 would need four ledger lines, so the treble clef is justified (PDF 21-22).
- Hn 3·4 bass clef at 23-26, 103-106, 162-166: Hn 4 goes down to F2, B2 and C3, which lie four or more ledger lines below the treble staff, so bass clef is needed. Each change is at a barline or a system end (PDF 21-23).
- Hn 4 D3 at 141: a single note on the fourth ledger line in a treble passage. Changing clef for one note would break Gould's "stay in one clef" rule.
- Bassoon, trombone and bass clarinet never go above F4 (two ledger lines in bass clef), so no tenor clef is needed (PDF 21).
