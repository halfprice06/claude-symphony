## What I studied

Besides the assigned unit, I compared the second theme with Tchaikovsky 6/i, bars 89–100 (Vln I, extracted from `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`). Tchaikovsky puts one accented dissonance in each two-bar unit: B4 over the D chord, approached by a falling third and resolving to A4, at 90, 92, 96 and 100. That is exactly the appoggiatura of Ex. 20-20. This score's second theme puts the same device in the same place in its phrase: A♭ over E♭7 resolving to G at 50 and 59, and B over F♯ resolving to A♯ in the development statement at 101. Its framework (row 1) is simple, and its surface (rows 8, 15 and 18) is what the book calls the "individual flavor". Unit 20 gives no reason to reopen the protected theme pitches.

I wrote two small scripts in /tmp/critic-111/scripts. One checks the spelling of every chromatic passing tone in the section files. The other sweeps Vln I and Vc against every staff of score.midi for parallels. The spelling check found exactly one place where the passing tone is spelled against the unit's rule (bar 60). The parallels sweep found nothing new.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 60, Vln I and Vln II: descending chromatic passing tone spelled A♮; it should be B♭♭. APPLIED.
- **Problem:** the line B♭5 (dotted half) – A♮5 (eighth) – A♭5 (61) is a chromatic passing tone between B♭ and A♭. The harmony under it is E♭ minor at 60 beats 3–4, going to D♭ minor at 61. Spelled A♮, the passing tone makes an augmented 2nd with the cello's G♭ on the same eighth and a tritone with the bass E♭. The reader sees a raised note in a passage whose chromatic notes are all flats: G♭, F♭ and C♭ in the same four bars.
- **Evidence:** Unit 20 §14 (p. 339, Ex. 20-22): "the chromatic half step comes first … flat it when moving down (A–A♭–G or E♭–E♭♭–D♭)." The score already follows this rule everywhere else, including in neighbouring bars. At 58 the cello has C–C♭–B♭ under the same melody, and at 78 Cl 1 has C♭–B♭–B♭♭–A♭, the very double flat the rule asks for. Bar 60 is the one exception. Review point 3 (p. 343) says the same: "The chromatic passing tone fills in a whole step."
- **Fix (tTwo.ily), applied in /tmp/critic-111/work:**
  - Vln I: `bes''2.~( bes''8 beses''8 | aes''2 ges''4 fes''8 es''8) |`
  - Vln II: `bes'2.~( bes'8 beses'8 | aes'2 ges'4 fes'8 es'8) |`

  Pitch, MIDI and layout are unchanged. The engraving shows B𝄫 under the existing slur (checked at 150 dpi).

### 2. Bars 98–99: the clarinet/violin fifths left open since cycle 102 are acceptable. NO CHANGE. Close the item.
- **Problem as recorded:** HISTORY cycle 102 lists "Open: clarinet arpeggio fifths 97–99". Vln I goes C♯5→B4 (the passing tone D–C♯–B) while the clarinets' last arpeggio eighth goes F♯4→E4 into 99.
- **Evidence:** Unit 20 §16 (p. 341): "Fifths—especially when produced by dissonant passing and neighboring tones … occur much more freely than unisons or octaves do. In particular, when the 5ths arise out of relatively rapid figuration, the listener can easily take in the basic voice leading; the seeming parallels, then, create no ill effect whatsoever." Review point 9b (p. 343) says the same: such fifths are "often good, in instrumental style, if the passing or neighboring tone is dissonant." Here C♯ is a dissonant 9th over the B-minor chord at 98, on a weak eighth, against a fast arpeggio. Brahms's Ex. 20-25 shows the same kind of surface fifths. The case the book forbids is fifths from a *consonant* passing tone (p. 341), and that does not apply here.
- **Fix:** none. Critic 102's alternative (Cl 98, last eighth `d'` concert) is not needed. Record the item as closed.

### 3. Bar 72: octaves A♭→E♭ by chordal skip between the top line and the bass. NO CHANGE.
- **Problem:** Fl 1 and Vln I play A♭6–E♭6–C6 while the cello, bass, tuba, bass trombone and timpani play A♭–E♭–A♭. Ex. 20-5 (p. 331) labels exactly this, soprano and bass skipping together, "causes parallel octaves". Review point 8 says "avoid them."
- **Why no change:** this is a tutti hammer-stroke on one tonic chord, and every pitch is doubled at several octaves. Unit 20 itself sets doublings in free texture apart from voice-leading parallels (p. 341, "we are not referring to doublings in free texture as discussed in Unit 5, section 14"). The codetta scoring 69–72 is protected (cycle 106). If the composer ever wants the outer voices independent, the minimal fix is the string and low-wind bass at 72 = `aes,4-> c4-> aes,2->`, which gives I–I6–I and 10ths with the top line. I do not recommend it.

## Verdict for this lens

Unit 20 confirms the figuration of all the melodic material: the motto, both themes, the transition sequence, the codetta, the development statements and the climax lines. Rows 1–24 apply 20 principles to the score. The only violation is a spelling at bar 60, now fixed in the scratch copy. Remaining melodic choices that might look like violations (the free G4 at 17, the A♭–B run at 32, the 4̂–2̂ skip at 67, the fifths at 98–99) are each covered by an explicit allowance in the unit.
