## The three A♭ climaxes

- **82 (ff).** This is the *Meistersinger* 1–9 model (row 18) more exactly than earlier reports said. The head is in Tpt 1 with its "affinity instruments" Ob 1 and Cl 1·2 at pitch (E♭5), plus Vln II. Fl 1·2 and Vln I are an octave above, and violas and Bsn 1 an octave below. The A♭ bass is in octaves (Cbsn and tuba A♭1, Bsn 2 and timpani A♭2 in quavers, Cb A♭1 in quavers, Vc A♭2). The held horns and trombones are f under the ff head (row 1). The upper octaves are not filled with chord tones, which keeps 82 below 122.
- **122 (fff).** The Mahler 1 and Holst model (rows 10 and 23). In the dump the A♭ is present in every octave from A♭3 (Bsn 1, Tbn 1, Vc) through A♭4 (Hn 1, Tpt 1) and A♭5 (Ob 1, Vln II) to A♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I). The bass is G1/G2 only, and all motion stops. From 123 the fade passes to instruments that can control it (row 11).
- **160 (fff).** The Brahms 3 and *Scheherazade* model (rows 9 and 22). The tune is in three octaves: Fl 1·2 + Vln I at A♭6; Tpt 1 + Ob 2 + Vln II at A♭5; Hn 1·2 + Vc at A♭4. Ob 1 and Cl 1 carry the counter, Hn 3·4 the counter an octave lower, the violas quavers, and the held brass are ff. The A♭ chord is complete in each choir.

The order 82 < 122 < 160 holds in density, top note and dynamic.

### Considered and kept

1. **56–59: the counterline is marked at the same level as the S tune.** The violins (I and II in unison, sul D, "molto cantabile") are p. Cl 1 + Hn 2 enter p "espr." on E♭4–G4 against them. Row 25 (Schoenberg, PDF 184) marks the principal gesture louder than the subordinate one, and row 1 (PDF 159) asks for the melody to be the most prominent. So I asked whether the counter should be pp, rising to p at 60. I keep it as it is, for three reasons:
   - The same rule's second clause gives the reason a subordinate line is made softer: it lies "in any kind of piercing register". This counter lies in the clarinet's throat and the horn's middle register, a sixth to an octave below the tune. Those are the least piercing places either instrument has.
   - The violins swell inside every bar (`c''2.~\<` at 56, `f''2--\<` at 57, `des''8\< f''8` at 58), so the tune rises above p on each long note while the counter stays flat. The printed shapes already separate the two lines; only the starting levels are equal.
   - Adler's *Meistersinger* 158–61 (PDF 177–178) puts its second theme on Vln I + Cl 1 and its third, less thematic element on oboes, clarinet and horns at similar printed levels. It relies on rhythm and colour to keep them apart. Here, too, the counter moves in different rhythms (minim, then quaver pickups against the tune's dotted figures) and in another colour (reed + horn against strings). A pp counter would also lose the Hn 2 colour that c447/c547 identify as the Verdi-type obbligato.

   The counterline is protected (c441, c471). Keep.

2. **103–106: the P head is in woodwinds only.** Row 21 (PDF 178) says each combined element mixes a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument. The S head (Hn 1 + Bsn 1 + Vc) does this; the P head (Ob 2 + Cl 1·2, f) has no string or brass. The violins and violas rest in 103–106, so a string doubling would mean bringing in Vln II on the head. That would take away the string re-entry at 107 (violas p espr., Vln II pp div.) that makes 107–110 a new colour (row 17). Adding a brass instrument to an f head over an mf horn line would turn the head into the foreground and cover Hn 1's S head, which is the line the passage develops. Bloch, at PDF 184, keeps his elements in separate choirs on purpose for clarity, and Adler presents that as a valid second method. Protected (c437 "103-110 combined heads", c441 "103-106 wind unison"). Keep.

3. **Fl 1 doubles Vln I at pitch for long spans: 23–31 (9 bars), 115–124 (10), 131–138 (8).** PDF 165 says that at-pitch doublings lose the colour of both instruments and that sustained doublings tire the ear. In each span, though, the doubling serves power in the high register: 23–25 is the mf hemiola (protected), 27–31 is the hammered tutti (quoted by the finale), 117–122 is the build to the A♭6 climax, and 131–134 is the counter over the low P theme. Moving the flute an octave above the violins in these bars would put it at E♭7–A♭7, which is out of range or too shrill. Adler's own *Missa* chord at m. 21 (PDF 160) and the *Jupiter* list (PDF 176, "first violin doubled by flute at pitch") use this same pairing for power. The other long runs in the scan are also protected: Cl 1 + Hn 2 56–68 is the counterline; Ob 2 + Cl 1 111–121 and B.cl + Bsn 2 97–106 are inner pads. Keep.

### Re-checks of earlier verdicts (no fault)

- Row 3: the only places where a trumpet doubles woodwind at pitch on a tune are 69–71 (oboe), 82 (oboe and both clarinets together) and 160 (Ob 2). There is no clarinet-only + trumpet pairing.
- Row 11: at 123 the flutes stop after two beats and Vln I holds G6 into the decay. At 162 the brass end ff>mp in one bar, and the strings and harp carry the fade.
- Row 15: at 82 the held brass (horns and trombones f on A♭–C–E♭) are the sustained pitches under the unison head that lead into the full chord strokes at 83. That is the Schubert 8/i device (PDF 169).
- Row 24: unchanged since c464.
- 156–157 (open, protected): this reading offers nothing that answers c544's analysis. *Meistersinger* 1–9's "hold back the highs and add them gradually" (PDF 172) would mean adding a flute or oboe at 157, and c511 and c544 rule that out. It stays a phrase-length question, not an orchestration one.
