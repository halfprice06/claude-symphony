## Holistic verdict

I chose Bruckner 8/i because, like this movement, it is a late-Romantic C minor first movement with a pianissimo ending. In the 1890 version a ff tutti climax in the recapitulation breaks off and leaves the brass hammering the main theme's rhythm. A coda for low winds and low strings follows, and the movement dies away on a repeated figure from the first subject. This score has the same shape: the ♭VI climax at 158–161; the dip at 163–166; the brass head over tremolo strings at 167–170, which stops on the fermata; the cello/cor anglais duet; the pp trombone, bass clarinet and cello chorale with tam-tam at 175–179; and the viola's E♮ at the end. The proportions and the order of these events would convince a conductor. The one place where Bruckner and Beethoven Op. 37 do something this score does not is the last soft pulse. In both models it is the main theme's rhythm. Here it is plain quarters (finding 1).

**Against v198:** The changes since v198 are the 115 top line (c180), the flute F♭5 at 75 (c182), the plain V at 41–42 (c183), the clarinet rests at 97–102 (c184), the breaths and pp wedge brass (c186), Cl 1 at 94–95 and 162 (c187) and slurs (c188). None of them made anything worse. The 97–100 density fell from 15.4 to 14.4 average voices. That is still the densest non-climax block after 82, and the B-minor theme's lower octave is clearer. The all-pairs voice-leading output is identical apart from the known items.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Coda 179–180: the timpani's last pulse should be the first group's rhythm (applied)
- **Problem:** The timpani play `c4 r4 c4 r4` twice under the final motto. This is a neutral tick. The Allegro's pulse, a quarter followed by an eighth rest and an eighth, runs through the first group: the basses at 15–22 (`c,4 r8 c,8`), timpani and trumpets at 27–30 and 131–134, the basses at 167–170. That pulse is never heard again after the stop at 170.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 503 (Unit 29, the note to Ex. 29-1) says that events at the close of a phase "recall the opening of the movement to the listener's mind," and that this prepares the next phase "motivically as well as harmonically". PDF 585 (Unit 32) says motivic design decides such details. Beethoven Op. 37/i, `timpani.ly`, coda after the cadenza fermata: the timpani play `c4\pp r8 g, c4 r8 g, c4 r r2` four times. This is the theme's rhythm, pp, under the soloist, as the movement's last soft pulse. Bruckner 8/i (1890) ends on a repeated first-subject figure (Wikipedia, Schoenzeler).
- **Fix (applied):** coda.ily, codaTimp: `c4\pp r8 g,8 c4 r4 | c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4 |`. Only the rhythm changes. G and C are both in the 179–180 chord (C pedal; Vln II G, Vla E♭), and G and C are on the existing drums. No tempo word was added.
- **Protection:** c150 protected the coda's notes and dynamics at 171–182. My answer is new evidence that no earlier cycle cited: two C minor master movements end their quiet pulse on the main rhythm. Nothing else in the coda changes. The dynamics and the 181–182 roll stay as they are.

### 2. Bar 44: doubled leading tone in the V4/2 of A♭ (applied)
- **Problem:** At 43–44, Hn 2 holds G4 for two bars (`g'1\p~ | g'1`). In 44 the chord is E♭7 over D♭ (V4/2 of A♭). Its leading tone G is already in the cellos (G3), and they resolve it to A♭3 at 45. The horn's G4 stops at 45 without resolving. The result is a doubled tendency tone that leaves the texture unresolved one bar before the second theme's key arrives.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 419 (Unit 25): "the temporary leading tone is not doubled"; the voice-leading of V applies. PDF 443 (Unit 26): the new key is confirmed by its cadence, so this V is a structural arrival.
- **Fix (applied):** trans.ily, transHnTwo: `g'1\p( | es'1) | R1*2 |`. E♭ is common to V and to the A♭ chord at 45, and it doubles Vln II's E♭4 in unison. The horn call, the dynamics and the lead-in line are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 160–161: the bass tritone A♭→D (open note from c172). Close it, no change
- c172 left the fff bass A♭→D open. The chord at 161 is vii°7 (D–F–A♭–B) with A♭ held over from 160, and A♭ falls to G at 161.3. A&S PDF 388 (Unit 23) asks that ♭6 reach 5 and not be replaced by ♮6 first. The score does this. The tritone in the bass belongs to the °7, whose positions the book treats as members of one chord (PDF 374). Keep as written.

### 4. Bar 116 → 117: the bass seventh C leaps to G (note only, protected)
- At 116 the chord is D–F♯–A♭/A over C, a French sixth that becomes V4/2 of V, and the C falls a fourth to the G pedal. A&S PDF 399 says a seventh normally descends. However, the C is the last step of a chromatic passing bass (PDF 552: such chords are passing, not functional), and the G pedal follows at once. No change: the wedge bass is protected (c112/c133), and the effect matches the book's chromatic-passing reading.
