## Findings

I applied no change. The scan, the pages and the examples show no conflict with the reading. Every candidate that c441 kept (Fl 1 exit 64–65, Cl 1+Hn 2 counter 56–68, three winds on the P head at 103–106, horn-call register, S in the horn) still stands on the same evidence, and I do not repeat those arguments. The findings below look at the themes from angles the earlier passes did not take. For each I say why the score should stay as it is.

### Finding 1. S is never given to a solo horn alone, though PDF 72 and Ex. 73 would allow it. Keep it.

**Question.** PDF 72 says the horn's piano tone "affords greater scope in the choice of melodies". Ex. 73 (*Antar*, PDF 245) puts a solo horn pp over a full, busy texture without any doubling. Masters often give the lyrical second theme to one horn (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, Brahms 1/ii). Here Hn 1 plays S only with the cellos (46.4–52) or with the violas (146.4–150). An uncovered horn at 47–50 would be the most personal colour in the movement.

**Assessment.** Movement II's theme A is Hn 1 solo over harp in A♭ (II 7–14), and coherence/mvt1.md (bars 46–52) records that movement II keeps that solo fresh because movement I always doubles the horn. If movement I gave S to the horn alone, movement II's main tune would lose its first-hearing effect. Within movement I, PDF 79 gives the reason for the present scoring: horns with cellos produce "a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone". PDF 66–67 lets a doubled soft melody stand when its expression is broad rather than intimate, and S at 47 is a broad period with a long upbeat and long notes. No change.

### Finding 2. The only rousing major brass tune is Tpt 1 on S at 69–72. Keep it single.

**Question.** Ex. 71 (*Sadko*) and Ex. 72 (*Snegourotchka*, PDF 244) show the kind of melody PDF 72 says suits the brass best: a diatonic major tune for one trumpet, mf–f, over a plain accompaniment. A listener remembers a tune like that. In movement I, S appears in a trumpet in major only in the codetta (Tpt 1 with Ob 1, Fl 1·2 and Vln I, f, 69–72). One could add a second such moment, for example Tpt 1 on the C-major S at 147–150.

**Assessment.** The finale reserves "no trumpet solo and no soft trumpet melody" and the major brass chorale for its C-major arrival (coherence/mvt1.md, Brass use and Do not reuse). The codetta keeps within those limits: the trumpet doubles Ob 1 and is doubled above by two flutes in the top register, as PDF 75 requires ("in the top register two flutes will suffice"). It is a tutti statement, not a trumpet solo. A trumpet at 147 would also break the soft C-major recapitulation (pp–mp, protected by c489 and by the coherence rule that movement I never confirms C major at forte), which has to stay below the finale's first forte C major. No change.

### Finding 3. 158–161: the brass doubles the climax of S, against PDF 73's "passion" rule and unlike Ex. 87. Keep it.

**Question.** PDF 73 says phrases charged with passion "become sickly and insipid when confided to the brass". The S climax (G4–E6 | E–F–G | C–A♭6 | F–D–B–C) is the most passionate phrase in the movement, and Tpt 1 (E5) and Hn 1·2 (E4) are on it. Ex. 87 (*Kashtchei*, PDF 261), Rimsky's own example of a ff melody "con tutta forza ed espressione" in three octaves, gives the tune to the winds and strings only. Its trumpets, trombones and tuba hold the harmony.

**Assessment.** PDF 73 is about melody given to the brass. Here the brass doubles a melody that the strings and woodwinds already carry in each octave. Top: Fl 1·2 + Vln I. Middle: Ob 2 + Vln II + Tpt 1. Bottom: Hn 1·2 + Vc. PDF 79–80 names exactly this "Vln + Ob + Trumpet" three-group unison "for preference in loud passages", and Ex. 93–94 (*Snegourotchka*) puts a trumpet on a violin tune. In each layer the strings carry the expression and the brass adds weight (PDF 74: the wind "softens and rarefies" the brass). The *Kashtchei* layout would take Tpt 1 and Hn 1·2 off the tune and leave a balance problem PDF 76 warns about: three octaves of strings and winds over ff held brass. Hn 3·4 already hold the counter with Ob 1 + Cl 1·2. The current layout is protected (c174, c266, c381, c441), and nothing in this reading answers the stated reasons. No change.

### Finding 4. P as a brass melody (131–134, 167–169) is chromatic, which PDF 73 calls less suitable. Keep it.

P's head (G–E♭–D–D♭–C) contains the motto's chromatic fall. PDF 73 accepts chromatic notes in the brass in "vigorous phrases in the form of a fanfare", and P's head is a fanfare figure: an upbeat, a leap, an accent, a short run. At 131–134 the trombones play it low with clarinets and bassoons in unison: dark and gloomy in the minor (PDF 72), with the woodwind giving legato (PDF 74–75) and no woodwind an octave above the trombone (PDF 75). At 167–169 it is Hn 1·2 + Tpt 1·2 ff over held winds, a fanfare. Both are protected and quoted by the finale. No change.

### Finding 5. What would keep a listener humming, judged against the masters. No change.

- **S** is the movement's tune. It is a period of long notes on strong beats, with a rising sixth in each phrase (E♭–C, A♭–F), and it peaks on D♭6 at 63 and on A♭6 at 160. It compares with the second themes of Dvořák 8/i and Tchaikovsky 5/i in breadth. Its settings move from blended soft colour (Hn+Vc, PDF 79) to violins with a horn-and-clarinet counter, then to f with winds and trumpet (69–72), to B minor ff (97–102), and to the three-octave climax (158–161). Each setting uses a combination this chapter recommends (rows 21, 22, 25, 28, 29). This is how Tchaikovsky makes a tune memorable: the notes stay the same and the colour grows.
- **Motto.** Six notes in a new single colour at every soft return (B.cl+Vc, C.a., Fl, trombones, Cl, Vln I), following PDF 64–67. A listener will keep it.
- **Horn call.** It grows from one horn mp (13–14) to four horns ff (41–42) to one high horn p (43–44), and Hn 1 at 46.4 turns its E♭3–C4 into the S upbeat at the same pitch. PDF 71–73 supports each step.
- **P** is the least singable theme, because of its quaver figures. The finale quotes 27–32 at pitch and the P head rhythm (protected in HISTORY.md), so a change is not available. The part a listener keeps is its head, which is the motto.

I also checked the one change since c441 that sits under a theme: the harp at mp at 47 (c519) under Hn 1 mp and Vc mf. The harp arpeggiates above the tune but is lighter at the same marking, and the horn-cello blend stays in front (PDF 79). It is fine.

I found no theme change that this reading supports and that would be worth notifying three other movements.
