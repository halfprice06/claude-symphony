## Holistic verdict against Mahler 1/i

Mahler 1/i and this movement have different aims. Mahler builds a long introduction (about 60 bars) on one static pad, with calls placed at different distances. This movement uses a 14-bar Lento that moves harmonically (motto, cor anglais, crescendo to the German sixth, horn call). That is the Tchaikovsky and Brahms tradition named in the brief, and it should not copy Mahler's stasis.

On the craft points the reading isolates, the score already works the way Mahler does:
- every soft solo or call is marked one level above its pad (principle 1);
- returning introduction material is placed over held pedals (principle 2: 73–76, 117–121, 175–179), as Mahler brings the drone and calls back in his development;
- the recapitulation is shortened, as in Mahler (Mahler 85 bars against a much longer exposition; here 36 bars against the exposition's 62).

A conductor reading this score would take it for the work of a fluent late-Romantic professional. The one thing on the page a conductor would point out at rehearsal is the engraving slip in finding 1.

**Compared with v208.** Nothing got worse. All 18 changes since v208 were checked: 27–28, 43–44, 59/110, 69–72 texts, 120, 124–125, 139–142, 150, 161–163, 171–174, 177–178 and 179–180. Two of them change register and colour, and the reading bears on both (findings 3 and 4). Neither is a regression.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 103, Cl. 1·2: "a2" printed twice (applied)
- **Problem.** The clarinet staff shows "a2 a2" over the first note of the theme-combination passage, one above the rest and one above the A4.
- **Cause.** devClOne carries a manual `^\markup \bold "a2"` on the eighth rest (added with critic 84's change). The part-combiner prints its own "a2" because Cl 1 and Cl 2 are identical.
- **Age.** The defect is old: it is already in v190 and v208 (checked on the PDFs). A bbox scan of all 18 pages found no other duplicated "a2", "1.", "2.", "Solo", "div." or "unis." label.
- **Evidence.** "a2" is one indication for both players (Adler, Appendix A, adler2 PDF 312). A doubled label looks like a typesetting error to a conductor.
- **Fix (applied).** In sections/dev.ily, devClOne at 103: `r8^\markup \bold "a2" a'8(\f f''4.->)` → `r8 a'8(\f f''4.->)`.
- **Result.** The combiner's single "a2" remains. The Cl 1 part prints no "a2" either way.

### 2. Graded layers (Mahler Fig. 6.1) — verified, no change
- **Reading.** Casanelles PDF 271–272: the softer marking makes the distant layer.
- **What I checked.** Every place where a soft line enters over a pad:
  - 1–4: timp. ppp under B.cl./Vc/Cb pp;
  - 5–8: C.a. p< over strings pp;
  - 13: Hn 1 mp over strings p>pp;
  - 73–76: Fl 1 p espr. over pp strings and Hn 4, with the timpani ppp at 76;
  - 77–81: B.cl. mf espr. over p tremolo;
  - 127–130: Cl 1 p over pp cellos and timpani;
  - 147–155: C.a. p answers between a mf/mp tune, over a pp sul tasto pad;
  - 167–170: Hn 1·2 + Tpt 1·2 ff on the head over a f tutti;
  - 171–174: Vc mp against C.a. p.
- **Result.** Every foreground line is one level above its pad, as in Fig. 6.1 and in Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 12–15. No change.

### 3. Bars 139–142: Fl 1 alone on A♭6 (c194) — decided: keep
- **Reading.** Since v214 the violins play the tune at A♭5 in unison with Ob 1 and Cl 1, and Fl 1 alone has A♭6. Casanelles PDF 277–278 says a single flute above a strong unison adds upper partials and is not heard as a separate melodic peak. That is the effect c194 wanted.
- **Result.** The climax register stays unused until the violins' A♭6 at 160 (c24/c61, c184, c191, c193).
- **Cost.** Some brilliance at 140 compared with v208. This is the intended trade-off, not a regression. No change.

### 4. Bars 162–163: Ob 1 doubling Vln II at A4 inside the brass (c197) — decided: keep
- **Problem.** At 162, Ob 1 and Vln II play A4 ff. That is inside the ff brass, which diminuendo to mp: Hn 1·2 and Tpt 2 on E4, Tpt 1 on C5, Cl 1 on E5. Vln I has the upper A5 alone. By Casanelles PDF 278 (masking within one frequency band), the oboe at A4 adds weight to the lower octave and is not heard as a line. That is what c197 wanted: Rimsky's "double the lower octave".
- **Check.** I rendered 158–162 at 85 dpi to see whether removing Fl 1 from the A5 (v208) weakens the top. It does not. The brass fade from ff to mp across the bar, so one violin section at ff carries A5, and the harp's f chord on beat 1 marks the change.
- **Result.** No reason under this lens to reverse a decision made two cycles ago. No change.

### 5. Bars 56–63: counterline in the tune's register — decided: keep (re-checked under principle 10)
- **Problem.** Cl 1 + Hn 2 (p espr.) move in E♭4–C5, inside the violins' range (E♭4–A♭5, p molto cantabile). That is the masking case of PDF 278.
- **Why it works.** The tune is two violin sections in unison against one clarinet and one horn. The counter moves mostly when the tune holds (56–57, 59). From 60 the violins rise to mf over the counter's p.
- **History.** c167 and c197 already closed this point as protected. The reading gives no evidence that the tune is masked, only a general warning. No change.

### 6. Introduction length and stasis compared with Mahler — decided: no change
- **The difference.** Mahler sustains a single pad for about 60 bars. A conductor might ask whether 1–14 could be longer or more static.
- **Why not.** The brief names Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák, Rachmaninoff and Elgar as models, not Mahler. HISTORY protects the intro length (c23, c43) and forbids new slow bars (c43, c83). The cyclic links in 1–14 depend on its harmonic motion (Ger6–V at 12–13, E♭/E♮ at 8–9).
- **Result.** Casanelles' point is about how layers are marked, not about length. Nothing to change.

### 7. Regression check against v208 — no item is worse
- **Pitch and voice-leading changes.** 27–28 Hn 3/Tbn 1 C→B; 44 Hn 2 E♭4; 178 Vln II/Vla; 150 Vln II `<f'' a''>`. All improve or keep the voice-leading.
- **Articulation and text changes.** 59/110 tenutos; 124–125 slur split; 171–174 Vc slurs per bar; 69–76 text. All are clearer.
- **Breath and rhythm changes.** 120 Cl 2 breath (Vla keep the triplets); 179–180 timpani rhythm. Neither weakens anything.
- **Register changes.** 139–142 and 162–163, discussed in findings 3 and 4.
