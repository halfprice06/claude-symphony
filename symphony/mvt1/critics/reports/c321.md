## Findings

### 1. The violins' S statement begins on no named string; the reading's model puts it on the D string, then the A (applied)

**Bars.** 55 beat 4 to 57 beat 1, Vln I and Vln II in unison, p, *molto cantabile*.

**What is wrong.** At 55.4 the violins take over the second theme from the cello and horn. They play the pickup E♭4 and the rising sixth to C5 held for most of a bar. Then come the turn C5–D♭5–C5–B♭4 and the A♭4 at 57.1. *Sul A* starts only at 57.2 (F5, c141). Without a mark, a section plays E♭4 on the D string, then crosses to the A string for C5 and returns to the D for B♭4 and A♭4. The interval that carries the movement's identity, the rising sixth of the motto and of the S head, then becomes a string crossing, and the turn is split across two strings.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov's model for a unison violin cantabile is Ex. 12, *Sheherazade* III: "Cantabile for Vn. I and II on the D string, then on the A" (PDF 57). The rendered example (PDF 186) shows *sul D* over the opening phrase of a p unison melody in the middle of the violin range, over sustained violas and divided cellos.
- PDF 54: a melody in the middle of the range needs a colour or doubling of its own to stand out. Here the line starts at E♭4–C5, the register of the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline (E♭4–B♭4) entering at 56. The covered D-string colour separates the tune from the counterline by timbre, and costs no change of dynamics or notes.
- The movement already plays its rising sixths on one string: the cello motto sul G at 1–3 (c251) and Vln I's last motto sul D at 177–182 (c251). Both follow the same reasoning, and both cite Adler PDF 73–75, where the D string is "warm and subdued". Bar 55.4 is the only exposed string statement of a rising-sixth theme head with no string marked.

**Why it matters for the melody.** The S statement at 56 is the movement's first strong lyrical moment for the violins. It is the tune that has to stay with a listener. On the D string the rising sixth becomes one shift on one string, and the turn C–D♭–C–B♭ (the motto's neighbour, c221) stays on one string. Then the line opens onto the A string at the tenuto F5 of 57, as in Ex. 12.

**Playability.** E♭4 is first position on the D string. C5, D♭5, C5, B♭4 and A♭4 lie in third to fourth position. This is a standard section shift at p and Poco meno mosso (crotchet = 112). It is not a fast same-string leap, which is the only hazard Adler names (PDF 82–83, cited by c141 and c251).

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/tTwo.ily, tTwoVlnOne and tTwoVlnTwo):**

```lilypond

\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul D"
es'4(\p^\markup \italic "molto cantabile"^\startTextSpan | c''2.~\< c''16\> des''16 c''16 bes'16 |
aes'4\!\stopTextSpan \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic \small "sul A" f''2--\<^\startTextSpan es''4\!) |
```

### Points examined and kept

- **15–22, P exposition.** Vln I is alone at f, the top voice, and the background is marked mf. This is the artificial means of PDF 54. The D♭6 peak at 20 is doubled an octave below by Vln II (PDF 58–59). The viola counter reaches f at 20, but it lies an octave and more below the D♭6, so the tune stands out by position. The graded build (c287) is protected. Keep.
- **47–55, S in Hn 1 and cellos.** The cello carries a passionate cantabile in its upper register, C4–F5 (PDF 56, Ex. 7–8). I considered *sul D* for the cellos' E♭3–C4 pickup as well, and rejected it. The phrase reaches A♭4 at 50 and C5 at 51, too high to sustain dolce on the D string, so the A string (the reading's string for this kind of melody) has to carry most of it. Keep.
- **The A♭6 peaks, 31/72/122/140/160.** Each has its octave below, as Note I (PDF 62) and PDF 58–59 require. The solo-desk upper octave of Ex. 24 and 26 (PDF 59–60) is a p–pp device and does not fit these f–fff peaks. Keep.
- **147–155, S recapitulated in C.** Violas with Hn 1, cellos an octave below, violins in a pp tremolo halo above. This is PDF 60 d) ("when the violins are otherwise employed"), and the tune's dynamics separate it from the halo (PDF 54). The violins' own C-major S is reserved for the finale. Keep.
- **167–169, the last forte P head in Hn 1·2 and violas.** The tune lies in the middle (G3–F4) under the violins' f tremolo. PDF 54 says such a melody is "not so prominent". But the tune is ff in horns against f strings, which is the artificial means, and the layout is the finale's reserve (c254, c279, c299). That is a cross-movement reason this reading does not answer. Keep.
- **171–174 and 177–182, the coda statements.** The solo cello is on the A string (PDF 56) beside the cor anglais's motto. The last motto is the top voice, sul D (PDF 54, position). Keep.
- **Memorability against the models.** The motto (rising sixth, chromatic fall), the P head (empty downbeat, D♭6 peak at 20) and the S head (rising sixth, turn, tenuto peaks) each have one fixed contour and recur at pitch levels the ear can follow. Their climaxes come at 20, 63–64 and 158–160, in a rising order. This is the economy of Tchaikovsky 5/i and Dvořák 8/i, and I found no theme whose shape or rhythm needs changing.
