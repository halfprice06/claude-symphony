## Findings

The assigned reading is about prolongation: how a chord is held through time by neighbour, passing, upper-fifth and 6/4 chords. For a form critic that is the question of what each section prolongs and whether its arrivals are prepared. I checked every structural arrival and every 6/4 sonority. I recommend no change to the score. The findings below rank the places where the reading bears most on the form, and give the reason each should stay.

### 1. The arrival of the recapitulation, 127–131: the dominant has no leading tone for its last ten beats (no change)

What the reading says. A&S PDF 258 (§11): V without its leading tone "does not tend very actively toward I", which makes it ineffective "in situations where the expectation of I becomes a factor". The recapitulation is the strongest such situation in the movement. From beat 2 of 128 to 131 no B♮ sounds: Cl 1 has the motto G3–E♭4–D4–C♯4–C4 (129–130), the violas, Bsn 1 and Vln II enter with the same figure, Hn 2 adds F4 at 130.3, and Cl 1 and the violas hold C4 over the cellos' G2 semiquavers. The last sonority is G–C–D–E♭–F: a 6/4 moving to a V7 whose 4th stays as an anticipation of the tonic.

Why it stays. The same unit gives the answer. PDF 322–323 (Ex. 19-30a, Chopin Op. 28/14): a 6/4 can stand for a V that "is not literally present but … nonetheless strongly implied" when "the bass progression suggests IV–V–I so unmistakably that the cadential function … can hardly be questioned". Here the bass has held G for fourteen bars (117–130). The major V is established as the pedal's chord: B♮ sounds at 117, 119, 122–124 and as the 4–3 at 125–126 (Hn 3, Cl 2, Vla, Vc), and 126 is a full V. Bars 127–130 are the 8–7–6–5 / 6–5–4–3 elaboration of PDF 313–315 (Ex. 19-15, 19-16) on a large scale, with the 6/4 resolving to D and F and the 4th held over as the motto's own goal C. The motto melody G–E♭–C is also the V–III–I arpeggiation that A&S PDF 252 places "before reprise sections and sonata recapitulations", here in the upper voices over the pedal instead of in the bass.

Corpus. Tchaikovsky 6/i prolongs its last dominant before the recapitulation of the second theme for 104 crotchets (cadence.csv: HC at 1110, resolve time 104) with the same alternation: i6/4 at 1118, 1158 and 1166, V7 and V between, VI6 at 1190 (harmony.csv). Beethoven 9/i reaches its ff D-major arrival at onset 600 (bar 301, where most analyses place the recapitulation; MCTL labels 600–628 "transition") from vii°7 (591–600), without a root-position V. A recapitulation entered from a V that is established by its pedal, not by a leading tone in its last beat, is within both models.

This answers c233's question from a different principle (c233 rejected Hn 3 B3 at 129–130 for the minor-second clash with the C4 anticipations). I tested no alternative voicing because the only free low voices (B.tbn, Bsn 2) would put B2 against C4 (a minor ninth) or create a low major third G2–B2 at f, and either would weaken the motto's C, which is the reason the passage works.

### 2. The recapitulation's climax 158–166 is one expanded cadential progression (no change)

What the reading says. PDF 306–307: the accented 6/4's "basic function [is] to delay the arrival of an expected melodic or harmonic event". PDF 317–319: the tension of the cadential 6/4 "can be enhanced by expanding it", and "interpolating material between a cadential 6/4 and the dominant to which it resolves forms the basis for the cadenza".

What the score does. 158 is the cadential 6/4 of C at ff (full brass, the reserved root-position C-major chord avoided). V7 at 159 goes to ♭VI at 160 (fff), °7/V and V7 at 161 go to vi at 162, and IV (163) – ii (164) – cadential 6/4 (164.3) – V7 (165) – I (166) completes the cadence at p. Between the first cadential 6/4 and the final V–I lie two deceptive moves and a subdominant approach: the interpolation principle of Ex. 19-23 and 19-24 on the scale of nine bars. This is also the main recapitulation change in the second group. In the exposition the peak (63–64) sits on vi after an I6/4 at 62 (sub. p), and the cadential 6/4 comes later (66) and is cut off at 68. The recapitulation moves the 6/4 onto the peak itself, so the climax is a delay of C, not a chord in another key. A&S PDF 258 (VI as upper neighbour to V) explains the ♭VI at 160 as the third of the three A♭ neighbours (82, 122, 160).

Why no expansion of 158 itself. A two-bar 6/4 would add a bar after 157 and renumber 158–182, which other movements cite (c243 counted 35 lines in their coherence files with movement I numbers between 110 and 189). The reading does not require it: the expansion is already there, between 158 and 166.

### 3. The coda 167–182 withholds a full close on purpose, and the reading supports it (no change)

What the reading says. PDF 323–324 (Ex. 19-30b, Schumann "Eusebius"): a 6/4 placed after the cadential V "stands for I, not for V", used "to avoid a definite conclusion that would be out of keeping" with the character of the piece. PDF 319–320 (Ex. 19-26): a 6/4 can come first and be stabilized "retrospectively" by the 5/3 that follows. PDF 252 (Ex. 16-3): V–III–I in the bass arpeggiates the tonic.

What the score does. 167–170 prolongs i over the C pedal by neighbouring chords: iv6/4 at 168.3–169 (PDF 309, the sustained-bass IV) and vii°7 at 170, resolving at 171. 171–174 is the movement's last cadential progression: i – iv7 – ii6/5 – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V. The V at 174 then goes to i6/4 at 175 over the same G: the Eusebius move. The bass then descends G–E♭–D–C (175–178), which is the motto and the V–(i6)–V4/3–I arpeggiation, and the 6/4 of 175 is stabilized at 178. Movement I runs attacca into the Adagio; a root-position V–i here would close what movement II continues. The difference from Chopin's Ex. 16-3 is deliberate: Chopin brings the major 3̂ into the bass; this score keeps E♮ in the violas only (182), because closing the E♭/E♮ thread is reserved for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md).

Corpus. Tchaikovsky 6/i also ends without a final authentic cadence: its coda alternates I and IV over the tonic (harmony.csv 1338–1410, cadence.csv PC at 1362), the same neighbouring-chord prolongation as 167–170 and 172. The coda's share of the time (13.6 %, c243) is the one proportion above the models, but its soft part is the slow frame that answers the 14-bar introduction, and the protected tempo descent (c098) depends on it.

### 4. The development's harmonic plan is built from the reading's three long-range 5/3 techniques (no change)

- Descending 3rds that arpeggiate the goal (PDF 251): C (80–81) – A♭ (82) – F (111), the I–VI–IV of the textbook, then F moves to V through the chromatic wedge 111–116, a long-range passing motion (PDF 256).
- Descending 5ths toward a goal (PDF 249–250): the fugato's G–C–F–B♭ entries, each prepared by its own dominant. The goal is not lost: it is shifted by a semitone to B minor at 97, the inflection c243 traced through the movement.
- Ascending 5ths as a rising stepwise line decorated by upper 5ths (PDF 250): D minor (103) – B (V of E, 106) – E minor (107) – C (V of F, 110) – F minor (111). The book says ascending 5ths are not goal-directed; the score gives this stretch the softest dynamics of the development (107–110 p) and lets the goal-directed descent of the wedge carry the climb. That is the right distribution.

The three A♭ climaxes are VI as upper neighbour to V (PDF 258) at three levels. I found nothing in the development that the reading would change.

### 5. The horn call is a cadential 6/4 turned into a melody, and it returns in the form at the right places (no change)

The horn call E♭3–C4–B3 over V at 13–14 is 6–4–3 over G: the 4th entered by leap and resolved by step, which PDF 315–316 (Ex. 19-18) describes as "the decoration of a stepwise line". At 41–42 the four horns play E♭3–C4–B♭3 over E♭: the same contour as a neighbouring 5–6–5 (PDF 308–309). In the coda Cl 1 plays E♭4–C5–B4 over the i6/4 and V4/3 (175–177), and its B4 is resolved by Vln I's C5 at 178: transferred resolution (PDF 316, Ex. 19-19). The intro, the medial caesura and the coda are tied by one 6/4 gesture. Keep.

### 6. Every 6/4 in the movement has a function (no change)

A&S PDF 324: "never use a 6/4 chord unless you have a clear idea of how it functions". The beat-by-beat search found 6/4 sonorities in 22 places (row 27). Each is an accented/cadential, neighbouring, passing, arpeggio, incomplete-neighbour or dominant-pedal 6/4 of rows 13–22, or one of the two "stands for" cases of row 26. Two were questioned earlier and are confirmed here: 102 (i6/4 over F♯ on the downbeat after V7) is the neighbouring 6/4 "repeated on the next strong beat" of Ex. 19-8 (PDF 309), and 56–57 (IV6/4 over A♭ going on to ii7) is the incomplete neighbour of Ex. 19-17 (PDF 315).
