## Findings

I made no edit. Every passage this reading bears on was checked. None falls short of a master's work strongly enough to justify a change, and the one candidate I tested (finding 3) is worse than the current text.

### 1. The horn recall at 125–126 sits inside a prolonged V, as Ex. 14-12's middle phrase does. Keep (c581).

PDF 224: in the Schumann quatrain "the B phrase prolongs (but does not tonicize) V", and the return of the tonic comes with the final A. PDF 220 (§11) adds that the Schubert's return "refers back to some of the opening material".

The G pedal 117–130 is that kind of phrase. The reduction shows no chord with F♯ in 117–130, so G is never tonicized; it is always V of C. c581 put Hn 1's E♭3–C4–B3 at 125–126 over the same 6/4→V as at 13–14. The listener therefore hears this dominant as the introduction's dominant come back. The recall refers back before the return, not after it, which makes 131 sound like the answer to 14. The B3 is left in the violas at 127.1–3 and in Cl 1 at 128.2 and is not resolved until the P head. The reading has no objection: a prolonged V is not required to resolve its leading tone inside the prolongation. Keep.

### 2. D minor at 103 is a key that "sets in without preparation" and is confirmed later by its dominant. Keep.

PDF 216 (§6): "At other times, the new tonic sets in without such preparation, and it is confirmed later by its dominant." No earlier pass named an example of this in the score. Bar 103 is one:
- 102 ends on F♯ (V of B minor, half cadence, ff).
- 103 starts D minor with both heads, f, with no pivot and no dominant first. This is phrase modulation as in Ex. 14-11 (PDF 223).
- 104.4 has A–C♯–E–G with F, V9 of D minor, confirming the key after it began.
- 105–106 leave by C and B to V of E minor.

The other key areas are prepared by their dominants (A♭ 46.3, C major 144–146) or confirmed by them inside the area (B minor 101). The development therefore uses both of §6's ways of setting up a key, which gives its key changes different weights. Keep.

### 3. The last two beats before the recapitulation have the 7th but no leading tone (130.3–4). I tested adding B and rejected it. Keep.

PDF 219–220 (§10): after a long dominant, the 7th (4̂) turns V into V7 of the main key, and "V7 has so strong a drive toward I that the listener is prepared for the return of the tonic." The score has the 7th: Hn 2 F4 at 130.3. But the per-instrument listing shows that from 128.3 to 130.4 no part sounds B. Beat 130.4 is G1–G2–G3–D3–C4 (Cl 1, violas)–F4–D5: a V7 with a 4th in place of its third, because the stretto's motto lines (violas E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4, Cl 1 D♭4–C4) arrive on C a beat early. The leading tone returns only at 132.2 in the P head.

I checked whether a B on 130.3–4 would make 131 land harder. Hn 3 is free in 130 and plays C4 at 131.1, so B3→C4 in Hn 3 would be the obvious voice. I rejected it:
- B3 would sound a semitone under Cl 1's C4, the resolution struck against its own preparation in the same register. A&S Unit 21 (PDF 469–470) and the c502/c522 fixes remove exactly this.
- The motto ends D–D♭–C. A B under the D♭–C would draw the ear to B–C and away from the motto's close, which the stretto exists to make.
- In Ex. 14-1 itself the return is driven by the 7th in the bass (V4/2, bar 11), not by the leading tone. The reading asks for 4̂, and 130.3 supplies it.

The drive into 131 comes from the stretto's rhythm (crotchets 127, quavers 128, triplets 129, semiquavers 130) and the timpani's G roll rising pp–mf–f. The protection (c473 "127-130 V with 6"; c493) stands. Keep.

### 4. Proportions: the second key arrives at the midpoint of the exposition, and the dominant before the return matches Tchaikovsky 6/i. Keep.

PDF 227 (§13) tells the student to "achieve the cadence in the dominant key midway". Section times from the v603 MIDI map:

- The V7–I that brings in A♭ at 46.3–47 falls at 128.4 s, 0.50 of the exposition's duration. In Tchaikovsky 6/i (MCTL annotation, onsets in crotchets) the second theme in D major starts at crotchet 350 of an exposition spanning 72–638, which is 0.49. The midpoint arrival here is the same as that model.
- The recapitulation's C-major arrival at 147 comes at 0.41 of the recapitulation. All of the shortening is in the first group (16 bars, against 32 for P and the transition). PDF 225–226 explains why: the bridge no longer has to modulate.
- The dominant pedal 117–130 is 14 of 182 bars (7.7%). Tchaikovsky 6/i stands on its dominant (the annotated HC from crotchet 1110 to 1214) for 26 of 354 bars (7.3%) before the recapitulation's second group. The pedal here is not too short, and it is not padded either.
- The c599 stringendo at 113–116 shortens the wedge by about 1 s and ends with "a tempo" at 117. That is a break in the flow that sets off the arrival on V (§6, PDF 216). It does not move any section boundary. Keep.
