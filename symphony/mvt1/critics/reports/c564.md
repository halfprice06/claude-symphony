## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. The three questions I tested hardest are below, each judged against the reading first. I then say where, within this lens, the movement still falls short of a master's.

### 1. Soft solo lines doubled by a like wind (no change)

**Question.** Adler PDF 249–250 says a unison of two like winds on an ordinary solo line "limits and impedes the expressive quality and prohibits any slight rubato". He allows it only in tuttis "where simple volume is required". The earlier passes did not check this on the MIDI.

**Test.** I listed every onset where Fl 1 = Fl 2, Ob 1 = Ob 2, Cl 1 = Cl 2, Bsn 1 = Bsn 2, Hn 1 = Hn 2 or Hn 3 = Hn 4 at the same pitch. Every case is f or louder (24, 28–32, 41–42, 69–72, 78–82, 96–102, 103–106, 115–121, 131–136, 140, 142, 157–161, 167–170), with two exceptions. At 68.4 the flutes a2 play a p< pickup into the f codetta, so the unison serves the crescendo into a tutti. At 163–165 the bassoons play a2 mp on the bass of the dissolution, which c479 protected. No solo melody is doubled by a like instrument. The rule is met.

### 2. Oboe and cor anglais in their lowest fifth (no change)

**Question.** Copland cuts the first oboe in a diminuendo because "it honks too much in that extreme low register" (Adler PDF 270, Ex. VIII-41). c274 and c364 each fixed one case. Are there any left?

**Test.** Ob 1 goes below G4 only twice: G♭4 at 97.4 and F4 at 133.4, both in ff tuttis. Ob 2 does so 14 times: 12.1 (G♭4, f), 28–32 and 132–133 (F4, ff tutti) and 117–120 (B3–F4, the P phrase with Fl 2 under the ff trombone motto). The cor anglais's lowest note is B3 at 99.1 (written F♯4), ff, in the register Adler's ch. 7 calls powerful. None of these is in a soft passage or a diminuendo. The rule is met.

### 3. The B-minor S at 97–102: should the clarinets fill the middle? (no change)

**Question.** Adler PDF 238 and Ex. VIII-39 (Dvořák, PDF 267–268) show oboes and clarinets filling the upper-middle harmony of a forte tutti. At 97–102 the tune is in three octaves (Fl 1; Ob 1 + Vln I; C.a. + Vln II + Vc). Between the D5 tune and the D6 flute there is nothing. Below that, only the violas' arpeggio and Hn 3·4 fill the middle. Cl 1·2, Ob 2 and Fl 2 rest.

**Assessment.** HISTORY settled this at c184 ("Accepted: clarinets rest 97–102 (register clash with the theme's lower octave) … Settled; do not reverse without new evidence"), and c364 lists "97-102 clarinets rest" as do-not-touch. The reading gives no new evidence. Ex. VIII-39 is a dance tune over a pedal with the whole wind section on the tune. Here the tune is a cantabile S in octaves, and Adler PDF 250–251 prefers octaves to filler for winds a 2. Held clarinets at B4–F♯5 would lie on the tune's middle octave (Ob 1 + Vln I, B4–D6) and blur it. The clarinets' rest also lets their f entry on the P head at 103 sound fresh (PDF 253: choirs heard apart, then together). Keep.

### Where the music falls short of a master's, within this lens

I looked for a colour a listener would not remember and for a woodwind device from the chapter the score has not yet used. The score already uses the horn as a wind pedal (73–76, 127–130), relief colour at the end (175–182), the Eroica ball-passing with a varied third gesture (77–82), choirs apart then together (103–111), a different scoring for each S statement, and a single colour for the B.cl/C.a. motto at each soft return. Two devices are unused. Brahms's single bassoon in octaves with the violins (PDF 267) would cover the Cl 1 + Hn 2 counterline at 56–63 or the exposed ppp motto at 177–182. Debussy's pulsating unison (PDF 262–265) is outside the style. The remaining distance from Dvořák or Tchaikovsky in this lens is small. It lies in the lean S peak at 64–66, which c109, c264 and c514 hold back on purpose for 158–161, and in the 156–157 climb that the form critics protect. Neither is a woodwind-scoring fault. I found no change that would improve the music rather than only alter it.

### Other sources

- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II (rimsky-principles PDF 74–75): a wind and a horn in unison give a finer legato than either alone. This supports keeping Cl 1 + Hn 2 on the counterline at 56–68 rather than adding Brahms's bassoon there.
- Rimsky-Korsakov ch. III (rimsky-principles PDF 85, 96): harmonic notes must not be missing from the upper parts, and close wood-wind harmony should have no void in forte. The 158 fix of c514 applied this. At 97–102 the void between the tune's octaves is a melodic octave doubling, not a gap in the harmony, so it does not apply.
- Adler ch. 7 (adler1 PDF 185, 205): the flute below about C♯5 has "little carrying power", and the cor anglais's lowest fifth has "tremendous expressive carrying power". These are the register limits behind findings 2 and 3 and the Fl 1 doubling at 109–110.
