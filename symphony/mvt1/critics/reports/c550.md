### 2. Considered and rejected: sul ponticello on the string tremolo at 77–81

**Bars:** 77–81 (solo winds and the head dialogue A♭ minor – E minor – C minor over string tremolo, with the scherzo seed at 78–79).

**Question.** Casanelles (PDF 223–224) treats an extended technique as a timbre with a precise meaning, such as sul ponticello for eeriness. Bars 77–81 are the development's first step into the A♭–E–C cycle and the place that foreshadows the scherzo. A ponticello colour there would be the movement's most vivid timbre, and the scherzo would recall it.

**Why I reject it.** (a) No movement uses sul ponticello (I grepped mvt2 v400, scherzo v400 and finale v400: only sul tasto and con sordino), so the colour would point to nothing later. A cyclic seed has to be answered (PDF 200). (b) PDF 238: a single unprepared foreign colour makes the sound space heterogeneous and weakens the effect. (c) In the model idiom (Brahms, Dvořák, Tchaikovsky, Elgar) the colour is rare in symphonic first movements. The ghost-like soft passages in those works, Elgar 2/i included (c459), use plain soft tremolo and winds, as 77–81 already does. (d) The layout is protected (c147 "solo winds over tremolo strings 77–81"; c439 77–130 scoring), and a timbre mark answers none of those reasons. It would add strangeness, not memorability.

**Applied in scratch copy:** no.

### 3. Considered and rejected: stating the timpani pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` before the coda

**Bars considered:** 125–130 (timpani G roll under the stretto) and 171–174.

**Question.** The finale quotes the pulse at IV 229–230 at pitch, and the scherzo's motive x leads to it. In I the C–G form sounds only once, pp, at 179–180. The ff form at 27, 30 and 131 is C–C. By the referential principle (PDF 200) the listener should know the source before the finale quotes it.

**Why I reject it.** The pulse reaches the finale along a chain, not from one hearing: I 27/30/131 (ff, C–C), I 179–180, II 84–85, III 9 and 363–371 (motive x), then IV 9, 143 and 178 (finale.md, quotation 13; mvt1.md seeds, bar 179–180). The listener therefore meets the figure many times before IV 229. In I, the pp pulse at Tempo I crotchet = 50 is the "heartbeat" of PDF 243: it fits the state, and it is new at that point. A C–G stroke inside the G pedal (125–130) would put the tonic C on the strong beats of a dominant pedal. That weakens the pedal and spends the reveal (PDF 235, 237–238). In 171–174 it would cover the solo cello and cor anglais duet (c400, coda harmony protected). The current single soft statement is the right weight.

**Applied in scratch copy:** no.

### Where the movement stands under this lens

I asked whether any moment of the cyclic design would be forgotten. The motto is clear, and its rising sixth is the interval the whole symphony keeps (Reading note 10): P, S and the horn call open with it, movement II's theme A fills it, the scherzo's trio fills it in E, and the finale's chorale turns it major (G–E). This is the opening-interval identity that Casanelles describes for the Superman theme (PDF 227), and it is why the later quotations will register. The emotional arc works. Movement I rises through three A♭ climaxes (82 < 122 < 160) and never confirms C major at forte. It ends ppp with one viola E♮ and crosses attacca into A♭. The finale keeps its reserves for the reveal (PDF 237–238): forte C major, C7, the trumpets' B→C, clashed cymbals and the major brass chorale. I found nothing within the lens that a master would change. The open weakness other lenses keep naming, the two-bar climb at 156–157, does not affect the cyclic design: the third climax still lands on ♭VI with the strongest scoring of the three.
