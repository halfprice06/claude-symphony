## Findings

### 1. Bar 158: Tbn 1 E4 instead of C4 (applied in the scratch copy)

**Bars.** 158 (recTwo, rehearsal K, the ff arrival of the second theme in C major over the G pedal).

**What is wrong.** The held brass on 158 are tuba G1, B.tbn G2, Tbn 2 G3, Tpt 3 C4, Tbn 1 C4 and Tpt 2 G4, all semibreves at ff. Tpt 1 and Hn 1·2 have the tune, and Hn 3·4 have the counter. Above the bass octave the held parts are G3, C4 doubled at pitch by a trumpet and a trombone, and G4. The sixth (E) of the cadential 6/4 is not in the held brass. On the downbeat, where the brass attack lands, the only E in the orchestra is the violas' E4 (c514). The tune reaches E only on beat 2. So the brass attack at the recapitulation's main arrival sounds as an open G–C–G.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- PDF 100: brass harmony "should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". G3–C4–G4 leaves a fifth empty in the held brass.
- PDF 101, diagram: Tbn 3 and tuba make the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts go to two trombones reinforced by a trumpet. At 158 the bass half of that layout is in place (B.tbn G2 + tuba G1), but the upper parts do not spell the chord. Two of the three are on the same note.
- PDF 104: a trumpet and a trombone in unison is "not so common, as this unites the two most powerful agents in the group". Earlier critics removed this unison at 159 (c169, c414), 160 (c154) and 161 (c169), citing this page. 158 is the one bar of the block where it still sounds for a whole bar at ff.
- PDF 114: balance matters most in long sustained chords. This is a held semibreve at ff.
- Supporting: c514 gave the violas E4 on 158 so that "the ff cadential 6/4 has its sixth on the downbeat" (RK PDF 85–86, 96). The same reasoning applies to the brass. Aldwell & Schachter PDF 154 say the 6 of a cadential 6/4 usually falls to 5. The same page says the bass is the best tone to double, then the 6th, and that "the 4th is not doubled except in very free or complex textures". PDF 316 adds that when an inner voice doubles the 4th, one of the two must move up. That is what happens now: Tbn 1 takes C4 up to D4 (c232, c353, c412), while Tpt 3 makes the 4–3.

**Fix.** In `recTwoTbnOne`, bar 158:
```
  R1*11 | e'1\ff | d'2 b2 \breathe | c'1\ff |
```
(was `c'1\ff`). Tbn 1 now plays E4–D4–B3–C4 through 158–160. The held brass become G1–G2–G3–C4–E4–G4: the bass in octaves with the three upper parts in close order, as in the PDF 101 diagram. Tpt 3 keeps the 4 (C4→B3, the motto tail protected by c530). Tbn 1 now resolves the 6 to 5 (E4→D4) instead of taking the doubled 4th up. The trumpet–trombone unison is gone from 158–161. At 162, Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 still share C4, the doubled third of vi (c324). Tbn 1 E4 at ff after eleven bars' rest is in the instrument's strong middle register. E4 joins Hn 1·2's E4 on the tune from beat 2, which is the horn-plus-trombone duplication of PDF 103–104. I added a comment above the variable.

**Earlier decisions this answers.** c124 and c414 kept the 158 unison. c414 gave three reasons:
- "The C is the dissonant fourth and needs weight." After the change, C is still in Tpt 3 and the violas on beats 1–2. From beat 3 it is also in Ob 1, Cl 1·2 (C6) and Hn 3·4 (C5): six players. What changes is that E, which had one section on the downbeat, now has two.
- "mvt1.md records the layout for the finale." The record is in the "Not used anywhere in movement I" paragraph. It explains that 158 is a cadential 6/4, not the root-position full-brass C major the finale keeps for itself. The chord is still a 6/4 over G, so that reserve is untouched. No other movement quotes 158 (I grepped finale.md, mvt2.md and scherzo.md).
- "The arrival is protected (c283)." That protection, and c186's "brass layout 158–161", cover the attack, the levels and the approach. All of these are unchanged: same players, same rhythm, ff, same breaths.

No earlier fix is undone. c232, c353 and c412 recorded Tbn 1's rising C4–D4 as an acceptable doubling, not as something chosen on purpose.
