## Findings

### F1. Bar 91: the third fugato entry starts inside the cello's held unison (applied)

**Bars:** 91, Vc against Vln II and C.a.

**Problem:** at 91 the free cello voice held c'2. from the downbeat. The third entry (Vln II C5 with C.a. C4) begins at 91.1.5 on the same C4. So the cor anglais's first note, the note that marks the entry, sounded inside a cello note already holding that pitch at the unison. The entry's attack was masked at the point where c197 asked for each entry to be clearly heard.

**Evidence:** Adler 229 says Schoenberg replaced unisons with octaves because at-pitch doubling of like colours makes lines "swallow each other up." Adler 213 says Dvořák delays the oboe's entrance so the tune is clearer and the texture truly polyphonic. Adler 239 and 245 describe Schoenberg giving each fugue statement its own colour so it can be heard; that only works if the statement's first note is not already sounding.

**Fix (dev.ily, recDevVc at 91):**
```
% was: c'2.\f es'4~( |
r4 c'2\f es'4~( |
```
The subject, countersubject, entries, colours and the free cello's pitches are unchanged. Only the cello's onset moves from the downbeat to beat 2, after the entry's head. This answers the protection of 85–96 ("free cello" kept): the line is the same and its counterpoint against the entry is kept, now in staggered entries, which is what the protection was meant to preserve.

**Applied:** yes.

### F2. Bar 152: the sul tasto halo sounds the tune's peak F5 before the tune reaches it (applied)

**Bars:** 152, Vln II halo against violas and Cl 1.

**Problem:** the Vln II halo `<f'' a''>1:32` held F5 through the whole bar. The violas and Cl 1 reach the phrase's peak on F5 at 152.4. So the peak arrived on a pitch the background had sounded at the unison for three beats, and it lost its weight. This is the same fault c194 fixed at 150.

**Evidence:** Adler 217 and 224 (Ravel): when tune and accompaniment are close, keep them apart in colour and pitch; the background overlaps, and the tune is handed on cleanly. Adler 229: at-pitch doubling of like colours swallows the line.

**Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoVlnTwo at 152):**
```
% was: <f'' a''>1:32
<f'' a''>2:32 <a'' c'''>2:32
```
The halo keeps the IV chord and moves to A5/C6 before the peak. I rejected these alternatives:
- `<a'' c'''>` for the whole bar gives parallel octaves with the C.a. (G4→A4 against G5→A5).
- `<d'' a''>` turns IV into ii6/5 and spoils c163's 5–6.
- `<c''' f'''>` still sounds F.
- `<c'' a''>` doubles the tune's C5 at the unison.
- `<e'' a''>` clashes with F5.

This answers the 147–155 protection (c381): the scoring, colour, dynamic and harmony stay as they were. Only the halo's position changes, as c194 did at 150.

**Applied:** yes.
