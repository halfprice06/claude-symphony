## Findings

I found nothing in v452 that the reading marks as wrong. I also found no place within this lens where a change would bring the score clearly nearer a master's work without undoing a protected passage whose stated reason still holds. The score is unchanged in my scratch copy.

Many cycles have found no change. So I checked three things that the earlier passes had not covered, and then looked for a master-level weakness. The findings are ranked by how close each comes to a fault.

### F1. Bars 9–13: the rising-sixth chain. Checked, kept.

Vln I states the motto's rising sixth three times, over the bass C–F–D:
- G4–E♭5 at 9–10;
- A♭4–F5 at 10–11;
- B4–A♭5 at 11.

This is Ex. 17-20 (PDF 278–279): up a fourth, down a third, root-position chords. It breaks off on the German sixth at 12 and reaches V at 13.

Against the reading:
- PDF 264: three statements, then the break, which is the norm.
- PDF 279: "the change of rhythm and the breaking off of the sequential pattern do not occur at the same time". Here the harmonic rhythm changes at 11.3 (two chords in the bar), half a bar before the pattern breaks at 12.
- PDF 268: the top voice's register change sets off the goal. Vln I leaps a diminished seventh, B4–A♭5, into the Ger6, and A♭5–G5 then gives V its top.
- PDF 282: rising from I in minor is hard because of II°. The iiø7 at 11 has its seventh C prepared from 10, and it arrives at the break-off as a pre-dominant. PDF 282 calls that use "good".

This is one of the score's best moments: the motto's sixth becomes the engine of the sequence. The notes are protected (c250, c252, c342, c411). No change.

### F2. A new check: fifths between successive strong beats. Checked, kept.

PDF 266 says the voice-leading chord on the weak beat "prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". c342 scanned only the bass and top line. I scanned every pair of parts, downbeat to downbeat, in v452. The scan found fifths at eight places. Each has an intermediate chord or a moving voice, which is what the reading prescribes:

- **28→29** (Bn 1, Vc D4–E♭4 against Hn 4 G3–A♭3). The vii°7 over D at 28.3 comes between. The bars are quoted at pitch by the finale and protected.
- **45→46** (Vc G3–A♭3 over C2–D♭2). The cellos' A♭ arrives on 45.3 as a 5-6 over C (Ex. 17-10c, PDF 273). c342 F4.
- **92→93** (Bn 1 A♭3–F3 against Vc E♭4–C4). This is inside the fugato's linear dominant at 92.3. Protected (fugato).
- **98→99** (Va F♯4–B4 over B–E). The viola's arpeggio goes F♯4–B3–F♯4–B3 inside bar 98, so the two fifths are never adjacent notes. Protected (B-minor block).
- **114→115** (Tbn 1 C4–B♭3 against G4–F4). The vii°7 over D at 114.3 comes between. Protected (wedge).
- **139→140** (C–A♭ over F–D♭ in several parts). The A♭1 6/3 at 139.3 comes between: the 5/3–6/3 alternation of Ex. 17-4 (PDF 268). c342 F4.
- **141→142** (A♭/D♭ → G/C in the brass and woodwinds, N6 to V). At 141.3 Hn 3, Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 move D♭4–C4 over the iiø6/5 on D while A♭ is held, so the fifth A♭/D♭ never moves directly to G/C. This is the reading's voice-leading chord (PDF 266, Ex. 17-1 sequence A). Protected (N6 settings, c372).
- **153→154** (C.a. C5–G4 against Vc F4–C4). The cello arpeggio F4–E4–C4–A3 comes between, under the held C5.

None of these needs a change.

### F3. A new check: doubled leading tones before goal tonics. Checked, kept.

PDF 266 and the summary on PDF 286 allow a doubled leading tone in a sequence "if not just before a goal tonic". I checked every V→I arrival on a new section or key: 26→27, 40→41, 46→47, 54→55, 109→110, 146→147 and 165→166.

- **145–146.** Bn 1 and Hn 3 meet on B3 at 145.3. Bn 1 leaves at the end of 145, so only Hn 3 holds B3 into the C-major arrival at 147, where it resolves to C4.
- **165→166.** Cl 1 has B4 at 165.3 but moves on to F4, the seventh, at 165.4. Only Hn 3 resolves B3–C4.
- **54→55.** The leading tone G sounds in the cellos, clarinet and viola at 54.2–54.4, all inside V7 with its arpeggio figuration. On the tonic only C4 (Hn 1, Vc) and A♭ are struck, and every G moves by step.
- **26→27 and 40→41.** These are tutti stop chords and arrivals. Their octave doublings are protected orchestral doublings (c222: "tutti doublings of tendency tones at 12–13, 26–27, 142–143").

No change.

### F4. The transition 33–46 as rising from III. Checked, kept.

The reading says III is "the most frequent point of origin for rising sequences in minor", and that ascending fifths from III can reach a goal V but "going further … is impossible" (PDF 284). The transition does this:
- **33–36: i–VI–VII–III.** The bass is C–A♭–B♭–E♭: a descending-fifths fragment, VI–VII–III, that delivers III. It sits under the bassoon's motto line, with harmonic sevenths and chromatic passing notes (E♮4 at 35.2).
- **36–41: rising from III.** III (36), e♭7 (37, mixture), f (38), B♭7 (39–40), E♭ (41). The bass E♭–F–B♭–E♭ rises by step and then makes a cadence. The ii–V–I of E♭ (38–41) is the cadence that PDF 268 names as the usual close of a series.
- **42–47: on to V of A♭.** The four-horn call over E♭, then V7 of A♭ (43), V4/2–I6 (44–45), IV–V (46) and A♭ (47).

A master's bridge also brings the goal back into a strong register. PDF 263–264 shows Bach's sequences returning the goal to the opening register. Here the jump to Vln I G6 and Fl 1 G6 at 41 does that, an octave above the previous peak D6 at 39.3. The route 33–46 is protected (c163, c371, c440). No change.

### F5. The falling thirds at 162–166 and the S-theme frame. Checked, kept.

- **162–166.** The bass falls A–F–D, then goes on by descending fifths, D–G–C. This is the reading's two prescriptions in sequence:
  - the falling-thirds series breaks off at II, and in minor II is reached through II7 (PDF 282); here it is the iiø7 with borrowed A♭;
  - the last three chords of a descending-fifths series form II–V–I (PDF 268).

  The outer voices have the tenth A5/F1 between the octaves (PDF 268, row 13).
- **47–54, 97–100 and 147–152.** All three S statements keep the Ex. 17-20 frame: up a fourth, down a third, with the principal chords on alternate bars (PDF 279).

Both passages are protected (158–166 climax harmony; 47–55 plain harmonisation). No change.

### F6. Where the music could come nearer a master: considered and rejected.

I looked for a sequential passage that a listener would not remember, or one with a mechanical fourth statement (PDF 264). Two candidates came close.

**Fugato 85–96 (four entries descending by fifths).** PDF 264 warns that more than three statements "can be tedious unless the pattern is very short". Two things answer this:
- The fourth entry is cut off at 96 by the ff B-minor restatement, so the listener hears three complete entries and the start of a fourth.
- The texture thickens at each entry: cellos, then violas, then Vln II with the cor anglais, then Vln I.

The fugato is protected (c142, c237, c264, c437). No change.

**111–116 wedge (six one-bar chromatic steps).** This is more than three statements, but the unit is one bar long and simple. That is the exception PDF 264 allows for Bach's sequence C. Its 6-5 alteration over the chromatic bass is the Brahms Op. 117/3 procedure (PDF 280). Protected. No change.

I did not find a passage in this lens where a tested change would be a real improvement. Earlier critics have already refined the sequences: c252 at 164, c382 at 115, c402 at 137 and 154. What still separates this score from a master's lies in the protected thematic and proportional choices, not in sequence technique.
