## Findings

The second pass of Appendix A finds no note outside Adler's professional ranges, as cycle 286 also found. I checked each instrument's three lowest and three highest notes, with their bars, against the charts read as images, not against the OCR. So I report no new fixes. The three items below record what I checked closely, and why the score should stay as it is.

### 1. Notes at the edge of the professional range are all prepared and marked (no change)
Six notes come within a semitone or two of an Adler limit: Ob 1 B3 at 117; Bsn 2 B♭1 at 92-96 and 105; B.cl B♭1 at 105 (written C3, the parenthesised extension on adler2 PDF 304); Hn 1 written B5 at 106; Fl 1 A♭6 at 140; harp D♭7 at 57-59. Each is in a register and at a dynamic the instrument manages. The low oboe and bassoon notes are at f or mf, not pp, and the oboe's least controllable low notes at soft dynamics were removed at 42-43 in c306. The flute's A♭6 is ff, where the top register sounds well. The harp's D♭7 has a damping plan from c246 and c336. The bass clarinet's extension is named three times (score staff, part title, part bar 92), so an orchestra that books a low-C instrument is told in time. Adler's footnote on PDF 302 says the professional top notes are "those usually called for in orchestral playing". None of these notes goes past them. Earlier cycles protected all of them (c146, c185, c256, c286, c306). Applied: no.

### 2. Timpani tuning fits Adler's standard four-drum set (no change)
Adler's chart (adler2 PDF 307) and the text (PDF 79: 30", 28", 25" and 23" are "commonly available in all orchestras") give a direct assignment: G2 on the 30", A♭2 on the 28", C3 on the 25", E♭3 on the 23". Every retuning in the movement uses the A♭ drum: A♭→B♭ at 34 (B♭2 first sounds at 40, about 11 s later at crotchet = 126), back at 44 (A♭ next needed at 69); A♭→B at 84 (B2 at 97), back at 98 (A♭ next at 115). B♭2 and B2 are inside the 28" drum's F2-C3 range, so no drum has to swap roles and the player never pedals a drum that is still sounding. The pitches per bar (measured from full.midi) are: 1 C3; 11-12 A♭2; 13 G2; 26-33 G2/C3; 40 B♭2; 41 E♭3; 62-63 E♭3; 69-76 A♭2/E♭3; 80-83 C3/A♭2/G2; 97 B2; 115-122 A♭2/G2; 131-144 C3/G2/A♭2; 158-161 G2/A♭2; 166-181 C3/G2. All fit. Applied: no (c276 and c366 protect the timpani part).

### 3. Terms agree with Adler's four-language list; mixing is acceptable (no change)
Adler's list (adler2 PDF 312) gives "Change C to E" (English) and "Muta in Mi" (Italian, solfège). The score writes "muta A♭ in B♭": Italian syntax with English letter names. It is the usual form in English and American scores, every timpanist reads it, and since c296 it is the same at 34, 44, 84 and 98. Changing it to "Change A♭ to B♭" would gain nothing, and changing it to solfège would be less clear. "étouffez" and "(m.g.)" are the harp's standard French terms. "metà", "div." and "unis." match the list. Applied: no.
