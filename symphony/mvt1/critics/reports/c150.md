## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 182, harp l.v. into movement II: drop the l.v. and let the harp stop with the basses (focus item, decided)
**Problem.** The harp's C2–G2–C3 at 182 is marked l.v. The basses, timpani and Hn 4, which double it, release at the end of the fermata. Only five parts are tied into movement II (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3). The harp is therefore a sixth sustaining part with no notes in movement II. About 11 s after its attack (bar 182 at the MIDI step ♩=30, then movement II bar 1, three beats at ♩=52), movement II's bass moves to A♭ (Cb A♭1, Vc A♭2) while the low harp strings are still ringing G2. Movement II (v44 intro.ily) works around this by asking the harpist, who has rested since the attacca, to damp "movement I's chord" at its bar 2.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 126–127: when the sound passes from one group to another, the parts must progress "as regular as if there were no leap ... specially to chromatic passages in order to avoid false relation". The harp's G2 is the one voice that has no continuation and ends up a semitone from the new bass. PDF 125–126: the parts that carry a phrase across a join are the ones whose last note coincides with the next part's first note; here those are the five tied parts. PDF 127: a diminuendo works by dropping a group, and the untied group (Cb, timpani, Hn 4, harp) dropping together at the barline does exactly that. PDF 21, Axiom II: an instruction that refers back to the previous movement, given in a bar the harpist rests, is not easy writing.
**Answer to cycle 140.** Cycle 140 protected the harp's l.v. because "the last chord is coloured by the harp entering l.v. at 182 over the held strings and horns" (Adler Ex. XV-37: a plucked attack colouring a held sound). That reason concerns the attack at 182, and the fix keeps the attack, the notes, the ppp and the fermata. Ringing across the attacca was not part of cycle 140's reason; that arrived later with movement II's A♭ at its bar 2.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, `codaHarpL`:
`R1*7 | << <c, g, c>1\ppp\fermata { s2. s4_\tweak self-alignment-X #RIGHT -\markup \small \italic "étouffez with Cb." } >> |`
**Relay to movement II:** delete the "étouffez (movement I's chord)" at its bar 2 (intro.ily `introHarpL`). Its bar 1 then sounds exactly what is written: the five tied parts.

### 2. Bars 167–170, the coda's tutti: the tenor line is covered; give it Bsn 1, as at 27–30
**Problem.** At 27–30 the tenor line `c'2 es'2 | d'2 f'2 | es'2 c'2` is played by Bsn 1 and Vc together at ff. At 167–170, the same layout's tenor line (`c'2 es'2 | es'2 f'2 | es'2 c'2 | d'2.`) is in the cellos alone at f. In the same register (G3–F4), Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and the violas play the head ff. Meanwhile Bsn 1 holds G3/A♭3, which Hn 4 and Tbn 2 already play.
**Evidence.** Rimsky's self-criticism in the editor's preface (PDF 18, Golden Cockerel 233): when the brass observe their dynamics, an inner counter-melody in violas and cellos "will hardly be heard". Cyclic-notes §3 asks movement III to reuse the 27–32/167–170 tutti layout and then turn it to C major, so the two statements in movement I should match. PDF 20: correct progression of parts and a melody detached from its setting.
**Answer to the protections (cycles 130 and 139: coda notes and tutti layouts 27–32 and 167–170).** No pitch of the head, the brass, the violins, the cellos or the bass changes. One doubling moves, and it moves to the layout cycle 139 protected at 27–30. G3/A♭3 remain in Hn 4 and Tbn 2.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, `codaBsnOne` 167–170: `c'2\f es'2 | es'2 f'2 | es'2\< c'2 | d'2.->\ff r4\fermata |`. The rest of the line (R1 ×3, `g1\pp` at 174) is unchanged.

### 3. Bars 8→9, introduction: join the cor anglais's answer to the violins' rising-sixth chain on their common G4
**Problem.** The CA answer (5–8) ends on G4 at the barline of 8. The violins' motto chain then starts on the same G4 three beats later (9, beat 4). The two halves of the introduction are separated by a gap on the very pitch that links them. In the coda, the same kind of join is overlapped (Bsn 1 → Tbn 1 G3 at 174/175; Tbn 1 → Hn 3 C4 at 180/181), so the introduction is the one place where the join is not made.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 125–126: "In order to connect the phrases on each instrument in the best possible way, the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one." Ex. 237 (PDF 407) hands a string figure down to the cellos and basses on common notes in the same way.
**Fix (applied).** intro.ily, `introCorAnglais`: `... g'2\< aes'4\> g'4~ | g'1~\pp | g'2.) r4 | R1*5 |`. The CA's G4 sits inside the bar-9 chord (Vln II E♭4, Vla C4, Bsn G3, Vc G3) and stops as Vln I takes the G. Notes of the answer, the motto 1–4 and the E♭/E♮ at 8–9 are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 181–182: mark the bow change for the strings tied across the attacca
**Problem.** Since cycle 130, Vln I, Vln II and Vc tie from 181 through 182 and into movement II. Vln II and Vc hold for about 16 s (4.8 s + 8 s + 3.5 s). Vln I holds for about 23 s, because it continues to movement II bar 3. Only the basses have "change bow at will" (179). Movement II prints it on Vln I at its bar 1, about 13 s into the note.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 21, Axiom II and Glazounov's first class: parts should sound well at first reading. Cycle 116 accepted shorter bows at 177–182 before the ties existed.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily: `c''1~\>^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will"` (Vln I), `g'1~\>^\markup ...` (Vln II), `c1~\>^\markup ...` (Vc), all at 181. The viola is not marked: it changes note (E♭→E♮) at 182 and so changes bow there anyway, which also lets the lone E♮ start on a fresh bow.
