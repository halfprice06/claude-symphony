## Findings

### 1. The 4–3 over V at 66–67 and 164–165 falls on the weak half-bar and resolves on the downbeat (applied)

**Bars:** 66–67 (exposition, end of S before the deceptive cadence) and 164–165 (the same cadence in the recapitulation, C major).

**What is wrong:**
- At 66.3 the bass moves to E♭. Vln I and II take A♭5/A♭4, the 4th above the bass, by step from B♭, so the A♭ enters as an accented passing tone, which is correct (A&S PDF 153).
- The 4th then resolves to G on the downbeat of 67, over the same E♭.
- At 164.3–165.1 the same happens over G, with C6/C5 resolving to B on the downbeat of 165.

The dissonant chord sits on beats 3–4 and the consonance on beat 1, while the bass is repeated from the weak beat to the strong one. That is the pattern Aldwell & Schachter mark "never!" in Ex. 10-6a (PDF 155). The text says: "the cadential 6/4 must appear in a metrically strong position; no weak-to-strong bass repetitions are permitted" (PDF 154, §6), and "The cadential 6/4 occurs on a strong beat relative to the chord of resolution" (PDF 160, review item 3). Unit 9 explains why this matters: repeating the bass from a weak to a strong beat while the chord over it changes "can contradict the meter" (PDF 149, §15, Ex. 9-24a "poor"; PDF 151 item 8). In practice the cadence's downbeat loses its weight. The accent moves to beat 3 of 66 and 164, and the downbeats of 67 and 165, which open the last bar before the cadence, arrive as releases. The same page explains the cure: "Suspensions, by definition, are held over or repeated from a weak to a strong beat; the dissonance produces enough contrast to give an accented quality to the downbeat despite the static bass" (PDF 149, Ex. 9-24b "good because of dissonant suspension"). The book's own examples put the dissonance on the strong beat: Haydn's II6–V8/6/4–7/5/3 (Ex. 10-9a, PDF 156) and the Mozart consequent (Ex. 10-14, PDF 159).

**Protection, answered:** "beat-3 dominant with 4–3 at 66/160" was introduced in c023 and has been protected since c032 (HISTORY lines 117, 159, 260). The stated reason was "bass to V on beat 3 … 4–3 suspension over six-beat dominant before EEC/ESC". The fix keeps that reason intact:
- the bass still moves to V on beat 3 of 66 and 164, so the dominant still lasts six beats;
- the 4th still enters on beat 3;
- the only change is that the 4th is tied over the barline and resolves on the second quaver of 67 and 165.

The downbeat now carries the dissonance, which makes it a true suspension, and the resolution falls on the weaker part of the beat. A&S allow this: "If the 6/4 resolves within a divided beat, the 6/4 will appear on the strong part of the beat and its resolution on a weaker part" (PDF 154). The violins' line becomes a continuous stepwise descent B♭–A♭–G–F–E♭–D♭ (D–C–B–A–G–F in the recap). No pitch of the tune is lost, and the only rhythm change is on beat 1.

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **54.1: the S tune leaps C5→A♭4 onto the 4th of V.** A&S say the 4th "normally enters as a common tone or by stepwise motion" (PDF 153). Here the 6th, C, is already in the tune on 53.3 over the ii6, so the C–A♭ is the 6/4 broken in the melody: the stepwise approach is "masked by figuration" (Ex. 10-3c, PDF 153). The resolution A♭→G falls from beat 1 to beat 2, which is correct (Ex. 10-6b). The S theme (47–55) is protected, and this point does not give a reason to reopen it.
- **174→175: V, then a 6/4 over the same G at the start of the chorale.** PDF 160 item 7 forbids approaching a *cadential* 6/4 from V, and Ex. 10-6a forbids the weak-to-strong repeated bass. 175, however, does not go to V. The motto's bass rises G→E♭ at 176 (i6), so 175 is a tonic 6/4 on the arpeggiated bass. It follows a completed half cadence (N6–V6/4–5/3 at 173–174, which A&S PDF 158 would call a back-relating close), so 175 begins a new phrase. The coda's notes are protected (c228, c229), and the chorale is the motto, so it stays.
- **7→8 (N6 over F to C major) and 178.3→179 (♭II to i).** Both break syntax rule 3, "Intermediate harmonies do not lead to I or I6" (PDF 148). Both, though, are neighbour chords under the motto's D♭→C (bass F→C at 7–8 is a minor-plagal close). They prolong the tonic and do not lead to V. The "motto Neapolitans" and the D♭ thread are protected, and this Phrygian colour is what gives the movement its character. Adding a V at 7.3 would make the intro sound like a textbook exercise. Keep.
- **158 and 142: the 4th (C) is doubled in three octaves in the fff tutti, and at 158–159 Tbn 1 takes C4 up to D4.** PDF 154 allows a doubled 4th "in very free or complex textures". In both bars every upper copy falls to B (Tpt 3, Vla, Ob 2, Hn 3·4, Cl 1 at 159.1), and Tbn 1's D4 gives the V7 its fifth (c169). Keep.
