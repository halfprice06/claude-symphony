## Findings

### 1. Bar 161: the counterline's A♭ leaves by leap to F, and Hn 3 then takes A♮ before reaching G (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 160–161 the counterline (Ob 2, Cl 1 an octave higher, Hn 3) plays `es'4 c''4 r8 c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2) f'2`. Under the A♭ the harmony is iiø7 (bass D). On beat 3 it becomes V7 (bass G), and the A♭ leaps to F, which is already in Vla, Tpt 2, Tbn 2 and Hn 4. At 162 (vi) A♮ sounds in the bass and the tune, and Hn 3 itself goes `\clef bass a1`. Hn 3's line is therefore A♭4–F4–A3: ♭6 replaced by ♮6 before 5̂. In every other voice 5̂ is reached only in the bass.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 388: ♭6 "is strongly active in the direction of 5. Once introduced, it tends to move to 5; it will not normally be replaced by ♮6 before 5 is reached". Ex. 23-2h shows this as the progression to avoid. PDF 395, Point 3: "♭6–♮6–5 is poor". In Mozart's "Madamina" (Ex. 23-6, PDF 391) the borrowed II6/5 after ♭VI goes on to V with its ♭6 resolving. Cycle 152 fixed the same fault three bars later (Hn 3 A♭3–G3 at 164), so the passage is now consistent. Tchaikovsky 6/i 318–324 resolves every ♭6 to 5̂. Hutchinson 29.2 gives the same rule.

**Fix (applied).** Bar 161 only, beats 3–4:
- Ob 2: `aes'2) g'2` (was `f'2`)
- Cl 1: `aes''2) g''2` (was `f''2`)
- Hn 3: `aes'2) g'2` (was `f'2`)

V7 keeps its seventh in Vla, Tpt 2, Tbn 2 and Hn 4. I checked the new motion: Hn 3 against Hn 4 moves contrary into the octave G4/G3; Cl 1 against Cl 2 moves d5→P4; Ob 2 against Ob 1 moves M6→P5. No new parallels. The tune, the bass, the 160 scoring and all dynamics are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 156: the clarinet's A♮ follows the cellos' A♭ across voices and clashes with the violins' D♭ (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 156 (♭VII, poco animando, mp<) the cellos fall A3–A♭3–G3 on beats 3.5–4.5, and the violins turn D6→D♭6 on beat 4.5. Cl 1 rises `r8 f'8( g'8 a'8 |` and then goes to `aes'2)` at 157. The cellos' A♭ on beat 4 is followed half a beat later by A♮ in the clarinet, a chromatic succession split between two voices. On beat 4.5, Cl 1's A♮ sounds against the violins' D♭ (an augmented fifth). The clarinet then returns to A♭ itself. In one beat the line states ♭6, ♮6 and ♭6 again, in two instruments, just as the music turns to iv (F minor) at 157.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a: a chromatic succession "can sound disagreeable if it is split between two voices". PDF 391: mixture works when its tones form one coherent mode-colour. Here the violins' D♭ and the cellos' A♭ already set the minor colour, and the A♮ contradicts it.

**Fix (applied).** Cl 1 at 156: `f'2\mp\< r8 f'8( g'8 bes'8 | aes'2) f'2 |` (A♮ becomes B♭). On beat 4.5 the sound is G3–B♭4–D♭6, one diminished triad that leads into F minor, with B♭→A♭ by step. The new intervals were checked against the violins and cellos: tenths and thirds, no parallels.

### 3. The mixture plan of 147–182 is sound and should stay (no change)

These points were checked against the reading because they are where a mixture fault would most hurt the movement's arc:
- 159–162, V7–♭VI–iiø7–V–vi: Ex. 23-6 exactly, with ♭VI before the plain deceptive vi (PDF 390–391).
- 163–164, IV (A♮) then iiø7 (A♭) then V: ♮6–♭6–5 in Hn 3 and the harp, the order A&S call good (PDF 395) and Hutchinson's "diatonic before borrowed".
- 166, E→E♭ in Vla, Cl 1, Hn 1 and the cello arpeggio: each a direct succession in one voice, "unobjectionable" (PDF 394).
- 171–174, the S head in minor after its major statement at 147–150: K. 311's change of mode on a repetition (PDF 392).
- 182, the viola's lone E♮: a Picardy third (PDF 391), held back to ppp in one voice. It rhymes with the C-major chord at 8 and hands E♮ to movement II.

A&S say the Picardy third "can create effects of startling power". Movement I deliberately does not spend that power; movement III should (see notes below).

## Notes for later movements (for cyclic-notes.md; not written there)

1. **Movement III, final arrival.** Make the C-major arrival a forte Picardy of the Don Giovanni kind (A&S PDF 392, Ex. 23-7): ♭VI – borrowed ii°6/5 (or iiø6/5) – V – I with E♮ in the trumpets. Movement I's major tonics (8, 147, 182) are all pp–mp and never preceded by V7 with ♭6 resolving in the outer voice. The ♭6–5 resolution should be in the top voice, which movement I gives only once (160, tune A♭6–G6).
2. **Movement III, the Brahms device.** Use a deliberate outer-voice E♭/E♮ cross relation once in the chorale (A&S PDF 394–395, Ex. 23-13): for example, the trombones' bass on the minor motto (`g,1 | es1 | d1 | c1`) while the trumpets' top line enters on the major `g'1 | e''1`. Movement I sets its four E♭/E♮ turns only inside a single inner voice, so this sound is unused.
3. **Movement III, mode on repetition.** Reverse movement I's order for the S head (major at 147 → minor at 171): state it first in minor, then repeat it in major (A&S PDF 392–393; Exercise 6, PDF 397, is the model in miniature).
4. **Movement II (A♭).** Its ♭6 is F♭, the same pitch as movement I's last E♮. Let F♭ resolve to E♭ (5̂ of A♭), never to F♮ first (PDF 388). This agrees with movement II's existing E/F♭→E♭ thread (relay c010).
5. **Any movement.** Avoid ♭6→♮6 in one line before 5̂ (fixed in movement I at 161 and 164), and do not split A♭/A♮ between voices in fast inner figures (fixed at 156).
