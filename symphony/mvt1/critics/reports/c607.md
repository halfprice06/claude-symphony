## Findings

### 1. Climax counter-line 160.2–161.2: two winds over two horns at fff (applied)

**Reading.** PDF 75: "If a single horn is used, the upper part is allotted to 2 clarinets, 2 oboes, or 2 flutes. But if there are two horns playing the lower octave in unison, three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages." The page image gives the formulas as 1 Ob + 1 Cl over **1** Horn and 2 Fl + 2 Cl over **2** Horns. The reading's own example, *Tsar Saltan* before [180], puts an Ob + Cl pair an octave above each of two horns (the horns in sixths), so there are four winds over the two horns. Ch. I (PDF 41) gives the reason: "in forte passages two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone". In weight, then, a horn pair at fff is a trumpet, and a trumpet's upper octave needs three or four winds (PDF 75, next rule).

**Score.** The counter-line under the climax is Hn 3·4 a2 at 158–161.2 (G4–C5 | B4–F4 | E♭4–C5–C5–B♭4–A♭4 | A♭4–G4). c407 applied this rule at 158–159 by adding Cl 2 to Ob 1 and Cl 1. On beats 2–4 of 160 and the first half of 161, though, Cl 2 still leaves the counter. It holds E♭5 and then C5 | D5 (c154: so that the woodwinds have the fifth of the A♭ chord). The counter's upper octave there is Ob 1 + Cl 1 alone, the one-horn formula, at the movement's only fff tutti on its strongest beat. c407 knew this and kept it for c154's reason. In those six beats the counter turns from a reed-and-horn line at 158–159 into a horn line with a thin top. That is the line that carries the S head's falling answer (C6–B♭5–A♭5 | A♭5–G5) against the tune's A♭6.

**Fix that keeps c154's reason.** c154 wanted the woodwinds to have the fifth of the A♭ chord, and Adler (adler2 PDF 164) says why: "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs". The fifth can go to Bsn 1. At 160 Bsn 1 only doubles the bass at A♭3 (with Bsn 2 A♭2, Cbsn A♭1, B.tbn, tuba and Cb). Moved to E♭4, it gives the woodwind choir its fifth. It then steps to D4 at 161 (as written, then G3). That puts it in unison with Tpt 3's E♭4–D4, the motto-tail line c530 recorded, which is a natural Tpt + Fag pair (PDF 74–75, rule 3). Cl 2 can then stay on the counter for all of 160–161, a2 with Cl 1.

### 2. The fugato's third entry, 91–93: every line a string–wind octave pair (keep)

The scan confirms what c407 found: at 91–93 the subject (Vln II C5–A5 over C.a. C4–A4) and the countersubject (Vla F4–C5 over Bsn 1 F3–C4) are both string-over-wind octave pairs, the case the editor's note (PDF 78) advises against. I tested two other layouts on paper. In the first, the C.a. moves up to unison with Vln II. That puts it at concert C5–A5 (written G5–E6), the top of the instrument and its thinnest sound, at f against a full string section. In the second, Cl 1 joins Vln II at the unison. That gives the formula of rule 14 in reverse and uses up the fresh clarinet colour c264 saves for the ff restatement at 96. Neither answers c307's and c407's reasons: Vln II stays on top and clear (rule 9), Bsn 1 fills the empty octave between the bass and the cellos, and the fugato's colours must stay fixed (Adler adler2 PDF 176). No change.

### 3. Retransition stretto 127–130: the entries' doubling (keep)

The stretto is Cl 1 alone (127–128, p), the violas alone (129, mf), then Vln II with Bsn 1 two octaves below (130, f), over the cellos' accelerating G and the G pedal. Bar 130 is another case of the editor's note (string and wind alone in octaves). Here it works the way the note says the classics used it, "to obtain balance of tone". The bassoon's G2–E♭3–D3 sits in the bass register under Vln II's G4–E♭5–D5 and gives the last entry a low root as the crescendo goes into the ff recapitulation at 131. The low P head at 131 then takes the same register in Vc + Cl + Bsn + Tbn (rule 19). The passage is protected (127–130, c473, c517). No change.

### 4. Where the texture still falls short of a master's (no further change)

Apart from Finding 1, I looked for an inner voice that should move, a missing counter-melody, and a weak bass.

- **Inner voices.** The static stretches (43–46, 47–55, 147–155, 171–174) are protected pads (c447), and each has a moving line of its own. At 47–55 the harp moves. At 147–152 the C.a. answers on each long note of the tune (147.3–4, 149.3–4, 151.3–4). At 171–174 the solo cello and the C.a. form a duet. In the recapitulation the C.a. fills land exactly where the exposition's harp moved, so the second statement gets a new counter instead of a copy. That is the procedure the reading's rule 11 gives for a doubling: "a new timbre of definite colour".
- **The fugato bass 91–96** (c597's open note). The bass is a minim harmonic bass in Cb + B.cl + Bsn 2: Cb + B.cl is a register-matched pair (PDF 77), and it lies under four real voices. A moving bass would make a fifth strand against three entries plus the cellos' free voice, and the subject would lose its clarity. Keep.
- **Counter-melodies** are where c507 listed them, and each is doubled with a pair the chapter names. Finding 1 was the one place where the doubling fell below what the chapter asks for, and it was at the movement's loudest moment.
