## Overall verdict

Against Schumann 4/i, this movement would pass a conductor's reading as a late-Romantic professional's work, and in two respects (the soft attacca join and the scoring variety of returning material) it is more polished than the model. It lacks Schumann's single continuous motor rhythm, but that is a difference of style, not a fault (the movement's models are Brahms and Tchaikovsky). Measured against the assigned reading, every brass, combined-group and tutti layout I checked follows Rimsky-Korsakov. Nothing has got worse since v239. I recommend no change.

## Findings

### 1. Cyclic design compared with Schumann (keep)
Schumann 4 is the model of a cyclic symphony played without breaks. Its first movement turns the slow introduction's figure into the Lebhaft's main theme, and its development introduces a new theme that becomes the finale's (BH score PDF 18–21, the wind flourishes answered by sf brass-and-string chords with fermatas, then the new lyrical idea). Movement I here does the first thing already: the P head (r8 g' es''4. d''8 des'' c'') is the motto G–E♭–D–(D♭)–C at speed, as Schumann's Lebhaft is his introduction at speed. It does not do the second: the development has no new theme. I recommend keeping it so. The finale already has its own themes, and c230 found and recorded the seeds movement I gives the later movements (79, 106, 108, 12, 37–44, 73–76). A new development theme would mean a new idea in a 54-bar development that is already sectional (c239 Finding 1), and the finale would need rewriting to take it up. Rimsky PDF 116 asks whether each passage is a complement, a contrast, a climax or a step; the development's six blocks each have one of those roles. Not applied.

### 2. Recurrences are scored differently, as Rimsky ch. IV asks (keep)
Rimsky PDF 118 lists five operations for re-scoring a returning idea (octave transfer, new key, added octaves, changed details, changed dynamics) and shows them in Ex. 176–203, and Ex. 205–208 show one tutti scored two ways. Schumann's practice is the opposite: his returns are mostly doubled at the unison in strings and wind with the same layout each time (BH PDF 3–5, 24–27). Here every return is changed:
- Motto: B.cl + Vc (1), C.a. (5), Fl 1 (73), Hn 1·2 + Tbn 1·2 + Bsn 1 with B.tbn below (117), trombone chorale (175), Vln I (177).
- P tutti: head in Vln I·II, Vla and wood-wind with brass hammering at 27; head low with violins above at 131; head in Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and Vla over tremolo strings at 167 (Rimsky PDF 113: tremolo strings under brass is the sanctioned string-brass combination).
- S: Hn + Vc (47), violins (56), ff in B minor (97), Hn + Va (147), three-octave tutti (158–161).
The c089 note that 27–32 and 167–170 are "alike on purpose" refers to the harmonic layout, which the finale quotes; the melody's scoring differs, which is exactly Rimsky's operation d). Keep.

### 3. Figure and ground at 167 and 69–72 (keep)
Rimsky PDF 115–116: when the tune is in the brass, the harmony belongs to the wood-wind, and the difference between tune and basis is made by dynamics. At 167 the head is ff in Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and violas, with wood-wind harmony (Fl E♭6/C6, Ob G5/E♭5) and sustained brass at f. At 69–72 Tpt 1's S head (f) sits over wood-wind and horn harmony. Both follow the rule. I considered raising the wood-wind at 167 to ff to match 27–32 (Rimsky PDF 112: wood-wind one degree louder than brass), but at 167 the wood-wind is harmony under a brass tune, not tune, so PDF 116 governs and the present f is right. HISTORY.md protects "coda 167–182 (notes, dynamics, fermatas, tempo)" and "graded brass ... 167"; this finding agrees with that protection. Not applied.

### 4. Trombone economy compared with Schumann (keep)
Schumann keeps his three trombones silent through the introduction and exposition of 4/i and brings them in for the development's sf chord blocks (BH PDF 18–21), so their entry is an event. Here the trombones play from bar 12 (mf, German sixth) and in every tutti. I do not recommend Schumann's restraint. The late-Romantic models score trombones into every full tutti (Rimsky PDF 119–120: a full tutti is all three groups), and the movement already saves two trombone events: the ff motto at 117–121 (brass tune, wood-wind harmony, Rimsky PDF 116) and the pp chorale at 175–179 (Rimsky Ex. 136 PDF 312 and PDF 105: three trombones "soft and sweet" as harmonic basis; Ex. 156 PDF 330: flutes and trombones pp). At 12 the trombones are graded one level below the wood-wind (protected "graded brass at 12"), and the bass doubling there is by Bsn 2 and Hn 4, as Rimsky PDF 101 asks. Keep.

### 5. Discords in the brass at 122 and 170 (keep)
Rimsky PDF 102: discords of the second or seventh go preferably to instruments of different colour. At 122 the A♭ over G is Tbn 1 A♭3 against Hn 4 G3 and B.tbn G2; the nearest clash (A♭3–G3) is trombone against horn. At 170 the B♮ is in Hn 1·2 (B3) and Tpt 1·2 (B4) against the C in B.tbn and tuba. Both follow the rule. Keep.

### 6. Linking technique compared with Schumann (keep)
Schumann builds his crescendos over held wind and brass chords while the strings run (BH PDF 24–26, into letter G). Rimsky PDF 108 gives the same rule: stationary parts to the brass, moving parts to the wood-wind. The retransition 117–130 does this: Hn 3·4 hold G4/G3 (Rimsky PDF 104: sustained octaves to two horns), Cl 2 and violas carry the triplet arpeggios, the motto is in the brass and the rising figure in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and violins. Keep (protected 117–130).

### 7. Ending and attacca compared with Schumann (keep)
Schumann ends 4/i loud in D major and starts the Romanze after a fermata. Movement I here ends ppp on C with five parts tied into the Adagio, which is a closer join than Schumann's and keeps the forte C-major arrival for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). Rimsky PDF 120: tutti belongs to f and ff; the last eight bars rightly use no tutti. Keep.
