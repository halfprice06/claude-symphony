## Findings

I checked every progression in 1–182 against the table and found no violation of the reading that is both audible and outside a protected passage. The five items below are ranked. Each is a place where the score departs from the book's normal rule, with the decision for each. No change is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. Bar 159→160: Tpt 3 leading tone B3 leaps up to E♭4 at the deceptive ♭VI (keep)

Problem: at the deceptive move V7 → ♭VI, Tpt 3 has the leading tone B3 and leaps a diminished fourth up to E♭4 instead of rising to C4.

Evidence: A&S PDF 201–203 (Ex. 13-12) say that after V(7) → VI, 7̂ goes to 8̂ and the 3rd of VI is doubled. PDF 165–166 say the same for VI–V in minor. The score does double the 3rd C in the strings and woodwinds, and the viola carries the real resolution B3→C4, as does the melody. So the rule is kept in the voices that define the progression, and the trumpet's B is a doubling. A&S treat a doubled leading tone as free to leave the line when another voice resolves it (PDF 165, where an inner voice gives way to avoid the A2).

Decision: keep. Tpt 3 B3→C4 would give the trumpets the B→C that coherence/mvt1.md reserves for the finale. HISTORY also protects the E♭ at 160 in Tpt 3 (accepted at cycle 241) and the held brass at the 160 climax (c244). E♭ is the 5th of ♭VI, which the brass chord needs at ff under the fff tune.

### 2. Bar 82→83: Tpt 3 E♭4 falls a diminished fourth to B3 at VI → V♭9 (keep)

Problem: the inner trumpet moves to the leading tone by a diminished fourth.

Evidence: PDF 165–166 (Ex. 11-5) ask only that ♭6̂ not reach ♮7̂ by an A2. Here no voice moves A♭→B: the C is doubled and the A♭ stays or falls to G. A falling diminished fourth onto the leading tone is a standard dissonant leap in an inner voice, and it is not one of the faults in Ex. 11-10d/e.

Decision: keep. HISTORY protects the brass layout at 82 (c244).

### 3. Bar 160→161: bass tritone A♭→D, ♭VI to vii°6/5 at fff (keep)

Problem: the bass leaps an augmented fourth.

Evidence: PDF 163–164 and 172 describe VI moving on to a pre-dominant by a falling third. Here ♭VI goes instead to a diminished seventh over D that prolongs V (V7 arrives at 161.3). A&S allow the °7 over 2̂ as a dominant substitute (PDF 186, Ex. 12-14). The leap stays inside one dissonant chord and resolves to G by step in the following voice.

Decision: keep. The passage is protected ("♭VI–°7–V climax at 160–161"), and the reading gives no reason to override it.

### 4. Bars 10 and 172: the 7th of iv7 (E♭5) enters by leap (keep)

Problem: the seventh of the subdominant seventh is not prepared in its own voice.

Evidence: PDF 177 asks for preparation by a common tone or 8–7 but allows a leap in free, instrumental textures. At 172 the cello holds E♭4 in 171, so the seventh is prepared an octave lower. At 10 the leap belongs to the motto head. Both sevenths resolve down by step (to D at 11 and in 173), which is the requirement A&S treat as firm. c242 kept bar 10 on the same grounds.

Decision: keep. The 9–13 chain is protected.

### 5. Bar 49→50: the 7th of ii7 (A♭4, Vln II) rises to B♭4 while the melody takes over A♭4 and resolves it to G4 (keep)

Problem: in its own voice, the seventh of ii7 moves up.

Evidence: PDF 182–183 (Ex. 12-8): the seventh of II7 is held into the dominant and falls there (4–3). In the score, the pitch A♭4 is held into bar 50 and falls to G4 on beat 3. The horn and cello melody sounds it while Vln II moves to the 5th of V. This transfer of the resolution to another voice is a texture A&S accept in free writing (PDF 177). Moving Vln II down to G4 would only double the melody in unison and take the 5th out of the V7.

Decision: keep. The melody head here is protected.

No other finding: the reading's main points of risk are the A2 at VI–V in minor, a doubled bass in IV6–V, unprepared or unresolved II7 sevenths, and a deceptive cadence that never reaches an authentic one. The score handles all of them correctly at every occurrence.
