### Mechanical check: entries after any rest

c508's script checked only entries after full-bar rests. I wrote a second one (/tmp/critic-608/scripts/scan.py). It walks every brass variable in the nine section files, tracks the last printed level and any open hairpin, and lists every note that follows a rest of a crotchet or more and carries no dynamic. It found 47 such places. I checked each one in the source and on the page. They fall into five groups, and none needs a mark:

- **Rests inside a figure** at its own level. Examples are the trumpets' crotchet–quaver pulse at 28–29 and 132–133, the strokes at 83.3, the head fragments at 80–81, and the B-minor counterline at 99 and 104–105. Adler's tutti trumpet examples (X-32, X-33, X-38, PDF 313, 315) mark the level once per figure.
- **Entries inside a written "cresc." or hairpin** that sets the level. Examples are Hn 3 at 61, Hn 4 at 113 and 116, Tbn 1 at 116 and tuba at 115.
- **Re-attacks after a breath at the passage's level.** Examples are the motto at 120–121 (ff) and the tuba at 123.
- **Part-only or tagged levels** that the script strips: Hn 4 mp at 67 and Tpt 2 ff at 83.
- **Parser misses.** These are levels printed after a `\clef` or on a rest, at 11, 19 and 82.

The one place I looked at most closely was the tuba at 123. It breathes after the fff dotted minim at 122 and restrikes G1 with a hairpin but no level. That restrike is the first moment of the pedal's decay. Bass trombone, contrabassoon and double basses hold the same G at fff into 123 with no break, so the tuba re-enters at the level around it. It is not a fresh accent inside a decay, as 124 and 163 were before c408 and c508. Adler's held-brass examples give the level where the decay starts (X-21 ff>p, X-40 ff ... >p, PDF 308, 316). Here the start level is the fff printed at 122. No change.

## Considered and kept (no change)

1. **A swell on the rising sixth of the horn call at 13 and 125 (es4\mp\< c'2).** Tchaikovsky's horn solo (X-24, PDF 309) puts a hairpin in nearly every bar, and Brahms (X-8, PDF 304) puts <f> on each phrase. The call is one beat of E♭3 rising to a held C4 that sighs to B3. Its expression is the > from C to B, which Ravel's "p>pp expressif" (X-11, PDF 305) also marks. A < over a single crotchet pickup would only tell the player to do what a rising sixth already does. The call is quoted by the other movements, and c221 and c308 protect its notes and dynamics. Not an improvement. Keep.
2. **Trombones f on the low P theme at 131–134 while bassoons and cellos play the same line ff.** Read against X-43 (PDF 317), this looks like the leading line marked below its doublings. But two trombones a2 in the middle register G3–F4 are "very strong" (X-69, PDF 329). At f they match the bassoons and cellos at ff; at ff they would cover the cellos and bassoons and compete with the ff upper line in flutes, oboes and violins. c144 decided this, and c264 and c291 kept it (c308 brought the bass trombone and tuba down to f to match). The accents on the theme's long notes mark it as the leading line, and the held brass carry no accents. Keep.
3. **Subito contrasts.** Mahler's fff then p (X-13, PDF 305) and Beethoven's crescendo into p dolce (X-9, PDF 305) are both present already: f to pp at 72→73, sub. p at 62 in the horn pad, and the graded fff collapse 122–126 (protected). Nothing to add.
4. **Stopped or muted horn, fourth look.** Adler gives single notes, soft passages and sf>p as its uses (PDF 308). I checked the soft brass places not looked at before: Hn 4's A♭ pedal under the flute motto at 73–76 and the Hn 3·4 legato line at 103–106. Each is a held or legato line that needs the open "accompaniment choir" sound (PDF 311–312), and a stopped colour would need a break inside a held note. At 76 the darkening to A♭ minor is already carried by the F♭ in the harmony. c508's note stands: in the models, stopped horn appears loud at a climax (Tchaikovsky 6/iv), not in a first movement's soft accompaniment. Keep.
5. **The loud fermata at 170 has no word like Wagner's "sehr stark ausgehalten" (X-25, PDF 310).** The chord is ff with accents in every brass part, under a fermata, followed by a silent beat 4. The word would repeat what the marks say. The stop chord is protected and quoted. Keep.
6. **The codetta phrase 69–72 (Tpt 1 with Ob 1, flutes and violins) at plain f with no hairpin.** Tchaikovsky's cornet tune (X-64, PDF 325) shapes mf < f > dim. This is a tutti codetta that ends on three accented cadence strokes at 72. All its doublings carry the same flat f (clos.ily, Vln I, Vln II, violas). The accents give the shape, and the f is kept below the ff of the A♭ climaxes. c187 protects the codetta doublings. Keep.
7. **Trumpets at the coda head 167–169: Tpt 1·2 a2 ff with Hn 1·2 ff, while Tpt 3 and the trombones hold f< into the ff stop chord.** This matches the Bruckner and Mahler grading (X-43, X-44, PDF 317) and Adler's warning that the trumpet dominates (PDF 316). Keep.
