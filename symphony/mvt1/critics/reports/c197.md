## Findings

### 1. Fugato 88–90: the second entry is covered by the countersubject above it (applied)

- **Bars:** 88–91.
- **Problem:** The subject's second entry (Vla + Bsn 1, G3–E♭4–D–D♭–C, mf) lies under the countersubject. The cellos play that countersubject at C4–G4 (88) and E♭4–F4 (89), on the A string, also mf. The cellos cross a fourth to a sixth above the violas. The entries at 85, 91 and 94 are each the top voice or doubled in octaves. This is the one entry that is not.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 42 says of the Hindemith fugato that the instruments are placed "so that the subject comes through clearly every time it is played". In Ex. XI-23 (PDF 43, mm. 170 and 178) the entering voices are marked mf and the voices that continue are marked p. In Bartók's fugato (PDF 45–47) every entry carries its own "f, ben marc.". Rimsky PDF 54 lists "crossing of parts (violoncellos above the violas)" as a way to make a line stand out, so here the crossing favours the countersubject over the subject. The corpus agrees: in Tchaikovsky 6/i 171–175 each string entry has its own dynamic.
- **Fix (dev.ily, devVc):** `c'4.\mp d'8 es'8( f'8) g'4 | es'2( f'8 es'8 d'8 es'8) | aes2\< g2 | c'2.\f es'4~( |`. The cellos drop one level under the entry, then join the basses' existing crescendo at 90 (Cb `mf\<`) and reach f with everyone else at 91. No notes change. The protected subject, countersubject, entries, entry colours and marcato articulation are all untouched; only the cello's dynamic changes.

### 2. Bars 162–163: move the single wind doubling to the tune's lower octave (applied; closes c187's optional item)

- **Problem:** After 161 the tune runs in octaves, Vln I A5 over Vln II A4. Fl 1 doubles the upper octave, which needs no help. The lower octave is one violin section among ff brass at E4–C5 (Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2, Tbn).
- **Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 78: when only one of two octave parts is doubled, double the lower one. Adler PDF 44: two horns in unison equal one trumpet or one trombone. The ff brass around A4 therefore weigh several trombones against one violin section. c187 tested this change. The protected reason for 158–166 (diminuendo by drop-out, c179) still holds: one wind leaves at 162 and one at 164, as before.
- **Fix (recTwo.ily):** Fl 1 `f'''2 d'''4 b''8 c'''8) | \partCombineAutomatic R1*5 |` (Fl 1·2 now end together at 161). Ob 1 `f''2 d''4 b'8 c''8) \breathe | a'2.~(\ff a'8 gis'8) | a'4.\>( g'8 f'2)\! | R1*3 |`. Ob 2 rests from 162, so the shared staff has no dynamic conflict.

### 3. Decisions on open texture items in HISTORY.md (Dan's rule: no item left for the composer)

- **c104, Cl 2 on the tenor line at 27–32: closed, no change.** c104 found the "block" effect came from rhythm, with twelve held notes. Since then the trumpets play the timpani rhythm at 27–30 (and 131–134, c174), which gives the brass the punctuation that Adler PDF 42 calls their "most common use". The tutti layout of 27–32 and 167–170 has been reaffirmed since (c110, c137).
- **c147, clarinets rest at 131–134: closed, no change.** The low theme is carried by Tbn 1·2 a2 at f against Hn 3·4 ff held on C4/G4 in the same register. By Adler PDF 44, two horns weigh as much as one trombone. Removing four woodwind voices from the theme would tip the balance toward the held horns.
- **c147, Bsn 1 with the horn counter at 69–70: closed, no change.** The low brass now rest at 69–71 (c161), so the "very rare" Bsn + Tbn unison is gone. The horn counter is already doubled by Cl a2, Vln II and Vla. Moving Bsn 1 would remove the held chord tone and add nothing to the counter.
- **c161, no fill at 150: closed, no change.** The recapitulation is plainer on purpose (cycle 82). The cor anglais answers already supply the dialogue.
- **c167, crowded middle register at 56–68: closed, no change.** The counterline (Cl 1 + Hn 2) is the only moving layer, and it is marked p espr. over a pp horn pad and pp viola dyads. That is the mf-over-p layering of Ex. XI-23 (PDF 43), so it can be heard.
- **c169, Fl 1 low pickups at 117–119: closed, no change.** Vln I and the lower octave (Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II) carry the subject. The flute's B4–E♭5 pickups at ff are inaudible but do no harm. Cutting them would leave Fl 1 entering on each accent and gain nothing that can be heard.
- **c173, Vln II chord register at 107–108: closed, no change.** 107–110 is double counterpoint: the second theme is on top (Vla, G4–C5) and the first-theme head in the bass (B.cl, B2–G3). Any pad has to sit between them. The proposed `<g b>1 | <g b>4( <a dis'>2.)` would double the B.cl's accented G3 at 107 and put A3 against its G3–F♯3 at 108, which moves the clash into the bass melody. The pad is sustained pp against a moving p line, the rhythmic contrast Adler accepts (adler2 PDF 274).
- **c174, Tpt 3/Tbn 1 held E♭4 at 167–169: closed, no change.** The seconds against the horns' lower octave of the head are passing notes against a chord tone. This is the held-line-against-motion relation of Ex. XI-21 (PDF 40–41). Tpt 1·2 carry the head's upper octave, so the head is not obscured.
- **c186, harp f at 162 under the ff tutti: closed, no change.** It is the first of the chain 162–166, with one rolled chord per harmony on beat 1. The later chords emerge as the brass fade (mp by the end of 162, out by 164). Delaying it would break the protected design and gain one chord that would still be covered.
- **c187, Fl 1/Ob 1 at 162–163:** applied (Finding 2).
