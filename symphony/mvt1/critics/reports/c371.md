# Critic 371: Melody and memorability (v371)

Lens: the motto, P, S and the horn call, their contour, rhythm, climaxes and development.
Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations, pedal point (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371), second pass. Book page = PDF page + 2.

Method. I read all 26 text pages in full, including the Points for Review (PDF 369) and the exercises (PDF 370–371). I rendered every page at 110 dpi and looked at each image (Ex. 21-1 to 21-42, the exercises), because the OCR loses the notes and figures. I read c281 (this lens, same reading) and c318 (expression, same reading) so as not to repeat them. I then checked v371's full.midi with three scripts written for this cycle (in /tmp/critic-371/scripts): `susp2.py` finds every note held across a bass change into a 2nd, 4th, 7th or 9th that then falls by step, and lists any other upper part that sounds the tone of resolution before it resolves; `suspup.py` does the same for upward resolutions; `appog.py` does the same for every dissonant note of a crotchet or longer in the melodic parts (Vln I, Vc, Hn 1, C.a., Fl 1, Cl 1, Ob 1, Tbn 1), whether tied, restruck or leapt to. `chords.py` prints the sounding pitches beat by beat. The scripts found one place that earlier critics did not treat: bar 44.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 44: the horn call's third statement is a 7–6 suspension sounded against its own resolution (applied)

**Bars.** 43–44, Hn 1 `r2 es'4( c''4~ | c''2\> bes'2)`, the horn call's rising sixth E♭4–C5 echoed over the dominant of A♭ (c271: the S head is prepared by 13–14 and 41–44). Under it the violas hold B♭3 in 43 and restrike it on the downbeat of 44 (`bes1\pp | bes1 |`).

**What is wrong.** At 44 the bass moves to D♭ (V4/2 of A♭). The held C5 becomes a major seventh above the bass, a 7–6 suspension that resolves to B♭4 on beat 3. The sounding pitches at 44.1 are D♭2 (Cb), G3 (Vc), B♭3 (Vla), E♭4 (Vln II, Hn 2), C5 (Hn 1). The tone of resolution, B♭, sounds from the downbeat, a ninth below the suspension, for the two beats the suspension lasts. In the next bar the line continues as an indirect suspension: Cl 1 restrikes B♭4 over the C bass and resolves it 7–6 to A♭4. c281 found the same fault in that second link (the cellos' A♭3 sounding under Cl 1's B♭4) and fixed it with a 5–6 in the cellos. It did not report the first link. Its script tested dissonance at the moment a note starts, and Hn 1's C5 starts at 43.4 over E♭7, where it is not yet a seventh above the bass. So 44 still has the fault that 45 no longer has.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 352, §12, Ex. 21-12c: a 7–6 with the 6 already present in another voice is marked "poor". Sounding the tone of resolution early "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The texture at 44 is the same as the one c281 judged at 45: a sustained pp string pad under one soft wind line, so the contrast exception on the same page does not apply here either. The pair 44–45 is the reading's 7–6 series over a descending bass, D♭–C (PDF 354–355, §16, Ex. 21-16), carried by two instruments as an indirect suspension (PDF 357, §17, Ex. 21-22: "more characteristic of instrumental than of vocal style"). Both links should resolve cleanly. The movement already treats the horn call this way at 13: c190 removed the violas' doubling of C–B so that the B♮ sounds only when the horn reaches it.

The point bears on the lens. 43–44 is the last statement of the horn call before the S head enters at 46.4. The call's character is the long C and its fall by a step. At 44 that fall should be the one thing that changes, and with the violas already on B♭ it changes nothing.

**Fix (trans.ily, transVla, bar 44):** `r2 bes2 |` (was `bes1 |`). The violas stop at the end of 43 and restrike the same B♭3 on beat 3 with the horn's B♭4. The pitch and the harmony are unchanged. Beats 1–2 of 44 are V4/2 with no fifth: D♭–G–E♭ with C suspended, the 7/6 that delays the 5th, as in the J. Strauss example (PDF 353–354, Ex. 21-15f). The fifth then arrives in two octaves at once on beat 3. The violas' line is now B♭3 (43) – rest – B♭3 (44.3) – C4 (45). At 45 they move B♭→C against Cl 1's restruck B♭4, which is oblique motion.

**Protection.** c163, c236 and c281 protect "lead-in 43–47" and "transition route 33–46". The route, the bass, the keys, V4/2–I6 at 44–45, every note of the horn, clarinet and cellos, and the S pickup are unchanged. Only one restrike in an inner part moves by two beats. That is less than c281 changed at 45, and it follows the same rule.

### 2. Bars 62–63: the S peak is the movement's one true melodic suspension, and it is right (no change)

The second theme's dissonances are mostly appoggiaturas: A♭ over E♭7 at 50, 54 and 59, reached by leap or by step, never prepared. Its peak is different. The C6 is prepared at 62.2 as the 6 of the cadential 6/4 and tied over the barline. At 63 it becomes the 7/6 over V7, which the reading calls "a beautiful suspension resolving into V7" (PDF 353–354, §15, Ex. 21-15f). It resolves with a decoration, C6–D♭6–B♭5 (PDF 359, §20, Ex. 21-26), as the bass turns to E♮. Hn 4 holds the delayed fifth, B♭3, from 63.1, two octaves and a step below, and the harp's B♭2 enters at 63.1½. These are pad notes in another choir, two octaves and more below the violins' line. That is the marked contrast of register and timbre that the reading allows (PDF 352), not the homogeneous case of Finding 1. Taking the B♭ out of Hn 4 would empty the V7 under the peak. Keep it. The short D♭6 is protected (c221, c301).

### 3. Bars 177–182: the last motto turns the motto's D♭ into a suspension with a moving bass (no change)

Vln I's final motto, G4 | E♭5 (tied) | E♭5 D5 | C5 D♭5 | C5, runs two bars after the trombone chorale's G3 | E♭4 | D4 | C4 D♭4 | C4. At 178.3 the chorale bass moves to D♭ under the violins' tied E♭5, so the E♭ becomes a 9. At 179 the bass returns to C and the E♭ becomes a 10. This is the reading's suspension released by the motion of the bass, 9–(10) (PDF 353, §14, Ex. 21-14b), and it is the reason the canon works: the motto's chromatic neighbour D♭ produces the dissonance in the bass, under the held top note. The D5 at 179.4 is a passing note over the C pedal. Its resolution C is in the bass octaves (Tbn 1, Vc, B.cl), which is the 9–8 case (PDF 352, Ex. 21-12d). The earlier link in the coda is also clean: Cl 1's 7–6 C5–B4 at 177 has no B sounding before beat 3 (`chords.py`). No change. The coda notes, levels and timing are protected (c228, c249, c369).

### 4. The themes judged against the reading and the lens (no change)

- **Motto.** This is the most memorable element. The rising minor sixth is followed by a long held note and a chromatic sigh. In the cor anglais form (5–8) the sigh is A♭–G, and the G is a dissonant anticipation of bar 8's chord, entered by step and tied to its main note (PDF 364, Ex. 21-35).
- **Horn call.** It has three notes and uses two of the reading's devices: delayed resolution (13–14, PDF 359–360) and transferred resolution (B3 → bass C at 15, PDF 360–361). Since Finding 1, its two suspended statements (13–14 and 43–44) let the falling step sound by itself. At 41–42 the four horns' C4 over E♭ is a consonant 6–5 (PDF 348: a consonant suspension may move either way).
- **P.** P is motivic rather than a tune, like the first theme of Brahms 1/i. It is the motto in diminution. It is remembered by its rhythm: the accented long note on 1-and is rhythmically strong and metrically weak (PDF 362–363, Ex. 21-32), and the C quaver anticipates every downbeat (PDF 363). The finale quotes it. Keep it.
- **S.** This is the tune a listener would hum. In each phrase one long note leans and then gives way (50, 54, 59), and the peak is a real suspension (Finding 2). Its contour is of the Dvořák and Brahms type: a long 3̂, a fall, a rising sixth, a fall, then a rising arpeggio into the 4–3. The tied 4–3s at 66→67 and 164→165 put the dissonance on the downbeat, as PDF 347 asks.

I found nothing in the reading that would make a theme more memorable by changing its notes. The theme heads are protected and quoted (c429, c474). I do not reopen 159 (E6–F6 rising, c281), 59 (Vc G3, c107/c281) or 45 (c281).
