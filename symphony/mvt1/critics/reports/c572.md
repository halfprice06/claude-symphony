### 1. Bar 141.3: iiø7 between the N6 and V, where the reading's model has an applied vii°7 over ♯4 (keep)

In the recapitulated P (140–142) the bass goes D♭ (♭II, 140) – F (N6, 141.1) – D (141.3) – G (142). The chord at 141.3 is D–F–A♭–C, iiø7 in root position. A&S PDF 484 (Ex. 28-2d, 28-2e) shows the usual expansion as N6 – vii°7/V – V over a rising chromatic bass F–F♯–G. The book says the rising bass against a falling chromatic soprano makes "an effective contrast" (Mozart K. 397, bar 52 ff.). The question is whether 141.3 should be F♯ with vii°7 of V (F♯–A–C–E♭).

Why keep it. The tune at 141.3 is F5 in Ob 1, Cl 1 and both violins (F6 in Fl 1), the P theme's own descent A♭–G–F–D–C. An F♯ in the bass under that F would be a cross-relation clash on the beat, not a passing colour. Replacing the theme's F with E♭ to fit vii°7/V would change the P theme at its recapitulation, which other movements quote. The present bass already sets ♭2 against ♮2: D♭ leaves the inner voices at 141.1–141.2 and D arrives in the bass at 141.3. PDF 484 says the cross relation "does not create a bad effect even in immediate succession. You may use it freely." Fl 1's D6 at 141.4 then puts ♮2 at the top just before V, the contrast of PDF 485 (Ex. 28-6). The iiø7 is also the diatonic chord an augmented sixth grows from (PDF 509, Ex. 29-15, Haydn: "II forms the diatonic preparation… the II intensified chromatically"). Bars 140–143 are protected (c239, c282). Recommendation: keep.

### 2. Bar 174.3: ♮2 is not in the top voice of V after the coda's N6 (keep)

At 173.3–174 the coda has iv – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V: the C.a. holds D♭5 (♭2) for two beats, falls to C5 on the 6/4, and rests on the V. The solo cello takes C5 to B4 over Bsn G2/G3 and viola E♭4–D4. A&S PDF 485 (Ex. 28-6, Mendelssohn Op. 102/4) recommends putting ♮2 in the soprano of V "to contrast ♭2 and ♮2". The C.a. could rise C5–D5 on 174.3.

Why keep it. The C.a. line 171–174 is the motto at the fifth (G4–E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5), and its last note is the C. A D5 after it would add a seventh note to the motto, and the motto is in the theme record. As written, 173–174 is already the other textbook form on PDF 484, Ex. 28-2c: the diminished third ♭2–1–♮7 filled over 6/4–5/3. It is split between the two soloists, D♭5–C5 in the C.a. and C5–B4 in the solo cello. ♮2 sounds in the viola's D4, an inner voice, which is the normal place for it. The book's rule that ♭2 must not go straight to ♮2 in the same voice (PDF 484) is met: nobody moves D♭→D. Bars 174–175 are protected (c463). Recommendation: keep.

### 3. The recapitulation and coda have no augmented sixth (keep)

Augmented-sixth sounds occur only at 12, 105.3, 109.4 and 116, all in the first half. A&S PDF 503 says augmented sixths "often occur just before important structural points", PDF 508 names IV as their most frequent preparation, and PDF 513 adds that they "frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI". So I asked whether the recapitulation's cadences (142, 158, 164–166, 174) are missing a colour a master would use. Bars 157 (c542, c553) and 160 (c282, c372, c482) have been tested before, so I looked at the one place not yet examined: 162–164, after the deceptive V–vi at 161–162.

Why keep it. In C major the deceptive chord at 162 is A minor, not ♭VI. Adding ♯4 to it (A–C–E–F♯) does not make an augmented sixth, because the chord needs ♭6 in the bass, and in major that comes only through mixture (PDF 505, Ex. 29-6). The mixture chord is already at 164.1: iiø7 over D with Hn 3's A♭3. Turning it into a French sixth (A♭ bass, F♯) would need F♯ against Hn 1's held F4. That F4 is the delayed 4–3 of 164–165, which is protected, and the bass D–G at 164 is part of the protected 158–166 cadence chain. More generally, the German sixth at 12 is the finale's first chord at the same pitch (IV 1). If the recapitulation keeps to the diatonic parent chords (ii, iiø7: PDF 509 calls the augmented sixth "a chromaticized" form of them), that chord stays tied to the introduction and to the finale's opening. Recommendation: keep.
