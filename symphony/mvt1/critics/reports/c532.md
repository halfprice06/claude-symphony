## Findings

I checked every progression in 1-182 against the table, using the beat-by-beat reduction, and re-ran the scans earlier critics used (vl.txt: one P5 entry, unchanged since earlier versions; analysis.txt: only the known bar 96 octaves). No progression breaks the reading in a way a listener would hear outside a protected passage. This is the fourth pass, and c442 already discussed bars 149, 53/153, 61 and 158-166, so I looked at places that report did not treat. Each item below ends in "keep", with the reason from the reading. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. Bar 10: the 7th of iv7 enters by leap and is dropped at 10.4. Keep, and correct the c332 record

What I found: at 10.1 Vln I leaps G4 → E♭5 (the motto's rising sixth). The E♭5 is the 7th of iv7 (F-A♭-C-E♭) and is the only E♭ in the chord. At 10.4 Vln I leaps down to A♭4 and the chord becomes a plain iv. The step resolution D arrives at 11.1, in other voices only (Cb D2, Bn 2 D3, Cl 2 and Hn 2 D4). HISTORY.md (c332) says the "iv7 sevenths by leap at 10 and 172 resolve down". That is true at 172 (C.a. E♭5 → D5 over the held F) but not at 10.

Reading: A&S PDF 177 says the 7th of II7/IV7 resolves "downward and by step", and "except in rather free or complex textures, the 7th will not enter by leap". PDF 188-189 (Ex. 12-17, 12-18) show 3̂ in the soprano over IV as the commonest layout, falling to 2̂.

Why keep: bars 9-13 are protected (chain of rising sixths G-E♭, A♭-F, c242 and later). The leap is the motto interval, and an orchestral texture of this kind is the "free" texture A&S exempt. The 7th does reach its resolution pitch class on the next downbeat, in the bass and the horn and clarinet middle register. A step resolution in Vln I would mean E♭5-D5 at 10.4-11.1 in place of A♭4-F5, which would break the second rising sixth. That costs more than it gains. The one change I recommend is to the record: HISTORY.md should say "10: iv7 seventh left by leap, D supplied by the bass at 11 (free treatment, A&S PDF 177)".

### 2. Bars 51-53 and 151-153: V6/5 of IV → IV → IV add 6 (ii6). Keep

What I checked: in the exposition, 51 is A♭7 over C, spelled with G♭ in Vln II (tTwo.ily line 121, `ges'1`) and the harp. The G♭4 falls to F4 at 52.1, so the applied 7th resolves down by step. The leading tone C rises to D♭ in the bass. In the recapitulation, 151 is C7 over E. Vln II's B♭5 falls to A5 at 152, and the bass E rises to F. Both phrases then run IV → IV with an added 6th (ii6) → V → I. The roots fall in 5ths and 3rds, as PDF 172 requires. The two statements of S thus differ in the second half: 49 has ii7, 149 has the lighter ii (c442 kept this), and 51/151 have matching applied chords.

Why keep: this is the progression PDF 183-185 describe (II7 or II6 extended within the subdominant; Ex. 12-11, 12-12). The 7ths of the applied chords resolve correctly. The S chain 47-56-97-147-158 and "S diatonic 47-55/147-155" are protected.

### 3. Bars 59-62: V7 → ♭VII maj7 → iv6 over F♭ → cadential 6/4. Keep

What I checked: at 59 the dominant 7th of A♭ moves at 60 to G♭-B♭-D♭-F (♭VII with a major 7th) over G♭2. The 7th D♭ of V7 is held (Hn 3 D♭4), B♭ is held, and G falls to G♭. At 61 the bass falls G♭ → F♭ under D♭-A♭ (iv6 in A♭, Phrygian approach). At 62 the bass reaches E♭ under the cadential 6/4. The bass E♭-G♭-F♭-E♭ circles the dominant. 

Reading: A&S PDF 167-168 (Ex. 11-8) explain the half-step bass into V as the strongest approach. PDF 201 says that a chord other than I after V7 prevents "a cadential effect too early in the passage" (Ex. 13-11b). Here the evaded V7 at 59-60 delays the cadence, and the Phrygian F♭-E♭ then states it with more force.

Why keep: this is the most vivid harmonic event in the second group, and it seeds movement II's F♭-E♭ thread (coherence/mvt1.md, 60-62). Nothing in it breaks the reading. c442 checked the F♭ doubling at 61.

### 4. Bars 100-102 (B minor): iiø7 → V7 with suspended 4th → cadential 6/4 → V. Keep

What I checked: 100 is C♯-E-G-B (iiø7 in root position, written D♭ in the MIDI names). The 7th B is held into 101, where it becomes the suspended 4th over F♯ (V7sus). It stays at 102.1 as the 4th of the cadential 6/4 (F♯-B-D) and falls to A♯ at 102.3.

Reading: A&S PDF 182 and Ex. 12-8 say the 7th of II7 "is repeated in the same voice to become the 4th of the 6/4 chord before resolving down to the 3rd of V". Here the delay is one stage longer (through a V7sus). That is the Schubert D. 804 model with one more step, and it is correct.

Why keep: it follows the reading, and the record from c413 ("iv-ii7-V7-cad6/4 ending on a half cadence") is accurate. 97-102 are protected.

### 5. Bars 104.4-105 and 109-110: deceptive V7 → iv6, and iv → cadential 6/4. Keep

What I checked: at 104.4 A-C♯-E-G (V7 of D minor) moves to B♭-D-G at 105 (iv6 in D minor). The leading tone C♯ (Hn 3) rises to D. The 7th G is held as a consonance in Vc/Bn 1 G3 and Hn 1 (A4 → G4). At 109 B♭ minor (iv of F minor) goes to C-F-A♭ at 110 (cadential 6/4). The 6th and 4th fall to G and E in Vla (A♭5 → G5) and Vln II (F4 → E4), and F minor follows at 111.

Reading: PDF 204-205 (Ex. 13-15): IV6 can replace VI at a deceptive cadence and turns the 7th of V7 into a consonance. PDF 167 (Ex. 11-7b): doubling the 6th of the cadential 6/4 after VI/iv avoids octaves. At 110 C is quadrupled (Cb, Bn 2, Vc, Hn 3, Vln II) and A♭ is single. That follows the reading.

Why keep: both are textbook. "109.4-110" and "110 crescendo through cadential 6/4" are protected.

### 6. The master-level question: does anything in my lens fall short?

I looked for a harmonic moment a listener would not remember, or a colour that could be stronger. The places where the harmony is thinnest are 33, 80-81, 84, 96 and 127-129, which use two-note sonorities. In each case the thinness is planned: the stop-and-restart texture after the tutti (33), the empty C minor before the 82 climax (80-81), the timpani G alone before the sub. p (84), the fugato close (96), and the retransition over the G pedal (127-129). All of them are protected for form or texture reasons. In the progressions that the reading covers, the score uses each device it teaches, and uses the strongest ones at the right places:
- the evaded V7 and Phrygian approach in the second group (59-62);
- two deceptive-cadence pairs, each resolved by an authentic cadence (64-72, 158-166, PDF 203-204);
- II7 sevenths held into the cadential 6/4 at every main cadence (PDF 182);
- a Phrygian plagal ending after the authentic cadences (PDF 197-198).

The two devices the movement does not use, I-IV6-I6 with the disjunct bass (PDF 199-200) and IV6 passing within V (PDF 200-201), are classical tonic-expansion formulas. They would weaken the minor-mode tension wherever they could go: the P sentence 15-22 and the C-major recap 147-155, both protected. I did not find a change that meets the bar of a real improvement, so I recommend none.
