## Findings

Ranked by how much each bears on the four-movement design. None is a score change. I looked for a change that would make movement I set up the later movements better and tested two candidates (Findings 3 and 4). Neither meets the bar.

### 1. Changes since the last cyclic review (v601 → v610) touch nothing another movement quotes. Keep

Adler's two colour lessons apply across the symphony. The slow movement's solo colour must be "very fresh, even after the first movement has been heard" (PDF 263). In Mozart's concerto "the solo flute will not encounter any color competition" because no orchestral instrument of its kind plays (PDF 267). So movement I must not use up what later movements present as new, and it must keep the material they quote at pitch.

### 2. The climax at 160 after c607 still follows the Bizet and Mozart rules. Keep

Bizet doubles the singer with the first violins an octave higher "only when the singer reaches her dramatic climax" (PDF 279). Movement I does that once, at 158–161, and makes 160 the movement's highest point (A♭6, fff, with 122). c607 added Cl 2's C6 and B♭5–A♭5 at 160. The C6 is an octave under the finale's C7 and sits inside the woodwind counter (Ob 1, Cl 1·2), not on top of the tune. It adds weight to the counter and changes neither the top note nor the colour that the finale saves (strings and flutes at C7 with the trumpets' B→C, IV 223). Movement I's three climaxes still land on A♭, II's key, and none confirms C major.

Fix: none.

### 3. Candidate tested: take Hn 3 off the unison under Hn 1's recall at 125.3–126. Rejected

Adler PDF 264: "Mozart never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn is playing ... so as not to interfere with the solo sound", while a doubling in "such a vast color contrast" as the first violins does not weaken the solo. At 125–126 (c581) Hn 1 recalls the horn call E♭3–C4–B3. From its C4 it sits at unison with Hn 3 (C4 from 125.1, B3 from 126.2), Cl 2, violas and cellos (full.midi). The score already applies Adler's rule at 104–105, where c347 rests Hn 3 on Hn 1's attacks. So I tested the same treatment here: Hn 3 keeps its resolution C4 on 125.1–2 and rests from 125.3, so the line has one horn plus contrasting colours.

I reject it for two reasons:
- c589 rejected the same change because the held C4 binds 124–126. I checked this reason on the MIDI. Cl 2, violas and cellos hold the C4 there too, so that reason alone would not decide it.
- The gain cannot be heard. Hn 1 enters on C4 at 125.3 just as Hn 3 would leave it. The same pitch in the same colour goes on without a break, so the listener still hears one horn sound on C4. Removing Hn 3 would make the recall no more distinct and would move a protected soft horn chord (c567). That fails the brief's test of a real improvement.

c594 records the remaining point: the recall is heard as an inner voice, not an event. Only a louder level or a wider register could change that. Both were rejected for stated reasons (c588: level agreement on a doubled line; c594: mf would compete with Ob 1/Vln I above).

Fix: none. Hn 3 at 125–126 stays as written.

### 4. Candidate examined: establish the timpani pulse before 179. Rejected

The reading's Bartók example (PDF 270–271) keeps "quietly pulsating timpani" under a whole passage to set its mood, and in Beethoven's concerto the timpani carry the joining motive (PDF 273). Movement I gives the soft pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4` once (179–180). The scherzo (III 9, 363–371) and the finale (IV 9, 143, 178, 229) build on it. One could put it under the solo cello at 171–174 as well. I reject this. The hammered form of the same rhythm at 27, 30 and 131 already prepares it, so 179 is a known figure heard softly, which is how the scherzo's closing timpani use it. A pulse under 171–174 would also break the Beethoven VC iii model of row 13, one long-note line against one active line over simple harmony. And c574 protects the single pulse.

Fix: none.

### 5. Where the movement still falls short in this lens

The two weakest points for the cyclic design are the ones recorded before. The horn call has no statement in the recapitulation: c580's reason holds, since a minor call at 144–146 clashes with the C-major arrival at 147 and a major one spends the finale's IV 243–246. And the recall at 125–126 is an inner voice (Finding 3). I found nothing in this reading that answers those reasons. The rest of the arc follows the reading: the soft, bare opening (PDF 269), the motto and horn call each given first to one timbre (PDF 257), the reserved colours (PDF 263, 267), the climax doubling saved for 158–161 (PDF 279), and the attacca chord that hands movement II its first pitch (PDF 275).

Fix: none.
