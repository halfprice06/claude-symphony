## Findings

### 1. Bars 56–61: the cello arpeggio and the basses are as loud as the violins' tune, and the cellos miss the crescendo at 60 (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 56 the violins restate S in unison, p, "molto cantabile", on the D string. The cellos play the broken-chord accompaniment at p in their tenor register (A♭2–E4, with a held E4 half note on beats 3–4). The basses hold the roots arco at p. The tune and its harmonic basis are therefore at the same level. The only background part marked below the tune is the Hn 3·4 pad (pp). The violas were also taken down to pp at 56–59 in an earlier cycle and now rest.

The exposition sets the model at 47. There the tune is mf/mp and the background is pp (Vln II metà, violas, pizz. basses), with only the harp at p. The recapitulation does the same at 147: tune mf/mp, Vln II, basses and Bsn 1 at pp. Bar 56 is the only lyrical statement of S whose string accompaniment is level with the tune.

Casanelles explains that a mix decides which source is heard first, even when the softer one is played louder (PDF 290). An acoustic score can do this only through its marks. Rimsky-Korsakov is specific: when the tune and the basis are of similar timbre, the basis "should be of inferior dynamic power" (rimsky-principles PDF 133). That applies directly here, because violins over cellos and basses are one string group.

The same bars have a second fault. At 60 the violins go mf<, and violas, Bsn 2, Hn 3·4, harp and basses all crescendo into the sub. p at 62, but the cello part has no mark. The reading notes that an expressive crescendo has to be asked for in every part that carries it (PDF 149–150). The cellos open each beat-1 group on the bass note (G♭2 at 60, F♭2 at 61), in unison with the basses. A flat cello under a crescendo in its own bass notes weakens the build into 62. Bar 58 has a smaller problem. The cello's motto in diminution (B♭2–F3–D♭4, C4–C♭4–B♭3, c067) has a swell, and its diminuendo has no end level (PDF 152).

**Fix (tTwo.ily):**
```
Vc:  \clef bass aes,8\pp( es aes c' e'2) | aes,8( des f aes des'2) |
     bes,8(\p\< f des'4.\>) c'8( ces'8 bes8\pp) | es,8( bes, des g bes2) |
     ges,8(\p\< des ges bes) ges,8( bes, ges es) | ...            % sub. p at 62 unchanged
Cb:  aes,,1\pp^"arco" | aes,,1 | bes,,2 des,2 | es,1 | ges,1\p\< | ...
```
The cello's motto at 58 rises to p and is heard against the pp arpeggio around it. At 60 cellos and basses start p<, the level at which violas and Bsn 2 enter there, so the whole string group grows into 62 together. Bsn 2 (p< at 60, c168) again matches the basses it doubles. Notes, slurs, the harp and the counterline are unchanged. The protections on 56–68 (c107 cello/harp accompaniment, c585 Cb sustain under the arpeggios, c273/c563 violins 56–63, c338 phrasing 47–63) concern notes, figures and phrasing, and all of them are kept. Applied in the scratch copy.

### 2. Bar 155.4: the violins' pickup to the recapitulated S is not slurred into the rising sixth (applied)

**What is wrong.** Every other statement of the S head puts the pickup and the arrival of the rising sixth under one slur. The examples are Hn 1/Vc at 46.4→47, the violins at 55.4→56 (sul D, so the sixth is also on one string, c321), the B-minor ff statement at 97.4→98 and Vla/Vc at 146.4→147. At 155.4 Vln I and II play `f''4\mf` (Vln II `f'4`) as a separate stroke, and the slur starts on the D6 of 156. This is the statement that leads into the climax at 158. Here the sixth F5→D6 is broken by a bow change, so its two notes are articulated apart.

Casanelles makes this interval the centre of the reading. The legato arc is a breath that turns pitches into a line (PDF 151). He also says that how a player connects a theme's first two notes "is almost an open door to the performer's inner feelings" (PDF 151, Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto). The lyrical string legato he describes moves "frictionlessly into a gentle swell on the note of arrival" (PDF 155). The score already writes that swell (`d'''2.~\<`), but the connection into it is missing. Adler adds the bowing point: in string parts a slur is a bowing, and an anacrusis is naturally up-bow (adler1 PDF 38). Slurring the pickup into 156 makes 155.4–156 one up-bow, and Adler gives the up-bow as the natural stroke for a crescendo (PDF 38).

**Fix (recTwo.ily):**
```
Vln I:   r2 r4 f''4\mf( | \recTwoMelody |        with recTwoMelodyB: d'''2.~\< d'''8 des'''8) | c'''2( bes''4 aes''8 g''8) | ...
Vln II:  <c'' e''>2:32 r4 f'4\mf(^\markup {"unis." \italic "ord."} | d''2.~\< d''8 des''8) | ...
```
The bow is five beats at about crotchet = 116 (2.6 s), mf<. Bar 157 then falls on a down-bow, and the marked down-bow at 158 needs a retake after 157's last quaver G5. 158 repeats that G as an accented ff downbeat, which is the double down-bow case Adler describes for a solid ff attack (PDF 38, Ex. II-20). The separate bows of 158–160 (protected) are unchanged. The protection on the "violins' approach 156–157" (c381) concerns its notes and length, and both stay as they were. Applied in the scratch copy.

### Considered and kept

- **C.a. motto 170.4–173 without swells, p under the solo cello's mp.** The two lines arrive on E♭ together at 171. Swells in both would make the duet one shape. The plain legato of the second voice is the accompanying legato of PDF 155. Coda levels are protected (c228, c249, c543).
- **Vln I's last motto 177–182 without a swell on the E♭5.** It is in canon with the pp trombone motto. c258 kept equal weight, and the coda's levels are protected.
- **C.a. fills at p inside the Vla/Vc swells at 147–153.** They lie above the tune in register and fall in its held notes. The C.a. answers are protected (c369).
- **No marked portamento** (principle 13), for example on the C6→A♭6 leap at 160 or the solo cello's sixth at 172. The models leave portamento to the players, and an editorial line would date the style.
- **Tempo plan.** Each tempo word has its `\mt` step. There is no "a tempo" after the largamente (c328), and there is no tempo word at 73 (c199). The S statement at 97 at the development's tempo is normal for a ff restatement inside a development. No change.
