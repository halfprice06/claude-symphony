# Critic 146 — playability (woodwinds), v165

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 6 "The Woodwind Choir" and ch. 7 "Individual Woodwinds" (adler1 PDF 168–232). I read every text page in the range. I rendered all 65 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one. I also re-rendered the oboe and cor anglais register charts (PDF 198 and 205) at 300 dpi, because the OCR loses their pitch boundaries.

## Method

I built a check score (scratch only) with one MIDI track per wind and brass player, without part-combining and with `\removeWithTag #'part`. I then measured three things:
- each player's range;
- every soft note in the lowest fifth of oboe and bassoon;
- every stretch of continuous sound, in seconds, using the `\mt` tempo map.

Ranges: Fl 1 B♭4–A♭6; Fl 2 G4–A♭6; Ob 1 B3–D6; Ob 2 E♭4–B5; CA B3–E♭5 (concert); Cl 1 G3–C6; Cl 2 G3–B5; B.cl. B♭1–B3; Bsn 1 C2–F4; Bsn 2 B♭1–F4; Cbsn C1–F2 (sounding). All are within Adler's ranges (PDF 168, 182, 188, 193, 200, 209, 214). The findings below come from the breath measurements.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 147–155: Bsn 2 sustains the pp bass for 18 s, then plays almost to the end of the movement (applied)
**Problem.** Bsn 2 holds whole notes `c1 f1 d1 g,1 e1 f1 d1 g1 c2` from 147 to 155 at pp with no rest: 8½ bars at ♩=112, 18.2 s. It is the only sustained bass under the Cb pizzicato, so the player cannot drop a note. From 156 the same player continues through the climax (fff at 160) and the coda (167–174). The busiest 60-second window for Bsn 2 starts at 147 and has 56 s of sound, the highest load of any woodwind. Bsn 1 rests for all of 147–155.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: the flute "requires a great deal of breath… give the flutist time to rest after especially difficult or sustained passages". In Ex. VII-9 (Brahms 1/iv) the second flute takes the held note so the first can breathe, "dovetailed with great care to preserve the illusion of a single" player. PDF 192: multiple players "relieve one another in long, fast, or sustained passages". PDF 175: double-reed players often need to exhale, which is another reason the long pp hold is a problem. The exposition already gives Bsn 2 rests at 62 and 68 (cycle 26).

**Fix (applied).** Hand the line over at the phrase join 150/151. The notes and scoring do not change, so the protected 147–155 accompaniment stays the same.
```
recTwoBsnOne = { \partCombineSoloI c1\pp^\markup \bold "1." | f1 | d1 | g,1 | \partCombineAutomatic R1*5 |  f,1\mp\< ...
recTwoBsnTwo = { R1*4 | e1\pp | f1 | d1 | g1 | c2 r2 |  bes,1\mp\< ...
```
The engraving shows "1." at 147 and "2." at 151.

### 2. Bars 9–12: both bassoons crescendo pp→f for 18–19 s at Lento without a breath (applied)
**Problem.** At ♩=50, Bsn 1 (`g1 aes1 aes1 c'2.`) sounds for 19.2 s and Bsn 2 (`c1 f1 d1 aes,2.`) for 18.0 s. The crescendo puts the f, where the air demand is greatest, at the end.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188 and 175, as in finding 1. Bsn 1 repeats A♭ across 10/11, which is the natural breath point. Vc and Cb double both lines (Vc `g1 aes1 aes1`, Cb `c,1 f,1 d,1`), so a comma leaves no hole. Cycle 87's rule holds: the line keeps sounding in another player.

**Fix (applied).** Breath marks only. Notes 9–13 are protected and stay unchanged.
```
introBsnOne = { R1*8 | g1\pp\< | aes1 \breathe | aes1 | c'2.\f c'4 | R1*2 | }
introBsnTwo = { R1*8 | c1\pp\< | f1 \breathe | d1 | aes,2.\f r4 | ... }
```
One comma prints on the shared staff after bar 10.

### 3. Bars 111–118: contrabassoon plays 15 s of crescendo, mp→ff, with no rest (applied)
**Problem.** Cbsn plays `f,1 e,1 es,1 d,1 des,1 c,1 g,,1~ g,,2.` from 111 to 118 beat 3, 14.8 s. Bsn 2 and B.cl. play the same line and both breathe at the end of 113 (`es2. r4`). The contrabassoon does not breathe.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 230: in the low register "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests". Staggering the breaths follows cycle 87.

**Fix (applied).** dev.ily, bar 115: `des,1` → `des,2. r4`. At that point Bsn 2, B.cl., Vc and Cb all hold D♭/C, and the cresc. spanner continues through the rest. The cbsn's later G-pedal breaths (120, 123, 126) are unchanged.

### 4. Score: the clarinet staff does not say which clarinet (applied)
**Problem.** The C score labels the staff "Clarinets 1·2". Copyists and conductors cannot tell whether the parts are for B♭ or A clarinets. That decision sets the written ranges and the key of the B-minor block (97–102).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 171: for clarinets, which "can change their interval of transposition from work to work… the interval of transposition must be clearly marked in the score and in the individual parts". PDF 174 and 210: flat keys call for B♭ clarinets, which suits C minor.

**Fix (applied).** score.ly: `instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Clarinets 1·2" "in B♭" }`. Notes stay at concert pitch. The layout is unchanged (17 pages, same breaks). The bass clarinet is a fixed B♭ instrument (Adler PDF 171 excludes it), so it needs no label.

### 5. Bar 40: Oboe 1 doubles the flute on a high D6 and then drops a ninth into a sixteenth run (applied; optional)
**Problem.** Ob 1 plays `d'''2->` (D6) in unison with Fl 1, then after a sixteenth rest jumps down to `c''16` and runs an octave below the flute. D6 lies in the oboe's "thin but clear" band. The flute already carries that pitch, so the oboe adds only intonation risk and a leap of a ninth at ♩=126.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 198: the most effective oboe range is "from the F in the first space to the C above the staff"; C6–F6 "are quite hard to control". PDF 200: the oboe can play almost any run "if the passage is not based on extraordinarily large skips".

**Fix (applied).** `| d''2-> r16 c''16( d'' es'' f'' es'' f'' fis'') |`. The oboe now sits an octave under the flute for the whole bar, as it does in its run, and moves by step into it. The flute keeps the imitation at pitch, so the protected half-bar chain 37–40 is unchanged. If the composer wants the oboe's edge on the answer's top note, reject this one; it is the least important of the five.

### Checked and not raised
- Fl 1 at 158–163 (13.6 s, fff, top register): the slurs break at 159/160 and 161/162. The climax scoring is protected.
- Cl 1 at 96–102 (12.9 s of tongued eighths): playable. Optionally, `aes''8-. r8` on beat 4 of 96 would give a breath before the arpeggios.
- B.cl. at 91–126: breath points every 3–4 bars.
- Bass clarinet low C at 92 and 105: the instrument is labelled; see table, row 17.
