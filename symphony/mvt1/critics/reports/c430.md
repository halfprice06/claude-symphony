## Findings

No finding needs a note to change. This is the third pass of this reading under this lens. c230 and c330 already recorded the main structural links: the key cycle as the motto's sixth, the wedge bass as the motto tail, the ♭VI at 160, and the common-tone join into II. I re-judged them against the reading and found nothing that has got worse in v422–v430. Findings 1 and 2 are cross-movement links that mvt1.md does not record yet. Finding 3 is the verification and the series-4 record line. Finding 4 lists the changes I considered and rejected.

### 1. Bars 77–80 rehearse the symphony's joins at their own pitches. Keep; record the III→IV link.

The reading's model for a common-tone modulation is Widmung (Ex. 32-3, image, PDF 586–587). It moves A♭ → E (F♭), keeps A♭/G♯ in the voice on top, and has an inner-voice inflection C→B (C–C♭). Those are the keys of the II→III join. Movement I plays the same move inside its development:

- 78.3: the bass goes A♭1 → E2, and Cl 1 holds A♭5 into the E-major beat, spelled G♯5 (full.midi: Cl 1 A♭5 at 78.3). Inside, Vln II steps E♭4 → E4 on the same beat (78.1–2 E♭4, 78.3 E4), and the C♭ of A♭ minor is kept as B (violas B3 at 78). The common tone is in the soprano and the inflection is in an inner voice, as on PDF 587. mvt1.md already records the G♯5 as a seed of the scherzo.
- 79→80: the bass goes E2 → C2 (E minor → C minor), and Vln II goes E4 → E♭4 at one pitch (Vln II E4 at 79, E♭4 at 80). This is the step the finale uses to leave the scherzo. The scherzo's last E4 (harp harmonic) is answered "at the same pitch by the finale's opening E♭4 (violas, Vln II, Cl 2, Hn 2)" (finale.md, Opening). Movement I already plays this E4→E♭4 in Vln II, at the same pitch, with the bass moving down a major third from E.
- 81→82: C minor → A♭ with C and E♭ held, the I→II key move.

So 77–82 holds the four-movement key cycle (A&S PDF 599–600, the Appassionata's major thirds), and the pitches of two of its joins as well. mvt1.md line 243 lists Vln II 77–80 only as part of the E♭/E thread, and line 225 gives only the bass. Neither names the III→IV link.

A second, related point. The B.cl at 107 plays G3–F♯3–F3–E3 in E minor (107.2–107.4.5), the P head transposed to E. These are the cello's pitches at 78–79, the scherzo's theme A bar 2 one octave below the violins. The scherzo's theme A is therefore heard twice in movement I in its own key. A fresh hint is not needed (see Finding 4c).

Fix: none in the score (77–83 is protected). Record lines for mvt1.md's seeds list:
- "79→80: Vln II E4→E♭4 over the bass E→C, the E-to-C-minor step at the pitch where the finale's opening E♭4 answers the scherzo's last E4."
- "107: B.cl G3–F♯3–F3–E3 in E minor, the 78–79 cello pitch."

Not applied, because the records are outside my write area.

### 2. The development's stations spell the stop chord, and the finale resolves that chord. Keep; record it.

The development settles on B minor (97, key signature), D minor (103), F minor (111) and the A♭ upper neighbour over the G pedal (122). That is B–D–F–A♭, the stop chord of 26, 138 and 170, spread over 26 bars, in minor thirds, landing on V. A&S PDF 598–599 (Haydn Op. 54/1, Ex. 32-18, image) show this kind of minor-third motion ending on the dominant. PDF 600 says key areas "project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece". The finale's arrival at 222→223 is that same chord (bass D, Tpt 1 B4) resolved to C major, which mvt1.md calls the first strong C major of the symphony. So the movement's large plan, its signature chord and the finale's goal are one object. c382's Do-not-touch "B–D–F–A♭ plan" protects the score here, but the cross-movement meaning is not in mvt1.md.

Fix: none in the score. Record line for mvt1.md's seeds: "97–122: development stations B (97) – D (103) – F (111) – A♭ over G (122), the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ spread over the development onto V; the finale's 222→223 resolves the same chord to C major." Not applied.

### 4. Considered and rejected

a. **Darken ♭VI at 160 to A♭ minor, as K. 542 turns G major to G minor (PDF 601).** Rejected. A♭ minor is already used where the reading's Appassionata model puts it: as the darkening of the second key going into the development (73–77; PDF 596, 599). Movement II needs A♭ major to arrive unspent. The climax harmony 158–166 is protected, and the reason given there still holds.

b. **Make the first E♮/E♭ confrontation abrupt (Hammerklavier, PDF 584–585).** Rejected. The reading also allows gentle inflections. E♮→E♭ is the falling, less intense kind (PDF 585), and movement I keeps it soft at 8→9, 79→80 and 182→II. The forceful version belongs to the finale: the Haydn footnote's "chaos to light" (PDF 597) happens at IV 222→223, and mvt1.md requires that movement I never confirm C major at forte. The one loud E→E♭, 166→167, darkens rather than brightens, which suits a first movement that withholds the light.

c. **Add hints of the finale's F1 or the scherzo's themes.** Rejected. The reading asks for "organic connection" (PDF 602), and the links that exist are organic: the scherzo's theme A is the P head in E (78–79, 107); its horn call is movement I's 13–14; F1's opening chord is I 12; F2's key colour is first heard at I 37. A planted pre-echo would weaken those links and could use up something the finale reserves (c330 gave the same reason).
