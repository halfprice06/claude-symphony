## Findings

The movement follows every principle of the assigned reading that bears on its form. I found nothing that stands between this score and a master's first movement strongly enough to justify a change to protected music, and almost every bar is now under a stated protection. The findings below record what I checked against the reading, ranked by how much each would matter if it were wrong; each ends with a verdict. No change is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. The movement's last tonic arrives by contrapuntal cadences only (175–182). Stays as it is.

What the reading says: "A stepwise bass motion to I — from VII6, V6, V6/5 or V4/3 — produces a much weaker articulation ... Such cadences are virtually never used at the end of a piece" (A&S 8 §11, PDF 129). The coda's consequent phrase reaches i at 178 over the motto bass D2→C2 (Vln II F4→E♭4, Vla A♭3→G3, Cl 1 B4 left hanging), then ♭II–i at 178–181. The last root-position V on the beat before a tonic is at 165 (→166, p, C major); the last one in C minor is 142→143 (ff, Fl 1 C6, a perfect authentic cadence).

Why it stays:
- A&S's rule concerns the end of a piece. Movement I does not end: five parts tie into movement II's first bar, attacca. The page still has the closure A&S asks for: the structural cadences 143 and 166 are root-position authentic cadences, and what follows is the "closing tonic" expanded (Ex. 6-4, PDF 95) by a bass arpeggiation of the tonic, G–E♭–(D)–C, which is the procedure of Ex. 7-8b (Brahms 3/ii, I–I6 down a sixth, PDF 110).
- The timpani pulse at 179–180 (C3, G2, C3) gives the 5̂–1̂ bass motion of an authentic cadence after the contrapuntal arrival.
- Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i ends with a plagal cadence over a tonic pedal (MCTL cadence.csv, PC at onset 1362, offset 1410), not with V–I.
- The only way to give 177 a root-position V is a G under the motto bass (for instance Cbsn `g,,1\pp` at 177 under the Cb D2). That turns the motto's D into the fifth of V, makes 177 a V♭9 instead of the protected i6 – V4/3 – i (c262) and touches the coda notes protected by c543, c559 and c585. c223 raised the same point and kept it; I add the A&S PDF 129 reason and still keep it.

### 2. The introduction ends on V7, not V (Vln II F4 at 14). Stays as it is.

A&S say V7 "is rarely used in a semicadence" and that when the melody comes to rest on 7̂, V "will generally produce a better effect than V7, which conveys an inappropriate feeling of activity" (6 §10, PDF 99–100). At 14 the horn call comes to rest on B3 (7̂) over G2–G3–D4–F4. The F4 is Vln II's G4→F4, a V8–7 (Ex. 6-18, PDF 101). The F rises to G4 at Vln II's entry at 15.2.5, while E♭ arrives in Vln I at 15.2.

It stays because 13–14 is not a semicadence that closes a paragraph. It is the dominant preparation of the Allegro, and here the "activity" that A&S avoid in a closed phrase is exactly what this bar has to do. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i's introduction holds V6/5 for its last three and a half bars into the Allegro (MCTL harmony.csv, onsets 56–70.5: V65 in E minor). The alternative (`g'1\p\>~ | g'2.\pp r4` in introVlnTwo, a bare V triad; not built, since I do not recommend it) would weaken the pull into 15 and leave the B3 in the horn as the only active tone. Not applied.

### 3. Large-scale I–V–I and the dominant section 117–130. Follows; keep.

Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95) shows the four functions the movement needs: an expanded opening tonic, a semicadence, an expanded V that "helps to shape the form", and an authentic cadence. The movement has all four on the large scale: i expanded over C in 1–9, HC 13–14; C – A♭ (VI, 47) – F (iv, 111) – G (117–130) – C (131). The dominant section is 14 bars (29 s) in root position over a G pedal (A&S 7 §8, PDF 112: a section-long expansion needs root position). Inside it, the i6/4 chords at 118, 121 and 125 are neighbour chords to V, the "I" of Ex. 8-14 (Jupiter 19–21, PDF 129–130), not tonic arrivals. The recapitulation then arrives by root-position V→i over the barline 130→131. Against the corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i's retransition dominant lasts 26 of its 144 development bars (MCTL HC 1110–1214), 18%; here it is 14 of 54 bars, 26%, a stronger dominant weighting suited to a shorter development.

### 4. Harmonic rhythm at phrase joints (weak–strong repetition). Follows; keep.

A&S: "Avoid repeating a chord from a weak to a strong beat" (6 §16, PDF 102); moving between positions of one chord weak→strong "creates an unintended syncopation" (7 §13, Ex. 7-18a, PDF 115–116), unless motivic grouping justifies it (Ex. 6-21, 7-18c). Every section and phrase joint changes harmony on the downbeat. There are four exceptions, and each is justified:
- 21.3→22.1: V held over the barline under the P tune's E♭5–D–C descent; the downbeat carries the ♭6 appoggiatura, which is the melodic accent of Ex. 7-18c. Protected (c221).
- 66.3→67.1: the cadential 6/4 tied over the bar; settled with A&S Ex. 10-6a by c232.
- 152→153: IV becomes ii6/5 by a 5–6 over F, the same correction c163 made at 52→53 in the exposition.
- 174.3→175.1: V to a tonic 6/4 over the held G, with the new phrase, the tam-tam and the chorale entering on the downbeat. 175 then works as the top of a descending tonic arpeggio (row 14), not as a second cadential 6/4.

### 5. Cadence hierarchy of the two groups. Follows; keep.

A&S rank perfect authentic cadences above imperfect ones and keep the most final for the end (6 §3, Ex. 6-3, PDF 94–95). Exposition S: tune on 3̂ at 55 (an imperfect cadence), deflections to vi at 64 and 68, then a perfect authentic cadence at 72 with A♭6 on top, after a whole bar of V7 (broadening, PDF 94). Recapitulation S: 3̂ at 155, deceptive cadences at 160 (♭VI) and 162 (vi), then 1̂ at 166. In both groups the weaker cadence comes first and the strongest last, and the recapitulation varies the deflections where the exposition repeats one. The two F-minor deflections at 64 and 68 are the one place where the exposition is less varied than the recapitulation. They sit inside the protected 56–68 (c226, c327), and A&S Units 6–8 give no reason to reopen them.

### 6. Transition goal and medial caesura (37–47). Follows; keep.

The transition reaches the dominant of A♭ as a tonicised E♭ (B♭7–E♭ at 40→41) and holds it in root position over an E♭ pedal at 41–43 (PDF 112). It then turns it into the A♭ approach: V7 at 43, V4/2–I6 at 44–45 (the commonest use of V4/2, A&S 8 §5, Ex. 8-7, PDF 125–126), IV–V7 at 46 and I at 47. The I6 at 45 is a passing tonic, and the S begins on root-position I, as 7 §5 (PDF 109) requires for a beginning.

### 7. Section openings in root position; contrapuntal arrival used for an opening (27). Follows; keep.

Every formal opening stands on a root-position chord (row 13). The one contrapuntal arrival that opens a block, vii°7→i at 26→27, is used where A&S place such cadences, at "a group of measures within a larger phrase" (PDF 129): the hammered tutti is the continuation of P, not its close.

Proportions, for the record (MIDI tempo map): introduction 67 s (16%), exposition 124 s, development 105 s (0.85 of the exposition), recapitulation 75 s (0.61), coda 59 s (14%); total 431 s. These sit inside the corpus range (Tchaikovsky 6/i: introduction 5% of the bars, coda 6%). Section lengths are protected (c043, c063, c243), and nothing in A&S Units 6–8 argues for changing them.
