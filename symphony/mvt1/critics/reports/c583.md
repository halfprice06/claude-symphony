## Findings

One change is applied in the scratch copy. The other findings record checks that found nothing to change.

### 1. 165: the recapitulation's last dominant has no low octave. Drop the double basses to G1 on beat 3. Applied.

**What is there.** 164–166 is the cadence that closes the C-major second group (ii – cadential 6/4 – V7 – I, mp to p under the rit.). In v583 the cellos, double basses and Bsn 2 all play the bass at the same pitch: D2 (164.1), G2 (164.3–165), C2 (166). The harp's G1 at 164.3–165.2 is damped on 165.3 (c446). So from 165.3 the dominant has nothing below G2. On the same beat Vln II passes G4–F4 (165.3), the 8–7 that brings in the seventh.

**The reading.** Ex. 6-18 (6 §14, PDF 101): V8–7 "happens frequently in an authentic cadence, where the introduction of the 7th can be emphasized by a downward leap of an octave in the bass". Sample Solution 2 (PDF 118) makes the same point about a phrase's final cadence: "the downward octave leap in the bass and the 8–7 passing motion in an inner voice help to intensify the harmonic resolution into the final tonic through rhythmic activity". It adds that "the contrast between the 5̂ above and the 5̂ below the tonic helps to give shape and direction to the bass line". Bar 165 already has the inner 8–7. It lacks the bass octave that those two passages pair with it.

**Why it matters for the form.** 166 is the closing tonic of the large I–V–I (Ex. 6-4, PDF 95): the PAC with 1̂ on top that ends the recapitulation's second group (Ex. 6-3a, PDF 94). Its exposition counterpart, 71→72, has the double basses an octave below the cellos (E♭2→A♭1 under E♭3→A♭3; clos.ily). The recapitulation's goal is the only structural PAC of the movement where the double basses lose their own octave. It therefore sounds thinner in the bass than the A♭ cadence it answers. The softness is right, because C major is never confirmed at forte in movement I (finale reserve). The register is not right. A low G1 at p adds weight without adding loudness.

**The fix (recTwo.ily, `recTwoCb`, last line):**

```
old:  f,,1 | d,2\mp g,2~ | g,1\> | c,2\p~\downbow c,2\<\upbow |
new:  f,,1 | d,2\mp g,2~ | g,2\> g,,2 | c,2\p~\downbow c,2\<\upbow |
```

The bass line 164–166 becomes D2–G2–G1–C2, the shape of Ex. 6-18a: V, the same V an octave lower as the seventh enters, then up a fourth to I. Nothing else changes:
- The cellos keep their arpeggio (G2–D3–F3–B3–F3).
- Bsn 2 keeps G2.
- The harp is still damped at 165.3, so no G1 rings into the C2 of 166, which was c446's purpose.
- With alternating bows (164.3 tied, 165.3 new bow), the marked down-bow at 166 (c338) still falls naturally.
- G1 is not new to this passage: the double basses play it ff at 158–159 and 161.3. It does not take away anything the coda needs; the coda's low C1 at 167 stays the bottom of the movement's last tutti.

### 2. c581's horn call at 125–126 checked against the reading. Keep.

c581 added Hn 1's E♭3–C4–B3 (mp espr. > p) over the G roll at 125–126. I checked it as a form event:
- **Function.** In the Kuhnau, the dominant after the double bar "is expanded in an almost identical way, but with the opening melody inverted" (6 §2, PDF 94). The expanded V of 117–130 now recalls, in order, the motto (trombones 117–121), the introduction's semicadence gesture (horn 125–126, as at 13–14) and the motto again in stretto (127–130). The dominant section becomes a recollection of the introduction before the tonic returns.
- **Harmony and meter.** Bar 125 is a whole-bar i6/4 over the pedal: a neighbour "I" inside V, as in the Jupiter, Ex. 8-14 (PDF 129–130). V arrives on the downbeat of 126 with the 4–3 (C→B). The chord changes on the strong beat (6 §16, PDF 102). This differs from 13–14, where the 6/4 changes on the half-bar. The difference suits the sostenuto.
- **Line.** A rising minor sixth followed by a step down, the change of direction after a tense leap that Ex. 8-16 asks for (PDF 131). The B3 is left unresolved over a continuing V, which is not V→I, so 6 §12's "exposed 7̂ must rise" does not apply.

### 3. 13–14: the introduction ends on V7, not V, and without a rit. Keep both.

6 §10 (PDF 99–100): "If the melodic line comes to rest on 7̂, 2̂, or 5̂, V will generally produce a better effect than V7 … V7 is rarely used in a semicadence." At 14 the horn rests on B3 (7̂) over V7 (Vln II's G4–F4, 13→14). This is a V8–7 (Ex. 6-18), not a resting semicadence. The V goes straight on to i at 15 across the double bar, with one crotchet rest. It is the dominant of an authentic connection whose tonic is the Allegro's first bar. The "inappropriate feeling of activity" that A&S warn against is the drive this seam needs.

The open item from c578 asks for a rit. at 13–14. A&S say that cadences broaden their values (6 §3, PDF 94). The horn call at crotchet = 50 already does this: its C4 and B3 are minims, after quavers and crotchets in 11–12. A printed rit. would slow a bar that already lasts 4.8 s. Keep.
