## Findings

The three main themes and the horn call are presented in brass exactly as this chapter teaches. Each theme gets one of Adler's combinations, at a structural joint, and none of those combinations is used twice in the same way:
- motto: dark horn/trombone unison at 117–121, then a soft trombone chorale at 175–179;
- P: tenor trombones at 131, trumpets an octave above the horns at 167;
- S: horn + cello at 47, trumpet + oboe at 69 and 158.

The colours the chapter calls most brilliant are all withheld for the finale: the high four-horn unison, the tuba as a melodic voice, and the full low-brass unison. That matches the "Do not reuse" list.

I found nothing in the reading that justifies a change in v291. Each finding below records a check, with the reason the bars should stay as they are. I applied nothing.

### 1. Bars 117–121: the motto's second form is the movement's most memorable brass moment. Keep it.

**Check.** Hindemith's passacaglia (Ex. XI-5, PDF 20) gives the theme to four horns and two trumpets in octaves with three trombones in unison. Adler says the "brilliance … comes from the unison of the horns and trombones rather than of blaring trumpets".

At 117–121 the motto (G3 | E♭4 for seven beats, D4 | D♭4 for three beats, C4) is in Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and Bsn 1 at pitch, with the bass trombone an octave below. The trumpets wait until 122. The bass is held by Bsn 2, Cbsn, tuba and Cb on G, as in the Ravel (PDF 26), so the trombones play in their strong G3–E♭4 octave.

**Melody.** Adler (PDF 23) asks which melody notes must dominate, and how. The two notes that make the motto recognisable are the rising-sixth goal E♭ and the chromatic D♭. Here they are the two longest notes, and each falls on a downbeat (118 and 120). The augmentation slows the tune down, so a listener who heard it at bar 1 under pp strings recognises it at ff. The S-rhythm line above (Fl 1, Cl 1, Vln I) is a counterpoint in the *1812* layout (Ex. XI-13, PDF 29–30) and does not compete in register.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bars 158–161: Hn 3·4 in unison up to written G5 at fff (Schuller). Keep; no new reason to reopen.

**Check.** Schuller (PDF 26) advises that high, loud horn unisons go to horns 1 and 3 alone, or to two horns with a trumpet. c181 and c266 judged this passage and kept it. I looked for a reason they did not consider and found none.

- The unison is on the countermelody (with Ob 1 and Cl 1), not on the tune, so a split note would not cost the theme.
- Written G5 is reached only on single strong beats (158.3, 160.2, 160.3), not as a sustained high line.
- Schuller's trumpet remedy would have to take Tpt 3 off the E♭4 it holds at 160 (c154).
- Removing Hn 4 would undo c174's balance (adler2 PDF 15: two horns per line above mf).
- The parts already carry "a2 with 3." for Hn 4.

**Fix.** None. This is not a new point, and I record it only because it is the one place where the reading's rule could be read against the score.

### 3. Bars 131–134: P in the tenor at the recapitulation (*1812* layout). Keep.

**Check.** In *1812* (Ex. XI-13, PDF 29–30) the horns join the trombones, bassoons and low strings on the tune. At 131 the P theme is in Cl a2, Bsn a2, Tbn 1·2 and Vc (G3–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 …), while Hn 1–4 hold G4/E♭4/C4/G3 at f in the same octave.

**Melody.** Is P still the main line here? The theme has eight players on it, with accents on E♭4, A♭3 and F4. The horns are one level below (f, since c264), and the trumpets repeat the harmony in rhythm. Hn 1·2 could take the tune in unison with the trombones, as in the Tchaikovsky. That would give the reprise of P four more brass players at the moment the listener most needs to recognise it.

I rejected this for three reasons:
- c144 lowered the trombones here because brass outweighed the blend, and four more players on the tune works against that.
- The horns' held G4/E♭4 are the only sustained upper-middle harmony under the eighth-note violas.
- Adler's own principle 13 (PDF 28) treats the horns in that example as part of a weight the tune needs only because the counterpoint is "very heavy". Ours is four instruments in octaves at one level.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 175–181: trombone chorale with the motto, answered by Vln I. Keep.

**Check.** Adler warns that thick brass writing must not get muddy in its last phrase (PDF 24), and that a canon works through colour contrast (Berlioz, Ex. XI-14, PDF 30–32).

The chorale is three trombones in open position, pp. Tbn 1 carries the motto (G3–E♭4–D4–C4/D♭4–C4) at the top of the brass. Vln I enters with the same motto an octave higher on beat 4 of 177, so the two statements overlap as a canon at the octave, brass against strings.

The corpus supports a soft low trombone chorale near the end of a minor-key first movement. Tchaikovsky 6/i, bars 336–339 (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml, parts P9/P10), has the trombones p "cantabile" in close position, G♯3–E4 over a bass-trombone E3.

**Melody.** This is the last time the listener hears the motto. It comes twice, low then high, with the horn call (Cl 1 E♭4–C5–B4) above the first statement. That gives the movement's cyclic material its best chance to stay in memory.

**Fix.** None (coda notes and levels are protected by c228, c249, c267 and c278).

### 5. Bars 69–72, 82 and 158–161: trumpet + oboe on the themes. Keep.

**Check.** Ex. XI-11 (PDF 28) shows trumpets doubling oboes to cut through a full texture.

- At 69–72 Tpt 1 and Ob 1 play the S head at the same pitch (C5–A♭4–F5 …), with the flutes and Vln I two octaves higher, over the horns' P head.
- At 82 Tpt 1 has the P head with Ob 1, Cl 1·2 and Vln II.
- At 158–161 Tpt 1 has the tune's middle octave with Ob 2 and Vln II (c241).

At each forte statement of a theme in the allegro, the theme's middle octave has this doubling. This is why S and P remain audible as tunes inside the tuttis, not only as top-line figures.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Melody overall: would a listener hum the themes afterwards?

The reading concerns presentation, not invention, so this is a judgement from the lens, not from the book.

- **Motto** (G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C): six notes, with a rising minor sixth and a chromatic return. It is as concise as the opening idea of Tchaikovsky 5 or Brahms 1/i, and it is heard often enough to stick: 1, 5, 73, 117, 175 and 177, plus the heads.
- **S** (E♭ | C–B♭ | A♭–F–E♭ | D♭–C–B♭–D♭–F | A♭–G): the tune most likely to be hummed. It opens with the same rising sixth, then steps down in a long arch. It is the kind of horn-and-cello melody that Dvořák (8/i second group) and Tchaikovsky (5/ii) give the horn.
- **P** is a driving figure rather than a song, which is right for an Allegro appassionato.
- **The horn call** is two bars, and the finale completes it.

I would not change any theme: every change would have to be reported to the other movements, and I found no gain to set against that cost.
