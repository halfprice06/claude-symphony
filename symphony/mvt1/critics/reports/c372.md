## Findings

No score change. The movement's Neapolitan and augmented-sixth settings follow Units 28–29 point by point. The items below are the places where the reading suggests a possible change; for each I give the change, the evidence, and why I recommend keeping the current text. Nothing was applied in scratch, so no build was needed.

### 1. Bars 60–62: Phrygian iv6 → cadential 6/4 in A♭ without ♯4 (keep)

Problem considered: bar 61 is iv7/6 of A♭ (F♭ bass, A♭, C♭, D♭) moving to the cadential 6/4 over E♭ at 62. A&S PDF 504 (Ex. 29-3) presents this progression as the origin of the augmented sixth, and PDF 507–508 (Ex. 29-12, 29-13) show that in major the aug6 resolving to a major 6/4 is spelled with the doubly augmented 4th. Adding D♮ (♯4) on 61.3 and respelling Cl 1/Hn 2 `ces'2` as `b2` would turn bar 61 into a German sixth with doubly augmented 4th.

Evidence for keeping it: the bass G♭–F♭–E♭ at 60–62 is a seed that movement II quotes (mvt1.md); violins 56–63, the counterline, violas 60–67 and 62–63 are protected (c273, c277, c327, c371). Inserting D♮ would add a fourth chromatic tone against the violins' B𝄫5–A♭5–G♭5–F♭5 line, which is already the chromatic descent of PDF 512 (Ex. 29-20, rising and falling chromatic lines against the aug6), and the plain Phrygian form is a valid stage in its own right (PDF 504). The C♭→C♮ in Cl 1/Hn 2 is mode mixture (minor iv → major 6/4), so it is correctly spelled as a lowered tone that is a chord member, not a raised passing tone; PDF 497 applies to passing chromatic tones. Recommendation: keep. Fix not applied.

### 2. Bar 12: German sixth with ♯4 in two octaves (keep)

Problem considered: at 12 F♯ sounds as F♯3 (Hn 3·4) and F♯4 (Ob 2, Vln II), and all go to G at 13. A&S PDF 505 warns that wrong doublings in an aug6 "lead to … parallel octaves." Evidence for keeping it: these are octave doublings of one line in a tutti, not two independent voices; vl.py lists them only as doublings, and the core four-part frame (A♭ bass, C, E♭, F♯) resolves to the cadential 6/4 as PDF 505–506 (Ex. 29-8) teaches. The passage is protected (c282 master line, c322 "Cadential 6/4s at 12–14"). Recommendation: keep.

### 3. Bars 160–166: no aug6 after the deceptive cadences (keep)

A&S PDF 513 (Ex. 29-22) says aug6 chords often follow deceptive cadences to VI. c282 already tested and rejected adding one here, and 160 and 165–166 are protected (c329). The fff ♭VI at 160 already carries the A♭ that would be the aug6 bass; an aug6 at 163 or 165 would repeat 12–13 and weaken the structural cadence at 166. Recommendation: keep.

### 4. Bars 172–174: N6 with doubled ♭2 (keep)

At 173.3 the N6 (F bass, A♭, D♭) doubles D♭ (Vc D♭4, Cor anglais D♭5). PDF 484 calls the bass the best doubling, but root doubling is allowed when neither ♭2 moves to ♮2. Here the Cor anglais takes D♭5 to C5 (the motto) and the cellos leap D♭4–F4, so neither line has the direct ♭2–♮2 that PDF 484 (Ex. 28-3) rules out. The cadential 6/4 at 172–174 is protected (c322) and this bar carries the motto. Recommendation: keep.

### 5. Bars 115–116: root-position ♭II to Fr6 over C, inner D♭–D (keep)

Hn 3/Tpt 3 move D♭4→D4 directly. PDF 484 and 488 (Ex. 28-12) allow this in an inner voice when ♭2 is doubled, which it is (D♭ in several octaves at 115). The C bass that does not go to G at once is the motivic bypass of PDF 516 (Ex. 29-27, K. 491), already kept by c282. Recommendation: keep.

## Cross-movement effects

None. No notes changed. The 60–62 bass (G♭–F♭–E♭), the F♭→E♭ thread and the motto at 172–174 that the other movements use are unchanged.

## Already at a master's level

The Neapolitan settings at 7, 20–22, 140–143, 172–174, 178 and 180, and the Ger6 → cadential 6/4 → V at 12–13, should not be touched.
