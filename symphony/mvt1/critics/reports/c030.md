## Findings

### 1. Coda 163–167: the brass lead turns the coda from "the theme returns" into "fate's verdict". The descent is earned, but its hinge needs time (coda section bar 4 = movement bar 166)
**Meaning.** In v48, the tutti at 163–165 carried T1 in winds and strings, so it was the protagonist returning. In v49, trumpets and horns carry the motto-derived head over string tremolo. That is the brass fanfare topic: Tchaikovsky's fate call (4/i, 5/i) and Dvořák 9/i 396–407.

The order is now:
- hope: the C-major second theme;
- its peak on ♭VI at 156;
- its cancellation: the E♮→E♭ turn at 162;
- fate's assertion: the brass at 163–166;
- submission: the minor second theme, the CA motto, then the pp trombone chorale under a tam-tam.

This is a clearer and stronger arc than v48. The brass lead also pays off at 171: the brass that asserted at ff return as a hushed chorale, so the chorale now reads as the same force transformed rather than as a new colour. For movement III this matters: the finale's C-major chorale is then fate redeemed, which is the Tchaikovsky 5 model.

**What is wrong.** Bar 166 is the third appearance of one gesture: an accented ff vii°7 over C, held for a dotted half, then a quarter rest.
- At 26 and 130 the same chord, the same rhythm and the trumpet's B♮ all launch the T1 tutti one beat later (27, 131). The listener has learned "this chord, then the tutti".
- At 166 the learned continuation is denied: a pp Più lento follows instead. This is the movement's best surprise, and it is built from the piece's own material, in the same way Beethoven 5/i's recapitulation oboe solo fills a fermata the exposition taught.
- At present, though, the denial passes in one quarter rest under a tempo change. A conductor will treat it as a breath. The moment where the tutti should have struck is not felt as empty, so the prediction failure (Huron's "prediction/reaction" stage) is blurred into a simple change of section.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i solves the same problem (ffff collapsing into the lyrical second theme) with a fermata on a rest at 304, before 305.

**Fix (tested).** Put a fermata on the beat-4 rest of 166 in every sounding part. Keep the allargando (cycle 8) and all pitches; this undoes nothing. In coda.ily, section bar 4:
- `...2.->\ff r4` becomes `...2.->\ff r4\fermata` in: codaFluteOne, codaFluteTwo, codaOboeOne, codaOboeTwo, codaClOne, codaClTwo, codaBassCl, codaBsnOne, codaBsnTwo, codaCbsn, codaHnOne (`b2.->\ff r4\fermata \partCombineAutomatic`), codaHnTwo, codaHnThree, codaHnFour, codaTptOne (`b'2.->\ff r4\fermata \partCombineAutomatic`), codaTptTwo, codaTptThree, codaTbnOne, codaTbnTwo, codaBassTbn, codaTuba, codaVlnOne (`<aes'' f'''>2.:32->\ff r4\fermata`), codaVlnTwo (`<d'' aes''>2.:32->\ff r4\fermata`), codaVc, codaCb.
- codaVla: `<d' f'>2.->\ff) r4\fermata`
- codaTimp: `c4->\ff r2.` becomes `c4->\ff r2 r4\fermata`

Shared staves get identical fermatas and no new dynamics. The silent CA, harp and percussion staves are hidden on that system. I tested this in /tmp/critic-030/v49 with `tools/build.sh /tmp/critic-030/v49 score.ly`: rc=0, 0 warnings, still 17 pages. Page 16 shows one fermata per staff at 166.

### 2. Is there a real surprise? Yes, several. I propose no new one
The movement already contains:
- 69: a subito forte tutti after the p cadence at 68, with no crescendo;
- 97: a direct semitone shift to B minor;
- 157–158: V–vi straight after the fff;
- 166→167: the denied tutti (finding 1);
- 178: the viola's E♮.

Every other ff (82, 109, 123, 154, 163) is reached by a crescendo. That is right for a movement whose subject is fate gathering. A Tchaikovsky-style unprepared ff would need a pppp extinction first, and the only candidate is 73–81. That spot is protected (cycles 21 and 24), and the plan uses it for a growing dialogue. Adding a further shock would also dilute 166→167, which should stay the one denial the listener carries away. Sharpen that one (finding 1) instead of adding another.

### 3. First-time listening: where attention may drift (no score change)
- **139–147.** This is the weakest stretch for a newcomer. It is the third hearing of the second theme's opening phrase:
  - same tempo, same horn+cello colour as 47–55 (plus viola);
  - MP3 RMS stays within −9.4 to −11.1 dB for nine bars;
  - mean MIDI velocity stays at 61–66;
  - the restatement at 56–68 has already used up the novelty of the tune.

  The sul tasto halo and the CA fills are the only new elements, and they are protected (cycles 18 and 27). Masters recolour the recapitulated second theme: Tchaikovsky 6/i 305 gives it to flutes and violins "con dolcezza". I record this as a risk, not a fix, because the protected items cover exactly this passage. If a later critic gets new evidence, that is the place to look.
- **Ceiling saturation.** From 96 to 166, 42 of 71 bars sit within about 4 dB of the movement's RMS maximum (96–116, 123–134, 154–158, 163–166). By Rozin et al., listeners remember the peak and the end. The structural peak, 156, has to stand out from this plateau, and it does so only by register, the cymbal and the tune, not by level.
- **Caution for the composer.** In score.midi, accented ff tutti chords outweigh the fff at 156. Mean velocities: 26 = 108, 72 = 98, 83 = 106, 130 = 99, 166 = 108, against 156 = 88. LilyPond adds an accent bonus that fff does not get. So the MP3 makes 166 sound like the movement's peak. In a live orchestra, the fff tutti with a crash at 156 will be louder. Do not "fix" the hierarchy by ear from the MP3.

### 4. A cyclic hook created by cycle 29, for movement III (add to cyclic-notes.md)
The trumpets' last note in the movement is the accented B♮ at 166, the leading tone. It is never resolved, because 167 begins without them. The trumpets have stated the head for the last time and are left on B.

Movement III's C-major chorale should let the trumpets make that resolution: B up to C on the arrival of the major motto (e.g. trumpets enter on `b'2.` and resolve to `c''` at the chorale's downbeat). That way the finale answers this exact sound, not only the motto's pitches. No change to this score.

### 5. Proportions of the symphony (plan note, outside the score)
The MP3 lasts 7:13 (MIDI 425 s, before any rit. or fermata is realised). The plan gives movement I about 6:00, movement II 5:00 and movement III 4:00. A four-minute finale that has to redeem a seven-minute first movement whose coda ends in submission would be weighted to the front. Tchaikovsky 5's finale is about as long as its first movement. Plan movement III at 6 minutes or more, with the chorale given room. Do not shorten movement I: the cycle-23 proportions are protected and sound.

## Do not touch
These are at a master's level: the brass-led coda opening 163–166 (cycle 29) with its vii°7 at 166 rhyming with 26 and 130; the cello theme against the CA motto at 167–170; the trombone chorale with tam-tam pp at 171–175; the E♮→E♭ turn at 162; the viola E♮ ending.
