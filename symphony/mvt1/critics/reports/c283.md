## Findings

No finding stands between this score and a master's work enough to justify a change. The items below are the form questions the reading bears on, ranked by weight. Each ends with a single recommendation.

### 1. Tension curve of the recapitulation 131–170 and the climax at 158–160 — stays as it is

What I checked: the recapitulation's first group is tutti from 131 to 143 (ff at 131–134, f at 135–138, ff at 139–143), and 139–141 are the densest bars of the movement in distinct track-pitch pairs in score.midi (58, 53, 63) against 48 and 59 at 160–161. All tuttis use 26–27 of the 30 players, so the count of players does not separate 140 from 160. A reader of the curve could conclude that 140 pre-empts the climax.

Evidence: Adler's chapter 17 locates the strength of an arrival in the freshness of its colour, not its thickness. The solo colour is "very fresh" when another choir precedes it (PDF 263); Mozart keeps the orchestral horns out "so as not to interfere with the solo sound" (PDF 264); the orchestra is reduced drastically once the soloist enters and the tutti is kept for the interludes (PDF 267). At 158 fourteen players re-enter after 11–15 bars' rest, more than at any other point, after twelve bars (144–155) in which no flute, trumpet, trombone or tuba plays and the timpani have only a soft roll at 144–146. At 139 only the bass clarinet is rested. The climax also has the only fff besides 122, the only cymbal, the largamente and the ♭VI bass. Bizet's practice (PDF 279) is met: the violins' A♭6 at 160 is new since 122, and at 140 Fl 1 has it alone (c194).

Recommendation: keep 131–143 as written. Thinning 139–142 would weaken the Neapolitan turn to iv that c223 and c233 protect, and it would gain nothing the rested brass at 158 does not already give.

### 2. Development opening 77–84 (open item "stop-start", critic 79) — stays as it is; close the item

The head passes through B.cl and Cl 1 (77), Hn 1·2 with the cellos (78), Ob 1 with Vln I (79), then short f entries in bassoons, horns, trombones, clarinets, oboe and flute (80–81), each entry in a new colour. The density is 10–21 track-pitch pairs a bar. Adler explains why such a passage does not sound empty: with constant colour change "the listener gets the feeling of contrapuntal density though the actual texture is very sparse" (PDF 195, after Ex. XV-42; Ex. XV-38 on PDF 191 colours each note of a tune with a different instrument). The ff outburst at 82–83 and the solo cellos at 84–85 work as the "orchestral interruptions" of an accompanied recitative (PDF 276, Ex. XVII-17). The passage is protected (77–83, rests 83→84, sub. p at 84), and the reading supports the protection. I recommend recording the open item as closed.

### 3. 103–110 after the ff B-minor S (open item "103–106 light after ff") — stays as it is; close the item

At 103 the texture drops from 51 to 21 track-pitch pairs and the upper strings stop. Adler's Mozart example does the same: after the tutti, "as soon as the flute enters, the orchestra is reduced drastically" (PDF 267). The two heads change roles at 107 (P head from the top to the bass clarinet, S head from the tenor to violas and Fl 1), which is the exchange of foreground and background Adler names as the normal procedure in successful concertos (PDF 262). The drop marks the start of the combination section D3a. The wedge 111–116 then rebuilds from mp. Keep.

### 4. The recapitulation reverses the exposition's dialogue — stays as it is

In the exposition the P theme is stated by strings alone (15–22) and answered by the tutti (27–32). In the recapitulation the tutti comes first (131) and the solo answer comes after the stop at 143: Bsn 1 at 144 takes the head that the bassoon introduced at 33. Adler's dialogue principle (PDF 257–258) needs the two colours in close succession, not a fixed order, and Ex. XVII-1 shows that the answer must not duplicate the first colour. Since c247 the violas no longer double the bassoon at 144. The reversal also gives the recapitulation a different course from the exposition without new bars. Keep.

### 5. Recapitulated second group "too complete" (open item) — stays as it is; close the item

By bar count the recapitulated second group (147–166, 20 bars) is almost as long as the exposition's (47–68, 22 bars), and by time it is 44.8 s against 47.4 s. But the recapitulation states S only once (147–155, nine bars), where the exposition states it twice (47–55, then the violins at 56–63). The violins' second statement is withheld for movement III (c080), and 156–166 is the climax and dissolution, material the exposition does not have. Tchaikovsky 6/i keeps about a fifth of the exposition in its recapitulation, Beethoven 9/i all of it. Movement I keeps three fifths, and the part it drops is the restatement. Adler's guidance on varying the doubling in a second phrase (Haydn, PDF 288) is met by the four different settings of S (47, 56, 147, 158). Keep.

### 6. Two-bar approach 156–157 into the main climax — stays as it is

The climax rises from p at 155 to ff at 158 in two bars, against eleven bars of wedge before 122. A longer approach was considered and rejected at c083, c143 and c243. The reading gives a further reason to keep it short. Bizet takes the first violins up to double the singer only at her climax (PDF 279). Here the violins have not played S since 97–102, and at 147–155 Vln I rests while Vln II plays only the sul tasto halo, so their entry with the S rhythm at 155.4 is itself the start of the climax. The growth into 158 comes from the rested brass and winds (Finding 1). Keep.

### 7. Coda 171–182 — stays as it is; bar 172 noted as borderline

The coda follows chapter 17 in three ways. The tutti stop at 170 is followed by a drastic reduction to a solo cello and the C.a. (PDF 267). The solo cello is a colour heard nowhere else in the movement (PDF 263). The timpani pulse at 179–180 joins the chorale to the last bars, as Beethoven's timpani join the two phrases in Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273). One detail is borderline under PDF 274: on the downbeat of 172 the violas move B3→C4 pp at the moment the solo cello plays its C4 mp, so the solo's first note of the bar is doubled at pitch by a section. It lasts one beat, the cello leaves at once to A♭4 (rhythmic contrast, which Adler says "lessens the importance of this consideration"), and the viola's B→C is the leading tone resolving under iv's arrival. The coda notes are protected (c228, c249, c251). Keep.
