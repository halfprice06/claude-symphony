# Critic 412: Harmony and voice leading (v412)

Reading: Aldwell & Schachter, Unit 9 "Leading to V: IV, II and II6" and Unit 10 "The Cadential 6/4" (aldwell-schachter PDF 134–162), second pass. I read the text file of every page from PDF 134 to 162 in full and rendered all 29 pages at 110 dpi to read the examples, because the OCR garbles the figures and labels (for example 6/4 comes out as "§"). One page is missing from the scan: book p. 151 (Unit 10 §§9–11: the cadential 6/4 and the submediant, harmonic rhythm against the 6/4, and the noncadential 6/4) falls between PDF 156 (book p. 150) and PDF 157 (book p. 152). Only the start of each heading shows at the right edge of the PDF 156 image. The rule "a 6/4 on the dominant is not restricted to cadences" is still stated on PDF 152 and in review point 8 on PDF 160.

Method:
- I dumped every beat and every half-beat of `v412/full.midi`. For each one I listed the lowest note, the pitch classes and each instrument's pitch, with onsets marked.
- I wrote four scans:
  - a six-four finder (bass plus a 4th and a 6th, with no 3rd or 5th);
  - weak-to-strong bass repetitions under a change of chord (Ex. 9-24);
  - melodic Ab↔B, Db↔E and Eb↔F# steps (the augmented second of Ex. 9-6c, 9-7f and 9-17b);
  - the bass that follows each inverted dominant or intermediate harmony (syntax rule 1, PDF 148).
- Critic 322 did a full pass with this same reading on v322 and changed nothing. So I rebuilt v323 in scratch and diffed its MIDI against v412. The diff has 150 note events, in bars 25, 35, 41–44, 61, 69–72, 82–83, 104–105, 115, 123–124, 135–137, 140–141, 144–145, 154, 156–161, 163 and 173–174. I checked those bars note by note and re-checked every cadential 6/4 and intermediate harmony in the movement.

## Findings

### Checked and kept (no change)

1. **61→63, the 4th of the cadential 6/4 of Ab in the horns.**
   - **What happens.** At 61 Hn 3 has Ab3 and Hn 4 has Fb3. At 62.1 Hn 3 leaps up to C4 and Hn 4 leaps up to Ab3. From 62.1 to 62.3 Hn 4's Ab3 is the only 4th, apart from the harp's Ab3 at 62.3. Hn 4 then rests on 62.4 and comes back on Bb3 at 63.1.
   - **Why it looks wrong.** Read literally, the 4th enters by leap and rises a step after a rest. PDF 153 asks for a common-tone or stepwise entry, and review 2 on PDF 160 for a fall.
   - **Why it is right.** Ab sounds as a common tone into 62 in the horn pair: Hn 3's Ab3 at 61 becomes Hn 4's Ab3 at 62, the same pitch on the same shared stave. At 62.4 Cl 1 and Hn 2 take the Ab up an octave (Ab4) at the moment Hn 4 releases. They resolve it Ab4→G4 at 63.2, while Vln I's C6 rises to Db6 (the 6th rising to the 7th, Ex. 10-4b). So there is one 4th, handed on and resolved, and Hn 4's Bb3 at 63 is a new entry: c371 kept it as the delayed fifth under the peak.
   - **The alternative.** Hn 3 holds Ab3, Hn 4 goes Fb3→Eb3. That would make the pad a bare fourth at the sub. p, and it would touch 62–63, which is protected (c364, c371).
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

2. **137.4→138: the bass D repeats from beat 4 into the stop chord (new since v403).**
   - **What happens.** c402 moved the bass on 137.4 from Ab to D. Now iiø7 on the weak beat goes to vii°6/5 on the downbeat of 138 over the same D. This is the configuration PDF 149 §15 warns about (Ex. 9-24a).
   - **Why it is allowed.** Both of the reading's reasons apply. First, the strong-beat chord is a diminished seventh, a dissonance with a fresh ff trumpet attack. That gives the downbeat its accent, the condition of Ex. 9-24b. Second, it is II moving over a stationary bass to an inverted dominant, which review 9 (PDF 151) calls good: "IV or II6 to V4/2". Mozart's K. 310 (Ex. 9-14, PDF 142) uses the same kind of move.
   - **What else is fixed.** The bass D at 138 is quoted by the finale, so it cannot change. The exposition's 25.4→26 (D→B) is a different case and needs no comment.
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

3. **25.2 and 137.2: Hn 1 Ab4 (new since v404) makes three Abs (6̂) in iv.**
   - **The reading.** It prefers the root of IV doubled (PDF 136), and warns that a doubled 6̂ in minor tends to produce an A2 (PDF 139, Ex. 9-7f).
   - **Why it does no harm here.** The Abs do not move on to B. They are held into vii°7 at 26 and 138, where Ab is the seventh, and then fall to G (Hn 1 Ab4→G4 at 27). The root F is also doubled in four octaves. No augmented second results.
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

4. **132.4→133.2: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I·II skip down B→Ab across a quaver rest.**
   - **Why it looks wrong.** It is a descending augmented second in the counter above the low P head.
   - **Why it is right.** The A2 of Ex. 9-6c, 9-7f and 9-17b (PDF 138–139, 144) replaces a step in a stepwise line. Here B5–Ab5–F5–D5 arpeggiates the vii°7 that runs from V♭9 at 132.4 to vii°4/2 over Ab at 133, with a rest before the Ab. That is a chordal skip.
   - **The earlier fix.** c242 fixed the trumpet's B4–Ab4–B4 for being two augmented seconds in a neighbour figure, which is a different case.
   - **Protection.** The notes of 131–134 are protected (c357, c398).
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

5. **158–161 (the Cl 2 counter added since v408, Bsn octaves, Tbn 1 B3→C4).**
   - Cl 2 takes C6→B5 at 158.3→159.1 with Ob 1 and Cl 1. The 4th falls.
   - The new bassoon octaves double the bass G (Ex. 10-5a).
   - Tbn 1's B3→C4 into ♭VI at 160 is a leading tone moving to a chord tone of the deceptive goal.
   - The 6th E holds over the 4–3 at 159.1 and rises to F at 159.2. This is Ex. 10-4b staggered by a beat, as at 13–14.
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

6. **69.3→70.1: Hn 4 Eb3→F3 against Bsn 1 Eb4→F4 (I6–ii7 in Ab).**
   - **Why it looks wrong.** These are two pad voices moving in octaves into an intermediate chord. PDF 141 warns about this for I6–IV in four-part writing.
   - **Why it is acceptable.** This is a forte tutti of about 25 parts, where both notes are chord-tone doublings inside the held harmony. The reading itself relaxes doubling rules for "very free or complex textures" (PDF 154). The motion is older than c322.
   - **Why the fixes fail.** Each re-voicing I tried creates new octaves with the violas' arpeggio at 70.2.5→70.3, or with the bass at 70.3→71.
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

7. **173→174: the C.a. holds Db5 for two beats (v401), then C5 on the 6/4.**
   - Over a bass F held for two bars, the upper voices go 5–6–♭6: iv7 at 172, iiø6/5 at 173.1, ♭II6 at 173.3. This is the 5–6 technique of Ex. 9-20, and ♭II6 is the chromatic II6.
   - The 4th C5 enters by step from ♭2̂ as an accented passing tone (Ex. 10-3b), on the downbeat of 174 (PDF 154 §6).
   - The solo cello, in unison, resolves it to B4.
   - **Verdict.** Keep (coda harmony protected, c400).

8. **124→126: V7, then a 6/4 over G, then V, in the collapse.**
   - **Why it looks wrong.** Review 7 (PDF 160) says the 6/4 should not be approached from V.
   - **Why it is right.** The chord is a neighbour 6/4 over the held pedal: B–C–B (Hn 3, c284) and D–Eb–D. The reading separates this from the cadential type (PDF 152, review 8).
   - **Verdict.** Keep. This is the same judgement c322 made for 101–102 and 117–125.
