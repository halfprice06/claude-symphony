# Critic 064, lens 4 (orchestration): spacing and balance of the f/ff/fff tutti chords, v83

## What I studied
I rebuilt v83 as a one-track-per-instrument MIDI with accents stripped, so that velocity shows only the written dynamic (`/tmp/critic-064/orig/v83/all2.ly`, using the dynamic table from score.ly). A script (`/tmp/critic-064/s/tutti.py`) lists every onset where 15 or more parts sound at f or louder and at least 8 attack together. That gives 126 onsets in these bars: 12, 25–33, 41–42, 69–72, 82–83, 96–102, 107–117, 123–135, 154–158 and 163–166. For each one I tabulated pitches by instrument, the pitch-class doubling, the spacing between adjacent pitches, and whether the brass alone holds the complete chord (`brass.py`). I ran the same measures (`stats.py`, `inv7.py`) on the MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`), counting onsets with 13 or more parts at f or louder. Corpus bar numbers are MusicXML measure numbers.

Sources:
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III, from https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/33900/pg33900.txt. I used "Number of harmonic parts – Duplication", "Wood-wind harmony" (rule 2: normal order of register; Remarks 1–2: no voids in forte, wide intervals in the bass, close intervals in the upper register), "Harmony in the brass" (the four horns as a balanced four-part chord; doubling the bass in octaves done by the bassoon) and "Combination of the three groups".
- Low interval limits: https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/low-interval-limit/. That page uses middle C = C3. In scientific pitch its limits are about C3 for a minor third and B2 for a major third.
- Adler's *Study of Orchestration* is not freely available online, so I did not consult it.

## Overall result
The tutti voicings are mostly sound, and generally cleaner than the models:

| measure | v83 | Tchaikovsky 6/i | Dvořák 9/i |
|---|---|---|---|
| close interval (< P4) below E3 | 6 / 126 onsets | 12 / 38 | 14 / 222 |
| void > octave between C3 and C6 | 3 | 8 | 0 |
| mean share of root / third / fifth (triads and sevenths) | .37 / .27 / .26 | .40 / .27 / .24 | .34 / .32 / .29 |

The brass alone forms the complete chord at every tutti. The only notes it leaves out are the melody's suspensions (C over V at 28, 32 and 134; D♭ at 124), and that is correct. Most suggestions this lens could produce would therefore be churn. The four findings below are local errors, and all four are applied in the scratch copy. After them I list the points I checked and found acceptable, so they do not get reopened.

## Findings

**1. Bar 157, beats 1–2: Bassoon 1 on F2, a minor third above the D2 bass at fff, and crossed below Bassoon 2 (D3).** (Applied.)
At the main climax the lowest four sounding pitches are D2 (Cb, Tuba, Cbsn), F2 (Bsn 1), G2 (timp) and D3. D2–F2 is the lowest close interval in any tutti of the movement. It is an octave below the minor-third limit (C3), and below anything in the models: Tchaikovsky's lowest close tutti interval is Tbn G2–B♭2 at 6/i m. 190. Bassoon 1 also sounds below Bassoon 2, against Rimsky-Korsakov's rule 2 ("the normal order of register must be followed"). Rimsky-Korsakov assigns the doubling of the bass at the octave to the bassoon.
Fix (recTwo.ily, recTwoBsnOne): `aes,1\fff | \partCombineAutomatic d2 g,2 |`, replacing `f,2 g,2`. The bassoons then play a2, D3 to G2.
Protection: the bass (D2–G1), the melody, the counterline, the number of instruments and the dynamics are all unchanged. One low note moves an octave to double the bass, so the climax keeps its weight with less low-register noise. Not reversing anything from earlier cycles: cycle 62's 4̂–5̂ step is at 150–151, not 157.

**2. Bar 26 (and 130): Horn 4 on B2, below the bass trombone, bassoon 2 and cellos on D3.** (Applied.)
The ff vii°7 over D at 26 has Hn 4 on B2 under the D3 of B.tbn, Bsn 2 and Vc. That is a minor third below C3, with the horn crossed under the trombone bass line. Its B2 also moves F2→B2 in octaves with Fl 1, Cl 1 and Vln I (four octave pairs reported by vl2.py). Rimsky-Korsakov: "the bass part should never mix with the others".
Fix (tOne.ily, tOneHnFour): bar 26 `f2.->\ff r4`, replacing `b,2.->\ff r4`. F2 rises an octave to F3, then F3 goes to G3 at 27. The brass still holds B♮ (Tpt 1 B4) and the full chord, as do the horns with Hn 1–3 on A♭4 F4 D4. For consistency, recOne.ily (recOneHnFour) bar 130: `f2.->\mf r4`, replacing `b,2.->\mf r4`. Here Hn 4 enters on F3 and holds it into 131.
Protection: the hemiola rhythm (23–26) and the graded brass dynamics are untouched. I first tried Hn 3 B3 with Hn 4 D3, but it created new octaves (Hn 4 against Vln II), so I rejected it.

**3. Bars 69–72: Clarinet 1 has no dynamic in the codetta tutti.** (Applied.)
Clarinet 1's last marking is `\p` at 68. Its counterline at 69, shared with Hn 1·2 and Vln II, has no marking of its own. The shared staff shows Cl 2's f, but Cl 1's part and the MIDI read p. This is the only instrument in any tutti whose written level is below mf (stale.py). In an extracted part the counterline would be played softly against a forte tutti.
Fix (clos.ily, closClOne): `\partCombineApart r8\f es'8 c''4.-> ...`. The f sits on the rest, simultaneous with Cl 2's `aes'2\f`, so the pair has equal dynamics.

**4. Bar 116, beat 1: a 14-semitone gap at fff between the melody's G5 and the next voice down (F4).** (Applied.)
At 115 the octave under the G6/G5 melody is filled (Vln II D5/G5, Fl 2 D5, Cl 1 D5, Cl 2 B4). At 116 Fl 2 drops out, and Cl 1 and Vln II fall to F4/D4 while Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I still hold G5 at fff. Rimsky-Korsakov, Remark 1: "Modern orchestrators do not allow any void in the intermediate parts ... These empty spaces create a bad effect especially in forte passages."
Fix (dev.ily, devClOne): `f''1\fff | d''1 | f''2.\> r4 | es'1 |`, replacing `f'2.`. Cl 1 stays on F5 for the start of the diminuendo, then rests a beat before E♭4 at 117.
Protection: the sigh (115–117) in Ob 1, Fl 1 and Vln I and the Vln II descent are unchanged.

## Checked, no change recommended (evidence for the composer)
- **114–115: B.tbn B2 over G2/G1 at fff.** This is a semitone below the major-third limit, so it is marginal, and Tchaikovsky does the same at the 6/i development peak (m. 190: Tbn B♭2–E♭3 over B.tbn G2, Tuba G1). Keep.
- **31, beat 1: A♭ (the third of iv) is 14 of 25 notes; 41: G (the third and leading note of V7/A♭) is 11 of 23.** In both bars the melody sits on that note in four octaves. Dvořák 9/i goes further: m. 61 has 70% third and m. 43 58%, and over the movement his tuttis average a 0.32 share for the third against 0.27 here. Keep.
- **72: A♭ tonic with the fifth only at E♭3/E♭4 (2 of 26).** The horns' A♭3 ending is the end of their counterline. Dvořák 9/i m. 173 ends a ff tonic with no fifth at all (G16 B7). Keep.
- **98: B minor with only one F♯ on the beat.** The clarinet and viola arpeggios supply F♯ every other eighth, so the chord is complete.
- **108 (Tbn 2 A♭2 under B.cl C3) and the 25 hemiola (Bsn 2 A♭2 over F2):** both are moving lines inside protected passages, at the limit, and short. Leave them.
- **Melody-to-tutti balance:** 27–32 has six melody instruments at ff over brass at f; 154–157 has ten of 25 at fff, including Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1; 163–166 has the brass head at ff over f. These match the graded-brass decisions and need no change.
