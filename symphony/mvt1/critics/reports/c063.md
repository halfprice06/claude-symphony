# Critic 063: lens 3 (form). The recapitulation as recomposition (v82)

## What I studied
I read every part of v82 sections tOne, trans, tTwo, clos, recOne, recTwo and coda, and pages 12–17 of score.pdf. For the corpus I used music21 to extract top lines and key signatures from `musicxml-converted/xml/` and the MCTL form tables in `annotations/*/form/form_analysis.csv`:
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: S 89–140, closing 141–160, recapitulated S 305–334, coda 335–354.
- Dvořák 9/i: S1 91, S2 149–166, closing 167–176. In the recapitulation, S1 is at 316 (G♯ minor), S2 at 374–391 (A♭), the closing at 392–399, and the coda starts at 400.
- Beethoven 9/i: S 80, closing 138–159. In the recapitulation, P is at 301, S at 339 (D major, turning to D minor from about 351), the closing at 407–426 in D minor, and the coda at 427.

Web sources:
- Hepokoski & Darcy, "Sonata form as a whole" (ch. 2 of *Elements*): https://www.some.ox.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/31.-Hepokoski-and-Darcy-2006-Sonata-form-as-a-whole.pdf. They call the recapitulation a "largely referential retracing" ("recapitulatory rotation"). They say the ESC is "most often a point parallel to the exposition's EEC", and that "Not all expositions contain C-modules".
- Mastic, MTO 21.2: https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.15.21.2/mto.15.21.2.mastic.html. It quotes H&D p. 244 ("recapitulatory deviations … of utmost interest") and the "crux" (pp. 239–41: the point where the recapitulation becomes verbatim, "normatively before the onset of S").
- SFCM recapitulation lecture: https://sfcm.edu/sites/default/files/sfcm-theory/analysis_lectures/19_sonata_form_recap/sonata_form_recapitulation.pdf. It gives the checklist "Closing Themes: are they all present …? If a coda exists, might there be a structural reason …?".

## Findings (ranked)

**1. 69–76 / 162–170: the closing zone is not missing; the coda takes its place by rotation. No note change.**
- **Problem examined:** the codetta 69–76 is never restated after 162.
- **Evidence:** 163–166 occupy the codetta's exact rotational slot.
  - They come straight after the S cadence (162 ↔ 68, both p).
  - They carry the same tempo word ("Tempo dell'Allegro" at 69 and 163) and the same jump to forte.
  - They last the same 4 bars, with Tpt 1 leading in both places.
  - 167–170 then answer 73–76 (soft motto after the tutti).
  - The changes have audible reasons. The exposition's darkening (major S, then motto in minor at 73) happens before the tutti in the recapitulation (162), so the tutti itself is now minor. The codetta's final I–V–I (72) is denied in the matching bar (166).
- **Corpus:** Tchaikovsky 6/i omits its closing zone (141–160) and goes from S 334 to the coda at 335. Beethoven 9/i (closing 407–426 in D minor) and Dvořák 9/i (392–399) restate it. Both of those closings are independent themes. Ours is built from the P and S heads, like Tchaikovsky's, so omitting it loses no theme. H&D note that expositions may lack C-modules and treat the coda as outside sonata space. Inserting a C-major codetta after 162 would put a major forte tutti after the E♮→E♭ turn and cancel it.
- **Fix (applied, comment only):** a rotation note in the coda.ily header so future cycles do not "restore" the codetta.

**2. 123–135: the first group's changes all have reasons; keep them.**
- **Evidence:** two passages are omitted, and both have reasons.
  - 19–22: the d♭''' peak is not lost. It moves into 132, which now holds both exposition peaks (D♭ and A♭6) in one bar over ♭II.
  - 27–28: 123 already gives the head at ff.
- **Corpus:** the compression to 50% of P+TR is close to the models: Dvořák 9/i 64% (67 to 43 bars), Beethoven 9/i 60% (63 to 38 bars).
- **Crux:** ours falls at S (139), which is the normative place H&D give.
- **Fix:** none. Do not restore 19–22 (cycle 38's phrase-correspondence reason still holds).

**3. 152–153: spell the passing note D♭, not C♯ (applied).**
- **Problem:** the literal transposition of 60–61 puts the motto's tail D–D♭–C (2̂–♭2̂–1̂) at its original pitch inside the C-major tune. It is spelled `cis'''`, which hides the reference from players and readers.
- **Spelling rule:** a descending chromatic passing note takes a flat (Gould, *Behind Bars*, accidentals chapter; not re-fetched). Here it also lies over B♭ → F minor, where D♭ is diatonic. The exposition keeps a'' because the correct spelling there would be B𝄫.
- **Answer to the protected "absence of D♭ in 139–162" (cycle 60):** its reason was no D♭ harmony or key in the recapitulation. The sound here is unchanged; only the spelling is.
- **Fix:** in recTwo.ily, `recTwoMelodyB` `d'''2.~( d'''8 des'''8 | c'''2 …` and recTwoVlnTwo `d''2.~( d''8 des''8 | c''2 …`, plus a header comment.

**4. 156–158: keep the only S deviations.**
- **What they are:** the 2-bar insertion (V–♭VI fff, then ii–V) is followed by a deceptive arrival on vi at 158 with 6̂–♯5̂. Together they form two evaded cadences before the ESC at 162.
- **Why keep:** this is the one place where the S zone is longer than in the exposition (24 against 22 bars). It answers the exposition's modest S peak (f at 64) and recalls the exposition's key, A♭, as the climax chord.
- **Fix:** none.

**5. 73–76 → 171–177: the exposition's open end is answered.**
- **The open end:** the flute's motto at 73–76 is an exact F-minor (iv) transposition (C–A♭–G–G♭–F) that stops on F.
- **The answer:** the coda's motto completes it on C (175). The coda's plagal harmony at 163–165 matches this iv→i relation.
- **Fix:** none.
