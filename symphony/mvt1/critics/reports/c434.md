### Record correction (no score change): bar 122 has single horns, not horn pairs

c234, c324 and c414 list bar 122 under "with the whole brass, double the horns" (PDF 102) and describe it as "122 with horn pairs" in their closing lines. The score has four different horn notes at 122: Hn 1 A♭4, Hn 2 F4, Hn 3 B3, Hn 4 G3 (score page 12, Hn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 staves; full.midi). Each of them doubles a trumpet or trombone: Hn 1 with Tpt 1 on A♭4, Hn 3 with Tpt 3 on B3, Hn 2 an octave above Tbn 2's F3, Hn 4 an octave above the bass trombone's G2. That is Rimsky's duplication, a horn chord beside the same chord in trumpets or trombones (PDF 103–104), not horn pairs. It still follows the reading. PDF 103's other method, horns marked a degree louder, cannot apply at fff. In brass weight (1 trumpet = 1 trombone = 2 horns, PDF 51), the fifth D has the least, only Tpt 2's low D4. Of the five notes of a V♭9 the fifth is the one to leave light (PDF 102: in five- to seven-part harmony some notes are left out). The low trumpets at 122 are protected (c244, c264, c284, c374: Adler adler2 PDF 15, 17). Keep the bar. The composer should use the wording "122: single horns duplicating trumpets and trombones" in later records.

### Kept after checking against the reading

- **140.3–142.4, Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 in unison on D♭4–C4–B3** (PDF 104). This is ten beats at f into the ff stroke, the longest trumpet–trombone unison in the movement. c324 put Tpt 3 on D♭ at 140.3 because no trumpet had the root, and c414 kept Tbn 1's C4–B3 at 142 (do-not-touch). I tried other notes for Tpt 3. It cannot take F4 or A♭4 at 140.3–141.2, because those are Tpt 2's and Tpt 1's notes, which would bring back c324's trumpet unison. It cannot take A♭3 at 141.3–142, because that is the trumpet's weak low register (Adler adler2 PDF 15) on the inner line that c414 just cleared. The 139–142 brass block is protected (c417). Keep.
- **162, Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 on C4 for the whole bar, ff** (PDF 104). This is the deceptive cadence. Tbn 1 resolves B3→C4 and Tpt 3 D4→C4, so the third of vi is doubled, which is the normal voice leading out of V7 (c324). Moving Tpt 3 to E4 makes a unison with Tpt 2; moving it to A3 puts it in the low register. Keep.
- **24.3–25.1 and 136.3–137.1, E♭4 in Cl 1, Cl 2, Hn 2, Vln II and violas.** Since c422 five parts share one pitch while the fifth G sits in Hn 1 G4, Hn 4 G2 and the tune. PDF 104 says a doubled middle note is offset by the mixed timbres. Here two clarinets, a horn and two string sections share it, and the 2 Hn + 2 Cl unison is one of PDF 106's listed combinations. Giving Cl 1 G4 back would undo c422's fix of the rising seventh. The bars last one beat and a half before the stop chord. Keep.
- **12, 27–32, 69–72, 82, 117–124, 131–134, 158–161, 167–170, 181–182.** These are protected or quoted. Every scan hit inside them was already decided by c234, c324, c414 or the passages' own protections, and nothing in this reading answers those reasons.
