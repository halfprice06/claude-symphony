## Reading notes

## What I studied

MOLA in full, as above. To test the score against these principles I wrote three LilyPond engravers in /tmp/critic-168/chk. They run over each single-player variable, the way MOLA says parts are made (PDF 7). They log, per player: entries after rests, the dynamic in force at each entry, hairpin endings, and dynamics, hairpins and articulations inside unison and octave doublings. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 89–105 (MusicXML, `musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`). At 90–101 the Fagotti, Corni I–IV and Basso under the con sordino second theme carry the same swells bar for bar: pp<mp>, then p<mf>. Dvořák 7/i (Mutopia) keeps a full dynamic line in each bassoon part. Web: Adam Silverman's copying guidelines (https://adambsilverman.com/?p=1447) say dynamics go "under each instrument's first note and under the first note of each new phrase that follows a rest of more than a single bar" and "never appear under rests or otherwise empty bars"; a MuseScore forum thread (https://musescore.org/en/node/313020) gives the player's reason. Other books: Adler adler1 PDF 226 (bassoon doubling cellos), as a check on finding 2.

General verdict: the dynamics are thorough. Only five entries after rests lack a level, and the articulation in doublings is consistent except at one note. What remains is small, and most of it concerns the parts.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 60–68, Bsn 2: the dynamics stay at pp while the bassoon doubles the bass through a full swell.** Applied.
Bsn 2 enters at 60 (c167) and doubles the Cb's arco roots two octaves up, note for note (G♭–E♭, F♭, E♭, E♭–E♮, F, D♭, B♭–E♭). The Cb has p< at 60, sub. p at 62, < at 63, mf at 64, > at 66 and p at 68. Bsn 2 has a single pp at 60 and nothing after it. Read as its own part (MOLA PDF 7) and proofread horizontally (PDF 8), the line ignores the phrase's climax at 64. Tchaikovsky 6/i 90–101 gives the bassoons the same hairpins as the basses and horns they double. Fix, in tTwo.ily (Bsn 1 rests, so there is no shared-staff conflict; one level under the Cb at 64, the same as Hn 3·4):
`ges2\p\< es2 | fes1 | es2.\subP r4 | es2\< e2 | f1\mp | des1 | bes,2 es2~\> | es1 | f2.\p r4 |`

**2. Bars 103–106, Bsn 1·2 f: Bsn 1 doubles the mf cellos in unison.** Applied (closes the open item from cycle 158).
Bsn 1 has the second-theme head in unison with the Vc (mf), an octave under Hn 1 (mf). Bsn 1 is marked f, and Bsn 2 also has to be f because they share a staff. In MOLA Ex. 6, all doubled parts carry the same level and the same sf. In Tchaikovsky 6/i 90–101, horns and bassoons have identical marks even though horns are stronger, so the level is not used to balance them. Adler (adler1 PDF 226) writes that when the bassoon doubles the cellos "the cello tone predominates, but the doubling bassoon or bassoons give added body to the sound even as they are absorbed in the whole". The bassoon is there to add body, not to stand out, so it does not need a louder mark than the cellos. The bass keeps its weight: B.cl f and Cb pizz. f stay unchanged. Fix in dev.ily: Bsn 1 `f2.~(\mf`, Bsn 2 `d,1\mf`.

**3. Bars 69, 107, 131, 135, 137: dynamics written under rests in single-player lines.** Applied for 7 marks.
In MOLA Ex. 3, each dynamic sits under the note it applies to. Silverman's rule is that a dynamic never goes under a rest. The exposition writes the first theme's ff on the note (tOne Vln I `r8 g''8\downbow\ff(`). The recapitulation puts it on the rest: Vln I and Vln II `r4\ff es'''2->` at 131 and 137, Vln II `r8\f g'8` at 135, Vla `r8\ff` and `r8\f` at 131 and 135. The same happens with B.cl `r8\p` at 107 and Cl 1·2 `r8\f` at 69. Fix: move each one onto the next note (`r4 es'''2->\ff\downbow`, `r8 g'8\f`, `r8 b,8(\p`, and so on). I left alone the 13 marks on shared staves where the other player attacks on the downbeat (Fl/Ob at 131, 139, etc.): there the mark belongs to that player's note, and moving one copy would print two dynamics.

**4. Bar 139, Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2: no crescendo into the ff at 140.** Applied.
All three first players, and Bsn 2, have f< from the eighth-note pickup to ff at 140. The three second players go from f straight to ff with no hairpin. The score shows the first player's hairpin, but the second players' parts would show an unprepared jump. Fix in recOne.ily, so the hairpin starts at the same moment as the first player's: `\after 8 \< aes''2\f` (Fl 2), `\after 8 \< c''1\f` (Ob 2), `\after 8 \< aes'1\f` (Cl 2). The score still prints a single hairpin, with no conflict warning.

**5. Bar 101, cor anglais: no tenuto on the appoggiatura.** Applied.
Cycle 148 put a tenuto on the B♭ at 101 in Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II. The C.a. doubles the same line and was missed. This was the only articulation mismatch the script found in any doubling. The cycle-88 rule and MOLA Ex. 6 both call for the same marks. Fix: `bes'2-- a'2)` in devCorAnglais.

**6. Bar 67, Hn 4: re-entry without a level.** Applied as a part-only mark.
Hn 4 rests at 66 and re-enters at 67 inside Hn 3's diminuendo, so its part shows no level (the last one was mp at 64). MOLA Ex. 3 puts a dynamic on every entry after rests. A printed mp in the score would interrupt Hn 3's hairpin, so I wrote it as a part-only mark: `bes1-\tag #'part \mp-\tag #'part \>` in tTwo.ily, with `\removeWithTag #'part \hnFour` on the Hn 3·4 staff in score.ly (the same mechanism as the C.a. cues). The score is unchanged.

**7. Bars 181–182, Vln II: the tie collides with the hairpin and the ppp.** Applied.
At 300 dpi, the G4 tie runs below the note through the diminuendo hairpin and into the ppp. This is the kind of collision MOLA Ex. 7 shows. Fix: `g'1^~\>` (tie above) in codaVlnTwo. This is a layout change only; the protected coda's notes, dynamics and ties are unchanged.

**8. Bars 127 and 182: one instruction in two languages.** Not applied.
The tam-tam's "damp" (127) and the harp's "étouffez" (62, 163, 164, 182) are the same instruction in English and French, and "étouffez with Cb." (182) mixes the two in one phrase. MOLA PDF 4 asks for a conventional language. Suggested fix: keep "étouffez" (the standard harp term), change 127 to `R1^\markup \small \italic "étouffez"`, and change 182 to "étouffez (avec Cb.)". I did not apply this because 182 is inside the protected coda and the change is only text.

Not raised: the C.a. re-enters at 149 and 151 without restating p (protected "plain p", and only a one-bar gap); the timpani at 30 and 32 inside a ff tutti.
