## Findings

### F1. Every accent off the downbeat is a syncopation, a group start or a stroke (kept)

**Checked.** A&S PDF 51: a rhythmic accent at a metrically weak place is syncopation, and the composer marks it (Ex. 3-6, sf on the tied-over note). PDF 56: anticipations are unaccented. The script found 662 accent marks. I listed the ones that do not fall on beat 1 of a bar.

**Found.** They fall into four kinds, and there are no others:
- The dotted crotchet of the P head on beat 2 or 3, held across the half-bar (Ex. 3-6 type). This covers every P statement: 15–21 (Vln I), 27–30, 33–39 (Bsn 1, Vla, Vln I), 69–71 (codetta), 77–82 (dialogue), 85–96 (each fugato entry, two accents each, on beats 2 and 3), 103–109, 117–120, 127–128, 131–133, 139–144, 167–169. The pattern is the same in every statement: an accent on bars 1 and 3 of the head at beat 2, on bar 2 at beat 3, and no accent in the cadence bar (18, 22, 134, 170).
- The starts of the three-crotchet groups at 23.4, 24.3, 25.2 and 135.4, 136.3, 137.2 (Ex. 3-8 type; see Reading notes 8).
- The beat-2 strokes at 32, 72, 83 and 142, which are short chords on beats, not off-beats.
- The B.cl's P head at 107.2 and 109.2.

No accent or new dynamic falls on an anticipation (3.4, 12.4, 22.2, 52.4, 152.4) or on a short passing note. The 103 tenuto marks fall in four places, and none is on a short note on a weak beat:
- the S head's syncopated minim on beat 2 of its second bar (48, 51, 57, 62, 70, 99, 102, 104, 148, 151, 158, 160, 172; c268);
- the P head's crotchet on the downbeat of its second bar (`c4--` at 16, 28, 34–36, 104, 128, 132, 144, 168);
- accented dissonances: the restruck suspension at 45 and the appoggiaturas at 59 and 101 (PDF 54–55);
- long notes that begin on beat 1 or 3 (78, 79.3, 108, 110, 161).

**Keep.**

### F4. 165–167: c368's reason for keeping the rit. into Tempo dell'Allegro was worded wrongly; the passage is right (records only)

**What c368 wrote.** In its "considered and kept" list, c368 kept 165–167 because "a crescendo into a slower arrival is an allargando into the coda". The arrival at 167 is not slower. global.ily slows to crotchet = 81 on 166.3–4 and then jumps to 126 at 167, which is 55% faster. That is the same kind of jump that c368 removed at 68–69.

**Why it is still right.** A&S PDF 52 (Ex. 3-9b, 3-11): a group that begins on a weak beat belongs to the strong beat it leads to, so a tempo change belongs where that group begins. At 68 the upbeat group was a semiquaver run (Vln I and flutes, 68.3–4) that had to be played in the new tempo, so the change was moved onto it. At 166 there is no such group. Beats 3–4 hold a chord (Vc E♭3, Vln II C4, Hn 3·4, Vla thirds, Cb C2) under a crescendo. The coda's P head brings its own pickup, the G on 167.1.5, inside the new tempo bar. So the group that "Tempo dell'Allegro" governs begins at 167, where the word is printed. A held chord that slows and swells, followed by a tempo on the next downbeat, is a common Romantic way into a new section. The jump to a faster tempo is natural here because nothing is played across the bar line.

**Fix.** No score change. For HISTORY.md: "165–167 kept because the upbeat at 166.3 is a held chord and the P head's pickup is inside 167 (A&S PDF 52)", replacing c368's wording.

### F5. Candidate improvements tested against the reading and the masters, and rejected

The task asks where, within this lens, the music falls short of a master's work. These are the candidates I weighed. None meets the bar.

- **A hairpin over each three-beat group at 23–25 and 135–137, as Brahms marks each five-beat group in Ex. 3-7 (PDF 52).** Rejected. Brahms's hairpins shape a soft (p, espressivo) cross-metre that does not lead anywhere. Here the four groups climb E♭–F–G–A♭ to the leading tone and the ff stop chord. One long crescendo, with accents on the group starts (the Ex. 3-8 model, fp on each group start), keeps both the grouping and the goal. Hairpins on each group would make three small dips inside a build that the protected stop chord at 26/138 needs (c7, c17, c391, c422).
- **"poco sostenuto" or "tranquillo" at 107–110, the only soft passage of the development.** Rejected. A&S PDF 49: tempo is heard from the rate of chord change and of the main melodic tones. Here the harmony changes once a bar and the B.cl and violas move in dotted minims, so at crotchet 126 the passage already sounds about half as fast as the fugato. A printed slowing would also have to be undone at 111, where the wedge needs the full tempo for its crescendo into 117.
- **A "rubato" or "con moto" word on the S theme at 47 and 147.** Rejected. PDF 47 (footnote): small departures from strict time are the rule in performance and need no mark. The score already prints "dolce", the per-bar swells, the S tenutos, poco rit. at 45–46/145–146 and Poco meno mosso. A rubato word would add only what the player already does and what these marks already ask for.
- **A crescendo or hairpin shape inside the codetta 69–72, which stays at f.** Rejected. The four-bar group ends on the tonic in its weak fourth bar (PDF 54, Ex. 3-13 type). The trombones and tuba enter f at 72, and the timpani play mf< at 71 into f at 72. That is a crescendo by adding instruments (Rimsky-Korsakov, rimsky-principles PDF 130), and with the beat-2 accents on 72 it gives the cadence its weight. A printed crescendo on top would make 72 louder than the ff climax at 82 that it prepares.
- **An extra accent or sf on the D♭6 syncopation at 20 in Vln II and violas.** Rejected. Vln II already has sf at 20.3 and the violas reach f at 20. PDF 50: a very high, chromatic, long note already carries a built-in accent three ways.
