## What I studied
- All nine section files of v66, score.ly (the harp is a PianoStaff with `harpR` on a treble staff and `harpL` on a bass staff), global.ily (`pedAflat` = D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭, `pedNat` = all naturals), score.pdf pages 5, 14 and 15, and HISTORY.md.
- Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`), exposition second theme bars 89–92 compared with the recapitulation, bars 305–308. Dvořák 9/i (`Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`), exposition flute theme bars 149–156. The corpus has no harp parts: Tchaikovsky 6 and Dvořák 9 have no harp in the first movement. I could not line up the recapitulation bar numbers of this MuseScore edition of Dvořák 9/i reliably, so I do not cite them.
- Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, harp section (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): "only four notes at the most can be played by each hand", "the notes of a chord should be written close together, with not too great a space between one hand and the other", "rapid figures can only be realised clearly and neatly in the upper register", and in the low octaves "the resonance … is slightly prolonged". Berklee, "Writing for the Harp" (https://www.berklee.edu/node/2392666): no more than four notes per hand, span no more than an octave and a fourth per hand, and fast low left-hand figures "will sound muddy and inarticulate". Timbre & Orchestration, harp scoring (https://timbreandorchestration.org/isfee/extreme-orchestration/harp/scoring): a single arpeggio line may be split between the hands in groups of four or fewer, and figures that keep one direction are easier. I found no online text of Adler's harp chapter; only catalogue entries came up.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Harp 56–68: one-hand figure in the low register, an octave below the cello (applied)
**Problem.** At 56–68 the harp plays the cello's figure note for note an octave lower (for example bar 56: harp `aes,,8 es, aes, c fes2`, cello `aes,8 es aes c' e'2`), all on the lower staff, between A♭1 and B♭3. This register is the one Rimsky-Korsakov and Berklee describe as resonant but unclear, and it lies on top of the arco double-bass A♭1. The harp adds no colour of its own here, after playing a distinct high-register figure at 47–55.
**Fix (in /tmp/critic-047/work/sections/tTwo.ily).**
- In one-chord bars, the left hand plays a bass octave on beat 1 and the right hand plays a four-note broken chord on beats 3–4. This fills the half note the cello holds, so the two parts interlock instead of doubling.
- In two-chord bars, both hands play rolled chords.
- At 62–63, where the cello is pizzicato, one rising line passes from the left hand (beats 1–2) to the right hand (beats 3–4).

### 2. Harp 148–162: unison doubling of the cello, and the same figure as the exposition (applied)
**Problem.** In v66 the recapitulation harp is the cello's line at the same pitch (148 `c8 g c' e' g'2` in both parts), except that at 149–150 the cello descends and the harp rises. So the harp's first entry in the recapitulation adds nothing that can be heard.
**Fix (in /tmp/critic-047/work/sections/recTwo.ily: new `recTwoHarpR`, rewritten `recTwoHarpL`).**
- One rolled chord per harmony on beat 1 in the upper register, over a bass octave.
- This is a different figure type from the exposition's broken eighths, and it leaves beats 3–4 to the viola fills of 148–151, so the cello (beats 1–2), the harp (beat 1) and the violas (beats 3–4) interlock.
- No chord at 151 contains B, and there is no chord on beat 3 of 160, so the suspensions c'''–b'' in the tune are not doubled at their resolution.
- 162 has no third in the chord, so nothing rings into the E♮→E♭ turn.

### 3. Recapitulation 158–161: violas copy the exposition's 62–67 syncopation exactly (applied)
**Problem.** In v66, recTwoSync at 158–161 is `r4 <c' e'>2 <c' e'>4` (four bars), the same rhythm and voicing as the violas at 62–67. These bars are not protected.
**Evidence.** In Tchaikovsky 6/i the exposition second theme (bars 89–92) is accompanied by syncopated horns over a D pedal. In the recapitulation (305–308) violas and cellos play a new rising stepwise eighth line and the woodwinds add a counter-line. The figure changes; the harmony does not.
**Fix.**
- 158–161 reuse this recapitulation's own fill figure from 148–151 (`r4 x4~ x8 x8 x8 x8`).
- The harmony of 158–161 (a–F–d/G–G7) echoes 148–151 (C–F–d–G7), so the fills frame the climax at both ends.

```
r4 e'4~\> e'8 d'8( c'8 b8) | r4 c'4~ c'8 a8( c'8 f'8) | r4 f'4~ f'8 e'8( d'8 g8) | r4 g4~ g8 b8( d'8 b8) | <e' g'>2\mp <es' g'>2\< |
```

**Checks.**
- In 158, b on the last eighth sits under g♯'' in the tune (E-major colour). No B sounds against the suspended c''' at 160.
- The last eighth of 161 is b, not f'. With f' the violas made unison f'→e' with Cl1 and Hn1.
- The all-voice check (tools/critic-scripts/vl.py on a fresh all.midi) shows no new parallels.

### 4. Survey: accompaniment figure types and where they recur (information, no change needed)
I count 15 distinct types:

1. Tied off-beat syncopation `r8 x8~ x4`: Vln II/Vla 15–22 only.
2. Long-short bass `x4 r8 x8`: Cb 15–22; Tpt/Timp 27–31; Timp 69, 123; Cb 131, 135.
3. Repeated bass or chord eighths: Cb 27–31, 82, 123–126, 132–134, 163–165; Vla 69–71.
4. Bowed tremolo pads: 37–42, 77–81, 103–108, 109–115, 139–147 (sul tasto), 163–166.
5. Sustained wind pads: throughout.
6. Pizzicato bass on beat 1: Cb 47–55, 85–87, 139–147; Vc 62–63.
7. Rising broken chord of four eighths plus a half note: harp 47–55; Vc 56–68 and 148–162.
8. Half-bar syncopation `r4 x2 x4`: Vla 56–59 and 62–67.
9. Rising four-eighth burst `r8 x x x x4 r4`: Vla 60–61 and 152–153.
10. Eighth fill `r4 x4~ x8 x x x`: Vla 148–151 (now also 158–161).
11. Afterbeat eighths: Vla 123–126, 135.
12. Neighbour-note eighths: Vla 103–108, 131–134.
13. Broken-chord eighths: Vla 97–102.
14. Triplet arpeggios: Vla 109–113.

Apart from the second group, no figure returns unchanged in a structurally parallel place. Variety is at the level of the models. With findings 1–3, the recapitulation's second group now differs from the exposition's in harp, violas and the use of the cello part, while the protected 139–147 and 154–157 are untouched.
