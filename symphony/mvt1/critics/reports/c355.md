## Findings

### 1. Bar 158, Hn 1·2: "a2" is printed over the second note, so the downbeat G3 reads as Horn 1 alone (applied)
**What is wrong.** At K both horns play the same line from beat 1: G3 crotchet, then E4 tied into 159 (`g4\ff e'2.--~` in both recTwoHnOne and recTwoHnTwo). The part-combiner prints the G3 with one stem and no label, then "a2" over the E4. A conductor reading the page sees Hn 1 alone on the G3 and both horns joining on beat 2. MOLA asks for the score to be proofread vertically (PDF 8), and Ex. 6 (PDF 11) puts "a2" over the first note of a unison entry (Fl. II and Fag. at bar 851). Gould (PDF 61, 65) gives the same rule. Hn 3·4 on the next staff were fixed for the same fault at c255 with `\partCombineUnisono`. This is the climax arrival, and it is the bar where a conductor most needs to know that both horns are on the line.
**Fix (sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoHnOne):** `\partCombineUnisono g4\ff e'2.--~ | \partCombineAutomatic e'4\< ...`. The score now shows one "a2" on beat 1 of 158, and the rest of the bar does not change. No pitch or rhythm changes. The parts are unaffected, because they drop the partCombine commands (c295).

### 2. Bar 146, Hn 3·4: Horn 4's whole-bar rest is printed above Horn 3's held B3 (applied)
**What is wrong.** At 145 Hn 4 holds G3 and Hn 3 enters on beat 3 with B3, labelled "3.", which it holds through 146 (c347/c348). At 146 the combiner prints Hn 4's whole-bar rest on the fourth line, above the B3, which sits one ledger line below the stave. In a two-part stave a rest above the notes belongs to the upper part (Gould PDF 49–51). So the bar reads as if Horn 3 rests and the B3 is Horn 4's, the reverse of the truth. This is the kind of misplaced anchored element MOLA PDF 8 (Ex. 7) warns about after automatic layout. The "3." label at 145 already names the player, so the score needs no rest for Horn 4 here.
**Fix (sections/recOne.ily, recOneHnFour):** `\tag #'score { \once \omit MultiMeasureRest } R1` at 146. The score shows the B3 alone under its "3." label. The Horn 4 part keeps its rest, because parts-lib.ily drops `\tag #'score` material. No notes change.

### 3. Violin I part: no cue before the last motto at 177 (applied, parts only)
**What is wrong.** Vln I rests 171–176 at Più lento (crotchet = 72), about 20 seconds, longer than the 8-bar Allegro rests (about 15 s) that c265 and c295 cued. It then enters alone, pp espr., on the pickup G4 into the final motto, inside a rit. MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest … Cues must be audible and obvious"; Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows a "Viol. I" cue just before an entry after a tempo change. The player has a multi-bar rest "6" and nothing to hear in it. The clarinet's horn-call recall at 175–176 (E♭4, then C5) is the clearest thing in those bars and lies in the violin's register.
**Fix (parts-gen.py, Vln I cues):** add `CL+("DOWN",175,2)`. The part now shows "4 (171–174)", then a small-note "Cl. 1" cue over 175–176, then the entry. The part is still 2 pages, and its last page is still even. The reflow also clears the near-touch between "Tempo I (Lento)" and "change bow at will" that c265 noted: "attacca" now sits under "change bow at will" at 182.

### 4. Front page: the cor anglais is listed without its key (applied)
**What is wrong.** MOLA PDF 6 asks the front matter to "identify … keys of all transposing instruments". The instrumentation lists "2 Clarinets in B♭", "Bass clarinet in B♭", "4 Horns in F" and "3 Trumpets in C", but only "Cor anglais", although the cor anglais part is written in F. The score is in C, so a librarian checking the part against the front page needs to see its key.
**Fix (score.ly):** `\line { "Cor anglais in F" }`.
