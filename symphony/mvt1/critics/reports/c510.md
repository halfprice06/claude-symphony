## Findings

Ranked by how much each bears on the four-movement design. All are verdicts to keep the score as it is, with the reason from the reading, or record items. I looked for a change that would make movement I set up the later movements better and found none that meets the bar; finding 3 and finding 5 are the two candidates I examined most closely and rejected.

### 1. Withheld material and reserved colours hold (whole movement). Keep

Adler's two colour lessons apply across the symphony. PDF 263: the slow movement's solo colour must be "very fresh, even after the first movement has been heard". PDF 267: Mozart "uses no orchestral flutes in this work, so that the solo flute will not encounter any color competition." Movement I must not use up what the later movements present as new.

Checks on v510 (scratch build, full.midi):
- Violins' highest note is A♭6 (MIDI 92, Vln I); Vln II's highest is C6. No C7 in the violins. The peak ladder A♭6 – B♭6 – B6 – C7 holds.
- No trumpet melody and no soft trumpet line, so IV's undoubled trumpet solo F2 (IV 51) meets no competition, as Mozart's flute does not.
- No clashed cymbal, no muted or stopped brass, no tuba melody, no four-horn high unison, no full-brass forte C-major chord: the v501 list in mvt1.md still describes v510.
- No hint of F1 or F2. c260 and c330 rejected seeding them; PDF 263 and 267 give the reason to keep that.
- Theme record: `themecheck.py themes-mvt1.ly v510 --beats 4` gives 35/35 unchanged.
- The source diff v501→v510 has three note or articulation changes, all in non-quoted parts: violas' 157 arpeggio (c502), Vc/Bsn 1 slur at 141–142 (c505), Hn 2/Hn 4 mf> at 163 (c508). None touches 1–14, 26–32, 138, 170 or 171–182.

Fix: none.

### 2. The attacca handover 182 → II 1 is the reading's "lead the soloist to the first pitch". Keep

PDF 275: "it is essential to lead a singer to his first pitch". The five tied parts (Vln I C5, Vln II G4, violas E4, cellos C3, Hn 3 C4) give movement II its first chord, and everything that would ring against II's A♭ bass at II 2 (basses, Hn 4, timpani, harp étouffez, tam-tam damped) stops at the end of 182. Movement II then follows PDF 263–264 exactly: strings, then the wind choir (B.cl, Cl 2, Hn 3·4) at II 3–6, then Hn 1's solo at II 7, as Dvořák and Tchaikovsky open their slow movements with the winds before the soloist (Ex. XVII-7, -8). The same device, a held horn note carrying the attacca, ends Tchaikovsky's Canzonetta (corpus: mutopia/TchaikovskyPI_O35/.../mvmt2/corniF.ily, last two bars, horns' held B pp under a fermata; contrabasso.ily line 64 "Attacca subito").

Fix: none. MIDI of 170–182 is unchanged since v401.

### 3. Horn call 13–14 sits inside the string chord. Examined; keep

This is the one place where the reading could argue for a change. The call that the scherzo opens with (III 3–6) and that the finale resolves (IV 243–246) is heard alone only here. At 13–14 the accompaniment holds V7 around the horn: cellos G3 below, violas C4/E♭4 then D4, Vln II G4 then F4 above. The horn's C4 and B3 lie a second under the violas. PDF 274 asks to "keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible".

I keep it, for three reasons from the same pages. (a) PDF 274 itself says "rhythmic contrast lessens the importance of this consideration": the strings hold and fade p > pp while only the horn moves. (b) The colour contrast is the strongest in the orchestra (horn against strings), the case Adler calls safe on PDF 264 (Mozart's horn against the first violins). (c) PDF 275: the violas' C4 on 13.1–2, leaving for D4 as the horn arrives on C4, is a point of pitch reference, not a doubling (c190). Moving the violas or Vln II out of the horn's octave would also leave the V7 without its seventh and fifth in the middle register, and 12–13 is quoted by the finale's first bars (Hn 3 F♯3→G3, violas E♭4 at 12). Mozart's rule is met: Hn 3 releases G3 on 13.1 before the call enters on beat 2, and Hn 2–4 are silent (Ex. XVII-9, PDF 264).

Fix: none.

### 4. Coda 171–182: duet, obbligato, chorale canon, pulse. Keep

Each part of the coda matches one of Adler's models, and each is what a later movement answers:
- 171–174: solo cello against the cor anglais in long notes (Beethoven Ex. XVII-15, PDF 274), answered by movement II's solo violin and Ob 1 at II 15–22.
- 175–177: Cl 1's horn-call recall as an obbligato over the held chorale (Verdi, PDF 278).
- 175–181: Fidelio-type canon (PDF 283), with the bass doubled in two octaves; the finale takes it over in major at IV 224–230.
- 179–180: the "quietly pulsating timpani" under the solo line (Bartók, PDF 270), which also "plays the motive that joins the two phrases" (Beethoven, PDF 273). It is motive x with its first quaver silent; III 9 and IV 9, 229 carry it on.

The one unison of the two soloists (C5 at 174) remains as c310 recorded it. It is the motto's last note against the cello's 6/4 and is protected.

Fix: none.

### 5. Should the horn call's coda recall go to a horn? Examined; keep Cl 1

PDF 257–258 (dialogue) values implanting a soloist's timbre. One could argue that the call should end movement I in its own colour, Hn 1, since the horn is the symphony's cyclic voice (II 7 Hn 1, III 1–6 Hn 1, IV 243 horns). I reject this. PDF 257 also asks that the return come "in a radically different orchestral color", and PDF 263 asks that the next movement's solo colour be fresh. Movement II's first melody is Hn 1 alone, about a minute after I 175 (I 175–182 plus II 1–6 at crotchet = 50–72). A horn solo at 175–177 would take that freshness away. Cl 1 is the right colour, and Hn 3·4 enter only as a ppp chord at 181.

Fix: none (c298, c470 keep the same reading).

### 6. Three A♭ climaxes and the violins' octave doubling. Keep

Bizet doubles the singer with the first violins an octave higher "only when the singer reaches her dramatic climax" (PDF 279). Movement I keeps the octave-doubled S for the climax at 158–161, after the horn+cello (47), violin (56) and horn+viola (147) statements. The three climaxes land on A♭ (82, 122, 160), which is movement II's key. The fff marks are only at 122 and 160, under the scherzo's single fff and the finale's fff and C7. This is the right rising order across the symphony.

Fix: none.
