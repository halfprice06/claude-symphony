## Findings

The movement follows every principle in the reading that applies to it. I found no change that would bring it closer to a master's work and would also survive the protections in HISTORY.md. The findings below are ranked by how much each passage decides the movement's form. Each one says what I checked, what I considered, and why it should stay as it is. Nothing was applied, so the scratch copy is v303 unchanged.

### 1. The key plan realises the III–VI–II–V–I chain. Keep. (whole movement)
A&S PDF 231 describes III as the midpoint of I–III–V, with VI as another route from I to V. PDF 236–237 (Ex. 15-9, Handel) describes the chain III–VI–II–V–I, in which VI is subordinate and II comes in 6/3 position so the bass can step up to V. The movement's main bass notes are: C at 15; E♭ at 36 and 41–43 (III); A♭ at 47–76 (VI, the key area); D at 103 (ii, the D-minor combination of heads); F at 111 (iv, which here does the work of II6); G at 117–130 (V); C at 131. That is the chain at the scale of the whole movement. The development's other keys fit inside it: B at 97, D at 103, F at 111 and A♭ at 116/122 spell out vii°7 of C over V (c292).

The second key area is VI, not the III that A&S call the normal choice in minor (PDF 232). That choice is justified. Beethoven 9/i and Dvořák 7/i also put the second group in VI (corpus: reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Flauto_I_II.ly, B♭-major material from Mark D, m. 73). Mozart 25/i and Tchaikovsky 6/i use III. The symphony's cycle of major thirds C–A♭–E–C depends on A♭. III still appears, as the stopover the reading describes. Fix: none.

### 2. Transition 33–46: III reached through natural VII, then turned into V of VI. Keep.
At 35, B♭ minor becomes B♭7 on beat 4 (viola D♭4 then D4, c142) and resolves to E♭ at 36. The B♭ pedal at 39–40 then reaches E♭ again at 41. This is natural VII as V of III (PDF 231, 240, Ex. 15-1 and 15-13), with its applied chords using no extra accidentals (PDF 233, Ex. 15-4). From 43, E♭ takes D♭ and becomes V7 of A♭ (V4/2 at 44). III is therefore the stopover described on PDF 232, and the medial caesura at 41–43 is a half cadence in the new key.

I considered whether the perfect cadence in E♭ at 36 weakens the sf arrival at 41. One option was to put E♭ in first inversion at 36 (bass G), so that root-position E♭ is held back for 41. I decided against it, for three reasons. First, 36 is p and ends the bassoon's four-bar motto phrase. Second, 37 turns E♭ minor at once (E♭–G♭–B♭–D♭), so the music does not settle there. Third, the pair 36/41 states one goal twice, quietly and then with force, which is the reading's point about one melodic segment heard in two key areas (PDF 232, exercise 6 on PDF 247). The passage is also protected for its route and the bassoon's notes (c253, c281 and earlier). The timing is right: 33–46 lasts 27 s, against 34 s for P and 47 s for S. Fix: none.

### 3. Development: the B♭→B shift at 96→97 uses natural VII as A&S describe. Keep.
The fugato's falling fifths end on B♭ minor at 94–96, which is natural VII. A&S say natural VII normally leads to III, or to V through a chromatic inflection of 7̂, and that this sounds "unexpected and, in retrospect, logical" (PDF 241, Ex. 15-14). At 97 the bass inflects B♭ to B, but B becomes the root of the ff B-minor statement of S rather than the third of V. This B is the first pillar of the vii°7 plan, and it reaches C only at the recapitulation (131). At movement scale this is the "unexpected but logical" inflection. Between 77 and 116 the development passes through about ten local keys in 76 seconds. Four pillars anchor them (A♭ at 77–82, B at 97, D at 103, F at 111), so the listener is not lost. Fix: none (key plan protected: c233, c263, c292).

### 4. Return to the tonic: a long root-position V pedal, with the contrapuntal III→V4/3→I form kept for the coda. Keep.
A&S show the return from a III key area through an inverted dominant (Mozart K. 511, Ex. 15-7, PDF 235). They add that inverted dominants are not goals and that V does not always need emphasis (PDF 234, 236). This movement does the opposite at 117–130: 14 bars and 29 s of root-position V, with the fff climax at 122. That is the right choice here, because the reprise is the movement's main structural arrival and needs a goal-directed dominant, as in Tchaikovsky 6/i and Brahms 1/i. The contrapuntal form the reading describes is used where it fits: in the coda, 175–178 runs i6/4 – i6 – V4/3 – i over the motto bass (G–E♭–D–C), with no root-position V–i. That gives the attacca the open, melodic close it needs (PDF 235–236), and the structural cadences stay at 142–143 and 165–166. Fix: none (c223, c293).

### 5. Recapitulation 156–159: ♭VII – iv – cadential 6/4 – V7, as in Ex. 15-15/15-16. Keep the root-position iv.
At 156 the bass B♭ carries ♭VII (Vln I D6, harp B♭–F). At 157 it falls to F (iv, under the violins' C6–B♭5–A♭5–G5), and 158–159 bring the cadential 6/4 and V7. Hn 4 holds F3, the fifth of ♭VII, through 156–157, and it returns as F4, the seventh of V7, at 159. A&S describe exactly this: "the 5th of natural VII (4̂) forms a good preparation for the 7th of V7" (PDF 242). Ex. 15-15 links ♭VII to V7 through a passing IV6, which makes the bass fall by step. Here that would be B♭–A♭–G, with A♭ in the bass at 157. I tested this idea against the climax and rejected it. A♭ in the bass at 157 would sound the bass of the fff ♭VI three beats before 160 and weaken the deceptive arrival. Root-position iv keeps A♭ fresh. The protections on 156–157 and 158–166 (c381, c389) agree. Fix: none.

### 6. Outer-voice cross-relation 59→60 (G5 against bass G♭). Keep; it is softened in the bass line.
The cross-relation scan found Vln I·II on G5 (♮7̂ of A♭) on beats 3–4 of 59, then the bass on G♭ at 60 (Cb G♭1, Vc G♭2, harp, Bsn 2). That is the soprano/bass pair that Ex. 15-8c marks "avoid" (PDF 236). The effect is softer than in the example, because the cello's own arpeggio at 59 has G3 on the fourth quaver and moves to G♭2 at 60. The chromatic step is therefore heard inside the bass voice, and the violins leap to B♭5 rather than to G♭. The other three ways to remove it would each break something:
- changing the violins' A♭5–G5 at 59 (S restatement, protected by c273);
- giving up the G♭–F♭–E♭ bass at 60–62 (movement II's climax bass, a seed in mvt1.md);
- adding a G3→G♭3 inner line in Hn 4, which would double the leading tone of V7 (A&S PDF 84–87).

The other outer-voice cross-relations are 27→28 (D♭6 over D2) and 29→30 (E♭6 over E2) in the hammered tutti that the finale quotes, and 96→97 and 108→109, which are protected seeds. Fix: none.

### 7. Phrase rhythm: asymmetric groups are already used where they help the form. Keep.
Exercise 2 (PDF 245–246) asks for phrases not built only from twos and fours. The movement already has 3+3 at 41–46 (caesura and fill), four 3-bar fugato units at 85–96, and 5-bar phrases at 51–55, 64–68, 117–121, 122–126, 139–143, 151–155 and 162–166. The 5-bar groups come at cadences and climaxes, where an extended bar delays the goal. The 4-bar groups carry the themes. Fix: none.
