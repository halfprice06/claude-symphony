## Findings

### 1. Ob 2 at 42.3-43.1: F4-E♭4 diminuendo to p in the oboe's lowest fifth (applied in scratch)

Bars 41-43, trans.ily, transOboeTwo: `g'1\sf\>~ | g'2\mf f'2\> | es'4\p r4 r2`.

Problem. After the sf chord at 41, Ob 2 takes the lower octave of Ob 1's G5-F5-E♭5 descent and ends on a p E♭4 at 43.1. F4 and E♭4 lie in the oboe's lowest fifth. The E♭4 is a soft attack at the end of a diminuendo, and no other instrument plays E♭4 there. MIDI at 43.1: Ob 2 E♭4 is the only E♭ between Bsn 2's E♭3 and the E♭5 of Ob 1 and Fl 2. So the oboe's least controllable note is exposed at the quietest point of the transition.

Evidence. Sebesky (PDF 71): the oboe "can be a bit coarse, especially in the lowest part of the range (F down to Bb). These notes are also the most difficult to control, and unless the reed player is an accomplished oboist, it would be wise to avoid writing them." Ex. 104 (PDF 79) makes the same point about the low oboe under a flute at mf: "a very harsh sound", "making this voicing impractical". The score has already applied this rule two bars later: c274 moved the 45-46 link from Ob 1 to Cl 1 because the oboe "honks" in this register at p (Adler PDF 270). The Ob 2 E♭4 at 43 is the one soft note in that register that was left.

Answer to the earlier keep. c256 kept "Ob 2 E♭4 p at 43" under Adler's narrower rule (no pp in the lowest fifth; adler1 PDF 198). Sebesky sets a stricter limit, which is to avoid the fifth when the note is not needed. This note is not needed:
- E♭ is held by Bsn 2, Cbsn and Cb.
- The line continues in Ob 1, Fl 2 and Vln II at E♭5, and in Vln I at E♭6.

The octave-doubling rule (Sebesky PDF 42-43) is met because the line goes on in instruments of equal or greater weight.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-306/work/sections/trans.ily):

```
transOboeTwo = {
  g'8->\ff r8 r4 r2 | R1*7 | g'1\sf\>~ | g'2\mf r2\! |
  R1*4 |
}
```

Ob 2 stops on beat 3 of 42. The diminuendo to p is made partly by players dropping out, as the score already does at 116 (c179). On the shared Ob 1·2 staff, 42 shows Ob 1's F5 with Ob 2's half rest, and 43 shows "1." with Ob 1 alone (page 5, checked).
