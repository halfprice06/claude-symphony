## Findings

### 1. Bars 19–22 and 107–110: violas above Violins II (Copland, PDF 270): follows, no change

**What the reading says.** In Copland's summation (Ex. VIII-41) the violas lie above the Violins II for four bars. Adler says the seconds "therefore need the heavy doubling of the second clarinet and the muted horn".

**What the score does.** A scan of full.midi finds the violas' top note above Vln II's in bars 19–22, 60–61, 65, 67–68, 98–100, 102, 107–110 and 166. In 60–68, 98–102 and 166 the overlap lasts a beat or two inside the S chain or the viola arpeggio, and those passages are protected (c377, c128/c186). That leaves two passages.
- At 19–22 the violas play their espressivo counterline A♭4–G4–F4–A♭4 above Vln II's syncopated tied notes (C4, D♭4, D4, G4). Hn 3, p, holds the same pitches as Vln II (C4 | D♭4 | D♭4–B3 | B3–D4–C4), with Hn 4 below. This is Copland's solution: one soft horn doubles the lower violins. At 20 Vln II also joins Vln I on the sf D♭5, so the line never sits below the violas without support.
- At 107–110 Vln II is a pp divided pad (B3/E4 to F4/C4) under the violas' treble-clef espressivo line. In Copland the second violins carry a melodic part, so their weakness matters. Here they are accompaniment, so a doubling would only thicken the pad and cover the B.cl/viola alternation, which c578 protects.

**Verdict.** Keep both.

### 2. Bars 111–116, the wedge: fifteen inner parts, only one or two moving per bar (Tchaikovsky 6, PDF 268–269; La Mer, PDF 262): follows, no change

**What I checked.** The inner-voice scan (inner.py) counts, for each bar, the parts other than the highest and lowest and how many of them have an onset after beat 1. The only Allegro bars with five or more inner parts and at most one moving are cadential or stop chords (26, 43, 116, 122–123, 138, 170), the transition's first ff stroke (33), the soft motto and chorale bars (6–10, 74–76, 145, 175–182), and the wedge at 111–112. Of these, only the wedge is a long loud build. So I compared it note by note (dump.py, bars 109–117) with Adler's Tchaikovsky image.

**What the score does.** The bass falls F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C by whole bars (Cb, Cbsn, Bsn 2, tuba). Vln I rises A♭5–B♭5–C6–C6–C♯6–D6 in the S-head rhythm. Ob 2, Cl 1 and Hn 1 move once or twice per bar on the inner line A♭4–B♭4–C5–C5/B4–B♭4/A♭4–A♭4, and the other winds hold. The strings keep the texture moving: Vln II plays double-stop tremolo and the violas repeat a quaver neighbour figure (C4–C4–B3–C4). Tchaikovsky does the same in Ex. VIII-40: held winds, moving strings, and the brass kept down until the top. Here the trumpets enter only at 113, pp crescendo, the third time the sequence unit is heard. That is also Adler's third-time rule (PDF 255). The violas' repeated quavers re-articulate what the winds sustain, the "pulsating" doubling of PDF 262.

**Verdict.** The wedge follows the reading. Its notes and layout are protected (c382, c452, c482: lament wedge, wedge bass, 111–117). Keep.

### 3. Soft wind voicings: interlocking and enclosure in weak registers (Ex. VIII-22/23, PDF 256): clean, no change

**What the reading says.** The diagrams mark a flute interlocked below a reed in the flute's low octave as "weak" or "bad, especially if the dynamic is pp". They also show that enclosing a weak instrument between two stronger ones gives "better balance".

**What I checked.** A scan of full.midi (winds.py) looked at every half-beat for a flute sounding below an oboe or clarinet at a soft MIDI level and below C5. It found no such moment. Where flutes play softly (64–66, 73–76, 109–110, 117–120), they are the top voice or play alone. The soft clarinet/horn chords that complete pads (145, 156–157) put the clarinets above the horns, the voicing PDF 256 calls good.

**Verdict.** Keep.

### 4. Where the texture could still fall short of a master's: candidates weighed, none applied

The task asks for more than rule compliance. These are the places where I thought a master might write differently, and why none of them is a change.
- **77–81, tremolo instead of an Eroica-style quaver figure under the passed head (PDF 254).** Beethoven's figure keeps forte momentum after a tutti. Bar 77 starts a soft, distant development after the flute motto over the A♭ pedal (73–76). Tremolo pp gives the passed head a mysterious background, and the quaver motion is saved for the violas at 107–116 and the stretto at 127–130. A running figure here would also anticipate the viola pulsation of the wedge. Keep.
- **91–96, the bass enters in half and whole notes rather than as a fugal voice.** In Britten's fugue the bassoons enter as the last fugal voice. Here the fugato has four voices (Vc, Vla, Vln II, Vln I) and only four entries, and adding a harmonic bass under the third and fourth entries is the normal Beethoven/Brahms practice. The bass makes the fifth rhythmic layer that c497 counted, and the "chain G–C–F–B♭" the bass outlines is what leads into the B minor at 97. The entry plan is protected (c417, c427). Keep.
- **176, 150 and 155, bars where nothing moves after beat 1 or beat 3.** c497 already answered these (Findings 2 and 3), and its reasons still hold in v597: nothing in those bars has changed since v497.
- **33–36, held strings under the bassoon solo.** c397 and c497 kept these held notes, and c577 left them open but protected. Adler's own rule on PDF 226 (an accompanied bassoon is easily swallowed; c151) answers the Eroica comparison, so I do not reopen it.
