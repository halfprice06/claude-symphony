## Findings

None of the items below calls for a change. They are ranked by how close each comes to a real gap.

### 1. The cor anglais never plays softly in its deep band (no change)

**What the reading says.** The lowest fifth or sixth of the cor anglais (written B3–G4, sounding E3–C4) "is richly beautiful, with tremendous expressive carrying power", and its upper register "sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality" (adler1 PDF 205, Ex. VII-47). Each of Adler's three solo models uses that low band at a soft or medium level: Berlioz *Roman Carnival* mf espressivo, Tristan p < f, Sibelius ff then mf and p (PDF 205–206, Ex. VII-48 to -50).

**What the score does.** The scan shows that the cor anglais uses its deep band only in the fugato entry at 91–93 (f) and once at 99 (B3, ff). All its soft writing lies higher: 5–9 in the mellow band (written A4–F5), 147–155 at written B4–A5, and 171–174 in the thin band (written D5–B♭5). In a symphony whose motto starts on a low G, a master of Dvořák's or Tchaikovsky's kind might give the instrument at least one soft phrase where it is darkest.

**Why it stays.**
- 5–9: the motto sits a fifth above the bass clarinet's statement at 1–4 (D4–B♭4–A4–G4). An octave lower would put it at D3, below the instrument's range (E3).
- 147–155: the fills answer Hn 1 and the violas, which carry S in sounding E4–C5. In the deep band the fills would lie under the tune and become an inner voice (PDF 185: do not cover the soloist). The plan is protected as "C.a. answers 147–153" (c158, c298).
- 171–174: c551 tested the octave below and found it crosses the solo cello at 172.1 and meets it in unison on D♭4 at 173.3. That reason holds.
- I found no other place where the cor anglais plays softly and the deep band would not cover a lead or break a protected colour plan. The gap is real but small, and this reading gives no place to close it. The finale or movement II, which have their own C.a. writing, are the place to use this colour (recorded for the composer, not a change here).

### 2. Bar 8: the cor anglais's held G4 is pp under the strings' p (no change)

**What I considered.** At 7 the strings swell to p on the C-major chord at 8 and fade to pp at 9. At the same time the cor anglais makes its A♭–G sigh with a diminuendo and holds G4 at pp from 8 to 9.3. For one bar the soloist's last note is a level below the accompaniment. Adler's *Sinfonia domestica* example (PDF 203, Ex. VII-45) keeps the soft solo a level above its accompaniment.

**Why it stays.**
- After the sigh at 7.3–7.4 the motto is finished. The held G is a common tone carried into 9.4, where Vln I takes the same G4 (Rimsky-Korsakov transfer on a common note, protected as the 9.4 hand-off, c470, c570).
- The strings' swell at 8 is the Picardy chord (E♮ in Vln II), which is the event of that bar. The bass clarinet's held C at 4 fades the same way and hands its tonic to the basses (c328). The two statements of the motto end alike.
- Raising the G to p would make the C.a. and the strings arrive together, and the E♮ would lose its moment.

### 3. Bassoon solo 33–36 and the bassoon's register warnings (no change)

**What I considered.** PDF 226 says the bassoon "has a tendency to get swallowed up" when accompanied. At 33 it enters f on G3 directly after the ff stroke, diminishes to mf at 34 and holds mf through 35–36 while the violas imitate at mp and Vln I joins at mf.

**Why it stays.** Its line F3–A♭3–G3–E♭3 lies in the "sweet, more subdued but expressive" band of Ex. VII-89, and the accompaniment is marked below it (Vla mp, Vc p, Cb fp to rest). c498 recorded the same result for the flat mf. What is new here is the downward-skip check (PDF 226): the solo moves by step and thirds, with no slurred downward skip over a third. Nothing to change.

### 4. Articulation limits in the reading (no change)

- Oboe repeated notes (PDF 200): the fastest are paired crotchets at 32 and 142.
- Clarinet double-tonguing (PDF 213): not needed anywhere. The Cl 2 triplets at 117–121 are arpeggios at about 6 notes a second.
- Contrabassoon repeated staccato (PDF 230): crotchets only, about 2 a second, at 27–32 and 131–135.
- Bassoon downward skips (PDF 226): the only slurred downward fifth is D3–G2 at 164, a2 and mp, in the middle register.

### 5. Woodwind exits by register (no change)

For each place where a woodwind leaves a loud passage, I checked that the last notes lie where the player can still shape the diminuendo. 41 (Fl 1 takes only the attack of G6), 123 (flutes leave G6/D6 after two beats, Fl 1 back on G5 mf), 162–163 (Ob 2 leaves after A4 at 163; Cl 1 E5 ff >) and 170 (all winds stop under the fermata) all follow PDF 185 (shrill extremes can only be loud). These were fixed by c179, c399 and c362 and still hold.

## Master level

The woodwind dynamics and articulation by register are at master level and should not be touched: every soft entry lies in a register that can play soft, every loud woodwind note in the shrill or weak extremes is an attack or a doubling, every solo has its accompaniment graded below it, and the soft statements of the motto (B.cl 1–4, C.a. 5–9, Fl 1 73–76, B.cl 77, Cl 1 175–177) are shaped as Adler's models are.
