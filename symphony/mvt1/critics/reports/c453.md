## Findings

All findings recommend keeping the score as it is. After checking every rule of the chapter against 1–110 bar by bar, I found no place where the score breaks a rule without a reason the chapter itself supports, and no change that would bring the first half nearer a master's work in this lens. They are ranked by how close each comes to a real weakness.

### 1. Development 77–110: tension curve and the plateau at 97–102: keep

The development before the wedge runs: 77–81 rising head dialogue (p chords, entries mf → f) → 82 ff (one bar) → 83 ff strokes → 84 sub. p → fugato 85–96 (mf → f at 91 → ff at 96) → 97–102 ff (S in B minor) → 103–106 f (winds, Cb pizz.) → 107–110 p (strings, B.cl, Fl 1) → 111 wedge. The closest thing to a weakness is 97–102: six bars of ff with a < > hairpin on nearly every bar, so it is a plateau rather than a rising line.

Why it stays. PDF 116 asks whether a passage is a climax or a step. 97 is scored as a step: the trumpets are silent (they rest 84–112, almost 29 bars, and return pp at 113 to start the real build); the timpani give one stroke, not a roll; the tuba is silent. The climax weight is kept for 122. Within the plateau the line still rises (Fl 1 / Vln I to D6 at 102), and the seam at 102→103 is protected (c323). A crescendo across 97–102 would put a second ff-to-fff arrival ten bars before 117–122 and weaken it. The descent 103 f → 107 p before the 111 wedge is the elimination of PDF 127 (brass and upper strings out, then woodwind), and the dip makes the wedge's PDF 130 crescendo (strings, woodwind, then trumpets and trombones pp at 113) possible.

### 2. Fugato 85–97 as a prolonged crescendo: keep

This is the clearest case in the first half of the PDF 130 rule. Strings alone 85–90 (Vc mf, Vla, Cb mf< at 90); woodwind join at 91 (C.a., B.cl, Bsn f) and 94 (Fl 1); clarinets at 96 ff; horns, trombones, bass trombone and contrabassoon at 96 f; tutti ff at 97. The entries also follow PDF 128's law of register: each higher entry (D, G, C, F) goes to a higher string section (Vc, Vla, Vln II, Vln I), and the woodwind doubling each entry is in the same register (C.a. with Vln II, Fl 1 with Vln I). The f brass entry at 96 after about 25 s of rest is the one point PDF 136 calls "less successful", but c328 kept it: the brass play the subject's last fragment as a harmonic layer under ff woodwind and strings, which PDF 133 asks to be "of inferior dynamic power". I agree and add nothing.

### 3. Introduction 1–14 and first group 15–32: keep

1–8 use the chapter's dark low register (PDF 124–125) only as a unison line, so the "bad effect" of close low harmony does not arise; the first chords at 6 are spaced open above G2. 9–12 are a diverging progression (PDF 131): Vln I climbs sul D while the bass falls to A♭2 at 12, and the widening space is filled in the PDF 130 order: strings, then Bsn (9), Cl, Fl, Hn, timpani (11), trombones (12). The trombones' mf at 12 is the clearest mf re-entry after a long rest (PDF 136) in the movement; it is protected (c228, c308, c359) as graded brass under the f tutti, and the chapter gives no stronger reason than c228 already answered.

15–26: strings alone for eight bars (PDF 136: strings are the favourite group the ear can bear longest), woodwind and horns join at 23, the trumpets, trombones and tuba enter ff at 26 after 25 bars of silence. That is PDF 136's ideal re-entry exactly, and the stop chord at 26 is a step into 27 (PDF 116). 27–32 is protected (the finale quotes it).

### 4. Second group 47–68: S peak at 64 held below the recapitulation: keep

The exposition's S rises to f at 64 in the strings with Fl 1, Cl 1 and the horns at mf/mp, and no trumpets or trombones. By PDF 116 this is correct for a passage whose role is a complement to the later 158–160, not a climax. Every entry in the group is soft (Hn 3·4 pp at 56, Hn 2 p at 56, Bsn 2 p at 60, timpani pp at 62), so PDF 136 is followed. The one wider-application point is Fl 1's mf return at 64 after about 45 s; it doubles the violins' peak and is marked under them on purpose (protected c441, "64-66 Fl 1 exit"). Phrasing 47–63 is protected (c321, c338).

### 5. Proportions of the exposition and development: keep

Measured from v453's full.midi (430.5 s): introduction 67.2 s (16%), exposition 15–76 = 123.5 s (29%), development 77–130 = 105.4 s (24%), recapitulation 131–166 = 75.5 s (18%), coda 167–182 = 58.8 s (14%). These are the same as at v343, so the series-4 changes have not altered pacing.

Inside the exposition: P 15–32 = 34.3 s (28%), transition 33–46 = 26.9 s (22%), S 47–68 = 47.1 s (38%), closing 69–76 = 15.2 s (12%). From the MCTL form annotations (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/*/form/form_analysis.csv, in beats): Beethoven 9/i exposition 0–374 has first group 39%, transition 4%, second group 44%, closing 13%; Tchaikovsky 6/i exposition 72–638 has first group 17%, transition 33%, second group 38%, closing 13%. Movement I lies between the two in every part, and its closing share matches both.

Inside the development: head dialogue 14%, fugato 22%, S in B minor 11%, combined heads 14%, wedge 11%, pedal/climax/retransition 28%. Beethoven 9/i gives its last 14% (600–628 beats) to a retransition and spends about half on the first theme; Tchaikovsky 6/i spends its development mostly on the first theme as well. Movement I spends about 64% (77–96, 103–106, 117–130) on the P head and motto and about 25% on S. That matches the models' emphasis. Section lengths are protected (c243, c329, c413, c433).

### 6. Second-half changes since c343: keep
