## Findings

No finding needs a change to the score. I checked each principle in the table bar by bar and found no place where the score breaks one. The points below are those where a form critic might want a change. I give the reason for keeping each.

1. **Order of IV and V in the development (77–130).** In the corpus, Beethoven 9/i reaches iv (G minor) straight after the exposition and only then goes through C minor, B♭, A minor and F (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1/form/harmony.csv, key spans g 318–401, c 401–444, B♭ 456–484, a 484–560, F 560–588, in quarter-note onsets). This movement puts iv last (111), directly before V (117). That matches the diagram on A&S PDF 454 (IV–V together at the end of the development) and the rule on PDF 451–452 that a tonicized IV moves on to V of the home key. The VI→V step at 82→83 already gives the development's opening the upper-neighbour motion of PDF 456. Keep.

2. **Remote keys 97–110.** A&S PDF 443 limits Unit 26 to related keys and defers remote ones. B minor (6 bars), D minor (4), E minor (2) and B♭ minor (2) are short, and each is entered as a phrase modulation (PDF 452) or through its own dominant (106 B; 110 C for F). Under PDF 447–448 they are passing modulations between VI and iv, not key areas that compete with them. The 96→97 semitone shift and the 102→103 seam are protected (c303, c309). Keep.

3. **Strongest transition arrival on III (41).** The sf E♭ at 41 with the four-horn call is a III that a listener could take for the second key (PDF 449: III is the usual second key in minor). The D♭ seventh at 43–44 and the IV–V–I at 46–47 turn it into V of A♭, as Ex. 26-4 does with C minor's tonic as III of A♭. That is the "springboard" of PDF 447–448 (Ex. 26-7). The route is protected (c253, c303). Keep.

4. **Leading tone before the recapitulation (127–130).** PDF 449 requires the leading tone before the return when V has been minor. Here V has been major since 117 (B♮ in the violas, Hn 3, Vln II). The two bars without B at 129–130 are the motto stretto, which c233 and c263 kept with reasons. Keep.

5. **Short recapitulation (0.61 of the exposition).** The recapitulation leaves out the transition, which in A&S's terms has nothing left to do because S returns in the tonic (the iv turn at 139–143 takes its place, a late IV as PDF 451–452 allows). The tonic region from 131 lasts 134 s against 202 s for 34–130, so the return balances the departure. Lengthening was considered and rejected in c243 (section lengths do-not-touch), and the reading gives no reason to reopen it. Keep.

6. **Cadential confirmation of each key.** A♭ is confirmed twice with IV/II and V (52–55, 70–72), C major twice (152–155, 163–166), C minor at 143 and in the coda by iv–N6–I6/4–V at 172–174. This matches A&S PDF 443 and Points 2 (PDF 456). Keep the coda without a root-position V–I (protected c183, c223).
