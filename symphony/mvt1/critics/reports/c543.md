# Critic 543: Form, pacing and proportion (v543)

Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. IV "Composition of the orchestra" (rimsky-principles PDF 115–136, book pp. 97–118), fourth pass. I read all 22 text pages in full. I rendered and viewed PDF 119 (the partial-tutti bracket), 127 (chords passed between groups; amplification and elimination) and 132 (the "melodic design / fundamental notes" example). The range has no other pages of music. Its numbered examples (Nos. 175–276) are in the examples volume, outside the range.

Coverage. c253 covered 33–46 and 69–76. c343 covered 111–182. c453 covered 1–110 bar by bar. This pass looks at the whole movement as one tension curve, measured in seconds, against the chapter's two rules on time: economy (PDF 136: the ear cannot stand the full orchestra for long; a group that has been silent gains fresh interest; re-entries after a long rest should be pp or ff) and climax versus step (PDF 116). I also re-checked the passages that depend on PDF 127, 130 and 131 (crescendo and diminuendo by adding and removing groups, and diverging and converging progressions) against v543.

Method. I built a scratch copy of v543 and measured its full.midi (430.5 s, the same file as v543's: identical md5). I used two scripts in /tmp/critic-543/scripts: curve.py (for each bar, the start time, the number of sounding players and the mean attack velocity) and entries.py (every wind, brass, timpani and harp entry after 110 that follows a rest of 12 s or more, with the length of the rest and the entry velocity). I read score pages 12–18 at 60 dpi.

## Findings

Every finding recommends keeping the score as it is. I tested two possible changes against the reading and rejected both (F1, F2). They are ranked by how close each comes to a real weakness.

### 1. Bars 113–170: how often the full orchestra plays in the second half (PDF 136, 116, 127): keep

**What I measured.** In the second half, five passages have 22 or more players sounding: 113–123 (about 20 s), 131–134, 138–143, 158–162 and 167–170. Between them the texture thins: 124–130 drops to 5–9 players, 135–137 to 14–16 players, 144–157 to 7–14 players, and 163–166 to 12–15 players. The longest stretch of heavy scoring is 113–143, about 60 s, of which roughly 44 s has 22 or more players. Critic 513 also named this span. By PDF 136 ("Neither musical feeling nor the ear itself can stand, for long, the full resources of the orchestra"), the weakest point is the recapitulation's first group. It comes 8 bars after the 122 climax and has 26–27 players in 9 of its first 13 bars. Its brass re-enter at f after 13–15 s of rest, which PDF 136 calls "less successful" than pp or ff.

**Candidate tested on paper.** Trumpets tacet at 131–134, so that they return fresh on the 138 stop chord, as they do at 26 after 25 bars of silence.

**Why I keep the score.**
- The reading's own test is "climax or merely a step" (PDF 116). Bar 131 is the structural return after a pp→ff stretto (127–130). Taking out the trumpets would weaken the arrival the stretto builds to. The chapter does not ask for that.
- PDF 136 is already observed one level up, where it counts most. Trumpets, trombones, tuba, flutes, Ob 2, contrabassoon and timpani rest 144–157, about 31 s, and all return ff at 158 (entry velocity 101 for every one of them). That is the re-entry the chapter recommends, and it falls on the recapitulation's main climax, not on 131 or 138.
- The f at 131 is a deliberate grade, not a careless one. Under ff woodwind and strings the brass are the "harmonic basis … of inferior dynamic power" (PDF 133), and the trombones' low theme is kept from dominating (c144, v309). The rest of 13–15 s is short, about 7 bars. c453 treated the same class of f re-entries in the first half (69, 72, 80, 96) the same way.
- Inside the block, 135–137 removes the low brass and timpani (14–16 players), which is the PDF 127 elimination that gives the ear the relief PDF 136 asks for.
- 131–134 (low P head, c431), the 139–142 brass block (c417, c434) and the recapitulation's length (c483) are protected. The reading gives no stronger reason than the ones given there.

### 2. Bars 166–170: timpani roll under the coda's P head, not the P rhythm (PDF 135, 117–118): keep

**What I looked at.** PDF 135 says that when part of the orchestra plays a rhythmic figure, the percussion should join it on the strong beats. At 27–32 and 131 the timpani hammer the P rhythm (`c4 r8 c8`). At 167–169, under the brass P head, they roll on C from 166.3 (p<) through f to the ff stroke at 170.

**Candidate.** `c4\ff r8 c8 c4 r8 c8` at 167–168, with the roll only at 169 into 170.

**Why I keep the score.**
- PDF 117–118 asks for each return of an idea to be scored differently, and this is the P head's third forte return. A third hammered statement would make 167 a copy of 27/131 in the drum, and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part.
- PDF 135 itself pairs drum tremolo with sustained brass chords. At 167–169 Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 hold E♭4 under the moving horn/trumpet head (c174), and the double basses already pulse in quavers. The roll is the C pedal and also carries the PDF 130 crescendo into the stop chord.
- Keeping the hammer out of 167 leaves the soft pulse at 179–180 (`c4 r8 g,8`) as the coda's only return of the drum rhythm. That fits the chapter's echo rule (PDF 128–129: weaker and related), and the pulse is quoted by the scherzo and the finale.
- 167–169 is protected (c389, c417, c470).

### 3. Bars 127–131: retransition crescendo order (PDF 130): keep

The groups come in the order the chapter gives: cellos pp in quavers and Cl 1·2 with the half-augmented motto at 127, horns 3·4 pp held, violas mf at 129, then Bsn 1 f, Hn 2 mf and Vln II f at 130, timpani pp→mf→f. The trumpets, trombones and tuba wait for 131. That is strings, then woodwind, then brass, with the brass turning the crescendo into the arrival (PDF 130: brass glorify crescendo chords into climaxes). The measured curve rises 5 → 6 → 9 → 26 players across 127–131. Protected (c403, c473, c479).

### 4. Bars 111–126: diverging wedge and converging collapse (PDF 131): keep

The wedge is a textbook diverging progression. The bass falls F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C by whole bars while Vln I rises to D6 at 116, and the widening space fills from 17 to 27 players: woodwind at 111, then trumpets, trombones and tuba pp at 111–113 (rests of 52–56 s, re-entering pp as PDF 136 asks). After 122 the convergence follows the chapter's last sentence closely. The doubling top parts leave first (flutes after two beats at 123), and the stationary middle group stays (Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1 f> at 124, Hn 3 B3→C4 at 125), down to 16 players at 126 and 5–6 at 127–128. This is the clearest use of PDF 131 in the movement.

### 5. Bars 156–157: the two-bar climb (PDF 127): keep, no new answer

Critics 523–542 name the two-bar climb as the movement's one remaining weakness. It is protected because its length is a matter of form (c523). The reading's tool for a short approach is amplification (PDF 127). That is already what happens: the violins lead at mf from 155.4, the horns enter p and crescendo more steeply, the harp goes mf→f, and the suspended-cymbal roll at 159 continues the build into 160. The players present rise from 8 (155) to 14 (156–157) to 26 (158). The chapter gives no answer to the stated reason, which is that adding bars would make 158 a second arrival and move the protected bar numbers. I do not reopen it.

### 6. Bars 171–182: the soft coda (PDF 122–123, 130, 135, 136): keep

One solo cello at mp with the cor anglais p and a pp pad is the "light, transparent accompaniment" a string solo needs (PDF 123). The tam-tam's single pp stroke at 175 sits with the trombone chorale, the pairing PDF 135 names (gong with trombone chords). Cl 1 has the morendo on B4 at 177 (PDF 130). Horns 3·4 return ppp at 181 after 39 s, and the movement ends ppp under a fermata (PDF 136: pp re-entry; no mf/mp ending).

### 7. Proportions: unchanged, keep

From v543's full.midi (430.5 s): introduction 67.2 s (15.6%), exposition 15–76 = 123.5 s (P 34.3, TR 26.9, S 47.1, K 15.2), development 77–130 = 105.3 s (24.5%), recapitulation 131–166 = 75.5 s (recP 30.7, recS 44.8), coda 58.9 s (13.7%). These match c453's figures to 0.1 s, so nothing since v453 has changed the pacing. The comparison with Beethoven 9/i and Tchaikovsky 6/i (MCTL annotations) in c453 still stands. The recapitulation ratio of 0.61 is noted and protected (c493, c503).

## Leave alone

The 111–131 span is at a master's level and should not be touched: the diverging wedge into the dominant pedal, the converging collapse after 122 that keeps the middle brass, and the strings–woodwind–brass retransition. So is the 144–158 brass silence that makes the 158 ff re-entry fresh.
