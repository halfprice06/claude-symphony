## Findings

### 1. 19, 37, 143: background notes still strike or sit on the P head's anacrusis (applied)

The P head's identity is its pickup eighth and the leap from it (G4–E♭5 at 15, A♭4–A♭5 at 19). Adler's analysis of Brahms 3/iii says the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the [anacrusis] figure undisturbed" (PDF 135), and that staying out of the melody's register matters most "when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color" (PDF 135). Clarity in counterpoint comes from rhythmic difference between the layers (PDF 144, item 4; PDF 142, Beethoven 7/ii). Cycle 227 applied this to 15 and 17 only: Vln II and violas now enter on beat 2-and, under the held note.

My anacrusis scan finds three places left where a string background attacks with the pickup or holds its pitch:
- **19.** Vln II strikes C4 on 1-and, the moment of Vln I's pickup A♭4. This is the case c227 corrected at 17 ("sounded A♭4 against it").
- **37.** Vln II's p tremolo on B♭4 starts on beat 1, so Vln I's pickup B♭4 on 1-and enters inside a same-pitch violin tremolo and has no onset of its own.
- **143.** This is the recapitulation's last full statement of the head, f, before Bsn 1's answer. Vln II's off-beat pulse begins with G4 on 1-and, the unison of Vln I's pickup G4, and the violas strike E♭4 with it. This is exactly the bar-15 situation c227 described ("Vln II doubled it at the unison").

Fix (rhythm only; no pitch changes):
- tOne.ily, Vln II bar 19: `r8 c'8~\mp c'4 r8 c'8~ c'4` → `r4 r8 c'8~\mp c'2` (the pattern of 15 and 17).
- trans.ily, Vln II bar 37: `bes'1:32\p\<` → `r4 bes'2.:32\p\<` (the tremolo enters with Vln I's G♭5; the violas' G♭4 tremolo still starts the bar).
- recOne.ily, Vln II bar 143: `r8 g'8\f r8 g'8 r8 g'8 r8 g'8` → `r4 r8 g'8\f r8 g'8 r8 g'8`. Violas, same bar: `r8 es'8\f …` → `r4 r8 es'8\f r8 es'8 r8 es'8`.

Left as is: the viola's held A♭4 at 19. It is the protected counterline (c038), it holds rather than strikes, and it is the only A♭ in the iv chord on the downbeat (Hn 3 C4, Hn 4 F3, Bsn 1 F3, Vc F2). Also left: Cl 2's F4 pad at 35, which is a different colour, p against mf, and held.

Applied in the scratch copy. After the fix the scan shows no attack with the pickup at 19, 37 or 143, and no same-pitch string at 37 or 143. The 143 change does not touch the c239 "143–150 lead-in events": the ff stroke, Bsn 1's answer and the rit. are unchanged.

## Checked and kept

**A♭6 at 72 (Adler PDF 129, 133–135).** The codetta puts the S head in Vln I at C6–A♭6, the same octave and peak as the climax at 158–160. Brahms "saves the higher violin color for the more important foreground statement" (PDF 135). I tested the obvious fix on paper: Vln I an octave lower at 69–72, with the flutes keeping the top. It puts the tune on C5–F5, where Vln II carries the P-head counter at E♭4–F5. The two would meet at the unison (C5 at 69 beats 2–3, F5 at 70 beat 3). Adler's first remedy for clarity is "separating theme and countertheme registerally" (PDF 144, item 3), so that fix trades one fault for a worse one. The 160 climax is set apart by fff, the A♭ bass, full brass and the cymbal, as c271 said. Keep as is.

**15–18, P on Vln I alone (PDF 133, 141).** Vln I at G4–F5 is the register Adler calls weak. But the line is marked f over mf, and the build to the tutti at 27 depends on this thin start (c287). With 19 fixed, the background no longer touches the anacrusis anywhere in 15–19. Shostakovich's octave doubling (PDF 141) would take the cellos off the bass. Keep.

**97–102, the viola arpeggio at D4–B4 inside the register of the tune's lower octave (PDF 135).** The lower octave (Vln II sul G, Vc, C.a.) doubles a tune already carried by Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1. The violas' eighths differ in rhythm (PDF 144, item 4), and every other register there is taken by the brass counter. Keep (protected c128, c186, c231).

**147–154, Vln II pad above the tune (PDF 135).** It is pp, sul tasto, and below the violins' high colour, which is first heard at 155–156 (c107, c271). Keep.

**158–161, violas' repeated dyads at C4–G4 near the cello's third octave of the tune (PDF 135, 165).** The cello line is a doubling, and the repeated chords are the idiom Adler recommends at speed (Ex. V-42/43). Keep (c178, c236).

**The three A♭ climaxes, strings only.** 82: the head in three string octaves over the A♭ bass. 122: the tremolo chord with Vln I alone on A♭6. 160: the tune in Vln I, Vln II and Vc octaves with the violas hammering. Each adds weight in the strings, as in the Leonore crescendo by octaves (PDF 137). No change.
