## Findings

No change. I found no expression change that the reading or the masters support as a real improvement. The candidates I tested against the reading:

### Considered and kept 1: the trombone motto at 117–121 (ff, plain long notes)

Ex. XI-21 (PDF 39–40, read at 220 dpi) gives the trombone cantus firmus plain dotted minims at fff, a2, with no accent, tenuto or hairpin, at the same level as the whole tutti. Our trombones play the motto a2 in plain whole notes at ff, with the winds, horns and bass at ff and the strings at f. The articulation matches Tchaikovsky's. The level is one step lower because 117 is not the climax: our climax is five bars later at 122, and fff is reserved for 122 and 160 (protected ladder 82 ff < 122 fff < 160 fff). In Tchaikovsky the cantus firmus begins at the climax itself. Adding accents would make the line sound like the accented P calls around it, when the point of Adler's principle 1 (PDF 37) is that it sounds new. Keep.

### Considered and kept 2: the four-horn call at 41–43

The call is "ff molto espr." (c408, after Adler adler1 PDF 305), with a diminuendo on the B♭ of 42 and p at 43, over the sf > mf > p chord in winds and strings. This is Adler's sforzando-with-long-notes scoring (PDF 189–190) with the loud colour handed to the horns as the chord decays. The seam into 43 is soft on both sides. Keep.

### Considered and kept 3: 102→103 and 143→144

At 102→103 the ff B-minor S stops and Ob/Cl f with Hn 1 and Vc mf start the combined heads. At 143→144 the ff stroke is followed by Bsn 1's mf answer over strings mp. Neither is a forte-subito-piano case in Adler's sense (PDF 190): both are new phrases after a stroke, and an overlapping p choir would blur the break. The "103–106 light after ff" point is a form question (open, c-form) and outside this lens.

### Considered and kept 4: 156–157 and 160–162

Already examined by c448 (and the climb is protected for form). The fresh ff entry of trumpets and trombones at 158 after 14 bars of rest is exactly Adler's held-back colour (PDF 37), and the crescendo hairpin in every staff at 159 matches Schubert's "cresc." in every part. The fff→ff drop on the barline at 162 coincides with the deceptive cadence, the harp entry and the brass fade, which is Adler's stated exception to hidden seams (PDF 192). Keep.
