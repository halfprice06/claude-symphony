# Critic 432: Harmony and voice leading (v432)

Reading: Aldwell & Schachter, Unit 9 "Leading to V: IV, II and II6" and Unit 10 "The Cadential 6/4" (aldwell-schachter PDF 134–162), third pass. I read the text file of every page from PDF 134 to 162 in full. I rendered every page with music examples at 110 dpi and read the examples from the images: Ex. 9-1 to 9-26, 10-1 to 10-14, and the exercise examples on PDF 160–162. The OCR drops or garbles most figures; for example, 6/4 comes out as "§".

As c412 noted, book p. 151 is missing from the scan. PDF 156 is book p. 150 and PDF 157 is book p. 152. Unit 10 §§9–11 (the cadential 6/4 and the submediant, harmonic rhythm against the 6/4, the noncadential 6/4) show only as clipped headings at the right edge of the PDF 156 image. Their rules are still covered by review points 7–8 on PDF 160.

Method. I built an unchanged scratch copy of v432 and dumped full.midi at every beat and every half-beat, listing the lowest note, the pitch classes and each player's pitch, with attacks marked. On the dump I ran four scans:
1. a six-four finder (a 4th and a 6th above the bass, with no 3rd or 5th): 51 beats, 14 places;
2. a V-to-intermediate retrogression scan (a dominant chord followed by a triad a step or a fifth lower);
3. a weak-to-strong bass-repetition scan under a change of chord: 114 places, most on pedals;
4. a check of how each voice holding the 4th of a cadential 6/4 leaves it, at the eighth.

c322 and c412 reviewed this same reading and changed nothing. So I concentrated on two things: the changes made since v413 (c414, c418, c422, c424), and on two scans earlier passes did not report, the retrogression scan and the eighth-level resolution check.

### Checked and kept (no change)

1. **Changes since v413 at intermediate harmonies and cadential 6/4s.**
   - **24.3–25.1 and 136.3–137.1** (c422: Cl 1 and Vln II on Eb4). Into iv at 25.2 the Eb4s move to F4 and to C4, against a bass moving C→F. The vl.txt entries at 25.2 and 137.2 are unison doublings: Cl 2 + Hn 2 + Vla, Cl 1 + Vln II, Bsn 1 + Vc. No two independent voices move in octaves. This is the contrary-motion IV approach of Ex. 9-3a (PDF 136).
   - **68** (c422: Vla Ab4). Leading tone G4 rises to Ab4 in the deceptive cadence to F minor. Correct.
   - **142** (c414: Tbn 2 G3). It doubles the bass of the 5/4 at 142.1. The bass is the preferred doubling (Ex. 10-5a, PDF 154).
   - **159–160** (c414: Tpt 3 hands B3 to Tbn 1). The 4th C of the 6/4 at 158 falls to B in Tpt 3, Vla, Hn 3·4, Ob 1 and Cl 1·2. Tbn 1's C4→D4 is a doubling (c412). B3→C4 into ♭VI at 160 is the leading tone moving to the doubled third of VI.
   - **162** (c424: Bsn 2 A2, Bsn 1 E3). These double the root and fifth of vi after V7 at 161. The 7th F falls in four voices.
   - **166** (c418: horns p). Dynamics only.
   - **Verdict.** All keep the reading.

2. **Retrogressions (syntax rule 2, PDF 148).** The scan found three places where V is followed by a chord a step lower.
   - **18→19.** V at the half cadence, then iv opening the consequent. This is an incomplete progression (Ex. 9-26, PDF 150), not V–IV inside one progression.
   - **138→139.** vii°6/5 over D, then iv. Closed by c232 and c292. The finale quotes it.
   - **104.4→105.1.** A7 on beat 4, then Bb–D–G (iv6 of D minor) at 105.1, then C7 at 105.3, which is reread as a German sixth of E minor at 105.4. It looks like V→iv. The bass rises G2–A2–Bb1–C2 under iv–V7–iv6, and the A7 is a passing chord on a weak beat inside an expanded iv. That is the device of Ex. 9-18 and 9-19 (PDF 145–146), with a passing dominant in place of the passing "I6". Its tendency tones resolve: Hn 3 C#4→D4, and the 7th G5→F5 on the second eighth of 104.4.
   - **Verdict.** Keep all three. 103–110 is protected (c342, c348).

3. **52→53 and 152→153: the bass repeats from a weak bar into a strong bar while IV becomes ii6.**
   - **What happens.** S is in two-bar hypermeter with 55 strong, so 52 is weak and 53 strong. The bass Db (Db/F/Ab, then Db/F/Bb) is restruck at 53 by the basses and harp.
   - **Why it looks wrong.** This is the shape Ex. 9-24a (PDF 149) calls poor, and review 9 (PDF 151) names IV–II6 as the example.
   - **Why it is right.** The book's own Ex. 9-20 (Mozart K. 387, PDF 146) puts IV in bar 32 and (II6) in bar 33 over the same bass. It marks the II6 in parentheses as an offshoot of IV: a 5–6 inside one harmony, not a new harmony on the downbeat. 52–53 is that case. The viola moves Ab3→Bb3 over the held Db, and the next real change is V at 54. Ex. 9-24a is a chorale-texture warning about a chord change on the beat.
   - **The alternative.** Moving the 5–6 to beat 3 of 52 would shorten the S phrase's subdominant plateau and change the protected phrasing of 47–63 (c321, c368).
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

4. **164.3–165: the soprano 4th of the cadential 6/4 in C.**
   - **Why it looks wrong.** A beat-level reading shows Vln I·II going C6/C5 to A5/A4. That would break the rule of PDF 153 and review 2 (PDF 160) that the 4th falls by step.
   - **Why it is right.** The half-beat dump shows C6/C5 tied over the barline and falling to B5/B4 on the second eighth of 165. Only then do the violins skip to A, the protected ninth at 165.2 (c392). This is the tied 4–3 of Ex. 10-6a that c232 introduced.
   - **Verdict.** Keep.

5. **109.4→110: the approach to the F-minor cadential 6/4.**
   - **What happens.** Bb minor (iv) at 109.1 moves to B–Db–F over B2 at 109.4: an Italian sixth with the B in the bass. Then comes the 6/4 at 110.
   - **Why it is right.** The 4th F is held in Vln II from 109 (common tone, PDF 153). It falls to E4 at 110.3, and Ab5 falls to G5. The bass B2→C3 resolves the augmented sixth outward with the Db3→C3 the bass clarinet played before it. The approach is from an intermediate chord (review 7, PDF 160).
   - **Verdict.** Keep. 103–110 is protected.

6. **72.2: Ab major over an Eb bass, in a forte tutti.**
   - **What happens.** The six-four finder flags it.
   - **Why it is not a cadential 6/4.** The bass leaps Ab1–Eb2–Ab1 on beats 1–3 under one I chord. This is an arpeggiating 6/4 inside I, so Unit 10's cadential rules do not apply.
   - **Verdict.** Keep. 69–72 is protected (c331, c347).
