## Findings

The brass and combined-group writing follows this chapter closely. Three earlier passes (c124, c234, c267) fixed the clear breaches. I found two smaller places in held chords where two brass of one family share a note while a chord note is thin, and I list the other scan hits with the reason each should stay.

### 1. Bar 140: Tpt 2 and Tpt 3 hold the same F4 on the ♭II chord (applied, from beat 3)

What is wrong. Bar 140 is the ff arrival on root-position D♭ (♭II, A♭6 in Fl 1 on top), held a whole bar in the brass. The trumpets are Tpt 1 A♭4, Tpt 2 F4, Tpt 3 F4: two of the three trumpets on one note, and the root D♭ is not in the trumpets. In the fourth octave F4 sounds in Tpt 2, Tpt 3, Hn 2 and the violas; D♭4 only in Hn 3 and Tbn 1. The trumpets are the loudest held brass here (ff; trombones, tuba and Hn 3·4 are f, c244).

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 100: brass harmony is "of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". PDF 101 (diagrams): "In the higher registers, four-part harmony, of which the two upper parts are given to the trumpets ... When 3 trumpets are available the fourth part should be allotted to one trombone, or two horns": three trumpets take three different chord notes. PDF 114: balance matters most in long sustained chords. At 139 the trumpets already hold a complete close triad (C5–A♭4–F4); at 140 the layout collapses onto two notes.

Fix (recOne.ily, recOneTptThree, bar 140):
```
R1*3 | d'2.->\f r4 | f'2\f\< f'2 | f'1\ff |            % before
R1*3 | d'2.->\f r4 | f'2\f\< f'2 | f'2\ff des'2~ |     % after
des'2 c'2 | c'2-> b2-> | ...                            % 141-142 unchanged
```
From beat 3 the trumpets hold A♭4–F4–D♭4, the close triad of PDF 101, and Tpt 3 becomes a half-note line F4–D♭4–(D♭4)–C4–C4–B3 into 142. I first tried D♭4 for the whole bar: it makes parallel fifths C5→A♭4 / F4→D♭4 between Tpt 1 and Tpt 3 on the downbeat (vl.txt), so the change is delayed to beat 3, where Tpt 1 holds A♭4 and Tbn 2's tenor line (c317) moves A♭3→F3 in sixths with it. The cost is a half bar of Tpt 3 in unison with Tbn 1 on D♭4 (PDF 104, "not so common"); the same unison is already at 141 (c234 kept it), and Hn 3 is on D♭4 too, so the three colours are mixed as PDF 103 prefers. Pitch classes, harmony, the N6 thread 140–143 and all dynamics are unchanged. HISTORY protects 139–146 for form and key plan (c223, c233) and keeps "dense 139–141" (c283); this touches neither. Rank: modest. It is the only held forte chord in the movement where two trumpets share a note while the chord root is missing from them.

### 2. Bar 124: Tbn 2 rises to G3, doubling Tbn 1, while the leading tone has one horn (applied)

What is wrong. At 124, in the diminuendo after the fff, the brass hold V over the G pedal: Hn 3 B3, Hn 4 G3, Tbn 1 G3, Tbn 2 G3, B.tbn G2, tuba G1. Five brass are on G and one horn has B. Tbn 2 gets there by taking its seventh F3 (held 122–123) up a step to G3, into a unison with Tbn 1, and then stops. The seventh F stays in Vln II and Cl 1 at 124 and resolves there to E♭ at 125.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 102: "If the instruments are mixed the number of horns should be doubled"; here the mix is two horns against four low brass, with the single horn on the only third. PDF 100: close order, no wasted doubling. PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained chords, and 124 is a held whole-note chord. Adler (adler2 PDF 44, cited by c234) gives the same weighting of two horns to one trombone.

Fix (dev.ily, devTbnTwo, bars 122–125):
```
c'2..\< r8\! | f1\fff | f1\> | g1 |      R1\! | R1*5 |   % before
c'2..\< r8\! | f1\fff | f1\> | R1\! |    R1 | R1*5 |    % after
```
Tbn 2 leaves at the end of 123 with the trumpets. The exit order stays "highest and heaviest first": trumpets and Tbn 2 after 123, Tbn 1 and B.tbn after 124, tuba after 126, horns last. HISTORY protects the diminuendo exit order 123–127 and c318's fade from 123; the order of groups is kept, and c318's hairpin starts are unchanged. At 124 the brass are now B3 (Hn 3), G3 (Hn 4, Tbn 1), G2, G1: the trombone pair no longer doubles itself, and the rising seventh disappears from the brass. No other part changes. Rank: small; it improves a fading chord, not a climax.
