# Critic 4 (lens 4: orchestration) on v23

## What I studied
I read all nine section files and looked at rendered pages 3, 4, 6, 10 and 14 of score.pdf (bars 27–36, 37–47, 67–77, 110–120, 153–163). Corpus: I dumped part-by-part pitches from the MusicXML files for Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 89–92 and 130–133, Tchaikovsky 6/iii bars 229–232 and Dvořák 9/i bars 32–43. Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Project Gutenberg text (https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/33900/pg33900.txt), especially the sections on relative strength of groups, octave doubling in the strings, and the harp. Adler's *Study of Orchestration* is under copyright and not online. I used course notes that quote and cite it (https://personal.stevens.edu/~abrick/orchestration1/files/HMU397_notes_11.html, citing Adler CD4-35: "from mf–fff two horns should be used in unison to balance a single brass").

The balance figures below use Rimsky-Korsakov's forte scale: 1 trumpet = 1 trombone = 1 tuba = 2 horns, 1 horn = 2 woodwinds, and a whole violin section = 2 woodwinds.

## Findings

### 1. The main-theme tuttis carry the tune in two octaves only, against full brass (bars 27–31, 163–165)
**What is wrong.** At 27–31 the theme is in:
- the upper octave: flute 1 and violins I;
- the lower octave: oboe 1, clarinet 1 and violins II.

On Rimsky-Korsakov's scale that is about 7 woodwind units. Against it:
- 4 horns ff and 3 trumpets ff (about 20 units);
- 2 trombones, bass trombone and tuba at f (about 16 units);
- the remaining woodwinds and the violas' repeated chords.

Every instrument below g' is on sustained harmony or bass. The coda's 163–165 repeats the same layout, and the brass is ff there.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 130–133: when the second theme returns ff, it is in violins I, violins II and violas in three octaves (F♯6/F♯5/F♯4). Winds and horns only throb the harmony.
- Tchaikovsky 6/iii bars 229–232: the march tune is in flutes, piccolo, clarinets and violins, and the violas add a lower octave (C6 in the violins against C4 in the violas, bar 230). Brass repeat chords.
- Rimsky-Korsakov on melody in double octaves ("Vns I / Vns II / Violas"): this layout "may be used for full cantabile melodies extremely tense in character, and in forte passages for choice."

**Fix.**
- **Violas take a third octave** in place of their r8-chords, which only duplicate the horns.
  - Bars 27–32:
    `r8 g8\ff es'4.-> d'8 des'8 c'8 | c'4 b8( c'8) aes4.-> g8 | r8 aes8 f'4.-> es'8 d'8 c'8 | bes8 c' des' bes g'4.-> f'8 | aes'4.-> g'8 f'4 d'4 | <d' f'>4-> <d' f'>4-> r2 |`
  - Bars 163–165, same idea:
    `r8 g8\ff es'4.-> d'8 des'8 c'8 | c'4 b8( c'8) aes4.-> g8 | r8 aes8 f'4.-> es'8 d'8 c'8 |`
- **Lower the brass one step** under the theme: trumpets 2–3 and horns 3–4 to f at 27–30 and 163–165. Trumpet 1 and horns 1–2 stay ff.
- The recapitulation's 123–126 already solves this (trombones, bassoons, clarinets and cellos on the tune), so the recapitulation still sounds heavier than the exposition.

### 2. ♭VI climax: cellos and harp are wasted, and the tune has no third octave (bars 154–157)
**What is wrong.** At the movement's single fff, the cellos play the arpeggio figure between G2 and C4. It is marked **f** at 154 (`recTwoArp`, the 7th bar) while everyone else is ff. The page-14 image shows the f under the ff tutti. The violas play off-beat syncopated chords that the horns already sustain.

The tune is in two octaves:
- upper: flute 1 and violins I;
- lower: flute 2, oboe 1, trumpet 1 and violins II.

Nothing below c''.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 130–133 again: violas on the tune's lowest octave at the climax.
- Rimsky-Korsakov's *Antar* example (Ex. 32): "Vns I / Vns II / Violas + 'Cellos" in octaves, for forte cantabile.

**Fix.** Violas and cellos take the tune two octaves below violins I. Clarinet 2 and horns 3–4 already hold the harmony that the violas leave.
- Bars 154–157, both parts:
  `g4\ff e'2.~ | e'4 f'4 g'2 | c'4\fff aes'2~ aes'8 g'8 | f'2 d'4 b8 c'8 |`
- From 158 they return to `\recTwoSync` and `\recTwoArp` with the diminuendo.
- The bass line stays covered by double basses, bassoons, contrabassoon, bass trombone and tuba.

### 3. The harp plays low arpeggios through the fff (bars 148–162)
**What is wrong.** `recTwoHarpL = \transpose c c, \recTwoArp` puts the harp an octave below the cellos, down to A♭1 at 156, at ff/fff.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov on the harp:
- "only the notes of the first to the fourth octave are used";
- low strings ring on, so at chord changes "the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect";
- "at least three, if not four harps in unison are necessary, if they are to be heard against a full orchestra playing forte."

One harp in octave 1 under fff brass is inaudible at best and blurs the harmony at worst.

**Fix.**
- Remove `\transpose c c,`.
- Rest from 154 to 157 and re-enter at 158 with the diminuendo:
  `recTwoHarpL = { R1*9 | c8\p( g c' e' g'2) | f,8( c f a c'2) | d8( a d' f' a'2) | g,8( d f b d'2) | bes,8( f bes d' f'2) | f,8( c f aes c'2) | R1*4 | a,8(\mf e a c' e'2) | f,8( c f a c'2) | d,8( a, d f a2) | g,8( d f b d'2) | c,8(\p g, c e g2) | }`

### 4. Codetta: the second theme's head on top is outweighed by its accompaniment (bars 69–72)
**What is wrong.** The v21 fix put the second-theme head on top, but only flute 1 and violins I carry it, with oboe 1 an octave below (about 5 units). Against it:
- trumpets, trombones and tuba play half-note chords at f (about 24 units);
- the first-theme head in horns 1–2 sits in E♭3–F4, the same register as trombones 1–2 (es' c' des').

The page-6 image shows the trombone chords stacked directly on the horn line.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov: two horns equal one trombone in forte, and Adler (via the notes above) says the same. Horns 1–2 a2 therefore equal one trombone, and they share their register with two.

**Fix.**
- Trumpet 1 doubles oboe 1 on the tune:
  `c''2.~\f c''8 bes'8 | aes'4 f''2 es''4 | des''4. c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''4-> es''4-> c''2-> |`
  Oboe 2's c''/des'' covers the harmony that trumpet 1 drops.
- Trombones 1–2, bass trombone and tuba go to mf for 69–71, and to f only on the accented chords in 72.

### 5. A second fff in the coda contradicts the v23 decision (bar 166)
**What is wrong.** Cycle 3's accepted fix made 156 the only fff after the development. Bar 166 carries `\fff` in every coda part, with the timpani crescendo into it. It therefore equals the ♭VI climax, with more brass weight, since all 3 trumpets, 4 horns and the trombones are on accented chords.

**Fix.** Change `\fff` to `\ff` at 166 in all coda parts, and end the timpani hairpin at `\ff`. With finding 1's viola octave added, 163–166 still sounds forceful without challenging 156.

### 6. Development D3: the tune's bottom octave is thin at ff (bars 97–102)
**What is wrong.** The tune is in:
- flute 1 (8va);
- oboe 1 and violins I;
- violins II alone on D4–A4.

Against it: horns 1–2, trombone 1 and bassoon 1 play the motto fragments ff in the same tenor register, and the trumpets rest. Violins II's lower octave is the only line that does not compete with brass of its own register, and it is a single section.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov's examples of octave doubling in forte ("Vns I + Fl. / Vns II + Ob.", *Tsar's Bride* 81, 166) reinforce each string octave with a woodwind.

**Fix.** Cor anglais doubles violins II for 98–102. Its range fits: written a fifth higher, the top is e''. Use violins II's notes from `devVlnTwo` (`\transpose c cis { des'2.~ des'8 c'8 | bes4 ges'2 f'4 | ... }`), at ff.
