## The measurement

| | T6/i | D9/i | B9/i | v120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| bars with a rising sixth in any melodic part | 19% | 19% | 22% | **42%** (76 of 182) |
| Vln I: rising sixths as a share of rising leaps ≥ 4th | 7% | 12% | — | **69%** (35 of 51) |
| Vln I: rising octaves | 18 | 22 | — | **0** |
| Vln I: rising fourths | 80 | 63 | — | **11** |

The top line has one large interval. Cycle 91 kept the theme pitches because, in its account, the sixth is an *unusual* gradient within a common arch contour. Its own source, Müllensiefen & Halpern, found that motives which are rare **within the set of melodies heard** predict correct recognition. At 69% of the violins' leaps, the sixth is no longer rare within this movement. Most of the sixths belong to the protected heads (P 15, S 48 and 51, the motto, the ladder 117–120, the climax 156–160) and should stay. The fixes below remove sixths that are **not** part of a head, so that the heads become rare again.

Aldwell & Schachter, Unit 5, "Treatment of leaps" (PDF 81): "A leap of a 6th or octave … generates considerable tension … Large leaps must be used sparingly … too many will create a disconnected, meaningless line. The melodic perfect 5th and perfect 4th are more moderate in their effect." Rimsky-Korsakov's Example 12 (PDF 186; ch. II "Melody", PDF 54–57) shows the opposite way of making a tune memorable. Scheherazade III gets its identity from its rhythm (dotted eighth, sixteenth, eighth) turning around d'', with no leap larger than a third. The memorable models in the corpus make the same point:
- **T6/i second theme, Vln I 89–97:** a falling scale, with one rising **octave** (A4–A5 at 93) kept for the approach to the high point.
- **D9/i, Fl 1 149–156:** repeated notes and a triad; no sixths.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 29, 139, 169: the loud statements of P change P's minor seventh into a sixth. APPLIED.
- **Problem:** in the first statement, the second cell is `r8 g'8( f''4.->)` (17), a minor seventh. Every forte restatement changes the pickup to A♭, which makes a sixth:
  - tutti 29: `r8 aes''8( f'''4.->)` in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II and Vla;
  - recapitulation 139: Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I and Vln II;
  - coda brass 169: Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2 and Vla.
- **Effect:** the loud statements are the ones a listener retains, and they are not the theme the listener first learned. They also add 16 sixths in three bars. The soft statements (131–134, the fugato, 140s) keep the seventh. No cycle in HISTORY.md mentions this A♭, so it was never a decision.
- **Fix, already in the scratch copy:** in all 16 parts, `r8 aes''8( f'''4.->)` → `r8 g''8( f'''4.->)` (and the same change in each octave). The pickup repeats the preceding g''8 across the rest, exactly as bars 16–17 already do. The A♭ sustained in other parts against it makes the same G/A♭ friction as bar 17 (Vln II `r8 aes'8~` against Vln I g').
- **Protections:** cycle 91 protects "P pitches in all statements", and this change restores them. Cycle 90 keeps 27–32 and 167–170 alike, and they remain alike.

### 2. Bar 19: grow P's leaps 6–7–6 into 6–7–8. APPLIED.
- **Problem:** P's three `r8 X Y4.` cells leap a minor sixth (15), a minor seventh (17) and a minor sixth again (19). The phrase drives to the D♭6 sf at 20, but its largest leap shrinks just before the peak.
- **Fix, already in the scratch copy (Vln I, tOne.ily):** `r8 c''8\<( aes''4.->)` → `r8 aes'8\<( aes''4.->)`. The pickup moves by step from g' (end of 18), leaps an octave, and then falls by step (g'' f'' es''), as A&S PDF 81 prescribes after a large leap.
- **Effect:** the theme now widens its leap one size per cell toward the peak, and the violins have their first rising octave. Tchaikovsky 6/i uses the octave in the same place, before the high point (Vln I 93).
- **Playability:** A♭4–A♭5 is an easy section shift (Adler ch. 3, "Skips", PDF 82–83, warns only about wide skips on one string or between extreme registers).
- **Effect on other statements:** the bar-19 cell appears only here (the recap and coda use bars 15–18), so no other statement changes.
- **Protections:** this reopens "notes of 15–22" (cycles 11, 49, 91). Those protections rest on the peak placement and the sentence structure, and both are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 38: liquidate the head in the transition. APPLIED (Vln I only).
- **Problem:** 33–38 state the P head six times in six bars: Bsn 33, Vla 34, Vln I 35, Vln I and Ob/Vla 37, Vln I and Cl/Vla 38. That is seven sixths. A&S, Unit 9 (PDF 151), describes the sentence's continuation as "shorter one-bar units, often containing only a fragment of the basic motive". Here the fragments keep the full leap to the end.
- **Fix, already in the scratch copy (trans.ily, Vln I):** `r8 c''8( aes''4.->) g''8( ges''8 f''8)` → `r8 g''8( aes''4.->) g''8( ges''8 f''8)`.
- **Effect:** the leap becomes the semitone G–A♭, the motto's 5–♭6. A&S, Unit 32 (PDF 600), shows Beethoven saturating the Appassionata with 5–6–5 (C–D♭–C) as a neighbour figure rather than a leap. The Cl 1/Vla answer at 38 beat 3 keeps its sixth, so the half-bar chain from cycle 77 is intact, and the head is still heard one last time before the arpeggio at 39.
- **Harmony:** Vla g'16 sounds an octave below the new pickup for a sixteenth and then moves in contrary motion. There are no parallels.

### 4. What should stay, and a rule for movements II and III. No change.
- **Measured result** (bars with a sixth: 76 → 73; Vln I sixths: 35 → 31; Vln I rising octaves: 0 → 1; rising sevenths: 1 → 3): the remaining sixths are now almost all heads, motto statements, the ladder at 117–120 and the climax sequence 156–160 (f''–d''', g''–e''', c'''–a♭'''). That sequence works only if the sixth is scarce before it, which is what findings 1–3 serve.
- **Why I stop there:** cutting further inside movement I would mean changing the S head (nine statements) or the motto, and I do not recommend that.
- **For movements II and III:** new themes should open with another interval: a step, a falling third, a fourth, or Tchaikovsky's octave. Each should have a rhythmic cell of its own that repeats within two bars (Scheherazade III; D9/i 149–151). The rising sixth should come back only at cyclic returns of the motto. The cycle-91 note on rhythmic repetition still applies.
