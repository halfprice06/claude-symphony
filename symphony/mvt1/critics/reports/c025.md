# Critic 25 — lens 5 (engraving), v44

## What I studied
I rendered all 18 pages of v44/score.pdf at 100 dpi and 200 dpi (/tmp/critic-025/pages, /tmp/critic-025/hi) and zoomed in on the dense spots. For corpus comparison I used the hand-engraved Mutopia Dvořák 7 (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1_Staves.ly line 61, which puts `Mark_engraver` and `Metronome_mark_engraver` on the Violino I staff; DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Violoncello.ly lines 123 and 190, where the cellos switch to tenor clef). On the web I read the LilyPond Notation Reference on automatic part combining (https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.25/Documentation/notation/automatic-part-combining) and the Part_combine_engraver internals (https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/internals/part_005fcombine_005fengraver). Searches for Gould excerpts (behindbarsnotation.co.uk, scribd TOC) gave only tables of contents, so the Gould points below are standard *Behind Bars* conventions cited from memory. I also read LilyPond 2.24's part-combiner.scm: a `\partCombineApart/Unisono/Automatic` in either part takes effect at the next moment where both parts have events.

**I tested the fixes for findings 1–5 together** in /tmp/critic-025/build/v44 (score test.pdf). The edit script is /tmp/critic-025/edit.py. The build gave no warnings, and I looked at each fixed spot. Pages tested separately: /tmp/critic-025/build/vb.

## Findings

### 1. Part-combiner artifacts in the tuttis (80, 82, 109–113, 123–126, 153–156)
Four kinds of fault, all on shared staves:
- **Unisons printed as two voices.** Hn 1·2 and Tbn at 109 show two whole notes side by side. Bsn at 153 and 154 also show two whole notes side by side. Fl at 154–155 has doubled slurs and no a2; the a2 only appears at 156, which hides the cycle-24 flutes a2. Cl at 123–126 is an exact unison, but the `\partCombineApart` there makes two voices with crossed slurs, doubled accents and stacked rests. Bsn at 123 gets its a2 only on beat 2.
- **Wrong "1."/"2." labels.** At 82, Ob and Tpt are labelled "2." although both players play. At 80–81, Bsn is labelled "2.". At 110–112, Fl is labelled "2.", and Fl 1's eighth rests are suppressed, so its line seems to start on the beat.
- **Doubled dynamics.** "ff ff" appears in Ob, Cl and Tpt at 82, and "f f" in Bsn at 80.

Gould: a shared staff shows one stem and "a2" for a unison, two stemmed voices with their own rests when the players differ, and one dynamic.

Fixes (tested):
- dev.ily, devHnOne and devTbnOne at 109: `\partCombineUnisono g1\ff | es'1~ | es'2. d'4 | des'2. c'4~ | c'1 | \partCombineAutomatic aes'1\fff` (Tbn: `... \partCombineAutomatic aes1\fff`).
- recTwo.ily, recTwoFluteOne: `\partCombineUnisono g''4\ff( e'''2.~` at 154 and `\partCombineAutomatic a''2.~(` at 158.
- recTwoBsnOne: `f,1\mf\< | \partCombineUnisono f,1 | g,1\ff | g,1 | aes,1\fff | \partCombineAutomatic f,2 g,2`.
- recOne.ily, recOneClOne: delete `\partCombineApart` before bar 123 and put it at bar 127 (`\partCombineApart g'4->\mf\<`).
- recOneBsnOne: start with `\partCombineUnisono r8 g8\ff(`, then `\partCombineAutomatic c4\mf\<` at 127.
- devFluteOne: `\partCombineApart r8 b'8 aes''4.->` at 109 and `\partCombineAutomatic aes'''1\fff` at 114.
- devOboeOne and devTptOne at 82: `\partCombineApart r8 g'8 es''4.-> d''8 des''8 c''8 | \partCombineAutomatic d''4->`. This also removes their `\ff`.
- devClTwo at 82: change `r8\ff es'8` to `r8 es'8\ff`.
- devBsnOne: `\partCombineApart r8 g,8 es4-> r2 | r8 g8 es'4-> r2 | \partCombineAutomatic c'2\ff`. This removes the `\f` at 80.

### 2. Cello melodies in bass clef up to six ledger lines (47–55, 139–147, 167–170)
At 52–53 the cello's f'' sits on six ledger lines in bass clef, and the coda phrase at 170 reaches c'' with four. Gould's rule is tenor clef for the cello tenor register and treble above it. Dvořák 7 (Violoncello lines 123, 190) uses tenor clef for this. Fixes (tested; the result is clean):
- tTwo.ily, tTwoVc: `\clef tenor c'2.~` at 47, `\clef treble es'4( c''2--` at 51, and `\clef bass aes,8\p(` at 56.
- recTwo.ily, recTwoVc: `\clef tenor e2.~` at 139 and `e1\p | \clef bass \recTwoArp`.
- coda.ily, codaVc: `\clef tenor es'2.~(\mp` at 167 and `\clef bass g,1\pp(` at 171.

### 3. Page turn splits the ♭VI climax right after K (154 | 155)
Page 15 ends with bar 154 squeezed into the last bar of the page. The climax 154–157 starts on one page and finishes on the next. Gould: page breaks should fall at rehearsal marks and phrase starts. Fix in global.ily: `s1*15 \pageBreak \mark \default` (mark K). On its own this reflows the score to 16 pages with crammed systems, so also add `page-count = 17` to `\paper` in score.ly. Tested: 17 pages, no warnings, and 154–162 sit together on one page. Without `page-count`, `\noPageBreak` at 155–157 produced a "compressing over-full page" warning, so I do not recommend it.

### 4. Tempo and rehearsal marks appear only at the top of the system
Rehearsal letters and tempo words print only above the flute staff, which is up to 30 staves away from the strings. Gould and printed full scores repeat them above the first violins. The Mutopia Dvořák 7 does this (Staves.ly line 61). Fix in score.ly, Violins I staff: `\with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" instrumentName = "Violins I" ... }`. Leave the Score-level engravers as they are. Tested: C, J and the tempo words print above both Fl and Vln I, with no warnings and no layout change.

### 5. Percussion: tam-tam strokes unlabelled, no l.v. ties
At 114 and 171 a whole note sits in a space of a staff named "Cymbals / Tam-tam" with only "l.v." above it, so the reader cannot tell which instrument plays. None of the three l.v. strokes has a laissez-vibrer tie. Gould: on a multi-instrument staff, name the instrument at each entry, and mark l.v. with ties to nothing. Fixes (tested):
- dev.ily: `tt1\fff\laissezVibrer^\markup { "Tam-tam" \italic "l.v." }`
- coda.ily: `tt1\pp\laissezVibrer^\markup { "Tam-tam" \italic "soft stick, l.v." }`
- recTwo.ily: `cymr1:32\pp\<^\markup { "2: Susp. cym." \italic "soft sticks" } | cymc1\fff\laissezVibrer^\markup { "1: Crash cym." \italic "l.v." }`

The shorter labels also stop the two stacked texts at 155–156 crowding each other.

### 6. Flute solo entries lack "1." (39, 77); the ottava at 40 is unnecessary
Fl 2 rests at 39–40 and 77–81, but the combiner prints no "1.", because it does not repeat a label after a rest. The ottava at 40 does follow the rule (Fl 2 rests). But it sets the 16th turn below the staff when it peaks at f♯''' on three ledger lines, which flutists read at sight, and the same figure in Ob/Vln I has no ottava. I tested removing it, and the bar reads cleanly. `\partCombineSoloI` did not print the label (tested), so add it explicitly:
- trans.ily, transFluteOne: `r8 d''8\mf\<^\markup \bold "1." bes''4->` and delete `\ottava #1` / `\ottava #0` in bar 40.
- dev.ily, devFluteOne bar 77: `r2 r8 es''8\mp^\markup \bold "1." ces'''4->~`.

The markup itself is untested, but it is plain LilyPond. This keeps the cycle-15 ottava rule (no `\ottava` where the other player sounds).

### 7. "largamente" at 156 sits only in the violins
"largamente" is a tempo change for the whole orchestra, but only the violins carry it. Move it to global.ily: replace `s1*15 \mark \default s1*7` with `s1*15 \mark \default s1*2 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "largamente" s1*5`, and delete the violins' markup. Also at 163: "Allegro appassionato (Tempo dell'Allegro)" says the same thing twice. "Tempo dell'Allegro", as at 69, is enough.

### 8. Bar numbers collide with high flute notes and rests (27–29, 109–112, 123)
"28" and "110" touch noteheads, and "109", "111" and "123" sit on top of eighth rests. Gould: bar numbers must clear the music. I did not test a fix. One option is `\override BarNumber.Y-offset` or putting the numbers below the bottom staff. Low priority.

## Checked and fine
pizz./arco placement and roman type; "(low C instr.)" on the C2 at 92; Bsn "1. solo, espr." at 77; "poco più tranquillo" and "Tempo dell'Allegro"; Vc 121–122 triplets and 16ths; "sostenuto" at 115; flute ottava 98–100 (Fl 2 rests); key-change cancellations at 47, 77, 97, 103, 139 and 163; harp pedal names; timpani mutas.

**Do not touch:** the harp pedal markings, the rehearsal letters skipping I, the staff-level dynamics setup from cycle 15, and the string technique words (div./unis., sul tasto/ord.).
