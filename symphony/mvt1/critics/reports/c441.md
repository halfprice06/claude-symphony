## Findings

No change is applied. The scan finds no conflict with the reading. I also looked for places where a theme could sing more. The candidates are listed below, ranked by how much each matters. For each I give the reason it stays, so later critics need not reopen it.

### Finding 1. Bars 64–66: Fl 1 leaves the S forte answer at 65.3, before the apex B♭5 at 66. Keep it.

**Question.** At 64 the violins begin the forte answer of S in octaves (A♭5/A♭4). Fl 1 joins Vln I at mf: A♭5–G5–F5–E♭5–D♭5, with a diminuendo from 65, and stops on D♭5 at 65.3. The violins then climb F5–A♭5 to the apex B♭5 at 66.1 (c378's swell) and fall to the deceptive cadence at 68 without wind. PDF 67 puts doubling where "expression and colour is broad", and PDF 76 lists Vln + Fl as a natural unison. One could argue that the flute should stay through the apex, so the colour does not drop out mid-phrase.

**Assessment.** The flute's two bars are the first phrase member, and that member is complete: A♭–G–F–E♭–D♭, closing on the D♭ chord at 65.3. The second member (65.4–68) carries the swell to B♭5, the diminuendo and the rit. 66–68.2 into the deceptive cadence. Its expression is falling and intimate, which is the case where PDF 66–67 wants a single timbre ("Phrases or melodies demanding diversity of expression alone should be entrusted to ... simple timbres"). Holding the flutes back from 65.4 to 68.3 also keeps the Fl 1·2 run at 68.4 fresh as the f arrival of the codetta. c149 set this entry for that reason (it moved the flute off the soft D♭6 at 62–63, PDF 142). Extending the flute would put its colour on 66–67 and use up that freshness. No change.

### Finding 2. Bars 56–68: the expressive counterline is Cl 1 + Hn 2 in unison, not a single colour. Keep it.

**Question.** PDF 66–67 says a phrase "demanding diversity of expression" should go to one plain instrument, and that "an instrument enjoys greater independence and freedom when used as a solo". The counterline under the violins' S at 56–68 is marked "espr." and is played by Cl 1 and Hn 2 at the same pitch.

**Assessment.** Three passages of the same chapter answer this. PDF 74–75: in wind + brass unison "the tone of the brass predominates", the wood-wind "softens and rarefies it", and the pair gives "a finer legato effect than when the latter instrument plays alone". Horn + clarinet is one of the pairs named. PDF 66: the clarinet is "weak in the middle compass", and this line lies on its throat tones (concert G4–B♭4, written A4–C5), so a solo clarinet would fade under the violins at 60–66. PDF 72: the horn in piano is "genial and poetic". Here the clarinet is the colour on the horn's legato line. It is not a second solo competing with the first. The line is protected (c147, c401), and nothing in the reading answers the stated reason. No change.

### Finding 3. Bars 103–106: three winds in unison on the P head. Keep it.

**Question.** Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 play the P head together at f (F5–A♭5) while Hn 1, Bsn 1 and the cellos play the S head at mf. By PDF 66, three timbres in unison lose individual expression.

**Assessment.** This is the passage where the two heads are combined, and PDF 67 places unison doubling in loud passages "where expression and colour is broad". The upper head has to balance a horn, a bassoon and the cellos below it. PDF 65 (combination c) describes oboe + clarinet as "a fuller quality than that of either instrument heard separately", with "the bright, 'chest' quality of the clarinet" prevailing in the high compass. This line lies there (F5–A♭5), and PDF 66 cites 2 Ob + 3 Cl as an example (Ex. 199–201). The layout keeps natural order: the woodwinds are on top and the horn with the cellos is below (rows 4 and 27). Protected by c107 and c437 ("103–110 combined heads"). No change.

### Finding 4. Bars 13–14 and 41–44: the horn call's register follows PDF 71–72. Keep it.

The call E♭3–C4–B3 is written B♭3–G4–F♯4 for horn in F. That is the horn's middle register, which PDF 71 names for "fanfares, trumpet calls or flourishes". The four-horn unison at 41–42 is the "extreme power and resonance" of PDF 73. Hn 1's p restatement an octave higher at 43–44 (written B♭4–G5–F5) moves into the "full, clear, ringing" upper register and links to S. This growth from low and solo, to four horns, to high and soft is the best brass writing in the movement. Movement II takes the E♭3–C4 span in the same player and at the same pitch (coherence/mvt1.md, bar 13). No change.

### Finding 5. Bars 46.4–52 and 146.4–150: S given to the horn, against PDF 73's "passion" rule. Keep it.

PDF 73 says phrases "charged with passion or geniality" become "sickly and insipid" in the brass. S is the movement's most lyrical tune. Each time, though, the horn plays it softly (p dolce, mp) and blended with strings: the cellos at 47, the violas at 147. PDF 72 makes the soft horn the exception ("greater scope in the choice of melodies"). PDF 79 calls horn + cellos "a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone", and Ex. 92 (PDF 269) has a horn line with the violas, pp. The horn is never alone on the tune, and it leaves before the cello's climb to F5 at 52.4, where Cl 1 takes over. Movement II's theme A depends on this colour (coherence/mvt1.md, bars 46–52). No change.

### Finding 6. The themes judged for memorability. No change.

- **Motto.** Six notes, one leap and a chromatic return, in a new single colour at each return (B.cl + Vc, C.a., Fl, trombones, Cl, Vln I). This follows PDF 64–67 on soft solo melody. It is the idea a listener keeps.
- **Horn call.** Three notes with an unresolved leading tone. It grows through 41–44 as described in Finding 4, and the scherzo and finale answer it.
- **S.** A period of long notes on strong beats, with a rising sixth in each phrase. It is in the lineage of Dvořák's and Brahms's lyrical second themes, and it is the tune most likely to be hummed. Its four settings each use a combination this chapter recommends (rows 21, 24, 25, 27, 28).
- **P.** The least singable theme because of its busier rhythm, but its head is the motto, and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch.

I did not find a theme change that the reading supports and that would justify notifying three other movements.
