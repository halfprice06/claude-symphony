## Findings

### 1. Bar 116: the augmented sixth turns into V4/2 of V by A♭ to A♮, then falls back to G (♭6–♮6–5). Applied in scratch.

**Bars.** 116 to 117.

**Problem.** Bar 116 opens on an inverted French sixth over the C bass: C–F♯–A♭–D, with D6 on top. On beat 3, Ob2, Cl1, Hn1, Tbn2 and Vln II move A♭ to A♮. The chord becomes V4/2 of V (D–F♯–A–C). Hn1 and Tbn2 then go to G at 117. Bar 115 is ♭II, and bar 117 brings A♭ back at once as the ♭9 over the G pedal. The A♮ is therefore a two-beat blip between two A♭s, in the voices that carry the ♭VI thread through the development.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 402 (Unit 23 review, point 3): "once introduced, ♭6 tends to move to 5"; the succession ♮6–♭6–5 is good and ♭6–♮6–5 is poor. Bar 116 is the poor form, A♭–A♮–G, in four wind voices and Vln II. The A♮ also undoes what the bar is for. A&S PDF 423–424 (Ex. 25-10) treats V4/2 of V as one way to support ♯4 before V, but here ♯4 (F♯) is already supported by the augmented sixth, which leads to V more strongly. Adding A♮ gives a weaker chord in place of a stronger one and creates a ♮6/♭6 cross relation with bar 117 in the same voices. The same score already uses the good order at 162–164 (Hn3 A♮–A♭–G), so at 116 the order is reversed for no reason. The bass C to G at 117 stays as it is. It is the transferred resolution that A&S PDF 407–408 allow (Ex. 24-13: the bass of a V4/2 skips, and the tone of resolution appears in an upper voice). B arrives in the Vln II, Ob1 and Cl1 pickup at 117 and in Cl2 and Va.

**Protection.** c223 and c252 protect the wedge at 111–117: its outer voices (bass C, top line to D6) and the F♯ doubling into 117. The fix keeps all three. Only the inner A♭ is held where it had moved to A♮.

**Fix** (sections/dev.ily):

```
Ob2  line 63:   aes'2\< a'2        ->  aes'1\<
Cl1  line 94:   aes'2\ff a'4 r4    ->  aes'2.\ff r4
Hn1  line 186:  aes'2 a'4 r4       ->  aes'2. r4
Tbn2 line 267:  aes,2 a,2          ->  aes,1
VlnII line 338: <fis' aes'>2:32 <fis' a'>2:32  ->  <fis' aes'>1:32
```

The bar now reads ♭II (115), then the augmented sixth for the whole bar (116), then V (117). A♭ is a common tone into the ♭9 at 117, and where it moves, it moves A♭ to G.

**Applied in scratch:** yes.

### 2. Bar 51: applied V6/5 of IV with the temporary leading tone tripled and the root missing in the sustained parts. Applied in scratch.

**Bars.** 51 to 52.

**Problem.** Bar 51 is V6/5 of IV (A♭7 over C, going to D♭ at 52). The sustained string chord is C–E♭–G♭ only. The root A♭ sounds once, in a harp arpeggio. C, the temporary leading tone, is in three places: the Cb and harp bass, the Va C4, and the tune in Hn1 and Vc on C5. The held sound is a diminished triad on C with the leading tone tripled, not a dominant 7th.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 419: do not double the temporary leading tone of an applied dominant. The Unit 25 review (PDF 439) repeats this. A&S PDF 422: an applied chord is heard as an altered diatonic chord, so its root needs to be present for the chord to read as V6/5 of IV rather than as a °6 on C. The tune's C5 is protected and resolves by transfer into the bass D♭ at 52, which A&S PDF 406–407 allow (resolution into the bass). This means the viola's C4 has no work to do.

**Protection.** "Neutral viola pad 47–52": the viola stays a single held note in the same register, at the same dynamic. Only its pitch changes, from C4 to A♭3. The tune is not touched.

**Fix** (sections/tTwo.ily, line 111, tTwoVla bar 51):

```
<c' es'>1   ->   <aes es'>1
```

A♭3 is held into bar 52 (`<aes f'>`) as a common tone. The sustained chord is now C–A♭–E♭–G♭, a complete V6/5 of IV with one leading tone in the bass, one in the tune, and none in the pad.

**Applied in scratch:** yes.
