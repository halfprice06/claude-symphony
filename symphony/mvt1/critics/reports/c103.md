## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 69–70: the codetta lands on F minor for the third time in eight bars. APPLIED.
**Problem.** The approach to the EEC (72) arrives on vi (F minor) three times:
- 63→64: E♮→F, at the S climax (protected);
- 67→68: the deceptive cadence onto F;
- 69→70: since cycle 102, the codetta's bass is A♭–E♮ | F, which is V6/5 of vi going to vi.

As a result, the tonic regained after the deceptive cadence lasts two beats (69, beats 1–2) before the music again turns toward F minor. The codetta's formal job is to supply the satisfactory PAC in A♭ (the EEC). Instead it starts by restating the problem the deceptive cadence created.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 203: deceptive cadences "produce a suspense that dissipates only when the total stability is regained, usually through an authentic cadence". PDF 204: what follows the deceptive cadence is "a varied repetition of the whole four-bar phrase". The phrase is repeated; the move to VI is not tonicized again.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i closing group, MCTL harmony onsets 530–602: I–vii°7/ii–ii–vii°7–I–vii°7/V–I6/4–V7–I, twice, then I–♯iv°–iv–I. The applied chords serve only ii and V, the tonic returns every second bar, and vi does not appear at all. That is the same passage critic 102 cited for adding a chromatic chord.
- Caplin (search summary above): a codetta is a tonic prolongation, often with a subdominant inflection.

**Fix** (clos.ily). Bars 69–71 become I–I6 | ii7–vii°7/V | V7. The bass is A♭–C | B♭–D♮ | E♭.
- The codetta keeps a chromatic applied chord, which was cycle 102's aim.
- The bass rises C … D–E♭, which reverses the motto's D–D♭–C.
- Melody (Fl1, Ob1, Tpt1, Vln I), counter (Cl1, Hn1·2, Vln II), bar 72, timpani and dynamics are unchanged.

### 2. 147–160: the approach to the recapitulation climax matches Tchaikovsky. No change.
**What I checked.** Since cycle 80, 147–155 run pp, the push begins at 156 (poco animando), 158 is ff and 160 is fff.

**Evidence.**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: 305 pp/p ("Andante come prima"), 309 "incalzando, poco cresc." at mf, 313 fff "incalzando". The push lasts 4 bars, as it does here (156–160).
- In both scores the climax is reached from a quiet recapitulated theme by a short, pressed crescendo.
- The order of entries at 156–158 follows Rimsky-Korsakov p. 112 (PDF 130): "gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass". Here the strings and harp enter at 156, Cl and Bsn at 157, and all brass at 158.

**Verdict.** This supports the protections of 147–157 (cycles 80 and 83).

### 3. 122–131: the climax, collapse and retransition match Dvořák 9/i. No change.
**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/i has the development climax ff at 260, the collapse at 261–268 (p/pp) and the retransition at 269–276 (pp → f), with the recapitulation at 277. That is 8 + 8 bars of 2/4, about 14 s. Here 123–126 sostenuto and 127–130 a tempo are 4 + 4 bars of 4/4, about 17 s.

**One difference.** The recapitulation arrives ff (131), as in Beethoven 9 and Brahms 1, where Dvořák's arrives mf/fp. This is a legitimate choice.

### 4. Large-scale tonal plan. No change.
**The plan.** i (C) – VI (A♭) – development: fugato G–C–F–B♭ minor, B minor, then a step-wise rise D–E–F (V of F minor at 110) to the V pedal at 117–130 – i.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 449: in minor, the second key "is not a final goal. Instead, it usually forms the midpoint of a bass-arpeggio motion from I to V, often expanded by a passing IV". In K. 457 (Ex. 26-8) the V is reached through IV and the leading tone is made prominent before the recapitulation. This score does the same with VI in place of III: F at 110–111, then G with B♮ from 117.

### 6. Saved colours. Verified.
**Evidence.** Adler, p. 350 (PDF 37): "One of the most successful ways of building climaxes is by holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event". His example is Schubert 9/i, where soft trombones mark the joins of the form.

**This score:**
- cymbals at 160 only;
- tam-tam at 122 (hard) and 175 (soft);
- the trombones' pp chorale only at 175–179.

**Option considered and rejected.** A soft trombone chord under the flute motto at 73–76 would rhyme the two closing zones, as Schubert does. I do not recommend it, because it would anticipate the chorale planted for movement III (cycle 20).

**Verdict.** Keep cycle 83's rule: add no slow time.
