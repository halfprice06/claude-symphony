### 2. The motto's tail is harmonised differently at each arrival, as Sebesky's Ex. 3/3A asks (keep)

Ex. 3 and 3A (PDF 19, read from the image) bring the same melody to the same resting note (a held C under a fermata) over two different chord successions; the text says "avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice". This is also the cyclic engine of the symphony: the listener recognises the motto's D–D♭–C by its line while its meaning changes. In v480 the four full arrivals of the tail are each harmonised differently:
- 3–4: bare octaves (B.cl, Vc, Cb), no harmony.
- 7–8: D♭ major over F (N6) → C major, the soft Picardy, under the cor anglais.
- 120–121: Tbn 1·2 D♭4 over the G pedal with Vln I/II and violas on B♭–D♭–F (B♭ minor over G) → C minor 6/4 over G at 121 into the fff 122.
- 178–182: the chorale bass moves C–D♭–C, so 178.3 is a root-position D♭ triad for two beats as a neighbour chord; at 180.3 Vln I's D♭5 is a neighbour over a C pedal; 182 is C major with the violas' E♮ alone.

The finale's coda then takes the same tail over its C pedal (IV 227, 229) in C major. Each answer is new, and none repeats another. The 8 and 182 Picardy chords are protected (c390), and they are approached by different harmony (a structural N6 at 7, a neighbour over the tonic pedal at 180), so the rule is met.

Fix: none. A precision for the record, not a change: mvt1.md calls 178 a "neighbour D♭" inside the tonic. In the MIDI the whole texture at 178.3–4 is a root-position D♭ triad (B.tbn, Vc D♭3, Cb D♭2, Tbn 2 F3, Vla A♭3, Tbn 1 D♭4, Vln II F4) over the moving bass. That is still a neighbour chord, not a key, so the "D♭ as a key" reserve holds, but the record should say "root-position neighbour chord at 178.3" so the finale thread does not count it as a passing tone.

### 3. No finale or scherzo theme should be planted further (keep)

The lens allows foreshadowing. Sebesky's rule is to vary a motive already heard before bringing in a new one (PDF 11). Movement I already carries the seeds listed in mvt1.md (78–79 scherzo theme A bar 2 in three octaves; P's second-bar rhythm = motive x; Hn 1 D♯5 at 106; the horn call's sixth filled by movement II's theme A and the trio tune; 37 the first E♭-minor sound; 12 the finale's first chord; 179–180 the pulse). Adding the finale's F1 or F2 would introduce a new idea into the movement that owns the motto, and it is protected (c450: "no seed of the finale's F1/F2"). No new evidence answers that reason.

Fix: none.

### 4. Considered and rejected: a second E♮ in the last chord (182)

Within this lens the last chord is the hinge of the whole arc. The question a master would ask is whether one viola E♮ at ppp is enough to make the turn to major heard, or whether a second voice (for example Vln II E5 instead of G4) would make the moment more vivid. I reject it on the reading and on the cycle:
- Sebesky's economy rule (PDF 12) asks whether a note "makes a legitimate difference". A second E♮ would make the major third louder, and that is exactly what the cycle needs kept back: "Closing the E♭/E♮ thread in the violas" is on the finale's reserve list, and movement II turns the violas' E♮ back to E♭ at its start. A stronger major third at 182 would partly close what movement II and the finale reopen and close.
- Sebesky's focus rule (PDF 13) is met as it stands: at 182 Vln I holds C5 and the violas' change from E♭4 (179–181, morendo) to E4 is the only moving line in the chord, so the ear goes to it.
- The last chord and the five tied parts are quoted (II 1; protected c410, c420), and Vln II's G4 crosses into movement II.

Applied in scratch copy: no.
