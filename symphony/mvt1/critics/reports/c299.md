## Findings

### 1. Holistic: against Franck, Symphony in D minor/i (no change)

Franck's first movement is the obvious model for a cyclic C-minor movement with a slow motto introduction, and this score holds up against it in the points a conductor checks first.

- The Allegro theme is the motto quickened. Franck's Allegro non troppo takes the Lento's three-note motto at a faster tempo; here the P head (`r8 g8( es'4.->) d'8( des'8 c'8`) is the motto G–E♭–D–D♭–C in diminution, so every P statement is also a motto statement. This is Franck's own procedure, done with the same economy.
- Every return is rescored, which is what the reading asks of a repeat (adler2 PDF 209, 213): the motto is heard in B.cl/Vc/Cb (1), C.a. (5), Fl 1 over the A♭ pedal (73), trombones and horns ff (117), the pp trombone chorale (175) and Vln I sul D (177). Franck repeats his Lento–Allegro pair in F minor with almost the same scoring; this score varies more.
- Franck's scoring is the usual objection to his symphony: whole sections doubled for long spans, close to organ registration. Adler warns against too much colour (PDF 203) but the band-versus-orchestra comparison (PDF 234) shows that the orchestral version wins by alternating choirs. This score alternates choirs at the seams (33, 44, 47, 56, 69, 73, 77, 85, 97, 103, 111, 117, 123, 131, 143, 147, 158, 163, 171, 175) and keeps one combination inside each section (PDF 208). On this count it is more transparent than Franck.
- Where Franck is larger: he states the Lento–Allegro pair twice in the exposition, has two second-group themes (the cantabile and the ff "faith" theme), and builds long sequential crescendos in the development; the movement lasts about 17 minutes. This one is 182 bars and about 7 minutes. The proportions were measured and protected (c215, c243, c283) because the movement ends attacca into the Adagio and the finale carries the symphony's weight. I do not reopen them: the four-movement plan answers the difference, and nothing in the assigned reading bears on length.
- Franck begins his recapitulation with the motto in canon, ff, and ends in D major ff. This movement prepares its recapitulation with the trombone motto ff at 117–121 and saves the major-key brass chorale for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md, "Do not reuse"). That is a cyclic decision for the whole symphony, not a weakness of this movement.

A conductor who knows the Franck would accept this movement as a master's compact first movement. The score should stay as it is in structure.

Score reference: Franck, Symphony in D minor, full score, https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_in_D_minor,_FWV_48_(Franck,_C%C3%A9sar).

### 2. Coda 175–182 returns the opening colours (no change)

Ravel ends "Petit Poucet" with "the return of the exact opening colours for the last five measures" (adler2 PDF 224; Ex. XVI-12, PDF 223, last system: muted violins and four solo violas pp). Bars 175–179 give the motto to B.cl, cellos sul G and basses, as at 1–4, under the trombone chorale, and the timpani end on a C roll ppp at 181–182, as at 1–4. Bar 171 starts with the cor anglais as at 5. The final chord (C2–G2–C3–G3–C4–E4–G4–C5) is spaced wide at the bottom and close at the top, the spacing Adler praises in Bizet's last chords (PDF 213). The solo cello's end at 174 is marked by Bsn 1 and the timpani joining, the device Adler notes in Schoenberg's Brahms (PDF 230, item 3). Keep (protected c228, c249, c543).

### 3. 27–32 and 167–170 have the same layout (no change)

The reading asks that a repeat be rescored (Dvořák's m. 17 "a bit more open", Bizet's repeat "fuller", PDF 209, 213). 167–170 repeats the 27–32 layout. Dvořák's own repeat keeps "the basic colour" (PDF 209), and here the alikeness is the point: the coda's forte head must sound as the exposition's tutti so that the stop chord at 170 rhymes with 26 and 138, and the finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part at its 199–204. 167–170 already differs in the top (Vln I·II div. tremolo instead of the head) and in the head's register (Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2 a2, not violins). Keep (protected c425).

### 4. 97–102: cellos at pitch with Vln II (no change)

Schoenberg doubles at the octave rather than at pitch "for he does not have two distinct colours … doubling at pitch, the violins and cellos would swallow each other up" (adler2 PDF 229). That concerns two soloistic string entries in succession. At 97–102 the B-minor S is a ff tutti statement in three colours at one pitch (C.a., Vln II sul G, Vc), with Fl 1 and Vln I an octave above and the largest doubling on the tune (PDF 209). The composer already rejected an octave-down cello at c296 for range reasons; the reading gives no new reason to move it. Keep (c231, c296).

### 5. 103–106: Ob 2 + Cl 1·2 unison on the P head (no change)

Schoenberg's Op. 43B replaces "a typically thick band colour of clarinets and oboes in unison with the clear solo voice of a trumpet", at p instead of f (adler2 PDF 239). Bars 103–106 have Ob 2 and both clarinets on the P head at f. I checked whether a single voice would be better. It would not: at 103–106 the P head sounds against the S head in Hn 1 + Bsn 1 + Vc (mf) with Hn 3·4 below, and the heads must be equally clear (layers ranked, PDF 203). A single oboe at f would lose to the horn; the Ob/Cl unison gives the P head the edge the combination needs, and 107–110 then thins to B.cl, violas and Fl 1, the drop-out Adler describes in Dvořák (PDF 213). Keep (c274, c279).
