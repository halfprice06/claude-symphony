## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bar 77: viola tremolo doubles the bassoon solo's head note

**Problem.** The development opens with the bassoon's motto solo, marked "1. Solo", mf espr.: `r8 es( ces'4.->) bes( beses aes) | aes2`. The accented C♭4 is the most important note of the statement. Under it the violas play `ces'1:32\p`, a tremolo on the same C♭4, and Vln II hold E♭4 a third above. So the bassoon's head note is in unison with a string tremolo, and the whole line lies inside the tremolo chord.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible ... for utmost effectiveness, especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments, it is an important idea to remember." PDF 267 (Mozart, XVII-10): when the wind soloist enters, "the orchestra is reduced drastically" and is set apart from the solo. In XVII-15 (PDF 274) Beethoven keeps the bassoon's middle-register line clear of the strings' simple chords. The 33–34 bassoon solo in this score already follows this rule: there it has only the double-bass pedal below it.

**Fix.** Put the viola on E♭3 for bar 77 only. The chord (A♭ minor: Cb A♭, Vla/Vln II E♭, bassoon C♭) stays complete, the C♭ is then carried by the soloist alone, and the viola moves to C♭4 in 78, where the bassoon holds A♭3 below it.

```
devVla = {
  es1:32\p | ces'2:32 b2:32 | b1:32 | c'1:32\< |   % was ces'1:32\p in bar 77
```

This does not touch the protected motto dialogue 77–83 or the key plan 77–82. Only a sustaining pitch inside the same chord changes.

### F2 (applied). Bar 173: viola crosses above the cello solo

**Problem.** In the coda duet (cello second-theme head against the cor anglais motto), the violas are the only string accompaniment. On 173 beats 3–4 the viola plays F4 (`c'2 f'2`). The cello's line there is `f'4. es'8 des'4 f'8 aes'8`, so on beat 3 the cello is on D♭4 and the viola sounds a third above it. For a beat the accompaniment is higher than the solo.

**Evidence.** The same rule, Adler PDF 274. All the solo examples in the chapter keep the accompaniment below or far above a string soloist: Tchaikovsky XVII-3 (PDF 259) and Sibelius XVII-11 (PDF 269). PDF 279 (Bizet) adds that the composer is "very careful to keep the instruments and the voice quite separate". Critic 112 chose F4 to double the bass F of the N6 and to remove octaves with the CA (c112 F4). My fix keeps that second gain and answers the first one differently.

**Fix.**

```
codaVla = { ... c'2\pp( b2 | c'1) | c'2 aes2 | es'2 d'2 | ...   % was c'2 f'2
```

The viola's A♭3 lies under the cello. The N6 is now complete (F in Bsn 2, A♭ in Vla, D♭ in CA and Vc), and critic 112's PDF 76 rule says complete chords sound best. The viola moves C4→A♭3 against the CA's C5→D♭5 in contrary motion, so c112's octaves do not return. A♭3→E♭4 into 174 is a fourth in an inner voice. Critic 112 wanted the bass F doubled; it is now single (Bsn 2), and ♭2̂ is doubled, not tripled as before c112. This revises one note of a c112 fix inside the protected coda 171–182. The duet's pitches, the motto and all dynamics are unchanged.

### Checked and left as written (reading-based verdicts)

- 47–55. The violas' dyads double the horn/cello tune's long notes at the unison (47 C4, 48 F4, 49 D♭4, 51 E♭4, 52 F4). Adler PDF 264 (Mozart XVII-9) allows heterophonic doubling of a soloist in a strongly contrasting colour: "does not diminish the soloist's role in the least". The horn and cello against pp divided strings is such a contrast, so no change. Hn 2–4 are tacet under the solo horn, as in Mozart.
- 56–59. The viola's protected E♭–E♮–F line is also the long-note skeleton of the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline. This is heterophony again (PDF 264), with different rhythms (PDF 260), so it follows the reading.
- 97–102. The Cl a2 and viola mirror arpeggios lie in the same octave as the tune's lowest doubling (Vln II + CA on D4). PDF 274 says rhythmic contrast "lessens the importance" of registral distance, and here the eighths are f under a ff tune. The block is protected, and I see no case strong enough to reopen it.
- 5–8. The CA solo has Vln II a step to a sixth below it, inside the protected string swell. The contrast of p espr. solo against pp sustained strings is enough (PDF 274), so no change.
- 145. The oboe's dolce entry grows out of the held G4 in Cl 1 and Vln I. This is the soloist emerging from the wind chord (PDF 273, XVII-13) and the protected G–E pivot, so no change.
