## Findings

No finding changes a note. They are ranked by how much they bear on the gap between this score and a master's.

### F1. The development's large-scale motion follows the reading's model of equal division held in check (77–130). Keep.

PDF 572 warns that "a predominance of equal division would weaken the gravitational attraction of the tonic", and says the masters fit such passages into music "mainly based on very different procedures". The development is 105.3 s long, and both of its plans are equal divisions:
- 77–82 (15 s): the bass falls A♭–E–C–A♭. That divides the octave into major thirds, descending, with the A♭ triad at both ends (PDF 572–573). The E is reached as a d4 from A♭, which PDF 573 allows "for ease of reading".
- 97–122 (about 60 s): stations on B (97), D (103), F (111) and A♭ over G (122). This is a minor-third division, and it spells the stop chord B–D–F–A♭.

The reading's tests are all met:
- **Inside a seventh chord, not a tonic.** PDF 575–576 says a minor-third division works best inside a seventh chord, with one chord functioning and the rest subordinate. Here the four stations compose out vii°7 of C, and only the last one acts: A♭ falls to G over the dominant pedal at 122–123.
- **Directed motion between the plans.** PDF 578–579 says a symmetric bass needs falling fifths around it for direction. Between the two plans, the fugato (85–96, 22.9 s) runs D–G–C–F to B♭ minor in falling fifths.
- **Tonal gravity restored before the return.** The equal-division material ends in the longest single harmony of the movement: the G pedal at 117–130, 29.2 s. The 111–116 descent leads into it.

In timing terms, about 75 of the development's 105 s rest on symmetric plans, but each one is cut off by a directed fifth progression or by V. That is the proportion PDF 572 asks for.

Two points in this plan are less usual, and both stay:
- The 77–80 heads are minor, where PDF 552 and 573 say the usual triads are major. c492 answered this: the minor heads make the A♭-major ff at 82 the release.
- The B station is reached at 96→97 by a direct semitone shift from B♭ minor. It is protected since cycle 2, and it is the step that puts the stations on the stop chord's pitches.

Fix: none.

### F2. Bars 156–157: the reading's own tools for this approach were tested. Both fail here. Keep (protected); new evidence for the protection.

**The shortfall.** Critics 523, 533, 541, 542 and 544 call the two-bar climb into K the movement's one remaining weakness. Through this reading, the reason is plain when the recapitulation is compared with the exposition:
- **Exposition, 60–62.** The S phrase reaches its cadential 6/4 over three whole bars of falling chromatic bass: G♭–F♭–E♭, ♭7–♭6–5 in A♭. This is the descent of PDF 552–553, and the dump shows iv6 over F♭ at 61. The peak follows at 64.
- **Recapitulation, 156–158.** The larger climax is approached by B♭–F–G (♭VII – iv – cadential 6/4). It leaves the exposition's pattern at 157, where the transposed parallel would put A♭ (♭6) in the bass. So the bigger arrival gets the shorter and less directed approach.

**The reading's two fixes.** Both need A♭ in the bass at 157:
- (a) Restore the exposition's ♭6: iv6 over A♭ at 157. The pitch classes F–A♭–C are unchanged; only the bass moves.
- (b) The m7–M10 voice exchange of Ex. 31-22 (PDF 566). It is exactly the progression at 156, ♭VII♭7 → V7 in major: bar 156 already has A♭ on beat 4. Its bass falls B♭–A–A♭–G.

**Test.** I ran (a), the smaller of the two, in a separate scratch copy, /tmp/critic-553/t1/work. Cb and Bsn 2 took A♭ for the whole of 157 (aes,,1 and aes,1; were f,,1 and f,1). The build had 0 warnings, 18 pages, and only the known bar-96 line in analysis.txt. The vl.txt diff against v553 added:
- `bar 157 beat 1.00 P8 clOne:Bb5->Ab5 cb:Bb1->Ab1` (and the same against the composite bass). Cl 1 carries an independent counter line here, F5–G5–B♭5 | A♭5–F5 (c337), so these are real parallel octaves, not doublings. They break the build rule.
- New octaves A♭→G into 158 and G→A♭ into 160, between Bsn 1/2 and the bass. These are doublings, but they tie the 157 bass to the 160 climax bass.

The dump also shows that the harp (F1/F2 on 157.1) and the cello arpeggio (F2 on 157.1) would have to move too. The cello and viola layout of 156–157 is protected (c228).

**Why it stays.** Three reasons:
- Fixing the Cl 1 octaves means rewriting the climax counter line, which leads into Ob 1 at 158 (c337, c408).
- A♭ in the bass at 157 uses up the step G→A♭ that makes 159→160 the climax (c542). PDF 573 (row 18) supports keeping ♭6 in the bass for the culminating moment.
- In the exposition, the same ♭6–5 bass needed a violin skip at 61.4 to avoid outer-voice octaves (c401). At 157 the outer voice is the protected S tune: A♭5 on 157.4, G5 on 157.4.5, G5 at 158. That tune cannot take a similar skip.

The weakness is real, but the reading's remedies cost more than they bring. Record line for HISTORY.md: "156–157: ♭6 bass at 157 (iv6, as at 61) and the ♭VII7–V7 m7–M10 exchange (A&S PDF 566) tested: Cl 1/bass octaves B♭→A♭ into 157, ♭6 bass spent before 160; keep."

### F3. The lament wedge 111–116 is used once, as a transition; Beethoven 9/i uses the same bass as a closing ostinato. Keep.

The movement's chromatic basses all sit in transitions and approaches: 27–31 rising, 60–62 falling, 111–116 falling, and the motto tail at 175–179. None of them is a goal. That is where PDF 567 places such passages ("the transitional element").

Bars 111–116 follow PDF 552–556 closely:
- The bass falls one step per bar, F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C (11.4 s).
- Inside that descent: C7/E (112), a 7–6 at 114, the single °7 at 114.3, D♭ at 115.
- The descent breaks into the Fr6 at 116 before V, as PDF 556 says it must.

**Corpus comparison.** Beethoven 9/i (reference corpus XML) uses the same lament bass, D–C♯–C–B | B♭–A–B–C♯ in crotchets, in its coda:
- a two-bar unit stated seven times (513–526);
- varied at 527–530;
- then cadencing at 531.

There the lament is the closing ostinato. Here it is a one-time descent into the dominant pedal. I considered whether the coda should take a lament ground in Beethoven's manner, and rejected it:
- The coda's motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C (175–179) is already this bass in short.
- The soft coda, the ppp attacca and the coda notes 167–182 are protected and quoted by the finale (mvt1.md).
- A ground repeated to ff would spend the C-minor tutti weight that the finale's 199–204 quotation needs.

Fix: none.

### F4. The stop chord follows the C. P. E. Bach model of a long-delayed resolution. Record only.

PDF 576–578 (Ex. 31-31b) describes a °7 that resolves at once on its first statement and is held for thirteen bars on its last. The B–D–F–A♭ chord here gets longer and less resolved as the movement goes on:
- 26: one bar, resolves to i.
- 138: one bar, turns to iv.
- 97–122: composed out across the development (F1).
- 161: two beats, as an embellishment of V7.
- 170: under a fermata over the C pedal, left open for the finale's B→C.

Its resolution is put off as long as possible, as in the Bach example. This adds reading support to the protections of 26/138/170 and to the "Do not reuse" item. Fix: none.

## Where the score stands against a master in this lens

The chromatic and symmetric techniques of Unit 31 are used where the reading says they work, and each is held in check by a directed progression. The development's equal divisions are subordinate to V. The chromatic basses serve as transitions. The stop chord's resolution is delayed to the end.

The one place a listener may feel the form hurry is still 156–157. This pass tested the reading's own remedies for it, and they create parallel octaves and spend the ♭6 bass needed at 160. I do not recommend a change.
