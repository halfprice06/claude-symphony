## Findings

The lens asks whether movement I sets up the later movements and keeps back what the finale needs. Read against Unit 26, the movement's key plan is a textbook case of the plans the unit describes, and its plan also prefigures the four-movement plan. I found nothing in the score that stands between it and a master's work under this lens, so I propose no score change. The findings below record what I checked, in order of weight, so the next cyclic critic does not have to repeat it.

### 1. The movement's key plan is Beethoven's I – VI ‖ IV – V – I, and the symphony repeats it one level up (keep)

Bars 1–131. A&S PDF 454 gives Beethoven's sonata plan for Op. 111 and the Ninth: exposition I – VI, development IV – V, recapitulation I, with VI as the first step of a descent in thirds from I to IV (PDF 452–453, Ex. 26-13, 26-14). v340 follows it: C minor (15–32), A♭ major (47–76), development ending in F minor (110–116), dominant pedal (117–130), C minor (131). The four movements then take the same descent in thirds one step further: C – A♭ – E (= F♭) – C. Movement I shows that cycle in small at 77–83 (bass A♭–E–C), which is Unit 26's "tonicized chords within a sequence by thirds" (PDF 447, Ex. 26-6) in chromatic form. Movement II's A♭ is therefore heard as the key that movement I's exposition already set as its second pole, and the scherzo's E as the next step of the same descent. This is the sense of "tonal plans are no more haphazard than the succession of chords in a single phrase" (PDF 448). Fix: none.

### 2. The attacca join is the mixture relation the reading describes (keep)

Bars 166, 179–182 and movement II bars 1–2. A&S PDF 443: A♭ "functions as VI in C minor and can therefore relate indirectly to C major through mixture." Movement I ends on C major with the violas' E4 the only E♮ (182); movement II holds that chord at its bar 1 and turns the violas' E4 to E♭4 at bar 2 over A♭1. The one-note change E♮→E♭ is that mixture step, made audible by one section at one pitch. It is also the end of the E♭/E♮ viola thread (12, 147, 166, 179–182). This is the strongest cyclic link in the movement. Fix: none. Keep the thread pitches and the last chord.

### 3. The leading tone reaches the retransition before the recapitulation (keep; answers nothing that is open)

Bars 117–131. A&S PDF 449 on K. 457: a minor V "does not lead forcefully to I; before the recapitulation, therefore, the leading tone must appear." PDF 456: when V returns it "often supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly to I". In v340 the dominant pedal is major from 117 (B sounds at 117; Hn 3 B3 at 122–124 resolving at 125), B returns at 126–128, and the seventh F is in the texture at 117 and on beats 3–4 of 130, into C minor at 131. c233 and c263 protected "no B♮ 128–131"; the MIDI shows B at 126–128 and none at 129–130, which is consistent with that protection and with the reading. Fix: none.

### 4. The development's C-major triad is V of iv, as Mozart's in K. 457 (keep; new record line)

Bars 105, 110–112. A&S PDF 449 notes that the development of K. 457 opens with the main idea "startlingly, in C major", which "does not function as a tonic, but rather as V of IV (F minor)". v340 has the same function at a different place: at 110 beat 3 a soft C-major triad over C2 (Bsn 2, Cb C2; B.cl C3; Hn 3 C4; Vln II C4/E4; Fl 1 and violas G5) is V of F minor, resolving to F minor at 111; 105 and 112 are C7 chords in the same role. For the cyclic plan this matters because the finale relies on movement I never confirming C major at forte (mvt1.md, last seed line). The only C-major triads over a C bass in movement I are 8 (pp), 110 (p, V of iv), 147–155 and 166 (soft cadences) and 182 (ppp). None is forte and none in full brass. Fix: none. Record suggestion for the composer: add 110 to the seed list in coherence/mvt1.md as "C major heard as V of F minor (p), as K. 457's development; not a tonic".

### 5. The Do-not-reuse list holds in v340 (keep)

Checked in v340/full.midi: violins top A♭6 (Vln I at 31, 72, 122, 160; Vln II C6 at 152), no C7 in the violins; tuba highest F2 (31), no tuba melody; no clashed cymbals; trumpets silent 171–182, last note B4 unresolved at 170; Tpt 1 B→C only into minor chords (26→27, 138→139, end of 161); no falling major motto in full brass at forte (147–150 is Hn 1/violas/cellos at mp–mf); no root-position V–I in the coda. Theme record 35/35 unchanged against v340. Fix: none.

### 6. A wording point in coherence/mvt1.md, for the composer (record only)

The "Do not reuse" list in coherence/mvt1.md includes "C major as a key (the scherzo is told not to use it)", but movement I itself has C major as a key at 147–166 (key signature, two soft cadences). A reader of the list alone could take it to mean C major is unused before the finale. Recommended wording: "C major as a key in movements II and III (movement I has it only softly, 147–166, never confirmed at forte; the finale's 222→223 is the first strong arrival)". This changes no note. I did not edit the file (critics write only their report).
