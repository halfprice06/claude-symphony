## Findings

### 1. Bars 125–126: the horn call has no statement between the introduction and the coda. Restore it over the G pedal. Applied in scratch.

**The gap.** The horn call is one of the four ideas the later movements answer (III 3–6, IV 243–246). In movement I it has the fewest clear statements:
- 13–14: Hn 1 solo;
- 41–42: four low horns, with a different rhythm (finding 2);
- 175–177: Cl 1, pp, inside the coda.

c570 and c580 named this as the weakest cyclic link and found no place for it. Within this lens it is also the clearest shortfall in memorability. A listener who hears a two-bar call once in the Lento, and next hears it 160 bars later in a clarinet, will not recognise it.

**The place.** Bars 125–126 already carry the harmony of 13–14. I compared the two bar by bar on full.midi:

| | 13 → 14 | 125 → 126 |
|---|---|---|
| Bass | G2 (Cb, Timp roll, Bsn 2) | G1/G2 (Cbsn, Bsn 2, tuba, Timp roll) |
| Chord | C4/E♭4 over G (6/4), to V at 14 | C4/E♭4/G over G (i6/4), to V at 126.2 |
| C4 → B3 | Hn 1 alone | Hn 3, Cl 2, violas (the 4–3, c284) |

So the call's C4–B3 already sounds at its own pitch, and in a horn, at the very beat where 14 has it (126.2). Only the pickup E♭3 and the rising sixth are missing.

**The reading.**
- Adler PDF 23 asks the orchestrator to know which melody notes matter and how they are made to dominate. At 125–126 the C–B is filler. With the E♭ pickup in a solo horn it becomes the cyclic idea.
- PDF 26 (Ravel, from the image) shows a timpani pedal on the dominant giving the brass melody an "introductory flavour" that the tonic later resolves. The call stood over the G roll at 13–14, resolved at 15. It would stand over the same roll at 125–126, resolved at 131.
- PDF 20: Hindemith brings a theme back with new doublings around the same brass line. Here the solo E♭–C is joined at the C by Hn 3, Cl 2 and violas, so 13–14 stays the only undoubled horn melody, as mvt1.md records.

**The masters.** In the *Eroica*, first movement, a solo horn plays the main theme softly over the dominant just before the recapitulation, as a reminder. Tchaikovsky 4/i brings the introduction's brass fanfare back inside the Allegro at the formal joins. This movement's introduction sets up the motto and the call. The development's collapse onto V is the natural place to recall the second of these, softly, before the stretto (127–130) recalls the motto and the recapitulation (131) resolves V.

### 2. Bars 41–42: the four-horn call shortens the rising sixth's goal. Tested and rejected.

Every rising sixth in the movement makes its goal the long note: the motto's E♭ (2), the call's C (13), P's E♭ (15), S's C (47) and the echo's C5 (43–44). The four-horn call at 41–42 is the exception: E♭3 crotchet, C4 crotchet, B♭3 dotted minim. Adler PDF 23 would favour stressing the C, the appoggiatura over the E♭ chord.

I tested `c'2 bes2` in all four horns at 42 (scratch copy /tmp/critic-581/work2). vl.txt gained 22 parallel fifths at 42.3: C4→B♭3 in each horn against G→F in Fl 2, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II. The upper line moves G→F on beat 3. The present rhythm (horns move on beat 2, the upper line on beat 3) is what avoids those fifths. `c'2. bes4` would clip the ff B♭ into the p chord at 43.1. The echo at 43–44 already restores the long C. Keep 41–42.

### 3. Bars 175–177: the coda's call stays in Cl 1. Keep.

c570 considered Hn 1 doubling Cl 1 here and rejected it. I agree, with a reason from the reading.
- **Berlioz (PDF 30, Ex. XI-14):** the canon works by colour contrast. The clarinet stands apart from the pp trombone chorale it sits on.
- **Hindemith (PDF 19–20):** horns and trombones in unison or close register fuse. A horn at E♭4–C5 over three trombones would merge into the chorale.

With finding 1 the horn colour of the call is heard twice before the coda, so the clarinet's last statement no longer carries the call's whole identity.
