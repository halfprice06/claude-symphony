- I wrote a LilyPond listener (a Voice engraver) that dumps every string event of v338 (notes, ties, slurs, articulations, bow marks, dynamics, hairpins, tremolos, text, tempo). A Python script then simulates the bowing of each string part with the habits Adler describes:
  - an unslurred note takes a new bow (PDF 37);
  - a slur is one bow, and a tie continues the bow;
  - a bow mark on a tied note is a bow change;
  - an entry after a rest on the downbeat is a down-bow, and an off-beat entry (an anacrusis) is an up-bow (PDF 38);
  - printed ⊓/V marks are obeyed.
  For each bow the script gives its length in seconds from the tempo map. It flags ff/sf arrivals on up-bows, long crescendos on down-bows, long diminuendos on up-bows, bows that are too long for their dynamic, and repeated notes under a slur without a dash.
- I ran the same tool on v239, the version after c238's bowing pass, and compared the two bow by bow. That shows which later changes turned bowings round.
- I looked at score pages 5, 6, 13, 14, 16 and 17 at 150 dpi before and after my changes.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 45–55: the cellos' second theme now starts down-bow and every bow of the statement is reversed. Applied.

**Problem.** c281 (v282) split the cellos' held A♭3 at 45 into G3–A♭3, as two bows. That changed the parity of the bowing that follows:
- 45: G3 down, A♭3 up;
- 46: A♭3 down, G3 up;
- the S pickup E♭3 at 46.4 (mf, "dolce") is a down-bow.

The cellos play the theme with Hn 1 (the protected horn + cello statement). Every bow after the pickup is now the opposite of v239 and of the recapitulation:

| | Pickup | 47–48 (crescendo on C4) | 49 | 51 | 52 (diminuendo) | 54 | 55 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v239 and recap 146.4–155 | up | up | down | up | down | up | down |
| v338 exposition | down | down | up | down | up | down | up |

So in v338 the first crescendo of the theme is drawn toward the tip, and the diminuendo from F5 at 52 is drawn toward the frog. No one changed the bowing on purpose. It is a side effect of a harmony fix.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: "A performer will naturally begin an anacrusis, or upbeat, with an up-bow".
- Same page: "The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural ... there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow". The composer marks bowing "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit".
- The recapitulation (146.4, after a rest) already bows the theme this way.

**Fix.** In trans.ily, `g2( aes2) | aes2 g4 \clef tenor es4(\mf |`. The 5–6 motion G3–A♭3 under the clarinet's 7–6 goes under one pp bow of 2.0 s. Then 46 is A♭3 up, G3 down and the pickup up. No note, rhythm or dynamic changes.

This answers the protection of 43–47 (c236, c281): c281's notes and its suspension are kept, and the slur only restores the bowing that existed before.

**Checked in the simulation.** 46.4–55 now matches v239 and 146.4–155 bow for bow.

### 2. 166–170: the double basses play the crescendo down-bow, start the forte coda up-bow and play the ff stop chord at 170 up-bow. Applied.

**Problem.** The basses' bows run as follows:
- 164.3–165: the tied G2, one up-bow;
- 166: the tied C2 (p<), one down-bow, so the crescendo is drawn toward the tip;
- 167–169: 24 repeated f quavers, which therefore start up-bow;
- 170: the accented ff stop chord under the fermata comes up-bow.

Every other string part has 170 on a down-bow: the violins after tremolo, the violas from the ⊓ head, and the cellos by alternation. The script shows the same bowing in v239, so c238 missed it.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*, viewed): "A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid".
- PDF 38: a crescendo goes naturally with the up-bow, and the frog is heavier.
- PDF 42–43 (Ex. II-29): heavy strokes on down-bows.

**Fix.** In recTwo.ily, `c,2\p~\downbow c,2\<\upbow`. The bow changes on the tie at beat 3, where the crescendo starts. That gives:
- the crescendo on an up-bow;
- the first f quaver of 167 down-bow;
- by alternation of the 24 quavers, the ff C2 at 170 down-bow with the rest of the strings.

The notation is the one c316 used for Vln II and Cb at 12. Only marks are added: the notes, the 167–170 layout, the stop chord and the finale's reserve are unchanged.

### 3. 143–144: the last first-theme head in the violins is the only string statement without ⊓ and the hook, so its accent falls on an up-bow. Applied.

**Problem.** The head is marked `r8 g\downbow( es4.->) … | c4--) b8\upbow( c8)` in every other string statement: 15, 27, 34–37 (c238), 131 (Vc), 167 (Vla). At 143 (Vln I, f) neither mark is printed. By habit the pickup after the rest is an up-bow (rule 3), so the accented E♭5 is an up-bow and the hooked figure at 144 is bowed the other way from every other statement.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38 (the anacrusis habit).
- PDF 37–38 (Ex. II-19, Schubert): "two up-bows are called for in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat".

**Fix.** In recOne.ily, `r8 g'8\downbow\f( es''4.->) d''8( des''8 c''8 | c''4--)\> b'8\upbow( c''8) aes'4. g'8 |`. The 142 strokes are ⊓ ⊓, so the pickup is a third down-bow, lifted in the quaver rest, as at 32→33. The 145 pp G4 still comes down-bow. No notes change. The c239 protection of the 143–150 lead-in concerns its events, and those are unchanged.

### 4. 54–55: Vln II's two halves bow the 55 chord in opposite directions, and the metà half then has no room for the up-bow pickup into the cantabile restatement. Applied.

**Problem.** c312 (v313) joined 54 into one held E♭4. The metà half of Vln II (whole notes 47–54) now reaches 55 on an up-bow. The tutti half enters on the same chord after rests, on a down-bow. Then, after a crotchet rest, both halves play the "molto cantabile" pickup E♭4 at 55.4, which begins a 4.8 s bow with the c258 swells. In v239 the metà half reached 55 down-bow.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-20: change bow on the long note so that the next attack comes the right way.
- PDF 38: the anacrusis is an up-bow.

**Fix.** In tTwo.ily, `es'2~\downbow es'2\upbow |` at 54. This is an inaudible bow change on the tie at pp, as at 12 and 166. Both halves then play 55 down-bow and the pickup up-bow, matching Vln I at 55.4. No notes change.
