## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Repeated notes inside one slur have no louré dash (the first-theme head in every statement, and two counterlines). Applied.

**Problem.** The head's second beat-group is written `d''8( des''8 c''8 | c''4)`. The C5 at the downbeat of 16 is played again, but it sits under the same slur as the C before it, with no tie and no mark. A string player reads the slur as one bow. Nothing tells the player whether the second C is to be re-articulated within the bow or simply held, and the notation looks like a missing tie.

The same figure appears in:
- 28, 132, 144 and 168;
- the transition heads at 34 (Bsn 1, C4), 35 (Vla, F4) and 36 (Vln I, B♭4);
- 78 (B.cl), 79 (Hn 1·2, Vc: `e8 e2)`), 104 (Ob 2, Cl 1·2), 108 (B.cl) and 128 (Cl 1);
- the counterlines `f'8( es'8 des'8 | des'2)` at 59 (Cl 1, Hn 2) and `c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2)` at 161 (Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 3·4).

full.midi plays every one of these notes again, and the theme record (themes-mvt1.ly `mvtOneThemeP`) has the C as a separate note. So the intention is a re-struck note.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 42: louré is "a legato stroke, but a slight separation of the notes is effected while the bow is being drawn. It is a very expressive bowing … indicated by dashes under or over each of the noteheads, with slurs to designate the bow changes".
- Ex. II-30 (Handel, PDF 43, viewed) puts a dash on each repeated note under each slur.
- PDF 38: "The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow". Without the dash, the slur over two equal pitches says only "one bow", and the note's re-articulation is left to guesswork.

**Fix.** Add a tenuto dash to the repeated note inside the slur, for example `c''8 | c''4--)` and `e8 e2--)`.
- There are 38 places, in all doubling parts, so the doubled parts keep identical articulation (the cycle 88 rule).
- The part-combined pairs (Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2) stay "a2".
- For the winds, the dash under a slur means a soft legato tongue.
- Files: tOne.ily, trans.ily, tTwo.ily, dev.ily, recOne.ily, recTwo.ily, coda.ily.
- No slur, accent or note changes. c118's hooked up-bow at 16/28/132/168 still works: the louré C ends the up-bow, and B–C continues on it.

### 2. 158–162: after the fff climax, the ff arrival at 162 falls on an up-bow, and the cellos doubling the climax line bow against the violins. Applied.

**Problem.** The violins are marked ⊓ at 158 and 160 (protected). From there the bows run as follows:
- 160: C6 down, A♭6 up, G6 down;
- 161: F6 up, `d'''4( b''8 c''')` down;
- 162: the ff A5 under "largamente" (bass A, ff diminuendo) is an up-bow, starting at the tip;
- 163: diminuendo down;
- 164–165: diminuendo up;
- 166: the p crescendo into the f at 167 is down.

That is three bows against Adler's habits. The cellos have the same line an octave down at 158–161 (`g4\ff e'2.~ … c'4\fff aes'2~`) with no marks. Counting from 155, they reach 158 and 160 on up-bows, the opposite of the violins they double. The double basses arrive on 162 up-bow.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: one plays "louder and heavier toward the frog"; a crescendo goes with the up-bow and a diminuendo with the down-bow, and the composer marks bowing "where he wishes to counteract the normal habit".
- PDF 38 Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*) and PDF 42–43 Ex. II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii): consecutive down-bows, with the bow lifted between them, for vigorous strokes.

**Fix.**
- recTwo.ily:
  - Vln I `f'''2\downbow d'''4( b''8 c'''8)` at 161; Vln II the same an octave lower.
  - Vc `g4\ff\downbow` at 158, `c'4\fff\downbow` at 160, `f'2\downbow` at 161.
  - Cb `d,2\downbow` at 161.
- The ⊓ at 161 follows the short G6 quaver, so it is the lifted double down-bow Adler describes.
- Result: 162 down (ff at the frog, diminuendo down-bow), 163 up, 164–165 down (diminuendo), 166 up (crescendo into 167), and the cellos bow with the violins.
- The protected separate bows and the ⊓ at 158/160 are unchanged. Only marks are added.

**Evidence.**
