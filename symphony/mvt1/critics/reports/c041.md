## Findings

### 1. Bars 39–40 use the same rising-sixth ladder as the development climb at 111–112 (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 39 (`r8 d'' bes''4-> r8 f'' d'''4->`) and bar 111 (`r8 d'' b''4-> r8 f'' d'''4->`) are the same line except for B♭ against B♮. Bar 40 (`r8 aes'' f'''4->`) and bar 112 (`r8 f'' des'''4-> r8 bes'' f'''4->`) both climb in sixths. Both ladders also aim at g''' (41 and 113). The listener therefore hears the approach to the movement's first big climax (114) as a replay of the transition. The ladder accounts for 8 of Vln I's 39 rising sixths, and only one of its two places is needed.

**Evidence.** Tchaikovsky 6/i reaches his transition high point (70–73) and his development peaks (192–195, 212–214) with different figures, so no climax is announced by a gesture already used in the exposition. Agawu's point applies too: a highpoint is a structural event, so its approach should belong to it alone.

**What T1 is missing.** Bar 20's figure (`des''8 es'' f'' aes'' des'''2`, scale and arpeggio into the held Neapolitan) is the part of T1 that is not the motto. In the melody it occurs at only 20, 70 (Vln II) and 132, against the head cell in 28 bars. Using it at 39 develops T1's second idea, and the sixth ladder then belongs to the development climb alone.

**Answer to cycle 21.** Cycle 21 kept 33–46 because it is "motivic, like the models". The new bars are still motivic: they come from bar 20, not the head. The 35–38 imitation and cycle 19's 16th turn on beat 4 of 40 are kept.

**Fix (applied).** In trans.ily, bars 39–40:
- transVlnOne: `d''8( es''8 f''8 bes''8) d'''2-> | f''8( g''8 aes''8 c'''8) \ottava #1 bes''16( c''' d''' es''' f''' es''' f''' fis''') |`
- transFluteOne: `\partCombineSoloI d''8\mf\<(^\markup \bold "1." es''8 f''8 bes''8) d'''2-> | f''8( g''8 aes''8 c'''8) bes''16( ... fis''') |`
- transOboeOne: `d''8( es''8 f''8 bes''8) d'''2-> | f'8( g'8 aes'8 c''8) bes'16( c'' d'' es'' f'' es'' f'' fis'') |` (bar 40 an octave lower, as before).

**Harmony and register.** The harmony is B♭7 throughout. The chord tones d'', f'', b♭'', d''' and a♭'' fall on the beats, and e♭'', g'' and c''' are passing notes. The top notes stay where they were (d''' at 39, f''' in the run), so the arrival on g''' at 41 is unchanged.

**Result.** Vln I rising sixths drop from 39 to 36 (Fl 1 from 26 to 23, Ob 1 from 29 to 26). clash.py and vl2.py give output identical to v60.

### 2. Is T1 as memorable as T2? Its head is; the whole sentence is heard only once (no score change; supports the pending repeat)
**The head.** The listener will hum T1's head, because it is the motto in diminution and occurs in 28 bars.

**The sentence.** The 8-bar sentence 15–22 sounds complete only once. The recapitulation gives the head in the tenor (123–126), the hemiola (127–130), and a varied continuation (131–134). T2, by contrast, is heard complete four times (47–55, 56–68, 139–147, 148–162). Margulis (cited in c031) and the earworm study both tie retention to repetition and exposure. Dvořák 9/i, whose first-movement tunes are among the best known in the repertoire, repeats its exposition.

**Recommendation.** The exposition repeat 15–76 is still pending with Dan (cycle 39). On melodic grounds it is the most effective single step toward making T1 as memorable as T2, and it changes no notes. No note change to the sentence is justified: its climax placement (0.70, held Neapolitan) matches the models.

### 3. The protected tunes: verdict
**Second theme 47–55.** It has three rising sixths in five bars, each answered by a stepwise fall, then the octave at 52. That is a distinctive rate of rise and fall between turning points, the feature the earworm study associates with tunes that stick. Keep it.

**Rising sixth elsewhere.** Apart from finding 1, I found no other place where the sixth is used as a mechanical filler outside a protected passage. The remaining uses are thematic.
