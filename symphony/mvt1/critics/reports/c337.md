## Findings

### 1. Bars 156–157: the counter that carries the climax is first heard in the clarinet's throat register, inside the horns and violas (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 158–161 the climax has three elements:
- the S tune in three octaves;
- a counter in Ob 1 + Cl 1 (G5–C6) and Hn 3·4 (G4–C5);
- the violas' repeated chords over held brass.

The counter is first heard two bars earlier, at 156–157, in Cl 1 alone, mp<: concert F4 | F4–G4–B♭4 | A♭4–F4, written G4–A4–C5–B♭4. Three problems follow.

1. **It is in a weak register.** Most of these notes are throat tones, which Adler calls "rather pale" (adler1 PDF 210, Ex. VII-56) and Sebesky calls "somewhat pale, cold" (PDF 60, Ex. 66).
2. **It is buried in the same register.** Hn 1 holds F4 through 156–157, so the counter starts inside a sounding unison. Hn 2 moves D4–D♭4–C4, Hn 3·4 hold B♭3/A♭3 and F3, and the violas' arpeggios pass through the counter's own pitches: B♭3–D4–F4–B♭4 at 156 and A♭3–C4–F4–A♭4 at 157. The violins are an octave above and below, on D6/D5.
3. **It breaks at the barline.** Cl 1 then leaps a ninth, F4 to G5, into its 158 statement with Ob 1. The listener hears the counter for the first time at 158, fully formed, with no exposure.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Adler PDF 184 (Schoenberg Op. 16): each fragment must go to "an instrument that will be clearly heard in the complex texture", and a weak register gets help ("the flutes can stand the help of the clarinets in their lower register").
- PDF 177 and 181: Wagner's and Bloch's elements are "exposed separately before this combination occurs", so the listener "is well aware of them".
- PDF 176: an element keeps its colour long enough to carry out its assignment.

At 156–157 the counter is technically exposed, but it is not audible. Its colour and octave also change at the moment it should take hold.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-337/work/sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoClOne).** Move Cl 1 up an octave for the two bars:

```
% was:  f'2\mp\< r8 f'8( g'8 bes'8 | aes'2) f'2 |
        f''2\mp\< r8 f''8( g''8 bes''8 | aes''2) f''2 |
```

The new notes are written G5–A5–C6–B♭5, in the clarino register.
- **It is clear of other lines.** The counter now lies in the empty space between Vln II (D5–C5–B♭4) and Vln I (D6–C6–B♭5). There it moves while the tune holds (156 beats 3–4) and holds while the tune moves (157), so the two rhythms stay distinct (PDF 175).
- **It joins its climax statement.** F5 steps to G5 at 158, which is Cl 1's own next note with Ob 1. The climax colour (Ob 1 + Cl 1 on the upper octave) now grows out of the same instrument at the same octave.
- **It changes no harmony.** The pitch classes are unchanged, so the harmony at 156–157 (c182, c227) is untouched, and c170's B♭ at 156.4.5 is kept. The harp's chord tones F5 and A♭5 on beats 1–2 coincide with the counter's half notes only at the attack and then decay, as the harp punctuation in Adler's *Scheherazade* example does (PDF 179).
- **It leaves the climax arrival as it was.** No player is added, so c283's "climax arrival 158 (14 players return)" is unchanged.

**Voice leading.** Two existing vl.txt lines change. Neither is new:
- 157.1: Cl 1 B♭→A♭ with Hn 3 B♭3→A♭3 is now two octaves apart instead of one.
- 157.4→158.1: Cl 1 F→G against the bass F→G. This compound octave was already in v337 as F4→G5 (with the leap) and is now F5→G5. The bass's F→G is doubled by Bsn 1, Bsn 2 and Cb, and the move into the cadential 6/4 is the same as before.

No other pair changes.
