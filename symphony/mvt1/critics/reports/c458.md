## Findings

### 1. Double basses 169: no crescendo into the ff stop chord at 170 (applied)

**Bars.** 167–170, double basses.

**What is wrong.** At 169 every sustaining part has a hairpin from f to the accented ff at 170: Fl, Ob, Cl, B.cl, Bsn, Cbsn, Hn 3·4, Tpt 3, Tbn, B.tbn, tuba, timpani, Vln I·II (tremolo) and cellos. The double basses, which play 24 repeated C2 quavers through 167–169, have only the f at 167 and the ff at 170. Their source (`codaCb`, coda.ily) has no `\<`. In the printed score the Cb staff at 169 is the only bass staff with no hairpin under it, and the Cb part has none either. A player reads three bars of one note at one level, then an ff accent with no preparation. The same approach at 23–25 and 135–137 has the Cb crescendo with everyone else, so this looks like an omission, not a choice.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 150 and 152: one note repeated at the same level is heard as mechanical. A good player varies the attacks by instinct, but that variation has no direction unless the shape is written. The 24 quavers at 167–169 are the longest run in the movement of one pitch at one marked level (a scan of full.midi for runs of 12 or more same-pitch, same-velocity notes finds only this one, Cb 131–132 (12, pitch changes at 132.3) and Vc 129 (12, one bar, then f at 130)).
- Casanelles PDF 149–150: blended dynamic layers still sound lifeless "in passages with very expressive crescendos or diminuendos". The shape has to come from the player, and a player only gives it when it is asked for (PDF 152: the composer gives start, end and words).
- c358's reading table says the Cb quavers at 167–169 follow principle 8 because "pitch or level changes each bar". That is wrong for 167–169: the pitch is C2 throughout and the level is f throughout.
- Inside this score, the two other approaches to a tutti stop chord (23–25 into 26, 135–137 into 138) give the double basses the same crescendo as the rest of the tutti. 169 is the only one where they lack it.

**Fix (applied in the scratch copy).** In `sections/coda.ily`, `codaCb`, bar 169:

```
c,8\f \repeat unfold 7 { c,8 } | \repeat unfold 8 { c,8 } | c,8\< \repeat unfold 7 { c,8 } | c,2.->\ff r4\fermata |
```
(was `\repeat unfold 8 { c,8 }` at 169), with a comment line citing PDF 150 and 152. The hairpin now prints under the Cb staff at 169, as under the cello staff.

**Protections.** "Coda notes, levels and timing" (c328) and the 27–32/167–170 "tutti layout alike on purpose" (c090) protect notes, levels and layout. This change keeps every note, the f at 167 and the ff at 170. It only gives the basses the crescendo the rest of the tutti already has. It does not undo any earlier fix.

### 2. No further change

I checked the reading against the places a third pass is most likely to find something, and also asked where the expression falls short of a master's. None of the following meets the bar for a change.

- **Timpani pulse 179–180, pp both bars.** Principle 8 would suggest a diminuendo so the second pulse is softer. Two statements are not a "machine gun" run, the pulse is a cross-movement item, and the step down already comes with the ppp roll at 181 and the violins' morendo. Coda levels are protected (c328). No change.
- **Vln I last motto, no swell on the E♭5 at 178.** Principle 14 would allow a small swell on the arrival note. c258 and c358 kept it flat because it is in canon with the pp trombone motto, and the trombones' E♭4 at 176 also has no swell. That reason still holds. No change.
- **S theme in B minor ff at 97–102 without an expressive word.** A Romantic composer might write "appassionato" or "con forza" here. The reading does not support it, and ff, "sul G" in Vln II, the long slurs and the swells already say what the word would say. No change.
- **Dynamic range.** The score uses eight marked levels (ppp to fff), more than the 3–5 that PDF 148–149 calls clearly distinct. In live performance the 8 marks are used one or two steps apart in adjacent parts and fff only at 122 and 160 (protected, c098). No change.

The movement's expression is already at a high level: the tempo plan (poco rit. 45–46, poco animando 62–63 and 156–157, rit. 66–68, allargando/sostenuto 121–126, largamente 160, rit. 165–166 and 177–178), the swells on every lyrical S statement against flat pads, the brass entering soft and crescendoing after the strings, and the shaded tam-tam and cymbal decays are what the reading describes as the player's part of expression, written into the score.
