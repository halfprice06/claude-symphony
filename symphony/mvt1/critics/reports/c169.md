## Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

Mostly yes. Set beside Brahms 3/i, the movement does the same large things. A cyclic motto frames the work, as Brahms's F–A♭–F does. The four settings of the second theme are each scored differently. The retransition (122–131) follows Tchaikovsky's 4/i formula as Adler describes it (PDF 38): the music breaks off, the strings dwindle to the cellos, and a tutti reprise enters with a new element on top. The final chord avoids the "dangerous" high-flute pianissimo that Adler notes in Brahms's own ending (PDF 162). Brass use is in proportion to Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i.

The one place where the score sounds less assured than Brahms is its own apotheosis, 158–161. Brahms's coda tutti (Ex. XV-14) has three properties that Adler names: the tune is uncovered, every chord is complete in every choir, and violas and cellos keep a repeated-note pulse under the held wind and brass chords. At 158–161 the tune is uncovered, but the dominant seventh at 159 has its fifth in only one clarinet, while its leading tone is doubled at pitch in the trumpet's weakest register. The tune's lower octave is also stated four times at pitch (Hn 1, Hn 2, Vla, Vc), and nothing in the texture moves faster than the tune. Findings 1 and 2 address this. Neither changes the tune, the counterline, the harmony, the dynamics or the percussion.

## Findings

### Finding 1 (APPLIED). Bars 159 and 161: Tpt 3 still doubles Tbn 1 at pitch on B3; the V7 at 159 has its fifth only in Cl 2

**Problem.** Cycle 154 found that Tpt 3 doubled Tbn 1 note for note at 158–162, but fixed only bar 160. At 159 (V7, G–B–D–F under the tune's F–G), Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 still both sit on B3, the trumpet's lowest and dullest fifth. The leading tone then sounds in five parts (Tpt 3, Tbn 1, Ob 2, Hn 3, Cl 1), while the chord's fifth, D, is played only by Cl 2's D5: no brass and no strings have it. At 161 the same B3 unison returns.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 164 on Brahms 3/i mm. 183–87: "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave." PDF 159 asks for "pitches … in their best registral positions." PDF 165: "doublings at pitch usually result in the loss of the individual color characteristics of both instruments. Further, sustained doublings tire the ear." PDF 161: Weber leaves instruments that sit well undoubled at pitch. This is the same reasoning as c154 F1, applied to the two bars that cycle left.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-169/work/sections/recTwo.ily).**
```
recTwoTbnOne:   R1*11 | c'1\ff | d'1\< \breathe | c'1\fff | b1 | ...   % 159 was b1
recTwoTptThree: R1*11 | c'1\ff | b1\< \breathe | es'1\fff | d'1 | ...  % 161 was b1
```
In the brass, 159 now has D4 (Tbn 1) and a single B3 (Tpt 3). Tpt 3 falls E♭4–D4–C4 through 160–162, the motto's own descent. I checked the voice leading. Tbn 1 D4→C4 and Tpt 3 B3→E♭4 cross, as inner parts of different instruments may. Tpt 3 D4→E♭4 at 159 would have made parallel twelfths with the bass G→A♭, which is why the D goes to Tbn 1. The voice-leading script shows no new fifths, only octave doublings. Protection: 158–166 is protected for the tune's register, the cymbal, the harmony of the peak and the horn question (c154's summary). This change touches none of them and completes c154's own fix.

### Finding 2 (APPLIED, reopens a protected layout). Bars 158–161: no inner pulse under the climax; the tune's lower octave stated four times at pitch

**Problem.** At 158–161 every part except the tune holds whole or half notes, and the only motion is the timpani roll. The two earlier settings of this theme have a broken-chord accompaniment (cello and harp arpeggios at 47–63), and so do the bars on either side (156–157 and 162–166, cello arpeggios). At the peak that layer disappears. The violas have joined the tune's lower octave, which Hn 1, Hn 2 and the cellos already play at E4.

**Evidence.** Brahms 3/i mm. 183–87 (Adler Ex. XV-14, PDF 163) keeps violas and cellos on repeated notes under the violins' tune while winds and brass hold the chords, and Adler says this scoring "neither overwhelms nor overshadows the tune." Smetana (PDF 154) puts sustained brass under a lively figure. Holst (PDF 181): "Figuration gives a wonderful buoyancy to this passage." PDF 179: "in order to insure clarity, the rhythms of the elements must be distinct." PDF 171 (Mahler 4/iv): a recurring passage is coloured differently each time, but "the recognizable texture … prevail[s]." PDF 165: sustained at-pitch doublings tire the ear. PDF 44: two horns in unison equal one trombone, so Hn a2 + Vc carry the lower octave without the violas.

**Fix (applied, `recTwoSync`, bars 158–161 of the viola).**
```
<c' g'>8[\ff <c' g'> <c' g'> <c' g'>] <c' g'>[ <c' g'> <c' g'> <c' g'>] |
<b f'>8[\< <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] <b f'>[ <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] |
<c' es'>8[\fff <c' es'> <c' es'> <c' es'>] <c' es'>[ <c' es'> <c' es'> <c' es'>] |
<d' f'>8[ <d' f'> <d' f'> <d' f'>] <b f'>[ <b f'> <b f'> <b f'>] |
```
These are non-divisi double stops on the G and D strings. 160's C–E♭ adds a third source of the fifth that c154 restored. Bar 162 onwards is unchanged.

**Protection answered.** c167 "lower-octave melody doubling 158–161": kept, by Hn 1·2 and Vc. c149 "the four settings of the second theme": the fourth setting stays distinct and now regains a figurative layer, as PDF 171 asks. c126 "Cb sustain under Vc arpeggios 158–166": the double basses are unchanged. "Separate string bows 158–160" concerns the violins, which are unchanged. If the composer prefers to keep the violas on the tune, the alternative is to apply Finding 1 alone.
