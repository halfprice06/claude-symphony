## Findings

### 1. Comparison with Beethoven 2/i introduction: no change (applied: nothing to apply)

These are the points where the two introductions can be compared from the published descriptions.

- **Length and weight.** Beethoven's introduction is 33 bars. At about 2'48" it is roughly a fifth of a 12-13-minute movement played with the exposition repeat. Ours is 14 bars of Lento at crotchet 50, about 67 s of 430 s (16%). That is close in proportion. The length is protected (c429: "intro length and bar 12"), and nothing in the comparison answers that reason.
- **Tonal range.** Beethoven travels: D, B♭ (♭VI at bar 11), G minor, C, F, then D minor. Ours stays in C minor and gets its darkening from chords: N6 at 7, the A♭ side at 10-11, and the German sixth at 12 into V at 13. In 14 bars this does the same job, putting the flat side ahead of the sonata. Beethoven's ♭VI ff outburst has a counterpart in our ♭VI climaxes on A♭ at 82, 122 and 160, which are reserved for the body of the movement. That plan is protected (A♭ climax ladder; c430, c484) and is better than spending ♭VI in the introduction.
- **Dynamic contrast.** Beethoven opens with ff unison strokes answered by soft winds (oboes and bassoons). Ours opens ppp/pp with the bare motto and reaches only f at 12. This is the late-Romantic choice (Tchaikovsky 6, Brahms 1 open soft or with a held pedal), it is protected (c409: "soft introduction"), and the finale's opening quotes bar 12. The contrast Beethoven gets from stroke against answer, ours gets at 12→13 (f tutti chord, then a solo horn p). That is the procedure of Rimsky's Ex. 8 (PDF 182-183): a forte tutti phrase hands over at once to a pp solo.
- **Link to the Allegro.** Beethoven's introduction is thematically tied to the Allegro theme, which the low strings then play. Ours ties in more closely. The motto's G-E♭ rise and D-D♭-C fall are the head of the hammered tutti at 27 (r8 g8 es'4. d'8 des'8 c'8). The horn call 13-14 (E♭3-C4-B3) prepares the S head (protected, c271: "S head prepared by 13-14"). A conductor would see the introduction as the source of the movement's material, which is the mark of a master's slow introduction.
- **The seam.** Beethoven reaches the Allegro after a dominant preparation that thins to wind trills. Ours ends on V7 (G pedal in Bsn 2/Vc/Cb, timpani roll p>pp, Hn 1's B3 unresolved) and the P pickup follows. The dominant is shorter, but the unresolved leading tone does the same work, and the seam is protected (c293: "intro ends on V7 at 14").

Against the reading, all 18 principles hold in bars 1-14. Rimsky's rule for solo winds (PDF 66-67, row 11) is the one most visible in the introduction: the motto, the C.a. answer and the horn call are each a single undoubled colour or a single unison mixture. The only wind doubling of the violin line comes at the crescendo into the f at 12. That matches Rimsky's advice to save doubling for loud passages.

### 2. Candidates looked at and rejected

- **Octave doubling under Vln I's rising sixths at 9-13 (R-K PDF 59, Ex. 22-23).** Rimsky asks for octave doubling for melodies in the very high register. A♭5 is in the normal E-string compass, far below Ex. 24's register (PDF 200). The line is doubled in unison by Fl 1 from 11.3, and Vln II is needed for the inner voice. These notes are protected (c429: "9-13 Vln I sixths") and lie inside the quoted bars 1-14 (MIDI = v321). Rejected: there is no gain, and it would cost a cross-movement change.
- **A Beethoven-style ff stroke or ♭VI outburst in the introduction.** This would undo the soft introduction (c409) and spend the A♭ colour the climax ladder keeps for 82/122/160. Bar 12 is the finale's first chord (IV 1). Rejected.
- **A longer dominant preparation before 15.** This would add bars to sections that the other movements cite by number (intro 14), against the protected proportions. Rejected.
