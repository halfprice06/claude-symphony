## Findings

The movement's key areas are entered, confirmed and left the way this unit teaches at every boundary, several times in textbook form. I found no change that would bring the score closer to a master's work under this reading, so I made no edits. The four items below are the places where the reading raises a question that a composer or conductor could ask; for each I give the answer and the reason to keep the passage. Items 1 and 2 are new observations; items 3 and 4 are checks against the reading of passages that were changed recently or that look irregular on the page.

### 1. The end of the exposition and the start of the development, 73–80: keep (rank 1)

Unit 14 says that after a long stay in a temporary key the minor seventh should be added to its tonic, because that tone "helps to neutralize the temporary key" (PDF 219–220 §10). A♭ is the key from 41 to 76 (about 63 s). At 75 Vln II and Fl 1 add G♭ to the A♭ chord. The flute lets it fall to F as a passing note, but Vln II carries it down by step, G♭4 (75) – F♭4 (76) – E♭4 (77), so the seventh first turns A♭ major into A♭ minor (iv6/4 with F♭ at 76) and then lands on the fifth of the A♭-minor chord that opens the development. The development then uses the 5–6 technique (PDF 217 §8): Vln II E♭4→E4 over the held A♭ (77–78), with the bass skipping to E at 78.3, as in Ex. 14-7, where the 5–6 is followed by a skip to the root of the new chord. Then C minor follows at 80 (PDF 219 §10: after a short stay the tonic can simply return).

The bass A♭ (77) – E (78–79) – C (80) is a chain of falling major thirds: the symphony's own key cycle C – A♭ – E – C, compressed into four bars. I checked whether 80 should be prepared by a dominant, as the unit says a longer modulation requires (PDF 216 §6). It should not: 80–81 is a return to the tonic after two short tonicizations, which PDF 219 §10 says needs no preparation, and the V7♭9 at 83 confirms C afterwards (row 8). Keep 73–80 as written.

### 2. The recapitulation's first group turns to the subdominant, 138–143: keep (rank 2)

In the exposition the hammered tutti (27–32) moves within C minor, and the bridge (33–46) modulates with the pivot at 34. In the recapitulation the modulation has to go (PDF 225–226: the second group returns in the tonic). The score compensates in the classical way: the tutti after the stop chord at 138 moves iv (139) – D♭, the Neapolitan region (140) – iiø7 (141) – V (142) – i (143). That is a subdominant turn in place of the exposition's move to VI, with Fl 1's A♭6 over the D♭ bass at 140. The cadence at 142–143 has the leading tone in the top (Fl 1 B5–C6), the 7̂–1̂ melody PDF 226 says supports a strong cadence. The V at 145 is then reached from a cadential 6/4 at 144 (PDF 215: a cadential 6/4 can embellish the goal V), and C major starts at 147 with the oboe's G–E♮.

The bridge is short: four bars (143–146, about 8 s) against fourteen in the exposition (33–46, 27 s). I compared this with the corpus. In Tchaikovsky 6/i the MCTL annotation puts the whole first-group recapitulation inside the development, so the recapitulation proper begins with the second theme (form_analysis.csv, onset 1214). Beethoven 9/i keeps a 12-quarter recapitulation transition (onsets 676–688) against a 14-quarter exposition transition (146–160). A four-bar bridge after a first group that has already done the subdominant work is within these models. Keep.

### 3. The coda reaches the minor tonic without a root-position V–i, 174–179: keep (rank 3)

PDF 219 §9 says most new keys are confirmed by an authentic cadence. In the coda the last dominant is at 174 (cadential 6/4 to V over G2). Its resolution is a C-minor 6/4 over G at 175, where the chorale begins on the motto's G. The tonic then arrives through the motto bass E♭ (176) – D (177, vii°6/5 with Cl 1's B4) – C (178) and D♭–C (178–179), without the leading tone resolving in its register.

This is not a defect under the reading. C minor at 167 is not a new key. It is a change of mode after a C-major PAC at 166, which v223 now completes (Hn 3 B3→C4, c222). That PAC is the cadence that confirms the tonic, at the place PDF 219 describes: an IAC (155), two deceptive cadences (160 ♭VI, 162 vi), then the authentic cadence (166), the same order as Ex. 14-1 bars 9–10. The coda's bass G–E♭–D–C / D♭–C is a 5̂–3̂–2̂–1̂ descent with dominant function on 2̂, so the tonic is approached by a dominant-function chord even without a root V. The corpus supports the plagal-type ending: the MCTL cadence list for Tchaikovsky 6/i ends with a plagal cadence in the coda (cadence.csv, onset 1362, "PC"), and its exposition's closing part also ends with one (onset 574). The final cadence bass 177–178 is protected (c183) for the cyclic reason, and the reading gives no ground against it. Keep.

### 4. The arrival of the dominant pedal, 116→117: keep (rank 4)

At 116 the chord is C–D–F♯–A♭, with A♭ moving to A on beat 3: a V4/2 of V over the bass C. PDF 214 says ♯4̂ "must not be doubled", and Ex. 14-3(f) resolves V4/2 of V to V6, with the bass falling by step. Here F♯ sounds in two octaves: F♯3 in Bsn 1, Hn 4, Tbn 1 and Vc, and F♯4 in Cl 2, Hn 2, Tpt 2 and Vln II. The bass seventh C2 leaps to G1. Every F♯3 resolves to G (Bsn 1, Hn 4 and Tbn 1 to G3, Vc to G2). The upper F♯4s do not: Cl 2 goes to B3, Hn 2 to G3, Tpt 2 rests, and Vln II rests and re-enters on B3. The G4 at 117 comes from Hn 3 (D4→G4).

I keep it, for three reasons.
1. This is an ff tutti, and Unit 5 (PDF 84–85) allows octave doublings for emphasis in orchestral texture. The leading-tone motion is clear in the F♯3 voices, three of them in the horn and trombone colours that start the trombone motto at 117.
2. The bass C2 is the end of the protected chromatic wedge F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C (111–116: c133, c341). Its leap to G works as 1̂–5̂ under the arrival, not as an unresolved seventh in a four-voice texture.
3. The only way to resolve the upper F♯ audibly is to give Tpt 2 a G4 on the downbeat of 117, and that would spend a trumpet before the protected trumpet fff entry at 122 (c143, c154).

Cycle 113 judged the same doubling "follows". I agree on these grounds.

### Checked: v223's three changes (c222) against this reading

- 46 (Vln II G4 on beat 4). The V7 of A♭ is complete at the moment the new key is entered, which strengthens the PDF 216 §6 requirement that the new tonic be set off by its own dominant.
- 145 (Vc D3 instead of B2). The recapitulation's half cadence keeps one leading tone, as PDF 214 and Unit 5 require.
- 165–166 (Hn 3 B3→C4). The leading tone now resolves at the PAC that confirms C major (PDF 219 §9).

None of the three changes a section length, a cadence type or a key boundary.
