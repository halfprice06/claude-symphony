## Findings

### 1. Bar 158, beat 1: the ff arrival of K sounds only G and C; the cadential 6/4 has no sixth until beat 2 (applied)

What is wrong. 158 is the arrival of the recapitulation's climax (K, ff, the S head in C major over the cadential 6/4, growing into the fff ♭VI at 160). On the downbeat every part plays G or C:

```
158.1  G1 Cb Cbn Tu | G2 B.tbn Bsn2 Timp | G3 Bsn1 Hn1·2 Tbn2 Vc | C4 Tbn1 Tpt3 Va | G4 Hn3·4 Ob2 Tpt1·2 Vln II Va | G5 Fl1·2 Ob1 Cl1·2 Vln I
```

So for the first beat the chord is G–C, a bare fourth over the bass. E♮, the note that makes the chord a C-major 6/4, arrives only on beat 2 with the tune's leap (E6, E5, E4). The other cadential 6/4s at full or growing strength have the sixth on the downbeat: 13, 62 (C4 in Cl 1, Hn 2, Hn 3 at 62.1), 110, 142, 154. Scan 3 found no other loud downbeat in the movement with only two pitch classes, apart from the motto's unisons at 121.3.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 85: "It is necessary to make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts", with three "to be avoided" chords whose third or sixth is missing above. PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages". PDF 96, remark 1: modern orchestrators "do not allow any void in the intermediate parts", and the void is worst in forte. Here the void is not of register but of harmony: twenty-eight players on two pitch classes at ff. Rimsky's model chord on PDF 85 fills the middle octave with the full triad; 158.1 has only G and C there (C4, G4). The tune still makes its rising sixth G→E above the chord, and that leap is the point of the bar. The movement's own practice is the better model here: every other loud or growing cadential 6/4 (13, 62, 110, 142, 154) has its sixth in a middle part on the downbeat, with the melody moving above it.

Fix. The violas' repeated quaver double stop at 158 becomes C4/E4 (was C4/G4). The violas are the one middle part that sounds the whole bar on every quaver and already hold the C4 from 157 (c232 row 13: the 4th enters as a common tone). G4 stays in Tpt 2, Hn 3·4, Ob 2 and Vln II. The voice leading is by step: C4–B3–C4 and E4–F4–E♭4 through 158–160, so the double stop now runs as two lines under the tune (it was G4–F4–E♭4, the same upper line from G instead of E). On beats 2–4 the violas' E4 is a unison with Hn 1·2 and the cellos on the tune's lower octave; c227's rule (the background stays under the tune's lower octave) still holds: the violas no longer sit above it. Rimsky PDF 51 puts one string section at ff at about half a horn, so this adds the sixth as a colour inside the chord. It does not anticipate the leap's arrival in the tune's octaves.
