## Findings

### 1. Recapitulation 131–134: held trumpet chord sits on the trombone theme (applied)

**Problem.** At 131–134 the first theme is in the tenor: Tbn 1·2 in unison with cellos and bassoons (E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4, F4–E♭4…). Above it Tpt 1·2·3 hold whole notes G4/G4/E♭4, G4/F4/E♭4–D4, A♭4/F4/D4, B4/G4/D4, at f — the same octave as the theme's long notes and a third above them. With the four horns (G4 E♭4 C4 G3) also sustained, seven brass instruments hold a chord in the theme's register. Tpt 3's E♭4 is the theme's own peak pitch.

**Evidence.** Adler Ex. XI-21 (adler2 PDF 39–41, Tchaikovsky 4/i mm. 277–90): when the trombones carry the cantus firmus, horns and trumpets play short rhythmic chords with rests, so the long trombone line has space. Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 263–266 (corpus) do the same: the trombones have the moving line in quarters and eighths, and trumpets and horns repeat one pitch in triplet figures. This score already does this at 27–30, where the trumpets play the timpani rhythm (`c''4 r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8`) under the theme on top. At 131 the theme is in the middle register, where a held chord covers it more, yet the trumpets sustain.

**Fix (pitches unchanged, rhythm only; the timpani already play this rhythm at 131–134):**
```
recOneTptOne   = { g'4\f r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8 | g'4 r4 g'4 b'4 | aes'1 | b'4 r8 b'8 b'4 r8 b'8 | ...
recOneTptTwo   = { g'4\f r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8 | g'4 r4 f'4 r4 | f'1 | g'4 r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8 | ...
recOneTptThree = { es'4\f r8 es'8 es'4 r8 es'8 | es'4 r4 d'4 r4 | d'1 | d'4 r8 d'8 d'4 r8 d'8 | ...
```
133 stays held, as 29 does at the exposition. Horns stay sustained, as at 27. Protection: 131–134 notes and layout are protected (cycles 19, 84, 139). No pitch, dynamic or entry changes. The layout gets closer to 27–30, which cycle 90 says are "alike on purpose".

### 2. ♭VI climax 158–161: one horn on the counterline against full brass (applied)

**Problem.** At 158–161 the counterline (G4–C5 | B4–F4 | E♭4–C5 … A♭4 | A♭4–G4) is played by Hn 3 alone, Ob 2 in unison and Cl 1 an octave higher. It plays at ff–fff against Tpt 1, Hn 1·2, flutes, oboe 1, both violin sections and cellos on the tune, with all other brass sustained. Meanwhile Hn 4 holds G3/A♭3/F3–G3. Tbn 2, the bass trombone, the tuba, bassoons and contrabassoon already double those pitches.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: "If the dynamic is louder than mf, it is advisable to double the horns, meaning that two horns in unison equal the power of one trumpet or one trombone"; repeated at PDF 44 for Bartók's brass. Rimsky-Korsakov ch. I: "two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone". On the tune, the score pairs Hn 1·2 at 158–161 and four horns at 41–42. The counterline is the only brass line at the climax that is not doubled.

**Fix:** Hn 4 doubles Hn 3 at 158–161 and ends the phrase an octave lower, so it falls a third to its E3 at 162:
```
recTwoHnFour = { R1*9 | f1\p\< | f2. r4 | g'2\ff c''2 | b'2\< f'2 | es'4\fff c''4 r8 c''8( bes'8 aes'8 | aes'2) g2 | e1\ff\> | ...
```
On the shared Hn 3·4 staff this prints as a2, with equal dynamics. This does not flip the counterline argument from cycles 7 and 14 (Hn 1·2 stay on the tune). It changes only the balance inside the protected 158–161 scoring, on the authority of Adler PDF 15 and 44 and Rimsky-Korsakov. Written G5 for Hn 4 (concert C5) is within the 4th horn's normal range (Adler App. A range chart, adler2 PDF 304). Hn 4 loses the breath it had after 158. Hn 3 already plays 158–161 with only the eighth rest at 160, so the load is the same as Hn 3's.

### 3. Trumpet 1's A♭5 is used at the codetta (72) before the climax (160) (not applied, protected)

**Problem.** Tpt 1 reaches A♭5 only twice: at 72 (ff codetta cadence, `aes''4-> es''4-> c''2->`) and at 160, the fff ♭VI climax. Its top note is already heard 88 bars before the climax.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 37 (Schubert 9/i): "holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event". PDF 17: continuous bombast "robs the climactic passages of their full power". Cycle 61 applied the same reasoning to the top line (A♭6 kept for its six bars).

**Fix (optional):** bar 72 Tpt 1 `es''4-> es''4-> c''2->`. The F5 at the end of 71 steps down to E♭5, and the flute and violins keep the A♭6. Not applied, because the codetta 69–72 and its trumpet tune are protected (cycles 14, 24, 103).

### 4. Coda 167–169: Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 hold E♭4 under the horns' head (note only)

The horns' lower octave of the head (G3–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4) moves against a sustained E♭4 in Tpt 3 and Tbn 1, which makes seconds in unison register on beats 3–4. By the same reasoning as Finding 1 (Ex. XI-21), Tpt 3 could articulate (`es'4 r8 es'8 es'4 r8 es'8`). It matters less here because the head's upper octave is in Tpt 1·2 at E♭5, and the timpani roll at 167 rather than playing the rhythm. Brass head 167–170 is protected; not applied.
