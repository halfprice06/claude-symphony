## Findings

I checked each row of the table bar by bar and found no place where the score breaks a principle of the reading. I propose no change and applied nothing in the scratch copy. The points below record new evidence (the Dvořák comparison) and one correction to an earlier report, so later form critics do not reopen settled questions.

1. **The plan i – VI – (tonic major) – i is the Dvořák 7/i plan, and its recapitulation is even shorter (whole movement).** A&S PDF 452–454 give tonicized VI as a regular second key in minor and I – VI ‖ IV–V – I as a sonata plan. Dvořák's D-minor first movement (6/8, 314 bars) has the same outline as this one: second group in B♭ major (VI) within an exposition of bars 1–148 (one-flat signature throughout, B♭ theme in Vln I from 83 after mark D at 73); development 149–195 (A-minor signature at 149, back to D minor at 196); recapitulation from mark K (196); second theme in D major at mark L (214, `\key d \major`); D minor again from mark M (237) and 248; coda from mark N (266), poco a poco accelerando, Tempo I at 287, ending pp on a held D at 314. In bars: recapitulation/exposition 70/148 = 0.47, coda 49/314 = 16 %. This movement's figures are 0.61 and 14 % (by time). Dvořák also recapitulates the first theme in full tutti (fff at 196) and reaches the major second theme 18 bars later, leaving out the quiet opening statement; here the tutti P at 131 replaces the soft sentence 15–22 and S arrives 16 bars later at 147. Earlier passes compared Beethoven 9/i and Tchaikovsky 6/i; Dvořák 7/i is the closest corpus model, being in minor, using VI, recapitulating S in the tonic major and ending softly. It supports keeping the section lengths (protected c243, c323, c413). Keep.

2. **Correction to c413 finding 3: B minor 97–102 has a cadential progression, but only a half cadence (bars 97–103).** c413 wrote that none of the remote keys "is confirmed by a cadence with II or IV and V". The MIDI shows iv at 99 (E–G–B over E2), iiø7 at 100 (C♯–E–G–B over C♯2), V with a 4–3 at 101 (F♯ bass) and a cadential 6/4 over F♯ at 102. The progression stops on V; 103 moves to D minor by phrase modulation (PDF 452). Under PDF 443 a key is confirmed by a cadence to its tonic, so B minor is still a passing key in the sense of PDF 447–448. The half cadence matters for form: it gives the ff B-minor S a closed phrase shape, and the dominant left hanging at 102 makes 103 a new beginning. The verdict of c413 stands; only its wording was wrong. Keep 97–103 (protected c393, c413).

3. **Arrivals approach 3̂ with rhythmic resolution (47, 131, 147).** PDF 445 asks for an arrival on 1̂ or 3̂ with a sense of rhythmic resolution. At 47 the S head (Hn 1 and cellos) moves E♭3–C4 into the downbeat and holds C4 (3̂ of A♭) for three and a half beats. At 130→131 the motto stretto ends on D5 in Vln II (130.4) and Cl 1 completes the tail to C4 at 130.2; the tutti C-minor chord sounds on 131.1 and E♭ (3̂) is the top note of the P head on 131.2, in Fl 1 and Vln I at E♭6. At 146→147 V (G–B–D–F, with Ob 1's E♮5 from 145.4) resolves to C major with the S head starting on E (3̂) in Hn 1, violas and cellos (E4/E3, three and a half beats) under Vln II's E5. Keep.

4. **The recapitulation's swerve to iv at 139 is the late IV of PDF 451–452 (bars 138–146).** In the exposition the stop chord at 26 resolves to i (27). In the recapitulation it resolves to iv (139), passes through the Neapolitan (140–141) to V (142) and to a ff perfect cadence in C minor (143), then the dominant is held softly 144–146 and turns major (Ob 1 E♮5 at 145.4) before 147. PDF 451–452 say a tonicized IV late in a piece moves on to V of the home key; Ex. 26-11 shows IV – transition – V – reprise. The recapitulation thus has a cadential close of the first group in the tonic (PDF 443) where the exposition had its modulating transition, which is the normal reason a recapitulation can be shorter. Keep.

5. **The development is a prolonged dominant with both neighbours (77–130).** Ex. 26-17 (PDF 456): V extended by VI above and IV below, then V7 to I. V is touched at 83 after A♭ (82), left for the fifths sequence and the passing keys, regained through iv (111–116) and the augmented sixth at 116, then held 117–130 with A♭ over G at 122 and the seventh F at 117–121 and 130.3. The dominant region is 26 % of the development's bars, close to Tchaikovsky 6/i's retransition share (c413). Keep (117–130 protected).
