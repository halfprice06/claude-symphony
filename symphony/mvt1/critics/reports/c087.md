# Critic 087 — lens 7 (texture): breath points from cycle 86, and a density map

## What I studied
Score: v106 (score.pdf pages 2–6, 10–14; sections/dev.ily, recTwo.ily; score.midi). I made a per-beat sounding count at every cycle-86 breath point (all players, brass, and four register bands), and a per-bar density map of all 182 bars from the MIDI: "parts" = staves sounding, a pair counted as 2 when both players sound, strings 1 per section; "layers" = distinct rhythmic onset patterns in the bar, so doublings and homorhythmic chords count once. I ran the same measurement on the MCTL MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`, 354 bars) and Dvořák 9/i (`.../Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`, 2/4 bars grouped in pairs to make 4/4 units). Bar numbers for those two works follow the MCTL files. I searched the same files for brass breaths taken on the last beat before a downbeat.
Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. I, "Wind instruments" (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): wind players "must take breath", so give them rests, and "the remarks on breathing ... apply with equal force to the brass". Staggered breathing, defined as players in a section breathing at different times "so the others in the section continue to sound long notes" (https://dev-dictionary.onmusic.org/terms/3327-staggered_breathing). Practitioners' advice to stagger breaths and hide them under other events (https://vi-control.net/community/threads/long-brass-sustains-and-how-to-break-them-up.80158/). I did not open Adler or Gould. I did not need them for this question.

## Findings

**1. Bar 116, beat 4: seven of the eleven brass breathe together during the crescendo into G (ff). Applied.**
Problem: cycle 86's rests for Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and tuba fall on the same beat as the older horn breaths (Hn 1, 2, 3; cycle 66) and the clarinets. Sounding players drop from 27 on beat 3 to 17 on beat 4. Brass drop from 11 to 4, and no brass instrument is left below F♯3. Most upper parts at 117 start with an eighth rest, so the crescendo empties out just before the arrival.
Evidence: Tchaikovsky 6/i 287–296. The tenor and bass trombones rest every other bar, and the tuba rests 289–294 while the bass trombone keeps the line. Horns and trumpets hold tied whole notes through every trombone rest, so the low brass never all breathe at once. Two of the four low-brass players did not need this breath at all: the tuba had played since 111 at p, and Tbn 2 could breathe one beat earlier.
Fix (dev.ily):
- Tuba 111–116: `f,1\p\cresc | e,1 | es,1 | d,2. r4 | des,1 | c,1 |`. The breath moves to 114, where B.tbn holds the same D2. The tuba then plays 115–119 beat 3, about 9 s.
- Tbn 2 113–116: `c1\mp\cresc | c2 b,2 | aes,2. r4 | aes,2 a,2 |`. It then plays 116–121 beat 3, about 11 s.
- Tbn 1 and B.tbn keep their breath at 116.
Result: beat 4 of 116 now has 19 players and 6 brass, with A2 and C2 still in the brass. Beat 4 of 115 loses 2 brass (Tbn 2, and Hn 4, which already rested there), and Tbn 1 holds F3.

**2. Bar 157, beat 4: all four horns and the violas stop together in the crescendo into K. Applied.**
Problem: the cycle-86 rests for Hn 1, 3 and 4 fall on the same beat as Hn 2's older rest and the violas' rest. The middle register C3–B3 is empty. 11 players drop to 6, and no brass sound before the ff at 158.
Evidence: Hn 3 entered only at 156 and has an eighth-rest breath inside 160, so it did not need this rest. Tchaikovsky 6/i 134: trumpets, trombones and tuba stop on the last half-beat while the horns tie G4/G5 across the barline into 135. Bar 68 of this score already does the same: Hn 2 holds C4 while Hn 3·4 and the bassoons breathe, and that bar has no hole.
Fix (recTwo.ily, Hn 3): `R1*9 | bes1\mf\< | aes1 | g'2\ff c''2 |`. Hn 1, 2 and 4 keep their breaths. Hn 4 needs its breath, because it plays without a rest from 158 to 164. This puts back the v105 note for one player only. It adds nothing new to 147–157, so it does not raise level or density there (cycle 83 rule).

**3. Bar 121, beat 4: under the allargando, Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn stop together for a full quarter, and so does the contrabassoon. Applied in shortened form.**
Problem: the motto's C keeps sounding in Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1. The trombone colour and the C3 octave drop out on the slowest beat before fff. This hole is milder than the ones at 116 and 157.
Evidence: Tchaikovsky 6/i 134. The tuba breathes in an eighth rest (D2 for 1½ beats, then an eighth rest) at the end of the bar.
Fix (dev.ily): Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 `c'2.. r8`, B.tbn `c2.. r8`. Pitches and ties in 117–124 are unchanged (cycle 86 rule). At the allargando tempo the eighth rest lasts about 0.28 s, which is enough for a catch breath.

**4. Bar 68 (Hn 3·4) and bars 100, 102, 113, 119: no hole. No change.**
68: Hn 2 C4, Cl C4, the full string chord and the harp all hold, so only the bassoons' A♭2 and the Hn 3·4 doubling go. 100: BCl and Bsn 2 breathe together, but Vc and B.tbn hold the same C♯3. 102: Hn 3·4 and Cbsn stop, while Cb, Bsn 2, BCl and B.tbn hold F♯. 113: Tuba and Cbsn hold E♭2 under Bsn 2's rest. 119: Cb, Bsn 2, BCl and the timpani hold G under the tuba's and Hn 4's rests.

**5. Density map: the overall shape is within the models' range. No change.**
Per 4/4 unit:

| | v106 | Tchaikovsky 6/i | Dvořák 9/i |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean / 95th-percentile peak | 0.51 | 0.56 | 0.56 |
| coefficient of variation | 0.58 | 0.45 | 0.48 |
| rhythmic layers, mean | 3.7 | 5.4 | 3.6 |
| units below 30 % of peak | 30 % | 15 % | 16 % |
| units at 80 % of peak or more | 23 % | 20 % | 27 % |

By tenths of the movement, v106 runs 0.26 0.62 0.41 0.57 0.29 0.44 0.76 0.73 0.51 0.47. Like Tchaikovsky, it peaks in the seventh and eighth tenths (development climax and recapitulation). The one clear difference is the larger thin share. The thin bars are 1–10, 14–18, 34–36, 44–54, 73–79, 84–90, 107–109, 127–129, 144 and 171–174. Outside the introduction and coda, they are almost all in protected passages: 47–55 plain exposition, 77–83 (decided: no countermelody), fugato 85–96, 103–110, 127–130 and the coda. I therefore recommend nothing for movement I. For movements II and III: keep more music at middle density (Tchaikovsky 65 %, Dvořák 57 %, v106 47 %).

**6. Flat stretches: the longest is 147–155 (9 bars at about 8 parts). Within the models. No change.**
The models' longest low, flat runs are Tchaikovsky 39–47 and 230–238 (9 units each, at 0.36–0.38 of peak) and Dvořák 201–215 (8 units). The full-tutti runs in v106 (26–32, 7 bars; 117–121, 5 bars) are shorter than Tchaikovsky 134–145 (12) and Dvořák 425–451 (14). The 147–155 run also changes inside the bar: there are 2–4 layers, and the cor anglais answers the theme. Cycle 83's rule stands.

**7. Sudden drops of half the peak or more between bars: 6 in v106 (33, 43, 72, 83, 143, 170), 0 in Tchaikovsky, 5 in Dvořák. No change.**
Per 100 units, that is 3.3 for v106 against 2.2 for Dvořák. Every v106 drop falls at a section joint: the end of the first group, the medial caesura, the end of the codetta, the subito p at 84 (protected), the hand-over to the second group, and the start of the coda. 143 is already staggered, because the strings carry the theme through the winds' cut-off. None of the drops lacks a musical reason. Tchaikovsky reduces the texture over several bars instead of cutting it. Movements II and III could use that approach once or twice for contrast.
