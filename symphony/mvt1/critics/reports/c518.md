I also wrote a new scanner (/tmp/critic-518/scripts/susp.py). It lists every note in full.midi that is held across a beat where the bass changes, is a 2nd, 4th, 7th or 9th above the new bass, and then falls by step to a consonance. For each one it compares the MIDI velocity of the preparation, which is the only attack a tied suspension has, with the velocity of the resolution. A second script (at.py) prints the pitches sounding on each beat.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 108: the violas crescendo into the resolution of a 9-8 (applied)

What is wrong. In the development's soft S statement (107–110, violas alone on the tune, p espr.), bar 108 is written `e'4\! c''2--\< b'4\!)`. The C5 enters on 108.2 over E minor (a 6th, consonant). On 108.3 the double basses and cellos move to B (B1/B2), and the harmony becomes V of E minor (B–A3–D♯4). The held C5 is now a 9 over B, a suspension by extension (PDF 347), and it resolves to B4 on 108.4 (9-8, PDF 351 §10). The hairpin grows through the 9 and ends on the B, so the resolution is the loudest note of the bar. My scan shows it as the only soft-dynamic case in v518 where the resolution is louder than its preparation (MIDI velocity 69 → 72). Every other flagged case is a planned forte crescendo or a hammer stroke (see Finding 3).

Why it happened. The dynamics are copied from the exposition's S head (48: `aes4\! f'2--\< es'4\!`) and the recapitulation (148: `c4\! a2--\< g4\!`). There the tenuto note is a consonant 3rd over a bass that does not move (F5 over D♭ at 48; A over F at 148). In the development the bass moves under the held note, and the same hairpin now lands on a resolution.

Evidence. "It follows naturally ... that its resolution should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity" (A&S PDF 348 §3). "Dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer" (PDF 348 §4). The 9-8 over the bass, with the bass already sounding the tone of resolution, is the reading's "good" case (Ex. 21-12d, PDF 352), so the voice leading is right and only the dynamic shape works against it. Beethoven's Op. 126/3 (Ex. 21-37, PDF 365) shapes the same way: the swell goes up and comes back down (< >), and it reaches p on the suspended chord, not after it.

Fix (applied in the scratch copy, sections/dev.ily line 403, bar 108 of devVla):
```
was:  e'4\! c''2--\< b'4\!)
now:  e'4\! << c''2-- { s4\< s4\> } >> b'4\!)
```
The swell rises through 108.2 to the 9 on 108.3 and falls back to the B, the same < > swell the theme has on its long notes at 47 and 107. Notes, slur, tenuto, the p espr. at 107 and the violas' 109–110 are unchanged. The engraving is checked at 130 dpi: the two hairpins sit under the C5 and close at the B, with no collisions. The MIDI has no velocity change inside a held note, so after the fix the B4 is equal to the C5 (69/69) instead of louder.

Protection. 107–110 is protected for notes and scoring: the Fl 1 doubling 109–110 (c241, c348), the Vln II D♯4→F4 seed at 108–109, and the 110 crescendo through the cadential 6/4 (c418 Finding 2). None of these is touched. This does not undo an earlier fix. No cycle chose the 108 hairpin; it was carried over from 48.

### 2. Bar 110: crescendo through the cadential 6/4 of F minor (no change)

### 4. Where the music could still go further (considered, not proposed)

I looked for a place where a suspension or a pedal could carry more feeling, using the reading's own models (Ex. 21-15f's 7/6 over V7, Ex. 21-27's delayed resolution, Ex. 21-37's suspended chord). The strongest suspensions are 13–14 (horn call 4-3), 66→67 and 164→165 (delayed cadential 4-3s with rit.), 63 and 159 (7/6 over V7) and 174 (unresolved solo-cello 4-3). Each one already has a tenuto or swell on the dissonance, a softer or delayed resolution, and a tempo easing where one is needed. All of them are protected. I found no change that improves on them and is supported by the reading.
