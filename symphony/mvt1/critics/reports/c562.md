# Critic 562: harmony and voice leading (v562)

Reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 24 "Remaining Uses of Seventh Chords" and Unit 25 "Applied V and VII" (aldwell-schachter PDF 398–441), fourth pass.

I read every text page in the range and rendered all 44 pages at 110 dpi (/tmp/critic-562/pg-398.png to pg-441.png). I looked at every page with examples and worked from the images, because the OCR loses most figures and chord labels. Notes on the scan: book page = PDF page + 6. PDF 402 is a stray page from Unit 23. PDF 403 repeats 401, and PDF 432 repeats 431. Book pages 432, 435 and 441 are missing. Of book 432, only the margin line "over a tonic pedal IV–V7 (or VII°7)–I" survives at the edge of PDF 428. The opening of §18 (rising sequences from V to I in minor, Ex. 25-33) is lost with book 441.

Method: I made a beat and half-beat dump of every sounding note in full.midi (/tmp/critic-562/s/dump.py, output /tmp/critic-562/s/dump.txt). I also scanned every onset of a note that is the 7th or the leading tone of a dominant-seventh, diminished-seventh or half-diminished sonority and listed those that do not move by the expected step when the harmony changes (/tmp/critic-562/s/sev2.py, output sev2.txt, 296 lines). I went through the list by hand. Most entries are arpeggios inside one chord, octave transfers in doubling parts, or the iiø7 of the minor key, where the scan's "leading tone" label does not apply. I also diffed v472 against v562 to find every pitch changed since the last pass of this reading.

## Findings

No change. The score obeys Units 24–25 everywhere outside the decisions earlier critics have already weighed and kept, and I found no place where a technique from this reading would be a clear gain. The details follow.

### 1. Pitch changes since the third pass (v472), checked against the reading

Since v472, four places have changed pitch: the violas at 157 (c502), 158 (c514) and 159 (c522), and Vln II at 177 (c512). All four follow the reading.

- **157 (c502).** The violas' arpeggio is F3–A♭3–C4–F4 in iv. There is no seventh chord, so nothing in the reading applies beyond the doubling rule, which it meets.
- **158–160 (c514, c522).** The violas play C4/E4 on the cadential 6/4, then B3/E4 → B3/F4 at 159, then C4/E♭4 at 160. The 7th F4 enters by step from the 6/4's sixth. It resolves down to E♭4 into ♭VI, and B3 rises to C4. V7 to ♭VI is root motion up a 2nd with the 7th falling (PDF 399–400, Ex. 24-2b), and the leading tone rises in the deceptive progression (PDF 427).
- **177 (c512).** Vln II plays A♭4 on beats 1–2 (the fifth of iiø7), then F4 from beat 3 inside vii°7 over D. F4 falls to E♭4 at 178. The A♭ that is the 7th of vii°7 goes to G in the violas on beat 4. The doubled F at 177.4 (Vln II and Tbn 2) is the one c309 and c472 kept, for the reasons they give.

I rebuilt the unchanged scratch copy, and its vl.txt is identical to v562's.

### 2. Bars 137.4→138: the 7th C4 has no stepwise resolution in its register (considered, not proposed)

**What I found.** At 137.4, iiø7 stands over D. Cl 2, Hn 2 and the violas hold C4 from the iv chord, and C4 becomes a 7th when the bass moves to D. At 25.4→26 the same C resolves into the bass B1, as in Ex. 24-12 (PDF 407). Since c402 the bass at 137.4 is D, held into the stop chord at 138, so the B is not in the bass there. At 138 Cl 2 goes C4→A♭4 and Hn 2 goes C4→F4. The violas go C4→F4/B4, so the resolving B comes in the same instrument a major seventh higher. B4 also sounds in Cl 1 and Tpt 1, and B5 in Fl 1 and Vln I. B3 is absent from the chord. By PDF 406–407 this is a weaker transfer than at 26: it is neither in the same register nor in the bass. No earlier report mentions it. c402 looked only at the bass leap.

**Why I do not propose a fix.** The only clean repair is the violas taking B3/F4 at 138 instead of F4/B4, so that C4 falls to B3. That changes the 138 stop-chord layout, which is a cross-movement record (coherence/mvt1.md lists "violas F4/B4" at 138, and the finale matches 138 at its 222). The other route is to drop C from 137.4, which undoes c402's identity of 25.4 and 137.4. Against that cost the gain is small. Bar 138 is a fresh ff tutti attack after a crescendo, every part re-voices, and B is present in four instruments. PDF 406 places transferred resolutions in "complex instrumental textures", and this is one. c402's reason for the bass D (no tritone leap, no octaves with Hn 3) still stands. Keep.

### 3. Bars 116→117: the applied chord's ♯4 is doubled in two octaves (checked, kept)

The augmented-sixth sonority over C at 116 (A♭–C–D–F♯) has F♯ at F♯3 in Bn 1, Hn 4, Tbn 2 and Vc, and at F♯4 in Cl 2, Hn 2, Tpt 2 and Vln II. The F♯3 voices rise to G3. The F♯4 voices leave or rest: Cl 2 goes to B3, Tpt 2 rests, and Vln II rests on 117.1 and then takes the ♭9 A♭4. Ex. 25-14 (PDF 425) is about doubled leading tones, and parallel and hidden fifths, in the chord of resolution. At 117 the leading tone B is single (Cl 2 and Va B3 in unison), and vl.txt has no fifths here. The upper F♯ is an octave doubling of a resolving voice, and the texture is cut back on purpose for the trombone motto at 117. Bars 111–117 are protected (wedge and G pedal). Keep.

### 4. ♭VII7 at 156 (Ex. 24-18, PDF 410–411), reconsidered

I viewed Ex. 24-18 as an image. Handel holds ♭VII7 over C for three bars with a passing F 6/4, so the ear expects F (III). It then moves the bass up a semitone to C♯ for V6/5. In this score, 156 is ♭VII (B♭ major, darkened to B♭ minor on 156.4.5), 157 is iv, 158 is the 6/4 and 159 is V7. A true ♭VII7 at 156 would be V7 of E♭ heard just before F minor, and the device works only when V6/5 arrives over the raised bass. That would need the bass B♭→B♮, which replaces iv and the 6/4. c553 also tested the ♭VII7–V7 exchange at 156–157 and rejected it. c472's reason is not answered by anything new, and 154–157 is protected. Keep.

### 5. Where the harmony could be more vivid: what I tested on paper

I looked for a passage where a device from these units would add colour that a listener would remember.

- **Deceptive cadence with an applied chord to V (§8, PDF 427).** The movement already delays its two main cadences: 63–68 before 72, and 159–162 (♭VI fff, vii°7 over D, vi) before 165→166. A VII°7 of V over a falling ♯4 bass in either place would replace the ♭VI or vi that the cross-movement plan depends on (the ♭VI thread 82/122/160).
- **Upper-voice pedal (§16, PDF 415).** The ending already uses it (the violas' E♮ over the tonic at 182, protected).
- **Applied chords in sequence (§16–19).** The two sequential passages, 27–32 and the fugato 85–96, use them already, and both are quoted or protected.

I found no unprotected bar where one of these would be a gain without displacing something the other movements depend on.
