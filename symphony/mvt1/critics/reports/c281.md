## Findings

### 1. Bar 45: the clarinet's 7–6 into the second theme is sounded against its own resolution (applied)

**Problem.** The link into S starts at 45 with Cl 1 `bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4)`. Its first note, B♭4, is prepared as a consonance by Hn 1's B♭4 on beat 3 of 44 (the 5th of V4/2 of A♭) and sounds over the C bass of the I6 at 45. B♭ over C is a 7th: this is a 7–6 suspension (an indirect one, A&S PDF 357), and it resolves to A♭4 on beat 3. But the cellos already hold A♭3 from the downbeat of 45. The sounding pitches at 45.1 are C2 (Cb), A♭3 (Vc), C4 (Vla), E♭4 (Vln II), B♭4 (Cl 1). The tone of resolution sounds two beats before the resolution, a major ninth below the suspension, in a soft, sustained, homogeneous texture.

These four notes B♭–A♭–G–F–E♭ are the melodic hinge between the horn-call echo (43–44) and the S pickup E♭3 at 46.4. The B♭ is the only dissonance in the link. It is the one note that should take a slight stress and then relax (A&S PDF 348, §4). With the A♭ already sounding in the cellos, it does not relax onto anything new.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 352, §12, Ex. 21-12c: a 7–6 with the 6 already present in another voice is marked "poor". The text says this "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The exception for "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics" does not apply well here. The clarinet at p and the cellos at pp are both sustained, in the same soft legato. The other exception, the 9–8 with the resolution in the bass, does not apply either: the A♭3 is not the bass (the bass is C2). A&S PDF 350–351, §9, Ex. 21-9c: a 5th may go with a 7–6 if it "moves out of the way". PDF 354, §16, Ex. 21-16a: a 7–6 series "normally begins with a 5–6". This is the four-part model for the fix.

**Fix (trans.ily, transVc, bar 45):** `g2 aes2 |` (was `aes1 |`). The cello holds G3, its note of 41–44, for two more beats and moves G→A♭ (5–6 over the C bass) exactly as the clarinet resolves B♭→A♭ (7–6). The two parts meet on the octave A♭3/A♭4 by contrary motion. Beats 1–2 now sound C–G–C–E♭ with B♭ suspended, and the I6 of A♭ arrives on beat 3 with the resolution. The cello line 41–46 becomes G (five bars) – A♭ – G – E♭, the pickup into S.

**Protection.** "Lead-in 43–47" and "transition route 33–46" were protected by c163, a form cycle that changed the cadence at 53. The route of 33–46 stays as it is: the bass, the keys, V4/2–I6 at 44–45, the link notes (c161/c274) and the cellos' pickup are all unchanged. Only the timing of one inner note changes. Its harmonic arrival moves by two beats, inside the same bar, and the 5–6 is the model the reading gives for this case. c236's protection of 43–47 concerned the double basses' arco→pizz. at 46→47, which is untouched.

Page 5 engraves cleanly. The parts build with 0 warnings. ### 2. Bar 159: the climax tune's E6 is a 7/6 over V7 that rises instead of resolving (no change)

At 158 the tune lands on E6 (with E5 and E4) over the cadential 6/4 of C, a consonant preparation. At 159.1 the chord under it becomes V7 (G1–G2–B3–D4–F4–B5, with D5 in Cl 2), so E is the "13th" that A&S call a 7/6 delaying the 5th (PDF 353–354, §15). The book's model is the J. Strauss waltz, where the 6 falls to the 5. Here E rises to F, the 7th, on beat 2 in eight parts, and then to G6. By the strict rule (PDF 348, §3; PDF 358, §19) this is an upward resolution that is not one of the allowed cases.

I recommend keeping it. The 5th D is already sounding (Tbn 1 D4, Cl 2 D5), so the ear does not wait for it. E–F–G is a stepwise ascent that ends in the drop C6 and the rising sixth C6–A♭6 at 160, the motto interval at the movement's peak. This climax sequence has been protected since c101 for its rising sixths. The tenutos at 158 and 160 (c268) are quoted by the finale. Replacing E–F–G with a falling E–D would flatten the approach to the fff ♭VI, and the finale's quotation would no longer match.

### 3. Bar 59: the cello arpeggio touches G3 under the violins' A♭5 4–3 (no change)

At 59 Vln I·II sing A♭5 over E♭7 on beats 1–2 and resolve to G5 on beat 3, as the horn and cellos did at 50. The cello figure `es,8( bes, des g bes2)` reaches G3 on beat 2½, half a beat early. c171 removed the same early G from the harp at 50. That G sounded above the suspension, at G5 over A♭4, which is the "poor" case. At 59 the G is two octaves and a minor ninth below, in a quaver arpeggio under a sustained violin line, which is the contrast exception A&S name (PDF 352: Beethoven lets a top-voice suspension sound "against the tone of resolution an octave lower in one of the inner voices"). The accompaniment 56–68 is protected (c107). Keep it.

### 4. The themes judged against the reading and the lens (no change)

- **Motto.** Its expressive point is the chromatic neighbour D♭–C, and in the C.a. form (5–8) A♭–G. At 7.4 the G is a dissonant anticipation of bar 8's C-major chord, entered by step and tied into its main note. That is exactly A&S Ex. 21-35 (PDF 364). The swell peaks on the consonant A♭ and fades on the anticipation, which is the right order.
- **P.** The C quaver before every downbeat of the head is a soprano anticipation (PDF 363–364). It is the head's rhythmic fingerprint and is kept in every statement. The accented dotted crotchet is a weak-beat note made rhythmically strong (PDF 362–363, Ex. 21-32).
- **S.** Its dissonances are all on long notes in strong positions: the A♭ appoggiatura at 50, 54, 59; the tied cadential 4 at 66→67 and 164→165 (since v233 the 4 falls on the downbeat, as PDF 347 asks); and the 7/6 C6 over V7 at 63 with the decorated resolution C–D♭–B♭ (PDF 359, Ex. 21-26). This is what makes the tune singable: in each phrase one long note leans and then gives way.
- **Horn call 13–14.** A cadential 6/4 with a delayed resolution: the 6 falls at 13.3, the horn holds the 4 into 14 and falls on beat 2 (PDF 359–360, §21). The leading tone is then left for the bass to resolve at 15 (transferred resolution, PDF 360, §22). It is three notes, built entirely from the reading's two strongest devices.

I found nothing in the reading that would make any theme more memorable by changing its notes. The themes are protected (c091, c101, c171), and I do not reopen that protection.
