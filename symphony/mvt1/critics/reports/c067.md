# Critic 067 — lens 7 (texture and counterpoint): motivic saturation of the non-melodic parts (v86)

## What I studied
All nine section files of v86, read part by part, and HISTORY.md with the earlier texture reports c037 and c047 (c047 §4 lists 15 accompaniment figure types). Corpus: `musicxml-converted/xml/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml` bars 24–31, 91–98 and 149–156, and `Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml` bars 80–87. All corpus bar numbers are the MusicXML measure numbers, extracted with music21. Web sources: Frisch, *Brahms and the Principle of Developing Variation* (UC Press, 1984; https://www.ucpress.edu/books/brahms-and-the-principle-of-developing-variation/paper); a summary of Schoenberg's "Brahms the Progressive" (https://anam.com.au/anam-blog/schoenberg-on-brahms); Brahms 4/i thirds-chain derivation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Brahms)). None of the web sources analyses accompaniment figures specifically, so the corpus comparisons carry the argument.

Classes used: **thematic** means a non-melodic line built from the motto (rising minor sixth, chromatic fall, or the E♭–E♮–F inner voice), the T1 head, the bar-16 turn, the bar-20 rising-eighths figure, or the T2 head. **Neutral** means a sustained chord, repeated notes, a generic arpeggio, or a tremolo pad.

The figures that make up the accompaniment itself are almost all neutral. Of the 15 figure types in c047 §4, only the neighbour-note turn (Vla 103–108, 131–134, from the turn in bar 16) is derived. In this score the motives sit in added counterlines and in the bass, not in the accompaniment figure.

Comparison:
- **Dvořák 9/i** handles lyric themes the same way. At 91–98 the G-minor theme has only a D pedal under it (violins and horns). At 149–156 the G-major flute theme has sustained string chords. At 28–30 the strings arpeggiate the tonic while the winds answer with a dotted figure taken from the theme.
- **Beethoven 9/i** goes further. At 80–87 the whole string accompaniment is one motive (four rising sixteenths to an eighth), and from 84 Vln II takes it in imitation half a bar after Vln I and Vla.

So the development and the recapitulation are at Beethoven's level, and the second group's accompaniment (Vc arpeggios, Vla syncopations, harp) is at Dvořák's. A wholesale change is not needed. A single motivic stroke inside the second group's accompaniment is the Brahms step (Frisch's "developing variation": accompanying voices take part in the motivic process).

### 2. Recap 152: the cello arpeggio can carry the motto in diminution under the tune's motto in long notes (applied)
**Problem.** At 152–153 the tune falls d'''–d♭'''–c''' (the motto's own pitches, cycle 63). The cello plays the neutral figure `bes,8( f bes d' f'2)`, already heard eleven times.
**Fix.** The cello states the motto a fifth lower in the T1-head rhythm: rising minor sixth D–B♭, then A–A♭–G. Augmentation (tune) and diminution (cello) sound together:
```
recTwoArp, bar 152:  bes,8(\< d bes4.) a8( aes8 g8) |
```
**Checks:**
- No new parallels. The Cl1 counter rises f'–g'–a' against the cello's a–a♭–g in contrary motion.
- Passing dissonances, each one eighth long: beat 4 has a♭ against Cl1 g' and against the B♭3 held by Hn3; beat 4.5 has g under the tune's d♭'''.
- 152 is not on any protected list (148–151, 154–157 and 156–158 are). No new D♭ is added.
- This is a recap-only variant, like the 32nd-note scale at 144 (cycle 31).

### 3. Exposition 58: the cello's ii arpeggio can carry the motto transposed to F (applied)
**Problem.** At 56–61 the cello repeats the rising-arpeggio figure. The exposition tune reaches its chromatic fall at 60–61 with nothing in the texture to prepare it.
**Fix.** In 58 (B♭ minor) the cello plays F–D♭–C–C♭–B♭, the motto (G–E♭–D–D♭–C) transposed down a whole step:
```
tTwoVc, bar 58:  bes,8( f des'4.) c'8( ces'8 bes8) |
```
**Checks:**
- The cello's c' (beat 3.5) and c♭' (beat 4) are passing notes over the D♭ bass, against Hn4 B♭3 and Hn3/Vla D♭4. This makes a one-eighth B♭–C–D♭ cluster at p. If the composer finds it thick, give the violas `r4 <des' f'>2 r4` in 58.
- No parallels with the Cl1/Hn2 counter (f'–e♭'–d♭') or the tune.
- 58 is unprotected (cycles 19 and 51 protect 64–68, 47–55 and the counter; Tpt1 60–63 is untouched).

### 4. Exposition 60–67 violas: leave them neutral (no change)
I tried motivic figures for the viola bursts and syncopations at 60–63, for example `r8 bes8( ges'4.) f'8( fes'8 es'8)` at 60. Every version lands in the counter's register (Cl1/Tpt1 f'–e♭'–d♭', fes'–ces'):
- G♭ against f' on beat 2;
- F♭ against e♭' on beat 4;
- unisons on beat 3.5.

Critic 14's register point (c037, "Checked") holds. Dvořák 9/i 91–98 shows that a neutral accompaniment under a lyric theme is normal. Do not churn these bars.

### 5. First theme 15–18: the one place a Brahms would make the accompaniment thematic, but it is protected (not applied)
Vln II/Vla `r8 x8~ x4` and the Cb `x4 r8 x8` are the most generic figures in the movement, at the movement's most exposed thematic point. Cycle 49 protects "notes of 15–22" without a stated texture reason, and the recap version (123–126) is also protected. I make no change. If the composer ever reopens 15–18, an inner voice there should trace E♭–D–D♭–C one bar behind the tune, not with it: in bar 15 the tune already falls D–D♭–C.
