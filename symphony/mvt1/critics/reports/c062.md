# Critic 062 — lens 2 (harmony and voice leading): the bass line as a melodic line

Version reviewed: v81. Scratch copy with fixes: /tmp/critic-062/work.

## What I studied
I extracted the lowest sounding note on every beat (timpani, percussion and harp excluded) from v81's MIDI, and did the same from the MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i, Dvořák 9/i and Beethoven 9/i (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/`). Every beat got a chord label from critic 52's labeller (`/tmp/critic-052/s/lab.py`), so the inversion counts are comparable across the four works. To re-check cycles 53–61 I rebuilt the one-track-per-instrument MIDI (`all.midi`) for v72 and v81 and compared all semitone, major-seventh and minor-ninth clashes between them (`clash8.py`). Note: the `all.midi` stored in v81 is stale. It predates cycle 61 and still has the flute at D6 in bar 23, so rebuild it before using it.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Bars 119–120: viola B3 grinds against the clarinet motto's C4 (applied)
Cycle 57 had the viola hold B (`c'4\p( b2.)~ | b1~ | b2. r4`) under Cl 1's motto G–E♭–D–D♭–C | C–B–C–A♭–G. The motto's C4 at 119 beat 4½, at the 120 downbeat and at 120 beat 2½ sounds a semitone above the viola's held B3, in the same octave, with almost nothing else sounding (pp). The C on the 120 downbeat is a 4 over the G pedal, and the clarinet resolves it to B on beat 2. The viola therefore sounds the resolution note against its own suspension. Cycles 2 and 12 removed exactly this fault at 14, 28, 123–124 and elsewhere. The v72→v81 clash diff shows it as one of only three new clashes. The other two are acceptable: the prepared 7–6 suspension in the cellos at 93, and the viola's lower neighbour against the bassoon's C at 167.
**Fix** (dev.ily, `devVla`): `c'4\p( b2.)~ | b2. r4 | R1 |`. The delayed leading tone at 118 and the hold under G–E♭–D stay. The B ends before the clarinet's D♭–C.

### 2. Bars 150–151: the ii6 bass leaps a seventh down into V (applied)
Cycle 52 put ii6 on beat 3 of 58 and 150. In the exposition the bass steps up into V (Cb `bes,,2 des,2 | es,1`, D♭2→E♭2). In the recapitulation it falls a seventh: Cb `d,2 f,2 | g,,1` (F2→G1) and Bsn 2 `d2 f2 | g,2.` (F3→G2). The reason for using ii6 is that 4̂ moves by step to 5̂, and the seventh loses it. Beethoven 9/i 528–530 puts 4̂ (G) in the bass under iiø6/5 and steps up to A.
**Fix** (recTwo.ily): Cb `d,2 f,,2 | g,,1`; Bsn 2 `d2 f,2 | g,2.`. The bass leaps D–F within one chord and then steps F1→G1. The cello arpeggio and the harp are unchanged, because the cello's figuration starts low in every bar. This also removes the Vc/Cb octave pair falling F→G.

### 3. Bar 100: root-position diminished ii° at f for a whole bar (applied, see protection note)
In the B-minor block, 100 is C♯–E–G over C♯ in Cb, Cbsn, Vc, Bsn 2, B.cl and B.tbn. There is no seventh, and the horns and counter triple the C♯. The tritone above the bass is exposed for four beats before V at 101. Root-position diminished triads take 2.9% of v81's beats, against 0.7–1.2% in the models. Bar 100 is the longest instance outside protected bars. Rogers (above) excludes ii° in root position in minor. Beethoven 9/i 498 and 528–530 uses the supertonic over 4̂. At 160 this score already uses a root-position iiø7, the usual Romantic form.
**Fix applied** (dev.ily, `devHnFour`, inside `\transpose c cis`): `bes1 | bes1 | bes1 | bes1 | bes2 a2 | bes2 a2`, which was `… c'1 | c'2 a2 …`. Horn 4 keeps B from iv (99) into 100, making iiø7 (C♯–E–G–B). It then holds B into 101 and falls to A♯ on beat 3, doubling the melody's own 4–3. The bass keeps its B–E–C♯–F♯ shape. Horn 4 re-articulates each bar, so breaths at the barlines are possible.
**Alternative:** use ii°6 by changing bar 100's `c` to `es` in every bass part (Cb, Cbsn, Vc, Bsn 2, B.cl, B.tbn). The bass then becomes E–E–F♯.
**Protection:** 97–102 is protected for its theme, the T1 tail and the brass counter (cycles 29, 31, 57). Neither version touches those; only a pad note changes.

### 4. Root position everywhere: 71% against 40–59% (diagnosis; no fix in unprotected bars)
The excess sits mostly in protected passages, where every bar is a root-position chord:
- the second theme 47–55;
- the codetta 69–72;
- the recapitulation's third hearing 139–147;
- the fifths sequence 33–40.

Tchaikovsky sets his second theme (6/i 90–100) over a tonic pedal with a cadential 6/4 (A1 under D at 96 and 100). Dvořák and Beethoven use 6 and 6/4 chords on about 13% of beats each. I propose no change in protected bars. If 139–147 is ever reopened, a bass of C–A–F–G | E–F–F–G | C (IV6 at 140, ii6 at 141, ii6/5 at 145) under the unchanged tune would give the third hearing a new harmonisation. It would also answer cycle 30's open "attention risk". For movements II and III, plan to use inversions from the start.

### 5. Direction and contour: sound (no action)
- The bass moves by step 40% of the time, between Dvořák and Tchaikovsky.
- It has three directed runs, at the right places: the chromatic ascent 27–31, the fugato subject 87–88, and F♯–F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C over 101–108, which echoes the motto's D–D♭–C.
- The large-scale line A♭ (73–78) – E (78–79) – C (80–81) – A♭ (82) – G (83–84) is a cycle of major thirds onto the dominant. It is well judged.

The only octave break inside a line is F♯1→F2 at 102→103. Bsn 2 already plays it as a semitone, the Cb cannot go lower, and 103 starts a new phrase at mf. Leave it.

### 6. Pedals: not overused (no action)
Every pedal in the movement:
- E♭, 41–43 (3 bars);
- A♭, 56–57 (2);
- A♭, 72–78 (6);
- F♯, 101–102 (2);
- G, 109–122 (14);
- G, 136–138 (3);
- C, 148–149 (2);
- G, 154–155 (2);
- C, 162/163–166 (4–5);
- C, 175–178 (4).

That is 26% of beats, against 36% in Tchaikovsky 6/i and 25% in Dvořák 9/i. The longest, 14 bars, is less than half of Tchaikovsky's F♯ pedal at 275–310. G1 is the lowest sound on 16% of beats because of the dominant pedals, which is normal for a C-minor movement.

### 7. Register plan: correct (no action)
C1 sounds only at 163–166 (contrabassoon), D♭1/D1 only at 132–133, and F♯1 is the lowest note before the recapitulation. Beethoven 9/i likewise keeps its lowest D♭1/D1 for the recapitulation and coda (225–227, 513–544).

### 8. Bass melody: present, but no Brahms-style countermelody (for later movements)
The bass carries real lines at 27–32, 101–108, 167–170 (the cello theme is the lowest voice) and 171–175 (augmented motto in Vc, Cb and B.tbn). It never sings a countermelody of its own under a theme. The unprotected bars (56–63, 152–153) already carry the Cl/Hn/Tpt counterline, so a bass countermelody there would overload the texture. Plan one for II or III.
