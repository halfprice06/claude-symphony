## What I studied

- **Assigned reading:** the Rimsky-Korsakov rules and example pages listed above.
- **Master movement for this cycle:** the first movement of Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto, Op. 35 (corpus: `mutopia/TchaikovskyPI_O35`, PDF `pdf/TchaikovskyPI_O35__tchai_op35.pdf`). I looked at the opening (bars 1–12, score pages 2–3), the tutti chords at 120–126, and the Moderato assai tutti from 127 (score pages 22–23). No earlier holistic critic has used this work. Its orchestral writing comes from the same years and the same Russian school as the reading.
- **Comparison with v148:** I diffed v148 against v158, file by file.
- **My own checks:** a MIDI scan for semitone clashes (two different instruments a semitone apart in the same octave for at least 1.5 beats), and a scan for wide unfilled gaps between parts.

## Verdict

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? For the orchestration, yes. Tchaikovsky's tutti at 127 does these things:

- the theme is in the violins and flutes in octaves;
- horns and trumpets play repeated chords as a rhythmic basis (principle 17);
- the bass falls on the strong beats;
- the string sf chords at 120–123 fall on downbeats after rests, never at the end of a run.

This score's tuttis at 27–32, 131–134 and 167–170 use the same methods: hammered trumpet and timpani rhythm, a chromatic bass in eighths, held horns. The crescendo and diminuendo orders follow Rimsky's rule (principle 13) at every build and collapse I checked.

The two flaws I found are both in detail work. A player or a careful conductor would notice them in rehearsal.

Between v148 and v158 nothing got worse in musical substance. One change made since v148 did make a passage worse: cycle 134's triple stop for Vln II at 33 (F1).

## Findings

### F1. Bar 32→33, Vln II: the slurred sixteenth run leads into a triple stop. Applied.

**Problem.** Cycle 134 changed Vln II at 33 from `c''8` to `<g es' c''>8->\downbow\ff`. The bar before ends with `r4 f'16(\< g' aes' b')`, a slurred upbeat run at ♩ = 126. The player must finish the slurred run and then attack a three-string chord with a fresh down-bow. There is no time to set the bow on three strings.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 130: anacrusis notes "in the strings … should not lead up to chords of three or four notes, as this would be awkward for the bow."
- His Ex. 254 (PDF 418): every string anacrusis run ends on a single note or a double stop.
- Tchaikovsky, Op. 35 i, 120–123: the multiple-stop sf chords come after rests.
- In this score, Vln I's run at 32 lands on a single `c'''`, and the Vla and Vc triple stops at 33 have no run before them. Only Vln II has the problem.

**Fix (applied in trans.ily).**
```
transVlnTwo = {
  <es' c''>8->\downbow\ff r8 r4 r2 | ...
```
The G3 is still sounded by the Vla (`<c g es'>`) and the Vc (`<c, g, es>`), so the chord keeps all its notes.

**History.** This partly undoes cycle 134's change. It does not answer against cycle 134's reason (Rimsky's tutti-chord appendix, where strings use multiple stops in tutti chords): the Vla and Vc keep their triple stops and Vln II keeps a double stop. Bar 33 is not on any do-not-touch list.

### F2. Bars 28 and 132, Hn 4: the horn holds the resolution note G3 under the theme's A♭3 appoggiatura in the same octave. Applied.

**Problem.** In the second bar of the first-theme tutti, the head's appoggiatura `aes4.-> g8` falls on beats 3–4. Hn 4 holds `g1` through it, in the same octave:
- at 28 under the violas, which carry the theme's third octave (A♭3);
- at 132 under Tbn 1·2, Bsn and Vc, which carry the low theme (A♭3).

At 132 both lines are brass. My MIDI scan found these as the only sustained same-octave semitone clashes in the movement that are not over a pedal or inside the fugato.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 132–133: a melodic design with appoggiaturas against "fundamental notes" in close register is discordant. Difference of timbre reduces this, but "between wood-wind and brass … the harmonic basis generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design, and should be of inferior dynamic power". This is even more true when both are brass.
- His Ex. 264–266 (PDF 430–433): the sustained basis lies in a different register from the moving line.
- Cycle 122 fixed the same fault (a resolution note doubled during its suspension) at 32, 141–142 and 54.

**Fix (applied).**
- tOne.ily `tOneHnFour`, bar 28: `g1` → `g2 r2`
- recOne.ily `recOneHnFour`, bar 132: `g1\f` → `g2\f r2`

On beats 3–4 the G remains in the bass and arrives in the melody on beat 4½. Hn 3 keeps B3 at 28 and `c'2. b4` at 132. The rest also gives Hn 4 a breath in a long ff/f stretch.

**History.** These bars are inside the protected 27–32 and 131–134. The protection covers the theme's notes, the tutti layout and the graded brass dynamics. All of those are unchanged: only the length of one horn's held note changes. I left 128 alone. There Hn 4's G3 is the dominant pedal (principle 16), which is protected.

### F3. Checked, no change: the clashes that remain fit the reading

- **92:** the cor anglais's D♭4 against the cellos' C4 is woodwind against strings. That is the timbre difference Rimsky accepts (PDF 133). It is also inside the protected fugato.
- **117 and 122:** the A♭ against G is the planned upper neighbour over the G pedal (principle 16).
- **97–102:** the clarinets' arpeggio F♯4 against the Vln II / cor anglais appoggiatura G4 at 98 moves in eighths. Rimsky says the faster the figure, the less harsh the clash (PDF 132). I propose nothing for this protected block.
