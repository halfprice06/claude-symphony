## Findings

### 1. The themes measured against the reading's model melodies (no change)

The reading's examples are among the best-known woodwind tunes in the repertoire: Dvořák 9/i (Ex. VII-3), Brahms 4/iv (VII-4), Brahms 1/iv alphorn (VII-9), Schumann 2/iii (VII-34), Berlioz *Roman Carnival* (VII-48), Tchaikovsky 5/i (VII-59) and 6/i (VII-93), Brahms *Haydn* (VII-99). Looked at as images, they share three things: a short rhythmic cell that comes back (Tchaikovsky 5 and 6, Dvořák 9's dotted figure); one peak, placed late and marked (Schumann's fp, Berlioz's sf, Tristan's f); and long notes on strong beats with a swell on each in slow melodies (Brahms 1/iv). I checked each theme against them.

- **Motto (1–4, then C.a. 5–8).** One cell (rising minor sixth, semitone fall, neighbour D♭), a swell on each slur, then the answer a fifth higher in a new colour. This is the Tchaikovsky 5/6 pattern (PDF 211, 227). It is short enough to recognise in any colour, and every return has a different woodwind (B.cl, C.a., Fl, low Cl 1 at 129–130, chorale, Vln I). It is at a master's level.
- **P (15–22).** The syncopated accent after the pickup (r8 G | E♭ dotted crotchet) is its cell; bar 17 repeats it a step higher and bar 19 a step higher again, and the consequent peaks at the sf D♭6 of 20, bar 6 of 8. That is the late marked peak of Ex. VII-34/VII-48.
- **S (46.4–55).** The pickup sixth E♭–C, the held C with a swell (Ex. VII-9's long note), the dotted figure at 49 and 52, and the peak F5 at 52 (bar 6 of 9) reached by an octave leap, where Cl 1 joins the cello. The clarinet entering at the peak is the reading's point that a woodwind gives the melody its colour (PDF 184). The violins' restatement peaks at C6–D♭6, 62–63.
- **Horn call (13–14).** Three notes, E♭3–C4–B3, which grow into the S head (E♭–C). It is not a tune like Brahms's alphorn call (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188), which is four bars of long notes. Here it works as a motive, and movement II's theme A fills the same E♭3–C4 span in the same horn (mvt1.md seeds), so I keep it.

I found nothing in this reading that would make a theme better. All four ideas begin with a rising sixth, which ties them together. What keeps them apart is the type-casting of PDF 184: P is violins at f, S is horn and cellos dolce over harp at Poco meno mosso, the motto has a low woodwind solo, and the call is a solo horn.

### 2. The oboe never states a theme alone in movement I (no change)

Adler calls the oboe "basically a lyrical instrument" and lists its solos (PDF 198–199, Ex. VII-33 to VII-35). In this movement Ob 1 has no solo theme. It doubles (23–25, 69–72, 135–137), answers for one bar (79), and plays the dolce link 145–146. Every other woodwind has a solo statement of the motto or P. A master's first movement would often give the oboe a phrase, so I checked whether one should be added. I recommend no change. Movement II gives Ob 1 its first solo, in dialogue with the solo violin at II 15–22 and with the theme B head at II 45–46 (coherence/mvt2.md). Keeping the oboe back here keeps that colour fresh, in the way Dvořák 9 keeps the cor anglais for its slow movement (Ex. VII-50 list, PDF 206). The cor anglais already supplies the double-reed solo colour in movement I (5–8, 147–153, 171–174). The places where an oboe solo would fit (S at 47–63, the recapitulated S at 147–155, the coda 171–174) are all protected (c088, c247, c311, c270).

### 3. The cor anglais motto at 5–8 is in the mellow band, not the rich low sixth (no change)

PDF 205 (Ex. VII-47) praises the cor anglais's lowest fifth or sixth, written B3 up to about G4, sounding E3–C4, for its "tremendous expressive carrying power". The motto at 5–8 lies at sounding D4–B♭4 (written A4–F5), in the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band. It cannot go an octave lower, because D3 is below the instrument. The same holds for the coda motto at 171–174 (G4–E♭5), which must start on the motto's own G. Both lines are within the instrument's best expressive band and are slurred and swelled like the Berlioz and Wagner solos (Ex. VII-48, VII-49). No change.

### 4. Exposed soft woodwind melody is never covered (no change)

PDF 185 and 226 ask that weak registers and accompanied bassoon solos not be covered. I checked each soft woodwind melody against its accompaniment in the score:
- Fl 1 73–76 (p espr., C5–A♭5): Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb and Hn 4 pp, all below it, as in the Dvořák and Brahms examples (PDF 186).
- Bsn 1 33–36 (f > mf, G3–E♭4): Cb fp, violas mp from 34, Vc p from 35.
- B.cl 77–78 (mf espr.): strings p tremolo.
- B.cl 107–109 and Fl 1 109–110: Vln II pp, violas p, Cb p pizz.
- Bsn 1 144: the strings mp > pp (c261).
- C.a. 147–153: it plays in the gaps of the Hn 1/viola/cello S, on beats 3–4.
- Cl 1 175–177 (pp espr.): pp trombones and strings below it; it enters on E♭4 alone (c247).

Each follows the reading. The changes since c261 (c347: Hn 3 rests under Hn 1 at 104–105; c348: Hn 3 takes Bsn 1's B3 from 145.3) do not cover a woodwind line. The second is the dovetail Adler describes in Ex. VII-9 (PDF 188).

### 5. Clarinet throat tones and the break in soft melody (no change)

PDF 210 calls the throat tones (written G4–B♭4) "rather pale" and the break a problem only for less expert players. Five passages touch them:
- the dolce link at 45–46 (written C5–F4), kept by c274 and c298 because the pale colour suits a dying link;
- the S consequent at 52–54 and 152–153 (written G5 down to A4 and back to E♭5, mp >), crossing the break twice but doubled by the cellos;
- the counterline 56–68 with Hn 2 (written F4–C5, mostly throat tones);
- the dissolution counter at 164–166;
- the horn-call recall at 175–177 (written F4 up to D5, pp).

At 56–68 the clarinet adds little of its own colour, but the line is carried by Hn 2 at the same pitch, so it is not lost. Moving Cl 1 up an octave would put the counterline above the violins' tune (C5–A♭5 at 56–59), which is worse, and c278 protects the equal p of tune and counterline there. In the other four passages the register fits the expression or the line is doubled. No change.

### 6. Melodic peaks and range extremes in the woodwinds (no change)

PDF 186 (flute above A6), 198 (oboe C6–F6), 205 (cor anglais top), 217 (bass clarinet above written G5) and 230 (contrabassoon above its lowest twelfth) mark the registers to avoid in melody. Every peak stays inside them:
- Fl 1's highest note is A♭6, at 31, 72, 122, 140 and 160, reached by scale at 122 and 140 and by the motto's sixth (doubled, fff) at 160.
- Ob 1 goes above C6 only at 102 (D6, ff, doubled).
- The cor anglais reaches written C6 only in tutti doublings (70, 94–95).
- The bass clarinet's top is G3 (written A4).
- The contrabassoon's top is F2.
