## Findings

All findings are "keep". After three passes of this reading I find no place within the form lens where a change would bring the score closer to a master's work. I tested one candidate change in the abstract (Finding 4) and do not recommend it.

### 1. The retransition follows the same plan as a master model: a 6/3 chain, an augmented-sixth bar, then a long dominant pedal (keep)

**Bars.** 111–116 (bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C, i – V6/5 of F – i6 of C – a voice-leading chord – D♭ 5/3 – Fr6), 117–130 (G pedal), 131 (recapitulation).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 290–292 make the 6/3 series the device for leaving a stable point and reaching a goal, with only the first and last chords emphasised. PDF 298 (Ex. 18-11) lengthens the applied chord before V so that V falls on a strong beat. The wedge does both. The Fr6 lasts all of 116, and V arrives on the downbeat of 117 at ff. The new evidence is the corpus. In the MCTL annotation of Mozart's Jupiter, first movement, bars 172–177 are a descending chain V4/2–I6 in E, D and C, so the bass falls by step under first-inversion arrivals. It reaches an Italian sixth at 178 and a G pedal from 179 to 186. The recapitulation begins at 189. This movement has the same order (6/3 chain → augmented sixth → dominant pedal → return) at a larger scale: 6 bars of chain (11.4 s) and 14 bars of pedal (29.2 s), against Mozart's 6 and 8. The longer pedal suits a Romantic movement that puts its second fff climax (122) inside the pedal. Mozart has no such climax. **Fix:** none.

### 2. The tension curve and the placement of 6/3 chords (keep, re-measured on v473)

**Bars.** Whole movement. Section durations from v473/full.midi: introduction 67.2 s, P 34.3 s, transition 26.9 s, S 47.1 s, codetta 15.2 s, development 105.3 s, recapitulation of the first group 30.7 s, recapitulation of the second group 44.8 s, coda 58.9 s (total about 430 s).

**Evidence.** The loudness and part-count curve (curve.txt) has three peaks of rising weight (82, 122, 160). Each is approached through chords that move (6/3s, a pedal, or a Phrygian approach) and left by a drop: 84 (two parts), 123–126 (sostenuto collapse), 162–166 (falling thirds). The arrivals at 15, 27, 47, 69, 117, 131, 147, 167 and 182 are root-position chords or pedals. This is the division the reading makes between chords that move and chords that arrive (PDF 290–291: "the 6/3s provide a sense of motion and the 8/5s sound like goals"). The development is 85% of the exposition's length (15–76) in time, and the recapitulation is 61% of it. Earlier critics measured and protected these proportions (c393, c413, c433, c443, c453, c463), and I find no reason in this reading to reopen them. **Fix:** none.

### 3. The retransition's "augmented III6" is the reading's dominant with 6 for 5 (keep)

**Bars.** 127 (E♭–G–B over G1), 129 (E♭ over G), 130 (C–D♭–D–E♭ over G).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 300–301: over V in minor a 6th that decorates or replaces the 5th gives an apparent augmented triad in 6/3 position, which "functions as V" (Bach, Ex. 18-18). At 127 the stretto of the half-augmented motto puts the motto's E♭ over the G pedal with B in the texture. That is Ex. 18-18's chord, and as in Bach, E♭ falls to D. This is what lets the motto's own interval (E♭–D) sound four times over one unchanging dominant without the harmony moving to III. The bars are thin (two to six parts at 127–130 against 26 at 131), so the recapitulation lands as a tutti after a quiet, dissonant dominant. That is a recognised Romantic approach to a recapitulation, and it is protected (c437, c467, c428). **Fix:** none.

### 4. No 7–6 suspension into the Phrygian iv6 at 61 (keep; a new candidate considered and rejected)

**Bars.** 60–62 in A♭: bass G♭ (60, v6/5 on beats 3–4 since c273) – F♭ (61, iv6) – E♭ (62, cadential 6/4).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 298 says the 7–6 into the 6th of the Phrygian iv6 is "almost invariably" present (Handel, Ex. 18-12). In Ex. 18-12 it is in the top voice. Here no voice holds E♭ over the F♭. c273 and c363 rejected the only line they considered, the protected Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline. I tested a new candidate on paper: Hn 3, which holds D♭4 for three beats of 60 and plays A♭3 at 61. It could take E♭4 on 60.4, tie it over the bar, and fall to D♭4 on 61.3. I reject this for three reasons. (a) At 61.1–2 the protected counterline plays F♭4 in Hn 2 and Cl 1 (inst.py), so Hn 3's E♭4 would sound a semitone below it in the same horn colour and register. The dissonance would then sit between two horns, not against the bass as in Handel. (b) Hn 3's A♭3 is the only brass third of the iv6, so the horns' chord would lose its third. (c) The tune already does what the suspension is for. Vln I holds B♭5 through 60 and falls by semitones, B♭5–A5–A♭5, onto the downbeat of 61 in 10ths with the bass G♭–F♭, so the downbeat already arrives by step from above. The 7–6 is a Baroque mark of this cadence. Tchaikovsky and Dvořák often write the Phrygian bass without it. **Fix:** none.

### 5. The recapitulated second group keeps the C–B♭–F–G approach, not the textbook Phrygian (keep; c303's reason still holds)

**Bars.** 155–158.

**Evidence.** PDF 298–299 would put A♭ (♭6̂) in the bass at 157 to complete 1̂–♭7̂–♭6̂–5̂. c303 and c363 rejected this because a bass A♭→G at 157→158 would play in advance, and reverse, the deceptive G→A♭ of 159→160, the fff ♭VI climax. I re-checked the bass in harm.txt. Its only A♭ between 147 and 166 is the A♭1 at 160 (Cbsn, Cb and timpani). The leap F→G at 157→158 keeps ♭6̂ new for the climax. In form, the third climax depends on that one pitch, so the reading's local rule gives way to the large-scale plan. **Fix:** none.

### 6. Introduction and coda: 6/3s as voice-leading chords around root-position goals (keep)

**Bars.** 6–8 (E♭/G, D♭/F → C major), 172–174 (iv – N6 – cadential 6/4 – V), 176–179 (i6 – V4/3 – i under the trombone chorale).

**Evidence.** Bars 6–7 are the Dufay pattern of Ex. 18-1 (PDF 290): two 6/3s in stepwise descent to an 8/5 goal, in three real voices over a doubled bass (PDF 295). The C major at 8 is the movement's first goal, and the coda's last chord at 182 repeats it. At 173 the 6 displaces the 5 (Ex. 18-19j) when the cor anglais moves D5→D♭5 over F. At 176–178 the passing V4/3 inverts Ex. 18-10, so the chorale moves without a root-position V–I until 179, as c440 recorded ("coda without root-position V–I"). The two ends of the movement use the same 6/3-to-goal motion, and this is part of why the frame holds together. All are protected and quoted by the other movements. **Fix:** none.
