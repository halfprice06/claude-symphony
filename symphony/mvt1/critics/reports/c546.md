# Critic 546: Playability, instrument by instrument (v546)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 6 The Woodwind Choir and ch. 7 Individual Woodwinds (adler1 PDF 168-232), fourth pass (cycles 256, 346 and 456 read the same chapters). I read the text of every page from 168 to 232 and rendered all 65 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one, with particular attention to the range and register charts (Ex. VII-1/2 PDF 184-185, VII-31/32 PDF 198, VII-46/47 PDF 204-205, VII-54/56 PDF 209-210, VII-73 PDF 216, VII-88/89 PDF 225-226, VII-97 PDF 230) and to Brahms's flute dovetailing (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188). Book page = PDF page - 16.

Method. I ran a MIDI script over v546/full.midi that lists, for each of the eleven wind parts, the range, the four longest stretches without a gap of 0.2 s, and every note in a register Adler marks as weak, thick, thin or pinched, and compared the output with the same script run on v456 (the version cycle 456 checked). I checked every long stretch against the `\breathe` marks and slur breaks in the .ily source. New in this pass: a scan of every clarinet move across the break (sounding A-flat4/A4, written B-flat4/B4) by a third or more in a quaver or less. I also diffed the wind variables in v456 and v546, checked the wind part page counts in v546/parts-run.txt, and checked the score's transposition labels against parts-gen.py. I looked at score pages 2, 3, 17 and 18.

Changes to the winds since v456 are small: Bsn 1 breath at the end of 22 (c496), Cl 1 crescendo 129-130 and its slur at 164-165 (c498), Bsn 1 slur ending on the tied C at 141-142 (c505). The script output for v456 and v546 differs only where those marks fall.

## Findings

### 1. No woodwind change is needed.

Every principle in the table that applies is followed in every bar I checked. The three wind changes since v456 all follow the reading:

- **Bsn 1 breath at the end of 22 (c496).** It splits 19-26 into 19-22 (about 7.6 s, mf) and 23-26, and it falls with Hn 3-4's breath. This is what Adler asks for (PDF 175, 188: rest the player after a sustained passage).
- **Cl 1 crescendo 129-130 into ff at 131 (c498).** It is in the clarion register, where the clarinet has its full dynamic range (PDF 175, 210).
- **Bsn 1 slur ending on the tied C at 141-142 (c505).** The B at 142 is now tongued. In the tenor register at f this is plain articulation (PDF 176).

Breathing. The longest stretches, after the `\breathe` marks and slur breaks in the source are taken into account, are the same as in c456's table. The exceptions are Bsn 1 19-26, which is now split, and Bsn 2 171-174: four semibreves C3-F3-F3-G2 at pp at crotchet = 72 (13.3 s). That is above the lowest fifth, and Bsn 2 rests from 175 to the end. Keep. I found nothing over 12 s that lacks a breath point. The two exceptions are the protected C.a. 5-9 (19.2 s, oboe-family breath, PDF 175) and the B.cl chalumeau lines at 1-5 and 175-180, which have slur breaks and are doubled.

Parts. The wind parts are one or two pages each (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1, Bsn 2 two; the rest one), so no wind player turns a page while playing.

### 2. Candidates tested against the reading and rejected

The task asks where a master would have done better within this lens. I tested these four and none is a real improvement:

- **Clarinet leaps across the break (new scan; PDF 210).** I found 19 fast moves across the break by a third or more. Sixteen are the P-head pickup (G4 or E-flat4 up to E-flat5/F5/C5, sounding) at f-ff in tutti statements, the gesture every wind plays at 27, 82, 117 and 139. Adler says the break "presents no difficulties whatsoever" once the player is experienced (PDF 210), and his own examples cross it freely: Mendelssohn's *Hebrides* (Ex. VII-63, PDF 212) and Tchaikovsky 5/i (Ex. VII-59, PDF 211). The soft ones are 66.4 (mp, in the S counterline, c401 protected) and 164.4 (mp, slurred G4-D5 sounding, c498). Both are routine for a professional. Keep.
- **Brahms-style dovetail of a second wind into the B.cl's held chalumeau notes at 177-180 (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188).** After the slur break at 176/177 the phrase is about 12.6 s at pp, under a rit. and Tempo I. That is a normal clarinet breath at pp, and the B.cl is doubled by the bass trombone and cellos, so a breath would not leave a hole. A second wind on these notes would thicken the chorale's colour plan (175-179, protected c398/c470). Keep.
- **Raise Bsn 2's C2 at 166 out of the lowest fifth (PDF 226).** Adler rules out pp there, not p, and asks only for a firmer attack. The C2 is the bass of the cadence under Vc/Cb at p after a mp diminuendo. Raising it would remove the 16-foot doubling of the bass at the cadence. Keep.
- **Fl 1-2 B4 pickup at 117.1 in the flute's weak low octave under the ff brass motto (PDF 185).** It is a quaver pickup that leaps a minor seventh to A-flat5, doubled by Ob 1, Cl 1-2 and violins, so the flute is not needed for volume on that note. Starting the flutes on the A-flat5 would break the a2 head that c424 set (Fl 2 on the whole head). Keep.

The woodwind writing has no remaining playability weakness that the reading identifies. Where a master would do more, the gap is in melody or form, not in this lens, and earlier critics have protected those passages (for example the 156-157 climb).
