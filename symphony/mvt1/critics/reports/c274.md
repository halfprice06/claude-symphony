## Findings

### 1. Bars 45–46: the link into the second theme belongs to Cl 1, not to the oboe in its lowest fifth (APPLIED)

**What is wrong.** Ob 1 plays the link alone, `bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4)`, at p with poco rit., over pp strings and as Hn 1·2 fade. The line goes down to F4 and E♭4, which is the oboe's lowest fifth (its lowest note is B♭3). At this soft, slowing point the oboe is at its thickest. The next sound is the dolce horn and cello theme at 47. This is the only solo the oboes have between 43 and 69.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Adler PDF 270–271, Ex. VIII-41 (Copland, *Appalachian Spring*): in a restful passage that is dying away, Copland takes the first oboe out "for it honks too much in that extreme low register" and substitutes a quieter instrument. Bars 45–46 are the same situation: the end of a diminuendo, the lowest notes, a p marking and a ritardando.
- Adler PDF 238 (Schubert 8/i): the woodwinds that lead into the second theme are the clarinets. Each thematic element is scored differently, and that makes the form clear. With the change the link is clarinet, the theme is horn and cello, and the restatement at 56 has Cl 1 + Hn 2 as its counter. The clarinet that leads into 47 is the colour that comes back at 56.
- Adler PDF 237: in combination "the clarinet would be the most poignant factor". B♭4–E♭4 lies in the clarinet's chalumeau and throat, which play softest. A diminuendo to the end of the phrase, which c198 asks for (A&S PDF 348), is easy there. On the oboe at E♭4 it is not.

**The earlier decision, and my answer to it.** c196 proposed moving the link to the cor anglais. c198 closed that proposal for three reasons: the low notes are only the last two, at p rather than pp; the cor anglais is the motto's colour; and the change would undo c161's choice. Copland's case answers the first reason: the oboe is taken out exactly at the soft close, even when only the last notes are low. The second reason does not apply, because the clarinet is not the motto's colour. The motto stays in C.a., B.cl/Vc and trombones (c261's do-not-touch on motto woodwind colours is kept). On the third reason, c161 changed the notes (the dotted cell `aes'4. g'8`), and those notes are kept exactly. Only the instrument changes.

**Fix (trans.ily).** `transOboeOne`: bars 45–46 become `R1*2 |`. `transClOne`: bar 44 `R1`, then `\partCombineSoloI \soloStemsOn bes'2(\p aes'4. g'8 | f'2 es'4) r4 \soloStemsOff \partCombineAutomatic |`. Cl 2 rests at 44–46, so the shared staff prints "1.". Cl 1's E♭4 on beat 3 of 46 still leads to Hn 1's E♭3 pickup, at the octave. Clarinet and horn blend (Adler PDF 234).

**Test.** 0 warnings, 18 PDF pages, analysis.txt shows only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1", and the vl.txt diff is empty (same pitches, another track). Parts: 0 warnings. The parts that cross the join keep even page counts (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Hn 3, Hn 4, timpani 2 pp; Cb 4 pp). The Ob 1 part now shows a two-bar rest at 45–46, and the Cl 1 part shows the line written C5–F4 with "poco rit.".

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **The three A♭ climaxes (82, 122, 160).** The reading supports all three. Each is scored differently (PDF 255). Each has sustained wind and horn harmony under the moving parts (PDF 238–241). At 122 and 160 the larger gaps are in the bass (PDF 257). At 160 the brass are held below the tune's dynamic (PDF 268). No change. The protections from c194, c244 and c264 stand.
- **103–106, P head in Ob 2 + Cl 1·2 at the unison, f.** PDF 249–250 warns against like-wind unisons on a solo line, but this statement is set at f against the S head in Hn 1, Bsn 1 and Vc (mf). Its purpose is volume against a horn, which is the exception Adler allows. Oboe + clarinet in unison is also the colour Adler praises in Schubert (PDF 235–236). Keep.
- **111–114, Ob 2 = Cl 1 = Hn 1 pad.** PDF 257 says an overlap "only results in a stronger pitch". Under a six-bar crescendo to ff, a stronger pitch is what is needed. c264's reason (octaves added gradually) holds. Keep.
- **23–25 and 135–137, Fl 1 with Ob 1 and Vln I at the unison.** This is Schubert's practice exactly (PDF 238): the flute is hidden in the crescendo and comes out on the stop chord. Keep (HISTORY cycle 59 rule).
- **139–142, four instruments at the unison under Fl 1 alone.** PDF 250's organ analogy would prefer more weight on the 4′ line, but c194 keeps the violins' A♭6 for 160, and Adler's own Schubert example (PDF 238) supports holding the top back. Keep.
