## The three A♭ climaxes

I checked 82, 122 and 160 against principles 1, 3, 6, 8, 9 and 21–22. They are scored as three different events. At 82 the P head is in three octaves (Fl 1·2 + Vln I at G5; Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Tpt 1 and Vln II at G4; violas at G3) over a held A♭ chord in horns and trombones, ff. At 122 everything stops on a sustained V♭9 at fff: A♭6 in Fl 1 and Vln I, a complete brass chord G3–A♭4, the tam-tam, and a five-instrument bass on G (Adler's Holst and Mahler 1 cases, PDF 164, 181). At 160 the tune is in three octaves, each with string, woodwind and brass (A♭6 in Fl 1·2 + Vln I; A♭5 in Ob 2 + Tpt 1 + Vln II; A♭4 in Hn 1·2 + Vc), the counter-melody is in Ob 1 + Cl 1 with Hn 3·4 an octave below, the violas have repeated eighths, and the held brass are ff under the fff tune. This is the *Scheherazade* layering of PDF 179 and the *Meistersinger* rule of PDF 178. I propose no change to any of them.

## Findings

### 1. Fugato 94–95: the cor anglais drops Vln II's line on its leading tone and Cl 1 takes the resolution (applied)

**Bars and problem.** The C.a. doubles Vln II's third entry an octave lower at 91–93. Its last bar is the rising scale F4–G4–A♭4–A♮4 (staccato), which leads to B♭4. At 94 the C.a. stops on the A♮, and the B♭4 and the rest of the countersubject (B♭4–C5–D♭5–E♭5–F5 | D♭5 …) go to Cl 1, in unison with Vln II. So the voice that has just been established changes colour at the moment the fourth entry (Vln I + Fl 1) begins, and the C.a.'s own line is left on a leading tone.

**Evidence.** Adler's analysis of the *Jupiter* finale (adler2 PDF 176) separates two stages. While the entries come in, each statement gets a new colour from a different string partner. "Once the web of counterpoint becomes truly involved … keep an established color intact long enough to carry out its assignment. The listener must not be overburdened with shifting colors." In mm. 389–400 every string part keeps one wind partner. The fugato here follows that plan in every other voice. Bsn 1 joins the violas at 88 and stays an octave below them through 95, and Fl 1 joins Vln I at 94. Only Vln II changes partner, from C.a. to Cl 1. PDF 177–178 (*Meistersinger*) makes the same point: each element is "clearly perceived because [it is] assigned to very distinctive color combinations". At 94–95 a double-reed colour on Vln II's line stands further from the new Fl 1 + Vln I entry than Cl 1 does.

**Fix (sections/dev.ily).** The C.a. continues 94–95 at pitch, in unison with Vln II. This is written F5–C6, a comfortable register. It rests at 96, which gives it a breath before its ff entry on the B-minor S at 97.4–98. Cl 1 rests at 94–95 and joins Vln II at the ff restatement at 96, as before:

**Earlier decisions.** c187 put Cl 1 in unison with Vln II at 94–95, replacing an octave-below doubling (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76–78: register-matched string + wind unisons; avoid one wind an octave from the strings). The C.a. unison keeps that reason: it is register-matched and at pitch. The protected entries and their colours (Vc 85, Vla + Bsn 1 at 88, Vln II + C.a. at 91, Vln I + Fl 1 at 94) are unchanged. The change carries the 91 entry's colour into its countersubject, which is what PDF 176 asks for.

### 2. Recapitulation 131–134: the accompaniment in the theme's own octave is louder than the theme's brass (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 131 the P head returns low, G3–E♭4, in Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2 and Vc at ff and Tbn 1·2 at f (c144: the trombones are graded so that the theme is a blend). Two accompanying parts lie on the theme's own pitches and are marked as loud as the theme or louder. The violas play off-beat E♭4/G4 dyads ff for four bars, which are the theme's first long note and its upbeat. Hn 3·4 attack C4/G3 ff (with a diminuendo to f at 132), inside the theme's range. The other accompanying brass are already graded, with Hn 1·2 at f and the trumpets f in the timpani rhythm (c174). So at the recapitulation the accompaniment in the theme's octave is graded more weakly than the theme's own trombones.

**Evidence.** PDF 159 rule 1: "The melody notes should be more prominent than the harmony notes." PDF 184 (Schoenberg Op. 16/5): the principal voice is marked louder than the subordinate ones, and a subordinate idea is marked softer still where its register makes it prominent. PDF 169 (Schubert 8/i): the ear separates melody from background when they are kept apart in colour and register. Here they share a register, so the dynamic has to make the separation. The movement already follows this rule at 97–102 (brass counter f under the ff tune) and 167–169 (held winds f under the ff brass head). Bar 131 is the exception.

**Fix (sections/recOne.ily).** Violas f at 131, Hn 3·4 f at 131 (the ff and the diminuendo are removed, and so is the now redundant f at 132):

```
recOneHnThree:  c'1\f | c'2. b4 | d'1 | d'1 \breathe |
recOneHnFour:   g1\f | g2 r2 | f1 | g1 |
recOneVla:      r8 <es' g'>8\f \repeat unfold 3 { r8 <es' g'>8 } | …
```

**Earlier decisions.** The theme parts, c144's trombone f, the counterline (Fl, Ob, Vln I·II ff), the bass and every note are unchanged. The protected items are "graded brass at 131", "tutti layout 131–134" and "notes of 131–134". This change extends the grading to two more accompanying parts and changes no layout or note. The violas' crescendo from 130 now leads to f. The hammered-tutti layout that the finale quotes is 27–32, which is not touched.

### Considered and kept

- **Tbn 1·2 f on the theme at 131.** PDF 159 rule 1 alone would raise them to ff. c144's reason, from PDF 147, is that the theme should be a blend of reeds, cellos and trombones and not a trombone solo. That reason still holds once Finding 2 lowers the competing parts, so I keep the f.
- **Ob 2 = Cl 1 = Hn 1 and Cl 2 = Hn 2 pad, 111–114.** The pad is triple-doubled at pitch under an undoubled Vln I. PDF 165 warns that at-pitch doubling loses colour, but this is a blended mp pad under a crescendo. The tune gains octaves step by step: Tpt 1 an octave below at 113, then flutes, Ob 1 and Tpt 1 at 115. That is Wagner's "upper octaves gradually double the violins" (PDF 172). I keep it.
- **Violas alone on the third octave of the head at 82.** They are inside the held brass chord and will barely be heard. They match the protected viola third octave at 27–32 (protected since cycle 14), and the doubling does no harm, so I keep it.
- **Trumpets low at 122; Tpt 1 A♭5 at 72; Vln I A♭6 at 31 and 72.** These are decided items (c244, c198, c194). Nothing in PDF 159–186 gives a new reason to reopen them. At 122 the trumpets close a complete brass chord under a woodwind and string stack that fills the upper octaves (PDF 164).
