# Critic 094, lens 4 (orchestration): how each instrument is used across the movement (v113)

## What I studied
- **Survey of v113.** I built a MIDI file with one track per player (scratch file `/tmp/critic-094/perinst.ly`; the shared staves no longer merge the two players). For every bar and player I counted:
  - whether the player sounds;
  - whether it has its own line, meaning no other player shares 60% of its onsets and pitch classes;
  - whether it is on the top line;
  - "exposed" bars: its own line with at most six other players sounding.
- **Corpus.** I ran the same script on `musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`. I read single bars with music21 (Dvořák 9/i 179–206; Tchaikovsky 6/i 125–128, 195–240 and 320–354; Beethoven 9/i 373–376).
- **Earlier survey.** Cycle 54's per-player survey of v73 (c054.md).
- **Web sources:**
  - Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm. On the bass clarinet: "darker colour in the low register", and special instruments "often used … as solo instruments, clearly exposed". On the trumpet: "in piano phrases the high notes are full and silvery".
  - The bass-clarinet tradition at Tchaikovsky 6/i bar 160, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._6_(Tchaikovsky).

**Models:**
- Tchaikovsky 6/i (354 bars):
  - timpani in 32% of bars (v113: 27%), trumpets in 30% (v113: 26%);
  - exposed bassoon solos at 1–10 and 160;
  - soft trombone chant at 201–204, with trumpets holding p;
  - exposed timpani figure at 327–334;
  - no harp, cymbal or tam-tam in this movement.
- Dvořák 9/i: no cor anglais, bass clarinet, harp or percussion in the movement. The trumpets have a signal in the string rests at 184 (f) and 188 (p), and the first-theme head pp at 200–206.

**Verdicts:**
- **Cor anglais, harp, timpani, percussion, contrabassoon:** these are not underused.
  - The cor anglais is kept for motto statements, and that is a clear cyclic role.
  - The harp belongs to the second theme (cycle 24).
  - The timpani have four exposed soft moments, which is more than Dvořák and the same kind of use as Tchaikovsky's coda.
  - There are already more percussion strokes than either model has.
  - The contrabassoon is a bass doubler, as in Brahms.
- **Bass clarinet:** genuinely underused (no line of its own anywhere).
- **Trumpets:** never heard soft on their own.
- **Overused on the melody:** Fl 1 and Vln I, together with Cl 1, which carries almost every counterline.

## Findings

### 1. Bass clarinet: no line of its own in 182 bars. Give it the first-theme head in the bass at 107–109 (applied)
**Problem.**
- The B.cl plays 45 bars and doubles in all of them.
- Cycle 54 noted this and deferred it because no unprotected place suited it.
- At 107–109 the first-theme head in the bass is played by Bsn 2 and Vc in unison: `r8 b,8( g4.) fis8 f8 e8 …`. The second-theme head sits above it in the violas and Fl 1 (low). The violas and cellos are both strings, so the two heads have similar colours and the cello line merges with the viola tune. The B.cl has just played 103–106 and rests at 107–110.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov: special instruments are used "as solo instruments, clearly exposed", and the bass clarinet's low register has a "darker colour".
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 160: the most exposed low-reed moment in the model is a bare low line in thin texture, and it is traditionally played on the bass clarinet (Wikipedia, above).
- When the second theme returns combined with another theme, as here, the two lines carry best in different families: the tune stays in strings and flute, and the bass head moves to a single reed.

**Protection.**
- Cycles 74 and 84 protect the notes and the f/p plan of 103–110. No pitch, octave, rhythm, dynamic or slur changes.
- Cycle 76 moved this line from Bsn 1 to Bsn 2 for endurance and noted that "the cellos double the line, so the colour does not change". This change is a deliberate change of colour.
- The cellos now rest 103–109 and re-enter at 110 into the crescendo. This is a fresh entry, as Vln I has at 111. The B.cl has rests at 106 beat 4 and 113 beat 4.
- Fallback if the composer wants a string colour in 107–109: keep Vc and give only Bsn 2's line to the B.cl.

**Fix** (`sections/dev.ily`):
- `devBassCl`: after `b,,2.\> r4\! |`, replace `R1*4 |` with
  `r8\p b,8(^\markup \italic "espr." g4.->) fis8( f8 e8 | e4) g8( fis8) b,2 | r8 f,8( des4.->) c8( ces8 bes,8 | c1\<) |`
- `devBsnTwo` 107–110: `R1\! | R1*2 | c,1\p\< |`
- `devVc` 107–110: `R1*3 | c2\p\< g2 |`

### 2. Bar 102 diminuendo, critic 93's proposal: tested, not recommended
**Test.** I built it in `/tmp/critic-094/test102`: a `\>` from beat 3 of 102 in Vln I/II, Vla, Vc, Cb, Hn 2, Hn 3·4 (equal on the shared staff), Tbn 1 and B.tbn, closed with `<>\!`. The build has 0 warnings and 17 pages, and it engraves correctly.

**Decision: keep the cut.**
1. The tune in 102 (`f''4 des'''2 c'''8 g''8`, transposed) is doubled by Fl 1, Ob 1 and the cor anglais. A hairpin only in strings and brass would give one line two dynamic shapes, which breaks the cycle-88 rule that doublings within a statement carry the same marks.
2. The decay already happens by subtraction. Cl 1·2 play the 97–102 accompaniment at **f** and continue at f at 103. The bassoons and B.cl, which also continue, are in 102 too. At the barline the ff layers stop and the f layer carries on.
3. The models cut ff tuttis into a new wind texture without hairpins:
   - Beethoven 9/i 374→375: tutti ff, then winds p;
   - Tchaikovsky 6/i 126→127: strings ff stop and the winds carry on.
4. The G♯ eighth is an upbeat into the protected join (cycle 72). A diminuendo would weaken it.

No change.

### 3. Trumpets are never heard soft and on their own (not applied)
**Problem.** Every trumpet bar is a tutti or a doubling. The p entries at 60–63 (with Cl 1) and 111–116 (an octave under Vln I) are doubled.

**Evidence.**
- Dvořák 9/i: the trumpet pair plays the first-theme head pp at 200–206, and the f/p signal at 184/188.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i 201–204: trumpets p under the trombone chant.
- Rimsky-Korsakov describes the trumpet in piano phrases as "full and silvery".

**Why not applied.** Every place that would suit this is protected: 41–46, 111–116 (cycle 73's graded entry and Vln I's re-entry), 117–130, and the coda. Recommend for movements II/III: one pp trumpet statement of the motto, alone.

### 4. Cl 1 is overused; Cl 2 and Ob 2 almost never lead (note)
**Problem.**
- Cl 1 plays 112 bars, the most of any wind. It carries:
  - the 77 motto entry;
  - the retransition head at 127–128;
  - the E♭–C–B at 175–177;
  - both long counterlines (52–68 and 147–166).
- Cl 2 has 4 own-line bars and Ob 2 has 2, and Ob 2's are its one lead (103–106).

All four of these Cl 1 passages are protected, so this is a note for later movements: give second players a lead, as Tchaikovsky gives the bassoon pair 6/i 1–10.

### 5. Flute 1 is on the top line in 74 of its 78 bars (open since cycle 44; no new proposal)
Fl 1's only solo is 73–76. This confirms cycle 44's open note. Do not add flute doublings anywhere.

### 6. Contrabassoon, harp, percussion, timpani, cor anglais (verdict: leave)
The survey shows these are used at or above the models' rate, and each has a consistent role:
- cor anglais: the motto, at 5–8, 147–155 and 171–174;
- harp: the second theme;
- percussion: the three structural strokes;
- timpani: the pedal swells and the soft coda pulse.

Adding lines to any of them would be decoration.
