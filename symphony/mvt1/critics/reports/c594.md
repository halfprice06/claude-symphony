## Findings

All four findings end in no change. Each "could it be better" candidate I tested either breaks a protection whose reason still holds or has no support in the reading.

### 1. Masking of solo lines (PDF 286–287): no change

**Reading.** "The fewer the instruments in a frequency range, the clearer and more present they become ... sounds in the same frequency range mask each other" (PDF 287).

**Scan.** For 20 exposed lines I counted every note of another part that sounds at the same time, within a fourth, not at the same pitch, and at a velocity no more than 6 below the solo. The lines were: C.a. 5–9, Hn 1 13–14, Bsn 1 33–35, Hn 1 and Vc 47–54, Fl 1 73–76, B.cl and Cl 1 77–78, Vc 85–87, B.cl 107–108, violas 107–110, Fl 1 109–110, Hn 1 125–126, Cl 1 127–130, Ob 1 145–146, C.a. 147–154, solo Vc and C.a. 171–174, Cl 1 175–177, Vln I 177–182.

**Result.** Twelve of the 20 lines have no masking note at all. The hits are these:
- **47–54:** Hn 1 and Vc against the harp's right hand. The harp arpeggios decay, and the tune is sustained. By PDF 290 the sustained line is the more present one.
- **127–130:** Cl 1 against the violas. This is the protected stretto. The violas answer the clarinet in canon.
- **147–154:** C.a. against violas and Hn 1. The fills come in the tune's long notes (c504 Finding 3).
- **171–174:** the C.a. against the solo cello (three notes). They are the two voices of the duet, lying within a fourth of each other there; the cello is mp in its tenor register and the C.a. p above it.
- **175–182:** Cl 1 and Vln I against Vln II and violas. These are the pp sustained inner parts under the last statements. The leading lines carry "espr.", and the inner parts are held notes.
- **125–126:** Hn 1's recall of the horn call has the most hits. Bsn 1, Hn 4, Vc, Cl 1 and Vln II all sound within a fourth of its E♭3 and C4. Its C4–B3 is at unison with Hn 3, Cl 2, the violas and the cellos. c581 designed it this way ("only the E♭3 pickup and the rising sixth are new"). c588 and c589 kept the marks and the unison for stated reasons: MOLA agreement of levels on a doubled line, the held C4 binding 124–126, and 13–14 staying the only undoubled horn melody. The reading would say the recall is an inner voice, not a signal. I considered raising Hn 1 to mf espr. That would make it louder than the line it doubles from 125.3, which is the disagreement c588 rejected. It would also make 125 compete with the Ob 1/Vln I E♭5–D5–C5 above it. Not proposed.

### 2. Harmonic-series layout of the climaxes and the last chord (PDF 259, 274): no change

**Reading.** Casanelles (after Fineberg) describes the orchestral chord as additive synthesis on the harmonic series. The first partials give the perfect intervals, then the thirds, then the seventh (PDF 274). Rimsky-Korsakov makes the same rule for chord layout ("the natural harmonic scale ... widely-spaced intervals lie in the lower part", rimsky-principles PDF 85).

**What the score does.** I listed the pitches sounding at each point from full.midi, with the number of instruments on each pitch.
- **82.2** (first A♭ climax): A♭1 (3), A♭2 (4), A♭3 (1), C4 (3), E♭4 (4), A♭4 (1), C5 (1), then the head's octaves E♭5 (5) and E♭6 (3). The held brass are partials 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 10 of A♭1.
- **160.2** (third climax): A♭1, A♭2, E♭3, A♭3, C4, E♭4, A♭4, C5 ... A♭6. This is partials 1 to 16 with nothing missing below C4.
- **182** (last chord): C2, G2, C3, G3, C4, E4, G4, C5. This is partials 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 16 of C2. Only partial 5 (E3) is left out, and leaving it out keeps the low register clear. The single E♮ in the violas sits at partial 10, where the series puts the third.
- **122** (second climax) departs from the series in the tenor. F3 (Tbn 2) lies below its natural place, partial 7 = F4. A♭3 (Bsn 1, Tbn 1, Vc) lies a semitone above Hn 4's G3. At fff in brass, that is the kind of dissonant density Casanelles places near the inharmonic end (PDF 266; Grisey's "harmony and timbre within a single entity", PDF 260). With the heavy-beater tam-tam this is the movement's one catastrophe chord, so the departure serves the form. It is also protected: "Hn 4 G3 under A♭3 at 122 (inner-pedal 9th)" (c302, c329), "122 tenor" (c404), and the cellos' A♭3–G3 at 122–123 (c444).

**Verdict.** The two major-key climaxes and the last chord are built the way the reading describes. The rough chord is the one meant to be rough. Nothing to change.

### 3. The flute's weak low register at loud levels (PDF 275, 277): no change

**Reading.** The low flute has a weak fundamental, and its pitch is heard lower (PDF 275, 277). Loud brass bring their upper partials forward (PDF 275, 289). A low flute line inside loud brass is therefore lost.

**Scan.** I listed every flute note below G5 at f or louder that sounds while a brass part plays at f or louder. Hits fall at 24–25, 31, 83, 96–102, 117–120, 131–134 and 141. Each one is a doubling of a line that also sounds in a stronger instrument:
- 96–102 S tune: Ob 1, C.a., Vln I·II, Vc.
- 117–120 P phrase: Ob 2 and Vln II (c424 already moved the flutes out of their weak register there).
- 131–134: the violins.
- The rest are short pickups into the high register.

No flute line depends on its low register being heard.

**Candidate tested and rejected: a low-flute colour.** c576 left open that "flutes never use low register". By the reading, the low flute's pitch blur and weak fundamental are a distinct dark colour, but only where nothing else sits in its band. The places where such a soft solo could stand already carry an established, protected colour in another wind:
- the motto returns 5–8 (C.a.) and 127–130 (Cl 1 chalumeau), protected as "woodwind colour of each motto return";
- 107–108 (B.cl, its only line of its own, c094);
- 145–146 (Ob 1 seed, c580);
- 175–177 (Cl 1, protected).

Putting the flute there would replace a colour, not add one. The only free bars are the A♭ pedal at 73–76, which is already the flute's own motto in its middle register. Moving that line down an octave would put the flute under the violas' C♭4 and Vln II's E♭4–G♭4–F♭4, in the band Casanelles warns about. Not proposed.

### 4. Graded marks where one colour must sound through another (PDF 271–272): no change

**Reading.** Mahler writes ppp for the trumpets and pp for the woodwinds because the mark gives the level that results, not the effort (PDF 271–272).

**Checked.** I looked at every place where a lead line and its backdrop carry the same mark: 175 (Cl 1 pp espr. over pp trombones) and 177 (Vln I pp espr. over pp Vln II and violas). In both, the lead is a single line in a clear band (scan 1: five notes within a fourth of each line over three bars, all from held or fading parts, none moving with it). It is marked "espr." and is the only moving part. Nothing in its band moves. The coda's levels and timing are protected (c328, c358, c369). The opposite gradings already in the score are the ones Mahler's example teaches:
- held brass f under the ff head at 82 (c304);
- the brass counter f after the ff stroke at 97–98 (c128);
- trombones, tuba and Tpt 3 ff under the fff tune at 160.
