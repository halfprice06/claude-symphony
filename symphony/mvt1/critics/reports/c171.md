## What I studied

**Books.** Main source: Aldwell & Schachter Unit 21, as above. Supporting: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, harp chapter (adler1 PDF 113: the orchestrator must make sure "the tempo will permit time for the changes"), and critic 136's report on the same pedal problem at 59–60.

**Corpus.** Mozart, Jupiter IV bars 9–13 (`musicxml-converted/xml/Mozart_Sym41_mvt4.musicxml`), the source of A&S Ex. 21-1, extracted with music21. Mozart keeps the resolution tone out of the whole orchestra until the winds resolve. In bar 10 the winds hold C over G and resolve to B on beat 3. Horns and trumpets sound G; the strings hold G and D. The B first appears in the bass scale on beat 4, after the resolution. Bar 12's 9–8 (D over C) has C only in the bass and in Horn II, which is the book's bass exception. Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 89–96 (`Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`): the second theme lies over a tonic pedal in the basses (A&S PDF 367, tonic pedal at a beginning). The theme's long notes are consonant, and its dissonances are short and resolve inside the beat.

**Web.** Harp Column forum, "string noise when using pedals" (https://harpcolumn.com/forums/topic/string-noise-when-using-pedals). The Hermes Experiment, "Writing for Harp" handout (https://www.thehermesexperiment.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/SONG-VCP-Writing-for-Harp-Handout.pdf). Both say that moving a pedal while its string is still ringing produces a buzz or an audible pitch slide. The slide is written deliberately only as a special effect.

**Score.** I read every section file and looked at all 17 music pages. I rebuilt full.midi in my scratch copy. Then I ran three scripts over every part. The first found held tones that become dissonant when the bass moves. The second listed every dissonance in the melodic line of each passage (CA, Vln I, Vc, Fl, Ob, Bsn, Hn) with its approach, length and resolution. The third flagged any voice that sounds or enters on a suspension's resolution pitch before the resolution.

**Overall verdict.** Unit 21 confirms the melodic craft of this movement. The T1 head contains a genuine 9–8 over the moving bass (bar 15) and a restruck anticipation (C before the barline). Those two figures carry the motto's rhythm through every statement. The cadential 4–3s sit where A&S place them, the pedals sit where A&S place them, and the exceptional usages (2̂→♭3̂ at 95, weak-beat cadential 4s at 66 and 164, transferred resolution at 125) all match cases the book documents. The scripts found one real fault, in bar 50.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 50: the harp sounds the resolution of the second theme's first 4–3 before the tune resolves, and the same G rings under the G♭ pedal change (applied)

**Problem.** At 50, Vc and Hn 1 sing A♭4 over E♭7 on beats 1–2 and resolve to G4 on beat 3. This is the "rising arpeggio + 4–3 suspension" that cycle 1 built into the head. The A♭ is an indirect suspension: Vln II holds A♭4 through 49 and hands it over (A&S PDF 357). The harp RH plays `es8( bes des' g' bes'2)` up an octave, so G5 sounds on beat 2½, half a beat before the tune reaches G. The suspension's tone of resolution is heard while the suspension still sounds, in the theme's very first dissonance. In the three other statements of the same 4–3 the resolution is clean: 54 (harp G after the resolution), 59 (harp on beats 3–4 only, since cycle 136) and 150 (no harp).

There is also a second problem in the same note. The "G♭" pedal mark sits on beat 4 of 50 (cycle 56), for the G♭4 at 51. The G5 string plucked at beat 2½ is still ringing at that moment, so the pedal slides it audibly to G♭ or makes it buzz. This is the fault cycle 136 fixed at 59–60, but bar 50 was not checked.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 352, §12 and Ex. 21-12: sounding the tone of resolution in another voice before the suspension resolves "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The allowed exceptions are the resolution tone in the bass, or an inner voice an octave lower in strongly contrasting texture. Here it comes in a voice above the suspension. A&S PDF 348, §4: a suspension brings its voice forward, and the resolution should sound like a relaxation. Jupiter IV bar 10 (corpus) keeps B out of every part until the winds resolve. Adler adler1 PDF 113 and the harp sources above cover the pedal.

**Fix (tTwo.ily, tTwoHarpR, bar 50, inside the existing `\transpose c c'`):** `es8( bes des' aes' bes'2) |` (was `g'`). The harp now doubles the suspension an octave above instead of anticipating its resolution. Its chord is E♭–B♭–D♭–A♭, a V7sus4, so the third arrives only in the tune on beat 3, which is how a 4–3 should sound. No G string is plucked in 47–50, so the G♭ change at the end of 50 is silent. Fingering and contour are unchanged: four rising eighths and a half note, with a span of a sixth. The pedal diagram and marks are untouched.

**On the protection.** "Harp notes 47–55" were protected by cycles 47, 56 and 136. Cycles 56 and 136 protected them after checking pedals and playability. This change fixes a pedal-on-ringing-string fault of exactly the kind cycle 136 removed at 59. Cycle 47 protected the texture of 47–55, and the texture is unchanged: same rhythm, register and figure, with one pitch class swapped for a chord tone already in the bar.

### 2. Bars 66–67 and 164–165: the cadential 4 falls on beat 3 and resolves on the downbeat (note only; keep)

The A♭5 at 66.3 resolves to G5 at 67.1, and the C6 at 164.3 resolves to B5 at 165.1. Under the basic rule (A&S PDF 347) the dissonance should be metrically stronger than its resolution, and here it is not. A&S PDF 362–363 (§24, Ex. 21-32) accept exactly this case when the weak beat is made rhythmically strong by a change of harmony and a long, high note. Both conditions hold: the bass moves to V on beat 3 (cycle 23) and the 4 is a half note at the top of the phrase. I record this so that a later harmony critic does not "correct" it. Moving the dissonance to the downbeat would undo cycle 23's six-beat dominant.

### 3. Bar 22: the first theme's cadence emphasizes ♭6–5 rather than a 4–3 leading tone (note only; protected)

A&S PDF 349–350 (§8) name the V4–3 as the standard way to strengthen a cadence through the leading tone. At 22, beat 1 has E♭5 over G (♭6), and B4 appears only as a passing eighth. That is the motto's E♭–D figure placed at the cadence, a deliberate cyclic choice. The notes of 15–22 are protected (cycles 11, 49, 91), and I do not propose a change. Every later cadence in the movement uses the 4–3 (32, 54, 67, 142, 155, 165, 174), so the leading tone is not missing from the movement's cadence vocabulary.

### 4. Bar 114: Tpt 1 reaches B4 half a beat before Hn 1/Cl 1/Ob 2 resolve C5→B4 (note only; acceptable)

At 114 the winds and Hn 1 hold C5 over D, a 7th, and resolve it to B4 on beat 3, after the 4–3 G→F on beat 2 in seven parts. These staggered double resolutions are good Bach-style writing (A&S Ex. 21-4). Tpt 1 doubles Vln I's eighth-note line an octave lower and touches B4 at 2½, against the held C5 at the same octave. A&S PDF 352 allows this when rhythm and timbre contrast strongly, and they do here: moving trumpet eighths against sustained half notes, mp crescendo. The wedge is protected (cycles 21, 92, 133). No change.

### Checked and confirmed (no change)

- **Pedal points.** The three pedals the book names as most important are where it puts them: a tonic pedal under the opening motto, a dominant pedal 117–130 before the reprise, and a tonic pedal at the end. Each starts and ends on the chord it prolongs (PDF 367–368).
- **Development suspensions.** Cycle 57's rule (suspensions in the development's non-thematic voices) is met. The 92–93 Vc 9–8 chain and the 95 viola 9–10 follow A&S PDF 353 and 358–359 exactly.
- **Anticipation as identity.** The restruck C before every head downbeat is A&S's soprano anticipation (Ex. 21-33). Tchaikovsky and Dvořák build their themes from fixed rhythmic cells in the same way. It should stay identical in every statement.
