# Critic 507: texture and counterpoint (v507)

Lens: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II, "Melody in different groups of instruments combined together", together with the end of the brass section before it (rimsky-principles PDF 74–80, book pages 56–62). This is the third pass; c307 and c407 read the same pages.

I read all seven text pages in full and rendered each one at 110 dpi (/tmp/critic-507/pg74-074.png … pg80-080.png). The pages hold no full music examples. The music is in the bracketed doubling formulas and the two trumpet/flute staves on PDF 75, and I read those from the images because the OCR garbles them. Examples Nos. 78–96 are printed in the second volume and are only cited here.

Method. I rebuilt v507's full.midi and wrote a whole-bar doubling scanner (/tmp/critic-507/scripts/dbl.py; output /tmp/critic-507/dbl.txt). It lists every pair of parts that play the same rhythm and contour in a bar, at the unison or at any octave. I checked each group against the formulas, then looked at score pages 3–8 and 12–17 at 80 dpi for the places the formulas do not cover: inner-voice motion, counter-melodies and the bass. I compared the list with c407's audit to find what has changed since v408. Since v408 the changes in my lens are c424 (Ob 1 / Ob 2 + Fl 2 at 117–121), c445 (cellos' tremolo at 122–123) and c503 (violas at 157). None of them sets up a new doubling the reading would object to.

## Findings

The doubling plan follows the chapter. The one breach of a rule the chapter states as a requirement, two winds over two horns at 158–160, was fixed in v408 and still holds. The remaining departures are the ones c307 and c407 listed and kept, and their reasons still stand. I propose no change. Findings 1–3 record what I checked beyond the earlier audits. Finding 4 answers the question of where, within this lens, the music still falls short of a master's work.

### 1. Changes since v408 checked against the reading (keep)

- **117–121 (c424).** Ob 1 holds F5–G5. Ob 2 and Fl 2 join Fl 1, Cl 1 and both violin sections on the P phrase, in two octaves (Fl 1·2 + Vln I at B4–F5; Cl 1 + Ob 2 + Vln II an octave lower). Every octave has a string group with winds. That is the "strings in octaves doubled by winds in octaves" layout of PDF 77–78, and no wind sits alone over a string. Cl 2 doubles the violas' inner figure at the unison (Violas + Cl, PDF 76). Follows.
- **122–123 (c445).** The cellos' tremolo on A♭3–G3 doubles nothing melodic. It is a chord tone, and the reading has nothing to say against it.
- **157 (c503).** The violas' arpeggio F3–A♭3–C4–F4 runs under the cellos' F2–C4 arpeggio. Both are accompaniment figures in contrary register, not a doubled line, and they leave Vln I·II alone in octaves on the tune. PDF 78 (rule 14) does not apply because no wind doubles the tune.

### 2. 113–114: violins alone an octave above Tpt 1 (keep)

**Reading.** PDF 75: "To double a trumpet in the upper octave three or four wind instruments are required, but in the top register two flutes will suffice."

**Score.** At 113–114 Vln I plays the S-head rhythm C6–B5 | C6–B5–F5–A♭5–C6, and Tpt 1 plays it an octave lower. Flutes and oboes join only at 115 (Fl 1·2 + Vln I over Ob 1 + Tpt 1, which is the rule's two-flute case).

**Why it stays.** The rule is about a trumpet melody that needs a matching upper octave. Here the violins lead and the trumpet adds colour underneath. It enters pp with cresc. (dev.ily, c186/c143), one level under the horns, and only reaches ff at 116. While the trumpet is that soft, a violin section balances it, and the flutes arrive at 115, exactly when the trumpet gets loud enough to need them. The wedge 111–116 is protected (c62, c92, c133). No change.

### 3. The fugato 85–96 measured against PDF 76's opening statement (keep)

PDF 76 says the strings-and-wind rules "apply equally to melody, harmony, counterpoint and polyphonic writing". The fugato is the movement's only polyphonic block. Each line is one string section with at most one wind at the unison (Vla + Bsn 1 at 88–90; Vln II + C.a. and Vla + Bsn 1 at 91–95; Vln I + Fl 1 at 94–96), and the cellos carry the free voice. By rule 9, the string predominates in every pair and the wind adds resonance. That is also the layout Adler describes in the coda of Mozart's *Jupiter* finale (adler2 PDF 176: "first violin doubled by flute at pitch … violas doubled by second oboe at pitch … cellos doubled by first bassoon at pitch"), together with his rule "to keep an established color intact long enough to carry out its assignment". The octave pairs at 91–95 (rule 16) were weighed by c407 and kept. No change.

### 4. Where the texture still falls short of a master's, and why I propose nothing (no change)

I looked for three things in turn: a line that could sing more, an inner voice a master would make move, and a counter-melody that is missing.

- **Static inner voices.** The longest stretches without inner motion are 47–55 (held divisi strings under harp), 147–155 (Vln II tremolo pad), 111–116 (held wind/brass chords over the viola ostinato) and 117–121 (dominant pedal). In each one the stillness is the point: 47–55 and 147–155 are the neutral settings of the second theme (c67, c247, c427, protected), 111–116 is a crescendo wedge whose motion is in the bass and top line, and 117–121 is a pedal. Brahms leaves the first statement of a lyrical second theme just as plain (Symphony No. 2/i, bar 82: cellos and violas in thirds over a plain accompaniment). Adding a moving inner part would cut against the "heavy piano" blend the chapter recommends for horn-and-string melody (PDF 79, rule 18).
- **Counter-melodies.** The movement has independent counter-lines at 19–22 (Vln II), 56–68 (Cl 1 + Hn 2), 97–102 (Hn + Tbn 1 + Bsn 1), 131–134 (Fl 1 + Vln I above the low head), 147–153 (C.a. answers), 158–161 (Ob 1 + Cl 1·2 over Hn 3·4) and 164–166 (Cl 1). Each is doubled with a pair the chapter names: Hn + Cl (PDF 75), wind over horn in octaves (PDF 75), Fl over Ob + Vn (PDF 78). I found no theme statement that lacks a counter where a counter would not also cover the theme.
- **The bass.** It is doubled by the chapter's register pairs throughout (Cb + Cbsn, Vc + Bsn, Vc + B.cl, B.tbn + Tuba; PDF 76–77, 79). The motto frames 1–4 and 175–179 use the "sweet soft" low layout of PDF 78–79.

The one place where I think a master might score differently is 23–25/135–137, where Fl 1 and Ob 1 both double Vln I at the unison. The reading says plainly that the strings will be "overpowered" (PDF 76). Moving Ob 1 down an octave would follow the PDF 78 formula more closely. But c407 kept this layout for a reason I cannot answer: the change of colour from string to woodwind is the second step of the protected graded build 15–27 (c287). The clarinets' thirds and the 3/4 grouping underneath are also protected (c391, c468). A third reading of the same page does not give a new answer to that reason, so I leave it.
