# Critic 022: v41, lens 2 (harmony and voice leading)

## What I studied
I built a MIDI with one staff per instrument variable (30 tracks, no part-combining) and ran `vl2.py`, `clash.py` and an unfiltered copy of `vl2.py` (fifth-run "doubling" filter switched off, as critic 12 did). I checked every hit in the passages changed since cycle 12 against a per-instrument note dump and a per-beat chord dump. Corpus: with music21 I extracted bass and chord content of Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`) and Dvořák 9/i (`.../Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`), and measured bass-pedal lengths across both movements. Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III, "Harmony in several parts" (https://gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm). It says that consecutive octaves between upper parts are not permissible, that consecutive fifths are harmless only when they come from doubling parts that move in sixths, and that "the bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts", which also applies to other seventh chords. The Open Music Theory page on augmented sixths returned 403.

**Tested.** All fixes below were applied together in `/tmp/critic-022/fix/v41/` and built with `tools/build.sh`: 0 warnings, and the outer-voice check still shows only the known bar-96 line. The all-voice re-run removes every fifth listed below and adds no new fifths. The only new octaves are unison or octave doublings of one line (146, 70).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 106–108: the wedge ends in 16 pairs of parallel fifths, ff tutti
**What is wrong.**
- At 107→108 the whole D♭ triad moves up a semitone to D7/C.
- Cl 1, Ob 2, Hn 1 and Vln II (A♭4→A4) move in fifths with Hn 3, Tpt 3 and Vla (D♭4→D4). These are inner voices at ff, under a top line that holds D.
- At 106 Tbn 2 drops to B2 on the downbeat. Ob 2, Cl 1, Hn 1 and Vln II hold the suspended C5 until beat 3, so the resolution note sounds under its own suspension. c002 and c012 fixed this same fault at 14, 28, 123–124.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov (above) accepts fifths only from parts doubled in sixths. This is planing of a root-position triad. In Tchaikovsky 6/i 101–108 the move to the dominant (C♯→B♭→B→E7→A) keeps the inner voices on common tones or chromatic steps against a moving bass. I first tried doubling the bass C in Hn 3/Tpt 3/Vla instead, but Rimsky's rule above forbids doubling the bass of a 4/2. The fix below keeps the 4/2 undoubled.

**Fix (dev bars 30 and 32).** The A♭ is held for two beats, which makes beats 1–2 a French sixth. It then rises to A, which breaks the fifths and continues the wedge's chromatic motion. The top line (Fl, Ob 1, Tpt 1, Vln I) does not change.
- `devTbnTwo` bar 30: `c2 b,2`; bar 32: `aes,2 a,2`
- `devClOne` bar 32: `aes'2\ff a'4 r4`
- `devOboeTwo`, `devHnOne` bar 32: `aes'2 a'2`
- `devVlnTwo` bar 32: `<fis' aes'>2:32 <fis' a'>2:32`

This touches the "wedge 103–108" do-not-touch item (cycle 21) only in inner parts. Cycle 21 protected the top line.

### 2. Bar 101: the second theme's 4–3 suspension (B→A♯) sounds against its resolution

- Hn 4 holds A♯3 for the whole bar.
- The cycle-17 viola arpeggio starts on A♯4, a minor 2nd under Vln II/CA B4.
- The clarinets play A♯4 on beat 2.

`clash.py` flags the bar. The exposition model (bar 50, A♭→G over E♭) keeps G out until it resolves.

- `devClOne`, `devClTwo`: `c' es' bes' es' c' es' a' es'`
- `devHnFour`: `c'2 a2` (C♯4 then A♯3, in unison with Hn 1·2)

### 3. Bars 148–149: the recapitulation lost the exposition's tonic pedal, and this produces fifths
**What is wrong.**
- c002 put the restatement at 56–57 over an A♭ pedal. At 148–149 the recapitulation instead moves the bass by root to F. This makes it the third root-position I–IV–ii–V in 11 bars (139–142, 143–146, 148–151).
- At the barline the melody's D5→C5 (Ob 1, Vln I/II) moves against the cello arpeggio's top G4 dropping to F2, which makes parallel fifths.
- Across both second-theme groups the bass runs I–IV–ii–V in one-bar root-position steps eight times (48–50, 52–54, 57–59, 65–67, 140–142, 144–146, 149–151, 159–161).
- By contrast, Tchaikovsky 6/i 89–100 keeps a D pedal for three and a half bars of each phrase and changes chord every half bar above it (D–B7♭9–D, D–A7/D, D–augmented). His bass moves only at the cadence (G♯ø7–A).

**Fix (recTwo bar 11), copying exposition 57.**
- `recTwoCb`: `c,1`
- `recTwoBsnTwo`: `c1`
- `recTwoArp` bar 2 (cellos): `c8( f a c' f'2)`
- `recTwoHarpL`: `c8( f a c' f'2)`

### 7. Harmonic rhythm and the bass line across the movement (judgement, no further fix)
- **Pedals.** The 14-bar dominant pedal 109–122 and the A♭ pedal 73–78 are in proportion. Tchaikovsky 6/i holds D for 19 bars (142–160) and F♯ for 41 (265–305). Dvořák 9/i holds pedals of 12–13 bars of 2/4 (137–148, 303–315).
- **Bass line.** The bass lines of the first theme (the chromatic rise 27–32), the transition (falling fifths 33–41), the fugato (85–96) and the wedge (chromatic fall 103–108) are strong.
- **Weak point.** The second-theme groups are the one weak area: a single diatonic predominant formula, always one chord per bar in root position (see finding 3). If the composer wants more than the fix in finding 3, the place to vary it is 159–161, for example over a G pedal from 160. I have not tested that.
