# Critic 049, lens 9 (holistic: Beethoven 9/i), on v68, compared with v58

## What I studied
I read all nine section files of v68, global.ily and score.ly, and score.pdf pages 2, 3 and 8. I diffed every v58 section file against v68.

From the corpus I used `musicxml-converted/xml/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`, with `ly/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.ly` as the cross-check. I wrote a bar-by-bar extractor, /tmp/critic-049/scripts/b9.py, and read bars 15–34, 218–219, 236, 301–302 and 539–547.

The movement's marking totals are: 4668 staccato, 1480 wedge marks (the converter writes them as "strong-accent", always on top of staccato), 945 sf and only 41 `>` accents. Its sf marks are concentrated in 90 bars, mostly where the tutti first theme comes back (21–34, 188–191, 401–421) and in the development fugato (218–244).

Web sources:
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration* (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm): the section on sforzando-piano chords. Percussion strokes should "correspond to the strong beats of the bar, highly-accented syncopated notes or disconnected sforzandi".
- Wikipedia, "Dynamics (music)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamics_(music)): `>` is an emphasis relative to the prevailing dynamic; sf/sfz is a forced accent.
- notat.io on Beethoven's dots and wedges (https://notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=6413): editions differ, and staccato marks in Beethoven can also act as a mild accent.
- Saskatoon Symphony programme note (https://saskatoonsymphony.org/breaking-down-beethoven-9/): the recapitulation returns ff in D major.

Gould and Adler were not reachable as full text.

## How Beethoven articulates, and what v68 lacks
Beethoven marks three things in 9/i, and he marks each one consistently.

1. **Short notes in figures get dots or wedges.** Examples:
   - the 16ths at 19;
   - the eighth-note arpeggio in every part at 20;
   - every 16th of the fugato subject at 218–219 and 236.
2. **Syncopated or off-beat weight gets sf, not `>`.** Examples:
   - 31–33, where an sf falls on every second quarter against the bar (a hemiola);
   - the fugato countersubject at 218–236, with sf on the tied eighth;
   - 544, with sf on every downbeat of the last tutti.
3. **Tutti punctuation is written short.** The chord is an eighth followed by rests (21–23, 27, 547). Trumpet and timpani rhythms carry no marks (27, 29, 541–542), because the rests already fix their length.

v68 has the third habit already: 33 and 135 are eighth chords followed by rests. It has none of the first two. Every kind of weight is a `>`: 253 on quarters, 106 on dotted quarters, 68 on dotted halves. Every short note is left to the player.

A conductor would not call this wrong. They would call it under-marked in exactly two places: where the rhythm must be crisp (the fugato), and where a figure runs through a tutti (bar 30). In both places a section will play legato or détaché by default, and the counterpoint will blur. A full articulation pass would be churn. A restrained pass at the places below is enough.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Fugato 85–96: subject eighths and rising quarters have no articulation (applied)
**Problem.** The subject (Vc 85, Vla and Bsn1 88, Vln II and CA 91, Fl1 and Vln I stretto 93–95, Cl1/Vln II/Vla 96) has these unslurred notes:
- the eighths after the dotted quarter (`a8 aes8 g8`, `d8`);
- the chromatic quarters `g4 a4 bes4 b4`.

They carry no mark. Each new entry starts while the previous voice is still moving in the same rhythm. If one section plays the notes broad and another short, the entries are not heard as the same subject.

**Evidence.**
- Beethoven 9/i 218–219 and 236: every voice that carries the fugato figure (Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb, Bsn, Fl, Cl) has dots or wedges on every detached note. The slurred notes stay slurred.
- Cycle 28 already made 85–96 a marcato place.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** In every entry, write `bes4.-> a8-. aes8-. g8-. | g4 fis8( g8) es4.-> d8-. | g4-. a4-. bes4-. b4-.`, and transpose the same pattern for each voice. In the stretto, write `des'''4.-> c'''8-. ces'''8-. bes''8-.` and `ges''4.-> f''8-.`. At 96, write `a8-.`.

No notes change. The cycle-27/37/47 protection of the fugato concerns its counterpoint, which is untouched.

### 2. Bar 30: the tutti eighth figure `bes c des bes` has no articulation (applied)
**Problem.** In the ff tutti, the only unslurred eighth group in the theme is marked nothing in six parts (Fl1, Ob1, Cl1, Vln I, Vln II, Vla). The slurred eighths around it are clear. This group will come out as a smear at ff in three octaves.

**Evidence.** Beethoven 9/i bar 20 (and 546) is the same situation: a four-eighth figure in an ff tutti. Every part carries a dot or a wedge.

**Fix (applied, tOne.ily).** `bes''8-. c'''-. des'''-. bes''-. g'''4.->`, and the same in the five other parts.

### 3. Bar 20: the syncopated peak of the first statement should be sf, not ff (applied; optional)
**Problem.** Strings alone at f reach a full ff on beat 3 of 20 and fall back to mf at 22. The phrase therefore uses up ff six bars before the real ff tutti at 26–27.

**Evidence.**
- Beethoven 9/i 31–33: syncopated weight inside an f passage is sf, and the level does not change.
- Rimsky-Korsakov likewise treats sf as an attack rather than a level.

**Fix (applied, tOne.ily, Vln I only).** `des'''2~\sf | des'''8\>( ...`. The crescendo from 19 now ends on the sf, and the hairpin to mf at 22 is kept.

This changes one marking inside the protected sentence 15–22. The notes and slurs are untouched. If the composer prefers the phrase to reach ff, reject this finding.

### 4. Tpt/Timp 27, 28, 30, 163 and Cb 15–22: no staccato needed (not applied)
c048 listed `c''4 r8 c''8-.` as a candidate. Beethoven leaves exactly these figures unmarked:
- trumpet and timpani dotted figures at 27, 29 and 541–542;
- repeated bass notes separated by rests.

The rests in v68 already fix the length, so a dot would add nothing.

### 5. Violas 69–71 and Cb 27–31 repeated eighths: leave unmarked (not applied)
Beethoven's repeated eighths under a sustained tune (24–26) are portato, meaning dots under a slur. At f and ff, the v68 eighths will be played détaché in any case. Adding portato or "marcato" would only change the colour. It would not make the rhythm clearer.

### 6. The 632 accents: keep the vocabulary, do not mass-replace (no change)
106 of the accents are the head's dotted quarter. Cycle 28 settled that articulation. Beethoven uses `>` only 41 times, but his first theme is a unison figure with no long syncopated note. The head's accented dotted quarter on beat 2 is this theme's identity.

Replacing `>` with sf across the score would make every statement louder than its context. Rimsky-Korsakov's sf-p device is for single colour effects, not for themes. A useful later step would be to leave the `>` off the dotted quarter in the doubling parts of the ff tuttis (27–31, 123–126, 163–165) and keep it in the line that carries the tune. I did not apply this, because it touches protected tutti scoring.

### 7. Tutti climaxes and motivic saturation compared with Beethoven (no change)
Beethoven's tuttis (17–20, 301–302, 539–547) are either unisons or octave doublings of the motive over a held chord with a timpani roll. v68's tuttis are built the same way:
- 27–31: the tune in three octaves over held brass, with the timpani rhythm in the trumpets;
- 114: fff over the G pedal with the tam-tam;
- 154–157: octave tune over ♭VI with the cymbal.

Saturation is also comparable. The motto, the T1 head and the second-theme head account for the accompaniment figures in the fugato, the wedge (103–108), the coda and the chorale. I found nothing that a conductor would take for a student's tutti.

### 8. v68 against v58: nothing got worse
Changes since v58:
- Fl1 left 19–22, and Ob1 left 58–61 and 139–153 (doubling reduced);
- the bar-20 figure replaced the sixth ladder at 39–40;
- harp 56–68 and 148–162 rewritten, with étouffez at 160;
- ff after the fff at 158;
- bowings, part-combine labels and dynamics on shared staves.

Each change is either neutral or an improvement. The harp at 148–162 is now in the violins' register, a''–f''', at f under an ff tune. That is acceptable for a harp, which colours the tune rather than carrying it. No notes regressed, and the parallels check is unchanged.

## Verdict
A conductor would accept the form, the harmony and the scoring as professional. The one tell was the articulation: the score had `>` and slurs and nothing else. With findings 1–3 applied, the score has 81 staccatos and one sf, placed where Beethoven marks his.
