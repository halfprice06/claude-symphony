### 2. The horn call's missing recapitulation statement: considered and rejected (no change)

This is the place where the movement falls furthest short of a master's cyclic design within this lens, and c570 left it open. In the exposition the horn call is heard twice before S: Hn 1 alone at 13–14 and four horns at 41–42. The recapitulation cuts the transition, so the call has no counterpart there; its only later statement is Cl 1 pp at 175–177. Of the ideas the later movements answer (III 3–6, IV 243–246), it is the least established.

The one free place for it is the link into the recapitulated S, 144–146, where the music is already soft (Ob 1 dolce, Hn 3 pp, Bsn 1 mf> pp). I tested the idea on paper and reject it:
- The link stands on V7 of C and goes to C major at 147 (Hn 3 holds B3 from 145.3 and resolves it to C4 at 147). A minor call (E♭–C–B) would put E♭ against the major arrival one bar later. A major call (E–C–B→C) in horn, resolving on C major, is the finale's own statement at IV 243–246 (Hn 2 at movement I's pitch). Sebesky's instrumentation rule is that an effect used earlier loses its surprise (PDF 11: "Had the strings been used earlier, the surprise effect ... would have been completely negated"). Spending the major call here would weaken the finale's arrival.
- Sebesky's focus rule (PDF 13) is met at 145–146 as it stands: Ob 1 dolce G4–E5–D5 is the only line above pp chords, and it already announces the C-major S head's rising sixth (see finding 3). A horn call beside it would make two primary elements in a two-bar link.
- The 145 soft horn chords are protected (c567), and the stated reason (the graded horn entries under Bsn 1) still holds.

The other places are protected too: the development scoring 77–130 and the climb 156–157 (c544, c563, c573, c579). The call's weakness is real but no addition meets the bar without costing a reserve. Applied in scratch copy: no.

### 3. Harmonic variety across the cyclic material holds (keep)

Ex. 3 and 3A (PDF 19, read from the image) bring one melody to the same resting note, a C under a fermata, over two different chord successions. Movement I applies the rule to every idea the later movements take up:
- The motto tail's D–D♭–C: bare octaves (3–4), N6 → C major (7–8), B♭ minor over G → i6/4 (120–121), a neighbour D♭ chord over the moving bass then C major with the violas' E♮ (178–182).
- The stop chord B–D–F–A♭: B bass (26), D bass (138), over a C pedal (170). The finale gives it a fourth harmonisation, D bass resolving to C major with the trumpets' B→C (IV 222–223), so the chain of Ex. 3-type variants completes only in the finale.
- The S head: A♭ major over harp (47), B minor ff (97), C major soft (147), C minor in one solo cello (171).

One soft foreshadow is not in the seed list: Ob 1 at 145.3–146 plays G4–E5–D5, p dolce, over V7 of C. It is the rising major sixth and step of the C-major S head (147) and of the finale's chorale top line (G–E–D, IV 224), in one soft reed, not in full brass at forte, so it does not touch the reserve. It should be recorded so the finale thread knows it is there (finding 5).

Fix: none.

### 4. Economy at the last chord (keep)

Sebesky's test (PDF 12) is whether each note makes a difference. At 182 the harp's C2–G2–C3 ppp is the only new attack in the last chord besides the violas' E♭4→E4. It marks the end of movement I as a close, while five parts carry the chord into movement II; the harp, double basses, Hn 4, timpani and tam-tam release with "étouffez" and "damp" at the end of the bar, so II begins without them (mvt2.md bar 1). Removing it would leave the attacca with no audible point of ending. Ex. 2A (PDF 18) also ends on a held chord under a fermata with a new colour (the flutes) entering only in that bar. Keep.
