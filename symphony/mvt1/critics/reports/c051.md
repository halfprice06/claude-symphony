# Critic 051 (lens 1: melody and memorability, secondary material), version v70

## What I studied
I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY, c021 and c041 (the earlier passes over the secondary material), the v70 critique, and the files trans.ily, tTwo.ily, clos.ily, dev.ily, recTwo.ily, coda.ily and the viola line in tOne.ily. I looked at pages 5–6 of the PDF. I wrote a script (/tmp/critic-051/s/coll.py) that reads v70/all.midi, which has one track per instrument. For every attack in a counterline it reports where that note sounds in unison or at the octave with Vln I or Vln II. I extracted these corpus passages with music21 from `musicxml-converted/xml/`:
- `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`: flute closing theme 149–156; violin restatement and its accompaniment 157–170; closing tutti 171–178.
- `Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`: second-theme counter-voices 89–100 (horns, violas); recapitulation counters 305–316; closing clarinet phrase 153–160.
- `Beethoven_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`: Vln I closing theme 150–160.

Web sources:
- Species-counterpoint summaries on unisons. In first species a unison is allowed only at the start or end. In second and third species it may fall on the weak part of the bar but never on the downbeat. https://rothfarb.faculty.music.ucsb.edu/courses/103/Second_Species(2v).html, https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Music/Music_Theory/Open_Music_Theory_2e_(Gotham_et_al.)/02%3A_Counterpoint_and_Galant_Schemas/2.04%3A_Third-Species_Counterpoint
- Programme notes on Dvořák 9/i. They agree that the G-major flute tune is the closing theme and that the development opens with its head in horn, piccolo and trumpet. https://www.eclassical.com/classics-explained-dvorak-symphony-no-9-from-the-new-world.html, https://arts.ucdavis.edu/post/dvorak-symphony-no-9-new-world

## Overall judgment
Almost all the secondary material this cycle names is already on a protected list. I checked each piece against the three questions: is the shape recognisable, does it relate to the main material, does it lead somewhere? On those grounds the protections hold. The one fault I found outside them is in the counterline of the second theme's second statement and its recapitulation. That fault is fixed and tested.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 57, 65, 66 (and 159, 160): the second-theme counterline lands on the tune's own notes on strong beats (applied)
**Problem.** The counterline (Cl 1 + Hn 2 at 56–68; Cl 1 at 158–161) sits in the same register as Vln II. Vln II doubles the tune in unison at 56–59 and an octave lower from 60. The script finds five strong-beat attacks where the counter's note is the tune's note:
- 57 beat 1: the counter rises e'–f'–g' into a♭4. Vln I and Vln II play the same a♭4. On beat 3 the counter's f'4 is the octave of the tune's f''.
- 65 beat 1: f'4, in unison with Vln II.
- 66 beat 3: a♭'4, in unison with Vln II. This is also the suspended 4th over the beat-3 dominant (cycle 23), so the suspension is doubled.
- 159 beat 1: a'4, in unison with Vln II.
- 160 beat 3: c''5, in unison with Vln II, again on the doubled 4–3 suspension.

At each of these points the counter loses its own line and becomes a doubling of the tune's skeleton. The worst case is 57, where it arrives in unison with both violin sections on the downbeat. vl2.py also lists the counter in octaves with the cello arpeggio at 65 and 159.

**Evidence.**
- *Species counterpoint* (sources above): a unison is not allowed on a downbeat except at the start or end.
- *Tchaikovsky 6/i 89–100* keeps the second theme in Vln I alone. The violas' three-note echoes (F♯3–G–A, 93 and 97) and the held horns lie below it, so no counter-voice shares the tune's register. In the recapitulation (305–316) the violas' chromatic eighths and the horns' rising line (313–316) run against the tune and do not meet it on beats.
- *Dvořák 9/i 157–166* doubles its closing theme in Vln I and II octaves and gives the winds only after-beats. He does not put a counter in the register of the lower octave.

The recapitulation's own opening counter (148–151, c''–b'–b♭'–a', cycle 27) never collides. The fix makes the rest of the line behave the same way.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-051/work).**
- tTwo.ily, `tTwoClOne` and `tTwoHnTwo` (identical changes):
  - 57: `f'2 aes'4 bes'4) |` (was `aes'2) f'2 |`). Over D♭ this is 3rd, 5th, added 6th. It moves in contrary motion against the tune's a♭'–f'' leap and leaves bar 58 on f' with no fifths. A first try, `f'2 aes'2`, made fifths A♭/D♭→F/B♭ with the cello into 58, so I rejected it.
  - 65: `des'2) aes'2 |` (was `f'2) aes'2 |`). The run c'–d'–e♭' now turns down to d♭', a third below Vln II's f'.
  - 66: `des'4\> f'8( g'8 bes'2~ |` (was `... aes'4 bes'4~`). On the dominant the counter takes the 5th, b♭', instead of doubling the suspended a♭'.
- recTwo.ily, `recTwoCounter` (Cl 1 only):
  - 159: `f'2) c''2 |` (was `a'2) c''2 |`).
  - 160: `f'4 a'8( b'8) d''2~ |` (was `... c''4 d''4 |`). This is the same correction transposed: the counter takes the 5th of G, d'', instead of the suspended c''.

No other part doubles these lines. Slurs stay balanced and dynamics are unchanged. The ranges are Cl 1 c'–d'' and Hn 2 up to b♭'. Harmony checked beat by beat against Vla, Vc, Cb and harp.

**Results (scratch build).**
- After the fix, the script finds no unison or octave between counter and tune in 56–68 or 156–162.
- vl2.py: the cycle-12 inner octaves at 65 and 159 are gone. It reports no new parallels.
- clash.py: no new minor-2nd clashes.
- The protected parts of these bars are unchanged: the 4–3 suspension over the beat-3 dominant at 66 and 160, and the maj7 at 60 that cycle 42 kept.

### Considered and not recommended
- **Planting the recapitulation's chromatic counter (148–151) at 56–59.** Its descent from the motto would make the recapitulation a recognition, not a new idea. Transposed to A♭, though, its g♭' at 58 clashes as a minor 2nd with the violas' f'. Tchaikovsky 6/i also varies its counters in the recapitulation (305–316 against 89–100). I left it.

## Verdicts on the rest of the material named for this cycle (no changes)
- **Codetta 69–72.** The top line is the T2 head. It ends with a falling minor sixth, A♭6–C6 through E♭, which is the motto's interval inverted. The T1 head runs below it in Hn 1·2, Cl 1 and Vln II. This is Dvořák 9/i's plan at 171–175: the closing theme's head on top, the head in the cellos, then a hammered cadence. It also does what Beethoven 9/i 150–157 does, building the closing idea from first-theme material. Keep.
- **Flute motto 73–76.** It descends over a pedal into a dissolving pad, as Tchaikovsky 6/i 153–160 liquidates its closing phrase downward. It leads directly into the bassoon's motto at 77. Keep.
- **Transition 33–46.** It imitates the T1 head (33–38), uses the bar-20 figure at 39–40, then the held g''' and the Hn 1 E♭–C sixth that prepares T2. Each element is derived and leads on. This confirms c021 and c041.
- **Development 77–84, 97–108, 115–122.** These lines are the motto dialogue, the T2 head in B minor with the T1 tail, the wedge with the bar-106 continuation cell, the head-rhythm sigh at 116–118, and the Cl 1 head and motto stretto at 119–122. Each has a fixed rhythm and contour, and each comes from main material.
- **Viola counterline 19–22.** It uses T2's head rhythm (`2.~ 8 8`) before T2 is heard, which is a real planting. No collisions with Vln I.
- **Counter 154–157.** g'–c'' then e♭'–c'' gives the rising sixth in the inner voice. The script finds the only octave contact on the tutti downbeat at 154. Keep.
- **Coda 173–178.** The violins' E♭–D–C–D♭–C closes the motto and the viola turns E♭ to E♮. Keep.
