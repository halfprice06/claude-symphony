## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a change. The findings are ranked by how much each would matter if it were wrong. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. The changes since v383 all agree with the reading. Keep.

Seventeen cycles changed notes after c383. I checked each change that touches harmony or cadence against Units 6–8:

- **44.3, violas take B♭3 with Hn 1's 7–6 (c371).** This is the V4/2 of A♭, and the seventh still passes into the bass and resolves to I6 at 45 (Ex. 8-8, PDF 126).
- **24.3–25.1 and 136.3–137.1, Cl 1 and Vln II E♭4 (c422).** The seventh F4 now steps down over the bass B–C (6 §9, PDF 98), where G4 used to sit and the F was left hanging.
- **135–137, Hn 4 G3 (c393).** This turns a bare B–D–F into V6/5, the neighbour chord with bass 8̂–7̂–8̂ (Ex. 7-9, PDF 111).
- **137.4, bass D (c402).** The bass D carries ii°7 into the vii°7 stop chord at 138. The pitch content changes (C→B), so the chord is new on the downbeat even though the bass repeats (6 §16, PDF 102). Protected.
- **154→155, Vln II B4→C5 (c402).** The V7 at 154.3 is now complete (D–F–G–B) and resolves to a tonic of C–E only at 155.1. This is A&S's Ex. 6-15a, a complete V7 going to an incomplete I (PDF 100).
- **159→160, Tbn 1 B3→C4 into ♭VI (c374).** The leading tone rises inside the deceptive cadence, as 7̂ must when it is exposed (6 §12, PDF 100).
- **61.4, violins F♭5→D♭5; 83, cellos G2; 162, bassoons.** These are register and inner-voice changes and do not affect any cadence.

### 2. Barline scan: no unintended weak-to-strong repetition. Keep.

A&S: "Avoid repeating a chord from a weak to a strong beat," while repetition from a strong beat through a weak beat to the next strong beat is "also possible" (6 §16, PDF 102). They add that rhythm-meter conflict is "a very important compositional resource" and that grouping can justify it (Ex. 6-21, PDF 103; Ex. 7-18c, PDF 116).

ws.py checked every barline from 15 to 182 for a chord that arrives on beat 3 or 4 and is held into the next downbeat. It found nine cases, and none is a fault:

- **21.3→22.1, 22.3→23.1, 136.3→137.1 and 164.3→165.1** arrive on beat 3, the half-bar, so they are the strong–(weak)–strong repetition A&S allow. 164.3→165.1 is the cadential 6/4 moving to V5/3.
- **23.4→24.1, 24.3→25.1 and 135.4→136.1** are the P hemiola: chords change at 23.4, 24.3 and 25.2, every three beats. This is deliberate metric conflict, and the horn entries mark its third cell. The hemiola is on the earliest do-not-touch lists ("hemiola 23–26"; held chords under it at 23–26 and 135–138).
- **78.4→79.1 and 85.4→86.1** are single-line passages: the E-minor head answer and the fugato subject alone.

No change of harmony at a phrase or section seam falls weak to strong. c383 checked the 19 seams; my scan covers every bar.

### 3. Cadence order in the two second groups. Keep.

Ex. 6-3 (PDF 94–95) ranks cadences: a PAC with 1̂ on top is most final, and 3̂ on top is less final. Both second groups use this order to keep the paragraph open until its last cadence:

- **Exposition:** 55.1 (C on top, 3̂ of A♭, imperfect), deceptive cadences at 64 and 68, then the PAC at 72 with A♭6 (1̂) on top, ff, the chord held a whole bar.
- **Recapitulation:** 155.1 (E5, 3̂, imperfect, a thin C–E chord at p), the deceptive ♭VI at 160 and vi at 162, then the PAC at 166 with C6 (1̂).
- **Recapitulation first group:** its PAC at 143 also has 1̂ on top (C6, ff), and V is then expanded for three bars (144–146) into the C-major S. This is the semicadence-then-expanded-V function of Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95).

The recapitulation reaches its climax three bars after the antecedent's cadence (155→158); the exposition takes nine (55→64). I checked whether this rushes the climax. In A&S's terms, 156–157 (♭VII–iv) is the pre-dominant of the cadential 6/4 at 158, and 158–166 is one expanded cadence (6/4–V7–♭VI–vii°–V7–vi–IV–ii–V7–I). That is the quicker chord succession at a point of arrival that Ex. 6-4 contrasts with the expanded spans (6 §4, PDF 95). Because 155 is imperfect, the motion is not closed there and does not need eight more bars to restart. The exposition's continuation 56–63 is withheld for movement III (cycle 80), and 155–158 is protected. Keep.

### 4. Cadences as rhythmic events. Keep.

"Many cadences ... show a broadening of time values; in most the final chord falls on a strong beat" (6 §3, PDF 94). Each structural cadence of the movement broadens:

- **22.3:** after chord changes every two beats in 20–22, i is held for six beats.
- **55.1:** A♭ is held for the whole bar after the half-bar changes of 53–54.
- **72.1:** after the I–I6 | ii7–vii°7/V | V7 crotchet changes of 69–71, A♭ is held for the whole bar, ff.
- **143.1:** after the quicker iv–ii°–V of 141–142, C minor is held through the bar.
- **166.1:** C major arrives under the rit. of 165–166.

All of them fall on a downbeat (22.3 on the half-bar). Nothing to change.

### 5. Retransition 129–130 has no leading tone. Keep, with the reason restated.

A&S 6 §7 (PDF 97): "In any progression where V goes to I ... 7̂ must be raised", and Ex. 6-9a is marked "wrong". At 129–130 the G pedal carries the motto's E♭–D–D♭–C stretto and F at 130.3, with no B♮. That is the letter of the rule broken.

The protection (c233, c263) stands on what the dump shows. B♮ sounds at 122–124 (Hn 3), 126 (G–B–D) and 127.1–128.1 (E♭–G–B). The dominant is therefore established as major across the pedal, and the last two bars colour it with the motto's ♭6 and ♭9 as passing tones over a pedal (8 §12, PDF 129–130: "I" chords and neighbours inside an expanded V). Adding B♮ at 130.4 would put the leading tone against the motto's D♭ in the same register and would touch the protected 117–130. Keep.

### 6. Proportions against Beethoven 9/i: the one place a master would weigh it differently. Keep.

Beethoven 9/i has the exposition 1–159, development 160–300, recapitulation 301–426 and coda 427–547 (MCTL places the coda at onset 852 = bar 427). That gives development/exposition 0.89, recapitulation/exposition 0.79, and a coda of 22 % of the bars. This movement gives 0.85, 0.61 and 13.7 % of the time. The return of the tonic (131–182, 134 s) is lighter than Beethoven's and lighter than Mozart 41/i's full-length recapitulation (c383). This is the main way in which the movement's form is smaller than a master's.

I do not propose a change, for three reasons:
- In A&S's large-scale terms (Ex. 6-4, PDF 95) the balance is still sound. The expanded dominant (117–130) carries the movement's fff climax, and the closing tonic spans 52 bars with three cadences of rising finality (143, 166, and the contrapuntal close at 178–182 that leads attacca into movement II).
- What a longer recapitulation or coda would need is reserved elsewhere. The violins' C-major S statement is withheld for movement III. The C-major brass chorale, the ff C-major arrival and the major hammered tutti are kept for the finale (coherence/mvt1.md "Do not reuse").
- Any added bar would renumber 147–182, which movements II–IV quote by bar number (138, 170, 175–182, 179–180).

The protected reason (proportions, c013/c043/c243/c433) is not answered by the reading, so it stands.
