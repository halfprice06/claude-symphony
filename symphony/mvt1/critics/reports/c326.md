Method: this is a second pass after c236. c236 checked ranges, clefs, divisi labels, sul-string marks and bass bow lengths. This pass adds three systematic checks taken from full.midi and from a slur/tie event dump of full.ly (a LilyPond listener engraver, scratch only):
1. Every string double stop that is not inside a "div." passage, fitted to the strings it must be played on (lowest position that places each note on its own adjacent string, with the finger offsets). There are 146 string chord onsets.
2. Every leap of an octave or more with less than a semiquaver between the notes.
3. Every bow unit (a slur plus any ties after its last note) longer than 5 s, measured with the tempo map, plus every single note longer than 5.5 s.

## Findings

The string writing of v326 follows nearly everything this chapter teaches. c236 and the later bowing and range cycles (c286, c305, c316) removed what a first pass would catch. The fit check found one place where a non-divisi double stop forces a hand position that the passage does not need. Everything else below was checked and should stay as it is, with the reason given.

### F1. Violas 166: the E4/G4 → E♭4/G4 thirds are written as double stops; mark them div. (applied)

recTwo.ily, `recTwoSync`, bar 166: `<e' g'>2\mp <es' g'>2\<`. Both notes are below the A string (A4), so the viola has to play them on the G and D strings. E4 lies below D4, so it can only be stopped on the G string: E4 is +9 semitones on the G string and G4 is +5 on the D string. That means third position, third finger on E4 and E♭4 and first finger on G4, with the E–E♭ semitone slid under the held G. This happens at mp, in a rit., in the bar that carries the violas' E♮→E♭ thread (coherence/mvt1.md: "E♮→E♭ in the violas at 166"). It is the most exposed moment of that thread before the coda.

- Adler PDF 76: orchestral double stops are kept to the easiest ones, for secco chords or sonorous passages. Passages that are hard for one player are simple when played divisi.
- Adler PDF 88: viola divisi practice is the same as the violin's.
- Adler PDF 85: on the viola the stretch and the left-hand tension are greater in the higher positions.

This bar is neither secco nor a loud sonorous passage, so the reason for a double stop does not apply. Played div., the lower half takes E4 → E♭4 on the D string in first position (+2 → +1 semitones, one finger moving back a semitone), and the upper half holds G4 on the D string (+5) or the G string. The E♮ is not thinned: Cl 1 and Hn 1 sound the same E4 → E♭4 in this bar, so halving the violas loses no line, and the viola G4 is the only G4 in the chord. The violas need "unis." again at 167, where they take the P head with the hammered tutti.

### Checked and kept (no change)

1. **Violas 41–42 (`<es' g'>1:32` sf> to mf>) and 131–132 (off-beat E♭4/G4 eighths, f).** The same E♭4/G4 shape also needs the G string in second or third position. These bars should stay as they are. Both are tremolo or repeated-chord accompaniment at sf or f, which is the "especially sonorous passage" use that Adler PDF 76 allows. The hand stays in one position for the whole figure, and divisi would halve a chord tone in a forte tutti. Bar 166 differs because it is soft, exposed, and changes a note inside the double stop.
2. **Leaps (PDF 82–83).** None of the 32 octave-plus leaps is one of Adler's three problem types for a section in an exposed passage:
   - Vln I 19, A♭4–A♭5 slurred: an octave across one intervening string in one hand position. It is the P head, which the finale quotes.
   - Vln I 82.4.5→83, C6 to the B4/A♭5 stroke: an accented chord after a downward shift, with bowing that works if the G5 pickup is taken up-bow, as it is at 15.
   - The cello arpeggio leaps at 56–68 and 157–166 all fall at slur breaks onto a new bass note. c236 reached the same verdict.
   - Vc 174→175 B4→G2 is a change of player (solo, then tutti).
3. **Cello and bass G2 pickup at 1 and 175 (PDF 73–74).** The first note of the motto is the open G string, pp, followed by stopped notes. It should stay. Adler says the open G is unavoidable on the violin, and on the cello G2 can only be stopped high on the C string, which would spoil the sul G line that c251 set up. B.cl and Cb double it at pitch and below, so the open-string colour does not stand alone.
4. **Bow lengths (PDF 108).** Over 5 s, the only unmarked bow units are in violins, violas and cellos. They are at most 9.6 s (Vc 3–4, already marked by c305), and the rest are 6.0–8.8 s at pp (Vln I 177.4–178, sul D; Vla 171; Vc 175). Adler's change-bow rule is for the shorter bass bow, and every bass unit over 5.5 s is marked.
5. **Double stops in Vln I·II and Vc.** All of them fit first to third position with standard fingerings (sixths, thirds, the 2–1 tritone, the 1–3 sixth shape at Vc 26). Every one that needs the fourth position or higher is inside a div. passage: Vln I 167–170, Vln II 111–116, Vln II 147–155.
