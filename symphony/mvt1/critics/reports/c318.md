## Findings

### 1. Bars 122–123: the fff minor ninth resolves at full strength (applied)

What is wrong. At 122 the A♭6 (Vln I, Fl 1; A♭ also in Ob 1, Vln II, Hn 1, Tpt 1, Tbn 1, Vc, Bsn 1) sounds fff as a minor ninth over the G pedal. At 123 (sostenuto) every one of these parts falls to G, the resolution, and holds it a whole bar still at fff. The diminuendo starts only at 124. The resolution bar is therefore as loud as the dissonance, and the brass (Hn 1·2, Tpt 1–3) are cut off at fff at the end of 123.

Evidence. The reading says the accented dissonance is followed by a resolution that "should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity" (A&S PDF 348, sec. 3), and that for the performer "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer" (PDF 348, sec. 4). Over a pedal, the dissonance against the bass is the point of tension, and the bass tone should persist through the chords above it (PDF 367–368). Bar 122 is this movement's largest dissonance against a pedal; it is the one place among the 57 found where the resolution is not softer than the dissonance. Rimsky-Korsakov gives the orchestral form of the same thing: a diminuendo is made by taking the instruments out in the order brass, woodwind, strings (rimsky-principles PDF 130), which here means the brass should fade on the bar on which they stop, not stop at fff.

Fix (scratch copy, dev.ily). On the downbeat of 123 every upper part that sounds the resolution or the V chord above the pedal starts a diminuendo; the pedal (Cb, Cbsn, tuba, B.tbn, B.cl, Bsn 1·2, timpani) and the tam-tam keep their fff to 124 as now, so the G pedal stands out while the chord above it relaxes. The end of the collapse (p at 126), the notes, the tempo words and the fff at 122 are unchanged.
- Parts that stop after 123 get a one-bar hairpin closed before the barline: Fl 2 `\after 2.. \! d'''1\>`, Ob 2 `\after 2.. \! d''1\>`, Hn 1 `\after 2.. \! g'1\>`, Hn 2 `\after 2.. \! f'1\>`, Tpt 1 `\after 2.. \! g'1\>`, Tpt 2 `\after 2.. \! d'1\>`, Tpt 3 `\after 2.. \! b1\>` (rests unchanged as `R1*7` / `R1*6`).
- Fl 1 and Ob 1 (shared staves with the parts above): `\after 2.. \! g'''1\> | g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8)\!` and `\after 2.. \! g''1\> | g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8)`. Closing the 123 hairpin and restarting at 124 keeps the part-combiner's "1." solo at 124 (a hairpin running from a shared bar into the solo bar split the staff into two voices with extra rests; checked on page 12).
- Parts that continue: the hairpin moves from 124 to 123 and runs to the existing p at 126. ### 2. Bar 45: Cl 1's 7–6 suspension has no weight (applied)

What is wrong. At 44 Hn 1 holds C5 over the D♭ bass and resolves 7–6 to B♭4 with a diminuendo (`c''2\> bes'2`). At 45 Cl 1 restrikes that B♭4 over the C bass, a second 7–6 that resolves to A♭4 on beat 3 (c281 made it a true suspension by moving the cellos' A♭ to beat 3). The clarinet enters `p dolce` with nothing on the B♭, so the dissonance sounds like the start of a new phrase rather than the continuation of the horn's suspension.

Evidence. The pair is an indirect 7–6 series over a descending bass (D♭–C), the instrumental form the reading describes at A&S PDF 356–357 (Ex. 21-16) and PDF 359 (Ex. 21-22: "suspensions transferred from one voice to another … more characteristic of instrumental than of vocal style"). A restruck suspension behaves like a tied one (PDF 347), and the dissonance takes "something of an accent", the resolution less (PDF 348). The score marks the same kind of note with a tenuto elsewhere (c268: tenutos on the syncopated long notes at 48, 51, 57, 59, 62, 99, 102, 148, 158, 160).

Fix (scratch copy, trans.ily): `bes'2--(\p_\markup \italic "dolce" aes'4. g'8 | f'2\> es'4)\! r4`. One tenuto, no new dynamic; the diminuendo into the hand-off at 46 (c298) is unchanged. On the page the tenuto sits between the note and the p (page 5); it is legible.

### 3. Bar 142: accents on both the 4 and the 3 (not applied; kept with reason)

At 142 the cadential C–B over G is accented in every doubling (Fl, Ob, Cl, Hn 3, Tpt 3, Tbn 1·2, Vln I·II, down-bow). Read literally, PDF 348 asks for the resolution B to be softer than the C. I recommend keeping it. The two notes are hammer strokes, the same gesture as 32 (`c4-> b4->`), which the finale quotes at pitch in every part at its 199–204, and 142 is the recapitulation's rhyme of it; the reading's rule is about a singing suspension, not a struck cadence, and the reading itself notes that a strongly marked rhythmic accent can override the metric pattern (PDF 362–363, Ex. 21-32). c118, c268, c278 and c288 kept or extended these accents; this answers the point without changing it.

### 4. Bars 63 and 159: crescendos through a resolution (not applied; no change needed)

At 63 the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline's A♭4 is tied from 62 and resolves to G4 inside a crescendo (`aes'4\< g'8 f'8`); at 159 Ob 1/Cl 1/Hn 3·4 resolve C to B under a crescendo into the fff at 160. In both places the whole orchestra is crescendoing to the next phrase's arrival (mf at 64, fff at 160), and the suspension is in a secondary line under the tune. The reading's rule describes the local shape of a suspension, and a phrase-length crescendo over it is normal; a local diminuendo here would contradict the leading parts. No change.
