# Critic 001: v20, lens 1 (melody and memorability)

## What I studied
I read every section file of v20, `critique.md`, `analysis.txt`, the plan, and rendered PDF page 13 (bars 143–153) to check bar numbers. From the corpus I pulled melodic lines with music21 from the MusicXML: Tchaikovsky 6/i Violin I, bars 89–100 (second theme) and 130–153 (its return and climax), plus the MCTL form annotation for that movement (`annotations/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1/form/form_analysis.csv`). From Dvořák 9/i I pulled the flute at bars 149–156 and 171–176, the oboe at 91–100 and the cellos at 24–32. Web sources: The Listeners' Club on the Tristan Prelude (https://thelistenersclub.com/2020/06/08/wagners-tristan-und-isolde-prelude-and-liebestod/); the UT Austin Tristan motive guide (https://www.laits.utexas.edu/tristan/motive1-3.php); Reiman on the Tristan opening (https://muse.jhu.edu/article/516276/summary); Ambrosini, *Melody* ch. 4, on how a melody approaches its climax (https://he.kendallhunt.com/sites/default/files/uploadedFiles/Kendall_Hunt/Content/Higher_Education/Uploads/Ambrosini_2e_Ch4.pdf); Secrets of Songwriting on placing the high point and its duration (https://www.secretsofsongwriting.com/?p=2602). I could not reach a text of Schoenberg's *Fundamentals* online.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 1–8: the slow motto is close to the opening of *Tristan*
**Problem.** Bars 1–4 give the motto as an unharmonised pp line in cellos, basses and bass clarinet, Lento: upbeat G, a minor sixth up to a long E♭, then D, D♭, C. Bars 5–8 have a double-reed answer (cor anglais). Wagner's Prelude opens with "a solitary solo cello line which begins with an aspiring minor sixth ascent" and then falls "back chromatically" (A–F–E–D♯). An oboe answers it (Listeners' Club; Reiman). G–E♭–D–D♭ is Wagner's A–F–E–D♯ a tone lower. It also shares the upbeat, the long–short–long rhythm, the scoring and the dynamic. Any conductor will hear an allusion that the work does nowhere else. A memorable motto has to be the composer's own. The plan's motto was diatonic, G–E♭–D–C, with the chromaticism in an inner voice ("harmonised over a C-minor chord whose inner voice slides E♭–E♮–F").

**Fix.** Keep every pitch, but break the long–short–long chromatic order in the slow statements only. The Allegro diminution (`r8 g es4. d8 des c`) does not sound like Wagner at tempo, so leave it. With this change D♭ becomes a lower-neighbour Neapolitan inflection. The motto also takes on the second theme's head rhythm (`c'2.~ c'8 bes`), which binds the motto to T2.
- Bars 1–4, bass clarinet and cellos (basses an octave lower): `r2. g,4( | es2.~ es8 d8 | c2 des4 c4~ | c1) |`
- Bars 5–8, cor anglais: `r2. d'4( | bes'2.~ bes'8 a'8 | g'2 aes'4 g'4~ | g'1) |`
- Coda, violins I at 176–180: `es''1~ | es''2. d''4 | c''2 des''2 | c''1~ | c''1\fermata)`. Cellos at 171–175: `g,1( | es1 | d1 | c2 des2 | c1)`.
- Fallback if the composer keeps the chromatic order: harmonise bars 1–4 as the plan specified, with violas and horns 3·4 pp on `<es' g'>1 | <e' g'>1 | <f' aes'>1 | <es' g'>1`, so the line never sounds unaccompanied.

### 2. Bars 154–157 (recap T2 climax), echoed at 60–63: the loudest bar is not the highest, and every peak slides down chromatically
**Problem.** The violins and flute reach e''' at 154 (ff) and then fall e'''–e♭'''–d'''. At bar 156, the fff, cymbal and ♭VI moment the plan calls "the emotional high point of the movement", the line only reaches e♭''', a semitone below the note already heard, and falls e♭'''–d'''–c'''–b''. The exposition is built the same way: b♭''–a''–a♭''–g♭''–f♭''–e♭'' at 60–61, then c'''–c♭'''–b♭'' at 63 (the composer's open weakness 2). Tchaikovsky 6/i does the opposite. At bars 134 and 138 a scalar rush lands on A6, the highest note of the theme, at the dynamic peak, and it descends diatonically (A6–G6–F♯6, F♯6–E6–G6–F♯6–E6). Ambrosini and Secrets of Songwriting both describe the climax as one dominant high point, often held, at the moment of greatest emphasis.

**Fix.** Use the motto's minor sixth (C–A♭) to take the line to a♭''' at 156, which is the pitch where the T1 climaxes peak at bars 31, 114 and 132. Change `recTwoMelodyB` bars 155–157 (and flute 1, oboe 1 and trumpet 1, which are written out separately) from
`e'''4 es'''4 d'''2 | c'''4\fff es'''2~ es'''8 d'''8 | d'''2 c'''4 b''8 c'''8`
to
`e'''4 f'''4 g'''2 | c'''4\fff aes'''2~ aes'''8 g'''8 | f'''2 d'''4 b''8 c'''8 |`
Bar 155 lies over G and bar 157 over D–G, so f''' sits on iiø and d''' on V. Bar 158 (a'') follows by step. Trumpet 1, an octave lower, reaches concert a♭'', which is safe at fff. In the exposition, keep 60–61 as the one chromatic descent, but make the peak at bar 63 lift instead of sinking: `c'''4 des'''4 bes''2 |`. The d♭''' is the seventh of E♭7 on beat 2 and a chord tone of E°7 at beat 3. This keeps v15's minor sixth in the recap.

### 3. Bars 69–72 and the exposition as a whole: only two rhythmic ideas
**Problem.** The T1 cell (eighth rest, leap, accented dotted quarter, three eighths) leads at 15, 17, 19, 27, 29, 35–39, 69–71, 77–83, the fugato 85–96, 109–112, 125, 131, 135 and 163–165. A grep of the section files finds about 70 part-entries. Apart from bar 123 it never changes metric position or note values. The codetta (69–72), which is the exposition's last forte, uses it too. The composer calls this intentional (weakness 5). The point here is narrower. Dvořák 9/i also builds on a few cells, but its exposition has three distinct melodic rhythms: the arpeggio at 24 (dotted), the G minor theme at 91 (sixteenth-note dance) and the G major flute theme at 149 (long–short). At 171–176 it ends the exposition by restating the flute theme ff two octaves up (D6, G6–G6–E6–D6). Tchaikovsky 6/i also gives its second theme a ternary frame, A 89–100, B 101–129, A′ 130 (MCTL form CSV). Here T2 never sounds at forte in its own key, so the exposition does not leave its best tune in the ear.

**Fix.** Put the T2 head on top of the codetta and keep the T1 cell beneath it, so v16's "both themes combined" survives. Flute 1, oboe 1 and violins I, bars 69–72:
`c'''2.~\f c'''8 bes''8 | aes''4 f'''2 es'''4 | des'''4. c'''8 bes''4 c'''8 des'''8 | es'''4-> c'''4-> aes''2-> |`
Move the current violin line (`r8 es'' c'''4.-> …`) to violins II, clarinets and horns 1·2 an octave lower. The harmony (A♭–F, D♭–B♭, E♭, A♭) supports every note. Bar 70's e♭''' over B♭ minor is the same appoggiatura as bar 48.

### 4. Bars 25–26 and 129–130: the movement's highest note is spent in its first tutti
**Problem.** Flute 1 reaches b''' (B6) at 26, twelve bars into the Allegro. `analysis.txt` confirms B6 as the movement's ceiling, reached only here and at 130. The development climax (114) and the recap peaks reach only a♭''', so the register never grows past bar 26. In Tchaikovsky 6/i the second theme's ceiling (A6) is withheld until its return at 134.
**Fix.** Bar 26, flute 1: `b''2.->\ff r4` (doubling the violins). Keep b''' at 130 only, so the recapitulation exceeds the exposition.

### 5. Bars 49–50 and 58–59: the third and fourth bars of T2 are filler
**Problem.** `des'4. c'8 bes4 c'8 des'8 | es'1` is stepwise motion around b♭ ending on a bare whole note. It comes in the bars where Dvořák's flute theme (151–152) puts its dotted rising arpeggio, the theme's most distinctive cell. Bar 59 repeats it in the violins.
**Fix.** Bars 49–50 (cello and horn 1): `des'4.( c'8 bes4 des'8 f'8 | aes'2 g'2) |`. The B♭ minor arpeggio adds a second upward impulse, and the a♭' becomes a 4–3 suspension over E♭7. Apply the same change at 58–59, an octave up, and in the recap: `f''4. e''8 d''4 f''8 a''8 | c'''2 b''2` in violins, with the horn and viola versions an octave lower.

## Leave untouched
The T2 statement in cellos and horn is at a master's level in bars 47–48 and 51–55, specifically the sixth leap at 48, the octave leap to f'' at 52 and the descent to c' at 55. So is T1's opening bar as the motto in diminution at bar 15. Leave both alone.
