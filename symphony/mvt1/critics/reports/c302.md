## Findings

### 1. Bar 28, beat 3: Tpt 2's G4 turns the vii°4/3 into an inverted V♭9 with the resolution tone a semitone under the 9th (applied)

**What is wrong.** In the hammered tutti, bar 28 beat 3 has the tune's A♭ (Fl 1·2 A♭5, Ob 1 and Cl 1 A♭4, Vln I A♭5, Vln II A♭4, violas A♭3) over D in the bass, with B (Tpt 1 B4, Hn 3 and Tbn 1 B3), D and F. That is vii°4/3, and the tune's A♭ falls to G on the last eighth of the bar. Since c224, Tpt 2 strikes G4 on that beat (`g'4 r4 g'4 r4`). The chord then holds all five tones of V♭9 with D in the bass, and the root G4 sits a minor second under the A♭4 of Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II, accented, f, in the same octave.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 469–470 (§7, Ex. 27-10): a seeming inversion of V9 is an inverted V7 with a neighbour or suspension; "with inverted ninth chords the dissonance always appears at the same time as the tone of resolution, which is never in the bass. The result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution." Chopin's mazurka gets away with it only through "the unusually wide space between melody and accompaniment". PDF 465 makes the same distinction: a 9th resolves well to a tone "already present in a lower voice and register", that is, a ninth or more below, not a semitone below. Here the tune's A♭→G on the last eighth arrives at a pitch the trumpet has already struck. The score itself follows this rule elsewhere. At 28.1 and 132.3 the root G sounds before the leading tone does, so the sonority reads as iiø7 over V (A&S PDF 471, Ex. 27-14; c192). At 132, Tpt 1's G4 sounds on beat 3 before any B, then moves to B4 on beat 4. Cycle 139 took Hn 4's G3 off beat 3 of 28 for the same reason. Cycle 224's Tpt 2 G4 put the clash back without noting c139.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/tOne.ily, tOneTptTwo, bar 28: `g'4 r4 g'4 r4` → `g'4 r4 f'4 r4`. Beat 3 is now a clean vii°4/3 in the brass (Tpt 1 B4, Tpt 2 F4, Tpt 3 F4, Hn 3 B3, Tbn 1 B3, Tbn 2 F3, B.tbn D3), and the tune's A♭→G resolves onto a G that has not already sounded above the bass. Tpt 2's line becomes G4 (27–28.1) – F4 – A♭4 (29), all by step. Tpt 1 B4 over Tpt 2 F4 is the trumpet layout of the stop chords at 26 and 138, so c224's reason (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 90 rule 2: the trumpets hold upper chord tones in normal order) still holds.

**Earlier fixes and protections.** This undoes one note of c224 (Tpt 2 G4 at 28). It keeps c224's other changes (Tpt 2 B♭4 at 30; Tpt 3 F4 F4 G4 A♭4–F4; Hn 2 G4 at 30). The protections on 27–32 (c224: "bass, tune and rhythm"; c270: climax scoring; the finale quotes the bars at pitch) are answered: bass, tune, rhythm, instruments and dynamics are unchanged. Only one inner pitch on one beat changes, and the finale is told below.

### Kept, with reasons from the reading

- **122, the fff V♭9 with Hn 4 G3 under the trombones', bassoon's and cellos' A♭3.** Hn 4 (with Hn 3 on G4 at 117–121) holds an inner G pedal from 117 to 130, so the A♭ is a 9th above a pedal point (A&S PDF 471, §9), not a struck root under its own dissonance as at 28. The top A♭6 resolves 9–8 to G6 at 123 (PDF 465–466). Protected climax (c264, c284).
- **83, V♭9 struck twice ff and dropped (84 is bare G).** The 9th is in the top voice. Tbn 1's A♭3 is taken up at the same pitch by the fugato subject's A♭3–G3 at 85, which is the compensation A&S describe on PDF 467. The 7th F becomes the subtonic of G minor (F♯ at 86) when the dominant is reinterpreted as a key. Dynamics at 82 are protected (c244).
- **106.3, B–D♯–G with G5 on top.** This is an unresolved 6th that replaces the 5th, in the soprano, sounding as an anticipation of E minor's 3rd at 107 (PDF 474–475, Ex. 27-17). Hn 1's D♯5 is a protected seed.
- **150, V9 with the major 9th A5 (Vln II) moving to B♭5 at 151.** The 9th is a common tone held from ii at 149 (Ex. 27-4c), and the 7th F passes to the bass E at 151 (transfer, PDF 466). 147–150 are kept harmonised as 47–50 (c277).
- **159.1, E6 over V7 rising E–F–G.** The 6th is in the soprano above the 7th (PDF 475). Cl 2's D5 sounds with it for one beat, but the E is a rising passing line out of the cadential 6/4, protected since c101/c281 and quoted by the finale.
- **178.3, Vln I E♭5 over the Neapolitan D♭.** This is a held soprano note (Chopin Ex. 27-18 type) that becomes consonant when the bass returns to C. Protected motto neighbour chord (c232).
