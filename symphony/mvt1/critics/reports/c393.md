## Findings

### 1. Recapitulation 135–137: Horn 4 drops out, so the 3/4 grouping's B-bass chord becomes a bare VII5/3. Applied.
**Bars.** Exposition 23–25 and recapitulation 135–137 are the same passage: four slurred three-crotchet groups over a chromatic bass C–B–C–F, then the stop chord at 26 and 138. In the exposition, Hn 4 plays C3 | G3 (from 23.4) tied to 24.2 | G2–F2. That G3 gives the chord over the B1 bass its root, so 23.4–24.2 is V6/5 (B–D–F–G). The recapitulation copies everything at pitch except Horn 4, which rests 135–137 (`R1*3`). The MIDI reduction of v393 gives [B D F] plus the melody's E♭5 at 135.4–136.2. No instrument holds G there. Vln II, Cl 1 and Cl 2 all move G→F and E♭→D at 135.4, and Hn 1·2 enter only at 136.3. For three beats the movement sounds a root-position diminished triad on the leading tone, the one place in the score where it does.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 243: "Diminished triads on the leading tone are seldom satisfactory in 5/3 position … V6/5 (which contains all three tones of the diminished VII) creates a far more pleasing sonority and is to be preferred." Points for Review 9 on PDF 245: "Because of their poor sonority … raised VII5/3 in minor are to be avoided; a leading tone in the bass should usually support V6/5." The exceptions on PDF 243–244 are three-part writing (Ex. 15-17) and an inner voice that would otherwise move badly (Ex. 15-18, 15-19). Neither applies to a tutti chord that copies a passage where the composer already wrote V6/5. Critic 303's table (row 17) said 135–136 "carries V6/5 with G". That was wrong for v303 and is wrong for v393. From the form side this is an unplanned recapitulation change. Every planned change in 131–146 has a reason in HISTORY (low brass and timpani rest, c129/c143; Hn 1·2 enter at the third cell, c177). No entry gives a reason for losing Horn 4 here, and the loss weakens the dominant just before the stop chord.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-393/work).**
- recOne.ily, `recOneHnFour`: `g1\f | g2 r2 | f1 | g1 \breathe |` then `c2.\mp\<-> g4->~ | g2 g,2->~ | g,4 f,2-> r4 | f2.->\f r4 |` (was `g1 | R1*3 |`). These are the notes, rhythm, dynamic and breath of 22–25, including the rest on 137.4.
- `recOneHnThree`: `\clef bass` at 135 and `\clef treble` at 138 in place of `\partCombineSoloI` / `\partCombineAutomatic`, as at 23 and 26.
- parts-clefs.ily, `hnFourClefs`: `s1*109 \clef bass s1*3 \clef treble` (bass clef 135–137, as 23–25).
- parts-gen.py, `LAYX`: `"15"` spacing-increment 1.45. At 1.6 the Horn 4 part ran onto a third page, which would break c201's attacca plan (Horn 4 enters movement II at bar 1 beat 2).

Result: 135.4–136.2 is now [B D F G], as at 23.4–24.2. This does not reopen c129/c143, because those concern trombones, tuba and timpani. It does not undo c177 or c297 either (Hn 1·2 still enter at 136.3).

### 2. The key plan is the III–VI–II–V–I chain with III as a stopover. Keep.
A&S PDF 231–233 and 236–237 (Ex. 15-9). III is reached at 35–36 and again at 39–41 through natural VII as V of III (PDF 240, Ex. 15-1). It is then reinterpreted as V of VI at 43–46 (V4/2 – I6 – ii6/5 – V7 of A♭). The bass then runs E♭ – A♭ – (development) D – F – G – C. This is the chain on Handel's Ex. 15-9 at the scale of the whole movement. Critic 303 described this in full, and I found nothing to add. Keep.

### 3. Recapitulation 156–159 follows Ex. 15-15 in the soprano as well as in the horn. Keep.
PDF 242 says that the fifth of natural VII (4̂) "forms a good preparation for the 7th of V7 and frequently appears in the soprano voice". At 156 the top note over the B♭ bass is F6. At 157 the top line falls C6–B♭5–A♭5–G5 over iv. At 158 it reaches E6 on the cadential 6/4, and at 159 it returns to F6 as the seventh of V7. Critic 303 noted the inner-voice form (Hn 4 F3 → F4). The outer voice does the same thing, so the soprano has the reading's characteristic line. Ex. 15-15's stepwise bass would put A♭ at 157. I agree with c303 that it should not be used, because it would anticipate the fff ♭VI at 160 (157 bass protected, c363). Keep.

### 4. Development seams 79→80, 96→97, 102→103, 108→109: chromatic steps stay inside one voice. Keep.
The scan of v393 lists four places where a soprano pitch is followed in the next eighth by its chromatic neighbour in the bass. A&S PDF 236 condemns the soprano/bass form because the chromatic succession "takes place between two voices rather than in a single one". At 96→97 (B♭1→B1) and 108→109 (B1→B♭1) the bass makes the chromatic step itself. At 79→80 Vln II makes it (E4→E♭4, the E♭/E thread). So in those three places no voice is left holding the old pitch against the new one, and the soprano's B4 at 108.4 (violas) moves on to D♭5. 102→103 (F♯ → F, B minor to D minor) is the one true split. It falls at a key change across a section seam, where the abrupt juxtaposition is the point of the passage, and it is protected (c323). Keep all four.

### 5. 96→97: natural VII inflected upward becomes a new key, not the third of V. Keep.
PDF 241 (Ex. 15-14) shows ♭7̂→♮7̂ moving into V6/5 and I, an effect that is "unexpected and, in retrospect, logical". At 96→97 the B becomes the root of a ff B-minor statement of S. It reaches C only at 131, through the vii°7 pillars B (97) – D (103) – F (111) – A♭ (116/122). Across the movement this is the same inflection the reading describes. It is protected (c233, c292, c323). Keep.

### 6. Pacing of dominants: 144–146 and 117–130. Keep.
PDF 234: "The force of dominant harmony is so great that it does not always require special emphasis through duration or accent." After the perfect cadence at 143, the movement stays on V for three bars (144–146, poco rit., Bsn 1 answering the head) before the C-major S at 147. This is the recapitulation's counterpart of the six bars on V of A♭ at 41–46, and it is half as long, in line with a recapitulation first group cut by half (16 bars against 32). The 14-bar root-position pedal at 117–130 (29 s, 27% of the development's 105 s) is the one long dominant. It prepares the movement's main arrival, so the emphasis the reading says V "does not always require" is wanted here. Keep (c223, c313).

### 7. Proportions against the corpus. Keep.
Times from v393's full.midi: introduction 67 s, exposition 124 s, development 105 s, recapitulation 75 s, coda 59 s, about 430 s in all. These are the same as c303's figures. The MCTL form analysis of Tchaikovsky 6/i (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1/form/form_analysis.csv, offsets in crotchets) gives an 18-bar introduction (0–72) and a 20-bar coda (1334–1414). It places the return of P inside the development's climax, and its recapitulation proper is the second group only (1214–1334). This movement's half-length first-group recapitulation and short coda follow the same reasoning. Proportions are protected (c215, c243, c283). Keep.
