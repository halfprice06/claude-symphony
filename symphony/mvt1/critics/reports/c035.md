# Critic 035: lens 5, engraving and notation (v54)

## What I studied
I rendered all 17 pages of v54/score.pdf at 90 dpi and zoomed to 200 dpi at bars 26–29, 33, 40–43, 73, 94–114, 123–138 and 163–178. In the local corpus I compared how rests are grouped after a short accented chord and how dynamics are placed on tied notes. Counts across the hand-engraved Mutopia sources: Dvořák 7 has `r8 r4 r2` 17 times and `r8 r2.` 0 times; Dvořák 9 has 12 and 0; Tchaikovsky Op. 35 has 42 and 0. Specific examples: Dvořák 7/ii, `DvorakSYMPH7M2_musique-Corno_G_III_IV.ly` line 57, `c16-> \ff c-> r8 r4 r2`; Dvořák 9/iv, `brass.notesF.ily` line 801, `e8\fz r8 r4 r2`; Dvořák 9/iv, `strings.notesF.ily` line 360, `<g b,>1\> ~ <g b,>\ppp`, where a dynamic falls on a tied note only as the target of a hairpin. Dvořák 7 `Common/Properties.ly` lines 33–34 and 52 show how a hand-made LilyPond conductor score sets up its bar numbers. For the web I used the CPDL engraving guide, which cites Gould, *Behind Bars* pp. 160–164 on rests: https://forums1.cpdl.org/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=19653. It gives "n4 r2." as not acceptable in 4/4 and says dotted half rests in simple time are deprecated. I could not find Gould's own text on where bar numbers go in a full score, so I do not cite it for that point.

All the fixes below are applied in /tmp/critic-035/work. Changed files: sections/trans.ily, sections/recOne.ily, sections/dev.ily, sections/clos.ily and score.ly. The build gives lilypond rc=0 and 0 warnings. The page count is still 17 and the page break before K still holds. The parallels check shows only the known "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1". No pitches or durations changed. Only rests were regrouped.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 33 and 135 (also 97 and 127): dotted half rests hide beat 3 (applied)
**Problem.** The new accented eighth chords from cycle 34 are written `X8->\ff r8 r2.`. That puts a dotted half rest across beats 2–4, so the middle of the bar is not shown. This happens in every wind, brass, timpani and string part at 33 and 135. The same pattern appears in four other places:
- dev bar 97: `ces'8\ff bes8 r2.` in four parts;
- timpani at 97: `bes,4\ff r2.`;
- timpani at 127: `c4->\mf r2.`.

**Evidence.** Gould pp. 160–164, via the CPDL guide above. The corpus counts in the first section: none of the hand-engraved Romantic scores uses `r8 r2.`.

**Fix.** In trans.ily and recOne.ily, change ` r8 r2.` to ` r8 r4 r2`. In dev.ily, use `bes8 r4 r2` and `bes,4\ff r4 r2`. In recOne.ily, use `c4->\mf r4 r2`. The `r2. X4` pickups at the start of a bar are left alone; Gould allows those.

### 2. Bar numbers collide with high flute notes (open since cycle 25) (applied in score.ly)
**Problem.** At 126 the number sits on the tie and noteheads of the Fl 1/Fl 2 second. At 127 it touches the slur and the c'''. Numbers also sit tight on rests at 27, 29 and 109–112, and on the slur at 53 and 74. Padding cannot fix this, because the number at the top of the score is placed level with notes on high ledger lines. With `self-alignment-X = #CENTER` and horizontal padding 0.6 the numbers still touched at 126 and 127.

**Fix (tested).** Print the bar numbers under the double basses, centred on each barline, below the dynamics:
- In the Score context, add `\remove "Bar_number_engraver"`.
- In the Double basses `\with`, add `\consists "Bar_number_engraver" \override BarNumber.direction = #DOWN \override BarNumber.self-alignment-X = #CENTER \override BarNumber.break-align-symbols = #'(staff-bar) \override BarNumber.break-visibility = ##(#f #t #t) \override BarNumber.font-size = #-1 \override BarNumber.padding = #1 \override BarNumber.outside-staff-priority = #1000`.

**Result.** No collisions with notes are left. The numbers at 41, 43 and 177 sit beside a dynamic or the start of a hairpin, which is tight but legible.

**Caveat.** If the Cb staff is ever hidden by RemoveEmptyStaves in a system, that system loses its numbers. In this layout every system shows the Cb staff.

**Alternative.** If the composer wants to keep the numbers on top, use `barNumberVisibility = #(every-nth-bar-number-visible 5)`, or show them only at system starts as the Dvořák 7 sources do. Either way there are far fewer chances of a collision.

### 3. Bars 113–114: tuba fff a bar early, and fff on tied notes with no hairpin (applied)
**Problem.** The tuba has `g,,1\fff~` at 113, while the woodwinds, timpani and violins crescendo through 113 into fff at 114. The tuba is then tied into 114 and never attacks the climax. The bass clarinet, Bsn 2 and Cb have `\fff` on a note tied from 113, with no hairpin leading to it. A new dynamic on a held note means nothing unless a hairpin leads to it.

**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/iv `strings.notesF.ily` line 360 (see first section).

**Fix.** In dev.ily:
- B.cl at 113: `g,1\<~`.
- Bsn 1 at 113: `c'1\<`. Bsn 2 at 113: `g,1\<~`. Both voices get the hairpin, so the shared staff stays equal.
- Cb at 113: `g,,1\<~`.
- Tuba 112–114: `g,,1~ | g,,1\<~ | g,,2.\fff r4`.

**Not applied (expression lens).** Cl, Hn, Tbn, B.tbn, Vla and Vc go from ff to fff at 114 on a new attack with no hairpin. That is a legitimate subito marking. The composer may want `\<` at 113 for one unified crescendo.

### 4. Bars 40–41, Vln I: the ottava stops and restarts at the barline (applied)
**Problem.** `\ottava #0` at the end of 40 and `\ottava #1` at 41 print two 8va brackets meeting at the barline, though the line stays high.

**Fix.** In trans.ily, delete both commands so one bracket runs from 40 beat 2 to 43 beat 1.

### 5. Bar 73: "espr." collides with "poco più tranquillo" (applied)
**Problem.** The flute's `^\markup \italic "espr."` stacks under the tempo words and touches them, and the "1." label and the number 74 crowd in as well.

**Fix.** In clos.ily, use `c''4(\p_\markup \italic "espr."`, so the text prints under the p, as Gould pairs expressive words with the dynamic.

### 6. Bar 135: ff restated where ff is already in force (not applied, minor)
**Problem.** Fl, Ob, Cl, Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2 have been ff since 132, and the accented chord at 135 repeats `\ff`. Gould advises against repeating a dynamic that has not changed. The accent already gives the weight. Tbn, B.tbn and Tba really do change from f to ff here, so they keep their mark.

**Fix.** Drop `\ff` on `X8->` at 135 in the parts already at ff. Both players on a shared staff must be changed together.

### 7. Bar 178: harp pp against ppp in every other part (not applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 18 set the final chord to ppp in all parts, but the harp prints pp.

**Fix.** Write ppp in the harp, or add a note in critique.md that pp is deliberate because the harp decays.

### 8. Items checked and found correct
- **div. at 163.** Both violin parts have double-stop tremolos, and the mark is on the first chord.
- **unis. at 167 (Vln II) and 173 (Vln I).** Each is on the first note after the divisi. Vln I correctly waits for its re-entry after the rests 167–172.
- **Other div./unis. marks.** Vln II div. 103 / unis. 109, Vc div. 116 / unis. 119 and Vla div. 47 are consistent.
- **Flute rests 103–106.** The shared staff shows one whole rest per bar. The two-note entry at 107 needs no label.
- **Ob 1 rests 94–96.** The staff is clean, and "1." is restated at the 97 entry. Fl "1." at 94 is correct.
- **Shared-staff dynamics.** They are equal at 33, 135 and 163, and the only ottavas on shared staves are where the other player rests.
- **Page layout.** The layout is 17 pages. The break before K (154) holds, page 16 (163–166) is about 88% full, and no page turn falls inside a phrase.

Do not touch: the boxed rehearsal letters and tempo marks doubled above Vln I, the part-combine modes at 80–82, 109–112, 123–126 and 153–156, the divisi/unis. scheme at 103–119 and 163–173, and the K page break with page-count 17.
