## What I studied

I studied the Adler pages above with all their examples. The most relevant were Ex. II-19 (Schubert 5/ii hooked bows), II-21–25 (Mendelssohn and Liszt notes per bow, divided slurs, staggered bowing), II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii down-bows) and II-55 (Brahms 1/iv pizz./arco).

From the corpus I measured, with music21, every string slur in Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i–ii (`musicxml-converted/xml/`) and the time left before each pizz. I also built an event list of this score's strings with LilyPond's event listener and converted every slur and tie to seconds using the tempo map in global.ily.

From the web I used Robin Hoffmann, "Bow Length Duration on Strings" (https://www.robin-hoffmann.com/?p=4043). It gives a violin bow stroke as about 3 s at mf and about 12 s at pp, and says cello and bass strokes are shorter. I also used the cello slur discussion at https://uidaho.pressbooks.pub/beckervioloncellospiels/chapter/on-the-meaning-of-slurs-in-string-literature-particularly-for-the-cello. I checked all 18 PDF pages; layout, ottavas and part-combine labels are clean.

## Findings

### 1. Cello duet 171–174: two-bar phrase slurs that cannot be one bow (applied)
**Problem.** `es'2.~( … d'8 | c'4 aes'2-- g'4)` and `f'4.( … aes'8 | c''2 b'2)` copy the exposition slurs of 47–50, but the tempo is now Più lento q = 72, not 112. Each slur lasts 8 beats, about 6.7 s, at mp espressivo with three hairpins inside. At 47–50 the same slurs last 4.3–4.8 s at p.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38 says to mark only bowing slurs, never phrase slurs, in strings. PDF 39 says to take care in slow passages and that cello bows are shorter. Ex. II-24 (PDF 40) splits such a slur bar by bar. Hoffmann gives about 3 s for a violin stroke at mf and less for a cello.

In Tchaikovsky 6/i the cantabile cello theme (89–100, teneramente, molto cantabile) has no slur longer than 2.5 beats. The cello's 8-beat slurs there (103–104, 111–112, 119–120) are two-note p sustained lines. Dvořák 9/ii shows the same pattern: its 8-beat Vc slurs at 41–43 are pp held notes.

**Protection.** Cycle 58 protected "slurring of 167–170" (old numbering; now 171–174) so that the duet matches the exposition. My answer is that the same notation gives a bow 40% longer here, at a louder dynamic. Splitting at each barline keeps the legato: Adler PDF 37 says bow changes need not be heard. The phrase shape and every note and hairpin stay as they are.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaVc):**
`\clef tenor es'2.~(\mp\<^\markup \italic "espr." es'8 d'8)\> | c'4(\! aes'2--\< g'4\!) | f'4.( es'8 des'4 f'8\< aes'8) | c''2(\> b'2\!) |`

The cor anglais keeps its phrase slurs. A wind slur is a breath, not a bow (cycle 88's "identical slurs" rule concerns doublings, and the cello is not doubled here). The pp held lines at 171–172 (Vla) and 175–176 (Vc) stay as they are, as in the Tchaikovsky and Dvořák sustained lines.

### 2. Double basses 102→103: arco ff straight into pizz. with no time (applied)
**Problem.** The basses bow F♯1 as a whole note at ff through bar 102 and must pluck on the downbeat of 103.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54 says a short time must be allowed for the change and that changes with no time "are rare and should be avoided if at all possible". In Tchaikovsky 6/i, all 19 string pizz. entries have time before them. In Dvořák 9/i, 3 of 14 have none, and all three come at p after a held note. Here the change comes at ff.

**Fix (dev.ily, devCb, inside the `\transpose c cis` block):** `f,,2. r4 } |`.

The contrabassoon already rests on this beat, and the cellos hold F♯ through the bar. The 102→103 join is otherwise unchanged. This shortens a note and changes no pitch; the parallels check is unchanged.

### 3. Long tied bass notes with no bowing instruction (applied)
**Problem.** The score marks "change bow at will" at Cb 117 and 179 and at Vln/Vc 181. It does not mark Cb 13–14 (G1 tied, p→pp, 8.4 s at Lento) or Vc/Cb 72–76 (A♭ pedal tied from 72.3, pp, 8.6 s). The cellos re-bow at 13 (`g1\p\> | g2.`), but the basses do not.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39 says bass bows are shorter. Ex. II-25 (PDF 40): "By dividing the section and staggering the bowing among the players, one can produce a very long and effective legato line."

**Fix.** Add `^\markup \small \italic "change bow at will"` to `g,1\p~\>` in introCb, and to `aes2\pp~` and `aes,,2\pp~` at bar 72 in closVc and closCb.

### 4. Vln II 73 and 124: two converging lines written as an unmarked double-stop (applied, optional)
**Problem.** `<d' f'>2(\pp es'2)` at 73 and `<d' f'>1\> | es'1` at 124–125 are two voices that meet on E♭. They come after "unis." (69 and 117), so by Adler's rule each player plays a sustained pp double-stop and then drops a finger.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: without "div." the player is correct to play the chord as a double-stop. Debussy's divided sustained chords (Ex. II-11, PDF 31) and Adler's divisi brackets (Ex. II-12) show the alternative. Divisi gives a steadier pp and lets each half move by step. The viola does the same 6–5 at 13, which I left alone because it is f→p and short.

**Fix.** At 73: `<d' f'>2(\pp^"div." es'2) | es'1^"unis."`. At 124: `<d' f'>1\>^"div." | es'1^"unis."`. To undo, delete the four text marks.

### 5. Vln I 124–125: one bow from fff down to p (not applied, note)
`g''2.~\>( g''8 f''8 | es''4. d''8 c''2)` lasts 4.8 s at sostenuto, starting from the fff of 122–123. Adler PDF 39 (Mendelssohn violas: "only six notes on a bow, since the dynamic is forte") argues for splitting at the barline. Ob 1 has the same slur, and cycle 88 requires identical slurs in doublings, so I did not change it. If the composer agrees that string bows may differ from wind phrasing, the fix is `g''8 f''8) | es''4.( d''8 c''2)`. The down-bow diminuendo is already the natural direction (Adler PDF 38).

### 6. Exposition and recap cantabile slurs 46–50, 55–61, 146–153 (keep)
These last 4.3–4.8 s at p–mf at q = 112. The violin bows alternate so that crescendos fall on up-bows and diminuendos on down-bows (Adler PDF 38). They are within the range of the examples and are protected. No change.
