## Findings

No finding leads to a change. They are ranked by how close each comes to a real fault or a real gain.

### F1. Bars 157.4–158: the climax arrives on a repeated G5. Tested an alternative; keep.

The violins reach the fff peak through 156–158: D6~ D6 D♭6 | C6 B♭5 A♭5 G5 | G5 E6~. The last quaver of 157 (G5) is struck again on the ff downbeat of 158. The question for this lens was whether a fresh pitch on the most important downbeat of the recapitulation would make the arrival land harder.

The reading offers an alternative. Change 157's last quaver to F5 (`aes''8 f''8`, and the same an octave lower in Vln II). G5 at 158 would then be preceded by both of its neighbours, A♭ and F. That is the double incomplete neighbour of Ex. 20-13c (Schubert, PDF 335).

I checked it against the other parts with at.py before building:
- At 157.4-and, Cl 1 holds F5 from the counterline.
- At 158.1, Cl 1 moves to G5.
- A violin F5 there would move F5/F5 → G5/G5 with Cl 1: parallel unisons. Vln II's F4 → G4 would do the same with the horns' and Ob 2's G4.

The F would be a chordal skip from A♭ inside the F-minor chord, and Point 8 (PDF 343) says parallels caused by chordal skips are faulty. Section 17 (PDF 341) adds that only a chordal skip is strong enough to avert octaves, so a skip cannot be allowed to cause them.

The written G has a melodic reason as well. G5 is the motto's first pitch, and 158 opens the motto's sixth in major (G–E6). The quaver G is an anticipation of that pickup, not filler.

Keep 157 as written. The other way to fix the parallels would be to change the protected counterline at 158 (c407/c408), and that costs more than the gain.

### F2. Bars 117–121: Ob 1's held F5–G5–F5–F5 over the G pedal (keep)

upapp.py flags 117.1: F5, the seventh of V7 over the G pedal (B3 in Cl 2 and the violas), goes up to G5 at 118. Read literally, that is a chord seventh rising, which section 10 and Ex. 20-18 (PDF 337) count as an error. The large-scale reading shows it is not:
- G5 at 118 is a consonant upper neighbour over the i6/4 on the pedal.
- F5 returns at 119–120.
- Ob 1 resolves the seventh F5 → E♭5 at 121, into the climb to the fff.

This is the enlarged neighbour figure of Ex. 20-11 (PDF 333–334): Bach's upper neighbour spread over four bars, with the resolution delayed to the end of the span. c424 gave this line to Ob 1 for orchestration reasons. The figuration agrees. Keep.

### F3. Bars 93→94: Vln II leaves A5 for B♭4 in the fugato's third entry (keep)

chromspell2.py and a look at the line found one place where a theme's chromatic climb breaks its register. Vln II and the cor anglais play the subject's third entry on C (91–95):
- 93: F G A♭ A, Vln II at F5–A5, C.a. an octave lower.
- 94: both reach B♭4 in unison.

So Vln II's leading tone A5 falls a major seventh. In the first two entries the climb stays in its octave:
- Vc 87–88: G A B♭ B | C.
- Vla 90–91: E♭ E F.

The reading accounts for this. Section 3 (PDF 330, Ex. 20-3b) allows the implied resolution to be supplied by context. Here the C.a. plays A4 → B♭4 in its own octave, and Vln II's upper octave is a doubling that closes to the unison (PDF 342, footnote to Ex. 20-27: "octaves by doubling"). The spelling A♭–A–B♭ is correct (Ex. 20-22: the chromatic half step comes first).

The alternative is to keep Vln II in octave 6: B♭5 C6 D♭6 E♭6 F6 | D♭6. That would put Vln II above Vln I's fourth entry, which starts on F5–D♭6 at 94. A new entry must not be covered. c264 kept the C.a. on this line for the same continuity. Keep.

### F4. Upward accented dissonances and large leaps (keep)

upapp.py found three more cases. None is an appoggiatura:
- **Vln II 49 → 50 and violas 50:** held pp pad tones that step to the next chord tone (A♭4 → B♭4 at ii7 → V). The theme's own A♭4 at 50 is the 4–3 appoggiatura, and it resolves down to G4.
- **Viola 140.2:** E♭4 between F4s in the quaver figure `f'8 f'8 es'8 f'8`, an accented lower neighbour. Ex. 20-15 (PDF 336) treats this as a displaced normal neighbour, and a lower neighbour resolves up by nature.

bigleap.py found two kinds of leap larger than an octave in the melodic parts:
- **Arpeggio returns** in the accompaniment: the cellos at 57–68 and 162–175, Vln II entering the stop chords at 26/138. These are allowed by section 2 (PDF 329).
- **Section and phrase seams:** Vln I 166→167, and Hn 1 150→151. Hn 1 drops B4 → G3 between the two phrases of the C-major S, because the horn moves to the cellos' octave at 151 (c186 keeps it below concert D5 in that phrase). The leap falls after a half-note phrase ending, not inside a phrase, so Ex. 20-2e (subdividing a leap inside a line) does not apply.

Keep.

### F5. Obligatory passing tones at the theme cadences (keep)

Ex. 20-8 (PDF 334) shows a 5–4–3–2–1 line that needs its passing tone to reach the goal by step. I checked the last two bars of every theme cadence:
- 22: E♭ D C B | C.
- 55: A♭ G B♭ D♭ | C. D♭ is the seventh of V7 and resolves to C.
- 68: the 7th D♭5 is resolved in Cl 1/Hn 2 at the lower octave (c421 F2).
- 155: C B D F | E.
- 166: C B A G F D | C.
- 174: B left open into the chorale (protected).

No cadence skips a step the line needs. Keep.

### Would a listener hum them afterwards?

The reading's Ex. 20-10 (Beethoven Op. 59/1) shows what makes a figurated tune sing: a long note, then quicker figuration that fills the span by step. S has this shape at 47 (C5 held three beats, then B♭ A♭ F E♭) and again at 56 with the turn. P's head widens from a sixth to a seventh to an octave and breaks through to D♭6 at 20, filled by the arpeggio D♭–E♭–F–A♭ that section 2 describes. The motto's neighbour C–D♭–C is enlarged across the movement as in Ex. 20-11.

The two places where a master might sing more are 157→158 (F1) and 93→94 (F3). In both, the written note has a voice-leading or texture reason that the alternative breaks. I found no change that clears the bar the task sets. That matches c421's result, now with four further checks.
