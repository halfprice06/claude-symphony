## Findings

### 1. Bars 139–142: the bass is in eight parts and the tenor register is empty (applied)

**Problem.** In the ff restatement of the second phrase (the A♭6 peak), the bass F–A♭ | D♭ | F–D | G is played by B.cl, Bsn 1, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, tuba, Vc and Cb, with Hn 4 doubling F3–D3 as well. Between the bass and the held horn/trombone chord there is no moving line; the only motion under the tune is the violas' neighbour-note eighths. The exposition's versions of this music both have one: the viola's espressivo counterline at 19–22 (protected) and the Vc + Bsn 1 tenor line at 27–30 (`c'2 es'2 | d'2 f'2 | es'2 c'2 | e'2 bes2`). The recapitulation drops both at its own ff climax.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12: "every note in an arrangement should be there for a specific reason, making a legitimate difference to the score as a whole." Eight unison/octave doublings of one bass line do not each make a difference; the cellos and the first bassoon are the two parts that can carry a tenor line in the right register (C3–C4). PDF 11 (melody): a fresh line should be a variation of what is already there; the new line's shape (falling third-span, then rising to the 4–3) is the inversion of the tune's contour, as the 27–30 tenor line is.

**Fix (applied in recOne.ily, Vc and Bsn 1, same notes):**
```
c'2.\f\<( bes4) | aes2\ff( f2~ | f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2 b2) |
```
Voice-leading: C4–B♭3 falls while the tune rises into 140; A♭3–F3 falls while the tune climbs D♭6–A♭6; F3–G3–A♭3–C4 rises while the tune falls A♭6–D6; the C4 is prepared on beat 4 of 141 (the seventh of iiø6/5) and resolves C–B with the other parts' 4–3 at 142. No parallels with the tune or the bass (checked beat by beat; the G3 at 141 beat 2 is a passing tone against the held A♭6). Cb, B.cl, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, tuba and Hn 4 keep the bass, so the bass is still in seven parts. No B sounds against the suspended C at 142 beats 1–2 (cycle 122's rule kept). Cost: Vc and Bsn 1 move B3 → C3 at 143, where they return to the bass for the head; this happens at the phrase join under the tutti cut-off, and the leading tone resolves to C4 in Tbn 1 and Ob 2. Dynamics on the shared bassoon staff stay equal (f< then ff in both).

### 2. Bars 56–59: the harp's broken chords lie inside the violins' tune (applied; re-raises protected material)

**Problem.** At 47–55 the harp's beat 1–2 broken chords lie above the cello/horn tune (harp A♭4–E♭6 over a tune at C4–A♭4). At 56 the tune moves up an octave to Vln I + II unison (C5–A♭5), but the harp's right-hand figure on beats 3–4 stays at C5–D♭6, so it now sits in the tune's register and attacks its notes: 57 harp F5 on beat 3½ against the violins' held F5, A♭5 against their E♭5; 58 harp F5–B♭5–D♭6 on beats 3½–4½ against the tune's own pickup B♭4–D♭5–F5; 59 harp B♭4–E♭5–B♭5–D♭6 across the held A♭5–G5. Nothing sounds above A♭5 in these bars.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the arranger must see that the listener's ear "is directed to the primary element and not diverted or confused by an overbusy background". In Ex. 2A (PDF 18) the harp flourishes and the bass-clarinet fills are placed where the tune holds or rests, and in a register away from it. Adler adler2 PDF 257, guideline 6: registral placement of the solo against the accompaniment.

**Fix (applied in tTwo.ily, harp RH 56–59, an octave up, same rhythm and pitch classes, no pedal change):**
```
r2 \ottava #1 c'''8( fes''' aes''' c'''') | r2 des'''8( f''' aes''' des'''') | r2 des'''8( f''' bes''' des'''') | r2 bes''8( es''' bes''' des'''') \ottava #0 |
```
**Answer to the protection.** Cycles 47, 107 and 127 protect the two-hand harp writing and the interlocking design (cello beats 1–2, harp beats 3–4) of 56–68; cycle 136 protects harp notes and voicings at 47–55 and 147–166, not 56–59. The fix keeps the design, the rhythm, the pitch classes, the pedalling and the hand distribution; it changes only the octave, which those cycles did not discuss. The top note, D♭7, is well inside the harp's range. 60–68 are unchanged (rolled chords below the tune, and the 65/67 figures that double the violins' pickup on purpose).

### 3. Bars 97–102: the clarinets' and violas' arpeggios exchange the same pitches in one register (not applied; protected block)

**Problem.** Cl 1·2 a2 play `des'8 f' bes' f'` and the violas play its inversion `bes'8 f' des' f'` (both transposed to B minor) in the same octave, at the same time, in five of the six bars (97, 99–102). Every other eighth is a unison (F♯4); the other eighths are the same dyad (D4 + B4) in both halves of the beat. The composite is a single oscillating dyad; the contrary motion that cycle 17 wanted cannot be heard. Both figures also lie inside the tune's lower octave (Vln II sul G + cor anglais, B3–D5), and the brass/bassoon counter (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1, Bsn 1) and the Hn 3·4 chord are in B3–E4. Five layers are packed into one octave and a half.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12–13, Ex. 1: removing one inner line made "the contrary motion of the remaining voices … more evident" and lightened the lower-middle register so the primary element dominated. Adler adler2 PDF 274: clarity comes from contrast in rhythmic activity between lines, which two identical-rhythm figures in one register do not give.

**Fix (recommended):** clarinets rest 97–102 (`R1*6` in devClOne/devClTwo inside the B-minor block), the violas keep their figure alone; the clarinets then re-enter fresh at 103 on the first-theme head (Sebesky PDF 11: a colour held back gains on its entrance). Alternative: keep the clarinets and give the violas the tune with Vln II (`des'2.~( des'8 c'8) | bes4( ges'2-- f'4) | …` from 98, inside the transpose), which strengthens the lower octave of the tune and leaves one arpeggio figure. Either undoes cycle 17's "inverted viola arpeggio". The same register crowding exists at 117–121 (Vla + Cl 2 triplets at B3–G4 under the tune's lower octave in Vln II/Ob 1/Cl 1); that passage is protected (G pedal 117–130) and I do not propose a change there.

### 4. Bars 113–116: Hn 3 and Tpt 3 in unison on E♭4–D4–D♭4–D4 (optional, not applied)

**Problem.** In the wedge, Tpt 3 enters at 113 on exactly Hn 3's notes and octave for four bars, then rests until its fff at 122. The F–G–F♯ middle line is already in seven parts (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 4, Tbn 1, Hn 2, Cl 2, Tpt 2).

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12–13 (economy; lower-middle register). The wedge's point is the contrary motion of the rising top (A♭5–D6) and the falling chromatic bass (F–C); the middle register between them is the thickest of the movement (25 voices on average at 113–116 in analysis.txt).

**Fix (optional):** `R1*4` for Tpt 3 at 113–116 in devTptThree (`R1*2 | R1*4 | R1*5 | b1\fff`), so Tpt 3's first sound since 83 is the fff B at 122. Tpt 1·2 still enter p at 113, so the "brass enter p and crescendo faster" design (cycles 108, 143, 156) is kept. I did not apply this because the wedge and its entry order are protected and the gain is small.
