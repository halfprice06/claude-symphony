## Findings

### 1. Bar 182: the common-tone link is notated as a stop and a re-attack, not as a held chord (applied)

**Problem.** The cyclic plan (cyclic-notes.md, cycle 20 relay) says that movement I's last chord "is held as movement II bar 1". Movement II v23 bar 1 has exactly that chord in five parts: Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3, Hn 3 C4 (`c''2.~\ppp^"change bow at will"`). Movement I's bar 182, however, ends with a fermata on every part, a final barline `\bar "|."` and no ties. Players read a final barline with fermata as a release. The conductor will cut off, the hall will ring (Casanelles PDF 242: no physical instrument can cut its sound and the room's reverberation at once), and movement II will re-attack the same chord ppp. The listener then hears the chord twice, with a gap between. The link becomes a repetition, and the E♮→E♭ turn at II:2 loses its point, because it no longer grows out of a sustained sound.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 206–208 (ADSR): every note has an attack and a release, and a re-attack is a new attack/decay event. Whether the sound is released at the join is the musical content of a common-tone link, and his sonic-analysis principle (PDF 204) treats such a timbral or envelope event as structural. Gould PDF 53: when two movements run on without a break, the first ends with a thin double barline, not a final one. The corpus attaccas follow that rule (Beethoven Op. 61 ii, Tchaikovsky Op. 35 ii, both `\bar "||"`).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-130/work).**
- global.ily, last line: `\bar "|."` → `\bar "||"`.
- coda.ily: the five parts that continue into movement II get a tie out of bar 182. LilyPond prints no tie to nothing, so `\laissezVibrer` draws it:
  - Vln I `c''1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer \textEndMark \markup \italic "attacca"`
  - Vln II `g'1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer`
  - Vla `e'1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer`
  - Vc `c1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer`
  - Hn 3 `c'1\fermata\laissezVibrer`
- Cb, Hn 4, harp, timpani and the rest keep their fermatas with no tie. They release, and that release (C2, G3 and the harp/timpani ring falling away) becomes the audible boundary between the movements. This is a subtractive change of spectrum, which is the kind of structural event principle 3 describes.
- Movement II needs the matching `\repeatTie` on its bar-1 notes in the same five parts (`c''2.\repeatTie~` etc.). I did not touch movement II. This should be relayed to its thread.
- When parts are extracted, "attacca" must appear in every part, not only in Vln I. Players who are tacet at the start of II need it most. Use `\tag #'part` in global.ily, so the score still prints it once.

Pitches, dynamics, fermatas and the "attacca" text are unchanged. This does not undo any earlier fix. It completes cycle 129's "attacca".

### 2. The join between the threads has no shared rendering (not a score change; process)

**Problem.** Movements I and II are built and heard (as MIDI/MP3) separately, so no rendering of 175–182 + II:1–8 exists. The fermata length (`\mt 30` in 182), the hold into II's Adagio ♩=52, and the point where the low instruments drop out have never been heard together.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 211–212: a sonic rendering should always be available, because it is how a team communicates. PDF 222: "conforming" merges material from separate sessions and writes glue so the result feels like one composer's work.

**Fix.** Add a join check to the tools: render the MIDI of I:175–182 followed by II:1–10 (with II's tempo map) to one file. Before rendering, check that the five held parts have equal pitches and that the dynamics agree (ppp both sides; they do now). Nothing to change in movement I.

### 3. Cyclic note for movement II (not a movement I change)

Both movements now end with the first violins alone on the motto's tail at the same pitches, ppp, over a held tonic: I:178–182 E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5, and II:89–92 E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5. Casanelles PDF 227–228 argues that a theme gets its meaning from its scoring: Zimmer's horn statement versus the upright piano. If II's tail uses the same instrument and register, the listener hears it as a repeat of I's ending rather than as the plagal answer to it. Movement II could give its last tail to the horn (warmer, "inward", PDF 227) or the clarinet, keeping the violins on the held C. I relay this to cyclic-notes and do not apply it.
