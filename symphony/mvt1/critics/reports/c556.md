## Findings

v556's brass writing follows the reading. c266, c356 and c466 added the breaths the chapter asks for, and nothing in the brass has changed since c466 except c508's levels at 163. I re-measured every player and found one place where the brass breathing is less even than everywhere else. I applied a part-only fix for it. I also give the results of the checks that found nothing, and my answer to the master question.

### 1. Tbn 1, 116–121: 11.3 s on one breath, the last 9.5 s at ff (applied, part only)

**Bars and problem.** Before the G pedal at 117, every brass player except Tbn 1 and the tuba takes a breath at 116.4: Hn 1 (`aes'2. r4`), Hn 2 (`fis'2. r4`), Tbn 2 (`fis2. r4`), B.tbn (`c,2. r4`). Hn 3 breathes at 116.4 and Hn 4 at 115.4. Tbn 1 took its breath at 115.4 instead, and that stagger is correct (c087's rule; c319 kept it when it swapped the Tbn 1/Tbn 2 lines). Tbn 1 then holds A♭3 through 116, which is already loud: the trumpets reach ff at 116. It attacks G3 ff at 117 and plays the motto G–E♭–D–D♭–C (ties 118–119.3 and 120.4–121.4) until the eighth rest at 121.4.5. Measured against the tempo map that is 11.3 s of blowing, the last 9.5 s at ff in the trombone's "very strong" middle register. No other brass part has a loud breath that long:
- Hn 1·2, which double the motto in unison, have a printed breath at 119.4 (c186), so their longest span is 9.1 s;
- Tbn 2 and B.tbn: 9.4 s (117.1–121.4);
- tuba: 9.0 s (115.1–119.3);
- trumpets: 7.7 s at most (159.4–162).

**Evidence.** Adler: "The brass instruments require a great deal more wind than the woodwinds … In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath in a slow tempo, for it does take more breath to play loudly than softly" (adler1 PDF 292). Rimsky-Korsakov makes the same point for wind players ("care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time", rimsky-principles PDF 37) and says it applies "with equal force to the brass" (PDF 44). The trombone has a large mouthpiece and bore (adler1 PDF 312; adler2 PDF 1, 3), so a ff breath runs out sooner than a horn's. Adler's tonguing rule (adler1 PDF 292, 321: unslurred notes are tongued) means the player can breathe at a note change. The only note changes inside 117–121.4 are 117.1, 118.1, 119.4, 120.1 and 120.4.

**Fix.** Give Tbn 1 a breath at 119.4, where Hn 1·2 already breathe. Tbn 2 is in unison with Tbn 1 on the same E♭4→D4 (the staff is `\partCombineUnisono` there), so the line does not break. That is c087's rule for staggered breaths: the same line keeps sounding in another player. Tbn 1's longest span drops to about 5.8 s (116.1–119.3), then 4 s to 121.4. The breath is printed in the Tbn 1 part only. On the shared Tbn 1·2 score staff a breath mark would read as both players breathing, and that would undo the stagger. So the score stays exactly as it is, and the score MIDI is identical.

In `sections/dev.ily`, devTbnOne, bar 119:
```
es'1~ | es'2. \tag #'part \breathe d'4 | des'2. c'4~ | c'2..\< r8\! |
```
In `score.ly`, the Tbn staff now filters Tbn 1 as it already filters Tbn 2: `\pc \removeWithTag #'part \tbnOne \removeWithTag #'part \tbnTwo`. I updated the comment listing the filtered variables. full.ly already filters tbnOne.

This adds to c186/c356's "119–123 staggered breaths" and does not undo it. The horns still breathe at 119.4, the low trombones and tuba at 121.4.5, and Tbn 2 and B.tbn hold through 119.4. Motto pitches, ties and dynamics (c086 "motto pitches and ties 117–124") are unchanged.
