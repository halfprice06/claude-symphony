## Schumann 2/i and this movement

What the Schumann score shows:
- Introduction (PDF 1): the motto is in the brass, pp, sustained: horns and trumpets in octaves with the alto trombone, over a chromatic quaver counterpoint in the strings. The woodwinds enter only at bar 6.
- Allegro (PDF 9): woodwinds double the strings' dotted figure at the unison almost throughout, the horns sustain, and the trombones are silent. One dotted rhythm drives the whole Allegro.
- Coda (PDF 42): horns and trumpets bring the introduction's motto back sf in long notes over running strings. The movement ends with sf tutti chords separated by rests and a held C (PDF 47).

Set against that, v389 holds up on every point that makes Schumann's movement a cyclic first movement:
- The motto opens the work softly in a low, sustained colour (B.cl and Vc, 1–4). It returns in the brass ff over string triplets at 117–121, the same layout as Schumann's coda (PDF 42).
- The movement ends with a soft brass statement of the motto (trombone chorale, 175–179) and not a triumphant one. That fits a symphony whose finale holds the major apotheosis ("Do not reuse" list in coherence/mvt1.md).
- Its returns are re-scored each time (Adler PDF 169–171). Schumann's are mostly the same unison doublings each time.
- Its stop chords at 26, 138 and 170 do the work of Schumann's final strokes (PDF 47): power from doubling the chord everywhere (Adler PDF 166–167).

Schumann's own handling (Adler PDF 161–162, Ex. XV-11) is to keep trombones and timpani back so the next phrase can be heavier. Here 15–26 keep the heavy brass out, and the 27 tutti is the heavier phrase. c249 has already rejected a full Schumann-style silence for the trombones (Finding 4 there), and I agree with its reasons.

A conductor reading this score would take it for the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer. Where it differs from Schumann, it is more careful: less unison doubling, more graded brass, a softer and more exact join to the next movement. What it lacks is Schumann's single motor rhythm. That is a difference of model (Brahms and Tchaikovsky), not a fault.

## Findings

No change is recommended. The points below are the ones the comparison and the reading raised, with my verdict on each.

### 1. Motto in the brass at the opening, as Schumann's (keep as it is)
Schumann opens with the motto pp in horns, trumpets and alto trombone (PDF 1). Here the brass are silent until the German sixth at 12. Adler PDF 37 (Schubert 9/i, Ex. XI-19) gives the reason to keep it so: a brass colour saved for a structural event clarifies the form. This movement saves the brass motto for the ff at 117–121 and the pp chorale at 175. The low B.cl/Vc opening is also protected (c229 "low motto 1–8", c250 "scoring of 1–4"), and the cellos' and basses' string marks are built on it (c251, c341). Not applied.

### 2. Triumphant brass return of the motto in the coda, as Schumann's (keep as it is)
Schumann's coda (PDF 42) puts the motto ff in the horns and trumpets over string runs. This movement already uses that layout at 117–121, in the development, where it leads to the fff at 122. The coda 167–170 gives the brass the P head over a C pedal (Adler PDF 171–172, Meistersinger: tune in the brass, strong bass in octaves). A ff brass motto in the coda would use up the finale's reserve, "the falling major motto G–E–D–C in full brass at forte" (coherence/mvt1.md). Not applied.

### 3. Schumann's unison wind doubling of the Allegro figure (keep the present variety)
In Schumann's Allegro (PDF 9) the flutes, oboes, clarinets and bassoons double the strings' dotted figure at the unison almost throughout. Adler PDF 165 warns that unison doubling loses individual colour and that sustained doublings tire the ear. This score does the opposite: in P (15–22) the first violins play alone with the pickup cleared (c227, c294), the wind join only at 23, and every tutti is varied (Adler PDF 146–147). Keep.

### 4. Bar 154: one beat of unison between the Vln II halo and the violas' tune (keep)
c384 moved the Vln II tremolo above the tune. A unison remains: Vln II's held D5 (beats 2–4) meets the violas' D5 on beat 3 for one beat. Before c384, Vln II doubled the tune at the unison for three beats. Adler PDF 165 objects to sustained unison doubling. One beat inside a pp sul tasto pad is not sustained, and the alternatives are worse:
- G5/B5 doubles the leading tone of the V7 an octave above the violas' B4.
- F5/G5 gives a pp second tremolo high above the tune.

Keep as it is.

### 5. Flute G6 at 41–43 under a diminuendo (keep; recorded because it is the reading's Ex. XV-16 case)
Adler PDF 164–165 (Debussy): a chord that must go from ff to pp needs instruments that can control it. Fl 1 holds G6 for only two beats of the sf at 41. Then Vln I (G6–F6–E♭6) and Fl 2/Ob 1 an octave lower carry the diminuendo to p. This is the reading's solution exactly. Keep.
