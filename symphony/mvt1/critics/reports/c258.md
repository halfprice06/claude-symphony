## Findings

### 1. Bars 56–57: the violins' S statement has none of the swells the other S statements have (applied)

**What is wrong.** Every other lyrical statement of the S head shapes its held notes with a swell on the arrival note: horn and cello at 47–52 (`c'2.~\< c'8 bes8\>` | `aes4\! f'2--\< es'4\!`), the ff B-minor statement at 97–102 (c248), the horn and violas at 147–152, and the cello at 171–174. The violins' first statement at 56 has the same melody, now with the added turn on beat 4 (c221). It plays its first two bars flat at p ("molto cantabile"). Shaping starts only at 58, where the long crescendo to mf at 60 begins. The held C5 at 56 and the tenuto F5 at 57 are exactly the arrival notes that Casanelles, quoting Buhler, says lyrical string legato swells into (PDF 155). He gives this "lyrical" legato as the string practice for highly melodic passages (PDF 156, 160) and contrasts it with the plain legato of accompaniment. The score already follows that division at 56: Vln II's earlier metà pad and the Vla div. crotchets are flat, the tune is not shaped. "Molto cantabile" names the practice, but notation cannot fix the shape of a dynamic change (PDF 152). The other four statements show the shape, and this one should match them.

**Fix (Vln I and Vln II, identical, tTwo.ily):**
```
c''2.~\< c''16\> des''16 c''16 bes'16 | aes'4\! … f''2--\<^\startTextSpan es''4\!) |
```
This is the 47–48 swell moved to the violins: the swell peaks into the turn, and the swell on F5 dies on E♭5. Bars 58–59 keep their crescendo to the mf at 60, so the second statement still builds where it did. Slurs, tenutos, "sul A" and the turn are unchanged, so c088's protected phrasing of 47–58 (its slurs and articulation) is kept. This adds dynamics, not phrasing. Applied in the scratch copy.

### 2. Bar 13: Fl 1 and Vln I fade from f to no stated level over the horn call (applied)

**What is wrong.** At 12 the German sixth resolves to V at f. At 13, Vln I's G5 (whole note) and Fl 1's G5 (half note) have a diminuendo hairpin that ends at `\!` with no level. Hn 1 enters on beat 2 with the cyclic horn call E♭3–C4–B3 at mp. That is the horn's low register, where mp is a soft, dark sound (Casanelles PDF 148: a soft horn is a different instrument). Everything else at 13 is already at p (Ob 2, Bsn 2, Hn 3, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb, timpani). So the only parts left without a stated level are the two loudest, at the top of the texture, and they are fading over the motive the later movements quote. How fast and how far a diminuendo falls cannot be read from a hairpin alone (PDF 152), so the end level has to be written. The score itself puts the solo a step above the background elsewhere (5–8, 73–76, 147, 171–174; Casanelles PDF 290; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 133). Bar 13 should do the same.

**Fix (intro.ily):**
```
Fl 1:  << g''2 { s4\> s4\p } >> r2 |
Vln I: << g''1\downbow { s4\> s2.\p } >> | R1 |
```
Both reach p on beat 2, as the horn enters at mp. The f arrival on the downbeat stays, and so does the c226 down-bow. Notes, slurs and the protected horn call 13–14 are unchanged. In the PDF the marks print as "> p" under the first beat of 13, in both staves. Applied in the scratch copy.

### Considered and kept

- **Vln I's last motto at 177–182 (pp, the same level as the chorale).** I considered raising it to p as the lead line (principle 17). It should stay. At 177–179 it is in canon with the trombone motto (protected), and voices in a canon need equal weight. After 179 the trombones stop and the motto is the top line anyway. The coda's levels are also protected (c228, c249, c251).
- **Repeated quavers in the hammered tutti (27–31, 131–134, 167–170).** Principle 5 does not call for any mark. The pitch changes every bar, the upper parts carry accents and portato (c238), and the players will vary their own attacks.
- **The S-head swells 97–102 and 147–155, the tempo plan (poco animando 62–63 and 156–157, largamente 160, rit. 165–166 and 177–178), "espr."/"dolce"/"molto cantabile" usage.** All are consistent with principles 3, 6, 8 and 15. No change.
