## Method

- I extracted every woodwind variable from the nine section files, stripped of comments, and read each one with its levels, hairpins, slurs and words. I read all 17 music pages at 80 dpi and pages 12 and 17 at 110 dpi.
- Scripted scan (rows 6, 15): soft passages of three or more consecutive tongued notes. Apart from sustained pad and bass notes, it found two melodic places: Cl 1 at 61 (C♭4 after the slur, before the sub. p) and Cl 1 at 163–166 (item 5 below).
- Crescendo plans (rows 2, 15, 17): for each woodwind line inside a stepped crescendo, I compared its hairpins with every other part sounding in the same bars. 127–131 is the only plan where a woodwind solo does not take part (finding 1).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 129–130, Cl 1: the leader of the motto stretto is the only part without a crescendo (applied)

**Problem.**
- 127–130 is the stepped crescendo into the reprise: pp < mf < f < ff (c368, protected).
- Cl 1 leads it. At 127 it plays the P head at p<, reaching mf at 129. At 129–130 it plays the motto G3–E♭4–D4 | D♭4–C4.
- From 129 on, every other sounding part has a crescendo:
  - Vla `g4(\mf\< es'4~` (the stretto answer, same pitches half a bar later);
  - Vln II, Bsn 1 and Vc at f< at 130;
  - Hn 2 at mf< at 130;
  - Cb at f< at 130;
  - timpani mf → f< at 130.
- Cl 1 alone stays at a flat mf (`g4\mf( es'2 d'4 | des'4 c'2.)`). It then jumps to ff on its own P head at 131.
- The result: the voice that started the stretto falls behind the voices answering it. Its held C4 at 130, low in the chalumeau, sits at mf under strings at f and growing.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 185: a register "described as weak" is a warning not to "cover" the instrument. Here the cover comes from the rest of the plan growing past the clarinet, not from its register.
- PDF 210: "a good clarinettist can play the entire dynamic spectrum ... beginning with the lowest", so a crescendo in the chalumeau is idiomatic.
- Every clarinet and bass clarinet solo the chapter prints is shaped by its hairpins and words, and each crescendo has a goal: Tchaikovsky 5/i p → più f (Ex. VII-59, PDF 211), Tannhäuser p < (Ex. VII-61, PDF 212), Tristan B.cl "p cresc. ... f" (Ex. VII-75, PDF 218).
- The score's own practice: the motto's rising sixth swells in each statement below f (B.cl 2, C.a. 5–6, Fl 1 73–74 after c398). It also swells in the viola answer one beat later at 129.

**Protection.** c368, c428 and c479 protect "127–131 crescendo" and the retransition. This change does not alter the plan. It brings the one part that was not following it into line. Notes, the p at 127 (c158), the mf at 129 and the ff at 131 are unchanged.

**Fix** (dev.ily, devClOne, bar 129):

```
... | g4\mf\<( es'2 d'4 |
  des'4 c'2.) |
```

The hairpin runs to the ff at 131.1.5. Cl 2 rests at 127–130, so the shared staff has one dynamic.

**Applied.** Page 12 shows the < from 129 through 130. In full.midi the Cl 1 velocity now rises 86 → 95 across 129–130 (it was flat at 86) and reaches 101 at 131.

### 2. Bars 164–165, Cl 1: the slur begins on the tied-to D5 (applied)

**Problem.** `d''2~ | d''2( b'4 f'4)`. The phrase D5–B4–F4 is slurred from the second note of the tie, so the attack at 164.3 looks tongued and outside the phrase.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 187: what is slurred is one legato breath. The slur should therefore start on the attack.
- The score writes the same figure correctly elsewhere: Cl 1 at 52.4 and 152.4, `f''4~(`.
- The playing does not change. The printed phrase does.

**Fix** (recTwo.ily, recTwoClOne): `f'4\mp aes'8( g'8) d''2~( | d''2 b'4 f'4) | e'2 es'2\< |`.

**Applied.** Page 17 renders the slur from the D5 at 164.

### 3. Bars 145–146, Ob 1 "dolce": no swell into E5 (no change)

**What I considered.** G4–E5 is the motto's sixth in major. By the c398 rule, a soft motto statement swells into its sixth. Schumann 2/iii (Ex. VII-34, PDF 199) puts fp on the expressive peaks.

**Why it stays.**
- The E5 enters on beat 4 under the poco rit., as a lead-in. The major motto itself belongs to 147 (Hn 1, violas, cellos; c10 protects the "violins' major motto 147–148").
- The diminuendo on the tied E5 hands the peak on to that arrival.
- The level is graded one step above the Cl pp chord and the strings' pp (row 13). The solo/background grading at 145–155 is protected (c158).

### 4. Bars 35–36, Bsn 1 at a flat mf under the violins' imitation (no change)

**What I considered.** Tchaikovsky 6/i (Ex. VII-93, PDF 227) shapes every bassoon figure. PDF 226 warns that an accompanied bassoon is swallowed.

**Why it stays.**
- From 35 the bassoon is the counter to Vln I's mf head. Its line F3–A♭3–G3–E♭3 lies in the "sweet, more subdued but expressive" register (Ex. VII-89).
- The accompaniment is graded below it (Vla mp, Vc p).
- The bassoon breathes at the end of 36 and starts again at mf< at 37. A diminuendo here would make the counter recede just as the violins' imitation needs it, and the 37 restart already shapes the phrase.

### 5. Bars 163–166, Cl 1: tongued C5, F4, D5, E4, E♭4 (no change)

**What I considered.** The scan flagged this as the one soft melodic woodwind line with several tongued notes (row 6).

**Why it stays.**
- Each tongued note starts a new gesture: C5 after the slurred E–F, F4 at the new mp level, D5 after the A♭–G sigh.
- At 166, E4 and E♭4 are the protected E♮→E♭ thread under the crescendo into 167. Tonguing the chromatic step gives it weight (c96, c406).

### 6. Bar 109, Fl 1 in its weak register over the violas (no change)

D♭5–C5–B♭4 lies in the flute's "sweet, little carrying power" register (PDF 185). The violas play p espr. in the same octave. The flute is marked mp, one level above. Vln II is pp and the cellos rest, which is the thin accompaniment that Dvořák and Brahms write (PDF 186). c398 recorded the same result.
