## Findings

I found no place in v436 where the string writing goes against this chapter. I propose no score change. The three string changes since c416 all hold up against the reading. Below are those checks and the places a critic might be tempted to change, each with the reason it should stay.

### 1. String changes since v416: keep

- **Vln II 23–26 and 135–138 (c422), E♭4 in place of G4 at 24.3–25.1 and 136.3–137.1.** The line is now G4 G4 G4 F4 | F4 F4 E♭4 E♭4 | E♭4 F4 F4 F4 in separate crotchets. On the D string in first position those notes are fingers 3, 2 and 1, with no string crossing and no shift. That is easier than before. The leap from F4 at 25.4 to the D5/A♭5 stop chord at 26 (and 137.4 to 138) is 0.48 s, but the hand stays in first position: D5 is the third finger on the A string and A♭5 the second finger on the E string. Adler's hard skips need a change of position (PDF 82–83, Ex. III-22 to III-24), and this skip needs none. The stop chord itself is the easy secco double stop of PDF 76. Twelve separate crotchets from 23.1 bring 26 back to the bow direction of 23.1, so the f chord falls on a down-bow without a retake.
- **Vla 68 (c422), C4/A♭4 at p.** It falls inside the "div." that starts at 62 (tTwo.ily, tTwoVla) and continues to "unis." at 69, so each player has one note (PDF 88: divisi practice as for violins). It also sits on the G and D strings, the viola's accompaniment strings (PDF 87).
- **Cb up-bow at 5 (c426).** I counted the bows from 5 to 13: 5 up, 6 down, 7 up (crescendo), 8 down (diminuendo), 9 up (crescendo), 10 down, 11 up, 12 down at f, then the beat-4 change c316 marked. The f arrives down-bow, and 13 carries "change bow at will" for the 8.4 s G2 (PDF 108). Keep.

### 2. Multiple stops and divisi, rescanned (PDF 76, 85–88, 96–97, 107): keep

The scan gives 7 Vln I, 41 Vln II, 93 Vla, 7 Vc and 0 Cb chord onsets. Every chord in a high position is inside a "div." span: Vln I 167–170, Vln II 107–116 and 147–155, Vla 47–55, 62–68 and 166, Vc 124–126. The undivided chords are the same set that c416 fitted to the strings: Vln I B4/A♭5 at 83; Vln II D5/A♭5 at 26, 122 and 138 ("non div." at 122, c406), E♭4/C5 at 33, D5/F5 at 83; Vla thirds and fourths at 12–13, 17, 32, 40–42, 131–134, 158–161 and 170; tritones at 26, 83 and 138; the triple stop at 33; Vc B2/A♭3 at 26 and D3/A♭3 at 138. Each is in the first to third position, loud and either secco or a sonorous held chord. That is the use PDF 76 permits. None is wider than a sixth low on the cello (PDF 97). The basses have none (PDF 107).

### 3. Cello clef at 88–92 against Ex. III-53 (PDF 96): keep

The fugato countersubject and free voice at 88–92 stay in bass clef and touch G4 once, on the third ledger line, at 88.4 (c'4. d'8 es'8 f'8 g'4). Every other note in those bars is E♭4 or lower, inside Adler's bass-clef span. A tenor clef here would add two clef changes in five bars for one note, against the cellos' clef plan that c115, c285 and c315 settled. Keep.

### 4. Leaps (PDF 82–83): keep

There are three violin leaps of an octave or more under 0.8 s: Vln I 19.1.5 A♭4→A♭5, an octave pickup on adjacent strings; and the Vln II F4→stop chord at 25.4 and 137.4 (Finding 1). The two viola leaps, 70.4.5 A♭4→G3 and 110.4 C5→C4, both fall to a lower string in an arpeggio or at a phrase end. The cello leaps at 56–68 and 156–166 are string crossings inside slurred arpeggios through the C–G–D–A strings, not shifts. Adler's three hard cases (same string, extremes, legato over a skipped string) do not occur, except the protected cello octave F4→F5 at 52.4. c416 answered that one with Adler's own thumb-octave passage (PDF 97). Keep it.

### 5. Bow lengths (PDF 108): keep

There are 16 string notes over 5.5 s, the same list as at v416. Every bass note in the list is marked "change bow at will", and so are the cello's A♭3 at 72–76 and the four upper strings' last notes at 181–182, which tie into movement II. They fit one violin or cello bow at pp. Adler gives the rule for the shorter, heavier bass bow, so they should stay as written.
