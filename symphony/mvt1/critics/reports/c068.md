# Critic 068 — lens 8 (expression): dynamic-state audit of v87

## What I studied
- **Probe.** I wrote a LilyPond probe score that puts each of the 31 parts on its own staff with a Scheme engraver. The engraver logs every note onset (with pitch), absolute dynamic, hairpin start/stop and text script: /tmp/critic-068/probe/probe.ly → log.txt. Four scripts in /tmp/critic-068/scripts/ read the log:
  - `audit.py`: entries after rests, and hairpin start/target levels;
  - `pairs.py`: parts playing the same rhythm and pitch classes in a bar at different dynamic levels;
  - `shared.py`: a dynamic given to one player of a part-combined pair where the partner enters in the same bar without one;
  - `dump.py`: bar-range listings.
- **Corpus.** I ran the same engraver over Dvořák 7/i, using the Mutopia per-player voices (`mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1/*_musique-*.ly`, copied to /tmp/critic-068/corpus/dv7). Two results:
  - Entries after a rest of two bars or more carry a dynamic in 340 of 346 cases (98%).
  - 90 of 316 crescendo hairpins are followed by a dynamic equal to or softer than the starting level, for example Viola bar 129 f< … f and Violoncello 119 f< … f. Restating the level after a crescendo is normal Dvořák practice.
- **v87 baseline.** 241 of 243 entries after two-bar rests have a dynamic. The harp right hand is excluded, because its dynamics sit on the left-hand staff by the cycle-48 decision.
- **Web.**
  - I could not find Gould's *Behind Bars* text on dynamics after rests online. The searches returned only the book's catalogue entries.
  - Adam Silverman's typesetting checklist (https://adambsilverman.com/category/blog-posts/, "Common Typesetting Mistakes") says dynamics should be placed "under each instrument's first note and under the first note of each new phrase that follows a rest of more than a single bar".
  - The MOLA *Music Preparation Guidelines* (https://s2.smu.edu/robfrank/MOLA.pdf) only require conventional-language dynamics.

## Findings

### 1. Player 1 enters on a shared staff with no dynamic of its own (80, 82, 109, 131)
**Problem.** At these points the second player has the dynamic on beat 1 and the first player enters on the following eighth with no dynamic. The shared staff prints the partner's mark, so the page looks right. But the first player's own dynamic is stale, and the MIDI plays it at the stale level:
- Bsn 1 at 80 is still at the mf of its 77 solo while Bsn 2, Vc, Hn 3·4 and Tbn are at f on the same motto.
- Ob 1 at 82 is at f while Fl, Cl and Vln play ff on the same line.
- Tpt 1 at 82 has had no dynamic for 9 bars; its last one was f in the codetta. This is the score's only real "entry after a long rest with no dynamic" in a wind part.
- Fl 1 at 109 is at f while Vln I and Ob 1 are at ff in unison.
- Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 at 131 still carry the mf of 127 while the second players and the violins are at f.

**Evidence.** `shared.py` reports exactly these seven cases. The convention cited above requires a dynamic on every entry, and Dvořák 7/i gives one at 98% of entries.

**Fix (applied).** The first player gets the same dynamic on its eighth rest, at the same moment as the partner's. Equal simultaneous dynamics merge on the Staff-level engraver, so nothing new prints. The score already uses this device at 123 (`r4\ff` in Fl 1 and Ob 1).
- dev.ily: Bsn 1 `\partCombineApart r8\f g,8 es4->` (80); Ob 1 and Tpt 1 `\partCombineApart r8\ff g'8 es''4.->` (82); Fl 1 `\partCombineApart r8\ff b'8` (109).
- recOne.ily: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 `r8\f aes''8\<(` / `r8\f aes'8\<(` (131).

### 2. Timpani roll at 107 has no dynamic after 9¾ bars of rest
**Problem.** `aes,1:32\<` starts a crescendo from nothing. The last timpani dynamic is the ff at 97, so the roll would start ff. The winds enter f at 107 and the strings are at mf<.

**Fix (applied).** `aes,1:32\mf\< | R1\! | g,1:32\ff~`.

### 3. Subito p at 62 is marked only in the violins (cycle-59 decision); 84 is not marked
**Problem.** Cycle 59 made 62 a subito p after the crescendo of 58–61. Vln I and II print "sub. p". Seven other parts also crescendo over 59/60–61 and arrive on a plain p: Cl 1, Hn 3, Hn 4, harp, Vla, Cb and Tpt 1. In those parts it reads as a crescendo back to the starting level, and the conductor sees two different instructions on the same downbeat. Cycle 59's bare G octave at 84 (Vc, Cb p straight after the ff at 83) is also a subito and is not marked as one.

**Fix (applied).** `\subP` replaces `\p` at 62 in Cl 1, Hn 3, Hn 4 (equal on the shared staff), harp LH, Vla, Cb and Tpt 1 (tTwo.ily). It also replaces `\p` at 84 in Vc and Cb (dev.ily). The Vc keeps a plain p at 62, because it did not crescendo. This only spells out the cycle-59 decision in every part.

### 4. Flutes and oboes hold a flat f through 108 while the tutti crescendos into 109 (open since cycle 48)
**Problem.** Strings, Hn 1·2, Tbn and Cl crescendo from 103 to the ff at 109. Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2 enter f at 107 and hold whole notes through 108 with no hairpin, then jump to ff.

**Fix (applied).**
- Fl 1 `d'''1\<`; Fl 2 `d''1\<`.
- Ob 1 `d''1\< | r8\ff b8`.
- Ob 2 `aes'2\< a'2 | R1\! | R1*4`.

### 5. Vln II at mp on the same line as Ob 2 and Cl 1 at mf (23–25)
**Problem.** In the hemiola, Vln II plays the same notes as Ob 2 and Cl 1. It still carries the mp of the accompaniment at 19, while the winds, Vla and Vln I are at mf<. The recap (127) has Vln II at mf.

**Fix (applied, tOne.ily).** `g'4\mf\<->` at 23. The notes and the cycle-38 lowering at 19–22 are untouched.

### 6. Cellos at mf doubling Bsn 1 at f (94–96)
**Problem.** The cello entered the fugato at mf (85) and never got a new dynamic. From 94 it plays Bsn 1's line note for note, while Bsn 1, Bsn 2 and Cb are at f.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** `bes2\f bes2` at 94, so the fugato crescendo 95–97 starts from the same level in all bass voices.

### 7. Cello motto at 58 and 152 (cycle 67) is softer than the parts around it
**Problem.**
- At 58 the motto in diminution is played flat at p, while the violins above crescendo. The other motto statements of this kind carry a swell (cycle 8: "motto hairpins"; see the intro cello at 2–4).
- At 152 the cello is still at the p of the arpeggio at 148, while Bsn and Hn enter mf and Vla and Cb are at mp.

**Fix (applied).**
- tTwo.ily, 58: `bes,8(\< f des'4.\>) c'8( ces'8 bes8\!)`.
- recTwo.ily `recTwoArp`, 152: `bes,8(\mp\< d bes4.)`.

### 8. Checked and left alone
- **Cl 1·2 and Vla f< … f at 95–97.** At 97 these parts change role, to the accompanying arpeggio in the tune's register. Dvořák writes the same restatement (Viola 129, Vc 119, Cl 116 in 7/i), and ff there would cover the tune. No change.
- **Diminuendos with no written target.** These end in a rest at the end of a phrase: Vln I/Fl 1 13–14, Fl 1 65, Cl 1 54, Fl 1 116, Ob 1 138, Fl 1 159. The same goes for the intro and second-theme swells. They are standard. No change.
- **Category (3) differences with a reason.** These are the graded brass at 12, 27–32, 69–70, 103–107 and 131–133 (protected), and horn below cello at 47–55 and 139–146 (rejected in v18). Hn 2 at mp under Cl 1 at mf (64–67) and Bsn 2 at pp under Cb p (58–63) are balance choices.
- **Hn 4 at 67.** It re-enters inside Hn 3's diminuendo without its own mark. Adding one would put a second, different dynamic on the shared staff. Left alone.
- **Pacing.** The trough 158–162 is intact: strings mp at 160 and p at 162; horns p at 160. The mp at 162 in Vla and Hn 1·2 belongs to the voices carrying the E♮→E♭ turn, and Cl 1 carries the 160 mp into the turn. No dynamic contradicts the 62 or 158–162 decisions after the fixes above.
