- **Violas at 37–39, 93–95, 97–102, 131–134 and 148–151.** At ♩=126 these are separate-bow eighths or short slurs. The 97–102 arpeggio (b'–f♯'–d' after the transposition) crosses neighbouring strings only (A–D–open D–G). This is easy bariolage.
- **The sixteenth-note turn at 40** (Fl1, Ob1, Vln I) is 8.4 notes a second under one slur, which is ordinary.
- **The cello sixteenths at 122** are on the open G string. They work as a measured tremolo, and an eighth rest follows before 123.
- **pizz./arco changes.** Each change back to arco has at least a beat's rest (64, 90, 148, 56). Changes from arco to pizz. at 62 and 85 are immediate, which is normal practice.
- **Flutes a2 at 154–157** (E6–A♭6, ff/fff) are in the flute's strongest register. Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i take both flutes to B6.
- **Second violins on A♭6 at 156.** This is above the corpus seconds (Tchaikovsky 6/i F♯6, Dvořák 9/i D♭6, Beethoven 9/i F6) but inside Vincent's A6 safe limit. It is playable. I leave it, given cycle 24's reason.
- **Double basses on G1/A♭1 at 154–157.** These are stopped notes on the E string, above E1, so no extension or five-string bass is needed. The lowest note in the whole part is F1.
- **Percussion.** The suspended cymbal (player 2) at 155 and the crash (player 1) at 156 need two players, and two are listed. The tam-tam strokes at 114 and 171 are 41 and 15 bars from any other stroke.
- **Timpani.** The C drum has been tuned since 103, so the roll into 163 is ready. The G→A♭→G rolls at 154–157 are on neighbouring drums.
- **Bassoon 1 solo at 77** (E♭3–C♭4, after four bars' rest) is fine.
- **Trombone chorale at 171–175** (pp, G3–E♭4). The longest breath, 173–175, is about 13 s at the rit. tempi. That is long at pp but normal.

## Findings

### 1. Violins: one bow for two bars at ff and fff on the ♭VI climax (154–157); the same at 63–64
- **Problem:** in `recTwoMelodyB` (Vln I) and `recTwoVlnTwo`, the slurs `g''4\ff( … g'''2)` and `c'''4(\fff … c'''8)` each cover eight beats. That is 4.8 s at ♩=100, and about 5.5 s under largamente, on E6–A♭6.
- **Evidence:** Timbre & Orchestration gives 1–3 s per bow at ff. Cycle 16 split the slurs at 97–102 for the same reason. The winds keep their phrase slurs.
- **Fix** (slurs only; recTwo bars 16–19 = 154–157). In `recTwoMelodyB`, and in `recTwoVlnTwo`:
```
g''4\ff e'''2.~ | e'''4 f'''4 g'''2 |
c'''4\fff aes'''2~ aes'''8 g'''8 | f'''2 d'''4( b''8 c'''8) |
```
- **Exposition 63–64 (tTwo bars 17–18), at f:**
  - `tTwoVlnOne`: `es''4\f( c'''2.~ | c'''4) des'''4( bes''2) |`
  - `tTwoVlnTwo`: `es'4\f( c''2.~ | c''4) des''4( bes'2) |`

### 2. Trombone 1 and bass trombone play 97–116 with no rest of two beats (about 38–40 s, ff–fff)
- **Problem:** `devBassTbn` has no rest at all from 97 to 116. `devTbnOne` has only eighth rests after 98. Both then carry the augmented motto at 109–113 and the fff at 114.
- **Evidence:** the longest stretch with no rest of two quarters or more is 48 quarters for Tchaikovsky's tenor trombones (6/i) and 64 for Dvořák's (9/i). Here trombone 1 has about 76 quarters and the bass trombone 80.
- **Fix:** the trombones rest during the soft start of the wedge (103–105). The notes they drop are still played: by Tpt2 and Hn2 an octave above trombone 1, and tuba, contrabassoon, bassoon 2 and double basses on the bass line. The trombones re-enter in the crescendo.
```
devTbnOne  (dev 27–32 = 103–108):  R1*2 | g1\mf\cresc | f1 | f1 | fis1 |
devBassTbn (dev 27–32):            R1*3 | d,1\mf\cresc | des,1 | c,1 |
```
- **Tested result:** trombone 1's worst stretch falls from 39 s to 23 s, and the bass trombone's from 40 s to 21 s.

### 3. Oboe 1 enters dolce p at 137 after 14 bars of f–ff with a one-second rest
- **Problem:** Ob1 plays 123–136 almost without a break, about 30 s. At 135–136 it doubles Vln I. Then it has only r2 before the exposed g'–e'' sixth at 137.
- **Evidence:** this is a lyric solo after a forte stretch. The player needs to exhale and take a fresh breath, which a half-bar does not allow. Vln I already carries 135–136.
- **Fix** (`recOneOboeOne`, recOne bars 13–14 = 135–136):
```
R1*2 | r2 g'4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" e''4~ | e''2\> d''2)\! |
```

### 4. All four horns and both bassoons play 152–166 without a written rest (about 37 s with the tempo words)
- **Problem:** the horns go mf< → fff → dim. → the ff coda. Horn 1 has just played the 139–147 solo.
- **Evidence:** the corpus run lengths allow this (Tchaikovsky's Corni 102 quarters). But with no rest anywhere, every player has to snatch a breath at the same moment. Cycle 6 solved the same problem in the held pedals by staggering rests.
- **Fix:** staggered single-bar rests in the diminuendo (158–161), where the violas hold the same pitches (`recTwoSync`). Simultaneous dynamics on each shared staff are equal (p at 160, mp at 162).
```
recTwoHnOne   bars 158–162:  e'1\> | R1 | f'1\p | f'1 | e'2\mp es'2\< |
recTwoHnTwo   bars 158–162:  c'1\> | c'1 | c'2\p r2 | R1 | c'1\mp |
recTwoHnThree bars 158–162:  a1\> | R1 | a1\p | b1 | g1\mp |
recTwoHnFour  bars 158–162:  e1\> | f1 | d2\p g2 | R1 | c1\mp |
```
- **Tested result:** each horn's longest stretch with no rest falls from 34 s to 17–24 s. The bassoons can breathe between their re-struck whole notes, so I leave them.
