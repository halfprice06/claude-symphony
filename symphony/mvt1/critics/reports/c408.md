## Findings

The brass expression in v408 is careful, and the earlier passes have left little to correct. I have two findings. Both are small, and both are applied in the scratch copy /tmp/critic-408/work.

### 1. Re-attacks without a level inside the collapse at 124 (dev)

**What is wrong.** At 122 the brass play fff. At 123 the upper brass diminuendo. Hn 3 (the leading tone B3) and Hn 4 (G3) breathe on beat 4 of 123 (c266), then re-attack at 124 with no dynamic, partway through a hairpin that runs on to p at 126. At the same bar Tbn 1 restrikes its G3 (it is not tied over from 123) inside its own hairpin. So three brass parts make a fresh attack at 124 and none is told how loud. The last printed level is fff. A horn player coming back after a breath in the middle of a diminuendo will either guess or re-enter close to the last mark, which makes a new brass accent in a bar where everything else is dying away (tam-tam "damp gradually", woodwinds and violins fading). That defeats the collapse that c239 and c289 protect.

**Evidence.** In every example in the reading, the composer marks where a long brass note sits in its decay: Siegfried's held fermatas f>p (PDF 310, X-25), Mahler 4 ff>p and f>>p (PDF 308, X-21), Tchaikovsky's held trumpet note ending >p after the ff (PDF 316, X-40), sff>p<ff (PDF 323, X-54). Adler's stopped-horn passage (PDF 308) treats the horn's attack and decay, sf>p, as something to write out. In the brass, an attack the composer does not mark is a level the player has to choose.

**Fix (applied).** dev.ily:
```
devHnThree:  b1\fff | b2.\> r4 | b1\f\> | c'1 | c'4\p( b2.) |        % was ... | b1 | c'1 | ...
devHnFour:   g1\fff | g2.\> r4 | g1\f\> | g1 | g2.\p r4 |             % was ... | g1 | g1 | ...
devTbnOne:   \partCombineChords g1\> | \partCombineAutomatic g1\f\> | R1\! |   % was g1 | R1\!
```
Hn 3 and Hn 4 share a staff and get the same f, as the brief requires. Tbn 2 rests at 124 (c324), so Tbn 1's f on the shared staff conflicts with nothing. I chose f, not mf, because the pedal parts (B.tbn, double basses) start their own fade from fff only at 124 (c319), and Hn 3's B3 must still be heard until it resolves to C4 at 125 (c284). No notes, breaths or hairpin end points change, so the protected collapse 123–126 keeps its design. The page was checked visually: "f >" prints at 124 on the Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 staves.

### 2. The four-horn call at 41–42 has no character word (trans)

**What is wrong.** The horn call E♭3–C4–B♭3 comes back at 41 in all four horns in unison, ff, as the climax of the transition. It is the call that Hn 1 states alone at 13, which has carried "espr." since c308. Every other thematic horn statement in the movement carries a word (13 espr., 47 dolce, 56 espr., 146 dolce). The four-horn statement, the loudest and most exposed of them, has only "ff", and the call's ">" on the B♭ at 42 is its only expressive shaping. The rest of the orchestra plays sf>mf at 41, so on paper "ff" makes the horns read as one more tutti part, not as the line.

**Evidence.** Adler's model for exactly this scoring, several horns in unison on a theme, is Don Juan: four horns, "ff molto espr.", with hairpins inside the ff (PDF 305, Ex. X-12). His solo examples all pair the dynamic with a word, "f sempre e passionato" (PDF 304, X-8) and "dolce con molto espr." (PDF 309, X-24). The rule c308 applied to the single call at 13 applies here as well.

**Fix (applied).** trans.ily, on the call's first note (41.4):
```
transHnOne:   \partCombineUnisono r2. es4(\ff_\markup \italic "molto espr." | c'4 bes2.)\> |
transHnTwo:   r2. es4(\ff_\markup \italic "molto espr." | c'4 bes2.)\> |
transHnThree: d'1\f\< | bes2.\ff es4(_\markup \italic "molto espr." | ...
transHnFour:  g2.\ff es4(-\tag #'part _\markup \italic "molto espr." | ...
```
The score prints the word once on each horn staff (checked on page 5: the part-combiner does not print Hn 2's copy twice, and Hn 4's copy is part-only). All four horn parts show it (checked with pdftotext on partsout/parts-12 to 15). Notes, slurs and dynamics are unchanged, so nothing in the "low four-horn call 41–42" protection (c239, c284) is touched.

## Considered and kept (no change)

- **Hn 1·2 ff on their held G4/E♭4 at 27–31 while the other held brass are f.** This looks like an exception to the grading at 27–32, 82, 131 and 167. I keep it. Uniform ff in a minor-key tutti is standard Romantic practice. Rimsky-Korsakov's rule that horns sit one degree above the other brass (cited by c186) supports it. Two single horns are each worth about half a trumpet (adler2 PDF 15, c284), and Adler PDF 311 says the horn carries in loud passages without needing more. The finale quotes 27–32 at pitch in every part, so a change here would mean work in another movement for very little gain. The grading at 27–32 is also protected.
- **No mutes or stopped horn** (PDF 307–308). The stopped horn suits single notes and sf>p. The one place it might add something is the collapse at 124–126, and c76, c308 and c319 have kept it out. I checked the local corpus. The Tchaikovsky 6/i score (musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml) has "con sordini" only in the strings, and the Dvořák 9/i score has no mute marks at all. That fits c308's reason, so I keep it. The soft brass moments here (the pads at 56–68 and 146–157, the chorale at 175–179) need the open middle-register "accompaniment choir" of PDF 311–312, not a nasal colour.
- **Full ff held under the fermata at 170.** The reading itself has a loud held brass fermata (Siegfried, "sehr stark ausgehalten", PDF 310, X-25). The stop-chord layout is quoted in the finale, and the silence on beat 4 does the job of a decay. Keep.
- **Brass motto at 117–121, ff and tongued; Tpt 2's held A♭4/G4 at 69–71.** Both are protected, and c308 and c394 answered them from this book. I found nothing new.
