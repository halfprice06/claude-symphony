## What I studied

- **Book.** Adler ch. 7 in full, as above. Supporting passages: Adler adler2 PDF 312 (term table, for Finding 2); Sebesky PDF 164, which was cycle 167's reason for the flute pp at 109.
- **Corpus.** I scanned the MusicXML of Tchaikovsky 6/i and Dvořák 9/i for soft woodwind writing in weak registers (music21, the latest dynamic at each note):
  - Tchaikovsky writes the oboes at p in their lowest fifth often (bars 56–64 and 144–146), and pp at 45 and 306. So the p marks at 43 and 45–46 of this score are normal practice, and I make no change there.
  - Dvořák's low flute pp (Fl 2 G4, bars 99–110) and Tchaikovsky's (bars 258–260 and 326–334) are sustained or harmonic parts. They are not unison doublings of a string melody, which is the case at 109–110 here.
- **Web.** Orchestration Online, "The case of the disappearing flute" (https://orchestrationonline.com/orchestration-tip-the-case-of-the-disappearing-flute/): a flute doubling a string line drops out of hearing and only "reappears" when it leaves the doubling. Forum advice gathered by the same search (https://www.youngcomposers.com/t20477/would-tremolo-strings-overshadow-this-flute-solo/) agrees that the flute's lower and middle register needs very light surroundings. These are informal sources and only supporting.

## Findings

On the reading's own terms (register, dynamic and articulation), v197 is clean. No woodwind is asked for a dynamic its register cannot give, apart from Finding 1. I report two findings, because I could not find more that the reading supports and that do not reopen protected material.

### 1. Bars 109–110: Fl 1 at pp in its weakest middle register, in unison with the violas at p (applied; reverses cycle 167)
- **Problem.** Fl 1 doubles the violas' second-theme head at the same pitch (D♭5–A♭5), marked `\pp`. The violas play it espr. at p, high on the A string. Adler's chart (PDF 185) puts this flute register in the "sweet and with little carrying power" band. PDF 185 says that in a weak register "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume". PDF 186 says this register is heard only when the composer does little elsewhere. At pp, one flute inside a viola section at p is not heard, so the colour change planned at 107–110 (cycle 74: "flute (low) + violas") does not happen.
- **Answer to cycle 167.** Cycle 167 lowered the flute from mp to pp by Sebesky's rule that a flute doubling a lead string line is marked a level lower (PDF 164). That rule assumes the flute projects in that register, as it does an octave above the violins. Here the doubling is at the unison, in the flute's weakest band, so the same written level produces silence instead of balance. Cycle 129's mp, against the violas' p, is the level at which Adler's register says the flute will just colour the line.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 185–186 and the chart Ex. VII-2.
  - Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76 (cited at cycle 109): in a flute–string unison the strings predominate.
  - The corpus low-flute pp parts above are sustained parts, not doublings.
  - Orchestration Online: the disappearing flute.
- **Fix (dev.ily, devFluteOne, bar 109):** `des''4.(\mp^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "espr." } c''8 bes'4 des''8 f''8 | aes''2\< g''4 c''4\!) |` (was `\pp`). Fl 2 rests here, so there is no dynamic conflict on the shared staff. If the composer prefers to keep the flute below the violas, the alternative is to let Fl 1 rest at 109–110 as well, since at pp it adds nothing.

### 2. Bar 182: harp "étouffez with Cb." mixes French and English in one instruction (applied; closes cycle 176's open item and cycle 168's wording item)
- **Problem.** The harp's other damping marks are French: "étouffez" at 62 and 163, and "étouffez (m.d.)" at 164. Only the last one adds an English cue.
- **Evidence.** Adler adler2 PDF 312: terms are given one language at a time. Cycle 176 proposed this and it was neither applied nor rejected.
- **Fix (coda.ily, codaHarpL, bar 182):** `-\markup \small \italic "étouffez"`, with the right alignment kept. The harp is damped at the conductor's release of the fermata, together with the basses.

## Notes (checked, no change recommended)

- **45–46, Ob 1 link ending on F4–E♭4 at p.** This is in the oboe's low fifth. Adler forbids pp there, not p, and Tchaikovsky 6/i does the same at p. The grading of 43–46 is protected (cycle 158).
- **147–155, cor anglais answers at plain p.** Adler's cor anglais solos all carry hairpins (PDF 205–206), but cycle 158 tried hairpins here and withdrew them because they collided with the slurs. The answers are short fills, so I leave them.
- **171–173, coda cor anglais peaking on written B♭5.** This is the "thin, pinched" register, where Adler says the cor anglais "loses its personality" (PDF 205). Cycle 14 raised it an octave because in its rich low register it was absorbed into the violas and cellos. Audibility outweighs colour here, and the coda is protected. I leave it.
- **82, Fl 2 at G4 ff.** This is in the flute's weak register. Cycles 144 and 164 already weighed this layout and protected it.
