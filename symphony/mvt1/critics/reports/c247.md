# Critic 247: texture and counterpoint (v247)

Lens: texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the fugato, imitation, the bass line.
Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 17 "The Orchestra as Accompanist" (adler2 PDF 256–290). I read every text page and looked at every page of examples as an image at 110 dpi (Ex. XVII-1 to XVII-25). The landscape pages (Bizet XVII-20, Fidelio XVII-22, Bach XVII-23, Orff XVII-25) are too small to read note by note; I took what the prose says about them.

This chapter has been used twice before on this movement (c127, c190). I did not repeat their points: the viola under the bass-clarinet head at 77, the viola under the cello at 173, the undoubled horn call at 13–14, and Vln II at 150 are done. Vln II at 107–108 was closed by c197 and I leave it closed.

Method beyond reading the pages: for each solo or answering line I listed every other part that sounds the solo's pitch, or a pitch above it or within a whole tone below it, while the solo note sounds (a script on full.midi, `/tmp/critic-247/scripts/solo.py`). Its output is the bar-by-bar evidence below.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 144: the violas hold the bassoon's answering head notes in unison (applied)

**Bars.** 143–146 (recOne).

**What is wrong.** At 143 Vln I states the P head f; at 144 Bsn 1 answers it, mf diminuendo: G3 (144.1½), E♭4 (144.2–3½), D4, C4, B3 at 145. This is the movement's own dialogue device: c077 wrote it "as Bsn/Vla/Vln I at 33–35". In v247 the violas hold `es'2.\> d'4 | d'1` at 144–145. They sound the bassoon's accented E♭4 for the whole of its length and then move to D4 with it, while Vln II (G4–F4) and Vln I (C5–A♭4–G4) lie a third above. So the answer is a woodwind line wrapped in strings in its own register, with no rhythmic contrast from the viola: both sustain. The script shows `144.2 Eb4 | vla:Eb4=` and `144.3½ D4 | vla:D4=`. The violas only double the cellos' E♭3–D3 an octave higher (vl.txt lists them as octaves at 144.4). They add no harmony.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible ... especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments." Rhythmic contrast lessens the problem, but here there is none.
- PDF 257–258 (Ex. XVII-1): in dialogue the answer is a new colour, and Beethoven even changes the strings to pizzicato "to avoid duplication of the solo sound". Here the answer is duplicated.
- PDF 274 (Ex. XVII-15): Beethoven's middle-register bassoon is clear because the strings around it are simple and do not take its notes.
- The score already follows the rule at 33–34, where the bassoon solo has only the basses under it, and c127 applied it to the same violas at 77.

**Fix (recOne.ily, `recOneVla`).**
```
r8 es'8\f r8 es'8 r8 es'8 r8 es'8 | g1\> | g1\pp | g2 r4 g4(\mf ...
```
(was `es'2.\> d'4 | d'1\pp | d'2 r4 g4`). The violas take G3, the dominant root, from 144 and hold it into their own G3 pickup at 146.4. The chord at 145 keeps D in Cl 2 and the cellos (D3), F in Vln II and Cl 1, and B in Bsn 1 and Hn 3. The leading tone is still not doubled (c222). No string now plays the bassoon's E♭4, D4 or C4 at its pitch.

**Protection answered.** 139–146 and 143–150 are protected (c223 form, c239 lead-in) for their harmony and pacing. Neither changes: same chords, same dynamics, same rhythm in every other part, same viola pickup at 146.4.

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-247/work. vl.txt diff: only the four 144 octave lines (Vla/Vc E♭–D) disappear; nothing is added.

### 2. Bar 175: Cl 1's recall of the horn call starts on a pitch Vln II is already holding (applied)

**Bars.** 175–176 (coda).

**What is wrong.** At 175.4 Cl 1 recalls the cyclic horn call E♭4–C5–B4, pp (cycle notes: the call's second appearance, after Hn 1 at 13–14). Its first note, E♭4, is the note Vln II has been holding since 175.1 (`es'1\pp`), at the same pitch. The script shows `175.4 Eb4 | vlnTwo:Eb4=`. The rising sixth that identifies the call therefore starts from a note that is already sounding in the violins. The clarinet's entry does not register; only its C5 at 176 does. c190 removed the same kind of unison doubling from the original call at 13–14 for this reason. The recall still has it.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: keep the accompaniment registrally distant from the soloist, especially a woodwind.
- PDF 283 (Mahler, Ex. XVII-21): the solo "is helped in pitch, yet not doubled".
- PDF 264 (Mozart, Ex. XVII-9): keep instruments out of the solo's way while the solo plays.

**Fix (coda.ily, `codaVlnTwo`).**
```
g'1\pp^"unis."~ | g'1 | f'1 | es'2 f'2 |
```
(was `es'1\pp^"unis." | g'1 | ...`). The tonic 6/4 at 175 keeps its third in Tbn 2 (E♭3) and now doubles its bass G (B.tbn, Tbn 1, Vln II), which is the normal doubling of a 6/4. Vln II holds G4 for 175–176, as it already did at 176. The clarinet's E♭4 is now a pitch of its own (the nearest other note is Tbn 2's E♭3, an octave lower). Vln II's F4 at 177, under Vln I's G4 pickup, is unchanged.

**Protection answered.** The coda notes (c150, c190, c229) protect the chorale, the canon, the motto and the last chord. Vln II's inner part is none of these, and c160 (180) and c192 (178) already re-voiced it for voice-leading reasons. The E♭/E♮ thread runs in the violas (179–182) and Vln II at 8–9, not at 175.

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-247/work. vl.txt: no new lines.

### 3. Bars 47–52: the viola pad shares the horn-and-cello tune's register (no change)

The divided violas (`<c' es'> | <des' f'> | <bes des'> ...`) land on the tune's pitch at 47.1 (C4), 48.2 (F4), 49.1 (D♭4), 49.3 (B♭3), 49.4 (D♭4) and 51.1 (E♭4). Vln II's A♭4 and the harp's arpeggios lie just above. By Adler PDF 274 the accompaniment encloses the tune.

It should stay as written, for three reasons:
- The pad is pp, sustained and divided against a p dolce moving tune. That is the rhythmic contrast Adler says "lessens the importance" of register (PDF 274), and the timbre contrast he gives in Ex. XVII-9 (horn against strings, PDF 264).
- The tune spans A♭3–C5. Any pad that keeps the A♭ chord full lies either inside that span or above it, which is the harp's register, or below it, on the viola's C string under a low horn.
- The design was judged under this same chapter by c127 and c190 and is protected (c127: "accompaniment design 47–68"; harp 47–55).

### 4. Bar 147: Hn 3 holds C4 for two beats under Hn 1's solo entry (no change)

Mozart keeps the orchestral horns silent while the solo horn plays (PDF 264). At 147 Hn 3 resolves its B3 (145–146) to C4 and stops at the half bar, while Hn 1 enters on E4. The overlap is the leading tone's resolution (c153, A&S PDF 220). It lasts two beats under a tune doubled by violas and cellos, so the solo horn colour is not in doubt. Keep it.

### 5. Bars 153, 172 and 177: brief unisons with a solo line (no change)

- **153.** The C.a.'s held C5 answer lies under the tune's descent (Vla, with Cl 1 doubling it from 152.4), which passes C5 on beat 3. This is a dialogue overlap, at different dynamics and rhythms.
- **172.** The violas' C4 is struck with the cello solo's C4. It is the end of the violas' neighbour figure C–B–C under the tonic. Taking the viola down to A♭3 would need the diminished third B3–A♭3, and c127 already moved the viola off the solo at 173.
- **177.** Cl 1's fading B4 sits above Vln I's G4 pickup for one beat. This is the unresolved cyclic B, faded by c228.

Each is a beat or less, with rhythmic or dynamic contrast (PDF 274). None needs a change.

### 6. Fugato and bass line (checked, no change)

- **Fugato 85–96.** It follows the reading's transparency rule (PDF 268–269): the subject is alone at 85–87 (c227). Each later entry has a different colour and its own rhythm, and the free voice is in the violas from 94 (c237).
- **Bass line.** It has no non-doubling parallel fifths or octaves. vl.txt's only P5 is the protected 162 doubling.
- **Accompaniment figures.** Those at 56–63, 97–102 and 139–142 neither duplicate nor imitate the tune's rhythm (Tchaikovsky and Mendelssohn, PDF 259–260).
