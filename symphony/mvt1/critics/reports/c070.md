## Decision: grow by 8 fast bars, a combination of the two theme heads, inserted between 102 and 103 (v89 numbering)

### Does the movement combine its two themes now?
Only in fragments, or slowly:
- **58 and 152:** one bar of the motto in eighths (the P head's rhythm) in the cellos under the S tune (cycle 67).
- **97–102:** the brass counter under the B-minor S has P-like cells (`r8 fis d' cis'`), but never the P head itself.
- **167–170:** the S head in the cellos against the cor anglais's motto, at Più lento and p.

The P head (`r8 g'8( es''4.->) d''8( des''8 c''8 | c''4) b'8( c''8) aes'4.-> g'8`) and the S head (`es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4 aes'2 g'4`) never sound together at Allegro tempo.

### What the corpus shows
- **Beethoven 9/i** and **Tchaikovsky 6/i** do not superimpose P and S in the development. Tchaikovsky keeps S out of the development entirely.
- **Dvořák 9/i** brings its two ideas closest. The closing-theme head (horn 193–196, flute 197–200) follows the violins' P-head figure at 189–192 over the same D♯/E pedal. At 217–228 the trombones' P head (217–220, 225–228) alternates bar by bar with the horns' closing-theme head (221–224). This is a dialogue at one bar's distance, not a superimposition.
- **Eroica i** (about 304–311) puts the main theme in the cellos and basses, in C major and f, under woodwind sf figures. The theme is set against other material, but not against its second subject.
- Full simultaneous combination is a later-Romantic device: Tchaikovsky's *Romeo and Juliet* development (feud theme with the Friar Laurence motif, Wikipedia above), the Berlioz *Fantastique* finale ("Dies irae et Ronde du Sabbat ensemble"), and the Dvořák 9/iv coda.
- So the models do not require a combination. A neo-Romantic movement whose two themes are both built from the motto, however, has an unusually easy one available, and not using it leaves the development with no passage where the themes interact.

### Why at 102/103, and why 8 bars
- **Proportions.** The development goes from 46 to 54 bars. Development/exposition becomes 0.87 in bars and about 0.83 in time (104 s against 125 s). That is still below Beethoven 9/i (0.89) and inside c043's range, and the total is 429 s.
- **c043 rules.** No slow music is added and no dominant bars are added; the G pedal (now 117–130) is untouched.
- **c053's ban on new ff bars in 97–136.** The passage starts at f and falls to p, so it adds a trough inside that 78-second loud block instead of lengthening it. The wedge (old 103, now 111) then builds from p rather than from ff.
- **Other insertion points are worse:**
  - 84/85 would cancel the subito p at 84.
  - 96/97 would split the brass P head that crosses into 97 (the semitone shift).
  - 108/109 lies inside the build to the G pedal.
  - c069's option (a), a counterstatement after 32, would be a third P statement in 18 bars, because 27–32 is already the tutti counterstatement.
- **Seams.** 102 ends on F♯7 with G♯5 on top, which falls naturally onto a D-minor 6 chord. The passage ends on V of F minor with G5 on top, which steps into the wedge's A♭5 at old 103. The wedge's top line (`aes''2.~ aes''8 g''8 | bes''2.~ …`) is itself the S head's first bar in F minor, so the passage turns the wedge into the last step of a rising sequence of S heads: E minor, then F minor.

### Emotional arc
- The exposition sets P (struggle) against S (consolation).
- The development so far treats them in separate blocks: the fugato on P, then S in B minor.
- **103–106 (new numbering):** the two themes meet in conflict, at f.
- **107–110:** they meet again, inverted and soft. S is on top, P mutters in the bass, and S's continuation lands on its own exposition pitches (49–50: D♭–C–B♭–D♭–F | A♭–G).
- The coda's slow pairing at 175–178 (old 167–170) now recalls this meeting in a resigned form instead of presenting the pairing for the first time. It is the same idea that *Romeo and Juliet* plays out.
- For movements II and III: a fast, full-brass combination of the two heads in major remains unused. It belongs to III (see cyclic-notes).
