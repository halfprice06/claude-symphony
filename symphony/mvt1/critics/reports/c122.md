## What I studied

Besides the assigned units, I analysed the v141 MIDI with three scripts kept in /tmp/critic-122/scripts:
- a beat-by-beat chord reduction;
- an all-voice scan for parallel fifths and octaves;
- a scan for 4ths over the bass that resolve down by step while their 3rd is already sounding.

I checked every hit against the .ily source. I did not use the corpus or the web this cycle. The findings rest on the voice-leading rules of Units 9–10 and need no outside comparison.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 32 and 141–142: the leading tone sounds in the brass under the melody's suspended C (applied)

**Problem.** At 32.1 the tune holds C over G in five octaves (Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I, Vln II) and resolves it to B on beat 2. But Hn 3 and Tbn 1 already play B3 on beat 1. In the recapitulation it is worse:
- At 141.3 Hn 3, Tbn 1, Tpt 3 and Ob 2 go D♭→B, so the pre-dominant becomes a vii°7 (D–F–A♭–B).
- At 142 the brass then holds B3 for two beats under the tune's C (c'''2 b''2) at ff.

**Evidence.** A&S explain the 4th over V as "delaying the leading tone at a cadence by means of a suspension" (PDF 153 §2). In 10-2a the 3rd is absent until the 4th moves. When B is already present, nothing is delayed: 32.1 and 142.1 sound as a V with an added 4th and a B3-against-C minor ninth.

In the recapitulation, the 4th also arrives after dominant harmony (vii°7), which A&S rule out (PDF 160 pt 7: approach "by intermediate or tonic harmony … not … by dominant harmony"). The exposition gets this right at 31.3, where iiø7 (D–F–A♭–C) keeps C as a common tone. The reading also says II6 "allow[s] … to be prepared as a common tone" (PDF 151 pt 1) and teaches the 2–1–7 line (PDF 160 pt 6). Here the brass can carry ♭2–1–7 (D♭–C–B).

**Fix (applied).**
- tOne.ily, Hn 3 and Tbn 1 bar 32: `c'4-> b4-> r2` (was `b4-> b4->`). C4 is held from 31 as a prepared suspension and resolves with the violins.
- recOne.ily, Hn 3, Tbn 1 and Tpt 3: `des'2 c'2 | c'2 b2` at 141–142 (was `des'2 b2 | b1`).
- recOne.ily, Ob 2: `des''2 c''2 | c''2 b'2` (was `des''2 b'2 | d''1`). Keeping `d''1` produced new fifths at 142.1 with the viola (C5–D5 against F4–G4).

All D♭s and the key plan of the protected recapitulation are kept, which answers the reason for protecting it (cycle 60: the D♭ thread). Cycle 52's rule "no more B–D–F–A♭ cadences" also favours this change: it removes one. The new C–B at 32 doubles the tune's line in another octave. A&S allow such doubling in "very free or complex textures" (PDF 154 §5).

### 2. Bar 54: the viola sounds G under the tune's 4–3 (applied)

**Problem.** At 54.1 the tune (Vc, Cl 1) has A♭4 over E♭, which resolves to G4 on beat 2. The divided violas hold `<g bes>1`, so G3 sounds against the suspended A♭ from the downbeat. The harp RH also adds G4 on the second eighth. The parallel bar 50 has no G before the resolution: cycle 2 removed it there. Bar 54 is the one inconsistency left.

**Evidence.** PDF 153 §2 and 10-2a, as in Finding 1.

**Fix (applied, tTwo.ily).**
- Vla 54: `<bes es'>4 <g bes>2.`
- Harp RH 54: `es8( bes des' g' bes'2)`. This copies bar 50's figure, so the pitch classes and pedals do not change and G comes after the resolution.

The plain bass, the viola rhythm of 47–55 and the harp pedalling (all protected) are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 12–14: after the German sixth, the cadential 6/4 is incomplete and its 4th enters by leap (applied)

**Problem.** At 12 the German sixth (A♭–C–E♭–F♯) contains C in Ob 1, Bsn 1, Tbn 1, Vc and Vla. At 13.1 every C stops, so the chord over G is only G–E♭. At 13.3 the 4th returns by a leap in Hn 1 (E♭3→C4) just as the viola's E♭ falls to D.

**Evidence.** "The 4th will most often enter as a suspension — held over or repeated from the preceding chord … even in more complex instrumental styles, the 4th normally enters as a common tone or by stepwise motion" (PDF 153 §3). Ex. 10-1 (Beethoven, PDF 152) staggers the resolution exactly as this passage wants to: 8–7, 6–5, 4–3 over a held V.

**Fix (applied, intro.ily, Vla 13–14).** `<c' es'>2\p\>( <c' d'>2) | <c' d'>4\pp <b d'>2 r4 |`

The viola holds its own C from 12, so 13.1 becomes a complete V6/4. The 6–5 stays on beat 3 (cycle 42's viola 6–5 is kept). The 4–3 lands with the horn's B at 14.2. The horn call E♭–C–B (protected) is untouched: its C now arrives on a note that is already sounding. The only cost is that G3 leaves the viola at 14; it is still in the cellos and Bsn 2.

### 4. Bar 144: Bsn 1 sounds the 6th E♭ against its own resolution (applied)

**Problem.** At 144 the cadential 6/4 (G–C–E♭) resolves E♭→D in Vla and Vc on beat 3. Bsn 1's answer to the head (r8 g8 es'4. d'4 c'8) holds E♭4 through beat 3, against the viola's D4 in the same octave.

**Evidence.** The 6th "will move down by step into the 5th" (PDF 153). Once resolved, it should not sound against its resolution note in the same register.

**Fix (applied, recOne.ily).** Vla `es'2.\> d'4` and Vc `es2.\> d4` at 144. The strings now resolve together with the bassoon on beat 4. Cycle 77's bassoon answer is kept.

### 5. Bars 101–102: a 6/4 after V on the same bass, in the weak bar (not applied)

**Problem.** In B minor:
- 100 is iiø7.
- 101 is V7 with a 4–3 (B→A♯).
- At 102.1 the brass counter (Bsn 1, Hn 1, Hn 3, Tbn 1) climbs back to B3, with D in Cl and Hn 3, over the same F♯. The result is a 6/4 after the V it should precede.

**Evidence.**
- The cadential 6/4 "should not be approached by dominant harmony" (PDF 160 pt 7).
- "No weak-to-strong bass repetitions are permitted" (PDF 154 §6).
- A whole-bar 6/4 belongs on the strong bar (PDF 155).

**Fix (for the composer).** In dev.ily, inside `\transpose c cis`, keep A♯ through 102 beats 1–2 in Bsn 1, Hn 1 and Tbn 1 (written `a2` instead of `bes2`). Put the clarinet/Hn 3 D and the viola B at 102.1 on C♯ and A♯. Bar 102 is then V with only the tune's 6–5 (D–C♯).

I did not apply this. It rewrites the protected brass counter of 97–102 (cycles 7, 66), and the composer may hear the B as a deliberate tonic arrival over the pedal before the swerve at 103.
