# Critic 118 — v137, lens 8 (expression); reading: Adler ch. 2, bowing and its expressive effect (adler1 PDF 36–55)

## What I studied

I read every text page of adler1 PDF 36–55 in full: bowing, the bow-direction habits, slur = bow, notes per bow, on-the-string and off-the-string strokes, trills, tremolo, sul tasto, ponticello, col legno, "ord.", and pizzicato. I rendered PDF 36–55 at 80–110 dpi and looked at every example page: Franck D minor (II-18), Schubert 5/ii (II-19), *Coriolanus* (II-20), Mendelssohn 4 (II-21/22), Liszt *Les Préludes* and Adler's two rebowings (II-23–25), Tchaikovsky *Romeo* (II-26), Bartók (II-27), Gluck *Iphigenia* au talon (II-28), Tchaikovsky 6/iii down-bows (II-29), Handel "Comfort ye" louré (II-30), the staccato and martelé examples (II-31–37), spiccato, jeté and arpeggiando (II-38–44), Hindemith, Debussy, Wagner and Brahms tremolos (II-45–50), sul tasto, ponticello and col legno (II-51–54), and Brahms 1/iv pizz./arco (II-55). Critic 116 read the same chapter under the playability lens and dealt with bow length. I did not repeat that work. I looked at what Adler says about the link between bow direction and expression: crescendo, diminuendo, accent weight and the target of a crescendo. I then went through the string parts of every section in v137, counting bows from each entry (anacrusis taken up-bow, Adler PDF 38), and checked the expression marks against the bowing they produce. I also viewed PDF pages 2, 4–8, 10–11, 13–14, 16 and 17 of the score. No corpus or web sources were needed. All findings rest on Adler.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 19–21 and 139–141: the first theme's peak falls on an up-bow, with its crescendo on a down-bow.** Applied.
- **Problem.** At 19 the Vln I entry `r8 aes'8\<( aes''4.->)` has no bow mark. By habit an anacrusis is played up-bow (Adler PDF 38), so the bows run as follows:
  - slur 1, 1.5 beats: up;
  - the seven-eighth slur 19–20, which carries most of the crescendo: down;
  - the protected D♭6 peak `des'''2~\sf`, with its diminuendo into 21: up, at the point of the bow.
  - The expression marks therefore run against all three of Adler's tendencies.
- **The recapitulation.** At 139–141 the same shape (`r8 g''8\f\<( f'''4.->)`, the seven-eighth slur into ff, then `aes'''2->~`) puts the ff accented A♭6 on an up-bow in Vln I and Vln II.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog… The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural… Conversely… a diminuendo with a down-bow… [the composer] should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". The normal habit here works against the marks.
- **Fix.** Add one down-bow on the entry:
  - tOne.ily, tOneVlnOne bar 19: `r8 aes'8\downbow\<( aes''4.->)`;
  - recOne.ily, recOneVlnOne and recOneVlnTwo bar 139: `r8 g''8\downbow\f\<(` and `r8 g'8\downbow\f\<(`.
- **Result.** The long crescendo slur is now up-bow. The sf/ff peak starts at the frog on a down-bow, and the diminuendo 20–21 stays on that down-bow. Bow lengths: 1.7 s up and 1.9 s down at q = 126.
- **Earlier decision.** Critic 116 left bar 19 alone because "its crescendo suits the natural up-bow". That is true only of the 1.5-beat first slur. The crescendo continues through the second slur, which habit plays down-bow. No pitch, slur or dynamic changes, so the protected D♭6 peak at 20 is untouched.

**2. Bars 16, 28, 132, 168: the accented A♭ in the head's second bar falls on an up-bow.** Applied.
- **Problem.** Critic 116 marked the head down-bow. From that down-bow the bows run:
  - `g'( es''4.)`: down;
  - `d''( des'' c'' | c''4)`: up;
  - `b'( c'')`: down;
  - `aes'4.->`: up, so the accented sigh note is played up-bow, at the tip, in every forte statement;
  - `g'8`: down. That is why 17 needs a retake down-bow.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38, on Schubert 5/ii (Ex. II-19, viewed): "two up-bows are called for in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat… The violinist will… stop his bow movement ever so briefly… before playing the [next note] while still in an up-bow motion." The frog is the heavier half (same page).
- **Fix.** A hooked up-bow on the slurred pair: `c''4) b'8\upbow( c''8) aes'4.-> g'8`. Applied at:
  - tOne.ily: Vln I 16; Vln I, Vln II and Vla 28;
  - recOne.ily: Vc 132;
  - coda.ily: Vla 168.
- **Result.** The A♭ is now a down-bow, `g'8` an up-bow, and the existing `\downbow` at 17/29/133/169 becomes the natural direction rather than a retake. The up-bow carries 3.5 beats (1.7 s), which is ordinary at f.
- **What stays.** Slurs and accents are unchanged, so the winds and the cycle 88 rule on identical slurs are unaffected. The fugato (85–96) and bar 36 already put the accent on a down-bow by habit, so they are left alone.

**3. Bars 32, 72, 83, 142: cadential hammer strokes alternate bows.** Applied.
- **Problem.** The closing strokes of the first theme (32: `c'''4-> b''4->`), of the codetta (72: `aes'''4-> es'''4-> c'''2->`), of the development's first tutti (83: chord–rest–chord) and of the recapitulation's first group (142: `c'''2-> b''2->`) are marked with equal accents. Unmarked, every second stroke is an up-bow, so the accents are unequal.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 42: "A very heavy and vigorous feeling is commonly achieved by a series of down-bows… with the bow raised between each down-bow. It is most often performed at the frog."
  - Ex. II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii, PDF 43, viewed) marks ⊓ on every ff stroke.
  - Ex. II-20 (PDF 38) gives the double down-bow for "two vigorous articulations".
  - This score already does it at 158/160.
- **Fix.** Add `\downbow` to every accented stroke in all string parts at 32 (tOne.ily), 72 (clos.ily) and 83 (dev.ily), and to Vln I/II at 142 (recOne.ily). For example:
  - 72: `aes'''4->\downbow es'''4->\downbow c'''2->\downbow`;
  - Cb 83: `g,,2->\downbow g,,2->\downbow`.
- **Result.** At 32, the up-bow run into 33 then follows naturally, with its crescendo on the up-bow (Adler PDF 38). The violas' 69–71 broken chords and the stop chords 26, 138 and 170 (single strokes) are unchanged.

**4. Bars 58–61: the violins' crescendo has no goal dynamic.** Applied.
- **Problem.** In the violins' statement of the second theme, the hairpin starts at 58 (p, molto cantabile) and runs unbroken through four bars and two bows to the protected sub. p at 62. The peak level, at the B♭5 of 60, is not stated. Every other part starts its crescendo at 59–60, so the violins' level at 60 is left to chance.
- **Bowing.** The bows are: 55–57 up (anacrusis), 58–59 down, 60–61 up. The first half of the crescendo is therefore against the down-bow's natural diminuendo.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 39–40 (Ex. II-23–25, viewed). Adler rebows Liszt's long crescendo over several bows and writes the crescendo across the bows, ending on a stated f. Brahms 1/iv (PDF 55) also states a dynamic at the end of each string crescendo.
- **Fix** (tTwo.ily, tTwoVlnOne and tTwoVlnTwo bar 60): `bes''2.~(\mf\< bes''8 beses''8 |` (Vln II `bes'2.~(\mf\<`).
- **Result.** 58–59 is now a moderate p→mf on the down-bow. The second crescendo runs on the up-bow 60–61 together with the clarinet, trumpet, violas and harp, and the sub. p at 62 keeps its fall. The pitches, slurs and protected sub. p are unchanged.

**Checked and left alone:**
- **Viola counterline 19–22.** Crescendo mf→f on a down-bow (19–20) and diminuendo on an up-bow (21–22), against Adler's tendencies. Reversing it would need a retake with no rest before 19, and the slurs are 3.8 s at mf–f, which a section manages. I left it, and the notes are protected.
- **Vln I 124–125.** The sigh diminuendo after the fff falls on the natural down-bow. It follows the reading.
- **Louré.** No repeated-note soft accompaniment exists for it (Adler PDF 42–43), so nothing should be invented.
