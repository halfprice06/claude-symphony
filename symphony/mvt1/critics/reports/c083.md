**Method.** I measured the onset time in seconds of every bar from the tempo map of `v102/score.midi`, which now includes the rit., allargando and fermata steps (scripts in `/tmp/critic-083/s`, `tmap.py`). For the models I timed every bar of `reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml` and `Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml` from their `<sound tempo>` marks. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Recapitulation balance after the cycle-80 cut is correct. (No change)
- **Overall:** the recapitulation takes 0.61 of the exposition's time. That is Tchaikovsky's ratio once his P return (229–304) is counted, and below Dvořák and Brahms (0.79–0.80), who recapitulate both groups fully.
- **First group (131–146):** 0.50 of 15–46, against Dvořák 0.46 and Brahms "almost half".
- **Second group (147–166):** 0.71 of 47–76, midway between Tchaikovsky (0.51) and Dvořák (1.07). The theme statement alone (147–157, 23.4 s) is 0.49 of 47–68 (47.4 s). The remaining 21.4 s (K) is new climax material, not repetition, which is the Tchaikovsky pattern (305–312 theme, then 313 fff).
- **Recommendation:** cut nothing more and restore nothing. Do not bring `recTwoMelodyA` back (cycle 80).

### 2. The coda is 13.7% of the time, against 9–10% in all three models. (No change, with a rule)
- **Why the measures disagree:** in bars the coda (167–182) is 8.8%, but 171–182 runs at 72 and then 50 ("Più lento", "Tempo I"), so 12 bars take 50.3 s. That is 11.7% of the movement for the slow tail alone. Cycle 43's "coda length within corpus ratios" was measured in bars. In time it is the one proportion outside the models.
- **Why I do not recommend a cut:**
  - In absolute terms the coda (59 s) lies between Dvořák (47 s) and Tchaikovsky (83 s, also slow: Andante mosso 335–354).
  - Its share is high because the whole movement is short (431 s), not because the coda is long.
  - The coda-to-intro ratio is 0.88, close to Tchaikovsky's 1.03. The slow frame rhymes with the 67-second introduction, which is the point of the cyclic design.
  - Shortening it would mean cutting the protected chorale or augmented motto (171–179).
- **Rule:** add no slow time to 167–182. That means no extra bars, no longer `\mt 30` fermata at 182, and no slower `\mt` steps in 177–178. If later cycles lengthen the Allegro, the coda's share falls on its own.

### 3. Climax placement in time is right. (No change)
- **Bar 122:** 276.9 s, 0.643 of the total and 0.69 of the fast music 15–166. That is between Dvořák (0.603) and Tchaikovsky's development peak (0.708), and the same as Brahms in bars (0.63).
- **K head (158) and ♭VI fff (160):** 0.814 and 0.822. They match Tchaikovsky 313 (0.805) and Dvořák 346 (0.796) to within 2.5% of the total.
- **After the late climax:** 18% of the time remains, against Tchaikovsky 19.5% and Dvořák 20%.
- **Between the two climaxes:** 78 s, with the recapitulation arriving 19.5 s after 122. The cycle-80 cut moved the ♭VI climax about 9 s earlier, from roughly 0.83 to 0.82, so it is now closer to the models.

### 4. The 67-second Lento is long enough. (No change)
- **Absolute length:** the introduction is the shortest of the three (Tchaikovsky 81 s, Dvořák 88 s).
- **Share:** 15.6%, between Tchaikovsky's 9.1% and Dvořák's 18.9%.
- **Introduction against the Allegro that follows (15–170):** 67/305 = 0.22, against Tchaikovsky 0.11 and Dvořák 0.27.
- **Pace:** at 4.8 s per bar, the four units each get a full span of time: motto 1–4 (19 s), CA answer 5–8 (19 s), prefiguration and German sixth 9–13 (24 s), and the silence at 14.
- **Recommendation:** lengthening it would raise the slow-frame share (already 27% with the coda's slow tail) further above Tchaikovsky's 18%. Keep it as it is (cycles 23, 50, 53).

### 5. The development share matches Tchaikovsky; the development-to-exposition ratio is high but stable. (No change; c073's rule stands)
- **Share:** 105.3 s, 24.5% of the total, against Tchaikovsky 24.8% (MCTL boundaries), Dvořák 18.3% and Brahms 30% (bars).
- **Ratio to the exposition:** 0.85, against Dvořák 0.63, Tchaikovsky 0.58 and Brahms about 1.0. This is unchanged since c073, and the rule "add no development bars" still holds.
- **Internal pacing:** E and F are 38.1 s each and G is 29.2 s, so the development's three spans are almost equal in time and its subdivision is regular.

### 6. By loudness, the recapitulation is the loudest section; this is acceptable here. (Report only)
- **The curves:** with c053's thresholds (≥ff and density ≥0.6 counts as loud; ≤p and density ≤0.35 counts as quiet), v102 shows:

  | | Loud | Quiet |
  |---|---|---|
  | Development | 29% | 7% |
  | Recapitulation | 35% | 3% |
  | — 131–146 | 50% | |
  | — 147–166 | 25% | 5% |

  The models' recapitulations measure 7–16% loud and 34–36% quiet.
- **Why this is acceptable:** Tchaikovsky's P return (229–304), which his analysts place in the development, is 51% loud with density 0.61, the same profile as v102's 131–146 (50%, 0.65). The difference is only where the label falls. v102's 147–155 scores as "not quiet" because the horn tune is mf. It is protected, and its p/mf swells are deliberate.
- **For later cycles:** do not raise the level or density of 147–157. It is the recapitulation's only relief before K.

### 7. One rehearsal span is short. (Report only)
- **The spans:** L (167–170) lasts 8.6 s and M (171–182) lasts 50.3 s. The models' marks fall every 15–60 s.
- **Why it stays:** L marks the brass head and M the tempo change. Both are working marks for the conductor, so no change.
