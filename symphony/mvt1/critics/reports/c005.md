# Critic 5 (lens 5: engraving and notation), review of v24

## What I studied
I rendered all 15 pages of `v24/score.pdf` at 100 dpi and zoomed to 200–220 dpi on pages 1, 3, 4, 6, 11 and 12. I read `score.ly`, `global.ily`, the woodwind, horn, timpani and percussion variables in `tOne`, `dev`, `recOne` and `recTwo`, and `tools/closehairpins.py`. I also wrote a script that lists hairpins left open into full-bar rests. Corpus: `pdf/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.pdf` pp. 1 and 3 and `pdf/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.pdf` p. 2. These are amateur LilyPond engravings, so I used them only for the layout conventions they share with commercial scores. Web: the LilyPond 2.24 notation manual on part combining and on hiding staves (https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/notation/multiple-voices, https://lilypond.org/doc/v2.24/Documentation/notation/modifying-single-staves), and the definition of Tempo primo (https://www.songtive.com/de/glossary/tempo-primo). I found only publisher listings for Gould's *Behind Bars* (https://www.ficksmusic.com/products/gould-behind-bars-faber, https://www.scoringnotes.com/podcast/behind-behind-bars-with-elaine-gould/) and no page text, so where I cite Gould below I am stating her conventions from my own knowledge of the book.

## Findings

### 1. The part combiner misreads the tutti woodwinds (27–30, 82, 123–126, 163–165)
In bar 27 flute 1 starts with an eighth rest and flute 2 holds g''1. LilyPond therefore labels the bar "2." (meaning player 2 alone) and then splits the staff, and flute 1's eighth rest never prints. The same happens in the oboes and clarinets at 27 and 29, and in flutes, oboes and clarinets at 123 and 125 and 163–165. At 123 the missing quarter rest makes flute 1's e♭''' look as if it falls on beat 1. At 30 (flutes) and 126 (flutes and oboes) a whole note and a quarter note sit on adjacent steps and their noteheads collide. In bar 82 the clarinet staff shows "1." twice. The LilyPond manual's known-issues note says the combiner "only knows when a note starts in a Voice", which causes these wrong Solo/a2 labels. Gould's rule for two parts on one staff is that each voice must show its complete rhythm, rests included. Tchaikovsky 6/i p. 3 (flutes and clarinets, bars 24–27) shows both voices with all their rests.
**Fix:** force two voices in these bars. In `tOneFluteOne`, `tOneOboeOne` and `tOneClOne`, put `\partCombineApart` before `r8 g''8\ff` (bar 27) and `\partCombineAutomatic` after bar 32. Do the same for `recOne*One` (123–130), the coda's first four bars (163–166), and `devClOne` bar 82.

### 2. Hairpins run across empty bars (14→27, 84→97, 107–108, 136→149)
- Timpani: `introTimp` ends `g,1:32\<` with no target. The crescendo runs across the whole of page 2 to the ff at 27. **Fix:** give it a target inside the bar: `<< g,1:32 { s2.\< s4\mf } >>`.
- Horns 3·4 and timpani: `devHnThree`, `devHnFour` and `devTimp` have `g'1\p\>` (and `g,1:32\>`) followed by `R1*12`. The diminuendo runs to bar 97 (pages 7–8). It also starts at p, so it should name its target. Fix for the timpani: `g,1:32\> | R1*12\!`. For the part-combined horns, end on a note: `<< g'1 { s2.\p\> s4\pp } >> | R1*12`.
- Timpani 107: `aes,1:32\< | R1 |` then ff. Fix: `aes,1:32\< | R1\! |`.
- Oboe 136: `recOneOboeOne` already has `R1\!`, but the combiner drops rests in combined parts, so the diminuendo prints through bar 149 (pages 12–13). Fix: end it on a note: `c''4\> b'8( c''8) aes'4. g'8\pp | R1 | R1 |`.
`tools/closehairpins.py` cannot catch either type of case. It resets at `}`, which hides an open hairpin at the end of a section, and its `\!` on a rest has no effect inside `\pc`. Extend it so that in part-combined parts it puts the closing on the last note.

### 3. Duplicated dynamics and hairpins on shared staves (nearly every tutti)
Wherever the combiner splits a staff, both parts print the same dynamic: ff above and ff below at 26 (flutes, horns 1·2), 41 (flutes, oboes), 69 (flutes, oboes, bassoons, horns 3·4, trumpets), 132, 154 and 166. Two parallel hairpins also print at 23–25 and 103–108. Gould's convention is one dynamic per staff when both players share it, and two only when they differ. The Dvořák 9/i engraving (p. 2) puts one dynamic below each pair.
**Fix:** in `assemble.py`, when a part-combined pair has the same dynamic or hairpin at the same moment, delete the one in part 2. This also answers the composer's v24 reason for keeping trumpet 2 at ff ("differing dynamics there conflict in the engraving"). Different dynamics on a shared staff are correct engraving: ff above for trumpet 1 and f below for trumpet 2. The orchestration decision from cycle 4 can therefore be carried out at 27–32.

### 4. The first page does not show the full orchestra, and the percussion staff appears on every page (whole score)
`\RemoveAllEmptyStaves` also hides staves on the first system. Page 1 therefore has no contrabassoon, trumpets, tuba or harp, so a conductor cannot see the full instrumentation. Tchaikovsky 6/i p. 1 prints every staff, the silent ones included. LilyPond's own manual distinguishes the two commands. Meanwhile the empty `DrumStaff` appears on all 15 pages, because the removal is set only in `\Staff`. It plays twice (114, 156), and it prints a ⊗ that does not say which instrument.
**Fix (score.ly):**
```
\context { \Staff \RemoveEmptyStaves soloText = "1." soloIIText = "2." }
\context { \RhythmicStaff \RemoveEmptyStaves }
```
Replace the DrumStaff with two staves: `\new RhythmicStaff \with { instrumentName = "Cymbals" shortInstrumentName = "Cym." }` and `\new RhythmicStaff \with { instrumentName = "Tam-tam" shortInstrumentName = "T.-t." }`. Write `c1\fff` with `-\markup\italic "l.v."` at 114 and 156.

### 5. Too few rehearsal marks, and they are too small (whole score; collisions at 15 and 123)
The score has six letters in 178 bars, and the 46 development bars (77–122) have none. The letters are unboxed and the same size as the tempo text. At bar 15 "A" runs straight into "Allegro appassionato", and at 123 "E" touches the flute's "2.". Gould recommends a mark at every structural point and roughly every 10–20 bars, larger than any other text and usually boxed. In orchestral scores the marks and tempos are often repeated above the strings.
**Fix (global.ily):** add `\mark \default` at 69 (codetta), 97 (B minor), 109 (dominant build), 139 (recap of the second theme), 155 (♭VI climax) and 167 (Più lento). In `\layout`:
```
\context { \Score
  rehearsalMarkFormatter = #format-mark-box-alphabet
  \override RehearsalMark.font-size = #3
  \override RehearsalMark.self-alignment-X = #CENTER }
```
Optionally, move `Mark_engraver` and `Metronome_mark_engraver` from Score into the Flutes staff and the Violins I staff, so the marks print above the woodwinds and again above the strings.

### 6. Tempo words (69, 73, 77, 163)
"Tempo I" means the movement's first tempo, which is Lento ♩ = 50, yet here it is used three times for ♩ = 126. "Poco ritenuto (♩ = 108)" puts a fixed metronome mark on a word that means holding back. **Fix:** 69 and 77: `\tempo "a tempo"`. 73: `\tempo "Poco più tranquillo" 4 = 108`. 163: `\tempo "Allegro appassionato (Tempo dell'Allegro)" 4 = 126`. 175: `\tempo "Tempo I (Lento)" 4 = 50`, which is the one place where Tempo I is correct.

### 7. System breaks put new sections at the right-hand edge (47, 77, 139, 163)
The second theme (47: letter C, new key, Poco meno mosso) is the last bar of a system. Letter D at 77 is also last, and 163 (F, Tempo, key change) is second to last. A conductor meets the new key and tempo while turning to the next system. Gould and publishers' house styles start such sections on a new system when possible. **Fix (global.ily):** put `\break` before the music of bars 47, 77, 139 and 163, and `\pageBreak` before 163 if the layout allows. Check that the count stays at 15–16 pages.

### 8. Ottava and clef in shared staves (31, 131–133; 11–12)
The flute 8va appears only at 31 and 131–133. Bars 29–30 reach g''' with four ledger lines and no ottava. Because ottava belongs to the staff, flute 2's a♭'' at 31 is printed as a♭' under the bracket, so the lower part is pulled under it. Fix: delete the `\ottava #1 … \ottava #0` pairs in `tOneFluteOne` and `recOneFluteOne` (a♭''' is readable with ledger lines), or apply ottava only during `\partCombineApart` passages where both parts lie high. Horns 3·4 at 11–12 put A♭2 in the treble clef with five ledger lines. Give that pair `\clef bass` for bars 11–12 and then `\clef treble`; Gould accepts bass clef at concert pitch for low horn notes.

## Leave alone
The string engraving of the second theme and the recapitulation (47–66, 139–162): slurs that follow the phrasing, espr./sul tasto/ord./pizz./arco in the right places, and clean key cancellations. It reads at a professional level.
