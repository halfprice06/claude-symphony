## Findings

The score has no cyclic fault that needs a note change. Every quotation the other movements make from movement I matches v260 note for note, and the join into movement II is exact. The findings below are therefore mostly record corrections, ranked by how much a wrong record could mislead another thread into a wrong decision. No change was applied in the scratch copy.

### 1. Bar 158 is a sustained ff C-major chord in the full brass; two records say no such chord exists before the finale (record only)

Bars: 158 (recTwo). What the score has: on 158 beat 2 every brass player sounds C, E or G, ff, for three beats over the G pedal: Tpt 1 E5, Tpt 2 G4, Tpt 3 C4, Hn 1·2 E4, Hn 3·4 G4 then C5, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 G3, B.tbn G2, tuba G1, with the timpani rolling G2 ff. It is a cadential 6/4. It goes to V7 at 159 and is denied by the deceptive ♭VI fff at 160.

What the records say: coherence/mvt1.md, "Brass use", lists among things not used in movement I "a sustained forte C-major chord in the full brass". finale.md (key plan) says "the first full-brass C-major triad is 223". Read across the symphony, both are wrong: the listener first hears C-major brass sonority at I 158.

Evidence: Adler's point that every chord and timbre is part of the composition (adler1 PDF 22) and that timbre clarifies form (PDF 19) applies here: the ff full-brass 6/4 at 158 is the movement's one forte promise of C major, and its denial at 160 (A♭, the key of movement II) is the strongest set-up the movement gives the finale. It should stay. The finale answers it at 219–223 (German sixth, 6/4 at 220, V, stop chord, I), turning 6/4 – V – ♭VI into 6/4 – V – I. The records should state this, so no thread removes 158 to honour a reserve that does not exist.

Fix (text, for the movement I thread to apply in coherence/mvt1.md, and to send to the finale thread):
- mvt1.md, "Not used anywhere in movement I": replace "a sustained forte C-major chord in the full brass" with "a root-position forte C-major chord in the full brass (bar 158 has the full brass ff on the cadential 6/4 of C, C–E–G over G, which goes to V7 at 159 and to ♭VI at 160; C major is never confirmed at forte)".
- finale.md (finale thread): "the first full-brass C-major triad is 223" → "the first root-position full-brass C-major triad in the symphony is 223 (movement I 158 has the cadential 6/4 in full brass ff, denied by ♭VI at 160)".
Score: no change.

### 2. The bar-108 seed in mvt1.md describes an enharmonic turn the score does not make (record only)

Bars: 107–109 (dev). The record (mvt1.md, "Seeds of later movements") says: "Bar 108: Vln II's D♯4 is reinterpreted as E♭4". In v260 Vln II (upper divisi) plays E4 (107), E4–D♯4 (108) and F4 at 109; the chord at 109 is B♭ minor (B♭, D♭, F) and no part sounds E♭ at 109 or 110. The D♯4 is the leading tone of E minor and moves up a diminished third (a whole step in sound) to F4.

Evidence: Adler, "hearing mentally" (adler1 PDF 20): a record that the other threads use as a fact must describe what is written. The finale (D♯4→E4 in its violas at IV 219–220) does not depend on an E♭ at I 109, so nothing breaks; but a later critic could "restore" a reinterpretation that never existed.

Fix (text, mvt1.md): "Bar 108: Vln II's D♯4 (the E♭4/E4 thread pitch, spelled as the leading tone of E minor) rises to F4 in B♭ minor at 109; the finale's violas take the same D♯4 to E4 at its 219–220." Score: no change. The notes of 103–110 are protected (c357, c398 and later) and I see no reason to touch them.

### 6. Foreshadowing: the seeds are at the right strength; add none (keep)

The scherzo seed at 78–79 (Hn 1·2 and cellos G4–F♯4–F4–E4, then Ob 1 and Vln I G5–F♯5–F5–E5, the scherzo's theme A bar 2 at pitch, after Cl 1's G♯5) grows out of the P head in E minor inside the A♭–E–C key circle: a derivation, not a quotation, so not too explicit. The other seeds (106, 12, 37–44, 73–76 = II 3–6) stand as mvt1.md lists them.

There is no seed of the finale's themes F1 (`c''2-> g'4. aes'8 | bes'4 aes'8 g'8 f'4 es'4`) or F2. I recommend adding none. In the models a finale's own themes are not planted in the first movement; it is the first movement's ideas that return (Tchaikovsky 4, on Adler's study list, adler2 PDF 195; Tchaikovsky 5; Brahms 1, whose finale tune is not in i). What movement I should foreshadow is the finale's goal, the motto in C major, and it does: in fragments, as in Strauss's Tod und Verklärung (also on the list): the soft major S head at 147–150, the denied ff 6/4 at 158 (finding 1), and the soft C-major cadences at 154→155 and 165→166. Bar 34's falling Bsn 1 line C4–B♭3–A♭3–G3–F3 already has the shape of F1's second bar a step higher; that is enough of a hint.

The motto's colours also prepare the later movements without being explicit. Adler says that the register and emotional intent decide the instrument (adler2 PDF 188). Movement I ends its last motto in Vln I sul D at E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5 (178–182). Movement II ends its last motto tail at the same pitch, E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5, in Cl 1 (II 85–89), the instrument whose B4 movement I leaves unresolved at 177. Keep.
