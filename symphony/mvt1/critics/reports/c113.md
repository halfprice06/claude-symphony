## Findings

The score follows this unit almost everywhere. Every principle the unit teaches about establishing, confirming, leaving and returning from a key area is applied correctly, several of them in textbook form (rows 5, 10, 12, 14). I found one real structural difference from the corpus. It cannot be fixed under the current rules, so I report it and propose no edit. After it I list the doubts I checked and rejected.

### F1. The development has no key area, only a chain of tonicizations (77–130). Rank 1 of 1. No edit proposed.

**Problem.** By the unit's own test (PDF 216 §6: own dominant, several chords, a cadence, a melody aiming at 1̂, a break in the flow; PDF 213 §4: "establishing a key requires time and musical content"), no key in the development is established. Each one is tonicized and then left. Key durations in v132:

| Bars | Key | Bars long | Confirmed by a cadence? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 77–79 | A♭ minor → F♭ (5–6) | 3 | No |
| 80–84 | C minor, ending on V | 5 | Half cadence |
| 85–96 | Fugato G / C / F / B♭ minor | 3 each | No |
| 97–102 | B minor | 6 | Cadential 6/4–V7 at 102, evaded into D minor |
| 103–110 | D minor, E minor, B♭ minor, V of F minor | 2–4 each | No |
| 111–116 | F minor | 1, then the chromatic bass | IAC at 111 (3̂ on top) |
| 117–130 | V pedal | 14 | Prolonged V, not a key |

**Evidence.** The MCTL annotations show each corpus development containing at least one key that holds for a long span:
- Beethoven 9/i (D minor, 2/4): G minor for 41 bars (160–201) and A minor for 38 (243–281).
- Tchaikovsky 6/i: D minor for 45 bars (161–205) and F♯ for 20 (219–239).
- Mozart 41/i: E♭ for 22 bars (121–143) and F for 13 (159–172).

At these tempos the longest plateau in each lasts about 30–70 seconds. The longest stable span in this development is the V pedal, about 30 seconds at the allargando/sostenuto tempi. The longest real key is B minor, about 11 seconds. The movement also never makes its own dominant a key area: G minor appears only for the fugato's first entry (85–87). Beethoven 9/i keeps v (A minor) for 38 bars.

**Why no edit.** There are three reasons.
1. The 102→103 evasion and the stepwise key rise D–E–F–G (103–117) are deliberate and protected (cycles 70, 72), and they are good. The B minor cadence is prepared with a full cadential 6/4–V7 and then evaded, which the unit itself allows (Ex. 14-8, PDF 218–219: "the boundaries between two key areas are not always sharply defined"). Turning bar 102 into a B-minor tonic would add a cadence one bar before the D-minor arrival and stall the drive into 103.
2. A plateau needs new bars, and the rule from cycles 43/73/83 (no more development bars) stands. Taking the bars from the fugato or the theme combination would undo protected material.
3. The tension curve does not suffer in practice. The 14-bar V pedal acts as the plateau, and with its fff climax at 122 it lies where cycle 73 judged the climax position right (0.63 of the duration).

**Recommendation.** Keep movement I as it is. For the composer's notes on movements II and III (cyclic-notes.md): if a later movement uses sonata form, its development should hold one key for 12–20 bars with its own cadence, the device every corpus development here uses. In a C-minor-centred symphony the dominant key (G) is the obvious unused candidate.

### Checked and kept (no findings)

- **Retransition 125–130.** The seventh F is last heard at 124, and 129–130 have no B♮: bar 130 is G + C + E♭ (the motto ending on C over the pedal). Unit 14's advice to add the seventh (PDF 219–220 §10) applies when V has been *tonicized as a key*. Here G is only ever a dominant: F♯ leads into it at 116, F and B♮ sound together 117–124, and no G-major or G-minor cadence occurs. The motto's arrival on C a bar early is the cyclic design. Adding B or F under the viola and clarinet C at 130 makes a semitone or ninth clash with the motto. Keep it.
- **Recap bridge 143–146.** V is reached from i (143) with no applied chord. PDF 215 says the diatonic approach makes V "want to continue moving". That suits a bridge whose second theme stays in the same key and begins at once (147), with the oboe's G–E♮ (145–146) turning the mode. Keep it.
- **Bar 40 leading tone.** D♮ (♯4̂ of A♭) sounds in Vla and Hn 3 at one unison. At 41 they part: the viola rises to E♭ and the horn falls to D♭ (the seventh of E♭7). This is not the doubled-and-parallel resolution PDF 214 warns against. Keep it.
- **Second key VI rather than III** (row 3). Beethoven 9/i uses the same plan: S in VI, the recapitulation's S in the tonic major (D major, 337–351 in the annotation), then the tonic minor for the coda. This score does the same (147 C major, 167 C minor). Keep it.
- **Exposition key proportions.** C minor and the bridge take 15–40 (26 bars, 42% of the exposition) and A♭ takes 41–76. Beethoven 9/i has D minor for about 39% of its exposition. Within range.
