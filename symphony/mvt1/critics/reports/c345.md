## Findings

Ranked by how much each stands between this page and a professionally engraved score. All are engraving only; no pitch, rhythm, dynamic or articulation changes.

### 1. Bar 123, Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1·2: two semibreves a second apart printed as two voices, upper note on the left — applied
**What is wrong.** At the collapse after the fff, Hn 1 holds G4 and Hn 2 F4, Tbn 1 G3 and Tbn 2 F3, all semibreves under the same diminuendo. The part-combiner keeps each pair apart, so the score prints the G to the left and the F displaced to the right. That is the layout for two independent stemmed parts. Eleven bars later, at 134, the same horns hold the same F4/G4 pair as a chord with the F on the left, which is the correct layout. The two bars disagree in the same movement.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 60: "Two or more notes of the same duration are combined onto one stem to form a chord"; separate stems are only for different durations. PDF 61: in a chord, "place the lower note on the left-hand side of the stem, the upper note on the right-hand side". PDF 63: "Stemless noteheads are arranged as if they were stemmed." The double-stem offset of PDF 62 (lower part to the right) applies only when the parts really are separate voices.
**Fix (applied).** dev.ily, `devHnOne` bar 123: `\partCombineChords \after 2.. \! g'1\> | \partCombineAutomatic R1*7 |`; `devTbnOne` bar 123: `\partCombineChords g1\> | \partCombineAutomatic g1 |`. At 400 dpi both bars now show F left and G right, as at 134. The hairpins are unchanged and still print once per staff. The parts are not affected, because parts-lib.ily strips the combiner switches (c295).

### 2. Bar 14→15: no double barline between the Lento introduction and the Allegro — applied
**What is wrong.** The score has thin double barlines at every key change (47, 77, 97, 103, 111, 147, 167), including 103 and 111, which fall inside the development. The largest section division of the movement, from the 14-bar Lento introduction to the Allegro appassionato exposition at A, has a plain barline. A reader leafing through the score sees a double barline at a mid-development key change but none where the introduction ends.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 53: thin double barlines "mark divisions between sections in a piece"; they should not appear "with every metre change", but here a real section change has none. The change of tempo, the first rehearsal letter and the new system all fall here, so this is the clearest section division in the movement. The recapitulation at 131 stays without one: it follows the stretto of 127–130 without a break and has no key change, so a double bar there would make a seam the music does not have.
**Fix (applied).** global.ily, after `s1*14`: `\bar "||"`. It falls at the end of the first system of page 2, so it changes no layout. Through partGlobal it also appears in every part.

### 3. Bar 68→69: the last stem of the sixteenth run is half a space from the barline — applied
**What is wrong.** The a2 flutes and Vln I end bar 68 with a rising sixteenth group into the f downbeat at D. At 300 dpi the last up-stem stands 11 px (0.75 space) from the barline in the flutes and 7 px (0.5 space) in Vln I. The stem and the barline read as one double line, just under rehearsal mark D and the "Tempo dell'Allegro" heading. Other runs into a barline (32→33, 40→41) keep 2.2–2.6 spaces, so only this one is cramped. The cause is the rit. bars 66–68 printed on a fixed eight-bar system.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 57: "Stems must never come closer to a barline than one space, because the close parallel lines are difficult to read" (shown with the "but not" example on that page). PDF 56: "allow a stave-space ... on either side of a barline before a notational symbol."
**Fix (applied).** global.ily, before `\mark \default` at 69: `\once \override Score.BarLine.extra-spacing-width = #'(-0.6 . 0)`. Now the flute stem is 1.0 space and the Vln I stem about 2 spaces from the barline. Bar 68 is 2–3 pt wider, no system or page break moves, and the score is still 17 pages of music.

### 4. Bars 117–120, Fl 1·2: Fl 2's semibreves overlap Fl 1's crossing quavers — tested, keep as is
Fl 2 holds F5, G5, F5 (117, 118, 120) while Fl 1 starts each half-bar below it (B4, E♭5, D5, F5 quavers). Gould PDF 67 says overlapping semibreves "must be divided into stemmed notes in order to show part progression". c145 left this open. I tested it: Fl 2 as `f''2~ f''2 | g''2~ g''2 | … | f''2~ f''2`, stems down in the apart voice. The down-curving ties then run through Fl 1's B♮4 and its natural at 117, touch the E♭5 head at 118, and cross Fl 1's unison F5 quaver at 120. That breaks PDF 55 ("no characters should collide") in three bars to fix a stemless head that the quaver rest above the stave already explains. I reverted it. The present layout follows PDF 65 (upper part up-stems even when the parts cross) and should stay. The scoring of 117–122 is protected (c341, c345).

### 5. Bars 147–162: an eleven-bar system next to a five-bar one — keep (protected layout)
A crotchet is about 25 px wide in 151 and about 55 px in 158, on facing systems with similar values. Gould PDF 54 asks for "a similar number of bars on adjacent systems" in like rhythms. The K page break and the fixed page ends are protected ("K page break, page-count 17", c173/c224/c274; "Page breaks (same last bar on every page)", c325/c335). The wide spacing also suits the fff climax 158–162 with its long tenuto values and many simultaneous texts. I do not reopen it.

### 6. Bars 76, 176, 182: bar-length notes at the left of the bar — keep (c255)
Gould PDF 55 places a lone bar-length note just left of centre. c255 kept 76 and 176 for a stated reason (under 1 mm of difference; a per-bar offset on about 20 staves that a later layout change would break). The same applies to 182, where the fermatas sit over the heads and the bar is narrow. I agree; no change.
