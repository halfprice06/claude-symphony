# Critic 096 — lens 6 (playability): first rehearsal read-through of v115

## What I studied
Score and sources of v115 (all section files, global.ily, score.ly, PDF pages 3, 10, 15, 16). I made a per-player MIDI with no part-combining (/tmp/critic-096/solo/solo.ly) and listed every rest of 16 bars or more before an entry, plus every high brass note and how long the player had rested before it. Corpus, using music21 on the MCTL MusicXML: Tchaikovsky 6/i (`musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt1.musicxml`): long rests before entries, and the triplet-against-duple bars 109–110. Dvořák 9/i (`Dvorak_Sym9_mvt1.musicxml`): long rests before entries. Dvořák 7/i Mutopia parts (`mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Clarinetto_I_II.ly` l. 191, `..._Corno_III_IV.ly` l. 126, `DvorakSYMPH7_conductor.ly` l. 186): how cues are written in parts and kept out of the conductor's score. Web: Gould, *Behind Bars* pp. 566–571 on cues, read only through a quotation on notat.io (https://www.notat.io/viewtopic.php?p=3239). Steinberg's Dorico manual on rests in cues (https://steinberg.help/dorico/v1/en/dorico/topics/notation_reference/notation_reference_cues_rests_c.html). Audition lists naming the Tchaikovsky 6/i passages that get practised: Dartmouth clarinet list, bars 54–69 and 153–160 (https://www.hop.dartmouth.edu/sites/hop.prod/files/hop/dso_20f_clarinet_excerpts.pdf); NYO2 viola list, bars 19–63 (https://www.carnegiehall.org/Education/Programs/National-Youth-Ensembles/NYO2/Apply/Audition-Excerpts). Bärenreiter's note that the horn rhythm in bar 4 of Dvořák 9/i is a known point of dispute (https://barenreiter.co.uk/symphony-no-9-in-e-minor-op-95-new-world-full-score.html). I found no published rehearsal guide for either symphony, so the comparisons with the models are my own, made from the corpus.

## Findings (ranked)

**1. Bars 162–167, Cl 1 has nowhere to breathe (the open item from cycle 86). Decided and applied.** After the eighth rest at 162 beat 3, Cl 1 plays to the end of 166 without a break. With the global.ily tempo map (100, then rit. to 81) that is about 11 s, which is long but playable at mp. The trouble is that Cl 1 then goes straight to g''1 f at 167 and holds it to 170 beat 3, about 7 s more at f–ff. In rehearsal the player will snatch a breath by clipping the protected E♭ at 166, or by coming in late at 167 without telling anyone. Ob 1 plays the same g''–a♭'' (codaOboeOne) from the downbeat of 167, so the breath can go there with no gap in the line, which meets the cycle-87 rule. Fix: `\partCombineApart r4\f g''2. | \partCombineAutomatic g''2 aes''2 |` in codaClOne. The \f sits on the rest, together with Cl 2's \f, so the shared staff gets one dynamic. Apart mode makes the rest print; automatic mode had printed "2." and hidden it. Conclusion: no rest inside 162–166 is needed, and the 166 E♭ is untouched. Applied.

**2. Bar 170, the general-pause fermata is missing in four parts. Applied.** Cycle 30 put the fermata "on the rest at 166 [now 170] in all parts", but codaCorAnglais (`R1*4`), codaPerc (`R1*8`), codaHarpR (`R1*15`) and codaHarpL (`R1 R1 R1 R1`) have none. You don't notice this in the full score, where the other staves carry the fermata. In parts it is a guaranteed stop. The cor anglais counts four bars and plays its exposed motto pickup at 171 beat 4 into the silence. The tam-tam player counts toward 175 and arrives early. Fix: `R1*3 | r2. r4\fermata |` in all four. I checked it in a test cor anglais part: bar 170 now prints with the fermata after a three-bar rest.

**3. Bar 91, the cor anglais enters after 82 bars' rest with an off-beat fugato entry at f (`r8 c'8\f`), and there is no cue. Cues applied, for the part only.** This is the longest rest before any exposed entry in the movement. Neither model asks for this. In Dvořák 9/i every brass and timpani entry after 45–106 bars' rest falls on a downbeat (bars 121, 171, 292, 346, 400). In Tchaikovsky 6/i the brass come in on beat 4 after 66 bars (MCTL m67), but that is a tutti entry. The same applies on a smaller scale to the cor anglais at 147, after 45 bars, answering with a pickup at p, and at 171, after the fermata. Gould, as quoted, says prominent entries make useful markers during long rests. The Dvořák 7 Mutopia edition writes such cues with `\cueDuring` inside `\tag #'partScore` and keeps them out of the conductor's score with `\keepWithTag #'conductorScore`. I copied that method. In dev.ily: `R1*12 | \tag #'score R1*2 \tag #'part \cueDuring #"cueVla" #DOWN { s4*0^\markup \fontsize #-3 "Vla." R1*2 } |`. I added a new `recOneCorAnglais` with an "Ob. 1" cue of the dolce G–E at 145–146, and a "Vc." cue in 171 beats 1–3. score.ly now reads `\removeWithTag #'part \corAnglais`, so the full score is unchanged. A part file needs `\addQuote "cueVla" { \vla }`, `"cueObOne" { \oboeOne }`, `"cueVc" { \vc }` and `\removeWithTag #'score`. My test part is /tmp/critic-096/parttest/ca.ly; it built with no warnings and the cues print small with their labels. Applied. Still a note for when parts are extracted: harp 69–155 (87 bars) needs a Vln I cue at 154–155.

**4. Bars 117–121, the off-beat head has no duple pulse to land against. Applied, inside a protected passage; see the reasoning.** At G, Fl, Ob 1, Vln I and II enter on the second eighth (`r8 b'8\ff`), while the only rhythmic grid on the beat is the Cl 1·2 and Vla triplets (`b8 d' f'`). Vc is unmeasured tremolo, Cb is held, timpani is a roll. Players who hear triplets put the "and" on the second triplet. Expect the conductor to stop and run violins with violas. Tchaikovsky 6/i 109–110 also sets triplets (clarinets, bassoons) against duples, but there Vln II and Vla keep a measured eighth–sixteenth duple figure on every beat, so the off-beat players can hear where they belong. Fix: Vc `g,1:16` (measured sixteenths) instead of `g,1:32` for 117–121. This changes no pitch, register or layer. The layering of 117–121 is protected for its motto layers, and those are untouched. The Vc's own measured acceleration at 127–130 already ends in sixteenths. Applied. If the composer wants the tremolo colour kept, the alternative is a rehearsal letter-rehearsal of violins and violas alone, with no change to the score.

**5. Bar 33, the bassoon solo starts inside the decay of the ff tutti chord. Applied.** At 33 the tutti plays an accented ff eighth, and Bsn 1 begins its solo on the very next eighth at mf. In a hall the brass and timpani chord rings on, and covers the G and the accented E♭ of the bassoon head. The bassoonist cannot see the problem from the part, which shows only rests before the solo. Fix: `r8 g8\f_\markup \italic "espr."( es'4.->) d'8(\> des'8 c'8 | c'4)\mf`. The notes stay as they are (cycle 54). Applied.

**6. Pickups and the hemiola: I checked them, and they need no change.** 32: Vln I and II slur four sixteenths into a down-bow tutti downbeat after a rest of a beat and a half. This is ordinary, and both violin sections have the same figure. 68→69: the Vln I sixteenth run falls in the slowest part of the rit. (88), and at 69 the violas' broken-chord eighths and the timpani start on the downbeat. That gives the Cl 1, Hn 1·2 and Vln II head (`r8 es'`) the new eighth to follow. 167 has the same support from the Cb eighths. Hemiola 23–26 and 135–138: every layer groups by three in the same places. The tied accented notes, the accented quarters in Vln II, Vla and Cb, the 3-beat slurs on top and the timpani at 135–137 all agree, and everyone lands together on the downbeat of 26/138. A professional orchestra will play it at sight. Rehearse it slowly once, not more.

**7. Intonation spots to schedule in a sectional (no change to the score).** 1–4: bass clarinet and cellos in unison pp, with the basses an octave below, under a ppp timpani roll. This is the first sound of the piece; compare the Tchaikovsky 6/i opening bassoon solo over divided basses (teachbassoon.com excerpt). 47–55: horn and cello in unison, dolce (protected). 103–106: Ob 2, Cl 1 and Cl 2 in unison at f on the chromatic head (e''–e♭''–d'', a♯''–b''). Three different reeds sliding by semitone is the most likely tuning stop in the development. If it stays rough after a sectional, put Cl 2 an octave lower. Not applied, because it would reverse cycle 84's balance decision without new evidence. Brass ranges are moderate: the highest horn note is concert E5 at 106, and there are no high notes after long rests.

**8. Bar 14→15: the Allegro starts out of silence with the upper strings off the beat.** Vln I, Vln II and Vla all begin with `r8`, and only Vc and Cb play the downbeat. The silent beat 4 of 14 is protected, and correctly so. The conductor must beat it as an Allegro upbeat, not as a Lento beat. No change to the score.
