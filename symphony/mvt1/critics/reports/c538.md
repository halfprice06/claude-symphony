I reused that script (copied to /tmp/critic-538/bow.py) on a fresh event dump of v538 (/tmp/critic-538/work/ev2). The script treats an unslurred note as a new bow, a slur as one bow, and a tie as continuing the bow unless the tied note carries a bow mark. An entry after a rest is down-bow on the beat and up-bow off it, and printed marks are obeyed. It flags crescendos on long down-bows, diminuendos on long up-bows, and accented or ff attacks on up-bows.
- I also ran the same script on c438's v438 dump and compared the two outputs bow by bow. This shows which bowings the 100 cycles since v438 have turned round without anyone marking it. Three places changed: the double basses 131–138 (a side effect of c516), Vln II 177–181 (c512's split of 177) and the cellos 143–145 (c505's fresh down-bow on B at 142). ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 131–138: the double basses take the recapitulation downbeat, the hemiola and the 138 stop chord on bows opposite to the cellos. Applied.

**Problem.** c516 put an up-bow on the basses' arco entry at 110 so that the ff G1 pedal at 117 lands down-bow. That fix is right, and it stays. But it also reversed the count of every bow after it. The pedal 117–130 is tied, with "change bow at will", and it runs straight into the ff quavers at 131 with no rest. Since c516 the bowing is:
- 131.1, the ff downbeat of the recapitulation: up-bow;
- 135.1 and 136.3, the accented hemiola beats: up-bow, while the cellos take 135.1 down-bow;
- 138, the f accented stop chord: up-bow, while the cellos take it down-bow.

In v438 (c438's simulation) the basses took 131, 135.1 and 138 down-bow, together with the cellos. This is the scheme c195 protected ("bowing scheme at 15–32 and 131–142"). The change was never marked or discussed.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: the bow is heavier at the frog, so a vigorous attack wants a down-bow. When two strong attacks meet, mark the bowing "so that the following attack will be solid" (Ex. II-20, *Coriolanus*, viewed: ⊓ V over the long note before the ff chord).
- PDF 39–40 and Ex. II-24/25: free or staggered bowing on a long note has to end at a defined point. "Change bow at will" leaves the bow direction at the end of 130 open. Without a mark, half the section may arrive at the recapitulation on an up-bow.
- At the exposition's tutti arrival (27) the basses come in after a rest, so they are down-bow by habit. The recapitulation has no rest, so it needs the mark.

**Fix (recOne.ily, recOneCb).** `c,8\ff\downbow \repeat unfold 7 c,8 | ...`. The mark ends the free bowing and puts 131, 135.1 and 138 back on down-bows with the cellos. It is one mark. 110–130 are unchanged, so c516's down-bow G1 at 117 is kept. The ⊓ prints clear above the first quaver on page 13.

### 2. 98–102: in the B-minor second theme, Violins II bow against the cellos they double in unison. Applied.

**Problem.** At 97.4–102 the B-minor statement of S is played by Vln I, and an octave lower by Vln II (sul G) and the cellos in unison. Vln I and the cellos have the F-sharp pickup at 97.4. Their slur carries it into the held D at 98, so 98 is an up-bow, and then 99 is down, 100 up, 101 down and 102 up. Vln II cannot play the pickup, because F-sharp3 is below the violin's range. It enters on the downbeat of 98 after a bar's rest, so by habit it starts down-bow, and it bows every bar 98–102 opposite to the cellos it doubles:
- 100 (crescendo up to the C-sharp/D peak) and 102 (crescendo into the A minor seam): down-bow, toward the tip;
- 101 (diminuendo from the peak): up-bow, toward the frog.

Vln I and the cellos have the natural direction at all three places. The unison pair Vln II + Vc therefore shapes each swell from opposite ends of the bow, at ff, in the one ff statement of S before the recapitulation.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: a crescendo is natural on the up-bow and a diminuendo on the down-bow; "indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". Here the habit of an entry after a rest gives the wrong direction, and one mark corrects it.
- PDF 37–38, Ex. II-19 (Schubert, viewed): the upbeat sets the bowing of the bars that follow. A part that doubles the line without the upbeat has to be marked to match.
- c338 and c438 made the same correction for the other S statements (46–55, 146–155, 171–174). 97–102 is the only one where a doubling part enters without the pickup.

**Fix (dev.ily, devVlnTwo).** `des'2.~(\ff\<\upbow^\startTextSpan des'8 c'8\>)` inside the `\transpose c cis` block. Then 98 is up, 99 down, 100 up, 101 down and 102 up, the same as the cellos and Vln I. The entry starts in the upper half and grows toward the frog, which is the shape the printed ff< asks for. It is one mark. The V prints under "sul G" without collision (page 9).

### Checked and left as they are

- **Vc 143–145 (after c505).** c505 made the B at 142 a fresh lifted down-bow. As a result the f whole-note C at 143 is up-bow, 144 (mp, diminuendo) is down-bow and the pp D at 144.4–145 is up-bow. Before c505 the pattern was the reverse. The new order is the better one: the diminuendo at 144 is now on a down-bow, as Adler's tendency predicts, and after a lifted ⊓ an up-bow sustained f is the normal continuation (Ex. II-20). Keep.
- **Vln II 177–181 (after c512).** Splitting 177 into A-flat4–F4 adds one bow, so 179–181 change direction. All of it is pp, and 181 carries "change bow at will", so the direction of the last held G4 does not matter. Keep.
- **Vln I and II 133.2, 134.2.** Each accented minim follows a crotchet rest at Allegro. Players take a fresh down-bow there, as at the marked 131.2, so a mark would only restate the habit (PDF 38). Keep.
- **Vla 94–95, Vln II 95 (fugato build).** The crescendo covers only the second half of a 3.8 s down-bow and ends at a rest before the ff entry at 96. c316 protects the two-bar slur. An up-bow mark at 94 would need a double up-bow on the repeated F at f. That costs more than it gains. Keep.
- **Vla and Vc 23–26, 135–138; Vc 26.** The hemiola accents fall on alternate bows and the exposition stop chord comes up-bow. Both are protected (c195, c238, c391), and martelé accents are made on either bow (PDF 45).
- **Vln I 21.3, Vc 122, Vla 162, Vc 1–4 and Cb 1–4.** These were judged by c438, c338 and c226. Nothing has changed there since.
- **Vln I and II 82.1.5, 117.1.5.** These are off-beat entries after a rest. Each is the start of a ff run, and up-bow is the normal habit there (PDF 38).

### Beyond compliance

I looked for a place where a bowing or string-colour instruction from this chapter would make a phrase sing more or land harder. I considered three:
- *au talon* on the 27–32 hammered tutti (Ex. II-28);
- louré in the 47–55 viola pad (Ex. II-30);
- sul ponticello on the 77–81 tremolos (PDF 52).

None of them passes. The tutti already uses lifted down-bows (Ex. II-29), which give the same weight. A louré pad would draw attention away from the horn and cello tune. 77–81 is protected (solo winds over tremolo strings), and the eerie colour would weaken the bar-122 tam-tam effect it leads to. The two marks above are the only changes this reading supports.
