## Section by section against Dvořák 7/i
- **Themes introduced.** Dvořák's first theme (bars 1–8) rises pp out of the violas and cellos over a pedal. Its hook is a neighbour-note cell (f8 e f d e g | f e f d4) that repeats within two bars. In v38 the motto and first theme do the same thing: the cell (rising sixth, then chromatic fall) is sequenced at once in 15/17. That is at the model's level of memorability.

  Dvořák's second theme (73, flutes, dolce) is built from two short cells, each repeated: the sigh d4 c8 es4 d8 | c4 bes8 a4 g8, and the turn f8. g16 f g. v38's second theme (47–55) is longer-breathed, Brahmsian rather than Dvořákian, and its rhythm changes in almost every bar. Its hook is the sixth-leap syncopation in 48 and 51. It is singable but harder to retain on one hearing. The codetta (69–72) brings its head back ff, as Dvořák does at 127–136. It is protected; no change.
- **Transition and retransition.**
  - Dvořák's transition (42–53) leads to a ff dominant block (54–60); v38's 33–46 does the same with the first-theme head in sequence and a medial caesura.
  - Dvořák's retransition (182–195) speeds up its surface rhythm, from tremolo to sixteenth-note figures and chromatic sequences, into a fff recapitulation (196). v38's 119–122 rebuilds from pp to ff in four bars over sustained notes only (Finding 1).
- **Climaxes.** Dvořák's are at about 196 (fff, 62%) and 266 (fff, 85%). v38's are at 114 (64%) and 156 (88%). The curve is the same.
- **Recapitulation.** Both movements recompose the first group. Both bring the second theme back in the tonic major with new accompaniment: Dvořák 214, with the theme's turn figure in sixteenths in Vln I; v38 139, sul tasto. v38's 123–134 (theme low, counterline above, Neapolitan peak at 132) is at master level.
- **Coda.** Dvořák brings his first theme back in its hushed original form (248), builds it to fff (266), then ends with fragments (287–314). v38 compresses the same arc into 163–178: four bars of ff head, the second theme in minor, then the augmented motto. That is valid. The protected proportions keep it short, but the opening four bars copy 27–29 (Finding 3).
- **Surface rhythm and colour.** This is where v38 is clearly not yet at the master's level (Findings 1 and 2).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 40 and 119–122: the Allegro never moves faster than eighth notes (highest priority)
**What is wrong.** Across 164 bars of "Allegro appassionato" there is no note value shorter than an eighth. The only exceptions are the measured tremolos and the triplets at 109–113 (a grep of all sections finds no 16ths). Arrivals are therefore prepared only by dynamics, never by a faster surface.

**Evidence.** Dvořák marks his arrivals with sixteenths: the Vln I sweeps at 28 and 31 (`f16 e cis bes a g`; `r16 a'' g f e d c bes a g f e`), the sixteenth figures driving the retransition 182–185, and the sixteenth arpeggios at the coda's fff (266–271). In v38 the retransition 119–122 is all held notes while it climbs pp→ff in four bars.

**Fixes** (tested; see the end of the report):
- Retransition: `devVc`, dev bars 43–46 (movement 119–122). The cellos make a measured accelerando on the G pedal; the Cb, timpani and motto entries stay as they are.
  ```
  g,4\pp\<^"unis." g,4 g,4 g,4 | g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 |
  \tuplet 3/2 { g,8\mf g,8 g,8 } \repeat unfold 3 { \tuplet 3/2 { g,8 g,8 g,8 } } | g,16\f\< g,16 g,16 g,16 \repeat unfold 12 { g,16 } |
  ```
  The cellos land on their own `r8 g8( es'4.` at 123.
- Transition peak: trans bar 8 (movement 40), with a turn into g''' at 41.
  - `transVlnOne` and `transFluteOne` (flute 2 rests, so the ottava is allowed): `r8 aes''8 \ottava #1 f'''4-> bes''16( c''' d''' es''' f''' es''' f''' fis''') \ottava #0 |`
  - `transOboeOne`: `r8 aes'8 f''4-> bes'16( c'' d'' es'' f'' es'' f'' fis'') |`

  The f♯ is a chromatic lower neighbour over B♭7. No new parallels result (analyze.py still reports only the known bar 96).

### 2. Bars 47–55 and 139–147: the basses are arco throughout; nearly no pizzicato in the movement
**What is wrong.** There are two pizz. marks in 178 bars (Vc 62, Cb 85). Under both lyrical themes the double basses hold arco whole notes pp.

**Evidence.** Dvořák changes his basses between pizz. and arco about twelve times in this movement (Contrabasso file, lines 54–445). Under the second theme the cellos play off-beat pizz. chords at 73–76 (`r8 bes f r c' bes`), arco sixteenth arpeggios at 81, and pizz. again with the basses at 83–86. Plucked bass is the standard Romantic support for a cantabile tenor or treble tune. In the exposition it also matches the harp. In the recapitulation, where the harp rests until 148, it replaces the harp.

**Fix.** Pitches are unchanged. The horn + cello + harp texture (protected) is untouched.
- `tTwoCb`, tTwo bars 1–10:
  ```
  aes,4\pp^"pizz." r4 r2 | des,4 r4 r2 | bes,,4 r4 r2 | es,4 r4 es,4 r4 |
  c,4 r4 r2 | des,4 r4 r2 | bes,,4 r4 r2 | es,4 r4 es,4 r4 |
  aes,,4 r4 r2 | aes,,1\p^"arco" |
  ```
- `recTwoCb`, recTwo bars 1–10:
  ```
  c,4\pp^"pizz." r4 r2 | f,4 r4 r2 | d,4 r4 r2 | g,,4 r4 g,,4 r4 |
  e,4 r4 r2 | f,4 r4 r2 | d,4 r4 r2 | g,4 r4 g,4 r4 |
  c,4 r4 r2 | c,1\mp^"arco" |
  ```
  Bassoon 2 keeps the sustained bass an octave up.

### 3. Bars 163–166: the coda's opening copies 27–29, and the cellos just double the bass (cycle 17 left this open)
**What is wrong.** The ff head at 163–165 is the fourth literal tutti statement at the same pitch (after 27 and 82). Dvořák never repeats a tutti statement of his theme unchanged: compare 16, 27, 196, 253 and 266.

**Fix.** Keep the protected plagal harmony over the tonic pedal (Cb, Cbsn, B.cl, tuba and timpani keep the C). Give the cellos a tenor line in the manner of 27–31. `codaVc`, coda bars 1–4:

`c'2\ff es'2 | es'2 f'2 | es'2 c'2 | d'2.->\ff r4 |`

I avoided the 27–31 shape (`d'2 f'2`) because d' would sit a semitone under trombone 1's held es' at 164. The f' at 164 joins the iv6/4 (aes in bassoons and horns). The d' at 166 belongs to the vii°7, and it rises to the cellos' espr. es' at 167.
