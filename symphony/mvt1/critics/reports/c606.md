# Critic 606: Playability, instrument by instrument (v606)

Reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 2 Bowed String Instruments: divisi, bowing, harmonics (adler1 PDF 24-70), fourth pass. I read every text page from PDF 24 to 70 in full. I rendered every page from PDF 27 to 70 at 110 dpi and looked at each one (Ex. II-1 to II-77). Earlier passes of this reading were c226, c316, c406, c426 and c516, and c438/c538 used it for bowing as expression. Their points are not repeated. The chapter covers only the strings, so this report checks the five string parts in the score and in the parts that build-parts.sh produces.

Method. The bow simulator that c516 wrote is no longer on disk, so I wrote a new one (/tmp/critic-606/scripts/bows.py). It reads the LilyPond event dump of v606 (the version folder's ev2/ev.ly with el.ly, re-run on v606; output in /tmp/critic-606/evrun/) and the tempo map of full.midi. It follows Adler's rules. An unslurred note takes a new bow (PDF 37) and a slur or tie continues the bow (PDF 37). An entry after a rest is down-bow on a main beat and up-bow off it (PDF 38). Printed down-bow and up-bow marks are obeyed. Otherwise the bows alternate. Each bow gets its length in seconds. The script lists long bows, more than six notes on a bow at f, loud attacks on an up-bow, and every marked bow that repeats the direction of the bow before it with no rest, which is a retake. Separately I listed every simultaneous pair in each string part and checked string, finger and divisi status.

## Findings

No change is applied. The four items below are the places where the reading raises a question about this version. I checked each one and recommend keeping it as written.

### 1. Double basses 160-161: a down-bow retake after the fff whole note. Checked and kept.

The bass line from the arco entry at 156 is a series of separate whole notes: B♭1 mp< (156, down-bow by habit), F1 (157, up), G1 ff (158, down), G1 cresc. (159, up), A♭1 fff (160, down), then D2 at 161 marked down-bow. 160→161 is the only place in the movement where a marked bow repeats the direction of a sustained loud note with no rest or phrase lift. My retake scan listed 48 such cases. All the others are accented down-bow series (32, 72, 83, 142), hooks, or new phrases after slurred quavers (158.1 after 157). The bass section must lift at the point at the end of the fff A♭1 and set the bow down again at the frog. Adler's remedy (PDF 38, Ex. II-20, Coriolanus) is to mark a down-bow and an up-bow over the long note, "so that the following attack will be solid". The score already does this for the basses at 12 (c316) and 166 (c338). The fix would be `aes,,2~\fff\downbow aes,,2\upbow | d,2\downbow g,,2`.

I am not recommending it, for three reasons:
- The bass group already breaks at this bar line on purpose. Tuba and bass trombone have `\breathe` at the end of 160 (and of 158). The contrabassoon and timpani move to D/G at 161 with a new attack. A seamless A♭1 in the basses would be the only bass line that does not articulate the tritone A♭→D. The retake gives the same short break as the brass and a heavy frog attack on the D2, which is what the down-bow mark asks for.
- The other basses leave the 161 attack weak only if it comes up-bow. Taking the mark away instead would put 162's ff diminuendo on an up-bow, against the habit (PDF 38).
- The note is 2.4 s at largamente (crotchet = 100) and fff. The bass bow is short (PDF 39), so the A♭1 already uses the whole down-bow. Splitting it into two half-bows halves the bow length per beat on the peak chord. That is a weaker sound on the ♭VI climax, whose string plan is protected (c584).

The record should say that the retake is intended and matches the brass breaths. Later critics should not flag it again.

### 2. Violas 158-161 after c604: the new B3/F4 double stop. Checked and kept.

c604 changed beats 1-2 of 161 from D4/F4 to B3/F4, so the violas now play four bars of détaché quaver double stops at ff-fff without divisi: C4/E4 (158), B3/E4 then B3/F4 (159), C4/E♭4 (160), B3/F4 (161). Each pair lies on the G and D strings (PDF 29-30). B3/F4 is a diminished fifth. It is fingered with two adjacent fingers a semitone apart across the strings, the second finger on B3 and the first on F4, so it is not the impossible same-finger case. The changes from C4/E♭4 to B3/F4 move by step in both voices inside one hand position. Eight bows per bar make an even number, so 161 starts down-bow with the violins' marked down-bow. Undivided double stops are protected (c416, c436), and Adler says unmarked double notes are played as double stops (PDF 30). That is the intended sound: every player holds the whole chord in the inner pulse of the climax. The change is playable as written.

### 3. Long soft bows in the coda. Checked and kept.

The longest bow without a "change bow at will" mark is Vln I 177.4-179.3: the sul D pickup G4 slurred to E♭5 and tied to 179.3. It lasts 8.8 s at pp under the rit. into Tempo I. Adler warns against overloading the bow even at soft dynamics in slow passages (PDF 39) and gives staggered bowing as the remedy (PDF 40, Ex. II-25). At pp, a violin full bow sustains this length near the fingerboard, and the section can change unnoticed on the tie at 179.1 where Tempo I begins. Every longer hold from 179 to 182 in all five parts carries "change bow at will". This is the last motto, protected in HISTORY.md ("Vln I's last motto 177-182"). Adding a printed bow change at 179.1 would flip the bowing of the D♭ neighbour 180.3 to a down-bow under the diminuendo, which is the habit's direction, but then 179.4's slurred D5-C5 would start up-bow at the start of Tempo I. That gains nothing. The viola's E♮4 at 182 is a new bow at ppp under the fermata, so the cyclic E♮ gets a fresh, quiet bow and is not clipped from a tie.

### 4. Colour devices from the chapter: is there a more vivid string colour a master would use? No change.

I looked again for places where a device from the chapter would make a passage more vivid in the manner of the models. I looked at harmonics (PDF 62-68), fingered and undulating tremolo (PDF 50-51, Debussy Ex. II-49, Brahms 1/iv Ex. II-50), sul ponticello (PDF 52), punta d'arco (PDF 41) and mutes (PDF 59).
- The development opening 77-81 (Vln II/Vla unmeasured tremolo under solo winds; c597 left "tremolo vs quaver figure" open) could take a slurred fingered tremolo E♭4-G♭4 / C♭4-E♭3 as in Ex. II-49. It would add a second moving layer under the solo winds, which are protected there ("solo winds over tremolo strings 77-81"). The texture's point is a still halo under a moving solo (Adler adler1 PDF 135, background radically different from foreground). The unmeasured tremolo is the right choice.
- Natural harmonics cannot give the C5, G4 or E4 held into movement II. The table on PDF 65-66 has no E4 harmonic on the viola and only stopped options for C5 on the D string. Sul D C5 at ppp is the right sound.
- The Brahms 1/iv example of pizzicato basses under a long arco line (PDF 54-55, Ex. II-55) is already the model for 47-55 and 147-155.
