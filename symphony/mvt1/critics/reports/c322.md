# Critic 322: Harmony and voice leading (v322)

Reading: Aldwell & Schachter, Unit 9 "Leading to V: IV, II and II6" and Unit 10 "The Cadential 6/4" (aldwell-schachter PDF 134–162), second pass. I read the text file of every page from PDF 134 to 162 and rendered every page as an image at 110 dpi. I also rendered Ex. 9-24 at 250 dpi, because the OCR loses its S/W marks. Ex. 9-24a is in 3/4: IV sits on the weak third beat and II6 follows on the next downbeat over the repeated bass. The PDF has no book page 151 (Unit 10 §§9–11, noncadential 6/4s): it jumps from book p. 150 (PDF 156) to p. 152 (PDF 157). PDF 151 is book p. 142, which covers the Schoenberg sentence and the Unit 9 review.

Method: I dumped every beat of `v322/full.midi`, and every half-beat in the cadence areas, listing the bass, the pitch classes and each instrument's pitch. I wrote three scripts:
- a six-four finder (bass + 4th + 6th, no 3rd or 5th);
- a scan for weak-to-strong bass repetitions under a change of chord (Ex. 9-24);
- a scan for melodic Ab↔B and Db↔E steps (the augmented second of Ex. 9-6c and 9-7f).

I then checked each intermediate harmony and each cadential 6/4 against the reading. vl.txt holds only doublings, plus the known bar-162 fifth (c222).

### Checked and kept (no change)

1. **52→53, 152→153, 172→173: IV (iv7) becomes II6 (ii6/5) on a downbeat over a repeated bass.** These are the only consonant weak-to-strong bass repetitions with a change of chord that the scan found outside pedals and suspensions. They match the letter of Ex. 9-24a, which is marked "poor" (PDF 149 §15, review 9 on PDF 151). The 250-dpi image shows what the example is about, though: there IV lasts only one weak beat of a 3/4 bar before the II6 downbeat. In this score IV holds a whole bar (52, 152, 172) and the 6 arrives at the barline over the held bass. That is the 5–6 motion A&S give as a model in Mozart's K. 387 (Ex. 9-20, PDF 146): I6 in bar 31, IV through bar 32, the 6 on the downbeat of 33, V in 34, with the same hypermetric position. The meter is not contradicted, because the bar of IV is itself a complete harmonic unit. Keep. (This confirms c232's row 9.)

2. **101→102: V7 of B minor, then a 6/4 over the same F♯ (B in Bn 1, Hn 1·2·4, Tbn 1, Va), then V7 at 102.3.** The approach breaks review item 7 (PDF 160): a cadential 6/4 is not approached from V. The 4th also enters from below (A♯3→B3), which PDF 153 does not list. But the chord does not work as a cadential 6/4. The bass is held, the 4th goes A♯–B–A♯, and the 6th goes C♯–D–C♯ (Fl/Ob/Vln I D6 at 102.2–3, C♯6 at 102.4). So it is a neighbour 6/4 that prolongs V. A&S separate this kind from the cadential 6/4 when they say that "a 6/4 on the dominant is not restricted to cadences" (PDF 152, §1). The point was raised at c122 and closed at c123 ("correct as a neighbouring 6/4"), and the block is protected (c231, c248: brass counter, viola arpeggio, bass). The reading gives no reason to reopen it. Keep. The same applies to the 6/4s inside the G pedal at 118, 121 and 125 (c263).

3. **174: the 4th C5 sounds in both the cor anglais (held from 173.4) and the solo cello (from A♭4).** Review item 4 on PDF 160 says "never double the 4th", and Ex. 10-5c marks a doubled 4th "wrong". Here the two instruments meet in unison: the cor anglais holds the prepared C (the motto's last C), stops at 174.3, and the cello alone resolves it to B4. In voice-leading terms this is one voice handed from one instrument to another, so there is only one 4th, and it is prepared and resolved. The viola's E♭4–D4 makes the 6–5. The coda notes are protected (c228, c249, c270 "solo cello's unresolved B4 at 174"). Keep.

4. **154.1: the 4th C in Vln II (lower), violas, Hn 1 and cellos.** This is the recapitulated S tune in octaves, with Vln II in unison with the violas, so the 4th is one melodic line and not a doubled chord tone (PDF 154 §5). It enters by step in Vln II (D5–C5, the APT of Ex. 10-3b). In the tune it comes back to C after an arpeggiation inside ii6/5 while the cor anglais holds C5 (Ex. 10-3c). Every copy falls to B on beat 2. Keep (c312 protects 154→155).

5. **142.1 and 106.1: 4–3 over V without a 6th (5/4).** Ex. 10-2a (PDF 153) derives the cadential 6/4 from exactly this 4–3 suspension. At 142 the C is held from iiø7 and doubled only in the fff tutti (PDF 154: "very free or complex textures"). At 106 the E is prepared at 105.3 in the same horn and arpeggiated up to E5. Both resolve down. Keep.

6. **22.2: C5 (1̂) over V on beat 2 of 22.** This is not a cadential 4th but an anticipation of the tonic at 22.3, which is held into the tonic. The accented 3̂ (E♭5 at 22.1, the 6th of V) satisfies §8 (PDF 156). Keep (P 15–22 is protected; the finale quotes the P head).
