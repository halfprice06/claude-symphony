## Findings

No fix is needed in this cycle. Nothing is applied in the scratch copy. The items below are the places where the reading or the Schubert comparison raised a real question. Each one is answered with a reason to keep the passage. They are ranked by how likely a later critic is to raise them again.

### 1. 117–121: brass ff against woodwind ff. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 112 says to mark the woodwind one degree louder than the brass in difficult balances. Ex. 180 (PDF 349) puts the trumpets and trombones at f under woodwind and violins at ff.

Score: at 117–121, Hn 1–4, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and Tuba hold G at ff. The woodwind and violins play the rising P calls, also ff.

Verdict: R-K's rule is for a woodwind melody over brass harmony. Here the brass line is the motto itself (protected as "two ff lines 117–121" and "brass-only motto"). The brass is a unison line, not a chord, so PDF 114 on balance in long chords does not apply. Ex. 142 (PDF 316) shows the same thing: R-K juxtaposes full wind and brass at f with two lines of equal weight. Lowering the brass to f would also flatten the climb 117 → 122 fff. No change.

### 2. 160: the third, C, is doubled in ten parts. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 101–103 sets out the brass layout for the whole group: close order, horns doubled in pairs, and horns one degree louder when they double the trumpets or trombones.

Score: the brass chord at 160 is Tpt C5–A♭4–E♭4, Tbn C4–E♭3, B.tbn A♭2, Tuba A♭1. Hn 1·2 are on C4 and Hn 3·4 on E♭4, so each pair doubles a brass note. The horns are fff, and Tpt 3, the trombones and the tuba are ff. This is R-K's model at PDF 102–103, note for note. C is doubled in ten parts, but it is the melody note (Fl, Vln I, Tpt 1), and R-K's tutti examples Ex. 202–206 double the melodic third the same way. The pitches of 156–166 are protected (c346). No change.

### 3. 122: the ♭9 (A♭) is doubled in nine parts. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 102 and the c249 notes say to give the discords of a chord different colours.

Score: A♭ sounds in Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Hn 1, Tpt 1, Bsn 1, Tbn 1 and Vc, across four octaves. The A♭ is the climax note of the movement (the three A♭ climaxes are protected) and the top of a ♭9 dominant that is listed as do-not-touch. Every A♭ lies at least a ninth above the G bass. Hn 1 and Tpt 1 share A♭4, which is the brass duplication R-K recommends at PDF 103. No change.

### 4. 41: horns ff over woodwind sf. Keep.

Reading: R-K PDF 108 says horns at f overpower the woodwind.

Score: at 41 the horns play the motto a2 ff. The woodwind and strings hold the chord sf > mf, and the trumpets and trombones are f. Here the horns overpowering the woodwind is the intended result. The woodwind is the harmony and the horns are the tune, which is R-K's split at PDF 115–116. No change.

### 5. 103–106: Hn 3 rests added by c347. Not a regression; keep.

Compared with v339: Hn 3 used to re-strike D4 at 104.1 and move straight on to E4 at 105.3. Hn 1 attacks the same pitches with the S head at those two points. Now Hn 3 rests there. R-K PDF 108 says the stationary part belongs in the brass and the moving part in the woodwind or the tune, and the rests keep that apart. The line is still slurred in two halves, so c188's legato inner line survives as two phrases. I heard no gap in the MIDI. Hn 4 and the bassoons hold through. No change.

### 6. Coda 171–182 against Schubert's più moto coda. Keep.

Schubert (score pages 87–103): the coda starts p in faster triplets (Più moto). It builds twice to ff on the trombone theme, then states the introduction's horn theme ff in unison strings, with fz on each note under sustained wind chords. It ends on short tutti chords.

Score: the coda here goes the other way: Più lento, Vc solo, trombone chorale pp, the motto ppp, attacca into movement II. A conductor would accept this as a planned difference, not a weaker copy. The loud recapitulation of the motto has already been heard at 167–170, ff in Hn and Tpt a2, which does Schubert's job of a brass theme over the tutti. The quiet end is protected and tied to movement II. R-K Ex. 209 (PDF 375) supports the 170 → 171 change: tutti ff into a fermata, then a solo. No change.

### 7. Re-scoring of returns, measured against Schubert. Keep.

Schubert moves the opening horn theme from two horns (p) to oboes and clarinets over strings, to a ff tutti, and in the coda to unison strings ff. Every return gets new scoring.

Score: the motto goes 13 Hn 1 solo mp → 41 Hn a2 ff → 117 Hn/Tbn ff → 167 Hn and Tpt a2 ff → 179–181 Tbn/Hn ppp. The S theme goes 47 Hn 1 + Vc with harp → 56 Cl + Hn 2 → 69 Fl and Vln I f → 103 Hn 1 + Vc → 107 Vla + Fl 1 → 147 Vla + Vc under Hn 1 → 158 tutti ff → 171 Vc solo. That is R-K PDF 118–119 (Ex. 176–203) in full, and as thorough as Schubert's plan. No change.

## Holistic verdict

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? On the evidence of this reading, yes.

- **Brass chords** at 27, 122 and 160 follow R-K's layouts on PDF 101–103 exactly: close order, paired horns doubling the trumpets and trombones, and the horns one degree louder where they double.
- **Group dynamics** keep the tune above the harmony in every tutti (PDF 112, 115–116), and the crescendo at 111–117 adds groups in R-K's order (Ex. 157).
- **Each return of a theme is re-scored**, as R-K requires (PDF 118–119). Schubert's first movement does the same, and this score does it as consistently.
- **What Schubert has and this movement lacks** is length and a loud closing apotheosis. Both differences are deliberate here: a 14% coda that leads attacca into movement II.
