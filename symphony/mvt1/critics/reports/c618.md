## Findings

No finding meets the bar for a score change. The items below are the places the reading bears on most directly, ranked by how much they could matter, with the reason each stays as it is.

### 1. Bars 91–93: the cellos' second countersubject is a Jupiter-type suspension chain, unmarked (no change)

What is there. In the fugato's third entry (subject in C.a./Vln II on C, 91), the cellos play `r4 c'2\f es'4~( | es'4 des'4) c'2~( | c'4 bes4) f2`. At 92.1 the tied E♭4 is a 4th over the bass B♭1 (Cb, Bsn 2, B.cl) and resolves to D♭4 on 92.2 (4-3 in B♭ minor, with F and A♭ above). At 93.1 the tied C4 is a major 7th over D♭2 and resolves to B♭3 on 93.2 (7-6, with F above, the "good" 7-6 doubling of Ex. 21-9a/b). The figure then passes to the violas' free voice at 94–95 (A♭4 tied into 95, 4-3 over E♭), an indirect continuation of the same device (PDF 357 §17). Neither earlier pass named this chain. The scan gives preparation and resolution equal velocity (95/95), while the subject's accented notes are at 115.

Why no mark is needed. This is the texture of Ex. 21-1 (PDF 346), Mozart's syncopated counterpoint against the Jupiter subject, which is marked only f, with ties and no accents. The reading's point (PDF 348 §4) is that a dissonant suspension brings its voice forward by itself; the accent and softer resolution are what the performer does once the suspension is recognised. A tenuto cannot be written on the tied half of a note, and an accent on the preparation (91.2, 92.3) would stress the consonant note, the opposite of what §4 asks. The fugato is protected for notes (c327, c337, c607); its marks follow the reading. Keep.

### 2. Bar 11: the violas' prepared seventh resolves inside the crescendo to the German sixth (no change)

What is there. The violas hold C4 from 10 into 11, where the bass moves to D: a prepared seventh over iiø7 (c132), resolving to B3 on 11.3 (PDF 357–358 §18, dissonant preparation type). The scan shows the resolution louder (84 → 89), the only louder soft-dynamic resolution not covered by c418/c518. The reason is the one-line pp< crescendo of the rising-sixth chain 9–12 that arrives f on the German sixth at 12.

Why keep it. This is the reading's own model for a suspension inside a dynamic approach: Mozart marks "cresc." through the preparation of the 7-6 chain (Ex. 21-17, PDF 355). The viola is an inner voice under a rising chain whose goal is 12; a > on the B would cut against the crescendo of every other part. Bars 1–14 are also quoted by the finale (bar 12 = IV 1; release checks require the MIDI of 1–14 to stay identical to v421), so a velocity change here would also touch quoted bars. Keep.

### 3. Bars 125–126: the horn-call recall over the G pedal (new since c518; no change)

Hn 1 `r4 es4(\mp espr. c'2~ | c'4\> b2)\p` (c581): the C4 is the 4 of the i6/4 over the G pedal and resolves to B3 on 126.2 as V arrives. The diminuendo starts on the tied C4 and reaches p on the B, so the dissonance is the peak and the resolution softer (PDF 348 §§3–4), as at 13–14. Hn 3, Cl 2, violas and cellos restrike C4 at p on 126.1 and resolve to B3 at equal level while the pedal parts fall to pp below them (velocity 69/69). The upper-voice dissonances over the pedal are prepared and resolved (PDF 368). It follows the reading. Keep.

### 4. Bars 158–159: Tbn 1 E4–D4 and the crescendo through V7 (no change)

Since c614 Tbn 1 holds E4 at 158 and moves to D4 in 159: the 6-5 of the cadential 6/4 (PDF 351–352 §11). The resolution chord V7 at 159 carries the crescendo into the fff ♭VI at 160. The resolution is louder, but the crescendo's goal is the deceptive arrival, not the V7. This is the same case c318 kept at 63 and 159 (phrase-length crescendo through a resolution, Ex. 21-17), and the violas' E4–F4 at 159 (c522) and the 7/6 E6 over V7 (Ex. 21-15f, PDF 353–354) are already shaped by the tenuto on E6. Keep.

### 6. Where the music could go further (considered and tested in thought, not proposed)

I looked for a moment where a suspension or pedal could carry more feeling, using the reading's own models.
- A < > swell on Vln I's D♭5 over the C pedal at 180 (as the cellos at bar 3). c418 rejected it because the coda's levels are protected and the ending should stay still; PDF 368 says a dissonance against a pedal need not be stressed or resolved. That reason stands.
- A tenuto on the 4 of the cadential 6/4 at 66.3 and 164.3. The phrase peaks on the note before and the diminuendo begins on the 6/4, which is already its loudest point; the tie gives the delayed resolution (Ex. 21-27, PDF 360). c518 named these bars as master-level. Adding a mark would not improve them.
- A subito p at the start of the wedge (111), as Mozart does at the start of his 7-6 chain (Ex. 21-17). The score already drops from the 110 crescendo to "mp cresc." at 111, and 110–117 is one protected crescendo; a stronger drop would weaken the run into 117.
- An accent on the leaping anticipations (Ex. 21-34). The anticipations at 12.4, 157.4½ and 161.4½ enter by step or are tied, so the Schubert case does not arise.

None of these improves the score enough to justify a change.
