## Overall
The cyclic planting is good. The motto (G–E♭–D–(D♭)–C) produces T1 (it is T1's head in diminution, bar 15), the intro's rising sixths (9–11, including A♭→F, which plants T2's own sixth), T2 (C–B♭–A♭, the falling step stretched), the A♭-major darkened motto in the flute (73–75), the development dialogue (77–84), the trombone augmentation (109–113), and the coda canon (167–177). One pivot ties the movement together: E♭ against E♮. The intro shows it at bar 8, where Vln II e' over C gives a C-major sonority before E♭ returns at 9. The coda shows it in the last bar, where the viola moves E♭→E♮. The violins' entry at 147–148 (g'→e''2.~ e''8 d''8 | c''4) is already the motto in C major. That is the seed the plan needs for movement III's major chorale. The movement holds the full major apotheosis of the motto back for the finale, as Tchaikovsky 5/i does. That restraint is correct.

I found one real weakness in the arc and one useful timbral improvement. Everything else I would leave alone.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 162 → 163: the movement's main reversal has no cause (recTwo bar 24)
**Problem.** The recapitulated T2 closes with a complete perfect cadence in C major: IV–ii–V–I at 159–162, top voice c''1 mf, under rit. Then at 163 the coda strikes C minor ff with no preparation. This is the emotional hinge of the whole movement: the major-mode hope of 139–162 is cancelled. At present it is a splice, not an event. The listener hears two blocks side by side rather than one turning into the other.

**Evidence.** In Dvořák 9/i (bars 400–423), the move from the long major-coloured pedal back to minor is caused by chromatic voice-leading: dim7 arpeggios at 408–411 and a chromatic fall from C7 at 412–415. Only after that do the brass restate the theme at 416–423. Tchaikovsky 6/i (305–347) recapitulates T2 in the tonic major and does not reverse, so he needs no agent. Here the movement does reverse, so it needs one. Tchaikovsky 5 makes the minor/major vacillation of the motto itself the narrative (sources above). This score already owns the agent: the E♭/E♮ inner voice of bar 8 and bar 178.

**Fix.** On beats 3–4 of 162, let the E♮ voices sink to E♭ and crescendo into the coda's ff. This also gives the 177–178 viola E♭→E♮ its mirror: E♮→E♭ opens the coda and E♭→E♮ closes it. Top C, bass and the other horns stay as they are.
- `recTwoSync`, last element: `<e' g'>1\mp |` → `<e' g'>2\mp <es' g'>2\< |` (violas)
- `recTwoCounter`, last element: `e'1\mp |` → `e'2\mp es'2\< |` (Cl 1)
- `recTwoHnOne`, last element: `e'1\mp |` → `e'2\mp es'2\< |`
- `recTwoArp`, last element: `c,8(\p g, c e g2) |` → `c,8(\p g, c e es2\<) |` (cellos)
- `recTwoHarpL`, last element: `c,8(\p g, c e g2) |` → `c,8(\p g, c e g4) r4 |` (so the harp's E♮ does not ring under the E♭)
- `recTwoTimp`: `{ R1*15 | g,1:32\ff | g,1:32 | aes,1:32\fff | g,2:32\> r2\! | R1*4 | r2 c2:32\p\< | }`

Each hairpin ends on the coda's existing `\ff` at 163. Keep the rit. at 161–162: a crescendo inside a ritardando, released into a tempo ff, is the standard gesture. This touches nothing on the do-not-touch list (154–158 and 163–166 are unchanged).

### 2. Coda 167–170: give the horn's motto to the cor anglais, which recalls the intro and separates the two combined themes
**Problem.** At 167–170 the cellos play T2's head in C minor (`es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'4( aes'2) g'4 ...`), and Hn 1 answers one bar later with the motto (`g4( | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'2 des'4 c'4~ | c'2 b2)`). This canon on the shared E♭–D is the best cyclic idea in the coda: it shows that T2 and the motto are one shape. But horn and cello share the same register (E♭3–C5) and a similar warm timbre, so the imitation blurs into a single line. Also, the cor anglais is the intro's answering voice (5–8, the motto on D, "espr."), and it never returns at the end, so the intro/coda frame lacks its most distinctive colour.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov's *Principles* (link above) treats contrast of timbre as the means of keeping simultaneous melodies distinct. In Dvořák 9/i the intro's cyclic pre-echo (16–18) is defined by colour (horns 3·4 with violas and cellos), and the movement's later statements keep that colour. The CA's G3 is low but idiomatic at p; its bottom note is E3.

**Fix.** Add the variable (16 bars):
```
codaCorAnglais = {
  R1*4 | r2. g4(\p^"espr." | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'2 des'4 c'4~ | c'2 b2) | R1*8 |
}
```
In `codaHnOne`, replace bars 167–170 `r2. g4(\p | es'2.~ es'8 d'8 | c'2 des'4 c'4~ | c'2 b2) |` with `R1*4 |`. If you would rather keep the horn colour, keep Hn 1 but mark it `\pp` under the CA `\p`. Hn 1 and Hn 2 are part-combined, and Hn 2 rests here, so there is no dynamic conflict.

## Notes for movements II and III (no change to this score)
- The ending is a tierce de Picardie, not the plan's "ends C minor". This is well precedented: Brahms 1/i ends in C major before a C-minor-to-major finale, and Tchaikovsky 6/i ends in major. Because the bare C-major chord comes from a viola E♮, movement II in A♭ can begin with that E♮ falling back to E♭ (the fifth of A♭). That is the same chromatic voice a third time and would make the link audible.
- Two slow forms of the motto coexist: G–E♭–D–C–D♭–C (1–4, 167–177) and G–E♭–D–D♭–C (109–113, the T1 head). Both are fine here; changing 109–113 would break the harmony (bar 112's B♭-D♭-F triplets need des'). For the movement-III chorale, use the intro form, transposed to major as G–E–D–C (it is already heard at 147–148). Tchaikovsky keeps his motto's rhythm and intervals fixed across movements and changes only mode and scoring.

## Do not touch
These are at a master's level: the E♭/E♮ pivot at bars 8–9 and the viola E♭→E♮ at 177–178; the violins' major-mode motto at 147–148; T2 heard in A♭, then B minor (97–102), C major (139) and C minor (167); the closing flute's A♭ motto with F♭ (73–76); the cello/horn canon on E♭–D (167–170, whoever plays the horn line); the augmented motto at 171–175 under the violins' last motto at 173–177.
