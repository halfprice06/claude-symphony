## Findings

### 1. Bars 156–166: the first bassoon plays below the second for four bars (applied)

What is wrong. In the recapitulation's climax and dissolution the two bassoons share a staff and play different notes at 156, 162, 164 and 166. Each time Bsn 1 has the lower note and Bsn 2 the upper one:

| Bar | Bsn 1 (v314) | Bsn 2 (v314) |
|---|---|---|
| 156 | F2 (mp <) | B♭2 |
| 162 | E2 (ff >, whole note) | A2 (dotted minim) |
| 164 | D2 (mp) | D3 |
| 166 | C2 (p) | C3 |

In the score the part-combiner prints these as chords (the bassoon staff on PDF pages 15–17), so a reader takes the upper note as Bsn 1, which is wrong. In the parts, the principal plays the low E2, D2 and C2 at ff > and p, while the second player, whose job is the low register, sits an octave above. At 166→167 Bsn 1 then has to leap two octaves, C2 to C4, into the Tempo dell'Allegro.

Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 90, wood-wind rule 2: "The normal order of register must be followed, except in the case of crossing or enclosure of parts", and PDF 98: the student should write "in its normal order of distribution ... use the methods of crossing and enclosure of timbres with full knowledge of what he is doing". A crossing inside one pair gains nothing, because both players have the same colour, and the crossing methods of PDF 91–93 are all about mixing two different colours. PDF 95 (diagram marked bad, viewed): crossing "is not so favourable, as octaves will be produced contrary to the natural order of register". At 164 and 166 the pair plays exactly such an octave, D2/D3 and C2/C3, with the first player on the bottom. Nothing in the bars explains the crossing. At 156 Bsn 2 doubles the cellos' B♭2 and Bsn 1 the horns' F3 an octave down, and either player could take either note.

This is not covered by any protection. "Climax scoring 158–166" and "harp voicings 147–166" concern the tune, the brass and the harp. c146's reason for the bassoon split ("neither sustains the pp bass for 18 s and Bsn 2 rests before its long 156–174 stretch") is about who plays when, and that does not change.

Fix (recTwo.ily). The two players swap notes in the four bars; rhythms, dynamics and the part-combine modes stay as they are. At 162 the whole note goes with the low E2 to Bsn 2, and the c135 rest override goes with the crotchet rest to Bsn 1.

### Kept after checking against the reading (no change recommended)

- **28–30, Bsn 1 + Vc on the bass's D4, E♭4, E4 on each downbeat.** c224 removed Tpt 3 and Hn 2 from the chromatic bass but left this line. It is not a copy of the bass. The basses play quavers, Bsn 1 and the cellos play half notes that start on the bass's step and leap a third up on beat 3. This is the case Rimsky allows on PDF 84, "a certain figure and its essentials, in simplified form, may proceed concurrently" (the example has the quaver figure, a crotchet form and a minim form together). The finale also quotes 27–32 at pitch. Keep.
- **122, Hn 4 G3 a semitone under the A♭3 of Tbn 1, Bsn 1 and Vc at fff.** PDF 85 and 96 want close intervals at the top, not in the tenor. But Hn 4's G3 is the inner pedal that runs 117–124, and PDF 84 says "upper and inner pedal notes are more effective on the orchestra than in pianoforte or chamber music". c302 kept it for the same reason. Keep.
- **161.1, vii°7 over D: D sounds in eleven players (Cb, Cbsn, tuba, Bsn 1·2, B.tbn, Tpt 3, Va, Cl 2) and the leading tone B only in Tbn 1.** This goes against PDF 83–84. But Tbn 1 counts for two horns (PDF 51), and the chord lasts two largamente beats. The cleanest fix, Tpt 3 B3 instead of D4, would make a forte trumpet B→C at 161→162, which is reserved for the finale (c242). The viola is the only other candidate, and it would lose its moving D4→B3. Keep.
- **131–134, Cl 1·2 in unison with Bsn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and Vc on the low P theme.** By PDF 51–52 the clarinets' colour is lost under two trombones. c147's objection to moving them up still holds: the counterline in Ob 1/Vln II runs at E♭5–A♭4 in a different rhythm, and the clarinets would cross it. Resting them was closed at c197. Keep.
- **35, Cl 1·2 bare fourth; 159.3, Ob 1 F5 under Ob 2 G5; harp f at 162.** These were already answered by c239, c241 and c197/c246, and the reading gives no new reason (rule 4 allows stressed discords; PDF 51 on the harp is offset because the brass fall to mp inside bar 162).
