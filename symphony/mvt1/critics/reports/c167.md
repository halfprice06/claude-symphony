- Web: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, Project Gutenberg text (https://www.gutenberg.org/files/33900/33900-h/33900-h.htm), ch. I–II: "the addition of one wind instrument to all or part of the strings in unison only thickens the resonance of the latter"; "melodies confided to the violas are generally doubled by other strings or by the wood-wind"; and "the large number of violins prevents the wood-wind predominating". That last point is why I limit Finding 2 to one flute over a soft viola line and over a single-octave violin answer, not a full tutti.

Summary: by Sebesky's voicing rules this string writing is sound. Divisi is economical, no seconds sit high, no close-block voicing, the lead is doubled an octave lower where it should be, and the violins are simple in the tuttis. The inner parts are independent: Vln II doubles Vln I less than in either model. I found two places where his rules on bass grounding and on wind-string balance are broken. A third item is a consequence of the first.

## Findings

### F1 (applied). 56–59: the bass root is sounded four times under the p cantabile violin tune

**Problem.** At 56–59 (violins in unison, p, *molto cantabile*, sul A) the bass note A♭/B♭/D♭/E♭ comes from four sources:
- Cb arco whole notes (p);
- Bsn 2 at the same pitch (pp);
- harp LH octaves on beat 1;
- the cello arpeggio, whose first note is the root.

This is Sebesky's "grounded" voicing. Ex. 195 (PDF 145): adding the root in the bottom cello "would have 'grounded' the voicing, making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating". PDF 140, rule 7: the root is "the least important note"; doubling it by habit "can also 'cloud' the clarity of the bass line"; "bottom-rich" sound is "fresher ... if used sparingly". PDF 146: the bottom cello's roots are for "temporary richness and depth" or chord identity, "but not to reinforce the bass". The Gutenberg Rimsky passage on wind thickening a string line points the same way, and so did c149 F4 (Rimsky PDF 139, sustained bass notes doubled by wind under a melody).

**Why the protection does not block it.** Cycle 149 proposed this and the composer did not apply it, citing c126 and c127:
- c126 protects the Cb's arco sustain under the cello arpeggios. That stays.
- c127 protects the accompaniment design (viola dyads, cello arpeggio, harp). None of that changes.

Bsn 2 at 56–59 is a doubling of the Cb, not part of the design. From 60, where the violins open into octaves at mf, the bassoon's extra weight is what rule 6 asks for, so it re-enters there.

**Fix (tTwo.ily, tTwoBsnTwo):** `R1*13 | ges2\pp^\markup \bold "2." es2 | fes1 | es2. r4 |` (was `R1*9 | aes,1\pp^"2." | aes,1 | bes,2 des2 | es1 | ges2 es2 | …`).

### F2 (applied). 64–65 and 109–110: a solo flute doubling a string line is marked as loud as, or louder than, the strings

**64–65.** Fl 1 doubles the Vln I answer at unison (A♭5–D♭5) at f, the violins' own level. Here Vln II is the lower octave, so the flute is a third of the line's weight at equal marking. Sebesky PDF 164: the flute on the lead violin "thickens the string tone considerably, sacrificing purity for extra body". He uses it at mf or louder, as here, but "to compensate somewhat for the difference in tonal weight, I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings". The reason the flute enters at 64 (c149: the soft peak at 62–63 stays unsupported, the f answer gets support) is kept.

**109–110.** Fl 1 joins the violas' *espr.* second-theme head in unison at **mp** over the violas' **p**. In the MIDI the flute is 8–15 velocity steps above the violas. c109 limited the flute to 109–110 because "strings predominate" (Rimsky PDF 76), and a louder marking works against that. This is the only place in the movement where a wind is marked above the string line it doubles in a soft passage.

**Fix.**
- tTwo.ily, tTwoFluteOne: `aes''2.~(\mf^\markup \bold "1." …` (was `\f`).
- dev.ily, devFluteOne at 109: `des''4.(\pp^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "espr." } …` (was `\mp`).

The f/p contrast of 103–110 (protected) is unchanged: 107–110 stays in the p group.

### F3 (note, no action). 56–68: horn pad and viola dyads share the counterline's register

Sebesky PDF 166 has horns in lower-middle chords alternate with violas and cellos as a colour change. He also wants each unit complete in itself (PDF 149, 168). At 56–68 three middle-register layers sound at once:
- the Hn 3·4 pad (C4–F4);
- the violas' off-beat dyads (D♭4–G4);
- the Cl 1/Hn 2 counterline (E♭4–B♭4).

All three are inside the same fifth. The counterline survives because it is the only one with a moving line, and earlier cycles fixed its unisons (c037, c051). Cycles 127, 147 and 87 protect this design, and I have no corpus case strong enough to reopen it. I record it so that movements II and III can alternate horn chords with string chords, as Sebesky suggests, rather than stacking them.
