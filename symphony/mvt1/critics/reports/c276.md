## Findings

### F1. Suspended cymbal at 160: an open fff l.v. that rings into the soft bars 163–166. Applied.

Bars 159–166 (`recTwoPerc`). The roll at 159 swells into one fff stroke at 160, written `cymr4->\fff\laissezVibrer^\markup \italic "l.v."` and then six bars of rest. Nothing says when the sound stops. A large suspended cymbal struck fff, even with a soft stick, rings for 15–25 seconds. full.midi puts bar 160 at 354.5 s, 164 at 364.1 s and 166 at 369.0 s. So the cymbal is still sounding when the strings reach mp at 164 and p at 166 (the protected "dip 163–166" and the E♮→E♭ turn at 166), and the player has to guess.

Evidence: Adler PDF 71 says to "specify exactly how long the sound should last" for any sustaining percussion instrument. Ex. XII-22 (PDF 70) writes every suspended-cymbal stroke with a value followed by rests. PDF 80 asks the same of the timpani ("how long a note may ring"). This movement already does this for the tam-tam at 122 (tied through 126, "damp" at 127, cycle 166, Ex. XII-29, PDF 73). The cymbal at 160 should follow the same rule. The harp makes the same choice at these bars: "étouffez" at 163 and 164.

The part also has a layout fault. In the percussion part, the "l.v." markup over 160 stacks above "Susp. cym. soft sticks" and "largamente" and touches the "128–130" bar range of the system above. Removing the markup leaves one level less of text there.

Fix (`recTwo.ily`):
```
recTwoPerc = \drummode { R1*12 | cymr1:32\pp\<^\markup { "Susp. cym." \italic "soft sticks" } | cymr1->\fff~ | cymr1~ | cymr1~ | cymr1 | R1^\markup \small \italic "damp" | R1*2 | }
```
The cymbal rings through the largamente 160–162 and the ff diminuendo at 163, and is damped at 164, where the bass reaches mp and the harp damps. The notes, the fff, the roll at 159 and the soft sticks are unchanged.

### F2. Tam-tam at 175: the only sound with no stop before the attacca. Applied.

Bars 175–182 (`codaPerc`). The tam-tam is struck pp with a soft stick and l.v. at 175, and the part then rests to the fermata at 182 with no end given. At 182 the score stops everything that should not cross into movement II: the harp is marked "étouffez", and the double basses, Horn 4 and the timpani release at the end of the bar (coherence/mvt1.md, "Into movement II"). The tam-tam is left out of this plan. From 175 to the end of 182 is about 37 s (full.midi: 393.9 s to 430.8 s, plus the fermata). A large tam-tam struck pp can still hum after that long, under movement II's bar 1 and its A♭ at bar 2.

Evidence: Adler PDF 71 (specify how long the sound lasts) and Ex. XII-29, PDF 73 (the tam-tam's written value ends where it is damped). Cycle 166 judged that the 175 stroke "decays before 182". That was before the harp étouffez at 182 was settled. It also does not account for the attacca: in an attacca the stroke has no silence to decay into. The cost is one word, and the player is not left to judge it in the hall.

Fix (`coda.ily`): last bar `R1\fermata _\markup \small \italic "damp" |`. The tam-tam stroke at 175, its pp and l.v., and the protected coda notes and dynamics are unchanged. I tried "damp at the release". Centred, it ran off the right margin of the percussion part; right-aligned, it collided with "179–181". The single word "damp" fits under the fermata rest.

- **Endurance.** The fourteen-bar G roll at 117–130 (fff at 122) and the C rolls at 167–169 are normal for the instrument.
