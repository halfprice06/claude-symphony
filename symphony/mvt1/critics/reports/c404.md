# Critic 404: Orchestration (v404)

Reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*. Chapter I: "Comparison of resonance in orchestral groups and combination of different tone qualities" (rimsky-principles PDF 51–53). Chapter III, "Harmony": general observations, number of parts and duplication, distribution of notes in chords, string harmony and wood-wind harmony (PDF 81–99). This is the third time a critic has read this range; c224 and c314 read it before. I read every text page in the range in full. I viewed every page with music examples as an image at 110 dpi (PDF 82–97). I enlarged the rule-4 example on PDF 90 to 220 dpi, and it shows two oboes a second apart above the clarinets. PDF 51–53 and 98–99 have no notation.

Method: I listed every sounding pitch, with its players and velocity, on every beat of 1–182 from v404/full.midi. I then ran five checks, each tied to the reading:
- register order inside each pair (PDF 90 rule 2);
- discords between the two players of a pair (PDF 90 rule 4);
- an inverted seventh or diminished seventh whose bass is doubled in an upper part (PDF 83–84);
- gaps of more than an octave at f or louder, and a bass more than an octave below the next part (PDF 85–86, 96);
- beats with eight or more players where the middle register holds only two pitch classes (PDF 85, 96 remark 1, 97 remark 4).

I checked every hit against the score pages (all 18 viewed at 80 dpi, 3 and 13 again at 110 dpi), against HISTORY.md, and against the reports of c224 and c314, so as not to repeat them.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 25 and 137: the middle of the iv and iiø7 chords is a bare fourth, with the third in one low horn (applied)

What is wrong. In the three-beat groups before the stop chords, beats 2–4 of bar 25 (and of 137 in the recapitulation) carry iv, then iiø7 over D on beat 4. The tune is in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I (A♭5–G5–F5). Under it, the middle register holds only C4 and F4:

| Pitch | Players in v404 at 25.2–25.4 |
|---|---|
| A♭5 | Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I (beat 2 only; then G5, F5) |
| F4 | Cl 1, Hn 1, Vln II |
| C4 | Cl 2, Hn 2, Vla |
| A♭3 | Hn 3 |
| F3 / F2 / F1 | Bsn 1, Vc / Bsn 2, Hn 4 / Cb (from beat 4 the bass is D) |

F sounds in eight players and the third, A♭, in one horn at A♭3. When the tune moves on to G5 and F5, the upper register has no A♭ at all. Both pairs of the pad, the clarinets and Horns 1·2, hold the same bare fourth C4–F4, at mf with a crescendo into the ff stop chord. Bar 137 is the same.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 97, remark 4: when the upper part goes to the strings, "it is desirable to provide the intermediate parts with a moderately full tone, choosing no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths". PDF 85: "It is necessary to make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts". PDF 90, rule 4: concords, not fourths or fifths, "should be given to instruments of the same kind". PDF 89: the harmonic texture "must possess a resonance equally distributed throughout", which a chord with eight Fs and one A♭ does not have. PDF 96, remark 1: "Modern orchestrators do not allow any void in the intermediate parts". c224 and c314 did not raise these two bars. Their rule-4 rows list 12, 35, 113–116, 122, 145–146, 159 and 170. c224 fixed the same fault at 145–146, where the clarinets held an open fifth, under the same remark 4.

Fix. Horn 1 moves up a step to A♭4 instead of down to F4 at 25.2. It then holds that note into the stop chord at 26, where it already plays A♭4. Horns 1·2 then hold a minor sixth, C4/A♭4, and the middle register reads A♭3–C4–F4–A♭4, a full close chord. The clarinets keep their syncopated G–F line. c119 kept that line in Cl 1 and Vln II when it took Ob 2 out of 23–25, and it is still doubled by Vln II, so nothing melodic is lost. With A♭4 directly above them, the clarinets' fourth becomes the inner part of a close triad (the horns enclose the clarinets, PDF 91–92).

### Kept after checking against the reading (no change recommended)

- **Bar 12: Ob 1 C5 against Ob 2 F♯4, a tritone at f for four beats.** This is the rule-4 case that Rimsky names, two oboes on a discord. c224 kept it as a stressed discord. I agree for a different reason: the only concord that keeps Ob 2's F♯4→G4 resolution is Ob 1 E♭5, which is spelled as a diminished seventh. The finale quotes this chord as its bar 1 (I 12 = IV 1, checked by MIDI at c310), and its harmony and spelling are protected (c222, c300). A one-player gain is not worth a cross-movement change. Keep.
- **Bar 122: F3–G3–A♭3–B3 in Tbn 2, Hn 4, Tbn 1 and Hn 3 at fff.** Seconds this low go against PDF 85 and PDF 96 remark 2. But G3 is the inner pedal (PDF 84, c302, c314) and A♭3 belongs to the A♭ that sounds in four octaves. Only Tbn 2's F3 could move, and the only places it could go either cross Tbn 1 or double the leading tone a fifth time. The bar is protected many times over (c254, c264, c294, c349, c374). Keep.
- **41–42: Cl 1·2 E♭5/B♭4, a fourth.** They are enclosed by the oboes' octave G4/G5 in a close chord (PDF 91 enclosure), after an sf. Keep.
- **82.4: Ob 1 D♭5 passing over Ob 2's held C5 for one beat.** Ob 2's C5 is the only third above C4 in that chord (PDF 85). Taking it away to avoid a passing semitone would empty the chord. Keep.
- **97–102: the Hn 1 + Tbn 1 counter at f against the ff tune.** By PDF 51 the trombone counts as two horns, so the counter weighs about as much as each octave of the tune. The tune is marked ff with swells (c248), and the counter is protected (c231, c307). Keep.
