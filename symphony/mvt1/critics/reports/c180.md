## Findings

### 1. Bars 115–116: the top line moves ♭2̂ straight up to ♮2̂ at the retransition (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 115 the harmony is root-position ♭II (bass D♭: Cb, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, tuba, B.cl). At 116 it is A♭–C–D–F♯ over C, becoming D7/C, the dominant preparation, before the G pedal at 117. The top line (Fl 1·2 a2, Ob 1, Tpt 1, Vln I) plays `des'''4 c'''4 es'''4 des'''4 | d'''1`. The last D♭ goes directly to D♮, f with a crescendo into the ff at 117. Hn 3 and Tpt 3 double that D♭4→D4 in octaves. This is the only place in the movement where ♭2̂ rises to ♮2̂. Everywhere else it falls: 21, 141–142, 173, 178, 180.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 484, §4: "Composers usually avoid the direct chromatic progression ♭2̂ (over ♭II) to ♮2̂ (over V), especially when it occurs in the soprano. The melodic motion seldom sounds convincing". Ex. 28-3c marks it "avoid". PDF 488, Ex. 28-12b, marks the soprano D♭–D♮ "avoid", and 28-12a shows the inner-voice version as "possible". Points for Review 3 (PDF 499): "the melodic succession ♭2̂–♮2̂ is best avoided, especially in the soprano." The OSU/Hutchinson and Rush pages say the same. PDF 485 (Mendelssohn, Ex. 28-6) shows the alternative: ♭2̂ sets off the ♮2̂, which the soprano reaches later, not by the direct step.

**Fix (applied).** Swap the last two quarters of 115 in the five top-line parts. Fl 1 (dev.ily line 28) and Fl 2 (37): `des'''4\f c'''4 des'''4 es'''4 | d'''1`. Ob 1 (50): `des''4\f c''4 des''4 es''4 | d''1\<`. Tpt 1 (215): `des''4 c''4 des''4 es''4 | d''1\ff`. Vln I (292): `des'''4 c'''4 des'''4 es'''4 | d'''1`. The bar keeps its rhythm, its pitches, the D♭–C fall of ♭2̂ on beats 1–2 and the D arrival. D now comes from E♭, the motto's own first falling step (E♭–D), while the bass carries the motto tail D–D♭–C. Hn 3/Tpt 3 keep D♭4→D4 in an inner voice, which A&S calls possible (Ex. 28-3d, 28-12a).

**Protected passage.** The wedge 111–116 is on do-not-touch lists (cycles 133, 142, 177). Those lists protect the bass plan (no A♭ in the bass before 122), the sequence and the scoring. This change leaves all three alone and swaps two quarter notes in the top line. The new evidence is A&S PDF 484/488/499.

### 2. For movement III: let the symphony's long-range ♭II go to V before C (note for cyclic-notes; no score change)

Movement I keeps ♭II local and never tonicizes it (cycle 60). Movement II supplies the large-scale ♭II as its D♭ middle section. Movement II itself is in A♭, ♭VI of C. The three-movement plan is therefore ♭VI → ♭II → (V) → I. That is the Verdi Kyrie progression of Ex. 28-14 (PDF 489–490: "lowered VI precedes ♭II and acts as its dominant") stretched over the symphony, with the Appassionata's I–♭II–V framework (Ex. 28-17, PDF 491–492). A&S stress that ♭II's intensity comes from ♭2̂ being kept from 1̂ until V moves to I (PDF 483). A direct ♭II → I is their other category, the neighbouring "♭II" inside the tonic (PDF 495, Ex. 28-20), and movement I already uses that at 3, 7–8, 178 and 180. The current plan for III ("forte D♭ major shifting directly to C major ff, cf. Eroica i 557–561") would close the symphony with the neighbour use, not the structural one. In the Eroica the D♭→C step lies inside E♭ and C is not a tonic (corpus PDF p. 27). Recommendation for III: route the D♭ episode ♭II6 → cadential 6/4 → V7 → I. The top voice goes D♭–C–B (filled d3, Pt 3), with the trumpets taking B→C on the first root-position V7–I in C since bar 135 (already planned). Do not repeat Finding 1's D♭→D in the top line there.

### 3. For movement II: the Neapolitan of C minor is IV of A♭ (note for cyclic-notes; no score change)

A&S PDF 494 (§14): "the most frequent use of ♭II as pivot: the Neapolitan 6th of a minor key becomes IV6 in the major key a 3rd lower." C minor → A♭ major is that relation. Movement I's last D♭ (Vln I, 180) is the N of C minor, and movement II's plagal D♭–A♭ close (its bar 87) uses the same triad as IV. Movement I always voices this chord with F in the bass at its structural points: 7, 21, 141, 173. If movement II states its D♭ chord once as a 6/3 with F in the bass, especially at the plagal close, the listener can hear the pivot. Movement II's own D♭ middle section then follows from it as a tonicized ♭II of the symphony's tonic (Ex. 28-16/17). This is a suggestion for the movement II thread.

### 4. Confirmed, no change: the Neapolitan arc and its rhymes

- 20–22 vs 140–142: exposition ♭II–♭II6–V7 with Hn 3's direct d3 (D♭4→B3); recap with the filled D♭–C–B and A♭6 above. This follows A&S PDF 484 and Pt 3, and the recap varies the exposition.
- 173–174: iv7 → N6 → cadential 6/4 → V with the cor anglais D♭→C (Ex. 28-2c). The V goes to i6/4 over G at 175, not to a root-position tonic. This is deliberate (cyclic-notes: no root V–i after 135), and it saves 1̂ "as a goal" for movement III (PDF 483).
- 7–8 and 178–182: the cor anglais phrase closes N6 → C major (F→E♮ in Vln II), and the movement closes ♭II → i, D♭ neighbour → the viola's E♮. Both are the coda use of Ex. 28-20 (PDF 495). The intro plants the sound of the ending.
- 178: the timpani's soft C on beat 3 sounds against root-position D♭ (B.tbn/Vc D♭3, Cb D♭2). Chopin's Ex. 28-20 has a tenor pedal over a moving bass, so this is an attested inner pedal at pp. Keep.
