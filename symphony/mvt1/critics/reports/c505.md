## Findings

The string notation follows Adler ch. 2 everywhere except one bar, and the string parts are clean. The c395 fixes (Vln II text order at 55, Vln I sul D dashes at 182) still hold in the parts. The series-4 bow marks (c406, c438) and the bar-122 cello tremolo (c444) also follow the reading. I found one fault.

### 1. Cellos 141–142: the hammered C–B is slurred in one 7-beat ff bow, while every other string section takes it as two down-bows (applied)

**Bars:** Vc (with Bsn 1) 141 beat 2 to 142 beat 4. This is the recapitulation's hammered C–B over the dominant, before the 143 cadence.

**What is wrong:** v505 has `f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2-> b2->)`. That is one bow from 141.2 to the end of 142: seven beats at ♩ = 126 and ff, with accents on the tied C and the B. On the same two half notes, Vln I and Vln II play `c''2->\downbow b'2->\downbow`, Tbn 1 plays a separate C4 and B3, and the oboes, clarinets and Tpt 3 attack each note. That gives three problems, each against the reading.
- Adler PDF 38: in string parts "the only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow". So this slur is an instruction to play the B♮ without a new stroke. The B♮ then gets a pressure accent at the end of a long bow, near the tip, where Adler PDF 38 says the stroke is weakest ("one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog").
- Adler PDF 39 (Ex. II-21/22): the forte dynamic cuts the number of notes per bow. Mendelssohn gives the violas only six quavers per bow at f. Adler adds that the cello bow is shorter. Seven crotchet beats at ff in one cello bow pushes against this.
- Adler PDF 42–43 (Ex. II-29): "a very heavy and vigorous feeling is commonly achieved by a series of down-bows". The violins already have that at 142. The cellos are the bass-register strings on this line, and they are the one string section that does not re-attack the leading tone. In the exposition's parallel bar 32, the cellos take G and G2 as two down-bows. So the slur at 142 is an inconsistency, not a choice the movement makes elsewhere.

From the listener's side, this is the last forte leading tone of the first group in the recapitulation. With the fix, the B♮ lands as one attack across the strings and Tbn 1, so the cadence into 143 hits harder.

**Fix (applied, recOne.ily, Vc and Bsn 1):**
```
Vc:    c'2.\f\<\upbow( bes4) | aes2\ff( f2~ | f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2->) b2->\downbow |
Bsn 1: c'2.\f\<( bes4) | aes2\ff( f2~ | f4) g4( aes4 c'4~ | c'2->) b2-> |
```
The bow order is: 139 up-bow (crescendo, as marked), 140 down-bow, 141.2–142.2 up-bow with the natural crescendo toward the frog onto the accented C, then 142.3 B♮ down-bow, the same stroke as the violins. Bsn 1 gets the same slur end so the doubled line keeps identical slurs (the c88 rule), and it now articulates the B♮ as Tbn 1 and the upper winds do. No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or accent changes. I checked the cello part at 130 dpi and score page 14 at 110 dpi: the slur ends on the tied C and the ⊓ sits clear above the B. This keeps c388's accents (it only moves the slur end), and it does not touch the protected Tbn 1 C4–B3 at 142 or the 140–143 Neapolitan setting.
