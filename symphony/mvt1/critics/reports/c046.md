# Critic 046 — lens 6, playability: each section leader reading their own part

Version: v65. Fixes tested in /tmp/critic-046/work (build clean: lilypond rc=0, 0 warnings,
17 pages, K at the top of page 15, parallels still only "bar 96: octaves F5->Bb2 over F2->Bb1").

## What I studied

Corpus: the Mutopia Dvořák 9 (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/`), whose `header.ily` lines 91–92
define `ub`/`db` as `-\tag #'part \upbow` / `\downbow` and whose string files carry 36 (mvt I),
33 (mvt II), 26 (mvt IV) such marks — i.e. a handful per movement, placed at accented attacks and
phrase restarts (`strings.notesA.ily` l. 157 `b4\fz->\db`, l. 159, l. 162 `g,,8.\p\db`, l. 289–290),
and tagged so they print in the parts, not the score. Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto
(`mutopia/TchaikovskyPI_O35/.../mvmt1/solo.ily` l. 254–259) marks `\downbow` on every ff chord of a
repeated-chord passage. Dvořák 7 (`mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1/`) shows the cue practice:
`\cueDuring` appears 13× in Trombone I·II, 11× in Tromba I·II, 6× in Corno I·II, 5× in Timpani, 5× in
Oboe I·II — one cue before each entry after a long rest. Dvořák 9's cymbal part does the same
(`percussion.notesF.ily` l. 198–202: a violin cue before the cymbal's entry after 60 bars' rest).
Web: orchestrationonline.com, "Strings: bowing styles and markings" and "Melodic and rhythmic bowing"
(https://orchestrationonline.com/strings-bowing-styles-and-markings/) — bowing is marked only at a
phrase start or where something unusual is wanted; an unmarked passage means détaché; chords are
rolled upwards and are therefore taken down-bow. "Horn wars" and "Horn section teamwork"
(https://orchestrationonline.com/horn-wars-scoring-12-34-vs-13-24/,
https://orchestrationonline.com/horn-section-teamwork/) on 1·3 as the high horns and on horn 3 as the
first horn's partner in exposed melodic writing. Searches for Gould's and Adler's own wording on
these points returned nothing usable; I say so rather than invent a citation.

## Findings

**1 (applied). No bowing mark anywhere in 178 bars; the attacks that need a down-bow do not get one.**
Bars 15, 33, 123, 154, 156, 163. `grep -c "downbow\|upbow"` over all `sections/*.ily` returns 0.
Dvořák's own text carries about thirty-five per movement. At bar 33 the violins have a single ff
accented eighth after two beats of rest (`transVlnOne`: `c'''8->\ff r8 r4 r2`): leader and section
will take it down-bow, but with nothing written, half the desk arrives up-bow from the bar-32 figure.
Applied, ten marks only:
`tOne.ily` 15 `r8 g'8\downbow\f(` (start of the Allegro); `trans.ily` 33 `c'''8->\downbow\ff` and
`c''8->\downbow\ff` (Vln I, Vln II); `recOne.ily` 123 `es'''2->\downbow`, `es''2->\downbow`;
`recTwo.ily` 154 `g''4\ff\downbow` and 156 `c'''4\fff\downbow` (Vln I via `recTwoMelodyB`, Vln II in
its own copy); `coda.ily` 163 `r8 g8\downbow\ff(`. The 156 mark is a retake, which is the point: it
guarantees the fff peak is not taken up-bow after the 155 crescendo.

**2 (applied). Violas 19–22: one slur over four bars, mf crescendo to f and back.**
`tOneVla` had `aes'2.~\mf\<( aes'8 g'8 | f'2\f aes'2 | aes'4\> g'4 f'2~ | f'2\mf es'2)` — sixteen
beats at crotchet 126, about 7.6 seconds, in one bow, with a crescendo to f in the middle of it. No
violist can do that; each desk will invent its own change and the new counterline (cycle 37) will
sound ragged on the first reading. Split into two two-bar bows at the hairpin joint, which is where
the line turns anyway:
`aes'2.~\mf\<(… aes'8 g'8 | f'2\f aes'2) | aes'4\>( g'4 f'2~ | f'2\mf es'2)`. No notes changed, so
cycle 38's protection of the 19–22 notes is respected. The 103–108 eighths need nothing: separate
bows with slurred neighbour pairs, four notes a second, entirely on the G and D strings.

**3 (applied). Oboe 1 has 1½ bars between a ff accent and the exposed p dolce solo at 137.**
Cycle 26 gave Ob 1 rests at 135–136 for exactly this reason; cycle 33's ff elision chord at 135 put
Ob 1 back in and ate half of it. Ob 1 played 123–135 at ff in the top octave and then had 8½ beats
(4 seconds) to come off the reed, relax and start the dolce sixth. Ob 2 doubles the 135 chord at the
same c'' in unison, so Ob 1 can drop out with no loss: `recOneOboeOne` bar 135–136 is now `R1*2`, and
the combiner prints "2." on the chord by itself. The solo now carries `^\markup { \bold "1."
\italic "dolce" }` because the automatic "1." no longer appears after a solo-II bar. Ob 1's other
re-entries are fine: 154 (ff, after 15 bars), 107 (f, after 4), 97 (ff, after 13), 69 (after 22).

**4 (not applied). The harp's part from bar 56 on is written entirely on the bass staff.**
Bars 56–68 and 148–159. `recTwoHarpL` 148 is `c8( g c' e' g'2)` — C3 up to G4, a twelfth, as are
149–151 and 157–159; `tTwoHarpL` 56–68 is the same shape. The right-hand staff is empty throughout
(`harpR` is silent from 57 to the end). A harpist reads that as left hand alone, and one placement
spans at most about a tenth, so the hand would have to shift inside every figure at crotchet 112.
Harpists will simply redistribute, but the part should say it: put the top two notes in the right
hand, which also lets the held top note ring while the left hand is free. E.g. 148:
`harpR` `r4 <e' g'>2.` or, written as it sounds, `recTwoHarpR = { R1*9 | r4 e'8 g'2. | … }` with
`recTwoHarpL` reduced to `c8\p( g c'4) r2`. I did not apply this: the pedal-change markups hang off
the left-hand staff and a 24-bar redistribution wants the composer's hand.

**5 (not applied; this is a parts matter, not a score change). Entries that a professional orchestra
will want cued.** Ranked by bars of rest before the entry: cor anglais 91 (82 bars, f, fugato
countersubject — the most exposed in the movement); harp 148 (79 bars) and the final chord at 178
(18 bars under a fermata); percussion player 1 at 114 (113 bars from the start, fff tam-tam) and
player 2 at 155 (first entry of the movement, pp roll); trumpet 3 at 82 (38); cor anglais 139 (36)
and 167 (19); trumpets 1·2 and tuba at 26 (25 bars — the trumpets' first note in the piece, ff);
trombones, tuba and trumpet 3 at 154 (18); oboe 2, trumpets and tuba at 103 (19); timpani at 97
(11 bars, and a drum change: the part must show "muta B♮" early enough). Dvořák 7's parts cue all of
these categories (counts above). The score itself needs no change; add
`\cueDuring #"…" #UP { … }` tagged `#'part` when parts are extracted, as in
`DvorakSYMPH7M1_musique-Timpani.ly`.

**6 (applied). Tam-tam at 114 has no beater; the 155 cymbal roll has no release.**
`devPerc` read `tt1\fff\laissezVibrer^\markup { "1: Tam-tam" \italic "l.v." }` — the loudest single
stroke in the movement, with no beater named, so the player must guess between a hard stick (a
clangy attack) and the big soft beater. Now `\italic "hard beater, l.v."`. The 155 suspended cymbal
is marked "soft sticks" but had no indication whether to damp it under the crash at 156; now
`cymr1:32\pp\<\laissezVibrer … "soft sticks, l.v."`. The crash at 156 and the tam-tam at 171 were
already fully specified; the instrument changes leave each player ample time (41 bars for player 1
between 114 and 156, 15 between 156 and 171).

**7 (not applied). Horn 2 has the exposed counterline at 148–151: concert c'' = written g'' at mp
espressivo.** The engraved page prints "2." over it. Cycle 16 moved this line from Hn 1 to Hn 2 to
save Hn 1's lip for the tune at 154–157; that reason holds, but it does not require the second horn,
who is the section's low player. Horn 3 — the other high chair, and the first horn's usual partner in
exposed lines — rests 139–151 and enters at 152 anyway. Fix: move `recTwoHnTwo` bars 148–151 into
`recTwoHnThree` (which currently has `R1*13`, so `R1*9` then the four bars), leaving Hn 2 `R1*4`
there; Hn 4's rest makes the combiner print "1." correctly. I did not apply it because it reverses a
reasoned earlier assignment and changes which staff the line appears on.

**8 (not applied, protected material). Phrase slurs longer than one bow at the given dynamic.**
Vln I 147–149 (2¼ bars from the mf re-entry), 150–151 (two bars with a crescendo), Vc 46–48 in the
tenor register at mf–f, Vc 138–140. At crotchet 112 each is four to five seconds of bow with a
growing dynamic. These are phrasing slurs over protected music (47–55, 139–147), and the right answer
is not to re-slur the score but to let the leaders write bow changes into the parts; if the composer
wants it in the score, the neutral places are the barlines 148|149 and 150|151.
