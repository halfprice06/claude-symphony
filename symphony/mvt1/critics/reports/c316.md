## Findings

### 1. Bar 12, Violins II and double basses: one 4.8-second bow at f in the Lento (applied)

What is wrong. Bar 12 is the German-sixth arrival, f, at crotchet = 50, so the bar lasts 4.8 s. Every other string part splits it. Vln I has `aes''2.\f\downbow g''4~` since c226. Violas and cellos play 2.+4 (`<c' es'>2. <c' es'>4`, `c'2. c'4`), as the winds and horns do. Vln II plays `fis'1\f` and the basses play `aes,1\f`: each holds one note for all 4.8 s at f on one bow. The Vln II F♯4 is the augmented sixth's ♯4, the note that leads to G at 13. If the players bow by plain alternation (10 down, 10.4–11.2 up, 11.3 down), this note also arrives at f on an up-bow, starting at the point.

Evidence. Adler PDF 39: "Even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow. Remember also that the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter." PDF 39–40, Ex. II-23/24 (viewed): Adler calls Liszt's long notes at a slow tempo "impossible to perform as the composer has marked" until they are split into bows. PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (Beethoven, *Coriolan*, viewed): "A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid." PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog." c226 fixed exactly this problem in Vln I 11–13. It did not apply the fix to the other parts of the same chord.

Fix, applied. Tie each note across beat 4 and mark the bow change on the tied crotchet. Vln II also gets Adler's double up-bow (PDF 38, Ex. II-19) at 11.3, so the f lands down-bow, as Vln I's does:

```
introVlnTwo:  es'1\pp\< | f'2. c'4( | aes'2) f'2\upbow | fis'2.~\f\downbow fis'4\upbow |
introCb:      c,1\pp\< | f,1 | d,1 | aes,2.~\f\downbow aes,4\upbow |
```

The bow plan from bar 12 is then the same in all five parts: down at f, up on beat 4 (where the violas, cellos and winds restrike), down at 13 with the diminuendo. That is the natural direction for a diminuendo (PDF 38). The pitches and the sound in the MIDI do not change. The bass's "change bow at will" at 13 stays.

### 2. Bar 182, violas: the E♮4 crossing into movement II has no "change bow at will" (applied)

What is wrong. Five parts tie into movement II. Vln I, Vln II and the cellos are marked "change bow at will" at 181. The violas are marked only "morendo". Their E♮4 is a new note at 182 (from E♭4), held for 4.8 s, then under the fermata, then tied through movement II bar 1 (3 beats at crotchet = 52, 3.5 s). That is about 10–12 s at ppp on one bow. It is the movement's last colour change and the note movement II turns back to E♭.

Evidence. Adler PDF 40, Ex. II-25 (viewed): "By dividing the section and staggering the bowing among the players, one can produce a very long and effective legato line." PDF 39: slow passages must not overload the bow, even when soft. The score already uses this device on every other long held string note (Cb 3, 13, 41, 72, 117, 179; Vln I, Vln II, Vc 181), so the violas are the only exception.

Fix, applied. The instruction goes at 181 with "morendo". Placed at 182, it ran off the right margin next to the fermata.

```
codaVla:  es'1 | es'1 | es'1\>^\markup \column { \small \italic "change bow at will" \italic "morendo" } | e'1\ppp\fermata\laissezVibrer |
```

The viola part stays at 2 pages.

### Checked and kept (with reasons from the reading)

- **Two-bar slurs at Allegro: Vla 19–22 (mf< f, 3.8 s), Vla 94–95 (f<, 3.8 s), Vc 103–106 (mf, 3.8 s), Vc 141–142 (ff, 3.3 s).** These are longer than Adler's forte example (PDF 39, Ex. II-22: six quick notes per bow). But each crescendo can fall on an up-bow (Vla 19, Vla 94 after the whole note at 93), and each diminuendo on a down-bow (Vla 21–22). That is the distribution Adler recommends on PDF 38. Bows of 3–4 s at f are ordinary orchestral practice at crotchet = 126, and c305 kept 140–142 for this reason. Splitting them would add slurs to lines another critic protected for their phrasing, for no audible gain. Keep.
- **The S head in the cellos at 46.4–48 and in violas and cellos at 146.4–148 (mf, about 4.8 s, with swells).** This is the longest bow at mf in the movement, on the shorter cello bow (PDF 39). But the anacrusis is up-bow (PDF 38) and carries the first swell's crescendo. The rest is dolce at mf, not f, and Hn 1 shares the slur under the identical-slur rule. The slurring of 47–55 is protected (cycle 58). Adler's limit concerns loud or slow bows, and this one is neither, so the reason for protecting it stands. Keep.
- **Vln I/II 58–59 (p<, 4.3 s on a down-bow).** The crescendo runs against the natural direction (PDF 38), but it goes only from p toward mf over two bars, which bow distribution handles. 60–61 then takes the mf crescendo up-bow. Keep.
- **160.4→161, Vln I, Vln II, Vc:** the quaver G is down-bow by alternation and 161 is marked down-bow (c238). This is a retake at fff largamente, the "series of down-bows" of Ex. II-29 (PDF 42–43). Keep.
- **Cb 46→47 arco to pizz. with no rest.** The up-bow at 46 is Adler's remedy (PDF 54: "an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog"). Kept by c236 and c305, and I find no reason against it.
- **Vln II 122–123 fff tremolo double stops without div.** (D5/A♭5, D5/G5 on the A and E strings) and Vla 158–161 repeated dyads: these are adjacent-string pairs, the case PDF 30 says players take as double stops. Keep.
- **Vc 117–121 measured semiquavers on the open G** (two beams, Ex. II-47 shorthand). The open string helps at f. Keep (c305).
- **Long pp bows in the coda** (Vln I 177.4–179.3, about 8 s; Vc 175–176, 6.7 s). This is within one bow at pp, and 181 carries the stagger instruction. Keep (c226).
