So I did not substitute another work. The comparison below uses only what is documented about Rachmaninoff 1/i:
- Wikipedia, "Symphony No. 1 (Rachmaninoff)" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Rachmaninoff)).
- The general description of the work: a seven-bar Grave introduction with a turn-figure cell and a *Dies irae*-derived motto; the motto fills the Allegro; a fugal working of it; a brass-chorale climax with bell-like sonority; a second subject in the violins that the full orchestra later repeats ff; orchestration that the composer himself called "worse than weak" and that critics call "clogged".

Every claim I make about Rachmaninoff is at that level of generality. I make no bar-level claims about his score.

## Rachmaninoff 1/i and this movement

The two movements share a design. Each has:
- a short, dark slow introduction that states a motto;
- a fast movement built from that motto;
- a fugal working of the motto in the development (here the fugato 85–96);
- a climax with bell-like percussion (here the tam-tam at 122);
- a lyric second theme in the strings that later returns ff with the whole orchestra (here 97–102 and 158–161).

On the documented points, this movement avoids Rachmaninoff 1's known weaknesses:
- **Orchestration.** The critics and the composer himself called his scoring clogged and brash. Here the tuttis are graded (82 ff, 122 fff open, 160 fff full; Adler PDF 160–161, 164). Reduced textures separate them (Adler PDF 146–147). The trombones are held back for the coda chorale (Adler PDF 37).
- **Themes.** Cui complained of "meaningless repetition of the same short tricks" and "the complete absence of themes". Here P, S and the horn call are distinct in rhythm and contour (Adler PDF 175–176: each element needs its own rhythmic profile). Each motto return is re-scored (Adler PDF 169–171).
- **Ending.** Rachmaninoff ends loud. This movement ends ppp and attacca, because the finale holds the loud apotheosis ("Do not reuse" list in coherence/mvt1.md). That is a difference of plan, not a fault.

Where Rachmaninoff 1/i is stronger is the second theme's colour. His theme uses a modal "gypsy" scale with augmented seconds, which gives it an exotic edge that a listener remembers. The S theme here is diatonic in A♭ with appoggiaturas (47–55). It is Brahmsian rather than Russian. Changing S's intervals would change a theme quoted by movements II and IV and protected by many cycles (c361, c381, c441, c471: "themes"; S chain 47–56–97–147–158). Adler gives no ground for it either, because the reading is about scoring, not melodic invention. I do not propose it.

A conductor who knew the late-Romantic repertoire would take this for a skilled, carefully revised first movement in the Brahms–Tchaikovsky line. It is more economical and better balanced than Rachmaninoff's First.

## Findings

No change is recommended. These are the points the comparison and the reading raised, ranked, with my verdict on each.

### 1. Second-theme colour against Rachmaninoff's modal S (keep)
Bars 47–55 and 147–155. Rachmaninoff's S is modal and exotic. This S is diatonic. A modal rewrite is out of scope for lens 9's reading (Adler ch. 11/15 concerns scoring), would change a theme that other movements quote, and would undo the protections of c361, c441 and c471. The scoring already makes S memorable by colour: horn and cello in unison (Adler PDF 27, Ex. XI-10), harp, and a viola pad. Not applied.

### 2. Brass chorale at the first-group climax, as in Rachmaninoff (keep)
Rachmaninoff 1/i brings a brass chorale at the climax of its first theme. This movement keeps its brass chorale for 175–179, pp, with tam-tam. Adler PDF 37 (Schubert 9/i, Ex. XI-19) is the reason: a brass colour saved for one structural seam clarifies the form. A ff brass chorale in the first group would also use up the finale's reserve. Not applied.

### 3. Bell sonority at the development climax (keep)
At 122 the tam-tam is struck fff with a heavy beater, damped gradually 124–126, over the open A♭-over-G chord. Rachmaninoff's bell effect is louder and longer, but Adler PDF 181–184 (Bloch) warns that loud percussion obliterates the rest of the orchestra. The present shading (fff, damp gradually, p at 126) also follows Adler PDF 164–165 on controlled diminuendo. Not applied.

### 4. 158–162 tutti against Brahms 3/i and Mahler 1/iv (keep)
I reduced 160 from the page. A♭6 sits in Fl 1·2 and Vln I, with Vln II and Ob 2 an octave below. The counter is in Ob 1 + Cl 1·2. Every upper octave is filled and the bass A♭ sits in Cbn, Tuba, Cb and B.tbn. This is the layout of Adler Ex. XV-14 and XV-15 (PDF 163–164): each chord in every choir, the lowest octave bare. The viola repeated quavers under sustained winds are Smetana's "adhesive" (PDF 154). Not applied.

### 5. Coda chorale 175–179, clarity of the last phrase (keep)
Adler PDF 24 warns that a thick chorale's last phrase can become muddy. At 178–179 the trombones close to D♭3–F3 and E♭3–C4 at pp, with B.cl/Vc doubling the bass trombone and Cb an octave lower. Tbn 2 and B.tbn drop out after 179 and Tbn 1 continues alone, which is Adler's Schubert diminuendo (PDF 28–29, Ex. XI-12). Not applied.

### 6. Final chord 182 (keep)
Adler PDF 162–163 (Ex. XV-13) calls a soft high wind chord "dangerous". Here the top of 182 is Vln I C5, with Horns 3·4 ppp and no flutes. That is the safe solution. Not applied.
