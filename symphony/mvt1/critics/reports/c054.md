### 2. Bars 152–153: flutes rest and enter fresh at the 154 climax (applied)
**Problem.** Fl 1·2 enter two bars early on the d''' line, in unison and an octave below Vln I and II, at mf. The ff a2 entry at 154 is then a continuation, not an arrival.

**Evidence.** Tchaikovsky 6/i 134 (MuseScore bar numbering): Fl 1 joins the violins only at the peak. Ob 1 already rests until 154 (cycle 44).

**Fix.** `recTwoFluteOne = { R1*15 | \partCombineUnisono g''4\ff( e'''2.~ | ...`, and `recTwoFluteTwo = { R1*15 | g''4\ff( ...`. The climax notes are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 158–162: thin the descent to the trough (applied; re-raises c044 Finding 5)
**Problem.** Fl 1 and Ob 1 both double Vln I in unison through the cycle-53 trough (mp at 160, p at 162), and Vln II also doubles it an octave below. The exposition counterpart (64–65) has Fl 1 for two bars only.

**Answer to the protection.** Cycle 19 protected "endings 158–161" for their melodic content. No violin note changes here, and the thinning carries out cycle 53's trough in the scoring.

**Fix.**
- `recTwoFluteOne`: `... a''2.~(\ff a''8 gis''8) | a''4.\>( g''8 f''2)\! | R1*3 |`. This mirrors 64–65 and ends on the chord root of F major.
- `recTwoOboeOne`: `... f''2 d''4 b'8 c''8) | R1*5 |`.

The flutes now enter fresh at 163.

### 4. Bars 60–61: Fl 1 enters at the forte peak at 62 (applied)
**Problem.** Fl 1 played the whole climb in unison with Vln I (60–65).

**Fix.** `tTwoFluteOne = { R1*15 | es''4\f( c'''2.~ | ...`. The flute now arrives with the f leap. This is Tchaikovsky's practice at 6/i 134–136, where the flutes take the scale and peak at f.

### 6. Moment 1: soft trumpet on the counterline, bars 60–63 (applied)
**Evidence.** Tchaikovsky 6/i 134–141 gives the trumpets and trombones p–mf rising figures under the climax of the second theme. Rimsky-Korsakov writes that in piano phrases the trumpet's tone is "full and silvery", and that a wind instrument added to brass "yields a finer legato effect".

**Fix.** Tpt 1 takes Hn 2's four bars of the Cl 1 counterline. Hn 2 rests at 60–63 and resumes at 64.
- `tTwoTptOne = { R1*13 | \partCombineSoloI f'2\p\< r8 f'8( es'8 des'8 | fes'2) ces'2 | c'4\mp es'8( f'8 g'4 aes'4~ | aes'4 g'8 f'8) g'2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1*5 | }`
- In `tTwoHnTwo`, replace bars 60–63 with `R1*4 |`, then `c'2\mp r8 c'8( ...`.

The written range is middle register. Tpt 1 has rested since 43 and enters f at 69. The dynamics match Hn 2's old ones.

### 7. Moment 2: bassoon solo, bars 33–34 (applied; inside protected 33–46)
**Problem.** Bsn 1 states the first-theme head at 33 in unison with the cellos, so the colour is never heard alone.

**Answer to the protection.** Cycle 51 protected 33–46 for its melodic content. No note changes. The imitation chain becomes Bsn → Vla (34) → Vln I (35), and it now opens with a wind voice. This is the Pathétique's signature use of the bassoon (6/i 1–10, exposed over low strings), and it anticipates the Bsn 1 solo at 77.

**Fix.**
- `transVc = { R1*2 | f2\mf aes2 | ...`
- `transBsnOne`: add `^\markup \italic "solo, espr."` to the first `g8`.

The double basses hold C and F underneath. If the composer prefers, revert only the cello change.

### 8. Bass clarinet (not applied)
The bass clarinet rests 5–68 and never leads. Within the stated constraints, no unprotected place suits it. For later movements, the low register that Rimsky-Korsakov calls "sinister" for the bassoon family suits a slow-movement or scherzo-trio solo better than this movement.

Do not touch: 27–32, 114, 154–157 and 163–166 scoring; horn+cello theme 47–55; fugato wind colours 88/91/94; codetta 69–72; flute motto 73–76.
