# Critic 33: v52, lens 3 (form and pacing at the phrase and hypermeter level)

## What I studied
I read every section file of v52, BRIEF, HISTORY, LENSES and the form reports c003, c013, c023 and c030, then mapped the length of every phrase from bar 1 to 178. Corpus comparisons:
- Beethoven, *Eroica* i, `mutopia/BeethovenLv_O55/.../BassMvtI.lyi`, bars 35–37: a V arrives on a ff tonic that is also bar 1 of the tutti theme. This is an elided cadence.

## Phrase map, bars 1–178
- **Intro:** 1–4 \| 5–8 \| 9–12 \| 13–14. Beat-4 upbeats make 2, 6, 10 and 13 the strong bars, so it is not square to the ear.

- **Transition:** 33–36 \| 37–40 (1+1+2 fragmentation) \| 41–42 (MC) \| 43–46 (fill). That is 4+4+2+4.

  - 97 (bass arrival with upbeat) + 98–101 + 102. Then 103–106 + 107–108, and 109–112 + 113–114. These are three 6-bar groups, each 4+2.
  - 115–118 \| 119–122.
- **Recapitulation, first group:** 123–126 \| 127–130 \| 131–134 \| 135–138.
- **Recapitulation, second group:** 139–142 \| 143–146 \| 147 \| 148–151 \| 152–153 \| 154–155 \| 156–157 \| 158–161 \| 162 (ESC).
- **Coda:** 163–166 (with fermata) \| 167–170 \| 171–175 (5 bars, augmented motto) overlapped by 173–178 (6 bars, violins).

**Verdict.** Rothstein's problem of an unbroken string of 4-bar units does not affect most of the movement. The 3-bar fugato hypermeter, the 1-bar suffixes at 55, 68 and 162, the 2-bar insertions at 31–32, 41–42 and 152–157, and the 4+2 groups at 97–114 are what a master does. The second-theme 4-bar units are critic 23's point (answered harmonically in v43), so I do not repeat it. One stretch remains squarely regular, and it is not protected: **123–138**.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 123–138: four end-stopped 4-bar units, all ending on V. Elide the cadence at 135 as the exposition does at 33.
**What is wrong.** Each recapitulation unit closes on the dominant:
- 126: `d''2` over G;
- 130: `b''2.`;
- 134: `c'''2-> b''2->` over `g,1`;
- 138: the oboe's `e''2 d''2`.

The tonic arrives at 135 (cello `c2`), but only in the strings at *f*, and the full ff tutti stops at 134. The ear therefore hears a half cadence at 134 and a soft new start at 135. The result is 16 bars of 4+4+4+4, and the first group has no C-minor arrival with any weight.

The exposition handles the same seam differently. At 32–33 the V falls onto a ff tutti `c'''4->` that is also the first bar of the transition, an elided cadence. In Rothstein's terms, the last hyperbeat of the theme is reinterpreted as the first of the next phrase. *Eroica* i does the same at 35–37: the V in 35–36 lands on ff E♭ at 37, which is also bar 1 of the tutti theme.

If 135 takes the exposition's elision, the recapitulation rhymes with its own exposition. The units then become 4+4+(4 elided into 135)+3, and this breaks the square run without adding a bar.

**Fix (tested).** At recOne section bar 13 (= 135), add one ff quarter-note chord in the winds, brass and timpani. The voicing copies trans bar 1 (= 33), and every voice resolves by step or common tone from 134. Strings, bassoons, Hn 3·4, Tpt 3, bass clarinet and the oboe pivot at 137–138 are unchanged.

### 2. Coda 163–166: the allargando defeats both the Tempo dell'Allegro and the fermata
**What is wrong.** Rough clock time:
- 163–165 at ♩=126: about 5.7 s.
- 166 allargando: about 2.5 s.
- Fermata: about 2 s.
- 167–172 at ♩=72: 20 s.
- 173–174 rit.: about 8 s.
- 175–178 at ♩=50: about 19 s, plus the final fermata.

The forte block is therefore about 13% of the coda's time against 25% of its bars. Three bars at tempo come between a rit. (161–162) and an allargando, which is too short to establish the tempo, as critic 13 noted.

The cycle-30 fermata works. It seals 163–166 as a self-contained 4-bar block, restarts the hypermeter at 167, and splits the coda cleanly into 4 \| 4 \| 5-over-6. The allargando before it, however, applies the brake twice. A broadening in 166 tells the listener that a cadence is coming, so the silence that follows is expected rather than a denial.

*Coriolan* 278–279 and 286–287 cut the chord off in tempo and let the silence do the work. Dvořák 7/i reaches its quiet ending with a single "Tempo I" and no broadening before it.

**Fix (tested).** In global.ily, coda line, change

`s1*3 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" s1`

to

`s1*4`

The forte block then runs four bars at one tempo, the vii°7 is cut off in tempo, and the fermata is the only brake. No notes, bar counts or sections.txt entries change.

Critic 30 kept the allargando only so as to "undo nothing" and gave no musical argument for it, so removing it answers that.

The other coda tempo words are well placed; keep them:
- Più lento at the phrase start 167.
- rit. 173–174, inside the augmentation.
- Tempo I at 175, where the augmented motto arrives on C and the timpani's quarter pulse makes ♩=50 audible.

I considered moving Lento to 171. It would add about 4 s of static time, which reverses critic 3's tightening, so I do not recommend it.
