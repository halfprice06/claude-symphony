## Findings (ranked)

**1. Coda 177–182: string slurs too long for a slow pp ending.** Applied.
- **Problem.** Vc 177–179 `d1( | c2 des2 | c1)` lasts about 12.7 s on one bow (bar 177 at 70/65, bar 178 at 59/53, bar 179 at 50). Vla 181–182 `es'1( | e'1)` lasts 4.8 s plus 8 s at the `\mt 30` step plus the fermata. Vln I 177–179 `g'4( es''1~ es''2.)` lasts about 8.8 s.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 39: "Even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow… the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter". PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts. … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow." The Cb already plays the same chorale line with no slurs. The wind and trombone slurs are breath phrasing and stay as they are.
- **Fix** (coda.ily):
  - Vc: `\clef bass g,1\pp( | es1) | d1 | c2( des2) | c1 |`
  - Vla: `es'1\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | e'1\ppp\fermata |`
  - Vln I: `r2. g'4(\pp^"unis." | es''1)~ | es''2. d''4( |` The bow changes on the tied E♭5, so the bows last 5.2 s and 3.6 s.
- **Protected material.** The viola's E♭→E♮ (protected) is unchanged in pitch and dynamic. Only the impossible single bow goes. At ppp a bow change is inaudible (Adler PDF 37), and the E♮ now starts on a fresh bow. If the composer wants a seamless join, the alternative from Adler PDF 40 is "div., stagger the bow changes".

**2. Recap 155–157: one bow for a crescendo from mf to ff.** Applied.
- **Problem.** Vln I and Vln II slur the pickup f''/f' through bar 157: 9 beats, about 4.7 s at q = 112–118, under `\<` from mf into the ff down-bow at 158.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 39–40, Ex. II-23: the Liszt *Les Préludes* passage "is impossible to perform as the composer has marked while still adhering to the crescendo marks, unless it is broken up into several bows" (Ex. II-24). PDF 39: at forte, fewer notes per bow. PDF 38: an anacrusis is taken up-bow, and a crescendo falls naturally on an up-bow.
- **Fix** (recTwo.ily, `recTwoMelodyB` and `recTwoVlnTwo`; notes unchanged): pickup `f''4\mf` (up), `d'''2.~(\< d'''8 des'''8)` (down), `c'''2( bes''4 aes''8 g''8)` (up, crescendo), and 158 lands down-bow as marked. Vln II gets the same an octave lower. No wind doubles these slurs (Hn 2 has none), so cycle 88's rule on identical slurs is kept. 158–160 already use separate string bows against wind slurs (protected since cycle 58).

**3. Cb 84→85: arco to pizzicato with no time.** Applied.
- **Problem.** The Cb hold `g,,1\subP` arco for the whole of 84 and play `d,4 pizz.` on the downbeat of 85.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck… There are instances… when no time is provided… these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible."
- **Fix** (dev.ily, devCb): `g,,2.\subP r4`. The subito-p bare octave stays for three beats, and the cellos hold G2 through the bar. The other pizzicato changes have at least a quarter rest, or an up-bow before them (Vc 61→62, which Adler PDF 54 endorses).

**4. Intro 3–4: Vc and Cb slur 8 beats at q = 50 with a hairpin swell.** Applied.
- **Problem.** `c2( des4\< c4~\> | c1)` lasts 9.6 s at pp, with a crescendo and diminuendo inside one bow, on the shortest bows in the section.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 39 (bass bows are shorter; do not overload slow passages) and PDF 40 (break a long swell into bows).
- **Fix** (intro.ily, introVc and introCb): `c2(\! des4\< c4)~\> | c1\!`. The bow changes on the tie, giving two bows of 4.8 s. The bass clarinet keeps its slur. The motto's register and scoring (protected) are unchanged.

**5. Violas 56–68: "unis." followed by eleven bars of two-note chords.** Applied.
- **Problem.** At 55 the violas are marked "unis.", so by Adler's rule they play every chord at 56–68 as a double stop at p. Bar 59's `<bes des'>` cannot be played in first position as a double stop: B♭3 and D♭4 both lie on the G string, so it needs fourth position on the C string with a flattened first finger on the G string.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: "If the word *divisi* does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop"; double notes "are usually divided between the two players on the same stand". See also the impossible cases in Ex. II-8 (PDF 30). The same chords at 47–54 are marked div.
- **Fix** (tTwo.ily, tTwoVla; clos.ily, closVla; no notes changed):
  - `^"div."` on the first chord of 56;
  - `^"unis."` on the arpeggio at 60;
  - `^"div."` at 62;
  - `^"unis."` at 69.
- The protected viola accompaniment (cycle 67) keeps its notes. Each pitch now gets 5–6 players, which suits p under the violins' tune.

**6. The first-theme head's down-bow is marked at 15 and 167 but not on the repeat a bar later.** Applied.
- **Problem.** After `aes'4.-> g'8` the next `r8 g'8(` falls on an up-bow by habit (anacrusis), so the accented long note of 17, 27, 29, 131, 133 and 169 is played up-bow while 15 and 167 are played down-bow.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-19/20: mark bow direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". The Schubert and Coriolanus examples show the retake that puts the accent on a down-bow.
- **Fix**: `r8 g'8\downbow(` on the head entries, in string parts only:
  - Vln I 17, 27, 29;
  - Vln II and Vla 27, 29 (tOne.ily);
  - Vc 131, 133 (recOne.ily);
  - Vla 169 (coda.ily).
- Bar 19 is left alone: its crescendo suits the natural up-bow (Adler PDF 38).

**Checked and left alone:**

- Vln I 66–67: a 4.6 s diminuendo from f, which falls on the natural down-bow.
- Vla 17–20: two-bar slurs of 3.8 s at mf–f.
- All double stops (Ex. II-7–10); all div./unis., metà/tutti and sul tasto/ord. marks; tremolo notation; ranges, including the Cb, whose lowest note is F1.
