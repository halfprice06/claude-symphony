## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–42: the trombones hold the horn call's own pitches; score the sforzando the way Adler describes (applied)
**Problem.** At 41 all brass play sf > mf on long notes while the four horns play their ff call (concert G3–E♭3–C4–B♭3, beat 4 of 41 into 42). Tbn 1 holds B♭3, Tbn 2 G3, B.tbn E♭3 through both bars. Those are the call's own notes, in the same octave. The call moves over a held chord built from its own pitches, so the motion G–E♭–C–B♭ is blurred. Lowering the brass from f to sf > mf (cycle 114) reduced the volume but did not remove the doubling.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 189: "Often an accent is not sufficient to emphasize sforzando … Here are some suggestions: 1. string pizzicato combined with a longer note in the winds; 2. a short sforzando trumpet note combined with long notes in the strings." Ex. XV-35 (PDF 190) shows trumpets I+II as a single sf eighth over violins holding the chord. PDF 189: at-pitch doubling "change[s] the basic tonal quality … and this should be seriously considered". The fix also follows cycle 108's rule at 43 (brass leave first).
**Fix (applied in trans.ily).** Trumpets, trombones and tuba give a short sf eighth on beat 1 of 41 and then rest. Strings, woodwinds and timpani keep their sf > mf > p chord, which still has every chord tone: E♭ and G in the strings and flutes/oboes, B♭ in Cl 2, the seventh D♭ in Cl 1 (and in Hn 3·4 on beats 1–3 of 41). The seventh is now carried by one clarinet instead of Cl 1 plus Tpt 3. If the composer wants it stronger, Vla can take `<des' g'>` instead of `<es' g'>` in 41–42; I did not test that.
```
transTptOne   … | bes'8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
transTptTwo   … | g'8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
transTptThree   R1*8 | des'8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 |
transTbnOne   … | bes1\f | bes8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
transTbnTwo   … | f1\f | g8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
transBassTbn  … | bes,1\f | es8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
transTuba     … | es,8->\sf r8 r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | R1*3 |
```
This changes cycle 114's brass dynamics at 41. It answers that cycle's stated reason ("not covered by seven brass parts") more fully: after beat 1 no brass apart from the horns is left. The horn call notes are not changed. Pairs on shared staves (Tpt 1·2, Tbn 1·2) have equal sf.

### 2. Bars 98–102: after the ff hit at 97 the brass counter stays at ff against the tune (applied)
**Problem.** At 97, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Bsn 1 play an accented ff eighth (C♯–B), then continue with their counter (half notes and eighth-note fills, D3–D4) with no new dynamic, so it stays ff for five bars. B.tbn holds the bass ff. Two horns and a trombone at ff in the tenor register compete with the B-minor tune (Vln I, Fl, Ob 1 ff; CA and Vln II an octave lower). Hn 3·4 are already marked f, so the horns have two levels on the same chord.
**Evidence.** Ex. XV-35 (PDF 190): the loud attack is the short note, and the parts that sustain afterwards are marked lower (strings "sf > pp"; violins pp under the violas' sff). PDF 188: "the dynamic also has a great deal to do with the choice" for a line's role. In the Franck climax (Ex. XI-20, PDF 38) the strings' tune is *f molto sostenuto* while the trumpet and cornet motives are mf with hairpins. The score already grades brass below the tune at 27–32 and 131.
**Fix (applied in dev.ily).** f on the first note of 98 in Hn 1, Hn 2 (shared staff, equal), Tbn 1 (Tbn 2 rests) and B.tbn. The ff eighth at 97 stays. Bsn 1·2 stay ff: the bassoon is the weakest of the counter's three colours, and its shared staff needs one dynamic for both players.
```
devHnOne/devHnTwo  \transpose c cis { ces'8\ff bes8 r4 r2 | bes2\f r8 f8 des'8 c'8 | …
devTbnOne          \transpose c cis { ces'8\ff bes8 r4 r2 | bes2\f r8 f8 des'8 c'8 | …
devBassTbn         \transpose c cis { bes,1\ff | bes,1\f | es1 | …
```
No notes change, so the protected counter (cycles 7, 29, 66, 84) keeps its notes and scoring.

### 3. Bar 135: the ff → mf drop at the recap hemiola is not marked as subito (not applied)
**Problem.** At 131–134 all parts are ff. At 135 they restart at mf (horns, trombones mp) with no diminuendo before and no "sub." The exposition prepares the same restart (23) with a diminuendo through 21–22. Here, parts that play straight across the barline (Vla eighths, Cb/Cbn, timpani, horns, tuba) will taper through 134 unless told otherwise. That softens the arrival and the hemiola's restart.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 190 (Ex. XV-36): a sudden drop works only when it is scored or marked so that the loud sound stops and the soft level is already there. In the same players, that can only be done by marking it.
**Fix.** In the parts that cross 134/135 without a rest, add `_\markup \italic "sub."` beside the existing `\mf`/`\mp` at 135, or add an eighth-rest gap at the end of 134 in Vla and timpani. I did not apply this: notes of 131–134 are protected (cycle 84), and a markup dynamic `subMf` would play at the p volume in the MIDI unless `dynamicAbsoluteVolumeFunction` in score.ly is extended.
