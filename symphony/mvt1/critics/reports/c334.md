## Findings

### 1. Climax 160: the bassoons are in unison on the bass and the tenor octave is empty (applied)

Bars 158–161. Both bassoons play G2 | G2 | A♭2 | D3–G2 in unison (`\partCombineUnisono` from 157). Bass trombone, timpani (from 160) and the cello arpeggio are on the same pitch. At the fff downbeat of 160 the sounding pitches below middle C are A♭1 (Cbsn, tuba, Cb), A♭2 (Bsn 1, Bsn 2, B.tbn, timpani) and E♭3 (Tbn 2 alone). Above that, the next note is C4 (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1, Vla, Vc on beat 1). The whole octave E♭3–C4 is empty, and the chord's root does not appear between A♭2 and A♭4. My scan of every tutti beat found this gap on all four beats of 160 and on no other climax bar. At 122 the same register holds F3, G3, A♭3 ×3 and B3 ×3. At 82 it holds A♭3 and C4 ×4.

Evidence:
- Rimsky-Korsakov's appendix never puts two bassoons on one note in a forte tutti chord. In chords 1, 3 and 4 (PDF 505, Fag. fff/ff) they take the bass in octaves, the upper note in the tenor. In chords 2 and 5 (PDF 505) they hold tenor chord tones. Chord 9 (PDF 506) is the one a2 bassoon note among the forte chords, and there the C-fag. holds the octave below it. This score already splits them in every other tutti: 26 (F3/D3), 27 (C4/C3), 82 (C4/A♭2), 122 (A♭3/G2), 138 (F3/D3), 170 (D4/C3). 158–161 is the one exception.
- PDF 85: chords follow the natural harmonic series, with wide intervals at the bottom closing upward, and the bass rarely more than an octave below the part above it. At 160 the intervals widen again above the fifth A♭2–E♭3, to a sixth E♭3–C4.
- PDF 96, remark 1: "Modern orchestrators do not allow any void in the intermediate parts."
- Rimsky's own big C-minor/A♭ chords (chords 1–5) fill the tenor from the trombones, bassoons and violas. At 160 the violas are on C4/E♭4, the cellos on the tune at A♭4 and the horns on A♭4/C5. That leaves the bassoons as the only free players for the tenor.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-334/work/sections/recTwo.ily, recTwoBsnOne): Bsn 1 plays the bass line an octave above Bsn 2 from 158 to 161. Bsn 2, the dynamics and everything else stay the same.

### 2. Cor anglais and bass clarinet silent in the 158–161 tutti (no change)

Neither plays at 158–161. In Rimsky-Korsakov's tutti chords 8–12 (PDF 506) the cor anglais takes a chord tone. In Ex. 206 (PDF 369) it doubles the fff tune with the oboes. I considered putting the cor anglais in unison with Cl 2 (E5–D5–C5/E♭5–D5), which would give the A♭ chord a second woodwind E♭5. It should stay as it is. PDF 119 says a tutti "does not mean every single instrument", and PDF 121 says woodwind adds little power against forte brass, so the gain at fff would be only colour that nobody hears. The cost is real: the cor anglais's next entry is the p espressivo duet with the solo cello at 171, and the bass clarinet's is the pp chorale bass at 175. Keeping both silent from 156 means those two colours return fresh after the forte coda. That matches the score's plan of returning to the opening colours at the end (c279).

### 3. Climaxes 122 and 160: different grading is correct (no change)

At 122 the heavy brass are fff. At 160 they are ff under a fff tune (c244). This might look as if the second climax is weaker. It is not, and the appendix supports both markings. Bar 122 is a chord climax (A♭ as the minor ninth over the G pedal, with no tune below Vln I/Fl 1). Its layout matches chords 4 and 5 (PDF 505), where every group, timpani included, is fff. Bar 160 is a melodic tutti, which matches chord 2 (woodwind and strings fff, brass ff) and Ex. 206. Bar 160 still sits above 122: the tune is in three octaves with Tpt 1 on A♭5, the bass arrival is new (♭VI), there is the suspended-cymbal stroke, and the tutti is wider (A♭1–A♭6 against G1–A♭6). This answers no earlier ruling; it confirms c244 and c318.
