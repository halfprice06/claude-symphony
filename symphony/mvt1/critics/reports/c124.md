Lens: orchestration (balance, doubling, register, colour, climaxes). Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III, "Harmony in the brass" and "Harmony in combined groups" (overlaying, crossing, enclosure), file "principles of form.pdf", PDF 100–114.

## What I studied

For the score I wrote small scripts over v143's MIDI. One prints every instrument group's sounding pitches each half bar for all 182 bars. Others list same-family brass seconds and sevenths, gaps in brass chords, clarinets placed inside the horns, horns placed inside the bassoons, and trumpet/trombone unisons. I checked the results against the PDF pages and the section files. I used no corpus files and no web sources: the assigned reading covered every finding.

## Findings

### F1. 158–161: Cl 2 sits inside the horns at the main climax (applied)

**Problem.** Cl 2 holds E4–D4–C4–D4 (`e'1 | d'1 | c'1 | d'1`), ff to fff. That register is inside Hn 3·4 (G3 to G4/C5). It also doubles Tpt 3 and Tbn 1 in unison on C4 at 160. Under fff brass the clarinet cannot be heard there, so it adds nothing.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns, but, in the upper register, and in the higher harmonic parts, a chord of four horns ... may be completed by clarinets." PDF 110: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets." The three tutti diagrams on PDF 111 put every woodwind above the trumpet notes.

**Fix.**
```
recTwoClTwo = { R1*11 | e''1\ff | d''1\< | c''1\fff | d''1 | R1*5 | }
```
The pitches and dynamics are unchanged. The part keeps the 6/4 chord's third (E) at 158, which no sustained brass part holds. It now joins the oboes and Cl 1 in the octave under the tune. This adds to c104 (Cl 1 up an octave) and undoes nothing.

**Protection.** "Climax scoring 158–166" is protected. My answer: no line, rhythm, dynamic or instrument changes. One doubling moves from a register the book advises against to the one it recommends.

### F2. 122–123: Ob 2 on F4 under the trumpets in the fff chord (applied)

**Problem.** In the only sustained fff chord of the development, Ob 2 enters on F4. That is below Tpt 1's A♭4, in unison with Hn 2, and in the middle of the brass. At 123 its F4 also sits a ninth under Ob 1's G5. The woodwind chord above the trumpets (A♭6 D6 | A♭5 F5 B4) lacks the D an octave below Fl 2.

**Evidence.** PDF 110–111 (row 22): woodwinds form the harmony above the trumpets. PDF 102: discords go to different colours. Cycle 119 already applied this to the oboe pair at 23–25 and 135–137.

**Fix.** In `devOboeTwo`:
```

```
(was `f'1\fff | f'1`). At 122 the woodwinds now sound a close B–D–F–A♭ above the brass, and at 123 the oboes are a fourth apart (G5/D5). The F still sounds in Hn 2 and Tbn 2.

**Protection.** 117–122 is protected (c104, layered dynamics c117). The dynamics, the brass and the A♭ neighbour are untouched. Only the second oboe's octave and degree change.

### F3. 39–40: Hn 4 doubles the bassoon's seventh, and the fifth is missing (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 3·4 and the bassoons form a soft four-part B♭7: Bsn 2 B♭2, Bsn 1 A♭3, Hn 4 A♭3, Hn 3 D4. The seventh A♭ is doubled in unison by horn and bassoon. There is no F at 39.1 in any part, so horns and bassoons give only three pitches.

**Evidence.** PDF 108: "Four-part harmony given to two bassoons and two horns, especially in soft passages, yields a finely-balanced tone ... crossing of parts is to be recommended ... the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons." The diagram on the same page shows horn, bassoon, horn, bassoon interlocked.

**Fix.** In `transHnFour`:
```
R1*4 | ges1\p\< | aes1 | f1 |
f1\f |
```
The voicing becomes B♭2 (Bsn) – F3 (Hn 4) – A♭3 (Bsn) – D4 (Hn 3). That is exactly the book's crossing: horns on the concords, the bassoon on the seventh. The seventh is no longer doubled. Hn 4 moves A♭3–F3–F3–G3 into the call at 41. At 40 it sits in unison with Tbn 2's F3, which is the PDF 103 juxtaposition.

**Protection.** 33–46 is protected (c51, melody), as are route 36–47 (c120) and Hn 3's D♭ at 37 (c42). No melody, key or bar-37 note changes.

### F4. 164: second between Hn 3 and Hn 4, p (applied)

**Problem.** On beat 3, Hn 3 moves A♭3→A3 while Hn 4 holds G3. That is a major second inside one horn pair, the only sustained same-colour second in the soft fade.

**Evidence.** PDF 102: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour." The PDF 103 diagram shows the dissonant note in horns and its neighbour in trombones.

**Fix.** In `recTwoHnFour`, `d2\p g2` becomes `d2\p r2`. G stays in the bassoons and in Vc/Cb (G2), so the ninth A3 now sounds against other colours. The brass-first fade and its dynamics are unchanged, and Hn 4 re-enters at 166.

### F6. 123: the seventh F sits a second under G in both horns and trombones (no change recommended)

Hn 1/2 hold G4/F4 and Tbn 1/2 hold G3/F3 for the whole bar. By PDF 102 the F belongs in a different colour from the G. Any move that clears one pair either drops the seventh from the brass or leaves the second in the other pair, and Hn 1's A♭→G resolution must stay. I am recording this as a knowing departure: Rimsky-Korsakov says "preferably", and the resolution bar of the climax carries the voice-leading.

### F7. 158–162: Tpt 3 in unison with Tbn 1 on C4/B3 (no change recommended)

PDF 104 calls trumpet/trombone juxtaposition "not so common, as this unites the two most powerful agents". Here Tpt 3 sits in its low register under Tpt 1·2 and the horns, and the unison gives the root and leading tone a firm core under the tune. The book does not forbid it, and the layout is protected, so I leave it.

### What the reading confirms

The brass writing follows the chapter closely in its main rules. Horns double trumpet and trombone notes in the tuttis (PDF 103–104). Where horns are not paired they are marked a degree louder (bar 12, f against trombones mf; 27–31). Woodwinds sit above the trumpets in every tutti except 122 and 158–161. Tremolo strings hold under the brass head at 167–170 (PDF 113). The horn quartet carries the German sixth at 12 (PDF 100). Horns are doubled by cellos or violas at 47–55 and 147–155, the one string/brass pairing PDF 113 calls "splendid".

Do not touch: the horn/trumpet/trombone juxtapositions and grading at 12, 27–32, 82, 140 and 167–170; the Hn 3·4 octave pedal 117–121; horn with cellos/violas at 47–55 and 147–155; the pp trombone chorale 175–179.
