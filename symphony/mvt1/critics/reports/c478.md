## What I checked, and how

1. **Articulation of doubled lines (Ex. 281, PDF 214–215).** Earlier scripted checks (c278, c378) compared dynamics and accents. Slurs were not compared. I regenerated the LilyPond event-listener output for v478 (/tmp/critic-478/ev/evcur-*.notes). For every pair of parts that doubles four or more consecutive notes in unison or octaves with the same rhythm, I compared slur starts and ends and articulation scripts on each onset (script: /tmp/critic-478/scripts/dbl.py). With bow marks set aside, the mismatches that remain are all intended:
   - 158–161: the woodwinds slur the climax tune while Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1 tongue it. The separate string bows and brass attacks at 158–160 are protected (c088, c118).
   - 98: the C.a., Vln II and cellos start their slur on 98.1 because they enter without the 97.4 pickup.
   - 72.3: the cellos and basses hold pp under the winds' accents (c278).
   - 40.3, 110 and 175–178: rest-based or bowing differences (Ob 1 enters after a semiquaver rest; B.cl closes its head slur; the double basses bow the chorale bass note by note under the slurred cellos).

   No pair voiced together articulates differently.
2. **Tempo words against `\mt` steps.** Every printed tempo word has a step. The MIDI keeps the animando tempo (118) through 64–65 until the rit. at 66, and the largamente (100) through 164 until the rit. at 165. This matches the print, because neither place has an "a tempo".
3. **Level of each restatement (Ex. 269: the repeat starts at a new level).** Every recurring idea enters at a different level in each statement (row 12). No restatement copies the dynamic shape of the one before it.
4. **Character words (Ex. 2A).** Every solo entry has a word (row 9). The vocabulary is espr., dolce, molto cantabile, molto espr. and morendo, kept for solos and for the last bars. Dvořák uses about the same set in Symphony No. 7/i (dolce, espressivo, marcato, con forza; reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M1).

## Findings

None of the points below reaches the bar of a real improvement. I list them so the next critic need not test them again.

### 1. Bars 52.4 and 152.4: the top note of the S answer phrase arrives straight after a diminuendo (no change)
**What I considered.** The cellos (52) and the violas and cellos (152) close the 4–3 sigh with `\>` and then leap or run up to F5, the highest note of the phrase. They have no hairpin into the F5. By Ex. 269 and Ex. 280 (PDF 205, 212), a lead's high point usually gets a crescendo into it.

**Why it stays.**
- At exactly that note Cl 1 enters on F5 mp, so the arrival is marked by a new colour rather than a hairpin. This is the device of Ex. 2A (PDF 18): the flutes' entry, not a louder lead, marks the moment, and Sebesky's variety principle (PDF 15) prefers it.
- The cellos reach F5 on the A string, which projects without a mark.
- A swell would make the answer phrase copy the shape of the antecedent at 49–50. Sebesky argues against that kind of repetition on PDF 19–20.
- The swells of 47–55 are protected (c018, c028, c068, c158, c168, c188). Nothing in the reading answers the reason they were kept.

### 2. Bars 171–174: the cor anglais motto has no hairpins under the solo cello's swells (no change)
**What I considered.** Ex. 280 (PDF 212) gives a background line its own hairpins, and c378 applied this to the C.a. fills at 147–152.

**Why it stays.** At 147–152 the fills move while the tune holds still. At 171–174 the cello moves and swells, and the C.a. holds the motto in long values. That is the situation of Ex. 277 (PDF 210): a long-note line under a moving lead, which needs character of contour, and the motto has it. Ex. 269 gives the counter-melody hairpins only because the lead there is static (PDF 207). This passage is protected as "C.a. answers at plain p" and as the solo/background grading at 171–174 (c158, c298, c358, c477). The reading supports that reason.

### 3. Bar 97: no character word on the ff S theme in B minor (no change)
**What I considered.** Dvořák 7/i writes "con forza" on its ff tutti restatements (Violino I part, line 70). In this movement the ff statements at 27, 82, 97, 117 and 131 carry no word.

**Why it stays.** In Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215) Sebesky marks loud ensemble writing with accents, sfz and hairpins, never with words, and words appear only on solo entries (Ex. 2A). At 97 the ff, the sul G in Vln II and the swells on every bar already tell players everything. A word would change nothing in the playing, and economy (PDF 12) argues against adding it. 97–102 is protected (c128, c186, c231, c248).

### 4. Bars 111–116: one six-bar "cresc." with few intermediate levels (no change)
**What I considered.** A master could stage the crescendo with marked levels along the way.

**Why it stays.** The winds already reach f at 115 and the timpani go mf < f at 115–116, so the staging exists (kept by c298). The rising S-head rhythm gives the "push into the next phrase" that Ex. 273 describes (PDF 208). "111-117 crescendo" is protected (c349).

### 5. Bar 12: the German sixth holds one flat f for the whole bar (no change)
**What I considered.** A hairpin through the intro's climax chord.

**Why it stays.** The diminuendo starts on the resolution at 13, which is where Ex. 284 (PDF 217–218) puts the drop in level. Bar 12 is the finale's first chord (IV 1), and its MIDI is to stay identical to v321 (c420).
