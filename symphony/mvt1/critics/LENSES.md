# Critic lenses (cycle N uses lens ((N-1) mod 10) + 1)
1. Melody and memorability: themes, motto, contour, rhythm, climaxes and development of melodic material. Compare with the great tunes of Tchaikovsky, Dvořák, Rachmaninoff and Elgar.
2. Harmony and voice leading: progressions, pedals, inner-voice motion, dissonance treatment, modulation plan.
3. Form, pacing and proportion: section lengths, the tension curve, transitions, the logic of the development, and recapitulation changes. Compare with corpus first movements.
4. Orchestration: balance, doubling, register, colour, use of each section, and climaxes. Sources: Rimsky-Korsakov and Adler.
5. Engraving and notation. Source: Gould's *Behind Bars*. Check dynamics, hairpins, slurs, beaming, ottavas, part-combining labels, rehearsal and tempo marks, and system and page layout. Inspect many pages of the PDF.
6. Playability, instrument by instrument: ranges, breathing, bowing, harp pedalling, timpani tuning, brass endurance, divisi, page turns.
7. Texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, countermelodies, fugato quality, imitation.
8. Expression: dynamics, articulation, phrasing slurs, tempo changes, rubato indications and expressive text.
9. Holistic comparison with one specific master first movement, a different one each time this lens comes up. The question: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Also compare with the version 10 cycles earlier and flag anything that got worse.
10. Cyclic design and emotional arc: the introduction, motto transformations, coda, and how the movement sets up a three-movement symphony.
