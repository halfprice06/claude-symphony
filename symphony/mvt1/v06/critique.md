## Changes from v5
- **Second-theme climax rewritten around the motto.** The consequent now breaks the two-bar rhythm. In the exposition: `es''4 c'''2.~ | c'''4 ces'''4 bes''2 | aes''2.~ …`. The major sixth E♭→C answers the motto's minor sixth G→E♭, and the slow chromatic fall C–C♭–B♭ is the motto's D–D♭–C. The phrase then lands deceptively on F minor. In the recapitulation the same gesture is `g''4 e'''2.~ | e'''4 es'''4 d'''2 | c'''4 es'''2~ …`, and the fff A♭ chord now arrives on its third (c''') before the leap up to e♭'''. That removes the fifths that V→♭VI produced.

## Problems now most visible
1. **The transition (bars 37–40) is a four-unit literal sequence:** the motto head on B♭, C, D, E♭, one bar each, identical rhythm. Four identical units is a student habit. Masters stop at two or three and then fragment the unit or shorten it to drive the climax (Beethoven, Brahms, Tchaikovsky 6/i bars 60–68). Rewrite: two full units, then half-bar fragments rising faster into the E♭7 arrival.
2. **The development's D1 dialogue** is also strictly sequential (A♭m, Em, Cm, each a one-bar question and answer). Major-third cycles are idiomatic (Liszt, Tchaikovsky), but the answers could vary in length or overlap the next question to tighten the momentum.
3. **Flute and oboe balance at bar 60.** The oboe is marked mf and the flute p on the same notes; the flute in that register needs the louder marking. Make them consistent.
4. **The A-section accompaniment harmony** is now good, but the harp RH figure in the A section begins each bar on a low note while the violas and second violins sustain. The texture may be thick between C4 and E♭5. Consider moving the harp up an octave in bars 51–55.
5. **The first-theme accompaniment** (syncopated tied chords, bars 15–22) is idiomatic, but it does not change for eight bars. A pizzicato cello figure or a horn pedal entry at bar 19 would mark the restart on F minor.

## Plan for v7
Rewrite the transition sequence (point 1). Then address points 3–5.
