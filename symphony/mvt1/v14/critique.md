## The second review (summary, ranked)
1. The first theme has no climax: its high point (b♭'' in bar 20) is on beat 3 and dropped at once, and every bar uses the same cell (rest, pickup, leap, chromatic fall). The same cell drives the transition, the fugato and the retransition.
2. The second theme's continuation (bars 51–55, and in 64–68, 140–143, 154–158) slides down by semitones for four bars (g'–g♭'–f'–f♭'–e♭'–d♭'–c'). It is predictable and sentimental. Six phrases also start with the same `2.~ 8 8` rhythm.
3. The development's build peaked below its own approach (bars 109–115). *Already fixed in v13.*
4. Bars 43–84 lose energy: almost nothing is forte, the closing group sits on an A♭ pedal at ppp for 8 bars, and the exposition has no cadential codetta.
5. The recapitulation drops the first theme's second phrase and its a♭''' peak; after bar 130 C minor never returns at forte, so the soft coda sounds like the music running out.
6. Long pedals (E♭ 41–46, A♭ 69–78, G 109–122, C 167–172); the second group moves one chord per bar; and in bar 12 the German sixth resolves early (F♯→G on beat 4 over the A♭ bass).
7. Over-doubling: contrabasses double the fugato subject at the cellos' octave (bars 85–87); five instruments play the second theme in D3 (97–102); brass only pad chords in 27–32 and 123–126; the first horn (mp) and cellos (mf) disagree on the same line at 47.
8. Accompaniment figures repeat for 13 bars (violas, cellos in 56–68 and 144–158), and the timpani part needs six pitches in the development, with some changes impossible to make in time.

I agree with all of them. Point 1 is the most important: a first theme without a peak is the clearest sign of a student work.
