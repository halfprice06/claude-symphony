# Critique of v11

Method: pages 12–13 (bars 145–172) read at 60 dpi, build log, `analysis.txt`. Length 7:12 (172 bars).

## Changes from v10 (review point 6)
- **Coda cut from 18 to 14 bars** (`sections.txt`: coda 14). Più lento for 8 bars, Lento for the last 6.
- Bars 159–162: the second theme's head returns in C minor in the cellos (e♭'–c'–a♭'–g'), against the horn motto (g–e♭'–d'–d♭'–c'–b). The two main ideas are heard together for the first time. The harmony is Cm, Fm, Fm (Neapolitan colour from the cellos' d♭'), G.
- Bars 163–167: the motto in augmentation (whole notes) in the cellos, bass clarinet and basses, G–E♭–D–D♭–C, while the clarinet plays its rising sixth e♭'–c'' and falls c''–d♭''–c''. The last statement of the motto is the slowest.
- Bars 167–172: the first violins play the motto's sixth one last time (g'–e♭'' then e♭''–d''–d♭''–c'') over a C pedal in basses, timpani roll ppp, horns on the open fifth.
- **Last chord:** C minor with the violas moving e♭' to e♮' under the fermata. The piece ends on an unresolved third: the major third enters only as the sound dies, so the ending is neither clearly minor nor major.

## What still looks weak
1. Bars 155–158 (end of the recapitulation) run at mf with harp arpeggios into a drop to p at bar 159. The joint is a full stop in texture. A one-bar diminuendo in bar 158 is written, but the harp's last arpeggio continues to the barline. Acceptable.
2. The violas' E♮ at the end is a single line inside a pp chord. Players will find it, but the listener may not. Doubling it in the second violins (who hold g') would weaken the effect; leave it.
3. The introduction (review point 7) is unchanged.

## Plan for v12
Introduction: break the rising-sixth sequence at bar 11; give the cor anglais the clarinet's answer in bars 5–8. Transition: shorten the E♭7 block at bars 41–44 if it can be done without changing the bar count.
