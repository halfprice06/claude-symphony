## Findings

### 1. 147–150: the dominant before the reprise is four bars of tied whole notes; the trumpets' entry is a block chord and the allargando cannot be heard (applied)

Problem. After the stretto (139–146, every bar a new entry with eighth-note figures and the timpani's motive x), 147–150 is ff V7 over G in whole notes in every part except the timpani roll and the bass pickup on 150 beat 4. Bars 149–150 carry the allargando, but 150 is tied in every part, so the broadening is only heard as a longer chord. The trumpets, which re-enter here after 33 bars' rest (last at 110–113), hold D5/B4/G4 like the horns and trombones. The top voice is F6–D6–F6–F6 (Fl 1) and leaps F6→C6 into the reprise.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 71: the natural-scale tones, "with the help of rhythm", give the fanfares and calls that are "best adapted to the character of brass instruments", in the middle and upper register of trumpets and horns; PDF 72 (No. 70, *Kitesh* [65], PDF 242) shows three trumpets and four horns in fanfare rhythm over held wind chords. PDF 73: the brass's qualities are "energetic power ... simplicity". Dvořák 9/iv 204–207 (corpus): a held ff wind chord is articulated by half-note restrikes in violas, cellos and basses and by single fz half-note trumpet strokes, one per bar, rising through the chord, before the fff entry at 208. Rimsky PDF 67–68 (natural order, flutes on top) for the top line.

Fix (applied, retr.ily):
```
retrTptOne = { R1*4 | R1*8 | d''1->\ff | d''2-> f''2-> | d''2-> f''2-> | g''2.-> r4 | }
retrTptTwo = { R1*4 | R1*8 | b'1->\ff | b'2-> d''2-> | b'2-> d''2-> | d''2.-> r4 | }
retrFluteOne = { ... | f'''1->\ff | d'''1 | f'''1 | d'''2. r4 | }
```
The trumpets now strike the dominant's natural-scale tones in half notes over the held tutti, D–F twice and then the root G5 held in the allargando bar, so the broadening is heard in the spacing of the strokes and the four bars have a line that peaks on 150. Fl 1 restrikes D6 at 150 (joining Fl 2's held D6), so the top steps D6–C6 into F1's head at 151; the seventh F stays in Ob 2, Tbn 1, Vln I and cellos. Harmony, bass G (c073), Tpt 3 and the stretto are unchanged. A first version that rose G4–B4–D5–F5 made octaves with the flutes and violins, which alternate chord positions at every barline; in the applied version every trumpet move at a barline is contrary or oblique to theirs. The pairwise check (tools/critic-scripts/c002/par.py) shows no new parallels; it removes two old ones at 150→151 (Fl 1 F6/Tbn 1 F4 → C6/C4 octaves, and fifths between Tpt 1·2 D5→G4 and Tpt 3 / Hn 3·4 G4→C4).

### 2. 199–210: the "poco a poco più animato" steps cut movement I's six-bar hammered tutti (applied)

Problem. 199–204 is movement I 27–32 (six bars, owed item 7); 205–210 is the F1-head build over the A♭ pedal in 2-bar units (205–206 horns, 207–208 winds and violins, 209–210 crescendo). global.ily writes `s1*4 \mt 172 s1*4 \mt 176 s1*4`, so the first tempo step falls at 203, the fifth bar of the quotation, and the second at 207. Dan hears the renders, so the MIDI steps matter. A quotation of movement I's tutti should keep one tempo; the acceleration belongs to the build that drives into 211.

Evidence. The phrase map above (Reading notes and "What else I studied"); the focus rule that tempo changes land on phrase boundaries applies to the MIDI steps as much as to the printed words. The printed "poco a poco" at 199 did not say where the motion starts.

Fix (applied, global.ily):
```
\tempo "Tempo I" 2 = 84
s1*6 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco a poco più animato" \mt 172 s1*2 \mt 176 s1*4
```
199–204 stay at 2 = 84; the text and the first step move to 205, the second step stays at 207 (2 = 88, as before). R is not touched. The new text at 205 sits clear of the strings' entry on page 11.

### 3. Confirmed, no change: the development's melodic rhythm

The two F2 statements (115–122 bassoon, 123–130 hand-over) are 8-bar antecedents ending on V, and the development never states a consequent; that is the right economy for a development and keeps F2's full 16-bar shape for 51, 67 and 171. The climax line at 110–113 (G6–A♭6–F6–E6–F6, A♭ syncopated across beat 2) has its own rhythm against the scale figure in the low strings. The 4+4+8+8+8+4 grouping of 99–134 needs no change.
