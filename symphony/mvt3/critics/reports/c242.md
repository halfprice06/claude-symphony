## Findings

### 1. Bars 167-170: the end of the recapitulation's first group goes from VI to vii°6/5 by a bare tritone leap in the bass; use a French sixth from VI (applied)

Problem. In v242, VI (A♭ in the bass, 167) moves at 168 to D-F-A♭ with D in the bass and then to B-D-F-A♭ over B (169-170) before i at 171. The bass leaps A♭ to D (a tritone) and then D to B. The music never sounds the dominant root G at this phrase end, and the B-D-F-A♭ sonority over B is sounded for two bars, which takes away from the stop chord's own bars before 222. The score has no French sixth at all, though this is the type A&S call the most pungent and the best neighbour of V.

Evidence. A&S PDF 512-513: "Another frequent point of departure for augmented 6ths is VI"; the chord is made by adding ♯4 to an extended VI, and "augmented 6ths frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI ... because they form a convincing transition to V." PDF 503: augmented sixths "signal the beginning or end of a phase in the tonal movement or the form", and 168-170 is the end of the recap P group. PDF 504-505: II4/3 over ♭6 with ♯4 is the French 4/3, and the 2̂ (D) in it removes the parallel fifths that a German sixth moving straight to V would give (PDF 506-507). PDF 515: the augmented sixth may move to V7, with the 7th in an inner voice. PDF 515-516: the bass may continue upward by step past the dominant.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-242/work/sections/recP.ily, recP bars 18-20 = 168-170):

```
VlnOne:  f''8 g'' aes'' f'' d''4) r4   ->  fis''8 g'' aes'' fis'' d''4) r4
VlnTwo:  f'8 g' aes' f' d'4) r4        ->  fis'8 g' aes' fis' d'4) r4
ClTwo:   es'1 | f'1\> | f'1~ | f'1\pp   ->  es'1 | d'1\> | f'1~ | f'1\pp
BsnOne:  es1\f | f1\> | f1~ | f2.\pp r4 ->  es1\f | fis1\> | g1 | f2.\pp r4
BsnTwo:  aes,1\f | d1\> | b,1~ | b,1\pp ->  aes,1~\f | aes,1\> | g,1 | b,1\pp
HnTwo:   es'1\f | f'1\> | d'1\p | R1    ->  es'1\f | d'1~\> | d'1\p | R1
Vc:      aes,1\f\upbow | d1\> | b,1~ | b,2.\p r4    ->  aes,1\f\upbow | aes,1\> | g,1( | b,2.)\p r4
Cb:      aes,,1\f\upbow | d,1\> | b,,1~ | b,,2.\p r4 ->  aes,,1\f\upbow | aes,,1\> | g,,1( | b,,2.)\p r4
```

with a "% c242" comment line after the "% 167-170: link" comment. The sounding result (MIDI slice): 167 A♭ major (VI); 168 A♭-C-D-F♯, a French sixth over the held A♭; 169 G-B-D-F-A♭, V7 with a minor ninth over G; 170 B-D-F-A♭ over B (V6/5 with ♭9, one bar only); 171 i. A♭ in the bass falls to G and F♯ in the violins and bassoon rises to G: the outward resolution to the octave on 5̂ (PDF 503). The bass line becomes A♭-A♭-G-B-C, a ♭6-5 motion that prepares the Phrygian cadence of the second theme at 177-178. The second theme still enters from an inverted dominant, so there is no root-position V-i in C here.

What was kept. Cl 1 (C5, then A♭4 held to the end), Hn 1 (C5, then A♭4 held 168-169), the violas (C4 C4 B3 D4; c202's C4-B3 at 169), all dynamics and the order of the diminuendo (c214), Cl 2 F4 and Bn 2 B2 held at 170 (c067), Bn 1 F3 to E♭3 at 171 (c164), and no G4 a semitone under A♭4 (c137). The violins' figure keeps its shape and rhythm; only F becomes F♯, and F♯ is spelled as a raised note going up to G (PDF 496-497). For c218's bowing point, the Vc and Cb G-B (169-170) are slurred in one up-bow that ends at the frog before the pizzicato. HISTORY.md lists "135-171" for c223 without a reason; this change does not touch the F1 melody, the recap's layout or any quoted material in that range.

Theme and quotation status: no quotation and no finale theme (F1, F2, the chorale top line) is changed.

### 2. Bars 1-6: the direct German sixth to V gives inner parallel fifths E♭-D (not applied)

Problem. At 1→2 and 5→6, oboe, Cl 2, Hn 2 and violas move E♭ to D against A♭ to G, which gives parallel fifths in the inner voices.

Evidence. PDF 506: moving straight from a German sixth to V gives parallel fifths. PDF 507: anticipating 2̂ (D) over the German sixth gives a passing French sixth, "a very frequent way" to avoid them. PDF 514-515 and the review on PDF 526: fifths in the inner voices are tolerated.

Decision. Not applied. Bars 1-6 are protected, c002 kept these fifths under PDF 526, and they are not in the outer voices. The possible fix would be, in the violas at bar 1 beat 4, `es'4` → `d'4`, but the German-sixth sound of the opening is the cyclic motto and should stay complete to the barline.

### 3. Bar 243: III♯ is approached from ii7 rather than an augmented sixth (not applied)

Problem. A&S PDF 523 shows an augmented sixth leading to III♯. At 243 the score reaches E major from ii7.

Decision. Not applied. The augmented sixth to E would need D♯ moving to E, and that motion is used deliberately at 219-220 (c220). Repeating it at 243 would weaken the earlier one. The bar is also inside the protected 235-247 plan.

### 4. Bars 245-246: V4/3 to I could take the Tchaikovsky French sixth (not applied)

Problem. PDF 522-523 shows a French sixth formed by inflecting the bass of V4/3 down by a semitone on its way to I. The score has a plain V4/3 to I at 245-246.

Decision. Not applied. 235-247 is protected and the plain V4/3 to I is part of the plan that keeps the root-position cadence for 261-263.

### 5. Bar 38: a German sixth under F1's cadence (not applied)

Problem. The phrase end at 38 goes to V by a diatonic iv6. A German sixth would give the end of the phrase more weight (PDF 503).

Decision. Not applied. The F1 melody has A♭ at that point; an F♯ in an inner voice would clash with the A♭ an augmented sixth's top-voice resolution needs, and the melody (a finale theme) would have to change.

### 6. Bars 57 and 177: Phrygian cadences could become Italian sixths (not applied)

Problem. PDF 504 derives the augmented sixth from the Phrygian cadence by adding ♯4. Both Phrygian cadences of the second theme are plain.

Decision. Not applied. c152 kept them plain on purpose, and after finding 1 the ♭6-5 bass of 168-169 already prepares 177-178. An augmented sixth at both 168 and 177 would be too many in ten bars.

### 7. Bars 147-150: augmented sixth before the recapitulation (not applied)

Problem. The retransition reaches V without an augmented sixth (PDF 516-518 shows the augmented sixth in a return to the tonic).

Decision. Not applied. 147-150 is protected, and the lament bass there already makes the approach (PDF 510-511).

### 8. Coda, 263-279: ♭2 augmented sixth to I (not applied)

Problem. PDF 522 shows an augmented sixth on ♭2 resolving to I near the end of a piece.

Decision. Not applied. The coda is protected, and its ♭2 neighbours over the tonic pedal already use the Neapolitan in the way PDF 495 describes.
