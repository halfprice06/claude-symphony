## What else I studied

Tchaikovsky 4/iv from memory of the score (no copy in the local corpus): the opening fff string-and-woodwind scales in several octaves with cymbals; the folk tune "In the field a birch tree stood" scored differently at every return (woodwinds, then strings, then with counter-figures, then brass); a development that rises by sequence to the return of movement I's fanfare in the brass; a coda in which the tune and the scales are stacked over hammered tonic and dominant. The two habits that matter here are Tchaikovsky's climaxes with the violins in octaves at the top of the texture and his rescoring of every return of a tune. I compared v59 with v49 by source diff (sections, global.ily, score.ly), by MIDI dumps of 87–91, 107–114, 151–155, 187–198, 247–279, and by the page images of both PDFs. I re-ran themecheck against themes-finale-quotes.ly (34/34) and checked the scherzo record against scherzo v38 (41/41). The scherzo quotation at 275–276 still matches theme A's head (scherzo Vln I 13–14, E–B–B–E | G–F♯–F → C–G–G–C | E–D–D♭–C, major, a major third down, values kept). No quotation needs updating.

v49 → v59, the five areas named in the focus:
- Development climax 110–113: better. It was f without trumpets; now ff with trumpets, tuba, timpani and a woodwind doubling (c053, c054, c056). One weakness remains and is now the main one: Finding 1.

But c057 moved Vln II up to Vln I's octave at 89–90, so the crescendo into the ff codetta lost the octave it had in v49. That is a regression: Finding 2.

## Findings

### 1. 110–113: the development climax has its melody in only one string octave, at G5 (applied)
Problem: the climax melody G–A♭–F–E–F is in Vln I at G5–A♭5, the middle of the violin. Vln II plays inner notes E5–C5–D♭5–C5, and those already sound in Ob 2, Cl 1 and Tpt 2. Only Fl 1 is at the upper octave. The brass are f and the strings ff. Here, at the loudest point of the development, the violins are in the register Adler calls nondescript. In Tchaikovsky 4/iv the violins carry a climax in octaves at the top of the texture.
Evidence: Adler PDF 152, ways to strengthen a string melody: item 2, Vln II doubled by Vln I an octave higher, and item 6, spread over several octaves. PDF 154, Tchaikovsky Serenade: melody notes always doubled. PDF 267, Dvořák Carnival: flutes an octave above the first violins. PDF 144: put the main melody in the instrument's best register.
Fix (dev.ily):
```
devVlnOne 110-113: g'''1\ff | aes'''2( f'''2 | f'''2 e'''2) | f'''2-> r2 |
devVlnTwo 110-113: g''1\ff | aes''2( f''2 | f''2 e''2) | f''2-> r2 |
```
Every pitch class, the harmony, the dynamics and c054's woodwind doubling stay the same. Vln I now doubles Fl 1, and Vln II doubles Fl 2 and Ob 1. On the do-not-touch "climax 107–113" (c058), which gives no reason: the dynamics (ff strings and winds, f brass), the timpani, the lament and the cadence are untouched. Only the violins' octave changes. p5.py now reports G5→G6 over C3→C4 at 109→110. That is the same fifth C–G moved up an octave in both voices, not a parallel.

### 2. 89–90: the crescendo into the codetta lost an octave since v49 (applied)
Problem: in v49 the violins played the scale figure in octaves through 87–90. Since c057, Vln II is at Vln I's pitch at 89–90. c057's reason was good: the low octave ran through the 6/3 voices and clashed A♮ against A♭. But the result is that the texture narrows just as the crescendo into the ff codetta at 91 starts.
Evidence: Adler PDF 137, Ex. V-14: a crescendo built by adding octaves. PDF 250–251: 8′+4′ octave doubling is transparent and forceful, where adding unisons only thickens.
Fix (clos.ily): keep c057's Vln II line and move Vln I and Fl 1 up an octave instead:
```
closVlnOne 89-90: r4 f'''8(\< es''' d''' c''') bes''( aes'' | bes''8 c''' d''' es''') f'''4-> f''4->\!
closFluteOne 89-90: r4 f'''8(\< es''' d''' c''') bes''( aes'' | bes''8 c''' d''' es''') f'''4-> r4\!
```
This also mirrors 87–88, which peaks on B♭5, a fourth higher. With the bass resting on beat 3 of 90, the analyser read the new F6 over Vln II's F5 as octaves after the E♭s of beat 2. So the bass now holds E♭ through beat 3 and is released for the pickup (PDF 240: the held bass gives the cadence weight; PDF 245, Strauss: the sustained part stops so the anacrusis is heard):
```

closBsnTwo 90: es2.-> r4\!   closBsnOne 90: g2.-> r4\!
```

### 3. 187–194: the C-major return of F2 is scored as its E♭ statement at 67–74 (applied)
Problem: 187–194 uses the scoring of 67–74 a minor third lower: Vln I tune, Vln II an octave below, violas, the cellos' counter-melody, Bn a2 bass, harp arpeggios, horns from 195. This is F2's fourth statement and the violins' second. It is the recapitulation's turn to C major, which points ahead to the coda, yet a listener hears nothing new. Tchaikovsky in 4/iv rescores every return of his tune.
Evidence: Adler PDF 238: Schubert scores each main thematic element differently "to enhance the clarity of the form". PDF 255: the third time, alter something. PDF 267: a single wind (Brahms: one bassoon) doubling the violin tune an octave lower adds warmth and brings the melody out. PDF 270, Copland: Vln II quietly doubled by a horn.
Fix (recS.ily): Hn 1 p dolce doubles Vln II, the tune's lower octave (C4–D5, the horn's singing register), and breathes after 190. It then continues into its existing 195 part:
```
recSHnOne = { R1*16 | g'2.(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" e'4) | d'4( c'4 e'4 g'4) | d''2( c''4 b'4) | a'2( g'2) \breathe | f'2.( a'4) | g'4( f'4 e'4 d'4) | e'2( c'2) | d'1 | c'1\p | ...
```
I chose a horn and not the clarinets because c057/c058 removed the clarinets from 187–198 as sustaining doublers, and this does not bring them back. The bassoon is out because the tune's lower octave would sit at G4–D5, the bassoon's strained top. Hn 2 rests, so the shared staff shows "1." and no dynamics conflict.

No other finding: the remaining principles in the table are followed, or were decided by earlier cycles for stated reasons that still hold (theme B's Ob/Cl/Tpt unison, kept by c019 and c029; the single-octave P head at 247–254, see above).
