## Findings

### 1. The finale's quotations of movements I and II have no record, so they can drift unnoticed over the remaining 170 cycles (coherence/themes-finale-quotes.ly)
**Problem.** The finale records and checks only its two scherzo quotations. The twelve quotations listed above, which carry most of the symphony's cyclic meaning, are checked by nobody. A later finale critic could re-voice 224–228, cut the pickup at 234 or re-rhythm 211–218, and themecheck would still report 8/8.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 211–212: the themes sit in one shared session with version numbers, so that a team's music "feels seamlessly composed by a single person". PDF 200: a reference works only if the listener can recognise the source. The scherzo's record catches its own changes; nothing catches the finale's changes to movement I or II material.
**Fix (prepared and tested).** /tmp/critic-030/themes-finale-quotes.ly is the current file plus 12 variables and 27 `%@` lines (hammered head and tenor line 199, theme B 211, chorale 224/226, pulse 229, S head 235, counter 235, bass 235, horn call 243, P head 247, brass S head 247, last chorale 255). Copy it over coherence/themes-finale-quotes.ly; that file belongs to this thread. `python3 themecheck.py themes-finale-quotes.ly <version> --beats 4` then gives 35/35 on v30 and on the scratch copy. Add the quotation list above to finale.md under "Themes quoted from other movements". The stop chord (a chord) and the B→C couplings stay as prose, because the checker compares single lines. This does not lock anything. A deliberate change is allowed, but it must be reported and the record refreshed, as the scherzo does.

### 2. The crescendo into the arrival (219–222) is linear and covers the violas' D♯→E (applied)
**Problem.** In v30 the woodwinds and strings start their hairpin on the sub. p at 219 and the brass at 220, and all run straight to fff at 223. The sub. p therefore lasts one beat. The owed E♭/E♮ closing (violas D♯4→E4 at 219–220, doubled by Ob 1 and Hn 3) is an inner voice that sounds while 26 parts are already growing.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 216–217, Fig. 5.7–5.8: a calculated crescendo begins slowly, becomes pronounced in its last third and is written with intermediate marks (pp … p … mf … ff), because a single hairpin is "vague". The allargando at 221–222 already makes the last two bars the longest.
**Fix (applied, codaTwo.ily, all 26 parts, equal dynamics on shared staves):** 219 `\subP` alone (no hairpin), 220 `\<`, 221 `\mp\<`, 222 `\f\<` → 223 `\fff` (unchanged). Example: `codaTwoTptOne … | c''1\subP | c''1\< | b'1\mp\< | b'1\f\< | }`. Sub. p at 219 and fff at 223 are kept as protected.

### 3. Five cymbal crashes at 265–273 waste the reserved colour (applied)
**Problem.** The cymbal is held back for the arrival (223) and was deliberately removed from 247 and 251 (c028). It then crashes at 263, 265, 267, 269, 271, 273 and 278: seven strokes in 16 bars, which turns the movement's arrival colour into a beat marker.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 227: the drum ensemble is absent at the first climax and enters later to "reward" the hero. PDF 235/237–238: synths are kept for "the only time … they make an overt appearance". PDF 243: absence is itself an aesthetic decision. Corpus: Dvořák 9/iv has one cymbal stroke (percussion.notesF.ily, `cymbalmF`, bar 64), and Tchaikovsky 5 has none. finale.md already says "cymbals and bass drum at 223, 263, 278".
**Fix (applied, finale.ily):** `finalePerc = \drummode { R1*16 | \tweak style #'xcircle cymc1->\fff | R1 | R1*12 | R1 | <\tweak style #'xcircle cymc~ bd>1->\fff | cymc4 r4 r2\fermata | }`. The timpani pulse, hammered basses and brass carry 265–273.

### 4. The brass statement of movement I's S head at 247 has no pickup, so its rising sixth is lost (applied; this changes a movement I quotation)
**Problem.** At 247 Tpt 1, Hn 1·2, Cl 1·2 and Vla start the S head on its E, after the trumpets' horn-call C6 (a falling sixth). Movement I's C-major statement (146–150, Hn 1 + Vla G3–E4) and the violins' statement here (234–235) both open with the rising sixth G→E, which is the falling major motto's first interval. Without it the brass statement does not start the way the violins' statement does.
**Evidence.** themes-mvt1.ly mvtOneThemeSCmajor, invariant: rhythm `4 | 2.~ 8 8 | 4 2 4` with the anacrusis. Casanelles PDF 200: recognition depends on what the listener heard before, and the listener heard the pickup at 234.
**Fix (applied, sHead.ily, bar 246):** Hn 2 `c'2.) g4` (G3, inside the V4/3); Cl 1 `f'2. g4\!` (the F's resolution to E comes at 247); Vla `… g'4) g4\!` (the violas leave the violins' run on beat 4). Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1 keep the horn call's C to the barline. Movement I's thread need not change anything, since its record already describes this form. Note it in finale.md.

### 5. finale.md has wrong bar numbers and descriptions for four movement I and scherzo links (record only)
**Problem.** (a) The chorale top line is at 224–228, not 223–227. (b) 138 and 222 present the stop chord in movement I's two different layouts (bass B as at I 26; bass D as at I 138), and finale.md does not say which. (c) 265–272 uses the head rhythm with G–E–D–C–B–C, not the literal head. (d) The scherzo link names a cello E3 that the scherzo v09 does not end on.
**Evidence.** themes-mvt1.md "Differences" (the v221 source wins); scherzo v09 coda.ily (`codaVc … e,4\pp` pizz. at 383).
**Fix.** Edit finale.md with the corrected wording in the quotation list above. No score change.
