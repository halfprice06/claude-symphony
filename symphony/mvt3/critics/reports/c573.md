## Findings

### F1 (applied). 235–242: the timpani rest at the start of the S head

- **Problem.** The timpani roll on C through 229–233 under the plagal close and end on a p quarter at 234.1. Three beats later, at 235, the same drum starts a new pp roll on the same C, "near the rim", and holds it to 238. The coda's quiet stage (Poco più mosso, molto cantabile: movement I's second-theme head) therefore begins with the close's colour still sounding. The new colours of this stage are the violins sul A, the clarinet and viola counter, and the harp's first notes since bar 134. The roll sits on C3, just under the harp's right-hand arpeggios (G3–G4), where a drum roll blurs an instrument with little sustaining power. The timpani had played at 223–224 and 229–234, and from 243 they play almost without a break to 279.
- **Reading.** PDF 136: "Neither musical feeling nor the ear itself can stand, for long, the full resources of the orchestra"; "a group of instruments which has been silent for some time gains fresh interest upon its reappearance"; kettle-drums come after the brass in the order of use, and the percussion "is seldom employed". PDF 116: what decides the scoring is "whether a given passage is a complement to or a contrast with what goes before and comes after, whether it forms a climax or merely a step". The S head is the coda's contrast after the fff arrival, and its scoring should mark the new stage rather than carry over the previous one.
- **Evidence.** The C pedal does not depend on the drum. It is held by the basses (C1, p, tied 235–238), the cellos (C2 at 235–236) and the harp's left hand (C1). The harmony is complete in Hn 3·4, the cellos and the harp. The roll was kept at c249 under Rimsky's general permission of a timpani tremolo as accompaniment (PDF 143). No one has weighed it against PDF 116 and 136, so removing it is not churn. With the rest, the timpani's G roll p < at 243.3, which leads the crescendo into the Più allegro at 247, becomes their first sound after eight and a half bars, as PDF 136 asks of a fresh entry.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-573/work/sections/sHead.ily).**

The "ord." at 243 is dropped because "near the rim" is gone. A c573 comment is added above the line. Nothing else changes: no other part, no theme or quotation, no layout. Page 13 has the same systems, and the timpani rest from 235 to 243.2.

### F2 (tested on paper, declined). 101–102 and 105–106: pizzicato cellos and basses under the wind answer

- **Point.** These are the only bars in the movement where woodwind and horns sound with no strings. PDF 121 says pizzicato cellos and basses "render the sustained notes in the wood-wind more distinct".
- **Why declined.**
  - The low strings have just played the arco sf chord on 101.1, which is Rimsky's own sf-p device (PDF 129). A pizzicato on 101.2, a quarter later at 2 = 84, is not playable.
  - On 102.1 the winds hold the neighbour chord (Hn A♭4/C4 over E), so a pluck there would stress a passing sonority.
  - The protection "the two ideas apart at 99–102" keeps the strings' call and the winds' answer in separate groups.
  - PDF 121 concerns a wind tutti, and these bars are a short answer, not a tutti.
- I did not apply it.
