## Findings

### 1. 243: the E-major chord hides the scherzo's last pitch in inner voices (applied)

**Bars.** 243, Vln I upper desk.

**Problem.** The E-major chord at 243 is the finale's one sound in the scherzo's key. Its chromatic tone G♯ falls to G only at G♯4 (Ob 1, lower Vln I) and G♯3 (Hn 4). The upper desk of Vln I holds E5, a pitch that Tpt 1 and Fl 2 already play.

The scherzo ends on Vln I G♯5 (369–376), and the finale's first move is Vln I A♭5 → G5 (bars 1–2). The most direct link between the last two movements is therefore absent where the score recalls the scherzo's key.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 600–601 (section 16): key areas "project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece". In a chromatic area the motive returns enharmonically reinterpreted: in the Appassionata, E♭–F♭–E♭ becomes E♭–E♮–E♭.
- PDF 593 (Waldstein, Ex. 32-12): III♯ "resolves" into its diatonic counterpart. Here that is G♯ → G♮ inside the E–G♯–B sonority.
- PDF 588: G♯ here is a true enharmonic of bar 1's A♭, because it falls as A♭ does.
- c083 (A&S Unit 15, PDF 233–234): III in major is recognized by its chromatic tone. That argues for putting the tone where it is heard.

### 2. Bars 1–2: the common tone is not on top; leave it as it is, and correct the do-not-touch wording (tested, not applied)

**Bars.** 1–2 and 5–6, flutes.

**What I tested.** Widmung's principle (PDF 586–587) is that the common tone A♭/G♯ is held in the top voice. At bar 1, Fl 1 C6 sits a third above the link pitch, and it has done so since v27. I tried Fl 1 A♭6→G6 and Fl 2 A♭5→G5.

**Why I rejected it.** The build reported outer-voice octaves at bars 2 and 6: A♭6→G6 over A♭1→G1. The link pitch is the German sixth's ♭6, and the bass resolves it, so it cannot lead the top voice without doubling the bass line. The join instead follows Schubert's variant (PDF 587, row 4): the held tone becomes the bass of the new chord. I reverted the change.

**Fix (text only).** HISTORY cycle 26 lists as do-not-touch "bars 1–2 voicing with A♭4/A♭5 on top continuing the scherzo's G♯". It should read "A♭4/A♭5 in Hn 1 and Vln I (Fl 1 C6 above), continuing the scherzo's G♯", so that a later critic does not repeat this experiment.
