## Findings

### 1. 263-264: after the last cadence nothing launches the close; bring back the opening scale figure (applied)

Bars: 263-264, Vln I, Vln II, Vla.

Problem: The symphony's only root-position V-I in C lands at 263 fff. Since c078 the winds and brass release on beat 4 of 264, so 264 is a held string chord with the timpani roll. Then 265 starts the C-major F1 ff. These two bars carry no motion and no material. A master usually uses this moment to start the coda with something from the movement's own opening. Beethoven 3/iv, bar 431: the Presto opens with the movement's first run in the strings over a tied ff wind chord. Here the movement's opening string figure (bars 3-4, G-F-E♭-D-C-B | C-D-E♭-F-G-G, Vln I at G5, Vln II and Vla at G4) never comes back after bar 4. The German sixth of bar 1 does come back at 261.

Evidence: Adler PDF 224: Ravel's "clever stroke is the return of the exact opening colors" at the end. Adler PDF 213: Bizet's scale in the clarinets and bassoons before the repeat "anticipates the new scoring", and his runs dovetail from bottom to top. Adler PDF 230: Schoenberg adds a figure built from the bar's own pitches to enrich a tutti. Corpus: Eroica finale p. 44, bar 431. The texture is the same as here: the winds hold ff while the strings run.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-079/work/sections/finale.ily): the cadence chord stays on beat 1 for all strings. Then the upper strings play bars 3-4 in C major, at the same octaves and with the same slurs and accents. Vc, Cb, the timpani, the winds and the brass are unchanged. Vln I leaves its octave doubling of the chorale's last C after one beat. The line itself keeps sounding for seven beats in Tpt 1·2, Fl 1 and Cl 1, and themecheck still passes choraleLast VlnOne. The protected top line's pitches and rhythm are unchanged.

```
finaleVlnOne 262-264:  b''1 | c'''4\fff g''8( f''8 e''8 d''8) c''8( b'8 | c''8 d''8 e''8 f''8) g''4-> g'4-> |
finaleVlnTwo 263-264:  <e'' g''>4\fff g'8( f'8 e'8 d'8) c'8( b8 | c'8 d'8 e'8 f'8) g'4-> g4-> |
finaleVla    263-264:  e'4\fff g'8( f'8 e'8 d'8) c'8( b8 | c'8 d'8 e'8 f'8) g'4-> g4-> |
```

The figure ends on G, the upbeat to F1's C at 265. In bar 4 the same G leads to the German sixth on A♭. The winds' release on beat 4 of 264 now coincides with the strings' last G, so the release and the run work together. For the composer: finale.md item 10 should say that Vln I doubles the last chorale only to 263 beat 1, and the "Opening" or "Ending" paragraph can note that the scale figure returns at 263-264 in C major. This adds a recall of the finale's own opening material. No theme (F1, F2, chorale top line) and no quotation of another movement changes, so the other movement threads need not be told.

### 2. 251-254: the trumpets leave the S head in the middle of its phrase (not applied)

Problem: Stravinsky changes colour only at phrase boundaries and "never breaks up a phrase needlessly" (Adler PDF 228). The S head's second phrase is 251-254. Tpt 1·2 play 251-252 and drop out at 253, and the oboes finish the phrase.

Decision: no change. The break is not needless. c076 gave the trumpets two bars before the ten-bar chorale at 255 for endurance. Moving the rest to 251-254 would empty half of owed item 6's brass statement. Horns 1·2, Cl 1, Ob 1·2 and the violas carry 253-254 at ff, so the line does not thin out. I record this so a later critic does not re-raise it without that reason.
