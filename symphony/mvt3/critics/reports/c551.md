## Findings

**1. Bars 39–40: violas join Vln II on the lower octave of F1's head (applied in the scratch copy).**

Problem: At 39–40 the violins answer the cellos' B♭-minor F1 head of 37–38 with the head in two octaves (Vln I F5 down to G4, Vln II F4 down to G3), ff > p, as the phrase fades into the sul G echo at 41–42. The lower octave is in Vln II alone. It ends on A♭3–G3, the bottom of the violin, inside a held ff wind chord. Meanwhile the violas hold D4, a doubling of Bn 1's D4.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 60 gives this exact case. Violins with violas in octaves is "a common method, especially when the lower octave in the melody happens to go below the open G string". He also says the distribution Vln I / Vln II+Violas is to "give the lower part a fuller and more cantabile quality". Ex. 28 (PDF 204–205, *Snegourotchka*) shows it: a soft cantabile with Vln I above, and Vln II with violas at the unison an octave below. PDF 55 adds that violas carry a line best when doubled by other strings.

At 39–40 the head is the last cantabile statement of F1 before the transition. It is the bridge between the cellos' low head at 37–38 and the violins + cellos echo at 41–42. With the violas, the lower octave keeps its weight all the way down, so the head is heard as a whole phrase and is not thinned at its end. The D of the V7 is still in Bn 1 (D4), Fl 2 and Ob 1 (D5) and Tbn 2 (D3). c371 declined violas on the cellos' head at 37–38, which is a different place. c254's staged drop-outs at 39–42 are about winds covering the violins at the unison, so a string doubling leaves them intact.

Fix (sections/pTwo.ily, pTwoVla, bars 39–40):
```
was:  ... | d'1\> | d'1 | aes1 | f2.\p r4 | }
now:  ... | f'2->(\ff\> bes4. c'8 | d'4-.) c'8( bes8 aes4-.) g4-. | aes1 | f2.\p r4 | }
```
41–42 is unchanged. G3→A♭3 at 40→41 is now a step into the seventh, where it was a leap from D4. Theme notes: F1's head at 39–40 is unchanged in pitch, rhythm and articulation. Only a unison doubling is added, so it needs no cross-movement notice. finale.md's P2 entry can add "39–40 violas with Vln II on F1's head".

Build: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages, outer-voice parallels 0, as in v551. par8.py on 37–43 reports nothing. analysis.txt differs from v551 only in the viola attack count and the 37–40 density line.

**2. Bars 207–208: Vln II on F1's head an octave below Vln I (declined after weighing it on paper).**

The B♭-minor head at 207 is in Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1 at the unison, with no lower octave. PDF 58–59 would add Vln II below. Vln II's rising mf scale at 207–210 is the other half of a dialogue: the violins' scale answers the horns' and cellos' head at 205–206. Doubling the head would remove that scale, and the B♭5–D♭5 head is not high enough for Note I (PDF 62) to require an octave. Not better.

**3. Bars 67–74: first violin statement of F2 in Vln I+II unison, with octaves from 75 (declined).**

Rimsky's Sheherazade model is unison first (Ex. 12, PDF 57), then octaves (PDF 59). I weighed this as a growth plan of unison → octaves (75) → three octaves (77). At B♭4 the unison would sit inside the harp's mf arpeggios and the viola/cello counter (G3–G4), which goes against PDF 54 on the middle register. At B♭5 it would leave F6 (69) without the octave below that PDF 58–59 asks for. The current octaves are right.

**4. Bars 187–198: Vln I / Vln II+violas for the recapitulated major F2 (not applied).**

PDF 60's second distribution would favour this for a cantabile. The passage is protected (c191, with its bow plan). The current Vln I+II / violas form gives the top octave Ob 1's dolce colour at the unison, which PDF 57 endorses. I have no reason that answers c191, so I leave it.

**5. Bars 41–42: cellos sul G under the violins (no change).**

PDF 60–61 prefers the cellos' A or D string for octaves with the violins. Here the G string is a deliberate dark colour for a p fade (c391, after Adler PDF 73–74), and the phrase is a two-bar echo, not a cantabile. Kept.
