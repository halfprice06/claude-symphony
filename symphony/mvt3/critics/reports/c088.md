## Answer to the focus question

- 110–113: correct as it stands. Strings and woodwinds ff carry the melody and the brass is f on the harmony (Adler PDF 311, 316). The step down to p at 114 is clean.
- 199–210: the dynamic plan is right, but three string staves miss the crescendo into 211, and Tpt 3 is out of step at 204 (Finding 3).
- 223: the sub. p–mp–f–fff path is the textbook staged climb of Ex. X-13 and should stay.
- 263: in v88, 263 is **not** the loudest point. It has the same marks as 223 and 278, but it is voiced an octave lower on top (Fl 1 and Vln I C6 against C7 at 223), its horns are in their "deep and solid" register, and strings and brass arrive at fff from an ff they have held for eight bars. With Findings 1 and 2, 263 matches 223 on top, holds fff for two bars against one at 223, and is the only fff reached by a staged climb in every section. 278 keeps the bass drum and the wider horn spacing. A final fermata chord as loud as the main climax is normal (Dvořák 9/iv: 305 and 331).

## Findings

### 1. 263: the last cadence chord is an octave lower on top than 223 and 278 (applied)
**Bars** 257–264. **Problem.** The sounding tops of the three fff chords:
- 223: Fl C7/G6, Vln I C7 (tremolo), Cl E6/C6, Hn 1·2 C5/G4.
- 278: Fl G6/E6, Vln I G6 triple stop, Hn 1+3 C5.
- 263: Fl C6/E5, Vln I C6 (a quarter, then the scale figure), Ob 2 E4, Cl 2 G4 (throat register), Hn 1·2 C4, Hn 3 E4, Hn 4 G3.

Adler's register charts show that the same mark gives less brilliance lower down. For the horn, written D4–B4 is "deep and solid" and the top is "brilliant and loud" (PDF 303, Ex. X-5). Trumpet and trombone get "brilliant but strident" and "very intense" only high up (PDF 318, 329). So the protected cadence of the movement sounds weaker than the arrival. The horns cannot move: Hn 1·2 end the chorale at its quotation pitch (C4), and Hn 3 resolves the V7's seventh F4→E4. The fix therefore goes to the winds.

**Fix.** Two changes in finale.ily:
- Fl 1 takes the chorale's last C two octaves above Tpt 1, as at 223: `b''1 | c''''1\fff~ | c''''2. r4` (was `c'''`). The leading tone B5 goes up to C7. The chorale's C6 stays in Cl 1 and Vln I.
- Cl 2 plays 257–264 an octave higher, out of the throat register: `e''1\mf\<~ | e''1 | f''1\f\<~ | f''1 | es''1 | g''1 | g''1\fff~ | g''2. r4`. It stays below Cl 1 (E6–C6).

No new top line appears over the chorale, which is still C on top, now doubled at C7. The quotation record checks Tpt 1 and Vln I, which are unchanged.

### 2. 255–262: strings and brass have no room to grow into 263 (applied)
**Bars** 255–262. **Problem.** At 255 the brass and all strings enter ff and hold it for four bars. At 259 they start a crescendo from ff to fff. Only the woodwinds are staged (mf 257, f 259; c034, c038). Adler's Mahler example puts f, then ff, then a crescendo to fff, keeping fff for the peak (PDF 305, Ex. X-13). Wagner's horn call grows from p to f in the same way (PDF 310, Ex. X-25). This movement already does it at 219–223. At 263 the climb in strings and brass is only ff→fff.

**Fix.** The accompanying strings, which are not the melody, drop one level at 255 and step up at 259. The brass chorale stays ff, so the last chorale statement keeps its weight, and Vln I stays ff with it (Adler PDF 311: brass is heard over the strings):
- Vln II `e'8(\f g' c'' e'')` at 255 and `aes'8(\ff\< d'' …` at 259.
- Vla `c''4\f` at 255 and `f'4\ff\<` at 259.
- Vc `e1\f~\cbaw` at 255 and `f,1\ff\<~` at 259.
- Cb `e,8\f` at 255 and `f,,8\ff\<` at 259.

The accompaniment now goes f–ff–fff alongside the winds' mf–f–fff. This does not undo c038: it carries c038's wind staging into the strings.

### 3. 204 and 209–210: sections out of step with themselves (applied)

### 4. 265: the clarinets print "ffff" (applied)
**Bar** 265. **Problem.** In the shared staff Cl 2's ff (beat 1) and Cl 1's ff (on G4, an eighth later) are printed side by side and read as "ffff" (page 15 of v88). That breaks the movement's rule that fff appears only at 223, 263 and 278, and it gives a wrong signal one bar after the climax. My scan of all staves found no other such collision.

**Fix.** In finale.ily Cl 1 takes its ff on the eighth rest, so it falls together with Cl 2's ff and prints once: `r8\ff g''8( e'''4.->)`. Bn 1 at 269 already uses this form. Verified on the scratch page 15.

### 5. 138: the stop chord's horns go sub. p under the strings' sf> (applied)
**Bars** 138–139. **Problem.** Hn 3·4 crescendo from pp over 135–137 and then drop to sub. p on D4/A♭3 at 138, while strings and oboe strike the stop chord sf>. The horns sound backward against the accent. Adler names exactly this case for stopped horn: single notes, "effective when one wishes to have the horn play sf>p", marked + and then o (PDF 308, Ex. X-19). The nasal, sharp stopped sound marks the stop chord at its first appearance. The resolution protected at 222→223 is untouched, and 138 is one of the bars allowed a strong mark.

**Fix** in retr.ily:
- Hn 3 `d'1\sf\>-\stopped | d'1\p-\open |`.
- Hn 4 `aes1\sf\>-\stopped | b1\p-\open |`.

Both players get equal dynamics on the shared staff. This replaces the sub. p at 138 in the horns only.
