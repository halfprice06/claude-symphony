## Findings

### 1. 72 beats 3–4: the harp sounds the root B♭4 a seventh above the violas' 9th C4 (applied)
**Problem.** At 72 the falling-fifth chain C7–Fm7–B♭9–E♭ (c232, c262) reaches its major-ninth dominant. On beat 3 the violas move D4–C4, and the 9th C4 falls with the cellos' 7th A♭3–G3 into 73: 9th and 7th resolve together, in the textbook way. The harp's second group in the same half bar is F4–A♭4–B♭4–D5. Its B♭4 on 72.4 sounds the 9th's tone of resolution a minor seventh above it, one beat before the violas reach B♭3. The 9th is no longer the highest dissonance leaning on its resolution: it sits under it, and the half bar sounds like a blurred B♭7 with an added C. This is exactly the fault c352 removed at 81 (harp B♭4 over the violas' ♭9 C♭4). There, finding 2 changed the harp's group to F4–A♭4–D5–F5. Bar 72 has the same figure in the same instrument, sixteen bars of the same statement earlier, and still has the fault.
**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 469–470 (sec. 7, Ex. 27-10): when the 9th sounds together with its tone of resolution "in a voice other than the bass", "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution of the dissonance". This refers to Unit 21 sec. 12: suspensions work best "if the tone of resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass".
- PDF 463: "The 9th appears mostly in the soprano."
- PDF 470, Ex. 27-11: in V9 the 9th stands above the other chord tones, not a seventh under the root's upper octave.
- PDF 464, Ex. 27-2(c): the clean five-voice form, with the 9th and 7th falling in parallel and nothing above the 9th anticipating its goal.
- The recapitulation's version at 192–193 has no harp. There Bn 1's 9th F3 has no E♭ above it in its own register, and the resolution is clear.

**Fix (sTwo.ily, applied).** `sTwoHarpR`, bar 72 (sTwo bar 6):
`f'8 bes'8 d''8 f''8 f'8 aes'8 bes'8 d''8` → `f'8 bes'8 d''8 f''8 f'8 aes'8 d''8 f''8`
- The harp now sounds D5 on beat 4 and F5 (with the tune's F5) on 4.5.
- B♭ remains in the bass (harp LH B♭1, basses, Bn 2), so the chord is complete.
- No new pedal change: D♮, F and A♭ are already set in the bar.
- Tune, violas, cellos, dynamics and c262's resolution are unchanged.
- After the change ninths.py no longer flags 72. allpairs_par.py over the whole score is identical to v442 (16 942 lines), and par8.py on 71–74 is identical (only the existing bassoon–bass octave doublings).

### 2. 128: a dominant ninth in the G-major statement of F2, like 72 and 192 (tested, not applied)
**Problem considered.** F2's major statements end their falling-fifth approach on a dominant ninth at 72 (E♭ major) and 192 (A♭ major) (c262). The development's G-major statement reaches the same point at 128 (Am7–D7–G), where the dominant is a plain D7. I tested the Ex. 27-2 form there: violas `c'2 e'2 | d'2\< r2` at 128–129, so the 9th E4 and Bn 1's 7th C4 fall together in thirds to D4/B3.
**Result.** The build is clean (0 warnings, 16 pages, all-pairs list identical, analysis unchanged except the viola note count). The sonority is worse, though. Vln II holds the 3rd F♯4 through beat 3, so the 9th E4 sits a whole step *under* the 3rd. That is the opposite of A&S's spacing (PDF 463 "mostly in the soprano"; Ex. 27-11 with the 9th on top). Under the clarinet's B4 on 128.3 the beat sounds as a D–E–F♯–A–B cluster that weakens the dominant of a pp hand-over. Unlike 72 and 192, the tune at 128 starts its descent on the root D5, not the 9th, so it gives no melodic reason for a 9th. Not applied. The candidate copy is /tmp/critic-442/workb (dev.ily); it is not part of the recommended changes.

### 3. 46.3: violas' 9th C4 under Cl 1's B♭4 (checked, no change)
The second ninths.py flag. Cl 1's run ends on B♭4 for one beat on 46.3 while the violas hold C4 and Vln II D4 over the B♭ pedal. The C4 does not resolve to B♭: it begins the chromatic inner line C–C♭ (47–48)–B♭ (49.3), which c352 accepted as 9–8 within V (Ex. 27-5). The B♭4 is a melodic arrival in another colour, and the soprano B♭5 lies more than an octave above, which is the wide spacing PDF 470 accepts. Changing the run would rewrite the transition figure that 44 and 89–90 share. No change.
