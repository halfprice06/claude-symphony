## Findings

### 1. Bar 5: the restated German-sixth tutti gets an A♭ timpani roll; bar 1 keeps its single stroke (applied)

Problem. Bars 1 and 5 are the only held ff tutti chords in the movement where the timpani play one quarter stroke and then rest. Twenty-six parts hold a whole-bar ff German sixth. The timpani give an attack that dies away in the first half of the bar. Every other held loud chord in the score has a roll: 147-150, 151-152, 209-210, 219-222, 223, 263 and 278. Bar 5 also repeats bar 1 note for note in every part. c213 kept that repetition for form reasons, but in orchestration terms the second statement is exactly the first.

Evidence.
- Appendix chords 1-4, 6, 9-12 and 16-20 (PDF 505-507): every held f, ff or fff tutti chord has "tr" in the timpani, on a chord tone, for the whole chord. No chord in the appendix has a single stroke under a held tutti.
- Ex. 205/206 (Sadko, PDF 368-369) is the same choral tutti scored twice. The second time it is louder, the cymbals and bass drum join, and the timpani strokes change.
- Ch. IV, PDF 118: the same idea recurs with "d) alteration of details (the most frequent method)". PDF 117: "one idea will be orchestrated in a different way every time it recurs."

Fix (sections/intro.ily, introTimp, bar 5; comment added):
```
introTimp = { aes,4->\ff^\markup \column { ... } r4 r2 | g,4-> r4 r2 | R1*2 |
  aes,1:32->\ff | g,4-> r4 r2 | g,4\mf r4 r2 | ...
```
I decided to leave bar 1 as it is. Its single A♭ stroke is the scherzo's G♯ taken up as A♭ in the drum retuned from G. The scherzo's timpani end with single strokes (motive x near the rim, a last E), so a stroke, not a roll, continues them across the pause. The roll at 5 then makes the restatement the larger of the two chords. This is the variation Ex. 205/206 shows, and it needs no fff, which is reserved for 223, 263 and 278. The roll is ff, the level of the brass, as in chords 2-4. A♭ is already on a drum (G, A♭, C, E♭), and bar 6 moves to the G drum as before.

Protections answered. Bars 1-6 are protected for the German-sixth harmony and voicing, the A♭4/A♭5 link in Hn 1 and Vln I, the links Hn 1/Cl 1 A♭4→G4 and Hn 4 F♯3→G3, the brass layout and the 5-6 repetition (c002, c014, c026, c213, c260). None of these changes: no pitch, voicing, layout, link or other part's dynamic is touched, and every part except the timpani still repeats bar 1 exactly. Bar 1's first timpani stroke, recorded in finale.md, is unchanged.

Applied in /tmp/critic-294/work/sections/intro.ily. The engraving shows the whole note with three tremolo strokes and ff on page 1. The MIDI holds A♭2 for the whole bar, as it does for the other rolls.

For finale.md (Opening): "Bar 5: the timpani roll A♭ ff through the restated German sixth (since v295); bar 1 keeps the single A♭ stroke." This changes no quotation and no note of F1, F2 or the chorale.

### Checked, no change (decided)

(a) Bar 1 roll. Not applied, for the reason given in Finding 1.

(b) Wind trills (row 10). I checked again every held loud wind chord outside protected bars. There is none apart from 1/5 and 209-210, and c204's reason for 209-210 (Fl 1's G♭6 seventh) still holds. A low bass-clarinet trill (chord 8) would fit only under the stop-chord pedal 144-146. Any trill there would have to touch C♯ or C before 223. Decision: no trill.

(c) Soft wind-and-brass chord with a pp roll (row 11). The only held soft chords where the timpani have a chord tone are 97-98 (C7, C drum) and 135-137. Both keep their strings for protected reasons: the violas' E♭→E at 97 and the dim7 chain. Decision: no change.

(d) Timpani roll into the codetta at 90 (row 18). Ex. 211 would support an E♭ roll crescendo on 90 into the ff strokes at 91. Bar 90 ends with a rest for the whole orchestra on beat 4, so the roll would stop before the ff and gain nothing; the f stroke at 90.1 already marks the arrival. Decision: no change.

(e) 91-94 tuba in unison with the bass trombone (row 4). I keep c194's decision for the reason stated in c204.

(f) String-free passages (rows 18, 21). The strings still play in 275 of 279 bars. Every place where a wind tutti would make sense is protected: 43-50, 51-66, 95-98, 101-106, 223-234 and 243-246. Decision: no change.
