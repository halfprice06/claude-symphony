## Findings

### 1. Bar 49: the violas' 9-8 has no shaping (applied)
- **Problem.** At 47–49, over the B♭ pedal, the violas hold C♭4 (the ♭9 of V7 of E♭) and resolve it to B♭3 on 49.3. Vln II's G♭→F has already resolved on 49.1, so the C♭ is the last dissonance in the transition before the trumpet solo. It is marked only by the pp from 43. Nothing tells the players that this note is the suspension and that the B♭ is its release.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 348 sec. 4: suspensions bring their voice forward, and "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer". PDF 351 sec. 10 and Ex. 21-12d: a 9-8 over the bass is the case where the resolution tone may already sound in the bass. Here it sounds only in the bass and in Vln I's harmonic B♭5. The suspensions the score already shapes this way are 81, 211, 227–229, 237 and 241. 49 was the gap. The C♭ is also the ♭6 of the E♭ minor the solo opens in (its fourth bar has C♭5), so a slight lean on it and a release at the B♭ connects the two passages.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-498/work/sections/trans.ily).**
  `transVla = { d'1\pp~ | d'1~ | d'1~ | d'2 c'2 | ces'1~ | ces'1~ | ces'2\> bes2~\! | bes2. r4 | }`
  Only a hairpin is added. Notes, ties, the pp level and the poco rit. are unchanged. Hn 1's stopped p D4 at 49 (c247) and Ob 1's pp A♭4 are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 87–89: the inner 7-6 chain (checked, no change)
Bn 1 (f) and the upper violas (mf) play the chain E♭/F, D/E♭, B♭/C (PDF 354–355, Ex. 21-16). Mozart's p on a chain (Ex. 21-17) does not fit, because this chain leads into the ff codetta at 91. c198 and c398 raised the violas' level and declined it, because Vln II's figure crosses their line at 87.4 and 88.4. That reason still holds. No change.

### 3. Bars 25 and 165: F1's cadential 4-3 is not marked (protected, no change)
The C over G at 25.2–25.3 is a cadential 4-3 (PDF 350, Ex. 21-8), and the book would mark its attack. F1's articulation at 11–26 and 151–166 is do-not-touch (c058). c398 declined the same change, and at 25 the trombones' ff > f already shapes the bar. No change.

### 4. Bar 112: the climax's 4-3 over V at ff (considered, not applied)
In the violins and doubling winds the F6 is held over the C bass from 112.3 and resolves to E6 on 112.4 (PDF 350 sec. 8). A diminuendo on that one beat would follow PDF 348 sec. 4 to the letter. But it would break the brass ff, which c098 restored for 110–113, and the climax 107–113 is protected. The slurs from c188 already join the 4 to the 3. No change.

### 5. Bars 220–222: C held from the 6/4 into V7 under a crescendo (considered, not applied)
The C6 held over the 6/4 at 220 becomes a 4-3 into B5 at 221 (PDF 351–352, Ex. 21-11), and the resolution is marked louder (mp <), not softer. This is the staggered crescendo into the arrival (205–223 dynamic path, 219–223 crescendo; do-not-touch). A drive toward a whole chord that is held back is Ex. 21-37's case, which A&S treats as a suspension in its own right (PDF 365). So a crescendo through the resolution is correct here. No change.

### 6. Pedal points (no change)
43–50 (B♭), 147–150 and 7–10 (G), 235–238 and 263–279 (C) all begin and end on their chord, and the upper dissonances over them are prepared and resolved (PDF 367–368). Finding 1 is the only expressive gap I found among them.
