## Findings

### 1. Bar 251, beats 1–2: the first-inversion tonic is a bare E in sixteen notes (applied)

**Bars.** 251.1–2, Hn 4 (Hn 3·4 staff).

**Problem.** The second phrase of the combination of movement I's two theme heads begins on I6: tuba, contrabassoon and basses move to E2 (`e,2 c,2`). Both themes also have E as their melody here. The second-theme head starts on E (Fl 1·2 E6, Ob 1 and Cl 1·2 E5, Hn 1·2 and violas E4), and on 251.2 the first-theme head's accented cry lands on E (Vln I·II E6, Bn 1, Tbn 1·2 and cellos E3). Hn 3 resolves the seventh F4 → E4 (c072), and Hn 4 also plays E4, so four horns and the violas share one E4. In the MIDI, 251.1 has 10 of 11 notes on E and 251.2 has 15 of 16. The only other pitch is Ob 2's C5 at a lower velocity (plus the timpani's C3), and there is no G until Hn 3 reaches G4 on beat 3. The parallel bar 247 has the root C in the bass. Bar 251 is the one harmonised tutti beat in the movement that sounds as an octave unison by accident.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 152 (Ex. V-27, looked at as an image): in I6, IV6 and V6 the doubled third belongs "near the bottom", which leaves "the 'open' sound so characteristic of this inversion intact". The other layout is printed as "not good". PDF 257 (Ex. VIII-25) gives the same rule for winds and marks the version with the third doubled toward the top "bad". In a first inversion the bass is already heard as the third, so the inner voices have to supply the root and fifth. PDF 257 also says unison overlapping "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". That is what Hn 4's E4 under Hn 1·2 and Hn 3 does here. The melody's E cannot move, because it belongs to both quoted themes. The remedy has to come from the free inner voice.

Hn 4 goes D4 (the fifth of G7 at 250.3) → G3 → C4. At 251.1–2 the chord is now E2 (three instruments) – E3 – G3 – C3 (timpani) – E4 – C5 – E5 – E6: a complete I6, with the bass doubled low and the melody octaves left as they were. On beat 3 Hn 4's C4 and Hn 3's G4 give a full C chord over C2, and 252 continues as before (Hn 4 C4 tied by common tone into the F chord). I tested and rejected two other versions. D4 → C4 directly makes octaves with Ob 2's D5 → C5 (250.3 → 251.1). G3 held through the bar makes contrary fifths with the bass into 252 (G3/C2 → C4/F1); c072 refused the same thing. With G3 → C4 on beat 3, G3 against E2 is a third, C4 against C2 is an octave reached in contrary motion, and C4 against F1 at 252 is held. Hn 3 keeps c072's F4 → E4 resolution of the seventh. ### 2. Holistic comparison with Tchaikovsky 4/iv: a conductor would accept the craft; the coda's design is the remaining difference (no change)

Three devices give Tchaikovsky's finale its force. The first is an opening outburst that comes straight back, its racing scales shared between strings and winds. The second is a second theme (the folk song) that returns many times, each time with a new scoring and counter-line. The third is the first movement's fate fanfare breaking in before a coda that drives to the end over the tonic. This score has the counterpart of each:
- The ff German sixth with the strings' scale answer (1–8). Following Adler PDF 255, the third return of the pattern is changed: there is no answer at 7, and F1's head starts in the low strings instead.
- F2 rescored at each return (trumpet solo 51, violins over harp 67, bassoon 115, muted trumpet with cor anglais 171, violins with oboe and horn 187), as Adler PDF 238 asks.
- Movement I's hammered tutti at 199 and the stop chord resolved only at 222→223, the counterpart of the fate fanfare.

The orchestral sound in the loud tuttis compares with Tchaikovsky's at all the points I checked, with one exception: Tchaikovsky's tutti chords never lose their root or fifth when the melody and bass coincide. Bar 251 did (Finding 1).

The difference that remains is one of design. Tchaikovsky's coda carries one idea and drives without a stop. This coda moves through arrival, chorale, second-theme head, horn call, combination, last chorale and the F1 close, with a full stop at 235. That design is protected (c253, c343, c359), and the cyclic plan of the symphony needs it, so I am not proposing a change. As c259 decided, I also leave out Tchaikovsky's constant cymbals and triangle: the cymbals sound only at 223, 263 and 278, because the first crash in the symphony is one of the owed items.
