## Findings (ranked)

### F1. 72 and 192: d5 → P5 in similar motion between upper voices. APPLIED

- **Bars:** 72 (sTwo.ily, `sTwoVla`) and 192 (recS.ily, `recSBsnOne`).
- **Problem:** At 72 (V7 of E♭) the violas step D4→C4 on beat 3, the 9th that c262 introduced. At the same moment F2 steps A♭→G in the violins. Violas against Vln II are D4–A♭4 falling to C4–G4: a diminished 5th to a perfect 5th in close position, both voices down by step. Against Vln I the same motion is compound. The recapitulation has the same motion a fifth lower at 192, where Bn 1 G3→F3 sits under D♭4→C4 in the violas and Hn 1. In my scan these are the only two such motions outside protected bars that are true voice leading, not neighbour notes over a held chord.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 375 and Ex. 22-3c: if the upper dissonance forms a diminished 5th, "the similar motion that follows creates an obtrusive set of hidden 5ths; avoid this voice leading." A&S PDF 465 says a 9th may also enter as a neighbour or passing tone, so moving the 9th to beat 4 keeps it legitimate. Ex. 27-2 (PDF 464) asks for the 9th and 7th to resolve in parallel 3rds into I, and that still happens: C4/A♭3 → B♭3/G3 at 72→73, F3/D♭3 → E♭3/C3 at 192→193.
- **Fix (applied):**
  - 72, violas: `d'2( c'2)` becomes `d'2.( c'4)`.
  - 192, Bn 1: `\transpose c aes, { b2 a2) }` becomes `\transpose c aes, { b2. a4) }`.
  - c262's D–C–B♭ passing line and its 9th are kept. F2, the bass, the harmony and the dynamics are unchanged.
- **Checks:**
  - MIDI: the only differences from v589 are these two notes.
  - par8.py on 71–73 and 191–193 gives the same output as v589.
  - analysis.txt is identical to v589's: 0 outer-voice parallels.
  - d5p5.py no longer reports 72 or 192.
  - allpairs_par.py lists two new lines, at 72.4 and 192.4, labelled "P5". They are parallel 4ths: viola D4/C4 under the tune's G5/F5, and Bn 1 G3/F3 under C/B♭. The tool reduces the signed interval modulo 12, so an inverted 5th reads as a P5. Parallel 4ths between upper voices are allowed.
  - Page images 4 and 10: layout unchanged, 16 pages.

### F2. 202→203: the same fault inside the protected hammered tutti. Not applied

- **Bars and problem:** 202 is VII°7 of iv (E–G–B♭–D♭) and 203 is iv. One horn of Hn 1·2 takes G4→F4 while Tpt 1·2 resolve D♭5→C5, so G4–D♭5 moves to F4–C5. This is exactly Ex. 22-3c: the 3rd and 7th of a °7 move in similar motion to a 5th.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 375, as in F1.
- **Decision:** I leave it. Bars 199–204 reproduce movement I 27–32 in every part (finale.md, quotation 1; do-not-touch since c156), and that reason outweighs a hidden 5th inside an ff brass tutti. The other horn's G4→A♭4 and the other strands of the brass chord carry the regular resolution. If movement I ever changes its own Hn 2 at I 31 to A♭4 (`aes'2`), the finale should follow at 203. I record this for the movement I thread and change nothing here.

### F3. Borodin 2/iv: a conductor would accept this finale beside it. No change

Borodin's finale is a major-key celebration from its first bar. Its strengths are:
- rhythm: dances in changing metre;
- unison and pentatonic tunes;
- percussion held back for this movement only.

This finale does the last of these in its own way: the cymbals and bass drum first sound at the arrival 223. It goes beyond Borodin in long-range harmonic design:
- the German-sixth opening returns at the last cadence;
- the C–A♭–E thirds cycle;
- the one root V–I at 262–263.

What Borodin does better is rhythmic surprise inside one fast pulse. This coda instead changes tempo by stages (protected since c043, c063 and c088), and its fast blocks (199–204, 247–254, 265–272) keep one rhythmic pattern each. I propose no change: a metric displacement in those blocks would cut across the quoted heads, which are protected.
