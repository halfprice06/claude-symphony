## What else I studied

I read the LilyPond 2.24 spacing rule behind the finding: the gap below a staff comes from its innermost StaffGroup's StaffGrouper (`staff-staff-spacing` inside the group, `staffgroup-staff-spacing` below its last live staff). ## Findings

### 1. Brass-to-next-choir gap collapses in reduced systems (applied)

Bars: 37–50 (page 3), 67–76 and 77–86 (page 4), 171–178 and 179–186 (page 10).

Problem: c325 drew the horn, trumpet and trombone squares as three nested StaffGroups. To stop every family getting a choir-sized gap, it set their `staffgroup-staff-spacing` to the plain distance. LilyPond takes the gap below a staff from the innermost group containing it. So when the lowest live brass staff belongs to a family (bass trombone with the tuba resting, Hn. 1·2 alone, Tpt. 1·2 alone), the gap from the brass to the percussion or harp is only the gap inside a family. Adler PDF 291 asks for the separation of choirs to be "immediately discernable", and PDF 292 says "it is imperative to leave space between the choirs ... between the brass and the percussion, between the percussion and the harp".

Evidence, measured at 150 dpi (staff space 7.3 px):
- Page 3, 37–50: B.tbn.→Timp. was 47 px, the same as Fl.→Ob. and Hn. 1·2→Hn. 3·4 (47 px).
- Page 4: Hn. 1·2→Harp was 66 px (67–76) and 71 px (77–86). Inside the woodwinds the gap is 61–62 px, and the harp's own staves are closer.
- Page 10, 171–178: Tpt. 1·2→Timp. was 41 px. That is less than Vla.→Vc. and Vc.→Cb. in the same system (43–44 px), so the timpani read as part of the brass.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-505/work/score.ly: the three nested `\new StaffGroup \with \familyBracket << ... >>` wrappers are removed. The brass StaffGroup draws the same squares with a delimiter hierarchy:

```lilypond
\new StaffGroup \with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver"
  systemStartDelimiterHierarchy = #'(SystemStartBracket (SystemStartSquare hnA hnB) (SystemStartSquare tpA tpB) (SystemStartSquare tbA tbB) tba)
  \override InstrumentName.padding = #1.2 } <<
  \new Staff = "Horns 1·2" ... \new Staff = "Tuba" ...
>>
```

Now one StaffGrouper spaces the brass. Between brass staves it gives the plain distance (default `staff-staff-spacing`, 9/7, the same as before), and below the lowest live brass staff it gives the choir distance (default `staffgroup-staff-spacing`, minimum 8), whichever staff that is. Results: 37–50 47→65 px; 67–76 66→81 px; 77–86 71→82 px; 171–178 41→55 px; 179–186 51→54 px. Every other gap on all 16 pages is unchanged to the pixel. On full pages (1, 12–16) the squares, the brass bracket, the names, the barlines and the tempo and rehearsal marks print exactly as before. `familyBracket` is now unused; I left its definition in place with a note. This is engraving only: score.midi is byte-identical and analysis.txt is identical.

### 2. Page 7 (128–143): B.tbn.→Timp. still about 4 spaces (tested, declined)

Page 7 is at its minimum spacing: B.tbn.→Timp. is 29–30 px, against 26–31 px inside the brass. I tested a brass `staffgroup-staff-spacing` minimum of 9, so that this gap would be 5 spaces. LilyPond then reported "compressing over-full page by 1.6 staff-spaces" for page 7, so I did not keep it. The minimum stays at the default 8. Page 7 has overfilled before (c465, c476, c491). Widening this gap would cost a new page break, and Adler's rule does not justify that.

### 3. Five ledger lines at 78, 213–214, 223 (no change)

Adler PDF 300 (8a) tells copyists to use 8va to avoid more than four ledger lines in parts. Gould PDF 47 is more specific: woodwind and strings may use up to five, and octave signs should not be transferred to playing parts. c115 and c415 settled this on Gould's ground, and Adler adds no new reason, so I made no change.

No other principle in the reading is violated. Trumpet and trombone staff layout (row 9) is kept for c235's reason.
