## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 247-254: the trombones' P head sits in the same octave as the S head and cannot be heard as a line (applied)
Problem: at 247-254 the listener should hear two themes at once: movement I's P head (Fl 1·2 and Vln I·II at G5-A6, Tbn 1·2 at G3-A4) and its S head (Tpt 1 and Ob 1 at E5, Tpt 2 and Ob 2 a third below, Hn 1·2, Cl 1·2 and Vla at E4-C5). The trombones' P head runs G3-E4-D4-D♭4-C4 ... A4 (252) ... G4. That is exactly the band where Hn 1·2, Cl and Vla play the S head, with Hn 3·4 (E4/G4) and Bsn 1 + cellos (C4-G4 half notes) also sustaining there. At ff, two different tunes in the same octave and in related brass colours (trombones against horns) mask each other. The trombones' fast eighths blur the horns' S head, and the P head does not come through as a brass line either. The owed item ("brass combination of the theme heads") sounds, but it is not heard.
Evidence: Adler's own example of a theme against a heavy counterpoint, *1812* (Ex. XI-13, PDF 28-29), puts the theme in trombones, horns, bassoons and low strings, clear of the upper counterpoint. The Schubert combination (Ex. XI-12, PDF 28-29) adds bassoons, violas, cellos and basses to the trombones for power. Hindemith (PDF 19-20) shows that the octave chosen decides whether a brass theme is dark and noble or bright. The Dvořák 9/iv coda (corpus, above) gives the trombones the main theme as a separate line.
Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-071/work/sections/finale.ily): Tbn 1·2 play the P head an octave lower, in bass clef (G2-A3, three octaves below the violins), and Bsn 1 and the cellos leave their C4-G4 half notes to double it:

```
r8 g,8( e4.->) d8( c8 b,8 | c4) e8( d8) a4.-> g8 | f4.-> e8 d4 g4 | g2.-> r4 |
```
The harmony stays complete: Hn 3·4, the S-head choir, the bass (B.tbn, tuba, Cbsn, Cb) and Tpt 3 still give every chord. The trombones now end on G3 at 254 and stay on G3 into the chorale at 255 (before, G4 to G3). No quotation notes change. themecheck still reports 36/36. The all-pairs parallel script (c032 p5.py) is identical before and after. The new octave pairs flagged by c002 par.py at 247 and 251 are the intended octave doubling of the P head, the same kind the trombones already made. The composer should update finale.md item 8 ("Tbn 1·2 a2 three octaves below the violins, with Bsn 1 and cellos") and tell the movement I thread that the P-head doubling moved an octave. F1, F2 and the chorale are unchanged.

### 2. 265-272: the trumpets play F1's head three times and then miss its continuation (applied)
Problem: Tpt 1·2 play the same two bars of F1's head at 265, 267 and 269. At 269 the violins and winds play it too. At 271-272, F1's continuation in major (the rising arpeggio to F and the fall E-D, the only new melodic bars of the close, under the allargando) has no trumpets because they rest there. The repetition comes in the brass and the melodic goal does not. A listener carries away the first two statements. The continuation decides whether F1 sounds finished in major.
Evidence: Adler, PDF 20: Hindemith keeps a colour (the tuba) back so that a later statement of the theme sounds fresh. Here the trumpets are used up on a repeat and are missing from the new phrase. PDF 19: brass in unison is the strongest presentation, so it belongs on the bars that need it most.
Fix (applied): the trumpets' rest moves two bars earlier. Their endurance is unchanged (two bars of rest either way), and they gain a breath before the Largamente.
```
finaleTptOne/TptTwo, 269-273:
R1*2 | d''4(\ff g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4) | f''2-> e''4( d''4) \breathe | \once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = ... c''1-> ...
```
The ff at 273 is removed in both trumpets (now continuous from 271). The dynamics on the shared staff are equal.

### 3. 273-276: Bassoon 1 makes a close low third under the closing chain (applied)
Problem: at 273, beat 3 (F♯°7 over the C pedal), Bsn 1 has E♭3 directly above C3 (B.tbn, Bsn 2, B.cl, cellos), with Tbn 2 F♯3 and Tbn 1 A3 above it: a diminished seventh in close position starting on C3. A minor third at C3-E♭3 is below the usual low limit for that interval. Bsn 1 also drops a sixth from C4 (272) to E3 (273), while its line E-E♭-F-E is already in Hn 4 and Cl 1 at E4. This is the movement's last phrase, and the chain G-F♯-F-E has to be heard clearly through it.
Evidence: Adler, PDF 24 (on the Stravinsky chorale): with thick brass writing "one has to take care that the last phrase is not muddy".
Fix (applied): Bsn 1 an octave higher, with Hn 4: `\partCombineAutomatic e'2-> es'2 | f'1-> | e'1~\< | e'1 |`. This also connects better with its C4 at 272 and 278. No pitch class changes, so the protected chain and the layout of 273-276 (c024, c042, c049) keep all their notes.
