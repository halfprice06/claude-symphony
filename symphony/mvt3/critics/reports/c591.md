## Findings

### 1. 130–131: F1's major-key head is masked by the horns. Half the cellos now double it an octave lower (applied)

**Bars:** 130–132 (development, G-major cadence over D7).

**Problem.** Here F1's head appears in major for the first time in the movement: D4–A3–B3 | C4–B3–A3–G3–F♯3 | G3, played by the violas and Hn 2 at the same pitch, mf < f. It is the only place before 247 where F1 and F2 sound together. It points ahead to the C-major F1 at 247 and 265, so it is one of the movement's most important melodic moments.

The head is not clear, because it runs through the middle of the held accompaniment:
- Hn 3 holds D4, the head's first note.
- Hn 4 repeats A3 quarters, so the head's A3 at 130.3 is a unison with it.
- Hn 1 holds F♯4 a third above.

Above all this, Vln I and Fl 1 hold A5, and Vln II holds A4. Sebesky warns against loud accompaniment in the featured line's register (PDF 219). His remedy for a moving line that has to come through is Ex. 273 (PDF 208): put the cellos in unison an octave below and accept some loss of balance for the sake of the melodic motion. At 130–131 the cellos only double the basses' D.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-591/work/sections/dev.ily, devVc).** Divide the cellos at 130–131:
- The upper half plays the head an octave below the violas: D3–A2–B2–C3–B2–A2–G2–F♯2.
- The upper half's last note runs into the G2 that all the cellos already play at 132.
- The lower half keeps the bass D with the basses.

```
b,2\< e4 es4 | << { \voiceOne d2->( a,4. b,8 | c4-.\f) b,8( a,8 g,4-.) fis,4-. } \new Voice { \voiceTwo d1_\markup \italic "div." | d2\f d2 } >> \oneVoice | g,4->\downbow^\markup \italic "unis." r4 r2 |
```

The head now has a lower octave below the horn chord, in the cellos' C and G strings, where nothing else moves. Other checks:
- The basses (D2), the harp and, at 131, Bn 2 still carry the bass, so the V7 keeps its root.
- The cellos' C3 at 131.1 is the chord's seventh. It falls to B2 together with the violas' C4, and Bn 1 still holds C4 into B3 at 132, so the seventh resolves in every voice.
- Nothing changes for F2's half cadence above, the horns, the hairpins or 129's iv6 (c432).

I tested this in the scratch build:
- The build has 0 warnings and stays at 16 pages and 422 s.
- analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels.
- par8.py over 128–133 reports only the cellos' and Bn 2's existing octave doublings of the bass, at 129–130 and 132. The same hits appear in v591, so nothing is new.
- The engraving at 130–131 is clean: "div." sits below the staff and "unis." above at 132.

**For the other threads:** this does not change the notes of F1 or of any quotation. It changes the scoring of F1's development statement. finale.md's F1 paragraph should add: "since v592 half the cellos double it an octave lower (D3–F♯2), the other half keep the bass D".

### 2. 128.4–130: Vln II doubling the half-cadence line an octave lower. Ex. 280 checked, not applied

Vln II doubles Vln I's A–B–G–A an octave lower. Ex. 279–280 (PDF 211–212) would replace a shadowing line with missing chord tones, so I checked that option on paper. This is a plain octave doubling of the lead, not a near-parallel echo of it. The chord tone Ex. 280 would add, the seventh C4, is already held by Bn 1 from 129.3 and resolves to B3 at 132. Any inner line for Vln II would also sit in the register that finding 1 clears for F1's head. No change.

### 3. 167–169: violins in descending sixths. Ex. 275 not applicable

The link before the recapitulated solo has Vln I and Vln II in sixths at 169 (D5–F5 down to G4–A♭3). Ex. 275 (PDF 209) adds passing tones to the lower line of thirds that move in longer values. Here both lines already move in eighths, and the cellos double Vln I's line, so there is nothing to fill. The descent ends on Vln II's G3 at 170, which also gives the cor anglais and muted trumpet their first note at 171 (PDF 222, Ex. 285). No change.

### 4. Memorability of the themes against the reading: no change

- **F2:** answers the soloist's held notes with moving inner voices (58, 66) or a counter-melody (67–76). This matches PDF 207 and Ex. 286.
- **F1:** has a clear antecedent–consequent shape with its peak at A♭6 (22).
- **Chorale:** its whole notes have the canon (226–229) and the portato pulse (231–233) under them, matching Ex. 271–273.

These tunes are the sort that stay with a listener: the falling-third, dotted-half opening of F2 works like the second theme of Dvořák 9/iv, and the chorale works like Brahms 1/iv. With finding 1, F1's major form is heard before its arrival at 247.
