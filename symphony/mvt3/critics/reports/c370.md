## Findings

### 1. 195–196: the restated opening of F2's A♭-major statement now uses mixture (applied). F2 changes in this statement.

**Problem.** 195–196 repeated 187–188 note for note in major, with the same I6 and V6/5–I. The minor turn at 198.3 (c293), which leads into the C-minor tutti, therefore came after eleven bars of unmixed A♭ major and lasted only two beats. A&S, PDF 392 (section 5, Ex. 23-8), says a change of mode is "particularly vivid when [it] varies what otherwise would be a repetition". PDF 386–387 describes mixture as a long-range way to set the two modes against each other. The score used this at large scale (51→67) but not inside a phrase. ♭3 in major (PDF 395 pt 2d) appeared only for two beats at 198.

**Change.** The two restated bars are shaded to A♭ minor: `es''2.( ces''4) | bes'4 aes'4 ces''4 es''4`. Major returns on 196.4 and carries the climb to E♭6 at 197–198. Then c293's C♭ comes back at 198.3 and rises to C at 199. The exposition goes from minor to major (51→67); the recapitulation's major statement is now shaded by minor before the C-minor tutti; the coda finally goes from minor to major (223). This follows the Mendelssohn pattern, where minor holds until a late major reassertion (PDF 386–387).

**Voice leading.**
- At 195 the bass V4/2 D♭ falls a step to C♭2, giving i6.
- At 196.3 the cellos play C♭3 then C3, so the chromatic step stays in one voice. That makes the C♭ (tune) against C (bass at 197) only an apparent cross relation (Ex. 23-12b, PDF 394).
- The counter-line C♭–D♭–E♭ takes the minor mode (PDF 397, Exercise 6).
- Cl 1's F♭4 at 194 (V7♭9) already brings in the minor colour.
