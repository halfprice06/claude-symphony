## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 277: the harp's ff glissando is a diatonic scale through the middle strings; make it a pentatonic chord glissando (applied)
- Bars: 277-278 (finale.ily, harp).
- Problem: the harp glides G3-G6 on all seven naturals (C D E F G A B), f < ff, into the fff C-major chord at 278, which the harp lets ring (G6 and C2-G2-C3 laissez vibrer). Every string from G3 up keeps sounding, so F and B ring against the tutti's C triad for the whole held bar.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 47: scale glissandi "produce a discordant medley of sound owing to the length of time the strings continue to vibrate"; forte scale glissandi through the lower and middle strings "are only permissible as embellishments", while "glissando passages in chords ... enharmonically obtained ... [allow] every dynamic shade of tone". PDF 46: the middle and low strings ring on. A C-major triad cannot be set on the harp (the D string has no chord tone), but the C pentatonic chord can: B♯ = C and F♭ = E.
- Fix: pedal diagram at 277 `\harp-pedal #"--v|-^--"` (was `"---|----"`); part text `"gliss. C D E G A (B♯ = C, F♭ = E)"`. Endpoints G3 and G6, rhythm, dynamics and the landing chord are unchanged; both strings ring as chord tones or as the added 6th and 9th, with no B or F. The harpist has 30 bars of rest to make the two pedal moves (B right foot, F left foot). This technique is new in the movement.

### 2. 147-158: Bassoon 2 plays twelve bars ff in its low register with no rest (applied)
- Bars: 147-150 (retr.ily) into 151-158 (recP.ily).
- Problem: Bn 2 holds G2 ff 147-150, takes the pickup D3 at 150 beat 4 and plays F1's bass in half notes ff to 158 beat 3: about 17 s at 2 = 84 with no breath. Bn 1 already rests on 150 beat 4.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37: wind players "cannot manage extremely long sustained passages ... care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time"; PDF 44 applies the same to the brass.
- Fix: `retrBsnTwo` last bar `g,2. r4` (was `g,2. d4`). The pickup D stays in cellos, bass clarinet, contrabassoon, tuba and basses. Bn 2 then plays 5 s and 11 s, as Bn 1 does.

### 3. 187-198: Bassoon 2 holds the A♭ statement's bass for twelve bars without a breath (applied)
- Bars: 187-198 (recS.ily).
- Problem: Bn 2 plays 187-198 beat 3 (p < f) with no rest or breath mark (about 17 s). Bn 1 and Hn 1 breathe at the end of 190, the end of the tune's first phrase.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37 and 44, as in Finding 2.
- Fix: `\transpose c aes, { f,2 e,2 \breathe }` at 190 in `recSBsnTwo`, so the pair breathes together at the phrase end.

### 4. 123-130 and 239-242: harp broken chords in the low and middle register, and one harp under a forte tutti (checked, kept)
Rimsky PDF 46 says rapid figures are clear only in the upper register, and PDF 47 says one harp is not heard against a forte orchestra. At 123-130 the harp's eighths lie at G2-A3. One octave higher they would sit on the cellos' sul D statement (D4-G3). Two octaves higher they would sit on the violas, Cl 1 and the violins as the tune is handed on (A4-B5). Every register is occupied, so I keep c174's solution (p, the cellos' level). At 239-242, c250 lowered the harp to mf to free movement I's counter, and that reason still stands. I add nothing.

### 5. 238: Violins at C6 on the A string (checked, kept)
The "sul A" spanner runs to the appoggiatura C6 and its B5. This is the fifth position, one semitone above Rimsky's fourth-position limit (PDF 26). It is a held half note in a slow cantabile melody, reached from A5, under a diminuendo. Taking it on the E string would put the phrase's softest, most expressive note on the brilliant string. I keep it.

### 6. 27-29: tuba E♭1-D1 tongued ff (checked, kept)
Table C (PDF 43) marks the tuba's lowest notes "little used". The two notes are staccato quarters at the bottom of the bass statement, doubled by the contrabassoon. A CC tuba plays them with the fourth valve. Rimsky PDF 42 praises "the strength and beauty of its low notes" when the tuba doubles the bass an octave lower. Kept.
