## Reading notes

The only music example in the range is Fig. 6.1 on PDF 272 (Mahler 1/i, bars 17-25), which I rendered at 110 dpi and looked at. In it the woodwinds hold a pp chord in falling fourths until bar 21 and then stop. The offstage trumpets enter in bar 22, marked ppp, which is softer than the woodwinds' pp. Throughout, the strings hold the pedal A in octaves from the basses up to the violins' harmonics under an 8va line. The trumpets therefore enter in a register the woodwinds have just left, inside a pedal that covers the whole range.

## Findings

### 1. 37-38: Hn 1·2's held F4/B♭3 cover the new horn statement of F1's head (applied; regression since v289)

**Problem.** In v289 F1's third head statement was in the violins and Fl 1. c297 gave it to Hn 3·4 a2 ff at B♭4, but Hn 1·2 kept their mf held notes in the same octave and timbre. Hn 1 held F4 from 37.1 to 38.2, then played G♭4-F4; Hn 2 held B♭3 and C4. Two things follow:
- The head's F4 at 37.3, a dotted quarter, sounds on the same pitch in the same instrument as Hn 1's held F4, so the head's fall of a fourth melts into the held note.
- The head's G♭4-F4 at 38.2 is followed half a beat later by Hn 1's G♭4-F4. One horn colour then sounds the same two pitches twice.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 277-278: a unison of like sounds in one register is heard as one note, and ensemble timbre depends on "our psychoacoustic capacity to discern content within a certain frequency area".
- Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272): Mahler clears the woodwinds before the soft brass call, so the call enters in a free register.
- The held notes are redundant. At 37 the clarinets already hold B♭3/F4 (MIDI: Cl shared B♭3 + F4, four beats), and Hn 2's C4 at 38.1 is Cl 2's C4.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 51 gives the relative strengths. In the MIDI the head's notes after the accent are v67-71 and Hn 1·2 are v56 in the same octave.

**Fix (applied in pTwo.ily).** Hn 1·2 rest from 37 to 38.2. They re-enter mf on 38.3 with the 6-5 of the cadential 6/4 (G♭4-F4 over B♭) under the head's 4-3. Bar 39 is unchanged. The horns also get a breath after eleven bars.
```
pTwoHnOne = { ... | aes'1 | g'2 aes'2 | R1 | r2 ges'4\mf f'4 \breathe | f'1~\> | ...
pTwoHnTwo = { ... | c'1 | bes2 c'2 | R1 | r2 bes2\mf \breathe | bes1~\> | ...
```
Both players enter on the shared staff with the same dynamic. F1's notes and the head's instrument are unchanged.

### 2. 38.1-2: a minor third at the bottom of the trombone chord (applied)

**Problem.** Tbn 2 plays A♭2 a minor third above F2, which is doubled by the bass trombone, contrabassoon and basses, at f for two beats. The low scan finds no other sustained brass third this low after c264's fix at 215-218.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 274: the harmonic series puts the octave and fifth at the bottom and the thirds higher up. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85 says the same. At 223 and 278 this movement's own brass chords follow that layout.

**Fix (applied).** Tbn 2 plays C3 at 38.1-2, in unison with Tbn 1, and B♭2 at 38.3 as before. A♭ stays in the head (A♭4) and in the countersubject.
```
pTwoTbnTwo = { ... | c2.~\ff\> c4\f | bes,2 c2 | bes,1 | c2 bes,2 | d1~\> | ...
```

### 3. 171-186: muted trumpet and cor anglais at the same written level (kept)

PDF 272 says an open woodwind sounds louder than a filtered brass instrument marked louder, so in a unison the woodwind could absorb the trumpet. I considered marking the muted trumpet mp. I kept it as it is, for three reasons:
- A straight mute takes away the low partials, not the high ones, so in the middle register a p muted trumpet keeps its edge against a p cor anglais. That is unlike Mahler's walls, which remove the highs (PDF 273).
- The passage is meant as a blend (owed item 1).
- It is protected (c4, c19).

### 4. Gradual change of bow position (PDF 276): not used in the score; not applied

Casanelles says the spectral envelope changes within a note with bow position and pressure. The score only switches abruptly: "sul tasto" at 43 and 171, and "ord." at 67, 181 and 199. A "poco a poco ord." on a held crescendo would be the new technique. The places that fit are:
- 141-146 (the retransition crescendo over the B pedal), which is protected;
- 9-10, where c279 builds the crescendo from staggered entries and no string holds long enough.

The v300 MIDI render would not carry the change in any case. Not applied.

### 5. 115-122: the bassoon's F2 in the development has no sustained pad (kept)

By PDF 280 a sustained background is the usual support for a soft solo. The bassoon solo is over pizzicato alone, while the trumpet solo at 51 and the recapitulation at 171 have wind pads. I kept it. The plain pizzicato is the development's contrast with the exposition and recapitulation, and the Vln I answers at 119 and 122 need the empty space above.

### 6. Elgar 1/iv: would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

Yes, in the same way as the last comparisons. The parallels are close:
- Elgar's finale turns its march into a calm melody in the development; this finale turns F2 into the G-major hand-over at 123-130.
- Elgar's motto appears softly before the end; here the oboe has the motto's tail alone at 95-98.
- Elgar's motto returns in full orchestra with the strings in running figuration around it; here 255-264 has the last chorale over Vln II's and Cl 1's eighth-note arpeggios, with violas in quarters and basses in eighths.

One difference remains. Elgar's apotheosis runs on without a break, while this coda has more stages, each ending on a cut (264.1, 272→273). That is the protected coda plan (c043, c063), which earlier critics have defended, and I leave it. The one point where a conductor might stop is the one in Finding 1: a horn statement doubled in its own register by held horns is not something Elgar, who wrote out every horn doubling with care, would leave.
