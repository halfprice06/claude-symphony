## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 247–254: bass trombone against Tbn 1·2 in the same register (applied)

Problem: since cycle 71, Tbn 1·2 play the P head in octaves at G2–A3, while the bass trombone holds roots at C3–G2. The result is seconds and crossings in the lowest brass:
- 247 beat 4: D♭3 against C3, a minor second.
- 248: C3 against D3.
- 249: G2 against F2.
- 251: B2 against C3.
- 253: E3 against D3.

Tbn 2's G2 also lies under the bass trombone's C3 at 247 and under its E3 at 251, so the real bass changes from voice to voice.

Evidence: A&S PDF 77 says close low spacing blurs the harmony. PDF 548–549 and 580 ask for a clear outer-voice frame under chromatic writing. The cello, double bass, contrabassoon and tuba already carry the roots, so the bass trombone adds nothing here except the clashes.

Fix (sections/finale.ily):
```
finaleBassTbn = { R1*8 | e1\ff~ ...
```
This replaces `c1\f | d1 | f,1 | g,1 | e2 c2 | f,1 | d1 | g,1`, so the bass trombone enters at 255 with the chorale. It keeps what cycle 71 wanted, the trombone P head in octaves, and removes only the doubling that conflicts with it.

### 2. Bars 262–264: the trumpet leading tone at the only root-position V–I is an inner voice (applied)

Problem: at the final cadence Tpt 2 goes D5→E5 above Tpt 1's B4→C5. The B→C that the cadence needs in the trumpets is therefore covered and crossed. The E5 on top also leaves C5 under E5 in the leading voice at the arrival. This contradicts the 222→223 layout, which has Tpt 1 on C5, Tpt 2 on G4 and Tpt 3 on E4.

Evidence: A&S PDF 87–88 on voice crossing. PDF 563–566: in the exchanges they show, the outer voices state V–I clearly. cyclic-notes.md asks for "I with E♮ in the trumpets" for the Don Giovanni arrival. That is satisfied as long as some trumpet holds the E♮; it does not have to be the top one.

Fix:
- Tpt 1·2: `\partCombineUnisono d''1\<~ | d''1 | c''1 | b'1 | c''1\fff~ | c''1 \partCombineAutomatic |` at 259–264. This also removes the force-hshift and tie overrides.
- Tpt 3: `g'2-> g'2 | e'1\fff~ | e'1 |` at 262–264.

The chorale top line itself is unchanged. Tpt 1·2 now play it a2, and Vln I and Fl 1 still double it.

### 3. Bar 271: the trumpets distort F1 and move from unison to octave by contrary motion (applied)

Problem: Tpt 1·2 start the F1 figure on D5 and fall to G4, while Vln I and Fl 1 go D5→G5. The trumpets therefore turn F1's rising fourth into a falling fifth. Against the violins, the move from a unison D5 to the G4–G5 octave is contrary motion.

Evidence: A&S PDF 86 says fifths and octaves by contrary motion are acceptable only as an exception, and "the same applies to motion between a unison and an octave". Here nothing calls for it, because the trumpets can take the theme's own interval. F1 begins with a rising fourth at bars 13 and 153.

Fix: `R1*2 | d'4(\ff g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4)` at 270–271 (was `d''4(` at the start of 271). This is a correction to F1's contour in one instrument. It is not a change to F1 itself; the theme stays as recorded, and themecheck reports 36/36 unchanged.

### 4. Bars 250→251: the seventh of V7 rises (applied)

Problem: at 250, Hn 3 holds F4, the seventh of G7. It moves up to G4 at 251, and no other voice takes F→E. At 251 the E sounds only in Hn 4.

Evidence: A&S treats the downward resolution of the seventh as the norm in every chromatic context in these units (PDF 558–560, consecutive sevenths: "each seventh resolves"). An unresolved seventh in the brass at forte is audible.

Fix: `finaleHnThree = { g'1\f | d'1 | a'1 | g'2 f'2 | e'2 g'2 | a'1 |` (was `g'1` at 251). I kept Hn 4 on E. Moving it to C would make octaves with Tpt 2, and moving it to G would make fifths with the bass.

### 5. Bar 163: Neapolitan voicing, Tbn 1 D♭4→B3 (applied)

Problem: the c069 "plain" voicing puts D♭4 in Tbn 1 for two beats, followed by B3. This is a diminished third between chord tones, and the D♭ sounds against the passing C in the woodwinds on beat 4.

Evidence: A&S PDF 536–537 says the Neapolitan is best prepared by an earlier plain use. Bar 156 provides that use, so the chord itself follows the book. PDF 537 adds that a chromatic tone is clearest when it moves inside a chromatic line, here D♭–C–B.

Fix (sections/recP.ily): `| c'2 des'4. c'8 | b1 |` (was `c'2 des'2 | b1`). The voicing and the chord are otherwise unchanged.

### Points judged sound, no change

- **273–276, common-tone °7 F♯–A–C–E♭ over C.** It follows the Brahms Op. 87 spelling (PDF 544–545). The F♯ descends through the protected 5–♯4–4–3 chain.
- **255–263, chorale harmony.** The chorale runs I6–vi–iiø6/5–Ger6–V7–I. Putting C in Ob 2 and Hn 4 at 259 keeps the iiø6/5 complete. Ger6 E♭→G avoids parallel fifths because the E♭ moves to D.
- **Breath-rest gaps at 266, 268, 270 and 272.** Each is one line over the pedal and adds no harmonic gap. The unison lead-in at 272 is idiomatic.
- **Scherzo quotations at 275–276 and the timpani at 143–145.** These are not edited, and themecheck still passes 36/36.
- **Changes to F1, F2, the chorale top line or any quotation.** None. The only note-level change to a theme statement is the trumpet entry at 271, which brings the trumpets back to F1's own interval.
