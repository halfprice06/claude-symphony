## Findings

### 1. 62–63 and 182–183: F2's climax group stops one note short of its goal. Let it run on to the downbeat and breathe after it (applied). This changes the phrasing of the finale's own theme F2; no note changes.

**Bars and problem.** In the trumpet solo F2's third group rises to its peak and falls: E♭5 | B♭5–A♭–G♭–F (61–62). Under 62 the harmony is iv7 → V7 (bass A♭–B♭, violas and Vln II F–B♭/D–A♭). At 63 the bass moves to C♭ and the trumpet plays E♭5: V7 → VI, a deceptive resolution, with the melody resolving 2 → 1. But the slur ends on F at 62.4, a breath mark follows, Fl 1's doubling ends its slur there too, and E♭ at 63 starts a new slur marked p. So the peak descent is cut off on the second degree over V7, and the note it resolves to is heard as the start of something new. In the recapitulation (cor anglais and muted Tpt 1, 179–182, with Fl 1 at 181.4–183) it is the same: G5–F–E♭–D | breath | C5, over V → i6 (bass G → E♭). This is the only place in F2 where the climax and its resolution are separated by a breath. The solo has no upbeat group and no overlapping group anywhere; it is all downbeat-starting groups separated by breaths after 54, 58 and 62.

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 52 (§7): rhythmic groups "form an important element in the design of a composition", they may extend beyond the bar (Ex. 3-10), and "one rhythmic group can merge into another; this happens when the last note of one also functions as the first note of the next. Such groups are said to overlap" (Ex. 3-11, Mendelssohn, where each group ends on the note that begins the next). PDF 54 (§8): "Very often the end of a group of measures coincides with a goal of tonal motion"; a group is a phrase when it ends on that goal. The goal of B♭–A♭–G♭–F over V7 is E♭ over VI, so the climax group should end on 63.1 and the last group can begin from it. PDF 50 (§3): the long note attracts the accent; E♭ at 63 is a dotted half on the strong first bar of a hypermeasure (PDF 53, §8), so it is the natural place for the climax to land, not the place for a fresh start after a breath. PDF 52 (Ex. 3-9b): groups may begin on an upbeat; with the breath after 63's E♭, the last group becomes the upbeat D♭ into the scale D♭–C♭–B♭–A♭–G♭–F, which the solo did not yet have. Tchaikovsky and Dvořák slow second themes commonly carry the peak's fall over the bar line onto a deceptive resolution in this way; the score's own E♭-major statement already connects 82 into 83 (overlap).
