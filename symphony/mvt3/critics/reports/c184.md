## Findings

### 1. 247–254: trombones ff outweigh both heads (applied)
Problem: in the two-head combination the P head's lower octave is Tbn 1-2 a2 ff (G2–A3, with Bn 1 and cellos). By Rimsky's scale (PDF 51) two trombones in forte equal four horns, eight wood-winds. The S head has Tpt 1-2 at f (c084 lowered them so they do not cover the P head's upper octave), plus Hn 1-2 ff, Ob, Cl, violas; the P head's upper octave has Fl 1-2 and Vln I-II (about three horn units by the same scale). So the loudest single line is the lowest octave of the P head, three octaves under its real register, and the trumpets were already marked down. Rimsky treats trombone and trumpet as equals (PDF 51), so the two brass pairs should carry the same mark.
Fix (applied): finale.ily `finaleTbnOne`/`finaleTbnTwo` bar 247 `r8 g,8(\f` (were `\ff`). The ff at 255 for the chorale is already written. No note changed; the c071 octave stays.

### 2. 259–260: the iiø6/5 doubles its bass F and leaves the borrowed A♭ to one trombone (applied)
Problem: over the bass F (tuba, B.tbn, Bn 2, B.cl, Vc, Cb) the sustained upper parts have F three times (Hn 3 F4 marcato halves, Tpt 3 F4, violas F4) and A♭ once (Tbn 2 A♭3); the arpeggios touch A♭ only as eighths. Rimsky PDF 83–84 (examples on PDF 83 and 84): the bass of an inverted dominant chord, and "also of other chords of the seventh", is not doubled in upper parts; PDF 85: the harmonic notes must not be lacking above. A♭ is the mixture tone that makes this chord iiø and links to the German sixth; it was the weakest note in the brass.
Fix (applied): finale.ily `finaleHnThree` bars 259–260 `aes2->\< aes2 | aes2-> aes2` (were `f'2->\< f'2 | f'2-> f'2`). Hn 3 goes C4–A♭3 by a third, joins Tbn 2 at the unison (a duplication unison, PDF 83 note; temporary doubling, PDF 97 point 3) and rises A♭3–E♭4 at 261 while Tbn 2 falls A♭3–E♭3. Tpt 3 and violas keep F–F♯–G, the chromatic line into the German sixth. The c048 accent pattern (accent on the first half note) is kept. Every chord keeps its pitch classes; 261–263, the top line and Tpt 1's B→C are untouched. This is an inner revoicing inside the protected cadence 259–263, of the same kind as c129 (262) and c182 (261). Notes: the crossing Hn 3 under Hn 4 on the shared staff is shown by separate stems (half notes against Hn 4's whole note), as c035 did at 262–264. par.py reports a C→A♭ octave of Hn 3 with the first eighth of the Vln II and Cl 1 arpeggios at 259.1; that is figuration of another texture (PDF 84) and of the same kind as many lines the script already reports in v184. The tenth F2–A♭3 above the bass (principle 15) stays: any filler held from 257–258 either moves A→C in octaves with Hn 4 or C→E♭ in fifths with the bass at 260→261, so I left it.

### 3. 242–243: Fl 2's F5 (checked, kept)
Fl 2 enters f for the ii7's third and falls to E5. By PDF 81–82 an added instrument for one chord is a risk, and by PDF 51 one flute at f is half a horn. But the F is also in the harp, and every stronger carrier fails: Vln II, horns or bassoon below Vln II's C5 make fifths F–C → E–B (the reason c182 gave); Ob 2 on F5 would make a fourth with Ob 1's C5 in the penetrating oboe pair (PDF 90, rule 4); Cl 2 would need a dynamic different from Cl 1's mf on the shared staff; Vln I divisi would send the F5 desk to G♯4 by a diminished seventh. The flute is the best of these. The only cost is the fifth C6/F5 inside the flute pair at 242.3 (PDF 90 rule 4), which is short and lies in the flutes' soft middle. Kept.
