## Findings

### 1. 7–10: the violins' run and the cellos' line are phrase slurs with the accented arrival inside (applied)
Vln I `r4 g'8(\mf\< aes' b' c'' d'' es'' | f'' g'' aes'' b'' d'''4->\f)` (Vln II an octave lower) puts ten eighths and the accented D6 in one bow under a crescendo from mf to f. The cellos slur all of 7–8 in one bow at mf and all of 9–10 in one bow under the crescendo, with the accented F4 inside. Adler PDF 38: a slur in a string part is one bow, never a phrase mark. PDF 39–40, Ex. II-23: Liszt's crescendo under a long slur "is impossible to perform as the composer has marked", and Ex. II-24 shows the remedy, a new bow at each step of the crescendo. PDF 39 also says cello bows are shorter. c085 fixed the same run shape at 103–104 (accent taken out of the slur); 9–10 were missed. Fix (intro.ily):
- Vln I `r4 g'8(\mf\< aes'8 b'8 c''8 d''8 es''8) | f''8( g''8 aes''8 b''8) d'''4->\f\downbow r4`, Vln II the same an octave lower. That gives 6 + 4 notes and the accent as a separate down-bow, the same layout as the scale figure at 3–4.
- Vc `d2\mf( g,4. aes,8) | b,4( c8 d8 es4 f4) | g4\<( aes4 b4 c'4) | d'4( es'4) f'4->\f\downbow d'4->\upbow`. Bn 1 keeps its slurs, which are breath slurs.

A `\downbow` on the first eighth of 9 caused a LilyPond "programming error" in the full score, so I left it off. Starting after the rest, the players reach the marked down-bow at 10 without it.

### 2. 110–113: the climax melody carries a two-bar ff slur in both violin sections (applied)
`g'''1\ff~ | g'''4( aes'''2 f'''4 | f'''2~ f'''4 e'''4)`, and the same in Vln II, is one bow of 2.9 s at ff, the loudest string passage of the development, after a whole bar at ff on the previous bow. Adler PDF 38–39: the bow holds far fewer notes at forte than at piano, and a long phrase slur must not be marked. Fix (dev.ily): `g'''4( aes'''2 f'''4) | f'''2~( f'''4 e'''4)` (Vln II an octave lower), one bow per bar. Pitches, ties, dynamics and the woodwind slurs are unchanged. The protected "climax 107–113" (cycle 58) and "lament 107–110" keep their notes and dynamics. Only the bow division changes.

### 3. 35–38: violas and cellos take eight eighths per bow at ff (applied)
The countersubject in Vla/Vc (`es'8( des' c' bes aes c' es' aes')`, one slur per bar) is still at the ff of 27, under the violins' accented F1 head. Adler PDF 39, Ex. II-22: at forte Mendelssohn gives the violas six notes per bow. Eight notes in 1.43 s at ff will be played at about mf, and the same countersubject in the violins at 27–34 is bowed four notes per bow. Fix (pTwo.ily): split each bar 4 + 4 in Vla and Vc, for example `es'8( des'8 c'8 bes8) aes8( c'8 es'8 aes'8)`. No pitch changes.

### 4. 128–130: the violins' crescendo runs under a slur of 2¼ bars into the f at 131 (applied)
`a''4(\mf\< | b''2 g''2 | a''1)` (Vln II an octave lower) is 3.2 s on one bow with a crescendo, which is Adler's Liszt case (PDF 39–40). PDF 38 adds that a crescendo comes naturally on an up-bow. Fix (dev.ily): `a''4(\mf\< | b''2 g''2) | a''1`. The pickup and 129 go up-bow with the crescendo, and 130 gets a fresh bow before the f cadence. The same in Vln II.

### 5. 135–138: unmarked, the stop chord's sf falls on an up-bow (applied)
All five string parts play four separate whole notes, pp < to sf > at 138, after a quarter rest at 134. Players start a downbeat after a rest down-bow, so 138 comes on an up-bow, starting at the tip, where Adler PDF 38 says the stroke is weakest. Movement I's stop chord should not sound there. PDF 38: mark bow direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". This is such a place. Fix (retr.ily): `\upbow` on the first note of 135 in Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb (e.g. `aes''1\pp\<\upbow`). The crescendo then starts up-bow as the reading recommends, and the sf at 138 comes on a down-bow at the frog. No other change.

### 6. 114→115: Vln II holds to the barline and plucks one beat later (applied)
Vln II `c''1\p\>` is followed by `r4 <d'' g''>4\pp pizz.`, which leaves 0.36 s at the tip of a diminuendo down-bow to get ready to pluck. Adler PDF 54: allow time for each change, with an up-bow before the pizzicato so the hand reaches the frog. c085 gave the violas `a2. r4` in this bar for the same reason, and the cellos and basses already stop there. Fix (dev.ily): `c''2.\p\> r4\!`. Vln I's F♯5 still holds the D7's third through the bar.

### 7. 150: the cellos' pickup D3–F3 is a double-stop the hand cannot reach from the preceding chord (applied)
Vc `<g, f>2. <d f>4` asks for D3 and F3 together. On one string this is impossible (Ex. II-9). Taking the D on the G string and the F on the D string needs fourth and first position at once. The only way is open D with F3 on the G string, the strings crossed, shifted from the first-position chord in one quarter at ff. Adler PDF 29–30: a double-stop must be playable on adjacent strings, and the good ones use the open string naturally. The pickup is otherwise a unison D in every bass instrument (Cb, Bn 2, B.cl, Cbn, tuba). Fix (retr.ily): `<g, f>2. d4`. The F is an inner note, and the outer voices are unchanged (analysis.txt: 0 parallels).

### 8. Parts book: the harp's entry at 123 has no cue after 40 bars of rest, and its page turn falls just before the entry (applied)
In the v96 harp part, 83–122 are multi-rests through two key changes, and the pp entry at 123 opens page 2, right after the turn. Cycle 75 cued every other long-rest entry (Tpt 1 at 47–48, harp at 63–66, B.cl at 145–146). This one is the same case. Adler PDF 53–54 asks for time and clear signals before a change of activity, and MOLA PDF 5 asks for cues that are audible and obvious. Fix (dev.ily, bass staff, part only): `devHarpL = { R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4 | \tag #'score { R1*4 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring "bsnOne" #UP { R1*4^\markup \tiny "Bn. 1" } } | ...`. This cues the bassoon solo at 119–122, the most audible line in those bars (the `bsnOne` quote already exists). I built the harp part from the scratch copy. The cue prints, and the page turn moves to 114/115, eight bars before the entry. The score is unchanged by this tag.
