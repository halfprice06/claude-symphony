## Holistic comparison with Elgar 1/iv

Form and proportion. Elgar's finale and this one share the plan that matters: a minor-key sonata movement whose real goal is the symphony's tonic major and its motto, reached in the coda. Here the coda is 31% of the time (131 of 422 s), close to Elgar's weighting, and the motto chorale at 223 arrives in C as Elgar's arrives in A♭. The recapitulation is short (69 s against 148 s), like Elgar's, where the return is recognised mainly by the second group. The slow-part/fast-part proportion of the coda was decided twice (c043, c063); I do not reopen it.

Climaxes. The hierarchy is clear and master-like: 110–113 ff (A♭6 top, brass on V7 of F, no C major), 223 fff (C7, first full-brass C major, cymbal and bass drum), 263 fff (C6 top, the only root V7–I, cymbal), 278 fff (G6 top, cymbal and bass drum). Elgar also puts the summit at the motto's return, not at the end. One weakness is acoustic, not structural: 263 and 278 stand on C2 while 223 stands on C1 (Finding 2).

The A♭ F2 at 187. Violins in unison mf with violas and Hn 1 (p, dolce) an octave lower and the cello counter-melody. By principle 1 the horn's p against the violas' mf is a correct balance instruction (a horn at p matches a viola section at mf), not an error. The 12-bar major statement ending f on E♭ over A♭ into the hammered tutti works as Elgar's short second-group returns do. No change.

The close 263–279 at R = 88. Since R is 2 = 88 and Tempo I is 84, F1 in C major at 265–268 is heard at almost its own tempo, which makes the theme easy to recognise; the extra speed comes from the stringendo 243–246 out of 2 = 66. The allargando 271–272 into Largamente 2 = 72 gives the last 7 bars the breadth Elgar gives his last motto statement. Strings moving under held winds at 263–264 (the scale figure) are the Elgarian texture of moving strings under the held theme. Keep R at 88.

What Elgar has and this score lacks. Elgar lets the motto return quietly inside the finale before its triumph. Here the motto's slow form (G–E♭–D–C in long values) never sounds before 224; I checked every part in the MIDI. I decided not to add it: the motto is present throughout in diminution (the F1 and movement-I P head, 199–204, 247–254), the only quiet "halt" (138–146) is bound by the stop-chord rule and the F1-head stretto (do-not-touch), and a minor-mode motto over the B–D–F pedal would sound E♭ and D♭ against D. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? In form, key plan, climax order and the recall of earlier themes, yes. The remaining tells are local balance details of the kind the reading is about, two of which I fixed.

## Findings

### 1. 110 and 112, bass trombone: ff sustain inside the scale figure's octave (applied)
Bars: 110 (beats 2–4), 112 (beats 3–4).
Problem: at 110 the scale figure C4–B♭3–A♭3–G3–F3–G3 is in violas, cellos and Bn 1 in unison; at 112 the cellos play it at C3–B♭2–A♭2–G2. The bass trombone holds C3 ff through 110 and strikes C3 ff on beat 3 of 112, which is the cellos' starting pitch, then holds it while they descend away. Since v99 (c098) it is ff, not f. Tbn 1·2 already strike and release here for exactly this reason (c094); the bass trombone was left holding.
Evidence: Casanelles PDF 277–278: masking inside one frequency area decides what the ear can separate; a held unison or near-unison takes over the line (the flute-and-violins example). PDF 275: loud brass put their energy in high partials, so an ff trombone around C3 covers the octave above it, where the violas' version lies. Rimsky-Korsakov (principles of form.pdf, PDF 51): in forte one trombone weighs as much as two horns, i.e. more than the whole cello section on a moving line. The tuba, contrabassoon and basses already hold the bass C, so the bass trombone adds no needed pitch.
Fix (sections/dev.ily, devBassTbn):
`c1\ff | f,2 des2 | bes,2 c2 | f,2-> r2` → `c4->\ff r4 r2 | f,2 des2 | bes,2 r2 | f,2-> r2`
This keeps c098's ff for the climax and the 4–3 at 112 (Tpt 1, Hn 2·4) and completes c094.

### 2. 263 and 278, contrabassoon: the last cadence and the last chord lack the arrival's low C1 (applied)
Bars: 263–264, 278–279.
Problem: at the arrival 223 the contrabassoon plays C1 under the basses' and tuba's C2. At 263, the movement's only root-position V7–I and the symphony's goal cadence, and in the final chord 278, the lowest note in the orchestra is C2; the contrabassoon even rises G1→C2 at 262→263, so the bass line of the cadence goes up a fourth at the bottom.
Evidence: Casanelles PDF 274: the major triad and its spacing come from the overtones of one fundamental; a C-major chord whose voices are spaced as partials of C1 (C2, G2–C3, E3 and up) is heard as complete only when that fundamental sounds. 223 already does this; 263 and 278 are spaced the same way above but stop an octave higher. A&S (harmony and voice leading.pdf, PDF 445, Unit 26) asks that the goal tonic be reached by a line that falls to 1 with rhythmic resolution; G1→C1 gives the bass a falling fifth under Tpt 1's B→C and timpani G→C. The contrabassoon's range in this score is B0–G2 and C1 is used at 223, so nothing new is asked of the player.
Fix (sections/finale.ily, finaleCbsn):
`g,,1 | c,1\fff~ | c,2. r4` → `g,,1 | c,,1\fff~ | c,,2. r4` (263–264) and `c,1->~\fff | c,4 r4 r2\fermata` → `c,,1->~\fff | c,,4 r4 r2\fermata` (278–279). Basses and tuba stay on C2. The percussion plan is untouched (no bass drum added at 263).
