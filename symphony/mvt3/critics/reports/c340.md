## Findings

### 1. 95–98: movement II's last tail ends in the oboe; its held C5 should pass to Clarinet 1 — applied

Bars: 95–98 (end of the closing group, before the development).

Problem: Ob 1 plays E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5, "solo, espress.", mf > p, over the violas' E♭4 → E4 (the E♭/E♮ thread's first E♮, 97). finale.md records this (since v251) as movement II's last tail at the same pitches. In movement II that tail is Clarinet 1, dolce, ppp < pp (II 85–90, latest v400), ending on a held C5. The finale gives the whole quotation to the oboe, which has just held a ff E♭6 for three and a half bars (91–94), and the held C5 at 97–98 stays in the oboe to its end. The pitches recall movement II, the colour does not. Theme B, the other quotation of movement II, is handed back to its own instrument (cor anglais, 217–220) for this reason (c130).

Evidence from the reading:
- Adler adler1 PDF 19: timbre and texture "clarify the form as well as the content"; adler1 PDF 22: once "every note, chord, timbre, and nuance" is part of the composition, the composer answers for which instrument plays each part.
- adler2 PDF 188 (Ex. XV-33): the solo instrument is chosen for "the emotional as well as the tonal intent". Here the intent is cyclic: the listener should hear movement II's closing clarinet again.
- adler2 PDF 191, Ex. XV-37 ("Held C colored"): a soft held C is passed between oboe, flute, bassoon, clarinet and cor anglais at one pitch, each entry overlapping the last. The finale's held note at 97–98 is a C5, the same written situation.
- adler2 PDF 192, Ex. XV-40: at a change of colour "make certain that the seams don't show", so the clarinet must enter before the oboe stops.
- adler1 PDF 210: the clarinet can play "from the faintest pianissimo"; it is the right instrument to fade the held note.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-340/work/sections/clos.ily): the oboe keeps its line, notes and dynamics, but stops at the end of 97. Cl 1 enters pp, "dolce" (movement II's word), on C5 at 97 beat 3 under the oboe's p C5 and holds it into 98 with a diminuendo to the rest on 98.4 (the oboe held to 98.3 before). The two instruments overlap for two beats. The tail now starts in the oboe, the finale's colour, and ends in Clarinet 1 on C5, the instrument and pitch that end it in movement II.

### 3. Heterophonic doubling (Ex. XV-34) — not applied, with the reason corrected

c240 rejected it because it "would change pitches recorded in themes-finale-quotes.ly". That reason is wrong: the record checks named parts only, so a decorated line in a part that the record does not name changes nothing in it. The real obstacle is that every cyclic place where it would fit is protected for its layout: 211–218 (theme B, top line D♭–C–B–C), 235–238 (S-head texture, on the do-not-touch list since the first series), 273–276 (closing chain with the scherzo quotation). In each, an added decorated line would compete with the single line that carries the quotation. Adler gives the device as a way to strengthen a tune (adler2 PDF 189), and none of these tunes is weak. I decided not to add it.

### 4. 223→224: fff to f/mf without overlap — checked, not changed

The woodwinds stop after the fff bar, the brass re-attack f on the chorale, and the string tremolo drops from fff to mf inside one sound. Adler's forte-subito-piano scoring (adler2 PDF 190, Ex. XV-36) would bring the soft group in under the loud chord. Here the soft group (the tremolo) is the same players. The bars are protected (223–234, c240 and earlier). The drop belongs to the arrival-then-chorale design of the Brahms 1/iv model, and the brass chorale enters f with its own attack. I decided to keep it.
