## Findings

### 1. 94→95: forte subito piano with overlapping chords (Adler PDF 190, Ex. XV-36). Tested and not applied.

**Problem.** Bar 94 is the only seam in the score where the same harmony goes straight from ff to soft. The tutti strikes the E♭ chord ff for two beats and stops on 94.3. After two beats of silence, the E♭ chord comes back at 95 at mp/p (Vln II G4, Vla B♭3/E♭4, Vc and Cb E♭, Bn 2 E♭3, Hn 3·4 G4/B♭3) under Ob 1's quotation of movement II's tail. That is the setting of Adler's Ex. XV-36. Adler's device has a second choir enter p under the ff chord before the chord stops, so the soft level "is already there". The score does not use the device at this seam. Critics c243, c328 and c333 kept the break-off because it is a "section end, not a piano continuation". That reason is only partly right: both sides of the seam carry the same chord.

**Test (in /tmp/critic-423/work/sections/clos.ily).** Hn 3·4 leave the 94 stroke. They enter pp under it on beat 2 and hold into 95 with the dynamics they already have:
```
closHnThree, 94: r4 g'2.\pp\<~ | g'1\p\>~ | g'1 | g'1\pp~ | g'2. r4 |   (was: bes2-> r2 | g'1\p\>~ | …)
closHnFour,  94: r4 bes2.\pp\<~ | bes1\p\>~ | bes1 | bes1\pp~ | bes2. r4 |   (was: g2-> r2 | bes1\p\>~ | …)
```
The build is clean: lilypond rc=0, build-warnings.txt is empty, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py over 92-97 gives the same output as v423. The theme record is unaffected.

**Verdict: not better.** I keep v423. There are three reasons.
(a) Form: the half-bar of silence at 94.3-4 is the exposition's only full stop. With the horns sustaining, the seam turns into an echo, and the end of the exposition is heard less clearly. My lens puts the clarity of the form first, and Adler gives it as the purpose of colour changes (PDF 258).
(b) Pacing: Adler offers the overlap for a piano that should sound like the forte's own continuation. Here the score wants the opposite. Movement II's tail (Ob 1, E♭5-D5-D♭5-C5) enters as a recollection, and the silence sets it apart. The same thing happens at the 113→114 break-off before the G-minor area, so the two silences match each other.
(c) Scoring: in the test, Hn 3·4 leave the stroke, so the codetta's last ff chord loses its middle horns. It has kept them in every bar since 91.

Adler PDF 267 is already in force at 95, where the soloist enters after the tutti over a held tonic in the horns and low strings. Nothing else is needed. This answers c333's reason with a new one and confirms the break-off; it is not a reversal.

### 3. Dialogue and climax doubling in the second-theme head, 235→239. Follows. No change.

Adler's dialogue (PDF 257-258) means repeating a tune "in a radically different orchestral color". At 239 the violins keep the tune, and Fl 1 and Ob 1 add the upper octave at f. That is not dialogue. It is Bizet's climax doubling (PDF 279), and it suits the coda's growth better than handing the tune over. The violins' C-major second-theme head over the C pedal is an owed item, and 235-246 is protected (c093, c873). The movement already has dialogue where it shapes the form (51→67, 171→187, 115→123).

### 4. Registral distance under the horn call, 243-246 (Adler PDF 274). Follows. No change.

The divided violins' chords (G4-A♭5) lie inside the trumpets' octave E5-C6. Adler's rule matters most for woodwind soloists, and he notes that rhythmic contrast reduces the need. Here the soloists are two trumpets with the horns an octave below. The violins have held chords that change only on half notes, and they turn into the stringendo run at 245.3. The layout is protected (c768, c776), and I see no fault to answer.

### 5. Forte subito piano at 138 and 217-219 (Ex. XV-36). Follows. No change.

At 138, Tpt 1·2 play a sf eighth over the strings' sustained sf > p stop chord. That is Ex. XV-35 item 2 and XV-36 together. At 217-219 the held chord is already fading under the f top line when the sub. p arrives at 219. Both places are protected, and I agree with protecting them.
