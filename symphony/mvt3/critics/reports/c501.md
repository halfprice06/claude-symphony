## Findings

### 1. Bar 85: the bass rises in sixths with F1's head instead of moving against it (applied)
**Problem.** In the closing group, F1's head in E♭ major climbs F5–B♭5/C6–D6–F6 at 85 (Fl, Ob, Cl, Vln I·II). The bass (Vc `bes,4 d4 f4 aes4`, Cb an octave below) climbs B♭–D–F–A♭ under it, so beats 2–4 are parallel sixths between tune and bass. Bar 84 already moves in similar motion (tune D–C–B♭–A♭–G over A♭–F–D–E♭). The result is two bars in which the bass shadows the tune.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 209 (Ex. 276): "Contrary motion, especially between the lead voice and the bass line, can provide a subtle touch of melodic character." PDF 211 (Ex. 279): a line that shadows the melody "too closely, resulting in an almost constant parallel motion … distorts the aural focus". The score's own model is F1's bar 13, the same figure on V7 (`d'4( g'8 aes'8 b'4 d''4)`), which has the bass G2–G3–F3–D3 turning down against it. So the closing group's version of F1 loses the bass shape that F1 itself has. PDF 206 supports keeping the tune's own material.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-501/work/sections/clos.ily).**
- Vc 85: `bes,4 bes4 aes4 f4` (was `bes,4 d4 f4 aes4`).
- Cb 85: `bes,,4 bes,4 aes,4 f,4` (was `bes,,4 d,4 f,4 aes,4`).
- Vla 85, second half: `d'8( f' bes' aes')` (was `d'8( f' bes' f')`).

The bass now mirrors F1's bar 13 (V–V8–7–5). The seventh A♭ moves from the bass (85.3) to the violas' last eighth (85.4.5) and resolves A♭4→G4 at 86.1 (A&S PDF 102, Ex. 6-19). The violas' old F4 would also have made octaves F–G with the new bass. c147's reason (no A♭ doubled in octaves with the bass) still holds: the violas' A♭ and the bass's A♭ never sound together. Also unchanged: c143's I6 at 86 (bass G), the tune, 83–84, Bn 1·2 and Hn 1·2's B♭ pedal.
**History.** c187 put 83–86 on its do-not-touch list without a reason; later cycles changed the bar (c254, c267, c324). c143 kept "83–85's quarter-note arpeggio" for the I6 at 86. The arpeggio rhythm and the I6 are both kept, and only one bar's direction changes, so this is not churn.
**Check.** Build clean, 16 pages. par8.py on 83–87 reports only the cellos' usual doubling of the basses. allpairs_par.py on 83–87 matches v501 exactly (the octave I first got from violas F4→G4 against the bass is gone). analysis.txt: 0 outer-voice parallels. Theme record 51/51 unchanged. F1's own pitches are not changed.

### 2. Bars 215–216: Vln I·II rise in strict parallel thirds (tested, no change)
**Problem.** Under theme B's last notes, Vln I (E♭5→E♭6) and Vln II (C5→C6) rise in quarters, a third apart, for eight beats. This is the case Sebesky's Ex. 275 describes (PDF 209: "Instead of just voicing the violins in parallel descending thirds … passing tones in the bottom line").
**Decision.** No change. A chromatic passing tone in Vln II would be E♮ (between E♭ and F at 215.3). That brings in E♮ before 220, where it must first arrive from D♯ (the E♭/E♮ thread). A diatonic eighth-note variant weakens the rising sweep into the held D♭6 at 217; Sebesky uses that kind of sweep himself ("sweeping the strings up momentarily", PDF 222). Vln II also already touches the tune's notes on 215.3–4 (E♭5, F5), as Ex. 280 recommends.

### 3. Bars 133–138: six bars of whole notes before the stop chord (checked, no change)
**Problem.** The scan finds no attack after a downbeat for six bars. This is the "goose-eggs" situation of PDF 208, and the top line A♭5–G5–G♭5–F5 is close to Sebesky's static Ex. 278.
**Decision.** No change. Every voice moves by step every bar, and the cellos rise against the fall (c367). The pp-to-sf stillness is what makes the stop chord at 138 and the horn's F1 head at 139 stand out. A moving inner voice would either anticipate that head or blur the three diminished sevenths. PDF 208 itself warns that the device "must not be overdone".

### 4. Bars 258–259: F2's coda phrase stops on D without its goal, and the cellos leap a thirteenth (checked, no change)
**Problem.** F2's third phrase (Vla, Vc, Hn 3·4, Bn 1, Vln II an octave higher) ends G4–F4–E4–D4 at 258. Its goal note C (corresponding to E♭ at 63) never comes. The cellos jump from D4 to F2 at 259 with no rest, no drop in level and no change of colour, the three conditions PDF 218 gives for such a leap.
**Decision.** No change. Any voice that resolves D→C at 259.1 makes fifths with the bass A→F (D/A to C/F). I checked this for the cellos and for Vln II's arpeggio, which would have to start on C5. That confirms c411's reason. The cellos' F2 is the only 8-foot F in the iiø6/5, so they cannot stay in the tenor register.

### 5. Bars 257–258: Tpt 3's marcato C4 inside F2's line (checked, kept)
Tpt 3 re-attacks C4 on 257.3 while the line holds C4 and then crosses above it (PDF 211, 219). It is f against the line's ff. c084 chose C4 so that the third of vi is not masked by six brass on E4. A4 would put a second trumpet note against Hn 1·2's E4 at the top of the brass. The marcato halves keep the pulse of c019. No change.
