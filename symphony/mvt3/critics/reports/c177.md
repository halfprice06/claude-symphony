## Findings

### 1. 207-208: the F1 head's lower octave is one clarinet in its throat register, crossed by held second players (applied)

Problem. Since c176 moved Ob 1 up, the head at 207-208 has Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I in unison at A♭5 and only Cl 1 at A♭4. Cl 1's first, accented note (concert A♭4, written B♭4) and its G♭4 are throat notes, the weakest on the clarinet. On the same staff Cl 2 holds A♭4, the head's own first pitch, and Cl 1 then descends below it to C4; on the flute staff Fl 2 holds E♭5 over Fl 1's D♭5-C5 at 208 beats 3-4. Vln II's run (mf) passes through the same register. The head is clear on top but has no body underneath, and the second players blur its descent.

Evidence. Adler PDF 251: the eight-foot/four-foot principle (melody in octaves). PDF 256: "one must be sure that the most prominent melody note is in a good register for the instrument to which it has been assigned." PDF 257: pitches must be assigned "so as not to upset the balance of the chord or the flow of the melody." PDF 267, Ex. VIII-38 (Brahms 1/ii): a single bassoon doubling the theme in octaves "brings out the melody even more clearly despite the rather thick harmonic background." At 205-206 the score already has the head in two octaves (horns A♭4, Tbn 1 A♭3); 207-208 drops the lower one.

Fix (codaOne.ily). Cl 2 plays the head a2 with Cl 1 (its held A♭4 stays in the violas); Bn 1 doubles it an octave lower (its held E♭4 stays in Hn 4 and the violas), taking over Tbn 1's octave from 205-206; Fl 2 rests 207-208 and enters at 209.

```
codaOneFluteTwo: ... R1*2 | R1*2 | es'''1\f\< | es'''2. r4\! | }
codaOneFluteOne (209): ges'''1\f\< | ges'''2 r4 r4\! | }
codaOneClTwo: ... R1*2 | aes'2->(\f es'4. f'8 | ges'4-.) f'8( es'8 des'4-.) c'4-. | es''1\< | ...
codaOneBsnOne: ... c'1\f | c'1 | aes2->( es4. f8 | ges4-.) f8( es8 des4-.) c4-. | ges1\< | ...
```

The head now sounds at A♭5 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I), A♭4 (Cl 1·2) and A♭3 (Bn 1), over the mf horn chord. F1's pitches and c176's oboe change are kept. Shared-staff dynamics are equal (Fl 1·2 f< at 209; Cl 1·2 f at 207; Bn 1·2 have no new mark). This does not touch c160's violas 205-208 or c176's A♭ pedal.

### 2. 255-256: four brass on G3 under the last chorale; the I6's bass third not doubled low (applied)

Problem. At 255 (I6 over E) Hn 1, Hn 2, Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 all hold G3, two octaves below the chorale's G4 in Tpt 1·2; at 256 three of them still do. Tbn 2 is the harmony voice (C3, A♭2, E♭3, G3 from 257 on) but spends 255-256 in unison with the line, then leaps a fifth to C3. The chord's third E is in the bass trombone and tuba only among the low brass.

Evidence. Adler PDF 257, Ex. VIII-25: in a first inversion "the third (bass) should be doubled toward the bottom rather than the top. The 'open' quality of the first inversion is thus preserved." PDF 257: unison overlapping "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch." c084 already took Hn 2 off G3 at 256 for the same reason ("four brass on G3"); Tbn 2 was left there.

Fix (finale.ily, Tbn 2 at 255-256):

```
... g2.-> r4 | e1\ff~ | e1 | c1~ | c1 \breathe | aes1\<~ ...
```

Tbn 2 doubles the bass trombone's E3, then steps down a third to C3 at 257. G stays in Tpt 1·2, Hn 1, Tbn 1 (and Hn 2 at 255) and Vln I. The chords (I6, iii), the chorale top line, the cut at 264.1 and the 36 quotation checks are unchanged. c161 and c169 list 235-279 / 247-279 as do-not-touch for melody and for the master comparison; this change moves no melodic line and no top line, only the doubling inside the chord, so it answers both reasons.
