## Findings

### 1. Development climax 110–113: the goal chord has no 16-foot octave (applied)
**Problem.** At the development's ff climax and its F-minor cadence, every bass instrument sits on one octave. 110: tuba, contrabassoon and basses C2 (bass trombone C3 struck and released). 111 beat 1: bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon and basses all F2. 113, the cadence's goal chord (ff, accented half note): bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon, cellos and basses all F2. Above them Fl 1 and Vln I play G6–A♭6–F6–E6–F6. The lowest note of the climax is F2 at 113 while the top is F6, and the contrabassoon, whose job in this orchestra is the 16-foot octave (C1 at 223, 263, 278), doubles the basses at the unison. The basses can reach F1 (their lowest string is E1) and the contrabassoon C1.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 101: "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves". PDF 112: "in every chord the parts in octaves strengthen one another, the harmonic sounds in the lowest register coinciding with and supporting those in the highest". PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained or rhythmic chords and is "not to be entirely neglected" in short ones; 113 ends three bars of held ff harmony. In this score the other ff tutti goals already work this way: 147 (tuba G1, bass trombone G2), 223, 263 and 278 (Cbsn C1 under tuba C2 and bass trombone C3). Analysis profile before: bars 109–112 bottom B♭1, 113–116 bottom D2 (113 itself F2); after: C1 and F1.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-194/work/sections/dev.ily).**
```

```
So 110 Cbsn C1 under the tuba and basses' C2; 111 beat 1 Cbsn and tuba F1 under the bass trombone and basses' F2 (beats 3–4 unchanged, D♭2/D♭3); 112 unchanged; 113 tuba, Cbsn and basses F1 under the bass trombone and cellos' F2. Nothing above the bass changes, so voice leading, the cyclic rules (no full-brass C major before 223, Tpt 3 B♭ at 110/112) and the earlier orchestration decisions on 110–113 (c054, c059, c094, c098, c099, c188) are untouched.
