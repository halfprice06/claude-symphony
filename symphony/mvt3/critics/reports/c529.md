**Dvořák 8/iv comparison.** Dvořák's finale is a set of variations on one tune with a fanfare introduction. Each return of the tune changes its scoring and character (cellos alone, then a ff tutti with trilling horns, a flute variation, the C-minor march, a quiet slow version, a short brass coda). This finale is a sonata form, but it treats F1, F2 and the chorale the same way: each return is rescored (row 19). Two things a conductor would notice in the comparison. First, Dvořák's coda is short and fast after its quiet slow stage. Here the slow stages (211-246) are longer than the fast end (247-279). c313 and c329 kept this because the coda carries the owed items, and the reason still stands. Second, Dvořák keeps his woodwind and horn colours clean in soft chords: a held horn chord is usually completed by woodwind above it or by bassoons inside it, not by a clarinet sitting in the middle of the horns. After c329 (43-47) and c429 (167-170), this score had one soft held chord left where a clarinet sat inside the horn pair. Finding 1 fixes it. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Yes, for the plan, the cyclic links and the scoring of the tuttis. What a conductor would still sense is that the coda lingers: an accepted, protected choice, not a flaw in the writing.

## Findings

### 1. 39-41: Cl 2 sits inside the horn pair in the soft end of the staged diminuendo (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 39-41 the B♭ chord (V of E♭) dies away f > p. Hn 1·2 hold F4/B♭3, Bn 1·2 hold D4/B♭2, Cl 1 holds F4 (unison with Hn 1), and Cl 2 holds D4, inside the horn pair and at the unison with Bn 1. In the quiet bars, where the colour is heard, the horn pair has a clarinet in its middle, which is the layout Rimsky advises against. The clarinet adds nothing to the chord there: its D4 is already in Bn 1 and the violas (Ob 1 and Fl 2 have D5 at 39-40).

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns". On the same page: "Bassoons may also be written inside the horns, but the inverse process is not to be recommended". Bn 1's D4 inside the horns is the allowed layout. Cl 2's D4 is not.
- Rimsky PDF 106 lists "2 Horns + 2 Cl." in unison among the normal pairings: "Wood-wind reinforces the brass, softens it". A softer horn tone is what a diminuendo into the pp transition (43) needs.
- Ex. 141 (PDF 316, *Tsar's Bride* 50: 4 horns + 2 Cl., 2 Fag.) and Ex. 143 (PDF 317, *Christmas Night* 165: 4 horns + Fl., Cl., Fag., dim. poco a poco to morendo). In both the clarinets double horn parts or sit above them, and the soft chord thins without a reed inside the horns.
- c329 (43-47) and c429 (167-170) fixed the other two soft chords of this kind. My scan shows 39-41 is the last soft held case.
