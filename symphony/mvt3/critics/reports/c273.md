## Findings

### 1. Bar 73: F2's E♭-major half cadence is approached diatonically. Use the bass 4–♯4–5 with V6/5 of V. Applied.

**Problem.** The E♭-major statement of F2 (67–82) is the exposition's main cantabile in the new key. Its antecedent ends with a half cadence on V at 74. Bar 73 is I (73.1) and vi (73.3), with the bass falling E♭–C to B♭. The tune is G5 | E♭5–F5, and since c261 its F enters on 73.4 as an anticipation. In this score, F2's E♭-major antecedent cadence and its consequent cadence (80–81) are the main structural V's of the second group. The consequent cadence already has V6/5 of V at 80.1 (c232). The antecedent cadence has none, so the half cadence of the theme's first phrase is the weaker of the two goals. It also sounds most like a continuation and least like a stop.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 215: "Among the most typical usages involving chords applied to V are those that emphasize dominant harmony (often at a cadence) by means of a rising chromatic progression, 4–♯4–5, in the bass." The text says IV or II6/5 can lead into it, which is Ex. 14-3d, IV → V6/5 of V.
- PDF 215 (on Ex. 14-4): with the diatonic chord "the V wants to continue moving; it doesn't sound nearly as much like a cadential goal as it does in the original".
- PDF 214 (Ex. 14-2, image): Mendelssohn's C–C♯–D bass into V, the half-bar applied chord leading to the cadence.
- PDF 220 §11: key areas and their cadences articulate the form. A half cadence that sounds like a goal closes the antecedent and sets off the consequent at 75.
- Dvořák 9/iv bars 60–67, as above.

**What the change does.** On 73.4 the anticipated F of the tune becomes the root of the applied chord, A♮–C–E♭–F. The anticipation from c261 is now a chord tone that the harmony takes up, not a tone against vi. The cadence's V at 74.1 then arrives as the resolution of ♯4, not as the next chord of a falling bass. This bar is a half-bar shorter than c232's applied chord at 80, so the two cadences stay different: the antecedent has a quick 4–♯4–5, and the consequent has the V6/5 of V on the downbeat and the 7–6 suspension (c241).
