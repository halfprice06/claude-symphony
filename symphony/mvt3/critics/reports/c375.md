## Findings

### 1. Steep beams on short groups: 95 groups of 2–4 notes cross two stave-lines (applied)
**Bars:** harp RH 67–82 and 235–242, harp LH 123–130; Vln I·II 17, 28, 157, 259–262; Vla 17, 19, 35, 157; Fl 1 28; Ob 1 157; Cl 1 162, 257–262; Bn 1 35; Vc 35.
**Problem:** LilyPond slopes a four-eighth arpeggio such as the harp's E♭4–G4–B♭4–E♭5 (67) by a full 2 spaces. That crosses two stave-lines in half a bar. The harp's 16 bars at 67–82 form the lattice that Gould shows as wrong.
**Evidence:** Gould PDF 33: "Beam angles should not deviate far from the horizontal ... Usually, they cross no more than one stave-line. Thus the wider the interval, the more flattened the beam angle." PDF 34: "Avoid steep angles through stave-lines, as these create an uncomfortable visual lattice effect", with a four-note rising group as the example; and "the beam of shorter groups should not cross more than one stave-space" ("1 and not 2"). PDF 35: "The stem that is going to be the shorter determines the starting point of the beam ... the other stem(s) must become [longer]."
**Fix (score.ly, applied):** a new callback `gould-max-slope`, set as `\context { \Staff \override Beam.positions = #gould-max-slope }` next to c285's damping override. It applies to a group of at most four stems that all point one way, is not broken across a line and has a quantized slope over 1.4 spaces. The end with the shorter stem stays where it is. The other end moves to exactly one space from it, so it keeps the same sit, hang or straddle relation to the lines (PDF 31–32, 34). Only that end's stem gets longer, so no beam rises higher on the page. Voice-level `\once \override Beam.positions` overrides still take precedence. Result: 0 short groups above 1.35 (before: 95 at 1.65–2.0). Long groups, kneed beams and the c285 and c105 overrides are unchanged. The MIDI is byte-identical. `parts-book.ly` does not include the callback, as with c285; add it there when the parts are next rebuilt.

### 2. Stem direction flips inside figuration that spans the stave (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 3.3 (motive x, E♭3–D3–C3–B2 inside the slur G–F | E♭–D–C–B); Vla 38.1 (C4–A♭3–F3–A♭3 in the countersubject); Vln II 163.3 (F4–A♭4–D♭5–A♭4) and 165.3 (G4–D4–G4–D4) in the recapitulation's arpeggio accompaniment 159–165.
**Problem:** In each of these passages every group has down-stems except one or two. LilyPond turns those groups up by the majority rule, so their slurs drop below the notes while the neighbouring slurs sit above them. In the cellos at 3–4, a single slurred motive changes direction twice (down, up, down) while the violins and violas play the same motive with one direction.
**Evidence:** Gould PDF 39, "visual considerations": "When groups within a passage together roughly span the width of the stave, visual considerations may suggest maintaining the same stem direction for the whole passage. ... this allows all slurs ... to remain next to their noteheads." PDF 39 also says "the context may determine the direction", and shows a group that takes the direction of the groups beside it.
**Fix (applied):**
- intro.ily (Vc, line 75): `\stemDown es8 d8) c8( b,8 \stemNeutral`
- pTwo.ily (Vla, line 29): `\stemDown c'8( aes8 f8 aes8) \stemNeutral`
- recP.ily (Vln II, line 83): `\stemDown f'( aes' des'' aes') \stemNeutral` and `\stemDown g'( d' g' d') \stemNeutral`

All slurs in these passages now lie above the notes, and stems below the stave reach at most a little over 3½ spaces. Pitches, slurs and MIDI are unchanged. I left the other direction changes alone (intro 9, 101, 131, 167, 169, 201–202, 207–210, 245–246). Those are scale runs that cross the middle line, where a change of direction follows the music's register and Gould's majority rule (PDF 38).

### Checked, no change
- The c285 overrides at ±0.25 (199–267) are 0.07 space off LilyPond's sit and hang values. Each beam still touches its line, so the white wedge Gould warns about on PDF 31 and 34 does not occur. Changing them would only redo c285's work.
- The D–D♭–C groups at 199 and 247 are three pitches on two staff positions. Gould's two-pitch rule (PDF 37) does not apply, and the ¼ slope follows the outer interval (PDF 35–36).
- No quotation, theme (F1, F2, chorale top line) or protected passage is touched by either finding.
