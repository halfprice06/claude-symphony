## Findings

### 1. Bars 36-37: A♭ to B♭ minor in root position with no voice-leading chord; three pad voices move in octaves with the bass (applied)

**Problem.** In the counterstatement, F1's head is stated on A♭ (35-36) and then on B♭ minor (37-38), a two-statement sequence rising by step toward V of E♭. Bar 36 ends on A♭ (beats 3-4, bass A♭1) and 37 starts on B♭ minor (bass B♭1). The wind pad's A♭ line (Ob 2 and Cl 1 A♭4, Hn 4 A♭3, all G→A♭ at 36.3) rises A♭→B♭ with the bass at 37.1, so there are parallel octaves in three instruments at ff. Bn 1, which doubled that line, goes to D♭4 instead. Neither the outer-voice analyser nor p5.py reports these octaves. The same voices then go into fifths: Cl 1 F4 (37.4) → E♭4 against Hn 2 B♭3 → A♭3 at 38.1.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267: repeated patterns where all voices take part bring 5ths and octaves, "therefore, voice-leading chords play an essential role". PDF 265-266, Ex. 17-2c, and PDF 273-275, Ex. 17-9 to 17-11: the ascending 5-6 is the standard way to rise by step. A 6/3 forms over the first bass note, the 6 comes on the weaker beat and is tied over (the "syncope"), and it becomes the fifth of the next chord. PDF 279 adds that root-position chords a step apart need a corrective chord between them. The score uses none of this. Here the 6/3 is F-A♭-C over A♭, ii6 of E♭, which falls naturally between IV (A♭) and v (B♭ minor) of the goal key.

**Fix (pTwo.ily; applied).**
- Fl 2: `es''1 | des''1 |` → `es''2. f''4~ | f''2 des''2 |`
- Ob 1: `des''2 es''2 | des''1 |` → `des''2 es''4 f''4~ | f''2 des''2 |`
- Tbn 1: `es2 es2 | des1 |` → `es2 es4 f4~ | f2 des2 |`
- Ob 2: `bes'1 | aes'2 bes'2~` (37-38) → `des''1 | c''2 bes'2~` (it follows Bn 1 to D♭, then D♭5-C5-B♭4)
- Hn 4: `bes1` (37) → `des'1` (a2 with Hn 3, following Bn 1)
- Cl 1: `bes'2. f'4` (37) → `f'1`. It still steps F→E♭ into the seventh at 38, as c132 required.
- Hn 2: `aes2 bes2` (38) → `c'2 bes2`. This removes the B♭3→A♭3 / F4→E♭4 fifths with Cl 1.

As a result, 36.4 is ii6 of E♭ under the violins' C5. The F is tied into 37 as the fifth of B♭ minor and falls to D♭ on 37.3. No part moves A♭→B♭ with the bass except the bass instruments (Cb, Bn 2, B. tbn, Cbsn). F1's notes, the countersubject, the dynamics and 38.3 (c162) are unchanged. On the Oboes 1·2 staff, 37 prints as a half-note chord F5/D♭5 and then D♭5. On the Horns 3·4 staff, 37 is a2. The page count is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 111-112: falling third D♭→B♭ with Cl 1 in octaves with the bass (applied)

**Problem.** At the development's climax the bass falls F (111.1) - D♭ (111.3) - B♭ (112.1) - C (112.3) - F (113). Cycle 32 removed octaves and fifths at the first falling third, F→D♭, by moving Cl 1 to C5-D♭5. That left Cl 1 on D♭5 at 111.3, and it falls to B♭4 at 112.1 while the bass falls D♭2→B♭1: parallel octaves at the second falling third. par8.py reports it; the outer-voice check does not.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 276-277 (the passage c032 cites in dev.ily): in falling-third progressions the upper voices move in 10ths or 5-6 over the bass, and in the root-position variant they must not move with the leaping bass. PDF 267 says the same of repeated patterns in general. The chord at 112.1 is iiø6/5 of F minor (G-B♭-D♭-F over B♭). D♭ is one of its tones, so Cl 1 can hold it.

**Fix (dev.ily, devClOne; applied).** `c''1\ff | c''2 des''2 | bes'2 g'2 | aes'2->` → `c''1\ff | c''2 des''2~ | des''2( c''2) | aes'2->`. Cl 1 now ties D♭5 over the barline and falls to C5 (the fifth of V7) with Ob 2 and Tpt 2, which already play D♭5-C5 with that slur (c188). It then falls C5→A♭4 into 113. B♭ and G stay in Hn 1·3 and Tpt 3 and in the violas' scale figure. Melody, bass and dynamics are unchanged.
