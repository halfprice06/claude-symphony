## Findings (ranked)

### F1. 80.4–81: the E♭-major F2 peak has a single suspension where Unit 21 gives a double one. APPLIED

- **Bars:** 80 beat 4 to 81 (sTwo.ily bars 14–15: `sTwoOboeOne`, `sTwoHnOne`).
- **What is there now:** c241's suspension. The tune (Fl 1, Vln I G5, Vln II G4, Vc G3) holds G across the bar over V7 (bass B♭, A♭ in Bn 1 and violas) and resolves to F on 81.2. Ob 1 doubles the tune an octave down to 80.4 and then stops. Hn 1 strikes D4 on 81.1, the note of resolution, under the suspended G.
- **Evidence:** In Ex. 21-2 (Bach, English Suite No. 2, PDF 347) two voices are suspended together in parallel 3rds, 9–8 over 7–6. PDF 348 adds that a dissonant suspension "brings into prominence the voice in which it occurs." The finale has many single suspensions (38.3, 81, 107–109, 210–211) and no double one, so the technique is unused. 80–81 is the right place for it: it is the last cadence of F2's E♭-major statement, before the animando at 82, and the bar is not protected.
- **Fix (applied):**
  - Ob 1: `c''4 bes'4 aes'4 g'4)\> | r1\! |` becomes `c''4 bes'4 aes'4 es''4~ | es''4\> d''2)\! r4 |`.
    - E♭5 sits a third under the tune's G5, so the pair is a 6–5 over 4–3 double suspension over V7. It resolves to D5 together with the tune's G5→F5 on 81.2.
    - The preparation falls on the weak 80.4. A&S allows a dissonant preparation ("typically, though not invariably, consonant", PDF 347).
  - Hn 1: `c'4 bes4 aes4\> g4) | d'1\p |` becomes `c'4 bes4 aes4\> g4~ | g4 f4) d'2\p |`.
    - Hn 1 already doubles the tune at the cellos' pitch, so now it also ties G3 into 81. Its D4 comes in on 81.3, after the resolution. This follows PDF 348 and c089's rule: the note of resolution does not sound against the suspension.
- **Unchanged:** F2's notes, c241's suspension, Hn 2's F3, the harp, the dynamics and 82 onward.
- **Checks:**
  - p5.py output is unchanged.
  - par8.py on 79–83 is unchanged.
  - c002/par.py loses the D4→E♭4 octaves that Hn 1 made with Fl 1 and Vln I at 81→82.
  - The page (p. 4) shows the tie and the oboe's half note clearly.

### F2. Borodin 2/iv comparison: a conductor would accept this as a master's finale. No change

Borodin's finale is a chain of dances. It works through rhythm, unison themes and colour held back for the tuttis, and it uses little harmonic planning. This finale meets that standard in its fast blocks: 199–204, 247–254 and the off-beat brass at 265–272. It goes beyond Borodin in its long-range design: the C–A♭–E thirds cycle, the two forms of III (row 14) and the one root V–I at 262–263.

The difference c189 named still stands. Borodin keeps one pulse, while this coda has several tempo stages. That plan is protected (c043, c063, R at 88), and I do not re-raise it. Borodin's pentatonic, drone-based tune has no counterpart that the cyclic plan here would allow, so I propose none.
