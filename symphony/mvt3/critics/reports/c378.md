## Findings

### 1. 239-246: the counter's second statement is unshaped, its crescendo has no goal, and the flute staff carries two levels at 242 (applied)

**Bars.** 239-246: Cl 1 with the violas; Fl 1 and Ob 1.

**Problem.** Movement I's second theme is stated twice. At 235-238 the chromatic counter (Cl 1 + violas) swells on its tied C5 and recedes as the violins climb (c278). At 239-242 the same counter is played f, a third lower, and it has no shaping at all. Its tied A4 (240.3-241.1) sits at one flat level, while the violins, Fl 1 and Ob 1 swell above it.

At 242 the violas diminuendo to mf for 243 (c368), but Cl 1, which plays the same notes, does not. Cl 1 then crescendos from f through 243-246 with no end level, so under the horn call it is a level louder than the violas, cellos and bassoons it was doubling. Those parts drop to mf at 243 and rebuild: bassoons to f at 246, strings to the ff at 247.

On the shared flute staff, Fl 2 enters at 242 with mf while Fl 1 is still in a diminuendo from f that runs into the rest at 243. The two players have different levels at the same moment. The MIDI shows a 6-step velocity difference there, the only such case in the score.

**Evidence.**
- Mendelssohn's Hebrides (Ex. VII-63, PDF 212) gives the second clarinet's independent line its own `< >` and its own word.
- Brahms 1/iv (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188) places the swell on the held note itself.
- Wagner's bass clarinet (Ex. VII-75, PDF 218) writes the long path out to its goal: "cresc. - - - f".
- Adler, PDF 188: two flutes must be "dovetailed with great care to preserve the illusion of a single flute".
- PDF 185: do not cover a register that cannot be made louder. Cl 1's G5-F5 pad sits under the trumpets' C6-B5, and at f with an open crescendo it competes with the horn call instead of supporting it.

**Fix (applied, sHead.ily).**
```
sHeadClOne = { ... | r4 c''4(\f b'4 bes'4 | a'2) a'2~(\< | a'4\> g'4 f'2\! | c'2\> d'2) \breathe | b'2\mf\< c''2 | g''1 | f''1 | f''2.\f g'4 | }
sHeadVla   = { ... | r4 c''4(\f b'4 bes'4 | a'2) a'2~(\< | a'4\> g'4 f'2)\! | c'2(\> d'2) | b2\mf\crescMolto ...
sHeadFluteOne: ... | d'''2\mf c'''2) | R1*2 | r2 r8 g''8(\f ...      (was: d'''2 c'''2) | R1*2\! |)
sHeadOboeOne:  ... | d''2\mf c''2) | gis'2->\f( g'2) | ...
```
- The swell matches the first statement: it rises with the violins' crescendo on 240.3 and recedes at 241 as the violins fall.
- Cl 1's path is now f, then > at 242, then mf at 243 rising to f at 246. This is the bassoons' path (c368), and it leads into the a2 ff at 247.
- Fl 1 and Ob 1 reach mf at 242, the level Fl 2 enters with, so the flute staff has one level.
- No notes, slurs or articulations changed. Cl 2 and Ob 2 rest in these bars.
- This touches only marks inside the protected 235-247 harmonic plan, as c278 and c368 did.

### 2. 102 and 106: the development's wind statements of the scale figure are the only ones without a crescendo on their rising half (applied)

**Bars.** 101-102 and 105-106: Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1.

**Problem.** The eighth-note scale figure falls and then climbs back to an accented top note. The score shapes its rising half wherever winds play it: Fl 1 and Cl 1 swell into their top notes at 44 and 46 (c278), and the winds' canon at 89-90 is f < (c377). The development's two wind answers to the F1 head, by contrast, are flat mf from start to finish. Their climb to the accented C6 (102) and F6 (106) has no direction. They do not lead into the strings' f head at 103 or into the lament at 107; they only stop on the accent.

**Evidence.**
- Brahms 4/iv (Ex. VII-4, PDF 186) gives `<` to every rising half-figure of the flute and `>` to its fall.
- Tchaikovsky 6/i (Ex. VII-93, PDF 227) gives each figure of the bassoon its own hairpin.
- Adler presents these marks as what keeps a solo wind line from sounding inert. Here the line is three winds in octaves with no strings, so nothing else in the texture gives it direction.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** The hairpin runs from the first rising eighth to the accented top. The level stays mf, so this adds no new strong mark: 101 and 105 keep their sf, and the limit on strong marks set in c008 stands.
```
devFluteOne: ... f''8\< g'' aes'' bes'' c'''4->)\! c''4-> | ... bes''8\< c''' des''' es''' f'''4->)\! f''4-> |
devOboeOne:  ... f'8\< g' aes' bes' c''4->)\! r4 | ... bes'8\< c'' des'' es'' f''4->)\! f'4-> |
devClOne:    ... f'8\< g' aes' bes' c''4->)\! c'4-> | ... bes8\< c' des' es' f'4->)\! f4-> |
```
- Fl 2, Ob 2 and Cl 2 rest at 101-102 and 105-106 (c178), so the shared staves get no unequal marks.
- c197 listed 99-109 as do-not-touch without stating a reason, under the texture lens. This change alters no note, no doubling and no texture; it only shapes three solo lines as every other wind statement of the figure is shaped.
