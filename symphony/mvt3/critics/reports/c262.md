## Findings

### 1. 72–73: the fifth chain's last 7th rises; make the chord a V9 whose 9th and 7th resolve together (applied)
**Problem.** c232 made 70–73 fall by fifths, C7–Fm7–B♭7–E♭. The first two 7ths resolve: the tune's B♭→A♭ at 71 and the counter-line's E♭4→D4 at 72. The last one does not. At 72 beats 3–4 the violas and cellos (unison counter-melody, mp espress.) hold the 7th A♭3 and go up to B♭3 at 73. No voice takes A♭ to G. The tune's G5 at 73 comes from F5, and the harp's A♭4 is a passing eighth. The second phrase's cadence (81→82, Bn 1 A♭3→G3) resolves correctly, so 72 is the one exception.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 465: the 7th "promotes progression to a new chord — one that contains the tone of resolution"; PDF 466 and 479 point 4: "unresolved 7ths occur most infrequently". PDF 463–464, Ex. 27-1 and 27-2: in V9 the 9th and 7th "resolve by parallel motion into the 5th and 3rd of tonic harmony". PDF 468, 479 point 7: 9ths fit root progressions by descending fifth, as here. PDF 465, Ex. 27-4d–e and exercise 1h (PDF 479): the 9th as a descending passing tone. The score does not yet have a major V9 as a real harmony (Reading notes row 5). This is the colour of Dvořák's and Schumann's dominants that the reading quotes (Ex. 27-9, 27-13).
**Fix (sTwo.ily).** `sTwoVla` bar 6 (72): `d'2( aes2)` → `d'2( c'2)`. `sTwoVc` bar 7 (73): `bes2( c'4 bes4)` → `g2( c'4 bes4)`. The violas pass D4–C4–B♭3 (3rd – 9th – 5th of I) and the cellos A♭3–G3 (7th – 3rd), in parallel thirds as in Ex. 27-2. The tune, harp, bass, rhythm, slurs and dynamics are unchanged. The viola–cello unison splits only for 72.3–73.2, and the 9th C4 sits a 9th above the bass with no D in its register at that moment.

### 2. 192–193: the A♭ recapitulation repeats the fault from a V6/5 that doubles the leading tone (applied)
**Problem.** 192 is the same counter-line a fifth lower (Bn 1 and cellos G3–D♭3 → E♭3), so the 7th D♭ again rises. Here the bass is G1 (V6/5 of A♭), so on beats 1–2 the cellos' and Bn 1's G3 double the leading tone in the bass. c253 rejected exactly that voicing at 72 for this reason (A&S PDF 122). The V6/5 dates from cycle 12 ("192 V6/5"), when 187–198 were in C and a root-position V–I had to be avoided. The passage has been in A♭ since v74, so that reason no longer holds.
**Evidence.** As Finding 1 (PDF 463–466, 479 pt 4). In addition PDF 469 sec. 7: V9 "appears most often in root position, not in inversion". An inverted V9 would put the 9th against its resolution tone.
**Fix (recS.ily).** `recSCb` bar 22: `\transpose c aes, { b,,1 }` → `es,1` (E♭2, root; the bass becomes B♭1–E♭2–A♭1, as 71–73's F–B♭–E♭). `recSBsnOne` bar 22: `\transpose c aes, { b2 f2) }` → `\transpose c aes, { b2 a2) }` (G3–F3, the 9th). `recSVc` bar 23: `\transpose c aes, { g2( a4 g4) }` → `\transpose c aes, { e2( a4 g4) }` (C3, so D♭3→C3). Bn 1 then falls F3→E♭3 in thirds with the cellos, as in 72–73. **Protection answered:** c224 protects 187–198 against further thickening and c232 keeps the key. This adds no part and no doubling, changes no dynamic, and keeps the passage in A♭.

### 3. 22 and 162: the root sounds a semitone under the 9th in the inverted V♭9 (applied)
**Problem.** At 22 (and 162) F1's peak A♭ sits over V6/5 with B in the bass, an inverted ninth chord. During the appoggiatura (beats 1–2) Hn 1·2 double A♭ at A♭4. At 22 Cl 1's broken chord plays G4 directly under it, and at 162 Tpt 1 plays a G4 off-beat (with Cl 1/Vln II's passing G4). The root has to stay in the chord, or the sound is the bare stop chord B–D–F–A♭ (c242 took that out of 169). But at G4 it blurs the resolution that the horns and violas make A♭4→G4 on beat 3. In the exposition the horn off-beats already avoid it (Hn 3·4 F3/D4 on beat 2, G3/D4 on beat 4).
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 469–470, Ex. 27-10: with inverted ninths "the dissonance always appears at the same time as the tone of resolution… The result is usually an unclear sonority", and "the unusually wide space between melody and accompaniment helps to maintain a clear texture". Unit 21 sec. 12 (cited there): a suspension is most effective when its resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass.
**Fix.** pOne.ily, `pOneClOne` bar 12 (22): `d'8( f' g' f') d'8( g' b' g')` → `d'8( f' aes' f') d'8( g' b' g')`. The root stays at G3 in the violas and Bn 1, a minor ninth below the A♭4. recP.ily, `recPTptOne` 162: `r4 g'4-. r4 g'4-.` → `r4 f'4-. r4 g'4-.`, and `recPHnFour` 162: `r4 f4-. r4 g4-.` → `r4 g4-. r4 g4-.`. The off-beat chord on beat 2 is now G3–B3–D4–D4–F4 under the A♭4. Tune, counter-line and dynamics are unchanged. **Protection answered:** c208 protects 159–166 for the trumpets' mf at 159, which stays. c223's 135–171 gives no reason, and c242 has already changed notes inside it.
