## Findings

### 1. 236: mark the portamento on the S head's rising sixth (applied)

**Problem.** The C-major statement of movement I's second theme at 235-238 is the finale's "molto cantabile" moment. Vln I·II play it sul A (c018), so the rising sixth C5→A5 at 236 is a shift of a sixth on one string, inside one slur, into the tenuto A5 with its swell (c138). The score already has the swell on the arrival note. It has no portamento anywhere: in the four movements no string slide is marked. The shift will be heard either way; the score does not say whether the player should hide it or use it.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 151: the transition between two notes, not the notes, carries the player's expression (Tchaikovsky's A-F♯). PDF 154: portamento and glissando are transition techniques in their own right. PDF 155: the lyrical legato slides into a swell on the note of arrival. Adler PDF 33: composers write "port." when they want the slide to be deliberate. The sul A here makes it the one place where the slide is already built into the fingering.

**Fix (applied, sections/sHead.ily, Vln I and Vln II).** `c''4(\!_\markup \italic "port." \glissando a''2--\< g''4\!)`. Once only. 240 (ord., an octave higher on the E string, f) gets none, so the slide marks the first, soft statement. Pitches, slurs, hairpins, the sul A spanner and the MIDI are unchanged (LilyPond does not play glissandi in MIDI). The line prints from C5 to A5 with "port." under it, clear of the hairpin (checked on page 13). The theme's notes are not changed; this is not a change to the movement I quotation, only a bowing colour.

### 2. 212-214: theme B's recall swells to its peak and recedes, as in movement II (applied)

**Problem.** At 211-215 movement II's theme B returns in Tpt 1·2, Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1 (cantabile) with Vln I an octave up from 212. The tune is ff at 211 and has no other mark until the diminuendo at 216. At Largamente 2 = 72 that is about 17 seconds at one level, through the rise to the B♭ peak at 213 and the descent at 214. In movement II the same phrase swells to the A♭ of bar 42 (mp) and recedes from 42.4, which in doubled values is 213.1 and 214.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 149-150: a line without its crescendo and diminuendo sounds lifeless. PDF 155: the lyrical line swells into its note of arrival. PDF 148: in brass the swell also brightens the tone, which is what a peak needs. Movement II bSec C.a. 41-42 (quoted above).

**Answer to the protection.** The 211-222 dynamic path (c009, c158, c178) is about levels: ff at 211, accompaniment f, the tune's diminuendo to f at 216-217 under the horn echo, sub. p at 219, fff at 223. All of these stay. The new hairpins sit inside the ff phrase and return to its level before the breath at 214.

### 3. 219-222: a technique change inside the held string chords (considered, not applied)
PDF 149 says strings change little in timbre between levels, and PDF 160 recommends long notes that change technique. A "sul tasto → ord." on the strings' sub. p chords at 219-222 would follow both. I decided against it. The sub. p's change of colour is already made by the brass, which change timbre most with level (PDF 148), and the strings need full tone for the crescendo that starts at 220 and the run into 223. A third process in four bars of the most protected approach in the score would be too much.

### 4. Vibrato marks (considered, not applied)
PDF 155 ties the swell to "progressive vibrato". No movement marks vibrato, and the models (Brahms, Dvořák, Tchaikovsky) leave it to the players. Adler PDF 33 says the same of portamento, which is why Finding 1 marks only one slide. I added no vibrato marks.

### 5. 51-62 and 171-178: the trumpet solo and the muted trumpet with cor anglais are at one level for eight bars (noted, not applied)
Principle 6 would suggest a swell toward 53's appoggiatura. Both solos are protected (c021, c031, c103: plain solo, p entry), and their first shaping comes at 60-62 and 180-182 by design, so the climax of the phrase is the only marked one. Their accompaniments are a level below (pp pizz., "dal niente" clarinets), as PDF 290 asks. No change.
