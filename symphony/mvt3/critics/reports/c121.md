## Findings

### 1. 265-270: F1's head is heard three times at one pitch (applied)

**Problem.** In v121 the C-major F1 is:
- 265-266: the head (C-G-A-B♭-A-G-F-E) in Tpt 1·2, Cl 2, Hn 1·2, Bn 1, Vla and Vc.
- 267-268: the same two bars again, unchanged, in the same instruments.
- 269-270: the same head a third time, in the violins and woodwinds.
- 271-272: only now the continuation.

The coda has already given the head at 205-206 (horns, trombones) and 207-208 (Vln I). So the last 70 bars contain five copies of these two bars and only one bar of anything that follows them. The theme's last statement sounds like a motive being hammered, not like the tune heard at 11.

**Evidence.**
- In the exposition (11-16) the head never repeats at one pitch. The climb (13-14) answers it, and the head then returns a minor third higher (15-16).
- In Brahms 1/iv (Adler PDF 74, Ex. III-4) the head C-B-C | A-G-C returns only after its continuation. The restatement also changes the continuation's third bar (E-D-E becomes E-F-E). The head is never heard twice back to back.

**Fix (applied).** 267-268 become the head a third higher, the major form of the sequence at 15-16:
- `e''2->( b'4. c''8 | d''4-.) c''8( b'8 a'4-.) g'4-.` in Tpt 1, Tpt 2 and Cl 2.
- `e'2->( b4. c'8 | d'4-.) c'8( b8 a4-.) g4-.` in Hn 1, Hn 2, Bn 1, Vla and Vc.
- Tpt 3 at 267 becomes `g'4-> r8 g'8 g'4-> r4` and at 268 `f'4-> r8 f'8 f'4-> r4`. This keeps the chord full (C-E-G at 267, not E in every upper part) and makes 268 a ii7 over the C pedal that falls back to I at 269.
- The head at 265 keeps its B♭ (c091). The violins' G-E-D-C-B-C figure (item 11 in finale.md) is unchanged.

The line now runs: head on C (265), head on E (267), head on C in the violins (269), climb (271). The top rises C5, E5, C6, then the peak in Finding 2.

**F1 change (tell the other threads).** In finale.md, "Themes this movement exports, F1 ... in C major at 265-268" needs a line: "267-268 the head a third higher (E-B-C-D-C-B-A-G)".

### 2. 272: the last F1 never reaches the tune's peak (applied)

**Problem.** In the minor, the tune's high point is the consequent's peak bar A♭6-G-F (22, 162; c001 protects it). The C-major statement at 271-272 uses the antecedent's ending instead: D-G-A-B-D | F-E-D, which peaks on F6. So the movement's last F1 stops a third short of the note the listener remembers from 22 and 162. The minor-to-major change of that peak (A♭ to A) is the clearest way this tune can do what Tchaikovsky 5/iv does with its motto, and it is missing.

**Evidence.**
- The first theme's climax is A♭6 at 22 and 162.
- In Adler's Brahms example (PDF 74) the second phrase is the one that changes and goes higher; the tune's later phrases carry the high point.
- The skip D6→A6 is the same skip the violins already play at 21-22 (D6→A♭6). Adler (PDF 82-83) warns about such skips for a section. Here the skip is accented, comes at the end of a rising arpeggio, falls in the allargando, and is doubled by both flutes, as at 22.

**Fix (applied).** Bar 272 becomes the consequent's peak bar in major. The F-E-D becomes A-G-F:
- `a'''2-> g'''4( f'''4)` in Vln I, Fl 1 and Fl 2.
- `a''2-> g''4( f''4)` in Vln II, Ob 1, Ob 2, Cl 1, Cl 2, Tpt 1 and Tpt 2. The breath marks are kept.

Bar 271 is unchanged. The line still falls to C at 273 (c031), and the seventh F resolves to E in Cl 1 (F5→E5 at 273). The top is now A6, below the C7 kept for 223. Bar 273 onward is untouched.

**F1 change (tell the other threads).** In finale.md: "272: the consequent's peak bar in major, A-G-F (as A♭-G-F at 22 and 162)".

### 3. 187-194: F2's counter-melody is too weak in its last statement (applied)

**Problem.** At 67 the cellos' counter-melody is played by violas and cellos together, mp under a mf tune in Vln I+II octaves: two sections against two. At 187 the violas have left it for the tune's lower octave (c074), and Hn 1 doubles that octave (c059). The tune now has Vln I, Vln II, violas and a horn. The counter has the cellos alone, still at mp (c114), and it opens on C3-D♭3-E♭3, the cello's G string. Adler (PDF 99) calls this the least strong string, one that "carries less well than the others". The counter also lies just under the violas' and horn's A♭3-F4 line. So at the last appearance of F2 its second voice, which made 67 a two-voice tune, is barely heard.

**Answer to c114's reason.** c114 based mp on Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 51 (one string section = one wind) with the counter in two sections, as at 67. Since c074 it is in one.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 91, Ex. III-39: violas and cellos coupled, p dolce, is "the very successful coupling" that 67 uses.
- Adler PDF 99: the G string is the cello's weakest.
- c074 had a reason for taking the violas off this line (the viola C string is sombre, PDF 86), so I keep the violas on the tune and give the cellos the level instead.

**Fix (applied, recS.ily).** recSVc at 187: `\mf` instead of `\mp` (with "arco, espress."). Bar 196 loses its `\<`, and bar 197 becomes `g2(\< c'4 d'4)` without `\mf`, so the cellos hold mf and crescendo to f at 198 with the others. No notes are changed.
