## Findings

### 1. Tutti entries after 24–140 bars of rest have no cues: 91, 143–147, 197–199 (applied)
Problem: the codetta tutti at 91 is entered ff by Tpt 2·3 after 90 bars of rest (they have not played at all), by the tuba after 84, Tbn 1·2 and bass trombone after 50, Ob 2 after 48 and the timpani (90) after 53. Hn 3·4 enter at 89 after 46 bars. In the retransition the cor anglais enters ff at 147 after 140 bars (its only notes before that are bars 1–6), the bass clarinet at 144 after 137, Tpt 1–3, tuba, Fl 2 and Ob 2 at 147 after 33, and the trombones at 143 after 29. Each player counts across D, E, an animando and Tempo I (or G, H, J and three key changes) with no cue. In the coda c105 cued the brass for the same situation at 197–198, but it left Ob 1 (30 bars rest, enters at 197) and the contrabassoon (32 bars, enters ff at 199) uncued.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 5, "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest ... Cues must be audible and obvious to the musician reading the part", and Example 4 (PDF 10), "Viol. I" cue notes before the entry. The project's own rule since c105 is a cue before entries after about 28 bars of rest.
Fix (part-only, the same pattern as c105): `\tag #'score { R1*2 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring "…" #UP { R1*2^\markup \tiny "…" } }` in place of the last two bars of rest.
- clos.ily: Tpt 1–3 and Ob 2, Vln I at 89–90 (`\transposedCueDuring "vlnOne" #UP c`, an octave lower, so F6 does not sit four ledger lines above the trumpet stave); Hn 3·4, Vln I at 87–88, an octave lower; Tbn 1·2, B.tbn. and tuba, Vc at 89–90; timpani, Vc at 88–89.
- retr.ily: Tpt 1–3, Ob 2, Fl 2 and C.a., Vln I's F1 head at 145–146, an octave lower; tuba, Vc at 145–146; Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn., Vc at 141–142; B.cl., Vc at 142–143.
- recS.ily: Ob 1, Vln I at 195–196; new `recSCbsn = { R1*26 | … \transposedCueDuring "vc" #UP c … }`, so that the octave-up part shows the cellos at sounding pitch.
I left the other uncued entries as they are. Fl 2 at 83 and at 199, and Cbsn at 87, enter beside a stand partner or the bass line that has played for several bars, at most four bars after a rehearsal letter.

### 2. Violin I and cello page turns fall inside melodies; Violin I's two 16-bar rests sit in the middle of pages (applied)
Problem: string parts use the free optimal breaker (c075: "the inside player turns"). In Violin I, page 1 ends at 78, so the first turn falls inside F2's first violin statement at the dotted peak 78–79 (c141). Page 3 ends at 213, inside the theme B recall. The rests 51–66 and 171–186 both fall in the middle of a page. The cellos turn at 72|73, inside their F2 counter-melody.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 7: "It is preferable for a multi-measure rest to be at the end of a page. If this is not possible, be sure that it is in a period of rest and/or an unexposed part of the music." Parts are double-sided with odd pages on the right (PDF 7), so in a 5-page part the only turns come after pages 1 and 3.
Fix (parts-book.ly only): in the Violin I book, add a third layer `{ s1*66 \pageBreak s1*120 \pageBreak s1*48 \pageBreak }`. Page 1 now ends with 51–66, page 3 with 171–186, and page 4 ends at 234, where pages 4 and 5 face each other. The 234 break also stops page 4 from being over-full (2.6 staff spaces of compression without it). The part becomes 5 pages and both turns cost no notes. In the Vc book, `{ s1*66 \pageBreak }` moves the first turn to the three beats of rest after the pizzicato in 66. The second turn is now at 228|229, in whole notes at Maestoso; it was at 240|241.

### 3. The ranges under multi-measure rests collide with the next system's letters and tempo marks (applied)
Problem: c135's range labels are added to the rest's stencil below the stave, but the rest's height (`ly:multi-measure-rest::height`) does not include them. The vertical spacing therefore does not see them. In the cor anglais part the E box is printed on top of "7–10", the G box on "43–48", and "Tempo I (♩ = 84)" of K sits on "115–122". In Flute 1, p. 2, the K box covers the "2" of "135–142".
Evidence: MOLA PDF 8, "the placement of any 'anchored' markings may not remain positioned as they were in the score, leading to collisions", and Example 7 (PDF 12); PDF 7, "avoid over-crowding to provide clarity".
Fix (parts-book.ly): `#(define (mmr-height-with-range grob) (let ((e (ly:multi-measure-rest::height grob))) (if (> (ly:grob-property grob 'measure-count 1) 1) (cons (min (car e) -5.0) (cdr e)) e)))` and `\override MultiMeasureRest.Y-extent = #(ly:make-unpure-pure-container mmr-height-with-range)` in the Staff context. The C.a. book gets `\paper { system-system-spacing.padding = #0.5 }`, the Fl 2 book the same, and the B.cl. book `system-system-spacing.padding = #0 markup-system-spacing.padding = #0.5`. Those three one- and two-page parts take the new cues without being compressed. All collisions are cleared, and no part changes its page count.

### 4. The percussion part has no bar ranges under its rests (applied)
Problem: the range override is in the `\Staff` context only. The percussion player is on a DrumStaff and rests for 222 bars in 15 numbered rests ("10", "16", "16", "6", "2" …) with no ranges. That player needs the ranges most.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 5, "in the case of multi-measure rests, where measure number ranges are helpful (e.g., '27–117')"; Example 3 (PDF 10).
Fix (parts-book.ly): add `\override MultiMeasureRest.stencil = #mmr-with-range \override MultiMeasureRest.Y-extent = #(ly:make-unpure-pure-container mmr-height-with-range)` to the `\DrumStaff` context. The part is still one page: 1–10, 11–26 … 264–270, 273–277.

### 5. "arco" for violins that were not playing pizzicato: Vln I at 67 and 187, Vln II at 187 (applied)
Problem: Violin I never plays pizz. in this movement, yet the score and the part mark "arco, cantabile" at its F2 entries 67 and 187. Violin II last plucked at 115–122, was bowed again from 127 ("arco"), and is marked "arco, unis. with Vln I" at 187. A player reads "arco" as an instruction to change, so after a rest it raises the question of a pizz. they may have missed. c065 removed the same leftover "arco" from the violas at 171 for this reason.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 8, "Look at the music through the eyes of a player", and proofreading of the parts (PDF 8).
Fix: sTwo.ily `sTwoVlnOne = { bes''2.(\mf^\markup \italic "cantabile" g''4) …`; recS.ily Vln I `^\markup \italic "cantabile"` and Vln II `^\markup \italic "unis. with Vln I"`. Vln II's "arco" at 67 (after the pizz. at 51–66) and the cellos' and basses' "arco" at 67 and 187 stay.

### 6. Vln II part, 81–82: "sul G" and "ord." print as one phrase, "sul G ord." (applied)
Problem: "sul G" is attached to the D4 on 81.3 and "ord." to 82.1. In the part bar 81 is narrow, so the two words stand side by side above the D4 and read as one instruction. In the score they are a bar apart.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 8 and Example 7 (PDF 12), anchored marks collide in extracted parts.
Fix (sTwo.ily): `f'2( \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" d'2\startTextSpan) | es'2\stopTextSpan\mf\< …`. This is a dashed spanner over the D4 that ends at 82, as "sul A" is at 235, so the "ord." is no longer needed. Score and part both checked.
