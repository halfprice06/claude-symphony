## Findings

### 1. 95-98: the oboe solo's harmony goes to the horns, not to a second clarinet (applied)

Bars 95-98. In v229, Ob 1 plays the motto tail E♭5-D5-D♭5-C5, "solo, espress.", mf >. Under it, Cl 2 (G4-E♭4-E4), Bn 2, Vln II, the violas and the low strings hold the chord mp >. The horns, which have just played the ff strokes at 91-94, rest. The solo and its support are therefore woodwind over woodwind. The only colour that separates the oboe from its harmony is the clarinet's, which is the reed sound nearest to the oboe's.

Evidence:
- Rimsky PDF 116: "if the phrase is given to the wood-wind the harmony should be entrusted to the horns". His model of a soft brass basis under a woodwind solo is Ex. 136 (Snegourotchka, PDF 312): four horns pp under a flute cadenza.
- Rimsky PDF 113 / Ex. 171: horns doubled by divided violas. Hn 3·4 here sit on the violas' B♭3 and E♭4/E4, so the horn tone is supported, not exposed.
- Dvořák uses the same device, with trombones pp under the woodwind at 9/iv mark 9.
- This is the one exposed woodwind solo in the movement with brass free to support it, and the score does not use the technique there.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-229/work/sections/clos.ily:

```
closClOne = { ... | bes'2-> r2 | R1*4 | }            % was \partCombineSoloII R1*4 \partCombineAutomatic
closClTwo = { ... | g'2-> r2 | R1*4 | }              % was g'1\mp\> | es'1 | e'1\p~ | e'2. r4
closHnThree = { ... | bes2-> r2 | g'1\p\> | es'1 | e'1\pp~ | e'2. r4 | }
closHnFour  = { ... | g2-> r2 | bes1\p\>~ | bes1 | bes1\pp~ | bes2. r4 | }
```

- Hn 3 takes Cl 2's exact line. Hn 4 holds B♭3 with the violas. The pitches of the chord are unchanged, and Bn 2, the strings and the violas' E♭4→E4 inflection at 97 are unchanged.
- The horns are marked one degree below the strings (p > pp against mp > p), following Rimsky PDF 102-103 on horn weight.
- Hn 3·4 have two beats' rest after the ff at 94. Both players share dynamics on the combined staff.

This answers c228's stated reason for protecting 95-98: the break-off at 94.3 (two beats of general pause, Adler PDF 38) stays exactly as it was, and the violas and Ob 1 are untouched (c221, c224, c227). The clarinets now rest 87-98 except the ff strokes. No quotation, F1, F2 or chorale top line changes. Note for finale.md: "95-98 held chord Hn 3·4 p > pp (was Cl 2); clarinets rest 95-98".

### 2. The loud coda has no soft brass passage before the final surge (not applied; protected)

Bars 197-279. Dvořák brings the trombones in pp, after 13 bars' rest, under the horn's quiet recall (the `fs,1\pp` hold) before the Meno mosso e maestoso. That gives the last ff a soft brass floor to rise from. Our coda goes from 197 to 279 with short dips only: 205-208 horns mp, 219-222 p within full brass, and 265-268 brass rests. Rimsky PDF 118 (e) lists the soft restatement of a loud idea as a rescoring tool (Ex. 186, the Saltan march in flute and clarinets over pizzicato). The finale uses it at 187 but not after 223.

No change. 216-223, 223-234, 259-263 and 273-279 are protected, and c129 already raised this and it was kept. The 219-222 p is the nearest equivalent and works as the floor before the arrival at 223. I judge the current shape acceptable for this movement's plan, which puts its quiet recall before the arrival, not after it.
