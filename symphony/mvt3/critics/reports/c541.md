## Findings

### 1. Bar 254: a five-note turn on the violins' held G6 (Ex. 20-21b, 20-12, 20-23). Tested in the scratch build, not better, reverted.

**Candidate.** The turn appears only once among the principal tunes, at 75. The double neighbour does not appear in any of them. c361 made the turn a motive by setting the 71–72 figure a step above it. Bar 254 has the movement's last long held tune note outside the protected chorale and close: Vln I·II hold G6 for three beats at the end of the P-head line in the combination. In /tmp/critic-541/work, finale.ily `finaleVlnOne` and `finaleVlnTwo` at 254 changed from `g'''2.-> r4` to `g'''4->( a'''16 g''' f''' g''' g'''4) r4`. I used F♮ rather than the chromatic F♯ of Ex. 20-23 because F♮ is the 7th of the V7 that sounds in the winds and brass on 254.1–2, and F♯ would make a cross-relation with it.

**Build.** lilypond rc=0, 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 252–255 shows nothing new. The engraving (page 15) is clean.

**Why it is not better.**
- The S head (Fl, Ob, Cl, Hn 1·2, violas) completes its cadence B→C at 254.1–3 (flutes B5 half, C6 half). Above that cadence the held G6 works as a pedal, so the S head's resolution can be heard. A sixteenth-note turn at ff in the same beats competes with it. Unit 20 places figuration where a voice is otherwise still (PDF 336, complementary rhythms). Here the turn would move at the same moment as the other theme instead of against a still voice.
- With F♮ the lower note is a chord tone, so the figure is no longer a neighbour figure in A&S's sense (PDF 338: the turn combines an accented and an unaccented neighbour). The correct chromatic form, F♯, clashes with the V7. Neither form works.
- It would bring F2's ornament into a passage that combines two other themes, just before the chorale enters at 255. That breaks c451's plan of one figure per phrase and A&S's caution against overdoing figuration (PDF 336).

Reverted. 254 stays `g'''2.-> r4`.

### 2. Bar 268.4: a double incomplete neighbour into F1's major restatement (Ex. 20-13c). Considered on paper, declined.

`c'''4 d'''8 b''8` for `c'''4 b''4` in Fl 1·2 and Vln I would approach 269's C6 from both sides, as in Schubert D. 894 (PDF 335). D6 fits the V over the C pedal at 268.4 (dump: C2 / G–B–D). The plain quarter B5 makes the accented C6 at 269 stronger.

### 3. F2: plain minor statements against the figurated major statements (PDF 328, Ex. 20-1). Checked, follows, no change.

The trumpet solo (51–66), the bassoon in G minor (115–122) and the cor anglais with muted trumpet (171–186) all state the framework. The violins' E♭-major statement adds one figure per phrase. This is the relation between Ex. 20-1a and its reduction 20-1b, heard in time. It is also why the major statement is the one a listener carries away, as with the second strains of the big tunes in Brahms 1/iv and Dvořák 9/iv. I did not add figuration to the bassoon solo. The literal restatement in a new key and colour is normal development practice, and 115–122 is protected.
