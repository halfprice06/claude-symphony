## Findings

### 1. 168-169: the 7th of the recapitulation's iiø7 rises instead of resolving (applied)
- **Problem.** At 168 the link out of the recapitulated first theme is iiø7 in root position: basses and cellos D, Bn 1 F3, Cl and Hn 1·2 F4/A♭4, and the violins' run F-A♭-D. The 7th C4 is in the violas alone. It is held from the A♭ chord at 167, so it is correctly prepared. But at 169, over the B bass of vii°7 (B-D-F-A♭), the violas went up to D4. No other voice holds C at 168, so the dissonance never resolves in the voice that carries it. The violins pass through C5-B4 in their eighths at 169, but that is a different voice, an octave higher, on a weak eighth.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 177: in II7 and IV7 "the process of resolution is exactly the same [as V7]: downward and by step". The 7th "most frequently ... enters as a common tone held over ... from the preceding chord". Here the preparation follows the book and the resolution does not. PDF 191, point 2: the motion to V is intensified by the 7th, "which resolves downward by step". Ex. 12-1 (Schubert, PDF 176) is the model: D is held over I and VI, becomes the dissonance of II6/5, and falls into the dominant. Cycle 112 fixed the same fault in the exposition (Hn 3 at 17-18). The recapitulation's own link was not checked then because 167-170 was rescored later (c133, c137).
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-202/work/sections/recP.ily, with a c202 comment).**
  ```
  recPVla = { ... | c'1\f | c'1\> | b1 | d'1\p | }   % was c'1\f | c'1\> | d'1 | d'1\p
  ```
  C4 now falls to B3 on the downbeat of 169. The violas take D4 at 170, where Hn 2 stops (Hn 1·2 rest at 170), so the D of V7♭9 is kept in the same register into 171. D is still present at 169 in Hn 2 (D4) and in the violins' run. The doubled B (bass and violas) does not resolve in octaves: the violas leave it for D at 170. Dynamics, the diminuendo order (c108) and the B-C bass link to 171 are unchanged. The two parallel-fifth and parallel-octave checks give the same results as on v202.

### 2. 16.3: bare root-position ii° on beat 3 (checked; not applied)
- At 16.3, after the N6 on beats 1-2, the chord over D is D-F-A♭ with no 7th. C arrives only in Hn 3 on beat 4. At 17.3 the clarinets' broken chord does contain C4, so 17 is complete. A&S PDF 165 and 179 (Ex. 12-4b) say that root-position II in minor wants its 7th. The recapitulation already has it, from Tbn 1's C4 at 156.3. In the exposition the only ways to add C on beat 3 are to leap into it (Cl 1 A♭4→C5) or to break the off-beat pattern of Hn 3·4 or Bn 1. PDF 177 forbids a 7th entering by leap. 11-18 is also protected (c187, texture). The chord lasts one quarter at f between two complete chords. Decision: no change.

### 3. IV6 in place of VI at a deceptive cadence (not used; not applied)
- The reading teaches V7-IV6 as a second form of the deceptive cadence, which makes the 7th of V7 consonant (PDF 204-205, Ex. 13-15; PDF 209 point 6a; Ex. 13-20i). The score never uses it. All its deceptive cadences go to VI: 26, 35, 63, 186 and 205. Three cadences go to i6 instead: 11, 166 and 183. I checked each VI. At 35, 186 and 205 the A♭ chord begins a passage in A♭ (F1's head in A♭, F2 in A♭, the A♭ pedal), and IV6 (A♭-C-F) would lose the E♭ that makes it a key. At 26 the A♭ is the goal of F1's consequent, and the 7-8 on top (B-C) is the strongest form (PDF 203). At 63 it lies inside the protected trumpet solo. Decision: no change. The VI is right in each place.

### 4. I-IV6-I6, the Haydn "Clock" finale progression (not used; not applied)
- PDF 199-200 (Ex. 13-7 to 13-9): IV6 supports a passing 3-4-5 inside an expanded tonic, with the bass leaping down a 6th from I to I6. The score has no melody that rises 3-4-5 over a tonic. F1's head falls from 1. F2 and theme B rise by arpeggio (5-3, 2-1-3-5). The chorale top line falls. The only stretches of expanded tonic outside protected bars are 67-68 and 187-188, under F2's 5-3-2-1-3-5, which already has I-V6/5-I. Adding a IV6 there would put 6 in the bass under 3 and 1 in the melody, which is not the reading's pattern. Decision: no change.

### 5. II7 to a cadential 6/4 with the 7th held as the 4th (not used; not applied)
- PDF 182 (Ex. 12-8, Schubert D. 804): the 7th of II7 is repeated in the same voice as the 4th of the 6/4, which delays its resolution. The score's cadential 6/4s are 109 (from the Ger6), 151 (i6/4 after V7 over the dominant), 220 (from the Ger6) and 243 (from III♯). All four are protected cadences tied to the cyclic plan (the Ger6 family from bar 1, the III♯ chord of the major-third cycle). Decision: no change.

### 6. 197-198: IV6/5-V6/5-I instead of the plagal close in A♭ (considered; not applied)
- PDF 191 (Ex. 12-22) presents IV6/5-V6/5-I over a rising bass as "a beautiful alternative to IV-V-I where a strong harmonic cadence is not needed". The A♭ statement is designed to avoid a perfect cadence (c162: no PAC in the recapitulation), so I tested this as an alternative to 197's I6-IV(add 6)-I (bass C2-D♭2-A♭1). It fails on three counts:
  - The soprano is A♭5-B♭5-E♭6 (1-2-5), but IV6/5 wants 4 on top (exercise 1j, PDF 193).
  - The bass would have to become F-G-A♭. That rewrites the protected bass and cello counter-line of 196-198 (c121, c124, the 187-198 bow plan).
  - It would remove the subdominant colour that 231-234 and 273-275 later take up in C.
  Decision: no change.

### 7. 73-74: no II6/5 in the antecedent's half cadence (checked; follows)
- I-VI-II6/5-V with 1 in the soprano is the reading's model approach to a cadential dominant (PDF 183, Ex. 12-9; exercise 1c, PDF 193). At 73 the harmony is I-vi | V7-V4/2 under G5-E♭5 | F5. On paper I tried an A♭-bass II6/5 on 73.4. The melody's E♭5 would become the dissonant 1 and then rise to F5, so the 7th would resolve upward, against PDF 177 and Ex. 12-3. With vi-V and the soprano rising 1-2, the bar already follows PDF 166 (Ex. 11-4, 11-5). Decision: no change.

### 8. 38.1 (c112's open item): the 7th of ii7 in E♭ (checked; no change)
- At 38.1 the clarinets' E♭4 comes from F4 by step as the bass reaches F, and it falls to D4 on beat 3 over B♭. That is a passing 8-7 compressed onto the beat, inside a ff texture where the strings carry the line. It meets PDF 177's condition that the 7th not enter by leap. Decision: no change.
