## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 249–250: ii6/5 with the 7th held into the cadential 6/4 (Hn 4). APPLIED

**Problem.**
- In the combination of themes, 248 is ii7 (D–F–A–C). At 249 the bass moves to F, and the chord loses its 7th: it is a plain ii6 (F–A–D) for a whole ff bar.
- At 250 the cadential 6/4 brings the C back, but only in the S head's melodic C.
- In v472, Hn 4 holds F3 at 249, doubling the tuba, basses and contrabassoon. It then leaps up a major seventh, F3–E4, into the 6/4 and falls E4–D4.
- So an inner voice leaps a 7th into a cadential 6/4 in a full tutti. The supertonic 7th, which the reading treats as the main source of drive into V, is missing for the bar that leads into the 6/4.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 173 (Ex. 12-3): II6/5 "derives a much richer sonority from the added dissonant tone; its progression to V highlights the leading tone by resolving into it from a dissonance". II6 is preferred only "if a light texture is appropriate". II6/5 "supports 6 in the soprano much more easily". At 249.4.5 the S head (Tpt 1, Hn 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1) is on A, which is 6.
- PDF 177 (Ex. 12-8): the 7th of II7 or II6/5 "is repeated in the same voice to become the 4th of the 6/4 chord before resolving down to the 3rd of V".
- PDF 179 (Ex. 12-12): supertonic 7th harmony "can be extended over fairly broad stretches of time"; II6/5 spans 2½ bars in the Mozart. Here 248–249 becomes one supertonic 7th harmony over two bars, ii7 to ii6/5.
- PDF 172 and 186 (Points for Review 2): the 7th resolves down by step. In v472 no voice carried the 7th from 248 to 250.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-472/work/sections/finale.ily, with a c472 comment).**

```
finaleHnFour = { e1\f | a1 | c'1 | c'2 b2 \breathe | ...     % was  e1\f | a1 | f1 | e'2 d'2 \breathe |
```

- Hn 4's line is now E3 (247), A3 (248), C4 (249), C4 (250.1, the 4th of the 6/4), B3 (250.3, the 3rd of V7), G3 (251).
- Hn 3 keeps A4 at 249 and G4–F4 at 250, so the horn pair moves A/C → G/C → F/B.
- The C4 is a 9th below Tpt 2's D5, not a second. c374 removed Tpt 3's C5 from this bar because of that second (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 102–103). This fix does not bring it back.
- The timpani already strike C3 through 249, so the C was half-present. The horn now carries it as a line.
- At 250.3, Hn 4 doubles Hn 2's B3. That is a unison doubling inside the S head's own C→B, not a new line.

**Unchanged.** The S head, the F1 line, the bass, Tpt 3, dynamics, articulation, rhythm and the bar count are all unchanged.

### 2. 77.3–79: IV–vi7–IV, a rising third (PDF 172). NOT APPLIED

**Problem.** In F2's second E♭ phrase, IV at 77.3 goes to vi7 at 78 (bass C, under the peak B♭6) and back to IV at 79. A&S PDF 172 calls IV–VI weak, because the new chord shares two tones with the old one.

**What I considered.** IV6 at 78, which would rhyme with 69 (IV6–IV).

**Why not.**
- It would make B♭6, the peak of the phrase, an appoggiatura.
- It would turn 77.3–79 into two and a half bars of one subdominant harmony.
- vi7 holds the peak as a consonant 7th, and the harp's arpeggio is built on it.
- Here vi7 works as a neighbour chord inside the subdominant. A&S say "at present avoid" (PDF 172); the restriction is written for exercises.

Fix: none.

### 3. 133–134: II6/5–V4/2 over a common bass (Ex. 12-14, PDF 181). NOT APPLIED

This is the one technique in the unit that the score never uses, and 133–134 is the only place with a held 4 in the bass before V.

**Why not.**
- Making 133 iiø6/5 (F–A♭–C–D) puts A♭ a minor ninth under the violins' held G5, which is an inverted pedal.
- It also brings in the A♭ that the 135 diminished-7th chain introduces.
- It changes the G7 that c003 accepted and the quiet held texture of 133–134 (c466, sul G).

Fix: none.

### 4. 16.3–17 and 156.3–157: iiø7 to iv, a rising third (PDF 172). NOT APPLIED

**Reading.** Over the bass F–D–F, ♭II6 → iiø7 → iv is one subdominant area with the supertonic as a passing chord inside it. It is not a goal-directed chain II → IV → V. The tune at 17 arpeggiates F minor (F–A♭–C–F), so iv is what the melody states.

**Tested in the reduction.** Adding D at 17.1–2, to make iiø6/5 and so a II7–II6/5 extension (Ex. 12-11), puts an added 6th against the tune's arpeggio.

Fix: none.

### 5. 7–10: the rising harmonic-minor run with A♭–B (PDF 166, 201–202). NOT APPLIED

**Problem.** The cellos, bassoons and violins rise through A♭–B (augmented 2nds) at 7.4–8.1, 9.2–9.3 and 10.2. The reading warns about 6 and 7 in minor (Ex. 11-5, 11-6, 13-17b, 13-18b).

**Why not.** These scales run over V♭9 (G–B–D–F–A♭) in the dominant preparation, so both A♭ and B are chord tones. A♮ would clash with the A♭ (♭9) in the other voices.

Fix: none.
