## Findings

### 1. 75-82: the horn and bassoon pad is not in four parts (applied)

Problem. From 75 the pad under the violins' F2 passes from clarinets to Hn 1-2 and Bn 1-2 (c047 cited Rimsky PDF 108 for this). Rimsky's soft four-part texture means four parts, crossed, with the concords in the horns and the discords in the bassoons (PDF 108, with the example image). In v147, Bn 1 is Hn 1 an octave lower in all eight bars (E♭–D–E♭–C–C–D–C–C–D–D–E♭), so there are three parts. The horns hold the discords: the tritone D4/A♭3 (V6/5 at 76 and V7 at 81) with A♭, the seventh, in Hn 2. The pad also moves in octaves with the bass three times: Hn 1 E♭4→D4 against E♭→D at 76.1, which doubles the leading tone over a D bass; Hn 2 G3→A♭3 at 77.3; and Hn 2 A♭3→B♭3 at 80.3. At 82.1 Hn 1 and Bn 1 move D→E♭ in octaves with the violins and flutes. At 79 the horns are on one note, C4. The c002 script flags 24 pairs in these bars.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 108. Ex. 152 (*Antar* 56, PDF 324) shows the horn basis holding concords while the clarinet and harp move. A&S PDF 76, 83.

Fix (sTwo.ily; tested: in 75-82 the script now flags only Bn 2's octave doubling of the bass at 76):
```
sTwoHnOne = { R1*6 | ...cue... | es'1\p | f'2 es'2 | es'2 c'2 | c'2 d'2 | es'1 | c'2 d'2 | d'1 | bes1\< \breathe | }
sTwoHnTwo = { R1*6 | ...cue... | g1\p | bes2 g2 | g2 f2 | g2 bes2 | c'1 | aes2 f2 | f1 | es1\< \breathe | }
sTwoBsnOne = { es1\p | d2 es2 | c1 | c2 es4 r4 | c1 | d1 | es2 c2 | d1 | bes1 | d2 es2 | bes2 aes2 | es2 f4 r4 | es1 | es2 bes2 | aes1 | g1\< | }
```
The horn pairs are now E♭4/G3, F4/B♭3, E♭4/G3, C4/F3, C4/G3, D4/B♭3, E♭4/C4, C4/A♭3, D4/F3, D4/F3 and B♭3/E♭3: thirds, fourths, fifths and sixths, with no seventh. The seventh of V6/5 at 76 stays in the violas and cellos. Bn 1 takes the seventh A♭3 at 81 inside the horns (D4/F3) and resolves it to G3 at 82, also inside the horns (B♭3/E♭3). At 78.3 it takes the F, a fourth over the C bass. It sits inside the horns at 75, 77, 80.3, 81 and 82, never above Hn 1 (PDF 108: "the inverse process is not to be recommended"). At 82 Hn 1 ends on B♭3 and Hn 2 on E♭3, so after the breath the horns re-enter at 83 on the B♭ pedal from notes of the same chord. The dynamics, the breath, the part cues and c114's crescendo are unchanged.

### 2. 57: Clarinet 2 doubles the "undoubled" trumpet solo at the octave (applied)

Problem. At 57 Cl 2 plays G♭3–E♭3 under the trumpet's G♭4–E♭4, so the solo is doubled at the lower octave for a whole bar and the two parts move in parallel octaves. The plan, and HISTORY's do-not-touch entries for 51-66, call the solo undoubled, with Fl 1 joining only at 61-63. The recapitulation at 177 (Cl 2 E♭3–C4 under E♭4–C4) never had this doubling.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 106: wood-wind joined to a brass line "softens it and reduces its characteristic qualities", which is the opposite of what the owed trumpet solo needs. The c002 script flags 57.3.

Fix (sOne.ily): `sOneClTwo` bar 7 `ges2 es2` → `bes2 es2`. B♭3 is the fifth of i. It falls to E♭3 on the Phrygian IV6 and leaps to B♭3 at 58 as before. I tested the alternatives: A♭3 on beat 3 makes fifths with the trumpet (A♭3–E♭4 → B♭3–F4), and C♭4 makes octaves with the bass C♭→B♭.

### 3. 62: parallel fifths in the pizzicato chords under the solo's climax (applied)

Problem. In the off-beat chords at 62 (iv7 → V, c142), the violas' upper C♭4 → B♭3 and Vln II's G♭4 → F4 move in parallel fifths, on beats 2 to 4 of the climax bar. The c002 script flags 62.4 and, as a consequence, 63.2.

Evidence. A&S PDF 76-77. Rimsky PDF 112: octaves in a chord reinforce one another, and these doubled pizz. chords are the only harmony under the solo.

Fix (sOne.ily): `sOneVlnTwo` bar 12, beat 4 `<d' f'>4` → `<d' aes'>4`. Vln II now has the seventh A♭4 (F stays in the violas), and it falls to G♭4 at 63.

### 4. 85: the bass's passing seventh is doubled in octaves by the violas (applied)

Problem. In the closing group's bass arpeggio (B♭–D–F–A♭ at 85, into I6 at 86, c143), the violas' eighth-note figure plays A♭4 on beat 4 at the same moment as the cellos' A♭3 and the basses' A♭2. That puts the seventh in three octaves, and violas, cellos and basses move F→A♭ in octaves together. c143 removed Bn 1's A♭ at 85 for this very reason, but the violas kept it.

Evidence. A&S PDF 83, 405 (the seventh is not doubled). Rimsky PDF 112. The c002 script flags 85.4 in eight pairs.

Fix (clos.ily): `closVla` bar 85, second group `d'8( f' aes' f')` → `d'8( f' bes' f')`. The seventh is now a passing note in the bass alone, and it falls to G at 86, the first chord of the 6/3 bridge. The arpeggio into the bridge is otherwise unchanged.

### 5. 231-234: the plagal close moves in every group at once (not applied)

Rimsky PDF 108 gives the stationary parts to the brass and the moving parts to the wood-wind. At 231-234 the neighbour lines A–G–A♭–G and F–E–F–E are in Tpt 1-2, Hn 1, 3 and 4 and Tbn 2 as well as in the wood-wind and strings, in three octaves each. I decided to keep it. Rimsky PDF 113 approves the three groups side by side for a full, firm chord, the passage is protected (c019, c034, c113), and the A♭ of the mixture belongs in the trumpets. The timpani roll f > mf on C doubles the C pedal and covers nothing, and c146's softened roll at 223 leaves the tremolo and the violas' E4 audible (Rimsky PDF 113: string tremolo under sustained brass). The arrival needs no texture change.

### 6. 83-86: the clarinets inside the horn octave (not applied)

The clarinets play F1's head at E♭4–F3, inside the horns' B♭3/B♭4 pedal. Rimsky's warning on PDF 108 is about clarinets as harmony parts inside a horn chord. Here they double the melody an octave below the oboes and Vln II, which is ordinary Brahmsian doubling. The other options are Cl 1 in unison with Ob 1 (the doubling c144 removed at 97) or no lower octave at all. Kept.
