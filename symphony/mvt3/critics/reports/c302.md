## Findings

### 1. Bars 207-208: F1's head is repeated at the same pitch over six bars of V7; restate it a step higher on ii7, as at 35-38 (applied; changes a statement of F1)

**Problem.** Bars 205-210 lead from the hammered C-minor tutti to the D♭ arrival at 211 ("poco a poco più animato", f → ff). Over the A♭ pedal, the horns state F1's head on A♭ (A♭-E♭-F-G♭ | F-E♭-D♭-C, 205-206). Then the woodwinds and Vln I repeat it note for note at the same pitch (207-208), and the A♭7 is held under both. The harmony is V7 for six bars and the melody marks time for four. Only the orchestration and the crescendo build toward 211. The exposition's bridge does this better: at 35-38 the same head goes A♭, then B♭ minor, and the rise carries the music into V of E♭.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 264: a sequence repeats a pattern "on different scale degrees" and so connects the beginning and the end of a passage. A literal repetition at one pitch does not do that. PDF 265: sequential Fortspinnung is the usual connective tissue "between more stable thematic and cadential areas", including the passages that lead to big cadences. PDF 266: in a diatonic sequence the chord qualities change to fit the key, here A♭ (V) to B♭ minor (the scale of D♭). PDF 279 (Ex. 17-20): a rising-by-step sequence works as a transition to a goal, and the pattern breaks off before the goal. Over the pedal, the step-up gives the progression V7 - ii7 - V7 - I (205-206, 207-208, 209-210, 211). The seventh of ii7, D♭, resolves to C at 209 (A&S Unit 14/15 practice, and the same as c212's tied F at 36-37). The two-bar harmonic rhythm is then even, and the music rises one step before the 209-210 run. This is the same rise as at 35-38, so the coda's approach to its D♭ episode recalls the exposition's approach to E♭.

**Fix (codaOne.ily; applied).**
- Fl 1, Ob 1: `aes''2->(\f es''4. f''8 | ges''4-.) f''8( es''8 des''4-.) c''4-.` → `bes''2->(\f f''4. ges''8 | aes''4-.) ges''8( f''8 es''4-.) des''4-.`
- Vln I: the same line without `\f`.
- Cl 1, Cl 2 (a2): `aes'2->(\f es'4. f'8 | ges'4-.) f'8( es'8 des'4-.) c'4-.` → `bes'2->(\f f'4. ges'8 | aes'4-.) ges'8( f'8 es'4-.) des'4-.` The head now starts on written C5 above the break, not on the throat B♭ that c177 worked around.
- Bn 1: `aes2->( es4. f8 | ges4-.) f8( es8 des4-.) c4-.` → `bes2->( f4. ges8 | aes4-.) ges8( f8 es4-.) des4-.`

- Hn 3: `c''1\mp | c''1 | c''1\<` → `bes'1\mp | bes'1 | c''1\<`. It rises to C5 at 209.
