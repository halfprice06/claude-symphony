## Findings

### 1. finale.md misdescribes the basses at 269–271 (record only; no score change)

**Problem.** finale.md (C-major close, 263–279) says the "foundation (tuba, Tpt 3, timpani, basses) f at 265, crescendo 269, ff at 271 (since v124)". In the score the basses do not take part in that crescendo. finaleCb has `c,8\f` at 265 and no further mark until `c,8\<` at 275. That is deliberate: the c123 comment in finale.ily says "Cb stays f 265-274 (no ff-to-f drop at the Largamente)". So the score is right, and the record is wrong.

**Evidence.** finale.ily, finaleCb, finaleTuba (`c,4->\<` at 269, `c,4->\ff` at 271), finaleTimp, finaleTptThree, and the c123 comment.

**Fix (finale.md, composer's file).** Replace "foundation (tuba, Tpt 3, timpani, basses) f at 265, crescendo 269, ff at 271 (since v124)" with "foundation (tuba, Tpt 3, timpani, basses) f at 265; tuba, Tpt 3 and timpani crescendo 269 to ff at 271 (since v124), the basses stay f 265–274 (c123)". This changes no quotation, key or shared rule, so the other movement threads do not need to be told.

### 2. 87–89: the 7–6 chain is mf in the violas and f in Bn 1 on the same line (considered; not changed)

**Problem.** In the bridge chain (7–6 over the descending bass G–F–E♭–D–C–B♭), Bn 1 (`es'2\f~ es'4 d'4~ | d'4 c'4 bes4 bes4~ | bes4\< aes4 …`) doubles the upper viola line (`<bes es'~>2\mf^"div."`) at the unison, a level louder. A&S PDF 348 §4 says the suspension voice should stand out, so the possible change would be the violas f at 87.

**Decision: no change.** The suspension line is already clear. Each of its dissonances is tied and falls on the beat, so the voice stands out on rhythm alone, as the reading says suspensions do (PDF 348). A bassoon at f in E♭4 is not louder than half a viola section at mf. c058 set the violas mf under the violins' f figure, and c167 lists Bn 1 at 87–90 as do-not-touch. The c062 resolution at 88 is correct, and the crescendo 89–90 into the ff codetta matches the reading's Ex. 21-17 pattern of a chain leading into a new level.

### 3. 210–211: the G♭6 suspension (no change; the passage matches the reading closely)

The A♭7's seventh is prepared in Fl 1 (209), taken over by Vln I at 210.3 (an indirect suspension, PDF 357, with a dissonant preparation, PDF 358) and tied into 211. Vln I marks it `ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4)` with f at 212, so the suspension carries the weight and the resolution is softer (PDF 348). The nearest F waits in Ob 2 until beat 3, and the F4/F3 below are two octaves lower in another texture (PDF 352 footnote). Leave it as it is.

### 4. 243–246: the horn call's 4–3 and anticipation (no change)

At 244 the C6 tied from 243 is the 4 of the cadential 6/4 and resolves to B on beat 2. On beat 1 no part sounds B (PDF 352). At 245.3 the C returns over V4/3 and is held to 246.3, an anticipation of the tonic of 247 on a weak beat (PDF 363–364). A diminuendo on 244 would follow PDF 348 literally, but it would cut into the stringendo crescendo and break the protected no-crescendo layout of the trumpets (c084). Keep.

### 5. 273–276: the chain 5–♯4–4–3 over the C pedal (no change)

F at 274 is the 4 of iv6/4 over the tonic pedal, accented (`f'1->`). The pedal is restruck there without an accent (c048), and E at 275 has no accent. This is the reading's accent-then-softer pattern, applied to a chord over a pedal (PDF 348, 368). The crescendo from 275 belongs to the scherzo quotation and the final cadence. The pedal begins and ends on I, as PDF 368 asks.

### 6. 135–138 and 222→223: whole chords suspended (no change)

At 135–138 the stop chord is approached pp < and struck sf >, with the winds at sub. p and the stopped horns sf >. This is Ex. 21-37's crescendo into the suspended chord, then down to p (PDF 365). Its resolution waits until 222→223, the reading's delayed resolution (PDF 359–360) at the scale of the movement. Both passages are protected; they are recorded here as confirmed.
