## Findings

### 1. 198: the recapitulation's A♭ area relaxes into the coda instead of being inflected to A♭ minor (applied)

**Bars.** 197.4–199, the last bars of F2's A♭-major statement (M) and the downbeat of the hammered C-minor tutti (N, 199). Parts: violas and cellos.

**Problem.** The A♭ area is ♭VI, reached by the deceptive cadence at 186. Twice it hints at A♭ minor: Cl 1 F♭4→E♭4 at 194, and violas and Hn 2 F♭3→E♭3 at 197.4–198 (c210, c270). The tonic itself never turns minor. 198 is a plain A♭-major chord, f <. In it the third C sounds only in the cellos' C4 and the violas' C4 on beats 3–4. Everything else plays E♭ or A♭.

So the step into the coda is VI → i. The two chords share C and E♭, and the most diatonic, least eventful joint in the movement leads into its most violent music, the hammered tutti in movement I's layout.

The F♭ mixture tones are left as colour without a consequence. A&S PDF 600–601 makes the point that the Appassionata's E♭–F♭–E♭ belongs to an area that has actually become minor.

**Evidence.**
- **The minor inflection is the textbook move.** A&S PDF 596 (Ex. 32-15): the Appassionata reaches A♭ major and "the A♭ major is then inflected so that it becomes minor", and the area closes in minor before the music moves on. PDF 601–602 (Ex. 32-21, 32-22, Mozart K. 542) is this movement's own situation. A deceptive cadence on ♭VI stops the second theme from resolving. Then "the G major is not followed by a quick relaxation of tension; most surprisingly, it is inflected to become G minor (♭VI♭)", and the chromatic line produced by the mixture is what ties the passage to the movement.
- **A rising inflection adds intensity and marks the key change.** A&S PDF 585: a rising inflection (5–♯5) increases intensity, and modulations by chromatic inflection "attract the listener's attention to the key change much more than diatonic modulations do". With C♭ at 198, the cellos rise C♭4→C4 into 199 while E♭6 stays on top. That joins the common-tone link already there (PDF 586–587) to a rising inflection. The crescendo f < ff at 198 now has a harmonic event to carry it into the tutti.
- **It answers the exposition's C♭.** A&S PDF 592: a remote area "will create tonal conflicts that the composer must somehow work out". The exposition's E♭-minor area is prepared by the violas' C♭4 at 47–49, which falls to B♭3. At 198 the violas' C♭4 returns at the same pitch, and the cellos take it up to C, the home tonic, as the coda begins. This is the movement's first C♭ to rise.
- **The mixture tones get their tonic.** A&S PDF 595 (Ex. 32-14): pitches placed before the double bar prepare the new area. Here the F♭s at 194 and 197.4 already prepare A♭ minor, so 197.4–198 now reads iv(♭6) → I → i.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-293/work/sections/recS.ily, bar 198 only, with a c293 comment above recSVla).** C4 is kept on the downbeat, so the area still reaches its major tonic first, as movement II's 87–89 does (iv → I, with the same F–F♭–E♭ line at pitch).
