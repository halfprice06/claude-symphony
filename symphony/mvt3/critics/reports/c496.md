## Findings

### 1. Trombones play 211–228 without a rest (about 33 s, ff to fff) after 199–210 (applied in scratch)

Bars: 211–228 (Tbn 1·2, bass trombone).

Problem: from the hammered tutti at 199 the three trombones play every bar to 228. 204 and 210 give them half-bar rests, but from 211 the Largamente (2 = 72) and the Maestoso (2 = 56) hold them on whole notes to 228 with breath marks only: about 33 seconds at ff, f, mp, p and then the fff arrival at 223 and the chorale. Adler says the brass "require a great deal more wind than the woodwinds ... one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath" (adler1 PDF 292), and of the tuba "frequent rests are strongly recommended between passages" (adler2 PDF 8). c406 applied exactly this to the tuba on the same 211–228 stretch (rest at 217–218) and the trombones were left with the stretch the tuba was relieved of. The trombones then have to make the fff arrival chord at 223 (the movement's protected climax) on tired lips, and that is the one entry in the movement that most needs a fresh attack.

There is a balance reason as well. At 217–218 the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 echo theme B (A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4). c414, c440 and c490 cleared every held part on or near that register (Fl 2, Ob 2, Tpt 1–3, Cl 2), and c406 and c483 cleared the tuba. But Tbn 1 still held F3 and Tbn 2 D♭3, a third and a fourth under the echo's A♭3, at f fading to mp. Adler notes that the full brass can cover the rest of the orchestra (PDF 292). Here they are the last held brass under a soft horn solo.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-496/work/sections/codaTwo.ily): the trombones end with the 216 diminuendo and rest 217–218, as Tpt 3 already does, and re-enter p at 219, where they already entered p (c108):

(was `... f1 | f1 | f1~\> | f1\mp \breathe | fis1\p ...`, and the same shape in Tbn 2 and the bass trombone). I added a c496 comment above `codaTwoTbnOne`.

What stays: the D♭-major chord at 217–218 is still complete without the trombones. F is in Vln I (F5), Bn 2 and Cb (F2), Cbsn (F1), Vc (F3). D♭ is in Vln I–II, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and the violas. A♭ is in Bn 1 and the violas. The bass stays F in three octaves. The 205–223 dynamic path keeps its levels (ff 211, f 212, diminuendo 216, sub. p/p 219, fff 223). Only the trombones' part of the f > mp fade moves from 217–218 into 216, which is where Tpt 1–3, Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2 and the tuba already make it. At 217–218 the only brass sounding is now the Hn 1·2 echo of theme B. The arrival at 222→223, the stop chord, the chorale and the 219–222 crescendo are not touched. After the change the trombones' longest unbroken stretches are 211–216 (about 10 s) and 219–228 (about 20 s, starting p).

### 2. Trumpet 1 at C6 at 243–246 and again at 250 with no rest between (checked, no change)

Adler: above C6 is "risky" (PDF 317, note to Ex. X-45), and soft, controlled attacks in the extreme high register are "fiendishly difficult" (PDF 284, 319). Tpt 1 holds C6 with a swell (f, > then <) at 243–246 and returns to C6 at 250 after a quarter rest. The register is at the accepted limit, the dynamic stays f (no soft attack on C6), and Tpt 1 rests 251–254 before the chorale. The passage is the owed horn call in the trumpets (cyclic-notes item 8, protected 239–247). I decided to keep it.

### 3. Horns: ranges, high/low division and endurance (checked, no change)

Concert range B2–D5 lies inside Ex. X-4. Four-horn chords keep I/III high and II/IV low (Ex. X-6; c316). The longest stretches (Hn 1 139–170, Hn 4 235–264) have quarter or half rests every few bars or marked breaths (c136, c226, c489). No leap or jagged run of the kind Adler warns about (PDF 304).

### 4. Trombone slide technique and bass trombone pedal (checked, no change)

The fastest trombone figure, 205–206 (A♭3–E♭3–F3–G♭3–F3–E♭3–D♭3–C3), uses positions III–VI and never alternates VII↔I (Ex. X-71, PDF 329). The bass trombone's lowest note is B♭1 (103, one f quarter), the bottom of Ex. X-72 and a usable pedal (adler2 PDF 2).

### 5. Mutes and stopped notes (checked, no change)

Every mute change is written in score and parts: Tpt 1 con sord. for 171–186 and senza sord. at 199. Stopped horns carry + and are followed by "open" (PDF 293, 295, 308, 321). No tuba or trombone mutes are used, so the tuba's mute-change time (adler2 PDF 10) does not arise.

### 6. Effects in the reading not used (considered, declined)

Cuivré, bells up, horn and trumpet lip glissandi, horn trills (Adler lists Dvořák 8/iv, adler2 PDF 13), flutter-tonguing and jazz mutes (PDF 291–296, 308–311). None fits a place in this score without crossing a protected passage or the Brahms/Tchaikovsky/Dvořák finale manner set for this movement. No held horn note in an unprotected loud passage asks for a trill. Earlier cycles (c266, c296, c336) declined cuivré and brass trills for the same reasons.
