## Findings

### 1. 75-77: Bn 1's inner line in the E♭-major horn pad (applied)
**Problem.** Since c147, Bn 1 is the fourth real part of the pad under F2's second phrase. Its line is B♭3 (75), D3-E♭3 (76), B♭3-A♭3 (77). At 76 it drops a minor sixth to join Bn 2 on the bass, then climbs a fifth back into the pad. In that bar it is not a real part: it doubles the bass at the unison with Bn 2, and the bass note D is the leading tone of V6/5. The pad is p-mp and slurred, so the zigzag is audible in the one passage where the bassoon is meant to sit between the horns.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 80: inner voices complete the chord, smooth connections favour "a preponderance of conjunct motion", and extended repetition is "not injurious" when the outer voices are good. Points for Review 6 (PDF 89): inner voices "will contain fewer leaps and will mostly consist of stepwise motion and repeated tones." PDF 76: doubling exceptions should serve smoother voice leading. B♭ is the root of V6/5 at 76.1 and the fifth of I at 76.3, so it can be held through the bar.
**Fix (sTwo.ily).** `sTwoBsnOne = { es1\p | d2 es2 | c1 | c2. r4 | c1 | d1 | es2 c2 | d1 | bes1~ | bes1~ | bes2\< aes2 | es2\mp f4 r4 | es1 | f2 bes2\> | aes1\p | g1\< \breathe | }` (bar 76 was `d2 es2`). Bn 1 now holds B♭3 from 75 into 77 and falls to A♭3, inside Hn 1·2 at 76.3 (G3-B♭3-E♭4), as c147 and Rimsky PDF 108 require. Bn 2, the basses and the harp keep the bass D-E♭.
This changes one detail of c147 ("the bass at 76") and drops the bassoons' a2 in bar 76, which c249 listed under the protected "67-82 balance". c249 kept the unisons because the line is the bass of the texture. That reason still holds, since Bn 2 keeps the bass. Notes and dynamics elsewhere are unchanged. Applied in /tmp/critic-272/work.

### 2. 9-10: leading tone tripled in the held winds (checked, not changed)
The V7 before F1 has B5 (Fl 2), B4 (Cl 1) and B3 (Hn 3), with the root in Cl 2/Hn 1 G4 and Hn 4 G3. PDF 76 calls the ban on doubling the leading tone "virtually absolute", because the doubled tones resolve in octaves. Here every wind stops on the accented quarter at 10.3. The only one that goes on is Hn 3, and it resolves B3→C4 at 11.2. That leaves no parallel, only a bright doubling in a tutti crescendo, which is the free-texture case of PDF 84. I tried Fl 2 G5 and Cl 1 D5 on paper. That puts the fifth D in five places and leaves B only in the horn, which weakens the pull into C. I decided to keep the bars as they are.

### 3. 77→78: Bn 1 crosses below Hn 2 (checked, not changed)
Bn 1 A♭3→E♭3 passes below Hn 2's F3→G3. This is the overlap of Ex. 5-31a (PDF 89). I tested both alternatives. G3 makes octaves with Cl 1 A♭4→G4 (c149's thirds). B♭3 doubles the seventh of vi7 (PDF 76), which the tune already holds as B♭6/B♭5. The crossing involves only inner voices and lasts one bar, which PDF 88 accepts. Kept.

### 4. 107-109: the lament's alto-tenor gap (protected; not re-raised)
The violas at C4-A♭3 sit a 13th-14th below Vln II for three bars. PDF 77 (Ex. 5-6b) warns that this can sound thin or muddy. c092 kept it under the expressive exception of PDF 79, as the p start of a crescendo that the winds fill at 110. I have no new reason that answers that, so I do not re-raise it.

### 5. 278: soprano position of the final chord (follows)
Fl 1 and the upper Vln I take G6, but the root is doubled throughout (Ob 1 C6, Vln I lower C6, Tpt 1, Hn 1·3 C5, the bass in octaves), as PDF 76 requires of a final tonic. G6 is the target of the harp glissando (c249), and 263 and 223 have 1̂ on top. Follows.

### 6. 10.4→11: cello and Bn 1 D4→E♭3 at the theme's entry (follows)
The bass leaps a seventh across the joint. PDF 80 allows disjunct bass motion at phrase ends. Cl 1 picks up E♭4 at pitch on 11.1, so the D4 is heard resolving by step.
