## Findings

### 1. Bar 22.4: French sixth on ♭2 in place of the plain V4/3 to i (applied)

Problem. The P1 consequent closes 22→23 with V4/3 (bass B–D) to i. The same bass B–D to C already closes the antecedent at 14→15. The approach to the consequent's cadence repeats the antecedent's, and the chord at 22.4 is the weakest dominant form in the phrase. This is the spot where A&S (PDF 522–523, Ex. 29-37, Tchaikovsky 5/ii) show the V4/3 being inflected: the bass 2̂ falls to ♭2 under the held G, B and F, which makes a French sixth on ♭2 that resolves straight to the tonic. The text gives it as the alternative to a root-position V. That fits this movement's cadence plan, which keeps root V–I in C for 261–263. The bass D–D♭–C also echoes the falling half-step tail of the motto and of the opening German sixth (A♭–G). The chord adds a pull towards ♭2 that the bass does not otherwise have before 205.

Evidence. MIDI of v512 at 22.4 is D2 [D G B] (V4/3). In the scratch build it is D♭2 [D♭ F G B], a French sixth on ♭2, going to C2 [C E♭ G] at 23.1. The voice leading follows A&S's resolution for this chord (PDF 522): D♭→C in the bass, B→C (Hn 3, Vla), F→E♭ (Vla), G held. The melody and the F1 theme are unchanged. The recapitulation (162–163) is left as it is, so the colour belongs to the exposition only. That is not churn: no earlier critic changed or protected 22. c280's worry about "a second ♭II colour" was about chords, and here only a passing bass note is added on the last beat.

### 2. Bars 113–114: Italian sixth at the pivot (tested on paper, declined)

A&S (PDF 499–501) present ♭II as a pivot. An Italian sixth here would repeat 121–122 and change a passage that HISTORY protects (c132). c283 and c453 already declined it, and I have no new reason. Not applied.

### 3. Bars 63–64: augmented sixth after the deceptive VI (declined)

A&S (PDF 513–514) approach the aug6 from VI after a deceptive cadence. This passage accompanies the trumpet solo, which is protected (c034/c276). Adding A♭ would also make a cross relation with the solo's A♮. Not applied.

### 4. Coda pedal 263–279: ♭II neighbour over the tonic pedal (declined)

Ex. 28-20 (PDF 501–502) shows ♭II as a neighbour chord over a closing tonic pedal. In C major after 263 a D♭ would bring back the minor-mode colour after the arrival. It would also weaken the major-mode close that the plan keeps (the D♭ episode is resolved at 222→223). Not applied.

### 5. Bar 261.4: French sixth by anticipating 2̂ (declined)

Ex. 29-11 (PDF 503) removes the German sixth's fifths by anticipating 2̂. At 261 the fifths are already handled by the leap to G (Ex. 29-24, PDF 514), and the cadence 259–263 is protected. Not applied.

### 6. Bar 15.3: III-root approach to the N6 at 16 (declined)

A&S (PDF 487–489) allow III as an approach. The present IV/VI approach is already correct, and the change would gain little. Not applied.
