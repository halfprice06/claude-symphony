Arrival points by time: 113 at 168 s (40%), 138 at 203 s (48%), 151 at 222 s (53%), 223 at 329 s (78%), 263 at 397 s (94%). The secondary arrivals are 199 (291 s), 211 (308 s), 247 (375 s) and 278 (419 s).

The development and retransition have the corpus share (18–20%). Their weight comes at the right end. In analysis.txt's density profile, the retransition's V7 at 145–150 averages 23–25 sounding voices. That is more than the development climax at 109–112 (19.6) and the recapitulated F1 at 153–164 (15–18). So the development ends on the movement's first real peak, and 151 arrives from it.

The recapitulation is shorter than Dvořák's, at 55% of the exposition against his 65%. It omits P2 and the closing group, and by Unit 14 neither function is needed or possible there.
- P2 is the modulating bridge (27–42 to V of E♭). PDF 225–226 places modulation in the exposition and the return in the development, so the recapitulation needs no bridge.
- The closing group confirms the second key with authentic cadences (81–82, 93–94; PDF 219 §9). The cyclic rule forbids a root-position V–i in C before 263, so the recapitulation cannot confirm C.

This is why 171–198 is the only thematic group with no authentic cadence. The A♭ consequent at 195–198 works as the springboard of PDF 222 into 199. The coda's extra 8–10% is that withheld confirmation moved to where the rule allows it. Its material also completes the recapitulation: F1's head in major over A♭ at 205–208 is the closing group's E♭-major F1 head (83–86), recapitulated a fifth lower.

The coda's stages build: 199 → 223 → 263 → 278, each louder or broader than the one before, with the protected dip at 231–238 between the first two. 199, 211 and 223 are 17–21 s apart, and 223→247 is 46 s, including the dip. Among the arrivals listed after 113, the longest gap is 151→199 (69 s), which is the recapitulation itself.

Decision: no bars added or removed, and no section moved. Earlier form critics (c043, c063, c123, c153) reached the same conclusion on length. My reason is new: Unit 14's account of what a recapitulation has to do. Within the development the reading does find one weakness, in how the V key area hands back to C.

## Findings

### 1. 133–134: after the G-major cadence, G stays in the bass and in Vln I for three bars, and the 7th that turns it back into V of C is a pp inner viola note. Applied.

**Problem.** The development is in G from 115 to 132, a cadenced key area of 18 bars with a perfect cadence at 131–132 and 1 in the top line. The dominant then continues to 154. Bar 132 strikes G in the full orchestra, and at 133–134 the strings hold G7 diminuendo:
- cellos G3 and basses G2 hold the root;
- Vln I holds G5;
- Vln II plays D5, then B4;
- the violas' F4 is the only F.

With G in both outer voices from 132.1 to 134.4, the ear keeps G as the tonic until the diminished-seventh chain at 135. This is the "settled G" that c153 described and that HISTORY has carried as an open item since cycle 153 ("raise again only with a form-lens case that keeps the plan's G area").

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 220: when V has been held as a key area, it reverts to its function "by turning the chord into a dominant seventh by adding a minor 7th (4 in the main key)". That tone is "the one scale degree in the tonic key that does not appear in the key of the dominant", so it "helps to neutralize the temporary key".
- In the Schubert example the reading cites, the 7th is in the bass. Ex. 14-1 (image, PDF 212) has its cadence in D with a fermata at bar 10. Bar 11 enters pp on V4/2, with C♮ in the bass under the held D.
- PDF 226–227 (Ex. 14-13) lists the step "the new 'tonic' is transformed into a V" as one of the five features of a phrase group that tonicizes V.
- PDF 219 §10: the last chord of the V area has a double meaning, I of V and V of I. The fix keeps that chord (132.1) and makes the reinterpretation start on the next downbeat.

**Fix (dev.ily, applied).** The 7th goes into the bass, so G is a tonic only on 132.1.
- `devVc` 133–134: `f1\>~ | f2. r4\!` (was `g1\>~ | g2. r4\!`)
- `devCb` 133–134: `f,1\>~ | f,2. r4\!` (was `g,1\>~ | g,2. r4\!`)
- `devVla` 133–134: `d'1\>~ | d'2. r4\!` (was `f'1\>~ | f'2. r4\!`).
  - The 7th is not doubled.
  - With Vln II's B4 (c092) and Vln I's G5, the V4/2 is complete at 134 after Hn 3·4 leave.
  - The violas' D4→F4 at 135 makes a voice exchange with the bass F→D. The bass F moves to D inside dominant harmony (G7 → B–D–F–A♭).
  - The 7th stays in Vln II, the violas, Cl 2 and Hn 3 at 135, and Vln II carries it down F5–E5–E♭5–D5 into the stop chord at 138.

**Protected passage.** c153 lists "G cadence 131–134" as do-not-touch, and c003 accepted "G7 at 133–134". The stated reason is to keep the plan's cadence in G and the G area. Both are kept:
- 131–132 is unchanged, timpani G included;
- 133–134 is still G7, only inverted;
- c039's diminuendo order (horns leave at 133, the strings finish) and c092's B4 at 134 are unchanged.

This closes the open item "131–132 settled G (from c153)" without c153's deceptive cadence, which the composer declined. No quotation is affected. F1's head in the violas and Hn 2 ends on 132.1, which is unchanged, and F1, F2 and the chorale are unchanged. The comment at dev.ily's head ("133–134 G7") can read "G7 (V4/2)". finale.md needs no change.
