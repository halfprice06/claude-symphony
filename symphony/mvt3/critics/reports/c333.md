## Findings

### 1. 251-254: the second phrase of the coda's theme combination is lighter than the first. Fixed with Haydn's switch of doubling. Applied.

Problem. At 247-254 the final-combination stage puts movement I's two theme heads together. This is the owed "brass combination of the theme heads". Head 1, the eighth-note first-theme head, is in Fl 1·2 and Vln I·II at G5-A6, with Bn 1, Vc and Tbn 1·2 below. Head 2, the second-theme head in long notes, is in Tpt 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Hn 1·2 and violas at E4-E5. At 251 Tpt 1·2 drop out of head 2 (R1*4) so that they come fresh to the chorale at 255. That rest is right (reading #13). But from 251 head 2's second phrase, which has the A peak at 252 and the fall F-E-D-C, sits only in the middle of the texture, while the top octave still repeats head 1. So the stage thins in the bars that should lead into the chorale, and the top line repeats the previous four bars exactly.

Evidence. Adler PDF 288 (Ex. XVII-24, Haydn, *The Creation*): in the second phrase the flute leaves the soprano tune and "doubles the tenor two octaves higher. Devices like these add great variety to accompaniments." Head 2 is the tenor here: Hn 1·2 and violas at E4. PDF 279 (Bizet): the upper octave is added where the line reaches its climax, and head 2's peak is 252. PDF 257 and 261: in a combination, both lines need a foreground role. The score already uses this device once, when the heads are exchanged at 265→269, so here it is "used weakly" rather than absent.

Fix (finale.ily, applied):
```
finaleFluteOne/Two, 251-254 (was the first-theme head, as Vln I):
  e'''2.~( e'''8 d'''8 | c'''4 a'''2-- g'''4 | f'''4. e'''8 d'''4 c'''4 | b''2 c'''2) \breathe |
```
Fl 1·2 a2 now double head 2 an octave above Ob 1 and Cl 1·2, in the natural order of flute above oboe. Head 1 keeps its three-octave spread in Vln I·II, Bn 1, Vc and Tbn 1·2. The flutes still rest 255-256 and enter on the chorale's E6 at 257. The F6 of 250 moves by step to E6 at 251.

Protection. Earlier cycles protected "247-254 layout / combination" for the trombones' octave (c071, c091), the bowings (c186), the Tbn and timpani dynamics (c184, c315) and the three-octave head doubling (c317). All of these are unchanged. Only the flutes at 251-254 change, and no theme pitches change. The record line pHeadMajor FluteOne checks 247-248, which is untouched (42/42).

Check. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py over 245-258 gives the same output as v333 (only the known bass-trombone doubling at 257.1). The density profile changes by 0.1 voice at 249-252. finale.md: no theme or quotation changed. One note is needed: "251-254 Fl 1·2 on the second-theme head (E6), Vln I·II alone on the first-theme head at the top".

### 2. Do the coda's stages build or sag? They build. No change.

Each stage begins below where the previous one ended and rises above it: 223 fff → 231-234 mf → 235 p (12.5 voices) → 243 f → 247 ff (17.8 voices) → 255-263 f-ff-fff (23.5) → 265 ff with low brass resting (12.2) → 273 full (26.6) → 278 fff. Adler PDF 267 puts the drastic reduction straight after the tutti, which is where the two dips (235, 265) fall. PDF 268-269 asks for a transparent texture before a new entry, and that is what 231-234 does before movement I's S head. The one weak spot was the thinning inside the combination stage, now fixed (Finding 1). The proportions are unchanged: exposition 35%, development and retransition 18%, recapitulation 16%, coda 31%. c313 and c323 compared these with Dvořák 9/iv, and the reason they were kept still holds: the plan saves C major and the only root-position V–I for the coda.

### 3. 217-219, forte subito piano. Follows. No change.

Adler PDF 190 (Ex. XV-36) asks that the soft chord already be sounding when the loud one stops. At 217-218 the held chord (low brass, Bn, Cbn, Vla, Vc, Cb) fades f > mp while the tune's D♭ in Tpt 1·2, Fl, Ob and Cl stays f. At 219 only that top layer drops to p, onto a held p sonority. That is the device in substance. The bars are protected (219 sub. p → 223 fff), and I agree with keeping them. The break-offs at 94→95 and 113→114 are section ends and not a piano continuation. c243 and c328 kept them, and so do I.

### 4. Beethoven's timpani link at 18, 34 and 158. Considered and not applied.

At the antecedent's half cadence (18, and 34 and 158) the timpani play G–G–G in quarter notes, one step away from motive x (crotchet, two quavers, crotchet). Adler PDF 273 would make this the motive that joins the phrases. I am not applying it. Motive x is kept scarce: at 9, 143-145, 178 and 276-277, each time as a signal. c321 kept that number deliberately, and 178 already does exactly what Beethoven does (between F2's phrases, pp near the rim). A statement in every F1 period would make the motive routine before the retransition.
