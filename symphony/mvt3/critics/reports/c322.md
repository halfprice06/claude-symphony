## Findings

### 1. Bar 20: the consequent of F1 reaches V7 without ♯4; add VII°7 of V on beat 4 (applied)

Problem. In C minor and C major the movement never approaches its dominant by a rising half step from F♯. Every V of C is reached from A♭ (iv6, Ger6, Fr6 at 1, 5, 168, 219, 261), from D♭ (N6) or from F (iv, iiø). The rising ♯4–5 bass appears only in E♭ (73.4, 80). At 20.3–4 the consequent goes iv6/5 – iv7 – V7 (bass A♭–F–G); the antecedent at 12 goes iv – iv7 – V over the same F.

Evidence. A&S PDF 428: "Perhaps the most important function of chords applied to V is to emphasize dominant harmony … by means of a rising half-step progression in the bass (♯4–5) … most often a chromatic passing tone leading from IV, II6, or II6/5 to V." Ex. 25-12a (image) is IV – VII°7 of V – V with the C of IV held and the bass rising F–F♯–G in its key. PDF 426: these chords "significantly increase their drive to the dominant". Here the drive leads into the consequent's V7 and its A♭6 peak at 22. PDF 425 (Ex. 25-14) warns that VII°7 → V with ♭3 on top (here the tune's E♭) gives d5→P5 and doubled leading tones if all voices step down. The remedies used are those of Ex. 25-14d: Hn 4 skips A3→F3, and Bn 1 reaches G3 only on 21.2. PDF 424 (Ex. 25-3c, 25-4) allows the outer cross relation F5 (tune, 20.3) / F♯ (bass, 20.4) because the soprano moves by step and the bass carries the leading tone. A♭→A♮ is kept in one voice (Cl 1, Ex. 25-3a).

Fix (pOne.ily, bar 20 only):
```
pOneVc / pOneBsnTwo:  | bes,2 f,4 fis,4 |      % was bes,2 aes,4 f,4
pOneCb / pOneCbsn:    | bes,,2 f,,4 fis,,4 |   % was bes,,2 aes,,4 f,,4
pOneClOne:            | g'2( aes'4 a'4) |      % was g'2( es'2)
pOneVla:              | d'2( f'4 c'4) |        % was d'2( f'2)
pOneHnFour:           | r4 g4-. r4 a4-. |      % was r4 g4-. r4 f4-.
pOneBsnOne:           | r4 bes4-. r4 a4-. |    % was r4 bes4-. r4 aes4-.
```
20.3 is now iv (F, A♭ in Cl 1, C in the violas, F in the tune). 20.4 is F♯–A–C–E♭: F♯ only in the bass line; A in Cl 1, Bn 1 and Hn 4; C in Hn 3 and violas; E♭ only in the tune. The tune's E♭ is still a seventh falling to D, which keeps c257's aim, and the consequent still differs from the antecedent's bar 12. This changes c257's bass E♭–D–C–B♭–A♭–F–G to E♭–D–C–B♭–F–F♯–G; c257's 20.3 iv6/5 gives way to iv. A first try with the violas D4–C4 made fifths with Hn 1·2 (G3–F3); p5.py caught them, and the line is now D4–F4–C4. The recapitulation's 160 is protected and stays diatonic. A different approach there is acceptable variety.

### 2. Bar 36: the 5–6 link from A♭ to B♭ minor gets a chromatic bass, V6/5 of B♭ minor on beat 4 (applied)

Problem. c212 made 36.4 a diatonic 5–6 link: E♭→F over the A♭ bass, F minor/A♭, into B♭ minor at 37. The step from the A♭ statement of F1's head to the B♭-minor statement therefore has no leading tone. B♭ minor at 37 arrives from its minor dominant.

Evidence. A&S PDF 435 (§17, Ex. 25-32b–c, image): "Stepwise sequential passages based on the ascending 5–6 progression are frequently intensified by chromatic passing tones that produce applied chords … ascending chromatic lines … produce the strongest effect when they are in the bass." Ex. 25-32c is exactly 5 – 6/5 with the bass rising by semitone. PDF 406 (Ex. 24-10) says the 7th of each 6/5 is best prepared, and PDF 400 shows a common-tone preparation. Here Ob 2 holds E♭5 from the A♭ chord into the 6/5 and falls to D♭5 at 37. PDF 419 and 439 (point 3): do not double the temporary leading tone or the 7th. A♮ is only in the bass line and E♭ only in Ob 2. The bass's A♭→A♮ is the chromatic step in one voice. The other A♭s at 36.3 are doublings of that bass (PDF 402, unobjectionable). The tune's D♭5 at 36.3 is the delayed 7th of 36's E♭7 (PDF 408–409). It still resolves to C5 on 36.4, now the 5th of F.

Fix (pTwo.ily, bar 36 only):
```
pTwoCb / pTwoCbsn:           | es,2 aes,,4 a,,4 |   % was es,2 aes,,2
pTwoBsnTwo / pTwoBassTbn:    | es2 aes,4 a,4 |      % was es2 aes,2
pTwoOboeTwo:                 | g'2 es''2 |          % was g'2 aes'2
pTwoClOne:                   | g'2 aes'4 f'4~ | f'1 |
pTwoHnOne:                   | g'2 aes'4 f'4 |
pTwoVla / pTwoVc / pTwoBsnOne: c'8( f'8 es'8 c'8)  % 36.3-4, was c'8( es'8 aes'8 c'8)
```
c212's 5–6 rise E♭→F in Ob 1 and Tbn 1 is kept: the 6 is now the root of the applied 6/5. My first countersubject (C–E♭–F–C) made octaves E♭→F with Ob 1 and Tbn 1, and allpairs_par.py caught them. C–F–E♭–C now runs in contrary motion to them, a plain voice exchange (PDF 421). F1's notes and the timpani are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 82: deceptive cadence on VII°7 of V (considered, not applied)

PDF 427 (Ex. 25-16) would turn F2's cadence 81→82 into V7 – VII°7/V (bass B♭→A♮) – V – I at 83. I decided against it for two reasons. First, A♮→B♭ already occurs at 80, so the same bass gesture would come twice within three bars. Second, 82's rising arpeggio belongs to F2's tail and the animando (c298), so it would have to change. No change.

### 4. ♮VII7 → V6/5 (PDF 410–411, Ex. 24-18): no free place (decided: no change)

The only unprotected dominant prolongation of the type, 21–22 (V7 – V6/5), carries the tune's B♮5 at 21.3, so B♭7 would make a soprano cross relation. 39–42, 147–150 and 169–170 are protected, the last because c242's French sixth must go to V.
