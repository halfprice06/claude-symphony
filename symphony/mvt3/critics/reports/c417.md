## Findings

### 1. 255–258: violas in thirds and sixths with Vln II (Jupiter coupling). Tested, not applied

Problem examined: Adler PDF 176 describes how Mozart strengthens the third theme as the texture thickens by putting the second violins and violas in sixths (Jupiter mm. 383–84, 387–88). This score does not use that technique anywhere. The best place for it is F2's last phrase under the chorale at 255–258. There the upper octave is Vln II alone (G4, sul G; c371, c376), and the violas are a third part on the lower octave with the cellos and Hn 3·4 (c327). PDF 184 asks for each fragment to go to an instrument that will be heard, and Vln II's G4 starts on the trumpets' own chorale note.

Candidate tested in /tmp/critic-417/work (saved as /tmp/critic-417/finale-candidateA.ily, PDF /tmp/critic-417/score-candidateA.pdf):

```
% finaleVla 255–258, was: g2.->(\ff^\markup \italic "espress." e4) | d4( c4 e4 g4) | c'2.( d'4) | g'4.( f'8 e'4 d'4)
e'2.->(\ff^\markup \italic "espress." g4) | b4( a4 g4 e'4) | e'2.( b'4) | e''4.( d''8 c''4 f'4)
```

Thirds and sixths under Vln II run throughout, and sixths above the cellos from 257. The line avoids octaves with the bass at 256.4→257.1 and 258.4→259.1, and it avoids moving in unison with Hn 2's C4→B3 at 256.1. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels; par8.py at 253–260 shows only the same bass doublings at 259.1 as v417.

Verdict: not better, so the score is unchanged.
- The all-pairs check (critic-scripts/c002/par.py) finds a new fifth at 258.1, Cl 1 E4→A4 (the last eighth of its arpeggio) against the violas' B4→E5.
- The coupled voice sits at E4–C4–B3 in 255–257. Hn 1·2 hold E4/C4–B3 there, Tpt 3 has C4/E4 and Tbn 1 has E4, so the thirds are not heard as thirds.
- To avoid the forbidden parallels, the line needs leaps (G3→E4, E4→B4, C5→F4) where the unison line was the theme itself.
- The other version I tried at 258, sixths below Vln II (B4–A4–G4–F4), puts B4 against Ob 2's C5 on the downbeat over A minor.
- It also gives up c327's viola-cello unison without a gain. Mozart's coupling works because his sixths have a register of their own, and here the coupling falls inside the held brass.

### 2. 247–254 and 255–258: Hauptstimme balance and doubling distance (PDF 184). Checked, no change

Movement I's head is doubled three octaves apart at 247–254: Fl and Vln I·II at G5–E6, Bn, Tbn and Vc at G2–E3, with nothing in octaves 3–4. Schoenberg's rule (PDF 184) allows doublings only at the unison or the octave. The gap is where the S head sits (Ob, Cl, Hn 1·2, Tpt 1·2, Vla at E4–E5). Giving each of two equal elements its own register is the Meistersinger method on PDF 177–178, where three elements are kept clear by colour and register. c317 kept this layout for the same reason, and c049/c371 protect it. At 255–258 the chorale and F2 are both ff. The chorale is the main voice, but it is held in two trumpets and the Vln I tremolo while F2 moves, so both can be heard. Changing F2 to f would reverse c327's reason (the line was covered) for no new gain. Decided: no change.

### 3. 247–254: character words for each element (PDF 177–178). Considered, not applied

Wagner writes a word for each element ("p (ausdrucksvoll)", "mf (sehr markiert)", "p scherzando"). At 247 the two heads already have different notated articulation: movement I's head has accents, short slurs and staccato eighths, and the S head has long slurs and tenuto. Tpt 3 and the timpani have accented quarter-eighth strokes at f. Words such as "marc." on the head and "sostenuto" on the S head would repeat what the notes already say, and they would crowd R's "Più allegro" line. Decided: no change.

### 4. 259–263: timpani rolls under the combined chorale (PDF 184, Bloch). Checked, no change

Bloch marks his timpani and bass-drum rolls p, then crescendo, so that the combined themes are not covered. Here the A♭ roll starts f with a crescendo at 259 under a ff tutti (brass, woodwinds and strings all ff from 259), and the G roll follows to the fff at 263. A roll starting at f under ff does not cover the arpeggios, and starting it at p would add nothing. 245–246 already starts p. Decided: no change.
