## Findings

### 1. Bar 17: replace the root-position ii°7 under the antecedent's half cadence with ♯IV7, the diminished-third chord (applied)

Problem. F1's antecedent arrives at its half cadence (18) through iv (17.1-2) and a root-position ii°7 on D (17.3-4: D-F-A♭-C under the tune's A♭5), so the bass runs F-D-G. c112 raised the root-position diminished triad at 17.3 as an open point and it was closed with no change. The score never uses ♯IV7, the one chord family in Unit 29 that it lacks, and this is the place the reading names for it: a subdominant leading to a cadential 6/4.

Evidence. A&S PDF 528-529 (Unit 29 sec. 19): ♯IV7 "contains the same scale degrees as the German 6/5"; "like the augmented 6th, it results from the use of ♯4 as a chromatic passing tone, only now in the bass"; Ex. 29-31a (seen as image) is exactly iv - ♯IV7 - V6/4-5/3 in D minor with the bass G-G♯-A. PDF 530: ♯IV7 "can replace a diatonic subdominant before a cadential V" (Ex. 29-32) and occurs "as neighbor to V (or the cadential 6/4)". PDF 528-529: the diminished third contracts to an octave. PDF 505: in minor the German-sixth sonority shares two common tones (1̂, ♭3̂) with the cadential 6/4, so C and E♭ hold into 18. The ii°7 it replaces is the "diatonic preparation" kind of PDF 509, which A&S treat as weaker than the chromatic form.

Why here and not elsewhere. The tune holds A♭5 (♭6̂) over 17.3-4. A German sixth would put A♭ in the bass under the same A♭ and give octaves into G; VII°7 of V (as at 20.4, c322) needs A♮ and clashes with the tune. ♯IV7 keeps the tune's A♭ as the chord's ♭6 and puts ♯4 in the bass, so the bass F♯ rises and the tune's A♭ falls into the octave G: the contraction A&S describe. The bass F-F♯-G at 17→18 now matches the consequent's F-F♯-G at 20→21 (c322), with the diminished-third form in the antecedent (A♭ kept) and the dominant-of-V form in the consequent (A♮), so the two halves rhyme without repeating the chord.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-332/work/sections/pOne.ily, bar 17 = pOne bar 7, with a "% c332" comment above "% FIRST THEME"):

```
pOneVc:     | f,2 d2 |                        ->  | f,2 fis,2 |
pOneCb:     | f,,2 d,2 |                      ->  | f,,2 fis,,2 |
pOneBsnTwo: | f,2 d2 |                        ->  | f,2 fis,2 |
pOneClOne:  | f'8( aes' c'' aes') d'8( f' aes' f') |  ->  | f'8( aes' c'' aes') c'8( es' aes' es') |
pOneClTwo:  | aes8( c' f' c') aes8( c' d' c') |      ->  | aes8( c' f' c') aes8( c' es' c') |
pOneHnFour: | r4 f4-. r4 f4-. |               ->  | r4 f4-. r4 es4-. |
```

Sounding result (MIDI slice): 17.1 F-A♭-C; 17.3 F♯1/F♯2 in the bass with A♭ (tune A♭5, violas and Hn 1·2 A♭4, Bn 1 A♭3), C (Cl, Hn 3 C4) and E♭ (Cl, Hn 4 E♭3); 18.1 the cadential 6/4 G-C-E♭ of c282, then V. Cl 2's last eighth is still C4 and Cl 1's is E♭4, so c282's held 6/4 tones are unchanged (Cl 1 now holds E♭ as a common tone instead of stepping F-E♭). No F remains above the F♯ bass on 17.3-4. F1's notes, rhythm, slurs and dynamics are unchanged; no quotation changes; theme record 42/42.

What was kept. 157 in the recapitulation keeps the ii°7 (c322 left the recapitulation's 160 alone for the same reason: the recapitulation varies the exposition elsewhere, with the N6 at 163). 16 keeps its N6 → ii°7 (the cross relation D♭-D♮ is between voices, which A&S PDF 484 allows). The tune, the 6/4 at 18 and the timpani are untouched.

### 2. Bar 23: a French sixth before the consequent's cadence (not applied)

Problem. 23.3-4 is ii°6 (F-A♭-D under the tune's D6-C6) going to V at 24. A&S PDF 504 derive the French 4/3 from II4/3 with ♯4, and PDF 506-507 call it the best neighbour of V. A♭-C-D-F♯ on 23.4 would carry both tune notes.

Decision. Not applied. With Finding 1 the theme would have F♯ in the bass at 17.3, 20.4 and 23.4, three chromatic approaches to G in ten bars; the deceptive cadence at 25-26 needs the plain approach to stay the surprise. The movement already has a French sixth at 168 (c242).

### 3. Bars 47-48: French sixth as a neighbour over the B♭ pedal (not applied)

Problem. A&S PDF 513 show augmented sixths, the French 4/3 first, as neighbours of a held V. Over the transition's B♭ pedal, 47-48 already sound C♭ (violas) and G♭ (Vln II) with E♭ and the held A♭3; with A♮ for A♭ and F for G♭ this would be C♭-E♭-F-A♮, a Fr6 of E♭ minor around the pedal.

Decision. Not applied. The held A♭3 (Hn 4 since c329) is V7's seventh and must fall to G♭ at 51 under the trumpet's first note; replacing it with A♮ for two bars would make it rise to B♭ instead, and the violas' ♮6-♭6-5 and Vln II's G♭-F (v163) were designed to bring the solo's E♭-minor tones into the dominant. The passage already does what A&S's neighbour chord does (♭6 above 5) without the augmented sixth.

### 4. Chromatic spelling and the existing aug6/N6 chords (no change)

Checked against PDF 496-499 and 503-516: C♭ in the violas at 47-49 and 198 is a lowered upper neighbour spelled as a minor second; G♭ in Hn 2 at 207-210 becomes F♯ at 219 when it rises, as the rule for raised notes going up asks; D♯ at 219 goes to the major 6/4 (PDF 507); the new F♯ at 17 rises to G. The Italian sixth at 121 doubles its third (G in Vln II and violas), the German sixth at 109 goes through the cadential 6/4, the one at 261 goes straight to a V7 whose seventh F is in an inner voice under B on top (PDF 525). The N6s at 16, 156 and 163 double the bass F or the tune at the octave only. Nothing to change.
