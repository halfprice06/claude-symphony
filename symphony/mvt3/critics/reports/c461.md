## Findings

### 1. 212-214: theme B has no string octave at its own pitch (applied)

**Problem.** Movement II's theme B is recalled once (211-218, ff, Largamente). The tune is in Tpt 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 at A♭4-B♭5, with Tpt 2, Ob 2 and Cl 2 in thirds below, and Fl 1·2 an octave higher. In the strings only Vln I has it, and only from 212, at F6-B♭6 on the top of the E string. Vln II plays an inner line in the tune's octave (F5-D♭5 | D♭5-G♭5-F5 | E♭5-D♭5), mostly a third or fourth below. So the tune has no string body in its main octave. This is the recalled theme that should be most recognisable in the coda, and here it sounds as a trumpet-and-reed line with a thin violin edge on top.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 58-59: Vln I and II in octaves is "a very common process used for all kinds of melodic figures, in particular those in the very high register"; the E string "diminishes in fulness of tone the higher it ascends", and very high violin lines "become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves. Such doubling secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre." PDF 62 Note I says the same of melodies at the top of the range. Ex. 23 (PDF 199, Saltan) shows the cantabile in Vln I and II an octave apart. PDF 57 adds that violins doubling a woodwind line keep the string quality on top. PDF 63 (Ex. 34) says thirds in a melody should stay in one tone colour, so I kept Vln II's line below the tune rather than dropping it. c051 and c054 removed a near-copy of the tune from Vln I and left "Vln II keeps its thirds below"; this finding keeps that line and does not undo either change.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily, applied in /tmp/critic-461/work).** Vln II divides at 212-214. The upper half plays the tune at Tpt 1's pitch, an octave under Vln I and the flutes. The lower half keeps the existing line. "unis." returns at 215:

```

```

Theme B's pitches (Tpt 1, record line), Vln I, the dynamics, the breath after 214, 215-222 and the protected D♭-C-B-C line are unchanged. The tune's notes already exist in Tpt 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1, so the change adds no new interval against the bass: par8.py on 210-216 prints the same lines as v461 (only the existing octave doublings of the bass), and analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. MIDI: both Vln II voices sound at the staff's levels (velocity 73-82 for the notes, 83-91 for the dynamics, as before). Engraving: two voices on the Vln II staff on page 12. The unison F5 on 212.1 prints as two heads, which is standard. Page 12 is not crowded and the score is still 16 pages. A first try with hidden dynamics in the lower voice caused four "conflict with event" warnings. I removed them because the staff's dynamics already apply to both voices.

**For the composer and the other threads:** this changes the doubling of a quotation (finale.md item 15, movement II's theme B), not its pitches. Add to item 15: "since v462 Vln II div. at 212-214, upper half on the tune at Tpt 1's pitch, lower half on the inner line."

### 2. 159-164: Vln I divided in octaves on F1's recapitulated consequent (checked, no change)

PDF 59-60 (b) warns that violins divided in octaves lose resonance. It allows the layout with woodwind doubling and in a high register. Both conditions hold: Fl 1·2 double the upper octave (C6-A♭6), Ob 1 doubles the lower, and Vln II plays its espressivo counter. c191 reached the same verdict. No change.

### 3. 27-34: F1 in the middle-low register under the violins' countersubject (checked, no change)

PDF 54 says a low melody stands out "to a less degree" than a high one, and that doubling and choice of timbre can make up for it. Here violas and cellos play the tune in unison at C4 (PDF 57: the cellos predominate), with Bn 1·2 and Hn 3·4, and the basses, bass clarinet and contrabassoon double it in lower octaves (PDF 61 f). This is the reading's remedy. No change.

### 4. 41-42 and 169-170: layout (h), Vln I+II in octaves with violas+cellos (considered, not applied)

PDF 61 (h) is the only string layout in the reading that the score never uses on a line. The one place where it could apply is F1's last fragment at 41-42 (violins in unison on F4, cellos an octave lower), with the violas joining the cellos. I did not build it. The violas hold A♭3 at 41, the seventh of V7 over the B♭ pedal; without it A♭ is left only in Fl 1. The passage also fades to p before the transition, where more weight is unwanted, and Tbn 1 already doubles the fragment (c431). It would not be better. At 169-170 the violas carry the sul G descent below the violins, so the same reasoning applies.

### 5. 235-242: the S head, unison then octaves (checked, no change)

235-238 put Vln I+II in unison sul A, p, with no woodwind on the tune (PDF 57; Ex. 12, PDF 186: unison violins on one string for a cantabile). 239-242 split the violins into octaves (E6/E5) with Fl 1 and Ob 1 (PDF 58-59). The tune grows from unison to octaves just as the movement I phrase rises, and this is the most hummable eight bars of the coda. No change. The layout is protected (c017).

### 6. 123-127: the G-major F2 handed from cellos to violas to Cl 1 (checked, no change)

PDF 55 says viola melodies are "generally doubled", but also that the viola suits "short characteristic phrases". The violas' A4-G4-F♯4-E4-D4 (125-126) is a two-bar link in a protected hand-over, p, sul D, over harp and held horn.
