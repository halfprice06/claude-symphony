## Findings

### 1. 231-234: the violins play through the plagal close and then carry the second-theme head, so the coda's third stage (235) starts in a colour that has sounded for 36 bars. Applied.

Problem. Every other theme entry in the movement comes in a colour that has rested or changed: the trumpet at 51 after 50 bars' rest, the violins at 67 after pizzicato, the violins at 187 after 16 bars' rest (171-186), theme B's trumpets at 211 after 205-210, the horn call's trumpets at 243 after 235-242, the chorale's trumpets at 255 after 251-254. The exception is movement I's second-theme head at 235. Vln I has played every bar from 199 (hammered tutti, theme B, the arrival's tremolo, the canon to 230), and at 231-234 it only doubles Fl 1 at pitch (A5-G5-A♭5-G5), a line that Tpt 1 and Hn 3 already carry an octave lower, then plays the pickup G4 into 235 at the same level.

Evidence. Adler PDF 263-264: composers introduce the string soloist with "a distinctly contrasting color combination", "the solo colour is therefore very fresh"; PDF 267: Mozart keeps the orchestral horns and flutes out of the texture around his horn and flute soloists; PDF 268-269: the solo enters into a sparse texture, "the background does not intrude at all". The score's own recapitulation uses this at 171-187. The coda's stages (arrival, chorale, S head, combination) are articulated by tempo and dynamics; a rested colour at 235 also marks the stage change by timbre, the device Adler says "aids in a clearer exposition of the form" (PDF 258).
