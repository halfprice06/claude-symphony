## Findings

### 1. F2 has no rhythmic feature of its own before its last bars: add a tied anticipation at the half cadence (73-74, 193-194). APPLIED. This changes the finale's own theme F2.

**Bars:** 73-74 (Vln I, Vln II, E♭ major) and 193-194 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Ob 1, Hn 1, A♭ major).

**Problem:** In every statement of F2 (51-66, 67-82, 123-130, 187-198), each note starts on a beat and every long note starts on a downbeat. The rhythm uses four patterns: dotted half + quarter, four quarters, half + two quarters, two halves. Each is a pattern of A&S PDF 51's "simplest and most natural" kind. The contour is good and the peak at 78 is strong. But rhythmically nothing in the first eight bars belongs only to this tune, and a listener remembers a tune partly by such a feature. Bars 73-74 make it worse: the half cadence, the goal of the antecedent (PDF 54), arrives on a plain whole note after two plain halves. The only syncopation in the theme is the suspension at 80-81, added by c241.

**Evidence:** PDF 51: a note held from a weak beat through a strong one conflicts with the metre. PDF 56 (Ex. 3-19 image): syncopation also produces the unaccented dissonance. A tone shifted to start before the strong beat is an anticipation, either tied over into the consonance or struck again, most often in the top part. PDF 54-55: the suspension is the accented counterpart. The consequent already ends with a suspension (G held into 81 over V7, resolving to F). With an anticipation the antecedent's cadence would push ahead of the beat, and the consequent's cadence would hold behind it. This uses both syncopation-derived dissonances from PDF 55-56, one in each half. Beethoven 9/iv 103-104 has the same figure, a stepwise rise in the top voice with the new note tied over the bar line one beat early, in the one finale tune that every listener can hum. Its rhythm is otherwise as square as F2's.

At 73 the harmony is I then vi (bass E♭ → C; harp E♭-G-C on beats 3-4), and at 74 it is V then V4/2 (bass B♭ → A♭). The anticipated F5 on 73.4 is a fourth above the bass C, a dissonance, and becomes the fifth of V on 74.1. That is the case in Ex. 3-19. The recapitulation is the same a third lower: B♭4 on 193.4 over F (vi), into V at 194, where Cl 1's F♭4→E♭4 (c210) still sounds under it.
