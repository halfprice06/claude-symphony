## What else I studied

I read HISTORY.md and the reports of the earlier Unit 21 passes (c198, c298, c398, c498) so as not to repeat them. c498's search for unmarked suspensions only looked at notes tied across the bar line. This time I wrote a detector, /tmp/critic-598/tools/susp.py (half-bar level) and susp4.py (quarter level). It reads the MIDI and reports every note held or restruck across a strong beat that is dissonant against the lowest sounding note and falls by step to a consonance on a weaker beat. On v598 it finds 22 places: 25, 49, 78, 81, 82, 87–89, 108–109, 112, 121, 165, 200–201, 204, 211, 214–215, 237, 244, 248, 250 and 258. I checked the source of each one. All are shaped already, protected, or not real suspensions (78 and 237 are accented passing or chord tones, 258 is a dotted figure), except 121. I also looked again at the dynamics A&S prints in Ex. 21-17 (Mozart K. 378) and Ex. 21-37 (Beethoven Op. 126/3).

## Findings

### 1. Bar 121: Cl 1's 7–6 into the G-minor half cadence has no shaping (applied)
- **Problem.** At 121 the bassoon solo reaches the Italian sixth E♭–G–C♯ before the Phrygian half cadence on D (c152, c233). Cl 1 enters pp on D6 on beat 2, over the passing F of the bass, where D is consonant. On beat 3 the bass moves to E♭ and D6 becomes a major 7th. On beat 4 it falls to C♯6, the augmented sixth, and then returns to D6 at 122. c323 added this line to make the Italian sixth audible as a 7–6. It is the only dissonant suspension in the score outside a protected passage that has no expressive marking, because it lies inside a single half note and no tie crosses the bar line. Played flat at pp under one slur, the moment D becomes the 7th, which is the point of the line, passes unmarked.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 350 sec. 4: suspensions bring their voice into prominence, and "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer". PDF 352–353 (Ex. 21-9) and PDF 368 (Ex. 21-39, Haydn): the 7–6 over a moving bass is the standard figure, and the suspension turns parallel motion into oblique motion. PDF 347 Ex. 21-3b–d shows that the preparation may come on a weak part of the bar, as it does here on beat 2. The score already shapes its other suspensions this way (49, 81, 211, 227–229, 237, 241). PDF 352 sec. 12: no part sounds the resolution tone C♯ before beat 4. Vln II's pizzicato C♯5 arrives with the clarinet's C♯6, an octave lower.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-598/work/sections/dev.ily, devClOne):**
  `r4 \after 4 \> d'''2(\pp\< cis'''4-\tweak stencil ##f \pp | d'''4.) r8 r2`
  (was `r4 d'''2(\pp cis'''4 | d'''4.) r8 r2`). The D6 swells into beat 3 and recedes on the C♯. The hidden pp ends the hairpin. Without it, LilyPond's MIDI played the C♯ and D louder (velocity 29 → 37), the opposite of what the marking asks for. With it, the MIDI note events are identical to v598. Notes, slur and the printed pp are unchanged, and so are the Italian sixth (do-not-touch since c152) and the bassoon solo. Cl 2 rests, so the shared staff has no unequal dynamics. In the engraving the swell prints as a small `<>` after the pp, under the D6.

### 2. Bar 82.3: Hn 1's E♭→D 4–3 over B♭ under the animando crescendo (checked, no change)

No change.
