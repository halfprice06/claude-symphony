## Findings

### 1. 39-42: the diminuendo ff > p on the V7 of E♭ is left to the players, and the violins' falling F1 fragment is covered by unisons (applied)

**Problem.** The ff half cadence on V of E♭ (39) dies away to the transition's pp (43), but the scoring does nothing to make it die away. In 39-40 sixteen wind parts hold the chord: Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Bn 1·2, Hn 1-4, Tbn 1·2, the bass trombone and the contrabassoon, together with the violas, cellos and basses. Only Tbn 1·2 and the contrabassoon stop after 40. In 41-42 thirteen winds are still holding, and each player has to make the diminuendo alone. Then at 43 the texture falls at once to five pp parts (Bn 1, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb) and the flute's motive x. Many of the held notes are unison doubles:
- Hn 3·4 double Hn 1·2 at the unison (F4, B♭3).
- Ob 1 doubles Fl 2 (D5).
- Cl 1 doubles Hn 1 and Hn 3 (F4).
- Cl 2 doubles Hn 2 and Hn 4 (B♭3).

At 41-42 the violins fall F4-D4-E♭4-F4, the end of F1's fragment. That puts the line on the same F4 that Cl 1, Hn 1 and Hn 3 are holding, so the line is lost inside the chord.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 201, consideration 3: a diminuendo "is made clearer when actually written out in an orchestral score". PDF 213 calls Dvořák's m. 33 (Ex. XVI-10, PDF 212) "a model of exactly how one accomplishes a diminuendo from fortissimo to piano": every part sits in a controllable range, and the strings drop out to give a bridge colour into the next section. Bizet's opening (Ex. XVI-6, PDF 202; PDF 213) has the diminuendo "clearly orchestrated, instead of being left to the players or conductor": the violins stop, then the cellos, and the violas are left pizz. pp. Ravel (PDF 224) also orchestrates his diminuendo by letting the instruments "descend and drop out". PDF 229 (Schoenberg, Op. 25): doubling at pitch, two colours "swallow each other up". PDF 203: study the layers and rank them. Here the violins' line ranks first and the doubled held notes rank last. The score already uses this method at 167-170 (protected).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-237/work/sections/pTwo.ily).** The pitches are unchanged and Fl 1's A♭5 at 39-42 is untouched (c201, c223). The chord thins in two steps:
- **After 40** (with Tbn 1·2 and the contrabassoon): Hn 3·4, Ob 1 and the bass trombone stop.
- **After 41**: Ob 2, Cl 1, Cl 2 and Hn 1 stop.
- **At 42** the chord is Fl 1 A♭5, Fl 2 D5, violins F4, Bn 1 D4, Hn 2 B♭3, Vla F3, Bn 2 and Vc B♭2, Cb B♭1. That is complete, with no unison on the violins' F4. Except for Fl 2 and Hn 2, it is already the transition's pp group (Bn 1, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb), so 42→43 dovetails as Ravel's backgrounds do (PDF 217).
