## Findings

### 1. Open D strings in the two p fades of F1 before F2 (41–42 and 169.4–170). Applied in scratch.

**Problem.** Both places close a statement of F1's music, fading to p, just before a solo statement of F2: the trumpet at 51 after the transition, and the cor anglais at 171.

- At 41–42 the violins (Vln I and Vln II in unison) play F4–D4.–E♭4–F4. The cellos play the same line an octave lower, ff > p. The dotted D4 is the open D string in the violins, and D3 is the open D string in the cellos.
- At 169.4–170 Vln I's falling line ends G4–F4–D4, and the D4 is held a whole bar at p. The violas hold B3–D4. Vln II holds its open G3.

Played the natural way, the bar is G3–D4–D4 on three open strings: an open fifth with nothing stopped in it, at the one point where the music has gone quiet.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 73–74: open strings "have a greater vibrating potential, but are not under the controlling influence provided by the finger". In soft, expressive passages "one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture", and an open string "stands out peculiarly". PDF 73 makes timbre the one reason to name a string. PDF 76 says how to mark it. PDF 74 and Ex. III-5 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, sul G) describe the G string as the thickest, most sonorous string. PDF 99: the cello's G string is its weakest, which suits a fade.

Neither place has a stopped unison that would cover the open string. That is the difference from c301's case at 67, where the cellos' stopped G3 and Hn 1 cover the violas' open G. The open strings here are not cover for anything either: the fade is the melody.

Each passage fits one position:
- violins at 41–42: 3rd position on G (C4, D4, E♭4, F4);
- cellos at 41–42: 4th position on G (D3 with the 1st finger, F3 with the 4th);
- Vln I at 169.4–170: 4th position on G (D4 to G4);
- violas at 169–170: B3 and D4 in 1st position on G.

Vln II's G3 at 170 is its lowest note and stays open (PDF 74: "unavoidable").

**Fix (applied).** In pTwo.ily, Vln I, Vln II and Vc at bar 41:
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" f'2(\startTextSpan d'4. es'8 | f'2.)\p\stopTextSpan r4 | }
```
The cellos get the same with `f2( d4. es8 | f2.)`.

In recP.ily, Vln I at 169.4:
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" g'(\startTextSpan f' | d'1)\p\stopTextSpan
```
Violas at 169:
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" b1\startTextSpan | d'2\p\stopTextSpan
```
Each file has a c391 comment line above it. No notes, slurs, dynamics or MIDI change.

The 167–170 protection (c214) covers the order of the diminuendo, and this fix does not touch it. No theme notes change: F1, F2, the chorale top lines and all quotations are unchanged, so the other movement threads need no notice.

### 2. The viola as the bass of the string section (Ex. III-36) is the one technique in the chapter the score never uses. Not applied.

**Problem.** A MIDI scan finds no bar in 1–279 where the violas sound without the cellos or basses. Mendelssohn's light texture (violins over a viola bass, PDF 89–90) is absent.

**Decision: no change.** Every place where a light string texture over a viola bass could stand is either protected or carries a pedal that defines the passage:
- 43–50: the B♭ pedal into 51;
- 51–66: the pizz. bass of the trumpet solo;
- 115–122: the bassoon solo's accompaniment;
- 67–82: balance (c211);
- 171–186.

In a con fuoco finale, the passages without a pedal (19–26, 83–90, 131–134) need the real bass. Writing one in would also undo c307 at 3–4, where the cellos were added an octave under the violas for exactly that reason.

### 3. F2's first violin statement (67–70) starts in octaves, while Brahms's model tune (Ex. III-4) starts in one low octave. Considered, not applied.

**Problem.** Adler's Ex. III-4 is Brahms 1/iv's big tune in the violins' lowest, most sonorous octave. At 67, F2 enters in Vln I (B♭5–F6, E string) over Vln II (B♭4–F5).

**Decision: no change.** Putting Vln I in unison with Vln II for 67–70 would leave the counter-melody (violas, cellos and Hn 1, G3–G4) a third or less under the tune. At 70 the tune's C5–B♭4 would sit right on the counter's E♭4–F4–G4. Vln I's upper octave is what keeps the tune clear. This is the balance c094, c098 and c121 settled and c211 protected. The upper octave also leaves Vln II's lower octave in the register Brahms uses.

### 4. Vln II's D4 at 46.3–4 (pp, transition). Considered, not applied.

**Problem.** Vln II holds F4 at 43–46, then D4 for two beats, then G♭4 at 47. The D4 is likely the open string.

**Decision: no change.** This is a sustained inner chord tone, not a melodic line (PDF 73 is about expressive lines). To mark it sul G would mean putting all eight bars on the G string. That would darken the pp layer that c264 made a single spectrum under Vln I's sul tasto harmonic. PDF 73 leaves this to the player.
