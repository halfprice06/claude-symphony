## Findings

Ranked by how much each bears on the melody. None is applied, because none clears the bar of "prefer no change to a marginal one" without disturbing a protected passage.

### 1. 247-254: P head three octaves apart (E6 and E3), with the middle octave given to the S head. Checked; no change.
- **What I checked.** Rimsky PDF 59 says high violin figures "become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves". PDF 63 says three-octave layouts are "very seldom found, and as a rule, only when supported by wind instruments". Here the P head is in Vln I+II and Fl 1·2 at E6-A6, and in Vc, Bn 1 and Tbn 1·2 at E3. The S head fills E4-E5 (Ob, Cl, Vla, Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2).
- **Why it holds.** The layout meets PDF 63's condition: the lower octave is brass and bassoon, and the upper is doubled by both flutes. Because the octave between is left to the other theme, the two heads sit in separate registers (cycle 24) and never meet in octaves (cycle 91). In Antar Ex. 32 (PDF 210) Rimsky builds the same kind of layout, with the strings in octaves over woodwind and brass chords.
- **Alternative rejected.** I considered Vln II on the P head at E5 (`r8 g'8( e''4.->) …`). It would start on the S head's own E5 at 247 and 251, and it would undo cycle 49 (Vln II up) inside a passage protected since cycle 177. Decision: no change.

### 2. 187-198: F2's major return sits a fourth lower than the exposition's statement and is in unison, not octaves. Checked; no change.
- **What I checked.** At 67-78 F2 is in octaves (Vln I B♭5 over Vln II B♭4) and reaches F6 at 69 and B♭6 at 78. At 187-196 it is Vln I+II in unison at E♭5, peaking at B♭5 (189), with violas and Hn 1 an octave lower and no upper woodwind until 197.
- **Why it holds.** Rimsky PDF 60 names this layout (Vln I+II over Vla in octaves) as the one "used to obtain greater brilliance in the upper part". PDF 57 says unison violins gain "power and richness". The lower peak follows from the A♭ key, which cycle 73 chose so that no C-major area comes before 223. The statement then climbs in octaves to E♭6/E♭5 at 198 into the tutti, which is the climax the plan wants here.
- **Alternatives rejected.** An octave split or a flute doubling at 187 would reach the high register before 198 and blunt that rise. It would also change the protected 187-198 bow plan. Decision: no change.

### 3. 159-164: Vln I divisi in octaves on F1's consequent. Checked; no change.
- **What I checked.** Rimsky PDF 59-60 (b) says violins divided in octaves "deprive the melody of resonance". It allows them "when the strings are doubled by the wood-wind, and when the melody falls in a sufficiently high register". Both conditions hold here: Fl 1·2 double the upper half (C6-A♭6) and Ob 1 the lower half, while Vln II plays the counter-line (cycles 107, 109, 167).
- **Decision.** No change. This passage is protected by cycle 164.

### 4. 27-34: F1 in the bass under unison violin eighths, ff. Checked; no change.
- **What I checked.** Rimsky PDF 54 says a low melody stands out "to a less degree" than a high one. Here the bass statement has cellos (A string, tenor clef) and violas in unison (PDF 57: the cellos predominate), Bn 1·2, Hn 3·4, and basses an octave lower with bass clarinet and contrabassoon. That is about 25 players plus five winds, against the violins alone (Vln I+II) on the countersubject, with the winds holding mf. PDF 54 lists exactly these "natural" means: strengthening by doubling and choice of timbre.
- **Decision.** No change. This passage is protected by cycle 7.
