## Findings

### 1. 167-170: the stopped horns are doubled at the unison by the clarinets, and the low part is in the bassoons only (applied)

**Bars and problem.** In the link before the muted trumpet and cor anglais at 171, Hn 1·2 play C5/E♭4 open f at 167 and A♭4/D4 stopped, > p, at 168-169 (c289). Cl 1 doubles Hn 1 at the unison (C5, A♭4). Cl 2 doubles Hn 2 at the unison (E♭4, D4), then holds F4 at 169-170. Under them only Bn 1 (E♭3–F3–F♯3–G3–F3) and Bn 2 (A♭2–G2–B2) fill the low middle. The stopped-horn colour, which is the reason for the bar, is mixed with an open clarinet at the same pitch. The dark low-wind sound in the bassoons is the version Rimsky says is weaker.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 107: "A beautiful dark tone is derived from the combination of middle notes in stopped horns and deep notes in the clarinet ... If bassoons are substituted for clarinets the effect loses part of its character." The diagram shows stopped horns in the treble staff over clarinets in the chalumeau.
- Rimsky PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns." Cl 2 sat on Hn 2's pitch, inside the horn pair.
- Ex. 148 (PDF 320, *Russian Easter Fête*): stopped horn and low trumpets over Ob/Cl in the chalumeau, poco sf, in a soft dark chord. Ex. 151 (PDF 323, *Antar*): Ob, C.a., 2 Fag. plus 4 stopped horns. In both, the stopped horns take the middle and the reeds take the other registers. No reed shares the horn pitch.
- The passage leads into the cor anglais and muted trumpet at 171 (rows 6, 11), which take up the same dark, nasal colour, so the link should already sound that colour.

### 3. Dvořák's single soft cymbal and the ppp final chord (considered, not applied)

Dvořák 9/iv has one cymbal stroke, mf, and ends with a wind diminuendo to ppp. Rimsky Ex. 148 and 151 show a soft cymbal played with a timpani stick inside a quiet chord. This score restricts the cymbals to the three fff crashes at 223, 263 and 278 (c30, kept since). The percussion header names crash cymbals only, and the ending 273-279 is protected. A soft suspended-cymbal colour would add an instrument and break a rule accepted for a stated reason. The score's 264 diminuendo already gives the Dvořák effect at the right place: the chord held by winds after the cut. Not applied.
