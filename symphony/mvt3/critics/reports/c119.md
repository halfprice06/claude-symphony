## Holistic comparison: Schumann, Symphony No. 4, finale

Schumann's finale grows out of a slow transition. Over held chords, with soft trombones, the first movement's figure rises in the violins to a dominant, and the Lebhaft arrives in D major. The main theme carries that first-movement figure as its constant accompaniment. The development works the head contrapuntally. The recapitulation is shortened. The coda speeds up twice (Schneller, then Presto), so the symphony ends at its fastest. The score is lean: the trombones are saved for the transition and the big tuttis, and the winds mostly double the strings.

Against that model:

- **Where v119 matches Schumann or does better.**
  - The approach to the arrival does the same work as Schumann's transition: 211–218 (theme B), the stop chord at 222 and C major at 223.
  - Brass are held back in the same way. There are no trumpets before 51, and no full-brass C major before 223.
  - The quotations are more exact than Schumann's: 36 checks pass, and movement I's pitches are kept.
  - A conductor would accept the orchestral writing as professional. The reading's rules hold almost everywhere (table above).
- **Where a conductor would see a difference.**
  - 115–134 sit at the centre of the development and are lyrical. Schumann's centre is contrapuntal working.
  - The coda broadens to Largamente where Schumann accelerates.
  - Both points are decided and protected: the 123–130 hand-over, the coda proportion (cycles 43, 63, 79) and R at 88 (Dan). I keep them. The finale's model for its last pages is Brahms 1/iv (arrival and chorale), and a Presto would undo the chorale's weight.

The three faults the reading exposes are local voicing faults in the two loudest dominant and tonic areas (147–150, 273–276) and one inner-voice doubling at 245. All three are fixed in the scratch copy.

## Findings

### 1. 147–150: the ff V7 before the recapitulation gives a second to Hn 3·4 and a tritone to the oboes (applied)

- **Problem.** The V7 is held for four bars of ff tutti. In it:
  - Hn 3·4 sustain G4/F4, a major second, on one shared staff.
  - Ob 1·2 sustain B5/F5, a tritone, at 147 and 149–150.
  - These are the movement's longest same-pair discords at ff. The pairs script finds no other sustained one in the score that is not a moving or suspended voice.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky PDF 90, rule 4: "Concords (octaves, thirds and sixths) and not discords (fifths, fourths, seconds and sevenths), should be given to instruments of the same kind or colour, except when discords are to be emphasised. This rule should be specially observed in writing for the oboe."
  - PDF 102, for brass: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour."
  - In the examples' held chords, the paired oboes and horns sit in thirds and sixths, with the discords spread across colours: Ex. 86 (PDF 260, f wind chord), Ex. 97 (PDF 274), Ex. 100 (PDF 278).
- **Fix (applied, retr.ily).**
  - `retrHnThree` 147–150: `f'1->\ff | f'1 | f'1~ | f'2. r4`. Hn 3 keeps the F4 it already holds at 145–146, so the seventh is prepared.
  - `retrHnFour` 147–150: `d'1->\ff | d'1 | d'1~ | d'2. r4`.
  - Hn 3·4 are now a third apart (F4/D4). G4 stays in Hn 1·2, Tpt 3, violas and Vln II.
  - `retrOboeTwo` 147–150: `d''1->\ff | b'1 | d''1~ | d''2. r4`. That is a sixth under Ob 1 in every bar (D5/B5, B4/G5). F5 stays in Vln I, Tpt 1's strokes and Fl 1. Ob 2 now steps D5→C5 into the F1 head at 151 (it leapt F5→C5 before).
  - No note of the trumpet strokes, the bass or the protected retransition harmony changes.

### 2. 273–276: undivided cellos and basses under divided upper strings, both ff (applied)

- **Problem.** At Largamente the upper strings are all divided:
  - Vln I `<g'' c'''>`, Vln II `<c'' g''>`, violas in two at 273–274.
  - Each line therefore has half a section. The protected top C6 has only Fl 1 and half of Vln I.
  - Cellos (tremolo C3) and basses (eighths C2) play undivided at the ff carried from 265, on a C that five wind instruments already double.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 88, on sustained and tremolando string chords: "If the harmony in the three upper parts, thus strengthened, is written for divided strings, the 'cellos and basses, playing non divisi will prove a trifle heavy; their tone must therefore be eased, either by marking the parts down or reducing the number of players." In Ex. 100 (PDF 278) only the solo cellos are divided, and the basses hold one quiet line under the full divisi.
- **Fix (applied, finale.ily).**
  - `finaleVc` 273: `c1:32->\f`. Its `\<` at 275 is kept.
  - `finaleCb` 273: first eighth `c,8\f`, and 275 first eighth `c,8\<`.
  - Both reach the existing fff at 278 with everyone else. No notes change, and fff stays only at 223, 263 and 278.

### 3. 245: Vln I doubles the bass of the V4/3 (applied)

- **Problem.** At 245–246 the harmony is V4/3 over D (Vc D3, Cb D2, Bn 2 D3). Vln I's divided chord `<d' b'>` puts the bass D in an upper part at D4.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 83: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts", with his "Bad" diagrams of V6/5, V4/3 and V4/2.
- **Fix (applied, sHead.ily).**
  - `sHeadVlnOne` 245: `<g' b'>2` instead of `<d' b'>2`. The lower note keeps the G4 of 244 `<g' d''>`, a common tone, so the lower divisi line no longer drops a fourth.
  - This is an inner divisi note inside the protected III♯ approach (241–247). The chord, the bass, the horn call in the trumpets and the run at 245–246 are unchanged.

### 4. v109 → v119: the chorale's top octave at 257–263 is thinner (not changed)

- **What changed.**
  - In v109 the trumpet line (E5–D5–C5–B4–C5) had its upper octave in Fl 1, Cl 1 and Vln I.
  - c109 moved Cl 1 down to the unison, which is correct by PDF 67, since a clarinet above the oboe inverts the registers.
  - c117 gave Fl 2 the Vln II arpeggio.
  - The upper octave is now Fl 1 and Vln I.
- **What the reading says.** Rimsky PDF 75 asks for three or four winds, or two flutes in the top register, above a trumpet.
- **Decision.** I keep v119. PDF 76 says strings predominate over winds of equal power, and the whole Vln I section is on the E6 line. c117's reason also stands: the arpeggio is the only middleground and needs a wind doubling. The passage is thinner on paper than v109 but not weaker in sound.
