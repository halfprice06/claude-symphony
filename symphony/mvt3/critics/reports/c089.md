## Holistic comparison with Sibelius 2/iv

Sibelius saves one registral and dynamic apex for the end of a long build, puts a moving string figure under held brass in the peroration, and broadens the tempo for the last bars. v89 does the last two: the strings' scale figure under the held tutti at 263–264 is Sibelian, and Largamente from 273 gives the close its breadth. The weak point is the apex. The arrival at 223 and the last cadence at 263 now have the same top pitch (C7) and the same dynamic (fff), and the final chord at 278 is lower (G6). A conductor would hear 263 as a copy of 223, not a confirmation, and would hear the coda's top come before its final chord. At 223 the C7 is approached by a scale and doubled by the violins; at 263 the flute jumps to it alone. That is the one item that has got worse since v79 (Finding 1). Nothing else in the 263–279 close has got worse; the R tempo of 88 stays.

## Findings

### 1. 263–264, Fl 1: C7 reached by a leap of a ninth, alone, duplicating the arrival (applied)
Problem: since v89 Fl 1 plays C7 at 263 (c088 Finding 1, "as at 223"). It comes from B5 at 262 by a leap of a minor ninth, and nothing sounds within an octave of it (Cl 1 C6; Vln I leaves C6 after one quarter). In v79 the flute had C6 here.
Evidence: Adler (adler1 PDF 185–186): the extreme top of the flute is shrill and can only be played very loud; "the best approach is by scale", and the highest fourth is the most problematic. At 223 the composer followed this (scale at 222, violins' C7 doubling); at 263 neither. A&S PDF 445 (Unit 26): a goal tonic is best reached by a line that gravitates to 1; the chorale line in the flute is B5→C6, and the leap breaks it. Cyclic plan (BRIEF owed item 2, finale.md): the C7 is the arrival's signature; repeating it 40 bars later in the same instrument at the same dynamic weakens it, and the final chord (G6) then sits below the last cadence.
Answer to c088's reason: the arrival's brightness at 263 does not need the C7. The cadence is the movement's first root V–I in C, fff with the cymbal crash, the bass drum and full brass; the string scale figure supplies the motion at the top.
Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-089/work/sections/finale.ily, finaleFluteOne):
`b''1 | c''''1\fff~ | c''''2. r4` → `b''1 | c'''1\fff~ | c'''2. r4`
The violins' C7 is untouched (only at 223).

### 2. 262: keep the last V7 free of A♭ (rule check, no change)
I tested adding a ♭9 (A♭5 → G5 in Fl 2) over the V7 at 262, following A&S Unit 27 (PDF 463–464: ♭9 in major by mixture, resolving by step to 5). It must not be done: with B and F already present it sounds B–F–A♭ over G, the stop chord, resolving to C a second time. The rule is that the stop chord resolves only at 222→223. The present voicing, where Fl 2 and Ob 2 take A♭→F at 261–262 and the A♭ resolves to G only in the bass (A&S PDF 503: the bass ♭6 opens to the octave on 5), is correct. Decided: no change; later critics should not add A♭ at 262.

### 3. 255–262, last chorale: no suspension in any voice (no change, reasons given)
A&S PDF 346–348 (Jupiter IV) shows how a master animates a whole-note subject: a tied, syncopated counterpoint making 4–3, 7 and 9–8. Here everyone moves in whole notes or repeated notes under the line G–E–D–C–B–C. I tried a syncopated counterpoint in the cellos and the free wind inner voices (Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2). Every candidate either sounded the suspension against its note of resolution in another voice (B3 in Hn 2·4 at 256; melody C4 in Hn 1/Tbn 1 at 261; G5 in Cl 2 at 262) or brought in the ♭9 at 262 (Finding 2). The chorale in whole-note homophony is also the Brahms 1/iv model this movement follows, and c087 kept it. Decided: keep as is. The Vln II arpeggios (A&S PDF 329, figuration) already give the motion.

### 4. 251–254, compared with v79: trumpets rest in the second S-head phrase (flagged, no change)
Since v87 Tpt 1·2 rest 251–254 and Ob 1 + Cl 1 carry the upper octave of the S head, so the brass combination of the two heads is lighter in its second phrase than in v79. Hn 1·2 (S head) and Tbn 1·2 (P head) keep the owed item. The rest gives the trumpets 5.5 seconds before the fff chorale at 255–264 and the F1 statement at 265–268. Decided: keep; the dip before the chorale is a gain and the endurance reason (c086, Adler) stands.
