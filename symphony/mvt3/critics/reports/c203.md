## Findings

### 1. 95–98: the movement's first E♮ is hidden, and the E♭/E♮ thread does not start in the violas (applied)

- **Bars:** 95–98, the four bars that join the exposition to the development (G, 99). Violas, Vln I, Ob 1.
- **Problem:** The movement's chromatic thread is E♭→E♮. The violas carry it: E♭4 in bar 1, D♯4→E4 at 219–220, E4 in the arrival chord at 223, and E4 again at the end of 273–276. The pitch E♮ is withheld for 96 bars. It first sounds at 97, as the third of C7 (V of F minor), where the development is reached. In v203 that E4 is in Cl 2 alone, p, under Ob 1's C5. The violas hold B♭3. So the first inflection E♭→E♮ is in an inner wind voice the ear cannot follow, and it is not at the thread's pitch in the thread's instrument.
  - Above it, Vln I and Ob 1 play E♭5–D♭5–C5 in whole notes. The line just misses movement I's motto tail. Movement I's second form of the motto (I 117–121, Tbn 1: `es'2. d'4 | des'2. c'4~ | c'`) has the D.
- **Evidence:**
  - A&S PDF 585: when the inflected pair is a motive of the piece (the Hammerklavier's F♮/F♯), the inflection is placed so the ear notices it. "To have softened this first confrontation would have weakened the compositional fabric."
  - A&S PDF 597–598 (Ex. 32-16, 32-17b and the note): Haydn ties the C-minor area into the piece through E♭/D♯, and "took great pains to emphasize D♯ as leading tone to E in the first section", so that the later enharmonic return is prepared. The note ties E♭ to C minor and E♮ to C major, which is this movement's own plan.
  - A&S PDF 600–601 (§16): the large plan relates to detail. A motive is enlarged across the key areas. Here the bar-1 E♭4 becomes E4 at the development's entry, then D♯4–E4 at the arrival, all in one instrument at one pitch.
- **Fix (applied, sections/clos.ily, bars 95–98 only, with a c203 comment):**
