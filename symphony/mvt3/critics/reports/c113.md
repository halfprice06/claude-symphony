## Findings

### 1. 80–83: the second theme's last bar is a held tonic under an animando and a diminuendo, so the exposition stops before the closing group (applied)

- **Bars:** 80–83. Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1, Ob 1 (the tune), violas, cellos, basses, Bn 1·2.
- **Problem:** The major statement of F2 ends with a diminuendo from 80 beat 4 to mf, and a whole-note E♭ at 82 in every part. "animando" (MIDI 72 → 76 → 80) is printed over these two bars, but they contain only half and whole notes and the music is getting softer. At 83 the closing group restarts f, at Tempo I, on the same E♭ tonic. The tempo rise therefore has no rhythm to carry it. The second group (51–82) ends on a closed tonic bar, and the closing group starts again from mf without a crescendo.
  - The score already handles the matching seam better in the recapitulation: 196–198 crescendo into 199 (c068).
  - This is the only place where the exposition's pacing works against its own markings. The other seams (42, 50, 66, 94, 98) are cadences or planned breaks that the dynamics support.
- **Evidence:**
  - A&S PDF 585: rhythm, texture and dynamics decide how a juncture is heard. Here they cancel the animando.
  - A&S PDF 592: large-scale progression should keep its "sense of directed, goal-oriented progression". The E♭ area runs from 67 to 94 and its goal is the ff codetta at 91. A full stop at 82 splits that area into two closed blocks.
  - Corpus, Dvořák 9/iv 89–92 (strings.notesF.ily): the second theme's last phrase is marked f< and runs into the ff codetta at 92 with no held tonic bar.
- **Fix (applied, sections/sTwo.ily, bar 82 only):**
  ```
  sTwoVlnOne  … | f''2( d''2) | es''2\mf\< g''8( aes''8 bes''8 d'''8) | }
  sTwoVlnTwo  … | f'2( d'2^\markup \italic "sul G") | es'2\mf^\markup \italic "ord."\< g'8( aes'8 bes'8 d''8) | }
  sTwoFluteOne … | f''2 d''2 | es''2)\mf\< g''8( aes''8 bes''8 d'''8) | }
  sTwoOboeOne  … | f'2 d'2 | es'2)\mf\< g'8( aes'8 bes'8 d''8) | }
  sTwoVla / sTwoVc: last bar g1\<     sTwoCb: es,1\< \ottava #0     sTwoBsnOne / sTwoBsnTwo: es1\<
  ```
  - **What the run does:** the tune keeps its final E♭ for a half note, then runs up E♭–G–A♭–B♭–D into the E♭6/E♭5 that opens the closing group at 83 (f in every part already). The animando now has eighth notes to drive. The run has F1's rising bar-3 shape, so it also announces the F1 head in major at 83.
  - **Voice leading:** the run moves over the held E♭ bass. At 82→83 the top voice goes D→E♭ against the violas' G→E♭, in contrary motion. Fl 1 and Ob 1 share no staff with a different part in these bars, so the shared-staff dynamics rule is met.
  - **Checks:** pairwise parallel check (c002/par.py) and true-fifth check (c032/p5.py) give the same output as v113. The diminuendo 80–82 to mf is kept, so the phrase still falls before it rises.
- **Theme note:** this changes the last bar of F2's major statement at 67–82 (E♭ whole note → E♭ half note + run). F2's opening, the trumpet solo 51–66 and the recapitulation statements 171–198 are unchanged. No other movement quotes F2, and finale.md lists F2 by its opening only. The other threads do not need to be told, but the composer should know that F2's ending changed here.
