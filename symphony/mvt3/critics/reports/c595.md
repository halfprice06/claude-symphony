## Findings

### 1. Hn 1·2, 247–249: the unison prints as two voices (applied)

**Problem.** At letter R both horns play movement I's second-theme head on the same notes, E4–D4–C4–A4–G4–F4. The finale file forces the part combiner apart (`\partCombineApart`) for 247–250. Bar 250 splits at the octave, and the slur ends there. As a result the score prints every note of 247–249 twice: stems up and down, two slurs, two ties at 247 and two tenutos at 248. The only label is the "2." left over from Hn 2's G3 pickup at 246.4, so the staff reads as Hn 2 alone with a stray second voice. Adler's Brahms page (Ex. XVIII-4, PDF 297) prints such a unison as one line marked "zu 2". App. A (PDF 312) gives "1., 2." as labels for one player only. c135 fixed the same fault at 83 on MOLA's rule. This was the only unintended forced-apart unison in the score.

**Fix (applied, finale.ily).** The combiner is held in unisono through 250. In the score, both players' 250 is written as the two-horn octave, so the slur stays in one voice. The parts keep their own notes.

```lilypond
finaleHnOne = { \tag #'score \partCombineUnisono e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8 | \tag #'score { <c' c''>2 <b b'>2) } \tag #'part { c''2 b'2) } \breathe | \partCombineAutomatic ...
finaleHnTwo = { \tag #'score \partCombineUnisono e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8 | \tag #'score { <c' c''>2 <b b'>2) } \tag #'part { c'2 b2) } \breathe | \partCombineAutomatic ...
```

Both parts carry the chord because in unisono LilyPond prints the player who played last, which here is Hn 2 (from 246.4). I tested that first. Switching from unisono to `\partCombineApart` or `\partCombineChords` at 250 gave "cannot end slur" warnings. Printing Hn 1's chord only lost the slur and the octave. The result is one line marked a2 with one slur and one tie, then the octave at 250. Sounding pitches are unchanged; the MIDI has one note-on per unison note instead of two. Page 14's layout is unchanged.

### 2. Ob 1 and Cl 1 at 101: no "1." label after letter G (applied)

**Problem.** At 101 the development's first wind answer is played by Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 alone, after the double bar, the key change to F minor and letter G. The flute staff prints "1." automatically. The oboe and clarinet staves print nothing, because their last state was already solo (Ob 1 at 95, Cl 1 at 97). On the page, three identical entries one above the other are labelled differently, and a single unlabelled line after a section break reads as a2. Adler PDF 291 item 3 requires every detail for every player, and App. A (PDF 312) gives "1." for the first player alone. c145 added the same score-only labels at 61, 77 and 105.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** `r4 c''8(\mf-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." bes' ...` in devOboeOne and in devClOne. These are score-only; the MIDI is unchanged.

### 3. Hn 1·2 at 81: two p's on one staff (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 2's diminuendo ends p on 81.1 (`f2~ f2\> | bes,1\p`), but Hn 1's ends on 81.3 (`aes4\> g4~ | g4 f4) d'2\p`). The shared staff therefore prints one hairpin to a p at 81.1 and a second p two beats later under Hn 1's D4. Adler PDF 294 and Ex. XVIII-10a (PDF 301) allow two sets of dynamics on one staff only when the players need different ones. The project rule also asks for equal simultaneous dynamics on shared staves. Here nothing requires the difference. Bn 1·2 reach p on 81.1 as well. Hn 1's G at 81.1 is a tied suspension, so reaching p there makes its resolution F the softer note, which is what c398 wanted.

**Fix (applied, sTwo.ily).** `aes4\> g4~ | g4\p f4) d'2 |`. The page now shows one hairpin and one p. The only MIDI change is that Hn 1 is p from 81.1 instead of 81.3.

### 4. Fl 1 at 197: no "1." after 21 bars' rest (applied)

**Problem.** Fl 1 last played at 174–175. It re-enters alone at 197, p < f, after letter M and a key change, and the combiner prints no label for the same reason as in Finding 2. The oboe above it re-enters after only four bars inside the same phrase, so it is left alone. The reading and precedent are the same as in Finding 2.
