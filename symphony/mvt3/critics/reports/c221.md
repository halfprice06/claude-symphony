## Findings

### 1. 95–98: the motto tail at the end of the exposition is a unison of Vln I and Ob 1; give it to the oboe alone (applied)

**Problem.** After the ff codetta (91–94) the exposition closes softly on E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5, movement I's motto tail in its second form (c203), mf > p over the strings' E♭7 → C7 and the violas' E♭4→E4 inflection. Vln I and Ob 1 play it at the unison. The oboe adds no weight to the violins at mf, and the violins cover its colour. The result is a neutral string line at the one point where the finale recalls the motto softly. In movement I, the soft statements of the motto are single woodwinds: the cor anglais at I 5–8 (p espressivo) and the flute at I 73–76 (mvt1.md). In the finale the oboe never plays a line alone.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184: an orchestrator should give each part to the instrument that sounds best on it and that no other instrument could replace. PDF 198–199: the oboe is "basically a lyrical instrument", with a long tradition of solos. The printed examples (Bach, Schumann 2/iii "p cantabile" with fp swells, Bartók) are single oboe lines over light accompaniment. PDF 198: its most effective range is F4 to C6, and E♭5–C5 lies in the middle of that band. PDF 201: as a doubler, the oboe's "distinctive nasal quality" serves the line it doubles, and its own colour is not heard. Here the doubled line is a recall that the listener should notice. Critic 144 already scored the clarinets' diminuendo at 97 by taking out a doubling instrument (clos.ily comment, Adler ch. 8 PDF 270, Copland). Taking Vln I out applies the same principle to the top line. Beethoven 5/i (the oboe's adagio phrase after the tutti stop in the recapitulation) is the classic case of a single oboe emerging from a ff tutti.

**Fix (applied, clos.ily).**
```
closVlnOne = { … | es'''2->\downbow r2 | R1*4 | }
closOboeOne = { … | g''2-> r2 | es''2.\mf\>(^\markup \italic "solo, espress." d''4 | des''1) | c''1\p~ | c''2. r4 | }
```
Pitches, rhythm and dynamics of the line are unchanged; Vln I rests 95–102 (it already rested 99–102). The harmony (Vln II G4, violas B♭3 with E♭4→E4, Cl 1 B♭4 at 95–96, Cl 2 E♭4→E4, Bn 2, cellos and basses E♭→C) is unchanged, so the viola thread at 97 is untouched. The oboe is the top voice by a fourth over Cl 1's B♭4, mf over mf > p strings and clarinets, so it carries. On the shared oboe staff the score prints "1." and "solo, espress." (page 5). The oboe line is 10.7 s at 2 = 84 after a half-bar rest, with two bars' rest after it (PDF 198: the oboist can hold long lines but needs rests). No new parallels: par8.py on 93–100 gives the same output as v221. The 16 pages are kept and the systems are not changed.

Note for finale.md: 95–98 the motto tail is an oboe solo (Vln I rests 95–102). F1, F2 and the chorale top line keep their notes. No quotation of another movement changes.

### 2. 171–186 and 217–220: the cor anglais registers (checked, no change)

With Ex. VII-47 read at 300 dpi, F2's minor statement (written D5–D6) lies in the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band (written C5–A5). Only the peak, written C6–D6 at 181–183, rises toward the "thin, pinched" band (written E6 and up). There Fl 1 doubles it since c041, so the peak does not depend on the cor anglais alone. The passage is protected (51–66/171–186), and the reading gives no reason to undo that protection. The cor anglais's "deep, rich, intense" register (PDF 205) is heard once, at 217 (sounding A♭3), in the theme-B answer. This is the right place, because it is movement II's own pitch. No change.

### 3. 95–98: two clarinets in low unison, as in Tchaikovsky 5/i (considered, rejected)

Adler PDF 211 prints Tchaikovsky's motto stated softly by two clarinets in unison in the chalumeau. I tried the same idea for the motto tail at 95–98 an octave lower (Cl 1·2 E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4). I rejected it. That octave is the violas' E♭4→E4 thread (c203: one pitch from bar 1 to 223), and Cl 2 already doubles the violas there. A clarinet line in that register would sound D4 and D♭4 against the held E♭4, and it would hide the thread's E4 at 97. At the written octave the line would be in the clarinets' clarino, which is no longer the dark colour of Tchaikovsky's example. The oboe solo of finding 1 keeps the register and gives the line a single woodwind voice.

### 4. The bass clarinet has no line (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 217–218 praises the bass clarinet's chalumeau for lyrical legato (Wagner, Tristan II, image), and movement I's motto is first played by the bass clarinet with the cellos (I 1–4). In the finale it plays only held or bass-doubling notes. Every soft, low passage where a bass-clarinet line could recall that colour (133–138, 167–170, 216–222) is protected or would double a protected bass. I found no unprotected place, so no change.

### 5. Oboe in its lowest fifth at 102 and 270–271 (checked, no change)

Ob 1 ends the development's scale figure on an accented C4 at 102 (mf), and Ob 1·2 pass E4–D4 in F1's major continuation at 270–271 (ff, with Cl 1·2 and Vln II at the unison and the flutes and Vln I an octave higher). Adler's rule for this band is that it must never be asked for pianissimo (PDF 198). Neither place is soft, and both are doublings. This agrees with c131's decision at 20–21 and 160–161. No change.
