## Findings

### 1. Bar 36: use the reading's ♮VII7 → V6/5 (A&S Unit 24 §12) — applied in scratch

**Problem.** Bar 36 ends F1's A♭-major head (35–36) and moves to the B♭-minor statement at 37. Since c322 it runs E♭7 (36.1) – A♭ (36.3) – F7/A (V6/5 of B♭ minor, 36.4) – B♭ minor (37), over the bass E♭–A♭–A♮–B♭. The A♭ on 36.3 is a plain triad. Nothing ties it to the V6/5 that follows except the bass's semitone. The F4 in the violas', cellos' and Bn 1's figure on 36.3.5 is only a 6th over A♭.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 410–411 (sec. 12, Ex. 24-18, Handel): the seventh chord on ♮7 in minor "is also V7 of III", so it raises an expectation of III. "When the ♮VII7 continues on to V6/5, the effect is surprising and dramatic". It is "a valuable alternative to extending dominant harmony through changes within V7 itself". Here ♮7 of B♭ minor is A♭ and III is D♭, so an A♭7 on 36.3 points to D♭ and then turns to F7/A and B♭ minor.

A&S PDF 433 and 437–438 (sec. 16, Ex. 25-28, 25-29): in successive dominants (E♭7 → A♭7), the 3rd of one becomes the 7th of the next by chromatic descent. Such descents are "the almost inevitable result" of chains of applied V7s. PDF 420 (Ex. 25-3a): keep that chromatic step in one voice. PDF 399: the 7th's resolution "intensifies the motion to the second chord". PDF 406–407 (sec. 9): a transferred resolution works best when the step appears in the next lower voice in the same register.

The score has never used ♮VII7 → V6/5; c322 recorded it as having "no free place". Bar 36 is that place. It is unprotected, its V6/5 is c322's own change, and the soprano does not hold the chord's 7th.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-592/work/sections/pTwo.ily):**
```
pTwoHnOne, bar 36:       g'2 aes'4 f'4            ->  g'2 ges'4 f'4
pTwoVla, pTwoVc, pTwoBsnOne, bar 36, second half:
                         c'8( f'8 es'8 c'8)       ->  c'8( ges'8 es'8 c'8)
```
Hn 1 now plays G4–G♭4–F4. The third of E♭7 falls chromatically to the 7th of A♭7, and on to the root of the V6/5. That line runs against the bass A♭–A♮–B♭, so a falling chromatic line in one voice meets the rising chromatic bass.

The figure now arpeggiates A♭7 (C–G♭–E♭–C), as its first half already arpeggiates E♭7 (B♭–G–E♭–D♭). Without this change, the figure's F4 would rub a semitone against the horn's G♭4. The figure's G♭ skips to E♭, and its step of resolution (G♭→F) is in Hn 1 in the same register, as PDF 407 prefers. The A♭ root stays in the basses, contrabassoon, bass trombone, Bn 2 and timpani.

G♭ also links bar 36 to the flat colour that follows: Hn 1's own G♭4–F4 at 38.3–4, the C♭/G♭ at 38, and E♭ minor at 51.

**Unchanged:** the tune (F1's head in A♭ in the violins, flutes and Cl 1), the bass, the dynamics, c322's V6/5 and c587's Cl 1. No theme and no quotation is changed. The figure is accompaniment, not F1. MIDI differs only in these four notes (36.5 Hn 1; 36.625 violas, cellos, Bn 1).

**Build:** clean. build-warnings.txt is empty and there are 16 pages. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 35–38 gives output identical to v592's (only existing bass doublings). The all-pairs parallel check shows no new item over the whole score (17310 lines before and after). The theme record is 51/51 unchanged. The engraving at 300% shows the written D♭5 in the horn part and flats in the alto and tenor clefs, with no collisions.

### 2. Deceptive cadences on applied chords (A&S PDF 427, Ex. 25-16) — no free place; no change

The technique would delay a goal and then return to it more strongly. I checked every authentic cadence outside the protected bars:
- **C minor:** excluded. The movement withholds root V–i in C until 262–263.
- **E♭ at 90:** a VII°7 of V on A♮ under the soprano's E♭ would sit under Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1's rising run B♭–C–D–E♭–F, which has D and F against E♭ and G♭. The run would have to be rewritten.
- **F minor at 110→111:** the only applied chords to V that put ♯4 in the bass are VII°7 of V, B–D–F–A♭, and V6/5 of V, G–B–D–F. The first is the stop chord, which may resolve to C only at 222→223. The second shares its B–D–F.

I decided not to add one.

### 3. Chromaticised voice exchange iv → V at 33.3–4 (A&S PDF 426, Ex. 25-15c) — tested on paper, declined

The bass's F–A♭ (F1 in the bass) against the countersubject's A♭–F could be chromaticised by giving the violins F♯ on 33.4 (`d''8( fis'' aes'' c''')`). That would make a French sixth on A♭ resolving to V at 34. I declined it for two reasons:
- It would be the score's tenth augmented-sixth sonority (1, 5, 16, 109, 121, 163, 168, 219, 261 already).
- The same melodic point of F1 at 17.3 already has c332's ♯IV7.

The fleeting eighth-note F♯ would weaken both.

### 4. Successive dominants at 101–103 (A&S PDF 431, sec. 16) — tested on paper, declined

Turning 102.3–4 into F7/A would make the development link C7 – F7 – B♭ minor. It would clash in two ways:
- The winds' rising line F–G–A♭–B♭–C at 102 would put A♭ against the new A♮ bass, a cross relation inside one chord that PDF 420 calls the harshest kind.
- It would break the parallel with 105–106, which stays V of the key just stated.

c383's reading of 99–106 as a two-step descending-fifth sequence with half closes holds.

### 5. The 70–73 chain (A&S PDF 431, 434, Ex. 25-30) — checked, no change
