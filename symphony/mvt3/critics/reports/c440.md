## Findings

### 1. 216–218: Tpt 1 still holds D♭5 at f over movement II's theme-B echo. Applied.

**Problem.** At 217–218 the cor anglais (theme B's own instrument in movement II) and Hn 1·2 answer the trumpets' recall at movement II's pitch: A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4, f espress. This is the last time movement II's voice speaks in the symphony, just before the German sixth of bar 1 returns at 219. Since c414, Fl 2, Ob 2 and Tpt 2 stop with the 216 diminuendo so that the echo is heard. Tpt 1 still holds D♭5 at f through 218. That is a fourth above the echo's top note and the strongest sustained colour in that register. One trumpet at f outweighs a cor anglais at f; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41 and 48 put one trumpet at about two horns.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 188: the solo instrument is chosen for "the emotional as well as the tonal intent", and "the dynamic also has a great deal to do with the choice". A cor anglais cannot carry its cyclic phrase under a held f trumpet.
- Adler PDF 191 (Ex. XV-37): a held note can change colour while it is held. Here the held D♭ can pass from trumpet-led to woodwinds and strings at 217 without losing its pitch. Fl 1 (D♭6), Ob 1 and Cl 1 (D♭5) and Vln I·II (D♭6, F5) still hold it. The protected top line D♭–C–B–C (Fl 1, violins) is not touched.
- Adler PDF 190 (Ex. XV-36): the soft chord sounds most subito when players who did not hold the loud chord take it. Tpt 1 now enters fresh on the 219 sub. p C5, as Tpt 2 has since c414. It then has two bars' rest (about 3.3 s at 2 = 72) before the protected C5–B4–B4→C5 at 219–223 and the fff arrival. Adler adler2 PDF 8 says brass need rests.
- Tpt 1·2 now share identical dynamics and rests at 216–218 on their shared staff.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-440/work/sections/codaTwo.ily, `codaTwoTptOne`, with a c440 comment):**

### 4. Heterophonic doubling (Ex. XV-34, PDF 189): considered, not applied

The finale and the whole symphony still have no heterophonic doubling. The only tunes where it could sit are cyclic quotations (211–218, 235–246, 247–254, 255–264) or the protected solos (51–66, 171–186). A decorated line beside a quotation would blur what the listener should recognise, which works against Adler's own first principle (adler1 19). There is no new place for it, so the earlier decisions stand.

### 5. Violas "sul G" for the thread step D♯4→E4 at 219–220 (PDF 191, string choice): considered, not applied

Playing D♯4–E4 on the G string would give the thread step a covered colour and a slide. But the step is already coloured: Hn 3's fresh p D♯4 doubles it. A darker G-string tone at p under the German sixth would make the one step meant to be heard less audible. On the D string in first position it speaks clearly.

### 6. Trumpet–timpani coupling (adler1 21) at the cyclic cadences: checked, no change

At 222→223 and 262→263, Tpt 1 B→C sounds with timpani G→C. At 199–204 the trumpets and timpani play `c4 r8 c8` together, as in movement I. At 276–277 motive x on G–G–G–C sounds under Tpt 1·2 C–D–C. The timpani pulse at 229–230 sounds without trumpets, as movement I 179–180 does; this is a quotation, so it is correct.

### 7. Forte subito piano at 223→224 (Ex. XV-36): checked, no change

The fff tutti at 223 drops to f/mf at 224 in the same players. Adler's overlap would need a fresh soft group under 223. That group would have to be the violins' mf tremolo, and it is protected (tremolo and C7 begin at 223). Earlier critics declined this for the same reason, and I have no new reason.
