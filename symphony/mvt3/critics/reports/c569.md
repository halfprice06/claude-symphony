## Holistic verdict (Franck)

A conductor who knows the Franck would accept this finale as the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer.

**What matches Franck.** It uses the devices Franck's finale relies on:
- a soft brass second theme at 51, set against a string theme;
- the slow movement recalled softly (95–98), then loudly (211–218);
- the motto turned to major over string tremolo at 223;
- choirs alternating in the development (99–106) and coda (205–208).

**Where it is stronger.** The harmonic plan is tighter than Franck's. The finale holds back the root-position cadence until 261–263, so the arrival has more weight than Franck's sequential climaxes.

**The one weakness against Franck.** The coda from 197 is close to continuous tutti. Franck thins to woodwinds and harp before the final recall. See finding 3. It is protected, so I do not change it.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 78, beat 3: harp A♭4 rubs a semitone against the melody's G4 in the same octave (applied)

**Problem.**
- c562 changed 78.3–4 to IV(7) and moved the harp's right hand to E♭4–A♭4–C5–E♭5.
- On beat 3 the melody is G4 in Cl, Hn 1 and Vc, doubled at G5 and G6. The harp's second eighth is A♭4, a minor second above the lowest melody octave, at the bar's expressive peak.
- v559 had harp F4–B♭4–D5 and Hn 2 B♭3, with no note inside the melody's octave a second away. This is the only thing in the v559→v569 changes that sounds worse.

**Evidence.**
- Adler's chord rules (PDF 158–159): the melody is the most prominent part, and the accompaniment is placed by acoustic affinity in its own register, out of the melody's way.
- PDF 154–156: the background must not muddy the foreground.
- Hn 2's A♭3 is an octave lower and does not clash. The harp's A♭4 does.

**Fix.** Keep c562's harmony and Hn 2 A♭3. Revoice only the second half of the harp's right hand so A♭ lies an octave above the melody. In `sections/sTwo.ily`, `sTwoHarpR`, bar 12 of the section:

(was `es'8 g'8 c''8 es''8 es'8 aes'8 c''8 es''8 |`)

**Result.**
- On beat 3 the harp now sounds E♭4 and C5 under G4, then E♭5 on beat 4, then A♭5 above the F5 resolution.

### 3. Coda 197–222 is close to unbroken tutti (observation; protected, not changed)

**Problem.** From 197 to 222, the full winds, brass and strings play in almost every bar. The only thinning is the choir alternation at 205–208.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 146 warns against unrelieved tutti.
- In Franck's finale and in Adler's Tchaikovsky 4 example (PDF 38), the strings dwindle before the final tutti and the new cantus firmus.

**Decision.** HISTORY.md protects 199–222 (c539 and earlier), and the stated reason still holds: 211–218 is the loud recall of movement II, and its continuity carries the stop chord into 223. I have no new reason to answer it, so I make no change. The thinning that already exists at 219–222 (Debussy-type controlled chord, PDF 165) is enough.

### 4. 273–274 string tremolo stops: "div." is correct (tested on paper, not changed)

**Candidate.** Following Schumann's non-divisi stops (PDF 161–162), I considered marking Vln I 273–274 non-div.

**Why not.** The stops G5–C6, F♯5–C6 and F5–C6 are a fourth, a tritone and a fifth high on the E and A strings. Played as tremolo double stops at ff they are awkward and lose the top. Divided players keep both notes. No change.

### 5. Tonic pedal in Hn 3·4 at 265–268 (candidate rejected)

**Candidate.** Jupiter's tonic–dominant pedal in horns, trumpets and timpani (PDF 175–176) suggested adding C to Hn 3·4 at 265–268.

**Why not.** Tpt 3 E4 and the timpani already supply the pedal and the third. The horns alternate in pairs: 3·4 rest at 265–268 and 1·2 rest at 269–272. That alternation is itself Adler's "two horns equal one trumpet" balance (PDF 44), and it gives the players rest before the last chords. Adding the pedal would break both. Not better; not applied.

### 6. 263.1: no G4 in the final tonic chord (candidate rejected)

**Candidate.** The 263 downbeat sounds C1 C2 C3 G3 C4 E4 C5 E5 G5 C6, so the middle octave has no fifth. Mahler 1 (PDF 164) fills the upper octaves.

**Why not.** Here the upper octaves are filled, and Hn 1·2 hold the chorale's C4 in unison as the HISTORY-protected chorale octave. Moving one horn to G4 would thin the chorale line on its last note. The fifth is present at G3 and G5. Not better; not applied.
