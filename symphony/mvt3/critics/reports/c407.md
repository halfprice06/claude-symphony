## Findings

### 1. 209–210: the last run into the D♭ arrival grows by only one octave (applied)

**Problem.** Over the A♭ pedal at 205–210 the violins' rising scale is heard three times. At 205–206 it is Vln I and Vln II in unison (one octave). At 207–208 it is Vln II alone, mf (one octave). At 209–210, f <, it is Vln I from A♭4 and Vln II from A♭3 (two octaves), into the ff at 211. Through all six bars the cellos sit on the pedal, on A♭2, which is already in Bn 2, the bass trombone and the timpani, with A♭1 in the basses, contrabassoon and tuba. The last statement of the figure, the one that has to carry the crescendo, has no low octave. Six instruments double the pedal while the moving line has none below A♭3.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 137, Ex. V-14 (Leonore 3, mm. 514–31): "Building an orchestral crescendo by adding octaves until triple forte is reached." The running figure starts in one violin section, then the second violins enter an octave below, then the violas, then cellos and basses, and each statement gains an octave. PDF 141, Ex. V-19 (Shostakovich 6): the cellos double the violins in octaves, which "contributes a feeling of greater resolution" and prepares what follows. PDF 135: the foreground should be in the instrument's best register; the cellos' A♭2–G♭4 is their strongest singing range. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129–130 supports this.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-407/work/sections/codaOne.ily).** The cellos double Vln II an octave lower at 209–210, with the same slurs and hairpin, and return to bass clef on the closing rest:

```
codaOneVc = { ... | aes,1\f | aes,1 | aes,1 | aes,1 | aes,8(\< bes, c des es f ges aes | \clef tenor bes8 c' des' es') ges'4-> \clef bass r4\! | }
```

So the three runs are one, one and three octaves (Vln I A♭4, Vln II A♭3, Vc A♭2), and the third reaches G♭6/G♭5/G♭4, c151's suspended seventh, before the D♭ chord at 211. The pedal keeps its A♭2 (Bn 2, bass trombone, timpani) and A♭1 (basses' eighths, contrabassoon, tuba). Nothing else changes: harmony (V7 of D♭), top line, dynamics (f < into ff), and the cellos' D♭3/D♭4 at 211. This is inside the "187–223 path" and "205–223 dynamic path" that earlier critics protected. Those protections name the harmonic route, the D♭–C–B–C top line, the dynamics and 222→223, and none of these is altered.

### 3. 19–26: Vln II only doubles the tune in the exposition's consequent (tested on paper, not applied)

Haydn's "melodizing" of the harmony in Vln II (PDF 139, Ex. V-16) is already used at 159–165, in the recapitulation's consequent. Giving 19–26 a similar line would remove the difference between the two statements, which is the point of Adler's example: the second statement grows. The current 19–26 matches Grieg's choice (PDF 154–155, Vln II doubling Vln I at the octave instead of adding harmony). I made no change.

### 4. 83–86: violas' broken chords in the register of the tune's lower octave (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 135 says to keep the background out of the melody's register when both have the same colour. The violas cross the lower octave of the tune (Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Vln II at E♭5) only on single eighths. They are already mf "leggiero" under an f tune (c255, c258), which is the remedy Adler names on PDF 144. Moving them down would put them into the cellos' walking quarters. I made no change.
