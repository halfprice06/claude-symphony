## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 96, 97, 136-138, 147, 221: bar-length notes stand against the barline (applied)

Problem: in these bars every part holds one bar-length note or rests, with no other duration (the MIDI shows every onset on beat 1 and nothing ending inside the bar). LilyPond puts the column of semibreves straight after the barline, and the rest of the bar is empty. 136-138 are the retransition's B-pedal bars before the stop chord; 221 is the allargando bar before the arrival.

Evidence: Gould PDF 55, "Notes of bar-length duration": "when a bar contains a single note and there are no other durations in any part, the note is placed just left of the centre of the bar ... This spacing creates a better balance than a single note positioned at the beginning of the bar." Her example shows the semibreve about 40% of the way into the bar. The score does not use this anywhere. 135 and 219 also qualify but start with a key change, and 223 starts a system, so in those three bars the note is already set off from the barline by the key signature or system start. I left them alone.

Fix (global.ily, applied): a score-only helper
`cen = \tag #'score { \once \override Score.BarLine.space-alist.next-note = #'(semi-fixed-space . 3.2) }`,
used before 96, 97, 136, 137, 138, 147 and 221 (`s1*9 \cen s1 \cen s1 s1` at 87-98; `\cen s1` three times at 136-138; `s1*3 \cen s1 s1` at 144-148; `s1*2 \cen \tempo ... "allargando"` at 221). The semibreves, their dynamics and hairpins now start about a third of the way into each bar. Parts are not affected (tagged 'score). Still 16 pages, and the systems keep the same bars.

### 2. 256 Hn 1·2 and 199-202 Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2: crossed semibreves do not show which player goes where (applied)

Problem: at 255-256 Hn 1 holds G3 (`g1~ | g1`) while Hn 2 moves G3 → B3, above Hn 1. The combiner draws 256 as one stemless "8" with the tie on the lower head, so the B3 reads as Hn 1's note and the held G3 as Hn 2's, which is the wrong way round. At 199-202 Fl 2 and Ob 2 hold semibreves (g''1, aes''1, aes''1, bes''1; g'1, f'1, aes'1, g'1) while Fl 1 and Ob 1 play movement I's hammered line, which drops below them to G at 200.4 and 201.1. The stemless semibreve does not show which voice it belongs to when the other part's eighths pass underneath it.

Evidence: Gould PDF 67: "Overlapping semibreves must be divided into stemmed notes in order to show part progression." Her example splits each semibreve into two tied stemmed minims. PDF 65: "the upper part always takes up-stems, the lower part down-stems, even when pitches overlap."

Fix (applied, score only, parts keep the semibreves):
- finale.ily `finaleHnOne`: `\partCombineApart \tag #'score { g2\ff~ g2~ | g2~ g2 } \tag #'part { g1\ff~ | g1 } \partCombineAutomatic`; `finaleHnTwo`: `\partCombineApart \tag #'score { g2\ff~ g2 | b2~ b2 } \tag #'part { g1\ff | b1 } \partCombineAutomatic`. Hn 1 is now up-stemmed on G3 throughout and Hn 2 down-stemmed rising to B3; "a2" returns at 257 as before.
- codaOne.ily `codaOneFluteTwo`: `\tag #'score { g''2\ff~ g''2 | aes''2~ aes''2 | aes''2~ aes''2 | bes''2~ bes''2 } \tag #'part { g''1\ff | aes''1 | aes''1 | bes''1 }`; `codaOneOboeTwo` likewise (`g'2\ff~ g'2 | f'2~ f'2 | aes'2~ aes'2 | g'2~ g'2`). I did all four bars, not only 200-201, so that the passage is written the same way throughout.
No sounding change. These are in protected passages (255-264 chorale statement; 199 hammered layout), but notes, rhythm and scoring are unchanged and only the stems are new, so the reasons for the protection still hold.

### 3. 103-104 Tbn 1·2: the solo label "1." appears in the middle of 104 (applied)

Problem: at 103 Tbn 1 starts F1's head (`bes2->(`) and Tbn 2 plays only the first crotchet on the same B♭ (`bes4-> r4 r2`). The staff is shown apart in 103 with Tbn 2's rests, but in 104 the rests disappear and "1." is printed over the second beat (the E♭ eighth), after the slur ends. A reader sees one part with no label on 104.1 and a solo label halfway through the bar.

Evidence: Gould PDF 65: in double-stemmed writing each part must be shown; the upper part takes up-stems, the lower part down-stems. The bar's rests or a label at the barline do the job of the lower part's stems; a label inside a slurred phrase shows neither.

Fix (dev.ily, applied): `devTbnOne`: `\partCombineApart bes2->(\f ... | aes4-.) ... | f4-.\sf r4 r2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1`; `devTbnTwo`: `\partCombineApart bes4->\f r4 r2 | R1*2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1`. Tbn 1 now has up-stems through 103-105 and Tbn 2's rests are shown below, with no label in mid-bar. (`\partCombineSoloI` at 104.1 would break the slur from 103; I tried it, it gave "cannot end slur", so I did not use it.)

### 4. 200 Hn 3·4: f sits on Hn 4's down-stem (applied)

Problem: the f at the end of the 199 diminuendo is printed at the hairpin's height, and Hn 4's G4 minim (`g2`) has a down-stem that runs through it.

Evidence: Gould PDF 55: "no characters should collide."

Fix (codaOne.ily, applied, score only): `c'4-\tag #'score -\tweak extra-offset #'(-0.3 . -1.6) \f` in Hn 3 and the same on Hn 4's `g2` (the shared staff prints one dynamic). The f now stands clear below the stem end. Raising `DynamicLineSpanner.staff-padding` had no effect here.

### 5. 170 Vla: "sul tasto" runs into the double barline at 171 (applied)

Problem: c214's new viola figure at 170.3 carries "sul tasto" left-aligned on its first eighth. The text runs to the double barline at L, and the p of the minim and the pp of the figure sit almost touching.

Evidence: Gould PDF 55 (no collisions, at least 1/2 space between characters) and PDF 57 (nothing closer to a barline than half a space).

Fix (recP.ily, applied): `\tag #'score { \once \override TextScript.self-alignment-X = #CENTER } d8(\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto" f d f)`. The text is centred over the figure and clear of the barline. I left p/pp as they are (about 1/2 space apart, which is Gould's minimum).
