## Findings

### 1. 43-50 Vln I: play the held B♭5 as an artificial harmonic (applied)

Problem. In c264's chord over the B♭ pedal (16th partial B♭5 in Vln I, harp harmonics B♭4 at 43, 45 and 47), the top partial is a stopped, vibrated sul tasto note played by 16 violins for 7¾ bars (about 11 s). With vibrato it sounds as a violin tone sitting on the chord. Without vibrato it would sound as one more partial of the chord. Adler PDF 46 describes the harmonic as an isolated overtone of the string. PDF 66 says artificial harmonics give "the same flutey, silvery sound as the natural harmonics". B♭ is not a natural harmonic on any violin string (PDF 65 table), so the book's method applies: stop the note with the first finger and touch the node a fourth above with the fourth finger, which sounds two octaves higher (PDF 66, Ex. II-72). The stopped B♭3 sits in second position on the G string and the touched node is E♭4, the most secure region of Ex. II-75's limits. The hand reaches that position from F4 at 42 during the beat of rest. Dvořák 9/i 91-98 and Mahler 1/i use a held violin harmonic in this situation. The harmonic is also an octave above the harp's B♭4 harmonic at 43, 45 and 47, so the two harmonics belong together.

Answer to c264's reason. Pitch, rhythm, dynamic, "change bow at will" and the release on 50.3 are unchanged, so the spectrum c264 built is kept, with its top partial now a purer tone. Casanelles's point that the violins give the harp's chime its sustain still holds because the harmonic is bowed and sustained. "ord., cantabile" at 67 already cancels the effect (PDF 53).

Fix (sections/trans.ily). The printed voice is silent in the MIDI, as in movement II's harp harmonics. A voice with no engravers plays the sounding B♭5, so score.midi stays byte-identical:

Notation follows Ex. II-73: the stopped B♭3, the diamond E♭4 and the sounding B♭5 small and in parentheses on the first bar. Page 3 keeps its layout and the score stays at 16 pages. For finale.md: from v277 the Vln I B♭5 at 43-50 is an artificial harmonic, the symphony's first string harmonic (no other movement has one). It is not a quotation and not a theme.

### 2. 51-66 and 115-122 Vln II pp pizz. double notes: mark "pizz. div." (applied)

Problem. Under the trumpet solo (51-66) and the bassoon solo (115-122), Vln II pluck thirds and sixths pp on beats 2 and 4 with only "pizz." printed, while the violas beside them say "pizz. div.". Adler PDF 30: without "div." the player is right to play the double note as a double stop. PDF 57 (Ex. II-59): a plucked chord is rolled from the bottom up unless the composer asks otherwise. So every second-violin off-beat becomes a small two-note roll under a pp solo, while the violas pluck single notes on the same beat. Some intervals also need awkward stops for a pp pluck, for example E♭4-F4 at 55 and 61 (E♭4 on the G string in third position).

Fix. sOne.ily `sOneVlnTwo`: "pizz." → "pizz. div."; dev.ily `devVlnTwo` at 115: "pizz." → "pizz. div.". PDF 30 also requires "unis." where the divisi ends: sTwo.ily 67 "arco, cantabile" → "arco, unis., cantabile"; dev.ily 127 "arco" → "arco, unis.". The single notes inside 115-122 (117, 119, 121) are then played by both halves. No note changes, and the MIDI is identical.

### 3. Considered and not applied

- 91-94, "al tallone" over the ff down-bow strokes (PDF 42, Ex. II-28, II-29). Every accented quarter there already has a down-bow, and players take a series of down-bows at the frog by habit. PDF 38 tells the orchestrator to mark only what counters the players' habit, so the extra words would be overmarking.
- 224-225 Vln II, undulating tremolo E5-G5 in place of the divided bowed tremolo (PDF 51, Brahms 1/iv 31-32). This is the one place where the reading's Brahms device could go, because tremolo is allowed only from 223. But the bars are inside the protected arrival (223-234; c… "violin tremolo mf at 224-225"), and a bowed divided tremolo at mf after the fff arrival is the stronger sound there. Kept.
- 95-98 violas. After "non div." at 91 they hold B♭3-E♭4 (later E4) as a double stop: second finger on the G string, first on the D string, a standard fourth, and E♭→E is a half-step of the first finger. It is playable, and 95-98 is protected, so no "div." is added.
- 50→51 Vc/Cb have one beat to go from arco to pizz. after the long hold. That is enough for arco→pizz. (PDF 54, the easier direction), and the cellos' "unis., pizz." is printed.
