## Findings

### 1. 39-40: the exposition's half cadence on V of E♭ has no timpani. Retune the A♭ drum to B♭ and roll the dominant with the wind bass (applied)

- **Bars:** 37 (mark), 39-40 (roll), 43 (mark back).
- **Problem:** P2 ends on V of E♭ at 39. Every bass part holds B♭ (contrabassoon, bass trombone, cellos, basses), the horns and trombones hold the dominant f with a diminuendo, and this is the medial caesura before the second theme. The timpani stop at 36 and stay silent until 90, because their four pitches (G, A♭, C, E♭) do not include B♭. Every other dominant arrival in the movement has the timpani: 7-10, 147-152 (V of C, roll ff), 219-222 and 261-262. So the exposition's one structural half cadence is the only one scored without them.
- **Evidence:** The timpani's role is "to strengthen the tonic and dominant bass notes and participate in strong tutti passages, especially in consummating climactic cadence points" (Adler ch. 12, PDF 79). Pedal drums "change pitches very quickly and easily. This innovation has greatly enhanced the function and potential of the instrument" (PDF 79). The change is marked in the rest (PDF 81, Ex. XII-51), and a long roll is tied to the stroke where it ends (PDF 80, Ex. XII-48). The 28" drum covers F2-C3 (PDF 79, Ex. XII-46), so B♭2 lies in its middle. The movement already retunes once (c236, 206/215), so this is the same technique used a second time, not a new rule. Dvořák 9/iv (corpus) does the same: its timpani take the dominant at each half cadence.
- **Fix (pTwo.ily, applied):**
  `pTwoTimp = { c4->\ff r4 r2 | R1*3 | c4-> r4 r2 | R1 | R1 | g,4-> g,4 g,4 r4 | aes,4->\f r4 r2 | es4 r4 aes,4 r4 | R1^\markup \small \italic "muta A♭ in B♭" | R1 | bes,1:32\f\>~ | bes,2:32~ bes,4:32\p r4 | R1*2 | }`
  trans.ily, new: `transTimp = { R1^\markup \small \italic "muta B♭ in A♭" | R1*7 | }`
- **Decisions:** The roll follows the contrabassoon, bass trombone and Hn 3·4 (f >, released at the end of 40), not the strings, which carry 41-42 alone. So the pp transition at 43, built as one harmonic spectrum with the harp chime (c206, c264, c276), stays as it is and has no timpani. The A♭ drum's last stroke is at 36.3, so the player has two and a half bars to retune. It goes back to A♭ at 43 and is next needed at 111. The bar-1 label and the opening tuning are not changed.
- **Note for the composer:** coherence/finale.md, section "Timpani", should read: "G, A♭, C, E♭ throughout except 37-43 (A♭ drum on B♭, roll 39-40) and 206-215 (C drum on D♭)". Two retunings, not one. This finding changes no theme or quotation.

### 2. 178: the pp motive x near the rim, which quotes the scherzo's soft ending, is played with hard sticks. Mark soft sticks for it (applied)

- **Bars:** 171 (mark), 178 (mark back on the closing rest).
- **Problem:** The only stick indications are "hard sticks" at 1, "medium sticks" at 215 and "hard sticks" at 255. So the player strikes 178 with the hard sticks of bar 1. That bar is the soft form of the scherzo's last statements of motive x (finale.md item 24), and the scherzo's timpanist plays those with soft sticks. Hard sticks near the rim at pp give a dry click. Soft sticks give the round pp that the scherzo has and that the near-the-rim marking asks for.
- **Evidence:** "special timpani mallets ... come in hard, medium, and soft varieties", and the tone depends on the mallet as well as the beating spot (Adler ch. 12, PDF 80). The composer names them in words (PDF 61, 88) and gives time to change (ch. 14, PDF 110-111; ch. 12, PDF 61 note). There are seven bars of rest before 178 and twenty after it before the ff tutti at 199.
- **Fix (recS.ily, applied):** `recSTimp = { R1^\markup \small \italic "soft sticks" | R1*6 | g,4\pp_\markup \small \italic "near the rim" g,8 g,8 g,4 r4^\markup \small \italic "hard sticks" | R1*18 | ...`. "hard sticks" is printed on 178's closing rest so that it shows in the score: the timpani staff is hidden in the systems from 179 to 198. Notes, dynamics and MIDI are unchanged.
