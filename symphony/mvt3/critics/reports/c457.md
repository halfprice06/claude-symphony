## Findings

### 1. 87–88: Bn 1 doubling the violas' suspension line (tested, not applied)

Problem examined: at 87–88 the violins carry the scale figure f (Vln II in octaves below Vln I, from B♭4 down to D4 and back up to B♭4). Bn 1 plays the upper voice of the 6/3 chain f (`es'2~ es'4 d'4~ | d'4 c'4 bes4 bes4~`) at the unison with the upper violas (mf, divided), in the same E♭4–B♭3 register as Vln II's figure. This is the case Sebesky describes in Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13): a background line in the lower-middle register taken out so that the main element comes forward. A&S PDF 291–292 supports a lighter middle of the chain, since in a 6/3 series only its start and end articulate.

Test: in /tmp/critic-457/work, `closBsnOne` 87–88 became `R1 | r2 bes2\f~ |`, so Bn 1 enters on 88.3 with the preparation of the last 7–6 and keeps the seventh A♭3 at 89–90. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, and par8.py on 85–91 shows only bass doublings, the same as v457. On the shared staff Bn 2 printed "2." at 87, and Bn 1 entered f at 88.3, the same level as Bn 2.

Verdict, not applied: the result is not clearly better.
- The 7–6 suspensions at 87.3 and 88.1 are the bridge's only dissonance (c062). Without the reed they rest on half the violas at mf, under a bass of four instruments at f and violins at f. Sebesky's criterion is a line that "detracts" (PDF 13), and this one holds the suspensions across the barline.
- The Bn 1 line at 87–90 has been on the do-not-touch list since c167 (c058). A change there needs a clear gain, which this test does not show.
- c377 already lets the violins carry the figure alone at 87–88 and hands it to the winds at 89–90, so the focus shift Sebesky asks for (PDF 14) is in place.

The scratch copy is back to v457. clos.ily is identical to the v457 file.

### 2. Checked, no change

- **187–194 lower-middle register (Ex. 1, focus).** Four layers sit between E♭3 and F4: violas and Hn 1 on F2's lower octave, cellos and Bn 1 on the counter, Cl 1's rocking eighths, and Cl 2 held. The tune is at the unison in Vln I, Vln II and Ob 1 above this, and the counter lies a sixth or more under the tune's lower octave, so they do not collide. Thinning the clarinets was declined in c357 (after c207 and c224). The scoring of 187–198 is protected, and I found no new reason to reopen it.
- **159–166 focus.** Three off-beat groups play under F1's consequent: Hn 3·4 f, Tpt 1·2 mf and Tpt 3 mf. The trumpets enter at 159 by design (c169: the recapitulation grows by addition), one level under the horns (c166), and they rest from 167 for the muted solo. The background is busy but stays under the theme, which is in four octaves. No change.
- **Harmonic variety at 153–158 and 189–194 (Ex. 3/3A).** 153–158 repeat 13–18's chords, and 189–194 repeat 69–74's chords a fifth lower. In both cases the opening bars and the cadence approach already differ (151 over the G pedal, 159 V4/3, 163 N6; 188 V4/2 of IV). A recapitulation keeping its theme's harmony is normal Classical practice, and Sebesky asks for variety "whenever possible", not in every bar. A further reharmonization belongs to lens 2 and would touch protected scoring. No change.
- **67–74 counter in unison (Vla, Vc, Hn 1 pp).** The three instruments split at 70.3 and 72.3–73, so they are two lines, not a redundant doubling. Hn 1 is a colour choice (c287) that sets this statement apart from 187. No change.
