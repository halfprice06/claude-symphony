## Findings

### 1. 211-214: the D♭ episode has no rhythmically independent accompaniment (applied)

Problem. In the eight bars of theme B (Largamente, 2 = 72, about 13 s) every part either doubles the trumpets' theme in its own rhythm (Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II in thirds and sixths) or holds whole notes; the only other motion is the violas' repeated half notes, which fall on the theme's own half-note beats. Where the theme holds a dotted half (212, 214) nothing moves at all. From 215 the score already does what the reading asks: the violins rise by scale under the held E♭-D♭ (215-216), and the horns and cor anglais answer under the held D♭ (217-218). 212 and 214 are the two holds that are left empty.

Evidence. Adler calls rhythmic independence "perhaps the most crucial" point of accompaniment (PDF 259) and praises Tchaikovsky's figuration, which "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part" (PDF 260, Ex. XVII-3). In Mendelssohn's Ex. XVII-4 (PDF 260, image) short accompanying figures enter in the strings where the solo line holds, while the winds sustain. In Ex. XVII-15 (PDF 274) "the great contrast in the rhythmic activity" of a middle-register bassoon against the solo gives the passage its clarity. Ex. XVII-5 (PDF 261, image) moves an inner string figure under a sustained wind chord and the theme. In 211-214 all the inner brass and bassoons are sustained chords, so a moving line in the tenor costs nothing in support.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-227/work/sections/codaTwo.ily). The tenor answers the theme's holds with two short slurred figures in Hn 3·4 and Bn 1, f, inside the existing ff > f path. At 212 a descending tetrachord A♭4-G♭4-F4-E♭4 (5-4-3-2 of D♭) leads to D♭4 at 213. At 214 the line rises A♭3-B♭3-C4, and the violins' rising scale at 215 takes over the motion. The result is a relay: tenor (212), tenor (214), violins (215-216), horn echo (217-218).

```
codaTwoHnThree = { aes1\ff\> | aes'4(\f ges'4 f'4 es'4) | des'1 | \partCombineApart aes4( bes4 c'2) \breathe | \partCombineAutomatic R1*4 | ...
codaTwoHnFour  = { aes1\ff\> | aes'4(\f ges'4 f'4 es'4) | bes1 | ges1 \breathe | R1*4 | ...
codaTwoBsnOne  = { f1\ff\> | aes4(\f ges4 f4 es4 | bes1) | aes4( bes4 c'2) \breathe | aes1 | ...
```

The previous parts were Hn 3·4 A♭3/A♭3 at 212, D♭4/G♭3 at 213 and C4/G♭3 at 214, and Bn 1 F3 at 212 and C4 at 214. Bn 1 plays the 212 figure an octave below the horns and joins Hn 3 at pitch at 214. Hn 4 keeps the A♭7's seventh G♭3 at 214, and Bn 1 still resolves to A♭3 at 215. Voice leading: 212.4→213.1 E♭→D♭/B♭ against Tpt 2 E♭5→F5 goes from an octave to a third and a fifth. 213→214 Hn 3 D♭4→A♭3 against the bass G♭→A♭ goes from a fifth to an octave in contrary motion. I tested two endings and rejected both. Continuing the line B♭3→A♭3 into 215 makes fifths with the theme (F5→E♭5). Continuing C4→A♭3 in the horn makes octaves with Tbn 2 (C3→A♭2). The horn line therefore stops on C4 with the breath the whole orchestra takes after 214 (c028).

Protections answered. The theme B recall 211-218, the D♭ episode and the G♭6-F6 suspension (c151, c155, c157, c171, c198) are untouched: theme notes, all doublings, Vln I and the 211 suspension are unchanged, and so is the ff-f dynamic path (c178). ### 2. 95-96: Cl 1 holds B♭4 directly under the oboe's first solo (applied)

Problem. The oboe's first solo line in the movement, E♭5-D5-D♭5-C5 (95-98, mf > p, c221), has Cl 1 holding B♭4 beneath it at 95-96. That is a fourth under E♭5 and a minor third under D♭5. It is a held note against a slow line, so there is no rhythmic contrast to separate the two, and oboe and clarinet blend in this register.

Evidence. Adler, PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible ... especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". PDF 267 (Mozart): when the soloist plays softly the orchestra is "reduced drastically". B♭ is already in the violas (B♭3), so Cl 1 adds no chord tone.

Fix (applied in clos.ily): Cl 1 rests 95-98, and Cl 2 carries the clarinet staff ("2." prints at 95).

```
closClOne = { ... | bes'2-> r2 | \partCombineSoloII R1*4 \partCombineAutomatic | }
```

The previous ending was `bes'1\mp\>~ | bes'1 | \partCombineSoloII R1\! | R1 \partCombineAutomatic |`. The chord under the solo is still complete: E♭ (Vc, Cb, Bn 2), B♭3/E♭4 (Vla), G4 (Cl 2, Vln II). Its top is now G4, a sixth below E♭5. This answers c224: its mp > still applies to every remaining part of the held chord. It also answers c221: the violas' E♭4→E4 at 97 and the oboe are unchanged, and E4 is now even more exposed.
