### 4. Relay from the scherzo thread: the 6–♭6–5 line for the cor anglais alone at 273–275, considered and declined
- **Bars:** 273–275 (protected 273–279).
- **Problem raised:** The scherzo (358–359) and movement II (87–89) give 6–♭6–5 to the cor anglais alone. The finale could do the same.
- **Evidence against:** At 273 the whole orchestra is ff at Largamente. Tpt 1 and Hn 3 hold C5, three trombones and the tuba are on the chord, and the strings play ff tremolo. A cor anglais alone cannot carry a line there. Unit 28's coda principle (§15, PDF 495) and Rimsky-Korsakov's balance rules both point to a line doubled in several octaves, which the finale already has (Fl 2, Cl 2, Tbn 1, lower violas, Tpt 2). Taking the line out of those parts to make it a solo would thin the final chain (c091, c217, c250). Adding the cor anglais as one more doubling (`finaleCorAnglais = { R1*26 | r2 a'2\ff | aes'1 | g'1~ | g'1 | R1*3 }`) would be inaudible inside Cl 2's unison. It would also break c430's reason: the cor anglais's last notes are theme B's echo and the sub. p at 217–220, the instrument of movement II's theme B taking leave. The link holds without it. The same scale degrees on the same chord carry it, and one movement's soloist turning into the finale's tutti is the arc the symphony aims for.
- **Decision:** Not applied. Record the link in finale.md (Finding 3, item 8).
