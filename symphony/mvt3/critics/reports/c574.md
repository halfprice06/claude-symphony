## Findings

### 1. Bar 271: the trumpets enter in the register of the brass F1 head (applied)

Problem. At 269–272 movement I's head runs in two octaves above the F1 head. The F1 head is in the violas, Bn 1, Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 a2 ff, and an octave lower in the cellos. At 271, Tpt 1·2 re-enter ff on D4 (`d'4( g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4)`), in the lower octave of the head with Vln II, Ob 2 and Cl 1·2. This puts two different brass lines at ff in the same register. The trumpets' D4 sits a second under the trombones' and horns' E4 (271.1–2), and their G4–A4 lies just above it. In the bar where the trombones join the F1 head (the step up that Barber uses, PDF 150), that head is covered by the trumpets in their dull low register. The weighting is also lopsided. At 271 the upper octave has Vln I, Ob 1 and the flutes in their weak D5–G5 register. The lower octave has six parts, two of them trumpets. Mozart's lesson (PDF 151) is that the heavily scored octave is the one the ear takes as the theme, and that each wind goes where every note it plays is effective. Adler PDF 156 adds that a colour is heard best when it enters after a rest or on an anacrusis.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-574/work/sections/finale.ily, finaleTptOne and finaleTptTwo, bar 271):
```
\partCombineApart R1*2 | \partCombineAutomatic r2 b'4(\ff d''4) | a''2-> g''4( f''4) \breathe |
```
(was `... \partCombineAutomatic d'4(\ff g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4) | ...`). The trumpets now enter on 271.3 with B4–D5 as an anacrusis to their A5 at 272, above the F1 head. The head is heard clearly in horns and trombones in the bar where the trombones are added. The line is still complete in Vln II, Ob 2 and Cl 1·2. This does not touch the protected horn and trombone rests at 265–272, the IV6/4 at 272, the allargando or the cut. The trumpets' A5 at 272 and the crescendo into 273 are unchanged. No quotation, F1, F2 or chorale line changes.

### 2. Bar 164: tuba octave skip G2→G1 (tested on paper, not applied)

Barber leaves octave skips out of the heavy brass (PDF 150, point 1), and c214 removed the bass trombone's G2–G3 leaps here. The tuba still plays `g,2 g,,2` at 164, with the contrabassoon and basses. I kept it. The drop leads the bass down to the deceptive E♭1 at 166 (Barber's point 4, the low entry that makes a close final). The bass trombone already holds G2, so the leap is a bass-line event, not a doubled skip in the middle of the texture.

### 3. Bar 267: enlarge the F1 head's doubling with the oboes (considered, declined)

Barber enlarges the doubling at each statement (PDF 150). The head at 267 has the same scoring as at 265. The enlargement at 269 and 271 already follows Barber.
