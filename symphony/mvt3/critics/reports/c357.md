## Findings

### 1. 188: F2's second bar has the same harmony in all ten statements; the A♭-major statement now gets a falling chromatic bass (applied)

**Problem.** F2's head (a held fifth, then a turn through the tonic) is harmonized i/I - V6/5 → i/I wherever it appears: 52, 60, 68, 76, 116, 124, 172, 180, 188, 196. In the recapitulation's A♭ statement the bass at 187-189 is A♭ | G-A♭ | F. The bass only names chord roots and changes direction twice in two bars. Sebesky's rule (PDF 19) is to avoid "harmonizing a melody the same way twice, whenever possible". His Ex. 3A keeps the tune and gives it a bass that falls by step and by semitone (B-B♭-A, F♯-F-E) to "the same resting place". PDF 20 recommends a substitution where the phrase needs "a little lift", and PDF 14 lets the bass rise from the tertiary to the secondary focus level. The texture lens leads to the same place. The tune climbs C5-E♭5 on 188.3-4 and leaps to its peak B♭5 at 189, while the bass falls A♭ → G and then rises back to A♭. That turn upward wastes the chance of contrary motion between the outer voices (Ex. 1, PDF 13).

**Fix (applied).** 188 beat 3: the bass takes G♭ instead of A♭, so the bar is V6/5 → V4/2 of IV (A♭7 over G♭), and the bass falls A♭-G-G♭-F from 187 to 189 under the rising tune. 189 is unchanged; it already has D♭3 in the cellos and Bn 1 over F, so the A♭7's seventh G♭ falls to F and its third C3 (cellos, Bn 1 on 188.3) rises to D♭3. That is the normal resolution, and the phrase reaches the same resting place. recS.ily, bar 188 only:
- `recSCb`: `\transpose c aes, { b,,2 c,2 }` → `g,,2 ges,,2`
- `recSBsnTwo`: `\transpose c aes, { b,2 c2 }` → `g,2 ges,2`

Nothing else changes: the tune (Vln I·II, Ob 1, violas, Hn 1), the counter-melody (cellos, Bn 1), Cl 1·2's background, the dynamics, the slurs and the 187-198 bow plan. The other upper notes on 188.3-4 (A♭3, C4, E♭3, E♭4, C5, E♭5) all belong to A♭7. Checks: the chromatic step stays in one voice (A&S PDF 420), and no other part has G♮ against the G♭. Outer voices: E♭5 over G♭ (sixth) goes to B♭5 over F (eleventh) in contrary motion. Cellos C3 → D♭3 against the bass G♭ → F is contrary motion. Cl 2 E♭3 → F3 reaches an octave over F by contrary motion. par8.py over 186-190 reports only Bn 2's own octave doubling of the bass, as in v357 (G2-A♭2 then, G2-G♭2-F2 now). 68 and 196 keep V6/5 → I, so the A♭ statement differs from its exposition model, and its second phrase (195-196) differs from its first.

Protection. HISTORY lists "187-198 strings and bassoons" (c207, c210) and "187-198 scoring as of v225" (c224). Those entries protect who plays what, the clarinet background and the bow plan. This fix moves one pitch in two parts that already play the bass, so none of those reasons is touched. Pitch changes inside 187-198 were accepted after those entries (c262 at 192, c270 at 197, c293 at 198). F2's tune is unchanged. Its harmony changes in one statement, and finale.md's F2 paragraph should say: "188: V6/5 - V4/2 of IV, bass A♭-G-G♭-F (since v358); every other statement keeps V6/5 - I."

### 3. The minor-mode line cliché of Ex. 3 (checked, no change)

The score uses Sebesky's chromatic inner line inside a held chord in major twice (95-97 and 235-242), but never in minor. The only places where a minor tonic is held under a held melodic note are the heads of F2 (51, 115, 171). There the line C-B-B♭-A (or G-F♯-F-E) arrives at the sixth A on the beat where the tune moves to E♭. A-E♭ then goes to G-D at the next downbeat, a diminished fifth moving to a perfect fifth in similar motion; c312 removed that motion at 178. The other resolution, A to A♭, would make the stop chord B-D-F-A♭ resolve to C before 222→223. 51-66 is protected in any case. I decided not to add it.
