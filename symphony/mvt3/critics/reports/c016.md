## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Held triple stop in the violas at the last chord; unmarked tritone double stops at 135–138 and 219–222
- Bars: 270–271 (Vla), 135–138 (Vla), 219–222 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla).
- Problem: at 270 the violas hold `<g e' c''>1->\fff~` into 271. A triple stop is rolled and only two strings sound after the roll (Adler PDF 77, footnote to Ex. III-11). The violins on the same chord are already marked "div. a3"; the violas are not, so the G or C will drop out. At 135–138 the violas play four bars of descending chromatic tritones pp (`<f' b'>`, `<e' bes'>`, `<es' a'>`, `<d' aes'>`), and at 219–222, after the sub. p, all three upper string parts hold four bars of tritones and sixths in a long crescendo to the arrival (`<fis'' c'''>`, `<f'' b''>`; `<c'' fis''>`, `<d'' aes''>`; `<c' dis'>`, `<b f'>`). Chromatic tritone double stops are in Adler's list of possible stops (Ex. III-10) but not "the most easily accessible" ones he restricts orchestral writing to, and he gives the remedy: "passages that would be quite difficult for one performer are quite simple played divisi" (PDF 76). Each pair is sustained and exposed, so intonation is the issue.
- Fix (applied): finale.ily `<g e' c''>1->\fff~^\markup \italic "div. a3"`. retr.ily: `<f' b'>1\pp\<^\markup \italic "div."` at 135 and `d'1\p^\markup \italic "unis."` at 139. codaTwo.ily: "div." on the first chord at 219 in Vln I, Vln II and Vla; Vln I `b''2^\markup \italic "unis."` at 222. arrival.ily: Vln II `g'1\ff^\markup \italic "unis."` at 226 (Vln II stays divided through the 223 tremolo; the violas already have "unis." at 224). No pitch changes.

### 2. Cellos and basses in bass clef with three and four ledger lines, 27–38
- Problem: in the bass statement of F1 (27–34) and the sequence after it (35–38) the cellos are in bass clef up to A♭4 (33) and B♭4 (36), mostly in eighths. Adler's clef chart (PDF 96, Ex. III-53) takes the cello out of bass clef at about F4/G4 and into tenor clef. The double basses double the line an octave lower and are written up to A♭4 and G4 (33–34), three ledger lines; Adler: "Change of clef is suggested when there are three or more ledger lines" (PDF 104). Page 3 of v16 shows the cellos' eighths at 35–38 hanging above the staff.
- Fix (applied, pTwo.ily): `pTwoVc = { \clef tenor c'2->(\ff …` and `\clef bass bes,1\>` at 39; `pTwoCb = { \clef tenor c2->(\ff …` and `\clef bass aes,,1` at 35. In tenor clef the highest cello note (B♭4) needs two ledger lines and the basses (written D3–A♭4) need at most one. The do-not-touch bass statement 27–34 is unchanged in sound.

### 3. Violins I leap from B4 to G6 between 144 and 145
- Problem: the first violins end the low statement of the F1 head with `b'4-.` (B4) and must play `g'''2->` (G6) on the next beat. Adler lists "wide skips between extreme ranges" as a standard problem passage for a section: "the entire left-hand position has to be completely altered … there are difficulties in simply hitting the correct pitch" (PDF 82–83, Ex. III-23). Here the shift is from first position on the A string to about seventh position on the E string, with a staccato gap only.
- Fix (applied, retr.ily bar 144): `f''4-.) es''8( d''8 c''4-.) r4 | g'''2->`. The B4 is still heard: the second violins hold `b'1\f` through 144. The rest gives the shift a quarter beat.

### 4. Violas run to B5 in 245–246 and drop to C4 with no gap
- Problem: the violas double the second violins in unison up to B5 (`g'8 … a''8 b''`), with a treble clef for a bar and a half, and then play C4 on the downbeat of 247. B5 is high on the A string where the viola's stretches and left-hand tension are greatest (PDF 85–86), and the B5→C4 drop is the same kind of extreme-register skip as finding 3 (PDF 82–83). The unison adds little: the line is already doubled at the octave by Vln I.
- Fix (applied, sHead.ily): `f2 r8 g8( a b | c'8 d' e' f' g'4 a'8 b')\! |`, removing both clef changes. The run now sounds in three octaves (Vla G3–B4, Vln II G4–B5, Vln I G5–B6), the violas cross over the cellos' held D3 cleanly, and they reach C4 at 247 by a seventh down from B4. This replaces the treble-clef layout accepted in cycle 5 (it is no longer needed). The all-pairs check shows only the new octave doubling Vla/Vln II at 245–246.

### 5. Long tied bass notes need "change bow at will"
- Problem: Adler: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter … This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will" (PDF 108). The score has three such places: 43–50, Vc and Cb `bes,1~` (`bes,,1~`) tied eight bars pp through the poco rit., about 11.7 s; 139–146, Cb `b,,1~` tied eight bars with a crescendo from p to f, about 11.4 s, and Vc `b,1~` tied 142–146 with the same crescendo (a crescendo to f on one bow is not possible at that length); 235–238, Cb `c,1~` tied four bars at 2 = 66, about 7.3 s.
- Fix (applied): `^\markup \italic "change bow at will"` on the first tied note in transVc, transCb (43), retrCb (139), retrVc (142) and sHeadCb (235). The ties stay, so the line sounds unbroken.

### 6. Viola pizzicato dyads at 51–66 and 115–122 without "div."
- Problem: the same accompaniment in the recapitulation (171) is marked "pizz. div." (cycle 6); the exposition (51) and the development's G-minor pizzicato (115) are not. Several of these dyads lie outside the first-position stops in Adler's viola table (Ex. III-33, PDF 88): A♭3–C♭4 (53, 54, 57, 63, 64) and G3–B♭3, G3–C4 (115–121) need the lower note on the C string in second or third position, because both notes cannot be on the G string. Divided, each player has one note, and the entries stay pp.
- Fix (applied): sOne.ily `"pizz. div."` at 51 and sTwo.ily `"arco, unis., espress."` at 67 (as recS 187); dev.ily `"pizz. div."` at 115 and `"arco, unis."` at 123.

### 7. Choose the A string for the violins' cantabile at 235–238
- Problem: the violins enter p "molto cantabile" with movement I's second-theme head on E5 held for a dotted half, which is the open E string unless the player chooses otherwise. Adler: open strings "stand out peculiarly" in slow expressive passages (PDF 73–74), and "in soft, lyrical, expressive passages, the composer will often prefer the higher pitches to be performed on the A-string rather than have the player cross to the E-string" (PDF 75). His example is Brahms 3/iii, marked "Sul A" (Ex. III-7), the same register and character. The phrase (E5–A5–C6) lies within the A string up to seventh position, which a section can play. At 239 the line goes up an octave (Vln I) or repeats f, where the E string is right.
- Fix (applied, sHead.ily): in both violin parts, `\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul A"` before `e''2.~(\startTextSpan` at 235, `\stopTextSpan` after `c'''2( b''2)` at 238, and `^\markup \italic "ord."` on the first note of 239.
