### 4. Beethoven 9/iv comparison: no further change
Shared devices: a dissonant ff outburst opens the finale (Beethoven's "Schreckensfanfare" on D minor with B♭; here the German sixth with A♭ over the scherzo's G♯); the strings answer it (Beethoven 8–16 in the low strings; here 3–4); earlier movements are recalled (Beethoven 30, 48, 63 in turn; here movement II's tail at 95, movement I's hammered tutti at 199, theme B at 211, movement I's second-theme head at 235, the horn call at 243, the scherzo head at 275); percussion is held back for the close (Beethoven's triangle, cymbals and bass drum in the last part; here the first crash at 223). One difference counts in this score's favour. Beethoven presents the joy theme bare in the low strings and adds counterpoints one by one (92, 116, 140, 164). This finale's strongest tune, the chorale, arrives fully scored at 223, but the earlier staged entries (7–10, 245–246, the brass stretto 265–272) do the accumulating work, and the six-stage coda is protected. A conductor would take the dramaturgy for a master's. The one weakness this lens found is the balance in Finding 1. Tested on paper and declined: a bass drum at 263 (declined in c385, and the crash and timpani already mark it); Beethoven's Prestissimo acceleration at the end (the Più allegro 263 → Largamente 273 shape is Brahms 1/iv's and is protected).
