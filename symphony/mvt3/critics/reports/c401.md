## Findings

No finding changes the score. After four passes on this chapter, the score already follows every rule the chapter states about registers, breath and dynamics. The two techniques it does not use have no place in this score where they would clearly improve the music. As the task allows, I applied nothing, and I did not test edits for the sake of having some.

**1. 141, 145, 187–194 and 95–98: no theme in the low clarinets (Adler PDF 195, Ex. VII-59; PDF 194).** Tchaikovsky 5 gives its motto to two clarinets in unison in the chalumeau, p. That colour is dark and soft, and it makes a motto easy to remember. The finale never uses it: the clarinets have only part of F2, in the clarino, at 126–127. I checked every quiet thematic place.
- 95–98: movement II's tail. The low clarinet unison was rejected twice (c221, c311) because it covers the violas' E♭→E thread.
- 141: the clarinet doubles Ob 1 on F1's head at G5, the stretto's woodwind entry. Moving it to the chalumeau (G3) would put it under Vc and Hn 1, which have the same head at 139–140, and the stretto would lose its upward registral climb (c397).
- 145: Cl 1 already doubles Hn 3·4 at G4, f, in its chalumeau and throat register, where it adds body but cannot be heard on its own (PDF 194: throat tones are "rather pale").
- 187–194: the clarinets' pp broken chords are the background to F2 in A♭, and both the line and its harmony are protected (c357, c370).
- The coda (199–279) is loud tutti.

Decision: not applied. No unprotected soft thematic passage exists where low clarinets would sound fresh rather than duplicate another colour.

**2. 181–183: the cor anglais reaches written D6 at F2's C-minor peak (Adler PDF 205, Ex. VII-47).** At the top the cor anglais "sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality". At 182 it plays concert G5 (written D6), and it plays F2's whole third phrase high. But Fl 1 and the muted Tpt 1 double it at the unison in exactly these bars (c011, c041), so the thin top is covered and the doubling carries the peak. The rich low register of PDF 205 is used at 217–220. Lowering only the cor anglais at 181–183 would break the protected cor anglais / muted trumpet duet (171–186, c004, c221) and the record line for the duet's link to movement I 171–174. Kept.

**3. 116–122: the bassoon solo follows PDF 210.** The peak A4 at 118 sits in the "thin but intense" band, below the pinched B4. It is marked mp, with pizz. strings pp and Vln I's "2 soli" pp answers two octaves above. No change.

**4. Breath and rests in oboe, flute and contrabassoon (PDF 188, 198, 214).** The longest stretches without a rest are:
- Ob 1, Fl 1 and Cl 1: 211–223, with breaths after 214 and 218 and with 224–225 free.
- Bn 1 and Cbsn: 211–230, whole notes with a breath every four bars (c396).
- Cor anglais: 171–186, with phrase breaths after 174, 178 and 182.

All are within Adler's limits. No change.

**5. The flute's low octave is not used (PDF 186, Ex. VII-3, VII-4).** The Dvořák and Brahms examples work because almost nothing else is playing. Every thin passage in this finale already has its solo colour, chosen for a cyclic reason: trumpet at 51, oboe at 95, bassoon at 116, harp, violas and clarinet at 123–130, cor anglais at 171. A low flute would compete with one of them. Not applied.

**6. Melodic check of the themes (lens 1), against this reading's solo examples.** F1, F2 and the chorale top lines are unchanged and in the record. Each statement of F2 sits in the strongest register of its instrument: the trumpet solo B♭4–B♭5; the violins sul A at 189–192; the bassoon below its pinched fifth; the cor anglais in its middle register, with the peak doubled. That is the principle of PDF 184, "the part ... could not be performed on any other instrument". I changed none of F1, F2, the chorale top line or any quotation, so the other movement threads have nothing to update.
