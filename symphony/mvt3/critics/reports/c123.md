### 1. 265–268: the top line repeats itself and leaves holes (applied)
Problem: Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I·II play movement I's head rhythm on G–E–D–C–B–C at 265–266 and then exactly the same at 267–268, same pitches and same scoring. Each statement stops on beat 1 of 266 and 268 and rests three beats. Right after the fff cadence, this makes the passage stop and start twice at one height. F1 underneath already rises a third at 267 (cycle 121); the top line does not.
Evidence: PDF 117–118: an idea is scored differently each time it recurs, by "alteration of details (the most frequent method)". PDF 125–126: in transference, the last note of one part meets the first note of the next. Here the 268 line breaks off and 269's C6 starts from nothing.
Fix (finale.ily, Fl 1, Fl 2, Vln I, bar 268): `c'''4->) r4 r2 |` → `c'''4->) e'''8( d'''8 c'''4 b''4) |`. The line runs E–D–C–B into the F1 head's C6 at 269, in tenths above F1's D–C–B–A–G. The implied harmony over the pedal is ii7–I–V7–IV–V, so the leading tone resolves into 269. Vln II, Ob 1 and Cl 1 keep their rest, because they take F1 at C5 in 269 and would otherwise leap down a seventh. The first statement (265–266) keeps its gap, so the passage closes up gradually: gap, then continuous. The movement I quotation (finale.md item 11: one bar plus one note) is unchanged; only the bar after it is filled. This is not a change to F1, F2 or the chorale line.

### 2. 265–272: dynamic plateau, and a drop at the Largamente (applied)
Problem: themes and accompaniment are both ff at 265, and nothing changes for eight bars. The accompaniment is Cb repeated eighths, the timpani pulse, Tpt 3 strokes, Bn 2 and tuba strokes. The analysis shows 12.5 then 13.1 voices on average, which is flat. Hn 1·2 rest at 269 as Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 enter, so the instrument count does not grow either. Evidence: PDF 116 and 133: the harmonic basis should differ from the melody in intensity and "should be of inferior dynamic power". PDF 130: a long crescendo is built by adding instruments. PDF 127: the added group's crescendo is reached faster. PDF 135: percussion at ff "is sufficient to overpower any orchestral tutti"; the timpani pulse at ff sits on the theme.
