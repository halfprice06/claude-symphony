## Findings

### 1. The bassoons' F1 statement at 33–38 is in bass clef in the score, on up to four ledger lines (applied)

- **Bars:** 33–38, Bn 1·2 shared staff.
- **Problem:** Gould PDF 21 names the bassoon as one of the instruments that use the tenor clef. He says to stay in one clef only while three ledger lines are enough. In the score, Bn 1's ff eighths at 33–36 reach A♭4 and B♭4, which is 3–4 ledger lines above the bass staff. These are the same notes that the cellos play on the staff below, and the cellos have been in tenor clef here since c016 for this same reason. c306 gave the clef to the Bn 1 part only, so the score and the part show different clefs. The score most likely kept bass clef because Bn 2 shares the staff. In tenor clef, however, Bn 2's lowest notes (A♭2 at 35, B♭2 at 37–38) need only two ledger lines and one ledger line. In bass clef Bn 1 needed four.
- **Evidence:** v365 page 2, second system: the eighths at 35–36 hang above the staff. In the scratch build, page 2 has the whole run inside the staff, and page 3 shows the bass clef returning before 39, where Bn 1 holds D4 and Bn 2 B♭2.
- **Fix (applied, sections/pTwo.ily):** I removed the `\tag #'part { }` wrappers. The clefs now apply to the score as well as the part:
  `… g4-. | \clef tenor f4( aes8 c'8 f'4 aes'4) | … ges8( es'8 f'8 d'8) | \clef bass d'1~\> | …`
  The clef stands at 33, where a new slur starts after the 32 group (Gould PDF 22, between phrases), and goes back at 39, the same barline where the cellos return to bass (PDF 22, 23). The Bn 1 part is unchanged because it already had these clefs, and the Bn 2 part is unchanged. A c365 comment records the change.
- **Build:** 0 warnings, 16 pages, score.midi byte-identical to v365. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, the same as before. par8.py on 33–38 gives the same output as for v365 (only the existing bass doublings in Bn 2 and the bass trombone at 36–37). No theme, quotation or protected bar is touched.

### 2. Checked, no change: the basses' tenor clef at 27–34

In bass clef the written line would need at most three ledger lines (F4 at 30 and 33, G4 at 34), so Gould PDF 21 would allow bass clef. c335 kept the tenor clef because it shows the basses' unison with the cellos note for note. That reason still holds, and finding 1 now gives the bassoon its tenor clef in the same bars. I did not re-raise it.

### 3. Checked, no change: string harmonics (PDF 25)

The score uses the diamond notehead only once (Vln I, 43–50). I looked for another soft place where a harmonic would fit the style: 95–98, 114, 167–170 and 219–222. Each one is protected, or the change would alter scoring that c340/c346 settled. Adding a harmonic would be an orchestration change, not a notation one, so I left it out.
