## Findings

### 1. 76: the harp's D1 sits on a string with no pedal and the part never says how to tune it (applied)

Problem. Since v404 the harp's left hand doubles the bass at 75–76 as `g,,2 f,,4 es,,4 | d,,2 es,,2` under the 8va bassa line, so beat 1 of 76 sounds D1. On a 47-string harp D1 is the second-lowest string. Adler says the two lowest strings "are not affected by the pedal mechanism and have to be tuned manually" (PDF 111, footnote). The D pedal in the 67 diagram is natural, but that pedal does not reach this string; nothing in the score or part tells the harpist to tune it to D♮ before the performance, so the note may sound D♭ (the basic C♭-major tuning, PDF 110) under V6/5 of E♭. c116 had already met this problem at 242 and solved it by removing the D1 and its tuning note; v404 brought a D1 back without noticing.

Evidence. Adler PDF 110 (C♭-major basic tuning), PDF 111 footnote (pedal-less lowest strings), PDF 112 (show the setting at the start of the work). HISTORY c116: "no low D1 string (242 now D2-D3, tuning note at 67 removed)". finale.md line 68 (v404).

Fix. Keep the notes (the D1–E♭1 step is the bass line that c403 built, and the harp's octave doubling with the basses is the colour of 67–82) and add the tuning instruction at the harp's first entry, in score and part, where the starting pedal setting already stands (sections/trans.ily, transHarpL, bar 43):

```
... ^\markup \small \italic "harm. (sound 8va)" _\markup \small "low D string (D1): tune D♮" } r4 r2 | ...
```

The string is not used at any other pitch in the movement, so one tuning before the start covers it. Alternative tested on paper and rejected: harp LH 76 `d,2 es,2` (a major-seventh leap from E♭1 and a sixth back to G1 at 77, breaking the doubling for one bar). Applied in /tmp/critic-476/work. MIDI byte-identical; the text prints under the left-hand staff at 43 without collision (page 3).
