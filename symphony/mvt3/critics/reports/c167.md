## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 91-94: the codetta's string chords sit inside the brass, and Vln II and the violas play the same dyad (applied)
**Problem.** At the ff codetta all the brass enter with the hammered rhythm. Tpt 1-2 play B♭4/G4, Tpt 3 E♭4, Hn 1-2 G4/E♭4, and the trombones and woodwinds fill G3-G6. The strings play double stops in the same register: Vln I B♭4/G5-A♭5, Vln II E♭4/B♭4, violas E♭4/B♭4 "non div." So Vln II and the violas double each other at the unison, and the violins' top note (G5) lies a tenth under the flutes' G6 and level with Ob 2. No string sound is above the brass. Sebesky PDF 168: "In any large mixed ensemble in which strings, reeds and brass are combined, the strings are acoustically the weakest element. In order to maintain as much string tone as possible ... the strings should be voiced as simply as possible in the high register." In Ex. 215 (PDF 169-170) the strings start on the brass chord and then climb above it. In Ex. 216 (PDF 171-173) the final chord has the strings "voiced very simply in the high register, where they can be heard above the big band". PDF 145 also keeps double stops out of the lead line.
**Fix (applied).** clos.ily: Vln I 91-94 play single notes at the top with Fl 1:

### 2. 159-165: the counter-line outweighs the tune's middle octave (applied)
**Problem.** The history explains the imbalance. c107 gave Vln II the slurred counter-line, and c108 restated it ff "espress." with Vln I still in unison on the tune. c109 then divided Vln I in octaves, so each octave of the consequent has half of the first violins: the C6 strand is 2 Fl + Vln I upper and the C5 strand is Ob 1 + Vln I lower. The counter-line runs in that same C5 octave (E♭5-D5-C5-B4 against the tune's C5 at 159, F5-D5-B4-G4 under A♭5 at 162) with all of Vln II at ff plus Cl 1 since c166. The subordinate line now has more string players than the line it accompanies, at the same dynamic and in the same register. Sebesky's rule 2 (PDF 138): "the lead violin line should be carried by more players than any other line in a concerted string chord". He calls even 8-4 against the melody "unbalanced", and here the ratio runs the other way. PDF 164: a wind that doubles a string line is marked one level softer, because one woodwind weighs as much as many violins.
**Fix (applied).** recP.ily: Vln II at 159 `es''8(\f^\markup \italic "espress."` (was `\ff`). Cl 1 at 159 `es''8(\mf^\markup \italic "espress."` and Cl 2 `es'1\mf` (equal on the shared staff; were f). Ob 2's held notes `recPOboeTwoDiv = { g'1\mf |` (was f). The tune stays ff in all its parts. The f restatements at 167 are unchanged. This answers c108: it marked the start of the line, and "espress." still does that. The clarinet doubling, which c108 did not have, carries the line through the brass off-beats. The Vln I divisi (protected) is untouched.

### 3. 77-82: wind doublings level with the violins at F2's string peak (applied)
**Problem.** The E♭-major F2 statement is the movement's main string cantabile. At its peak (78) Fl 1 doubles Vln I at the unison on B♭6, Ob 1 doubles Vln II on B♭5, and Cl 1-2 add thirds. All four winds crescendo p < f to the violins' own f. Sebesky PDF 164: "The addition of a single flute to the lead violin line ... thickens the string tone considerably, sacrificing purity for extra body", and "to compensate somewhat for the difference in tonal weight, I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings". A flute at f on B♭6, its shrill top (Adler ch. 7, PDF 185-186, cited by c041), will be the colour of the climax rather than an addition to the violins. The F1 statements (19-26, 151) are different: there the winds are meant to give body, and Sebesky allows that. This bar is a cantabile peak.
**Fix (applied).** sTwo.ily: 78 `bes'''4.\mf` in Fl 1 and `bes''4.\mf` in Ob 1 (were `\f`). Cl 1 `g'2\mf` and Cl 2 `es'2\mf` (were `\f`). At 82, Fl 1 `es''2)\mp\<` and Ob 1 `g'8(\mp\<` (were mf), one level under the violins' mf < run into 83. Vln I-II keep mf < f at 77-78, the down-bow peak and the B♭6 (protected).
