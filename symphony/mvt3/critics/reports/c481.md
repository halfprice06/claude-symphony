## Findings

On the melody itself: F1, F2, the chorale and the S head do the job of finale tunes. F1 has a hummable head with a falling sixth, a sequence and the A♭–G sigh at its peak. F2's violin statement (67–82) climbs to its peak in two waves, F6 at 69 and then a dotted B♭6 at 78. That is the shape of Brahms's and Dvořák's lyrical second themes: a second, higher wave after a first peak. The chorale keeps its four-note G–E–D–C core, which the last statement makes into a cadence. The places where a string technique from this chapter could make these tunes clearer are already either treated or protected. Previous passes (c211, c301, c376, c391) used the string-colour marks. I looked for the techniques the chapter teaches that the score does not use. Those are single solo strings and the viola as bass. I tested the two solo candidates in a scratch build. Neither is better, so the score stays unchanged.

### 1. A single solo violin (Adler PDF 81, Ex. III-20) at 119 and 122: tested and declined

**Bars.** 119 and 122, Vln I's pp answers to the bassoon's G-minor F2.

**What the reading offers.** Brahms 1/ii (Ex. III-20) sets one solo violin, the concertmaster, against a woodwind melody, with the section accompanying. The score never uses a single solo string. The upper octave at 119 and 122 is "4 soli", and the rest of Vln I play it an octave lower ("gli altri").

**Test.** In `/tmp/critic-481/work/sections/dev.ily` I changed `^\markup \italic "dolce, 4 soli"` to `"dolce, solo"` and `"4 soli"` to `"solo"`. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, MIDI identical, 0 parallels.

**Verdict: not better.** In Brahms the solo violin stands over an accompaniment, not over its own section doubling the line an octave lower. Here the line sits over "gli altri" on the same notes an octave below. One player at G6 pp above about twelve players at G5 would be covered. The upper octave is the reason the passage is divided at all (c209, Rimsky Ex. 26). c438 already chose four players over two on Casanelles's rule: use one instrument or three or more. Going to one would reverse that for no gain in the answer's audibility. Kept as v481.

### 2. A solo cello opening F2 in G major (Adler PDF 102, Ex. III-63; PDF 99) at 123–124: tested and declined

**Bars.** 123–124.3: the cellos begin the G-major hand-over (Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I) "arco, espress., sul D", p.

**What the reading offers.** The cello as an occasional soloist (Don Quixote, Ex. III-63; Brahms Piano Concerto 2/iii, PDF 103). This would be a lyrical island in the development.

**Test.** In the scratch `devVc` I marked the 123 entry "solo, arco, espress." and the 125 counter "tutti, dolce". Notes, harp and the hand-over order are unchanged. Build: clean, 16 pages.

**Verdict: not better.** At 123 the harp's left-hand arpeggios run G2–B3, inside the tune's B3–D4 notes. One cello at p would be masked where the section is not. Adler (PDF 99) puts the cello's beauty first in "solo passages featuring the entire section", and on the D string, which c211 already set. A solo would also make the hand-over start thinner than the viola section that follows at 124.4, which reverses the order of colours c211 protected. Kept as v481.

### 3. F1's peak in the bass statement, 33.4 (Adler PDF 105–106 against c166): no change

**Bars.** 33 beat 4. The cellos and violas reach A♭4 and fall to G4 at 34, F1's sigh. The basses repeat F3 instead of rising to A♭3.

**Reading.** Adler gives the practical bass range up to written B♭4, which sounds B♭3 (PDF 105). He asks that the top be approached "by small-scale steps or small skips" (PDF 106). F3→A♭3 is a minor third, so Adler allows it. c166 forbade it on Rimsky-Korsakov's "never by skip".

**Verdict.** The sigh is not lost in the 16-foot octave: the bass clarinet plays F3–A♭3–G3 there (`pTwoBassCl`, bar 33). Writing A♭3 in the basses would add a third instrument on the bass clarinet's note and nothing a listener would hear as new. No change. I record that Adler would permit `f,4( aes,8 c8 f4 aes4)` if the bass clarinet were ever taken off.

### 4. F2's violin statement against Brahms 1/iv (Ex. III-4): follows, no change

**Bars.** 67–82.

Brahms's tune (Ex. III-4) sits in the violins' lowest octave, G3–E5, with the open G. F2 at 67 is in Vln I on the E string (B♭5–F6) over Vln II (B♭4–F5), with the counter-melody in violas, cellos and Hn 1 in G3–G4. c391 already tested the low-octave layout and showed the counter would crowd the tune. The register also earns the second wave: the E string's brilliance (PDF 75) reaches B♭6 f at 78, and Vln II, the cellos (A string, PDF 99), Fl 1 and Ob 1 double it. This is the one climax in the movement where a violin tune is at the top of the E string at forte, as Don Juan's is (Ex. III-8). It is earned and placed once. No change.

### 5. The S head at 235–242 (Ex. III-7): follows

Vln I·II in unison, sul A, p, with the portamento sixth at 236 and the C6 peak at 238 kept on the A string. The second phrase is "ord." an octave higher, with Vln II in the lower octave. This is Brahms 3/iii's practice (Ex. III-7) for the soft statement, followed by the E string for the louder one. The counter-melody (Cl 1 + violas, C5–A4) lies on the viola's A string, which Adler says "combines beautifully with woodwind instruments" (PDF 87). Protected (c018: "sul A/ord. 235/239"). No change.

### 6. Open strings in the tunes (PDF 73–74): no change

I scanned the tunes for open-string notes on expressive long values. The S head's held E5 (235) is stopped because it is marked sul A. F2's violin statement (E♭ major) has no open-string pitches in Vln I or Vln II. F1's D4 at 13 is open, but it is a quarter note inside a forte phrase at 2 = 84, the "fast passage" case where Adler says the open string "may not make any difference" (PDF 73). The violas' G3 at 67–68 was settled by c301. Nothing to change.

### 7. The viola as string bass (Ex. III-36): unused, no place for it

This is the one device in the chapter the score has never used. c391 listed every possible place and found each one protected or needing a real bass. I found no new place in v481. No change.

### 8. F2's last appearance stops at 259 (melody, compared with Dvořák 9/iv): considered, no change

At 255–258 F2's third phrase (G3–E3–D3–C3–E3–G3 | C4–D4 | G4–F4–E4–D4) runs under the last chorale in Hn 3·4, violas, cellos and Vln II. At 259 the counter-melody gives way to arpeggios and repeated notes. In Dvořák's and Tchaikovsky's codas a recalled tune runs to the cadence. F2's fourth phrase, transposed (C4–B3 | A3–G3–F3–E3 | E3–D3–B2 | C3), would put A♮3 against the A♭ of the iiø6/5 at 259–260, a cross-relation inside the protected last cadence (259–263). Bending it to A♭ would make the phrase a different melody. The chorale's own descent to the cadence carries the line there already. No change.
