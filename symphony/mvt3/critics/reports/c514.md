## Findings

### 1. Bar 222: the stop chord's F has a single horn in the brass. Hn 3 leaves the line it doubles. Applied in scratch.
- **Problem.** At 222 the brass crescendo mf < into the fff arrival on the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ over D. The brass weights are D 4 units (Tpt 3, Hn 2, Hn 3, bass trombone, tuba), B 2.5, A♭ 2 and F 0.5 (Hn 1 only). F is the tone that resolves to E, the major third of the arrival. In the strings and winds it is held by the violas (F4, the protected F4→E4), Cl 2 and Fl 2. Fl 2 releases on beat 4. Hn 3 doubles the violas' line D♯4–E4–F4 at 219–221, then drops to D4 at 222, adding a fourth D, and steps back up to E4 at 223. None of the composer comments in codaTwo.ily gives a reason for the D. c104 and c108 concern Tpt 3 and the dynamics.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: above mf, two horns in unison equal one trumpet or trombone. A chord tone carried by one horn is half a unit against the doubled D. Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) balances the chord's tones across the brass. PDF 17: give the horns more than static tones. Here Hn 3 keeps its own line instead of filling in a doubling.
- **Fix (applied).** In sections/codaTwo.ily, Hn 3 at 222 plays F4 instead of D4:
  `codaTwoHnThree = { … R1*4 | dis'1\p | e'1 | f'1\< | f'1\mf\< | }`
  - The brass weights at 222 become D 3.5, F 1 (Hn 1 and Hn 3 in unison), B 2.5, A♭ 2. D still has Tpt 3, Hn 2, the bass trombone and the tuba.
  - Hn 3 now plays D♯4–E4–F4–F4–E4 across 219–223, the violas' line at pitch, so the brass also sound the stop chord's F resolving to E at 222→223.
  - Hn 3 restrikes F4 at 222 like Hn 4's B3, with the same mf <, so the shared staff keeps equal dynamics.
  - Unchanged: the stop chord's pitches, its resolution only at 222→223, Tpt 1 B→C, the timpani G→C, the violas' F4→E4, the dynamics and the arrival chord at 223.
- **Answer to the protection of 216–223.** It protects the stop chord and its single resolution, the B→C and G→C couplings and the dynamic path. All of these are kept. Only one inner horn pitch changes, and it makes the resolution clearer. c440, c483 and c496 changed inner parts in the same span on the same basis.
- **Check.** par8.py on 219–224 gives the same list before and after: only the existing bass doublings at 220, 222 and 223 are flagged, and Hn 3 is not. Hn 3 F4→E4 moves in tenths with the bass D→C and in thirds with Tpt 2's A♭4→G4. analysis.txt is identical to v514's (0 outer-voice parallels). The engraved Hn 3·4 staff at 222 shows F4 over B3.

### 2. Bar 92.1: the common-tone diminished seventh's C has one horn in the brass. Tested on paper, not applied.
The ff E♭–F♯–A–C on beat 1 has C only in Hn 3 (0.5 units), against E♭ 5 and F♯ 2.5 (PDF 15). The only brass voice that could take C is Tbn 2, which holds the common tone E♭3. C3 there would have to go to B♭2 with the strings, which makes fifths by contrary motion into F3 over the B♭ bass at 92.3, or leap back to E♭3, which leaves the 6 unresolved. The chord lasts one quarter and the winds and strings carry C (Vln II, Cl 2). The change is not worth it.

### 3. Bar 247: the tonic chord's G has one horn. Tested on paper, not applied.
Both theme heads start on E, so at 247 E has about 3.5 units in the brass and G only Hn 3 G4 (PDF 15). Hn 4 G3 instead of E3 would move G3→A3 against the bass C→D at 248, which is parallel fifths. Hn 4 C4 adds root, not fifth. The thematic third is right for the combination, so I left it.

### 4. Bars 247–254: F1's head in Tbn 1·2 lies at G2–A3, not the trombone's best register (Hindemith, PDF 42). Checked, no change.
An octave higher (G3–A4), the head would run through Hn 1·2's S head (E4–C5) and Hn 3·4's chords. At its present pitch it is doubled by Bn 1·2 and the cellos, and the violins carry it two octaves up. c424 (finding 5) and c471 found the balance holds. I agree.

### 5. Bars 265–268: Handel's antiphony between horns and trumpets (PDF 17). Considered, not applied.
Splitting F1's head between Tpt 1·2 (265–266) and Hn 1·2 (267–268) would thin the last full statement of the head in C major. Exchange already follows at 269–272 (Hn 3·4, then Tbn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2).

### 6. Bars 211–213: theme B's IV chord (213) has B♭ only in Hn 4 in the brass. Checked, no change.
At 213.1 the brass hold G♭ at 4 units and B♭ at 0.5. Tpt 1 reaches B♭5 on beat 3, the violas hold B♭4, and Bn 1 has B♭. The chord is complete and the melody carries the third, so the brass weight does not matter here.
