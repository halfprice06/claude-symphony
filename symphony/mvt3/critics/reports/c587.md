## Findings

### 1. 35–36: F1's A♭-major head has one flute above the violins and no wind at their pitch. Applied.

**Problem.** At 35, F1's head enters in A♭ major. This is the counterstatement's first new key, and the violins move from the countersubject to the theme. Vln I·II play it in unison at A♭5, ff, with the grace glide (c208). Fl 1 plays it an octave higher at A♭6 (c394). No other instrument has the line. So the melody has strings alone on one octave and a single flute alone on the other. Cl 1 and Ob 2 both hold A♭4 under it at 35, and both hold G4 on 36.1–2.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 78: when only one of two octave parts is doubled in the soprano, "it is better to allow the wood-wind to progress in octaves, the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups". His formulas are Picc. / Fl. + Vn and Fl. / Ob. (Cl.) + Vn. The editor's footnote on the same page says the opposite layout, a wind on one octave and strings on the other (Fl over Vn), "is not to be recommended, as the tone quality of the two groups is so widely different". PDF 76: a wind in unison with a string group "increases the resonance of the latter and amplifies its tone", and PDF 76–77 list Vn + Cl among the best unisons. The score already uses this layout for the same theme at 19–26 (Fl 1 + Vln I, Ob 1·2 + Vln II) and 151–158 (Fl 1·2 / Ob 1 + cor anglais + violins). Only the A♭ statement lacks it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-587/work/sections/pTwo.ily).** Cl 1 plays the head with the violins at A♭5, including their glide, f (Fl 1's level). It then returns to its F4 pad at 37:

```
pTwoClOne = { R1*8 | \grace { es''16( f'' g'' } aes''2->\f es''4. f''8 | g''4-.) f''8( es''8 des''4-.) c''4-. | f'1\mf | es'2. f'4~ | ...
pTwoClTwo = { R1*8 | \grace { s16 s16 s16 } c'1\f | bes2 c'2 | bes1\mf | ...
```

Cl 1's old notes are not lost. Its A♭4 at 35 and G4 at 36.1–2 stay in Ob 2. Its A♭4–F4 at 36.3–4 stays in Hn 1, and the F at 36.4 also stays in Ob 1 and Tbn 1 (c212, c322). Cl 1 re-attacks F4 at 37 instead of tying it from 36.4. It still steps F→E♭ into 38's seventh (c132). It does not leap down from E♭5 to A♭4 on 36.3, which would have made octaves with the bass E♭→A♭. Cl 2 gets matching grace spacers and the same f at 35 and mf at 37, so the shared staff has equal simultaneous dynamics. Cl 1 sits above the oboes for these two bars. Rimsky's own formula Fl. / Cl. + Vn places it there (PDF 78). The flutes stay above the oboes, and only that order matters for c309's PDF 65 point. F1's notes, the violins, Fl 1's octave (c394), the glide (c208, c209), the harmony and Fl 2's rest (c311, c486) are unchanged. The file carries a c587 comment.

### 2. 37–38: Hn 3·4's B♭-minor head has no wind above it (Rimsky PDF 75: two horns in unison need three or four winds an octave above in forte). Checked, no change.

The free player would be Ob 1. Its F5–D♭5–C♭5 at 37–38 mostly duplicates Ob 2. But Rimsky's own count makes one oboe over two ff horns wrong, and the clarinets and flutes hold the iiø7 notes (c492) and the dovetailed B♭5 (c486). The head's single horn colour is also the point of the strings–horns–strings dialogue at 35–40 (pTwo comment line 53). Rimsky's rule applies when the composer wants a rich upper octave, and here the score wants a change of colour. No change.

### 3. 110–113: add Hn 3·4 to the violas' scale figure for a full three-group unison (PDF 79 E). Declined.

The figure already has the violas, Cl 2 (c187, from this reading), Bn 1 and, at 110 and 112, the cellos. Taking Hn 3·4 away would undo c064's reason: they double Hn 1·2 so that the third and fifth of V7 of F weigh as much as the single trumpet and trombone notes. The figure is heard already, so the gain is small. No change.

### 4. 11–18: F1's first statement is strings + horns with no woodwind (PDF 79 D–E). Declined.

Violas + Hn 1·2 is Rimsky's best string–brass pair, and the winds join at 19 as the plan's second step (Fl 1, Ob 1·2). The free woodwinds would be the clarinets, and they carry the movement's accompaniment figure. Moving them to the tune would remove that figure. No change.

### 5. 243–246: Cl 1 in unison with Tpt 1 on the horn call for a finer legato (PDF 75). Declined.

Rimsky also says the wind "softens and rarefies" the brass (PDF 74). The call is the owed item "the horn call in major in the trumpets", and its layout follows his brass-octave formula (principle 1, c014). Cl 1's G5–F5 inside the cresc. molto chord at 244–246 would be lost. No change.
