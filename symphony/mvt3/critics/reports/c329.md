**Dvořák 8/iv comparison.** A conductor would accept the large plan. Dvořák keeps one tune and gives each return a new colour; this score does the same with F1, F2 and the chorale (rows 15-16). Dvořák saves the full brass for the coda; this score keeps the trumpets back for the solo at 51 and saves the tremolo and C7 for 223. Dvořák's slow return before the coda is short and the fast coda after it is shorter still. This score's slow stages (211-246) are longer than its fast end (247-279). That proportion was examined in c313 and is protected. The one texture where the score is weaker than Dvořák is its soft woodwind solos. Dvořák puts a woodwind solo over string or horn harmony, never over another woodwind's held note. This score had one such place left, which finding 1 fixes.

## Findings

### 1. 43-47: the woodwind motive x sits on a bassoon pad; the held seventh goes to Hn 4 (applied)

In the transition, motive x passes Fl 1 (43-44), Cl 1 (45-46) and Ob 1 (47-48) over the B♭ pedal. The strings hold pp, with harmonics in Vln I and the harp's harmonics on 43, 45 and 47. The one wind pad under the motive is Bn 1's A♭3 pp, held 43-47.2. That note is the seventh of V7 of E♭, the only dissonance in the chord. The result is a woodwind solo carried by another woodwind's held note. The flute at 43-44 is also in its weak low register (A♭4-F5), where a reed pad an octave below has the same kind of sound.

Evidence:
- Rimsky PDF 116: "if the phrase is given to the wood-wind (oboes and clarinets) the harmony should be entrusted to the horns". The harmonic basis "should differ from the melody not only in fullness and intensity of tone, but also in colour".
- Rimsky PDF 105 b) and Ex. 136 (PDF 312, Snegourotchka): four horns pp as the basis under a flute solo. Ex. 140 (PDF 315, Kitesh 248): soft brass sustained under running clarinets and muted strings, which is the texture of 43-48.
- Rimsky PDF 108: "in a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind."
- c229 applied the same principle at 95-98 (HISTORY, cycle 229). This is the other exposed woodwind solo in the exposition with a free horn.
- In Dvořák 8/iv the flute's solo variation is carried by strings, not by another woodwind pad.

### 2. A soft muted-brass chord (Ex. 132, 138, 149, 174) is not used (not applied)

Rimsky shows muted trumpets, horns and trombones as a soft chord colour (PDF 107; Ex. 149 at PDF 321, three muted trumpets inside oboes, cor anglais and clarinets over harp). The score uses a mute only for Tpt 1's unison with the cor anglais at 171-186, and Tpt 2 sits muted and silent there. Giving Tpt 2 a muted held line in that passage would put the harmony in the melody's own colour, which goes against rule 12 (PDF 116), and 171-186 is protected. Every other soft stretch has no time for mutes, or is protected (219-222). Decided: no change.

### 3. Trills on held tutti notes (Ex. 133, 180; Dvořák 8's horn trills) (not applied)

c321 rejected trills because "no free held note needs one". The only held tutti note where a trill would work as it does in Ex. 180 is the G6 at 254, before the chorale. The 254→255 switch to the chorale is protected (c8, as 250→251). A trill there would also imitate Dvořák's signature rather than serve this score. Decided: no change.

### 4. The slow coda is long next to Dvořák's (not applied; protected)

Dvořák's quiet return before the coda lasts a few bars. Here 211-246 is slow and 247-279 fast. c313 kept this proportion because the plan saves C major and the only root-position V–I for the coda. That reason still stands, and the owed items (arrival, chorale, second-theme head) need the slow bars. Decided: no change.
