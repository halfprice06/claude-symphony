## Findings

### 1. Bar 20: add the °4/3 on ♮4 before c322's VII°7 of V (applied)

**Problem.** In F1's consequent, 20.3–4 runs iv → VII°7 of V → V. The bass is F–F♯–G (c322). The iv on 20.3 is thin: bass F, the tune's F, violas F4 and Cl 1 A♭4, with no C. So only one voice, the bass, makes the chromatic step into the °7. Bar 17, the antecedent, already approaches V from the same F with ♯IV7 (c332). As a result, the consequent's approach is no stronger than the antecedent's, even though it leads to the movement's highest point of the theme (A♭6 at 22).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 561 (§8, Ex. 31-14, image): "An applied °7 on ♯4—one of the most important intensifications of V—is itself often preceded and intensified by another °7 on ♮4. This represents by far the most frequent and characteristic usage of consecutive rising °7's. The first °7 is in 4/3 position and usually comes from a IV or a II in first inversion." PDF 561–562 (Ex. 31-15, Schubert) shows that the °4/3 can stand directly for IV or II: "Progressions like this one … are by no means rare." In the pair of rising °7s every upper voice rises a semitone: F→F♯, A♭→A, B→C. The d7 B–A♭ becomes the M6 C–A, the enharmonic ambiguity of PDF 560 (§7). PDF 537: an altered tone inside a chromatic line is less disruptive. Nowhere does the score use ♮4–♯4 °7s in C. 202 (E°7 → F) is a single °7, and it lies inside protected 199–204.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-342/work/sections/pOne.ily, bar 20, violas only):**
```
pOneVla bar 20:  | d'4( bes4 b4 c'4) |     % was | d'2( f'4 c'4) |
```
After the change, 20.3 is vii°4/3 over F, incomplete: F (bass and tune), A♭4 (Cl 1), B3 (violas). 20.4 is unchanged: F♯–A–C–E♭. The voice leading is as follows:
- Cl 1's A♭4–A4 and the violas' B3–C4 rise together, a diminished seventh moving to a major sixth.
- The B♮ comes inside the violas' own line B♭3–B3–C4. On 20.2 the violas join Bn 1's B♭3, so the B♭/B♮ cross relation stays in one voice.
- Nobody doubles the leading tone B.

c322's reason for the violas' D–F–C line was fifths with Hn 1·2 (G3–F3) at 20.2–3. That danger is avoided: the violas' D4 (20.1) moves to B♭3 against the horns' A♭3/G3, and B3 lies an augmented 4th above their F3. The tune, bass, F♯ chord, dynamics, slur span and the recapitulation's 160 (protected) are unchanged. No theme or quotation changes. The exposition's two approaches to V now rise in intensity, ♯IV7 at 17 and two °7s at 20, while the recapitulation keeps the diatonic form, as c322 and c332 intended.

### 2. Common-tone °7 embellishing V7 is unused (checked, not applied)

**Problem.** A&S call the move from °7 to V7 with "all the upper tones ascending a half step" "a particularly important possibility" (PDF 546, Ex. 30-23). Ex. 30-24 is Tchaikovsky 5/iii, one of this movement's models. The score never uses it.

**Evidence and decision.** I checked every V or V7 that lasts at least two beats:
- 24–25 (V pedal before the deceptive cadence). The °7 here is G–B♭–C♯–E. Its E♮ would sound against the tune's E♭5/E♭6 at 25.1. It would also put an E♭→E♮ step in C minor, a step held back for the viola thread at 219–220.
- 39–42. The V7 enters under the violas' F1 tail (C5–D5 at 40), which collides with C♯ and E, and Fl 1's A♭5 there is protected.
- 147–150, 221, 254 and 262 are protected (147–150 notes and voicing; 216–223; 255–263).

No change.

### 3. Common-tone augmented sixth over the final tonic (checked, not applied)

**Problem.** PDF 547 (Ex. 30-25, 30-26) shows a common-tone A6 over I whose augmented sixth expands to the tonic's fifth. In C, its pitches would be A♭–C–E♭–F♯ over C. That is bar 1's German sixth, resolved at the end into C major instead of onto V.

**Evidence and decision.** The only place it fits is 273.3, the common-tone °7 C–E♭–F♯–A. That chord leads on to iv6/4 at 274 by the step A→A♭. If A were changed to A♭, the A6 would have to expand to G at once, and 274's A♭ would follow as a repetition. c252 protected 273 with its E♭ spelling, and 273–279 is on every recent do-not-touch list. The change would also add a third Ger6 sound (1, 261, 273) in the last 20 bars. No change.

### 4. 135–138 and 20.3–4: spelling of consecutive °7s (checked, no change)

PDF 558–560 (Ex. 31-11, 31-12) ask that each 7th in a °7 chain resolve normally, which needs alternating positions and no remote accidentals.
- 135–138 (D–F–A♭–B → C♯–E–G–B♭ → C–E♭–F♯–A → B–D–F–A♭) meets this: A♭→G, B♭→A and E♭→D each resolve in the next chord, and the chain ends on the stop chord's root position.
- At 20.3–4 the new pair is spelled B–D–F–A♭ (D omitted) → F♯–A–C–E♭. That is °4/3 → °7, the notation of Ex. 31-14, with A♭ and B kept as the d7 the ear hears.

No change.
