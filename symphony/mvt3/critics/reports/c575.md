## Findings

### 1. Bars 217–221: semibreve bars wider than minim bars on the same system (applied)

**Problem.** In the system 209–222 (page 12), the bars with only semibreves and hairpins are much wider than the bars with minims and crotchets. Measured from the bar-number positions in v575: 217 = 32.2 pt and 218 = 37.9 pt (Hn 1·2 and cor anglais `aes2( des'2 | f'2. ges'4)`, the espress. echo of movement II's theme B), but 220 = 49.1 pt and 221 = 59.1 pt (one semibreve per part and one hairpin), and 213 = 37.7 pt (crotchets). So a semibreve bar takes about 1.5–1.8 times the space of a bar of two minims. The cause is c045's `\override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #13` over 219–222. Thirteen spaces is longer than a semibreve needs at this system's spacing, so the hairpins set the width of the bar.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 53: durations are spaced in relative proportion, a semibreve 7 units and a minim 5, so two minims (10) take more room than one semibreve (7). PDF 54: spacing must not contradict duration; a longer value gets more space than its shorter neighbour, or at least as much, and equal durations get equal space across the whole system, even through a tempo change (here Largamente, then allargando at 221). PDF 55: bars with one bar-length note are "by definition fairly narrow". In v575 the minim at 220 (C.a. `g'2`) gets about 1.5 times the space of the minims at 217. The bars that carry the music, the theme B echo at 217–218 and the trumpets' and flutes' Largamente phrase at 212–216, are squeezed so that four bars of held chords can be wide.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-575/work/global.ily, line 164).**
```
  \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #8 \override Staff.Hairpin.springs-and-rods = #ly:spanner::set-spacing-rods
```
(was `#13`; the `\revert` after 222 is unchanged). The scratch build measures 220 = 35.5 pt and 221 = 43.3 pt (including \cen's 3.2 spaces). The space goes back to 209–218: 211 is 73.3 pt, 212 is 51.2, 213 is 41.9, 214 is 55.6, 215 is 48.5, 216 is 48.4, 217 is 36.2 and 218 is 41.4. The semibreve bars now take about as much space as the minim bars, Gould's acceptable case (b) on PDF 54. They no longer take half again as much. c045's reason is still met. At 200 dpi the hairpins in 220 are about 10 mm long, and the shortest, after "mp" at 221, is about 6 mm. c045 fixed hairpins about 1 mm long. Bar 219 stays wider (57 pt) because it holds the cancellation of five flats and the "sub. p" texts, which PDF 56 asks to be given room. Only page 12 changes: bar widths on the other 15 pages are identical, the system and page breaks are unchanged, and the score is still 16 pages. The MIDI is byte-identical.

### 2. Bar 219, Hn 3·4 C4 under D♯4 (checked, no change)

Hn 3's D♯4 and Hn 4's C4 are part-combined into one stemless chord. The C4 head stands on the left and the D♯4 head on the right, and the sharp sits to the left of the chord. I weighed printing the parts apart with the lower part displaced to the right (Gould PDF 62), so the sharp would stand right beside its own head (PDF 64). I decided against it. PDF 62's displacement rule is for double-stemmed writing. Two parts with the same rhythm on a shared staff are written as a single-stemmed chord, where PDF 61 asks for exactly what is printed: lower note left, upper note right. The sharp is at D's height, which is the standard place for an accidental before an adjacent-note chord. The players read separate parts. The violas' `<c' dis'>1` in the same bar follows the same rule. Bar 219 also lies inside the protected D♯→E thread (219–220), and I would not touch it for a layout preference.

### 3. Dots, stems and unisons in the rest of the score (checked, no change)

The dotted chords at 211–216 (violas `<des' f'>2--` pairs, Ob and Tpt dotted minims), Fl 2's `d''2.` under Fl 1's crotchets at 222 and the dotted chords at 273–277 have their dots in spaces, with the lower dot dropped in adjacent-note chords (PDF 67–68). Bar 55's E♭4–F4 second has the lower note on the left (PDF 61). I found no new crossings, merged semibreves or stem–semibreve contacts beyond those fixed in c215, c305 and c485 (PDF 65–67).
