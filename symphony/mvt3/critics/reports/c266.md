## Findings

### 1. Bars 269-271: both oboes play F1 in unison down to E4 and D4 at ff (applied)

**Problem.** At 269-272, F1 (C major, "a2") sits in two octaves:
- C6 octave: Fl 1·2 and Vln I.
- C5 octave: Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Vln II, with Bn 1 an octave lower.

The oboes are in unison through 270-271 and reach E4 (270.4) and D4 (271.1) together at ff. Those are the oboe's coarsest and least controllable notes, and the two players are on them at the same pitch. Meanwhile the C5 octave has five instruments and the C6 octave three, all of them light in that register except the violins.

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 71 calls the oboe's notes from F4 down to B♭3 "coarse" and "the most difficult to control". On the same page he says: "I don't recommend voicing oboes in unison unless ... an especially harsh effect is required."
- PDF 80 says octave couplings of flute, oboe and clarinet are "usually even more pleasant sounding than unisons ... There is also less chance of an intonation conflict." The same page says flute-oboe unisons from C5 up are "strong and useful".
- PDF 79 (Ex. 104) shows the oboe's low register spoiling the flute above it, and the balance improving as the voicing goes up.
- The score already has two precedents. c041 took Ob 1 up to Fl 1/Vln I's octave at 24-26 to avoid the low fifth. c086 took the oboes out of a unison at 265 and 267.

**Fix** (sections/finale.ily, `finaleOboeOne`, bars 269-271; Ob 2 unchanged):
```
c'''2->(\ff g''4. a''8 | bes''4-.) a''8( g''8 f''4-.) e''4-. | d''4( g''8 a''8 b''4 d'''4) | a''2-> g''4( f''4) \breathe |
```
The old line was `c''2->(\ff g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | d'4( g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4) | a''2->`.

Ob 1's top note is D6, the limit PDF 71 gives. At 272 Ob 1 drops a fourth from D6 to A5 and joins Ob 2 again. This mirrors c041's change at 24-26 and gives each octave four instruments. Both oboes keep `\ff` at 269, so the shared staff has equal dynamics; LilyPond engraves the octaves as chords with one slur. The change only doubles the flutes' line, so it adds no parallels; par8.py over 268-278 shows none before or after.

### 2. Bar 277: all four horns play the eighth-note upbeat of the brass figure at fff; Hn 3 has its highest note on it (applied)

**Problem.** At 277 the brass play the hammered figure `c4-> r8 d8 c4-> r4` into the last chord. The horns play it too: Hn 1 and Hn 3 C5-D5-C5, Hn 2 and Hn 4 E4-D4-E4. Two things go wrong:
- The D is a single eighth on the second half of beat 2, between two accented quarters, at fff.
- For Hn 3, the low horn, this D5 (written A5, in the extended range) is the highest note in the part. Hn 3 then holds C5 (written G5) fff in the final chord.

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 32: the horn "speak[s] a little late, creating a time problem when the horn is combined with other brass in ... staccato passages".
- PDF 46, on Ex. 51: "The two sixteenth notes on the upbeat ... are omitted, because of the tendency of the horns to 'speak' a little late ... It would be very hard for the horn section to pounce on the upbeat ... as the other brass can." In that example the horns rejoin on the next downbeat and the trumpets and trombones carry the rhythm.
- PDF 32-33 (Ex. 35) puts written A5 above the horn's normal range.
- The score already applied this rule at 269 and 271 (c086: "no horn pickups 269/271").

Without the horns, the eighth is still played by Tpt 1·3, Tbn 1, the flutes, the oboes, Cl 1, the violas and (as G) Tpt 2, Tbn 2, Bn 2 and the timpani. Nothing is lost from the figure. The horns land squarely on beats 1 and 3, and Hn 3 does not have to place a written A5 on an eighth before its held G5.

**Fix** (sections/finale.ily, bar 277):
```
finaleHnOne:   c''4-> r4 c''4-> r4 |   (was c''4-> r8 d''8 c''4-> r4)
finaleHnThree: c''4-> r4 c''4-> r4 |   (was c''4-> r8 d''8 c''4-> r4)
finaleHnTwo:   e'4-> r4 e'4-> r4 |     (was e'4-> r8 d'8 e'4-> r4)
finaleHnFour:  e'4-> r4 e'4-> r4 |     (was e'4-> r8 d'8 e'4-> r4)
```
This touches bars 273-279, which earlier critics listed as do-not-touch. Their reason was the close's harmony (I - F♯°7 - iv6/4 - I over the C pedal, the 5-♯4-4-3 line), the harp glissando and the final chord. None of these changes: the horns lose only a passing D that six other parts still play, and Hn 3's range drops from D3-D5 to D3-C5.
