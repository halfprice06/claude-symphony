## What else I studied

Aldwell & Schachter PDF 352: in a suspension, sounding the tone of resolution in another voice before the suspension resolves "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". Beethoven allows it only an octave lower in an inner voice. A&S PDF 406–407: a transferred resolution works best when the resolution comes in the next voice in the same register. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 271–272: F1's C-major statement repeats its two-bar head four times and never continues (applied; changes how F1 is stated)

**Problem.** After the last cadence, F1's head `c2 g4. a8 | bes4 a8 g8 f4 e4` sounds four times in a row: 265–266 and 267–268 (Tpt 1·2, Hn, Vla), then 269–270 and 271–272 (Fl, Ob, Cl, violins; trumpets also at 269–270). Each statement stops on E. In this movement F1 gets past its head only at 11–26 and 151–170, both in minor. The major apotheosis is the head alone, so the theme the listener should take home never shows its rising continuation (`d4 g8 a8 b4 d4 | f2 e4 d4`) in major. The models carry their tunes on. Brahms 1/iv, which Adler prints at full length (Ex. III-4, PDF 74, mm. 61–78), answers its first phrase with a continuation, not with a copy of the head. Tchaikovsky 5/iv states the motto in major as a complete phrase.

**Fix (applied).** At 271–272, the upper line plays F1's third and fourth bars in major instead of the head again. The line then falls F–E–D to the C on top of the protected IV6/4 at 273. That completes F1's first four-bar phrase in C major: `c'''2 g''4. a''8 | bes''4 a''8 g''8 f''4 e''4 | d''4 g''8 a''8 b''4 d'''4 | f'''2 e'''4 d'''4 | c'''` (273). Over the C pedal, the B♭ of 270 becomes B♮ at 271 and the F at 272 is a 4–3 over C. In finale.ily:

- finaleVlnOne, finaleFluteOne, finaleFluteTwo: 271 `d''4( g''8 a''8 b''4 d'''4)`; 272 `f'''2-> e'''4( d'''4)`
- finaleVlnTwo, finaleOboeOne, finaleOboeTwo, finaleClOne, finaleClTwo: 271 `d'4( g'8 a'8 b'4 d''4)`; 272 `f''2-> e''4( d''4)`
- finaleTptThree 271 and 272: `g'4-> r8 g'8 g'4-> r4` (were `e'…f'…e'` and `e'…e'`). At 272 its E4 would have sounded the tone of resolution under the melody's F (A&S PDF 352). An F would have doubled the 4–3 at the octave. G is consonant with both F and E.

### 2. 250: in the final combination, the P head's B sits a semitone under the S head's suspended C (applied)

**Problem.** At 250 the brass S head (Tpt 1 C6, Hn 1·2, Cl 1·2, Vla C5) holds C over V and resolves it to B on beat 3: a 4–3 suspension. On the downbeat, the P head (Fl 1·2, Vln I B5; Ob 1·2, Vln II B4) plays `b c d b`, so the resolution B sounds directly under the suspension, a minor second below the trumpet, in two octaves. A&S PDF 352 says this weakens the resolution and makes the sound unclear. Beethoven allows it only an octave lower in an inner voice, not a semitone under the top line at ff. par.py also finds four parallel fifths here (Fl/Vln I with Tpt 2, C6–F5 → B5–E5). Bar 250 is free continuation, not part of the recorded quotation (finale.md item 8: the quotation is 247–249).

**Fix (applied).** Start the bar on the suspension's C. This keeps the B→G rising sixth into beat 3:
- finaleVlnOne, finaleFluteOne, finaleFluteTwo 250: `c'''8-. d'''-. c'''-. b''-. g'''4.-> f'''8`
- finaleVlnTwo, finaleOboeOne, finaleOboeTwo 250: `c''8-. d''-. c''-. b'-. g''4.-> f''8`

The B now falls on the fourth eighth, a weak passing note. The P head lands on G as the brass resolves to B.

### 3. 246: the stringendo run stops on B6 and the leading tone never resolves in its register (applied)

**Problem.** Vln I and Fl 1 run `c''' d''' e''' f''' g'''4 a'''8 b'''` over V4/3 (D bass) and stop on B6, the leading tone. At 247 the top players begin movement I's head with an eighth rest and then G5. The only C at the downbeat is Tpt 2's C5, two octaves down. A&S PDF 406–407: a transferred resolution works only if the tone of resolution comes in the same register. Vln II has the same problem (B5). C7 cannot be used: it belongs to the arrival alone (223).

**Fix (applied).** End the run on the dominant and hold it into the Allegro molto. sHead.ily:
- sHeadVlnOne and sHeadFluteOne 246: `c'''8 d''' e''' f''' g'''2)` (the beam override on `a'''8 b'''` is removed)
- sHeadVlnTwo 246: `c''8 d'' e'' f'' g''2)`

G6 to the P head's G5 is a plain octave drop within the dominant. The violas' pickup G3 (c030) is unchanged.

### 4. 81 and 194: F2's lower octave ends on the open D string (applied)

**Problem.** Vln II doubles F2 an octave below Vln I. Its cadence notes are D4: a half note at 81 (`f'2( d'2)`, diminuendo) and a whole note at 194 (`d'1`, mf cantabile). In first position D4 is the open string. Adler PDF 73–74: in slow, expressive passages the open string "stands out peculiarly", and the composer wants "all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture". A held open D at the end of the phrase is exactly that case. Specifying the string is justified here because it is about timbre (PDF 73).

**Fix (applied).** Mark the D on the G string (Adler PDF 76):
- sTwo.ily sTwoVlnTwo 81–82: `f'2( d'2^\markup \italic "sul G") | es'1\mf^\markup \italic "ord." |`
- recS.ily recSVlnTwo 194–195: `d'1^\markup \italic "sul G" | g'2.(^\markup \italic "ord." e'4) |`

The violas' open G3 in the counter-melody (67–82) stays: the cellos double it at the unison with stopped notes, which covers the open-string colour.
