## Findings

### 1. 101–102 and 105–106: the wind answers have no bass weight (applied)

**Problem.** These are the only places where the strings drop out. The answering chord there is Bn 1·2 plus Hn 1·2 at p, and its lowest note is the bassoon. The flute, oboe and clarinet figure in the bar before is mf and spans more than two octaves. So the choir that follows has a light, thin bottom and does not sound like a full replacement for the strings it stands in for.

**Evidence.**
- Adler 257 and Ex. VIII-25: in a wind chorale, leave wider gaps at the bottom and double the bass towards the bottom, with the bass clarinet under the bassoons as the example.
- Adler 260 (William Schuman): holding the bass clarinet back makes its entry a fresh colour. This score has held it back for 100 bars.
- Adler 252–253: a relief passage needs a complete choir so the strings can "melt" back in afterwards.

**Fix (applied).** I added a new variable in sections/dev.ily (bassCl used to fall through to rests). The bass clarinet doubles Bn 1 an octave below, p, tied:
```
devBassCl = { R1*2 | r4 e,2.~\p | e,1 | R1*2 | r4 a,2.~\p | a,1 | R1*28 | }
```
The notes are E2 and A2, inside the bass-clarinet range. The dynamic matches the bassoons and horns. par8 now reports "8ve Bassoons" at 105.2 and 107.1. These are octave doublings of the bass (Bn A3 over B.cl A2), not parallels; the same kind of line already appears at 99–100. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 2. 105–106: the third statement of the scale figure copies the first (applied)

**Problem.** The scale figure appears three times: winds at 101, violins at 103, winds at 105. The scoring at 105 copied 101: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 all in the same register, transposed. The listener hears an exact repeat at the point where the passage should move on.

**Evidence.**
- Adler 255: when a figure comes back a third time, change something about it (Beethoven 3 and the "throwing the ball around" passage, 253–255).
- Adler 250–251 (Rossini, Ex. VIII-15–17): 8'-4'-2' octave doubling gives a wider and stronger line than a close unison doubling.
- Adler 237 (Ex. VIII-4/5): the three-octave Fl / Cl / Bn spread.

**Fix (applied).** In devClOne at 105–106, Cl 1 moves down an octave:
```
r4 f'8(\mf es' des' c' bes a | bes8 c' des' es' f'4->) f4->
```
It was `r4 f''8(\mf es'' des'' c'' bes' a' | bes'8 c'' des'' es'' f''4->) f'4->`. Bar 105 now sounds F6 (Fl 1), F5 (Ob 1) and F4 (Cl 1) over the bass clarinet's A2. The clarinet's chalumeau colour also gives the figure a darker bottom this time (Adler 258, bright and dark hues). Cl 2 rests here, so the part-combined staff has no clash; the staff prints "1." and fits on page 6. The lowest note is F3, well above the clarinet's floor. No new parallels on the par8 run for 99–110.

### 3. No wind choir longer than 7 beats without strings (not applied)

**Problem.** Adler 233 and 252–260 treat the string-free wind choir as one of the main colour changes in a symphonic movement. Both of the chapter's chorale models (Ex. VIII-21–25) are winds alone. In this movement the strings play in every bar, and the longest string-free moment is the 7-beat answer at 101–102. That leaves a narrow colour range across 279 bars.

**Decision.** I am not applying a new passage. Every place where a longer wind choir would fit (77–82, 87–90, 135–138, 187–198, 216–234, 247–279) is protected in HISTORY.md, and the reasons given there (the balance of the pad, the 7-6 chain, the F2 solos, the arrival) still hold. Findings 1 and 2 strengthen the two existing windows instead. That is the change the reading supports without reopening protected material.

### 4. 82→83: the pad's crescendo hides the anacrusis (not applied)

**Problem.** Adler 245 (Fledermaus) suspends the pedal so the upbeat comes through. At 82 the sustained pad crescendos straight through the upbeat into 83.

**Decision.** I rejected the fix. The surge into 83 is intended, and the balance of 77–82 is protected. Cutting the pad on beat 4 would weaken the arrival at 83 more than it would clarify the upbeat.

### 5. 87–90: no wind sustains the 7-6 chain (not applied)

**Evidence.** Adler 241–243: sustained wind harmony behind the string motion, as in Figaro.

**Decision.** I rejected it. The chain is protected. An Ob/Cl sustain in this register would cross the Vln II line and blur the suspensions it is meant to support.

### 6. 131: close f chord with one wind per note (not applied)

**Evidence.** Adler 257 (Ex. VIII-24) warns against a close chord in which every note has a different timbre. At 131 the upper winds come close to that.

**Decision.** I did not change it. At f with string doubling the problem is hard to hear, and respacing the chord would move the top line out of the register c165 settled.
