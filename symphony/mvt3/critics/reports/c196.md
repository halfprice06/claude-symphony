The basses hold whole and half notes while the cellos play the scale figure in eighths at 110 and 112. That is Adler's split for fast cello figures (PDF 105, Ex. III-69), so the low octave stays clear at 2 = 84. The contrabassoon's C1 at 110 follows a C2 half note, an octave drop on a new attack with no slur. It plays seven bars (107-113, about 10 s at 2 = 84) and then rests from 114 to 142, which meets PDF 230. The tuba's F1 (111, 113) sits in the normal low range; it rests for 21 bars after 113. Nothing to fix.

**2. 91-94, violas "non div." fifths E♭4-B♭4 and fourths F4-B♭4, ff: keep.** Adler's viola double-stop table (PDF 88, Ex. III-33) includes the one-finger fifth. These are short accented strokes (quarter, eighth, quarter), not held chords, so intonation is not exposed. Divisi would halve the attack the strokes need. A change would be marginal.

**3. 144-150, sustained double stops at ff in all four upper string parts: keep.** Vln I plays the sixths F5-D6 and D5-B5, Vln II plays B4-G5 and G4-F5 (a seventh on fingers 1 and 3 in third position), the violas play D4-G4 (fingers 4 and 3), and the cellos play G2-F3 and G2-G3 on the open G. All of them are in the easy tables (PDF 76 Ex. III-9; PDF 97-98 Ex. III-56), and the cellos' seventh does not run into the PDF 97 warning because one note is open. Sounding the double stop in every player gives the "especially sonorous passage" that PDF 76 names as the reason for orchestral double stops. The strokes at 147-150 are protected.

**4. 2 and 6, Vln II triple stop `<g d' b'>4->\downbow`: keep.** It is rolled (PDF 77). **5. Bass bow length (PDF 108): complete, no change.** Every bass hold longer than two bars carries "change bow at will": 43-50, 139-146, 149-150, 217-218, 224-234, 235-238. **6. Open strings in soft holds (PDF 73-74): no marking.** The violas' held D4 at 43-46 (pp) and 139-144 (p) and the cellos' D3 at 128-130 are open-string pitches. Adler says the open string "stands out peculiarly" in slow expressive passages. He also says fingering is best left to the player except for a special timbre (PDF 73), and every violist will stop D4 with the fourth finger on the G string in a pp hold. A "sul G" or fingering mark would only add clutter (MOLA). No change.
