## Findings

### 1. The development's G-minor half cadence has no sounding augmented sixth and no 7–6 suspension (bars 121–122). Applied.

**Problem.** The bassoon solo's G-minor period closes with a Phrygian-type half cadence: i (121.1, bass G) – passing i4/2 (121.2, bass F, c233) – Italian sixth (121.3–4, bass E♭) – V (122, bass D). The finale.md entry calls 121 an Italian sixth E♭–G–C♯ (since v153). In the score the accompaniment is pizzicato on alternate beats: cellos and basses pluck E♭ on beat 3 and rest on beat 4; Vln II and violas pluck G and C♯ on beat 4. The MIDI dump shows the two notes that define the chord are never heard together: at 121.3 only E♭2/E♭3 and the bassoon's G3 sound, at 121.4 only G3, G4, C♯5 and G5. The bass of the chord is gone when its augmented sixth arrives, so the cadence sounds like a G-minor chord followed by an A-major colour, not like ♭6 and ♯4 opening onto 5. The 7–6 that c233 found implied in Vln II (D5 on 121.2, C♯5 on 121.4) is not a suspension either, because the D is plucked over F and is silent when the E♭ enters.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 297–298, Ex. 18-12 (Handel): the passing minor dominant leads i to the iv6 of a Phrygian cadence by the stepwise bass 1–♭7–♭6–5, and "a characteristic feature ... is the 7–6 suspension into the 6th of IV6. Such suspensions are almost invariably found in connection with this progression." The Handel example holds the upper note across the bass's arrival and resolves it on the next beat. PDF 291 and 301: the line, not the single chord, carries a passage of this kind, and a chromatic tone spelled ♯ rises (C♯ to D). In form terms this is the end of the development's first key area and the only cadence the G-minor section has; c233 made its bass stepwise and c152 gave it the augmented sixth, but neither can be heard as a progression while the outer voices never overlap. Adler (adler1 PDF 210): the clarinet plays from the faintest pianissimo in every register, so a held pp line at concert D6 (written E6, clarion register, well above the break) is safe and does not need a louder dynamic.

**Fix (dev.ily, applied).** Cl 1 sustains the 7–6 as the top voice:

- `devClOne`, bars 115–122: `R1 | R1*8 | R1*3 | r2 d''2(\p\<` becomes `R1 | R1*6 | r4 d'''2(\pp cis'''4 | d'''4) r4 r2 | R1*3 | r2 d''2(\p\<`

So D6 enters on 121.2 as a consonant 6th over the passing F (the outer voices in 6ths, row 3), is held across the bass E♭ as a 7th, falls to C♯6 on beat 4 and opens to D6 over the bass D on 122.1; the augmented sixth E♭–C♯ now sounds between the outer voices, which is where the progression puts it. Vln I's two solo desks take D6 from the clarinet on 122.2 for their pp answer, so the line passes from one instrument to the next at the same pitch. The bassoon solo, every pizzicato note, the passing F, the Italian sixth's pitches and the 123–130 hand-over are unchanged. On the shared staff the part combiner prints "1." at 121 and no longer repeats it at 126, which is correct, since Cl 1 is still playing alone.

This answers the protection of "the bassoon solo 115–122" (c157, c233): the solo keeps its notes, register and dynamics, and the new note sits a tenth or more above it (D6 against the solo's G3–A3), at pp, entering after the solo's B♭3 has sounded. It does not touch the protected hand-over order (Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I from 123), because the clarinet's 126 entry and notes are unchanged.
