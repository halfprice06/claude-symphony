### 1. 194: the recapitulation's A♭ area never states the movement's 5–♭6–5 in its own key; Cl 1 takes F♭4→E♭4 over V7 (applied)

- **Bars:** 194, beat 1, Cl 1 (the c207 pp background), over V7 of A♭ before V4/2 at 194.3 and I6 at 195.
- **Problem:** A♭ (♭VI) is the finale's largest chromatic area: 187–198, then the A♭ pedal 205–210. F1's 5–♭6–5 (G–A♭–G) gives the whole plan its shape (bar 1 A♭→G; ♭VI 187 … V 220). Inside the A♭ area that figure never sounds in A♭'s own degrees (E♭–F♭–E♭). Movements I and II both colour their A♭ areas with F♭→E♭: movement I's flute motto over the A♭ pedal (I 73–76), and movement II's F♭→E♭ thread, including its V minor-ninth at II 80–81 (Hn 3 F♭4→E♭4). The finale's A♭ statement is the one A♭ area in the symphony without it. F♭ is also E♮ spelled flat, so it is the thread pitch E4.
- **Evidence:**
  - A&S PDF 600–601 (§16): in the Appassionata the turn to A♭ "permits a transposition of the basic motive to E♭–F♭–E♭", and the development then reinterprets it as E♭–E♮–E♭.
  - A&S PDF 597–598 (Ex. 32-16, 32-17 and the note): Haydn joins two areas through one pitch that is spelled two ways, E♭/D♯. The note links C minor to E♭ and C major to E♮, which is this symphony's plan.
  - A&S PDF 603 (Review 5): remote areas are integrated by relating them to the motivic design.
- **Effect:** F♭4 sounds once and falls straight back to E♭4, while the flat spelling still holds. Spelled D♯4, the same pitch class rises to E4 only at 219–220, into the arrival's E4 at 223. The pitch matches movement II's F♭4→E♭4 at 80–81, and the chord matches it too (V minor-ninth of A♭). The cyclic rule "every F♭ resolves to E♭" is kept.
- **Fix (applied, sections/recS.ily, recSClOne bar 194 only, with a c210 comment):**

  The F♭4 is on the second eighth of beat 1, over the cellos' D♭3, the E♭ bass and the tune's held B♭4: an E♭7♭9 sonority. This does not undo c207: only the figure's last bar gains the neighbour note. No quotation and none of the finale's own themes (F1, F2, the chorale top lines) changes.
