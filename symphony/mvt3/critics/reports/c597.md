## Findings

### 1. 103-104: the brass's F1 head is doubled by the strings that stated it at 99 (applied)

- **Bars:** 103-104 (development, letter G).
- **Problem:** The development opens as a dialogue between groups:
  - 99-100: F1's head in F minor in violas, cellos, basses and bassoons.
  - 101-102: the winds answer with the scale figure.
  - 103-104: the head goes up a fourth into the brass (Hn 3-4 a2 and Tbn 1, "ben marc.", c097 and c397).
  - 103.4: the violins enter with the scale answer.

  At 103, however, the violas (B-flat 4), cellos (B-flat 3) and basses (B-flat 2) play the same notes as the horns and Tbn 1, in the same octaves. The brass entry therefore sounds like the strings' 99 statement with brass added, not like a new group's entry. The violins' answer at 103.4 also loses its contrast, because it comes out of a string texture that is already sounding.
- **Evidence:** In Adler's models the brass state a contrapuntal subject alone, pair by pair, "f, ben marc.". Strings and winds give only punctuating chords and never double the subject:
  - Bartók, Concerto for Orchestra 342-364 (Ex. XI-24, PDF 46).
  - Hindemith, Symphonic Metamorphoses 158-188 (Ex. XI-23, PDF 42-43).

  On the same pages Adler says two horns in unison balance one trombone (PDF 44). Hn 3-4 a2 at B-flat 4, Tbn 1 at B-flat 3 and the bass trombone at B-flat 2 therefore give three balanced octaves without string support. The best-register rule (PDF 42) is met: B-flat 4 for the horns, B-flat 3 for the tenor trombone, B-flat 2 to D-flat 2 for the bass trombone.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-597/work/sections/dev.ily):**
  - The violas and cellos rest at 103-104 (`R1*2`). They return on their sf chord at 105.
  - The bass trombone takes the head with the basses at B-flat 2: `bes,2->(\f^\markup \italic "ben marc." f,4. ges,8 | aes,4-.) ges,8( f,8 es,4-.) des,4-. | bes,,4-.\sf r4 r2 | R1 |`. Before, it had a single B-flat 1 stroke on 103.1. Its sf B-flat 1 at 105 now joins the basses' B-flat 1 and Tbn 1's sf.
  - Unchanged: Bn 2 (with Tbn 1), the basses (bass line), Hn 3-4, Tbn 1-2, the violins' scale at 103.4-105, the Cl 2 pedal, the 105 sf chord and all dynamics.

  The 99-106 sequence now runs strings, winds, brass, strings with winds. That is the order of three groups the plan describes ("call and response between wind and string groups"), with the brass as the third group. F1's notes are unchanged; only the orchestration of this one statement changes. The other movement threads need no update.
- **Build:**
  - 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt (as in v597).
  - par8 on 101-106 lists only the head's own octave doublings, as v597 did, now in fewer parts.
  - Its new 103.1 line (Bn E3 to B-flat 3 against the bass E2 to B-flat 2) is a change of player inside the part-combined bassoons and the bass clarinet's E2 ending. It is not one voice moving in octaves.
  - The lowest note on 103.1 is now B-flat 2 (it was the bass trombone's B-flat 1). That is the register of the 99 statement (F2), so the two statements now match.

### 2. 247-254: the trombones double the violins' head two octaves down (not applied, protected)

At R the first-movement head sounds in Fl, Vln I and II (top) and in Tbn 1-2 a2, Bn 1 and the cellos (bottom), and the second-theme head sounds in the middle. The outer voices therefore move in octaves for eight bars. Schoenberg's trombones (PDF 44) and Hindemith's fugato (PDF 42-43) would point to a staggered or independent trombone line. The passage is on cycle 197's do-not-touch list (same reading), and cycle 317 kept "the three-octave head doubling at 247-254". The tuba and contrabassoon give an independent harmonic bass (C-D-F-G), so the octave doubling is a deliberate unison of a quoted theme, and analysis.txt counts it as a doubling run. I did not reopen it.
