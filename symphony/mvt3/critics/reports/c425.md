## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Cellos 209-210: tenor clef inside a continuous run that needs only three ledger lines (applied)

**Bars:** 209-210, Vc (codaOne.ily).

**Problem:** since c407 the cellos double Vln II's rising run an octave lower: A♭2 up to the accented G♭4 on 210.3, eighth notes slurred 4 + 8 (c418). A tenor clef stands at the 209|210 barline, in the middle of the second slur, and a bass clef comes back at 210.4 before the quarter rest. In bass clef the highest note, G♭4, needs three ledger lines. The tenor clef removes them but breaks the line's shape on the page. The run turns down into the middle of the staff just where it is rising to its peak, and it no longer looks like Vln II's run directly above it. The bass clef before the rest is Gould's "and not" example.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches, which a change of clef would obscure." PDF 22: "Between beats, change the clef after rests", and "In a passage of continuous playing, place a new clef between phrases ... It enables the reader to see the pitch contour of the phrase intact." The same score's tenor clefs at 27-38 and 77-81 are justified by B♭4 (four ledger lines); this one is not.

**Fix (applied):** codaOne.ily, `codaOneVc`:
`... aes,8(\< bes, c des) es( f ges aes | bes8 c' des' es') ges'4-> r4\! | }`
(both `\clef tenor` and `\clef bass` removed). At 160 dpi the run now has the same shape as Vln II's an octave lower, with G♭4 on the third ledger line. Notes, slurs, the hairpin and MIDI are unchanged. The cello part changes the same way, which is also correct there.

### 2. Horns 1·2 79-82: B♭2 on five ledger lines in a concert-pitch treble staff (applied)

**Bars:** 79-82, Hn 1·2 staff (sTwo.ily).

**Problem:** c398 moved Hn 2 at 81 to B♭2, and c403 kept B♭2 on 82.4. In the concert-pitch score B♭2 sits on the fifth ledger line below the treble staff (81: semibreve; 82: quarter after an E♭3). The score already has a rule for this case. Since c095, Hn 1·2 take a score-only bass clef at 25-26 and 165-166, where B2–E♭3 needed three to five ledger lines, and the F parts stay in treble. 81-82 is the one place where the rule is not applied.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 21 (three ledger lines, quoted above); PDF 22-23: change clef between phrases, and "Avoid changing a clef during a tied note". Hn 1's G3 is tied from 80 into 81, so the clef cannot go at 81. Gould PDF 13: "Effective communication results from establishing a convention and adopting a consistent approach." The convention here is c095's.

**Fix (applied):** sTwo.ily, score-only clefs in both horn voices, as in pOne.ily:
- `sTwoHnOne`: `... f'4) \breathe | \tag #'score \clef bass es'2.( d'4 | c'4 bes4 aes4\> g4~ | g4 f4) d'2\p | es'2.\< d'4 \breathe | \tag #'score \clef treble }`
- `sTwoHnTwo`: `... | g2\mp bes2 | \tag #'score \clef bass c'1 | f2~ f2\> | bes,1\p | es2.\< bes,4 \breathe | \tag #'score \clef treble }`

The bass clef comes at 78|79, right after Hn 1's breath mark at the end of 78, at the start of the slurred phrase 79-81. The treble clef returns at the 82|83 double bar, after the breath at 82. In 79-82 the staff now needs at most two ledger lines (Hn 1's E♭4) instead of five. The B♭2 sits on the second line. Checked at 160 dpi: the clef is clear of the breath mark and the barline. Parts are unchanged: in F, B♭2 reads F3, three ledger lines, so they stay in treble, as at 25-26. MIDI is unchanged.
