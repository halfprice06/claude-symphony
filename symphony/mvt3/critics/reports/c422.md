## Findings

### 1. Applied. Bar 112: the development's climax cadence in F minor uses ii°6/5 where the reading's climax chord is ♭II6

- **Bars:** 111–113.
- **Problem:** 110–113 is the goal of the development: ff, full brass, lament bass arriving on V7 of F, cadence on F at 113. The bass runs F–D♭–B♭–C–F, a falling-third line (c212). Bar 112.1 sounds B♭–D♭–F–G (ii°6/5), with the top F tied over as a 4–3 above C.
  - A&S give ♭II6 as the chord of exactly this moment: "the frequent use of ♭II6 at climactic moments" (PDF 483). Their model, Ex. 28-1 (Mozart K. 397, bar 52; PDF 482), goes VI → ♭II6 → V at the forte climax.
  - Bar 111.3 is already VI (D♭–F–A♭) of F minor. Only the G of 112.1 stands between this score and that model.
- **Evidence:**
  - With G♭, the soprano at 112 runs G♭6–F6–E6. That is ♭2–1–♯7 with the diminished third filled in, "peculiarly intense … especially when it occurs in the soprano" (PDF 484, Ex. 28-2c).
  - The bass B♭ is the doubled tone (PDF 484, point 4 on PDF 499). ♭2 never moves straight to ♮2 in the soprano (PDF 484, Ex. 28-3).
  - The development's cadence then repeats, a fourth higher, the movement's own large path: ♭VI (A♭, 205–210) – ♭II (D♭, 211–218) – V – I (219–223). Here it is D♭ (VI) – G♭6 (♭II6) – C – F.
  - This is not one Neapolitan too many in the same key, which is why c380 declined one at 133–134. It is in F minor, 50 bars from 163 and 70 bars before the D♭ area.
- **Cost:** the prepared 4–3 at 112 becomes an accented passing F over C, still resolving to E. c188's slurs and the 4–3 on beat 3 are kept as dissonance and resolution.
- **Fix (dev.ily, bar 112 only):**
  - Fl 1, Vln I: `f'''2~( f'''4 e'''4)` → `ges'''2( f'''4 e'''4)`
  - Fl 2, Ob 1, Vln II, Tpt 1: `f''2~( f''4 e''4)` → `ges''2( f''4 e''4)`
  - Hn 2, Hn 4: `f'2~( f'4 e'4)` → `des'2( c'2)`. As G♭–F under Tpt 2's D♭–C they would make fifths. As D♭–C they double Ob 2, Cl 1 and Tpt 2.
  - Vla and Cl 2: `g2 c'8( bes aes g)` → `des'2 c'8( bes aes g)`
  - Bn 1: `f2 c'8( bes aes g)` → `des'2 c'8( bes aes g)`. I first tried `bes2`; it made new octaves with the bass B♭→C at 112.3, so I rejected it.
  - Ob 2, Cl 1, Tpt 2, Tbn 1 (D♭–C), Hn 1·3, Tbn 2 and Tpt 3 (B♭), the bass and the timpani are unchanged.
  - The old comment at dev.ily lines 62–63 (c062: "Bn 1 holds F3 to beat 4") no longer applies to bar 112; the composer should update it.
- **Tested:**
  - Scratch build of /tmp/critic-422/work: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages, and analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels.
  - par8.py on 110–113: same 14 lines as v422, all existing octave doublings of the bass.
  - A direction-aware all-pairs check on 110–113 finds no new fifths; the new octaves are the melody and the D♭–C line doubled in octaves.
  - Page 6 inspected: G♭ engraved with its slur, nothing crowded.
- **Alternative tested and rejected:** the "apparent Neapolitan 6/5" (PDF 495–496, Ex. 28-21). This keeps the top F suspension and puts G♭–G♮ in the viola. G♭3 then sits a minor second from Bn 1's F3 at ff and is covered by the brass, so the ♭II colour would not be heard.
