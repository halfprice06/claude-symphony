## Findings

### 1. 186: applied V6/5 of A♭ before the recapitulation's A♭ area. Tested in the scratch build; not applied.
The unit's Ex. 26-3 (PDF 445) puts an applied chord in front of the pivot to strengthen a modulation. PDF 445 also says a temporary key "requires a strong dominant in the new key". The score does not do this at the one modulation where it could: 185 (V) → 186 (VI, deceptive) → 187, where F2 starts in A♭ on the same chord. I tested the strongest form of it. On 186 beats 3–4, the cellos and basses play pizz. G; Cl 1 moves C4→B♭3; Bn 1 enters pp on D♭3, the seventh, and resolves it to C3, its first note at 187; Cl 2 keeps E♭3. That gives V6/5 of A♭ with clean voice leading:
```
recSBsnOne: ... | r2 des2\pp | \transpose c aes, { e2(\p f4 g4 } | ...
recSClOne:  ... | c'2 bes2 | ...
recSVc:     ... | aes,4 r4 g,4 r4 | ...
recSCb:     ... | aes,,4 r4 g,,4 r4 | ...
```
Result: 0 warnings and no new parallels (par8.py, bars 184–188, shows only the existing octave doublings at 185 and 188). But the score grew to **17 pages**: the bassoon staff appears in the 179–186 system, and 187–198 moves to a page of its own. The music is also worse:
- PDF 443 says the confirming cadence "can appear after intervening material". A♭ already gets a root-position ii–V–I at 191–193, so 186 needs no dominant.
- The point of Ex. 26-7 (PDF 447–448) is that the listener first hears the chord in the old key (here VI after a deceptive cadence) and only later as a "tonic". An applied dominant at 186.3 removes that reinterpretation. It also breaks the V→VI chain 26 / 34→35 / 186→187 (c463).
- It fills the half-bar breath (Cl 2 and bass clarinet `\breathe`; the violas stop on 186.2) before the strings' cantabile entry.
- The exposition's matching seam, 66→67, has no dominant either.

Decision: reverted. No change.

### 2. Proportions: the coda (81 bars) is longer than the recapitulation (48). No change.
Dvořák 9/iv from the corpus file: introduction 9 bars, exposition 118, development 70, recapitulation 77, coda 74. This finale: introduction 10, exposition 88, development and retransition 52, recapitulation 48, coda 81. The recapitulation is 55% of the exposition, against Dvořák's 65%. The difference is planned and the reading supports it. The tonic major is held back until 223, and 199–222 completes the recapitulation's tonal path VI–♭II–Ger6–V–I. That path is the K. 488 plan and Beethoven's I–VI ‖ IV–V–I (PDF 453–454, Ex. 26-14), with ♭II in place of IV. Counting 199–222 as the end of the recapitulation gives 72 bars of recapitulation and 57 of coda, close to Dvořák's balance. The coda stages build: density goes from 20.4 voices (217–220) to 25.6 (221–224), drops to 12–13 at the S-head stage (233–240), and climbs back to 23.5 (257–264) and 26.6 (273–276). The S-head stage is the only sag, and it is the owed soft C-major second-theme head. No change.
