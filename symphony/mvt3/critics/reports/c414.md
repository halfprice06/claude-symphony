## Findings

### 1. 217–218: three sustained doublers cover the echo of theme B (applied)

**Bars.** 216–218. The coda's theme B statement (211–216, trumpets with Fl a2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 and Vln I) ends on D♭, held for three bars. At 217–218 the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 play theme B's head an octave lower, *espress.* and f (A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4).

**Problem.** During those two bars Fl 1·2 (D♭6 a2), Ob 1·2 (D♭5 a2), Cl 1·2 (D♭5/F4) and Tpt 1·2 (D♭5/A♭4) all hold at f from the end of the 216 diminuendo. The trombones, Tpt 3, violas and cellos go down to mp. Tpt 2's A♭4 sits at the top of the echo's register, and two trumpets at f sound louder than two horns at f (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41, 48). So the only melodic event in these bars sits under the loudest sustained sound. The a2 unisons were there for volume in the ff tutti (Adler PDF 250: a2 is for "tutti sections where simple volume is required"). Once the line has stopped on a held note, they only thicken the sound (PDF 249, 257: unison "usually only results in a stronger pitch").

**Evidence from the reading.** Strauss, *Fledermaus* (PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12, m. 83) "suspends the pedal for a moment so that the anacrusis to the second statement ... is clearly heard", which also gives a breathing space. Copland, *Appalachian Spring* (PDF 270, Ex. VIII-41) "orchestrates a diminuendo by cutting out" a doubling instead of only marking it. The score has neither device here. It drops only the tuba (c406). Both devices fit 217–218: the doublers stop and the echo is answered by the rest of the texture.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-414/work/sections/codaTwo.ily).** Fl 2, Ob 2 and Tpt 2 end their D♭/A♭ with the 216 diminuendo, rest at 217–218 and re-enter on the sub. p chord at 219 on their old notes (F♯5, C5, F♯4):

```
codaTwoFluteTwo: ... es'''2.(\ff f'''4 | des'''1)\> | R1\! | R1 | fis''1\subP | ...
codaTwoOboeTwo:  ... es''1\ff | des''1\> | R1\! | R1 | c''1\subP | ...
codaTwoTptTwo:   ... c''2.(\ff des''4 | aes'1)\> | R1\! | R1 | fis'1\subP | ...
```

What stays: the top line D♭–C–B–C (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Tpt 1 and the violins hold D♭ through 218), the D♭ triad (A♭ in the violas and Bn 1, F in Cl 2, Tpt 3, Tbn 1 and the bass), every printed dynamic of the protected 205–223 path, Hn 3·4's rest at 215–218, the cor anglais at 217–218 and the 219 sub. p. The three players also come in fresh on the subito piano, which is easier than dropping from a held f. Tpt 2 had played 211–218 without a rest at f–ff, so the rest also helps endurance (c406, adler2 PDF 8). On the shared staves the second voice prints as a whole rest under the held note (page 12, checked at 110 dpi).

### 2. 187–190: Ob 1 doubles the violins' F2 at pitch (checked, not changed)

Adler PDF 265–266 says octave doublings are clearer than unisons. But his Berlioz example (Ex. VIII-37) shows a flute unison with the first violins adding warmth that the line lacks without it. Here Ob 1 (*dolce*, p) colours the first four bars of a mf violin unison and then leaves, while Hn 1 and the violas carry the lower octave. Moving the oboe up an octave would put it on E♭6 at p, where it is weak. Dropping it would make 187 sound like the strings-only statement at 67. No change.

### 3. 219–222: overlapped wind pairs at sub. p (checked, not changed)

Ob 1·2 a2 on C5, Tpt 1 with them, and Cl 2, Tpt 2 and Hn 2 on F♯4: this is the overlapped voicing of Ex. VIII-21d. Adler PDF 257 says it is "still common" in full tutti, and the chord is a full-orchestra German sixth heading for the fff arrival. Splitting the a2 here would thin the crescendo that c198 protects. No change.
