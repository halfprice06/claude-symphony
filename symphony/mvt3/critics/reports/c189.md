## Holistic comparison with Borodin 2/iv, and v189 against v179

Borodin wins his finale with rhythm and block scoring: themes in unison or octaves, plain held harmony, syncopated accents, and colour saved for the dance tuttis. This finale meets that standard in its fast tuttis. The hammered tutti at 199–204, the combination at 247–254 with off-beat P-head entries, and the strokes at 147–150 are scored in clear blocks. It also saves a colour for one place, as Borodin saves his cymbals: string tremolo and C7 only at 223. It goes beyond Borodin in harmonic planning. The C – A♭ – E thirds cycle, the two forms of III (E♭ in the exposition, E at 243, A&S PDF 531), and the one root-position V–I at 262–263 are a larger design than his dance chain. Where it differs from Borodin is tempo: this coda has five tempo marks where Borodin keeps one pulse. That plan is protected (c043, c063, c173, c179, Dan's note on R), and I do not re-raise it.

Has batch 181–188 made the movement fussier? No. Most of the batch removed attacks or faults rather than adding detail:
- c188 ties held common tones instead of re-striking them every bar, which is plainer, not fussier. I checked each new tie at 27–34: every tied tone stays consonant (reading note 3).
- c181 removed the parallel fifths at 127 (Bn 1/violas) and the augmented second B3–A♭3 in the violas at 26.
- c182 removed the parallel fifths at 239 (Hn 3·4 against the cellos and basses) and the rising sevenths in Bn 2/Cbsn at 225–227.
- c183's V4/2 at 133–134 gives the G area its 7th before the chain. A&S Unit 22 (PDF 375): the chain is then plainly directed.
- c188's slurs at 111–112 let the climax suspension resolve softly in every doubling (A&S PDF 348).

The added doublings are few and each has a reason: bass clarinet 27–34, Ob 2 19–26, Cl 2 110–113, Bn 1 99–100, Fl 2 242. The checkers find no new fifths or octaves from them that are not doublings of one line.

Only one thing got worse than v179, and it is in the protected last cadence. c184 moved Hn 3 to A♭3 at 259–260, and c185 then swapped the Hn 3·4 notes to avoid a crossing. Together they left Hn 3 on C4 at 259–260, leaping to E♭4 at 261 while the bass goes F→A♭. That is a parallel fifth against seven bass instruments at ff, in the approach to the symphony's only authentic cadence. In v179 Hn 3 went F4→E♭4 and Hn 4 C4→A♭3, with no fifths. p5.py shows the change: v179 has nothing at 261, v189 has 28 pairs at 261.1.

## Findings

### 1. 259–261, Hn 3: parallel fifths with the bass into the German sixth (applied)
Problem: Hn 3 holds C4 at 259–260 (iiø6/5 over F) and leaps up a minor third to E♭4 at 261 (German sixth over A♭). The bass (Tuba, B.tbn, Bn 2, Cbsn, Vc, Cb, B.cl) rises F→A♭, so Hn 3 makes a perfect 12th → 12th with each bass part. That is 28 pairs in p5.py, new since c184/c185. The fifth is exposed: Hn 3 plays marcato ff half notes (c019), and nothing else in the chord moves in that rhythm.
Evidence: A&S Unit 29, PDF 505: the German sixth "typically moves to a V expanded by a cadential 6/4", and its tones come by half step (PDF 503: "leading-tone chromaticism"). The iiø6/5→Ger6 should be voiced the same way: the 7th C held as the common tone (Unit 24, PDF 405, the 7th's resolution governs the succession), D rising a half step to E♭, A♭ held. A&S Unit 5 forbids parallel 5ths between any two voices. Unit 20, PDF 329 shows the only licence, a chordal skip that breaks up the motion, and this leap is not one.
Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-189/work/sections/finale.ily, finaleHnThree): Hn 3 takes D4 at 259–260 and steps D4→E♭4 into 261:
`c'2->\< c'2 | c'2-> c'2 | es'2-> es'2` → `d'2->\< d'2 | d'2-> d'2 | es'2-> es'2`
Hn 4 keeps A♭3 (c184's two brass on the borrowed A♭: Tbn 2, Hn 4), so there is no crossing (c185). Hn 3 doubles Hn 1's D4 for two bars and then splits off to E♭. The 7th C stays in Ob 2, Cl 2, Vln II and violas. The top line D–C–B–C, the bass, the cut at 264.1 and the cadence are untouched. Result: p5.py shows no fifths at 261. par.py adds only two artefacts of Cl 1's arpeggio and the part-combined Hn 3·4 staff (Hn 4's A♭3 is held, not moved).

### 2. 27–34, bass clarinet on the bass statement of F1 (c187): keep
I checked whether the new doubling makes the passage thicker than a master would write. It does not. The bass clarinet takes the basses' C3 octave, which c187 found bare under six parts at C4, and it is silent before and after. The doubling is a whole line in one octave, as Borodin's tuttis are, not an added inner voice. Rimsky-Korsakov (rimsky-principles PDF 77, cited by c187) names basses with bass clarinet as one of the best unison pairs. Decided: keep. par.py's new octaves at 30.3 and 33.4 are between the bass statement and the violins' countersubject, and they already existed for the cellos.

### 3. 247–254, bow marks (c186): keep, and add no more
These are the only marks in the batch that make the page look busier: down-bows on the pickups, two up-bows in a row, retaken down-bows. They put the accents on down-bows at the fastest tempo of the movement (2 = 88), which is the case Adler gives for marking bowings (adler1 PDF 38). Decided: keep them. Later critics should not add bow marks in 255–279, where the long notes need none.

### 4. 9–10, the c188 ties in the dominant crescendo: keep
The held dominant is now tied from 9 into 10, with one accented attack on 10.3. That attack is shared by every wind and the violas, so the crescendo arrives on a single stroke, as at 2 and 6. A&S PDF 347: a tied preparation that stays consonant is not a suspension, and there is no dissonance to treat. Decided: keep.
