## Findings

### 1. 120: complete the V7 of G minor under the bassoon solo as a V♭9 whose 9th and 7th resolve together (applied)
**Problem.** The development's G-minor episode reaches its dominant at 120. The bassoon solo falls D4–C4–B♭3–A3 over the D bass, Vln I has its pp answer on F♯5, and Vln II and the violas play two identical pizzicato off-beat chords, D5/F♯5 over A4/D5. The second chord, 120.4, has nothing new to say. The score's only root-position V♭9 in a minor key that goes to its tonic (169) keeps the A♭ as a held common tone from the French sixth and drops it before 171, so this form never sounds in the piece. Unit 27 gives it first: a 9th that enters after V has begun, as a neighbour 5–♭6–5, with the 9th and 7th falling in parallel into the 5th and 3rd of i.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 463–464, Ex. 27-1 and 27-2: "The 9th and 7th resolve by parallel motion into the 5th and 3rd of tonic harmony"; "if the 9th appears in an inner voice, parallel 6ths become possible" (here parallel 3rds). PDF 464 sec. 2: "the most characteristic function of 6̂ is to form an upper neighbor to 5̂; using V9 gives us a new way to harmonize 5̂–6̂–5̂". PDF 467 sec. 4: unprepared 9ths are frequent "if the 9th enters ... after dominant harmony has begun to sound". PDF 470, Ex. 27-11: the upper four notes form vii°7, as at 120.4. Dvořák's Op. 88/iv in Ex. 27-9 (PDF 469) is the orchestral model: pizzicato and wind minor ninths over a sustained bass.
**Fix (dev.ily, applied).** `devVlnTwo` bar 120: `r4 <d'' fis''>4 r4 <d'' fis''>4` → `r4 <d'' fis''>4 r4 <es'' fis''>4`. `devVla` bar 120: `r4 <a' d''>4 r4 <a' d''>4` → `r4 <a' d''>4 r4 <a' c''>4`. At 120.4 the chord is F♯–A–C–E♭ over the D bass of 120.1. At 121.2 Vln II's E♭5 falls to D5 and the violas' C5 to B♭4, in parallel thirds into the existing chord D5/G5 over G4/B♭4. F♯5 goes to G5 and A4 to G4. Both double stops are third shapes (4–1 and 4–2) in first position and are easy pizzicato. The bassoon solo, Vln I, the bass, the dynamics and c323's 121–122 are unchanged. **Protection answered:** c209 narrowed the old "115–130" to "115–118 and the bassoon solo", and c172 and c323 have already changed accompaniment notes at 115 and 121. This change touches only two off-beat accompaniment notes in 120.

### 2. 81: the harp strikes the root B♭4 a major seventh above the violas' minor ninth (applied)
**Problem.** At 81, the V7 that closes the E♭-major statement of F2, the violas take the mixture ♭9 C♭4 as a neighbour (A♭3–C♭4–B♭3, A&S Ex. 27-3). On the same beat the harp's arpeggio strikes B♭4, the C♭'s own tone of resolution, a major seventh above it. Hn 1 also holds D4, the 3rd, above it. So the 9th sits under both its resolution and the 3rd. It sounds as a blurred major seventh, not as a ♭6̂ leaning on 5̂. In the recapitulation's version at 194, Cl 1's F♭4 has no E♭ above it in its own register, and the 9th is clear.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 463: "The 9th appears mostly in the soprano". PDF 469–470 and Ex. 27-10: when the 9th sounds together with its tone of resolution in a voice other than the bass, "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution", unless wide spacing keeps the texture clear (Unit 21 sec. 12, cited there). PDF 470, Ex. 27-11: in V♭9 the 9th stands above the root's upper octave, never a seventh below it.
**Fix (sTwo.ily, applied).** `sTwoHarpR` bar 81, first group: `f'8 aes'8 bes'8 d''8` → `f'8 aes'8 d''8 f''8`. The harp sounds D5 on beat 2 and F5 (with the tune) on 2.5, and returns to F4–A♭4–B♭4–D5 on beats 3–4 when the violas have resolved to B♭3. There are no new pedal changes (D♮, F, A♭ are already set). The violas' line, tune, dynamics and c241's suspension are unchanged.
