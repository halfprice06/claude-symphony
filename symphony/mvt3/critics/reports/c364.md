## Findings

### 1. 154 and 162: Tbn 1 holds the leading tone over the leading-tone bass (applied)
**Problem.** In the recapitulation's F1, Tbn 1 holds B3 for four bars (151–154: `c'1 | c'1 | b1 | b1`, and the same at 159–162). At 153 and 161 the bass is G or F, so B is the chord's third and is fine. At 154 and 162 the bass moves to B (Bn, B.tbn, Vc, Cbsn, Tuba, Cb). The chord at 154 is V6/5, and at 162 it is V♭9 over B, with A♭ in the tune. In both bars the trombone keeps a sustained B3 above the B bass. The exposition (13–14) has no trombones, so the problem is only in the recapitulation.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 83: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts". The good and bad examples at V6/5, V4/3 and V2 show this. PDF 84 extends the rule to other seventh and diminished-seventh chords. A held brass note is the most audible place for this doubling. The moving clarinet and Vln II figures that touch B are the "considerable licence … in rapid passages of different texture" that PDF 84 allows, so I left them alone. c362 fixed the same doubling at 141–144 (Hn 2, Tbn 2).

**Fix (recP.ily).**
```
recPTbnOne = { c'1\mf | c'1 | b1 | d'1 | c'2 bes2 | ... | c'2 b4 c'4 | d'2 c'2 | b1 | d'1 | c'2 des'4. c'8 | ...
```
Tbn 1 now plays D4 at 154 and 162, an octave above Tbn 2's D3 (a concord in one colour, PDF 90 rule 4). It moves by step to C4 at 155 and 163. The tune, bass, dynamics and every other part are unchanged.

### 2. 136–138: clarinets and bassoons each move in parallel tritones (applied)
**Problem.** In the retransition's falling diminished-seventh chain, the wind pairs each hold a tritone pp for three bars. Cl 1·2 play B♭4/E4, A4/E♭4 and A♭4/D4, and Bn 1·2 play G3/C♯3, G♭3/C3 and F3/B2. Each pair of one colour has the dissonance inside it. The chord is in wide four-part spacing.

**Evidence.** PDF 90 rule 4: "Concords (octaves, thirds and sixths) and not discords (fifths, fourths, seconds and sevenths), should be given to instruments of the same kind or colour". PDF 91: "When chords are in widely-divided four-part harmony notes may be allotted in pairs to two different tone qualities, adhering to the normal order of register". The "Good" example on that page has each pair a third or sixth apart. In a diminished seventh both minor thirds are concords, so each pair can take one.

**Fix (retr.ily).**
```
retrClTwo  = { R1 | g'1\p\< | fis'1 | f'1\subP | ...
retrBsnOne = { R1 | e1\p\< | es1 | d1\subP | ...
```
The clarinets now play B♭4/G4, A4/F♯4 and A♭4/F4, and the bassoons E3/C♯3, E♭3/C3 and D3/B2. Each pair moves in minor thirds, and the two colours stay in register order. The pitch classes of every chord, the strings, the stopped horns, the dynamics and the chain itself are unchanged. At 139 Bn 1 rises D3→F3, a third, into its held F. c198 and c223 listed 135–138 as do-not-touch for its harmony (the chain into the stop chord), and that harmony is untouched. Rule 4's exception ("when discords are to be emphasised") does not apply: the strings already carry the tritones (violas F4/B4…E♭4/A4), and the winds only colour the chain.

### 3. The three-of-a-family chord with C.A. or B.cl. is never used (not applied)
PDF 93–95 teaches a close three-part chord in one family whose lowest member is the low instrument (2 Ob + C.A., 2 Cl + B.cl.), or three of one colour plus a fourth. The score never uses the cor anglais or bass clarinet this way. The soft held-chord places where it would fit are all protected: 167–170 (c198 diminuendo order, stopped horns c108/c210), 95–98 (c315 horn pad), 219–222 (c349 oboe unison; 205–223 dynamic path) and 235–242 (S-head texture). In every other soft passage the clarinets play figures, not held chords. I decided not to add it. Putting it into a protected bar would break more than the colour adds.
