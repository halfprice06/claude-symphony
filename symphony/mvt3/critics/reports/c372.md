## Findings

**1. 129 beats 3–4: the 6/4 on the weak half of the bar becomes IV6, and the 7th of 130's V7 is prepared (applied).**

Problem. In the development's G-major F2 (123–132), bar 129 is I6 (bass B) on beats 1–2. On beats 3–4 the cellos and basses move to D under the tune's G5 (Vln I, Fl 1, Vln II an octave lower), so beats 3–4 sound D–G–B, a 6/4. It resolves to V7 on the downbeat of 130, over the same bass D. The 6/4 falls on the weak half-note beat and its resolution on the strong one, with the bass repeated from weak to strong. This is the pattern A&S mark "never!" in Ex. 10-6a (PDF 155). The rules are on PDF 154: "the cadential 6/4 must appear in a metrically strong position; no weak-to-strong bass repetitions are permitted". See also PDF 160 point 3, PDF 149 section 15 and PDF 151 point 9. The harmony also reached V from I6 through an arpeggiated tonic, which A&S call the less intense I–I6–V (PDF 135), with no intermediate harmony before the half cadence.

Why not the obvious fixes. A C bass on beat 3 (IV or II6/5, the stepwise 3–4–5 of Ex. 9-12) moves C–G to D–A in outer fifths against the tune's G–A, which is the danger PDF 136 warns of. A whole-bar 6/4 at 129 would sit on the strong seventh bar, but it would be approached by V7 (128) over the same D, against PDF 160 point 7 and PDF 154. I tested IV6 instead: an intermediate harmony (PDF 135) with the bass falling a step to V, in contrary motion to the tune (E–G, a 10th, to D–A).
