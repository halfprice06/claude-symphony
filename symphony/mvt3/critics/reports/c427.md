## Findings

### 1. Parts book: "crescendo too small" at bar 149 in the bass clarinet (applied)

Bars: 149 (sections/retr.ily line 43, `retrBassCl`), the `sfp <` added in c408.

Problem: `lilypond parts-book.ly` printed one warning, `retr.ily:43:172: warning: crescendo too small`. In the bass clarinet's one-page part, bar 149 starts a system that also holds the long numbered rests 151-210. The spacer compresses 149 so much that the hairpin from the tied G2 to the ff at 150 falls below its minimum length and is not drawn. The player sees "sfp" with no crescendo to "ff".

Evidence: MOLA's rule (cited by c165, c315) that extracted parts are proofread for collisions and missing marks. The score already handles the same problem in the intro: `introFluteOne` etc. at bar 10 (sections/intro.ily line 22) uses a part-only minimum hairpin length with spacing rods.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-427/work/sections/retr.ily), same idiom as intro.ily line 22, part-only:

```lilypond
% c427 (parts book 'crescendo too small' at 149 since c408): part-only minimum hairpin length with spacing rods, as at intro 10 (score and MIDI unchanged).
retrBassCl = { ... | g,1->\ff | g,1 | \tag #'part { \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #6 \once \override Staff.Hairpin.springs-and-rods = #ly:spanner::set-spacing-rods } g,1~\sfp\< | g,2.\ff d4 | }
```

Result: the parts book builds with 0 warnings (was 1). The bass clarinet part stays on one page, and the hairpin is now drawn between "sfp" and "ff" at 149-150 (checked as an image). The score is unchanged: score.midi is byte-identical to v427's, build-warnings.txt is empty, 16 pages, and analysis.txt matches v427 apart from blank lines.

### 2. 181-183: octave doubling above the cor anglais at its climax (tested, not applied)

Bars: 181.4-183, Fl 1 (`recSFluteOne`).

Candidate: Bizet (PDF 279) keeps the instruments apart from the voice and doubles it an octave higher only at its dramatic climax. At 181-183 Fl 1 joins the cor anglais and muted Tpt 1 at the unison (D5 | G5-F5-E♭5-D5 | C5-B♭4), p < mf >. The test put Fl 1 an octave higher (D6 | G6-F6-E♭6-D6 | C6-B♭5):

```lilypond
r2 r4 ... d'''4(\p\< | g'''4\mf f'''4\> es'''4 d'''4) | c'''2.(\p bes''4) |
```

Build of the candidate (/tmp/critic-427/cand): 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels; par8.py over 180-184 reports nothing; the only analysis change is the 181-184 top note (G5 → G6).

Why it is not better: Bizet's doubling is a string section above a voice, one colour family over another, and the voice keeps the lower octave as its own. Here the doubling instrument would be a flute in its bright third octave, mf, over a muted trumpet and a cor anglais at p-mf. The flute would become the top voice and take the climax away from the soloists, which is what PDF 267 (no flutes against a solo flute, "no color competition"), PDF 274 (keep the accompaniment registrally apart from a woodwind solo) and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 140-141 (a solo's own timbre should not be replaced by a mixed one) warn against. At the unison the flute only reinforces the peak in the soloists' register. That is the Liszt heterophony of PDF 273-274, and it already follows the chapter. The candidate also falls in 171-186, which is protected (c349, c063). Decision: keep v427.
