## What else I studied

## Findings

### 1. Timpani at the combined heads, 247-254: ff, one level above the themes it accompanies (applied)

**Bars:** 247-254 (timpani C/G strokes at 247, 249, 250, 251, 254).

**Problem:** At 247 the timpani play the nonthematic tonic-dominant pulse `c4 r8 c8 c4 r8 c8` at ff. Tpt 3 plays the same rhythm on the same pitch class at f. The trombones state the P head at f (c185) in the same C3 register as the timpani. In the MIDI the timpani are at velocity 89 against Tpt 3's 71. The accompaniment figure is therefore louder than the theme sharing its register, and it covers the trombones' head on the downbeats where they enter together. The sHead roll at 246 already ends at f, so the ff at 247 is also a sudden step up that no other part takes.

**Evidence:**
- Bloch (adler2 184): in a passage that combines themes, the timpani and bass drum are marked softer because a fortissimo would cover the combination.
- Jupiter coda (adler2 176): the horns, trumpets and timpani supply a cohesive tonic-dominant pedal *under* the thematic entries.
- Schoenberg's Hauptstimme principle (adler2 184): the subsidiary voice is marked below the principal ones.
- d'Indy (adler2 147-148): balance in a tutti is done with dynamics as well as with notes. Here that has already been done for the trombones (c185) and for Tpt 3 (c227), but not for the timpani.

**Answer to the protection:** HISTORY.md protects the "247-254 layout": the notes, octaves and choirs of the three heads. This fix changes no note, rest, octave or instrument. It only brings the timpani into line with the f/ff balance that c185 and c227 set for this passage.

**Fix** (sections/finale.ily, `finaleTimp`, first bar):
```
finaleTimp = { c4\f r8 c8 c4 r8 c8 | R1 | ...
```
(was `c4\ff`). Nothing else changes. The next timpani dynamic is the `aes,1:32\f\<` roll at 261, so the roll and fff at 263 are untouched. The timpani have their own staff, so the shared-staff dynamic rule does not apply.

### 2. The finale's own subjects are combined only once, briefly, at 130-132 (not applied)

**Bars:** 130-132; coda 223-279.

**Problem:** Mozart's coda is convincing because it combines the finale's own material (adler2 172-176). Ours combines the heads of movement I at 247-254. F1 and F2 meet only for three bars in the development.

**Decision:** I am not applying a change. 223-254 and the development layout are protected. Adding F2 against 247-254 would break Adler's rule (adler2 177-178) that each combined theme needs its own choir and articulation, because every choir is already used there. The cyclic combination is a deliberate plan of this symphony and suits it. No change.

### 3. Tpt 3 rests at 251-254 inside the pedal (not applied)

**Bars:** 251-254.

**Problem:** Mozart keeps the pedal continuous through the combination. Here Tpt 3 drops out after four bars.

**Decision:** I am not applying a change. Tpt 3 rests so that it can re-enter fresh at 255 (c227). That follows Adler's own principle of holding back a colour (adler2 37, 155-156). The timpani keep the pedal at 254, so the pulse does not stop. No change.
