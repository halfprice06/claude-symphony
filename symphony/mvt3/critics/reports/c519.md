## Findings

### 1. 101–102 and 105–106: the soft pad puts the leading tone in three octaves (applied in /tmp/critic-519/work)

- **Problem.** At 101.2–102 the pad under the wind scale figure is B.cl E2, Bn 1 E3 and Hn 1·2 G4/E4. At 105.2–106 it is A2/A3 with horns C5/A4.
  - The leading tone of V6/5 of F (and of B♭) sounds in three octaves, and one of them is in an upper part.
  - The pad has no root and no seventh. Those come only as passing eighths in the scale figure.
  - My scan flags 105.2 and 106.3 for this.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky PDF 83, with a "Bad" example: "the bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts".
  - PDF 85: "make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts".
  - PDF 90 rule 4: give concords to instruments of one kind.
- **Earlier decisions.** c247 kept this pad (Rimsky PDF 108). Its reason was that the horns' F/A♭ over E is F minor's tonic over its leading tone, and that "the bass E has no common tone with the neighbour chord, so no brass part can be stationary". That reason is answered here:
  - Hn 1's neighbour G4–A♭4–G4 stays. So does the F-minor neighbour chord over E, and the same at 105–106.
  - Only Hn 2 moves. It now holds the chord's seventh a sixth below Hn 1, so the horns move in sixths (concords).
- **Result.**
  - The pad is a complete V6/5 (C7/E, F7/A), with the leading tone only in the bass line (B.cl and Bn 1 in octaves).
  - Hn 2's B♭3 at 101–102 is the pitch that Tbn 1 takes for the B♭-minor head at 103.
  - c239's sf-p reason still holds: the dynamics are unchanged.
- **Fix (sections/dev.ily, devHnTwo):**
  `r4 e'2.\p | f'2 e'2 | R1*2 | r4 a'2.\p | bes'2 a'2 |` → `r4 bes2.\p | c'2 bes2 | R1*2 | r4 es'2.\p | f'2 es'2 |`
  with a c519 comment above the variable.
- **Checks.**
  - par8 on 99–108 is identical to v519 (only bass doublings).
  - allpairs_par on 99–108 is identical (328 lines).
  - The dominant-inversion scan now flags only the bassoon's bass octave.
  - Page 6 viewed: the horn sixths engrave cleanly.

### 2. Holistic comparison with Schumann 4/iv (no change)

- **What Schumann does.** The finale arrives through a Langsam transition over a dominant pedal. In it the first movement's figure and a brass motto build to the Lebhaft. The main theme grows from the first movement. The development works the dotted figure contrapuntally. The coda speeds up twice (Schneller, Presto) and ends on short tutti chords.
- **Where v519 differs.** It opens after a pause with a loud German sixth, its first theme is new, and its coda broadens (Largamente 273–279). All three follow binding cyclic rules (BRIEF; c319 recorded the same result) and stay.
- **Where v519 is closer to the books than Schumann.** Schumann doubles strings and winds in unison almost throughout. This score keeps one wind colour per string group and puts its themes in separate registers (77–80, 247–254, 265–272), as PDF 76–78 recommend.
- **Verdict.** A conductor would take this for a professional late-Romantic finale. Two things still mark it as less than a master's work: the six-stage coda (protected; noted since c339) and some soft pads built from doubled bass notes. Finding 1 fixes the clearest example of the second.

### 3. 245.3–246: the oboes hold an open fifth under the violins' run (tested, declined)

- **Problem.** Ob 1 G5 and Ob 2 C5 hold an open fifth for six beats, p<, while Vln I·II and Fl 1 run up to G6. This goes against PDF 97 item 4 and PDF 90 rule 4.
- **Alternatives I tested and declined:**
  - Ob 2 F5 doubles the seventh with Cl 1's F5, and only one of them can resolve to E at 247.
  - Ob 2 E5 makes a minor second with Cl 1.
  - Ob 2 keeps B4, which clashes with the horn call's held C in Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2.
- **Why the fifth stays.** Ob 2's B→C doubles the horn call's own B→C, and under the stringendo tutti the fifth is not heard as hollow.

### 4. 247–254: the bass trombone in unison with Tbn 1·2 (tested on paper, declined)

- **The idea.** Rimsky PDF 73 (Ex. 75, 77) shows three trombones in unison as the strongest brass line.
- **Why not.** Adding the bass trombone to Tbn 1·2's movement-I head at G2–A3 would cover the S head in Tpt 1, Hn 1·2, Cl and Vla, which is the point of the combination (c024: separate registers). The bass trombone's rest from 247 to 254 also gives it a fresh entry at 255. No change.
