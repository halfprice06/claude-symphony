I re-raise none of these except the c051 note at 58, and I answer its reason in Finding 2.

How the themes stand against the models: F1's head (falling fourth plus dotted rhythm) is the movement's hook, and it is heard more than ten times. The chorale is the motto, so its memorability is earned in movement I. F2 is the one tune a listener should hum on the way out, but its consequent is rhythmically flat. Bars 59–64 and 75–80 repeat "dotted half + quarter | four quarters" three times, and the peak bar (62, 78) has the same four even quarters as 60, 64, 76 and 80. In Dvořák 9/iv's clarinet theme, Tchaikovsky 5/iv's second theme and Brahms 1/iv's big tune, the climax note is longer than the notes around it. Here it is a quarter at 2 = 72.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 78: F2's climax in the violins is four even quarters, the same rhythm as the bars around it (applied; F2 changed)

**Problem.** The violins' major statement (67–82) uses the trumpet's note values throughout. Its climax bar 78 (B♭6 reached by the rising fourth, f, down-bow) is `bes4 aes4 g4 f4`. That is the rhythm of 68, 72, 76 and 80, so the peak gets no more time than a passing bar. Sebesky PDF 210 (Ex. 277 vs 278): a lead line in even values loses its character, and what the good version has and the dull one lacks is a change of durations. PDF 207–208 (Ex. 273): a change of note values is what "gives extra push" into the next phrase. PDF 204: the string statement should not be one that a trumpet could play just as well. PDF 220, question 4: a restated tune should be made fresh without overdoing it.

**Fix (sTwo.ily).** 78 in Vln I `bes'''4.(\f\downbow aes'''8 g'''4 f'''4)`, Vln II `bes''4.(\f\downbow aes''8 g''4 f''4)`, Fl 1 `bes'''4.\f aes'''8 g'''4 f'''4)`, Ob 1 `bes''4.\f aes''8 g''4 f''4)`. The peak lasts half as long again. The A♭ becomes a short passing note and meets the harp's E♭5 instead of its C5. The bar now has F1's own dotted head rhythm (`g'4. aes'8`), so the second theme's climax echoes the first theme. Pitches, slur, down-bow (c018), the swell plan (c138) and the breath after 78 (c126) are kept. I left the trumpet's 62 and the recapitulation's 182 plain: the solo is protected (c041, c057, c001's "the solo stays plain"), and the difference between the soft solo and the violins' f restatement is the point of PDF 220 q. 4. 187–198 does not reach this bar. **This changes F2** (the violins' major statement only). finale.md's F2 entry should say "78: dotted peak B♭–A♭ in Vln I·II, Fl 1, Ob 1 since v142". No other movement quotes F2.

### 2. 58 and 178: under the solo's whole-bar half cadence nothing moves but the pizzicato (applied)

**Problem.** At 58 the trumpet holds F4 for a whole bar (V of E♭ minor, the end of the antecedent). Underneath, Cl 1 holds D4 and rests on beat 4, Bn 1 holds D3, Cl 2 has two half notes, and the cellos pluck once. 178 (muted Tpt + cor anglais on D4, V of C minor) is the same. Since c051 the major statements push into their consequent at 74 and 194 (counter A♭–G–F–A♭ in quarters). The minor statements do not, so the solo's antecedent ends in a dead bar. This is Sebesky's case on PDF 207 ("carry one bar into the next"), PDF 207–208 (Ex. 271, a diatonic inner melody inside the chord), PDF 212 (Ex. 280, the background moves where the solo holds) and PDF 222–223 (Ex. 286, the fill goes where the voice holds for six beats).

**Answer to the protection (c004, c007, c051).** The protection keeps the accompaniment's scoring: an undoubled trumpet, pizzicato strings, pp clarinets and bassoons, and nothing in the trumpet's register. All of that stays. One pad voice that already moves (Cl 2, B♭3–A♭3) moves in quarters instead of halves, below the solo, at pp, with no new instrument and no new dynamic.

**Fix.** sOne.ily `sOneClTwo` bar 58: `bes4( aes4 f4 aes4)` (was `bes2 aes2`). recS.ily `recSClTwo` bar 178: `g4( f4 d4 f4) \breathe` (was `g2 f2 \breathe`). The seventh falls to the tonic third at 59 and 179, as at 74 and 194. The D3 at 178 is the clarinet's written low E, which is safe at pp.

### 3. 81: the counter-melody moves in plain parallel sixths with Vln II on the bar before the animando (applied)

**Problem.** At 81 the tune falls F–D in half notes (Vln II F4–D4 sul G, Vln I an octave higher). The Vla+Vc counter falls A♭3–F3 in half notes, so the two lines run in major sixths, and the bar loses the counter's independence just as the animando begins. Sebesky PDF 209 (Ex. 275): "instead of just voicing the violins in parallel descending thirds", put passing tones in the lower line. PDF 208 (Ex. 273) asks for quarters to push into the next phrase. c051 left 81 alone. Copying 74's A♭–G–F–A♭ here would not work. G3 would rub a semitone against Hn 2's held A♭3, and beat 4's A♭3→G3 would move in unison with Hn 2's A♭3→G3.

**Fix (sTwo.ily, `sTwoVla` and `sTwoVc`).** 81 `aes4( bes4 aes4 f4)` (was `aes2( f2)`). Every note is a tone of V7, and the neighbour B♭ breaks the parallel. Beat 4's F rises to G at 82 in sixths with the tune's D→E♭ and against Hn 2's A♭→G. The all-pairs fifths/octaves list is unchanged.
