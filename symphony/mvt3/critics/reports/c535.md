## Findings

**1. Bar 204, violas: unmarked double notes just before "div." (applied).**
Problem: after single-note lines in 199–203 the violas play two ff down-bow strokes on D4/F4 with no label, and "div." follows at 205.1. Read in sequence, a player cannot tell whether 204 is already divided. Adler PDF 30 says an unmarked chord is a double-stop and that "it is safer to write non div.", and Ex. II-12 labels both cases. The score already does this at its other ff string strokes (1, 5 since c366; 91; 277 since c274). D4 with F4 is an easy minor third across the G and D strings.
Fix (codaOne.ily, codaOneVla): `<d' f'>4->\downbow^\markup \italic "non div." <d' f'>4->\downbow r2`. Text only. The protected hammered layout of 199–204 has the same notes, bowing and dynamics; MIDI is identical. On page 11 the text sits above the down-bow signs with no collision.

**2. 247–262, 265–275 (and 199–208) basses: measured-tremolo shorthand (tested, declined).**
Adler PDF 50 (Ex. II-47) presents the measured tremolo as shorthand for repeated détaché notes, so I tested `c,2:8 c,2:8` for the basses' written-out eighths on paper and against the build. I did not apply it, for three reasons. First, the written eighths are already clear and take no extra horizontal space, because the violins set the spacing in the same bars. Second, a single stroke next to the violins' three-stroke unmeasured tremolo (255–262) is easy to misread at sight. Third, LilyPond's MIDI plays stem tremolo as one held note (score.ly, c325), so the basses' drive would disappear from score.midi. The score stays as it is.

**3. Bar 211, violas: second "div." (checked, no change).**
"div." is printed at 205 and again at 211 with no "unis." between them. Adler PDF 30 asks only for the change. At 211, though, the reprint comes after a new section and a change of texture, and a part restates its divisi there by courtesy. It is harmless, so I left it.
