## Findings

### 1. Violas 211–218: restate "div." (applied)
Bars: 211–218. The violas hold thirds and fourths in half notes, ff then f, for eight bars: D♭4–F4, G♭4–B♭4, A♭4–C5, A♭4–D♭5. The only "div." is at 205, six bars earlier; between it and 211 come a double bar, a key change to D♭, letter O, Largamente and a page turn in the score. A player reading 211 sees double stops without any marking. Adler PDF 76 says orchestral double stops should be only the easy ones and that what is hard for one player is simple divisi; PDF 88 applies the same practice to the viola. Fourths in particular (A♭4–D♭5 at 215–218, no open string) are an intonation risk if the whole section plays them as double stops for four bars. The violas already restate "div." at 219, so 211 was the gap.
Fix (codaTwo.ily): `codaTwoVla = { <des' f'>2\ff\>^\markup \italic "div." q | ...`. No sounding change.

### 2. Double basses 217–218: "change bow at will" on the tied F (applied)
Bars: 217–218. Basses hold F2 (written F3) tied across two bars at f in Largamente 2 = 72, about 3.3 s, then breathe into the sub. p at 219. Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter", and "the way to do this [a lengthy tenuto] is to ask the players to change bow at will." The score already uses `\cbaw` for the same case at 139 and 142 (retransition), 224–227 (arrival) and 235. The cellos have a longer bow and keep the tie unmarked.
Fix (codaTwo.ily): `... | f,1~\cbaw | f,1 | ...` in `codaTwoCb`. No sounding change.

Nothing else in 151–222 needs changing under this reading. The checks that led to "no change" are below, so the composer does not need to repeat them.

### Checked and kept (decided, no change)
- **Violins in A♭, 187–198.** The tune lies B♭4–B♭5, one slur per bar (1.4 s at 2 = 84), alternating bows from 187 so that 198's E♭6 falls on the planned down-bow (c018). No open A or E in the line (Adler PDF 73–74). Sul A (PDF 75) would put B♭5 at 189 in 7th position for the whole section, so I leave the string choice to the players. Vln II's octave drop at 197 (A♭4–B♭4–E♭5) avoids a unison E♭6 in both sections and is kept.
- **Vln I extremes.** A♭6 at 162 and 203 and B♭6 at 213 are inside Adler's orchestral range (PDF 72). The 222 run reaches C7 by step after an eighth rest, which gives the shift time (PDF 82–83).
- **Cellos and basses 199–222.** The Cb eighths at 199–210 run under cellos that play a different line or hold whole notes. The contrabassoon plays the same line in quarters or whole notes, which is the simplification Adler recommends (PDF 105, Ex. III-69). The cellos' bass-clef C4–F4 at 199–203 (two ledger lines) matches movement I's 27–32 and stays.
- **Basses below E1** (F1 at 157, 160, 174, 181–184, 189, 216). The movement uses F1/G♭1 throughout. Adler PDF 108 says most orchestras have two or three C-extension basses, and players without one take the octave above. At each of these bars another bass instrument (tuba, contrabassoon or bass trombone) also has the low F, or the line stays clear an octave up. Kept, with no marking.
- **Brass endurance 199–223** (timed from global.ily):
  - Hn 1·2 rest 211–214 (c046).
  - Tpt 1·2·3 rest 205–210. Tpt 1 then plays 211–228 (about 33 s, B♭5 once at 213) with breath marks after 214 and 218. That is normal for a first trumpet with Ob 1/Cl 1 doubling the tune at the unison.

  - Giving Hn 3·4 a rest at 211–212 would undo c104's paired A♭3 fifth. Resting at 213–214 would thin the D♭/G♭ harmony under theme B's peak. I keep both.
  - The fff at 223 comes after four bars at sub. p–mf (219–222), which is a recovery for every brass player.
- **Timpani.** G, A♭, C, E♭ are enough for 199–223 (rolls A♭→G at 219–220, G→C at 222→223, protected). No retuning. No percussion before 223.
