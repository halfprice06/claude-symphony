## Findings

**1. Bar 38, beats 1-2: the chord before the E♭-minor cadential 6/4 is ii7 with C♮. Make it iiø7 with C♭, and let every C♭ fall to B♭. Applied in /tmp/critic-492/work.**

Problem. Bars 35-38 move from A♭ to B♭ minor and close on the half cadence of E♭ at 39. The E♭ here is the minor mode of the trumpet solo at 51, and the transition's C♭ and G♭ at 47-48 already belong to that mode. At 38 the bar has two halves:
- Beats 1-2 are F–A♭–C–E♭ (ii7 of E♭ major, or v7 of B♭ minor).
- Beats 3-4 are the cadential 6/4 B♭–E♭–G♭ of E♭ minor (c223).

So within one bar the score uses the major form of 6̂ (C♮) and then the minor form of 3̂ (G♭). The C♮ is then held as a 9th over the B♭ bass, against the G♭ of the 6/4 (Ob 1-2 since c297; Cl 2 holds C4 to beat 4). The bar mixes modes without purpose. It also leaves 38.3 open to a hearing as iv6/4 of B♭ minor rather than as E♭ minor's cadential 6/4.

Evidence:
- PDF 387, sec. 2: ♭6 "creates new forms of subdominant and supertonic harmony: IV♭, II6♭". Ex. 23-2d is the IIø6/5 coloured by ♭6.
- PDF 395, Points for review 2a: "♭6 to color and intensify IV, II6, II7".
- PDF 388 and Point 3: ♭6 is "strongly active in the direction of 5̂". With C♭, the oboes' held note becomes ♭6–5 (C♭5–B♭4) over the dominant, and Ob 2's line becomes D♭5–C♭5–B♭4.
- PDF 387: through mixture "the active melodic progression 6-5 in minor" is gained. Here it is gained in the passage that leads into the minor-mode solo.
- PDF 374: ♭6 is one of the two characteristic tones of minor. C♭ is foreign to B♭ minor, so 38.3 can now only be heard as E♭ minor's 6/4.

**2. Bar 38: VII°4/2 (C♭–D–F–A♭) into the cadential 6/4 (Ex. 22-8b, PDF 378). Considered, not applied.**

This is the one position of °7 the score never uses. At 38 it would need the bass C♭ instead of F. That turns the ii–V bass leap into a B♭–C♭–B♭ neighbour on the pedal, which weakens the half cadence. It would also replace the countersubject's F-minor arpeggio with D. A&S themselves call the °4/2 "problematic" as a goal and the least used position (PDF 378, Point 3). Finding 1 takes the same ♭6 colour with the stronger bass, so I chose it instead.
