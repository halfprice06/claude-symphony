## Answer to the focus question

**Is the closing group 83–98 long enough to settle E♭?** Yes. I decided not to add bars. E♭ takes the same share of the exposition as G does in Dvořák 9/iv (47 %). It is prepared by 12 bars of its dominant (39–50) and confirmed by cadences at 66 (minor), 82, 90 and 94. The second group (76 s) is slightly longer than the first (72 s), which is the normal relation. Adding a four-bar extension to the closing group would renumber every protected bar after 98, the 36-check quotation record and finale.md, all to gain 6 s.

**What is wrong is how the closing group is articulated, not its length.** Its 16 bars fall into four end-stopped 4-bar blocks. Two of them (83–86 and 87–90) each end with a root-position V–I in E♭ directly after S2's own perfect cadence at 82. That makes three identical cadences in nine bars, and 86 is followed by a two-beat silence in the whole orchestra. Finding 1 deals with this.

## Findings

### 1. The closing group's first phrase stops on a redundant perfect cadence and a general pause, so the 6/3 bridge does not grow out of the tonic (bars 85–87). Applied.

**Problem.** At 85–86 the F1 head ends V7–I in root position, and the bass leaps from the seventh A♭ (85, beat 4) down to E♭. On beats 3–4 of 86 nothing sounds anywhere in the orchestra. The bridge then starts at 87 on I6 as a new four-bar block. The same E♭ root-position cadence has just been heard at 82 (the end of S2) and comes again at 90. The closing group therefore sounds like three restarts (83, 87, 91), not one phrase that drives to its cadence. This end-stopping, not the number of bars, is why the closing feels short.

**Reading.** In the Mendelssohn model (Ex. 18-2, 18-3a, PDF 290–292) the 6/3 series comes straight out of the tonic (I5/3 → I6 → series → II6 → V7 → I). "The I6 that begins the series is important as an expansion of the initial tonic." Only the beginning and the end of a series are points of articulation (PDF 291), and a 5/3 sounds like a goal while a 6/3 keeps the music moving (PDF 290–291, Dufay). Ex. 18-19e (PDF 301) shows the 6/3 expanding the 5/3. A passing or neighbouring motion connects the root-position tonic with its first inversion (PDF 297).

**Fix (clos.ily, bar 86; applied).** The seventh in the bass resolves to G. I6 is held through 86 into 87's G, so the series starts from the tonic it expands. 83–90 becomes one eight-bar phrase: I – V7 – I6 – 6/3 series – IV6 – V7 – I. The cadences now rise in strength: 86 is I6 with E♭ on top (imperfect), 90 has B♭ on top and runs into 91, and 94 is the only perfect cadence with the tonic on top.

```

closBsnTwo = { es1\f | aes,2 bes,2 | d2 bes,2 | g,2 r2 | …                (86 was es2 r2)
```

Bn 1 holds B♭3 through 85, so the seventh A♭ is not doubled while the bass resolves it (PDF 293; with A♭3–G3 in Bn 1 the all-pairs check showed octaves with the basses). The violas' arpeggio on I6 (G4–B♭4–E♭5) avoids the bass's B♭–E♭–B♭ octaves on the beats. It also removes the v143 octaves between the violas and the melody, F→E♭ at 85.4→86.1 (Vla against Vln I·II, Fl, Ob), which the all-pairs check flags in v143.

F1's melody is unchanged. Only its harmonisation under the last note changes (I → I6), so no other movement thread needs to be told. finale.md's line "its head in E♭ major opens the closing group at 83–86" still holds. Nothing in the recapitulation corresponds to these bars.

### Checked, no change (decided)

I left IV6 at a half bar (reading row 10). A full bar would need an added bar, and three beats would leave V7 one beat at Tempo I.

I considered replacing the V on beats 3 of 91–92 with a neighbouring 5–6–5 over a held E♭ (Ex. 18-19a, PDF 301; Dvořák's codetta 92–103 expands I over a G pedal, harmony.csv). I kept I–V. The top line G–A♭ fits V7 but not the neighbouring 6/3. The change would rewrite about twenty parts for a stock gesture that already ends on the closing group's one perfect cadence (94).
- **The departure 95–98.** At 95 the tonic E♭ is still a pure triad, the seventh D♭ arrives at 96, and C7 (V7 of F) holds for two bars into the f development at 99. The E♭ tonic is held five bars (91–95) after the bridge's cadence. No change.

This is symmetrical with the two 16-bar statements of F1 at 11–42 (46 s), and the second group rises steadily from p (51) to ff (91). V6 as a half-step neighbour at 52 and 68 follows PDF 296.
