## Findings

### 1. 243-246: Cl 1 is inside the horns in its throat register under the ff horn call. Move it up an octave (applied)

Problem. At 243-246, Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2 play movement I's horn call E-C-B-C (Tpt 1 E5-C6-B5-C6, Tpt 2 and Hn 1 E4-C5-B4-C5, Hn 2 E3-C4-B3-C4). Cl 1 holds G4 (244) and F4 (245-246), with B3-C4 at 243. These pitches sit inside the horns' C4-C5 octave. Concert G4 and F4 are written A4 and G4 on the B♭ clarinet: throat tones. In the same bars the upper woodwind is silent above the horns and Tpt 2. Fl 1 rests until its run at 246.3, Fl 2 and Ob 1 drop out after 243, and Cl 2 rests. The clarinet holds the V7's seventh F, which also sounds in Hn 4 F3, Bn 1 F3, Vln II F4 and the violas F3. As written, that seventh never resolves in Cl 1's octave: F4 goes to the pickup G4 and then to E5 at 247.

Evidence.
- Rimsky PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns, but, in the upper register, and in the higher harmonic parts, a chord of four horns ... may be completed by clarinets as effectively as by oboes or flutes." The diagram puts the clarinets above the horns.
- Rimsky PDF 110-111: with trumpets and trombones in the chord, "flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets". All three systems of diagrams on PDF 111 show the woodwind above or around the trumpets, never under the horns.
- Adler adler1 PDF 210, Ex. VII-56: the throat tones are "rather pale" and the clarino register is "bright, incisive, expressive".

- Cl 1 now plays B4-C5 at 243, after its breath at 242. B4 is the chord's fifth, also in Vln II. C5 doubles the call's C5 in Tpt 2 and Hn 1.
- At 244-246 Cl 1 holds G5 and F5 above all four horns and Tpt 2, and below Tpt 1's C6/B5. Its intervals with Tpt 1 are a fourth, a third, a tritone and a fifth, with no parallels.
- The seventh F5 now resolves to E5, the first note of Cl 1's S head at 247, in the same octave.

### 2. Checked, no change (decided)

- **11-26, the off-beat dominant sevenths at 13-14, 21-22 and 24-25.** Principle 15 prefers the seventh F3 in the bassoon and the root G3 in Hn 4, the reverse of the present scoring. I decided to keep it. These are staccato off-beat quarters, and Rimsky PDF 114 says resonance matters less in short detached chords. A swap would make the bassoon's seventh rise to G3 at 15 and 23. The seventh and the root are already in different colours, as PDF 102 asks.
- **42, Hn 2 B♭3 between Bn 2 B♭2 and Bn 1 D4.** Kept. It is the last p note of the staged diminuendo (c008), tied from 39, and the bassoons release on beat 4. The enclosure lasts three beats at p.
- **169, Cl 2 F4 inside Hn 2 D4 / Hn 1 A♭4.** Kept. The pad fades to pp, Hn 1's A♭4 is c137's fix (it avoids G4 a semitone under Cl 1's A♭4), and the horns rest at 170 while Cl 2's F4 carries on.
- **219-222 and 231-234, single horns not one degree louder (principle 7).** Not applied. Both passages are protected (the 205-223 dynamic path with sub. p at 219; the arrival and chorale 223-234). The horns double trumpet or trombone notes there, so the PDF 103-104 duplication principle covers them.
- **Stopped horns with low clarinets (principle 14).** Not applied. The one soft passage with low clarinets is 171-186, where Cl 2's low pedal sits under the protected muted trumpet and cor anglais (c004, c019). Stopped horns there would be a second muted-brass colour on top of the protected one.
- **Low flute thirds inside trumpet octaves (principle 17).** Not applicable. The only soft sustained trumpet octave is Tpt 3 C4 with Tpt 1 C5 at 219-220. Clarinets already sit between them there, and the passage is protected.
- **110: Tpt 3 B♭4 against Tpt 1·2 C5 (principle 6).** Protected (c194: upper parts and trumpets at 110-113). The climax lasts one beat before the line moves.
