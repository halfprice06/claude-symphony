## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Flat beams on ledger-line groups (Gould PDF 35) — applied
**Bars:** Vln I 199, 201, 222, 246 (two groups), 247, 249, 251, 252, 265, 267; Fl 1 246 (two groups), 247, 249, 251, 252, 265, 267.
**Problem:** every eighth group written wholly above the first ledger line (C6–B6) is printed with a horizontal beam lying on the middle line, although the notes rise or fall: G6–A6–B6 into the arrival at 222, the C6–F6 scale at 246, the D6–D♭6–C6 / E6–D6–C6 / D6–C6–B5 tails of the F1 head at 247–267. Gould PDF 35: "When all the notes of a beam fall outside the first ledger line, the beam takes only a slight slope… Intervals of a second take a slope of ¼ stave-space; all wider intervals take a slope of ½ stave-space." A flat beam under a rising run reads as repeated pitch at a glance, exactly where the reader is counting ledger lines.
**Evidence:** the default position, printed with a `grob-transformer` probe in /tmp/critic-015/t/t.ly, is `(0 . 0)` for each of these groups.
**Fix (applied):** before the first note of each group, ends chosen so that both ends touch the middle line (hang / sit or straddle, PDF 34):
- rising third or wider: `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.25 . 0.25)` (codaTwo Vln I 222; sHead Fl 1 and Vln I 246, C–F)
- rising second: `#'(-0.25 . 0)` (sHead 246, A–B)
- falling second: `#'(0 . -0.25)` (codaOne Vln I 199; finale Fl 1 and Vln I 247 D–D♭–C, 252 E–D)
- falling third: `#'(0.25 . -0.25)` (codaOne Vln I 201; finale 249, 251, 265, 267)
Fl 1 at 199–204 is left alone: there it shares the stave with Fl 2 (`\partCombineApart`), stems up, beams outside the stave (PDF 35, "beams outside the stave"), where LilyPond already slopes them.

### 2. Harp arpeggios change stem direction inside the figuration (Gould PDF 39) — applied
**Bars:** harp RH 67–82 (the F–B♭–D–F groups at 72, 74, 78, 80 turn stems down; all others up); harp LH 123–130 (127–128 turn down).
**Problem:** Gould PDF 39: "The context may determine the direction… notes adjacent to the group… may take stems in one particular direction"; for figuration across a passage, "maintaining the same stem direction for the whole passage". The per-group rule (PDF 38, furthest note decides) flips single groups of an unchanging accompaniment pattern, so the beam line jumps from above to below the notes and back.
**Fix (applied):** sTwo.ily `sTwoHarpR = { \stemUp es'8 … es''8 \stemNeutral | }`; dev.ily `devHarpL = { R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | \stemUp g,8 … fis8 | \stemNeutral R1*4 | }`. 187–198 and 235–242 already keep one direction.

### 3. Double-bass repeated eighths flip direction bar by bar (Gould PDF 39) — applied
**Bars:** Cb 199–210 and 247–262.
**Problem:** the same repeated-eighth pedal pattern takes stems down on D3, E3, F3 (199–204, 248, 249, 251 first half, 252, 253, 255, 256) and up on C3, G2, A2 (250, 251 second half, 254, 257–262): ten direction changes in one ostinato. Same rule as Finding 2 (PDF 39, context and visual consistency). Up-stems are the right single choice: all notes lie between G2 and F3, so no stem passes 3½ spaces above the top line (PDF 38), and the bar numbers under the Cb staff stay clear.
**Fix (applied):** codaOne.ily `codaOneCb = { \stemUp c,8\ff … aes,,8 \stemNeutral | }`; finale.ily `finaleCb = { \stemUp c,8\ff … c,8 \stemNeutral | c,1->\fff~ …}`.

### 4. Hairpins on the one-beat pickup print as a tick (61, 181) — applied
**Bars:** Fl 1 at 61→62; Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 181→182.
**Problem:** the `\p\<` on the pickup quarter ends at the `\mf` on the next downbeat. Between the p and the mf there is room for only a stroke: at 181 the hairpin is a vertical line ("p | mf"), at 61 (where v15 already has `minimum-length = #3.5`) a 1-space wedge. A collapsed hairpin is an unreadable sign (MOLA PDF 4: instructions in conventional form).
**Fix (applied):** sOne.ily raise `Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length` from 3.5 to 7 at 61; recS.ily add `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.springs-and-rods = #ly:spanner::set-spacing-rods \once \override Staff.Hairpin.minimum-length = #7` before `d'''4(` (Fl 1) and `d''4(` (Ob 1) at 181. Both now print a clear crescendo wedge; the systems still fit (16 pages).

### 5. Shared staves print the same dynamic twice (11, 101) — applied
**Bars:** Bn 1·2 at 11 ("mf mf"); Cl 1·2 at 101 ("mf mf").
**Problem:** with the Dynamic_engraver at staff level, a dynamic in each player at a different onset prints twice side by side. One mf at the first entry covers both players on a shared staff.
**Fix (applied, MIDI unchanged):** keep the event for playback, hide the second print: pOne.ily `pOneBsnOne = { \partCombineApart r4 g4-.-\tweak stencil ##f \mf …`; dev.ily `devClOne = { R1*2 | \partCombineApart r4 c''8(-\tweak stencil ##f \mf …`.

### 6. Wrong player label "2." over clarinet 1 at 101 and 105 — applied
**Bars:** Cl 1·2 101–106.
**Problem:** Cl 2 enters alone on beat 1 (e'1), so the part-combiner prints "2." (solo II); Cl 1 enters a beat later on the upper voice and the "2." stands over Cl 1's run. The label names the wrong player for the line it sits on.
**Fix (applied):** dev.ily, both voices apart for these bars: `devClOne = { R1*2 | \partCombineApart r4 c''8( … f''4->) f'4-> | \partCombineAutomatic R1*3 | c''1\f …}` and `devClTwo = { R1*2 | \partCombineApart e'1\mf … bes'2 a'2 | \partCombineAutomatic R1*8 …}`. Now Cl 1 shows a quarter rest and its run, Cl 2 its whole note, no label.
