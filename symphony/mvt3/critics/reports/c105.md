## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Clarinet broken-chord beams slope although every group is concave (151-166; same figure at 11-26). Applied.
**Problem.** Each Cl 1·2 group is an arpeggio that goes up and comes back: E♭4-G4-C5-G4 over G3-C4-E♭4-C4, D4-F4-G4-F4, D4-G4-B4-G4, F4-A♭4-C5-A♭4, D♭4-F4-A♭4-F4. LilyPond draws every one with a beam rising about ½ space (page 8, bars 151-157 at 300 dpi).
**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 36 (c): a group whose inner note is nearer the beam than either outer note "forms a concave shape" and takes a horizontal beam.
- PDF 38: in chords, the note nearest the beam decides. Here that is the Cl 1 top line, and its inner C5, G4 or B4 is always the highest note.
- PDF 37: horizontal beams "can look better in repeated figuration". This figure is repeated for 16 bars.

The present slope also points the beam toward the last note of the group, which is lower than the peak. That is the "most extreme interval" reading Gould rejects on PDF 36.

**Fix (applied).** In `recPClOne`/`recPClTwo` and `pOneClOne`/`pOneClTwo`, the line opens with `\override Staff.Beam.damping = #+inf.0` and closes with `\revert Staff.Beam.damping` before the brace. It is a Staff-level override, so it holds on the part-combined staff and in each clarinet part. Every group is now horizontal. No sounding change.

### 2. Beams beyond the ledger lines: Vln I 206 and 210 drawn flat, Fl 1 199 not matching Vln I. Applied.
**Problem.** Vln I at 206 and 210 (B♭5-C6-D♭6-E♭6, rising into the accented F6) gets a horizontal beam, because LilyPond holds both stems at the middle line. At 199, Fl 1's D6-D♭6-C6 (stems up on the shared staff) is horizontal, while Vln I plays the same figure with the ¼-space slope that cycle 15 set.
**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 35: when every note of a group is beyond the first ledger line, the beam takes "a slight slope": ¼ space for a second, ½ space for wider intervals.
- PDF 36: the beam slopes in the direction of the outer interval. Here that is a rising sixth.
- PDF 35, last paragraph: matching angles look better.

**Fix (applied), codaOne.ily.**
- 206 and 210: `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.25 . 0.25)` before `bes''8 c''' des''' es'''`. These are the same values as Vln I 222 and Fl 1 246.
- 199, Fl 1: `\tag #'score { \once \override Beam.positions = #'(8 . 7.75) } \tag #'part { \once \override Beam.positions = #'(0 . -0.25) }` before `d'''8(`. Stems point up in the score and down in the part, so the part uses Vln I's values.

At 300 dpi both groups now slope as intended and the stems are 3½ spaces or longer. The first version, without the tag, gave "weird stem size" warnings in the flute part, which is why the override is now tagged.

### 3. Brass and timpani enter at N (199) after 31-32 bars' rest with no cue. Applied.
**Problem.** Tpt 2, Tpt 3, Hn 3, Hn 4, Tbn 1, Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba rest from 167 to 198, which is 32 bars. The timpani rest from 168, 31 bars. The rest runs across L, M, the key change to A♭ and a 2-bar poco rit. Then all of them enter f or ff on the downbeat of 199. Their parts show only "4 | L 16 | M 10 | 2". The cycle-75 cues cover other places.
**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest... transposed to the reading key... audible and obvious." Tempo changes must be shown in every part.
**Fix (applied), recS.ily.** These variables had no definition in recS, so each part got 28 plain bars of rest. Each is now `R1*26 | \tag #'score { R1*2 } \tag #'part { ... } |`:
- Tpt 2 and Tpt 3: `\cueDuring "vlnOne" #UP` with "Vln. I". This is the climb A♭5-B♭5 to E♭6 f.
- Hn 3 and Hn 4: `\transposedCueDuring "vlnOne" #UP c`. The cue sounds an octave lower and is written E♭5-F5-B♭5 in F, so it stays on the stave.
- Tbn 1, Tbn 2, B.tbn, Tuba and timpani: `\cueDuring "vc" #UP` with "Vc.", the cellos' rising line to C at 198.

A new quote, `\addQuote "vc" { \removeWithTag #'part \vc }`, goes in score.ly and parts-head.ily next to the others. The score and MIDI do not change. Part page counts are unchanged and the page turns still fall in rests. I checked the rendered cues in the Hn 3, Tpt 3, Tbn 1 and timpani parts.

### 4. Score-only tied minims printed in the single-player parts (211 Ob 2, Cl 2; 215 Hn 3, Hn 4). Applied.
**Problem.** Cycle 35 wrote these as `x2~ x2` so that the shared staves show a crossing or unison with stems (Gould PDF 65-67). Each of these parts has one player, so the part shows a minim tied to a minim where a semibreve fills the bar. Examples: Hn 3 part 215 (written C5), Ob 2 part 211.
**Evidence.**
- Gould PDF 66-67: the stems and ties are there only to separate two parts on one staff.
- MOLA PDF 5: parts are prepared for the individual player.
- Gould PDF 31: notation should show rhythm plainly. A bar-long note written as two tied halves suggests a re-articulation that is not there.

**Fix (applied), codaTwo.ily.** For example, `\tag #'score { f2~ f2 } \tag #'part { f1 }` for Hn 3, and likewise `aes2~ aes2` for Hn 4, `f''2\ff~ f''2` for Ob 2 and `aes'2\ff~ aes'2` for Cl 2. The score is unchanged.

### 5. Two hairpin ends on one shared staff at 210 (Fl 1·2, Cl 1·2). Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 1 and Cl 1 drop their 7th G♭ on beat 3 of 210 (c042) and end their crescendo there with `r2\!`. Fl 2 and Cl 2 end theirs on beat 4 with `r4\!`. This is the only unequal simultaneous dynamic I found in any shared staff between 151 and 222, using the event-listener comparison.
**Evidence.** The rule for shared staves in the brief requires equal simultaneous dynamics. MOLA PDF 5 asks for one consistent marking.
**Fix (applied).** In Fl 1 and Cl 1, `ges'''2 r2\!` becomes `ges'''2 r4 r4\!` (and `ges''2 r4 r4\!`), so all four players end on beat 4. Pitches and timing are identical. In the MIDI, the velocities of two notes per part (209, 211) move slightly toward their partners' values.

### 6. The percussion part lacks the score's drum-staff settings. Applied.
**Problem.** The part's DrumStaff has no `Stem.direction = #UP` or `Tie.direction = #UP`. At 279 the cymbal quarter's stem hangs down through the bass-drum space under the single line, and the stems point the opposite way from the score's.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 38-39: keep one stem direction through a passage when that keeps markings next to the notes. On a one-line staff that holds two instruments (cymbal above, bass drum below), the stem side has to stay fixed.
**Fix (applied).** I added `\override Stem.direction = #UP \override Tie.direction = #UP \override LaissezVibrerTie.direction = #UP` to the percussion DrumStaff in parts-book.ly, and the same in mkparts.py so a regenerated book keeps it. It is still one page.

### 7. Not changed: the short ff>f hairpin at 211 in the brass parts
In the Tpt 3, Hn 3·4, Tbn and Tuba parts, the decrescendo inside the one-semibreve bar 211 is drawn as a stub of about 2 mm. I tested `\override Hairpin.minimum-length = #5` with spacing rods in the parts' Staff layout. Page counts did not change, but neither did the stub, because the ff and the following f take up the bar. I decided to leave it. The marking can still be read and the score is correct.
