## Findings

### 1. Violas 95–98: the soft sul tasto chord is still under the "non div." of 91 (applied in scratch)

- Bars: 95–99, violas (clos.ily `closVla`, dev.ily `devVla`).
- Problem: at 91 the violas are marked "non div." for the ff chords of the codetta, and every viola plays both notes. At 95 the violas hold B♭3–E♭4, then B♭3–E4 at 97 (the E♭/E♮ step), mp > p, sul tasto since c444. No new instruction is given, so the "non div." of 91 still applies, and the whole section must play a held, soft double stop over the fingerboard for four bars. At 99 the violas go back to single notes with no "unis.".
- Evidence: Adler PDF 30: "double notes (double-stops) are usually divided between the two players on the same stand ... If the word divisi does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop. When divisi is no longer in order ... unis. should appear". Ex. II-12 (PDF 31) shows "div." and "non div." as changes of state that must each be written. The score already follows this everywhere else: "div." for the soft held chords at 43 (Vc), 135 (Vla, in `\retrTastoOrdDiv`), 219 (Vln II) and 223 (Vc), and "unis." after each. At 95–98 one bowed stroke for two notes, sul tasto and pp, gives an unsteady double stop. Divided, each player sustains one note, which is what c444 meant by the distant sul tasto colour. Both notes stay equally strong, because the two players on each stand split them.
- Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-445/work):
  - clos.ily `closVla`, bar 95: `<bes es'>1\mp\>~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "sul tasto," "div." }`. Two lines, because a one-line "sul tasto, div." ran past the 95|96 barline (Gould PDF 55).
  - dev.ily `devVla`, bar 99: `f'2->(\f^\markup \italic "ord., unis." c'4. des'8`. The sf chords at 101 and 105 are then double stops again, as in the cellos.
- 95–98 is on the do-not-touch list for its scoring and pitches. Those are unchanged: no note, rhythm, dynamic or bowing changes, and the MIDI is note-for-note identical. Only the instruction for how the existing notes are shared out changes.

### 2. Basses 266–276: measured-tremolo shorthand for the repeated eighths (tested, declined)

- Bars: 247–277, basses (finale.ily `finaleCb`), 8 eighths a bar, 266–276 all C1.
- Candidate: Adler PDF 50, Ex. II-47 calls bowed measured tremolo "simply a convenient shorthand for writing a series of repeated détaché notes". `c,2:8 c,2:8` or `\repeat tremolo 4 c,8` would remove about 90 noteheads from page 16. It would not break the cyclic rule, because string tremolo is allowed from 223.
- Test: in a test file (/tmp/critic-445/t/t.ly), LilyPond's MIDI plays both `c,2:8` and `\repeat tremolo 4 c,8` as one held half note. The MIDI-only score in score.ly does not unfold repeats, so the basses' pulse would disappear from score.midi and from analysis.txt's density profile. The figure also changes pitch (D, F, G, E, A♭ at 247–262; G1 inside 277), and crescendos start in the middle of it, so the written-out eighths read more clearly. Page 16 is not crowded enough to need the space. Not better; not applied.
