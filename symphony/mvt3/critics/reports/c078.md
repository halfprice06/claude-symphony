## Findings

### 1. 263–265: the fff cadence chord is held to the barline and attacked again at once (applied)

Problem: after the movement's only root-position V7–I (262–263), every wind and brass part holds the fff tonic chord as a whole note tied through 264. Twelve of them start again at 265 with no gap:
- Tpt 1·2 `c''2->`, Hn 1·2 `c''2->`, Tpt 3, Tuba and Bn 2 on the downbeat;
- Fl, Ob and Cl after only an eighth rest, leaping to E6.

Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2 and Ob 1 have held whole notes since 255 or 257 (8–10 bars at 2 = 88). As written, the cadence has no release, so the fff → ff change at 265 happens inside one breath.

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 187: tied or slurred notes are played on one breath.
- PDF 188: give the flutist time to rest after sustained passages. Brahms 1/iv (Ex. VII-9) arranges the flute parts so the player can breathe without losing the line.
- PDF 198: the oboe needs a breathing point and rests.
- Every phrase-ending example in the chapter (VII-9, -34, -50, -93) has a written release before the next phrase.

The strings can change bow, so they can keep holding.

Fix (finale.ily, bar 264, in Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, B.cl, Bn 1·2, Cbn, Hn 1–4, Tpt 1–3, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, Tuba): `c''1\fff~ | c''1 |` becomes `c''1\fff~ | c''2. r4 |`, with each part's own pitch or chord. Strings, double basses and timpani keep holding through 264. The cymbal crash at 263 is unchanged. No pitch, harmony or cadence changes, so the protected 261–263 cadence is intact.

### 2. 272: the woodwinds play through the end of the allargando phrase (applied)

Problem: at 272 Tpt 1·2 have a breath mark after `f''2-> e''4( d''4)`. The flutes, oboes and clarinets play the same phrase in the same rhythm with no breath mark. They then go straight into the Largamente at 273 and the held chain to 279. That is 11 bars for Ob 1 with no rest, after 257–268, where its only rests are at 266 and 268. The phrase end at the allargando, the most important articulation in the close, is marked in one family and not in the others that double it.

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 198: rest periods are mandatory for the oboe.
- PDF 188: the flute needs breath.
- PDF 187: a slur is one breath, so the breath belongs at the end of the slur.
- The examples mark phrase ends identically in doubling parts (Ex. VII-63, -64, two clarinets).

Fix (finale.ily, Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2): `f'''2-> e'''4( d'''4) |` becomes `f'''2-> e'''4( d'''4) \breathe |`, using each part's own octave. The breath now matches the trumpets at the same point.
