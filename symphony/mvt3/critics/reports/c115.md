## Findings

### 1. Vln I 223: drop the 8va on the C7 and put the tremolo strokes above the note (applied)

Bars 222-223. In v115 the Vln I run into the arrival, G6-A♭6-B6 at 222, is written at pitch with four and five ledger lines, and its goal, the C7 at 223 (the owed violins' C7, the only C7 in the movement), is written as C6 under "8-┐". On the page the line reads as a step up to B6 and then a drop of a seventh. Gould's "incorrect" example on PDF 47 is exactly this case: the octave sign starts on the last note of a figure, and an octave change "in the wrong place obscures the relationship between the intervals". The sign also goes to the player: the Violin I part prints "8-┐" over the single C7 (part page 4, system 2). PDF 47 says that in performance material isolated notes take ledger lines, not octave signs, and that the octave signs a full score uses to save space "should not be transferred to a playing part". The 8va does not save space either. String parts may use up to five ledger lines (PDF 47), and C7 needs exactly five. Fl 1 plays the same C7 at 223 with five ledger lines and no sign, and Vln I's own B6 at 222 also has five. The 8va was the only octave sign in the movement outside the harp.

When the 8va is simply removed, the whole-note tremolo strokes (stem down by default) fall on the five ledger lines and the note becomes hard to read. With the stem set up, the three strokes sit above the notehead, clear of the ledger lines and below the "Maestoso" mark.

Fix (arrival.ily):
```
arrivalVlnOne = { \once \override Stem.direction = #UP c''''1:32\fff | R1*2 | ...
```
(was `\ottava #1 c''''1:32\fff \ottava #0 |`). The pitch, the MIDI and the protected rule "the violins' C7 only at 223" are unchanged. The score layout is unchanged (page 13 has the same systems). In the part, rehearsal mark P now sits just left of the C7. This reverses no earlier fix: cycle 5 kept ledger lines (no 8va) for Fl 1/Vln I at B6-C7, and this restores that.

### 2. sTwoCb (82): remove the stray `\ottava #0` (applied)

Bar 82, double basses. `sTwoCb` ends `es,1\< \ottava #0 |`, but no octave sign is open on the Cb staff. It prints nothing today, but it is left over from an older harp/bass copy, and Gould PDF 48 makes the cancelling of a transposition a visible event ("loco"). An orphan cancel invites a wrong "loco" or bracket if someone edits the bass line. Fix: `es,1\< | }` (applied; no visible change; the parts build unchanged).
