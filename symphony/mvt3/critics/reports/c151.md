## Findings

### F1. The A♭7 build reaches theme B without a line: the top 7th is dropped and its resolution sounded early (209-211). Applied.

**Problem.**
- 205-208 state F1's head twice, horns and then winds and violins. At 209-210 the top of the texture is the flutes' held G♭6, the 7th of A♭7 (V7 of D♭).
- On 210.3 the winds let G♭ go, and the violins' run lands on F6/F5, the 7th's tone of resolution, a beat and a half before the D♭ chord.
- At 211 no part has F6. Vln I starts D♭6, the flutes drop to the tune's A♭5, and Cl 1 drops from G♭5 to A♭4.
- So the six-bar build (marked poco a poco più animato) ends in a hole. The highest, most tense note of the passage leads nowhere, and theme B begins after a pause instead of out of the line before it.
- The inner 7ths do resolve (violas, Bn 1 G♭→F), so the fault is only in the register the listener follows.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 357-358 §18, Ex. 21-23a (image): the 7th of V7 is frequently suspended to form a 4-3 over I.
- PDF 352 §12: anticipating the tone of resolution "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution".
- PDF 359-360 §21: a delayed resolution builds tension.
- PDF 357 §17: an indirect suspension, passed from one voice to another, is typical of instrumental writing.
- PDF 367-368 §28: over a pedal, the dissonances among the upper parts are prepared and resolved.

**Fix (applied).**
- codaOne.ily, 210, Vln I: `bes''8 c''' des''' es''') ges'''4->~ ges'''4 | }` (was `… f'''4-> r4\! | }`). The crescendo from 209 now runs to the ff at 211.
- codaOne.ily, 210, Vln II: `… es'') ges''4-> r4\! | }` (was `f''4->`).
- codaTwo.ily, 211, Vln I: `ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4) des'''4 c'''4 |` (was `des'''2\ff\> des'''4 c'''4`).

**Result.**
- G♭6 is held from 209 (Fl 1) to 211.1 (Vln I, tied across the caesura that everyone else keeps).
- It sounds as a 4 over D♭ against the ff tutti and resolves to F6 on beat 2, then goes on D♭6-C6 into 212's F6 as before.
- At 211.1 the tone of resolution F is present only a ninth and more below (Ob 2 F5, violas F4), in other timbres. This is the exception PDF 352 allows.
- Vln I at 211 is not part of the theme B quotation: the record has Tpt 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and the flutes, and themecheck reports 36/36.

### F2. The development's one combination of F1 with F2 cannot be heard (130-132). Applied.

**Problem.**
- At 130-131 the violas play F1's head in G (D-A-B | C-B-A-G-F♯) under F2's half cadence (Fl 1 and Vln I A5). This is the only place before the coda where the first theme sounds against the second, and it points ahead to the head combination at 247.
- The head lies at D4-F♯3, inside Hn 3·4's repeated D4/A3 quarters, Bn 1's held C4 and the harp's arpeggios, all at p-f in the same register.
- Vln II's run at 131 starts on F♯4 across it. Nothing in the texture gives this inner line any prominence.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 348 §4: an inner line needs something that "bring[s it] into prominence". A&S does this with suspensions, but the head has none and adding one would alter F1.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 113: horns doubled by violas give a "splendid effect"; strings and brass combine well in that one case.

**Fix (applied).** dev.ily:
- Hn 2: `R1*16 | d'2->(\mf\< a4. b8 | c'4-.\f) b8( a8 g4-.) fis4-. | g4-> r4 r2 | R1*2 | }`. This was `R1*17 | d'2\f d'2 | g'4-> …`. Hn 2 now doubles the violas at pitch and takes F♯3 up to G3 at 132.
- Hn 1, 130: `fis'1\mf\<`, so the shared horn staff has equal simultaneous dynamics (mf < at 130, f at 131).

**Effect on the rest of the bar.**
- D7 at 131 keeps its root in Hn 3 (D4), the cellos and the basses.
- The 123-130 hand-over of F2 (Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I/Fl 1, protected since c111) is unchanged, note for note. Hn 2 adds no note to the F2 line.
- F1 itself is not changed: its head is only doubled, so the other movement threads need no notice.
