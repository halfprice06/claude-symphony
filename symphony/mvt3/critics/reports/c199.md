## Master comparison (Beethoven 9/iv)

Beethoven builds his finale by recall and then by layering one tune: low strings alone, then one counter-line, then more instruments, then the tutti. This finale works the other way round: its large gesture is the minor-to-major turn through the stop chord (222→223) and a chorale, closer to Brahms 1/iv, which the brief names as the model. The parts that correspond to Beethoven's method are present and handled at a professional level: the recall of earlier movements (II's theme B at 211–218, the scherzo's motive x at 143–145, its theme A head at 275–276) is set as a structural event, not decoration, and the second theme is introduced by a single soft solo instrument with a minimal accompaniment (51–66), which is Beethoven's 92–115 principle (one unadorned voice first) and Casanelles' principle 3 (a new colour enters alone over a held layer). Beethoven's Prestissimo ends by acceleration and percussion; this finale ends with a Largamente over a C pedal (273–279). That is a different, defensible choice, protected since many cycles, and I do not propose to change it. A conductor reading pages 12–16 would see a coherent late-Romantic coda with correct doubling, spacing and dynamics. I found nothing in v199 that would make a conductor doubt the hand, and nothing in the cycle 189–198 changes that is weaker than v189.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 110–113, 16-foot octave (c194): improved, keep. Not changed.
v189 had tuba, contrabassoon, basses and bass trombone all at C2/F2 under Fl 1 and Vln I at A♭6–F6, so the F-minor goal of the development had no sub-octave. v199 puts Cbsn C1 at 110, Cbsn and tuba F1 at 111.1 and tuba, Cbsn and basses F1 at 113 (analysis.txt: bass of 109–112 now C1, of 113–116 F1). Casanelles PDF 274 (principle 5): the lowest partials form the octave and fifth that make a chord sound full; a chord spaced with the widest interval at the bottom matches the series. Rimsky PDF 101 and 112 say the same for the bass in octaves. The page image (score page 6) shows the octave entering exactly on the two structural beats (the cadential 6/4 bass at 110 and the goal at 113), with 111.3–112 at unison, which keeps the tuba line out of its weakest bottom notes (D♭1 and B♭0 would be below or at the edge of the basses' and tuba's useful range). Casanelles PDF 277–278 (principle 11) warns about masking in one band; the extra octave sits a full octave below the rest of the bass, so it adds weight without masking. Ranges: contrabassoon C1 and tuba F1 are inside their compasses; basses F1 is above their E1. Verdict: better than v189.

### 2. 160 Ob 2 C5 (c192): improved, keep. Not changed.
v189 had Ob 2 A♭4–G4 into 161 in parallel fifths with the tune's upper octave E♭5–D5 (iv7→V; A&S PDF 136 and 151: in IV–V the voices have no common tone and parallel fifths come easily). v199's F4–C5 then G4 removes them. On page 9 the line still lies under the tune's top octave and inside the iv7 (F–A♭–C–E♭ over the bass F at 160.3). Casanelles PDF 271–272 (principle 2): the mf of the second oboe against the ff tune is the resulting balance intended, and the C5 sits in the oboe's clear middle register (PDF 274–275, principle 6), not higher than the tune's lower octave in a way that would mask it. The leap C5–G4 is to the root of V and the only new interval. Verdict: better.

### 3. 222 Fl 1 beam (c195): improved, keep. Not changed.
The flute's G6–A♭6–B6 now has the half-space rise Gould gives for notes beyond the first ledger line with intervals wider than a second (Behind Bars PDF 35), matching the Vln I beam on the same notes below. Casanelles PDF 277–278 (principle 10) treats Fl 1 plus Vln I in unison as one note; the score now also shows them as one shape. Sound unchanged (identical analysis.txt). Verdict: better.
