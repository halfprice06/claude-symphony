## Overall judgment

Set beside Franck's finale, this movement has the same kind of cyclic plan: a soft brass-coloured second theme, a slow movement's theme that interrupts and is then transformed, a chorale apotheosis, and a canon in the coda. Its scoring is often more careful than Franck's organ-registration blocks. A conductor would believe the large design. Franck's terraced orchestration has a lesson in his own example XI-20: the choirs take turns, and the trumpets and trombones are not needed for every forte. That is the one place where this score falls short: the start of the recapitulation (Finding 1).

On the batch 161-168, compared with v159: the movement has improved, and it is not fussier. None of the changes makes a passage worse in harmony, balance or form.

- The joints at 74 and 194 (V4/2 resolving to I6) and the codetta 91-94 (tune on top in Vln I) are real improvements.
- The other changes are small: the breaths, the bass trombone hold at 41-42, the B♭/D/C♭ details, and the dynamics at 69-74, 103 and 166.
- The marking density has hardly changed: 127 → 133 breath marks and 481 → 481 hairpins. Most of the 23 new markups are part-only cues.

## Findings

### 1. 151-158: the trumpets' off-beats thicken the recapitulation's first phrase and remove its growth (applied)
- **Problem.**
  - At 151-166 Tpt 1-3 play off-beat quarters mf: G4, E♭4 and C4 in the low register. They duplicate Hn 3-4's off-beats (C4/G3; Tpt 3 is at the unison with Hn 3) for 16 bars, all the way from the strokes at 147-150 to the mute change at 167.
  - In the exposition (11-26) the off-beats are Hn 3-4 alone. In the recapitulation, the antecedent (151-158) and the consequent (159-166) are therefore scored the same. The only thing that grows is the counter-line at 159.
  - The trumpets, which have just played their G5 peak at 150, are absorbed into a background layer.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 38 (Franck XI-20): the choirs alternate, and the forte is built without trumpets and trombones.
  - PDF 162 (Schumann holds back instruments "to build an even heavier chord in the next phrase").
  - PDF 172 (Wagner holds back the highs and adds them one at a time).
  - PDF 165 (sustained doublings at pitch "tire the ear") and PDF 146 (the whole body used "for too long an unrelieved stretch").
  - In Franck's finale, as in the programme note above, the main theme gains instruments in stages rather than appearing with every choir at once.
- **Fix (applied; recP.ily, recPTptOne/Two/Three).** The trumpets rest 151-158 and enter mf at 159 with the consequent and the counter-line, so the recapitulation grows by addition:
  `recPTptOne = { R1*8 | r4 g'4-.\mf r4 g'4-. | r4 bes'4-. r4 aes'4-. | … | r4 g'4-. r2 | R1*4^\markup \italic "metti sord." | }`
  Tpt 2 and Tpt 3 change the same way. Bars 159-166 keep v169's consequent notes, and Hn 3-4 are unchanged.
- **Answer to c166.** c166 lowered the trumpets to mf at 151 so that they would not cover the tune. This goes one step further, and the mf stays at 159.
- **Effect on the trumpets.** Tpt 1 gets eight bars of rest between the 147-150 strokes and the muted solo at 171.
- **What it does not touch.** No quotation, no F1 note, and no protected passage. The strokes 147-150 and the return over the dominant are unchanged.

### 3. 91-94 codetta: Vln I single G6 line with Fl 1 (c167) is an improvement (no change)
- **Problem in v159.** Vln I's double stops B♭4/G5 put the tune's top inside the brass.
- **v169.** Vln I plays G6–A♭6–E♭6–D6 with Fl 1, and Vln II takes the double stops. The melody note is now the most prominent pitch (PDF 159, rule 1).
- **Doubling.** Beethoven's lesson (PDF 152) is that a high flute octave needs no doubling. The Vln I doubling here is still right, because the hammered strokes need string attack.
- **Decision.** Keep it.

### 4. 159-166: Ob 2's held line G4–D4 after c166 (no change)
- **What changed.** c166 gave the counter-line to Cl 1 and moved the held line to Ob 2 (score staff "Oboe 2"), which puts the oboe in its low register.
- **Evidence.** PDF 159, rule 2 asks for each pitch in a register where it can be played at the marked level. A low oboe at mf can do that. The clarinet's throat tones G4–A♭4 (sounding), which held this line in v159, are weaker.
- **Decision.** The change is neutral to good, and I keep it.

### 5. The coda's loud stretch 247-279 judged against PDF 146 (no change)
- **The stretch.** The coda runs 33 bars at ff or more with basses in eighths.
- **Why it is not unrelieved.** It has three breaks:
  - the woodwind rest 255-256;
  - the cut at 264.1, where the brass, low reeds and timpani stop under the scale figure (protected since c128);
  - the thinner 265-272, about 12 voices against 24 at 257-264 in analysis.txt.
- **Franck.** His coda keeps the full orchestra for a similar length after the apotheosis.
- **Decision.** No change.
