## Findings

### 1. 143-145: the trombones' crescendo crosses the 143|144 page turn as a bare wedge (applied)

**Problem.** In the retransition the build from pp to the f of F1's stretto entry at 145 runs over the page turn between pages 7 and 8. c268 wrote that crescendo as "cresc." with dashes in the bassoons, contrabassoon, timpani, violas, cellos and basses because the wedges crossed the turn. c405 then made the continuation on page 8 print "(cresc.)". Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone enter at 143, pp, on the same G-major pedal attack as the contrabassoon (`d'1\pp\<`, `g1\pp\<`, `g,1\pp\<`). They were left with hairpins, so they are the only parts in the build that do not follow the rule. On page 7 the contrabassoon's B0 reads "pp cresc. - -" and the trombones' D4/G3 and the bass trombone's G2 directly below read "pp <". On page 8, bar 144 opens the trombone staves with the tail of a wedge that has no beginning. It is the only unlabelled dynamic fragment after a turn in the score. The LilyPond break log lists exactly two broken Hairpins in the whole score, both at 143 and both in these staves.

**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 7: the layout must let the player turn and read on, so after the turn the part must still say what the line is doing. MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 6 (PDF 11): proofread the vertical line, so that one attack has one mark. Here the trombones and contrabassoon enter together on the same chord at the same level, and the marks differ. MOLA Ex. 7 (PDF 12): a longer dynamic change written as words. Adler part 2, PDF 201, item 3: a crescendo is clearer written out. Adler part 2, PDF 299: page turns are a practical problem for the player. This also completes c268's and c405's own convention.

**Fix (applied, retr.ily, three edits):**
- `retrTbnOne`: `d'1\pp\cresc | d'1 | f'1\f` (was `d'1\pp\< | d'1 | f'1\f`)
- `retrTbnTwo`: `g1\pp\cresc | g1 | g1\f`
- `retrBassTbn`: `g,1\pp\cresc | g,1 | g,1\f`

A source comment (c468) is added after `retrTbnOne`. Both players on the shared Tbn 1·2 staff carry the same marks. Page 7 now reads "pp cresc. - -" in the trombones, bass trombone and contrabassoon alike, and page 8 restates "(cresc.) - - f" in all eight parts of the build (c405's callback). LilyPond plays `\cresc` like a hairpin, and score.midi is identical to v468's, every note-on and velocity included.

**Scratch build** (`/tmp/critic-468/work`): 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt is empty), 16 pages, analysis.txt identical to v468's (0 outer-voice parallels), par8.py over 141-146 reports nothing, theme record 51/51. Bars and notes are unchanged.

## Considered and not applied

- **Hn 3·4 at 271.2.** They re-enter after a one-bar rest with no dynamic. Tbn 1·2 enter with the same figure at 271.1 marked ff, and Hn 3·4's own ff is at 269.2. Ex. 3 restates a level after a *numbered* rest. Here the rest is shorter than a bar and the level has not changed. A second ff two bars after the first would be the kind of duplicate c068 removed. No change.
- **"dim." for the staged diminuendo at 39-42** (strings ff → p over three bars, winds f → p). The score has never used "dim.". Ex. 7 would allow words. But these wedges sit on one system, end on printed levels, and are staggered by group as c008 planned. Words would hide the stagger that the wedges show. The tested change at 143 was better. This one is not, so it was not applied.
- **"a tempo" at 151 and 199** (MOLA Ex. 7). c075 accepted "Tempo I" as cancelling the rit. at both places, and the tempo is again the movement's first tempo. Changing the word would be churn without a new reason.
- **Articulation differences on doubled attacks.** The tuba tongues F1 at 27-32 while the other basses slur. Horns and trumpets accent the horn call at 243 while the flutes and violins hold. The trombones resolve the seventh at 151 under the accented head. These are role differences and not proofreading errors.
