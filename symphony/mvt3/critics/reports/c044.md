## What else I studied
- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, PDF 74–75. When a woodwind plays in unison with a brass instrument, the brass tone wins and the woodwind only softens it. To get a rich upper octave above horns or trumpets in forte, "three or four wind instruments will be necessary above". This supports Finding 1.
- Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 139: a timpani tremolo can overpower any other group, so the roll in Finding 3 is marked f, under the ff.
- The v44 page images 13 and 15, and my scratch build's pages 10, 13 and 15, for crowding.
- tools/critic-scripts/c032/p5.py, run on both MIDIs to check every pair of staves for fifths.

## Findings

### 1. 257–264, the last cadence chord: the upper woodwinds double the trumpets at pitch, and the top octave is thin (applied)
**Problem.** At 263 (fff, the movement's only root V–I) Ob 1 and Cl 1 both play C5 at pitch with Tpt 1, Fl 2 plays E5 with Tpt 2, Cl 2 plays G4 with Tpt 3, and Ob 2 plays E4. Above the trumpets there is only C6, in Fl 1 and Vln I. At the arrival (223) Fl, Ob and Cl ran from C6 to C7. Here the cadence that is meant to crown the movement has four of its seven woodwinds inside the brass unisons, in their middle register, under fff brass.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 164 (Mahler 1): the high brass are doubled an octave higher by the violins and upper winds, and every upper octave is filled.
- PDF 165: doubling at pitch loses the colour of both instruments; for power, octave doubling is "clearer and more brilliant".
- PDF 159, rule 2: each pitch goes to a register that can play it at the dynamic wanted.
- PDF 161 (Weber): oboe, not clarinet, is the trumpet's sympathetic doubling at pitch.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 74: in forte, several winds are needed in the octave above the brass.

**Fix (applied).** Cl 1 moves up an octave for the whole chorale statement, joining Fl 1 and Vln I (E6, D6, C6 and B5 are all in the clarinet's strong upper register; Cl 1 already holds E6 at 223). Ob 1 stays with Tpt 1. The chorale top line is unchanged in Tpt 1, Fl 1 and Vln I. In `finaleClOne`:
```
e'''1\mf\<~ | e'''1 | d'''1\f\<~ | d'''1 | c'''1 | b''1 | c'''1\fff~ | c'''1
```
(was `e''1 … c''1`).

### 2. 226–228, the canon's second players sit inside the brass chorale (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 42 moved Fl 2 and Ob 2 onto G at 226 and changed 227's second half. As a result:
- Ob 2 is on G4, in unison with Tpt 2 and Hn 1.
- Cl 2 plays D4, then E4–F4, then A4, in unison with Hn 2·3/Tbn 1, then Hn 1, then Tpt 2.

Both are marked ff while the brass play f. They belong neither to the canon (G5–E6, woodwinds and violins) nor to the chorale. They thicken the brass at pitch in the clarinet's weakest register, and they blur the separation of the two ideas by choir.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 169: two ideas separate when scored in opposing timbres and different designs.
- PDF 177–178 (Meistersinger): each element keeps its own colour combination.
- PDF 159 rule 2 and PDF 165, as in Finding 1.

**Fix (applied).** For 226–228, while the brass sound, Ob 2 and Cl 2 move up an octave, under the canon. Ob 2 is then in unison with Fl 2: Fl + Ob is a woodwind affinity, PDF 159. At 229, when the brass rest, both return to their written octave and fill the middle. I first kept Cl 2 high through 230, but p5.py found fifths at 229–230 (Cl 2 F5–E5 over Ob 2 B♭4–A4), so I dropped that.

### 3. 151–152, the new G pedal is held only by the low strings and bassoons; the timpani strike once (applied)
**Problem.** Since c043 the recapitulation begins as a cadential 6/4 over G.
- The pedal is in Bn 1·2 (G2, a2), Cbn and Cb (G1), Vc and B.tbn (G2). The tuba is held back until 159, which is good (Schumann, PDF 162).
- The timpani give a single G at 151 and then rest until 155.
- Above the pedal, F1 is ff in nine parts across three octaves (C4, C5, C6), with brass off-beats and clarinet arpeggios at f.
- Balance is acceptable, but the pedal is heard as repeated bass minims, not as a held dominant.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 168–169 (Schubert 8/i, 170–176): the move into harmony is carried by "the dominant pitch in the timpani".
- PDF 176 (Jupiter): horns, trumpets and timpani give the "cohesive tonic-dominant, nonthematic pedal notes".
- PDF 184 (Bloch) and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139: a ff roll covers everything, so the roll goes one level below the tutti.
- c043's own model, Beethoven 9/i at its recapitulation, holds the timpani roll under the returning theme.

**Fix (applied).** In `recPTimp`:
```
g,1:32->\f | g,2.:32 r4 | R1*2 | c4->\ff r4 r2 | …
```
(was `g,4->\ff r4 r2 | R1*3 | c4-> …`). The timpani hold the G through the i6/4 and the iv over the pedal, stop before the bass moves at 153, and re-enter ff on the tonic root at 155. No pitches change.
