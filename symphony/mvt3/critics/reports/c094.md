## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 135–138: Vln II doubles the bass line two octaves up, so B is doubled in the stop chord (applied)
**Problem.** In the diminished-seventh chain the bass moves D–D♭–C–B (Vc D3, Cb D2, Bn 2). Vln II plays `d''1 | des''1 | c''1 | b'1`, which is the same line two octaves higher. The four real parts are already complete without it: Vln I/Bn 1 have A♭–G–G♭–F, Cl 2/Hn 3/lower violas have F–E–E♭–D, and Cl 1/upper violas/Hn 4 have B–B♭–A–A♭. Vln II is a fifth part that doubles only the bass. At 138 this puts B in B1, B2, B4 and B5. B is the bass of a diminished seventh (and the third of the implied V9).
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 82: extra parts double "the three upper parts", "the bass being doubled in the lower octave only". The note under the PDF 82 example (image only) says doubling the bass "creates an effect of heaviness. The bass part should never mix with the others." PDF 83–84: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts. This applies also to … diminished seventh." par.py lists the Vln II–Cb and Vln II–Vc octaves at 136 in v94.
**Fix (applied, retr.ily).** `retrVlnTwo = { f''1\pp\< | e''1 | es''1 | d''1\sf\> | …`. Vln II now doubles the F–E–E♭–D line an octave higher (Rimsky PDF 82, 97 remark 3). At 138 B sounds only in the bass and in Ob 1's protected top B5 (cycle 10). The chord's pitch classes, dynamics and the 222→223 resolution are unchanged.

### 2. 91–92 beat 3: cellos and Bn 2 play D3 over the basses' B♭1, which makes a V6 inside the bass (applied)
**Problem.** The codetta's V7 of E♭ has B♭ in the 16' bass (Cb, Cbn, B.tbn, tuba B♭1). The 8' bass (Vc, Bn 2) plays `d4` (D3) instead of B♭2. Those players carry the bass line at every other point of 83–98, so the ear hears the D as a first-inversion V. The same D is doubled in Tpt 3 and Vln II (D4) and Cl 1 (D5). At 93 the cellos do take B♭2, so the bar is inconsistent with its neighbours.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 82: the bass is doubled only in the lower octave. PDF 83: the bass of an inverted dominant is never doubled above. PDF 85: the bass rarely lies more than an octave below the next part (B♭1 to D3 is a tenth).
**Fix (applied, clos.ily).** In closVc and closBsnTwo, bars 91 and 92: `es4->\ff r8 es8 bes,4-> r4 | es4-> r8 es8 bes,4-> r4`. par.py now shows the cellos moving E♭–B♭ in octaves with the basses, which is a true bass doubling.

### 3. 91–92: Ob 2 on G4/A♭4 sits under both clarinets (applied)
**Problem.** The woodwind order at 91 beat 1 is Fl 1 G6, Fl 2 E♭6, Ob 1 B♭5, Cl 1 E♭5, Cl 2 B♭4, then Ob 2 G4. The two clarinets are enclosed between the two oboes, an octave and a half apart. Ob 2 also lies in a weaker register than Ob 1 in an ff chord. At 93–94 Ob 2 is back above the clarinets (E♭5/D5), so 91–92 is the odd layout.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 90, rule 2: normal order of register. PDF 91: a wide chord in pairs of colours keeps register order; "any other distribution will result unquestionably in a grievous lack of relationship between registers". PDF 92: an enclosed colour "must be between two different timbres". PDF 95–96: the classical layout is 2 Fl over 2 Ob over 2 Cl.
**Fix (applied, clos.ily).** `closOboeTwo = { R1*4 | R1*4 | g''4->\ff r8 g''8 aes''4-> r4 | g''4-> r8 g''8 aes''4-> r4 | …`. The block is now Fl 1·2 / Ob 1·2 / Cl 1·2, overlaid in order. No new parallels. (The analysis's top-line voice is not affected.)

### 4. 110 and 112: the scale figure in the cellos and violas is covered by Tbn 1·2 in the same register (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 57 gave the climax a low-string scale figure: Vc `c'8 bes aes g f g` at 110 and Vla `c'8 bes aes g` at 112, in the C4–G3 octave. Tbn 1 (C4) and Tbn 2 (G3) hold whole and half notes f in exactly that octave, with four horns f just above. By Rimsky's ratios a string section in forte equals two woodwinds (PDF 51), and one trombone equals two horns, so the two trombones alone are several times the weight of one string section. The figure is written out but cannot be heard.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 51 (ratios); PDF 52 (strings and brass side by side stay apart, but the brass is the most resonant group); PDF 90 rule 1, with its exception "when one of the harmonic parts is to be made prominent"; PDF 98 (short chords separated by rests hide the layout).
**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** Tbn 1·2 strike the chord and release: 110 `c'4->\f r4 r2` / `g4->\f r4 r2`, and 112 `des'2 r2` / `bes2 r2`. Horns, trumpets, bass trombone and tuba still hold the V7. Bn 1 doubles the figure at the unison, which thickens the strings as PDF 52 describes: `\partCombineApart e4\ff c'8( bes aes g f g) | f2 f2 | f2 c'8( bes aes g) | aes2-> r2 |`. The brass stays f and the climax keeps every pitch. Cycles 54 and 64 (brass f, horns doubled) are kept.

### 5. 67–74: the counter-melody is as heavy as the tune (applied)
**Problem.** F2 is in Vln I and Vln II in octaves, mf. The counter-melody is in violas and cellos in unison, also mf "espress.", lying G3–F4. That is the cellos' strongest singing register and the violas' upper middle. Two sections in one unison carry more than two sections spread over an octave. Below the tune there is only the harp and Bn 1·2 p, so nothing else covers the counter-melody.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 51: in soft passages one string section equals one wind, so the tune and the counter-melody have the same nominal weight, and the unison concentrates it. PDF 53: the strings are the most expressive group, so the louder line takes the ear.
**Fix (applied, sTwo.ily).** Vla and Vc start `\mp` at 67 (same markup) and add `aes2(\< g2) | bes2(\mf …` at 76–77, so they grow with the violins' crescendo to f at 78. Cycles 27 and 51 (the line itself) are kept.

### 6. 53–56: the soft wind trio under the trumpet solo has open fourths and a doubled leading tone (applied)
**Problem.** Rimsky says a three-part wind basis over a separate (here pizz.) bass should be complete in itself. At 53 (iv6 over C♭) Cl 1·2 + Bn 1 sound E♭3–A♭3–E♭4: an open fourth and octave with no C♭. At 56 (V7 over B♭) they sound D3–B♭3–D4: the leading tone in octaves and no F or A♭. D4 is also in Vln II's pizz.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 97, remark 4: the three-part wind harmony "should form an independent whole, receiving no assistance from the bass; in this manner intervals of open fourths and fifths will be obviated". PDF 92 (image): three-part chords go to two of one colour and one of another (Cl 1·2 + Bn 1 already do this).
**Fix (applied, sOne.ily).** 53 Bn 1 `aes1` and Cl 2 `ces'1`, with Cl 2 at 54 `ces'2 ges2`, so the trio is A♭3–C♭4–E♭4. 56 Bn 1 `f1`, so the trio is F3–B♭3/A♭3–D4. My first try put C♭4 in Cl 1, but that moves E♭–C♭ in octaves with the pizz. bass (Rimsky PDF 83), so I discarded it. The texture, rhythm and pp dynamics are unchanged. This answers the reason behind the do-not-touch notes (cycles 4, 7, 58: the pizz. off-beat texture and the pp sustained pad stay). Only two held pitches change, as cycle 93 did at 52 and 60.
