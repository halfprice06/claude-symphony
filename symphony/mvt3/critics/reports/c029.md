A conductor would accept the key plan, the cadence plan and the brass writing. The plainest gap is in what the coda brings back. ## Findings

### 1. The scherzo has no audible recall in the finale's summing-up (275–276) — applied

**Problem.** The coda gathers movement I (235–254, 265–272) and movement II (211–218). The scherzo appears only at 143–145, in the timpani, as repeated notes. Dan wants the four movements to sound like one work. Dvořák 9/iv brings its slow movement back so that it is heard (300–306).

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 117–118 says a recurring idea is made new by a new key and by "alteration of details" (Ex. 176–200).
- Rimsky PDF 115–116: a short brass figure over string tremolo goes to the trumpet, doubled when power is needed, with the harmony in the woodwind so the figure stands out by colour.
- Bars 275–276 are the right place for this. They are a C-major tonic (after IV6/4 – iv6/4 over the C pedal), the strings are in tremolo, the woodwind hold the chord, and nothing melodic is going on.
- The head of the scherzo's theme A (scherzo VlnOne 13: `e''4 b'8 b'8 e''4 | g''2 fis''8 f''8 | e''4`), moved down a major third and turned to major, becomes C–G–G–C | E–D–D♭–C. Its tail is movement I's motto tail with E♮, and the following bar (277, `c4 r8 g8 c4`) echoes its rhythm.

**Fix (applied, sections/finale.ily, bars 275–276).**
- Tpt 1, Tpt 2 (a2) and Cl 1: `c''4->\< g'8-. g'8-. c''4-. e''4->~( | e''4 d''8 des''8 c''4-.) r4 |`
- Fl 1, an octave higher: `c'''4->\< g''8-. g''8-. c'''4-. e'''4->~( | e'''4 d'''8 des'''8 c'''4-.) r4 |`
- Hn 1, Hn 2 (a2), an octave lower: `c'4->\< g8-. g8-. c'4-. e'4->~( | e'4 d'8 des'8 c'4-.) r4 |`
- Everything else is unchanged. The A–A♭–G inner line still sounds at 273–274 in Hn 1, Tpt 2, Fl 2, Ob 1 and Vln I, and the G of 275–276 is held in Fl 2, Vln I, Vla and Tbn 1.
- No V–I is added (the bass stays on C). The quotation comes after the last cadence (261–263), and the cadence plan is untouched.

### 2. The chorale's first brass chord has a hole and a low doubled third (224) — applied

**Problem.** At 224 (f, brass only) the chord is C2 C3 | E3 E3 G3 G3 | E4 E4 G4 G4. Nothing sounds between G3 and E4, and the third E is doubled below the alto in Hn 4 and Tbn 2. The chorale's first sound is thick at the bottom and hollow in the middle.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 100: brass writing "should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals".
- Rimsky PDF 82: added parts duplicate the upper parts in the adjacent upper octave; only the bass is doubled below.
- Bar 225 is already written that way (C4 in Hn 3 and Tbn 2).

**Fix (applied, sections/arrival.ily).**
- `arrivalHnThree`: 224 `g1\f` → `c'1\f`.
- `arrivalHnFour`: 224 `e1\f` → `c'1\f`.
- Hn 3 now holds C4 into 225, and Hn 4 moves C4 → G3. The arrival chord (223), the chorale top line and Tbn 2's E3 are unchanged.

### 3. Accents on tied notes in the last bar (279) — applied

**Problem.** In every part except the timpani, the final chord is written `X1->\fff~ | X4-> r4 r2\fermata`. A tied note cannot be attacked again, so the accent on the tied quarter is meaningless and makes players ask whether to re-strike. The cymbal had the same accent. The timpani roll tied into an accented release stroke is normal practice and stays.

**Fix (applied, sections/finale.ily).** `4-> r4 r2\fermata` → `4 r4 r2\fermata` in all lines except `finaleTimp`, and `cymc4->` → `cymc4`.

This goes against Rimsky PDF 116, as noted in the comparison with v19 above. I decided to keep it: recognisability was the reason, and it outweighs the colour contrast here. No change.
