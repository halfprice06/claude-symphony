## Findings

### 1. 242: the ii7 before the E-major chord becomes iiø7 (A♭), so movement II's A♭ turns into the scherzo's G♯ in one horn. Applied.

**Bars:** 241–243 (sHead.ily, bar 8 of the section).

**Problem.** The C-major second-theme passage runs vi – IV – ii – ii7 (239–242) into the E-major chord at 243 (III♯), which recalls the scherzo's key. c20 put that chord there to close the C – A♭ – E cycle of the four movements' keys. But in the bars themselves the A♭ side of the cycle is missing: 239–242 are diatonic C major, and the G♯ at 243 arrives as a plain semitone below A (Hn 4 A3→G♯3). The finale's first bar does the opposite move: the scherzo's G♯4 becomes A♭4 and falls to G4 in Hn 1. Nothing in the coda answers that.

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 387 and Points 2a (PDF 395): ♭6 "colours and intensifies" II7, which becomes II7♭ (D–F–A♭–C), and it needs no preparation. PDF 387–388, Ex. 23-2f: ♭6 can enter as an inflection of ♮6. PDF 388 and Points 3: once ♭6 is introduced it must reach 5 before any ♮6, so ♮6–♭6–5 is good. Points 5–6: III♯ is approached by II, which this keeps. The chosen line in Hn 4 is A3 (241, ♮6) – A♭3 (242, ♭6) – G♯3 (243.1, the same pitch as the third of III♯) – G3 (243.3, 5̂ in the cadential 6/4). That is the textbook ♮6–♭6–5, with the ♭6 held over as G♯ for half a bar. It is also the mixture move of PDF 387 ("two different tones represent the same scale degree … dramatic juxtaposition"), here as the same pitch under two names. A♭ is movement II's key and G♯ the third of the scherzo's E. The pitch sounds in one player at one octave and then falls to G, as the scherzo's G♯→A♭→G does across the pause at 1–2 (finale.md, Opening). The half-diminished chord is also the last cadence's pre-dominant (259–260); see finding 3.

### 3. The E♭/E♮ thread and the arc after the change

The reading's main long-range point (PDF 387) is that one scale degree in two forms can carry a conflict through a piece. In this finale that degree is 3̂: E♭ (C minor, bar 1's German sixth in the violas) against E (C major). The conflict closes in the same violas at 219–220 and on the E4 at 223 (Ex. 23-12b: a chromatic step in one voice). After 223, E♭ returns only inside the bar-1 German sixth at 261 (Hn 3 E♭4, Tbn 2 E♭3), which goes to V7 and then I. So the thread ends with the same chord it began with, and I did not change it. Finding 1 adds the second mixture pair, ♭6/♯5 (A♭/G♯), on the same model. That pair links the keys of movements II and III, as the E♭/E pair links movements I and IV. ♭6 now acts at three stages of the coda, each time moving to G: 233 (iv in the plagal close), 242 (iiø7 → III♯), and 259–261 (iiø6/5 → Ger6 → V7). The first stays plagal, the second is turned aside, and only the third reaches V–I. That is a graded approach to the one root V–I, not a repetition.
