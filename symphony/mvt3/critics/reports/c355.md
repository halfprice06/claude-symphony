## Findings

**1. Bars 131–132, Vln I and Vln II: the cadence note is the last note of a long slur. Applied.**
At 131 the violins run up six eighths at f (Vln I A5–F♯6, Vln II F♯4–D5), and the slur goes on to the accented quarter on 132.1, the G-major cadence of the development (Vln I G6, Vln II D5). Under one slur the accent cannot be a new bow attack. Six eighths and a quarter at f is more than Adler allows: "only six notes on a bow, since the dynamic is forte" (PDF 39, Ex. II-22). His rules (PDF 38, Ex. II-19, II-20) put the accented arrival on a fresh down-bow. Vln II is also unclear: its slur runs from the eighth D5 into the quarter D5, which reads like a tie. The score already does this correctly at 9–10, where the run's slur stops before the accented D6 (c218). Fix in `dev.ily`: end the slur on the sixth eighth.
Vln I: `r4 a''8(\f b'' … e''' fis''') | g'''4-> d'''4-> b''4-> g''4->`
Vln II: `r4 fis'8(\f g' a' b' c'' d'') | d''4-> b'4-> g'4-> d'4->`
The run is an anacrusis, so players take it up-bow (PDF 38), and 132.1 then falls on a down-bow without a mark. The diminuendo at 133 comes on a down-bow, which suits it. No bow mark is added, so the part is not overmarked (PDF 38). Pitches and dynamics are unchanged.

**2. Bars 211–218, violas: the repeated half-note thirds under theme B take a new bow on every chord. Use louré, a technique the score uses only once. Applied (third-series technique).**
Under theme B's recall (trumpets, flutes, oboe, clarinet, all *cantabile*, Largamente 2 = 72), the violas, divided, repeat each third in half notes for eight bars, ff > f, then f > mp. Every chord is a separate bow, so the only moving string accompaniment is chopped while the wind and brass around it are sustained (the winds hold whole notes; c106 describes these as "held thirds"). Adler's louré is meant for this kind of accompaniment: "a legato stroke, but a slight separation of the notes is effected while the bow is being drawn … very expressive … used quite often in accompaniments", marked with "dashes … with slurs to designate the bow changes" (PDF 42–43; Ex. II-30, Handel, repeated notes under the voice, PDF 43). The score uses louré only for the violas at 224–228 and Vln II at 231–233, so 211–218 also prepares the arrival's viola pulse. Fix in `codaTwo.ily`: one bar per bow.
`codaTwoVla = { <des' f'>2--(\ff\>^\markup \italic "div." <des' f'>2--) | <des' f'>2--(\f <des' f'>2--) | <ges' bes'>2--( <ges' bes'>2--) | <aes' c''>2--( <aes' c''>2--) | <aes' c''>2--( <aes' c''>2--) | <aes' des''>2--( <aes' des''>2--) | <aes' des''>2--(\> <aes' des''>2--) | <aes' des''>2--(\mp <aes' des''>2--) | …`
Bow plan: 211 ⊓ with the ff diminuendo, which is natural on a down-bow (PDF 38), then alternate bows, so 217's diminuendo to mp is also a down-bow. Each bow lasts 1.7 s at f, well within a viola's bow (PDF 39). Pitches, *div.*, dynamics and c138's diminuendo are unchanged. The engraved page shows the dashes inside the slurs on both chords, so the slurs cannot be read as ties. The c155/c156 protections of 211–222 cover the dynamic path and 222→223, and both are unchanged.

**3. Bar 236, Vln I and Vln II: "port." is printed below the staff, away from its line. Applied.**
The S head's C5→A5 slide on the A string is a true one-string glissando (PDF 34), and a conscious slide is the case where Adler accepts a printed *port.* (PDF 33). But the word sits below the staff, next to the crescendo hairpin, while the line runs between the noteheads inside the staff. Adler: a line "with or without the word glissando (gliss.) above the line" (PDF 33; Ravel's Ex. II-15, PDF 34, prints *gliss.* along the line). Fix in `sHead.ily`, both violin parts: `c''4(\!-\tweak outside-staff-priority #340 ^\markup \italic "port." \glissando a''2--\< g''4\!)`. The word now stands just above the slur and below the *sul A* line, over the slide (checked at 250 dpi on page 13).
