## Findings

### 1. 66: the bar before F2's major statement holds still. One chromatic inner voice should turn the chord to major and hand its pitch to the counter-melody (applied)

**Problem.** At 66 the trumpet solo ends on a whole-note E♭4 (i of E♭ minor after V at 65). Under it Cl 1 holds B♭3, Cl 2 G♭3 and Bn 1 E♭3. The pizzicato strings play once on beats 1–2. After beat 2 nothing moves, and then 67 starts the violins' E♭-major statement, mf, with the violas' and cellos' counter-melody starting on G3. This is the "static lead" Sebesky asks the arranger to help, so that one bar carries into the next (PDF 207, Ex. 269). It is also the place for a fill, since the lead holds for a whole bar (PDF 222–223, Ex. 286). The reading names two exact means:
- Ex. 272 (PDF 207): a chromatic inner melody that changes the chord's quality while the lead holds.
- Note C (PDF 216): one voice moves alone while the others sustain, and "this line also sets up" the next section's entry.

PDF 222 (Ex. 285, bar 10) adds that the line can hand the next entry its first note. The score already uses Ex. 272 at its close, 273–276 (G–F♯–F–E over the C pedal). It does not use it at the minor-to-major turn of its most lyrical theme. c141 put motion into the antecedent's half cadences (58, 178), but 66 stayed static.

**Answer to the protection of 51–66** (c004, c007, c041, c057, c201: undoubled trumpet solo over pp pizzicato and pp clarinet/bassoon pads):
- The change uses an instrument that already plays, at the same level (pp).
- It moves below the solo and does not double it.
- It begins after the trumpet has landed on its last note.
- The trumpet, pizzicato, Cl 1, Bn 1, ties and dynamics are unchanged.

This is the same answer c141 gave at 58.

**Fix (applied, sections/sOne.ily, `sOneClTwo`, bar 66).** `ges1` becomes `ges2( g2)`.

On beat 3 the clarinet's G♭3 rises to G3. The held chord becomes E♭ major under the trumpet's E♭4, one soft voice ahead of the violins. Cl 2 then rests at 67, where the violas and cellos start their counter-melody on the same G3, so the clarinet passes its pitch to them. The step G♭→G stays in one voice, so the pizzicato G♭3 on beat 2 makes no cross relation in separate voices. No parallel is created (par8 on 64–68 gives the same list as v231, all existing bass doublings). The recapitulation needs no copy: 186 is already the A♭-major chord (VI) under the held C4, so it has no minor-to-major turn to prepare. The change does not affect F1, F2 or the chorale top line (F2's notes are unchanged; only its accompaniment moves). It changes no quotation, so the other movement threads need nothing. For finale.md, F2 bullet: "66: Cl 2 G♭3–G3 on beat 3 (since v232), E♭ minor turned to major under the solo's last note; G3 passes to the counter-melody at 67."

### Considered and left unchanged

- **Pad top line 51–66 (PDF 210, Ex. 277–278).** Cl 1 sits on E♭4 for most of the solo, which is Ex. 278's dull pattern. I keep it. PDF 219 governs a background under a soloist, and a moving top line in the solo's register (B♭3–B♭5) would compete with the undoubled trumpet. That is the reason the passage is protected.
- **Codetta 91–94 (PDF 210).** The top line is G6–A♭6 twice and then E♭6–D6–E♭6. I tried a rising version (B♭6 at 92, G6–F6 at 93). It leaves V7's seventh A♭6 unresolved at 92 and adds nothing to the brass hammer strokes, which are not a soft background. No change.
- **No string colour change (PDF 224).** The score never uses ponticello or senza vibrato. The suitable moments (113–114, 133–138) are protected and belong to lens 8.
- **F2 does not return in the coda (PDF 206–207).** This is c069's decision. The coda is protected, and the reading only allows a recurring figure; it does not demand one.
