## Findings

### 1. Pedal resets before 123 and 277 are printed only at the entry; mark them in the preceding rests (applied in scratch)

Bars: 115 (for 123) and 247 (for 277).

Problem. Two of the harp's entries need several pedals moved during a long rest, and the score and part show the new setting only on the entry's first note:
- 82 → 123: from E♭ major with C♭ (D C♭ B♭ / E♭ F G A♭) to G major (D C B / E F♯ G A). That is five changes: C and B with the left foot, E, F and A with the right foot.
- 242 → 277: from C major with G♯ (D C B / E F G♯ A) to the glissando tuning (D C B♯ / E F♭ G A). That is three changes: B with the left foot, F and G with the right foot.

Adler PDF 112: one foot changes only one pedal at a time ("avoid requesting that the player change the E and G pedal at the same moment"). PDF 113: the writer must make sure the harpist never has to change two pedals on the same side at once, and that the tempo leaves time. A diagram printed on 123.1 or on 277.3 (where the glissando starts) asks for three right-foot moves (123) or two (277) at the moment of playing. The score already places single changes ahead of the note ("C♭" under 81 for 82, "G♯" under 241 for 242). The two multi-pedal resets are the only ones without an advance mark. The harpist can work them out by comparing diagrams 40 and 34 bars apart, but the part does not show them.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-566/work), as text under the harp's left hand in the same "Ped.: left / right" form the part already uses at 43:
- sections/dev.ily, `devHarpL`: `R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4 |` → `R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4_\markup \small "Ped.: C♮ B♮ / E♮ F♯ A♮" |`. The text sits at 115 (letter H, top of page 7, the bassoon solo), eight bars before 123, about 11 s at half = 84.
- sections/finale.ily, `finaleHarpL`: `R1*30 |` → `R1*30_\markup \small "Ped.: B♯ / F♭ G♮" |`. The text sits at 247 (letter R, Più allegro), the harp's first full rest bar after the étouffez at 243, 30 bars before the glissando.

The diagrams at 123 and 277 and the part's glissando text stay as they are. In the part the texts stand on the multi-bar rests, so the harpist sees what to change and when.

### Tested candidate, declined: a ringing E-major harp chord at 243

Reason to try it: at 242 the harp's pedals (C D E F G♯ A B) already hold E–G♯–B. The III♯ at 243 closes the C–A♭–E cycle, and the scherzo ends on harp harmonics E–G♯–B (finale.md). A solid three-note chord in each hand (PDF 114) on 243.1 would need no pedal change.

Why I declined it. Under the f/ff horn call (Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2) a harmonic would not be heard (PDF 116: harmonics must be accompanied very lightly). A plain chord would undo the étouffez at 243 and the harp diminuendo at 242 (c116 and c238, accepted to clear the call). The horn call 243–246 is protected. The gain is a colour echo that no listener could pick out at that dynamic, so it does not clearly improve the music. I did not apply it.
