### 1. 175–177: root-position ii–V7–i in C minor under the F2 recap (applied)
**Problem.** The pizzicato bass under the cor anglais and muted trumpet runs D (175) – G (176) – C (177) on three downbeats. Over it are iiø7, V7 (Bsn 1 B2, Vla D/F) and i (Vla E♭, Bsn C), with the melody F–A♭ | G–F–E♭–D | E♭–C. That is a complete authentic progression in C with roots in the bass. The cadence plan allows the first one only at 262–263. The same phrase already avoids it at 172 and 180, where the pizz. bass plays B (V6). c012 changed 14, 22, 154 and 162 for exactly this reason. The melody's 3̂ on 177 does not cancel the effect, because the bass roots make it.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 149–150 §16: if a subordinate progression "threatens to produce an inappropriate cadential effect … an inversion of V(7) or VII6 in place of the V". PDF 142 §9: inverted dominants are "particularly useful in avoiding an unwanted cadential effect". PDF 148 rule 1: the bass of an inverted V7 continues by step to I, here B→C.

**Fix (applied).** recS.ily, bar 6 of the section (176), pizz. bass only:
- `recSVc`: `| d,4 r4 r2 | g,4 r4 r2 | c4 r4 aes,4 r4` → `| d,4 r4 r2 | b,4 r4 r2 | c4 r4 aes,4 r4`
- `recSCb`: `| d,4 r4 r2 | g,,4 r4 r2 | c,4` → `| d,4 r4 r2 | b,,4 r4 r2 | c,4`

The bass becomes D–B–C–A♭–G, the same V6/5 colour as 172 and 180, and Bsn 1's sustained B2 is now the bass. After the change, the scan and a part-by-part check find no root V–i in C before 262–263. I left the exposition counterpart (56, E♭ minor) alone, because the rule is about C.

### 2. 220→221: the cadential 6/4's 4th rises in Hn 2 and Tpt 3 (applied)
**Problem.** At the 6/4 before the stop chord, C sounds in ten parts. Eight of them fall to B at 221. Hn 2 and Tpt 3 rise C4→D4, in the same octave where Hn 4, Bsn 1 and the violas fall C4→B3. The brass therefore splits the 4th both ways at the crescendo into the arrival. c012 (finding 5) fixed the violins and violas here and left the horn on D. The trumpet's rising C has the same fault and is the more audible of the two.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153 §3: the 4th "is dissonant … it will resolve by step to the 3rd of dominant harmony". PDF 160 pt 2: it "must resolve downward by step". PDF 154 §5 and Ex. 10-5: the 4th may be doubled only in very free textures. Here the doubling is the common tone held over from the Ger6, which is fine, but then every doubled C must fall.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily, bar 11 of the section (221):
- `codaTwoTptThree`: `… c'1\< | d'1\mp\< | d'1\f\<` → `… c'1\< | b1\mp\< | d'1\f\<`

### 3. 151–152: hold the recap 6/4's 4th; removes the trombone/clarinet fifths against F1 (applied)
**Problem.** The recap opens with i6/4 over the G pedal (C in Tbn 1, Tpt 3, Hn 3, Cl 2). In 152 the C leaves upward. Tbn 1 and Hn 3 go to D4, Tpt 3 falls to B♭3 and harmonises F1's passing B♭ as a minor v7. All of them come back to C on beat 3. In that return, Tbn 1 and Cl 2 move D4→C4 while F1's lowest octave (Hn 1·2, violas) moves G3→F3, which gives parallel fifths in held lines. c032 kept the 152/160 fifths as "clarinets' broken-chord eighths", but the trombone's whole notes and Cl 2's slurred halves are not figuration, so that reason does not cover them.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153 §3: the 4th enters as a common tone and resolves down by step. PDF 156 (Ex. 10-8): a prolonged 6/4 "is still dependent on the dominant to which it resolves" and generates tension, which needs the 4th held. PDF 136 §3 and PDF 151 pt 2: avoid fifths between voices moving by step.

**Fix (applied).** recP.ily, bar 2 of the section (152) only. 160 has a different bass (VII6–iv) and stays as it is.

### 4. 163→164: the Neapolitan's D♭ rises chromatically in Hn 3 (applied)
**Problem.** The recap's N6 (163, beats 3–4) resolves well in the melody (D♭–C–B) and in Tbn 1 (D♭4–C4–B3), and Tpt 3 takes D♭4→B3. Hn 3, in the same rhythm and octave as Tpt 3, moves D♭4→D4 on 164 beat 2. That is ♭2̂ rising to ♮2̂: a chromatic correction in one voice, sounding against the D♭–C–B resolution.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 138 §5: "In II6–V the upper voices usually descend, 2̂ moving to 7̂". The N6 is the chromatic II6, so its 2̂ has the stronger pull downward. PDF 139 (Ex. 9-7) on II6 doubling in minor warns about the melodic problems that follow an awkward doubling of the upper parts.

**Fix (applied).** recP.ily, bar 14 of the section (164): `recPHnThree` `| r4 c'4-. r4 des'4-. | r4 d'4-. r4 d'4-. |` → `| r4 c'4-. r4 des'4-. | r4 b4-. r4 d'4-. |`. Hn 3 now joins Tpt 3 on B3 for beat 2 and still takes D4 on beat 4. Hn 4 (G3, F3) stays below. No theme changes.
