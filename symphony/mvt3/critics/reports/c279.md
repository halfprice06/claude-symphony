## Holistic comparison with Beethoven 3/iv

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this finale? Mostly yes. The Eroica finale shows three things that this score does as well:

- A theme's colour changes with each restatement, and the previous choir becomes the accompaniment. Beethoven moves the bass theme from pizzicato strings (12) to the woodwinds (20). This score does the same with F2 (trumpet solo 51, violins 67, the harp hand-over at 123, cor anglais with muted trumpet 171, violins in A♭ 187) and with F1 (strings and horns 11, woodwinds join 19, bass statement 27, recapitulation over the dominant 151, horns 205, trumpets in C major 265).
- Loud and soft are set against each other by changing the choir as well as the dynamic. Beethoven puts ff tutti strokes against a p strain. This score does it at 91–98 (ff codetta, then the oboe solo) and at 113–115.
- Before the fast end there is a broad stage. Beethoven's Poco Andante (349) has the theme ff in the horns, then the Presto. This score has the Maestoso arrival and chorale, then Poco più mosso, Più allegro (247) and the Largamente close. The coda's proportion (about 30%) is close to the Eroica's (Poco Andante plus Presto, about 22% of 473 bars). The extra length is justified because the coda carries the symphony's owed items.

Where Beethoven is plainer than this score, and better for it, is the way a block of sound is built. At the end of his first statement Beethoven does not swell one held chord: the orchestra enters in layers and then strikes. This score's dominant preparation (9–10) has thirteen parts entering together mf on one downbeat and leaves the growth to hairpins. Adler's transcription examples (Brahms, Bizet) teach the alternative, and that is Finding 1.

Comparison with v269: the nine changes since v269 (c269–c278: timpani f at 247, 277–278 non-div. chords and oboe thirds, cymbal/bass-drum voices at 278–279, Bn 1 on the countersubject at 35–38 with Hn 3·4 f, the 43–50 artificial harmonic, Vln II pizz. div. at 51 and 115, the turn at 75, the 73 IV–V6/5 of V approach with the rising bass, the 197.4 F♭ mixture, the 236–237 swells and 43–48 hairpins) are all improvements or neutral. Nothing got worse. One check: at 73 the harp's left hand rests on beat 4 (A♮ in the basses) and the right hand's A♭4 falls on 73.3 second eighth, before the A♮, so there is no false relation inside the harp.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 9–10: orchestrate the crescendo by staggered entries (applied)
**Problem.** After the bassoon and cello line of 7–8, all the held winds enter together on 9.1 at mf: Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Hn 1–4, violas, contrabassoon and basses. The crescendo to f on 10.3 is left to the hairpins. The downbeat of 9 thickens all at once, and bar 10 brings no new colour, so the swell depends on the players alone.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 205 (Brahms, *Hungarian Dance 1*): "the entire harmony is not sounded until the first offbeat in m. 3, so as not to thicken the downbeat suddenly." PDF 213 (Bizet): the diminuendo is "clearly orchestrated, instead of being left to the players or conductor... he did the same for the crescendo, mm. 13–18." Ex. XVI-11b (PDF 215–216) shows flutes, then oboe, clarinet, bassoon and horns joining one after another. Ravel (PDF 224) builds his crescendo the same way. In Beethoven 3/iv 7–11 the full chord also arrives as a stroke, not as a swell of the whole orchestra.
**Fix (applied in intro.ily).** Horns, violas, contrabassoon and basses keep 9.1, which is the held V chord. The clarinets join on 9.3. The oboes and flutes join on 10.1 with the V7♭9 top, so the harmony grows from V to V7♭9 as the sound grows:
```
introFluteOne = { ... | R1*3 | f'''2\mf\< d'''4->\f r4 | }
introFluteTwo = { ... | R1*3 | b''2\mf\< b''4->\f r4 | }
introOboeOne  = { ... | R1*3 | aes''2\mf\< f''4->\f r4 | }
introOboeTwo  = { ... | R1*3 | d''2\mf\< d''4->\f r4 | }
introClOne    = { ... | R1*2 | r2 b'2~\mf\< | b'2 b'4->\f r4 | }
introClTwo    = { ... | R1*2 | r2 g'2~\mf\< | g'2 g'4->\f r4 | }
```
This does not undo c189. Its rule was that no held part re-attacks on 10.1, and the horns, violas, contrabassoon and basses still tie through 9–10. The attacks on 9.3 and 10.1 are new instruments entering. The timpani's motive x at 9 (a scherzo link, protected) and the f accents on 10.3 are unchanged. No quotation or theme is affected. The bar 9 seventh F is still in the bassoon and cello line at 10, and the V7 arrives with the flutes' F6.

### 2. Bars 66–67: dovetail the background across the seam (applied)
**Problem.** At 66 the trumpet solo ends and every background part stops on 66.4: pizzicato strings, Cl 1 B♭3, Cl 2 G♮3, Bn 1 E♭3. At 67 the violins, harp, violas and cellos enter together. The tune should change colour with a fresh attack, and it does. The background, however, also stops completely, so the joint sounds like a cut rather than a hand-over.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 217 (Ravel, *Petit Poucet*): "The background dovetails or overlaps at [1], while the melody is taken from the oboe by the darker-hued English horn without overlap and a fresh articulation... The sustained G... is held by the clarinet into the next measure, an effect comparable to that produced by the damper pedal." PDF 224: "the background sometimes overlaps, while other times there is a new articulation." c228 applied the same idea of a held note passed between colours at 47.
**Fix (applied).** Cl 2's G♮3 is the major third reached at 66.3, the turn from E♭ minor to E♭ major. It is now tied into 67.1, where the violas and cellos enter on the same G3. So the clarinet hands its note to the strings:
```
sOne.ily:  sOneClTwo ... | ges2( g2)~ | }
sTwo.ily:  sTwoClTwo = { g4 r4 r2 | R1*9 | ...
```
Cl 2 is pp, under the strings' mp, and nothing else changes: not the 51–66 accompaniment's scoring or level, not Cl 1, and not the bassoons' fresh a2 attack at 67. Engraving cost: the clarinet staff now shows on the first system of page 4 (67–76) for this one quarter note. The page count stays 16.

- Brahms's contrary-motion woodwind arpeggio (PDF 208) for the clarinets at 11–26. Not applied: 11–18 is protected, and the clarinets' leggiero figure already fills the part.
- Schoenberg's doubling of the middle parts to signal a solo's end (PDF 230) at 65–66 or 185–186. Not applied: both solos are protected, and the flute already joins at the trumpet's peak (61–63).
- The exchange of high and low tessitura in successive loud phrases (PDF 239). The only candidates are 83–90, 199–204 and 265–272, which are all protected.
