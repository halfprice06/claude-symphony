## Findings

### 1. 243–246: the horn call in major has no swell on its long notes (applied)

- **Problem.** The owed horn call (E–C–B→C, movement I's 13–14 in major, augmented) is played by Hn 1·2 ff and Tpt 1·2 f in octaves over strings that start mf and grow louder ("cresc. molto"). Its two long notes, C (243.3 to 244.1) and B (244.2 to 245.2), both enter off the beat. c348 notes that no part marks the beat in these bars. The brass hold both notes at one level, so the call sounds like a held chord rather than a melody, and the off-beat entries are not shaped.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 304, Ex. X-8 (image checked): the call in Brahms 1/iv, the main model for this finale's C-major coda, is marked "f sempre e passionato", with a swell (< >) on the first long note and "< f >" on each later one. PDF 305, Ex. X-12: four horns in Don Juan "ff molto espr." have hairpins inside the ff. In both, the brass call is shaped note by note within one dynamic level. Movement I's own call (Hn 1, 13–14) has a hairpin on its held B. Adler PDF 309: one slur is one breath, and the four-bar slur stays as it is.
- **Fix (sections/sHead.ily, applied).** I changed no note, level or slur. The swells return to the printed level, so the balance set by c076 and c084 (horns ff, trumpets f, the trumpets with no crescendo into 247) is kept. The resolving C at 245.3 is held at the pair's level into 247. "molto espr." prints once per staff (Hn 2 and Tpt 2 have it as a part-only tag). `\partCombineChords` keeps each pair's octaves as one chord with one set of hairpins. Without it, the combiner split the pairs at 243 and moved the a2 notes of 251–266 out of the shared MIDI voice. `\partCombineSoloII` keeps Hn 2's G3 pickup at 246.4 printed as "2.", as before.
  ```
  sHeadTptOne = { R1*8 | \partCombineChords e''2->(\f^\markup \italic "molto espr." \after 4 \> c'''2~\< | c'''4 \after 2 \> b''2.~\< | b''2 c'''2~\! | c'''2.) \partCombineAutomatic r4 | }
  sHeadTptTwo = { R1*8 | \partCombineChords e'2->(\f-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "molto espr." \after 4 \> c''2~\< | c''4 \after 2 \> b'2.~\< | b'2 c''2~\! | c''2.) \partCombineAutomatic r4 | }
  sHeadHnOne = { R1*8 | \partCombineChords e'2->(\ff^\markup \italic "molto espr." \after 4 \> c''2~\< | c''4 \after 2 \> b'2.~\< | b'2 c''2~\! | c''2.) \partCombineSoloII r4 | }
  ```

  Each swell rises over the first part of its note and falls back before the next note: the C's < covers 243.3, its > runs 243.4 to 244.2; the B's < covers 244.2–244.3, its > runs 244.4 to 245.3. The finale's first bar already starts both pairs with `\partCombineApart`.

It does not change F1, F2, the chorale top line or any quotation, and the call's pitches and rhythm are unchanged.

### 3. Muted horns (con sordino): still unused; not applied

- Adler PDF 307 (Ex. X-17, Debussy; Ex. X-18, Mahler 4) gives muted horn as a separate colour and says a fast change is made by hand, not with a mute. The one unprotected soft horn note c288 did not consider is Hn 4's held A♭3 "dolce" pp at 43–47. Hn 4 rests only at 41–42, which is 2.9 s at 2 = 84 after the f > at 39–40, and that is too short to put in a mute with certainty. Its "dolce" asks for the open, warm sound under the strings' harmonics, and a stopped note follows in Hn 1 at 49–50, so 43–50 would get three horn colours in eight bars. I decided against it.
