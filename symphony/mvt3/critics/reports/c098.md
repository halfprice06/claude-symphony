## Findings

### 1. 110–113: the development climax has its brass one level below the strings (applied)

**Problem.** The climax is V7 of F minor (110), a 9–10 (G6 rising to A♭6 over F, 111) and the cadential 4–3 (F6–E6 over C, 112), then i (113). Horns, trumpets, trombones and tuba are marked f under ff strings and woodwinds (c054). In print the development's goal is therefore weaker than the exposition codetta (91–94, full brass ff) and the retransition dominant (147, full brass ff). c053 raised exactly this point, but the f in the brass brought it back.

The reading puts the most weight on the cadential suspension (PDF 350 §8). It says the suspension, not the resolution, gets the accent (PDF 348 §4). Here Tpt 1 (F5–E5) and Hn 2 (F4–E4) double the 4–3, so they are the parts that should carry it.

**Answer to c054's reason** (brass kept down so the strings and woodwinds come through): the melody's top octave (Vln I and Fl 1, G6–A♭6–F6–E6–F6) lies more than an octave above the highest brass note (Tpt 1 F5). Tpt 1 itself doubles the lower octave's F5–E5–F5 at 111–113. Dvořák 9/iv marks trumpets and trombones ff with the strings at 176, 182 and 190. The protected rules are unaffected: no fff, no C-major brass triad (Tpt 3 stays on B♭), and the timpani stay f (c056).

**Fix (dev.ily).** At 110, `\f` → `\ff` in devHnOne `g'1\ff`, devHnTwo `e'1\ff`, devHnThree `g'1\ff`, devHnFour `e'1\ff`, devTptOne `e''1\ff`, devTptTwo `c''1\ff`, devTptThree `bes'1\ff`, devTbnOne `c'4->\ff`, devTbnTwo `g4->\ff`, devBassTbn `c1\ff` and devTuba `c,1\ff`. Each shared staff gets equal dynamics.

### 2. 95–97: the strings carry ff into the soft link while the winds enter p on the same line (applied)

**Problem.** After the ff codetta and a half rest, the strings' E♭7 (95) has no dynamic of its own, only `\>`. So Vln I plays E♭5–D♭5 starting from ff while Ob 1 doubles it at the unison marked p, and Cl 1·2 and Bn 2 also enter p. The MIDI confirms the strings start at the codetta level (95 velocities 87–111).

In Ex. 21-17 (PDF 355) Mozart writes the new level on the first note after the crescendo. The phrase here (E♭–D♭–C, the 7th falling into V of F minor) is a relaxation, the "decrease in intensity" of PDF 348.

**Fix (clos.ily).** At 95, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb `\mf\>` (Vln I `es''1\mf\>(`). Ob 1 `es''1\mf\>(`, Cl 1 `bes'1\mf\>~`, Cl 2 `g'1\mf\>~` and Bn 2 `es1\mf\>~` all reach `\p` at 97.

### 3. 105: the new brass doubling of the B♭-minor head ends without the strings' sf (applied)

**Problem.** Since c097, Hn 3·4 (a2) and Tbn 1 double the low strings' head at 103–105. The strings end it `-.\sf` (Vla, Vc, Cb at 105, as at 101), but the horns and trombone end with a plain staccato, so the doubling parts disagree on the accent that closes the call.

**Fix (dev.ily).** At 105, devHnThree `f'4-.\sf`, devHnFour `des'4-.\sf` and devTbnOne `f4-.\sf`. Tbn 2 rests, so Tbn 1 prints alone on its staff.

### 4. 67–77: the basses are louder than the counter-melody (applied)

### 5. 108: the restruck 7th of the lament's 7–6 is not marked (applied)

**Problem.** Vln I restrikes C6 on beat 3 over the bass D (7–6 into IV6, B♭5 on beat 4) inside the p < swell. The reading says the restruck suspension is the same dissonance as a tied one (PDF 347) and should receive "something of an accent" (PDF 348). Without a mark, players treat the repeated C as a neutral repeat.

**Fix (dev.ily).** devVlnOne bar 108 `c'''2 c'''4--( bes''4)`. A tenuto inside the slur, with no new dynamic, leaves the protected lament's notes and its crescendo unchanged.
