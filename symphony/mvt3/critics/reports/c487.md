## Findings

### 1. 255–256: F2's last phrase has no woodwind in its first half. Applied.

Bars and problem: at 255–256, F2's tonic-major phrase (G3–E3–D3–C3–E3–G3) is in the violas, cellos and Hn 3·4 a2 at the unison, ff, with Vln II an octave higher. Nothing in the woodwind plays it: the whole bassoon staff, the clarinets, the bass clarinet, oboes and flutes rest at 255–256. Bn 1 joins the line only at 257, the third bar of the four-bar phrase. So the first half is strings plus brass alone, and the sound of the line changes halfway through.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 79 (D): strings and brass in unison "can never yield such a perfect blend as that produced by the union of strings and wood-wind", and "each can be heard separately". PDF 79 (E): the most natural three-group unison is "Violas (or 'Cellos) + Cl. (Eng. horn) + Horn", with "the presence of the wood-wind imparting a fuller and more evenly blended tone". PDF 76: "'Cellos + Fag." is one of the best unisons. PDF 75: "Horn + Fag.". The score already uses this formula one phrase earlier at 247–250, where the S head has violas + Cl 2 + Hn 1·2 on E4. At 255 it is missing.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-487/work/sections/finale.ily. Bn 2 has rested since 247 and enters on A2 at 257. It now plays the first half of the phrase with the violas, cellos and horns, breathes with Hn 3·4, and goes down to its A2 at 257 as before. Bn 1 takes over the line at 257 as it already did (c317), so the phrase has one bassoon in every bar, and the two players hand over at the half-phrase.

### 2. 41–42: Tbn 1's soft F1 fragment with the cellos has no wind (Rimsky PDF 75: a wind added to brass gives a finer legato; Mlada, "3 Trombones + Bass cl."). Tested in thought and declined.

At 41–42 Tbn 1 plays F3–D3–E♭3–F3 with the cellos (c431), fading to p. The candidates were the bass clarinet (resting 35–50) or Bn 2 (taken off its B♭2). Both would bring a new instrument in during the two bars that c237 thins out step by step into the transition's pp group. Bn 2 would also leave the B♭ pedal to the basses alone. Rimsky's point on legato matters less than the drop-out diminuendo here. No change.

### 3. 77–82 and 247–250: "woodwinds in octaves, only the lower part doubled by strings" (Rimsky PDF 78). Declined.

At 77–82 this would take Vln I off F2's B♭6 peak and leave Fl 1 alone on top. That reverses c011's accepted climax and gives no new reason, so it would be churn. At 247–250, putting Fl 1 an octave above the violins' G5 would take it to E7 at 247 and A7 at 252. No change.

### 4. 11–26, 151–166: "the melody simplified in the horns" (Rimsky PDF 80). Declined.

Hn 1·2 double F1 with the violas (Viola + Horn, PDF 79). Its fastest notes are paired eighths at 2 = 84, which horns play cleanly. Simplifying them would weaken the head's rhythm in the middle register and would not make the line clearer. No change.
