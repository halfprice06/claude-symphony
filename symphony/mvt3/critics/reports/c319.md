## Holistic comparison: Schumann 4, finale

Schumann's finale is reached through a slow bridge. Soft held brass, trombones included, sit under the first movement's rising figure, and the crescendo bursts into the Lebhaft. This finale does the same work at 211–223, with theme B, the German sixth, the stop chord and the C-major arrival, and it saves its brass with the same care: no trumpets before 51, no full-brass C major before 223.

Schumann ties his symphony together by running a first-movement figure through the accompaniment of the main theme. This score relies more on direct quotation: movement I at 199–204, 224–228 and 235–247, and the scherzo at 143–145 and 275–276. Its quotations are more exact than Schumann's, which a conductor would notice as a strength.

There are two clear differences:

- **The development.** Schumann's centre is a contrapuntal working (fugato). This score's centre (115–134) is lyrical: a bassoon solo, then a harp passage in G major.
- **The ending.** Schumann's coda accelerates twice, Schneller then Presto. This score broadens to Largamente.

Both were raised in c119 and are protected, and Brahms 1/iv is the model for these last pages, so I keep them.

At the level of single bars, the orchestration would pass a conductor as professional. The two faults the reading exposes are small:

- A bare fifth held by the clarinet pair in a soft diminuendo (finding 2).
- One passage where a newly added counter-line moves inside the held chord in its own colour (finding 1).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 255–258: the returning F2 phrase moves in unison with held notes in its own colour (partly applied)

**Problem.** c317 gave F2's third phrase to the cellos and Hn 3·4 (Bn 1 from 257), f, under the ff chorale. The line is an arpeggio of the chords it sounds over, so most of its notes are already held by the brass, and in the same colour:

- 255: G3 is held by Hn 1·2 a2 and Tbn 2.
- 255.4 and 256: E3 and G3 are held by Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone.
- 257: C4 for three beats, with the violas repeating C4 in quarters and Tpt 3 repeating C4.
- 258.3: E4 is held by Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1.

Only D3, C3 (256), D4 (257.4) and G4–F4–D4 (258) sound alone. The horns that carry the moving line have the same colour as the horns holding the chord around it.

**Evidence.**

- Rimsky PDF 91: "one tone quality should be devoted to the stationary and another to the moving parts".
- PDF 81: harmony needs "transparence, accuracy and purity in the movement of each part".
- Ex. 90 (PDF 265–267, Sheherazade IV) is the model for c317's own combination, cellos + two horns. There the line lies in a register that only figuration and a sustained oboe chord occupy, not inside a held brass chord.

**Fix (applied, finale.ily, `finaleVla` 257–258).** The violas play `e'4\< e' e' e' | e'4 e' e' e'` (was C4). The line's held C4 at 257 then has only Tpt 3's repeated C4 beside it.

- I avoided A3: from E4 at 256, a move to A3 over the bass E→A would make octaves with the bass.
- E4 is the chorale's own note at 257–258, so doubling it costs nothing.
- The violas rise E4→F4 into 259 against the bass falling A→F (contrary motion). Before the change they leapt C4→F4.
- par8.py over 254–260 gives the same report as v319: only the bass trombone's, bassoon's and bass clarinet's octave doublings of the bass.

**Not applied: 255–256.** The phrase's head G is the chorale's own G (c317's stated link). Moving Tbn 1·2 off E3/G3 would change the voicing of the protected last chorale statement.

**Note for the composer.** F2's notes are unchanged; only an accompanying viola part changed. The other threads need not be told.

### 2. 39–41: the clarinet pair holds a bare fifth through the staged diminuendo (applied)

**Problem.** From 38.4 to 41, Cl 1·2 hold F4/B♭3, an open fifth, for 13 beats, mf diminuendo. The same register already has six root and fifth notes on B♭3 and F4: Hn 1·2, and Hn 3·4 at 39–40. The chord's third, D, is in the middle register only in Bn 1 and the violas (D4). That is the root and fifth of the dominant of E♭ six times over, and its third twice.

**Evidence.**

- Rimsky PDF 90, rule 4: concords, not "fifths, fourths, seconds and sevenths", go to two instruments of the same kind.
- PDF 97, point 4: the wind's middle parts take "no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths", so that "open fourths and fifths will be obviated".
- PDF 66: the clarinet is "weak in the middle compass", which makes an empty fifth there weaker still.
- In Ex. 113–114 (PDF 288) and Ex. 120 (PDF 292–293) the clarinet pair holds thirds and sixths.

**Fix (applied, pTwo.ily, `pTwoClTwo` 38–41).** `c'2. d'4~ | d'1~ | d'1~\> | d'1` (was `c'2. bes4~ | bes1~ | bes1~\> | bes1`).

- The pair now holds D4/F4, a minor third. Cl 2 steps C4→D4 on 38.4, where Hn 3·4 and Bn 1 reach D4.
- At 40–41 the third is part of the V7 with the A♭ above.
- The B♭ is still in the cellos, basses, Bn 2, Hn 2 and Hn 4.
- The dynamics match Cl 1's, so the shared staff is unchanged in layout.
- 39–42 are protected for Fl 1's held A♭5 and the order of the diminuendo, and neither changes.
- par8.py over 36–43 gives the same report as v319.

### 3. The cor anglais never doubles the violas (checked, not applied)

**Observation.** The cor anglais plays only 1–2, 5–6, 147–150, 171–186 and 217–220. Rimsky names violas + cor anglais among the most natural unisons (PDF 76; Ex. 82–83, PDF 255–256, Sadko, viola melody with cor anglais under flute figuration).

**Places I checked.** The violas carry a line at 67–74 (with the cellos and Hn 1), 99–100 (with Bn 1) and 130–132 (with Hn 2).

**Decision: keep the cor anglais silent.** Each of these lines already has one wind, and PDF 76 says that "if several wind instruments play in unison with one group of strings, the latter will be overpowered". The instrument's first melody is reserved for the recapitulated F2 at 171, and its theme-B echo for 217–220. c274 kept its silence in the coda for the same reason.
