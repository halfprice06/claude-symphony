## Findings

### 1. Bars 74-76: the E♭-major consequent of F2 has no passing chord between I6 and I (applied)

**Problem.** At 75 the violins begin F2's consequent over I6 (Cb, harp and Bn 2 on G). On beat 3 the bass leaps up a sixth to E♭2, a beat before the upper voices reach I.
- 75.3 sounds E♭ under F-A♭-D, the turn's B♭ and the harp's F-D. That is V over an early tonic bass, a minor 9th D-E♭ between Hn 1 and Bn 2, resolved only on 75.4.
- The bass of 74 already falls B♭-A♭ (V-V4/2), and G at 75 continues the line. The line then stops on a leap, where the reading offers a step.
- This is the only place in the second group where the passing technique of the reading is missing. It is also the point where the theme's second phrase should move most smoothly.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 309-310 (Ex. 19-4 bar 2, Ex. 19-9) and the summary on PDF 324, point 5: the V6/4 connecting I and I6 is the most important passing 6/4. Its 4th, here the turn's B♭, is stable. The bass is the active tone, and its bass tone is often doubled.
- PDF 255-256, sec. 8: over a bass passing tone a 6/4 or 6/3 works better than a 5/3.
- PDF 256-257: a stepwise bass is an expanded passing motion that gives continuity over a longer span. With the fix, the bass runs B♭-A♭-G-F-E♭ from 74.1 to 75.4, then D-E♭ at 76. That is the antecedent's neighbour figure (E♭ | D-E♭ at 67-68) with a descent of a third leading into it.
- PDF 319-320 (Ex. 19-25) treats a bass that arrives at its goal early as a special effect for one context, not a norm. Here the early arrival produced only the D-E♭ clash.
- Corpus: Mozart 41/iv (harmony.csv onsets 20-26 and 916-924) uses the same half-bar passing V between I6 and I.

**Fix** (sections/sTwo.ily, bars 75-76 = the 9th and 10th bars of each variable):
```
sTwoCb:      g,,2 es,2 | d,2\< es,2        ->  g,,2 f,,4 es,,4 | d,,2\< es,,2
sTwoHarpL:   g,,2 es,2 | d,2 es,2          ->  g,,2 f,,4 es,,4 | d,,2 es,,2
sTwoBsnTwo:  g,2 es2 | d2 es2              ->  g2 f4 es4 | d2 es2
sTwoHnTwo:   g2\pp f4 g4 | bes2 g2         ->  g2\pp bes2~ | bes2 g2
```
- 75.3 is now F (Cb, harp, Bn 1 and Bn 2 F3), with B♭ (the turn, Hn 2), D (Hn 1, harp) and the violas' and cellos' passing A♭.
- 75.4 is E♭-G-B♭.
- Hn 2's old G3-F3 would have made octaves with the new bass G-F, so it now holds B♭3 into 76.
- The Cb's D1-E♭1 at 76 uses the extension, as the part already does at 166 and 223. analysis.txt now counts 9 notes below E1 instead of 6.
- Bn 2 now moves stepwise G3-F3-E♭3-D3-E♭3.
- F2's tune, the counter-melody, the dynamics and the harp's right hand are unchanged.
- I did not copy the change to the A♭-major statement at 195. That bar is a different chord (minor I6 over C♭, by mixture), and its scoring is protected (187-198 as of v225).

### 2. Bars 89-90: the exposition's structural cadence has no cadential 6/4 (considered, not applied)

**What the reading offers.** The closing group reaches its V7-I at 89.3-90.1 from ii4/3 over C. An accented 6/4 on 89.3 (B♭ under E♭-G) would delay the arrival, as PDF 307 describes.

**Why I reject it.** On 89.3 Fl 1 plays D6-C6, the start of critic 377's wind canon. D6 against the 6/4's E♭-G makes a second, and the canon leads straight through the cadence. The cadence is already prepared by the 6/3 bridge at 87-88 and the stepwise bass G-F-E♭-D-C-B♭. A 6/4 here would add a second delaying device that the canon cannot carry.

**Decision.** No change.

### 3. Long dominants at 39-42 and 147-150: upper-5th expansion or V7-6/4-V4/3 (considered, not applied)

**What the reading offers.** PDF 253 (16-5) shows a V expanded through its upper 5th. PDF 312-313 (19-14) shows a passing 6/4 over a held dominant.

**Why I reject them.**
- At 39-42 the protected Fl 1 A♭5 is the 7th of V. Any 6/4 over B♭ would put G under it.
- 147-150 is protected (135-171; c313 rejected a neighbouring i6/4 there). Movement in its upper voices would also anticipate the accented i6/4 at 151.

**Decision.** No change.
