## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Development climax 110–113: one rhythmic layer, no motive (applied)
Bars: 110–113 (approach 107–109).
Problem: after the lament (107–109, half notes), the ff climax has every part in whole and half notes: melody G5–A♭5–F5–E5–F5, inner voices, brass and bass all move together. Only the timpani give any rhythm. The development's two ideas (F1's head and the scale figure, 99–106) are both absent at its high point. The melody line is new and does not come from either idea.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 11 says to vary a motive already used before bringing in a new one, so the piece grows from its germ. PDF 14 describes primary, secondary and tertiary layers. Here all three collapse into one. Ex. 2A (PDF 18) fills the melody's held notes with other lines. Dvořák 9/iv's development climax keeps a moving figure under the held melody.
Fix (applied): violas and cellos (ff, in octaves) play the scale figure under the held V chord at 110 and again under V at 112 (beats 3–4). The cellos come back to the bass at 111.
```
devVla 110–113: g4\ff c'8( bes aes g f g) | f8( g aes bes aes2) | g2 c'8( bes aes g) | aes2-> r2
devVc  110–113: c'4\ff c'8( bes aes g f g) | f2 des2 | bes,2 c8( bes, aes, g,) | f,2-> r2
```
The figure ends F–G rather than F–E so that it does not add another octave to the leading tone, which Ob 2, Tpt 1 and Hn 2 already resolve E→F at 111. The basses, tuba, contrabassoon and bass trombone keep the C pedal at 110. Result: the climax combines the development's motive with the cadence line. No new fifths or octaves (par.py; the remaining flags at 111–112 are the cellos' octave doubling of the bass).

### 2. Closing bridge 87–90: Vln II's low octave runs through the 6/3 series, and A♮ sounds against A♭ (applied)
Bars: 89–90.
Problem: at 89 the scale figure is a fifth lower. Vln II's octave (F4 down to A3, then B♭3–F4) moves straight through the 6/3 voices (Vla F3–B♭3, E♭3–A♭3; Bn 1 A♭3), so the parallel 6/3 series that cycle 53 brought in can no longer be heard as a line. On beat 4 of 89 both violins (A4, A3) and Fl 1 (A4) play A♮ while Bn 1 holds A♭3, the only seventh of V7. That is an augmented unison in the same octave.
Evidence: Sebesky Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) cut a line in the lower-middle register. The remaining voices' contrary motion "became more evident" and the primary element came forward. The same applies here, with the 6/3 series as the voices to clear. The economy rule (PDF 12) is the same point.
Fix (applied): Vln II plays Vln I's notes at 89–90, and the figure's lower neighbour becomes diatonic A♭ in all three parts (a tonal sequence of 87, which uses only E♭-major notes):
```
closVlnOne/closVlnTwo 89–90: r4 f''8( es'' d'' c'') bes'( aes' | bes'8 c'' d'' es'') f''4-> f'4->
closFluteOne 89: r4 f''8( es'' d'' c'') bes'( aes' |
```
At 87–88 Vln II keeps its lower octave, which stays above the violas.

### 3. Clarinet pad 67–74 and 187–194 repeats 51–58 note for note (applied)
Bars: 67–74 (and 187–194 in the recapitulation).
Problem: Cl 1 holds E♭4–D4–E♭4 at 67–74 with exactly the notes it held under the trumpet solo at 51–58. In the recapitulation the clarinets hold C4/E♭3–G3 from 171 to 198 without a break. At 67 the harp's eighths (E♭4–F5), Vln II's lower octave of F2 (E♭4–F5) and the violas' and cellos' counter-line (G3–E♭4) already fill that register. The pp clarinet notes add nothing that is not already sounding, and they thicken the middle under the tune and the counter-melody.
Evidence: economy (PDF 12: "every note … making a legitimate difference"), focus (PDF 13: the background must not obscure the primary element) and timbre variety (PDF 15: change the combination). Cycle 47 already removed the clarinets at 75–82 for a related reason.
Fix (applied): `sTwoClOne = { R1*16 | }` and `sTwoClTwo = { R1*16 | }`. In recS, Cl 1·2 rest 187–194 (`R1*8`) and come back pp at 195. The pad at 67–74 is now bassoons and harp. The colour change from minor (clarinets) to major (harp, bassoons, counter-melody) is also clearer.

### 4. First group 19–26: accompaniment identical to 11–18; strings without inner parts (applied)
Bars: 19–26.
Problem: in 11–26 all the upper strings play the tune (Vln I·II, and violas an octave lower). The only inner parts are the wind figures: clarinet broken chords, and off-beats in Hn 3·4 and Bn 1. In the consequent, 19–22 repeat 11–14's clarinet bars note for note. The accompaniment is more than block chords, but it is the same eight bars twice, and the strings never take part in it.
Evidence: timbre variety (PDF 15) and harmonic variety (PDF 19). Formal balance (PDF 11) says to distribute colours rather than repeat them.
Fix (applied): at 19 the violas leave the tune and take Cl 2's broken-chord figure (mf). Cl 2 rests 19–26. Hn 1·2 alone carry the tune's lower octave and are marked f at 19. In 20, 23, 24 and 25 the figure's notes are changed to avoid octaves and fifths that Cl 2 had (par.py: 9 parallels removed at 20, 23, 24, including the fifths at 20 and 24):

### 5. Second group bass: independent where it matters (no change)
Bars: 51–66, 67–82, 171–198.
At 51–66 the pizz. bass on the beat is the protected accompaniment of the trumpet solo (cycles 4 and 7), and it is right for a soft solo (PDF 13, focus). At 67–82 the basses, Bn 2 and the harp's left hand double one root bass in whole and half notes. Its second phrase (75) already has a different bass from 67 (G–E♭ against E♭), so Sebesky's harmonic variety (PDF 19) is partly met. No change. The tripled root bass is not a fault, because the counter-melody keeps the octave above it free.
