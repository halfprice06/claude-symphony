## What else I studied

I counted the bowing of every string part through all of v318, bar by bar. I followed the slurs and took the players' habits from PDF 38: a downbeat after a rest is retaken down-bow, and an upbeat after a rest is taken up-bow. I then compared the result with the bow signs the score already prints. The signs that earlier critics placed (c018, c118, c175, c186, c218) agree with the count everywhere except the places below.

Several of these errors show up as conflicts with the score's own signs. At 149 the score marks `\dnup` (down, then up), which assumes 147 is a down-bow, but the count gives an up-bow at 147. At 263 the score marks `\dnup` in the cellos, which needs an up-bow at 262, but the count gives a down-bow at 262.

I checked HISTORY.md for earlier bowing passes (c085, c096, c118, c175, c186, c218, c265, c276) so as not to repeat them.

Corpus: the Mutopia editions of Dvořák 9/iv and Coriolan print no bow signs. Dvořák 9/iv has no string trill in its finale (`strings.notesF.ily`), so I do not add one here.

Theme record: `themecheck.py themes-finale-quotes.ly` gives 41/41 unchanged on my scratch copy. The scherzo's theme changes c321 and c331 affect only the trio tune, which the finale does not quote. Every finding below changes bow signs only, plus one slur in the violas at 198. The scratch build's MIDI is byte-identical to v318.

## Findings

### 1. 259-263: the cellos cannot reach the cadence's down-bow, and the violins have no regroup sign (applied)
Problem. 262→263 is the symphony's only root-position V–I in C. The cellos hold F2 for two bars, ff < (259-260), on one bow. At 2 = 88 that is eight beats, 2.7 s, and by the count it is a down-bow. The count continues: A♭ at 261 is up and G at 262 is down. The score then marks `c1\fff\dnup~` at 263, which starts with a second down-bow straight after 262's down-bow, with no rest between them. The violins hold the chorale top line under "change bow at will" from 255, so their bows are scattered when the fff C arrives at 263, and nothing tells them to regroup.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38-39 says the cello bow is shorter, that a crescendo belongs on the up-bow, and that loud notes need fewer beats per bow. Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus) prescribes down-bow then up-bow over the long note before a ff attack. PDF 40 is the source of the staggered bowing that "change bow at will" stands for. The staggered section needs a common stroke where the line ends.
Fix (finale.ily). Vc `f,1\ff\<\dnup~ | f,1 | aes,1 | g,1 | c1\fff\dnup~`. The bow changes to an up-bow inside the held F, the crescendo grows toward the frog, 261 is a down-bow, 262 an up-bow, and the existing `\dnup` at 263 now works. Vln I `c'''4\fff\downbow g''8(`. The basses (eighths) and the second violins and violas (even groups) already arrive down-bow.

### 2. 265-272: the final combination's accented heads fall on up-bows (applied)
Problem. In 263-264 the violas and cellos come out on an up-bow. Their F1 head at 265 is a down-bow, but the sequence head E–B–C–D at 267 (`e'2->(`) follows the staccato E at 266.4 with no rest, so it falls on an up-bow. In Vln I, the slur `e'''8( d'''8 c'''4 b''4)` at 268 is a down-bow, so the F1 head `c'''2->(` at 269 is an up-bow. The pickups into the movement-I head at 267 (Vln I-II) and at 269 and 271 (Vla, Vc) come after rests, where players take an upbeat up-bow. At 247 and 199 the composer marks these pickups `\downbow` so that the accent lands down-bow, but here they are unmarked.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38 on the upbeat habit and on hooked bowing (Ex. II-19). PDF 42-43 and Ex. II-29 on heavy attacks as down-bows at the frog. This passage repeats the bowing that c186 and c218 set at 247-254 and 199-204.
Fix (finale.ily). Vln I-II 267 `r8 g''8\downbow( e'''4.->)`. Vln I 269 `c'''2->-\tweak avoid-slur #'ignore \downbow( g''4. a''8`. Vla and Vc 267 `e'2->-\tweak avoid-slur #'ignore \downbow( b4. c'8`. Vla 269 and 271 `r8 g8_\downbow( e'4.->)`. Vc 269 and 271 `r8 g,8\downbow( e4.->)`.
The tweak is needed. With score.ly's c125 `stem-side-y-offset` callback, a second script on the first note of a slur on a half note makes LilyPond drop the slur silently. The build gives no warning; I found it on the page image and reproduced it in a test file. `avoid-slur #'ignore` keeps the slur, and the sign prints above it.

### 3. 198→199: the crescendo into the hammered tutti ends at the tip (applied)
Problem. Vln I-II play `es'''1\f\<\downbow`, a whole-bar crescendo on a down-bow, then `r8 g''8\downbow(` at 199. The players must carry the bow from the tip back to the frog within an eighth rest (0.18 s) at the moment of the owed hammered tutti. The violas have the same problem: by the count, their slur `es2( ces'2)` is a down-bow, followed by 199's down-bow pickup.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-20 describe exactly this case: mark down then up over the long note, so that the bow is "ready to give a ff attack". A crescendo belongs on the up-bow (PDF 38).
Fix (recS.ily). Vln I `es'''1\f\<\dnup`, Vln II `es''1\f\<\dnup`, Vla `es2\f\< ces'2\upbow` (the slur is removed). This keeps c118's down-bow attack on the peak E♭, which was the reason for protecting the 187-198 bow plan. Only the second half of the note changes direction.

### 4. 147: the ff dominant arrives on an up-bow (applied)
Problem. At 145-146 Vln I-II play F1's head and end on the staccato B at 146.4, which is a down-bow. With no rest before it, the ff V6/5 at 147 is an up-bow. The composer's `\dnup` at 149 assumes 147 is a down-bow and 148 an up-bow; as written, 148 and 149 are two down-bows on legato whole notes.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-19 (hook, or mark the down-bow). PDF 42-43 and Ex. II-29 (heavy attacks at the frog).
Fix (retr.ily). `<f'' d'''>1->\ff\downbow` (Vln I), `<b' g''>1->\ff\downbow` (Vln II). The violas and cellos are already right.

### 5. 110: two string parts reach the development's ff climax on up-bows (applied)
Problem. Vln I, the cellos and the basses arrive at 110 down-bow. Vln II (after `f''2.( e''4)`) and the violas (after `aes4 g4`) arrive up-bow.
Evidence. As for finding 4 (PDF 38, 42-43; Ex. II-29).
Fix (dev.ily). Vln II `g''1\ff\downbow~`, Vla `g4\ff\downbow c'8(`. This places a lift before the ff. No note or dynamic changes, so the protected lament 107-110 is unaffected.

### 6. 101, 105: the development's sf chords come on up-bows (applied)
Problem. The F1 head in the violas, cellos and basses ends `des'8( c'8 bes4-.) aes4-.`. The staccato A♭ is a down-bow, so the sf chord on the next downbeat, one of the score's seven strong marks (c008), is an up-bow. At 105 the violins' run ends `f'''4-> f''4->`, the gesture that c218 bowed as two down-bows at 90. Here the second F is an up-bow.
Evidence. Adler PDF 45 (martelé, toward the frog), PDF 42-43 and Ex. II-29 (series of down-bows), Ex. II-20.
Fix (dev.ily). `<aes c'>4-.\sf\downbow`, `<des' f'>4-.\sf\downbow` (Vla). `<f c'>4-.\sf\downbow`, `<bes f'>4-.\sf\downbow` (Vc). `f,4-.\sf\downbow`, `bes,,4-.\sf\downbow` (Cb). `f'''4->\downbow f''4->\downbow` (Vln I) and `f''4->\downbow f'4->\downbow` (Vln II) at 105.

## Considered, not applied
- **Techniques in the reading that the score does not use.** Punta d'arco, the Weber dotted figure at the tip, spiccato, jeté and arpeggiando all need a soft detached or bouncing string figure, and the score has none outside protected passages (51-66, 115-130, 171-186 are pizzicato or protected). Fingered and undulating tremolo are allowed only from 223, and the free bars after 223 are protected (223-234, 235-242, 243-246) or already have tremolo. Sul ponticello and col legno do not fit the Brahms–Dvořák idiom. String trills: the models' finales do not use them (Dvořák 9/iv has none), and the place where one would make sense (221-222) is protected. So this pass applies the reading's bow-direction rules (PDF 38, Ex. II-19, II-20, II-29), which the score uses but not everywhere it needs them.
- **277-278.** The quarter rests before 277 and 278 let the players retake down-bow by habit, so marking them would be overmarking (PDF 38).
- **27-34.** The basses play F1's five-beat ff slur, which is long for a bass bow at ff (PDF 39). This is protected (c007), and the cellos, violas and tuba carry the line, so I made no change.
- **145, violas.** The head falls on an up-bow by strict count. The held notes from 139 to 144 leave the players free to plan their bows, and the stretto is protected.
