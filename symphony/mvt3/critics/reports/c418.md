## Findings

### 1. 209–210: the cellos play 12 eighths on one bow at f <, and the accented G♭ at 210.3 comes on an up-bow in Vln I and the cellos (applied)

**Problem.** c407 added the cellos an octave below Vln II on the last rising run into the Largamente. Its report says "with the same slurs and hairpin", but the part as written has one slur over all twelve eighths (`aes,8(\< bes, c des es f ges aes | bes8 c' des' es')`), while Vln II slurs 4 + 8. At about 2 = 92 those twelve eighths take two seconds of bow at f with a crescendo, so the cellos have the least bow exactly where the crescendo has to grow most. They also arrive up-bow on the accented G♭4 at 210.3. Vln I has the same up-bow problem for a different reason. It plays straight on from the staccato D♭ at 208.4 (down-bow), so its first group at 209 is up, its eight-note group is down, and the accented G♭6 at 210.3, which is tied into the ff at 211 and has its diminuendo there, comes up-bow. Vln II, which enters after a rest, has the opposite bowing. So at the run's goal the three string lines arrive in two different bow directions.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38–39 and Ex. II-21/II-22: at forte, Mendelssohn puts six notes on a bow where the piano passage had many more. PDF 38: strong attacks belong on a down-bow, and the composer marks a direction where it counters the players' habit. With 4 + 8 in all three lines, the accent at 210.3 is down-bow in each, and the tie into 211 carries the ff and its diminuendo (G♭–F, the 4–3) on that same down-bow, which suits a diminuendo (PDF 38).

**Fix (applied, codaOne.ily).**
- Cellos: `aes,8(\< bes, c des) es( f ges aes | \clef tenor bes8 c' des' es') ges'4->`
- Vln I, 209: `aes'8(\<\downbow bes' c'' des'')` (a lifted retake after the staccato quarter at 208.4)

No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. This finishes what c407 intended and does not reverse it. The protection on 205–210 (c224) covers the scoring, which is unchanged.

### 2. 113.1 and 132.1: the lower strings arrive up-bow on the accented goal chords (applied)

**Problem.** In two places a phrase ends on an accented arrival chord, and my bow simulation shows the lower strings reaching it up-bow while the violins reach it down-bow:
- 113, the F-minor goal chord of the climax (`aes2->`, `f,,2->`). The violas come from a down-bow slur `c'8( bes aes g)`, and the basses from `bes,,2 c,2` on alternate bows. The cellos and violins already arrive down-bow.
- 132, the G-major cadence chord. The violas come from the staccato F♯ at 131.4 (down-bow); the cellos and basses come from `d2 d2`. The violins arrive down-bow after their up-bow run.

c318 marked the down-bows at 101, 105, 110 and 147 for this reason. These two arrivals were left out.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38 (Ex. II-20): when two strong attacks follow each other, the second must still be a solid down-bow. PDF 42–43 (Ex. II-29): heavy chords are taken as down-bows from the frog, with the bow lifted in between. PDF 38: mark only where the habit has to be countered. These are the only two arrivals where the habit gives an up-bow.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily, signs only).** `aes2->\downbow` (violas, 113), `f,,2->\downbow` (basses, 113); `<d' b'>4->\downbow` (violas, 132), `g,4->\downbow` (cellos, 132), `g,,4->\downbow` (basses, 132). Before each there is a quarter or half note to lift from. 107–113 and 130–132 are protected for notes and scoring (c248's list, c154). Bow signs touch neither, as with c318's marks at 110.

### 3. Tested and kept: violins' eight-note bows at f, 205–206 and 209–210

The violins put 8 eighths on one bow (about 1.3 s) at f, more than Adler's six (PDF 39). I ran a 4 + 4 + 4 split through the bow simulation (it was not built, because it changes no notes or MIDI). That split puts the accented F6 at 206.3 and G♭6 at 210.3 on up-bows, which is the fault fixed in Finding 1. Eight eighths in 1.3 s is within an orchestral violinist's f bow. Not changed.

### 4. Checked and kept: Vln II and violas 43–50, tied pp holds without "change bow at will"

Vln II holds F4 and the violas D4 for 3½ bars (about 5 s at pp). Vln I, the cellos and the basses carry "change bow at will" there. Adler warns against overloading the bow in slow passages (PDF 39), but at pp a 5-second bow is normal, and the bow changes fall where the pitch moves (46, 47, 49). Adding the text would add two more labels to a page that already has many. Not changed.

### 5. Checked and kept: 219–222, one bow per bar under a four-bar crescendo

Two of the four whole-note bows go down under the crescendo (PDF 38). Each is only about 2 s long, the bow changes coincide with the harmony changes, and the bars are protected (222→223). Not changed.
