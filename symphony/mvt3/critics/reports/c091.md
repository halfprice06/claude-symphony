## Findings

### 1. Bar 253: the two heads of movement I collapse into octaves (applied)

**Problem.** At 247–254 the P head (Fl 1·2, Vln I·II, and three octaves lower Tbn 1·2, Bn 1, Vc) is combined with the S head (Ob, Cl, Hn 1·2, Vla). This combination is owed item 6, and it is the finale's main display of the two themes together. In every other bar of the eight, the two lines take turns: one holds while the other moves. At 253 both play `f4. e8 d4`, the same rhythm and the same pitches, in four octaves (F6, F5, F4, F3). For a beat and a half the combination becomes a unison and stops being two themes.
- allpairs_par.py lists 322 parallel-octave pairs at 253 in v91, and none in 247–252.
- 253 is the seventh bar of the phrase, just before the cadence bar, which is where the counterpoint should be most active.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 336, §9, Ex. 20-16: independence of the parts comes from figuration in different voices, "rhythms in different voices that combine into a continuous movement". At 253 the combined rhythm is only `4. 8 4 4`, and both lines stop together.
- A&S PDF 341: unisons and octaves caused by figuration "you should avoid".
- The finale's own 247–252 already follow the right model. At 247 the P line moves on the off-beats while the S head holds E. At 249 the P line follows the S head's F–E–D an eighth late.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-091/work/sections/finale.ily, bar 253 only).** The P line takes its own head rhythm (8 8 4. 8 8 8), so its moves fall where the S head holds. The low doubling holds F under the S head's descent.
- finaleFluteOne, finaleFluteTwo, finaleVlnOne, finaleVlnTwo: `f'''4.-> e'''8 d'''4 g'''4 |` becomes `f'''8( g'''8 a'''4.->) g'''8( f'''8 e'''8) |`
- finaleTbnOne, finaleTbnTwo, finaleBsnOne, finaleVc: `f4.-> e8 d4 g4 |` becomes `d8( e8 f2.->) |`

Result:
- Attacks now fall on every eighth across the two lines.
- The intervals with the S head are a 3rd, a 5th, a 6th and a 3rd, and the only octave is the shared downbeat F.
- The low line moves in parallel 10ths with the upper P line (A&S PDF 336).
- The upper line ends E6 → G6 and the low line F3 → G3 into 254, which is unchanged.

My first version gave the low line `d8 e8 f4. e8 d8 c8`. p5.py found fifths E3/B4 → D3/A4 against Ob 2, which A&S PDF 341 rules out because they come from consonant passing motion. The held F removes them.

### 2. 275–276: where the scherzo quotation sits in the bar (checked, no change)

When theme A's head is rebarred from 3/4 into 2/2, the quotation's long note E falls on beat 4, tied over the barline. I looked at starting the quotation one beat later (`r4 c4 g8 g8 c4 | e2 d8 des8 c4`), which would put E on the downbeat as in the scherzo. I kept the present placement, for three reasons:
- A&S PDF 337 (Ex. 20-18) reads a figurated line from its chord tones on the strong beats. Now the head's first C is on 275's downbeat and its last C is on beat 3 of 276; both are strong beats. The later start would put the last C on beat 4 of 276, straight into the hammered C at 277 with no rest between.
- The chain's E5 arrives on 275's downbeat together with the head's C. finale.md describes this hand-over, and the record checks it (36/36).
- The eighths at 276 sound at quarter = 144, which is slower than the scherzo's tempo and easy to hear.

### 3. 265–270: B♭ in F1's major form (checked, no change)

The C-major F1 keeps B♭ (`bes'4 a'8 g'8`) on its way down and uses B♮ on its way up at 271 (G–A–B–D). This matches A&S PDF 340 (§15): the form of the sixth and seventh degrees follows the direction of the line unless the harmony decides otherwise. The B♭ also fits the music around it:
- On the downbeat it is an accented passing tone over IV6/4 (PDF 336).
- The motto figure in the violins ends on C on the same downbeat, which would clash with a B♮.

### 4. 271: the V7 arpeggio over the tonic-pedal figure (checked, no change)

At 271 the melody arpeggiates V. Underneath, Hn 3·4, the trombones, Bn 1, violas and cellos play G–E–D–C–B–C over the C pedal, so B4 sounds against E4 at mid-bar and D5 against C4 at beat 3. Both clashes are weak-beat passing dissonances over a pedal. The melody's seventh, F, resolves to E at 272 beat 3, as A&S PDF 337 (Ex. 20-18) requires. No change.
