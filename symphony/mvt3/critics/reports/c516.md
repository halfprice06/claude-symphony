## Findings

### 1. 251-258: the tuba plays 8 bars ff with no rest or breath, down to F1 (applied)

**Problem.** In the Più allegro (2 = 88, 1.36 s a bar) the tuba plays `e,2 c,2 | f,,1 | d,1 | g,,1 | e,1\ff~ | e,1 | a,,1~ | a,,1 \breathe`. That is 8 bars with no break, about 11 seconds at ff. It includes a whole-note F1 at 252 (the bottom of Adler's tuba chart, adler2 PDF 306) and two two-bar holds (E2, A1) at 255-258. My span scan shows this is the longest unbroken passage for any low brass part in the movement. No other brass player has anything like it here. The bass trombone rests 247-254. Tbn 1·2 rest on 254.4. The horns breathe at 250 and 254. Tpt 1·2 rest 251-254. The contrabassoon, which doubles the tuba, rests 255-256. Adler says the tuba needs more breath than any other brass instrument and recommends frequent rests (adler2 PDF 8), so in practice the player would have to snatch a breath somewhere in the chorale's first held note. The natural place is 254→255, where the S head ends, the chorale's last statement starts (letter S) and the bass trombone enters on the same E2.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-516/work/sections/finale.ily, with a c516 comment).**
```
finaleTuba = { c,1\ff | d,1 | f,,1 | g,,1 \breathe | e,2 c,2 | f,,1 | d,1 | g,,1 \breathe | e,1\ff~ | e,1 | a,,1~ | a,,1 \breathe | ...
```
This adds one `\breathe` after 254. The tuba's ff at 255 already restates the dynamic, and 255 is already a new attack. Spans are now 5.5 s, 5.4 s, 5.4 s and 7.2 s. Notes, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. The bass at 255.1 still has the bass trombone, contrabassoon (from 257), cellos and basses. This change is new and undoes nothing.

### 2. Range extremes against the chart (checked, no change)

I compared every part with the professional and nonprofessional columns. Every note is inside the professional column, except the accepted E♭1 in the tuba (166, held) and the double basses' C-extension notes, which the header names. The highest notes appear only where the music peaks: C7 in Fl 1 and Vln I at the arrival (223), Tpt 1 C6 at 243-250 (kept by c426: rests before and after, approached by step), and Hn 1 written A5 at 141-147, which is protected (141-142 since c940) and reached under the woodwind motto. The low soft notes sit in good registers: clarinet chalumeau pp at 171-193, Bn 2 B♭1 pp at 191 (c216), and the cor anglais A♭3 f at 217. The double basses' written G4 (sounding G3) at 34 is inside the professional range, in the protected bass statement 27-34.

### 3. Terms of PDF 312 (checked, no change)

The score already uses almost every term on the list. The unused ones are col legno, ponticello, campane in aria, près de la table, cuivré and punta d'arco. Earlier cycles declined al tallone (c456) and la metà (c355, c366); c499's "last 2 desks" now does what la metà does. I looked for a place where punta d'arco would help, meaning soft, short, detached string notes. The strings have no such passage: every p/pp string line is slurred, held, pizzicato or tremolo. So it would only add a word. The other unused terms belong to a later style than the Brahms-Dvořák models.

### 4. Timpani, harp and percussion (checked, no change)

Every timpani pitch and muta fits its drum in the PDF 307 chart (G on 38", A♭/B♭ on 28", C/D♭ on 25", E♭ on 23"). The harp's lowest notes (D1, E♭1, F1 at 67-82) fit the pedal diagram, and the D1 string's tuning is marked at 43. Each pedal change between harp entries (82→123, 131→235, 242→277) has at least 40 bars of rest. The two simultaneous crash-and-bass-drum strokes (223, 278) match the header's two players.
