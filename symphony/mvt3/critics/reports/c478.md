## Findings

### 1. 95–97: the oboe's motto tail breaks its slur before the resolution (applied)

- **Problem.** Ob 1 plays `es''2.\mf\>( d''4 | des''1) | c''1\p`. The slur ends on D♭5, so the resolving C5 at 97.1 is tongued again (Adler PDF 187: written-out notes are tongued, slurred ones are legato on one breath). A re-attacked note on the resolution of a chromatic sigh puts an accent where the line should relax, and it falls exactly where the diminuendo should end.
- **Second problem.** Two beats later Cl 1 enters pp dolce on the same C5 to take the note over (c341's dovetail, the Brahms 1/iv model of PDF 188). The listener then hears two attacks on C5 within one bar. The oboe's attack is the louder one, and the dovetail is meant to hide the hand-over.
- **Evidence.**
  - The Schumann oboe solo (PDF 199, Ex. VII-34, "p cantabile") keeps the slur through its fp and dim. to the phrase's last note.
  - The cor anglais solos on PDF 205–206 (Ex. VII-48 to VII-50) slur the resolving step.
  - The source of this quotation, movement II's last tail (Cl 1, II 85–90; coherence/themes-mvt2.ly `lastTail`), slurs D♭5 into C5.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-478/work/sections/clos.ily, closOboeOne):**
  `es''2.\mf\>(^\markup \italic "solo, espress." d''4 | des''1 | c''1\p) | R1 |`
  The slur now closes on the C5. Notes, dynamics, the hairpin, the Cl 1 hand-over and the violas' E♭4→E4 are unchanged.
- **Breath.** The phrase is three bars at 2 = 84, about 4.3 seconds, which is easy on one breath (PDF 198).
- **Quotation note.** This touches the articulation of the finale's quotation of movement II's `lastTail` (IV 95–97). The pitches and rhythm are unchanged, so the theme record is not affected. The other threads only need to know that the finale's slur now matches movement II's D♭–C slur.

### 2. 119–121: per-figure swell in the bassoon's second phrase (tested, not applied)

Following Ex. VII-93 (PDF 227), I tried a < > on Bn 1's C4–E♭4 at 119 so that the consequent also gets a shape. I did not apply it. In all four F2 statements (55–58, 71–74, 119–122, 175–178) only the first phrase swells and the second phrase falls back unshaded. This is a consistent shape across the movement, and one added hairpin would break it. The solo is already marked p < mp > p at 115–118 and > at 122, and its pp pizzicato accompaniment meets PDF 226.
