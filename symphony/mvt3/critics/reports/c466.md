## Findings

### 1. Bars 8–10: the violas' new answer ends on a held, crescendoing open D (applied)

**Problem.** Since c437, the violas enter at 8 with `g'2(\mf d'4. es'8) | d'1~\<\upbow | d'2 d'4->\f`, answering the cellos' F1-head figure. The E♭4 falls to D4, which they hold for six beats, mf to the f accent on 10.3. In first position, E♭4 is the first finger on the D string, so D4 is the open D string. The phrase's longest note and its crescendo therefore land on the one pitch the player cannot vibrate.

**Evidence.** This is Adler's case. Open strings "are not under the controlling influence provided by the finger which oscillates a stopped note" and "stand out peculiarly" where "one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture" (PDF 73–74, Ex. III-4). The held note is also an expressive 6–5 resolution over V, not a passing note in a fast run. c286 listed this D4 under "ordinary fingering avoids them", but that check predates c437, which turned the D4 into the goal of a phrase. The viola G string "can also be exploited for its dark quality" (PDF 87, Ex. III-31). The figure is the cellos' answer two octaves up, so the darker string matches the imitation. c391 set the precedent of a sul-string mark rather than a note change.

**Fix (applied).** In intro.ily, mark the phrase sul G from 8.1 to the accent on 10.3. On the G string in 4th position (D4 first finger, E♭4 second, G4 fourth) the whole phrase lies under one hand:

```
R1 | \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" g'2(\mf\downbow^\markup \italic "unis."\startTextSpan d'4. es'8) | d'1~\<\upbow | d'2 d'4->\f\stopTextSpan r4 | }
```

Notes, bowing and MIDI are unchanged. Hn 2 still holds D4 p< under it.

### 2. Bars 133–134: the violas' D4 diminuendo to p sits on the open D (applied)

**Problem.** The development ends with `<d' b'>4-> r4 r2 | d'1\>~ | d'2.\p r4`. Every other string fades on a stopped note: Vln I G5, Vln II D5 to B4, cellos F3, basses F2. The violas fade on their open D, just before the pp sul tasto retransition at 135.

**Evidence.** This is the same rule (PDF 73–74). It is also the situation c391 fixed at 41–42 and 169.4–170, "open strings at soft fades".

**Fix (applied).** In dev.ily, mark sul G for the held D4. After the rest in 132 it is the fourth finger in first position on the G string:

```
<d' b'>4->\downbow r4 r2 | \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" d'1\>~\startTextSpan | d'2.\p\stopTextSpan r4 | }
```

The engraving is checked: page 7, the line runs over 133–134 and clears the "sul tasto" texts at 135.

### 3. Bars 255–256: open strings in the violas' and cellos' F2 statement (tested, declined)

**Candidate.** The violas (G3 open, C3 open) and cellos (D3 open) touch open strings in F2's last statement, where c376 put Vln II sul G. I considered "sul C" for the violas and a G-string fingering for the cellos.

**Decision: declined.**
- Adler limits the warning to slow, expressive passages (PDF 73), and this is ff under the brass chorale.
- The violas' C3 is the open C string whatever is marked.
- A one-string mark would need three string changes in four bars (C, G, D strings) for a line of G3–G4.
- Adler adds that the open string "lends itself to great exploitation" (PDF 74), and at ff its ring helps the statement carry.

### 4. Bars 115–122: senza basso for the pizz. bass under the 4 soli (considered, declined)

**Candidate.** Adler PDF 106: older scores wrote "senza basso" for a lighter bass line, and a bass alone sounds "thin and distant" (PDF 105).

**Decision: declined.** The basses here double the cellos' pp pizz. an octave down on the beats. Without them, the soft passage loses the 16-foot bass the rest of the development has, and nothing is muddied: they are plucked crotchets, not a fast arco line (Ex. III-68). The cyclic review keeps 51–66 and the 171–186 bass as they are.
