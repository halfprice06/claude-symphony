## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 263–264: the scale figure at fff carries six eighths across the barline in one bow (applied)
Vln I, Vln II and violas play `g8( f e d) c8( b | c d e f) g4-> g4->`: the second slur starts on the last beat of 263, holds six eighths, and runs through 264's downbeat. Adler PDF 38–39: a slur is one bow, and how many notes fit in it depends on dynamic: Mendelssohn's violas at forte take six (Ex. II-22), and here the dynamic is fff against full brass and timpani. At fff, six notes in one bow cannot be played as loud as the separate notes around them, and the barline falls in the middle of a stroke. The figure at 3–4 is f, where six notes still fit, so I leave that one. Fix (finale.ily, three parts): `c''8( b'8) | c''8( d''8 e''8 f''8)` in Vln I, `c'8( b8) | c'8( d'8 e'8 f'8)` in Vln II and violas. Bowing is then 4 + 2 + 4 + separate accents, and 264 starts with a new bow. No change in pitch or rhythm.

### 2. 87–94: violas' two-voice suspension line has no "div.", and the hammered chords after it have no "non div." (applied)
At 87–90 the violas carry a two-voice chain (`<bes es'~>2 <aes es'>4 <aes d'~>4 | <g d'>4 <g c'>4 ...`) with ties and suspensions, written as chords with no mark. Adler PDF 30: if *divisi* does not appear, the player is right to play the chord as a double-stop, so every viola would have to hold tied double-stops with fifths stopped by one finger (A♭3–E♭4, E♭3–B♭3) through a legato mf line. The divided reading is clearly intended. At 91–94 the ff hammered chords (as in the violins) are meant as double-stops for weight, and Adler gives *non div.* for that (PDF 30, Ex. II-12). Fix (clos.ily): `<bes es'~>2\mf^\markup \italic "div."` at 87 and `<es' bes'>4->\ff^\markup \italic "non div."` at 91. 95 is a single note, so no "unis." is needed after "non div.".

### 3. 223 and 277–279: cellos' C3–G3 chords are the only string chords there without a divisi mark (applied)

II-9 shows which cello double-stops work); a stopped fifth is the double-stop most exposed in intonation, in a tremolo at the arrival and in the held final chord. ### 4. 114→115: violas hold arco to the barline and must play pizz. a quarter later (applied)
Violas play `a1\p\>` through 114 and enter "pizz. div." on beat 2 of 115, which leaves one quarter (0.36 s at 2 = 84) at the tip of a down-bow diminuendo. Fix (dev.ily): `a2.\p\> r4\!`, which gives the violas half a bar. The diminuendo still ends p before the pp entry.

### 5. 103–104: the violins' run puts its accented arrival note inside the slur (applied)
