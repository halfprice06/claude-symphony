## What else I studied

Movement I v221: its climaxes are marked fff (`\fff` 28 times in dev.ily, e.g. its line 30 `aes'''1\fff`, and 28 times in recTwo.ily). Movement III v08 never goes above ff and has no sf, sfz, subito or fff anywhere (one `\fp` at 101); its loudest moments, the arrival at 219 and the first root-position V7–I at 254–255, are therefore softer on paper than movement I's peaks. Rimsky Ex. 256 (*Tsar's Bride* 102, PDF 419) shows the staggered "pp cresc. poco" by which he enters each group in turn.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The C-major arrival has no dynamic preparation (205–219). Applied.
Bars 199–218 are ff in every part, so the hairpins at 209 and 217 start from ff and go nowhere, and 219 is the same ff as the twenty bars before it. Rimsky PDF 130: a prolonged crescendo is built by adding groups, strings, woodwind, then brass; PDF 136: the ear cannot bear the full orchestra for long. The arrival is the symphony's main event (owed item 2) and needs both a drop and a build, and it needs to exceed movement I's fff peaks.
Fix (applied in codaOne.ily, codaTwo.ily, arrival.ily):
- 205–208: `\ff` → `\f` in all parts (Cb, Vla get `\f` on their first note of 205); the existing 209–210 hairpins now rise into ff at 211.
- 215: `\subP` in every part (Timp `aes,1:32\subP`); strings and woodwind `\subP\<` at 215, brass and timpani start `\<` at 216 (Rimsky's order); the old `\<` at 217 and `\!` at 218 removed, so the hairpins run through the allargando into 219.
- 219: `\ff` → `\fff` in bar 1 of every arrival part (and the cymbal/bass-drum stroke). The chorale at 220 keeps its f.
The 210 beat-4 crash stays: it now ends a crescendo from f, which is the "detached sforzando" use of PDF 135.

### 2. The last cadence (251–255) and final chord have no climax. Applied.
251–262 is flat ff; 254–255, the symphony's first root-position V7–I in C since movement I bar 135, is not marked above anything around it. Rimsky PDF 130: brass crescendo chords become "the most brilliant sforzando climaxes".
Fix (finale.ily): `\<` on the first note of 253 in every sounding part; `\fff` on 255 (Timp: 254 `aes,2:32\f\< g,2:32`, 255 `c1:32\fff`); `\ff` restored at 256 where a part had none (Bsn 2, Vla, Vc, Cb, Tuba); 261 final chord `\fff` in all parts (PDF 136: end ff or louder, never mf).

### 3. sf-p chords missing where the texture already asks for them (101, 105, 138). Applied.
At 101 and 105 the strings strike a staccato double-stop chord exactly as the winds start their held chord; Rimsky's device (PDF 129 a, Ex. 253) is to make that string chord sf and the wind chord piano. v08 has the winds at mixed mp/mf/fp and the strings unaccented. At 138 the retransition's crescendo arrives at movement I's stop chord B–D–F–A♭ on a plain mf, then 139 repeats mf; the stop chord passes without weight.
Fix (dev.ily): Vla, Vc, Cb `\sf` on the first note of 101 and 105 (not the bassoon, which shares a staff with Bsn 1 at p); Hn 1·2 `\mp` → `\p`, Bsn 1 `\fp` → `\p` at 101; Cl 2 stays mf to match Cl 1 on the shared staff.
(retr.ily): 138 strings `\mf` → `\sf`, winds and Hn 3·4 `\mf` → `\subP` (sf-dim, PDF 129); 139 all `\mf` → `\p` including the horns' head; sustained Bsn, Hn 3·4, Vla, Cb `\<` from 141, Vc from 142, into the existing f at 145.

### 4. Trombones re-enter mf after 28 bars' rest (143). Applied.
Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn last played at 114 and come back at 143 marked mf, with the contrabassoon and timpani also mf. Rimsky PDF 136 names exactly this: a mezzo-forte brass re-entry is "colourless and common-place"; pp or ff is right, and PDF 130 gives the brass the crescendo.
Fix (retr.ily): Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, Cbsn and Timp at 143 `\mf` → `\pp\<`, reaching the existing f at 145 and ff at 147.

### 5. Diminuendo 39–42 in the wrong order. Applied.
Strings, bassoons and Hn 3·4 diminish from 39, but flutes, oboes, clarinets and Hn 1·2 hold f to the end of 42 and the strings stop at mf, so the transition's p/pp at 43 arrives as a cut-off. Rimsky PDF 130: diminuendo removes brass first, then woodwind, then strings.
Fix (pTwo.ily): Hn 1·2 `\>` at 39, `\p` at 41; Fl, Ob, Cl 1·2 `\>` at 40, `\p` at 42; strings, bassoons, Hn 3·4 target `\mf` → `\p` at 42.

### 6. Woodwind doublings enter mp and jump to f. Applied.
Fl 1 at 61, Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 77, Ob 1 at 181, Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 197 enter `\mp` to double the climax of F2, and at 78 and 198 go straight to f without a hairpin while the violins crescendo. PDF 136 on mp entries; PDF 130 on adding woodwind inside a crescendo.
Fix (sOne, sTwo, recS): `\mp` → `\p\<` at each entry; `\mf` added on the next downbeat at 62 and 182 to match the trumpet and cor anglais peaks.

### 7. Tuba has no dynamic at its recapitulation entry (159). Applied.
recPTuba enters at 159 with nothing printed; its last mark is ff at 147, while the bass trombone it doubles is f. Fix: `es,2\f` at 159.

### 8. Overall vocabulary (not a separate edit).
After these changes the score uses sf, sub. p and fff only at 101, 105, 138, 215, 219, 255 and 261. That is the economy PDF 136 asks for; no further accents should be added.
