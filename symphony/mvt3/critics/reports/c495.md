## Findings

### 1. Page 1: the score has no key for the one-line percussion stave (MOLA PDF 6, PDF 5) — applied

- **Problem.** MOLA PDF 6 says: "If there are deviations from standard musical notation, an explanation should appear following the instrumentation page." PDF 5 asks for a notation key for the percussion. The drum stave carries two players on one line, crash cymbals with a circled-x head (`\tweak style #'xcircle cymc1`) above and bass drum with a normal head below. It appears only three times (223, 263, 278) and is hidden for the other 276 bars. Since c255 the percussion part has printed this key. The score has not, so a conductor first meets the circled head at the arrival and has no key to read it by. The page 1 header already lists the instruments and the number of players, which is where MOLA puts the explanation.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-495/work/score.ly, `\header` subsubtitle):** one line after the instrumentation, using the same glyphs as the part's key:
  ```
  \small \line { "Percussion stave (one line):" \musicglyph "noteheads.s2xcircle" "above the line = crash cymbals (pair);" \musicglyph "noteheads.s2" "below the line = bass drum" }
  ```
  with a comment `% c495 (MOLA PDF 6 … PDF 5 …)` in front of the c345 comment.
- **Build:** 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, score.midi byte-identical to v495. Pages 2–16 are pixel-identical at 40 dpi. Page 1 still holds bars 1–13 with the bar-number row on the page. No notes changed, so par8.py has nothing to check.

### 2. The harp pedal diagrams against MOLA PDF 5 ("harp pedaling should be left to the performer and not printed") — tested on paper, declined

The diagrams at 43, 67, 123, 187, 231 and the glissando diagram at 277 go against this sentence. I keep them. The c6 relay from movement II added them so that all four movements follow one convention. Each diagram marks a place where the pedals must already be set: the D♮ low string at 43, the G♯ at 241–243 for III♯, and the enharmonic pentatonic glissando at 277, which cannot be played in any other setting (c256). There a printed setting tells the player what the composer needs; it does not take a pedalling choice away from them. No change.

### 3. No rehearsal letter at 123 (MOLA PDF 4: letters at the landmarks) — considered, declined

At 123 there is a double bar, the key changes to G major and the harp's hand-over begins. H runs 20 bars (115–134), which ties for the longest stretch without a letter. A letter there would rename J–T to K–U. Those letters are referred to in the parts (c315's `\lt`), in coherence/finale.md and in about 150 entries in HISTORY. A plain "I" is ruled out because it reads as 1, which is why LilyPond and Gould skip it. Since every bar is numbered below the system (MOLA PDF 4), a rehearsal can start at "123" without a letter. The cost is larger than the gain, so no change.
