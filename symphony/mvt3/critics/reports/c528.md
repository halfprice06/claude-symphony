## Findings

1. **198→199, timpani roll into the last C-minor tutti (tested in scratch, not applied).** Ex. XI-21 (PDF 38–40) begins the big tutti with a timpani roll. At 199 the timpani come in ff on C after 20 bars' rest (179–198), and bar 198 has A♭1 in the bass (Cb, f <), with the A♭ drum already tuned (rolled at 167–168, struck at 205). I tested `aes,1:32\p\<` at 198, replacing the score's `R1*2` at 197–198 with `R1 | aes,1:32\p\<` in `recSTimp`. The build has 0 warnings and 0 outer-voice parallels, but the score grows to **17 pages**: once the timpani play, their stave appears in the 187–198 system, which then no longer fits on page 10 and moves to its own page. There are two musical reasons against it as well. The same device (an A♭ roll crescendo into a ff tutti) already happens 12 bars later at 209–210 → 211 (c494), and it would lose its effect if heard twice. And principle 1 (PDF 37) favours the timpani's ff entry at 199 after a long silence, which is the colour being held back. Declined. Test outputs are in /tmp/critic-528/test-roll198/.

2. **113→114, overlapping soft strings with the ff cut (worked out on paper, not applied).** Ex. XV-36 (PDF 190) would have the strings enter p on 113.3 under the ff half-note chord. But 113 is F minor and 114 is D (V of G minor). The half-bar of silence is what separates the two keys, and it is Tchaikovsky's own break-off before the low strings continue (PDF 38). c228 and c328 kept it for the same reason. No change.

3. **138, the stop chord (checked, follows).** This bar is the reading's sforzando recipe 2 (a short sf trumpet note over long string notes, PDF 189–190) together with the subito-piano overlap of Ex. XV-36: clarinets and bassoons hold sub. p under the sf > of the strings, oboe and stopped horns. This is a textbook realisation. Do not touch.

4. **149–151, retransition into the recapitulation (checked, follows).** All the long notes play sfp < under Tpt 1·2's accented ff halves, and the timpani roll re-attacks sfp (c346, c508). Then comes the ff recapitulation with a G roll at 151–152. This is Ex. XI-21's tutti-plus-roll, and Ex. XV-35's short trumpet attack combined with long notes. No change.

5. **234→235, end of the chorale into Q (checked, follows).** The brass stop on 234.1 p, but Hn 3·4 hold p into 235 and the timpani roll goes p → pp. The soft chord is therefore already sounding when the loud brass stop (Ex. XV-36, XV-40). No change.

6. **166, mixed f and ff at the end of the recapitulated first group (checked, follows).** Vln II's ff on 166 brings it back to the ff tutti after its f espress. figuration (159–165). The f marks in the cor anglais, Cl 2 and trombones are entry levels of secondary parts. This is balanced, and consistent with exposition 38–39. No change.

7. **243–246, the horn call in the trumpets (checked, follows).** Hn 1·2 are marked ff and Tpt 1·2 f, both "molto espr." with < > swells, over the strings' "cresc. molto". This keeps the owed trumpet statement audible without the trumpets covering it, as Franck shapes his climax by groups (Ex. XI-20, PDF 38). The trombones are held back until 247, so they arrive as a fresh colour at the Più allegro (principle 1). No change.
