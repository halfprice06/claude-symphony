## Findings

### 1. 239-243: the second-theme head's second phrase is at one level, and its accompaniment crescendos from f under the horn call (applied)

**Problem.** Since c138, the violins' first phrase of movement I's S head (235-238) has movement I's swells. The second phrase, 239-242, is the same tune an octave higher in Vln I-II, with Fl 1 and Ob 1. It runs four bars at f with no dynamic shape and arrives at 243 still f. At 243 the E-major chord (III♯) and the horn call begin (Hn 1·2 ff, Tpt 1·2 f, E–C–B–C). Every accompanying part starts its crescendo to 247 from f in that same bar: Vln I-II divided (G♯4/E5, around the call's register, with E5 in unison with Tpt 1), Vc, Cb and Bn 1·2. The call, which is the point of 243 and closes the C–A♭–E cycle, has only one level above its accompaniment, and that accompaniment is already growing. The crescendo into R also has only one step to cover (f to ff).

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 155: the lyrical legato swells into the note of arrival. PDF 149-150: a line held at one level sounds lifeless. PDF 290: a line is heard when the other parts are made quieter. PDF 152: a dynamic process has to be written in stages. Movement I phrases this second phrase with swells (recTwo.ily, quoted above). Dvořák 9/iv 66-80 shapes every bar of its lyrical theme and keeps the accompaniment two or more levels below it.

### 2. 243: Ob 1's G♯ keeps f (decided, part of Finding 1)
I considered giving Ob 1 mf with the strings. I decided against it. The G♯ is the third of III♯, and c083 put it in Ob 1 because one half-section of violins was too weak against four brass on E. With the violins now at mf, the oboe's accented f carries that tone.

### 3. 226-230: the wind statement of the canon has no slur (checked, no change)
Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 play G–E–D–C–D♭–C in whole notes, ff, with each note tongued. The violins play the same line with "change bow at will". The reading's legato principle (PDF 151) would suggest a slur. At 2 = 56 the five bars last about 21 s, and the only breath mark is after 230. A slur would leave the flutes no place to breathe. Detached whole notes in a chorale texture are the normal notation. This is also inside the protected 223-234.

### 4. 247-277 Cb eighths, 255-262 viola quarters, 11-26 horn off-beats (checked, no change)
These are the score's longest repeated-note figures (PDF 150, 152: the "machine gun" effect). Each one has hairpins that follow the harmony and the phrase: Cb at 257, 259, 269 and 275, violas at 257 and 259. Each also changes pitch with the harmony at least once every bar or every two bars. The off-beats at 11-26 move with the chords under a tune that has an accent on every strong bar. Nothing needs to change.

### 5. 231-234: uniform f > mf diminuendo in the brass (noted, not applied)
PDF 149 says brass blend worst across levels, so a brass diminuendo is better scored by thinning, as c237 did at 39-42. The plagal close 231-234 is protected (do-not-touch since c022 and c042, and inside 223-234), and it ends mf before a p entry at 235, so the change of timbre is small. I did not change it.
