## Comparison with Saint-Saëns 3/iv and with v169

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? In the areas the reading covers, mostly yes. Like Saint-Saëns, the finale builds its last pages from one transformed motto (the chorale top line G–E–D–C, then the last statement in doubled values over string and clarinet figuration, 255–264). That matches Adler's point 10: a repeated statement gets a new distribution but keeps its basic colour. Brass are saved for the entries that matter (point 20). The final chord is spaced as Adler praises in Bizet (point 12). The one place where this finale reads less like Saint-Saëns is the number of printed tempo changes in 199–279 (ten). That plan is protected (R at 88 by Dan; Largamente 273 since c038), so I do not reopen it.

Batch 171–178 against v169:
- Development 114–146 (c171, c172, c174): Vln I's answers now come at 119 and 122, pp, over the bassoon's held notes. This is Adler's point 13 (fresh colour, no overlap with the solo) and point 21. Hn 4's G3 at 139–142 matches the bass trombone's G2 from 141, so the chord is a clean V6/5, and Hn 3·4 resting at 143–144 clears the horns' F1 head. Better than v169.
- 205–210 (c173, c176, c177, c178): the notes are better than in v169. Ob 1 no longer sits low under Ob 2, and the head's lower octave is a real octave (Cl 1·2 a2 + Bn 1), as in Schoenberg's octave doubling (point 16). The brass mp under the f winds is a normal accompaniment level. But these two bars have been rescored in three cycles in a row. Brahms keeps a colour combination for long stretches (point 8), and the passage is now finished. It should not be touched again (see the last line). The MIDI step at 205 is the only part that got worse in the realisation (Finding 1).
- Last chorale (c177 Tbn 2 E3 at 255–256; breaths c126/c178): the breaths are staggered, and Tbn 2 now steps E3–C3 instead of doubling. Not fussier.
- Marks (c175 cleanup at 199, 219, 229): the removed "unis."/"div." marks were redundant (recS ends single-line; the violas stay divided from 211), so nothing was lost.

Nothing got worse against v169 in the notes, the voice leading (identical p5.py output), the page layout (16 pages) or the quotations.

## Findings

### 1. 205: the MIDI tempo jumps 5% on the first bar of "poco a poco più animato" (applied)
- **Bars:** 205–210 (global.ily, MIDI only).
- **Problem:** Since c173 the MIDI goes from 2 = 84 at 204 straight to 88 at 205, then 90 at 207 and 92 at 209. The printed word is "poco a poco", but the largest single step falls on its first bar. That is a "subito" step, not a gradual one.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 201–202: a gradual change is clearer when "actually written out" step by step (Mahler, Ex. XVI-4; Beethoven 6, Ex. XVI-5, where the horns' p cresc. comes before the tutti). Adler PDF 213: Bizet orchestrates his crescendo at 13–18 by stages instead of leaving it to the players. The orchestration of 205–210 already does this: the head rises from A♭4 (horns, 205) to A♭5 (winds, Vln I, 207), and the crescendo starts at 209. A tempo realisation should have equal steps too.
- **Fix (applied):** `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco a poco più animato" \mt 172 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak \mt 176 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak \mt 180 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak s1 \tag #'score \pageBreak \mt 184 s1`. That gives 86 (205), 88 (206), 90 (207–208), 92 (209–210). The arrival tempo before Largamente and all printed marks are unchanged, and R stays 88. Checked in the scratch MIDI: tempo events 168 (199), 172 (205), 176 (206), 180 (207), 184 (209), 144 (211).

I found no other defect that the assigned reading supports and that earlier cycles have not already settled. I decided not to change anything else.
