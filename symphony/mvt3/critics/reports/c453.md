## Findings

### 1. 129: the G-major cadence keeps ♮6–♭6–5 (IV6 – iv6 – V7); the 4–♯4–5 alternative was tested and is worse (no change)

- Problem tested: A&S PDF 215 (Ex. 14-2, 14-3d) gives IV – V6/5 of V – V, bass 4–♯4–5, as the typical way to strengthen V at a cadence, and the G-major area 123–132 is the score's one long key area on V. At 129 beats 3–4 the bass goes E–E♭–D (IV6 – iv6 in G), the same E♭→D the G-minor half cadence used at 121–122.
- Test (in /tmp/critic-453/test, not applied): Vc `b,2\< c4 cis4`, Cb `b,,2\< c,4 cis,4`, Hn 4 `g4\p\< g4 e4 e4`, Bn 1 `b2\< c'4 a4 | c'1)`, harp LH beats 3–4 `c8 e8 a8 e'8`. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels.
- Result: worse. The tune (Fl 1, Vln I G5; Vln II G4) holds G on 129.4 and rises to A at 130. Over C♯ the G is the seventh of A7 and rises instead of falling to F♯ (A&S Unit 14 PDF 214 treats the applied V7 like any V7), and the outer voices go C♯2–G5 (diminished fifth) to D2–A5 (perfect fifth), the d5→P5 that A&S forbid between bass and soprano. par8.py also shows a new octave pair, Bn 1 B3→C4 with the bass B1→C2 at 129.3. The present iv6 keeps G as a common tone, lets the bass fall by step into the 7th C4 of the V7 at 130, and recalls G minor's ♭6 inside G major. Keep it.

### 2. 113–115: the move from F minor to G minor stays a phrase modulation; a pivot chord was tested and is worse (no change)

- Problem tested: A&S PDF 216–217 (§7) prefers a pivot chord that leads to V of the new key. At 113 the F-minor PAC is cut off after two beats, and at 114 the strings play a bare D7 (p >), with no chord common to the two keys.
- Test (not applied): 114 as C minor 6 (v6 in F minor = iv6 in G minor) to V7: Vc `es2\p\>\upbow d4 r4\!`, Cb `es,2\p\>\upbow d,4 r4\!`, Vla `g2\p\> a4 r4\!`, Vln I `g''2\p\> fis''2`, Vln II unchanged. Build clean, 16 pages, no new parallels in the outer voices (the cellos double the basses' new E♭→D, as they do everywhere).
- Result: worse. A&S PDF 219 (§10, Ex. 14-11) accepts a phrase modulation when "strong contrasts in rhythm and texture" make the new section "begin anew". That is what 113–115 does: fff tutti, two beats of silence, a bare p dominant in the strings, then a bassoon solo over pizzicato. The pivot adds a third E♭→D in nine bars (114, 117 E♭ bass, 121–122 It6) and so weakens the 121–122 half cadence, which is the G-minor section's goal. Keep the abrupt F6→F♯5 in Vln I.

### 3. 133–134: the return from V as a key follows A&S PDF 220 exactly (no change)

- After the G-major PAC at 132 (harp ff, timpani G), Vc and Cb fall to F under G–B–D (Vln I G5, Vln II D5–B4, Vla D4, Hn 3·4 D4/B3), V4/2 as in Schubert's Heidenröslein bar 11 (Ex. 14-1). F is "the one scale degree in the tonic key that does not appear in the key of the dominant" (PDF 220). I considered adding F4 in Hn 3 to make the seventh audible above the bass; that doubles the seventh of V7, which A&S do not allow, and Hn 3's D4 is the only D below Vln II. Not tested further. Keep.

### 4. 168: the French sixth's ♯4 is one line, not a doubled chord tone (no change)

- A&S PDF 214: "♯4 ... must not be doubled". At 168.1 F♯ sounds as F♯3 (Bn 1 and Cl 2 at the unison, the line E♭–F–F♯–G and A♭–F♯–G), F♯4 (Vln II) and F♯5 (Vln I). The violins' F♯ is the first note of their eighth-note figure F♯–G–A♭–F♯–D, an octave doubling of one line that itself steps to G; the structural ♯4 resolves to G at 169 in Bn 1 and Cl 2. Giving Cl 2 C4 instead (the safe doubling in an augmented sixth) would break its unison with Bn 1 and leave it a tritone leap B3–F3 into 170. Not better. Keep.

### 5. Form and pacing: proportions, the dominant region and the coda (no change)

- See "Proportions" above. The development's G area (115–134) plus the retransition (135–150) is a 36-bar dominant region; A&S PDF 216 and 220 explain why that works here: G is confirmed as a key by its own dominant and a PAC, then converted by the seventh, and the retransition prolongs V without a second cadence on G (Ex. 14-12's B phrase). The recapitulation's entry over the G pedal (151–154, i6/4, accepted at c043; 151–158 layout protected since c134) continues that dominant and is the right answer to the rule that no root-position V–i in C comes before 261–263. The coda builds; its quiet stage is short and its loudest marks are where the plan puts them (219/223, 255, 263).
