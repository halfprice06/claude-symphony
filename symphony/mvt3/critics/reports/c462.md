## Findings

**1. Bar 82: the E♭-major cadence that ends the second group has no cadential 6/4, and its dominant lasts one quarter (applied).**

Problem. 67–82 is F2 in E♭ major. 83 is its authentic cadence and the start of the closing group (root V–I in E♭ is allowed; only C is restricted). In v462, bar 82 holds iv6 (C♭–E♭–A♭: Cb, Vc, Bn 2, Vla on C♭, Hn 1·2 E♭, Bn 1 A♭3) for three beats. V comes only on 82.4, as a bare quarter under the end of the tune's run. Meanwhile the tune (Vln I·II, Fl 1, Ob 1, from c113) runs G–A♭–B♭–D from 82.3. So on 82.3 the tune's G5/G4 sounds against Bn 1's A♭3 over C♭ in the bass, a second and an augmented fifth on the strong half of the bar. The harmony does not support the tune's G (3̂ of E♭) where the tune places it.

Reading. A&S say the cadential 6/4 "most characteristically decorates the V chord at an authentic or half cadence" and "usually support[s] an accented 3̂ or 1̂" (PDF 152, 155, Ex. 10-7). Its 4th "most often enter[s] as a suspension—held over or repeated from the preceding chord", and the 6th "will move down by step into the 5th" (PDF 153, Ex. 10-3a, IV → V6/4–5/3). It "should be approached by intermediate or tonic harmony" (PDF 160 #7). It must stand on the strong part of the beat or bar, with the resolution on a weaker part (PDF 154, Ex. 10-6), and the bass is the tone to double, never the 4th (PDF 154, Ex. 10-5). Bar 82 already has every ingredient: iv6 is the intermediate harmony, its E♭ is the common tone that becomes the 4th, its A♭ steps to G as the 6th, and the tune puts G on 82.3. The score uses the cadential 6/4 at every C-minor and C-major cadence (18, 38, 109, 158, 220, 243, 250), but not at the cadence that confirms the second key.

**2. 80.4–81.1: checked, no change.**
The scan lists 81.1 as a 6/4 (B♭ with E♭ and G) that follows V7 at 80.3, which PDF 160 #7 forbids for a cadential 6/4. It is not one. The tune's A♭5 on 80.3 is V7's 7th (Hn 1 A♭3 with it), and the E♭ (Ob 1) and G (tune, Hn 1) that enter on 80.4 are held over the barline and resolve to D and F on 81.2. These are the 4–3 and 6–5 suspensions over a sounding V7 from which A&S derive the 6/4 (PDF 153, Ex. 10-2a), not a 6/4 chord. Holding the violas' E♭4 at 80.3 to make 80.3 a real 6/4 would put the tune's A♭ against it as a dissonant 7th on beat 3, and it would change the c141 counter. Declined.
