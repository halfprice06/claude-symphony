## What else I studied

The Jupiter finale from the local corpus (reference-corpus/pdf/Mozart_Sym41_mvt4.pdf and musicxml-converted/ly/Mozart_Sym41_mvt4.ly), with Adler's analysis of its coda (adler2 173–176, Ex. XV-24/25). I compared v69 with v59 by source diff of all sections, global.ily and score.ly, by note listings from both MIDI files at 23–26, 86–90, 107–113, 161–166 and 255–264, and by page images of both PDFs (pages 6, 9–16). I re-ran themecheck: finale quotations 34/34 (v69 and my scratch copy), scherzo record 41/41 against scherzo v47, so the scherzo quotation at 275–276 and the timpani motive x at 143–145 still match; no quotation needs updating.

Holistic comparison. Mozart's finale is built from five short elements, each with its own rhythm, each exposed alone, and they meet in the coda in invertible counterpoint with one constant colour each (adler2 175–176). v69 follows that method at 247–254 and 265–272: two heads with different rhythms, already heard alone, in contrasting registers and colours, over an independent bass. A conductor would accept these pages. Where the two works differ: Mozart combines the finale's own themes, while this coda combines movement I's heads and never brings back the finale's own second theme F2 after 198. I considered adding F2's head as a cantus firmus at 265–268 and decided against it. The coda already carries nine quotations and three thematic stages (chorale, heads, F1 in major), and adler2 146 warns that too much variety "puts the structure in jeopardy". Mozart's ending stays in tempo; this one broadens at 273 (protected since c038/c049, and the chain close is the symphony's cyclic answer), so I leave it. What separates v69 from a master's score are details, not the design: one harmony left behind when the melody changed (163), the low horn sitting at its ceiling in loud unisons (250, 265–268), and a cantus whose colour changes mid-line (259–260).

v59 → v69. The development climax (110–113: violins in octaves, tie and 4–3, horns doubled, German sixth at 109) is better. The F1 consequent's 4–3 at 25 and 165 is clean: no B sounds under the suspended C at 165, beat 3. The 7–6 chain at 87–89, the 170 voicing, the recap S rescoring (171–198) and the breath marks (c066) are not worse. The breaths at 214 and 218 now lift the whole wind choir together, but the strings hold the bass, so the sound does not break. The one change that made the score worse is the Neapolitan at 163 (Finding 1). The Tpt 2 doubling at 255–258 (c064) is right in itself but stops halfway through the line (Finding 3).

## Findings

### 1. 163: the Neapolitan D♭ grinds against a C held in the brass (worse since v63; applied)
Problem. c062 changed the melody's D to D♭ at 163 (E♭–D♭–C–B) in Fl, Ob, Vln I·II, Hn 1·2, violas and clarinets, but the accompaniment kept the exposition's chord (23: iiø6/5 with C). Beats 3–4 of 163 now sound F–A♭–C–D♭ with C4 in Tbn 1 (held), Tpt 3 and Hn 3 directly against D♭4 in Hn 1·2, violas and Cl 2, at ff. Cl 2 also arpeggiates A♭–C–D♭–C. The result is a semitone in the middle of the brass (MIDI at 163, beat 4: Tbn 1 C4, Tpt 3 C4, Hn 3 C4 against Hn 1·2/Vla/Cl D♭4), not a Neapolitan sixth. In v59 the same parts made C–D, a whole tone inside a correct iiø6/5.
Evidence. adler2 159: in homophonic scoring "the melody notes should be more prominent than the harmony"; here the harmony contradicts the melody note. adler2 161–162 (Schumann 1): the scored progression must be "according to the book". The book chord at this point is the N6 F–A♭–D♭, with D♭ going to the leading tone B by a diminished third (A&S Unit 28).
Fix (applied, recP.ily, bar 163 only; 23 keeps D):
```
recPTbnOne   bar 163: c'1                    -> c'2 des'2
recPTptThree bar 163: r4 c'4-. r4 c'4-.      -> r4 c'4-. r4 des'4-.
recPHnThree  bar 163: r4 c'4-. r4 c'4-.      -> r4 c'4-. r4 des'4-.
recPClTwo    bar 163: aes8( c' des' c')      -> aes8( des' f' des')
```
Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 then go D♭→B into 164; Hn 3 goes D♭→D. No new parallels (analysis: 0).

### 2. 250 and 265–268: Hn 2 on written G5 ff in unison with Hn 1 (applied)
Problem. On the S head (247–250) and on F1 in C major (265–268) Hn 1 and Hn 2 play the same line, so the low horn (Hn 2) plays concert C5 = written G5 at ff in unison with Hn 1: at 250 (c''2 b'2) and at the head note of 265 and 267, after 18 bars of loud playing from 247. Written G5 is the low horn's ceiling (c046 cited adler1 301 for this). These are the only places in the score where Hn 2 sits there in a loud unison; at 243–246 the composer already puts Hn 2 an octave under Hn 1.
Evidence. adler2 26 (Schuller): loud high unison horns interfere and crack; such passages go to Hn 1 and 3 alone or are reinforced otherwise. adler2 27: horn and cello are the natural pair on a sustained line. At 265–268 the cellos already play F1 an octave lower.
Fix (applied, finale.ily): Hn 2 an octave lower at 250 (`c'2 b2`) and at 265–268 (`c'2->(\ff g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. |` twice), with the cellos. Hn 1·2 are set `\partCombineApart` for 247–250 (and back to `\partCombineAutomatic` at 251), because the slur from 247 otherwise splits mid-phrase and LilyPond warns "already have slur". The upper octave keeps Hn 1, Tpt 1·2 and violas at 265–268, so the line is no weaker.

### 3. 259–260: the last chorale's top line changes colour in its middle (applied)
Problem. Since c064 Tpt 2 doubles Tpt 1 on the protected top line G–E (255–258), leaves it for C5 at 259–260 (Tpt 1 alone on D5), rejoins on C at 261, and splits again at 262 (the protected B→C under D→E). The line that leads to the symphony's only V–I loses half its trumpet weight on its fifth and sixth bars, then regains it. The listener hears the doubling switch on and off inside one phrase.
Evidence. adler2 176: in the Jupiter coda Mozart "keep[s] an established color intact long enough to carry out its assignment"; "the listener must not be overburdened with shifting colors". adler2 38–40, the Tchaikovsky 4/i passage that c064 cited, keeps the cantus in the same brass unison throughout.
Fix (applied, finale.ily): Tpt 2 `d''1\<~ | d''1` at 259–260 (with Tpt 1), so the two trumpets stay together G–E–D–C until the protected split at 262. The seventh C of the iiø6/5 goes to Ob 2 (`c''1\f\<~ | c''1`, already on C5 at 257–258, so it now holds the common tone) and Hn 4 (`c'1\<~ | c'1`, was A♭3; A♭ stays in Fl 2, Tbn 2, Vln II). `\partCombineUnisono` at 259–261 (back to `\partCombineAutomatic` at 262) prints the unison as one notehead under the existing a2 instead of two shifted semibreves. The protected line, the Ger6 at 261 (Tpt 3 and violas F♯) and the 262–263 B→C are unchanged.
