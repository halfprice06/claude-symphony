## Findings

### 1. 125-130: no counter-voice under the violas' and clarinet's entries; the cellos only double the basses (applied)

**Problem.** After passing F2 to the violas at 124, the cellos hold E3-C3-A2-D3-G3-D3 an octave above the basses. Under the violas' G4-F♯4-E4-D4 (125-126) the only other sound is the harp arpeggio, in the 8va bassa register. Nothing sits between E3 and G4, and no second line answers the theme.

**Evidence.** Every model in the chapter gives a passed-on tune a second line in another colour:
- Brahms, Hungarian Dance 1: an independent cello part over a steady bass (PDF 205).
- Dvořák, Slavonic Dance 8: sustained "harmonic counterpoint" (PDF 209).
- Stravinsky, *Pulcinella*: "the little counterpoint of the bassoon", which never interferes with the melody (PDF 228).
- Schoenberg's countertheme from the bar's pitches "having its own rhythm", which "continues to enrich the texture … first in the second horn and the bassoons" (PDF 230-231).

This score already does it at 69-70 and 189-190 (c027).

**Fix.** Bassoon 1 gets a counter-line. It is a different colour from the viola and clarinet tunes, so it does not merge with them (PDF 217), and it continues the instrument's G-minor solo from 115-122. The cellos keep the bass. In `devBsnOne`:

```
… bes2 g2 | a1) | R1*2 | e2(\p^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "dolce" } fis4 g4 | c'2 e2 | a2 b4 c'4 | a2 c'2 | g1\< | c'1) | c'2\f a2 | …
```

The line moves when the tune holds: E3-F♯3-G3 under the viola's G4, rising C4 and A3-B3-C4 against the falling tune. The C4 at 128 is the seventh of D7 and falls to G3. It leads into the existing f C4 at 131. The all-pairs scripts show no new fifths or octaves.

### 2. 123-124: the violas' held B3-C4 sits on and above the cellos' entry (applied)

**Problem.** The cellos open the hand-over with D4-B3 | A3-G3-B3-D4. The violas hold B3 under it (the same pitch as the tune's beat-4 B3), then C4 dotted half above the tune's A3-G3. The tune and its background are in the same family, in the same register and in similar values, and at 124 they cross.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 217: in that situation "highly contrasting colors … are very necessary to separate the two elements". PDF 229: at pitch, similar strings "swallow each other up". PDF 213 and 228: delaying an entry clarifies the tune and makes the next entry "very fresh". c034 removed Vln II's held G4 at 123-126 for the same reason and left this viola note.

**Fix.** The violas rest and enter only with their own phrase. In `devVla`:

```
… r4 <a c'>4 r2 | r1 | r2. d'4(\p^\markup \italic "arco, unis." | g'2. fis'4 | e'2 d'2) | …
```

The hand-over is unchanged: Vc 123, Vla 124 beat 4, Cl 1 126, Vln I/Fl 128-129, with the same notes and entry points (do-not-touch c034). Only the accompaniment changes. The viola also gets 1¾ bars to change from pizz. to arco.

### 3. 129-130: the crescendo into 131 has no new rhythmic layer, and the inner held notes double at the unison (applied)

**Problem.** The scoring crescendo of c033 ends in hairpins on whole notes. Hn 1 doubles Vln II at the unison (G4-F♯4), and Hn 3·4 sit out until the f at 131. The crescendo adds weight but no rhythmic drive.

**Evidence.** Beethoven 6/i 304-312 (Ex. XVI-5, PDF 202) builds its crescendo by bringing the horns in p cresc. with a new repeated figure before the winds join. Schoenberg "introduces a completely new rhythm … which lends great excitement to the crescendo" (PDF 230). Bizet gives a held pedal rhythm in one horn (PDF 213).

**Fix.** Hn 3·4 enter two bars early with repeated quarters on G and D7 chord tones (D4/B3, then D4/C4), p cresc. into their existing f D4/C4 at 131. The equal dynamics suit the shared staff.

```
devHnThree: … R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*6 | d'4\p\< d'4 d'4 d'4 | d'4 d'4 d'4 d'4 | d'1\f | …
devHnFour:  … R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*6 | b4\p\< b4 b4 b4 | c'4 c'4 c'4 c'4 | c'1\f | …
```

Hn 4's B3→C4→(131) C4→(132) B3 resolves the seventh. Hn 3·4 still rest from 107 to 128, so their colour is still saved for the cadence (PDF 236).

### 4. 115-130: the bass has one root per bar for 16 bars, and 123-125 loses the stepwise descent of its minor model (applied)

**Problem.** The G-minor statement has the bass G-F♯-E♭-C-A-D-G-D (115-122). The G-major statement has G-D-E-C-A-D-G-D (123-130), all in leaps. The parallel with the minor statement breaks at its one linear point, and the bass is reduced to roots.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 203: implied lines should be drawn out, and the transcriber should use "harmonic and melodic hints". PDF 205: Brahms's bass is steady, but it is a line.

**Fix.** At 124 the bass takes F♯ (V6/5). The bass then descends G-F♯-E like 115-117, and the harp's C falls to B at 125.

```
devCb:    … g,1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | fis,1 | e,1 | …
devHarpL: (124) fis,8 a,8 c8 d8 fis,8 a,8 c8 d8 |
```

The harp arpeggio starts on the bass note, as at 123. The cellos' tune (A3-G3-B3-D4) fits D7/F♯. The harp pedals are unchanged.
