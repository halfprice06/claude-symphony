## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 74→75 and 194→195: V7 to I6 at the seam of F2's period

**Problem.** Bar 74 is F2's eighth bar, the end of its antecedent, with the full V7 of E♭ (harp right hand F–A♭–B♭–D, melody F, bass B♭). Bar 75 starts the consequent on I6 (bass G). That is V7–I6 in root position to first inversion, on a downbeat, at the most exposed joint of the theme. The recap repeats the same move in A♭ at 194→195 (E♭ to C). The bass leaps a third down from 5 to 3. The 7th (A♭ in the cellos and violas) does reach G, but over a bass that has moved under it, so the ear hears an outer-voice octave approached by similar motion.

**Evidence.** A&S, PDF 109 (Ex. 7-7): "V7–I6" makes hidden octaves and is weak "especially on a strong beat". PDF 125-126 (Ex. 8-7, 8-8): the standard way from V7 to I6 is V7–V4/2–I6, with the 7th taken into the bass, which then falls to 3. PDF 99: V7 is rarely a goal; the antecedent here is open, so it needs a clear move into the consequent. A root I at 75 is not an option: the melody F over B♭ to B♭ over E♭ would make parallel fifths in the outer voices.

**Fix (applied).** The bass passes through the 7th on beat 3, so 74 becomes V7–V4/2 and 75 is its I6.
- sTwo.ily: `sTwoCb` bar 74 `bes,,1` → `bes,,2 aes,,2`. `sTwoHarpL` bar 74 likewise `bes,,2 aes,,2`.
- sTwo.ily: `sTwoVc` and `sTwoVla` bar 74 `aes4( g4 f4 aes4)` → `aes4( g4 f2)`. The counter-line now moves F→G against the bass A♭→G, in contrary motion, so A♭ is not doubled on beat 3.
- recS.ily: `recSCb` bar 194 `es,1` → `es,2 des,2`. `recSVc` bar 194 `\transpose c aes, { f4( e4 d4 f4) }` → `\transpose c aes, { f4( e4 d2) }`.
- Bassoon 2 (B♭2 / E♭2) is unchanged and holds the chord's root above the moving bass.

This settles the point c142 left open. It does not touch the 187-223 key path.

### 2. Bars 165→166: the recap's V7 leaves its 7th hanging

**Problem.** At 165 the whole orchestra is on V of C minor (bass G, melody E♭–D–C–B). Vln II and Ob 2 (div.) arpeggiate B–D–F–D, G–D–F–D, and Hn 4 repeats F. At 166 they go to C4 and G3 over E♭ in the bass (i6). The F does not go to E♭ in any of these voices. It leaps up or down, or the part stops. In the exposition (25→26) the F resolves to E♭. This difference came in with c133's rescoring of 159-166.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 98 and 103: the 7th of V7 must resolve down by step, and the resolution must be in the voice that has the 7th. PDF 109: V7–I6 is a weak connection in any case, and on a downbeat ff it is at its most audible. A plain V–i6 at the arrival is correct. The melody's 6-5 / 4-3 figure already carries the tension.

**Fix (applied).** V is used without the 7th in bar 165.
- recP.ily: `recPHnFour` bar 165 `r4 f4-. r4 f4-.` → `r4 g4-. r4 g4-.`
- recP.ily: `recPVlnTwo` bar 165 and `recPOboeTwoDiv` bar 165 (its 7th bar) `b'8( d'' f'' d'') g'( d' f' d')` → `b'8( d'' g'' d'') g'( d' g' d')`

### 3. Bars 170→171: Bassoon 1's 7th leaps away

**Problem.** Bassoon 1 holds F3 through 168-170 (the 7th of the V that leads into recS, marked pp at 170). At 171 it jumps down a fourth to C3. Nothing else takes the F to E♭, so this is the one voice with the 7th, and it does not resolve. The exposition joint (50→51) was fixed by c093 to resolve in the line that had the 7th. The recap has not had the same fix.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 98: the 7th resolves down by step. PDF 102 (Ex. 6-19): when the 7th moves between voices, the last voice that has it resolves it. Here Bassoon 1 is that voice.

**Fix (applied).** recS.ily: `recSBsnOne` bar 171 `c1\pp` → `es2\pp c2`. The F goes to E♭ on the downbeat, then falls to C on beat 3, which restores the rest of the original line. The change is in an inner voice only. The bass into 171 is unchanged, so the cyclic rule is not affected.

### 4. Bars 46.3-48: the dominant pedal loses its 3rd

**Problem.** After c162 moved the viola from D4 to C4 at 46.3 (D–C–C♭–B♭), no voice plays D from 46.3 through 48. Over the G pedal, the chord at 46.3 is G–C–F, and at 47-48 it is G♭–C♭ over the pedal. The dominant sounds as a bare fourth and fifth for two and a half bars, just before the 9-8 at 49 and Hn 1's D4 entry.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 98 (Ex. 6-10): the 3rd of V is not left out. In minor it is the raised leading tone, which defines the dominant (PDF 97, 100). PDF 127: inversions and neighbour chords must keep the chord complete.

**Fix (applied).** trans.ily: `transVlnTwo` bar 46 `f'1` → `f'2 d'2`. Vln II takes the D4 the violas leave. At 47 it leaps D4→G♭4, a diminished fourth, and the line then turns down (G♭→F at 49). PDF 131 says this is a good dissonant leap when the line changes direction. Bars 47-48 can now be heard as a neighbour harmony inside the expanded V (PDF 130). c162's viola descent and the 9-8 at 49 are unchanged.

## Considered and not changed

- **Square phrasing.** F1, F2 and the closing group are all built in 4-bar units. Adding a bar would renumber the whole movement and break the protected bar numbers from 199 on, so I left it.
- **The 12-bar V pedal (39-50).** Dvořák's dissolve takes about 5 bars. Here 12 bars give a 12-second transition, which is about 8 per cent of the exposition and is in proportion. Kuhnau's expanded V (PDF 94) gets more active over time, and this one gets less active. That is a deliberate dissolve into F2 and it works at this length, so I left it.
- **The thin closing group.** It has 16 bars and only 4 are ff. Against the 35 per cent exposition and the 32 per cent coda, a heavier close would weaken the development's entry. Left as is.
- **No perfect authentic cadence in the recap.** This is by design: no root V–i in C before 261-263, and the path is protected.
- **64→65 and 80→81.** V moves weak to strong, but 80→81 is V8–7 (PDF 101), and 64→65 adds the 7th, so both follow the book.
- **58→59 and 178→179.** These V7–i6 moves are covered by melodic interpolation (PDF 101, Ex. 6-16) and are fine.
- **The B–D–F–A♭ chord at 169.** It is a soft inverted V♭9. The protected event is the root B→C arrival at 222→223. Left as is.
