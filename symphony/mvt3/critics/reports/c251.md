## Findings

### 1. 27-32: the tuba doubles F1's bass statement, tongued, at the contrabassoon's pitch (applied)

**Problem.** At 27-34 F1 is in the bass under the violins' ff countersubject (doubled by Cl 1·2 a2). This is the tune's second statement and the listener's first chance to recognise it in a new register. The line sits at C4 (violas, cellos, Bn 1·2, Hn 3·4) and C3 (basses, bass clarinet). Its 16-foot octave is the contrabassoon alone, the slowest-speaking wind, and everything is slurred. The head's rhythm (half note, dotted quarter, eighth) is the most recognisable part of F1, and it has no attack at the bottom of the texture. The tuba has rested since bar 6 and never carries a theme in the movement.

**Evidence from the reading.** Hindemith "saves the tuba to give fresh color in its presentation of the theme two octaves lower" (adler2 PDF 20). Here the tune is presented two octaves below its C4 line. Wagner's *Siegfried* III prelude doubles one low line in bass clarinet, bassoons and tuba, "with two different articulations. While the winds are slurred, the tuba is asked to articulate every note" (PDF 33, Ex. XI-16). That is exactly the group at 27-32 (bass clarinet, Bn 1·2, Cbsn), minus the tuba. Tuba with bassoons, cellos and basses is "one of the most powerful combinations in the symphony orchestra" (PDF 33-34), and the *1812* example puts the anthem in the low choir against "a very heavy orchestration of the counterpoint" (PDF 28, Ex. XI-13), which describes 27-34. The tuba also strengthens the bass (PDF 32, Ex. XI-15). Dvořák 9 makes the same point: its tuba plays only in the Largo, so its few notes are heard as a new colour.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-251/work/sections/pTwo.ily).** A new variable; until now pTwo had no tuba part, so assemble.py filled 16 bars of rest:

```
pTwoTuba = { c,2->\ff g,,4.-- aes,,8-- | bes,,4-. aes,,8-. g,,8-. f,,4-. es,,4-. | d,,4-- g,,8-. aes,,8-. b,,4-- d,4-- | f,2-> es,4-- d,4-- | es,2-> bes,,4.-- c,8-- | des,4-. c,8-. bes,,8-. aes,,4-. g,,4-. | R1*10 | }
```
I decided the following:
- Same pitches as the contrabassoon (C2 level).
- ff like every other F1 part. After 20 bars of rest a brass re-entry is ff or pp (Rimsky PDF 136, as c208 used for the trombones at 35).
- Tenuto on the long notes and staccato on the short ones, no slur, following the Wagner model. The winds keep their slurs.
- The tuba stops after 32 with the contrabassoon, so the ff re-entry of the trombones and contrabassoon at 35 (c208) keeps its effect. It does not join 35-42.

F1's notes, the countersubject and every other part are unchanged.
