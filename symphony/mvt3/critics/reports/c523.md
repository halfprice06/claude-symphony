## Findings

### 1. 58→59 and 178→179: the minor statements of F2 join antecedent and consequent with V7–i6 (applied in the scratch copy)

**Bars.** 58 (exposition, E♭ minor, trumpet solo) and 178 (recapitulation, C minor, cor anglais and muted trumpet). In both bars the antecedent ends on V. The consequent starts at 59/179 on i6.

**Problem.** In both bars the bass plays only beat 1 (B♭1, G1) and then leaps to the 3rd (G♭1 at 59, E♭1 at 179). Over that leap the upper voices hold the 7th.
- **58.4:** the 7th A♭3 is in Cl 2 and in the violas' pizz. chord <F3 A♭3>, and no voice has the root. At 59 both A♭s rise to B♭3. The violas never resolve theirs. Cl 2 reaches G♭3 only on 59.3, through c142's interpolation.
- **178:** the violas' last eighths are D3–F3. Their F3 falls to E♭3 on the downbeat of 179 while the bass falls G→E♭. That is similar motion into the doubled 3rd, the hidden octaves the reading marks "avoid". Cl 2's F3 rises to G3.

The two major statements of the same theme (74→75 and 194→195) already join these seams correctly. Their bass passes B♭–A♭–G (A♭–G♭–F at 194), so V becomes V4/2 and the 7th resolves in the bass. The minor statements are the only places where F2's half cadence leads into the consequent through V7–i6. Without the 7th in the bass, the four statements of the theme do not join their two halves the same way.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 109, Ex. 7-7: "Avoid the progression V7–I6 … the resolution of the 7th of V7 (4–3), combined with the bass motion 5–3, creates a bad set of hidden octaves, particularly if I6 is in a rhythmically strong position." Both 59 and 179 are downbeats.
- PDF 117, Ex. 8-8: "V4/2 occasionally passes from V7 … to I6. The 7th of V7 transfers from one of the upper voices to the bass of V4/2 and, of course, resolves in the bass. This provides a convenient way of moving from V7 to I6 (which cannot be done directly)."
- PDF 98, 100: the 7th must fall by step.
- PDF 104 (checking box): do not double the 7th.
- PDF 116, Ex. 8-7: V4/2's bass is a "descending passing motion from V to I6".
- Dvořák 9/iv bars 68–76 (above): its second theme's seams use inverted dominants.

**Fix, as applied in /tmp/critic-523/work:**
- **sections/sOne.ily, bar 58:**
  - Vc: `bes,4 r4 r4 aes,4` (was `bes,4 r4 r2`).
  - Cb: `bes,,4 r4 r4 aes,,4` (was `bes,,4 r4 r2`).
  - Vla: the beat-4 chord is `<f bes>4` (was `<f aes>4`), so V4/2 has its root and the violas do not double the 7th.

  The bass now falls B♭–A♭–G♭ into 59, pp pizz., with the upper strings' beat-4 chord.
- **sections/recS.ily, bar 178:**
  - Cb: `g,,4 r4 r4 f,,4` (was `g,,4 r4 r2`). The basses keep beat 1 (c301), and the F1 comes after the cellos' and timpani's motive x (178.1–3), on the beat where motive x rests.
  - Vla: `d8( g d g) d8( g d g)` (was `… d8( f d f)`), which removes the hidden octaves and the octaves the new F1–E♭1 would make with them.

### 2. 58.1 and 178.1: the half-cadence chord carries the 7th held over from iv6 (checked, no change)

The reading says V, not V7, should be the goal of a semicadence when the melody rests on 5̂ (PDF 99, 103 point 8). At 58.1 and 178.1, Cl 2 ties A♭3 (F3) over from the iv6, so the goal chord sounds with a 7th on its downbeat. I decided against changing it, for two reasons:
- The tied note rises to the root on beat 2, so the chord is plain V from 58.2 (178.2). The tie is the end of c413's 7–6 into iv6, which A&S Unit 18 (PDF 297–298, Ex. 18-12) calls almost invariable with that progression.
- Cutting the tie would re-attack Cl 2 under the solo's held 5̂, which c188 removed for the soft pad.
