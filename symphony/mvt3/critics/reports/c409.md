## Findings

### 1. Bars 67-76: a solo cello with Vln II on F2 (Rimsky Ex. 18), tested and not applied

**Candidate.** This is the reading's one string technique that the finale has never used. PDF 58 says "the combination of the solo 'cello with the violins gives the latter a touch of the 'cello timbre". Ex. 18 (PDF 195, *May Night* III) shows a "V.c. Solo" playing the violins' line while "Altri V.celli" keep their own part. The best place for it in this score is the E♭-major string statement of F2 at 67-76. There, Vln II's lower octave (B♭4-F5) lies on the cello's A string, which PDF 56 calls the cello's richest register. The other cellos stay on the counter-melody with the violas and Hn 1.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-409/test-vc/sections/sTwo.ily, `sTwoVc` for 67-76 became two voices in tenor clef:

```
sTwoVc = { \clef tenor << { \voiceOne bes'2.(\mp^\markup \italic "1 solo, arco, espress." g'4) | f'4( es'4 g'4 bes'4) | f''2( es''4 d''4) | c''2( bes'2) | aes'2.( c''8 d''8) | c''8( bes'8 aes'4 g'4 f'4) | g'2( es'4 f'4~ | f'1) | bes'2( c''16 bes' aes' bes' g'4) | f'4(\< es'4 g'4 bes'4) }
  \new Voice { \voiceTwo g2(\mp_\markup \italic "altri, arco, espress." aes4 bes4) | ... | aes2(\< g2) } >> \oneVoice | es'2.\mf\< ...
```

At 77 the solo player rejoins the section on the cellos' F2 octave (E♭4), so nothing else changes. The build had 0 warnings and 16 pages. analysis.txt showed 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py for 66-77 printed the same list as for v409 (bassoon octaves with the bass only).

**Verdict: not better, so not applied.**
- One cello cannot be heard as a colour inside a whole Vln II section at mf. Rimsky's example is a soft, thin texture with Vln I and a chorus, not a mf string cantabile.
- The counter-melody would lose its first desk. That line was balanced over many cycles (Vla + Vc + Hn 1, c094/c098/c281).
- Page 4 gets a divided cello staff with high tenor-clef ledger lines for no audible gain.
- Brahms keeps his cellos whole in this kind of texture. In 1/iv the big tune's cellos are either on the bass line or on the tune's lower octave, never split one against the rest.

/tmp/critic-409/work is v409 unchanged; the test copy is kept in /tmp/critic-409/test-vc.

### 2. Holistic comparison with Brahms 1/iv: a conductor would accept the work; no change

**Overall plan.** Brahms reaches C major in his introduction: the alphorn theme over string tremolo and a timpani roll, then the pp trombone chorale. He then spends the Allegro winning that C major back. This finale reverses the order on purpose. It opens with the ff German sixth (a cyclic rule), keeps tremolo, the violins' C7 and the root-position cadence back, and saves the arrival, the roll, the tremolo and the chorale for 223. A conductor who knows Brahms 1 would see the model and the inversion.

**Where it stands with Brahms.**
- The chorale's top line comes from movement I.
- The withheld trumpets (51) work like Brahms's withheld trombones.
- The plagal close of the first chorale (231-234) is Brahms's kind of cadence.
- The last chorale at 255-264 is in full winds over moving strings, as in Brahms's Più allegro.
- The orchestral detail now meets the standard Rimsky's examples set. Every scan in the table is clean.

**Where it is not Brahms.**
- F1 is a con fuoco tune with Tchaikovsky's energy, not Brahms's broad hymn on the G string. The brief's models allow this.
- The coda is about 31% of the movement; Brahms's Più allegro is shorter. Earlier critics checked the proportions (c393, c403). Each stage of the coda is an owed cyclic item, so I do not propose cuts.
