## Findings

### 1. 27–38: Vln II's lower octave of the countersubject runs through the subject's register (applied)
Problem. At 27–34 F1 is in the bass voice at C4–A♭4 (violas, cellos in tenor clef, Bn 1·2, Hn 3·4). Vln II plays the countersubject an octave below Vln I, at B♭3–E♭5, so it crosses the subject and meets it at the unison: 28 beat 1 B♭3, 29 beat 4 D4, 30 beats 2–3 F4 and E♭4, 33 beat 4 A♭4. At 30, the subject's accented F4 peak, Vln II runs B3–C4–D4–F4 underneath it and then starts its arpeggio on the subject's E♭4. At 35–38 the parts swap roles, but Vln II's lower octave of the A♭ head (C4–B♭4) lies inside the violas' and cellos' countersubject (F3–B♭4), and they cross in every bar.
Evidence. Adler PDF 42: in Hindemith's fugato "all the instruments are in their very best registers, so that the subject comes through clearly every time it is played". In XI-23 and in Bartók XI-24 (PDF 45–47) the subject and the counter-voices stay in separate registers. Here the two lines of an invertible pair share the same octave, and the higher one is only an octave doubling.
Fix (applied in pTwo.ily). Vln II plays Vln I's notes for 27–38, marked "unis. with Vln I", and goes back to its own line (the lower octave) at 39:
```
pTwoVlnTwo = { c'''8(\ff^\markup \italic "unis. with Vln I" bes'' aes'' g'') f''( d'' b' d'') | ... (= pTwoVlnOne bars 1–12) ... | f'2->(\> bes4. c'8 | d'4-.) c'8( bes8 aes4-.) g4-. | f'2( d'4. es'8 | f'2.)\p r4 | }
```
The countersubject now lies at B4–E♭6, clear of the subject. At 35–38 the head sounds in one octave, played by both violin sections, above the violas' and cellos' countersubject. Critic 7 protected "27–34 bass statement with countersubject". This change keeps every pitch of both lines and only removes a doubling, so it does not undo that.

### 2. 70–73 (and 190–193): the counter-melody lands on the tune's pitch on strong beats (applied)
Problem. The violas' and cellos' counter under F2 in E♭ major reaches the tune's pitch class on the downbeats of 70 (C under C), 71 (A♭ under A♭) and 73 (G under G), and on beat 3 of 68 and 74. In the eight bars where it should be most independent, the counter merges with the tune in octaves five times, so it sounds like a lower doubling that drops in and out rather than a second line. The recapitulation (190–193 in A♭, the cellos) repeats the same pattern.
Evidence. Adler PDF 44 points out "the wonderful independence" of Schoenberg's trombones against the trumpets and winds (XI-25, PDF 48–49), where no two lines share a note on a strong beat. In Bartók XI-24 the free counterpoint against each fugato entry moves mostly in thirds, sixths and tenths. Cycles 27 and 51 already made this counter-melody move against the tune's rhythm. This finding does the same for its intervals.
Fix (applied in sTwo.ily and recS.ily). Exposition, Vla and Vc:
```
70: es'4( f'4 g'2)   (was c'4( d'4 es'2))
71: es'2( c'4 es'4)  (was aes2( c'4 es'4))  E♭ is the 7th of ii7 over F and falls to D at 72
73: bes2( c'4 bes4)  (was g2( bes4 aes4))
```
Recapitulation, Vc, in the C template inside `\transpose c aes,`: 190 `c'4( d'4 e'2)`, 191 `c'2( a4 c'4)`, 193 `g2( a4 g4)`. The counter now sits a sixth or a tenth under the tune on every downbeat of 70–73. My first version of 73 (`bes2( g4 aes4)`) gave fifths with the bass at E♭–C; the version above has none (p5.py shows no new fifths). Bars 75–82 are unchanged, and so is the F2 tune.

### 3. 103–104: the second statement of the F1 head stays in the same group; the brass only punctuate (applied)
Problem. The development opens with F1's head in F minor in the low strings and Bn 2 (99), and repeats it in B♭ minor in the same instruments (103). At both entries the horns and trombones play a single quarter-note chord on the downbeat. The repeat at 103 adds nothing to the texture except the violins' stretto entry on beat 4, and the brass never take part in the counterpoint until 110.
Evidence. Adler PDF 44: in the Bartók the fugato "is presented by the trumpets and trombones, then inverted by the horns plus the entire choir", so each new statement comes in a new colour. The image on PDF 45 shows each entry marked "f, ben marc.". The same page gives the weight rule: two horns in unison equal one trombone. Principle 15 (PDF 52) asks for the brass to be treated as equals.
Fix (applied in dev.ily). Hn 3·4 in unison double the violas' B♭4 head, and Tbn 1 doubles the cellos' B♭3 head, in place of the punctuation chord. Tbn 2, the bass trombone and the timpani keep their downbeat stroke. The phrase ends on the strings' sf chord at 105.
```
devHnThree: ... | bes'2->(\f f'4. ges'8 | aes'4-.) ges'8( f'8 es'4-.) des'4-. | f'4-. r4 r2 | R1 | ...
devHnFour:  ... | bes'2->(\f f'4. ges'8 | aes'4-.) ges'8( f'8 es'4-.) des'4-. | des'4-. r4 r2 | R1 | ...
devTbnOne:  ... | bes2->(\f f4. ges8 | aes4-.) ges8( f8 es4-.) des4-. | f4-. r4 r2 | R1 | ...
```
Statement 1 is now strings alone and statement 2 is strings plus brass: a scoring crescendo toward the lament and the ff at 110. The two ideas stay apart at 99–102 (cycle 17). There are no trumpets and no C-major sound. The brass stay at f, as c075 set at 103. The violins' x at 103–104 (F6/F5, mf) lies well above the brass (D♭3–B♭4).

### 4. 27–38: the pads are at f in the subject's register (applied)
Problem. Under the ff bass statement the flutes, oboes, clarinets and Hn 1·2 hold whole-note harmony at f. Hn 2 and Cl 2 start on C4, the subject's own first note (in Hn 3·4, the same instrument), and then hold B3–B♭3–C4 inside the line.
Evidence. Adler PDF 43 (Hindemith, mm. 165–188): when a voice takes the subject, the other brass drop to p and their repeated chords are marked p. Cycle 4 made the same choice for 11–26 (Hn 1·2 mf under F1).
Fix (applied in pTwo.ily). Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 and Hn 1·2 are mf at 27 (all shared staves change together, so their dynamics stay equal). The existing diminuendo at 39–41 to p is unchanged. The trombones, bass trombone and contrabassoon stay f at 35.
