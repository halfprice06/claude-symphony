## Findings

### 1. 263-264: the strings' scale figure is buried under a held fff tutti; the move to 265 has no break-off (applied)

**Problem.** At 263 the strings play the opening scale figure (bars 3-4) in C major, the upbeat that leads into F1 at 265 (c080). In v128 every wind and brass player (21 parts) holds the fff cadence chord through 264 beat 3, the timpani roll fff through all of 264, and Vc/Cb hold C fff through 264. Then at 265 the foundation is f and the themes ff. So (a) the figure, a single eighth-note line in the violins and violas between B4 and G5, sounds under trumpets, horns, trombones and woodwinds all at fff in the same register, and (b) the bass and timpani go from fff straight to f on the downbeat of 265 with nothing to make the change.

**Evidence.** Adler's Tchaikovsky example (PDF 38-40, Ex. XI-21): the big climax "suddenly breaks off, and the strings dwindle down to the cellos and basses only", and only then the tutti returns with the timpani roll. Adler PDF 190 (Ex. XV-36): a drop from a loud chord to a soft level works when the loud choir is cut off over sound that is already at the new level. The score's own model, bars 1-4, does exactly this: tutti chord, cut-off at 2 beat 1, figure alone in the strings. At 263-264 the figure keeps its notes but not its texture.

**Fix (applied in sections/finale.ily).**
- Harmony winds and brass and timpani release on 264 beat 1: B.cl `c4 r4 r2`, Bn 1·2 `c'4 r4 r2` / `c4 r4 r2`, Cbsn `c,,4 r4 r2`, Hn 1-4 `c'4 / c'4 / e'4 / g4 r4 r2`, Tpt 3 `e'4 r4 r2`, Tbn 1·2 `c'4 / g4 r4 r2`, B.tbn `c4 r4 r2`, tuba `c,4 r4 r2`, timpani `c1:32\fff~ | c4 r4 r2`.
- The chorale top line keeps its full length (protected line, c117/c121): Tpt 1·2 `c''2.\> r4\!`, Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 the same pattern (`c'''2.\> r4\!` etc.), so the line's last C and the woodwind colour stay but give way to the figure.
- Vc `c1\>` and Cb `c,1\>` in 264, reaching ff (Vc, F1) and f (Cb, foundation) at 265: the bass "dwindles" under the figure, and 265's f foundation is reached by a diminuendo instead of a jump.

This extends c078 (release before 265) and does not undo it; it does not touch the chorale's pitches, the cadence 262→263, the fff at 263 or the cymbal.

### 2. 257-259: the string accompaniment steps from f to ff at 259 while the winds and the top line crescendo into it (applied)

**Problem.** In the last chorale the winds enter at 257 mf with a hairpin to f at 259, then crescendo again to fff at 263; the brass hold ff and crescendo from 259. Vln II (with Cl 1 in unison on its arpeggio since c127), Vla, Vc and Cb stay flat f through 255-258 and are then marked `\ff\<` at 259. Cl 1 crescendos from mf at 257 inside a Vln II line that does not move, and at 259 the strings jump a level while everything around them arrives there by hairpin.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 192: at a change of colour or level between groups "make certain that the seams don't show". PDF 187: instruments are brought in so the line is smooth. In this passage the seam at 259 is in the strings only.

**Fix (applied).** Hairpins from 257 into 259's ff: Vln II `a'8(\< c'' e'' a'') …`, Vla `c'4\< c' c' c'`, Vc `a,1\<~`, Cb `a,,8\< a,,8 …`. c088's plan (strings f at 255, ff at 259) stays; the step becomes a two-bar crescendo, as in the winds.

### 3. 247: contrabassoon f under the tuba's ff on the same line (applied)

**Problem.** At R (247-254) the contrabassoon and the tuba play the same bass line at the same pitch (`c,1 | d,1 | f,,1 | g,,1 | e,2 c,2 | …`). c123 raised the tuba to ff at 247; the contrabassoon stayed f. The weaker instrument in its weakest register is marked a level below the one it doubles, in an ff tutti (Cb, timpani, F1 heads all ff).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: the choice of instrument for a line depends on the dynamic it has to carry and on the instrument's strength in that register. A doubling marked softer than its partner in a ff tutti does not sound as a doubling.

**Fix (applied).** `finaleCbsn = { c,1\ff | …`.

### 4. 275-277: B.cl, Cbsn and B.tbn hairpins run across their rest bar (applied)

**Problem.** These three crescendo on the held C at 275-276, rest at 277 and re-enter fff at 278. The hairpin is ended only by the fff, so it is drawn across the empty bar 277 (page 16 of v128).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 195: dynamics "must be strictly observed"; a hairpin over a rest asks for a crescendo nobody plays. HISTORY c068 already put dynamics on notes, not rests.

**Fix (applied).** `R1\!` at 277 in finaleBassCl, finaleCbsn, finaleBassTbn. Bn 1 also rests at 277 but shares a staff with Bn 2, who plays there, so its hairpin stays as it is (shared-staff rule).
