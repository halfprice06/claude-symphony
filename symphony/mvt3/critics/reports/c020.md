## Findings

### 1. The major-third cycle is never closed: there is no III♯ in the C-major pages (235–247). Applied.

**Problem.** Movement I ends in C and movement II is in A♭, with F♭/E as its recurring colour (II 62–64, 78, 88). Together that is C – A♭ – E, three terms of the equal division of the octave. Movement III v20 has no E-major chord: its only G♯ is a passing note in the counter-line at 239. The cycle stops one step short of its return to C.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 450 (Ex. 26-9, Brahms Op. 117/1) and PDF 457 point 7: 19th-century composers brought the minor pattern I – tonicized III – V into major. PDF 395 points 5–6: in major the III is usually the secondary-mixture III♯ in the arpeggiation I–III♯–V, approached by II6. PDF 445–446 (§4, Ex. 26-4a): the path III → II6 → cadential V 6/4–5/3 → I is the textbook way to drive to a tonic.

**Where.** I put it in the S head and not in the plagal close 231–234. The close is protected (c012, c019), its woodwind canon carries the chorale top line C–D♭–C at 229–230, and C/D♭ cannot stand against E–G♯–B. In the S head the second phrase already runs 239 vi – 240 IV – 241 ii – 242 V6/5 of V – 243 cadential 6/4 – 246 V4/3 – 247 I.

**Fix (applied, sHead.ily).**
- 241: bass `d1` (ii for the whole bar; was `d2 f,2`).
- 242: bass `f,1`, so the harmony is II6/5 (F–A–C–D). The melody d'''–c''' and the counter c'–d' are unchanged and are chord tones. The harp LH has `f,,1` and the "F♯" pedal mark is removed.
- 243 first half: E major, with the trumpets' and horns' E of the horn call as its root. Second half: the cadential 6/4. V lasts to 246 and I follows at 247. That gives I (235) – II6 – III♯ – V – I.
- Vc and Bsn 2: `d1 | f,1 | e,2\< g,2`. Cb: `d,1 | f,,1 | e,,2\< g,,2`.
- Vln I: `<gis' e''>2\< <g' e''>2`. This is the only G♯, and it moves to G in the same voice, so there is no split cross relation (PDF 394) and no doubled chromatic tone.
- Vln II: `<e' e''>2\< <c' e''>2`. Vla: `b2\< c'2`. Cl 1: `b2\< c'2`. Bsn 1: `b2\< c'2`.
- Timpani: `r2 g,2:32\p\<`. The drums are tuned G, A♭, C, E♭, so they have no E and wait for the G.

**Voice leading.** F→E in the bass and A (Hn 4, harp) → G♯ show II6 → III♯ with no G→G♯ step (point 6). The G♯ heard at 239 in the counter-line foreshadows the chord, as the B♮–C in K. 498 foreshadows its tonicization (PDF 448). The trumpets' E is now harmonised by its own major triad, which states the E♮ thread in its brightest form on the way to C.

**Answering c003.** This undoes c003's V6/5 of V at 242 (the bass's 4̂–♯4̂–5̂). Its stated aim was to drive into the cadential 6/4 rather than close on I6. II6/5 – III♯ – 6/4 has the same effect: there is no I6, and the B of III♯ rises to C in the 6/4.

**Rules kept.** No root V–I is added: 238 V7 → 239 vi is deceptive, and 246 V4/3 → 247 I is inverted. The chorale top lines, 223, and 261–263 are untouched. The S-head texture (c017) and the horn call are unchanged.

**Checks.** allpairs_par.py finds no new parallel fifths or octaves. The only new entries are bass doublings and the octave doubling of the violin melody at 242→243. A first voicing, with Vln I and Vln II on B over the bass's F→E, gave fifths C/F → B/E, and I removed it.
