## Findings

### 1. 107-110: the woodwind join the crescendo into the development's climax (applied)

**Bars.** 107-110.

**Problem.** The lament bass (107-109) crescendos from p to ff with the strings, Bn 2 and Cbsn only. At 110 the flutes, oboes, clarinets, all four horns, three trumpets, trombones and tuba enter together, ff. So the movement's one prolonged crescendo inside the development skips a stage. The strings rise, and then everything else arrives at once on the climax chord. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Prolonged orchestral crescendi are obtained by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass", and the brass are the group that turns "crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". PDF 127 explains how to add a group: "While the first group effects the crescendo gradually, the second group enters piano or pianissimo and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes." Dvořák 9/iv bars 8-9 do the same: the winds enter f with a crescendo two bars before the ff at 10. This is the development's climax (PDF 116: whether a passage "forms a climax or merely a step"), so how it is reached shapes the whole development.

**Fix (dev.ily).** Ob 1 and Cl 1 double Vln II's suspension line at the unison from 108, p <, into their existing ff notes at 110. Each gets a score-only "1." because its partner rests.
- Ob 1, 107-110: `R1 | g''2~\p\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." g''4( f''4~ | f''2. e''4) | g''1\ff~`
- Cl 1, 107-110: `\partCombineAutomatic R1 | g''2~\p\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." g''4( f''4~ | f''2. e''4) | c''1\ff`

The line is the 4-3 suspension G5→F5 over D at 108.3-108.4, and F5→E5 into the cadential chord at 109. I used one oboe and one clarinet in unison, the pairing PDF 115 gives for a soft woodwind line. The brass still enter only at 110, ff, after rests of 7 to 16 bars: the fresh ff entry that PDF 136 recommends.

**Why this is allowed in a protected passage.** c212 protected the lament bass at 107-110 for its harmony. The bass, the harmony and the string parts are unchanged, and no new pitch class is added. par8.py (85-112) shows no new parallel in any part against the bass; the only entries are the existing octave doublings of the bass. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. The flutes stay out because B5-C6 at p would not be heard.

### 2. 89: the horns' re-entry after 46 bars is mf (applied)

**Bars.** 89-91.

**Problem.** Hn 3-4 stop at 42 and return at 89 with the off-beat accented quarters of the 6/3 bridge. Hn 1-2 play the same figure after a two-bar rest. Both pairs are marked mf <, under strings at f, and reach ff at 91. PDF 136: "re-introducing these instruments mezzo-forte or mezzo-piano produces a colourless and common-place effect". The codetta at 91 is the exposition's last tutti and the brass's first ff in the movement. Here the horns are the group that is added (PDF 127), and they should start below the strings and close the gap quickly.

**Fix (clos.ily, bar 89).** Change `\mf\<` to `\p\<` in all four horns:
- Hn 1: `r4 aes'4->\p\<`
- Hn 2: `r4 f'4->\p\<`
- Hn 3: `r4 c'4->\p\<`
- Hn 4: `r4 aes4->\p\<`

Both shared staves keep equal dynamics. No note changed. 83-90 was protected by c212 for its sequence harmony, which this change does not touch.
