### 5. Tested and not applied: tonicizing IV by melodic figuration at 270–272 (A&S PDF 446 sec. 5; PDF 451 sec. 11; PDF 457 Point 9)
Bars: 270–272.
The technique: this is the one technique in the reading that the score uses only weakly. In the C-major coda (223–279) IV is never tonicized. A&S says a tonicized IV "creates no problems of tonal balance" late in a major piece (PDF 451). The mildest form is a top line that borrows tones from the key in which the following chord would be tonic (PDF 446). c373 declined a tonicized IV here partly because "271 has B♮".
The test: the major F1 already has B♭ at 270 (C7 over the pedal). I kept it in 271, so 270–272 becomes an F-major scale line into the IV6/4 at 272. In finale.ily, all ten statements of F1's third bar at 271 were changed: `g''8 a''8 b''4` → `g''8 a''8 bes''4`, and the same at the other octaves, in Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1·2 and Tpt 1·2.
Result: the build had 0 warnings, 16 pages and 0 outer-voice parallels, and par8.py found nothing at 269–273. But the MIDI shows B♭5/B♭4 at 271.5–271.75 against B3 at 271.875 in Hn 3·4, Tbn 1·2, Bn 1, violas and cellos. Those parts play movement I's head in major (G–E–D–C–B–C, quotation 11), so the change creates a ff cross relation half a beat apart. The change also removes F1's own rising leading tone (D–G–A–B–D) from its last statement. The B♮ cannot leave the lower head without changing the quotation. Not better, so reverted. F1 is unchanged.
