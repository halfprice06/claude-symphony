## Findings

### 1. 71.4–72.1 and 191.4–192.1: F2's second phrase gets the escape-tone + accented-passing-tone idiom (applied; this changes F2)

- **Problem.** Ex. 20-17 is the one figuration idiom of the unit that the movement never uses. c271 looked for a place in F1 and F2 and found none outside protected bars, but it missed one. In both violin statements of F2, the second phrase moves by step across a chord change, from C6 at 71.4 (over ii7) to B♭5 at 72.1 (over V7). In A♭ the same step is F5 at 191.4 to E♭5 at 192.1. Bars 71–72 are plain quarters and repeat 55–56 of the solo note for note. Three bars later, the turn at 75 is F2's only neighbour figure, and it is heard once.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 337, Ex. 20-17: the idiom, an escape tone followed by an accented passing tone, decorates a falling second. In example (b) it is C–D | C–B in eighths across a change of chord.
  - PDF 333: the neighbour figure "is often treated motivically".
  - PDF 336: a leap emphasises a dissonance, so the figure belongs in the tune.
  - PDF 343–345, exercise 4: vary a theme by figuration.
  - The new figure C–D–C–B♭ has the shape of 75's turn (B♭–C–B♭–A♭) a step higher and twice as slow. The second phrase therefore prepares the turn, and the turn becomes a motive instead of a single ornament.
- **Fix** (applied):

  ```
  sTwo.ily  sTwoVlnOne  aes''2.( c'''4) | bes''4( aes''4 g''4 f''4)
                     →  aes''2.( c'''8 d'''8) | c'''8( bes''8 aes''4 g''4 f''4)
            sTwoVlnTwo  the same an octave lower
  recS.ily  recSVlnOne, recSVlnTwo (written in C, \transpose c aes,):
            { f''2.( a''4) } | { g''4( f''4 e''4 d''4)… }
         →  { f''2.( a''8 b''8) } | { a''8( g''8 f''4 e''4 d''4)… }
            recSVla, recSHnOne: the same an octave lower
  ```

- **Voice leading.** D6 at 71.4.5 is a dissonant escape tone: a sixth over ii7 and a major seventh above the violas' and cellos' E♭4. C6 on 72.1 is the ninth over V7 and resolves to B♭5 on the off-beat, in contrary motion to the harp's F4–B♭4. In A♭, G5 makes a tritone with Cl 1's D♭4, and F5 on 192.1 is the ninth over E♭7. par8.py shows no change at 70–73 or 190–194. allpairs_par.py lists only the tune's own octave doublings (Vln I/II at 71–72; violins, violas and Hn 1 at 191–192).
- **Decisions.**
  - The trumpet solo (51–66) and the muted trumpet with cor anglais (171–186) stay plain, as cycle 1 decided.
  - Bowing keeps one slur per bar. The accented passing tone starts the new bow, which gives it its stress.
- **For the other threads:** F2 changes in its two major-key violin statements, at 71–72 and 191–192 (violins, and in A♭ also the violas and Hn 1). The head, the solo, the turn at 75 and the coda phrase 255–258 are unchanged. No record line covers these bars.

### 2. 19.3: Cl 1's chordal skip makes fifths with F1's head (applied)

- **Problem.** On the first bar of F1's consequent, the head falls C4→G3 in Hn 1·2 (f), the lower octave of the tune. At the same moment Cl 1's broken chord goes G4 (19.2.5) → D4 (19.3), so the fifth C/G moves to the fifth G/D. p5.py flags it (Hn 1·2 and violas). The tune's notes are the head's main notes and the arrival falls on a strong beat. This is not the weak-position case of Ex. 20-6.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 331: "5ths and octaves created by chordal skips count as faulty voice leading and must be avoided. Never write chordal skips without checking for forbidden parallels" (Ex. 20-5). PDF 341: 5ths produced by a consonant tone, "like those produced by a chordal skip … you should avoid them".
- **Fix** (pOne.ily, pOneClOne, applied): `es'8( g' c'' g') d'8( g' es' g')` → `es'8( g' c'' g') b8( g' es' g')`. B3 is the third of c313's passing V6/4 over D. The violas take it over on 19.3.5, so the leading tone is not doubled at the same moment. p5.py no longer reports bar 19, and par8.py at 18–20 is unchanged.

### 3. 191→192: Cl 2 in octaves with F2's falling step (applied)

- **Problem.** In the A♭ statement, Cl 2's pp held line (c208) fell F3→E♭3 at 192.1 in octaves with the tune's F→E♭ in the violins, the violas and Hn 1 (allpairs_par.py, beat 764 in v361). After Finding 1 the tune reaches E♭ half a beat later. A&S says that is no cure.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 342, sec. 17: octaves "require a stronger tonal event than do passing or neighboring tones". Review point 11 (PDF 343): "No passing or neighboring tone can break up octaves or unisons."
- **Fix** (recS.ily, recSClTwo, applied): `f1 | es1~ | es2 f2` → `f1 | g1 | es2 f2`. Cl 2 rises to G3, the third of V7, against the falling tune, then returns to E♭3 on 193.1 under I. The doubling of the 7th's resolution, the dynamics and the clarinet figure in Cl 1 are unchanged. allpairs_par.py no longer lists beat 764.

### 4. 255: the turn in F2's coda phrase (considered, not applied)

- **Point considered.** 255–258 is F2's 75–78 a sixth lower, and c351 restored 78's dotted rhythm at 258 so the theme is recognised. The same reasoning would put 75's turn on 255.3.
- **Why not.** At 255 the line is G3 in violas, cellos, Hn 3·4 and Bn 1. The turn's lower neighbour F3 would sound against E3 in Tbn 2 and the bass trombone and E2 in the tuba and basses, under the ff brass chorale. That is a semitone in the low register, unlike the airy B♭5 at 75. The figure would also blur against the horns' doubled line.
- **Decision.** Not applied. With Finding 1 the neighbour figure is now heard at 71–72, 75 and 191–192.

### 5. 272→273: F1's major peak (checked, no change)

- **Point considered.** In minor, the consequent's peak A♭6 is the seventh of vii°7 over B, a leaned-on dissonance (PDF 336, 338). In major, the peak A6 at 272 is a chord tone of IV6/4 over the C pedal, so the climax has less charge. The flutes' and Vln I's F6 then drops to C6.
- **Why no change.** The F does resolve: Cl 1·2 move A5–G5–F5 → E5 at 273, with E4 in the violas, bassoons and Hn 4. This is the implied resolution in an inner voice that PDF 330 (Ex. 20-3) allows. Making the peak dissonant would mean replacing c123's IV6/4, which sits inside the protected 263–279 progression. The protected top C6 at 273–274 rules out an E6 there. No change.

### 6. 12.3, 16.1, 156.1, 160.3: other surface fifths (checked, kept)

p5.py also reports these. At 12.3 and 160.3 the tune's G is a dissonant unaccented passing tone (PDF 341, Ex. 20-25: such 5ths "create no ill effect"). At 16.1 and 156.1 the clarinet's G4 and the tune's C4 are both last eighths before the ♭II6 downbeat, and the C is a dissonant passing tone. This is the weak-position case of Ex. 20-6 (PDF 331). c032 kept them, and I agree.
