## Findings

### 1. 15→16 and 155→156: parallel fifths into the Neapolitan, created by the sequential restatement (applied)

**Problem.** F1's bars 15–16 repeat bars 11–12 a third higher: the head sequence that leads into the N6 (D♭/F) at 16.1. The clarinet figure keeps its own shape (`es'8( g' bes' g')`), so its last eighth G4 steps to A♭4 on 16.1. At the same moment the theme's lower octave (Hn 1·2, violas) passes C4→D♭4. The result is open parallel fifths, G4/C4 to A♭4/D♭4, on the downbeat of the Neapolitan. The recapitulation repeats them at 155.4.5→156.1 under the same figure. p5.py flags both (16.1 and 156.1). The same figure in 11→12 makes no fifths: the pattern's repetition created them.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267, §5: "all the voices above a sequential bass are themselves sequential. And with any repetitive patterns where all the voices participate, 5ths and octaves can become a problem. Therefore, voice-leading chords play an essential role." See also Ex. 17-1, sequence A, where the chord on the fourth eighth "prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". c032 kept fifths at "12–20, 152–165" because they were "figuration within one chord, not a line". That reason does not hold here. The chord changes at 16.1 (E♭/G to D♭/F), and G4→A♭4 is a step into the clarinet's held half note (whole note at 156), so it is a line.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-572/work).** Cl 1, last eighth of 15 and 155: E♭4 instead of G4. The group still arpeggiates E♭/G and stays under Vln II's tune. The clarinet then leaps a fourth up to A♭4 against the theme's C4→D♭4 (a third, then a fifth, so no parallels).
- sections/pOne.ily, `pOneClOne`, bar 15: `es'8( g' bes' g')` → `es'8( g' bes' es')`
- sections/recP.ily, `recPClOne`, bar 155: `es'8( g' bes' g')` → `es'8( g' bes' es')`

The theme, bass, harmony, dynamics and beaming are unchanged. Against the other parts: E♭4 is in unison with Hn 3's offbeat E♭4, which then rests, and at 155 it lies above Tbn 1's E♭3 with no parallel octave into 156.

### 2. 12.2.5→12.3: Cl 2's held D4 moves with the theme's passing G3 in fifths (applied)

**Problem.** In bar 12, Cl 2 holds D4 and falls to C4 on beat 3 (VII6 → iv). The theme's lower octave (Hn 1·2, violas) passes G3→F3 at the same moment, so the motion is D4/G3 to C4/F3. p5.py flags it at 12.3. The D4 also doubles the bass of the 6/3 chord B♭/D.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267, as above. The reading's 6/3 settings (Ex. 17-2c, 17-4, PDF 265–269) double the root or the fifth of a 6/3 chord in the upper voices, not its bass. As in Finding 1, the chord changes, so c032's "within one chord" reason does not apply.

**Fix (applied).** sections/pOne.ily, `pOneClTwo`, bar 12: `d'2( c'2)` → `bes2( c'2)`. Cl 2 now moves C4–B♭3–C4 out of bar 11. The outer motion against the theme is a third, then a fifth; B♭3 is the root of VII6, and the parallel fifths are gone. The same pair at 160.3 (recapitulation, Cl 2 D4→C4 against Hn G3→F3) is inside the protected 159–166 and has a different harmony (c342 changed only 20). I leave it, as c032 did.

### 3. 19–20: a complete falling-third (descending 5-6) sequence under the consequent (tested on paper, declined)

The only unprotected stepwise descending bass in a main theme is 19–20, E♭–D–C–B♭. Fully sequential, it would read i6 → v6 → VI → III6 (PDF 276–278, Ex. 17-15, 17-16). That would replace 19.3's passing V6/4, which supports the tune's G5. It would also replace 20's v6 (the natural minor dominant that Ex. 17-17 itself uses in minor) and c342's rising °7s at 20.3–4. The tune's G5 over a C bass would turn the passing chord into a static i. The current setting already follows PDF 278, with a descending bass harmonized in 5-6 fashion that breaks off before the cadence. Not applied.

### 4. A third statement at 99–106 or 205–208 (declined)

PDF 265 says sequences usually have three statements or two plus the start of a third. Both passages have two statements, then their goal: the lament at 107, and the German-sixth area at 209. c383 rejected a D♭ third statement at 103–106, because D♭ belongs to the coda (205–223). 205–210 is protected (c970). Both passages also fit the book's "two plus" pattern, so they are not faults.

### 5. An ascending-5ths sequence, the one type the score does not use (declined)

PDF 270–272 (I–V–ii–vi–iii, soprano steps down with suspensions) suits a rising build. The candidate places are the dominant preparation at 7–10, the G-major build at 129–131 and the stringendo at 239–242. They are a G pedal (protected timpani and entries), protected harp notes (123–130, 130–132) and the protected S head (235–247). A rising-fifths chain in a C-major coda would also call up the E-major chord at 243 too early, which is the reason an earlier cycle declined a tonicizing sequence at 235–242 (HISTORY.md, "Not applied: ... a descending-third sequence of tonicized chords at 235–242"). Not applied.

### 6. 6-5 syncopes in place of 7-6 at the lament, 107–110 (declined)

PDF 280 (Brahms Op. 117/3) offers a descending 6-5 series as an alternative. At 107–110 the protected lament bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C carries 7-6 suspensions. They are more dissonant and give a stronger pull into the ff climax at 110 than 6-5s would. Not applied.
