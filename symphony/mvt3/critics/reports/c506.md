## Findings

### 1. 99: the development opens on F minor, and the timpani repeat the old bass C although the F drum is already tuned (applied)

- **Problem.** Bars 97–98 hold C in the basses (p, last desk). At 99 the bass moves to F, and the development starts f in F minor with F1's head in violas, cellos and basses. The bass trombone strikes F2. The timpani play C3 (`c4->\f`). The 30" drum was retuned G→F in the rest at 95 (c416), but F is first used only at 113. So on the downbeat where the bass resolves C→F, the timpani keep the dominant of the bar before. They double only the fifth that Hn 3 and Tbn 1 hold above.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 79: the timpani "strengthen the tonic and dominant bass notes", and pedal drums "greatly enhanced" what they can do.
  - PDF 58: Strauss's out-of-harmony notes were a result of no time to retune, and no one would write them on modern drums. C at 99 is in the chord, so it is not that error. But the reason for using the fifth is gone: the tonic is on a tuned drum, and the drum is free.
  - Ex. XII-46 (PDF 79): F2 lies in the middle of the 30" drum.
- **Fix** (applied, sections/dev.ily):
  ```
  devTimp = { f,4->\f r4 r2 | R1*7 | R1*3 | c4\f r4 c4 r4 | …
  ```
  - With a comment line tagged c506 above the variable.
  - The stroke now doubles the bass trombone's F2 and the string bass on the tonic. The F-minor area has the timpani on F at its first bar (99) and at its cadence (113).
  - The muta at 95 and the return to G at 114 are unchanged. No other part changes.
- **Not changed.** 103, the head in B♭ minor, stays without timpani (no B♭ or F tonic role; B♭ went back to A♭ at 95). 111.1 keeps C, as c416 decided.

The closing group starts at f with no trumpets, and the timpani are coupled with the trumpets (PDF 79).
