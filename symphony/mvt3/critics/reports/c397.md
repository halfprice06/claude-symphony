## Findings

### 1. 145-146: the stretto's last entry has no brass voice and is covered by held brass in its own octave (applied)
**Problem.** The retransition's stretto passes F1's head on G through the groups: Hn 1 (139), cellos (140), Ob 1 and Cl 1 (141), Vln II (142), Hn 1·2 a2 with Vln I (143), and then a woodwind-string tutti at 145 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Bn 1, Vln I·II, violas, G3 to G6). At 145-146 the brass do not take part. Six brass notes sit inside the head's lower octaves, all at f: Hn 1 G4, Hn 2 D4, Hn 3 F4, Hn 4 D4, Tbn 1 F4 and Tbn 2 G3. The head's G4 octave (Cl 1, violas) and G3 octave (Bn 1) are therefore masked, and only the woodwind and violin octaves above come through. Hn 3·4 re-enter at 145 after resting 143-144 (c175), but only to hold a chord.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 42 (Hindemith, Ex. XI-23): every entry is placed so that the subject "comes through clearly every time it is played".
- PDF 44 and Ex. XI-24 (PDF 46-47, mm. 342-364): the subject passes between brass groups and the horn pairs enter a2 "f, ben marc." in turn. PDF 44: two horns in unison equal one trumpet or one trombone.
- PDF 41: brass counterpoint means imitation, not held blocks.

The score already alternates the horns at 139 (Hn 1) and 143 (Hn 1·2). The fourth entry is the natural place for the other pair.

**Fix (applied, sections/retr.ily).** `retrHnThree` and `retrHnFour`, bars 145-146: `f'1\f | f'1 \breathe |` and `d'1\f | d'1 \breathe |` become
`g'2->(\f^\markup \italic "ben marc." d'4. es'8 | f'4-.) es'8( d'8 c'4-.) b4-. \breathe |` in both parts (a2 at G4, with Cl 1 and the violas, an octave above Bn 1).

The V7 stays complete in the held brass: Hn 1 G4-F4, Hn 2 D4, Tbn 1 F4, Tbn 2 G3 (c374's gap-filler kept) and B.tbn G2. The B bass is unchanged. The entry bar, rhythm, f, the rest at 143-144, the breath after 146 and the horns' D4 at 147-150 are unchanged.

**Answer to the protection.** The stretto 139-150 is protected (c197, c297).
- c197 declined a trombone entry at 143 because it "would double the horns' head in the same bar and thicken the texture before 147". At 145 the head is not in the horns, no instrument is added, and the brass still have six parts: two moving and four held, where before all six held.
- c297 kept the stretto because overlapping entries "would blur" the two-bar crescendo steps. I add no overlap: the 145 entry starts where it did.

**Checks.**
- par8 (141-148): the only output is 147.1, the cellos' B2→G2 octave with the basses. It is identical in v397.
- c002/par.py: the only new pairs are Bn 1 against Hn 3·4 at 145.4.5 and 146.2.5. They are the octave doubling of the same head.
- p5.py: unchanged.

F1's notes are not changed; only the scoring of this one head entry is.

### 2. 103: the brass answer of F1's head is not marked as the leading voice (applied)
At 103-104 Hn 3·4 a2 (B♭4) and Tbn 1 (B♭3) answer the violas' F-minor head, and the violins' scale figure overlaps them (c017). Bartók marks every brass entry "f, ben marc." (PDF 45-47). The score does this only at 37 (c297). c097 cited the marking for this bar but did not print it.

**Fix (applied, sections/dev.ily):** in `devHnThree`, `devHnFour` and `devTbnOne`, `bes'2->(\f` / `bes2->(\f` becomes `bes'2->(\f^\markup \italic "ben marc."` (and the same for Tbn 1). Text only; MIDI unchanged; page 6 has room.

I also tried the marking on Hn 1·2 at 143. It made LilyPond compress page 7 ("page 7 has been compressed"), so I took it out. The 145 entry carries the marking instead.

### 3. Inversion of F1's head by the horns (Bartók, PDF 44): considered, not applied
The finale never inverts F1. In Bartók the inversion follows statements of the subject in its original form, as a combination; it does not replace one. The places where an inverted head could sound against the original are 99-106, 139-146, 205-208 and 265-272. All four are protected, and each already carries the head in its original form in every voice. c297's reason still holds: the head's falling fourth is what makes the stretto and the coda entries recognisable. I did not apply it.

### 4. Staggered horn figure as an accompaniment layer (Hindemith mm. 173-180, PDF 43): considered, not applied
This is the one texture in the reading that the score has never used. The bars where it would fit are already scored:
- 7-10 is a crescendo built by staggered sustained entries (c279, c188), with motive x in the timpani.
- 129-130 has Hn 3·4's repeated quarters (c037, c372).
- 209-210 has the held A♭7 build (c179 after three rescorings).

A rippling horn figure in any of these would replace a layer that works, so I did not add one.

### 5. 130-132: F1's head in one horn (two horns = one trumpet, PDF 44): checked, no change
Hn 2 plays the head with the violas, inside Hn 1, Hn 3 and Hn 4 held at f. c154 doubled it with Hn 4 and c159 undid that. c159's answer still stands: the violas carry the head, and the repeated-quarter layer makes the entry stand out by rhythm. A third reversal is not justified.

### 6. Register of every brass thematic entry (PDF 42): checked, no change
Every brass statement lies in a good register (see row 4). The one low one is Tbn 1·2 a2 on the P head at 247-250 (G2-G3). It is deliberate: the horns' S head occupies E4-A4 at the same moment, and the cellos and Bn 1 double the trombones. The layout is protected (c071, c091, c191, c197).

### 7. Mutes, stopped horn and the sf > p introduction (PDF 50-54): checked, no change
Every stopped or muted passage is cancelled at the next entry, and both soft brass solos are introduced by stopped horns. Nothing is missing.
