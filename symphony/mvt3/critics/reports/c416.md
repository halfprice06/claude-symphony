## Findings

### 1. 91–94: the timpani fall silent on every dominant of the E♭ codetta (applied)
- **Problem.** The ff codetta hammers I–V in E♭ in the trumpets, bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon, cellos and basses (`es,4 r8 es,8 bes,,4 r4` ×2, then `r4 es,2 bes,,4`). The timpani play only the E♭: `es4 r8 es8 r2 | es4 r8 es8 r2 | r4 es2 r4`. They stop on 91.3, 92.3 and 93.4, the beats where the trumpets and tuba strike V, because the 28" drum went back from B♭ to A♭ at 43.
- **Why it matters.** A♭ is not needed again until 110, so the gap is a tuning accident. It is the kind of hole Adler (PDF 58) cites from Strauss: a gap left because there was no time to retune. Here there were 47 bars of rest.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 79: the timpani "strengthen the tonic and dominant bass notes and participate in strong tutti passages, especially in consummating climactic cadence points", coupled with the trumpets; pedal drums make the change easy. Adler PDF 81, Ex. XII-51: mark the tuning change.
- **Fix** (applied):
  - trans.ily: `transTimp = { R1*8 | }` (the "muta B♭ in A♭" at 43 is removed).
  - clos.ily:
    ```
    closTimp = { R1*5 | \tag #'score { R1*2 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring "vc" #UP { R1*2^\markup \tiny "Vc." } } | es4\f r4 r2 | es4->\ff r8 es8 bes,4-> r4 | es4-> r8 es8 bes,4-> r4 | r4 es2-> bes,4-> | es2-> r2 | R1^\markup \small \italic \column { "muta B♭ in A♭" "muta G in F" } | R1*3 | }
    ```
  - The drum stays on B♭2 from 37 to 94. It returns to A♭ in the 15 bars of rest from 95.
- **What does not change.** c249's rhythm at 93 (I on beat 2, V on beat 4) now also holds in the timpani. No other part changes.

### 2. 113: the F-minor cadence of the development's climax has the timpani on C, not on the tonic F (applied)
- **Problem.** At 112–113 the bass makes V–i in F (`bes,,2 c,2 | f,,2->`) under the ff climax. The timpani play `r2 c4-> c4 | c4-> r4 r2`, so on the cadence's tonic they repeat the dominant C over the tuba, contrabassoon and basses' F1. No F was available on the drums (G, A♭, C, E♭).
- **Evidence.** Same as finding 1 (Adler PDF 79: consummate the cadence on the tonic). Adler Ex. XII-46 (PDF 79): F2 lies well on the 30" drum.
- **Fix** (applied, dev.ily):
  ```
  devTimp = { c4->\f r4 r2 | R1*7 | R1*3 | c4\f r4 c4 r4 | c4-> r4 aes,4-> r4 | r2 c4-> c4 | f,4-> r4 r2 | R1^\markup \small \italic "muta F in G" | …
  ```
  - The G drum goes to F at 95, in the same rest as finding 1's retuning, and back to G at 114. G is next needed at 132, so the player has 18 bars.
  - The timpani now play C–C on the V and F2 on the i, a falling fifth into the bass's F.
- **Protection.** 110–113 is protected for its upper parts, its low octave and the lament. This change touches only the single timpani stroke at 113.1. It moves that stroke onto the pitch the tuba, contrabassoon and basses already play, so it does not touch what those protections guard.
- **Declined.** I kept 111.1 on C, the fifth of F minor, mid-phrase, because it keeps the C–A♭ motion of that bar.
