## Findings

### 1. 43-47: the finale never uses harp harmonics; put three on the transition's B♭ pedal (applied)

Problem. Adler PDF 116 teaches the harmonic as the harp's typical soft colour, "beautifully bell-like", best between written C3 and A5, and only effective "accompanied very lightly or played a cappella". The finale has no harmonics, although both earlier movements that use the harp give it harmonics at their softest point (movement II at 83, the scherzo's last chord at 375). The transition 43-50 is the finale's quietest and barest passage: pp held strings on a B♭ pedal ("change bow at will"), Bn 1 A♭3, and one wind at a time playing motive x (Fl 43-44, Cl 45-46, Ob 47-48), each run starting on beat 2. Beat 1 of 43, 45 and 47 is empty in the winds. The harp is silent from bar 1 to 66, so the first thing the harpist plays is the mf arpeggio at 67.

Evidence. Adler PDF 116 (range, notation, light accompaniment); Rimsky PDF 47 (charm, little resonance, only soft) and PDF 50 (soft peal of bells). The accompaniment at 43-47 is four pp sustained string parts and Bn 1, the "very light" accompaniment Adler asks for. Sounding B♭4 sits between Vln II F4 and the wind runs; it is the pedal tone, so it adds no new harmony and does not touch the viola descent 46-49 (protected since c163): the violas, Vln II and the 9-8 at 49 are unchanged. The three chimes mark the 2+2+2 hand-over and stop before 49, so the Ob 1 A♭5 and Hn 1 entries at 49 and the poco rit. into the trumpet solo are as before.

Fix (in trans.ily):

```
harmOctF = #(define-music-function (m) (ly:music?) #{ \set PianoStaff.midiMaximumVolume = #0.6 \transposition c'' #m \transposition c' \set PianoStaff.midiMaximumVolume = #1.0 #})
transHarpR = { R1*8 | }

(sound 8va)" } r4 r2 | R1 | \harmOctF { bes4-\flageolet } r4 r2 | R1 | \harmOctF { bes4-\flageolet } r4 r2 | R1*3 | }
```

Written B♭3 with the circle, sounding B♭4, as Adler PDF 116 prescribes. It is a left-hand harmonic (palm on the node, thumb plucks), the standard way in this register. The pedal setting at 43 is the E♭-major setting of 67, so the harpist makes no change before 67, and the diagram at 67 (protected, c116) stays as a restatement. Following Adler PDF 112, the part gets the starting setting in letters; this does not reverse c075: MOLA PDF 5 is about the pedal changes during the piece, not the opening tuning. `\harmOctF` follows the scherzo's `\harmOct`: the MIDI plays B♭4 softly, and the print is unchanged. analysis.txt now shows Harp LH F1-B♭4, which is the MIDI sounding pitch. For finale.md: the finale's harp now starts with harmonics at 43, in the line of II 83 and III 375. No quotation, theme, key or cadence changes.

### 2. 131: the low D7 arpeggio rings into the G-major cadence and the gap at 132-134; damp it (applied)

Problem. At 123-130 the harp plays left-hand arpeggios at G2-A3, the register Adler calls "somber, dark and sonorous" (PDF 113), the register that rings longest. Bar 130 is the D7 arpeggio A2-C3-D3-F♯3 with a crescendo. Nothing damps it. At 131 the tutti plays the G-major cadence f; after the accented 132 beat 1 the orchestra nearly stops, and 133-134 turn G into V4/2 of C with F in the bass. A ringing C3 against the G chord and F♯3 against the cellos' and basses' F♮ at 133 is a clash that is exposed in the rests. The score's own practice is to damp at the end of each harp passage (étouffez at 82 and 243, accepted at c116), but 131 was missed.

Evidence. Adler PDF 117, sons étouffés (the hand damps the strings); c116 used the same principle at 82 and 243.

Fix (dev.ily, end of devHarpL): `| \stemNeutral r1\!^\markup \italic "étouffez" R1*3 | }` in place of `| \stemNeutral R1\! R1*3 | }`. The notes and pedals of 123-130 (protected, c116) do not change.

### 3. 278: the harp's last chord is a bare octave; play C2-G2-C3, movement I's last harp chord (applied)

Problem. Adler PDF 114: "Three-note chords (per hand) spread wide are the most effective" (Ex. IV-5 is all wide three-note chords per hand). After the glissando the left hand plays only C2-C3 at 278. Movement I ends with the harp chord C2-G2-C3 at I 182 (mvt1.md), so the finale's last harp chord can be the same chord at the same pitches.

Evidence. Adler PDF 114 and Ex. IV-5; mvt1.md, movement I's final chord (harp C2, G2, C3). The octave is three fingers wide, and the G2 doubles the bass trombone and timpani fifth without changing the outer voices (analysis.txt: 0 parallels).

Fix (finale.ily): `<c, g, c>4\laissezVibrer` in place of `<c, c>4\laissezVibrer`. The bars 273-279 are protected "apart from the harp glissando" (c204). This chord is part of that harp gesture, and no other part changes. For finale.md: the finale's last harp chord equals movement I's last harp chord.

### 4. 277: the harp part has a glissando with no tuning (applied)

Problem. Adler PDF 117: "Whenever a glissando is written, one must specify how the harp is to be tuned, and give the starting pitch as well as the ending one" (Ex. IV-13 prints the letters above the glissando). The score shows the setting as a score-only diagram at 277. The harp part (parts-book.ly removes everything tagged 'score') shows only C4 to G6 with no tuning. The tuning of a glissando sets which pitches sound. It is part of the notes, not a pedalling choice, so MOLA PDF 5 does not cover it.

Fix (finale.ily): `c'4-\tag #'part ^\markup \small "gliss. on C D E F G A B"\glissando`. It appears in the part only, and the score keeps its diagram. I checked it in a scratch harp-part build (4 pages; the part also shows "Ped.: D C B♭ / E♭ F G A♭" and "harm. (sound 8va)" at 43).

## Checked and not changed

- Pedal mechanics (Adler PDF 111-113): there is no chromatic conflict inside any passage. No two pedals change on one side at once, because every change falls in a long rest (82→123: B, E, A natural and F♯ over 40 bars; 130→235; 242→277 none). Lowest note F1, above the two unpedalled strings.

The places where block harp chords would be heard (the trumpet solo 51-66, the C-minor statement 171-186, F2 in A♭ 187-198) are protected scorings or protected harp rests (c116, c063b). In the ff chorales and tuttis harp chords would not be audible (Adler PDF 114: the top's ff is a middle mf). I add none.
- Glissando speed at 277: C4-G6 is 18 strings in one quarter at 2 = 72 (about 0.4 s), which is normal speed for a glissando.
- Bisbigliando and tremolo (PDF 118): not used. Tremolo stays reserved for the arrival at 223.
