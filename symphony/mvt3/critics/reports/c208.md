## Findings

### 1. 35: the trombones' return after 28 bars is set at f (applied)

**Problem.** The trombones and bass trombone stop on 6.1. They come back at 35, where the counterstatement turns to A♭ (VI) and F1's head moves into the violins. They re-enter f under strings at ff. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136: "After a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful." At pp they would not be heard under ff strings. A sustained ff, though, would break the rule on PDF 133 that the harmonic basis stays "of inferior dynamic power" than the melody.

**Fix.** Use the sf-dim attack (PDF 129), the same way c108 handled the brass re-entry at 199: attack ff and fall to f within the bar. In pTwo.ily:
- Tbn 1: `es2.~\ff\> es4~\f | es2 es2`
- Tbn 2: `c2.~\ff\> c4\f | bes,2 c2`
- B.tbn: `aes,2.~\ff\> aes,4\f | es2 aes,2`
- Cbsn, which doubles the bass trombone: `aes,,2.~\ff\> aes,,4\f | es,2 aes,,2`

The notes are tied, so there is no new attack, and no pitch changes. The timpani stroke stays f.

The f is placed on beat 4 of bar 35 on purpose. With the f on 36.1, page 2 (bars 14-36, already full) no longer held its two systems, and the score grew to 17 pages. Writing breaks into global.ily did not help either. With the f inside bar 35, the score keeps v208's exact page layout (16 pages, the same bars on every page).

### 2. 35: grace-note glide into F1's head on VI (applied; changes how F1 is presented)

**Problem.** At 27-34 the violins play the running countersubject at ff. At 35 they take over F1's head in A♭ at the same ff, with the same accent as at 11 and 27. Only Fl 1, added in c201 at f, marks the change. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129-130: "In order to stress or emphasise a certain note ... a chain of three or four grace notes, in the form of a scale, either in strings or wood-wind. These unstressed notes (anacrusis), generally written very small, form a kind of upward glide ... connected to the main note by a slur." Ex. 254 (PDF 418) shows it in the strings at f. Of the methods on this page, the score has never used this one.

**Fix (pTwo.ily, Vln I and Vln II, unison).** `\grace { es''16( f'' g'' } aes''2-> es''4. f''8 | g''4-.)`. The existing slur now starts on the first grace note, so the glide and the head are one bow. The glide comes in the quarter rest at 34.4, it ends on a single note (not a chord, as PDF 130 requires), and its notes belong to the A♭ chord. Fl 1 keeps its plain note.

I did not add a glide at 37, the head in B♭ minor. A glide F-G-A♭ into B♭5 made outer-voice octaves A♭→B♭ with the bass (36.3→37), and analysis.txt flagged them. With the glide at 35 only, analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, the same as v208.

**Theme note for finale.md.** F1's notes and rhythm are unchanged; this is an ornament. Under the F1 entries, the line for 35 should read: "35 Vln I-II grace-note glide E♭-F-G into A♭5 (c208)". No other movement quotes F1.
