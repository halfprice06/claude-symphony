## Findings

### 1. 117: the bassoon's G-minor F2 lacks the theme's third bar (applied; this changes an F2 statement)

**Problem.** F2's third bar is a leap of a fifth to an appoggiatura, which then falls by step: 53 F5–E♭5–D♭5, 69 F6–E♭6–D6, 125 A4–G4–F♯4 (restored by c041), 173 D5–C5–B♭4. The bassoon at 117 plays `g'2. f'4`, a fourth to a chord tone. Its contour (D up to G, then down) is the opening bar's arpeggio again, so the statement loses its one high point. This is the only minor-mode statement of F2 in the development, and it is the only statement anywhere without the appoggiatura.

**Evidence.** Critic 041 kept G4 because it read A4 as the bassoon's "thin, pinched" edge. At 300 dpi, Adler's chart (PDF 226, Ex. VII-89) brackets "thin, but intense" from C4 to B♭4 and starts "thin and often pinched" at B♮4. A4 therefore lies in the same band as the G4 the solo already reaches. Adler's condition for this band is a matter of dynamics: "If a bassoon solo is accompanied in that register, care must be taken to mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist" (PDF 226). The accompaniment here is pp pizzicato. Adler's printed solos (PDF 227–228: Tchaikovsky 6/i, Stravinsky) go higher than A4. With the leap of a fifth, the statement also stops being a melody the bassoon merely borrows from the trumpet (PDF 184): the peak is where "thin, but intense" sounds best.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily, devBsnOne).**
```
d'2.(\p^\markup \italic "solo, espress." bes4 | a4 g4\< bes4 d'4 | a'2\mp g'4 f'4\> | es'2 d'2\p) \breathe | …
```
The harmony of 117 (E♭, VI) is unchanged. A4 over E♭ matches F5 over C♭ at 53. Vln I's answer at 117 is unchanged. Its passing E♭6 on beat 2 meets A4 as a diminished fifth, which then opens to the fifth D6/G4 on beat 3. Neither voice is the bass, and the pairwise fifths check (c032 p5.py) shows no new parallels. Composer: please note in finale.md, under F2's development entry, that Bn 1 at 115–122 now has the third bar A4–G4–F4.

### 2. 83–86: Flute 2 doubles the closing group's F1 in its weak low octave (applied)

**Problem.** The closing group gives the movement's first F1 in major (E♭), a return the listener should notice. Fl 1 and Vln I play it at E♭6. Fl 2, Ob 1 and Vln II play it at E♭5, falling to F4 at 85, and Cl 1·2 play it an octave lower again, all f over a tutti with violas and low strings. Fl 2 is the only instrument in its weak register. It adds nothing to Ob 1 and Vln II at the unison, while the top octave, which carries the tune, has one flute.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 185 (Ex. VII-2, image): the flute's lowest octave is "weak, but luscious", and a weak register must not be covered, "for it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume out of his instrument in that register". PDF 192: multiple flutes "double at the octave or the unison". PDF 193: the flute is a most successful doubler. In the same f tutti, the clarinets lie in their "deep and rich" chalumeau (PDF 210) and the oboe in its "most effective" band (PDF 198), so they need no help.

**Fix (applied, clos.ily, closFluteTwo).**
```
closFluteTwo = { es'''2->(\f bes''4. c'''8 | d'''4-.) c'''8( bes''8 aes''4-.) g''4-. | f''4( bes''8 c'''8 d'''4 f'''4) | es'''2-> r2 | R1*4 | …
```
The rest of the part (91 onward) is unchanged. On the shared staff the part combiner prints a2 from 84. No pitch class is lost, since Ob 1 and Vln II keep E♭5.

### 3. Checked, no change: 20–21 and 160–161, the oboe in its lowest fifth

Ob 1 (and Ob 1·2 at 160–161) touch E♭4–D4–F4 inside F1's consequent, in the "thick, heavy" band (PDF 198). Adler's warning about this band concerns soft playing ("one should never require a pianissimo"). Here the band is f/ff, lasts six quarter notes, and is doubled by Vln II, so I left it. Moving the oboe up an octave would put it on A♭6 at 22, above its range.
