## Findings

### 1. 217–218: the horn echo of theme B is buried in the held chord (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 107 gave Hn 1 an echo of the trumpets' theme-B opening, A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4. Its level, f under the trumpets' ff, is correct for an echo (Rimsky PDF 128–129: "the second instrument should be weaker than the first, but the two should possess some sort of affinity"). But each echo note coincides with a held note at the same dynamic in the same timbre. A♭3 doubles Hn 4 and Bn 1, D♭4 doubles Hn 2's held D♭4 on the same staff, and F4 doubles Tpt 3. All of them hold f, and the only mark is "espress." Rimsky PDF 133: where melody and basis differ little in timbre (wood-wind against brass, and still less brass against brass), "the harmonic basis generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design, and should be of inferior dynamic power." Neither condition holds, so the echo sounds as a ripple inside the D♭ chord rather than as a phrase.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily).** Hn 2 joins the echo a2 (`aes2( des'2 | f'2. ges'4) \breathe`, replacing `des'1~ | des'1`), which removes the D♭4 unison. The held chord diminishes with the sf-dim method of PDF 129 (`\>` on the re-struck 217, `\mp` at 218) in Tpt 3, Hn 3·4, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, tuba, Bn 1·2 and Cbn, e.g. `f1~\> | f1\mp \breathe | dis'1\p`. These parts now enter 219 at `\p`, because "sub. p" after mp would be wrong. Tpt 1·2 and the upper woodwinds keep ff on D♭ through 218, and the strings keep f, so the protected sub. p at 219 is still a drop from ff on top. Hn 1·2 keep f, Hn 1's A♭4 at 219 and the top line D♭–C–B–C. finale.md item 15 should read "Hn 1·2 answer" instead of "Hn 1 answers". The quotation itself is unchanged, and the theme record is 36/36.

### 2. 219–222: the four-bar crescendo moves every group at once; the brass should arrive last (applied)
**Problem.** From sub. p at 219 every part has the same hairpins: `\<` 220, `mp\<` 221, `f\<` 222, fff 223. Rimsky PDF 130 says a prolonged crescendo is built by adding groups "in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass", and that the brass are the group that turns "crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". Here the brass swell from 220 with the strings. The fff at 223 therefore has no fresh brass growth behind it. The violas' D♯→E at 220, the thread note, also sounds under brass that are already swelling. The passage is protected as the "219–223 crescendo" (cycles 30, 38) and the "205–223 dynamic path" (cycle 48). Those protections concern sub. p 219, the stepped rise and the fff arrival, and all three are kept.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily).** Only the brass staves change: Hn 1·2, Hn 3·4, Tpt 1·2, Tpt 3, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and tuba. They hold p through 220 (no hairpin), begin `\<` at 221 and are `\mf\<` at 222, e.g. Tpt 1 `c''1\subP | c''1 | b'1\< | b'1\mf\< |`. Strings, woodwinds and the timpani roll keep p < mp < f < fff, so the strings and woodwinds lead and the brass make the last swell into 223. Tpt 1's B→C with the timpani is unchanged.

### 3. 199: the brass re-enter f after 32 bars of rest (applied)

### 4. 167–170: the link fades as one block; the brass should leave first and the clarinets fade last (applied)
**Problem.** Over VI – iiø6/5 – V6/5 the horns, clarinets, bassoons and strings all go f > p together, and the horns hold to 170. Rimsky PDF 130: diminuendo "by the elimination of the instruments in the reverse order (brass, wood-wind, strings)", and "clarinets specialise in diminuendo effects and are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)". **Fix (recP.ily).** Hn 1·2 become `c''1\f | aes'1\> | g'1\p | R1` (and `es'1\f | f'1\> | d'1\p | R1`), so they reach p at 169 and leave first. Bar 170 still has G (Vln II), B (Bn 2, Vc, Cb), D, F and A♭; the leading tone B2 into 171 (c067) is kept.

### 5. 159: the new Vln II counter-line has no mark of its own (applied)
**Problem.** The cycle-107 counter-line starts at 159 after a rest. Its only dynamic is the ff of 151, and it plays alone against the tune, which is doubled in Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Vln I, Vla and Hn 1·2. Rimsky PDF 133 approves an ornamental line that "moves independently of the concurrent melodic design", and this one does. PDF 129–130 lists the marks used to bring out such a part. **Fix (recP.ily).** `es''8(\ff^\markup \italic "espress." d'' c'' b')` at 159. I left the clarinets' ff eighths at 159–165 (cycle 104) unchanged: they lie an octave below the counter-line and are a basis of chord tones, not a second florid line (PDF 132).
