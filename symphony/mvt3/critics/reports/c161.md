## Findings

### 1. 39-42: the counterstatement's diminuendo drops all three trombones at once; keep the bass trombone on the pedal (applied)

Problem. F1's counterstatement (27-42) closes over the B♭ pedal with a diminuendo from f (35) to p (42), into the pp transition at 43. Tbn 1, Tbn 2 and the bass trombone hold V of E♭ f at 35-40 with a hairpin and then all stop together at the end of 40, with the contrabassoon. At 41-42 the pedal is left to the cellos, basses and Bn 2. The brass layer under F1's last B♭-minor head therefore breaks off two bars before the phrase ends, instead of fading.

Evidence. Adler PDF 28, item 3, with Ex. XI-12 (Schubert, *Rosamunde* Entr'acte I, 106-23, image PDF 29): the trombones double bassoons and low strings for power, and Schubert "orchestrates a beautiful diminuendo" "by dropping out two trombones and leaving only the third to bolster the lower strings". In the example, Trombone III goes on with the violas, cellos and basses into the pp after Tbn I-II stop. PDF 35-36 (*Lohengrin*) makes the same point: the brass are faded out by their doublings, not cut off. Here the cellos already hold B♭2 to 42 with a hairpin to p, so a bass trombone at the unison only completes the fade.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-161/work/sections/pTwo.ily). Tbn 1·2 stop after 40 as before. The bass trombone holds B♭2 through 41 and ends with the cellos on 42 beat 3, p:
```
pTwoBassTbn = { R1*8 | aes,1\f | es2 aes,2 | bes,1 | f,2 bes,2 | bes,1\> | bes,1 | bes,1 | bes,2.\p r4 | }
```
The bass trombone has its own staff, so the change raises no part-combining question. No theme, quotation or cyclic rule changes. The transition 43-50 is untouched.
