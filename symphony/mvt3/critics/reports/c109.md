**Brahms 2/iv against 151–279.** Brahms starts his recapitulation sotto voce. The tranquillo before it has already let the tension down, so the forte at 244 is felt as a second arrival. His coda saves the final climax for the movement's own second theme in the brass, over driving string figuration, and then holds the tonic with the brass high.

This finale makes the opposite choices, and each one is earned:
- The recapitulation enters ff over the dominant pedal (151, i6/4). It grows out of the ff V6/5 at 147–150, so a sotto voce start would need a new retransition.
- The climax is the chorale (the motto in major), not F2. Cycle 69 decided "no F2 return in the coda", and the motto is the symphony's real subject.
- The approach to the last climax (255–263) is laid out as a master would lay it out:
  - strings f with Vln II arpeggios, ff at 259;
  - the melody in three octaves (Vln I/Fl 1 – Tpt 1·2/Ob 1 – Hn 1·2/Tbn 1);
  - every bass instrument on the iiø6/5 – Ger6 – V7 – I.
  A conductor would accept this.

The places where a conductor would notice a weakness are the doubling details below. Each one is a point that Rimsky-Korsakov's melody chapter treats directly.

**Regression check against v99.** Every change since v99 in 151–279 is small: Cl ff at 151; held fourth at 152; Vln II counter-line 159–165; fade at 167–170; bass E♭ at 183; brass ff at 199; horns mf at 207; horn echo at 217–218; brass p at 219–220; Vln II G5 at 223; Cbsn C1 at 263/278. One change made the sound worse: c107's Vln II counter-line at 159–165 took away the string doubling an octave below the high Vln I (finding 1). Nothing else got worse.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 159–164: Vln I alone at C6–A♭6 with no string octave below (regression since v99)

**Problem.** Until v108, Vln II played F1's consequent an octave below Vln I (C5 level). c107 gave Vln II a counter-line, which is good in itself. Since then the strings carry the consequent only at C6–A♭6 (Vln I, peak A♭6 at 162) and two octaves lower in the violas. The C5 octave is left to Ob 1·2, and Ob 1 is a third below the tune at 162.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 58–59: "melodic figures in the very high register of the violins become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves. Such doubling secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre." His note to Ex. 24 (Sadko, PDF 200) gives the remedy: "One or two desks of the 1st Violins are sufficient to double the melody in the upper octave, all the other 1st Violins can play the octave below." Ex. 26 (PDF 202) prints exactly that layout. PDF 59 (b) allows violins divided in octaves "when the strings are doubled by the wood-wind, and when the melody falls in a sufficiently high register". Here both conditions hold: flutes double the upper octave and oboes the lower.

**Fix (applied).** In `recPVlnOne`, bars 159–164 beat 2 become octave chords marked "div.", and "unis." returns at 164 beat 3, where the line drops to G5:

`<c'' c'''>2->(^\markup \italic "div." <g' g''>4. <aes' aes''>8 | <bes' bes''>4-.) <aes' aes''>8( <g' g''>8 <f' f''>4-.) <es' es''>4-. | <d' d''>4( <f' f''>8 <aes' aes''>8 <b' b''>4 <d'' d'''>4) | <aes'' aes'''>2-> <g'' g'''>4( <f'' f'''>4) | <es'' es'''>2->( <des'' des'''>4. <c'' c'''>8 | <b' b''>4-.) <c'' c'''>8( <d'' d'''>8 g''4-.^\markup \italic "unis.") f''4-.`

Vln II's counter-line (c107, c108) is kept. The F1 pitches are unchanged; F1 is only doubled. The top voice is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 257–264: the chorale's clarinet an octave above the oboe

**Problem.** In the last chorale, Cl 1 plays the top line at E6–C6 (since c044) with Fl 1 and Vln I. Ob 1 plays the same line an octave lower with Tpt 1·2. So the clarinet sits an octave above the oboe, in its piercing high register (written F♯6), for eight bars of the movement's main climax.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 67, on wood-wind in octaves: "Deviation from the natural order, such as placing the bassoon above the clarinet or oboe, the clarinet above the oboe or flute etc., creates an unnatural resonance occasioned by the confusion of registers." The natural pairs he lists all have Fl or Ob above Cl (PDF 67), and his four-octave Ex. 64 (PDF 235) puts "2 Ob. + Cl." together under the flutes.
- PDF 74–75: a clarinet added to the trumpet gives "a finer legato" and softens the brass, which is what the chorale's middle octave needs.
- PDF 75: with Vln I in the top octave, Fl 1 + Vln I is enough there ("in the top register two flutes will suffice" for a trumpet line, and here the whole first-violin section is on it).

**Fix (applied).** This undoes c044's octave for the clarinet melody. c044 recorded no reason based on register, and Rimsky's rule answers it.
- The clarinet line moves to E5, at the unison with Ob 1 and Tpt 1·2.
- To avoid a crossing on the shared staff, Cl 2 takes the line and Cl 1 takes Cl 2's harmony notes. These are the same pitches as before.
- In `finaleClOne`, 255–264: `R1*2 | e''1\mf\<~ | e''1 | f''1\f\<~ | f''1 | es''1 | g''1 | g''1\fff~ | g''2. r4`.
- In `finaleClTwo`, 255–264: `R1*2 | e''1\mf\<~ | e''1 | d''1\f\<~ | d''1 | c''1 | b'1 | c''1\fff~ | c''2. r4`.
- Cl 2 reaches C5 at 263 with the trumpets' B→C. Fl 1 and Vln I keep C6 on top. The chorale top line (protected) and its trumpet B→C are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 265–268: violas at C5 under two ff trumpets on F1

**Problem.** The C-major F1 at 265–268 is at C5 in Tpt 1·2, Cl 2 and the violas, and an octave lower (C4) in Hn 1·2, Bn 1 and the cellos.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 79: string and brass in unison "can never yield such a perfect blend"; "each can be heard separately". The pairs that match are "Violin + Trumpet; Viola + Horn". Against two trumpets ff, the violas on their A string add a separate, covered sound, not weight.
- His natural three-group unisons (PDF 79–80, Ex. 93–95) put the violas with clarinet and horn.
- PDF 57: violas + cellos in unison give "a rich full resonance".

**Fix (applied).** In `finaleVla`, 265–268, the violas move down an octave and join Hn 1·2, Bn 1 and the cellos:

`c'2->(\ff g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | c'2->( g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-.`

The scale figure at 264 already ends on G3 and now steps up to C4 instead of leaping to C5. Bars 269–272 are already at this octave (`r8 g8( e'4.->)`), so the violas stay in one register through 265–272. The C5 octave stays Tpt 1·2 + Cl 2 (trumpet + clarinet, PDF 74).

F1's pitches are unchanged; only its scoring is. finale.md "Ending" needs one edit: "F1 at C5 in Tpt 1·2 and Cl 2, an octave lower in Hn 1·2, Bn 1, violas and cellos". Item 10 also needs one edit: "Fl 1 and Vln I an octave higher; Cl 2 at the unison with Ob 1 and Tpt 1·2 at 257–264".
