## Findings

### 1. Bar 8: the dominant preparation has one line, and the violas now answer it in imitation (applied)

**Problem.** In 7–8 the only moving voice is the cellos with Bn 1: D3 half, G2 dotted quarter, A♭2 eighth, then a rising line to F3. This is F1's head rhythm on V. Under it there is only Bn 2's G2 and single timpani strokes; violins, violas, winds and brass rest until 9. The figure that will open the theme at 11 is heard once, in one voice, and the passage reads as a bass line without a partner. In 9–10 the build is made by staggered entries on held notes (c279) and the violins' scale. Nowhere in 1–10 do two voices state the same idea in imitation.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 213 on Ex. XVI-10 (Dvořák, Slavonic Dance 8, mm. 25–32, PDF 211): a late entry with its own answer "makes the last four measures sound truly polyphonic". 7–10 are the last four bars before the theme.
- PDF 203 (Ex. XVI-7, Chopin): a line that is only implied should be sounded and sustained by its own instrument. PDF 230–231 (Schoenberg Op. 25, m. 880): an added counter-voice is built from the pitches already in the bar.
- PDF 213 (Bizet): the bar before a new texture anticipates it. An entry at 8 prepares the violas' held D4 at 9 and F1's head at 11.

**Fix (applied, sections/intro.ily, introVla).** The violas enter one bar earlier, at 8. They imitate the cellos' figure a fourth higher and two octaves up, with the same intervals: G4 half, D4 dotted quarter, E♭4 eighth (the cellos have D3, G2, A♭2). The E♭4 is ♭6 over the G pedal, as the cellos' A♭ is, and it falls to D4, the violas' held note at 9, which is a 6–5 over V. The crescendo and the f at 10 are unchanged. The bow change is at the barline.

```lilypond
introVla = { ... | <d' g'>4->\downbow r4 r2 | R1 | g'2(\mf\downbow^\markup \italic "unis." d'4. es'8) | d'1~\<\upbow | d'2 d'4->\f r4 | }
```

**Decisions.**
- *Violas, not a wind.* c337 found that winds doubling strings at pitch blur both; the violas have rested since the ff stroke at 6 and are free. A horn on the cello line at 7–8 was declined earlier as a doubling; this is an imitation, not a doubling.
- *Entry at 8, not 7.* At 7 the answer would collide with the cellos' first note. At 8 it overlaps the cellos' rise (B2–F3), so the two voices move in contrary motion at a distance of 1–2 octaves.
- *Pitch.* G4–D4–E♭4 is exact interval imitation (falling fourth, rising semitone). It lies on the violas' A and D strings and is clear above Bn 1 and the cellos at mf. On 8.3 the cellos' passing E♭3 meets the viola's D4; this is a passing dissonance over the G pedal.
- *Unchanged.* Bars 1–6 (protected German sixths and the 3–4 string answer), the cellos and bassoons, the timpani motive x at 9, c279's staggered entries for every other part, and the f at 10.

**Check.** Scratch build: build-warnings.txt is empty; 16 pages. analysis.txt differs from v437's only in the violas' note count (973) and the density line for 5–8; outer-voice parallels 0. par8.py on 6–10 prints nothing, as for v437. allpairs_par.py on 6–10 gives identical output for v437 and the scratch build. Page 1 checked as an image: "unis." and the down-bow are at 8 in the viola staff. No theme, F1, F2, chorale note or quotation changed.
