## Findings

### 1. No score change from Unit 28 this cycle (decided)

Bars 16, 32, 156, 163, 205–223 and 227–276. The reading's Neapolitan techniques are already in place, at both levels:

- **Local:** N6 with ♭2̂ on top falls through ♭2̂–1̂–♯7̂ to V (163–164, PDF 484). The bass is the best-doubled tone (PDF 484). ♮2̂ follows over V as the soprano tone (164.2, Ex. 28-6).
- **Long range:**
  - ♭VI acts as dominant of ♭II (205–210, Ex. 28-14).
  - ♭II is tonicized at length and still goes to V (211–223, PDF 491).
  - The echo drops into the low register (217–218, Ex. 28-16).
  - ♭II's own scale is used in the long tonicization (215–216, PDF 493).
  - In the coda, "♭II" appears only as tones over the tonic pedal (229, 275–276, Ex. 28-20).
  - Every spelling follows sections 18–21.

The techniques not yet used are N6 → V4/2 over a held bass, the Neapolitan 6/4, a theme restated on ♭II, ♭II in a rising sequence, and ♭II as pivot. For each, the only site is a protected passage (11–18, 186–187, 199–208, 87–90, 216–223), or the change would alter F1's tune. I checked whether any of those protections could be answered. None can without removing what the protection keeps: the bar-1 chord at 219, the deceptive cadence into A♭, or F1's notes. So nothing is applied, and the scratch copy was not needed.

### 2. Considered and rejected: a Neapolitan 6/4 at 167.3–4 (not applied)

- **Bars:** 167–168, the recapitulation's link (VI – Fr6 – V7♭9 – V6/5 – i, c242).
- **The technique:** a Neapolitan 6/4 whose A♭ bass is the upper neighbour of V (PDF 490–491, Ex. 28-15). It is the only Unit 28 technique with a site that is not protected for pitches: 167.3–4, over the held A♭.
- **Lines it would give:**
  - Inner lines C–D♭–C (violas, a neighbour written as a minor second, PDF 496 sec. 18).
  - E♭–F–F♯–G (Bn 1, rising under the violins' falling run, the contrast in Ex. 28-2e).
  - Fix tested on paper: `recPVla` 167 `c'2 des'2`; `recPBsnOne` 167 `es2 f2`; `recPClTwo`/`recPHnTwo` 167 `es'2 f'2`; `recPClOne`/`recPHnOne` 167 `c''2 des''2`; and the violins' run would have to read `es'''8 des''' c''' …` to avoid D♮6 against the inner D♭.
- **Why I rejected it:**
  - It puts a second ♭II sound four bars after 163, the recapitulation's one Neapolitan colour (finale.md; c063). Unit 28 ties the chord's force to its special placement at climaxes and before silences (PDF 483). Used twice in five bars it becomes routine, and 163 loses its weight.
  - It changes Hn 1's C5–A♭4 at 167–168, which has been protected since v138.
  - It changes c242's falling C-minor run, accepted twelve cycles ago.
- Decision: keep 167–170 as in v280.

### 4. Movement I's theme record shows a false change at I 13 (tooling; not a score change)

- **Bars:** movement I v321, bar 13 (horn call). The finale quotes it at 243–246.
- **Problem:** `python3 themecheck.py themes-mvt1.ly /mnt/project-files/symphony/mvt1/v321 --beats 4` reports 34/35, "CHANGED mvtOneHornCall HnOne 13", although record and version print the same notes (E♭3/1 C4/3 B3/2).
- **Cause:** v309 added `\set Staff.soloText = "1. Solo"` and `"1."`. lyparse.strip() removes the quoted text but leaves `Staff.soloText`, and the tokenizer reads "solo" as a spacer `s`, which shifts the onset.
- **Evidence:** with the two `\set` lines removed from a scratch copy (/tmp/critic-280/m1), the same command gives 35/35.
- **Fix for the coherence owner** (I did not edit it): in lyparse.py `strip()`, before the string removal, add
  `text = re.sub(r'\\(?:set|unset)\s+[\w.]+(?:\s*=\s*(?:#\S+|"(?:\\.|[^"\\])*"))?', ' ', text)`.
  I tested this line in a patched copy of the parser (/tmp/critic-280/scripts). It gives movement I v321 35/35, the finale v280 38/38 and the scherzo v300 51/51. The finale's horn call needs no change.

The scratch copy holds only the movement I test; there is no finale build to report, since no score change was made. v280 stands at 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels and 16 pages.
