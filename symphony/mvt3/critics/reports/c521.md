## Findings

### 1. 205–206: F1's head launching the A♭7 build has no string colour (applied)

**Bars:** 205–206 (codaOne.ily, `codaOneVc`).

**Problem:**
- After the hammered C-minor tutti, F1's head returns f over the A♭ pedal (A♭–E♭–F | G♭–F–E♭–D♭–C). It is played by Hn 1–4 (A♭4), Tbn 1·2 a2 and Bn 2 (A♭3).
- Every string part accompanies: Vln I·II scale counterpoint, violas repeated chords, basses A♭ eighths.
- The cellos hold A♭2 for four bars. The bass trombone doubles that note at the unison, and the tuba and basses sound it an octave lower.
- F1 is a string theme everywhere else (violins 11 and 151, cellos and basses 27, violas 99 and 130, cellos 140). Here it loses its own colour, and the string section adds nothing to the line the listener has to follow.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 28, item 4, with Ex. XI-13 (images PDF 29–30): "Another most powerful combination of trombones, horns, bassoons, and lower strings presents the 'national anthem' against a very heavy orchestration of the counterpoint". In the example the low strings take the anthem with the trombones, horns and bassoons, and the upper strings and woodwinds run the counterpoint.
- 205–206 has three of those four colours and the upper-string counterpoint, but the lower strings sit on a doubled pedal.
- The same chapter (PDF 27) gives horn and cello as the usual pair on a sustained melody.
- The score already uses the full combination at 247–254: Tbn 1·2, Bn 1 and cellos on movement I's head.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-521/work/sections/codaOne.ily):**
- The cellos play the head at A♭3 with Tbn 1·2 and Bn 2, an octave under the four horns. They use the strings' F1 articulation from 99, take a down-bow on the accent, and go back to the pedal at 207:

  `codaOneVc = { … | g4->\downbow g,4->\downbow r2 | aes2->(\f\downbow es4. f8 | ges4-.) f8( es8 des4-.) c4-. | aes,1 | aes,1 | aes,8(\< … }` (was `aes,1\f | aes,1 | aes,1 | aes,1 |`)

- The A♭ pedal stays in the bass trombone (A♭2), the tuba and the basses (A♭1).
- The 207–208 answer in the winds, the 209–210 run and the D♭ episode are unchanged.
- No parallels are possible against the bass, which is a pedal. par8.py over 203–209 reports only the existing doubling runs of the protected 203–204 quotation.

**Result:**
- The head now has a horn, trombone, bassoon and string colour against the violins' scale.
- The build hands the next statement (207, B♭ minor) to the woodwinds alone, so the two statements contrast in colour as well as pitch.
- This changes the scoring of an F1 statement, not F1's notes. The other threads need no notice, but finale.md's scoring notes for 205–206 should list the cellos.

### 2. Checked and declined: the last chorale's top line doubled an octave lower in Tbn 1 (Elgar's octave and double-octave doubling, PDF 24)

**Bars:** 255–263.

At 224–228 Tbn 1 carries the chorale at movement I's pitch under Tpt 1. In the last statement Tbn 1 holds harmony instead. I worked it out on paper (E4–D4–C4–B3 against the present C4–A♭3–A♭3–G3):
- It removes the root and third that Tbn 1 gives the vi and iiø6/5 chords.
- It changes the voicing of the protected approach 255–263 (c22). The only gain would be a third instance of the line, which Ob 1 and Cl 2 already double at pitch from 257.

Not better. No change.
