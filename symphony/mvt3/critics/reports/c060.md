## Findings

### 1. 110 and 112 (second half): a root-position C-major triad in full brass before the arrival. Applied.

- **Problem.** At 110, and again on beats 3–4 of 112, nine brass players hold C–E–G with C in the bass, at f, with the timpani on C:
  - Tpt E5 C5 G4
  - Hn G4 E4
  - Tbn C4 G3
  - B.tbn C3
  - Tuba C2
  - A scan of every beat before 223 finds no other such chord.
- **Why it matters.** arrival.ily calls 223 "the first sustained forte major chord in full brass". mvt1.md lists "a sustained forte C-major chord in the full brass" as something movement I never used, so that the finale can have it. The trumpets and tuba were added at 110–113 in cycle 53. That gave away the colour the symphony saves for its C-major arrival, 113 bars early.
- **Reading.** Sebesky's chief rule on instrumentation is to hold a colour back so its entrance surprises (PDF 11). Ch. 9 asks the arranger to compare the intention with the result (PDF 240). Here the comment and the notes disagree.
- **Fix.** Tpt 3 holds B♭4, so the brass sound V7 of F. The seventh falls to A♭4 at 111 and 113. The strings already pass through B♭ in the same bars. In dev.ily:
  `devTptThree = { R1*11 | bes'1\f | aes'2 aes'2 | bes'1 | aes'2-> r2 | R1*21 | }` (was `g'1\f | aes'2 aes'2 | bes'2 g'2 |`)
- **Protected passage.** 107–113 is protected (c053, c058) because the crescendo and climax are "well placed". The dynamics, the melody, the scoring weights and the V→i cadence in F are all unchanged. Only Tpt 3 changes, and only its pitch.

### 2. 219–220: F♯ falls to E in Cl 2 and in Vln I (lower divisi), blurring the E♭/E♮ thread. Applied.

- **Problem.** The German sixth at 219 has F♯ in six parts. Four of them rise to G at 220. Cl 2 (F♯4→E4) and Vln I lower (F♯5→E5) fall to E. Cl 2 arrives on E4, the violas' own thread pitch, from above while the violas arrive from D♯ below. So the closing E4 of the thread does not belong to the violas: one part converges on it from each side.
- **Reading.**
  - A&S Unit 29 (PDF 504): ♯4 in an augmented sixth is a passing tone to 5.
  - Sebesky, economy (PDF 12–13): Ex. 1 cut a redundant inner line so that the contrary motion of the others "became more evident".
  - Sebesky, focus (PDF 13–14).
- **Effect of the fix.** E4 at 220 now arrives by step only from D♯ (violas, and Hn 3 at the same pitch). Hn 3's D♯4→E4 also resolves the scherzo horn call's D♯4, which the scherzo leaves unresolved since its v31, at the scherzo's own pitch.
- **Fix in codaTwo.ily.**
  - Cl 2: `fis'1\subP | g'1\< | f'1\mp\< |`
  - Vln I: `<fis'' c'''>1\subP^\markup \italic "div." | <g'' c'''>1\< |`
  - Cl 1·2 now print "a2" on G4 at 220, with equal dynamics.
- **Protected passage.** The 219–223 path is protected for its dynamics, the D♭–C–B–C top line and the violas' D♯→E. None of these changes.

### 3. 143–145: the scherzo's motive x is pp under Vln I's f, so the only scherzo reference before the coda is not heard. Applied.

- **Problem.**
  - finale.md item 13 says "timpani alone". In the score the timpani play pp < at 143 under Vln I's f F1 head and Fl 1 mf.
  - At 144 Vln I plays F1's second bar in the same rhythm, which hides the motive further.
  - The timpani reach f only at 145, where the stretto is tutti.
  - On paper the scherzo returns here. In sound it does not.
  - The quotation at 275–276 is the scherzo's only other appearance. It needs this earlier hearing of the rhythm (Sebesky principle 1, PDF 11: vary a motive already used).
- **Reading.**
  - Sebesky, focus (PDF 13–14): the background element may "rise to the primary level temporarily".
  - Sebesky ch. 9 (PDF 241): trust the ear, not the eye.
- **Fix in retr.ily.** `retrTimp = { R1*4 | R1*4 | g,4\mf\< g,8 g,8 g,4 r4 | ...` (was `\pp\<`). The crescendo to f at 145 is kept.
- **Protected passage.** The stretto 139–146 is protected for its texture, which is unchanged.
- The scherzo thread can be told that the quotation is now audible. Its notes are unchanged and the record still gives 34/34.
