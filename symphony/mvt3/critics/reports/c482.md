## Findings

### 1. 239–240: parallel 5ths between the outer voices at the f restatement of the S head. Use a 5-6 over the held A. Applied in the scratch copy.

**Problem.** In the second, f statement of movement I's second-theme head, Vln I, Fl 1, Vln II and Ob 1 hold E (E6/E5) over the bass A (Vc A2, Cb and Bn 2 A1, harp A0/A1) for all of 239. At 240.1 the tune lands on C while the bass falls to F. That gives perfect 5ths on two successive downbeats (E/A → C/F), with only the tune's passing eighth D6 at 239.4.5 between them. par8.py flags all four melody parts at 240.1. The analyser's outer-voice check misses the pair because the eighth breaks its note-to-note comparison. In the first, p statement (235–236) the bass is the C pedal, so the fault only appears when 239 reharmonizes the tune over falling thirds (vi–IV–ii).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267: in sequences, voice-leading chords are needed because "5ths and octaves can become a problem"; the extra chord in Ex. 17-1 "prevents 5ths between successive strong beats." PDF 269: 8-5 frames "might sound empty unless decorated," and frames with imperfect consonances are preferable. PDF 274–275 (Ex. 17-9, 17-12): the 5-6 motion over a held bass turns the 5/3 into a 6/3 and "often it prevents parallel 5ths." PDF 277 (Ex. 17-16): falling-third sequences put the outer voices in 10ths or 10-6, never 5-5.

**Fix (applied).** The bass holds A over the bar line and falls to F on 240 beat 2, where the tune's portamento lands on A6. Bn 1's E3 → F3 (already written) becomes a 5→6 over the held A. 240.1 is therefore IV6 (A–C–F: Hn 4 A3, Hn 3 C4, Bn 1 F3, harp RH A–C–F–A, tune C6), then IV at 240.2. The outer frame runs 5 (239) – 10 (240.1) – 10 (240.2) instead of 5–5, and the falling thirds A–F–D of 239–241 stay as they are. sHead.ily:
```
sHeadVc     = { … | a,1\f~ | a,4 f,2. | d1 | … }        % was a,1\f | f,1
sHeadCb     = { … | a,,1\f~ | a,,4 f,,2. | d,1 | … }     % was a,,1\f | f,,1
sHeadBsnTwo = { R1*4 | a,1\f~ | a,4 f,2. | d1 | … }     % was a,1\f | f,1
sHeadHarpL  = { … <a,, a,>1\mf | r4 <f,, f,>2. | … }    % was <a,, a,>1\mf | <f,, f,>1
```
The 6th falls on 240's downbeat, but 240 is the weak bar of the 239–240 pair, so the 5-6 keeps A&S's placement of the 6th on the weaker beat at the two-bar level. The bass now moves on the second quarter with the tune's syncopated A6, which pushes into the crescendo. The tune, the counter-line (Cl 1, Vla), the horns, Bn 1, the harp's right hand, 241–247 (III♯ approach), and the timbre and instruments of 235–242 are all unchanged.

### 2. Ascending 5ths and 6-5 syncopes are unused; no place for them. Not applied.

A&S PDF 271–273 and 281 teach two types the score does not use. I checked where each could go. An ascending-5ths chain (I–V–ii–vi–iii) only fits in major and would need a new passage. The rising passages in the score (35–38, 205–208, 199–203) are protected or quote movement I. A 6-5 descending series (Ex. 17-21) needs a held bass under a descending upper line. The only such places are the B♭ pedal 43–50, the B/G pedals 139–150 (stretto, protected) and the C pedal 265–272. On the C pedal a 6-5 series would add another IV6/4–I neighbour motion to the five already there (266, 268, 270, 272, 274). No change.

### 3. 239–241: two other falling-third settings I considered and rejected

- **Stepwise descending 5-6, bass A–G–F–E–D.** The bass G at 239.3 puts the passing D6 a 5th above G and moves it to C6 over F at 240.1, which is a new pair of 5ths. The tune's A6 at 240.2–3 would then sit a 4th over E. Rejected.
- **Root-position variant, A–E–F–C–D (A&S PDF 279, Ex. 17-18).** This puts E in the bass at 239.3, four bars before the single E-major chord (III♯) at 243. That is the reason c373 gave for declining a descending-third sequence at 235–242, and it still holds. Rejected.

The held-A 5-6 in Finding 1 is the only setting that removes the 5ths without changing the bass's degrees.
