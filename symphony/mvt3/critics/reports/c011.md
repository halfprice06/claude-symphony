## Findings (ranked)

### 1. F2's climax is only a semitone above its first phrase (62, 78, 182). Applied.
**Problem.** The tune's first phrase leaps a fifth to F5 at 53 (B♭4→F5, with an appoggiatura). Its intended climax at 61-62 rises by step, E♭5–F5–G♭5, and then falls. The goal is one semitone above a note the listener heard eight bars earlier by a stronger leap. The same holds at 78 (G6 against F6 at 69) and at 182 (E♭5 against D5 at 173). Bar 62 is the only point where the flute octave doubling is added, so the score treats it as the peak. The great finale tunes put their peak on new ground: the Dvořák 9/iv counterstatement above, and Brahms 1/iv's big tune, whose continuation climbs above its opening phrase.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 54: a melody gains relief by its position, so the upper part stands out by "position alone". PDF 58-59: an octave doubling secures expression in the high register. At 62 the octave doubling is spent on a note that adds almost no height.

**Fix (applied).** I replaced the climax bar with a rising fourth to a new top note. It has two appoggiaturas: a 9th over iv7 resolving to the root, then ♭6 over V resolving to 5. The rhythm stays in quarters, as in the rest of the tune.

- sTwo.ily 78: `sTwoVlnOne` `bes'''4(\f aes'''4 g'''4 f'''4)`, `sTwoVlnTwo` `bes''4(\f aes''4 g''4 f''4)`, `sTwoFluteOne` `bes'''4\f aes'''4 g'''4 f'''4)`, `sTwoOboeOne` `bes''4\f aes''4 g''4 f''4)`. Vln I now reaches B♭6, still below the C7 reserved for 219. I updated the file comment.

### 2. F1's only C-major statement is hidden in the middle register (256-259). Applied.
**Problem.** The movement's own first theme turns to C major exactly once, at 256-259 (critic 001's addition), in Hn 1·2 at C5 and Hn 3·4/Tbn 1·2 at C4. Above it, flutes, oboes, clarinets, violins and violas repeat movement I's head, which was already heard eight times at 243-250. In the same octave, Tpt 1·2 hammer C5/G4. The tune the listener should leave humming is the one least audible in the last tutti.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 54: in the middle of the orchestral range a melody "is not so prominent", and doubling or timbre must help. PDF 60 d): violas and cellos in octaves are "of special use when the violins are otherwise employed", which is the situation here. PDF 57 c): violas plus cellos give a rich, full resonance with the cellos predominant.

**Fix (applied).** finale.ily: violas move from movement I's head (still in Vln I/II and all the woodwinds) to F1 in unison with Hn 1·2, and cellos move from the hammered C to F1 an octave lower, in unison with Hn 3·4/Tbn:

### 3. The recapitulation's F2 climax has no octave doubling (181-183). Applied.
**Problem.** recS.ily's comment says "oboe 1 doubles the climax at the octave (181-183)", but Ob 1 plays in unison with the trumpet and cor anglais. In the exposition the climax gets Fl 1 an octave above (61-63). In the recapitulation it gets only a unison colour, so the climax does not rise in register.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 58-59: octave doubling of a high melodic figure "secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre". PDF 62 Note I: high melody is doubled at the octave.

**Fix (applied).** recS.ily `recSFluteOne`: `R1*10 | r2 r4 d'''4(\p\< | g'''4\mf f'''4 es'''4 d'''4)\> | c'''2.(\p\! bes''4) | R1*3 | R1*10 | …` (the bar count is unchanged at 28). Ob 1 stays in unison. I corrected the comment.

### 4. F2's cadence is in unison with the held Cl 1 (65-66, 185-186). Applied.
**Problem.** The tune ends `f'2 d'2 | es'1` (65-66) and `d'2 b2 | c'1` (185-186). In both places Cl 1's held notes are the same pitches (`d'1 | es'1`; `b1 | c'1`), and the Vln II pizz. chords lie above the tune. The cadence of the movement's most lyrical theme merges with its accompaniment just when it should close in the clear.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 54: a melody must stand out "in relief", by contrast of timbre or crossing. A sustained unison in the middle register does the opposite.

**Fix (applied).** sOne.ily `sOneClOne` 65-66: `d'1 | es'1` → `bes1 | bes1`. recS.ily `recSClOne` 185-186: `b1 | c'1` → `g1 | g1`. This touches cycle 4's protected "accompaniment of the trumpet solo 51-66". The pizzicato strings and held winds that cycle 4 protected are unchanged; only one held note moves a third or fourth below the melody's unison, for the last two bars. The par script now reports the existing Cl 1/Bsn 1 octave E♭→D at 67→68 as a new run start, because the run no longer begins at 66. It is the same octave doubling of the bass as in v11.
