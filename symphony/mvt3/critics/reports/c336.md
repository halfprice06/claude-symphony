## Findings

### 1. 28.4-29.1: the tuba's E♭1 and D1 lie below Adler's professional range, on fast tongued notes (applied)

**Problem.** In F1's bass statement (27-32, ff, tenuto/staccato since c251), the tuba goes down the scale B♭1-A♭1-G1-F1-E♭1 to D1 on 29.1. The last two notes are quarter notes at 2 = 84 (0.36 s each).

**Evidence.** Adler's App. A chart gives the tuba's professional range from F1 (written F2 under "8 basso") to F4 (adler2 PDF 306, image). Ex. X-85 and X-86 (PDF 7-8) put the same bottom under "deep and heavy", and the text says the instrument "speaks a bit sluggishly at the bottom of its range", while low notes "can be very well controlled" when "used as slow-moving bass notes".

Critic 256 kept these notes because a CC tuba reaches them with the fourth valve, and because Rimsky-Korsakov praises low tuba doublings. Both points are true, but neither answers the chart: E♭1 and D1 are fourth-valve combinations below the range Adler lists as "usually called for in orchestral playing" (PDF 302 footnote). Here they are not slow bass notes but tongued quarters in a fast line, the case PDF 8 warns against. The contrabassoon (range down to B♭0, PDF 304) plays the same E♭1-D1 at pitch, so the 16-foot line stays complete without the tuba. The tuba has also played 1.75 bars of ff tonguing at this point with no breath.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-336/work/sections/pTwo.ily).**
```
pTwoTuba = { c,2->\ff g,,4.-- aes,,8-- | bes,,4-. aes,,8-. g,,8-. f,,4-. r4 | d,4-- g,,8-. aes,,8-. b,,4-- d,4-- | ...
```
The tuba breathes on 28.4 and comes back on the downbeat of 29 on D2, doubling the bass clarinet's D2, then falls a fifth to G1 and continues as before. Contrabassoon, basses and every other part are unchanged. The tuba's range is now E♭1-G2; the remaining E♭1 at 166 is an accented half note at the deceptive cadence, a slow bass note of the kind Adler allows, so I kept it. par8.py over 27-30 gives the same unison-statement octave doublings as v336, nothing new. This re-raises the point c256 kept, and the paragraph above answers c256's reason.

### 2. 51: the owed trumpet solo prints only "1.", not "solo" (applied)

**Problem.** The trumpets' first melody in the symphony, owed item 1, is printed as "1." (the part combiner's soloText) and "dolce cantabile". The oboe solo at 95 and the bassoon solo at 115 are both marked "solo, espress.".

**Evidence.** Adler's table of terms lists "Solo" (Seul, Allein) and "1. (first only), 2." as separate directions (adler2 PDF 312). "1." only says which player plays. "Solo" tells the player and the conductor that the line is exposed and leads over the pp pizzicato. That is what bars 51-66 are, and it is how the score already treats its other two wind solos.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-336/work/sections/sOne.ily).** `sOneTptOne = { bes'2.(\p^\markup \italic "solo, dolce cantabile" ...`. The page shows "1." above "solo, dolce cantabile" with no collision (page 3). Notes and dynamics are unchanged. I tested the same word at 171 and removed it again: there the cor anglais doubles the muted trumpet in unison (protected), so the line is a duet, not a solo.

### 3. Timpani drum ranges and retunings (checked, no change)

The App. A chart (PDF 307, image) gives each drum a fifth. G2 is near the top of the largest drum, A♭2 and B♭2 are on the 28", C3 and D♭3 on the 25", and E♭3 is on the 23". Each muta keeps its pitch on the same drum: A♭→B♭ (37, two bars' rest before the roll at 39), B♭→A♭ (43), C→D♭ (206, five bars before 211), D♭→C (215, eight bars before 223). The pause retuning in the bar-1 label (low E→G, G→A♭, B→C, high E→E♭) moves every drum by one or three semitones within its range. No change.

### 4. Double-bass C extension at 166 and 174-183 (checked, no change)

App. A marks the low notes with "†Not found on every instrument" (PDF 302). I considered a note in the part for players without the extension, tested it, and dropped it. Adler states that players without the attachment "automatically" take such notes an octave higher (adler1 PDF 108), and the score header already names the bars. c246's decision stands.

### 5. La metà and leggio (checked, not applicable)

App. A lists "la metà" (half a string section) and desk directions (PDF 312). The score already reduces strings where it needs to: solo desks at 119-122, pizz. div. under the solos at 51 and 171. Halving a section anywhere else would thin a protected texture (the trumpet solo, the arrival or the chorale) or a tutti. No change.

### 6. Brass colour terms and the language of the directions (checked, no change)

Cuivré and campane in aria (PDF 312) belong to later idioms than the Brahms/Tchaikovsky/Dvořák models, and the passages where they could go (265-268, 273-279) are protected. The English stick directions ("hard sticks", "soft sticks", "medium sticks", "near the rim") stay in English. Adler's Italian for "hard sticks" is "bacchette di legno" (PDF 312), and a timpanist reads that as wooden sticks, a different sound from the hard felt intended at bar 1, 178 and 255. Changing the words would change the sound.

### 7. Highest horn and trumpet notes (checked, no change)

The horns never go above written A5, the nonprofessional top of the chart (PDF 305). The highest is concert D5 at 141-142 and 147, ff in a tutti. The soft high entry, Hn 1 written G5-A♭5 p at 105-106, is a sustained chord tone in a professional first-horn register. Tpt 1's C6 at 243, 245 and 250 is the chart's professional top (PDF 305), used under a slur with breaths at 246 and 254 (c046). Both are within range and protected. No change.
