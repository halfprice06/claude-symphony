## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 257–262: the Vln II arpeggio figure, the only middleground in the last chorale, has no support (applied)
- **Problem.** At 255–262 three layers sound. The foreground is the chorale: Tpt 1·2 a2, Hn 1·2 a2, Tbn 1, Vln I, and from 257 also Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1·2 and Bn 1. The background is the viola quarters, the Tpt 3 and Hn 3·4 strokes and the Cb eighths. The middleground is the slurred arpeggio figure, played by Vln II alone, f (ff from 259). It sits at A4–A5, the same octave as the E5 chorale doubling in oboe, clarinets and trumpets, under nine brass at ff. It is the element that gives the passage motion, and as scored it will not be heard.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 179: the *Scheherazade* "dance figure" (the middleground) is played by piccolo, two flutes and both violin sections, and "if we want to emulate their 'big' orchestral sound … it is essential that the doubling be of a mixed color". PDF 181: Holst's buoyant figuration is in piccolos, flutes, oboes, clarinets and both violin sections, with glockenspiel. PDF 176: "each theme must be heard at good strength".
- **Fix (applied).** From 257, when the woodwinds come back in (the 255–256 woodwind rest is kept, c037), Fl 2 doubles Vln II's figure an octave higher with the same slurs and the same dynamics as Fl 1 (mf<, f< at 259, fff at 263):

### 2. 143–144: Hn 1·2 double Vln I's f entry of the F1 head at p (applied)
- **Problem.** In the stretto, Vln I enters f at 143 on F1's head at G5, and Hn 1·2 a2 play the same head an octave below at G4. The horns still carry the p of 139/141, with no new mark until the f on their held notes at 145. A brass doubling of the principal voice marked three levels below it adds nothing, and in a part it reads as an echo, not a doubling. It also cuts against the build the stretto is making (Vln II mf 142, timpani motive x mf< from 143, tutti f 145).
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 176: each rendition of the stretto's theme is coloured by its own doubling, and "each theme must be heard at good strength". PDF 184 (Schoenberg): the principal gesture is marked louder than the subsidiary ones. A doubling of the principal belongs at the principal's dynamic. PDF 178: string plus brass on one theme makes its colour, but only if both are heard.
- **Fix (applied).** In retr.ily: Hn 1 `d''1\<` and Hn 2 `b'1\<` at 142, both `g'2->(\f` at 143. The now-redundant `\f` at 145 is removed from both. The dynamics are equal on the shared staff. No note of the protected stretto (139–146) changes, and the timpani motive x keeps its mf<.

### 3. 275: Vln II's C5 tremolo sits on the scherzo quotation's pitches (applied)
- **Problem.** At 275 the scherzo's theme-A head is in Fl 1 + Vln I (C6) and in Tpt 1·2 + Cl 1 (C5: C5–G4–G4–C5–E5). Vln II holds `<c'' e''>` in tremolo. That is the quotation's own C5 and E5 in the same octave, so the lower statement's attacks on C5 and its arrival on E5 merge into a sustained tremolo of the same pitches. The upper octave is clear. The lower one, which carries the trumpets, is blurred. Hn 3's held C5 is enough for the chord.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 184: in a dense texture each fragment goes "to an instrument that will be clearly heard", and a subsidiary part in the principal's register is kept out of its way. PDF 178–179: distinct rhythm and articulation are what separate the elements. Here a held tremolo on the same two pitches removes that difference.
- **Fix (applied).** Vln II at 275: `e''1:32\<^\markup \italic "unis."`, and the "unis." on 276 is removed. The chain line G–F♯–F–E–D–D♭–C in Vln II (finale.md) is unchanged. C5 stays in Hn 3.
