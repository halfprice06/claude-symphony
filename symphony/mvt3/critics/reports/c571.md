## Findings

On the tunes as tunes: F1 (falling head, sequence, the A♭–G sigh at its peak), F2 (two waves, F6 then B♭6, as in Brahms's and Dvořák's lyrical second themes), the chorale's G–E–D–C core and movement I's S head in C major all do the job of finale tunes. Their notes are protected and I do not reopen them. The chapter has one teaching the score applies thoroughly to D4s but has not yet applied to the other open strings in its tunes (PDF 73–74). Two such notes sit in the one place where the S head, the owed C-major statement of movement I's second theme, is played molto cantabile.

### 1. 239–242, Vln II: keep "sul A" through the S head's second phrase (applied)

**Problem.** At 239 Vln I goes up an octave and switches to "ord." for the E string. Vln II stays at the original octave, and the same "ord." sends its held E5 (`e''2.~ e''8`, three and a half beats, f, with a < > swell) to the open E string. A held, swelled note at the start of a molto cantabile phrase is the case Adler describes: the open string is "not under the controlling influence provided by the finger which oscillates" and "stands out peculiarly" (PDF 73–74). It is the only held open E5 in a tune in the score (MIDI scan). Bars 235–238 play the same pitches sul A, and the score already stops every soft held D4 for this reason (c391, c466, c556).

**Reading.** PDF 73–74 (open strings in slow expressive passages). PDF 75: the A string is "quite strong in first position", where E5, D5 and C5 lie, so the f is not weakened. PDF 76: mark the passage sul A.

**Fix (applied in `sections/sHead.ily`, `sHeadVlnTwo`).** Vln II's 235 sul A spanner runs to the end of 242 instead of stopping at 238, and Vln II's "ord." at 239 is removed:
`c'''2(\> b''2)\! | e''2.~(\f\< e''8\> d''8) | c''4(\! a''2--\< g''4\>) | f''4.( e''8 d''4 c''4) | d''2( c''2)\stopTextSpan |`
Vln I keeps "ord." and the E string at 239. The second phrase gets its brightness from the upper octave, and the lower octave keeps the colour it had in 235–238.

**Protection.** This touches c018's "sul A/ord. 235/239". c018 kept that plan because "ord." correctly cancels "sul A" (Adler ch. 2, PDF 51–53) and because the second phrase brightens. Both still hold for Vln I, who plays the octave above. The new reason, the open E5 in Vln II, was not part of c018's reasoning. This is not a reversal without cause.

### 2. 235–237, violas: the chromatic counter's first phrase "sul D" (applied)

**Problem.** Movement I's chromatic counter (Cl 1 + violas, C5–B4–B♭4–A4, p, molto cantabile under the violins' sul A) ends each of its first two descents on a half-note A4 (236.1 and 237.3, the second at the end of a diminuendo). On the viola's A string in first position, which is where C5–B4–B♭4 naturally lie, that A4 is the open string. So the goal note of each chromatic descent is the one note without vibrato. On the D string the whole phrase lies in third position (C5 4th finger down to A4) with no shift. Adler gives the viola's D string for a "dark" cantabile (PDF 87, Ex. III-31). Under the violins' A-string tune, that also separates the counter from the tune by string colour as well as by register.

**Fix (applied in `sections/sHead.ily`, `sHeadVla`).**
`r4 \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul D" c''4(\p\startTextSpan b'4 bes'4 | a'2) c''2~(\< | c''4\> bes'4 a'2)\!\stopTextSpan |`

**Second phrase kept on the A string.** At 239–241 the counter is f. c250 raised it to f so that it detaches from the harp, and Adler names the viola A string's "carrying power" and its blend with the clarinet (PDF 87). The D string is the weaker one. I weighed a sul D spanner at 239.2–241 and did not apply it.

### 3. 130, Vln II's whole-note A4: considered, no change

Vln II's `a'1` at 130 (mf, crescendo into the f run at 131) is an open A4 under Vln I's A5. It ends a four-note cadential figure at Tempo I and is not a cantabile line. The run that follows takes the crescendo. The upper octave carries the line. Adler's rule is for slow, expressive notes (PDF 73), so I made no change.

### 4. Melody check against the masters: no change

Brahms 1/iv (Ex. III-4) gives the big tune first to the violins in their low octave and only later moves it up. This score's owed C-major S head does the same thing within one statement: unison sul A at 235, then octaves with the E string at 239. With Finding 1 that shape is clearer, because the move to the E string is now in one part only. F2's violin statement (67–82) gets its height from the E string at 78, as Don Juan's tune does (Ex. III-8). The F2 recapitulation (187–198) stays veiled sul A at its peak (Ex. III-7). Both follow the reading, and both are protected.
