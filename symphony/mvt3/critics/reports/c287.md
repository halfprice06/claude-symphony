## Findings

### 1. 67-74: the counter-melody under F2 in E♭ major has no wind colour; give it Hn 1 at the unison with the cellos, pp dolce (applied)

**Bars:** 67-74 (sTwo.ily, `sTwoHnOne`).

**Problem:** At E the violins play F2 in octaves, mf cantabile, and the violas and cellos play the counter-melody in unison, mp espress. (G3-A♭3-B♭3 | A♭3-G3 | ... | B♭3-A♭3-G3-F3), over the harp and the bassoons on the bass. The counter is the only real second voice in these bars, but it stays in the same string colour as the tune above it, so it reads as an accompaniment and the two lines do not answer each other as groups. The recapitulation of the same texture (187-194) gives the counter Bn 1 (c217); the exposition gives it nothing. The horns rest 51-74 and enter at 75 on the pad.

**Evidence:** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 79: "The combination of horns and 'cellos, frequently employed, produces a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone." His examples on the same page and on PDF 78 put a horn on a string line at a soft level (No. 92, violas con sord. + horn; No. 90, Scheherazade iv, cellos + 2 horns). PDF 76 sets the limit: one wind with a string group lets the strings predominate, while several winds overpower them. So one horn, pp, under two string sections at mp. The range G3-F4 is the horn's middle register (written D4-C5). The score has no other soft horn-and-cello unison, so this is a colour it has not used. It also makes the two statements of F2 in the major different: horn on the counter at 67, bassoon at 187.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-287/work):**

```lilypond
sTwoHnOne = { g2(\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." ^\markup \italic "dolce" aes4 bes4 | aes2 g2) | aes4( bes4 c'2 | es'4 f'4 g'2) | es'2( c'4 es'4 | d'2 aes2) | g2( c'2 | bes4 aes4 g4 f4) \breathe | es'1\pp | f'2 es'2 | ... (rest unchanged) }
```

The horn follows the cellos, with one exception: at 70 beats 3-4 it takes the violas' G4 instead of the cellos' E4. The violas' E♮ thread (first E4 at 97, c203/c239) is then not underlined by a horn in the movement's first E♮ bar (the same reason c230 gave for removing Hn 3's E4 at 95-98). At 72-73 it follows the cellos' seventh A♭3→G3 (c262). It breathes after 74 and plays the pad from 75 as before. Hn 2 still enters pp at 75, so c239's pp entry stands, and Hn 1 itself enters pp after a 16-bar rest, which follows the same rule (Rimsky PDF 136). The part's "Vc." cue over Hn 1's old rests at 73-74 goes with the rests; Hn 2's cue is unchanged. No string, harp or bassoon note changed, and neither did F2.

**Checks:** build 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages, analysis.txt 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 66-76 gives the same list before and after: only the bassoons' existing octave doubling of the bass, nothing from Hn 1. Page 4 checked as an image: the "1." and "dolce" print, the slurs close, and no staff was added to the system, since the Hn 1·2 staff was already shown.

**Earlier decisions:** This does not undo c239. It touches 67-74, which is not on a do-not-touch list; c224's description of 67-82 as "strings + harp" was already loose, because Bn a2 and Hn 1·2 from 75 belong to it. 187-198 is unchanged.

### 2. 247-250: F1's head sounds at G2 and G5 only (no change, decided)

**Bars:** 247-250.

**Problem:** Tbn 1·2, Bn 1 and the cellos have the head at G2-A3 and the flutes and violins at G5-A6, with no octave in between. Rimsky PDF 76 warns that three or four octaves are hard to balance, and the two-octave gap is hollow. PDF 75 rule 7 (no woodwind an octave over the trombones) is not broken.

**Decision:** No change. 247-254 is protected (c217, with the c185 trombone level), and the gap is filled by the S head in the horns, clarinets and violas (Rimsky PDF 79-80 three-group unison, row 19), which is the point of the combination. Filling it with a third F1 octave would cover the S head.

### 3. 123-126: a soft low melody in the cellos (not applicable, decided)

Rimsky PDF 78 scores a soft low melody as cellos + bassoon over undoubled basses. At 123 the basses carry an independent bass line, not the melody an octave down, and the 123-130 hand-over (Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I) is protected for its order and notes. No change.
