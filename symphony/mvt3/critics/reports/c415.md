## Other sources

I used Gould, *Behind Bars*, PDF 46–47 (ledger lines against octave signs) to judge item 16, and PDF 41, 55 and 57 (no character may collide with another, and added text should not distort the spacing) for Finding 1. I looked at all 16 pages of the v415 PDF at 75 dpi, and at 150–250 dpi for pages 7, 8 and 13. Cycles 35, 325 and 395 had already fixed collisions of this text at 217, 224 and 226. Their fixes were incomplete, and in the case of 149 never reached it.

## Findings

### 1. "change bow at will" crosses the barline at 139 (Cb), 149 (Vc, Cb), 224 (Vc, Cb) and touches it at 226 (Vln II). Applied.
- **Problem:** Adler (PDF 291 item 1, PDF 301) asks for a score that is clear and proofread. Gould (PDF 41, 55) says no character may collide. Here the two-line text from global.ily (`\cbaw`, "change bow" / "at will") and `\cbawU` ("unis., change bow" / "at will") are wider than the whole-note bars they stand in:
  - Page 7, bar 139: the Cb's text reads "change b|ow", with the 139|140 barline through the "b".
  - Page 8, bar 149 (the narrow allargando bar before letter K): Vc and Cb both read "change b|ow".
  - Page 13, bar 224 (Maestoso): Vc "unis., change bo|w" and Cb "change bo|w".
  - Page 13, bar 226: Vln II's "change bow" ends on the 226|227 barline.
  
  Cycle 395 fixed the same fault at 217 with the three-line `\cbawN`, but did not reach these bars.
- **Fix (applied):**
  - global.ily: a new four-line form after `\cbawN`: `cbawUN = ^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "unis.," "change" "bow" "at will" }`.
  - retr.ily, Vc 149: `<g, f>1~\cbawN`. Cb 149: `g,,1~\cbawN`.
  - retr.ily, Cb 139: the text moves to 140 as `b,,1\p~ | b,,1~\cbawN |`. I tested two other ways and rejected both:
    - `\cbawN` at 139 over-filled page 7 by 1.6 staff-spaces, because the third line meets the cellos' p in the same bar.
    - `\cbawN` with `-\tweak X-offset #-1.3` made it 2.1. Each version gave a build warning.
    
    At 140 the text stands under the cellos' half note, inside the same tied B♭ (139–146), and the page has room.
  - arrival.ily: Vln II 226 `g''1~\ff\cbawUN`, Vc 224 `c1\f-\tweak X-offset #-0.5 \cbawUN`, Cb 224 `c,1\f-\tweak X-offset #-0.5 \cbawN`.
- **Result:** at 220 dpi every one of these texts now ends inside its bar, and nothing else moved. The text forms in use are now `\cbaw` (where the bar is wide enough: 43, 142, 226 Vln I, 235), `\cbawN` and `\cbawUN`.
- **Build:** 16 pages, build-warnings.txt empty. score.midi is byte-identical to v415's, so no sound changed and analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 139–150 and 223–227 reports only the existing bass octave doublings.

### 2. Considered and declined: octave signs for the five-ledger-line notes in the parts (Adler PDF 300, item 8a)
Fl 1·2 and Vln I play B♭6 at 78 and 213–214 and C7 at 223 with five ledger lines, in the score and in the parts. Adler's limit is four. Gould (PDF 47) is more specific: woodwinds and strings may use five ledger lines, octave signs in playing parts are "unhelpful", and players read at least four lines at a glance. Cycles 5, 115 and 235 made the same decision, so I made no change.

### 3. Checked, no change: timpani "muta" timing (Adler PDF 294) and the opening labels (PDF 292)
Each retuning instruction comes in the first rest after the last stroke on the old pitch, above the staff:
- A♭ is struck at 36, "muta A♭ in B♭" stands at 37, and the B♭ roll comes at 39.
- B♭→A♭ at 43, for the A♭ needed later.
- C is last struck at 202; the muta stands at 206 and D♭ is played at 211.
- D♭→C at 215, for the C at 223.

No earlier place exists. Page 1 names the four drums, their pitches, the retuning in the pause, both percussion instruments and the two players.
