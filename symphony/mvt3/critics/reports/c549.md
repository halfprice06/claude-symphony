## Findings

### 1. Applied: the muted trumpet leaves F2's last phrase, 183.4–186
- **Problem.** In the recapitulated F2 (171–186), the muted trumpet doubles the cor anglais in unison for 12 of 16 bars. Since c349 it rests only at 175–178. Its last phrase, the descent to the deceptive cadence (bes'4 at 183.4, then 184–186), is still doubled.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 140–141 says doubling is "only permissible in isolated phrases", because constant doubling "replaces by a mixed timbre the rare characteristic qualities" of the solo. His examples show the doubling supporting the climb and the culminating point, then dropping away (Ex. 281, PDF 449, at the Allargando: the doubling woodwinds go "dim." once the peak is past; Ex. 284, PDF 457). c349 applied the same passage to 175–178 but left the closing phrase doubled.
- **Practical gain.** The doubled notes at 185–186 are D4–B3–C4, the muted trumpet's dullest and least secure register.
- **Result of the change.** The statement now runs 4 bars duet, 4 bars cor anglais, 4 bars duet with Fl 1 at the climax, then 4 bars cor anglais. Fl 1 and the trumpet end together on the climax phrase's goal, C5 at 183. The cor anglais alone carries the deceptive cadence into the A♭ statement. The mute now comes off in the rest from 184 instead of 187, which gives the player more time before the senza sord. entry at 199.
- **Fix** in sections/recS.ily, recSTptOne: replace
  `c''2.\p) \breathe bes'4( | aes'4 g'4 f'4 es'4 | d'2 b2 | c'1) | R1*12^\markup \italic "togli sord." | }`
  with
  `c''2.\p) r4 | R1*3^\markup \italic "togli sord." | R1*12 | }`
- **Unchanged.** F2's notes (the cor anglais keeps the whole tune), the slurs, Fl 1, the accompaniment and the dynamics.
- **For the composer.** This is a scoring change to F2, one of the finale's own themes, not to its pitches. finale.md's line "since v350 the muted trumpet rests 175–178 … and rejoins at 179" needs "and rests again from 183.4".
- **Build.** Applied in /tmp/critic-549/work. 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 16 pages, theme record 51/51 unchanged. par8.py on 182–187 reports only the same 185.1 Cl 1 entry that v539 and v549 already show. That entry is an artefact: the bass is a short pizz. G1, not a held line.

### 2. Holistic (Bruckner 4/iv): the coda's soft troughs are short. No change
- **Comparison.** Bruckner's coda starts quietly in the minor and builds in two long waves to the first movement's horn call in major brass. This finale's coda has two quiet troughs, each four bars long: 219–222 (sub. p into the 223 arrival) and 235–238 (p under the second-theme head). Everything else from 199 to 279 is f to fff. A listener who knows Bruckner may feel that the C-major arrival is reached quickly.
- **Decision: no change.** The movement's stated models are Brahms 1/iv and Tchaikovsky 5/iv, whose arrivals are also sudden. 219–223 is protected (c014, c152). The chain of coda stages was checked at c513 and c543. A longer soft build would add a page.
- **Where the comparison favours the score.** It does what Bruckner's coda does in its own way: the first movement's motto (the horn call, 243–246) and the finale's theme heads combine at 247–254, and F1 returns in major at 265.

### 4. v539 → v549 check: c544 shifted the resolution at 170→171. Nothing got worse. No change
- **What changed.** In v539, Bn 1 held F3 at 170 and resolved it to E♭3 at 171, as v164 intended. Since c544, Bn 1 stops at 170. The E♭3 at 171 is now sounded by Cl 2, which resolves its own F3, together with the entering bass clarinet. The two are in unison on 171.1–2 only.
- **Rimsky's rule.** PDF 139 advises against two clarinets in unison forming one harmonic part under a soloist. Here it lasts one pp beat, and the resolution is heard at pitch in Cl 2.
- **Decision.** I kept it.
- **Other changes c539–c548.** Pages, MIDI and analysis show no new parallels and no new range flags. The cor anglais now reaches F3 at 157, written C4, inside its range. Nothing else got worse.
