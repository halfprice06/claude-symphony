## Findings

### 1. Tested and rejected: Neapolitan 6/4 at 168 (A&S PDF 490–491, Ex. 28-15)
- **Bars:** 167–170, the link after the recapitulated P.
- **Idea:** A&S §10 shows the 6/4 position with the bass as upper neighbour to V. The scherzo has its own N6/4 (C–F–A at 331) before its last dominant. The finale's 168 is a French sixth over the same A♭ bass, the place where a D♭–F–A♭ chord over A♭ would answer the scherzo.
- **Build:** In /tmp/critic-480/tested/recP-N64.ily I made these changes at 168: Vln I `f''8 g'' aes'' f'' des''4)`, Vln II the same an octave lower, Cl 2 and Bn 1 `f1`, Hn 2 `des'1` (stopped, tie to 169 removed), violas `des'1`. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 16 pages, and par8.py found only the bass doublings at 169–170. 168 then sounded A♭–D♭–F, resolving to V at 169.
- **Why it is worse:**
  1. It puts a second ♭II sound five bars after 163's ff ♭II6, the reason c280 gave against an N6 at 167. A 6/4 position does not answer that reason, because the colour is the same D♭ triad.
  2. The violins' line becomes D♭5 (168.3), then D5 after a quarter rest (169.2), the soprano ♭2̂→♮2̂ that A&S PDF 484 and 488 tell us to avoid.
  3. It removes the augmented sixth A♭/F♯. That interval links the link to bar 1's German sixth and to 219, where the same A♭/F♯ chord prepares the stop chord.
- **Decision:** Not applied; recP.ily restored. The finale has enough Neapolitan tonicization and ♭II6 already, and this extra one does not improve it.

### 2. Considered, not built: apparent Neapolitan 6/5 at 12.3–4 and 152.3–4 (A&S PDF 495–496, Ex. 28-21)
- **Sites:** iv7 at 12.3–4 (F–A♭–C–E♭) goes to V at 13. Cl 2 holds C4 there and moves to D4, which would allow a rising C4–D♭4–D4.
- **Why not:** Hn 3 enters on C4 at 12.3, so D♭4 would make a minor second with it and a major second with the tune's E♭4 in the violins, in the same register. 152 has the same layout with Tbn 1 on C4.
- **Bar 33:** iv → iiø6/5 → V has no voice moving C→D; Hn 2 C4 falls to B3.
- **Decision:** No site carries the technique without clashing.
