## Findings

### 1. 145: the violas play F1's head with no dynamic, and their crescendo runs on to the ff at 147 (applied)

**Problem.** In the retransition stretto the violas start a crescendo at 141 (`f'1\<`). At 145 they play F1's head `g'2->( d'4. es'8 | ...` with Vln I, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Bn 1. Each of those parts is marked f at 145, and Bn 2, Vc, Cb, Cbsn, the timpani and the B.cl. also reach f there. The violas have no mark at 145, so their hairpin continues for six bars until the ff at 147. On page 8 the viola hairpin runs under the head through 145-146. In the MIDI the violas keep rising through 145-146 (velocity 79→82 at 145, while the other parts that play the head are flat at f).

**Evidence.** MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): every hairpin ends on a printed level. Ex. 6 (PDF 11): staves that share an attack carry the same mark. PDF 8: proofread the vertical line. The violas' 145 is the same entry as the bassoons' `g2->(\f` and Vln I's `g''2->(\f`.

**Fix (applied, retr.ily).** `retrVla`: `... | d'1 | d'1 | g'2->(\f d'4. es'8 | ...`. The ff at 147 is unchanged. No pitch changed. The viola track is the only MIDI difference in the build.

### 2. 141-145: the stretto's long crescendo, written as "cresc." across the page turn (applied; a technique the score did not use)

**Problem.** The crescendo from 141 (Bn 1·2, violas, basses; cellos from 142; Cbsn and timpani from 143) to f at 145 is written as hairpins three to four bars long. They break at the 143|144 page turn. After the turn, page 8 opens with hairpin pieces that have no visible start, under a passage that is otherwise held notes. Players turning a page in a part see the same thing. The score has no written "cresc." anywhere, even where a crescendo lasts four bars or more.

**Evidence.** MOLA Ex. 7 (PDF 12) writes a long crescendo as the word "cresc. molto". PDF 7-8: the page turn and the extracted part must be readable as the player reads them. Adler (adler2 PDF 10, Ex. X-92) writes "poco a poco cresc." for a long build, and adler2 PDF 201 says that a crescendo is clearer when written out. With the word "cresc." at its start and dashes after it, the reader knows on both sides of the turn what the dashes mean. The short hairpins in the same bars (Hn 1·2 142-143, Hn 3·4 141-142, trombones 141-143, B.cl. 144-145) do not cross the turn and stay as hairpins. Short swells as hairpins and long builds as words is the usual mix in Brahms and Dvořák scores.

**Fix (applied, retr.ily).** `\<` becomes `\cresc` in `retrBsnOne` (`d'1\cresc`, 141), `retrBsnTwo` (`b,1\cresc`, 141), `retrVla` (`f'1\cresc`, 141), `retrCb` (`b,,1\cresc~`, 141), `retrVc` (`b,1\cresc~\cbaw`, 142), `retrCbsn` (`b,,1\pp\cresc~`, 143) and `retrTimp` (`g,4\mf\cresc`, 143). Each still ends at its f on 145. On the page images, the shared bassoon staff prints one "cresc.", and the dashes continue on page 8 to the f. MIDI velocities are identical to v268 in every track except the violas, whose change comes from Finding 1, so the text crescendo plays exactly like the hairpin.

### 3. 269: Hn 3·4 re-enter with the ff written on the rest (applied)

**Problem.** `finaleHnThree` and `finaleHnFour` at 269 read `r4\ff e'4.->`, so the ff prints under the beat-1 rest and not under the entry on beat 2. Both horns rest from 265 to 268, and their last printed level is the fff of 263. My script found 7 dynamics on rests in the score. The other 6 (199 Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1; 265 Cl 1; 269 Bn 1) are on shared staves where the partner attacks on beat 1 at the same level, so the single mark belongs to that attack, and I left them. Hn 3·4 at 269 is the only one where nobody on the staff plays at the moment the mark stands.

**Evidence.** MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): the dynamic is under the entry that follows the rest. c068 accepted "dynamics on notes, not rests" as a rule for this score (42, 170).

**Fix (applied, finale.ily).** In both horns, `r4 e'4.->\ff d'8( c'8 b8 |`. The staff is shared and both voices are identical, so the shared-staff rule holds.
