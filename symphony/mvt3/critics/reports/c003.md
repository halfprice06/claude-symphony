## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 256–262: the last bars are misaligned, so the final chord is spread over three bars (applied)
- **Problem.** The finale section should be 20 bars long. Vln I, Vln II, Va and Fl 1 have 20 bars; Fl 2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, B.cl, Bn 1, Cbn, Cb, all trumpets, horns, trombones, timpani and cymbals have 19; Bn 2, Vc and Tuba have 18. Because this is the last section, nothing follows to trigger a bar-check warning. In the engraved v03 (page 16) the final tutti chord starts at 259 in the cellos, Bn 2 and tuba, at 260 in the winds, brass, timpani and basses, and at 261 in the violins and violas. Fermatas fall on 260, 261 and 262. The movement's last chord, the goal of the whole symphony, does not sound together. A&S PDF 220 also asks the closing bars to round off the piece with a varied repetition of the final cadence, but 256–262 never returns to V after 254–255.
- **Evidence.** A per-variable bar count of finale.ily; page 16 of v03/score.pdf.
- **Fix (applied).** Every part now has 20 bars: 256–259 as before, 260 the cadence-echo bar, the final chord at 261 tied into 262, and the fermata on 262 (global.ily already puts it there). Parts that rested keep resting through 260. In 260 the neighbour on the off-beat eighth becomes V (G in the bass, matching the timpani's existing `c4 r8 g,8`). This gives the chorale's closing C–B–C in diminution in the violins. Examples:
  - `finaleVlnOne` 260: `<e'' c'''>4-> r8 <d'' b''>8 <e'' c'''>4-> r4`
  - `finaleVc` 256–262: `c4-> r4 r2 | c4-> r4 r2 | c4-> r4 r2 | c4-> r4 r2 | c4-> r8 g,8 <c g>4-> r4 | <c g>1->~ | <c g>4-> r4 r2\fermata`
  - `finaleOboeOne` 256–262: `R1*5 | c'''1->~\ff | c'''4-> r4 r2\fermata`
  - `finaleTptOne` 256–262: `c''4->\ff r8 c''8 c''4-> r4 | c''4-> r8 c''8 c''4-> r4 | c''4-> r8 c''8 c''4-> r4 | c''4-> r8 c''8 c''4-> r4 | c''4-> r8 d''8 c''4-> r4 | c''1->~ | c''4-> r4 r2\fermata`

  A root V–I after 255 is allowed: the cyclic rule only forbids one before 254–255. Trumpet 1 avoids B in 260, so B→C stays unique to 254–255.

### 2. 237–238: the second-theme stage closes on the tonic and stops the coda (applied)
- **Problem.** The plan and the sHead.ily header promise "235–238: a continuation to A6". The notes instead repeat 231–234 an octave higher and end `b''2 c'''2` over `d2 g,2 | e1`, which is V–I6 with a 7̂–1̂ top line. A&S PDF 226 says such a line over V–I makes a cadence. So stage 3 of the coda ends on the tonic at 238, and the horn call at 239 has to begin again from a fresh G pedal. The same 4-bar head is also heard four times in 20 bars (231, 235, 243, 247). Coming after the arrival's dying fall to mf at 229–230, this is the one place where the coda sags.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 215: the bass 4̂–♯4̂–5̂ with V6/5 of V is the standard way to drive into V at a cadence, and a cadential 6/4 can embellish the goal. Bar 239 is already I6/4 over G, so the progression fits as it stands. I chose V6/5 of V rather than the planned augmented sixth because Ger6 → cadential 6/4 in C is the owed event at 215–216 (violas D♯→E), and repeating it 22 bars later would weaken it.
- **Fix (applied).** In sHead.ily, bar 7 = 237 and bar 8 = 238:
  - Vc `d2 f,2 | fis,1`, Cb `d,2 f,,2 | fis,,1`, Bn 2 `d2 f,2 | fis,1`
  - Vln I `d'''2 c'''2)`, Vln II `d''2 c''2)`, Fl 1 `d'''2 c'''2)`, Ob 1 `d''2 c''2)`
  - Cl 1 and Va `c'2 d'2)`, Bn 1 `a1 | d'1`, Hn 3 `a1 | c'1`, Hn 4 `f1 | a1`
  - Harp RH 238 `a8 c'8 d'8 a'8 a'8 d'8 c'8 a8`, LH 238 `fis,,1` (one F-pedal change at the barline; the harp then rests)

  F♯ appears only in the bass instruments, following A&S PDF 214 (no doubled ♯4̂). Bar 238's C, the seventh of V6/5 of V, is held in the 6/4 by the trumpets and falls to B at 240. No V–I in C is added.

### 3. 133–135: the G key area is not turned back into V (applied)
- **Problem.** 115–134 is a real key area. It is prepared by D at 114, contains two 8-bar themes and is confirmed by D7–G at 131–132 (this follows A&S PDF 216 §6 and PDF 219 §9). It then sits on a bare G major triad at 133–134 dying away to p. A&S PDF 219–220: after V has been held as a key, it is "best accomplished by turning the chord into a dominant seventh by adding a minor 7th (4̂ in the main key)", because 4̂ is the one degree the dominant key lacks. In v03 the F first comes at 135, inside the dim7, so the listener goes straight from a tonic of G into the chain.
- **Fix (applied).** `devVla` 133–134: `f'1\>~ | f'2. r4\p` (was `b`; Hn 4 keeps the B). Bars 133–134 are now G7, and 133→135 moves only G→A♭ in Vln I: the F is held into `<f' b'>` and the D in Vln II is held. This is V7 → vii°4/3 with no root V–i.

### 4. 129–131: the development's goal cadence comes subito f out of 16 bars of pp (applied)
- **Problem.** 115–130 is pp throughout (bassoon solo, then harp solo), and every part jumps to f at 131. In the density profile, 113–140 is the lowest stretch outside the second theme (4.0–10.0 voices, 31–84 attacks per 4 bars). The cadence that should punctuate and confirm the G area (A&S PDF 216 §6) arrives without any approach, and then dies away at once. Dvořák 9/iv approaches its development's end through a sustained build to a half cadence (cadence.csv row 15).
- **Fix (applied).** Add `\<` at 129 in `devVlnTwo` (`b'1\<`), `devVla` (`g'1\<`), `devVc` (`g1\<`), `devCb` (`g,1\<`) and `devHnOne` (`g'1\<`), ending at the existing `\f` at 131. The harp has already finished its phrase at 130 and is left alone.

### 5. 131–132: the doubled ♯4̂ resolves in octaves (applied)
- **Problem.** At 131 (V7 of G = V7 of V in C) F♯ sounds in Hn 2 (`fis'2 fis'2`), Cl 1 (`fis'2`), Ob 1 (`fis''2`) and the top of the Vln I run. Hn 2, Cl 1 and Vln I all resolve F♯→G at 132, which par.py reports as parallel octaves. A&S PDF 214: "Because ♯4̂ functions (temporarily) as a leading tone, it must not be doubled."
- **Fix (applied).** `devHnTwo` 131 `d'2\f d'2`; `devClOne` 131 `d''1\f`. My first attempt, Cl 1 `a'`, made octaves with Fl 1 A→G, so I rejected it. Ob 1 keeps `fis''2 c''2` (it moves F♯→C inside the chord), and the Vln I run keeps its leading tone into G.

### Points examined and not changed
