## Findings

### 1. 157–158: the recapitulation weakens F1's half cadence that the exposition strengthened (applied)

**Problem.** Since c332, the exposition's antecedent ends 16 F–D | 17 F–**F♯** | 18 G. That is the bass 4–♯4–5 of A&S Ex. 14-2, under ♯IV7 (F♯–A♭–C–E♭, a chord applied to V). The recapitulation's 156–157 repeats F–D twice (F–D | F–D | G), so its half cadence comes from a root-position ii°7. A&S PDF 215 (Ex. 14-4 and the paragraph before it): without the ♯4 the V "wants to continue moving" and does not sound like a cadential goal. A recapitulation that is "fuller" (plan) but cadences more weakly than the exposition, with the bass pattern repeated literally, reads as an oversight. c332 noted that "the recapitulation's 157 keeps the old chord" without giving a reason.

**Fix (in recP.ily, bar 157; tune, dynamics and scoring unchanged).**
- recPBsnOne, recPBsnTwo, recPBassTbn, recPVc: `f,2 d2` → `f,2 fis,2`
- recPCbsn, recPCb: `f,,2 d,2` → `f,,2 fis,,2`
- recPHnFour: `r4 f4-. r4 f4-.` → `r4 f4-. r4 es4-.` (as at 17)
- recPTbnTwo: `f2 d2` → `f2 es2~`, tied into 158's `es4 d4`, so the 7th E♭ falls to D
- recPClOne: `d'8( f' aes' f')` → `c'8( es' aes' es')`; recPClTwo: `aes8( c' d' c')` → `aes8( c' es' c')` (the clarinets' figures at 17)

**Result.** 157 has the same chord and bass as 17, F1's A♭ falls to G over the 4–♯4–5, and Tbn 1's C–B suspension and the timpani G–G–G at 158 are unchanged. The F/F♯ cross-relation between the tune's eighth F and the bass is the same as at 17.

### 2. 193–194: the A♭-major recapitulation of F2 lacks the 4–♯4–5 that c273 gave its model, 73–74 (applied)

**Problem.** At 73.3–74, F2's E♭-major half cadence runs IV – V6/5 of V – V, with the bass A♭–A♮–B♭ (c273, Ex. 14-2, 14-3d). The A♭ statement at 193 runs I – vi – (F with B♭/C/E♭ over it) – V7. That is a static F bass under the anticipated B♭, the weaker diatonic approach of A&S PDF 215. c273 declined this bar for two reasons. (a) The 187–198 scoring is protected. This change keeps the scoring and alters only pitches, as c262, c270, c293 and c357 did inside 187–198. (b) 194 is already a V7 with D♭ in the bass on beat 3. That is the goal chord. Ex. 14-3c/d shows V6/5 of V leading to V7 just as well.

**Fix (in recS.ily, bar 193 only).**
- recSBsnTwo: `\transpose c aes, { c2 a,2 }` → `\transpose c aes, { c2 f,4 fis,4 }` (A♭2–D♭2–D♮2 → E♭2)
- recSCb: `aes,,2 f,2` → `aes,,2 des,4 d,4`
- recSVc: `\transpose c aes, { e2( a4 g4) }` → `\transpose c aes, { e2( a2) }`; recSBsnOne: `\transpose c aes, { g2( a4 g4 }` → `\transpose c aes, { g2( a2 }` (the counter-line holds F3, so no E♭ sounds against the D♮)
- recSClOne, second half of 193: `r8 aes8( c'8 aes8)` → `r8 aes8( f8 aes8)`. The C4 clashed with both new chords. A first try with B♭3 made octaves with the tune's A♭–B♭, so F3 is used, which keeps the rocking third.

**Result.** IV (D♭–F–A♭) on 193.3 with the tune's A♭, then V6/5 of V (D–F–A♭–B♭) on 193.4 with the tune's anticipated B♭, then V7♭9 at 194. ♯4 (D♮) sounds only in the bass line (Bn 2, Cb). The tune, Hn 1, the Vln/Vla doubling, Cl 2, the bowing and the dynamics are unchanged. Cl 1's F♭4 at 194 is untouched.
