## Findings (ranked)

### F1. 213→214: Bn 1 moves in parallel 12ths with the tune's passing tone. APPLIED
- **Bars:** 213 beat 3 → 214 beat 1, codaTwo.ily, `codaTwoBsnOne`.
- **Problem:** Bn 1 holds B♭3 through bar 213 and steps to A♭3 at 214. At the same moment Tpt 2 and Vln II move F5→E♭5. That makes parallel 12ths (compound 5ths) between Bn 1 and two melodic voices at ff. p5.py reports it at (214, 1.0). The 12ths are clear to hear, because the bassoon is the only wind voice in that register and does not belong to the bass doubling.
- **Evidence:**
  - Unit 20 (PDF 327–329, Ex. 20-2b/c) shows this repair: a voice that would form parallel 5ths with a passing tone moves by chordal skip within the same harmony, and the parallel is gone.
  - Bar 213 is a G♭ chord, so G♭ is a chord tone. Moving Bn 1 to G♭3 on beat 3 doubles Tbn 1 in unison. Bn 1 then rises to A♭3 at 214 in unison with the cellos.
- **Fix (applied):**
  - Change `bes1)` to `bes2 ges2)` in bar 213 of `codaTwoBsnOne`. The slur is unchanged, and a dated comment is added.
  - The line now reads `codaTwoBsnOne = { f1\ff\> | aes4(\f ges4 f4 es4 | bes2 ges2) | aes4( bes4 c'2) \breathe | ...`.
- **Result:**
  - p5.py: the (214, 1.0) entry is gone and nothing else changed.
  - par8.py on 211–216: the only new lines are Bn 1 G♭3→A♭3 at 214.1, in unison with the cellos, i.e. a bass doubling.
  - analysis.txt: 0 outer-voice parallels.
  - This is not a protected bar (216–234 are protected; 213 is not), and no cyclic rule is affected.

### F2. 70–72 and 190–192: the Unit 24 chain of 7ths has a single line where the book alternates two instruments. Not applied
- **Problem:** The descending-5ths passage with interlocking 7ths sits in the strings alone. Unit 24 (PDF 398–400) shows the 7th chain with two voices alternating to pass through the connecting 3rds, a technique this finale uses only weakly.
- **Decision: not applied, for two reasons.**
  - At 70–74, c287 has just added Hn 1 to the counter-melody. A second wind colour here would anticipate the wind entry at 77 and dull it.
  - At 190–192 the passage leads into the protected 199–204 build, and its present thin scoring prepares that build.
- **Fix, for a later cycle that wants it:** at 190–192 only, have Ob 1 and Cl 1 take the connecting 3rds in alternate half-bars at p (for example `r2 es''2 | r2 c''2`). Leave 70–72 unchanged.

### F3. 139–150: no augmented 6th before the recapitulation. Not applied
- **Problem:** Unit 29 (PDF 503–505) presents the augmented 6th as the usual approach to structural dominants, and the Mozart footnote notes that it can recall the opening. The finale already uses one at 1–2, 109, 121, 168, 219 and 261, but not at the main retransition.
- **Decision: keep.** 139–146 and 147–150 are protected in HISTORY.md and c242's proposal to change it was rejected. I have no new answer to the stated reason for protecting it, so I leave the passage as it is.

### F4. 12.3 and 160.3: passing-eighth 5ths in inner voices. Kept
- **Problem:** At 12.3 there are 5ths between Cl 2 and Hn/Vla (D4→C4 against G3→F3). At 160.3 the same figure also involves Tbn.
- **Decision: keep.** These are passing eighths under the tune, the same kind of 5ths as the ones kept at 15→16 and 155→156. They fall off the beat and are hidden under the tune's octave doubling, unlike the on-beat 12ths fixed in F1. No change.

### F5. 135–138: parallel diminished sevenths. Kept
- Unit 31 (PDF 551–553) treats chromatic parallel chords as legitimate when the outer voices are clear, and they are clear here. The passage is protected. No change.

### F6. Sibelius 2/iv comparison: the recapitulation's ostinato stays pp. Kept
- **Comparison:** Sibelius builds his second-theme recapitulation over a scalar ostinato to the main climax. This finale's viola ostinato recap at 171–186 stays pp, and its build comes later, at 199–223. The finale's choice is a defensible variant: it saves the climax for the arrival at 223, which carries the cyclic weight.
- **Conductor's view:** A conductor would accept the coda as the work of a skilled composer. The brass chorale and the C-major arrival work as Sibelius's coda does: a chorale built on the first theme's rising head, held back until the last pages.
- Both passages are protected. No change.
