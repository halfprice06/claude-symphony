## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 265-268: movement I's head crosses F1 in the same octave (applied)
**Problem.** The upper half of the head is in Fl 1-2 and Vln I (G5-E6). The lower half is in Ob 1-2, Cl 1-2 and Vln II (G4-E5), and it lies inside the range of F1 (Tpt 1-2 a2, Hn 1-2, violas, C5 down to E4). The two themes cross. On the accented downbeats of 266 and 268 the head's C5 sounds against F1's B♭4, a second in the same octave. At the last eighth of 265 and 267 the head's B4 sounds against F1's A4. With four brass at ff on F1, that woodwind and Vln II octave is covered, and what remains audible is the clash.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 106: wood-wind and brass combinations "possess the same features as combinations in the melodic line". Two lines of different groups stay clear only when each keeps its own register. Crossing (PDF 108-109) is recommended only to blend parts of one chord, not two themes. PDF 110-111: woodwind belongs above the trumpets. Cycle 24 already separated the heads by register at 269-272. 265-268 is the only place where they still cross.
**Fix (applied).** In finale.ily, bars 19-22 of `finaleOboeOne`, `finaleOboeTwo`, `finaleClOne`, `finaleClTwo` and `finaleVlnTwo`: `r8 g''8(\ff e'''4.->) d'''8( c'''8 b''8 | c'''4->) r4 r2 |` (twice), replacing the G4-E5 version. Ob E6 at ff is within the normal range. The head is now a seven-part unison at G5-E6 above F1, and the seconds become ninths.

### 2. 273-274: in the closing chain, Cl 1 doubles a stationary brass note while the E-E♭-F line has no woodwind (applied)
**Problem.** Cl 1 holds C5 at 273-274, already held by Tpt 1 and Hn 3 (and by Fl 1/Vln I an octave higher). Of the three moving inner lines, G-F♯-F-E has Ob 1-2 and C-A-A♭-G has Fl 2 and Cl 2. E-E♭-F-E has only Hn 4 (E4) and Bn 1 (E3), so it is the weakest line, although its E♭ is what makes the F♯°7 at 273.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." PDF 110-111: woodwind forms the harmonic part above the trumpets.
**Fix (applied).** In `finaleClOne`, `\once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #1.2 c''1-> | c''1->` becomes `e''2-> es''2 | f''1->`. 275-276 (the scherzo quotation in Cl 1) is unchanged. The upper woodwinds now play the whole F♯°7 (C6, A5, F♯5, E♭5) with every line moving except the protected top C6. Pitch classes, the top C6 and the brass chain are unchanged. Cl 1 meets the oboes on F5 at 274, which is the meeting cycle 37 kept.

### 3. 75-82: clarinets sit inside the horns (applied)
**Problem.** When Hn 1-2 enter p at 75, the clarinets keep the pp pad they have held since 51. Cl 2 then lies between Hn 2 and Hn 1 (75: B♭3 between G3 and E♭4; also 77, 78, 82). In the other bars both clarinets double the horns at the unison (76, 79-81). The extra parts add no line, and the clarinets' E♭-D alternation has run unchanged for 24 bars.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns". A soft chord for two horns and two bassoons "yields a finely-balanced tone". Bn 1 and Bn 2 already give that layout here, with Bn 2 on the bass.
**Fix (applied).** In sTwo.ily, `sTwoClOne` and `sTwoClTwo` bars 9-16 become `R1*8`. The wind pad now passes from clarinets (67-74) to horns and bassoons (75-82), a change of timbre between groups instead of an added doubling. Each wind chord loses no pitch class that is not also in the tune or the harp. The clarinets rest eight bars before their f entry at 83.

### 5. Coda brass rests 211-214, 246, 265-268: the texture thins where intended; no change
