## Findings

### 1. Recapitulation F1 consequent (159–166): no middleground; the second violins only double the tune. Applied.

Problem. In 151–166 F1 sounds in three octaves in Fl, Ob, Hn 1·2, Vln I, Vln II and violas for all sixteen bars; under it are only the clarinets' broken chords, the brass off-beats and the bass. The consequent (159–166) has the same layout as the antecedent, with Vln I and the flutes an octave higher. There is no countertheme anywhere in the recapitulation of P, although the exposition gave F1 a countersubject in eighths (27–34). The focus for this cycle names "the recap's F1 with its countersubject"; in v107 that countersubject does not exist.

Evidence. Adler PDF 136 (Ex. V-12): an octave statement for the antecedent, a change of texture for the consequent. PDF 139 (Haydn 103, Ex. V-16): "melodizing" the harmony in the second violins sets off the second statement. PDF 142–144: the middleground is a countertheme with its own rhythm; when theme and countertheme share a register, rhythmic difference keeps them apart (Beethoven 7/ii, Ex. V-20). PDF 155 (Grieg): antecedent and consequent doubled differently.

Fix (recP.ily, `recPVlnTwo`, bars 159–165; 166 unchanged). Vln II leaves the tune, which stays in Fl, Ob, Vln I, violas and Hn 1·2, and plays slurred four-eighth groups after P2's countersubject (scale segment, then arpeggio), fitted to the consequent's harmony (i6–i | B♭/D–iv | V7 | V6/5–V4/3 | i–N6 | V | V7 with the 4–3):

```
| es''8( d'' c'' b') c''( es'' g'' es'') | bes'8( d'' f'' d'') aes'( c'' f'' d'') | b'8( g' b' d'') f''( d'' b' d'') | f''8( d'' b' g') d''( b' g' b') | es''8( c'' g' c'') f'( aes' des'' aes') | d''8( b' g' b') d''( f'' d'' b') | b'8( d'' f'' d'') g'( d' f' d') | c'2-> r2 |
```

### 3. 205–210: the head and the run alternate instead of sounding together. Applied.

Problem. The "canon-like layering" of 205–210 is an antiphony. At 205–206 the horns and Tbn 1 play F1's head while both violin sections run up the A♭ Mixolydian scale. At 207–208 both violin sections leave the run to join the head in the winds, so the run stops. At 209–210 the run returns while the winds hold. Head and run are never heard together, and there is no imitation at a time interval.

Evidence. Adler PDF 144, points 3 and 4: theme and countertheme apart in register and different in rhythm, so they can be stated together. PDF 147 (Weber, Ex. V-23): the composer separates the elements by rhythm and states them together. PDF 137 (Ex. V-14): a crescendo built by adding layers.

Fix (codaOne.ily, `codaOneVlnTwo`, 207–208). Vln II keeps the run, so it runs through 205–210 under the head in Fl, Ob, Cl 1 and Vln I:

```
| aes8( bes c' des') es'( f' ges' aes' | bes'8 c'' des'' es'') f''4-> r4 |
```

The head stays in four instruments in two octaves (A♭4 and A♭5), and Vln II's eighths are rhythmically distinct from it. This removes two octave pairs between Fl 2 and Vln II (207–208 in v107) and adds none. I decided against a true stretto here (head entries one bar apart): the retransition stretto at 139–146 already uses that device (protected), and a second F1-head stretto 60 bars later would weaken it.
