## Findings

### 1. 89 beat 2: the 6/3 bridge's last 7-6 should resolve into V4/3 of V, not diatonic II6/5 (applied)

**Problem.** The closing group's bridge (87-89) descends in 6/3 chords with a 7-6 chain (c061, c062) over the bass G-F | E♭-D | C-B♭ into the E♭ cadence at 90. From 88.1 the bass runs I-7-6-5 of E♭ (E♭, D, C, B♭), with V6 already on D at 88.3. Over C at 89.1 Bn 1 and the upper violas hold B♭3 as a 7th and resolve it to A♭3, which with the horns' A♭-F-C makes II6/5 (C-E♭-F-A♭). This is the diatonic form of the progression. The chain's last suspension, which leads into the cadence, has the same colour as the three before it, so the dominant at 89.3 has no leading tone of its own.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 423 (section 5, Ex. 25-11a, Handel's G-major chaconne): "VII6 (or V4/3) of V can be very useful in harmonizing 6 in a bass line that descends from I to V; a passing V6 makes possible a stepwise line ... the 7-6 suspension into the applied VII6 is an almost invariable addition to this progression." The rendered example shows exactly this score's bass shape: I, (V6), suspension into VII6 of V, V. PDF 424 (Ex. 25-11b) gives the II7 version as the "harmonic" realization of the same structure, which is what v502 has. PDF 422 (section 4): chords applied to V contain ♯4 and "significantly increase their drive to the dominant". PDF 419 and 439 (point 3): do not double the temporary leading tone. PDF 420 (Ex. 25-3a): keep the chromatic step in one voice.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-502/work/sections/clos.ily, with a c502 comment at the top).**
- Violas 89: `<es bes>4\< <es a>4 <f bes>2` (was `<es aes>4`). The upper voice's 7-6 now goes B♭3→A♮3 and the A♮ rises to B♭3 on beat 3.
- Bn 1 89: `bes4\< a4 bes4 aes4` (was `bes4\< aes4 aes2`). It doubles the violas' line at the unison (one voice, not a doubled leading tone). It then passes B♭3-A♭3 (8-7 over V) and takes the 7th into G3 at 90 as before. The chromatic A♮-B♭-A♭ stays in one voice.
- Hn 1 89.2: `f'4` (was `aes'4`); Hn 4 89.2: `f4` (was `aes4`). The horns' off-beat chord is now F4 (a2) - C4 - F3. No horn doubles the A♮. Both horns move to B♭ on 89.4 as before.

At 89.2 the chord is C (bass) - E♭3 (violas) - F (horns, Fl 1's F6) - A♮3 (violas, Bn 1): V4/3 of V. Its 7th E♭ resolves to D in Fl 1's E♭6→D6 and in the oboes' and clarinets' runs, as the II6/5's E♭ did before (row 6). The bass, the 6/3 series, the violins' and winds' scale figures, the slurs, the dynamics and the 90 cadence are unchanged. No theme is touched.
