## Findings

**1. Violas 171-186: impossible double stops (applied).** `<d f>` (172, 175, 176, 178, 180, 181, 184, 185) and `<es f>` (182) put both notes below G3, so both must be stopped on the C string. Adler's charts mark these "not possible: both pitches on the same string" (Ex. II-8, PDF 30), and a pizz. dyad needs two strings just like a bowed one (PDF 29). Without *div.* the players must try to play them as double stops (PDF 30). Fix: `recSVla = { r4 <es g>4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz. div."`. The division runs through the arco dyads 187-198, and *unis.* is added at 199: `codaOneVla = { r8 g8(\ff^\markup \italic "unis." …`. No note changes.

**2. Final chord 261-262, violins: triple stops that cannot be played (applied).** Vln I `<c''' e''' g'''>` (C6-E6-G6) would need C6 on the D string. Vln II `<c'' e'' g''>` needs C5 on the D string, E5 on the A string and G5 on the E string, which no single position covers. Both are held for five beats ff, and the bow sustains only two strings (PDF 29). Adler: "When triple- or quadruple-stops are to be divided … mark div. a3" (PDF 31-32, Ex. II-12). Fix in finale.ily: `<c''' e''' g'''>1->~^\markup \italic "div. a3"` and `<c'' e'' g''>1->~^\markup \italic "div. a3"`. The viola chord `<g e' c''>` stays as written: open G plus first position, which Adler calls the most successful kind (PDF 29).

**3. Harp pedal settings (open item from movement II; applied).** The harp first plays at 67 and has no pedal setting anywhere. Its music needs four settings: E♭ major 67-82; G major 123-130; C major 187-197, then E♭ and A♭ at 198; C major 231-237, then F♯ at 238. Adler part 1 PDF 112 asks for the starting setting, and movement II prints one. Fix, as in movement II (diagram above the LH staff, letter names for single changes):
- sTwo: `sTwoHarpL = { es,1\mf^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"--^|^--^"`
- dev 123: `g,8\p^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"---|-v--" b,8 …`
- recS 187: `c,1\mf^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"---|----"`; 197 `f,2_\markup \small "E♭"`; 198 `aes,,1_\markup \small "A♭"`
- sHead 231: `c,1\mf^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"---|----"`; 238 `fis,,1_\markup \small "F♯"`

E and A are both right-foot pedals (PDF 112), so they cannot change together at 198. E is free from beat 3 of 197 (the RH there is `c' f' a' d''`), and the last A♮ falls on beat 4. So E♭ is marked at beat 3 and A♭ at the barline. Each setting change between sections falls in rests of 16 bars or more.

**4. Pizz. to arco: no "arco" in the cellos, and no time for Vln II and violas (applied).** The cellos play pizz. 51-66 and 171-186, then bowed half notes at 67 and 187 marked only "espress." Adler: "the word arco must be printed" (PDF 54). Vln II plucks on beat 4 of 66, 122 and 186 and bows on the next downbeat, and the violas do the same at 122→123: a quarter note (0.36-0.42 s) to take up the bow. Adler calls pizz. to arco the more awkward change, one to avoid without time (PDF 54). Fix: sTwo and recS `\markup \italic "arco, espress."` on the cellos. Drop the last pizz. chord of the bar: sOne 66 Vln II `r4 <es' ges'>4 r2`; recS 186 Vln II `r4 <c' es'>4 r2`; dev 122 Vln II `r4 <d' fis'>4 r2` and Vla `r4 <a c'>4 r2`. The off-beat on beat 4 is still played by the violas at 66 and 186, and at 122 the cello pizz. on beat 1 carries the bar. Each player now has a half note or more.

**5. Long phrase slurs in the violins at the second-theme statements (applied).** Vln I/II 67-70, 71-74, 75-78, 79-82, 187-190, 191-194, 195-198 and the pickup 230 through 234, then 235-238, are each one slur over four bars: 6.7 s at 2 = 72 and 7.3 s at 2 = 66. Two of them carry a crescendo to f (75-78, 195-198) and one is marked f throughout (235-238). Adler: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow" (PDF 38). His Liszt example shows a crescendo under one long slur as "impossible to perform as the composer has marked" (PDF 39-40, Ex. II-23). Fix: one bow per bar, e.g. sTwo Vln I `bes''2.(\mf… g''4) | f''4( es''4 g''4 bes''4) | f'''2( es'''4 d'''4) | c'''2( bes''2) | …`. In sHead, `e''2.~( e''8 d''8) | c''4( a''2-- g''4) | f''4.( e''8 d''4 f''8 a''8) | c'''2( b''2)`, and the 230 pickup `g'4` is unslurred (up-bow, PDF 38). The tenuto on `a''2` inside the bar slur is now a louré (PDF 42). Bars at 1.7-1.8 s per bow are comfortable at mf and f, and the line stays legato. The cellos' two-bar slurs (3.3 s, mf) are fine.

**6. Trumpet 1: no "senza sord." after the muted solo (applied).** "con sord." is printed at 171. The next entry, 199 f, has no "senza sord.", so the player would stay muted through the hammered tutti. Adler's rule for mutes (PDF 59-60: mark the change and allow time) and his rule that every special effect is cancelled in the score (PDF 53, *naturale*) apply here. The 12-bar rest at 187-198 gives enough time. Fix: `codaOneTptOne = { c''4\f^\markup \italic "senza sord." r8 …`. This does not touch the protected muted trumpet with cor anglais at 171-186.

**7. Bars 1-2 and 5-6: the ff resolution falls on a weak up-bow (applied).** All strings take the German sixth `1->\downbow` ff, and the bow ends at the tip. The resolution chord to V on the next downbeat, the goal of the gesture, then falls on an up-bow at the tip. Adler: one plays louder at the frog (PDF 38), and for two vigorous attacks in a row, mark the long note so the following attack is a down-bow (Ex. II-20, Coriolanus). Heavy ff chords are taken as a series of down-bows (PDF 42, Ex. II-29, Tchaikovsky 6). Fix: `\downbow` on the bar-2 and bar-6 quarters in Vln I `<b' g''>4->\downbow`, Vln II `<g d' b'>4->\downbow`, Vla `<d' g'>4->\downbow` and Vc `g,4->\downbow`. The players lift and retake during the whole note's release. No note or dynamic changes in the protected German sixth.

**8. Viola double stops at 1, 5 and 205-219; winds without breath 167-198 (applied).** (a) Vla `<es' fis'>` ff at 1 and 5 is an augmented second with E♭4 on the G string and F♯4 on the D string. That needs a crossed fourth/first finger stretch. `<es' aes'>` at 205-208, hammered ff for four bars, is a fourth in fourth position. Both are playable only with strain, and Adler's normal practice is to divide such dyads between the stand partners (PDF 30). Fix: intro `<es' fis'>1->\ff\downbow^\markup \italic "div."` at 1 and 5, *unis.* at 3 and 9; codaOne `<es' aes'>4^\markup \italic "div." r8 …` at 205, kept through codaTwo's dyads (including the protected D♯→E at 215-216, notes unchanged), and `e'1\f^\markup \italic "unis."` at 220. (b) Cl 1, Cl 2 and Bn 1 hold the pp pad 171-198, continuing from 167, for about 60 s with no rest. Adler sets this against the "tireless" strings: wind players "need to stop from time to time to breathe" (PDF 25). Fix: staggered breaths that never leave the C/B pedal unsupported: Cl 1 `b2. r4` at 178 and `c'2. r4` at 190; Bn 1 `c2 b,4 r4` at 182 and `b,2. r4` at 194; Cl 2 `es2 g4 r4` at 182 and `g2 f4 r4` at 194.
