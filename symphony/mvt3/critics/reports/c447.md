## Findings

### 1. 13–15 and 21–23: the seventh of V7 sits in Hn 4 in the off-beat chords, and its resolution is lost (applied)

**Problem.** Under F1 (mf, "leggiero"), Hn 3·4 and Bn 1 play off-beat chords. Over the G7 at 13–14 and 21–22, Hn 4 holds the seventh F3 while Bn 1 doubles the root G3, so the horn carries the discord and the bassoon a concord. Rimsky-Korsakov's figure on PDF 108 shows exactly this layout as the one to avoid. The horn's F3 then rises to G3 at 14.4 and 22.4 instead of resolving. At 15.2 and 23.2 the off-beat C-minor chord is C4–G3–G3, with no third.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 108: for soft four-part harmony in horns and bassoons, "crossing of parts is to be recommended for the purposes of blend, the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons". The same page says that in a chain of chords the stationary parts go to the brass and the moving parts to the woodwind. The bassoon may go below or inside the horns (PDF 108, second figure). In the forte recapitulation (151–166) Hn 3·4 play the off-beats alone, which also fits PDF 108, so only the exposition has the mixed horn-bassoon layout.

**Fix (in sections/pOne.ily).** Hn 4 holds the concord G3 under Hn 3's D4. Bn 1 takes the seventh F3 below them and resolves it to E♭3 on the next tonic off-beat, which also gives that chord its third:

```
pOneHnFour  bars 13–14:  r4 g4-. r4 g4-. | r4 g4-. r4 g4-.      (was f f | f g)
            bars 21–22:  r4 g4-. r4 g4-. | r4 g4-. r4 g4-.      (was f f | f g)
pOneBsnOne  bars 13–15:  r4 f4-. r4 f4-. | r4 f4-. r4 f4-. | r4 es4-. r4 bes4-.   (was g g | g g | g bes)
            bars 21–23:  r4 f4-. r4 f4-. | r4 f4-. r4 f4-. | r4 es4-. r4 aes4-.   (was g g | g g | g aes)
```

- **Kept, 24–26:** Hn 4's F3→E♭3 into the deceptive VI at 26 stays in the horn. A swap there gives octaves between Hn 4 (G3→A♭3) and the bass (G2→A♭2), or leaves the seventh unresolved.
- **c262 still holds at 22:** the root G3 stays in the off-beat chord under the violins' A♭, now in Hn 4 instead of Bn 1.
- **Unchanged:** the tune, the bass, the clarinets and the recapitulation.

**Result.** The horns now hold an open G–D, the stationary brass part. The reed carries the dissonance and resolves it E♭ by step. The off-beat voice leading is F3–E♭3 instead of F3–G3–G3.

### 2. 136–138 chromatic chain into the stop chord (checked, no change)

The clarinets are above and the bassoons below, and Hn 3·4 enter between them at 137. Every part moves by semitone, so the stationary/moving rule does not apply. Kept.
