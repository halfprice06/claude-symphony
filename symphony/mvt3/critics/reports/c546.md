## Findings

### 1. Bars 168–170, cellos: the bow arrives at the tip before the pizzicato at 171 (applied)

**Problem.** c218 bowed 167–170 in the cellos and basses on Adler PDF 54: 167 up, 168 down with the diminuendo, and 169–170 a held up-bow ending at the frog, a quarter rest (0.36 s at 2 = 84) before "pizz." on 171.1. c434 later gave the cellos Vln I's descent an octave lower: `g,4 d'8( c' b aes g f | d2.)`. With no bow marks the strokes now fall like this:
- 168: A♭ down.
- 169.1: G up.
- The slurred run and the held D3: down-bow, ending at the tip, 0.36 s before the pluck.

The basses still end up-bow at the frog (`g,,1( | b,,2.)`), so only the cellos lost c218's preparation. Adler PDF 54 says it is "much easier to change from arco to pizzicato if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog". PDF 38 says a diminuendo lies naturally on the down-bow.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-546/work/sections/recP.ily).** Slur the French sixth's bass A♭ into the G of V on the same down-bow, and mark the run up-bow:

```
aes,1\f\upbow | aes,1\>( | g,4) d'8(\upbow c' b aes g f | d2.)\p r4 |
```

- The diminuendo stays on the down-bow (A♭ held into G, 5 beats, about 1.8 s).
- The run and the held D3 take one up-bow at p (6 beats, about 2.1 s) and end at the frog.
- The semitone A♭→G is the augmented sixth's bass resolving to the dominant, so legato is its natural reading. The basses still give G1 a fresh attack on 169.1.
- Pitches, rhythm, dynamics, c434's line and the MIDI are unchanged.

This restores c218 without undoing c434.

**Alternative tested and rejected.** I also tried Adler's two up-bows (Ex. II-19, PDF 38): `g,4--\upbow d'8(\upbow …`. It keeps the G detached but puts 7 beats on one up-bow with a stop just before the run, and it separates the A♭→G resolution. The slur is better.

### 2. Candidates from the reading tested on paper and not applied

Each technique the score does not use was weighed against the bars where it could go:

- **Cellos "sul G" for the held D3 at 170 (PDF 33: an open string cannot vibrate).** Rejected. It needs a shift to 4th position on the G string straight after F3 on the D string, inside the p up-bow of finding 1. The D3 is an inner doubling a third under Vln II, and Vln I already carries the sul G colour (c391).
- **String trill on the V6/5 at 147–150 (PDF 48, Ex. II-45).** Rejected. The retransition into the recapitulation over the dominant is protected, and c321/c329/c429's reason (no free held note needs one) still holds.
- **Senza vibrato under Ob 1's recollection of movement II at 95–98 (PDF 33).** Rejected. The chord is already sul tasto and back desks only. A third modifier would make the pale, 20th-century sound the point of the bar, under an espressivo oboe line.
- **A natural harmonic in place of the artificial one at 43–50 (PDF 62–66).** Not possible. No partial of G, D, A or E gives B♭5 on the violin, so the stopped B♭3 with E♭4 touched (Ex. II-72–75) is the correct method and within Adler's limit.
- **A punta d'arco for the violas' leggiero eighths at 19–26 and 159–166 (PDF 41, Ex. II-27, II-34).** Rejected. Those are slurred four-note groups (legato) under an f tune, and tip lightness would thin the one moving inner line.
- **Mutes, la metà, l.v., ponticello, col legno, scordatura, off-the-string bowings (PDF 32, 46–48, 52–53, 58–61).** No unprotected bar in this loud finale gains from them. The declines in c366 and c456 still hold.
