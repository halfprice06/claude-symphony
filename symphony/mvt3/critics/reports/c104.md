## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 207–208: the answer of F1's head is enclosed by a forte horn chord (applied)
- Problem: at 207 the woodwinds and violins take F1's head (Fl 1 + Vln I on A♭5; Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II on A♭4 falling to C4), all f. The four horns at the same moment hold E♭5–C5–G♭4–E♭4, f, and Tbn 1·2 hold C4–G♭3, f. The lower octave of the head (A♭4–E♭4–F–G♭–F–E♭–D♭–C) runs inside the horns' chord, with Hn 1 E♭5 and Hn 3 C5 sounding above it.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 108: clarinets "should rarely be set inside the horns"; "played forte, the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind" (PDF 108–109). PDF 110: with brass in the chord, the woodwind should lie above it. PDF 112: balance "may be secured by judicious dynamic grading, marking the wood-wind one degree louder than the brass". At 205–206 the head is in the horns themselves; at 207 the horns change role to a held chord but keep the same dynamic as the tune.
- Fix (codaOne.ily): mark the held brass mf at 207; the crescendo already starts at 209.
