## Findings

### 1. 222→223, violas: the stop chord's F leaps a seventh instead of falling to E. Applied.
**Bars:** 222–224, violas.
**Problem:** Since v81 the violas go `<d' f'>` (222) → `<e' e''>` (223). The lower desk's D4→E4 is good (c080). The upper desk takes F4 up a major seventh to E5. F (4̂) is a dissonant member of the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ (diminished 5th with B). At 223 every other F in the orchestra also leaves by leap: Hn 1 F4→C5, Cl 2 F4→C6, Fl 2 F5→G6. So in v81 the chord's F resolves by step in no voice. A♭ and B do resolve in their own octaves (Tpt 2, Tbn 1 A♭→G; Tpt 1, Fl 1, Bn 1 B→C).
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 54: dissonances "normally arise out of stepwise motion; this rule governs the melodic aspect of dissonance treatment." Unit 22, PDF 377: in vii°7 and its inversions the diminished 5th "tends to resolve normally" (B→C, F→E). PDF 376 allows a transferred resolution, but only when another voice takes it over, and here no voice does.
**Fix (applied):** arrival.ily, `arrivalVla = { e'1:32\fff^\markup \italic "unis." | e'4--(\f e'4-- …` (was `<e' e''>1:32\fff | e'4--(\f^\markup \italic "unis." …`). Both desks now land on E4: the upper desk F4→E4 and the lower D4→E4. E5 is still present in Vln II (`<e'' c'''>`), so the chord keeps every pitch class and register except the violas' doubling of E5. The thread pitch E4 that c080 placed on the tonic is now in all the violas, and it continues into 224's unison E4. This touches the arrival chord (protected since cycle 4) only in the violas, as c080 did. Every other part, the tremolo, the C7 and the 222→223 B→C/G→C couplings are unchanged.

### 2. 254, Hn 4: the S head's closing C grinds against D4 for a half bar. Applied.
**Bars:** 253–255, Hn 3·4 (with Hn 1·2, Vla, Cl 2, Ob 1·2 on the S head).
**Problem:** The second S-head statement ends `b2 c2` over the dominant (bass G, 254). The C is an anticipation of the I6 at 255, which keeps the last root V–I for 262–263. But Hn 4 holds D4 through the whole bar, so on beat 3 the S head's C4 (Hn 1·2, Vla, Cl 2) sounds a major second against D4 inside the horn section, ff, for a half note. A&S defines the anticipation as brief and unaccented. This one falls on the half-bar beat and lasts a half note. That is acceptable in a Romantic texture only if the chord around it gives way, and the held D4 prevents that.
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 54 (dissonance brief and on weak beats), PDF 56 (anticipation, Ex. 3-19/3-20: the anticipated tone is restruck on the strong beat). PDF 50: beat 3 of a quadruple bar, the second beat in 2/2, carries a secondary accent, so this second is heard.
**Fix (applied):** finale.ily, `finaleHnFour`, bar 254 `d'2 c'2` (was `d'1`). Beat 3 becomes F–A–C over G (Hn 3 F4, Ob 2 A4, C in Ob 1/Cl/Hn/Vla, over Tpt 3 G and bass G): the IV-over-V sonority of the Romantic half close. It contains no B, so it does not cadence on I. Hn 4 then restrikes C4 at 255 (`c'1\ff`), so the anticipation is restruck as in Ex. 3-20. The S-head melody itself is unchanged.
