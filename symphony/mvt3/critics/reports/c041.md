## What else I studied

The full v41 sources (pOne, sOne, sTwo, dev, recP, recS, codaTwo, arrival, sHead, finale), the 16-page PDF, HISTORY.md and the do-not-touch list, coherence/finale.md, themes-scherzo.ly and themes-finale-quotes.ly. `themecheck.py` gives 35/35 finale quotations unchanged and 41/41 for the scherzo record against scherzo v18. The scherzo's theme A head (`e4 b8 b8 e4 | g2 fis8 f8 | e4`) still matches the finale's 275–276 (`c4 g8 g8 c4 e4~ | e4 d8 des8 c4`, transposed to C, major), and motive x at 143–145 is unchanged. No quotation needs updating.

Melody across the movement: F1's head (C–G, dotted, then a stepwise fall) is clear in every statement, and the consequent peak A♭6 at 22/162 is strong. F2 is the movement's most singable tune. Its shape is a falling third, a rising arpeggio, a leap of a fifth to an appoggiatura, then a stepwise fall. It reaches the listener clearly at 51, 67 and 187. The weak points I found are where woodwind scoring hides or blurs a statement: the trumpet's first climax, the cor anglais's climax, the G-major hand-over and the end of F1's consequent. The tunes a listener carries away are F1's head, F2 and the chorale top line. All three are restated in the coda except F2, whose C-major statement at 187 is its last. I did not add F2 to the coda. The coda's stages are protected, and F2's major statement at 187 sits close enough to the coda to be remembered.

## Findings

### 1. 61–63: the flute's octave doubling puts B♭6 above the soft trumpet solo (applied)
Problem: at F2's first climax Fl 1 doubles the p–mf trumpet an octave higher. It leaps F6→B♭6 and descends B♭6–A♭6–G♭6–F6–E♭6.
Evidence: Adler's flute register chart (PDF 185, Ex. VII-2) marks G6–C7 as "a bit shrill", and the text says such registers can "only play extremely loud". PDF 186 adds that pitches above A6 "are difficult to produce… The best approach is by scale", and B♭6 here is approached by a leap of a fourth. The owed item is a soft, undoubled trumpet melody. At B♭6 the flute is the highest and most penetrating note in the bar, so the climax a listener hears is a flute's, not the trumpet's. Brahms 1/iv (Adler PDF 188, Ex. VII-9) keeps its flute statement in the brilliant G5–E6 band.
Fix (applied, sOne.ily): Fl 1 at the unison.
```
sOneFluteOne = { R1*10 | r2 r4 … f''4(\p\< | bes''4\mf aes''4 ges''4 f''4)\> | es''2.(\p\! des''4) | R1*3 | }
```
F5–B♭5 is the flute's "sweet" to "clear and brilliant" band. It colours the trumpet's B♭5 without sitting above it. The trumpet line, its p entry and its appoggiaturas are unchanged.

### 2. 181–183: unison oboe and G6 flute cover the cor anglais at its climax (applied; revises the cycle-11 octave at 181–183)
Problem: at the recapitulation's F2 climax the cor anglais and muted trumpet are doubled by Ob 1 at the unison and Fl 1 at the octave (G6).
Evidence: the cor anglais's G5 is written D6. Adler's chart (PDF 205, Ex. VII-47) puts this in its "thin, pinched" register, where "it sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality". The oboe's G5 is in its "warm, prominent" band (PDF 198, Ex. VII-32). PDF 201 says the oboe "can add great articulation and cutting edge… great care must be given… so that an instrument that needs to be featured more prominently is not overshadowed." At 182, the bar where the cor anglais colour matters most, the listener hears an oboe. The flute's G6 starts the shrill band (PDF 185).
Fix (applied, recS.ily): Ob 1 rests at 181–183 (`recSOboeOne = { R1*26 | c''2.(\p\< d''4 | es''1)\f | }`), and Fl 1 doubles at the unison (`d''4(\p\< | g''4\mf f''4 es''4 d''4)\> | c''2.(\p\! bes'4)`). The cor anglais and muted trumpet (protected) are unchanged. 61–63 and 181–183 are now scored the same way.

### 3. 125: the G-major hand-over drops F2's characteristic appoggiatura (applied; this changes an F2 statement)
Problem: in the development's upward hand-over the violas take F2's third bar as `g'2. fis'4 | e'2 d'2`. F2's third bar is a leap of a fifth to an appoggiatura that resolves down (53: F5–E♭5–D♭5; 69: F6–E♭6–D6). The violas' version leaps only a fourth to a chord tone, so the bar that makes F2 recognisable is missing from its G-major statement. That statement is the development's lyrical goal, building to 131.
Evidence: the bassoon solo at 117 uses the same variant (`g'2. f'4`). Adler PDF 226 justifies it there: the bassoon's "extreme upper perfect fifth does not project", and A4 is its lower edge (Ex. VII-89: "thin but intense" turning "thin and often pinched"). The violas have no such limit. With A4 restored, the hand-over states the tune whole once, as at 53 and 69, after the bassoon's register-limited version.
Fix (applied, dev.ily, devVla):
```
r2. d'4(\p^\markup \italic "arco, unis." | a'2 g'4 fis'4 | e'2 d'2) |
```
A4 sits over the E-minor harmony of 125 (Cb E2, harp G–B–E–G) as an appoggiatura to G4, like F5 over A♭ minor at 53. The bassoon solo at 115–122 is kept as it is. Composer: this restores the notes of F2's third bar in one development statement; F2's exported form in finale.md is unchanged.

### 4. 24–26 and 164–166: the oboes end F1's consequent in their honking lowest fifth (applied)
Problem: Ob 1 (and both oboes in the recapitulation) double Vln II and close the consequent on E♭4–D4–C4–B3–D4–C4.
Evidence: Adler PDF 198 says the oboe "becomes thick and tends to 'honk' in the very lowest fifth of its range". The chart's "thick, heavy" band is B♭3–F4, and "the most effective range is from the F in the first space to the C above the staff". The cadence of F1's consequent (26, 166) is the bar where the tune must close cleanly. Here the oboe's coarsest notes sit under it, while Fl 1 and Vln I have the line an octave higher.
Fix (applied): from bar 24 beat 3, Ob 1 (and Ob 1·2 at 164) move up an octave into unison with Fl 1 and Vln I:
```
… | b'4-.) c''8( d''8 g''4-.) f''4-. | es''4( d''8 c''8 b'4 d''4) | c''2-> r2 | }
```
(pOne.ily pOneOboeOne; recP.ily recPOboeOne and recPOboeTwo.) Vln II, violas and Hn 1·2 keep the lower octave, so no pitch class is lost. The ob-1-a-third-below at 162 (critic 001) is untouched.

### 5. 125–130: the new bassoon counter-line (checked, no change)
Bn 1's E3–C4 line lies in the bassoon's "sweet, more subdued, but expressive" register (PDF 226, Ex. VII-89). Adler warns that an accompanied bassoon "gets swallowed up". Here that suits a middleground line under a p tune. It is audible because the harp is pp and the violas and Vln II hold whole notes pp. Its first note E3 doubles the cellos' bass E3 at 125. That lasts a half note and the line separates at once (F♯3–G3–C4), so I left it. Raising the bassoon to mp would compete with the violas' tune. Raising it an octave would cross the violas and Cl 1. Both are worse.

### 6. 273–279: the closing chain and the scherzo quotation (checked, no change)
The woodwinds there are in their best registers: Fl 1 C6–E6 ("clear and brilliant"), Ob 1 G5–E5 ("warm"), Cl 1 written D5 (clarino), bass clarinet written D4 (chalumeau, PDF 217), contrabassoon C2 (lowest twelfth, PDF 230). The top line G–F♯–F–E–D–D♭–C is clear, and the scherzo quotation matches scherzo v18.
