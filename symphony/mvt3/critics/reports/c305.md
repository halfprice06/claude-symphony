## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 32, 65, 185, 214, 251: a semibreve touches the other player's stem (applied)

Problem: on five shared staves LilyPond sets a semibreve directly against a stem, with no gap:
- 32 Fl 1·2: Fl 1 F5 minim (up-stem) on the left, Fl 2's F5 semibreve against that stem.
- 251 Hn 3·4: Hn 3 E4 minim (up-stem) on the left, Hn 4's E4 semibreve against it (the start of the finale's horn statement).
- 65 Cl 1·2 and 185 Cl 1·2: Cl 1's semibreve (B♭3; G3) on the left, Cl 2's minim on the same note to the right with its down-stem on the left side of its head, so the stem touches the semibreve.
- 214 Hn 3·4: Hn 3's A♭3 crotchet (up-stem) on the left, Hn 4's G♭3 semibreve a second below against the stem.
In each case the semibreve reads as if it had a stem, so a player can take it for a minim.

Evidence: Gould PDF 66: "Keep noteheads separate; stems should not touch. Semibreves in particular should not appear to be joined to stems." Her examples set the semibreve beside the head, not the stem, and she gives the remedy: "Some editions reverse the order of noteheads so that the semibreve is never adjacent to a stem. The part on the left aligns vertically with a part on another stave." The score already uses that order at 212 and 215 (Ob 1·2, semibreve left of Ob 1's up-stemmed minim), and earlier cycles separated other unisons by hand with `force-hshift` at 111 and 273. These five bars had not been handled.

Fix (applied, score only through `\tag #'score`; parts and MIDI unchanged): reverse the order so the semibreve sits on the stem-free side, 0.15-0.2 space from the other head (measured by the collision report):
- pTwo.ily: `pTwoFluteOne` bar 32 `\tag #'score { \once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #1.1 } f''2 aes''2~`; `pTwoFluteTwo` `\tag #'score { \once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #0 } f''1~`.
- finale.ily: `finaleHnThree` bar 251 `\tag #'score { \once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #1.1 } e'2 g'2`; `finaleHnFour` `\tag #'score { \once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #0 } e'1`.
- sOne.ily (65) and recS.ily (185): `force-hshift = #1.1` before Cl 1's `bes1~` and `g1`; `#0` before Cl 2's `bes2 aes2` and `g2 f2`, so the minim with its down-stem stands left and the semibreve right of its head.
- codaTwo.ily (214): `#1.1` before Hn 3's `aes4( bes4 c'2)`, `#0` before Hn 4's `ges1`; this is Gould's reversed "adjacent notes" example (semibreve lower left, crotchet upper right).
A c305 comment above each changed definition gives the reason. No note, rhythm, slur or dynamic changed; the build's score.midi is byte-identical to v305's.

### 2. 219: the bar-length notes after the key change (tested, no change needed; closes c245's deferred item)

c245 left 219 as the one bar-length bar not given `\cen`, with the fix `\once \override Score.KeyCancellation.space-alist.next-note = #'(semi-fixed-space . 3.2)`. I applied that override in the scratch copy: the page did not change at all (the empty C-major key signature, not the cancellation, is the last item before the notes). Measuring the v305 page instead: in 219 the semibreves start about 41% of the way into the bar, because the five naturals of the cancellation stand before them. That is what Gould PDF 55 asks for ("just left of the centre of the bar"). I reverted the override; 219 follows the rule and needs nothing.

### 3. Unisons and seconds with meeting stems at 24, 38, 103, 112, 200, 202 (checked, no change)

The collision report also lists eight places where an up-stem and a down-stem meet at the join of two heads (for example 38.3, Tbn 1's B♭2 crotchet and Tbn 2's B♭2 minim). Gould's first example on PDF 66 ("unison notes", "adjacent notes") is set exactly this way: the lower part is offset to the right and its down-stem continues the upper part's up-stem. Her "stems should not touch" applies to stems meeting a head, which is the semibreve case in Finding 1. These follow the book and should not be "fixed".
