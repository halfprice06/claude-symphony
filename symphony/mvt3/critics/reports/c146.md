## Findings

### 1. 223: the timpani roll fff together with the bass drum fff covers the arrival's owed sounds (applied)
- **Bars:** 223.
- **Problem:** At the arrival the timpani roll fff for a whole Maestoso bar (about 2.1 s) and the bass drum strikes fff at the same moment as the crash. The sounds this bar exists for are thin or inner ones: the string tremolo, the violins' C7 and the violas' E4 that closes the E♭/E♮ thread. Tpt 1's C5 is the only loud one.
- **Evidence:** This is the combination Piston warns against, quoted twice in the assigned reading: "When you write a fortissimo for the timpani doubled by bass drum, don't expect to hear anything else from the rest of the orchestra" (Adler ch. 12, PDF 55). "If you hit the bass drum hard, it does not make any difference what the chord is above it" (PDF 84). "A loud bass drum or cymbal roll can obliterate the entire orchestra, no matter how loud the tutti may be playing" (ch. 14, PDF 141). The movement already uses this level-below rule for the timpani roll at 151 (c044, Adler PDF 184). At 278, the last chord, nothing inner has to come through, so the same doubling there caps the climax (PDF 116) and stays.
- **Fix (arrival.ily):**
  `arrivalTimp = { c1:32\fff\> | c4\f ...` (the roll is attacked fff and dies to the f already printed at 224)
  `arrivalPerc = \drummode { << { \tweak style #'xcircle cymc1^\fff } \\ { bd1_\ff } >> | R1*11 | }`
  The crash stays fff and the bar's fff stays in the whole orchestra, so the protected path from sub. p at 219 to fff at 223, and 222→223, are not touched. Only the two drums are weighted below the tutti.

### 2. No mallet indication anywhere: the timpanist's last instruction is the scherzo's "soft sticks" (applied)
- **Bars:** 1, 215, 255.
- **Problem:** The finale never names a stick. The scherzo's timpani part ends "soft sticks" (scherzo coda, from its 341), so a player who follows the parts begins the finale's accented ff A♭ (bar 1) with soft sticks. Movement I ("medium sticks", "soft sticks") and the scherzo ("hard sticks", "soft sticks") both name them. The soft rolls 219-246 (sub. p, pp at 235) and the ff cadence 261-279 need different sticks, and there are rests long enough to change: four bars of Largamente at 215-218, six bars at 255-260.
- **Evidence:** "Usually, special timpani mallets are used. These come in hard, medium, and soft varieties" (Adler ch. 12, PDF 80). The composer should specify "hard, medium, soft" (PDF 61), and beater indications save the players time (PDF 88). The player needs time to change mallets (PDF 61 note; ch. 14, PDF 110-111).
- **Fix:** intro.ily, a third line in the bar-1 label: `... \italic "(retune in the pause: low E → G, G → A♭, B → C, high E → E♭)" \italic "hard sticks" }`. codaTwo.ily 215: `R1^\markup \small \italic "medium sticks" | R1*3 |`. finale.ily 255: `R1^\markup \small \italic "hard sticks" |`. The bar-1 label is protected so that it matches the scherzo's muta label. The tuning text is unchanged, and the scherzo's own bar-1 label has the same three lines (tuning, retune, "hard sticks"), so the two labels now match more closely.

### 3. 278-279: the bass drum stops on 279 beat 1 while the cymbals and the orchestra hold to beat 2 (applied)
- **Bars:** 278-279.
- **Problem:** `<cymc~ bd>1` ties only the cymbals into the final quarter. The bass drum's whole note therefore ends, and is damped, on 279.1, under the orchestra's last quarter. The cymbals ring on to 279.2 and stop with everyone, as written. The 279 cymbal note also loses the circled head it has at 278.
- **Evidence:** The part must show "how long a note may ring" (Adler ch. 12, PDF 80). The finale's other percussion releases (223→224, 263→264.1) all end with the music.
- **Fix (finale.ily):** `<\tweak style #'xcircle cymc~ bd~>1->\fff | <\tweak style #'xcircle cymc bd>4 r4 r2\fermata | }`

No other finding: every other point in the reading notes is followed (see the table and "Checked, no change").
