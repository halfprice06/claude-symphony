## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Root-position V–i and V–I in C at 14→15, 22→23, 154→155, 162→163 and 191→193 (applied)
**Problem.** The cadence plan's central rule is that no root-position V–I or V–i in C comes before 254–255. The beat scan finds six root-position V→I(i) in C, and only the last one (254→255) is allowed:
- 14 b. 4 G–B–D, bass G1 → 15 b. 1 C–E♭–G, bass C2 (the timpani C3 marks the downbeat).
- 22 b. 4 G–B–D–F → 23 C minor, same layout.
- 154→155 and 162→163, the ff recapitulation, with the tuba on the bass at 162.
- 191–193: ii7 (D1) – V7 (G1) – I (C2) in C major. This is a complete expanded cadence of the Example 9-2 type, with the melody F | G F E D | E.

The rule exists so that the final cadence sounds new. As things stand it is the sixth root-position dominant-to-tonic in C, not the first.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 149–150 §16: a subordinate progression inside a phrase that "threatens to produce an inappropriate cadential effect" takes "an inversion of V(7) or VII6 in place of the V". PDF 142 §9 says the same: inverted dominants are "particularly useful in avoiding an unwanted cadential effect". PDF 148 rule 1 gives the voice leading: an inverted V's bass moves by step, or arpeggiates V first.

**Fix (applied).** The bass moves to D in the second half of the bar, so the chord is V4/3 and the bass D steps down to C. The melody, the F1 head and the consequent peak are unchanged.
- pOne.ily, Vc, Bsn 2 at 14 and 22: `b,2 g,2` → `b,2 d2`. Cb, and Cbsn at 22 only: `b,,2 g,,2` → `b,,2 d,2`.
- recP.ily (154, 162), same change in Bsn 1, Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, Vc, Cb, and in the tuba at 162.
- recS.ily at 192 (V6/5 for the whole bar): Cb and Hp LH `g,,1` → `b,,1`, Bsn 2 `g,1` → `b,1`.

After the change, the scan finds 254→255 as the only root-position V–I in C.

### 2. Last chorale 251–253: parallel fifths with the top line and parallel octaves with the bass (applied)
**Problem.** Tpt 3 and Cl 2 move C4→A4 (251→252) while Tpt 1·2 and the top line move G→E. These are real parallel fifths inside the brass chorale. Then at 252→253 (vi → iiø6/5) Tpt 3, Cl 2 and Vla move A4→F4 in parallel octaves with the bass A→F.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 136 (§3, Ex. 9-3b and 9-3c "not", "even worse") and PDF 137 (§4, Ex. 9-5): when two chords share no common tone, lead the upper voices in contrary motion to the bass. PDF 141 warns about parallel octaves when moving into IV or II6. The last chorale is the most exposed four-part writing in the movement. Only its top line is protected.

**Fix (applied).** finale.ily bar 252 (section bar 10) in Tpt 3, Cl 2 and Vla: `a'1` → `e'1`. The inner line becomes E–F–F♯–G (252–254), a rising chromatic tenor into the German sixth and V. The top line `… c'2 b2 | c'1`, the bass E–A–F–A♭/G–C, and the F♯ in Tpt 3 and Vla at 254 are unchanged.

### 3. 239–240: the cadential 6/4 of the horn call sounds over its own leading tone (applied)
**Problem.** Over the G bass at 239–240, Bsn 1 holds B3 from the first beat of 239, under the strings' 6/4 (Vln II C4, Vln I E5) and under the trumpets' and horns' C. So the 4th and its resolution sound together for five beats. Also, when Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2 resolve C→B at 240 beat 2, the violins keep C4 and E5 until 241. The trumpets' B5 then sounds against an unresolved 6/4.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153 §3: the 4th "is dissonant when it sounds between the bass and one of the upper parts … it will resolve by step to the 3rd of dominant harmony". The 6/4 "functions as a dissonant chord". PDF 154 §6 says it resolves over a stationary bass. Example 10-2 derives the 6/4 from a 4–3 suspension, and a suspension does not sound together with its resolution tone.

**Fix (applied).** sHead.ily, bars 9–10 of the section:
- Bsn 1 `b1\< | b1` → `c'1\< | c'4 b2.`
- Vln I `<g' e''>1` (240) → `<g' e''>4 <g' d''>2.`
- Vln II `<c' g'>1` (240) → `<c' g'>4 <b g'>2.`

Everyone now resolves 6–5 / 4–3 together with the trumpets on 240 beat 2. The bass stays on G, and 241 is still V4/3 → I at 243 (Ex. 10-11 principle). The horn call E–C–B→C is unchanged.

### 4. 246: the 6/4 in the theme combination sits on a full V7 (applied in part)

**Fix (applied).** finale.ily bar 4 of the section:

### 5. 216→217: the 6/4's 4th rises in three parts (applied in two)
**Problem.** At the cadential 6/4 after the German sixth (215→216→217), the 4th C is in nine parts. Six of them fall to B, but Hn 1·2 (C4→D4), Vln II (C5→D5) and Vla (C4→D4) rise. Vln II's C5→D5 runs against Tpt 1's C5→B4 in the same octave.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153 §3 and PDF 160 point 2: the 4th "must resolve downward by step". PDF 154 §5 says the 4th is doubled only in free textures. The orchestra here is free, so the doubling is acceptable, but the doubled 4th still falls.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily, 217: Vln II `<d'' g''>1` → `<b' g''>1`, Vla `<d' f'>1` → `<b f'>1`. I left Hn 1·2 on D4: B is already in six parts, and D stays in Ob, Tpt 3 and Hn 1. The violas' D♯→E (215–216) and the top line D♭–C–B–C are unchanged.

### 6. 150→151: the V6/5 bass leaps to E♭ (applied)
**Problem.** The retransition holds V6/5 ff over B1 for four bars. The bass then leaps a diminished 4th to E♭ (i6) at the reprise and reaches C only on beat 3.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 148, harmonic-syntax rule 1: "The bass tones of V6, VII6, and the inversions of V7 must continue by step to I or I6 unless the bass tones occur within an arpeggiation that expands V or V7."

**Fix (applied).** retr.ily, last bar: B.cl and Bsn 2 `b,2. r4` → `b,2. d4`; Cbsn, Tuba and Cb `b,,2. r4` → `b,,2. d,4`; Vc `<b, f>2. r4` → `<b, f>2. <d f>4`. The bass arpeggiates V7 (B→D) and D steps up to E♭ on the downbeat, a V4/3–i6 pickup. The upper parts keep their quarter rest.

The first version, with the D on beat 3 or as a half note, pushed the score to a 17th page. The quarter-note pickup builds with 0 warnings and 16 pages.
