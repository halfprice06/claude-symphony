## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 205-206: Vln II's lower-octave run shadows the horns' F1 head in its own register (applied)
**Problem.** At 205-206 the F1 head is in Hn 1-4 (A♭4-E♭4-F4 | G♭4-F4-E♭4-D♭4-C4, f) and Tbn 1 an octave below (A♭3). The woodwinds rest, so nothing doubles the head above. Vln II plays the rising run an octave under Vln I, A♭3-B♭3-C4-D♭4 | E♭4-F4-G♭4-A♭4 in 205. That run starts on Tbn 1's A♭3 and then climbs through the horns' range. On beat 3 of 205 it meets the head's E♭4 at the unison, and on the next eighths it sits a second away from the head (F4 against E♭4, G♭4 against F4). This is the shadowing Sebesky warns against (PDF 211, Ex. 279), and it is also the case in Ex. 1 (PDF 12-13): a moving inner line in the soloist's register that should go so that the primary element comes through. At 207-208 the head is doubled at A♭5 by Fl 1 and Vln I, so a run in the lower register does no harm there. At 205-206 the run is the problem.
**Fix (applied).** codaOne.ily, `codaOneVlnTwo`, 205-206 in unison with Vln I:
`aes'8(\f bes' c'' des'') es''( f'' ges'' aes'' | bes''8 c''' des''' es''') f'''4-> r4 |`
From 207, Vln II returns to the lower octave (A♭3) as before, so c107's continuous run is kept. The leap down to A♭3 comes after a quarter rest.

### 2. 205-206: violas strike the head's first two pitches inside its register (applied)
**Problem.** The violas div. continue the hammered rhythm on E♭4/A♭4, six attacks a bar at f. These are the pitches of the head's accented A♭4 half note and its E♭4, and the dyad sits between the head's top (A♭4) and its last note (C4). With the Vln II run also in this register, the four horns' head was surrounded by three string layers in its own octave. By Sebesky's focus rule (PDF 13) the background must not divert the ear from the primary element. By Ex. 1 (PDF 13) the cure is to lighten the middle register, not to add weight to the theme.
**Fix (applied).** codaOne.ily, `codaOneVla`, 205-206 `<aes' c''>` in the same rhythm (`<aes' c''>4\f^\markup \italic "div." r8 <aes' c''>8 <aes' c''>4 r8 <aes' c''>8 | <aes' c''>4 r8 <aes' c''>8 <aes' c''>4 r8 <aes' c''>8 |`). The dyad now lies on and above the head's starting note, and the head moves away from it. 207-210 are unchanged (E♭4/A♭4, then E♭4/G♭4 into 211's D♭4/F4), because there the head's primary octave is A♭5.

### 3. 130: Hn 3's repeated D4 quarters sit on the F1 head (applied)
**Problem.** c037 gave Hn 3-4 repeated quarters at 129-130 as a crescendo layer into 131. Later, c151 and c154 put F1's head at 130 into Hn 2 + Hn 4 + violas, starting on D4 (`d'2-> a4. b8`). Hn 4 left the quarter layer to take the head. Hn 3 kept it, so on the same staff it strikes D4 on all four beats: on the head's first pitch at beat 1, and across its dotted rhythm on beats 3-4. The score shows the result clearly (page 7): one horn staff with the head's half note and dotted quarter under a row of quarters on the same D. The added head now does the crescendo layer's job. This is Sebesky's economy rule (PDF 12) and his warning against treating an earlier note as sacred (PDF 13).
**Fix (applied).** dev.ily, `devHnThree`, 130: `d'1\mf\<` (was `d'4\mf\< d'4 d'4 d'4`), then `d'1\f` at 131 as before. The dynamics are equal to Hn 4's `\mf\<` on the shared staff. The D7 is complete without the quarters (F♯ in Hn 1 and the harp, A in the flute and violins, C in Bn 1).

### 4. 131: Bn 1 leaves the seventh C4 for A3 inside the head's continuation (applied)
**Problem.** At 131 the head goes on C4-B3-A3-G3-F♯3 in Hn 2-4 and the violas (f). Bn 1 played `c'2 a2`, so its A3 on beat 3 lay a second above the head's G3 and over its F♯3, in the same tenor register. Then it rose to B3 at 132, which left the seventh C unresolved in that voice. This is a small case of Ex. 1 (PDF 12-13). It is also the same kind of change c137 made at 126 (Bn 1 kept off the melody's notes).
**Fix (applied).** dev.ily, `devBsnOne`, 131: `c'1\f`, then `b4->` at 132 as before. The seventh is held over the head and resolves C4→B3. The A of D7 stays in Hn 1 (A4), Fl 1, the violins and the harp.
