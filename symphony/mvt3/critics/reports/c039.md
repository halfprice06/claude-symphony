## What else I studied

**Beethoven 5/iv.** Its finale adds piccolo, contrabassoon and trombones for the first time in the symphony. It brings back the scherzo, pp and in its own metre, just before the recapitulation. Its coda compresses the themes, speeds up to Presto, and ends with 29 bars of C-major chords ([Lumen notes](https://courses.lumenlearning.com/vccs-tcc-mus121-1/chapter/symphony-no-5-in-c-minor/), [Aspen notes](https://www.aspenmusicfestival.com/program_notes/view/ludwig-van-beethoven-symphony-no.-5-in-c-minor-op.-67)).

This finale matches Beethoven's design in the places that matter. Motive x returns on the timpani in the retransition (143–145), where Beethoven puts his scherzo recall. The C-major arrival (223) introduces colours held back until then: string tremolo, C7 and the first cymbal. The only root-position V–I comes very late (262–263). A conductor would accept the large shape.

The two points where it falls short of Beethoven's certainty are both at the end:
- Beethoven never lets the tempo fall without preparing it. Here the tempo drops from 2 = 100 to 2 = 72 at 273 with no warning.
- The last bars blur the tonic with a semitone clash that Beethoven's plain C major avoids.

## Findings

**1. 271–273: the Largamente arrives with no preparation (new since v38).**
- Problem: Allegro molto 2 = 100 runs to the end of 272, and then 2 = 72 starts on the downbeat of 273. That is a 28% drop in one beat, in the middle of the F1 continuation (D6 at 272 falling to C6 at 273). Nothing in the score or the MIDI prepares it.
- Evidence: Rimsky's broad tutti endings write the broadening out first: Ex. 209 (PDF 375) "allargando assai" before the fermata chord, and Ex. 272 (PDF 438) "riten. poco" before the held final chord. This score does the same itself at 221–222 (allargando into the Maestoso). Beethoven 5/iv never changes tempo without a transition.
- Fix, applied in global.ily: `s1*10 s1*6 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" \mt 184 s1 \mt 164 s1 \tempo "Largamente" 2 = 72 s1*6 s1`. This keeps the c038 Largamente and its tempo.

**2. 276: the quotation's D–D♭ clashes with a held C in the same octave and the same brass colour.**
- Problem: at 276 Tpt 1·2 play E5–D5–D♭5–C5. At the same time Hn 3 holds C5 and Vln II holds C5–E5. Fl 1 plays E6–D6–D♭6–C6 against Vln I's tremolo C6. Both clashes are minor seconds at the same octave, in a crescendo towards fff.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 132–134 says passing notes against harmony notes in the same register are discordant, that the effect is harsher the slower the passage, and that between wood and brass the harmony should sit an octave away and be softer. At 2 = 100 these eighths lasted 0.15 s. At the Largamente they last 0.21 s, so the clash is more exposed than in v38. Beethoven's last chords are pure triads.
- Fix, applied in finale.ily (276 only): Hn 3 `c''1\< | g'1` (no longer tied), Vln I `<e'' g''>1:32`, Vln II `<g' e''>1:32`. Every pitch class of the chord stays, C remains in the bass and in the quotation itself, and 277 is unchanged. The protected top C6 over 273–274 and the quotation are untouched.

**3. 279: the timpani still has an accent on a tied note.**
- Problem: c029 removed accents on tied notes at 279, but the timpani still reads `c1:32\fff~ | c4->`.
- Evidence: an accent cannot be re-attacked on a tied note. Gould PDF 45–47 (ch. 2, ties) and c029.
- Fix, applied: `c4 r4 r2\fermata`.

**4. 133–134: the diminuendo keeps the horns to the end.**
- Problem: the woodwinds stop at 132, but Hn 3·4 hold D4/B3 with the strings until beat 4 of 134.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 130 says a diminuendo removes instruments in the order brass, woodwinds, strings.
- Fix, applied in dev.ily: Hn 3 `d'2.\> r4\! | R1` and Hn 4 `b2.\> r4\! | R1`. The strings carry the diminuendo alone into the pp at 135. The c038 rule (hairpins end on a rest) still holds.

**5. 67–186: the harp's 8 bassa line runs over 100 bars of rests.**
- Problem: `sTwoHarpL` opens `\ottava #-1` and never closes it. The dotted line therefore crosses the closing group, the development and the retransition rests until recS resets it. On page 6 it is printed under 119–130.
- Evidence: Gould PDF 45 says that across long rests the notes take separate octave signs. The development arpeggios (lowest note G2) read on the bass staff without a sign.
- Fix, applied: `... bes,,1 | es,1 \ottava #0 | }` in sTwo.ily. The sound does not change.

**Not changed (decided):**
- The scherzo quotation at quarter = 144 is 1.8 times slower than the scherzo. The head's rhythm (crotchet, two quavers, crotchet | minim, two quavers) stays intact, and broadening it is the transformation, as Beethoven's Presto turns the earlier themes into a summation. I keep it.
