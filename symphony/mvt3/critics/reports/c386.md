## What else I studied

Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, PDF 46-47. The resonance of the middle and low strings "is slightly prolonged, and dies away gradually", so the player stops the strings by hand when the harmony changes. Chords not to be rolled are marked "non arpeggiato". Harmonic chords have at most three notes, two in the left hand and one in the right. Harmonics are "only possible played quite softly". A forte glissando through the lower strings is "only permissible as an embellishment". I also read PDF 138 and 140, where pizzicato strings with harp are the setting for a solo voice, and MOLA PDF 5 (harp pedalling is not printed in the part).

## Findings

### 1. 278-279: the harp's last chord rings into the closing fermata; damp it with the tutti (applied)

Problem. At 278 the harp plucks C2-G2-C3 ff in the left hand, with G6 at the top of the glissando in the right hand. Both carry `\laissezVibrer`, and the harp's 279 is `r4 r4 r2\fermata`. The orchestra holds the fff chord through 279 beat 1 and stops together (`c4 r4 r2\fermata` in every part; the cymbal and bass drum stop as quarters). Then the score has a fermata on silence. The harp is the only instrument that keeps sounding there, because nothing tells the harpist to damp: "l.v." means let it ring, and a written rest does not stop a harp string. Its lowest three strings, wire-wound gut in the register Adler calls "somber, dark, and sonorous", ring for several seconds after the tutti cut-off, so the finale's last silence would carry a C2-G2-C3 hum.

Evidence. Adler PDF 117: a harp string sounds until the hand damps it, and the damping must be asked for (sons étouffés, Ex. IV-12). PDF 112 and 113-114: the low strings are wire-wound and sonorous. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: the middle and low strings ring on, and the player stops them by hand. The score already damps at the end of every other harp passage: 82 (c116), 131 (c206), 134.3 (c296) and 243 (c116). Movement I damps the same chord, C2-G2-C3, with the basses and timpani at its last bar (I 182, "étouffez"; mvt1.md). Only the finale's last chord is left to ring.

Fix (finale.ily, end of finaleHarpL):
```
<c, g, c>4\ff_\markup \small \italic "non arpegg."\laissezVibrer r4 r2 | r4 \once \override TextScript.self-alignment-X = #RIGHT r4^\markup \italic "étouffez" r2\fermata | }
```
The word sits on 279 beat 2, where the tutti releases, and is right-aligned so it ends at that rest instead of crossing the final barline. The l.v. through 278 and the tie into 279.1 are unchanged, so the chord still sounds for the whole held fff, as c204-c257 intended. Pitches, the glissando, the pedal diagram, "non arpegg." and the MIDI are unchanged (score.midi is byte-identical to v386). For finale.md: the finale's last harp chord now damps at 279.2, as movement I's last harp chord does at I 182.

## Checked and decided (no change)

- Harmonic chords (Adler PDF 116; Rimsky PDF 47). This is the one technique in the reading that the score uses only in its simplest form: single B♭ harmonics at 43, 45 and 47. A C-major harmonic chord (C-E-G) would answer movement II's A♭-C-E♭ (II 83) and the scherzo's E-G♯-B (scherzo 375), and so would complete the C-A♭-E cycle in harp harmonics. I looked for a place where it would be "accompanied very lightly" (PDF 116) and decided against every one:
  - 43-47 as two-note chords B♭-F: c228 protects the harmonics, and c296 found that F5 adds nothing over the violas' C♭4/G♭4.
  - A fourth chime at 49: c206 ends the chimes before 49 on purpose, so that Ob 1's A♭5 and Hn 1's entry start the approach to the solo.
  - 169-170: the chord is V♭9 with A♭4 in Cl 1 and the stopped horn. Every consonant harmonic pair there either rubs a semitone against the A♭4 or leaves B4 ringing over the C-minor downbeat at 171.
  - 234.3: the strings hold E5 mf, which is the harmonic's own pitch.
  - 234.4: the harp would play a bell chord on an upbeat, under the violins' mf G4 pickup, in the protected plagal close, one beat before its own arpeggio. That is an ornament, not an improvement.
- Multiple glissandi (Ex. IV-14). A contrary-motion left-hand glissando at 277 cannot end in time to place C2-G2-C3 on 278.1. Making that chord a single C2 would undo movement I's chord (c206). A double-note glissando in the right hand would land a second note beside G6, and nothing is gained under an fff tutti (PDF 114: ff at the top equals mf in the middle).
- Bisbigliando and trills (PDF 118). The soft places that fit (135-138, 171-186, 219-222, 223-234) are protected. Before 223, a tremolo colour would also blur the rule that string tremolo first arrives at 223.
- Harp with pizzicato strings under the bassoon solo at 115-122 (Adler PDF 117; Rimsky PDF 138, 140; movement II's harp with the pizzicato basses at II 37-44). The pedals would work: A and F♯ change in the 32 bars after 82, and B♮ and E♮ change together, one with each foot, in 122's rests. I rejected it because the change from dry pizzicato to the ringing harp arpeggios at 123 is the point of "bassoon, then harp" (global.ily at 115). Starting the harp eight bars early would weaken its entry.
- Pedals, ringing and damping. The settings follow each other in rests of 18 bars or more, and no two pedals on one side move together. 70.3, 123-130 and 235-242 keep the clashes inside the bar that normal half-bar damping deals with (c296 reached the same result). 132's tenth-spaced solid chord and its damp at 134.3 follow PDF 114-115.
- Other instruments. The timpani's notes match G, A♭, C, E♭ with the retunings at 37/43 and 206/215. Non-divisi double stops at 91-94 and 277 lie on adjacent strings. Brass at 247-279 can breathe: horns 1·2 at 250, 254 and 260; trumpets 1·2 rest at 251-254 and 269-270 and breathe at 258; horns 1·2 rest at 269-272 and horns 3·4 at 265-268. The ranges are as accepted (Tpt C6, Cb C extension).
