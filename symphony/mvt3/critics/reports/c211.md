## Findings

### 1. 123-124: the cellos' F2 entry should be on the D string (applied)

**Problem.** The development's F2 statement begins with the cellos alone, p, "arco, espress.": D4-B3 | A3-G3-B3-D4. The violas take over on 124 beat 4. Without a string mark, a cellist plays D4 and B3 on the A string, takes the open A3 on 124's downbeat, crosses to the D string for G3, and goes back to the A string. That makes three colours in six notes, and the theme's lowest strong-beat note is an open string. Adler calls the cello's D string "the most musically captivating string on this instrument; it has a warm and lyrical quality", and the A string "the most brilliant and piercing" (PDF 99). He says an open string "stands out peculiarly" in an expressive line, where "one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture" (PDF 73-74). He gives timbre as the one reason to tell a player which string to use (PDF 73). Tchaikovsky marks the string for a cantabile finale entry in the same way (Tchaikovsky 6/iv bar 148, sul G).

**Why it matters for the melody.** This is the one place where F2 is played by low strings alone, and it is the start of the hand-over Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I + Fl 1 (c033), which builds by adding brighter instruments. If the cellos start on the warm D string, each later entry is brighter than the one before. As it stands, the first entry already has the A string's edge and an open-string accent.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily, devVc):**
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul D"
d'2.(\p^\markup \italic "arco, espress."\startTextSpan b4 | a4 g4 b4 d'4)\stopTextSpan |
```
The dashed line ends on the phrase's last note, so no "ord." is needed (the convention of c165 at 81 and of 235). On the D string the phrase runs from first position (G3) to fourth position (D4), which is easy for a section. The 123-130 protection rests on the hand-over order (c033), the fifths fix at 126-127 (c181) and the entry points. This change keeps all three: no note, rhythm, dynamic or entry moves. In the build, the spanner prints under "arco, espress." with no collision and the page count stays at 16.

### 2. 67-76: Vln II sul D on F2's lower octave, considered and rejected

Adler's D string is "warm and rather subdued" (PDF 75), and Vln II's octave E♭4-F5 at 67-76 fits it (sixth position at most, at 69). It would make the tune's colour open up at the 77-78 peak. But Adler also calls the D string "possibly the least powerful of the four". The balance of 67-82 was set by c094, c098 and c121: the counter-melody is mp in violas and cellos in unison, against the tune in two violin octaves at mf. Weakening the tune's lower octave would undo that balance. **Decision: no change.**

### 3. 11-16: sul G on F1's first statement, considered and rejected

F1 at 11-16 lies in the G string's compass (D4-F5), and the G string's "intense and stringent" high positions (PDF 74) would give the theme a timbre of its own. But F1 is con fuoco, built from accented halves and staccato quarters. Its arpeggio at 17 rises to A♭5, which has to cross strings. And 11-18 is protected (c187, c202). The theme already has its colour from the violin-viola-horn octaves (PDF 89). **Decision: no change.**

### 4. 124.4-126: the violas' open D4 and A4 in the hand-over, no mark

Two of the violas' notes in their F2 bars are open-string pitches: D4 at 124.4 and A4 at 125.1, a half note. At 2 = 84 that half note lasts 0.7 s, and violists stop such notes themselves in an espressivo line. c196 rejected open-string marks in the soft viola holds for the same reason (PDF 73: fingering is best left to the player). A "sul G" would ask for the "accompaniment strings" (PDF 87), which is not the colour this rising hand-over needs. **No change.**

### 5. Memorability of the themes against the great finale tunes, no change

- F1 has three things a listener can hum: the falling fourth C-G, the dotted figure, and the rising arpeggio to the A♭6 peak (22, 162). Its C-major form at 265-272, which reaches A6 (c121), works the way Tchaikovsky 5/iv's motto does.
- F2's violin peak at 78 is on the E string at mf, where Adler says the E string is "clear and luminous when one plays softly" (PDF 75).
- The coda's cantabile S head is sul A, which is Brahms 3/iii's choice for a soft high line (PDF 75), and goes back to ord. at 239 for the f continuation.

Every string theme is already on the string Adler would choose, apart from 123-124 (Finding 1). The chorale, the horn call and the brass combination are not string material and are protected. **No change.**
