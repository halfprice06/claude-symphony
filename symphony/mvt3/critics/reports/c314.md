## Findings

### 1. 37-38: F1's third head statement is a brass-only line inside the strings' eighths (applied)

Problem. Since c297 the third statement of F1's head (B♭ minor, 37-38) is in Hn 3·4 a2 ff alone. Every string, including the cellos, plays the countersubject in eighths, and the cellos double the violas at the unison (E♭4-A♭4, the horns' own register, which c298 already flagged). This statement is the only F1 head in 11-42 with no string member, and the only one played in a single octave.

Evidence. Adler PDF 176: in the Jupiter coda "each rendition of the first theme is colored somewhat differently and supported by one member of the string choir". PDF 177-178: in the Meistersinger combination "the color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument", and the second theme has "octave-lower cellos plus Horn I". PDF 178-179, Ex. XV-27: Rimsky-Korsakov pairs the cellos with the horns on the main theme. PDF 165: when the aim of a doubling is power, an octave doubling is "much clearer and more brilliant" than a unison. Here the cellos' unison with the violas adds nothing to the countersubject, which keeps Vln I·II, violas and Bn 1.

Fix (pTwo.ily). The cellos double the horns' head an octave below (B♭3), ff, with the horns' articulation, and return to the held B♭ at 39 at f with the violas and basses. The joint 36.4 → 37.1 is a step (C4 → B♭3).
```
pTwoVc (37-39):  bes2->(\ff f4. ges8 | aes4-.) ges8( f8 es4-.) d4-. | \clef bass bes,1\f\> |
```
The new cello line made fifths with Cl 2 at 38.3 (F3-C4 → E♭3-B♭3). Cl 2 now holds C4 to beat 4, the same 9-8 over B♭ that c297 gave Fl 2 and both oboes:
```
pTwoClTwo (38):  c'2. bes4 |
```
The head's notes, the horns, c298's dynamics and the 38.3 cadential 6/4 (E♭ in the head, G♭ in the violas and Bn 1) are unchanged. This is not a change to F1 itself, only to its doubling. finale.md's 27-42 description should add "cellos with Hn 3·4 an octave below at 37-38". No other movement quotes F1.

### 2. 273-276: the timpani roll on C is ff under the closing chain (applied)

Problem. At Largamente (273) the timpani roll on C carries over the ff of 271-272 for four bars (about 6.7 s at 2 = 72) and only then crescendos into the fff at 278. Above it, Ob 1, Hn 1·2, Vln I's lower notes and the violas play the cyclic closing chain G-F♯-F-E, the finale's answer to the closes of movements II and III. c119 already lowered the cellos' and basses' C to f at 273 so that the divided upper strings would speak. The roll is the one part of that C pedal still at ff.

Evidence. Adler PDF 184 (Bloch, Schelomo): the composer "wisely marks both percussion instruments piano and then crescendo, because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra and completely change the emphasis of this passage". PDF 164-165: a chord has to be distributed so that its important notes stay "under control". PDF 184 (Schoenberg): the principal line is marked louder than the parts around it. The score already does this at 243 (roll p <) and 259-262 (roll f <). At 273 the pedal is background and the chain is foreground.

Fix (finale.ily, timpani). The roll starts at f, like the cellos and basses, and keeps its crescendo from 275 to fff at 278:
```
finaleTimp (272-275):  c4-> r8 c8 c4 r4 | c1:32->\f~ | c1:32~ | c1:32\<~ |
```
This touches the protected close (273-279). It keeps every reason given for that protection: the chain, the scherzo quotation at 275-276, the top C6, the fff only at 278 and the harp glissando. It changes only the level of the roll, as c119 did for the low strings.
