## Findings

### 1. The development has no climax (bars 107–114). Applied.
**Problem.** The development peaks at f. The exposition reaches ff at 27 and 91, and the retransition at 147. Between them, the loudest point of the development is the F-minor cadence 110–113. Hn 1·2, trombones and timpani play there, but the trumpets and tuba rest through all 36 bars. After 113 come 16 bars of pp–p F2 (115–130), so the tension curve sinks in the section that should carry it. In Dvořák 9/iv the development ends ff with the trumpets (177–191).
**Reading.** In a 6/3 series only the beginning and the end are points of articulation, and the end matters for its cadential function (PDF 291–292, Ex. 18-3). The lament 107–109 is that kind of series: a passing v6 to IV6 with 7–6 suspensions (Ex. 18-8a, 18-12, PDF 295, 298–299). Its goal is V at 110 and the cadence 111–113, and v53 gives that goal no more weight than the start of the development at 99.
**Fix (dev.ily).** Every f at 110 becomes ff (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Bn 1·2, Cbn, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, timpani, all strings). New parts:
```
devTptOne = { R1*11 | e''1\ff | f''1 | f''2 e''2 | f''2-> r2 | R1*21 | }
devTptTwo = { R1*11 | c''1\ff | c''2 des''2 | des''2 c''2 | c''2-> r2 | R1*21 | }
devTptThree = { R1*11 | g'1\ff | aes'2 aes'2 | bes'2 g'2 | aes'2-> r2 | R1*21 | }
devTuba = { R1*11 | c,1\ff | f,2 des,2 | bes,,2 c,2 | f,2-> r2 | R1*21 | }
devTimp = { … R1*3 | c4\ff r4 c4 r4 | c4-> r4 aes,4-> r4 | r2 c4-> c4 | c4-> r4 r2 | R1 | … }
```
The trumpets double existing lines (Tpt 1 = Ob 1/Fl 1, Tpt 2 = Tbn 1 up an octave and Vln II, Tpt 3 = Hn 1), so the harmony is unchanged. This is not a root V–I in C (it is in F minor), and no sf or fff marks are added (cycle 8 list).

### 2. The closing group leads into the development with the development's own opening (bars 87–90 against 99). Applied.
**Problem.** At 87–90 the closing restates F1's head in Vla, Vc, Cb and bassoons at f. Nine bars later the development opens (99) with the same head in the same instruments at the same dynamic. Only the key (E♭ major → F minor) signals that a new section has begun. The closing has no gesture of its own after 83–86, which already state the head once.
**Reading.** A&S's Mendelssohn example (Ex. 18-2/18-3, PDF 290–292) is the model for an approach to a cadence: a descending 6/3 series from I6 forms "a bridge" to the cadence, divided into two-bar groups. The upper voices move in 4ths (PDF 292), the passage is reduced to three real voices (PDF 295), and the leading tone and the 7th are not doubled (PDF 293).
**Fix (clos.ily, 87–90).** The violins' scale figure is unchanged. Below it: I6 – vii°6 – vi6 – V6 – ii4/3 – V7 – I over a descending bass.
```
closVc   … | g2\f f2 | es2 d2 | c2 bes,2 | es2-> r2 | …   (Bn 2 the same; Cb and Cbn: g,2 f,2 | es,2 d,2 | c,2 bes,,2 | es,2-> r2)
closBsnOne … | es'2\f d'2 | c'2 bes2 | aes2 aes2 | g2-> r2 | …   (Tbn 1 the same)
closTbnTwo … | bes2\f aes2 | g2 f2 | es2 f2 | es2-> r2 | …
closVla  … | <bes es'>2\f <aes d'>2 | <g c'>2 <f bes>2 | <es aes>2 <f bes>2 | <es bes>2-> r2 | …   (all playable double stops)
closHnOne..Four 89–90: r4 aes'/f'/c'/aes r4 bes'/f'/d'/bes | r4 g'/es'/bes/g r4 bes'/f'/d'/bes
```
This also corrects the horns at 89–90. In v53 they played an E♭ chord on beat 4 of 89 over the bass F, and a B♭ chord on beat 2 of 90 over the sustained E♭. At 89b the lower viola note is F rather than D, because D under the violins' A–B♭ made fifths into 90 (found by p5.py and corrected). Note for the other threads: this removes one statement of F1's head (in E♭, low strings) from the closing. F1 itself is not changed.

### 3. The direct ff start drops to p four bars before the theme (bars 7–10). Applied.
**Problem.** Bars 1–6 are ff. At 7–8 only Bn 1·2 and cellos play, p, with a timpani G. The movement in effect starts a second time, softly, which the cyclic rule ("no soft opening") was meant to prevent. F1 then enters at f, so the theme sounds lower than the opening rather than arriving as its goal. Dvořák 9/iv bars 1–10 stay between f and ffz and crescendo into the theme at ff.
**Reading.** The low bass that anticipates F1's rhythm on V (7–8) is a dominant expansion, and it is followed by the theme's i6 at 11 (PDF 299–300, Ex. 18-15: a low bass heard as a harmonic support). It is preparation, not a new beginning, and should not be marked as one.
**Fix (intro.ily).** Every `\p` in 7–10 becomes `\mf` (Bn 1·2, Vc and timpani at 7; winds, horns, Cbn, violins, violas and Cb at 9). The crescendo of 9–10 to F1's f at 11 is unchanged.
