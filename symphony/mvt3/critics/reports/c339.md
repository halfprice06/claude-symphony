## Findings

### 1. Bar 194: the oboe echoes a string phrase (regression since v329). Applied.
- **Problem.** c337 gave Ob 1 an echo of the violins' bar 192 (E♭5–D♭5–C5–B♭4, mp espress.) at pitch in 194, while the violins hold B♭4. Rimsky-Korsakov states the opposite rule in plain terms (PDF 128–129): "A wood-wind instrument cannot be used to echo the strings, or vice versa, on account of the dissimilarity in timbre." An echo needs a weaker instrument "of some affinity". Here the oboe is the brighter instrument, and it ends on the violins' own held B♭4, so the two meet in a unison.
- **Evidence.** 194 also already has its own answer in the strings. The cellos and Bn 1 play D♭3–C3–B♭2, the tail of bar 192 two octaves lower. Rimsky (PDF 129) calls this kind of answer, "imitation in octaves (with a decrease in resonance)", the echo-like effect. In 187–198 Rachmaninoff 2/iv does not double or answer the strings' lyric theme with a solo reed. Its winds sustain under it. c337 cited Adler's delayed Dvořák oboe (adler2 PDF 211, 213), but that oboe enters as a voice in the polyphony, not as an echo of a string phrase.
- **This undoes c337.** The reason above answers c337's argument with the assigned book. 191–196 returns to the v329 oboe part. The strings and bassoons (protected) are untouched.
- **Fix (recS.ily, `recSOboeOne`):** replace `| R1*3 | r4 es''8(\mp^\markup \italic "espress." des''8 c''4 bes'4) | \tag #'score { R1*2 }` with `| R1*4 | \tag #'score { R1*2 }`. The c339 comment line goes above the variable. I tested giving the echo to one solo violin instead. That breaks the 187–198 string protection, so I dropped it.

### 2. Bar 110: the lament's goal gets the string grace-note glide. Applied.
- **Problem.** The development's chromatic lament (107–109, strings p < with Ob/Cl from 108.4) arrives on the ff G-major sonority at 110, where the brass enter. In the violins, the arrival G6/G5 is a tied whole note reached from G5/E5. The accent is carried only by the dynamic and the brass entry.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 129–130: to "stress or emphasise a certain note", "a chain of three or four grace notes, in the form of a scale" may be used, "a kind of upward glide", slurred to the main note and not leading to string chords. Ex. 254 (*Tsar's Bride* 142, PDF 418) shows the violins rushing up into the ff downbeat as the full orchestra enters, which is the situation at 110. The score uses this device once, at 35 (c208). Rachmaninoff 2/iv marks its big arrivals the same way, with string upbeat rushes into the downbeat.
- **Fix (dev.ily).** Vln I: `b''2 c'''4( g''4) | \grace { d'''16( e''' f''' } g'''1)\ff~ |`. Vln II: `f''2.( e''4) | \grace { d''16( e'' f'' } g''1)\ff\downbow~ |`. Both are single notes, as the book requires. The down-bow, tie, ff and every main note are unchanged. MIDI: three short notes per section before 110.1. par8.py output for 105–115 is identical to v339's.
