## Findings

**1. The contrabassoon plays 209–230 without a rest: 22 bars, about 40 seconds, ending in its loudest and lowest notes (codaTwo 217–218). Applied.**
Problem: after its rest at 205–208 (c486), the contrabassoon holds whole notes in its low register from 209 to 230. The stretch runs A♭1 f <, then ff → f at 211–216, then F1 tied f > mp at 217–218. From the subito p at 219 it climbs to the fff C1 at 223, then plays the chorale bass at 224–230. Its only relief is breath marks. Adler PDF 230: "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests so that the player can replenish his breath more easily." The same rule was applied to the tuba at 217–218 (c406, adler2 PDF 8) and to the trombones (c492). The contrabassoon was the one low wind that still held through the echo of theme B.
Evidence that the rest costs nothing: at 217–218 the chord is D♭ over F (first inversion; D♭ in the violins and violas). The basses and Bn 2 hold F2 and the cellos F3, so the bass line is still complete. Without the 32-foot F1 the echo is lighter, which is what c108 asked of it (an echo weaker than the statement). The contrabassoon then re-enters p on A♭1 at 219, the same p re-entry as the trombones and the bass trombone. Nothing changes in the stop chord at 222 or in the 222→223 resolution, so the protected approach keeps its pitches and voicing. The rest is in the same two bars where the tuba and trombones already rest.
Fix (sections/codaTwo.ily, contrabassoon only, the same shape as the tuba):
`codaTwoCbsn = { des,1\ff\> | des,1\f | ges,,1 | aes,,1 \breathe | f,,1 | f,,1\> | R1\! | R1 | aes,,1\p | g,,1\< | g,,1\mp\< \breathe | d,,1\f\< | }`
(was `… f,,1 | f,,1 | f,,1~\> | f,,1\mp \breathe | aes,,1\p …`). The contrabassoon now plays 209–216 (8 bars), rests 217–218, then plays 219–230 (12 bars). I added a c576 comment above the line in the scratch copy.

**2. Fl 1's B♭6 at 78 is reached by a leap of a fourth at mf. Tested; not applied.**
Adler PDF 186: above A6 "extreme caution must be used in approaching them. The best approach is by scale". c041 used this rule at 61–63 (the same F6→B♭6 leap at p). Bar 78 is the only place in the score where a flute leaps above A6 at less than f. I built Fl 1 an octave lower at 77–78 (`es''2.(\p\< f''4 | bes''4.\mf aes''8 g''4 f''4) \breathe`, in unison with Ob 1), back on E♭6 at 79. The build was clean and par8 showed no change. I rejected it for musical reasons. F2's E♭-major peak loses the flute's top octave over the violins. The flute vanishes into the oboe unison for exactly the peak bar. Its return at 79 is an audible octave join mid-phrase. Unlike 61–63, this is mf inside a crescendo, doubled by Vln I at f, and a professional flute plays that leap without trouble. I restored v576's bars.

**3. Thin top notes in oboe and cor anglais. Checked; not applied.**
Ob 1 holds E6 and D6 with swells at 227–229 (PDF 198: C6–F6 is "thin ... hard to control"). It plays in a five-part unison with Fl 1, Cl 1 and both violin sections, and the breaths are staggered (c396). Taking it down an octave puts it a semitone from Cl 2's E5/F5 at 227, so I left it. The cor anglais's G5 (written D6) at 182 is in its oboe-like top (PDF 205). It is the climax of the recapitulated F2 inside protected 171–186, and the reading gives no reason strong enough to reopen that. Bn 1's B♭4 at 36 is in the top fifth (PDF 226), but it only doubles the cellos at pitch inside a ff tutti. c526 already declined moving that line lower.

**4. No other conflicts with the reading.** Registers, pp entries, breath spans, solo/1./a2 markings, bass clarinet notation, leaps and contrabassoon range all follow chapters 6–7 (table above). The woodwind writing is idiomatic throughout.

Quotations: I changed no quotation and none of F1, F2 or the chorale top line, so the other movement threads have nothing to update. c570 is the last cyclic check against the scherzo record.
