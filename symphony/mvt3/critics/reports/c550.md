## Findings

### 1. 243–245: Vln I's G♯5, the scherzo's first and last sound, takes the scherzo's string. Applied.

**Problem.** Since v311 the upper desk of Vln I plays G♯5→G5 at 243, the scherzo's last pitch in its own instrument, falling as bar 1's A♭5 does. Since scherzo v527 (c526) the scherzo marks that G♯5 "sul A" at its opening (1–7) and its close (364–376), so the pitch has a fixed colour in the symphony: the veiled A-string sound. The finale gives the pitch but not the colour; the violinist will take G♯5 on the E string in first position.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 20: orchestration is "part of the very soul of the work ... certain tone-colours being inseparable from it in the mind of its creator". The cyclic G♯ is an idea of this symphony, and its colour is now fixed in the scherzo.
- Rimsky PDF 125–126: a passed note keeps its place in the line it continues. At 242.3–243 Fl 2 hands G♯5 to Vln I, as the scherzo's Fl 1 hands it to Vln I at 364.
- Playability (PDF 21): the line G♯5–G5 | E5–D5 | B4 lies in fourth and first position on the A string. The dashed line ends before the unison run at 245.3, which needs the E string. The lower desk's G♯4–G4 lie below the A string, so the mark can only apply to the upper desk.

### 4. Amplification into 247 (Rimsky PDF 127, Ex. 244): tested on paper, declined

Rimsky's second group enters pp and crescendos faster. The obvious place for this is the low brass (trombones, bass trombone, tuba), which rest 235–246 and enter at 247. Entering pp < on the V4/3 at 246 would put B and G under the call's held C (Tpt 1 C6, Tpt 2 and Hn 1 C5, Hn 2 C4) at 245.3–246. That brings back the blur that c450 removed when it moved Ob 2's B4 to C5. It would also spend the fresh low-brass attack on the Più allegro downbeat. The call already carries the crescendo with the oboes p <, the timpani roll p < f and the strings' "cresc. molto". This would make the bars worse, so I did not build it.

### 5. Transference of the major call from horns to trumpets (Rimsky PDF 125–126): considered, declined

Splitting the call so that the horns alone play E–C and the trumpets take over on the tied C would make the "coinciding note" hand-over literal. It would also echo the call's horn origin in movements I and III. But the owed item is the call in the trumpets, and the record line hornCallMajor TptOne 243 would fail. The whole call in Tpt 1 two octaves above Hn 2, which is at movement I's pitch, is the point (Rimsky PDF 22). The present layout is better.
