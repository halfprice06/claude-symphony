## Findings

**1. 241–243, III♯ approach: the K. 467 voice exchange was tested and declined.** Unit 32 PDF 594–595 (Ex. 32-13) describes the progression the score already uses at 235–241: a descending-thirds bass I–VI–IV–II (C–A–F–D). Mozart then uses a voice exchange to bring in an augmented sixth before the new V. PDF 593 adds that a half-step bass into the goal chord makes it stronger. Candidate tested in the scratch copy: at 242, put the bass on F (`sHeadVc` `f,1\>`, `sHeadCb` `f,1\>`, `sHeadBsnTwo` `f,1\>`, harp LH `<f, f>1`). This gives a voice exchange with the top line's F6 (241) → D6 (242), turns the chord into iiø6/5 F–A♭–C–D, and makes the bass F→E a half step into the E-major chord at 243. Result: 0 warnings and 16 pages, but par8.py reports new parallel fifths at 242.3→243.1. The bass goes F2→E2 while the seventh resolves C→B in Bn 1 (C4→B3) and Vln II (C5→B4): a fifth F–C followed by a fifth E–B. These fifths come from resolving the seventh over a falling bass, so moving notes around would not remove them. That is the reason c021 chose ii7 over the root D. The candidate also makes F-bass iiø6/5 sound a third time in nine bars (249, 242, 259), which takes some weight from the last cadence. Decision: keep v473. The reverted file matches v473.

**2. 113–115, the development hinge: resolving the leading tone in its own voice was tested and declined.** At 113→114 the score has a direct chromatic inflection, as in the Hammerklavier example (PDF 584–585): the violas go A♭3→A3, and the F5 of 113 becomes Vln I's F♯5 at 114. Vln I's F♯5 then fades into the rest. The G arrives on 115.1 in the cellos and on 115.2 in Vln II's pizz. G5. Candidate tested: `devVlnOne` `fis''1\p\>( | g''4)\! r4 r2 | R1 | R1*2 |`, so that F♯5 resolves to G5 in Vln I on the downbeat of the G-minor bassoon solo. The build was clean (0 warnings, 0 parallels, 16 pages). It was not better, for three reasons. Unit 32 accepts transferred resolutions in exactly this situation (PDF 598, Ex. 32-18: "some transferred resolutions"). The G5 already sounds one beat later in Vln II. And the new slur crosses the page turn from page 6 to page 7 into the protected start of the bassoon solo (c111 "pivot 113–114", c210 "115–118 and the bassoon solo"). Decision: keep v473; reverted.

**3. 187–223, the coda's chromatic plan follows the reading. No change.** This is the Widmung plan in minor (PDF 593: "♭VI moves to V7 through ♭II"): A♭ (VI, entered by the deceptive cadence of 185–186; Chopin model, PDF 592–593) → A♭7 as V7 of D♭ (205–210) → D♭ (211–218). The same A♭ sonority then returns at 219 as the German sixth of C. Its G♭ (Hn 2) is now F♯ and rises to G at 220: the V7→Ger6 change down a half step of Ex. 32-10a (PDF 591–592). The bass ♭6–5 (A♭→G at 219→220) gives the half-step approach to V that PDF 593 recommends. Over the whole span, F1's A♭–G becomes A♭ (187) → G (220), and the D♭–C motive becomes D♭ (211) → C (223), which is the motivic enlargement of PDF 600–601. Dvořák 9/iv places its tonicized ♭II at the same point (harmony.csv, coda bars 297–304). The plan is at the level of the corpus; nothing should change.

**4. E♭/E thread: the Creation model (PDF 596–598) is complete. No change.** Haydn ties a remote C-minor section into A major by holding E♭ in the top voice and respelling it as D♯ leading to E. The finale does the same in one instrument at one pitch: the violas' E♭4 in the bar-1 German sixth, E4 at 97 (p, as the third of V of F), D♯4→E4 at 219–220 in the same German-sixth sonority, and E4 in the arrival chord at 223. Bar 273.3 has an E♭ inside the common-tone °7 over the C pedal (Cl 1, Bn 1, Hn 4: E–E♭–F–E). It works as a lower-neighbour inflection in the close, not a return of the thread. c310 checked it, and it lies in the protected 273–279.

**5. Proportions and the coda's stages. No change.** The arrival (223, fff) is followed by the chorale at f, the plagal close (f to mf), the second-theme head (p, rising to f at 239), the horn call (243, with the stringendo from 245) and the ff Più allegro at 247. The music stays at f or below for 23 bars (224–246) between the fff arrival and the next ff. Each stage has its own tempo and texture: Maestoso 56 with the brass chorale, Poco più mosso 66 with the violins and harp, then 88 with the full brass. The stages are planned contrasts, not a sag. In Dvořák 9/iv the coda also includes a quieter recall of earlier themes before its last tutti. The development is short (12.2%, or 17.7% with the retransition) compared with Dvořák's 20%. Lengthening it would need bars inside protected passages (99–109, 115–134, 139–150) and would probably cost a page. c219 already recorded it. I make no proposal.

**6. 151–170 and 167–171: ♭VI to V by an augmented sixth, and the recapitulation over the dominant. No change.** At 167–169 the progression is A♭ (VI) → Fr6 on A♭ → V, which is the "most natural way" PDF 593 describes. The recapitulation starts over the G pedal (151–153, 4–3 and 6–5), as in Tchaikovsky 4/i (PDF 600: "the first theme sounding over a dominant pedal"). Both follow the reading.

**7. 51–98, III♭ made stable. No change.** PDF 596 says III♭ is usually made stable by stating III in major first and then turning it to minor. The finale states E♭ minor first, so it needs the staged approach of PDF 596 §13. It has one: the violas' C♭4 (♭6 of E♭) over the B♭ pedal at 47–49 brings in the minor before the solo. The inflection to major (Cl 2 G♭3→G3 at 66) is in one voice at one pitch, the gentle kind of inflection described on PDF 585.
