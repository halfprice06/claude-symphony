## Findings

### 1. 209–210: the timpani roll starts f under the woodwinds' fp (applied)

Problem. Since c216 the flutes, oboes and clarinets take the A♭7 at 209 fp < so that the swell into the Largamente at 211 is heard over two bars. The timpani A♭2 roll in the same bar is marked f <. A low roll at f is broadband noise under the woodwinds' dip; it fills the dip that c216 made and leaves the roll itself only one step (f → ff) of crescendo. In the v494 MIDI the roll moves from velocity 84 to 89 across the two bars, almost flat.

Evidence. Adler part 2 PDF 184 (Bloch, Schelomo 228–43): the crescendos to 235 and 243 "are greatly aided by a timpani roll and then a bass-drum roll … The composer wisely marks both percussion instruments piano and then crescendo, because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra." PDF 164–165 (Debussy): a chord whose level changes must be given to instruments that can make the change. The score already applies the same rule twice: c346 made the 149 roll re-strike sfp < with the brass, and c474 made the 167–168 roll f > p with the link chords.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-494/work/sections/codaOne.ily, timpani only):

```
codaOneTimp = { … | aes,1:32\fp\< | aes,2.:32 r4\! | }
```

(was `aes,1:32\f\<`). The f attack stays, matching the f strokes at 205 and 207 and the woodwinds' fp; the roll then grows from p with the whole orchestra into the ff D♭ stroke at 211. The roll in the MIDI now rises from velocity 62 to 88. Notes, rhythm, the muta at 206 and every other part unchanged; 16 pages, no system moved.

### 2. 209–210: high flutes in the fp chord (considered, not changed)

The flutes hold F♯6/E♭6 fp at 209, the case of Adler's "dangerous" Brahms chord (PDF 162: flutes cannot play softly that high, the chord sounds louder than written). I did not move them: the fp is a stroke followed by a crescendo, not a held soft chord; the flutes' F♯6 is the seventh that Vln I reaches at 210.3 and Fl 1 releases there (c042); and fp in the upper woodwinds was decided by c216 and c288 and kept by c328. Finding 1 removes the louder problem under them.

### 3. 265–268: F1's head has no brass colour (tested, not changed)

Adler PDF 178 (Meistersinger 158–61): each combined theme has at least one string, one woodwind and one brass instrument. At 265–268 the F1 head is in Fl 1·2, Cl 1 and both violin sections (G5–E6) while the motto has Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2. The only free brass is Tpt 3; on the head an octave lower (G4–E5) it would cross Tpt 1·2's motto (C5–E5 at 265 and 267) in the same timbre and register, against PDF 169 (two ideas separate only with opposing timbres and designs), and it would lose the motto-rhythm pedal that Tpt 3 and the timpani give (PDF 176, Jupiter). The horns' and trombones' rests at 265–268 are protected (c036, c046, c124). The head at G5–E6 in four parts is heard over a middle-register motto; no change.
