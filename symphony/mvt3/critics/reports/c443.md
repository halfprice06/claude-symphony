## Findings

### 1. Bar 16, beat 3: a bare root-position diminished II in F1's antecedent (applied in scratch)

- **Bars:** 16 (exposition only).
- **Problem:** On 16.3 the sounding chord is D–F–A♭ alone: cellos, basses and Bn 2 on D, Cl 2 on F4, the tune's A♭4 in violins, violas and Hn 1·2, and Cl 1 also on A♭4. The seventh C comes only on the off-beat (Hn 3, beat 4). So for a quarter note on the half-bar's strong beat, at f, the theme stands on a root-position diminished triad. The recapitulation already completes the chord at 156.3 with Tbn 1's D♭4–C4, so the two statements of the theme differ here for no reason.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 236, sec. 6 and Ex. 15-9: Handel avoids the root-position diminished II, and A&S say it is acceptable only inside a chain. PDF 243, sec. 12: a diminished 5/3 is "seldom satisfactory", and the fuller chord is "far more pleasing". Ex. 15-17 (PDF 243) and Ex. 15-18, 15-19 (PDF 244): where the texture leaves the triad bare, the continuo supplies the missing tone. PDF 245, point 7.
- **Fix (applied):** In `sections/pOne.ily`, bar 6 of each clarinet part:
  - `pOneClOne`: `aes'1` → `aes'2( f'2)`
  - `pOneClTwo`: `f'1` → `f'2( c'2)`
  
  On 16.3 the chord is now D–F–A♭–C, a complete half-diminished ii7, the same as 156.3. Cl 1 also stops doubling the tune's A♭4 at that moment and reaches 17's first note F4 by common tone. Cl 2's C4 goes to 17's A♭3. The slurred half notes match the clarinets' writing at 12 (`f'2( aes'2)`, `d'2( c'2)`). The tune, bass, dynamics and off-beat layer are unchanged. I added a comment line (c443) above `pOneClTwo`.
- **Check:** The scan of the scratch MIDI gives 16.3 as D:036t (D–F–A♭–C), identical to 156.3. par8.py on 15–17 reports only the cellos' and Bn 2's octave doubling of the bass, as in v443, and no new parallels. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels; the only differences are the clarinet note counts. The build has 0 warnings and 16 pages. No theme changes; this is a voicing inside F1's accompaniment.

### 2. Natural VII → IV6 → V (Ex. 15-15) in place of 16.3–17: tested, not applied

- **Bars:** 16.3–18.
- **Candidate:** Bass B♭ at 16.3 (natural VII, with the tune's A♭ as its 7th), A♭ at 17 (iv6), F♯ above the held A♭ at 17.3, then G at 18. This gives the stepwise falling bass of A&S PDF 242, Ex. 15-15 and 15-16.
- **Why not:** The chord at 17.3 then becomes A♭–C–E♭–F♯, the German sixth. That chord is the movement's motto: bars 1 and 5, the 215–216 approach and 261 (all protected). Placing it at F1's half cadence at 18 and 158 would spend it on an ordinary phrase ending. It would also replace the bass's chromatic rise F–F♯–G, which leads into the half cadence. The current iiø7–iv–vii°7/V–V is better, so I left it.

### 3. Rising thirds from weak to strong beats: checked, no change

- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 231 and 240: a rising-third root progression sounds like one harmony continuing, and from a weak beat to a strong one it goes against the metre.
- **Result of the full scan:**
  - **16.4→17 and 156.4→157 (iiø7 → iv):** This is a predominant prolonged over the bass F–D–F. The downbeat of 17 carries the arpeggio climb to the A♭ peak, so the metre is not weakened. Kept.
  - **198→199 (A♭ minor → C minor):** This is the section joint into the coda. c263 and c353 checked it against this same unit, and I have no new reason to re-raise it.
  - **255→256 (I6 → iii over the held E):** This is A&S's 5–6/6–5 relation of I6 and III (PDF 239–240, Ex. 15-12d), carrying the inner line C–B–A into vi at 257. At the two-bar hypermetre level the change falls on a weak bar, which A&S's rule allows. Follows.
  - Every other flagged motion is a German sixth or vii°7 moving to a cadential 6/4 or to V. These are not root progressions in A&S's sense.

### 4. VI–(III)–IV under the passing 7 at 69 (Ex. 15-10d): tested on paper, not applied

- **Bars:** 69–70, E♭ major.
- **Candidate:** The tune falls E♭6–D6–C6–B♭5 over a bass that stays on C from 69.1 to 69.4. The D6 at 69.4 sounds over only C2 and the counter-melody's C4. Ex. 15-10d (PDF 238) would support the passing 7 with III: basses G1 on 69.4 (`c,2. g,,4`), then A♭1 at 70.
- **Why not:** The cellos' and violas' counter-melody holds C4 through 69.3–4 and then rises to E♭4 at 70. Over a G bass that C4 becomes an unprepared fourth that does not resolve down. Rewriting the counter-melody is not justified for one quarter note. Kept.

### 5. Large-scale plan and proportions: follows, no change

- **What the plan does:** It is A&S's minor-mode plan exactly (PDF 230–233, 244 points 1–3): i (11–42), III expanded into a key area (51–98, entered through natural VII at 43–50), a passing iv as the first development area (99–114), then V as a key (115–134) turned back into the dominant (133–150). III is entered and left through intervening chords, never directly.
- **Proportions:** The recapitulation (16%) is shorter than the exposition (35%) because it drops P2, the transition and the closing group. The coda (31%) takes over their role. This is the same balance as Brahms 1/iv and Tchaikovsky 5/iv, where the coda carries the major-key resolution. The coda's stages lengthen toward the end: 26 s for the arrival, then 21 s, then 47 s from 247, with the last root-position V–I at 262–263. They build rather than sag. No change.
