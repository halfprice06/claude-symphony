## Findings

### 1. 255–258: Hn 3·4 carry F2's last phrase with no expressive word, while every string on the line is "espress." (applied)

- **Problem.** At 255 F2's last phrase (G3–E3 | D3–C3–E3–G3 | C4–D4 | G4–F4–E4–D4) is played ff by Vln II (an octave up), violas, cellos and Hn 3·4 a2, under the last chorale in Tpt 1·2. The three string parts are marked "espress." (c321, c372). The horns, the only brass on the tune, have only `ff` and an accent. Around them Hn 3·4 hold f chords (247–254) and play marcato halves (259–262), and Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2 and Tpt 3 hold the chorale's harmony at f/mf. Nothing in the horn part tells the pair that these four bars are a sung theme rather than another marcato support line, so in an ff brass tutti they will play it as one.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 305, Ex. X-12 (Strauss, Don Juan 530–540): horns in unison carrying a lyrical theme inside a loud tutti are marked "ff molto espr.", with swells. PDF 304, Ex. X-8 (Brahms 1/iv, this movement's model): the horn's tune is "f sempre e passionato". PDF 309: the slur shows one sung breath; the word shows the character. The score already does this for the horns at 217 (c138) and 243 (c388).
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-488/work/sections/finale.ily).** Hn 3 and Hn 4 at 255:
  ```
  finaleHnThree = { ... | \tag #'score \clef bass g2.->(\ff^\markup \italic "espress." e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4) \breathe | ...
  finaleHnFour  = { ... | \tag #'score \clef bass g2.->(\ff^\markup \italic "espress." e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4) \breathe | ...
  ```
  Both horns carry the word, untagged. My first build put it on Hn 3 only (and on Hn 4 as a part-only tag), and the a2 staff printed nothing, which is the combiner behaviour c138 found at 217. With the word on both, "espress." prints once above "a2" at 255, clear of Hn 1·2's f. I used the strings' word, not "molto espr.", so all four parts on the line match; I added no swells, because the strings have none and c484 has just set the trombones' level under this phrase.
- **Build.** 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages, MIDI byte-identical to v488, analysis.txt unchanged (0 outer-voice parallels). par8.py on 253–259 reports only the pre-existing bass-clarinet and Bn 2 octave doublings of the bass at 259.1. Notes, dynamics and the chorale are unchanged. F2's pitches are unchanged; this is a word, not a theme change.

### 2. 224–228 and 230–234: phrase slurs on the brass chorale. Considered and declined

- **Candidate.** The arrival chorale is unslurred, so every whole note is tongued separately. Movement I's source (I 175–179) slurs its trombone chorale by phrase (G–E♭ | D–C–D♭–C), and Adler's soft horn hymns (Ex. X-15, X-16) are slurred. I wrote out the same slurs (`g1( | e''1) | d''1( | c''2 des''2 | c''1)`) for every brass part and checked each against its notes. I did not build this one, because it fails on the notes alone.
- **Why I decline it.** In this chorale the inner brass voices hold common tones: Hn 1 G4 at 224–226, Hn 2 E4 and Hn 3 C4 at 224–225, Tpt 2 and Hn 1 A4/F4 at 227–228, bass trombone and tuba C at 224–225. A slur joining two equal whole notes looks like a tie in print, so those parts would lose the re-attack that marks the chorale's harmonic rhythm. They would need either ties, which change the sound and the MIDI in bars protected since c019, or tenuto signs under the slur on brass. If only the moving lines (Tpt 1, Tbn 1·2) were slurred, the chord would be articulated two ways. The source is also a different case: movement I's chorale is pp "dolce" in three trombones, and this one is f Maestoso in ten brass after the fff arrival, where separate soft-tongued whole notes are normal. The gain is not clear enough to change 223–234. No change.
