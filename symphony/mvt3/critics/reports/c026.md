## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 1–2 and 5–6: the scherzo's G♯ was not in the opening chord's prominent voices (applied)
Problem: the scherzo ends with only Hn 1 G♯4 and Vln I G♯5 sounding. In v26, Hn 1 enters at 1 on F♯4 and Vln I on C5/F♯5. The A♭ sits in Hn 4 (A♭3) and Cl 1, so the players who held the G♯ do not continue it.
Evidence: Adler PDF 113 gives G♯ = A♭ as a standard enharmonic identity (Ex. IV-2: the same string sounds under either name). Here the same idea is carried by players instead of strings, and that only works if the same players and the same pitches continue. The scherzo's other chord tones (harp E3–G♯3–B3, pizz. E at its bar 383) already move by step into v26's voicing: Vc E3→E♭3, B→C in Bsn 1, Tbn 2 and Hn 3. Only the G♯ was missing.
Fix (intro.ily):
```
introHnOne  = { aes'1->\ff | g'4-> r4 r2 | R1*2 | aes'1-> | g'4-> r4 r2 | ...
introHnFour = { fis1->\ff  | g4-> r4 r2  | R1*2 | fis1->  | g4-> r4 r2  | ...
introVlnOne = { <c'' aes''>1->\ff\dnup | <b' g''>4->\downbow r4 r2 | ... | <c'' aes''>1->\ff\dnup | <b' g''>4->\downbow ...
```
The chord's pitch content and the bass are unchanged. F♯ is still in Fl 2, cor anglais, Hn 4 and violas. C5 plus A♭5 is a minor sixth (2nd finger on the A string, low 3rd on the E string), then B4 plus G5. The par.py fifths at 1→2 are the German sixth's accepted ones (cycle 2), and there are now fewer of them. This changes cycle 14's do-not-touch "German-sixth brass layout 1–2". The answer to its reason: the chord now continues the scherzo's last pitch in the same horn.
Decision on a quiet held A♭ first: no; start ff directly. The scherzo already holds the G♯ pp for 17 bars (368–384). After a pause, a second quiet A♭ would repeat that ending instead of answering it. Beethoven 5's quiet pedal into the finale works because its scherzo runs attacca. Here the movements are separated by a pause, and the cyclic rule (mvt II critics 120 and 130) forbids a soft opening.

### 2. Bar 1: the timpani label still gave the retuning from movement II (applied)
Problem: "retune D♭ → G" assumed movement II's A♭, C, D♭, E♭. The scherzo leaves the drums on E2, G2, B2, E3.
Evidence: Adler PDF 112 says to state the instrument's setting at the start of a work. Each drum must stay within its range. 32″ E2→G2 (+3), 29″ G2→A♭2 (+1), 26″ B2→C3 (+1), 23″ E3→E♭3 (−1): every move is a minor third or less, and each pitch lies inside the drum's normal range.
Fix: `aes,4->\ff^\markup \column { \italic "Timpani: G, A♭, C, E♭" \italic "(retune in the pause: E → G, G → A♭, B → C, E → E♭)" }`. Checked: the finale uses only G2, A♭2, C3 and E♭3 throughout.

### 3. Bars 143–145: scherzo motive x in the timpani (applied)
Problem: the finale used no scherzo material, so the third movement had no audible consequence in the fourth.
Fix (retr.ily): `retrTimp = { R1*4 | R1*4 | g,4\pp\< g,8 g,8 g,4 r4 | g,4 g,8 g,8 g,4 r4 | g,4\f g,8 g,8 g,4 g,8 g,8 | g,8 ...` (146 onward unchanged). Playability: one drum (G2), strokes no faster than eighths at 2 = 84, single sticking. The density grows from x (143–144) to double x (145), to eighths (146), to the roll (147), a written-out acceleration. The retransition stretto entries (protected, c017) are untouched.

### 4. Bar 1: "senza sord." in Hn 3 is now wrong information (applied)
Problem: the marking existed because movement II ended with Hn 3·4 muted. The scherzo puts them senza sord. at its bar 73 and ends with them open (v04 coda). Cyclic-notes ("Four movements") moves this marking to the scherzo.
Fix: `introHnThree = { c'1->\ff | ...` (marking removed). This changes the v01 do-not-touch item. Its reason (movement II's mutes) no longer applies.

### 5. Bars 243–279: Trumpets 1·2 play about 90 s of ff/fff without a rest (applied)
Problem: from 243 to 279, Tpt 1 plays the horn call with C6 at 244–246, the combined heads with C6 at 250, the chorale (255–264), four F1 statements (265–272) and the held close, with no bar's rest. Before that, 211–234 is also continuous, and the only break is 235–242 (about 15 s).
Evidence: Adler adler1 PDF 303, writing about the horn: playing high all the time is most taxing, "so periodic rests are most welcome". That applies even more to C trumpet after three C6s.
Fix (finale.ily): the fourth F1 statement in Tpt 1·2 becomes `R1*2` at 271–272. Fl/Ob/Cl/violins keep it on top, so c024's separate registers at 269–272 are kept. The trumpets re-enter fresh on `c''1->` at 273 for the fff close.

### 6. Bars 191 and 242: harp D1 is on a string without a pedal (applied)
Problem: the LH plays D1 (`d,,1`) at 191 and 242. Adler PDF 111 (footnote): the two lowest strings are not moved by the pedals and must be tuned by hand. The diagrams set the D pedal, but that does not reach D1.
Fix (sTwo.ily, first harp entry 67): `es,1\mf^\markup \small \line { \harp-pedal #"--^|^--^" \italic "(low D string: D♮)" }`. The harpist tunes it before the movement.

### 7. Bars 197–198: the A♭ pedal is marked on the note that needs it (applied)
Problem: the A pedal must move after the RH a' (197, 7th eighth) and before the A♭1/A♭4 on the downbeat of 198. The mark sat on that downbeat. Both 197–198 changes (E, then A) are right-foot pedals, which is legal one after the other (Adler PDF 112–113).
Evidence: Adler PDF 113: the harpist changes "just a split second before playing the note", and the composer must make certain the tempo leaves time. The mark should show where the change can be made.
Fix (recS.ily): `e,2 << { f,2_\markup \small "E♭" } { s4. s8_\markup \small "A♭" } >> | aes,,1 \ottava #0 |`.

### 8. Bars 123–130: harp solo checked, no change
The melody lies at D5–G5, p, over pp sustained strings and one pp horn. By Adler PDF 113–114 that is the top of the warm middle register, and the accompaniment is light enough for it to carry (PDF 117: melodic use). An RH octave doubling would add body, but the RH's lower octave would land on the strings the LH is still sounding (G3 at 129, A3 at 128). I leave it.
