## Findings

### 1. 172 and 180: the recapitulated F2 puts a root-position diminished triad on its second downbeat (applied)

Problem. In the C-minor F2 (171-186) bars 172 and 180 begin with the neighbour chord over the bass B: Bn 1 B2, pizz. B1/B2, Cl 2 F3, violas D3-F3, melody D4 (cor anglais and muted trumpet). With no G anywhere, this is B-D-F, VII5/3 in root position, held for a half bar on a strong beat in the softest and thinnest scoring of the movement (analysis.txt: 6-8 voices at 169-180).

Evidence. A&S PDF 243: "Diminished triads on the leading tone are seldom satisfactory in 5/3 position. ... V6/5 (which contains all three tones of the diminished VII) creates a far more pleasing sonority and is to be preferred". Review point 9 (PDF 245): "a leading tone in the bass should usually support V6/5". The exposition already does this. At 52 and 60, the same neighbour chord over D in E♭ minor is V6/5: Cl 1 B♭3 (the sixth) and Cl 2 A♭3 (the seventh), the c093 fix. The recapitulation's Cl 1 rests until 181, so the sixth was lost when the passage was transposed. 176.3-4 and 178.3-4 also have B-D-F over B, but each comes after a V or V6 that holds G earlier in the same bar. That is the case of Ex. 15-19 (PDF 244: "because it grows out of the strong V at the beginning of the bar, the diminished chord would be heard as representing a V6/5"), so I kept both.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-263/work/sections/recS.ily). Cl 2 takes the root G3 at 172 and 180. The seventh F stays in the violas (F3 → E♭3 on beat 3), and Cl 2 still reaches E♭3 on beat 3:

```
recSClTwo = { es1\pp^\markup \italic "dal niente" | g2 es2 | f1 | f2 es2 | f1 | g2 f2 | es2 c'2 | g4( f4 d4 f4) \breathe | g2 es2 | g2 es2 | es2 aes2~ | ...
```
(bars 172 and 180 were `f2 es2`.) The chord is now B2-D3-F3-G3 under D4 (V6/5), as at 52 and 60. No other part, level or rhythm changes. par8.py on 170-182 gives the same output as v263: one existing analyser line at 178.1, where Cl 2 falls C4→G3 to the pizz. G1. That is not a real parallel: Cl 2 is moving against a newly entering bass voice, while the held bass line is Bn 1 C3→B2.

### 2. 83: F1's head could be heard in III at its own pitch (considered, not applied)

The reading (PDF 232; Exercise 6, PDF 247) says one segment in natural minor can be harmonised in i and again in III with the same pitches. F1's head C5-G4-A♭4 | B♭4-A♭4-G4-F4-E♭4 lies entirely in E♭ major and ends on E♭, so the closing group could restate it untransposed over E♭ harmony. I decided against it. The closing head at 83 has to put the new tonic on top after the cadence at 82 (E♭6 in Vln I and flutes). At C pitch the closing theme would begin on 6̂ over I, which weakens the confirmation of III that principle 6 asks for. Bars 83-86 have also been settled across several cycles (c132, c144, c145, c165, c255). No change.

### 3. 198→199: VI to i by rising third into the coda's downbeat (kept)

PDF 231 and 240 say that root progressions by rising third (I-III, and by extension VI-I) sound like one chord and contradict the metre from weak to strong. The A♭ chord of 198 goes straight to the hammered C minor at 199. I kept it. The joint is a section boundary with a full change of texture, not a chord change inside a phrase. The reading's alternative is an inverted dominant (PDF 235), and the only one available would be V6/5, whose bass B after A♭ is an augmented second. Root V-i is barred before 263.

### 4. 223-279: III as support for a passing 7̂ is not used in the C-major coda (kept)

PDF 237-238 (Ex. 15-10) gives III its main small-scale use: supporting 7̂ in a falling 8-7-6 between I and IV or II6. The coda's falling lines from C are on a C pedal (229-238, 263-279), where iii has no bass of its own, or are protected (223-234 arrival and chorale; 255-264 cadence; 265-279 close). The coda already uses III in its two other roles: as a substitute for I6 at 255-256 and as the head of III-VI-II-V-I at 255-263. No change.

### Form verdict on the lens

The tonal plan is the one Unit 15 describes for minor: III as the exposition's key area, then passing IV, then V. It is confirmed by root cadences in E♭ (65-66, 81-82, 91-94) and rises by step E♭-F-G to the dominant (Ex. 15-2b, 15-3). Its proportions are close to Dvořák 9/iv's. The coda grades its goals as PDF 235-236 describes. The arrival (222→223) and the S-head stage (245-247) reach I through inverted dominants, which are not goals. The combination stage ends V7 → I6 (250-251, 254-255). Only the last stage has root V7-I (262-263). The coda builds rather than sags. I found nothing at the level of form that the reading would change.
