## Findings

### 1. 187-194: F2 in A♭ has no woodwind in its colour. Add Ob 1 at pitch with the violins (applied)

**Problem.** At its recapitulation major statement, F2 is in Vln I·II at the unison (E♭5 level), with the violas and Hn 1 an octave lower. The flutes and oboes rest from 187 to 196, and the clarinets play the pp background figure (c207). So the theme has strings and one horn, and no woodwind.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 178, *Meistersinger* 158-61: "the color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument". Wagner's second theme is Vln I with Cl 1 at pitch, plus cellos and Hn 1 an octave lower. That is this score's layout less the woodwind.
- PDF 179: to get the "big" sound "whether the dynamic be forte or piano, it is essential that the doubling be of a mixed color".
- PDF 176, Jupiter 389-400: Vln II is doubled by Ob 1 at pitch.
- PDF 170-171, Mahler 4/iv: a returning passage keeps its texture but changes its colour. The exposition's F2 (67-76) is strings and harp alone; adding an oboe here makes the recapitulation's statement a different colour without touching the strings.

**Why the oboe.** The clarinets already carry the background, which is protected. In A♭4-B♭5 the flute is in its weak low register, while the oboe is in its best cantabile register. The oboe has also been silent since 170.

**Fix (applied, recS.ily).** Ob 1 doubles Vln I at pitch for 187-194, p dolce. That is one level under the violins' mf, so it tints the line rather than leading it (PDF 184). It copies the violins' hairpins at 188-189, breathes after 190 with Hn 1, and rests 195-196 before re-entering at 197 as before:

Nothing protected changes: the strings, bassoons, bow plan, clarinet figure and harp rest at 187-198 are all as before. This is a change to the scoring of F2 (one added doubling, no new notes) for finale.md. No other movement quotes F2.

### 2. 95-96: the chord under the oboe solo is as loud as the solo. Mark it mp (applied)

**Problem.** At 95 Ob 1 plays its solo (c221) at mf. Seven parts hold the chord under it at the same mf: Cl 1·2, Bn 2, Vln II, the violas (div.), the cellos and the basses. Cl 1's B♭4 and Vln II's G4 lie just under the solo's E♭5-C5.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 159, consideration 1: melody notes must be more prominent than harmony notes.
- PDF 184, Schoenberg op. 16/5: the principal gestures are marked louder than the subordinate ones.
- PDF 161, Weber: an instrument in a good register is left undoubled to stand out alone. That only works if its accompaniment sits below it in dynamic as well as in pitch.

**Fix (applied, clos.ily).** Each of these parts goes from `1\mf\>` to `1\mp\>` at 95: Cl 1 `bes'1\mp\>~`, Cl 2 `g'1\mp\>`, Bn 2 `es1\mp\>~`, Vln II `g'1\mp\>~`, violas `<bes es'>1\mp\>~`, cellos `es1\mp\>~`, basses `es,1\mp\>~`. The p at 97 is unchanged in all parts, and the solo stays mf > p.

The clarinets share a staff and keep equal dynamics. The violas are protected at 95-98 (c221) for their E♭4→E4 thread. Their pitches and the step at 97 are unchanged; only the starting dynamic moves one level, which does not touch that reason.

### 3. 205-206: the F1 head is brass only (not applied)

At 205-206 the head is in Hn 1-4 a2 and Tbn 1, while Bn 1 holds C4 and the cellos hold the A♭2 pedal. By PDF 178 the head lacks a string and a woodwind; giving it to Bn 1 and the cellos with Tbn 1 would also keep Bn 1 on the head through 207-208 (PDF 176, keep a colour). I leave it alone. 205-210 is protected (c179, c197: "rescored in c176, c177, c178"), and the four-horn unison is itself the Mahler 4 "weight" effect of PDF 165-166 (c201 protects it). This is recorded so a later critic does not raise it again without answering that reason.
