## Findings

### 1. Clarinet 1's quaver rests at 187-194 stand two spaces above the centre, far from the figure they belong to. Applied.
**Bars:** 187-194 (Cl 1·2 staff, 16 rests); also 222 beat 4 (Fl 1·2 staff, 1 rest).
**Problem:** In the A♭ statement of F2, Cl 1 plays the rocking figure A♭3-F4 (C4 and below, on ledger lines) after a quaver rest on each half-bar. Cl 2 holds the chord's low note below the stave. LilyPond gives an upper-voice rest a fixed raised position, so all 16 of Cl 1's rests stand two spaces above the centre line (log: +4 half-spaces), while the notes they introduce are at -5 to -9. On the page the rests look as if they belong to a different, higher part. At 222.4, Fl 2's crotchet rest sits two spaces below the centre (−4) while Fl 2's own F5 is on the top line and Fl 1 plays on ledger lines above.
**Evidence:** Gould PDF 51: "The space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches." PDF 50: "When one part lies outside the stave (on ledger lines), crotchet, quaver, and shorter-value rests for the other part may move back to the centre of the stave." In both places the other part is outside the stave (Cl 2 below, Fl 1 above), so the centre line is where the rest belongs. The score already applies this rule at 58, where c205 moved Cl 1's rest in the same exposition passage to the centre. The 187-194 figure was added later (c207), so it never got the same treatment. The log found no other voiced rest that breaks the rule.
**Fix (applied):** recS.ily, `recSClOne`: `\partCombineApart \tag #'score { \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . -2) } r8 aes8(...` and `... r8 bes8( es'8 bes8) \tag #'score { \revert Rest.extra-offset } \partCombineAutomatic`. codaTwo.ily, `codaTwoFluteTwo`: `f''2.\f\< \tag #'score { \once \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . 2) } r4\! | }`. This is the same score-only mechanism c205 used at 58. Parts and MIDI are unchanged. I checked at 220-300 dpi: the rests now sit on the middle line and collide with nothing.

### 2. Dotted minim rests across the half-bar at 234, and one at 124. Applied.
**Bars:** 234: Tpt 1, Tpt 2, Hn 1-4, Tbn 1, Tbn 2, B. tbn, tuba and timpani (`x4\p r2.`), and Vln I (`r2. g'4`). 124: violas (`r2.` before the sul D entry).
**Problem:** In 2/2 the beat is the minim. A dotted minim rest after a crotchet on beat 1 starts on the second crotchet and runs across the middle of the bar, so the beat where the second half-bar begins is not shown. At 234 the same notation is printed eleven times in a column, under the last chord of the plagal close. Everywhere else the score writes this rhythm as `x4 r4 r2` (238 times), and it writes a rest before a beat-4 entry as `r2 r4` (three times in dev). These 13 rests are the only exceptions.
**Evidence:** Gould PDF 51 groups and aligns rests by beat (the bracketed examples: "Rests that are part of a beat ... should align horizontally"). PDF 52 treats dotted rests as a matter of placement only and sends their use to "Grouping rests according to the metre" (p. 160, outside the excerpt). The score's own convention, used 238 times, is the half-bar grouping that rule asks for.
**Fix (applied):** arrival.ily, lines for `arrivalTptOne`, `arrivalTptTwo`, `arrivalHnOne`-`Four`, `arrivalTbnOne`, `arrivalTbnTwo`, `arrivalBassTbn`, `arrivalTuba`, `arrivalTimp`: `...4\p r2. |` becomes `...4\p r4 r2 |`. `arrivalVlnOne`: `| r2. g'4(\mf |` becomes `| r2 r4 g'4(\mf |`. dev.ily, `devVla`: `r1 | r2. \once ...` becomes `r1 | r2 r4 \once ...`. Only rests change. Notes, dynamics, the plagal close and the MIDI are unchanged, and the parts get the same grouping.
