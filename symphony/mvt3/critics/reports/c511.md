## Findings

### 1. Bar 81: the harp sounds the resolution F under F2's suspended G (applied in scratch)
- **Problem.** At 80.4–81 the tune's G is tied into 81 over V7 and resolves to F on 81.2: Vln I and Fl 1 G5, Vln II G4, cellos and Hn 1 G3. This is the 7/6 delay of 2̂ over V7 that A&S give as typical of 19th-century melody (PDF 353–354, Ex. 21-15e/f), and the climax cadence of the movement's main lyrical tune. On the downbeat, though, the harp's first eighth is F4. That is the tone of resolution, a whole tone under Vln II's G4 and a ninth above the G3 in the cellos and horn. A&S PDF 352 (Ex. 21-12, marked "poor"): sounding the resolution in another voice before the suspension resolves "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution."
- **Evidence.** Same bar, same reason: c398 moved Hn 2 off F3 because the suspended G3 "sounded against its own resolution note." c352 (A&S Unit 27) later rewrote the harp group as F4–A♭4–D5–F5 to keep B♭ away from the violas' C♭4. That change brought an F back onto the downbeat, and nobody checked it against the c241 suspension. The harp arpeggio does not contrast enough to excuse it: the strings are mf, the harp is in the same register, and the G4–F4 clash is a unison-octave step. Score.midi in v511: Harp RH 81.1 F4 (0.5 beat) against G5/G4/G3.
- **Fix (applied).** sections/sTwo.ily, `sTwoHarpR`, bar 81 (15th bar of the variable):
  `f'8 aes'8 d''8 f''8 f'8 aes'8 bes'8 d''8` → `g'8 aes'8 d''8 f''8 f'8 aes'8 bes'8 d''8`.
  The harp now re-strikes the tune's G (a restruck suspension, A&S PDF 347), and its F5 arrives on 81.2.5, after the resolution. Its lowest notes run F–F | G–F | E♭ (80–82), the same 6–5 that the tune plays. The change keeps c352's rule (no B♭ in the first group, because of the violas' C♭4), adds no D before Ob 1's E♭5→D5 resolves (D5 still falls on 81.2), and needs no new pedal change (G♮ is already set). A comment line in sTwo.ily records the change. No theme or quotation changes.

### 2. Bar 211: F under the G♭6 suspension — no change
In the ff tutti that recalls theme B, F sounds in F3, F4 and F5 (Bn, Tbn, Vla, Tpt; Ob 2 from 211.3) while Vln I hold G♭6 into 211.2. A&S PDF 352 allow this "in a texture with marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics" and cite Beethoven. Here the suspension is alone at the top, two octaves clear, against held brass. It follows the exception, and the bar is protected (c151/c152). Leave it.

### 3. Bars 170→171: upward 2̂–3̂ resolution (Ex. 21-25) — considered, declined
A&S PDF 358–359 show 2̂ held over i and rising to 3̂ in minor. The violins' D4 at 170 could tie into 171 and rise to E♭. I declined this. The violas' E♭3 arpeggio starts on 171.1, so the resolution tone would sound against the suspension (PDF 352 again). The added ninth would also blur the first note of the muted trumpet and cor anglais solo (protected, 171–186), whose entry is meant to be clean.

### 4. Bar 238: the S head's C6–B5 is an appoggiatura, not a prepared suspension — no change
The S head approaches C6 by a leap from A5 (237.4), so it is a 4–3 without preparation (PDF 347, 349–350). Preparing it would mean rewriting 237's F–A pickup, which is the head's own continuation, and the S-head texture 235–242 is protected. The leap also gives the head its lift, which Dvořák 9/iv's E-minor second theme shares. Leave it.

### 5. Bar 272→273: transferred resolution — follows
F6 at 272.4 (Vln I, Fl 1, Ob 1, Tpt 1) drops to the C6 of 273. Its E is taken by the violas' E4 inside the motto chain. This is the transferred resolution of Ex. 21-28b/c (PDF 360–361), and it is protected (273–279).

### 6. Melody overall
Measured against the reading, the tunes now use the unit well. F1's 4–3 at 25 and the ♭9 peak at 22 give the consequent a goal. F2's two halves end against each other: an anticipation at 73–74 that pushes ahead and a 7/6 suspension at 80–81 that holds back, the pairing in Tchaikovsky 5/iv's and Brahms 1/iv's big tunes. The chorale canon (226–230) takes the Jupiter-finale device of Ex. 21-1 directly. With finding 1, F2's main cadence now sounds its suspension without a competing F, which is what a listener carries away from the tune. I found nothing else in the reading that would clearly improve an unprotected melodic bar.
