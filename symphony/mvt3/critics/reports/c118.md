## Findings

### F1 (applied). 212-214: the violins play the theme B recall detached, against the winds' slur
Problem: at 212-214 Vln I doubles the flutes on movement II's theme B (finale.md item 15, Largamente, cantabile). Vln II supports it a sixth below, in half notes and quarters with no slurs. The flutes, oboes, clarinets and trumpets play the same bars under one phrase slur, with the breath after 214 (c028). At 215-216 the same violins slur four quarters to a bow.
Evidence: Adler PDF 37, rule 1: with no slurs "each note requires a change in the direction of the bow". PDF 41: in détaché "one hears the articulation of the bow changes". PDF 38: "the only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow". The violins chop a quoted cantabile line into accented bow strokes, and only the winds sound legato. At 2 = 72 a bar lasts 1.67 s, an easy single bow at f.
Fix (codaTwo.ily): Vln I `f'''2.(\f ges'''4) | aes'''2( bes'''4 aes'''4) | ges'''2.( f'''4)`. Vln II `f''2(\f des''2) | des''2( ges''4 f''4) | es''2.( des''4)`. Bar 211 stays as it is: its repeated D♭ under ff> needs separate strokes. Pitches and dynamics are unchanged, so the 205-223 dynamic path and the quotation record are not touched.

### F2 (applied). 245-246: the stringendo run puts seven eighths and a held G6 on one bow under f < ff

The violas slur `c'8 d' e' f' g'4)` and then take G3 on a separate bow, so they come to the ff downbeat of 247 (`e'2.~(`) on an up-bow.

### F3 (applied). 149-150 and 263-264: held ff/fff notes on one bow before a new attack
Problem: at 149-150, Vln I-II and the violas hold tied ff double stops about 2.8 s under the allargando, then attack F1 ff at 151. The cellos and basses hold G for the same 2.8 s. At 263-264 the cellos and basses hold the fff C for two bars at 2 = 88, about 2.7 s, under the strings' scale figure, then attack ff at 265. Nothing marks the bowing, so each hold is one bow. A single bow at fff decays, and 263 is one of the three climaxes.
Evidence: PDF 38, Coriolanus Ex. II-20. The example has tied whole notes followed by an ff attack, and Adler writes: "A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid". The score already uses this at 1 and 5 (`\dnup`, c018). PDF 39 adds: "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow", and cello and bass bows are shorter.
Fix: retr.ily, Vln I `<f'' d'''>1\dnup~`, Vln II `<b' g''>1\dnup~`, Vla `<d' g'>1\dnup~`. The cellos and basses get `\cbaw` instead of `\dnup` (`<g, f>1~\cbaw`, `g,,1~\cbaw`), because their D pickup on beat 4 of 150 must stay an up-bow. finale.ily, Vc `c1\fff\dnup~`, Cb `c,1\fff\dnup~`, so the pedal stays fff to the end of 264 and 265 starts on a down-bow. No notes change, and the strokes 147-150 are untouched.

### F4 (applied). 276: Vln II articulates the closing chain differently from the parts it doubles
Problem: Vln II plays `e''4 d''8( des''8) c''4-.`, which is three bows. Ob 1·2 play the same E5-D5-D♭5-C5 as `e''4( d''8 des''8 c''4-.)`, and Vln I plays its octave in one bow. finale.md says this line "sounds in one voice" (since v91).
Evidence: PDF 37, rule 2: notes under one slur are one bow, legato. PDF 45: in a slurred staccato the dot goes only on the last short note, which is how the oboes and Vln I are written. The separate E and C put two bow changes into the chromatic tail of the scherzo quotation.
Fix (finale.ily): Vln II `e''4( d''8 des''8 c''4-.)`.

### F5 (applied). 217-218: held f notes in Vln I-II and cellos have no bow instruction; only the basses do
