## Findings

### 1. Bars 83–84, flute staff: a unison printed as two voices with every mark doubled (applied)
Problem: since c131 Fl 2 plays Fl 1's line at 83–86 note for note, with the same marks. Fl 1's crescendo starts at 82 and ends at the f on 83, so the part combiner reads the two flutes as different at 83 and prints two voices: two accents on the E♭6 at 83 (the second under the staff next to the f), two slurs, two staccato dots on 84.1, and "a2" only at 84 beat 2. The conductor sees a two-voice texture that does not exist.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 8 and Example 7 (PDF 12): combined or extracted material must be proofread for doubled and floating marks; Example 6 (PDF 11): a unison on a shared staff is one line marked "a2".
Fix (clos.ily, score only, so the parts are unchanged): `closFluteOne = { \tag #'score \partCombineUnisono es'''2->(\f … | es'''2-> r2 | \tag #'score \partCombineAutomatic r4 bes''8(\f …`. The score now shows one line with "a2" at 83. The MIDI now has the two flutes as one track for 83–84.1, the same as for the rest of the unison.

### 2. Bars 159–166, oboe staff: two crossing, independent lines on one staff (applied)
Problem: since c134 Ob 2 doubles Vln II's eighth-note counter-line while Ob 1 plays F1. On the shared staff the two lines cross in 159 and in every bar from 161 to 165. The staff carries two sets of slurs, accents and staccatos, and at 162–163 a half-note head and an eighth-note head share a pitch, so they are pushed side by side. Of all 16 pages, this staff is the hardest to read.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 7: avoid crowding so each measure is clear; complicated divisions go on separate staves with continuous barlines. PDF 8: proofread the vertical lines as well as the horizontal. Adler part 2 PDF 294: two players share a staff only when their marks can be shown as one set.
Fix: recP.ily defines `recPOboeTwoDiv` (the 8 bars, with `\ff^\markup \italic "espress."` at 159, as Vln II has). `recPOboeTwo` uses `\tag #'part { \recPOboeTwoDiv } \tag #'score { R1*8 }` for 159–166. score.ly adds, after "Oboes 1·2": `\new Staff = "Oboe 2" \with { instrumentName = "Oboe 2" shortInstrumentName = "Ob. 2" midiInstrument = "oboe" \override VerticalAxisGroup.remove-first = ##t } << \global { R1*158 \recPOboeTwoDiv R1*113 } >>`. The new staff shows only in the system with 158–170, and the combined staff shows Ob 1 alone, labelled "1.", at 159. The Ob 2 part is unchanged except for "ff espress." at 159. Note counts in the MIDI are identical (526 oboe notes before and after). The page layout stays at 16 pages; page 9 now starts at 158 (page 8 ends at 157). If section lengths before 159 change, the composer must update `R1*158` and `R1*113`; bar-check warnings will show it.

### 3. Page 2: bar number 13 under the system separator, and the number row drifts toward the next system (applied)
Problem: the bar numbers sit 7 staff spaces below the Cb staff. Page 2 has two full systems close together, so the row for 13–24 is nearer to system 2 than to system 1: "15" stands beside system 2's letter B (bar 27). The slash separator is printed over "13", the first bar number of the system.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 4: bar numbers below the system, placed the same way throughout; the first bar of each system is the number a reader looks for. Adler part 2 PDF 295: the two slash marks separate the systems "so that it will be easy for the eye to separate them immediately". Here they cover a number.

Page 2 now has the row directly under system 1 and the slash clear of "13". Still 16 pages.

### 4. Ob 2 part: 32 bars of rest (167–198) and an ff entry at Tempo I with no cue (applied)

### 5. All parts: multi-measure rests without bar ranges (applied in parts-book.ly)
Problem: the parts number each multi-measure rest with its count ("16") but not with its bars. MOLA asks for ranges, and Example 3 prints "9 - 11" under the rest. Players count long rests against the conductor's bar numbers, for example after a stop in rehearsal.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 5: "Numbering each measure should be avoided, except in the case of multi-measure rests, where measure number ranges are helpful (e.g., '27–117')". Example 3, PDF 10. c075 read "ranges" as numbered rests; the example shows the actual range.
Fix (parts-book.ly): a stencil callback `mmr-with-range`. It adds "first–last" in a small font, centred under every rest of two or more bars, taking the first bar from the left column's `rhythmic-location` and the length from `measure-count`. It is installed with `\context { \Staff \override MultiMeasureRest.stencil = #mmr-with-range }` in the parts' `\layout`. Checked in the Ob 2 part: 3–4, 7–8, 11–26, 43–48, 49–50, 51–66, 67–80, 81–82, 83–90, 95–98, 99–109, 115–122, 123–134, 135–146, 167–170, 171–186, 187–196, 205–206, 224–225, 235–242, 243–246, 255–256, 265–268, all correct. The score is unaffected.
