## Findings

### 1. Bar 25: Hn 1·2 drop to B2 under the consequent's cadence (tested, not applied)

**Bars.** 25–26, Hn 1·2 (`es4( d8 c8~ c4 b,4) | c2->` in bass clef).

**Problem tested.** The horns follow F1's consequent an octave below Vln II down to concert B2. Adler PDF 256–257 says the most prominent melody note must lie in a good register for its instrument, and PDF 141 (Ex. V-19) shows the cellos taking over when a tune falls low. I tested Hn 1·2 an octave higher, `es'4( d'8 c'8~ c'4 b4) | c'2-> r2` (treble clef kept).

**Result.** No new parallels (par8.py at 24–26 lists only the existing bass octaves at 26.1), and the page count stays 16. But the build reports "compressing over-full page by 1.3 staff-spaces" on page 2, and the change is not better as music. The raised horns double Vln II, Ob 1·2 at unison, and Adler PDF 257 says unison overlap "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". The cadence also loses the low octave that c187 placed under the three winds above. I reverted it. The scratch copy is identical to v559 and builds clean.

### 5. Holistic comparison with Mahler 1/iv (no change)

The finale shares Mahler's main devices:
- an outburst after a soft end (the scherzo's pp G♯ answered by the ff German sixth; Mahler's cymbal crash after the first movement's close);
- a lyrical second theme in a flat key over strings and harp (67, E♭);
- recalls of the first movement (199–204, 235–246, the horn call at 243);
- a false goal (the stop chord at 138, as Mahler's early D-major breakthrough collapses);
- a brass chorale that wins the end (223, 255).

Mahler saves his percussion and full brass for the breakthrough; this score does the same: cymbals only at 223, 263 and 278, trumpets tacet until 51. Mahler's coda is one long rise, and this one moves in stages (protected, c359). Mahler doubles the chorale with the horns standing. Here, the cut at 264.1 and the hammered close 273–279 do the equivalent job within Brahms's and Tchaikovsky's manner, which the brief names as models. I found nothing that a conductor would take for a beginner's mistake. The weakest pages are still the dense coda pages 15–16, where 23–27 voices sound on average (analysis 257–276). They are protected and balanced by c516's and c226's brass rests.
