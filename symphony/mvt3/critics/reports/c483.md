## Findings

### F1 (applied). 217–222: the tuba enters the crescendo into the arrival instead of sitting in the soft tutti
- **Problem.** At 219–220 the whole orchestra plays the cyclic German sixth and the 6/4 sub. p, tuba included, on A♭1 and then G1. There the tuba only doubles the contrabassoon and the basses at the unison, and it needs a breath at 220|221 (c316). From 221, every group crescendoes together toward 223. No colour is added during the crescendo. The protected stepped crescendo is built from levels alone.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky PDF 120: tutti "is used essentially in forte and fortissimo, rarely in pianissimo and piano".
  - PDF 127 (Amplification): "the second group enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes."
  - PDF 130: a long crescendo adds groups with the brass last.
  - PDF 136: after a rest, brass re-enter pp or ff.
  - Adler's advice on tuba endurance (c406) points the same way. The tuba played 199–216 and then 219–234.
- **Fix.** codaTwo.ily:
  `codaTwoTuba = { des,1\ff\> | des,1\f | ges,,1 | aes,,1 \breathe | f,,1 | f,,1\> | R1\! | R1 | R1*2 | g,,1\pp\< | d,1\mf\< | }`
  The tuba now rests 217–220 and re-enters pp < on G1 at 221, where the allargando and the brass crescendo begin. It reaches mf < at 222, the stop chord over D2, and goes into its fff C2 at 223. The sub. p bars become lighter, and the last two bars of the build gain the low brass's darkest colour as a new timbre, as PDF 127 describes.
- **What stays the same.** The A♭1 and G1 are still sounded by the contrabassoon and basses, and A♭2 and G2 by the bass trombone and Bn 2. Unchanged: the sub. p at 219, the 205–223 dynamic path, the D♭–C–B–C top line, the D bass of the stop chord and the 222→223 resolution.
- **Tested and declined: tuba resting until 223.** I tested this first. It goes against PDF 130, because the brass should carry the crescendo into the peak, and the 221–222 dominant would lose its deepest brass.
- **Build.** The scratch build has 0 warnings and 16 pages. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 217–224 shows no new flags (one fewer doubling flag, the tuba's at 223.1).

### F2 (no change). The coda's stages build; the dip is placed where the reading allows it
- **Proportions.** The coda is long (31% against Dvořák's 21%), and the recapitulation is short (16% against 22%). This was accepted at c219, and the owed items need the room.
- **Stage by stage.** Every loud stage brings something new, as PDF 136 asks:
  - 211: trumpets ff after five bars' rest, Largamente.
  - 223: first crash, tremolo, C7, bass clarinet after 72 bars.
  - 247: tuba ff after a rest, Più allegro, the combination.
  - 255: bass trombone ff after 21 bars.
  - 263: crash, first root-position V–I.
  - 273: contrabassoon and bass trombone ff after a rest, Largamente.
  - 278: harp glissando, crash.
- **The dip.** The one quiet stretch, 231–246, follows the fff arrival and the chorale and comes before the fast close. That matches PDF 136 ("neither musical feeling nor the ear itself can stand, for long, the full resources"). The slow and fast halves after 223 are equal in length (47 s each).

### F3 (observation, no change). 147–151, a step scored heavier than its goal
- **What happens.** By the measure of PDF 116 (climax or step), the ff dominant at 147–150 is the densest tutti before 209: 24.8 voices, with trumpets, tuba and cor anglais. The recapitulation at 151 then thins, with no trumpets or tuba until 159.
- **Why it stays.** This is the planned shape: the recapitulation starts over the dominant (i6/4, c18) and reaches the tonic root at 155, and the trumpets rest 151–158 (c169). The weight properly falls on the dominant that is resolved later, and the protections stand.
