## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 43: the flute's A♮ tonicizes B♭ against the held 7th A♭ (applied)
- **Problem.** The flute's scale figure at 43 runs F–E♭–D–C–B♭–**A♮**–B♭. Bassoon 1 holds A♭3, the 7th of V7 of E♭, through 43–50, so A♮4 sounds against A♭3. The figure names B♭ as its tonic, with its own leading tone. This is the only bar in 39–50 that does not point to E♭.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 214: ♯4 (here A♮) is the leading tone of V, and the chords that contain it tonicize V. PDF 220: 4 (A♭) is "the one scale degree in the tonic key that does not appear in the key of the dominant" and neutralizes V as a tonic, while V7 has "so strong a drive toward I". Sounding ♯4 and 4 together in the same bar is self-contradictory.
- **The score's own model.** At 3–4, 101 and 105 the figure ends on the tonic of the key being prepared (C, F, B♭ minor) over that key's dominant. At 87 and 45 it does the same for E♭. At 89 the same F-to-B♭ figure over V7 of E♭ already uses A♭. Dvořák 9/iv 60–68 keeps ♯4 and 4 apart (see above).
- **Fix (applied).** In trans.ily: `transFluteOne = { r4 f''8(\p es''8 d''8 c''8 bes'8 aes'8 | bes'8 c''8 d''8 es''8 f''4) r4 | R1*6 | }`. Contour, register and the darkening Fl → Cl (G♭) → Ob (C♭, D♭) are unchanged, and every note of 43–50 now belongs to E♭.

### 2. Bars 52, 60 and 116: leading tone doubled over the neighbour bass (applied)
- **Problem.** At 52 and 60 (F2's second bar and its repeat) the bass falls E♭–D–E♭. Over the D, Clarinet 1 (D4) and Violin II pizz. (D4–F4) also take D, and all three rise to E♭ together. At 116 (G minor, V6/5 over F♯) Violin II's pizz. F♯5 doubles the bass F♯, and both rise to G a beat apart. Cycle 92 fixed the same fault at 65 (Bn 1 D3 under the solo's D4). The recapitulation already avoids it: at 172 and 180 the leading tone B is only in the bass (Vc, Bn 1 at the unison).
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 214 NOTE and PDF 228 point 2: a leading tone of a tonicized degree "must not be doubled". PDF 211 Ex. 14-3 (e, f) shows the inverted V7 forms with the leading tone in one voice. The textbook chord over a neighbour bass 1–7–1 is V6/5, with the leading tone in the bass only.
- **Fix (applied).**
  - sOne.ily: Cl 1 at 52 and 60 `bes2 es'2` (was `d'2 es'2`). This completes V6/5 (D–F–A♭–B♭) and keeps Cl 1 above Cl 2.
  - Vln II at 52 and 60, beat 2: `<f' aes'>4` (was `<d' f'>4`). It moves in thirds to `<es' ges'>` on beat 4.
  - dev.ily: Vln II at 116, beat 2: `<d'' a''>4` (was `<d'' fis''>4`). A falls to G on beat 4.
  - Bn 1 stays at the unison with the bass, as in the recapitulation.
- **Protected passage.** The 51–66 accompaniment is on the do-not-touch list (cycles 4 and 7) for its pizz. off-beat pp texture. Rhythm, dynamics, articulation and scoring are unchanged; only these pitches move, as cycle 92 already did at 65. The trumpet solo stays undoubled: no added note coincides with its pitches on the same beat.

### 3. Bars 50→51: the 7th of the long dominant does not fall into the second theme (applied)
- **Problem.** After 12 bars of V7 of E♭, Bassoon 1 leaps from its held 7th A♭3 down to E♭3 on the downbeat of 51. Oboe 1's A♭4 stops at 50. The step A♭→G♭ is heard only in the off-beat pizz. chords on beat 2. The arrival of the exposition's new key is the one V7→i of the movement where the 7th has had the most time to build its pull.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 216: the dominant "precedes the new tonic and helps to prepare it". PDF 220: the 7th gives V7 its drive toward I, and that drive is realized by the 7th falling by step.
- **Fix (applied).** In sOne.ily: `sOneBsnOne = { ges2\pp es2 | d2 es2 | …`. Bassoon 1 resolves A♭3→G♭3 on the downbeat, the same G♭3 as Clarinet 2, then joins the pad's E♭ on beat 3. Nothing else in 51 changes.
