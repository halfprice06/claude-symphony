## Findings

### 1. 222→223, bass trombone: the stop chord's resolution has hidden fifths in the trombones (applied)

- **Problem.** In 222 the bass trombone holds D3 directly under Tbn 1's A♭3, a d5 within one choir. Both fall a step into the arrival's C3–G3 open fifth. This is the voice leading A&S marks "avoid" (PDF 375, Ex. 22-3c: an upper d5 moving in similar motion to a P5 gives "an obtrusive set of hidden 5ths") and "poor" (PDF 380, Ex. 22-4b: VII°6/5 to a root-position I with the d5 above the bass moving to a P5).
- **A second fault.** Tbn 2's leading tone B2 leaps to E3, so the trombones never resolve 7→8 at the symphony's main arrival. The trumpets, horns, flutes and violins all do.
- **Fix.** The bass trombone takes the chord's B2 instead of D3: `codaTwoBassTbn … g,1\< | b,1\mf\< | }` (was `d1\mf\<`). B2–A♭3 is now the chord's d7, and it resolves inward to the P5 C3–G3 exactly as Ex. 22-2 (PDF 375) prescribes. The bass trombone's line becomes G2–B2–C3 (7→8). Tbn 1 keeps A♭→G (c370) and Tbn 2 keeps B2→E3. The D bass stays in the tuba, the basses, Bn 2, the contrabassoon and the cellos.
- **Protected material.** The stop chord's pitches, its resolution only at 222→223, the trumpets' B→C, the timpani G→C, the 223 chord scoring and the dynamics are all unchanged. The protection exists so that this chord resolves only here and in this way; the fix changes only which chord tone one doubling instrument plays in the bar before.
- **Checks.** par8.py on 219–224: the only change is that the bass trombone's octave with the bass at 223 is gone. The other lines are the same orchestral doublings as in v582. allpairs: the bass trombone's B2→C3 now moves in octaves with the other B→C leading-tone voices, which is doubling of the resolving leading tone.
- **Build.** Clean: lilypond rc 0, build-warnings.txt empty, 0 outer-voice parallels (analysis.txt otherwise identical), 16 pages, finale quote record 51/51 unchanged. I checked page 12 visually.

### 2. 14.3–4 and 154.3–4: VII°6/5 in place of V4/3 under F1 (tested on paper, declined)

- **Candidate.** Following Ex. 22-1 (Beethoven Op. 10/1, PDF 373–374: °7 positions inside a C-minor allegro theme) and the 5–♭6–5 inner neighbour (PDF 381), Cl 1 `d'8( aes' b' aes')` and Hn 4 A♭ would replace G on the second half of 14, turning the V4/3 into VII°6/5 → i at 15.
- **Why declined.** The bass moves D→C to a root-position i and the soprano moves E♭–D–E♭ (3–2–3), not 8–7–8, in spread position. This is Ex. 22-4b, which the book calls poor (PDF 380): the D–A♭ d5 involving the bass would fall in similar motion to C–G, breaking the box rule "all dissonances involving the bass must resolve" (PDF 381).
- **The recapitulation.** At 154 Tbn 1's G3→C4 (c364) cannot take A♭ without leaving the seventh unresolved. That bar would become V♭9 rather than VII°7. The current V4/3 is the better chord in both places. No change.

### 3. 238.3–4 and 268.4: VII°7 over the tonic pedal in place of V7 (declined)

- **238.** Ex. 22-10 (PDF 383) puts VII°7 over a tonic pedal in place of V7 in I–IV–VII°7–I. The S head's 235–238 is exactly I–IV–ii–V7 over the C pedal. But 238 moves to vi (A bass) at 239, so a ♭6 A♭ would have to rise to A: the ♭6–♮6 that Ex. 23-2h calls the thing to avoid (PDF 388).
- **238, the other options.** VIIø7 (A♮) would sit over the cellos' G2 and sound as V9, not as a leading-tone chord.
- **268.4.** The F1 head has G on that beat, so any A♭ makes a V♭9 over the pedal. It would also bring ♭6 in five bars before the 273–275 mixture close (F♯°7 – iv – I), which needs to be the first ♭6 since 262. No change.

### 4. 222→223, cellos D3 against Tbn 1 A♭3 (considered, kept)

The cellos' D3→C3 under Tbn 1's A♭3→G3 is the same interval pattern, but it is spread across two choirs. Vla F4→E4 and Vln II D5→E5 move in parallel above the bass, which is the softening Ex. 22-4d names (PDF 380). Moving the cellos to F3 or B2 would take the D out of the tenor register, or force F→G. Kept.
