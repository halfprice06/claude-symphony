## Findings

### 1. Bar 19: the counterstatement of F1 repeats the antecedent's bass leap; use the passing V6/4 (applied)

**Problem.** Bars 19-26 are F1's counterstatement: an octave higher, f, with the flutes and oboes added. Its bass starts with the antecedent's own leap E♭-C (`es2 c2`, as at 11), and then continues B♭-A♭-F at 20. So the first group's second phrase begins with no new bass motion. The bass needs one passing step, D, to become a continuous descent E♭-D-C-B♭-A♭ from 19.1 to 20.3, which then carries the phrase to the V at 21. The score has no passing V6/4 anywhere. A&S calls it the most important passing 6/4.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 309-310 (Ex. 19-4 bar 2, Ex. 19-9) and the summary on PDF 324, point 5: V6/4 connects I and I6 over a stepwise bass. The 4th above the bass (here G, the melody's note on beat 3) is stable. The dissonant element is the passing bass. The bass of the 6/4 is often doubled.
- PDF 256-257: stepwise bass motions create continuity over longer spans and work as expanded passing motions. With 20's existing quarters, the line becomes E♭-D-C | B♭-A♭-F.
- PDF 258-259 (Ex. 16-16a): the B♭ at 20.1 is the minor v that carries ♮7 down to A♭. With the new bass, that descent starts one beat earlier, at the D.
- The cyclic rule is untouched. D is the bass of a 6/4, not a root-position V. The melody, F1's notes and the dynamics are unchanged.

**Fix** (sections/pOne.ily, bar 19 = the 9th bar of each variable):

```
pOneVc:      es2 c2                         ->  es2 d4 c4
pOneCb:      es,2 c,2                       ->  es,2 d,4 c,4
pOneBsnTwo:  es2 c2                         ->  es2 d4 c4
pOneCbsn:    es,2\mf c,2                    ->  es,2\mf d,4 c,4
pOneVla:     g8( c' es' c') g8( c' es' g)   ->  g8( c' es' c') g8( b c' es')
pOneClOne:   es'8( g' c'' g') es'8( g' c'' g')  ->  es'8( g' c'' g') d'8( g' es' g')
```

- Beat 3 now sounds D-G-B: the melody G, violas G3-B3, Cl 1 D4-G4 (which doubles the bass) and the bass D. Beat 4 sounds C-E♭-G under the melody's G-A♭.
- The leading tone B3 is in the violas only and rises to C4.
- Hn 3·4 and Bn 1 play their off-beats on beats 2 and 4, where the old harmony stands, so they need no change.
- The recapitulation's 159 already differs from 19 (Vln II's counter-line E♭-D-C-B over the E♭ bass), so I leave 159, which is protected, as it is.

### 2. Proportions and the coda's stages: follows, no change

**Assessment.**
- The coda (31%) is longer than the recapitulation (16%). In Dvořák 9/iv the four parts are nearly equal (34/20/22/21).
- The plan explains the difference:
  - The recapitulation carries F2 in C minor and then in A♭.
  - The first stable C, the first C major and the only root-position V-I are all saved for the coda (cyclic rules: 223, 262→263).
  - A&S PDF 252 describes how a long bass descent is divided into stages. The coda's bass, C (199), A♭ (205-210), D♭ (211-218), A♭ again (219), G (220-221) and C (223), is divided that way.
- The tension curve:
  - It falls once, at the arrival's plagal close 231-234.
  - It rises from 239 through the stringendo into 247, and again from 255 to 263 and from 269 to 278.
- The four 12-bar stages from 199 and the final stage from 247 each have their own tempo (Tempo I, then 72, 56, 66 and 88). None repeats the one before.

**Decision.** No change. The slow/fast balance was settled in c043 and c063 and confirmed in c223. I find nothing in this reading that argues against it.

### 3. Coda tonic pedals: the neighbouring IV6/4 eight times (no change, with a corpus answer)

**Assessment.** Critic 223 (finding 3) flagged the same prolongation, I-IV6/4-I over the C pedal, at 231, 233, 236, 266, 268, 270, 272 and 274. It is correct by A&S PDF 308-309: the 6/4 is unaccented, the bass is doubled, and it is the sustained-bass form of I-IV-I.

**Evidence.** The corpus shows that the habit is in style. Dvořák 9/iv's recapitulation codetta alternates I and iv6/4 five times over its tonic (harmony.csv, onsets 1000-1052) before its coda.

**Decision.** No change. The bars are protected (223-234; 263-279), and the repetition works as the plagal colour that closes each stage.

### 4. 257-259: the passing 6/4 between IV6 and II6/5 (considered, rejected)

**What the reading offers.** A&S PDF 310-311 (Ex. 19-11) calls this passing 6/4 very frequent, with the outer voices in sixths. The bass of the last chorale statement leaps A (257-258) to F (259). A passing C/G on 258.3 would fill the leap, giving A-G-F under the held E5-D5.

**Why I reject it.** It puts G in the bass four bars before the structural V at 262. The bass would then approach that G from G itself, F and A♭, and the movement's only root-position V-I would lose its first-time weight. PDF 315-316 says a strong V-I needs its V unweakened.

**Decision.** Not applied. The cadence 259-263 is protected for the same reason.

### 5. 147-150: a neighbouring i6/4 inside the retransition's V7 (considered, rejected)

**What the reading offers.** A&S PDF 256-257 (Ex. 16-13) shows a "I" used as a neighbour chord inside a prolonged V. Dvořák 9/iv's coda does the same over its dominant, alternating i6/4 and v twelve times in a row, one beat each (harmony.csv 1120-1144).

**Why I reject it.** The recapitulation itself begins on i6/4 over G (151-152), the movement's structural accented 6/4 (PDF 306-307). A neighbouring i6/4 inside 147-150 would anticipate it and take away its effect.

**Decision.** Not applied. The bars are protected (135-171; 147-150 notes and voicing).

### 6. 220-223 and 243-247: expanded cadential 6/4 and transferred resolution follow the reading

**Assessment.**
- **220-223.** The whole-bar cadential 6/4 at 220 resolves to V7 at 221. Then the stop chord at 222 is interpolated before I. This is the cadenza principle of A&S PDF 317-319, with a neighbouring chord between the dominant and its goal (Ex. 19-23).
- **243-247.** The coda's next cadence uses a short 6/4 (243.3-244.2). Its V is then held as V4/3 over D (245-246) and goes to I at 247. That is the resolution "into the bass" of PDF 315-316, which the book calls right where no strong V-I is wanted. Here none is wanted: the cyclic rule saves the root-position V-I for 262-263.

**Decision.** No change (235-247 protected).
