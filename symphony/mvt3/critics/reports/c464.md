## Findings

### 1. 262–264: the cadence chord has no woodwind third above the trumpets (applied)

- Bars: 262–264 (the only root V7–I in C, fff at 263).
- Problem: at 263 the trumpets hold C5 (Tpt 1·2), E4 (Tpt 3) and the trombones C4/G3. Above the trumpets, the woodwinds give only C6 (Fl 1·2) and G5 (Cl 1). Ob 1 and Cl 2 sit at C5, in unison with the trumpets. Ob 2 sits on E4 at fff, in the oboe's coarse lowest fifth and under the horns and trombones. The chord's third sounds at E4 (Ob 2, Hn 3, Tpt 3, violas) and at E5 only in Vln II. Since c404 moved Fl 2 off E5, no wind instrument plays E above the trumpets.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 110–111: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets." The PDF 111 diagrams show this layout, and the examples include a final C-major chord. PDF 114: balance matters most in long held chords, and 263–264 is a held fff. c044's aim was that the 263 chord should not have woodwinds inside the trumpets' unisons. Sebesky PDF 71: the oboe's F4–B♭3 fifth is coarse.
- Fix (applied): the oboes exchange roles at 262. Ob 1 takes V7's seventh as F5 and resolves it to E5. Ob 2 takes the chorale line B4–C5 at the trumpets' pitch, where Ob 1 was.

  - `finaleOboeTwo`: `... c''1 | f'1 | e'1\fff~ | e'2.\> r4\! |` becomes `... c''1 | b'1 | c''1\fff~ | c''2.\> r4\! |`
- What stays the same: the seventh F still resolves to E (c182's aim), an octave higher, with Hn 3's F4–E4. The chorale line C–B–C keeps an oboe at trumpet pitch. Bass, progression, top line and the cut at 264.1 are unchanged. Neither oboe crosses the other. The new pair checks show only octave doublings of the chorale line and of the seventh's resolution (Ob 1 F5–E5 over Hn 3 F4–E4, which was a unison with Ob 2 before).

### 2. 259–260: a gap in the brass and a fourfold D; the seventh belongs in a horn (applied)

- Bars: 259–260 (iiø6/5 F–A♭–C–D, ff, chorale's D).
- Problem: from the top, the brass play D5 (Tpt 1·2), F4 (Tpt 3), then D4 in Hn 1, Hn 2, Hn 3 and Tbn 1, then A♭3, C3 (bass trombone) and F1. A sixth F4–D5 is left empty in the brass. The chord's seventh C is heard only from the bass trombone's C3 (c454) and Ob 2.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 100: brass harmony should be in close order "with no empty spaces in the intervals". PDF 102–103: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour." A horn C5 against the trumpets' D5 is that case. PDF 102 asks for doubled horns in mixed brass, which Hn 1·2 a2 already provide on D4, so Hn 3 is the spare D.
- Fix (applied): `finaleHnThree` at 259–260: `d'2-> d'2 | d'2-> d'2` becomes `c''2-> c''2 | c''2-> c''2` (concert C5, written G5 for Hn 3). At 261 the part returns to its E♭4 as before.
- Consequences: C is the common tone between iiø6/5 and the German sixth, so the horn's C needs no resolution. The leap C5–E♭4 into 261 is an inner-part horn leap at ff. The pair check loses two flagged octaves at 261 (Hn 3·4 against Vln II, D–A♭) and gains none. par8 shows no change against the bass.

### 3. 257: woodwind f under brass ff (checked, no change)

Rimsky PDF 112 would mark the woodwind a degree louder than the brass when balance fails. At 257 the woodwinds enter f < under trumpets at ff. This entry is a staged crescendo (Rimsky PDF 130–131; c449's principle): the woodwinds reach ff at 259 and fff with the brass at 263, so balance is restored where the chord is held. I made no change.
