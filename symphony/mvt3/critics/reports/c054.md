## Findings

### 1. 110–113: the development climax hides its melody under the trumpets (applied)

**Problem.** Cycle 53 made 110–113 the development's goal at ff, with Tpt 1–3, tuba and timpani. The melody of the cadence (G5 | A♭5 F5 | F5 E5 | F5, the end of the lament's suspension line) is in only two players, Vln I and Fl 1. Nine brass play ff underneath. Tpt 1 is on E5–F5–F5/E5–F5, a third below the melody and in the trumpet's strongest register. Ob 1 and Vln II are in unison with that trumpet line, so three parts sit on E5 and the tune has the fewest players. Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 255: "the melody must come through … and not be obscured by the harmony." PDF 267–268 (Ex. VIII-39, Dvořák, *Carnival*): violins doubled by flutes an octave higher, oboes and clarinets filling the upper octave, and "the trick is to keep the volume of the brass down enough to let the strings and winds through." PDF 250: 8' + 4' coupling for a strong, clear line.

**Fix (dev.ily).** Fl 1 an octave above the melody (`g'''1\ff | aes'''2 f'''2 | f'''2 e'''2 | f'''2-> r2`). New `devFluteTwo` and Ob 1 take the melody at pitch with Vln I (`g''1\ff | aes''2 f''2 | f''2 e''2 | f''2-> r2`). New `devOboeTwo` takes Ob 1's old line (`e''1\ff | f''1 | des''2 c''2 | c''2-> r2`). Tpt 1–3, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, bass trombone and tuba are `\f` at 110 (were `\ff`). Strings, woodwinds and timpani stay ff. The climax stays the loudest point of the development, and the trumpets still sound, but the melody now has five players across two octaves. This revises one detail of cycle 53 (the brass dynamic) and keeps its decision (climax with trumpets, tuba, timpani). The timpani at 110–113 (C, C–A♭, C C, C) fit V–i–iv6–V–i in F and are kept.

### 2. 87–90: the trombones cover the violins' scale figure (applied)

**Problem.** Since cycle 53 the closing bridge is a 6/3 descent under the violins' scale figure. Tbn 1·2 play the two upper 6/3 voices at f in unison with Bn 1 and with the violas' double stops. On every voice, trombone, bassoon and viola play the same pitch. They sit at E♭4 down to G3, which is where Vln II's lower octave of the figure lies (B♭4 down to A3). No upper woodwind plays in 87–90, so the foreground figure has two violin sections against ten parts below and in the middle. Using the trombones at 87 also spends their entry before the ff codetta at 91.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257: unison overlapping "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". PDF 255: the melody must come through. PDF 266 (Ex. VIII-37, Berlioz): a flute doubling the first violins at pitch gives "warmth and richness … especially as the line goes up". PDF 233: function 4 of the winds is to double.

**Fix (clos.ily).** `closTbnOne` and `closTbnTwo` rest 87–90 (`R1*8 |` at the start). Bn 1 and the violas keep the 6/3 voices, and the horns' off-beats at 89–90 keep the brass colour. Fl 1 doubles Vln I's figure at pitch without the low octave quarters: `r4 bes''8(\f aes'' g'' f'') es''( d'' | es''8 f'' g'' aes'') bes''4-> r4 | r4 f''8( es'' d'' c'') bes'( a' | bes'8 c'' d'' es'') f''4-> r4 |`. The trombones and Fl 1 at ff in 91 are now new colours on the codetta.

### 3. 212–214: Vln I half-doubles theme B at the unison (applied)

**Problem.** In the one recall of movement II's theme B (Tpt 1 + Ob 1 + Cl 1, flutes an octave above), Vln I plays B♭5–A♭5–G♭5 | F5–B♭5–A♭5 | G♭5–F5. It meets the tune at the unison on some beats and lies a third beside it on others. Cycle 51 stopped the violins from moving a beat early, but the line is still a shadow of the tune in the tune's own octave. It thickens the trumpet line at pitch without adding a register.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 265–266: in winds doubling strings, octave doublings are "more successful" and at-pitch doublings "detract from the clarity of a line". PDF 250: the 8' + 4' principle. Rimsky-Korsakov (c021's citation, PDF 75) already put the flutes at the upper octave for this tune.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily).** 212–214 Vln I `f'''2.\f ges'''4 | aes'''2 bes'''4 aes'''4 | ges'''2. f'''4`, in unison with the flutes an octave above the trumpet. Vln II keeps its thirds below. 211 (D♭6–C6), 215–222 and the D♭–C–B–C top line are not touched. Vln I steps down from F6 to E♭5 at 215, at the phrase break the winds already breathe on. This revises cycle 51's violin line and keeps its aim (nothing moves against the tune). Theme B's notes and the quotation record are unchanged.

### 4. 7–8: no dominant pedal, and the bassoons double the cellos in unison (applied)

**Problem.** In the dominant preparation, 7–8 (mf) is Bn 1·2 a2 plus cellos on one line (D3–G2–A♭2–B2–C3–D3–E♭3–F3) with timpani strokes on G. Nothing holds the dominant until the wind chord at 9. Two bassoons and the cellos on the same pitches thicken the line without adding anything.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 224–225 (Mozart 29, Ex. VIII-8/9): without the sustained tones, "notice the lack of stability and grandeur". PDF 229: use the pedal effect "to great advantage whenever possible" in this style. PDF 249–250: two like winds in unison thicken and muddy the line.

**Fix (intro.ily).** Bn 2 holds the pedal from 7: `g,1\mf~ | g,1~ | g,1\<~ | g,2 g,4-> r4\!`. Bn 1 stays with the cellos. The pedal joins the existing Bn 2/Cbsn/Cb G at 9–10. The mf of cycle 53 is kept.
