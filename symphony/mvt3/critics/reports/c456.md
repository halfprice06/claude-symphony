## Findings

### 1. 113–114 Vln I·II: the accented arrival falls on an up-bow, and Vln II reaches the pizzicato at the tip (applied)

**Bars.** 110–115, Vln I, Vln II, violas.

**Problem.** In the F-minor climax the violins hold the ff G6/G5 from 110 (Vln II marked down-bow) and then play `g4( aes2 f4)` at 111, `f2~( f4 e4)` at 112 and the accented `f2->` at 113. Counting one bow per slur, 111 is up, 112 down and 113 up. The violas and basses have printed down-bows on 113 (c418, whose note says "matching the violins", but the violins have no sign), and the cellos reach 113 down-bow by count. So the string section's accented arrival on F is split between down-bows below and up-bows above. Counting on, Vln II's diminuendo half at 114 is a down-bow, so the players finish at the tip and have about two beats to change to "pizz. div." on 115.2. The cellos and basses already have an up-bow at 114 for this reason (c085). The violas reach 114 up-bow by count but have no sign.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: strong attacks are heavier at the frog, and a bow mark is needed where the natural order would not give the down-bow. Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus) shows a retaken down-bow before a vigorous attack. PDF 42–43: accented arrivals are taken down-bow. PDF 54: "it is much easier to change from arco to pizzicato if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog."

**Fix (sections/dev.ily, applied).**
```
devVlnOne: … f'''2~( f'''4 e'''4) | f'''2->\downbow r2 | fis''1\p\> | …
devVlnTwo: … f''2~( f''4 e''4) | f''2->\downbow r2 | c''2.\p\>\upbow r4\! | …
devVla:    … aes2->\downbow r2 | a2.\p\>\upbow r4\! | …
```
The violins retake down-bow after the slur at 112, as in Ex. II-20. All five string parts now strike 113 down-bow, and all four parts that go pizz. at 115 arrive at the frog. I added a comment line (c456) above devVlnOne. These are bow signs only: score.midi is byte-identical, and the notes, dynamics and the protected 107–113 progression are unchanged. Bow signs in these bars were accepted before (c418).

## Considered and not applied

- **Violas 101, A♭3–C4 sf after "unis." (Adler PDF 29–30, Ex. II-6, II-8).** A chord with no "div." is played as a double stop. In first position both notes lie on the G string, so I tested whether "div." was needed. It is not. The violas' F1 statement at 99–101 (F4, C4, D♭4, E♭4, D♭4, C4, B♭3, A♭3) lies in third position on the G and C strings, and from there the third is the standard 3–1 fingering (A♭3 on the C string, C4 on the G string) with no shift. The double stop keeps the whole section on both notes. 105 (D♭4–F4) is a first-position 4–2 third. No change.
- **"al tallone" at 91–94 (PDF 42, Ex. II-28, II-29).** The down-bows on every accented quarter already put the stroke at the frog, which is where Adler says such series are "most often performed". PDF 38 says to mark only against habit. No change.
- **A violin trill on the E♭5 at 82 (PDF 48).** That E♭ is the tonic arrival of F2's closing cadence. A trill would decorate the resolution, not the dominant, and blur the G–A♭–B♭–D run into 83. No change.
- **"change bow at will" or staggered bowing at 219–221 (Ex. II-25).** Each bar has a new note, so the bow changes fall on the note changes. No change.
- **Natural harmonics, mutes, la metà, non vibrato (PDF 32–33, 59–60, 62–66).** I found no new place beyond c366's survey: no soft held string note on a natural-harmonic pitch outside protected bars, and no string rest long enough for mutes before a muted passage. I have no new reason to re-raise these.
