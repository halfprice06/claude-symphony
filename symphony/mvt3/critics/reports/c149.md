Would a conductor take this for a master's finale? In most places, yes:

- The retransition is built like Ex. 261.
- The trumpet solo's accompaniment is a textbook case of ch. V.
- The last chorale and the C-pedal close are well spaced.

The two weak spots are both moments where a climax arrives and the texture under or around it thins at the wrong time.

## Findings

### 1. 224-225: the arrival loses its high layer the bar after the fff chord (applied)

**Problem.** At 223 the full orchestra plays fff with the violins' C7 and Vln II E5/G5 tremolo. At 224 every woodwind and both violin sections stop. They stay silent until the canon enters ff at 226. For two bars nothing sounds above G4 at 224 or above E5 (Tpt 1) at 225. The music drops from a full-range tutti to a mid-register brass block for the chorale's first two bars. A listener hears the bottom fall out of the arrival rather than a chorale emerging from it.

Bruckner 4/iv keeps the string figuration running under the brass chorale of the coda. The arrival in Brahms 1/iv keeps a string layer under its brass. Neither stops the upper strings when the brass takes the theme.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 131: in a converging progression a sustained part "guarantees unity". Here the gap at 223→224 has no held part above the brass.
- PDF 133 and Ex. 264 (PDF 430-431): the harmonic basis sits in a different timbre and octave from the melody. Here a high string layer would place the C-major harmony above the brass chorale, not inside it.
- PDF 141 and Ex. 281 (PDF 449, Sadko, Allargando): the climax is supported by brass f with a string tremolo.

The tremolo rule (from 223 only) allows this, and C7 stays only at 223.

**On the protection.** "Arrival and chorale 223-234" is protected for the reason "master level". This change answers that reason directly with the model passages. No pitch of the chorale, the canon, the violas' portato, the bass or the 222→223 resolution changes.

**Fix** (arrival.ily):
```
arrivalVlnOne = { ... c''''1:32\fff | c'''1:32\mf | c'''1:32 | g''1\ff\cbaw | ...
arrivalVlnTwo = { <e'' g''>1:32\fff | <e'' g''>1:32\mf | <e'' g''>1:32 | g''1\ff\cbawU | ...
```
Vln I holds C6 and Vln II E5/G5 in tremolo, mf under the brass f, for two bars. Both C major chords fit 224 and 225 (chorale G, E over C, C). The ff canon at 226 then starts from a layer that is already sounding.

### 2. 77-79: the F2 peak in the violins has an empty middle (applied)

**Problem.** At the dotted peak of F2, at 78, Vln I and Fl 1 sound B♭6, and Vln II and Ob 1 sound B♭5. The harmony stops at E♭4/C4 (violas, cellos, horns, harp). The gap is about an octave and a fifth, and nothing harmonic sits in the melody's register. The clarinets rest for all of 67-82.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 141, with Ex. 280 (PDF 447-448): a climax is supported harmonically "in the register in which the voice is placed".
- PDF 159: two-part writing moves in 3rds and 6ths and avoids empty 4ths and 5ths on strong beats.

**Fix** (sTwo.ily):
```
sTwoClOne = { R1*10 | bes'2\p\< aes'2 | g'2\f bes'2 | c''2\> r2\! | R1*3 | }
sTwoClTwo = { R1*10 | g'2\p\< f'2 | es'2\f f'2 | aes'2\> r2\! | R1*3 | }
```
The clarinets follow the harmony: E♭/G, Fm7/A♭, Cm7, B♭/C, A♭. They move in 3rds, with one 4th on a weak half (78.3), and match the p< f hairpin of Fl 1 and Ob 1. They double the bassoon and horn pad an octave higher. Against the bass (G1, A♭1, C2, C2, A♭1) there are no parallel octaves or fifths: Cl 2 moves G→F against G→A♭, and Cl 1 moves B♭→A♭ in contrary motion into the octave. They rest at 80-82, so their closing-theme entry at 83 still comes after silence (PDF 135-136).

### 3. 266.1: brief cross-relation B♮5 against B♭4 (not applied)

The violins and flute have B♮5 while the trumpet and horn sound B♭4 on the same beat. It lasts an eighth, in the F1-in-C-major passage. c122 kept the related 267.4 point on purpose, and the F1 shape depends on it. I leave it as it is.

### 4. Coherence note: finale.md calls 263 "the movement's only root-position V-I" (not applied; wording only)

At 277 an eighth-note G-bass V goes to I in root position. It comes after 263, so the cyclic rule is kept. The sentence in finale.md is slightly inexact. The composer should say "the first" instead of "the only" when finale.md is next updated. The score needs no change.

### 5. Form: the coda after 223 is loud almost throughout (not applied)

45 of the 57 bars from 223 to 279 are f or louder. Bruckner reaches his last statement through one long pp-to-ff wave. This coda instead has one soft island (235-246). The form is protected and the plan sets these stages, so I only note the point. Finding 1 lightens 224-225 to mf in the new layer and does not add volume.

### 6. 278-279: the final chord is short next to Bruckner's (not applied)

At 2 = 60 the last chord lasts about two seconds before the quarter and the rest with fermata. Bruckner holds the final tonic for many bars. The close 273-279 with the scherzo quotation is protected, and its brevity matches the Allegro con fuoco character. I leave it.
