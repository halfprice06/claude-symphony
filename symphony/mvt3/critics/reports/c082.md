The deceptive cadence at 186 opens A♭, which is established only weakly: V6/5–I at 192–193, V7–I6 at 194–195, and a plagal close at 197–198, with no root-position V–I in A♭. The bars that follow reinterpret it. After the quoted C-minor tutti (199–204), A♭ returns as A♭7, the dominant of D♭ (205–210). ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 238→239: the 7th of V7 over the C pedal is never resolved (applied)
**Bars:** 238–239 (Q, second-theme head).
**Problem:** 238 is V7 over the tonic pedal (Vc G2, harp G–B–D, Hn 4 F3 whole bar, Cl 1/Vla F4 on beats 3–4). At 239 the bass moves to A (vi), which contains E, the 7th's tone of resolution. No part takes F to E. Hn 4 leaps F3→C4, Cl 1 and the violas break off to rest and restart the counter on C5, and E4 arrives in Hn 3 from G3 by a sixth.
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 465: a 7th "cannot resolve within the chord" and "promotes progression to a new chord—one that contains the tone of resolution". PDF 466: "unresolved 7ths occur most infrequently". Unit 21, PDF 368: over a pedal, "dissonances among the upper parts themselves must be prepared and resolved normally" (G is in the cellos above the C pedal, so the F–G 7th is between upper parts). Every other V7 in 187–279 resolves its 7th (Reading notes row 4).
**Fix (sHead.ily):** Hn 4 at 239 E3 instead of C4:
`sHeadHnFour = { e1\p | f1 | f1 | f1 | e1\mf | a1 | f1 | a1 | R1*4 | }` (was `c'1\mf` in bar 5).
C stays in vi through Bn 1 C4 and the counter's C5 on beat 2. Hn 4 E3 to A3 at 240 is a plain fourth, and there are no new fifths or octaves (all-pairs check unchanged).

### 2. 245–246: the horn call's C over V4/3 sounds with the 3rd it replaces (applied)
**Bars:** 245 beat 3 to 246 (end of Q, into R).
**Problem:** Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2 reach the horn call's C (C6, C5, C5, C4) at 245 beat 3 and hold it over V4/3 (D bass, G timpani, F in Cl 1). Following the book, the C is an unresolved 4th that anticipates the tonic of 247. But Bn 1 holds B3, the 3rd, for six beats under it, a semitone below Hn 2's C4. The anticipation and its tone of resolution sound together, which the book calls an unclear sonority. Bn 1 then leaves B3 by leaping to G2 at 247, so the B3 never even resolves.
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 473–474, Ex. 27-16c and 27-17: unresolved 4ths over V7 "sound like anticipations of the following chord tones". PDF 476 and point 11 (PDF 479): such 4ths "replace, rather than resolve to, 3rds". Ex. 27-16c shows V7 with the 4th and no 3rd. PDF 469: a dissonance sounding together with its resolution tone gives "an unclear sonority". The same bars already show the correct form at 254, where B moves to C in every part and no B remains on beats 3–4 (Reading notes row 15).
**Fix (sHead.ily):** `sHeadBsnOne = { R1*4 | c'1\f | c'1 | a1 | d'1 | b2\< c'2 | c'4 b2. | b2 f2 | f1\! \breathe | }` (was `| b1 | b1\! \breathe |`).
Bn 1 keeps B3 while the trumpets hold B (245 beats 1–2) and then takes the 7th F3 under the anticipated C. That F3 falls to the head's E3 at 247 after the G2 eighth. It gives the V4/3's 7th a lower-register voice that resolves: until now only Cl 1 had F4, and it rises to the pickup G4.

### 3. 238: the harp sounds B3 under the tune's C6 appoggiatura (applied)
**Bars:** 238, first half.
**Problem:** The violins' C6 (beats 1–2) resolves to B5 on beat 3 over V7. The harp's first-half arpeggio (G3–B3–D4–G4) already sounds the resolution tone B3 under it, so the 4–3 is blurred.
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 469 and Unit 21 §12: a suspension or appoggiatura is most effective when "the tone of resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass". Rows 10 and 15 of the reading notes.
**Fix (sHead.ily, `sHeadHarpR`, bar 4):** `g8 c'8 d'8 g'8 g'8 d'8 b8 g8` (was `g8 b8 d'8 g'8 …`). The first half doubles the tune's C an octave and a half lower, and B returns on beats 3–4 with the tune. Harp pedals unchanged (C♮).
