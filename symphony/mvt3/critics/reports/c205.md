## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 58, 170, 190, 198: upper-part crotchet rests stand across the top line, far from both parts (applied)

Problem. On shared staves the rest of the resting upper part is drawn two or three spaces above the centre, half outside the stave. This happens even where both parts are low and nothing collides:
- 58.4 Cl 1·2: Cl 1 has D4 and Cl 2 has B♭3-A♭3-F3-A♭3, both below the stave, and Cl 1's rest stands over the top line.
- 170.4 Cl 1·2: Cl 1 A♭4 and Cl 2 F4, both in the lower half; the rest stands over the top line, next to the "morendo".
- 190.4 and 198.4 Bn 1·2: Bn 1's rest stands above the top line while both bassoons sit in the bottom space or below the stave.

The reader's eye has to jump an octave to find where Cl 1 or Bn 1 stops.

Evidence. Gould PDF 50: "upper-part rests are usually placed above the centre stave-line", and they move further out only "should parts otherwise collide". Also PDF 50: "When one part lies outside the stave (on ledger lines), crotchet … rests for the other part may move back to the centre of the stave". PDF 49: rests move "an exact number of stave-spaces". PDF 51: a rest's distance from the stave should be "on the same level as surrounding pitches". In Gould's own example (PDF 50) the upper crotchet rest is one space above the centre.

LilyPond places these rests itself. `Rest.voiced-position` and `Rest.staff-position` have no effect here, because the rest-collision code sets the height afterwards; I tested both. Only `extra-offset` moves them. The fix is score-only (tag 'score), so the single-line parts keep their centred rests.

Fix (applied):
- sOne.ily, Cl 1, bar 58: `d'2. \tag #'score { \once \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . -2) } r4`. This puts the rest in the centre, because both parts are on ledger lines.
- recP.ily, Cl 1, bar 170: `aes'2.\pp^\markup \italic "morendo" \tag #'score { \once \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . -1) } r4`.
- recS.ily, Bn 1, bar 190: `\transpose c aes, { a,2 c4 \tag #'score { \once \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . -1) } r4 }`.
- recS.ily, Bn 1, bar 198: `es2.\f\< \tag #'score { \once \override Rest.extra-offset = #'(0 . -1) } r4`.

I checked the result at 300 dpi. Each rest now sits one space above the centre (two at 58) and touches nothing. Bar 194 is left alone on purpose: Bn 2's E♭3 crosses above Bn 1's G2 there, so the high rest is correct by PDF 51 (a rest displaced by the other part moves further out).

This technique is new to the score. Earlier passes over this reading (c115) checked rest shapes and shared rests, but not where a voiced rest sits relative to the pitches around it.

### 2. 126, Cl 1·2: "1." printed twice (applied)

Problem. Cl 1's entry at 126 shows two "1." labels, one above the other. One comes from the part-combiner. Since `\partCombineAutomatic` at 111 the combiner prints its solo label here. The other is the score-only markup added in c035/c075, from before the combiner did so.

Evidence. Part-combining labels are part of this lens. Gould PDF 43 says marks must never cut through or be stacked on other symbols in a way that confuses. MOLA PDF 8 asks for proofreading for duplicated marks.

Fix (applied, dev.ily): `r2 d''2(\p\< |` (the `-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."` is removed). One "1." remains. The Cl 1 part never carried this markup (tag 'score), so the parts do not change. The bassoon's "1. dolce" at 125, which is a separate markup, is untouched.

### 3. Checked and kept, with reasons from the reading (no change)
- Harp 8 bassa 239-242. At 241-242 the D2-D3 octaves would need only one ledger line at pitch (PDF 46: at least three before a sign). But 239-242 is one f phrase in octaves, and PDF 47-48 says to keep a phrase at one octave. Splitting it at 241 would show the contour wrongly. Keep.
- Harp "loco" after 82 and 242. PDF 48 does not need loco where rests follow and the next notes (123 G2-G3 pattern; 277 C2-C3) differ clearly in pitch. Keep without loco.
- Octave clef for Cb/Cbn (PDF 46). It is allowed in a full score, but the basses change into tenor clef at 27-34, Gould warns that the clef goes unnoticed, and the title block already states the octave. Not adopted.
- Score-only 8va to save space (PDF 47). Not needed: the 16-page layout holds and page 5 has free space. Not adopted, and it would undo c115 at 222-223.
- Brass at pitch with four ledger lines (tuba C1 at 110-113 and 147-150, horn D3). PDF 47 says brass never need octave signs and that players read four ledger lines at a glance. Keep.
