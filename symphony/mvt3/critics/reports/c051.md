## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 212, 214: the violins anticipate theme B and clash with it (applied)

**Problem.** In the one recall of movement II's theme B (Tpt 1 + Ob 1 + Cl 1, flutes an octave above), Vln I and II play a near-copy of the tune that moves a beat early. At 214 beat 3, Vln I is already on F5 while the tune holds G♭5 (a minor second at the same pitch level), and Vln II is on D♭5 against Tpt 2's E♭5. At 212 beat 4, Vln I A♭5 sounds against the tune's passing G♭5. This is the case of Sebesky's Ex. 279 (PDF 211): unison violins "shadow the melody too closely" in near-parallel motion, which "distorts the aural focus". His Ex. 280 (PDF 212) is the fix: the background touches the solo's notes briefly and otherwise does not run against them. The recall is a cyclic moment the listener has to recognise, so the blur matters.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily).** 212 Vln I `bes''2\f aes''4 ges''4` (was `aes''2`). 214 Vln I `ges''2. f''4` (was `ges''2 f''2`) and Vln II `es''2. des''4` (was `es''2 des''2`). The violins now reach the tune's note together with it, and 212→213 and 214→215 move by step. I also tried Vln II E♭5 at 212 beat 4, but it made fifths with Cl 2 (A♭4→G♭4) into 213, so Vln II keeps `des''2` there. The D♭6–C6 at 211 and the 205–223 top line are not touched.

### 2. 266, 268, 270: Tpt 3 hammers E4 a semitone under F1's F4 (applied)

**Problem.** In F1's major apotheosis, Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2, violas and cellos carry `bes'4 a'8 g'8 f'4 e'4`. On beat 3 of the same bar, Tpt 3's pulse strikes an accented E4 against the theme's F4. This is a minor second in the same instrument and the same octave at ff, three times. On beat 4 the theme then moves to the E that Tpt 3 has already played, so the theme's last step sounds pre-empted. Sebesky (PDF 211) says the background must not "interfere in any way with the melodic motion" of the lead. On PDF 219 he says not to put loud brass inside a melody's register. On PDF 213 he calls voice leading the clearest mark of professional craft.

**Fix (finale.ily, `finaleTptThree`).** 266 `f'4-> r8 f'8 f'4-> r4`, 268 `e'4-> r8 e'8 f'4-> r4`, 270 `f'4-> r8 f'8 f'4-> r4` (beat 3 was `e'4->`). Tpt 3 now joins F1's F, F1 moves alone to E on beat 4, and the pulse rhythm is kept. This does not touch c042's decision to keep F1's ♭7 against the motto head's B♮.

### 3. 74 and 194: F2's held note before the consequent stalls; the counter-line should push into it (applied)

**Problem.** F2 has four 4-bar phrases with almost the same rhythm (dotted minim + crotchet, four crotchets, …). In the major statements the violins hold a whole note before the consequent: F5 at 74, D5 at 194. Under that note the cello/viola counter-melody also moves in half notes (`aes2( f2)`, `f2( d2)`), so nothing carries the bar into the consequent. This is the case of Sebesky's Ex. 273/274 (PDF 208): "the cellos change from half note to quarter note values in order to give extra push into the next phrase". It also matches PDF 207 ("enough character to carry one bar into the next") and PDF 222–223 (fill the slack where the lead holds). Sebesky's question 6 (PDF 220) asks how the arrangement can make up for a weak spot in a tune. That is the right approach here, because the solo at 51–62 is protected as plain.

**Fix.** sTwo.ily, `sTwoVc` and `sTwoVla` at 74: `aes4( g4 f4 aes4)` (was `aes2( f2)`). recS.ily, `recSVc` and `recSVla` at 194: `f4( e4 d4 f4)` (was `f2( d2)`). The seventh of V7 falls on beat 4 and resolves by step to the third of I at 75 and 195. The tune is unchanged. The bar-81 `aes2( f2)` is left as it is.
