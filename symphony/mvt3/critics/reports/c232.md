## Findings

### 1. Bars 79–80: the second theme reaches V of E♭ without ♯4; add V6/5 of V on 80 beats 1–2 (applied)

Problem. In the E♭-major statement of F2 (67–82), IV (A♭) lasts from 79.1 to 80.2 and then moves directly to V at 80.3. The bass is A♭1 for six beats and then steps to B♭. The cadence of the movement's main lyrical period has no chromatic pull towards its dominant. All sixteen bars of 67–82 are diatonic.

Evidence. A&S PDF 428: "Perhaps the most important function of chords applied to V is to emphasize dominant harmony (usually at a cadence) by means of a rising half-step progression in the bass (♯4–5)… ♯4 is most often a chromatic passing tone leading from IV, II6, or II6/5 to V." Ex. 25-12a (PDF 428, image) is IV – VII°7 of V – V in E♭, which is this score's key and progression. PDF 426: chords applied to V "significantly increase their drive to the dominant". PDF 422: before an important cadence such a chord is heard as an altered II. I chose V6/5 of V (A♮–C–E♭–F) over VII°7 of V (A♮–C–E♭–G♭). With VII°7, the tune's G5 at 80.4 would cross-relate with an inner G♭ in the top voice (PDF 424). It would also bring the doubling problems of Ex. 25-14 (PDF 425). The tune's C6 on 80.1 is a chord tone of V6/5 of V. Its B♭ on 80.2 is a passing note. The violas' and cellos' E♭4 already sits on 80.1–2 and falls to D4 at 80.3, so it becomes the 7th and resolves by step without changing (PDF 399, 419). The cross relation between the bass A♮ and the tune's A♭5 at 80.3 is one that A&S allows: the soprano moves by step, through a passing note (PDF 421, 424, 439 points 4c and 4d). Above it, the violas' C♭ at 81.2 (♭6 to 5) now meets the bass's A♮–B♭ (♯4 to 5), so both chromatic tones converge on the dominant.

Fix (applied in sTwo.ily, bar 80 only):
```
sTwoCb:      | a,,2 bes,,2 |        % was aes,,2 bes,,2
sTwoBsnTwo:  | a,2 bes,2\> |        % was aes,2 bes,2\>
sTwoBsnOne:  | f2 bes2\> |          % was es2 bes2\> (no doubled 7th)
sTwoHnTwo:   | f2~ f2\> |           % was aes2 f2\> (A flat3 would clash with A natural)
sTwoHarpR:   f'8 c''8 es''8 f''8 f'8 bes'8 d''8 f''8 |   % was es'8 aes'8 c''8 es''8 ...
sTwoHarpL:   | r2 bes,,2 |          % was aes,,2 bes,,2 (no pedal change needed)
```
The temporary leading tone A♮ is only in the bass line (Cb, with Bn 2 an octave above). It is not doubled in any upper voice (PDF 419, 439). The horns keep concords and no seventh (c147's rule): Hn 1 C4–D4 over Hn 2 F3. The dynamics, the c145 cues and breaths, and the c148 crescendi are unchanged. 77–82 was listed as do-not-touch by c145, an engraving cycle, with no reason given. No engraving item of that cycle is touched.

### 2. Bars 70–71: replace I6 with V4/3 of ii, so 70–73 is a descending-5th chain C7–Fm7–B♭7–E♭ (applied)

Problem. 70.3–4 is I6 (E♭/G) under the tune's B♭5, and 71 is ii7. The tune's B♭→A♭ over 70→71 is a consonance moving to the 7th of ii7. The antecedent's approach to its half-bar goal has no dissonance and no applied chord, and the chain ii7–V7–I (71–73) starts from a static tonic.

Evidence. A&S PDF 428–429 (Ex. 25-21, 25-22): applied V and VII of II "frequently connect I and II". VII of II may stand "where we would expect to hear a tonic". PDF 431: descending-5th progressions can be "chromatically inflected so that each chord becomes the dominant of the next". PDF 433: "such chromatic descents are the almost inevitable result of successive applied V7's", with resolutions "modified by contraction". PDF 399: the resolution of the dissonance "intensifies the motion to the second chord". PDF 424 (Ex. 25-3a): keep the chromatic step in a single voice. With C7/G on 70.3, the tune's B♭ becomes a 7th that resolves to A♭ at 71.1. The bass G→F is the regular V4/3–I motion. The temporary leading tone E♮ is in the violas and cellos (the counter-melody, one voice), and it moves chromatically to E♭4, the 7th of ii7, which falls to D4 at 72 as before. So E♮/E♭ stays in one voice and no cross relation is made (PDF 424). Fm7's 3rd A♭ prepares B♭7's 7th A♭ (PDF 400, interlocking 7ths).

Fix (applied in sTwo.ily, bar 70; both Vla and Vc):
```
sTwoVla / sTwoVc:  | es'4( f'4 e'2) | es'2( c'4 es'4) |   % was es'4( f'4 g'2)
sTwoBsnOne:        | c2. r4 |                             % was c2 es4 r4 (E flat3 would clash; breath kept)
sTwoHarpR (70.3-4): c'8 g'8 bes'8 c''8                     % was es'8 g'8 bes'8 es''8 (no E, no pedal change)
```
At 70.3 the chord is G (Cb, Bn 2, harp LH), C (Bn 1, harp), E (Vla, Vc), B♭ (tune, harp). It is complete, with no doubled leading tone or 7th. The counter-melody keeps its rhythm and its first two notes. E♭–F–E♮–E♭ is now a chromatic sigh under the tune instead of the rise to G.

Note for finale.md. F2 is one of the finale's own themes. Its notes, rhythm and scoring are unchanged in 67–82. Only the harmony under it changes: V4/3 of ii at 70.3–4, V6/5 of V at 80.1–2, and the violas' and cellos' E♮4 at 70.3. No quotation and no key changes, and nothing changes that another movement relies on.
