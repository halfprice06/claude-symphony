## Findings

### 1. Bar 38: the medial half cadence has no accented 6/4 (applied)

**Problem.** The exposition reaches its half cadence on B♭ (V of E♭) at 39-42 straight from F minor (38.1-2). Beat 3 of bar 38 sets the melody's E♭5 over D in five voices (Bn 1, Hn 3, Tbn 1, Cl 1, violas and cellos), so the sound is [B♭ D E♭ F]. The E♭ is an unprepared upper neighbour over a root-position V that has already arrived. The cadence comes half a bar early, and the long held V at 39-42 does not sound like a goal. The whole exposition (1-98) has no cadential 6/4. The first one comes at 151, the start of the recapitulation, so the device the recapitulation relies on is never set up.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 306-307: an accented 6/4 delays the expected V and resolves over a stationary bass from strong to weak. Bar 38 has the stationary bass (Cb and Bn 2 on B♭ from 38.3), and the melody's E♭5-D5 is already the 4-3 the 6/4 needs. Only the inner voices are missing.
- PDF 314: the Unit 10 rules on doubling and voice leading still apply in instrumental textures.
- PDF 316-317: when the 4th is doubled, one 4th moves up while the other moves down. Here the melody's E♭ falls to D, so Cl 1 holds its E♭ and rises to F.
- PDF 259: a half cadence needs the major V with its leading tone, not minor v. The resolution keeps D natural.
- I take the 6th from mixture: G♭, not G, because the melody has G♭5 at 38.25. G natural would make a cross-relation. The minor-mode 6/4 B♭-E♭-G♭ also prepares the E♭ minor of the trumpet solo at 51 and the G♭/C♭ colour of 47-48.

**Fix** (sections/pTwo.ily, bar 38 = 12th bar of each variable):

```
pTwoHnOne:   f'1 \breathe              ->  f'2 ges'4 f'4 \breathe
pTwoBsnOne:  c'2 d'2                   ->  c'2 bes4 d'4
pTwoHnThree: c'2 d'2                   ->  c'2 bes4 d'4
pTwoTbnOne:  c2 d2                     ->  c2 bes,4 d4
pTwoClOne:   es'2 d'2                  ->  es'2. f'4~      (bar 39 f'1~ unchanged)
pTwoVla:     f8( d'8 f'8 d'8)          ->  ges8( es'8 f'8 d'8)   (2nd half of the bar)
pTwoVc:      f8( d'8 f'8 bes8)         ->  ges8( es'8 f'8 bes8)  (2nd half of the bar)
```

Beat 3 now sounds [B♭ E♭ G♭] and beat 4 [B♭ D F]: a cadential 6/4 in minor over the B♭ bass, resolving to V. The 6th falls (G♭4 to F4 in Hn 1; G♭3 to F3 in Vla and Vc). The doubled 4th splits, with the melody falling to D and Cl 1 rising to F. The bass voices B♭ in Bn 1, Hn 3 and Tbn 1 move up to D, giving a doubled root on beat 3 and a complete V on beat 4.

F1's melody (Vln I-II, Fl 1, all of 35-38) is not changed. Every changed part is accompaniment. The protected Fl 1 A♭5 at 39-42 and the bass trombone B♭ at 41-42 are not touched. No new dynamics are added, so the part-combined horn staff keeps equal dynamics.

### 2. Proportions and coda build: follows the reading, no change

**Assessment.**
- The coda is 31% of the duration, against 18% for the development and retransition.
- That would be heavy for a Classical finale. It is normal for a finale that has to reach C major from C minor, as Dvořák 9/iv does with its long major coda.
- The four 12-bar stages (199-210, 211-222, 223-234, 235-246) are equal in length. They are not equal in pace:
  - Largamente 72 at 211;
  - Maestoso 56 at 223;
  - Poco più mosso 66 at 235, with stringendo 243-246;
  - Più allegro 88 at 247.
- The tension curve falls once at the arrival, as it should, and then rises without a break to 247.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 252 says a long bass descent is divided into stages. The coda's bass, C (199), A♭ (211-220), the stop chord (222) and C (223), is divided that way. The cadential 6/4 is held off at 243-244 and resolves into 247, which is the cadenza principle of PDF 317-319.

**Decision.** Keep it. The slow/fast balance was settled twice (c043, c063), and I find no reason to reopen it.

### 3. Coda tonic pedals: the same neighbouring IV6/4 six times (not applied)

**Problem.** Every stage over the C pedal decorates the tonic in the same way, with the neighbouring IV6/4 (iv6/4 in 231 and 233) at 231, 233, 236, 268, 272 and 274. That is a correct neighbouring 6/4: it is unaccented, doubles the bass and is the sustained-bass form of I-IV-I (A&S PDF 309). But it is the only prolongation the coda uses over the pedal. A&S PDF 314-315 gives the other sustained-bass pattern, 8-7-6-5 over 6-5-4-3, which would vary the last pedal.

**Possible fix.** In the upper strings at 272, replace the IV6/4 with c'''4 bes''4 a''4 g''4 over e''4 d''4 c''4 bes'4 above the C pedal.

**Decision.** Not applied. Bars 263-279 are protected (the c198 C pedal, and the horn and trombone rests at 265-272). The repeated IV6/4 there also works as an amen-type plagal colour after the chorale, so the reason for the protection still holds. I record the weakness but would not change it.

### 4. Recapitulation and retransition: follows

**Assessment.**
- The retransition (135-150) prolongs G with an upper-fifth chord and stepwise bass motion, as on A&S PDF 253 and 257.
- The recapitulation opens with the accented 6/4 at 151, as on PDF 306-307. This is the only point where the form depends on a 6/4, and with finding 1 the exposition now prepares it.
- At 166, V-i6 sends the resolution into the bass, which A&S PDF 315-316 calls too weak for a strong V-I. Here that is the purpose: the cyclic rule saves root-position V-I until 261-263.
- The descending-fifths link at 167-171 begins and ends on chords with weight, as on PDF 249.

**Decision.** No change.
