## What else I studied

I rendered all 90 pages (PDF 21–110) and viewed them as two-page images, with the text dumps for the prose. I read c086, c176 and c266, the earlier passes on this chapter, to avoid repeating their fixes. I built a per-player MIDI (one staff per wind and brass variable) and a custom LilyPond engraver that logs every breath mark by staff and moment; from those I measured unbroken playing chains for each wind player, counting breaths and rests (longest about 20 s, Cl 1 181–195, with eighth rests inside). I checked flute/oboe crossings (only 36 and 93, passing), the Tpt 1 load from 199 to the end (rests at 205–210, 229, 235–242, 251–254, 269–270), and grepped every horn variable for short upbeats, staccato eighths and syncopations against the other brass. The only horn `r8 x8` figures in the score are Hn 3·4 at 91–92.

## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bars 91–92: Hn 3·4 drop the ff eighth upbeats

Problem. In the closing-group codetta at 91–92 the trumpets, trombones, bass trombone, tuba and timpani hammer the figure `x4-> r8 x8 x4-> r4` at ff. Hn 3·4 double Tpt 1·2 an octave lower and play the same eighth upbeat. Hn 1·2 hold E♭ through the bars and are not affected.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 32: the horn "speaks late" and has trouble with the other brass in staccato or syncopated passages. PDF 46, Ex. 51: the horns leave out the fast upbeat notes so that they land squarely on the next downbeat while the trumpets and trombones carry the pickup. Fn 1 on PDF 32 gives the balance reason: at mf and above a trumpet or trombone has about twice a horn's power, so the eighth is carried fully by three trumpets and three trombones and is lost from the horns only as a slightly late, smeared attack. The score already follows this rule everywhere else: 199–204 (horns sustain), 269 and 271 (c086), 277 (c266). Bars 91–92 are the one exception.

Fix (in /tmp/critic-356/work/sections/clos.ily):

```
closHnThree = { ... | bes4->\ff r4 bes4-> r4 | c'4-> r4 bes4-> r4 | r4 bes2-> bes4-> | ... }
closHnFour  = { ... | g4->\ff r4 f4-> r4 | fis4-> r4 f4-> r4 | r4 g2-> f4-> | ... }
```

with a source comment citing c356 and Sebesky PDF 32, 46. Bar 93 (`r4 x2-> x4->`) stays: it is a held half-note on beat 2, which a horn plays cleanly, and it matches the trumpets. Pitch content is unchanged (the dropped eighths are B♭/G, already in the trumpets an octave higher). analysis.txt changes only in attack counts (Hn 3·4 357 → 353; bars 89–92 231 → 227); par8.py over 89–94 is identical to v356. No theme or quotation changes.
