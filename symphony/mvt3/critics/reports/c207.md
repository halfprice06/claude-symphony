## Findings

### 1. 187-194: F2's major statement is the only cantabile major-theme passage with no background layer (applied)

Bars: 187-194 (letter M, A♭ major, mf).

Problem: Adler's first principle for string scoring (PDF 132) is that a passage is heard as foreground, middleground and background, and his main example (PDF 132-135, Brahms 3/iii) shows the background as a soft, simple, repeated figure in a pale register, in a rhythm unlike the tune's, which "adds intensity to the longer notes of the melody" and is "dovetailed to create extreme smoothness without any pauses in the texture". At 187-194 the score has the foreground (Vln I+II in unison, with the violas and Hn 1 an octave lower), a middleground (the cellos' counter-melody) and the bass (basses, bassoons in octaves). It has no background. The harmony is carried by three lines only, and the tune's long notes (dotted halves at 187 and 191, the half notes of 190 and 193, the whole note at 194) stand over nothing that moves except the cello line. Each of the movement's other cantabile statements in major has a background figure: 67-74 (harp), 123-130 (harp), 235-242 (harp, horns). On page 10 the passage looks like a string quartet with two bassoons, after 16 bars (171-186) that were already reduced to violas, cellos, basses and a wind pad. Earlier critics thinned 187-198 for reasons that still hold: c057 removed a clarinet pad that held the same notes as 51-58 and crowded a middle already filled by the harp and Vln II's lower octave; c063 silenced the harp so 187-198 would not copy 67-78 and the harp's eighths would be fresh at 235. Neither reason applies to a new figure in another colour, since the harp and Vln II's lower octave are no longer here and the harp stays silent.

Evidence: Adler PDF 132 (three layers), PDF 135 (background figure: pale register, contrasting rhythm, repetition, dovetailing), PDF 163, Ex. V-39 version 3 (one part sustains the low note of the broken chord while the other plays the figure, so it sounds like a held pedal), PDF 158-159, Ex. V-34 (sustained harmony notes plus an arpeggiated figure), PDF 136-137, Ex. V-13 (Mozart stops the accompaniment figure so the next phrase sounds changed). The clarinets take it because they rest from 187 to 198 and are a colour not yet used in this statement.

Fix (applied, recS.ily; a c207 comment above recSClOne gives the reason):
- `recSClOne`, bars 187-194 (replacing the first 8 bars of `R1*12`):
  `\partCombineApart r8 aes8(-\tag #'part \pp c'8 aes8) r8 aes8( c'8 aes8) | r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) r8 aes8( c'8 aes8) | r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) | r8 des'8( f'8 des'8) r8 aes8( es'8 aes8) | r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) | r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) r8 bes8( des'8 bes8) | r8 aes8( c'8 aes8) r8 aes8( c'8 aes8) | r8 bes8( es'8 bes8) r8 bes8( es'8 bes8) \partCombineAutomatic | R1*4 |`
- `recSClTwo`, bars 187-194: `es1\pp~ | es1 | f1 | aes2 es2 | f1 | es1~ | es2 f2 | g1 | R1*4 |`

What it does: Cl 2 holds the chord's low tone (E♭3-F3-A♭3, a held pedal in Adler's sense), Cl 1 plays a rocking three-eighth figure after an eighth rest (A♭3-F4), so between them an eighth-note motion runs under the tune with no gap. Both are pp in the chalumeau, below the violins and mostly below the violas' lower octave, in a rhythm unlike the tune's dotted halves and quarters. Harmony followed bar by bar: I | V6/5-I | ii6 | IV-I6 | ii7 | V6/5 | I-vi | V-V4/2. The seventh D♭4 at 188 and 192 resolves to C4 in Cl 1. I checked every move of the clarinets against the bass for octaves and fifths. While choosing the notes I found three places where the obvious chord tone would have made octaves with the bass (A♭-G at 187→188, D♭-C at 190, B♭-G at 191→192), so the figure takes B♭3 at 188 and 192 and E♭4 on 190 beats 3-4. The figure stops at 195, where the head returns with Hn 1-2, then Ob 1 and Fl 1. So the change of texture marks the restatement (Ex. V-13), and the crescendo by addition into 199 stays as it was. Cl 1's pp is in the part only, since on the shared score staff Cl 2's pp on the downbeat covers both (equal dynamics). The voices are kept apart on the staff so the combiner prints no false "Solo"/"2." labels. The Cl 1 and Cl 2 parts compile cleanly, and the Cl 1 part shows the pp at 187.

Does it undo an earlier fix? It does not undo c057 (no held pad doubling 171-178; the middle at 187 is now empty, not crowded), c058 (clarinets still rest at 195-198, where they would double the horns), c063 (the harp still rests 187-198 and enters fresh at 235) or c074/c121 (strings unchanged). F1, F2 and the chorale top line are unchanged, and so is every quotation. This is a scoring change inside the finale only, so no other movement thread needs to be told.

### 2. 235-238: the S head already follows the Brahms 3 model (no change)

Foreground: violins in unison sul A, E5-C6, the instrument's best singing string. Middleground: Cl 1 + violas on the chromatic counter, in their own rhythm. Background: harp arpeggios, Hn 3-4 and the C pedal, pp to p. This is Adler PDF 132-135 point by point. Nothing to add.

### 3. 27-38: bass statement and countersubject follow the contrapuntal rules (no change)

At 35-38 the tune (Vln I+II, Fl 1) and the eighth-note countersubject (Vla+Vc) are an octave or more apart and rhythmically opposite (half and dotted notes against eighths), over a held wind and brass chord. These are Adler PDF 144's rules 1, 3 and 4. Protected since c007/c191.

### 4. 139-146: stretto entries alternate register and colour (no change)

Hn 1 (G4), Vc (G3), Ob 1+Cl 1 (G5), Vln II (G4), Hn 1-2+Vln I, then the tutti unison at 145 and the V7 strokes. Each entry is a bar after the last and in a new colour, then the passage ends in a homophonic climax. This matches Adler PDF 144-145 (Vivaldi, Bach) and PDF 147 (Weber: counterpoint, then homophony). Protected.

### 5. 226-230: the canon is separated from the chorale by register and by two bars (no change)

The canon in violins and upper winds (G5 at 226) lies above the chorale's top line (Tpt 1 D5), and the chorale moves in whole notes while the canon's attack falls two bars later. This matches Adler PDF 144 rule 3 and PDF 148 (canonic imitation builds tension until a new event, here the timpani pulse at 229). Protected.

### 6. 255-262: background under the last chorale is idiomatic (no change)

Vln II's arpeggios (with Cl 1 from 257), the violas' repeated quarters and the basses' eighths under held brass follow Adler PDF 159 (Ex. V-34) and PDF 165-166 (repeated notes at speed). The arpeggio is not dovetailed, but under ff brass a dovetail would not be heard. Kept.
