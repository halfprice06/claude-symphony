## Findings

### 1. Bars 187–198: the C-major second theme spends the arrival's C major before 223 — applied

**Problem.** After the deceptive cadence at 186 (V–VI), F2 is restated for 12 bars in C major (violins mf, horn, cellos and violas). This is the only C-major key area before the arrival. The movement's own rules treat C major as the arrival's property: "no full-brass C-major triad before 223" (c060), and finale.md asks the other movements for "no C major before the finale's arrival (223)". At 187 the violas and cellos also open the counter-melody on E3 (E♮ as the third of a C-major tonic), 32 bars before the violas close the E♭/E♮ thread at 219–220. It is also the third time F2 turns from minor to major (67–82, 115–130, 171–198), the reason the recapitulated second group sounds like a repeat (cycle 43 noted this but left it, because the passages were protected; 187–198 is not protected).

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 95, Ex. 6-4: a tonic's meaning comes from its function, as opening tonic or closing tonic (goal). The cyclic rule leaves the C tonic at 187 unable to cadence (it ends with the contrapuntal V6/5–I of 196 and a turn to A♭, PDF 129). So it sounds as C major without the goal function that 223 and 263 depend on. The deceptive cadence already opens A♭ at 186, and in A♭ the statement can close normally.

**Fix (applied).** In recS.ily, bars 187–197 of every part are wrapped in `\transpose c aes, { … }` (down a major third). Bar 198, an A♭ chord with E♭6 on top, stays as written and is now the statement's own tonic, reached plagally (197 I6–IV, 198 I) and still VI → i into 199. Range fixes: Cb 190 `des,2 c,2`, 193 `aes,,2 f,2`, 194 `es,1`; violas 194 `des4( c4 des2)`. In global.ily, 187 is `\key aes \major`. The cost is that A♭ now leads into the coda's A♭7 build (205–210), but that A♭ has a new function there (Ger6 of C, then D♭).
**This changes the key of one of the finale's own themes:** F2's major statement in the recapitulation is in A♭, no longer C. finale.md ("F2 … C major at 187", key plan "C major (187–198)") and the plan table need updating. No quotation is affected (36/36).

### 2. Bars 138–150: the retransition's dominant never stands on its root — applied at 147–150

**Problem.** From the stop chord (138) to the reprise, the bass holds B for 13 bars, including the ff dominant at 147–150 (25 voices, the loudest point before the coda). The root G first sounds under the reprise at 151. The structural dominant of the whole movement therefore rests on its leading tone, and the recapitulation's G pedal (151–153) arrives by a leap B→G instead of continuing a pedal.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 112: brief 6/3 expansions are common, "however, for large-scale expansions where the chord becomes the basis of an entire section (like the V in Example 6-1, bars 17–27), the stable sonority of the root position is necessary". PDF 95, Ex. 6-4: the expanded V shapes the form. PDF 129–130 (Jupiter I): "I" chords inside an expanded V act as neighbour chords, which is what the i6/4 at 151 does once G is already in the bass. Mozart 41/iv also puts root-position V7 under the two bars before its reprise (harmony.csv, onsets 888–896).

**Fix (applied).** In retr.ily, B stays in the bass 138–146 (stop-chord layout as in movement I bar 26; stretto 139–146 untouched). At 147–150 the bass moves to G: Cb, Cbn, Tuba `g,,1->\ff | g,,1 | g,,1~ | g,,2. d,4`, B.cl and Bn 2 `g,1…`, Vc `<g, f>1 | <g, g>1 | <g, f>1~ | <g, f>2. <d f>4`. B remains in Ob 1, Tpt 2 and the violins, so the V7 is complete. The bass is then G for 147–153. The tonic root still arrives only at 155 (V6/5–i), so the cadence rule is kept. This revises the plan's "V6/5 ff at 147–150" and does not touch any protected item: the retransition still enters the reprise over the dominant (c053), and the doubled leading tone that c052 removed stays removed.

### 3. Bars 169–171: the recapitulated second theme replays the exposition's pacing — applied

**Problem.** The recapitulated second group at 171 brakes in the same way as 51: a poco rit. (169–170), then Poco meno mosso 2 = 72, for the same 16-bar tune with the same harmony a third lower. Together with 51–82 and 211–246, this puts about 42% of an Allegro con fuoco movement below Tempo I.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 102 and 115: chord change and the rhythm of chord succession define the rhythmic impression. At 171–186 the chords and phrase lengths repeat 51–66 exactly, so only tempo and colour can make the second hearing different. The colour was changed in c063, but the tempo was not. Dvořák 9/iv and 7/iv keep their second themes at tempo (see above).

**Fix (applied).** In global.ily: no poco rit. at 169–170 and no tempo change at 171 (the muted trumpet and cor anglais keep "dolce cantabile"). The poco rit. at 197–198 now steps `\mt 160`, `\mt 150` from Tempo I, and "Tempo I" at 199 stands as before. The recapitulated second group shortens from 47.0 s to 40.2 s, and the whole movement from 424 s to 417 s (6:57; the minimum is 6 minutes).

- **Balance of sections.** After these changes: exposition 35%, development and retransition 18%, recapitulation 16%, coda 30%. The development is shorter than Dvořák's 20%. Cycles 43 and 63 kept it because its passages are protected, and I do not re-raise it. Finding 2 gives the development's end a firmer dominant instead.
- **The coda's stages.** At the large span (A&S PDF 95), 199–223 is one cadential progression: i – A♭7 (Ger6) – N (D♭) – Ger6 – cadential 6/4 – V7 – stop chord – I. The stages build. 223–246 is an expanded closing tonic over a C pedal (PDF 93–94). Its one dip (233–240, about 12 voices) was kept twice as deliberate, and I leave it.
- **The Largamente close.** It follows the only perfect authentic cadence (263) and expands the closing tonic. A&S PDF 94 (cadences broaden their values) supports it, and the ending is earned. With Finding 1, C major is now first heard as a key at 219–223, which strengthens the arrival the close completes.
- **263 voice leading** (reading note 11): not changed, because A&S limit this rule to transparent textures (PDF 101).
