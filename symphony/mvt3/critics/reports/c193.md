## Findings

None of these calls for a note change. I list the four points I weighed, in order of how much each would matter if it were wrong, with my decision on each.

### 1. The slow stretch 211–246 (keep)
- **Bars:** 211–246.
- **Concern:** After 199–210 (16.6 s at 2 = 84–92), three slower stages follow in a row: Largamente 2 = 72 at 211, Maestoso 2 = 56 at 223, and Poco più mosso 2 = 66 at 235. Together they last 67 s, 16% of the movement, before Più allegro at 247.
- **Evidence for keeping it:**
  - A&S PDF 456 §15: the move to I is made convincing by the predominant and the dominant seventh before it. Here that preparation (219–222) and the arrival itself (223) need the broad tempo.
  - The stretch builds one harmonic arc, VI – ♭II – Ger6 – V – I (A&S PDF 454, Beethoven's I – VI – IV–V – I), followed by the I – III♯ – V – I of 241–247 (PDF 450, Ex. 26-9). Neither is a static area.
  - The density profile does not sag. The average voice count is 23–26 at 209–224 and falls to 12.5 only at 237–240. That is the dip c033 kept on the Dvořák 9/iv model, and the stringendo 243–246 lifts out of it.
  - c043 raised the slow-to-fast ratio of the coda, and it was kept because the passages are protected.
- **Decision:** keep. A faster tempo at 235 would be a marginal change inside the do-not-touch zone of c183/c190.

### 2. F2 never sounds in C major (keep)
- **Bars:** 171–198.
- **Concern:** The recapitulated second theme is in C minor (171–186), and its major statement is in A♭ (187–198), not in the tonic. That departs from the textbook resolution of S (A&S PDF 449: III in the exposition).
- **Evidence for keeping it:**
  - c073 moved the major statement to A♭ so that C major is heard first at 223. This report's rule 13 shows the A♭ is structural: it is the VI of the plan VI – ♭II – V – I, which closes at 223 (PDF 452–454, Ex. 26-14 Mozart K. 488: tonicized VI leads on to the predominant).
  - The C-major resolution of second-theme material is given to movement I's S head (235–238, 247–254), an owed item.
- **Decision:** keep. Do not move 187 to C major. That would undo c073 and spend the arrival.

### 3. The recapitulation begins over the dominant (keep)
- **Bars:** 151–155.
- **Concern:** A&S PDF 445 asks for an arrival that reaches 1 with rhythmic resolution. At 151, F1's C6 enters over i6/4 on a G pedal, and the tonic root comes only at 155.
- **Evidence for keeping it:**
  - The top line does reach 1 on the downbeat.
  - The bass B♮ under V6/5 (147–150) has already supplied the leading tone that PDF 449 requires before the reprise.
  - The real tonic arrival in this movement is meant to be 223. A dominant-based return keeps 151 from competing with it.
  - The passage is protected since c133.
- **Decision:** keep.

### 4. No rehearsal letter at 265 (keep)
- **Bars:** 255–273.
- **Concern:** Letters S (255) and T (273) are 18 bars apart, and the F1-in-C-major stage begins at 265 without a letter.
- **Evidence for keeping it:** 265 continues the Più allegro and the C pedal straight on from the cadence at 263. Letters average 12–18 bars elsewhere (L–M is 16, Q–R 12). A letter there would be engraving only, and it would rename T in all 30 parts at the last cycle.
- **Decision:** keep, as a marginal change.
