## Findings

### 1. 165–166: the recapitulated F1 ends with the exposition's deceptive cadence, whose purpose is gone (applied)

**Problem.** At 26 the consequent's deceptive cadence V→VI (A♭) keeps the music moving into P2, the counterstatement in the bass. The recapitulation leaves P2 out, but 166 still lands on the same ff A♭ chord. That A♭ then just moves on (167 VI – 168 iiø6/5 – 169–170 V6/5). As a result:

- The recapitulation's first group never closes on its tonic. Root-position C minor appears at 155, 157 and 163 only inside the phrase, and the 16 bars end on VI exactly as at 26. This is one reason 151–166 sounds like a repeat and not like a reprise.
- The recapitulation has two deceptive cadences onto the same A♭ chord, 20 bars apart (166 and 186). The one at 186 does the structural work: it opens the A♭-major statement and the protected 187–223 path (♭VI–♭II–Ger6–V–I). The A♭ at 166 takes away its surprise. Cycle 103 removed a third deceptive cadence at 183 for this reason; 166 is the remaining duplicate.

**Evidence from the reading.**

- PDF 250: in a chain, the chords that end a motion carry the weight (full voicing, low register), and the others connect. At 166 the ff weight and the bass register sit on VI, a connecting chord.
- PDF 249–250: VI–II–V is a goal-directed segment of the descending-fifths chain. That is exactly the link 167–170, so VI belongs at the head of the link (167) and not at the end of the theme.
- PDF 259: deceptive and half cadences depend on the expectation of I. The expectation is only spent well once in each statement.
- Corpus: Dvořák 9/iv changes its first group the most in the recapitulation (50 bars to 10). Here the bar count is fixed by the protected numbering, so the ending is the place to change.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-133/work/sections/recP.ily).** The tune, its articulation and bowing, and the 4–3 at 165 are unchanged; only the harmony under its last note changes. 166 becomes V–i6. The bass falls G→E♭, so there is no root-position V–i in C and the cyclic rule holds. The link 167–170 then reads i6 – VI – iiø6/5 – V6/5, the descending-fifths segment of PDF 249–250.

- `recPVc`, `recPBassTbn`, `recPBsnOne`, `recPBsnTwo`: bar 16 `aes,2-> r2` → `es,2-> r2`
- `recPCbsn`, `recPTuba`: `aes,,2-> r2` → `es,,2-> r2`
- `recPCb`: `aes,,2-> r2` → `es,2-> r2`. E♭1 lies below the basses' E string, and this score uses no extension; the 16-foot E♭1 is in the tuba and contrabassoon.
- `recPTimp`: `aes,4-> r4 r2` → `es4-> r4 r2` (the E♭ drum, already tuned)
- `recPClOne`: `es'8( aes' c'' aes') r2` → `es'8( g' c'' g') r2`; `recPClTwo`: `aes8( c' es' c') r2` → `g8( c' es' c') r2`
- `recPTptOne`: `r4 es'4-. r2` → `r4 g'4-. r2`; `recPHnFour`: `r4 es4-. r2` → `r4 g4-. r2`. These keep the fifth G and avoid octaves with the bass's falling third G→E♭ (PDF 254).
- The header comments in recP.ily are updated (c133 comment).

**Checks.** The bar now sounds E♭1: C E♭ G. The p5.py output is identical to v133. allpairs_par.py shows no new pairs; only the Cb/Vc and Cb/B.tbn octave doublings at 166 drop out. The outer-voice parallels stay at 0.
