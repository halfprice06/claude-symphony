## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hn 1 tenth leaps into held high notes, 140-141 and 206-207 (applied)

**Problem.** At 140 beat 4 Hn 1 plays B3 (written F#4), staccato. At 141 beat 1 it attacks D5 (written A5) p and holds it for two bars. At 206 beat 4 Hn 1 and Hn 3 play C4, then at 207 Hn 1 jumps to Eb5 mf and Hn 3 to C5. Each Hn 1 move is a tenth into the high register with no time between, and the 141 attack is soft.

**Evidence.** Adler says horn players find wide leaps difficult because each note must be heard and found (PDF 304). He also says soft high attacks are the hardest thing to control (PDF 284), and that the high horn register is taxing and "periodic rests are most welcome" (PDF 303). These are the only two such leaps in the horn parts.

**Fix (applied).**
- retr.ily: Hn 1 141 is now `\partCombineApart r4 d''2. | \partCombineAutomatic d''1\<`. Hn 2 at 141 carries the matching `\partCombineApart b'1\p | \partCombineAutomatic b'1\<`. Hn 2 holds B4 on the downbeat, and Hn 1 enters on beat 2 with a beat to set the lip. Without the apart switch the combiner printed "2." over both notes; the score now shows two voices.
- codaOne.ily: `des'4-.) c'4-. | es''1\mf` becomes `des'4-.) r4 |` in Hn 1, and the same in Hn 3. Hn 2 and Hn 4 still play the C4 at 206.4, so F1's head is complete and unchanged.

### 2. Hn 3 and Hn 4 endurance 199-264: rest at 215-218 (applied)

**Problem.** In v136 Hn 4 plays from 199 to 264 with one bar's rest (229), about 108 seconds of loud playing through the fff arrival. Hn 3 plays 199-243 without a rest, about 79 seconds. The trombones and tuba get rests at 204, 210 and 229 in the same span. At 215-218 Hn 3 doubles Tbn 1 in unison and Hn 4 doubles Bn 1, so neither line is needed there.

**Evidence.** Brass need frequent rests to recover the lips (adler1 PDF 292). The high horn register is taxing and "periodic rests are most welcome" (PDF 303). This span ends in the fff arrival 222-223 and the chorale, where the horns most need fresh lips.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily: in Hn 3, `\tag #'score { f2~ f2 } \tag #'part { f1 } | f1 | f1~\> | f1\mp \breathe |` becomes `R1*4 |`. In Hn 4, `\tag #'score { aes2~ aes2 } \tag #'part { aes1 } | aes1 | aes1~\> | aes1\mp \breathe |` becomes `R1*4 |`. Both re-enter p at 219 as before. Tbn 1 and Bn 1 still carry those notes, so the harmony at 215-218 is unchanged. The 187-223 path keeps all its pitches.

### 3. Brass breaths before the ff at 147 and after the fff at 223 (applied, notation only)

**Problem.** At 143-146 the brass hold whole notes with a crescendo and go straight into the ff V6/5 at 147 and the trumpet strokes 147-150. Nothing in the parts shows where to breathe. At 223 the brass hold an fff chord and then play the f chorale from 224 with no breath marked. The contrabassoon already has a breath before 147, and the bass trombone and tuba already breathe after 225.

**Evidence.** In a loud slow passage do not give too many notes to one breath, and a slurred phrase takes one breath (adler1 PDF 292, 309-310). The trumpet is fast to speak (PDF 320-321), but horns and trombones need the breath marked to take it together.

**Fix (applied).** retr.ily: `\breathe` at the end of 146 in Hn 1-4, Tbn 1-2 and B.tbn. arrival.ily: `\breathe` after the 223 chord in Tpt 1-2, Hn 1-4 and Tbn 1-2 (for example `c''1\fff \breathe | g'1\f`). The strokes 147-150 and 222→223 are on the do-not-touch list because of their notes, harmony and timing. This change does not alter a pitch, rhythm or dynamic, and the bar still lasts a full whole note in the MIDI. So the protection reason is respected.

### 4. Cl 2 part, page 2 over-full (applied, parts only)

**Problem.** c135 left this open. In the parts book the Cl 2 part's page 2 from about 135 is squeezed to fit, with systems packed tightly.

**Evidence.** This is a parts-layout issue, not an Adler point. A part must be readable at the stand.

**Fix (applied).** parts-book.ly, Cl 2 book: `\paper { max-systems-per-page = 10 }` after `\bookOutputName "part-clTwo"`. In my test build (pt136b.ly) the part has 3 pages: 10 systems on page 2, 4 on page 3, no over-full warning, and the page turns stay in rests. The score is not affected.

### 5. Tpt 1 at 243-250: C6 load (no change)

Tpt 1 plays C6 at 243, 245 and 250, with B5 and A5 around them, at f-ff. Adler gives C6 as the C-trumpet top and calls notes above it risky (PDF 317). He also says loud high notes are easier than soft high ones (PDF 319). The passage sits at the limit, but never goes above it, is loud, and lies inside the protected combination 247-254. It follows. No change.

### 6. Focus passages that follow (no change)

- **Hn 1-2 at 165-166.** Eb4-D4-C4-B3-C4 concert (written Bb4-G4 range), middle register, step motion, f at 167 on C5. Middle register is the horn's most secure (PDF 303 chart). Follows.
- **Trumpet strokes 147-150.** The trumpets come in fresh after 33 bars' rest on D5/F5/G5 ff, which is the "clear bright" register (PDF 318), with a breath now marked before them (Finding 3). Follows.
- **Tpt 1-2 on the chorale top line 255-264.** Whole and half notes G4-E5, a breath after 258, then about 7.8 seconds of tongued notes to 264. Middle register, any dynamic works (PDF 319). Follows.
- **F1 at 265-268 and the close 271-279.** F1 enters after one beat's rest. The close has A5 at ff with a breath after 272. That is loud high, the easier case (PDF 319). Follows.
- **Mutes 167-199.** metti sord., con sord., togli sord. and senza sord. are all printed in score and parts with enough rest (PDF 293-295, 307-308). Follows.
