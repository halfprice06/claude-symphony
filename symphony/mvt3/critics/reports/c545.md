## Findings

### 1. Basses' bass clef at 35 printed after the barline (applied)
- **Bars:** 35 (double basses). The same fault hit the bassoon clef when I first moved it to 35 (finding 2).
- **Problem:** Gould PDF 22 says the clef always goes before the barline, even when rests come before the entry. The only exceptions are cue clefs and changes after repeats. At 35, Fl 1 and Vln I·II begin with three grace sixteenths (A♭ head, c209). LilyPond prints the barline at the grace moment but sets a clef written on the main beat after the grace column, so the basses' return to bass clef has stood just after the 34|35 barline since c016. In every other bar the score's clefs come before the barline (the c365 Bn clef at 33, Hn 1·2 at 25). Bar 35 is the only bar that combines a clef change with grace notes at its start, apart from 110, which has no clef change.
- **Evidence:** v545 page 2, double-bass staff at 35, viewed at 300 dpi (the F clef sits right of the barline, over the A♭ whole note). The diagnostic log lists bars 35 and 110 as the only bars with grace notes at their start.
- **Fix (pTwo.ily, pTwoCb):** put the clef at the grace moment with spacer graces:
  `… g2.-> r4 | \grace { \clef bass s16 s16 s16 } aes,,1 | …` (was `\clef bass aes,,1`). The scratch build prints the clef before the barline. Spacers only, so the MIDI is byte-identical.

### 2. Bassoons' tenor clef moved from 33 to 35 (applied)
- **Bars:** 33–35 (Bn 1·2).
- **Problem:** Gould PDF 21 says performance material should stay in one clef as long as practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines, because a clef change hides the contour. PDF 22 says that in continuous playing the clef goes between phrases. Bars 33–34 are bars 7–8 of the F1 statement 27–34, which both bassoons play a2 with the cellos (the cadence bar `f4( aes8 c'8 f'4 aes'4) | g'2.-> r4`, as at 17–18). c365 put the tenor clef at 33 as "the phrase start", but that is not a phrase start. The clef broke the theme's last rising arpeggio just before its peak, in the score and in the Bn 1 part. In bass clef, 33–34 need at most three ledger lines (A♭4 at 33.4, G4 at 34). The notes that need tenor clef, the eighths up to B♭4 (four ledger lines), begin at 35, after both players' rest at 34.4. That is where Gould places a clef: after rests, between phrases. c365's reason for the tenor clef (the eighths at 35–38) still holds, and the clef now arrives exactly where those eighths begin. This answers c365's reason; it does not reverse it.
- **Evidence:** pTwo.ily lines for pTwoBsnOne; v545 page 2 at 33 (tenor clef inside the a2 theme). The scratch page 2 shows 33–34 in bass clef with A♭4 on three ledger lines, matching the bassoons' own 27–32, and the tenor clef before the 35 barline.
- **Fix (pTwo.ily, pTwoBsnOne):** `… g4-. | f4( aes8 c'8 f'4 aes'4) | g'2.-> r4 | \grace { \clef tenor s16 s16 s16 } es'8( des'8 …` (the `\clef tenor` before `f4(` at 33 removed). The spacer graces are needed for the reason in finding 1. Without them, my first scratch build printed the new clef after the barline. Bn 2's A♭2 at 35 needs one ledger line in tenor clef. The bass clef at 39 is unchanged.
