## Findings

### 1. 95–98: lower strings alone under the oboe's recollection of movement II (Fauré, Ex. V-38). Tested, not applied

**Candidate.** Adler PDF 162: "In order to produce a somber background, the lower strings only may be used to accompany the melody" (Fauré, violas and cellos divided, basses). At 95–98 the held chord under Ob 1 (E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5) is Vln II G4, violas B♭3/E♭4 → B♭3/E4, cellos E♭3 → C3, basses E♭2 → C2, all sul tasto from the back desks (c444, c499), with Hn 3·4 G4/B♭3 and Bn 2. I took the violins out, so the background is lower strings only. The cellos divide and take over the third, which keeps the triad in the strings. Both violin sections would then rest from 95 to 103. Test in /tmp/critic-507/workB:

The violas' E♭4→E4, the basses, bassoon, horns and the oboe are unchanged. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 93–104 gives the same bass-doubling list as v507, except that the cellos' E♭3→C3 octave with the basses at 97.1 is gone.

**Why I declined it.**
- With the third in the divided cellos, the string chord has E♭3–G3–B♭3 in close position just above the bass. That goes against Adler's spacing rule (PDF 151: wider spaces between the lower instruments). It also halves the two back desks to two players on each note.
- The register separation the reading asks for is already there (PDF 135, 144 rule 3). Vln II's G4 is a sixth and more below the oboe's line, and it gives the chord a soft top that the violas cannot replace without dividing in three.
- The fresh violin entry at 103 is already there for Vln I. For Vln II the gain is small.
- Fauré's dark colour suits a requiem text. At the end of this exposition the recollection is a quiet memory between the codetta's ff and the development's f, and the veiled violin G4 (c444, c499) is the right colour for it.

The result is a different colour, not a better one, so v507 stays as it is.

### 2. 171–186: stopping the violas' figure for the second phrase (Ex. V-13). Considered, not applied

Mozart sets off his second phrase by stopping the Alberti figure (PDF 136–137). The violas' eighth figure runs through both phrases of the C-minor F2, but it already changes for the second phrase: ord. at 181, sul tasto pp at 183, Cl 2 moves at 181–182, and Fl 1 joins the peak at 182. Stopping it at 183 would leave held notes under the deceptive cadence at 186, whose voicing c022 set ("deceptive-cadence voicing 26, 63, 166, 183, 186"). It would also take away the motion that leads into the violas' tune at 187. No change.

### 3. 71–74: coupling the counter-melody in thirds or sixths (Ex. V-12). Tested on paper, not applied

Adler says that pairing an accompaniment in thirds after unison writing gives a strong contrast (PDF 136). At 67–76 the violas and cellos play the counter-melody in unison, and they already split into thirds at 70, 72 and 73. I worked out full coupling for the consequent at 71–74. Sixths above the cellos put the violas on A♭4/C5 at 71, the notes of Vln II's lower octave of the tune, which goes against PDF 144 rule 3 (separate theme and countertheme in register). Thirds above put G4 against ii7 (F–A♭–C–E♭) on the downbeat of 71. Neither is better than the present texture, where the tune and the counter-melody take turns moving in every bar (PDF 144 rule 4, Ex. V-20). No change.

### 4. 75–76: imitation of F2's head in the cellos. Tested on paper, not applied

The reading notes that canonic and fugal writing suits the strings (PDF 143). F2 is never imitated in this movement. I tried a cello entry on F2's head half a bar after the violins at 75. Its second beat at 76 puts G3 against the B♭ chord (V6 over D) as an unprepared dissonance, and the entry runs into 77, where the cellos must join the tune. A two-bar canon does not fit before the climax. No change.

### 5. Checks against the reading that pass

- 67–82 is a full three-layer texture (PDF 132–135): tune in Vln I·II octaves, counter-melody in violas, cellos and Hn 1, background in harp arpeggios with bassoon harmony (PDF 136). At 77–82 the counterpoint thins to the violas so the tune breaks through (PDF 144 rule 2).
- 247–254 places F2's head in horns, trumpets and violas between F1's head above (violins, flutes) and below (cellos, Bn 1, trombones). Each layer has its own rhythm (PDF 142–144).
- 259–262 is Adler's "sustained harmony plus agitated violin figures" (PDF 158): brass chorale, Vln II and clarinet figures, violas' repeated quarters, basses' eighths.
- 205–210: the violas' off-beat chords are the on/off-beat formula (PDF 158). The head imitation every two bars and c407's three-octave run follow PDF 137.
