## Other study

I looked at all 16 pages of v265 at 60 dpi, and at 110-220 dpi at pages 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 12, 13, 15 and 16, where the strings carry *div.*, bowings and text. I wrote three scripts in /tmp/critic-265/tools: `divscan.py` (the *div.*/*unis.* state of every string chord in section order), `slurlen.py` (string slur lengths) and a search for dotted notes with staccato. MOLA PDF 10 (Example 7: no colliding markings) supports F1. I checked the finale's quotations against themes-scherzo.ly as updated today: theme A's head at 275-276 and motive x at 9 and 143-145 still match. Nothing to change there.

Three options I considered and decided against, so the composer need not test them:
- *al tallone* at 91 (PDF 42). Adler says a down-bow series is "most often performed at the frog". The ⊓ marks already say it, and more text in a ff bar is the overmarking that PDF 38 warns against.
- Measured-tremolo shorthand for the basses' eighths at 247-277 (PDF 50, Ex. II-47). It would save ink, but LilyPond's MIDI would play whole notes. Before 223 it would also look like the tremolo that is reserved for the arrival. The eighths stay written out.
- Fingered or undulating tremolo (PDF 50-51, Brahms 1/iv). The only tremolos are the fff arrival and the 273-276 close. There, divided bowed tremolo gives more sound than one player rocking between two notes.

## Findings

**1. Bars 217-218, Vln II, cellos, basses: the barline cuts through "change bow at will".** At Largamente the bar 217 is narrower than the text column, so the 217|218 barline, which runs through the string group, crosses "bow" in all three staves. Vln I's copy sits above the top staff and is clear. The 220 dpi crop of page 12 shows this. The instruction itself is right: two tied bars at 2 = 72 last 3.3 s at f with a diminuendo, the slow, loud case where Adler warns against overloading the bow (PDF 39). So I keep the text and make room for it (MOLA PDF 10: no colliding markings). Fix in `codaTwo.ily`: `\once \textLengthOn des'''1~\cbaw` (Vln II), `\once \textLengthOn f1~\cbaw\>` (Vc), `\once \textLengthOn f,1~\cbaw\>` (Cb). Bar 217 widens and the system 209-222 stays on page 12. **Applied.**

**2. Bars 119 and 122, Vln I: the soli division is shown as octave chords, and the "div." at 122 contradicts the "2 soli upper note" at 119.** At 119 the octave chords are marked "dolce, div.: 2 soli upper note": two players take the line at pitch and the rest take the octave below (dev.ily comment c063/c132). At 122 the same octave answer has only "div.". No *unis.* comes between them, so Adler's rule (PDF 30) makes that mark redundant. Worse, a plain *div.* means the outside/inside split, half and half (PDF 30), which is a different division from the one at 119. Adler says to state how a division other than outside/inside is made (PDF 31-32) and to write such lines out separately with their direction (PDF 32, Ex. II-13 Strauss, "4 Soli" on its own stems). Fix in `dev.ily`, the same pitches as two voices:
`r4 << { g'''8(^\markup \italic "dolce, 2 soli" f''' es''' d''' c''' a'' | fis''4) } \\ { g''8(\pp_\markup \italic "gli altri" f'' es'' d'' c'' a' | fis'4) } >> r4 r2 | R1 | r4 << { d'''8(^\markup \italic "2 soli" c''' b'' a'' g'' fis'' | g''4) } \\ { d''8(\pp_\markup \italic "gli altri" c'' b' a' g' fis' | g'4) } >> r4 r2 |`
Each line now has its own stems and slur. The part shows who plays which note in both bars. *unis.* at 128 is unchanged. **Applied.** This is a technique the score has not used before: Adler's written-out division with a label on each line.

**3. Bar 92, all strings: the bowing of 91 is printed again in full. Use *sim.*, which the score has never used.** Bars 91 and 92 have the same rhythm and the same accented down-bows on beats 1 and 3 in Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb (c218's down-bow series, Adler PDF 42-43). Adler's examples mark such a pattern once and then write *sim.* (PDF 43 Handel Ex. II-30; PDF 44 Stravinsky Ex. II-34; PDF 45 Berlioz Ex. II-35, *simile*; PDF 37 Franck, "etc."). PDF 38 asks for bowings "without overmarking the parts". Bar 93 changes the pattern (half note ⊓, quarter V), so its marks stay and end the *sim.* Fix in `clos.ily`, bar 92 in each string part: drop both `\downbow` and put `^\markup \italic "sim."` on the first note, e.g. Vln I `fis'''4->^\markup \italic "sim." r8 g'''8 aes'''4-> r4`. This removes ten marks from a crowded ff bar. **Applied.**

**4. Bar 91, Vln II: double stops without *non div.* beside the violas' *non div.*** From 91 to 94 both sections play ff accented double stops on down-bows. The violas carry *non div.* (they had been divided since 87). Vln II, whose chords are just as much meant as double stops for every player, has no mark. By default an unmarked part plays the double stop, but Adler (PDF 30) says that when every player is to play it "it is safer to write *non div.*" A Vln II leader who sees *div.* a few bars earlier in other parts, or who reads the score, should not have to guess. Fix in `clos.ily`: `<bes' g''>4->\downbow\ff^\markup \italic "non div."` at 91. **Applied.**

No quotation, theme (F1, F2, the chorale top lines) or protected passage is changed. All four findings are notation only. The MIDI has the same 9,076 note-ons at the same times as v265. The file is not byte-identical because the two voices at 119 and 122 add a voice context.
