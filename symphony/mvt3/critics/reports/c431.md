## Findings

### 1. 39-42: the trombones' held V7 stops dead under F1's last fragment; one trombone should carry the fragment into the diminuendo (applied)

**Bars and problem.** At 39-40 Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone hold V7 of E♭ (F3, D3, B♭2), f >. At 41-42 the violins (F4, sul G) and the cellos (F3, sul G) play F1's last fragment F–D–E♭–F, fading to p before the transition at 43. All three trombones stop together at the end of 40 (c237's staged diminuendo), so the brass colour that has carried the counterstatement since 35 drops out in one step, and the fragment, which is the last thing the listener hears of F1 before the second theme, has strings only on its lower octave. Tbn 1's F3 at 40 is the fragment's first note.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Adler PDF 28-29, Ex. XI-12 (Schubert, *Rosamunde*, Entr'acte I, image PDF 29): "by dropping out two trombones and leaving only the third to bolster the lower strings, Schubert orchestrates a beautiful diminuendo." In the example the remaining trombone plays the violas', cellos' and basses' melodic figure with them, p, then pp. The score does the first half (drop-outs) but not the second (one trombone staying on the strings' line).
- Adler PDF 30-32, Ex. XI-14 (Berlioz): the trombones enter p as a melodic colour against the bassoons; "the effectiveness ... is due more to the color contrasts". The finale has no soft melodic trombone phrase anywhere; its trombones play themes only at f-ff (103, 205, 224, 247, 255).
- Adler PDF 20: in later statements of a theme Hindemith "doubles the brasses with other members of the orchestra to give new colors". F1's tail at 41-42 has never had a brass colour; at 167-170 (the recapitulation's parallel fade) the order is protected, so 41-42 is the one place for it.
- The trombones are already in the texture at 35-40, so the question is only how they leave it, which is what Ex. XI-12 answers.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-431/work/sections/pTwo.ily, `pTwoTbnOne`).** Tbn 1 keeps its F3 from 40 and plays the fragment with the cellos, at their pitch, under the same diminuendo; Tbn 2 and the bass trombone still stop after 40, as c237 set.

```
was:  ... | c2 bes,4 d4 | f1~\> | f1 | R1*2\! | }
now:  ... | c2 bes,4 d4 | f1~\> | f1 | f2( d4. es8 | f2.)\p r4 | }
```

- The hairpin from 39 continues to p at 42, the cellos' level; the trombone ends with the strings on 42.3 and rests at 42.4, as they do.
- It doubles the cellos at the unison (F3–D3–E♭3–F3) and the violas' F3 at 42; it is an octave below the violins' F4, so c237's reason (the violins' F4 no longer covered at the unison) still holds.
- Tbn 2 rests at 41-42, so the shared staff shows Tbn 1 with stems up and Tbn 2's rest below (checked at 220 dpi). Equal dynamics on the shared staff: Tbn 2 is silent while Tbn 1 plays.
- This modifies c237 for one player (Tbn 1 now stops after 42, not 40). The new reason is Adler PDF 28-29: the brass way to finish a staged diminuendo is to leave one trombone on the lower strings' line. It does not restore c161's bass trombone B♭2, which c237 removed.
- F1's notes are unchanged; only a doubling is added. For finale.md: "41-42: Tbn 1 doubles the cellos on F1's last fragment, p (c431)". No other movement quotes this fragment, so no other thread needs a message.

### 3. Checked, not changed: trombones on F1's B♭-minor head at 37-38 (PDF 19-20, Hindemith's horn-trombone unison)

c161's reason for keeping trombones off F1 ("their first melody is the chorale at 224") no longer holds: Tbn 1 has played the head at 103 and Tbn 1·2 at 205 for many versions. But 37-38 already has the head ff in Hn 3·4, cellos and Bn 2 against ff violin eighths, and it has been rescored six times (c297, c337, c345, c387 and others); adding Tbn 1 would only thicken it and would take the trombones' first thematic entry away from the development, where it is now a new colour. I decided not to change it. The composer should note in HISTORY that c161's stated reason is out of date and the real reason is the one above.
