## Focus questions

**III and VII across the movement.** The large-scale plan is the book's minor-mode frame, i – III – (IV) – V – i. Natural VII (B♭: B♭ minor at 37, then V7 of E♭ over the B♭ pedal 39–50) introduces III, as in Ex. 15-1. III (E♭, 51–98) moves to V through the development's F minor, which acts as the passing IV of Ex. 15-2b. On the small scale, F1's bar 12 is the book's VII6 passing chord (i – VII6 – iv – V), and bar 14's E♭ over V6 is the "apparent III+" that expresses the dominant. The movement uses III and VII the way the reading describes. In major, VII appears only as colour: ♭vii at 229 and the ♭7 in F1 at 265–268. Both are fine.

**A♭ at 187.** A♭ is VI, not III. Its link to 199 is the rising-third case on PDF 231: A♭ → C minor shares two tones, so 199's C minor sounds like an offshoot of A♭ (iii of A♭) and not like a tonic arrival. That fits the plan: C must not arrive before 223, and critic 082 showed that 187–222 is one predominant span (VI – iii – V7/♭II – ♭II – Ger6 – V). Keep it.

**E at 243.** The progression follows the book: ii7 – III♯ – cadential 6/4 – V – V4/3 – I matches PDF 234–235 (direct III→V in 19th-century style; III to I through an inverted V). As proportion, E is the shortest member of the C–A♭–E cycle: half a bar at about 2 = 70, against twelve bars of A♭. It cannot be lengthened without moving the horn call (protected). What can be fixed is its identity, which rests entirely on G♯ (Finding 1).

**Wind and brass balance, 243–264, at R = 88.**
- 243–246: Hn 1·2 are ff against Tpt 1·2 f. That is Rimsky's PDF 41 rule and is correct.
- 247–254: the S head in Tpt 1·2 ff stands against the P head in Tbn 1·2 ff. One trumpet equals one trombone in forte, so the brass combination of the heads is balanced. Ob/Cl reinforce the S head, and Fl/Vln I·II carry the P head on top. At 88 the eighth-note figures are clearer than at 100.
- 255–262: Tpt 1·2 in unison ff carry the chorale, with Hn 1·2 and Tbn 1 an octave lower. The woodwinds' mf/f entries are the protected scoring crescendo into 263.

I found no balance fault apart from 243 (Finding 1). R stays 88.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 243: the third of III♯ is almost inaudible (applied)
**Bars:** 243, first half.
**Problem:** The E-major chord that closes the C–A♭–E cycle sounds as an open fifth. E is in Tpt 1·2 (f, accented), Hn 1·2 (ff, accented), Vln I upper, Vln II lower, cellos, basses and Bn 2. B is in Ob 1, Hn 3, Bn 1, Cl 1, violas and Vln II upper. G♯, the only tone that makes the chord III♯ and not an E with no third, is in the lower half of Vln I alone, about seven players at G♯4, f with a crescendo, under four brass on E.
**Evidence:** A&S PDF 233–234: III in major exists only through its accidentals, and of Ex. 15-5 the book says "a complete chord would require a D♯ as well". The chromatic tone is the chord's identity. Rimsky PDF 87: the harmonic parts must be "equally balanced". Rimsky PDF 90 names the oboe's "penetrating quality".
**Fix (sHead.ily):** Ob 1 takes G♯4 at the unison with Vln I's lower divisi, then G4 with it:
`sHeadOboeOne = { R1*4 | e''2.~(\f e''8 d''8 | c''4 a''2-- g''4 | f''4. e''8 d''4 c''4 | d''2 c''2) | gis'2->( g'2) | R1*3 | }` (was `| b'2 r2 |`).
- The unison keeps the chromatic tone in one voice, so the composer's own rule (no doubled chromatic tone, A&S PDF 394) still holds.
- Ob 1's old B4 was the fifth, already in five parts.
- The 7th C of 242's ii7 still resolves to B at its own pitch in Vln II (C5→B4) and in Hn 3 (C4→B3).
- A side effect: the octave pair Hn 3 C4→B3 / Ob 1 C5→B4 disappears from the all-pairs check.

This is inside the protected "III♯ approach 241–247" (cycle 42). It changes no chord and no line of the progression. It only makes the protected III♯ audible, which is the reason for protecting it.

### 2. 255–257: repeated tonic under the chorale's first note; III gives the 8–7–6 (applied)
**Bars:** 256 (S), with Hn 4 C–B–A over 255–257.
**Problem:** The last chorale statement opens with two bars of I6 over the E bass, and every inner part repeats 1̂: violas C5 in eight ff quarters, Tpt 3 C4 in four marcato halves, Hn 4 C4 tied, Vln II's arpeggios through C5. The violas then leap C5→E4 into vi.
**Evidence:**
- A&S PDF 239: III is the substitute for I6 "when the stepwise descent 8–7–6 in the soprano or in an inner voice is wanted, or when excessive repetitions of 1̂ in any of the upper voices would otherwise result."
- PDF 237, Ex. 15-10b: I – (III) with 8–7–6 in an inner voice.
- PDF 236–237, Ex. 15-9: III – VI – II – V – I, the chain of descending fifths into the cadence.
- PDF 240: avoid I→III weak-to-strong. Here it is strong (255) to weak (256), so the rule is met.

With the change, 255–262 is I6 – iii – vi – iiø6/5 – Ger6 – V7, so the bass's descent into 263 also gets the book's fifth-chain.
**Fix (finale.ily, bar 256 only):**
- `finaleHnFour`: `c'1\ff | b1 |` (was `c'1\ff~ | c'1 |`), so Hn 4 plays C4–B3–A3 (255–257), the 8–7–6.
- `finaleTptThree`: `| e'2-> e'2 |` (was `c'2-> c'2`).
- `finaleVla`: `| e'4 e' e' e' |` (was `c''4 c'' c'' c''`). The violas reach their thread pitch E4 one bar early and hold it into 257–258.
- `finaleVlnTwo`: `| e'8( g' b' e'') g''( e'' b' g') |`.

No C remains at 256, so the chord is a clean E–G–B. The chorale's G (Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, Vln I) and the E bass are unchanged.

- B goes B→A in Hn 4. Neither Tpt 3 nor the violas take B→E, which would make fifths with the bass E→A.
- p5.py gives the same 38 lines as v83. Outer-voice parallels: 0.
- par.py lists six new "P8" lines at 257 for "Horns 3·4_" against B.tbn, tuba and cellos. They are artifacts of the shared staff: Hn 3 re-attacks E4 at 256 beat 3 while Hn 4 holds B3, and the script pairs that E4 with Hn 4's A3. The real lines are Hn 3 E4→C4 and Hn 4 B3→A3, with no parallels.

This is inside cycle 22's protected "final approach 255–263 (I6–vi–iiø6/5–Ger6–V7–I)". I answer it as follows: every listed chord stays on its bar (I6 still opens 255), and so do the chorale top line, the iiø6/5 with A♭, the Ger6, the V7 free of A♭ and the only root V7–I at 262–263.
