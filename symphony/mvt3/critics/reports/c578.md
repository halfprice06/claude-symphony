## Findings

### 1. Bar 47: Cl 2's entry on Hn 4's A♭3 has no niente attack (applied)

Problem. In the transition over the B♭ pedal, Hn 4 has held A♭3 pp since 43 and, since c538, fades out over the first half of 47 (`aes2\> r2\!`) while Cl 2 enters on the same A♭3 at 47.1 and holds it pp to 50. c538 wanted the change from horn to clarinet colour to be a crossfade rather than a step. As written, Cl 2 gives a normal tongued pp attack on the downbeat. Nothing else in the bar moves at 47.1 (strings hold harmonics and pp pedals; Ob 1 starts on 47.2), so that attack is the only articulation in the texture, at the same pitch as the fading horn note. The re-attack is what a crossfade is meant to hide.

Evidence. Adler PDF 211 and Ex. VII-57: "One effect that can be accomplished on the clarinet better than on any other woodwind instrument is the niente attack, in which the tone starts from almost complete silence without articulation" (the example is notated N < p > N). The score already asks for this at the two other places where a clarinet pad starts out of silence, 51 (Cl 1·2, "dal niente") and 171 (Cl 2 and bass clarinet). Bar 47 is the one place where a clarinet takes over a held note from another instrument at the same pitch. That is the case the niente attack fits best, and here it was not marked. Adler PDF 188 (Ex. VII-9, Brahms 1/iv) makes the same point about a hand-over: the two flutes "must be dovetailed with great care to preserve the illusion of a single flute performing the passage".

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-578/work/sections/trans.ily):

```
transClTwo = { R1*4 | aes1\pp~^\markup \italic "dal niente" | aes1~ | aes1~ | aes2. r4 | }
```

I added a header comment (c578). Only the text changes; pitches, the pp level and Hn 4's diminuendo stay as they are. The words print above the clarinet staff over the "2." solo label, as at 51. I also considered a circled-tip hairpin rising to pp on 47.3 (Adler's own notation) and kept the text instead. The score writes "dal niente" with pp at 51 and 171, and a hairpin would need a hidden starting dynamic, which changes the MIDI for no audible gain.

A mf on the doubling follows the score's rule that doublings are marked one degree lower (c008), and Ob 1 and Cl 1 have the same marking. A player will make B♭6 sound full at mf anyway. c576's octave change was rejected for a different reason (it lost the top octave), and this proposal does not touch that.
