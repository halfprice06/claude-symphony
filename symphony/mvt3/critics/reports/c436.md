## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 255–257: Vln II "sul G" climbs to the seventh and eighth positions at ff, Allegro molto (applied)
- Bars: 255–257 (finale.ily, `finaleVlnTwo`).
- Problem: c376 marked Vln II's ff octave of F2 "sul G" through 257 so that it would not lie on the weak D string. The line is G4–E4 | D4–C4–E4–G4 | C5–D5. On the G string C5 is the eleventh and D5 the twelfth above the open string, so the section must shift from the octave position into seventh and eighth position on the thickest string at 2 = 88, under the ff brass chorale, and then cross to G5 at 258 anyway.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 26: "A limit should be set to the use of a high note on any one of the three lower strings on violins, violas and 'cellos. This note should be the one in the fourth position, either the octave note or the ninth of the open string." The same page allows higher notes only "when they are of long value ... slow, flowing melodies", which this Allegro molto is not. This answers c376: everything up to 256's G4 (the octave, the fourth position) stays on the G string as c376 wanted; only the two notes past the limit move. On the D string C5 and D5 lie in third and fourth position, and the D string is strongest in its upper half.
- Fix: end the spanner on 256's G4: `d'4( c'4 e'4 g'4\stopTextSpan) | c''2.( d''4) |` (was `d'4( c'4 e'4 g'4) | c''2.( d''4\stopTextSpan) |`). Notes, slurs, dynamics and MIDI unchanged. No theme note changed.

### 2. 102–103: Cl 1 leaps an eleventh from an accented chalumeau C4 to a soft held clarion F5 (applied)
- Bars: 102.4–104 (dev.ily, `devClOne`, `devClTwo`).
- Problem: Cl 1 ends the scale figure with the octave drop C5–C4 (accented, at the top of a crescendo), and one beat (0.36 s) later has to place the soft held F5 that c239 gave it at 103–104 (written D4 to G5, across the break, from accent to p). Cl 2 rests from 95 to 108.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 36, note b: "On account of its construction the clarinet is not well adapted to sudden leaps from one octave to another; these skips are easier on flutes, oboes and bassoons." PDF 36 also says the clarinet "can reduce volume of tone to a mere breath", which a fresh attack does best. The per-player scan found this as the only clarinet leap of more than an octave in under half a second that lands on a held soft note.
- Fix: the held F5 goes to Cl 2: `devClTwo = { R1*4 | f''1~\p | f''1 | R1*2 | R1*3 | ...` and `devClOne` has `... c''4->)\! c'4-> | R1*2 | r4 f'8(\mf ...`. Sounding pitches and dynamics are the same; the shared staff prints "2." at 103 and "1." at 105. c239's held F, c178's Cl 2 rests at 101–102 and 105–106, and c304's oboe rest at 102.4 are unchanged. The octave drop itself stays in Cl 1. It is tongued and marked, and moving it would split the figure.

### 3. Checked, no change
- 238 and 192: sul A to C6 is one semitone over the PDF 26 limit, on a long note at the top of a slow phrase, which is the exception the same page allows. c256 kept it, and I agree.
- Timpani: every muta has at least five seconds (37: A♭→B♭ with 2.5 bars to 39; 206 and 215: five and eight bars). Two drums retune together at 95, with 17 bars before 113. The range is F2–E♭3, inside PDF 48.
- Breathing: no change since c396. The longest unbroken stretch is 14.5 s, which is soft or held.
- Reed arpeggios at 21, 161 (oboes) and 29–38 (bassoons) are F1's own figure at the theme's speed. The bassoons' legato countersubject is a melodic line, not a florid arpeggio (PDF 36: "these general directions should not deter the orchestrator").

### 4. Considered and declined (new techniques from the reading)
- Timpani coperti (PDF 48) at the pp rolls of 177 and 235–238: the timpanist already has soft sticks and plays near the rim there. A muffled drum would turn the C pedal under the second-theme head into a dull thud, which is worse.
- Cello pizzicato natural harmonics (PDF 45) at the end of the pizzicato passages (66, 122, 186): each ending is protected (c004 and c007: 51–66, bassoon solo 115–122, the 171–186 cadence), and a weak chime would add nothing that the harp's harmonics at 43 do not already give.
- Four-note pizzicato chords (PDF 45): every pizzicato in this movement is soft accompaniment; a struck chord would be a new gesture with no place to go.
- Wind mutes (PDF 39): no passage asks for a pianissimo the oboes or bassoons cannot already reach.
