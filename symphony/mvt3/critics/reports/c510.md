## Findings

### 1. Bars 242–243: hold the A♭ = G♯ link high, in Fl 2 (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's E-major chord at 243 is the finale's only sound in the scherzo's key, and its G♯ is the scherzo's last pitch. In v510 the common tone between 242's iiø7 (D–F–A♭–C) and 243's E major is held only in Hn 4: A♭3 tied as G♯3, eight or more parts down in the texture. The G♯ above it is attacked fresh at 243 by Vln I's upper desk (G♯5) and Ob 1 (G♯4), after Vln I falls from C6 and Ob 1 from C5 (a diminished fourth down).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 586–587 (Ex. 32-3, Widmung): when there is no pivot chord, "an important common element helps to integrate the two tonal regions: it is the melody tone A♭/G♯, sustained … above the chord progression A♭–E (F♭)". The common tone "will appear in an exposed position, usually in the soprano" (PDF 587). The finale already does this at the movement joint: the scherzo's G♯5 continues as Vln I A♭5 at bar 1, then falls to G5. At 242–243 the same pitch (A♭5 = G♯5 → G5) can be sustained in one high voice. That recalls bar 1 at its own pitch, in reverse spelling, just before the second-theme combination.

**Fix (in /tmp/critic-510/work/sections/sHead.ily).**
```
sHeadFluteTwo = { R1*7 | f''1\mf | e''2 r2 | R1*3 | }
→
sHeadFluteTwo = { R1*7 | f''2\mf aes''2~ | gis''2 g''2 | R1*3 | }
```
Fl 2 keeps iiø7's third F5 on beats 1–2 (c183). It then takes the ♭6 A♭5 under Fl 1's C6, holds it as G♯5 into 243, and falls to G5 on 243.3 with Vln I's upper desk. The spelling follows Hn 4 (`aes1~ | gis2`). The flute is a voice under the top (Fl 1 D6–C6), not the soprano. In this register it is the highest held note through the bar line, since the violins re-attack.

### 3. Bar 243: III♯ → passing IV → V (worked out and declined)

A&S PDF 594 says III♯ "moves on to V, usually through a passing IV". At 243 the bass leaps E2→G2 into the cadential 6/4. I worked out a passing F under 243.3 (Vc/Cb `e,2 f,4 g,4`-type motion). It would put F under Vln I's G5 and Fl 2's new G5, making IV with an added ninth. Avoiding that means moving Vln I's G♯5 to A5, which undoes c311's G♯5→G5 fall. c473 also declined an F bass at 242, because it produced fifths and a third F-bass ii chord in nine bars. Here the E chord is a single chord, not an area, so the book's rule for an expanded III♯ does not require the passing IV. Declined. No other candidate from the reading improves the score: the joint 1–2 (c310), 186→187 (c410) and 198→199 already follow it.
