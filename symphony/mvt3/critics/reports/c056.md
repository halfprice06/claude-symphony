## Findings

### 1. 243–245: violin double stops without "div.", one of them impossible (applied)
**Problem.** The violins move from single lines into two-note chords at 243 with no "div." mark. Vln II at 243, second half, has C4–E5. That is a tenth: C4 is on the G string and E5 is on the A or E string. Those strings are not adjacent, so it cannot be played as a double stop. The sHead.ily comment says that Vln I's G♯ is in "the lower divisi", so divisi was intended, but the part does not say so. Under Adler part 1 PDF 30, the players are entitled to read every chord here as a double stop.

**Fix.** 243: `<gis' e''>2\<^\markup \italic "div."` in Vln I and `<e' b'>2\<^\markup \italic "div."` in Vln II. 245: `r8 g''8(^\markup \italic "unis." …` in Vln I and `r8 g'8(^\markup \italic "unis." …` in Vln II, at the stringendo run.

### 2. 273–277: divisi lines written as double stops (applied)
**Problem.** finale.md describes the closing chain as upper and lower lines: "Vln I (lower note)", "upper violas", "lower violas". The score has no "div." at 273, only "div. a 3" at 278, so 273–277 would be played as double-stop tremolo. Two of the parts are awkward that way:
- Vln I: G5/C6 → F♯5/C6 → F5/C6. In third position, C6 and both F♯5 and F5 need the third finger at the same time. The fifth then has to be stopped by one finger, all under tremolo.
- Vln II: C5 is held under G5 → F♯5. Low C5 on the A string and high F♯5 on the E string both need the second finger.

Adler part 1 PDF 30 says to mark "div." whenever two players are meant.

**Fix.** 273: `<g'' c'''>2:32->^\markup \italic "div."` (Vln I), `<c'' g''>2:32->^\markup \italic "div."` (Vln II) and `<e' g'>2:32->^\markup \italic "div."` (Vla). The divisi runs on into "div. a 3" at 278. Pitches are unchanged.

### 3. 110–113: timpani ff above brass held at f (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 54 set the trumpets, horns, trombones and tuba to f at the development climax so that the ff strings and woodwinds carry the melody G5–A♭5–F5–E5–F5. The timpani strike C and A♭ at ff on the strong beats under that melody. That is the case Piston warns about (adler2 PDF 55, 84) and the opposite of Shostakovich 5/iv (PDF 138), where the timpani work at a lower level than the tutti. The strokes are already accented (111–113 `c4->`), so they keep their edge at f.

**Fix.** devTimp at 110: `c4\f r4 c4 r4` (was `\ff`). The accents and the C and A♭ pitches stay.

No more findings. The percussion writing follows every principle in the assigned reading (table above), and the other string, wind and brass points in the focus are playable as written. I make no change to the bass drum at 223: Adler's warning concerns masking, and an fff C-major chord has nothing in it to mask. The crash at 223 is protected.
