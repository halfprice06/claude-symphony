## Findings

### 1. 259–262: every group starts the last crescendo at the same moment; the brass should hold ff and make the final crescendo from 261 (applied)

Bars: 259–263, the last cadence (iiø6/5 259–260, Ger6 261, V7 262, I 263 fff).

Problem: at 259 the woodwinds, the strings and all the brass (Tpt 1–3, Hn 1–4, Tbn 1–2, bass trombone, tuba) start one four-bar hairpin from ff to fff. Only the timpani wait for 261. The held brass already crescendo f → ff over 257–258 (c321), so the brass have no reserve left when the harmony reaches its strongest point (Ger6 → V7). A four-bar crescendo from ff in brass flattens out early. Hn 1·2 also breathe after 260 in the middle of that crescendo.

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 37, Ex. XI-19 (seen at 250 dpi): in Schubert's build, "cresc. poco a poco" is written into the strings and woodwinds first and into the horns and trumpets bars later; the trombones' new colour comes last.
- PDF 38–40, Ex. XI-21: the brass and the timpani roll make the climactic entrance together, with the cantus firmus in the brass.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: the brass make the final crescendo.
- This score already uses the same staging for its other great arrival. At 220–222 (c108) the brass hold p through 220 and crescendo from 221 while the strings, woodwinds and timpani are already rising. The last cadence should get the same treatment.
