## Findings

### 1. Bars 61.3–62.3 and 181.3–182.3: the iv7 under the solo's climax has no 3rd, the 7th is tripled, and the leading tone is doubled (applied)

Problem. Beats 61.3–62.2 are iv7 in E♭ minor (A♭–C♭–E♭–G♭). It lies under the solo's peak and leads to V on 62.3. In v142 the chord has no C♭ in any voice. The 7th G♭ is tripled: Cl 2 G♭3 and G♭4, Vla G♭3, Vln II G♭4. The Vla G♭3 and Vln II G♭4 then both move to F in octaves. Cl 2's G♭ leaps up to B♭ instead of resolving down. On 62.3 the leading tone D is doubled (Bsn 1 D3 and Cl 1 D4) and both resolve to E♭ in octaves. In the recap (181.3–182.3, C minor) the pattern is the same: iv7 with no A♭, and the leading tone B doubled (Bsn 1 B2 and Cl 1 B3).

Evidence. An incomplete 7th chord keeps the 3rd and drops the 5th (A&S PDF 400, Ex. 24-3a). Here the 3rd is the note dropped. The 7th must be free to resolve down (PDF 399), and neither the 7th nor the leading tone should be doubled (PDF 439, Points for Review; PDF 419). The missing note is the minor-mode ^6, and ♭6 tends to move to 5 (PDF 402). That ^6–5 step is the strongest voice-leading motion available into this half cadence, and it is absent.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-142/work):
```
% sections/sOne.ily, sOneClTwo bars 59–62
| bes2 ges2 | aes2 ges2 | ges2 ces'2~ | ces'2 bes4 r4 |
% sOneVla bar 62
r4 <aes ces'>4 r4 <f bes>4
% sOneBsnOne bar 62
es2 bes,4 r4
% sections/recS.ily, recSClTwo bars 179–182
| g2 es2 | f2 es2 | es2 aes2~ | aes2 g4 r4 |
% recSVla bar 182
es8(\mp aes es aes)\>
% recSBsnOne bar 182
c2 g,4 r4
```
Cl 2 now holds ^6 (C♭4 / A♭3) through the iv7 and falls to ^5 on V. The Vla takes the 3rd on the off-beats. Bsn 1 takes the root of V instead of a second leading tone. The leading tone now sounds only in Cl 1. The 7th G♭ remains in Vln II, Cl 1's line and the Vla's lower note, and it resolves to F. The trumpet solo and the pizzicato scoring are unchanged. These are pitch changes in the accompaniment, which c093, c094 and c141 accepted as allowed.

### 2. Bar 84: in the closing F1 head in E♭, the bass resolves the 7th a beat early under the melody's 4̂ (applied)

Problem. Bar 84 has V4/2 (bass A♭) going to I. On beat 3 the melody sounds A♭ (4̂), which is the 7th carried over from the V4/2. Under it, the bass has already moved to G (I6). The 7th and its note of resolution sound together: A♭5/A♭4 in the melody and Cl A♭3 against Vc G3 and Cb G2. These are minor 9ths and a minor 2nd between the bass and the line that should be resolving into it.

Evidence. A&S separate a transferred resolution (the 7th resolves in another voice, PDF 406–407) from a transferred 7th, where the 7th moves to another voice and resolves there (PDF 408, Ex. 24-14b). Here the bass takes the resolution while the melody still holds the 7th. That is neither procedure: the dissonance and its resolution sound at the same time. In bar 12 (C minor) the same melodic 4̂ is harmonised with a subdominant, so the 4̂ is consonant there.

Fix (applied):
```
% sections/clos.ily bar 84
closVc:  aes4 f4 d4 es4
closCb:  aes,4 f,4 d,4 es,4
closVla: f'8( aes' bes' aes') f'8( bes' g' es')
```
The bass now runs A♭–F–D–E♭ (V4/2–V4/3–V6/5–I). The 7th passes from the bass to the melody and resolves A♭→G over I. The diminished 5th between the outer voices (D–A♭) resolves inward to E♭–G. I rejected B♭ on beat 3 because it makes F→B♭ octaves with Bsn 1. D, the leading tone, is only in the bass. The Vla follows the V6/5 and then resolves.

### 3. Bars 58→59 and 178→179: V7–i6 with the 7th falling into the bass's note (applied)

Problem. At 58→59 the Cl 2 fill ends on A♭ (the 7th of B♭7) and resolves down to G♭. At the same moment the bass leaps B♭→G♭ (i6 on the downbeat). This is the V7–I6 hidden octave, on a strong beat. The recap (178→179) does the same with Cl 2 F→E♭ over the bass leap G→E♭.

Evidence. A&S PDF 109 (Ex. 7-7) warns that V7–I6, with the 7th resolving 4–3 while the bass moves 5–3, "creates a bad set of hidden octaves, particularly if I6 is in a rhythmically strong position." Units 24–25 give the remedy. When the bass skips, the 7th's resolution may be transferred, often to the top voice or to the next lower voice (PDF 406–408, Ex. 24-12). A delayed resolution is also allowed (PDF 408–409).

Fix (applied): see the Cl 2 lines in finding 1. Bar 59 now begins `bes2 ges2` (recap `g2 es2`). Cl 2 rises A♭→B♭ (F→G) at the barline, so the 7th is not doubled into the bass's note. G♭ (E♭) arrives on beat 3 as a delayed resolution, and the Vla's upper voice still resolves to the 3rd. The change leaves a hidden octave between Cl 2 and the trumpet's top voice (an inner voice moving to the top, pp). I judged that acceptable. I left the same pattern at 74→75 and 194→195 as it is. Every alternative I tried there made octaves with the tune's B♭ (c097), doubled the leading tone, or needed an anticipation.
