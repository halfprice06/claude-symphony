## Findings

The findings are ranked by how much each point matters to the movement's texture. None needs a change. For each, I name the candidate change I tested on paper and why I did not apply it.

### 1. 139-150, the stretto: correct as written (no change)
The F1 head enters on G in Hn 1 (G4, p, 139), then in Ob 1/Cl 1 (141), then in Hn 1·2 a2 and Vln II (143, f), over held strings and the timpani's motive x, and reaches the ff chord at 147. This is the layout of Adler's Bartók example (PDF 44-47): entries in the horns' best octave (row 4), each marked louder than the last (row 7), held strings underneath, the stretto as the climax (row 8). Candidate: add Tbn 1 to the 143 entry an octave lower, as Adler's Hindemith fugato gives the whole choir. Not applied. The trombones' crescendo chord from 143 already carries the harmony, a trombone entry would double the horns' head in the same bar and thicken the texture before 147, and the stretto is protected (c255, c468).

### 2. 247-254, the combination of the two heads: balanced (no change)
Tpt 1·2 (f) with Hn 1·2 (ff) play the S head against Tbn 1·2 a2 (f) on the P head, with Hn 3·4 held. By Adler's equivalence (PDF 44, row 6) two horns are one trumpet, so at 247-250 the S head in the brass weighs about two trumpets against two trombones on P. At 251-254 the trumpets rest and Hn 1·2 alone carry S against the two trombones, which by that equivalence is half the weight of P in the brass. Candidate: Hn 3·4 a2 join the S head at 251-254 (`e'2.~( e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 c'4 | b2 c'2)` in place of their held notes). Not applied. Ob 1, Cl 1·2 and the violas also carry S at 251-254, so the head is not weak overall. Taking Hn 3·4 off the held chord would leave the harmony there to the tuba and the low strings, and the 247-254 layout is protected (c071, c091, c191).

### 3. 99-106, development entries: real imitation (no change)
The F1 head in F minor (violas, Bn 1, Vc, Cb, 99) is answered by Hn 3·4 a2 at B♭4 with Tbn 1 at B♭3 (103), a fourth higher. Each entry is followed by the scale figure in the other group (101-102 woodwinds, 105-106 woodwinds). This is the imitation that Adler's section opens with (PDF 41, row 1), and both brass entries lie in the best register (row 4). Candidate: a third entry in Tpt 1 at 107. Not applied. 107-110 is the protected lament (c259, c327), and the trumpets' first melody after the solo should not be spent here.

### 4. 265-272, staggered brass entries in the close: follows the Bartók model (no change)
At 265, Tpt 1·2 a2 play F1 at C5 with Hn 1·2 an octave lower. At 269, Hn 3·4 a2 take movement I's head rhythm. At 271, Tbn 1·2 a2 take the same rhythm while Tpt 1·2 take F1's continuation. One brass group enters every two bars and the other groups rest (Hn 1·2 rest 269-272, Tpt 1·2 rest 269-270). This keeps the head clear at each entry. It is the procedure of Ex. XI-24 (PDF 46-47: trumpets and trombones first, then the horns plus the choir). Tpt 3's repeated head rhythm under it is Hindemith's punctuation (row 5). No candidate change was worth the risk in protected bars (c159, c169: coda 247-279).

### 5. 205-208, brass F1 head and woodwind answer: balanced (no change)
Hn 1-4 (A♭4, both pairs a2) and Tbn 1 (A♭3) state the head f at 205-206, and the woodwinds answer at 207-208 with the horns held mp. Four horns in unison are two trumpets' weight (row 6), so the statement is strong. The answer has five woodwind lines in octaves with Vln II and the violas at mf, and is weaker than the statement, which suits a dialogue that builds toward 211. Candidate: Tbn 2 doubles Tbn 1 on the head instead of holding E♭3. Not applied: the held E♭3 is the only brass bass under the head, and 205-210 is protected (c179 after three rescorings).

### 6. Density between sections: no gap that needs filling (no change)
The profile runs from 2.5 voices (117-120, the bassoon solo) to 26.6 (273-276). Each large drop follows a cadence or a stop: 41-44 → 45-48 (transition over the B♭ pedal), 109-112 → 113-116 (after the climax), 165-168 → 169-172 (into the muted trumpet), 222 → 223 (in reverse: the arrival). Each large rise is prepared by a crescendo or a hammered upbeat (90, 146, 198, 246). The brass are silent or held through the thin passages (45-66, 115-138, 169-198), so the brass counterpoint passages (99-106, 139-150, 205-208, 247-254, 265-272) start from contrast, as Adler's examples do (the Hindemith fugato starts with the trombones over a single viola line and adds the horns and trumpets one group at a time, PDF 42-43). Nothing to change.

### 7. Brass punctuation under F1 (11-26, 151-166): correct (no change)
Hn 3·4 off-beat chords, and from 159 the trumpets' off-beats at mf, punctuate F1 in the strings and horns without covering it. This is the device Adler calls the commonest use of the brass (PDF 42, Hindemith m. 192, row 5). The bass trombone doubles the bass line in half notes at 151-166, which follows Brahms's practice and does not compete with the tune. Candidate: give the bass trombone an independent line at 159-165 (row 9). Not applied. The bass line there is F1's harmonic bass and should stay in octaves, and 151-166 is protected (c134, c169, c192).
