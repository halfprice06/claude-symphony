## Overall judgment

Set beside Mahler 1/iv, this finale is more conservative and more closely worked: it is a Brahms and Dvořák finale, not a Mahler one, which suits the brief. A conductor would accept the large shape: the withheld C major, the stop chord that resolves only at 223, the chorale, and the quotations. Mahler's lesson is that the climaxes get simpler as they grow. The places where this score falls short of that are all places where layers have been added a little at a time.

On the batch 151-158, compared with v149: three changes are clear improvements. The G♭6 suspension 210→211 now resolves properly. 217-218 put D♭-C-B-C on top. At 229, the C under the timpani pulse no longer gives the timpani a ninth over B♭. The violins' unison at 205-206 and the mf at 207-208 also help. Three spots got fussier, and none of them is protected:

- 130-132. Four cycles (c151, c154, c157, c158) went into one two-bar viola entry. Three horns now converge on D4, and the horns' quarter pulse is gone.
- 231-233. Two string parts now pulse in the same portato rhythm, two octaves apart, under the plagal close.
- 205-206. The violas were moved, but they still strike the horns' first head note six times a bar.

I fix all three below. Nothing from 151-158 is worse in harmony or form, and the page count is unchanged.

## Findings

### 1. 130-131: three horns at the unison on F1's head; the background pulse is lost (applied)
- **Problem.** In v149 the violas brought in F1's head alone, mf <, over Hn 3-4's repeated D4/B3-A3 quarters. That was a clear foreground with a background in a rhythm of its own. In v159:
  - Hn 2 (c151) and Hn 4 (c154) both double the violas at the unison: D4-A3-B3 | C4-B3-A3-G3-F♯3.
  - Hn 3 holds D4 (c157), so at 130.1 Hn 2, Hn 3, Hn 4 and the violas all strike D4.
  - The quarter-note layer has disappeared from 130.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 249-250 and 257: two like instruments at the unison "thicken" the sound, "muddy" the overtones and "usually only result in a stronger pitch". Octave coupling is the better reinforcement. PDF 142-143 (Beethoven 7/ii): the background keeps "its own characteristic rhythm". PDF 144 no. 4: the countertheme must differ rhythmically from the theme.
- **Mahler comparison.** He doubles horns for weight only in the chorale, never on an inner entry like this one.
- **Answer to c154.** c154's reason was that two horns in unison equal one trumpet above mf. The head already has the violas plus Hn 2 (Rimsky's "horns doubled by divided violas", c151's own source), so one horn is enough. Restoring the pulse makes the entry stand out by rhythm rather than by mass. This partly undoes c154. It keeps c151 (Hn 2), c157 (Hn 3 held D4) and c158's mf.
- **Fix (dev.ily, devHnFour):** `b4\p\< b4 b4 b4 | d'2->(\mf\< a4. b8 | c'4-.\f) b8( a8 g4-.) fis4-. | g4->` → `b4\p\< b4 b4 b4 | a4\mf\< a4 a4 a4 | a1\f | b4->`. Hn 4's dynamics match Hn 3's on the shared staff.

### 2. 231-233: two portato pulses in the plagal close (applied)
- **Problem.** c153 gave the violas portato quarters (A3-G3-A♭3) inside the f brass. c154 found them unheard and gave the same rhythm to Vln II (F5-E5-F5) but left the violas pulsing too. The result is one background rhythm doubled two octaves apart. The violas' copy lies inside Hn 3-4 and Tbn 1-2 and adds only bow noise.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 144 no. 2 ("thinning the counterpoint to let the main theme break through"). PDF 154 (Tchaikovsky: the texture is thinned by the scoring). PDF 142 (each layer has one rhythm and one articulation).
- **Mahler comparison.** He states a background figure once, in one register.
- **Answer to c153.** c153's purpose, a pulse through the four whole-note bars until the harp at 235, is kept by Vln II.
- **Fix (arrival.ily, arrivalVla):** `a4--(\f a4-- a4-- a4--) | g4--( g4-- g4-- g4--) | aes4--(\> aes4-- aes4-- aes4--)` → `a1\f | g1 | aes1\>` (v149).

### 3. 205-206: the violas' repeated chord sits on the horns' head (applied)
- **Problem.** All four horns (a2, f) and Tbn 1 an octave lower play F1's head A♭4-E♭4-F4-G♭4-F4-E♭4-D♭4-C4. c157 moved the violas' repeated chord from E♭4/A♭4 to A♭4/C5. They still strike A♭4, the head's first and accented note, six times a bar at f, at the horns' own pitch.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 144 no. 3: separate theme and accompaniment by register. PDF 241-243 (Figaro): the sustained harmony is placed at a distance from the theme. Sebesky's focus rule, which c157 cited, points the same way.
- **Fix (codaOne.ily, codaOneVla, bars 7-8 of the section):** `<aes' c''>` → `<c'' es''>` (C5/E♭5), wholly above the head. 207-210 are unchanged. No new parallels.

### 4. Mahler's simplicity at the climax: no further additions at 223-234 (no change)
- **Assessment.** 223-230 are now arranged the way Mahler arranges his chorale: one tremolo layer (Vln I·II 223-225), one moving middle layer (violas' portato 224-228), block brass and a bass. With Finding 2 applied, the plagal close has one pulse.
- **Recommendation.** No further inner layers should be added in 223-234. The arrival is protected, and Adler PDF 255 says the melody "must come through" before anything else.
- **v149 check.** The changes since v149 that sit inside the protected arrival (c149 tremolo 224-225, c154 C at 229) leave the chorale top line and the canon untouched.

### 5. 209-211: the c151/c156 suspension is an improvement; the 211 beat-4 C6 is kept (no change)
- **What is there.** Vln I G♭6 tied into 211 and resolving to F6 fixes a real fault in v149, which sounded the resolution F a bar early (A&S Unit 21, as c151 said). Vln I's C6 on 211 beat 4 still rubs against the flutes' D♭6 (a2). The same rub was in v149.
- **Decision.** It is a passing note in the violins' descent G♭-F-D♭-C to the theme's F6 at 212, and I keep it.
