## Findings

### 1. Bars 160→161: parallel 5ths in IV–V (applied)

**Problem.** In the recapitulation, 160 moves iv(7) to V at 161. Oboe 2 holds A♭4 for the second half of 160 and falls to G4 on the downbeat of 161. The tune's upper octave (Fl 1·2 and Vln I upper) moves E♭5→D5 at the same moment. The result is A♭4–E♭5 to G4–D5, parallel perfect 5ths between two sustained, audible voices.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 136 §3 says IV and V share no common tone, so the upper voices must move against the bass or parallels come easily. Ex. 9-3 shows the standard contrary layout.
- The review list at PDF 151, point 2, repeats the warning for IV–V.
- PDF 138 adds that the upper voices of the predominant should move down toward 7̂ or across to 2̂, not in parallel with the melody.

This pair was not raised before. The 151–166 layout protected by c134, c167 and c169 concerns the scoring and the tune, not this inner pitch.

**Fix (applied).** In /tmp/critic-192/work/sections/recP.ily, `recPOboeTwoDiv` bar 160 changes from `f'2( aes'2)` to `f'2( c''2)`. Oboe 2 now moves F4–C5 and then leaps C5→G4 to the root of V. Against E♭5→D5 that is a 6th to a 4th, not a 5th to a 5th. The change keeps the slur, the dynamic (no new dynamic, so the part-combined oboe staff still has equal simultaneous dynamics), the bar length and the harmony (C is in iv7). A comment citing the reading was added on line 19.
