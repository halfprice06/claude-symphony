## Findings

### 1. 43-50: the dominant pedal before the trumpet solo has no upper octaves (applied)

Problem. This passage is the score's closest match to the Mahler opening that Casanelles analyses (PDF 271-273, Fig. 6.1): soft solo winds over a held dominant pedal, then the movement's first trumpet sound, soft. In Mahler the pedal is spread over the full range, up to the violins' high harmonics, so the soft brass and winds sound inside a defined space. Here the sustained parts are B♭1 (Cb), B♭2 (Vc), A♭3 (Bn 1, then Cl 2), D4 (Vla), F4 (Vln II), with the harp's B♭4 harmonic struck on beat 1 of 43, 45 and 47 and Ob 1 A♭4 at 49. These are partials 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 of B♭1, but nothing sustains above B♭4. Vln I rests from 43 to 66. Rimsky PDF 85 says chords follow the harmonic scale and "the harmonic notes must not be lacking in the upper parts". The three motive-x runs (Fl 1 up to F5, Cl 1 up to B♭4, Ob 1 up to G♭5) pass through an empty upper register. The harp harmonic also decays at once, so its chime has no sustain.

Fix (applied in trans.ily). Vln I hold B♭5, the 16th partial, pp sul tasto, 43 to 50 beat 3, and are released with the others. Sul tasto is the acoustic filter Casanelles lists with the mute (PDF 286); a mute is not possible because Vln I play up to 42. The harp harmonic on 43.1 now gives the attack that the pp sul tasto entry lacks, and the violins give the chime a sustain (PDF 275-276, 269-270). The pedal now runs B♭1 to B♭5, all the runs move inside it (highest G♭5), and the trumpet still enters at 51 on its own, since the violins stop with the strings on 50 beat 4.
```
transVlnOne = { bes''1\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto"~\cbaw | bes''1~ | bes''1~ | bes''1~ | bes''1~ | bes''1~ | bes''1~ | bes''2. r4 | }
```
sTwo.ily, 67: Vln I's mark becomes `"ord., cantabile"`. Vln I's rest before F2 in the violins is now 16 bars instead of 24, which still keeps their entry at 67 fresh. Not affected: trumpets tacet to 50; tremolo and C7 only from 223; the B♭ pedal key plan; the solo 51-66.

### 2. 151-164: held f trombones cover the recapitulation's clarinet and horn accompaniment (applied)

Problem. The recapitulated F1 adds Tbn 1·2 holding the harmony f (C4/E♭3, D3-F3, B3-D3 and so on) to the exposition's layers. The trombones sit in the same octave as the clarinets' leggiero broken chords (Cl 2 G3-E♭4, Cl 1 E♭4-C5, f) and Hn 3·4's f off-beats (G3-C4). By Rimsky PDF 51, in forte one trombone equals two horns equals four clarinets, so the two sustained trombones outweigh both moving layers in that octave. Casanelles says sounds in one range mask each other (PDF 286-287), and loud brass are harmonically dense and come to the foreground (PDF 288-289). The masking scan flags every bar from 151 to 158 (clarinet notes with louder trombones within a sixth below). c166 already put the trumpets mf under Hn 3·4 f on the same reasoning (Rimsky PDF 41). The trombones were left at f.

Fix (applied in recP.ily). Tbn 1·2 mf at 151 (the trumpets' level from 159), crescendo on 165 and f on the cadence chord 166. The bass trombone stays f with the bass. No note changed. F1's notes, its bowing and the peak at 162 (the reasons this passage is protected) are untouched.

recPTbnTwo = { es1\mf | ... | d1 | d1\< | c2\f r2 | R1*4 | }

### 3. 215-218: a minor third at the bottom of the brass chord (applied)

Problem. Under theme B's close and the horn echo, the low brass hold tuba F1, bass trombone F2, Tbn 2 A♭2 and Tbn 1 F3 for four bars (ff > f > mp). A♭2 lies a minor third above F2, about 87 and 104 Hz. The low partials of F1 are F2, C3 and F3 (Casanelles PDF 274; Rimsky PDF 85: widely spaced intervals belong in the low part of the chord). The harmonic scale gives the third no place below A3. Bars 223 and 278 use exactly this layout (C1-C2-C3-E3-G3), so 215-218 is the movement's one sustained brass chord that breaks its own rule. A♭ is not needed there: Bn 1 A♭3, the horns, trumpets and violas carry it.

Fix (applied in codaTwo.ily). Tbn 2 keeps C3 from 214 at 215 (F minor 7: F2-C3-F3, partials 2-3-4), rises to D♭3 for the D♭ 6/3 chord at 216-218, and falls to C3 at 219. This follows the movement's rule that every doubled D♭ falls at 219. 218→219 is contrary motion to the bass F→A♭. Dynamics and breath unchanged.
```
codaTwoTbnTwo = { des1\ff\> | des1\f | des1 | c1 \breathe | c1 | des1 | des1~\> | des1\mp \breathe | c1\p | ...
```
This is inside the 216-223 do-not-touch range (c199/c200). That range protects the D♭-C-B-C top line, the violas' D♯→E and the 222→223 resolution. This change touches none of them, and the D♭3 is an octave below the echo's D♭4 at mp, under the "loudest in its register" echo (c108).
