## Findings

### 1. 217-218: the tuba plays 211-228 without a rest (applied)

**Problem.** In the coda the tuba plays every bar from 199 to 228. Its only rests are the half bar at 204 and a quarter at 210. From 211 it holds D♭1-G♭1-A♭1-F1 ff to mp under the trumpets' theme B. It goes straight into the sub. p A♭1 at 219 and the crescendo to the fff C1 at 223, then holds the chorale bass to 228. That is about 33 s (12 bars at 2 = 72, then 6 at 2 = 56) with breath marks only, at 214, 218, 220 and 225. In 215-218 it holds F1, the bass of the D♭ 6/3 chord, four bars at the bottom of its range while the music thins to mp under the horns' f echo.

**Evidence.** Adler on the tuba: "It takes a great deal of breath to play this largest of all brass instruments, and special care must be exercised not to tire out the performer. Frequent rests are strongly recommended between passages" (adler2 PDF 8). For all brass: "allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath ... it does take more breath to play loudly than softly" (adler1 PDF 292). The score already applies this to the horns at exactly this point: Hn 1·2 rest 211-214 (c046) and Hn 3·4 rest 215-218 (c136). The tuba is the only bass brass player with no rest of a bar or more in this stretch. The F1 is not needed in the tuba at 217-218. The contrabassoon holds F1, and the bass trombone, double basses and bassoon 2 hold F2. The echo is marked weaker than the statement, and its harmonic basis is meant to be softer (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129, 133, cited by c108). A lighter bass therefore helps the echo come through. 215 keeps the tuba's F1 for c264's F1-F2-C3-F3 layout.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-406/work/sections/codaTwo.ily).**

```
codaTwoTuba = { des,1\ff\> | des,1\f | ges,,1 | aes,,1 \breathe | f,,1 | f,,1\> | R1\! | R1 | aes,,1\p | g,,1 \breathe | g,,1\< | d,1\mf\< | }
```

The tuba was `f,,1 | f,,1~\> | f,,1\mp \breathe | aes,,1\p` and now fades through 216, rests 217-218 (3.3 s), and re-enters p on A♭1 at 219 with the bass trombone, the contrabassoon and the strings. Its longest stretches are now 199-216 (about 27 s, with the short rests at 204 and 210) and 219-228 (about 20 s). The bass trombone keeps its line, because it is the trombones' bass. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes in any other part. The 205-223 dynamic path, the sub. p at 219, 222→223 and the arrival are untouched.
