## Findings

### 1. 199-204: the hammered tutti lacks movement I's bowing (applied)
Problem. 199-204 is movement I's 27-32 with the same pitches, and it is an owed item ("the hammered tutti in movement I's layout"). In v218 the strings carry no bow signs there. By the default Adler states on PDF 38 (an anacrusis is taken up-bow), 199's eighth pickup G is an up-bow, so the accented E♭6/E♭5/E♭4 that follows under the slur also falls on an up-bow. With plain alternation, 204's second accented quarter (B/D–F/G) also falls on an up-bow.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38 and Ex. II-19: two up-bows are hooked so that the accent lands on a down-bow. Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus): mark a down-bow so the next vigorous attack is solid. PDF 42-43, Ex. II-29: a run of heavy attacks is played as a series of lifted down-bows. Movement I marks exactly this at I 27-32: `g''8\downbow`, `b''8\upbow`, `c'''4->\downbow b''4->\downbow`. The finale already does it at 247-254 (c186). c175 called these signs "not needed". Since then c186 bowed 247-254 this way, so v218 bows the major statement but not the minor one. The source in movement I marks them as well.
Fix (codaOne.ily):
- Vln I: `r8\! g''8\downbow(\ff es'''4.->)` … `c'''4--) b''8\upbow( c'''8)` … `c'''4->\downbow b''4->\downbow r2`. Vln II and Vla take the same signs an octave and two octaves lower.
- The Vla's 200 up-bow is set below the note: `b8_\upbow(`. Set above, it triggers LilyPond's "minimise_least_squares" programming error, the same spacing error c186 met at Vc 248.
- Vc: `g4->\downbow g,4->\downbow r2`. Cb: `g,,4->\downbow g,,4->\downbow r2`.
- 201's pickup is left unmarked because it comes on a down-bow by alternation, as at 249.

### 2. 90-94: the codetta's ff hammer strokes alternate bows (applied)
Problem. At 90 the violins play two accented quarters, F6/F5, and go straight into the ff codetta at 91 with no rest. The codetta has accented quarters on beats 1 and 3, with an eighth pickup or a rest between them. By plain alternation these accents fall on up-bows: 91.1 (after 90's two quarters), 92.1, 93.3 and 94.1. This passage is the exposition's loudest hammered moment, and it is played half at the light end of the bow.
Evidence. Adler PDF 42: "A very heavy and vigorous feeling is commonly achieved by a series of down-bows … with the bow raised between each down-bow … most often performed at the frog." Ex. II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii 108-112) marks a down-bow on every ff stroke. Ex. II-28 (Gluck) is the same heavy-stroke idea, marked "au talon". PDF 38: an eighth pickup is taken up-bow. The figure at 90 (two accented quarters) is the figure that movement I bows as two down-bows at I 32. The finale bows it the same way at 204 after finding 1.
Fix (clos.ily): `\downbow` on every accented quarter in Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb from 90 beat 3 to 94. In Vln I that is `f'''4->\downbow f''4->\downbow\! | g'''4->\downbow\ff r8 g'''8 aes'''4->\downbow r4 | g'''4->\downbow r8 g'''8 aes'''4->\downbow r4 | es'''4->\downbow r4 d'''4->\downbow r4 | es'''2->\downbow r2`. The other parts take the same signs on their own notes. The eighth pickups are unmarked, so they are up-bows. I did not add the words "au talon" or "al tallone": the down-bow series already asks for the frog stroke, and the score uses no French or Italian bowing-position terms elsewhere. c167/c168 protected the codetta's layout. Notes, dynamics, double stops and "non div." are unchanged, and the page breaks did not move.

### 3. 167-171: cellos and basses reach the pizz. at the tip (applied)
Problem. At 171 the cellos and basses start pizz. pp, a quarter rest (0.36 s at 2 = 84) after the held B at 169-170. By alternation (A♭ at 167 down after the half rest, D at 168 up, B at 169-170 down), the B ends on a down-bow at the tip. The players then have to bring the hand back and pluck in a third of a second. In the same situation at 114 → 115, the score marks `\upbow` (c175).
Evidence. Adler PDF 54: going from arco to pizzicato "is much easier … if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog". PDF 38: a diminuendo lies naturally on a down-bow.
Fix (recP.ily): `aes,1\f\upbow` (Vc) and `aes,,1\f\upbow` (Cb) at 167. The diminuendo at 168 is then a down-bow, which suits it, and the held B at 169-170 is an up-bow ending at the frog. c214 protected the order of the diminuendo at 167-170. It is unchanged; only one bow sign is added in each part.

### 4. 9-10: the dominant crescendo starts down-bow (applied)
Problem. At 9 the violas (D4) and basses (G1) enter after two bars of rest. Each holds a tied note mf < to the accented f on 10 beat 3. Players take a downbeat after rests down-bow, so the crescendo runs toward the tip and the f accent comes on an up-bow.
Evidence. Adler PDF 38: "The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … the composer … should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players." The score does this at 135 (`\upbow` under the pp crescendo, c018) and at 197-198. PDF 39 warns that the bass bow is shorter. Two and a half bars at mf < is within one up-bow at this tempo.
Fix (intro.ily): `d'1~\mf\<\upbow` (Vla) and `g,,1\mf\<\upbow~` (Cb). The accent at 10.3 then comes on a fresh down-bow. Bars 1-6 are not touched.
