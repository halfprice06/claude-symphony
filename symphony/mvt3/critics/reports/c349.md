**Bruckner 4/iv.** I compared the score with Bruckner's Fourth Symphony finale, as I know the score, together with the programme notes listed at the end.

- The two finales share the main moves: a quiet lyrical second group over pizzicato strings, brass chorales, cyclic recalls of earlier movements, and a coda that ends on the symphony's opening material in the major.
- In handling instrumental colour, the comparison shows one weakness. Bruckner's second-group melodies pass from instrument to instrument and are doubled only phrase by phrase. This finale's C-minor F2 holds one mixed colour, cor anglais plus muted trumpet, unbroken for 16 bars (Finding 1).
- Bruckner's coda saves a single long crescendo for the end. This coda has four ff/fff peaks in 56 bars (223, 247, 263, 278). That is closer to Brahms 1/iv, the movement's declared model, and the coda's plan is protected, so I make no finding about it.
- A conductor would accept the exposition, the development and the C-major coda as professional work in Brahms's manner.

## Findings

### 1. 171–186: the cor anglais and the muted trumpet play in unison for all 16 bars (applied)

**Problem.** The recapitulated F2 is one mixed colour from its first note to its last. Nothing in the passage changes colour until the flute joins at 181.

**Evidence.** Rimsky, PDF 140–141: doubling of a solo line "is only permissible in isolated phrases". Unbroken duplication "replaces by a mixed timbre the rare characteristic qualities" of the solo, and doubling limited to a few special phrases "supports the voice and endows it with beauty and colour". The examples do the same:

- Ex. 289 (PDF 467): the cor anglais joins the voice "colla voce" for chosen phrases only.
- Ex. 284 (PDF 457): the second instrument is an obbligato, not a permanent unison.

The cyclic sources point the same way:

- cyclic-notes.md only asks that on a repeat the solo "could be a muted trumpet doubling the cor anglais", so the cor anglais is the main voice and the trumpet is the doubler.
- Movement I's coda (171–174), which this passage answers, sets the cor anglais beside a different instrument with a line of its own, not in a 16-bar unison.

**Fix (applied).** The muted trumpet rests during F2's second phrase, so 175–178 is the cor anglais alone. This is the only phrase of F2 the cor anglais ever has to itself; its pitches, F4–C4–D4, lie in its best register. The trumpet comes back at 179 for the climb to the peak, where Fl 1 joins at 181–183. The colour then builds phrase by phrase: CA + Tpt, CA alone, CA + Tpt, CA + Tpt + Fl. The timpani's pp motive x at 178 now falls in the cor anglais's breath.

In recS.ily, recSTptOne:

```
| aes'2 g'2) \breathe | R1*4 | g'2.(\p-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." es'4 | d'4 c'4 es'4 g'4\< | ...
```

This replaces `f'2.( aes'4 | g'4 f'4 es'4 d'4 | es'2 c'2 | d'1) \breathe | g'2.( es'4 | ...`.

The cor anglais, the accompaniment, the dynamics, the "con sord." and "togli sord." marks, and the timpani are unchanged.

**Protection.** Cycles 4, 19 and 42 put "muted trumpet with cor anglais 171–186" on the do-not-touch list without stating a reason beyond the sound itself. The pairing stays in 12 of the 16 bars, including the first phrase and the peak. The pp accompaniment that later lists protect is not touched.

**Build.** 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, record 42/42. par8.py on 170–187 shows only the false positives that v349 already had.

**For the other threads.** No theme changes: F2's notes are unchanged and the cor anglais still plays all of them. finale.md item 25 (the cor anglais link with movement I 171–174) is still true. Add one line: "since v350 the muted trumpet rests 175–178, the cor anglais alone".

### 2. No further changes

I looked for other places to apply the reading. Every other place where it suggests a change is protected, and in each case the score already follows the principle or the gain would be small:

- **51–66:** Cl 1's pp E♭4 is inside the trumpet solo's register (table, row 6). At pp it cannot cover the trumpet.
- **219–222:** the oboes play in unison on one harmonic part (row 7). This sits inside the protected 219–223 crescendo, and a second oboe re-entering at 221 would change that crescendo's scoring.
- **115–122:** the bassoon solo has no obbligato instrument beside it (row 15). These bars are protected, and the Vln I answers at 119 and 122 already apply row 18.

I applied none of these. One more is worth recording rather than changing: the coda's several peaks set against Bruckner's single long crescendo (see above). The coda's plan is protected.

**Do not touch:** the arrival and chorale at 223–234 with 222→223, the soft trumpet solo at 51–66 with its pizzicato accompaniment, and the C-major close at 273–279.
