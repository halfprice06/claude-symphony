## Findings

### 1. 211–214, Vln I: theme B's crescendo is on a down-bow and its ff peak on an up-bow, the reverse of Vln II and the violas (applied)

**Problem.** Vln I's ff G♭6 at 210.3 is a down-bow (c418) and is tied into 211, so the down-bow continues. The slur G♭–F then takes 211.1–2 on the same down-bow, the D♭6 half note is up-bow, and the bars of theme B follow:
- 212 (f < ) on a down-bow,
- 213, the ff A♭6 peak (c338's swell), on an up-bow,
- 214 (>) on a down-bow.

Vln II has an odd number of strokes in 211 (half, quarter, quarter after a rest), so it plays 212 up-bow, 213 down-bow and 214 up-bow. Since c461 the upper half of Vln II doubles theme B one octave below Vln I. The violas' louré (one bow per bar from 211) also gives 212 up-bow and 213 down-bow. So the two violin sections bow the same tune in opposite directions, and Vln I has its bows the wrong way round.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: "The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural... there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow". One plays "louder and heavier toward the frog".
- Adler PDF 38, Ex. II-19 and II-20: mark the bow so that the strong attack gets the down-bow.

**Fix.** Change bow on the tied note at the barline, where the Largamente, the key change and the trumpets' entry already fall:

```
codaTwoVlnOne = { ges'''4\ff\>\upbow( f'''4) des'''2 | f'''2.(\f\< ges'''4) | ...
```

The bowing is now:
- 211: G♭–F up-bow (diminuendo, 0.8 s), and theme B's goal note D♭6 on a fresh down-bow;
- 212: crescendo on the up-bow;
- 213: the ff A♭6 down-bow at the frog;
- 214: diminuendo on the up-bow;
- 215: the new rising phrase on a down-bow.

All three upper string lines now bow alike. Notes, slurs, dynamics, the D♭–C–B–C top line and the 205–223 dynamic path are unchanged. The engraving check at 110 dpi shows the up-bow sign at 211, clear of "Largamente" and the slur.

### 2. 212–213, cellos: a wind phrase slur over two Largamente bars at f (applied)

**Problem.** c517 gave the cellos Bn 1's counter line "with its slurs". At 212–213 that is the bassoon's breath slur, A♭3–G♭3–F3–E♭3 | B♭3–G♭3: six notes over two bars at 2 = 72, about 3.3 s on one cello bow at f, starting on a down-bow. The longest held notes of the counter (B♭3–G♭3 at 213) would come at the tip of a long down-bow. That is where the stroke is weakest and where it tends to fade (PDF 38), just as the counter answers theme B's held notes.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts... The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow".
- Adler PDF 39: at forte, fewer notes to a bow (Ex. II-22), and "the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter".

**Fix.** One bar per bow, as the violins and violas have here:

```
codaTwoVc = { des2\ff\> des'2 | aes4(\f ges4 f4 es4) | bes2( ges2) | aes4( bes4 c'2) | ...
```

Bn 1 and Hn 3·4 keep the phrase slur, which is their breath. The pitches, the f, and c517's scoring are unchanged. This is not churn: it corrects how c517's new line is notated for strings and keeps the line.
