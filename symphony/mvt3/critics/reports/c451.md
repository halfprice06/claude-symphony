## Findings

### 1. Bar 69: the bass now passes through B♭ in 10ths with the tune (applied)

- **Problem.** In 69 the violins sing F6 (an appoggiatura), then E♭6 and the passing D6, into C6 at 70. Under them the basses, Bn 2 and the harp's left hand held C2 for the whole bar and then leapt down a 3rd to A♭1 at 70. The harmony is IV6 → IV, one chord. So the tune's only quarter-note passing motion in the bar moved over a still bass. The cello counter-melody had finished its move (A♭–B♭–C) on beat 3.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 332, Ex. 20-7a: a passing tone "fills in the interval of a 3rd ... within one chord". The example is a bass that steps through the 3rd as the chord moves to its other position. PDF 336, sec. 9 and Ex. 20-16: "Note the passing motions in parallel 10ths between the bass and alto ... These create an excellent effect ... as long as they are not overdone". PDF 345, exercise 1e: add "accented and normal passing tones in complementary rhythms to the outer voices".
- **Fix.** The bass falls C2 (69.1–3) – B♭1 (69.4) – A♭1 (70). B♭1 sounds a 10th under the tune's D6 and moves with it into C6/A♭1. The line continues as the bar's descending tetrachord C–B♭–A♭–G under the tune's F–E♭–D–C–B♭. In sections/sTwo.ily, bar 69:
  - `sTwoCb`: `c,1` → `c,2. bes,,4`
  - `sTwoBsnTwo`: `c1` → `c2. bes,4`
  - `sTwoHarpL`: `c,1` → `c,2. bes,,4` (B♭ is already flat in the 67 pedal setting, so no pedal change)
- **Check.** The cellos rise A♭3–B♭3–C4 against the falling bass, in contrary motion. The C4s held in cellos, violas, Hn 1 and Bn 1 make a passing 9th over B♭ on the weak beat. par8.py on 68–71 reports nothing new except Bn 2 doubling the basses. analysis.txt is unchanged except note counts and the 69–72 density line. The tune, the counter-melody, dynamics, and the recapitulation's 189 (inside the protected 187–198) are unchanged. 69 is outside the 77–82 protection (c167, c187).
- **Status.** Applied in /tmp/critic-451/work. The build has 0 warnings, 16 pages, and the theme record is 51/51. No theme, quotation or record line changed, so the other movement threads need no notice.

### 2. Bar 77.4: a passing B♭ toward the climax (tested, declined)

- **Candidate.** The bass rises G–A♭ at 77, then leaps to C under the B♭6 climax at 78. A passing B♭ at 77.4 would give a rising IV–V–vi bass, A♭–B♭–C, like the solo's G♭–A♭–B♭–C♭ at 61–63.
- **Why not.** The tune moves E♭6 → F6 at 77.3→77.4. Over A♭1 → B♭1 that makes parallel 5ths in the outer voices. F6 over B♭ is a consonant tone, so A&S PDF 341 applies: "5ths produced by such a consonant tone ... are more problematic ... and you should avoid them". PDF 341–342 and Ex. 20-26 add that a passing tone cannot correct them.
- **Decision.** The leap A♭→C stays.

### 3. Double neighbour in a principal tune (considered, not added)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 20-12 (PDF 334) is the one figure the tunes do not use; the score has it only in an inner voice at 168. The one unprotected place where it could decorate a held tune note is the violins' F5 tied over 73→74.
- **Why not.** There the violins hold while the counter-melody moves B♭–A♭–F–G (c421). That is the complementary rhythm of Ex. 20-16, and a double neighbour in the tune would cover it.
- **Other places.** The trumpet solo (51–66) and 187–198 are protected. F2's violin statement already has one figure per phrase. A&S's warning against overdoing figuration (PDF 336) applies.
- **Decision.** No change.
