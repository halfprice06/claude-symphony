## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 43-50: the dominant pedal is two octaves of B♭ with a seventh above it (applied)

**Problem.** Over the transition's dominant pedal the cellos (B♭2) and basses (B♭1) hold the same pitch class for eight bars, pp. The next note up is the held A♭3 (Bn 1, then Cl 2 from 47), a minor seventh above the cellos; the fifth F first sounds at F4 (Vln II). So the low-middle register of this V7 is empty while the root is doubled. Sebesky's rule 7 (PDF 140): "the least important note of a string voicing is the chordal root ... Many arrangers make a habit of automatically doubling the bass notes with the lowest cello ... this is usually unnecessary and sometimes can be detrimental to the overall sound, 'anchoring' or 'grounding' it." PDF 147 on the gap: "Had I omitted the E♭ in the cellos, the gap of a seventh between the cellos (A♭ and B♭) would have diluted the sonority of the overall string ensemble sound." PDF 145-146: the second cello adds a chord tone "for temporary richness and depth ... but not to reinforce the bass". His note on PDF 139 (the basses cannot sustain a line alone in performance) means the cellos should not all leave the B♭.

This is the technique the score uses only once, at the arrival (223, cellos div. C3/G3): the second cello on a chord tone above the bass. At 43-50 it fills the gap with the fifth, which is also the third partial of the basses' B♭1, so it fits c264's reason for the Vln I B♭5 (the dominant heard as one spectrum).

**Harmony check.** F3 is a chord tone of the V7 and V9 at 43-46 and 49-50. Under the iv7 colour at 47-48 (violas C♭4, Vln II G♭4, A♭3/A♭4 in the winds) it sounds with the B♭ as a double pedal, as the horn's stopped D4 does at 49-50. The note is held throughout, so it makes no parallels, and the outer voices (Vln I B♭5 over the basses' B♭1) are unchanged.

**Fix (applied).** trans.ily:
```
transVc = { <bes, f>1\pp^\markup \italic "div."~\cbaw | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>2. r4 | }
```
sOne.ily, 51: `sOneVc = { es4\pp^\markup \italic "unis., pizz."` (was "pizz."), so the cellos know they rejoin. The violas' 46-49 line, the harp harmonics and the Vln I B♭5 (protected) are unchanged.

### 2. 83-90: two flutes on the violins' E♭6 at the violins' level (applied)

**Problem.** The closing group opens with F1's head in E♭ major: Vln I on E♭6 with Fl 1·2 in unison, both f, then the run at 87 in Fl 1 and Vln I, f. Sebesky PDF 164: "The addition of a single flute to the lead violin line of a string ensemble thickens the string tone considerably, sacrificing purity for extra body ... To compensate somewhat for the difference in tonal weight, I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings." Here there are two flutes, in their strong third octave, at the same level as the first violins, so the top octave sounds as a flute line with violins. The score already applies this rule at the other string-led statements: c167 at 78 (winds mf under the violins' f), c067 at 129, and Fl 1 at 35 is f under the violins' ff. Only 83-90 was left at equal level.

**Fix (applied).** clos.ily: 83 `es'''2->(\mf` in Fl 1 and Fl 2 (were `\f`; equal on the shared staff); 87 Fl 1 `r4 bes''8(\mf` (was `\f`). The crescendo at 89 to ff at 91 is unchanged, so the flutes reach the codetta with everyone. Ob 1 and Cl 1·2 on Vln II's E♭5 octave stay f: they carry the tune's lower octave against the horns' pedal, which Sebesky treats as an ensemble voicing (Ex. 214), not as a flute thickening a violin line.
