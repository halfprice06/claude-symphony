**Holistic verdict.** Dvořák's finale keeps D minor until ten bars from the end and then closes on a plagal, chromatic bass (G♯–G–D) under held tremolo chords and four short fz chords. This movement turns to C major 57 bars before the end, in several stages. Its close (273–279: F♯°7 – iv6/4 – I over the C pedal) has the same plagal, chromatic shape as Dvořák's Molto maestoso, and its final-chord layout is as well spaced as his.

Where it does not yet read like a master is the Allegro molto, 247–262:
- the two combined heads share a register;
- the last chorale statement is eight bars of whole notes in nearly every part.

Dvořák's Poco animato (410–415) puts the tune in long notes over arpeggiated eighths in Vln II, Vla and Vc. That is also Rimsky's tutti form (Ex. 291).

The coda has eight tempo words in its last 69 bars, against Dvořák's two. Each one has a recorded reason in HISTORY (cycles 9, 13, 38, 39), so I am not proposing a change.

## Findings

### 1. 247–254: the P head's lower octave crosses the trumpets' S head and makes seconds with it (applied)

**Bars:** 247–254, Ob 1·2 and Vln II.

**Problem:** Movement I's P head is doubled at G4–A5 in Ob 1·2 and Vln II. That is the register of the S head in Tpt 1·2 (C5–C6). The two heads cross for eight bars, sometimes in unison and sometimes a second apart:
- 247: Ob D♭5 against Tpt 2 C5;
- 249: Ob F5 against Tpt 1 E5;
- 250: Ob D5 against Tpt 2 E5;
- 251: Ob B4 against Tpt 2 C5.

Where they meet in unison (249, 252–253), the two themes merge into one line.

**Evidence:**
- Rimsky PDF 166 and 157: parts keep the natural order of register, and crossing is brief and only to bring a line forward.
- Ex. 309–310 (PDF 499–500, text PDF 165): the same music in a new register has its doublings redistributed.
- c047 removed exactly this defect at 265–268, the second combined statement. The first statement is the one heard at the climax, and it kept the defect.

**Fix:** Vln II takes Vln I's line, an octave up, so the P head is in Fl 1·2, Vln I and Vln II at G5–A6. The oboes cannot go up an octave: the line reaches G6 and A6 at 252–254, above the oboe's usable ff range. So Ob 1 joins Tpt 1 and Ob 2 joins Tpt 2 on the S head. That is the oboe-with-trumpet pairing that c044 used at 257–264.

### 2. 255–262: the last chorale statement is static; Vln II should break its chords into eighths (applied)

**Bars:** 255–262, Vln II.

**Problem:** The chorale's last statement is in doubled values at Allegro molto. Brass, wood-wind, Vln I, Vln II and cellos all hold whole notes for eight bars. Only the violas (quarters), the basses (eighths) and the Hn 3/Tpt 3 half notes move. Vln II adds nothing: it holds chord notes the brass already hold.

**Evidence:**
- Rimsky PDF 144 and Ex. 289–291 (PDF 467–470): in the tutti form of a broad melody the tune sits on top, the brass sustain, and the middle strings run.
- Dvořák 7/iv, Poco animato 410–413 (corpus PDF 204): Vln I has the theme in half and whole notes over slurred eighth arpeggios in Vln II, Vla and Vc. This is the same situation, a long-note theme in a fast coda, written by the assigned master.

**Fix:** Slurred four-note arpeggios, kept below the chorale's top line, with the crescendo from 259 as before:

```lilypond
| e'8(\ff g' c'' e'') g''( e'' c'' g') | e'8( g' c'' e'') g''( e'' c'' g')
| a'8( c'' e'' a'') e''( c'' a' e') | a'8( c'' e'' a'') e''( c'' a' c'')
| aes'8(\< d'' f'' aes'') f''( d'' aes' f') | aes'8( d'' f'' aes'') f''( d'' aes' d'')
| aes'8( c'' es'' fis'') aes''( fis'' es'' c'') | g'8( b' d'' f'') g''( f'' d'' b') |
```

My first draft started 257 on E4 and made fifths with Hn 4 (C–G → A–E). Starting 257 on A4 removes them. The joins 258→259 and 260→261 are written to avoid octaves with the bass (A→F, F→A♭). Untouched: the chorale's top line, the violas' quarters (c017), Hn 3/Tpt 3 (c019, c048), and the wood-wind rest at 255–256 with mf at 257 (c038).

### 3. 235–238: the bassoons double the cellos and horns under the violins' p cantabile (applied)

**Bars:** 235–238, Bn 1·2.

**Problem:** Under the violins' molto cantabile S head (p, sul A), the following parts all sustain at the same time:
- Bn 2 plays the cello line note for note;
- Bn 1 doubles Hn 3 (G3, A3, then A3/G3);
- Hn 3·4 hold the chord;
- the cellos move, and the basses hold a four-bar C pedal;
- the timpani roll and the harp arpeggiates.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 138–139 names "strings doubled in the wood-wind" and "doubling of wood-wind and horns ... to form one harmonic part" as the combinations that cover a soft line. Under a soft melody the harmonic parts should be reduced, keeping the pedal (PDF 139). The bassoons add no pitch: the harmony stays complete in Hn 3·4, cellos, basses and harp.

**Fix:**

```lilypond
sHeadBsnOne = { R1*4 | c'1\f | ...
sHeadBsnTwo = { R1*4 | a,1\f | ...
```

The bassoons then rest 231–238 and enter f at 239 with the second phrase, which also gives them a fresher entry. The texture that c017 protected (tune, counter, bass, pedal) is unchanged; only a doubling goes.
