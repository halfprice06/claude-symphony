## Findings

### 1. Bar 189: chromatic voice exchange inside IV, with a French sixth on beat 2 (applied)

**Bars:** 189, in the recapitulation's A♭-major statement of F2.

**Problem.** Bar 188 already runs the bass down chromatically, A♭–G–G♭–F (c357, from Sebesky). The line then stops: the bass holds F1 for the whole of 189 and leaps to D♭ at 190. Meanwhile the counter-melody (cellos, Bn 1) rises D♭3–E♭3–F3. The bar is a static ii/IV6 under the tune's peak (B♭5–A♭5–G5).

A&S PDF 537 says an altered chord is least disruptive when its altered tone belongs to a chromatic line. PDF 563–564 (Ex. 31-17) shows the setting this bar is missing: IV6 → Ger./Fr. 6 → passing 6/4 → IV, built from contrary motion between the outer voices inside the subdominant.

The exposition counterpart (69) is diatonic, with the bass C–B♭–A♭ under a harp texture whose notes and pedals are protected. So this is the one place where the recapitulation can intensify the phrase's peak harmonically without repeating the exposition.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-522/work/sections/recS.ily; sounding pitch).**
- Cb: `\transpose c aes, { a,,1 }` → `f,,4 fes,,4 es,,4 des,,4`. 190 is unchanged (`des,2 c,2`).
- Bn 2: `\transpose c aes, { a,1 }` → `f,4 fes,4 es,4 des,4~`, tied into 190's D♭2.
- Vc: `\transpose c aes, { f4( g4 a2) }` → `des4( d4 es4 f4)`.
- Bn 1: the same line, with its slur continuing into 190 as before (`des4( d4 es4 f4 |`).
- Cl 2: `f1` → `f4 aes2.~`, tied into 190's A♭3.
- Cl 1: the D♭4 of the first half of 189 → D4 (`r8 bes8( d'8 bes8)`), so that it does not collide with the cellos' D♮. This is the collision A&S PDF 567–571 warns about.
- score.ly header: "C extension at 75–76, 166, 174–183 and 189".

**Result** (from the MIDI dump):
- 189.1 F / D♭ / B♭: IV6 (ii) as before.
- 189.2 F♭–D–A♭–B♭: a French sixth, which is V7♭5 of E♭ in 4/3 position (PDF 543, Ex. 30-18). The tune's held B♭5 is a chord tone and falls to A♭5 on beat 3.
- 189.3 E♭ / E♭ / A♭: the passing 6/4 at the octave point (PDF 566).
- 189.4 D♭–F–A♭ (G5 passing): root-position IV.

The counter-melody against the bass runs m6 – A6 – P8 – M10. This is the interval series of Ex. 31-19 (PDF 564–565), applied inside IV as in Ex. 31-17. The bass's line is now A♭–G–G♭–F–F♭–E♭–D♭ across 187–189, one chromatic descent from I to IV.

The F♭ resolves to E♭, as the cyclic rule requires. It also gives an early trace of the C♭/F♭ colour of the A♭-minor turn at 195–198. That turn's notes are unchanged.

**What is unchanged:**
- the tune (Vln I·II, Ob 1, Hn 1, violas);
- dynamics, hairpins, the sul A marking and the 187–198 bow plan;
- the harp rest;
- the scoring (no instrument added or removed).

HISTORY's "strings and bassoons 187–198" protection covers scoring, and c357 records that pitch changes inside it were accepted in c262, c270 and c293. c223's "189.1 passing 6/4" is not touched, because 189.1 keeps its F bass.

**Range.** The double basses go to E♭1 and D♭1 on the C extension. That is two more out-of-range flags in analysis.txt, both noted in the header, like 75–76.

**Verdict.** Better: the peak bar now has the chromatic tension the recapitulation's statement should have, and it comes from the voice leading, not from added doubling.
