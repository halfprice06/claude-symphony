## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–42: the cellos double the violins' low-register close an octave below (applied)

**Problem.** P2 ends at 39–42 with the violins descending from F5 (ff >) into their low register. At 41–42 both sections are in unison on F4–D4–E♭4–F4, dying to p. In v344 the cellos hold B♭2 from 39 to 42. Three other parts already hold B♭ there: the basses (B♭1, tied into 43 since c329), Bn 2 (B♭2) and Hn 2 (B♭3). The violins' last phrase is therefore unsupported in the register where they are weakest. Bar 43 then begins the woodwind scale figure (Fl → Cl → Ob) over the cellos' divided B♭2/F3.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 141, Ex. V-19 (Shostakovich 6/iii), describes this exact situation. As the violins come down into their low register, the cellos double them an octave below, which gives "greater resolution" to the line before a woodwind scale passage takes over. On the same page, Schumann 2/iii doubles violins whose register is nondescript. PDF 152 lists octave doubling by a lower string section as a standard way to strengthen a melody. The Tchaikovsky examples (PDF 152–154) show a diminuendo written into the scoring while the line keeps a doubling, and that is what c237's drop-outs need at this point.

**Answer to the protected reasons.**
- c254 rejected "cellos on the violins' descent at 39–42 (B♭ pedal; c237)". The B♭ pedal is intact. The basses keep B♭1 through 42 into 43, Bn 2 keeps B♭2 and Hn 2 keeps B♭3, and the cellos still hold B♭2 at 39–40.
- Only the low-register bars 41–42 change. These are the bars Ex. V-19 is about.
- c237's drop-outs are untouched. Their stated aim (HISTORY line 1040) was to stop covering the violins' F4 at the unison. The cellos sit an octave below at F3, so they reinforce the violins without covering them.
- The cello F3 at 42 is the upper note of the cellos' own divided B♭2/F3 at 43, so the doubling hands over into the transition without a leap.
- Fl 1's A♭5 (do-not-touch, lines 841 and 964) is not affected.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-344/work/sections/pTwo.ily, pTwoVc):**

```
% was:  \clef bass bes,1\f\> | bes,1 | bes,1 | bes,2.\p r4 | }
\clef bass bes,1\f\> | bes,1 | f2( d4. es8 | f2.)\p r4 | }
```

The f > p hairpin from 39 continues unchanged and ends at p on 42, the same as the violins, the violas and the basses. A c344 comment citing Adler PDF 141 is above the variable.

**Voice leading.** At 41.4 the cello's E♭3 moves to F3 while the violas fall from A♭3 to F3. That is a unison reached by contrary motion. The bass part (B♭1) does not move. The part-against-bass check (par8.py, 39–44) reports nothing, and its whole-score output is identical to v344's. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. act.py shows the cellos at D3–F3 at 41 and F3 at 42, under violins at D4–F4. The engraving checks out on page 3. Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages.

### 2. Bars 115–122: the third use of one accompaniment formula (not applied)

**Problem.** The bassoon solo's string background reuses the pizzicato on/off-beat formula of 51–66, which also appears in the restatement material. Adler PDF 158–160 presents a range of accompaniment patterns. Two of them would give this passage its own character: sustained harmony under agitated violins (PDF 158–159), and agitated imitative string counterpoint as in Figaro (PDF 159–160, which c307 also proposed).

**Evidence for leaving it.** The bassoon solo 115–122 is on the do-not-touch list (HISTORY lines 654, 714 and 743), and no reason is given that a critic could answer. The present background also satisfies Brahms 3/iii (PDF 133–135). It is pale, sits well away from the bassoon's register, has a rhythm clearly different from the solo's, and uses a pizzicato bass. A Figaro texture would make the background compete with a solo bassoon, and PDF 143–145 warns against exactly that.

**Decision:** keep it. The sameness is the formula's only weakness, and the change at 123 (the F2 hand-over, with harp pp) already supplies the contrast Adler asks for (PDF 136–137, sudden stop of the figure).

### 3. Bars 83–86: violas balanced by dynamics, not register (not applied)

**Problem.** Since c254 the violas at 83–86 are mf, a level chosen for balance against the flutes' unison E♭6 line, the horn pedal and the clarinet octave. Adler PDF 128 and PDF 144 say that balance should come from register and spacing, and that dynamics are the weakest means.

**Evidence for leaving it.** 83–86 is protected (HISTORY lines 609, 694 and 781: "horn pedal and clarinet octave 83–86", "83–86"). In this passage the register separation is already wide: flutes at E♭6, clarinets in octaves, the horn pedal in the middle and the violas below it. So the dynamic marking is a refinement on top of a registral solution, not a substitute for one. **Decision:** no change.
