## Findings

### 1. 226-230: the chorale's canon repeats the brass in the brass's own whole notes. Applied. This changes the rhythm of a movement I quotation (the canon).

**Problem.** The chorale (224-228, brass, G-E-D-C-D♭-C in whole notes) is answered two bars later by Vln I·II, Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 with the same notes in the same whole notes (226-230). The second statement differs only in register and colour. Against the brass's next chords its notes are plain consonances: G over V6, E over vi, D over IV (an added sixth), C over I. A listener hears an echo, not a second line, and the chorale, the movement's main melodic goal, gets no more intense when it is heard the second time.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 346-347, sec. 1 and Ex. 21-1: in the Jupiter finale a line in tied halves, displaced by half a bar against the whole-note subject, makes suspensions (A&S's figures 4-3, 7, 9-8) and is "a counterpoint to the whole notes". With the suspension "rhythm rather than melody assumes paramount importance" and it serves "to animate and individualize the voice". PDF 347: suspensions arise "out of an extension rather than from a shift" too. PDF 348: the resolution "should sound like a relaxation". PDF 350-351: the 7-6 goes with a 3rd. PDF 351: the 9-8 may hold the 6th through. PDF 352: no tone of resolution in another voice before it resolves, except the 9-8 over the bass. PDF 365-366, sec. 27: suspensions turn parallel motion into oblique motion. Adler part 2 PDF 175 (cited by c017): each element of a multi-element texture needs its own rhythmic make-up.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-331/work/sections/arrival.ily, with a c331 comment above arrivalVlnOne).** The canon keeps its entry on G5 at 226.1 (two bars after the brass, as before). Each later note arrives half a bar late and is tied over the barline. Its last two notes become quarters, so the line is back on C6 at 230:
```
arrivalVlnOne / arrivalVlnTwo / arrivalFluteOne / arrivalOboeOne / arrivalClOne, 226-230:
  g''1~\ff | g''2 e'''2~ | e'''2 d'''2~ | d'''2 c'''4 des'''4 | c'''1
  (Vln I keeps \cbaw, Vln II \cbawU; Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 keep \breathe after 230)
arrivalFluteTwo, arrivalOboeTwo 226-227:  g''1~\ff | g''2 a''2      (were g''1 | c'''2 a''2)
arrivalClTwo 229:                         g'2. f'4                  (was g'2 f'2)
```
- 227.1-2: G5 held over vi (A-C-E): the "7" of Ex. 21-1, a consonant preparation over V6 at 226.
- 228.1-2: E6 held over IV (F-A-C), a major 7th, resolving down by step to D6 at 228.3: 7-6 with the 3rd A in Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2 and the violas. No D sounds before 228.3.
- 229.1-2: D6 held over the C bass, resolving to C6 at 229.3: 9-8 with the 6th A5 held (Fl 2, Ob 2). The tone of resolution is only in the bass, the case the reading allows (PDF 352). This is the bar where the brass rest, so the 9-8 is heard in the strings and winds alone.
- 229.3-4: C6-D♭6 in quarters, on the B♭-minor half bar as before; 230: C6. Shifting the last notes by half a bar would have put D♭6 on 230's C-major brass chord, against E4 in the horns, so the motto's chromatic neighbour is kept but shortened.
- Fl 2 and Ob 2 at 227.1-2: their C6 would lie above the canon's held G5, so they tie G5 with it. I tried E5 first; it made octaves G→E with Hn 1 and the violas, so I rejected it.
- Cl 2 at 229 holds G4 to beat 4. With Cl 1's new D6-C6 its G4-F4 on beat 3 made fifths (D/G → C/F).
- Side effect, from the reading's sec. 27: v331 has fifths D6/A3 → C6/G3 between the violins (canon) and the violas at 228→229, and fifths between Cl 2 and the flutes' shared voice into 230.1. The held D6 turns the first pair into oblique motion; allpairs_par.py no longer reports either pair.

### 2. Memorability: where the themes stand (no change).

F1 has a clear head rhythm (half, dotted quarter, eighth), a peak at 22 and a suspended leading tone at 25. F2 now has four individual details in its major statement (73.4 anticipation, 75 turn, 78 dotted peak, 81 7/6). The chorale is short enough to sing after one hearing. Compared with the great finale tunes of Brahms 1/iv, Dvořák 9/iv and Tchaikovsky 5/iv, what the movement lacked was the moment where a hymn heard a second time grows instead of repeating. Finding 1 supplies that at the one place where it can be done without touching the brass. I would leave F1, F2 and the chorale top line as they are now.

### 3. Bass suspension 5/2 (PDF 352-353): still unused; decided not to apply.

It is the only main technique of the unit that the movement never uses. The places where I → V6 has a stepwise bass are 51-52 (pizzicato, protected trumpet solo), 67-68 and 75-76 (F2 in the violins), 115-116 and 123-124 (protected development solos). At 68 and 76 the V6/5 lasts two beats. A suspended bass would leave the dominant only beat 2, and the half-bar harmonic rhythm of the theme's first phrase (I | V6/5 - I) would become a passing chord. The harp's 68.1 arpeggio also sounds D5, the leading tone, before the bass would reach it, which PDF 353 calls the poorest case. c241 rejected this with a weaker reason. I reject it for these two.

### 4. Anticipation at F1's cadences (PDF 363-365): agree with c241; not applied.

The reading places the anticipation where the next tone is "highly predictable", most often in the soprano. F1's deceptive cadence (25-26, 165-166) already holds C over the beat as a 4-3. An eighth-note anticipation would have to be added to eight doubling parts in con fuoco eighths, for a figure the reading calls "not as important as the suspension". 11-18 is protected.

### 5. Alternating suspensions in a rising sequence and the 7-6 chain (PDF 355-357): no free place.

The rising sequences are 35-38 (protected F1 statements, c297-c314) and the stretto 139-146 (protected). The descending ones are 87-90 and 107-109 (protected chains, which already follow the reading). No change.
