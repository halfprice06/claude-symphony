## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 27-34: the bass statement of F1 has a thin lower octave (applied)
**Problem.** F1 in the bass is ff at C4 in six players: violas, cellos (tenor clef), Bn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 a2. Its lower octave (C3) is the double basses alone, and the contrabassoon plays two octaves down (C2). The theme therefore sounds as one heavy tenor line with a weak bass under it, which is Rimsky's warning about melodies spread over several octaves (PDF 76). His pairing for the bass octave is "D. basses + Bass cl." (PDF 77). In Ex. 96 and the Ivan overture (PDF 80) a reed goes with the basses under a horn-and-reed line. The bass clarinet is silent from bar 7 to 143, which is 136 bars.
**Evidence.** Stems at 28-33: Cb -34 to -39 dB, against a C4 octave of Vla -34 to -38, Vc -30 to -39 and Hn 3·4 -28 to -35 (plus 3 dB for the a2). The C3 octave is about 8-10 dB under the C4 octave.
**Fix (applied, pTwo.ily).** The bass clarinet doubles the basses at C3, ff, 27-34, and takes the cellos' A♭ at 33 (the basses repeat F there, c166). Its lowest note is D2, inside the instrument's range.
```
pTwoBassCl = { c2->(\ff g,4. aes,8 | bes,4-.) aes,8( g,8 f,4-.) es,4-. | d,4( g,8 aes,8 b,4 d4) | f2-> es4( d4) | es2->( bes,4. c8 | des4-.) c8( bes,8 aes,4-.) g,4-. | f,4( aes,8 c8 f4 aes4) | g2.-> r4 | R1*8 | }
```
The bass clarinet stem is -29 to -40 dB at 27-34. This adds a doubling and does not touch the protected statement or countersubject (c007), and it is not a trombone (c161).

### 2. 19-26: two bare horns under F1's consequent (applied)
**Problem.** At 19 the violas leave the tune for figuration (c057), and Hn 1·2 a2 at f carry F1's lowest octave (C4) alone. Above them are Ob 1 + Vln II (C5) and Fl 1 + Vln I (C6), so there is one wind per octave. Rimsky PDF 75: "if there are two horns playing the lower octave in unison, three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages." c057 raised the horns to f to keep the octave present; Rimsky's remedy is more winds above, not louder horns.
**Evidence.** Stems at 19-26: Hn 1·2 -29 to -36 dB (about -27 to -33 for two real players), against Vln II -30 to -34 and Ob 1 -33 to -39. The horns are the loudest single colour in the tune.
**Fix (applied, pOne.ily).** Ob 2 plays a2 with Ob 1 from 19 to 26 at f, including Ob 1's move up an octave at 24.3 (c041). That puts three winds (Fl 1, Ob 1·2) above the two horns. The new variable is `pOneOboeTwo = { R1*8 | <Ob 1's 19-26 line> }`. The shared staff now prints "a2" with one f. Ob 2 is otherwise free from 11 to 26. The FluidSynth MIDI plays a2 notes once, so the render cannot show this change.
**Coherence note.** finale.md and scherzo.md say "bars 1-26 unchanged since finale v160". This is a scoring change inside 19-26 that touches no linked bar (1-2), so the composer should update that line and tell the scherzo thread.

### 3. 110-113: the scale figure is covered by the ff brass (applied)
**Problem.** At the climax the movement's scale figure (c057) runs in violas, cellos and Bn 1 at C4-F3 (110, 111, 112 beats 3-4). Above it are four horns (G4/E4), three trumpets and the tuba, all ff. Rimsky PDF 76: a wind in unison with a string group "increases the resonance … and amplifies its tone". PDF 79 gives the loud three-group unison (Violas or 'Cellos + Cl. + Horn).
**Evidence.** Stems: violas -36/-36/-35/-33 dB and cellos -36/-31/-34/-33, against Hn 1·2 -29 to -23, trumpets -28 to -24 and low brass -19 to -17 at 110-113. Only the woodwind half of Rimsky's formula (Bn 1) is present, and Cl 2 rests through the whole development.
**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** Cl 2 doubles the violas' figure at pitch, ff (the same level as Cl 1's C5 on the shared staff):
```
devClTwo = { R1*8 | R1*3 | g4\ff c'8( bes aes g f g) | f8( g aes bes aes2) | g2 c'8( bes aes g) | aes2-> r2 | R1 | R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4 | }
```
The horns are left as they are (3=1, 4=2 since c099; finale.md records that layout). The written range is A3-D5 for the B♭ clarinet. The clarinet stem rises from -32.8 to -30.3 dB at 112 and from -30.6 to -24.1 at 113.

### 4. 99-100: the development's first F1 entry has an undoubled top octave (applied)
**Problem.** The F-minor head is in violas (F4) over cellos + Bn 2 (F3) and basses (F2). Rimsky's Ex. 91 (Tsar Saltan [92], PDF 79 and score PDF 268) gives the same three-octave bass melody as Violas + Fag. / 'Cellos + Fag. / D. basses, with a bassoon on each of the two upper string octaves. Here the violas' octave has no reed, and Bn 1 rests.
**Evidence.** Stems at 99: violas -35 dB, cellos -29, basses -30. The top note of the head is 6 dB under the line below it. The second entry (103-104) is already doubled (Hn 3·4 on the violas, Tbn 1 + Bn 2 on the cellos).
**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** Bn 1 doubles the violas at F4, f, 99-100, with the parts kept apart through 101 so the shared staff prints two clear voices (Bn 2's sf, then Bn 1's p entry at 101.2):
```
devBsnOne = { \partCombineApart f'2->(\f c'4. des'8 | es'4-.) des'8( c'8 bes4-.) aes4-. | r4 e2.\p \partCombineAutomatic | e2 e2 | ...
```
The bassoon stem rises from -39 to -35 dB at 99.
