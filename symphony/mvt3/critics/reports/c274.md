## Findings

### 1. 277: short ff string chords should be double stops, not divided (applied)
Problem: bar 277 has two short accented ff tonic chords with a dominant eighth between them (C – G – C), the last strokes before the fff chord. The strings are marked "div.": Vln I E5/C6 and D5/B5, Vln II E4/C5 and D4/B4, violas G3/E4 and G3/D4. Each pitch is therefore played by half a section. Rimsky PDF 87: short arco chords "only sound well when played forte (sf)" with wind support. Each player may be given "double notes or chords of three and four notes". They are "usually assigned to 1st and 2nd violins and violas", and "notes on the gut strings are the most powerful". Balance and part-writing matter less in short chords than the resonance of the chord itself and how easy it is to play. All six double stops are easy sixths. The violas' G3/D4 is on two open strings and G3/E4 on one, and Vln II's D4/B4 uses the open D. As double stops, every player strikes both notes and the open strings ring. The score already does this in the ff codetta at 91–94 (Vln II and violas "non div.", c265). Bar 277 was the one short-chord tutti left divided. This partly reverses c085, whose reason was notation: without a marking, a two-note chord reads as a double stop. Writing "non div." keeps the notation explicit.
Fix (applied, finale.ily): `finaleVlnOne` 277 `<e'' c'''>4->^\markup \italic "non div." r8 <d'' b''>8 <e'' c'''>4-> r4`; `finaleVlnTwo` 277 `<e' c''>4->^\markup \italic "non div." r8 <d' b'>8 <e' c''>4-> r4`; `finaleVla` 277 `<g e'>4->^\markup \italic "non div." r8 <g d'>8 <g e'>4-> r4`. The "div. a 3" at 278 stays for the held chord (PDF 88: sustained chords divided the same way in every section). Cellos stay "div." (PDF 87: the cello usually takes the low note with the basses). No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes, and the MIDI is the same.

### 2. 277–278: oboe pair in its low register, then a fourth at the top of the final chord (applied)
Problem: at 277 Ob 1 plays C5–B4–C5 over Ob 2 E4–D4–E4, so Ob 2 sits in the oboe's thick lowest fifth at ff (the loudest low oboe notes in the movement, MIDI velocity 120–124). The pair is also a sixth apart in two different registers, which PDF 90 rule 3 tells us to avoid. At 278 the oboes hold G5 under C6 at fff, a fourth inside one colour. PDF 90 rule 4: give one kind of instrument concords (octaves, thirds, sixths), not fifths, fourths, seconds or sevenths, and observe this "specially ... in writing for the oboe with its penetrating quality of tone". PDF 91 (a): do not couple the weak register of one instrument with the piercing register of another. The flute pair (G6/E6) and the clarinet pair (E5/C5) are thirds; only the oboes break the rule. Rimsky's own four-part tutti chord on PDF 95–96 gives the oboes a third (G/E) doubled at the unison by the second flute and first clarinet.
Fix (applied, finale.ily): `finaleOboeOne` 277 `e''4-> r8 d''8 e''4-> r4` (was `c''4-> r8 b'8 c''4-> r4`); `finaleOboeTwo` 277–279 `c''4-> r8 b'8 c''4-> r4 | e''1->\fff~ | e''4 r4 r2\fermata` (was `e'4-> r8 d'8 e'4-> r4 | g''1->\fff~ | g''4 …`). Bar 277 now has the oboes a third apart, E5/C5 and D5/B4, both in their middle register, coming out of the C5 they both reach at 276. At 278 Ob 1 keeps C6, and Ob 2 takes E5, a minor sixth below it and at the unison with Cl 1 (a mixed Ob + Cl unison, PDF 95, "Excellent"). The wood-wind chord is now G6 E6 | C6 E5 | E5 C5 | C4 C3, all concords within each pair. I did not use Ob 1 E6 because c126 (Adler PDF 198) keeps the oboes out of C6–F6 in held fff notes. G5 leaves the wood-wind but is still in Vln II (div. a 3) and the harp's glissando goes to G6. Neither oboe moves in parallel with the bass (the bass is a C pedal), and par8.py reports nothing at 275–279. Harmony, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. This is an inner-voice change inside the protected close 273–279, of the same kind as c266 at 277. The close's harmony, line, glissando and the final chord's pitches are unchanged.

### 3. Wood-wind in threes: the cor anglais is silent from 221 to the end (checked, not applied)
The cor anglais plays 1–2, 5–6, 147–150, 171–186 and 217–220, and rests from 221 to the end. PDF 93–94 recommends groups of three of one colour, with a low third instrument (cor anglais, bass clarinet) completing the oboe or clarinet group. PDF 97 shows a full wood-wind tutti chord with the cor anglais as the third member of the oboe group. In this score, the only place where it would fill a gap in the wood-wind is the C4–C5 octave of the final chord (G4). There it would double Tpt 2, Vln II and the violas at fff and could not be heard (PDF 51: in forte one wood-wind is half a horn). Its entry would also go against PDF 81–82: a player added for one chord after 58 bars' rest. Every other coda passage where a three-reed chord could sound (223–234, 235–247, 255–262) is protected. Its silence after theme B's echo at 217–220, the instrument's own theme from movement II, is a defensible dramatic choice (Dvořák 9 does the same). I decided not to add it.
