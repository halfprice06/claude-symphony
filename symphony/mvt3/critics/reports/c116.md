## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 82: the harp's rest does not stop the sound (applied)
- Problem: c114 put the harp RH's rest on beats 3-4 so that its E♭ arpeggio (G4, E♭5) would not sound against the run's A♭4 and D5 (Vln II, Ob 1) and D6 (Vln I, Fl 1). A harp string rings until it is damped. A plain rest does not stop it, so the G4 still sounds under the A♭4 at mf. The fix c114 intended only works if the harpist damps.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 117, sons étouffés: the sound stops only when the player damps each string. Damping is a separate action and has its own marking. PDF 114: the middle register, where these notes lie, is the harp's "rich and warm" register with the most sustain.
- Fix (sTwo.ily, `sTwoHarpR`, last bar): `es'8 g'8 bes'8 es''8 r2^\markup \italic "étouffez" \stemNeutral |`. The LH E♭2 keeps ringing into 83, which is correct because 83 is an E♭ chord.

### 2. Bar 242: the harp's D1 is a string with no pedal (applied)
- Problem: the LH ends the sHead passage on `d,,1` (D1), an octave below the cellos, basses and Bn 2 (all D2). D1 is one of the two lowest strings, which have no pedal and are tuned by hand. The score asks for it only through "(low D string: D♮)" at bar 67. That marking sits inside the score-only pedal-diagram tag, so the harp part never shows it, and it is 175 bars away from the only note that needs it.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 111 footnote: "The two lowest strings on the instrument are not affected by the pedal mechanism and have to be tuned manually." PDF 113-114: the lowest octave is dark and adds weight but no pitch definition under a D2 already held by three bass instruments.
- Fix (sHead.ily, `sHeadHarpL`): bar 242 `<d, d>1` (D2-D3; see Finding 3) instead of `d,,1`. In sTwo.ily, the bar-67 marking becomes just `\harp-pedal #"--^|^--^"` with no "(low D string: D♮)". After the change the harp's lowest note in the movement is F1 (MIDI 29), so it uses no hand-tuned string and needs no tuning note.

### 3. Bars 239-243: harp mf under an f tutti, and the D7 arpeggio rings into the E-major chord (applied)
- Problem: at 239 the strings (except violas), flutes, oboes, bassoons and basses go to f. The harp stays at mf, although its eighths are the passage's only continuous motion (the tune and the counter move in halves and quarters). The harp works at G3–A4 there, which is the register Adler says has the power. A single low note per bar in the LH also leaves the harp thin against an f bass. At 243 the harp stops after a D7 arpeggio (A, C, D) over a low D, and the orchestra moves straight to the E-major chord (E, G♯, B). Undamped C and D ring against G♯ and B.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 113-114: the middle two octaves are "very rich and warm", the top has little dynamic range. A harp marked one level below the tutti in its strong register is wasted. PDF 114: octaves and tenths are easy for one hand (Ex. IV-5, Maestoso, has LH octaves under chords). PDF 117: the sound stops only when damped.
- Fix (sHead.ily, `sHeadHarpL`, under the existing 8 bassa line): `<a,, a,>1\f | <f,, f,>1 | <d, d>1 | <d, d>1 \ottava #0 | r1^\markup \italic "étouffez" | R1*3 |`. The single "étouffez" sits between the staves, where the harp's dynamics print, and covers both hands. RH unchanged. The octaves double the bass line already in Vc/Cb and Bn 2, so no new voice-leading interval appears (analysis.txt: still 0 outer-voice parallels).

No other harp problem: the pedal settings, the changes between passages, the hand spans and the registers all meet the reading (table rows 2, 4, 7, 8, 11). I make no findings beyond these three. Points 4-8 would only repeat confirmations.
