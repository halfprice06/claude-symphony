## Findings

### 1. Horn call 243–246: the double octave is empty in the middle (applied)
Bars 243–246. Tpt 1·2 play E5–C6–B5–C6 in unison and Hn 1·2 play E3–C4–B3–C4 in unison, two octaves below. No brass instrument plays the middle octave. Rimsky PDF 104 gives the layout for sustained notes in double octaves: two trumpets in octaves above two horns in octaves, so the middle note is doubled by trumpet and horn, "the imperfect balance … compensated for by the mixture of timbres". He also says (PDF 104) that the octave "is sometimes formed by trumpets and horns acting together". Two trumpets in unison add little over one; two horns in unison at E3 (written B3) sit in the horns' dull lower-middle register at f. Dvořák 9/iv splits the trumpets into octaves for the theme's second statement (bars 18–25). The owed item (the horn call in the trumpets) stays on top in Tpt 1.
Fix (sHead.ily):
```
sHeadTptTwo = { R1*8 | e'2->(\f c''2~ | c''4 b'2.~ | b'2 c''2~ | c''1) | }
sHeadHnOne = { R1*8 | e'2->(\f c''2~ | c''4 b'2.~ | b'2 c''2~ | c''1) | }
```
Result: E5 (Tpt 1), E4 (Tpt 2 + Hn 1), E3 (Hn 2). Hn 1 reaches concert C5 (written G5). Dynamics stay equal on both shared staves.

### 2. Last chorale 255–256: Tbn 2 below the bass trombone, and parallel fifths into 255 (applied)
Bars 254–256. Tbn 2 has C3 at 255–256 while the bass trombone has the bass E3 (I6) with the tuba on E2. Rimsky PDF 101: the third trombone and tuba form the bass in octaves and the other trombones take upper parts. Here Tbn 2 sits under the bass trombone's bass note. Also, par.py on v14 reports three parallel fifths at 254→255: Tbn 2 F3→C3 against Hn 1·2 C4→G3 and against Tpt 1 C5→G4. These are at the start of the last chorale.
Fix (finale.ily, `finaleTbnTwo`, bars 255–256): `c1\ff~ | c1` → `g1\ff~ | g1` (bars 257–258 stay `c1~ | c1`). Tbn 2 moves F3→G3 by step, joins Tbn 1 on G3 (a2), then falls to C3 at 257 above the B.tbn's A2. Brass at 255 now reads E2 / E3 / G3 / C4 / E4 / G4 in close order. par.py: the three fifths at 255 are gone.

### 3. Arrival chorale 227: empty sixth between the trumpets (applied)
Bar 227. In 224–228 the brass play alone (woodwind and violins enter at 226). At 227 Tpt 1 has C5–D♭5 and Tpt 2 has E4–F4, a sixth below, with nothing in the brass between them. The top voice stands alone a sixth above the choir, which breaks Rimsky's first rule for brass harmony (PDF 100: close order, "no empty spaces in the intervals"). His high-register diagrams (PDF 101) keep the two trumpets adjacent. Tpt 2's E–F already sounds in Hn 1, Hn 4 and Tbn 2.
Fix (arrival.ily): `arrivalTptTwo = { g'1\fff | e'1\f | c''1 | g'1 | a'1 | a'1 | R1 | …`. A4 belongs to both halves of the bar (A minor, then A–D♭–F) and moves G4→A4 against Tpt 1's D5→C5 in contrary motion. par.py: this removes the octaves Hn 3·4/Tpt 2 at 226→227 (G→E) and at 227→228 (F→A). It adds one A→G octave between Hn 3·4 and Tpt 2 from 228 to 230 across the rest bar 229. That is an inner-voice doubling separated by a full bar's rest, and I accept it.

### 4. Arrival 224–228: the violas' pulse is marked below the brass (applied)
Bars 224–228. The only motion under the brass-only chorale is the violas' repeated quarters (added in cycle 7), marked mf against seven brass parts, bassoons, cellos and basses at f. Rimsky PDF 113: strings and brass are seldom combined except as tremolo under sustained brass or as short sf chords. PDF 114: balance matters most "with long sustained chords or those of rhythmic design". At mf, a unison viola line on E4–A3 inside an f brass choir in the same register (Hn 2, Tbn 1, Tpt 2) will not be heard, so the layer cycle 7 added has no effect.
Fix (arrival.ily, `arrivalVla`, bar 224): `e'4\mf^\markup \italic "unis."` → `e'4\f^\markup \italic "unis."`. The violas then match the brass. Their existing `g2\f` at 230 is now redundant but harmless; I left it.

Marking the horns ff (PDF 103) would put them level with the ff canon at 226 and undo the cycle 4 balance, so I made no change.
