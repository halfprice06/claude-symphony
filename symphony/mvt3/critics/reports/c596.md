## Findings

### 1. 278: the bass drum is fff under the fff timpani roll and covers the final chord (applied)
**Problem.** At 278 the bass drum strikes fff (`bd1->_\fff~`) together with the timpani's fff roll on C. That is the combination Adler quotes Piston against twice: "When you write a fortissimo for the timpani doubled by bass drum, don't expect to hear anything else from the rest of the orchestra" (adler2 PDF 55), and "if you hit the bass drum hard, it does not make any difference what the chord is above it" (PDF 84). Ch. 14 repeats it: "a loud bass drum or cymbal roll can obliterate the entire orchestra, no matter how loud the tutti may be playing" (PDF 141). The chord being covered is the one earlier cycles voiced most carefully: the woodwinds interlocked from G6 down to C5 (c584), the divided strings, and the harp's ff chord after its glissando.

**Evidence.** The score already answered this question at the arrival. c146 set the bass drum to ff under the fff crash at 223, using the same Adler pages, "so the string tremolo, the violins' C7 and the violas' E4 sound through the arrival chord". The final chord has the same instruments and the same problem, but 278 kept fff. Brahms 2/iv, Adler's model for the end of a symphony (Ex. XIV-23, PDF 135–136), gets its last chord from brass and timpani alone.

**Fix (applied in sections/finale.ily, finalePerc, bar 278).**
```
<< { \tweak style #'xcircle cymc1->^\fff~ | cymc4 r4 r2\fermata } \\ { bd1->_\ff~ | bd4 s4 s2 } >>
```
The crash, the timpani roll and every other part stay fff, so c171's rule that fff appears only at 278 in the close still holds. The protection of 273–279 covers the chain's pitches, the held C6, the quotation and the fff arrival, and this change touches none of them. It only applies to 278 the reason the protected 223 already uses. Only page 16 changes. MIDI changes only in the bass drum's velocity at 278.

### 3. Two-drum stroke at 279: tested on paper, declined
Ex. XII-50 (PDF 81) shows that two drums can sound together. I considered `<g, c>4` as the timpani's last stroke at 279. I declined it. The roll at 278 is on C alone, and the timpani's C under the trumpets' C is the symphony's closing tonic. A G on the very last quarter would add nothing the tutti does not already have. 273–279 is also protected.
