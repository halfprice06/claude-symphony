## Findings

### 1. Bar 255: the held G that starts F2's last phrase has no swell (applied)

**Problem.** At 255 the last statement of F2 enters in C major. It is played ff "espress." in unison and octaves by Hn 3·4 a2, Vln II (sul G), the violas and the cellos, under the brass chorale's last statement. The phrase opens with an accented dotted minim G (`g2.->( e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4)`). After the accent there is no marking on the held note. The strings' "espress." asks for vibrato and bow pressure, but a horn has no vibrato. Its only way to sing a held note is to shape it with the breath. As printed, the horns attack the G and then just hold it inside the ff brass chord, and that held note is the start of the theme.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 305, Ex. X-12 (Don Juan 530–540) shows exactly this case: four horns in unison carry the lyrical theme in a loud tutti. They have an accent on the first note, "ff molto espr.", and a < under each held note. Adler PDF 304, Ex. X-8 (Brahms 1/iv) gives the horn theme "f sempre e passionato" with < f > on each phrase. The score already follows these examples at the coda's other passionate brass unison, the horn call at 243–246 (c388: swells on the held notes in Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1·2). c488 gave Hn 3·4 the word at 255 from the same two examples but no swell, so the second of the two lyrical brass unisons in the coda is still unshaped. The rest of the phrase already grows: Cl 2 and Bn 1 enter f < at 257, and everything reaches ff < at 259 into the fff at 263. So the swell has to come back to ff by 256 and must not lift the whole phrase.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-588/work/sections/finale.ily).** In finaleVlnTwo, finaleVla, finaleVc, finaleHnThree and finaleHnFour, bar 255 changes as follows:

```
\after 2 \> g2.->(\ff\<^\markup \italic "espress." e4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff) | d4( c4 e4 g4) ...
```

Vln II has the same change at its octave, `\after 2 \> g'2.->(\ff\<^\markup \italic "espress."\startTextSpan e'4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff)`. Hn 3 and Hn 4 get identical marks, because they share a staff. The < > prints once under the a2 horns and under each string staff. The hidden ff on the E is needed. My first build used a plain `\<` closed by `\!`, and it raised the MIDI velocity of 255.4–257 from 67 to 84 in the horns and from 91 to 120 in Vln II, which means the whole phrase got louder. With the hidden ff, every note from 255.4 on has its old velocity in all five parts. The only MIDI difference is in the part-combiner's split Hn 3·4 voices at 259–262, which are 2–3 velocity steps lower (for example 79 instead of 82). That is inaudible, and it comes from the voices now inheriting a plain ff. Notes, slurs, the accent, the printed ff, the espress. words and the sul G spanner are unchanged. A c588 header comment was added.

I checked the pages at 130 dpi. On page 15 the hairpin sits under the ff and does not collide with anything. The page count is still 16.

### 2. Bar 235: no "dolce" on the Hn 3·4 pad under the S head (considered, not applied)

Adler PDF 306 (Ex. X-16, "sehr weich") and PDF 312 (the horns as a soft accompaniment choir) would support a character word on the p horn pad under the violins' "molto cantabile" at 235. I decided against it. The pad is protected (Hn 3·4 at 235–242). Its p, its middle register and the tempo heading "Poco più mosso, molto cantabile" over the system already give the character. A text-only mark there would add print without changing the playing.

### 3. Bar 105: stopped sf in Hn 3·4 (considered, not applied)

Adler PDF 308 (stopped horn for single notes and sf > p) and PDF 310 (Ex. X-27, loud stopped notes) would allow a stopped sf on Hn 3·4's staccato F4/D♭4 at 105, where the strings play sf–p. I decided against it. The sf at 105 is one of the protected strong marks (c008: 101, 105 and 138 in the development and retransition). The trombones play the same sf, and a stopped horn there would be thinner than the trombones, so the chord would lose weight. The one stopped sf the score has, at 138 on the stop chord, should stay unique, because it signals the stop chord.
