### 3. Heterophonic doubling and a solo string over winds: the one unused technique (considered, not applied)

**Problem.** Adler teaches two devices the score lacks.
- Heterophony: Mozart's Vln I against the solo horn (Ex. XVII-9, PDF 264), Liszt's flute "emphasizing the most important pitches" (Ex. XVII-14, PDF 273–274), and the Fidelio doublings "directly or heterophonically" (PDF 283).
- A solo string at the top of a woodwind chord (Ex. XVII-13, PDF 273).

cyclic-notes.md lists heterophonic doubling as a colour still unused. No movement has used it (searched in every coherence file).

**Places I tested against the reading:**
- **211–218 (theme B), 235–238 (movement I's S head), 275–276 (the scherzo's head).** These are the only places where a decorated second line would be heard against a plain one. They are exact quotations and protected. A decorated version would also blur the at-pitch match that the other threads rely on (finding 1).
- **175–178.** Here the C.a. plays alone (c349). A solo cello partner would reproduce movement I 171–174, where one cello plays beside the cor anglais (mvt1.md line 220). That would be Ex. XVII-13's solo string with winds, and a cyclic link. I rejected it for three reasons:
  - movement I's cello line (the S head over i–iv–N6–V) does not fit the finale's D–B–C/A♭–G bass;
  - a cello in the C.a.'s register (F4–A♭4) breaks Adler's own rule of registral distance (PDF 274);
  - it would cover the timpani's and pizz. cellos' motive x at 178, which is Ex. XVII-13's joining motive. c350 refused a trumpet echo there for the same reason.
- **83–86 and 265–272.** A heterophonic flute against the f/ff violins and full winds would not be heard. It would only take a doubler away from the tune.

**Decision:** no change.

### 4. Bar 9: motive x and the violins' run start together (considered, not applied)

Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273) shows the timpani's motive heard because the other parts hold. At 9 the scherzo's motive x (timpani mf <) has its two quavers on beat 2. That is also where Vln I·II start the rising scale G–A♭–B–C–D–E♭ and Bn 1/Vc move in quarters. This brushes against PDF 259–260 (the accompaniment should not duplicate the line's rhythm).

I kept it for three reasons:
- At 8 the bassoon and cellos already play motive x's rhythm (B2 C3–D3 E♭3), so 8→9 is a dialogue in the same rhythm between bass and timpani.
- The run's beat-2 start mirrors the scale figure at 3, which also starts on beat 2.
- Moving the run to beat 3 shortens the crescendo that c280 staged.

**Decision:** no change.

### 5. Colour reserve for the cyclic soloist (checked, no change)

Mozart keeps his orchestral flutes and horns out of the soloist's way (PDF 264, 267). The cor anglais is the symphony's cyclic soloist: movement I's motto at I 5, movement II's theme B, movement I's duet at 171–174. In the finale it plays:
- the tutti chords at 1–2 and 5–6;
- one doubling inside the ff V7 at 147–149;
- its solo at 171–186;
- theme B's answer at 217–220.

This is Mozart's practice: the colour is used in tuttis and kept fresh for the solos. **Decision:** no change.
