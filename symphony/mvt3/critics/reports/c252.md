## What else I studied

I read every text page from PDF 530 to 583 and looked at every example page as a 110-dpi image; the OCR of the chord labels is unreliable. I read c072 and c162, the two earlier critics on this reading, so as not to repeat their fixes (c162 applied the C♭ at 81 and the viola line at 46–47). With my own script I listed every augmented-triad and diminished-seventh sonority in the MIDI at eighth-note resolution, to find what Unit 30 teaches that the score lacks. ## Findings

### 1. 91–92: two identical codetta strokes; the common-tone °7 is used only once in the movement (applied)

**Problem.** The ff codetta at 91–94 hammers I–V7 in E♭. Bars 91 and 92 are the same chord pair note for note in all 26 parts (I on beat 1, I on the eighth pickup, V7 on beat 3), and then 93 displaces the metre (c248). The second bar adds nothing harmonically. The common-tone °7 is the chromatic embellishment of a major tonic that A&S call "one of the most frequently used" (PDF 544). Yet the score uses it only once, at 273, over the final C pedal.

**Evidence.** PDF 543–544 (Ex. 30-19): the common-tone °7 is an apparent seventh chord over a stable bass, "the dissonances are created by the neighboring ♯2 and ♯4 in the upper voices", and the two half steps ♯2–3 and ♯4–5 "create a powerful resolution into the tonic". PDF 544, Ex. 30-20 shows the voicings: ♯2→3, ♯4→5 and 6→5 over a held bass. PDF 544 gives the notation: build a 6/4/2 on the root of the goal chord and raise the 2nd and 4th. Over E♭ that is F♯–A–C. The ♭3 spelling (G♭) from PDF 544–545 belongs where mixture is the point, as at 273; here E♭ major is plain, so F♯ is right.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-252/work/sections/clos.ily).** 92.1 becomes E♭–F♯–A–C over the unchanged E♭ bass; the eighth pickup 92.2.5 returns to I, and beat 3 is V7 as before. Voices on G go down to F♯, voices on B♭ go down to A or up to C, and voices on E♭ hold:

### 2. The augmented triad as a passing chord in major is absent (checked, not applied)

**Problem.** Unit 30's main new chord is the major-key augmented triad: I–I+–IV, V+–I, the I6♯5 → IV, and 5–♯5–6 over a held bass (PDF 537–540). The score has none. Every augmented sonority in it is a minor-key ♭6 over V.

**Evidence and decision.** I tested every spot where the progression occurs in a major key:
- **77** (E♭, I6 → ii6/5, Vla/Vc B♭3 → C4). This is the best fit: the Schumann altered 6/3 of Ex. 30-13, PDF 538–539. But B♭ is also held at 77.1–2 by Bn 1 (B♭3, unison, in the protected horn/bassoon pad 75–82), by Cl 1 (B♭4, c150's thirds line, which then falls to A♭4) and by the harp (B♭4). A B♮ in the strings would sound against held B♭s in three other parts. Removing them means rewriting the protected pad, and Cl 1's B♮ cannot go on to A♭.
- **212** (D♭ I → IV, Cl 2 A♭–A♮–B♭). This would be the Bizet I–I+–IV of PDF 538. It falls inside the protected theme-B doublings 211–218 and collides with the E♭/G♭ of the trumpets and melody at 212.4.
- **197** (A♭, E♭–E♮–F) and **209–210** (A♭7 → D♭ as V+7, E♭–E♮–F). Both would use the E♭→E♮ step that is reserved for the thread at 219–220 (c220, c230; c239 reverted the viola E♮ at 70).
- **81.4** (V+7 F–F♯–G). Hn 2 holds F3 and the harp has F4.
- **250** (D♯–E). This is the reserved thread itself.
- **271** (5–♯5–6 in Tpt 3 over the C pedal, PDF 539–540). The G♯ forms an E-major triad against the melody's B, and Tpt 3 has a rest before the A.

**Fix.** None. I leave the augmented triad unused rather than force it against held unisons or the reserved E♮. Later critics on this reading should not re-test these spots; a new spot would have to come from a recomposed passage.

### 3. V7♯5 and the embellishing V7 (checked, not applied)

**Problem.** V7♯5 (PDF 542) and the V7 embellished by half steps (PDF 546) are unused.

**Evidence and decision.** A&S place the ♯5 normally in the soprano (PDF 542). The only plain V7–I with 5 → 6 in the top voice outside protected bars are 72.4 → 73.1 and 93.4 → 94.1. At 72 the top voice is F2's tune (F5–G5), and an F♯ there would change the theme. At 93.4 it would put a second chromatic F♯–G one bar after Finding 1, at the closing group's only perfect cadence with the tonic on top (c143). One chromatic chord in the codetta is enough. No change.
