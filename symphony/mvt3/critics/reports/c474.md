## Findings

### 1. 167–168: single timpani stroke under the held link chords (applied in scratch)

**Bars.** 167 (VI, A♭ in the bass, f) and 168 (French sixth A♭–C–D–F♯ over the same A♭). Both fade toward the p of 170.

**Problem.** The timpani strike A♭ once, f, on 167.1 and then rest until 171. Around them the cellos, basses, Bn 2, Cl 2 and Hn 1·2 hold the chord while the violins fall. The drum's one stroke dies after about a second, so the bass loses its weight at the point where the diminuendo should be continuous.

**Evidence.**
- In Rimsky's appendix (PDF 505–507), every held f–fff chord with timpani has a roll, and none has a lone stroke (Principle 7). In Ex. 209 (PDF 375) the roll under a held tutti is marked "dimin.", and Ex. 210 (PDF 377) ends with a roll "poco a poco dim."
- Inside this score, the exposition's matching link (39–40, V of E♭ fading into the transition) already has a roll, `bes,1:32\f\>~ | bes,2:32~ bes,4:32\p r4`. The recapitulation's link was the only fading link that had a single stroke.
- Every other augmented sixth over A♭ in the movement has an A♭ roll: 5, 219 (sub. p) and 261. The French sixth at 168 was the exception.

**Fix.** recP.ily, `recPTimp`, last four bars:
```
… es4-> r4 r2 | aes,1:32\f\>~ | aes,2:32~ aes,4:32\p r4 | R1*2 | }
```
The bar was `aes,4\f r4 r2 | R1*3 |`. I added a comment above `recPTimp` that cites the reading.

**What stays the same.**
- The roll ends at 168.3, before the horns fade at 169. That keeps c108's diminuendo order (brass and drums first, then woodwind, strings last; protected).
- The 166 E♭ stroke (protected) is unchanged.
- The two bars 169–170 still leave time for the soft sticks at 171 and the near-the-rim motive x at 178.
- No pitch is added: A♭ is in the timpani's recapitulation tuning (G, A♭, C, E♭), and it is the bass of both chords.

### 2. 259–260: held ff iiø6/5 without timpani (tested on paper, declined)

**Problem.** Principle 7 would put a roll under this held ff chord.

**Why I declined.** The drum can give only C (the seventh) or A♭ (not a chord tone until the German sixth at 261). Rimsky's own crescendo method (PDF 130, adding instruments) is already served by the A♭ roll entering f< at 261 and growing to fff at 263. A roll from 259 would spend that entry. 259–263 is also protected.

### 3. 110: two strokes under the held V7 of F (declined)

**Problem.** The appendix suggests a roll under a held chord.

**Why I declined.** 110 is not a single held chord. The violas, cellos, Cl 2 and Bn 1 run in eighths, and the trombones and bass trombone strike and release (c099). The two strokes on beats 1 and 3 give that bar its pulse. c056 and c228 set the level for balance, and 110–113 is protected. Changing it would be churn.

### 4. 102 and 106: wind answers without strings (declined)

**Problem.** Principle 4 would add pizzicato cellos and basses under a wind tutti.

**Why I declined.** These two bars are the woodwind group's half of the protected call and response (99–102). Pizzicato strings would erase the contrast between the groups, which is the point of the passage.

### 5. 166: tutti strike followed by silence (checked, no change)

**Problem.** Chord 8 (PDF 506) would let the winds hold the chord after the strike.

**Why I kept it.** 166 is the i6 that ends F1. Its cut-off before the link is the phrase ending, and the E♭ bass and timpani at 166 are protected. The roll in Finding 1 now carries the link's own bass from 167.
