## Findings

### 1. 205–206: four horns over one trombone on F1's head (applied)

**Problem.** Over the A♭ pedal, F1's head (A♭–E♭–F | G♭–F–E♭–D♭–C) is in Hn 1–4 a2 at A♭4, f, with Tbn 1 alone an octave lower at A♭3. Tbn 2 holds E♭3 under them. That makes four horns to one trombone. The head is the last statement of the movement's own first theme before the D♭ episode. Its lower octave is the weak half. At 207–208 the winds answer with the head at A♭5 and A♭4.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 74: horns above trombones in octaves are "not so reliable" and are given only as 2 Horns / 1 Trombone or 4 Horns / 2 Trombones. With four horns he puts two trombones on the lower octave. Ex. 77 (PDF 248–249) uses two trombones a2 with tuba for a maestoso tune. The E♭ that Tbn 2 held is also in the head itself (beat 3 of 205, 206.2) and in the violas' C5/E♭5. So the A♭ triad does not lose its fifth.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-381/work/sections/codaOne.ily).**

### 2. 37–38: the B♭-minor F1 head in Hn 3·4 a2 has no wind octave above it (not applied)

**Problem.** Rimsky PDF 75: when two horns play the lower octave in unison, three or four winds are needed above, especially in forte. At 37–38 Hn 3·4 a2 play the head ff at B♭4 with the cellos, and no wind plays B♭5–D5 above them. Fl 1 holds B♭5, and the oboes and clarinets hold the chord.

**Decision: no change.** The upper octave B♭5–F5–G♭5–A♭5 lies in the same register as the violins' f eighth figure (F6 down to B♭5, 37–38) and Fl 1's held B♭5. Oboes there would cross the figure on every beat and turn two lines into one blur. The horns ff in their strong register already carry the head over the f chords. c371 made the same decision for the strings.

### 3. 217–218: the theme-B echo in cor anglais + Hn 1·2 a2 (checked, kept)

Rimsky PDF 66–67 (an expressive phrase loses expression when doubled) and PDF 74–75 (in wind + brass unison the brass predominates) suggest that the cor anglais, theme B's own instrument, is covered by two horns.
