## Findings

### 1. 207–208: the head's lower octave sits in the clarinets' throat tones, inside an mf brass pad (applied)

Problem:
- At 207–208 the F1 head answers the horns in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I (A♭5).
- Since c177 it is also in Cl 1·2 and Bn 1, which form its lower octave. The clarinets start on the open throat B♭ (concert A♭4) and stay in the throat and upper chalumeau (concert G♭4, F4, E♭4–C4). Bn 1 plays an octave lower, from A♭3.
- That octave lies inside the held chord of Hn 4 (E♭4), Hn 2 (G♭4), Hn 3 (C5) and Tbn 1 (C4). The horns and Tbn 1·2 are marked mf, one level under the head's f (c104, from Rimsky).

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 185: a weak register cannot be played louder, so the orchestrator must not cover it.
- PDF 210, Ex. VII-56: the throat tones are "rather pale".
- PDF 226: an accompanied bassoon gets swallowed up because it blends so well.
- So c177's doubling is covered by four horns and a trombone at mf in its own octave. The f in the clarinets cannot be pushed any further.
- The strings' background stays mf (c158). Only the brass, which lie in that octave, need to come down.

Fix (codaOne.ily, bar 207, Hn 1–4 and Tbn 1·2): `es''1\mf` becomes `es''1\mp`, with each part's own pitch. In order: Hn 1 E♭5, Hn 2 G♭4, Hn 3 C5, Hn 4 E♭4, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 E♭3. The crescendo from 209 is unchanged, so the build into the ff at 211 starts from lower and is longer. The bass trombone, tuba and basses on the A♭ pedal stay f (protected, c176). The shared Hn and Tbn staves keep equal dynamics.

### 2. 222: Fl 2 has no breath before its fff G6 at the arrival (applied)

Problem:
- From the sub. p at 219, Fl 2 holds F♯5–G5–F5–F5 as whole notes through the allargando (MIDI 2 = 72, 62, 56), about 7 s with a crescendo from p to f.
- It then attacks G6 fff at 223 with no gap and holds it for a bar of Maestoso.
- Fl 1 has an eighth rest at 222.3 before its scale to C7. Fl 2 has none.
- Its F5 on beat 4 of 222 adds nothing: the stop chord's F is in the violas and Cl 2, and Fl 1 and Vln I are playing the scale.

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 188: "the flute requires a great deal of breath ... give the flutist time to rest". Ex. VII-9 (Brahms 1/iv, this movement's model) shows a held note handed between the two flutes so that each can breathe.
- PDF 186: the top of the flute must be approached with care. The fff G6 needs a fresh breath.

Fix (codaTwo.ily, Fl 2, bar 222): `f''1\f\<` becomes `f''2.\f\< r4\!`. The hairpin still runs to 223 in Fl 1 on the shared staff. The dynamics stay equal (f < in both flutes). Pitches elsewhere, the 216–223 top line and 222→223 are unchanged.

### 3. 101–102 and 105–106: Cl 2 doubles Hn 2 at the unison at mf, while the rest of the pad is p (applied)

Problem:
- Since c132, Hn 1·2, Bn 1 and Cl 2 enter together on beat 2 with the held chord under the woodwinds' scale figure, which is marked mf.
- Hn 1·2 and Bn 1 are p. Cl 2 plays exactly Hn 2's notes (E4–F4–E4, then A4–B♭4–A4).
- Cl 2 is marked mf: hidden in the score and printed in the part. That mark is only there because Cl 2 shares a staff with Cl 1's mf scale figure.
- So one player in the pad is a level louder than its unison partner, and the chalumeau clarinet colours a p horn note.

Evidence:
- Adler PDF 210–211: the clarinet plays every dynamic from pp to ff in every register. Nothing in the instrument asks for mf here, so the mf is a notation artefact.
- The examples (Ex. VII-63, -64, two clarinets) give every voice of a held pad the same dynamic.
- The project rule that shared staves carry equal dynamics means Cl 2 cannot simply be p.

Fix (dev.ily): Cl 2 rests 101–106 (`devClTwo = { R1*2 | R1*2 | R1*2 | R1*2 | R1*8 | … }`). `\partCombineApart` is removed from Cl 1 at 101, so the staff prints Cl 1's mf scale figure as "1.". The chord is complete without Cl 2 (Hn 1·2, Bn 1). c132's beat-2 entry and the sf at 101 and 105 are unchanged.
