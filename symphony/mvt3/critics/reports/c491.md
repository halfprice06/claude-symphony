## Findings

**1. 140–143: bass clarinet on the cellos' F1-head entry of the retransition stretto. Tested; not applied.**
The reading (PDF 217) calls the bass clarinet's chalumeau "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective sound ... mysterious and shadowy", and PDF 184 asks that each woodwind be used to present melodic material. In this finale the bass clarinet never has a melodic phrase. It only holds pedals or doubles the bass. The most likely place for a first phrase is the soft head imitation over the stop chord's B pedal. There the cellos' entry (140–141, G3–D3–E♭3–F3–E♭3–D3–C3–B2, p) lies inside held p winds at the same pitches: Hn 4 G3, Bn 1 F3, Hn 3 and the violas on D4. I tested this in /tmp/critic-491/work/sections/retr.ily:

```
retrBassCl = { R1*4 | R1 | g2->(\p d4. es8 | f4-.) es8( d8 c4-.) b,4~ | b,1\cresc~ | b,1~ | b,1~ | b,1\f | b,2. r4 | ...
```

With this edit the bass clarinet doubles the cellos at pitch, and the head's last note B2 becomes the pedal it already plays from 144. The build had no parallels (par8.py 138–147 gives the same single bass-octave flag at 147.1 as v491), and the theme record was unchanged. I declined it for two reasons:
- Layout: the bass clarinet staff, hidden on page 7 (128–143), now appears there. The build then warns "compressing over-full page by 8.2 staff-spaces / page 7 has been compressed". This is the same page that made c476 decline a wider harp chord at 132.
- Sound: the reading's own rule on wind-cello doublings (PDF 226) is that "the cello tone predominates" and the wind "is absorbed in the whole". So the chalumeau colour, the reason for the change, would hardly be heard at the unison. The bass clarinet would have to play the entry alone to be heard as a colour, and that would take the stretto's low entry from the cellos (c017, c397).

The gain does not pay for a compressed page. I reverted the change.

**2. Soft high oboe (C6–F6, PDF 198) and low flute (PDF 186). Considered; no candidate.**
Neither colour appears at a soft melodic point. I checked the remaining soft thematic passages for a place where one could replace an existing colour without covering another line:
- The bassoon solo's answers at 119 and 122 run from G6 down. That is above the oboe's useful range, and the answers are protected after c209, c438 and c481.
- 244–246: Ob 1's G5 swell under the horn call is a held harmony note, not a line.
- 43–44: the transition's scale figure already puts Fl 1 in its low-middle register over pp strings, as Ex. VII-3 does.

Not applied, as c401 also concluded for the flute.

**3. F2's woodwind statements follow the register rules. No change.**
- Bn 1 at 115–122 reaches only A4, below the pinched top, at mp over pizz. pp. The violas rest on 117.2 under the peak (PDF 226).
- The cor anglais at 171–186 is in its sonorous middle register. Its high third phrase (181–183) is doubled by Fl 1 and muted Tpt 1 (PDF 205). That doubling is protected.
- The cor anglais and Hn 1·2 echo of theme B (217–218) uses the cor anglais's "richly beautiful" lowest sixth.

**4. Breath and endurance. No change.**
The longest woodwind spans are Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 207–223 and 245–254, with marked breaths. Fl 2 relieves Fl 1 at 37–38 and on the chorale canon 226–230 (PDF 188). The contrabassoon rests at 205–208 (PDF 230).

I changed no theme: F1, F2, the chorale top line and every quotation are as in v491, so the other movement threads have nothing to update.
