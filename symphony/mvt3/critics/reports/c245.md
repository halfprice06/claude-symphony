## Findings

### 1. Duplicate tempo texts collide with flute notes (81, 83, 197). Applied.

Problem. The group that holds timpani and percussion carries `\consists "Metronome_mark_engraver"`. In bars 37-127 and 171-198 both drum staves are hidden by RemoveEmptyStaves. A StaffGroup-level engraver with no live staff puts its grobs on the top staff of the system. So a second "animando" (81) and "Tempo I (♩ = 84)" (83) print inside the Fl 1·2 staff on page 4, and a second "poco rit." prints through Fl 1's notes at 197 on page 10. Each mark therefore printed twice on the same staff, once at the top of the score and once among the notes.

Evidence. Gould PDF 41 and 55-57: symbols must not collide and must be spaced at least half a space apart. Gould PDF 43: a sign must never cut through other symbols. A text that sits on the notes also breaks the rule that tempo marks appear at the top of the score and above designated staves, not inside one.

Fix (score.ly):
```
\new StaffGroup <<
\new Staff = "Timpani" \with { \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" \remove "Key_engraver" instrumentName = "Timpani" ... }
```
(was `\new StaffGroup \with { \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" } <<`). When the timpani staff is shown the mark sits over it as before (pages 1 and 11-16). When it is hidden the mark disappears with it. The other groups still carry their marks. Checked on pages 4 and 10: one mark per group, nothing inside a stave.

### 2. Middle-line stems use LilyPond's default (down) instead of the context (92 stems). Applied.

Problem. LilyPond puts every unbeamed note on the middle line with its stem down. In many places such a note sits between two up-stemmed notes, so the stem flips down for one note and back up. Examples: Vc and Cb B♭/D on the middle line in 12 and 20; Bn and Cbsn 52-88; Vla 128-165; Vln II and Ob 188-196; low strings 265-270. The search found 92 such stems in bars 12, 14, 15, 17, 20, 22, 27, 29, 31, 32, 52, 56, 60, 68, 72, 73, 76, 79, 81, 85, 87, 88, 99, 111, 128, 129, 131, 146, 150, 152, 154, 155, 157, 160, 162, 165, 188, 189, 192-194, 196, 200 and 265-270.

Evidence.
- Gould PDF 27-28: a note on the middle line takes the stem direction of the notes around it. Keep the same direction within a beat or half-bar. Use the down-stem only when the context does not decide.
- Gould PDF 61 applies the same rule to chords.
- Gould PDF 13: the engraver must override software defaults that do not follow the convention.

This is a technique the score had not used. No earlier critic raised it in HISTORY.md.

Fix (score.ly). A Voice engraver, `gould-mid-stems`, is defined before `\layout` and registered with
```
\context { \Voice \consists #gould-mid-stems }  % c245, see gould-mid-stems
```
It changes only one kind of stem: unbeamed, visible, not forced (no `\stemUp`/`\stemDown`/`\voiceOne`, which keeps part-combined and divisi stems unchanged), with heads that centre on the middle line (min + max staff-position = 0). It looks at the nearest visible, non-middle neighbours that touch the note in time on each side:
- If both neighbours point the same way, the note follows them.
- If they differ, the note follows the neighbour in the same bar and half-bar, when exactly one neighbour qualifies.
- Otherwise the default stays.

Rests break a run, so an isolated middle-line note keeps its down-stem, as Gould says.

Layout. The extra up-stems at 12 and 20 made page 2 over-full by 1.2 staff-spaces (2 warnings). I removed the overflow with 2 mm more page height and kept the rule free of exceptions:
```
top-margin = 4\mm      % was default 5 mm
bottom-margin = 5\mm   % was default 6 mm
```
The page breaks are unchanged: 16 pages, the same last bar on every page.

### 3. Bar 219 whole notes are not centred. Not applied.

Problem. In 219 every part holds a bar-length note and the note stands at the left of the bar. c215 centred the other bars of this kind with `\cen` (Gould PDF 55: a bar-length note goes just left of centre). Bar 219 was left out because it opens with a key change to C major. The space after a key cancellation is set by `KeyCancellation.space-alist`, which has no `next-note` entry in 2.24.3, so `\cen`, which overrides `BarLine.space-alist.next-note`, has no effect there.

Decision. I did not apply it. The fix needs a new `\once \override Score.KeyCancellation.space-alist.next-note = #'(semi-fixed-space . 3.2)` in global.ily, and then `s1*2` must be split into `s1 s1`. Page 12 is full, and wider spacing in 219 risks a new system break. The gain is one bar, so I chose not to risk the 16-page layout. If a later cycle frees space on page 12, the override above is the fix.
