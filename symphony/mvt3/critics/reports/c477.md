## Findings

### 1. 105–106: the development's wind statement of the scale figure repeats 101–102 as the same block (applied)

Bars: 101–102 and 105–106, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1.

Problem: at 101–102 Fl 1 (C6), Ob 1 and Cl 1 (both C5) play the scale figure in rhythmic unison, mf. At 105–106 the same three play it in rhythmic unison again, a fourth higher. The violins' statement at 103.4–104 sits between them, so 105 is the figure's third statement in the development. The only change is c234's: Cl 1 an octave lower. At mf in three octaves that change is hard to hear. The exposition's version of the same process (87–90) has the winds in canon since c377, so the figure is treated more simply in the development than in the exposition.

Evidence: Adler PDF 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration … the third time it must be a bit different, in order to renew interest". PDF 271: "since each of the woodwinds has such a unique color, contrapuntal writing, especially rhythmical, is very effective" (Mozart 38/iii, Ex. VIII-42, oboes and bassoons answering each other over the strings). PDF 256–257: an enclosed voicing "provides added color interest". This answers c377's "further wind imitation … too close to this one". The two passages are 15 bars apart, with the ff codetta and the development's first head and answers between them. The two canons also have different shapes. At 89–90 one leader (Fl 1) has two followers. At 105–106 two leaders (Fl 1 and Cl 1, in octaves) have one follower (Ob 1) inside them. Here the imitation also does a job: it gives the third statement the change Adler asks for, just before the lament at 107.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-477/work/sections/dev.ily, Ob 1 only). The oboe enters a beat later and follows at the octave, as a strict canon. It leaves out the octave drop and ends on F5 at 106.4, where Fl 1 and Cl 1 drop to F5 and F3:

Fl 1, Cl 1, the horns, Bn 1, the bass clarinet, the hairpins and the mf all stay as they were.

### 2. 67–74: the counter-melody at the unison in violas, cellos and Hn 1 (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 257 says unison overlap gives only "a stronger pitch", and PDF 265–266 prefers octave doublings. I considered putting the cellos an octave under the violas. I am keeping the unison. The counter is mp under an mf tune and needs that stronger pitch. Hn 1 was set at the cellos' pitch on purpose so that the strings lead (c287, Rimsky PDF 76, 79). The cellos an octave lower would also crowd the E♭1–B♭2 bass that the harp and basses double.

### 3. 101–102: Ob 1 and Cl 1 in unison on C5 (checked, no change)

This is the same kind of overlap as in finding 2. I keep it. Once 105 is a canon, the plain block at 101 is the reference that the third statement departs from. Moving Cl 1 down here would make 101 and 105 the same layout again and undo c234.

### 4. 27–34: countersubject at pitch in Vln I, Vln II and Fl 1·2 (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 257: "In full-orchestra tutti this practice is still common." The bass statement is a ff tutti. c307 and c310 placed the flutes there with the oboe out of their way. No change.

### 5. 187–194: single wind in octaves with the violins (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 267 (Brahms 1/ii, a single bassoon an octave below the violins) is already met here by Hn 1 p dolce on Vln II's lower octave. Adding Bn 1 would double the horn and take the bassoon off its inner line. No change.

### 6. Articulation devices (PDF 262–265): not applicable

A slurred line against the same pitches tongued, or an oboe "pulsating" a flute's held notes, belongs to Debussy and Stravinsky, not to this score's Brahms–Dvořák idiom. The repeated-note background the style does use is already in Tpt 3 at 247–254 and the clarinets at 255–262. No change.
