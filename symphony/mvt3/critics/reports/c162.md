### 1. Bar 64 beat 4: the trumpet's passing G♭4 sounds against an F4 (sOne.ily, Vln II)

**Problem.** At 64.3-4 the solo trumpet passes A♭4-G♭4 over V of E♭ minor. Vln II pizz plays `<d' f'>` on beat 4, so the G♭4 sounds a minor second above F4 in the same octave. A&S say the ♭6 displaces the 5th of V in minor (PDF 537 §7; PDF 549 point 4). In this score, bar 62.3 does this correctly, with no F against the G♭. At 64.4 the 5th and its displacing tone sound together, and this is the most audible part of the exposition (protected solo, p strings).

**Fix (applied).** Vln II, bar 64, beat 4:
```
% was  <d' f'>4
<d' bes'>4
```
The passing G♭ now lies inside a B♭-D-G♭ sonority. The violas keep F3/A♭3 an octave below, out of the way. The solo is not touched.

### 2. Bar 38 beat 3: flute 2 and oboe 1 anticipate the violins' resolution (pTwo.ily)

**Problem.** At 38.3 the violins hold an accented E♭5 over B♭ (4-3), resolving to D5 at 38.4. Fl 2 and Ob 1 move to D5 on 38.3, so the resolution is already sounding in the same octave. A&S warn against sounding the tone of resolution with the suspension (PDF 352). In the parallel bar, 36.3, the woodwinds avoid this, so 38 is also inconsistent.

**Fix (applied).** pTwoFluteTwo and pTwoOboeOne, bar 38:
```
% was  c''2 d''2
c''2 bes'2
```
Fl 1 keeps D6. It is two octaves up and a different colour, which A&S call "much less problematic" (PDF 352). Moving Fl 1 to B♭5 would give fifths with the violins (C6/F5 → B♭5/E♭5).

### 3. Bars 46-47: the viola leaps an augmented second (trans.ily)

**Problem.** The viola held D4 through 46 and moved to C♭4 at 47, a melodic augmented second. A&S forbid this in a voice-leading part (PDF 82, 89). The C♭ is the mixture tone that turns the music toward E♭ minor. A&S say such a tone works best as part of a chromatic line (PDF 537 §5).

**Fix (applied).** transVla:
```
% was  { d'1\pp~ | d'1~ | d'1~ | d'1 | ces'1~ | ... }
{ d'1\pp~ | d'1~ | d'1~ | d'2 c'2 | ces'1~ | ces'1~ | ces'2 bes2~ | bes2. r4 | }
```
The line becomes D-C-C♭-B♭. Bar 46.3-4 becomes ii over the pedal, and C♭ arrives by half step. Bars stay complete, the c052 9-8 at 49 is kept, and the parallel fourths with Vln II F→G♭ are between upper voices only.

### 4. Bar 81 beat 2: a trace of the minor in the E♭-major statement (sTwo.ily, Vla and Vc)

**Problem.** The major statement of F2 (67-82) is entirely diatonic. The minor statement's characteristic tone, C♭, disappears completely, so the change of mode is a block contrast rather than a remembered one. A&S call mixture in major a special colour device (PDF 542). They show ♭6 as a neighbour to 5 over V (PDF 572-573). Their Brahms Op. 87 example carries the minor quality into a major movement (PDF 544-545), and association with a prior normal use helps the ear (PDF 536-537).

**Fix (applied).** sTwoVla and sTwoVc, bar 81:
```
% was  aes4( bes4 aes4 f4)
aes4( ces'4 bes4 f4)
```
Over B♭7 this gives a passing V7♭9 (C♭3 against the bass B♭1) that returns to B♭. 81.4 F→G at 82 is kept, so the 7th is still not doubled (c141). The harp's C5 struck in bar 80 still rings at 81.2. It is soft and an octave higher, so I accept it. I did not copy the change to the recap (197), because 197-198 crescendo into the tutti at 199, and a soft colour tone would be lost there. The change is in the counter-melody, not in F2.
