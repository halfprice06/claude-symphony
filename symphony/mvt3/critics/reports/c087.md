## Findings

### 1. 251–254: the S head's upper octave is too thin after the trumpets stopped (applied)
Problem: since v87, Tpt 1·2 rest for 251–254. In the second phrase the S head's lower octave is Hn 1·2 a2 ff at E4 with Cl 2 and violas. The upper octave (E5) is only Ob 1 + Cl 1, with Ob 2 a third below. Above them, Fl 1·2 and Vln I·II play the P head ff at E6, and Tpt 3 (f) repeats notes in the E4–A4 register.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 75 says that when two horns play the lower octave in unison, "three or four wind instruments will be necessary above, especially in forte passages". His examples are 1 Ob + 1 Cl over one horn, and 2 Fl + 2 Cl over two horns. In 247–250 Tpt 1 supplied that weight (principle 3: Tpt + Ob + Cl). In 251–254 nothing replaces it. Without its upper octave the S head is heard mainly as a horn line in the middle of the texture, under a P head that has four ff instruments.
Fix: Cl 2 goes up an octave for 251–254, in unison with Cl 1 and Ob 1. That gives three winds over two horns. The trumpets keep the rest that c086 gave them. Hn 1·2 and the violas still hold the lower octave, which is Rimsky's Vla + Hn pairing (PDF 79). Cl 2 also moves by a smaller interval: from 250's B4 it now rises a fourth to E5 instead of falling a fifth to E4.
```
% finaleClTwo, bars 251-254
| e''2.~( e''8 d''8 | c''4 a''2-- g''4 | f''4. e''8 d''4 c''4 | b'2 c''2) \breathe |
```
The record checks (sHeadBrass HnOne/Vla 247) do not read Cl 2. Pitches and the S head itself are unchanged.

### 2. 265–268: F1, the finale's main theme, is in brass and strings only, with no woodwind (applied)
Problem: at 265–268 the C-major F1 head is in Tpt 1·2 + Vla (C5) and Hn 1·2 + Vc (C4). Every woodwind except Bn 2's quarter-note hits plays movement I's head on top at E6 (Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1·2, with Vln I·II). Cl 2 sits there at E6 (written F♯6). It is the fourth or fifth unison on a line that two violin sections already carry, and it repeats the G5→E6 leaps that c086 removed from Ob 2.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 74–75 says Tpt + Cl is the most common wind doubling of a trumpet, and that "the addition of a wind to a brass instrument yields a finer legato effect". F1 here is slurred (`c''2->( g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.)`). Rimsky PDF 79 says three groups in unison give "a fuller and more evenly blended tone", preferably in loud passages. F1 at 265–268 has two groups. At 269–272, where F1 moves to the top, it has woodwinds in both octaves (principle 11), so the main theme changes colour between 268 and 269 for no reason.
Fix: Cl 2 takes F1 with Tpt 1·2 and the violas at C5 for 265–268 and continues straight into its existing F1 at 269. The clarinet line now runs unbroken for 265–272. Cl 1 stays on movement I's head.
```
% finaleClTwo, bars 265-269
\partCombineApart c''2->(\ff g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | c''2->( g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | \partCombineAutomatic c''2->( g'4. a'8 ...
```
Cl 1 and Cl 2 are both ff at 265, so the shared staff carries equal dynamics. Engraved as two voices with no collisions (checked on page 16 of the scratch build).

### 3. 265–268: F1's lower octave has no bassoon, so the bass-register doubling of 247–254 and 269–272 lapses (applied)
Problem: Bn 1 rests for 265–268 between its chorale C4 (263–264) and its movement I head at 269. F1's lower octave is Hn 1·2 + Vc at C4–E3.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 76–77 names Vc + Bn as the most natural unison of cellos and woodwind. PDF 75 lists Hn + Bn. PDF 79 lists Vc + Cl/Bn + Hn as one of the most natural three-group combinations for loud passages. With Bn 1 added, F1 has the same three-group make-up in both octaves (Tpt + Cl 2 + Vla over Hn + Bn 1 + Vc). It matches 247–254, where Bn 1 doubles Vc and the trombones, and 269–272, where it doubles the violas and Hn 3·4. Bn 1 has rested since 264 beat 3, and woodwind endurance is not an issue here.
Fix:
```
% finaleBsnOne, bars 265-269 (replaces R1 | R1 | R1 | R1 |)
\partCombineApart c'2->(\ff g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | c'2->( g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | r8\ff g8( e'4.->) ...
```
Bn 2 has ff at 265 and 269, so the dynamics are equal. The existing \partCombineApart moves from 269 to 265.
