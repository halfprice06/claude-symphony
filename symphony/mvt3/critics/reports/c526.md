## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 146: the woodwinds that play 141–150 have no marked breath; the brass breathe at 146 (applied)
- Bars: 146 (retr.ily: `retrFluteOne`, `retrOboeOne`, `retrClOne`, `retrBsnOne`).
- Problem: in the retransition stretto, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Bn 1 play every bar from 141 to 150.3: F1's head mf, held F's, the head again f, then four ff bars with the sfp < ff swell at 149 into the Tempo I at 151. That is about 14 s with no marked breath (breath.py: 13.9 s, the longest stretch in the movement), and Ob 1 starts F1 ff again on 151.1 after a single quarter rest. Fl 1 plays 143–150 the same way. c136 gave the brass a breath at the end of 146, before the ff V6/5, and the contrabassoon and bass clarinet have one there too. Without the same mark, the woodwinds' ff attack at 147 is not prepared together with the brass. Cl 1 also has to jump a minor thirteenth, from 146.4's B3 (under the break) to 147's ff G5 (in the clarion).
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37: "Wood-wind players cannot manage extremely long sustained passages, as they are compelled to take breath; care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time." PDF 44: "The remarks on breathing, in the section devoted to the wood-wind, apply with equal force to the brass", which is why c136 put the brass breath at 146. PDF 36(b): "the clarinet is not well adapted to sudden leaps from one octave to another". The breath gives Cl 1 the moment it needs before the clarion attack.
- Fix (applied): `\breathe` after the staccato B at the end of 146 in four parts: `c'''4-.) b''4-. \breathe | f'''1->\ff` (Fl 1), `c''4-.) b'4-. \breathe | b''1->\ff` (Ob 1), `c'4-.) b4-. \breathe | g''1->\ff` (Cl 1), `c4-.) b,4-. \breathe | f1->\ff` (Bn 1), plus a c526 header comment. The note is already staccato, so no note value changes. The marks print on the shared staves at the same place as the brass, Cbsn and B.cl. breaths (page 8). The MIDI is identical to v526 note for note.

### 2. 35–36: the cellos' countersubject reaches B♭4 in eighths at ff, a semitone over Rimsky's practical cello limit (tested, not applied)
- Bars: 35–36 (pTwo.ily, `pTwoVc`).
- Problem: under the violins' F1 head in A♭, the cellos double the violas and Bn 1 at pitch in the eighth-note countersubject `es'8( des' c' bes) aes( c' es' aes') | bes'( g' es' des') c'( f' es' c')`. That puts A♭4 and B♭4 into a fast arpeggio at 2 = 84, with a skip of a fourth just before it.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 26 puts the cello's practical top at A4 (page image checked at 600 dpi). Notes above it are for "long value ... tremolando, slow, flowing melodies, ... not too rapid sequence of scales ... Skips should always be avoided."
- Test: in /tmp/critic-526/work I put the cellos an octave below the violas at 35–36 (`\clef bass es8( des8 c8 bes,8) aes,8( c8 es8 aes8) | bes8( g8 es8 des8) c8( f8 es8 c8)`, bass clef from 35 instead of 39). The build was clean, 16 pages, and par8.py found no new parallels (the 36.1 fifth E♭–B♭ over A♭–E♭ is in v526 too).
- Why not applied: the change makes the countersubject worse. Rimsky PDF 27 says the cello's A string is "bright" with a "chest-voice" timbre, and that string is what gives the line its edge at ff. Moved down an octave, the cellos play inside the held E♭3/F3 of Tbn 1·2 and Hn 4's A♭3 at f, where they add weight but cannot be heard clearly. Their move into 37's head also becomes a leap of a seventh (C3 → B♭3) where v526 has a step (C4 → B♭3). B♭4 reached by step on the A string is routine for a professional section. I reverted it. 35–36 stays as it is.

### 3. 219–222: horns at the same level as the trumpets and trombones (checked, no change)
- My dynamics scan found one passage besides c346's 207–210 where all the brass share one mark: the sub. p German sixth at 219, p through 220, < mf at 222. Rimsky PDF 41's "one degree stronger" applies to forte ("horns, in forte passages, are about one half as strong, but piano, they have the same weight as other brass instruments played softly"). 219–220 are p, and mf at 222 is a stage in c108's crescendo, in which the brass come in last. The sub. p, the 222 stop chord and 222→223 are protected, and the reading does not give a reason to change them. No change.
