## Findings

### 1. Bar 278–279: the final chord's woodwinds have no fifth, and G5 is the thinnest note of the chord (applied)

**Problem.** The last fff chord at 278 (concert pitch) is laid out as follows:

| Section | Pitches |
|---|---|
| Woodwinds | Fl 1 G6, Fl 2 E6, Ob 1 C6, Ob 2 E5, Cl 1 E5, Cl 2 C5, Bn 1 C4, Bn 2 and bass clarinet C3, contrabassoon C1 |
| Brass | Hn 1·3 and Tpt 1 C5, Tpt 2 G4, Hn 2·4 and Tpt 3 E4, Tbn 1 C4, Tbn 2 G3, bass trombone C3, tuba C2 |
| Strings (div. a 3, tremolo) | Vln I C6–E6–G6, Vln II C5–E5–G5, violas G3–E4–C5, cellos C3–G3, basses C2 |

- Below Fl 1's G6 the woodwind choir has no G at all. E5 is played three times (Ob 2, Cl 1, a third of Vln II).
- G5 is played once, by a third of the divided second violins, under eleven brass players at fff.
- The cadence chord at 263 is complete in the woodwinds (Cl 1 G5, Ob 1 E5, Ob 2 and Cl 2 C5). So the last chord of the symphony is thinner in its upper middle than the chord fifteen bars before it.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 164 (Ex. XV-15, Mahler 1/iv): in a brilliant tutti chord "all the upper octaves are completely filled in", each choir complete. PDF 159, consideration 2: every pitch goes to an instrument that can play it at the required dynamic. A divided violin third cannot hold G5 against fff brass.
- Adler PDF 160–161 (Ex. XV-10, Weber, *Freischütz* m. 284): the strings and brass are stacked, and "only the oboes and clarinets are interlocked". That is exactly the layout this change gives: Ob 1 C6, Cl 1 G5, Ob 2 E5, Cl 2 C5.
- The clarinet's G5 (written A5) is the strong top of its clarion register, where it carries at fff.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-584/work/sections/finale.ily, line 115, `finaleClOne`).** Old ending, then new ending:

```
c''4-> r8 b'8 c''4-> r4 | e''1->\fff~ | e''4 r4 r2\fermata | }
c''4-> r8 b'8 c''4-> r4 | g''1->\fff~ | g''4 r4 r2\fermata | }
```

What the change does and does not do:
- Cl 1 moves C5→G5 from 277.3 to 278, in octaves with Fl 1 (C6→G6) and in unison with Vln II's top note. These are doublings, not new voice-leading pairs.
- The woodwind choir now spells the full chord in close position from G6 down to C5, with the oboes and clarinets interlocked, as in Weber.
- Every other part is unchanged: the oboe pair keeps c274's sixth (Ob 1 C6, Ob 2 E5), and Ob 2 still has E5, now doubled by Vln II rather than Cl 1. The harmony, the top line, the dynamics, the trumpets' B→C at 262–263 and the 263 chord are also unchanged.
- The clarinets end a fifth apart (G5/C5), as they already are in the 263 cadence chord. Here the fifth is interlocked, with Ob 2's E5 between the two clarinets, which is Weber's layout, not an exposed fifth in one colour.

**Tested and rejected first: Ob 2 G5 instead of Cl 1.** It would reverse c274 (which moved Ob 2 from G5 to E5 to avoid a fourth in the oboe pair, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 90 rule 4) without a new reason. That would be churn, so I reverted it before building.
