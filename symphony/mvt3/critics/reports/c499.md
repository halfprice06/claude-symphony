## Holistic comparison with Elgar 1/iv

Elgar's finale and this one share a plan: a minor-key Allegro whose goal is a major-key apotheosis of a cyclic theme (Elgar's motto in A♭; here movement I's motto as the C-major brass chorale at 223 and 255–264), a lyrical second group, a development with remote soft passages, and a coda that states the cyclic theme in full brass over moving strings (Elgar's violins swirl under the grandioso motto; here Vln I's tremolo and Vln II's arpeggios under the last chorale, 255–262) before a short fast close (273–279). Differences: Elgar starts Lento and pp; this finale starts ff by cyclic rule (protected, bars 1–2). Elgar's motto is glimpsed softly before its final statement; here the major motto is withheld until 223, which the owed-item rule requires (it sounds once, in full brass), so I do not ask for a premonition. A conductor would believe a master planned this movement. The one Elgar device the reading also teaches, and which the score did not use, is spatial distance inside the orchestra: Elgar gives a remembered theme to the last desks only, so it sounds from behind the orchestra, not merely softer. That is Finding 1.

## Findings

### 1. 95–98: the movement II recollection should come from a distance (applied)
- Bars: 95–98 (closing group, after the ff codetta 91–94). The oboe recalls movement II (E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5, mf espress.) over a held E♭ chord in Vln II, violas div., cellos and basses, sul tasto mp > p since c444, with Bn 2 mp and Hn 3·4 p.
- Problem: the full string sections play the chord directly after the full tutti. Sul tasto takes off the upper partials, but the same players at the same place play it, so it reads as the tutti playing softly, not as a remembered sound from elsewhere. Casanelles (PDF 272) says a quieter dynamic played in place "would substantially alter the sound" against a distant source and "invalidate the effect"; distance changes both level and spectrum (PDF 272–273). Elgar's answer in 1/iv is the "last desk only" return of the motto, which is placement, not a dynamic.
- Evidence: Casanelles PDF 271–273, Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272); Elgar 1/iv (LA Opus review above). Prior use of this reading at the same bars (c444, sul tasto) is kept; this adds the spatial half of the same principle.
- Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-499/work/sections/clos.ily and dev.ily; text only, notes, dynamics and MIDI identical, MIDI verified byte-for-byte equal per track):
  - 95 Vln II: `g'1\mp\>~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "sul tasto," "last 2 desks" }`
  - 95 Vla: `\column { "sul tasto, div.," "last 2 desks" }` (was "sul tasto," "div.")
  - 95 Vc: `es1\mp\>~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "sul tasto," "last 2 desks" }`
  - 95 Cb: `es,1\mp\>~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "sul tasto," "last desk" }`
  - Returns: dev.ily 99 Vla `"ord., unis., tutti"`, 99 Vc and Cb `"ord., tutti"`, 104 Vln II `"ord., tutti"`.
  - A c499 comment line before `closVlnTwo` in clos.ily.
- Why the printed dynamics stay mp > p: by Casanelles's rule (PDF 271) the printed level is the result heard; fewer, more distant players at mp give the remote level without asking anyone to play under the bow's control. The chord is still complete (E♭–G–B♭, then C–E–B♭ at 97) and Bn 2 and Hn 3·4 still bind it; Vln II rests from 98 beat 4 to 104 beat 4, so its front desks rejoin easily; the violas, cellos and basses have one beat (98.4) before 99, enough for the front desks, which have rested since 94, to come in on the f downbeat. Page 5 engraves cleanly; page count 16.

### 2. 217–218 and 219–222: a soft bass-drum roll, as Elgar's finale opens (tested in reasoning, not built; declined)
- Elgar 1/iv begins over a ppp bass-drum roll, an inharmonic low pad (Casanelles PDF 275, 277: the bass drum has a spectral identity and is heard as low). The candidate place here is the approach to the arrival, a pp bass-drum roll from 219 into the bd stroke at 223.
- Declined: the timpani already roll G p < f at 219–222 (protected 222→223 with Tpt B→C and timpani G→C), and the score's plan has no percussion before 223, so the crash and bass drum at 223 are the first percussion sounds of the movement. A roll would add nothing the timpani do not already do and would spend that first sound early.

### 3. 217–218: thinning the held D♭ above the theme B echo (considered; declined)
- Fig. 6.1 suggests removing the near voices when the distant call enters. Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I hold D♭ (and F) at f above the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 echo.
- Declined: the held notes lie an octave and more above the echo (A♭3–G♭4), where masking is weak; c414, c440, c490 and c496 have already cleared every part on or near the echo's register; and a diminuendo would blunt the protected sub. p at 219.
