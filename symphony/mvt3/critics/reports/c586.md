## Findings

### 1. Bar 209: the held brass take the forte-piano attack with the rest of the chord (applied)

Problem. At 209 the A♭7 arrives for the two-bar swell into the Largamente ff at 211. Flutes, oboes and clarinets play fp < (c216) and the timpani roll fp < (c494), but the four horns (C5, G♭4, E♭4, A♭3) stay mf and crescendo, Tbn 1·2 (C4, E♭3) stay mp and crescendo, and the bass trombone and tuba hold the A♭ pedal f <. Bn 1·2 and the violas' E♭4/G♭4 also hold the chord at f/mf <. Six held brass parts in the chord's own register fill the woodwinds' dip, so the fp is not heard and the brass swell begins from mf instead of p.

Evidence. Adler lists the forte-piano attack first among the brass attacks (adler1 PDF 290, Ex. IX-9: four horns, three trumpets, three trombones and tuba, sff>pp<ff on held chords); Ex. X-54 (PDF 323) gives it to the trumpet and Ex. X-87 (adler2 PDF 8) to the tuba. The score already uses it once, at 149, where c408 (woodwinds) and c508 (strings) extended the dip to every sustained part because "the dip must be audible" (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129, sf-p by orchestration). MIDI before the change: horns 56 at 209 (no dip), after: 44, rising to 55–57 at 210.

Answer to c288. c288 declined "sfp in the brass at 209" because 205–210 and the 205–223 dynamic path were protected. The protections are of the 205–210 scoring (c178: instruments and notes, unchanged here) and of the 211–223 dynamic path (c238, which starts after this bar). Since c288 the timpani have taken fp < at 209 (c494), so the brass and the held strings are now the only sustained parts without the dip. fp is not a new strong mark (c008); the woodwinds already have it.

Fix (applied, sections/codaOne.ily, bar 209 only):

### 2. Bar 90.4 → 91.1: Hn 2 no longer leaps a 13th into the ff (applied)

Problem. In the 6/3 bridge's off-beat horn chords Hn 2 plays F4, F4, E♭4, F4 (written C5, C5, B♭4, C5), above Hn 3, and from 90.4 drops a 13th to the ff E♭3 (written B♭3) that it holds at 91–93.
