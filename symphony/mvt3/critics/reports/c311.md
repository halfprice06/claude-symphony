## Findings

### 1. Fl 2 at 35-38 doubles Ob 1's pad at the unison in the flute's weak octave (applied)

Bars: 35-38 (F1's restatement in B flat minor, strings ff, Hn 3-4 ff "ben marc." with F1's head at 37-38).

Problem: Fl 2 held C5 | E flat5-F5 | F5-D flat5 | C5-B flat4 at f. Ob 1 holds C5 | D flat5-E flat5-F5 | F5-D flat5 | C5-B flat4 at mf in the same bars, so the two parts are the same notes except at 36.1-2. Fl 2 sits in the octave Adler describes as "sweet and with little carrying power" (PDF 185, Ex. VII-2), under ff strings and the ff horns, so it adds nothing a listener can hear. It is also the same defect c306 removed at 27-34 (flutes padding in their weak octave next to the oboes). Fl 2 had been playing without a break since 27, mostly slurred quavers, and Adler says the flute in long-note writing needs breath and rest (PDF 188), and that two flutes can relieve one another (PDF 192). Adler PDF 201 says to double with the oboe only a significant line, so the featured instrument is not overshadowed. The pad at 35-38 is not such a line, and having two winds on it only thickens the middle register that F1's head (Hn 3-4 at B flat4-F4, Cl 1 at F4-E flat4) has to pass through. Removing it gives the head more room and leaves Fl 1's F1 statement (A flat5 at 35) as the only flute colour, so the restated theme stands out more in the texture.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-311/work/sections/pTwo.ily):

```lilypond

% Fl 1 restates f at 39, so the shared staff has equal dynamics when Fl 2 re-enters:
pTwoFluteOne = { ... | g''4-.) f''8( es''8 des''4-.) c''4-. | bes''1\f | aes''2 bes''2~ | ... }
```

Fl 2 re-enters on D5 at 39 and holds it through 42. Ob 1 holds the same D5 at 39-40 and stops at 41, so Fl 2 now takes the long note over from the oboe, as in the Brahms 1/iv dovetail Adler shows (PDF 188). It is also the voice that continues the D5 to the p at 42 once the oboe has gone. Ob 1 and Tbn 1 keep c202's E flat-F link at 36.4, and Ob 2, Cl 1-2, Hn 1-2 and the strings are unchanged. F1, F2 and the chorale top line do not change.

Build check: build-warnings.txt is empty, pdfinfo shows 16 pages, the outer-voice parallel lines in analysis.txt are identical to v311's, `par8.py score.midi 34 40` gives the same output as v311 (only the existing bassoon and bass-trombone octaves with the bass at 37.1, which are doublings of the bass), and themecheck on the scratch copy is 39/39 unchanged. On the rendered page 3 the shared flute staff shows the two parts and one f at 39.

### 2. Fl 1 reaches B flat6 at 78 by a leap from F6 (no change)

Bars: 77-78 (F2's climax in the violins, Fl 1 and Ob 1 doubling).

Adler says notes above A6 are difficult and are best approached by step (PDF 186), and that the extreme top "can only be played loud" (PDF 185). Here B flat6 comes at mf at the end of a crescendo from p, and it doubles Vln I and Ob 1 an octave lower, so the flute is not exposed. The rising fourth to B flat6 at 78 and the dotted peak (Vln I-II, Fl 1, Ob 1) are accepted items in HISTORY (F2 climax; c167 77-82 balance), and the leap is what makes the climax recognisable. I see no reason to override the protection. The breath after 78 (accepted) also covers the flute's need to recover.

### 3. Tchaikovsky's low-clarinet unison for a motto (no change)

Adler PDF 211 shows Tchaikovsky 5/i: the clarinets a2, p, in the low chalumeau. The finale has no such colour for F1 or F2. The only place where it would fit without touching a protected passage is 95-98, and c221 rejected it there because it collides with the violas' E flat4-E4 thread. That reason still holds in v311, and the passage now has c221's oboe solo. I did not apply it.

### 4. Bass clarinet as a lyrical chalumeau voice (no change)

Adler PDF 217-218 describes the bass clarinet's chalumeau as its most distinctive register for legato melody. coherence/mvt1.md (line 224) says the finale must not add another bass-clarinet motto tail at E flat3. The other candidate places (151-170, 171-198, the chorale) are protected. The instrument is used well where it already is, doubling the bass at 223 and 278 with the low C an octave up (accepted). I made no change.

### 5. Cor anglais in its low register (no change)

Adler PDF 205 calls the low fifth or sixth of the cor anglais "deep, rich, intense" with great carrying power. The finale uses it at 171-186 in that register, protected and confirmed by c221. It also links to movement II's theme B on the cor anglais (II 41-44). That is correct, and I made no change.

### 6. Wind chorale over pizzicato strings (no change)

Adler PDF 231 cites Brahms's Haydn Variations: a chorale for oboes, bassoons, contrabassoon and horns over pizzicato strings. Scoring the finale's chorale (247-264) that way would change its role as the fff tutti peroration and break several protected items (205-223 dynamic path, 255-264 staggered breaths, chorale top lines). I rejected it.
