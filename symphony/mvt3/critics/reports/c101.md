## Findings

### 1. 263-264: the scale figure after the last cadence is in the layout for a cantabile lower part, not for a brilliant upper part (applied)

Bars: 263-264, Vln II.

Problem: after the fff cadence chord, the strings play the opening scale figure (bars 3-4) in C major, which leads into F1 at 265. Vln I plays the upper octave alone (C6, then G5 down to B4 and back up to G5). Vln II and the violas play the octave below in unison. The whole wind and brass section holds C fff through 263 and three beats of 264. Fl 1 and Cl 1 hold C6, and Ob, Tpt, Hn and Cl 2 hold C5-G5. The upper octave of the run therefore sits inside the held chord in one string section. The lower octave, which is less audible, has two sections. At bar 3 the same layout is right, because there the strings play alone at f.

Evidence: Rimsky ch. II, PDF 60, "Violins and Violas in octaves": the two layouts "are not exactly the same. The first [Vns I+II / Violas] should be used to obtain greater brilliance in the upper part, the second [Vn I / Vn II + Violas] to give the lower part a fuller and more cantabile quality." Ex. 27 (Sadko, PDF 204) is a "quick animated passage, forte", and it puts Vln I+II in unison over the violas. A run at fff over a held tutti is that case. Rimsky ch. II, PDF 54: a line in the middle of the orchestra is not prominent from its position alone, so resonance must be added by doubling. Bar 263 is the hypermetric downbeat of the last group (see Reading note 19), and the run is the only moving line in the bar.
