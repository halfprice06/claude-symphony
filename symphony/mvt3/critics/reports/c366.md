## Findings

### 1. Bar 50→51, cellos and basses: arco→pizz in one quarter with no bow plan (applied)

**Problem.** From 43 the cellos (div. B♭2/F3) and basses (B♭1) hold the pedal pp, tied for eight bars under "change bow at will". At 50 they stop on beat 4 and play "unis., pizz." / "pizz." on 51.1. That attack is the bass of the trumpet solo's first chord, and the clarinets enter dal niente, so it is the only attack on that downbeat. Between the release and the pizz there is one quarter (♩ = 150 under the poco rit., about 0.4 s). Because the bowing is staggered, every player arrives at 50 with a different bow direction and a different part of the bow.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: "it is much easier to change from arco to pizzicato, if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog. They then have enough leverage on the bow to make the switch more quickly." PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (Coriolan): a bow mark over a long note "so that the following attack will be solid". The score already follows this at the two other one-beat switches: 114→115 (c085, c096: `d2.\p\>\upbow r4`) and 167→171 (c318). Bar 50 is the only one left out.

**Fix (trans.ily).** The tie and the staggered bowing stay. Each player arranges to take bar 50 as an up-bow that ends at the frog.
```
transVc = { ... | <bes, f>1~ | <bes, f>2.\upbow r4 | }
transCb = { ... | bes,,1~ | bes,,2.\upbow r4 | }
```

### 2. Bars 1 and 5, Vln I, Vln II, cellos: the opening double stops do not say "non div." (applied)

**Problem.** In the two ff German-sixth chords, Vln I (C5–A♭5), Vln II (E♭4–C5) and the cellos (A♭2–E♭3) carry no divisi word. The violas, on the same chord, are marked "div.". Players who follow the custom Adler describes will split the violins and cellos at the stand, and then only half the first violins hold A♭5. finale.md (Opening) states the link this way: the scherzo's last G♯5 "continues as A♭ in the same players at the same pitch (… Vln I A♭5)". The link therefore needs every first violin on A♭5. All three double stops are easy, on adjacent strings: a minor sixth, a major sixth, and a fifth on the C and G strings.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: "double notes (double-stops) are usually divided between the two players on the same stand … If the word divisi does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop … Some composers wish to have each player perform double-stops. In that case, it is safer to write non div." The score already writes "non div." for its other full-section double stops (91 c265, 138 c307, 277 c274). PDF 29: a triple stop needs f or louder. Vln II's G3–D4–B4 at bar 2 is ff and has two open strings, so it stays a triple stop.

**Fix (intro.ily).** The word lasts through 5–6. Bar 3 is single notes, so no "unis." is needed. The violas keep "div." and "unis.". This is text only.
```
introVlnOne = { <c'' aes''>1->\ff\dnup^\markup \italic "non div." | ...
introVlnTwo = { <es' c''>1->\ff\dnup^\markup \italic "non div." | ...
introVc = { <aes, es>1->\ff\dnup^\markup \italic "arco, non div." | ...
```
This touches the protected bars 1–2 only by adding a performance word. The pitches, the voicing, the \dnup bowing and the German sixth are unchanged.

### Considered and not applied (decided)

- **Undulating tremolo under the horn call (243–245), from Brahms 1/iv 31–32 (Adler PDF 51, Ex. II-50).** This is the device the movement's own model uses under its alphorn call, and the cyclic rule allows tremolo after 223. Not applied. At 243–244 each violin player has one note (div. octaves G♯→G, then V chords), and a fingered or undulating tremolo needs two notes per player, so the notes would have to be rewritten. c355 and c359 kept the strings at mf in a moving harmony under a stringendo. Brahms uses the device pp over a static chord in a slow introduction, a different function.
- **String trills (PDF 48).** Every held string note where a trill would make sense lies in protected bars (107–113, 139–150, 205–210, 216–223, 255–262). Elsewhere no held note is free.
- **Natural harmonics (PDF 62–66).** The only soft held high string notes are B♭5 at 43–50, which is not a natural harmonic and is already artificial, and the protected 95–98. 133–134's G5 is tied from an mf attack, so it cannot change to a harmonic in the middle.
- **Mutes (PDF 59–60).** The violins rest 171–186 and could put mutes on, but they play 187–198 without a break into the ff at 199, so there is no time to take them off.
- **la metà, non vibrato, sul ponticello, col legno (PDF 32–33, 52–53).** Considered at 43–50, 135–137 and 171–186. Non vibrato would contradict the crescendo at 135–137, and the rest are protected or outside the idiom. No change.
- **Violas at 95–98.** They hold B♭3–E♭4 and then B♭3–E4 as double stops, because "non div." from 91 still applies. That reading is the right one: c230 has the violas carry the E♭4→E4 thread "alone". No change.
