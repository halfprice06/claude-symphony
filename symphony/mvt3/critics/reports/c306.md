## Findings

**1. 27-34: the flutes hold an inaudible pad in their weak octave while the clarinets play the violins' countersubject a2 in their shrill top. Swap the roles: the flutes take the countersubject and the clarinets rest. (Applied.)**

*Problem.* Bars 27-34 have F1 in the bass at ff (violas, cellos, Bn, Hn 3-4, B.cl, Cbsn, tuba) and the violins' countersubject at ff. Since c217, Cl 1-2 a2 double the countersubject at pitch. That line rises to concert C6-F6 in every bar from 30 to 32, which is written D6-G6. Fl 1-2 meanwhile hold a mf pad: Fl 1 has G5-B♭5-A♭5, the same notes as Ob 2 an octave lower, and Fl 2 has E♭5-D5-F5, which is Ob 1's part in unison, note for note.

*Evidence.* Adler PDF 184: "A great orchestrator tries to assign a part to each instrument which sounds best on it." PDF 185, Ex. VII-2: the flute's middle octave is "sweet with little carrying power", and a register described as weak must not be covered, "for it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume out of his instrument in that register". At mf under ff violins, horns and bass, the flutes' G5/E♭5 cannot be heard, and they only duplicate the oboes. The flute's upper octave is "clear and brilliant" (Ex. VII-2) and agile (PDF 187, Ex. VII-7, Rossini). PDF 193: the flute is a "most successful doubler". Ex. VII-56 (PDF 210) puts the clarinet's written D6 upward in the band "piercing, shrill", and PDF 174 says intonation is "much harder to control in extreme registers ... more true of the highest". Two clarinets in unison on written F6-G6, after 16 bars of slurred eighths at 11-26 (c216's scan), are the riskiest wind writing on the page.

*Answer to c217.* c217's aim was a wind colour at pitch on the countersubject, as in Adler's Wagner example (PDF 178). That aim is kept, now with the instrument whose brilliant octave matches the line. c217 also wanted to stop the clarinets doubling the horns, and the clarinets still do not double them, because they rest. The countersubject and the bass statement (protected by c007 and c222) keep every note. Only the doubling instruments change.

*Fix (applied, sections/pTwo.ily).* Fl 1-2 a2, f, take the countersubject:
`c'''8(\f bes'' aes'' g'') f''( d'' b' d'') | bes'8( d'' f'' aes'') c'''( aes'' f'' c'') | b'8( d'' f'' d'') g''( f'' es'' d'') | … | b''8( c''' d''' b'' g''4) r4 |`
To give the flutes breath (PDF 188, 192), the two players stagger their rests. Fl 2 drops the last eighth of 28 (`c'''( aes'' f'') r8`), and the violins keep that eighth. In 30, Fl 1 holds `aes''2 g''4 r4` above the line, which are the old pad's notes, and rejoins at 31. My first version kept the flutes a2 in bar 30, but then the countersubject's F5-E♭5 became the top voice in octaves with the bass F2-E♭2, and analysis.txt reported one outer-voice parallel. Fl 1's held A♭5-G5 removes that parallel, and the result is 0 again. Cl 1-2 get `R1*8` for 27-34 and re-enter unchanged at 35, mf, on a fresh breath. In the score Fl 1 is stemmed up and Fl 2 down, with no crossing (checked at 300 dpi).

*For finale.md:* "Cl a2 with the countersubject 27-34 (c217)" becomes "Fl 1-2 a2 with the countersubject 27-34 (c306); clarinets rest 27-34". F1, F2, the chorale and the quotations are unchanged.

**2. 33-38 Bassoon 1 part: eighths up to B♭4 in bass clef. Use tenor clef in the part. (Applied, part only.)**

From 33.3 to 38, Bn 1 runs in slurred eighths up to A♭4 and B♭4. In bass clef that means three or four ledger lines at 2 = 84. Adler PDF 225: "when the ledger lines begin to accumulate, the tenor clef is used (from about the G above middle C upward)." The shared score staff must stay in bass clef because Bn 2 has A♭2 at 35-36, so the change applies to the part only. Fix in pTwoBsnOne: `\tag #'part { \clef tenor }` before `f4( aes8 c'8 f'4 aes'4)` at 33, and `\tag #'part { \clef bass }` before `d'1~\>` at 39. I built the Bassoon 1 part alone: 4 pages, no warnings, tenor clef 33-38. The score and the MIDI are unchanged. (The 115-122 solo reaches A4 only once, at 117, so it stays in bass clef, as c216 decided.)

**3. 43-44: the flute's low register. Checked; no change.** Fl 1's motive x falls to A♭4 at p. Under it are a pp violin harmonic, pp held strings and a single harp harmonic per bar. This is the setting Adler shows for the low flute (PDF 186, Ex. VII-3/4: "careful to let the flute come through by doing little with the other instruments").

**4. Contrabassoon G2 at 24, 87 and 164. Checked; no change.** PDF 230 advises keeping the contrabassoon in "the lowest twelfth". G2 is a tone above that twelfth (B♭0-F2). Each G2 is a single note, doubling the cellos and bassoons, and at 87 it starts the protected 6/3 descent G2-F2-E♭2-D2. The contrabassoon is not left to carry a high line alone, so this follows the book.

**5. The bass clarinet still has no line of its own. Checked again; no change.** Adler PDF 217-218 calls the chalumeau the bass clarinet's "most distinctive, the warmest" register, "mysterious and shadowy", and shows it in legato lines (*Tristan* II, Ex. VII-75). In this finale the instrument plays held or bass-doubling notes only, in 1-6, 27-34, 101-106, 144-150, 223 and 257-279. I looked again at every soft, low passage that is not protected. At 43-50 a fourth statement of motive x in the bass clarinet (49-50) would fill the gap the plan leaves before the trumpet solo ("strings thin out"). At 115-122 any added low line covers the bassoon solo, against PDF 226. At 235-238 the bassoons and contrabassoon rest on purpose (c036, c243). The other places (135-138, 167-170, 216-222) are protected, as c221 found. I decided to leave it.

**6. Clarinets at written E6-F♯6 in 227-230 and 265-268. Checked; no change.** These notes are in the shrill band too, but they are ff in full tuttis with Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I on the same notes. PDF 210 gives the clarinet its full dynamic range in every register. Both passages are also protected (223-234; 265-272).
