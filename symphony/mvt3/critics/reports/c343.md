## Findings

### 1. Bar 159: the recapitulation's consequent has a bass leap where the reading's passing V4/3 belongs (applied)

**Problem.** In the exposition, F1's consequent (19) has had a stepwise bass E♭-D-C since c313, through a passing V6/4. At the recapitulation's consequent (159), the strongest tutti statement of F1, the bass still leaps i6-i (E♭2-C2 in Vc, Cb, Bn, Cbsn, B.tbn and Tuba). The upper parts simply hold C minor for the whole bar. The recapitulation is the place to intensify the exposition, but here it is the weaker of the two statements.

**Evidence.** A&S Unit 8 (PDF 124, Ex. 8-5) gives V4/3 as the passing chord from I6 down to I over 3̂-2̂-1̂, with the 7th falling to 3̂. PDF 121-122 shows V7 inversions prolonging the tonic. PDF 127 asks for complete inversions with 5̂ held as a common tone. Ex. 8-10 shows the bass figure itself. The score has never used V4/3 this way; c163 and c253 recorded it as missing. Using V4/3 here, rather than repeating 19's V6/4, adds the 7th. That makes the recapitulation's consequent more tense than the exposition's, as the form requires. The chord is inverted and falls on beat 3, so it creates no cadence in mid-phrase (PDF 115, 119 rule 5). It also does not break the cyclic rule against root-position V-I in C before 261.

**Fix (in sections/recP.ily, bar 159 only):**
- Vc, Bn 1, Bn 2, B.tbn: `es2 d4 c4` (was `es2 c2`).
- Cb, Cbsn: `es,2 d,4 c,4`. Tuba: `es,2\f d,4 c,4`.
- Vln II and Cl 1, second half: `f''( d'' es'' c'')` (was `c''( es'' g'' es'')`). The 7th F5 falls on beat 3 and resolves to E♭5 on beat 4. It is reached by the diminished fifth B4-F5 and left by the turn down to D. A&S PDF 131, Ex. 8-16: a dissonant leap is followed by a change of direction. The final C5 leads by step to the B♭4 at 160.
- Tbn 1: `c'2 b4 c'4`. The leading tone is in this voice only and resolves up.
- Tbn 2: `es2 g4 g4`. Cl 2: `es'2\mf g'4 g'4`.

The result at 159.3 is a complete D-F-G-B with G held in the tune. At 159.4, the E♭ is doubled by contrary motion (D-E♭ against F-E♭), which PDF 101 (Ex. 6-17) calls good.

**First try rejected.** I first gave the D to Cl 2 (E♭4-D4-E♭4). par8 flagged it as moving in octaves with the bass (E♭-D), so Cl 2 now goes to G instead.

**Protection.** HISTORY protects 159-166 for its scoring, tune and Vln I divisi (c107-c109, c134, c166, c167). All of these are unchanged: tune, divisi, dynamics, instrumentation and the counter-line's first half. Only the counter-line's beats 3-4 changed, by four eighths. F1 and the other own themes are unchanged. The Vln II/Cl 1 counter-line is not one of the finale's own themes.

### 2. VII6 as a passing or neighbour chord is not used; no place for it (not applied)

**Problem.** A&S Unit 7 (PDF 112-116, Ex. 7-12 to 7-17) teaches VII6 as the passing chord between I and I6, or as a neighbour to I. The score never uses it.

**Places checked.** I checked each unprotected place:
- 3-8: unison and pedal intro with no I-I6 motion.
- 43-50: a B♭ pedal under V7 of E♭, so VII6 would contradict the pedal.
- 67-76: E♭ pedal and F2's harmony. The bass at 70-76 leaps by design, and c312 already placed a VIIø7 at 75.3.
- 167-170: a chromatic VI-Fr6-V7♭9-V6/5 approach, where VII6 would weaken the augmented sixth.

**Decision.** No change. At 159 the passing V4/3 does the same job as a VII6 would, and PDF 115-116 (Ex. 7-18) counts V4/3 and VII6 as one harmony there, so 159 is the better choice.
