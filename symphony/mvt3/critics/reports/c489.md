## Findings (ranked)

### F1. 242→243: the common tone of the move to III♯ is broken. APPLIED

- **Bars:** 240–243, sHead.ily (`sHeadHnThree`, `sHeadHnFour`).
- **Problem:** Since c470, 242 is iiø7 (D–F–A♭–C) and 243 is E major (III♯), which closes the symphony's C–A♭–E cycle. In the strings and woodwinds the only link between the two chords is the A♭/G♯. In v489 that tone sounds at 242 only in Hn 4 (A♭3) and the harp (spelled G♯4). Hn 4 then breathes at the barline and re-strikes G♯3 f at 243, so the common tone is never held across the change of key.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 586, Ex. 32-3 (*Widmung*): a change of key by a third with no pivot chord "does not sound abrupt, for an important common element helps to integrate the two tonal regions: the melody tone A♭/G♯, sustained … above the chord progression A♭–E (F♭)". It is the same pair of tones and the same third relation (A♭ to E) as here. The same pitch class also joins the scherzo's last G♯ to this movement's opening A♭ (coherence/finale.md, Opening), so holding it at 242→243 repeats the symphony's own link.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-489/work):**
  - Hn 4: `c'1\mf | a1 | a1 | aes1 \breathe | gis2\f( g2~ |` becomes `c'1\mf | a1 \breathe | a1 | aes1~\< | gis2\f( g2~ |`. A♭3 is tied, enharmonically, into G♯3. The breath moves to after 240, where the horn repeats A3 anyway.
  - Hn 3, so that the shared staff has equal marks: `c'1\mf | c'1 | d'1 | d'1 \breathe |` becomes `c'1\mf | c'1 \breathe | d'1 | d'1\< \breathe |`.
  - Pitches are unchanged, and c470's ♮6–♭6–5 line (A–A♭–G♯–G) stays.
  - Hn 4 now holds 241–246 for about 11 s at mf–f in the low register, which is playable.
- **Checks:** 16 pages and 0 warnings. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 240–244 lists only bass doublings, the same as v489. The page image (p. 14) shows the tie written E♭5→D♯5 at horn pitch, with the crescendo on both horns.

### F2. Sibelius 2/iv: a conductor would accept this finale beside it. No change

Sibelius builds his whole finale toward one release. The lamenting second theme over a scale ostinato returns in the tonic minor and is pushed for many pages before D major breaks through over a long tonic pedal.

This finale reaches the same kind of goal by another route.
- The minor material is exhausted in the hammered tutti at 199–204.
- The A♭7 build and the D♭ episode follow, then sub. p at 219 and the crescendo into 223.
- The release is one prepared event: the stop chord resolves, there is tremolo and the C7, and the chorale enters.

Where Sibelius uses one theme over one pedal, this coda has four stages: arrival, chorale, second-theme head and combination. That was accepted at c219 and is protected, and I do not re-raise it. What this score has and Sibelius's finale lacks:
- the cyclic quotations;
- an earned single root-position V–I (262–263).

What Sibelius does better:
- the length of the build into the major (his ostinato build runs far longer than 205–223);
- the economy of one memorable line.

Changing either would break protected passages (205–223, 223–234). The fourth series is meant for local work, so I decided on no structural change.

### F4. 240: a passing V4/3 of IV between IV6 and IV (Ex. 25-1, PDF 419). Tested, declined

Since c482 the bass at 240 moves A (IV6) → F (IV) on beat 2. Beethoven's bar 10 suggests passing through G with an applied V4/3 of IV (G–B♭–C–E). Over that chord the tune's accented A6 (240.2–3, movement I's quoted head) would sound against B♭ as a minor ninth, and the step G→F under the held A6 would leave no room for c482's 5–6. Not better; no change.
