## Findings

### 1. 35-42: Fl 1 has no rest for eight bars at f after sixteen hard bars (applied)
Problem: Fl 1 plays F1's consequent at 19-26 (to A♭6 at 22), the countersubject at 27-34 and the A♭6 head at 35-36, then holds B♭5-A♭5 for six bars at f to p (37-42) before its p scale at 43. From 34.4 to 43.1 there is no rest (11.4 s by breath.py), the second-longest flute span in the score and the longest in a passage that is not protected. Fl 2 rests 35-38 (c311).
Evidence: Adler PDF 188: "the flute requires a great deal of breath... give the flutist time to rest after especially difficult or sustained passages"; Ex. VII-9 (Brahms 1/iv 38-46): the second flute holds the long note so the first can breathe, the two "dovetailed with great care to preserve the illusion of a single flute". PDF 192: flutes "relieve one another in long, fast, or sustained passages".
Fix (applied, pTwo.ily): Fl 2 enters at 37.1 on the same B♭5 and holds the line through 38; Fl 1 stops at 37.3 and re-enters on 38.3 under Fl 2's attack, tied into 39 as before. Fl 2 still rests 35-36 (c311's reason kept), and goes to its D5 at 39 with its f carried from 37. Fl 1's step C6 → B♭5 at 36.4-37.1 (c394) is kept.

### 2. 205-208: the contrabassoon has 32 bars without a rest; four of them only double the tuba at the unison (applied)
Problem: the Cbsn plays every bar from 199 to 230 (hammered tutti, A♭ pedal, D♭ episode, stop chord, arrival and chorale bass), with breath marks only, mostly at A♭1, F1, D1 and C1. At 205-208 it holds A♭1 at f at the unison with the tuba and the basses, so it adds no octave.
Evidence: Adler PDF 230: "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests"; the contrabassoon is a "32-foot" foundation "when it doubles cellos, basses, and bassoons at the octave". Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: a group re-entering with a crescendo adds to it. The same reasoning gave the tuba its rest at 217-220 (c406, c483).
Fix (applied, codaOne.ily): `codaOneCbsn = { ... g,,4-> g,,4-> r2 | R1*4 | aes,,1\f\< | aes,,2. r4\! | }`. The Cbsn now rests 204.3-208 and re-enters f < on A♭1 at 209 with the crescendo into 211. A♭1 stays in the tuba and basses, A♭2 in the bass trombone (and Bn 2 from 207). The horns' F1 head at 205-206 (protected since c224) and the 205-223 dynamic path are unchanged.

### 3. 239-246: both bassoons hold eight bars without a breath (applied)
Problem: Bn 1 (E3, F3, F3, F3-C4, ...) and Bn 2 (A2 tied, F2, D3, D2, ...) hold eight bars at f to mf under the second-theme head; the only breath is after 246. At 2 = 66 that is about 14.5 s, the longest unbroken wind span in the score by breath.py.
Evidence: Adler PDF 175 (careful consideration of the limitations placed on the player), PDF 188 and 192 (stagger the breaths of a pair so the sound does not drop).
Fix (applied, sHead.ily, score marks only): `sHeadBsnOne = { R1*4 | e1\f | f1 | f1 \breathe | f2 ...` (on the re-struck F3) and `sHeadBsnTwo = { R1*4 | a,1\f~ | a,4 f,2. | d1 | d,1\> \breathe | e,2 ...` (at the leap D2-E2). The two breaths fall a bar apart. Notes, the 235-247 plan and the III♯ approach are unchanged.
