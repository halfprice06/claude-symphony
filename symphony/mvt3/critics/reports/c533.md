## Findings

### 1. Bar 176: a bare leading-tone triad in 5/3 position under the recapitulated F2 (applied)
- **Problem.** At 176 beats 3–4 the cor anglais and muted trumpet pass E♭4–D4 over V6. Cl 2 falls G3→F3 together with the violas (D3/F3), and Bn 1 holds B2. For half a bar the harmony is B–D–F with no G, and the seventh F sits in unison in Cl 2 and the violas. That is a diminished triad on the leading tone in 5/3 position.
- **Evidence.** The reading calls this sonority "seldom satisfactory". It says V6/5, "which contains all three tones of the diminished VII", "creates a far more pleasing sonority and is to be preferred". VII5/3 belongs only in three-part writing, and then with its third doubled, not a dissonant tone (PDF 243 sec. 12, PDF 244 Ex. 15-18, PDF 245 Points 9: "a leading tone in the bass should usually support V6/5"). c263 applied the same rule at 172 and 180 but missed 176. My scan of all 279 bars finds no other root-position diminished triad: 20.3 and 30.1 are incomplete vii°4/3 chords over F. The exposition's matching bar 56 has the root B♭ in the bass, so the problem exists only in the recapitulation.
- **Fix (recS.ily, recSClTwo, sixth bar):** `| f1 | g2 f2 | es4 g4~` → `| f1 | g1 | es4 g4~`.
  - The bar is now a complete V6/5 (B2, D3, F3, G3) under the passing E♭4.
  - The seventh F is in the violas only and resolves to E♭3 at 177, where Cl 2 also arrives on E♭3 from G3. This removes the earlier F3→E♭3 unison motion of Cl 2 and the violas.
  - Tune, dynamics and the pp scoring are unchanged. The MIDI diff is one note: the F3 attack at 176.3 is gone.

### 2. Bars 175–177: ♮VII–V6/5–i with a chromatic bass B♭–B–C (tested, declined)
- **Candidate.** This is the one place where Ex. 15-14 (PDF 241) fits without the B♮-in-the-tune objection c353 raised at 13, 21, 153 and 161: the cor anglais at 175–177 has no B. The change would be bass B♭ at 175 (Cb B♭1, Vc B♭2), Bn 1 D3, under the tune's F4–A♭4, making B♭7 → V6/5 → i.
- **Why declined.** At present 174–176 have the stepwise bass F–E♭–D–(B)–C under iv–i6–iiø7–V6. Putting B♭ at 175 breaks that descent with a falling fourth. It also replaces the iiø7, a true predominant, with VII7, which the reading describes as a "Baroque" and weaker approach to V (PDF 241: "especially in Baroque music"). It loses more than it gains. No change.

### 3. Bar 20.1: III under the tune's descending natural 7 (tested, declined)
- **Candidate.** PDF 237–238 (Ex. 15-10, 15-11b): III is the typical support for a passing natural 7 in a falling line. At 20.1 the tune's B♭5 (start of B♭–A♭–G–F–E♭) has a B♭ bass (v6), so 7 is doubled in the bass. An E♭ bass (III) would put 7 a fifth above the bass.
- **Why declined.** It would move from i on 19.3 to III on the downbeat of 20, a weak-to-strong I→III that the reading says "tends to contradict the meter" (PDF 240). It would also undo c257's falling tetrachord and c322's F–F♯–G bass. The present v6 is the better chord. No change.

### 4. Bar 96: a passing IV6 between ♮VII and V7 of F minor (tested, declined)
- **Candidate.** 95–98 is E♭7 → C7 → F minor. That is ♮VII → V7 in the development's key, with E♭'s fifth B♭ held as C7's seventh, as PDF 242 describes ("the 5th of natural VII forms a good preparation for the 7th of V7"). Ex. 15-15 would add a passing D♭ bass on 96.3 (IV6 of F minor) to make the bass step E♭–D♭–C.
- **Why declined.** The top D♭5–C5 at 96–97 is Ob 1's recollection of movement II. A D♭ bass under it gives outer-voice octaves D♭→C. No change.

### 5. Form and proportion against the unit (checked, no change)
- The exposition makes III a key area and confirms it twice (66, 90). The development moves III → iv → V. The recapitulation drops the III stopover and turns to VI (187), as a minor-mode recapitulation should once the tonic must hold. The coda's chain III–VI–II–V–I (255–263) and its III♯ approach (243) are the reading's major-mode uses, in the protected places.
- The coda stages build in order: hammered tutti, theme B, arrival and chorale, second-theme head, combination, last chorale, close. The slow-to-fast ratio inside the coda was raised and kept before (c219, c179), and I found no new reason to reopen it.
