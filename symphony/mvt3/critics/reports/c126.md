## What else I studied

I built a MIDI file with each wind and brass player on its own staff, ran it through LilyPond's articulate.ly, and listed every stretch where a player sounds for more than 9 seconds without a gap. That list is how I found the passages below; I then read each passage in the score and the sources, because MIDI does not show breath marks or tongued note changes. I also looked at pages 14-16 of v126 and of my scratch build, the composer's notes in finale.ily, sTwo.ily and recS.ily, and HISTORY.md (in particular c046, c066, c078 and c086, which already added some breaths and rests in the coda).

## Findings

**1. 257-264 (brass 255-264): the chorale is held 8 bars by the woodwinds and 10 by the brass with no breath, crescendo mf to fff.** At 2 = 88 that is 10.4 s for Fl 1 (E6-D6-C6-B5-C6), Ob 1-2, Cl 1-2, B.cl., Bn 1-2 and Cbsn, and 13.2 s for Tpt 1-2, Tbn 1-2, B.tbn and tuba. The loudest notes come last (fff at 263). The players would each take a breath at a note change, all at the same places and with no instruction. The composer marks breaths every four bars in the D♭ episode 211-218 at a slower tempo (2 = 72, 6.7 s), so these chorale bars are the exception. Adler PDF 188 (Ex. VII-9, Brahms 1/iv) is exactly this case: one player holds while the other breathes, "so that the first flute may breathe without diminishing the intensity of the passage"; PDF 192 says doubled flutes "relieve one another in long... or sustained passages"; PDF 230 says the low contrabassoon needs periodic breaths. Fix (applied, finale.ily), breath marks at tie ends, in two groups so that every pitch keeps a carrier through each breath:
- after 258: Fl 1, Cl 1-2, Cbsn, Tpt 1-2, Tbn 1-2, tuba (e.g. `e'''1\mf\<~ | e'''1 \breathe | d'''1\f\<~`);
- after 260: Ob 1-2, B.cl., Bn 1-2, Hn 1-2, B.tbn (e.g. `d''1\f\<~ | d''1 \breathe | c''1`);
- Fl 1 also after 261 (`c'''1 \breathe | b''1`), so the fff C6 from 263 starts on fresh breath.
The top line stays in Vln I, Ob 1/Cl 2 (E5) and the group that is not breathing; Tpt 3 and Hn 3-4 re-articulate half notes and need no mark. No pitch or rhythm changes, so the protected chorale top line 255-264 is unchanged.

**2. 257-263 Fl 2: six bars of eighth-note arpeggios run straight into the fff E5 at 263 with no place to breathe.** Each bar is one slur on one breath (PDF 176). The whole stretch is 10.4 s, much of it on A6 and A♭6. The figure is doubled by Vln II, which plays it throughout (c117). Fix (applied): Fl 2 drops the last eighth of 259 and of 261, `f'''( d''' aes'') r8 |` and `aes'''( fis''' es''') r8 |`. These breaths fall at different points from Fl 1's (258, 261 end of bar), so the two flutes do not breathe on the same beat, which is what PDF 192 asks for. Vln II keeps the complete figure.

**3. 265-268 Ob 1: G5 leaps a sixth to E6 at ff on movement I's head, after 18 bars of playing with only a 2-bar rest.** From 247 to 276 Ob 1 rests only at 255-256. PDF 198 says rest periods are "mandatory" for the oboist. The same page puts C6-F6 in the "thin... quite hard to control" register. PDF 200 says oboe runs work when they are "not based on extraordinarily large skips". At 265-268 the head already has five carriers on E6 (Fl 1-2, Cl 1, Vln I-II), so a thin oboe E6 adds nothing that can be heard. c086 (Sebesky) rested Ob 2 here and kept Ob 1 "with Fl, Cl, Vln". I am going one step further, for the reasons above. Moving Ob 1 down an octave is not an option, because that would undo c047, which at 265-268 moved the head up so it no longer crosses F1 in the trumpets, horns and violas. Fix (applied): Ob 1 `R1*4 | c''2->(\ff ...` at 265-269. Both oboes now re-enter a2 and fresh at 269 on F1, and the a2 label prints there.

**4. 135-158 Bn 2: about 34 s without a rest.** That is 16 bars of whole notes in the retransition, pp to f, then the ff G at 147-150 and the ff recapitulation 151-158 (the first rest is at 158.4). PDF 175 says to give "careful consideration... to the limitations placed on the player". The contrabassoon breathes at 146 (c066), but Bn 2, which shares that bass B, does not. Fix (applied, retr.ily): `b,1\f | b,2. r4 | g,1->\ff`. The B♮ bass at 146 stays in Cbsn, Vc and Cb, so the protected B♮ bass 138-146 is kept.

**5. 77-86 Fl 1 and Ob 1: 14.7 s without a rest, and the 82 run goes straight into the f closing theme at 83.** The flute enters p on E♭6 and climbs by a slurred fourth F6-B♭6 to f at 78. That is the "top fourth" which PDF 186 says to approach by scale. It then carries the F2 continuation, the run G-A♭-B♭-D (82, c113) and the closing theme 83-86. A flutist cannot breathe before 83 without spoiling the run. The only clean place is the slur end at 78.4, where Vln I also changes bow. Fix (applied, sTwo.ily): `bes'''4\f aes'''4 g'''4 f'''4) \breathe |`, with the same in Ob 1. I kept the B♭6. It is in the professional range, it is doubled at pitch by Vln I at f, and the slur leads up through E♭6-F6 toward it. Rewriting 77 as a scale would change F2 in one doubling only.

**6. Contrabassoon (and tuba) 247-254, 257-264, 226-230: long low blocks without breath marks.** At 247-254 the Cbsn and tuba play eight whole notes at f under the two heads, then (after two bars' rest) A1-F1-A♭1-G1-C1 up to fff. At 226-230 the Cbsn holds B0-A1-F1-E1/B♭1-C1 at 2 = 56 (10.7 s) after the breath at 225. PDF 230: these low notes take "considerable" breath and need "quite a few periodic rests". Fix (applied): Cbsn and tuba `g,,1 \breathe | e,2 c,2` after 250 (where Tpt 1-2 and Hn 1-2 breathe since c046); Cbsn after 258 (finding 1); Cbsn `f,,1 \breathe | e,,2 bes,,2` after 228 (arrival.ily). These are breath marks only; the arrival chord at 223 is not touched.

**7. 167-182 Cl 2 and 171-182 Bn 1: soft pads 16 and 12 bars long with no breath.** Cl 2 goes from the recapitulation's end (167-170, tied F4) straight into the pp pad under the cor anglais and muted trumpet. That is 22.5 s; Bn 1's pad is 16.7 s. The soloists breathe at 174 and 178 and the pads do not. A clarinet at pp uses little air, but PDF 175 asks that these limits be considered all the same. Fix (applied): Cl 2 `f'1\pp \breathe |` at 170 (the section's double bar), and Cl 2 and Bn 1 breathe after 178 together with the solo (`g2 f2 \breathe`, `b,1 \breathe`).

**8. The rewritten close 265-279 (the focus): checked, no change needed beyond finding 3.**
- 268: Fl 1-2's E6-D6-C6-B5 is a slurred step line leading into 269. That is easy.
- 270: the staccato quarters give the flutes their breath before 271. They then breathe again at the comma after 272 (c078) and rest at 277.
- 271-272: the flutes' A6 is reached through the run D5-G5-A5-B5-D6 and then a tongued fifth. A6 is the bottom of the range PDF 186 warns about, not inside it, and both flutes play it at ff in a tutti. This follows the book.
- 269-271: the oboes' a2 descent to E4-D4 is at ff, which PDF 198 allows. It only rules out pp in that register.
- 265-268: Cl 1's concert E6 (written F♯6) is ff, and PDF 210 gives the clarinet its full dynamic range in every register.
- 273-279: Bn 1's E4 line and the Cbsn's C1 at 278 after a bar's rest (277) follow PDF 226 and 230.
