## Findings

### 1. Bars 195-196: the recapitulated F2 has no passing chord between i6 and i, where the exposition now has one (applied)

**Problem.** Critic 403 gave the exposition's E♭-major F2 a stepwise bass at 75-76: I6 - passing V6/4 - I - V6/5 - I over G-F-E♭-D-E♭. The matching bars of the A♭ statement in the recapitulation, 195-196 (turned to A♭ minor by c370), still hold C♭2 for the whole of 195 and then leap a diminished 4th to G1 at 196. Critic 403 left 195 alone for two reasons: the chord is different (minor i6, by mixture), and 187-198 scoring is protected. Neither reason stops the technique. The passing 6/4 works in minor as in major, and the change adds no instrument and no doubling (the protection of c207/c225 is against thickening). The held bass also has a fault the exposition never had: Bn 1 doubles the bass on C♭3 and moves C♭3-B♭2 at 195.4→196.1 while the violins and violas move C♭-B♭. That is a pair of octaves between the bass doubling and the tune (allpairs_par.py, beat 780 in v493).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 309-310 (Ex. 19-4 bar 2, Ex. 19-9) and PDF 324 point 5: the passing V6/4 between I6 and I is the most important passing 6/4. Its 4th is stable (here the tune's held E♭, the 5th of i), the bass is the active tone, and the bass tone is often doubled.
- A&S PDF 256-257: a stepwise bass is an expanded passing motion that gives continuity over a span. With the fix the bass runs C♭-B♭-A♭-G-A♭, the exposition's G-F-E♭-D-E♭ a third lower, so the recapitulation answers the exposition bar for bar in the place where the restated phrase begins.
- A&S PDF 258 sec. 12: an 8-7-8 neighbour calls for raised 7 and major V. Hn 1 now plays A♭3-G♮3-A♭3, as Hn 1 plays E♭4-D4-E♭4 at 75.
- The cellos' existing rising line C♭3-D♭3-E♭3 becomes the passing 7th against the falling bass, as the violas' and cellos' A♭ does at 75.3.

**Fix** (sections/recS.ily, bar 195 = the 25th bar of each variable; all inside the existing `\transpose c aes,`):
```
recSCb:      \transpose c aes, { es,1 }      ->  \transpose c aes, { es,2 d,4 c,4 }     % C♭2 B♭1 A♭1
recSBsnOne:  \transpose c aes, { es1 }       ->  \transpose c aes, { es2 d4 c4 }        % C♭3 B♭2 A♭2 (doubles the bass)
recSBsnTwo:  \transpose c aes, { c1 }        ->  \transpose c aes, { c2 g,2 }           % A♭2 E♭2 (stable 4th, then 5th)
recSHnOne:   \transpose c aes, { c'1\p }     ->  \transpose c aes, { c'2\p b4 c'4 }     % A♭3 G3 A♭3
```
- 195.1-2: i6 over C♭, as before (c370's minor colour keeps the first half of the bar).
- 195.3: B♭1 with E♭ (tune, violas, Hn 2, Bn 2), G (Hn 1) and D♭ (cellos): passing V6/4 with a passing 7th, the sonority of 75.3 transposed.
- 195.4: A♭ minor root position. 196 unchanged (V6/5 - i).
- Bn 2 takes E♭2, not A♭2, so it makes no octaves with the bass A♭1→G1 into 196.
- Tune, cellos, violas, Hn 2, dynamics, the harp's and clarinets' rests, and 197-198 (protected bass line) are unchanged.

### 2. Proportions: the development and recapitulation are short and the coda long, by design (no change)

The development with the retransition is 17.7% of the time, against 20.1% in Dvořák 9/iv; the recapitulation is 16.3% against 22.1%; the coda is 31.2% against 21.3%. Critics 43, 219, 313 and 473 recorded this. The reason still holds: the plan keeps C major, the brass chorale and the only root-position V-I for the coda, so the coda carries the movement's goal. Shortening the coda or lengthening the development would touch protected bars (199-279, the development key plan). The reading supports the large plan: the key areas run III - iv - V, the rising step line that A&S PDF 250-251 (Ex. 16-2b) calls the useful form of ascending motion, with the IV area as the passing chord between III and V. No change.

### 3. The coda's stages build; their bass plans differ (no change)

Each stage has its own tempo and bass. The arrival (2 = 56) is a C pedal with a plagal close. The second-theme head (66) descends C-A-F-D in 3rds, the motion "down a 7th from I to II" of A&S PDF 253, then III♯ before the 6/4; the I-III♯-V problem of PDF 251 is avoided because the cadential 6/4 is at 243.3 and V4/3 follows at 245. The combination (88) has a stepwise bass with two evaded cadences on I6 (251, 255). The last chorale descends E-A-F-A♭-G-C to the only root V-I. The tempo runs 56 → 66 → 88 → 72 and the dynamics fff (223) → p (235) → ff (247) → fff (263, 278), so each stage starts lower and reaches higher. One weakness remains: from 223 to the end, about 30 of 57 bars stand on a C pedal (223-225, 229-238, 263-279), and both pedals are decorated by neighbouring IV6/4s (A&S PDF 308-309). Critics 223 and 403 noted this and the bars are protected. The second pedal carries new material (F1 in major, the closing chain, the scherzo quotation), so it does not sag. No change.

### 4. The last cadence 259-263 has no cadential 6/4 (considered, not applied)

A&S PDF 317-319 says the tension of a structural cadence can be raised by expanding the cadential 6/4 or by putting material between the 6/4 and its V. Bars 259-263 go iiø6/5 (two bars), German sixth, V7, I, with no 6/4. A 6/4 on 262.1 would sound C-E over G under the chorale's protected whole-note B in Tpt 1 (c'1 | b1 | c'1), so the 6/4's C would clash with the B. To avoid that, the B would have to come on 262.3, which changes the chorale's last statement and the owed B→C. The cadence already gets its weight from the two-bar iiø6/5, the German sixth of bar 1 and the timpani roll. No change.

### 5. The long dominants at 39-50 and 139-150 (no change)

No change.
