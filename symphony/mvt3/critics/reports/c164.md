## Findings

### 1. 83-86: the clarinets' octave of F1 is buried in its own background (applied)
Problem: the closing group's F1 head is in three octaves: Fl 1·2 + Vln I (E♭6), Ob 1 + Vln II (E♭5), Cl 1·2 a2 (E♭4 down to F3). The clarinet octave lies in the same register as the cellos' f quarter-note arpeggios (E♭3-E♭4), Bn 1's f E♭4/F4/B♭3, Hn 2's B♭3 pedal and, just above, the violas' f eighths (E♭4-B♭4). Four background layers at f sit on a two-clarinet chalumeau line, so the lowest octave adds mud, not melody.
Evidence: Adler PDF 135 (keep the background out of the melody's register; foreground in the best register, background in the palest), PDF 144 nos. 1-3 (best register, thin the counterpoint, separate theme and counterpart by register), PDF 154 (Tchaikovsky: the accompaniment cannot interfere because it is in a different, non-brilliant register).
Fix (clos.ily, closClOne and closClTwo bars 83-86): up an octave, unison with Ob 1 and Vln II:
`es''2->(\f bes'4. c''8 | d''4-.) c''8( bes'8 aes'4-.) g'4-. | f'4( bes'8 c''8 d''4 f''4) | es''2-> r2 |`
The E♭5 octave is now as strong as the E♭6 octave (three instruments plus a violin section each). Range E♭5-F5 clarion. Horn pedal and Bn 1 (c147 protected) untouched; F1's notes unchanged, only a doubling moved, so no other thread is affected.

### 2. 151-158: clarinet broken chords ff inside the tune's octave (applied)
Problem: at the recapitulation the clarinets' eighths (Cl 1 E♭4-C5, Cl 2 G3-E♭4) are ff, the same level as F1 in Vln I·II and Ob 1·2, which lies in exactly that octave (D4-F5 at 152-154, the violins on the G and D strings). Every other accompanying layer (Tpt 1-3, Hn 3·4 off-beats, Tbn 1·2) is f. In the exposition the same figure is mf under the strings' f at 11.
Evidence: Adler PDF 128 and 132 (Haydn: the scoring and register decide which voice dominates), PDF 133 (violins weak in this low register), PDF 135 (background in the palest register).
Fix (recP.ily): `recPClOne ... es'8(\f`, `recPClTwo ... g8(\f` at 151 (were `\ff`). Same notes; one shared-staff dynamic, equal in both players.

### 3. 194: c163's V4/2 puts a major second at the bottom (applied)
Problem: the new joint gives the Cb E♭2-D♭2 while Bn 2 holds E♭2 all bar, so on beats 3-4 the bottom of the chord is D♭2-E♭2-G2 (Bn 1)-B♭2 (Vc): a second, then thirds, under an octave gap to B♭3 and B♭4. The exposition's joint at 74 is spaced A♭1 - B♭2 - D3 - F3 and is fine.
Evidence: Adler PDF 135, Ex. V-26 (greater space between the lower instruments, as in the overtone series).
Fix (recS.ily, recSBsnTwo bar 194): `\transpose c aes, { g,2 g2 }` (E♭2 with the Cb on beats 1-2, then E♭3, a ninth above the D♭2, as Bn 2's B♭2 over A♭1 at 74). The root stays in the chord; bottom now D♭2-G2-B♭2-E♭3. 195 continues to A♭2 as before.

### 4. 159-166: Vln I divided in octaves while full Vln II plays the counter-line in the tune's middle octave (not applied)
Adler PDF 152 says divisi weakens the first-violin line, and PDF 144 no. 3 asks for registral separation of theme and countertheme. Against that, c109's Rimsky reason (octave divisi is acceptable when the woodwind doubles and the line lies high) holds here: Fl 1·2 and Ob 1 double both halves. I checked it and keep it; no change, so c107, c109 and c134 stand.
