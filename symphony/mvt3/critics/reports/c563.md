## Findings

### 1. 113–115, the development's hinge: the F–F♯–G inflection in one part. Tested in the scratch build; not applied.
PDF 584–585 (Ex. 32-1): Beethoven's F♮→F♯ is a direct inflection in one inner voice of the pivotal D triad. The score has the same chord and the same pair: F minor (113) becomes D7 (114) and leads to G minor (115). But the pair is split between parts. Vln II has F5 at 113, Vln I takes F♯5 at 114, and the G5 arrives on 115.2 in Vln II's pizz. c473 had tested resolving F♯5→G5 inside Vln I and declined it (the slur would cross the page turn). I tested the other way of keeping the line in one part: Vln II plays the whole D7 top, so F5–F♯5–G5 and the seventh's C5–B♭4 (c172) are both in Vln II, and Vln I rests at 114:
```
devVlnOne: ... f'''2->\downbow r2 | R1 | R1*2 | ...            (was fis''1\p\> | R1*2\! |)
devVlnTwo: ... f''2->\downbow r2 | <c'' fis''>2.\p\>\upbow r4\! | r4 <bes' g''>4 ...   (was c''2.)
```
Result: 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 16 pages. par8.py at 112–116 shows only the existing bass octaves at 112.3, as in v563. Kept as /tmp/critic-563/test114-score.pdf. It is not better:
- The pitches and register are unchanged. The ear already hears F5→F♯5→G5 in the violin register, so the one-part version changes the notation and not the sound. PDF 585 says rhythm, texture and dynamics, not part allocation, decide how an inflection sounds.
- It loses the only sound on 114.4. Vln I's whole-note F♯5 bridges the hinge into the bassoon solo. In the test every string part stops on 114.3 for the pizz. change, so the hinge goes dead for a beat.
- A full Vln I section on one note becomes a pp diminuendo double stop (C5/F♯5, a tritone) in Vln II. That is less secure and thinner.

Decision: reverted.

### 2. Proportions and the coda's stages: they build. No change.
Timed from v563's MIDI: introduction 14.3 s (3.4%), exposition 133.4 s (31.5%), development 51.4 s (12.2%), retransition 23.3 s (5.5%), recapitulation 68.8 s (16.3%), coda 131.8 s (31.2%). Dvořák 9/iv (corpus form file, bars = onset/4 + 1): exposition 34%, development 20%, recapitulation 22%, coda 21%. This coda is longer because the recapitulation's tonal path (VI – ♭II – Ger6 – V – I) runs to 223. That path is the Widmung plan the reading names (PDF 593: ♭VI goes to V7 through ♭II) and Chopin's plan (PDF 593: ♭VI comes back as an augmented sixth to V). So 199–222 belongs to the recapitulation's harmony, and with it counted there the proportions are close to Dvořák's (c553 made the same count).

The coda's stages: 199–210 16.6 s, 211–222 20.7 s, 223–234 25.7 s, 235–246 21.4 s, 247–279 47.4 s. In the density profile the arrival is at the top (25.6 voices at 221–224), the S-head stage at 233–240 dips (12–13), and the music builds back to 23.5 at 257–264 and 26.6 at 273–276. The single quiet stage is 12 bars long and ends in a stringendo. It does not sag. PDF 603 (Points 5) asks that remote areas be tied to the motives. The D♭ stage enlarges movement I's D♭–C, and the E chord at 243 is the scherzo's key, taken with the scherzo's G♯5 in the scherzo's instrument.

### 3. The A♭ area 187–198 follows K. 542 and the Appassionata. No change.
PDF 596 and 601–602: after a deceptive cadence the ♭VI area does not relax; it turns minor, and the music moves on. The score: deceptive VI at 185→187, A♭ major 187–194, minor at 195–196 (c370), major climb at 197 with F♭ in the iv (c270), and a minor turn at 198.3 (c293). Then the cellos rise C♭4→C4 into 199 under a held E♭6, which is the common-tone link of PDF 585–587. On paper I considered keeping the minor from 195 to the end (one inflection, as K. 542 does), with C♭2 in the basses and C♭4 in Hn 1 at 197. I declined it: it would undo three accepted changes without a new reason. It would also take away the major at 197, which is what makes the C♭ at 198.3 heard as a change. That change is the inflection that PDF 585 says catches the ear.

### 4. 121–123, G minor to G major: the minor third returns as the major third in the same theme. No change.
PDF 597–598 (Creation) and 601–602 (K. 542): the flat note of the minor area comes back transformed and is tied to a motive. The bassoon solo has B♭ (115: `d'2.( bes4`; 121: `bes2 g2`). On paper I tested an A♯ (= B♭ respelled) on 122.4 leading to B at 123. I declined it: it sounds an augmented octave against Bn 1's held A3, and 115–130 is protected.
