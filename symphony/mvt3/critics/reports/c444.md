## Findings

### 1. 95–98: the strings play sul tasto under the oboe's recollection of movement II (applied)
- **Bars:** 95–98 (Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb), with "ord." at 99 (Vla, Vc, Cb) and 103 (Vln II).
- **Problem:** At 95–98 Ob 1 plays alone the tail E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5 at the pitch of movement II's last tail (II 85–89, Cl 1 dolce). It is the finale's only quotation that is a memory rather than a statement: the codetta's ff strokes have just ended and the development starts f at 99. The bed under it is ordinary bowing at mp > p: Vln II G4, violas B♭3/E♭4 (→ E4 at 97), cellos E♭3 → C3, basses E♭2 → C2, with Hn 3·4 G4/B♭3 p > pp. This is the same sound as the many other soft held chords in the movement, so nothing in the colour tells the listener that this is a recollection. Most of the string partials also lie in the oboe's own register. Vln II's G4 has its 2nd and 3rd partials at G5 and D6, around and above the oboe's E♭5–C5, and the viola E♭4's 2nd partial is E♭5, the oboe's first note. By PDF 287 ("the fewer the instruments in a frequency range, the clearer and more present they become") they thin the oboe's space.
- **Evidence from the reading:** Casanelles PDF 286 names sul tasto (with the mute and the solo player) as the orchestra's own tool for "a clearer or more diffused sound", the acoustic counterpart of cutting the upper frequencies. PDF 287 (*Shawshank*, "Brooks Was Here") shows the effect wanted here: the solo line stays close and keeps its detail, while the loss of high frequencies brings "nostalgia and distance". PDF 272–273 and 283–284 (Mahler's distant trumpets; the *Social Network* theme moved further away at each return) say that lost highs, more than lower loudness, are what makes a sound seem distant or remembered. Mutes cannot be used here: the strings play ff until 94.3 and f again at 99. Sul tasto needs no time and costs no notes. The score already uses it this way for colour at 43, 135 and 170–186 (c264, c399, c063).
- **Fix (applied):** a text mark on the first note of each part. Pitches, dynamics and MIDI are unchanged.
  - clos.ily: `closVlnTwo … g'1\mp\>~^\markup \italic "sul tasto" | g'1 | g'1\p~ | g'2. r4 |`; `closVla … <bes es'>1\mp\>~^\markup \italic "sul tasto"`; `closVc … es1\mp\>~^\markup \italic "sul tasto"`; `closCb … es,1\mp\>~^\markup \italic "sul tasto"` (Bn 2's identical line left untouched).
  - dev.ily: `devVla = { f'2->(\f^\markup \italic "ord." …`, `devVc = { f2->(\f^\markup \italic "ord." …`, `devCb = { f,2->(\f^\markup \italic "ord." …`, `devVlnTwo = { R1*4 | r2 r4 f''8(\mf^\markup \italic "ord." …`.
- **Result:** in the scratch build the oboe is the only bright sound in its octave for the four bars. The E♭ chord stays a harmonic-series voicing (principle 2), now with its upper partials thinned, and the development's f entry at 99 gets its full bow position back. Vln I already rests from 95 to 102 (an earlier cycle took it off the oboe's unison), so this finishes that earlier decision with the reading's own technique.

### 2. Checked: the recurrences already move further away (no change)

### 3. Tested by reasoning and declined: stopped Hn 3·4 at 95–98
A stopped horn is the orchestra's built-in "distant" brass colour (Adler PDF 308; it is used at 49–50 and 168–169). Under the oboe, though, stopped G4/B♭3 at p is reedy and nasal, close to the oboe's own spectrum and in the register just below it. That runs against PDF 286–287 (separate the frequency areas), so I did not apply it. The open horns p > pp stay.

### 4. Checked: ensemble unisons and foreground (no change)
The flute doublings of the violins at 77–80, 83–86 and 239–242 behave as PDF 277–278 describes: the flute adds upper partials and the pair is heard as one line. At 205–208, horns and trombones drop to mf/mp when the woodwinds take F1, and Tbn 1·2 play f under the ff brass head at 247–254. Both keep the harmonically denser brass from covering the line (PDF 289). The ff codetta's brass at 91–94 is a tutti, where density is the goal.
