## Findings

### 1. Bars 169–170: the violins' descent into their low register is left without the cellos (applied)

Problem. At the end of the recapitulation's first group the violins fall alone, p, sul G, into their weak register (Vln I D5 down to D4, Vln II a sixth lower down to G3) while the cellos sit on a held G2–B2 that duplicates the basses an octave higher. Adler PDF 141 (Ex. V-19, Shostakovich 6/iii) describes exactly this moment: when the first violins reach into their lower register the cellos double them in octaves, which "contributes a feeling of greater resolution" and prepares the following wind passage. The exposition's counterpart, 41–42, already does this (c344: cellos double the violins' F–D–E♭–F before the woodwind passage at 43). The recapitulation, which leads into the cor anglais solo at 171, does not, so the two ends of the first group are scored differently for no structural reason (Adler PDF 166: orchestration must clarify the form).

Evidence. v434 recPVc `aes,1\f\upbow | aes,1\> | g,1( | b,2.)\p r4`; recPCb `... g,,1( | b,,2.)`; Bn 2 `g,1 | b,1`. At 170 the V6/5's bass B (the leading tone, the chord's third) is in Cb B1, Vc B2 and Bn 2 B2: three octaves of the third, against Adler PDF 152 (double the third of a first-inversion chord only near the bottom and keep the open sound).

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-434/work/sections/recP.ily):
```
recPVc = { ... aes,1\f\upbow | aes,1\> | g,4 d'8( c' b aes g f | d2.)\p r4 | }
```
The cellos keep G2 on the downbeat of 169, then double Vln I an octave lower (D4–C4–B3–A♭3–G3–F3, a third under Vln II), ending on D3 at 170. G and B stay in the basses (G1–B1) and Bn 2 (G2–B2), so the bass of V7♭9 and of V6/5 is unchanged and the leading tone still resolves in Bn 2 and the basses into 171's C. The cellos now play the fifth D3 in the V6/5, so the third B is doubled only at the bottom. Dynamics (the diminuendo from 168 to p at 170) unchanged; the cellos have beat 4 of 170 and the barline to change to pizz. for 171 as before. This replaces c218's one-bow slur over 169–170 for the cellos only (the basses keep it).

Build: see the last section.

### 2. Bars 187–198: the major statement of F2 is not at the violins' best register (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 133–135 says the second statement of a theme should be fuller and higher than the first. In the recapitulation the major statement lies a third lower than the exposition's (violins in unison at E♭5, violas E♭4) because it is transposed to A♭; the octave lift is withheld for 247–258 and the arrival. That follows Adler PDF 129 (save the extremes for the end). No change.

### 3. Bars 231–234: Tchaikovsky's bass drop-out on the tonic (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 154 shows a diminuendo made by the basses dropping out each time a repeated cadential formula reaches the tonic. The only repeated cadence in a diminuendo here is the plagal close 231–234 over a held C pedal; the basses carry the pedal, so there is no tonic arrival to drop out on, and the passage is protected (c114, c072). No change.

### 4. Voice crossing for colour (checked, no change)

Adler PDF 125 and 128 name voice crossing (a viola tune above the violins, Haydn Var. III) as a special effect. The obvious place, the violas' F2 hand-over at 124–126, already has the violins resting, so the violas are the top string voice without crossing. Putting the violas above Vln II at 67–82 would move the cello–viola counter-melody into the tune's octave (Adler PDF 135, 144 no. 3). No change.
