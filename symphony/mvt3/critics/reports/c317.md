## Findings

### 1. 255–258: the last chorale statement has a foreground and a background but no middleground. F2 is never combined with anything. (Applied.)

**Problem.** At 255–262 the chorale's last statement G–E–D–C–B–C in doubled values is a whole-note cantus firmus. Ten instruments double it across four octaves: Tpt 1·2, Fl 1·2 (from 257), Vln I, Ob 1, Cl 2, Hn 1·2, Tbn 1 and Bn 1. Below it there is only accompaniment: the Vln II and Cl 1 arpeggios, the violas' repeated quarters, and the repeated halves in Hn 3·4 and Tpt 3. The cellos double the tuba, bass trombone, contrabassoon and basses in whole notes. Adler presents exactly this situation, a long-note cantus firmus, as the place where the masters bring in a second thematic element (Jupiter, PDF 175–176). His three-layer model (PDF 179, 181) puts a subsidiary theme between the main theme and the figuration: Scheherazade has a "dance figure", and Holst passes his middleground from two trombones to six horns in unison. The score uses that model at 247–254 and does not use it at 255.

The finale's own second theme F2 (the trumpet solo at 51, the violins at 67, the recapitulation at 187) disappears after 198. The coda combines movement I's two heads (247) and brings back F1 (265), but F2 is never heard in C major. F2's head is 5–3–2–1–3–5, and the chorale is 5–3–2–1, so F2's head under the chorale is the same motive in quicker values. Adler requires each element to have been heard on its own first (PDF 177, 181) and to have its own rhythm (PDF 175, 179): dotted half and quarters against whole notes, eighth-note arpeggios and repeated notes. F2 meets both conditions.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-317/work/sections/finale.ily).** At 255–258, the cellos and Hn 3·4 a2 play F2's third phrase (sOne 59–62, sTwo 75–78) in C major in the tenor register, f, a level below the ff chorale (Schoenberg, PDF 184). Bn 1 joins at 257, when the woodwinds re-enter, so the line has a string, a brass and a woodwind instrument (PDF 178).

### 2. 247–254: the P head's doublings are three octaves apart. (Checked; kept.)

The P head sits at G5 (Fl 1·2, Vln I·II) and three octaves lower at G2 (Tbn 1·2, Bn 1, Vc), with nothing in between. Schoenberg's doublings are "only at pitch or one octave apart" (PDF 184). Moving Vln II down an octave would put the P head across the S head in Ob 1, Cl 1 and Tpt 1 (E5–C6) and blur the two elements, which Bloch's choir separation (PDF 184) and Wagner's separate colours (PDF 177) are meant to prevent. The gap is the price of keeping the two heads in separate registers. I kept it. The passage is protected (c117, c217).

### 3. 223–230: the canon in strings and woodwinds against the chorale in brass. (Checked; kept.)

This follows Bloch's choir separation (PDF 184) and the rule that elements must be heard separately before they are combined (PDF 177). The canon's second entry, two bars after the chorale (226), has a different colour from the chorale's first statement, as Mozart varies each cantus statement (PDF 176). It is protected, and I changed nothing.

### 4. 265–272: F1 in C major against the motto's head rhythm. (Checked; kept.)

Each element has its own rhythm and articulation (PDF 175, 178). The bass is the basses' own repeated eighths (PDF 176). The timpani and Tpt 3 take movement I's pulse as background rhythm (PDF 181 footnote). The colours stay fixed through 265–272 and change at 273, as Mozart's do at bar 400 (PDF 176). No change.
