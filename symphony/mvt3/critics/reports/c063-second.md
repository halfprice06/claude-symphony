## Verdicts on the three focus questions

**Development (99-134).** The order (calls 99-106, lament 107-109, German sixth at 109, climax 110-113, bassoon 115-122, hand-over 123-130, G cadence 131-134, retransition) is right, and the dynamic hierarchy is right: climax ff at 110 (density 18.9) below the retransition's ff at 147 (25.5) and the arrival. The proportion to the exposition matches Dvořák 9/iv. The fault is in the middle: from 114 to 130 the development's own material (the scale figure and F1's head) is absent for 17 bars, and 115-130 replays the exposition's second-group plan (F2 in minor as a solo over off-beat pizzicato, then in major with harp) that the recapitulation plays again at 171/187. Finding 1 keeps the order and lengths and brings the development's call into that span.

**Recapitulation (151-210).** The first group differs enough (half the length, over the G pedal, D♭ at 163, tutti). The second group does not: 171-186 is 51-66 a third lower in every accompanying part, and 187-198 is 67-78 with the same harp arpeggios. c063.md removed Vln II at 171-186; Findings 2 and 3 change what remains.

**Coda.** The slow stages (211-246, 67 s) outweigh the fast one (247-272, 32 s) about 2:1, the reverse of Dvořák 9/iv and Brahms 1/iv, close to Tchaikovsky 5/iv's coda. The stages still build: each ends louder or denser than it began except the protected dip at 231-238, and 247-279 is 43 s of continuous ff-fff tutti. Each slow stage carries an owed item, and adding fast bars would renumber the protected close and the quotation at 275-276. I agree with c043 and c063.md: no change.

## Findings

### 1. 115-122: the bassoon solo is not answered, so the development's centre is a third exposition of F2 (applied)

**Problem.** After the climax the development's own ideas stop. For 17 bars (114-130) neither the scale figure (the woodwinds' call at 101 and 105) nor F1's head is heard; the bassoon's F2 in G minor over off-beat pizzicato (115-122) followed by F2 in G major with harp (123-130) is the exposition's 51/67 plan in a new key. The bassoon holds notes at 117 (G4 for three beats) and 122 (A3 for a bar), and nothing answers there.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257-258: dialogue, a phrase answered at once in another colour, "clarifies the melodic material, and aids in a clearer exposition of the form"; Beethoven turns the answering strings to a different articulation (pizzicato) so the answer does not duplicate the solo. PDF 274 (Ex. XVII-15): Beethoven sets the middle-register bassoon against a contrasting activity, and the accompaniment is kept registrally distant from a woodwind solo. Dvořák 9/iv's development mixes its lyrical recalls with the main material instead of restating them whole.

**Fix (applied, dev.ily `devVlnOne`).** Vln I, arco p dolce (against Vln II's pizzicato), answers the two held notes with the scale figure, two octaves above the solo:
- 114-127 `fis''1\p\> | R1*8\! | R1*5 |` becomes
  `fis''1\p\> | R1*2\! | r4 f'''8(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" es''' d''' c''' bes'' aes'' | g''4) r4 r2 | R1*3 | r4 d'''8( c''' b'' a'' g'' fis'' | g''4) r4 r2 | R1*4 |`

At 117 the line F6-A♭5-G5 moves in sixths and fourths over the solo's G4-F4-E♭4 (a first version starting on G6 made fifths D6-C6 over G4-F4; I changed it). At 122 the line D6-F♯5 uses B♮ over D7 and lands on G5 at 123, so the turn to G major is heard first in the violins' answer and then in the cellos' F2. Vln I then rests until its protected hand-over entry at 128. The bassoon line, the pizzicato and the hand-over are unchanged.

### 2. 171-186: the recapitulated C-minor statement keeps the exposition's background, a third lower (applied)

**Problem.** Every accompanying part at 171-186 is 51-66 transposed: the same off-beat pizzicato dyads in Vln II and violas, the same pizzicato bass, the same clarinet and bassoon pad. Only the solo colour changes. It is the third time this background is heard (51, 115, 171), and the movement's eighth-note motion is absent for the 16 bars.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 288 (Haydn, Ex. XVII-24): on the repeat the flute leaves the soprano and doubles the tenor; "Devices like these add great variety to accompaniments." PDF 268-270 (Sibelius VC, Ex. XVII-11; Bartók PC 3, Ex. XVII-12): a soft, rocking or "weaving" string background in steady short notes, rhythmically independent of the solo, lets the melody "soar freely". PDF 259-260: the accompaniment neither duplicates nor imitates the solo's rhythm.

**Fix (applied, recS.ily `recSVla`).** The violas play their existing dyads as arco sul tasto pp eighths, one slur per half bar, on the same pitches (E♭3-A♭3, C4 at most), an octave and more below the cor anglais and muted trumpet:
- `r4 <es g>4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz. div." r4 <es g>4 | r4 <d f>4 r4 <es g>4 | ...` becomes
  `es8(\pp^\markup \italic "arco, sul tasto" g es g) es8( g es g) | d8( f d f) es8( g es g) | ...` through 186 (`aes8( c' aes c') r2`), generated half bar by half bar from the old dyads, so the harmony is unchanged.
- 187: the violas' marking "arco, unis., espress." becomes "ord., espress." (they are already arco and on one line).

With c063.md's change in v64 (Vln II rests 171-186) the two fit: the only pizzicato left is the bass, and the background becomes cellos and basses pizz., violas arco weave, wind pad. The protected solo (cor anglais + muted trumpet) is not touched.

### 3. 187-198: the harp's one texture is used for the fourth time and copies 67-78 (applied)

**Problem.** The harp has a single texture in the movement: broken-chord eighths under a major-mode cantabile, at 67-82, 123-130, 187-198 and 235-242. At 187 it repeats 67-78 note for note in its pattern (a third lower), so the recapitulation's C-major statement is again the exposition's scoring, and by 235, under the owed C-major second-theme head of movement I, the colour has been used three times, the last only 36 bars before.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 263-264, 267: composers introduce a soloist with a distinct colour "so the solo color is fresh", and Mozart keeps the orchestral horns and flutes out while the solo horn or flute plays so its colour meets no competition. PDF 261-262: in successful works foreground and background roles are exchanged, not repeated. PDF 288 (variety on the repeat).

**Fix (applied, recS.ily).** `recSHarpR` and `recSHarpL` become `{ R1*16 | R1*12 | }` (the 187 pedal diagram, 8 bassa and the E♭/A♭ pedal notes at 197 go with them; the harp's next entry at 235 has its own diagram). The C-major statement keeps violins, Hn 1, violas and cellos' counter-melody, bassoons and basses. The eighth-note motion that the harp gave the major half in the exposition now belongs to the minor half (violas, Finding 2): the roles are exchanged. The harp is then silent from 131 to 234, so at 235 it returns after 104 bars.

### 4. 113→114 (decided, no change)

The half-bar silence after the ff cadence is not Adler's forte-subito-piano (PDF 190 needs an overlap only when the soft chord is meant to be already sounding). Here key and colour change together (F minor tutti → D7 strings, c058), so the silence is a deliberate break. I keep it, as c063.md did.
