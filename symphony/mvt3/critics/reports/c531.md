## Findings

### 1. 18.4 and 158.4: an upbeat group into F1's consequent (Ex. 3-9b, 3-11). Tested in the scratch build; not better, reverted.

**Candidate.** F1's groups all begin on the downbeat. Ex. 3-9b shows groups that begin on an unaccented beat, and Ex. 3-11 groups that run into one another. Brahms 1/iv joins its tune's phrases with the tune's own upbeat. I tested a pickup into the consequent: pOne.ily `pOneVlnOne` 18 `g''2.-> g''8-.\upbow b''8-. |`, `pOneVlnTwo` `g'8-. b'8-.`; recP.ily `recPVlnOne` 158 `<g' g''>8-. <b' b''>8-.` (Vln II plays a counter-line at 159 and was left alone).

**Why it is not better.** Bar 18.4 is the only moment in F1 where the whole orchestra is silent. Every part stops there: the timpani's G–G–G ends on 18.3, the cellos' and basses' G on 18.3, Hn 1·2 and violas after the dotted half. The consequent then enters f in flutes and oboes for the first time. A&S PDF 50 says the accent comes from the beginning (attack) of a note, and an attack after silence is the strongest one. The rest is what gives 19 its weight. With the pickup, two bare violin eighths in octaves fill the silence. They soften the wind entry and turn the head into an upbeat group. Every other statement of F1's head (11, 27, 83, 99, 151, 265, 269) begins on the downbeat, so the theme's best-known feature would change in its own exposition. The build also showed problems. analysis.txt reported new outer-voice parallel octaves at 18 and 158, because the octave pickup was the entire texture. A page-2 compression warning appeared. par8.py reported only the existing bass doublings at 17–20 and 157–160. Reverted. F1 at 11–18 stays as it is (c212's do-not-touch holds).

### 2. 62 and 78: F2's climaxes fall on the fourth bar of a four-bar group (PDF 53). Checked, no change.

Hypermetrically, both peaks sit on weak bars: B♭5 at 62 in the solo and B♭6 at 78 in the violins. A&S PDF 50 lists the other accents that can carry a peak: height, length and dissonance. The violins' peak at 78 has all three: B♭6, a dotted quarter, the appoggiatura of c011, plus f and a down-bow. The solo's peak at 62 is high but only a quarter long. c441 joined its fall to the goal at 63 and declined the dotted figure for a stated reason: the plain minor solo is meant to grow into the ornate major statement. I agree with that reason. Moving either peak to a strong bar would mean rewriting F2's second half, and 78 is protected. No change.

### 3. F1's rhythm against the metre (PDF 51, Ex. 3-1). Follows; no change.

F1 puts its long notes on the half-note beats in every bar. The figure 4 8 8 4 4 appears in six of its sixteen bars, and there is a single syncopation, the 4–3 at 25. A&S PDF 51 calls this "the simplest and most natural" kind of rhythm. For a tune meant to be hummed it is the right choice. Beethoven 9/iv's theme works the same way, as do the downbeat themes of Tchaikovsky 5/iv and Dvořák 9/iv. Variety comes from register, not rhythm: the consequent sits an octave up and peaks on A♭6 at 22. Bar 17 combines all three of PDF 50's accent sources on the weak half-bar: a long note, a high note and a ♭6 over the German sixth. That gives the antecedent a lift before the accented G at 18. No change.

### 4. Chorale tempo by main tones (PDF 49). Follows; no change.

A&S PDF 49 says the felt tempo comes from the beats, together with the pace of chord changes and of the melody's main tones. The arrival chorale (223–227, 2 = 56) moves one main tone per bar, 56 a minute. The last statement (255–264) runs at 2 = 88 in doubled values, one tone per two bars, 44 a minute. Its line therefore moves more broadly than the first statement while the beat underneath runs faster. That matches Brahms 1/iv's coda, where the chorale returns ff in the Più allegro in long values. Both top lines are protected, and the reading supports them. No change.

### 5. 17.4→18 anticipation (Ex. 3-19, 3-20) and 70 suspension by lengthening (Ex. 3-17a). Considered on paper; declined.

- 17–18: anticipating the half-cadence G (`aes''4. g''8~ | g''2.`) would resolve the ♭6 before the bass moves F♯→G. It also removes the accented G on 18.1, the antecedent's goal (PDF 54). c441's reason against the suspension version applies here too. Declined.
- 70 (F2 in E♭, end of the first group): `c'''2.( bes''4)` would make C6 a 4–3 over the I6 on 70.3. But 70.3 is the applied diminished chord G–B♭–E♮ into ii at 71 (cellos E♮3). Holding C would remove the violins' B♭ from it and leave a C-major 6/4 sound on the beat. Declined; 70 keeps `c'''2( bes''2)`.
