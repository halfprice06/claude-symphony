## Findings

### 1. 249: Tpt 3's hammered C5 makes a second inside the trumpet trio (applied)

Problem. At 247–250 Tpt 1·2 play movement I's second-theme head in thirds and sixths while Tpt 3 hammers chord tones with the timpani (C5, F4, C5, G4). At 249 the head runs F5–E5–D5 over D5–C5–A4, and Tpt 3's C5 sounds a major second under Tpt 2's D5 on beat 1 and under Tpt 1's D5 on beat 3. On beat 3 it also crosses above Tpt 2's A4. The trumpet trio sounds F5/D5/C5 and then D5/C5/A4: two seconds in one colour, at f, in the bar the head reaches its turn. My scan of seconds within the trumpet section finds this bar and otherwise only 110 and 112 (kept by c194 for the cyclic reason), 200 and 204 (movement I's hammered layout, quoted part by part) and a passing eighth at 247.4.5.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 102–103: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour". PDF 100: brass part-writing in close order. PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained or rhythmic chords, and these are rhythmic chords. c182 already moved Tpt 2 at 249.3 from B4 to A4 "against Tpt 3's C5". That removed one clash and left these two.

Fix (applied, finale.ily, `finaleTptThree`, third bar):
```
c''4 r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8   ->   a'4 r8 a'8 a'4 r8 a'8
```
The trio becomes F5/D5/A4 and D5/A4/A4, with no crossing. The C stays in the timpani's strokes on the same rhythm, so the discord is now in a different colour, as Rimsky asks. A4 doubles Hn 3's held A4. Tpt 3's line is C5–F4–A4–G4. A4→G4 at 250 is in contrary motion to the bass F→G (F4 would have made octaves with the bass). Tune, rhythm, dynamics and the timpani are unchanged.

### 2. 145–146: twelve empty semitones in the brass V7 (applied)

Problem. At 145–146 every part takes F1's head on G and the brass hold V7 at f. The bass trombone has G2, and the next brass note is D4: Hn 4 and Tbn 2 on D4, Hn 3 and Tbn 1 on F4. That leaves a twelfth with nothing in it, and two pairs of instruments in unison above. Tbn 2 had held G3 at 143–144 (c362) and leapt to D4 at 145 only to double Hn 4 and the violas.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 100: "part writing should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". PDF 101: the low trombone takes the bass in octaves and the others take the upper parts. c194 found the same gap at 147–150 and kept it there because that passage is protected. 145–146 is not covered by that protection. Inner-voice changes in 139–146 have been accepted before (c172, c347, c362).

Fix (applied, retr.ily, `retrTbnTwo`, bars 145–146):
```
g1\pp\< | g1 | d'1\f | d'1 \breathe | d'1->\ff   ->   g1\pp\< | g1 | g1\f | g1 \breathe | d'1->\ff
```
The brass chord is now G2–G3–D4–F4–G4. Tbn 2 holds its common tone and rises to D4 at 147 with the ff, so 147–150 are unchanged. No B is added above the B bass (c362's rule is kept). On page 8 the shared trombone staff shows F4 over G3.

### 4. Rimsky's preferred layout, two horns and tuba as the bass under three trombones, is not used (decided: no change)

PDF 101 calls this "eminently satisfactory" and its resonance "beautiful". The only places where the trombones hold a soft three-part chord are 143–144, 207–210 and 219–222. At 143–144 the bass is B in the strings and bassoons, so the brass bass would double the bass of an inversion. 219–222 is protected. At 207–210 the four horns carry the upper half of the A♭ chord (B♭4, G♭4, E♭4, A♭3; c346 balanced them mf against the trombones mp). Putting two of them on A♭2/A♭3 would leave that half to the woodwinds, which are fp and rising. The layout would cost more than it gains in any of these places.

### 5. 211–212: Tbn 2 doubles the bass trombone's D♭3 at the unison (decided: no change)

PDF 101 gives the second trombone an upper part. Here tuba D♭2, bass trombone D♭3 and Tbn 2 D♭3 all play the root, and only Tbn 1 (F3) sits above it in the trombones. I tried each alternative on the voice-leading checks. An A♭ voice (Tbn 2 A♭2 or A♭3, or the bass trombone holding A♭2 from 210) has nowhere to go at 213. To G♭ it makes fifths with Hn 3's E♭4→D♭4. To B♭ it makes fifths with Tpt 2's E♭5→F5 in theme B. A♭2→D♭3 makes fifths against the tuba. PDF 112 says that where the melodic position keeps changing, "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". The unison is the correct-progression choice, so I kept it.
