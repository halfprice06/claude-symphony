## Findings

### 1. 198: the crescendo into the coda stops one bar early (applied)
**Bars:** 196–199 (recS.ily, codaOne.ily).
**Problem.** The recapitulated second theme's last group, 195–198, crescendos from 196 and reaches f on the downbeat of 198. Then every part holds a whole note at a flat f: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I–II on E♭, Hn 1–2, the bassoons, violas, cellos and basses. After that comes the ff tutti at 199. c068 and c113 describe this seam as "full crescendo 196–198" and "crescendos into 199", but the notation ends the crescendo at 198.1. The exposition's matching seam (82→83) crescendos through its last bar into the downbeat.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 53–54: 195–198 is one four-bar hypermeasure, and its goal is the next strong bar, 199, where the hammered C-minor tutti starts the coda. 198 is the weak fourth bar. A&S PDF 50: the start of a note is stronger than its continuation. A whole note held at the same marked level therefore sounds like a slight fall just before the ff, under the poco rit. The page image (p. 10 of v148) shows every hairpin of 196–197 closing at 198.1.
**Fix (applied).** `\<` on the held note of 198 in every playing part: Fl 1 and Ob 1 `es'''1)\f\<` / `es''1)\f\<`, Bn 1 `es2.\f\< r4`, Bn 2 `aes,1\f\<`, Hn 1 `es'1\f\<`, Hn 2 `aes1\f\<`, Vln I `es'''1\f\<\downbow`, Vln II `es''1\f\<\downbow`, Vla and Vc `c'1\f\<`, Cb `aes,,1\f\<`. The hairpins end at 199's ff. In codaOne.ily the strings' downbeat rest gets `\!` (`r8\! g''8(\ff`), so no hairpin stub prints between the rest and the ff (checked on p. 11). The down-bow at 198 and c018's bow plan are unchanged. The hairpins in the shared staves are equal (Bn 1·2, Hn 1·2).

### 2. 77–81: the horn and bassoon pad stays p under the dotted peak (applied)
**Bars:** 75–82 (sTwo.ily).
**Problem.** At 77 the violins crescendo from mf to f, Fl 1 and Ob 1 enter p < f, and the violas, cellos and basses have already risen to mf. Then 78 brings the dotted B♭6 peak (c141). The four-part pad in Hn 1–2 and Bn 1–2 (c147's crossed harmony) stays p through all of this and moves only at 82. The harmony that supports the peak is the one layer that does not take part in the four-bar arc. In the recapitulation's version of this group (195–198), Hn 1 and the bassoons crescendo with the tune to f at 198. The two statements of the same phrase are shaped differently, and the reason is not the change of key or scoring.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 53: 75–78 is a four-bar hypermeasure that drives to the peak at 78. PDF 50 and 54–55: the peak is a high, long, accented dissonance (B♭6 over the C bass, a seventh falling by step), and that peak carries the group's stress. The whole texture should rise towards it. The pad should not rise above the tune, so mp is enough, and the brass still join last (Rimsky PDF 130, cited by c114). The diminuendo goes back to p with the violins' diminuendo, so the pad is soft again at the cadence (81–82) and can make its existing crescendo into 83.
**Fix (applied).** Hn 1 `es'2\< c'2 | c'2\mp d'2 | es'1 | c'2 d'2\> | d'1\p | bes1\< \breathe`; Hn 2 `g2\< f2 | g2\mp bes2 | c'1 | aes2 f2\> | f1\p | es1\<`; Bn 1 `bes2\< aes2 | es2\mp f4 r4 | es1 | es2 bes2\> | aes1\p | g1\<`; Bn 2 `g,2\< aes,2 | c1\mp | aes,1 | aes,2 bes,2\> | bes,1\p | es1\<`. Pitches, the c147 voicing and the c145 breath are unchanged, and the dynamics are equal in both shared staves. The page image (p. 4 of my build) shows the combined staves unchanged.
