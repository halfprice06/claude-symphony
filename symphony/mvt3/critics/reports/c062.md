## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 109: the lament bass reaches the development's climactic dominant without an augmented sixth (applied)
Problem. The lament bass F–E–E♭–D–D♭–C (107–109) arrives at the movement's loudest development chord, V7 of F at 110 ff. Over D♭ (109, beats 1–2) the chord is a plain iv6 (D♭–F–B♭). Then Vln I's B♭5 is held over C as a 7th and leaps to G5 without resolving.
Evidence. A&S PDF 503 (Ex. 29-1) describes a development's climactic dominant "ushered in" by an augmented sixth. PDF 510–511 (Ex. 29-18) is the chromatic lament bass from I to V, whose ♭6̂ carries the aug-6th. PDF 511–512 (Ex. 29-20) has a soprano rising chromatically against the falling bass. PDF 505 and 29-8: Ger6 → cadential 6/4 → V avoids the fifths. The bass and the 7–6 suspensions of 107–108 are unchanged. This answers the "lament 107–110 do-not-touch" of c053: only the upper voices of 109 change, and the lament becomes more idiomatic for A&S's model.
Fix (dev.ily):
```
devVlnOne: c'''2 c'''4( bes''4)~ | bes''2 bes''4( g''4)  ->  c'''2 c'''4( bes''4) | b''2 c'''4( g''4)
devVla:    c'2 bes2 | bes2 bes2 |                       ->  c'2 bes2 | aes2 aes4 g4 |
```
Result: 109 is D♭–F–A♭–B♮ (Ger6), then C–F–A♭–C (cadential 6/4, Vln II F→E 4–3), then V, then V7 at 110. Vln I's line is B♭–B♮–C.

### 2. 163: F1's consequent in the recapitulation gets the Neapolitan colour (applied; this changes F1 in the recap only)
Problem. At 23 and 163 the consequent's top line E♭–D–C–B sits on a diatonic ii°6 (23) or iiø6/5 (163) over F, just before V and the deceptive cadence. The recap restatement (ff, fuller) is harmonically identical to the exposition.
Evidence. A&S PDF 482–483: Mozart replaces the ii°6 with ♭II6 because the diatonic chord is too thin for an emphasised moment, and ♭II6 belongs at climaxes. PDF 495 §16: iv plus ♭2 as a 6th is the "apparent Neapolitan 6/5". PDF 484: the diminished 3rd ♭2–7 filled by 1 is an intense line. PDF 485 (Ex. 28-6): ♮2 should come back as the tone of V.
