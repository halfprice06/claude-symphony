## Findings

### 1. Bar 117: Vln I's answer covers the solo's appoggiatura. Move it to 119 (applied)

**Problem.** c063 wrote Vln I's first answer to the bassoon (117: `r4 f'''8( es''' d''' c''' bes'' a''! | g''4)`) at a time when bar 117 of the solo was a held G4. Since c131 the solo has F2's own third bar again, A4 (half note)–G4–F4. That A4 is F2's appoggiatura at its first peak, which c001 and c131 both protected. The answer now starts one beat after the A4, runs through the appoggiatura's resolution to G4 on beat 3, and ends over the F4. The violins play p dolce two octaves above an mp bassoon.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 50 (sec. 3): unusually high notes, and dissonant notes, attract the accent. The appoggiatura is the accented dissonance of PDF 54–55 (sec. 9, Ex. 3-15c), and its accent needs a clear field. Here a line two octaves higher begins its own group on beat 2 and is still moving when the resolution falls on beat 3. That puts two rhythmic groups (PDF 52, sec. 7) across each other at the solo's peak. c063's own rule was "answer the bassoon's held notes", and 122 (over the held A3) still follows it. Since c131, bar 117 no longer does. The solo's held notes are now at 115, 119 and 122 (F2's bars 1, 5 and 8). Adler (PDF 274, cited by c063) asks for the answer to stay apart from the soloist's activity, which it no longer does at 117.

**Fix (dev.ily, devVlnOne).** Vln I rests at 117–118. The answer moves to 119, beats 2–4, over the solo's held C4 (the first note of the consequent, parallel to the held A3 that 122 answers). It runs down the iiø7 (A–C–E♭–G) and lands on F♯5 at 120 (V). Like the 122 answer, it ends on F♯:

```
| R1*2\! | R1*2 | r4 g'''8(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" f''' es''' d''' c''' a'' | fis''4) r4 r2 | R1 | r4 d'''8(\p c''' ...
```

(It was `| R1*2\! | r4 f'''8(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" es''' d''' c''' bes'' a''! | g''4) r4 r2 | R1*3 | r4 d'''8(...`.) I first tried ending B♭5–A5. That made fifths with the solo's E♭4–D4 (the analyser flagged bar 120), so the run ends C6–A5–F♯5 instead. The passing tones F6, D6 and A5 fall on weak eighths (principle 11). The solo's notes, the pizzicato and the 122 answer are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 211: Vln I's C6 against theme B's first goal note. Hold D♭6 (applied)

**Problem.** At 211 theme B's recall opens with its rising fourth A♭–D♭ (flutes A♭5–D♭6; Tpt 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 an octave lower). Vln I is the highest line: `ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4) des'''4 c'''4`. On beat 3 it meets the flutes on D♭6. On beat 4 it moves to C6, a minor second against the flutes' held D♭6 in the same octave. Then it leaps to F6 at 212. The theme's first group ends on its long note D♭. The top line strikes a new note under it and makes the top of the texture C–F rather than D♭–F.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 52 (sec. 7): a rhythmic group ends on its long note, and here the group is the theme's incipit, A♭–D♭. PDF 50 (sec. 3): the highest line takes the accent, so Vln I's beat-4 attack is what is heard at the top. By PDF 54 (sec. 9) the C6 is a legal unaccented escape tone, but it falls against the theme's own pitch in the same octave, and in a recall the listener has to recognise from its first two notes. c151's reason for this bar was the G♭6 suspension resolving to F6 on beats 1–2. My fix keeps that suspension.

**Fix (codaTwo.ily, codaTwoVlnOne, first bar).** `ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4) des'''2 |` (was `... des'''4 c'''4 |`). Vln I joins the theme's D♭6 on beat 3 and holds it, so 212's F6 comes from the theme's own leap D♭–F. The all-pairs check (tools/critic-scripts/c002/par.py) only moves the existing octave doubling of the theme (Vln I over Cl, Ob, Tpt) from 212.4 to 212.1. There are no new parallels against independent lines.

Neither finding changes F1, F2, the chorale top lines or any quotation. Theme B's notes in Tpt 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 and the flutes are unchanged, so the other movement threads need no message.
