## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 82: the harp's E♭ arpeggio clashes with the new run (applied)
Problem: cycle 113 replaced the violins' held E♭ at 82 with the run G-A♭-B♭-D (Vln I and Fl 1 at G5-D6, Vln II and Ob 1 at G4-D5). The harp's right hand kept its tonic arpeggio on beats 3-4, `es'8 g'8 bes'8 es''8`. Taken eighth by eighth against Vln II and Ob 1 this gives E♭4/G4, then G4/A♭4 (a minor second), then B♭4/B♭4, then E♭5/D5 (a minor second, with the leading tone against the tonic in the same octave and D6 an octave above). The harp sits at mf in the run's own register.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 129-130 says the anacrusis scale leads to the stressed note. That only works if the run is the only moving part in its register. PDF 51-52 says plucked strings against bowed strings "do not produce a satisfactory blend, both qualities being heard independently", so the harp's E♭5 will be heard as a separate wrong note and not covered. The harp passage was written for the held E♭, which no longer exists.
Fix (sTwo.ily, sTwoHarpR, last bar): `es'8 g'8 bes'8 es''8 r2 \stemNeutral | }`. The left hand's E♭1 stays (the bass is E♭ through 82). The harp has nothing at 83, so this is its last note in the exposition anyway.

### 2. Bars 148-150: Hn 1 holds Tpt 1's stroke pitch (applied)
Problem: Tpt 1 strikes D5 at 148 and 149 (beat 1) and Tpt 2 strikes D5 at 148-150. Hn 1 holds D5 ff through 147-150 as one tied block. Two of Tpt 1's four strokes are therefore restrikes of a pitch that a horn is sustaining at ff in the same octave. Only the F5 and G5 strokes are new pitches in the brass. Hn 1 is also the top note of the held brass chord, so the strokes stand above a brass unison, not above a chord of another colour.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 115-116 says that for a trumpet figure over held harmony, "the harmonic basis should differ from the melody not only in fullness and intensity of tone, but also in colour". The appendix chords (PDF 505) keep the trumpets on top and the horns in the middle. PDF 51 rates a horn at half a trumpet, so moving the horn costs the chord little weight. A held D5 for Hn 1 (written A5) at ff over four bars also tires the player.
Fix (retr.ily, retrHnOne 148-150): `d''1->\ff | g'1 | g'1~ | g'2. r4 |` (Hn 1 G4 a2 with Hn 2 from 148; 147 unchanged). D stays in the brass at every moment through Tpt 1 or Tpt 2 and Tbn 2, and B is in Tpt 2 at each downbeat. All motion is oblique, so there are no new parallels. c111's strokes, harmony and bass G are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 187-197: in the A♭-major statement the lower layers are as loud as the tune (applied)
Problem: at 187 the tune is in Vln I and Vln II in unison at mf, in its lowest register of the four statements (E♭5-B♭5; at 67 it was B♭4/B♭5 in octaves). Under it the violas play the lower octave at mf with Hn 1 p, the cellos play the counter-melody alone at mf, and the basses are at mf. At 67 cycles 94 and 98 set the counter-melody (violas and cellos) and the basses to mp for exactly this reason: "the counter-melody in unison outweighs the tune". At 187 the counter is still level with the tune, and the lower octave adds a second string section plus a horn. Using Rimsky's soft-passage weights, the tune has two sections (in unison, so it gains fullness rather than volume), the lower octave has one section plus one horn, and the counter-melody has one section. The tune is no longer on top in weight, and the statement thickens downwards when it should open the way into the ff tutti at 199.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 51 says that in piano passages one string department equals one wind instrument. PDF 52 says strings and brass side by side are each heard distinctly, so Hn 1 makes the lower octave more prominent, not less. PDF 118 (operation e) and Ex. 207/208 show that a repeated statement can change its dynamic layering, but the melody keeps the lead.
Fix (recS.ily): 187 violas `\mp^\markup \italic "ord., espress."`, cellos `\mp^\markup \italic "arco, espress."`, basses `\mp^\markup \italic "arco"` (all were mf). Add a crescendo at 196 (Vla `d'4(\< c'4 e'4 g'4)`, Vc `f2(\< e2)`, Cb `b,,2\< c,2`, all inside `\transpose c aes,`), then mf at 197 (`g2(\mf\< c'4 d'4)` in Vla and Vc, `e,2\mf\< f,2` in Cb) into the existing f at 198. This matches 67-77 a third lower and in A♭. Hn 1 stays p dolce. The violins, Hn 1, bassoons and the 197-198 wind entries are unchanged. This does not change F2.

### 4. Bar 82: the horns stay p through the crescendo into the closing group (applied)
Problem: from 75 Hn 1·2 hold the soft pad (p). At 82 every other part crescendos into 83 f: violins mf<, Fl 1 and Ob 1 mf<, violas, cellos, basses and bassoons <. The horns hold the E♭ chord at p with no hairpin, so the only brass in the bar fades relative to the rest as the music drives into Tempo I.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 130 says that after the strings, "the brass is the group most facile in producing dynamic shades of expression, glorifying crescendo chords", and that in a crescendo they join last. The appendix's f and ff chords (PDF 505-507) never leave the brass a level below the strings at the moment of arrival.
Fix: sTwo.ily `sTwoHnOne` last bar `es'1\<`, `sTwoHnTwo` last bar `g1\<`. In clos.ily `closHnOne = { R1*4\! | ...` and `closHnTwo = { R1*4\! | ...` end the hairpin on the rest. The horns swell and release at 83. They re-enter at 89 as before. The shared staff has equal dynamics.
