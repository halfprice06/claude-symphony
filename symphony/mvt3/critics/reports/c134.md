## Findings

### 1. 159-166: Vln II's counter-line has no wind doubling and is covered (applied)
Problem: from 159 Vln II leaves F1 for the slurred eighth-note counter-line from P2 (c107). It is played by one string section alone, ff, in the same octave as the tune's middle voice (Ob 1-2, Vln I lower half at C5-A♭5). It also sits next to the clarinets' eighth-note arpeggios (E♭4-C5) and under the off-beat trumpets and horns. The tune has nine parts, the counter-line one.
Evidence: in the Jupiter coda (PDF 176) every line has a wind at pitch: "second violin doubled by first oboe at pitch". Adler gives the reason: "each theme must be heard at good strength". In Meistersinger (PDF 178) each theme's colour combines at least one string and one woodwind. PDF 169: "The ear is able to separate two ideas presented simultaneously if they are scored with opposing timbres and differing designs." The counter-line has a different design (eighths). It does not have its own timbre, because it is strings against a tune that is also in the strings.
Fix (recP.ily, `recPOboeTwo`, bars 159-166): Ob 2 leaves the tune and doubles Vln II at pitch. Bars 159-165 copy Vln II's eighths from `es''8( d'' c'' b') c''( es'' g'' es'')` to `b'8( d'' f'' d'') g'( d' f' d')`, and bar 166 is `c'2-> r2`. The range is D4-G5, the oboe's strongest. Ob 1 keeps the tune, including its third below at 162 (kept since cycle 7). The tune still has Fl 1-2 + Vln I upper (C6 level), Ob 1 + Vln I lower (C5), and violas + Hn 1-2 (C4). F1's notes and articulation are unchanged.

### 2. 165-166: Hn 1-2 drop to E♭3-B2 just above the bass (applied)
Problem: at 165 Hn 1-2 follow F1's falling seventh into the bass clef: E♭3-D3-C3-B2, then C3 at 166, ff. The bass is G2 (Bn, B.tbn, Vc) with G1 below, so the horns play the 4-3 suspension a fourth and a third above the bass, in the lowest octave of the chord. The violas, which doubled the horns until 164, go back up to E♭4 at 165, so the horns are two octaves below the violas and the tune's middle octave thins. Then 166→167 is a two-octave leap for the horns, C3 to C5.
Evidence: PDF 164 (Ex. XV-15, Mahler 1): in the big tutti "only the lowest octave ... is left bare". PDF 162-163 (Brahms 3 final chord): the layout gives "the separation of high and low perceived by the ear". PDF 159, consideration 2: each pitch goes to an instrument in a register where it can play the dynamic. Concert E♭3-B2 is the horns' dull low-middle range, and it masks the suspension in the bass.
Fix (recP.ily, `recPHnOne` and `recPHnTwo`, 165-166): replace `\tag #'score \clef bass \once \stemUp es4( d8 c8~ c4 b,4) | c2-> r2 \tag #'score \clef treble` with `es'4( d'8 c'8~ c'4 b4) | c'2-> r2`. The horns stay in unison with the violas (written B♭4-F♯4), and 166→167 becomes C4→C5. The bass clef switch is no longer needed. The exposition's 25-26 stays as it is: there the horns are the tune's only lower octave (the violas play the eighth figure at 19-26), and the dynamic is f.

### 3. 165 beat 2: Tpt 3 B3 sounds against F1's suspended C5 (applied)
Problem: the off-beat Tpt 3 plays B3 on beat 2 of 165, the bar in which F1 takes up C at 2.5 for the 4-3 suspension. The trumpet quarter is still sounding when the C enters. Cycle 62 removed B from the clarinets at this point for the same reason, but the trumpet B remained. A trumpet is the most penetrating part in this chord.
