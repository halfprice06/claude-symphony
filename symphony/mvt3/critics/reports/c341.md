## Findings

### 1. 77-80: F2's climax phrase in the cellos has no horn, although the horn doubled the same cellos' line four bars earlier (applied)

**Problem.** At 67-82 the violins play F2 for the first time in E♭ major, after the trumpet's E♭-minor solo. The phrase 77-80 is the tune's climax: B♭6 at 78 in Vln I, and the line descends through 79-80. It sounds in three octaves:
- Vln I and Fl 1 at E♭6.
- Vln II and Ob 1 at E♭5.
- The cellos with Cl 1 at E♭4-B♭4 (tenor clef, f at 78).

At 67-74 Hn 1 doubles the cellos' counter-melody, pp dolce (c287). At 77-80, when the cellos take the tune itself, Hn 1 leaves them and plays pad notes (E♭4-C4 | C4-D4 | E♭4 | C4-D4). So the horn colour is on the subordinate line and absent from the climax. F2 was introduced by a brass instrument at 51, and in the exposition its major-key climax has no brass on the line.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 27: "Usually the horn and cello double on long sustained melodies." In Ex. XI-10 (image PDF 27) the horns double the solo cello's single line, which gives it "added articulation" and "personalities".
- PDF 19 (Ex. XI-4): in Beethoven's major finale theme the horns join the melody in octaves. They do not stay on harmony.
- PDF 26: the brass used "as soloists ... in combination" are "colors he may use whenever he wishes", and this is a colour the score already uses for the counter-melody.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 79, cited in c287: horns with cellos produce "a beautifully blended, soft quality of tone".
- The recapitulation's F2 in A♭ major (187-194) already has Hn 1 on the tune. The exposition's climax is the one statement where the line goes without it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-341/work/sections/sTwo.ily, sTwoHnOne).** Hn 1 doubles the cellos and Cl 1 at pitch on 77-80 and breathes with Cl 1 after 78. At 81 it returns to its pad note D4. The dynamics stay exactly as before (crescendo from 77, mp at 78, diminuendo from 80 beat 3, p at 81), so they still match Hn 2 on the shared staff. As written now, the horn colours the line and does not dominate it.

```
was:  ... \breathe | es'2\pp d'4 es'4 | f'2 es'2 | es'2\< c'2 | c'2\mp d'2 | es'1 | c'2 d'2\> | d'1\p | bes1\< \breathe | }
now:  ... \breathe | es'2\pp d'4 es'4 | f'2 es'2 | es'2.(\< f'4 | bes'4.\mp aes'8 g'4 f'4) \breathe | es'2.( d'4 | c'4 bes4 aes4\> g4) | d'1\p | bes1\< \breathe | }
```

- **Hn 2.** Its pad G3-F3 | G3-B♭3 | C4 | F3 is unchanged. Hn 1 stays above it at every point, so the parts do not cross, and the lowest note, G3 at 80.4, is still above Hn 2's F3.
- **Harmony.** Hn 1's pad notes are covered by Bn 1·2, the violas, the harp and Hn 2, so the harmony stays complete.
- **What does not change.** F2's notes, the counter-melody, Cl 1 and every other part. The c147 voice-leading notes for 76-82 still hold for Hn 2, and the c148 crescendo to mp at 78 is kept.
- **For finale.md.** F2 itself is unchanged. Only its doubling changes: "77-80 Hn 1 doubles the cellos and Cl 1 on F2's climax phrase." No other movement quotes F2, so no other thread needs a message.
