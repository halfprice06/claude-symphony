## Findings

### 1. 16, 20, 156: the off-beat horn chords strike a semitone against F1's passing notes in the horns (applied)
- **Problem.** In F1's antecedent and consequent, Hn 1·2 carry the tune at C4 with the violas, and Hn 3·4 play staccato off-beat chords in the same octave. Three times an off-beat attack lands a semitone from the tune's passing eighth, inside the horn section and in one register. At 16.2 and 156.2, Hn 3 strikes D♭4 as the tune passes D♭–C–B♭ (C4 against D♭4). At 20.2, Hn 4 strikes G3 as the tune passes B♭–A♭–G (A♭3 against G3). The scan finds no other same-family semitone attacks under F1 except 164.2 (finding 3).
- **Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132: a florid line "comprising notes foreign to the harmony, passing or grace notes" cannot run against a line "reduced to essential and fundamental notes" without discord. Transposing the florid part an octave away reduces it. Timbre difference reduces it most (PDF 132–133). Between related groups, "the harmonic basis generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design" (PDF 133). Here the basis and the design are the same instrument in the same octave, which is the case the text warns against. Ex. 265 and 273–274 (Tsar Saltan, PDF 432, 439–440) keep the horns' repeated chords out of the tune's register.
- **Fix (applied).**
  - pOne.ily, `pOneHnThree` bar 16: `| r4 des'4-. r4 c'4-. |` → `| r4 f'4-. r4 c'4-. |`.
  - pOne.ily, `pOneHnFour` bar 20: `r4 g4-. r4 a4-.` → `r4 d'4-. r4 a4-.`.
  - recP.ily, `recPHnThree` bar 156: `| r4 des'4-. r4 c'4-. |` → `| r4 f'4-. r4 c'4-. |`.
- **What stays the same.** At 16.2 and 156.2 F4 doubles Cl 2's held F4, so the chord keeps F and A♭. The ♭II6 colour stays where finale.md records it, in the tune's D♭ over the bass F on the downbeat. At 20.2 Hn 4 joins Hn 3 on D4; G is still sounding in Cl 1's held G4 and in the tune's next eighth. Hn 3 moves E♭4–F4–C4 and Hn 4 moves G3–D4–A3, both by easy steps for off-beat staccato. The tune, bass, clarinets, dynamics and protected F1 bowing are unchanged, and both shared horn staves keep equal dynamics. This answers the 11–18 protection (c781, c902) the way c2009 did: only off-beat accompaniment notes change.

### 2. 157–158: c536 put the cor anglais in its thinnest register, doubling three parts (v529 → v539 regression; applied)
- **Problem.** c536 gave Ob 2's unison copy of F1 at 151–158 to the cor anglais and described it as "written G5–A4, its middle register". At 157–158, however, the line climbs to written E♭6 and D6 (sounding A♭5 and G5), held ff at the end of an eight-bar ff phrase. There it doubles Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II at the unison. At 159 the instrument then drops an octave to G4 for its inner line. v529's analysis gives the cor anglais a top of G5; v539's gives A♭5.
- **Evidence.** Adler part 1, PDF 204–205 (Ex. VII-46, VII-47): the top of the range is marked "thin, pinched", and "in its upper register, the English horn sounds so much like an oboe that it loses its personality". In unison with Ob 1 and both violin sections, it adds no audible colour there and costs the player effort.
- **Fix (applied, recP.ily, `recPCorAnglais`).** `f'8( aes'8 c''8 f''8 aes''2) | g''2.-> r4` → `f8( aes8 c'8 f'8 aes'2) | g'2.-> r4`. The cor anglais now plays the arpeggio with Hn 1·2 and the violas (written C4–E♭5, its sonorous register). Its G4 at 158 leads straight into the inner line on G4 at 159. c536's point is kept: Ob 2 still rests and no oboe unison returns. This is a correction of c536's register, not a reversal of the change.

### 5. Rachmaninoff 2/iv: would a conductor believe a master wrote this?
- **Shared features.** Rachmaninoff's finale is cyclic: it recalls the Adagio's theme quietly before its lyric second theme, and its coda gathers earlier material into a broad major-key apotheosis. This finale does the same. Movement II's tail comes back at 95–98, theme B at 211–218 and movement I's heads at 235–254. The scherzo's motive x is in the retransition at 143–145, and the arrival and chorale give the C-major apotheosis. Rachmaninoff builds his climaxes by adding layers over held harmony (descending scales stacked at several speeds). Here 209–210 (scales diverging over the A♭ pedal) and 263–264 do the same thing on a smaller scale.
- **Differences.** Rachmaninoff brings his big second theme back in full in the tonic major. Here F2 has only 255–258 under the chorale (c339 kept this: the Brahms 1 model, protected bars, page cap; I agree). Rachmaninoff's accompaniment textures are denser and more ornamental. This score is closer to Brahms in economy, and that suits the movement's plan.
- **Verdict.** A conductor would take this as professional late-Romantic work. The places where the craft fell below that level were small: the inner-horn semitone attacks under the main theme (finding 1), which a horn section would hear at every rehearsal, and the cor anglais's register at the recapitulation's peak (finding 2).
