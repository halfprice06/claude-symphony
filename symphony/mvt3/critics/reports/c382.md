## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 81-83: V7-IV6 deceptive cadence before the closing theme. APPLIED

**Problem.** F2's last statement in E♭ cadences V7-I at 81→82. Bar 82 then holds a whole bar of E♭ in the bass while the violins hold E♭ and run up. Bar 83, the closing theme (f), starts on E♭ again and stays on it for a whole bar. That gives two downbeat tonics in a row. The closing theme's arrival on 83 then starts from a tonic that has already sounded, so it is not reached as a goal.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 201 says VI in place of I after V7 "can forestall excessive repetitions of I" and "carries the line on" (Ex. 13-11a).
- PDF 200-201 and 204-205 (Ex. 13-15, Ex. 13-20i) give IV6 as the second form of the deceptive cadence. It "has the effect of temporarily turning the 7th of V7 into a consonance".
- PDF 203: the deceptive cadence is strongest with 7-1 on top. Here the tune is D5→E♭5. The suspense it creates ends only with an authentic cadence.
- PDF 170 (Ex. 11-12d): IV6-V7 with the 6th of IV6 preparing the 7th.
- PDF 191 (Ex. 12-1): phrase overlap, where one tonic both ends one phrase and starts the next.
- Every earlier pass noted that the score never uses V7-IV6. Its six deceptive cadences all go to VI or i6.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-382/work/sections/sTwo.ily, with a c382 comment).** Bar 82 becomes IV6 for three beats and V on beat 4, so the cadence lands on 83:

```
sTwoCb     ... | bes,,1 | c,2.\< bes,,4 | }                 % was es,1\<
sTwoVc     ... | \clef bass c2.\mp\< bes,4 | }             % was es1\mp\<
sTwoBsnTwo ... | bes,1\p | c2.\< bes,4 \breathe | }        % was es1\<
sTwoBsnOne ... | aes1\p~ | aes2.\< f'4 \breathe | }        % was aes1\p | g1\<
sTwoHnOne  ... | d'1\p | es'2.\< bes4 \breathe | }         % was es'1\<
sTwoHnTwo  ... | f1\p | es2.\< bes,4 \breathe | }          % was es1\<
sTwoVla    ... | c'2.\mp\< d'4 | }                         % was g1\mp\<
sTwoHarpR  ... | es'8 aes'8 c''8 es''8 r2^\markup \italic "étouffez" ... % was es' g' bes' es''
sTwoHarpL  ... | << c,2 { s8 \ottava #0 } >> r2 | }         % was es,2
```

**Voice leading.**
- At 82.1 the IV6 is C (basses, cellos, Bsn 2, harp), E♭ and A♭. E♭ is doubled as the 3rd above the bass (PDF 169), in Hn 1·2, the tune, Fl 1 and Vln II.
- Bsn 1 holds the 7th A♭3 of 81 as the consonant root of IV6.
- The violins' held E♭ and their run G-A♭ fit the IV6: G is a passing note and A♭ a chord tone. The run's B♭-D falls on beat 4 over V.
- At 82.4 the leading tone D4 (violas) and F4 (Bsn 1) both resolve to E♭4 on 83. The run's last note D6 goes to E♭6 over B♭→E♭ (7-8 over 5-1).
- The tune, the run, the animando, all dynamics and bar 83 are unchanged.

**What this undoes, and why the trade is worth it.**
- c362's "incomplete tonic with tripled root" moves from 82 to 83. Bar 83.1 sounds E♭ in octaves with B♭ in the horns, and every voice reaches it by step or common tone. So the device stays, one bar later.
- c113's held E♭ bass under the run is replaced by the moving C-B♭ bass.
- The cellos now double the bass at 82 instead of rejoining the violas' counter. finale.md line 22 ("rejoin the violas' counter at 82") will need that clause changed.

**Tests.**
- The build is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty and the score is 16 pages.
- analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. It differs from v382's only in note counts, the horns' lowest note (B♭2) and one density row.
- par8.py over 80-84 shows two kinds of new lines:
  - At 82.1, 82.4 and 83.1, Bsn 2 and the cellos double the bass. These are octave doublings of the same line, like the existing cello lines at 83-84.
  - At 83.1, the oboe and violins show B♭→E♭. This is a quarter-grid artefact. The eighth-grid listing shows these voices move D→E♭.
- The finale theme record is 51/51.

### 6. II6/5-V4/2 over a common bass (Ex. 12-14, PDF 186). NOT APPLIED

### 7. 69 and 189: apparent II-IV root motion. CHECKED, FOLLOWS

At 69 the chord is C-E♭-(F)-A♭ before A♭ at 70. At 189 it is F-(B♭)-D♭ before D♭ at 190. On paper these look like II-IV, the rising-3rd order that PDF 172 warns against. On the page, both are IV6 reached by the 5-6 technique, moving to root-position IV with the tune's 6 as an appoggiatura. That is Ex. 11-12a (PDF 168-170). Fix: none.
