## Findings

### 1. 75: F2's second half repeats 67-68 note for note. Give it the five-note turn (applied; changes F2)

- **Problem.** The E♭-major F2 (67-82) is sixteen bars of quarters and halves. Its second half opens at 75-76 with exactly the notes and rhythm of 67-68 (B♭5 dotted half, G | F E♭ G B♭), and the only rhythmic event in the theme is the dotted peak at 78. In each half, the first two bars are where the listener learns the tune. A master's lyrical finale theme varies the repeat there: Dvořák 9/iv bar 39 (above), and the Romantic turn in Tchaikovsky, Mahler and Elgar. In this tune nothing between 67 and 78 is shorter than a quarter. The figure that would make it recognisable on its second hearing is missing.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 338-339, sec. 13 and Ex. 20-21b (Mahler, Das Lied von der Erde VI): "In nineteenth-century music, the turn often becomes a five-note ornament consisting of main tone, upper neighbor, main tone, lower neighbor, and main tone."
  - PDF 333, sec. 6: the neighbour figure "is often treated motivically".
  - PDF 328: figuration is "part of the essential substance of music", the means by which a framework is animated.
  - PDF 345, exercise 4: varying a given theme by adding figuration to its outer voice.
  - The turn is the one figuration type in the unit that this movement never uses (table, row 18).
  - Corpus: Dvořák 9/iv 39, neighbour figuration on the varied repeat.
- **Fix** (sections/sTwo.ily, applied):

  ```
  sTwoVlnOne … | bes''2.( g''4) | f''4( es''4 g''4 bes''4) | …
            →  … | bes''2( c'''16 bes'' aes'' bes'' g''4) | f''4( es''4 g''4 bes''4) | …
  sTwoVlnTwo … | bes'2.( g'4) | …   →   … | bes'2( c''16 bes' aes' bes' g'4) | …
  ```

  The five-note turn B♭-C-B♭-A♭-B♭ sits on beat 3, under the existing slur, so the bowing, the dynamics and the bow plan do not change. At 2 = 72 (Poco meno mosso) the sixteenths run at about 9.6 notes a second, an easy cantabile turn for the section.
- **Decisions.**
  - A♭ is diatonic, not the chromatic A♮ of Ex. 20-23, because the violas and cellos hold A♭3 on beat 3 (`g2( aes4 bes4)`). A♮ would make a simultaneous cross-relation.
  - The violins' A♭ meets the violas' A♭ for one sixteenth by oblique motion. A&S PDF 341 forbids 8ves made by parallel figuration, and this is not one.
  - The harp's G4 at 75.3.5 sounds under the turn's A♭ as an ordinary neighbour-tone dissonance.
  - The trumpet solo (51-66, protected) stays plain, and so does the muted-trumpet statement at 171-186. That keeps cycle 1's point that the solo carries no ornament.
  - The A♭-major statement 187-198 stays plain. Its strings and bow plan are protected, and its twelve bars also lack 78's dotted peak, so both events stay with the exposition's major statement.
  - 77-82 (dotted peak, c241 suspension, c261 anticipation at 73) are unchanged.
- **Answer to cycle 1's rejection.** That rejection was about the trumpet solo (64, 184) and the cadence bar 80. Bar 75 is in neither: it is not the solo, and it is not a cadence. The appoggiaturas at 53 and 69 decorate the first-phrase peaks, so the second half's opening is the one place in F2 without figuration.
- **For the other threads:** this changes F2, a theme the finale exports, at one bar of its E♭-major violin statement (75). Its head, the trumpet solo and every other statement are unchanged.

### 2. Escape tone plus accented passing tone (Ex. 20-17): no unprotected place, not applied

- **Problem.** The movement never uses the idiom of Ex. 20-17 (PDF 337: "An important idiom consists of an incomplete neighbor (escape tone) followed by an accented passing tone, the two tones forming the decoration of a melodic progression of a second, usually a descending one").
- **Where it would go.** The figure needs a falling second in the tune across a change of chord, with the first note a chord tone. In the open bars of F1 and F2 there is none:
  - 73-74 already has c261's anticipation.
  - 80-81 already has c241's suspension.
  - 79's E♭6-D6 stays over one chord (IV), so an "escape tone" E♭ there would be consonant. Ex. 20-17a's C is dissonant.
  - The one clean place is F1's closing variant at 85.4-86.1, F6 (V7) to E♭6 (I6) (`f'''8 g''' | f'''8-> es'''8~ es'''4`). It lies inside 83-86, which c187 protected.
- **Decision.** Not applied. I record it so that a later pass does not search the same bars again.
