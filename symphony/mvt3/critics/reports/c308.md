## Findings

### 1. 135-137: the crescendo into the stop chord starts with every group at once (applied)

**Problem.** At 133-134 the strings play alone (f > p, c288). At 135 the diminished-seventh chain starts pp < in the strings, and in the same bar Cl 1-2 and Bn 1-2 (doubling the violins' and cellos' lines) and Hn 3-4 (doubling the violas) enter pp < with them. The three-bar crescendo to the sf at 138 therefore has only the players' own swell; the texture is complete from its first note.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Short crescendi and diminuendi are generally produced by natural dynamic means; when prolonged, they are obtained by this method combined with other orchestral devices ... Prolonged orchestral crescendi are obtained by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass." He also notes that the brass are best at "glorifying crescendo chords" into the sforzando, so the brass should be the last added. The score already builds its other long crescendi this way (123-131, 219-222, the 39-42 diminuendo in reverse). At 135 the added doublings bring no pitch that the strings lack: Cl 1 B♭4/Cl 2 E4/Bn 1 G3/Bn 2 D♭3 at 136 and Hn 3 E♭4/Hn 4 A3 at 137 are all in the strings' chord.

**Fix (retr.ily, applied).**
- Cl 1: `R1 | bes'1\p\< | a'1 | aes'1\subP |` (was `b'1\pp\< | bes'1 | a'1 | ...`)
- Cl 2: `R1 | e'1\p\< | es'1 | d'1\subP |`
- Bn 1: `R1 | g1\p\< | ges1 | f1\subP |`
- Bn 2: `R1 | des1\p\< | c1 | b,1\subP |`
- Hn 3: `R1*2 | es'1\p\< | d'1\sf\>-\stopped |`
- Hn 4: `R1*2 | a1\p\< | aes1\sf\>-\stopped |`

Strings unchanged: pp < from 135, sf > at 138. The added groups enter p, a bar apart, because the strings have already grown by then (Rimsky PDF 127, cited by c139: the added group enters p and grows faster). c198's protected curve, pp < into the sf > with the winds at sub. p and the stopped horns at 138, is kept; only the order of entry changes. The stretch the strings start alone continues the strings-only 133-134, so the dim7 chain begins in the colour the G-major close ended in. A side effect: Bn 1-2 and Hn 3-4, which held 135-146 with one breath (c166), now start one and two bars later.

### 2. 138: the strings' sf on the stop chord is not a compound chord (applied)

**Problem.** At 138 the winds take the stop chord sub. p and the strings attack it sf >, the sf-p method (a) (c008, c028). But Vln I (F5) and Vln II (D5) play single notes; only the violas have a double stop. The two largest string sections attack with one note each.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129: "At the moment when the wood-wind begins a piano chord, the strings attack it sforzando, a compound chord for preference, either arco or pizz." Ex. 253 (PDF 418) and Ex. 255 (PDF 419) both show the strings' sf as multiple stops. With the violins on double stops each section attacks two notes, so the sf has more weight without adding a player or lengthening the chord.

**Fix (retr.ily, applied).** Vln I `<aes' f''>1\sf\>^\markup \italic "non div."`, Vln II `<f' d''>1\sf\>^\markup \italic "non div."`. Vln I A♭4-F5 is a sixth on the D and A strings in second position; Vln II F4-D5 a sixth in first position. Both violin sections rest from 139, so the added notes need no resolution and create no voice-leading. The violins now attack the complete diminished seventh between them (A♭4, F5, F4, D5 over the cellos' and basses' B). The top line A♭5-G5-G♭5-F5 and Vln II's F5-E5-E♭5-D5 are unchanged. The stop chord's bass B (movement I 26), its unresolved state until 222→223, the winds' sub. p and the stopped horns are unchanged.
