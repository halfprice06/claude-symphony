## Findings

### 1. The development's G-minor half cadence reaches the Italian sixth by a leap, so the G-minor area does not take up the development's own lament line (bar 121). Applied.

**Problem.** The bassoon solo's G-minor section closes with a half cadence at 121–122: i (121.1, bass G) – Italian sixth (121.3, bass E♭, c152) – V (122, bass D). The bass leaps G–E♭. All three of the movement's Phrygian/augmented-sixth half cadences (57–58, 121–122, 177–178) approach the ♭6 by this same leap from 1.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 298–299 (Ex. 18-12, Handel): a passing chord over ♭7 leads from i to the iv6 of a Phrygian cadence, "thus producing a completely stepwise bass motion through the descending 4th 1–5". Unit 18 PDF 290–291: a stepwise series turns separate chords into one transitional line, which suits a passage that leaves a key area. In form terms, the development opens its climax with the chromatic lament bass F–E–E♭–D–C♯–C (107–109). With a passing F, the G-minor area ends on the diatonic form of that line, G–F–E♭–D, so the second key area of the development closes with the first area's figure. That ties the development's two halves together and makes 121–122 a cadence rather than a stop. In Ex. 18-12 the passing chord is a minor v6. Here the bassoon holds B♭3 from 121.1 to 121.2, which would be a 4th over the F of a v6 (F–A–D). So the passing chord is i4/2: the upper strings' pizzicato G4–B♭4 / D5–G5 already on 121.2, with B♭3 as a chord tone. The bass on 1–♭7–♭6–5 and the Italian sixth's ♭6–5 are unchanged. Vln II's D5 (121.2) → C♯5 (121.4) keeps the reading's 7–6 shape in the voice above.

**Fix (dev.ily, applied):**
- `devVc`, bar 121: `g4 r4 es4 r4` → `g4 f4 es4 r4`
- `devCb`, bar 121: `g,4 r4 es,4 r4` → `g,4 f,4 es,4 r4`

The bassoon solo, its register, the off-beat pizzicato layout, the Italian sixth and its resolution at 122 (c152) do not change. This answers c157's "bassoon solo 115–122" protection: the only change is one passing pizzicato quarter in the bass after the solo's B♭3 has sounded, under the chord already there, below the solo's register (F3 is not one of the solo's pitches, c063's concern).

### 2. The closing bridge accents a passing chord in the middle of its 6/3 series (bar 88, beats 3–4). Applied.

**Problem.** The series 87–89 runs I6 (87) – vii°6 – vi6 (88.1) – V6 (88.3) – IV6 with 7–6 (89.1) – V (89.3) – I (90). Inside it, the only accents are on 88 beats 3–4: Vln I B♭5–B♭4, Vln II B♭4–B♭3 and Fl 1 B♭5, all `->`, over the passing V6 (bass D). They give the closing key's dominant, in octaves, the weight of a half close halfway through the bridge. The bridge sounds like two two-bar blocks (87–88, 89–90) instead of one line from the I6 of 86–87 to the V–I of 89–90. The same accented octave figure at 90 marks the real end.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 291–292: in a 6/3 series "only the beginning and the end of the series will normally receive enough emphasis to form a point of articulation"; the I6 that begins it expands the tonic, and the chord at the end matters for its cadential function. Ex. 18-3a shows Mendelssohn's two-bar groups landing on the underlying third-chain (I6–VI6–IV6–II6), not on the passing chords between them. Here the underlying chain is I6 (87) – vi6 (88.1) – IV6 (89.1), and 88.3 is the passing chord between vi6 and IV6. c143 (Finding 1) joined 83–90 into one phrase by making 86 an I6. Without accents inside it, the series sounds as that one phrase, and the crescendo from 89 leads to the only accents at 90 and the ff at 91.

**Fix (clos.ily, applied):**
- `closVlnOne`, bar 88: `aes'') bes''4-> bes'4->` → `aes'') bes''4 bes'4`
- `closVlnTwo`, bar 88: `aes') bes'4-> bes4->` → `aes') bes'4 bes4`
- `closFluteOne`, bar 88: `aes'') bes''4-> r4` → `aes'') bes''4 r4`

Notes, slurs, dynamics and the accents at 90 are unchanged. The protected "6/3 bridge 83–90 harmony" (c212, c213) is untouched.
