## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The cymbals at 247 and 251 spend the colour saved for the last cadence (applied)
- **Bars:** 247, 251.
- **Problem:** The crash cymbals are the movement's held-back colour. Their first crash is the arrival at 223. In v28 they crash again at 247 (Allegro molto) and 251, inside the brass combination of the heads, before the last cadence at 263. So 263, the symphony's first root-position V–I in C, gets a cymbal that the listener heard sixteen bars earlier. coherence/finale.md also tells the other movements "Cymbals and bass drum at 223, 263, 278", so the score contradicts the state file.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 37: "One of the most successful ways of building climaxes is by holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event." The Tchaikovsky tutti (PDF 38–40) adds its new element only at the climax.
- **Fix (applied, finale.ily):** `finalePerc = \drummode { R1 | R1 | R1 | R1 | R1 | R1 | …` (bars 247 and 251 are now rests). I kept the crashes every other bar at 265–273, after the cadence, where they celebrate it.

### 2. The theme B recall is one 8-bar slur at ff with no breath (applied; articulation of a quotation)
- **Bars:** 211–218 (Tpt 1·2, Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Cl 1).
- **Problem:** The trumpets play theme B under a single slur at Largamente 2 = 72. That is about 13 s at ff with no place to breathe. It also hides the two halves that movement II gives the theme: movement II v180 bSec phrases it in two slurs with `\breathe` after its bar 58 (c036). In this augmented quotation that point is the end of bar 214.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 188: the dynamic and the instrument's strengths and weaknesses decide how a tune is assigned and shaped. A ff trumpet line of 13 s is outside what one breath can hold. Movement II's own phrasing is the source.
- **Fix (applied, codaTwo.ily):** `… ges''2. f''4) \breathe | es''2.( f''4 | des''1~ | des''1~ | des''1) |` in Tpt 1, Fl 1, Fl 2, Ob 1 and Cl 1 (octaves as written), and `… es''2. des''4) \breathe | c''2.( des''4 | aes'1~ …` in Tpt 2. **Quotation note for the movement II thread:** only the articulation of the finale's theme B quotation changed (two slurs and a breath). No notes changed.

### 3. The woodwinds that double the trumpets' tune drop to f under it (applied; partly revises c009)
- **Bars:** 211–212.
- **Problem:** Fl 1·2, Ob 1 and Cl 1 double Tpt 1's theme B (c019, c021) but carry the accompaniment's `\ff\>` … `\f`. From 212 they play the tune at f while the trumpets play it at ff. The doubling was added to strengthen the line and colour its legato. Marking it softer than the line it doubles works against that.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 188–189: doubling at pitch with instruments that suit the leader, and octave doubling, are the ways to strengthen a tune, and the doubling has to be meant.
- **Fix (applied, codaTwo.ily):** Fl 1·2, Ob 1 and Cl 1 are `aes'2->(\ff …` with no `\>` and no `\f` at 212. Ob 2 and Cl 2 share staves with them, so they also stay ff (`f''1\ff | f''1 | …`, `aes'1\ff | aes'1 | …`), which keeps the shared-staff dynamics equal. Brass, strings, bassoons and timpani keep c009's ff → f under the trumpets. This partly revises c009 for the upper woodwinds only.

### 4. The timpani's release stroke at 224 is fff under the chorale's f (applied)
- **Bars:** 224.
- **Problem:** `arrivalTimp = { c1:32\fff | c4 r4 r2 |` gives the stroke at 224 no dynamic, so it inherits fff and lands on the chorale's first chord, which every other part plays f.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 190: in a loud-to-soft change the loud sound has to give way to the new level. Adler PDF 195: dynamics "must be strictly observed".
- **Fix (applied, arrival.ily):** `c1:32\fff | c4\f r4 r2 |`.

### 5. The stop chord's sf at 138 is held without a decay, which covers the winds' sub. p (applied)
- **Bars:** 138.
- **Problem:** At the stop chord the winds and horns go to sub. p, while Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb and Ob 1 hold whole notes marked only sf. A held sf leaves the level for the rest of the bar undefined. Adler's forte-subito-piano only works if the loud chord gives way while the soft one sustains.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 190, Ex. XV-35: the held string chord is marked "sf > pp" against a short oboe sf. Ex. XV-36: the ff chord ends and the pp chord, already sounding, remains.
- **Fix (applied, retr.ily):** `\sf\>` at 138 in Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb. In Vla, Vc and Cb the hairpin ends at their existing `\p` at 139. In Ob 1 and the violins it ends on the following rest (`R1*2\!`, `R1*4\!`, `R1\!`). This keeps c008's sf and c010's B5 on top.

### 6. The diminuendo at 233–234 ends on "mf" printed under a rest (applied)
- **Bars:** 234, in 23 parts (winds, brass, timpani, violas, cellos, basses).
- **Problem:** `g'2. r4\mf` puts the mf on the beat-4 rest, after the players have stopped. The level they should reach on the last chord is not marked on that chord.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 195: every dynamic should serve the logic of the passage. A dynamic on silence does not.
- **Fix (applied, arrival.ily):** `g'2.\mf r4` in every part, and `c2.:32\mf r4` in the timpani. The 233 hairpin now ends at mf on 234's downbeat. This matches the violins, which already had `g''2.\mf g'4`.
