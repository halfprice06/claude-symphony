## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Parts book: multi-measure rests are split at invisible points (applied)
- **Bars:** every part. Examples: Tpt 1 81 | 82 and 83–86 | 87–90; Hn 1 81 | 82; percussion 239–246 as four single bars, 247–262 as sixteen single bars, 199–210 as 4 | 4 | 4; harp 49 | 50.
- **Problem:** cycle 75 tagged the `\mt` steps and the score's breaks as 'score so that the parts would show rests as single numbered ranges. But `\global` still splits its spacers at those points (`s1 s1`, `s1*4 s1*12`), and each spacer starts a new rest even with the tagged material removed. Percussion and other sources also write `R1 | R1 | …` bar by bar.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5 asks for rests as numbered ranges. Gould PDF 23 says the player must find the next entry quickly; this is the point of returning to the usual clef before an entry. My test file t3.ly showed `s1*14 s1 s1 s1*4 s1*12` against `R1*16 R1*8 R1*8` engraving as 14 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 4 | 8.
- **Fix (applied):** I added `\mergeRests` to global.ily. It joins adjacent plain skips, and adjacent plain full-bar rests that carry no text, dynamic or fermata, and drops the bar checks between them. Marks, tempo marks, key changes and double bars still divide rests. parts-book.ly and mkparts.py now feed `\mergeRests \removeWithTag #'score` for `\global` and for each player:
  ```
  \score { \new Staff << { \clef bass \mergeRests \removeWithTag #'score \global } { \clef bass \mergeRests \removeWithTag #'score \tbnOne } >> … }
  ```
- **Result:** Tpt 1 now reads E 14 | animando 2 | F 8. Percussion reads R 8 | S 8 and N 12. No part changes its page count, except the contrabassoon, which goes from 3 pages to 2. The score is unaffected.

### 2. Horn parts: needless bass clef at 25–26 and 165–166 (applied)
- **Bars:** Hn 1–2 parts, 25–26 and 165–166.
- **Problem:** at concert pitch the F1 tail E♭3–D3–C3–B2 needs the bass clef in the score. In the F parts the same notes read B♭3–A3–G3–F♯3, which is at most three ledger lines below the treble staff. The parts still switch to bass clef for two bars and back. For horn, a bass clef also raises the old- or new-notation question for the player.
- **Evidence:** Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently". PDF 23: a change away from the standard clef is kept only where it is needed. I rendered the v95 Hn 1 part: bass clef at the end of line 3, then treble again before 27.
- **Fix (applied, score unchanged):** in pOne.ily and recP.ily, Hn 1 and Hn 2:
  `| \tag #'score \clef bass \once \stemUp es4( d8 c8~ c4 b,4) | c2-> r2 \tag #'score \clef treble |`

### 3. Hn 1·2 bars 25 and 165: mixed stems within a half-bar (applied)
- **Problem:** E♭3 (one step above the middle line) is stemmed down, and the D–C quavers in the same minim are stemmed up, so the phrase slur flips above. Gould PDF 28: when stem direction varies within a bar, keep the direction of the notes in the same beat or half-bar.
- **Fix (applied):** `\once \stemUp` on E♭3, in the line quoted in finding 2. The slur now runs below the whole bar.

### 4. Violins I/II bar 76: hairpin stub at the system end (applied)
- **Problem:** `bes''4)\<` started the crescendo on the last crotchet of 76, the last bar of system 1 on p. 4. It printed as a 2 mm wedge after the last note, then started again on the next system.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5 (avoid over-crowding) and PDF 10, Example 7: marks must be readable. Gould PDF 22 asks for the same in another context: place a change where it does not split a gesture across a break.
- **Fix (applied):** sTwo.ily, Vln I `f''4( es''4 g''4 bes''4) | es'''2.\< f'''4\upbow |`, and the same in Vln II an octave lower. The crescendo still reaches f at 78. Vla/Vc keep their crescendo in 76 to mf at 77, which cycle 94 accepted.
