## Findings

### 1. 69–70 and 189–190: the counter-melody moves in octaves with the tune (applied)
Problem. The violas' and cellos' counter-melody under F2 (67–82, again a minor third lower at 187–198) copies the tune's rhythm and, for four notes, its pitches. At 69–70 the tune is `f'''2 es'''4 d'''4 | c'''2 bes''2` and the counter is `c'2 es'4 d'4 | c'2 bes2`: E♭–D–C–B♭ in the same rhythm, two and three octaves below the violins. At 189–190 it is C–B–A–G against `d'''2 c'''4 b''4 | a''2 g''2`. par.py lists these as P8 between Violas/Violoncellos and both violin parts (v27 lines "69:4.0 P8 ... Eb4-Eb6 -> D4-D6", "189:4.0 P8 ... C4-C6 -> B3-B5").
Evidence. Adler PDF 259–260: the accompanying counterpoint "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part". A&S PDF 83: in parallel octaves one part duplicates the other and they are not heard as two voices. For two bars the only independent line under the movement's lyrical theme disappears into a doubling.
Fix (sTwo.ily `sTwoVla`, `sTwoVc`; recS.ily `recSVla`, `recSVc`): move in quarters where the tune holds and hold where it moves.
- 69–70: `c'2( es'4 d'4) | c'2( bes2)` → `aes4( bes4 c'2) | c'4( d'4 es'2)`
- 189–190: `a2( c'4 b4) | a2( g2)` → `f4( g4 a2) | a4( b4 c'2)`
Against the tune: sixth, fifth (passing), tenth, ninth (tune passing) in 69; octave by oblique motion, passing seventh, fifth by contrary motion in 70. The B♮ at 190 and the D at 70 are passing tones over the IV → I6 bass. The tune itself (F2) is not changed.

### 2. 227–228: the canon's lower octave crosses above the chorale's top line (applied)
Problem. The canon at 226–230 is doubled in two octaves: Vln I and Fl 1 at G5–E6, and Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II at G4–E5. The lower octave is in the trumpets' register. At 227 it has E5 (ff, three instruments) while Tpt 1 plays the chorale's C5→D♭5 at f; at 228 it has D5 over the trumpet's C5. For two bars the protected chorale top line, including its D♭ (the motto's Neapolitan note), is not the top of its own octave and is covered by a louder unison a step or third above it.
Evidence. Adler PDF 274: keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the main line; PDF 283 (Fidelio canon): the texture around the canonic voices is kept "extremely light", and doublings may sit in other octaves. Here the doubling sits inside the other canonic voice.
Reason for touching a protected passage. Cycles 19 and 25 protect 223–234 because the arrival and chorale are at a master's level. This change keeps everything they protect (arrival chord at 223, chorale top lines, canon pitches and rhythm, 222→223, dynamics) and only takes the lower doubling of the canon out of the chorale's register, so that the protected line is heard.
Fix (arrival.ily): Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II 226–230 up an octave, into unison with Fl 1 and Vln I:
`g'1\ff | e''1 | d''1 | c''2 des''2 | c''1` → `g''1\ff | e'''1 | d'''1 | c'''2 des'''2 | c'''1` in `arrivalOboeOne` and `arrivalClOne`, and the same in `arrivalVlnTwo` (keeping its "unis., change bow at will"). Ob 1 reaches E6 once (227), within range at ff. Ob 2, Cl 2 and Fl 2 are unchanged.

### 3. 115–122: the bassoon solo is accompanied inside its own register and colour (applied)
Problem. Bassoon 1 plays F2 p, "solo, espress.", in its middle register (G3–G4, mostly D4–G4). Around it: Cl 1·2 hold pp whole notes at G3–B♭3 (`bes1 | a1 | ...`, `g1 | fis1 | ...`), the same woodwind colour in the solo's lower half; Vln II pizz. dyads at D4–A4 lie on its upper half; violas pizz. at G3–C4. Every accompanying layer except the basses lies within the bassoon's own octave.
Evidence. Adler PDF 274: keep the accompaniment registrally distant, "especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". PDF 264 and 267: Mozart uses no orchestral horns under the solo horn and no orchestral flutes in the flute concerto, so the soloist meets no "colour competition"; when the soloist is in a soft part of its range the orchestra is "reduced drastically". PDF 268–269: the solo enters in a sparse, transparent texture.
Fix (dev.ily): `devClOne` and `devClTwo` 115–122 → `R1*8`. `devVlnTwo` pizz. dyads 115–122 up an octave (`<d'' g''>`, `<d'' fis''>`, `<es'' g''>` ...), above the solo; at 119 `<es' a'>` becomes `<c'' es''>` (the octave-higher A5 made a new top-to-bass A→D octave into 120 in analysis.txt; C–E♭ keeps the half-diminished chord). Violas, cellos and basses unchanged. The solo now has pizz. chords above, pizz. bass below and nothing in its register.

### 4. 186: clarinet 1 sits above the solo's final note (applied)
Problem. At the deceptive cadence that ends the muted trumpet and cor anglais statement, the solo descends to C4 (`d'2 b2 | c'1`, 185–186), the weakest part of both instruments' ranges. Cl 1 moves G3 → E♭4 and holds E♭4 above the solo's closing C4 for the whole bar. At 66 (the same place in the exposition) Cl 1 has B♭3 below the trumpet's E♭4, so 186 is the exception.
Evidence. Adler PDF 274 (keep the accompaniment registrally distant from a woodwind solo); PDF 275 (the lowest part of a range must be lightly accompanied).
Fix (recS.ily `recSClOne`): `g1 | es'1 | c'1\pp` → `g1 | aes1 | c'1\pp`. A♭3 is the root of the A♭ chord (Cl 2 E♭3, Bsn 1 A♭2) and is reached by step from G3. par.py flags a fifth Cl 1/Vln II A♭3–E♭4 → C4–G4 into 187; Vln II's E♭4 is a pizz. quarter on beat 2 followed by two beats of rest, so there is no audible parallel. The protected muted trumpet and cor anglais line is not touched.
