## What else I studied

I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY, the plan, coherence/finale.md, the c029 report (the first pass of this reading), every v129 section file and analysis.txt.

**Dvořák 8/iv.** I compared the score with the Dvořák finale's form: trumpet fanfare, cello theme, variations, the C-minor march episode, and a coda that drops to pp before the last presto. v129 shares its economy: one main theme, rescored on each return, with loud passages built by addition. A conductor would accept 197–279 as competent late-Romantic tutti writing. The clear difference is pacing. Dvořák lets the music fall to real quiet before each surge. v129 stays loud from 197 to 279, with soft dips only four bars long (235–238, 255–256, 264). That is a form decision protected by HISTORY (223–234, 259–263, 273–276), and the dips that exist do their job, so I make no change for it.

**v119.** I diffed v119 against v129, file by file. The changes are breaths, the cellos' mf at 187, the inner-voice repairs at 147–150, 238 and 245, and c121–c128's work on 257–272. Nothing got clearly worse. The one doubtful point is c128's cut of the harmony on 264.1 (finding 3).

**Scripts.** I wrote small scripts over score.midi, with part-combined tracks merged by staff. They check seconds and sevenths inside each brass family, trumpets above woodwind, and gaps in the brass upper voices.

**Quotations.** themecheck on v129 gives 36/36 matches, and themes-scherzo.ly matches scherzo v87 (41/41). No quotation needs updating, and **no quotation, F1, F2 or chorale top line was changed** by this report.

## Findings

### 1. Same-colour held discords inside one brass family (130–131, 147–150, 207–210, 238) — applied

**Problem.** In four places a sustained second or seventh sits between two players of the same brass family:
- Hn 3/Hn 4 C4–D4 at 130–131;
- Hn 2 G4 against Hn 3 F4 at 147–150;
- Tbn 2 G♭3 against B.tbn A♭2 at 207–210;
- Hn 3/Hn 4 F3–G3 at 238.

**Evidence.** Rimsky says such discords should be given to instruments of different colour, so that they sound as a mixture rather than a rub (PDF 102–103, Ex. 133–136). With bassoons and horns, the discord belongs in the bassoons (PDF 108). In each case the dissonant note is already doubled in another family (bassoon, viola or a second horn pair), so it can leave the brass without the chord losing it.

**Fix.**
- dev.ily, devHnFour, 130–131: `c'4 c'4 c'4 c'4 | c'1\f` → `a4 a4 a4 a4 | a1\f`. Hn 4 now sounds A3 under Hn 3 D4. C stays in the bassoons and violas.
- retr.ily, retrHnThree, 147–150: `f'1->\ff | f'1 | f'1~ | f'2. r4` → `d'1->\ff | d'1 | d'1~ | d'2. r4`. D4 falls to C4 at 151. F stays in Bsn 1 and Ob 2.
- codaOne.ily, codaOneTbnTwo, 207–210: `ges1\mf | ges1 | ges1\< | ges2.` → `es1\mf | es1 | es1\< | es2.`. The G♭ seventh stays in Hn 2, Bsn 1 and violas.
- sHead.ily, bar 238: Hn 3 `g1` → `d'1` and Hn 4 `f1` → `g1`. The horns now hold D4/G3, and the F stays in the bassoon.

Protected discords at 1, 5, 219, 221, 259 and 262 are untouched. I kept trumpets B♭4/C5 at 110 and 112 because the alternative would complete a C-major brass triad before 223 against the cyclic rule. I kept trombones D3/C4 at 152 and 157 (two beats only) and the short chords at 91–93, since Rimsky says balance matters less in short chords (PDF 114). Applied; the build is clean.

### 2. Woodwind mf under ff brass at the last chorale (257–262) — applied; this reopens a protected point

**Problem.** The woodwind return at 257 after the 255–256 rest marked `\mf\<` and reach only `\f\<` at 259, while the brass hold the chorale ff. On paper and in the MIDI render, the woodwind harmony above the trumpets is covered for its first two bars. The chorale's final appearance then sounds like brass alone, which is close to how it sounded at 223.

**Evidence.** Rimsky says that when the woodwind take the harmony above trumpets and trombones they should be marked one degree louder than the brass in difficult cases (PDF 112). The hymn in Ex. 166 (Snegourotchka) is the model this passage follows. It first appears in horns and trombones mf, and on its return the full woodwind enter ff above ff brass, which is what gives the return its new colour (PDF 110–111, 118). c036's own argument for the low reeds also applies here: a weak entry under ff brass is covered.

**Reason in HISTORY and my answer.** c038 protected the rest and the mf re-entry, and c033 relied on Rimsky PDF 127 and 130 (crescendo by addition). I keep the rest at 255–256, the re-entry at 257 and the staged crescendo to fff. Only the starting level moves one degree, so the crescendo by addition is unchanged.

**Fix.** In finale.ily, in finaleFluteOne, FluteTwo, OboeOne, OboeTwo, ClOne, ClTwo, BassCl, BsnOne, BsnTwo and Cbsn: `\mf\<` at 257 → `\f\<`, and `\f\<` at 259 → `\ff\<`. Partners on shared staves change together, so their dynamics stay equal. Applied; the build is clean.

### 3. The harmony cut at 264.1 (c128) — not applied, kept deliberately

**Problem.** Since c128, the brass leave the cadence chord on 264.1, and the cadence's resolution lasts one beat in the brass.

**Evidence.** In Ex. 173 (Sadko), Rimsky holds the brass on the ff chord while strings and woodwind run the scale.

**Decision.** At 264, though, the cut is what gives the soft F1 at 265 its room, and that F1 is one of the four short dips the form has. Keeping the dip matters more here than the Sadko model, so I leave it as c128 wrote it. No fix.

### 4. Moving chain in the brass at 273–276 — not applied (protected)

The close moves the trombones through a chord chain, while Rimsky would give the moving parts to the woodwind and keep the brass stationary (PDF 108). HISTORY protects 273–276 for the cadence's weight, and the chain is short and loud enough that the rule's concern about clarity does not bite. No change.

### 5. Clarinet seconds at 52 and 60 — not applied

The clarinets hold a second for two beats under the trumpet solo. They are one colour, but the passage is p, short, and the second is the colouring Rimsky asks for against a fanfare (PDF 115–116). No change.
