## Findings

### 1. 164: bass trombone E♭2 a minor sixth over G1 (applied)

**Problem.** In the recapitulation's last F1 bar the tuba, contrabassoon and basses fall to G1 on beat 3 (`g,2 g,,2`). The bass trombone doubles the cellos' and bassoons' `es4 d4` an octave lower, so E♭2 (78 Hz) sounds f a minor sixth above G1 (49 Hz), with D2 after it. The exposition's bar 24 has this line only in the cellos (E♭3), a thirteenth above the bass. The recapitulation's added brass bass brought it down into the register where only octaves and fifths sound clear.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 274–275: the low partials of G1 are G2, D3, G3 and B3, and a non-partial interval at the bottom of a loud chord muddies the whole chord. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85: widely spaced intervals belong at the bottom. c264 fixed the same fault at 215–218 (Tbn 2 A♭2 over F2). This bar is the only remaining loud, unprotected case in the scan.

**Fix (recP.ily, `recPBassTbn`, bar 14 of the section).** `| g,2 g,2 |` replaces `| g,2 es,4 d,4 |`. The bass trombone holds G2, the octave above the basses. E♭3–D3 stays in the cellos and bassoons. c214 had written E♭2–D2 to avoid an octave leap G2–G3 and a passing F2 under the basses' G2. Holding G2 has neither problem, so c214's reason still holds. No theme note changes.

### 2. 235–242: Hn 3·4 hold E3–G3 over the C pedal (not applied, protected)

Under movement I's second-theme head in the violins, Hn 3·4 hold E3/G3 at p over Cb C2 and Vc C3. E3 is not a partial of C2 below E4 (row 4). G3/C4 or E4/G4 would follow the series. The passage is on the do-not-touch list ("S-head texture 235–242"), and at p the third is soft, so I made no change. The composer may want to look at it the next time this passage is open.

### 3. Techniques the score does not use: location, mutes, low flute, a string-free passage (considered, not applied)

The third series asks for one technique from the reading that the score does not use yet. I checked four.

- **Movement across the space** (PDF 261, 273, 284, Fig. 6.3). The orchestral equivalent is antiphony between sections that sit apart, as in Tchaikovsky 6/iv. The places where Vln I and Vln II share a sequence are 87–90 (the bridge figure in octaves with Fl 1 and Ob 1, crescendo into the ff at 91), 205–206 and 209–210 (runs into the next climax) and 265–268 (ff). Splitting any of them between the sections would thin a crescendo or a tutti. The places where an antiphonal split would help (27–34, 199–204, 247–254) are protected quotations. Decision: no change.
- **Mutes and high-cut colour** (PDF 286–287). Vln I rest 51–66 and Vln I·II rest 171–186, long enough to fit mutes. In both cases the next entry (67, 187) is the cantabile F2, which c301 already veils with sul A at 189–192. Muted violins there would remove the warmth these statements are for. Decision: no change.
- **The low flute** (PDF 275, 277: weak fundamental, more air, lower perceived pitch). The movement never writes the flutes below A4. The only soft, remote passages where it would fit (95–98, 167–170, 171–186) already have their own filter: Ob 1 to Cl 1, stopped horn with sul tasto, and muted trumpet. A further colour would be added for its own sake. Decision: no change.
- **A string-free passage** (PDF 257, 280, 286–287). Strings are absent only in bars 102 and 106. The passages a master might give to winds and brass alone, as Brahms 1/iv gives its first chorale, are the arrival chorale and plagal close 223–234, which are protected. Decision: no change.

### 4. 153.3 and 161.3: bass trombone F2 under Tbn 2 D3 (checked, no change)

When the basses rest on beat 3, the bass trombone's F2 becomes the bass, with D3 a major sixth above. This is the exposition's passing V4/2 (13, 21) an octave lower, at about 87 and 147 Hz. That is above the register where a sixth muddies the chord, so I left it.
