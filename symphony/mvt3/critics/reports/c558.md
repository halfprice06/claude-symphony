## Findings

### 1. Bar 82: under "animando" the bass holds two half notes into 83's walking quarters (applied)

**Problem.** "Animando" stands at 82 (c298). The MIDI steps to quarter = 152, then 160 on 82.3. The violins, Fl 1 and Ob 1 hold E♭ as a half note and then run in eighths on 82.3–4 (c113).

The bass of the bar is two half notes, C♭–B♭, in the cellos and basses, and in Bn 2 and the violas as well. On 83 the cellos and basses start the closing group with walking quarters, E♭–G–B♭–G, f. So the first half of the animando bar has no attack after the downbeat. Nothing low marks the quickening beat, and the bass reaches the new phrase's quarter-note motion with no preparation.

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 208, Ex. 273: "In the third and fourth bars the cellos change from half note to quarter note values in order to give extra push into the next phrase". Ex. 274 shows the same passage without that motion, and the book calls it "much less interesting to the ear".
- PDF 207: "projecting enough character to carry one bar into the next with as much flow as possible".
- The score already uses this device in the same situation: under the stringendo at 245–246 the cellos and basses restrike their D in tenuto halves and then quarters (c358, citing this same Ex. 273 and A&S PDF 49). Bar 82 is the only other place where the score marks an acceleration, and there the bass still holds.
