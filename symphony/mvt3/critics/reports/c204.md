## Findings

### 1. 277-278: the harp is silent for the last 36 bars; give it one glissando into the final chord (applied)

Problem. The harp last plays at 243 (étouffez) and sits through the whole C-major close. At 277, the V-I restatement before the final chord, every part stops on beat 4 except the basses' eighths. That quarter rest is the only gap in the close, and nothing fills it. The final chord at 278 is made of the full orchestra only. Rimsky's two final chords that close a whole work in triumph (chords 17 and 18, Snegourotchka and Sadko, PDF 507) both run the harp up into the chord, one by glissando and one by arpeggio.

Evidence. Appendix chords 17 and 18 (PDF 507). Ch. IV, PDF 134: harp glissandi "are used from the fact that long glissandi are more resonant and brilliant than short ones", even when their notes do not match the harmony. PDF 136: a group "which has been silent for some time gains fresh interest upon its reappearance". Ex. 209 (PDF 375) also gives the harp one f chord on the climax's held chord after a long rest.

Cycle 114 left the harp out of 278 for three reasons, and the fix answers each one:
- PDF 51 says sustained groups cover the harp. That is true of the chord, and that is why the glissando sits on 277 beat 4, where only the basses sound in the low octave. The harp arrives with the orchestra and is then covered, as intended.
- PDF 119 leaves the harp out of the tutti. That sentence only says that harps and percussion "do not come under discussion" when Rimsky classifies tutti; his own appendix puts them into final chords.
- The models end without a harp. Brahms 1, Tchaikovsky 5 and Dvořák 9 have no harp in their orchestras at all, so they say nothing about how to end with one. This orchestra has a harp and leaves it idle.

No protected material changes: the chain, the quotation at 275-276, the held top C, the pitches of 278 and the fff are all as they were. G6 stays below the C7 reserved for 223. The pedals are still all naturals from the 235 diagram, so a C-major glissando needs no pedal change. A diatonic run of 19 strings (C4-G6) in one quarter at Largamente (quarter = 144, about 0.4 s) is easy.

### Checked, no change (decided)

(a) Wind trills (row 8). The score has none. Rimsky puts them on top of a held fff chord (chord 2) or inside one (chord 8). Only five held loud wind chords sit outside 223-279: 1/5 (protected), 110 (protected), 147-150 (protected strokes) and 209-210. At 209-210, Fl 1's G♭6 is the seventh whose suspension into 211 is the point (c151). A trill would blur that G♭6 and step onto the F that resolves it. 198 is the cantabile end of F2 under poco rit., and a trill would turn it into a fanfare. Decision: no trill.

(b) 91-94, tuba in unison with the bass trombone (row 2). This is the only exception in the movement to the appendix's Tbn III + tuba octave. I keep c194's decision. The alternatives are B♭0 in the tuba, a rising fourth in place of the bass's falling fifth, or the bass trombone doubling Tbn 2's E♭3. The strokes are short, and PDF 114 says resonance matters least in short detached chords.

(c) String-free passages (rows 17, 24). The strings rest only at 102 and 106, and the cellos alone play at 7-8. Ex. 210 and PDF 121 and 136 would support a wind tutti somewhere. Every place where one would make sense is protected or carries a thread: 43-50 (viola descent 46-49), 95-98 (the violas' E♭-E inflection, c203), 51-66 (pizz. under the solo) and 223-234. Decision: no change.

(d) Percussion with the rhythmic tuttis (row 13). Ex. 204 and PDF 135 would add cymbals and bass drum to 199 and 247. The movement's plan keeps the crash for 223, 263 and 278, which is Rimsky's own economy rule (PDF 136). Decision: no change.
