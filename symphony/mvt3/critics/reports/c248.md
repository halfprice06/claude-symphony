## Findings

### 1. Bar 93: the codetta has no metric conflict before its last tonic. Displace I and V by one quarter (applied)

**Bars:** 91–94 (closing group, E♭ major). Every part plays the same pattern: `I4-> r8 I8 V4-> r4` at 91 and at 92, then `I4-> r4 V4-> r4` at 93 and `I2-> r2` at 94.

**Problem:** These four ff bars close the exposition and set it apart from the oboe solo at 95. Every accent falls where the metre already puts its weight: bar 93 repeats the half-bar strokes of 91–92 without the eighth, so the strokes stay on the beats. The reading's main device for loud closing music is absent from the whole movement: a rhythmic accent against the metre that is then resolved (rows 7–8).

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 51: the most important syncopations are notes that begin on a weak beat and are held through the next strong beat. These conflict "sometimes very strongly" with the metre, and Beethoven marks them sf (Ex. 3-6, Op. 69: tied notes from beat 3 to beat 1). PDF 51–52: counter-accents that keep to their own pattern are heard as a short change of metre (Ex. 3-7). In Ex. 3-8 Mozart moves the accent away just before the cadence and returns it at bar 10. PDF 53–54 (Ex. 3-13): the goal is the arrival at the tonic on the last bar of the group. The Eroica passages cited above use the same device in an exposition tutti. If the I chord starts on beat 2 and is held through the half-bar beat, the metre is displaced for one bar. V then sounds as an upbeat on beat 4, and the tonic on 94.1 puts the metre back. That makes the cadence stronger without new pitches.

**Fix (applied in sections/clos.ily, 26 parts and the timpani):** bar 93 only.
```
% before (e.g. Vln I, Vc, Fl 1, Hn 1, timpani)
es'''4->\downbow r4 d'''4->\downbow r4 |      es4->\downbow r4 bes,4->\downbow r4 |
es'''4-> r4 d'''4-> r4 |   g'4-> r4 aes'4-> r4 |   es4-> r4 r2 |
% after
r4 es'''2->\downbow d'''4->\upbow |           r4 es2->\downbow bes,4->\upbow |
r4 es'''2-> d'''4-> |      r4 g'2-> aes'4-> |      r4 es2-> r4 |
```
In the strings, the V on beat 4 is up-bow, so 94 starts down-bow. This changes c219's down-bow on every accented quarter for this one bar only. The mark is ">" and not sf, because cycle 8 limits the strong marks to 101, 105, 138, 215, 219, 255 and 261. Pitches, dynamics and bars 91, 92 and 94 are unchanged, and so is the break-off 94→95 (kept by c228 and c243). c231 kept the codetta's material; this changes only its placement in the bar. Analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 90–95 now reports cellos with basses at 93.4–94.1, but that is the cellos doubling the bass an octave above, the same kind of entry it already reports at 83 in v248. The page layout is unchanged (page 5 checked as an image).

### 2. Bars 80–81: the diminuendo begins on the suspension's preparation, so the dissonance is softer than the note that prepares it (applied)

**Bars:** 80–81, Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1 (E♭-major F2, the phrase 75–82). c241 tied G from 80 beat 4 into 81, where it resolves to F on beat 2 over V7. The hairpin `\>` stayed where it had been before the tie, on 80 beat 4, and runs to mf (Fl 1: mp) at 82.

**Problem:** The G on 81.1 is now a suspension. It is the most expressive dissonance in the cadence of the phrase, and it falls on the strong beat of 81, the strong seventh bar of 75–82. Because the diminuendo starts on the weak preparation, the dissonance arrives already falling and is weaker than its own preparation. That reverses the accent of a suspension.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 55 (Ex. 3-16 to 3-18): a suspension is a dissonance displaced onto the accented beat, and it is the one type of dissonance that "almost always appears in metrically accented positions". PDF 54–55 (Ex. 3-15): an accented dissonance carries the stress and its resolution is lighter. PDF 53: in the hypermeasures 75–78 and 79–82, 81 is the strong third bar of its group. c048 and c148 already apply this rule to F2's peak at 62/182 and to the pad at 77–81. The pad (Hn 1·2, Bn) still reaches p at 81, so the suspension sounds over a softer background.

**Fix (applied in sections/sTwo.ily):**
```
Vln I:  c'''4( bes''4 aes''4 g''4~) | g''4( f''4\> d''2) | es''2\mf\< ...
Vln II: c''4( bes'4 aes'4 g'4~) | g'4( f'4\> \once ... d'2\startTextSpan) | es'2\stopTextSpan\mf\< ...
Fl 1:   c'''4 bes''4 aes''4 g''4~ | g''4 f''4\> d''2 | es''2)\mp\< ...
```
The hairpin now starts on the resolution F (81.2). The end dynamics at 82, the "animando", the sul G and the pad are unchanged. The flutes' shared staff carries only Fl 1 here, so the rule of equal dynamics on a shared staff is not affected. This finishes c241's change and does not undo it. Page 4 was checked as an image.
