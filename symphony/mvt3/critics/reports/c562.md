## Findings

### 1. Bar 78 beats 3–4: replace the dominant sonority over the vi bass with IV6 by 5–6 (applied)

**Bars:** 78 (exposition, F2 in E♭ major at its f peak).

**Problem:** F2's climax bar has the bass on C for the whole bar. On beats 1–2 the chord is vi7 (C–E♭–G–B♭, with the tune's B♭6 as the 7th). On beats 3–4 the harp plays F4–B♭4–D5–F5, Hn 2 B♭3, the violas G3 and Bn 1 F3: a B♭ triad with G over C, which sounds as a dominant (with the leading tone D5) between vi and the IV at 79. That puts V before IV, a retrogression; the reading's syntax rule is that intermediate harmonies on the way to V move down by 3rds and 5ths (PDF 172). It also undercuts the vi → IV → V/V → V plan of 78–80 (C–A♭–A–B♭). Two smaller faults follow from it. The harp's D5 leading tone goes to E♭5 over an A♭ chord, not over a tonic. The harp's F5 at 78.4.5 also makes parallel octaves with Vln I (F6→E♭6) into 79.1; the all-pairs script reports four P8 at 79.1 in v562.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 168: VI and IV6 relate as IV and II6 do, and VI becomes IV6 by the 5–6 technique (the 5th of VI moves up a step). PDF 170, Ex. 11-12a: IV6 expands IV. PDF 183–185, 191, Ex. 12-18 and 12-21: the most important use of IV7 is subdominant harmony with 3 in the soprano, often as an accented 8–7 passing tone over IV. That fits the tune's G6 on 78.3 exactly: B♭6–A♭6–G6–F6–E♭6 over C, then A♭. The coda's version of this phrase (257–258) keeps vi under the same descending line and goes to a subdominant at 259, so 78 is the only statement that puts a dominant over the 6 bass.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-562/work/sections/sTwo.ily):**
- Hn 2, bar 78: `g2\mp bes2` → `g2\mp aes2`. G3 → A♭3 is the 5–6 that turns vi into IV6, and the horn keeps a concord as c147 asked.
- Violas, bar 78: `es'2( g2)` → `es'1`. E♭4 is the common tone of vi and IV6, held into C4 at 79. c281 set G3 to avoid fifths; the held E♭ makes none (checked).
- Harp RH, bar 78, second group: `f'8 bes'8 d''8 f''8` → `es'8 aes'8 c''8 es''8`. This is the arpeggio of 79; the A♭ pedal is already set.
- Bn 1 keeps its F3 on 78.3 (c147's discord in the bassoon), so beat 3 sounds C–F–A♭–E♭ with the tune's G as the passing 7th over A♭. That is the IV7-with-3-in-the-soprano colour of PDF 183.

The result is vi7 – IV6 (5–6) – IV – V6/5 of V – V across 78–80, all descending or stepwise as the reading orders it. F2's notes, the cello, clarinet and horn doubling of the tune, the bass, the dynamics and the slurs of every other part are unchanged. Bar 78 is in neither of the theme records (F2's pitches are untouched), so no other movement thread is affected.

On protection: c166 listed "horn/bassoon pad 75–82" for playability. The change is one step in Hn 2 with the same rhythm and breath, so that reason is unaffected. The pad has also been rescored since (c341, c398). c22's "bass C at 78" is kept. This change does not undo any accepted change; it replaces a sonority that no accepted entry argued for.

**Build:** 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 16 pages. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 76–80 gives output identical to v562 (only the known bassoon doublings of the bass). The all-pairs check shows four fewer parallel octaves (harp/Vln I into 79.1) and none new.

### 2. Bar 71: the ii7's seventh in the cellos enters from E♮ (not applied)

At 70.3 the cellos' E4 (the 3rd of C7/G, V4/3 of ii) falls to E♭4, the 7th of ii7 at 71. The reading wants the 7th prepared or passing (PDF 177), and an applied leading tone should rise. Here, though, the line F4–E4–E♭4 (–C4–E♭4)–D4 is a chromatic passing descent, and the 7th resolves to D4 at 72.1. That counts as the reading's passing-tone origin, stretched chromatically. The line is also the cello counter-melody that c287 doubles in Hn 1 (the violas and horn have G4 there). Kept.

### 3. Bars 91–93: V7's seventh A♭6 leaps in the codetta strokes (not applied)

Vln I's A♭6 (7th of V7) leaps to F♯6 at 92.1 and to E♭6 at 93.2 instead of falling to G. These are back-relating I–V hammer strokes ending ii6/5–V7–I (c552, A&S Ex. 9-2c, 10-10). The 7th in the strokes is a colour that returns to the tonic octave, and its preparation and resolution happen in the oboes and Hn 1, which hold E♭. Rewriting the strokes would reverse c248/c252/c552. Kept.

### 5. Tested on paper and declined: vi held through 78 instead of IV6

Keeping vi (C–E♭–G) under 78.3–4 matches the coda's 258 literally, but it repeats one chord for a whole bar at the peak, and IV then arrives at 79 without preparation. The 5–6 to IV6 is the reading's own connection (PDF 168) and gives the tune's G its expressive dissonance. Finding 1 is the better of the two.
