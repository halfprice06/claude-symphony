**Brahms 2/iv against this finale.** A conductor would accept the large plan. Both finales end a symphony with a long, rising major coda in which brass take over thematic material at the end. Brahms gets there with less: one second theme, one tranquillo, then the brass. This finale has more stages (arrival, chorale, movement I's second theme, theme combination, close), but the cyclic plan requires each one. Brahms's two main differences cannot be copied here. His sotto voce opening is ruled out by the loud start after the pause (BRIEF.md), and his second theme returns in the tonic at full brass, which this finale gives to the chorale (cycle 69 rejected an F2 apotheosis). In orchestral detail the score is now at Brahms's level of care. Every melody is doubled at the unison or octave by instruments in their natural order, and soft melodies keep single colours. That is the thing Rimsky's examples 1-60 teach, and the scans above found it observed almost everywhere. The one place where a recent change put a woodwind pad above a melody is finding 1.

## Findings

### 1. 27-30: the oboe pad sits above the flutes' countersubject (applied; regression since v299)

**Bars:** 27.3-30.2, Ob 1.

**Problem.** Since c306, Fl 1·2 a2 double the violins' countersubject at pitch, f, over F1 in the bass. Ob 1 kept its mf pad: E♭5, then D5 held from 27.3 to 28.2 and again through all of 29 into 30. The countersubject falls to its low points under that D5: B4 at 27.4, B♭4 at 28.1, B4 at 29.1, and B4-C5 at 30.1 (Fl 2). Four times in four bars the woodwind order is turned upside down. The oboe holds a note above the flutes, and the flutes carry the line in their weakest octave, where it passes through the oboe's held pitch. In v299 the flutes held a pad above the oboes, so the order was right. The scan of each family's top note finds Fl under Ob nowhere else in the movement.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 54: a melody "stands out from the very fact of position alone", and a part crossing above it takes away that relief.
- PDF 65: placing "flutes below oboes and clarinets ... creates a far-fetched, unnatural tone". PDF 67: crossing registers "creates an unnatural resonance occasioned by the confusion of registers".
- Ex. 3 (PDF 177) and Ex. 8 (PDF 182): where woodwind double a violin line, the held woodwind and brass chords lie below the doubled line, never inside it.
- The pad does not need Ob 1 there. Ob 2 (G4, B♭4-A♭4, G4) and Hn 1·2 (G4, F4 / C4, B3) hold the same harmony below the line.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-309/work/sections/pTwo.ily, comment "c309").** Ob 1 keeps its first half note, rests 27.3-30.2, and re-enters mf on E♭5 at 30.3, where the line has risen above it again (Fl 2 E♭5-G5-C6-E♭6). It also gets a breath in a stretch that otherwise runs twelve bars without a rest.

```
% was:
pTwoOboeOne = { es''2\mf d''2~ | d''2 f''2 | d''1~ | d''2 es''2~ | es''1 | ...
% now:
pTwoOboeOne = { es''2\mf r2 | R1*2 | r2 es''2~\mf | es''1 | ...
```

Bars 31-38 are unchanged. F1, the countersubject and c306's flute doubling are unchanged. Only the pad's top note is removed while the line is below it. On the shared staff Ob 2 has the same mf, so the part-combined dynamics stay equal.

**Checks.** The scratch build has 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt is empty) and 16 pages. I looked at page 2 as an image: the Ob 1·2 staff reads cleanly, with "2." at 28. analysis.txt has 0 outer-voice parallels; it differs from v309 only in the oboe note counts. par8.py on 26-31 lists only F1's intended octaves in the bass instruments, the same as in v309. The woodwind-order scan no longer finds Fl under Ob anywhere. Theme record 39/39.

### Considered and not changed (decided)

- **Muted strings (Ex. 2, 6, 13, 16, 40, 43, 58, 66).** This is the one technique of the reading that the finale never uses. Rimsky's Ex. 40 (PDF 218), a woodwind solo over muted sustained strings, matches 43-50 and 115-122. At 43 there is no time to put on mutes: the strings play until 42.3. At 51 the pizzicato continues straight into arco at 67. 115-130 is protected. At 171 the violas have no rest before or after. A mute that cannot be put on or taken off in time is worse than none. Not applied.
- **Bass clarinet solo (Ex. 47-48, PDF 222-223).** The bass clarinet doubles the bass throughout. No unprotected passage calls for a low solo voice, and the movement's solos (trumpet 51, bassoon 115, cor anglais 171) are part of the cyclic plan. Not applied.
- **Brahms's second theme in violins and violas in unison (Ex. 14, PDF 189-190).** F2's string statement 67-82 is in Vln I/II octaves with the Vla + Vc counter-melody (balance protected since c094/c098, peak since c281). The recapitulated A♭ statement 187-198 is protected. Not applied.
- **27-29 flutes an octave higher instead.** This would put the countersubject's peaks at E♭7-F7. Rejected in favour of finding 1.
- **87-88, Fl 1 doubles Vln I while Vln II has no wind until Ob 1 joins at 89.** This is a graded doubling into the ff at 91, the plan Rimsky describes on PDF 61 (h): a melody first not doubled, then doubled in the woodwind as it grows. Kept.
- **Same-pair octaves at 32, 38, 57, 77-85.** These last one beat each and are pad notes, which PDF 68 allows for "sustained passages". Kept.
