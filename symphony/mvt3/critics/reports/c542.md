## Findings

### 1. Bar 29: the V6/5 doubles its leading tone and has no 5th (applied)

**Bars:** 29, beats 3–4 (P2, the F1 bass statement with the countersubject, ff; horn pad mf).

**Problem:** On 29.3 the bass statement (violas, cellos, bassoons, Hn 3·4, bass clarinet, basses, contrabassoon, tuba) reaches B, which makes a V6/5 of C minor. Hn 2 has held B3 since 29.1 in the Hn 1·2 / Ob 2 pad, so the chord on 29.3 is B1–B2–B3 (bass group) + B3 (Hn 2) – F4 (Hn 1) – G4 (Ob 2) – G5. The leading tone is doubled in an independent sustaining part, and no part has D. My scan found no other place in the movement where a pad holds the leading tone over a leading-tone bass. The same kind of fault was fixed at 141–144 (c362) and 154/162 (c364), and the same incomplete-V6/5 fault at 176 (c533).

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 76: the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V or VII or their inversions"; Ex. 5-4 marks such a chord "wrong". PDF 76: "Inversions of seventh chords are usually complete, with exceptions occurring even less often than with inversions of triads." Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 83 states the same rule for orchestral writing. With the change, Hn 1·2 also form a minor third (F4/D4) on that beat instead of the tritone F4/B3 (Rimsky PDF 90).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-542/work/sections/pTwo.ily):**
```
pTwoHnTwo = { c'2\mf b2 | bes2 c'2 | b2 d'4 b4~ | b2 c'2~ | ...   % was: b1~ | b2 c'2~
```
Hn 2 takes D4 on 29.3 only, which completes the V6/5 (B–D–F–G), and returns to B3 on 29.4. This is a voice exchange with the theme's B3–D4. B3 is then tied into 30 as before, so c222's VII°4/3 on 30.1–2 keeps its held B, and Hn 2 resolves B3–C4 on 30.3 as before. Result: 29.3 = B1 B2 B3 D4 F4 G4 G5; 29.4 = D2 D3 B3 D4 F4 G4 E♭5 (V4/3, B only in Hn 2).

I tested two other versions in the build and rejected both:
- (a) `b2 d'2 | b2 c'2~`, returning to B3 on 30.1. allpairs_par.py found new parallel octaves between Hn 2 (D4→B3) and the violins' and flutes' countersubject (D5→B4) at 29.4.5→30.1. This is the fault Ex. 5-18 forbids.
- (b) `b2 d'2~ | d'2 c'2~`. This removes the held B from c222's VII°4/3 on 30.1–2 and would partly undo that accepted change.

### 3. Spacing gaps (checked, no change)

### 4. Bar 133.4: V4/2 without B for one beat (checked, no change)

The 133–134 V4/2 of C (bass F) loses B on 133.4: Hn 3's B3 ends and Vln II's B4 enters on 134.1. A&S PDF 76 says inversions of sevenths are usually complete, but the gap is one beat inside a two-bar diminuendo, and the chord was accepted by c183 and confirmed by c453. Filling it would mean Hn 3 holding into 134 under Vln II's new B4, which doubles the leading tone again (finding 1's fault). No change.
