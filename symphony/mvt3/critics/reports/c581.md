## Findings

### 1. 217–218: Ob 1's held D♭5 covers the cor anglais and horn echo of theme B (applied)

- **Problem.** At 217–218, the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 play the "espress." echo of movement II's theme B at f (A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4). Ob 1 holds D♭5 above them for both bars at f. This is the same pitch and the same distance (a fourth above the echo's top note) for which c440 took Tpt 1 out of these bars. The oboe has a cutting middle register, and it is the cor anglais's own family, so its sustained D♭5 is the strongest double-reed sound in the bar and sits on top of the cor anglais line.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 201: the oboe is "a most poignant doubler because of its distinctive nasal quality ... great care must be given to have it double a significant line so that an instrument that needs to be featured more prominently is not overshadowed." PDF 205: the cor anglais's low register has "tremendous expressive carrying power", but only if it is not masked. PDF 186: composers "let the flute come through by doing little with the other instruments". The echo is the last time a theme of movement II is heard in the symphony, so it is the line that has to be heard here.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-581/work/sections/codaTwo.ily).** Ob 1 ends its tie with the 216 diminuendo and rests 217–218, as Ob 2 already does. It re-enters on its old C5 at the 219 sub. p.

  The D♭ still sounds in Fl 1 (D♭6, the top of the protected D♭–C–B–C line), Cl 1 (D♭5), Vln I·II and the violas, so the chord and the top line do not change. ### 3. F1, F2, chorale, S head, final combination (checked, no change)

- **F1 (11–26, 151–166).** The flutes play the head and the peak A♭6 at f/ff with the violins. This is the flute's "clear and brilliant" register (Ex. VII-2), so it is not shrill at that level.
- **F2.** The trumpet solo stays undoubled. Fl 1 doubles it at the unison only at 61–63 (PDF 185–186). The violins' major statement adds Fl 1 an octave up only from 77, approached by step.
- **Recapitulation (171–186).** The cor anglais carries F2 in its "mellow, reedy, sonorous" middle register (Ex. VII-47).
- **S head (235–242).** Cl 1 doubles the violas' chromatic counter in the clarino register. This follows Ex. VII-63, clarinets as contrapuntal partners.
- **Final combination.** Each wind stays in its strong register. The chorale's held flute notes at 253–260 use staggered breaths, which is Brahms's practice in Ex. VII-9.
