## Findings

**1. The large tonal plan of exposition and development is the plan of Ex. 26-8. No change.**
Bars 11–151. A&S PDF 449 gives Mozart's K. 457 plan as I – III – passing IV – V, with V appearing first as a minor chord. The minor V "does not lead forcefully to I", so the leading tone must enter prominently in the bass before the recapitulation. This finale does each step: i (11), III (E♭ minor/major, 51–98), V7 of iv as the exposition's last chord (97–98, violas E♮4 over C), iv (99–113), v (G minor, 115–122), V (G major, 123–132), V4/2 of C (133–134), and the leading tone B held in the bass for nine bars (138–146) under the stop chord before V7 at 147. Mozart opens his development with the first idea on V of IV. Here the V of IV ends the exposition and F1's head opens the development on iv itself (99), which keeps C major unheard until 223, as the cyclic rule requires. Point 7 (PDF 457) calls a passing, often tonicized, IV between III and V "the most frequent tonal plan in minor". The 15-bar F-minor stretch, confirmed by a German sixth and V7 at 109–113 and left after one bar of V7 of G at 114, is exactly that. c283 tested a pivot at 113–114 and rejected it, and I agree. Dvořák 9/iv (annotations above) goes III in the exposition and G – F – G minor in the development. That plan is more remote than this one and no clearer. Decision: keep.

**2. The plan from the recapitulation to the arrival is K. 488's VI → IV → augmented sixth → V → I (Ex. 26-14), with ♭II in place of IV. No change.**
Bars 171–223. PDF 452–453: tonicized VI "leads down in 3rds from I to IV", and in K. 488 the tonicized IV goes to V "through an augmented 6th chord". This score has i (171), VI (A♭ major, 187–198), a return to i for movement I's hammered tutti (199–204), VI again as a pedal (205–210), ♭II (D♭, 211–218), and then the same A♭7 respelled as the German sixth of C (219), V (220–222) and I (223). The Neapolitan does the job of the IV, and the German sixth is the K. 488 link. The C-minor interruption at 199–204 is a contrapuntal neighbour inside the VI prolongation (PDF 456, sec. 16: a return without a dominant), and it is protected as the quotation of movement I 27–32. Dvořák's recapitulation puts its second theme in the tonic major (227). This finale cannot do that, because C major is saved for 223 (c073). Its recapitulation is therefore shorter (17% of the bars against Dvořák's 22%) and its coda longer (29% against 21%). c313 and c343 accepted this proportion for the same reason, and the reading supports it: the large plan is still an expansion of I–V–I (PDF 449: "even modulations to areas other than V … normally function within a larger framework of tonic-dominant"). Decision: keep.

**3. Arrivals and confirmations follow the reading at every key change. No change.**
Bars as in rows 2 and 5 of the table. PDF 443 asks for a confirming cadence with II or IV, and PDF 445 asks for a line that lands on 1̂ or 3̂ "with a sense of rhythmic resolution". I checked every key change bar by bar. Five areas are confirmed with a predominant: E♭ minor at 63–66, E♭ major at 79–82 and 89–90, F minor at 109–113, G major at 129–132 (c372) and A♭ at 193–198. G minor (115–122) and D♭ (211–218) are transient and have only half cadences or none, which is what PDF 447–448 describes for passing modulations. Each new tonic is reached by a resolving line (row 5). The one root-position V–I in C (262→263) is the cadence that confirms the whole movement. Decision: keep.

**4. Unused technique: a tonicized IV in the C-major coda (PDF 451, 457 pt 9). Tested in the score text and not applied.**
Bars 223–279. From the arrival to the end (57 bars, about 2 minutes 10 seconds), IV appears at 227–234 (plagal close), 240, 249, 252, 272 and 274 (iv6/4), but never with its own V7 (C–E–G–B♭). The reading says a tonicized IV "creates no problems of tonal balance if it occurs later in the piece", and such a IV would give the long tonic area a departure. I checked every unprotected place:
- 250→251 and 251.3–4 (V7 of IV before 252's IV) would put a B♭ against the P head's B5 on 251.4.5 (Vln I, Fl 1) and against the S head's held E.
- 248.3–4 lies under A5 in both heads, and c363 protected the applied chord at 249–250.
- 271 has B♮ in F1's third bar (Tpt 1·2, D–G–A–B–D).
- 265–272 is over a tonic pedal, where a V7 of IV is only a coloured I. F1's major head already supplies that colour at 266 (B♭4 in Tpt 1·2, Cl 2, violas, cellos).

Every other bar of 223–264 is protected (chorale, 223–234, the 235–247 plan, 255–263). Decision: not applied. The IV's weight in this coda comes from the plagal close at 227–234 and the inner A–A♭–G of 273–276, and the cyclic plan places the departures (♭VI, the German sixth, E major at 243) there on purpose.

**5. Unused technique: a sequence of tonicized chords descending by thirds (Ex. 26-6, PDF 447; pt 6, 457). Not applied.**
Bars 235–242. The coda's second-theme stage has the bass C (235–238) – A (239) – F (240) – D (241–242), which is I – vi – IV – ii7, the descending-third chain of Ex. 26-6b, but nothing in it is tonicized. Tonicizing vi at 239 needs G♯ on 238, which would anticipate the single E-major chord at 243 (c220, c320). Tonicizing IV at 240 needs V4/3 of IV on 239.3 (bass A–G–F), which would contradict the harp's A-minor arpeggio across 239 and change the protected 235–247 bass. The counter-line's B♮–B♭ on 239.3–4 (Cl 1, violas: movement I's chromatic counter) already gives the melodic tonicization of PDF 446 (row 6) without a chord. Decision: not applied.

**6. Unused technique: a tonicized VI as upper neighbour inside the development's dominant (Ex. 26-17, PDF 456). Not applied.**
Bars 115–150. The reading says developments often extend V through a tonicized VI (upper neighbour) and IV (lower neighbour), and that the returning V carries a 7th. This finale has IV before the V area (99–113, Finding 1) and the 7th (V4/2 at 133, V7 at 147). VI appears only melodically: Vln I A♭5→G5 at 135–136, and the stop chord's A♭3→G3 in Hn 4 at 138–139. A tonicized A♭ inside 115–150 would add at least four bars to a development that is already 52 bars (19%). It would also bring A♭ major between the G-major harp section and the retransition, which weakens the minor-V-to-major-V turn of row 21. Decision: not applied. The melodic A♭–G neighbours are the right scale for a 52-bar development.

**7. Coda stages: they build in tonal terms. No change.**
Bars 199–279. The reading's point (PDF 448–449) is that key areas are placed purposefully over long spans. Before 223 the coda moves VI → ♭II → Ger6 → V. That is the strongest pre-dominant chain in the movement, and it carries the tension up to the arrival. After 223 every stage is C major, but each has its own harmonic aim: plagal close (234), III♯ → V → I (243–247), I–ii–IV–V twice (247–254), the descending-fifth chain into the only V–I (255–263), and the pedal close with ♭6 inner lines (273–276). Over 80 bars this is tonic prolongation with rising cadential weight, not a sag. Decision: keep.
