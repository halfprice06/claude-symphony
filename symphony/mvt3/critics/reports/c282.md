In the local corpus I compared Dvořák 9/iv, whose main theme's first half-cadences are bare dominants. Brahms 1/iv's Allegro theme (bars 61-77) uses the plain half cadence the first time and a 6/4-coloured dominant later. So a 6/4 at the antecedent's close is idiomatic but not obligatory, and the case for Finding 1 rests on A&S PDF 158-159 and on the link with bar 151.

## Findings

**1. 18 and 158: F1's antecedent half cadence gets a cadential 6/4 (applied).**
Problem: both statements of F1's period (11-26 and 151-166) end the antecedent on a bare root V (18, 158), reached from iv-iiø7 at 17/157. A&S calls the cadential 6/4 "an intensification of V" (PDF 152) and notes that "cadential 6/4s often occur prominently in antecedent-consequent groups; by intensifying the dominant chords, they give emphasis to the cadences" (PDF 158-159; point 9, PDF 160; semicadence forms, Ex. 10-7, PDF 155). The tune holds G (5̂) at 18 and 158, which is the octave of the bass, so the 6/4 does not clash with the melody. This is not true of F2's half cadences at 74 and 122, where the tune holds 2̂. The recapitulation already opens on the same 6/4 over G (151-153, with Tbn 1 C4→B3). The 6/4 at 158 therefore closes the antecedent the way it began. There was also a voice-leading fault: at 17.4→18.1 and 157.4→158.1, Cl 2 moved D4→G3 in octaves with the bass D2→G1 (par8.py), and the change removes it.
Evidence: the 4th enters as a common tone, held from the previous chord (PDF 153, Ex. 10-3a), and falls by step on the next, weaker beat (PDF 154: "if the chords change every beat or so, the 6/4 will appear on a strong beat and will resolve on the following weak beat"). The figuration may interrupt the step (PDF 153, Ex. 10-3c). The bass is doubled and the 4th is not doubled in another octave (PDF 154).
Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-282/work):
- pOne.ily, 18: Cl 1 `es'8( g' d' g') r2` (was `d'8( g' b' g') r2`), so F4 (17.4) goes to E♭4 (the 6th) and then to D4 on beat 2. Cl 2 `c'8( es' b d') r2` (was `g8( b d' b) r2`), so C4, held from 17's last eighth, is the 4th and resolves to B3 on beat 2 together with Hn 3's B3.
- recP.ily, 158: the same two clarinet bars. Tbn 1 `c'1~ | c'4 b4 r2` (was `c'1 | b2 r2`), a tied 4-3 suspension at ff. Tbn 2 `es4 d4 r2` (was `d2 r2`), 6-5. Without the trombone change, their held B3/D3 would sound against the clarinets' C4/E♭4.
The tune, bass, rhythm, slurs, dynamics and scoring are unchanged. This touches critics' do-not-touch lists "11-18" (c202) and "151-166 scoring and tune" (c192). Their reasons were the F1 tune, its bowing and its scoring, and none of these change. No theme or quotation changes, so the other movement threads do not need to be told. The cadence plan is unaffected because this is a half cadence.

**2. 219-222: the cadential 6/4 at 220 lasts a whole bar and falls on a weak bar (not applied; kept).**
In the four-bar group 219-222 before the downbeat at 223, the 6/4 (220) falls on the weak bar and its resolution, V7 (221), falls on the strong bar. The bass G is held across the change. This is the pattern A&S marks "never!" (Ex. 10-6a, PDF 155: "Cadential 6/4 chords that last a whole bar fall, in principle, on a strong or accented bar"). Moving it would mean holding the German sixth through 220 and putting the 6/4 at 221. That would move the violas' E♮ (the E♭/E♮ thread at 219-220) and remove 221's V7 before the stop chord, both cyclic protections. I decided to keep it. The dominant lasts three bars (220-222, ending on the stop chord over D), so the weight of the passage falls on 223 and the weak-bar 6/4 is heard as the start of a prolonged dominant (PDF 156). This is recorded so later critics do not raise it again without that answer.

**3. 74 and 194: F2's joint V→V4/2 without the 6/4 (not applied).**
The joint between F2's phrases is the procedure of Ex. 10-12/10-13 (PDF 157-158): a dominant that goes to V4/2 instead of cadencing. A&S's form has a 6/4 before the 4/2. A 6/4 on 74.1 would put its 6th, G, against the violins' F, which is anticipated from 73.4 (c261), so 6 and 5 would sound together. I decided not to apply it: c261's anticipation matters more here than the textbook form.

**4. 243.3-244.2: the 4th of the cadential 6/4 enters by leap and is doubled (no change).**
The horn call E-C-B→C brings the 4th in by leap (PDF 153 asks for a common tone or a step). It is doubled in three octaves, and the violas leave it by leap (C4→G3 at 244.1). These are the exceptions A&S allows for free textures (PDF 154) and for a quoted line. The passage (235-247) and the quotation are protected, so there is no change.

**5. 151: a 6/4 after V7 over the same bass (no change).**
Taken as a cadential 6/4, this would break points 3 and 7 (PDF 160). It is a pedal 6/4 at the start of the recapitulation, a 6/4 on the dominant outside a cadence (PDF 152). Finding 1 now repeats it at 158.
