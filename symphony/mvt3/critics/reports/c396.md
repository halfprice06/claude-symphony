## What else I studied

I compiled the eleven woodwind parts on their own (not part-combined) to a MIDI file. With small scripts kept outside the work folder I scanned them for ranges, low-register notes with their dynamics, notes above A6 and how they are approached, connected leaps of an octave or more, and repeated-note runs. I also ran c216's breathing scan, which reads `\breathe` marks. HISTORY.md shows that the earlier Adler ch. 6–7 passes (c36, c126, c216, c306) placed breaths in the last chorale 257–262 (c126/c127), in the bassoons and clarinets, and in the flutes at 27–34. They never reached the first chorale's woodwind canon or the low reeds before the arrival. Those are the two places where the reading's breathing rules still fail.

## Findings

### 1. 226–230: the woodwind canon has no breath in five tied bars. Applied.

**Bars:** 226–230 (Maestoso, 2 = 56). Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 play the chorale line in canon in unison: `g''1~ | g''2 e'''2~ | e'''2 d'''2~ | d'''2 c'''4 des'''4 | c'''1`.

**Problem:** The line is ff, with crescendo–diminuendo swells on every tie. It lasts 10.7 s and the first breath comes after 230. Fl 1 has just played the fff C7 at 223, with only two bars of rest before this. All three players are tied the whole way, so none of them can breathe without a gap in the line.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 188 (Ex. VII-9, Brahms 1/iv) describes this exact situation in the model movement for this finale. In long notes with crescendo–diminuendo, one flute holds while the other breathes, "dovetailed with great care to preserve the illusion of a single" instrument. PDF 192: several flutes "relieve one another in long … or sustained passages". PDF 198 and 175: the oboe "is most taxing to play" and needs frequent rest. c127 already uses this relief at 257–262 in the last chorale, but the first chorale's woodwinds lack it.

**Fix:** Each of the three players takes one eighth-note breath while the other two hold. The breaths fall at different points, so the line never stops and no pitch changes. Hairpins stay on the same beats as the partner on each shared staff.
- Fl 1, 227: `g''2 e'''2~\<` becomes `g''4. r8 e'''2~\<`. Ob 1 and Cl 1 hold the G.
- Cl 1, 228: `e'''2\> d'''2~\<` becomes `e'''4.\> r8 d'''2~\<`. Fl 1 and Ob 1 hold the E.
- Ob 1, 229: `d'''2\> c'''4\! des'''4` becomes `d'''4.\> r8 c'''4\! des'''4`. Fl 1 and Cl 1 hold the D.

The brass chorale top line (Tpt 1 224–228), 222→223 and every pitch are unchanged. This answers the 223–234 protection: no note, dynamic level or scoring changes, only breath placement within a three-fold unison.

### 2. 219–222: no breath for the low reeds in the crescendo to the arrival. Applied.

**Bars:** 219–225.
- Cbsn: `aes,,1\p | g,,1\< | g,,1\mp\< | d,,1\f\<` then `c,,1\fff | c,,1\f | c,1 \breathe`.
- Bn 2 plays the same line an octave higher.
- Bn 1: `c'1 | c'1 | b1 | b1`.

**Problem:** c216's breath scan finds 13.1 s without a breath for Cbsn, Bn 1 and Bn 2 (219–226). The line sits in the contrabassoon's lowest octave and rises from p to the fff C1 at 223. No breath is possible at 222→223.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 230: in the contrabassoon's lower range "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable … provide quite a few periodic rests". PDF 226: the bassoon's lowest notes need a firmer attack, which also costs air.

**Fix:** Score-only breath marks, placed where the note is re-attacked anyway and staggered so the bass is never empty:
- Bn 2 after 220 (`g,1\< \breathe | g,1`, repeated G);
- Bn 1 after 221 (`b1\mp\< \breathe | b1`, repeated B);
- Cbsn after 221 (`g,,1\mp\< \breathe | d,,1`).

The contrabassoon now plays 222–225 on one breath, about 8 s instead of 13 s. The basses and cellos hold the bass line throughout. Neither 222→223 nor the 205–223 dynamic path changes, and the MIDI is unchanged.

### Checked, no change

- 78: Fl 1 slurs F6→B♭6 at mf. Adler PDF 186 says to approach notes above A6 by scale. The violins carry the same climax, and c311 kept the leap as part of the protected F2 climax. A rising fourth slurred at mf is routine for a professional flutist, so I keep it.
- 27–30: the flutes' B4–B♭4 sits in their weak octave under ff strings (PDF 185). It is the unison doubling that c306 chose, and the line climbs out of that octave at once. No change.
- Soft tonguing (PDF 177) and an oboe pedal in octaves with the horns (PDF 200–201) have no unprotected place where they would add something. The score has no wind repeated-note figure to apply soft tonguing to, and I did not invent one.
