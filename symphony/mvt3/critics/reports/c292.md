## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 128-132: two root-position V7-I cadences in G within four bars. APPLIED

Problem. In v292, bar 128 is V7 of G (D7, its 7th C4 in the violas and Bn 1), and bar 129 is a root-position G-major triad on whole-note G in the cellos (G3) and basses (G2). The harp LH starts the bar on G2 and Hn 4 holds B3. Bars 131-132 then cadence V7-I again in G. That cadence is the end of the development's G-major area and the arrival the movement plan names. The cadence at 128-129 has the same bass, the same chord and the same dynamic level, so the later one sounds like a repeat of it rather than the goal.

Evidence from the reading. A&S PDF 201-202 (Unit 13, section 5, Ex. 13-11a/b, the Waldstein Sonata) say that after V7 a chord other than the root-position tonic is used "to prevent a cadential effect too early in the passage". The example keeps the harmonic motion going until the real cadence. PDF 203-204 make the same point for the deceptive cadence: it creates a need for a varied repeat that ends with a full cadence. The full cadence must not be given away earlier.

What I chose and why. The book's first option is VI. At 129 that fails (finding 2). The next option, which the book also lists, is a tonic in inversion, so that the bass does not move by 5th. I wrote 129 as I6 to I6/4. The bass moves B (129.1-2) to D (129.3), and the D then holds through 130 and 131 (I6/4, V, V7). This is the standard cadential approach of PDF 182 (Ex. 12-8). The root-position G now comes only at 132.

LilyPond (sections/dev.ily, bar 129 only):

% devCb   bar 129: g,1\<           ->  b,,2\< d,2

% devHnFour bar 129: b4\p\< b4 b4 b4  ->  g4\p\< g4 b4 b4

Voice leading of the result. The 7th C4 of bar 128 (violas, Bn 1) still falls to B3 at 129. Hn 4 changes from B3 to G3 on the first half, because a B3 there tripled the bass B. At 130, Hn 1's G4 to F#4 and Hn 4's B3 to A3 resolve the 6/4 while the tune's G5 rises to A5. This is the upward resolution of the 4th shown in Ex. 12-11 (PDF 184). Another voice takes the falling 4-3, so the 6/4 still resolves.

Tests. Two earlier versions failed, and I rejected both:
- I6 for the whole bar made parallel octaves between the violas and the bass at 130.1 (B3-D4 against B1-D2), shown by par8.
- Moving the violas B3 to G3 instead made octaves against the tune and against Hn 4, shown by allpairs.

The cyclic rule (no root-position V-I in C before 261-263) does not apply, because this passage is in G.

### 2. Bar 129 as VI (E minor), the deceptive option from PDF 201-204: NOT APPLIED

Problem. V7-vi is the reading's main way to avoid an early cadence (PDF 201-205), so I tested it first. The tune at 129-130 is G5 then A5-B5 across the bar line, over the bass. If the bass is D (128) then E (129), the tune's A5-B5 runs in parallel 5ths with D-E. A&S PDF 167 (Ex. 11-7) and PDF 202 (Ex. 13-12) name exactly this risk, and doubling the 3rd of VI does not fix the outer voices. Changing the tune is not allowed, because the 123-130 hand-over is protected. IV6 in place of VI (PDF 204-205) would put C under the tune's G and make the 128 7th C4 a doubled, unresolved note. Neither is better than finding 1.

Fix: none. Finding 1 is the version that works.

### 3. Bar 38: ii-ø7 to the minor cadential 6/4 to V. CHECKED, NO CHANGE

The 7th Eb is held in Cl 1 as the 6/4's 4th. The doubled 4th falls Eb5-D5 in the violins and flute, while Cl 1 rises to F4. This is Ex. 12-8 (PDF 182) together with the upward 4th of Ex. 12-11 (PDF 184), and the rise avoids octaves with the violins. c202 also left this bar unchanged. Fix: none.

### 4. Bars 17.4-18 and 157.4-158: II7 to the cadential 6/4. CHECKED, NO CHANGE

At 158, Tbn 1 holds C4 from ii-ø7 into the 6/4 and resolves it to B3. That is the full technique of PDF 182. At 18 the same C4 passes from Hn 3 to Cl 2, because the horns play an off-beat pattern there and that texture is protected (HISTORY.md). A tie in Hn 3 (`c'4~ c'2`) would follow the book more literally, but it would break the protected pattern, and the line is already held across the two instruments. Fix: none.

### 5. Bar 82: the I-IV6-I6 "Clock" progression (PDF 199-200). NOT APPLIED

The bass at 82 is a held pedal that c113 put in on purpose, and the upper parts move in eighth notes. Moving the bass I-IV6-I6 there would remove the pedal and clash with that rhythm. c202 rejected this for the same reasons. Fix: none.

### 6. Bars 67 and 75: IV6 or IV6/5 over a rising bass (PDF 169-171, 191). NOT APPLIED

Both bars are possible places for a 6-7-8 bass under IV6-V6/5-I. In both, F2's counter-melody is in the low register, and a stepping bass would cross it or double it in octaves. The existing root-position IV-V is correct for these bars. Fix: none.

### 7. Bars 249-250: a 7th left by leap. CHECKED, NO CHANGE

Tpt 3 leaps off C5, the 7th of the chord, at 249-250. This breaks the PDF 177 rule (resolve down by step) in that one part. But C5 continues in Hn 1 and the violas and resolves there, and the leap is Tpt 3's timpani-like motivic figure. Fix: none.
