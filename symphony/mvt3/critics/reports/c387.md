## Findings

### 1. 37-38: the B♭-minor F1 head's lower octave is the cellos alone under two ff horns; Bn 2 joins the cellos at the unison (applied)

**Bars:** 36-38, `pTwoBsnTwo` (pTwo.ily).

**Problem:** At 37 the head moves to B♭ minor in Hn 3·4 a2, ff "ben marc." (B♭4), with the cellos ff an octave lower (B♭3) and nothing else on the line. The violas and Bn 1 play the scale figure at f in the same register (F4 down to B♭3), so the cellos' octave sits under the figure and is the only octave of the head with no wind. Everywhere else the score gives each octave of the head its own partner. At 99-100 the violas have Bn 1 and the cellos Bn 2. At 103-104 the violas have Hn 3·4 and the cellos Bn 2 and Tbn 1. At 11-18 the violas have Hn 1·2. Meanwhile Bn 2 at 37-38 only adds a fourth instrument to the bass B♭2/F2, which Cb, Cbsn, B. tbn and Tbn 2 already play.

**Evidence:** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76: a wind in unison with a string group "increases the resonance of the latter and amplifies its tone", and "'Cellos + Fag." is one of the best pairs (PDF 76-77). PDF 77-78: in a melody in octaves, each octave takes its own wind doubling. PDF 79: horns and cellos blend well. With the bassoon, the horn octave above has a firmer lower octave under it. One wind on one string group keeps the strings on top (PDF 76).

**Fix (applied):**
```lilypond
% pTwoBsnTwo, bars 35-39
| aes,1 | es2 aes,4 r4 | bes2->(\f f4. ges8 | aes4-.) ges8( f8 es4-.) d4-. | bes,1~\> |
```
Bn 2 is marked f, the same as Bn 1 on the shared staff (equal dynamics). It rests on 36 beat 4, so it breathes before the entry and avoids a minor-ninth leap; the A♮ stays in Cb, Cbsn and B. tbn. From 39 it holds B♭2 with the cellos as before. The tenor clef already on the shared staff (c365) covers B♭3-D3. F1's notes are unchanged, since this is a doubling only.

### 2. 205-206: four horns over two trombones in octaves; Bn 2 joins the trombones on the head (applied)

**Bars:** 205-206, `codaOneBsnTwo` (codaOne.ily).

**Problem:** c381 set the head over the A♭ pedal as Hn 1-4 A♭4 over Tbn 1·2 A♭3, citing Rimsky PDF 74. The same sentence on that page calls horns over trombones in octaves "not so reliable": the two brass colours do not match across the octave. Bn 2 meanwhile doubles the A♭2 pedal already held by B. tbn and Vc.

**Evidence:** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 74-75: a woodwind with a brass instrument "softens and rarefies" it and gives "a finer legato"; "Trombone + Fag." is named. The bassoon pulls the trombone octave toward the horn tone above it. Bn 1 already takes the head at 207-208 with the woodwinds (B♭3), so now the head passes Bn 2 → Bn 1 within the bassoon staff, as it passes from brass to woodwinds.

**Fix (applied):**
```lilypond
% codaOneBsnTwo, bars 204-207
| g,4-> g,4-> r2 | aes2->(\f es4. f8 | ges4-.) f8( es8 des4-.) c4-. | aes,1 |
```
f, the same as Bn 1's held C4. Bn 2 returns to the A♭2 pedal at 207 (C3 → A♭2). Horns, trombones, the pedal and 207-210 are unchanged, and this does not undo c381.
