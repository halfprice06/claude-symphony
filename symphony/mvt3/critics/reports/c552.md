## Findings

### 1. Bar 93: the exposition's only perfect cadence with the tonic on top has no intermediate harmony. It is now ii6/5–V7–I (applied)

**Bars:** 91–94 (ff codetta, E♭ major).

**Problem.** Since c252 and c248 the codetta runs:
- 91: I–V7
- 92: common-tone °7 → I, then V7
- 93: I (displaced to beat 2, held across the half-bar), then V on beat 4
- 94: I

So the last cadence of the exposition is I–V–I. c143 notes that 93–94 is the closing group's only perfect cadence with the tonic on top, and nothing intermediate leads into its V. The first three V's close off the tonic before them; A&S call these back-relating dominants (PDF 158). In the passage the unit uses to show back-relating dominants, Mozart K. 332, I, 94–101 (Ex. 10-10, PDF 157), I–V | I–V is followed by **II6–V7–I**: the cadence gets its own predominant. A&S PDF 134–135 (Ex. 9-1, 9-2c) say the same in general terms. IV, II and II6 "lead into and intensify dominant harmony", and in a cadence they come "immediately before" the cadential V. At 93 the codetta repeats the tonic a third time where the reading puts II6. The 4̂ that would become V7's prepared seventh (PDF 139–140, Ex. 9-8) is also missing.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-552/work/sections/clos.ily, bar 93 only).** The chord on 93 beats 2–3 becomes ii6/5, A♭–C–E♭–F. Rhythm, accents, bowings and dynamics stay as they are.

The bass now steps A♭ up to B♭, the IV–V bass that A&S PDF 138 give for II6–V:

- Cb `r4 aes,,2->\downbow bes,,4->\upbow`
- Bn 2 `r4 aes,2-> bes,4->`
- Cbn `r4 aes,,2-> bes,,4->`
- B. Tbn `r4 aes,2-> bes,4->`
- Tuba `r4 aes,,2-> bes,,4->`

Two voices hold 4̂ (A♭) into V7, so its seventh is prepared:

- Vln II `r4 <c'' es''>2->\downbow <aes' d''>4->\upbow`

Both fall to G at 94.

These parts take C and fall to B♭:

- Bn 1, Tpt 3, the timpani's E♭/B♭, and c324's held E♭ in Ob 1·2 and Hn 1·2. E♭ is a chord tone of ii6/5.

The top line stays E♭6–D6 | E♭6 (1̂–7̂–1̂), now over ii6/5–V7–I.

I chose ii6/5 rather than IV. It keeps the top E♭ as a dissonance that resolves (PDF 139), and it adds F, which V shares, so four voices hold or step by a semitone into V. The voices that would have moved B♭→A♭ in octaves with the bass (92.3→93.2: Vln II, Cl 2) were rewritten, so I added no octaves across the rest. The upper voices move against the rising bass on 93.4 (PDF 136, Ex. 9-3a).
