## Findings

**1. Bar 117: the restored A4 in the bassoon clashes with the accompaniment. Applied.**
Problem: c131 restored the theme's A4–G4–F4 in Bn 1. That accompaniment was written for the old G4. Two things now go wrong in the bar. (a) On beat 2 the violas' pizz. G4–B♭4 sound around the solo's A4, so the appoggiatura is heard together with its own resolution tone G4 at the same pitch, inside a cluster G–A–B♭. (b) Vln I's answer runs F6–E♭6–D6–C6–B♭5–A♭5. Its A♭5 on beat 4 contradicts the solo's A♮4 from beats 1-2 of the same bar: a cross relation between the top voice and the melody.
Evidence: on (b), A&S PDF 394-395, Ex. 23-12a. On (a), PDF 376, Ex. 22-5: a resolution may pass to another part, but the dissonance and its resolution tone are kept apart. In the models (53, 125, 173) the accompaniment lies an octave or more from the appoggiatura.
Fix (dev.ily):
- `devVla` 117: `r4 <g' bes'>4 r4 <g' bes'>4` → `r2 r4 <g' bes'>4`. Vln II's E♭5–G5 still carries beat 2.
- `devVlnOne` 117: `... c''' bes'' aes'' |` → `... c''' bes'' a''! |`. A♮ is diatonic in G minor. The forced natural is there for the layout: with plain `a''` the line breaker moved bars 126-127 to the next system and the score grew to 17 pages. With `a''!` it stays at 16.
- Checked and kept: Vln I's E♭6 over A4 (117.2) moving to D6 over G4 (117.3) is a d5→P5 in similar motion, the "avoid" case of PDF 375. Here one of the two notes is a passing eighth, two octaves from the solo, so I left it.

**2. Bar 119, beat 4: the solo's E♭4–D4 is doubled in octaves again. Applied.**
Problem: c063 had removed Vln II's E♭5 on 119.4 because it moved E♭5→D5 in octaves with the bassoon's E♭4→D4 into 120. c112 then wrote `<es'' g''>` on both beats of 119 to make the iiø7 complete, and that brought the octaves back. c112's own aim needs only the G on beat 4.
Evidence: A&S PDF 377 (the 7th resolves down by step, in one voice). The parallel octaves are the fault c063 named.
Fix (dev.ily): `devVlnTwo` 119: `r4 <es'' g''>4 r4 <es'' g''>4` → `r4 <es'' g''>4 r4 g''4`. On beat 4 the chord is still A–C–E♭–G: the bass A, Vla A4–C5, the solo E♭4 and Vln II G5. G5 still falls to F♯5 at 120. This keeps c112's iiø7 and restores c063's fix.

**3. Bars 101 and 105, beat 1: the response chord sounds under the call's last note. Applied.**
Problem: the strings end the F1 head with an sf F-minor chord at 101.1 (a B♭-minor chord at 105.1). Hn 1-2 and Cl 2 attack their V6 chord at the same instant: E4–G4 against F–A♭–C at 101, A4–C5 against B♭–D♭–F at 105. E4 makes a major 7th with the bass F, and A4 with the bass B♭. The bass then stops, so the dissonance never resolves. Bn 1, playing the same chord, already waits for beat 2, as the woodwinds' scale figure does.
Evidence: A&S PDF 377 box ("all dissonances involving the bass must resolve"). PDF 382-383: an overlap of phrases works when one chord stands for both (elision). Here two chords sound against each other.
Fix (dev.ily):
- `devHnOne`: `g'1\p` → `r4 g'2.\p` (101) and `c''1` → `r4 c''2.` (105).
- `devHnTwo`: `e'1\p` → `r4 e'2.\p` and `a'1` → `r4 a'2.`.
- `devClTwo`: `e'1\mf` → `r4-\tag #'score -\tweak stencil ##f \mf e'2.-\tag #'part \mf`, and the same with `a'` at 105.
- `devClOne`: both hidden-mf tag pairs become a plain `\mf`. With two dynamics on one moment of the shared staff the build gave 4 warnings; now Cl 1's mf is the one printed, and each part prints its own.
- The two ideas stay apart (c017's do-not-touch). The response now starts after the call ends.

**4. Bars 96-97: cross relation E♭ (bass) → E♮ (Cl 2). Applied.**

**6. Bars 16.3 and 17.3 (c112 open point): root-position ii° in F1's accompaniment. Decided: no change.**
The harmonic unit here is the half bar. Within it, the off-beat on beat 4 (Hn 3-4 F3–C4) adds the 7th, so each half bar is iiø7, the normal form in minor (PDF 381). The d5 D–A♭ is treated regularly (PDF 375). At 16→17 the bass rises D→F under the held A♭, so the interval contracts to a third. At 17→18, A♭ falls to G over the bass D→G. A change would touch the F1 accompaniment and the protected F1 bowing, for no gain.

**7. Bar 78.1 (c112 open point): the gap below the melody's lower octave. Decided: no change.**
The gap is between Vln II/Ob B♭5 (the melody's octave doubling) and the sustained E♭4 in Vla/Vc. A&S PDF 77 warns of thinness when wide spacing lasts "beyond one or two chords". Here it lasts one beat, the upper voice is a doubling of the melody rather than a separate harmonic voice, and the harp's broken chord fills C5–E♭5 on every beat. Closed.
