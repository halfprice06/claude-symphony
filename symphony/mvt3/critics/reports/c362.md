## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 167–168: augmented second in Bassoon 1 (applied)
Problem: at the link to V6/5 the first bassoon holds E♭3 for a whole bar (167, f) and moves to F♯3 (168). That is a melodic augmented second in an inner voice, exposed because the bassoon is the only moving voice at that point.
Evidence: A&S PDF 82 says the augmented second is not used melodically in four-part writing and lists it with the dissonant leaps to avoid; PDF 80 asks for smooth inner voices where the soprano is static, which it is here.
Fix (sections/recP.ily, recPBsnOne):
`es,2-> r2 | es1\f | fis1\> |` → `es,2-> r2 | es2\f f2 | fis1\> |`
The line becomes E♭–F–F♯–G, a chromatic ascent in contrary motion against the falling bass A♭–A♭–G. par8.py on 166–170 is unchanged (only bass doublings).

### 2. Bars 141–144: doubled leading tone in the retransition pads (applied)
Problem: over the B♮ bass of the dominant region, Horn 2 holds B4 (141–142) and Trombone 2 holds B3 (143–144). With the bass B, the leading tone of C is doubled for four bars in sustained brass, and the horn B4 does not resolve to C.
Evidence: A&S PDF 76 and Ex. 5-4: never double the leading tone in V or VII; the doubled LT either resolves into parallel octaves or one copy is left hanging, which is what happens here. HISTORY.md protects the stretto head entries and the timpani in 139–146, not these pads; the pads are filler in the sense of Ex. 5-26 (PDF 85–86) and can take another chord tone.
Fix (sections/retr.ily):
`retrHnTwo … \partCombineApart b'1\p | \partCombineAutomatic b'1\< |` → `\partCombineApart g'1\p | \partCombineAutomatic g'1\< |`
`retrTbnTwo … b1\pp\< | b1 | d'1\f` → `g1\pp\< | g1 | d'1\f`
Horn 2's G4 is now held into its own F1 head entry at 143 (g'2->), so the entry is prepared by a common tone. Dynamics in the shared horn staff are unchanged and equal. par8.py on 140–146 is identical to v362.

### 3. Bars 81–82: the incomplete tonic of Ex. 5-5 (applied; the technique this pass adds)
Problem: S2 ends on V7 of E♭ (B♭–D–F–A♭) resolving to E♭ at 82. In v362 the horn/bassoon pad moved Hn 1 D4→B♭3 (the leading tone falls a third instead of rising), and the cellos leapt D3→G3, so the soft pad that closes the section resolved with no voice moving by step into the tonic root and a doubled G.
Evidence: A&S PDF 76–77 and Ex. 5-5 (Bach, Chorale 250) teach the incomplete tonic with tripled root after V7: the seventh falls, the leading tone rises, the second degree falls to the root, and every upper voice moves by step. The score had not used this resolution anywhere; its V7–I joins either keep the complete tonic by leaps in an inner voice or are elided. This is the place where the texture is thin and slow enough (whole notes, p) for the voice leading to be heard.
Fix (sections/sTwo.ily):
`sTwoHnOne … d'1\p | bes1\< \breathe | }` → `d'1\p | es'1\< \breathe | }`
`sTwoVc … \clef bass g1\mp\< | }` → `\clef bass es1\mp\< | }`
Bar 82 now sounds E♭–E♭–E♭–G in the Hn 1·2/Bn 1·2 pad, each by step from V7; the cellos' D3 rises to E♭3. The harp right hand still supplies B♭4 at 82.1.5, so the chord is complete in the orchestra as a whole. The player-level check finds only new octave doublings at 82.1 (Hn 1 joins the tune's D→E♭ with flute, violins and cellos) and 83.1 (Hn 1·2 octave pair); these are doublings in a free texture (PDF 84) and not faulty parallels. The tune itself is not changed.

### 4. Bar 114: wide gap between Violin II and Viola (not applied)
Problem: in the fading chord at 114, Violin II C5 and Viola A3 are 15 semitones apart, more than an octave between adjacent upper voices.
Evidence: A&S PDF 77 (Ex. 5-6) sets the octave limit between adjacent upper voices; PDF 79 (Ex. 5-9, 5-10) allows wider spacing briefly for a reason. Here it is one chord, diminuendo, in the lament, where the gap thins the sound on purpose.
Decision: not applied. If a later critic wants it filled, the fix is Violin II `<a' c''>2.` (divisi) in that bar.

### 5. Bars 72.3 and 192.3: d5→P5 in the counter-melody (not applied)
Problem: Viola D4 against Violin A♭5 moves to C4/G5 (72.3), and Bassoon 1 G3 against Violin D♭5 moves to F3/C5 (192.3).
Evidence: A&S PDF 83–84 (Ex. 5-20) avoids d5→P5 mainly where the bass is involved or the voices are close; neither applies (inner voices, more than two octaves apart, passing motion in the cello/viola counter-melody accepted at c027).
Decision: keep.

### 6. Bar 198→199: fifths by contrary motion into the coda (not applied)
Problem: Horn 1 E♭4→G4 against bass A♭1→C2 gives fifths by contrary motion.
Evidence: A&S PDF 86–87 (Ex. 5-27) treats such fifths as avoidable in strict style but common at an articulation in free textures. The bar 199 head is a protected quotation of movement I's hammered layout (kept by c182).
Decision: keep.

### 7. Bar 144: doubled leading tone and successive leaps in Violin II (not applied)
Problem: Violin II has B4 against the B bass and leaps B3–B4–G5.
Evidence: A&S PDF 76 (doubling) and 81–82 (Ex. 5-12, successive leaps only as arpeggiation). The leaps do arpeggiate G7, and the bar is inside the protected stretto entries 139–146, whose reason (the head imitation must enter at the given pitches) I do not answer.
Decision: keep.
