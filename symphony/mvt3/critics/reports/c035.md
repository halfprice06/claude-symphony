## Findings

### 1. 262-264: the trumpets' crossing is invisible, so Tpt 1's protected B→C reads as Tpt 2's line (applied)
Tpt 1 plays B4→C5 (the protected resolution with the timpani) while Tpt 2 plays D5→E5 above it. On the shared staff both are semibreves, so 262 shows a D/B chord and 263-264 an E/C chord; a conductor reads the upper note as Tpt 1. Gould PDF 65: "The upper part always takes up-stems, the lower part down-stems, even when pitches overlap"; PDF 67: "Overlapping semibreves must be divided into stemmed notes in order to show part progression". The checker confirms the only trumpet crossings in the movement are 262-263.
Fix (finale.ily): Tpt 1 `| b'2~ b'2 | c''2\fff~ c''2~ | c''2~ c''2 |`, Tpt 2 `| d''2~ d''2 | e''2\fff~ e''2~ | e''2~ e''2 |`. Tpt 1 now has up-stems from B and C, Tpt 2 down-stems from D and E. Pitches, ties and MIDI are unchanged (421.55 s before and after).

### 2. 273 and 211: semibreves touching the stem of a unison minim (applied)
At 273 Fl 1, Cl 1 and Tpt 1 hold a semibreve C while Fl 2, Cl 2 and Tpt 2 play the minims C-A in unison. LilyPond puts the semibreve left and the down-stemmed minim right, so the minim's stem touches the semibreve: in the 400 dpi image the two heads look like one chord with a stem through it. At 211 Cl 2's semibreve A♭ touches Cl 1's up-stem. Gould PDF 66: "Keep noteheads separate; stems should not touch. Semibreves in particular should not appear to be joined to stems", and he gives the remedy: "reverse the order of noteheads so that the semibreve is never adjacent to a stem".
Fix: 273 in finale.ily, voice one `\once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #1.2 c'''1->` (Fl 1; `c''1->` Cl 1 and Tpt 1), voice two `\once \override NoteColumn.force-hshift = #0 c'''2-> a''2` (Fl 2; Cl 2, Tpt 2 likewise). The minim now stands left with its stem on the far side and the semibreve to its right. 211 in codaTwo.ily: Cl 2 `aes'2\ff~ aes'2`, so beat 1 is two minims sharing one head with two stems, which Gould PDF 65 allows ("Two parts with the same note-value may share a notehead as long as they take two stems"). The held C6 over 273-274 and the quotation 275-276 are unchanged in sound.

### 3. "change bow at will" runs across barlines in nine places (applied)
At this spacing the direction is wider than a semibreve bar and crosses the next barline at 224 (Vc, Cb), 226 (Vln I; Vln II "unis., change bow at will"), 229 (Vla, through the barline and touching the D♭ minim's flat), 235 (Cb), and 255 (Vln I, Vln II, Vc). The same text on the held notes at 43 (Vc, Cb), 139 (Vc) and 143 (Cb) is changed too, so the score uses one form. Gould PDF 55: "the distance between characters should not be less than ½ stave-space and no characters should collide"; PDF 57: nothing closer than a space to a barline.
Fix (global.ily, used in arrival, finale, sHead, retr, trans): `cbaw = ^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "change bow" "at will" }` and `cbawU = ... \column { "unis., change" "bow at will" }`; every `^\markup \italic "change bow at will"` becomes `\cbaw`. In the rebuilt pages each direction ends inside its own bar. No system grew; the page layout is the same bar for bar (1-13, 14-36, 37-65, 66-88, 89-103, 104-130, 131-145, 146-158, 159-170, 171-196, 197-207, 208-222, 223-238, 239-252, 253-266, 267-279).

### 4. Other crossings hidden in single-stemmed chords (applied)
The part combiner turns two parts with the same rhythm into one-stem chords, which hides a crossing. Gould PDF 60 (notes of different parts take separate stems, upper part up) and PDF 65 (upper part up-stems even when pitches overlap). Cases and fixes:
- 211, Ob 2's F5 semibreve above Ob 1's A♭4 minim, drawn on Ob 1's stem: Ob 2 `f''2\ff~ f''2` (codaTwo.ily). Now Ob 2 has its own down-stem.
- 215-218, Hn 3 F3 below Hn 4 A♭3 under a semibreve chord: Hn 3 `f2~ f2`, Hn 4 `aes2~ aes2` at 215 (codaTwo.ily), so the stems show the crossing where it starts. I first exchanged the two notes instead, but that made Hn 4's G♭3→F3 a compound fifth with Tpt and Vln II C♯5→C5 (p5.py), so I kept the pitches.
- 111-114, Bn 1 C3 below Bn 2 F3 in one-stem chords: `\partCombineApart` from 111 to 114 in devBsnOne, `\partCombineAutomatic` before the bar-115 `\partCombineSoloII`.
- 195 and 197, Bn 1 C3 below Bn 2 E3 / E3-F3: the two players exchange those notes (recSBsnOne `e1` at 195, `e2 f2` at 197; recSBsnTwo `c1`, `c2 c2`). Same chord, Bn 1 stays on top, and both lines move by step. p5.py reports exactly the same fifths as v35.
Not changed: Ob 162 (the third below that cycle 1 kept), and the single crossed beats at Fl 200-201 and 208, Ob 201 and 207-208, Cl 102, 106 and 207-208, and Tbn 206. All of them are stemmed notes in moving passages that the combiner already prints with two stems.

### 5. Missing "1." after long rests at 110, 126 and 129 (applied)
Fl 2 rests through the whole development, so the combiner prints "1." once at 101 and never again. Fl 1's entries at 110 (f, after eight bars' rest) and 129 (mf, after eighteen bars' rest, on a new system) have no label. Cl 1's entry at 126 in the upward hand-over (cellos → violas → Cl 1 → Vln I/Fl 1, protected since cycle 34) has none either, although Cl 2 has rested since 107. Hn 1 at 127 does print "1." in the same passage. In the MOLA example score (PDF 10) every shared-staff entry is labelled. Gould PDF 65 sets the same aim: the reader must be able to tell which part is which on a shared staff.
Fix (dev.ily): `g''1\f^\markup \bold "1."` (Fl 1 at 110), `b''2(\mf\<^\markup \bold "1."` (Fl 1 at 129), `r2 d''2(\p\<^\markup \bold "1."` (Cl 1 at 126). The labels match the combiner's "1." style.
