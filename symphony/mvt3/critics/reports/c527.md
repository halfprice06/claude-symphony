## Findings

### 1. 139–140: Hn 3's held D4 runs inside Hn 1's solo F1 head (applied)

**Problem.** After the stop chord at 138, Hn 1 plays F1's head alone and p: G4–D4–E♭4 | F4–E♭4–D4–C4–B3. This is the stretto's first entry, and c009 made it "Hn 1 alone". Under it Hn 3·4 hold the open fifth D4/G3, also p, in the same colour. Hn 3's D4 is in unison with the head's D4 on 139.3, and the head passes through it again on 140.2.5, with E♭4 and C4 a second either side. The head therefore sounds like the moving top of a horn chord, not a horn solo. Four bars later c174 found the same clash (Hn 3·4 held inside Hn 1·2's F1 head at 143–144: unisons on D4 and F4, seconds, crossed by its C4–B3) and rested Hn 3·4 there. 139–140 was left as it was.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 264, Ex. XVII-9: when a solo horn plays, Mozart does not use the orchestral horns "so as not to interfere with the solo sound". PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist". PDF 268: the solo should stand apart from a transparent accompaniment. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132–133 (cited by c239) says a melodic line should not run against a held note of the same colour in the same register.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-527/work/sections/retr.ily).** Hn 3 rests 139–140 and re-enters open, p <, on D4 at 141, where the crescendo to mf at 142 begins. Hn 4 is unchanged: its stopped A♭3 at 138 still opens to G3 at 139, which keeps c088's + → o sf>p effect and c172's 9–8 resolution in the horns. G3 lies below every note of the head. Hn 4 restates p < at 141 after its breath, so the shared staff has the same dynamics in both parts. The violas still hold D4 in another colour, so the G7/B chord stays complete (B in Cb, Vc, Bn 2; G in Vc, Hn 4; D in Vla; F in Bn 1).

### 2. 119–122: bassoon solo against solo violins (checked, no change)

This passage follows Adler's Ex. XVII-15 (PDF 274, Beethoven VC iii) closely. Bn 1 plays sustained cantabile C4–A3 in its middle register, the four solo violins play ornamental descending runs (119.2–120.1, 122.2–4), and the pizz. strings give simple harmony. The difference in rhythmic activity keeps it clear, as the reading says. No change.

### 3. 191–194: Haydn's device on F2's second phrase in A♭ (tested on paper, declined)

Adler PDF 288 (Ex. XVII-24): in the second phrase the flute leaves the soprano and doubles the tenor two octaves up. At 187–190 Ob 1 doubles the violins' tune, then rests 191–194. I worked out Ob 1 (or Fl 1) on the cellos' counter-line at 191–194 two octaves up: A♭5–F5–A♭5 | G5–D♭5 | C5–F5 | D♭5–C5–B♭4. It sits on and across the violins' tune (D♭5–G5 at 191–194), which breaks principle 10. One octave up lands inside the violas' doubling at D♭4–G4. c124 also protects the cellos' mf counter at 187–198. Declined.

### 4. 74: timpani motive joining F2's phrases in the exposition (considered, declined)

Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13): the timpani play the motive that joins two phrases of the melody. 178 already does this with motive x pp near the rim. That is a recorded quotation (scherzo's motive x). Putting a copy at 74 would pre-empt it, so that 178 becomes a repeat, and it would add an unrecorded quotation. Declined.

### 5. 115: sustained horn tonic at the bassoon's entry (considered, declined)

Adler PDF 267 (Ex. XVII-10): a sustained tonic in the horns and low strings when the soloist enters. Here a horn G3/D4 would sit in the bassoon's own register (D4–A4 at 115–118). That breaks PDF 274, and horn and bassoon blend, so the horn would mask the solo. The pizz. bass G already marks the tonic. Declined.

### 6. 187–190: heterophonic oboe on the A♭-major F2 (considered, declined)

Adler PDF 264, 273–274 (Mozart, Liszt): heterophonic doubling in a contrasting colour. Ob 1 already doubles the violins in unison at 187–190. A decorated or simplified oboe line against two violin sections in unison would blur the tune's first major-key bars rather than colour them, and the reading's examples double a single soloist, not a section. Declined.
