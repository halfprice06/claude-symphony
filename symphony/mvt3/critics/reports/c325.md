## Findings

### 1. Timpani rolls play as single held strokes in score.midi (MIDI focus). Applied.
**Bars:** every roll: 5, 147-152, 209-210, 219-223, 231-238, 243-246, 261-263, 273-278.
**Problem:** LilyPond plays `c1:32` as one note held for its length, so each roll sounded as one stroke that died away after about a second. A crescendo roll could not grow (e.g. 220-222 sub. p → f, 261-263 f → fff), and the long f rolls at 231-233 and 273-276 went silent after their first beat.
**Evidence:** base build: Timpani track 172 note-ons, one for each printed roll. Adler PDF 291 item 3 asks that the score contain "every detail for every instrument". The roll is such a detail, and score.midi is the only place this loop can test it.
**Fix (applied):** rolls-midi.ily is copied into the version directory, identical to coherence/rolls-midi.ily (checked with diff); I chose a local copy so every vNNN builds wherever it is copied. In score.ly the whole `<< ... >>` becomes `fullScore = #(define-music-function (timpMusic) ...)`, and the timpani staff reads `<< \clef bass \global #timpMusic >>`. Then:
```
\score { \fullScore \removeWithTag #'part \timp
  \layout { }
}
\score { \fullScore \rollStrokesByBar #rollStrokeTable \removeWithTag #'part \timp
  \midi { ...unchanged... }
}
```
I did not apply `\rollStrokes` to the whole music, as the literal recipe would. That would also turn the string tremolo of 223-234 and 273-278 into repeated notes, and the task requires that only the timpani change. One n does not fit the movement either. At 2 = 84-92, semiquavers give 11.2-12.3 strokes a second, but at 2 = 56-72 they give only 7.5-9.6. So `rollStrokesByBar` walks the timpani music, keeps each note's start (2/2 throughout, so bar = whole notes elapsed + 1), and passes each roll to the unchanged `\rollStrokes` with that bar's value:
`rollStrokeTable = #'((1 . 16) (211 . 32) (261 . 16) (271 . 32))`.
Measured in the new MIDI (strokes per second): 5: 11.2; 147-152: 11.2; 209-210: 12.3; 219-222: 19.2/16.5/14.9; 223 and 231-234: 14.9; 235-238: 17.6; 243-246: 18.7 rising to 22.4 with the stringendo (a crescendo roll that quickens; switching to semiquavers at 245 would halve the rate inside the crescendo); 261-263: 11.7; 273-278: 19.2. Powers of two only: rolls-midi.ily's `roll-plan` gives wrong stroke lengths for n = 24. Velocities now follow the hairpins: 220-222 rise 62 → 113, 261-263 84 → 114, 275-276 84 → 103, and 223 falls fff → f.
**Checks:** all 16 pages are pixel-identical to the v325 build at 100 dpi (before findings 2 and 3 were added). Comparing every MIDI track event by event, only "Timpani:" differs (358 → 2018 events, 172 → 1002 note-ons). Duration 421 s; par8.py output over 1-279 is identical to v325's. arrivalPerc, codaTwoPerc and finalePerc are unchanged and keep their names. Two notes for the render thread. (a) A printed roll is now 8-64 MIDI notes, so if r07's roll builder or score_events.py expects one MIDI note per printed roll, it should be told. (b) Dan's render rule (rolls settle below their attack unless a hairpin says otherwise) is not modelled here: un-hairpinned rolls hold their printed level.

### 2. The brass families have no sub-brackets. Applied.
**Bars:** every system with brass.
**Problem:** Adler PDF 294: "heavy brackets set off the choirs; and an additional brace clarifies instruments of a family". Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) joins horns I-II with III-IV, the three trumpets and the three trombones. The finale draws one bracket around seven brass staves. The scherzo has had square sub-brackets on its horns and trombones since its c145, so the two movements also disagree.
**Fix (applied, score.ly):**
```
familyBracket = \with { systemStartDelimiter = #'SystemStartSquare \override InstrumentName.padding = #1.2
  \override StaffGrouper.staffgroup-staff-spacing = #'((basic-distance . 9) (minimum-distance . 7) (padding . 1) (stretchability . 5)) }
```
Inside the brass StaffGroup, three `\new StaffGroup \with \familyBracket << ... >>` wrap Hn 1·2 + 3·4, Tpt 1·2 + 3 and Tbn 1·2 + B.tbn; the tuba stays alone. The staffgroup-staff-spacing line is needed. Without it each family got the choir gap below it, every system grew taller, and the choir separation of principle 6 was blurred. With it, horizontal spacing and every system and page break are unchanged. Pages 3, 4, 7 and 10 move by 3-8 pt vertically. A family with one visible staff (e.g. Tpt 1·2 alone at 51-66) draws no bracket. No sound change.

### 3. Bowing texts cross the barline at 224 and 226. Applied.
**Bars:** 224 Vc ("unis., change bow at will") and Cb ("change bow at will"); 226 Vln II ("unis., change bow at will").
**Problem:** In these narrow whole-note bars the two-line `\cbawU` ("unis., change" / "bow at will") was wider than the bar. It crossed the barline at 225 (Vc) and at 227 (Vln II), where it touched the E6 ledger lines. The Cb text ran 0.5 mm past the 225 barline. Adler PDF 301 (proofreading) and PDF 291 item 1 (the score must be clear); Gould PDF 41, 55 (no collisions; text stays within its bar). c035 put `\cbaw` on two lines for this reason, but `\cbawU` kept the wider "unis., change".
**Fix (applied):** global.ily: `cbawU = ^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.3) \column { "unis.," "change bow" "at will" }`, so it is no wider than `\cbaw`. arrival.ily 224: `c1\f-\tweak X-offset #-0.5 \cbawU` (Vc) and `c,1\f-\tweak X-offset #-0.5 \cbaw` (Cb), so the text is centred between the barlines of 224. Checked at 200 dpi. No other use of `\cbaw` (43, 137-138, 217, 235, 255) collides. No sound change.
