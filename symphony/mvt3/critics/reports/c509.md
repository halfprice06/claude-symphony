## Findings

### 1. Bars 265-268: Vln II an octave below Vln I (PDF 58-59, Note I PDF 62). Tested, not applied

**Candidate.** high.py finds one unprotected place where the violins' top line has no lower octave anywhere in the orchestra. At 265-268 the line G5-E6-D6-C6-B5-C6 is played by Vln I and Vln II in unison, with Fl 1·2 and Cl 1. Below it, F1's head sounds at C5 (Tpt 1·2, Cl 2) and at C4 (Hn 1·2, Bn 1, Vla, Vc). PDF 58 recommends Vln I and II in octaves "in particular [for] those in the very high register", for "expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre".

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-509/t1/sections/finale.ily, Vln II at 265 and 267 became `r8 g'8(\ff e''4.->) d''8( c''8 b'8 | c''4->) r4 r2`, one octave lower. The build had 0 warnings and 16 pages. analysis.txt showed 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 264-269 printed nothing, as for v509 (the bass is the C pedal).

**Verdict: not better, so not applied.**
- The lower octave G4-E5 lands right on the trumpets' and Cl 2's F1 head (C5-B♭4-A4-G4), a third to a sixth above it in the same register. That blurs the two themes, which PDF 54 says are separated by position. At present they lie a fifth to an octave apart.
- The top line is not in the extreme register that Note I (PDF 62) means: it peaks at E6. It already has Fl 1·2 and Cl 1 at pitch. Taking the second violins off it removes half its string weight in a ff tutti, against PDF 57.
- 247-254 has the same layout (both themes, violins in unison on top). Changing only the restatement would make the two inconsistent.

/tmp/critic-509/work is v509 unchanged. The test copy is kept in /tmp/critic-509/t1.

### 3. Bar 194, beats 3-4: the double basses alone on the bass seventh (PDF 56). Noted, not changed

cb.py finds one real bass note without cello or bassoon support. At 194.3 the basses' D♭2, the seventh of E♭7 in third inversion (V4/2 of A♭), stands alone for two beats. The cellos and Bn 1 are on B♭2 and Bn 2 has leapt to E♭3. PDF 56: "there is no phrase of any importance given to the double bass without the support of 'cellos or bassoons."

I worked out the obvious fix on paper: Bn 2 `\transpose c aes, { g,2 f2 }` at 194, D♭3 on beat 3. It doubles the seventh, and Bn 2 then has to leave it by leap to A♭2 at 195 (c493's voicing). That breaks A&S's rule that the seventh resolves down by step, in an audible register. The other option is Bn 1 on D♭3, but then it leaves the cellos' D♭-C-B♭ line it doubles. The bass note is short and soft, and it is still heard in the basses with the cellos a sixth above. Neither fix is better, so I leave the bar as it is. I tested nothing here.

### 4. Holistic comparison with Brahms 2/iv. A conductor would accept the work; no change

**Plan.** Brahms opens sotto voce in the strings and breaks into a sudden ff tutti. His second theme is a broad, low string tune in violins and violas. The finale drives to a coda where the brass take over, with trumpets and trombones on the final D-major pages. This finale does the reverse at the start, by cyclic rule: ff German sixth, no soft opening. But it has Brahms's other two traits.
- Its broad string tune is F2 at 187-196: Vln I+II with the violas an octave below, the layout of PDF 60 (c). It is the same register idea as Brahms's low violin-and-viola tune.
- Its brass are held back for the end: trumpets silent until 51, and the first full-brass root-position C-major chord at 223.

**Where it matches Brahms's craft.**
- The development calms down before the retransition: the bassoon solo, the harp-accompanied G-major hand-over, 115-134. Brahms has a quiet episode in his development too.
- The coda's combination at 247-254 and 265-272 sets two themes in fixed registers two octaves apart over a pedal. That is the kind of contrapuntal coda Brahms writes.
- The Rimsky scans in the table are clean apart from the half bar in finding 3.

**Where it is not Brahms.** F1 is a fiery, Tchaikovsky-like tune rather than Brahms's understated D-major opening. The coda is long because it carries the owed cyclic items, and Brahms has no such obligation. Both follow from the brief, not from weak craft. No change.
