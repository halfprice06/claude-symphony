## Findings

### 1. 125–128: the cellos double the basses' roots while Bn 1 carries the development's counter line alone (applied)

Bars and problem. At 123–124 the cellos open the G-major F2 hand-over (sul D, espress.). From 125 the tune is in the violas, then Cl 1 (126.3), then Vln I and Fl 1 (128), and Bn 1 plays a dolce counter line against it, E3–F♯3–G3 | C4–D4–A3 | A3–B3–C4 | A3–C4. The cellos meanwhile drop to pp whole notes an octave above the basses (E | C–B | A | D). So the only string counterpoint to the tune is gone at the moment the tune leaves the cellos, and the cellos add nothing but a second octave on the roots. The thirds of these chords (G over E, E over C, C over A, F♯ over D) are already in the harp and the inner parts.

Evidence. Sebesky's rule 7 (PDF 140): the root "is the least important note of a string voicing"; doubling the bass with the lowest cello "is usually unnecessary", "anchors" the sound and can "cloud the clarity of the bass line"; a bottom-rich sound is "fresher… if used sparingly". In Ex. 194 (PDF 144–146) the cellos either sustain chord tones or play "an important unison counter line", and the bottom cello plays only eight roots in the whole arrangement, never "to reinforce the bass"; at the end "the arco bass, supplying the chordal roots all by himself, is now part of the string ensemble" (PDF 146). PDF 166: "A bass clarinet or bassoon can strengthen a low register cello passage." The score already uses the bassoon-plus-cello counter-melody at 187–194 (since v218) and, with horn, at 67–74. At 125–128 it gives the counter line to a single bassoon instead.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-467/work/sections/dev.ily, devVc). The cellos leave the bass after their solo, start on the bass's own E3, double Bn 1's counter line at pitch, pp dolce, and rejoin the basses on D3 at 128.3 for the crescendo into 131:

```
was:  e1\pp | c2 b,2 | a,1 | d1 |
now:  e2(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" fis4 g4 | c'2 d'4 a4~ | a2) b4( c'4 | a2) d2 |
```

From 129 the bass is unchanged: c432's E–E♭–D chromatic bass in cellos and basses, the 129.3 IV6 (c372) and the D held to 131 (c292). Basses, harp, Bn 1, the tune and every dynamic are unchanged.

Protection answered. The protected item at 123–130 is the hand-over's order and its notes, with the harp and the scoring crescendo (c033, c034, c044, c157, c211). The tune's order and notes are unchanged. No instrument enters or drops out, so the crescendo by addition is unchanged: the cellos sound throughout as before and only move from the roots to the counter line. Accompaniment changes inside 123–131 have been accepted on this basis before (c286, c292, c372, c432). c367 listed "cellos on the bass at 125–131" as not applied because of the protection. I take the opposite view because the protection covers the tune and its order, not the cellos' octave on the roots. On balance: the cellos are marked pp against the violas' p tune. They start on the bass note E3, not near the violas' A4, and they only meet the tune at D4 on 126.3, where the bassoon already meets it. Adler's warning that a bass alone is thin (adler1 PDF 105, c466 table row 20) is about exposed bass lines. Here the basses play pp at E2–A1 under the harp's arpeggios for three and a half bars, and the cellos rejoin them before the crescendo.

Checks. The build has 0 warnings and analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels; the page count is still 16 and page 7 is not crowded. In par8.py on bars 123–132, the only flags are the cellos' own octave doubling of the bass, which now starts at 129 instead of 126, and the Bn 2 doubling at 132 that the original also has. Across the movement, c002/par.py differs from the original only in those cello–bass doubling rows. No quotation, F1, F2 or chorale note changed.

### 2. 19–26 and 151–166: Vln II as a descending counter line under F1's consequent (tested on paper, declined)

Sebesky Ex. 214 (PDF 167–168) gives the lead to the first violins while "the second violins [provide] a descending counter line". F1's consequent at 19–26 has Vln I·II in unison. But F1's bowing and layout at 11–26 and 151–166 are protected (c018, c134, c211), and the unison gives the first statement of the main theme its weight. Octave reinforcement (rule 6) already comes from the flutes and oboes at 19. No change.

### 3. 119 and 122: a solo string quartet answering the bassoon (declined)

Ex. 212 (PDF 162–163) sets a solo quartet against the full section. The only fitting place is the echo after the bassoon solo. There, Vln II and the violas are on pizz. div. off-beats, and the bassoon solo at 115–122 is protected (c157, c171). Taking solo players out of those sections would break the pizzicato chords, and c438 already settled the "4 soli" colour. Lead-line weight (rule 2, PDF 138) does not apply to a pp echo in octaves. No change.

### 4. 87–90: cellos and basses in octaves under the violins' 6/3 bridge (checked, no change)

Rule 7 does not apply here. The passage is f, the bass line is the 6/3 chain's real bass, and Bn 2 and the contrabassoon double it. PDF 140 itself allows a "full, bottom-rich" sound at such moments. c377's woodwind canon at 89–90 is unaffected.

### 5. 247–254 and 265–272: strings in the combination tuttis (checked, no change)
