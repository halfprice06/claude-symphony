## Findings

### 1. 96–97: Hn 3's E♭4→E4 comes before the horn's reserved D♯4→E4 at 219–220 (applied)

Problem. c229 moved the held chord under the oboe solo from Cl 2 to the horns, so Hn 3 now plays G4–E♭4–E4 at 95–97. In the v230 MIDI, Hn 3 is the only horn in the movement that steps into E4 from a semitone below before the arrival, and it does so at the same pitch and in the same player as the cyclic step D♯4→E4 at 219–220. finale.md says that step "is heard in Hn 3 and the violas only" and that it "answers the scherzo horn call's phrase … at its own pitch", the D♯4 the scherzo leaves unresolved (scherzo.md, "What the finale relies on"). So the horn takes that step 123 bars early, p, as an inner part. The violas' 97 inflection (c203) is a different matter: it is the thread's own line at one pitch, and finale.md records it.

Evidence. Casanelles PDF 227: the drum ensemble is absent from the first part of the climax and given as a reward in the last. PDF 235: Annihilation keeps its synths for "the only time in the score that synths … make an overt appearance". PDF 217: a sound's effect depends on where it is placed. A reserved sound loses its force when it is heard early, even quietly.

Fix (applied, sections/clos.ily, Hn 3 at 95–98):
```
closHnThree = { … | g'1\p\>~ | g'1 | g'1\pp~ | g'2. r4 | }
```
(It was `g'1\p\> | es'1 | e'1\pp~ | e'2. r4`.) Hn 3 holds G4 with Vln II. The horns re-strike together at 97 (Hn 4's B♭3 already did), and the dynamics on the shared staff stay equal. The pitch classes of the chord are unchanged: E♭4 and E4 stay in the violas, which now carry the inflection alone, as the thread's description requires. This answers the "95–98 scoring settled" protection: the scoring stays as c229 made it (horns, not clarinets, at the same dynamics). Only Hn 3's inner line changes, and the reason is the cyclic rule, which c229 did not consider.

### 2. Bar 9: the scherzo's last timpani figure becomes the finale's first drive (applied; new quotation)

Problem. The finale takes the scherzo's last pitch (G♯ as A♭, bar 1) but none of its last rhythm. The scherzo's timpani end with motive x ppp (B–B–B–E at its 363, 367 and 371) and a last E. The finale's timpani then play one A♭ stroke, a G, single Gs at 7–8, and quarter strokes at 9. The listener's freshest memory of the scherzo, its dying pulse, is not taken up at the join. The finale first quotes motive x at 143–145, more than two minutes later.

Evidence. Casanelles PDF 200 (Sadoff): a reference counts only when the listener has the background to catch it, and that background is strongest seconds after the scherzo. PDF 211–212: in a score made by several hands, shared material and written "glue" at the joins make the result sound as if one composer wrote it. PDF 239 and 227–228: the same material in a new setting changes its meaning (minor chord to nostalgia; Zimmer's theme turned inward). Here a ppp ending figure becomes a crescendo into F1. PDF 243: a pulse can carry a dramatic state. PDF 216–217 (Fig. 5.7): put most of the growth in the last part.

Fix (applied, sections/intro.ily, timpani bar 9):
```
introTimp = { … | g,4\mf r4 r2 | g,4 r4 r2 | g,4\< g,8 g,8 g,4 r4 | g,8 g,8 g,8 g,8 g,4->\f r4 | }
```
(Bar 9 was `g,4\< r4 g,4 r4`.) The strokes per bar at 7–10 now go 1, 1, 4, 5, so the dominant preparation tightens where Fig. 5.7 puts the growth. It is the same form and pitch as the quotation at 143–145 (`g,4 g,8 g,8 g,4`, the retransition form a major third below the scherzo's B). Both dominant preparations that lead into F1 (7–10 → 11, 143–150 → 151) now carry it, so 143 is heard as a return. This answers c153's concern about the freshness of 143: that concern was about a stray single G at 132, outside any preparation. No pitch changes, no other part changes, and bars 1–8 are untouched.
