## Findings

### 1. 210→211, Vln I: the G♭6 is not tied into 211 (applied)
**Problem.** c151 meant the G♭6 that ends the 209-210 run to be tied over into 211 and to resolve to F6 on beat 2 (finale.md, c155 critique: "the G♭6 tied 210→211"). The source does not do that. codaOne.ily ends Vln I with `ges'''4->~ ges'''4 | }`, a quarter tied to a quarter inside bar 210, and codaTwo.ily begins `ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4)` with no tie in front of it. The page-12 image shows this: a tie inside 210 and a new attack at 211. The players get an extra bow attack at the double bar and the change to Largamente, so the suspension the composer describes is struck again instead of held, and a half note on beats 3-4 is split into two tied quarters for no reason.
**Evidence.** Adler App. A PDF 302 (violin, G♭6 is easy in the professional range) means the note itself is fine. The fault is in the notation, not the range. The intended sound is in c151's report (A&S PDF 357-358: the 7th of V7 tied over as a suspension).
**Fix (applied).** codaOne.ily, 210: `bes''8 c''' des''' es''') ges'''2->~ | }`. codaTwo.ily stays as it is (`ges'''4\ff\>( f'''4) …`), so the tie lands on the ff note and the slur runs to F6. The bow is three quarters plus one, about 1.5 s, which is easy. Vln I note count 749 → 748 in analysis.txt. This is the only sound change.

### 2. 226-230, Bassoons 1·2: five bars on one breath (applied)
**Problem.** At 223 the bassoons have `c | c | c \breathe | b, | a, | f | c | c` (Bn 1 C3, Bn 2 C2-B1). After the breath at 225 they hold five whole notes, 226-230, at Maestoso 2 = 56. That is about 21 s at f, much of it in the bassoon's lowest octave (Bn 2 B1-C2). The contrabassoon on the same line already breathes after 225 and after 228; the bassoons do not.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 304 (App. A): the bassoon bottom is B♭1, so Bn 2 is within a tone of it, the register Adler tells writers to watch. Adler adler1 PDF 230: low notes take "considerable" breath, so give periodic rests. Adler adler1 PDF 198: frequent rest periods are mandatory for double reeds. 228→229 is the chorale's plagal step F→C. All three low reeds take the breath there, and the cellos, basses (C) and timpani keep the bass going through it.
**Fix (applied).** arrival.ily: `arrivalBsnOne = { c1\fff | c1\f | c1 \breathe | b,1 | a,1 | f1 \breathe | c1 | c1 | R1*4 | }` and `arrivalBsnTwo = { c,1\fff | c,1\f | c,1 \breathe | b,,1 | a,1 | f,1 \breathe | c,1 | c,1 | R1*4 | }`. The two parts breathe at the same point, so the shared staff prints one mark. c154's held C at 229 is kept: the bassoons still play C on 229.1.

### 3. 243-246, Tpt 1 C6: at Adler's professional top. Decided: keep
Tpt 1 holds C6 for about seven beats (243-246, f, stringendo). Adler PDF 305 puts the C trumpet's professional top at C6 and the amateur top at B♭5. This explains the analyser's flag: its B♭5 limit is the amateur column. For a professional orchestra the note is in range. It comes after eight bars of rest (235-242, about 29 s), at f rather than ff, and Tpt 1 has another rest at 251-254 before the last chorale. The approach E5→C6 (a minor sixth) is the horn call's own interval (protected quotation item 7). I make no change.

### 4. 207-210, Hn 1 written B♭5 held for four bars. Decided: keep
Hn 1 holds E♭5 sounding (written B♭5) mf < for 207-210. This is a tone below Adler's professional top (PDF 305, written C6) and above his amateur limit. Adler adler1 PDF 303 calls the high register taxing and asks for periodic rests. The part has them: Hn 1 rests on 206.4 (c136) and again 211-214, and the note is doubled at pitch by Fl 2 (207-208) and Cl 2 (209-210), so it needs no forcing. No change.

### 5. 222-223, Fl 1 and Vln I run to C7 fff. Decided: keep
Fl 1 reaches C7, exactly Adler's professional top (PDF 303), and holds it fff for one bar at 2 = 56 (about 4 s). Vln I takes the same C7 in tremolo, well inside the violin's range (PDF 302, up to about E7). The flute's C7 is a strong note with a standard fingering; the hard notes are those above it, which the score does not use. The flute rests at 224-225, so it can breathe after the held note. This is the owed C7 (protected). No change.

### 6. 231-233, portato quarters in Vln II and violas; 229, held C. Decided: keep
Four portato quarters under one slur per bar at 2 = 56 is a 4.3 s bow at f. Played in the middle of the bow in the middle register (Vln II F5-E5, violas A3-G3), this is normal professional bowing, and 233 has a diminuendo to mf, which makes the last bow lighter. The violas use the same pattern from 224 and nobody has reported trouble with it. The held C at 229 is in range for every player (Bn C3/C2, Cbsn C1, Vc C3, Cb C2), and Finding 2 deals with its one breathing problem. No change.

### 7. 235-247, harp. Decided: keep
The pedal diagram at 235 sets all naturals; every note 235-242 is a natural, and the harp has 36 bars of rest (199-234) to set the pedals from A♭ major. The LH octaves A1-A2, F1-F2 and D2-D3 at 239-242 are above the harp's C♭1 (Adler PDF 303). The 8va bassa from 239 is the "8 basso" notation of Adler's own chart, and the bracket closes at 242 before the "étouffez" at 243, which sits on the rest where the hand damps. The broken-chord figure is protected (c116). No change.

### 8. 199-222, brass endurance. Decided: keep
Tpt 1 plays 199-204 ff, rests 205-210, plays 211-228 with breath marks after 214, 218 and 223, rests 229, and rests again 235-242. The trombones and tuba play nearly continuously 199-228 (about two minutes), with marked breaths after 214, 218 and 223 (tuba and bass trombone after 225) and repeated whole notes at 205-210 where they can breathe, then rest 235-246. Every note is in Adler's professional range (PDF 305-306). Tpt 1's 219-223 (C5-C5-B4-B4 → C5 fff) has no breath mark, but 221 and 222 are separate whole notes, so the player can breathe at either barline without touching the protected B→C at 222→223. No change.
