## Findings

### 1. 245–246: since c449, Ob 2 holds the leading tone B4 a semitone under the horn call's resolution to C5. Applied.

**Problem.** The horn call in major (owed item: movement I's call E–C–B with ♭3 raised and B resolved) reaches its C at 245.3. Tpt 1 takes it at C6, Tpt 2 and Hn 1 at C5, Hn 2 at C4, and they hold it to 246.3. The other sounding pitches there are D (Vc, Cb, Bn 2), F (Cl 1, Bn 1, Hn 4), G (Ob 1 G5, timpani roll) and C (the call). In v449 every B in the texture (Vln I B4, Vln II B3, Bn 1 B3) stopped on 245.3, exactly when the call moved to C. c449 added Ob 2 on B4, p <, tied through 246. Ob 2 enters in unison with Tpt 2 and Hn 1 on the call's B4. When they rise to C5, it stays on B4, and the minor second B4–C5 sounds for six beats in a crescendo. It is the only B left, and it sits under the very note that resolves the call's B. The cyclic point of 243–246 is "B resolved", and this note works against it.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 126–127: when parts pass from one group to another, "the progression of parts … remains as regular as if there were no leap". Ob 2 takes the call's B4 from the brass at 245.1, so it should follow the brass's progression B→C.
- Rimsky PDF 19–20: the treatise teaches how "to detach a melody from its harmonic setting". Here a held B blurs the call's C.
- Rimsky PDF 125–126 (transference): the oboe's note should coincide with the note it takes over, and here that note is C5.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-450/work/sections/sHead.ily, `sHeadOboeTwo`, with a c450 comment):**
```
sHeadOboeTwo = { R1*10 | b'1~\p\< | b'1 | }
```
becomes
```
sHeadOboeTwo = { R1*10 | b'2(\p\< c''2~ | c''1) | }
```
- c449's aim stays: the oboes still enter p < at 245 and grow into their ff at 247. Ob 1 still holds G5, so the oboes make a sixth G5/B4 on 245.1–2 and a fifth G5/C5 from 245.3, and they move to E5/C5 at 247.
- This does not undo c449. It changes one note for a reason c449 did not consider.
- Shared staff: both oboes are p < at 245.1, and there is no ottava.

### 5. Relays over the orchestral range (Ex. 237, 238): considered, not applied

The score has no relay of the kind Rimsky describes on PDF 126, where each instrument takes a wide passage over from the last on a common note. I considered the four places where one could go:
- **245–246 (into the combination of themes):** a relay violas → Vln II → Vln I would need sixteenths to cover three octaves in six beats. That would break the eighth-note scale figure of bars 3–4, and it gives up the parallel-octave weight that the ff at 247 needs.
- **7–10:** c350 declined this, and it would replace c280's staggered crescendo, which Rimsky PDF 127 (amplification) supports.
- **222:** the stop chord, the 219–223 crescendo and its resolutions at pitch are protected.
- **205–210:** the three runs are already staggered by c408 and c393.

Rimsky gives the relay for passages "too wide to be performed on any one instrument", and none of these passages is that wide. The transference itself (the common-note hand-over) is already used well at 1–2, 95–98, 123–130 and 275–276.

Do not touch: the link at 1–2, the violas' E♭4–D♯4–E4 thread (1, 97, 219–220, 223), 222→223 with Tpt 1 B4→C5 and the timpani G→C, both chorale top lines, the 217–218 cor anglais and horn echo, and the closing chain with the scherzo quotation at 273–277.
