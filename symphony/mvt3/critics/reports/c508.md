## Findings

### 1. Applied. Bar 149: the strings attack the sfp chord and swell with it instead of holding ff over it

**Problem.** In the allargando bar before the recapitulation, c226 (brass), c346 (timpani) and c408 (all woodwinds) gave 21 parts `\sfp\<` into ff at 150. The strings still re-bow their dominant chord ff (`\dnup`) for the whole bar: Vln I F5/D6, Vln II B4/G5, violas D4/G4, cellos G2/F3, basses G1. Those are the registers of the wind chord itself: flutes D6/F6, oboes B5/D5, Hn 1·2 G4, Tbn 1 F4, tuba G1. A full string section holding ff at the same pitches as winds and brass at p covers the dip, so the bar sounds as a steady ff with a faint swell inside it. The effect that three cycles wrote into 21 parts is not heard.

**Reading.** Rimsky PDF 129, method (a): when the wood-wind begin a piano chord, the strings *attack* it sforzando. The method gives the attack to the strings and the sustained piano to the winds; nowhere does it keep the strings loud through the winds' piano. Ex. 253 (PDF 418) writes that attack as **sfp** in the cellos and basses, under an accented p string tremolo. PDF 130: the brass carry a crescendo chord into its sf climax, which here is the ff of 150 and F1's return at 151. c408 kept the strings at ff because "the strings' re-bowed ff chord ... give the attack". The strings still give that attack in the fix, sf on the down-bow; they no longer sustain ff after it. That answers c408's reason and does not reverse its wind change.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-508/work/sections/retr.ily).** The strings get `\sfp\<` on the 149 chord and `\ff` on 150. Pitches, ties, bowings (`\dnup`: sf down-bow, crescendo up-bow, the natural bowing for an sfp <) and "change bow at will" are unchanged:

retrVlnTwo ... | <b' g''>1\sfp\<\dnup~ | <b' g''>2.\ff r4 | }

A source comment (c508) cites the reading. Tpt 1·2 keep their accented ff half-note strokes (D5–F5, B4–D5), so the bar still has a loud attack running through it, and their line peaks on G5 at 150 over the swell. Because the strings' sfp starts loud, the MIDI velocities hardly change (Vln I 91 → 90); the change is to what the players read and do.

### 2. Considered, not applied. Bar 217: the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 echo re-enter f

Rimsky PDF 136 rates an f re-entry below pp or ff, and the cor anglais has rested 30 bars before 217. But PDF 129 defines the echo as octave imitation with *less* resonance than the statement, and the statement (Tpt 1, 211–212) is ff. The echo at f is that one step down. Marking it p would leave nothing for the sub. p at 219 to drop from, and the 219–223 crescendo is protected. I kept f.

### 3. Considered, not applied. Bar 234: the woodwinds end the plagal close on an mp half note

PDF 136 advises against ending on mf or mp and says the advice applies more widely. Bar 234, however, does not end a piece. It is the overlap into 235, where the strings carry their dotted half into "Poco più mosso, molto cantabile". The staggered release (brass p, woodwinds mp, strings sustained) is PDF 130's reverse-order diminuendo, and 230–234 is protected. No change.

### 4. Considered, not applied. Bar 151: a violin glide into the recapitulation
