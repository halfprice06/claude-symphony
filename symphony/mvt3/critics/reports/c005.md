## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 219, 255, 261-262: the cymbal semibreves have no readable duration and collide with the bass drum (applied)
**Problem.** Gould PDF 26 says the white area of a minim or semibreve cannot hold a cross, so the cross is drawn inside a thin circle. v05 prints bare crosses for `cymc1` at 219, 255 and 261, so they read as quarter-note crosses without stems. The drumStyleTable also puts the cymbal on the line (position 0) and the bass drum at -2. On a one-line stave, position -2 gets a ledger line through the bass-drum note, and the cross sits directly on top of it. I checked this at 400 dpi on page 13, bar 219. The arrival bar is the one place where the percussion has to read without doubt.
**Fix (applied).** In score.ly, put the cymbal above the line and the bass drum below it, and turn ties up:
```
drumStyleTable = #(alist->hash-table '((crashcymbal cross #f 1) (ridecymbal cross #f 1) (bassdrum default #f -1)))
\override Tie.direction = #UP
```
arrival.ily: `<\tweak style #'xcircle cymc bd>1\ff`; finale.ily: `\tweak style #'xcircle cymc1->` (255) and `<\tweak style #'xcircle cymc~ bd>1->` (261). A `\override NoteHead.style` has no effect because the drum engraver resets the style; `\tweak` works (I tested it).

### 2. Bars 11-26: Bassoons 1·2, part-combine labels on every half-bar and two dynamics at once (applied)
**Problem.** Bn 2 plays the bass in half notes (`es2 c2`, marked f) while Bn 1 plays off-beat quarters (`r4 g4-.`, marked mf). The part-combiner prints "2." on every beat 1 and "a2" where both play g (13, 21), more than 40 labels on page 2. Each label sits above the stave over Bn 1's notes, so it seems to name the wrong player. The stems also switch between shared and split inside each half-bar, which breaks Gould PDF 28: keep one stem direction per beat or half-bar, and in two-part writing each part keeps its own. The two simultaneous dynamics (f under mf) break the shared-stave rule.
**Fix (applied).** In pOne.ily, `\partCombineApart` at the start of pOneBsnOne and pOneBsnTwo and `\partCombineAutomatic` after their last bar. Bn 2 `es2\mf`, so both players are mf, the same level as the clarinets and horns in this accompaniment (c004 set Hn 1·2 to mf here). The strings carry the bass at f. The stave now shows Bn 1 stems up with its rests and Bn 2 stems down, with no labels.
The same dynamic clash occurs at bar 101: Bn 2's last quarter is still f while Bn 1 enters mf. **Applied:** dev.ily, Bn 1 `e1\fp`, which matches the f attack and then sits under the horns' mp.

### 3. Bars 25-26 and 165-166: Horns 1·2 at 4-5 ledger lines below the treble stave (applied)
**Problem.** At concert pitch the a2 horns reach `c` and `b,` (C3, B2) in `es4( d8 c8 b,4 d4) | c2->`. That needs four and five ledger lines below the treble stave, on a staff that already has chords and slurs. Gould PDF 21 allows "up to at least three ledger lines" and no more. My script found no other horn note past four ledger lines.
**Fix (applied).** pOne.ily and recP.ily, Hn 1 and Hn 2: `| \clef bass es4( d8 c8 b,4 d4) | c2-> r2 \clef treble |`. The bass clef is printed before the 24|25 barline, at the start of the phrase (PDF 22). The treble clef comes back at the barline before the next entry, 27 and 167 (PDF 23). I left the single `d` at 13, 21, 153 and 161 alone: at four ledger lines it is one note, and Gould prefers ledger lines to frequent changes.

### 4. Bar 242: viola run to B5 in alto clef (applied)
**Problem.** `c''8 d'' e'' f'' g''4 a''8 b''` puts a'' and b'' on four ledger lines above the alto stave (ledger.py; visible on page 15). Gould PDF 21 calls for a clef change beyond three ledger lines.
**Fix (applied).** sHead.ily: `f2 r8 \clef treble g'8( a' b' | c''8 d'' e'' f'' g''4 a''8 b'')\! \clef alto |`. The clef comes after the rest, at a half-beat, and at the start of the phrase (PDF 22). It returns to alto at the 242|243 barline, before the ff entry (PDF 23).

### 5. Bar 262: two fermatas on every stave; on the drum stave one collides with the stem (applied)
**Problem.** global.ily ends with `s1*6 s1\fermata`, and every part also has `r2\fermata`. As a result every staff in bar 262 prints a fermata over the final quarter and a second one over the half rest. On the drum stave the first one sits on the up-stem of the cymbal note. The convention is a single fermata over the final rest, and Gould PDF 24-26 requires symbols that do not collide.
**Fix (applied).** global.ily: `s1*6 s1` (the fermata stays in each part's `r2\fermata`).

### 6. Bar 219: the arrival is the last three bars of a crowded page (applied)
**Problem.** In v05, page 13 holds 208-221: fourteen bars, three key-signature changes, and the arrival squeezed into the right edge. The owed item of the symphony begins mid-page with no space around it.
**Fix (applied).** global.ily: `\bar "||" \pageBreak \mark \default % O 219`. score.ly `\paper`: `page-count = 16`. Without page-count, LilyPond packed the score into 15 tighter pages. With it, the score stays at 16 pages, the cap. Page 13 is now 207-218 (12 bars), page 14 opens with the Maestoso at 219, and no other page holds more bars than in v05.

### 7. Bar 1: "senza sord." printed twice on the Hn 3·4 staff (applied)
**Problem.** Both hnThree and hnFour carry `^\markup \italic "senza sord."`, so the same text is stacked twice above the staff. One instruction on the shared stave covers both players.
**Fix (applied).** intro.ily: remove the markup from introHnFour. The cyclic rule "Hn 3·4 senza sord. at their first entry" is still met.

### 8. Not changed: very high notes in Fl 1 and Vln I (219 C7, 218 and 242 B6)
These notes take five ledger lines. Gould covers octave signs on PDF 41-48, outside my assigned range, and the C7 at 219 is a protected owed item. I leave them as they are.
