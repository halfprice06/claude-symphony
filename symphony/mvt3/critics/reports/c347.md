## Findings

### 1. 141–142: the dominant seventh's discord sits only in the violas, enclosed by the horns (applied)

**Bars:** 141–142.

**Problem:** Over the B bass, the four horns hold G3–D4–B4–D5 (Hn 4, Hn 3, Hn 2, Hn 1). These are the concords of V7. Bn 1 doubles Hn 3's D4 at the unison. The seventh F4 is only in the violas (cresc.), in the middle of the horn chord. The two bassoons, which Rimsky calls the instruments that reconcile woodwind and brass, therefore play only concords, and the one dissonant tone is a string note inside brass.

**Evidence:**
- PDF 108: in soft four-part horn and bassoon harmony, cross the parts with "the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons"; "bassoons may also be written inside the horns".
- PDF 113: strings and brass have no affinity of tone and are seldom combined by crossing or enclosure.
- The score does the right thing two bars earlier, at 139–140 (Bn 1 F3 under Hn 3·4 D4/G3), and at 130 (Bn 1 C4, the seventh of D7, under the horns).
- At 141 the horn chord also has a sixth-wide gap, D4–B4, with nothing in it but the violas.

**Fix (applied):** In retr.ily, `retrBsnOne` bars 141–142 change from `d'1\cresc | d'1` to `f'1\cresc | f'1`. Bn 1 then takes the seventh F4 with the violas, inside the horns' D4–B4 gap, and drops back to F3 at 143 as before. Bn 2 B2, the horns, the dynamics and the breath after 140 are unchanged. The seventh does not resolve until 151 (F→E♭ in the i6/4), so a doubled F4 is a unison and creates no voice-leading fault.

**Checks:** The build has 0 warnings and 16 pages. analysis.txt has 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py reports nothing new on 135–150: its whole-movement output is 952 lines, as in v347. The c002 all-pairs parallel report is identical to v347's. The theme record is 42/42.

### 2. The soft "mysterious" enclosure inside trumpet octaves (Ex. 159, 160, 162) is not used in its pure form (decided: no change)

**Bars:** 219–221, 230–234, 243–246.

**What the reading teaches:** PDF 108 and Ex. 159 (Snegourotchka), Ex. 160 (Sadko) and Ex. 162 (Tsar Saltan) all use pp trumpets holding an octave pedal while flutes, sometimes with clarinets, move in thirds between them.

**Where the score stands:** The closest thing in the score is 219–221: Tpt 3 C4 and Tpt 1 C5, then B3 and B4, sub. p, with Cl 1·2 (A♭4/F♯4, then G4) inside the octave. That is enclosure of clarinets within trumpets as Ex. 162–163 show it, so verdict 8 is "follows".

**Why I add nothing:**
- No other soft place exists where trumpets can hold an octave pedal without breaking the trumpet plan: tacet 1–50, the owed solo at 51, muting during 167–170 for 171.
- 230–234 is a protected plagal close with the trumpets in thirds.
- 235–238 is the protected S-head texture. A pp trumpet pedal there would also pre-empt the trumpets' horn-call entry at 243.
- At 243–246 the trumpets do hold octaves, but at f under a stringendo. Rimsky gives the low-flute thirds for soft passages only, and Cl 1 and the divided violins already fill the octave there.

### 3. 97–98: the seventh B♭3 is in Hn 4, not in a bassoon (decided: no change)

**Bars:** 97–98.

**Problem:** Rimsky's "and not" figure (PDF 108) puts the discord in the horns, which is the case here. Over C (Vc, Cb, Bn 2) Hn 3·4 hold G4/B♭3 pp and the violas hold B♭3/E4.

**Why I keep it:**
- c340 protects the horn chord and the violas' E♭4→E4 thread under the Cl 1 quotation of movement II's tail.
- Only one bassoon plays, so this is not four-part horn-and-bassoon writing.
- The violas double Hn 4's B♭3, which is the string–horn doubling PDF 113 allows ("horns doubled by divided violas").
- The B♭ and G are common tones held from the E♭ chord at 95–96, which is a different case from a seventh placed in the horns.

### 4. 168–169: stopped horns doubled at the unison by clarinets, not over deep clarinet notes or with the cor anglais (decided: no change)

**What the reading teaches:** PDF 107 gives two things: stopped horn middle notes over deep clarinet notes for "a beautiful dark tone", and stopped horns together with oboe or cor anglais (PDF 110, Ex. 164).

**Why I keep it:**

- The stopped-to-muted colour link is already made at 49–51 (stopped Hn 1 + Ob 1 → open trumpet solo).

### 5. 39–41: clarinets inside the horns' B♭3–F4 (decided: no change)

**Bars:** 39–41.

**Problem:** In the staged diminuendo, Cl 1 F4 and Cl 2 D4 sit inside four horns that hold only B♭3 and F4. PDF 108 says clarinets "should rarely be set inside the horns" and that a soft horn chord is better completed above.

**Fix considered:** Cl 1·2 an octave higher, F5/D5, from 39.

**Why I keep it:**
- Every clarinet pitch here is a unison doubling of a part that stays: F4 doubles Hn 1·3, D4 doubles Bn 1 and the violas. So the clarinets are not a separate colour inside the horns. They thicken unisons, and the chord's upper part is already above the horns (Fl 1 B♭5/A♭5, Fl 2 and Ob 1 D5, Ob 2 B♭4/A♭4).
- Raising them would put the held F5/D5 across the violins' falling F1 head (F5–B♭4–C5–D5–C5–B♭4–A♭4) at 39–40.
- It would also break the tie from 38.4 that cycle 319 set up.
