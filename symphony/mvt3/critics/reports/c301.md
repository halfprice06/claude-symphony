## Findings

### 1. 189-192: put the recapitulated F2's peak and descent on the A string (applied)

**Problem.** At 187-196 F2 returns in A♭ major, Vln I and II in unison, mf cantabile, with Ob 1 at pitch p dolce and Hn 1 with the violas an octave below. With no string marked, a section plays the peak bar 189 (B♭5-A♭5-G5) and 190 (F5-E♭5) on the E string, so the statement crosses from the A string to the E string at its peak and has the same bright colour as the E♭-major statement at 69-72, which is entirely on the E string (F6-E♭6-D6). The two violin statements of the tune are therefore the same colour a third apart.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75: "In soft, lyrical, expressive passages, the composer will often prefer the higher pitches to be performed on the A-string rather than have the player cross to the E-string, where these upper notes take on a different, more brilliant timbre" (Ex. III-7, Brahms 3/iii, "sul A, p espress.", which I checked on the image: the line stays on the A string up to about D6). He also says the A string "loses some of its brilliance in the upper positions" (PDF 75), which here helps the oboe in unison blend with the violins instead of sitting on top of an E-string tone. PDF 73: timbre is the reason to name a string. c211 and c286 used the same argument for sul D at 123-126.

**Why it matters for the melody.** F2's recall in the recapitulation is the theme's last lyrical statement before the coda. On the A string its peak is veiled and covered, so it sounds like a remembered version of the bright 67-74, not a transposed copy. The shift E♭5 (188.4) to B♭5 (189.1) on one string is a fifth in 1st to 5th position, the kind of expressive shift Adler's Brahms example has.

**Fix (applied, recS.ily, recSVlnOne and recSVlnTwo):**
```
\transpose c aes, { \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul A" d'''2(\>\startTextSpan c'''4 b''4) } | ...
\transpose c aes, { g''4( f''4 e''4 d''4)\stopTextSpan } |
```
The spanner covers B♭5 down to B♭4 only; the A♭4s at 188, 193 and 196, which cannot be played on the A string, lie outside it, and the dashed line ends on the phrase's last note (the convention of 81, 123 and 235), so no "ord." is needed. 197-198 rise to E♭6 on the E string for the f crescendo into 199, as before. No note, slur, bowing (c085's down-bow at 198), dynamic or doubling changes, so the 187-198 protections (bow plan, scoring as of v225, strings and bassoons) are kept. The spanner prints cleanly above both violin staves on page 10. F2's notes are unchanged.

### 2. 178: the pizzicato cellos join the timpani's motive x (applied)

**Problem.** c291 put the scherzo's closing motive x into the gap between the recapitulated F2's phrases: timpani `g,4 g,8 g,8 g,4 r4` pp near the rim, while the cor anglais and muted trumpet hold D4. Near the rim and pp, a timpano gives little pitch. The cellos are playing pizz. G2 on that bar's downbeat anyway and then stop. The recalled figure, one of the symphony's cyclic cells, is heard as a soft knock, not as a figure on G.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 103: "The most widely used couplings are cello and double bass, cello and bassoon, cello and clarinet or bass clarinet, cello and horn, and pizzicato cello and timpani." Every other coupling on that list is in this score; pizzicato cello with timpani is not used anywhere in v301 (the timpani rest through every pizzicato passage, 51-66, 115-122 and 171-186, except this bar). The scherzo ends this way: its last timpani E is joined by pizz. E2 in the cellos and E1 in the basses (scherzo 375, coherence/scherzo.md). So the coupling quotes the sound of the scherzo's last bar as well as its rhythm.

**Fix (applied, recS.ily, recSVc, bar 178):** `g,4 r4 r2` → `g,4 g,8 g,8 g,4 r4` (pp from 171, pizz.). Same pitch as the timpani (G2), the root of the V under the held D4, so the harmony and the outer voices do not change (par8.py output for 170-198 is identical to v301's). The basses keep their single G1 (Adler PDF 105: a bass section alone is thin and slow to speak; doubling the eighths there would thicken the pp). The timpani line and the record line `motiveXretr Timp 178` are unchanged. The 171-186 protections (muted trumpet with cor anglais, pp accompaniment) are kept: the new notes are pp, in the bar where the soloists hold, and they repeat a pitch the cellos already play.

### 3. Open strings in expressive lines: no change

Adler PDF 73-74 warns that an open string stands out in a slow expressive line. Two places come close: the violas' counter-melody at 67 starts on G3, their open G string, but the cellos play the same G3 stopped on the D string and Hn 1 doubles it pp, so the open note is covered; and Vln II's portato F5-E5-F5 at 231-233 touches the open E5. In both, players take a stopped note on their own (fourth finger on the C string, fourth finger on the A string) when the line asks for it, and Adler says that, except for timbre, "the actual fingering is best left to the performer" (PDF 73). 231-234 is also protected (plagal close). **Decision: no change.**
