## Findings

### 1. 255–258: F2's last phrase sits in the tenor, inside the brass chorale, with no upper octave (applied)

**Problem.** Under the last chorale statement (fff/ff), F2's tonic-major phrase (`g2. e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4 | c'2. d'4 | g'4. f'8 e'4 d'4`, C3–G4) is played only by violas, cellos and Hn 3·4 a2, with Bn 1 from 257. These are the same register and bars as Tbn 1·2's held G3/E3 (255–256) and E4/C4 (257–258), Hn 1·2's E4/C4, Tpt 3's C4 and Bn 2's C3. Above them, Tpt 1·2 hold the chorale's G4 and E5, and Vln II and Cl 1 play eighth-note arpeggios across E4–A5. This is the second theme's last appearance. It is the trumpet's tune from 51, and the theme a listener should take away. Here it is a middle-register line covered by sustained brass in its own octave.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 54: a melody "in the middle of the orchestral range ... is not so prominent", and it must be detached by doubling, timbre or crossing. PDF 61 (h): middle-range melodies go to the violins in octaves with violas and cellos. PDF 63: an unequal distribution of the octaves does not matter. Ex. 8 (PDF 182) shows the same situation (a cantabile against held trombones and tuba) solved with the strings in octaves above the brass. c011 (the first Rimsky ch. II pass) put the violas and cellos on this phrase. It did not give the phrase an upper octave.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-371/work/sections/finale.ily, Vln II).** For 255–258 the second violins play the phrase an octave above the violas, ff espress., and return to the arpeggio at 259 (Cl 1 has carried it since 257):

F2's notes, the chorale top line, the harmony (I6–iii–vi–iiø6/5–Ger6–V7–I), the woodwind rest at 255–256, Vln I's tremolo and the cut at 264.1 are unchanged. The phrase now sounds in two octaves, G3–G4 and G4–G5, with Vln II on the D and A strings. At 258 its G5 rises above the trumpets' E5 but stays below the top octave of the chorale (E6, Fl 1·2 and Vln I), so nothing sits above the chorale top line (c127's rule). The cost is that the arpeggio is gone at 255–256, where Vln II was its only player. ### 2. 247–254: the first-theme head is in one high octave with a three-octave gap below (not applied; the protection's reason holds)

**Problem.** Vln I·II in unison with Fl 1·2 play movement I's head at G5–A6. The next statement down is Tbn 1·2, Bn 1 and the cellos at G2–A3. Rimsky PDF 59 and Note I (PDF 62) say a very high violin melody should be doubled an octave below.

**Decision: no change.** The octave below (G4–A5) is where the S head sits in Ob, Tpt, Cl, Hn and violas. The composer moved Vln II up at 247–254 in c049 (finale.ily comment: at G4–A5 the P head's lower octave crossed the S head) so that the two heads do not cross. That reason answers Rimsky's rule: putting back the lower octave would merge the two themes the combination keeps apart. The passage is also on the do-not-touch list (combination 247–254).

### 3. 37–38: F1's B♭-minor head in the cellos alone among the strings (not applied)

**Problem.** The third head of P2 (B♭3, ff, tenor clef) is in the cellos and Hn 3·4 a2. Tbn 1·2's f chords (F3–D♭3, C3–B♭2) are in its octave. Rimsky PDF 57 (c) and Ex. 15 suggest violas + cellos in unison for a fuller tenor line.

**Decision: no change.** The violas and Bn 1 are the only lower octave (F4) of the violins' f eighth-note figure at F6. If the violas moved to the head, the figure would keep one bassoon under violins that are isolated two octaves higher (PDF 59). The horns a2 ff already double the head an octave above, which is Rimsky's own remedy for a tenor line (doubling above, PDF 54).

### 4. 187–194: layout of F2's A♭ statement (checked, no change)

The violins in unison over the violas is Rimsky's layout "for greater brilliance in the upper part" (PDF 60). His alternative for a "fuller and more cantabile" lower part is Vln I over Vln II + violas. That layout would suit a dolce statement. The passage is protected (c074, c273, bow plan 187–198), and c355's "sul A" veiling depends on both violin sections on the A string. I keep it.
