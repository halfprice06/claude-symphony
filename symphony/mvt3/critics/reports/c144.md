## Findings

### 1. 83–86: the horns crescendo into the f downbeat and stop; F1's head has no sustained harmony but the bassoons (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 1·2 hold the pad 75–82 and crescendo through 82 (c114). At 83 they rest, and stay out until 89. For four bars at f all five upper winds (Fl a2, Ob 1, Cl a2) and both violin sections play F1's head in three octaves. The harmony is left to the violas' eighths, the bass arpeggio and two bassoons. The horns' crescendo leads into silence.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 234: horns are "often … assigned pedal dominant or tonic notes" in the wind choir. PDF 238–241: in forte tuttis the winds and horns "strengthen the harmony, providing pedals which gave a strong, continuous, and solid quality". Ex. VIII-8 against VIII-9 (Mozart K. 201, images) shows "the lack of stability and grandeur without those sustained tones". PDF 268: keep the brass below the strings and winds. B♭ fits every chord of 83–86: I, V4/2, V6/5 and I at 84, V7 at 85, I6 at 86.
**Fix (applied, clos.ily).**
```
closHnOne = { bes'1~\mf | bes'1~ | bes'1~ | bes'2 r2 | R1*2 | r4 aes'4->\mf\< …
closHnTwo = { bes1~\mf | bes1~ | bes1~ | bes2 r2 | R1*2 | r4 f'4->\mf\< …
```
The dominant pedal B♭4/B♭3 is held mf under the f tune and released with the tune at 86.2. The horns' crescendo from 82 now ends on a note. They still come back fresh at 89, and the brass entry at 91 is unchanged.

### 2. 81–82.2: Ob 1 sinks into its lowest fifth (F4–D4–E♭4) during the diminuendo, doubling Vln II sul G (applied)
**Problem.** From 77 Ob 1 doubles Vln II at the unison. In the animando diminuendo of 81 it goes down with them to F4–D4 and holds E♭4 at 82. These are the bottom notes of the oboe, under a diminuendo and on top of the colour the composer asked for in Vln II ("sul G").
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 270, Ex. VIII-41 (Copland, image): Copland "orchestrates a diminuendo by cutting out the first oboe … for it honks too much in that extreme low register". Ch. 7, PDF 198: in the lowest fifth the oboe "becomes thick and tends to 'honk' … one should never require a pianissimo dynamic in this register". Fl 1 already carries the Vln I doubling here.
**Fix (applied, sTwo.ily).**
```
sTwoOboeOne = { R1*10 | es''2.(\p\< f''4 | bes''4.\f aes''8 g''4 f''4) \breathe | es''2.( d''4 | c''4 bes'4 aes'4 g'4)\> | r1\! | r2 g'8(\mf\< aes'8 bes'8 d''8) | }
```
The oboe re-enters for the run G4–D5 into 83 (its good range) after six beats of rest. The dotted peak at 78 is unchanged.

### 3. 58→59: the half cadence doubles the leading tone D in octaves, and D3/D4 rise together to E♭ (applied)
**Problem.** At 58 (V, trumpet holding F4) Bn 1 holds D3 under Cl 1's D4 and Vln II's pizz. D4. At 59 both winds go to E♭ (D3→E♭3, D4→E♭4). Cl 1 rests on beat 4, but the ear still hears the leading tone in octaves resolving in octaves. c092 removed exactly this at 65 (Bn 1 D3–B♭2). The recapitulation (178, leading tone B2 in Bn 1 only) is already clean, so the exposition is the only statement with the fault.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257: give each pitch so as "not to upset the balance of the chord or the flow of the melody". A&S PDF 76: the leading tone "should never be doubled when" the tonic follows. A&S PDF 83: parallel octaves. I checked c092's other remedy (D3–B♭2): B♭2→E♭3 against the trumpet's F4→B♭4 makes compound fifths, so I used F.
**Fix (applied, sOne.ily).** `sOneBsnOne = { … | es1 | d2 f2 | es1 | …}`. Bn 1 moves D3–F3 and falls F3→E♭3 by step. Cl 2's new quarter line at 58 and the protected trumpet part are untouched.

### 4. 97–98: Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I hold C5 in unison, p, at the end of the closing group (applied)
**Problem.** On the C7 (V7 of F) the top C5 is played by oboe, clarinet and first violins in unison through the diminuendo to p. The third E is only in Cl 2.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 235: oboe and clarinet in unison is the doubling every book warns against, because the oboe covers the clarinet and "clarinet and oboe will have great difficulty staying in tune". On a held p note that is the main risk. Adler admits Schubert's use as a deliberate melodic colour, but this is a held pitch in a fade. PDF 257: unison doubling "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch", which a p chord does not need. PDF 270: the diminuendo is scored by removing a doubling. I checked the alternative (Cl 1 G4): B♭4→G4 makes compound fifths with the bass E♭→C, so Cl 1 rests instead.
**Fix (applied, clos.ily).** `closClOne = { … | bes'1\mf\>~ | bes'1 | r1\! | r1 | }`. The C5 stays with Ob 1 and Vln I, and c052's undoubled seventh and c132's E♭–E line in Cl 2 are kept.
