## Findings

### 1. 168-169: the last horn chord before the muted second theme is played open; make it stopped (applied)
- **Problem.** At 167-170 the recapitulation's transition fades out (c108: brass first, then winds, strings last), and the trumpet puts in its mute for the second theme at 171. Hn 1·2 play C5/E♭4 f at 167. At 168 they take a fresh attack on A♭4/D4 (the French sixth A♭-C-D-F♯, c242), tie it into 169's V7♭9 and fade to p. That is f > p, the dynamic shape Adler gives to the stopped horn. The exposition's matching bars (49-50) have a stopped Hn 1 D4 in the last chord before the open trumpet solo (c247). The recapitulation drops the colour just where the solo comes back muted. As a result the change from open to muted brass at 171 has nothing in the horns to lead into it.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 308: stopped horn "is used for single notes or soft passages. It is also effective when one wishes to have the horn play sf>p", marked +. Ex. X-19 (PDF 308): Rimsky-Korsakov writes the same horn passage open mf and then stopped p. The open sound is followed by its covered, softer form, which is the 167 → 168 succession here. PDF 307: a stopped horn sounds different from a muted one, so the trumpet's straight mute at 171 (PDF 319) is a related colour, not a repeat. Ex. X-48 (PDF 320) is Mahler's open/muted pairing of one trumpet passage, which this finale already makes between 51 and 171. Each pitch is a fresh attack held under a tie, so this fits Adler's "single notes", and the hand stop needs no preparation time (PDF 307, "a fast switch").
- **Fix (recP.ily).** `recPHnOne … | c''1\f | aes'1\>-\stopped~ | aes'1\p | R1 | }` and `recPHnTwo … | es'1\f | d'1~\>-\stopped | d'1\p | R1 | }`. On the shared staff the + prints once, above the chord at 168. There is no "o" afterwards, because + applies to the marked note and its tie only, as at 49-50. Hn 1's next entry is the open "dolce" at 187, 17 bars later. Pitches, rhythm, f at 167 and p at 169 are unchanged, and nothing at 171-186 (protected) changes. This undoes no earlier fix: c108's order of the diminuendo and c242's harmony stay.

### 2. 133-134: the development's last chord fades from an unstated level to nothing (applied)
- **Problem.** After the f stroke on 132.1, the V4/2 of C (since v184: Vln I G5, Vln II D5-B4, violas D4, cellos and basses F) is held at 133-134 under a diminuendo that ends on a closing `\!` in the rest. Hn 3·4 re-enter at 133 after one and a half beats of rest on D4/B3 with only a `\>` and no level. The player cannot tell where the horns start, or where the strings should arrive before the retransition's pp at 135.
- **Evidence.** Adler ch. 10 gives every brass diminuendo both ends: Ex. X-11 (PDF 305) p > pp; Ex. X-25 (PDF 310) f > p; Ex. X-40 (PDF 316) > p; Ex. X-44 (PDF 317) ff > p; Ex. X-48 (PDF 320) mf > pp; Ex. X-13 (PDF 305) fff, then p. MOLA PDF 8 (and c268 at 269): a dynamic is restated at an entry after rests.
- **Fix (dev.ily).** `devHnThree … | d'4-> r4 r2 | d'2.\f\> r4\! | R1 | }`, `devHnFour … | b2.\f\> r4\! | R1 | }`. In the strings the hairpin lands on p at 134: `devVlnOne … | g''1\>~ | g''2.\p r4 | }`, `devVlnTwo … | d''1\> | b'2.\p r4 | }`, `devVla … | d'1\>~ | d'2.\p r4 | }`, `devVc … | f1\>~ | f2.\p r4 | }`, `devCb … | f,1\>~ | f,2.\p r4 | }`. The ladder then runs f (132) > p (134) to pp (135), so the retransition's pp is a further step down, not a cut-off. Notes unchanged. I checked page 7 of the build: the marks print clearly and nothing collides.

### 3. Techniques of the chapter not applied, and why (no change)
- **Muted horns (PDF 307, Ex. X-17, X-18).** The only unprotected soft horn chord long enough for a mute is 95-98 (Hn 3·4 p > pp under the oboe), and it follows the ff stroke on 94.1-2 with half a bar to spare. Adler PDF 307 says the hand, not a mute, is used for a fast switch. A stopped chord there would copy the oboe's colour (Rimsky PDF 107) under movement II's quoted tail. Not applied.
- **Rimsky's open-then-stopped echo of a phrase (Ex. X-19)** would fit Hn 1's p closing head at 139-140 after the f statements. That is inside "horns 138-150" (protected, c255) and directly after the stopped stop chord at 138, so it would repeat that effect at once. Not applied.
- **Cuivré, bells up, horn glissando, horn trills (PDF 308-311).** These are for "very loud and boisterous" or comic passages. The finale's loud peaks are the protected fff points 223, 263, 278, where the models (Brahms 1/iv, Dvořák 9/iv) use none of these. Not applied.
- **sfp < in the brass at 209 with the woodwinds' fp < (Ex. X-54).** It would sharpen the approach to 211, but 205-210 and the 205-223 dynamic path are protected (c238, c178, c225) and four critics have already rescored those bars. Not applied.
