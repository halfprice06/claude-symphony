## Findings

### 1. 187-196: the A-flat statement puts Vln II in its weakest register and the violas on their C string (applied)
Problem. Since v74 the major statement is a fifth lower than at 67. Vln II plays the tune's lower octave from E-flat4 down to A-flat3 and B-flat3 (192-194, marked sul G), the bottom of the instrument. The violas double the cellos' counter-melody in unison at C3-A-flat3, on their C string. Under them, Bn 1-2 a2 on A-flat2 and the basses arco mf. The lower strings are in their good registers and the violins are not, which is the bass-heavy case Adler describes.
Evidence. Adler PDF 127: Haydn gives the line to the cello and not the second violin, because "the second violin would be in its weakest register". PDF 134 (Ex. V-11): the viola in its low register "is quite nondescript and, though mellow, lacks intensity". PDF 151, point 1: with violas, cellos and basses "in a much better register than the violins", a loud setting is "very bass-heavy". PDF 141 (Ex. V-18, Schumann 2/iii): violins in a weak register are doubled by both sections to give them body. PDF 152 (Ex. V-28, 3a): unison of both violin sections strengthens a melody.
Decision. I keep A-flat (c073's form decision) and the harp's rest (c063). I do not add a wind pad: Adler PDF 132-135 (Brahms 3/iii) shows that three elements work without filling when each one is in its best register. I re-assign the strings instead.
Fix (applied, recS.ily):
- Vln II 187-196 play the tune in unison with Vln I: `recSVlnTwo = { R1*16 | \transpose c aes, { g''2.(\mf^\markup \italic "arco, unis. with Vln I" e''4) } | ... | \transpose c aes, { d''4( c''4 e''4 g''4)\< } | \transpose c aes, { c''2. d''4\upbow } | es''1\f\downbow | }` (197-198 unchanged; the "sul G"/"ord." marks go).
- Violas 187-196 take the old Vln II line (E-flat4 to B-flat3, their G and D strings), with Hn 1 (unchanged) doubling them: `\transpose c aes, { g'2.(\mf^\markup \italic "ord., espress." e'4) } | ... | \transpose c aes, { d'4( c'4 e'4 g'4) }`, then rejoin the cellos' counter at 197: `\transpose c aes, { g2(\< c'4 d'4) } | c'1\f`.
- Cellos keep the counter-melody alone (unchanged).
The tune is now Vln I+II at E-flat5 with Vla+Hn 1 an octave lower; the counter is in the cellos; the bass is unchanged. F2's notes are unchanged (no theme change to report).

### 2. 171-180: Cl 1's C4/B3 line lies inside the cor anglais and muted trumpet's register (applied)
Problem. The solo (cor anglais + muted Tpt 1) moves between C4 and G4 in 171-180 and lands on C4 at 172, 177 and 180. Cl 1 holds C4/B3 throughout, so the background meets the melody at the unison; at 177 Cl 1, Cl 2 (C4 since c032) and the solo are all on C4. Cl 1's line is the same as Bn 1's an octave lower, so it adds no harmony. c063 removed Vln II's pizz. C4-F4 for this same reason (inside the cor anglais's low fifth) and left Cl 1 there.
Evidence. Adler PDF 135: "staying away from the register of the melody line ... especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color, this is good advice"; the background goes to "the palest or most nondescript registers". PDF 128: Haydn balances by register "so that the desired voice dominates", not by dynamic marks (here the pp is doing the work).
Fix (applied): `recSClOne = { R1*10 | c'1\pp | c'2 b2 | c'1 | c'2 b2 | g1 | aes1 | R1*12 | }`. Cl 1 returns at 181, where the solo is at C5-G5, above it. The harmony stays complete: Bn 1 C3/B2, Cl 2 E-flat3-G3, violas E-flat3-A-flat3, pizz. Vc/Cb.

### 3. 257-258: five brass parts on the chorale's E4, two of them re-attacking it, and the chord's third almost missing (applied)
Problem. At 257 (vi, A-C-E, under the last chorale's E) the brass have E5 in Tpt 1-2; E4 tied in Hn 1, Hn 2 and Tbn 1; and E4 accented half notes in Hn 3 and Tpt 3. The root A is in Tuba, B.tbn and Hn 4; the third C only in Tbn 2 (C3). The re-attacked E4s break the melody's held note in the same octave, and the brass chord sounds as an open fifth A-E.
Evidence. Adler PDF 135: the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the figure undisturbed". PDF 152 and Ex. V-27: "special attention should be given to the spacing and doubling" when there are more than four voices.
Fix (applied, finale.ily): Hn 3 at 257-258 `c'2-> c'2 | c'2-> c'2 |` (was `e'2-> e'2` twice). Hn 3's line becomes E-C-F-E-flat-G; Tpt 3 keeps its rising E-F-F-sharp-G. The chorale top line and the 255-264 trumpet unison are unchanged.

### 5. 247-254 and 255-264: coda layout (checked; only Finding 3 changed)
At 247-254 the P head is in two registers (Fl/Vln I-II at G5-A6; Tbn 1-2, Bn 1, Vc at G2-A3), with the S head in the middle (Tpt, Hn 1-2, Ob, Cl, Vla) in long notes against the P head's eighths. This meets all four of Adler's conditions for contrapuntal clarity (PDF 144). After the trombone change (c071) and with the bass trombone resting (c072), the tuba alone carries the bass at 247-254; the Cb eighths double it, which is enough. At 255-256 (I6) the doubled third E sits at the bottom (Tuba E2, B.tbn/Vc E3), which is what PDF 152 recommends. 262-264 (Tpt 1-2 unison, Tpt 3 on E) is well spaced.
