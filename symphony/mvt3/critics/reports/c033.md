## Answer to the focus questions

**Exposition to development.** The length is right. A 148-s exposition without a repeat, followed by 74 s of development and retransition, keeps the movement moving after the scherzo, and the ratio is close to Dvořák 9/iv. What is wrong is the content of the development's middle (Finding 1).

**The coda.** It builds in tempo: 84 (199), 72 (211), 56 (223), 66 (235), stringendo, 100 (247). It builds in dynamics through three fff points: 223, 263 and 278. The one dip is 230-238 (plagal close mf, then the S head at p). It follows the model of Dvořák 9/iv's quiet reminiscence before the last Allegro, and it is protected (cycles 17, 19, 28), so I leave it. The new G-F♯-F-E close (273-276) does not sag: it is the densest moment of the movement (28 voices on average), with a crescendo from 275 into the fff of 278. The one place where the coda stands still is 255-258 (Finding 2).

## Findings

### 1. Development 115-130: F2 stated twice as soft solos repeats the exposition's plan, and the harp solo copies the scherzo (applied)

**Bars.** 115-130.

**Problem.** After 16 bars of real development (99-114), the development states F2 whole twice at pp:
- in G minor, bassoon solo over pizzicato (115-122);
- in G major, "harp solo, cantabile" with strings pp (123-130).

This is the exposition's S-group unit again (F2 in minor at 51, then in major at 67), and the recapitulation repeats it a third time (171, 187). The density profile shows the development's middle as the thinnest passage of the movement: 2.0-6.5 voices at 113-128. The crescendo does not start until 129. So the development's tension falls in its centre instead of rising.

There is also a new problem caused by the four-movement plan. dev.ily calls the harp's line "its first melodic line in the symphony (cyclic-notes 'Still unused')". That is no longer true. The scherzo's development (scherzo v12, sB.ily 73-80) already has "the harp [with] the theme head as a melody... (still unused colour: a melodic harp line)", without strings, in a key a step from its tonic. About five minutes later, the finale's development repeats the same device at the same point in the form.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 116: the scoring must follow from whether a passage is "a complement to or a contrast with what goes before and comes after, whether it forms a climax or merely a step". A development's middle should be a step towards the retransition, not another exposition-style statement.
- PDF 136: a colour "gains fresh interest upon its reappearance" only after a long silence. Within one symphony, the harp melody is now spent.
- PDF 125-126 (passing a phrase between instruments), 128 (law of register) and 130 (crescendo by adding strings, then woodwind, then brass) give the tools for a building version.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-033/work/sections/dev.ily).** I kept the bassoon solo (115-122) as the development's one soft statement. At 123-130 the same F2 pitches climb through the orchestra with a crescendo by addition. Each new instrument enters on the previous one's last note. The harp keeps its arpeggios as accompaniment.
```
devHarpR  ... R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4 |            % melody removed; devHarpL arpeggios and pedal diagram unchanged
devVc 123-124:  d'2.(\p^\markup \italic "arco, espress." b4 | a4 g4 b4 d'4) | e1\pp | c1 | a,1 | d1 | g1\< | d1 |
devVla 124-127: c'2. d'4(\p | g'2. fis'4 | e'2 d'2) | e'1\pp | c'1 | b1\< | ...   % 127 E4 (was C4) avoids octaves D-C with Cl 1
devClOne 126-128: r2 d''2(\p\< | c''2.\mf e''4 | d''4 c''4 b'4 a'4) | R1*2 |
devFluteOne 129-130: b''2(\mf\< g''2 | a''1) |   then a''1\f at 131 as before
devVlnOne 128-130: r2 r4 a''4(\mf\< | b''2 g''2 | a''1) |  then r4 a''8(\f ... at 131 as before
```
The order of entries is Vc (123), Vla (124), Cl 1 (126), Hn 1 (127, existing pp), then Fl 1 and Vln I (129). That is strings, woodwind, brass, rising an octave at each change of instrument, into the f cadence at 131.

**For the other threads.** F2's notes and harmony are unchanged; only the scoring of the development statement changes. The harp no longer has a melody in the finale. The cyclic-notes item "Still unused: harp melody" is now fulfilled by the scherzo (73-80). The finale's dev.ily header comment should say so.

### 2. Last chorale 255-262: an ff plateau with a four-bar hairpin; use amplification (applied)

**Bars.** 255-263.

**Problem.** At 247-254 everyone plays ff with active figuration (258 attacks per four bars). At 255 everyone moves to the chorale's whole notes, still ff, and the activity drops (154, then 106 attacks). The crescendo starts only at 259. So 255-258 is four bars (about 5 s) of lower activity at the same level, directly before the movement's main cadence. In the coda this is the one place where the music stands still instead of building.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 127: "While the first group effects the crescendo gradually, the second group enters piano or pianissimo and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes."
- PDF 130: brass is the group that turns "crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes".
- PDF 121: the brass alone reach "an extraordinary volume", so the ff chorale does not need the woodwind at 255.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-033/work/sections/finale.ily).** Every woodwind part rests at 255-256, after eight bars of ff heads. All of them re-enter at 257 on their existing notes, p with one hairpin to the existing fff at 263. The later `\<` at 259 is removed from these parts. Brass, strings and timpani are unchanged: ff from 255, hairpin from 259. Example (Fl 1):
```
finaleFluteOne 255-263: R1*2 | e'''1\p\<~ | e'''1 | d'''1~ | d'''1 | c'''1 | b''1 | c'''1\fff~
```
The same pattern applies to Fl 2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, B.cl., Bn 1·2 and Cbsn. Shared staves get equal dynamics. The chorale top line (Tpt 1, Vln I) is untouched, as are fff only at 223/263/278 and the 261-263 cadence. The flutes' octave doubling of the top line now starts at 257 instead of 255.
