## Findings (ranked)

### 1. F1 never sounds in C major; the finale's own first theme is missing from the apotheosis (243–262)
**Problem.** F1 is the theme a listener would hum on the way out, but its last appearance is the head over the A♭ pedal at 205–208. From 219 to 262 the material is the chorale, movement I's second-theme head (231, 235, 243, 247: four statements in twenty bars) and movement I's P head. A listener leaves with movement I's tunes and the chorale. The finale's own theme stays in minor and is not confirmed at the end.
**Evidence.** Dvořák 9/iv brings its main theme back in major in the coda's trumpets (corpus file above, bar 10 against about bar 333), and Tchaikovsky 5/iv does the same. In 256–259 of v01 the horns and trombones rest (R1*4) while the trumpets hammer C–(IV6/4)–C and the violins play fragments of movement I's head. A&S PDF 336 (20-15): accented passing and neighbouring tones are displacements of chord tones, and this is how F1's second bar fits over the trumpets' IV6/4: the B♭ on the downbeat of 257 is an accented upper neighbour of the A on beat 2, and the F on beat 3 is an accented passing tone to E.
**Fix (applied).** F1's first two bars in C major, twice, in Hn 1·2 (a2), with Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 an octave below, ff, 256–259. The B♭ keeps F1's own pitch (the minor theme's B♭) as mixture over IV, as the plagal close at 227–230 does. Owed item 6 (243–250) and the violins' link use of movement I's head are unchanged. In finale.ily, replace `R1*4 | c''1->~\ff` (finaleHnOne; HnTwo the same with `g'1`) with
`c''2->(\ff g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | c''2->( g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | c''1->~`
and in finaleHnThree, HnFour, TbnOne, TbnTwo (keeping each held note at 260: `e'`, `c'`, `c'`, `g`):
`c'2->(\ff g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | c'2->( g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | e'1->~`

### 2. F1's consequent has no climax of its own; 22 is bar 14 an octave higher, and the line turns back up (19–26, 159–166)
**Problem.** Bars 19–22 repeat 11–14 an octave higher (only 21 changes G to F for the diminished seventh). The peak F6 at 22 has the same shape (`f2-> es4 d4`) and harmony (V6/5) as F5 at 14, so the consequent has no new high point. After the D at the end of 22 the line goes back up to E♭ at 23 (E♭–D–C), which breaks the descent to the cadence.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 337 (sec. 10, 20-18): a figurated melody is read by its large-scale line. PDF 332 (20-8): a descent to the goal tone needs stepwise continuity. In v01 the large-scale line of 22–23 is F–E♭–D | E♭–D–C, two overlapping three-note descents. Brahms 1/iv's tune and Elgar's big tunes put the consequent's peak on a note the antecedent never reached.
**Fix (applied).** Bar 22 (and 162): `aes'''2-> g'''4( f'''4)` in place of `f'''2-> es'''4( d'''4)`, at each doubling's octave. A♭6 is heard nowhere else in F1 and is the ♭9 of the V6/5 (a chord tone after the diminished-seventh arpeggio of 21). The line now runs A♭–G–F | E♭–D–C, 6̂ to 1̂ by step across 22–23. Changed in pOne (VlnOne, VlnTwo, Vla, HnOne, HnTwo, FluteOne, OboeOne) and in recP (FluteOne, FluteTwo, OboeTwo, ClOne, ClTwo, HnOne, HnTwo, VlnOne, VlnTwo, Vla). Oboe 1 in recP keeps `f'''2-> es'''4( d'''4)` at 162, a third below the flutes, because A♭6 is beyond the oboe.

### 3. F2 is written in chorale style: no figuration, no accented dissonance at its peak (51–82, 171–198)
**Problem.** F2 uses only dotted halves, halves and quarters. Every odd bar is `2. 4`, and every note is a chord tone or an unaccented passing tone. Its surface is the same as the brass chorale's (219), which removes the contrast between the lyrical second theme and the chorale it should prepare. The peak of the first phrase (E♭5 at 53) arrives on a plain chord tone after a leap of a fourth.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 334: compound neighbour figures are idiomatic "in many styles. Not in chorale style." PDF 338 (sec. 12, 20-20): the appoggiatura, approached by leap and resolved down by step, "occurs frequently, but not in chorale style". PDF 336: "a dissonance is emphasized when it is preceded or followed by a leap". PDF 328: Schumann's melody gets its character from "emphasized dissonances". Dvořák 9/iv's and Tchaikovsky 5/iv's second themes both lean on accented non-chord tones.
**Fix (applied).** An appoggiatura on the peak of the first phrase, in all four statements. The leap B♭→F (a fifth) lands on F over iv6 (A♭–C♭–E♭ over C♭), against the E♭ in Vln II and Cl 1, and resolves to E♭: `f''2 es''4 des''4` for `es''2. des''4` (sOneTptOne, bar 53). sTwoVlnOne/VlnTwo bar 69: `f'''2 es'''4 d'''4` / `f''2 es''4 d''4` over IV6. recS bar 173 (CorAnglais and TptOne): `d''2 c''4 bes'4`. recS bar 189 (VlnOne/VlnTwo): `d'''2 c'''4 b''4` / `d''2 c''4 b'4`, against Cl 1's C. I did not do this at 63, 79 or 183: there the note before is the same F, not a leap, and A&S (PDF 338) defines the appoggiatura by its leap approach.

### 4. F1 uses three bar-rhythms in a fixed two-bar order, and its eighths are covered by the clarinets (11–26)
**Problem.** The bars run `2 4. 8` / `4 8 8 4 4` / `4 8 8 4 4` / `2 4 4` four times. The `4 8 8 4 4` bar is 8 of the 16. The arpeggio bars (13, 17, 21) and the stepwise bars (12, 16, 20) have the same rhythm, so nothing marks the rise to the antecedent's peak (A♭5 at 17). The clarinets' unbroken eighths in every bar sound under the melody's eighths at the same moments.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 329 (20-2a): chordal skips "intensify rhythmic activity" and "connect contrasting registers in a widely ranging melody". PDF 336 (sec. 9): figuration in different voices should be complementary, not coincident.
**Fix (applied, melody only).** Bar 17 (and 157): the F-minor arpeggio in eighths to a held A♭, `f'8( aes'8 c''8 f''8 aes''2)` for `f'4( aes'8 c''8 f''4 aes''4)` (each part at its octave), so the antecedent's peak is a half note reached by a rush of eighths, then G (18). Applied in pOne (VlnOne, VlnTwo, Vla, HnOne, HnTwo) and in all eleven recP parts with the tune. Recommended, not applied: in pOneClOne/ClTwo, bars 12, 16, 20 and 24, replace the second-half eighths with a held half on the same top note (for example bar 12 `d'8( f' bes' f') f'2`), so the melody's eighths on beat 2 are heard alone.

### 5. F2's stepwise quarter descents (56, 64) could carry the nineteenth-century turn
**Problem.** Bars 56 and 64 (`bes'4 aes'4 ges'4 f'4`, `ces''4 bes'4 aes'4 ges'4`) are plain scale segments in quarters, the least distinctive bars of the theme.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 338–339 (sec. 13, 20-21b): in nineteenth-century music the turn becomes a five-note figure (main tone, upper neighbour, main tone, lower neighbour, main tone), as in Mahler.
**Fix (recommended, not applied, to keep this cycle's changes to F2 to one).** Bar 64 (and 80, 184): `ces''4 bes'8( ces''8 bes'8 aes'8) ges'4`, i.e. a turn on B♭ in place of the plain B♭–A♭. In the trumpet at p this lies well.
