## Findings

**1. Tested and declined: an applied V6/5 of A♭ on 34.4 (A&S PDF 444–445, §3, Ex. 26-3; PDF 445, sequences "using applied V's").**

*Bars:* 34–35.

*What I tested:* The sequence in the counterstatement has an applied chord only before its second member (36.4, V6/5 of B♭ into 37). Its first member, A♭ at 35, follows a general rest on 34.4 after G. I tested the reading's model of an applied chord before the goal. In /tmp/critic-463/work/sections/pTwo.ily, bar 34 beat 4 became V6/5 of A♭ (E♭–G–B♭–D♭ over G):
- Ob 1: `d''2. des''4 | c''1`
- violas: `g'2.-> es'4`
- cellos: `g'2.-> bes4`
- basses: `g2.-> g,4`

The violins' grace notes E♭–F–G into 35 fit the chord.

*Build:* 0 warnings and 16 pages. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels; the doubling runs fell from 3 to 2 because the general rest is gone. par8.py on 33–36 gives the same output as v463.

*Why it is not better:*
- A&S PDF 447–448 (Ex. 26-7): Mozart's twice-stated V6/5→VI is a deceptive motion, and its repetition foreshadows the transient key. The score already does this. V→VI to A♭ at 26 and again at 34→35 prepares the A♭ statement at 187 (after the V→VI at 186). An applied dominant turns the second V→VI into an expected tonicization and breaks that chain.
- A&S PDF 445: the sequence as a whole forms the transition, and one directional element is enough. Here that element is the A♮ at 36.4.
- The rest on 34.4 ends the bass statement of F1 (27–34) as F1's own phrases end, and the ff A♭ entry of the trombones and tuba at 35 depends on it.
- The new chord also puts a close ff seventh chord (G2, B♭3, E♭4) in the low strings.

*Decision:* not applied, and the scratch change is not proposed.

**3. The dominant prolongation 115–150 has no VI area: no change (A&S PDF 456, §15, Ex. 26-17).**

*Bars:* 115–150.

*Evidence:* The reading's typical prolonged V reaches an upper-neighbour VI. Here the upper neighbour A♭ appears only inside the stop chord (138) and in the 139–142 9–8 of Hn 4 (c172). A tonicized A♭ would have to fall within 135–146, where the stretto is protected (c255, c468), or within 123–130, where the hand-over is protected. A fourth A♭ area would also weaken 187 and 205, which already hold VI.

**4. Arrivals in new keys: no change (A&S PDF 445, §4).**

*Bars:* 82–83, 113, 131–132, 210–211, 262–263.

*Evidence:* Each new tonic is reached by a line moving to 1 or 3 with a rhythmic resolution. At 210–211, G♭6 is held over the barline and falls to F6 (3 of D♭) on 211.2, which is the reading's "rhythmic resolution". Since c462, the cadence at 82–83 has its iv6–V6/4–V.

**5. Melodic tonicization over pedals: no change (A&S PDF 446, §5).**

*Bars:* 43–50, 205–208, 265–266.

*Evidence:* Over the B♭ pedal, motive x passes from the flutes (A♭: V7 of E♭) to the clarinet (G♭) and then the oboe (D♭ and C♭). The figuration therefore moves the held B♭ from V of E♭ major to V of E♭ minor before the trumpet solo, which is the device the reading describes. 205–208 does the same for D♭.

**6. Coda pacing: no change (A&S PDF 451–452, §11; corpus).**
