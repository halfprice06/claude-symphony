## Findings

### 1. 255–256: the held trombones sit in the register of F2's last phrase, at f (applied)

**Problem.** At 255 the last chorale statement starts. The violas, cellos and Hn 3·4 play F2's third phrase ff, and Vln II plays it an octave higher: G3 (dotted half), E3 | D3–C3–E3–G3 (coherence/finale.md, the 255–264 entry). Under it Tbn 1 holds G3 and Tbn 2 holds E3 at f (finale.ily, Tbn 1·2 at 255–256). These are the same pitches the phrase starts on and returns to. A held brass note in a melody's own register absorbs that melody's notes into the chord, so for two bars the phrase is heard only through its moving notes D3–C3. The other held brass do not overlap it: Hn 1·2 (E4/C4–B3) and Tpt 3 (C4) are above, and the bass trombone and tuba (E2) are below.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 147 and Ex. XV-1 (PDF 148): D'Indy marks the trombones ppp inside a f sempre unison "so as not to dominate the entire orchestra". The image shows the same contrast as the text: trombones ppp, everyone else f. Adler PDF 154: a foreground needs a background that is distinct from it. Here it is distinct in colour but not in register or level. The trombones have just played F1's head at f (247–254) and hold straight on into the chord at 255, so their only difference from the foreground is one dynamic level.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-484/work/sections/finale.ily).** Tbn 1·2 enter the chorale chord mf. Their existing crescendo from 257 to ff at 259 is unchanged.
```
finaleTbnOne: … g2.-> r4 | g1\mf~ | g1 | e'1~\< | e'1 \breathe | d'1\ff~ …   (was g1\f~)
finaleTbnTwo: … g2.-> r4 | e1\mf~ | e1 | c1~\< | c1 \breathe | aes1\ff~ …   (was e1\f~)
```
This keeps the shared staff's simultaneous dynamics equal. Unchanged: notes, the chorale top line (Tpt 1·2 ff), the bass trombone's and tuba's ff, Hn 1·2 and Tpt 3 at f, the 259 ff and the 263 fff. It continues the direction of v322, which lowered the held brass from ff to f; it does not reverse it. ### 2. 269–271: Ob 2 in its low fifth (E4, D4) inside the octave tutti (tested, declined)

**Problem considered.** In F1's continuation in octaves, Ob 2 doubles Vln II and the clarinets down to E4 at 270 and D4 at 271. Adler PDF 151 (Mozart 40/iv): oboes on low notes "would have added a raucous quality". Mozart places each instrument "exactly where" it fills the space.

**Candidate.** Ob 2 takes Ob 1's line a2 at 269–271, which is Mozart's m. 125 layout of oboes with the flute and Vln I.

**Why declined.** Ob 2 would then have to make the same octave drop at 272 (D6 → A5) that Ob 1 already makes. That drop is the very break Mozart's distribution avoids, and it is a break Ob 2 does not have now (its D5 → A5 at 271→272 is the line's own fifth). The candidate also takes the reed out of the middle octave. By the same Mozart lesson that octave should be the heavier one, because it carries the line into the trumpets at 271 and into 273. c952 kept these loud low oboe notes. No change.

### 3. 271: trumpets' low-register entry beside the trombones' S head (considered, declined)

**Problem considered.** At 271 Tpt 1·2 enter on D4 for F1's continuation. In the same bar Tbn 1·2 enter ff on the S head (G3–E4), so two brass pairs meet around E4. Barber 3 (Adler PDF 150) leaves heavy brass out so they do not take the weight off the high note.

**Why declined.** Bringing the trumpets in only at 272 (on A5) would reverse c071/c072. Their reason still holds: F1's continuation in major is the melodic goal of the close and needs the trumpets for both bars. The 272 peak is already carried by Fl 1·2 and Vln I at A6. No change.

### 4. 265–268: no held chord under the combination (considered, declined)

**Problem considered.** Smetana's held brass (Adler PDF 154, Ex. XV-5) bind a moving unison together. At 265–268 nothing is held: the S head is on top, F1's head is in the middle, and below there are only the C2 eighths and strokes.

**Why declined.** Adler gives the lighter texture without held notes as a valid choice ("a more brittle quality"), and these bars are the planned relief before 273 (principle 2). Every free instrument would lose a fresh entry if it held here: Hn 3·4 at 269, Tbn 1·2 at 271, and the bass trombone, contrabassoon and bass clarinet at 273 (principles 15 and 21). A trombone chord on E3–F3 would also collide with F1's head, which descends to E3 at 266 in the cellos, violas, Bn 1 and Hn 1·2. No change.

### 5. 266→267: octave rise in horns and trumpets (checked, no change)

Barber 1 (PDF 150) warns against octave skips in heavy brass. Here the octave rise is F1's sequence ("c g a b♭ a g f e" followed by "e b c d c b a g"), not a doubling choice. The trumpets cannot take the second statement in the lower octave (it would reach G3), and the horns would go below C3. No change.
