## What else I studied

Tchaikovsky 5/iv from memory and two program notes ([codaweb](https://codaweb.org/download/symphony-no-5-in-e-minor-op-64/), [interlude.hk](https://interlude.hk/pytor-ilyich-tchaikovsky-symphony-5-e-minor-op-64/), [FW Symphony](https://fwsymphony.org/program-notes/tchaikovsky-peter-ilyich-symphony-no-5-in-e-minor-opus-64)): the motto's major statement in the coda (Moderato assai e molto maestoso) is given to the whole string body with a triplet figure in the winds, and the movement then closes with a long tonic section that brings back the first movement's theme in major. I compared v19 with v09 line by line (all 16 section files, global.ily, the last four pages), and checked every change against HISTORY.md.

Holistic verdict: the plan is at the level of the model. The cyclic return, the withheld cadence, the chorale and the combination of heads are planned with Tchaikovsky's and Brahms's logic. What gives away a less experienced hand is scoring in three places where the colour does not carry the meaning: a song from the Adagio played by two bare trumpets, a final chorale in which the whole orchestra holds whole notes for ten bars, and a combination of themes in which all six upper woodwinds take one theme and none the other. A conductor would also notice that the tonic after the symphony's first root-position V–I lasts only seven bars (263–271, about 9 seconds), where Tchaikovsky's major close runs for minutes.

v09 → v19: nothing got worse. The changes since v09 (theme B augmented, last chorale at Allegro molto in doubled values, violas on the second-theme head, bowings, horn-call layout, p at 235) are all improvements or neutral. The one weakness that the v14 augmentation made larger is finding 1: in v09 the bare trumpets played theme B for 6.7 s, in v19 for about 13 s.

## Findings

### 1. 211–218: theme B from the Adagio is played by two trumpets with no doubling (applied)
Problem: the recall of movement II's theme B is a lyrical, slurred song. Since v14 it lasts eight bars. Trumpets 1·2 play it alone in thirds and sixths, ff, while the winds hold chords and the violins play a counter-line.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 73: brass "lack the capacity to express passion or geniality. Phrases charged with these sentiments become sickly and insipid when confided to the brass." PDF 74–75: a wood-wind added to a brass melody "softens and rarefies it" and "yields a finer legato effect"; Ex. 78 doubles legato brass lines with clarinets and bassoons. PDF 79 and Ex. 93–94: the natural three-group unison in loud passages is violin (or clarinet) plus oboe plus trumpet. Tchaikovsky never gives his song-like motto to bare brass; in the coda the whole string body plays it.
Fix (applied in codaTwo.ily): Ob 1 and Cl 1 double trumpet 1 at the unison; their held chord notes are still covered by Ob 2, Cl 2, the flutes and the horns. The dynamics match Ob 2 and Cl 2 on the shared staves.
```
codaTwoOboeOne = { aes'2->(\ff\> des''2 | f''2.\f ges''4 | aes''2 bes''4 aes''4 | ges''2. f''4 | es''2. f''4 | des''1~ | des''1~ | des''1) | dis''1\subP\< | e''1 | d''1 | d''1 | }
codaTwoClOne = { aes'2->(\ff\> des''2 | f''2.\f ges''4 | aes''2 bes''4 aes''4 | ges''2. f''4 | es''2. f''4 | des''1~ | des''1~ | des''1) | aes'1\subP\< | g'1 | g'1 | aes'1 | }
```
The violins' counter-line, the D♭–C–B–C top line and the 219 sub. p are unchanged.

### 2. 255–262: the last chorale is ten bars of held whole notes (applied in part)
Problem: at Allegro molto in doubled values, every wind, brass and violin part holds whole notes tied in pairs. Only the violas' quarters and the basses' eighths move. The climax of the symphony is static for about ten seconds.
Evidence: Rimsky Ex. 91 (Tsar Saltan 92), Ex. 98 (Saltan 135, Maestoso con moto) and Ex. 116 (Kitesh 167) all set a broad, sustained melody over brass, winds or strings that repeat the harmony in quarters. In Tchaikovsky 5/iv the major motto is held in the strings over a repeated triplet figure in the winds.
Fix (applied in finale.ily): horn 3 and trumpet 3 restate their chorale notes as marcato half notes on the alla-breve beat. They join the violas' quarters and the basses' eighths, so the texture has three rates of pulse under the held melody.
```
finaleHnThree (255–262) = e'2->\ff e'2-> | e'2-> e'2-> | e'2-> e'2-> | e'2-> e'2-> | f'2->\< f'2-> | f'2-> f'2-> | es'2-> es'2-> | g'2-> g'2-> |
finaleTptThree (255–262) = c'2->\ff c'2-> | c'2-> c'2-> | e'2-> e'2-> | e'2-> e'2-> | f'2->\< f'2-> | f'2-> f'2-> | fis'2-> fis'2-> | g'2-> g'2-> |
```
I first scored quarters and added Hn 4 and Tbn 2. Every version that put repeated low horn notes (A3, A♭3, with stems below the staff) or a second rhythm on the Tbn 1·2 staff made LilyPond report "too few pages: 16 (should have at least 17)". That is why Hn 4 and Tbn 2 stay held. 263–264, the cadence and the protected chorale top line are unchanged.

### 3. 247–254: all six upper woodwinds play the first-theme head and none plays the second (applied)
Problem: in the combination of the two heads of movement I, Fl 1·2 (unison), Ob 1·2 and Cl 1·2 (four players in one unison) all play the first-theme head. The second-theme head (Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2, violas) has no woodwind. The four-player middle unison (G4–A5) also lies in the same register as trumpet 1's E5–C6.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 67 (against "duplicating all the wood-wind"); PDF 79: "Violas (or 'Cellos) + Cl. (Eng. horn) + Horn" is one of the most natural three-group unisons; PDF 75: clarinets doubling horns give a richer tone. In Rimsky Ex. 64 and 65 a four-octave melody uses each pair of woodwinds once.
Fix (applied): Cl 1·2 (a2) double horns 1·2 and the violas on the second-theme head. The first-theme head keeps flutes, oboes and violins.
```
finaleClOne/Two (247–254) = e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8 | c''2 b'2) | e'2.~( e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 c'4 | b2 c'2) |
```
The pairwise check (tools/critic-scripts/c002/par.py) shows only octave doublings between the two heads. These existed before; the clarinets' clash simply moves from the horns to the flutes. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 4. 263–271: the tonic after the first V–I is too short (tested, not applied)
Problem: the symphony withholds a root-position V–I in C until 262→263. After it come two held bars, two 2-bar fragments (265–268), a cadence echo and the final chord: about 9 seconds. Tchaikovsky 5/iv follows the major motto with a Presto and a closing section that brings back movement I's theme in major. Brahms 1/iv has about 40 bars after its chorale.
Tested: (a) four bars 269–272 that repeat 265–268 with the heads exchanged (this movement's head on top in Fl/Ob/Cl/violins as `c'''2->( g''4. a''8 | bes''4-.) a''8( g''8 f''4-.) e''4-.`, movement I's head below in Hn/Tbn/Vla/Vc), sections.txt `finale 29 4`, global `s1*10 s1*10 s1`; (b) the same with two bars; (c) the exchange in place at 267–268 with no bars added. With the current layout all three produce LilyPond's "too few pages: 16 (should have at least 17)" warning, and changing the spacing (1.3→1.0 at 223, 0.9–1.2 at 247) did not help. Decision: under the 16-page cap this is not done in v20. The score keeps 263–271 as it is. I record it for the form lens (cycle 23) together with the page cost: the extension (a) is worth a 17th page only if that critic also finds no other way to reclaim a system.
