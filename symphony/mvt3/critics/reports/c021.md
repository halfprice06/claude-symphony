## Findings (ranked)

### 1. F1's only C-major statement has no trumpet, and the trumpets hammer C5 on top of its head note (265-268). Applied.
**Problem.** The movement's own first theme turns to C major once, at 265-268. It is in Hn 1·2 and violas at C5, and Hn 3·4, Tbn 1·2 and cellos at C4 (critic 011 added the strings). In the same bars Tpt 1·2 repeat `c''4-> r8 c''8 c''4-> r4` and `g'4 r8 g'8 ...`, ff. Tpt 1's repeated C5 sits exactly on F1's head note (`c''2->`) and its accents fall on beat 3, where the theme has its dotted figure `g'4. a'8`. The brightest brass instrument is therefore working against the tune the listener should take away. The upper woodwinds and violins play movement I's head only in bars 265 and 267 and rest in 266 and 268, so F1 is the main line there.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 72: after fanfares, brass suit "unmodulated diatonic" melodies, "rousing and triumphant in the major key"; F1 in C major is exactly that. PDF 74: brass of different kinds in octaves work in normal register order (Trumpet/2 Horns, Trumpet/Trombone, 2 Trumpets/2 Trombones), while horns above trombones in octaves is "not so reliable". Here horns are the top brass octave over the trombones. PDF 73 and Ex. 75 (PDF 246): kindred brass in unison give extreme power. PDF 79: Violin + Trumpet and Viola + Horn are the string-brass pairs that correspond.

**Fix (applied).** finale.ily: Tpt 1 and Tpt 2 play F1 a2 in unison with Hn 1·2 and violas for 265-268. Tpt 3 keeps the hammered E4, and the C pulse stays in Bsn 2, tuba, timpani and the basses' eighths.
- `finaleTptOne` 265-268: `c''2->(\ff g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. | c''2->( g'4. a'8 | bes'4-.) a'8( g'8 f'4-.) e'4-. |`
- `finaleTptTwo` 265-268: the same notes (a2).

With Tpt 1 alone on F1 and Tpt 2 hammering, the shared staff split into two voices and the score needed 17 pages (build warning "too few pages"). With both trumpets a2 it stays at 16 pages with no warnings. Bar 269 (`c''4-> r8 d''8 c''4->`) and the last chord are unchanged. The trumpets' B→C at 262-263 is not touched.

### 2. Theme B's single recall lies under the flutes' held notes and gets no octave doubling (211-218). Applied.
**Problem.** Movement II's theme B is recalled once, augmented, in Tpt 1 with Ob 1 and Cl 1 in unison (A♭4 to B♭5). Above it, Fl 1·2 hold `f'''1 | f'''1 | f'''1 | es'''1 | f'''1 | aes'''1 | f'''1~ | f'''1` and `des'''1 ...`, ff then f, and Vln I starts a separate line at D♭6. The recall has to be recognised after a whole movement (cycle 11's open item and cycle 13's augmentation were both about recognition). Here the highest sounds in 211-218 are held chord tones, and the theme is in the middle register.

**Evidence from the reading.** Rimsky PDF 75 (diagram checked as image): "To double a trumpet in the upper octave three or four wind instruments are required, but in the top register two flutes will suffice." The theme's octave above runs A♭5 to B♭6, the flute's top register. PDF 74-75: the Ob 1 + Cl 1 unison already gives the trumpet a finer legato; adding the flutes above completes the layout Rimsky prints. PDF 65: flutes on top keep the natural order.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily, Fl 1 and Fl 2 a2 an octave above Tpt 1:
- `codaTwoFluteOne` and `codaTwoFluteTwo` 211-218: `aes''2->(\ff\> des'''2 | f'''2.\f ges'''4 | aes'''2 bes'''4 aes'''4 | ges'''2. f'''4 | es'''2. f'''4 | des'''1~ | des'''1~ | des'''1) |`
219-222 are unchanged. The flutes now end 216-218 on D♭6 and go to C6 at 219 and B5 at 221. That puts the protected D♭–C–B–C line (205-222 path, cycle 10) in the top woodwind voice, which strengthens it. The dynamics (ff then f) match the trumpet's. The highest flute note is B♭6, below C7.
