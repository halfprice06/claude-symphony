## Findings

### 1. 182–183: the third of four identical G→A♭ deceptive moves in 40 bars (applied)

**Problem.** From the recapitulation to the D♭ episode the bass moves G→A♭ as V→VI at 165→166, 182→183, 185→186 and 204→205. All four land on the same A♭ chord. Three of them also have the top voice B→C or D→C. In the exposition the same places land on different chords (26 on A♭, 63 on C♭) and are separated by an authentic cadence (66). In the recapitulation, 183 and 186 are two identical deceptive cadences three bars apart in one 16-bar theme. The second one, at 186, is the cadence that opens the A♭ area, and it repeats a move the listener heard three bars earlier.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 448, Ex. 26-7: Mozart states V–VI twice (bars 16–19) and then makes VI a key. Two statements foreshadow the tonicization, and the third is the tonicization itself. The finale has the right pair already: 166 (end of F1) and 186 (opening A♭). The extra statement at 183 weakens that pattern.
- A&S PDF 456, Point 4 and §15: the A♭ that follows at 205 has a different job (upper neighbour to V, springboard to D♭). So 204→205 stays.
- In the exposition the phrase pair goes from deceptive (63) to authentic (66). In the recapitulation it now goes from deceptive (183) to deceptive (186), so the second cadence gives no increase.
- Dvořák 9/iv has one deceptive cadence among 24 (cadence.csv, bar 279), and it is in the coda.

**Fix (applied in `sections/recS.ily`).** 183 closes on the inverted tonic, V–i6. This is allowed: the rule bans only root-position V–i in C before 262–263. The bass now rises E♭–F–G–A♭ across 183–186 into the deceptive cadence:

```
recSVc  bar 183: es,4 r4 r2    (was aes,4 r4 r2)
recSCb  bar 183: es,4 r4 r2    (was aes,,4 r4 r2)
recSVla bar 183: g8(\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto" c' g c') g8( c' g c')   (was es8(... c' es c') es8( c' es c'))
```

The violas take G3–C4 so that the chord has its 5th and the violas do not move G→E♭ in octaves with the cellos. The melody (cor anglais, muted trumpet, flute), the clarinets and the bassoon are unchanged. The F2 theme is not changed.

Result: 182 iv–V → 183 i6 → 184 iv–V → 185 V → 186 VI. The C-minor second theme now reaches its tonic once, at the point where the exposition had its first cadence. That makes the deceptive turn into A♭ at 186 the surprise it is meant to be. G→A♭ now occurs three times (166, 186, 205), and each has a different job: end of F1, pivot into A♭, springboard to D♭.
