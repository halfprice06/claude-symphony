## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 216-218: the Neapolitan's ♭2 is not the top voice. The protected line D♭-C-B-C lies under Vln I's F6 (applied)

**Problem.** The design for 216-223 (HISTORY c010, c113, c140) is a top line D♭-C-B-C over N6 - Ger6 - 6/4 - V7 - stop chord - I. In the score, Vln I ends its run on F6 at 217 and holds it through 218, a third above the flutes' and Vln II's D♭6. The highest voice is therefore F6 (216-218) → C6 (219). The D♭ never sounds on top, and the F6 drops a diminished octave to F♯5 at 219 (lower desk). The MIDI slice shows the top as F6 on every beat of 217-218.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 483: "Placing ♭2 in the soprano creates the greatest intensity; therefore, that arrangement is the most usual, especially at cadences." PDF 484 and 499 point 3: ♭2 moves down, and the diminished third ♭2-♯7 is filled by a passing 1̂, the line D♭-C-B that this passage is built on. PDF 518 and 526 point 3a: the augmented sixth comes from the chromatic step 4̂-♯4̂, which here is split across an octave (F6 → F♯5).

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily, Vln I 217-218 divided, with the lower desk on F5:
```

The lower desk plays F5-F♯5-G5 (218-220) in one octave, beside Cl 2 and Tbn 1. The theme B quotation (Tpt 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, flutes) is unchanged. ### 2. 121-122: the G-minor half cadence is a bare i-V. It needs the ♭6 that the exposition has, and the augmented sixth that grows from it (applied)

**Problem.** In the exposition F2's half cadence is Phrygian: bass ♭6→5 at 57-58 and 177-178 (c022). In the development's G-minor statement, the bassoon solo's bar 121 has the bass on G only, then i again, then V7 at 122. The development's one lyrical minor-key phrase loses the cadence colour that the theme has everywhere else. Bar 122 is also the end of a phase, the turn to G major at 123.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 504, Ex. 29-3: the augmented sixth originates in the Phrygian cadence with ♯4 added as a chromatic passing tone. PDF 503: augmented sixths "signal the beginning or end of a phase". PDF 505: the Italian sixth, third doubled, "is usually the best of the augmented 6ths for places that need a thin, transparent sonority" and goes straight to V. PDF 510: in minor the augmented sixth often follows I directly, the bass skipping from 1̂ to ♭6̂.

### 3. 211: Ob 2 sounds F5, the resolution tone, under Vln I's suspended G♭6 (applied)

**Problem.** Critic 151 tied Vln I's G♭6 (the 7th of A♭7) into 211, where it resolves to F6 on beat 2. Their stated rule was that the tone of resolution must not sound first. On 211 beat 1, Ob 2 holds F5, a minor ninth under the suspension in the next register. Tpt 2, the violas, Bn 1 and Tbn 1 have F4/F3 further down.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 352 sec. 12: sounding the tone of resolution in another voice "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The same page allows it "an octave lower in one of the inner voices" in a texture with marked contrast. That covers the F4/F3 parts but not the nearest one.
