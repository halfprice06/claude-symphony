## Verdicts on the three focus questions

**Development balance (99-134).** The order is right: calls, lament, climax, bassoon solo, hand-over, retransition. It is Dvořák 9/iv's plan in small: that development also falls into two spans, the first closing with an authentic cadence and the second building to a half cadence.
- The working-out (99-113) lasts 21 s, the G area (114-134) 30 s and the retransition 23 s. The climax comes at bar 12 of 36. Rise, peak, valley and rebuild is a coherent arc.
- The valley 117-124 is the thinnest texture of the movement (2.2-2.5 voices on average). That is right for a soft bassoon solo (reading 15-16).
- Its fault is local: the accompaniment sits on the solo's pitches (Finding 2).
- The hand-over 123-131 is protected (c034, c044) and works by reading 1 and 9. I would not change the proportions.

**Recapitulation (151-210).**
- The first group differs enough. It is half the exposition's length (20 bars against 40), begins over the G pedal, has the Neapolitan D♭ at 163, and is fuller scored.
- The C-major statement 187-198 differs too: Hn 1 doubles, and it turns to E♭ over A♭ into the coda.
- The C-minor second theme 171-186 changes only the solo colour. Its background is 51-66's, note for note, a minor third lower. That is the one place where the recapitulation repeats rather than re-hears (Finding 1).

**Coda stages.** Each stage ends louder or denser than it began, except the dip 231-238, which is kept deliberately (c033, Dvořák's Un poco sostenuto).
- The slow stages (211-246, 67 s) outweigh the Allegro molto (31.6 s) by about 2:1. In Dvořák 9/iv the proportion is reversed: about 21 slow bars against 70 fast ones after the sostenuto.
- I do not ask for a change. Every slow stage carries an owed item at a tempo it needs:
  - Theme B in doubled values (c013).
  - The arrival and chorale, which are protected.
  - Movement I's second-theme head. Its note values already run at quarter = 132 against movement I's 112, so a faster 235 would no longer be cantabile.
- The last 33 bars (247-279) are continuous ff-fff tutti for 43 s, which is enough weight for the end. Adding fast bars would renumber the protected close and the quotation at 275-276, and push the score to a 17th page. The gain does not justify that.

**Weight of F2.** F2 takes 76 bars (about 123 s, 29% of the movement) in six statements, against roughly 100 s for F1. The four exposition and recapitulation statements are owed or protected, and the two development statements are F2's only development, so I leave the count. Finding 1 makes the fifth hearing sound different instead.

## Findings

### 1. 171-186: the recapitulation's C-minor second theme repeats the exposition's background a third lower (applied)

**Problem.** At 51-66 the trumpet's tune sits mostly at B♭4-B♭5, above Vln II's off-beat pizzicato at E♭4-A♭4. At 171 the tune is a third lower, in the cor anglais and muted trumpet (B3-G5). The whole background was transposed with it, so Vln II's pizzicato <c' es'>, <b d'>, <c' f'> now lies on the tune's own notes:
- 172: D4-C4-E♭4
- 175-176: F4-E♭4-D4
- 177-180
- 185-186: D4-B3-C4

The cor anglais is playing in its lowest, weakest fifth there.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274 (and 287, Ex. XVII-15): keep the accompaniment registrally distant from a woodwind soloist.
- PDF 267 (Mozart, Ex. XVII-10): reduce the orchestra drastically when the solo lies in its soft register.
- PDF 257-258 (Ex. XVII-1): the second hearing answers in a changed colour.
- PDF 288 (Haydn, Ex. XVII-24): change the doubling on the repeat for variety.
- The score's own rule at 115 (c027) already keeps same-register accompaniment away from a soft woodwind solo.

**Fix (applied).** In recS.ily, Vln II rests 171-186:

```
recSVlnTwo = { R1*16 | g'2.(\mf^\markup \italic "arco" e'4) | ...
```

**Result.**
- The harmony stays complete in the violas' pizz. div., the cellos and basses, and the pad in Cl 1-2 and Bn 1. At 175, the C of the ii∅7 is in Cl 1.
- With both violin staves empty, the 171-186 system drops them. Letter L and the tempo mark then stand over the violas, which is allowed by the c045 rule on the top live string staff.
- The tune sounds above everything but the clarinet pad. Vln II's arco entry at 187 becomes a new colour.
- The C-minor statement now differs audibly from 51-66.

This finding does not touch the protected solo (cor anglais + muted trumpet, c041).

### 2. 114-122: the bassoon solo's background (applied)

**Problem (a).** At 114, Bn 2 holds D3 p in the D7 pivot. The solo bassoon then enters on D4 at 115, so its colour is heard one bar early.

**Problem (b).** At 115-122 the violas' off-beat pizzicato lies at G3-D4, the solo's register, and strikes the solo's pitches:
- B♭3 at 115 and 121
- C4 at 119
- D4 at 120
- A3 at 122

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 263-264: Tchaikovsky and Dvořák bring in another choir before a soloist so the solo colour is fresh, and Mozart keeps the orchestral horns silent under the solo horn.
- PDF 274 and 287: keep the accompaniment registrally distant, especially from a woodwind soloist. Beethoven's middle-register bassoon is set against a distant texture.

**Fix (applied) in dev.ily.**
- **Bn 2 at 114.** `devBsnTwo`: bar 114 `d1\p\>` becomes `R1`, and the following `R1*8\!` becomes `R1*8`. `devBsnOne`: bar 114 `\partCombineSoloII R1 \partCombineAutomatic` becomes `R1`. The strings and the basses' D still carry the pivot.
- **Violas up an octave.** `devVla` 115-122 moves up an octave, between the solo (≤ G4) and Vln II (D5-G5):
  ```
  r4 <g' bes'>4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz. div." r4 <g' bes'>4 | r4 <a' c''>4 r4 <g' bes'>4 | r4 <g' bes'>4 r4 <g' bes'>4 | r4 <g' c''>4 r4 <g' bes'>4 | r4 <a' c''>4 r4 <a' c''>4 | r4 <a' d''>4 r4 <a' d''>4 | r4 <g' bes'>4 r4 <g' bes'>4 | r4 <a' c''>4 r2
  ```
- **Vln II at 119.** `devVlnTwo` 119, beat 4: `<c'' es''>` becomes `<a' c''>`. Once the violas left the solo's register, the analyser found octaves between Vln II's E♭5 (119, beat 4) → D5 (120, beat 2) and the solo's E♭4 → D4. They were real all along; the low violas had hidden them. With A4-C5 the top moves C5 → D5 against the solo's E♭4 → D4, in contrary motion. The c027 aim of not moving A-D in octaves with the bass into 120 still holds: the top is C, not A.

The build after this change shows 0 outer-voice parallels. The solo line, the harmony and the pizzicato rhythm are unchanged.

### 3. 113-114: the break after the development's climax (no change, decided)

The ff F-minor cadence ends on beat 2 of 113, and the pivot at 114 starts p after a half-bar rest. Adler's forte-subito-piano (PDF 190, Ex. XV-36) needs an overlap only when the soft chord is meant to sound as if it were already there. Here the change of key (F minor → D7 of G) and of colour (tutti → strings, c058) make the silence a deliberate break, so the overlap does not apply. I decided to keep it. Finding 2 removes the bassoon's anticipation in the same bar, so the pivot is now strings only, and the break reads more clearly as a break.
