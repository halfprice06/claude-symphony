## Findings

### 1. 239-242: movement I's chromatic counter is buried under its own accompaniment (applied)
Bars: 239-242. In the first phrase (235-238) the counter quoted from movement I v99 156-159 (Cl 1 + violas, C5-B4-B♭4 | A4-C5 ...) is p, like the tune. In the second phrase it continues C5-B4-B♭4 | A4 | A4-G4-F4 | C4-D4 at mf, while the tune (Vln I·II, Fl 1, Ob 1), the bassoons, the low strings and the harp are f. The harp's right hand plays A3-A4 and D4-A4 broken chords in the counter's own octave. The counter's A4-G4-F4 and C4-D4 fall into that octave and to the level of Hn 3·4 (C4, D4, A3) and Bn 1 (F3→C4). The clarinet's part of the line lies on its throat notes (B♭4, A4, G4). This is the case Steinberg records as the author's own mistake (PDF 18): a counter-melody in the violas, doubled by woodwind, that "will hardly be heard" when the other parts keep their marks. It also fails the preface's aim of detaching a melody from its harmonic setting (PDF 19-20). Under the lens this matters because the line is movement I's withheld C-major counter. The finale is the only place where it sounds, and its second phrase is the one heard at f.
Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-250/work/sections/sHead.ily): Cl 1 and violas take f with the tune at 239, and the harp's broken chords drop to mf. The harp's diminuendo at 242 (c238), the swells, every note, Hn 3·4 mf and Bn 1 f are unchanged.
