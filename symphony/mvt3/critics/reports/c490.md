## Findings

### 1. 217–218: the echo of theme B has a clarinet and a trumpet sitting on its F4 (applied)

**Problem.** At 217–218 the cor anglais (theme B's own instrument in movement II) and Hn 1·2 answer the trumpets' theme B at movement II's pitch, A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4, marked f espress. Earlier cycles cleared the space for this echo:
- c108 and c138: the held chord diminishes f > mp.
- c414: Fl 2, Ob 2 and Tpt 2 rest.
- c440: Tpt 1 rests.
- c483: the tuba rests.

Two parts were left inside the echo's register:
- Cl 2 holds F4 at **f** through both bars (`f'1~\f | f'1`). It is the only wind still at f in that octave.
- Tpt 3 holds F4 f > mp.

At 218 both sit in unison with the echo's F4, and on 218.4 they are a sustained minor second under the echo's G♭4.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments".
- PDF 264: Mozart never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn plays, "so as not to interfere with the solo sound".
- PDF 267, points 3–4: as soon as the soft-register soloist enters, "the orchestra is reduced drastically"; no instrument of a competing colour.
- The cor anglais here is in its low fifth, its weakest register. The clarinet is the wind that blends most with it, and the trumpet is the colour the echo is meant to contrast with (principle 1, PDF 257: the answer is in a radically different colour).
- c090 used PDF 264/267 to take Tpt 3 off the violas' E4 at 220. The same reason applies to its F4 at 218.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-490/work/sections/codaTwo.ily):**
```
codaTwoTptThree = { des'1\ff\> | des'1\f | ges'1 | es'1 \breathe | aes'1 | f'1\> | R1\! | R1 | c'1~\p | c'1 | b1\< | d'1\mf\< | }
codaTwoClTwo = { … | f'1\ff | f'1\> | R1\! | R1 | fis'1\subP | g'1\< | f'1\mp\< | f'1\f\< | }
```
Both parts end with the 216 diminuendo, as Fl 2, Ob 2 and Tpt 1·2 do, and come back on the 219 chord: Cl 2 sub. p on F♯4, Tpt 3 p on C4. Both were already struck there, so their entries are unchanged.

**What is not touched.** F is still held in Vln I (F5), Tbn 1, Bn 2, contrabassoon, bass trombone, cellos and basses. Unchanged:
- the D♭6–C6–B5–C7 top line
- the 219 sub. p, the violas' D♯→E and Hn 3's D♯4→E4
- the stop chord and 222→223
- theme B and its doublings

Earlier changes in 217–218 (c414, c440, c483) were accepted inside c227's "216–223" range, and this one takes away no protected element. It reverses nothing; it carries c414 and c440 through to the last two parts on the echo's pitch.

**Note for the composer.** This changes no theme and no quotation, so the other threads need not be told. Only the scoring under the echo is thinner.

### 4. 58: timpani motive x between F2's exposition phrases (Beethoven, PDF 273): tested on paper, declined

**The candidate.** The recapitulation has the Beethoven Violin Concerto device at 178: timpani motive x pp near the rim, joining F2's two phrases (c290). The matching exposition bar, 58, could take `bes,4\pp bes,8 bes,8 bes,4 r4`. The drum is already tuned to B♭ for 37–94.

**Why declined.**
1. The finale's motive-x chain is recorded as G-only, closing to C at 276–277 (finale.md item 13). A B♭ statement would break that.
2. sOne's own design note keeps timpani and all other brass away from the owed trumpet solo (Adler PDF 264, 267). The trumpet's traditional partner should not appear under its first melody in the symphony.
3. Bar 58 already has the only background motion it needs: Cl 2's quarters under the held F4 (c141).

So the device stays at 178 only, where the soloist is the cor anglais.
