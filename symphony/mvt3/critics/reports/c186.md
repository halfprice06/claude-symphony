## Findings

### 1. 247-254: the P head's accents fall on up-bows at 247 and 252-254 (Vln I, Vln II, Vc). Applied.
Problem: with no bow marks, the slurs give this alternation from 246's down-bow G6 (c118): 247 accented E6 up-bow; 248 A5, 249 F6, 250 G6 and 251 E6 down-bows; then 252 A6, 253 A6 and 254 G6 (the head's peak and its held arrival) all up-bows. Each bar has an odd number of strokes, so the direction flips every two bars. The cellos (same head two octaves lower) have the same pattern at 247 and 252-253.
Evidence: Adler PDF 38: playing is "louder and heavier toward the frog"; the composer marks bow direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players". Ex. II-19 (Schubert 5/ii, PDF 37-38) marks two up-bows in a row "in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat"; PDF 42 and Ex. II-29: down-bows can be retaken quickly with the bow lifted. The ff peak of a movement-I theme played in C major for the first time should not sit on the weak end of the bow.
Fix (finale.ily): Vln I and Vln II `r8 g''8\downbow(\ff e'''4.->)` at 247 (retaken in the eighth rest); `c'''4) b''8\upbow( c'''8)` at 248 (second up-bow, hooked); `e'''8\upbow( d'''8) a'''4.->` at 252. Vc: `r8 g,8\downbow(\ff e4.->)`, `b,8_\upbow( c8)` at 248, `e8\upbow( d8) a4.->` at 252. Result: every accent 247-254 is on a down-bow in the violins (the cellos' 254 G3 is up-bow after the down-bow F at 253, a crescendo-friendly direction, PDF 38). The cellos' 248 mark is placed below the staff because above it triggers a LilyPond "minimise_least_squares" programming error; below it builds clean. No pitch, slur or dynamic changed.

### 2. 247-263: Hn 3·4 have no breath anywhere; Hn 4 plays from 235 to 264 without a rest. Applied.
Problem: c126 staggered breaths for every wind group in the last chorale (group A after 258, group B after 260) and left Hn 3·4 to "cover each gap". But Hn 4 has not rested since 234 (235-242 pedal, 243-246 slurred f, 247-254 held f, 255-263 ff to fff), about 34 s, and Hn 3 plays 247-264 without a rest, about 23 s. Since the c185 swap, Hn 4 holds the tied A♭3 at 259-260 and again at 261, then G3 into the fff 263; Hn 3 has the 16 marcato C4/E♭4 halves. Nothing marks where either may breathe, so each player takes a breath wherever they can, possibly at the same moment as Hn 1·2 (after 260) or the trumpets (after 258).
Evidence: Adler PDF 25: strings can play "without a rest, while a wind or brass player usually needs to stop from time to time to breathe"; adler1 PDF 292 (used by c136): give brass breaths in a loud passage; Rimsky ch. I PDF 37, 44.
Fix (finale.ily): `\breathe` after 250 in Hn 3 (`g'2 f'2 \breathe`) and Hn 4 (`e'2 d'2 \breathe`), with Hn 1·2, Cbsn and tuba, which already breathe there (c046, c126); and after 256 in Hn 3 (`e'2-> e'2 \breathe`) and Hn 4 (`b1 \breathe`), where no other part breathes, so Hn 1·2, the trumpets and trombones hold the iii → vi change. The spans become 247-250 (about 5.5 s), 251-256 (about 8 s) and 257-263 (about 10 s at ff to fff). One mark prints on the shared staff. The c126 scheme and c185's pitches are unchanged.
