## Findings

### 1. Bar 72: a root-position V7-I inside the antecedent of F2's E♭-major period (applied)

**Problem.** F2 at 67-82 is a period. The antecedent ends on V at 74 (melody F, a whole note), and the consequent ends with the authentic cadence 81→82. At 72, one bar before the half cadence, the bass is root B♭ under V7. At 73 it moves to E♭ under the melody's 5-4-3-2 | 3, so the listener hears a full V7-I in the middle of the antecedent.

This has three effects:
- The phrase closes twice (an authentic close at 73, then a half close at 74).
- The half cadence at 74 is not the antecedent's first root-position dominant.
- The fifth-motion B♭→E♭ that should mark the end of the period at 81→82 has already sounded.

In the recapitulation this bar is already V6/5 (192, bass G under A♭ major). Only the exposition has the root.

The counter-line also has a fault. In violas and cellos at unison, its 7th A♭3 (72.3) rises to B♭3 at 73. Against the bass, that is A♭/B♭ over B♭→E♭: the 7th goes the wrong way.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 110, Unit 7 section 7: root-position V should appear "where a clear expression of the harmonic 5th relationship is needed (as in most cadences)", but "near the beginning or the middle of phrases, V6 often functions more effectively".
- PDF 115, section 12, and PDF 119, rule 5: "Try not to create the effect of an authentic cadence in the middle of a phrase".
- PDF 129: a stepwise bass into I (from V6, V6/5 and so on) is the contrapuntal cadence that ends a group inside a larger phrase.
- PDF 107: a bass with more scale degrees lets the listener "sense a distinction between goals of motion and intermediate steps".
- PDF 98: the 7th resolves down by step.
- PDF 122: in V6/5 the bass rises to 1 and the diminished 5th with 4 resolves inward.
- Dvořák 9/iv bars 68-70 set the second theme's internal dominant as V6/5.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-253/work, sections/sTwo.ily only).**
- `sTwoCb` bar 72: `bes,,1` → `d,1`. `sTwoHarpL` bar 72: `bes,,1` → `d,1`. Bar 72 becomes V6/5, and the bass reads F | D | E♭ C | B♭ A♭ | G. This matches the recapitulation's B♭ | G | A♭ F at 191-193.
- `sTwoBsnOne` bar 72: `d1` → `f1`. With D in the bass, Bn 1's D3 would move D-E♭ in octaves with it.
- `sTwoVla` and `sTwoVc` bar 73: `bes2( c'4 bes4)` → `g2( c'4 bes4)`. The counter-line's A♭3 now falls to G3. The tritone D/A♭ resolves inward to E♭/G, and the melody's F→G against A♭→G is the good doubled 3rd of Ex. 6-17a.

Bn 2 keeps its B♭2 (the root) and every dynamic is unchanged. F2's tune in violins, flute and oboe is unchanged. The cellos' D4 on 72.1 doubles the bass's leading tone for half a bar and then leaves to the 7th. Bassoon 1 and the cellos do the same at 192, so I kept it.

The composer should note one change for finale.md: the counter-line's first note at 73 is G3, not B♭3.

### 2. Bars 56→57 and 128→129: the same mid-phrase root V7-I (not applied)

**Problem.** The E♭-minor trumpet solo has the same harmony at 56→57, one bar before its Phrygian half cadence at 58. So does the G-major hand-over at 128→129, before the half cadence at 130 and the cadence at 132. The recapitulation's minor statement already uses V6/5 there (176).

**Decision.** Keep both. At 56, moving the pizz. bass to D would make Cl 1's D4 and Vln II's D4 move D-E♭ in octaves with it. Those are two more pitch changes inside the protected trumpet-solo accompaniment (c004, c007, c216). At 128, Hn 1's F♯4→G4 and the harp would need changing, and both are in the protected 123-130 hand-over (c116, c182). Bar 72 has no such protection and is where the period is clearest, with an f consequent and the climax at 78, so I applied the fix there only.

### 3. Bars 192→193: the recapitulation's counter-line 7th rises (not applied)

**Problem.** Over the V6/5 at 192, the counter-line's 7th D♭3 (Bn 1 and cellos) rises to E♭3 at 193, while the bass goes G1→A♭1. The diminished 5th becomes a perfect 5th, which PDF 114 (Ex. 7-16a) marks as poor. PDF 98 requires the 7th to fall.

**Decision.** Keep it. The obvious fix, C3 at 193.1, then leaps up to F3 at 193.3 in similar motion with the bass's A♭1→F2 and lands on a hidden octave. Bars 187-198 are also protected ("strings and bassoons 187-198"). The fault is an inner voice at mf under the tune, and the exposition now has the correct version.

### 5. Bars 76→77: tonic repeated from a weak to a strong beat (no change)

**Problem.** At 76.3 the chord is I (bass E♭). At 77.1 it is I6 (bass G). This is the weak-to-strong move between two positions of one chord that Ex. 7-18a calls poor (PDF 115).

**Decision.** Keep it. Vln I's leap to the held E♭6 at 77.1 accents the downbeat, and IV arrives at 77.3. That is the motivic exception of Ex. 7-18c. The 77-82 balance is also protected (c167).
