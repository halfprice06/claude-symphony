## Findings

### 1. 184: ♭II6 in place of iv at the last minor cadence of the recapitulated F2 (applied)

**Bars:** 184 beats 1–2, recapitulation S. F2 in C minor, cor anglais with muted trumpet.

**Problem.** None of F2's minor statements uses ♭II6 to reach its dominant:
- 64 and 184 go through iv.
- 57 and 177 are Phrygian, through iv6.
- 121 goes through an Italian sixth.

The score has the Neapolitan only in F1 (16, 156, 163) and in the coda.

A&S gives the reason to use ♭II6 even where the diatonic chord is correct (PDF 483): the tension between ♭2̂'s pull to 1̂ and the chord's pull to V. That reason is "often a compelling reason for using ♭II6". 184 is the last pre-dominant before the deceptive cadence at 186, which turns the music to A♭. Section 14 (PDF 494–495) shows the same D♭/F chord as IV6 of the major key a third lower. Movement II uses exactly this double meaning at its 87 (mvt2.md: "D♭/F heard as IV6 of A♭"). The finale's own turn from C minor to A♭ at 184–189 did not use it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-380/work/sections/recS.ily):**
- `recSClOne` bar 184: `c'2 b2` → `des'2 b2`.
- `recSBsnOne` bar 184: `c2 b,2` → `des2 b,2`.

**Result:**
- 184.1–2 is ♭II6: F in the bass, Cl 2 and violas, A♭ in the tune and violas, D♭ in Cl 1 and Bn 1.
- D♭ skips to the leading tone B at 184.3 over V7. This is Ex. 28-2a/b, and the cross relation "does not create a bad effect … you may use it freely" (PDF 484).
- The tune's D4 at 185.1 then sounds as ♮2̂ over V, the contrast of Ex. 28-6.
- After the deceptive cadence, the same D♭/F returns as IV6 of A♭ at 189 (bass F1, cellos and Bn 1 D♭3, under the tune's B♭–A♭ appoggiatura).

**Checks:**
- I tried D♭–C–B first. D♭4–C4 against the tune's A♭4–G4 makes parallel fifths, so D♭ is held for two beats instead.
- Doubling: the bass F is the most doubled tone (PDF 484).
- c280 rejected an N6 at 167 as "a second ♭II sound four bars after 163". 184 is 21 bars later, pp against ff, in the other theme. The exposition (64) keeps iv, so the Neapolitan stays a recapitulation colour, as at 23/163.
- F2's tune, rhythms, dynamics, the deceptive cadence and the scoring of 171–186 (protected) are unchanged. No quotation is touched. F1, F2 and the chorale top lines keep their notes.

**Build:** 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, quote record 51/51.

### 3. Considered and rejected: N6 → V4/2 over a held bass at 133–134 (Ex. 28-9)

The only bars with a held 4̂ bass before a dominant inversion are 133–134 (c183's V4/2 of C after the G cadence). Putting D♭–F–A♭ over the F at 133 would introduce the technique, but I rejected it for three reasons:
- It clashes with the harp's ff G chord, which rings through 133–134 (c297).
- It removes c183's stated reason: the G area takes its seventh and turns back into the dominant.
- It plays the coda's long-range D♭ before the stop chord at 138 has been heard.

Decided: keep v380.

### 4. Considered and rejected: ♭II at 64 (exposition) and in the A♭ statement (Ex. 28-13)

- **64:** In E♭ minor, ♭II is F♭ major. The rule "every F♭ resolves to E♭" forbids the skip to D that makes 184 work. The legal F♭–E♭–D clashes with the tied E♭3 (Bn 1, 63–64) and Vln II's pizz. E♭4 at 64.2, so it needs re-attacks that c188 removed. The trumpet solo's accompaniment is also protected (c034, c276).
- **195–197:** A♭ minor at 195 would prepare B♭♭ exactly as in Bach WTC II/17. However, the tune has ♮2̂ (B♭) at 196.1 and 197.4, so ♭II would cross it in the soprano (PDF 484). Avoiding that means changing F2's tune.

Decided: no change.
