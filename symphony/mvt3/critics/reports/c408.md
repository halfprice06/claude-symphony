## Findings

### 1. Applied. Bar 149: the wood-wind join the sfp < ff of the brass and timpani

**Problem.** In the allargando bar before the recapitulation, c226 gave the horns, Tpt 3, trombones and tuba `\sfp\<` to ff at 150, and a later cycle gave the timpani the same. The whole wood-wind (Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, cor anglais, Cl 1·2, bass clarinet, Bn 1·2, contrabassoon) still re-attacked or held at ff in the same registers. Ob 2, the cor anglais and Cl 2 sit on D5 with Hn 3·4. The bass clarinet and contrabassoon double the tuba's G1/G2 at ff. So the dip was covered: most of the band stayed at ff while the brass played sfp.

**Reading.** Rimsky PDF 129, sf-p method (a): the wood-wind begin the piano chord and the strings attack it sf. PDF 130: a crescendo grows from the strings through the wood-wind to the brass, and the brass carry the crescendo into the climax. In 149 the strings already re-bow their double-stopped ff chord (`\dnup`) and Tpt 1·2 play accented ff strokes, so the attack is there. What is missing is a wind chord that actually starts piano.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-408/work/sections/retr.ily).** Each wood-wind's 149 note gets `\sfp\<` and its 150 note gets `\ff`, as the brass have:
```
retrFluteOne  ... | f'''1\sfp\< | d'''2.\ff r4 | }
retrFluteTwo  ... | d'''1~\sfp\< | d'''2.\ff r4 | }
retrOboeOne   ... | b''1~\sfp\< | b''2.\ff r4 | }
retrOboeTwo / retrCorAnglais / retrClTwo  ... | d''1~\sfp\< | d''2.\ff r4 | }
retrClOne     ... | g''1~\sfp\< | g''2.\ff r4 | }
retrBassCl    ... | g,1~\sfp\< | g,2.\ff d4 | }
retrBsnOne    ... | f1~\sfp\< | f2.\ff r4 | }
retrBsnTwo    ... | g,1~\sfp\< | g,2.\ff r4 | }
retrCbsn      ... | g,,1~\sfp\< | g,,2.\ff d,4 | }
```
Notes, rhythm, the strings' ff re-bow and Tpt 1·2's strokes are unchanged, and a source comment cites the reading. Shared staves (Fl, Ob, Cl, Bn 1·2) have equal dynamics in both players. I decided against an extra `\sf` in the strings. Their ff re-attack already is the sf of method (a), and an sf printed after ff would read as a drop.

Page 8 shows `sfp <` / `ff` lined up across wood-wind, brass and timpani at 149–150.

### 2. Considered, not applied. Bar 187: Hn 1 enters p after 17 bars' rest

Rimsky PDF 136 prefers pp or ff for a horn re-entry, and Hn 1 enters at 67 pp. At 187, however, Hn 1 "p dolce" doubles the tune an octave lower under violins at mf. At pp the doubling would no longer be heard, and the doubling is the reason the horn is there. I kept p.

### 3. Considered, not applied. Bar 247: Tpt 3 enters f after 23 bars' rest

Rimsky's re-entry rule (PDF 136) points to ff. But Tpt 3 plays the repeated-note rhythm with the timpani under the theme in the wood-wind, Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2 and violins. PDF 133 asks for that harmonic basis to be dynamically weaker than the melody, and c184/c208 set the 247–254 accompaniment brass at f for balance. The balance reason is stronger here, so I kept f.

### 4. Considered, not applied. Bars 39–42: clarinets as the last wood-wind (morendo)

PDF 130 makes the clarinets the diminuendo instrument. At 41–42 the flutes (A♭5/D5, the seventh and third of V7 of E♭) and the bassoons are the last winds, and the clarinets stop at 41. The flutes' held line is the top voice of the ff theme's descent, and their middle register is easy at p. Handing the last bar to the clarinets would move the top line down an octave and repeat the morendo that 170 already uses. I left it as it is.
