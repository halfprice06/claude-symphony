## Findings

### 1. 273: the violas undo the E♭/E♮ thread at its own pitch after closing it at 219–220 (applied)

**Problem.** The owed item is "the E♭/E♮ thread closed in the violas". Its history in the violas, all at E4:
- Movement I ends on E♭4→E♮4 at 179–182.
- Movement II, bar 2, turns that E4 back to E♭4.
- The finale closes the thread with D♯4→E4 at 219–220.

After 220 the violas play no E♭ until 273. There, on the second half of the bar, they move <e' g'> → <es' a'>: E4→E♭4, then on through F4 (274) to E4 (275). This is movement II's bar-2 gesture, in the same instrument and at the same pitch, inside the symphony's last six bars. A listener who has followed the violas' E hears it lost once more after it was won.

**Evidence.** Adler ch. 1 (adler1 PDF 19): timbre and texture clarify form as well as content. Here the closing colour of a cyclic thread contradicts the form. A grep of v40 finds no other viola E♭ from 221 to 279; the German sixth at 261 gives the violas F♯, not E♭. In the scherzo's close (365–368) the matching line is a different voice (violas E–G–A–G♯ over the E pedal), so nothing in the source requires the violas to carry the E→E♭ step.

**Fix.**
- The upper violas join the 5–♯4–4–3 chain at the horns' pitch: G4–F♯4–F4–E4, with Hn 1·2 and Tpt 3.
- The lower violas take the C–A–A♭–G line at Tbn 1's pitch (A3–A♭3–G3).
- E♭ at 273 stays in Hn 4 (E♭4) and Bn 1 (E♭3), so every chord keeps its pitch classes.
- The violas' last melodic step in the symphony becomes F4→E4. The chain's 3̂ then lands on E4: movement I's last viola note and the finale's 220.
- This makes the finale match the other two closes. In movement II the chain's 3̂ (C5) is the symphony's link pitch, which Vln I takes at 89. In the scherzo the 3̂ (G♯4) is the pitch that leads into the finale. In the finale the 3̂ becomes the thread pitch E4, in the thread's instrument.
- Adler ch. 15 (adler2 PDF 188–189) lists viola among the at-pitch partners of horn-like and reed lines, and a string in the horns' octave covers the seam at 275, where Hn 1·2 leave the chain for the quotation (adler2 PDF 192).

### 3. The chain across three movements under the new Largamente and allargando: checked, no change

**Durations.**
- Movement II (Adagio, crotchet = 52, 3/4): E♭5, D5, D♭5, C5 in Cl 1 over 85–90. That is one bar, one bar, two bars, two bars, about 3.5 s per bar.
- Scherzo (Meno mosso, dotted minim = 76, with the rit. only from 378): B4, A♯4, A4, G♯4 in Hn 1, one bar each, about 0.8 s per note.
- Finale at Largamente 2 = 72: G4, F♯4 (half bars, 0.83 s each), F4 (one bar, 1.67 s), E4 (two bars).
- Without the Largamente (2 = 100) the first two notes would last 0.6 s, faster than the scherzo's statement. With it, the finale's line moves at the scherzo's speed for its first two notes and in movement II's 1:1:2:2 proportion overall.

**The slowing.** The allargando at 271–272 finishes before the chain starts, so the line runs at one steady tempo, as in II (rit. only at the 3̂) and III (rit. only after it).

**Linking tempo words.** "Largamente 2 = 72" is the marking of the theme B recall at 211, and "largamente" also marks movement I's last climax (160–162) and movement II's climax (61). The word links the four movements.

**Instruments.** Adler ch. 15 (adler2 PDF 189): a three-octave spread is the strongest doubling. The chain sits in G3/G4/G5 at ff, a deliberate transformation of the p solos of II and III (choice by intent, adler2 PDF 188). Each movement gives the chain its own colour (Cl 1, Hn 1, then horns, oboes and strings), and the line keeps one colour within each statement. That follows Monteverdi's rule as quoted by Adler (adler1 PDF 20). Keep.
