## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 99–106: the development's two ideas alternate and never sound together. Applied.
Problem: the low strings' F1 head (99–100, 103–104) and the woodwinds' motive x (101–102, 105–106) take turns, and every entry starts after the previous one has stopped. Adler's whole subject in PDF 172–186 is several elements sounding at once. He gives two conditions for it. First, each element is exposed alone (172, 177, 181). Second, the elements must differ in rhythm and articulation (175, 178). Bars 99–102 already meet the first condition, and the head (half note, dotted quarter, accents) and x (slurred eighths) already meet the second. Only the combination is missing.

Test: critic 007's suggestion (Vln I with x on the downbeat of 103) puts A♮ against the head's G♭ at 103 and B♭ against A♭ on the downbeat of 104. I tested all seven diatonic transpositions and four offsets. The only clean fit is x at its own pitch level (F–E♭–D♭–C–B♭–A), entering half a bar after the head, which in 2/2 is the metrically equivalent beat. Its strong eighths are then all consonant with the head (F/F, D♭ over A♭, B♭/G♭, B♭/E♭, D♭/D♭). The one 4th, D♭ over A♭ at 104, is a 6/4 passing sonority. The x line ends with F on the downbeat of 105, inside the strings' B♭-minor sf chord. The winds' own x at 105 then becomes a second stretto entry, one and a half bars later. Bars 99–102 are unchanged and serve as the exposure, so the texture grows from alternation (99–102) to overlap (103–106), as Adler describes for the Jupiter coda (PDF 176).

Fix (sections/dev.ily):
```
devVlnOne 99-106: R1*4 | r2 r4 f'''8(\mf es'''8 | des'''8 c''' bes'' a'' bes'' c''' des''' es''' | f'''4->) f''4-> r2 | R1 |
devVlnTwo 99-106: R1*4 | r2 r4 f''8(\mf es''8 | des''8 c'' bes' a' bes' c'' des'' es'' | f''4->) f'4-> r2 | R1 |
devHnOne  103-104: f'1~\p | f'1 |      devHnTwo 103-104: f'1~\p | f'1 |
```
The horns' F is Adler's non-thematic dominant pedal (PDF 176, Mozart's horns, trumpets and timpani). It is p, so it stays behind the f head and the mf x. It also fixes a false flag in analyze.py: without it, the staccato D♭ in the basses at the end of 104 is released before the violins' E♭, the violins become the "bass", and the tool reports octaves at 104. Vln II plays x an octave below Vln I and stays above the violas' head (lowest note A4 against the violas' F4), so the two do not cross. The violins have rested since 98, so x gets a new colour, as each Jupiter entry does (PDF 176). The violins' next entry, c'''/aes'' p at 107, is unchanged.

### 2. 247–254: the second-theme head has no string colour, and the violas only double a tenor line. Applied.
Problem: movement I's second-theme head is in Tpt 1·2 and Hn 1·2 only. The first-theme head is in flutes, oboes, clarinets and violins only. Adler PDF 178: "the color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument". PDF 179: a big sound needs mixed doubling. PDF 176: each theme must be heard "at good strength". The violas sit on the cellos' tenor half-note line (critic 007 finding 3), which already has cellos and bassoon 1.
Fix: `finaleVla` 247–254 doubles Hn 1·2 at pitch:
```
e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8 | c''2 b'2) | e'2.~( e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 c'4 | b2 c'2) |
```
This does not undo critic 007's point, which was to keep the violas out of the first-theme head's lowest octave. They now stay on the horns' notes. par.py: the only new pairs are the ones the horns already had (for example 253 against the F-head), because the violas copy the horns.

### 3. 247–254: no dynamic hierarchy between the two themes and the background. Applied.
Problem: both heads, the held Hn 3·4 and trombone chords, bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon and the Tpt 3 repeated-note figure are all ff. Adler PDF 184 (Schoenberg): principal voices are marked louder than subordinate ones. PDF 184 (Bloch): background material must not take over a passage "meant to develop the themes and combine them". The held brass sits in exactly the horns' and violas' octave (g'–e', c'–e).
Fix (sections/finale.ily, bar 247 only): `finaleHnThree g'1\f`, `finaleHnFour e'1\f`, `finaleTbnOne c'1\f`, `finaleTbnTwo e1\f`, `finaleBassTbn c1\f`, `finaleTuba c,1\f`, `finaleCbsn c,1\f`, `finaleTptThree e'4\f`. The part-combined pairs (Hn 3·4, Tbn 1·2) change together, so their simultaneous dynamics stay equal. Bassoons 1·2 stay ff because bassoon 1 carries the tenor line. Every one of these parts already has ff at 255, so the change ends there.

### 4. 255–262: the last chorale has no background figure, only the double basses move. Applied.
Problem: for eight bars at Allegro molto every part except the basses' eighths has whole notes. Adler PDF 181 (Holst): figuration in the background "gives a wonderful buoyancy". The same passage says "the chord at m. 156 is ... glorious ... because all motion ceases". Here motion has already almost stopped before the cadence, so the stop at 263 does not register. Critic 007 (accepted) added repeated viola quarters under the first chorale at 224–228 for this reason.
Fix: `finaleVla` 255–262 = `c''4\ff c'' c'' c'' | c''4 c'' c'' c'' | e'4 e' e' e' | e'4 e' e' e' | f'4\< f' f' f' | f'4 f' f' f' | fis'4 fis' fis' fis' | g'4 g' g' g' |`, then `e'1\fff~ | e'1` at 263–264 as before. These are the violas' own pitches (the F♯ of the German sixth at 261 is kept), so the chorale top line and the last cadence are unchanged. They are repeated quarters, not tremolo.

### 5. 223–230: the canon is in the chorale's own rhythm. Not applied (observation).
By Adler PDF 175 and 178, the violins' canon at 226 differs from the brass only in colour and register, not in rhythm or articulation. The violas' quarters (224–228) partly meet PDF 179's middleground. Changing the canon's rhythm would remake an owed item and an accepted balance (cycle 4), so I leave it.
