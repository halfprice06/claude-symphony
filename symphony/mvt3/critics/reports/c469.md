## Findings

### F1. Mozart 41/iv holistic comparison (243–279): no change
Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? For the coda, mostly yes. The finale does what Adler says makes the Jupiter coda work (PDF 175–178):
- The heads are short elements, each shown on its own earlier: F1 at 11, the second-theme head at 235, the chorale at 224, F2 at 51.
- Each has its own rhythm: the P head in dotted crotchets and eighths, the S head legato, the chorale in whole notes.
- Each is combined with a fixed colour and its own articulation: accented in the P head, slurred in the S head.
- Tpt 3 and the timpani's repeated notes give the tonic–dominant cohesion that Mozart gives to horns, trumpets and timpani (PDF 176, item 6).

At 255–258 the long-note chorale against F2's third phrase is Mozart's cantus firmus against a moving theme.

There are two differences:
- Mozart's elements change voices (PDF 176: Vln II with Ob 1 plays the third theme, then the fourth). Here each head stays in one register and colour for the whole of 247–254, so the combination is a superimposition, not invertible counterpoint.
- Mozart's close after the combination is short and mainly harmonic. This coda runs 57 bars after the arrival in six stages, the same observation made at c339, c359 and c379. Adler PDF 146 warns against the full orchestra "for too long an unrelieved stretch."

Both differences are in protected passages (247–254 layout; coda plan and 265–279). The combination at 247–254 sits under the stringendo at 2 = 88, where a voice exchange would not be heard. I did not test an inversion and propose none. Recorded as an observation only.

### F3. Tested and declined: a ppp timpani pedal on B♭ at 43–46
The candidate comes from the Kiev example, where the timpani hold "almost a pedal on B♭ … an introductory flavor" (PDF 26), and from the Tchaikovsky example, a soft melody "accompanied by sustained lower strings, bassoon, and timpani" (PDF 156). The timpani's A♭ drum is already tuned to B♭ from 37 to 95.


The build was clean: 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, par8.py on 42–47 silent, 16 pages.

I declined it, for three reasons:
- On the page the new roll starts two bars after the f > p B♭ roll at 39–40. It turns the hushed transition into a continuation of that diminuendo, where now the bars-41–42 rest gives the new colour room (PDF 156: a rest before a new colour enters).
- c264 and c276 built 43–50 as a harmonic spectrum on B♭1 with harp-harmonic attacks. A roll adds noise to it: "maximum brilliance … with a minimum of color" (PDF 146).
- The timpani's next entries (90 f, 91 ff) are fresher after fifty bars of rest (PDF 37, holding a colour back).

Not applied. /tmp/critic-469/work is unchanged from v469.

### F4. Theme colours in the combinations: checked, no change
I checked Adler PDF 178's rule (one string, one woodwind, one brass per theme; a distinct articulation for each) against every place where two themes sound together:
- 37–38: B♭-minor head in Vc, Bn 2 and Hn 3·4.
- 247–254: the P head in Vln I·II, Vc, Fl, Bn 1 and Tbn 1·2; the S head in Vla, Cl, Ob, Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1.
- 255–258: the chorale in Vln I, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 2 and Tpt 1·2; F2 in Vln II, Vla, Vc, Bn 1 and Hn 3·4.

All three follow the rule. The 35–36 A♭ head (Fl 1 and violins, no brass) is the one entry without brass. It answers Mozart's flute–oboe–violin entry (PDF 176, item d) and is lighter on purpose before the horns' ff at 37. It is also protected (c902). No change.
