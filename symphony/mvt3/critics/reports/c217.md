What the score does not yet do is the chapter's central doubling technique applied to its secondary lines: Mozart and Wagner give a counter-element its own wind (or brass) partner at pitch, so that each element has a mixed colour of its own. In this score the main tunes always have that; two counter-lines do not.

### 1. 187–194: the cello counter-melody to F2 has no wind partner, while Bn 1 duplicates the bass (applied)

Problem. In the A♭-major F2 the tune is Vln I+II at E♭5 with violas and Hn 1 an octave lower (strings + brass). The counter-melody C–D♭–E♭ | D♭–C | … is in the cellos alone, the same colour as in the exposition (67–76). Meanwhile Bn 1 holds A♭2–G2–F2–… which are the bass notes an octave above the basses, the same notes Bn 2 already plays in 187–189 and 192 (par8 lists Bn 2's octaves with the bass at 188.1, 188.3, 189.1, 193.1); in 190, 191, 193 and 194 its F, F, A♭ and G are in other parts.
