## Findings

### 1. 205–210: the "poco a poco più animato" barely accelerates (applied)

Problem. Bars 205–210 are the coda's only accelerating passage before the arrival: the A♭7 build out of the hammered tutti into the D♭ episode. The score prints "poco a poco più animato" at 205, but the MIDI goes only from 2 = 84 to 86 (205) and 88 (207). That is +5% over six bars, about the smallest tempo change a listener can hear. The build therefore sounds like Tempo I. The Largamente at 211 then broadens by only 18% (88 → 72), and the push that is meant to give the D♭ arrival weight is missing. The rendered audio is what Dan listens to, so the MIDI should do what the printed word says.

Evidence. In score.midi the tempo events at 199–211 are 168, 172, 176, 144 quarters per minute. The printed tempo word and the two-bar units of 205–210 (F1's head in the horns, then in the winds, then the A♭7 crescendo) call for an audible increase. Unit 15 is about harmony and gives no rule on tempo. Its point on goals (PDF 234–236: an important arrival is set up by what leads to it, as in Brahms's Op. 119/3, where "the rush of quick notes" brings the V7 "forcefully to our attention") fits a build that actually speeds up. Dan's limit (R at 247 not above 88) is not affected: R stays at 2 = 88, and the faster tempo lasts only through the two crescendo bars before a Largamente.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-173/work/global.ily, bar 205 line). No printed change:

```
\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco a poco più animato" \mt 176 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak s1 \tag #'score \noBreak \mt 180 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak s1 \tag #'score \pageBreak \mt 184 s1 \tag #'score \noBreak s1
```

This gives 2 = 88 (205), 90 (207) and 92 (209–210). The Largamente 2 = 72 at 211 now broadens by 22%. A c173 comment line was added above it. The MIDI tempo list of the scratch build reads 168, 176, 180, 184, 144 at 199–211.
