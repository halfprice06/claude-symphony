## Findings

### 1. Bars 247–254: bass trombone in unison with Tbn 1·2 on F1's head (tested, not applied)

Problem examined: the score never uses Rimsky's three-trombone unison melody (PDF 73: "the combination of 3 trombones or 4 horns in unison ... produces extreme power and resonance of tone"; Ex. 75, Sadko, trombones a3 ff). At 247–254 the low octave of F1's head (G2–A3) is Tbn 1·2 a2 f, with Bn 1 and the cellos. The bass trombone rests from 247 and enters at 255 with the chorale.

Test: in /tmp/critic-471/work, `finaleBassTbn` started with Tbn 1's eight bars, `r8 g,8(\f e4.->) d8( des8 c8 | c4--) b,8( c8) a,4.-> g,8 | … | g2.-> r4 |`, and the existing `e,1\ff~` followed at 255. The build had 0 warnings, 16 pages and 0 outer-voice parallels; par8.py over 246–255 printed nothing; the record stayed 51/51.

Verdict: not better, so I reverted it. (a) The line falls to G2 and A2 at 247, 248 and 251, a fifth or sixth above the C2 pedal in tuba, contrabassoon and basses. A third, heavier trombone thickens the same low band that c072 cleared (A&S PDF 77: close low spacing blurs the harmony). (b) PDF 74 already counts two trombones of one kind in unison as a full brass line, and with Bn 1 and the cellos they give PDF 75's "Trombone + Fag." legato. (c) Hn 1·2 carry the S head an octave and a sixth above at E4, and the trumpets are marked f there (finding 4). The owed item is a combination of two heads, and more weight under the S head works against it. (d) The bass trombone's eight bars of rest give the fresh ff entry at 255 that c393 asked for (Rimsky PDF 136). Ex. 75 puts the trombones a3 under wood-wind figuration only, with no second theme in the brass, so it is no model for a passage that combines two themes.

### 2. Bars 247–250: unequal doubling of the S head's thirds (considered, not applied)

The S head runs in thirds. The upper line (E5) is Ob 1 + Cl 1 (+ Tpt 1 f); the lower line (C5) is Ob 2 (+ Tpt 2 f) alone. PDF 70 asks for doubled thirds to be doubled alike ("Fl.+Ob. over Fl.+Ob."), which would put Cl 2 on the C5 line. But Cl 2 is on E4 with the violas and Hn 1·2 for a reason from this same chapter: c019 placed it there as PDF 79's natural three-group unison (violas + clarinet + horn). At 251 it moves up to E5 (c087, PDF 75: two horns under three winds). Moving it now would reverse an accepted change on the same reading without a new reason, which counts as churn. The trumpets' thirds are already equal (Tpt 1/Tpt 2), and they set the balance of that pair. No change.

### 3. Bars 51–66 and 243–246: brass and expressive melody (no change)

Rimsky PDF 73 warns that "phrases charged with [passion or geniality] become sickly and insipid when confided to the brass". Two places in this score carry an expressive marking on brass: the "solo, dolce cantabile" trumpet F2 (51–66) and the "molto espr." horn call at 243–246. Both are owed items and are protected. Each also meets Rimsky's conditions for a brass melody. F2 is diatonic E♭ minor with no modulation, "dark ... in the minor" (PDF 72). It is undoubled, in short four-bar slurs with breaths, over pp pizzicato. Rimsky's own Ex. 71 and Ex. 74 give the trumpet a soft solo over a light string background in the same way. The horn call E–C–B–C is a natural-scale fanfare figure (PDF 71), so the marking colours a simple shape instead of asking the brass for chromatic pathos. The only chromatic note in a brass melody is the chorale's C–D♭–C neighbour (227), one passing degree, which PDF 73 allows ("vigorous phrases ... although introducing chromatic notes sound singularly beautiful on the brass"). No change.

### 4. Bar 247: Tpt 1 f against Ob 1 and Cl 1 ff (confirmed)

The editor's note to Ex. 75 (PDF 73) records Rimsky's own correction: when the clarinets joined the trumpet in unison, the trumpet was remarked forte instead of fortissimo. At 247–250 this score does the same: Tpt 1·2 f, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Hn 1·2 ff. The S head keeps its brass core and the wood-wind gives it legato (PDF 75). Do not raise the trumpets to ff.

### 5. Melodic memorability of F1, F2, the chorale and the combination (no change)

F1 (11–26): a falling fifth with a dotted upbeat, then a stepwise answer and a rising arpeggio to F5 at 14. The consequent peaks at A♭6 (22). The head rhythm (half note, dotted quarter and eighth) is the cell the coda reuses at 247–254 and 265–272, the way Brahms 1/iv reuses its tune's opening. The tune is singable at first hearing and recurs often enough to be remembered. F2 (51–66, 67–82): the falling third B♭–G♭ and the arch to F5 (later B♭6 at 78) form a lyrical contrast. Its minor-then-major presentation is the movement's warmest moment, comparable to the second subject of Dvořák 9/iv in function. The chorale (223–228) is the motto G–E–D–C made into a hymn line. It is plain enough to return as the last statement (255–264) and be recognised at once, as Brahms's chorale is. The combination (247–254) presents both heads in separate registers: F1 at G5–E6 and G2–E3, the S head at E4–E5. Each head was heard alone first (11, 235), the condition c469 drew from the Jupiter finale. PDF 76 warns that a melody over three or four octaves is hard to balance. Here the gap between F1's two octaves is filled by the second theme, not left empty, so this is the intended layout. I found no place where a theme's contour, rhythm or climax needs a change, and no change from this reading would make the tunes easier to remember.
