## Findings

### 1. 49-50: the transition's last chord is the reading's oboe and stopped-horn chord, played open (applied)

Problem. At 49-50, poco rit., the transition ends on a held pp V7 of E♭: Ob 1 A♭4 (end of its motive figure), Hn 1 D4, Cl 2 A♭3, Vln II F4, violas C♭4→B♭3, B♭ pedal. The composer's own plan for 43-50 is that each wind statement is darker than the last (trans.ily header: G♭ at 45, C♭ at 47 bring in E♭ minor). The chord is already the chord Rimsky gives on PDF 110: oboe above, horn below, p, with a low clarinet under the horn (PDF 107). He says stopped horn notes resemble oboe and cor anglais in quality and that the two "may be used simultaneously in one and the same chord, played p or sfp". He also says stopped middle horn notes over low clarinet notes give "a beautiful dark tone". The score uses stopped horn once only, at 138 sf> (c088). At 49-50 the open horn is the one bright colour in the chord, and it is a brass sound heard two beats before the trumpet's first open melody at 51.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 107 (stopped/muted brass with oboe and cor anglais; stopped horn + low clarinet), PDF 110 and Ex. 164 (PDF 334, *Kitesh*: two oboes, cor anglais and three stopped horns in one soft chord). Adler adler1 PDF 308: stopped horn is for single notes and soft passages. Written A4 for horn in F lies in the register where stopping speaks well. The recapitulation pairs muted trumpet with cor anglais at 171 (Rimsky PDF 107 again), so the exposition now has the related colour at the same seam.

Fix (trans.ily, applied):
```
transHnOne = { R1*6 | d'1\p-\stopped~ | d'2. r4 | }
```
I raised the printed level from pp to p because a stopped note is about an open pp in level (Adler: "soft"), and Rimsky marks the combination p. Pitch, rhythm, the other parts and the trumpet solo are unchanged. Hn 1's next entry (75) is a new note after 24 bars' rest and needs no "o".

### 2. 239-242: the bassoon and horn pad moves where it should hold, and triples C4 (applied)

Problem. In the second phrase of movement I's S head (239-242, f, the tune in violins, flutes and oboe; counter in Cl 1 and violas), the middle harmony is Hn 3·4 and Bn 1·2, Bn 2 doubling the cellos' bass. This is Rimsky's two-horn, two-bassoon four-part texture. In v247 Bn 1 is C4-C4-A3-D4: at 239 it is in unison with both horns (C4 three times, the chord's fifth E absent between A2 and E5), and at 241 in unison with Hn 3 (A3). The horns leap C4-F4-A3-D4-C4 (Hn 3) and C4-A3-F3-A3 (Hn 4) while the bassoon also moves, so neither group holds. At 242 the seventh C (ii7 into III♯ at 243) is in Hn 3, and Bn 1 holds D4 against it.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 108: four-part harmony in two bassoons and two horns, crossed, "the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons"; bassoons may be inside the horns, not the reverse; "in a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind". PDF 112: in a changing succession "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". That is why I kept c182's horn unison C4 at 239: my search over every Bn 1/Hn 3/Hn 4 voicing of 239-241 found no split horn pair on 239 that avoids the fifths D4-E4 against the cellos' G2-A2 or G3-E3 against the basses' C2-A1 (the reason c182 gave), except with Hn 3 crossed below Hn 4.

Fix (sHead.ily, applied):
```
sHeadBsnOne  = { R1*4 | e1\f | f1 | f1 | f2\> c'2 | b2\mf\< c'2 | c'4 b2. | b2 f2 | f1\! \breathe | }
sHeadHnThree = { g1\p | a1 | a1 | d'1 | c'1\mf | c'1 | d'1 | d'1 \breathe | b2\f r2 | R1*3 | }
sHeadHnFour  = { e1\p | f1 | f1 | g1 | c'1\mf | a1 | a1 | a1 \breathe | gis2\f( g2~ | g1 | f1~ | f1) \breathe | }
```
The pad is now 239 A2/E3/C4 (horns a2), 240 F2/F3/A3/C4, 241 D3/F3/A3/D4, 242 D2/F3→C4/A3/D4. Each bar has a complete triad in the middle register. The horns hold (Hn 3 C4 239-240 and D4 241-242, Hn 4 A3 240-242) and stay on concords (unison, minor third, fourth). The bassoon moves by step E3-F3, sits below the horns, so they are not enclosed, and takes the discord: the seventh C4 on 242.3, resolved to B3 at 243 in its own part (Bn 1's B3 there is unchanged). Vln II still resolves its C5 to B4. The S-head layers protected since c017 (violins' tune, Cl 1 + violas' counter, cello bass and C pedal, harp, swells from c238) and Hn 3·4's breath after 242 are unchanged; only the inner pad's distribution changes. par8 on 236-246 reports nothing.

### 3. 101-102 and 105-106: horns move the neighbour chord over a held bassoon (not applied)

Rimsky's chain-of-chords rule (PDF 108) would give the held E (E2/E3 in bass clarinet and Bn 1) to brass and the moving part to woodwind; here Hn 1·2 move E4/G4-F4/A♭4-E4/G4 while the woodwind scale also moves. I kept it. The horns' F/A♭ over E is F minor's tonic over its leading tone, and holding the horns on E/G would lose that harmony; the bass E has no common tone with the neighbour chord, so no brass part can be stationary without cutting it. c239 kept these pads for the sf-p scoring reason as well.
