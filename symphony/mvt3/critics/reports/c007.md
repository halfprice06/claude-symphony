## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 35–38: the countersubject disappears when the theme moves up; A♮ against A♭ at 38. Applied.
Problem: in 27–34 F1 is in the bass and the violins have a running eighth-note countersubject: a correct texture by Adler PDF 144 (registral separation, different rhythm). At 35 the head moves to the violins (A♭, then B♭ minor) and every other part, the bass included, plays whole notes. The counterpoint stops exactly where a master would exchange the parts (theme high, counter-theme low; Adler PDF 144 point 3; Ex. V-21, PDF 144–145, where the countersubject passes between the voices). Also at 38 Ob 2, Hn 2, Hn 4 and Tbn 2 hold A♮ (F major) under the violins' A♭ on the downbeat.
Fix (pTwo.ily, Vc and Vla in unison, 35–38; Cb, Bsn 2, Cbsn and B.tbn keep the bass):
```
pTwoVc  35–38: es'8( des'8 c'8 bes8 aes8 c'8 es'8 aes'8) | bes'8( g'8 es'8 des'8 c'8 es'8 aes'8 c'8) | f'8( es'8 des'8 c'8 bes8 des'8 f'8 bes8) | c'8( aes8 f8 aes8 f8 d'8 f'8 bes8)
pTwoVla 35–38: same, last bar ending ... f'8 d'8)  (into its held d'1 at 39)
pTwoOboeTwo 38: aes'2 bes'2   pTwoHnTwo / pTwoHnFour 38: aes2 bes2   pTwoTbnTwo 38: aes,2 bes,2
```
The line keeps the countersubject's shape (falling scale, rising arpeggio), stays below c'' (the violins' lowest note), and resolves the E♭7 seventh (des'→c') and the F-minor seventh (es→d). Checked with par.py: no new parallels.

### 2. 151–166: the recapitulation of F1 has no background figure. Applied.
Problem: at 151 Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Hn 1·2 all play the tune, and the only motion beneath it is off-beat quarters. The exposition (11–26) had the clarinets' broken-chord eighths, the figure Adler describes at PDF 135 ("repetitious and simple... adds intensity to the longer notes of the melody"). The recapitulation drops it and just doubles the tune more. Adler PDF 144 point 2 says to thin the counterpoint so the theme breaks through, and PDF 166 says orchestration should make the form clear. Here the return sounds louder but has less going on inside it than the exposition did.
Fix: recPClOne/recPClTwo 151–166 = pOneClOne/pOneClTwo bars 11–26, first dynamic `\f`. The bass line is identical to 11–26, so the figure fits as it did there. The tune keeps seven doublings.

### 3. 243–250: in the combination of the two heads, the lowest octave of movement I's first-theme head is in the horns' register. Applied.
Problem: the first-theme head runs in four octaves (Fl/Vln I g''–a''', Ob/Cl/Vln II g'–a'', violas g–a'). The violas sit on the horns' lower octave of the second-theme head (e'–c''). Adler PDF 144 points 2–3: separate theme and counter-theme by register and thin the counterpoint. The two heads already differ in rhythm (point 4), so register is the missing condition.
Fix: `finaleVla` 243–250 = `c'2\ff e'2 | d'2 f'2 | c'2 a2 | b2 d'2 | c'2 g2 | a2 c'2 | f2 a2 | b2 g2`. This is the cellos' and Bsn 1's tenor line, so it adds no new pitches.

### 4. 231–234: Vln II's lower octave crowds the chromatic counter. Applied.
Problem: Vln II doubles the S-head at e'–c'' from 231. The counter (Cl 1, violas, c''–f') and the harp arpeggios (g–a') are in the same octave, so the counter sits between the two melody octaves and gets covered (Adler PDF 135, 144 point 3). Brahms (Ex. V-7, PDF 132–135) keeps the higher octave for the second statement. Here the octave doubling is used from the first bar, and the change to octaves at 235 adds less.
Fix: `sHeadVlnTwo` 231–234 = Vln I's notes (unison, Adler PDF 152 option 3a); `arrivalVlnTwo` 230 pickup `g'4` (was `g4`). From 235 Vln II returns to its written lower octave, so the octave doubling arrives with the f statement.

### 5. 67–82 and 187–198: viola off-beats play the same notes as the cello counter-melody. Applied.
Problem: the cello counter-melody (g–f' at 67, e–d' at 187) is the middleground. The violas' off-beat dyads are g–d' (67) and e–c' (187), the same notes in the same colour, on every off-beat. Adler PDF 135: keep the background out of the register of the line when the colour is the same. At 187 the violas play `<e g>` under the cello's `e2 f4 g4`.
Fix: violas double the cellos at the unison (Adler PDF 152 option 3d): `sTwoVla` = `sTwoVc` (67–82, `\mf`, "arco, espress."); `recSVla` 187–198 = `recSVc` 187–198 ("arco, unis., espress."). Harp, clarinets and bassoons still supply the harmony. Bars 66 (`sOneVla`) and 186 (`recSVla`) end `r4 <…>4 r2` so there is a half note for pizz.→arco (c006 rule).

### 6. 220–224: the brass chorale has no background figure. Applied.
Problem: from 220 to 230 every part has whole notes, apart from the timpani at 225–226. Adler PDF 135 and 158–159 (Ex. V-33, V-34: sustained harmony plus a moving figure) and Brahms 1/iv's own coda chorale (strings move under the brass) both put a simple repeated figure under the long notes. The canon at 222 is in the same whole-note rhythm as the chorale and is separated from it only by register and dynamics, which Adler PDF 144 calls the weaker means.
Fix: `arrivalVla` 220–224 = `e'4\mf^\markup\italic "unis." e' e' e' | c'4 ×4 | g4 ×4 | e4 e f f | a4 ×4`, then `g2\f bes2` at 225. These are repeated quarters on the violas' existing notes (Adler PDF 165–166), not tremolo, so the tremolo rule for 219 is unchanged. I tried eighths first; they pushed the score past 16 pages.

### 7. 139–146: the retransition's "imitation" is a chain of separate statements. Applied.
Problem: horns 139, Ob/Cl 141, violins 143, tutti 145: each entry starts only when the previous one has ended, so no two voices ever overlap. Adler PDF 148 (Bartók, Ex. V-24) says imitation builds tension by overlapping entries, and Ex. V-21 (PDF 144) shows entries ordered by register.
Fix: a stretto at one bar. `retrVc` 139–141: `b,1\mf | g2->( d4. es8 | f4-.) es8( d8 c4-.) b,4-.` (Cb, Bsn 2 and Hn 4 keep the B pedal). `retrVlnTwo` 142–144: `g'2->(\mf d'4. es'8 | f'4-.) es'8( d'8 c'4-.) b4-. | b'1\f`. The entries are now Hn 139, Vc 140, Ob/Cl 141, Vln II 142, Vln I 143 and tutti 145, alternating middle, low and high registers over the static G7/B.
