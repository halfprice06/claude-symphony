## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 247–254 violas: four-bar phrase slurs at ff (Adler PDF 38, 39)
Problem: cycle 17 gave the violas the horns' slurs, `e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8 | c'4 a'2-- g'4 | f'4. e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8 | c''2 b'2)` and the same at 251–254. Each slur lasts 4.8 s at Allegro molto, at ff, against full brass. Adler: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow" (PDF 38). In forte he allows about six notes per bow (PDF 39). Players would either ignore the slur or play the theme too softly to balance Hn 1·2.
Fix (applied, finale.ily `finaleVla`): bar-length bow slurs, matching the violins' bowing of the same theme at 235–242:
`e'2.~(\ff e'8 d'8) | c'4( a'2-- g'4) | f'4.( e'8 d'4 f'8 a'8) | c''2( b'2) | e'2.~( e'8 d'8) | c'4( a'2-- g'4) | f'4.( e'8 d'4 c'4) | b2( c'2) |`
The horns keep their phrase slurs, which is right for wind players.

### 2. 114→115 and 170→171 cellos and basses: arco whole note straight into pizz. (Adler PDF 54)
Problem: `d1\>` (114) is followed by `g4\pp pizz.` on the next downbeat (115). `b,1\p` (170) is followed by `c4\pp pizz.` (171), at the poco rit. into Poco meno mosso. Adler: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck … There are instances … when no time is provided … but these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible" (PDF 54). The violas and Vln II already have a quarter rest at both places. The cellos and basses are the only parts left without one.
Fix (applied): dev.ily `devVc` 114 `d2.\> r4\!`, `devCb` `d,2.\> r4\!`. recP.ily `recPVc` 170 `b,2.\p r4`, `recPCb` `b,,2.\p r4`. Bassoon 2 holds the same bass note through the bar at both places (D at 114, B at 170), so the bass does not disappear. analysis.txt is unchanged.

### 3. 77–78 and 197–198: the second theme's peak falls on an up-bow (Adler PDF 38)
Problem: in 67–82 every bar is one bow, starting down-bow at 67. So the crescendo bar 76 is up-bow, which is natural (PDF 38), but 77 `es'''2.( f'''4)` is down-bow and the f climax at 78 (`bes'''4(\f`, the B♭6 appoggiatura from cycle 11) arrives on an up-bow, at the tip. The same happens at 197–198: `c'''2.( d'''4) | es'''1\f` puts the f arrival on an up-bow. Adler: an anacrusis is naturally up-bow, the frog is heavier, and the composer "should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players" (PDF 38). This is such a place.
Fix (applied, Vln I and II): unslur the pickup so it becomes an up-bow anacrusis and the peak gets a down-bow. sTwo.ily: `es'''2. f'''4\upbow | bes'''4(\f\downbow aes''' g''' f''')` (Vln II an octave lower). recS.ily: `c'''2. d'''4\upbow | es'''1\f\downbow` (Vln II an octave lower). From 79 the bowing then falls naturally: the up-bow at 79 and the diminuendo bar 80 on a down-bow, as Adler describes.

### 4. Fast runs in f/ff with 8 to 12 notes under one slur (Adler PDF 38–39)
Problem: Adler's rule is that the louder the passage, the fewer notes per bow; his forte example has six (PDF 39). In this score:
- 27–34 Vln I/II countersubject: eight eighths per slur at ff, against F1 in the low strings at ff.
- 87–90 Vln I/II: `r4 bes''8( … | … bes''4->)`, ten notes at f.
- 167–169 Vln I/II: ten notes at f, then eight notes with a diminuendo.
- 205–206 and 209–210 Vln I/II: twelve notes at f in the più animato.
- 215–216 Vln I/II: eight quarters under one slur at f, Largamente 2 = 72 (3.3 s).
Long slurs at these dynamics make the violins play softer than the marking.
Fix (applied):
- pTwo.ily: each bar becomes two slurs of four, e.g. `c'''8(\ff bes'' aes'' g'') f''( d'' b' d'')`.
- clos.ily: `r4 bes''8( aes'' g'' f'') es''( d'' | es''8 f'' g'' aes'') bes''4-> bes'4->`, and the same for the F run.
- recP.ily: `r4 es'''8(\f d''' c''' bes'') aes''( g'' | f''8 g'' aes'' f'' d''4) r4`.
- codaOne.ily: `aes'8(\f bes' c'' des'') es''( f'' ges'' aes'' | bes''8 c''' des''' es''') f'''4-> r4`.
- codaTwo.ily: one bar per bow, `es''4( f'' ges'' aes'') | bes''( c''' des''' es''')`.
The wind doublings keep their long slurs. The intro runs (3–4, 9–10) and the mf run at 103–105 are left as they are: one is soft and rising on an up-bow, the others are short enough.

### 5. Long held notes at f to fff in slow or broad tempo, with no bowing guidance (Adler PDF 39–40)
Problem: at the Maestoso (2 = 56) each whole note lasts 2.14 s per half note, so 4.3 s per bar. This applies to the violins' canon from 226 (ff), the violas' held notes from 229 (f), and the cellos and basses from 224 (f). In the last chorale (255–264) the violins and cellos hold tied whole notes of 4.8 s at ff, and 263–264 is fff. Adler warns that "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow", that "the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter", and that "by dividing the section and staggering the bowing among the players, one can produce a very long and effective legato line" (PDF 39–40). The score already uses "change bow at will" for its pedals (43, 142, 235). It is missing exactly where the bow is most overloaded.
Fix (applied, the score's own wording):
- arrival.ily: `g''1\ff^\markup \italic "change bow at will"` (Vln I 226); Vln II 226 `"unis., change bow at will"`; Vc and Cb 224 `c1\f^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`; Vla 229 `g2\f^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`.
- finale.ily, 255: Vln I `g''1\ff~^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`, and the same on Vln II and Vc.
I chose not to use string tremolo at 263–264 (Adler PDF 50, measured tremolo for volume), because the plan keeps the string tremolo as an owed item for the arrival.

### 7. 1–2 and 5–6: German-sixth whole note marked down-bow, followed by another down-bow (Adler PDF 38, 42)
Problem: Vln I/II, Vla and Vc have `\downbow` on the ff whole note and `\downbow` again on the quarter resolution (cycle 6). To play a second down-bow, the players must lift and retake at the end of a 1.43 s ff whole note, which clips it. This is Adler's double down-bow case from the *Coriolan* Overture (PDF 38): "A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid." The basses had no bowing marks at all.
Fix (applied): global.ily gets `dnup = ^\markup \concat { \musicglyph "scripts.downbow" \hspace #1.2 \musicglyph "scripts.upbow" }`. intro.ily uses `<c'' fis''>1->\ff\dnup | <b' g''>4->\downbow` in all five string parts at 1 and 5, with Cb added. The resolution still lands on a fresh down-bow at the frog (PDF 42: heavy attacks from the frog). Bars 1–2 and 5–6 keep their pitches and dynamics. This change only affects bowing, so it does not touch the protected German-sixth join.

### 8. 224–228 violas: repeated quarters under the chorale bowed separately (Adler PDF 42–43)
Problem: the violas' pulse (cycle 7) is `e'4 e'4 e'4 e'4 | c'4 …` at f in the Maestoso, with no articulation, so each note is a separate bow (PDF 37). Under a sustained brass chorale this produces a series of separate strokes. Adler's louré/portato is "a very expressive bowing used quite often in accompaniments", notated with dashes under slurs (PDF 42, Handel ex. II-30). It keeps the pulse but stays inside the chorale's sostenuto. At 2.14 s per bar, four quarters fit easily on one bow at f.
Fix (applied, arrival.ily): `e'4--(\f e'4-- e'4-- e'4--) | c'4--( c'4-- c'4-- c'4--) | g4--( g4-- g4-- g4--) | e4--( e4-- f4-- f4--) | a4--( a4-- a4-- a4--) |`. The ff détaché quarters at 255–262 in Allegro molto stay as they are (PDF 41).
