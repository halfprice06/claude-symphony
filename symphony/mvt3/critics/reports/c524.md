## Findings

### 1. Bars 263–264: the bar 3–4 scale figure returns inside the fff chord instead of above it (applied)

Problem. After the last cadence's fff tonic, Vln I and II play the opening scale figure of bars 3–4 in C major (c357). They play it at the same pitch as bar 3, G5 down to B4 and back up to G5, with the violas an octave lower. On 263 that whole range is already filled by the held fff chord: Cl 1 G5, Ob 1·2 E5/C5, Cl 2 C5, Tpt 1·2 C5, Tpt 3 and Hn 3 E4, Fl 1·2 C6 above it (MIDI dump of v524 263.1–264.1). The figure is the only moving line, but at 263 it sits under the flutes and among the oboes, clarinets and trumpets. c534 cut the harmony on 264.1 so the figure is heard in 264. The descent through 263 stays covered.

Evidence. Adler PDF 129 (Haydn, Op. 76/3, Var. IV): Haydn "restrained himself from using the octave transposition until these last moments in the movement, and how glorious it sounds here, and how climactic". Too often the extremes are used early and the final build is anticlimactic. This figure is the movement's opening gesture, and 263–264 is its last appearance. PDF 132 and 135 say the foreground must sit in the register where it is heard, and the Brahms 3 rescorings (PDF 133–135) show the same line losing force in the wrong octave. PDF 137 (Ex. V-14, Leonore 3) builds the climax by taking the strings up an octave. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 60, which c101 cited when it paired Vln II with Vln I here, gives a quick forte figure to both violin sections over the violas. The change keeps that pairing.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-524/work/sections/finale.ily, with a c524 comment):
```
finaleVlnOne (263–264): c'''4\fff\downbow g'''8( f'''8 e'''8 d'''8) c'''8( b''8) | c'''8( d'''8 e'''8 f'''8) g'''4-> g''4-> |
finaleVlnTwo (263–264): <e'' g''>4\fff g'''8( f'''8 e'''8 d'''8) c'''8( b''8) | c'''8( d'''8 e'''8 f'''8) g'''4-> g''4-> |
```
Vln I keeps its C6 on 263.1, the chorale's last note with Tpt 1's B→C, so no chorale pitch changes. Fl 1·2 still hold C6 through 264. Only beats 2–4 move up an octave, so the figure runs G6–B5–G6 and ends on G5 at 264.4. That G5 is now the pitch on which the F2 head starts at 265 (before, 264.4 was G4 and the head leapt an octave). The violas keep G4–B3, which is bar 3's Vln II and viola pitch, so the figure is still heard at its first pitch underneath. Unchanged: c085's 4+2+4 bowing, c101's Vln I+II pairing, c534's cut on 264.1, the dynamics and the cymbal stroke. The top note is G6, below Vln I's A6 at 252–253 and B♭6 at 78. C7 still sounds only at 223.

This does not reverse an earlier change. c101 and c428 doubled the line, and c534 cleared bar 264. This change completes their aim in bar 263. The figure is the finale's own opening material, not a quotation, F1, F2 or the chorale. No other movement thread is affected. finale.md's line "the opening scale returning at 263–264" needs "an octave higher in the violins" added.

### 2. Bars 75–76: Grieg's change of doubling at the consequent (tested on paper, declined)

Adler PDF 154–155 (Ex. V-30) doubles antecedent and consequent differently. At 75 F2's consequent starts with the opening's notes and the strings are scored as at 67 (Vln I+II in octaves, Vla+Vc counter-melody in unison). I tried violas on Vln II's B♭4 for 75–76. To do that the violas must break their tie from 74.4 and leap G3→B♭4, and then fall a sixth to the D♭4 seventh at 77 (c412). The counter-melody would be left to the cellos for two bars, and the middleground (PDF 132, 144 no. 3) would be weakened exactly where the tune thickens. With violas an octave lower (B♭3), the tune would enter the counter-melody's own register, which PDF 144 no. 3 rules out. The consequent already changes colour at 75 (Hn 1 to harmony) and at 77 (cellos on the tune, winds). No change.

### 3. Bar 129.1–2: the I6 of G has every string on B (checked, no change)

The MIDI scan flags 129.1: Cb B1, Vc B2, Vla B3, Vln II B4, Vln I B5, Fl 1 B5, Bn 1 B3. The root and fifth are only in the horns (G4, G3, D4). Adler PDF 152 (Ex. V-27) asks for the bass of a 6-chord to be doubled near the bottom only. The cello's B2 is the recommended doubling. The upper B's are the tune in octaves (A–B–G–A) and the violas' and Bn 1's resolution of the D7's seventh C4→B3. Moving any of them breaks a line, and the cello D3 alternative removes the doubling Adler advises. The bar is also inside c181's protected 123–131 hand-over. No change.

### 4. Bass drop-outs at 97–98 and 171–178 (considered, declined)

Adler PDF 130–131 drops the bass for a lighter texture. At 95–98 only the last desk plays, sul tasto, and 97–98 is the V7 of F before the development. Haydn's model (PDF 130) brings the bass back for exactly such a dominant pedal, so it stays. At 171–186 the pizz. basses mirror the protected 51–66 accompaniment, and since c523 they carry the V4/2's seventh F1 at 178.4. Removing them would undo both. No change.

### 5. Bars 247–254 and 265–272: contrapuntal clarity (checked, no change)

PDF 144's four means are all present. Head A is in Vln I+II (G5–A6) and Vc/Bn 1/Tbn (G2–A3). Head B sits between them (E4–C6: Ob, Cl, Hn, Tpt, Vla), and the rhythms differ. At 265–272 the two heads swap register groups. Nothing to change.
