## Findings

### 1. 24–26, 35–36, 164–166: Fl 1 (both flutes at 164–166) falls into its weak octave at the unison with Vln I just as the trombones enter ff (applied)

**Problem.** From 151 to 164.2 the flutes a2 play F1 an octave above the violins' C5 strand. This is Beethoven's superimposed top octave (Ex. XV-4). At 164.3 they drop with Vln I's unison to G5–F5 | E♭5–D5–C5–B4 | C5, the same pitches as Ob 1 and the violins, and play the cadence in the flute's low-middle register under f/ff trombones, tuba and full strings. The exposition does the same thing. Fl 1 doubles Vln I at the unison through the consequent, and at 24.3–26 it follows the tune down to E♭5–B4–C5. Since c041 both oboes are on the same E♭5, and the trombones and contrabassoon enter ff at 25. At 35–36 Fl 1 doubles F1's A♭-major head at the unison with both violin sections (A♭5 down to C5) under the trombones' ff re-entry, while Fl 2 rests.

**Evidence.** Adler's comment on Ex. XV-3 (PDF 151): if Mozart "had kept the unison of the flute and the violins … it would have added nothing to the heavy string scoring". He puts each instrument where "every note in this passage [is] played effectively by every instrument that he uses". On Ex. XV-4 (PDF 152), Beethoven "superimposes an octave on the high octave … with only two flutes … this high octave in the flute is so piercing it need not be doubled by any other instrument to cut through." Barber note 2 (PDF 150): do not let the flutes be forced away from the line before its last notes. Here the flutes stay with the line, but in the register where they cannot be heard. In the flute scan, 25, 30, 165 and 166 are the only places where the flute spends a whole bar below F5 at f or louder.

**Fix (applied).** Only the flute octave changes. F1's tune, its pitch classes, every other part and the dynamics stay as they are.
