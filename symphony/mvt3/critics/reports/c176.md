## Findings

### 1. Bars 207-208: Ob 1 plays F1's head in the oboe's coarse low register (applied)

**Problem.** At 207-208 Ob 1 plays the head of F1 at f from A flat4 down to D flat4 and C4. Three things go wrong:
- Ob 1 sits below Ob 2, which holds C5, so the parts cross.
- Ob 1 is two octaves below Fl 1 and Vln I, which play the same line at A flat5.
- At 209 Ob 1 jumps from C4 to C6.

The flute staff plays the same head with Fl 1 at A flat5 over Fl 2 at E flat5. The oboe staff is the only woodwind staff where the line drops into the instrument's worst register.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 71 says the oboe's lowest notes, roughly F4 down to B flat3, are "coarse" and hard to control, and should not carry a line. Sebesky PDF 79 (Ex. 104) shows the oboe's low register sounding harsh against the flute and improving an octave higher. Sebesky PDF 80 says a flute and oboe in unison from C5 upward is a strong, sure blend. Here, D flat4 and C4 at f in a running figure are exactly the low notes the book warns against. Raising the line an octave puts Ob 1 in unison with Fl 1 and Vln I in the register the book recommends. Ob 1 then sits above Ob 2 again, and the leap into C6 at 209 becomes a plain octave. Cl 1 keeps the lower octave in the clarinet's chalumeau and throat register, which the book calls dependable (PDF 60).

**Fix** (sections/codaOne.ily, codaOneOboeOne, bars 207-208):
```
aes''2->(\f es''4. f''8 | ges''4-.) f''8( es''8 des''4-.) c''4-. |
```
The old notes were `aes'2->(\f es'4. f'8 | ges'4-.) f'8( es'8 des'4-.) c'4-.`. Both oboes still have f at 207, so the shared staff keeps equal dynamics. Slurs, rhythm and the protected 199-204 are unchanged. This does not change F1 itself, only one doubling's octave. The scratch build shows Ob 1's range in analysis.txt moving from C4-E6 to G4-E6, and the engraved oboe staff now matches the flute staff's shape.

### 2. Bars 205-210: Tuba and Cbsn have no breath in six bars of low A flat (applied)

**Problem.** At 205-210 the tuba (A flat1) and contrabassoon (A flat0) hold a pedal at f and crescendo from 209, with no breath until the quarter rest at the end of 210. At the MIDI tempo of 2 = 88-92 that is about 8 s. The lowest notes of both instruments use the most air, and the crescendo at 209-210 comes at the very end of the breath, which is the worst moment. The bass trombone and double basses double the same pedal, so a staggered breath costs nothing in sound.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 70 tells the arranger to allow low winds "as many breathing spaces as possible". Sebesky PDF 39-40 describes the tuba's low register as needing much air, and PDF 46-47 says it should be used sparingly. The score already puts breaths in the same instruments' long holds at 214 and 218 (codaTwo) and at 225 (arrival), so this follows the movement's own practice.

**Fix** (sections/codaOne.ily, codaOneTuba and codaOneCbsn, bars 205-210):
```
aes,,1\f | aes,,1 | aes,,1 | aes,,1 \breathe | aes,,1\< | aes,,2. r4\! |
```
Before the fix, bar 208 had no breath mark. The Bass Tbn and Cb hold the pedal through. The breath falls on the system end before the page break after 208, where it engraves cleanly as a comma at the end of the line. Pitches and dynamics are unchanged.
