## Findings

### 1. 41–42: the violas finish the lower octave they started at 39; the cellos take the held A♭3 (applied in scratch)

Bars: 39–42 (end of pTwo, F1's last head in B♭ before the transition).

Problem: since c551 the violas double Vln II's lower octave of F1's last head at 39–40 (F4–B♭3–C4 | D4–C4–B♭3–A♭3–G3). At 41 they leave it to hold A♭3, and the cellos, which held the B♭2 pedal with the basses, Bn 2 and Hn 2 at 39–40, take over the lower octave (F3–D3–E♭3 | F3, c344) with Tbn 1 (c431). The violas then rejoin the cellos on F3 at 42. So the lower octave changes colour on the line's own step G3→F3, one bar before its end. Sebesky PDF 152–153 (Ex. 203) addresses exactly this: "In order to maintain the same string ensemble color throughout the passage, I did not shift the melody back and forth between the violas and cellos"; he uses cross voicing between violas and cellos instead. The c344 change came before c551, so the hand-over was never a decision; it is what remains of two separate fixes.

Fix (pitches and harmony unchanged; the two parts swap their bar-41 material):
```
pTwoVla, 41–42:  ... aes4-.) g4-. | f2( d4. es8 | f2.)\p r4 |      (was | aes1 | f2.\p r4 |)
pTwoVc,  41–42:  \clef bass bes,1\f\> | bes,1 | aes1 | f2.\p r4 |  (was | sul G f2( d4. es8 | f2.)\p |)
```
The violas' line now runs unbroken 39–42 down to F3 on their C string (D3 and E♭3 exist only there, so no string mark is needed). The cellos hold the seventh A♭3 above the violas at 41 (Sebesky's cross voicing, as in Ex. 200 bar 7 and Ex. 203), so the strings keep the held seventh that the violas had, and both meet on F3 at 42 as before. The cellos also leave the pedal that four other parts already hold (rule 7, PDF 140). c344's aim (the low violin line doubled an octave below, Adler Ex. V-19) is still met; c431's Tbn 1 doubling, c237's staggered drop-outs, c391's "sul G" in both violins and the 43–50 entries are unchanged. The cellos' "sul G" goes with the line. This reverses part of c344, for the new reason above (the seam c551 created), so it is not churn.

### 2. 19–23: flute on the lead violin at the same level (tested on paper, not applied)

Sebesky PDF 164 marks a flute doubling the lead violins one level softer, and c167/c267 applied this at 78, 83, 87 and 129. At 19–23 Fl 1 doubles Vln I at pitch (C6, up to A♭6 at 22), both f. I declined the change: the score uses the softer flute only where the strings carry a lyrical line at mf or with a crescendo. In F1's f and ff statements (19–26, 151–166) the flutes match the violins, and the recapitulation does the same at ff. A mf flute at 19 would make the exposition differ from the recapitulation for no musical reason. At 24.3–26 Fl 1 leaves the unison and goes an octave above (c-history, Adler part 2), where it is a separate top line and needs its f.

### 3. 133–134: cellos off the basses' F (rule 7; declined on paper)

Rule 7 (PDF 140) suggests the cellos need not double the basses on the held F of the soft V4/2. I worked out cellos on G3 (root) over the basses' F2. Declined: c183 put the seventh F in both cellos and basses so that the G-major area turns back into V of C. With the basses alone at p under a cello G3, the ear would take G as the bass and the 4/2 would be lost. c367 also declined a cello double stop here.
