## Findings

### 1. 170-171: the accompaniment should start before the cor anglais and muted trumpet (applied)

Problem: L (171) is F2's recapitulation in C minor, in the cor anglais with muted Tpt 1 in unison. The violas' pp sul tasto eighths, Cl 2, Bn 1 and the pizzicato bass all start on 171.1 together with the melody. Bar 170 is a held V6/5 fading to pp (B in the bass), so for one beat everything is new at once.

Evidence: Adler PDF 156-157 on Weber, Ex. XV-7: "Notice that here the accompaniment begins first, and the entrance of the two horns sounds very fresh." PDF 156 on Tchaikovsky: "An anacrusis or a rest before a certain color enters are two excellent ways of introducing new elements and making sure they are clearly perceived." The exposition's 51 is protected and stays as it is: there the trumpet's first melody of the symphony enters with its pizzicato. The recapitulation's accompaniment is already different, with violas arco sul tasto since c063b, so it can take this change too.

Fix (recP.ily `recPVla`, bar 170; recS.ily `recSVla`, bar 171): `d'1\p` → `d'2\p d8(\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto" f d f)`. At 171, `es8(\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto" g es g)` → `es8( g es g)`, because the marking and the dynamic now sit at 170. The violas hold c203's D4 for two beats and then play the figure on the dominant, D3-F3. The seventh F3 falls to E♭3 at 171.1. This answers c134's protection of the 167-170 diminuendo order: the horns still leave first, the clarinets and bassoons still fade to pp morendo, and the violas still reach p on D4. The only addition is a pp figure inside the strings' last half-bar. No other part changes.

### 2. 153, 161, 164: the bass trombone leaps an octave and crosses Tbn 2 (applied)

Problem: in the recapitulation's bass line the bass trombone doubles the cellos and bassoons on `g,4 g4 f4 d4` (153, 161) and `g4 f4 es4 d4` (164), at f under the ff tune. Each time it leaps G2-G3. On beats 2-3 (G3, F3), and at 164 on beats 1-3, it sits above Tbn 2's held D3. That puts the lowest trombone above the middle one. The basses, contrabassoon and tuba avoid the leap (`g,,2 r4 g,,4`).

Evidence: Adler PDF 150, Barber note 1: "Barber does not write octave skips … in the horns, trombones, or tuba, perhaps because that would make the gesture too heavy and pompous." The 151-158 layout protected by c134 ("strings on tune and bass only, clarinet eighths, brass off-beats, held trombones") is kept: the bass trombone stays on the bass line, and only its register at the leap changes.

Fix (recP.ily `recPBassTbn`): 153 and 161 `g,4 g4 f4 d4` → `g,4 g,4 f,4 d,4`. 164 `g4 f4 es4 d4` → `g,2 es,4 d,4`. At 164 the bass trombone holds G2 for two beats, so its passing F2 does not sound below the basses' G2 on beat 2, and it takes E♭2-D2 over their G1. The line now stays below Tbn 2. par8.py on 151-172 shows no new parallel, and one bass-trombone octave entry at 164.1 has gone.

### 3. 265-268: no sustained part under the C-major F1 (no change)

Problem: by Adler PDF 154 (Smetana: "Sustained notes or chords are the adhesive that provides cohesion in a tutti passage"), 265-268 is brittle. Every part moves or strikes, and nothing holds.

Decision: keep it. The rests that leave 265-268 without a held part are earlier decisions with stated reasons: Hn 3·4 rest so they enter at 269 (c036); Tbn 1·2 rest 265-268 (c046) and 269-270, entering ff at 271 (c124); the bass trombone and contrabassoon wait for 273. These follow the same chapter's PDF 156 (a rest before a colour enters). The held Largamente chord at 273 is the sustained answer to these four bars. 265-272 is also on the do-not-touch list (c197, c205). Adding Hn 3·4 or trombone holds here would take away the fresh brass entries at 269, 271 and 273, which matter more.
