## Findings

### 1. 89–90: the second statement of the scale figure has the same two colours as the first; give Vln II's octave a wind (applied)

**Problem.** The closing bridge states the movement's germ motive, the scale figure, twice: 87–88 on B♭ and 89–90 on F, a fifth higher, crescendo into the ff codetta at 91. The scoring is the same both times, Fl 1 with Vln I on top and Vln II alone an octave lower. c059 already raised the second statement an octave. Under the f strings' 6/3 chords Vln II's octave (F5–A♭4) is the weaker of the two, and the upper woodwinds first sound at 91.

**Evidence.** Barber "very subtly enlarges the doubling slightly in every measure in order to exploit varied timbres each time the little germ motive is played" (adler2 PDF 150; Ex. XV-2, PDF 149: each return of the figure adds a colour, then the brass and timpani join). In Mozart's octave tutti the flute and oboes sit on the violins' octave "simply for sheen and brilliance" (PDF 151). The oboe's best range is F4–C6 (adler1 PDF 198), and Vln II's line at 89–90 lies inside it. The low octave must not go to a clarinet. Below F5 the line enters the violas' and Bn 1's 6/3 voices (B♭3–A♭3), which c057 kept Vln II out of. An oboe–clarinet unison is also ruled out: the oboe covers the clarinet (c144, adler1 PDF 235). So the addition is one oboe, at Vln II's pitch.

**Fix (clos.ily, closOboeOne, bars 89–90; was `R1*4` for 87–90):**
```
R1*2 | r4 f''8(\mf\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." es'' d'' c'') bes'( aes' | bes'8 c'' d'' es'') f''4-> r4\! |
```
Ob 1 leaves out the low F4 on 90.4, as Fl 1 leaves out Vln I's, so it does not leap from F4 to its B♭5 at 91. The 87–88 statement keeps two colours. At 89–90 each octave now has a wind, while the horns enter p <. This is Rimsky's order of addition, strings, then woodwind, then brass, which c213 used at 108–109. No pitch class, chord or bass changes, and Bn 1 (c058) and the 6/3 harmony are untouched. The "1." label follows c145's rule for the first entry after a page turn.

### 2. 102.4: Ob 1's accented C4 is the oboe's honking bottom; leave the low note to Cl 1 (applied)

**Problem.** In the development's first wind answer (101–102) Fl 1 plays at C6 and Ob 1 and Cl 1 play in unison at C5. The figure ends with the accented octave drop `c''4-> c'4->`. So Ob 1 ends on C4, mf and accented, with no string doubling. C4 is a tone above the oboe's lowest note.

**Evidence.** Mozart writes the oboes away from the passage's last low note: "the oboes would have had to be dropped since they cannot play the last note, although they would have added a raucous quality to the low C♯ ... the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument" (adler2 PDF 151). Adler part 1: the oboe "becomes thick and tends to 'honk' in the very lowest fifth of its range" (adler1 PDF 198). The clarinet's low C4 is in its chalumeau, and Adler says the clarinet has "the most homogeneous range of any of the woodwinds" and plays "the entire dynamic spectrum ... beginning with the lowest" (adler1 PDF 210). At 105–106 the same drop lands on F4 for the oboe and F3 for the clarinet, both good notes. Only 102 has the problem.

**Fix (dev.ily, devOboeOne, bar 102):** `f'8 g' aes' bes' c''4->) c'4->` → `f'8 g' aes' bes' c''4->) r4`. The drop is still heard as C5 in Fl 1 and Ob 1 and C4 in Cl 1. Because the oboe's last note is now the unison C5, the oboe and the clarinet split on the last beat, the oboe silent and the clarinet alone below. That is Mozart's distribution.

## Kept (checked against the reading, no change)

- **Mixed articulation in a unison (principle 9).** This is the one technique in the reading the score does not use. The places it could go are F1's unisons: 27–34 (protected), 99–100 and 103–104. In each, the slur on the head's half note through the next downbeat is F1's articulation in every statement (11–26 and 151–166 are protected for their bowing). Detaching the strings there would change the theme, not colour it. d'Indy's viola tremolo is ruled out before 223 by the cyclic rule. Decided: not applied.
- **Length of the coda tutti (principle 2).** 197–234 is the movement's longest stretch at full weight. It is relieved by the D♭ episode's f > mp, the sub. p at 219 and c303's staged fade at 234. All of it is protected, and each stage changes its leading colour (brass melody at 211, the trumpet and timpani at 223, the trombones' chorale at 224). No change.
- **263–264.** The summarising statement of the scale figure is in the violins and violas over held winds and brass, which is Smetana's layout (principle 17), and the basses are left out (principle 7). This is protected; c129 thinned 264 so the figure is heard. No change.
- **20–21 and 152–153, 160–161.** The oboes touch E♭4–D4 at f in a tutti, doubled by Vln II. Adler forbids only a soft dynamic there (adler1 PDF 198). No change.
