## Findings

### 1. The half cadences that end F2's antecedent have no 7–6 into the iv6 (bars 57–58 and 177–178). Applied.

**Problem.** F2's first phrase (51–58, trumpet solo; 171–178, cor anglais) ends on a Phrygian half cadence: i (57.1) – iv6 (57.3) – V (58). This cadence is the S theme's first point of articulation, the place where the antecedent stops and the consequent begins. In the exposition the iv6 has almost no sound of its own. On 57.3 only the pizzicato bass C♭ and held E♭s sound (Cl 1 E♭4, Bn 1 E♭3, the solo's E♭4, Cl 2's E♭3). The chord's 6th, A♭, arrives only on beat 4 in the pizzicato violas and Vln II. The cadence chord is an E♭ octave over a plucked C♭, so it has no pull toward the dominant. The recapitulation is the same at 177 (C minor: bass A♭ on 177.3, Cl 2 C4, the violas' eighths already on F3). The pp pad (Cl 2 inside Cl 1 and Bn 1) is the only sustained voice that could carry the suspension. In both places it moves to a consonance on the bass's beat instead.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 297–298, Ex. 18-12 (Handel, Suite No. 2): a passing minor dominant leads i to the iv6 of a Phrygian cadence, and "a characteristic feature of this excerpt is the 7–6 suspension into the 6th of IV6. Such suspensions are almost invariably found in connection with this progression." In the example the upper note is held across the bass's arrival on ♭6 and resolves on the next beat. PDF 294–295 (Ex. 18-8a) says the same about the chord before V: it is "emphasized ... by the suspension (7–6) that delays its 6th". The movement already does this at its third Phrygian cadence, 121–122 (c323), so the exposition and recapitulation now match the development. In form terms, a half cadence that closes an antecedent needs a stronger cadence chord than the passing chords inside the phrase (A&S PDF 291: only the beginning and the end of a contrapuntal span are articulated). The suspension gives 57.3 and 177.3 a dissonance that resolves into the cadence while the solo holds.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-413/work).**

- sOne.ily, `sOneClTwo`, bars 57–58: `bes2 es2 | bes4( aes4 f4 aes4) \breathe` becomes `bes2~ bes4 aes4~ | aes4( bes4 f4 aes4) \breathe`. Cl 2's B♭3, prepared on 57.1 over E♭, is held over the bass C♭ on 57.3 (a major 7th). It resolves to A♭3 on 57.4 together with the strings' pizzicato A♭3, so the resolution note does not sound against the suspension. The A♭ is tied into 58 as V7's prepared 7th, then B♭3–F3–A♭3 continues c141's quarter-note line and resolves to G♭ at 59 as before. With the solo: A♭3–E♭4 (P5) moves to A♭3–F4 (M6), so there are no 5ths into V. That was c032's reason for avoiding A♭ on beat 3 (B♭3–F4 on 58.1 would have made them).
- recS.ily, `recSClTwo`, bars 177–178: `es2 c'2 | g4( f4 d4 f4) \breathe` becomes `es4 g4~ g4 f4~ | f4( g4 d4 f4) \breathe`. E♭3 stays on 177.1. Starting on G3 would make d5→P5 with the bass B2→C3 at 176→177. G3 is taken on beat 2, held over the bass A♭ on 177.3 (7th), and falls to F3 on 177.4, tied into 178 (F3–C4 to F3–D4 with the cor anglais: no 5ths).
- recS.ily, `recSVla`, bar 177 beats 3–4: `f8( c' f c')` becomes `g8( c' f c')`. The violas' broken chord starts on the suspension G3 with the clarinet and reaches F3 on beat 4 with it, so the viola no longer sounds the resolution against the suspension.

Answer to the protection of 51–66 and 171–186 (c004, c007, c323): the solo stays undoubled and soft, the pizzicato chords, bass, rhythms and dynamics are unchanged, and the change is inside the pp clarinet pad that c032, c141 and c147 already revised. This does not undo c032: its aim, no 5ths into V, is kept by tying the A♭ (F in the recapitulation) across the barline. c141's moving quarters at 58/178 remain, with their first two notes swapped. par8.py on 175–180 now reports nothing; v413 had octaves at 178.1 (Cl 2 C4→G3 against the bass C3→G1), and the new line removes them. 55–60 reports nothing in either version.
