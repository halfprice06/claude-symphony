## Findings

### 1. Lower-part semibreve and minim rests hang outside the stave (Gould PDF 50–51). Applied.
- Bars: 217–218 (Fl 1·2: Fl 2's R1 hung under the stave while Fl 1 held D♭6 on ledger lines above it; Ob 1·2: Ob 2's R1 hung under the bottom line while Ob 1 held D♭5 on the fourth line), and other single minim rests on part-combined staves on pages 2, 3, 4, 6, 11, 15 and 16 (e.g. the bassoon/clarinet staves at 102–105 on p. 6, where both players' half rests now sit on either side of the centre line, one stave line apart).
- Problem: LilyPond places a voiced semibreve rest of the lower part 2 spaces below its normal position, hanging from the bottom stave line, and its minim rest on the bottom line, whatever the other player is doing. Gould PDF 50: "upper-part rests are usually placed above the centre stave-line, lower-part rests below the centre line"; PDF 51: a rest goes outside the stave only when a note of the other part displaces it, and "the space in which a rest centres, or its distance from the stave, should be on the same level as surrounding pitches". Her first example on PDF 51 shows exactly the 217–218 case: a semibreve in the upper part, the lower part's semibreve rest inside the stave below the centre line. At 217–218 Fl 2's rest sat about four spaces from Fl 1's note and away from both the flute's dynamic and its own player's next entry at 219, so it read like part of the staff below. c295's `gould-rest-grid` treated only crotchet and shorter rests and assumed LilyPond already placed the longer ones correctly.
- Fix (score.ly, layout block, after the c295 line):
  ```
  \context { \Staff \override Rest.voiced-position = #(lambda (g) (if (< (ly:grob-property g 'duration-log 2) 2) 2 4)) \override MultiMeasureRest.voiced-position = #2 }  % c475
  ```
  Semibreve and minim rests (and whole-bar R1 rests of a part-combined player) start one space nearer the centre: the lower part's semibreve hangs from the second line and its minim sits on it; upper-part rests stay where they were. LilyPond's rest-collision still moves them out when a note is in the way, so no rest crosses the centre line or touches a note (checked on every changed page). Crotchet and shorter rests keep c295's placement (the lambda returns LilyPond's default 4 for them). The parts have one voice per stave and do not change.
- Result in /tmp/critic-475/work: Fl 2's and Ob 2's rests at 217–218 now hang from the second line inside their staves, under the held notes; on p. 6 the two players' half rests now sit on either side of the centre line, as in Gould's PDF 50 example. Changes appear only on pages 2, 3, 4, 6, 11, 12, 15 and 16; no system or page moved.

### 2. Harp 8 bassa at 239–242 (Gould PDF 44). Tested, no change.
I suspected the line ran to the 242|243 barline (PDF 44: end with a corner right after the last notehead, "avoid terminating the line at the end of the duration"; her example is a held semibreve). At 200 dpi the corner already sits right after the 242 chord, and closing it with a spacer (`<< <d, d>1 { s8 \ottava #0 } >>`, as c335 did at 82) produced the same image. I reverted it; sHead.ily is unchanged.

### 3. Ledger-line weight (Gould PDF 40). Not changed.
At this score's staff size (14 pt) LilyPond's ledger line is 0.80 pt against a 0.45 pt stave line, 1.8×; Gould asks for "about twice". A `\override StaffSymbol.ledger-line-thickness = #'(1.0 . 0.13)` would give 2.0×. I did not apply it: the difference is not visible at A3 reading size, and the five-ledger-line passages (78, 213–214, 222–223) already count easily on the page images. This is a note, not a recommendation.

Everything else in the reading's range (octave-sign placement, system breaks, short rests under the line, clefs instead of 8va, ledger-line limits, brass without octave signs, beat-aligned rests, single-line percussion rests) follows, as the table shows. No theme, quotation, F1, F2 or chorale top line changed.
