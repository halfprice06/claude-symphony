## Findings

### 1. Recapitulation 151–154: the return of F1 starts over the dominant, and the tonic root is delayed to 155 (applied)

**Problem.** The scherzo lasts five minutes, and the finale's exposition has already stated F1 twice. In v43, 151–166 repeated 11–26 note for note, adding only doubling and ff. The bass outlined E♭–C | D–F, exactly as in bars 11–12, so the recapitulation sounds like a da capo. The retransition prolongs G from 135 to 150 (pp, then a stretto to ff). It ends on V6/5 and then gives way to a root-position C-minor tonic at 151. A&S PDF 306 and 316–317 teach that an accented 6/4 is used to keep the expected event waiting: the dominant goes on under the new material. PDF 320 (the Brahms case) shows a section opening on a 6/4 that is made stable only afterwards. PDF 257 (Ex. 16-13) shows a tonic sonority treated as a neighbour inside a V prolongation. Bars 139–146 already do exactly this. Beethoven 9/i withholds the root-position tonic at its reprise (i6 in the corpus harmony table).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-043/work/sections/recP.ily).** At 151–152 the bass instruments stay on G. F1's head and all upper parts are unchanged. Bar 151 now reads i6/4 over G, and bar 152 reads iv over the G pedal (D F G A♭ B♭ / C E♭ F A♭). Bar 153 is V and bar 154 is V6–V4/3, both as before. The tonic root arrives at 155, with the timpani's C. Changes:

- `recPBsnOne`, `recPBsnTwo`, `recPVc`: `es2\ff c2 | d2 f,2` → `g,2\ff g,2 | g,2 g,2`
- `recPCbsn`, `recPCb`: `es,2\ff c,2 | d,2 f,,2` → `g,,2\ff g,,2 | g,,2 g,,2`
- `recPBassTbn`: `es2\f c2 | d2 f,2` → `g,2\f g,2 | g,2 g,2`
- `recPTimp` at 151: `c4->\ff` → `g,4->\ff`

The theme's return now overlaps the end of the retransition. This is the "double return" weakened on purpose. The first two bars sound different from 11–12, and the recapitulation already omits the counterstatement and the closing group, so it does not read as a literal repeat.

**Trade-off.** The tonic is confirmed at 155, at 159–163 and in the C-minor second group from 171. No root-position V–I in C is created: 154→155 remains V4/3 → i, so the cyclic rule holds.

**Checks.** p5.py output is identical to v43. The all-pairs check finds no new parallels; only octave doublings at 151–152 disappear. F1's melody is not changed. The composer should update finale.md's key plan to "recap begins over the dominant (i6/4); tonic root at 155".

### 2. Bars 187–194 are a literal transposition of 67–74 (no change)

S2 returns in C exactly as in E♭, apart from the transposition. In a reprise this is normal: A&S's prolongation principles concern the tonal frame, and the second group's job in the recapitulation is to be stated in the tonic. Dvořák 9/iv also restates its second theme with little change. The colours in 171–186 (muted trumpet with cor anglais) are protected and already set the passage apart from the exposition. The recapitulation is 76 s against 134 s for the exposition (57%), which is already compressed. I decided to keep 187–194 as written.

### 3. The development's arc after the rescoring of 115–131 (no change)

The development has these stages:

- 99–114 rises to f at 110–113, over the lament bass 107–110 (PDF 252, 259–260: minor V as pillar, stepwise bass in stages).
- 115–130 is the F2 episode: G minor at 115, G major at 123, rising from pp to f.
- 131–138 reaches V through D (PDF 253–254, an expansion of V by its upper fifth).
- The retransition builds from pp at 135 to ff at 147.

The weak point is length rather than shape. From 115 to 142 the music is soft for about 40 s, broken only by two bars of f at 131. Bars 113–124 are the sparsest passage in the movement (2–5 voices). The development is 12% of the movement against Dvořák's 20%. However, the low point before the stretto is a standard Dvořák shape. The pp at 135 was set by c038/c039, and 123–131 and 139–146 are protected. A dynamics-only change would not alter the proportion. I decided not to change it.

### 4. Transition and second group 35–98 (follows; no change)

Bars 35–38 are an ascending-fifths sequence used as a rising line (PDF 250). Bars 39–50 prolong the dominant of E♭ over 12 bars of B♭ pedal, with neighbour chords at 47–48 (PDF 257–258). This is a clear medial area with the right weight. S (51–82, 53 s) is longer than P (11–42, 46 s), and the closing group (83–98, 23 s) uses the stepwise bass E♭–D♭–C (PDF 256). The proportions fit a Romantic finale.

### 5. F2 is overexposed (noted; constrained by protections)

F2's head bar sounds about ten times. Its minor-to-major turn happens three times: 51/67, 115/123 and 171/187. That gives the development's episode less news value. The only places to change it are protected (123–131, 171–186), and Finding 1 takes the literalness out of the reprise without touching F2. I am not proposing a change.
