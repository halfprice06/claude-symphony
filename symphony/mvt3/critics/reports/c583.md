## What else I studied

- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, PDF 132–133. A florid melody with notes foreign to the harmony should not run against a part reduced to the fundamental notes. The clash is harsher the closer the two are in register and timbre. Difference of timbre (strings against wind) softens it; strings against strings does not.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 25 and 165: the end of the first-theme group sounds V under the tune's 6–5–4–3 (applied)

**Bars.** 25 (exposition), 165 (recapitulation).

**Problem.** These bars are where the first-theme group stands on V before its cadences: the deceptive V–VI at 26, and V–i6 at 166 (c133). Over the held G, F1 descends E♭–D–C–B (the 6–5–4–3, with c061's 4–3 on C). In v583 the accompaniment plays plain V under that line.
- At 25.1, Vla B3 and Cl 1 D4 sound against Vln II's E♭4 (a diminished 4th and a semitone). Vla D4 at 25.1.5 does the same.
- At 25.2.5–25.3.5, Cl 1 and Vla D4 sound against the suspended C4 in Vln II and the horns.
- At 165, Vln II and Cl 1 play B4–D5 against Vln I's E♭5, and D4 against the violas' and horns' suspended C4. Tbn 1 holds B3 under the violas' E♭4 on 165.1.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 306–307 (Ex. 19-4): an accented 6/4's 6th and 4th "move down by step to the 5th and 3rd". The 5th and 3rd arrive with the resolution, not with the dissonance.
- PDF 313–315 (Ex. 19-15a, Haydn Op. 76/5; Ex. 19-16, Mozart K. 525): a single line like this one implies two 6/4s over the held bass, and the accompanying voices move with it (8–7–6–5 over 6–5–4–3).
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132–133: an appoggiatura line against fundamental notes in the same register and the same string timbre is the harsh case. At 25 the violas sit under the second violins; at 165 the second violins sit under the firsts.
- Form: the antecedent's half cadence at 18 already has a real cadential 6/4 (c282). The consequent's dominant had none, so the two halves of F1 prolonged V unequally.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-583/work).**
- `sections/pOne.ily`, bar 25:
  - `pOneVla`: `b8( d' f' d') g8( d' f' d')` → `\once \override NoteHead.extra-spacing-width = #'(-1.7 . 0.5) c'8( es' f' es') g8( es' f' d')`
  - `pOneClOne`: `d'8( f' g' d') d'8( f' g' f')` → `es'8( g' f' g') es'8( f' g' f')`
- `sections/recP.ily`, bar 165:
  - `recPClOne`: `b'8( d'' g'' d'') g'( d' g' d')` → `c''8( es'' g'' es'') g'( es' g' d')`
  - `recPVlnTwo`: `b'8( d'' g'' d'') \stemDown g'( d' g' d')` → `c''8( es'' g'' es'') \stemDown g'( es' g' d')`
  - `recPTbnOne`: `b1 | b4\< g2.` → `b1 | c'4\< g2.`

**Result.** Bar 25 now runs 6/4 (G–C–E♭) – V – 6/4 (under the suspended C) – V7 on 25.4, and 165 does the same.
- No B or D sounds against the E♭ or the C in the same octave.
- c062's aim (no B against the C) and c181's (the violas double neither the suspended C nor the leading tone) are kept.
- Tune, bass, horns, rhythm, slurs and dynamics are unchanged.

**Tested variants.** I built three voicings before this one:
- Violas G3 on 25.1 made octaves with the cellos' and Bn 2's D3→G2. The all-pairs check caught it, so I dropped that voicing.
- Every voicing without the spacing override left page 2 over-full by 0.4–0.6 staff spaces. Bar 25 had lost the natural on B3, and the narrower bar moved the system-1 bass items under system 2's flutes. The override gives the bar back its old width. The other pitch changes alone build clean.

This finding changes no theme: F1, F2, the chorale lines and the quotations are untouched (record 51/51).
