## Findings

### 1. Bar 129, beat 4: make the predominant before V7 minor iv6, so the bass moves ♮6–♭6–5 — APPLIED

**Bars:** 129–130 (development, G-major area, just before V7 and the F1 head at 131).

**Problem:** In v432, bar 129 is ii-type material followed by IV6 (bass E, from c372), and IV6 goes straight to V7 at 130. In this soft passage the G-major colour does not change, although three bars later the music turns to C minor (133–135, E♭ prominent). The step from IV6 to V7 uses only the plain diatonic bass E–D.

**Evidence:**
- A&S PDF 563–564 (Ex. 31-17, Beethoven) shows the bass going ♮6–♭6–5 inside an expanded IV: "the major IV6 and the augmented 6th result from chromatic passing tones in the outer voices."
- PDF 532 says mixture in major is a special effect that darkens the mode.
- PDF 537 §5 says that when the altered tone "forms part of a chromatic melodic progression, the chord will create a less disruptive effect."
- PDF 537 §6 says a mixture chord aimed at another chord adds drive.

Here E♭ passes chromatically between E and D. As a result, iv6 aims straight at V7 and introduces the E♭ of the C-minor return two bars early. It also matches c402's iv6 over C♭ at 82 in E♭ major, so mixture in the predominant becomes a consistent feature of the score.

I tested a German sixth (adding C♯) and rejected it, because the C♯ clashes with Bn 1's held C4, which becomes V7's seventh at 130.

**Fix (sections/dev.ily):**
- `devVc`: `b,2\< e2 |` → `b,2\< e4 es4 |`
- `devCb`: `b,,2\< e,2 |` → `b,,2\< e,4 es,4 |`
- `devHnFour`: `g4\p\< g4 e4 e4 |` → `g4\p\< g4 e4 c'4 |`. My first try was `g4` on beat 4, which made parallel octaves Fl 1 G5→A5 against Hn 4 G3→A3 at 130.1. With C4, Hn 4 doubles the chord root with Bn 1 and falls to A3.
- `devHarpL`: `e8 g8 c'8 e'8` → `e8 g8 c'8 g'8`, so the harp plays no E♮ against the E♭ and needs no pedal change.

**Voice leading:**
- The bass E♭→D moves against the violins and flute G→A in contrary motion.
- Hn 1 G4→F♯4 moves in tenths with the bass.
- Bn 1 C4 is held as V7's seventh.
- The tune (B–G in Vln I/II and Fl 1), so the F2 hand-over at 123–130 is unchanged, and so are the dynamics.

**Check:**
- The scratch build is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty and pdfinfo gives 16 pages.
- analysis.txt differs from v432 only in the Vc/Cb note counts and the attack count for bars 129–132. It shows 0 outer-voice parallels.
- par8.py on 127–131 reports only the Vc–Cb octave doubling at 129.4/130.1.

No quotation or theme (F1, F2, chorale top line) changes.

### 2. Bars 146→147: m6–M10 exchange from V6/5 to root V7 — tested on paper, not applied

**Bars:** 146–147 (end of the retransition stretto, B pedal 139–146, ff arrival on G at 147).

**Problem:** This is the textbook place for the A&S PDF 564 exchange (Ex. 31-19: V6/5 to root V7 by a voice exchange, inner voices held).

**Evidence against:** For a contrary exchange, the soprano would have to rise while the bass falls B–A♭(–G). The F1 head on top falls F–E♭–D–C–B, so the outer voices would move in similar motion, not the contrary motion that defines the technique (PDF 564). Moving the bass off B on beat 3 also weakens the ff arrival of G at 147, which is the strongest dominant arrival before the recapitulation. The passage is protected (stretto 139–150, HISTORY.md).

**Fix:** none. v432 stays as it is.
