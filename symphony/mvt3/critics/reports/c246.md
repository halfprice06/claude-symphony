## What else I studied

Adler adler1 PDF 108 ("The Five-string Bass"): two or three basses in most orchestras have the C attachment; it was added "so that all the doubled cello passages could be performed an octave lower than written without the necessity of switching octaves in the middle of the passage"; if no player has it, the notes "automatically will be played an octave higher". Adler adler1 PDF 226 on the bassoon's lowest fifth. The earlier App. A reports c066 and c156 and the woodwind report c216 (191 Bn 2 B♭1), so I do not repeat them. The exposition's pizzicato bass under the trumpet solo (sOne.ily, 51-66), against which I compared the recapitulation bar by bar. I ran MIDI scripts for every instrument's highest and lowest note with bar and velocity, for cello/bass unisons in the low register, and for the timpani pitch sequence against the tuning. Every note is inside Adler's professional ranges. The one technique the chart offers that the score has never used is the bass's low extension.

## Findings

### 1. 171-186, double basses: the pizzicato line folds up to the cellos' unison on its lowest notes (applied)

**Problem.** Under the muted trumpet and cor anglais the cellos and basses play F2's pizzicato bass, the basses an octave below the cellos. This is the exposition's 51-66 a minor third lower. In the exposition every bass note is inside the range (lowest G♭1 and F1). In the recapitulation the transposition takes five notes below E1, and the source folds them up to the cellos' pitch: 174.3 E♭2, 175 D2, 179 E♭2, 181.1 E♭2, 183 E♭2 (`es,4`, `d,4`). The basses' line at 173-176 reads A♭1-F1-E♭2-D2-B1: a rising seventh and a falling sixth where the cellos move by step, and on exactly the lowest notes of the phrase the 16-foot octave disappears. No other 16-foot instrument plays here (contrabassoon and tuba rest 171-198), so for those beats the bass is only the cellos' 8-foot pitch.

**Evidence.** Adler App. A, adler2 PDF 302: the professional bass range runs below E1 with the † low C, "not found on every instrument". Adler adler1 PDF 108 gives the reason the extension exists: so that doubled cello passages stay an octave below "without the necessity of switching octaves in the middle of the passage", which is this passage. The same page says that players without the extension take the notes an octave higher automatically, so writing the low notes loses nothing in an orchestra without it: those players play what the score has now, and the two or three extension players add the octave. Dvořák 9/i 13-14 writes the basses down to sounding E♭1.

**Fix (applied).** recS.ily, `recSCb`: `f,,4 r4 es,,4 r4 | d,,4 r4 r2 | b,,4 r4 r2 | c,4 r4 aes,,4 r4 | g,,4 r4 r2 | es,,4 r4 r2 | b,,4 r4 c,4 r4 | es,,4 r4 f,,4 r4 | f,,4 r4 g,,4 r4 | es,,4 r4 r2 |` (bars 174-183; was `es,4`, `d,4`, `es,4`, `es,4`, `es,4`), with a comment line citing the books. Pizz. pp on the extension strings is easy and rings well. Pitch classes, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged, so the harmony and the protected trumpet/cor anglais doubling are untouched.

### 2. 166, double basses: the accented E♭ of the deceptive cadence is the one string bass note folded up (applied)

**Problem.** At 166 (`es,2->`, ff) the cellos and basses both play E♭2 while the tuba and contrabassoon play E♭1. Everywhere else in 151-166 the basses are an octave below the cellos. An earlier cycle wrote this note up on purpose ("E♭1 lies below the basses' E string, and this score uses no extension").

**Evidence.** That reason is answered by Adler App. A, adler2 PDF 302 and adler1 PDF 108: E♭1 is inside the professional range on the extension, and players without it fall back to E♭2, which is what the score has now. The accent marks the deceptive cadence that turns away from C minor's tonic (166 → 167-170), so it is the place where the full string 16-foot octave matters most, together with the tuba and contrabassoon on the same E♭1.

**Fix (applied).** recP.ily, `recPCb`: `g,,2 g,,2 | es,,2-> r2 | aes,,1\f\upbow` (was `es,2->`), with a comment line.

### 3. Header: tell the players where the extension is needed (applied)

**Problem.** Adler's own chart marks the extension notes with † because not every bass has them. A section without enough extension instruments should know at a glance where they are needed, and the bass part should not surprise anyone.

**Evidence.** Adler adler2 PDF 302 (†); adler1 PDF 108 (players without it take the octave above). The score's header already explains the octave transposition of the contrabassoon and basses.

**Fix (applied).** score.ly, subsubtitle: `\small "Contrabassoon and double basses sound an octave lower than written (double basses: C extension at 166 and 174–183)"`. It fits on the existing line; page 1 and the page count are unchanged. I decided against "C ext." markups in the staff: page 10 has no room, and the header plus the low ledger notes are enough.

### 4. analysis.txt now flags six bass notes below E1: accept them, as the trumpet C6 is accepted

**Problem.** analyze.py gives the double bass the range E1-C4, so the build now reports "6 notes out of range (double bass E1-C4), first at bar 166: E♭1" (166, 174, 175, 179, 181, 183).

**Evidence.** Its trumpet limit (B♭5) is Adler's nonprofessional top, and its bass limit is the four-string bottom; Adler's professional column (adler2 PDF 302, 305) includes both the trumpet C6 and the bass's extension. The trumpet flag has been accepted since v01 for the same reason.

**Fix.** No score change. Decided: record the six bass flags in critique.md next to the trumpet C6 as accepted for the C extension. These are the only new lines in analysis.txt (plus the profile's lowest note: D1 at 173-176, E♭1 at 177-184).

### 5. 241-243, basses on D2 and E2 with the cellos: checked, keep

At 242 the cellos drop D3→D2 onto the basses' held D2, and at 243 both play E2-G2 before the basses return to G1 at 244. D1 at 242 would need the extension; E1 at 243 would not. I decided to keep both. 242 is the diminuendo into the horn call, and the cellos' octave drop is meant to land on the basses' pitch. At 243 an E1 under the I6 at mf would be the thick lowest register that Adler adler1 PDF 108 warns about, under the horn call (243-246, protected).
