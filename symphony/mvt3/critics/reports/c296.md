## What else I studied

Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, PDF 46: "In changes of harmony the player stops the vibration of the strings with his hands, but, in quick modulations, this method is not feasible, and the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect". This applies directly to 73 (finding 2). Same page: the notes of a chord are close together in each hand, and chords not to be rolled are marked "non arpeggiato". That is the older convention; Adler PDF 114-115 gives the modern one I follow at 132. Rimsky PDF 47 says a single harp is "never a very powerful instrument" against a forte orchestra, which is why the chord at 132 is ff and why it matters most in the thin bars after it. I also read critics/reports/c206.md (the third pass of this reading), HISTORY.md for every harp entry (c26, c116, c204, c206, c249, c256), coherence/finale.md items 2 and 7, and the scores of v296 at 43-50, 67-86, 115-143 and 209-238. I did not open the corpus, because the findings concern harp mechanics rather than comparison with a master work.

## Findings

### 1. 129-134: the harp's G-major crescendo ends in a damped rest; give the cadence at 132 one solid ff chord (applied)

Problem. At 123-130 the harp plays its longest low passage: G-area broken chords, p, with a crescendo at 129-130 (b,8\< ... a,8 c8 d8 fis8). The crescendo ends on the "étouffez" rest at 131, so its goal is silence, and the harp has no part in 132, the G-major area's only authentic cadence (c292). The finale's only solid harp chord is the last one at 278. The bars after 132 are almost bare: the tutti cuts off after its accented quarter, and 133-134 hold V4/2 (G-B-D over F) with a diminuendo into the pp retransition. A harp chord rings longest in exactly that kind of space.

Evidence. Adler PDF 114: "Chords may be arpeggiated or played solidly"; "Three-note chords (per hand) spread wide are the most effective"; "tenths are quite simple to reach" (Ex. IV-5 is all wide three-note chords). PDF 115, Ex. IV-7: the Brahms Requiem's harp arpeggios close on plucked chords at the cadence. PDF 113: the middle two octaves are "very rich and warm". Rimsky PDF 46-47: the resonance of the middle and low strings "is slightly prolonged, and dies away gradually"; a single harp is weak against a forte tutti. So the ff attack is partly covered at 132.1, but the ring is heard through 132.2-134, where only Vln I-II quarters, Hn 3-4 and the strings' diminuendo remain.

Fix (dev.ily):
```
devHarpR = { R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | R1 | <d' g' b'>4-> \laissezVibrer r4 r2 | R1*2 | }
... | \stemNeutral r1\!^\markup \italic "étouffez" | <g, d b>4\ff-> \laissezVibrer r4 r2 | R1 | r2 r2^\markup \italic "étouffez" | }
```
(The second line replaces the end of devHarpL, `| \stemNeutral r1\!^\markup \italic "étouffez" R1*3 | }`.) The LH plays G2-D3-B3 (a tenth) and the RH D4-G4-B4: three notes per hand, in the warm middle. It uses the G-major setting already in place, so there is no pedal change. G, B and D belong to both the G triad at 132 and the V4/2 at 133-134, and the lowest note stays the basses' F2 (G2 sits above it), so c183's V4/2 is unchanged. The harp damps on 134.3, before the protected pp at 135, so the retransition and its stop chord (138) are untouched. c206's damp at 131 still clears the low D7 arpeggio before the cadence. The harp has about 1.4 s (one bar at 2 = 84) to place this chord. The notes and pedals of 123-130 (protected, c206) and every other part's notes and dynamics in 130-132 (protected, c208, for the horns' dynamics) are unchanged; the change only adds harp notes. For finale.md: the harp has a solid chord at 132.

### 2. 73: the harp's A♭4 rings into the A♮ of c273's V6/5 of V (applied)

Problem. c273 made 73.3-4 IV – V6/5 of V, with the bass rising A♭–A♮–B♭, and noted that "no A♭ sounds against A♮" and that the "harp needs no pedal change". The harp plays no A♮, but its RH group on 73.3-4 was E♭4-**A♭4**-C5-F5. The A♭4 is plucked on the second eighth of beat 3, about 0.36 s before the basses' and Bn 2's A♮ on beat 4, and it keeps ringing, because the hand moves on up to C5-F5 and does not damp it. The harp cannot replace it with A♮, because its A strings are set to A♭ in every octave.

Evidence. Adler PDF 111 (one pedal per letter name, so the harp cannot have A♭ and A♮ together); PDF 117 (damping is a deliberate act that needs time); Rimsky PDF 46 ("in quick modulations" the harpist cannot stop the strings, "and the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect").

Fix (sTwo.ily, sTwoHarpR bar 73): `es'8 g'8 bes'8 es''8 es'8 c''8 es''8 f''8` (was `... es'8 aes'8 c''8 f''8`). E♭, C and F belong to both IV and V6/5 of V. The A♭ is still heard on 73.3 in the basses, Bn 2 and the harp's LH A♭1, whose written rest on 73.4 is the harpist's damp. The bar keeps the same shape, rising from E♭4.

### 3. 82: the left hand holds a whole note through the "étouffez" (applied)

Problem. The RH ends on 82.2 and prints "étouffez" (damp) on 82.3, but the LH E♭2 (8 bassa) is a whole note, which tells the harpist to let the lowest string ring through the damp. Damping applies to the whole instrument, so the part contradicts itself.

Evidence. Adler PDF 117 (sons étouffés, the hand stops the strings); 131 and 243 are already written consistently (both hands rest under "étouffez").

Fix (sTwo.ily, sTwoHarpL bar 82): `es,2 r2 \ottava #0` (was `es,1 \ottava #0`). The basses keep the E♭ to 83.

## Checked and not changed

- Pedalling (Adler PDF 111-113): every passage is diatonic on one setting, and every change falls in a long rest. 82→123 needs B, E, A and F♯ (one left-foot and three right-foot changes) in 40 bars. 130→235 needs F. 242→277 needs B♯ (left) and F♭ (right). No two pedals change on one side at once.
- Harmonics (PDF 116): written B♭3, in the best range, p over pp strings. They could be two-note harmonics (B♭3-F4 is a fifth, one hand), which would answer the scherzo's three-note harmonic chord at its 375. I did not apply this: c228 protects the harp harmonics at 43-47, and at 47 a sounding F5 would add nothing to the violas' C♭4/G♭4 colour.
- Bisbigliando and trills (PDF 118): the passages where they would fit (171-186, 223-234, 135-138) are protected scorings. Before 223 any tremolo-like colour would also blur the rule that tremolo arrives at 223.
- 70.3: the harp's E♭4/E♭5 ring briefly against the cellos' E♮4 (A♭ → C7/G). Here the only common tone is C, so every way of rewriting the arpeggio leaves a semitone ringing. The harpist damps at the half bar as part of normal technique (Rimsky PDF 46), and the change is a full half bar, not a quick modulation, so I left it.
- 235-242: the RH turns on a repeated top note (g'8 g'8, a'8 a'8) at 2 = 66, about 4.4 notes per second. The thumb can re-strike it at that speed, so I made no change.
- 277-278: the glissando tuning, start and end are given (PDF 117), and the LH's final chord is three notes spread wide (PDF 114).
