## Findings

### 1. 124.4–126: the violas' hand-over of F2 lands on the open A string (applied)

Problem. c211 put the cellos' F2 entry at 123–124 on the D string ("sul D") so the hand-over Vc → Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I starts warm. The violas take over at 124.4, p espress., `d'4( | a'2 g'4 fis'4 | e'2 d'2)`. With no string named, the line's longest and highest note, the A4 half note on 125.1, falls on the open A string, which is exactly the note Adler warns about: in a slow expressive line the open string has "a characteristic sound ... [that] stands out peculiarly" and cannot be vibrated like the stopped notes around it (PDF 73–74, Ex. III-4). The viola's A string is also its "piercing and nasal" string (PDF 87), the opposite of the cellos' D-string colour that the hand-over is meant to continue; the G and D strings are the viola's dark cantabile strings (PDF 87, Ex. III-31). Adler allows naming a string only for timbre (PDF 73); here timbre is the whole point of c211's sul D.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-286/work/sections/dev.ily, devVla): the same spanner the cellos have, over the pickup to 126.

```lilypond
r2. \once \override TextScript.avoid-slur = #'outside \once \override TextScript.outside-staff-priority = #600 \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul D" d'4(\p^\markup \italic "arco, unis."\startTextSpan | a'2 g'4 fis'4 | e'2 d'2)\stopTextSpan |
```

A4 on the viola's D string is the 3rd–4th position, G4–F♯4–E4 lie under the hand, and D4 is the string's own note. The whole phrase now sits on one string, an octave above the cellos' D-string phrase it continues. Notes, dynamics, slurs, order and MIDI are unchanged (c211's "hand-over order and notes" protection is respected). The text sits under "arco, unis." exactly as the cellos' "sul D" sits under "arco, espress." at 123 (checked on page 7).

### 2. Other open-string pitches in lyrical lines: checked, no mark (not applied)

I listed every string note on an open-string pitch of a half note or longer under p, pp or mp. Apart from 125, all are cases where Adler's rule (PDF 73) says to leave the fingering to the player, because ordinary fingering already stops them, or because naming a string would cost something:
- 130 Vln II A4 whole note (mf crescendo, octave below Vln I): a violinist takes it with the 4th finger on the D string by default; "sul D" for 128–130 would weaken the lower octave on the way to f at 131 (D string "least powerful", PDF 75).
- 67–68, 75–76 Vla G3 in unison with the cellos: "sul C" would match the cellos but darken the counter that c211 and earlier critics balanced at 67–82; the usual 4th finger on the C string does the job without a mark.
- 236–237 Vla A4 in the chromatic counter (with Cl 1, movement I's quotation): the rest of the line (C5–B4–B♭4) belongs on the A string, which Adler says blends with woodwinds (PDF 87); players stop the A4 on the D string in a slur like this.
- 136 Vln II E5 (pp chord), 44–46 Vla D4 (pp under the harmonic): inner held notes that players stop as a matter of routine.

Decision: no further string marks. Adding them would clutter the parts without changing what an orchestra plays.
