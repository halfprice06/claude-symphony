## Findings

### 1. 222: the run into the C7 contradicts the stop chord (A♮6 against A♭) — applied
Bars: 222, Fl 1 and Vln I, `r8 g'''8( a''' b''')`.
Problem: the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ holds A♭ in Vln II (A♭5), Tpt 2 and Cl 1 (A♭4) and Tbn 1 (A♭3) for the whole bar. The run at its end plays A♮6 over it: an augmented octave with Vln II, in the top voice, at the most exposed moment of the symphony. For one eighth the run turns VII°7 into VIIø7.
Evidence: A&S PDF 388 and 395: once introduced, ♭6 goes to 5 and is not replaced by ♮6 (♭6–♮6–5 is "poor"). PDF 393: VII°7 enters major only through ♭6. PDF 380 (Ex. 22-10, Bach): the melodic augmented second is acceptable when it arpeggiates inside VII°7, which A♭–B does.
Fix (both parts in codaTwo.ily):
```
codaTwoFluteOne: ... b''2\f\< r8 g'''8( aes''' b''') |
codaTwoVlnOne:   ... b''2\f\<^\markup \italic "unis." r8 \once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.25 . 0.25) g'''8( aes''' b''') |
```
The stop chord, its resolution 222→223, the C7 and the scale shape (c036) are unchanged; the change makes the run belong to the protected chord.

### 2. 116, 118, 124, 126: F2's second and fourth bars sit on one held chord — applied
Bars: 115–118 (G minor, bassoon solo) and 123–126 (G major hand-over).
Problem: in the exposition F2's second bar changes chord on beat 3 (V6/5→I, bass 7→1) and its fourth bar changes from IV to I6. In the development both bars hold one chord. At 116 and 124 the tune's tonic notes (B♭–D, B–D) sound over a held V6/5, against its A and C. The bass then drops from the leading tone: at 116→117 by a descending augmented second (F♯→E♭), and at 124→125 the bass-to-C d5 F♯–C moves to the fifth E–B (harp C3→B2 over F♯→E), as at 116→117 (Vla C4→B♭3). At 118 and 126 the tune's D (beats 3–4) is an unresolved ninth over a held C chord.
Evidence: A&S PDF 375 and 383 (the V6/5/°7 bass is the lower neighbour of 1 and returns to it); PDF 377 box ("all dissonances involving the bass must resolve"; d5 inward); PDF 380 (an A2 is acceptable only within °7 or from II/IV/VI into it); the composer's own model at 52, 54, 68, 70.
Fix (dev.ily):
```

           | e1\pp | c2 b,2 | a,1 |                                     (125-127)
devCb:     | fis,4 r4 g,4 r4 | es,4 r4 r2 | c,4 r4 bes,4 r4 | ...     (116-118)
           | g,1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | fis,2 g,2 | e,1 | c,2 b,,2 | a,,1 |   (123-127)
devVlnTwo: 116 r4 <d'' fis''>4 r4 <d'' g''>4 | 118 r4 <es'' g''>4 r4 <bes' g''>4
devVla:    116 r4 <a c'>4 r4 <g bes>4 | 118 r4 <g c'>4 r4 <g bes>4
```

Vln II at 118 takes B♭4, not D5, so it does not move D–C in octaves with the solo into 119. This partly revises cycle 37 ("bass F♯ at 124"): the F♯ stays on beats 1–2, and the bass now runs G–F♯–G–E–C–B–A–D–G, the shape of the exposition (E♭–D–E♭–C♭… at 51–54).

### 3. 226–227: the canon's supporting notes clash with the chorale — applied
Bars: 226 Fl 2, Ob 2; 227 Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2.
Problem: at 226 Fl 2 and Ob 2 enter on E (a third under the canon's G) against the chorale's D (Tpt 1 D5; Hn 2, Hn 3, Tbn 1, Cl 2 D4): a major second in the same octave, unprepared and left by leap. At 227 the brass moves A minor → A–D♭–F while Fl 2 and Ob 2 hold C (C5 against Tpt 1 D♭5 in the same octave) and Cl 2 holds E4 against Hn 1 F4. So the motto's chromatic C→D♭ is heard against its own C, and the half bar is a cluster A–C–D♭–E–F.
Evidence: A&S PDF 377–378 (a dissonance enters as neighbour, common tone or passing tone); PDF 394 (a chromatic succession split between voices is avoided; only one voice should carry it, Ex. 23-12b).
Fix (arrival.ily):
```
arrivalFluteTwo: ... | g''1\ff | c'''2 a''2 | a''1 | ...
arrivalOboeTwo:  ... | g'1\ff | c''2 a'2 | a'1 | ...
arrivalClTwo:    ... | d'1\ff | e'2 f'2 | a'1 | ...
```
The canon's E6 in the second half of 227 is now a prepared suspension that falls to D at 228. Protection: cycles 19 and 34 list "arrival and chorale 223–234" as do-not-touch because they are at master level, with no other reason given. This change leaves the arrival chord, the brass chorale, the canon line (Vln I·II, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1), dynamics and tremolo untouched. It changes only three second-player notes, and it removes the one place where the chorale's D♭ is blurred.

### 4. 126–129: the bassoon counter-line — applied
Bars: 126, 128–129, Bn 1.
Problem: at 128 Bn 1 rises A3→C4 on beat 3. That doubles the seventh of D7 with the violas' C4, and the C then leaps to G3 at 129 while the violas resolve to B3. With finding 2, 126's second half becomes I6, and Bn 1's E3 would be a fourth over B.
Evidence: A&S PDF 375 and 377 (the seventh resolves by stepwise descent; dissonances resolve).
Fix: `devBsnOne: ... | e2(\p... fis4 g4 | c'2 d'2 | a2 b4 c'4 | a2 c'2 | b1\< | c'1) |`. I first tried G3 at 126; p5.py then found fifths G3–A3 against the violas' D4–E4 into 127, so it is D4. The line is now E–F♯–G | C–D | A–B–C | A–C | B | C, and B→C at 129–130 is a step.

### 5. 210: the seventh G♭ sounds against its own resolution — applied
Bars: 210, Fl 1 (G♭6), Cl 1 (G♭5) against Vln I F6 and Vln II F5 on beat 3.
Problem: the violins' run lands on F (the resolution of A♭7's seventh) while Fl 1 and Cl 1 still hold G♭ a semitone above, in the same octaves, at the crescendo's peak.
Evidence: A&S PDF 375 (the seventh resolves down by step); PDF 376, Ex. 22-5 (Beethoven op. 95): a resolution may be transferred to another instrument, provided the dissonance stops where the resolution arrives.
Fix (codaOne.ily): `codaOneFluteOne: ... ges'''1\< | ges'''2 r2\! |` and `codaOneClOne: ... ges''1\< | ges''2 r2\! |`.
