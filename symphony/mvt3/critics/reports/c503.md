## Findings

### 1. 198.3–4: a chromatic 6/3 in the bass into the coda (tested in the scratch build; not better; not applied)

**Candidate.** The recapitulation's A♭ statement of F2 ends at 198. The bass holds A♭1 (Cb) and A♭2 (Bn 2) for the whole bar under the turn to A♭ minor: since c294, violas and cellos have B3 (C♭) from 198.3, spelled as a rising tone, and the cellos move B3→C4 at 199. So the bass leaps A♭→C into the hammered C-minor tutti. The reading suggested two things. A rising chromatic tone is a 6/3 of contrapuntal meaning, spelled sharp or natural (PDF 302, Mendelssohn 18-2, bar 99). And a 5/3 can be expanded by its own 6/3 (Ex. 18-19e). Together they suggest putting the B in the bass for the last half bar, so the bass also rises by semitone into 199.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-503/work/sections/recS.ily I used Cb `aes,,2\f\< b,,2`, Bn 2 `aes,2\f\< b,4 r4`, and Bn 1 `es2\f\< aes4` (to keep the root A♭ in the chord). Build: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. The test file is kept at /tmp/critic-503/recS-c1-test.ily.

**Result.** It is worse, for three reasons. (a) At 198.3 the B that leads to C sounds in B1, B2 and twice at B3. A&S PDF 293 says not to double the leading tone when the tonic follows, and par8 now flags 198.3 (Bn 2 octaves with the bass) and 199.1 (cellos B3–C4 with the bass B1–C2). (b) On 198.4 Bn 1 has released, so the chord is a bare B–E♭ third. A♭ minor, the point of c294's turn, is no longer heard. (c) A&S PDF 290–291 says the 5/3 is the chord that sounds like a goal. The end of the recapitulation should sound like an ending before the coda starts again from C minor at full strength. The root-position VI (A♭) moving to i (C) up a major third is the stronger join, and it is movement I's own A♭–C relation. Reverted; the scratch sections are identical to v503.

### 2. The three descending 6/3 series follow the reading; no change

87–89 (I6–vii6–vi6–V6–IV6 with 7–6 suspensions, into V4/3 of V since c502), the lament 107–109, and the chain of diminished sevenths 135–138 each run from a stable point to a goal (E♭ at 90, the F-minor cadential 6/4 at 109.3, the stop chord at 138). Each is articulated only at its ends: no accents inside 87–89 since c233, and the sf at 138 only. Each has a compound upper line over a stepwise bass (PDF 290–293, Ex. 18-2 to 18-6). In 135–137 the parts move in minor thirds or rise against the bass (c364, c367). There are no doubled tones and no augmented seconds. No fix.

### 3. The Phrygian 7–6 and the 18-11 descents are in place; no change

The cadence that the reading says almost always has a 7–6 (PDF 297–298, Ex. 18-12) has it at all three places in the score: 57–58 and 177–178 (Cl 2 since c413) and 121–122 (Cl 1 since c323). Ex. 18-11a, the passing V6 between I and VI, is the bass of the arrival chorale, 224–227 (C–C–B–A). Ex. 18-11b, the lengthened applied chord with a suspension into V's leading tone, has no free place. Its only candidates are F1's approaches to V at 20 and 160, where the tune's F5 on beat 3 blocks an F♯ bass, as c413 found, and 160 is protected (c192, c412). No fix.

### 4. The development's two soft dominant interludes (101–102, 105–106); checked, kept

After each F1-head statement the music stops on an sf chord and holds V6 of the key just heard (E bass at 101–102, A bass at 105–106) under the scale figure. The next statement then enters a fifth lower (B♭ minor at 103, the F-minor lament at 107), so neither leading-tone bass resolves. In the reading's terms these are 6/3s that stand for V (PDF 300–301) and are left by a sequence step. The descending-fifth sequence F–B♭ (c383) and the soft pause before each new head shape the development's first half. A resolving bass E–F at 102.3 would anticipate F and blunt the B♭-minor entry. Not tested beyond the MIDI dump; no fix.

### 5. Proportions and the coda's stages; no change

See the timings above. The development has Dvořák 9/iv's proportion to the exposition. The coda is long by design, and each fast stage outgrows the one before it. Under the cyclic rule the stages must avoid a root-position V–I until 262–263. They do this with inverted dominants (V4/3 at 245–246 into 247, V6/5 at 147 and 170) and with the plagal close at 231–234, so the single root V–I at 262→263 keeps its weight. No fix.
