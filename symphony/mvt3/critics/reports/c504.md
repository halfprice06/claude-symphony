## Findings

### 1. Bar 85: a major second at the bottom of the f tutti (applied)
Problem: on 85 beats 3–4 the basses and cellos pass B♭–A♭–F (c501's change, F1's own bar-13 bass shape) under Bn 2, which still holds B♭2 from the earlier bass. At 85.3 the bottom of the chord is A♭2 (Cb) with B♭2 (Bn 2) a whole tone above it, and A♭3 (Vc) under B♭3 (Bn 1). Nothing else sits that low.
Evidence: Adler PDF 257: "the greater gaps exist between the bass and the next highest voice. This spacing is preferred"; Ex. VIII-25 (PDF 257) marks a low close bassoon/bass-clarinet voicing "bad", and Ex. VIII-26/27 (PDF 258) show the bass a fifth or more below the next voice. c501 changed the bass but not Bn 2, so the second is new since v502, not a protected texture.
Fix (clos.ily, `closBsnTwo`, bar 3 of the section): `d2 bes,2` → `d1`. Bn 2 holds D3 (the chord's third) for the bar, so 85.3 is A♭2–D3 (a tritone, the V4/2 shape) and 85.4 F2–D3; Bn 2 goes D3→G2 into the I6 at 86 as before. B♭ is still present in Bn 1, Hn 1·2 and the violas. Bass, tune, dynamics and c501's line are unchanged. par8.py output for 84–87 is identical to v504's.

### 2. Bar 192: Bn 2's G2 is left over from the old V6/5 (applied)
Problem: in the A♭ statement of F2, bar 192 is V7 of A♭ with E♭2 in the basses. Bn 2 plays G2, a third above that low bass at p. The G2 is a relic: until c262 the bass at 192 was G (a V6/5, a holdover from cycle 12 when 187–198 were in C); c262 put the root E♭2 in the basses and moved the cellos, but left Bn 2 on the old bass note. The result is the low close third E♭2–G2.
Evidence: Adler PDF 257 and Ex. VIII-25 (as above); Ex. VIII-26/27 (PDF 258). recS.ily's own c262 comment states that the root belongs in the bass and that the cellos' G3 no longer doubles a leading-tone bass; Bn 2 was not part of that change.
Fix (recS.ily, `recSBsnTwo`, sixth bar of the A♭ statement): `\transpose c aes, { b,1 }` → `\transpose c aes, { d1 }`, so Bn 2 plays B♭2, a fifth over the bass, and steps to A♭2 at 193 as its line already does. The third G stays in Bn 1 and the cellos (G3). par8.py output for 190–194 is identical to v504's.

### 3. Candidate tested and declined: 8′/4′ flutes on the closing theme, 83–86
I took Adler PDF 250–251 (Ex. VIII-15 to VIII-17: two like winds are better in octaves than in unison; Rossini's own scoring puts the flute above) as a candidate for Fl 1·2, which double Vln I at pitch a2 at 83–86 (E♭6–F6). Moving Fl 1 up an octave puts it on E♭7–F7, past the practical top, and even a partial lift would anticipate the register expansion that follows: Fl 1's run reaches F6 at 89 and the flutes' G6/A♭6 strokes at 91 are the section's top. Adler PDF 238 (Ex. VIII-4, VIII-5) calls such an early register expansion unacceptable. This is also a forte tutti with Vln I on the line, the exception Adler makes on PDF 250. No change.

### 4. Checked, no change: the cor anglais inside the tune at 159–166
Adler PDF 241–243 (Figaro, Ex. VIII-10) says sustained wind harmony should sit at a distance from the active theme. At 159–166 the cor anglais holds G4–F4–C5 at mf inside the lower octave of F1's consequent (Ob 1, Vln I lower divisi, which descend through it to D4). I decided against moving it. An octave up it would cross the flutes and Vln I, and an octave down it would sit in the horns and violas. At mf under an f/ff tutti it adds only a soft held inner voice. It also dates from c446 (Ob 2's line moved to the cor anglais), and I have no new reason to reverse that.

### 5. Checked, no change: a2 unisons at soft dynamics
The scan finds soft a2 unisons only at 67–75 (bassoons, p, on the bass; kept by c277) and 219–222 (oboes, clarinets and horns overlapped in the crescendo from the sub. p, checked by c414 and protected). The flutes' a2 on the plagal close top line 231–234 is doubled by Tpt 1 two octaves below at A4, so the line comes through (PDF 255).
