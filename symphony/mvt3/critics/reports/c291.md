## Findings

### 1. 77-80: F2's peak has three string octaves but winds on only two. Cl 1·2's thirds cross the cellos' tune octave. APPLIED

**Bars:** 77-80 (sTwo.ily, clarinets only).

**Problem:** Since v282 (c281) the cellos double F2 an octave below Vln II, so the tune's climax is in three octaves: B♭6 (Vln I + Fl 1), B♭5 (Vln II + Ob 1) and B♭4 (cellos, tenor clef). Fl 1 and Ob 1 each give their octave one wind; the lowest octave has none. In that same register the clarinets still play c149's thirds, B♭4/G4 – A♭4/F4 | G4/E♭4 – B♭4/F4 | C5/A♭4. Cl 2 meets the cellos' F4 at the unison on 77.4 and 78.4, and Cl 1's B♭4 crosses above the cellos' G4–F4 on 78.3–4. The tune's lowest octave and a counter line in thirds therefore weave through the same fifth (E♭4–B♭4), just where the melody should be clearest.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 69: a melody in three octaves keeps the natural order Fl / Ob / Cl. PDF 76-77: "'Cellos + Cl." is one of "the best and most natural" unisons, and one wind per string group keeps the strings dominant. PDF 67: placing parts across each other's registers "creates an unnatural resonance". PDF 78, Servilia [93]: each octave of a string tune gets its own wind (Vns + Fl / Violas + Ob / Cellos + Fag). Sheherazade No. 90 (PDF 265) shows the same principle with clarinets on the low octave. The reason c149 gave for the thirds was "Cl 1-2 fill the gap of an octave and a fifth between the pad (E♭4) and the F2 peak (B♭5)" (sTwo.ily comment, Rimsky PDF 141). Since v282 that gap is filled by the tune itself, so the reason no longer applies. Answering the protection: "77-82 balance" (c094, c098, c187) protects the tune against its counter-melodies. This change takes weight from a counter and gives it to the tune, the same direction as c281.

**Fix (applied):** Cl 1 doubles the cellos at pitch for as long as Ob 1 doubles Vln II, with Ob 1's slurs, breath and dynamics. Cl 2 rests.

F2 is now Fl + Vln I / Ob + Vln II / Cl + Vc at its peak. F2's notes do not change; only its doubling does, so the other movement threads need not be told. The harmony that the thirds supplied (G, B♭, E♭, F, A♭, C) is still sounded by the violas, Hn 1·2, Bn 1·2 and the harp's broken chords. Cl 1's lowest note is G3 (written A3), and it ends at 80 as Ob 1 does, two bars before the clarinets' f entry at 83.

The finale record is 39/39. Page 4 checked as an image: the clarinet staff shows "1." and the written line, and the system spacing is unchanged.

### 2. 224-228: the chorale's motto line has no woodwind, while Bn 1 doubles Bn 2 at the unison. Considered; no change

Rimsky PDF 75 says "Trombone + Fag." gives "a finer legato", and Brahms 1/iv puts the bassoons with the trombones' chorale. At 225-227 Bn 1 plays C3–B2–A2 in unison with Bn 2, which is redundant. I decided against moving it to the motto line G3–E4–D4–C4–D♭4–C4. The chorale is the owed *brass* chorale, and its first two bars are meant to sound as pure brass over the tremolo. The woodwinds' first entry is the canon at 226 (Fl, Ob, Cl), so a bassoon on the motto at 224 would take away the moment when the woodwind enters. The passage is also protected (223-234, c103, c127, c181).

### 3. Brass melody (PDF 71-73). Checked; no change

The trumpet solo 51-66 is diatonic in E♭ minor, like Rimsky's Ex. 71-72. Both chorale statements are diatonic apart from the protected D♭. F1 in C major (265-268) in Tpt 1·2 is diatonic, with B♭ as a passing note, and so is "rousing and triumphant in the major" (PDF 72). The fanfare material (147-150, 243-246, 277) uses natural-scale tones in two or three parts, as in Ex. 70. Theme B at 211-218 is the one chromatic, cantabile brass tune, a type PDF 73 warns against. The score already applies the reading's remedy: Ob 1 + Cl 1 in unison for a finer legato and two flutes above in the top register (PDF 75). Protected (205-223); no change.

### 4. Soft horn melody (PDF 72) is used only as doubling. Considered; no change

As c201 found, the passages where a soft horn would fit (139-150, 187-198, 216-223, 75-82) are protected, and since v288 Hn 1 carries the counter-melody at 67-74. I looked again at 119 and 122. Those bars are answers to the bassoon's F2 in the violins' high register, and a horn there would sit in the bassoon's register and blur the dialogue. No change.

### 5. No theme is ever stated in parallel thirds (PDF 70-71). Decided not to add one

Brahms and Dvořák often state a tune in thirds. In this score every place where a theme could take a third below is either protected (187-198, 231-234, 83-86, 255-264) or a quotation whose pitches are fixed (95-98 is movement II's tail at pitch). At the one free place, 77-79, thirds were the problem that finding 1 removes. No change.

### 6. 19-26: two horns at f under only two oboes (PDF 75). Checked; no change

PDF 75 asks for three or four winds an octave above two horns in unison at f. At 19-26 the C5 octave has Ob 1·2 a2 plus all of Vln II, so the octave already has the weight the rule asks for. Adding the free Cl 2 would put an f tune on the same staff as Cl 1's mf leggiero broken chords, which breaks the equal-dynamics rule for shared staves. No change.

### Melodic verdict

F1 (head cell, sequence, A♭6 peak at 22/162), F2 (one contour from trumpet to violins, dotted peak at 78, suspension at 80-81) and the motto chorale are each scored so that the tune is the top line or the loudest line in its register. After finding 1 this is also true of F2's lowest octave. The final combination (247-254) follows the reading's three-group unison (Va + Cl + Hn on the S head).
