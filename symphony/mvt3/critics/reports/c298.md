## Findings

### 1. 37-39: since v298 the head at 39 is the softest line in the tutti, and at 37-38 the horns' head competes with an ff countersubject in its own register (applied)

**Problem.** c297 gave F1's B♭-minor head at 37-38 to Hn 3·4 a2, ff "ben marc.", and marked the violins' countersubject f. It left two dynamic points behind:
- At 37-38 the same countersubject in violas, cellos and Bn 1 is still ff from bar 27. It runs at E♭4-A♭4, the horns' register (B♭4-F4-G♭4-A♭4-E♭4-D4), so three string and wind parts at ff cover two horns at ff. Adler weighs two unison horns as one trumpet (adler2 PDF 44), which c297 itself cites.
- At 39 the violins take the head back over the held V7 on the B♭ pedal. They are still at c297's f, while violas, cellos, basses and bassoons hold ff > and Hn 3·4 come out of their head at ff and hold F4/B♭3 ff >. Vln II's F4 at 39 is in unison with Hn 3·4's F4. In v297 the violins were ff here and Hn 3·4 were f (c277). The tune is now one level under its own accompaniment, the reverse of the v297 balance.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348 §4: the voice that carries the suspension comes forward, and performers accent the suspension and play its resolution softer. At 38.3-4 that voice is the horns' E♭4-D4, a cadential 4-3 over B♭ into the half cadence at 39. PDF 350 §8 calls this the suspension's most important use, because it stresses the leading tone D. PDF 367-368 §28: a pedal is the static substructure while the other voices move. At 39-42 the pedal chord is static and the head moves over it, so the head has to sound above it. adler2 PDF 201 (point 3): a diminuendo is clearer when it is written out. c237 wrote out this one by drop-outs, and it assumes the head on top.

**Fix (applied, sections/pTwo.ily, with a c298 comment).**
```
pTwoVla, pTwoVc, pTwoBsnOne  37:  f'8(\f es'8 des'8 c'8) bes8( des'8 f'8 bes8)
pTwoBsnTwo                   37:  bes,1\f            % shared staff with Bn 1
pTwoVlnOne                   39:  f''2->(\ff\> bes'4. c''8
pTwoVlnTwo                   39:  f'2->(\ff\> bes4. c'8
pTwoHnThree / pTwoHnFour     39:  f'1~\f\>  /  bes1~\f\>
```
The countersubject now has one level, f, in all three octaves (violins, violas and cellos, Bn 1), under the horns' ff head. At 39 the violins' head is ff > p over a held chord that starts at f or below. The only exception is the basses, which stay ff on the pedal. No note changed. Both Bn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 have equal simultaneous dynamics on their shared staves. F1's notes and c297's layout are unchanged. Only levels change, so the other movement threads need not be told.

### 2. 81: "animando" presses on the bar where the 7/6 suspension resolves (applied)

**Problem.** c241 gave F2's E♭-major cadence a 7/6 suspension over V7. G is tied from 80.4 into 81 and falls to F on 81.2, with the diminuendo starting on the resolution (c248). The printed "animando" (MIDI 152) starts on that same bar, so the tempo speeds up while the line relaxes. The crescendo and the run G-A♭-B♭-D into Tempo I come in bar 82. c113 wrote that run so that it would carry the animando ("animando now carried").

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348 §3: the resolution "should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity". An accelerando adds intensity. PDF 359-360 §21: delaying a resolution builds tension, and PDF 359 Ex. 21-25 holds the suspended tone under a fermata. PDF 353-354 Ex. 21-15e-f: in the Strauss waltz the 7/6 over V7 takes its full bar. Tempo and dynamics now go the same way: in tempo with the diminuendo at 81, then animando with the crescendo at 82.

### 3. 108: Vln II's 4-3 resolution falls on a bow change, while its wind doubling is slurred (considered, not applied)

At 108.3-4 Vln II's G5 (tied from 107.3) is a 4 over D and resolves to F5. In the part, `g''2~ g''4 f''4~`, the F starts a new bow. Ob 1 and Cl 1, which double it at pitch since c213, slur G-F-E (`g''4( f''4~ | f''2. e''4)`). At 109 the violins do slur F-E. By PDF 348 §4 the resolution should be the softer note, and a new bow adds an attack. I decided not to change it. The lament 107-110 is protected (c053, c072, c151, c197, c213: "lament bass and strings unchanged"). One bow from 107.3 to 109.4 is eight quarters in a crescendo from p to the ff at 110. That would starve the crescendo, and the bow change is the players' way of building it. The winds' slur is enough to keep the resolution legato in the combined sound.
