## Findings

### 1. 224-228: the major motto, the chorale's cantus at movement I's pitch, is carried by one trombone (applied)

**Problem.** At the arrival, the line the whole cycle has been heading for is the motto in major, G-E-D-C-D♭-C, in Tbn 1 at movement I's pitch, with Tpt 1 an octave above. Against it, Tbn 2 plays a separate tenor part: E3, C4, G3, E3/F3, F3. All five of those notes are already held by a horn (Hn 3 C4 at 225; Hn 4 G3, E3/F3, F3 at 226-228), except the E3 at 224. That E3 is a low doubled third under the alto, the same doubling c029 took out of the horns at 224.

Measured with Adler's weights, the cantus's lower octave is 1 unit at 224 (Tbn 1 G3) and 1.5 units from 225 (Tbn 1 + Hn 2). The inner G4 and C5 have Tpt 2 + Hn 1 (1.5 units), and D4 at 226 has three players. So the line that quotes movement I is no stronger than the harmony around it. The canon is ff in the winds and violins two bars later, so from 226 the brass statement also competes with it.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 38-41, Ex. XI-21 (Tchaikovsky 4/i 277-290). This is Adler's model for exactly this moment: a break-off, then a huge tutti with a timpani roll and a long-note cantus firmus against earlier fragments. The cantus is in the tenor trombones marked "a 2", in unison, with the bass trombone and tuba below. It is not one trombone among chord tones.
- Adler PDF 15: above mf one trombone equals two horns. A cantus in two trombones therefore outweighs any single inner part.
- Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15-16): at f the trombones sit close inside the chord, with no doubled third below the alto.
- Movement I's own chorale (I 175-179) gives its motto two trombones (Tbn 1 + bass trombone in octaves). In the finale's 224-228 the bass trombone has the independent bass C-C-B-A-F, so Tbn 2 is the player who can restore that weight.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/arrival.ily, Tbn 2 doubles Tbn 1 at the unison for 224-228, and the staff prints "a2":
```
arrivalTbnTwo = { e1\fff \breathe | g1\f | e'1 | d'1 | c'2 des'2 | c'1 | R1 | g1\f | f1 | g1 | f1\> | e2.\mf r4 | }
```
It was `e1\fff \breathe | e1\f | c'1 | g1 | e2 f2 | f1 | R1 | ...`.

**What the change keeps.**
- Notes: the top line, Tbn 1, the bass, the canon, the horns, all dynamics and the breaths are unchanged.
- Harmony: every pitch class of every chord stays, because Hn 3 and Hn 4 already doubled Tbn 2's notes. Only the low E3 at 224 is gone.
- Layers: no layer is added (c159's "no more inner layers 223-234"). One duplicate inner part becomes a doubling of the cantus.

**Answer to the 223-234 protection.** The protected items are the arrival chord, the chorale top line, Tbn 1 at movement I's pitch, the canon two bars later, 222→223, and the timpani quotation and C pedal at 229-230. None of them changes.

**Note for the other threads.** The chorale top line (a finale theme) keeps its notes. Its lower octave, item 4 of finale.md ("Tbn 1 is at movement I's pitch"), becomes "Tbn 1·2 a2 at movement I's pitch". The record line choraleFirst TbnOne 224 still passes.
