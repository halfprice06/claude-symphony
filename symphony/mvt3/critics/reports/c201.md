## Findings

### 1. 35-38: F1's head in A♭ and B♭ minor lies under a held flute line, so it is not the top part (applied)
- **Problem.** At 35-38, F1's head is in Vln I+II in unison at A♭5 and B♭5, ff. It answers the bass statement at 27-34 and is the head's only sequence in the exposition (A♭ major, then B♭ minor). Above it, Fl 1 held A♭5 | G5-A♭5 | D♭6 | C6-D6 at mf. From beat 3 of 35 the head falls to E♭5-F5 | G5-F5-E♭5-D♭5-C5 | F5-G♭5 | E♭5-D5, so for most of these four bars the top sounding line was the flute's chord tones (D♭6-C6-D6 over the head's B♭5-F5-G♭5-A♭5). The rest of the wind section held mf chords, and no wind doubled the tune at ff.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 54 (the string pages, which this chapter builds on): a melody in the top part stands out by position alone. PDF 67: doubling belongs to loud passages. PDF 76-77: "Vns + Fl" is one of the most natural unisons, and the strings predominate when the powers are equal. In the exposition's other forte statements the tune is the top line and is doubled: 19-26 (Fl 1 8va, Ob 1·2 at Vln II's octave) and 83-86 (Fl 1·2 with Vln I). Cycle 191 marked 35-38 as "winds hold, no doubling" under the rule that doubling is optional "at a soft dynamic", but 35-38 is ff. For memorability: F1's head is heard here in two new keys, and this is its only sequential continuation in the exposition. A listener follows the top line, which here was a held D♭6 rather than the theme.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-201/work/sections/pTwo.ily).** Fl 1 plays the head in unison with the violins, with their articulation, at f (one level under the strings' ff). Fl 2 keeps its chord tones and is marked f with it, because the staff is shared.
  ```
  pTwoFluteOne = { ... | g''2. r4 | aes''2->(\f es''4. f''8 | g''4-.) f''8( es''8 des''4-.) c''4-. | bes''2->( f''4. ges''8 | aes''4-.) ges''8( f''8 es''4-.) d''4-. | bes''1 | ... }
  pTwoFluteTwo = { ... | d''2. r4 | c''1\f | es''1 | des''1 | c''2 bes'2 | ... }
  ```
  The oboes, clarinets and Hn 1·2 keep their chords unchanged. The D♭ of 36 (Ob 1) stays, so V7 of A♭ keeps its seventh. At 39 Fl 1 returns to B♭5 as before. F1's notes are unchanged. Only its doubling changes, so no other movement thread needs to be told.
- **Alternative tested and rejected.** I first scored Rimsky PDF 78's distribution: Fl 1 an octave above (A♭6 to B♭6) and Ob 1 in unison with the violins. That build reached 17 pages. With Fl 1 in unison and Ob 1 still on the head, the build had 16 pages but warned that page 2 was compressed. Both versions add a second rhythmic voice to a shared staff in the densest system of page 2. The applied version is the one that keeps 16 pages with 0 warnings. It also follows PDF 76 more closely: one wind per string group, with the strings still predominant.
