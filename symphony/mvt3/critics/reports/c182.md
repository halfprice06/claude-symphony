## Findings

### 1. 242: the ii7 before III♯ has no third (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 242 should be ii7 (D-F-A-C) going to III♯ (E major) at 243. That move closes the C-A♭-E cycle. But no part plays F. The harp has A3-C4-D4-A4. Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1 and Ob 1 play the tune D-C. Hn 3 has D4-C4 and Hn 4 A3. Cl 1 and the violas have C4-D4, Bn 1 D4, and the bass D. The chord sounds as an open D-A-C with no quality, a fifth with a seventh. It has been like this since c020 moved the bass from F♯ to D. **Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "Because of the empty sound and the lack of major or minor quality, the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects", and root-position seventh chords omit the fifth, not the third. finale.md and c113 describe the chord as ii7, so the omission is not an intended effect. **Fix.** sHead.ily:
- `sHeadHarpR` bar 242: `a8 c'8 f'8 a'8 a'8 f'8 c'8 a8` (was `d'`). F♮ is already set on the pedal diagram.
- New `sHeadFluteTwo = { R1*7 | f''1\f | e''2 r2 | R1*3 | }`: F5 falls to E5, the root of III♯.

The F has to lie above the seventh. Vln II's C5-B4 and Hn 3's C4-B3 carry the seventh, and an F below them makes fifths F-C to E-B. I tried Bn 1 F4-E4 first, and my check found exactly those fifths against Vln II. Fl 2 at F5 moves in fourths with Vln II, which PDF 85 permits between upper voices. Fl 2 rested through 235-246, so no line is lost. This changes nothing c106/c215 protect: the progression ii7-III♯-V, the single G♯ in Vln I/Ob 1, the brass E-major chord and the seventh's resolution all stay. The chord now has its third.

### 2. 252→253: the two themes move in octaves into 253 (applied)
**Problem.** The P head ends 252 with A6 (dotted quarter) and then G6 (Fl 1·2, Vln I·II). The S head ends 252 with G on beat 4 (Ob 1, Cl 1·2 G5; Vla, Hn 1·2 G4). Both fall to F on 253's downbeat. That is G-G to F-F in octaves and double octaves between two independent themes. c091 kept "the shared downbeat F" but did not check how it is approached. **Evidence.** A&S PDF 83: parallel octaves mean one part "duplicates the pitch and motion of the other... not enough to give the feeling of two individual voices". PDF 84 exempts doublings only, and these are the two different heads of owed item 6. In 247-251 the heads never move in parallel. **Fix.** finale.ily, bar 252 in finaleFluteOne, finaleFluteTwo, finaleVlnOne and finaleVlnTwo: `a'''4.-> e'''8 |` (was `g'''8`). The P line leaps down a fourth from its accented A6 and then rises by step, E6-F6-G6-A6, into 253 (PDF 81). The two lines meet the F by contrary motion. The heads still meet on F, but there is no parallel. This changes one note of the P head's free continuation, which is not in the record (pHeadMajor is 247-248). It changes no finale theme. The finale.md item 8 description should note it.

### 3. 238→239: two pairs of fifths as the tutti enters (applied)
**Problem.** At 238 (V7 over the C pedal) Hn 3 has D4 and Hn 4 G3, from c129. At 239 they go to E4 and E3 while the cellos go G2→A2 and the basses C2→A1. That makes Hn 3 and the cellos D4/G2 → E4/A2 (twelfths) and Hn 4 and the basses G3/C2 → E3/A1 (twelfths). Both are similar-motion parallels with the bass instruments, on the f entry of 239. **Evidence.** A&S PDF 83 (Ex. 5-19). These are real voices. The harp's fifths with the horns at the same spot are fillers (PDF 85), but the horns are not. **Fix.** sHead.ily: `sHeadHnThree` bar 239 `c'1\mf` (was `e'1`), and `sHeadHnFour` bar 239 `c'1\mf` (was `e1`). Hn 3 steps D4→C4 and Hn 4 rises a fourth G3→C4. The vi chord keeps the root A doubled in the bass, with the third C in the horns and Bn 1 and the fifth E in the tune and harp. At 240 the horns divide to F4/A3 in contrary motion to the bass. c082's reason for E3 (to resolve a seventh F3) lapsed when c129 moved Hn 4 to G3; the seventh is now in Cl 1/Vla.

### 4. 260→261: the doubled seventh of iiø6/5 falls in octaves (applied)
**Problem.** At 259-260 the seventh C of iiø6/5 is doubled, Hn 4 C4 and Ob 2 C5 (c069). Both then leap down to A♭ at 261 (C4→A♭3, C5→A♭4), so there are parallel octaves just before the last cadence. **Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: the seventh "must never be doubled". The reason is this kind of shared motion, and PDF 83 forbids the octaves themselves. At 261 C is the German sixth's common tone and needs no resolution. **Fix.** finaleOboeTwo bar 261: `c''1 |` (was `aes'1`). Ob 2 holds the common tone C5 and goes to F4, the V7's seventh, at 262, then E4 at 263. Hn 4 keeps its own line C4-A♭3-G3. I first tried holding C4 in Hn 4 instead, but that made C-G octaves with the first and last notes of Vln II's arpeggio at 261→262, so I reverted it. The progression, the top line D-C-B-C, Tpt 1's B→C, the timpani and the brass cadence chord (protected, 259-263) are unchanged.

### 5. 249.3: an inner third-below line makes a non-chord B against C (applied)
**Problem.** Ob 2 and Tpt 2 shadow the S head a third below. On beat 3 of 249 that gives B4 (`d''4. c''8 b'4 d''8 f''8`). It sounds against Tpt 3's repeated C5, the bass F and the chord F-A-C-D, on the strong half of the bar, and it is left by leap. The result is a minor second inside the trumpet section and a tritone with the bass. **Evidence.** A&S PDF 80: the inner voices' "main function... is to complete the tones of the chord framed by the bass and soprano". PDF 82: parallel thirds are useful, but here the third-below shadow produces a non-chord tone that is neither prepared nor resolved. At 253 the same figure (C5-B4-A4) is a passing tone and stays. **Fix.** finale.ily, finaleOboeTwo and finaleTptTwo bar 249: `d''4. c''8 a'4 d''8 f''8 |`. A4 is a chord tone, at the unison with Hn 3. The recorded S head (Tpt 1, Hn 1, Vla) is untouched, and the record gives 36/36.

### 6. 226-227 and 249-250: sevenths in the bass doublings (applied)
**Problem.** The bass at 226-227 is B→A. The contrabassoon (B0) and Bn 2 (B1) cannot go lower, so they leap up a minor seventh to A1 and A2. At 249→250 the basses leap down a seventh, F2→G1, while tuba and contrabassoon go F1→G1. In the chorale the contrabassoon's rising seventh also makes the outer voices' only hidden fifth with a leaping soprano, at 227 (G5→E6 over B0→A1). **Evidence.** A&S PDF 82: dissonant leaps are excluded from simple settings. PDF 81: an octave leap is acceptable when the line then turns back by step. PDF 87-88: hidden fifths in the outer voices only with a stepwise soprano. **Fix.** arrival.ily: `arrivalBsnTwo = { c,1\fff | c,1\f | c1 \breathe | b,1 | a,1 | ...` and `arrivalCbsn = { c,,1\fff | c,,1\f | c,1 \breathe | b,,1 | a,,1 | ...`. Both take the octave at 225, on a repeated C, and then go B-A by step. Bn 1·2 play a2 for 225-227, and the contrabassoon joins the tuba. In finale.ily, finaleCb bar 249: `f,,8` ×8 (F1, was F2), so the basses go D2-F1-G1 with the tuba. Nothing protected at the arrival changes: the 223 chord, tremolo, C7, chorale top line, canon and the C pedal under the timpani at 229-230 are all as before.
