## Findings

Most of the string writing already does what this chapter teaches, and most of what it does not do lies in protected bars. I found one real problem in an unprotected passage.

### 1. 83-86: the violas' f eighths run through the lower octave of F1's head (applied)

Problem. In the closing group F1's head in E♭ has two octaves. Vln I and Fl 1·2 play at E♭6. Vln II, Ob 1 and Cl 1·2 play at E♭5 and fall to G4 (84.4) and F4 (85.1). The violas' broken chords at f lie in exactly that octave: E♭4-B♭4 at 83, F4-B♭4-G4-E♭4 at 84, D4-F4-B♭4 at 85. At 84 beats 3-4 the violas' B♭4 sits above Vln II's A♭4 (the tune's seventh) and then meets its G4. At 85.1 and 85.2 the violas strike F4 and B♭4 together with the tune. The figure is marked f, the same level as the tune. At 19-26 the same figure under F1's consequent is mf.

Evidence. Adler PDF 135 says that when foreground and background are played by instruments of similar colour, the background should be kept out of the melody's register. Vln II and violas are the closest colours in the orchestra. PDF 144 says that where registers cannot be separated, the remedy is a lower dynamic for the background ("forte for the foreground and piano for the background material"). Here the registers cannot be separated. An octave lower, the figure would double the cellos' f quarters at E♭3-B♭3; an octave higher, it would run into the E♭5-E♭6 octaves. So the dynamic is the fix. Cycle 164 applied PDF 135 to this same passage by moving the clarinets up an octave because "the violas' f eighths covered" their low octave. That move solved the clarinets' problem but left the violas' dynamic as it was. Cycle 187's do-not-touch on 83-86 gives no reason, and no note changes here.

Fix (in /tmp/critic-254/work):
- clos.ily, closVla bar 83: `es'8(\f g' bes' g')` → `es'8(\mf g' bes' g')`; bar 87: `<bes es'~>2\mf^\markup \italic "div."` → `<bes es'~>2^\markup \italic "div."` (mf is now already in force; c058's mf at 87 is kept).
- sTwo.ily, sTwoVla bars 81-82: `aes4( ces'4 bes4 f4) | g1\< |` → `aes4( ces'4\> bes4 f4) | g1\mp\< |`. The violas diminish with the violins from 81.2, as cycle 248 set, so the crescendo at 82 lands on a higher printed level (Sebesky rule, c058) instead of going from mf to mf.

The cellos and basses stay f, since the walking quarters are the bass. Vln II, Ob 1 and Cl 1·2 stay f on the tune.
