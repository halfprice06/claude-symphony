## Findings

### 1. 217–218: Bn 1 doubles the first note of movement II's theme-B echo and holds inside its register (applied)

**Problem.** At 217 the cor anglais and Hn 1·2 answer the trumpets' theme B at movement II's own pitch and in its own instrument, f espress.: A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4. Bn 1 re-attacks A♭3 on 217.1, the same pitch at the same moment (`aes1 | aes1 | aes1~\> | aes1\mp`). It then holds A♭3 for two bars, inside the echo's A♭3–G♭4 span. So the echo's first note is a three-colour unison with a bassoon, and the bassoon stays in the echo's register.

**Evidence.** Adler II 274: keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist, especially with wind soloists. Adler II 264: Mozart never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn plays. Cycles c415, c441, c490 and c496 applied the same rule to this echo. They took out Fl 2, Ob 2, Tpt 1·2, Cl 2, Tpt 3, the trombones and the tuba, but missed Bn 1. Rimsky 126: when a part ends on the note the next part begins on, the line passes over cleanly.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-500/work/sections/codaTwo.ily).**
```
codaTwoBsnOne = { f1\ff\> | aes4(\f ges4 f4 es4 | bes2 ges2) | aes4( bes4 c'2) \breathe | aes1 | aes1 | R1 | R1 | c'1\p | ...
```
Before the change, bars 217–218 read `aes1~\> | aes1\mp \breathe`.

**Result.** Bn 1's f A♭3 ends at 216 and the echo enters on a fresh A♭3. Bn 2's F2 diminuendo to mp prints alone as "2." on the shared staff, so no simultaneous dynamics differ. The D♭6 chord stays complete: D♭ in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, the violins and violas; F in Vln I, cellos, basses, Bn 2 and contrabassoon; A♭ in the violas' A♭4. Bn 1 gets a two-bar rest before its p entry at 219, as the brass do.

**Protection.** 216–223 is protected for its path (German sixth, cadential 6/4, V7, stop chord, 222→223), its D♭–C–B–C top line and its dynamic path. All three are unchanged.

**Theme status.** No quotation or theme of the finale changes. Theme B, the echo and the cor anglais notes are as before.

**Build.** 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 214–220 shows only the existing octave doublings of the bass, and one fewer at 219.1. analysis.txt differs only in the bassoon note counts and the 217–220 density line.

### 4. Tool note: lyparse.py does not expand `\repeat unfold` (no score change)

Movement I v564 writes the double basses at I 27–32 as `c,8\ff \repeat unfold 7 c,8 | \repeat unfold 8 d,8 …` (tOne.ily). coherence/lyparse.py reads each `\repeat unfold N x` as one note, so a part-by-part comparison of 199–204 reports a false difference in the double basses. The record does not cover this part. A future movement I change written with `\repeat unfold` would therefore not be caught by themecheck. Fix for the coherence owner: in `lyparse.strip`, before tokenizing, add `text = re.sub(r"\\repeat\s+unfold\s+(\d+)\s+([a-gr]\w*[',]*\d*\.*)", lambda m: ' '.join([m.group(2)] * int(m.group(1))), text)`. That covers the single-note case movement I uses.
