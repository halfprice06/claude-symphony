## Findings

### 1. 35-40: the B♭-minor statement of the head passes to the horns, and the violins keep the countersubject (applied)
**Problem.** At 35-40 F1's head is sequenced three times: A♭ (35), B♭ minor (37) and B♭ over the pedal (39). All three are in the violins, the first two doubled by Fl 1. Meanwhile seven brass parts hold whole and half notes, and the eighth-note countersubject in the violas, cellos and Bn 1 runs underneath. This is the antiphonal block writing that Adler sets aside on PDF 41. In the brass it is not even antiphonal, because the brass only pad. In Adler's main example (Bartók, PDF 44-46, mm. 313-342), trombone II states the subject "f, ben marc.", the trumpets take it in turn, and each voice goes on in free counterpoint after its entry. c277 also noted that the countersubject at 35-38 lies "under ten held wind and brass parts in its register".

**Fix (applied).** Hn 3·4 a2 play the B♭-minor head at B♭4, ff "ben marc.": `bes'2->(\ff^\markup \italic "ben marc." f'4. ges'8 | aes'4-.) ges'8( f'8 es'4-.) d'4-.`. These are the players and register of the development's answer at 103, so 37 now prepares 103, and per Adler PDF 44 two horns in unison weigh as one trumpet. Vln I·II go on in the countersubject, f, two octaves above the violas, cellos and Bn 1: `f'''8(\f es''' des''' c''') bes''( des''' f''' bes'') | c'''8( aes'' f'' aes'') ges''( es''' f''' d''')`. The violins take the head again at 39 as before. The result is a dialogue of strings, then horns, then strings, and the countersubject becomes a real line against the head.

**Adjustments.**
- Hn 3·4 rest on 36.3-4 (`bes2 r2`, `g2 r2`) and breathe there. Without the rest, Hn 4's A♭3 leaping to B♭4 made octaves with the bass's A♭ to B♭.
- Fl 1 drops out of the head and holds `bes''1 | aes''2 bes''2~` into its B♭5 at 39.
- At 38.3 Fl 2, Ob 1 and Ob 2 hold C5 to beat 4 (`c''2. bes'4`). This makes a 9–8 over B♭ beside the head's own 4–3 (E♭–D), and it removes the parallel fifths between their C5 to B♭4 and the horns' F4 to E♭4.
- The chord tones Hn 3·4 gave up are still covered: D♭ by Ob 1·2, Fl 2 and Tbn 1; C by Ob 2 and Tbn 1; A♭ by Tbn 2.

**Checks.** par8 shows no new part-against-bass parallels. c002/par.py shows that this change removes fourteen old octave doublings in 38-39. The only new entries are the violins doubling Bn 1 and the violas' line (intended), and one octave B♭→C that the doubled line makes with the held Cl 2 and Hn 2. Those two parts already move with the violas at the unison there.

**For the other threads.** This changes F1's scoring, not its notes. The B♭-minor statement of the head at 37-38 is now in Hn 3·4 at B♭4 instead of the violins at B♭5. No other movement quotes this bar.

### 2. 67, 187, 195: "o" after stopped horn (applied)
Adler says open and closed brass must be marked exactly in every part (PDF 50, 52-54; the Druckman example marks every open and closed note). Hn 1 is stopped at 49-50 and next plays at 67. Hn 1·2 are stopped at 168-169 and next play at 187 (Hn 1) and 195 (Hn 2). None of these entries cancels the "+", so a player could carry stopped horn into the dolce lines. The retransition already does this correctly (`d'1\p-\open` at 139). Fix: `-\open` on `sTwoHnOne`'s first note, on `recSHnOne`'s first note at 187, and on `recSHnTwo`'s first note at 195. This is notation only; the MIDI is unchanged.

### 3. 139-146, the stretto: entries do not overlap (not applied)
The heads enter every two bars (139, 141, 143, 145), and each head is two bars long. They meet end to start, so this is a sequence of entries, not a stretto in Adler's sense (Bartók mm. 363-386, PDF 47: the entries overlap by a bar). I decided not to change it. The passage is protected (c255, c468, c197). It runs over a stop chord and the timpani's motive x, and each two-bar entry carries one step of the crescendo to 147. Overlapping entries would blur those steps. I did not build an overlapped version here. At 35-36 I checked a half-bar overlap on paper and rejected it: the second entry's G4 at 36.3 lands against the A♭ chord (a minor second with Ob 2's A♭4 and a tritone with the violins' D♭5). That is why finding 1 uses a hand-over instead of an overlap.

### 4. No inversion of F1 (decided against)
Adler's Bartók example has the fugato "then inverted by the horns plus the entire choir" (PDF 44). The finale never inverts F1's head. The one natural place is the development's answer at 103 (Hn 3·4, Tbn 1, low strings). I decided not to apply it. The development is where F1's head is worked as a recognisable subject (99, 103, 130-132, 139-146). An inverted head, which opens with a rising fourth, loses the falling-fourth identity on which the stretto and the coda's entries depend, and 99-109 is protected (c197).

### 5. 211-218: trombones hold whole-note chords under theme B (no change)
Ex. XI-25 values trombone lines that are independent of the trumpets. At 211-218 Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone hold the harmony in whole notes under the trumpets' theme B, while Hn 3·4 have the only counter-figure (212, 214). I kept it. This is a quotation of movement II at Largamente, ff dropping to f. A trombone counter-line would compete with the trumpets in their own register, and the passage is protected (205-218 top line, 211-218 recall).
