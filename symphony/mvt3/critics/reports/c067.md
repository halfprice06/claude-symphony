## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 170, beat 4: the V7♭9 thins to a bare fifth; the leading tone disappears before 171 (applied)
Problem: c066 gave Cl 1·2 and Bn 1·2 a quarter rest on beat 4 of 170. The cellos and basses already rested there (`b,2.\p r4`). On that beat only G3 (Vln II) and D4 (Vln I, Vla) sound: no third, no seventh, no ninth, no bass. The B2 of Bn 2 was the only leading tone that met C3 at 171, where the bassoon pad starts the C-minor second theme.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 144: chordal fullness depends on "the presence of the chordal third somewhere in the ensemble"; PDF 145: a double stop was kept at a resting place "to help bring the line to a stop". MIDI comparison: v66 170.4 = B2 F3 F4 A♭4 G3 D4; v67 170.4 = G3 D4.
Fix (stagger the breaths instead of taking them together): Bn 2 and Cl 2 rest at 171–186 or enter on a new attack, so they do not need the breath.
```
recPBsnTwo: ... d1\> | b,1~ | b,1\p | }      (was b,2.\p r4)
recPClTwo:  ... f'1\> | f'1~ | f'1\p | }      (was f'2.\p r4)
```
Cl 1 and Bn 1 keep c066's rest. Now 170.4 = B2 G3 D4 F4. Equal dynamics on both shared staves.

### 2. Bars 128–130: the last stage of the G-major hand-over lacks weight; Vln II doubles a horn pad (applied)
Problem: the tune reaches Vln I + Fl 1 at 128 beat 4 (A5 | B5 G5 | A5) as the climax of the scoring crescendo into the f at 131. Vln II holds G4 / F♯4 pp at 129–130, which Hn 1 already holds at the same pitch (`g'1 | fis'1`). The lead line has no lower octave, while Rimsky's reason for the hand-over (cycle 33/34: crescendo by addition) calls for more weight at each step.
Evidence: Sebesky rule 6, PDF 140: "Whenever a particularly strong ensemble sound is necessary, especially when the strings are playing a concerted melodic passage, the lead violin line should be reinforced an octave lower." Ex. 202, PDF 142: the build's climax is strengthened by reinforcing the lead line an octave lower. Hn 1 keeps every pitch class Vln II gave up.
Fix:
```
devVlnTwo: ... g'1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | fis'2. a'4(\mf\< | b'2 g'2 | a'1) | r4 fis'8(\f ...
```
This adds to the protected 123–131 hand-over without changing its order or any entry; it serves the stated reason (crescendo by addition). No new parallels (c032 p5.py output identical to v67's).

### 3. Bars 181–182: the violas stay sul tasto pp through the solo's climax (applied)
Problem: the cor anglais and muted trumpet rise to G5 mf at 182 with Fl 1 joining, but the violas' weaving eighths remain sul tasto pp from 171 to 186. The accompaniment does not follow the one swell of the phrase.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 144 (Ex. 194): sul tasto in the low register gives the "soft glowing sound" (this is what 171 does, correctly). PDF 142 (Ex. 202): "Extra tonal body is also achieved by changing from sul tasto to a natural sound" at the climax.
Fix:
```
recSVla bars 181-183: es8(\p\<^\markup \italic "ord." g es g) d8( f d f) | es8(\mp f es f)\> d8( g d g) | es8(\pp^\markup \italic "sul tasto" c' es c') ...
```
The "ord." at 187 stays valid. Pitches unchanged.

### 4. Bars 129–130: Fl 1 on Vln I's line at the same dynamic (applied)
Problem: Fl 1 doubles Vln I at the unison (B5 G5 | A5) at mf <, the same marking as the violins, at the point where the violins first take the tune.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 164: one woodwind weighs about sixteen violins; a flute on the lead violin "thickens the string tone considerably"; "To compensate ... I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings."
Fix: `devFluteOne: ... b''2(\mp\< g''2 | a''1) | a''1\f` (was `\mf\<`). Both reach f at 131.
