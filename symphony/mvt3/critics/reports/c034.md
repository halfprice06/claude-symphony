## Findings

### 1. 273–276: the closing chain G–F♯–F–E is outweighed by the line above it (applied)
**Problem.** The chain is meant to be heard in one voice (finale.md; cycle 30/32). In the G5–C6 octave, directly under the held C6, the moving lines in v34 are scored like this. The chain G5–F♯5–F5–E5 has Ob 2 and the top of Vln II. The line C–A–A♭–G has Fl 2, Ob 1 and the lower note of Vln I (`<a'' c'''>`, `<aes'' c'''>`). That line moves a second or third above the chain, in the same timbres and in the same octave, so it is the higher moving voice and the one the ear follows. In the brass the two lines are about even (chain: Hn 1·2, Tpt 3, Tbn 2; other line: Tpt 2, Tbn 1). The result is that the 5–♯4–4–3 that closes the cross-movement chain sits under a louder parallel line in the very octave where it should speak.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151 (Mozart 40/iv, Ex. XV-3): the heaviest scoring goes in the octave the composer "wants the listener to understand the theme to be", and the other instruments add sheen. Beethoven 9/i (PDF 152) keeps the top octave for a single piercing line. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 115 says the same: how many instruments double a phrase depends on how much power it needs.
**Fix (applied, finale.ily).** Vln I's lower note and Ob 1 take the chain. The C–A–A♭–G line keeps Fl 2, Cl 2, Tpt 2, Tbn 1 and the violas, so cycle 23's inner line is still complete.
```
finaleVlnOne 273–276: <g'' c'''>2:32-> <fis'' c'''>2:32 | <f'' c'''>1:32-> | <e'' c'''>1:32\< | <e'' c'''>1:32 |
finaleOboeOne 273–276: g''2-> fis''2 | f''1-> | e''1~\< | e''1 |
```
The top C6 (Fl 1, Vln I), the bass, the quotation at 275–276 and fff at 278 are unchanged. Vln I and Vln II are now in unison on the chain under C6, and the oboes play it a2. The outer voices did not change, and the all-pairs fifths check (c032/p5.py) prints the same 44 lines as v34.

### 2. 123–126: Vln II's held G4 covers the violas' F2 phrase (applied)
**Problem.** In the hand-over, the violas take F2 at 124 beat 4 and play G4–F♯4–E4–D4 (125–126). Vln II holds `g'1 | fis'1 | g'1 | g'1` pp arco from 123, at the same pitch as the melody's top note and above the rest of it, in the same string colour. The melody's G4 starts in unison with the pedal, and its F♯4 and E4 rub against it from below. The result is that the violas' entry, the one hand-over with no change of colour, cannot be told from the pad. The harp (sounding G2–G3), the cellos' held notes and the double-bass roots already give the full harmony, so the pad adds nothing that is needed. The cello entry at 123 is clear because it lies below Vln II, and the Cl 1 (126) and Vln I (128) entries are clear because they lie above it. Only the viola entry is masked.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 154: the accompanying colour should contrast with the melody. PDF 156: a rest before a colour enters makes it perceived. PDF 158 (Mahler): in a chamber-music distribution no two instruments share a part for more than a few notes.
**Fix (applied, dev.ily).** Vln II rests 123–126 and comes in pp arco at 127 under Cl 1. Bars 127–130 are unchanged.
```
devVlnTwo ... r4 <d'' fis''>4 r2 | R1*4 | g'1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | fis'1 | g'1\< | fis'1 | ...
```
This also adds one more step to cycle 33's crescendo by addition: Vln II re-enters at 127.

### 3. 257: the woodwinds re-enter p under ff brass and ff strings (applied)
**Problem.** Cycle 33 rested the woodwinds at 255–256 and brought them back p< at 257. Everything else is ff there: brass chorale, strings, Cb eighths. At p, the flutes' E6 and the oboes' and clarinets' E5 (doubling the chorale line of Vln I and Tpt 1) cannot be heard for about three bars, so the two-bar rest buys no audible re-entry.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 147–148 (d'Indy, Ex. XV-1): grade each group's dynamic by its strength in the tutti, as the trombones are marked ppp so they do not dominate. Here the grading is reversed, and the weakest group gets the lowest marking. PDF 156: the rest before an entry exists so that the entry is heard. Cycle 33's argument (a second group entering softer and catching up in the crescendo) still works if that group starts at mf.
**Fix (applied, finale.ily).** At 257, Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2 and Cl 1·2 enter `\mf\<` instead of `\p\<`, with the same dynamic on both players of each shared staff. B.cl, Bsn 1·2 and Cbsn stay p: they double ff basses, where their entry is not the point. The hairpin to fff at 263 is unchanged. This partly revises cycle 33: I keep the rest and the crescendo and change only the starting level.
