## Holistic comparison

A conductor who knows Tchaikovsky 5/iv will recognise the same plan: a motto turned to major (224-228 and 255-264, Tchaikovsky's Andante maestoso and Moderato assai), a held dominant and stop before the arrival (219-222; Tchaikovsky's B-major dominant and pause), and a first-movement theme returning in major near the end (247-254, 265-272; Tchaikovsky's Molto meno mosso). This score is tighter than Tchaikovsky's finale: the arrival is prepared by one dominant, not a repeated false ending, and the coda's stages each bring new material. Where Tchaikovsky is reliably better is the weight of his loud tuttis: his bass and tenor registers are always full under the brass. In v219 there is one bar where they are not (finding 1). The other findings are voicing details of the kind Rimsky singles out, and I found nothing larger that is not on the protected list.

Regression check against v209 (cycles 209-218): nothing got worse. The changes I checked against the reading: Cl a2 with the violins' countersubject 27-34 (Rimsky PDF 76: Vn+Cl is a good unison, two clarinets do not overpower two violin sections); Bn 1 with the cellos 187-194 (PDF 76, 78-79, Ex. 91); divided Vln I answers 119/122; bass trombone without octave leaps 153/161/164; the 36-37 5-6 link; 108-109 wind doubling; bowings 9-10, 90-94, 167, 199-204. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 272: empty tenor under the ff IV6/4 (applied)
Problem: on 272 beats 2-4 the whole orchestra above the basses' C2 eighths starts at F4 (Bn 1, Hn 4, violas F4; Hn 3, violas A4; winds and violins above). Cellos, Bn 2, tuba and trombones all stop after beat 1, so for three beats under the allargando there is a gap of two octaves and a fourth (C2-F4), with only the timpani's C3 stroke inside it. 270 has a similar gap, but there the texture is a melody in octaves over the pedal (a general unison, Ex. 79), not a chord.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 85: the bass "should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it", and chords are spaced like the harmonic series, wide below and close above; PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages". Ex. 77 and 86 (PDF 248-250, 260) keep bassoons, trombones and low strings sounding under loud upper chords. Tchaikovsky's maestoso tuttis in 5/iv keep the low strings and bassoons holding.
Fix (finale.ily): cellos hold the C3 on which they end F1 (`b,8 | c4->) r4 r2 |` → `b,8 | c1->) |`), and Bn 2 holds A3 for the bar instead of its last C quarter stroke (the eighth `c4-> r4 r2` before `c1->` becomes `a1->`). 272 now sounds C2 (Cb), C3 (Vc), A3 (Bn 2), F4/A4 and up: the chord third in the tenor, spaced as PDF 85 describes. Bn 2 then falls A3→C3 into 273, which is unchanged. This answers the 265-272 protection (c036, c046, c124: horn and trombone rests, the f foundation): no rest of the horns or trombones is filled, the basses keep their f eighths, and the quarter strokes of Bn 2 and the tuba stay unchanged through 271. Only 272, the allargando bar, holds.

### 2. 91-92: oboes a second apart in the hammered codetta (applied)
Problem: on beat 3 of 91 and 92 (B♭7) Ob 1 holds B♭5 over Ob 2's A♭5, a major second inside the oboe pair at ff. The wind chord also has a fifth's void between B♭5 and the flutes' F6.
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 90 rule 4: concords to instruments of the same kind, discords to different colours, "specially observed in writing for the oboe with its penetrating quality of tone"; PDF 96, remark 1: no void in the intermediate parts in close harmony, "especially in forte". Ex. 113 and 114 (PDF 288) give the oboes thirds and sixths inside ff wind chords.
Fix (clos.ily): Ob 1 `d'''4->` on 91 and 92 beat 3 (was `bes''4->`), Ob 2 `bes''4->` (was `aes''4->`). The wind chord is now A♭6 F6 | D6 B♭5 | D5 B♭4, overlaid in thirds pair by pair with no gap. The seventh A♭ stays in Fl 1, Vln I, Vln II, Hn 2, Tbn 2 and Bn 1, all resolving to G. This does not touch the c167/c168 codetta layout (Vln I with Fl 1 on top, dynamics, bowing) or the low brass (c194).

### 3. 208: oboe pair a minor second apart (applied)
Problem: Ob 2 holds C5 through 207-208 under Ob 1's F1 head, so on 208 beat 3 Ob 1's D♭5 sounds a minor second above Ob 2's C5 (and the two meet in unison on beat 4).
Evidence: Rimsky PDF 90 rule 4 (as finding 2); PDF 102: sevenths and seconds are preferably given to instruments of different colour.
Fix (codaOne.ily, Ob 2): `c''1\f | c''1 | aes''1\fp\<` → `c''1\f | c''2 r2 | aes''1\fp\<`. Ob 2 also gains a breath before its fp A♭5 at 209 (c216). The C is still sounded by the violas and horns. The score prints "1." over Ob 1 for the last two beats. This answers the 205-208 protection (c160, c179, c197: the violas' chord and the rescoring of c176-c178): only two beats of Ob 2's held note change.
