## What else I studied

**Brahms 1/iv.** This is a comparison from my knowledge of the score. The local corpus has no Brahms. Brahms plants his chorale early: the trombones play it pp in the introduction, long before the coda. He also keeps his coda (Più allegro) in tempo when the chorale returns ff, with the strings moving under it. Movement III shares the outline: C-minor struggle, a C-major arrival, a chorale, and a fast close. A conductor would accept its two solo episodes (trumpet at 51, bassoon at 115) and the harmonic plan. Three things would read as student work: the static whole-note blocks at 211–230, two balance faults where the tune is buried or not lifted (211–214, 231–234), and woodwinds out of their natural order at the approach to the arrival (211–218). Findings 1–3 address these. I also considered removing the Maestoso stop at 251, which is the main departure from Brahms's in-tempo chorale. I am keeping it. The chorale at 2 = 100 would pass in about six seconds. The symphony's first V7–I at 254–255 needs that breadth. The stop also lasts only five bars.

**v01 against v09.** I compared every changed variable (a script listed the bars) and both scores' last three pages. Nothing has got worse in what this reading covers. Two changes alter a melody's doubling, and both remain within Rimsky's practice. At 231–234, Vln II moved from the lower octave to unison with Vln I (row 4). At 67–82, the violas left their off-beat chords to double the cellos' counter-melody (row 8). One defect has been present since v01: the doubled ff at 199 (Finding 4). Length (402 s MIDI) and page count (16) are unchanged.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 211–214: the trumpets' recall of movement II is buried in an ff tutti (applied)
Problem: theme B of movement II is recalled once, by Tpt 1·2 in thirds (a♭'–b♭''). This is the middle of the chord. Ob 1 holds a♭''–b♭'' directly above the tune. Four horns, three trombones, tuba and every woodwind hold whole notes ff. The violins play their own ff descant from d♭''' down, in the most prominent register. Rimsky PDF 54: a melody in the middle register does not stand out by position. It needs dynamic contrast, doubling or timbre. Critic 008 already called this "borderline (full ff basis under the trumpets)".
Fix (codaTwo.ily): the tutti strikes the D♭ chord ff and recedes under the trumpets. On the first note of 211, every part except Tpt 1·2 (Fl, Ob, Cl, Bn, Cbsn, Hn 1–4, Tpt 3, Tbn, B.tbn, Tuba, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb) gets `\ff\>`, and its first note of 212 gets `\f`. Example: `codaTwoHnOne = { f'1\ff\> | ges'2\f ges'2 | ...`, `codaTwoVlnOne = { des'''4\ff\> des'''8 c''' bes''4 aes''4 | ges''4\f bes''8 ...`. Timpani: `aes,4\f` on 212. Tpt 1·2 stay ff. The 209–210 crescendo still arrives at ff on the downbeat of 211, and 215 sub. p is unchanged.

### 2. 211–218: both flutes sit below both oboes (applied)
Problem: at 211–214, Fl 1·2 hold f''/d♭'', under Ob 1·2 at a♭''/f''. At 215–218 the flutes (f♯''–f'', c''–d'') sit inside the oboes (c'''–b'', e♭''–d''). Rimsky PDF 65 and 67 (Ex. 56–59): flutes below oboes give an unnatural tone. At 211 the flute's low second octave is also inaudible in an ff tutti.
Fix (codaTwo.ily): at 211–214 the flutes go up an octave, into their strong register above the oboes: `codaTwoFluteOne = { f'''1\ff\> | ges'''2\f es'''2 | f'''2 aes'''2 | f'''1 | ...`, `codaTwoFluteTwo = { des'''1\ff\> | des'''2\f c'''2 | c'''2 f'''2 | des'''1 | ...`. At 213, Fl 2 takes f''' (the doubled third of the Neapolitan sixth) and not d♭'''. The par.py check found that d♭''' made parallel fifths with the Vln II scale. At 215–218 the four lines are reassigned in register order. The pitches of the chord are unchanged:
- Fl 1 `c'''1\subP\< | c'''1 | b''1 | b''1`
- Fl 2 `fis''1\subP\< | g''1 | f''1 | f''1`
- Ob 1 `es''1\subP\< | e''1 | d''1 | d''1`
- Ob 2 `c''1\subP\< | e''1 | d''1 | d''1`

The oboe also leaves C6, which critic 004 judged thin. The leading tone B now goes from Fl 1 to C7 at 219. The trumpets' B→C and the violas' D♯→E are untouched.

### 3. 231–234: movement I's second theme does not rise above its accompaniment (applied)
Problem: the owed statement of the violins' C-major second-theme head is played by Vln I+II in unison at mf, carried over from 230. The chromatic counter (Cl 1 + Vla) is also mf, and so are the C pedal (Bn 1·2, Vc, Cb) and the harp arpeggios. No woodwind doubles the violins. Rimsky PDF 57: the violin unison is "usually attended by doubling ... in the wood-wind". PDF 54: without that, dynamics must separate the melody.
Fix (sHead.ily): at 231, Cl 1 and Vla `r4 c''4(\p`, Vc `c1\p`, Cb `c,1\p~`, Bn 1 `g1\p`, Bn 2 `c1\p`, harp `c,1\p`. At 235, where the theme moves up an octave with Fl 1 and Ob 1 at f, Cl 1 and Vla take `r4 a'4(\mf` and the harp `a,,1\mf`. The violins keep their mf.

### 4. 199: "ffff" and a false "2." in Fl, Ob and Cl (applied)
Problem: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 have `\ff` on the second eighth (`r8 g''8(\ff`), and their partners have `\ff` on the downbeat. The shared staves therefore print two ff marks side by side, which read as "ffff" (page 12). That contradicts the rule that the strongest mark is fff, and only at 219, 255 and 261. Because voice 1 begins with a rest, the part-combiner also labels the theme "2.", although the theme belongs to player 1. This has been present since v01.
Fix (codaOne.ily): `codaOneFluteOne = { \partCombineApart r8\ff g''8( es'''4.->) ...`. Do the same for OboeOne and ClOne. FluteTwo, OboeTwo and ClTwo begin with `\partCombineApart`. All six get `\partCombineAutomatic` before their `R1*2` at 205. One ff now prints, and there is no solo label.

### 5. 139–140: two horns in unison on a soft, individual phrase (applied)
Problem: the retransition passes the F1 head from instrument to instrument, entering p: Hn 139, Vc 140, Ob+Cl 141, Vln II 142, then Hn 1·2 with the brass crescendo from 143. At 139 the head is a2. Rimsky PDF 66–67: coupling two instruments of one kind adds power but reduces expression. Doubling belongs to loud passages, and soft individual phrases go to a solo instrument (Ex. 40 and 49 show his soft solos undoubled). The a2 belongs at 143, where the crescendo begins.
Fix (retr.ily): `retrHnTwo = { R1*4 | R1*2 | b'1\p | b'1 | g'2->( d'4. es'8 | ...`. Hn 1 now plays alone, printed "1." p. Hn 2 joins on the held b' at 141.
