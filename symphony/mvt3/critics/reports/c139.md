## What else I studied

- **Rachmaninoff comparison.** I compared the score as a whole with the finale of Rachmaninoff's Second Symphony (Allegro vivace, E major). Rachmaninoff's coda builds on the second theme returned in full in the tonic, and ends fast with a short rhythmic close. This score's craft is at a level a conductor would accept as professional: the arrival at 223, the stretto over the pedal at 139–146 and the last cadence 259–263 are well prepared and well scored. It falls short of Rachmaninoff in two ways.
  - The coda leans on quoted material and on tempo changes (ten tempo steps in the last 80 bars), while F1 sounds for only eight bars and F2 does not come back.
  - The ending is a Largamente rather than a fast close.
  
  Both are design decisions already recorded in HISTORY (c069 on F2; the Largamente close and the 273–279 scherzo quotation are protected). I record them as observations, not findings.

## Findings

### 1. The bass clarinet is almost unused outside tutti (bars 135–146). Applied, in reduced form.

**Problem.** The bass clarinet plays 18 notes in 279 bars, all of them doublings inside tutti: 1–2, 5–6, 147–150, 223, 257–264 and 273–279. It never has a passage of its own colour, so its entry at 147 brings nothing new.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky lists the bass clarinet among the dark low voices of the lower extreme (PDF 124–125).
- He says a group that has been silent for a while gains fresh interest when it returns (PDF 136).
- In a crescendo, the added group enters p and grows faster than the rest (PDF 127, Ex. 245–250).
- His examples use the Cl. basso to double the bassoon in a held bass (Ex. 265; Ex. 273–274).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-139/work/sections/retr.ily).** The bass clarinet doubles Bn 2's held B1 from 144, entering p inside the crescendo, before its ff at 147:

```
retrBassCl = { R1*4 | R1*5 | b,1\p\< | b,1\f | b,2. r4 | g,1->\ff | g,1 | g,1~ | g,2. d4 | }
```

This replaces the part-only Bn 1 cue at 145–146, which is no longer needed. The c075 comment in retr.ily that describes that cue is now out of date, and the composer should delete it when merging.

I first doubled the whole pedal from 135. That build was clean but had 17 pages, because the bass clarinet staff then appears in the 133–143 system, which RemoveEmptyStaves hides otherwise. Starting at 144 keeps the staff within the 144–157 system, where it already prints, and keeps the score at 16 pages. The stretto voices and the timpani motive are unchanged.

### 2. Trumpet 3 sounds the horns' S head at the same pitch (bars 247–249, 252). Applied in 247–249.

**Problem.** In the f brass basis under the ff S head, Tpt 3's repeated notes (E4, A4, A4) land on the head's own pitches: the E4 at 247 and the tenuto A4 peak at 248. A trumpet in unison with the horn line takes the edge off the horn colour and turns the accompaniment into a doubling.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky: when the melody and its harmonic basis are in the same family, the basis stays an octave away from the melody and one degree softer (PDF 133, Ex. 264).
- A florid melody against held harmony notes sounds less harsh when the two differ in colour or register (PDF 132).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-139/work/sections/finale.ily, `finaleTptThree`).** Tpt 3 takes other chord tones: 247 C5 (was E4), 248 F4 (was A4), 249 C5 (was A4). Bars 250–254 are unchanged.

```
finaleTptThree = { c''4\f r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8 | f'4 r8 f'8 f'4 r8 f'8 | c''4 r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8 | g'4 r8 g'8 ...
```

Bar 252 stays A4, for these reasons:
- My first version also moved 247 to G4 and 252 to F4. p5.py flagged parallel fifths G4/D5 → F4/C5 against the top line at 247→248 and 251→252.
- Moving 252 to C5 made fifths with the double basses at 251→253.
- With 247 C5, 248 F4, 249 C5 and 252 A4, p5.py's output is identical to v139's: no new fifths.

**Protection.** The 247–254 combination is protected (c017, c117). The protected content is the two ff horn heads with held brass, tuba, contrabassoon and Tpt 3 at f underneath. This change keeps Tpt 3's level, rhythm and role, and changes only three accompaniment pitches. The heads, F1, F2 and the chorale top lines are not touched.

### 3. Coda proportions against the master model (bars 199–279). Not applied (protected or decided design).

**Problem.** Measured against Rachmaninoff 2/iv, the coda depends on quotations and tempo changes more than on the finale's own themes: F1 is heard for 8 bars and F2 not at all.

**Evidence.** Rimsky's first rescoring principle is to bring back a phrase changed in octave, key or dynamics, not to drop it (PDF 118). Rachmaninoff's coda follows that principle with the second theme.

**Fix.** None. c069 decided against bringing F2 back, and the 273–279 close is protected. I record it so the next lens-9 critic does not raise it again without new grounds.
