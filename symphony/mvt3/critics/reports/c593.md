## Findings

### 1. The development's two soft dominants (101–102, 105–106): resolving them was tested in the scratch build and is worse. Not applied.

**Candidate.** After each F1-head module the winds hold a soft V6/5 (the E bass at 101–102, the A bass at 105–106), and the next module enters a fifth lower without resolving it. The reading's neighbouring 6/3 returns to its 5/3 (Ex. 18-9, Ex. 18-19a, PDF 296, 302). c503 left this "not tested beyond the MIDI dump", so I built it.

The test changed dev.ily at 102.4: bass clarinet `e,2. f,4`, Bn 1 `e2 e4 f4`, Hn 1 `aes'2 g'4 aes'4`, Hn 2 `c'2 bes4 aes4`. The V6/5 of F then resolves to an F-minor chord on the last beat before the B♭-minor head at 103. The test file is kept at /tmp/critic-593/dev-c1-test.ily and its MIDI at /tmp/critic-593/score-c1-test.midi.

**Build result.** 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. par8 adds only Bn 1's octave doubling of the bass clarinet at 102.4.

**Why it is worse.**
- (a) The development is a transition. PDF 290–291 says the 6/3 suits transitional passages because it is less stable than the 5/3, and that the 5/3 sounds like a goal. The candidate puts a goal chord at the end of a module whose job is to move on.
- (b) The open V6/5 already carries the reading's ♭6–5 colour over V (Hn 1 A♭4→G4 over E at 102, D♭5→C5 over A at 106; PDF 301, Ex. 18-18). The F1 head then enters f on B♭ out of an unresolved dominant, so the step to B♭ minor sounds like a new key. After a one-beat F-minor chord it sounds like iv of F, a continuation in F.
- (c) The same resolution at 106.4 would put a B♭-minor chord before the F-minor lament at 107 and weaken that arrival in the same way.

I reverted the change. The scratch sections are identical to v593 (`diff -r -q`), and the rebuilt score.midi is byte-identical to v593's.

### 2. Coda candidates from the reading, checked on paper and declined

- **251: the bass holds E (I6) through the bar into F at 252, expanding I6 instead of falling to C** (Ex. 18-19e, PDF 302). The cellos' movement I head ends B2→C3 at 251.4→252.1, so a held E1 makes parallel fifths E/B → F/C. The present C on 251.3 avoids them.
- **252.3: a passing E between IV (F) and ii (D)** (PDF 297: a passing 6/3 connects bass tones a third apart). The accented A6/A3 of the head on 252.3 would become a fourth over the bass.
- **254.3: an F bass under the S head's final C, so the bass falls G–F–E into the last chorale's I6.** This is a V–IV retrogression before I6, and 254→255 is protected (c007, c491).
- **238.3: a passing B (V6) between the C pedal and the A of 239** (Ex. 18-11a). It would make octaves C/C → B/B with the tune's C6–B5, and it breaks the owed C pedal (c122).
- **129.3: a stepwise bass B–C–D (I6–IV–V7) instead of B–E–D.** It would make outer fifths C2/G5 → D2/A5. The present IV6 is chosen to avoid them.
- **149: ♭6–5 over V before the recapitulation** (Ex. 18-18). Declined: 147–150 notes and voicing are protected (c226), and c313 rejected a neighbouring i6/4 there because it anticipates the 6/4 at 151. An E♭ at 149 would do the same.

### 3. Proportions and the coda's stages: no change

The timings above match the four previous form passes. The reading's points about where 6/3 series belong (transitions, articulated only at their ends) are met at 87–89, 107–109 and 135–138.

The coda keeps its single root V–I at 262→263. Every earlier tonic arrival in C is inverted, plagal or prepared by an augmented sixth. That is the cadence plan the reading's "6 substituting for 5" (PDF 300) supports at 251 and 255.

### 4. Note for later critics: par8's 5ths at 36.1 are a sampling artifact

par8 flags 36.1 (Bn 1, violas and cellos E♭4→B♭4 against the bass A♭1→E♭2). The figure's last eighth in 35 is A♭4, so the real succession is A♭/A♭ (octave) → B♭/E♭ (fifth), not parallel 5ths. No fix. The flag has been present since at least v400 and should not be "fixed" in a later cycle.
