## Findings

### 1. 279: the cymbal quarter has a circled cross (applied)
- Problem: `<\tweak style #'xcircle cymc bd>4` at 279. c146 kept "the circled head of 278" on the quarter. Gould PDF 26 says the circle exists because a white notehead's open centre cannot hold a cross. It stands for minim and semibreve durations only. A crotchet takes the plain cross (PDF 25, the black-note examples). With a circle, the 279 note reads as a white value with a stem, which is not a duration that exists. The tie from 278 already shows that it is the same stroke.
- Fix (finale.ily, finalePerc): `cymc4` with the default cross head (see the full line under finding 2).

### 2. 278-279: cymbals and bass drum written as one chord, and the bass-drum tie is drawn above the cymbal (applied)
- Problem: at 278-279 the two players share one up-stem, `<cymc~ bd~>1 | <cymc bd>4`. The staff's `Tie.direction = #UP` puts both ties above the line, so the bass drum's tie (from a note below the line) sits over the cymbal's tie and reads as a second cymbal tie. At 223 the same pair is written as two voices, cymbal up and bass drum down. Gould PDF 27 gives the rule (note below the centre line → stem on the other side from the note above). PDF 65 adds that two parts on one stave take up-stems for the upper and down-stems for the lower part. The final bars should use the 223 layout.
- Fix (finale.ily, last bars of finalePerc):
  `R1 | << { \tweak style #'xcircle cymc1->^\fff~ | cymc4 r4 r2\fermata } \\ { bd1->_\fff~ | bd4 s4 s2 } >> | }`
  Each tie now sits on its own notehead's side (cymbal above, bass drum below), as at 223. c146's intention is kept: both instruments are tied into 279 and stop on beat 2. The sounding MIDI is unchanged: the same note 36 and cymbal note on channel 10 at the same ticks and velocity. Only the bass drum's MIDI track changes, to the second-voice track the bass drum already uses at 223.
