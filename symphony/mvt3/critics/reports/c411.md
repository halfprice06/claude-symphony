## Findings

### 1. 259–262: continuing F2 past 258 into the final cadence. Tested in a scratch build and declined.

**Candidate.** F2's coda phrase in the violas and cellos (Vln II an octave higher, Hn 3·4 doubling) ends at 258.4 on D4: G–E | D C E G | C D | G F E D. At 259 the violas drop to repeated F4 quarters and the cellos go down to the bass F2. Sebesky's Ex. 273 (PDF 208) and his rule to "keep something going which carries one bar into the next" (PDF 207) suggested a test. The cellos would leave the held bass and keep the line moving in unison with the violas, playing F2's fourth phrase down to the cadence. I built it in /tmp/critic-411/test-c1 (finale.ily, violas and cellos):

```
finaleVla 259–262:  c'2.(\< bes4 | aes4 g4 f4 es4~ | es2 c2 | d2 f2) | e'4\fff ...
finaleVc  259–262:  c'2.(\< bes4 | aes4 g4 f4 es4~ | es2 c2 | d2 f2) | <c, c>1\fff ...
```

The build has 0 warnings and 16 pages. The analyser shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8 on 257–264 finds only the bass doublings that v411 also has.

**Why it was declined:**
1. The tune's own ending does not fit the protected cadence. F2 closes 2̂–7̂–1̂ (F D | E♭, 65–66; in C, D B | C). Over 262's V7 the B would double the chorale's B5 (Fl, Tpt 1, Vln I), and B→C in both voices makes parallel octaves into 263. The only other route through the German sixth, E♭→D over A♭→G, makes fifths with the bass. I therefore had to end the line D–F→E. That is not F2's ending, so the "completion" is just a descending C-minor scale.
2. Under the chorale the line is not heard. At 259–262 the brass are ff, rising to fff (c321, c129). The new line sits at C4–C3 in two string sections, in the same register as Tbn 1·2, Hn 3·4 and Bn 1. This is the masking problem c321 fixed at 255–258 by dropping the brass to f. Sebesky (PDF 210–211) warns against loud brass on a lead in its weak low register, and that warning applies here. The protected crescendo cannot be lowered again.
3. It takes away two things that work. One is c017's viola pulse, F–F♯–G, which is Sebesky's Ex. 272 chromatic inner line and pushes into 263. The other is the cellos' 8-foot bass under the hammered basses.
4. The present ending already works melodically. F2's last note, D4, is a chord tone of the iiø6/5 at 259. Hn 3, Bn 1 and Tbn 1 hold it, and it is the chorale's D5/D6 in the same bar. So F2 hands its last note to the chorale. It does not stop in the air.

The score is unchanged at 255–264. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-411/work is v411 as it stands.

### 2. Colour fills, the trill and string-colour shading (PDF 213–215, Ex. 286; PDF 215 "Midnight Sun") remain unused. No change.

Sebesky saves colour for the long note at the end of a section ("prayer", six beats). He passes it up where the line moves fast ("carousel"). The score already follows this:
- Cymbals sound only at 223, 263 and 278.
- The harp glissando comes only at 277.4.
- The one solo echo inside a held note is at 217–218 (Ex. 288 cont'd 3).

The long holds at section ends are 18, 42, 49–50, 94, 198 and 216–218. Each is protected, or it is a deliberate stop before a contrast. At 42 and 49–50 a fill would spoil the pp before the owed trumpet solo, whose colour must enter fresh (c063). At 198 the f < wall would lose its force before the ff tutti at 199. Sebesky's flute run and trill, and his ponticello or senza-vibrato shading, are scoring for light songs. They do not belong in this idiom. The other neo-Romantic models do not use them at such points either. No change.
