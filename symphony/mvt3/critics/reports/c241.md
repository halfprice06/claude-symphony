## Findings

### 1. F2's major statement reaches its only full cadence with no held-over tone (80-81). Applied. This changes F2.

**Problem.** At 67-82 the violins (with Fl 1 from 77) repeat the trumpet's minor tune in E♭ major almost note for note. The only rhythmic change is c141's dotted peak at 78. The phrase ends `c'''4 bes'' aes'' g'' | f''2 d''2 | es''2`, the same contour and rhythm as the trumpet's `ces''4 bes' aes' ges' | f'2 d'2 | es'1` at 64-66. The cadence the movement's second theme reaches in the major is the least individual bar of the tune. A listener has heard it once already, and nothing new happens on the dominant.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 353-354, section 15 and Ex. 21-15e/f (image): the 7/6 over V7 "occurs rather frequently—especially in nineteenth-century music—as a delay of 2̂ over V7". In Josef Strauss's *Dynamiden* the 6 is held over the V7 bass and resolves to 5 ("a beautiful suspension"). The score already has every element of that figure at 80-81:
- the tune's G on 80 beat 4 is a consonant 6th over the B♭ bass. This is the preparation (PDF 347: "typically ... consonant");
- at 81 the bass stays on B♭, and the cellos, violas and Bn 1 sound the 7th A♭3, so 81 is V7. Their A♭ resolves to G at 82;
- F on 81 beat 2 is the 5 of the figure, and D is a chordal skip, a decorated resolution (PDF 359, Ex. 21-26).

PDF 346: the suspension "animates and individualizes" its voice. PDF 348: the suspension carries an accent and its resolution is softer, which is what the existing diminuendo from 80.4 to the mf at 82 already does. PDF 352: F4 (harp arpeggio) and F3 (Hn 2) sound under the suspended G6/G5. These are the "marked contrast in timbre" and lower-octave case that the reading allows, and neither doubles the violins' register. The change also makes the major statement differ from the minor one at its cadence, as the restatements of the big tunes in Tchaikovsky 5/iv and Dvořák 9/iv do. The trumpet's 64-65 stays plain, because the solo is protected.

### Considered and rejected (decided)

- **Anticipation at F1's deceptive cadence (25, 165; A&S PDF 363-366, Ex. 21-33 to 21-35).** `c''4 b'8 c''8 | c''2` would add a Brahms-style anticipation to the 4-3 that c061 already wrote. I rejected it. F1 is con fuoco and accented, and its consequent already holds C over the beat. The eighth would have to go into eight doubling parts at 25 and again at 165, for a figure the reading itself calls "not as important as the suspension" (PDF 363).
- **9-8 at F1's half cadence (17→18).** Tying the A♭5 of 17 into 18 would give a prepared 9-8 over V with B in the clarinets and Hn 3 (PDF 351). I rejected it: it softens the accented G5 that marks the antecedent's end at 18 and 158, and a sigh there works against the theme's character.
- **Bass suspension 5/2 (PDF 352-353, Ex. 21-13a).** The movement has none. The candidates are the V6 bars of F2 (68, 76), where E♭ would be held into the V6. There the tune's F5 and E♭5 would sit a 9th over the suspended bass, against the 6/3 voicing the reading asks for, and the passage is part of the 67-82 balance that earlier critics protected. Not applied.
- **Shifted counterpoint against the chorale (PDF 346, Ex. 21-1, Jupiter).** This is the obvious use of the reading's first example. 223-234 and both chorale lines are protected, so I did not touch them.
- **Alternating 4-3s in a rising sequence (PDF 355-357).** The only rising sequences are 35-38 (already a 5-6 link, c212) and the stretto 139-146 (protected).
