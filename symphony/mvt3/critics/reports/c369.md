## What else I studied

I compared the movement with the finale of Franck's Symphony in D minor, from memory of the score and from Adler's Ex. XI-20 (PDF 38). I built v359 at /tmp/critic-369/v359 (0 warnings, 16 pages) and compared it with v369 section by section. The only differences are the cycle 359-368 changes:
- Vln I tremolo at 255-262;
- escape tones at 71-72 and 191-192;
- Tbn 1 D4 at 154 and 162 (c362-c364);
- clefs;
- "non div." marks;
- upper cellos at 136-139;
- printed swell levels at 211-215 and `\crescMolto` at 243-246.

I checked the quotations with coherence/themecheck.py. The finale record is 50/50 unchanged before and after my edit. The scherzo themes in /mnt/project-files/symphony/scherzo/latest (v400) also match: 52/52 unchanged. No quotation needs updating.

Franck's finale uses four cyclic devices, and this score has all four:
- a soft brass second theme (Franck's trumpet-and-horn theme; here the trumpet solo at 51);
- a return of the slow movement, here twice: soft at 95-98 and loud at 211-218;
- the first-movement motto turned major in the brass over string tremolo (223);
- choirs alternating phrase by phrase in the manner of Ex. XI-20.

The main difference is pacing. Franck sets his apotheosis between long stretches of lighter scoring. This coda is close to continuous tutti from 197 to 279, with relief only at 219-222 and 231-242. Adler warns about tutti fatigue (PDF 146). The passage is protected, and the relief points are well placed, so I record this as an observation only. Overall, a conductor would accept this as competent late-Romantic writing. The one thing that would mark it as student work is in finding 1.

## Findings

### 1. Parallel octaves between Tbn 1 and the bass at 154-155, 159-160 and 162-163 (regression since v359; applied)

**Problem.** Cycles 362-364 gave Tbn 1 a D4 whole note in bars 154 and 162. The bass moves to D2 on beat 3 and then falls to C at the next bar line. Tbn 1 D4 doubles that bass D and falls to C4 with it. This makes exposed octaves in the brass at 155.1 and 163.1. The 163.1 fault is new since v359.

Bars 159.4-160.1 have the same fault: Tbn 1 C4 to D4 over bass C to D. That one already existed in v359.

The other cycle 359-368 changes did not make anything worse.

**Evidence.** Adler (PDF 160-162) says full-orchestra chords should be voice-led "according to the book", as in four-part writing. In the Schumann and Weber examples, the trombones keep common tones or move by step against the bass. They never double the bass's own motion. A doubled bass line belongs in the bassoons, contra and low strings (PDF 26, Great Gate). There it does no harm, because the line is a doubling for weight rather than an independent voice.

I found the fault by dumping the MIDI notes. par8 had missed 155.1 because the part combiner split the trombones onto a different track.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-369/work/sections/recP.ily, `recPTbnOne`).**
- Bars 154 and 162: `d'1` becomes `d'2 g2`. Tbn 1 takes G3 on beat 3 and rises a fourth to C4. The bass falls from D to C, so the two lines now move in contrary motion. At 154.3 the chord is G3 in Tbn 1 and D3 in Tbn 2. At 155.1 it is C4 and E♭3.
- Bar 160: `d'2 c'2` becomes `f'2 c'2`. F4 is a chord tone of the 160.1 harmony and moves in contrary motion to the bass.

A comment above the variable cites this report.

**Check.**
- The build is clean: 0 warnings, 16 pages, and analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels.
- par8.py on 151-166 now reports no trombone lines.
- The allpairs diff removes the old octaves at bars 160.1 and 163.1. It now also lists octaves at 155.1 between "Trombones 1·2:two" and the upper strings and winds. These come from Tbn 2 D3 to E♭3, which doubles the melody's D to E♭. Tbn 2 is unchanged from v369. The pair is listed only because Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 now sit on separate combiner tracks. It is a melody doubling in an inner voice, not a new fault.

No quotation, F1, F2 or chorale top line changed.

### 2. Coda tutti density, 197-279 (observation, not applied)

**Problem.** Against Franck, the last 80 bars carry nearly continuous full scoring.

**Evidence.** Adler (PDF 146) says a sustained tutti loses its effect. Adler (PDF 37 and 172) recommends holding colours and registers back.

**Decision.** The score already holds back the cymbals and the top register until 223 and 255, and the passage is protected in HISTORY.md. Thinning it would undo protected work for a small gain. Not applied.

### 3. Techniques considered and rejected (not applied)

I tried several book techniques the score does not use and decided against each one:
- A sustained low horn under 3-4, as in Adler's Smetana example (PDF 154). Bars 1-6 are protected, and the opening is clearer without it.
- High flute doubling on the downbeats at 67-76, from the Brahms 3 example (PDF 162). It would take away the fresh woodwind colour at 77.
- A Lohengrin-style hand-over of the chorale doubling at 231-234 (PDF 35). The passage is protected.
- A timpani pedal at 255-260 (Jupiter, PDF 176). The passage is protected, and Tpt 3 already carries the non-thematic pedal there.
