## Findings

**1. Bar 82: the IV6 before the closing group's V contradicts the C♭ of bar 81. Make it minor iv6 over C♭ (A&S Ex. 23-2b). Applied.**

Problem: F2's E♭-major repeat ends 81-82 with V7♭9 (the violas' C♭3 on 81.2, the D-F-A♭-C♭ of Ex. 23-9 over the B♭ bass), then IV6 over a C♮ bass (Vc, Cb, Bn 2, harp, violas C4) on 82.1, then V on 82.4 and I at 83. So the listener hears C♭ (81.2) → B♭ (81.3) → C♮ in the bass (82.1) → B♭ (82.4). The minor colour introduced one beat earlier is cancelled by the bass before the line reaches 5. That is the ♭6 → ♮6 → 5 succession A&S mark "poor" (Ex. 23-2h), spread over two beats and two voices. It also wastes the strongest form of the mixture, ♭6 in the bass falling a semitone to 5, which the score never uses: the ♭6 basses in major keys are all augmented sixths (261) or inner-voice ♭6s (197.4, 233, 259, 274).

Evidence:
- PDF 387, sec. 2: ♭6 "creates new forms of subdominant and supertonic harmony: IV♭, II6♭ ... very often functions as upper neighbor to 5"; chords with ♭6 need no special preparation.
- PDF 388, Ex. 23-2b ("minIV6": bass ♭6 to 5) and the text: "Once introduced, it tends to move to 5; it will not normally be replaced by ♮6 before 5 is reached. Example 23-2h shows you what to avoid."
- PDF 387: through mixture "the active melodic progression 6-5 in minor can occur in major". PDF 395, Points for review 2a and 3.
- The recapitulation's counterpart already does this: F2's A♭-major statement closes with IV → iv (F♭ in violas and Hn 2) at 197.4-198 (c270). The exposition's statement now closes the same way, so the two ends of F2 in major agree.

Fix (sections/sTwo.ily, bar 82 = 16th bar of the section):
- `sTwoVc`: `\clef bass c2.\mp\< bes,4` → `\clef bass ces2.\mp\< bes,4`
- `sTwoCb`: `c,2.\< bes,,4` → `ces,2.\< bes,,4`
- `sTwoBsnTwo`: `c2.\< bes,4 \breathe` → `ces2.\< bes,4 \breathe`
- `sTwoVla`: `c'2.\mp\< d'4` → `ces'2.\mp\< bes4` (the violas double the cellos an octave up, as they do at 67-76; C♭4-D4 would be an augmented second, PDF 380)
- `sTwoHnOne`: `es'2.\< bes4 \breathe` → `es'2.\< d'4 \breathe` (Hn 1 takes the V's third that the violas gave up; E♭4-D4 by step, in tenths with the bass)
- `sTwoHarpR`: `es'8 aes'8 c''8 es''8 r2` → `es'8 aes'8 ces''8 es''8 r2`; `sTwoHarpL`: `<< c,2 ...` → `<< ces,2 ...`, and bar 81 `bes,,1` → `bes,,1-\tag #'score _\markup \small "C♭"` (the harp has no C in 81; its next entry at 123 carries its own pedal diagram).

Result: 82.1 is A♭-C♭-E♭ over C♭ (Bn 1's tied A♭3, Hn 1·2 and violins' E♭), and C♭ falls to B♭ in the bass on 82.4 into V. F2's tune, its run G-A♭-B♭-D, the rhythm, the dynamics (mp <, animando) and the closing group at 83 are unchanged. The run's G5 on 82.3 now passes over C♭ (E♭-G-C♭ for one eighth); it is a passing note to A♭ and is the mixture's juxtaposition of ♮3 and ♭6 that PDF 387 describes. par8.py on 80-83: the only new lines are the violas' octave doubling of the bass (C♭-B♭, then B♭3-E♭4 at 83.1, the same motion Vln I, Vln II, Ob 1 and Vc already make there); no new fifths. Outer-voice parallels: 0, as v402. F2 is the finale's own theme: its melody is not changed, only the harmony under its last bar.

**2. Bar 131: a ♭9 (E♭) in the V7 of G as the mixture form of VII°7 (Ex. 22-9, Ex. 23-9). Considered, not applied.**

The G-major area 123-134 is the one major key with no ♭6. At 131 the only E is the violins' passing E6 in the scale A5-G6 on 131.4. Making it E♭ would turn the scale into a harmonic-minor run with an augmented second E♭-F♯, and the ♭6 would go up to F♯ rather than down to 5 (D), against PDF 388. A held E♭ elsewhere (Ob 1 replacing its F♯5 on 131.1) would remove the V7's only third on that beat. 123-131 is also protected (c181, c223). No change.

**3. Bar 135: ♮7 decorating ♭6 over the B°7 (Ex. 22-7h, PDF 377-378). Considered, not applied.**

The candidate was Vln I B♭5→A♭5 as a half-note appoggiatura over the violas' B4 at the start of the falling chain. In v402 every line of 135-138 falls by semitone in parallel (c094, c399: pp sul tasto, crescendo to the sf stop chord). A B♭5 would add a new peak above the chain's G5-A♭5-G5-G♭5 line and break the sequence in its first bar only; the poignant clash belongs to a cadential °7, and the chain's goal, the stop chord at 138, is protected. No change.

**4. Bars 19 and 159: VII°6/5 as the passing chord between i6 and i (PDF 375, Ex. 22-1, Ex. 22-4c). Considered, not applied.**

The bass E♭-D-C under F1's second statement passes through V6/4 (19, c313) or V4/3 (159). °6/5 would replace 5 by 6, but F1's melody holds G (5) on beat 3 over that D. F1's head is protected. No change.

**5. °4/2 (Ex. 22-8) and voice exchange (Ex. 22-11). Not applicable.**

Every place where ♭6 in the bass goes to a cadential 6/4 is an augmented sixth (1-2, 5-6, 109, 219-220, 261), protected or quoted. The only °7s held long enough for a voice exchange are the chain 135-138 and the stop chord; the stretto 139-146 above the B is protected.
