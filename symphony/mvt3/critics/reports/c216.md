## What else I studied

I wrote a parser for the wind and brass parts in sections/*.ily. It uses score tags, ties, rests, breath marks and the tempo of each section, and lists every stretch longer than 9 s that has no rest or breath mark. Most of the stretches it finds are restruck whole notes, where a player breathes between notes without a mark (for example Hn 4 235-251), and I left those alone. The four stretches in finding 3 are the ones where the music offers no such place, or where the only clean breath falls at a structural joint that should be marked. I also read HISTORY.md, in particular c036 and c126 (the first and second passes on this reading, which settled most breathing questions), c008 (strong marks limited) and the do-not-touch lists for 51-66, 171-186 and 205-210. I looked at pages 3, 4 and 12 of my scratch build.

## Findings

**1. 209-210: the upper winds hold the A♭7 at f < into the ff of 211; give them the woodwind envelope fp <.** Adler PDF 178 names sfp and sf > p < f as the envelope best suited to the woodwinds. Ex. VI-10 (Beethoven 1/i) and Ex. VI-9 (Mendelssohn 4/i, winds fp then f, PDF 177) show winds holding a chord that drops after the attack and then swells. At 209 Fl 1-2, Ob 1-2 and Cl 1-2 hold G♭6/E♭6, C6/A♭5 and G♭5/E♭5 at f, so the two-bar crescendo can only go from f to ff. Those same pitches also cover the lower half of the violins' run A♭4-G♭6 (209-210, c151), which climbs straight through them. With fp the winds attack, drop below the run, and then swell over two bars up to the 210.3 cut, alongside the horns and trombones, which already crescendo from mp. This makes the swell into Largamente 211 audible. The marking is fp, not sf, so c008's rule limiting the strong marks (101, 105, 138, 215, 219, 255, 261 in old numbers) still holds. No note or scoring changes, so c176-c179's protection of the 205-210 scoring is not touched. Fix (applied, codaOne.ily): `ges'''1\fp\<` (Fl 1), `es'''1\fp\<` (Fl 2), `c'''1\fp\<` (Ob 1), `aes''1\fp\<` (Ob 2), `ges''1\fp\<` (Cl 1), `es''1\fp\<` (Cl 2). The bassoons stay at f <, because they are the bass with the cellos and share one staff.

**2. 51 and 171: the clarinets' pp pads start with an articulated attack on the solo's first note; mark them dal niente.** At 51 the trumpet's first note as a soloist (owed item 1) is attacked together with Cl 1-2 (E♭4, G♭3), Bn 1 and the pizzicato bass. At 171 Cl 2 restrikes with the cor anglais and muted trumpet. Adler PDF 211 (Ex. VII-57) says the clarinet can start a tone "from almost complete silence without articulation", better than any other woodwind, and PDF 214 calls its low chalumeau "phenomenally quiet". With a niente start, the only attacks on these downbeats are the soloist's and the pizzicato bass. The pad is in place a moment later at the same pp. This answers the protection of 51-66 and 171-186 (soft undoubled solo, pp accompaniment, muted trumpet with cor anglais): the scoring and the pp are unchanged, and the solo is clearer. Fix (applied): sOne.ily `es'1\pp^\markup \italic "dal niente"` (Cl 1) and `ges1\pp-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "dal niente"` (Cl 2; the text prints once on the shared staff, in both parts); recS.ily `es1\pp^\markup \italic "dal niente"` (Cl 2). This is text only: the MIDI does not change.

**3. Four places where a wind player goes 12 bars or more with no place to breathe (Adler PDF 175, 188, 226).** Fix (all applied):
- 198 Bn 2 (recS.ily): it plays the bass line from 187 to 204 with no rest, crescendo, into the ff hammered tutti, at 2 = 84. Bn 1 already rests on 198.4. Change to `aes,2.\f\< r4` so both bassoons attack 199 on a fresh breath. The cellos and basses keep the bass.
- 82 Bn 1-2 (sTwo.ily): 75-86 runs 12 bars from p to the f closing theme. Mark `g1\< \breathe` and `es1\< \breathe`, where Hn 1-2 already breathe (c145).
- 242 Cl 1 (sHead.ily): 239-250 runs 12 bars, through the stringendo, into the ff S head. Mark `c'2 d'2) \breathe` at the slur end. The violas play the same notes (PDF 188: another player carries the line while one breathes).
- 186 Cl 2 (recS.ily): 183-194 runs pp with no rest. Mark `es1 \breathe` at the double bar before M; it restrikes at 187 in any case.
These marks remove no pitches. The par8.py check shows no new parallels (the only difference at 199.1 is a removed Bn 2 octave motion).

**4. 191 Bn 2 B♭1 at p under F2 in A♭: checked, no change.** This is the bassoon's lowest note, inside the fifth that Adler PDF 226 says cannot be played pp ("expect a firmer attack"). The marking is p, not pp, and the cellos double the note, so the firmer attack is covered. Raising the note would change the protected 187-198 bass (c207, c210).

**5. 227-228 Ob 1 E6-D6 at ff in the chorale: checked, no change.** Adler's chart (PDF 198, Ex. VII-32) puts this in the "thin but clear" band, above the most effective range F4-C6. Fl 1 and Cl 1 double it at ff, and the arrival is protected, so I made no change.

**6. 199-203 Cbsn repeated quarters at ff: checked, no change.** Adler PDF 230 warns against fast repeated staccato. These notes are non-staccato quarters at 2.8 per second on C2-F2, inside the lowest twelfth, and the basses carry the repeated eighths. This follows the book.
