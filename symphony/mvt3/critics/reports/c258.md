## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 211: theme B, movement II's cor anglais song, enters ff in the trumpets and winds with no word, while its own echo at 217 is marked "espress." (applied)

**Bars:** 211-216 (codaTwo.ily).

**Problem.** At 211 theme B's second phrase (II 41-44, in doubled values) is played ff, Largamente, by Tpt 1·2, Fl 1·2, Ob 1 and Cl 1. The only signs are ff, an accent on the first note and the slur. Every other statement of a song-like theme in the movement names its character: the trumpet solo at 51 ("dolce cantabile"), the violins at 67 and 187 ("cantabile"), the muted trumpet at 171 ("con sord., dolce cantabile"), movement I's S head at 235 ("molto cantabile"). The horn and cor anglais echo of this same phrase at 217 is "espress.". So the main statement of the Adagio's tune, the one place where it sounds in brass, is the one statement without a word. At ff with an accented first note, trumpets will read it as a fanfare.

The same downbeat has a section mismatch: Ob 1 and Cl 1 are accented, while Ob 2 (D♭5) and Cl 2 (A♭4) strike the same chord on the same staff unaccented.

**Evidence.** In Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18) the line moves from cor anglais to flutes and on, and each new colour gets its own word: "(SWEETLY)", "(PRETTY)", "(EASY)". The word tells the new player what the line was. PDF 204: a line should keep its own character, and a line moved to another group without that care sounds "bland". Ex. 281 (PDF 214-215) gives identical accents to every voice of a section that attacks together.

**Fix (applied).** Text and accents only; dynamics, notes and slurs unchanged:
- `codaTwoTptOne`: `aes'2->(\ff^\markup \italic "cantabile" des''2`; `codaTwoTptTwo`: `f'2->(\ff-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "cantabile" aes'2`
- `codaTwoFluteOne`: `aes''2->(\ff^\markup \italic "cantabile"`; `codaTwoFluteTwo`: the same text with `-\tag #'part`
- `codaTwoOboeOne` and `codaTwoClOne`: `aes'2->(\ff^\markup \italic "cantabile"`
- `codaTwoOboeTwo`: `des''2->\ff f''2`; `codaTwoClTwo`: `\tag #'score { aes'2->\ff~ aes'2 } \tag #'part { aes'1->\ff }`

The text prints once per staff in the score (voice 2 carries it in its part only, the convention of sOne.ily's "dal niente"). This does not touch the protected 211-222 dynamic path, the D♭–C–B–C top line or the 217 echo.

### 2. 11, 19, 83, 151: F1's broken-chord accompaniment runs inside the tune's register with nothing to say it is background (applied)

**Bars:** 11-18 (Cl 1·2), 19-26 (violas, with Cl 1), 83-86 (violas), 151-158 (Cl 1·2).

**Problem.** Every statement of F1 in the tonic or E♭ is accompanied by the same figure: slurred four-note broken chords in eighths. It is mf at 11, 19 and 83 and f at 151, and it runs through the tune's lower octave. At 11-18 the Cl 1 figure E♭4-G4-C5 sits on the tune's C5-G4. At 83-86 the violas' E♭4-B♭4 sits on the clarinets' E♭5 octave of the head. c254 found that the 83-86 figure cannot be moved out of that register and lowered it to mf. Even so, a slurred eighth figure in a fast Allegro played mf by two clarinets or a viola section is as active as the tune and close to its level. The only mark the players get is a dynamic, and a dynamic does not tell them the figure is a background.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the listener's ear must "not be diverted or confused by an overbusy background", and PDF 14 says the background is the secondary level. Ex. 279-280 (PDF 211-212): a background that runs in the solo's register distorts the focus. When the notes cannot change, Ex. 2A (PDF 18) shows the other tool: a word for the way the figure is played. Its harp figure is marked "(EASY)". In this idiom the word is "leggiero". Dvořák 9/iv marks its own accompaniment figures with a character word ("f marcato", strings.notesF.ily 98, 158) instead of relying on the dynamic alone.

**Fix (applied).** Add `^\markup \italic "leggiero"` to the first note of the figure:
- `pOneClOne` 11 `es'8(\mf^\markup \italic "leggiero"`; `pOneClTwo` 11 the same with `-\tag #'part`
- `pOneVla` 19 `g8(\mf^\markup \italic "leggiero"`
- `closVla` 83 `es'8(\mf^\markup \italic "leggiero"`
- `recPClOne` 151 `es'8(\f^\markup \italic "leggiero"`; `recPClTwo` 151 the same with `-\tag #'part`

No dynamic, slur or note changes, and F1's own articulation (protected since c058) is untouched. The counter-lines that are melodies (27-34 Cl 1·2 with the violins, 159-165 Cl 1 with Vln II "espress.") are deliberately left without the word.

### 3. 67-82: harp mf at the lead's level, above the mp counter-melody. Checked, no change

Ex. 269 (PDF 205) gives lead, counter and accompaniment separate levels, and on paper the harp's mf ranks above the violas' and cellos' mp espress. counter. But a harp's mf broken chords sound well below a string section's mf (Adler's rule, applied in c175 and c254). The counter is two sections in unison, and the harp is an octave apart from it. I decided to keep this as it is.
