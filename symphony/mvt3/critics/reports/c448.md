## Findings

### 1. 272.2: the syncopated subdominant enters unaccented after an accented beat (applied)

Bars: 272 (Bn 1, Hn 3·4, violas).

Problem. In 272 the inner parts finish the F1 head with an accented C4 on beat 1 (`c'4->`). They then move to F4/A4 on beat 2 and hold it into the Largamente at 273 (Bn 1 `f'2.`, Hn 3 `a'2.`, Hn 4 `f'2.`, Vla `<f' a'>2.`). Only these notes complete the IV chord. On 272.1 the harmony is only C and A: the bass C2, the cellos' C3 and the melody's accented A6. All of this happens under "allargando". In the MIDI the held F/A sounds at velocity 54–67, against 87–111 for the accented C before it and for the violins' A6. A player reads an unmarked note after an accented one as the end of the accent, so the subdominant sounds like a decay just where the allargando needs its weight.

Evidence. A&S PDF 51: a note that begins on a weak beat and is held through the next strong beat "conflicts — sometimes very strongly — with the meter". In Ex. 3-6 (image) Beethoven marks exactly such a tied note sf at Allegro molto. PDF 50: "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation", so the attack is the only chance to place the chord. This score marks its other syncopated long notes the same way: the tenuto on F1's `a2--`, the accented `e''4->~` at 275, and the accented B♭ at 93 (c248). 272.2 was the one syncopated chord entry in the coda left unmarked.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-448/work/sections/finale.ily):
- Bn 1: `c'4->) f'2.->`
- Hn 3: `c'4->) a'2.->`
- Hn 4: `c'4->) f'2.->`
- Vla: `c'4->) <f' a'>2.->^\markup \italic "div."`

Notes, dynamics, tempo and the Largamente's chords are unchanged. The horns share a staff and both carry the accent, so the part-combine is unaffected. The MIDI now gives the held F/A velocity 74–107. A comment line "% c448 ..." has been added at the top of finale.ily.

Tested alternative: a tenuto (`f'2.--`), as on F1's syncopated half. I rejected it because every neighbouring note in 269–273 is accented, and a tenuto would still read as lighter than the accented C before it.

### 2. Four-bar grid, dynamics and tempo words: checked, no change

Every section after the intro starts on a bar ≡ 3 (mod 4), so the four-bar hypermeasure of A&S PDF 53 runs from 11 to 264 without a break. dyn.py places every ff, fff, sf and sfp arrival on a strong bar (bar 1 or 3 of a group), with five exceptions, all at the end of a group: 110 (the lament's ff, protected), 138 (stop chord, protected), 150 (retransition, protected), 226 (the canon's ff, protected) and 278 (the close, protected). These end-accented arrivals are deliberate goals.

Every tempo word (poco rit. 49–50, animando 82, poco rit. 197–198, allargando 221–222, stringendo 245–246, allargando 271–272) covers the last two bars of a group, and the new tempo starts on a group downbeat. 273 is the exception: by the bar count it is bar 3 of the grid from 263. By the phrases it starts a new group, since 263–264 is a two-bar release and the groups then begin at 265, 269, 273 and 277 (A&S PDF 54: grouping follows the tonal goals). No change.

### 3. 87–89: the 7–6 chain in Bn 1 and the violas: considered, declined

Bn 1 and the violas form a suspension chain of the Ex. 3-16 type (E♭4 over F at 87.3, D4 tied into 88, B♭3 tied into 89), with no articulation. Accents on the tied entries at 87.4 and 88.4 would follow Ex. 3-6. I did not apply them. c233 (A&S PDF 291–292) set this 6/3 series so that only its start and end are articulated, and the violins' scale figure is the line in front. Accents in the middle of the series would undo that without a new reason.

### 4. 265–272: accompaniment accents on every downbeat: considered, declined

Tuba, Bn 2 and timpani accent beat 1 of every bar, including weak bars 2 and 4 of the groups. A&S PDF 53 would allow lightening 266 and 268. I did not apply it: the even strokes hold the metre against the syncopated F1 head (`r8 g''( e'''4.->)`), and that conflict is what PDF 51 describes as the purpose of syncopation. Movement I's hammered tutti, which this passage recalls, is laid out the same way.
