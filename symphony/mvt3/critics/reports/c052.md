## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 95–97: outer voices in octaves, and the 7th of the applied V7 doubled (applied)
**Problem.** The closing group ends with Ob 1 and Vln I on E♭5–D♭5–C5 over Vc, Cb and Bn 2 on E♭2–D♭2–C2: three bars of octaves between the top voice and the bass, p and in a thin texture. At 96 the D♭ is the 7th of E♭7 (V4/2 of A♭). It is doubled in the soprano and the bass, and both copies resolve to C. The chord at 96 also has no E♭ in the upper voices, so only D♭–G–B♭ is heard. At 97 the 7th of C7 (B♭) is doubled again, in the violas (B♭3) and Cl 1 (B♭4).
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 439, point 3: "Don't double the temporary leading tone or the 7th." A doubled 7th must either resolve in octaves or leave one copy unresolved. PDF 434–435: in stepwise descents, applied chords are there to break up "the 5ths and octaves that would otherwise occur". analysis.txt counted this passage as one of its four "doubling runs ignored". After the fix the count is 3.
**Fix (applied).** The bass stays on E♭ at 96, so the chord is E♭7 in root position, its 7th D♭ is only in the top voice, and it resolves to C over C7. This is the descending-3rds pattern of Ex. 24-9 (PDF 406), with B♭ held as a common tone into C7's 7th. Cl 1 moves up to C at 97 so B♭ is single. The top line E♭–D♭–C is kept. In clos.ily:
- `closVc`: `es1\>( | des1) | c1\p~` → `es1\>~ | es1 | c1\p~`
- `closCb`: `es,1\> | des,1 | c,1\p~` → `es,1\>~ | es,1 | c,1\p~`
- `closBsnTwo`: `es1\p | des1 | c1~` → `es1\p~ | es1 | c1~`
- `closClOne`: `bes'1\p~ | bes'1 | bes'1~ | bes'2. r4` → `bes'1\p~ | bes'1 | c''1~ | c''2. r4`

The soprano D♭→C over the bass E♭→C is a direct octave approached by step in the soprano, which is the allowed form.

### 2. 47–50: the neighbour chord over the B♭ pedal does not return, and V7 of E♭ ends with a tripled 7th and no 5th (applied)
**Problem.** At 47–48 the violas move D→C♭ and Vln II F→G♭ over the B♭ pedal, which makes iv7 of E♭ minor over the dominant. At 49 neither neighbour returns. The violas leap C♭→A♭ and Vln II stops. The V7 that should prepare the second theme is B♭–D–A♭, and A♭ sits in three parts (Ob A♭4, Va A♭3, Bn A♭3). The ♭9 (C♭) and ♭13 (G♭) are left unresolved just before the trumpet's soft entry.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 413–414 (sec. 15, Ex. 24-22 and 24-23): an apparent chord over a held bass comes from neighbour and passing tones, and the neighbours return to the chord they decorate. PDF 439, point 3: do not double the 7th. PDF 410 and 428: B♭7 here is ♮VII7 of C minor and V7 of III, so it is a real dominant and should be voiced like one.
**Fix (applied).** C♭ becomes a 9-8 suspension: tied into 49, falling to B♭ on beat 3. G♭ falls to F at 49. My first version moved both at the barline, and p5.py showed parallel 5ths (C♭/G♭ → B♭/F), so the suspension is needed. In trans.ily:
- `transVla`: `... ces'1~ | ces'1 | aes1\pp~ | aes2. r4` → `... ces'1~ | ces'1~ | ces'2 bes2~ | bes2. r4`
- `transVlnTwo`: `... ges'1~ | ges'1 | R1*2` → `... ges'1~ | ges'1 | f'1~ | f'2. r4`

The V7 at 49–50 is now complete: B♭, A♭3, B♭3, D4, F4, A♭4. Vln II and the violas still have two beats to take pizz. for 51.

### 3. 214: Ob 2 holds the 7th G♭5 against Ob 1's F5, its resolution (applied)
**Problem.** In the theme B recall, Ob 2 holds G♭5 (the 7th of A♭7) through all of 214. Ob 1 takes the tune's F5 on beat 4, a minor 2nd below it on the same staff, ff. Ob 2 then leaps G♭→E♭ at 215. Cycle 51 removed the same kind of clash from the violins at 214 but left it in the oboes.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 406–408: a resolution can be transferred to the next voice in the same register, but the 7th should not sound together with its own tone of resolution. PDF 405: the 7th moves down by step.
**Fix (applied).** In codaTwo.ily, `codaTwoOboeTwo` bar 214: `ges''1` → `ges''2. f''4`. Both oboes now sound F5 on beat 4 and E♭5 at 215, which is G♭–F–E♭ by step. Theme B (Ob 1, Tpt 1, Cl 1, flutes) is unchanged.

### 4. 147–150: the leading tone is doubled above the bass B in the ff V6/5 (applied)
**Problem.** In the retransition's last four bars, the bass already holds B in six instruments (Cbn, Tuba, Cb, B. cl., Bn 2, Vc). B is also in Ob 1 (B5), Vln II, Hn 2, Tpt 2 and the violas (B4), while the root G is only in B. tbn, Tpt 3, Hn 3·4 and Vln II/Cl on top. The chord sounds weighted toward the leading tone just before the recapitulation.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 423 and 439, point 3: the leading tone is not doubled. Ex. 25-14c (PDF 425) shows the doubled leading tone as the main danger when VII°7/V7 resolves.
**Fix (applied).** In retr.ily:
- `retrHnTwo` 147–150: `b'1->\ff | b'1 | b'1~ | b'2.` → `g'1->\ff | g'1 | g'1~ | g'2.`
- `retrVla` 147, 149–150: `<d' b'>` → `<d' g'>`

B stays in the bass, Ob 1 (the stop-chord colour from 138), Vln II and Tpt 2. The stretto 139–146 and the timpani are untouched.

- **266–270 (Tpt 3 F on beat 3).** 268 is C7 → IV6/4 over the tonic pedal, with Tpt 3 E4→F4 as the temporary leading tone of IV and the tune's B♭→A as the 7th. This is A&S's own exercise ("tonic pedal using I♭7 as V of IV", PDF 439, ex. 1b; Ex. 25-9c).
