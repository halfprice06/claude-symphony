## Findings

### 1. 263–271: the tonic after the symphony's first V–I is too short (applied)
Bars: 263–271 in v23. Problem: after 262→263, the first root-position V–I in C since movement I bar 135, the tonic lasts nine bars: two held, two 2-bar fragments of F1 (265–268), one echo bar, the final chord. About 11 s. It is the goal of a plan that has passed through D♭ (211), E (243), and A♭ (259–261).
Evidence: A&S PDF 592: the masters exploit remote areas "without sacrificing the underlying diatonicism that gives unity and a sense of directed, goal-oriented progression"; each remote area creates "tonal conflicts that the composer must somehow work out". After a ♭VI, III♯ and ♭II, the tonic must last long enough to settle them. A&S PDF 600–601 and PDF 603 point 5: tie the large-scale plan to the motive. The movement's opening motive is A♭→G (German sixth → V, bars 1–2), and ♭VI–Ger6–V at 259–262 enlarges it; the close never states it over the tonic. A&S PDF 586–587: the connection of two chords a third apart through a common tone held in the soprano. Corpus: Dvořák 9/iv 16 bars of 4/4 of tonic with a chromatic inflection over it; Tchaikovsky 6/iii 18 bars of 4/4; both about 25–28 s.
