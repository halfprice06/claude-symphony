## Findings

### 1. Violas' pp open D held 3.5 bars at 43–46 (applied in scratch)

Bars: 43–46 (trans, letter C).

Problem: the violas hold D4 pp from 43 to 46.2 under the violins' touch-fourth harmonic, Vln II's F4 and the cellos' divided B♭2/F3. Nothing marks a string, so the section plays the open D. Of all soft held notes on an open string in the score, this is the longest and the softest (MIDI velocity 37). It is the third of the dominant chord that the trumpet solo at 51 waits for.

Evidence: Adler PDF 73–74 says that in slow or soft expressive passages the open string, which the finger's vibrato does not control, "stands out peculiarly". PDF 76 says to mark sul G when a passage should stay on one string. PDF 87 calls the viola's G and D strings the "accompaniment strings" that "can also be exploited for their dark quality". The score already applies this rule to every other soft held D4 in an unprotected passage: violas at 8–10 and 133–134 (c466), violins and cellos at 41–42 and violins and violas at 169–170 (c391), Vln II at 81. The bars at 43–46 were missed. They come two bars after the sul G fade at 41–42. On the G string the D4 is the 4th finger in first position, so the change costs nothing technically. The C4 at 46.3 (3rd finger) stays on the same string. The C♭4 and B♭3 that follow can only be played on the G string.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-556/work/sections/trans.ily, with a c556 comment above it):

```lilypond
transVla = { \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul G" d'1\pp\startTextSpan~ | d'1~ | d'1~ | d'2 c'2\stopTextSpan | ces'1~ | ces'1~ | ces'2\> bes2~\! | bes2. r4 | }
```

Result: text only. The MIDI is byte-identical. The page-3 image at 200 dpi shows "sul G" over the viola's pp at 43 with no collision, and the dashed line ends on the C4 at 46. No system or page moved; there are still 16 pages and 0 warnings.

### 2. Double stops and divisi: all orchestral-easy (no change)

Bars: 1–2, 5–6, 91–94, 119–123, 136–139, 147–151, 159–164, 205–222, 263, 273–278.

Adler PDF 76–78 says to use only the easy double stops and to give anything hard to divisi. PDF 97 says cello stops wider than a sixth in the low register are hard. PDF 107 says basses should avoid double stops. Every violin, viola and cello double stop in the score is one of these:
- a third, fourth, fifth or sixth on adjacent strings in first to third position;
- a stop with an open string (Vln II G3–D4–B4 at 2; Vc G2–F3 at 147/149; Vc C2–C3 at 263);
- marked div. or "4 soli / gli altri" (the octaves at 119–123 and 159–164; Vc B2–A♭3 at 138, inside the div. from 136; violas E♭4–F♯4 at 1 and 209).

The basses have none. No change.

### 3. Wide skips in the violins and cellos (no change; one candidate declined on paper)

Bars: 36→37 and 38→39 (both violin sections), 206→207 and 208→209 (Vln II), 169 and 258→259 (cellos).

Adler PDF 82–83 (Ex. III-22, III-23) says fast wide skips are hard for a section, worst on one string or between extreme registers. The 36→37 C5→F6 was cleared at c376. At 206→207 and 208→209 a quarter rest precedes the low entry. At 169 the cellos leap from the open G, so no shift is needed. At 258→259 the tempo is Maestoso.

The closest case is Vln II's D6 (last slurred eighth of 38) to the ff accented F4 on 39.1. That is a shift from third position on the E string to first position on the D string, with a bow lift between the slur's end and a new down-bow. It is a routine section shift, not the same-string or extreme-register skip Adler warns about. I declined moving Vln II's second half of 38 down an octave: it would break the violins' unison on F1's countersubject, which c551 left as it is. No change.
