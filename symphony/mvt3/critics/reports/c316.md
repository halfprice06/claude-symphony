## What else I studied

I built a brass-only score from the v316 sources (one staff per player, no part-combining) and logged every note, slur, breath mark and dynamic with LilyPond's event listener, with the tempo map taken from the MIDI. From that I listed each player's range, every soft entry above the comfortable register, every slur longer than 4.5 seconds, and every stretch of continuous sound with no rest and no breath mark longer than 9 seconds. I also checked the order of the four horns in every four-part horn chord.

Three results are recorded here because they do not lead to a change:

- Horns are stacked 1-2-3-4 (Hn 2 above Hn 3) in 28 bars: 1-2, 5-6, 9-10, 89-94, 141-142, 199-204, 219 and 223-234. This is the pairs layout Brahms used (1·2 and 3·4 crooked as pairs), and nearly all of these bars are protected (German sixth layout, hammered tutti, stretto, arrival and chorale). Swapping the inner voices at 89-94 would not change the sound and would make both shared staves harder to read. The one bar where the pairs layout costs anything is 141, where Hn 2 enters cold on written F♯5 p. That is inside the protected stretto, and the alternative (Hn 3 leaping a sixth from its held D4 after the breath at 140) is no easier. Kept.
- Adler's special effects (glissando, horn and trumpet trills, flutter-tonguing, cuivré, bells up, muted horns and trombones, muted ff brass) are not used. I looked for a place where one would help. The places where they would be most audible are protected: the arrival (222-223), the last cadence (261-263) and the close (273-279). Adler also warns against these effects: the trombone glissando is "a well-worn cliché" (PDF 296, adler2 5), horn trills sound "comical or sardonic" (PDF 311), and bells up belong to "very loud and boisterous passages" (PDF 308). I also considered a horn rip into 273, from Hn 3's C4 at 272 up to its A4. The harp already has a glissando into 278 (c249), and a second sweep this close would turn the Largamente into an effect. I did not add any of these. Dvořák 9/iv and Brahms 1/iv, the movement's models, do not use them either.
- The tuba never goes above G2, so its "very strong" middle register (adler2 8, Ex. X-86) is never used. In this style the tuba is the bass of the trombones, as Adler describes it ("an excellent bass for them", adler2 8), and every bar where it would move up is a bass line. Kept.

Quotations: `themecheck.py themes-finale-quotes.ly` against my scratch copy reports 41/41 unchanged. The scherzo's motive x (G-G-G-G at 9, 143-144, 178) and theme A's head (275-276) still match themes-scherzo.ly. No quotation and none of F1, F2 or the chorale top line is changed.

## Findings

### 1. 207-210: Hn 1 holds written B♭5 mp for four bars (applied)

**Problem.** After F1's second head (205-206), the horns hold the ii7 and V7 chord over the A♭ pedal at mp, with a crescendo from 209: Hn 1 E♭5 (written B♭5), Hn 3 B♭4 then C5, Hn 2 G♭4, Hn 4 E♭4. Hn 1 enters on written B♭5 after a quarter rest, coming from D♭4 (written A♭4) on 206.3. It holds that note softly for four bars, about 10.5 seconds at 2 = 88-92.

**Evidence.** Adler says a soft attack in the extreme high register is "fiendishly difficult, because of the firmness of the lips and the velocity of the breath required" (adler1 PDF 284). His register chart (Ex. X-5, PDF 303) labels written E5-C6 "brilliant and loud", and he adds that "it is most taxing to play in the higher register all the time" (PDF 303). He also warns about leaps the hornist has to "hear" and "find" (PDF 304). Here the note is a ninth above the previous one. c136 saw the leap and inserted the quarter rest at 206.4, but the held soft written B♭5 is still there. The E♭5 is not needed in the horns. E♭ is already in Hn 2 (after the fix), Tbn 2, the violas and, at 209-210, Cl 2 and Fl 2. At 207-208 the woodwinds and Vln I carry F1's head above the horns, and Adler's warning that the brass can cover everything else (PDF 292) favours a lower horn top under it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-316/work/sections/codaOne.ily).** The chord is re-voiced with the same pitch classes. It is interlocked in Adler's high/low order (I and III high, II and IV low, the third between the first and the second, PDF 301, 303, Ex. X-2 and X-6):

```
codaOneHnOne   ... des'4-.) r4  | bes'1\mp | bes'1 | c''1\<  | c''2. r4\! | }
codaOneHnTwo   ... des'4-.) c'4-. | es'1\mp | es'1 | es'1\<  | es'2. r4\! | }
codaOneHnThree ... des'4-.) r4  | ges'1\mp | ges'1 | ges'1\< | ges'2. r4\! | }
codaOneHnFour  ... des'4-.) c'4-. | aes1\mp | aes1 | aes1\<   | aes2. r4\! | }
```

Hn 1 now plays written F5-G5 ("bright and heroic"), approached by a sixth after its rest. Hn 3 moves a fourth, Hn 2 a third and Hn 4 a third. Hn 4's A♭3 doubles the pedal inside the trombones. Tbn 1's seventh D♭4→C4 at 209 is unchanged. The shared staves keep equal dynamics (mp, < at 209). The page prints cleanly (page 11).

### 2. 219-225 tuba and bass trombone, 255-264 Tpt 3: loud slow passages with no breath (applied)

**Problem.** The tuba and bass trombone play 219-225 without a rest or a breath mark: A♭ p (219), G, G <, D mf <, C fff (the arrival), C f, C. That is seven whole notes at 2 = 72 and then 56, about 13 seconds, and it includes the crescendo to fff. Every other brass player breathes after 223 (c136). These two keep the C pedal and breathe only after 225. Tpt 3 plays ten bars of accented half notes at ff into fff at 255-264 (12.6 seconds) with no breath mark. Hn 3, which plays the same rhythm, has one after 256.

**Evidence.** Adler: "allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath ... In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath in a slow tempo, for it does take more breath to play loudly than softly" (adler1 PDF 292). On the tuba: "It takes a great deal of breath to play this largest of all brass instruments, and special care must be exercised not to tire out the performer" (adler2 PDF 8). The score marks breaths everywhere else in the brass (c046, c136, c226). These are the two longest unmarked loud stretches my scan found.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily: `g,1 \breathe | g,1\<` in the bass trombone and `g,,1 \breathe | g,,1\<` in the tuba, between the two G's at 220|221, where the crescendo starts. The cellos, basses and contrabassoon hold the bass through the breath, and the C pedal from 223 to 225 is not interrupted. finale.ily: Tpt 3 `c'2-> c'2 \breathe | f'2->\<` at 258|259, before its crescendo. No pitch, dynamic or rhythm changes. The 205-223 dynamic path, the arrival at 223 and the 255-263 progression are untouched.
