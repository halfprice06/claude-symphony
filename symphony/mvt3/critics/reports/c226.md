## Findings

### 1. 149-150: the held dominant before the reprise has no brass attack shape (applied)

**Bars:** 147-150 (Hn 1-4, Tpt 3, Tbn 1-2, bass trombone, tuba).

**Problem:** The whole orchestra holds V of C ff for four bars (147-150) under the allargando. Only Tpt 1-2 re-strike on the half bars. The brass re-attack at 149 (tied into 150) at the same ff as 147, so the last two bars before the recapitulation are a static block.

**Evidence:** The first attack Adler lists for all brass is the sforzando and forte-piano attack, written sff > pp < fff on a held chord (PDF 290, Ex. IX-9). He gives the same envelope as idiomatic for the trumpet (PDF 323, Ex. X-54, sff > p < ff) and the tuba (adler2 PDF 8, Ex. X-87). Stopped horn sf > p (PDF 308) is the only related attack this score uses (138). The fp-crescendo on a held dominant into a reprise is standard Tchaikovsky practice. The strings already change bow at 149 ("change bow at will"), so 149 is already a point of re-articulation.

**Fix (applied in retr.ily; no pitch changed):**
```
retrHnOne   ... g'1~\sfp\< | g'2.\ff r4 |     (Hn 2 the same)
retrHnThree ... d'1~\sfp\< | d'2.\ff r4 |     (Hn 4 the same)
retrTptThree ... g'1~\sfp\< | g'2.\ff r4 |
retrTbnOne  ... f'1~\sfp\< | f'2.\ff r4 |     (Tbn 2 d'1~ ..., B. tbn g,1~ ...)
retrTuba    ... g,,1~\sfp\< | g,,2.\ff d,4 |
```
Tpt 1-2 keep their ff strokes on 149 (d''2-> f''2->), which now sound through the dropped brass chord. Woodwinds, strings and the timpani roll stay ff. The shared staves (Hn 1·2, Hn 3·4, Tbn 1·2) have equal dynamics.

**Protections answered:** the 147-150 protections (c117 "strokes 147-150", c171 "no ♭9", c194) cover pitches, voicing and the trumpets' and timpani's strokes, and all of these are unchanged. The cycle 8 limit on strong marks aimed to keep accents for structural points. 149 is the structural point that ends the retransition, and sfp lowers the level there before rebuilding it, so it does not add a new loud peak.

### 2. 251-254: Tpt 3 plays 33 bars of f-fff with no bar's rest (applied)

**Bars:** 247-279.

**Problem:** Tpt 3 plays strokes from 247, the last chorale in halves ff 255-262, fff 263-264, strokes 265-272 and the held close 273-279. That is about 46 s with no rest of a bar. Tpt 1-2 were given rests at 251-254 for this reason (c076, c087) and at 269-270 (c071).

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 292: brass playing "is rather taxing, one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate". The same advice appears for the tuba (adler2 PDF 8) and the high horn (PDF 303).

**Fix (applied in finale.ily):** `finaleTptThree = { c''4\f r8 c''8 c''4 r8 c''8 | f'4 ... | c''4 ... | g'4 r8 g'8 g'4 r8 g'8 | R1*4 | c'2->\ff ...`. Its G4/A4 at 251-254 is already in the woodwinds, horns and violas. The timpani keep their strokes at 251 and 254. All three trumpets now enter the last chorale together at 255.

**Protection answered:** the c217/c218 protection of 247-254 concerns the S-head texture and the violins' bowing, which are unchanged. This only extends c087's trumpet rest to the third player.

### 3. 242 and 246: Hn 3-4 have no breath in 235-250 (applied)

**Bars:** 235-250.

**Problem:** Hn 4 holds whole notes from 235 to 250 (about 27 s, p rising to f) with no breath mark. At 243-246 it holds G♯3-G3-F3 under one slur at f during the stringendo. Hn 3 holds 235-242 without a breath.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 292 and PDF 309: "all phrases under one slur will automatically be performed on one breath", and in a loud passage too many notes on one breath should be avoided.

**Fix (applied in sHead.ily; no note changed):** `sHeadHnThree = { ... | d'2 c'2 \breathe | b2\f r2 | R1*3 | }` and `sHeadHnFour = { ... | a1 \breathe | gis2\f( g2~ | g1 | f1~ | f1) \breathe | }`. The breath at 242 falls at the phrase boundary before the new f entry at 243. The breath at 246 matches c186's existing breath after 250.

### 4. Trumpet parts: key signatures (applied, parts only)

**Problem:** The parts for trumpet in C print the key signature (six flats for the solo at 51-66), while the horn parts are keyless.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 297-298: "Usually, horns and trumpets are written without key signatures... Most performers prefer this manner of notation", while trombones and tuba read key signatures.

**Fix (applied):** In mkparts.py the three trumpet entries have `key` set to False. parts-book.ly has `\new Staff \with { \remove "Key_engraver" }` for part-tptOne, part-tptTwo and part-tptThree. I test-compiled the Trumpet 1 part: 0 warnings, 2 pages, and every accidental is printed at 51-66 and 171-186. Score and MIDI are unchanged.

### Kept (checked, no change)

- **224-228 chorale:** brass hold 10.7 s at f on separate, tongued whole notes at Maestoso (Adler PDF 292). The notes are not slurred, so players can breathe between them. The passage is protected and I made no change.
- **185 Tpt 1 muted B3 at p:** this is a soft note in the low register (PDF 319), but it is doubled by the cor anglais and protected (171-186).
- **Horn trills, glissandi, cuivré, bells up and loud muted brass:** Adler teaches all of them (PDF 296-297, 308-311), but each points to Strauss/Mahler or to a "comical or sardonic" effect. None suits a Brahms/Tchaikovsky/Dvořák finale, so I did not add them.
