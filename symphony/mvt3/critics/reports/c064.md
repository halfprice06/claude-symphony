## Findings

### 1. Development climax 110-113: the horns are single at f while Hn 3·4 rest (applied)
- **Problem.** At 110-113 the brass play V7 of F at f (c054 kept them under the ff strings and winds). The chord's E4 and G4 are in Hn 2 and Hn 1, one player each. Every other note has a trumpet or a trombone: B♭4 Tpt 3, C5 Tpt 2, E5 Tpt 1, C4 Tbn 1, G3 Tbn 2, C3 B.tbn, C2 tuba. Adler says that above mf "it is advisable to double the horns, meaning that two horns in unison equal the power of one trumpet or one trombone" (PDF 15), and he repeats the point for Bartók's loud brass (PDF 44). As written, the two inner notes are about half the weight of the others. The middle of the chord thins out between the trombones and the trumpets. That is the middle register the climax melody (G5/G6) needs filled underneath it. Hn 3·4 rest from 104 to 128, so they are free.
- **Evidence.** dev.ily `devHnThree`/`devHnFour` had `R1*8` for 107-114. In Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15-16), every f and ff chord has its horn notes paired ("2 Hn.", "4 Hn.") or doubling a trumpet.
- **Fix (applied).** Hn 3 = Hn 1 and Hn 4 = Hn 2 at 110-113:
  `devHnThree = { … | R1*3 | g'1\f | aes'2 aes'2 | bes'2 g'2 | aes'2-> r2 | R1 | R1*8 | …`
  `devHnFour  = { … | R1*3 | e'1\f | f'2 f'2 | f'2~ f'4 e'4 | f'2-> r2 | R1 | R1*8 | …`
  The dynamic stays f (c054's balance holds), and the doubling lies below the melody's register. The pitches and harmony do not change. Hn 4 keeps Hn 2's F4 to beat 4 at 112, so c062's suspension rule is kept.

### 2. Last chorale 255-258: the protected top line is one trumpet against four instruments an octave below (applied)
- **Problem.** At 255-256 the chorale's top line starts on G4 in Tpt 1 alone. Directly below it, Hn 1·2 a2, Tbn 1 and Tbn 2 all hold G3. By Adler's weights (PDF 15) that is one unit above three. The lower octave is louder than the line, and at G4 the trumpet is in its middle-low register. Vln I's G5 is the only other instrument on the line, because the upper woodwinds rest at 255-256 (protected, c038). At 257-258 (E5) the lower octave is still Hn 1·2 a2 + Tbn 1 + Hn 3, against Tpt 1 + Ob 1. Adler's model for a climax with a long-note line over busy accompaniment is Tchaikovsky 4/i 277-290 (Ex. XI-21, PDF 39-41). There the cantus firmus is in the trombones a2, in unison, against rhythmic chords in the woodwinds, horns and trumpets. That is the layout of 255-262 (held line over Vln II eighths, viola quarters and marcato Hn 3/Tpt 3), except that the line here is in one player.
- **Fix (applied).** Tpt 2 doubles Tpt 1 for the line's first two notes, then returns to its harmony note:
  `finaleTptTwo = { … \breathe | g'1\ff~ | g'1 | e''1~ | e''1 | c''1\<~ | …` (was `e'1\ff~ | e'1 | c''1~ | c''1 |`).
  Tpt 2 cannot double the line any longer: at 259-260 its C5 is the only C in the iiø6/5 chord, so it goes back to C5 at 259. Nothing is lost at 255-258. The E of I6 stays in Hn 3, B.tbn, tuba, Vc and Vln II. The C of vi stays in Ob 2, Fl 2, Tbn 2 and Vln II. The top line's pitches (protected) and Tpt 2's E5 at 263 do not change. The staff now prints "a2" at 255.

### 3. Checked and left as they are (no change)
- **114-122, the bassoon solo's accompaniment.** The brass rest. The pizzicato violas and Vln II lie above the solo and the bass two octaves below it, so nothing sustained sits in the solo's register. Adler's use 2 (a brass pedal, PDF 16) would need a held note, and that would hit the F♯ of the V6/5 chords at 116 and 124 (a G pedal) or the solo's D4 (a D pedal). I decided against adding a horn pedal.
- **171-186.** The muted Tpt 1 p in unison with the cor anglais is Adler's muted-solo colour (PDF 50-51) at a dynamic where one brass player per line is enough (PDF 15). The thinner accompaniment from c063 leaves no brass in the solo's register.
- **187-194.** Hn 1 p dolce on Vln II's octave is a single horn at p doubling a mf string line. At that level it blends and does not need a second horn (PDF 15). Its range G4-D5 is the horn's best singing register.
- **247-254, woodwinds against brass.** The S head has Tpt 1·2 ff (E5-C6) with Ob 1·2 at pitch and, an octave lower, Hn 1·2 a2 + Cl 1·2 + violas. The P head has Fl 1·2 + Vln I·II (G5-A6). I considered lowering the trumpets to f. That would cancel the crescendo into 247 (c048) and weaken the owed brass combination, so I kept it. The P head lies above the trumpets and moves in eighths against their long notes, so register and rhythm keep it apart. Moving Cl 1·2 to the P head would put them at written B6 (252), outside their best register (PDF 42-44).
- **265-272.** Here the balance is right by Adler's weights. At 265-268 F1 is in Tpt 1·2 a2 + Hn 1·2 a2 + violas, against movement I's head in all woodwinds and violins an octave or more above. At 269-272 F1 is in Tpt a2 against Hn 3·4 a2 + Tbn 1 + Tbn 2 (8vb), and the trombones play an independent line (PDF 44).
