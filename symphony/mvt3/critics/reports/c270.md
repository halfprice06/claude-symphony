## Findings

### 1. Applied. 197–198: close movement II's key with movement II's own mixture (F3–F♭3–E♭3)

- **Bars:** 197 beat 4 to 198, A♭-major statement of F2 (recapitulation).
- **Problem:** The A♭ statement (♭VI of C, movement II's key) ends with a plain I6–IV–I plagal close at 197–198. The movement's mixture is then used only in C and E♭. Movement II's own close is IV6 – iv – I with the inner line F3–F♭3–E♭3 (II 87–89, lower violas and Hn 4). The finale's three closes are meant to share the ♮6–♭6–5̂ line (mvt2.md "What the finale relies on"), but the finale never sounds it in A♭.
- **Reading:** A&S PDF 390, Ex. 23-5 (Brahms 3/ii) calls a plagal IV♭–I after a turn to minor "a very characteristic use of mixture". The new beat-4 chord is Ex. 23-2e, II6/5 with ♭6 (B♭–D♭–F♭–A♭ over D♭), reached by inflecting ♮6 inside the subdominant (Ex. 23-2f). The line F–F♭–E♭ is the "good" ♮6–♭6–5̂ (PDF 388, PDF 395 pt 3). F♭ acts as the upper neighbour of E♭, as in Chopin Op. 17/3, which is also in A♭ (Ex. 23-9, PDF 393).
- **Musical effect:** The ♭6 darkens the A♭ chord one beat before the C-minor hammered tutti at 199. This is the Mendelssohn principle (PDF 386–387): the minor runs through the major. It also puts movement II's F♭→E♭ thread at movement II's pitch inside the finale's movement-II key.
- **Fix:** Notes are inside `\transpose c aes,`, so `aes` there sounds F♭.
  - Violas: `\transpose c aes, { g2(\mf\< a4 aes4) } | es2(\f\< c'2) | }`. This gives E♭3 F3 F♭3 | E♭3 C4. C4 comes back on 198 beat 3, so the chord keeps its third.
  - Violoncellos: `\transpose c aes, { g2(\< a4 c'4) } | c'1\f\< | }`. This gives E♭3 F3 A♭3 | C4, so no F♮ sounds against the F♭.
  - Horn 2: `\transpose c aes, { g2\< a4 aes4 } | es1\f\< \breathe | }`. This gives F3–F♭3–E♭3, movement II's Hn 4 line. Hn 2 breathes after 198 and re-enters on E♭4 at 199.
- **Unchanged:** F2's tune, the 197–198 bass line (protected in c203), the bow plan's down-bow peak at 198 (the violas' 198 stays one bow), dynamics, and the harp rest.
- **Rules:** The new F♭ resolves to E♭ ("Every F♭ resolves to E♭"). The F→F♭ step is in one voice, and the cellos' F that leaves at the same moment is only a doubling (Ex. 23-12b).
- **Checks:**
  - par8.py on 196–199 lists the same pre-existing bass-doubling octaves as v270 and nothing new.
  - The outer-voice parallel count stays 0.
  - The engraving at 197 is clean (F♭ printed in the horns and violas, page 10).
- **For the composer:** finale.md item 5 of 201–210 ("the movement's only F♭") would need updating, and the movement II thread should be told that its 87–89 line is now echoed at 197–198. No theme or quotation changes.
