## Findings

### 1. 218→219 is a root-position V(♭9)–I cadence in C (bars 216–219). Applied.
Problem: 216 cadential 6/4 over G, 217 V7 over G, 218 V7♭9 over G, 219 C over C, ff, with the timpani G→C. That is a perfect authentic cadence in root position, so the last cadence (254–255) is not the symphony's first root-position V–I since movement I bar 135, which the brief and the plan require. The same bars also have parallel fifths: Hn 1·2, Ob 2, Fl 2 and the violas move D→G while the bass and the bass trombone move G→C (A&S PDF 83). Putting the bass on B♮ would make octaves B→C with trumpet 1, so that is ruled out too (PDF 83).
Fix: in 218, put the bass on D. Bar 218 then sounds movement I's stop chord B–D–F–A♭ itself, as vii°6/5 over the timpani's G roll. It resolves at 218→219 as the cyclic rule requires, the bass moves D→C by step, and trumpet 1 still takes B→C.
```

codaTwoBsnTwo … g,1\< | d,1\! |     codaTwoVc   … g,1\<  | d1\! |
```

After the change, 219 has no fifths.

### 2. The final cadence 253–255 has fifths, octaves and a doubled seventh. Applied.
Problem: at 254 the chord ♭VI (A♭–C–E♭) goes to V7. Vla, Cl 2 and Hn 3 move E♭→D against the bass A♭→G (fifths, PDF 83; A&S PDF 506 and 526 on this progression). Fl 2, Ob 2 and Vln II move A♭→G with the bass (octaves between the alto and the bass). The seventh F is doubled in Tbn 2 and Tpt 3, and Tpt 3 takes it up to G (PDF 76: "The chord 7th … must never be doubled"). This is the symphony's last cadence and the one the whole cadence plan leads to.
Fix: the alto line takes the seventh, and the E♭ voices skip up to G (A&S PDF 514).
```
finaleFluteTwo … aes''2 f''2 | e''1     finaleOboeTwo … aes'2 f'2 | e'1
finaleVlnTwo   … aes''2 f''2 | e''1     finaleTbnTwo  … es2 g2 | g1
finaleTptThree … es'2 g'2 | g'1         finaleVla / finaleClTwo / finaleHnThree … es'2 g'2 | e'1
```
The result is a complete V7 (G bass, B on top, D in Tpt 2, a single F), and the chorale top line `c'2 b2 | c'1` and Tpt 1 B→C are unchanged.

### 3. The melody and the bass move in parallel fifths at three cadences (57–58, 177–178, 247–248). Applied.
Problem: in the trumpet solo, iv in root position goes to V while the melody moves E♭→F over A♭→B♭ (57–58). The cor anglais and muted trumpet do the same with C→D over F→G (177–178). Cl 2 doubles these fifths in both places. Over pizzicato strings the texture is thin, so the fifths are exposed, and they are outer-voice fifths. At 247–248 the flute and violin theme moves B→C (an eighth into the downbeat) over the bass E→F. PDF 83 forbids all of these.
Fix: use iv6 (a Phrygian approach) at 57 and 177, and move the bass to C on the second half of 247.
```

recSVc  c4 r4 aes,4 r4   recSCb  c,4 r4 aes,,4 r4   recSClTwo  es2 aes2

```
The F2 melody and movement I's head are unchanged. The bass still hammers eighths.

### 5. Root-position step shifts in block brass chords make parallel fifths (36–37, 111–112, 165–166, 243–249). Applied.
Problem: when a root-position chord moves to another a step away, any voice on the fifth makes fifths with the bass (PDF 83). This happens at 36–37 (A♭→B♭ minor: Tbn 1 and Fl 2 E♭→F), at 111–112 (D♭→B♭→C: Tbn 2 has two successive fifths), at 165–166 (in the deceptive cadence Tbn 2 and Tpt 2 move D→E♭ with the bass, so VI doubles its fifth instead of its third) and at 243–249 (Tbn 1 and Tpt 3 at 243–244, Hn 4 at 245–246, and the tenor at 248–249).

### 6. The D♭ episode has trumpet 2 in fifths with Cl 2 and Hn 3 (211–212). Applied.

### 7. The German sixth's E♭→D makes fifths in the cello double stop and the low brass (1–2, 5–6). Partly applied.
Problem: every E♭ falls to D while A♭ falls to G. The worst cases are the cellos, whose double stop `<aes, es>`→`<g, d>` is a parallel fifth played by a single player, and Tbn 1 E♭→D against the bass trombone and tuba, ff in block chords. A&S PDF 506 says such fifths are "evident" in block chords, and PDF 526 calls them tolerable only away from the outer voices.
Fix (applied): `introTbnOne … es1-> | g4-> …` (both bars) and `introVc … <aes, es>1-> | g,4-> …` (both bars). This uses the leap from the fifth to the root of V (PDF 514). The German sixth A♭–C–E♭–F♯ and its resolution to V are unchanged, so the protected passage keeps its stated reason. I left the fifths between the upper winds, horns and violas. Those voices are not outer voices (PDF 526), and removing every E♭→D would leave V with no fifth in any voice.
