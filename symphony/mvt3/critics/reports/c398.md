## Findings

### 1. Bar 81: Hn 2's F3 sounds the tone of resolution against the 7/6 suspension G3 (applied)
- **Problem.** At 80.4–81.1 F2's 7/6 over V7 is suspended in three octaves: Vln I and Fl 1 G5, Vln II G4, and the cellos and Hn 1 G3 (Hn 1 on the cellos' line since c341, tied by c389). Hn 2 holds F3 through 80–81 on the same staff. So Hn 1 and the cellos hold G3 a major second above Hn 2's F3, in one register and, for the horns, one colour. On 81.2 they fall into unison with it.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 352 sec. 12 and Ex. 21-12a–c mark this layout "poor": the tone of resolution sounding before the suspension resolves "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The book allows it only for a 9–8 over the bass or with "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics" (Points for review 5, PDF 369). c241 tied the G on exactly that ground: "the resolution tone F4/F3 in harp and Hn 2 is an octave or more lower in another timbre". That held while only the violins and flute had the G. c341 and c389 put the same suspension at G3 beside Hn 2, so c241's reason no longer covers it.
- **Fix (applied in sections/sTwo.ily).** Hn 2 takes B♭2, the V7's root, for bar 81 and keeps p. It then rises to E♭3 at 82 as before.
  `sTwoHnTwo = { … | f2~ f2\> | bes,1\p | es2.\< bes,4 \breathe | }` (was `f1\p` at 81)
  The F is still heard on 81.1 in the harp's broken chord, which is different in timbre and rhythm, as c241 intended. Hn 2's motion F3–B♭2–E♭3 is oblique to the bass B♭1 and then makes an octave followed by a tenth with the bass rising to C2. par8.py on 78–84 gives the same output before and after (only the existing bass doublings). Written F3 for horn in F is in the low horn's sustained register (Adler part 1 PDF 303).

### 2. Bar 81: the double suspension in Ob 1 is shaped against the tune (applied)
- **Problem.** c389 added Ob 1's E♭5, tied into 81 and resolving to D5 on 81.2, a third under the tune's G5→F5. It is a double suspension in the manner of A&S Ex. 21-2. c248 had set the tune (Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1) to hold its level through the suspended G and start the diminuendo on the resolution F (81.2). Ob 1's hairpin, however, starts on the tied E♭ at 81.1. The two voices of one figure therefore relax at different moments, and the oboe's dissonance starts fading while the flute a third above it is still at full level.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 347: double suspensions "appear at the same time in two voices", typically in parallel 3rds or 6ths. PDF 348 sec. 4: "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer". PDF 348 sec. 3: the resolution is the relaxation. c248 applied this to the same bar.
- **Fix (applied).** `sTwoOboeOne … es''4~ | es''4 d''2\>) r4\! |` (was `es''4\> d''2)\! r4`). The level, the notes and the p at 82 are unchanged. The hairpin now runs under D5, as Fl 1's runs under F5.

### 3. Bars 25 and 165: F1's cadential 4–3 is not marked (not applied: protected)
- The C of `es''4( d''8 c''8~ c''4 b'4)` enters on 25.2.5 already dissonant over the G bass. It is a suspended passing tone (A&S PDF 358, Ex. 21-23b) that serves as the cadential V4–3 (PDF 350, Ex. 21-8). The book would mark its syncopated attack (PDF 348 sec. 4). A Romantic score would mark it `c''8->~`.
- I did not apply it. "F1 articulation 11–26, 151–166" is on the do-not-touch list (c058). At 25 the trombones' and bass trombone's ff > f already shapes the bar. At 165 the brass crescendo into the V–i6 at 166 is a push to the tonic, and a release on B would work against it. No change.

### 4. Bars 87–89: the inner 7–6 chain (checked, no change)
- Bn 1 (f) and the upper violas (mf, div.) play the same 7–6 chain at the unison: E♭4/F, D4/E♭, B♭3/C (A&S PDF 354–355, Ex. 21-16, 21-17; c062). PDF 348 sec. 4 would argue for bringing it forward by raising the violas to f. c058 and c254 kept the violas at mf "under the violins' and flute's figure". I checked that reason and it holds: Vln II's figure descends to E♭4–D4 on 87.4 and to B♭3 on 88.4, crossing the violas' line. At f the violas would cover it. The bassoon's f is about the strings' mf. No change.

### 5. Bar 121: Cl 1's D6 over the Italian sixth (considered, not applied)
- The pp D6 enters on 121.2 after a rest. It is an unprepared dissonance (an appoggiatura), not a suspension. Its resolution C♯ sounds two octaves lower in Vln II's pizz. on 121.3, which is the contrast of timbre and rhythm that PDF 352 permits. A swell on a solo pp line in Tempo I would add little, so I made no change.
