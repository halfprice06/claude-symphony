## Findings

### 1. 171–186: the pad under the recapitulated F2 uses three different timbres; give Bn 1's held line to the bass clarinet (applied)

**Problem.** At 171–186 the C.A. carries F2, doubled by the muted Tpt 1 in 171–174 and 179–186. Under it, two winds hold a pp pad:
- Cl 2: E♭3–G3, starting "dal niente".
- Bn 1: the inner C3 pedal with its B2/D3/D♭3 neighbours.

With the melody, this makes three-part wind harmony in three different colours: C.A., clarinet and bassoon. The bassoon is the C.A.'s own double-reed family, so the held bass of the pad and the moving melody share a colour. When the melody comes down to C4 and B3 at 185–186, it sounds in octaves over the bassoon's C3 and B2. Bn 1 also starts its pp pedal on the same E♭3 as Cl 2's "dal niente" entry, which the bassoon cannot match.

The exposition's statement (51–66) has the same pad scheme, Cl 1·2 + Bn 1 under the trumpet, so the recapitulation repeats its colour. Bn 1 then goes straight on into the counter-melody at 187–198, so it plays 28 bars with a single quarter rest at 182.4.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 92 (image): three-part chords "are generally given to two instruments of one timbre and a third instrument of another, but never to three different timbres".
- PDF 93–94: three of a family is satisfactory in wider spacing when "the third instrument is of low register (Bass Fl., Eng. horn, Bass cl., or C-fag.)".
- PDF 91 b: in a succession of chords, "one tone quality should be devoted to the stationary and another to the moving parts".
- PDF 53: simple combinations give "infinitely greater scope for variety", and wind tone soon becomes wearisome.
- Supporting: PDF 36 (the clarinet can reduce its tone "to a mere breath"), PDF 37 (give the winds a rest), PDF 38 (the bass clarinet is darker in its low register, which suits the C-minor recapitulation).

**Fix (applied; recS.ily).** I moved Bn 1's first 16 bars, note for note, into a new `recSBassCl`. The section file had no such variable, so `\sec` had been filling it with rests. Bn 1 rests those bars and enters as before at 187:

What changes:
- The pad is now Cl 2 + B.cl., one family, starting "dal niente" together on E♭3. From 181, Cl 1's C4/D♭4 makes it a three-clarinet chord with the low member at the bottom (PDF 94). The C.A. and muted trumpet stand apart from it in colour.
- The written B.cl. range is A3–E♭4, the bass clarinet's chalumeau, its smoothest pp register.
- Bn 1 gets 16 bars of rest before its p counter-melody with the cellos at 187.
- The recapitulation's pad now differs in colour from the exposition's (51–66, Cl + Bn), which stays as it is.

What does not change:
- All pitches, dynamics (pp), the breath at 178 and the harmony: c092's 185–186, c312's 178, c380's ♭II6 at 184, c413's 177–178 and c533's 176 are all kept.
- The C.A. melody, the muted trumpet, the viola eighths, the pizz. bass and the timpani at 178.

This answers the "pp accompaniment" protection of 171–186: the level and notes are kept and only the instrument changes. No quotation, no note of F1, F2 or theme B, and no chorale line is touched.

### 2. 173–179: the two sustained middle parts make open fourths and fifths (checked, kept)
PDF 97 remark 4 asks the sustained middle parts to move in 2nds, 7ths, 3rds and 6ths, and a woodwind basis to stand without the bass. Cl 2 over the held C3 gives F3/C3 at 173–175 and G3/C3 at 177.3 and 179. In every one of these bars the violas' slurred eighths supply the missing third: F–A♭ at 173–174, D–F at 175, G at 177 and E♭ at 179. The whole texture is therefore complete; only the wind pair taken alone is open. I tested alternatives on paper:
- D3 for C3 at 173 drops the seventh of iiø4/3.
- A♭3 in Cl 2 doubles the violas and loses c413's suspension at 177.
I kept the parts.
