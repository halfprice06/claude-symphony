## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Horns 1-2 hold 27-42 with no rest, straight after F1 at 11-26 (applied)
- Bars: 27-42 (pTwo.ily).
- Problem: Hn 1-2 play 16 bars of held whole and half notes (about 23 s at 2 = 84) with no rest. Before that they played F1 at 11-26, with only a quarter rest at 18.4 and a half rest at 26. Every other part (flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons, violins, violas, cellos, basses) rests on 34 beat 4. The horns are the only part that does not.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37: wind players "cannot manage extremely long sustained passages... care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time". PDF 44: this applies "with equal force to the brass".
- Fix: `pTwoHnOne` bar 34 `g'2. r4` (was `g'1`); `pTwoHnTwo` bar 34 `b2. r4` (was `b1`). The horns then breathe with the rest of the orchestra. The harmony (V of C minor) is complete on beats 1-3.

### 2. Bassoons and Hn 3-4 have no breath in the retransition pad, 135-146 (applied)
- Bars: 135-146 (retr.ily).
- Problem: Bn 1, Bn 2, Hn 3 and Hn 4 each hold re-attacked whole notes for 12 bars (about 17 s, pp < f, with the stopped sf at 138) before the breath at 146 (horns) or the staccato at 146 (bassoons). Bn 1 then goes on ff to 150.3, about 23 s in total. Bn 2 sits on its low B2, which needs a lot of air.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37 and 44, as in Finding 1.
- Fix: add `\breathe` at the end of 140 in `retrBsnOne` (`f1 \breathe | d'1\<`), `retrBsnTwo` (`b,1 \breathe | b,1\<`), `retrHnThree` (`d'1 \breathe | d'1\<`) and `retrHnFour` (`b1 \breathe | b1\<`). At 140-141 the cellos' F1 head, the violas' D4-F4 and the basses' held B1 carry the sound, and Hn 1 enters at 141 beat 2.

### 3. Horn 1 plays 191-204 without a rest, into the ff tutti (applied)
- Bars: 191-198 (recS.ily) into 199-204 (codaOne).
- Problem: after the breath at 190, Hn 1 plays the A♭ statement's support and crescendo (191-198), then the hammered ff tutti 199-204, with no rest: about 20 s ending ff. Bn 1 already rests on 198 beat 4.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 44 (breathing applies to the brass); PDF 41 (the brass swell well, so the crescendo survives a breath before the downbeat).
- Fix: `recSHnOne` last bar `es'1\f\< \breathe`; `recSHnTwo` last bar `aes1\f\< \breathe`, so the pair breathes together. The 199 layout is unchanged (protected).

### 4. Oboe 2 plays legato broken chords at 159-165: give them to Clarinet 1 (applied)
- Bars: 159-166 (recP.ily; score-only Oboe 2 staff).
- Problem: since c134, Ob 2 doubles Vln II's counter-line at pitch: slurred eighth-note broken chords (B4-G4-B4-D5, F5-D5-B4-G4...), ending at 165 with legato G4-D4-G4-D4, the oboe's low register under a ff tutti. At the same time Cl 1 holds plain whole and half notes (c137).
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 37 (c): "Arpeggios and rapid alternation of two intervals legato sound well on flutes and clarinets, but not on oboes and bassoons". PDF 36: "the flutes and clarinets excel in well-sustained legato phrases". Table B (PDF 34): the oboe's lowest register is "rough, thick".
- Fix (decided): swap the two roles. Cl 1 doubles Vln II at pitch: `es''8(^\markup \italic "espress." d'' c'' b') ...` through 165, then `c'2-> r2` at 166, at f (the same level as Cl 2 on the shared staff). Ob 2 (`recPOboeTwoDiv`) takes Cl 1's held notes `g'1\f | f'2( aes'2) | g'1 | f'1 | es'2( aes'2) | g'2( f'2) | d'1 | es'2-> r2`. The counter-line keeps a wind at pitch (Adler's string-plus-wind pairing, the reason for c134) and the harmony stays held (c137's reason). Cl 1 doubles Vln II note for note rather than adding a second figure in G4-C5, so c138's clarity point is not undone. The two clarinet voices do not cross. Ob 2 keeps its own score staff, so it does not cross Ob 1.

### 5. Trumpets and horns 3-4 share the off-beat chord at the same dynamic at 151 (applied)
- Bars: 151-166 (recP.ily).
- Problem: Tpt 1-3 and Hn 3-4 play the same off-beat staccato chord, all marked f. In forte, horns are about half as strong as trumpets, so the chord sounds as trumpets with a little horn. Tpt 1 then has four bars to fit a mute and play the soft solo at 171.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 41: horns in forte "are about one half as strong", so "the marks of expression in the horns should be one degree stronger than in the rest of the brass".
- Fix: `recPTptOne/Two/Three` first note `\mf` (were `\f`); Hn 3-4 stay f, and both stay under the ff theme. This keeps c164's principle that the accompaniment sits one level below the tune. The 151-158 notes and layout are unchanged (only the trumpets' dynamic changes).

### 6. Basses at written A♭4 by a skip at 33 (applied)
- Bars: 33 (pTwo.ily, the bass statement of F1 in tenor clef).
- Problem: the basses' octave of F1 climbs to written A♭4 (sounding A♭3) on beat 4 by a skip of a third, ff, after an eighth-note arpeggio. This is one semitone above Rimsky's practical limit for the bass, and it is the only note above it in 1-198.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 26 (image): the practical limit for double basses is G (written); "skips should always be avoided" above it. PDF 27: the bass "usually" moves in octaves with the cellos, so it can drop a note of the line.
- Fix: `pTwoCb` bar 33 `f,4( aes,8 c8 f4 f4)` (was `... f4 aes4`). The A♭ stays in the cellos, violas, bassoons and Hn 3-4, and 33→34 is now F→G by step at written F4-G4. F1 itself is not changed; only the basses' doubling is.

### 7. Violins I at B♭6 by a skip at 78: checked, no change
- Rimsky PDF 26 sets A6 as the practical violin limit and says skips to higher notes should be avoided. At 77.4→78 Vln I goes F6 (up-bow) → B♭6 (down-bow, dotted quarter, f). I keep it. It is F2's own contour (F→B♭ at the solo's peak, 61→62). It is a long value at the climax of a slow, flowing melody, which is one of the cases Rimsky allows. Fl 1 doubles it at pitch, which supports the intonation. The alternatives would either change F2 or take the top octave off the climax, and that would cost more than it gains.
