## Focus answers

**265–268, F1 a third higher at 267–268.** This is scored correctly. F1 has three carriers at C5/E5 (Tpt 1·2 a2, Cl 2) and five in the octave below (Hn 1·2, Bn 1, Vla, Vc), which is Mozart's weighting of the middle register (PDF 151). The counter, movement I's head rhythm, sits above in Fl a2, Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln I·II at G5–E6. The two lines are an octave and more apart and do not cross. At 267 beats 2–3 the counter's E6 and F1's E5 form an octave for a bar's half. This is a short merge on the chord tone. It does not need a change. The 267.4 C–B against B–C clash stays open as c122 decided.

**265–272, the f foundation.** For eight bars nothing is held: the Cb eighths and Tpt 3's repeated notes carry the harmony, and the tuba, Bn 2 and timpani strike once or twice a bar. Smetana's sustained brass (PDF 154) is the textbook way to hold such a tutti together. Here the bars between two held fff blocks (263–264 and 273–279) are the close's lightest texture, and the low brass entering at 273 after eight bars' rest is Barber's finality device (PDF 150). That entry is worth more than a held pad at 265. I decided not to change it.

**272, IV6/4.** The chord itself is well spaced: A6/A5 melody, A4 (Hn 3, Tpt 3, Vla), F4 (Hn 4, Bn 1, Vla), C in the bass. The timpani are wrong (Finding 2).

**187–198, cellos.** Balanced; no change. The tune is mf in two violin sections in unison. The counter is mf in the cellos alone (C3–C4, at its peak on the A string at 190). The tune's lower octave is mp in the violas with Hn 1 p, a fourth to a sixth above the counter and never crossing it. The bassoons (p) and basses (mp) are below it. The counter is a level below the tune in weight, one section against two, and a level above the inner octave. That is the foreground/middleground/background order Adler asks for (PDF 154). The horn and bassoons give the colour contrast with the strings. Cycle 121's mf is right. Going back to mp would bury the counter under the violas and the horn.

## Findings

### 1. 269–270: the trombones cover F1 while the trumpets rest (applied)

**Bars:** 269–271, Tbn 1·2.

**Problem:** At 269 F1 moves to Fl a2 and Vln I (C6) and Ob a2, Cl a2 and Vln II (C5). Tpt 1·2 rest until 271. Underneath, movement I's head enters ff in Hn 3·4 a2, Tbn 1 (G3–E4), Tbn 2 (G2–E3), Bn 1, violas and cellos. Its eighths D–C–B are played by four brass ff. With one trombone equal to two horns in forte (c064, c086), the counter has about six horn-units of brass against six woodwinds on F1. For two bars the counter becomes the foreground, in the movement's last statement of its own theme.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 147: d'Indy marks the trombones ppp in a unison tutti "so as not to dominate the entire orchestra". PDF 151: Mozart weights the octave that is meant to be heard as the theme. PDF 154: brass doubling the fast notes adds "too much weight to the eighth notes". PDF 150: Barber makes his crescendo by adding brass. The 269–271 crescendo (Tpt 3, tuba, timpani, since c123) is currently made by dynamics alone. Lowering the trombones to f at 269 would break Rimsky's rule that a brass re-entry after rests is pp or ff (PDF 136, c123's table item 21). Moving the entry does not.

**Fix:** Tbn 1·2 rest 269–270 and enter ff at 271 with Tpt 1·2. In finaleTbnOne, `R1*4 | r8\ff g8( e'4.->) d'8( c'8 b8 | c'4->) r4 r2 | r8 g8( e'4.->)` becomes `R1*4 | R1*2 | r8 g8(\ff e'4.->)`, and the same change goes in finaleTbnTwo (`r8\ff g,8( e4.->) … | r8 g,8( e4.->)` → `R1*2 | r8 g,8(\ff e4.->)`). At 269–270 the counter keeps Hn 3·4 ff, Bn 1, violas and cellos, the same weight F1 had below it at 265–268. At 271 both brass pairs enter together on the last bar of the crescendo, one pair on each line. The trombones also rest six bars instead of four before the Largamente.

### 2. 272: a timpani G struck under the IV6/4 (applied)

**Bars:** 272, timpani.

**Problem:** Since c122, 272 is IV6/4 over the C pedal from beat 2 (F–A over C). The timpani keep the pulse `c4 r8 g,8 c4 r4`, so a G2 is struck ff on beat 2.5, together with Tpt 3's A4, under F4, A4 and A6. It is the only foreign note in the bar, and it falls in the bass in the allargando bar, where every attack is heard.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 154: the struck and sustained notes under a tutti carry its harmony. In this score the pulse's G is right under I and V (265–271, 277), not under IV. Only 229 of the pulse is in the quotation record (timpaniPulse Timp 229). Elsewhere the pulse is a use, not a check.

**Fix:** In finaleTimp, 272 `c4-> r8 g,8 c4 r4` becomes `c4-> r8 c8 c4 r4`. The rhythm is kept. finale.md item 5 should say the pulse pattern is at 265–271 and 277, with C–C at 272.
