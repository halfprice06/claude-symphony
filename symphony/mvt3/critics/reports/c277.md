## Findings

### 1. 35–38: the countersubject loses its wind colour just as it moves into the thick middle register (applied)

**Problem.** At 27–34 the violins' eighth-note countersubject is doubled at pitch by Cl 1·2 (c217). At 35 the countersubject moves to the violas and cellos (in unison, E♭4–B♭4, then down to A♭3), while the violins and Fl 1 take F1's head in A♭ and B♭ minor. From here the clarinets hold chord tones. So the moving line now has strings only, and ten held wind and brass parts sit in its register: Fl 2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Bn 1, Hn 1–4 and Tbn 1·2, between A♭3 and C5. Bn 1's held notes there (A♭3 | G3–A♭3 | D♭4 | C4–B♭3–D4) are exactly Hn 4's notes in 35–37 and Hn 3's in 38, at the unison. Bn 1 adds no harmony.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 267–268, Ex. VIII-39 (Dvořák, *Carnival*), the "normal" tutti doubling: "the bassoons doubling the cellos at pitch plus an octave higher". In the example (PDF 268) the bassoons follow the cellos' line note for note in the tutti.
- PDF 255: a line that matters "must come through … and not be obscured by the harmony".
- PDF 257: a unison doubling "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". Bn 1 on Hn 4's note is that case.
- PDF 271: rhythmic counterpoint in a woodwind is effective because the wind has its own colour.
- Continuity: Bn 1 doubles the violas' F1 at 27–34 and their D4 at 39–41. It drops the violas only for these four bars.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/pTwo.ily, Bn 1 plays the violas' line at 35–38. The chord tones it held stay in Hn 3·4, Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 (c212's D♭ at 37 is still in Hn 4 and Ob 2):
```

```
The range is A♭3–B♭4, the bassoon's strong tenor register at ff. The shared staff prints Bn 1's eighths stems up over Bn 2's held bass. No new dynamic is needed, since both bassoons are ff from 27. par8 shows one fifth for Bn 1 at 36.1 (E♭4→B♭4 over A♭1→E♭2). The violas and cellos already have the same fifth, inside the broken-chord eighths, and it has been there since c007. Bn 1 adds no new pair.

### 2. 35–38: Hn 3·4 stay ff on the countersubject's pitches while the rest of the held brass has dropped (applied)

**Problem.** After F1's bass statement, Hn 3·4 keep the ff of 27 into the held chord at 35 (C4/A♭3). The trombones attack ff and fall to f within 35 (c208), Hn 1·2 are mf and the clarinets mf. That leaves the two lower horns as the loudest sustained sound, at the exact pitches the countersubject passes through.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 268 (Tchaikovsky 6/i, VIII-40): "the trick is to keep the volume of the brass down enough to let the strings and winds through". PDF 255, as in Finding 1.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/pTwo.ily, 35 `c'1\f` in Hn 3 and `aes1\f` in Hn 4. These are fresh attacks after the rest on 34.4, with equal dynamics on the shared staff. The diminuendo at 39 is unchanged.

### 3. 67–69: Bn 1·2 a2 in unison under F2 (checked, no change)

The two bassoons double the basses' E♭–D–E♭–C an octave up, at the unison, p. Adler PDF 257 says a unison only strengthens the pitch. But here the passage is soft and the pair blend into one soft colour, and they are the only wind sound until the horns enter at 75. That entry stays fresh, as PDF 260 (W. Schuman) asks. Moving Bn 1 onto the violas' and cellos' counter-melody would make the mp counter as strong as the mf tune. Kept.

### 4. 95–98: Copland's horn on the third (checked, not applicable)
