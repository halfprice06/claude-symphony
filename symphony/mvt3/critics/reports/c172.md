## Findings

### 1. 138–146: the stop chord's 9th leaps by augmented second to a doubled leading tone (applied)
**Problem.** At 138 Hn 4 holds the stop chord's A♭3, stopped, sf >. At 139 it goes up an augmented second to B3 and holds B3 to 146, over the B bass (Cb, Vc, Bn 2, and later Cbn and B.cl.). In the same bar the violas' A♭4 goes to D4 and Cl 1 rests. So none of the voices holding the 9th A♭ takes it to G, and for eight bars the horns double the bass's leading tone, which c052 had already taken out of 147–150. The G root of "the held G area" (retr.ily comment) only sounds in passing in the head entries.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 465–466 and Ex. 27-5 (image: Schubert, V9 then 8 over the same bass): "frequently, a 9th above V resolves to an octave within dominant harmony." PDF 479 point 4: "In principle, 9ths resolve down by step." PDF 470 sec. 8: B–D–F–A♭ is the top of V♭9. PDF 471 and Ex. 27-12: a composer may add V beneath the leading-tone seventh chord.
**Fix (retr.ily, `retrHnFour`).** In 139–146 replace `b1\p-\open | b1 \breathe | b1\< | b1 | b1 | b1 | b1\f | b1 \breathe` with `g1\p-\open | g1 \breathe | g1\< | g1 | d'1 | d'1 | d'1\f | d'1 \breathe`. Now A♭3→G3 resolves 9–8 in its own voice, the G area has its root from 139, and at 143 Hn 4 moves to D4, a third under Hn 3's F4. G3 under F4 would have been a seventh inside the pair, which c119 and c129 took out of the horns. Dynamics stay equal to Hn 3's. The bass keeps B to 146 and no voice reaches C, so the protected rule (the stop chord resolves to C only at 222→223) is untouched. This is the Hn 4 note under the stretto, not the stretto (139–150), which stays protected: no head entry, rhythm or dynamic changes.

### 2. 117: Vln II sounds the resolution G5 above the solo's appoggiatura A4 (applied)
**Problem.** At 117 the bassoon solo's A4 over E♭ is the accented dissonance of F2's third bar (c131, c171). On beat 2 Vln II plucks E♭5/G5, so the tone of resolution sounds a seventh above the dissonance at the moment it is held. c132 took the violas' G4 off this beat for the same reason. The G5 left in Vln II is above the solo, which is worse: in the exposition (53, 173) the resolution tone is below the tune.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 469 sec. 7 (referring to Unit 21 sec. 12): a suspension works best when its tone of resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass; otherwise "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution of the dissonance." PDF 470, Ex. 27-10 (image): only wide spacing between melody and accompaniment keeps such a sonority clear. Here the space is a seventh.
**Fix (dev.ily, `devVlnTwo`, 117).** `r4 <es'' g''>4 r4 <es'' g''>4` becomes `r4 es''4 r4 <es'' g''>4`. The full E♭ chord comes on beat 4, after A4–G4.

### 3. 114→115: the 7th of the D7 pivot rises (applied)
**Problem.** The pivot chord at 114 is D–A3–C5–F♯5 (Vc/Cb, Vla, Vln II, Vln I). Vln II's C5 is the 7th, and its next note is D5, on 115 beat 2. The resolution B♭ appears only in the violas' pizz (B♭4) and in the solo's B♭3 on beat 4.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 465: the 7th "promotes progression to a new chord — one that contains the tone of resolution". PDF 466: "unresolved 7ths occur most infrequently". PDF 479 point 4.
**Fix (dev.ily, `devVlnTwo`, 115).** The first pizz chord becomes `<bes' g''>4` (was `<d'' g''>4`), so C5 falls to B♭4 in its own part. The top G5 and beat 4 (`<d'' g''>`) stay. The fifth D is in the solo's D4. The pivot itself (113–114, protected since c132) is unchanged.
