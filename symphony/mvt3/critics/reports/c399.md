## Findings (ranked)

### F1. 135–138: the falling diminished-seventh chain into the stop chord changes loudness but not colour. APPLIED

- **Bars:** 135–138, all strings (retr.ily: `retrVlnOne`, `retrVlnTwo`, `retrVla`, `retrVc`, `retrCb`).
- **Problem:** The chain starts pp in the strings alone and grows into the sf stop chord, with Cl/Bn joining at 136 and Hn 3·4 at 137 (c308). Only the level rises. The score changes bow position only by switching from one note to the next (sul tasto at 43, 171, 183; ord. at 67, 181, 199). It never changes bow position gradually during a note.
- **Evidence:**
  - Casanelles PDF 276: the spectral envelope of a note changes with "the amount of pressure applied to the bow or its location".
  - PDF 272–273: a sound heard from far away has lost its upper partials first. Mahler uses that loss to mean distance.
  - The retransition's chain is the music's approach to the stop chord, the movement's turning point (138; resolved only at 222→223). Starting it veiled and bringing in the bite as the strings crescendo makes the approach audible in colour as well as level. The winds and horns that join at 136–137 blend with a flautando string sound.
  - The four whole notes give the bow time to travel. A crescendo on an up-bow already moves toward the bridge.
  - The bars are not on the current do-not-touch lists (c389, c398).
- **Fix (applied):** a text spanner "sul tasto - - - ord." in each string staff from 135 to the sf at 138.

```
retrTastoOrd = { \once \override TextSpanner.font-size = #-1 \once \override TextSpanner.outside-staff-padding = #0.25 \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.padding = #2.7 \once \override TextSpanner.outside-staff-horizontal-padding = #0 \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul tasto" \once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.right.text = \markup \italic "ord." }
retrVc = { \retrTastoOrd d1\pp\<\upbow\startTextSpan \tag #'part \noBreak | <des e>1^\markup \italic "div." | <c ges>1 | <b, aes>1\sf\>\stopTextSpan | ...
```

- **Variants:**
  - `retrTastoOrdND` ("ord., non div."): Vln I·II, replacing the separate "non div." at 138.
  - `retrTastoOrdDiv` ("div., sul tasto", left padding 3.6): violas, replacing the separate "div." at 135.
- **Layout:** With a stacked text line in every string staff, page 7 was compressed by 5.2 staff-spaces. The text now sits on the line of the up-bow signs, after them. Folding in the existing div. texts brings page 7 back to normal height without compression.
- **Parts:** `\tag #'part \noBreak` stops the cello part from breaking inside the spanner, where "sul tasto" had run past the right margin at the end of the line.
- **Unchanged:** notes, bowings, dynamics, slurs and MIDI (byte-identical). This undoes no earlier fix.

### F3. Beethoven 9/iv comparison: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Yes. No change

The two finales share three devices:
- a violent opening sonority answered by the strings: Beethoven 1–8 (fanfare, recitative); here 1–4 (German sixth ff, scale-figure answer);
- recalls of the earlier movements as structural events: Beethoven 30–64; here 9, 143–145, 178, 199–204, 211–218 and 275–276;
- a main tune first given in one plain colour and only later taken into the tutti: Beethoven 92–187; here 51 (solo trumpet over pizz.) → 67 (violins) → 187, and the chorale 224 → 255.

The difference is in the large plan:
- Beethoven builds by adding layers and register to one tune, from the basses upward, and ends with a Prestissimo that keeps accelerating.
- This finale builds toward a single withheld event (222→223, and the only root V–I at 262–263) and ends Largamente.

That plan is the brief's (Brahms 1/iv model) and is protected, so I do not re-raise it. Where the two can be compared directly, the handling is at the same level:
- the soft solo second theme (Casanelles row 2);
- the retransition's approach to the turning point, now also in colour (F1).

One page detail a conductor would notice is that the score carries many small performance texts ("change bow at will", stacked div. marks). F1 removes two stacked marks instead of adding to them.
