## Findings

### 1. Tested and rejected: Fl 1 an octave above the violins at 191–194
- Bars: 191–194, the second half of F2's A♭-major statement after Ob 1 leaves at 191.
- Idea: Adler PDF 265–266 (Berlioz, Ex. VIII-37): a flute on the violin line adds warmth. Here the violins play in unison from 191 to 194 with only violas and Hn 1 below.
- Test: in /tmp/critic-594/work/sections/recS.ily I replaced the flute's `R1*10` at 187–196 with `R1*4 | \transpose c aes, { f'''2.(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a'''8 b'''8 } | \transpose c aes, { a'''8 g'''8 f'''4 e'''4 d'''4 } | \transpose c aes, { e'''2 c'''4 d'''4~ } | \transpose c aes, { d'''1)\> } | R1*2\! |`. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 0 parallels, 16 pages, and par8 found nothing new at 190–195.
- Why it is worse:
  - Adler's warmth argument is for a rising line ("especially as the line goes up", PDF 266). At 191–194 the line falls from D♭5 to B♭4, with a diminuendo.
  - Adding the flute at D♭6–F6 puts the phrase's highest colour two bars after its peak, B♭5 at 189.
  - Adler PDF 238 (Ex. VIII-4/5, rejected for this reason): the extra octave would come before the registral expansion the passage is built toward. In the score that expansion happens at 197–198, where Fl 1 enters p< and is first heard on E♭6 at 198. That is Schubert's model of a flute entering almost unnoticed for the crescendo, and it would lose its effect.
- Reverted. The test PDF is kept at /tmp/critic-594/test-fl191.pdf.

### 2. Declined on paper: a single bassoon on F2 at 67–74 (Brahms 1/ii model)
- Adler PDF 267, Ex. VIII-38: one bassoon doubling the violins' theme in octaves gives warmth and clarity.
- At 67–74 the violins play F2 in octaves (B♭5/B♭4), and the nearest free register, an octave below Vln II, is E♭3–F4. The cello and viola counter-melody with Hn 1 sits at G3–G4. A bassoon there would cross or double that counter on almost every beat. In Brahms's example the bassoon's register holds no second line.
- The winds also rest at 67–76 on purpose: the clarinet rest was accepted early on, and Fl, Ob and Cl join in three octaves at 77. That is Adler's alternation of choirs (PDF 252–253) and the Schubert entry for the crescendo (PDF 238).
- No change.

### 3. Declined on paper: unison wind pairs on F1 (19–26 Ob a2; 151–158 Fl a2)
- Adler PDF 249–251 prefers octave coupling to like-instrument unisons.
- Both passages are f/ff tuttis, which is the exception Adler names ("not tutti sections where simple volume is required", PDF 250; "In full-orchestra tutti this practice is still common", PDF 257).
- The range also rules out the octave version. Ob 1 cannot take the top octave, which reaches A♭6 at 22 and at 157. Fl 2 has no higher octave to take, and its lower octave (C5) already carries four instruments at 151.
- The layout at 151 is Adler's "normal doubling" of Ex. VIII-39 (PDF 267): flutes an octave above violins that play in unison.
- No change.
