## Findings

### 1. 245–246: the stringendo has no beat in the bass (applied)

**Problem.** 245–246 are the movement's largest tempo change. The MIDI goes 2 = 66 → 68, 73, 78, 83 on each half bar, and 88 at R. But the cellos and basses hold whole notes from 244 to 246 (G at 244, then the V4/3 bass D at 245 and again at 246), with Bn 2 tied under them. Above them, the horn call is held in tied notes, Hn 3·4 and the woodwinds hold, the timpani roll, and the violins and violas run in eighths only from 245.3. So for three bars nothing in the low register marks a beat. The quickening is printed, but the orchestra plays no beats that show it. On the downbeat of 247 the basses jump from a whole note straight to eighths.

**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 273 (PDF 208): "In the third and fourth bars the cellos change from half note to quarter note values in order to give extra push into the next phrase". PDF 207: "keep something going … projecting enough character to carry one bar into the next". PDF 208 adds that a held-note texture needs relief "from 'goose-eggs' or 'footballs' (whole notes and half notes)". A&S PDF 49: tempo is heard in the pace of the beats. The score already does this at 259–262 (violas' quarters into 263), so the device belongs to its own idiom.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-358/work/sections/sHead.ily).** The cellos and basses restrike their bass D in tenuto halves at 245 and in quarters at 246. With the basses' eighths at 247 this gives whole, half, quarter, eighth: the stringendo is now written into the bass, and each MIDI tempo step falls on a bass stroke.

```
sHeadVc = { c1\p | c1 | d2 f,2 | g,1 | a,1\f | f,1 | d1 | d,1\> | e,2\mf\< g,2 | g,1 | d2-- d2-- | d4-- d4-- d4-- d4--\! | }
sHeadCb = { c,1\p~\cbaw | c,1~ | c,1~ | c,1 | a,,1\f | f,,1 | d,1 | d,1\> | e,2\mf\< g,2 | g,,1 | d,2-- d,2-- | d,4-- d,4-- d,4-- d,4--\! | }
```

**What stays the same.** Pitches and harmony stay as they are, and so does the 243 mf < ff hairpin. Bn 2 still holds D3, so the wind bass stays legato under the bowed strokes. The horn call (protected, c084), the violins' run and the timpani roll are untouched. The protected "III♯ approach 241–247" is protected for its harmony (I – III♯ – V), and the change restrikes the same bass notes without altering it. The cellos stay in their D3 register, below the horn call (Hn 1·2 E3–C5, Tpt 1·2 E4–C6), so the call is not covered. I used the device once only. Sebesky (PDF 208) warns that it "must not be overdone", which is the reason for the next two decisions.

### Checked and decided against (no change)

- **82 (animando, one bar).** The bass also holds a whole note here (Cb E♭, Vc G). But the push is already carried by the violins', flute's and oboe's run, and the closing group at 83 starts with the cellos' and basses' quarter-note arpeggio. Quarters at 82 would anticipate that and blunt its fresh start. No change.
- **209–210 (end of "poco a poco più animato").** The cellos hold A♭ while the basses already play eighths from 205. Cello quarters would only double a pulse that is already there, and 211 is a broadening (Largamente), not a faster phrase. No change.
- **51–66, Cl 1 E♭4/D4 under the trumpet solo.** This is Ex. 278's static background lead. But Sebesky himself excludes an attention-getting background behind a featured soloist (PDF 207), and the passage is protected. No change.
- **Marcato (^), Ex. 281.** The score uses > and sf throughout (Brahms's and Dvořák's practice). A second accent sign only in the coda would add a convention without a musical gain. No change.

No quotation, theme (F1, F2, chorale top lines), key, cadence or cyclic rule is changed. The scherzo's coherence file (v322) records no change to a theme the finale quotes.
