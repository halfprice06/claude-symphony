## Findings

### 1. Bars 1 and 5: the German sixth's F♯ has half a unit of brass against about seven units on the A♭ triad. Applied in scratch.
- **Problem.** At ff the brass hold Tuba A♭1, B.tbn A♭2, Tbn 2 C3, Tbn 1 E♭3, Hn 4 F♯3, Hn 3 C4, Hn 2 E♭4, Hn 1 A♭4. F♯ is the only tone that turns movement II's A♭ chord (here the scherzo's G♯ taken as A♭) into the augmented sixth, and in the brass it is one horn in its middle-low register. Across the whole orchestra A♭ sounds in 10 parts, C in 7, E♭ in 7 and F♯ in 4 (Fl 2, cor anglais, violas, Hn 4).
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 15: above mf one horn is half a trumpet or trombone. PDF 14: the brass easily overshadow the rest, so the brass block sounds as an A♭ major triad with a faint F♯. PDF 17: the trombones are the versatile brass. Ex. XI-1's ff chords give the characteristic tones trombone or doubled-horn weight.
- **Answer to c244.** c244 left the point because both horns on F♯ would move F♯→G in horn octaves, and because bars 1–2 hold link pitches. This fix touches neither: no horn changes, and Hn 1 A♭4, Hn 2 E♭4, Vln I A♭5, Vc E♭3 and the E♭4 answer are untouched.
- **Fix (applied).** In sections/intro.ily, Tbn 1 plays F♯3 instead of E♭3 in bars 1 and 5. It rises a step to its unchanged G3 in bars 2 and 6, where before it leapt E♭3→G3:
  `introTbnOne = { fis1->\ff | g4-> r4 r2 | R1*2 | fis1->\ff | g4-> r4 r2 | R1*4 | }`
  F♯ now has Tbn 1 plus Hn 4 in the brass (1.5 units). E♭ keeps Hn 2, Ob 1, Cl 2, Vln II, the violas and Vc E♭3, so the chord is still complete. Tbn 1 against the bass trombone and tuba is A♭–F♯ resolving outward to the octave G, which is the augmented sixth's own resolution. The engraved Tbn 1·2 staff shows F♯3 over C3, then G3 over B♮2. Only the brass weighting changes; the chord, its spelling, the dynamics and bars 2–10 are as before.
- **Check.** par8.py on bars 1–6 lists the same items before and after: the pre-existing German-sixth fifths in Ob 1, Cl 2, Hn 2 and the violas at 2.1 and 6.1, and orchestral octave doublings. Tbn 1 makes none. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 2. Bar 261: the same thin F♯ in the last cadence's German sixth. Not applied (protected).
At 261 the brass F♯ is a single F♯4 against A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–C4×2–E♭4–C5 (PDF 15). Every rescoring touches the protected last cadence (259–263: Tbn 2, Hn 3 D→E♭, Hn 4 A♭3). The woodwinds and strings at ff carry F♯ there, and the crescendo to fff makes the horn weaker in proportion than at bar 1. I record it and leave it.

### 3. Bars 2 and 6: the V chord's B is in the low register (Tbn 2 B2 over B.tbn G2). Tested on paper, not applied.
Ex. XI-1 keeps the lowest brass on root and fifth. Tbn 2 C3→D3 would give G1–G2–D3, and the voice leading is clean. However, C→B is the German sixth's own semitone, and bars 1–2 are the protected join. The stroke is a quarter note, so the gain is too small to justify the change.

### 4. Bars 231–234: antiphonal brass in the plagal close (Handel, PDF 17). Considered, not applied.
Splitting the four plagal chords between brass and woodwinds would follow the Handel model. The plagal close (old 227–230) is on the do-not-touch list, and the f > mp block chords there end the chorale stage cleanly.

### 5. Bars 247–254: F1's head in Tbn 1·2 a2 at f against ff elsewhere. Checked, no change.
By PDF 15 the balance holds: Tbn 1·2 a2 at f is two units, Bn 1·2 and the cellos double it, and the S head has Tpt 1 and Tpt 2 at f on separate notes plus the horns a2 at ff. A Tchaikovsky-style lower octave in the bass trombone (Ex. XI-21) would fall to G1–C2 and muddy the C pedal, so I did not apply it.
