## Findings

### 1. Bar 10: the dominant preparation's ♭9 should fall to the octave (applied)

**Problem.** At 10.1 the flutes and oboes enter on the V♭9 top that the staged crescendo (c279) builds to: Fl 1 F6, Fl 2 B5, Ob 1 A♭5, Ob 2 D5. Over the G pedal this is vii°7 over V, which is Ex. 27-12's sonority. On the f stroke at 10.3, Ob 1 leaps from the 9th A♭5 down to the 7th F5 and then rests. Neither note is resolved in its own line, and no other line takes A♭ to G at 10.3. Fl 1's 7th F6 also leaps to D6. So the last dominant before F1 leaves both of its dissonances in the oboe hanging. F1's first two bars then rest on 6̂–5̂ (A♭–G at 11.4–12).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 464: "the most characteristic function of 6̂ is to form an upper neighbor to 5̂; using V9 gives us a new way to harmonize 5̂–6̂–5̂."
- PDF 465–466, Ex. 27-5: "a 9th above V resolves to an octave within dominant harmony … think of the 9th as an ordinary neighbor or suspension." Schubert's 9ths fall 9–8 while V is still sounding, which is the case here because 10.3 is still V.
- PDF 466–467, Ex. 27-6b: a 9th may be followed by a passing octave that leads down to the 7th, but "the 7th … resolves in the soprano line". The oboe's A♭5–F5 does the first half and then stops. PDF 466 adds that unresolved 7ths "occur most infrequently".
- PDF 463: the 9ths "last for the full duration of dominant harmony and resolve, by stepwise descent".

**Fix (intro.ily, applied).**
- `introOboeOne` bar 10: `aes''2\mf\< f''4->\f r4` → `aes''2\mf\< g''4->\f r4`
- `introOboeTwo` bar 10: `d''2\mf\< d''4->\f r4` → `d''2\mf\< f''4->\f r4`

Ob 1 now sings A♭5–G5, a 9–8 within V. That is the 6̂–5̂ sigh F1's head takes up two bars later. Ob 2 takes the 7th F5, so the f stroke on 10.3 is still a complete V7 in the treble (Fl D6/B5, Ob G5/F5). D is still sounded by Fl 1, Vln I–II, Hn 2 and the violas.

Other parts, dynamics, the part-only hairpin override, the timpani motive x and c279's entry order are unchanged. 10→11 is still V → i6 over E♭, so no root-position V–i in C is added. Ob 2's D5→F5 makes a hidden octave with Bn 1's E♭4→F4 in the inner parts only; the outer voices are untouched. I tested Ob 1 alone first, with Ob 2 kept on D5. That build was also clean, but it left the 10.3 stroke as a bare G–B–D in the treble. I prefer the version with Ob 2 on F5.

**Result.** I checked the page-1 engraving at 150 dpi: the oboes print G5/F5 on one stem. build-warnings.txt is empty. Against v532, analysis.txt is unchanged apart from the density lines, par8.py on 9–11 is identical, and allpairs_par.py over the whole score is identical (16 949 lines).

### 2. Bars 7–8: II7 over the dominant pedal (Ex. 27-14, 27-21), considered and not applied

The intro's 7–8 has only the cellos' and Bn 1's F1 rhythm, the violas' answer and Bn 2's G2, all mf. Schumann's Op. 17 plan (a held II7 over V, then V7) would fill these bars. I decided against it, for three reasons:
- It would put held winds where c053, c279 and c439 deliberately keep two thin bars before the staged entries of 9–10.
- It would cover the low strings' anticipation of F1's rhythm.
- The movement already uses that plan, at 205–211 (c352 row 14).

### 3. 36.4: an applied V♭9 of B♭ minor, considered and not applied

Adding G♭ to the F7/A at 36.4 (Ex. 27-7, 27-9) would make A–C–E♭–G♭ over A. But F sounds at the same time in Tbn 1 F3, Ob 1 F5, Cl 1 F4 and Hn 1 F4. That is the inverted ninth sounding against its own resolution tone, which PDF 469–470 calls "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution".

### 4. 235.4: D♭ added for a V♭9 of IV over the C pedal (Ex. 27-13), considered and not applied

235 is already V7 of IV over the pedal: the counter's B♭4, with the tune's passing D5. A D♭ there would sound against the tune's D5 and the harp's C4 in its own octave, which is the Ex. 27-10 fault. 235–242 is also protected (c17, c020).

### 5. 133–134: an A♭ neighbour over the V4/2 (Ex. 27-4a), considered and not applied

It would bring in 135's A♭ two bars early. The change from G to A♭ at 135, where the diminished-seventh chain begins, is the point of 135–138 (c259).
