## Findings

### 1. Bar 229: the 9–8 over I has no 3rd, and Cl 2 and the violas move in octaves into it (applied)

- **Bars:** 228–230 (arrival, the bar between the chorale's phrases where the brass rest and the timpani play movement I's pulse).
- **Problem:** At 229 the canon (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I–II) holds D6 over the C pedal and resolves it to C6 on beat 3, with the A of 228 held in Fl 2 and Ob 2.
  - No part sounds E anywhere in the bar.
  - 229.1–2 sound C–G–A–D, and the resolution at 229.3 lands on a bare C–G–B♭ (no3.py lists 229.1, 229.2 and 229.3; there is no other missing-3rd chord in the score outside pedal points).
  - The bar sits between full C-major brass chords at 228 and 230, so the empty sound stands out. It is not a deliberate effect: c331's own note calls the bar "9-8 over I".
- **Second fault:** Cl 2 (A5 → G4) and the violas (A3 → G3) move in parallel octaves at 228→229. These are two independent inner lines, not one doubled line (A&S Ex. 5-18). allpairs_par.py flags them at quarter 912.
- **Evidence:**
  - A&S PDF 76 sec. 4: the 3rd is never omitted "except for special effects".
  - A&S Unit 21, PDF 351 sec. 10: the 9–8 "is always accompanied by a 3rd" (Ex. 21-10c has the 6th held, as here).
  - A&S PDF 83 sec. 13 (parallel octaves).
  - A&S PDF 81 sec. 11: after a leap, change direction.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-452/work/sections/arrival.ily):** violas at 229 take the 3rd E3 under the 9–8, then F3 for the B♭-minor half bar, slurred into their G3 at 230:

  ```
  arrivalVla … | a4--( a4-- a4-- a4--) | e2.( f4 | g1) | a1\f | …   % was: | g2 bes2 | g1 |
  ```

- **Result:**
  - 229.1–2 is 9/6/3 over C (C–E–G–A–D).
  - 229.3 is a complete C7 (C–E–G–B♭) under the resolved C6.
  - 229.4 keeps B♭ minor over C (F3 in the violas, F4 in Cl 2, B♭ in Fl 2 and Ob 2, D♭6 on top). Its E→F half step leads into the iv colour, and the C7 points toward the IV of the plagal close at 231.
  - The violas' line A3–E3–F3–G3 is a fourth down, then steps back up.
  - The octaves with Cl 2 are gone, because E3/G4 is a 10th.
  - B♭ is no longer tripled at 229.3; it stays in Fl 2 and Ob 2.
- **Unchanged:** canon, chorale, bass, timpani pulse (timpaniPulse record), Cl 2, all dynamics, and the stop-chord and arrival events at 222→223.
- **Checks:**
  - par8.py on 227–231 gives the same output as v452 (bass doublings only).
  - allpairs_par.py over the whole score differs from v452 only in the removed Cl 2/viola octaves.
  - analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels and is unchanged from v452.
  - Build: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt is empty); 16 pages.
  - I checked page 13 by eye: the dotted half and slur are clear, and the system is not crowded.
- **Earlier changes:** this does not undo c331. The held D6, the held A and Cl 2's G4 stay. It adds the chord member that c331's own source requires. The violas' old G3–B♭3 doubled Cl 2's G4 and Fl 2/Ob 2's B♭, so no chord tone is lost.

### 2. Bars 228→229: Cl 2 leaps a 9th (tested, not applied)

- **Problem:** Cl 2 goes from A5 (tied through 228, part of c044's octave-up canon support) to G4 at 229. A&S PDF 81 says leaps larger than an octave "are not permitted", and PDF 89 point 7 repeats it.
- **Tested in analysis:**
  - Cl 2 `g''2. f''4 | e''1` and Cl 2 `e''2. f''4 | e''1` both make parallel 5ths with Ob 2 at 229.4→230 (F5/B♭4 → E5/A4).
  - Cl 2 E4 at 229.1 makes an 11th leap from A5.
  - Every version that removes the 9th is worse.
- **Decision:** keep Cl 2. The leap is a return to the register after a tie, under a held canon note, and Finding 1 has already taken away its parallel octaves with the violas.

### 3. Bar 151: Tbn 2 C4–E♭3–D3, a 6th followed by a step in the same direction (tested, not applied)

- **Problem:** c433's trombone exchange (Tbn 1 E♭4–C4, Tbn 2 C4–E♭3) makes Tbn 2 fall a major 6th and then continue down by step to D3 at 152. This is the "poor" pattern of A&S Ex. 5-11b (PDF 81).
- **Alternatives tried:**
  - G3 at 151.3 makes two falling 4ths in a row, C4–G3–D3 (Ex. 5-12 "poor"). It also loses the E♭–D 6–5 that the trombones carry at 151–152.
  - E♭4 at 151.3 crosses above Tbn 1 for one and a half bars (Ex. 5-30).
- **Decision:** keep. The line resolves the long V7's 7th at 151 (c433), and the 6–5 needs the E♭.

### 4. Bar 73.3: hidden 5th in the outer voices, with both voices leaping (no change)

- **Problem:** Over IV in E♭ (c273's IV – V6/5 of V – V), F2 falls G5→E♭5 while the bass falls E♭2→A♭1. Similar motion arrives at a 5th, and both voices leap. Under A&S PDF 87 points 5 and 7 this is the most noticeable kind of hidden 5th. The same pattern occurs at 11.3 (F1's head), 58.1 and 65.1.
- **Decision:** no change. A&S points 2 and 6 apply: the harp's eighths and the cello and viola counter-melody fill the texture, and E♭ is a common tone held in the harp. The tune and the A♭–A♮–B♭ bass are settled (c273), and A&S rates hidden 5ths "far less drastic" than parallels (PDF 87). Hidden octaves in the outer voices, which A&S treats as the stricter case, all have a stepwise top voice (table, item 25).
