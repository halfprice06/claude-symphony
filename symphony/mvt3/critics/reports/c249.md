- the Poco animato puts arpeggiated strings under held wind notes;
- the Molto maestoso has timpani rolls, string tremolo and fz chords;
- cross-accents push against the 2/2 throughout.

The finale does the same things:

- 255-262 has the arpeggiated basis;
- 273-279 has the held, rolled and tremolo final block;
- 93 has the c248 syncopated I chord across the half-bar.

A conductor would accept 255-279 next to Dvořák. The one place where Dvořák is more generous is the sweep into the last chord. That is my finding.

### 1. 277-278: the harp glissando is too short to do its job (applied)

**Problem.** The harp has one entry in the movement: a glissando from C4 on beat 4 of 277 to G6, landing on the final chord at 278. It lasts one quarter note at Largamente (half note = 72), about 0.4 s. A pedal glissando over a whole fff tutti that short is heard as a click, not a sweep. c204 put it in the close's only quarter rest so that it would be heard by itself. But the orchestra is silent on beat 4 anyway, so the rest gives the sweep space rather than needing to be the whole of its length.

**Evidence.**

- Rimsky, ch. IV, PDF 134: long glissandi on the harp are more resonant and brilliant than short ones, and the glissando's notes need not agree with the orchestra's harmony, because it is heard as a sweep, not as a chord.
- Ex. 275 (Pan Voyevoda, PDF 441-442): each glissando lasts half a bar. It runs under the sounding orchestra, not in a gap, and it carries a crescendo into the arrival.
- Dvořák 7/iv's final block uses rushes of sound to push into the last chord. Rimsky (PDF 141; Ex. 280-281) names a rush of strings or a brass entry as the support for a loud climax. The strings cannot take that role here: their figure at 277 is fixed, and 273-279 is protected except for the harp glissando.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-249/work/sections/finale.ily).** The glissando now starts on beat 3 with the orchestra's chord, from G3 as a half note, three octaves to G6. It is marked f < into ff on 278. The top note, the pedal setting (C D E F G A B), the landing chord <c, g, c>, the laissez-vibrer and every other part are unchanged.

```lilypond
% Harp RH, 277-278
r2 g2-\tag #'part ^\markup \small "gliss. on C D E F G A B"\glissando | g'''4\laissezVibrer
% Harp LH, 277-278 (the LH dynamics drive both hands in MIDI)
r2 s2\f\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"---|----" | <c, g, c>4\ff\laissezVibrer
```

The sweep overlaps the orchestra's beat-3 chord and its eighth note. Rimsky says the glissando's notes need not match the harmony, and here they do match anyway, because the white-note C-major setting fits the C-major chord. It then fills the beat-4 rest and peaks on the downbeat of 278, as before. The engraving on page 16 is clean: one gliss line from G3 to G6 with the pedal diagram unchanged.

Priority: low. It improves the final arrival, and nothing else depends on it.

### Considered and kept (not findings)

I checked three places against PDF 136-143 and left them as they are:

- **Tpt 3 f at 247.** Rimsky's rule (PDF 136) is that brass should enter at pp or ff. But Tpt 3 doubles horns there, and Rimsky's own balance table (PDF 41: one trumpet weighs about two horns) argues for f rather than ff. The Tbn f at the same bar is protected (c185).
- **Bn a2 unison at 67-69, 73 and 76.** PDF 139 warns against unison pairs under a soft line. This passage falls under the protected "67-82 balance". In each bar the line is the bass of the texture, not an accompaniment to a soft line, so the warning does not apply.
- **Timpani roll with Cb pedal at 235-238.** PDF 143 allows a kettle-drum tremolo to accompany, and it sits two octaves under the solo.
