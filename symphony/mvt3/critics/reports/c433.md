## Findings

### 1. 147–151: the retransition's dominant seventh does not resolve into the recapitulation (applied in the scratch copy)

**Bars.** 147–150 (V7 over G, ff, sfp < at 149), going into 151 (recapitulation, i6/4 over the same G).

**Problem.** The movement expands V7 of C from 133 (V4/2, F in the bass) through the diminished-seventh chain, the stop chord and the stretto over B♮, up to root-position V7 at 147–150. At 150 its 7th, F, is held in Tbn 1 (F4), Bn 1 (F3) and the cellos' upper note (F3), as well as in Vln I (F5), which then has to take F1's head. At the downbeat of 151 every one of them leaves the F by leap: Tbn 1 F4→C4, Bn 1 F3→G2, Vc F3→G2, Vln I F5→C5. The only E♭s at 151 are Cl 1's leggiero eighth (E♭4, f) and Tbn 2, which arrives at E♭3 by a falling major seventh from D4. So the movement's longest dominant (18 bars) loses its 7th at the most important joint of the sonata form. The 6/4 that c043 and c282 built (C4 as the 4th, E♭3 as the 6th) also has its 6th only in an inner voice that gets there by leap.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 98: "the 7th, which represents a descending passing tone, must therefore resolve". PDF 100: "4, which forms a dissonance with the bass, must move down by step to 3". PDF 102, Ex. 6-19: when V7 is expanded and the 7th passes from voice to voice, "the 7th will resolve in the last voice in which it occurs". PDF 117, sec. 6 says the same for expansions through inversions, which is what 133–150 is. PDF 121, Ex. 8-14 (Jupiter) and PDF 94 (Kuhnau bar 28) back the plan of a tonic that comes back inside a continuing dominant, and that is why the voices should resolve to the 6/4 and not jump past it. The Dvořák 9/iv joint above uses the same 6/4 plan.
