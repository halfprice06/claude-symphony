## Findings

### 1. Trumpets leave the S-head phrase in its middle (251-254). Applied.
Problem: Tpt 1·2 start the second S-head phrase at 251 and stop after 252 (c076 rest 253-254), so the octave-doubled lead drops out halfway through a four-bar phrase that the horns, oboes and clarinets continue.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 42-43: once a lead line is doubled "you shouldn't noticeably interrupt or terminate the line unless the character or sound of the music changes"; PDF 51: dropping a doubling is unnoticeable only "because this beat represents the dividing line between two phrases". PDF 44: with several trumpets, rest the lead player. c076's reason (a rest before the chorale at 255) is kept and doubled.
Fix (finale.ily, Tpt 1 and Tpt 2): `c'''2 b''2) | R1*4 | g'1\ff~` (Tpt 2 `e''2 d''2) | R1*4 | g'1\ff~`). Ob 1 (E5 line, at Tpt 1's pitch), Cl 1 (an octave higher) and Hn 1·2 ff carry 251-254. The breath marks after 250 go, since a rest follows. finale.md item 9 must change "Tpt 1·2 rest at 253-254" to "rest at 251-254". The sHeadBrass check (Tpt 1, 247-250) is unaffected.

### 2. Both oboes leap G5-E6 in unison at ff (265, 267). Applied.
Problem: at 265-268 Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 and Vln I·II all play movement I's head on one pitch, G5-E6. Ob 2 makes a sixth leap to E6 ff, twice, in unison with Ob 1.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 71: above the high D the oboe "is also hard to control, and it is best not to write too long or too difficult a passage up there, particularly one which includes skips"; "I don't recommend voicing oboes in unison unless ... an especially harsh effect is required". PDF 80: unisons carry more chance of intonation conflict than octaves. The line has seven other instruments on it, so Ob 2 adds risk and no weight.
Fix: finaleOboeTwo `e'2. r4 | R1*4 | c''2->(\ff g'4. a'8 | ...` (rest 265-268, ff restated at 269). Ob 1 keeps the head.

### 3. Hn 1 doubles two trumpets at the unison on written G5 ff (265-268). Applied.
Problem: after playing every bar from 243 at ff-fff, Hn 1 plays F1 at C5-E4 (written G5) ff in unison with Tpt 1·2 and the violas. It is the most tiring spot in Hn 1's part and contributes little.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 32, footnote 1: "A trumpet or trombone playing mf or louder has approximately twice the power of a French horn"; PDF 46: compensate by putting two horns on a line; PDF 33: horns in unison are strong.
Fix: finaleHnOne 265-268 `c'2->(\ff g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. | c'2->( g4. a8 | bes4-.) a8( g8 f4-.) e4-. |`, in unison with Hn 2 and the cellos (the shared staff prints a2). It also follows c069's reason (no loud high horn unison). finale.md "Ending" should read "Hn 1·2 an octave lower with the cellos".

### 4. Horns 3·4 have an eighth-note pickup at ff (269, 271). Applied.
Problem: Hn 3·4 begin movement I's head with `r8 g8( e'4.->)` at 2 = 88: a low G3 eighth (0.17 s) into an accented E4. Tbn 1·2, Bn 1, violas and cellos play the same pickup.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 46: in Ex. 51 the quick notes "are omitted, because of the tendency of the horns to 'speak' a little late. By omitting these notes, the horns have a better opportunity to land squarely"; the omission "will not be noticed" when the rest of the brass have it. PDF 32: the late speech is worst "in highly syncopated or staccato passages".
Fix: finaleHnThree and finaleHnFour `r4\ff e'4.-> d'8( c'8 b8 | c'4->) r4 r2 | r4 e'4.-> d'8( c'8 b8 | c'4->) r4 r2 |`.

### 5. Final cadence: one horn per note, and the high C5 is given to the tired horn (277-279). Applied.
Problem: at 277-279 the horns are C5 (Hn 1), G4 (Hn 2), E4 (Hn 3), C4 (Hn 4), one per line at fff against three trumpets and three trombones. The top C5 (written G5) goes to Hn 1, which has played almost without a break since 243, while Hn 3 has already been holding that C5 at 273-275.
Evidence: Sebesky PDF 46, the climactic cadence of Ex. 51: "we give them two big, beautiful unisons, especially strong in the top two horns on the high D", to compensate for horns being half as strong as trumpets and trombones; the minor sixth inside the horn section "is very rich".
Fix: finaleHnThree `c''4-> r8 d''8 c''4-> r4 | c''1->\fff~ | c''4 r4 r2\fermata`, finaleHnTwo and finaleHnFour `e'4-> r8 d'8 e'4-> r4 | e'1->\fff~ | e'4 r4 r2\fermata` (Hn 1 unchanged). Voice leading from 276 is smoother (Hn 3 G4→C5, Hn 2 and Hn 4 stay on E4). This changes the close 273-279 that c049 marked do-not-touch.
