## Findings

### 1. 255-256: four horns and a trombone on one G3, the first note of F2's phrase, while the I6's root has one low trumpet (applied)

**Problem.** The last chorale statement opens at 255 on I6, f, with the woodwinds resting (protected). The brass are: Tuba E2, bass trombone E3, Tbn 2 E3, Tbn 1 G3, Hn 1 G3, Hn 2 G3, Hn 3·4 a2 G3 (F2's phrase, ff, with violas and cellos), Tpt 3 C4, Tpt 1·2 G4. So G3 has Hn 1-4 and Tbn 1, three units by Adler's measure, and the chord's root C has only Tpt 3 on C4, a trumpet in its low register. Nothing in the brass sits between G3 and G4 except that low C4. At 256 (iii) Hn 1 still holds G3 with Tbn 1.

Two consequences. The first note of F2's returning phrase (G3, a dotted half) begins inside a held unison of two more horns and a trombone, so it is not heard as an entry; c317 put the phrase there and c321 raised it to ff and lowered the held brass to f because it was "covered by held brass ff on its own pitches", but the pitches were not moved. And the brass chord is hollow in the middle exactly where the woodwinds are silent.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 15: above mf two horns equal one trumpet or one trombone; each brass may have its own pitch only with good "register placement", which "does not hold true if the trumpet is in a low register".
- Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15-16): in every f and ff layout the horns fill the middle of the chord in pairs or double the trumpets; none puts four horns on one pitch with a trombone while another chord tone rests on one low trumpet.
- The score's own history: c084 moved Hn 2 to B3 at 256 "against four brass on G3"; c319 found the same masking at 257 (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 81, 91) and left 255-256 only because it read them as protected chorale voicing.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/finale.ily, Hn 1 holds E4 and Hn 2 takes C4 at 255; Hn 2 steps to B3 at 256 as before; Hn 1 re-attacks E4 at 257 with its crescendo, joining the chorale's lower octave there:

```
finaleHnOne: \partCombineApart \tag #'score { e'2\f~ e'2~ | e'2~ e'2 } \tag #'part { e'1\f~ | e'1 } \partCombineAutomatic | e'1~\< | ...
finaleHnTwo: \partCombineApart \tag #'score { c'2\f~ c'2 | b2~ b2 } \tag #'part { c'1\f | b1 } \partCombineAutomatic | e'1~\< | ...
```

They were `g2\f~ g2~ | g2~ g2` (Hn 1) and `g2\f~ g2 | b2~ b2` (Hn 2).

**Result.** At 255 the brass weights become C 1.5 (Tpt 3 + Hn 2), E 0.5 above the bass, G 4 (Tpt 1·2, Tbn 1, Hn 3·4); F2's G3 shares its pitch with one held trombone instead of two horns and a trombone. Voice leading: Hn 2 holds C4 from 254 (common tone) and steps C4-B3; Hn 1 goes C4 (254) up a third to E4 and keeps it into 257. The chorale top line (Tpt 1·2 G4), Tbn 1's G3 (its lower octave at 255-256), the progression I6-iii-vi, F2's phrase, the bass, all dynamics and the woodwind rest are unchanged.

**Answer to the protection.** The protected items at 255-264 are the chorale top lines, the progression I6-iii-vi-iiø6/5-Ger6-V7-I, the cut at 264.1, the woodwind rest at 255-256 and the cadence 259-263 with Tpt 1 B→C. None changes; only two inner horn notes move, as c084 already did at 256.

### Checked against the reading and left unchanged

- **262, V7 of the last cadence without D in the brass (Ex. XI-1).** The brass have G and B, plus Hn 3's F4 from beat 3 (c084). Every way to add D (Hn 3 or Tbn 2 E♭→D) makes the Mozart fifths A♭-E♭ → G-D with Hn 4 or the bass trombone; the leaps E♭→G are what avoid them. D is in the woodwinds and strings. Protected cadence; kept.
- **159-166, eleven brass under the ff consequent, trumpets low at mf (PDF 14, 15 "(weaker)", 17).** The trumpets' off-beats (Tpt 3 B♭3-C4) only thicken Hn 3·4's chord. "Trumpets mf at 159" is protected (c170) as the recapitulation's intensification; the strings carry the tune at ff above. Not re-raised.
- **Schubert's soft trombone theme at a seam (PDF 37).** Still unused. The seams where it would fit are all taken: 83-86 and 95-98 (protected; 95-98 carries movement II's last tail in Ob 1), 143 (pp trombone re-entry under the protected stretto), 167-170 (stopped horns before the muted trumpet), 219-222 (the dark low-brass chord). Adding it elsewhere would mean a new soft brass theme in the coda, rejected in c229.
- **Handel's string-free brass choir (PDF 17-18).** The strings play almost every beat of the movement; the only string-free beats are wind answers at 101-102 and 105-106 and cut-offs. The places where a brass-and-timpani choir alone would fit (205-206, 224-228, 231-234) are protected; 205-206 is already brass with only the cellos' pedal and is answered by winds and violins, which is Handel's antiphony.
- **Hindemith's punctuation of a timpani theme (PDF 42-44).** At 229 the brass rest under the timpani's movement-I pulse; movement I's 179-180 has no brass punctuation there either, so the quotation stays as it is.
- **Druckman's marking (PDF 50, 53-54).** All mute and stopped/open changes are marked, including "open" at the re-entries after stopped notes (67, 187).
