## Findings

### 1. Bar 254: the last ♭VI goes straight to V7. Make it the German sixth of bar 1 (applied)

**Problem.** The movement opens with movement II's last chord turned into a German sixth, A♭–C–E♭–F♯, which goes to V (1–2, 5–6) and never reaches I. At the last cadence (254) the same bass A♭ carries a plain A♭ triad, ♭VI, which goes to V7 on beat 3 and then to I. The symphony's last cadence therefore does not complete the progression that opened the finale. cyclic-notes ("Final approach") asks for "an enlarged version of movement I's ♭VI → German sixth → V (bars 10–13)", and that step is missing here.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 593: "The usual goal of ♭VI is a cadential V … A most natural way of leading ♭VI to V is to transform it into an augmented 6th chord." PDF 600–601: the large-scale plan should project the piece's characteristic relationships. Here that relationship is A♭→G with the F♯, first heard at the join to movement II. PDF 589–591: the German sixth and V7 are the same sonority, and the coda already uses this (A♭7 as V7 of D♭ at 210, as Ger6 of C at 215). The last cadence is the place for the chord to reach the tonic.

**Fix (applied).** Keep the bass, the top line `c'2 b2 | c'1`, the A♭→F seventh in Fl 2, Ob 2 and Vln II, and the E♭ in Cl 2, Hn 3 and Tbn 2. Raise E♭ to F♯ in two voices that go to G anyway:
- finale.ily `finaleTptThree`, bar 254: `es'2 g'2` → `fis'2 g'2`
- finale.ily `finaleVla`, bar 254: `es'2 g'2` → `fis'2 g'2`

Bar 254 now reads Ger6 (A♭–C–E♭–F♯) → V7 → I (255). The trumpets, tacet in bar 1, now play the German sixth themselves. par.py shows no new parallels. The change removes four pairs of octaves (Tbn 2 against Tpt 3 and Vla, E♭3/E♭4 → G3/G4). This revises cycle 2's accepted "254 cadence voice leading" in two inner voices only. The bass, the top line and the seventh that cycle 2 set are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 215: the German sixth is spelled two ways, so the E♭→D♯→E thread is unclear (applied)

**Problem.** At 215 the violas have D♯4 (`<c' dis'>`), but Ob 1 (`es''`) and Hn 3 (`es'`) have E♭. All three rise to E at 216. The chord is spelled both ways, and the most exposed voice of the thread (Ob 1, E5, under only the flutes) is written as E♭→E♮. That is an augmented unison, not the D♯→E leading-tone step that closes the thread.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 588: a true enharmonic "would be heard even if [it] were not expressed in the notation". Here it is heard, because the note rises to E over the cadential 6/4, so the notation should say D♯. PDF 597–598 (Haydn, Ex. 32-17): the remote area is integrated by a top-voice pitch. E♭ is heard first, then becomes D♯ in the augmented sixth, and D♯ rises to E. Trumpet 1's E♭5 at 213 → Ob 1 D♯5 (215) → E5 (216) is that line at one pitch. c110 (movement II) asked for D♯ on top at this chord. The top voice here is the protected D♭–C–B–C line (Fl 1, Vln I, Tpt 1), so I decided against moving D♯ to the top. Ob 1 is the highest voice that can carry it.

**Fix (applied).** codaTwo.ily bar 215: `codaTwoOboeOne` `es''1\subP\<` → `dis''1\subP\<`; `codaTwoHnThree` `es'1\subP` → `dis'1\subP`. The violas still close the thread (owed item 9), now doubled by the oboe an octave above.

### 3. Bar 210: the cymbal crash into the D♭ episode marks D♭ as a goal (applied)

**Problem.** The movement's first cymbal crash (ff, 210 beat 4) falls on the upbeat to D♭ at 211. The next one is the arrival at 219. The colour that marks the C-major arrival is used nine bars earlier on ♭II, so the arrival does not bring a new sound.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 589: the transitional E♭ in K. 421 is "a stop on the way from F major to A minor". PDF 593: Widmung's ♭VI moves to V7 through ♭II. The D♭ episode has this role: ♭II between the ♭VI pedal (205–210) and V (216–218), with D♭–C as the motivic link (PDF 600). Its orchestration should not give it the weight of the goal. cyclic-notes keep the arrival as the first sustained forte major chord in full brass. The timpani roll and the ff tutti already mark 211.

**Fix (applied).** codaOne.ily: `codaOnePerc = \drummode { R1*11 | r2 r4 cymc4->\ff | }` → `codaOnePerc = \drummode { R1*12 | }`. In movement III the first crash is now the arrival at 219.

### 4. Bar 138: the stop chord has F on top, not movement I's B (applied)

**Problem.** In movement I the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ has B5 on top (26, 138). In movement II, c111 put B4 on top at 33 so that the recall is heard. In movement III at 138 the top note is Vln I F5, the end of the chromatic line A♭–G–G♭–F, and the B is inside, in Vln II. At 218 the chord again has B on top (Fl 1 B5, Tpt 1 B4), so only one of III's two statements sounds like movement I's chord. The unresolved top B at 138 should point to the top B that resolves at 218→219.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 597–598: a recurring element is held to the whole "because of a most subtle connection … among the main pitches of the uppermost voice". PDF 601–602 (K. 542): the high points form a line that is the motive enlarged. The B at 138 and 218 is one pitch that waits for its C. The oboe B5 doubles Vln II's B4 an octave up and leaves the violins' chromatic line unchanged.

**Fix (applied).** retr.ily: `retrOboeOne = { R1*4 | R1*2 | …` → `retrOboeOne = { R1*3 | b''1\sf | R1*2 | …`. The sf matches the strings' sf at 138, Ob 2 rests (no shared-dynamic issue), and Ob 1's entry at 141 is unchanged.
