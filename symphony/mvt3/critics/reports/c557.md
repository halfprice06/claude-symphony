## Findings

### 1. 207–208: cellos leave the A♭ pedal for a rising line under the winds' answer (tested, not applied)

Bars: 207–208 (codaOne). Problem examined: F1's head is stated low at 205–206 (Hn 1–4, Tbn 1·2, Bn 2 and, since c521, the cellos). The winds and Vln I answer at 207–208 in B♭ minor over the A♭ pedal (ii7, c302). The cellos then fall from the head's last C3 back to the pedal, `aes,1 | aes,1`, where five other parts already hold it (basses, bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon, Bn 2). They rise again with the run at 209. Sebesky's economy (PDF 12) and Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13: contrary motion in the background is "more interesting") suggested a cello line rising against the falling answer. The line would continue the head's last notes (E♭–D♭–C) in inversion and lead into the run.

Test: in /tmp/critic-557/work, `codaOneVc` 207–208 became `des1\mf | es1` (C3 at 206.4, then D♭3, E♭3, then the run from A♭2 at 209). Build result: 0 warnings, 16 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. The all-pairs parallel listing (tools/critic-scripts/allpairs_par.py) was identical to v557's, and par8.py on 205–211 reports nothing, the same as v557 (the bass is the static pedal). Theme record 51/51. The page engraved cleanly.

Verdict, not applied. The result is not better, for three reasons I checked against alternatives:
- At 207 the D♭ is the seventh of ii7. The cellos double Tbn 1's D♭4, and their D♭3 then rises to E♭3, while Tbn 1 resolves it to C4 at 209 (c302). The score avoids doubled and rising sevenths throughout (c129, c147, c412). Starting on E♭3 avoids this, but `es1 | ges1` and `es1 | es2 ges2` give no more than a rising third.
- The only free register for a moving cello line is D♭3–B♭3, the octave in which Bn 1 alone carries the head's lowest copy (c387; Bn 1 takes it from Bn 2 at 207). A mf cello line there crosses and covers that bassoon (at 208.1 G♭3 against A♭3, and above it from 208.3). Sebesky's focus rule (PDF 13) forbids this, and c158 applied the same rule (with ch. 6, PDF 211) to clear this very register at 207–208.
- In the first version D♭3 sits a second below Tbn 2's E♭3 in the low middle register. Different timbres make this tolerable, but it thickens the chord and adds nothing to the dialogue, which the winds and Vln II's run already carry (c107).

The scratch copy is restored to v557: sections/ is identical to v557, and the test build is kept as /tmp/critic-557/test-score.pdf.

### 2. 151–158: the 27–34 countersubject inverted under F1 in the recapitulation (declined on paper)

Bars: 151–158. F1 sounds in four octaves: Fl 1·2; Vln I·II, Ob 1 and cor anglais at C5; Hn 1·2 and violas at C4. Below that the only material is the clarinets' broken chords and the off-beat chords. A master's recapitulation might bring back the countersubject that ran above F1-in-the-bass at 27–34, now below the tune in the violas, which Sebesky's principle 1 (PDF 11: reuse a motive before inventing one) would support. Declined:
- The countersubject was written for the harmony that F1 makes as a bass. At 152 its B♭–D–F–A♭ runs against the iv/ii° colour over the G pedal (Cl 1 F4–A♭4, Cl 2 C4), so D clashes with C. It would need rewriting bar by bar, and then it is no longer the countersubject.
- Sebesky PDF 13 warns against an "overbusy background or counter-line". The clarinets' eighth-note broken chords already occupy C4–C5, and a second eighth-note line in the same octave would blur both.
- The recapitulation's plan is growth by addition at 159 (c169), with the cor anglais inner line at 159–166 (c446, c536). That plan is already in place.

### 5. Economy checks, no change

- **41–42 Tbn 1 on F1's last fragment with the sul G cellos.** It doubles the cellos at pitch, p. This is c431's deliberate Rosamunde-type scoring (Adler part 2 PDF 28–29): one trombone stays with the lower strings as the others drop out. The trombone and the sul G string colour are different timbres, so the doubling adds colour rather than mass. Removing it would undo an accepted change without a reason Sebesky adds. Kept.
- **9–10 violas' held D4 with Hn 2.** It is the string body under the staged crescendo (c279). A moving viola line would clash with Hn 3's held B3 (C4 against B3) or move in parallel with the cellos' rise. Kept.
- **205–206 four horns a2 on A♭4 over Tbn 1·2.** This is Rimsky's 4-over-2 layout (c381), and every line has a reason. Kept.
