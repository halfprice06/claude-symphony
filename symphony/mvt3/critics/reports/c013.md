### 1. Required: augment theme B's recall, 211–218 (applied)

At 2 = 72 the 8 bars last 13.3 s by the MIDI.

The accompaniment is rebuilt for 8 bars, with one harmony per bar where v13 had one per half bar:

- 211–212: D♭. The chord is struck ff on 211 with a diminuendo and struck again f at 212. This keeps the dynamic plan: ff on the downbeat of 211, then f under the trumpets.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 446 (Ex. 26-5) tonicizes a triad with "the progression IV–V⁶–I"; 211–216 is I–IV7–V7–iii7–I6 in D♭. ### 2. The last chorale stays in Allegro molto, written in doubled values, 255–264 (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's tempo plan sags at the end. Measured in v13 (new numbers):

- 211–246 is 36 consecutive bars at 2 = 72, 56 or 66: about 67 s of broad or moderate music after the augmentation.
- The fast peroration is then broken in three: Allegro molto for 8 bars (9.6 s), Maestoso for 5 bars (10.0 s), Allegro molto for 7 bars (8.4 s).

So the symphony's final structural cadence comes after a tempo change and before another one. A&S PDF 445 asks that the goal tonic be reached "with a sense of rhythmic resolution". PDF 448 asks for "purposeful motion over large as well as small musical spans". In Dvořák 9/iv the last cadence (bar 329) sits inside one continuous closing phrase (bars 299–348) with no tempo break. Brahms 1/iv, the model named for this chorale, brings the chorale back at 407 inside the Più allegro, in long values, rather than slowing down.

**This re-raises a point cycle 9 kept, and answers its reason.** Cycle 9 kept the Maestoso because at Allegro molto the chorale "would last about six seconds". Written in doubled values at 2 = 100, it lasts 12.0 s (MIDI), longer than the Maestoso's 10.0 s.

**Fix (applied in `sections/finale.ily` and `global.ily`).**

### 3. The development's centre restates F2 twice without developing it: one head entry at 130–131 (applied)

Bars 115–130 (16 bars, about 23 s, 4–6.5 voices, pp) are two complete statements of F2: bassoon in G minor, then harp in G major. A&S PDF 456 says that development sections extend the dominant "on a much larger scale" through subordinate motion. Here the dominant area 123–150 is 28 bars, and its first half has no motivic event at all.

**Fix (applied in `sections/dev.ily`).** I decided to keep both solos: the harp's is the owed "still unused" item. In the solo's last two bars, where the harp holds A5 over D7, the violas enter with F1's head in major and lead the cadence into G at 132:


This replaces `c'1 | <d' a'>2\f <c' a'>2`. The line runs against the violins' rising run at 131, in contrary motion. The pairwise check finds no parallels for the violas at 129–132.
