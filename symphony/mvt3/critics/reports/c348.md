## Findings

### 1. 243–246: the stringendo starts where nothing can show it. Start it at 245, with the run (applied)

**Bars:** 243–246 (Q, Poco più mosso 2 = 66, into R, Più allegro 2 = 88 at 247). "poco a poco stringendo" is printed at 243. The MIDI steps are 70, 75, 80 and 84 in 243–246.

**Problem:** In 243–244.5 there is one E-major chord and then a cadential 6/4 held for three bars. Over it the horn call in augmentation (Tpt 1·2, Hn 1·2: E–C–B–C) enters on 243.1, 243.3, 244.2 and 245.3, so three of its four notes start off the beat and are tied over. No part marks the beat from 244.2 until the violins' and violas' eighth-note run begins on 245.2.5. The timpani roll and the long notes do not mark it either. A stringendo over this cannot be heard. All it can do is hurry the horn call, the owed item that the augmentation is there to broaden. The acceleration can only be heard from the run at 245.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 49: "the impression of a quick, moderate, or slow tempo comes from the pace of the beats, not from the speed of the quickest notes. The pace of the beats relates to tonal movement, to the frequency with which chords change, or to the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody." At 243–245 all three are at their slowest in the coda: one chord change in three bars, and main tones every 1¼ to 1½ bars. PDF 51 explains why the syncopated entries need a steady beat: a syncopation conflicts with the metre only when the metre is heard. The movement's other tempo changes already follow this. The "poco a poco più animato" at 205–210 is carried by the F1 heads in two-bar units and the violin scales. The "allargando" at 221–222 and 271–272 falls on cadential motion.

**Fix (applied, global.ily only):**
```
% before
s1*4 \tag #'score \pageBreak s1*4 \tempo \markup \medium \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "poco a poco" "stringendo" } \mt 140 s1 \mt 150 s1 \mt 160 s1 \mt 168 s1
% after
s1*4 \tag #'score \pageBreak s1*4 s1*2 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "stringendo" \mt 136 s2 \mt 146 s2 \mt 156 s2 \mt 166 s2
```
243–244 stay at 2 = 66. The MIDI tempo is now 68 at 245, 73 at 245.3, 78 at 246, 83 at 246.3, and 88 at R as before (checked in score.midi). The printed word fits on one line, so c225's two-line text is no longer needed. Page 14 is checked as an image: "stringendo" stands above the violins' "unis." and collides with nothing. The parts were not rebuilt. The single word is narrower than the old two-line text, so it cannot add a collision in the parts that the old text did not have.

**Protection answered:** "tempi 211–246" (c193, form) protects the tempo plan: Poco più mosso, then a push, then Più allegro 88 at 247. That plan stays, and only the start of the push moves two bars later. The horn call's notes, layout and dynamics are unchanged. The MIDI is 0.6 s longer.

### 2. 227–229: the canon's suspensions are one flat ff. Swell each tied note into its dissonance (applied)

**Bars:** 226–230, the canon on the chorale (Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Vln I·II). c331 displaced it by half a bar: E enters on 227.3 and is tied into 228, where it is a 7th over IV and falls to D on 228.3 (7–6). D is tied into 229, where it is a 9th over I and falls to C on 229.3 (9–8). The c331 comment at 226 calls the first of these a 7th over vi, and it is G tied from 226 into 227. All the notes are a plain ff, the strings have "change bow at will", and there are no slurs or hairpins.

**Problem:** c331 made the canon syncopated so that it would not sound like an echo of the brass. On the page nothing shapes that displacement. A held ff tie under free bowing does not make the weak-beat entry stronger than the downbeat it is tied over, nor the downbeat dissonance stronger than its resolution. The 7–6 and 9–8 are the movement's only suspension chain in a melody line at the arrival, and they are played like the chorale's whole notes.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 51: a note held from a weak through a strong beat conflicts with the metre. PDF 55 (Ex. 3-16, image): the suspension is that syncopation turned into an accented dissonance. Ex. 3-7 (image, Brahms Op. 77, I, 304–308) shows how Brahms writes it: the strings' tied, syncopated chords carry a swell < > across each bar line, peaking where the tie meets the downbeat. Ex. 3-6 (Beethoven) marks the same kind of tied note sf. Brahms's swell is the right device here: the passage is cantabile and Maestoso, and an sf would cut across the chorale. The score already follows the related rule once, at 80–81 (c248: the diminuendo starts on the resolution). It had not been applied to the canon.

**Fix (applied, sections/arrival.ily):**
```
Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II:  g''1~\ff | g''2 e'''2~\< | e'''2\> d'''2~\< | d'''2\> c'''4\! des'''4 | c'''1
Fl 2:  g''1~\ff | g''2 a''2\< | a''2~\> a''2\< | a''2\> bes''2\! | a''1
Ob 2:  g''1~\ff | g''2 a''2\< | a''2~\> a''2\< | a'2\> bes'2\! | a'1
Cl 2:  d''1\ff | e''2 f''2\< | a''2~\> a''2\< | g'2.\> f'4\! | e'1
```
The second players' whole notes at 228 are written as tied halves, so they can carry the same hairpins. The sound is the same (A&S PDF 49: tied notes may replace a single value where that shows the rhythm more clearly), and the shared staves keep equal dynamics. I first also put a hairpin on the G at 226–227. The part combiner then dropped the flutes' and oboes' "a2" at 226 and printed two note heads, so the G keeps its plain ff. The flutes' unison at 231 had the same problem, and `\partCombineUnisono` at 231 with `\partCombineAutomatic` at 232 restores its "a2" (Fl 1 only). Notes, ties, the ff level, the brass chorale and the 222→223 resolution are unchanged. Page 13 is checked as an image: the hairpins sit between the staves, and 226 and 231 show "a2" as in v348.

**Protection answered:** "arrival and chorale 223–234" and "canon 226–230" protect the arrival's notes, scoring and the 222→223 resolution, and c331 has since changed the canon's rhythm. This adds hairpins only. analysis.txt shows one moment moved from "shared" to "one/two" on each wind staff (part-combiner channels), with the same notes.
