## Findings

### F1 (applied). 159–166: the held inner line moves from Oboe 2 to the cor anglais

Problem. Since c166 Ob 2 holds an inner line under the recapitulated F1 (G4, F4–C5, G4, F4, E♭4–A♭4, G4–F4, D4, E♭4; mf, f at 166), on a separate "Oboe 2" staff that c135 added for these eight bars only. Its last three bars lie in the oboe's lowest fifth: E♭4 at 163, a whole bar of D4 at 165, E♭4 at 166. The cor anglais rests from 151 to 170 and enters with the solo at 171.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 70–71: the oboe's lowest notes, F down to B♭, are "a bit coarse" and "the most difficult to control". PDF 72: the cor anglais "does not become coarse or nasal in the bottom of its range, which makes it extremely useful as the lowest voice in a three-way voicing in place of an oboe". PDF 63, 72, 106: it blends best with the clarinet, which is the voice under this line (Cl 2). The score has not used the cor anglais this way before. It has used it only in tutti doublings (1–6, 147–150) and as a soloist (171–186, 217–220). For the cor anglais the line lies at written D5 down to A4 and B♭4, its middle register. The oboe player loses nothing: Ob 2 now rests from 159.

### F2 (tested and declined). 121–122: Cl 1's pp D6 (written E6)

c323's 7–6 suspension sits in the register Sebesky calls "shrill, piercing" (PDF 60, Ex. 66; PDF 106: "use only when a shrill quality is desired"). The candidate was Fl 1 at the same pitch, since it rests 114–128 and Vln I's soli take D6 from it at 122.2. I declined it. Sebesky himself calls the flute "rather intense" in that octave for quiet lines (PDF 67, Ex. 80), and D6 begins the flute's third octave. Adler (adler1 PDF 210) states that a good clarinettist plays "from the faintest pianissimo ... extending to the highest range". The candidate would trade one register risk for another, so it is not better. No change.

### F3 (checked, kept). 143–150: woodwind second at 143 and high trombones

It lasts one bar under the violins' f F1 head, and at 144 the flute's A♭5 makes it a third. Ob 1's F5 continues its own F1 line from 141–142. Kept. The trumpets lie above them (G4 to F5), there is a breath after 144, and from 151 the trombones play mf. c433's F4–D4 → E♭4–C4 resolution into 151 needs that register. Kept.
