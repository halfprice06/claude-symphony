## Findings

### 1. Hn 3·4, 255–258: F2's last statement hangs on 2–4 ledger lines below the treble stave (applied)

**Problem.** Since c371, F2's last statement (G3–E3 | D3–C3–E3–G3 | C4–D4 | G4–F4–E4–D4, ff, a2) is played by Hn 3·4 together with Bn 1, the violas and the cellos. In the concert-pitch score, the Hn 3·4 stave stays in treble clef. The first seven notes (255–256) all sit below the stave: G3 on two ledger lines, E3 on three, D3 and C3 on four. These are the lowest notes of the passage, and they fall in the bars where the phrase opens. The cellos and Bn 1 play the same line in bass clef on the staves below, but the horns' line looks different from theirs. The score already uses score-only bass clefs for the horns in this situation: c095 at 25–26 (Hn 1·2, E♭3–B2) and c425 at 79–82 (Hn 2, B♭2).

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21: "stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently". At 256 the passage goes past that limit for a whole half-bar, and in bass clef it needs at most three ledger lines (G4 at 258). PDF 22: the clef "always goes before the barline", and in continuous playing it goes "between phrases": 254|255 is where the a2 statement begins after the held chord, and 258|259 is where its slur ends and the chorale's repeated accented halves begin. PDF 23: a non-standard clef is kept only as long as it is needed. From 259 on (D4/A♭3, E♭4, G4) treble needs at most two ledger lines, and bass clef would need three. Adler part 2, PDF 27: horns doubling a cello line are written in bass clef.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-455/work/sections/finale.ily, with a c455 comment above finaleHnThree).** Only the tagged clefs were added; the notes are the same:
```
finaleHnThree = { … f'1 | f'1 | \tag #'score \clef bass g2.->(\ff e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4) \breathe | c'2.( d'4 | g'4. f'8 e'4 d'4) | \tag #'score \clef treble d'2-> d'2 | …
finaleHnFour  = { … d'2 c'2 | \tag #'score \clef bass g2.->(\ff e4 | d4 c4 e4 g4) \breathe | c'2.( d'4 | g'4. f'8 e'4 d'4) | \tag #'score \clef treble aes1~ | …
```
Result at 300 dpi (page 15): 255–257 sit inside the stave. The highest note, G4 at 258, is on three ledger lines. Both clefs stand before their barlines. At 256 the stems follow PDF 28: D3 on the middle line takes an up-stem with C3 in its half-bar, and E3 and G3 take down-stems. The part's written F pitches (D4–G3, at most two ledger lines) stay in treble, because the clefs are `\tag #'score`, the same as at 25–26 and 79–82. During testing I also tried moving the clefs into a Staff-level skip voice in score.ly. A 200-dpi crop had made the clef look as if it stood after the barline, but a 300-dpi crop showed it was before the barline in both versions. I kept the simpler in-part version, so score.ly is unchanged.

**Build.** /tmp/critic-455/work builds with 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt is empty) on 16 pages. score.midi is byte-identical to v455's. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. par8.py on 254–260 gives the same four lines as v455: the bass clarinet's and Bn 2's existing octave doubling of the bass at 259.1. The theme record is 51/51.

### 2. Hn 1·2 at 13, 21, 153, 161 and Hn 3·4 at 29: one D3 on four ledger lines (checked, no change)

Each is the single lowest note of F1's third bar (`d4( g8 aes8 b4 d'4)`), in the middle of a slurred phrase that otherwise uses at most three ledger lines in treble. In bass clef the same phrase would need four ledger lines for A♭4 at 17. A clef change would therefore come inside the phrase twice, which Gould PDF 21–22 rules out ("change clef between phrases"; do not change "frequently"). I left these unchanged. The clarinets' concert D3 at 178 (their lowest written E3) also stays in treble: the clarinet's clef never changes.
