## Findings

### 1. Bars 243–246: the upper wood-wind fall silent during the four-bar crescendo into 247 (applied)
- **Problem.** 243–246 are a four-bar *cresc. molto* and *stringendo* from mf to the ff tutti of 247, with the trumpets' and horns' horn call on top and the violins' run rising to G6 over the D bass of V4/3. While the crescendo grows, the upper wood-wind thin out: Fl 2 stops at 243.3, Ob 1 at 244.1, Hn 3 at 243.3; from 244 to 246.3 nothing sustains between Cl 1's F5 and the trumpets' B5–C6 except the moving violin run. The flutes and oboes then enter ff at 247 from silence. The crescendo is carried by strings and brass only, and the space that widens between the run and the D bass is not filled.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Prolonged orchestral crescendi are obtained by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass"; here wood-wind are subtracted inside the crescendo. PDF 131 (diverging progressions): "The intermediate intervals are filled up by the introduction of fresh parts as the distance widens, so that the upper parts become doubled or trebled"; Ex. 262 (Antar, PDF 429) fills its ff wedge with flutes, oboes and clarinets in held and moving parts. Ex. 245–250 (cited at PDF 127, c139/c439): an added group enters p and grows faster.
- **Fix (applied, sections/sHead.ily).** `sHeadOboeOne`: `gis'2->\f( g'2) | R1*3 | }` → `gis'2->\f( g'2) | R1 | g''1~\p\< | g''1 | }`. New variable `sHeadOboeTwo = { R1*10 | b'1~\p\< | b'1 | }`. The oboes hold G5 and B4 (a sixth, a concord for a pair of oboes, Rimsky PDF 90 rule 4; no D, which would double the bass of the V4/3, PDF 83), swelling p < into their own ff entry at 247 (E5/C5): G5 falls to E5 and B4 rises to C5, both by the normal resolution. Equal simultaneous dynamics on the shared oboe staff. Horn call, run, strings and every other part unchanged; no new page.
- **Check.** Page 14 shows the oboe chord with p and a hairpin to the ff at 247 (verified at 200 dpi). par8.py on 243–248: identical to v439. Outer-voice parallels 0. The Ob 2 B4 is in unison with Hn 1's B4 (already sounding), so the leading tone gains no new octave.

### 2. Dvořák 7/iv holistic comparison: a conductor would accept this finale; no score change
- Dvořák's finale opens p espressivo in the cellos over held fz chords and dim. (corpus PDF 147); this finale opens ff by the cyclic rule (bars 1–2 protected). Both close with a tonic pedal, string octaves hammered against timpani tonic–dominant strokes (Dvořák 416–423; here 265–272) and a broad last stage whose bass moves chromatically under a held high tonic chord (Dvořák's Molto maestoso, bass G♯–G–D, plagal; here 273–276, IV6/4–iv6/4–I over the C pedal). The plans correspond closely: both last stages are plagal. This score places its only root V–I at 262–263 (protected) and ends plagally after it. (The corpus engraving leaves the wind staves empty in 409–438, so I compared the strings, trombone, timpani and bass lines there, not the wind scoring.)
- Differences that are design, not faults: Dvořák's finale is much longer (about 437 bars in the corpus engraving, against 279) and its development carries more weight; this finale's development (99–134) is compact and its weight sits in the four-stage coda, as the plan intends (protected proportions). F2 changes colour at each return (trumpet, strings, cor anglais, strings), the practice Rimsky shows in Ex. 289–291 (PDF 467–470).

### 3. Percussion with the rhythmic tuttis (Rimsky PDF 135): considered, declined
- PDF 135 says percussion "should always be employed concurrently" with rhythmic figures, bass drum and cymbals on strong beats and disconnected sforzandi. The hammered passages at 91–94, 199–204 and 247–272 have timpani only; cymbals and bass drum sound at 223, 263 and 278.
- Declined: PDF 136 (economy) ranks percussion last and says it is "seldom employed"; the first crash must stay at 223 (protected since cycle 10); 199–204 is movement I's tutti part by part (protected); and the models (Brahms 1/iv, Dvořák 7/iv, Tchaikovsky 5/iv) use no cymbals in their finales or, in Dvořák 9/iv, one stroke. Three strokes at the three goals is the stronger choice.

### 4. Brass re-entries (Rimsky PDF 136): checked, one forte entry kept
- The MIDI scan finds every horn, trombone and tuba re-entry after a long rest at pp or ff (43, 67, 127, 143 pp; 91, 199, 211 ff), as PDF 136 asks. The exception is Tbn 1·2 at 247, *f* after twelve bars' rest, inside an ff tutti. It is kept: the trombones double the cellos' and Bn 1's F1 head while the S head sounds in Hn 1·2, oboes and clarinets; f in the trombones equals the strings' ff in loudness, and ff would cover the S head (Adler ch. 15, PDF 176: each theme of a combination at good strength). Rimsky's rule concerns the character of the entry; a forte inside a ff tutti is not the colourless mf/mp entry he warns against.
