# Critic lenses (cycle N uses lens ((N-1) mod 10) + 1)
1. Melody and memorability: the first theme F1, the second theme F2 (trumpet solo, then violins), the chorale, the recalled themes and the final combination; contour, rhythm, climaxes and continuation; would a listener hum them afterwards? Compare with the great finale tunes of Brahms, Tchaikovsky, Dvořák, Beethoven and Elgar.
2. Harmony and voice leading: progressions, chromatic lines, pedals, inner voices, dissonance treatment, the key plan (C minor – E♭ – F minor – G – C minor – C major, with the D♭ episode), the German-sixth opening, the cadence plan (no root-position V–I in C before 254–255).
3. Form, pacing and proportion: sonata form plus a long coda; section lengths, the tension curve, the development and retransition, the recapitulation, the arrival, and whether the coda's several stages (arrival, chorale, second-theme head, final combination) build or sag. Compare with corpus finales.
4. Orchestration: balance, doubling, register, colour, each section's use, the tuttis, the brass chorale, the arrival, string-free passages, percussion. Sources: Rimsky-Korsakov and Adler.
5. Engraving and notation (Gould's *Behind Bars*, MOLA): dynamics, hairpins, slurs, beaming in 2/2, tremolo notation, percussion staff, clefs, part-combining labels, rehearsal and tempo marks, system and page layout, the page cap. Inspect many pages of the PDF.
6. Playability, instrument by instrument: ranges, breathing, bowing, harp pedalling, timpani tuning and retuning, percussion, brass endurance in a loud finale, divisi, solos, mutes.
7. Texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the bass line, imitation, the canon at the chorale, the combination of themes, dialogue between groups.
8. Expression: dynamics, articulation, phrasing slurs, tempo changes (meno mosso, Maestoso, stringendo, Allegro molto), and expressive text.
9. Holistic comparison with one specific master finale, a different one each time this lens comes up (critics/MASTERS.txt). Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Also compare with the version 10 cycles earlier and flag anything that got worse.
10. Cyclic design and emotional arc: the start after movement II, the owed items from cyclic-notes.md, the quotations of movements I and II, the resolution of the stop chord and the E♭/E♮ thread, the C-major arrival, and how the finale completes the symphony.
