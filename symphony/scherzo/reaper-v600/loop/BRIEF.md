# Movement III (Scherzo) mockup loop: brief for every critic and actor

## Goal

The mockup is movement III (Scherzo: Allegro vivace, E minor, with a Trio in E major) of the symphony, played in REAPER by EastWest Hollywood Orchestra Opus Edition (Diamond) through the Opus plugin and improved by actor-critic cycles. Each cycle makes it sound more like a real orchestra playing this score well. The score is fixed. Only the rendering changes.

Dan (2026-11-07): actor-critic loops on the scherzo mockup, the same method as movements I and II. Cycle c00 is the port baseline: the REAPER/EastWest pipeline of Movement I series 2 and Movement II series 2 set up for this score, with no per-note tuning (`cycles/c00/PORT.md`, `cycles/c00/CHANGES.md`, `loop/HISTORY.md`). Cycles c01-c20 (at least 20) are the improvement loops. Each cycle's critiques are written by three independent critic subagents (lenses in `loop/LENSES.md`), then one actor subagent edits, rebuilds and renders (`loop/actor_prompt.md`). Dan will judge the result by ear, so favour changes a listener hears; his priorities are articulations, per-track volume and blend, and long timpani rolls must not drown other parts.

Read `mockup-kit/LEARNINGS.md` (from "What a listener noticed that measurement missed" on) before your first change: it records the measured lessons of movement II series 1 and movement I about this library and pipeline. Movement I's later lessons are in `symphony/mvt1/reaper-v621/loop/LEARNINGS.md` and Movement II's in `symphony/mvt2/reaper-v600/loop/LEARNINGS.md` (both read only). Do not relearn them. This movement is much faster than either: the Allegro vivace runs at quarter 264 (dotted half 88), so one eighth lasts 0.114 s, and lead, gap and fill rules written in seconds for Movement I's quarter 126 take twice the share of a note here.

Dan's standing priorities for renders (from `symphony/render/BRIEF.md`, written for the earlier FluidSynth/VPO renders, still his rules):
1. Articulations must be audible and correct: slurs/legato, tenuto, accents, tremolo, pizzicato, as printed.
2. Each track's level must match its printed dynamic and its role (melody above accompaniment) at every dynamic level.
3. Blend: one ensemble in one room (common reverb, seating, matched colours, nothing sticking out because of a sample set).
4. Accents: emphasis and weight, not a hard click (about 2-4 dB, no sharp transient).
5. Timpani rolls: start at the printed dynamic, then settle under the texture. Only a printed crescendo makes a roll grow. A long roll is never the loudest stem unless the timpani lead.
6. The delivered mp3 must be comfortable at one headphone volume (Dan, 2026-10-11, after Movement II series 1 c07: "I have to turn my head phones to max volume to hear the soft parts well, which makes the loudest parts almost unbearable"): soft passages clearly audible, loud passages not painful. Targets (`loop/DYNAMICS-RESEARCH.md`): delivered LRA 14-16 LU, 16-19 dB from the 10th-percentile short-term loudness to the loudest, no reversed crescendos or diminuendos, the climax (the coda's fff summit, bar 336, qn 1005) still clearly the loudest, delivery limiter at most about 3 dB. `render_cycle.py` masters the mp3 with `tools/deliver_master.py`; do part of the work in the mix (soft_trim_db, solo roles), not with CC11. pp must still sound soft through timbre and texture.
7. Memory: keep REAPER's settled footprint under about 45 GB on this 96 GB Mac (unused articulation rows are unloaded: `UNLOAD_UNUSED_ROWS`; Movement II's 32 instances settled at 44 GB). Do not add Opus instances without removing others. Restart REAPER before every render and quit it after (save first); record REAPER memory after the render in CHANGES.md. Other user processes (the Movement I, Movement II and finale threads, Steam) may hold several GB; check `top -o mem` before rendering.

Dan's listening notes on movement II series 1 (2026-10-12, after c24; each came from a person hearing a render after the measurements had passed it):
8. **No audible sample-loop pulsing on long held notes** ("sounds like there's some kind of weird bowing effect being applied on the last note"). Hollywood Strings sustains loop every 3.95 s; one sample held for many seconds pulses in level and tone colour, and sections that start a chord together pulse in step. `score_to_parts.py` re-bows every string note over 4.5 s (`AUTO_REBOW`; held tremolos and the listed points excepted), staggered per section (`tools/space_rebows.py`). Check every held note over about 6 s on the stems and the master: the 100-300 Hz band level and the spectral centroid folded at the loop period (autocorrelation at 3.95 s), and the level dip and step at each re-bow. In the scherzo the held notes are the Violins I ppp G sharp 5 of bars 1-8 (qn 0-23, re-bowed at qn 6.5) and of bars 364-376 (qn 1089-1128, re-bowed at 1103.9, 1116.4, 1125.1), the Violins I harmonic E6 of bars 141-149 (qn 420-445, re-bowed at 433.2), the celli's held notes at 605-624 and 1070-1091 (re-bowed), Hn 1's G sharp 4 in bars 1-2 and 372-376, the trio's horn drone (bars 153-168), the codetta's E pedal (bars 225-240) and the timpani rolls. Brass, winds and timpani rolls loop too.
9. **Brass accents soft: weight, not bite** ("the accented notes the trombones play during the call back to movement 1 at the climax: the accents are too harsh", Movement II). Every brass, wind and string accent plays the plain sustain with a volume swell (`GENTLE_ACCENT`, Movement I's settings). The scherzo's score itself keeps accents and fff off the brass at the coda summit (score coda.ily, c200: "no brass plays fff, ff on an attack, sfz or accents (Dan: weight, not bite)"); its 321 accents are in the strings (Violins I 47, Violins II 37, violas 31, celli 13, basses 18), woodwinds (Flute 1 36, cor anglais 29, Oboe 1 28, Clarinet 2 18 ...) and timpani (4). Measure 2-5 kHz energy over the first 80 ms of each accent against the note's body and against the other sections, as well as level over the neighbours.

Dan's listening notes on movement I series 2 (2026-11-06, from hearing its c34-c35), standing checks for every lens here too:
- **S1. Repeated notes must be heard as separate notes.** Check every same-pitch repeat (winds, brass and strings, slurred or not) for an audible gap or re-attack in the stem and in the master. The library's repetition rows (`loop/MANUAL-GAPS.md` #2) have not been tried here.
- **S2. The perceived centre of each attack must sit on the beat.** Measure the perceptual onset (where the attack reaches its body level, or the energy centroid of the first 80-150 ms), not the MIDI note-on, against the beat grid, per section and per articulation; the leads align note starts, not perceived attacks. At quarter 264 a 0.05 s error is almost half an eighth: check staccato strings against staccato winds and pizzicato in the theme statements, and the trio's legato tune (Clarinet 1 bars 153-168, celli 169-184, violins 209-224) against the harp's off-beat chords.
- **S3. A subito must not sound like a glitch.** A sudden drop must keep the hall's ring of the louder level decaying under the softer one. Mechanism (Movement I c36, measured): CC7 and CC11 act inside Opus, before its hall, so a drop there leaves the tail ringing; REAPER's track volume envelope (vol_db: roles, trims, hairpin dB ramps, fills) acts after the hall and cuts the tail with the note. Put sudden drops in CC11/CC7, never as a fast fall in the volume envelope. The scherzo prints "sub. p" twice, at the second idea in E minor after a forte (both read by `score_to_parts.py`: `subito_fresh` / `subito_falls` in parts.json):

From c01 the shared hall return (mix.json `reverb.fx_volume`) is 0.095 instead of 0.19 (-6 dB, linear amplitude; the shared value from the Movement I thread, mockup-kit README item 7), with sends, IR, wet 2.0, 5.5 kHz high cut and true stereo unchanged; do not raise sends to win depth back. Targets: the master drops at least 13 dB 0.3 s after each tutti cut-off (recordings 13-17 dB; about 9-10 dB at 0.19), late decay 1.5-2 s (a shorter IR is a later option if it stays over 2 s). Also from c01: the hall's input low cut at 160 Hz (`reverb.low_cut_hz`, Opus Bandpass lowFilterFreq/lowCutFreq; high cut 5500 kept), so the bass reaches the hall less. Re-check S1 repeated-note dips and join notches against c00 with the drier hall. Do not add hall back without Dan's word. Next steps Movement I is testing (later cycles here): (a) a shorter hall, 1.5-2 s late decay, through Opus's IR envelope (Opus manual p.133-134); (b) string same-pitch repeats that are not under one slur re-attacked on Sus MAX after a min(0.06 s, 25%) gap instead of being merged into the previous legato note with a CC11 notch (the scherzo's c01 parts.json carries CC11 notches (merged repeats and printed bow marks inside held notes): Violins II 7, violas 53, celli 37, Violas div 15, Violins I and basses 0; check which are unslurred repeats and measure them on the stems).   Other sudden drops (S3): the tutti sfp at bar 301 (qn 900: woodwinds, strings; an sf attack falling to p under a crescendo, read as FP_SPIKE with the sf level), the G.P. at bar 340 after the fff summit (qn 1017: the hall must ring into the silence), the cut-offs before the rests of the theme head, and every role or trim row that ends with a step under a sounding chord.

## Facts already established (details and numbers in mockup-kit/LEARNINGS.md and the two movements' loop/LEARNINGS.md)

- HO: CC11 crossfades the dynamic layers and sets volume; CC1 crossfades a non-vibrato and a vibrato layer in the woodwind and string KS Masters (not a depth control; on the strings the Molto Vib set is recorded louder). In the Hollywood Brass KS Masters and the Clarinet KS Master CC1 crossfades the dynamic layers (`CC1_DYN_TRACKS`, on here from c00; the horns take Movement II's c31 soft map, CC1 57 or more, because their legato transitions are 6-13 dB hot at CC1 28-54). Make balance corrections in the volume envelope so CC11 keeps each printed dynamic's timbre.
- Legato patches are monophonic; transitions need overlap; legato targets start early by measured per-family leads; a same-pitch re-attack on Leg Slur MAX plays the slow first-note layer.
- KS Masters load only some rows; `build_reaper.py` loads the rows a track uses and unloads the rest, so a track whose set of rows changes is fully rebuilt.
- Hollywood Strings with no CC70 plays finger position 4 (CC70 127); the lanes rest at 127 and the printed "sul A" spans (`SUL_SPANS`) take each note's string from the patch's table. Opus stores start-mode controller values in the saved state: end every such lane on its resting value.
- A silent 3 qn pre-roll (at 60 bpm, its own 3/4 bar) carries each track's first keyswitch and CC values and the CC36 round-robin reset; `render_cycle.py` cuts it off, so the rendered files are on score time.
- The audio device is part of the render conditions: `render_cycle.py` refuses to render unless REAPER's device is 48000 Hz, 512-sample blocks, no input; REAPER closes the device by itself when it is not the frontmost app (`reaper.Audio_Init()` in a bridge job reopens it just before the render), and a Bluetooth headset as the system input wrecked a Movement I render (24 kHz mic opened, every output shifted 256 samples). Never save with `Main_SaveProjectEx` and a path (it writes a copy elsewhere); use `reaper_ctl.py save`.

## Rules

- Only the actor drives REAPER. Critics never run `rpr.py` or `reaper_ctl.py`, never touch the bridge folder, never write into the `.rpp`, and never start renders. Critics may run their own analysis scripts on the rendered files (`~/.venvs/claude-composes-reaper/bin/python` has numpy, soundfile, mido; also ffmpeg, lilypond).
- REAPER is shared by the Movement I, Movement II and finale threads through `~/.rpr-bridge/REAPER.lock`. Before any REAPER step the actor runs `~/.venvs/claude-composes-reaper/bin/python symphony/scherzo/reaper-v600/tools/reaper_ctl.py lock "scherzo cNN actor"` (owner "scherzo"; it waits up to 6 h while another thread holds REAPER), keeps every bridge job's `.lua` file inside `symphony/scherzo/reaper-v600/`, and after the render and any `--mix-only` write for a stem fix runs `reaper_ctl.py quit` and then `reaper_ctl.py unlock`. Never break, edit or remove another owner's lock. Do all non-REAPER work (edits, parts.json, diffs, simulations) before taking the lock, so the lock is held only for the build, the restart, the render and the save.
- Never edit the score or anything in `symphony/scherzo/v600/`, `symphony/mvt1/`, `symphony/mvt2/`, `symphony/mvt3/`, `symphony/render/`, `mockup-kit/`. Never commit or push, and never switch the git branch (other threads use this checkout). Never delete files outside your own cycle folder and your own scratch folder.
- Do not install software (no pip, brew or downloads). No GUI or computer use; REAPER is driven only through the bridge and `reaper_ctl.py`.
- Everything that changes the result lives in scripts or config files under `symphony/scherzo/reaper-v600/` (tools, `build/score_config.py`, `build/*.json`), so the project can be rebuilt from scratch. A setting made only inside REAPER is lost when a track is rebuilt. Keep generic rules generic: a rule that would help any score goes in `tools/` with a default, its scherzo values in `build/score_config.py`.
- The printed notes, dynamics, articulations and tempos are the reference. A rendering must not contradict them. Small liberties that real players take (shaping inside a hairpin, a slight swell on a long note, breath before a breath mark, a little rubato inside a printed rit.) are allowed if the score supports them.
- One render per cycle, from a freshly restarted REAPER; fix level-only problems on the stems afterwards (`tools/stem_fix.py`, mockup-kit LEARNINGS section 15) instead of rendering twice.
- After each render the orchestrating thread messages the Movement I thread the new mp3 path for the shared mockups artifact (Dan, 2026-11-07); actors do not publish anything.
- Keep scratch under 2 GB per agent, in `/private/tmp/claude-501/mockup-loop/scherzo-<your label>/`, and delete your scratch folder when you finish. Before rendering, check `df -h /System/Volumes/Data`: at least 30 GB must be free. Stem fixes keep copies only of the stems they replace.
