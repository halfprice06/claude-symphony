## Findings

### 1. Theme A's staccato heads replay the identical samples phrase after phrase (bars 13-28, qn 36-84; less in the reprise 253-268, qn 756-804), Violins I (measured; mechanism inferred)

Cross-correlation of the first 60 ms of each staccato note (level-normalised, Violins I stem): the head notes of bars 13, 17 and 25 (qn 36, 48, 72) are the same samples, xcorr 0.98-1.00, for all four notes of the head (E 36/48/72, B 37/49/73, B 37.5/49.5/73.5, E 38/50/74); bars 19, 22 and 27 likewise (D sharp 55/64/79 and 55.5/64.5/79.5; E 42/63/78). In the reprise two of four heads repeat (B 769/793, E 768/792, 770/794). Within a pair the two eighths differ (xcorr 0.43), so the round robin advances inside a phrase but returns to the same position at the next phrase. Over the whole piece, two staccatissimo notes with the same index counted from the last row change into "Staccatissimo RRx9" are identical (xcorr > 0.95) 37 % of the time against 6 % for different indices; the c02 move of the half-note heads to "Sus Marc LITE" puts a row change before every phrase. The manual says Opus continues the cycle (p.145; DIGEST), so this looks like a per-row restart on re-selection rather than the global cycle: inferred, but the identical samples are measured. A listener hears the theme's opening four bars stamped out three times: this is the "repeated staccato eighths that sound like one sample" the lens names, in the movement's main theme at mp, where nothing covers it.

Fix (no new memory): alternate the staccato row by four-bar phrase in the p/mp statements. `build/score_config.py` `ART_SPANS["Violins I"]`: "Stac RRx14" (already loaded for the tuttis, `ROW_TRIM_DB` -3.0 at 80 like "Staccatissimo RRx9") with `"mark": "staccato"` over qn 48-60, 72-84, 768-780 and 792-804 (the second and fourth phrase of each statement; the Marc LITE heads stay). Check after the render: the 13/17/25 head xcorr under 0.7, and the 2-5 kHz level of the RRx14 phrases within 2 dB of the RRx9 phrases (StacSs is the shorter stroke; if it is brighter or shorter by ear, try "Stac On Bow RRx9", one more row of a few thousand regions). If the actor can show the restart on the stem, a generic alternative is a `STAC_RR_OFFSET` that starts each run after a row change at a different count (a silent advance note); but test that first on a scratch track.

### 3. The G.P. (bar 340) is the timpani's undamped ring, 13-24 dB over the orchestra's hall (measured; open since c03)

From the last stroke at qn 1014: everything except the timpani is 20.6 dB down at +0.6 s and 40 dB down at +1.0 s; the timpani stem is the whole master from +0.25 s on (master -42.8 / timpani -43.0 / all others -56.1 dB at G.P. +0.3 s; -55.1 / -55.1 / -79.2 at +1.0 s). The ring comes from the Lng strokes at 1008, 1010 and 1012 (B2 at v121, accents); the printed staccato E chord at 1014 already plays on "Timpani Flt Sht" (c03), but it cannot damp the drums struck before it. A timpanist damps everything on a staccato chord before a G.P.; the score's G.P. (quarter 96, 1.9 s) is meant to be the hall's silence after the fff, and with the drier hall that silence would now be clean. The manual's Short row is the tool for "realistic stops and dampening" (p.124).

Fix: (a) the accented half note at qn 1012 on "Timpani Flt Sht" as well (`TIMP_STAC_ROW` only takes printed staccatos; add an `ART_SPANS["Timpani"]` entry 1011.9-1012.1 with the Sht row, or a `TIMP_SHT_AT = [1012.0]` list in `tools/score_to_parts.py`); (b) a pre-hall damp of the earlier strokes: the timpani's CC7 lane falls to 1 from qn 1014.6 to 1014.8 (after the Sht chord has spoken; c03 LEARNINGS: a volume-envelope cut is undone by the tail hold, CC7 acts before the hall) and returns to its table value at qn 1080 (next timpani note 1086). `CC7_POINTS` is documented for tracks other than the timpani; either merge it into the timpani's computed lane or add a generic `TIMP_DAMP_AT = {1014.6: 0.2}`. Check: timpani stem at G.P. +0.3 s at least 10 dB under its c03 value, and the master's drop 0.3 s after qn 1016 at least 13 dB (now -6.3 dB at +0.3 s from the 1014 stroke).
