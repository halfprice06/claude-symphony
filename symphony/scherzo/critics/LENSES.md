# Critic lenses (cycle N uses lens ((N-1) mod 10) + 1)
1. Melody and memorability: theme A (motive x and the chromatic tail), the second idea, the trio tune T, the harp melody and the coda's return of T; contour, rhythm, phrase lengths, climaxes and continuation; would a listener hum them afterwards? Compare with the great scherzo and trio tunes of Beethoven, Brahms, Dvořák, Tchaikovsky and Bruckner.
2. Harmony and voice leading: progressions, chromatic lines, pedals, inner voices, dissonance treatment, the key plan (E minor – G – G minor/D minor/A minor – F (Neapolitan) – V – E minor; trio E major – C♯ minor – B – E), the hemiola cadences, the plagal close with the motto's tail, and the rule of no C-major triad.
3. Form, pacing and proportion: scherzo–trio–scherzo–coda; section lengths and repeats, the development and the F-major episode, the link and the retransition, the shortened reprise, the two-stage coda; whether the movement keeps moving and whether its length (about 5 minutes) is right between the Adagio and the finale. Compare with corpus scherzi.
4. Orchestration: balance, doubling, register, colour, each section's use, the tuttis, string-free passages, the harp, the timpani as a melodic voice, the trio's colours. Sources: Rimsky-Korsakov and Adler.
5. Engraving and notation (Gould's *Behind Bars*, MOLA): dynamics, hairpins, slurs, beaming and rests in fast 3/4, pizz./arco, part-combining labels, rehearsal and tempo marks, system and page layout, the page count. Inspect many pages of the PDF.
6. Playability, instrument by instrument: ranges, breathing, bowing at speed, spiccato, pizz./arco changes, harp pedalling, timpani tuning and retuning, horn stopping and mutes, brass endurance.
7. Texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, the fugato and its countersubject, the stretto ostinato, heterophony, call and response, the bass line, dialogue between groups.
8. Expression: dynamics, articulation (staccato, spiccato, accents), phrasing slurs, tempo changes (poco rit., accelerando, Più mosso, Meno mosso), and expressive text.
9. Holistic comparison with one specific master scherzo, a different one each time this lens comes up (critics/MASTERS.txt). Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Also compare with the version 10 cycles earlier and flag anything that got worse.
10. Cyclic design and the arc of four movements: the start after movement II (G♯ = A♭), the quotations of the motto, what is kept for the finale, the link into the finale, the scherzo's place between the Adagio and the finale, and how its themes could serve the finale's rework.
