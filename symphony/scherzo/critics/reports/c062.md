## Findings

### 1. 241–245: the return to the scherzo has no augmented sixth, and the movement has none at all (applied)

**Bars.** In v62, 243–245 is iv7 (bass A, Hn 1 G4, Vla E4), then iv6 at 244 (Vc C3+A3, Cb C2, both pizz.), then V at 245.

**Problem.** A&S PDF 503: augmented sixths "often occur just before important structural points; composers can use them to signal the beginning or end of a phase in the tonal movement or the form." The retransition from the trio to the scherzo is the movement's largest seam.

- A&S PDF 516–517 (sec. 17, Ex. 29-28a) gives the standard plan for such a return. The IV turns into an augmented sixth through a chromaticized voice exchange and goes to the home dominant.
- PDF 504 (Ex. 29-3) shows that the Phrygian iv6–V the score already has at 244 becomes an augmented sixth by adding ♯4̂ as a passing tone.
- The score stops one step short. A beat-by-beat scan of v62 finds no augmented-sixth chord anywhere in 376 bars. Dvořák's E-minor scherzo uses one at its coda's climax.

**Constraint.** The German sixth of E is C–E–G–A♯. Its C–E–G breaks the no-C-major rule, so only the Italian sixth (C–E–A♯) and the French sixth (C–E–F♯–A♯) are available. PDF 505 recommends the Italian sixth for thin textures. PDF 507 gives the French sixth 2̂ as a common tone with V and calls it "a very effective neighbouring chord of V."

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-062/work/sections/retr.ily).** I chose the French sixth, because Hn 1's held G (the iv7's seventh at 243) can then resolve down by step to F♯ instead of dropping out. The cellos' A becomes A♯ against the bass's C: the voice exchange of Ex. 29-14.

### Considered and left unchanged (decided, with reasons from the reading)

- **84–88, the Neapolitan.** The reading supports every choice here:
  - ♭II6 over the doubled 4̂ (PDF 484), then VII°4/3 over the held A (PDF 487, Ex. 28-9), then the dominant pedal;
  - tonic-scale figuration (B♮) on the way out (PDF 493).
  - F→F♯ in Hn 4 is the inner-voice "possible" variant (Ex. 28-3d). A&S would allow F→D♯ with the cross relation used freely (PDF 484). In a pp fourth horn the diminished third would not be heard, so undoing c042 gains nothing.
- **Trio 201–203: A, then A minor, then B.** This is the first half of Ex. 29-6: ♭6̂ comes in through mixture. An Italian sixth by voice exchange (bass A–C–B, A♯ in Bsn 1, Hn 4 and Vln I) would work. It would give the trio's return (209) and the scherzo's return (253) the same bass and the same chord, and the scherzo's return should keep that sound to itself (PDF 503: the chord signals a structural boundary). I keep the trio's plain mixture.
- **Reprise 297–301: A major ff, then B7.** Ex. 29-28 would put an augmented sixth between them. Bar 300 is the decaying ff chord. At 2. = 88 the only room is beat 3, about a quarter of a second, with every wind voice cut short. The A major is heard as V of D after the fugato, so the step to B7 reads as a sideways shift, not as a weak IV–V. Not changed.
- **Coda 331, iv6.** An augmented sixth needs A♯ in the motive-x ostinato in four string parts (E–A♯ tritones, C–A♯ in Vln I). That would rewrite c032's tenths design. Not changed.
- **Trio codetta 235.** Ex. 29-36 (an augmented sixth on ♭2̂ before a final major tonic) would need F under Hn 3·4's E pedal, a minor ninth. c042's iv already varies the third plagal statement and prepares 241. Not changed.
- **86, Cl 1 B♭4.** By sec. 20 the E-minor chromatic would be A♯. Over the sounding F-major chord the lowered-descending spelling of sec. 19 reads better. A&S PDF 496 says the principles can conflict. Not changed.
