## Findings

Under the reading, the musical design holds. Every modulation in the movement is one of Unit 32's types, and the reason for each is motivic:
- F♮/F♯ at 86–87.
- G/G♯ at 7, 145, 241 and 343.
- A as common tone with C♯→C at 72–73.
- B→B♭ into the motto tail on G at 57 and 289.
- K.542's B–A♯–A–G♯ at 205–209 and 357–360.

I found no musical change that the reading supports and the protections allow. The two findings are coherence mismatches between the documents and the score. BRIEF (coherence section) requires lens-10 critics to report these.

## Considered and kept

- **288–289, E minor → G minor without the G-major stage of 56–57.** A&S PDF 596–597 allows direct confrontation when the piece needs it. Here the inflection B→B♭ runs straight into the subject B♭–A–A♭–G, which is the motto tail on G. That is the Hammerklavier case (PDF 585: the abrupt form is kept because it is motivic). c103's protection stands.
- **339–341, E minor ff, G.P., A major p.** The inflections C♮ (N6/4, 331) → C♯ (Hn 1 at 341) and G♮ (Hn 1 G4 at 339) → G♯ (Hn 1 G♯4 at 343) stay in one player at one pitch. This is the abrupt but motivic juxtaposition of PDF 584–585.
- **The exposition's key areas G (45) – F (73) – E (105).** They state the tail G–F♯–F–E in enlarged form without its F♯ as a key area. The F♯ comes as the inner inflection F→F♯ (86–87) and as V's fifth. A&S PDF 600 calls such enlargements typical, not required. The key plan is protected (c083).
- **Note for the finale only.** A&S PDF 594 says III♯ "moves on to V, usually through a passing IV". The finale's E-major chord at 243 goes to V through the cadential 6/4. That is the finale's choice; I leave it to its critics.
