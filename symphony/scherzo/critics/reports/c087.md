## Findings

### 1. 209–224: the cellos' forte countermelody has no wind partner, while Bsn 2 duplicates the basses in the cellos' register (applied)

**Problem.** In T's forte return the cellos play their countermelody (B2–A3, f). Every other strand is doubled: the tune by Vln II + Cl 1 (and Fl 1 from 217), the harmony by Hn 1·2 + Bsn 1, the bass by the arco basses + harp left hand. Bsn 2 holds the basses' notes an octave higher (E3, B2, A2, F♯2…), so it adds nothing to the harmony. It also lies in the cellos' own register and crosses their line: it is in unison with the cellos at 209 (E3) and 216 (B2), and sits above them at 213–215.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76–77 names "'Cellos + Fag." among the best unison pairs. Its purposes are "a new timbre of definite colour" and "to strengthen the resonance of the strings". The strings predominate when the two are of equal power, and he gives "a bassoon with the 'cellos" as the example. PDF 78–79: a low melody goes to the cellos doubled by a bassoon over undoubled basses. The score already uses this pair for the bass line at 121 and 277. The forte countermelody at 209 is the one cello melody that lacks it. c071 rejected Hn 4 here because of the horn's weak low octave and to save the horn–cello colour for 281. The bassoon is strong in this register, and Bsn 1 keeps the countermelody's colour as a solo for 345–348, a fourth higher (c077).

**Fix (tC.ily).** Bsn 2 plays the cellos' line in unison, in four-bar wind slurs (c078's rule), with a breath after 216 where the horns also breathe. Both bassoons are mf, a degree under the cellos' f, as c067 set for doubling winds. They use the same hairpins because they share a staff.
```
tCBsnTwo = { \partCombineAutomatic b,4(\mf e2 | gis2. | fis2 a4 | gis2 fis4) | e4( cis2~ | cis4 a,2 | ais,2 b,4 | dis2 b,4) \breathe | e4(\< gis2~ | gis4 e2 | a2\f\> gis4 | fis2 cis4) | b,2(\mf cis4 | a,4 b,2 | gis,2.~\> | gis,2)\! r4 | R2.*16 | }
tCBsnOne = { \partCombineAutomatic e'2.\mf | e'2. | dis'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | dis'2 r4 | cis'2.\< | b2. | a2.\f\> | a2. | b2.\mf | a2. | gis2.\> | gis2\! r4 | ... (233 onward unchanged)
```
Bsn 1's notes and c014's four-part wind harmony are unchanged. The wind chord now stands on the bassoon's moving line, and the true bass stays in the basses and harp an octave or more below. No harmony is lost, because Bsn 2's old notes were the basses' notes.

### 2. 333–338: the lowest octave of the coda's summing-up statement is violas alone under four ff horns (applied)

**Problem.** At 333–335 theme A's head is in three string octaves: Vln I E6, Vln II E5, Vla E4. Two of them are reinforced by woodwinds (Fl 1 on E6; Ob 1 and Cl 1 on E5). The violas' E4 octave has no partner. It lies in exactly the register of the horns' ff chord (Hn 2 G4, Hn 3 E4, Hn 4 B3) and of Tpt 2's motive x on E4. c084 judged it covered and kept it only for attack. The falling line at 336–338 (violas B4–A4–F♯4–D♯4, from c071) sits under Tpt 1's B4–A4 and Hn 1's B4–C5–B4 at fff. Meanwhile the cor anglais, which doubled this same lower octave in both tuttis (105–120, 305–320; c021), has rested since 323 and stays silent to the end.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76: a melody over three octaves is hard to balance. PDF 79–80: "Violas (or 'Cellos) + Cl. (Eng. horn) + Horn" is one of the most natural unisons of the three groups, "used for preference in loud passages", and the woodwind gives "a fuller and more evenly blended tone". PDF 77–78 with Ex. 87 (*Kashtchei*, PDF 261, viewed): the melody is ff in octaves over held brass and timpani, and the lowest octave carries the most instruments: violas, cellos, 2 Cl, C.a. and Fag. PDF 76: a wind in unison with a string group "increases the resonance of the latter and amplifies its tone".

**Fix (coda.ily, new variable; parts.ily already calls `codaCorAnglais`).**
```
codaCorAnglais = { R2.*8 | e'4-.\ff b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->( fis'8 f'8 | e'4-.) b8-. b-. g'4-. | b'2.->\fff | a'2-> fis'4->~ | fis'4 dis'2-> | e'4-> r4 r4 | R2. | R2.*36 | }
```
The woodwinds now have the head in three octaves, as the strings do, and the lowest octave has a double reed in it, as at 105 and 305. The violas stay as c034 and c084 left them. The C.a.'s range here is B3–B4. The last stroke at 339 is E4, the chord root, which Hn 2 also plays.
