## Findings

No finding requires a change this cycle. I checked each statement of theme A, the second idea, T, the harp head and the coda's T bar by bar against the 22 principles above. The scans found only the cases below, and each one is kept for a reason the reading itself gives. I propose no theme change.

### 1. Bars 21–24 (and 257–264): theme A's peak C6 in Vln I alone, with no lower octave (considered, kept)

**Rule.** The scan lists 18, 21, 23–24 and 26 (G5–C6) as violin notes above F♯5 with nothing an octave below. By PDF 58–59 and Note I (PDF 62), a melody that high is normally doubled at the octave.

**Why it is kept.**
- Rimsky gives the octave rule for "broad and expressive phrases" (PDF 59). The high violin goes thin and isolated when it has to sustain a cantabile.
- Theme A at 13–28 is *p leggiero* staccato, and C6 is its only minim. This is Ex. 1's case (PDF 173–174): Vln I *p grazioso* alone over off-beat Vln II, violas and pizz. bass, which Rimsky prints as his model of a single-section violin melody.
- An octave below in Vln II would take Vln II out of the off-beat layer that row 4 describes.
- The reprise (257–264) already has Fl 1's heterophony on top, and the tutti (113–116) has the wind octaves.

No change.

### 2. Bars 198–200: the trio call at C♯6–G♯5 in Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1, with no lower octave (considered, kept)

**Rule.** At *f*/*mf* the call has its lower octave only in bar 197 (Ob 1). From 198 it is three instruments at one pitch, a case for PDF 58–59.

**Why it is kept.**
- The register an octave below (C♯5–G♯4) holds T's first phrase in Vln II, violas and Cl 1. Doubling the call there would cross the tune in the same colour, which c107 removed.
- Rimsky's first means of relief, position (PDF 54), already holds: the call is the top voice and T is the middle one, in distinct rhythms.
- C♯6–G♯5 lies just above F♯5, not at the extreme top that Note I means.
- The passage is protected (c117, c131).

No change.

### 3. Bars 105–120 and 305–320: tutti theme A switches between Vln I/II unison and octaves (considered, kept)

The violins play in unison at 105–106 and 109–112. They move to octaves where the theme's low dips would fall under G3 or where it climbs (107–108, 113–120). This is Rimsky's own pairing: unison for power with wind doubling (PDF 57, here Ob 1, Cl 2 and C.a.), and octaves for the high register (PDF 58). It also follows PDF 60, which uses an octave layout when the lower part would go below the open G. c010 protects the octave change on the head's leap. No change.

### 4. Bars 57–68 and 289–296: the fugato heads in single string sections (considered, kept)

Each head enters in one section alone, *pp*–*p*: Vc, then Vla, then Vln II. PDF 54 says each section except the basses can carry a melody alone. PDF 55 says violas suit "short characteristic phrases", which a four-bar head is. The cellos open the fugato with a figure rather than a cantabile (row 6), but Rimsky writes "more often", and Dvořák and Beethoven open their scherzo fugatos in the low strings. The strings-only fugato is protected (c254, c267, c279). No change.

### 5. Bars 341–356: the coda's T (considered, kept)

The coda states T in rising registers: Hn 3 (C♯4, 341), then Ob 1 (D5, 345), then Vln I with Vln II and Cl 1 an octave below (C♯6/C♯5, 349). The violin phrase follows rows 11 and 18. Each new group enters a register higher, which is c150's reading of PDF 128. Nothing in ch. II's string section argues against this. c191 already considered the one-desk upper octave (Ex. 24, 26) and rejected it. No change.
