## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Best candidate, built and rejected: Fl 1 at 253 to mp, matching Vln I
- **Bars:** 253–268 (reprise of theme A, letter Q).
- **Problem considered:** this is the one place in the score where two parts playing the same theme at the same moment print different levels: Vln I mp leggiero, Fl 1 p leggiero an octave above in heterophony. MOLA's Ex. 6 (PDF 11) shows the editor adding bracketed "[sf]" so that every part playing a figure carries the same mark, and PDF 8 asks for the vertical line to be proofread as carefully as the horizontal. Of all the points this reading raises, this is the one a listener would hear: it changes the balance of the reprise's first 16 bars.
- **Test:** `dAFluteOne = { e'''4-.\mp^\markup \italic "leggiero" ...` (was `\p`). The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, the same analyser parallels, theme record 52/52.
- **Why it makes the music worse:** the two parts are not one line in unison. The flute plays an ornamented variant an octave above, at E6–A6, which is the flute's loud third octave. The violins carry the theme, and that theme is the recorded statement the finale quotes (themecheck VlnOne 254). c069 set the violins one level above the flute for that reason (Adler II PDF 184: in piercing registers the principal voice is marked louder than subsidiary ones), and c036 kept the flute at p. MOLA's bracketed marks apply to parts that double a figure, as in Ex. 6, where flutes, oboes and clarinets play the same crotchets. They do not apply to a principal voice and its descant. Raising the flute would put the heterophony in front of the theme. Not kept; dA.ily is back to v405 and the rebuild is identical to v405.

### 2. Winds a level under the strings when they double a string tune (45, 125, 179–181, 197–200): no change
MOLA PDF 8's vertical proofreading flags these as different levels. They follow one rule throughout: winds doubling a string melody print one level under it (45 Fl/Ob mf under Vln f; 125 Fl/Ob pp under Vln p; 179 Fl/Ob mp under Vln I mf; 197 the same, set by c404). Each is a deliberate balance. None is a slip of the kind Ex. 6's brackets correct.

### 3. Strings sfp at 301 against winds p: no change
Ex. 6 shows the same accent in every part. At 301 only the strings have sfp, coming off their ff chord at 300. The winds, horns and timpani enter p under them. The strings sound the B pedal's attack, and the winds start the 301–304 crescendo from below. The two marks do different jobs, and the exposition at 101 uses a different start level on purpose (c393).

### 4. Brass f under the fff at 336: no change
This is the largest dynamic difference on any page. BRIEF.md (Dan, c123) rules out louder brass entries, and the f with doubling is the intended weight.

### 5. Harp pedal diagrams in the score (73, 141, 189–190, 225, 233): no change
MOLA PDF 5 says pedalling should not be printed, and it is stripped from the harp part. In the score the diagrams help the conductor check the harp's spelling (c026, c045), and the rule is written for parts.

### 6. Rehearsal letters inside the 24-bar spans M 185–208 and Q 253–276: no change
MOLA PDF 4 asks for letters at landmarks. c315's reason still holds: every bar is numbered, and a new letter would rename N–W across the score, the parts, the cues and the notes for the other movements.

No further findings. Every other principle in the table is met. v405 is the cleanest engraving I have seen in this series, and this reading supports no score change.
