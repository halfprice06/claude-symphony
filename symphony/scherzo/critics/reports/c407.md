## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 169–184: the cellos' T has the same background rhythm as the clarinet's T at 153–168, and the background has a gap on every downbeat. Applied.

**Problem.** At 153–168 the harp plays off-beat crotchet chords (r4 x x) under Cl 1's T. At 169–184 the cellos take T and Vln II plays off-beat louré dyads (r4 x x), B4–E5. That is the same rhythm, register and density as the harp before it. The only thing that changes between the two statements is the colour of the off-beats. The rhythm is also close to T's own pacing: T moves minim–crotchet, so its third beat sounds together with the background's second chord in most bars. Every downbeat has only the pizz. bass under the tune's attack, so the texture stops and starts in every bar.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135, on the same layout (cello tune, other strings as background): "Notice the radical rhythmic and expressive difference between the foreground and the background elements. The swirling accompaniment is repetitious and simple. It serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody … It is also important to notice how the arpeggiation is dovetailed to create extreme smoothness without any pauses in the texture." The image of Ex. V-7 (PDF 132–133) shows what Brahms writes: after the beat, a short rising arpeggio handed between Vln I, Vln II and violas, in the middle register over a pizz. bass. PDF 139 (Ex. V-16, Haydn 103) adds that a second statement is set off by "melodizing" the inner harmony. PDF 158–159 (Ex. V-34) lists "sustained harmony notes plus arpeggiated violin figures" as the standard accompaniment for a sustained tune. Two of Adler's points are therefore missing at 169–184: the rhythmic contrast and the dovetailing. Without them the second statement gives the listener only a new instrument on the tune.

**Fix (applied; tA.ily `tAVlnTwo`, comment `% c407`).** Vln II, not divided, plays one slurred bar-long arpeggio pp dolce. It starts on the second quaver of each bar, so the downbeat stays with the pizz. bass and the tune's attack, and it fills the tune's minims and its held A. It uses the same chord tones as the old dyads, B4–G♯5 (F♯4 at 171 and 182), above the cellos' T and the violas' doubling (which reach A4 at 178–179):

The harmony is unchanged. The IV on beat 3 of 169 (E–C♯ under T's A), the E7 on beat 3 of 172 (D5), the F♯7 at 175 and the V7's seventh E5 on beat 3 of 182 all stay where c344 put them. The quaver after the bar line at 172 is B4, not A or G♯, so nothing doubles the held 4–3 suspension (the c331/c398 rule). At 178 beat 3 the B4–E5 over T's held A4 is the same rub the old dyad had. Fl 1's answers at 172, 176 and 183–184 sit an octave or more above the figure, and their quaver–quaver–crotchet rhythm stays distinct from the even quavers. The figure is a bowed arpeggio of six quavers a bar at 2. = 76, one bow a bar. It is not tremolo, so the finale's tremolo stays unused here. It is a different figure from the scherzo's own quaver arpeggios at 95–100, which are crotchets, and the trio return at 209 still brings the tune to the violins with louré viola off-beats, so the three statements now have three different backgrounds. I also removed the "unis." on Vln II at 193 in tB.ily, because Vln II is no longer divided before it.

This does not undo an earlier fix. c344 chose the register (above the cellos, B4–E5), c331 chose the notes around the held A, and c349 gave the flute its answers. All three are kept. What is replaced is the rhythm, which no cycle gave a reason for: c344 moved the violas' old off-beat dyads up an octave with their rhythm unchanged. The do-not-touch item "trio layering 169–184" (c007, c107) protects the order of the layers (tune below, background above, flute answers, pizz. bass), and that order is unchanged.

Why this and not "no change": cycles 400–406 changed almost nothing a listener would notice. This change gives the trio's central statement a moving middle-register layer for 16 bars, at the point where the plan says the trio gets its warmth from the cellos. It is the one place where the movement follows Adler's lead example in the foreground and not in the background.

### 2. Bars 13–28: theme A's consequent (21–28) keeps the antecedent's texture. Not applied.

Adler PDF 136 (V-12, V-13) and 154–155 (V-30) recommend a change of texture or doubling between antecedent and consequent. At 13–28 the off-beats in Vln II and violas and the pizz. downbeats are the same throughout. I tested nothing here because c259 keeps "the light opening and the four-stage build of theme A". The next stage (29–36, call and response between winds and strings) already gives the change of texture Adler asks for, one phrase later. The off-beats also stop at 20 and 28 for the timpani's answer, which is Adler's V-13 device. Kept.

### 3. Bars 89–100: the retransition's background (violas' repeated B, Vln II's B–F♯–D♯ crotchets) is in the same crotchet pulse as the theme head in Vln I at 95 and 98. Not applied.

Adler PDF 144 asks for a rhythmic difference between theme and countertheme. Here the head's quavers on beat 2 are the only quavers in the strings, so the head still comes through. c237 already gave Vln II contrary arpeggios at 99–100. Kept.

### 4. Fugato 57–68 and stretto 69–72: checked against PDF 143–145. No change.

The subject (staccato, with motive x's quavers), the countersubject (legato crotchets) and the third voice (minims) differ in rhythm and articulation. The entries go low to high, the strings play alone, and the passage ends in a homophonic ff climax at 72, which matches V-21, V-22 and V-23. c307's viola fix at 70/298 holds.
