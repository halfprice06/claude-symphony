## Findings

### 1. Bars 127–128 and 283–284: the E-minor second idea reaches its hemiola cadence without an applied chord, and its leading tone leaps away in the top line (applied in /tmp/critic-412/work)

- **Problem.** At 127.3–128.1 the tune holds D♯6 (Fl 1, Vln I) and D♯5 (Ob 1, Vln II) over V4/3. At 128.2 all four leap down to B over i6, then go through a C♯ neighbour quaver back to B at 129. The leading tone is never resolved in its register: the only E at 128.2 is E4 (violas, Hn 2), and it comes from B3. A&S PDF 406–407 says a transferred resolution works only when the tone of resolution is in the next lower voice and in the same register. Here the next lower voice is the same line an octave down, and it leaps too. The harmony is also static at the point of the crescendo. i6 → i6/4 is one chord with the bass leaping G→B, so the cadence 129–131 arrives with no drive.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 424 (§6): "Perhaps the most important function of chords applied to V is to emphasize dominant harmony (usually at a cadence) by means of a rising half-step progression in the bass (♯4–5)… possibly embellished by 6/4" (Ex. 25-12a: IV – VII°7 of V – V). PDF 422 (§4): chords applied to V "significantly increase their drive to the dominant". PDF 419 and 439: an applied chord can connect two statements of its own tonic; here the two statements are V4/3 at 127.3 and V6/4–5/3 at 129–130. PDF 425 (Ex. 25-14): VII°7 of V resolved through a 6/4 avoids the 5ths and the doubled leading tone. PDF 420 (Ex. 25-4): a cross relation between an inner voice and the bass is acceptable when the bass has the applied leading tone. The movement's own G-major statement does exactly this at 52: V6/5 of V (A7/C♯, bass C♯→D) under F♯6–E6–D6, with the quaver anticipating the 6/4's D. The E-minor statement is the only one of the three with no applied chord before its 6/4, and the only one whose tune does not descend by step into the hemiola. The score already uses the same chord the same way at 88→89 (A♯°7 → i6/4).
- **Fix (applied).** Bar 128 (sC.ily) and bar 284 (dA.ily), second minim: VII°7 of V, A♯–C♯–E–G over the bass A♯, into i6/4. The tune becomes D♯–C♯–B, the transposition of the G-major F♯–E–D. The leading tone now moves by step inside an extended dominant and resolves at 131 as before.

### 2. Bars 164, 180, 220, 352: T's cadence ii6/5 → I6/4 without ♯4 (kept)
- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-12c (PDF 424): II6/5 – V6/5 of V – V. Here that would put A♯ in the bass on beat 3 of T's bar 12.
- **Why kept.** T's bar 12 has A on beat 3 in the tune (C♯–B–A). A♯ under it would be a chromatic clash in one chord, not a cross relation that A&S allow (PDF 419–420). T's notes are not worth changing for this. The trio already has ♯4 in the bass at 214–216.

### 3. Bars 201–203: IV – iv – V in the trio's middle section (kept)
- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-12a, IV – VII°7 of V – V.
- **Why kept.** It would take away the C♮ of the mixture line A4–C5–B4, which c070/c177/c402 kept on purpose. The ♯4 rise comes 13 bars later at 214–216.

### 4. Bars 221–225: no deceptive cadence on an applied chord before the codetta (kept)
- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-16 (PDF 427) would delay T's last cadence.
- **Why kept.** It needs either an added bar, which would move every protected bar number after 224 (357–360, 363–371, the finale's records), or a changed last note of T. The codetta relay 225–236 is protected.

### 5. Bars 329–332: applied sevenths on the falling bass E–D–C–B (kept)
- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-31 (PDF 434).
- **Why kept.** G7 before the bass C would form a C-major triad, which the cyclic rule forbids. The Fr+6 on the C at 331 already supplies A♯ → B, and F at 331 is protected for finale IV 272.

### 6. Bar 280→281: the cellos leave the temporary leading tone G♯2 by leap to C4 (kept)
- **Check.** The scan flagged this V6/5 of iv → iv (c383).
- **Why kept.** The bass line resolves: Bsn 2 G♯2→A2, and Cb and Cbsn enter on A. The cellos leave to start the tenor octave of the tune. A&S PDF 419 asks for normal voice leading in the bass line, and it has it.

### 7. Bars 89–100: apparent 7ths over the B pedal (kept)
- **Candidate.** Ex. 24-22/24-23 (PDF 413–414): neighbouring apparent chords over a stationary bass could colour the twelve bars of i6/4–V.
- **Why kept.** The ritmo di tre battute is bare by design: timpani and the rising heads (c019, c237). Chords would thicken it before the first tutti.
