## Findings

### 1. T's third phrase has no climax (162-163; 178-179; 218-219; 358-359). Applied. THEME CHANGE.
Problem: T has 16 bars in three phrases. Bars 1-3, 5-7 and 9-11 have the same rhythm (minim-crotchet, minim-crotchet, dotted crotchet-quaver-crotchet), and each phrase starts one step higher (G♯, A, C♯). The top note, G♯5 in bar 10, is a crotchet on the weak third beat, and the line goes straight down from it. Nothing in the tune is longer or higher than what came before, so it has no point to remember it by. Cycle 1 left this open.
Evidence: Dvořák 9/iii trio (above), where the last phrase breaks the pattern with the tune's only top note. Rimsky (PDF 54) asks that the melody "stand out in relief". Here the melody is already on top of the texture, so the only remaining means is the line itself: a longer and higher note.
Fix (applied): bar 10 becomes crotchet-minim, holding G♯5 for two beats, and bar 11 rises to A5, the tune's new and only peak. A5 is the seventh of the B7 chord already under bar 11, it falls at once to G♯ and F♯, and bar 11 keeps the dotted rhythm of bars 3 and 7.
- Clarinet octave: `e''2 gis''4) | fis''4.( e''8 dis''4)` → `e''4 gis''2) | a''4.( gis''8 fis''4)` in tAClOne, tCClOne and tCVlnTwo.
- An octave up: `e'''4 gis'''2) | a'''4.( gis'''8 fis'''4)` in tAVlnOne, tCVlnOne, tCFluteOne, codaVlnOne and codaFluteOne.
- tAFluteOne bar 179 (heterophony): `a'''4.( gis'''8 fis'''4)`. The flute joins the violins at the peak.
- To avoid doubling the seventh: the cellos' countermelody in bar 11 goes `a2( fis4` → `fis2( dis4` (tAVc 179, tCVc 219), so D♯3 rises to E3 under the A chord, and Hn 1 at 219 goes `a'2.` → `b'2.` (tCHnOne).
- The violins' new peak is A6, under the B6 that stays only at 344. The cadence bars 12-16 are unchanged.

### 2. T's second statement in Vln I has no lower octave (169-184). Applied.
Problem: Vln I plays T at G♯5-A6 with Fl 1 heterophony at the same pitch. Under it there are only pp off-beats (Vln II B4, violas E4) and the cellos' line below G♯3, so the tune hangs more than an octave above everything else.
Evidence: Note I (PDF 62) says melodies above the middle of the 5th octave are doubled an octave below. That holds whether the 5th octave is read as C5-B5 or C6-B6, because bars 170-171 and 178-179 reach E6-A6. PDF 59 says high violin melody is "too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves". Ex. 22 and 23 (PDF 198-199) are soft dolce espressivo tunes in Vln I and II in octaves. The score already does this at 209 (cycle 9) and 45 and 121.
Fix (applied): tAVlnTwo bars 169-184 play T (with the change from finding 1) at the clarinet's octave, `gis'2(\mp^\markup \italic "espressivo" a'4 | ...`. tAVla becomes `div.`, holding both off-beat notes below the tune: `r4 <b e'>4--\pp ... <a dis'> <b e'> <a e'> <a fis'> <ais e'> <b dis'> <gis e'> <b e'> <a dis'> <a e'> <b e'> <a dis'> <gis e'> <gis e'>`. This is the old Vln II note an octave lower with the old viola note, so the harmony is unchanged and the viola's top note never rises above the tune (top F♯4, tune never below G♯4). This still differs from 209, which is f with clarinet and horns.

### 3. The coda's return of T has the same gap (357-364). Applied.

### 4. The horns hammer the tail's goal note under the chromatic tail (106, 118; 310, 322). Applied.
Problem: in the tutti theme A, Hn 1 (E5) and Hn 4 (E4) play an accented E on beat 1 and a staccato E on beat 3 of every bar. In bars 2 and 14 of the theme, beat 3 falls on the quavers F♯5-F5 of the motto's tail G-F♯-F-E. The f horn E5 sounds the tail's goal under its last two notes and rubs a semitone against F5 in the same octave. The tail, the motto quotation the movement depends on, is blurred exactly where the listener should hear it.
Evidence: PDF 54: the melody "should always stand out in relief". A loud accompaniment note in the melody's own register, on the melody's goal pitch, works against that. The horn stays on E in other bars, where the melody holds a chord tone.
Fix (applied): in sCHnOne, sCHnFour, dCHnOne and dCHnFour, bars 2 and 14 of the section go `e''4-> r4 e''4-.` → `e''4-> r4 r4` (`e'4-> r4 r4` in Hn 4). Hn 2's B4 stays. The trumpets already rest in those bars.

### 5. The trio's cello answer lies too low to be heard as a melody (189-192). Applied.
Problem: Ob 1 calls at E5-G♯4 (185). The cellos answer "a step lower" but two octaves down, D♯3-F♯2, on the C and G strings, under the harp's chords (B3-F♯4) and only just above the pizz. B1. The cor anglais doubles at D♯4, but it is the weaker partner.
Evidence: PDF 54 says a melody in the low register stands out less. PDF 56 says cello cantabile is usually laid out for the A string, "wonderfully rich 'chest' quality". PDF 57-58 (Ex. 15, 17) says cor anglais added to cellos in unison "makes no essential difference", and the cellos stand out.
Fix (applied): tBVc bars 189-192 move up an octave, in unison with the cor anglais: `dis'2(\p cis'4 | b2 fis4) | gis4.( a8 b4) | fis2.`. The answer is now one octave and a step below the call, on the A and D strings. The bass at 193 (`fis,2.`) is unchanged.
