## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hn 1 holds 20 bars without a breath at the end (365–384)
Problem: Hn 1 slurs the motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ (365–368) and then holds G♯4 tied through 384, under a rit. to ♩ = 140 and a final fermata: about 21 s of pp with no place to breathe. Adler's first premise about the strings (PDF 25) is the contrast: "a wind or brass player usually needs to stop from time to time to breathe." A horn at pp can hold 10–12 s safely; 21 s at the quietest dynamic, ending on the movement's last sound (the pivot G♯ into the finale), invites a shaky or cut-off final note. The Vln I G♯5 (377–384, ppp, about 11 s with the rit. and fermata) is one unslurred tied note; Adler PDF 40 (Ex. II-25) shows the remedy for a long line: stagger the bows across the section.
Fix (applied): Hn 3 takes G♯4 pp under Hn 1 at 373–377, Hn 1 breathes at 375 and comes back pp at 376, so Hn 1 still holds the last G♯ 376–384 (do-not-touch kept: the last sound is still Hn 1 and Vln I on G♯; the motto tail 365–368 is unchanged).
```
codaHnOne: ... b'2.(\p | ais'2. | a'2. | gis'2.)~ | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4 | R2. | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ ×7 | gis'2. | }
codaHnThree: ... e'4-> r4 r4 | R2. | R2.*24 | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4 | R2.*7 | }
codaVlnOne (377): gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "change bow freely"
```

### 2. Arco→pizz. in the basses without time (Cb 204→205; Vc 224→225)
Problem: Cb holds arco B1 through 204 and plucks B1 on the downbeat of 205 with no rest at all. Vc holds G♯2 arco through beat 2 of 224 and plucks at 225 after one crotchet (0.26 s at ♩ = 228). Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck … There are instances … when no time is provided … but these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible"; the change is easiest when the last arco note is an up-bow, which brings the hand to the frog.
Fix (applied): Cb 204 `b,,2\upbow r4` (Vc and Vla keep the B; Cb loses only the last crotchet); Vc 223 `gis,2.~\upbow`. The trio's arco→pizz. now matches Brahms's own practice (Adler Ex. II-55).

### 3. Pizz.→arco in one crotchet (Vln II, Vla 276→277)
Problem: in the reprise (257–276) Vln II and violas play the off-beats pizz.; the last pluck is beat 3 of 276 and the arco f off-beats begin on beat 2 of 277: one crotchet, 0.23 s. Adler PDF 54 singles out this direction: "the return from pizzicato to arco … is a bit more awkward … because the bow must be rearranged into playing position." Everywhere else in the score (Vc/Cb at 45, 101, 169, 193, 281) the composer allows two crotchets; 277 is the one place below that standard. (The arco at 277 is cycle 3's accepted fix; this keeps it.)
Fix (applied): drop the last pluck of 276: Vln II `r4 a'4 r4`, Vla `r4 fis4 r4` (dA.ily bar 20). The B in the bass and winds still carries the harmony.

### 4. The second idea's Vln I slur is a four-bar phrase slur at f (45–52, 121–128, 281–288)
Problem: Vln I plays the second idea in the sixth octave (up to A6) forte, with one slur over four bars (eight notes, 2.7 s per bow). Vln II, violas and cellos play the same hemiola in two-bar slurs. Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow"; PDF 39 (Mendelssohn 4, Ex. II-22): at forte "only six notes on a bow." A four-bar bow at f high on the E string forces the leader to thin the tone or break the slur at random, and the two violin sections would bow differently.
Fix (applied, Vln I only; flutes and oboes keep their breath slurs):
```
sA 45–52: d'''2->(\f b''4~ | b''4 g'''2) | fis'''2( e'''4~ | e'''4 d'''2) | e'''2->( cis'''4~ | cis'''4 a'''2) | g'''2( fis'''4~ | fis'''4 e'''2) |
sC 121–128 and dA 281–288: b''2->(\f g''4~ | g''4 e'''2) | dis'''2( cis'''4~ | cis'''4 b''2) | c'''2->( a''4~ | a''4 fis'''2) | e'''2( dis'''4~ | dis'''4 b''2) |
```
The two-bar bows coincide with the hemiola minims and with Vln II. The themes' notes and rhythms are unchanged (themecheck: 43/43).

### 5. Harp: B♯ at 188 cannot be pedalled
Problem: tB 188 harp RH `<gis bis dis'>` (beats 2–3), then 189 LH B1 and RH B on beat 1. B♯ and B♮ are the same string: the B pedal would have to move from sharp to natural between beat 3 of 188 and the downbeat of 189, on a ringing string. The harp carries the B♯ alone in that bar (only Ob 1, Cb and the harp play), so the chord cannot simply drop it. The C pedal is free in 188 (C♯ was last used on beat 3 of 187 and is next needed at 192).
Fix (applied): spell it C♮ in the harp: `r4 <gis c' dis'>4_\markup \small "C♮" <gis c' dis'>4`; mark "C♯ A♯" at 190 (for 192), "A♮" at 209 (for 211); add pedal settings at the harp's entries: 73 "Ped.: D C B♭ · E F G A", 149 "Ped.: D♯ C♯ B · E F♯ G♯ A". This is the same practice as Adler PDF 54 on pizz./arco: what the player must do with the instrument is written where it happens.

### 6. Bass clarinet B♭1 at 82
Problem: sB bar 26 (82) bass clarinet `bes,,2.` sounds B♭1, written low C, the extension. Adler adler1 PDF 216: "one can reasonably expect every bass clarinet to have a low E♭, but writing all the way to C is still risky, for not all symphony orchestras have instruments with such extensions." The harp LH already has B♭1 in that bar.
Fix (applied): `bes,2.` (B♭2) at 82; the line f–f–g–g–a–d–e–f–d–B♭–g–a stays inside one octave.

### 7. Timpani: soft sticks never cancelled; the G♯ drum is retuned but never struck
Problem: "soft sticks" at 153 is never cancelled, so the f motive x at 289 and the ff of 308–347 read as soft sticks. Adler PDF 53: after any special mode, "one must insert the indication naturale or in modo ordinario … at the point where the player should resume his normal playing attitude." Also the timpanist retunes G→G♯ at 145 and back at 245 (BRIEF: G→G♯ for the trio) but plays only E and B in the trio, so the retune gives nothing; Adler PDF 60 asks for retunings to be stated and timed, which the score does, but a retune should earn its time.
Fix (applied): retr 246 `R2.^\markup \small \italic "hard sticks"` (four bars of rest, in the same rest as the "muta"); tB 188 `gis,4\pp r4 r4` (G♯ root of V of C♯ minor, with harp LH and Cb) and tB 200 `gis,4\f r4 r4` (G♯7 before the C♯-minor return at 201). Both are single strokes on the drum already tuned to G♯.

### 8. Bassoon 1 plays 36 bars without a rest (193–228)
Problem: Bsn 1 has an unbroken chain of dotted minims through tB and tC: 36 bars, about 28 s in the trio tempo, with no notated breath, and the line continues into the p T statement at 233. Adler PDF 25, as in finding 1. A bassoonist will snatch breaths by clipping notes at random, which in this exposed bass line (209–224 is the bass under T's forte return) is audible.
Fix (applied): `fis2 r4` at 196, `b,2 r4` at 216 (phrase end of T's first half), `e2 r4` at 224 (end of T). Vc, Cb and Bsn 2 keep the bass at those points.
