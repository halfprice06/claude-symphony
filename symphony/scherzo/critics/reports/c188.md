## Findings

All three findings are about articulation, not dynamics. Following Dan's rule, none of them adds an accent, an sfz or a louder dynamic to any brass part. They put the brass weight into soft-tongued, joined notes.

### 1. Bars 101–104 and 301–304: the held horn and trombone chords are re-tongued unmarked on every downbeat under the hemiola (applied)

- **Problem.** In the "ritmo di quattro battute" build onto V, the strings, Fl 1, C.a. and the clarinets play the accented hemiola minims (c148). The brass hold the dominant: Hn 1–4 `fis'2.\p\< | fis'2. | fis'2. | fis'2.\ff` (F♯4/D♯4/B3/F♯3), Tbn 1·2 `fis2.\pp\< | fis2. | a2. | a2.\f`, B.tbn `b,2.` four times. They carry no slur, tie or articulation. Under Adler's rule an unslurred note is tongued, so each brass player gives a plain "tu" attack on the downbeats of 102, 103 and 104. That is seven brass parts restriking the bar line at the moment the hemiola's syncopated minims (beat 3 of 101 and 103, held across the bar) need to be heard over it. Everywhere else the score writes held horn harmony as soft-tongued legato (`--(` … `)`, c088): the second idea, the F-major episode, the trio. The build to the first tutti is the one place where the brass weight should grow in one line, and it is notated as four separate attacks.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 (one slur, one breath; hard and soft tonguing must be distinguished in the notation) and PDF 321 (the same for all winds and brass). Ex. X-15 and X-16 (PDF 306–307) show held horn harmony slurred, with swells across the phrase. Breath: four bars at 2. = 88 last 2.7 s, well within one breath at p<ff (PDF 303 gives the middle register as the easiest). Dvořák 9/iii, hornImS bars 1–4 and 41–50: held horn notes are tied, or the horns join the hemiola.
- **Fix (applied).** Slur over the four-bar build, tenuto on each note, dynamics unchanged. In sections/sB.ily:
  `sBHnOne … fis'2.--(\p\< | fis'2.-- | fis'2.-- | fis'2.--\ff) | }`, `sBHnTwo … dis'2.--(\p\< | dis'2.-- | dis'2.-- | dis'2.--\ff) | }`,
  `sBHnThree … b2.--(\p\< | b2.-- | b2.-- | b2.--\ff) \breathe | }`, `sBHnFour … fis2.--(\p\< | fis2.-- | fis2.-- | fis2.--\ff) \breathe | }`,
  `sBTbnOne … fis2.--(\pp\< | fis2.-- | a2.-- | a2.--\f) | }`, `sBTbnTwo … dis2.--(\pp\< | dis2.-- | fis2.-- | fis2.--\f) | }`, `sBBassTbn … b,2.--(\pp\< | b,2.-- | b,2.-- | b,2.--\f) | }`.
  sections/dB.ily (301–304, horns only, since the trombones rest in the reprise): the same four horn lines. I chose slur plus tenuto, not ties, for two reasons. It is the score's own sign for soft horn harmony (c088), and a tie would freeze the MIDI velocity at p through the crescendo. c136's breath after 104 and c097's held brass are kept.

### 2. Bars 331–338 (coda peak): horns and low brass re-tongued unmarked under ff, fff and the hemiola 337–338 (applied)

- **Problem.** The horns hold `b'2.\p\< | b'2.\ff | b'2. | b'2.\< | b'2.\fff | c''2. | b'2.` (Hn 1, likewise Hn 2–4 from 331/332). Trombones 1·2, bass trombone and tuba hold `g2.\f | g2. | g2.` at 333–335. None of these notes is marked. So at the loudest point of the movement every downbeat gets a fresh hard attack at ff/fff from up to eight brass players. At 337–338 the whole orchestra except horns and upper woodwinds plays the coda hemiola (c048), and the horns restrike across it as at 101–104. The trumpets in the same bars already have tenutos (c128: "tenutos not accents"), so the brass choir is articulated two ways at once. Unmarked ff/fff attacks repeated bar after bar are the bite Dan asked to avoid, even with no accent printed.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 and 321 (above). PDF 311: the horn carries over almost anything in loud passages, so its weight needs no extra attack. Ex. X-13 (PDF 305) shows the Mahler horn line growing to fff in held, joined values. The BRIEF's rule: brass entries shaped by dynamics and doubling, weight rather than bite.
- **Fix (applied), sections/coda.ily.** Two soft-tongued groups per horn, with the slur breaking where the fff arrives, so the peak gets its own (soft) attack and the players have a place to snatch a breath. Then the low brass group.
  `codaHnOne … b'2.--(\p\< | b'2.--\ff | b'2.-- | b'2.--)\< | b'2.--(\fff | c''2.-- | b'2.--) | b'2--\> …`
  `codaHnTwo … fis'2.--(\p\< | g'2.--\ff | g'2.-- | g'2.--)\< | fis'2.--(\fff | fis'2.-- | fis'2.--) | e'2--\> …`
  `codaHnThree … f'2.--(\pp\< | dis'2.-- | e'2.--\ff | e'2.-- | e'2.--)\< | fis'2.--(\fff | fis'2.-- | fis'2.--) | e'2--\> …`
  `codaHnFour … a2.--(\pp\< | b2.-- | b2.--\ff | b2.-- | b2.--)\< | b2.--(\fff | b2.-- | b2.--) | b2--\> …`
  `codaTbnOne … g2.--(\f | g2.-- | g2.--) …`, `codaTbnTwo … e2.--(\f | e2.-- | e2.--) …`, `codaBassTbn … e,2.^-(\f | e,2.^- | b,2.^-) …`, `codaTuba … e,,2.^-(\f | e,,2.^- | b,,2.^-) …` (tenutos above for the ledger-line notes, as c125).
  The longest group is 331–335: five bars at 2. = 96, 3.1 s. Pitches, dynamics, the fff at 336 and the brass rest at 336–338 are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 120: Trombones 1·2 lose the tenuto on the last tutti downbeat (applied)

- **Problem.** In the tutti 105–120 every brass downbeat is a tenuto crotchet (c124). At 120, Tbn 1·2 have `g4 r4 r4` / `e4 r4 r4` with no tenuto, while the bass trombone, tuba and horns keep theirs. The trombones are the only brass that would play the last chord before the sub. p at 121 shorter and lighter than the rest of their choir. Nothing in the comments explains the difference.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 and 321: the notation must say how each note is tongued, and one choir should not be told two things on one chord.
- **Fix (applied), sections/sC.ily:** `… fis4-- r4 r4 | g4-- r4 r4 | R2. …` (Tbn 1) and `… b,4-- r4 r4 | e4-- r4 r4 | R2. …` (Tbn 2).

### Tested and kept

- The ff→pp diminuendo in one bar at 72 (Hn 1·2). Adler PDF 308 offers stopped horn for sf>p, but that is a bite effect and c149 rejected stopped horns. It works open, as c088 found.
- Horn staccato quavers in motive x at 71/299, entering f (c176; Dvořák 9/iii does the same). They are not accented, and the entry is not ff.
- No mutes anywhere in the scherzo. The plain senza-sord. horn choir already supplies the soft colours that Adler PDF 307–308 and 319–321 give to muted brass. A muted trumpet echo (Ex. X-48) would put the trumpets outside tutti rhythm and motive x.
- Hn 1's last G♯ 368–376 (about 11–12 s, pp→ppp, middle register, with Hn 3 covering a breath at 365–369) is within one breath for a professional (PDF 303, 309). Protected.
- The horn's coda statement of T at 341–344 has no hairpin. That matches T's first phrase in every other statement (153, 169, 209). The swells are on the second and third phrases (c038, c178).
