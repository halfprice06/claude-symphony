## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 72 and 300: the bassoons and contrabassoon play the ff arrival without the accent every other woodwind and string has (applied)

Problem: the stretto lands on A major ff at 72 (and at 300 in the reprise). Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2 and all the strings have `->`. Vc and Cb, on the same bass A, have `->` with a down-bow. Bsn 1 (A3), Bsn 2 (A2) and Cbsn (A1) print `\ff` with nothing else. On the page the bassoon staff and the contrabassoon staff are the only woodwind staves without the accent. A bassoonist reading the part sees a plain ff minim under an orchestra marked to hit the chord.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11). In Beethoven's tutti, the sf, including the editorial [sf], is restated in every staff from piccolo down to Fag. and Cfg., and the strings carry the same marks. MOLA PDF 8: proofread the vertical line as carefully as the horizontal. The horns stay unaccented. That is Dan's rule for brass (soft accents, weight not bite; c123) and does not cover woodwinds.
Fix (applied): sB.ily `sBBsnOne` `a2->\ff\> r4`, `sBBsnTwo` `a,2.->\ff\>`, `sBCbsn` `a,,2.->\ff\>`; dB.ily `dBBsnOne` `a2->\ff r4`, `dBBsnTwo` `a,2->\ff r4`, `dBCbsn` `a,,2->\ff r4`.

### 2. Retransition 247–252: two hairpins that do not lead to a printed level (applied)

Problem (a), 247–249: Cl 1 enters pp at 245 with the theme's head, holds E5 from 247, and at 248 has a diminuendo into D♯5 at 249, marked pp. So the printed shape is pp > pp, a diminuendo that ends at the level it started from. c098 and c161 wanted this E to be shaped as a suspension: swell into the dissonance, softer resolution (A&S PDF 348). The print gives only the second half of that. Cl 1 cannot take a softer target than pp at 249, because Cl 2 enters there at pp on the same staff and shared staves need equal dynamics.
Problem (b), 250–252: Vln I enters pp at 249 and crescendos 250–252. The hairpin is closed by `\!` on the rest at 252 with no level. In the same bars Cl 1·2, timpani, cellos and basses crescendo to a printed p at 251–252. In the Vln I part the player sees pp < followed by mp at 253 and has to guess where the crescendo should stop.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): every hairpin ends on a printed level that differs from where it began (mf > p; pp < mp). PDF 8: read the music through the player's eyes. The Vln I part shows nothing between pp and the mp at 253.
Fix (applied, retr.ily):
- `retrClOne` `e''2.)~\< | e''2.(\p\> | dis''2.)\pp |` (was `e''2.)~ | e''2.(\> | dis''2.)\pp |`). The printed shape is now pp < p > pp, and Cl 1·2 still meet at pp at 249.
- `retrVlnOne` `dis''4-.\p b'-. r4 |` (was `dis''4-. b'-. r4\! |`). Vln I reaches p at 252 with the rest of the texture, and Tempo I's mp follows at 253.

### 3. Bars 28 and 268: the timpani's second answer has no dynamic after seven bars' rest (applied)

Problem: the timpani answer theme A's cadences at 20 and 28 (and 260 and 268) with the same figure B–B–B–E, each after a seven-bar rest. In the timpani part, 20 carries p after its multi-rest and 28 carries nothing after its "21–27" multi-rest. The same holds for 260 and 268.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): after the multi-rest "9–11" the entry has pp. MOLA PDF 5: "Cues must be audible and obvious". The same reasoning applies to the level of an exposed entry. The two answers are identical, so they should be marked identically.
Fix (applied): sA.ily `sATimp` second answer `r4 b,8\p b,8 e,4`; dA.ily `dATimp` second answer `r4 b,8\p b,8 e,4`. The MIDI is unchanged, because the level was already p.

### 4. Bar 376: the woodwind barline runs through the clarinets' "muta in B♭ (for the Finale)" (applied)

Problem: in the score the muta text sits above the clarinet staff, right-aligned to the last bar. It is wider than bar 376, so the span bar between the flute and clarinet staves at 375|376 crosses "muta in B♭" and "Finale". This is the collision Example 7 warns against. c255 already moved the text below the stave in the parts. In the score it is still struck through.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 8 with Ex. 7 (PDF 12): anchored markings collide. MOLA PDF 7 and Adler II PDF 294: barlines run through each complete choir. That is why the woodwind span bar is there and why the text has to make room for it, not the other way round.
Fix (applied, coda.ily `codaClOne` before the last `R2.`): `\once \override MultiMeasureRestText.whiteout = #1 \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.layer = #3`. The span bar now stops above and below the text. Nothing else on the closing page moved, and its music, dynamics and spacing are unchanged.
