## Findings

### 1. Bars 141–144: the link's pp wind chord has a hole of an octave and a sixth in the middle (applied)

**Problem.** For the first four bars of the link nothing sounds except E2 (bass clarinet), B2 (Bsn 1) and G4 (Hn 1), all pp. No strings, harp or timpani play. The only close interval is the fifth at the bottom. Between the bassoon's B2 and the horn's G4 there are 20 semitones with nothing in them, so the horn's G (the note that turns to G♯ at 145) hangs alone above two low notes. The scan shows this is the only wind-only chord in the movement shaped this way, apart from places where the top note is a melody note (30, 34) and the deliberately dark 87–88 (c144).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257 says "the greater gaps exist between the bass and the next highest voice. This spacing is preferred, and it is usual to voice the upper woodwinds or the upper notes of the chord in close position". Ex. VIII-24 marks a chord whose wide gap lies above a close bottom "difficult, and not very good". Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85–86 and 96 says the same: no empty intervals in the middle of a chord. The link is "the intro in reverse" (link.ily header), and the intro's turn to E minor at 7 is voiced B2–B3–G4–E5, with Bsn 1 on B3. Adler PDF 258 (Ex. VIII-29) gets a dark hue from low close winds, and moving the B up an octave keeps the chord low and dark.

**Fix (applied).** In link.ily, `linkBsnOne`: `b,2.\pp^\markup \bold "1." | b,2. | b,2. | b,2 r4 |` becomes `b2.\pp^\markup \bold "1." | b2. | b2. | b2 r4 |`. The chord is now E2–B3–G4. The large interval is at the bottom, and the bassoon lies a third under the horn, a pairing that blends. At 145 Bsn 1 rises B3 → E4 and Bsn 2 enters on the same B3, so the B passes between the bassoons as the common tone of E minor and E major. Rhythm, dynamics, the "1." label and the breath at 144 are unchanged. HISTORY lists "link 141–148" among kept points (c140s). That entry gives no reason that covers the spacing. The change keeps the link's notes, its pitch classes, Hn 1's G → G♯, and the bass clarinet's E2 pedal (c036).

### Checked and kept (no change)

2. **Bars 189–192: cor anglais F♯3–B3 at p.** This is the instrument's lowest fifth. c221 and c224 moved its held B3 up an octave in dying chords. Adler's own account (adler1 PDF 205) calls the lowest fifth "richly beautiful, with tremendous expressive carrying power". That is what an expressive answer with the cellos needs (ch. 8 function 3, PDF 233). The dying-chord rule does not apply. Kept.

3. **Bars 125–128 and 129–132: Fl 1 enters pp< at C6–F♯6 on Vln I, then joins Ob 1 in unison.** The entry follows Adler's description of Schubert (PDF 238): the flute "enters almost undetected" to help a crescendo and is heard at the cadence. Flute and oboe in unison round the oboe (PDF 236). Ob 1's jump from B4 to G5 at 129 keeps it out of E4–D♯4, which c214 already settled. Kept.

4. **Bars 105–120 and 305–320: identical tutti scoring.** Adler's third-time rule (PDF 255) does not apply. Theme A is scored four different ways (13 strings; 105 tutti; 253 heterophony with pizz.; 305 tutti), and the tutti appears only twice. The layout is protected (c214, c224). Kept.

5. **Bars 186–188: oboe G♯4–A4 at p, the trio call.** This is inside Adler's best oboe range, "from the F in the first space to the C above the staff" (adler1 PDF 198), and not the pp low oboe of Ex. VIII-22. Kept.
