## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 79: C7/E is the movement's only C–E–G sonority. Make it vii°7 of F (applied)
Problem: in the F-major episode, bar 79 is E2 (B. cl.), G3 (Hn 4), B♭3 (Hn 3), C4 (Bsn 1), with harp E–C–G–B♭. That is C7 in first inversion: the C-major triad with a seventh, sounding for a full bar at pp under the harp's melody. cmajor.py misses it because the set is {C, E, G, B♭}. The cyclic rule is that C major belongs to the finale's arrival, and this is the one place where the scherzo sounds C–E–G.
Evidence: A&S PDF 374: "we can transform V6/5 into VII°7 merely by replacing 5 with 6". In F, that means C becomes D♭. PDF 393 (sec. 6, Ex. 23-9): vii°7 in major through ♭6 "happens frequently" and "creates no new problems". PDF 387–388, 395: ♭6 arises as an inflection of ♮6 and falls to 5, so D–D♭–C is the good order.
Fix (applied, sB.ily):
- Hn 3 at 78–80: `d'2. | des'2. | c'2 r4` (was `d'2. | bes2. | a2 r4`)
- Bsn 1 at 78–80: `a2. | bes2. | a2 r4` (was `a2. | c'2. | c'2 r4`)
- Harp LH at 79: `e,4 <e g bes>2` (was `<c g bes>`; no D♭ pedal is needed)

Every dissonance now resolves as PDF 375 and 377 require: E→F, D♭→C (d7 to P5 with the bass), B♭→A (the d5 with the bass goes inward to a 3rd, not to the hidden 5th of Ex. 22-3c), G→F. The episode's two °7s, of F at 79 and of E at 87, now lie a semitone apart. The C-major-triad check prints none.
