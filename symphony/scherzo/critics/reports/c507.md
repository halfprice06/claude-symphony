## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 255–256: Vln II's pizzicato off-beats meet theme A's dip in unison. Applied.

**Problem.** Theme A's third and fourth bars descend E5–B4–B4–G4 | F♯4–D♯4–B3 into the half cadence. In the reprise, Vln I plays this at 255–256 and Vln II plays r4 g'4 g'4 | r4 b4 b4 pizz. The G4 on beat 3 of 255 and the B3 on beat 3 of 256 are the tune's own notes in the same register, and at 256 Vln II's B3 on beat 2 sits under the tune's D♯4 inside its compass. The half cadence's last two notes are the ones that merge with the accompaniment.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135: "Many professional orchestrators recommend staying away from the register of the melody line. In some instances, especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color, this is good advice." The image of Ex. V-7 (PDF 132–133) shows Brahms's background kept clear of the cellos' line in the middle register. The score already applies this rule at the same bars of every other quiet statement: c227 changed 15–16 and 31–32 (sA.ily comment: the descent "ended in unison with the off-beats … so the half cadence's last notes merged into the accompaniment"), and the reprise's counterstatement at 271–272 has r4 g'4 r4 | R2. Only 255–256 was left in the pre-c227 form. Pizzicato against staccato arco is a change of attack, not of colour family; both are the second-violin and first-violin sound in the same octave, which is the case Adler names.

**Fix (applied; dA.ily `dAVlnTwo`, comment `% c507`).**
```
old: r4 g'4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." g'4 | r4 g'4 g'4 | r4 g'4 g'4 | r4 b4 b4 | r4 g'4 g'4 | ...
new: r4 g'4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." g'4 | r4 g'4 g'4 | r4 g'4 r4 | R2. | r4 g'4 g'4 | ...
```
The violas keep B3 (255) and F♯3 (256) below the dip, so the harmony (i, then V) is complete, as at 15–16. Vln II plays again at 257, so no cue is needed (parts-cues.json has Vln II cues only at 193). The MIDI loses three pizz. notes; analysis.txt's density for 253–256 falls from 65 to 62 notes.

Why this is the change I kept: I looked for the change under this reading that a listener would hear most, and every larger candidate (findings 2 and 3) either breaks a recorded reason or makes the passage worse. This one gives the reprise's first half cadence the same clear ending the exposition has had since c227. It is small, but it is the one place in the movement where the reading's background rule is broken in a quiet passage that a listener has already heard done the other way.

### 2. Bars 108 and 308: in the tutti the violas' dyad B3/A4 lies above the theme's lower octave (Vln II, C.a., Cl 2: F♯4–D♯4–B3). Not applied.

Adler PDF 151 asks for each instrument in its traditional place, and c207 fixed the same crossing at 40. Here the A4 on beats 2–3 is above Vln II's D♯4 and B3, and the B3 on beat 3 is Vln II's note. I did not change it. The tune sounds in four octaves at ff (Fl a2, Vln I, Vln II, Ob 1, C.a., Cl 2), so the violas cannot cover it. The violas' A4 is the seventh of V7 and resolves to G4 at 109 in their own register (c112). The only lower voicing, F♯3/A3, puts the seventh in unison with the cellos' tenor A3 (Vc, Hn 3·4, Bsn 1), which doubles the seventh at the unison in a loud chord. Adler PDF 151 itself warns that close loud chords in violas and cellos sound bass-heavy. Kept.

### 3. Bars 95–100: the retransition's background is all detached crotchets (Vln II B4–F♯4–D♯4, violas' repeated B3, pizz. B). Not applied.

PDF 135 asks for a background that differs in rhythm and character, and PDF 159 (Ex. V-34) lists sustained harmony plus a violin arpeggio as a stock pattern, so a held viola B3 is a possible reading. I rejected it. PDF 164–166 (Ex. V-40 to V-43) says that at speed repeated notes are the idiomatic string pulse. The violas' repeated B is the only string motor under the timpani's x before the hemiola at 101 starts the arco minims. A held B here would take that motor away and anticipate the sustained hemiola. The tune's quavers are the only quavers in the strings, so the head still stands out. c407 reached the same verdict.

### 4. Fugato 57–68 and the reprise 289–296: checked against PDF 143–145. No change.

The exposition is for strings alone. It enters low to high (Ex. V-21). The subject is staccato with motive x's quavers, the countersubject is in legato crotchets an octave or more away, and the third voice has minims and ties, so the three layers differ in rhythm and articulation as PDF 144 asks. The stretto ends in the homophonic ff block at 72, which is the Bach/Weber alternation (PDF 145, 147). The reprise colours the subject's downbeats with Bsn 1 and Cl 1, pp. Adler PDF 143 allows this once the exposition has been heard plain. c307's violas at 70/298 hold.

### 5. Trio background: the harp's off-beat figure (r4 chord chord, pizz. bass) recurs at 153–168, 185–192, 225–232 and 341–348. No change.

PDF 142 says to limit the colours of a passage that returns. The figure is the trio's own accompaniment, and the statements that need contrast already have it: 169–184 has the Vln II arpeggios (c407), and 209–224 has divided violas, the cello countermelody and the horns' thirds. The codetta thins the figure from two chords to one to the downbeat (225, 229, 233), which is Adler's diminuendo by scoring (PDF 154).

### 6. Bars 121–128: the second idea's bass (Vc, Bsn 2) and Cl 2 share the tune's syncopated rhythm (minim, crotchet tied | crotchet, minim). No change.

PDF 142–143 asks for each layer to have its own rhythm. Here the middle layer gives that contrast: violas and horns hold dotted minims. The bass takes the tune's syncopation in every statement of the second idea (45, 121, 277) on purpose, so that the hemiola cadence (53–55, 129–131, protected) arrives in every voice. Changing the bass's rhythm here would change the theme's harmonic rhythm, which is not a texture question.

### 7. Coda 329–332: all four string groups play motive x in the same rhythm. No change.

Ex. V-24 (PDF 148) says lines alike in rhythm give contemplation, not drive. But the stretto at 325–328 enters bar by bar (Vc, Vla, Vln II, Vln I), and 329–332 is the block before the ff at 333, the alternation PDF 145 and 147 recommend. The rising harmony (bass E–D–C–B, c443) gives these bars their drive.
