This is the fifth expression pass on the unit. c098, c198, c298 and c398 read it for expression, and c331 and c421 read it for melody. I read the OCR text of all 26 pages. The images show these expressive details:

- Beethoven, Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365): `<>`, then cresc., then `>` p on the repeated V7 that stands in for the tonic.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The link's horn foreshadows T's head but not T's suspension; make its neighbour A a suspended 4-3 (applied)

**Bars.** 147–148, Hn 1 solo over the pp E2–B3–E4 pad (bass clarinet, Bsn 1·2), just before the poco rit. and the trio.

**Problem.** The suspension is now the trio tune's defining gesture. Since c331 and c421, each of T's phrase ends ties a note over the bar line: the A held into the tonic and falling to G♯ at bars 3–4, the A♯ rising to B at 7–8, the A at 10–11, the E at 13–14. c051 gave the link's horn T's first bar at T's pitch (G♯4–A4) so that T grows out of the pivot G♯. In the link, though, the A is a plain upper neighbour on beat 3 that falls back to G♯ on the next downbeat. The horn has the only moving notes in a static four-bar pad. That one A is the first time the trio's world is heard, and it carries no weight. The bar line passes without any lean, so the trio's characteristic sigh first arrives with Cl 1 at 155–156.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 358, sec. 18 and Ex. 21-23b: "sometimes neighboring and passing tones can be suspended before resolving". The Bach shows a neighbour note held over the bar line. PDF 346, sec. 1: a suspension "animate[s] and individualize[s]" a line through rhythm alone. That fits a pad with nothing else moving. PDF 349, sec. 7: the 4-3 is accompanied by a 5th, with the bass usually doubled. The pad gives exactly that: E2, B3, E4 under A4. PDF 352, sec. 12: no other voice sounds G♯ before the resolution, since the pad is a bare fifth and octave. PDF 348, sec. 4: the suspension takes an accent and its resolution is softer. PDF 367–368: a dissonance against a tonic pedal is good when it is prepared and resolved. The new A4 is prepared on beat 3 of 147 and resolved on beat 2 of 148.

**Fix (applied).** link.ily, Hn 1:
```
gis'2.~\< | gis'2.~ | gis'2(\p a'4\<~ | a'4-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> gis'2~ | gis'4)-\tweak stencil ##f \pp r4 r4 |
```
It was `gis'2(\p\> a'4 | gis'2.~ | gis'4) r4\! r4`. The printed p at 147 stays. The `<` on the A and the `>` from 148's downbeat follow T's own shading at 155–156 (c398). The hidden mp peak and the hidden pp end at 149 affect the MIDI only.

Other things stay as they were:
- c340's hand-over is unchanged: G♯ is held into beat 1 of 149, where the Hn 3·4 drone and the harp's harmonic G♯4 take it.
- 148 beat 3 is still Hn 1 alone.
- c051's T head G♯–A at 147 is unchanged.

The listener now hears T's head and T's tied A–G♯ cell together at T's pitch, in the horn. Six bars later the clarinet answers with the same cell at 155–156.

**Checks.** Hn 2 rests on the shared staff, so the dynamics rule is met. No line is added or silenced, so parts-cues.json is unaffected. At 200 dpi the tie, the ♯ on 148's G and the two hairpins print clear of the Hn 3·4 staff.

### 3. Bar 200: the top voice's G♯5 sounds while T holds its A (no change)

**Bars.** 199–200 (tB). T (Cl 1, Vln II, Vla, Hn 1) ties A over G♯ in the bass, a ♭9-8. At the same moment, Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1 arrive on G♯5, the tone of resolution.

**Reading.** Ex. 21-12 calls this poor in a homogeneous texture. PDF 352 accepts it "in a texture with marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics". Here the top line differs in rhythm (quavers B–C♯ before it), in colour (flute, oboe and Vln I against clarinet, viola and horn) and in level (mf/mp against T's f, c406). The 9-8 over the bass is the other stated exception.

**Why no change.** I looked for a top-line alternative. Holding C♯6 into 200 makes a 4th over the G♯-major chord that would have to leap to G♯. Moving to B5 clashes with Cl 2's B♯. Both are worse than the present line. The line stays.

### 4. Bars 111/311: B4 in trumpets and Hn 1 against the tenor's ♭9-8 (no change)

The cellos, Hn 3·4 and Bsn 1 tie C4 over B. Tpt 1 and Hn 1 sound B4 on the downbeat in motive x: staccato and tenuto against a legato tie. That is the rhythmic and timbral contrast PDF 352 allows. The swell into the tie (c198, c468) brings the suspension out. Removing Hn 1's B4 would leave the trumpets' B4 in place, so it would gain nothing. The tutti layout is also protected (c454, c487).

### 6. The suspended dominant at 336–338 (no change)

The V held for three bars before the tonic at 339 is the entire-chord suspension of Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365). Beethoven softens his suspended V7 (`>` p) because his tonic arrives quietly. Here the tonic is the scherzo's last ff stroke before the G.P., and 336 holds the single B6/fff (protected). A diminuendo would weaken the arrival. No change.
