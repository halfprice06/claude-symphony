## Findings

All four findings are about articulation only. No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or theme changes. Nothing is added to the brass beyond Hn 2's tie at 361. Dan's soft-brass rule is unaffected.

### 1. Bars 101–104 and 301–304: the woodwinds' held V chord is re-tongued on every downbeat across the hemiola (applied)

- **Problem.** In the "ritmo di quattro battute" build, Fl 1, C.a., Cl 1 and the strings play the accented hemiola minims. Attacks fall on beat 1 and beat 3 of 101 and 103 and on beat 2 of 102 and 104. The downbeats of 102 and 104 are tied over. c188 slurred the held brass chord through these bars for exactly this reason. Six woodwind parts still hold the same chord as unmarked dotted minims: Fl 2 `fis''2.\f\< | fis''2. | fis''2. | fis''2.\ff`, Ob 1 the same, Ob 2 D♯5, Cl 2 D♯4 at 101–102, Bsn 1 B3–A3–B3–A3, Bsn 2 B2, Cbsn B1. Under Adler's rule each of these is a separate tongued attack. So six winds strike the downbeats of 102 and 104 at f< ff, which are the two points where the hemiola needs silence on the beat to be heard. The brass in the same bars are soft-tongued under one slur, so one chord is notated two ways.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 321: "As in all wind and brass instruments, all slurred notes are performed on one breath, while all separate notes are tongued." PDF 309: hard and soft tonguing must be told apart in the notation. Ex. X-15 and X-16 (PDF 306–307): held harmony under slurs. Dvořák 9/iii (above): held wind notes tied, and no wind restrikes the hemiola's tied beat.
- **Fix (applied).** Slur over the build and tenuto on each bar, as c188 did for the brass; dynamics unchanged. sections/sB.ily:
  `sBFluteTwo … fis''2.--(\f\< | fis''2.-- | fis''2.-- | fis''2.--\ff) |`, `sBOboeOne` the same, `sBOboeTwo … dis''2.--(\f\< | dis''2.-- | dis''2.-- | dis''2.--\ff) |`, `sBClTwo … dis'2.--(\f\< | dis'2.--) | fis'2-> …`, `sBBsnOne … b2.--(\f\< | a2.-- | b2.-- | a2.--\ff) \breathe`, `sBBsnTwo … b,2.--(\f\< | b,2.-- | b,2.-- | b,2.--\ff) \breathe`, `sBCbsn … b,,2.--(\f\< | b,,2.-- | b,,2.-- | b,,2.--\ff) |`.
  sections/dB.ily (301–304): the same seven lines, keeping their `\p\<` start. I used slur with tenuto, not ties, so the MIDI keeps its steps through the crescendo (c188's reason) and the woodwinds use the same sign as the brass.

### 4. Bars 361–362: the winds re-tongue the final E-major chord one bar after it arrives (applied; protected passage)

- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 309, 321. Ex. X-11 (PDF 305): a soft horn line ends "p > pp" on held, tied values. Dvořák 9/iii Bsn 2 `gs2.\p ~ | gs2.` (line 1060).

### Tested and kept

- Hn 1·2 ff>pp in one bar at 72, open. Adler PDF 308 offers stopped horn for sf>p, but c149 rejected stopped horns and this is the bite Dan's rule excludes. As c188 found, it works open.
- No mutes and no stopped echo (Ex. X-19, X-48). Muted trumpets would take the trumpets outside tutti rhythm and motive x. The plain horn choir already supplies the soft brass colour.
- The F-major episode horns, Hn 3·4 pp at 73–88 without hairpins. They are an accompaniment choir under the harp's swelling melody (PDF 311–312), one level under it, as c099 set.
- Trumpet motive x pp at 101/103 with staccato dots: soft repeated notes, as in Ex. X-28. Tpt 2's F♯4 lies in the register where pp works (PDF 319).
- Cl 2 249–252 and Cl 1 251–252 in the retransition. The crotchet at 252 is the marked arrival (p) of the crescendo, so its re-attack is meant.
