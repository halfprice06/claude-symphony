## Findings

### 1. Bars 205–209: Hn 1 plays the motto-tail preview alone; Fl 1 rests (applied)

- **Problem.** At 205–208, Hn 1 plays G♯4–A4–B4 | B4 | B4–A♯4 | A4 and resolves to G♯4 at 209. That is T's head turning into the motto's tail. It previews the close, where the tail is B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 in Hn 1 alone at 357–360.
  - Fl 1 doubles the preview an octave higher, p espress., and lands on G♯5 at 209.
  - Nothing else in 205–208 is above A4. Cl 2 holds D♯4, violas F♯3, cellos B, bassoons and timpani are below. So the flute is the top voice, and the ear takes the tail at B5–A♯5–A5–G♯5.
  - When the real tail comes at 357, it is heard in a register and colour that the preview did not establish.
  - At 205 the flute also makes Hn 1's G♯4 into a G♯4 + G♯5 octave pair, which is the frame's sound (1–7, 364–376). c510 keeps that pair for the frame alone.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler II PDF 188: if a tune "appeared an octave higher … the choice would of necessity be a different one". Register is part of a line's identity.
  - Adler II PDF 189: octave ("organ") doubling is a way to strengthen a passage. A p espress. preview over a pp pad needs no strength.
  - Adler I PDF 20: Monteverdi's one mood, one unchanging combination; and Piston's "hear what you put on that page". The page shows a horn line; the ear hears a flute line.
  - Adler II PDF 187: isolate the principal voice. 357–360 does this (Hn 1 alone, c058/c060).
  - The scherzo's own reasons point the same way. c324 moved the preview to Hn 1 for "one colour for one recurring gesture" (Adler I PDF 261–262). c510 keeps the G♯ octave pair for the frame.
  - c414's rule ("one flute, not two") was about a unison of two flutes. It does not argue for the flute. Its reason that the flute is "the 4-foot colour of the horn's line" is the organ doubling that PDF 189 reserves for strength.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-540/work).**
  - sections/tB.ily: `tBFluteOne` bars 205–208 were `gis''2(\p^\markup \italic "espress." a''4 | b''2.~ | b''2\< ais''4 | a''2.) |`. They are now `R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. |`. I added a c540 comment above the variable.
  - sections/tC.ily: `tCFluteOne = { gis''2\mf r4 | R2.*7 |` is now `tCFluteOne = { R2.*8 |`, with a comment.
  - parts-cues.json, part 01 (Fl 1): added `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",213,4]`.
- **What it does.**
  - 205–208 is Hn 1 alone above the pad, p espress., crescendo, at the pitch, in the player and in the colour of 357–360. The listener meets the tail at 357 as something already heard.
  - At 209 the forte T arrives in Vln I and Cl 1 with the top register fresh, which is a wanted seam (XV-40).
  - No pitch class is lost: the flute only doubled the horn. Hn 1 still resolves A4→G♯4 at 209.
  - Fl 1 now rests 201–216 and enters at 217 with T's third phrase.
- **Why this is the best candidate, and why it does not make the music worse.** 205–209 is the only place before the coda where the motto's tail sounds as a melody, so its colour decides whether the close at 357 is heard as a return. What is lost is a soft flute shine on four bars. The texture becomes a horn line over a low pad, the movement's frame sound, and the trio's forte return gains contrast.

- **What it is.** The countermelody now opens G3–A3–B3–E4, which is T's head in E minor.

  - The minor form spans G3–E4, a major sixth. That is the same interval as movement I's horn call E♭3–C4 (I 13), which T's major head also fills (G♯4–E5, a minor sixth).
