## Findings

### 1. 106, 110, 118 and 306, 310, 318: the motto tail's lowest octave has no string support in the tuttis; give it to the violas (applied)

**Problem.** In both theme-A tuttis the tune lies in three octaves. Fl 1·2 take the top. Ob 1 and Vln I (+ Vln II) take the middle. The cor anglais and Cl 2 take the lowest (G4). On the head bars (105, 107, 109, …), Tpt 2's motive x sits on E4 in the head's own rhythm, so the bottom octave is weighted by brass + C.a. That is Rimsky's own layout for three octaves. On the tail bars (106, 110, 118 and their reprise bars) the trumpets rest. There the motto's chromatic tail G4–F♯4–F4 (A4–G♯4–G4 at 110) is carried in that octave by two reeds only. In the same octave sit Hn 1·2 (G4/E4), the tenor countermelody's tied E4 in Bsn 1, Hn 3·4 and Vc, and the violas' staccato off-beats. Since c287 the violas' beat 3 has been a bare B3 (C4–E4 at 110). That removed the clash, but no string section plays the tail in that octave. These are the six bars in which the movement's cyclic idea (protected: "theme A's tail G–F♯–F–E") is least supported where it most needs support.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 76: three or four octaves are "difficult to balance". Rimsky's two examples weight the bottom octave (*Tsar Saltan* [228]: Trumpet + Eng. horn under 2 Fl. + 2 Ob. and Picc.). The score has this weight on the head bars and loses it on the tail bars.
- PDF 78: "of the two parts in octaves, only one is doubled … the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups". The lower octave is the one that needs a string group.
- PDF 76: "Violas + Ob. (Eng. horn), Violas + Cl." are among the "best and most natural" unisons. At equal power "the strings will predominate". A bowed viola section on the tail gives the reeds' line the string body that the off-beat chord was covering.
- c287 (same reading) diagnosed this octave: the bowed viola chord outweighed "the two winds that carry the chromatic step". c287 cut the clashing note. This change puts the violas on the line itself.

**Fix (applied).** sections/sC.ily `sCVla` and sections/dC.ily `dCVla`. Only beat 3 of three bars changes in each:
```
106 / 306, 118 / 318:  r4 <b g'>4-. b4-.        ->  r4 <b g'>4-. fis'8( f'8)
110 / 310:             r4 <c' a'>4-. <c' e'>4-.  ->  r4 <c' a'>4-. gis'8( g'8)
```
The violas reach the tail from their beat-2 G4 (A4 at 110), which is the tail's own first note, so there is no leap. They slur the two quavers as the tune does and resume their off-beats on beat 2 of the next bar. The head bars, beat 2 of every bar, the tenor countermelody and the harmony are unchanged. I added a comment with these reasons above both variables. This revises c287's fix and answers its reason: no note now rubs against the tail, because the violas play the tail. The minor second F4/E4 against the tenor's tied E4 on beat 3 was already present in C.a. and Cl 2 (c287 Finding 2 kept it). It is now heard as part of a chromatic line in the viola section rather than as a two-reed cluster. The engraving at 106 and 110 (page 4) looks clean.

### 3. 205–208: one flute over Hn 1's motto-tail preview (checked, kept)

PDF 75 gives one horn two winds above it. Besides "2 Ob. or 2 Cl. or 2 Fl.", the image shows "1 Ob. + 1 Cl. / 1 Horn" (*Snegourotchka* [71]). c387 applied two flutes, and c414 reverted it. c414's reason holds against a like-instrument unison: Adler PDF 249–250 (printed pp. 233–234) says two like winds in unison alter the solo timbre and impede its rubato. The mixed Ob + Cl option avoids that objection, but here it fails for other reasons. Cl 1 rests 201–208 to enter fresh with T at 209 (c036). Ob 1 at G♯5–B5 p would turn a horn preview into an oboe line, against 357–360, where the same line is the horn alone. Kept, and not flipped again.

### 4. 177–184: Vln I's octave of T inside Vln II's quaver figuration (checked, kept)

The clash scan found seconds between Vln I's T (C♯5–A5, down to D♯4–B4 at 181–183) and Vln II's con sord. arpeggios (B4–G♯5). The layout is Rimsky's own Ex. 7 (PDF 180–181), which c461 cites: the cello tune is doubled by Vln I an octave higher over Vln II/viola figuration in the same octave. The mute (Vln II) against senza sord. sul A (Vln I) separates them. Moving either line breaks c461's and c407's protected choices for no reading-based gain. Kept.

### 5. 341–344: T in Hn 3 alone (checked, kept)

PDF 75 ("a finer legato") and PDF 79 (Ex. 92, violas + horn) would allow a unison partner. The c438 do-not-touch (single players carry the coda's T) and the answer by Hn 1 alone at 357–360 are stronger reasons than "as a rule". Kept.

### 6. 101 beat 1: Cl 1 A4 a second under the hemiola tune's lower octave B4 (checked, kept)

Cl 1 enters on the V7's seventh under C.a. + Vln II's B4 and moves in thirds with them from beat 3 (PDF 78, thirds for wood with strings). This is the seventh's correct place in the chord, not a doubled seventh. Kept.
