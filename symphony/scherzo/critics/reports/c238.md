## Findings

### 1. The swell on the trio tune's phrase-2 peak (bars 158, 174, 214, 346) is printed but sounds in neither render. Applied.

**Problem.** c178 put a crescendo–diminuendo on the held peak note of phrase 2 (F♯5 in Cl 1 at 158, F♯6/F♯5 in the violins at 174 and 214, B5 in Ob 1 at 346). Its hidden end dynamic sits at the current level, and the hidden dynamic on the peak note itself is also the current level: `cis''4\< fis''2-\tweak stencil ##f \p\>` at p. c178 says so ("the printed shape changes, but the MIDI velocity does not"). Measured in v238's score.midi: Cl 1 at 158 = 37 on every note, peak included; Vln I at 174 = 63 throughout; at 214 = 83 throughout; Ob 1 at 346 = 37. The phrase-3 peak right after it does swell (Cl 1 162–163: 37→43; Vln I 178–179: 63→71), because c138 gave it a target one level up. So in both renders the tune's second phrase is flat at the one point where the print asks it to lean, and the third phrase leans.

**Evidence.** A render of an expressive crescendo sounds lifeless unless the swell is given a size (Casanelles PDF 149–150). Notation does not carry the curve, so the composer marks the peak (PDF 152). The lyrical legato that melodies want "transitions frictionlessly into a gentle swell on the note or arrival" (PDF 155). The r07 renderer takes its CC11 curve from these velocities, so its render of the trio will be flat at these notes too. The phrase-3 peaks show the method that works.

**Fix (applied).** No printed change. The hidden dynamic on the peak note goes one level above the local level, as at the phrase-3 peaks (c138). The return to the local level on the next note stays as it is.
```
% tA.ily tAClOne 158:       cis''4\< fis''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\>      (was \p)
% tA.ily tAVlnOne 174:      cis'''4\< fis'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mf\>)   (was \mp)
% tA.ily tAVlnTwo 174:      cis''4\< fis''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mf\>)     (was \mp)
% tC.ily tCClOne, tCVlnTwo 214: fis''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>; tCVlnOne fis'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>   (were \f)
% coda.ily codaOboeOne 346: fis''4\< b''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\>        (was \p)
```
After the change: Cl 1 158 F♯5 = 44 (back to 37 at 159), Vln I/II 174 = 73, 214 = 91, Ob 1 346 = 44. Every other note is unchanged. The second players of the shared staves (Cl 2 at 158 and 214, Ob 2 at 346) rest, so no shared staff gets unequal dynamics.

### 2. The B pedal and the timpani stay at one level while the strings crescendo into the hemiola (98–100) and into the reprise (249–252). Applied.

**Problem.** At 98–100 Vln I, Vln II and the violas crescendo from p/pp to the f at 101. The timpani play motive x on B three bars running (98, 99, 100) at the p printed at 95: velocity 62 on all twelve strokes. The pizzicato B in the cellos and basses stays pp at 98–99 (velocity 50), then comes in arco f at 101. The same thing happens at 249–252: the timpani crescendo pp→p and the clarinets pp<p, but the basses' four plucked B's (and the cellos' three) stay at pp, and the basses enter p at 253. In both places the bass and the repeated timpani figure are the parts that make the pedal, and they are the parts that do not take part in the build.

**Evidence.** The same sound repeated at one level is heard as a machine; players vary repetitions according to "the musicality of a passage" (Casanelles PDF 150, 152). Here the music grows and the repetitions should grow with it. Dynamics also change timbre (PDF 148–149). A pedal that holds pp under strings reaching f changes the balance of the chord, not just its loudness. The timpani roll at 101 is the layer that holds the level steady (Fig. 6.6, PDF 290). It should start soft and build with the horns (p<), as the reprise's roll does at 301, rather than carry on at whatever level 98–100 reached. Bars 101–104 already build in every group (c028).

**Fix (applied).**
```
% sB.ily sBTimp 98-101: b,4\< b,8 b,8 b,4 | b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4 | b,4\mf b,8 b,8 b,4 | b,2.:32\p\<~ ...
%   (the roll at 101 keeps the p it had; the p is now printed, as at 301 in dB.ily)
% sB.ily sBVc 98-99:  b,4\< r4 r4 | b,4\mp r4 r4 | R2. | b,2->\f\<^\markup \italic "arco" ...
% sB.ily sBCb 98-99:  b,,4\< r4 r4 | b,,4\mp r4 r4 | R2. | b,,2->\f\<^\markup \italic "arco" ...
% retr.ily retrVc 249-251: b,4\< r4 r4 | b,4 r4 r4 | b,4\p r4 r4 | R2.
% retr.ily retrCb 249-252: b,,4\< r4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r4 | b,,4\p r4 r4
```
After the change: timpani 98–100 = 62→76, then the roll starts at 62 at 101. Cb 99 = 63. Cb 249–252 = 50→57, matching the p at 253, which is now a reminder. Vc 249–251 = 50→57, then the cellos' arco sostenuto enters pp at 253 as a new layer. The exposition is now built like the reprise's approach at 297–301. No notes changed.
