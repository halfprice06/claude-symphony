## Findings (ranked)

### 1. String parts: 12 systems on page 2 while page 3 is half empty (applied, parts only)
- **Bars / pages:** Vln I page 2 (153–317, 12 systems) then page 3 (318–376, 4 systems); Vln II 12 then 4; Vla 12 then 5; Cb page 1 11, page 2 12, page 3 8.
- **Problem:** The violin is MOLA's own example of an instrument "subject to multiple ledger lines"; its pages should hold eight to ten systems, and twelve only when nothing is crowded. Page 2 of Vln I carries T's statements up to F♯6 and G♯6 with hairpins under almost every bar and the ledger notes of the reprise; the systems sit close. c165 kept the 12-system viola page because it was not crowded. That reason holds only when 12 is needed. Here it is not: pages 2 and 3 face each other, so the music can be shared out between them with no new page and no new turn, and page 3 is mostly white.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 7: "No more than eight to ten staves per page should be used for any instrument that is subject to multiple ledger lines (i.e. flute, violin, tuba). Up to twelve staves may be used as long as symbols are not crowded and clarity of the notational elements is maintained." PDF 7 also asks for a consistent amount of music per system and for turns in rests; the 1→2 turn is the only one these parts have.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-315/work):** parts-gen.py, after `LAYX = {}`:
  ```
  PAPX = {s: r"\paper { max-systems-per-page = 10 }" for s in ("26", "27", "28", "30")}
  ```
  and in the book template, after the `\header` line: `{PAPX.get(suf[:2], "")}`. Result: Vln I 10/10/6, Vln II 10/10/6, Vla 10/10/7, Cb 10/10/10. Page counts unchanged (3 each). The turns stay where they were (Vln I, Vln II, Vla 152/153 after 16 bars' rest; Cb 140/141). Checked at 55 and 150 dpi: page 3 now starts at 298/299 (Vln), 284 (Vla), 252 (Cb), nothing crowded or collides.
- **Not applied to other parts, by test:** with 10 systems the cello's turn moves from 152/153 (16 bars' rest) to 140/141 (about 9), so the cello keeps 12/12/6 (it is not a ledger-line instrument). Fl 1, Fl 2 and Bsn 1 would gain a page (Fl 1 9/10/2, Bsn 1 9/10/1, Fl 2 8/5), and Ob 1 and Cl 1 would turn at 196/197 and 208/209, in shorter rests or into an entry. Their 12-system first pages are what the turn needs and are not crowded (c135, c225).

### Considered, no change
- **Rehearsal letters inside the two 24-bar spans (M 185–208, Q 253–276), e.g. at 201 or 269.** MOLA PDF 4 asks for letters at landmarks. Every bar is numbered, and a new letter would rename every later letter (N–W) in the score, the parts, the cue lists and the cross-references in the other movements' notes. The gain does not justify that.
- **"draft v315" with no date (MOLA PDF 6).** The working copies carry only the version; promote.sh dates the release copies (v201, v300). That is the right split.
- **Harp pedal settings and bow marks in the score (MOLA PDF 5).** MOLA's rules apply to parts, where both are handled (pedals stripped; bowings kept as specific effects, c268).
- **Timpani G♯ f at 200 against the strings' diminuendo.** The strings' f from 197 still stands at 200's downbeat, where their hairpin starts, so the vertical line agrees.
- **Viola f at 41 to the right of the other staves' f.** It sits under the viola's first note, on beat 2 (Gould's rule), not on the rest.
