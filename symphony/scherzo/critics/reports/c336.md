## Findings

### 1. Bars 145 and 226: the timpani's muta labels put *muta* before the old pitch. Applied.

**Problem.** The retuning labels read "muta G in G♯" (link, 145) and "muta G♯ in G" (tC, 226, over "medium sticks"). Adler's table of terms gives the instruction as "Change C to E: Sol muta in Mi" (adler2 PDF 312): the drum's present pitch, then *muta in*, then the new pitch. That is the order players read in printed parts ("G muta in G♯", "La muta in Si♭"). The score's order leaves a timpanist to work out which pitch is the old one; with "G in G♯" the meaning is still clear, but the term is printed in a form that is not the standard one.

**Evidence.** adler2 PDF 312, row "Change C to E / Sol Muta in Mi / Changez Do en Mi / C nach E Umstimmen". The closing label at 376 ("muta: low E in G, G in A♭, B in C, high E in E♭") is a list headed by *muta* and can stay: each item already reads old pitch → new pitch.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-336/work).** sections/link.ily, `linkTimp`: `R2.^\markup \small \italic "G muta in G♯"`; sections/tC.ily, `tCTimp`: `\column { "G♯ muta in G" "medium sticks" }`, each with a `% c336:` comment. Print only; score.midi is byte-identical to v336's, and the labels have the same width (checked on page 6 at 160 dpi).
