## What else I studied

Tchaikovsky 4/iii is not in the local corpus. I worked from my knowledge of the score and checked the main facts against https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Tchaikovsky): the strings play pizzicato throughout, the woodwinds bring the trio, the brass come in very quietly and staccato, timpani is the only percussion, and the movement ends quietly on pizzicato strings. In Adler, the Tchaikovsky 4/i example (XI-21, PDF 39–41) showed the composer's tutti habits in this symphony: strings thinned to the low instruments before the tutti, and choirs given different material. I also compared v69 with v59: I diffed every section file and score.ly, and checked page count, analyser output and theme record.

## Holistic judgement against Tchaikovsky 4/iii

Tchaikovsky's movement depends on keeping the choirs apart. Strings, woodwinds and brass each get their own music, the brass stay soft, and the climaxes come from the choirs alternating rather than from long tuttis. v69 shares more of this than its models list suggests. The counterstatement 29–36 alternates winds and strings. The F-major episode 73–88 is string-free. The trio's first statement is a clarinet over harp off-beats and a horn drone. The reprise at 253 is a pizzicato texture: Vln II and Vla pizz., Cb pizz., with sustained cellos. The movement closes on a pizzicato E with harp harmonics. A conductor would not doubt the overall plan. Since c063 the dynamic shape of 101–132 is no longer one long loud block. The low brass are saved for the coda, as Adler's Beethoven and Schubert examples recommend (PDF 17, 37).

The two places where v69 sounds less than masterly are both about colour, and both are small. First, the trio tune's forte return is doubled at pitch four ways from its first note, so its real peak at 217–220 gets no new colour. Second, the reprise puts a busy flute line an octave above the returning theme at the same dynamic. Tchaikovsky's trombones play only soft staccato in this movement. Here the trombones are used only for accented strokes and the coda chords. I record this and do not change it: the cyclic rules keep the brass chorale and a low-brass unison for the finale, and c033 and c064 decided to keep the brass out of 89–100 and the reprise.

## Findings

### 1. Trio return 209–224: Fl 1 rests until the third phrase (applied)
- Problem: at 209 the tune enters f in Vln I and Fl 1 at pitch, and in Vln II and Cl 1 at pitch an octave lower. That is 16 bars of four-way sustained doubling. The phrase that climbs to A6 at 217–220, the high point of the trio, enters in the same colour that has been sounding for eight bars, so nothing marks it.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 165 says doublings at pitch lose each instrument's colour and sustained doublings tire the ear. PDF 37 recommends holding a colour back for a specific event. PDF 156 says a new colour entering after a rest is clearly perceived. PDF 170–171 (Mahler 4/iv) re-scores a recurring passage each time. The 169 statement already has Vln I/II in octaves with Fl 1 heterophony joining at 177. Without the flute, 209 is the strings and clarinet, and the flute's entry at 217 becomes the trio's peak. Fl 1 already rests at 201–208 (c036), so it now waits 16 bars and comes in fresh.
- Fix (tC.ily): `tCFluteOne = { R2.*8 | cis'''2(\mf\< dis'''4 | e'''4 gis'''2) | ...` (bars 217–224 unchanged; the \mf moves to 217). Fl 2's G♯5 at 209 stays, as c050 and c065 require; the shared staff prints it as "2.".
- Record: the recorded statement "trioT FluteOne 209" now begins at 217. themecheck reports it CHANGED because the first eight bars are rests. The notes are unchanged, so this is not a theme change. The composer moves the record line to bar 217 (from the tune's note 19).

### 2. Reprise 253: Vln I mp, not p (applied)
- Problem: at Q the theme returns p leggiero in Vln I. At the same time Fl 1 plays a continuous slurred quaver heterophony, also p, an octave higher (E6–A6). The theme is the principal voice. The flute is the subsidiary line, and it lies in the flute's piercing octave, so at equal dynamics it draws the ear away from the returning theme.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 184 says the principal voice is marked louder, and subsidiary voices in a piercing register are marked softer. PDF 152 says the high flute octave cuts through without help. c036 asked for p, not pp, on high flute lines, so I raise the violins instead of lowering the flute. At 13 the same theme has no flute above it, and p is right there.
- Fix (dA.ily): `dAVlnOne = { e''4-.\mp^\markup \italic "leggiero" ...`. Vln I's p at 270 and its later dynamics are unchanged.
