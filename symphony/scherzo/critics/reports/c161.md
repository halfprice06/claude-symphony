## Findings

### 1. Bars 245–249: the second head entry of the retransition is in the same string colour as the entries around it (applied)

**Problem.** Theme A's head comes back three times, a bar group apart, over the timpani's B pedal. Twice this happens before a return of theme A. At 89–95 the entries pass viola → oboe → first violins, because c077 gave the second entry to Ob 1. At 241–249, the retransition out of the trio, the same entries pass viola → second violins → first violins. Strings answer strings, and the second entry starts in the register where the viola's held E4 has just been. The listener hears one string texture growing, not the head handed from voice to voice. This is the passage where the scherzo's head has to come back out of the trio's world. In that world the clarinet was T's first voice (153) and the codetta's second (229).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30, on the Berlioz canon (Ex. XI-14, viewed): "the effectiveness of the canon is due more to the color contrasts than to any other musical parameter". In the example the bassoons begin the line p and the trombones answer p over the strings' figuration. Adler PDF 20: Hindemith gives later statements new colours by new doublings. The exposition already follows the principle at 92 with the oboe. The reprise should not simply copy that, since c029's rule is that the reprise differs. So the reprise gets the clarinet: E5–G5 is its clarion register, it is soft at pp, and it is the colour the trio has just used for T. The entry keeps every structural detail of the passage. c037's dovetail stays: the held E5 at 247–248 resolves to D♯5 at 249 as Vln I enters. c098's diminuendo into that resolution stays. The pitches and harmony stay, as do the French sixth at 244 and the B pedal. The second violins rest until their pizzicato at 253, which also gives them more time for the change.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-161/work/sections/retr.ily, with a c161 comment).** Cl 1 takes Vln II's line at the same pitch. Cl 1 reaches pp at 249 together with Cl 2 (shared staff, equal dynamics) and then holds its B4 from 250 as before. ### Points checked and kept (my decisions)

- **209–216, Hn 1's held B4/C♯5 above T's lower octave (row 4).** In half of these bars the horn sits a second or third above Cl 1 and Vln II. The tune itself is in Vln I an octave higher with the top register to itself. The horns are marked p under the strings' f, and the Hn 1·2 thirds and sixths here are protected (c004, c094). Rewriting them would cost more than the doubling line loses. Kept.
- **339, low brass re-enter ff (row 18).** Tenuto, diminuendo, a level under the fff around them, and doubled by the combination of row 16. The weight comes from doubling and not from attack, as Dan's rule asks. c128's decision stands.
- **253–268, a soft horn on the cellos' sustained countermelody (row 9).** I considered it and decided against it. Horn colour would make the leggiero reprise heavier, and the horns are saved for 277–288 and the tutti at 299.
- **189–192, a horn on the cellos' answer in the trio (row 9).** I decided against it. The answer turns in quavers (D♯–E–D♯–C♯–D♯), which a horn would blur at this tempo, and c011's C.a. doubling already colours it.
- **341–344, Hn 1 alone with T (rows 2, 17).** This is the saved colour, and the hand-over to Ob 1 and the violins is the *Lohengrin* pattern. Kept (c111, c131, c141).
