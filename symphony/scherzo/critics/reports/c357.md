## Findings

**1. Bar 343, harp RH beat 2: a low second under the solo horn (applied).**
Problem: the harp plays `<d e gis>` (D3-E3-G sharp 3) on beat 2. This chord is the trio's 155 `<a b dis'>` moved down a fifth. It is the only harp chord in the movement with a second below G3 (my harp-chord scan). Its D3 grinds against the violas' held E3, which c347 added. It doubles the dominant seventh, D, that Hn 3 sounds on beat 3, and the D3 then drops to B2 without resolving.
Evidence: Sebesky's Ex. 1 (PDF 12-13) removes a line that adds nothing. This lightens the lower-middle register so the focal instrument comes through. Here the focal instrument is Hn 1's statement of T at 341. On economy (PDF 12), a voicing that looks right on paper can sound wrong. Close seconds around D3-E3 under a horn are that case. The bars around it (341, 342, 344) repeat one chord on beats 2 and 3.
Fix: `r4 <d e gis>4 <b, e gis>4` becomes `r4 <b, e gis>4 <b, e gis>4` in codaHarpR. The seventh stays in the horn alone, and the bar follows the repeated-chord pattern of its neighbours.

**2. Bar 176, Fl 1's answer sits inside the Vln II dyad (applied).**
Problem: c349 gave Fl 1 three answers in T's holds: 172 (G sharp-A-B), 176 (D sharp-E-F sharp) and 183-184. At 176 the answer starts at D sharp 5, in unison with the Vln II divisi off-beat dyad `<b' dis''>`. Its E5 then rubs a semitone against that held D sharp 5. The answers at 172 and 183 are clear. At 176 the answer blends into the background and can't be heard as an answer.
Evidence: on focus (PDF 13-14), the answer is a secondary element stepping up for a moment, and it needs its own space to do so. In Ex. 2A (PDF 17-18) the flute and harp fills move while the lead holds, in a register of their own, not inside the accompaniment.
Fix: `r4 dis''8( e''8 fis''4)` becomes `r4 dis'''8( e'''8 fis'''4)` in tAFluteOne. The figure stays T's head (3-4-5 of B), and F sharp 6 stays below the A6 kept for 217. My scan confirms the flute now has 176 to itself above the strings.
