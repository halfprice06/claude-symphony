## What else I studied

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 36 and 39: Vln II's bowed off-beats land on the tune's own note (economy, focus). Applied.

**Problem.** In the counterstatement the strings' answer 35–36 descends D5–A4–A4–F♯4, and its last note F♯4 (36, beat 3) is played at the same moment by Vln II's staccato off-beat F♯4. At 39 the tune G5–D5–D5–B4 ends its bar on B4, again together with Vln II's off-beat B4 (and with Cl 1's held B4). Both are the same colour (bowed violins, staccato crotchets), so the tune's note merges into the accompaniment. This is the case c227 fixed at 15–16 and 31–32 (the same theme's half cadence). It missed these two bars, which `touch.py` shows are now the only bowed cases left in the movement.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12: every note must make "a legitimate difference to the score as a whole"; an off-beat that only repeats the tune's own note does not. PDF 13: the arranger must see that the ear goes to the primary element "and not [be] diverted or confused by an overbusy background". PDF 12: a voicing "may look great on paper" and still sound wrong. On paper the off-beat looks like plain chord filling. Adler II PDF 259–260, 274, as cited by c227.

**Fix (applied, sA.ily `sAVlnTwo`).**
```
36: r4 fis'4-. r4      (was r4 fis'4-. fis'4-.)   -- the answer's last note is the tune's alone, as at 15 and 31
39: r4 b'4-. g'4-.     (was r4 b'4-. b'4-.)       -- G4, a third under the tune's B4, keeps the off-beat pulse into the f at 41
```
The violas' D4 off-beats and the held wind chord keep the harmony complete. No new parallels.

### 2. Bars 209–211: the forte return of T repeats 169–171's harmony and bass, and has inner octaves (harmonic variety, Ex. 1). Applied.

**Problem.** T's first phrase is heard at 153, 169 and 209. c089 kept 153 plain as the reference. 169–172 and 209–212 are the same: I – I – V7 – I over the bass E–E–B–E, with the same cello countermelody (B2–E3 | G♯3 | F♯3–A3 | G♯3–F♯3). Since c157, the second phrase of the return (213–216) has its own harmony. The first phrase, at the trio's loudest moment, is still a louder copy of 169. It also has two consecutive octaves that the per-player scan finds. The cellos and Bsn 2 move E3→G♯3→F♯3 while Hn 2 moves E4→G♯4→F♯4 (209→210→211). That makes octaves in the tenor, under the tune's lower octave, in the register Ex. 1 says to lighten.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19: "I usually try to avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice". Ex. 3A keeps Ex. 3's melody and changes the bass under it, and both reach the same Cmaj9. PDF 20: use substitutions "whenever you feel the need for a little lift in a musical phrase". PDF 12–13, Ex. 1: taking one middle line out of parallel motion made "the contrary motion of the remaining voices … more evident, thus more interesting".

**Fix (applied, tC.ily).** At 210 the bass rises to G♯ (I6) under the held B. The cellos and Bsn 2 exchange voices with the bass (G♯3 → E3 against E2 → G♯2), and Hn 2 holds E4:
```

tCCb:            e,2.\mf | gis,2. | b,,2. | ...                (was e,2. | e,2.)
tCCbsn:          e,2.--(\mp | gis,2.-- | ...                   (was e,2.--)
tCHarpL:         e,4\mf r4 r4 | gis,4 r4 r4 | ...              (was e,4)
tCHnTwo:         e'2.--(\p | e'2.-- | fis'2.--) | ...          (was gis'2.--)
```
- The bass now rises E–G♯–B into V, against the tune's rise to E6 and fall to B. The phrase still comes to rest on I at 212.
- The cellos' G♯3 returns on beat 3 of 210. My first version held E3 through the bar. The all-pairs scan then found new octaves E3–F♯3 against Bsn 1 and Hn 2 at 211, so I cut that version (principle 9).
