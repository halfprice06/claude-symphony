## What else I studied

**Tchaikovsky 5/iii (Valse, Allegro moderato, A major, 3/4).** I worked from my knowledge of the score and give no bar numbers. It has five features that matter here. The waltz is a string melody over a light accompaniment: bass on the beat and chords off the beat. The woodwinds take over and colour later phrases. The middle section is a string spiccato figure passed between the sections. On the waltz's return that figure continues against the tune, so the reprise changes in detail without changing in form. The motto comes back near the end as a quiet, dark quotation in low clarinets and bassoons. The brass are kept mostly in reserve. This scherzo has the same plan, and in places it is more ambitious: the motivic timpani, the fugato and its stretto, the ritmo di tre battute, and a trio tune that returns in the coda. A conductor would not doubt its form or its themes. Two of Tchaikovsky's habits are missing. His inner parts never sit on the note a melodic appoggiatura is leaning against, in the melody's own octave. Here that happens at the main cadence of the second idea (131, 287), and less clearly at 334. His horns usually slip in under a crescendo instead of arriving at the climax already playing. Here, at the trio's forte (197), Hn 1·2 come in at mf after 44 bars of rest.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 131 and 287: the second idea's last appoggiatura sits between held E4 and G4 (applied)
**Problem.** At the f cadence of the E-minor second idea (129-131, 285-287), Vln II doubles the tune an octave below Vln I (c010). On beat 1 of 131 it plays the appoggiatura F♯4 resolving to E4. At the same moment, in the same octave, Cl 1 and Hn 3 hold G4 (the chord's third) and Hn 2 holds E4 (the note of resolution). The result is a three-note cluster, E4-F♯4-G4, played f by clarinet, horn and violins, and it lasts a full crotchet. At 287 the same thing happens with Hn 1 and Cl 1 on G4. There is also a second clash an octave lower: the tenor tune in Hn 2 and the cellos (c071) has F♯3 against Bsn 1's held E3. This is the case Rimsky-Korsakov describes on PDF 132. His example even uses E: a line with non-chord notes runs against held chord notes in the same octave, and "the thirds of the fundamental one (E) are more prominent from their proximity with the notes extraneous". His remedy on PDF 133 is to keep the basis an octave away, or else in a strongly different colour. The difference between woodwind and brass "is less important". In Ex. 273-274 (PDF 439-440) the held winds lie an octave away from the moving figures. Tchaikovsky's tuttis follow the same practice.

**Fix (applied).** The held parts take the appoggiatura as a prepared suspension. They hold A/F♯ over the bar line and resolve with the tune to G/E on beat 2, so beat 1 has a 9/4 over E instead of a cluster. The notes of every chord after beat 1 are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 195-197: horns 1·2 should enter pp under the crescendo, not at mf on the forte (applied)
**Problem.** At 193-196 the strings, Cl 1·2 and Bsn 1 crescendo from p into the trio's forte statement at 197. Hn 1·2 have been silent since 152. They enter on the downbeat of 197 at mf, already sustaining. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127 (amplification) says that the group joining a crescendo "enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly". Ex. 248 (PDF 413) and Ex. 258 (PDF 421-422) show brass entering pp cresc. under the strings' crescendo. PDF 136 adds that brass returning after a long rest should come in pp or ff, because an mf return "produces a colourless and common-place effect". The mf level at 197 is a balance choice (c047, c129: brass one degree under the woodwind) and stays. Only the entry changes.

**Fix (applied, tB.ily).** The horns enter two bars early, pp, on the cadential 6/4 and its resolution. Hn 1 plays G♯4→F♯4 and Hn 2 plays E4→D♯4, the 6/4's correct resolution (c102). They double Cl 2 and Bsn 1, which already move this way.
```
tBHnOne = { R2.*10 | gis'2.(\pp\< | fis'2.) | gis'2(\mf a'4 | ...    % was R2. at 195-196
tBHnTwo = { R2.*10 | e'2.(\pp\< | dis'2.) | e'2.--(\mf | ...
```
The two horns have equal simultaneous dynamics on the shared staff. In parts-cues.json the Vln II cue in the horn parts (parts 12 and 13) is shortened from 193 + 4 bars to 193 + 2 bars, so it ends before the new entry. The protected layering at 197-200 (c117) is unchanged.

### 3. Bar 334: Ob 1 holds G5 against Ob 2's G-F♯-F in the same octave and colour (applied)
**Problem.** At the coda's ff arrival (333-335), Ob 2 plays theme A in the G5 octave, and its bar 334 is the motto's tail G5-F♯5-F5. Ob 1 holds G5 through all three bars, on the same staff. The chromatic quavers therefore rub against their own starting note in the same instrument. Rimsky's text (PDF 132-133) makes this the worst case: no difference of register and no difference of timbre. The violas and cor anglais make the same rub against the horns' held G4 and E4. I leave that one because it is string and woodwind against brass, and the notes are quavers at 2. = 96 (PDF 132: "the quicker the passage is played the less harsh the effect").

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
```
codaOboeOne = { ... g''2.\ff | g''2( fis''8 f''8) | g''2.\< | b''2.->\fff ...   % was g''2. at 334
```
Ob 1 joins Ob 2 for the tail and returns to its held G5 at 335.
