## Findings

### 1. Reprise of the second idea, 277–288: the bowing is the reverse of the exposition's, and the violas' hemiola falls on up-bows (applied)
**Bars:** Vln I and Vln II 277–284, violas 283–287.
**Problem:** In the exposition the same juncture, an f bar followed by a sub. p accented minim, is bowed V at 121. c218 then split the slur at 128 so that the hemiola at 129–131 lands on down-bows. The reprise has ⊓ at 277. Bar 276 has three separate crotchets (⊓ V ⊓), so the natural next bow is V, and the printed ⊓ makes the violins retake to the frog. That is the heavy end of the bow on a sub. p. It also puts the crescendo at 281–282 on a down-bow, which runs against the habit. The violas never had c218's split at 284 (`b2--( b4--~ | b4 e'2)`): their bows run ⊓ V ⊓ V from 277, so at 285–287 the minims dis'2 and b2. are up-bows. That reverses c085's protected plan of one down-bow per hemiola minim. Meanwhile the violins over the same bars are on down-bows.
**Answer to the recorded reasons:** c118 kept ⊓ because "its crescendo at 281–284 spans two slurs, so neither starting bow is better". c265 kept it because the sign "matches the natural direction" and gets 285 down-bow. The natural direction after 276 is V, not ⊓, and c218's split shows how to reach 285 down-bow from a V start. The crescendo is not neutral either: with V at 277 it starts on an up-bow, where Adler says a crescendo is natural.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; "the tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural"; mark the bow only "where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". PDF 37, Ex. II-19: the bowing puts ⊓ on the stressed beats. PDF 38: an upbeat quaver taken as its own up-bow gives "a down-bow on the accented first beat of the next measure". Dvořák 9/iii marks its soft entries up-bow (strings.notesS.ily lines 18, 36).
**Fix (applied):** I changed slurs and one sign only, with no notes or dynamics. In dA.ily, `dAVlnOne` 277 has `b''2->\subP\upbow( g''4~` (it had `\dnb`), and 284 has `dis'''4 b''4.) cis'''8 |`. `dAVlnTwo` has `b'2->\subP\upbow( g'4~` and `dis''4 b'4.) cis''8 |`. `dAVla` 283–284 has `b2--( b4--~ | b4) e'2 |`. The simulation now gives the exposition's plan in all three parts. At 277 V, 279 ⊓, 281 V (crescendo), 283 ⊓, 284 the quaver V, 285 ⊓ f. The violas' minims at 286 and 287 fall on down-bows. This undoes c265's "keep the down-bow at 277" and gives the reason above.

### 2. Reprise fugato, 293–297: the cellos' crescendo is on a down-bow and their f stretto entry on an up-bow (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 293–300.
**Problem:** In the shortened reprise fugato the cellos have the countersubject in one two-bar slur at 293–294 (`d'4(\pp c' bes | a4 bes g)`). That bow is V after the three crotchets of 292, so 295–296, the pp < into the stretto, is a down-bow. The f entry at 297, the stretto's first downbeat (`a,4-.\f`), then starts up-bow. The marked ⊓ at 300 becomes a retake. In the exposition the cellos at 67–72 bow the other way: the crescendo is V at 67, the f is ⊓ at 69, and the ⊓ at 72 is natural.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: crescendo on the up-bow; the frog for weight; mark only against habit, and here the slurring can set the habit without a sign. PDF 37: the strong beat gets the down-bow.
**Fix (applied):** In dB.ily, `dBVc` 293–294 is now `d'4(\pp c' bes) | a4( bes g)`, one bow per bar. Each bow is 0.68 s at pp. The simulation gives 293 V, 294 ⊓, 295 V with the crescendo, 297 ⊓ f, 298 V, 299 ⊓ V ⊓ V, and 300 ⊓, which is now natural. The notes and the protected harmony at 61–68/293–296 do not change.

### 3. Trio, forte return, 217–220: the basses' f arrival and diminuendo fall on an up-bow, against the cellos (applied)
**Bars:** Cb 216–223.
**Problem:** The basses play one dotted minim per bow. Bar 216 has two notes (`b,,2 fis,,4`), so 217 starts up-bow (crescendo, good), 218 is a down-bow, and 219, the f peak under the violins' A6 with its diminuendo, is an up-bow. c218 rebowed the cellos so that their crescendo is V at 217 and their diminuendo is ⊓ at 219. c118 bowed the violins' climax ⊓. At the trio's loudest bar the bass group is the one part bowing against the habit.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: a diminuendo comes naturally on a down-bow. PDF 37: one slur is one bow. PDF 39: cello and bass bows are shorter. A two-bar bow at mf to f is 1.58 s, the same length as the cellos' bows here.
**Fix (applied):** In tC.ily, `tCCb` is now `cis,2.(\< | gis,,2.) | fis,,2.(\f\> | a,,2.) |`. The simulation gives 217 V (crescendo), 219 ⊓ (f, diminuendo), 221 V, 222 ⊓, and 223 V. That last up-bow is now the natural one, the bow before the pizz. at 225 that c096 marked (Adler PDF 54).
