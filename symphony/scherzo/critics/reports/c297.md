### A. Bars 305–320: one horn on the tenor countermelody (tested, kept)
c097 put Hn 3 and Hn 4 in unison on the countermelody at 105–120 and 305–320, citing Adler PDF 44 (two horns equal one trumpet or trombone). c114 later moved Hn 4 at 305–320 to hold the bass an octave above Bsn 2 (E3, B2, A2, F♯3, G3), so the reprise has one horn on the line with the cellos and Bsn 1. By row 10 that halves the line's brass weight. The surroundings are different from 105–120, though. At 305–320 the trombones, bass trombone and tuba rest (c244), so the brass competing with the line are Hn 1·2's crotchets on beats 1 and 3 and the two trumpets' motive x in the odd bars. In Bartók's terms (PDF 44, 46), one horn sustaining a legato line in its middle register (sounding G3–E4) beside two articulated trumpets that rest every other bar is not overmatched. The cellos espress. and Bsn 1 also play the line. Restoring the a2 would leave the octave above the bass empty in a tutti, which c114 and the Rimsky appendix rule protect, because without the trombones Hn 4 is the only part at E3. Keep. c114's reason stands.

### B. Bars 329–332: the coda's stretto turns homorhythmic (tested, kept)
In Bartók's stretto (PDF 47, mm. 363–379) the trumpets and trombones keep independent, offset rhythms up to the climax. In the coda here the four string entries of motive x (Vc 325, Vla 326, Vln II 327, Vln I 328) all play the figure in the same rhythm from 328, so 329–332 is a four-part chord progression (E minor, D/A, F, B) in motive x rhythm. The figure is a one-bar ostinato, not a subject with a continuation, so once all four entries are in there is no line left to offset. The texture that Bartók adds at this point matches the reading (row 11): sustained winds come in underneath, p<, from 329 (Cl 1·2, B.cl., Bsn 1·2, Cbsn), the horns from 331, and the woodwinds reach the climax at 336. The passage is also protected (c129, c279), and its parallels at 326–328 are the known by-design ones in analysis.txt. Keep.

### C. Bars 57–68: no brass in the fugato (tested, kept)
Hindemith's fugato (PDF 42–44) and Bartók's (PDF 45–46) are brass fugatos. Here the fugato is for strings alone, and the brass join only at the stretto's last entry (Hn 1·2 at 71/299). The strings-only exposition is protected (c008, c254, c269) for its pp colour before the F-major episode. It also follows the reading's order in Bartók, where the horns enter after the exposition, at the stretto stage (PDF 46, m. 342, then the stretto from m. 363). Brass in the exposition would also be the louder group under a p subject, against row 4. Keep.
