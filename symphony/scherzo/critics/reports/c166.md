## Findings

### 1. Bar 335: Vln I leaps into B6 above its practical limit; Fl 1 rubs against it on beat 3 (applied)

Problem. Vln I plays E6–B5–B5–G6 at 335 and then B6 fff at 336. That is a minor sixth (B5→G6) and a major third (G6→B6) in quavers and crotchets at 2. = 88, all above the violins' practical limit of about G6. Fl 1 doubles Vln I at pitch but plays G6–A6 quavers on beat 3 against Vln I's G6 crotchet, so the two top parts rub a second apart on the last quaver before the peak.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 26: higher notes "should only be used with caution, that is to say when they are of long value … in not too rapid sequence of scales … Skips should always be avoided". The B6 itself is a dotted minim (long value), so it is allowed; the approach to it is not. c126 fixed the flute's approach (Adler I PDF 186) but kept Vln I on motive x. This fix answers c126's reason: Vln I keeps motive x's rhythm, a crotchet, two quavers and a crotchet, and only the pitches become a scale. The motive's pitches E–B–B–G stay in Vln II, violas, Cl 1, Ob 2 and the trumpets in the same bar.

Fix (coda.ily):

codaFluteOne 335: e'''4-.)\< fis'''8-. g'''-. a'''4( | b'''2.->\fff)

### 2. Bars 121–128: Hn 3 has no rest between the tutti and its f B4 (applied)

Problem. Hn 3 holds B2. p< to ff at 101–104, plays the ff tenor countermelody in minims at 105–120, rests two beats, holds B3/C4 sub. p at 121–128, then leaps an octave to B4 f at 129–132. It sounds in 96 % of bars 101–132, more than any other brass player in the movement, and its only high, exposed note in the passage comes at the end. Hn 4 plays the same tenor line at 105–120 and then rests from 121 to 140.

Evidence. Rimsky PDF 37: wind players "cannot manage extremely long sustained passages … care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time"; PDF 44: "the remarks on breathing, in the section devoted to the wood-wind, apply with equal force to the brass". Even horns take the low held notes (Table C, PDF 43: B3/C4 sound as written F♯4/G4, in Hn 4's comfortable range). The reprise already rests Hn 3 at 281–284 before its B4 at 285 (c157). The exposition lacked that rest.
