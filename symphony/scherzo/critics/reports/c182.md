## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 200→201: violas and bass in parallel octaves G♯–A. This is a side effect of c177. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 200 the violas double the 9–8 line A4→G♯4 (with Cl 1, Vln II and Hn 1) over the bass G♯. c177 then gave the violas A4 at 201 to carry Vln I's top line. Viola G♯4→A4 now moves in octaves with the bass G♯1/G♯2→A1/A2 (Cb, Vc, Bsn 2), both by step, at the start of the p phrase. In v177 the violas went to E4 and there were no octaves.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18: parallel octaves are "wrong". The free-texture allowance (PDF 84–85) covers a part that keeps doubling another. The violas here are an independent line: the 9–8 resolution, then the ♭6–5 line.
**Fix.**
```
tB.ily  tBVla  bar 201:  e'4(\p a'2)      (was a'2.\p)
```
G♯4→E4 moves against the rising bass. The violas take A4 on beat 2, at the moment Vln I's tenuto A4 crotchet ends. So the line A4–C5–B4 (c070, c177) still sounds without a break, and the violas do not overlap Vln I's note (PDF 88–89). E4→A4 is a 4th over a held bass, followed by a step (Ex. 5-11c).

### 2. 48→49: Hn 1 moves B4→A4 in octaves with the bass B→A in the G-major second idea. APPLIED.
**Problem.** Hn 1 holds dotted minims against the hemiola. At 48 it returns to B4, the third of I6, over the bass B (Vc, Cb, Bsn 1, Cbsn on beats 2–3). At 49 both fall to A (V/V). This is a stepwise parallel octave between an inner voice and the bass, f, in an exposed homophonic passage.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18. PDF 80: inner voices "complete the tones of the chord" and repeat or step. G4 completes both of bar 48's chords (E minor, then G/B).
**Fix.**
```
sA.ily  sAHnOne  bar 48:  g'2.--)        (was b'2.--))
```
Hn 1 now reads B–B–A–G | A A A A and rises against the falling bass. B stays in the bass and Bsn 1. Hn 1 and Cl 1 now meet on G4→A4 at the unison (PDF 85: unison doublings are normal in orchestral writing). The protected hemiola cadence 53–55 and c032's bass are unchanged.

### 3. 124–125 and 280–281: Cl 1 shadows the bass G–A–C in octaves for three notes. APPLIED.
**Problem.** Cl 1's counter-line (F♯4 G4 A4 C5 A4 G4 F♯4 E4) moves exactly with the bass G2–A2–C3 (Vc, Bsn 2, Cb) at 124.2–125.3 in the E-minor second idea, and again at 280–281. The last move is a leap in both voices (A→C). Cl 1 is otherwise an independent line: it holds F♯4 over B–A at 123 and moves against the bass at 126–128. So this is three parallel octaves, not a doubling.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18. PDF 87, rule 7: skips in both voices emphasise the octave. PDF 84 distinguishes a persistent doubling voice (Ex. 5-26, the left hand's low notes) from a line that joins another briefly.
**Fix.**
```
sC.ily  sCClOne  bars 125-126:  c''2(\< a'4~ | a'2.)     (was a'2(\< c''4~ | c''4 a'2))
dA.ily  dAClOne  bars 281-282:  c''2\<( a'4~ | a'2.)     (was a'2\<( c''4~ | c''4 a'2))
```
Cl 1 now exchanges voices with the bass (C5–A4 over A–C). It holds A4 into 126, so the seventh of c082's V♭9 is prepared by common tone and still falls to G4 at 127 (127 and the V♭9 voicing are untouched; c132's do-not-touch holds). For two minims Cl 1 is in unison with Ob 1 and Vln II, which is a doubling (PDF 85). G4→C5 is a 4th followed by a step down (Ex. 5-11c).

### 4. 216: the leading tone is doubled at the trio's forte half cadence. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 216 (V of E), Bsn 1 and Hn 2 resolve V6/5 of V's seventh E4→D♯4, as they must. The cellos' countermelody, doubled by Bsn 2, also arrives on D♯3 from C♯3. So two independent lines hold the leading tone, approached by contrary motion. The second statement (176) has D♯3 in the cellos with only the violas' off-beat filler above it, so the doubling came in when the forte return added the horn and bassoon choir.
**Evidence.** PDF 76, Ex. 5-4: the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V or VII or their inversions".
**Fix.**
```
tC.ily  tCVc      bar 216:  fis2 b,4)            (was dis2 b,4))
tC.ily  tCBsnTwo  bar 216:  fis2 b,4) \breathe   (was dis2 b,4) \breathe)
```
The cellos take F♯3, the 5th of V, and still move on beat 3 (c051). Their line C♯3–F♯3–B2–E3 echoes the C♯–F♯ of 214, and each leap changes direction (PDF 81). D♯ is now one pitch, D♯4 (Bsn 1, Hn 2, and the violas' off-beat filler in unison). I rejected two alternatives. B2–F♯3 makes octaves by contrary motion with the basses' B1–F♯1 (PDF 86, Ex. 5-27). B2–D♯3 doubles the leading tone again and then moves D♯→E in octaves with Hn 2 at 217.

### 5. 229–232: the codetta's tonic has no third in any sustained part. APPLIED.
**Problem.** While Cl 1 plays T's head, the sustained chord is Vln II E5, violas B3, Hn 4 E3 and the bass E. G♯ is heard only on the harp's beat-2 pluck and in the clarinet's first note. In 230 and 232, where Cl 1 is on B4, beats 1 and 3 are bare E–B. The neighbouring four-bar units (225–228, 233–236) have G♯4 in the violas. c047 lowered the violas to B3 only to clear the clarinet's register.
**Evidence.** PDF 76: the 3rd "is never omitted except for special effects" because of "the empty sound and the lack of major or minor quality". Ex. 5-5 omits the 5th, never the 3rd.
**Fix.**
```
tC.ily  tCVla  bars 229-232:  gis2. | gis2. | a2. | gis2.     (was b2. | b2. | a2. | b2.)
```
The violas stay below Cl 1, so c047 and c127 hold. The A3 at 231 (IV) is unchanged. The fifth is now in Cl 1, or omitted, which PDF 76 allows.
