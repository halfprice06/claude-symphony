## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Coda peak 336-339: low brass cover the B6 and take the weight away from the final chord. Applied.
**Bars 336-338, 339.** At 336, Tbn 1·2 (F♯3, D♯3), the bass trombone (B2) and the tuba (B1) all hold fff under Vln I and Fl 1's B6, the movement's one peak. They also hold through the hemiola (337-338). The final E-minor chord at 339 then arrives in the same weight that has been sounding for three bars.
**Evidence.** Barber, Adler PDF 150, points 3 and 4. Barber leaves trombones and tuba out of the bar of the high note so that the high note is not made heavy and keeps its emphasis; it is held up by the horns. The entry of the low brass and timpani on the last low note then "really reinforces the finality". d'Indy (PDF 147-148) marks his trombones ppp inside a tutti so they do not dominate. The horns (B4, F♯4, D♯4, B3), trumpets, timpani roll, contrabassoon, cellos and basses still give the V chord a full fff bass.
**Fix (coda.ily).** Tbn 1·2, the bass trombone and the tuba rest at 336-338 and come back fff on 339:
`codaTbnOne = { R2.*8 | g2.\ff | g2. | g2. | R2.*3 | g4->\fff r4 r4 | ...`. Make the same change in `codaTbnTwo` (`e4->\fff`), `codaBassTbn` (`e,4->\fff`) and `codaTuba` (`e,4->\fff`).
This keeps c033 (the low brass are still held back through the reprise and return ff at 333). It changes c028's "fff in all parts at 336" for these four parts only.

### 2. Coda 333-335: the summing-up statement of theme A's head is carried by four parts against a held chord. Applied.
**Bars 333-335.** Only Vln I and Fl 1 (E6) and Vln II and Ob 1 (E5) play the head. Clarinets, bassoons, violas, horns, trombones and tuba all hold E-minor dotted minims ff, and the trumpets add motive x. Adler PDF 150 describes this place exactly: a multi-octave unison that "summarizes an old idea". Here the figure is a thin line on top of a block chord.
**Evidence.** Smetana (PDF 154, XV-5): the agile instruments play the figure in octaves while the brass sustain, and that sustained brass is what holds the tutti together. Mozart (PDF 151): the octave with the most instruments is the one the listener hears as the theme.
**Fix (coda.ily).** Cl 1 doubles Vln II and Ob 1 at E5, and the violas take the octave below. Cl 2, the bassoons, the horns and the low brass keep the sustained chord.
`codaClOne`, 333-335: `e''4-.\ff b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | g''2->( fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.) b'8-. b'-. g''4-. |` (then D♯5 at 336 as before).
`codaVla`, 333-335: `e'4-.\ff b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->( fis'8 f'8 | e'4-.) b8-. b-. g'4-. |` (then D♯4 at 336 as before).
Hn 3 (E4), Hn 2 (G4), Hn 1 (B4), Cl 2, Fl 2 and Ob 2 still hold the chord. The chromatic F♮ against the held E minor is the same clash every tutti statement of theme A already has (105, 305). The analyser reports no new parallels.

### 3. Reprise 258 and 261: Fl 1's heterophony reaches B6 at pp and anticipates the peak note. Applied.
**Bars 258, 261.** The dA heterophony puts the flute an octave above the p violins, in the flute's most penetrating register. At 258 (`a'''8( b''' a''' gis''' g'''4)`) and 261 (`a'''8( b''' a''' g''' fis'''4)`) it touches B6. That is the only B6 in the movement apart from the coda peak at 336, where Fl 1 plays it again with Vln I.
**Evidence.** Adler I PDF 185-186: above A6 the notes are difficult, and extreme high notes can only be played loud, so pp B6 is not playable as written. Adler II PDF 152: the high flute octave "is so piercing it need not be doubled... to cut through", so a decoration in that octave is heard ahead of the theme. PDF 146 (minimum colour) and PDF 150 (Barber keeps the high note's emphasis): the movement's top note belongs to 336.
**Fix (dA.ily, Fl 1).** Turn below instead of above: 258 `a'''8( gis''' a''' gis''' g'''4)`, 261 `a'''8( g''' a''' g''' fis'''4)`. The flute's highest note in 253-268 is now A6. The analyser shows the flutes' range ending at A6 except for the shared B6 at 336.

### 4. Trio 197-200: the forte return of the call has Vln I alone on top, and Fl 2 holds a C♯5 that adds nothing. Applied.
**Bars 197-200.** This is the trio's only f before 209. Vln I has the call at E6 alone. Ob 1 and Vln II are at E5, under Hn 1·2 f, clarinets and bassoons. Fl 1 rests (`\partCombineSoloII`). Fl 2 holds C♯5 f, the weak low-middle register of the flute (Adler I PDF 185-186), where the horns cover it.
**Evidence.** Adler II PDF 151-152. Mozart puts his flute where it fills the texture, not where it "would have added nothing". Beethoven gives the top octave to the flutes alone because the high flute carries without help. The trio's colours otherwise reserve the flute for heterophony and T's doubling; one bright top octave at its single forte marks the climax of 185-208.
**Fix (tB.ily).** `tBFluteOne`, bars 13-17 of the section: `e'''4(\f dis'''8 cis'''8 dis'''8 e'''8 | cis'''2 gis''4) | a''4.( b''8 cis'''4) | gis''2.\> | R2.\! |`, replacing the `\partCombineSoloII` rests. Fl 2 keeps its C♯5 under it. Both flutes are f at 197 and diminuendo at 200, so the shared staff has equal dynamics (checked on page 7).
