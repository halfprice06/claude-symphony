## Findings

### 1. 247–248: the retransition's accented 6/4 has no 6th (applied)

**Problem.** The second dominant preparation (245–252) is a four-bar 6/4 over the B pedal, which falls to V at 249. At 247–248 the only notes over the bass clarinet's B2 and the pizz./timpani B are Cl 1's held E5: a bare 4th. The G sounds only as Cl 1's accented passing G5 at 246, inside the head, and that beat is a V with ♭6 (the violas already have D♯4).

Unit 19 §3 (PDF 306) defines the accented 6/4 as the tonic's upper voices over the dominant. Its 6th and 4th "move down by step to the 5th and 3rd", and its function is "to delay the arrival of an expected melodic or harmonic event". With only the 4th, 247–248 sounds as V sus4, not as the delayed E-minor tonic. The first retransition's 6/4 is complete at 89 (Bsn 1 G3). The tonic E minor also needs this confirmation here, because the music is coming out of 88 bars of E major. c493 Finding 3 saw the gap and declined it for two reasons:
- Hn 1 G4 would bend the protected G♯–G–F♯ descent (240–245) back up.
- A Bsn 1 G3 would be inaudible.

The fix below answers both: it uses neither the horn nor the bassoon.

**Fix (applied, retr.ily, retrVlnTwo).**
- old: `retrVlnTwo = { R2.*12 | }`
- new: `retrVlnTwo = { R2.*6 | g'2.~\ppp\< | g'2.(\pp\> | fis'4)\ppp r4 r4 | R2.*3 | }`

**Effect.**
- Vln II's G4 lies a 6th under Cl 1's E5. The two fall together, 6–5 and 4–3 in parallel 6ths, into V at 249: the four-voice form of Ex. 19-3 and Ex. 19-4.
- Vln II's F♯4 meets Cl 2's F♯4 entering at 249, so the string hands its note to the clarinet (c037's dovetail).
- Its swell is one level under Cl 1's pp < p > pp (c268), so it stays background.
- Vln II has rested only since 240, so it needs no cue. It has 250–252 to take pizz. for 253, and its senza sord. holds from 197.
- Nothing is silenced or moved, and parts-cues.json has no cue on Vln II at these bars.
- Hn 1, Cl 1, the violas, the timpani (c427) and the bass are unchanged. MIDI check: 247.1–248.3 now scan as a complete Em6/4.

**Why this is the change to build.** Among the points the reading supports, it is the one a listener would hear most: the minor tonic colour sounds under the clarinet just before the dominant that brings the scherzo back. It breaks no rule and no protection. Findings 3–6 give the reasons against the other candidates.
