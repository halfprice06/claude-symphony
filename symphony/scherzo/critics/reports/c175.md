## Findings

### 1. Unmarked ff double stops at the stretto peaks 72 and 300 (applied)
**Bars:** 72 and 300; Vln II E5–C♯6, violas E4–A4, cellos A2–E3.
**Problem:** These are the two loudest string chords before the coda, written as double stops on purpose (c044: "strings in double stops on the ff A-major chord"). The score marks "div." at 169, 209, 349 and "non div." at 105 and 305, so a section leader reading 72 cannot tell which is meant, and in a loud tutti many sections divide two-note chords by default, which halves each note.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 30: "Some composers wish to have each player perform double-stops. In that case, it is safer to write non div. so that no division between the two players will occur." c085 applied the same rule at 105/305.
**Fix (applied):** sB.ily `<e'' cis'''>2.->\ff\dnb\>^\markup \italic "non div."` (and the same markup on the violas' `<e' a'>` and cellos' `<a, e>`); dB.ily bar 300 `<e'' cis'''>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div."~` and the same for violas and cellos. The text prints above the ⊓ sign without collisions (checked on pages 3 and 10).

### 2. Violins II: pizz. to arco in three beats at 271–273 (applied)
**Bars:** Vln II 271–273.
**Problem:** Vln II plucks G4 on beats 2 and 3 of 271, rests in 272, and plays the theme arco f on the downbeat of 273: three silent beats, 0.68 s at 2. = 88, to pick up the bow for a forte entry. The violas in the same bars enter on beat 2 of 273 and have five beats from their last pluck; every other pizz.→arco change in the movement has five beats or more (c018, c085).
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck and then again to resume the natural way of playing … should be avoided if at all possible. (This is especially true of the return from pizzicato to arco, which is a bit more awkward …)".
**Fix (applied):** dA.ily `dAVlnTwo` bar 271 `r4 g'4\pp\< r4` (was `r4 g'4\pp\< g'4`). The violas keep B3 on that beat and Vln I has the theme, so the chord is unchanged. Five beats from pluck to bow, as the violas.

### 3. Violas: single notes inside the divisi at 352–353 (applied)
**Bars:** 349–356.
**Problem:** The violas are "div." from 349 (thirds and fifths), then have a single E4 for two bars (352–353), then double stops again (354–356) until "unis." at 357. With nothing at 352 the inside players do not know whether to play the E4 or rest.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 30: "When divisi is no longer in order and the part returns to single notes, the word unisoni (or more commonly the abbreviated, unis.) should appear on the part." The score does this at 193, 225, 245 and 357.
**Fix (applied):** coda.ily `codaVla`: `e'2.^\markup \italic "unis." | e'2. | <gis e'>2.^\markup \italic "div."`. Checked on pages 12–13 and in the viola part.

### 4. Cellos and basses: the closing diminuendo falls on an up-bow (applied)
**Bars:** Vc and Cb 358–364.
**Problem:** Both enter at 358 after a rest (the basses also from pizz.), play three separate E's (358, 359, 360) and then hold E for four bars (361–364) under a diminuendo to nothing. Starting down-bow by habit, the held E is an up-bow, so the fade runs toward the frog, where the arm's weight grows. Vln II and violas, fading at the same moment, reach their held note on a down-bow.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … Conversely, there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow. … [the composer] should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players."
**Fix (applied):** coda.ily `codaVc` bar 358 `e2.\pp\upbow`, `codaCb` bar 358 `e,2.\pp\upbow^\markup \italic "arco"`. The held E 361–364 is now a down-bow diminuendo. One sign per part, no other bowing changed.

### 5. Violas: staccato dots on pizz. notes at 253–271 (applied)
**Bars:** violas 253–271.
**Problem:** In the reprise the violas and Vln II play the same off-beat crotchets pizz. The viola notes carry staccato dots (copied from the arco exposition 13–28), the Vln II notes do not, and no other pizz. note in the score has a dot. Two string parts playing the same figure look as if they are to be played differently.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 53–55: pizz. examples (Berlioz, Ex. II-54; Brahms 1/iv, Ex. II-55) write plucked notes plain; PDF 54: "Whenever pizzicato is desired, the entire word … must appear in both the score and the parts", the word alone sets the stroke. c018 kept the dots only because removing them made the analyser report a false parallel at 263; I removed them in a test build and the current analyser reports the same seven parallels as v175, so that reason no longer holds.
**Fix (applied):** dA.ily `dAVla` bars 253–271: dots removed from the pizz. notes (`r4 b4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." b4 | …`); the arco notes from 273 keep theirs.
