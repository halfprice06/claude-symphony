## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Trio tune T, third phrase: V7 goes back to IV (163–164, 179–180, 219–220, 359–360). Applied.
**Problem.** Cycle 11 moved T's peak to A5 at bar 11 of the tune, and the bass under it stayed B. Bar 163 is now V7 (B, D♯, with A5 as its 7th), and bar 164 is IV (A–C♯–E) before the cadential 6/4 at 165. That is the retrogression the reading forbids: "Thus: I-IV-V-I ... but not I-V-IV-I" (p. 135), and intermediate harmonies "do not follow these chords" (p. 148, rule 2). The 7th A5 does not resolve; it moves to C♯5. The obvious fix, IV at 163, makes parallel octaves G♯→A between the melody and the bass (162 → 163), which is the "wrong" Example 9-11a on p. 141.
**Fix.** Use ii at 163 (bass F♯) and ii6 at 164 (bass A): the supertonic expanded from II to II6 (p. 145, §11). Melody A–G♯–F♯ is chord tone, passing tone, root; C♯–B–A over ii6 is the same. The 4th of the 6/4 at 165 (E) then enters by step from F♯, which is the accented passing tone the reading gives for a 6/4 that follows II6 (p. 153). The melody and rhythm do not change.

### 2. The coda's ff cadence has no intensification of V (341–347). Applied.
**Problem.** The scherzo's strongest cadence is three bars of i (341–343), then three bars of V7 (344–346), then i (347). There is no intermediate harmony and no 6/4 between the tonic and the dominant. The reading presents I–V–I as the plainest form of the basic progression (pp. 134–135) and the cadential 6/4 as the device that "embellish[es] and intensif[ies] the dominant" (p. 152). The other two hemiola cadences (53, 129) already use it, so the climax is the weakest of the three.
**Fix.** Change only the bass of bar 343 to B. The upper voices at 343 (E–G–B, and the melody E6–B5–G6) then form a cadential 6/4 on a strong bar, approached from tonic (p. 160, pt. 7), and resolve at 344 by 8–7, 6–5, 4–3 (Ex. 10-4a, p. 154). Cl 1 E5→D♯5, Hn 3 E4→D♯4, Tbn 2 E→D♯, Vla E4→D♯4, Tpt 2 E4→D♯4; Bsn 1, Ob 2, Hn 2, Tbn 1 G→F♯; Cl 2 and the B6 → A6 melody 8–7. B6 stays at 344. In coda.ily bar 11: `codaBsnTwo b,2.`, `codaCbsn b,,2.`, `codaBassTbn b,2.`, `codaTuba b,,2.`, `codaVc b,2.`, `codaCb b,,2.`, `codaTimp b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4` (motive x on the dominant drum; the E drum against a B bass would sound the 4th in the bass register).

### 3. Second idea in E minor: the cadential 6/4 is approached from V (127–129, 287–289). Applied.
**Problem.** The hemiola minims are i (127.1) – V (127.3) – V (128.2) – 6/4 (129) – V7 – i. The 6/4 "should not be approached by dominant harmony" (p. 160, pt. 7). Its 4th (E in Cl 2 and Vla) enters from D♯ below, not as a common tone or by step from above (p. 153). The G-major version at 51–53 reaches its 6/4 through V6/V, so the E-minor version is the weaker of the two.
**Fix.** Make the bass rise by step, as in Ex. 9-12 (p. 141): E (i) – F♯ (passing V6/4 under the melody's D♯6) – G (i6 under B5) – B (6/4). The 6/4 is now approached from tonic, and its 4th E is held from the i6 in the violas (a suspension, p. 153). The melody does not change. In sC bars 23–24 and dA bars 31–32: Vc `e2( fis4~ | fis4 g2)`, Cb and Cbsn `e,2( fis,4~ | fis,4 g,2)`, Bsn 1 `e2( fis4~ | fis4 g2)`, Bsn 2 `e2 fis4~ | fis4 g2`, Vla bar 24/32 `b4 e'2)`, Cl 1 `fis'4 e'2)`, Cl 2 `b4 g2)`, Hn 2 `e'2 b4~ | b4 e'2`. My parallel check found the first versions of the inner voices made octaves (Hn 2 and Cl 1 moving F♯→G with the bass) and fifths (Cl 1 against Vla), so these are the corrected versions.

### 4. Melodic augmented 2nd C→D♯ from ii°6 to V7 at theme A's peak (24, 116, 268, 320). Applied.
**Problem.** Under the melody's C6 appoggiatura, the violas' off-beats go from C4 (ii°6) to D♯4 (V7) in all four statements, and Hn 1 goes from C5 to D♯5 at 116 and 320. In II6–V in minor "6̂ can (and usually should) descend to 5̂" (p. 138), and the augmented 2nd is marked "not" in Ex. 9-6c and "poor" in Ex. 9-7f (p. 139).
**Fix.** `sAVla`, `sCVla`, `dAVla`, `dCVla` bar 12: `r4 b4-. dis'4-.` (C→B, then the leading tone by a third). `sCHnOne`, `dCHnOne` bar 11: `e''4-> r4 e''4-.` (E5 belongs to ii°6/5 and steps down to D♯5 at 116/320).

### 5. Root-position diminished triad on a strong bar in the F-major episode (79). Applied.
**Problem.** Bar 79 is E–G–B♭ for a whole bar: vii° in root position in F, with the leading tone E in the bass and doubled in the harp LH (E3) and Hn 3 (E4). The reading calls the root-position diminished triad "at once harsh and thin" and allows it only without rhythmic stress (p. 137; §13, p. 148). Bar 79 begins a two-bar unit.
**Fix.** Add the root C so that the chord is V6/5 of F, with the E single in the bass: `sBHnThree` bar 23 `c'2.`, `sBHarpL` bar 23 `e,4 <c g bes>2` (D–F–A → C–G–B♭ → C–F–A; B♭ falls to A as the 7th should). The harp's melody (73–80, protected) does not change.

### 6. The link sits on an unsupported tonic 6/4 for four bars (145–148). Applied.
**Problem.** When Hn 1 turns G to G♯ at 145, the bassoons stop. The chord is then Cl B3–E4 with Hn G♯4, so B3 is the bass: an E-major 6/4 for four bars that resolves to root-position E at 149. The reading says such a 6/4 is a dissonant chord that belongs to V, not a form of I (p. 152). Here it reverses that meaning just as the trio's key arrives.
**Fix.** `linkBsnTwo` bars 5–8: `e,2.\pp | e,2. | e,2. | e,2 r4`. Bsn 2 keeps the E pedal it held at 141–144.

### 7. Not applied, recorded so nobody "fixes" it. At 53 and 129 the melody arpeggiates the 6/4 (D–B–G, B–G–E), and its 4th then rises a step to the 7th of V7. The reading's rule is about the voice that holds the 4th (p. 153). In both bars that voice is the violas, which resolve G→F♯ and E→D♯, so the voice leading is correct. The doubling of the 4th is the "free texture" exception (p. 154).
