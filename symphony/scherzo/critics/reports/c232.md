## Findings

None of the 35 principles is violated in a way that a change would improve. Every applied chord resolves its temporary leading tone and its 7th, and none doubles the leading tone in two real voices. Every 7th moves in one of the ways the units show: it falls by step, is decorated, transferred, delayed or extended, or is held as a common tone. The reprise's ♮VII7 → V6/5 at 273–276 is Ex. 24-18's device in its exact role, set against the exposition's D7 → G at 41–45. The trio's 196→197 and 199→201 are textbook deceptive motions, with B♯→C♯ and D♯→C♯ giving the doubled 3rd.

I tested and rejected the points below, ranked by how close they came. No file in /tmp/critic-232/work changed.

### 1. Bar 354→355: the 6/4's 4th rises in the violas (kept)
- **Issue.** Over B, the violas' E4 goes to F♯4 and G♯3 goes to A3. The 4–3 (E→D♯) and 6–5 (G♯→F♯) happen only in the tune (Vln I/II, Cl 1, E6/E5 → D♯ on beat 2 of 355). That is two octaves above the violas. A&S PDF 406–407 (Unit 24 §9) says a transferred resolution works best "in the next lower voice and in the same register".
- **Fix considered (worked out on paper, not built).** At 355, change the violas from A3/F♯4 to `<a dis'>2.`, so their E4 falls to D♯4.
- **Why rejected.**
  - It doubles the leading tone D♯ against the tune's D♯5/D♯6 on beat 2. That breaks the doubling rule on PDF 439.
  - It moves the violas onto the leading tone a beat before the tune reaches it.
  - The coda cadence voicing 354–355 is protected (c092).

### 2. Bars 103–105: the dominant 7th A doubled (kept)
- **Issue.** Cl 1 has A4/A5 and Tbn 1 (with Bsn 1 at 104) A3. They resolve together, A→G, at 105. PDF 439 says "Don't double ... the 7th".
- **Why rejected.** This is one resolving line doubled at two octaves in a ff tutti, not two voices. c019 already took the brass off doubled sevenths, and c093 protects Cl 1's G5 as the resolution in register while Vln I leaps to theme A's E5 (Ex. 24-11's exact case: the soprano leaves the 7th to begin a theme on 5̂). Removing Tbn 1's A3 would leave the trombone chord without its 7th under the hemiola build.

### 3. Bar 276: the V6/5's bass D♯ is thin (kept)
- **Issue.** Cellos and basses rest at 276 (pizz.→arco change), so D♯ sits only in Bsn 2 (one crotchet) and the violas' off-beats. The tune's D♮5 on beat 3 of 275 comes just before it. PDF 420 makes outer-voice cross relations tolerable when the bass leading tone is active.
- **Why rejected.** Bsn 2 carries D3→D♯3 inside one voice, so the cross relation comes from doubling. A&S calls that unobjectionable (PDF 402, Ex. 23-12b; Ex. 25-3a). A cello D♯ at 276 would take away the five beats c085 gives every pizz.→arco change.

### 4. Bars 276→277: Vln II's A3, the 7th of V6/5, leaps to B4 (kept)
- **Why rejected.** The leap is a change of role: Vln II goes from off-beat accompaniment to the second idea's lower octave. Cl 1 resolves A4→G4 across its rest, in the same register. That is the transfer A&S approves (PDF 406–407).

### 5. Bars 21→22, 113→114 and 261→262: G♮ in the tune against G♯ in the bass (kept)
- **Issue.** This is an outer-voice cross relation, which Ex. 25-3b marks "avoid".
- **Why rejected.** The tune moves G–F♯–E by step (Ex. 25-3c "better"), and the G♯ is the applied V6's leading tone in the bass (Ex. 25-4). A&S's two conditions on PDF 420 are both met. This was c143's change, and it stays.

### 6. Codetta 225–236: apparent °4/3 between IV and I (Ex. 24-23, PDF 414) (not applied)
- **Idea.** A D♯°7 over A (A–C–D♯–F♯) between IV and I would add a leading-tone sound to the plagal alternations.
- **Why rejected.** The codetta's IV, IV, iv order and its three T heads are protected (c153, c157, c170). The close at 357–360 already uses a common-tone °7 over the E pedal, and a second one here would anticipate it. c142 rejected the related added-6th IV.
