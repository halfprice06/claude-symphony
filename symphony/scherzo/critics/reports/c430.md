## Findings

### 1. The opening G♯ should be the same sound as the ending's G♯: Vln I G♯5 ppp with Hn 1 at 1–2 (applied)

- **Bars.** 1–2.
- **Problem.** The scherzo ends on Hn 1 G♯4 with Vln I G♯5 ppp (368–376). The finale takes that exact pair as A♭ at IV 1 (Hn 1 A♭4, Vln I A♭5 over C5). The scherzo begins on Hn 1 G♯4 alone. That is the same sound as the horn call that starts at bar 3, so the pivot and the quotation of movement I share one colour at the start and have two different colours at the end. The frame is right in pitch, but the listener only hears it as a frame at one end.
- **Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 224–225 (Fig. 5.9): a hyperinstrument couples one specific sound with one specific meaning. PDF 215: the composer crafts "an explicit sound that is attached to a specific narrative meaning". PDF 226 and 234: two instruments fused into one colour (instrumental synthesis). Here the meaning is the G♯ = A♭ pivot, and the score has already built its sound at the end. PDF 222: material shared at its finished state is what makes the work sound "composed by a single person". The finale keeps that sound in the same players at the same pitch, so it should be the scherzo's first sound too. The do-not-touch reason for bars 1–7 is "the pivot from movement II". This change serves that reason more strongly: the pivot gets its own colour, and the call keeps the single horn (c090; Adler II PDF 264). The violins stop with the horn at the end of the fermata, so the call at 3–6 is still one horn line. The reversal also matches the ending. At 368–369 the horn sounds first and the violins join (c150's transfer). At 1–2 they sound together and the violins leave.
- **Fix (applied).** sections/intro.ily, a new variable (parts.ily already assembles `introVlnOne`):
  `introVlnOne = { gis''2.~\ppp | gis''2. | R2.*10 | }`
  The global fermata on bar 2 prints on the note. Hn 1, the call, the timpani label and everything from bar 3 are unchanged.
- **Alternatives tested and rejected.**
  - Vln II G♯4 in unison: it continues movement II's last Vln II A♭4 in the same player. In unison under the horn it is hardly heard as a separate colour, and it does not form the ending's octave.
  - Vln I held into bar 3: it clashes. G♯ is held against the D♯ of V at 5–6, and it covers the call's G♯3 start.
- **Check.** MIDI velocity at bar 1: Vln I 31 under Hn 1's 37, so the horn still leads. Vln I's next entry is theme A, leggiero p at 13, after ten bars' rest, so it is still fresh.

### 4. Crescendo shapes at 89–104 and 325–339: keep

Fig. 5.7–5.8 (PDF 216–217) asks for a slow rise, a steep last third and an abrupt end. Both builds already have that shape:

- **89–104.** p/pp, a slight swell to mp at 99–100, then strings f< over woodwinds p< and brass pp< at 101–104 (c408 order).
- **325–339.** pp, mf, f, ff, fff, then the stroke at 339 and the G.P. at 340, the *Gravity* cut into silence (PDF 242).

A steeper start would weaken the hemiola build that c408 protects. No change.

### 5. How the scherzo's themes serve the finale's rework: no new request

The finale already quotes the scherzo's own material at pitch: motive x, theme A's head and bar 2, the horn call's D♯4 and the G♯. With Finding 1 the G♯ pivot is one sound at three places: III 1, III 369 and IV 1. The finale's IV 243 (Vln I G♯5→G5) is a fourth appearance. I decided not to ask the finale to quote T or the second idea. PDF 245–248 (keep a marked resource for its own world) argues for keeping the trio's E-major world inside the scherzo.
