## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 345-348 (coda, T's second phrase in A major): give it to Ob 1, not Cl 1. Applied.
**Problem.** In the coda, T is passed Hn 1 (341) → Cl 1 (345) → Vln I (349). Cl 1's phrase is D5-E5-F♯5-B5-A5-G♯5-F♯5-D♯5-E5 at p, and its top note B5 is written D6 on the A clarinet. It is the only soft clarinet note above written C6 in the movement, and it is the peak of the phrase. In the whole movement the oboe, the lyrical woodwind, never sings a phrase of T, the tune the listener is meant to keep. Its trio material is a four-bar call (185) and T's head in the codetta (225).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 210 (Ex. VII-56): the clarino is "bright, incisive, expressive" up to C6 written, and above it the clarinet is "piercing, shrill". PDF 185: such a register is not one in which a player can shade a soft line. PDF 198 (Ex. VII-32): F4-C6 is the oboe's "most effective range", "warm, prominent, reedy and poignant", and "the oboe is basically a lyrical instrument". Its examples include Beethoven 6/iii mm. 91-98, an oboe tune in a 3/4 scherzo. PDF 184: assign each phrase to the instrument it sounds best on. In the corpus, Dvořák 9/iii gives its E-major cantabile to Ob 1 (68-75). D5-B5 lies wholly within the oboe's best range, so the peak B5 sounds full and soft instead of strained. The coda's recollection now passes through horn, oboe and violins: three colours, and one more in the T sequence than Cl 1 already provides at 153 and 209.
**Fix (sections/coda.ily).** `codaOboeOne`: after `e''4-> r4 r4 | R2. |` write `R2.*4 | d''2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" e''4 | fis''2 b''4) | a''4.( gis''8 fis''4) | dis''4( e''2) | R2.*28 |` (it was `R2.*36`). `codaClOne`: the four bars become `R2.*4`. Ob 2 rests, so the combiner prints "1." itself; I removed a duplicate "1." markup after looking at page 12. Ob 1 has five bars' rest after the fff (335-339) plus the G.P.

### 2. Bars 269-270 (reprise, the compressed counterstatement): the call goes to Bsn 1 solo, staccato, on E3. Applied.
**Problem.** The reprise's single wind call (`e''4 b'8 b' e''4 | g''2 fis''8 f''8 | e''4`) is Ob 1 at the pitch the oboe already played at 29. This scherzo states theme A and motive x more than twenty times, but outside the tuttis the call is only ever heard in oboe and flute. The bassoon's scherzo character, its dry, humorous staccato, is never used for the movement's main motive. That colour is a large part of why scherzo themes in Beethoven and Dvořák stay in the ear: the same motive comes back in a new voice.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 226: the bassoon "rivals the oboe in the incisive way it can produce attacks and staccato passages" and has staccato passages "that truly sound humorous". Ex. VII-95 (Dukas, three bassoons staccato in a fast 3/8) and Ex. VII-96 (Bartók, "Allegretto scherzando", bassoons p staccato) are scherzo writing of exactly this kind. The see-also list (PDF 228) includes Tchaikovsky 5/iii, a 3/4 waltz movement. Ex. VII-89: E3-G3 is the "sweet, more subdued, but expressive" register. PDF 226: when the bassoon is accompanied, "mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist". The strings' answer at 271 (E5) now comes two octaves above the call, so the call and response also contrast in register.
**Fix (sections/dA.ily).** `dAOboeOne` bars 17-19 of the section become `R2. | R2. | R2.`. `dABsnOne` becomes `\partCombineSoloI e4-.\p^\markup \bold "1." b,8-. b,-. e4-. | g2->( fis8 f8 | e4-.) r4 r4 \partCombineAutomatic` (it was the held `b2.~ | b2. | b4`). `dACorAnglais` takes the held B: `b2.~\pp | b2. | b4 r4 r4`, in its "richly beautiful" low register (PDF 205). Cl 1 `g'2.~\pp` and Cl 2 `e'2.~\pp` (they were p), with equal dynamics on the shared staff. The harmony (E-G-B) is unchanged. The call is not a recorded theme statement.

### Checked, no change (decided)
- **153-168, T in Cl 1.** The tune begins on written B4, the first clarino note, and peaks on written C6, the last. It never crosses the break. This matches Adler PDF 210 exactly. Keep it.
- **225-236, T's head descending Ob 1 (G♯5) → Cl 1 (G♯4) → Bsn 1 (G♯3).** Each instrument plays the head in its singing register (PDF 198, 210, 226). This is the type-casting of PDF 184 at a master's level.
- **209-224, T forte.** I considered adding Ob 1 to the lower octave (PDF 201, "poignant doubler"). I decided against it. After finding 1 the oboe has its own phrase of T in the coda, and adding it here would weaken that entry. Ob 1 would also have to play T's head at 225 without a breath (PDF 198: frequent rests are mandatory).
- **81-88, Cl 1 dolce after the harp melody.** The line ends on concert F♯4/D♯4, written A4/F♯4, on throat and top-chalumeau notes, "rather pale" (PDF 210). The pale colour fits the poco rit. fading into vii°7 of E. Keep it.
- **169-184, Fl 1 heterophony in unison with Vln I.** In the flute's "sweet" middle register at p, the turns (`gis''4 fis''8 gis'' a''4`) put motive x's rhythm onto T's minims. This links trio and scherzo and does not hide the tune, which Vln I and Vln II carry in octaves. Keep it.
