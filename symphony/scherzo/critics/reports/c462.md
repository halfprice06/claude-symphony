## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 26: the fourth phrase of theme A reaches its cadential 6/4 from i6 with an arpeggiated bass. Applied.

**Problem.**
- The theme's closing cadence 25–28 is i – i6 – 6/4 – V7 – i. The bass leaps G–B, and nothing lies between the tonic and the dominant.
- A&S calls this the least intense way to reach V (PDF 135). They name the I6 → II6 connection at cadences as one of I6's most important uses (PDF 141 §7, Ex. 9-6e, 9-2c).
- The melody already gives the chord: on beat 3 of 26 the theme sings F♯5 (2̂) and then the motto's passing F5. Over the held G, both pass unharmonised.
- This is the first, bare statement of the theme. A listener hears its bass line and harmony more clearly here than anywhere else in the movement.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 135: I–II–V "more intense" than I–I6–V.
- PDF 141 §§7–8 and Ex. 9-12: the bass rises by step from I to V, and the harmonic rhythm speeds up toward the cadence.
- PDF 153, Ex. 10-3b: coming from II6, the 4th of the 6/4 enters by stepwise descent as an accented passing tone.
- PDF 139, Ex. 9-8a: 4̂ held as a common tone becomes the prepared 7th of V7.
- PDF 138: 6̂ falls to 5̂.

**Fix (applied, sA.ily, sA bar 14 = bar 26).**
```
sAVc     ... | g,4 r4 a,4 | ...          (was g,4 r4 r4)
sACb     ... | g,,4 r4 a,,4 | ...        (was g,,4 r4 r4)
sAVlnTwo ... | r4 b'4-. a'4-. | ...      (was r4 g'4-. g'4-.)
sAVla    ... | r4 b4-. c'4-. | ...       (was r4 b4-. b4-.)
```

**Result.**
- **Bass.** It rises G–A–B into the 6/4. Beat 3 is ii°6 (A–C–A–F♯) with the bass doubled (Ex. 9-7d).
- **Neapolitan colour.** On the half-beat, the theme's F♮ over A–C sounds as a passing Neapolitan sixth.
- **Inner voices.** Vln II goes B4–A4 and holds the A as the V7's prepared seventh at 27, which resolves to G4 at 28 (c163 kept). The violas go C4–B3 (6̂–5̂), and their B3 at 27 stays the lowest off-beat note (c323 kept).
- **Vln II on beat 2.** It takes B4, not G4, so that Vln II and the bass do not move in octaves G→A.
- **Unchanged.** The texture stays bare (same five string parts, same dynamics, one extra pizzicato note), and the theme's notes are unchanged.
- **Other statements.** The later statements (118, 266, 318) keep the bass G. There the cellos' countermelody (with Hn 3 and Bsn 1) moves G3 on beat 3 to A3 at the next bar, so the 3̂–4̂ step is already heard in the tenor, and an A bass would make a seventh against that G3.

### 2. Bar 355: the violas' held E4 (the 4th of the cadential 6/4) rises to F♯4. Applied.

**Problem.**
- In the coda's last T cadence, the violas hold E4 from 351 into the prolonged 6/4 at 353–354. This is the protected common-tone preparation, which is correct (PDF 153).
- At 355 the E moves up to F♯4, while the tune's E6 and E5 fall to D♯ on beat 2.
- The texture is thin (pp: solo violin and flute, Vln II, divided violas, cello pedal), so the rising inner 4th can be heard.

**Evidence.** PDF 153 and PDF 160 points 2 and 4: the 4th must resolve downward by step and is not doubled. Ex. 10-4c: 6–7 over 4–3.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
```
codaVla ... | <gis e'>2.~ | <gis e'~>2. | <a~ e'>4 <a dis'>2 | <gis e'>2. | ...   (355 was <a fis'>2.)
```

**Result.**
- G♯3 rises to A3 on the downbeat (6–7).
- E4 is suspended over the bar and falls to D♯4 on beat 2 together with the tune.
- D♯4 then rises to E4 and A3 falls to G♯3 at 356.
- Nothing new sounds against the tune's suspended E.

### 3. Bar 222: the violas leave the 6/4's E4 for F♯4. Not applied.

The same point arises in the f return of T. Here, however, the violas play off-beat chords with a rest on the downbeat of 222, so their E4–F♯4 is a change of chord voicing, not a held line. The 4th resolves in four octaves of the tune (Fl, Vln I, Vln II, Cl). PDF 154 allows freedom in free orchestral textures, and the turn at 221–222 is protected (c372). I kept it.

### 4. Bars 127–128 (and 51–52, 283–284): V4/3 → VII°7 of V → cadential 6/4 puts an applied chord after V (syntax rule 2, PDF 148). Not applied.

**What happens.**
- The bass F♯ of the V4/3 goes to A♯ by a leap.
- The tune's D♯6–C♯6–B5 over VII°7 of V is protected (c413), and so is the rising semitone A♯–B (c412, c452).
- The VII°7 of V acts as a chromatic neighbour chord inside the dominant, so the dominant is prolonged rather than reversed.

**Why no change.** A predominant on beat 3 of 127 cannot carry the tune's D♯. The G-major version at 51–52 does the same over V of V. Kept.

### 5. Bars 165–166: the drone E3 in Hn 4 does not resolve under the V7. Not applied.

The 4th of the 6/4 resolves properly in the harp and clarinet (E→D♯). The horn's E3 is the trio's tonic drone (153–168), a pedal outside the 6/4 rule. Breaking it for one beat would cost the musette effect.

### 6. Whole-movement checks: no other violation found.

All twelve cadential 6/4s follow the rules:
- they fall on strong beats or strong bars;
- they are approached from tonic or intermediate harmony, never from V;
- they resolve over a stationary bass.

The weak-to-strong bass repetitions the scan found are of three kinds:
- strong-to-weak bars (75→76);
- pedals;
- the permitted case of II6 to V4/2 over a stationary bass (86→87, Ex. 9-14).
