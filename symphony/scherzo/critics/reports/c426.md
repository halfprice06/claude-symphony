## Findings

### 1. Bar 185: Vln II takes the mute off with no instruction where to do it. Applied.

**Problem.** Since c409, Vln II plays the arpeggios at 169–184 "con sord., dolce", rests 185–192, and enters at 193 marked "senza sord.". The part says the state at the entry but not when to take the mute off. In the part, 185–192 is one eight-bar multirest, and the player first reads "senza sord." after it, over the entry.

**Evidence.** Adler's table of terms (adler2 PDF 312) gives two different instructions: "Take off mutes: Via Sordini" and "Without mute: Senza Sordino". The first is the action, written where it is to happen; the second is the state at the entry. The eight bars (about 6 s at 2. = 76) are enough time, and the mute should come off at the start of them, not just before the f entry at 197 (193 is p<). Movement II uses "via sord." in the same way (II 91, mvt2 c136). The horns at 73 are a different case: their mutes come off in the pause after movement II (c336), so the state word alone is correct there.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-426/work).** sections/tB.ily, `tBVlnTwo`, first bar:
```
tBVlnTwo = { R2. -\tag #'score ^\markup \with-dimensions #'(0 . 0) #'(0 . 0) \italic "via sord." -\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "via sord." | R2. | ...
```
"senza sord." at 193 stays. score.ly: Vln II staff now reads `<< \global \removeWithTag #'part \vlnTwo \chordFadeMidi >>`, as the other staves do. The score's markup has zero size because a full-size markup over the bar pushed page 7 over and the score went to 14 pages. Bar 185's Vln II staff has nothing above it (Vln I rests there), and on page 7 at 160 dpi the text stands clear. In the part, 185 becomes a one-bar rest with "via sord." and 186–192 a seven-bar rest. Print only: score.midi is byte-identical to v426's.

### 2. Bars 213–216: Hn 1's held C♯5 [G♯5], the only soft sustained wind note above the nonprofessional column. Built, tested, not kept.

**The candidate.** Of all the notes in row 2, this is the one a listener could hear change. Hn 1 holds written G♯5 for 3⅓ bars at mp, a semitone above Adler's amateur top (written G5, PDF 305). It sits inside the lower octave of T (Cl 1, Vln II: A4–B4 at 213, C♯5–F♯5 at 214, E5–D♯5–A♯4 at 215), and c406 lowered it to mp so that it would not cover that line. A high horn note is harder to keep soft and blended than a note inside the comfortable column, so the reading gives a reason to lower it.

**What I built.** Hn 1 213–216 `a'2.-- | a'2.-- | ais'2.~ | ais'4\> b'2`, with Hn 2 at 213 `e'2.--` (was a') to avoid the unison. Build: 0 warnings, 13 pages, parts lengths match, no C-major triad, no new outer-voice parallel.

**Why it makes the music worse.** (a) C♯ is the only tone common to all three chords, A (213), F♯/A♯ (214) and F♯7/A♯ (215), so the held C♯ ties the passage together. No other single note does this. (b) The new line doubles the tune instead of sustaining against it: unison A4 with Vln II/Cl 1 at 213, then A♯4–B4 in unison with T's 7–8 suspension at 215–216. (c) At 215 Hn 1's A♯ comes in two beats before the tune's A♯ and over the bass A♯, so the leading tone of V7/V sounds in three parts, and the tied suspension that c421 made a feature (do-not-touch: T's two tied suspensions) loses its preparation. (d) Hn 2 loses its stepwise line (G♯4–E4 leap). The note is inside Adler's professional column, reached by step inside one slur, and already balanced at mp (c406), so the reading's reason is weaker than these losses. I reverted it; tC.ily in the scratch copy is identical to v426's.
