## Findings

### 1. Bar 300: the reprise's ff A-major arrival has an incomplete brass chord, two horns, and is scored as a copy of 72 (applied)

- **Bars:** 300 (dB.ily, `dBHnThree`, `dBHnFour`; parts-cues.json parts 14 and 15).
- **Problem:** 300 is the ff goal of the shortened development: the stretto arrives on A major, and c422's Italian sixth and c473's ♭II6 then lead straight to the home dominant at 301-304. Woodwind, strings, bassoons and contrabassoon play ff, and the strings play double stops. The brass is Hn 1 C#5 and Hn 2 A4 only. Trumpets and low brass are tacet through the reprise for good reasons (c033, c428), but Hn 3·4 are free in 300 and rest. So the brass sounds a bare third, with no fifth and no lower root. The middle register is thin: between Bsn 1's A3 and the A4 the only note is the violas' E4. The bar is also scored exactly as 72. At 72 the A-major chord is only a step into the F-major episode, and Hn 3·4 cannot play there because their first entry, "senza sord.", is at 73 (c384 K2). That reason does not apply at 300. Hn 3·4 have played since 253, and they enter at 301 anyway.
- **Evidence:** In every loud appendix chord the brass on its own sounds the complete chord, with the horns filling the middle (chords 2-5, 9-12, 17-20, PDF 505-507). Chords 2 and 3 put the four horns at ff under fff woodwind (PDF 505), so the horns give weight by their number, one degree under the tutti. Ch. IV PDF 116: a passage is scored for its place in the form, "whether it forms a climax or merely a step". PDF 100: the same idea is scored differently when it returns, by "duplication of parts". In Exs. 205-206 (PDF 368-369) the restatement is scored fuller than the first statement.
- **Fix (applied):** Hn 3 E4 and Hn 4 A3, f, minim on beats 1-2, rest on beat 3:
  `dBHnThree = { R2. ×11 | e'2\f r4 | c'2.--(\p\< ...`
  `dBHnFour = { R2. ×11 | a2\f r4 | \tag #'score \clef bass f2.--(\p\< ...`
  The four horns now sound A3-E4-A4-C#5 (root, fifth, root, third), a complete close chord under the woodwind. The dynamic is f, not ff, and there is no accent, which follows Dan's rule for brass entries (c123: weight by doubling, not by attack). They stop on beat 2 with the winds (c126), so c422's Italian sixth on beat 3 stays in the strings alone, and they have a beat to breathe before their p at 301 (C4/F3, a third and a step away). Both horns on the shared staff have the same dynamics (f at 300, p at 301). Bar 72 is unchanged. The Vc cue before the entry in the Horn 3 and Horn 4 parts is shortened from 4 bars to 3 (297-299, where the cellos play the stretto), so it ends before the new entry at 300.
- **Best-candidate test:** this was the change that this reading supports and that a listener would hear most clearly. The four-bar build at 301-304 now starts from a full horn chord rather than from silence in Hn 3·4, and the ff arrival in the reprise sounds louder than at 72.

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **K1, 336-338, low brass rest at the fff peak.** Every fff appendix chord has trombones and tuba (chords 1, 4, 5, 17-19). But c034's reason still holds and answers it: Barber's rule (Adler PDF 150) keeps the low brass out of the bar of the high note and brings them back on the final low chord (339). The timpani roll, the contrabassoon, the cellos and the basses still give the V chord a full bass. I did not undo it.
- **K2, 72.** Kept for the senza-sord. reason above. It now also serves T1: the exposition's step is lighter than the reprise's goal.
- **K3, timpani at 72/300.** The E drum is the fifth of A and could roll there (A5). At 300, though, a roll would have to stop on beat 2 with the winds, and c473 keeps the timpani silent through 301-302 (no F or A). At 72 the roll would carry into the decay of the string-free F episode. So the change adds little to finding 1. Rejected.
- **K4, beats 2-3 of the theme-A tuttis (105-120, 305-320).** On beats 2 and 3 the bass is Bsn 2's E2 alone, 15-19 semitones under the tenor countermelody. The low instruments strike only the downbeat. Ex. 221 (PDF 390) does the same (contrabassoon and basses mark the downbeats only), and so do Ex. 207 and Dvořák 9/iii. Sustaining the bass, as in Ex. 205, would weigh down the leggiero one-in-a-bar dance. Kept.
- **K5, trio peak 217-224 without timpani.** A B roll at 221-222 into an E at 223 would echo the roll at 205-208 (c248). But the bass is already mf with a diminuendo at 221 (`tCCb`), so this is a falling cadence and not a tutti chord, and the soft trio is the movement's plan. Rejected after I looked at the dynamics.
- **K6, 305/307/312/317, E2 to G3.** These open tenths come from c362's voice-leading fix. Chords 5 and 7 also have an octave plus a fifth at the bottom. Kept (as c384 K1).
