## What else I studied

Dvořák 9/iii in the local corpus (mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/, strings.notesS.ily and woods.notesS.ily, bar checks from header.ily: 49, 99, 123, 154), with its form annotations (musicxml-converted/annotations/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt3/form/). Points used below: in the ff tutti statement of the main theme (the bars before the bar check at 49) the theme never goes below E5, and the two violin sections play it in octaves (Vln I E6–B6, Vln II with the flutes E5–B5; pitches worked out from the relative-mode source); the soft opening keeps a pp crotchet pedal on D4 in Vln I (from bar 9) under the woodwind canon. I also compared v09 bar by bar with v01 (a per-bar diff of every section file).

Overall verdict. A conductor who knows Dvořák 9/iii would accept the plan, the key scheme, the hemiolas, the timpani writing and the soft trio. The main way v09 still shows it was not written by a master is in the loud melodic passages. Each big tune gets one violin section, and the other section doubles a clarinet harmony part. The tutti theme also drops below the brass. Dvořák never does either. All four findings are about doubling and register, and none changes a note of the themes.

## Findings

### 1. Second idea (45–56, 121–132, 281–292): Vln II doubles Cl 1's inner part instead of the tune's lower octave
- Problem: the movement's lyrical forte tune is in Vln I (D6–A6 at 45–52, B5–F♯6 at 121–128) with Fl 1 at pitch. Its lower octave is Ob 1 alone, against Hn 1·2, four woodwind and the hemiola bass, all at f. Vln II plays b'2( d''4~ … at 45 and g'2( b'4~ … at 121 and 281, which are exactly Cl 1's notes. The second section therefore adds nothing.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 58–59 (violins in octaves "secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre"; Ex. 22, 25), Note I PDF 62, and PDF 78 (Vln I+Fl over Vln II+Ob, the Sheherazade iii distribution). In Dvořák's ff statement the two violin sections play the tune in octaves.
- Fix (applied): Vln II takes Ob 1's line with Vln I's two-bar slurs. sA.ily `sAVlnTwo` bars 45–56:
  `d''2->( b'4~ | b'4 g''2) | fis''2( e''4~ | e''4 d''2) | e''2->( cis''4~ | cis''4 a''2) | g''2( fis''4~ | fis''4 e''2) | d''4( b'4 g'4 | a'2 fis'4 | a'4 g'2) | g'4-. r4 r4`
  sC.ily `sCVlnTwo` 121–132 and dA.ily `dAVlnTwo` 281–292:
  `b'2->(\f g'4~ | g'4 e''2) | dis''2( cis''4~ | cis''4 b'2) | c''2->( a'4~ | a'4 fis''2) | e''2( dis''4~ | dis''4 b'2) | b'4( g'4 e'4 | fis'2 dis'4 | fis'4 e'2) | e'4-. r4 r4`
  The harmony is unchanged because Cl 1 still plays it. With the oboe's line now carried by a string section, the places where Cl 1 crosses above Ob 1 (46, 50; Rimsky PDF 65) no longer weaken the tune.

### 2. Coda climax (341–346): the movement's top line, E6–B6, is in Vln I alone
- Problem: at the ff head of theme A and the B6 peak (344), Vln I plays the tune with Fl 1. Vln II sits on g''2./fis''2. (doubled by Ob 2 anyway), and full brass sustain ff chords. The tune's lower octave is a single oboe. Rimsky's case for doubling below is strongest exactly here, in the extreme register and against brass.
- Evidence: PDF 59 ("melodic figures in the very high register … become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves"); Note I PDF 62; Ex. 24 (Sadko 207, PDF 200), whose note puts most of the section in the lower octave.
- Fix (applied), coda.ily `codaVlnTwo` 341–347:
  `e''4-.\ff b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | g''2->( fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.) b'8-. b'-. g''4-. | b''2.-> | a''2-> fis''4~ | fis''4 dis''2 | e''4-> r4 r4`
  B6 stays the only B6 (Vln I 344).

### 3. Tutti theme A (107–108, 311–312): the tune falls to B3 under the horns and trumpets
- Problem: in the ff reprise the tune goes e''4 b'8 b' g'4 | fis'4 dis' b in Vln I+II and Ob 1. On those beats Hn 1 sounds E5/D♯5 and Hn 2 B4, Tpt 1 B4, and Vla and Ob 2/Cl 2 have off-beats above the tune. Only the flutes rise, and only in 108; in 107 they play G4. So for two of the first four bars of the climax, the theme is the lowest moving voice above the bass. In the p statement (15–16) the same dip is effective and stays as it is.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 54 (melody in the middle register is not prominent and needs doubling or a higher position); Ex. 19 (PDF 196). Dvořák's ff statement never goes below E5.
- Fix (applied): Vln I and Fl 1·2 take 107 beats 2–3 and 108 an octave higher. Vln II, Ob 1 and Cl 1 keep the written line, so the violins are in octaves for those two bars. sC.ily and dC.ily, `sCVlnOne`/`dCVlnOne` bars 107–108 (311–312):
  `e''4-.) b''8-. b''-. g''4-. | fis''4-. dis''-. b'-. |`
  `sCFluteOne`, `sCFluteTwo`, `dCFluteOne`, `dCFluteTwo` bar 107 (311): `e''4-.) b''8-. b''-. g''4-.` (bar 108 already reads fis'' dis'' b').

### 4. Trio return (209–224): T forte in Vln I+Fl 1 with only Cl 1 below; Vln II on off-beats
- Problem: the trio's forte espressivo return has the same layout as finding 1. The tune (G♯5–G♯6) is in Vln I and Fl 1, with Cl 1 alone an octave lower. Vln II plays tenuto off-beats with the violas.
- Evidence: Rimsky PDF 78 (strings belong on the lower octave when only one octave is doubled; double both when possible), PDF 58–59.
- Fix (applied), tC.ily: `tCVlnTwo` 209–224 doubles Cl 1 (`gis'2(\f a'4 | b'2 e''4) | dis''4.( cis''8 b'4) | a'4( gis'2) | … | e''2.~ | e''2\> r4\!`, the notes of Cl 1 209–224). The off-beat chords move to the violas divided: `tCVla` 209–224 `r4 <e' b'>4--\mf^\markup \italic "div." <e' b'>4-- | …` (each chord = the old Vln II note over the old Vla note), and "unis." at 225.

### v09 against v01
I compared every section file. Nothing got worse.
