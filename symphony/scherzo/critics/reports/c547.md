## Findings

### 1. Bars 141-144: the link's E-minor pad has three parts and no root inside it; give it the four-part voicing of 145-148 (applied)

**Problem.** The link reverses the intro: Hn 1's G4 (141-144) turns to G♯4 (145), E minor to E major. The pad under it is:
- 141-144: E2 (B. cl.), B3 (Bsn 1), G4 (Hn 1). Three parts, a twelfth between the bass and the next voice, and no E above the bass.
- 145-148: E2 (B. cl.), B3 (Bsn 2), E4 (Bsn 1), G♯4 (Hn 1). Four parts.

So at 145 three things happen at once. Hn 1 goes G→G♯. Bsn 1 leaps B3→E4. Bsn 2 enters on B3. The G♯, which is the whole point of the link and the pivot of the symphony (movement II's A♭), is one change among three. The minor half also sounds thinner than the major half, for no musical reason. The intro chord this passage reverses (bar 7: B2, B3, G4, E5 and the violins' G5) is complete.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 108: four-part harmony for bassoons and horn "especially in soft passages, yields a finely-balanced tone recalling the effect of a quartet of horns, but possessing slightly greater transparence." 141-144 is the one soft horn-bassoon passage in the movement that is not in four parts.
- Rimsky PDF 112: "in every chord the parts in octaves strengthen one another, the harmonic sounds in the lowest register coinciding with and supporting those in the highest." With the E4 the bass clarinet's E2 has its octave inside the chord.
- Rimsky PDF 108, first diagram (crossing): the discord goes to the bassoons. The bassoon pair holds the fourth B3/E4 and the horn sits a third above. This is the layout c544 kept at 145-148, now for all eight bars.
- c234's reason holds, and is strengthened: the wide gap stays between the bass and the next voice, and the upper voices are close.

### 2. Bars 76, 79, 87: the horn pair, not the bassoons, holds the tritone in the F-major chorale (not applied; decision: keep)

Row 6 asks for the concords in the horns. At 76 (E3/B♭3), 79 (G3/D♭4) and 87 (F♯3/C4), Hn 4 and Hn 3 hold a tritone, while Bsn 1 and the bass clarinet hold a concord.

Every swap that moves the tritone into the reeds costs a line:
- 79: Hn 3 would lose the chromatic D4-D♭4-C4, and Bsn 1 would leap A3-D♭4-A3 instead of its neighbour A-B♭-A.
- 87: Hn 4 would lose F-F♯-E, which c180 protects.
- 76: Hn 3 would leave its common tone B♭3. That B♭ rises to C4 in tenths with the bass G-A, the correct V4/3-I6 motion.

PDF 112 settles it: "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". The episode is also protected (c104, c147, c347). Keep.

### 3. Bars 229-240: the codetta's E pedal is in Hn 4 alone, with no string double (considered, rejected)

### 6. Bars 101-104 and 301-304: Hn 4 F♯3 lies between Bsn 2 B2 and Bsn 1 B3/A3 (checked, follows)

PDF 108 does not recommend horns inside bassoons. Here, though, Bsn 2 doubles the bass with the bass trombone, the tuba and the contrabassoon, so it is not a chord voice. Bsn 1's B3-A3 carries the dominant's seventh, the discord, as row 6 asks. Four horns hold the forte chord (row 5), and Fl 2/Ob 1/Ob 2 double the upper parts (row 11). Follows.

### 7. Bars 45-54, 121-124: Cl 2 moves inside Hn 1·2's held chord (checked, follows)

PDF 108 says clarinets should rarely sit inside the horns "on account of their tone quality". The same page also says the moving parts go to the woodwind while the brass hold. Cl 2 is a moving inner line, not a held chord tone. Follows.
