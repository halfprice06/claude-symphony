## Findings

### 1. 333-335: the coda's summing-up unison has no low octave; the cellos leave the theme for a held E (applied)

**Problem.** At 333 theme A's head returns ff after the motive-x build, as the movement's last full statement. The theme sounds in three octaves: E6 (Vln I, Fl 1), E5 (Vln II, Ob 2, Cl 1) and E4 (violas, cor anglais). Below E4 there is only the pedal: cellos E2 arco, basses, contrabassoon, tuba, bass trombone and Bsn 2 on E, Tbn 2 on B2, Bsn 1 and Tbn 1 on G3. The cellos played motive x on E3 from 325 to start the build, then drop to a held E2 on the arrival. At 105 and 305 the cellos have the tenor countermelody, so the theme has never had a low string octave in any tutti.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 150: a unison spread over several octaves is used "to summarize an old" idea, and 333 is that summary. PDF 151, Mozart 40/iv: the octave "below that is more heavily scored for the cellos and both bassoons, supported yet an octave lower by the double basses". The weight of each octave tells the listener which one carries the theme. Ex. XV-4 (PDF 152, read from the image): in Beethoven 9/i the cellos and basses play the theme in the full ff tutti, while horns, trumpets and timpani hold tonic and dominant around it. PDF 150, Barber: the doubling is enlarged each time the germ motive returns. Here the voice that began the build stops at the arrival. c087 cites Rimsky-Korsakov's Kashtchei example (PDF 261) for the cor anglais at 333. That same example puts the cellos in the lowest melodic octave.

**Answer to c267.** c267 kept the cellos on the bass because a fourth octave "would put them in the trombones' register" (Sebesky PDF 168: strings are the weakest element of a mixed tutti). Three things answer this. First, the trombones hold one sustained chord at f, a degree under the strings' ff (c086). The cellos play a detached line against it, which is d'Indy's contrast of an articulated string line against sustained winds and brass (PDF 148). Second, Mozart's and Beethoven's layouts above put the cellos on the theme for exactly this purpose. Third, the bass E keeps five instruments (basses, contrabassoon, tuba, bass trombone, Bsn 2), so the cellos are not needed for the pedal at 333-335.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-484/work/sections/coda.ily, codaVc).** The cellos double the violas an octave lower for three bars and return to the bass B2 on the fff downbeat of 336:
```
old: ... b,4-. fis,8-. fis,-. b,4-. | e,2.\ff\dnb | e,2.\dnb | b,2.\<\upbow | b,2.\fff\dnb ...
new: ... b,4-. fis,8-. fis,-. b,4-. | e4-.\ff\dnb b,8-. b,-. e4-. | g2->\dnb( fis8 f8 | e4-.)\< fis8-. g-. a4-. | b,2.\fff\dnb ...
```
The notes are E3-B2-E3 | G3-F♯3-F3 | E3-F♯3-G3-A3 | B2. They have the same articulation and slur as Vln I and the violas. The fall A3 to B2 at 336 puts the cellos back on the hemiola bass (337-338) and the non-div. chord (339), both unchanged. The cellos are never the lowest voice, so the outer-voice analysis does not change. The pitches are the theme's own pitches doubled, so this is not a theme change: themecheck reports 52/52 unchanged. No cue in parts-cues.json points at the cellos in 332-336, and the cello part still plays in the same bars, so no cue changes are needed.
