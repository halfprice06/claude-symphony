## Holistic verdict against Rachmaninoff

The two movements belong to different genres. Rachmaninoff writes a rondo-scherzo whose trio is louder and harder than its scherzo. This movement follows the Dvořák 9 / Beethoven 9 model the brief chose: a scherzo with a fugato, then a lyrical trio.

The parallels that matter are already present:
- A clarinet leads into the lyrical section (153).
- The motto is quoted at the close (357–360).
- The ending dissolves the way Rachmaninoff's does, with timpani motive x at 363, 367 and 371 spaced further apart.
- The scherzo's lyric second idea returns twice, in new colours each time: 45 forte in G, 121 subito piano, 277 in E major.

Importing Rachmaninoff's loud trio or his brass chorale would break the protected plan and the cyclic trumpet rule. I recommend neither.

Where Rachmaninoff is plainly better is the bass under his lyric themes. His basses move as independent lines in tenths and sixths. They do not shadow the melody. Our second idea fails this test, and it is the largest improvement the reading supports.

## Findings, ranked

### 1. Bass shadows the second idea in octaves and doubles the applied leading tone (bars 45–52, 121–122, 277–278). APPLIED.

**Problem.** In the hemiola minims of the second idea, Vc, Cb, Bsn 1 and Cbsn arpeggiate the same chord as the melody, in contrary motion. They land on the melody's pitch class on 6 of 12 minims:

| Bar.beat | Shared pitch class |
|---|---|
| 45.3 | B |
| 46.2 | G |
| 47.3 | E |
| 49.3 | C♯ |
| 50.2 | A |
| 51.3 | F♯ |

The result is hollow octaves at exactly the point where the idea should open out into its first forte cantabile. The worst case is 49.3–50.2. A becomes A7 at 51 and resolves to D at 51.3, where Cl 1 moves C♯5→D5. So the C♯ there is a temporary leading tone, and it is doubled in the bass, which then leaps away from it. Vla C♯4 doubles it again at 50.2.

This answers the c363 note that called the C♯ "the third of a prolonged A chord". The chord does not stay A: it gains its 7th and goes to D. In v389, the old MIDI shows octave pairs between the basses and Vln I, Vln II, Fl and Ob at 46.1 and 50.1, and between Vc/Bsn 2 and Vln at 122.1 and 278.1.

**Evidence.**
- A&S Unit 25, PDF 419: in an applied V the temporary leading tone is not doubled, and it should resolve within its own voice.
- A&S Unit 20, Ex. 20-2b/c, PDF 329: chordal skips in figuration are the means of breaking up parallel octaves. Here the skips create them.
- A&S Unit 21, PDF 348: independence of parts is what gives a texture its life.
- Rachmaninoff's lyric themes stand over basses in tenths, not over mirrored arpeggios.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-389/work).** Rhythm, slurs, hemiola minims and the root of every chord stay the same; only the pitch of the second minim and the third note change.

sA.ily, sABsnOne and sAVc (sACbsn and sACb are the same an octave lower; Vc and Cb keep "arco"):
```
old: g,2(\f b,4~ | b,4 g,2) | d2( e4~ | e4 b,2) | a,2( cis4~ | cis4 a,2) | e2( fis4~ | fis4 cis2)
new: g,2(\f g4~  | g4 b,2)  | d2( e4~ | e4 b,2) | a,2( a4~   | a4 cis2)   | e2( d4~   | d4 cis2)
```

sAVla:
```
old: cis'2( a'4~ | a'4 cis'2) | a'2( d'4~ | d'4) g'2
new: cis'2( a'4~ | a'4 e'2)   | a'2( fis'4~ | fis'4) g'2
```

sC.ily, sCVc and sCBsnTwo (121–122):
```
old: e,2(\subP g,4~ | g,4 e,2)
new: e,2(\subP e4~ | e4 g,2)
```

dA.ily, dAVc and dABsnTwo (277–278):
```
old: e,2(\subP gis,4~ | gis,4 e,2)
new: e,2(\subP e4~ | e4 gis,2)
```

**What the change does.**
- The bass now rises to the octave of the root and then falls to the third, so it moves against the melody instead of mirroring it.
- At 51–52 the bass falls G–F♯–E under the melody's E–D–C♯ in parallel tenths (Cl 1 D5 over bass D3 at 51.3 is approached by contrary motion).
- C♯ is now sounded only once, by the melody, and it resolves to D. The bass takes C♯ only at 50.2, once the melody has left it, and from there it leads into the cadence. The viola moves to E and F♯, so it neither doubles the C♯ nor sits on the D before it resolves.
- One octave E/E remains at 47.3, the arrival on vi. I left it because c262 placed the horns there.

**Check.** After the change, the pairwise scan finds no octave or fifth between any bass line and Vln I/II, Fl or Ob in 44–53, 120–125 or 276–281. The flags that remain come from merged chord tracks (Cl 1·2 swapping notes; horns holding A, which is oblique motion) or are older than this change (281.2 is the same in v389). Bars 123–124 and 279–280 were already in tenths, and Cb and Cbsn rest at 121–124 and 277–280, so nothing else needed to change.

### 2. Accented dissonance in the second idea (bars 45–52). NOT APPLIED.

Schumann's Ex. 20-1 (Unit 20, PDF 327–328) puts accented dissonance at the head of a lyric phrase, and the melody begins ahead of the bass. Our second idea is all consonant minims, so its warmth comes only from orchestration. One appoggiatura on the downbeat of 47 would do it: Ob 1/Vln I G5 over the D chord, resolving to F♯. That would change the melody, though, and earlier critics' melody rewrites of this idea were rejected. Finding 1 already gives the passage motion from below, so I leave this as an observation.

### 3. Cl 1 tie at 345–346 (c382). KEEP.

The sus.py scan flags A4 held into 346 against a G♯ chord. It is the 7th of a V7 whose resolution is decorated, a case Unit 24 (PDF 399–400, Mozart K. 491) shows. No change.
