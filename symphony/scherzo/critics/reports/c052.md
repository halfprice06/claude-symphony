## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Reprise 272–277: five bars of B7 where the reading's subtonic-seventh move fits exactly (applied)
- **Bars:** dA 273–276 (the exposition's 41–44 sung a minor third lower).
- **Problem:** In v52, 272 is V7 of E (= 32), and 273–276 repeat the f approach to the second idea a minor third lower, also on B7. That gives five bars of the same dominant seventh, extended only by arpeggiating it. The exposition already stretched V7 for four bars at 101–104, and the movement does so again at 301–304 and 336–338.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 410–411, sec. 12 and Ex. 24-18 (Handel, Suite No. 3). In minor, a seventh chord on ♮7̂ is held for several bars. Because it is also V7 of III, the listener expects III. It then moves on to V6/5, and the reading calls the effect "surprising and dramatic". The book offers this as "a valuable alternative to extending dominant harmony through changes within V7 itself". The score is set up for it. At the exposition's own pitch, 41–43 is D7, which is ♮VII7 in E minor, and there it did go to G (III) at 45. In the reprise the second idea stays in E minor (c003). Playing 273–275 at the exposition's pitch makes the listener expect G again. Turning at 276 to V6/5 of E delivers the change of course that c003 introduced. The interpolated D7 sits between B7's 7th and its resolution, as in PDF 409 sec. 11: Bsn 1 holds A3 at 273–274, and Cl 1 holds A4 from 275 through 276 and falls to G4 at 277. The D7's own 7th resolves (Bsn 1 C4→B3). D→D♯ stays in one voice (Vc and Bsn 2 D3→D♯3), as PDF 420 (Ex. 25-3a) asks, and the cross relation is covered by the leading tone in the bass of the V6/5 (PDF 420, Ex. 25-4).
- **Fix (applied, dA.ily):** In every part, the `\transpose d b, { … }` that wrapped 273–276 now wraps only 276. Changes at 276:
  - Cl 1 `c''2.~\f | c''2. | a'2.~ | a'4 r4 r4`
  - Bsn 1 `… c'2. | b4 r4 r4`
  - Bsn 2 `… d2. | dis4 r4 r4`
  - Hn 1 `… fis'2. | fis'4 r4 r4`
  - Hn 2 `… d'2. | b4 r4 r4`
  - Vc `d4\f r4 r4 | d4 r4 r4 | d4 r4 r4 | dis4 r4 r4` (pizz.)
  - Cb `d,4\f r4 r4 | d,4 r4 r4 | d,4 r4 r4 | R2.`

  Result: 272 V7 – 273–275 ♮VII7 (D7, f) – 276 V6/5 – 277 i. No C–E–G sonority; the C6 at 274 sits over D–F♯–A.

### 2. 104→105 and 304→305: the soprano's 7th leaps away at the tutti arrival (applied)
- **Bars:** 104→105 (sB→sC) and 304→305 (dB→dC).
- **Problem:** The hemiola build ends with Fl 1 and Vln I on A5, the 7th of B7. Both leap down to E5 for theme A. At 105.1 the tone of resolution, G, sounds only as G3 (Bsn 1, Hn 3·4, Tbn, Vc) and as G4 on beat 2 (Ob 2, Cl 1). The top voice therefore has no G5.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 406–407, sec. 9 and Ex. 24-11. When the soprano has to leave the 7th to start a theme, the tone of resolution must appear "in the same register", in the next lower voice or in the bass. PDF 408 sec. 10 adds that a 7th in the soprano needs a real resolution. PDF 416 point 6a states the same rule.
- **Fix (applied, sC.ily and dC.ily):** `sCFluteOne = { g''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | …` and the same at the start of `dCFluteOne`. Fl 1 now resolves A5→G5 on the downbeat, a chord tone above the theme's E5, and joins motive x on beat 2. The part combiner prints the split and then "a2". Fl 1 at 105 is not in the theme record (c036).

### 3. 44→45: the 7th of V6/5 of G is passed from voice to voice and never resolves (applied)
- **Bars:** 41–45 (sA).
- **Problem:** The 7th C is handed from Cl 1 (C5, 41–42) to Bsn 1 (C4, 43) and then to Vln II (C4, 44.2–3). A&S PDF 408 accepts this as a transferred 7th. But Vln II then leaps to D5 for the second idea, and at 45 no instrument has B3. The violas carry the bass of the V6/5 (F♯3→G4), and the Cl 2 line enters on G4.
- **Evidence:** A&S PDF 399: the progression "must accommodate the descending resolution of the 7th". PDF 406–407: a transfer works when the resolution is in the next voice and in the same register. PDF 419: applied chords follow the voice leading of normal V7.
- **Fix (applied, sA.ily):** `sAClTwo`, bar 45: `b2( g'4~ | g'4 g'2)`, replacing `g'2( g'4~ …`. Cl 2 goes D4→B3, so Vln II's C4 resolves to B3 one voice lower in the same register. Cl 2's hemiola rhythm is unchanged; the G is still in the violas, the bass and Cl 2's second minim.
