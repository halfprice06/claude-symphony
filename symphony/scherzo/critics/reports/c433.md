## Findings

### 1. Bar 170: the second trio statement's bass arpeggiates the tonic (APPLIED)

Bars 169–172. The cellos and Hn 3 play T's head G♯3–A3 | B3–E4 | D♯4–F♯3–A3 | A3–G♯3 over arco basses pp. The bass is E2–E2–B1–E2, so it uses only 1̂ and 5̂. That is the bass A&S Ex. 7-1(a) calls "rather primitive. Confined to two tones, it can provide only the minimal harmonies implied by the melody" (PDF 106). It is also the same root bass as the first statement (153–156), where it belongs: there the harp plucks it under the Hn 3·4 E–B drone, a musette. At 169 the drone is gone and the basses bow a held line (c429), so the line itself is heard.

The reading's remedy is in Unit 7. "Perhaps the most frequent and important way of expanding any major or minor triad is by moving the bass between the root and 3rd ... it makes it possible to continue the same harmony without monotony" (PDF 107, Ex. 7-2). Ex. 7-8 (PDF 110) gives the exact line. The bass arpeggiates I–I6–V, it may go "down a 6th from 1̂ to I6" (Ex. 7-8b), and continuing to 1̂ "produces a larger arpeggiation ... that sounds more self-contained" (Ex. 7-8c). Bar 2 of T (B3–E4) is pure tonic, so I6 needs no other change. The cellos' B3 lies a compound 10th over G♯1, the outer-voice interval PDF 108 ranks first (Ex. 7-4a). The cellos rise a third while the bass falls a sixth, so the motion is contrary. The I6 falls on the weak bar 170 and V on the strong bar 171. That is Ex. 7-18b's "good" order, not 7-18a's weak-to-strong change of position (PDF 116). The G♯ doubled with Vln II's arpeggio is allowed: "any tone of I6 may be doubled" (PDF 108).

Form: T now gains bass motion over its statements. The first has a root drone (153). The second has I–I6–V–I (169). The third has I–vi–I6–V–I (209, c257). The coda's E statement starts on vi (349). This is how Dan's "allowed to develop" can apply to T without changing a note of the tune. c429's do-not-touch (the two statements differ in bass) is kept and sharpened.

Fix (tA.ily, `tACb`, bar 18 of the section = 170):
`e,2.\pp\upbow^\markup \italic "arco" | e,2. | b,,2. | e,2. |` → `e,2.\pp\upbow^\markup \italic "arco" | gis,,2. | b,,2. | e,2. |`
Each bar keeps its own bow, so 170 stays a down-bow. G♯1 is well inside the bass's range. No other part changes. No C-major triad.

### 2. Theme A's opening bass E–E–E–B (13–16, 105–108, 253–256, 305–308): kept

The same Ex. 7-8a could make 15/107 an I6 (E–E–G–B). I rejected it on the reading's own terms. PDF 102: "weak-strong repetition of an initial tonic emphasizes the tonality and is therefore justifiable". Each of these four bars is a tonic arrival: the movement's start, the reprise after sixteen bars of V, the return after the trio, and the final tutti. The theme's last phrase already has the I6 (E–G–B–E at 25–28), and 313–316 already varies the bass. Changing the opening would weaken the arrivals, so this candidate makes the music worse.

### 3. Kuhnau's inverted melody over the expanded V (89–100, 245–252): kept

Read again for Dan's development request. c343's reason still holds: an inversion of the head's bar 2 develops the motto's tail, which the cyclic rules forbid. Inverting motive x alone (E–A–A–E) is too short to be heard as development. The three head entries rising Vla–Ob–Vln I already do the Kuhnau job of a V "expanded so that it becomes a significant portion of the piece" (PDF 95).

### 4. Long tonic spans 133–152, 225–240 and 357–376: kept

They remain the slowest harmonic rhythm in the movement (about 14, 15 and 21 s). Each sits on a root-position pedal, as PDF 112 requires for a chord that carries a section. Each carries motivic groups that change on strong bars (Ex. 6-21, 7-18c). I checked for a 5–6–5 in the E-minor half of the link (141–144) to match c413's C♯ at 146. Over E minor it would sound E–G–C, a C-major triad (c413's rule), so nothing is available there.

### 5. Proportions and length: kept

Trio 0.80 of the first scherzo, reprise 0.56, coda 0.51, total 4:38, the same as c343 measured. Dvořák 9/iii has a relatively shorter trio and coda (c253). Ours is longer because of the protected codetta and soft ending. No bar is added or cut: the finale and movement I quote bars after 240, so cuts would renumber them, and an exposition repeat was rejected for length and balance (c183), which I cannot answer.
