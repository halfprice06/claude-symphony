## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 281–287: the second idea's last statement has the same colour as 121 and never sounds in the tenor (applied)

**Problem.** The second idea is the scherzo's only long legato tune, and 277–288 is the last time it is heard. Theme A returns in the coda, but the second idea does not. Its two E-minor statements are scored almost alike: violins in octaves at B5/B4, Fl 1 and Ob 1 joining (125/121 and 281/285), horns holding pads, cellos on the bass. The tune is always in the treble. Nothing in the movement plants it in a second register, so its last statement brings no new sound. A listener carries a tune away when it returns in a new voice. Dvořák's bassoon does this in 9/iii, as do the horn and cello tenors of Brahms and Tchaikovsky.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 27: "Usually the horn and cello double on long sustained melodies." In Ex. XI-10 (viewed) the horns double the solo cello's line and add articulation to it. PDF 20: Hindemith "sometimes doubles the brasses with other members of the orchestra to give new colors" in later statements, and saves an instrument "to give fresh color" to a later one. The horn–cello colour is unused in the scherzo's melodies. It appears only in the tutti countermelody (105–120), so it is still fresh at 281. The change keeps c044's plan, in which the doublings join as the statement grows: violins alone at 277, then Ob 1 and the tenor at 281 with the crescendo, then Fl 1 at 285. It also matches Dvořák 9/iii, where a later statement of the second tune is given to a tenor voice.

**Fix (applied).** In dA.ily, Hn 2 (the low horn, Adler PDF 303–304 via c046) and the cellos take Vln II's line an octave lower from 281 to 287, sounding C4–A3–F♯4–E4–D♯4 …, cadencing on E3. The basses keep Cb, Bsn 2 and Cbsn. Hn 1's C5/B4 pad, Cl 1, the violas and Bsn 1 keep the harmony. The Hn 1·2 shared staff has equal dynamics (both \< from the sub. p, both f at 285).

### 2. Bars 336–338: the movement's single fff peak line is in two octaves while five instruments hold D♯ (applied)

**Problem.** At the B6 peak the falling line B–A–F♯–D♯ (to E at 339) is the melodic climax of the movement. It sounds only at B6 (Fl 1, Vln I) and B5 (Ob 1, Vln II). The violas hold D♯4 for three bars fff. That makes the leading tone the most doubled pitch in the chord: Fl 2 D♯6, Cl 1 D♯5, Hn 3, Tpt 2 and the violas D♯4. The line's lower octave is empty.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 24: in Elgar's "W.M.B." coda, "notice the octave and double-octave doublings so very effective in obtaining a massive sound". In Ex. XI-8 (viewed), the climax line runs in flutes, oboes and violins across three octaves. PDF 20: in Bruckner 7/iv, "we finally build the climax with octaves". PDF 23: know which melody notes are to be stressed and how they are made to dominate. Here the stressed notes are B6–A6–F♯6–D♯6. Aldwell & Schachter (Unit 5, the leading tone not doubled) supports moving the violas off D♯; c002 fixed leading-tone doubling at 64, 68 and 308. The violas' B4–A4 lies on the A string, their strongest notes for fff.

**Fix (applied).** In coda.ily, codaVla 336–338:
```

b'2.->\fff | a'2-> fis'4->~ | fis'4 dis'2-> |
```
The hemiola rhythm (c049) and bar 339 (`<b g' e''>4->`) are unchanged. The B6 is still the only one (336). The low brass rest of c034 and the trumpets are untouched.

## Decided against

- **209–224, Hn 4 doubling the cellos' countermelody in T's forte return (row 9).** I considered it and decided against it. The line lies G♯2–A3, the horn's weak low octave, under a forte tutti. A second horn–cello doubling would also spend the colour that Finding 1 saves for the second idea's last statement (row 2).
- **Theme A, T and the harp melody 73–80.** Theme A's motive x and tail, T's peak and cadence (c011, c061), and the harp's major-mode head (do-not-touch since cycle 1) all meet the reading's points on stressed notes (row 4). This brass chapter gives no reason to change their notes. T's rhythmic sameness in phrases 1–2 remains a known weakness; c051 and c061 weighed it, and nothing in this reading answers their reason for keeping it.
- **Fugato 57–72 in strings alone (row 13).** Berlioz's colour contrast in canon would mean adding winds or brass entries. That would undo c008, whose reason (the fugato builds from pp in one colour toward the ff stretto) still holds.
- **Coda T 341–356 (row 17).** The hand-over Hn 1 → Ob 1 → violins with Cl 1 is the *Lohengrin* pattern exactly. Keep it.
