## Findings

I found no fault to fix under this reading. Nothing is changed in /tmp/critic-252/work, and its build is clean.

Every chromatic chord in the movement is prepared and resolved as Units 30–31 teach:

- **79–89.** The °7 of F at 79 is spelled with D♭ and prepares the F episode (PDF 536–537, 558–560). The approach to the dominant at 86–89 is the ♮4–♯4 pattern of Ex. 31-14 (PDF 561), and the °7 on ♯4 goes to the cadential 6/4 (§14, PDF 545).
- **Trio.** The trio uses III♯ at 188 and plain iv at 202, 235 and 359, the mixture pair of Ex. 30-1 (PDF 530–531, 534). It has no CT°7 on a minor triad and no augmented sixth over E (§§15, 17).
- **345–347.** The coda's approach to the final dominant is the ♭VII – v – V7 – I of Ex. 30-3 and 30-4 (PDF 532–533). The D–D♯ step is taken in one line (Bsn 1), the A is held into V7, and Ob 1 resolves the seventh to G♯5.
- **273–276.** The m7–M10 exchange ♮VII7 → V6/5 matches Ex. 31-22 (PDF 566).
- **244–246.** The Fr6 → augmented triad → V is the "6 displacing the 5th of V in minor" case (PDF 538–539).

I examined four more candidates and rejected each one:

1. **Bars 199–200, B♮ against B♯.** At 199 the tune descends B4–A4 (Cl 1, Vln II, Vla), and the melody passes B5–C♯6 over ii∅6/5. At 200, Cl 2 takes B♯4 in V of C♯ minor. The B♮ is the descending form of the melodic minor on a weak beat, and the B♯ belongs to the next chord in another register. This is ordinary minor-mode practice, not a cross relation in the sense of Units 30–31.
2. **Bar 296 → 297, the violas' G♯3.** The leading tone ends the fugato subject and is followed by C5, the countersubject's head. The tonic A arrives in the same register in Bsn 1 (A3) and in the violas' own A4 on beat 2. c073 already gave this bar a V of A, and the resolution is complete.
3. **Bar 358, the CT°7.** The chord does not fully follow PDF 543–544: G and C♯ do not move as neighbours to the tonic tones, and the chord acts as a passing chord to iv6/4. But the minor-3rd spelling is the case the book allows when ♭3/♮3 mixture is in play (PDF 544). The passage is protected (c070, plagal close 357–360), and nothing in this reading answers the reason it was protected.
4. **Bar 331, the ♭II6/4.** In Vln II and Vc the F leaps a tritone to B inside the motive-x figuration. Cl 1, Hn 3 and Vln I take the F to D♯, so the chord's resolution is heard. The bar is protected (c081), and c242 already rejected thinning it.

Several Unit 30–31 devices are not used: CT°7 on V (§16), chromatic 6/3 or 7–6 chains, minor-third cycles and whole-tone or octatonic passages. Their absence is not a fault. The movement's chromatic language is mixture plus applied °7s, which fits a neo-Romantic scherzo whose harmonic plan is already set. Adding one of these devices would mean recomposing a section that works, and several of them are blocked by the cyclic rules. Over E, a CT augmented sixth contains C–E–G; ♭VI in E major is C major; and the °7 over B in a common-tone role is the banned B–D–F–G♯ chord.
