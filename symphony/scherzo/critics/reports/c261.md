## Findings

The melodies follow every principle in the table that applies. Earlier passes on this unit already fixed the points it raises: c081 (the climax rhythm of T) and c148 (the hemiola accents). I tested the places below against the reading. None needs a change, and I have not padded the list. They are ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. Theme A, 13–28 (and 105, 253, 305): every long note, the climax included, falls on an even, weak bar of the hypermeasure (kept)

**What I checked.** The theme moves in four-bar hypermeasures (PDF 53). Every accented long note falls on an even bar: the chromatic minims at 14, 18 and 26 on bar 2 of their group, and the climax C6 at 24 on bar 4 of the group 21–24. PDF 50 lists three things that make a note accented: length, high register and dissonance. The C6 is all three, a long, high, accented ♭9 over V, so the theme's strongest built-in accent sits on its weakest bar. That is a syncopation at the level of the bar group (PDF 51).

**Why it stays.** PDF 51 says a counter-accent that keeps a consistent pattern sets up a pattern of its own and does not obscure the metre. Here the pattern never varies. Each odd bar is motive x (short notes, the strong bar) and each even bar is the long chromatic note. Motive x and the timpani make the downbeat bars plain, so the listener hears a deliberate snap on the second bar, as in Dvořák 9/iii's `e8 e e2~ | e2.`. The climax at 24 resolves into the head's return at 25. The tonal goal and the strong bar therefore coincide at 25, and the climax pushes into it, as Ex. 3-13's line pushes to its eighth bar. Theme A is also protected (c001, c121, c161), and its bar 2 is a pitch-fixed link to movements I and IV. No change.

### 2. The second idea, 45–56: eight bars of continuous 3/2 before a hemiola cadence (kept)

**What I checked.** 45–52 is a hemiola throughout. Tune, violas and the bass (g,2 b,4~ | b,4 g,2) all move in minims across the bar line. PDF 51 (Ex. 3-7) calls this a temporary change of metre. I checked whether that leaves the cadential hemiola at 53–55 without contrast. Ex. 3-8 works because the 3/2 interrupts a regular 3/4.

**Why it stays.** The second bar of each pair breaks the 3/2: e''' d'''4. e'''8 at 48 and fis''' e'''4. d'''8 at 52, each with a restruck anticipation (Ex. 3-20). The horns hold dotted minims on every downbeat (Hn 2 g2. | g2. | fis2. …), as the strings mark Brahms's 3/4 under the soloist's 5/4 in Ex. 3-7. At 53 the tune returns to plain crotchets (d''' b'' g''), and the bass holds D and then G in dotted minims. That re-established 3/4 is what lets Cl 1's three minims C5–A4–B4 at 54–55 sound as a cadential hemiola. The cadence is protected (c002, c085). No change.

### 3. Phrase lengths: every theme is in four-bar multiples (kept)

**What I checked.** PDF 54 (Ex. 3-14) shows asymmetric phrases of five and seven bars. Theme A, T, the second idea, the harp melody and the counterstatement are all 4 × n. I considered a one-bar extension of T's last statement at 349–356 (for example, holding the cadential 6/4 one bar longer at 354) to make the movement's last tune statement asymmetric.

**Why it stays.** The movement already varies its group lengths in the place where a scherzo should. The retransition 89–100 is in three-bar groups (Ex. 3-12) and returns to four-bar groups at 101, as Beethoven 9/ii does with "Ritmo di tre battute" followed by "Ritmo di quattro battute". The coda statement already stretches its cadence: since v44 the cadential 6/4 is held for two bars (353–354), so the tonic arrives on the sixteenth bar, 356, not the fifteenth. An extra bar would move the motto tail (357–360) and the closing timpani figure (363–371). Both are bar-fixed links that the finale quotes. It would also risk a fourteenth page. No change.

### 4. No theme begins with an upbeat (kept)

**What I checked.** PDF 52 (Ex. 3-9b) notes that rhythmic groups often begin on an unaccented beat. All five tunes start on a downbeat (13, 45, 73, 153, 185). Dvořák 9/iii's scherzo theme begins on beat 2.

**Why it stays.** At one beat per bar, beat 3 works as an internal upbeat. T uses it in every bar of its first phrase (a'4 → b'2, e''4 → dis''4.), and its third phrase overlaps into the cadence (164→165, Ex. 3-11). Theme A's downbeat start is its Beethoven 9/ii character and is protected (c171 reached the same decision for theme A). No change.

### 5. T, 153–168: the full set of the reading's dissonance types (kept)

T has an accented passing tone (A over E at 156, c001), an appoggiatura (A♯ at 160), an anticipation held over (the A at 162, c081) and a held suspension (E over B at 165–166, c061). Each is placed where PDF 54–56 places it. Each falls in the corresponding bar of a different phrase (bars 4, 8, 10 and 13–14 of the tune), so every phrase ending has its own colour. This is the main reason T is memorable, and nothing in the reading suggests changing it. Protected (c081, c121).

### 6. Ob 1 at 129 and 285: B4 up a sixth to G5 inside the cadence (kept)

Vln I plays B5–G5–E5. Ob 1 plays B4–G5–E5, rising a sixth on beat 2 to join the top line at 129–131 (c131). The leap falls on a weak beat into a chord tone, and the oboe then doubles the hemiola cadence in the tune's own register. Weak-beat placement is what PDF 54 asks of anything that is not a structural note. No change.
