## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 333–334 and 339: Tbn 2 triples the bass, so the trombones' sustained chord has no fifth (applied)
**Problem.** At the coda's tutti (333–335, held f under slurred tenutos) and at the last held minim (339), the low brass read tuba E1, bass trombone E2, Tbn 2 E3, Tbn 1 G3. Three brass instruments are on the root in three octaves, and above them the trombones have only G. The fifth B is not in the trombones. In the brass it appears only from Hn 4 B3 upwards, so the brass has an empty octave E2–E3 at the bottom of a sustained ff/fff chord. The bass already has three octaves without Tbn 2: tuba and contrabassoon on E1, bass trombone, cellos and Bsn 2 on E2, Bsn 2 or Bsn 1 on E3.
**Evidence.** PDF 101: when the bass is doubled in octaves, "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts are generally allotted to the two remaining trombones". The diagrams there show tuba plus trombones in close position above it. PDF 100: brass part-writing "of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". PDF 114: balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords". Supporting point: the scherzo may not use "a full low-brass unison bass" (cyclic rule). Three low brass instruments on E in octaves for three held bars comes closer to that than it needs to.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, `codaTbnTwo`: `e2.--(\f | e2.-- | e2.--) | R2.*3 | e2--\f\> r4\!` → `b,2.--(\f | b,2.-- | e2.--) | R2.*3 | b,2--\f\> r4\!`. The trombones now hold E2–B2–G3 over the tuba (open fifth at the bottom, then a sixth: the harmonic-series spacing of PDF 85). At 335, when the bass moves to B, Tbn 2 rises to E3 over the bass trombone's B2, so the 6/4 stays complete. **Answer to c114** ("do not touch the chord spacing at 333–339", reason: the octave above the bass must be filled): E3 is still sounded by Bsn 2 at 333–334 and by Bsn 1 at 339, and Tbn 2's B2 now fills the fifth inside that octave. No outer-voice change; the analyser shows the same 7 parallels.

### 2. Bars 335–338: the trumpets crescendo to fff with the horns, so the unpaired horns are not marked above them (applied)
**Problem.** At 336–338 four horns play on three pitches (Hn 1 B4/C5, Hn 2 + Hn 3 F♯4, Hn 4 B3) against two trumpets (Tpt 1 B4–A4, Tpt 2 D♯4). Since c128 the trumpets crescendo from f at 335 to fff at 336, the same level as the horns. At 333 the score follows Rimsky's rule (horns ff, trumpets and trombones f, c086). At the peak it drops the rule, so Tpt 2's low D♯4, the leading tone, carries more weight than any other note in the brass.
**Evidence.** PDF 103: when "the orchestra only includes two trumpets, it is impossible for the horns to proceed in pairs. In such cases ... the horns being marked one degree louder than the other instruments". The diagram shows trumpets and trombones f with horns ff, and "the same method should be followed whenever the use of horns in pairs fails". PDF 112: grade the dynamics, "marking the wood-wind one degree louder than the brass". Here the wood-wind is fff, the same as the trumpets. Dan's rule in BRIEF (weight, not bite) points the same way: the trumpets add weight on Hn 1's B4 without getting louder.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily: `codaTptOne` 335–336 `b'4-.\< b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'2.--\fff` → `b'4-. b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'2.--`, and `codaTptTwo` `e'4-.\< e'8-. e'-. e'4-. | dis'2.--\fff` → `e'4-. e'8-. e'-. e'4-. | dis'2.--`. The trumpets stay at their f from 333 through 338, and the diminuendo at 339 now starts from f. The shared staff keeps equal dynamics. This lowers a brass dynamic: there is no new dynamic, accent or entry. The single fff at 336 stays in the strings, wood-wind, horns and timpani (c028, c168). c128's concern (the trumpets jumping f → fff without a crescendo) no longer applies, because they now stay at f.
