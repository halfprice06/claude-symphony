## What else I studied

- **Master comparison.** Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 2, iii (Scherzo, Allegro molto vivace), in the full score from IMSLP #36733 (PDF pages 110–144, 482 bars in 3/8, C minor). https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.2,_Op.17_(Tchaikovsky,_Pyotr). I looked at the opening page and the last two pages at readable size, and at the rest at low resolution.
  - **Opening.** Strings alone, f, for 16 bars. At letter A the full woodwind takes the harmony in a staccato block.
  - **Coda (449–482).** It builds in exactly Rimsky's order (principle 14): a pp timpani figure; strings pp, cresc.; horns p; oboes and clarinets mf; then flutes, piccolo and bassoons. It ends with groups taking turns. First the woodwind plays repeated quaver chords ff against pizz. string off-beats ff (Rimsky Ex. 210–218). Then horns and trumpets alone. Then a tutti arco chord and a closing woodwind flourish.
  - **The revision.** Tchaikovsky wrote of it that "the Scherzo is radically altered". The Tchaikovsky Research note says the changes were mostly to instrumentation and to variation in the repeated sections (https://en.tchaikovsky-research.net/pages/2nd_Symphony; Wikipedia gives the form as da capo scherzo and trio with coda).
- **v329 against v339.** I compared them file by file. The changes are c331 (T's tied 4–3), c332 (French sixth at 331), c333 (Hn 3·4 drone from 149), c335–336 (labels), c337 (pp wind notes 289–296), c338 (T level, Vln I cresc. 329), plus the part-combiner solo flags at 341–344.
  - Nothing got worse. Outer-voice parallels are unchanged: 7, the same bars. There are no build warnings and the page count is still 13.
  - c337's single wind notes are Rimsky's own device for emphasis (PDF 129–130, "short notes in the wood-wind").
  - The French sixth at 331 keeps every bass instrument on C (Cb, Cbsn, Bsn 2) with the cellos' C on beat 3, and makes no C-major triad.
- I also viewed all 13 pages of v339 at 80 dpi.

## Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

On orchestral craft, mostly yes, and in the same ways Tchaikovsky's scherzo is masterly:
- The crescendo at 325–333 adds strings, then winds, then horns, in Rimsky's and Tchaikovsky's order.
- The diminuendo at 131–140 removes brass, then winds, then strings.
- The hand-overs keep each part's line regular across groups.
- Tutti appears only at f and ff.

Tchaikovsky does two things this score does not:
1. **He varies the scoring of his returns.** The second idea's third statement (277–288) repeats the scoring of its second (121–132) almost note for note in its first four bars. That is the one place where the assigned reading (principle 1) and the master both point to a concrete improvement. It is Finding 1, applied below.
2. **He ends with block alternation of whole groups at ff.** This score's ending is soft by design (cyclic G♯), so I do not propose that.

The gap later critics keep naming (T and the second idea are well made but not memorable, c289–c319) is a matter of melody. This reading does not address it, and I do not reopen it here.

## Findings

### 1. 277–280: the second idea's third statement copies the second statement's scoring (applied)
- **Bars:** 277–280 (compare 121–124).
- **Problem:** In both places, Vln I and Vln II play the antecedent in octaves, sub. p and up-bow, over the same Vla/Vc/Cl 1·2/Hn accompaniment. The only difference in those four bars is which horn holds the B (Hn 4 at 121, Hn 3 at 277). The consequent at 281–288 already differs (the tenor line in Cl 2/Hn 2/Vc, c274), so the listener notices the literal repeat exactly where the phrase begins.
- **Evidence:**
  - Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 119: "The process of scoring the same or similar ideas in different ways is the source of numerous musical operations ... interchange of tone qualities, variation of tone colour". Ex. 202–203 and 213–214 (Kitesh 294 and 312, "compare") show the same theme rescored on its return.
  - Tchaikovsky's revision of 2/iii changed mostly instrumentation and the repeated sections (Tchaikovsky Research).
  - The exposition already gives the idea a strings-first order: strings at 121, winds joining at 125 (c063). Reversing that order in the reprise is the variation Rimsky describes.
- **Fix (applied in dA.ily):**
  - Fl 1 plays the antecedent at the violins' pitch: `b''2->\subP( g''4~ | g''4 e'''2) | dis'''2( cis'''4~ | cis'''4 b''4. d'''8) | R2.*4`.
  - Ob 1 plays it an octave lower and breathes on the last quaver: `b'2->\subP( g'4~ | g'4 e''2) | dis''2( cis''4~ | cis''4 b'4.) r8`. Its existing 281 entry continues as `c''2\<->( a'4~` (the redundant pp is removed; Ob 1 is already at p).
  - Vln I and Vln II rest 277–279. They re-enter with the up-beat D on the last quaver of 280, which is the flute's last note, so the transfer follows Rimsky's rule that "the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one" (PDF 125–126; Ex. 237–238). The code is `R2. | R2. | R2. | r4 r4 r8 d'''8(\p\upbow | c'''2\<-> a''4~ | a''4 fis'''2)`, and the same an octave lower for Vln II.
  - Fl 1 rests 281–284 and keeps its f entry at 285.
  - Nothing else changes: notes, harmony, accompaniment, dynamics at 285, and the hemiola.
- **Effects:**
  - Flute and oboe in octaves carry the legato line at p over the existing pp/sub. p accompaniment.
  - The violins come back after three and a half bars' rest and take the climb to F♯6 with the consequent.
  - Fl 1's B5–E6 is its clear middle-upper register, and Ob 1's B4–E5 is its best register.
- **Earlier decisions:**
  - This moves c318's Vln I/II up-bow at 277 to the up-beat at 280; the bow direction into 281 is the same, down-bow on the accented C.
  - It keeps c039's sub. p at 277 and c302's protected harmony at 277–284.
- **Build:** clean (0 warnings, 13 pages, C-major scan "none", outer-voice parallels unchanged at 7, part lengths all match).

### 3. 357–376: the diminuendo order is the reverse of Rimsky's (no change; protected)
- **Bars:** 357–376.
- **What the rule says:** PDF 130 removes brass first, then woodwind, then strings.
- **What the score does:** here the woodwind and the lower strings leave at 364, and the horn stays to the end with Vln I.
- **Why it stays:** the cyclic rule (the movement ends on G♯ in the horn, which the finale takes as A♭) is a stronger reason than the textbook order. Rimsky's own Ex. 236 (Snegourotchka) also ends a passage on a held high violin over soft winds and harp. No change.
