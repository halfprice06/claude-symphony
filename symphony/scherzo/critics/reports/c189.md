## Holistic verdict: Elgar 1/ii

Elgar's movement keeps one unbroken stream of quavers. It is menacing rather than playful. Its trio sits in a remote key (B♭ against F♯ minor), it is scored lightly with flutes and strings, and at the end the scherzo theme slows and becomes the Adagio's theme without a pause. This scherzo follows a different model. Its form is the Beethoven 9 and Dvořák 9 type: sonata scherzo, trio in the tonic major, a da capo that is written out and varied, and a coda. Within that model it does the main things Elgar does. The trio T is a real contrast in texture and register, the retransition at 241–252 builds pressure the way Elgar's returns do, and the final hinge on G♯ ties the movement to its neighbour, as Elgar's join does. A conductor would accept the large shape and the orchestration as professional. What still sounds like a student at speed is the inner voice-leading in the second group of the da capo (sC/dC, 105–140 and 305–324): the tenor countermelody and the horn line are written against the theme rather than with it. The three findings below are all there. I do not recommend imitating Elgar's continuous motion or his remote-key trio. Either would undo protected decisions (the tonic-major trio, the hemiola cadences) and would gain nothing over the present model.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Ob 2 leading tone left hanging at 104→105 and 304→305 (regression since v179)

**Bars:** 104–105, 304–305.
**Problem:** In v179 Ob 2 held the seventh A4 at 101–104, and c057 had added G4 on beat 2 of 105 to resolve it. Then c184 changed Ob 2 at 101–104 to the leading tone D♯5 but did not touch 105. So the most exposed voice of the dominant now leaps D♯5 to G4, an augmented fifth after a rest, while the rest of the woodwinds land on E. The leading tone does not resolve, and the G4 now resolves nothing.
**Evidence:** A&S Unit 22 (PDF 373) gives the rule that in the dominant the leading tone rises and the 7th falls. Unit 25 (PDF 419) repeats it for temporary leading tones and asks that the chromatic step be kept in one voice. scan2 reported "Ob2 105.2 dis'' -> g'" on v189, and the line is gone after the fix.
**Fix (applied):** sC.ily and dC.ily:
`sCOboeTwo = { \partCombineApart e''4-. r4 r4 | ...` (was `r4 g'4-. r4`), and the same in `dCOboeTwo`. D♯5 rises to E5 on the downbeat, in unison with Ob 1's E5, and both are already at the same dynamic.

### 2. Seventh of B7 abandoned in the tenor countermelody, 116.3→117 and 316.3→317

**Bars:** 116–118, 316–318.
**Problem:** Bsn 1, Hn 3, Hn 4 and Vc carry the tenor countermelody. It ends its phrase on A3, the 7th of B7, then leaps up to E4 and holds it for two bars. The 7th resolves only in Tbn 1's G3, which is covered by the woodwinds. To the ear the countermelody's 7th goes up, the opposite of where A&S say it must go. It also breaks the countermelody's own shape: it began G→E at 105–106, and that opening never comes back.
**Evidence:** A&S Unit 24 (PDF 398–399, the seventh-chord sequence in the opening example) says the 7th resolves down by step in its own voice; a decorated resolution is allowed, but the step must arrive in the same voice. Unit 21 (PDF 347) adds that the resolution should be softer than the dissonance; a leap up to a held E4 is the strongest gesture in the bar. scan10 listed "116.3 Bn1/Hn3/Hn4/Vc a -> e' at 117.1" on v189, and these lines are gone after the fix.
**Fix (applied):** In sCBsnOne, sCHnThree, sCHnFour and sCVc, bar 117, and in dCBsnOne, dCHnThree and dCVc, bar 317:
`... b2 a4) \breathe | g2( e'4~ | e'2 g4 |` (was `... b2 a4) \breathe | e'2.~( | e'2 g4 |`). In Vc, without the breath and slur: `b2 a4) | g2 e'4~ | e'2 g4 |`. A3 resolves to G3, and the phrase restarts with the G→E motive from 105–106. The E4 arrives one beat later and is still held into 118. Horns 3/4 gain one attack and Vc gains two (counted 33→34 and 432→434). The new G3 is the 3rd of the E-minor chord over the E bass and joins Tbn 1's G3 in unison. par.py shows no new parallels.

### 3. Obtrusive hidden fifth d5→P5 in the upper voices, 113→114 and 313→314

**Bars:** 113–114, 313–314.
**Problem:** On beat 3 of 113, Hn 1 has C5 and moves to B4 on the downbeat of 114. At the same time CA, Cl 2 and Vln II pass F♯4→E4. C5 over F♯4 is a diminished fifth, and B4 over E4 is a perfect fifth, both reached in similar motion in the middle of the texture. The horn's C5 also sits above the line in the octave where it stands out.
**Evidence:** A&S Unit 22 (PDF 375, Ex. 22-3c) names exactly this: d5 to P5 between upper voices in similar motion is to be avoided because the fifth is exposed. scan9 flagged "114.1 Hn1 CA/Cl2/V2 c'' fis' -> b' e'" in v189, and these lines are gone after the fix.
**Fix (applied):** sCHnOne bar 113 and dCHnOne bar 313: `c''4-- r4 a'4-.` (was `c''4-- r4 c''4-.`). A4 belongs to the iv chord on that beat and moves up to B4, in contrary motion to the falling F♯→E. The C5 on beat 1 stays, so the horn's accent figure is unchanged.

No further findings. I checked every other principle in the table against the score with the scans and with the rendered score, and the score follows them. c089 found an A2 at 331–332; it is a d3 Neapolitan resolution (Unit 28, PDF 484), not an A2, and needs no change.
