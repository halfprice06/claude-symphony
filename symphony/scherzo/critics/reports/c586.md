## Findings

**1. 317–320: Hn 4 lip-slurs a falling seventh into the low register on a crotchet at ff (applied).**
- Problem. In the reprise tutti Hn 4 holds the brass bass an octave above Bsn 2 (c114, c362). Its last phrase is one slur: G3 (317) – G3 minim + A2 crotchet (318) – B2 (319) – E3 (320), written D4–D4–E3–F♯3–B3. At 318 the slur drops a seventh on beat 3, from written D4 to written E3, onto a crotchet that lasts 0.23 s at 2. = 88, inside the ff tutti. The A2 has to be found by the lips alone, below written C4, with no tongue stroke, and it has to speak at once.
- Evidence. Adler PDF 304: wide leaps are hard because the hornist must "hear" and then "find" each note. PDF 303: the low register is "most effective when sustained or played in slow-moving passages" and should be avoided in fast passages. PDF 284: the loose embouchure of low notes makes their attack the problem. PDF 309: a slur is one legato gesture on one breath. c496 split the identical slurred G3→A2 at 314|315 on these pages. That leap fell onto a dotted minim on the downbeat; this one falls onto an off-beat crotchet. It is now the widest slurred leap in any brass part, and the only slurred leap of more than a sixth.
- Pitches kept. c570's A2 doubles the bass A–B into 319 and keeps the held F♯ off the motto tail. A3 would double the tenor's prepared seventh (Bsn 1, Hn 3, Vc A3). So only the articulation changes.
- Fix (dC.ily): `dCHnFour = { ... | g2.( | g2) a,4( | b,2. | e4) r4 r4 | R2.*4 | }` (was `g2.( | g2 a,4 | b,2. | e4)`). The A2 is tongued and starts the slur into 319–320, as at 315. Hn 4 shares the staff with Hn 3; Hn 3's slur and every dynamic are unchanged. No line is silenced or moved, so parts-cues.json is unaffected.
- What a listener hears: a clean A2 on beat 3, not a late or cracked one, in the bass that the brass gives the reprise tutti.

**2. The best candidate for a change a listener would hear: Hn 4 joins Hn 3 on the countermelody at 305–320, a2 as at 105–120 (not built in; it breaks a protection).**
- The reading supports it. PDF 311: the horn "does possess enough brilliance and carrying power in loud passages to be heard over almost any combination". Ex. X-12 and X-13: horns in unison on a line. X-5: it would take Hn 4 out of the "unfocused" low register for 16 bars of ff. It would also remove Finding 1's leap entirely.
- Rejected:
  - Hn 4's pitches at 305–320 are protected (c114, c362, c496). The stated reason is spacing: with the trombones and tuba held back in the reprise, Hn 4 fills the gap between the bass (E1/E2) and the tenor (G3–E4) at 307, 313, 317 and 318. Adler's brass chapters say nothing about chord spacing, so the reading gives no stronger reason.
  - The a2 countermelody would make the reprise tutti a copy of the exposition's (105–120). The reprise deliberately differs from it: no trumpets, low brass held back for the coda.
- No change beyond Finding 1.

**3. Endurance at the coda peak and the close (no change).**
- Low brass at 332–339: the tuba and bass trombone play eight bars without a rest (pp< at 332, f at 333, fff at 336, f> at 339): 5.4 s at 2. = 96, with the slur 333–338 lasting 3.8 s. At this tempo that is within one loud breath (PDF 292 asks for care "in a slow tempo"). The tuba plays only 28 bars in the movement (adler2 PDF 8).
- Hn 1 at 357–376: the motto tail and G♯ to beat 2 of 366 is one 7.6 s phrase at p–pp. After a rest, the G♯4 (written D♯5) at 368 is held pp→ppp through the rit. and fermata, about 12 s in the MIDI. Hn 3 covers the breath at 365–369. Adler PDF 292 notes that soft playing takes less breath than loud, and the note lies in the middle register (PDF 303). It is protected and playable.

**4. Stopped horn, mutes, four-horn choir, tuba octave (not proposed again).**
- c316, c406 and c496 rejected these for reasons the reading does not answer:
  - stopped or muted horn at 241 (soft-brass rule; the G♯ = A♭ turn);
  - four soft horns at 81–88 (the brass chorale is kept for the finale);
  - tuba E1 → E2 at 105–120 (the 16' weight).
- On the last one, adler2 PDF 8 says the bottom speaks "a bit sluggishly", but the same page allows low notes "used as slow-moving bass notes" at any dynamic. Here they are one per bar, attacked together with the contrabassoon and basses.
