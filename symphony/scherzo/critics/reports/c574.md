## Findings

### 1. Bar 168: the second trio statement's background starts on the same downbeat as its melody (applied)

**Problem.** The first statement of T (153-168) ends with Cl 1 holding E5 over the harp's last off-beat chord, the horn drone and single E2 strokes in the harp's left hand and the timpani. At 169 everything changes at once: the harp and horns stop, and the cellos and Hn 3 take T on the same downbeat that Vln II starts its muted quaver arpeggio and the basses start bowing. The new melody and the new background arrive together, so the ear has to take in two new colours on one beat. The cellos' T then begins as one more element of a new texture, not as a melody entering over a texture already in place.

**Evidence.** PDF 156, Ex. XV-7 (Weber, read from the image): the violins' arpeggio and the sustained lower strings start before the horns. Adler: "Notice that here the accompaniment begins first, and the entrance of the two horns sounds very fresh." The score already does this at 149-153, where Hn 3·4's drone starts four bars before Cl 1. PDF 156 on the Tchaikovsky example says that "timing and placement or assignment of color" decide whether a new element is "clearly perceived". The trio's second statement is the place in the movement where this matters most, because its melody is a low cello line (G♯3-A4) under a background that lies above it. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 125 (transfer: the last note of one group coincides with the first of the next) gives the means. The figure can pass from harp to violins inside bar 168, where the first statement is only holding its last note.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-574/work/sections/tA.ily).**
```
tAVlnTwo, old: R2.*16 | r8 b'8(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord., dolce" e''8 gis''8 e''8 cis''8) | ...
tAVlnTwo, new: R2.*15 | r8 e'8(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord., dolce" gis'8 b'8 gis'8 e'8) | r8 b'8( e''8 gis''8 e''8 cis''8) | ...
tAHarpR, bar 168, old: r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4
tAHarpR, bar 168, new: R2.
```
At 168 Vln II plays the arpeggio figure of 169-184 on the E-major chord: E4-G♯4-B4-G♯4-E4, pp, muted. It lies under Cl 1's held E5, so it does not cover the end of the tune, and it takes the register of the harp chord it replaces (G♯3-B3-E4). At 169 the same figure opens up an octave, and the cellos and Hn 3 enter over it. The harp's left-hand E2 and the timpani's E2 on the downbeat of 168 stay, so the first statement still ends on its I chord with the same strokes (c429, c453). The harp now ends its off-beats at 167, which makes its return at 185 (c094) one bar fresher.

**Rules and protections.**
- c407's do-not-touch holds: the two statements still differ in background rhythm, with the harp's off-beats at 153-167 and Vln II's quavers at 168-184.
- c409 (Vln II con sord. 169-192) now starts at 168. Vln II has rested since 132, so there is time to fit the mute.
- No string tremolo. No new pitch class: the figure is a plain E-major arpeggio over the E bass. No part-combined staff is involved.
- parts-cues.json: Vln II's Cl 1 cue was 165 for 4 bars, which would have run into its own entry at 168. I shortened it to 3 bars (165-167). The Cl 1 cues for Bsn 2 (part 10) and the cellos (part 29) still end at 168, before those parts enter at 169, so they are unchanged. The harp has no cue in 160-185. The cue check was done by reading parts-cues.json against the sections, not with kit/cuecheck.py.

**Why this is the best candidate.** This is the change from this reading that a listener would hear most. The other candidates were all settled by c394, c404, c446, c484 or c564 for reasons this reading does not answer (see Findings 2-5). The change keeps the trio's layering and colours, so nothing is lost, and the cellos' T now enters over a running background as in Weber.

### 2. 113-116: Fl 1·2 leave the top octave in the exposition tutti (considered, kept)

Two flutes at A5-C6 (E5-D♯5 at 114) in unison with Vln I and Ob 1 add little at ff, and Beethoven's layout (PDF 152) keeps the flutes on top. But 115-116 would reach B6 and C7, both protected. c404 tested the only legal version, with the upper octave at 113-114 only, and found that it takes the top off the theme's C6 peak at 116. c446 found that it breaks the doubling in mid-phrase. Mozart (PDF 151) also places the flute where it can finish the gesture. Kept.

### 3. 41-44: six instruments at A5 with only the cor anglais an octave lower (considered, kept)

This is Mozart's first layout (PDF 151): violins, flute and oboes in unison, with the octave below sparsely scored. c394 kept it on that reason and c367 added the cor anglais. Kept.

### 4. 177-184: no timpani or bassoon under the violins' continuation of T (considered, kept)

Tchaikovsky (PDF 156) uses a bassoon and soft timpani in the background of the continuation. c304 rejected a held bassoon, because it would weaken the drop to wind colours at 185. c484 rejected a roll, because it would blur the single strokes at 168 and 184 that mark the two cadences (c453). Finding 1 adds the new background at the start of the statement instead. Kept.

### 5. 105-120: trumpets on motive x, Cl 1 and Ob 2 resting (considered, kept)

Smetana's warning (PDF 154) about brass on the fast notes is answered by the cyclic rule, which allows the trumpets only motive x or the tutti rhythm. Hn 3·4's tenor line is the adhesive. c564's reason for the woodwind rests is economy, since their earlier off-beats doubled the violas. Kept.
