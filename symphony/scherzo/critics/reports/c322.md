## Findings

None of the 35 principles is violated in a way that a change would improve. c321's new T bar is sound by every rule in the reading. Every flag from the scans is either a 7th inside an extended dominant (Unit 24 §11), a transferred or delayed resolution of the kinds Unit 24 §9–10 shows, or something earlier critics already kept for stated reasons. I made no score change. These are the points I tested, ranked by how close they came to needing a change.

### 1. Bars 155, 171, 199, 211, 343: c321's new T bar 3 against V7 (kept)
- **Check.** The leading tone D♯ is now left by a falling sixth (D♯5–F♯4–B4, or G♯4–B3–E4 in A at 343). A&S's doubling and resolution rules (PDF 419, 439) ask where the leading tone and the 7th go.
- **Evidence.** The leap stays inside V7, so it arpeggiates the chord rather than abandoning the leading tone. In each statement the leading tone resolves in an inner part:
  - harp D♯4→E4 at 155→156;
  - violas D♯4→E4 at 171→172 and 211→212;
  - harp G♯3→A3 at 343→344.

  At 199 the bar sits on iiø6/5 of C♯ minor, so D♯ is a root there, not a leading tone; Hn 2 and Bsn 1 keep D♯4 as the 5th of G♯ at 200.

  The 7th A resolves to G♯ in its own register: harp A3 at 155→156, violas A3 at 171→172 and 211→212. T's A4 on the next downbeat then gives the decorated resolution of the 7th that Ex. 24-4b shows (PDF 400). At 199.3 the new B4 (Vln II/Cl 1/Vla) meets Vln I's passing B5 in similar motion. That is a direct octave between the top voice and an inner voice. A&S object to direct octaves only between the outer voices.
- **Decision.** Keep c321's bar as it is.

### 2. Bars 198→199: the 7th of C♯m7 (kept, and should be protected)
- **Check.** T's head B4 (the 7th of C♯m7) leaps up to E5 instead of falling. A&S PDF 399 says the 7th must fall.
- **Evidence.** Hn 1 holds B4 under it and falls to A4 at 199, the 3rd of the iiø6/5. That is Ex. 24-11's case exactly: the top voice leaps to continue a theme, and the 7th resolves "in the next lower voice and in the same register" (PDF 406–407).
- **Decision.** Keep. Hn 1's B4→A4 at 198–199 should not be moved.

### 3. Bars 49–52: outer-voice doublings in the hemiola sequence (kept; open since c052)
- **Issue.** At 49.3 C♯ (the temporary leading tone of D) is in both the bass (C♯2/C♯3) and the top (C♯6). At 51.1→51.3, A7/E → D/F♯ (V4/3 → I6 of D) brings the 7th G6 down to F♯6, the bass's own pitch class. That gives outer-voice octaves on the 3rd of I6. The reading's model (PDF 407: "I–V4/3–I6 with parallel 10ths") lets the 7th rise to A instead.
- **Fix considered.** Vln I/II, Fl, Ob G6→A6 at 51.3 (`a'''2` for `fis'''2`), keeping the bass.
- **Why rejected.**
  - The second idea's melody 45–55 is protected (c302), it is in the theme record, and the finale quotes it.
  - At 49–50 the doubled C♯ belongs to an arpeggiated A chord (A–C♯–A against E–C♯–A). It is not the moment of resolution.
  - At 51.3, D is a local tonic for two beats, so this is a doubled 3rd of I6 in a major key, not a doubled leading tone. Ex. 25-9 (PDF 422) supports reading these chords locally.
- **Decision.** Kept.

### 4. Bars 104→105 and 304→305: the dominant 7th A leaps in Vln I/II and cor anglais (kept)
- **Check.** The scan flags A5/A4→E5 and A4→E4 in Vln I/II and the cor anglais.
- **Evidence.** Cl 1 A→G resolves the 7th in register (c052, c093), while the violins leap to begin theme A on 5̂. This is Ex. 24-11's situation exactly.
- **Decision.** No change.

### 5. Bars 212→213: the violas' D4, the 7th of E7 (V7 of IV) (kept)
- **Check.** D4 is followed by a crotchet rest, then C♯4 on beat 2.
- **Evidence.** This is a delayed resolution in the same voice and register (PDF 408, §10). At 172→173 the same line resolves directly. The applied chord passes inside the trio tune's IV and connects two statements of its tonic (PDF 419).
- **Decision.** No change.

### 6. Bars 188→189: B♯ in V of C♯ minor followed by the B-major response (kept)
- **Check.** The leading tone B♯ (harp C4) does not rise to C♯.
- **Evidence.** The call ends on a half cadence, so V is the goal, and the response starts a new phrase in B. The chromatic step B♯→B♮ stays in one voice (harp RH), which is the arrangement Ex. 25-3a calls "good" and Ex. 25-5 treats as mild (PDF 420–421).
- **Decision.** No change. 185–196 is protected.

### 7. Bars 61, 66, 70, 86, 293, 298: A against B♭ between top and bass (kept)
- **Check.** These are the only outer-voice chromatic cross relations the scan finds.
- **Evidence.** Each comes from the passing B♭ of the chromatic tail in a fugato or stretto voice against a held or moving A in another register. They are softened by passing motion, as in Ex. 25-6 (PDF 421).
- **Decision.** Already kept by c232. No change.
