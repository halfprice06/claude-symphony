## What else I studied

I wrote a bow simulator in /tmp/critic-418/scan/bows.py. It joins the five string parts by sections.txt and follows Adler's rules on PDF 37–38. An unslurred note takes a new bow and a slur or tie is one bow. A printed sign overrides the count, and a sign on a continuing note counts as a retake. After a rest, a downbeat starts down-bow and an off-beat up-bow. Pizzicato is skipped. The simulator lists every hairpin on a bow of 0.6 s or more that runs against the habit, and every bow longer than 2.5 s. I checked each case against the source. I compared its counts with the plans recorded by c118 (trio climax), c218 (cellos 216–223) and c318 (basses 217–220). Two of those plans no longer hold in v418. c118's plan was undone by a later slur merge (c331, report line 56: "String slurs now span both bars so the tie is inside one bow"). c318's plan never took effect, because bar 209 has two notes in the basses. I also checked the coda statement at 341, which keeps its printed up-bow and is correct.

## Findings

### 1. Trio tune, second and third phrases (173–178 and 213–218): the climax is bowed backwards again (applied)
**Bars:** Vln I and II 213–220; Vc 173–180; Vla 177–180.
**Problem:** In v332, c331 tied the trio tune's bar-3 A over the bar line and joined 171–172 and 211–212 into one bow each. That removed one bow from every statement. From the down-bow entry at 209 the bows are now ⊓ 209, V 211, ⊓ 213, V 215, ⊓ 217. The third phrase's crescendo at 217 (`cis'''2(\< dis'''4 | e'''4 gis'''4)`) is drawn on a down-bow toward the tip. The ff A6 on beat 3 of 218, the trio's loudest and most expressive note, is then attacked up-bow at the tip, and its diminuendo is drawn toward the frog. The second phrase's crescendo to the ff F♯6 at 214 also falls in the second half of a two-bar down-bow. The cellos' statement at 169 (violas joining at 177) has the same count. This is the problem c118 fixed (its Finding 2), now back. These bars carry T at its peak, so of all the bowing in the movement they are the ones a listener hears most.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; "the tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural ... a diminuendo with a down-bow"; mark bowings "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". PDF 37, Ex. II-19: Schubert's bowing puts ⊓ on the stressed notes. PDF 39–40, Ex. II-23 to II-25: a crescendo needs fresh bow, so it is split into more strokes. The coda statement at 341 is correct because it carries a printed `\upbow`, so the score's own model is already there.
**Fix (applied):** This changes slurs and one sign, with no notes, rhythms or dynamics.
- tC.ily `tCVlnOne`: `a''2-\tweak stencil ##f \f( b''4) | cis'''4(\< fis'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>) |` (was one slur over 213–214); `tCVlnTwo` the same an octave lower: `a'2-\tweak stencil ##f \f( b'4) | cis''4(\< fis''2 ...`.
- tA.ily `tAVc`: `a2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp( b4) | cis'4(\< fis'2-\tweak stencil ##f \mf\>) |`.
- tA.ily `tAVla` 177: `cis'2(\mp\<_\upbow^\markup \italic "espress."`. The sign goes below the staff. Above, it raised the system and pushed the score to 14 pages, which I tested.

The bowing is now ⊓ 213 (f), V 214 (crescendo to ff and its swell), ⊓ 215–216 (cadence), V 217 (crescendo), ⊓ on beat 3 of 218 (the A6 at the frog, diminuendo), V 221 (c398's swell into the 4–3), ⊓ 223 (diminuendo). The cellos have the same plan from 173, and the violas have it from 177. Each new bow at f is one bar of 3 notes, well inside Adler's limit (PDF 39).

### 2. Basses at the trio's return, 217–220: c318's fix did not take (applied)
**Bars:** Cb 209–223.
**Problem:** c318 slurred the basses in two-bar bows at 217–220 so that the crescendo at 217 would be up-bow and the f at 219, with its diminuendo, down-bow, with the cellos. But bar 209 has two notes (`e,2 cis,4`), so the count from the arco entry is ⊓ V at 209, and so on to ⊓ V at 216, which puts 217 on ⊓. In v418 the basses therefore draw the crescendo on a down-bow and the f and its diminuendo up-bow, against the cellos (V 217, ⊓ 219, c218). The printed up-bow at 223 (c096, before the pizz.) is also a retake.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38, as in Finding 1. PDF 37: a slur is one bow. PDF 54: the bow before a pizz. should arrive at the frog by an up-bow.
**Fix (applied):** tC.ily `tCCb`: `e,2(\mf^\markup \italic "arco" cis,4) |`, one bow, as the contrabassoon already slurs it. The count is now V 217 (crescendo), ⊓ 219 (f, diminuendo), V 221, ⊓ 222, and V 223, so the printed up-bow is the natural one.

### 3. B pedal into the trio's return, 205–208: the crescendo is a down-bow (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 205–208, Vla 205–208.
**Problem:** Both parts re-bow every bar of the pp pedal (`b,2.\pp | b,2. | b,2.\< | b,2.`). From the count after 200, the crescendo at 207 into the return at 209 falls on a down-bow. Bsn 2 and the contrabassoon join here pp< (c179), so the strings should grow with them.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38 (crescendo up-bow). PDF 42–43, Ex. II-30: louré, a slur over repeated notes with dashes, is "very expressive ... used quite often in accompaniments" and re-articulates within one bow. PDF 39–40: a crescendo needs its own bow.
**Fix (applied):** tB.ily `tBVc`: `b,2.--(\pp | b,2.--) | b,2.--(\< | b,2.--) |`; `tBVla`: `fis2.--(\pp | fis2.--) | b2.--(\< | b2.--) |`. The dashes keep the slur from reading as a tie (Adler's notation of louré). The count is now ⊓ 205, V 207 (crescendo), and ⊓ 209 as before, so c218's plan for the cellos from 209 is unchanged. I first tried ties, which did not change the page count, but the louré keeps the soft pulse of the repeated notes under the motto-tail preview.
