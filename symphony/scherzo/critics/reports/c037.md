Adler's chapter is about moving music between media. What transfers to this score is what the masters do when they turn a keyboard texture into orchestral parts: give the cellos their own part, hold harmony notes as counterpoint, overlap the background when the foreground changes, and add a countertheme of the bar's own pitches. The score already does most of this. Below are the four places where it does not.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Reprise of theme A, 253–268: the cellos double the basses' pizzicato for the whole statement, as at 13–28 (applied)

**Problem.** 13–28 and 253–268 are the two full piano statements of theme A, 32 bars in all. Both have the same three-layer accompaniment:
- Vln II on G/A off-beats,
- violas on B/C off-beats,
- cellos and basses in octaves on the downbeat only.

The reprise changes Vln II to pizzicato and adds the flute's heterophony, so every accompanying sound in it is now plucked or staccato. Nothing sustains and nothing in the bass register moves on its own.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 205 (Ex. XVI-9, viewed on PDF 206–207): Brahms sets "the steady pizzicato bass and the independent cello part".
- PDF 209: Dvořák's soft strain has "a light accompaniment, and sustained 'harmonic counterpoint' in the strings… to complete the harmony without making it heavy".
- PDF 209 and 213: the repeat is given "a slightly different hue" and "a different flavor".
- PDF 230–231: Schoenberg's countertheme "made up of the pitches found in the measure, but having its own rhythm".

**Fix (applied).** The basses keep the pizz. downbeats; I left the exposition at 13–28 lean, since its first statement should be the plainer one. The cellos take an arco pp tenor line of chord tones in dotted minims, which is the only legato voice under the leggiero theme:

```
dAVc = { b2.~\pp^\markup \italic "arco, sostenuto" | b2.( | g2. | fis2.) | g2.( | e2. | a2. | g2.) | e2.( | g2. | c'2. | a2. | g2. | b2. | a2. | g2) r4 | R2. | R2. | e,4\p\<^\markup \italic "pizz." r4 r4 | …
```

- Every note is a chord tone or a prepared 7th: A over B7 at 259, 264 and 267 falls to G.
- No cello note forms parallel fifths or octaves with the bass or with the theme. I checked downbeat by downbeat.
- I first wrote C4 at 258. The analyser then reported a fifth at 259 (C4/G6 to B1/F♯6), because it reads the held C as the bass while the pizz. A is silent. E3 removes it.
- To give time for the change from pizz. to arco: Vc rests at 252 and the basses play that bar alone (retr.ily), and "pizz." is marked again at 271 after two bars' rest.

### 2. Retransition 241–252: bars 244 and 248 are empty in every part, so the three head entries do not connect (applied)

**Problem.**
- The violas' head (241–243) stops on a staccato E with rests, and bar 244 is silent in the whole orchestra.
- Vln II (245–247) does the same, and 248 is silent again.
- Under a poco a poco accelerando, the music stops twice and the stretto-like rise Vla → Vln II → Vln I reads as three separate entries, not one line of counterpoint.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 217 and 224 (Petit Poucet, viewed on PDF 220–223): "the background dovetails or overlaps", and "the background sometimes overlaps" with each new entrance.
- PDF 213: Dvořák's delayed entrance makes the passage "sound truly polyphonic".
- PDF 209: harmonic counterpoint, held notes that "complete the harmony".

**Fix (applied).** Each outgoing voice holds the head's last E as a 4–3 suspension over the B pedal and resolves it to D♯ under the next entry:

```
retrVla = { e'4-.\pp b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->( fis'8 f'8 | e'2.)~ | e'2.~ | e'2.( | dis'2) r4 | R2.*6 | }
retrVlnTwo = { R2.*4 | e''4-.\pp b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | g''2->( fis''8 f''8 | e''2.)~ | e''2.( | dis''2.) | R2.*3 | }
```

- Vln II's D♯5 resolves on the bar where Vln I enters on B (249).
- The recorded first two bars of each head are unchanged (theme record 39/39).
- The dynamic stays pp, so the hush of the passage is kept.

### 3. Trio, 197–200: at the trio's forte every part except the call holds dotted minims (applied)

**Problem.**
- From 193 to 208 the inner and lower parts move only in dotted minims.
- At the forte, 197–200, Fl 1, Ob 1 and both violins carry the oboe's call. Ob 2, the clarinets, the bassoons, the horns, the violas and the basses all sustain, and Cl 1 holds G♯–G♯–A–G♯.
- This is the trio's climax and its texture is one line over block chords.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 230–231: Schoenberg "adds a nice counterpoint made up of the pitches found in the measure, but having its own rhythm. This 'countertheme' continues to enrich the texture… in the second horn and the bassoons, then in the violas".
- PDF 203: "inner or even outer thematic material… can be made to sound and sustain simultaneously".

**Fix (applied).** Cl 1 and the violas, in unison, play T's first phrase at its own pitch against the call, over C♯ minor – F♯ minor – G♯:

```
tBClOne: … | gis'2(\f a'4 | b'2 e''4) | dis''4.( cis''8 b'4) | a'4(\> gis'2) | …
tBVla:   … | gis'2(\f_\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | b'2 e''4) | dis''4.( cis''8 b'4) | a'4(\> gis'2) | …
```

- B over C♯ minor at 198 is a 7th chord.
- D♯ over F♯ minor at 199 is the raised 6th of melodic minor.
- A over G♯ at 200 is a ♭9 appoggiatura that falls to G♯.
- The call keeps four instruments in two octaves; the countertheme has two in one, so the order of importance stays as it is.
- The leading tone B♯ is still in Cl 2 and Bsn 1.
- The two dynamics on the shared clarinet staff are equal (f, then > at 200).

### 4. Trio, 153–168: the cellos double the harp's left hand and the basses note for note, then switch to arco for their countermelody (applied)

**Problem.**
- For 16 bars Vc pizz. plays exactly the harp's left-hand note one octave up and the basses' note two octaves up. That is three instruments on one bass line under a dolce clarinet.
- The cellos' arco countermelody at 169, one of the movement's best inner lines, then arrives straight out of a pizz. part. It is not a new colour.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 228: Stravinsky lightens the texture "dropping out the winds and horns plus the double bass… so that when the oboe and bassoon play the solos… their entrance is very fresh".
- PDF 205: the bass belongs to the pizzicato basses, and the cello part is independent.

**Fix (applied).** `tAVc = { R2.*16 | b,4(\p^\markup \italic "arco" e2 | …` The harp's left hand and the basses keep the bass, and the musette under Cl 1 is lighter. The pizz.–arco change at 168 is no longer needed.

**Not raised.**
- The stretto at 69–72 and 297–300 still brings in Vln I and Vln II together, a fourth apart, where Dvořák's delayed entrance (PDF 213) would suggest staggering them. A one-bar delay would put Vln II's third bar on the ff arrival chord at 72. c017 settled this passage, and Adler PDF 245 (the cellos alone mark a pseudo-stretto) supports what is there now.
- Vln II's octave doubling of T from 169 (reading note 17) was accepted in c011, and I leave it.
