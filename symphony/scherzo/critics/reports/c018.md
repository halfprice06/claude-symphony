## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Phrase slurs in the strings are used as bowings (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 105–120 and 309–324; Vla 121–126 and 281–286; Vc/Vla 65–68; Vc 297–300.

**Problem:** The cellos' tenor countermelody in the ff tutti sits under three slurs: 105–109 (5 bars), 110–112 and 113–120 (8 bars). The violas' dotted minims at 121–126 and 281–286 are one 6-bar slur at f. A string player reads every slur as one bow. Eight bars at 2. = 88 is about 5.5 s of ff on one cello bow under full brass, which cannot be played. The player will rebow by guesswork, and the sound weakens at the end of each long stroke.

At 65–68 (Vc, Vla) and 297–300 (Vc), a crescendo into the f stretto sits under a single 4-bar slur.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow."
- PDF 39: Mendelssohn 4 allows only six notes per bow at f.
- PDF 39–40: the Liszt passage with a crescendo under long slurs "is impossible to perform … unless it is broken up into several bows" (Ex. II-24, II-25).
- PDF 38: a crescendo comes naturally on an up-bow. Splitting a 4-bar slur in half puts the crescendo on the second bow, which is an up-bow, and the f arrival at 69 on a down-bow.

**Fix:** sC and dC Vc:
```
g2.^\markup \italic "espress." | b2( e'4~ | e'2 b4) | a2( fis4 | g2.) | a2( c'4 | b2 a4) | g2. | c'2. | b2. | a2( c'4 | b2 a4) | e'2.~ | e'2 g4 | a2( fis4 | g4) r4 r4
```
sC Vla 121–126 and dA Vla 281–286: `e'2.( | g'2.) | fis'2.( | e'2.) | e'2.( | c'2.)` (dA keeps `\f` on the first note).

sB Vc: `e2( d4 | c2 d4) | f2.(\< | e2.)`. sB Vla: `a'4(\pp g' f' | e'4 f' d') | c'4(\< b a | b2 e'4)`. dB Vc: `d'4(\pp c' bes | a4 bes g) | f4(\< e d | a,2 e4)`.

Notes and rhythms are unchanged.

### 2. Theme A's accented minims and the ff arrivals fall on up-bows (applied)
**Bars:**
- Vln I: 18, 24, 26, 42, 110, 116, 118, 262, 268, 270, 278, 314, 320, 322, 342, 344, 345, 347.
- Vln II: 70, 110, 116, 118, 314, 320, 322, 345, 347.
- Vla: 70, 302.
- All strings at 72 and 304.
- Vln I/II at the second idea, 121 and 281.

**Problem:** Theme A alternates bars of four strokes and bars of three strokes (16, 20, 28, 40 …). Counted strictly, the bow arrives at the accented minim, which carries the theme's chromatic tail, on an up-bow in half of its statements. At 24/116/268/320, the peak C6 that cycle 1 added is one of these. The same count puts the ff arrival of the stretto (72, 304) on an up-bow in most string parts, and the coda's B6 peak (344) and its hemiola (345–347) on up-bows.

The accent, the f/ff arrival and the climax should come from the frog. The players will fix this in rehearsal, but differently in each desk. A master's score marks it.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 38: "two up-bows are called for in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat" (Schubert 5/ii, Ex. II-19). The same page says to play "louder and heavier toward the frog", and to "indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit".
- PDF 42–43: a heavy effect comes from "a series of down-bows … quite fast, with the bow raised between each down-bow … at the frog" (Tchaikovsky 6/iii, Ex. II-29).
- PDF 38: double down-bows for successive vigorous attacks (Coriolan, Ex. II-20).
- Dvořák 9/iii marks a down-bow at every ff entry of its theme (corpus, lines 26, 212, 422, 615).

**Fix:** global.ily gets a down-bow macro. `avoid-slur #'ignore` is needed: with the default setting, the stem-side Y-offset callback from c125 dropped the slur on the high C and printed "Nothing to minimise". `'inside` caused a cyclic slur dependency instead.
```
dnb = -\tweak avoid-slur #'ignore \downbow
```
Then mark the accented notes only where the stroke count gives an up-bow, for example sA Vln I `a''2->\dnb( gis''8 g''8`, `c'''2->\dnb( b''8 fis''8)`, `g''2->\dnb( fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.) dis''8`. Mark the ff arrivals with `e'''2.->\ff\dnb` (sB/dB, all five string parts) and the coda with `g'''2->\dnb(`, `b'''2.->\dnb`, `a'''2->\dnb … e'''4->\dnb`.

That is 36 signs in a 384-bar movement, each at a point where the habit would go wrong. The simulation now finds no accented string note on an up-bow, apart from the basses' accented downbeats with rests between them (row 15: the players retake there by habit).

### 3. Pizz. → arco with two crotchets at speed (applied)
**Bars:** Vc/Cb 44→45, 100→101, 280→281, 364→365; Vc 168→169.

**Problem:** In each case the basses pluck on beat 1 and must play arco on the next downbeat. In the first three, the arco entry is f with slurs. The gap is two crotchet rests: 0.45 s at 2. = 88, or 0.53 s at the trio tempo. Cycle 6 fixed 204, 223–224 and 276, but these five remain.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed … There are instances … when no time is provided … these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible (This is especially true of the return from pizzicato to arco …)." Brahms 1/iv (Ex. II-55, PDF 54–55) puts rests around each change.

**Fix:** The pizz. note in the bar before the arco becomes `R2.`. Other parts still sound the bass in each of those bars:
- 44: Bsn 2 D.
- 100: timpani B.
- 168: harp and Cb E.
- 280: Bsn 2 B.
- 364: the pizz. E of 363 is still ringing under T's held E6.

For example sA Vc: `| R2. | g,2(\f^\markup \italic "arco"`. Bar 364 lies outside the protected 365–384.

### 4. Trio viola off-beats: louré instead of separate tenuto bows (applied)
**Bars:** Vla div. 169–184 (pp) and 209–224 (mf).

**Problem:** The off-beat pairs are marked `--` without slurs, so each crotchet takes its own bow change. In the trio's quiet musette, that gives the accompaniment the same articulation as the bass and harp.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 42: louré "is a legato stroke, but a slight separation of the notes is effected while the bow is being drawn. It is a very expressive bowing used quite often in accompaniments … indicated by dashes … with slurs to designate the bow changes". Handel's "Comfort ye" accompaniment (Ex. II-30, PDF 43) is the model.

**Fix:** tA and tC Vla `r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--)` in every bar. The first bar keeps `\pp^\markup \italic "div."` (tA) or `\mf` (tC), and 223 keeps its `\>`. The rest on beat 1 gives time to retake the bow, so each pair starts in the same direction.
