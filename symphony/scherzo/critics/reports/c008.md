## Other study

Beethoven 9/ii (corpus PDF `Beethoven_Sym9_mvt2.pdf`, pp. 2–4, bars 9–57): every fugato entry is *pp*, horns and trumpets come in "sempre pp", and a written *cresc.* runs about twelve bars in every part before the *ff* at 57. That is principles 5, 6 and 13 in a scherzo that is one of this movement's models. Rimsky Ex. 253 (Kitesh, PDF 418) shows the *sf* string attack on a soft woodwind chord. Ex. 255 (Sheherazade II, PDF 419) shows short *sf* string chords punctuating *p* winds.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hemiola builds 101–104 and 305–308: no crescendo, brass enters on a flat *f* (applied)
**Problem.** Both builds to the tutti theme reach *ff* at 104 and 308 with no crescendo in any part except the timpani roll. At 101 the trombones play for the first time in the movement, together with the trumpets and Hn 1·2, all marked *f*. At 305 the whole orchestra plays *f* one bar after the *ff* chord at 304, so the music steps down a level and climbs back with no shape.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: the brass is the group "most facile in producing dynamic shades … glorifying crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". Rimsky PDF 136: brass re-entries should be *pp* or *ff*, not *f*. Rimsky PDF 129: the strings attack *sf* as the winds begin a *p* chord. Beethoven 9/ii bars 45–57 has the same shape.
**Fix.** sB bar 45 (=101): Hn 1–4 and Tpt 1·2 `\p\<`; Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn `\pp\<` (e.g. `fis2.\pp\<`), each ending at the existing `\ff` on 104. Strings and woodwinds stay *f* at 101, after their crescendo in 97–100. dB bar 13 (=305): strings `b''2\sfp\<` (Vln I) and likewise for Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb; woodwinds and horns `\p\<`; Tpt `b'4-.\p\<`; trombones `\pp\<`; timpani `b,2.:32\p\<`; all end on the existing `\ff` at 308. score.ly: `("sfp" . 0.42)` added to the MIDI dynamic map so the new mark has a MIDI volume.

### 2. Fugato 57–72 and 293–304: flat dynamics, a missing *f*, and a horn chord with no crescendo (applied)
**Problem.** The entries pile up (Vc 57, Vla 61, Vln II 65) but every part stays *p* until a sudden *f* at 69. Vln II has no *f* at 69 (it keeps its *p* from 65 while every other part plays *f*), and it enters at 301 with no dynamic at all. Hn 1·2 come back at 71 and 303 at *f* and then play *ff* on the next bar with no crescendo between.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130 (long crescendo through added instruments; brass crescendo chords). Rimsky PDF 136 (*f* re-entry). Beethoven 9/ii bar 45: *cresc.* in every part before the *ff*.
**Fix.** sB bar 11 (=67): `\<` in Vln II (`a''4-.)\<`), Vla (`c'4\<`), Vc and Cb (`d,2.\<`), up to the *f* at 69. Add `\f` to Vln II at 69 (`e''4-.\f`) and at 301. dB bar 7 (=299): `\<` in Vc and Vla. Bars 71 and 303: Hn 1·2 `cis''2.\p\<` / `a'2.\p\<` into the *ff*, and `\<` on the second crotchet in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc, and on the held notes of the bassoons, contrabassoon and Cb.

### 3. 273–277: the shortened counterstatement jumps from *p* to *f* (applied)
**Problem.** At 37–40 the counterstatement has a four-bar crescendo into the *f* on V at 41. The reprise cut those bars, so 275–276 (Vln I, Vln II/Vla pizz., Vc, Cb) stay *p* and 277 starts *f* in every part.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: short crescendi are made "by natural dynamic means", so a two-bar hairpin is enough. The model bars are this score's own 37–40.
**Fix.** dA bar 19 (=275): `e''4-.\p\<` (Vln I), `r4 g'4\< g'4` (Vln II pizz.), `r4 b4-.\<` (Vla), `e,4\p\<` (Vc) and `e,,4\p\<` (Cb), ending at the existing `\f` at 277.

### 4. Trio return 209–224: the accompaniment is as loud as the tune, and only two parts make the diminuendo (applied)
**Problem.** At 209 the Vln II/Vla off-beat crotchets and the Cb are *f*, the same as T in Vln I, Fl 1 and Cl 1. The harp LH has no mark, so it carries the *p* from 153 into a *forte* tutti. At 223–224 only Fl 1 and Vln I have the hairpin into the *pp* at 225. Cl 1 (the same tune), Vc, Vln II, Vla and Cb arrive at *pp* from *f* with no diminuendo.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 132–133: where melody and harmonic basis are close in timbre (here strings with strings), the basis should be "of inferior dynamic power". The score already does this at 13 (*pp* under *p*) and 169 (*pp* under *mp*). Rimsky PDF 130 on the diminuendo.
**Fix.** tC bar 1 (=209): Vln II and Vla `b'4--\mf`, `e'4--\mf`; Cb `e,4\mf`; harp LH `e,4\mf`, and harp LH `e,4\pp` at 225. Bars 223–224: Cl 1 `e''2\> r4\!`; Vc `gis,2.~\upbow\>`; Vln II, Vla and Cb `\>` from 223. All end on the existing `\pp` at 225. The Vc countermelody stays *f*. Hn 1·2 and the bassoons are already *p*.

### 5. Trio 200–201: *f* to *p* with no diminuendo (applied)
**Problem.** The trio's *forte* (197–200) ends on a held G♯ chord in the whole wind and string group, and 201 starts *p* with no hairpin and no "sub.". The tB comment says "diminuendo", but the score does not mark one.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: a diminuendo removes brass, then woodwind, then strings. The horns, Fl 2 and the oboes do drop out at 201, so a diminuendo on bar 200 completes the effect the scoring already sets up.
**Fix.** tB bar 16 (=200): `\>` on the held note in Fl 2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Bn 1·2, Hn 1·2, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb. Parts that rest at 201 end it with `R2.\!`; the others end on their `\p`.

### 6. Coda 337: the falling bass E–D–C♯–B is *p* under an *mf* crescendo (applied)
**Problem.** At 337 Vln I is *mf*, and the bassoons and bass clarinet play the bass E–D–C♯–B at `\mf\<`. The double basses play the same line at *p* and jump to *f* at 340.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130 (crescendo by added instruments: the basses are part of the string layer that carries it). The plan names this bass line as structural ("bass E–D–C♯–B").
**Fix.** coda bar 5 (=337): Cb `e,4-.\mf\<`, ending at the existing `\f` at 340.

### 7. Last bars 376–384: the horn stays *pp* over *ppp* violins through the rit. (applied)
**Problem.** The movement ends on G♯ in Hn 1 (*pp* from 376) and Vln I (*ppp* from 377). The horn never fades, so it stays louder than the violins until the last chord.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: a diminuendo removes the brass first and the strings last. Rimsky PDF 136: an ending at a soft extreme. Changing the horn's dynamic leaves the protected notes alone and keeps the G♯ in both instruments to the end.
**Fix.** coda bar 48 (=380): Hn 1 `gis'2.~\>`; bar 51 (=383): `gis'2.~\ppp`. Notes, ties and rhythm are unchanged.

### 8. F-major episode 87–88 and coda 369–372: clarinets end without a diminuendo (not applied)
**Problem.** Cl 1's dolce phrase ends on a held F♯ at 88 under the poco rit., and the clarinets' held E/B at 369–372 just stops. Rimsky PDF 130 calls diminuendo to a breath the clarinets' speciality.
**Decision.** Not applied. At 87 Cl 2 holds a *pp* D♯ on the same staff, and a hairpin in Cl 1 only would give the shared staff unequal dynamics. At 369 the clarinets are already *pp* under a horn *pp*, so a hairpin would change little. I leave both as they are.
