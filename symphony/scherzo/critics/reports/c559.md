## Verdict against Borodin

Borodin's scherzo holds a single fast pulse together with a few devices:
- a held or repeated horn note under the light, staccato strings;
- abrupt contrasts between loud wind chords and soft strings;
- irregular phrase groups;
- a trio that is one cantabile wind tune over harp and held horns.

This score has a close match for most of these:
- the leggiero theme over pizzicato and off-beats (13–28);
- the horns' held D under the counterstatement (29–31);
- the ritmo di tre battute (89–100) as its irregular grouping;
- the trio's clarinet over harp and horn drone (153–184).

A conductor would believe a skilled late-Romantic composer wrote it.

There is one place where Borodin, and the Strauss example on Adler PDF 245, would score differently: the retransition, 89–100. For 12 bars it has only:
- the three head entries;
- pizzicato B crotchets;
- the timpani;
- the cor anglais's arpeggio from 95.

Nothing is sustained (density 2.4–3.1 voices, analysis.txt). Then the hemiola at 101 jumps to 25 voices. Borodin keeps a horn sounding under such passages, so that the pedal binds the quick, detached figures together. That is Finding 1.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 92–100: no sustained pedal under the retransition's head entries. Applied.

**Problem.** From 89 to 100 the B pedal sounds only as pizzicato crotchets and timpani strokes. The three head entries are heard as isolated fragments over a dry, dotted bass, and nothing grows toward the f hemiola at 101.

There are two other weaknesses:
- Vln I's entry at 95 is scored like the two before it, so the third time brings no change (PDF 255).
- Hn 3 reaches B3 at 89, at the end of the F-major episode, then drops out for 11 bars. It comes back on the same B3 at 101.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12 (Fledermaus): a pedal "ties [a light passage] together harmonically, while pointing up the spiccato nature of the tune and accompaniment by contrast". Strauss "suspends the pedal for a moment so that the anacrusis to the second statement … is clearly heard", and "its omission later on contributes to the tension".
- Adler PDF 238–241, Ex. VIII-7–9: a held horn and wind pedal gives stability and adds power to the following cadence.
- Adler PDF 145: the subject heard alone at the start carries weight.
- Borodin's scherzo keeps a held horn note under its staccato strings.

c557 cut the violas' B3 crotchets here for economy. Their reason was that the crotchets doubled the pedal in the cor anglais's rhythm. A held horn note is a different case: it adds sustain and colour, not another attack in the same rhythm.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-559/work/sections/sB.ily, `sBHnThree`).** Bar 89 is unchanged, so the violas' subject is heard alone. Hn 3 holds B3 pp under Ob 1's answer (92–94) and breaks at the end of 94, so that Vln I's entry at 95 sounds as a new statement. From 95 the pedal runs without a break: it is tied, swells from 98, and leads into the existing p crescendo at 101. This is the "omission later" of PDF 245.
