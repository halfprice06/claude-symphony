## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Exposition tutti 113–120: give the lower octave of theme A to Vln II (applied)
**Problem.** At 105–120 the theme's upper octave has five parts: Fl 1, Fl 2, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II, all in unison. The lower octave has only the cor anglais and Cl 2. Underneath, Hn 1–3 and both trumpets play accented crotchets in the same octave (B4/G4/E4), and the trombones and tuba play the downbeats. The theme's lower octave is two reeds in the middle of the brass.
**Evidence.** Sebesky rule 6 (PDF 140): "Whenever a particularly strong ensemble sound is necessary, especially when the strings are playing a concerted melodic passage, the lead violin line should be reinforced an octave lower." PDF 168: "In any large mixed ensemble … the strings are acoustically the weakest element." Ex. 202 (PDF 151, image checked) adds the lower octave partway through a build: "reinforcing the lead line an octave lower, first with the top cello, and then with the unison violas". The reprise already does this (c029, 305–320).
**Answer to c029's reason** (the reprise should not copy the exposition). The bars 105–112 stay in unison. The reprise still differs: it is in octaves from its first bar, Fl 1 takes the top octave (c024), and it has no low brass (c033). The exposition now grows within itself, as in Ex. 202.

### 2. Flute on the lead violin line: one degree softer at 45 and 209 (applied)
**Problem.** At 45–52 Fl 1 doubles Vln I's f espressivo second idea at the unison, up to G6 (46) and A6 (50), and is marked f like the strings. At 209–224 Fl 1 doubles Vln I's forte trio tune at the unison, up to A6 at 219, and is also marked f. In the 6th octave a flute at f takes over the violins' tone, which is the colour these two lyrical climaxes depend on.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 164: "The addition of a single flute to the lead violin line of a string ensemble thickens the string tone considerably, sacrificing purity for extra body and intensity … To compensate somewhat for the difference in tonal weight, I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings." At 177–184 the score already does this (Fl 1 p against the violins' mp).
**Fix.**

- tC.ily: `tCFluteOne = { gis''2(\mf a''4 …` and `tCFluteTwo = { gis''2\mf r4 …`.

Fl 2's G♯ at 209 (do-not-touch, c065) keeps its note. Only its dynamic changes, so the shared staff has equal simultaneous dynamics. The existing crescendo at 217 still carries the flute into the A6 climb.

### 3. Bsn 1 stops shadowing the bass under the sub. p second idea, 121–124 and 277–280 (applied)
**Problem.** At 121 the second idea drops to sub. p (c063). The bass line E–G–B–A–G is then played by Cb and Cbsn (E1), Vc and Bsn 2 (E2), and Bsn 1 an octave higher (E3): three octaves of the same line under a deliberately soft passage. Bsn 1 adds nothing to the harmony in 121–124, because every one of its notes is already in the bass. Its own line begins only at 125 (A–E–D♯, where it gives V its third at 126).
**Evidence.** Sebesky rule 7 (PDF 140): the bass is the least important note. Doubling it automatically "anchors" or "grounds" the sound and "can also cloud the clarity of the bass line". Full, bottom-heavy writing is "fresher sounding and more effective if used sparingly". PDF 145–146 (Ex. 195): an extra root "would have grounded the voicing, making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating". Rule 1 and PDF 145 (economy): when a doubling is no longer needed, drop it.
**Fix.**
- sC.ily `sCBsnOne`: `… g4) r4 r4 | R2.*4 | a2(\p\< e4~ | e4 dis2) | …`. `sCBsnTwo` at 125: `a,2\p\< c4~`, so both players on the shared staff have the same dynamic.
- dA.ily, the same change: `dABsnOne` `R2.*4 | a2\p\<( e4~ …` at 277–281, and `dABsnTwo` 281 `a,2\p\< c4~`.

Bsn 1 now enters at 125 and 281 together with the Fl 1/Ob 1 entries (c063, c044), which strengthens their layered growth.

No further findings. Apart from these three places, the strings follow the reading's rules: few divisions, lead lines carried by more players, no high seconds, left-hand and right-hand groups each complete, horns with cellos used twice only, and high reeds saved for the coda peak.
