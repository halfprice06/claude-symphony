## Findings

None of the 21 principles is broken, so no finding calls for a change to the score.

Since c183 read this unit, about 90 cycles have changed the score. I re-checked against the reading every bar those cycles touched that matters for key areas: 47, 52, 197, 210, 214–216, 240, 253, 259/264, 341–347. The six decisions below are mine; the composer should keep all six as they are.

### 1. The ♯4 rule holds after the c184–c272 changes: keep

A&S PDF 214 (and PDF 228, point 2): "Don't double ♯4!" My scan flags only the following, and none of them is a doubled chord tone:
- Line doublings: 176 and 216 (T's A♯ in the violins and Cl 1), 207 (motto tail A♯ in Fl 2/Cl 2), 214–215 (the bass A♯ in Cb, Cbsn and harp), 298 (the stretto line).
- 52: C♯ only in the bass line (Vc, Bsn, Cbsn, Cb). This is V6/5 of V in G, the 4–♯4–5 bass of Ex. 14-2. c272's Hn 2 A3 sits above it.
- 49: the sequence case c122 closed.
- 192 (Cl 1 takes the harp's A♯4) and 244 (Bsn 1 sustains the cellos' pizz. A♯3): unisons on one line, made on purpose by c042 and c152.

c263's Bsn 1 F♯4 at 215 completes V6/5 of V over the bass A♯, the chord PDF 215 names for 4–♯4–5. No ♯4 is doubled at 213–216 (dump checked).

### 2. The trio's middle is built on vi, not on V: keep

A&S PDF 224 lets the B of a quatrain "continue to tonicize V", but does not require it, and Ex. 14-12's B only prolongs V. This trio puts V in its second A (169–184, the modulating consequent of PDF 222). Its middle (185–208) then works through vi:
- C♯ minor call, answered in B;
- the strings' C♯ minor call;
- deceptive V7–vi at 196–197;
- G♯ → A, A minor, B7.

The Jupiter Menuetto's trio does the same: its second half opens in A minor (vi), not G. So the plan matches a master's trio as well as the reading. The springboard, the A♮ at 193 and the B7 at 196 and 205–208 are the PDF 220 return devices. Decision: keep.

### 3. The coda's A-major statement and the E chord at 348: keep

T's first half in A major (341–348) ends on an E-major triad. That chord is V of A, and it becomes I of E at 349, where T's third phrase continues in E. This is PDF 219's "double meaning" of the last chord of a key area, used here from IV. PDF 219 also says a return after a brief stay "requires no special preparation", and none is added. PDF 220 calls the coda a varied repetition of the final cadence: 353–356, then 357–360 plagal. Decision: keep (357–376 is protected for the link to the finale).

### 4. The return to E minor after the trio: keep

The retransition reaches V through iv7 (243) and the French sixth (244). The bassoon has 4–♯4–5 (A3–A♯3–B3), the bass line of PDF 215 with the chromatic chord on ♯4. V then becomes V7 at 251–252, with the minor seventh A, as PDF 220 asks once a long E-major span must give way. The pacing is 12 bars, about 9.6 s, with the accelerando. That is the shortest join between trio and scherzo in the corpus I know, and c023 already cut 4 bars here. Decision: keep.

### 5. The reprise is half the length of the first part (49 s against 97 s): keep

This is the one proportion in which the movement departs from the corpus: Dvořák 9/iii and Beethoven 9/ii bring the whole scherzo back. A&S PDF 220 explains why the departure exists ("without the contrast of the tonicized V, there would be no impression of departure and return"). The reprise keeps a departure, the 16-bar excursion G minor → A → V at 289–304. It does not need the G-major key area again, because the first part already completed the departure and return at 37–105.

Restoring the full development (32 more bars, F-major episode included) would add about 22 s and bring the movement to 5:00. It would also repeat a contrast already heard. The reprise's own new detail (Vln II pizz., cello tenor, Fl 1 heterophony, the sub. p build at 277) is what makes it worth hearing again.

Between a 5:45 Adagio and a seven-minute finale, 4:38 is right. Decision: keep, as c003, c183 and c263 did.

### 6. No repeat signs: keep

A&S PDF 224: in dance-movement quatrains "both halves will usually repeat". Repeating the trio's second half (185–224) would add about 31 s. That would make the trio block (link to retransition) 119 s against a 49 s reprise, and repeat the forte return of T at 209 that is the trio's one climax. The trio already repeats its first half with a change (169, ending in B), which is the varied repeat the reading's examples allow (Ex. 14-12 repeats bars 9–16 with the same notes; here the second A modulates instead). Decision: no repeats, as c183.
