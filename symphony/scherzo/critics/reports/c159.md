## Verdict against Borodin

Borodin's scherzo works with a few clear devices:
- It opens with a loud brass chord that dies away into a horn's repeated-note pedal under pizzicato strings.
- Short staccato wind chords at f are answered by strings at mf.
- A legato, *appassionato* string tune runs over a pizzicato bass ostinato, with soft trombone chords under it.
- The 6/4 trio is a solo oboe cantabile over harp, held horns and clarinets, and a ppp triangle.
- The coda moves from the ff tutti to long held winds and sighing strings, ending ppp.

This score has a close match for most of these:
- The leggiero staccato theme over pizzicato and off-beat chords (13–28).
- The winds answering the strings in their own colours (29–36).
- The trio's clarinet solo over harp and horn drone (153–184).
- The long thinning close from 357 to the ppp G♯.

The control of rhythm is professional, and in one respect it goes beyond Borodin's movement: the hemiola cadences and the three-bar phrasing at 89–100 keep the 3/4 alive. The control of colour is also professional, and the harmony carries a real long-range plan (the French sixth at 243–245, the V6/5 of V at 214–215).

A conductor would believe a skilled late-Romantic master wrote this. Borodin is more direct in one way. He states his trio tune once, plainly, and leaves it alone, while this score's trio returns at 341–356 with a third orchestration. That return is earned by Adler PDF 255, so I do not count it against the score. The one place where a conductor would hear a balance problem in rehearsal is the reprise tutti at 305. There the theme's head is weaker than in the exposition tutti at 105 that it repeats. Borodin's own tuttis always put both violin sections on the tune. That is Finding 1.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 305–308: the theme's head in the reprise tutti has only two players on the top line. Applied.

**Problem.** The reprise tutti at 305 restates the exposition tutti at 105. At 105–108 the head of theme A is played by Vln I, Vln II (in unison with Vln I) and Ob 1. At 305–308 three things change:
- Vln II plays it an octave lower (c029).
- The flutes rest until 309 (c124).
- Underneath are ff Hn 1·2, the Hn 3·4 a2 countermelody, both trumpets and timpani.

So the top line is Vln I and Ob 1 alone, at the point where the reprise should land hardest. In MIDI scans of bar 305 beat 1, the brass and lower strings outnumber the top-line players four to one.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 152 names unison doubling of the violins as the first way to strengthen a string melody.
- Adler PDF 268 (Tchaikovsky 6/i) says the brass must be kept down, or thinned, so the strings and winds carrying the tune come through.
- Adler PDF 255 says that in a tutti the melody must come through.

Borodin's tuttis (IMSLP pp. 53–54) put both violin sections on the tune. c029's reason was that the reprise should not be a copy of 105–120. The change keeps that difference:
- The lower Vln II octave now starts at 309 instead of 113.
- The flutes enter above at 309.
- The reprise has no low brass and keeps Hn 4's bass octave.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-159/work/sections/dC.ily).** The first four bars of `dCVlnTwo` now copy sC's 105–108:

### 2. dC.ily line 27: the comment says the bass trombone plays with the tuba in 309–324, but both rest. Applied.

**Problem.** The c014 comment says the bass trombone plays the accented downbeats with the tuba in 309–324. Since c034 the trombones and tuba rest through all of dC, held back for the coda. A future editor reading the comment could restore low brass that was removed on purpose. The brief's cyclic list keeps full low-brass unison bass for the finale.

**Evidence.** In `dCTbnOne`, `dCTbnTwo` and `dCTuba`, every part is `R2.*20`. Adler PDF 268 says to keep the brass down so the strings and winds come through, and that is the reason the low brass is absent here.

**Fix (applied).** The comment now reads: "c014: 309-324 the sustained bass goes to Bsn 2 (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 101, 104); c159: the trombones and tuba rest through dC since c034, so the old note about the bass trombone doubling the tuba no longer applies; Tpt 2 E4 ...". No notes change.

### Examined and kept (decisions, no change)

- **Vln I's low dip at 16 and 32 (Adler PDF 141).** It is kept, as c059 decided. The dip is the theme's own contour. A cello doubling would weigh down the leggiero opening, which Borodin's opening also keeps light.
- **Doubled temporary leading tones at 49 and 51 (PDF 152, 257).** They are passing, they go by at speed, and they resolve correctly in both voices.
- **A6 at 50 before the climaxes (PDF 238).** The second idea's peak is f, not ff, and the true top (B6) is saved for 336.
- **Wind chord spacing at 72, 104 and 339 (PDF 151, 257).** It is wide at the bottom and close at the top.
- **Tutti doubling at 105–120 (PDF 267–268).** The flutes cannot go an octave above Vln I at 107 and 116 without passing B6.
- **Timpani f G♯ at 200.** It is the trio's one structural arrival and stays.
- **The a2 unisons at 37–44 and 273–275.** They are protected (c107/c111) and correct by PDF 249–251, because they are tutti, not soft solo, writing.
