## Findings

### 1. 106, 110, 118 and 306, 310, 318: the violas' off-beat chord covers the lower octave of the motto's tail (applied)

**Problem.** In the tutti statements of theme A (105–120, 305–320), theme A has a lower octave played by the cor anglais and Cl 2, joined by Vln II from 113 and 313. In each bar that carries the tail, that octave plays it in quavers on beat 3: G4–F♯4–F4 at 106/118/306/318 and A4–G♯4–G4 at 110/310. On that same beat 3, the violas, all playing non div., strike their staccato off-beat double stop <B3 G4> or <C4 A4>. Its top note is the tail's own starting note, now a semitone or a tone above the quavers, in the same octave and at ff. At 118 and 318 Vln II is on the lower octave, so two string sections play G4 against F♯4–F4. The scan found these six bars, and only these, among all bars where a doubled theme line meets a nearby attack from the accompaniment's off-beats.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76: when a string and a wind instrument of equal power combine, "the strings will predominate". Here the strings are not on the line. In the octave of the tail, the bowed viola chord outweighs the two winds that carry the chromatic step, so the ear takes in G against F♯–F rather than the descent.
- PDF 78: of a melody's two octaves, it is the lower one that needs the support of a second group. At 106 and 110 that octave has only the cor anglais and Cl 2, and the string group in its register is playing something else.
- PDF 76 ("fundamental rules which apply equally to melody, harmony, counterpoint and polyphonic writing"): the tail G–F♯–F–E is the motto's chromatic tail. It is the movement's cyclic idea, and HISTORY.md protects it.
- The score already applies this rule to the horns. c011 removed the horns' E and G from beat 3 of 106/118 and their reprise bars (now 306/318) "under the chromatic tail" (sC.ily, sCHnOne and sCHnThree comments: "Hn 3 beat 1 only at 106/118 (G against the tail F♯–F)"). The violas were never given the same treatment.
- The quiet statements already do it: at 14, 26, 254 and 266 the accompaniment's beat 3 lies an octave or more away from the tail.

**Fix (applied).** In sC.ily (`sCVla`) and dC.ily (`dCVla`), change only the beat-3 chord in three bars of each:
```
106 / 306:  r4 <b g'>4-. <b g'>4-.   ->   r4 <b g'>4-. b4-.
110 / 310:  r4 <c' a'>4-. <c' a'>4-.  ->   r4 <c' a'>4-. <c' e'>4-.
118 / 318:  r4 <b g'>4-. <b g'>4-.   ->   r4 <b g'>4-. b4-.
```
On beat 3, B3 is a fifth or a diminished fifth below F♯4/F4. C4–E4 is a third under G♯4/G4 and belongs to the iv chord already sounding (A in the bass, C4 in the tenor, E4 in Hn 2). The off-beat pulse stays and the harmony does not change. Only the clashing upper note goes. A comment with these reasons is added above each variable.

### 2. 106 and 306: the tenor countermelody's E4 on beat 3 (decided: kept)

At 106 and 306 the tenor (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3, and Hn 4 at 106) also moves to E4 on beat 3, against the same F♯4–F4. I kept it, for two reasons. First, the E is the countermelody's own tied anticipation (G–B–E⌒E, its signature shape). Second, it is a legato consonance with the bass E in a different colour, not a staccato re-attack of a pitch that belongs to the tail. Removing it would mean rewriting the tenor's rhythm, and c017 and c187 asked for that line to be kept. With the violas' G gone, beat 3 of 106 now holds only the tail and one sustained lower neighbour, not a cluster E–F–F♯–G.

### 3. Checked and left as they are (decided)

- 333–337: the coda's lower octave (C.a. + violas) plays the tail against the horns' held G4 and E4. This is a sustained tonic chord under passing chromatic quavers, and the tail is carried in two higher octaves by six instruments. Kept.
- 113–116: three winds (Fl 1·2, Ob 1) on Vln I's line. PDF 76 says these will overpower the strings, but here that is the intended ff tutti colour, and the lower octave has two winds and Vln II. Kept.
- 129/285: Ob 1 leaves the lower octave after the first minim (B4–G5). c214 did this to keep the oboe off its coarse E4. Kept.
- 349–355: the coda's T has the lower octave doubled (Cl 1 + Vln II) and the upper one plain (Vln I). This is Rimsky's PDF 78 layout. Kept.
