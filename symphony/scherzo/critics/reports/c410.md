## Findings

### 1. 56: the G major → G minor turn into the fugato is not heard in any voice. Hn 1 now inflects B4 to B♭4 inside the cut. Applied.

**Problem.** The second idea cadences in G major at 55–56. Bar 56 is one staccato crotchet chord, with B4 in Cl 1 and Hn 1, and then silence. The cellos begin the fugato alone at 57 with G3–D3–D3–G3, which is a bare fifth. G minor's B♭ arrives only at 58, an octave lower (B♭3) and in another instrument. The movement's first change of mode within a key area therefore has no voice that moves B→B♭, although the next three notes of the subject (B♭–A–A♭–G) are the motto's chromatic tail.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 596, Ex. 32-15 (Appassionata): III major becomes III♭ by a single step in the top voice, which is "the most frequent way of stabilizing" III♭. The movement takes the same route, G major (45–56) → G minor (57), without the step.
- A&S PDF 601–602, Ex. 32-21/22 (K. 542, in E): Mozart's G major → G minor exists to produce the line B–B♭–A–(G)–G♯. A&S: "Without the mixture of G major and minor, the melodic progression B♮–B♭ and the motivic reference could never have been achieved." With a B→B♭ in Hn 1 at 56, the B♭–A–A♭–G of the cellos' subject (58) hangs on that line, in the same keys and pitch classes as Mozart's. Mozart's figure is the scherzo's own closing motto tail (357–360).
- A&S PDF 584–585, Ex. 32-2: when the pitch pair is a motive, the direct inflection in one voice is the stronger form. PDF 587, Ex. 32-4a (Schubert): the chord thins to one tone, and that tone carries into the new section.
- The movement already uses this device at the next key change: Hn 1 C♯5→C5 at 72–73 (c042, A&S PDF 394), from the A-major ff into F major. With the change, both key changes of the development begin with a one-voice horn inflection.

**Protections answered.**
- The strings-only fugato (c007, c017, c254, c269) rests on Adler PDF 143 and 145: the exposition is given to the strings, and a subject first heard alone carries weight. The horn ends on the bar line of 57, so the cellos still enter alone. An earlier version I built, with the B♭4 held over 57, broke that rule, and I replaced it.
- The hemiola cadence 53–55 (c002, c012) is untouched. Horns stay soft: mf dying away, no accent (Dan, c123).
- No C-major triad, no stop chord (scan of score.midi: none). No theme notes change.
- Cyclic rule "quote the motto, do not develop it": the B and B♭ are the cadence chord's third and its minor inflection. The motto's four notes stay where they were, in the cellos' subject at 58. Nothing new is stated in the motto's rhythm.
