## Findings

### 1. Coda 337–338: the last hemiola cadence is played only by the tune (applied)

**Problem.** The plan calls 337–338 the coda's hemiola cadence. It is the last hemiola in the movement and comes straight after the fff B6. Only Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II play the three minims, A6 – F♯6 (tied over the bar line) – D♯6. Everything else holds dotted minims and restrikes them on 338's downbeat at fff: Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, the four horns, Tpt 1·2, violas, cellos and basses. The timpani roll through both bars. Nothing marks the syncopated minims: the F♯ starting on beat 3 of 337 and the D♯ on beat 2 of 338. So the bar-line accent that the hemiola should cancel is the loudest sound in the bar.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 51: a note "held from a weak through a strong beat conflicts... with the meter" only because "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation". At 338 the continuation in the tune is covered by about twenty fresh attacks.
- PDF 51–52, Ex. 3-8 (Mozart K. 283, bars 8–9): the left hand also plays in 3/2 (beats 1 and 3, then beat 2), and the hemiola bars are marked *f*.
- Ex. 3-6 (Beethoven Op. 69): *sf* on the syncopated note.
- This score already does what the book shows at its other hemiolas. At 45–52 the basses, cellos and bassoons play the hemiola, and only the horns keep the bar. At 101–104 and 301–304 all the strings play it (`b,2 b,4~ | b,4 b,2`). Only 337–338 leaves the bass on the bar line, and it is the most important hemiola in the movement.

**Fix (in /tmp/critic-048/work/sections/coda.ily).** Pitches are unchanged; only the rhythm and the accents change.
- Tune: `a'''2->\dnb fis'''4->~ | fis'''4 dis'''2-> |` in Vln I. The same accents go in Fl 1 (`fis'''4->~ ... dis'''2->`), Ob 1 and Vln II (`fis''4->~ ... dis''2->`).
- Bass and middle in the hemiola:
  - Vc `b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->`
  - Cb and Cbsn `b,,2-> b,,4->~ | b,,4 b,,2->`
  - Bsn 1 `fis2-> fis4->~ | fis4 fis2->`, Bsn 2 `b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->` (shared staff, same rhythm in both)
  - violas `dis'2-> dis'4->~ | dis'4 dis'2->`
  - Tpt 1 `a'2-> a'4->~ | a'4 a'2->`, Tpt 2 `dis'2-> dis'4->~ | dis'4 dis'2->`. This is a tutti rhythm, so the trumpet rule holds.
  - Timpani `b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->` in place of the roll; the roll stays at 336.
- The horns, Fl 2, Ob 2 and Cl 1·2 keep the dotted minims. They hold the bar against the hemiola, as the horns do at 45.
- No dynamics change: everything is still fff from 336. The low brass rest is kept (c034), and the B6 at 336 and the chord at 339 are untouched.

This does not undo an earlier fix. Cycle 34 only took the low brass out of 336–338, and cycle 29 only set pitches in the 338 chord.

### 2. Coda 353: the final cadential 6/4 has no stress (applied)

**Problem.** At 353 the last statement of the trio tune reaches the cadential 6/4. Cycle 44 expanded it to two bars: G♯6 – F♯ – E over B in Vln I, an octave lower in Vln II and Cl 1. This is the strongest accented dissonance of the closing cadence, and it falls on the strong first bar of the four-bar group 353–356. But the swell has already closed at 352 (`gis''4\!`), so the 6/4 arrives with no mark in pp, at the same weight as its resolution. The two-note slurs that shape the tune's own appoggiaturas (156, 160) do not reach this spot, because it lies inside a longer slur.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 54–55, Ex. 3-15: dissonances on the strong beat (accented passing notes, neighbours, appoggiaturas) are the accented ones.
- PDF 54: the phrase ends on its tonal goal, so the stress belongs before the arrival, not on it.
- Cycle 38 already gave the tune a melody-only swell on its third phrase. The 6/4 needs a smaller sign: a tenuto, not a second hairpin two bars after the first.

**Fix.** At 353, `gis'''2-- fis'''8 e'''` in Vln I and `gis''2-- fis''8 e''8` in Vln II and Cl 1 (Cl 2 rests, so the shared staff has no conflict). Notes, slurs and dynamics are unchanged.
