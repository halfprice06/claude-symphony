## What else I studied

- Schumann, Symphony No. 2, ii is not in the local corpus. I worked from program notes and analyses: Indianapolis Symphony (https://www.indianapolissymphony.org/backstage/program-notes/schumann-symphony-no-2/), Aspen (https://www.aspenmusicfestival.com/program_notes/view/schumann-symphony-no.-2-in-c-major-op.-61) and The Listeners' Club (https://thelistenersclub.com/2019/03/01/schumanns-second-symphony-a-journey-towards-triumph/). They agree on these points: a perpetual-motion semiquaver scherzo; two trios (the second contrapuntal, with Bach associations); the scherzo returning between them; and the first movement's brass motto recalled at the end.
- I read the whole v89 source and the 13 pages of the PDF, and v80–v89 critique.md.
- I diffed every section of v79 against v89, part by part, with comments stripped.
- I ran a script over every part that finds melodic augmented 2nds.

**Holistic verdict against Schumann.** The comparison flatters this score in its design. Both scherzi recall the first movement's motto at the close; Schumann does it in brass, this one with the horn's chromatic tail at 357–360, as the cyclic rules require. Both use fugato (57–72 here, Schumann's second trio). Both put call and response between wind and strings (29–36). Schumann gets momentum from the unbroken semiquavers. This score gets it from motive x over pizzicato downbeats, punctuated by timpani answers. That is the Beethoven 9/Dvořák 9 model, a legitimate choice, and it does not need to copy Schumann's moto perpetuo. The harmonic grammar is now clean: every chromatic chord I checked against Units 22–32 resolves as the book teaches (table rows 5–18). Two things remain where a conductor could catch the hand of a non-master:
1. One forbidden melodic interval, in the top line of the coda build.
2. A trio tune whose 16-bar harmonisation is copied unchanged through all three trio statements. Schumann's and Beethoven's returning tunes keep the melody but colour the harmony.

**Comparison with v79.** The changes from v80 to v89 are:
- oboes lower at 72/300;
- ♭II6/4 at 331;
- the tied A in the trio tune;
- the 4–3 and ♭9 refinements at 116/119/126/337;
- ♮VII at 330;
- horns under the second idea;
- Fl 2 a2 at 197;
- bowings at 53/129/261/285;
- cor anglais at 44/276 and 333–339;
- Bsn 2 on the cellos' line at 209–224;
- coda low brass f at 333;
- horn phrase slurs.

None of these makes the score worse. I checked one possible loss: since v88 the bass of the forte trio return (209–224) is carried by the double basses (arco) and the harp's downbeats only, without an 8' bassoon. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 78–79, which c087 cites, accepts undoubled basses under a cello melody, and the bass line is in the basses' normal octave (E2–F♯1). I decided to leave it. The single augmented 2nd at 331–332 was already present in v79 (the top voice was C6 over the iv6 then), so it is not a regression. It is the last item of the class c022 removed.

## Findings

### 1. 331→332: the score's only melodic augmented 2nd, in the top voice (applied)

- **Bars.** Coda 331–332, Violins I.
- **Problem.** Vln I plays `c'''4-. a''8-. a''-. c'''4-. | dis'''4-. b''8-. b''-. r4`, so C6 on beat 3 of 331 moves to D♯6 on the downbeat of 332. That is a melodic augmented 2nd in the highest voice, one bar before the ff head at 333. The chord at 331 is the Neapolitan 6/4 (C–F–A); at 332 it is V7.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 89 (Unit 5 summary): "The melodic augmented 2nd is forbidden."
  - PDF 514 allows it only above or into an augmented-6th chord, and 331 is not one.
  - PDF 484 names the melodic augmented 2nd as the reason to avoid ♭2̂→♮2̂ in the soprano.
  - PDF 483–484, Ex. 28-2: ♭2̂ in the soprano is "the most usual" and most intense position for ♭II, and its fall by a diminished 3rd to ♯7̂ makes "a peculiarly intense melodic line, especially when it occurs in the soprano".
  - My script scan of all parts finds no other melodic augmented 2nd in the movement (c022 removed the others). This one stands out at the loudest approach in the piece.
- **Fix.** sections/coda.ily, codaVlnOne, seventh bar: replace `c'''4-. a''8-. a''-. c'''4-.` with `f'''4-. c'''8-. c'''-. f'''4-.`
  - The top line now runs G5–A5–F6–D♯6–E6. ♭2̂ falls a diminished 3rd to the leading tone and rises to the tonic: Mozart's line in Ex. 28-1, here on the motto's F–E.
  - The inner leap is a 4th, like the theme head e–b–b–e.
  - Outer voices 331→332: F6 over C (11th) to D♯6 over B (10th); no octaves or fifths. The bass C stays doubled in Vln I's quavers (c080, row 5 there).
  - F6 is far below the B6 peak, which stays at 336 only.
  - Applied. The build is clean and the analyser parallels are unchanged.

### 2. 169–184 and 209–224: the trio tune's harmony is identical in all three statements (applied at 172 and 212)

- **Bars.** Trio 153–168 (clarinet and harp), 169–184 (strings), 209–224 (forte tutti).
- **Problem.**
  - The tune's bass is E E B E | A F♯ F♯ B | C♯ G♯ F♯ A | B B E E, with the same chords, in all three statements. That is 48 of the trio's 88 bars on one harmonisation.
  - The texture changes well each time (harp; strings with the cello counter-line; tutti with the horn choir and Bsn 2 on the cellos' line), but the harmony never does.
  - Bars 1–4 of the tune are the plainest part: I, I, V7, I.
  - A master keeps a returning tune's notes and gives its harmony at least one new colour on the return. Schumann's returning sections in 2/ii change their scoring and dynamics; the Beethoven theme of Ex. 25-1 carries applied chords inside a plain 4-bar unit.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 418–419, Ex. 25-1: applied chords in a simple Andante theme (bar 3 V4/3 of vi; bar 10 an applied chord passing between two statements of its tonic).
  - PDF 399–400: the 7th resolves by step down.
  - PDF 419: "the temporary leading tone is not doubled".
- **Fix.** In the second and third statements only, add the passing seventh on beat 3 of the tune's fourth bar: I – V7/IV – IV. The first statement (153–156) stays plain, so the change is heard as a variation.
  - sections/tA.ily, tAVla, bars 172–173: `r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a e'>4--( <a e'>4--)` → `r4 <b e'>4--( <b d'>4--) | r4 <a cis'>4--( <a cis'>4--)`
  - sections/tC.ily, tCVla, bars 212–213: the same replacement.
  - On beat 3 of 172/212 the cellos (and Bsn 2 at 212) have already left G♯ for F♯. G♯ therefore sounds only in the tune and its octave doubling, and in Hn 2 at 212, which rises to A with the tune. F♯ (the 9th) falls to E and D falls to C♯ at 173/213. The violas keep their louré off-beats, divisi and dynamics (c016/c018 untouched).
  - No theme notes change; themecheck 41/41.
  - Applied. The build is clean, cmajor.py "none", no new analyser parallels.
