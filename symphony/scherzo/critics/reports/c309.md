## Holistic comparison with Mendelssohn 1/iii

- **Minuet character.** Mendelssohn's minuet is forceful from the first bar. This scherzo opens pp (horn G♯, the horn call, timpani alone), then has a p leggiero theme over pizz. That follows the Dvořák 9/iii and Midsummer Night's Dream models and the cyclic plan, not Mendelssohn's. A conductor would accept either opening. The forte tutti of theme A arrives at 105 and is scored as Rimsky's Ex. 3 recommends: Vln I high with the high woodwind, and octaves below (rows 2, 9).
- **Trio.** Mendelssohn's trio sets a slow wind chant against faster rippling strings, so time seems suspended. This trio does the same with different layers: Cl 1 alone (T) over harp off-beats and a horn drone at 153; then the strings with a cello countermelody at 169; then the Ob 1/C.a. calls answered by the cellos at 185–192. On Rimsky's terms it is the better choice for a p dolce tune: one solo colour, not a coupled one (row 18, PDF 66–67). The four colourings of T are protected and I see no reason against them.
- **The link back.** Mendelssohn's long return with timpani taps and a crescendo is the same idea as this retransition at 241–252. Here the timpani play motive x on B pp, the theme head climbs Vla → Vln II → Vln I (Rimsky's Ex. 27–28 order, row 11), and the music accelerates poco a poco with timpani and bass crescendos at 249–252 (c238). Mendelssohn's link is longer. This one is 12 bars of one beat each. That suits a movement of about 4½ minutes between an Adagio and a finale; a longer link would add a page.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** On orchestration, yes. I checked this cycle's 21 principles from Rimsky's examples by scanning the MIDI and found no breach:
  - no divided strings in octaves;
  - no inverted woodwind order except one deliberate suspension;
  - no same-wind octave pairs;
  - high violin melodies always doubled an octave below.

  The gap noted at c289 and c299 remains: T and the second idea are well made, but not tunes one hums for life. That is a question of melody (lens 1), not of anything this reading teaches, and the finale quotes both. I do not reopen it here.

## Findings

None meets the bar of a clear improvement. Every principle in the assigned reading that applies to this score is followed (table above). The one place where the letter of a rule is touched, Cb without cellos at 341–348 (row 5), is a single-note bass line doubled by the harp LH. Its silent cellos were a deliberate choice (c267): they make the cellos' entry at 349 fresh. Rimsky's rule concerns melodic phrases given to the basses, so it gives no reason to undo c267.
