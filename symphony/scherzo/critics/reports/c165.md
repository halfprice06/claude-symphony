## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Three entries after 40 to 76 bars' rest have no cue, and the harp cue sits on the fourth ledger line (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** Cor anglais 44 (rests 1–43, then plays one bar of three crotchets f); Horn 2 at 41 (rests 1–40); Contrabassoon at 209 (rests 133–208, through the link, the poco rit., the key change to E major and the trio tempo); harp cue at 337–339.
- **Problem:** c075 set the rule for this set of parts: a cue at every entry after 40 or more bars' rest. These three entries have none. The contrabassoon case is the worst: 76 bars of rest across a key and tempo change, then an mp entry with the trio tune's return. The cor anglais has to place one isolated bar in a loud tutti after counting 43 bars. The harp's cue before its entry at 341 is Vln I at A6–D♯6, four ledger lines above the treble staff, which breaks c075's own three-ledger-line limit for cues and reaches Adler's four-line maximum.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest, the cues being transposed to the reading key of the instrument. Cues must be audible and obvious." Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows the cue in small notes with its label. Adler PDF 299 item 6 says cues help "re-entry after a long period of rest" and are "transposed to fit the part"; PDF 300 item 7 says to choose the cued line by register. In the cor anglais (a fifth up) and horn (a fifth up), Vln I at 37–43 would read up to G6 (c''' written g''', four ledger lines). An octave lower it sits on the staff.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-165/work):**
  - parts-gen.py, after `\addQuote "cueCA" { \corAnglais }`: `\addQuote "cueVlnOneLow" { \transpose c' c \vlnOne }`.
  - parts-cues.json: "05" (C.a.) adds `["cueVlnOneLow","Vln. I (8vb)","UP",41,3]` (the theme a'' d'' d'' a'' | c''' b'' b♭'' | a'' f♯'' f♯'' d'', written e'' a' a' e'' | g'' f♯'' f'' | e'' c♯'' c♯'' a'); "13" (Hn 2) adds `["cueVlnOneLow","Vln. I (8vb)","UP",37,4]`; "11" (Cbn) adds `["cueTimp","Timp.","UP",205,4]` (the B roll pp < mf at 205–208, the most audible event there; the cellos only hold B2 pp); "25" (harp) replaces `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",337,3]` with `["cueVlnOneLow","Vln. I (8vb)","UP",337,3]`.
  - Checked in the PDFs: every new cue is on or next to the staff, the timpani cue shows its tremolo, and the label says the cue is an octave lower. The audit's ledger-line warning for the harp at 337 is gone. Page counts and page-turn bars of all 29 parts are unchanged.

### 2. Horns 1–4 restate ff at 105 and 305, one bar after reaching it (applied, score and parts)
- **Bars:** 104–105 and 304–305.
- **Problem:** Each horn crescendos p < ff over 101–104 (301–304), so ff is printed at 104. At 105 (G) and 305 (T) all four horns print ff again: in the score on both horn staves (Hn 1·2, and Hn 3·4 under "a2"), and in all four parts. No other staff restates the dynamic at 105 or 305: winds, trumpets, trombones and strings carry the ff from 104. A ff printed again after a breath reads as a new attack, which goes against the soft-brass rule (BRIEF.md, c123). c056 added the ff at 104; the ff at 105 was already there and now duplicates it.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 8: proofread "both the vertical and horizontal lines of music"; the vertical line at 105 has two staves that disagree with all the others. MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10) prints a dynamic only where the level changes (mf, p, ff, pp, mp). The dynamics scan found no other repeat of this kind in the movement.
- **Fix (applied):** sC.ily `sCHnOne = { b'4--\ff` → `b'4--`; `sCHnTwo = { e'4--\ff` → `e'4--`; `sCHnThree`/`sCHnFour = { g2.(\ff` → `g2.(`. dC.ily: `dCHnOne = { b'4--`, `dCHnTwo = { e'4--`, `dCHnThree = { g2.(`, `dCHnFour = { e2.(`. A comment above each Hn 1 line gives the reason. score.midi is byte-identical to v165's, because the staff-level dynamic stays ff from 104 (c124).

### 3. The harp's "étouffez" is printed after its page turn (applied, score and parts)
- **Bars:** 237–241.
- **Problem:** The harp's last trio chord (237, arpeggiated) rings through 238–240, and "étouffez" stands on the rest at 241. In the harp part the only page turn falls exactly at 240/241 (forced by c075 so that it falls in the 96-bar rest), so the instruction is on the far side of the turn. The harpist has to turn with the strings still sounding and only then reads that they should have damped them.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 7: "Consider the tempo of the music and the playing technique of the instrument when determining how many bars of rest must be at the page-turn." Damping uses both hands, like the turn, so the damping should come first and be readable before the turn. Adler PDF 299 item 5: the player must have what they need before turning.
- **Fix (applied):** tC.ily, last bar of tCHarpR: `\tag #'part \once \override MultiMeasureRest.minimum-length = #16 R2._\markup \small \italic "étouffez"`. retr.ily: `retrHarpR = { R2.*12 | }`. score.ly, harp RH: `<< \global \removeWithTag #'part \harpR >>`, as the other staves already do, so the part-only widening of bar 240 does not touch the score. The damping now comes in 240, at the end of the trio's poco rit. and one bar earlier than before. The chord still rings for three bars. The score's system breaks are unchanged (pages 7–8 still break at 192/193, 212/213, 232/233, 252/253). The harp part keeps four pages and its turn at 240/241, and the text no longer crosses the barline.

### Considered, no change
- **Composer line on part fronts (MOLA PDF 6: "title, composer, and instrument name").** No movement of the symphony, score or parts, carries a composer line, and the parts must match the score. Adding one is a decision for the whole set, not for this movement's files. Decided: no change here.
- **Multi-rest ranges ("27–117", MOLA PDF 5).** MOLA calls them "helpful". c075's reason still holds: each line starts with a bar number and the letters cut every rest at 8–24 bars.
- **Vln I part, bar 370.** The tempo word "rit." stands above "change bow freely" on the last system, near the hairpins of the system above. Nothing collides, and it sits directly over its own bar. Adding system padding would reflow other parts. No change.
- **Viola part pages 2–3 (12 systems, then 3).** The two pages face each other, so there is no turn, and page 2 is not crowded at 150 dpi.
- **Timpani 332.** The crescendo roll has no starting dynamic, but mf at 329, three bars earlier, still stands. Not a hairpin-only entry from nothing.
- **Cue labels "Vc." and "Cb." (MOLA Ex. 2 uses "Vlc." and "D.B.").** These are examples, not rules, and the labels match the score's short names.
