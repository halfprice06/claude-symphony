### 2. A stronger reason for "no ♭II in the trio or the E-major coda" (record correction; no score change)

c280 kept the trio and the E-major coda free of ♭II "because ♭II in major would need C major". The reading does not support that reason. A&S PDF 490 says that in major "the best strategy is to approach ♭II6 from I6", which needs no ♭VI, and F–A–C is not a C-major triad. A ♭II6 could therefore be written in the trio without breaking the cyclic rule.

The reading gives a better reason. Ex. 28-5 (PDF 485) shows a trio in the parallel major whose melody brings ♮3̂ and ♮2̂ after the minor section. Section 5 continues "Another way to contrast ♭2̂ and ♮2̂", so the trio's ♮2̂ is presented as a reply over a long span to the minuet's ♭2̂. The scherzo already does exactly this, at the same pitches:
- ♭3̂ → ♮3̂: Hn 1 G4 (141–144) → G♯4 (145), the pivot into the trio.
- ♭2̂ → ♮2̂: theme A's F5 (Vln I 14, passing G5–F♯5–F5–E5) → T's second-phrase peak F♯5. That F♯5 is held over ii in root position (Cl 1 158: C♯5, then F♯5 on beats 2–3 over F♯1 in the basses and harp).

The MIDI scan confirms that F♮ sounds in 14–118, 242–334 and the episode, and never in 153–240 or 341–376. Any F♮ in the trio or the E-major coda would blur that contrast. So the decision stands, but its reason in the record should change.

**Fix (record).** In HISTORY.md under c380, record: "Kept: no ♭II or F♮ in the trio (153–240) or E-major coda (341–376). Reason: the trio's ♮2̂ (T's F♯5 at 158, the pitch of theme A's F5) answers the scherzo's ♭2̂, as Ex. 28-5 (A&S PDF 485); this replaces c280's 'would need C major' (PDF 490: ♭II6 from I6 needs no ♭VI)."

### 3. Weighed and rejected: Verdi's ♭VI–♭II–V7–I ending (Ex. 28-14) or a Chopin coda "♭II" (Ex. 28-20) at 352–360 (decided: no change)

The reading's two models for ♭II at the end of a movement in major are both possible in principle here. One is ♭II6 instead of ii6 before the last cadential 6/4 at 352; the other is an F-major neighbour chord over the E pedal in place of iv at 359. I rejected both for three reasons:
1. At 352 the melody is T's C♯6–B5–A5 (♮6̂). A ♭II6 (F–A–C) under it would need a change to T's coda cadence, which is in the theme record (v44, v62) and protected by c093 ("coda cadence voicing 354–355"), and would put C♮ against C♯6.
2. It would cancel Finding 2's ♭2̂/♮2̂ answer in the last section.
3. In the four-movement plan the finale's coda owns the ♭2̂ neighbour over the final tonic (IV 227, 229, 247, 249, 275–276: C–D♭–C and E–D–D♭–C over C). That is the finale's use of Ex. 28-20. If the scherzo's E-major close also used it, the finale's ending would repeat it a major third lower.

The scherzo's close keeps the mixture iv (A minor, C♮ = ♭6̂) with the motto tail. It is a different chromatic colour and leaves ♭2̂ to the finale.

### 4. Weighed and not sent: a theme-A quotation on ♭II in the finale's D♭ area (Ex. 28-17) (decided: no message)

Ex. 28-17 (PDF 492–493) restates a theme a half step higher on ♭II. The scherzo does this with its own head (E at 13, F at 73). The finale tonicizes D♭ at length (IV 205–218). In principle it could take theme A's head there as well. I do not recommend it, for two reasons:
- The finale quotes theme A once, in C major at IV 275–276. A second quotation in D♭ sixty bars earlier would anticipate that quotation and weaken it.
- The finale's D♭ area is built from movement I's material, which completes movement I's N6–6/4–V (finale.md, Neapolitan thread).

No message is sent.
