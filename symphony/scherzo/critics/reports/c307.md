## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 70 and 298: Vln II's stretto entry is hidden inside the violas. Applied.

**Problem.** At 69–71 the stretto has entries a bar apart: Vln I with Fl 1 and Ob 1 on A at 69, Vln II with Cl 1 on E at 70, cellos with Bsn 1 and Hn 1·2 on A at 71 (c097). The violas keep the line they had before c097, a sixth under Vln I: C5–A4–A4, then E5–D5 at 70, then C♯5–D5–E5. So at 70 Vln II's entry E5–B4–B4–E5 starts on the violas' own E5. It runs under the violas' held E5 on beat 2 (B4 quavers under E5), and on beat 3 it rubs a second against the violas' D5 (E5/D5). The only string entry of the stretto that begins mid-texture starts in unison with another string section, in the same colour and register. Cl 1 alone carries its attack. The reprise copies the bar at 298.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 144: in contrapuntal string writing, clarity comes from "separating theme and countertheme registerally (one high, one low; or vice versa)" and from rhythmic difference, and "it is important to understand that although the group is homogenous, the subject comes through because of the rhythmic differences between the foreground (subject), the middleground (countersubject), and the background". Here the violas move in the same crotchet pulse as the entry and sit on top of it. PDF 135: stay away from the melody's register "especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color". PDF 142: the layers must reach the listener "in the exact order of priority". The scans confirm it: 70 and 298 are the only bars in the movement where a fugato or stretto entry crosses another string section (`strings.py cross`), and the violas' E5 is the only unison with an entry's first note (`strings.py uni`). The fugato exposition at 61–68 does what PDF 144 asks: the viola subject at D5 lies above the cellos' countersubject at D4, and Vln II at A5 above the violas' A4.

**Fix (applied; sB.ily `sBVla` bar 70 and dB.ily `dBVla` bar 298, with a `% c307` comment above each variable).**
```
old: c''4-.\f a'-. a'-. | e''2->\dnb d''4-. | cis''4-. d''-.\< e''-. |
new: c''4-.\f a'-. a'-. | a'2->\dnb gis'4-. | cis''4-. d''-.\< e''-. |
```
The violas stay below the entry for the whole bar: A4 against E5 on beat 1 and G♯4 against E5 on beat 3. On beat 2, Vln II's B4 quavers pass over the violas' A4. The bar now sounds A minor (Vln I C6, Vln II E5, violas A4, bass A) and then E major over the A pedal (E, G♯, B/B♭) under Vln I's chromatic tail. That is a clearer dominant into the A-major chord of 71–72 than the old D5, which made a fourth over the pedal. The violas still couple Vln I a sixth lower at 69 and 71. Their G♯4 goes up a fourth to C♯5 at 71, so the leading tone leaves by leap in an inner voice while A sounds in Vln I and the cellos. Articulation, the accent and the down-bow are kept. No wind doubles the old viola line, so nothing else changes. The whole-movement scan shows no new fifths or octaves between any pair of players. analysis.txt is byte-identical to v307's, so its outer voices are unchanged.

This completes c097: it gave Vln II the 70 entry ("they had doubled Vln I a fourth/third below") but left the violas on the old coupling line, so it does not undo any earlier fix.
