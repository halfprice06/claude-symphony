## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 181–182: the cadential 6/4 and V7 of B have no sustained bass (regression). Applied.

**Problem.** Since c344 the cellos carry T at 169–184. At 181–182 the only F♯ in the bass is the pizzicato F♯1 on each downbeat, which dies at once. The lowest sustained voice is the cellos' melody: D♯3–F♯3–B3, then B3–A♯3–C♯4. So beat 3 of 181 sounds as a root-position B triad and 182 as V6, not as 6/4–V7 over F♯. The cadence the antecedent prepares (I–V at 172, 176) loses its strongest moment. c192 had fixed this same bar with a bowed F♯. At 165, in the first statement, the harp's low B1 rings under the 6/4, but the harp rests at 169–184.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 152, §1: the cadential 6/4 is V with an embellishment. With a different bass it is a different chord ("play with a D in the bass and hear how different").
- PDF 154–155, §§5–6: the 6/4 "resolves over a stationary bass"; the bass holds while 6–5 and 4–3 move above it.
- Review points 1 and 4 on PDF 160 repeat this.
- A pizzicato attack does not hold the bass, so the 6th and 4th have nothing to resolve over.

**Fix (applied, tA.ily).**
```
tABsnOne = { R2.*28 | \partCombineSoloII R2.*4 | \partCombineAutomatic }
tABsnTwo = { R2.*28 | \partCombineSoloII fis,2.~\p( | fis,2. | b,2.~ | b,2) r4 | \partCombineAutomatic }
```

**Result.**
- Bsn 2 holds F♯2 under 181–182 at p, an octave above the pizz. F♯1, and resolves to B2 under the cellos' cadence.
- The staff prints "2." on page 7, and Bsn 1 is silent.
- The bassoons were tacet from 153 to 192, so the entry is fresh and does not cover the cellos' tenor register.

### 2. Bars 76→77: ii of F moves straight to I6 (syntax rule 3). Applied, answering the protection.

**Problem.** In the F-major episode the harmony is I I ii ii I6 vi vii°7 I. Bar 76 is root-position ii (G–B♭–D, with D4 in Bsn 1) over G2 in the bass clarinet and harp. Bar 77 is I6 over A2. An intermediate harmony goes to a tonic, and the rising bass F–G–A has no dominant in it.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 148, rule 3: intermediate harmonies do not lead to I or I6. Ex. 9-23b marks this "poor".
- PDF 141 and Ex. 9-12: a stepwise bass rising from I to I6 takes a passing V4/3, or "VII6 would also be possible", over 2̂.

**Why the protection does not cover it.**
- c192 and c282 left the bar because 73–88 is protected. The recorded reasons are the episode's colour and spacing, the harp melody, and the F key for the finale's IV.
- c122 noted that V4/3 of F contains C–E–G.
- VII6 (E–G–B♭ over G) answers both: it has no C, and it leaves the harp melody, the bass line, the spacing and the key unchanged.
- The reading gives a stronger reason than the lock.

**Fix (applied, sB.ily; bar 76 = 20th bar of sB).**
- Harp LH `g,4 <d g bes>2` → `g,4 <e g bes>2`.
- Bsn 1 `d'2.` → `bes2.` (B♭3 to A3).
- Hn 4 `g2-- f4--)` → `e2.--)`.

**Result.**
- The leading tone E3 is in Hn 4 and the harp, and Hn 4 resolves it E3–F3 at 77.
- B♭ is doubled (Hn 3, Bsn 1 and the harp), which is a normal VII6 doubling.
- The melody's A5 on beat 3 is a passing note.
- No D sounds against the E, the bass G2 still moves by step to A2 (rule 1, PDF 148), and cmajor.py prints "none".

### 3. Bar 129: Cl 2 leaps a 6th into a doubled 4th of the cadential 6/4. Applied.

**Problem.** At 128 beat 2 to 129, Cl 2 goes G3–E4 into the hemiola cadence's 6/4 over B1, doubling the violas' E4. The violas already hold E4 as a common tone, and the bass B is not doubled in the woodwinds.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 153: the 4th enters as a common tone or by step from above, "even in complex instrumental styles".
- PDF 154: double the bass first; the 4th "never" except in very free textures (Ex. 10-5c "wrong").

**Fix (applied, sC.ily, sCClTwo bar 25).** `e'4(\f e' e'` → `b4(\f b b`.
- Cl 2 holds B3 from 128 beat 1 and rises a 3rd to D♯4 at 130.
- The hemiola rhythm (protected) is unchanged.
- At 285 the clarinets have another figure, so that bar needs no change.

### 4. Bars 221–222: A2 in Vc, Bsn 2 and Hn 4 doubles the V7's seventh and leaves upward. Not applied.

**Problem.** Over the B1 pedal (Cb), the cello line B2–C♯3 | A2–B2 puts A2 under the V7 at 222, while Bsn 1 and the violas resolve A3–G♯3. PDF 139–140 asks that the 7th resolve down and not be doubled.

**Decision: kept.**
- The A2 is the lower neighbour of the bass line's B over a sounding B1 pedal, a turn B–C♯–A–B. It is not a chord member in the bass.
- The structural seventh resolves correctly in Bsn 1 and the violas.
- The alternative, `fis,4) b,2` in Vc, Bsn 2 and Hn 4, spoils the turn that 353 repeats.

### 5. Bars 192→193: the bass repeats F♯ from weak to strong under a chord change. Not applied.

**Problem.** V of B at 192 is followed by ii of E (F♯ bass) at 193. Ex. 9-24a (PDF 149) calls a weak-to-strong bass repetition under a new chord poor.

**Decision: kept.**
- 192–193 is a phrase seam. The cellos rest, and the bass re-enters arco with a new phrase and a new key, so the ear does not hear one bass held across a change.
- Bars 185–196 are also protected (trio layering).
