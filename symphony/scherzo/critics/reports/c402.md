## Findings

None of the 28 principles is broken in a way that a change would improve. I looked for the largest improvement this reading could support, not only for faults. That search leads to the trio, where the °7 and ø7 colours the reading recommends for major (Ex. 22-10, 22-14, 23-9) are absent. I tested that idea against the score, below, and decided against it. The ranked points below are the ones I considered; none is applied.

### Considered, no change (ranked)

1. **vii°7 over the tonic pedal in the trio codetta, 233–236** (A&S PDF 379, Ex. 22-10 I–IV–°7–I over a pedal; PDF 377, Ex. 22-7a, the 7th as a common tone after IV; PDF 393, Ex. 23-9, °7 in major through ♭6). This is the strongest use of the reading available. The Bsn 1 relay G♯3–A3–B3–C4–B3 could keep iv at 235 and add D♯–F♯–A–C over the E pedal on a new beat before 236. The A and C would be held as common tones, and the ♭6 would still reach 5. I decided against it, for a reason stronger than "protected" (c170, c312). The codetta already teaches Ex. 23-8. Its three relays repeat one phrase and change only the mode of the middle chord (IV at 227, IV at 231, iv at 235). That iv is the one the plagal close takes up at 359 (E – A♯°7 – iv – E under the motto tail). A D♯°7 at 235–236 would put E minor's own leading-tone chord, the sound of the scherzo's V♭9 at 126, 282 and 337, into the trio's last bars. It would also give the trio its only °7 just before the close uses a different one (the common-tone A♯°7 at 358). Ex. 22-10 offers the °7 as a substitute for V7. This codetta deliberately has no V at all, as a plagal preview, so the substitution has nothing to replace. Keep.

2. **viiø7 as the neighbour chord under the codetta's 5–6–5, 226–228 and 230–232** (A&S PDF 381–382, Ex. 22-14). Ob 1 and Cl 1 sing B–C♯–B over E–A–E. Unit 22 §14 names viiø7 (D♯–F♯–A–C♯) as the typical neighbour chord under 5–6–5 in major. IV is the other diatonic harmonisation. Here IV is needed: it is the major form that the iv at 235 changes (Ex. 23-8), and a viiø7 would turn the plagal codetta into a dominant one. Keep.

3. **44→45: Vln II's 7th C4 rises to D5 while the violas go F♯3 → G4, a tritone to a P5 in similar motion** (A&S PDF 375, Ex. 22-3c; the 7th "resolves by stepwise descent"). The C4 is the only 7th of the D7 on beats 2–3 of 44. Cl 2's B3 at 45, in the same register, takes its resolution, which is Ex. 22-5's transferred resolution (PDF 376). Both string parts leap more than an octave into a new f espress. phrase, so no line connects across the bar line and the hidden 5ths cannot be heard as a voice-leading event. Ex. 22-3c concerns a stepwise resolution inside one texture. Keep.

4. **331: F♮ (Cl 1, Hn 3, Vln I, Vc) on beats 1–2 against F♯ (Cl 2, Hn 4, Vla) on beat 3** (A&S PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a). The chromatic step passes between voices, but only inner ones. The top voice moves F6→E6 (♭2→1) and the bass C→B by step, so the outer voices carry no cross relation. Moving the Neapolitan into a French sixth on the same bass is the normal succession (F at 331 is protected, c400). Keep.

5. **102→103 and 302→303: Bsn 1 A3→B3 against Cl 2 D♯4→F♯4** (A&S PDF 375). Both parts arpeggiate one V7 that is held through four bars of hemiola, so nothing resolves at that point. This is settled by c312 and c097. Keep.

6. **87: °4/2 (D♯°7 over A) going to A♯°7 rather than straight to a 6/4** (A&S PDF 378, Ex. 22-8). The bass rises A–A♯–B, and the cadential 6/4 arrives at 89 (Bsn 1 G3 held, c162). This is Ex. 22-8b's passing approach to a cadential 6/4, stretched over two bars. Hn 3's C4–C♯4–B3 at 87–89 is E minor's diatonic 6 turning into the 3rd of vii°7 of V. It is not the ♭6–♮6–5 that PDF 388 warns against in major (c270). Keep.
