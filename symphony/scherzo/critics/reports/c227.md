### 1. 15–16 and 31–32: theme A's half cadence ends in unison with the off-beat accompaniment (applied)

**Problem.** In the third and fourth bars of theme A the tune falls B4–G4 | F♯4–D♯4–B3 (Vln I, bowed staccato, p leggiero). Vln II's off-beats are bowed staccato too, `r4 g'4 g'4 | r4 b4 b4`. On beat 3 of 15 (and 31) the tune's G4 sounds with Vln II's G4, and on beat 3 of 16 (and 32) the tune's last note B3 sounds with Vln II's B3. In bar 16 the accompaniment also takes the tune's own crotchet pulse on beats 2 and 3. So the last two notes of the antecedent, the half cadence, are played at the same pitch, in the same colour and with the same stroke by the accompaniment, and they do not stand out from it. c007 had moved these off-beats under the dip (A3 at 16); c073 rightly removed the seventh (V, not V7, at a half cadence) and chose B3, which made the unison.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible. It is true that rhythmic contrast lessens the importance of this consideration." Here there is no rhythmic contrast either: PDF 259–260 (Ex. XVII-3, Tchaikovsky) asks for accompaniment that "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part". PDF 257 (Ex. XVII-1): Beethoven's answering strings play pizzicato "to avoid duplication of the solo sound", which is why the reprise's pizzicato off-beats at 255–256 are a different case and stay. The score already has the solution at 271–272, where Vln II plays `r4 g'4 r4` and then rests under the same descent.

**Fix (sA.ily, `sAVlnTwo`).** Bars 15 and 31: `r4 g'4-. g'4-.` becomes `r4 g'4-. r4`. Bars 16 and 32: `r4 b4-. b4-.` becomes `R2.`. The violas' F♯3 off-beats stay, so V (B–D♯–F♯) is complete with the tune on every beat of 16 and 32, there is no seventh (c073 kept) and nothing doubles the tune's B3. Vln II re-enters at 17 and 33 as before. The half cadence is now heard with the accompaniment thinned under it, as at 271–272.

### 2. 241–243: the violas' head rises into Hn 1's held G4 (applied)

**Problem.** The retransition starts with Hn 1 turning the trio's G♯4 to G4 (c170) and holding it through 241–243, while the violas play theme A's head pp: `e'4 b8 b e'4 | g'2-> fis'8 f' | e'2.`. At 242 the head's accented minim G4 is in unison with the held horn G4, and its tail F♯4–F4 rubs a semitone and a tone under it. The horn and the violas blend in this register, so the head's peak is not heard as a new note; it sounds as a swell in the horn. The first retransition (89–91) has the head alone at the same point (bar 90).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274 (registral distance, above). PDF 273: instruments other than the piano "have a strong tendency to blend when used in certain registers and with acoustically sympathetic orchestral combinations"; horn and viola at G4 are such a combination. PDF 268–269 (Ex. XVII-11, -12): the line comes through because "the background does not intrude at all"; PDF 267: the orchestra is reduced while the solo line plays.

**Fix (retr.ily, `retrHnOne`).** `g'2.~\pp | g'2.~ | g'2.( | fis'2.~ | fis'4) r4 r4` becomes `g'2.\pp | R2. | g'2.( | fis'2.~ | fis'4) r4 r4`. Hn 1 still turns G♯4 (240) to G4 at 241 with the E-minor chord, so the inflection c170 protects is unchanged in the same voice and register; it rests at 242, where the violas' head is alone as at 90; it takes G4 again at 243 as the seventh of iv7 over the bass A (c013, c062) and falls to F♯4 at 244 as before. The seventh is prepared in register by the violas' G4 one bar earlier. No new parallels; no G sounds at 244 (c053).
