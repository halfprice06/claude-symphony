## Findings

**1. 209–216: the horn pair under the trio's forte return is marked two levels under its own choir (applied).**
- Problem. At 209 the strings play T at f (peaks ff) and Cl 1 doubles it mf. The four-part horn–bassoon choir moves under them (c014, c047, c088): Bsn 1 mf, Bsn 2 mf with the cellos' mf countermelody, and the violas mf. Hn 1·2 hold thirds and sixths in that choir, but are marked p.
- The score's own rule, from c047 at 197, is horns one degree under the woodwinds they join. At 209 they are two degrees under the bassoons and three under the strings.
- Evidence. Adler PDF 292: the brass have great dynamic power, so balance with woodwinds and strings must be kept in mind. PDF 311–312: in the middle register the horns make a velvet accompaniment choir. Marked p inside an mf choir under an f tune, the horns hold back and do not give the climax that weight. Dvořák 9/iii marks its trio horns at the orchestra's level.
- Dan's rule asks for weight, not bite. A held, tenuto, slurred mp gives weight with no attack.
- Why not mf. Hn 1's C♯5 at 213–216 lies in the octave of T's lower line (Vln II, Cl 1), and Adler says the horn carries "over almost any combination" (PDF 311). The horns must stay a level under the woodwind that doubles the tune, so the step is mp.
- Fix (tC.ily): `tCHnOne = { gis'2--(\mp cis''4-- ...` and `tCHnTwo = { e'2.--(\mp | ...`. Both players are on one shared staff, so they get equal dynamics. The diminuendo at 216 and the hidden pp< at 217 are unchanged. The third phrase still peaks at mf at 219, above the new mp.
- Applied in /tmp/critic-406/work. It undoes no earlier fix. The horn pair's pitches, slurs and breath (c004, c087, c094) are untouched.

**2. 72–74: Hn 1's slur from written G♯5 ff to G5 pp, at the fade into the F-major episode (no change).**
- Adler PDF 284 warns about soft, controlled playing in the very high register. PDF 290 (Ex. IX-9) makes the sff>pp decay on one held note.
- At 72–73 the decay happens on C♯5 under the slur. The C♮5 (written G5) arrives at the end of a diminuendo, not as a fresh soft attack. Written G5 is the bottom of Adler's "brilliant" band (X-5), not the extreme top.
- The one-voice chromatic step is c042's harmonic reason, and c136/c266 kept it. The reading gives no stronger reason. Kept.

**3. Brass endurance and breathing (no change).**
- Longest spans: Hn 2 101–132 (about 22 s) and Hn 4 101–128.
- Both have crotchet rests in 105–120, slur breaks, and breath marks at 104 and 116. Every loud slur is at most five bars.
- Hn 1 always has a rest after a high tutti: 121–140, 321–331 and 340–356 (Adler PDF 292, 303).
- The tuba plays 25 bars in all (PDF 8).

**4. Rejected candidates for an audible brass change.**
- Stopped horn on the G♯→G turn at 241 (PDF 307–308, Ex. X-19). This changes the colour of the inflection on which the G♯ = A♭ frame turns, and it is a sf>p effect, against the soft-brass rule (c316).
- Hn 1 doubling T at 209 (PDF 311, horn as doubler). The trio horn pair and the coda hand-over of T to the horn are protected (c087, c251). A horn on T here would take away the horn's first statement of T at 341.
- A soft trombone–tuba chord (PDF 292, Ex. X-80 Brahms 1/iv, trombones p dolce). This is the brass chorale kept for the finale.
- Bass clef for Hn 4's part at 217–224 and 305–320 (PDF 304). It is at most three ledger lines (written E3) and readable; this is a parts-engraving matter, not one a listener hears.
