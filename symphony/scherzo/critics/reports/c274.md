## Findings

### 1. 281–287: the tenor octave of the second idea is horn and cellos with no woodwind, while Cl 2 only doubles the violas (applied)

**Problem.** In the reprise's last statement of the second idea, c071 put the tenor octave in Hn 2 and the cellos (C4–A3–F♯4–E4–D♯4–B3–C♯4 | B3–G3–E3–F♯3–D♯3–F♯3–E3). No woodwind is on that line. Meanwhile Cl 2 holds E4–C4–B3–G3–E4–D♯4–B3. That is the violas' line note for note except at 284, and its G3 there is also in both bassoons. With Cl 1 above it, Cl 2 also makes the pair's only open intervals in the passage: A4/E4 (fifth) at 281, F♯4/B3 (fourth) at 283–284, and A4/D♯4 (tritone) at 286, the same tritone as at 130.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 52: "The strings do not blend so well with the brass, and when the two groups are placed side by side, each is heard too distinctly. The combination of the three different timbres in unison produces a rich, mellow and coherent tone." PDF 51–52: woodwind timbre "is closely allied to the strings on the one hand, and to the brass on the other"; reinforcing both, it "thickens the strings and softens the brass." PDF 90, rule 4: concords, not fifths, fourths or sevenths, go to instruments of the same kind. PDF 97, remark 4: when strings have the top part, the wind's middle parts should use thirds, sixths, seconds or sevenths, "not open fourths and fifths". Adler's own Examples XI-12 and XI-13 (adler2 PDF 28) have bassoons inside the brass–low-string doublings. At 197–200 this score already does what Rimsky describes: the tune is in Vln II, violas, Cl 1 and Hn 1 together. Only 281–287 leaves the horn and cellos without a wind.

**Fix (applied).** Cl 2 moves from the violas' notes onto the tenor line in unison with Hn 2 and the cellos. Its dynamics and slurs match the shared clarinet staff (Cl 1's sub. p, < at 281, f at 285). At 288 it ends on B3, the fifth of the closing chord. sections/dA.ily, dAClTwo, bars 281–288:

Nothing in the harmony is lost. The violas keep E4–C4–B3–B3–E4–D♯4–B3, and the bassoons keep G3 at 284. Bsn 1 still has the only D♯ of the V♭9 at 282 (c082), and that bar is untouched. Against Cl 1 the pair now moves mostly in thirds and sixths: A4/F♯4, G4/E4, F♯4/D♯4, G4/B3, A4/F♯3. The lowest note is D♯3, well inside the A clarinet's range (lowest C♯3) and in its full chalumeau at the f hemiola. This does not touch c161's protection of the tenor statement 281–287: the notes of Hn 2 and the cellos are unchanged, and the line gets a third colour. It goes further in c071's direction and undoes nothing.

### Considered and left (my decisions)

- 209–224: the harp's left hand plucks the bass at mf under arco basses, cellos and contrabassoon. PDF 52 says plucked and bowed sounds are heard separately, and PDF 53 says to use plucked strings at intervals. Here, though, the contrabassoon is on the same notes, and PDF 52 says winds "strengthen and clarify" the harp. c033 wanted the plucked downbeat under the sustained bass. No change.
- 129–131 (and 285–287): Bsn 1 B3 → E3 puts the bass in three octaves at the f hemiola cadence (PDF 82 note: doubling the bass makes it heavy). This is a dominant pedal at a cadence, and the hemiola cadences are protected (c002 onward). No change.
- 45–56 and 121–128: the clarinet pair often moves in fourths and fifths. It moves in the melody's rhythm (homorhythmic second idea, c027) and sits inside the horns (c014). PDF 91 b) allows this in moving parts against held ones. No change.
- 123/279: the wind middle at V is an open B–F♯. The third, D♯, is in the melody (D♯6/D♯5), and adding it to Hn 4 would triple the leading tone. PDF 97 remark 3 accepts a short loss of balance for the sake of correct progression. No change.
- 101–104/301–304: Fl 2 and Ob 1 double the held F♯5 while every other chord note is single. This is rule 1's stated exception (one part made prominent: the top of the hemiola chord). No change.
