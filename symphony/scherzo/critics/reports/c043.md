## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The movement's last authentic cadence is a copy of the trio tune's internal cadence (bars 353–356). Applied.

**Problem.** Coda stage 2 holds back a root-position E-major tonic for 14 bars:
- 341–348 are in A major (IV).
- 349–352 run vi – I6 – ii7 – ii6.

The goal of the whole movement is therefore the I at 355. Yet that cadence is one bar of cadential 6/4 (353), one bar of V7 (354), and I (355). That is the same shape and length as the trio's own phrase endings at 165–167, 181–183 and 221–223, so the movement closes with the tune's ordinary half-time cadence.

The printed slowing comes only at 370–376, where Hn 1 and Vln I hold a single G♯ and nothing moves.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- The accented 6/4 exists "to delay the arrival of an expected melodic or harmonic event" (PDF 307).
- "The tension created by the cadential 6/4 can be enhanced by expanding it" (PDF 317, Ex. 19-21). Brahms does this at the very end of the Requiem, where a double neighbour prolongs the 6/4's bass (PDF 318, Ex. 19-22).
- Over a held bass, the 6/4's upper voice can be elaborated 6-5-4-3 (PDF 314–315, Ex. 19-15, 19-16).

**Fix (no bar added, no renumbering).** The tonic now arrives at 356 and runs straight into the motto tail at 357, which starts on E.

- Vln I and Fl 1, 353–356: `gis''4\!( b'' e''' | gis'''2 fis'''8 e''' | dis'''2 fis'''4) | e'''2 r4 |`
  - Was: `gis''4\!( b'' e''') | dis'''2( fis'''4) | e'''2.~ | e'''2 r4 |`.
  - The 6/4 now holds for two bars. The upper voice falls G♯–F♯–E (6-5-4) to D♯ (3) at V, in theme A's minim-and-two-quavers rhythm.
- Vln II, the same an octave lower: `gis'4\!( b'4 e''4 | gis''2 fis''8 e''8 | dis''2 fis''4) | e''2 r4 |`
- Violas, 353–356: `e'2. | <gis e'>2. | <a fis'>2. | <gis e'>2.`
  - Was `e' | <a fis'> | <gis e'> | <gis e'>`.
  - The inner E rises to F♯ while Vln I's E falls to D♯ (PDF 317).
- Vc, 353–358: `b,4 r4 r4 | R2. | b,4 r4 r4 | e4 r4 r4 | R2. | e2.\p^\markup \italic "arco" | e2. | e2. | e2.~\pp`
- Cb: the same, an octave down.
  - Bsn 2 already holds E at 357, so the strings re-enter arco at 358. Pizz. to arco keeps four beats, as before.
- I added a c043 comment in coda.ily.

### 2. The scherzo's close stops dead before the link restarts on the same chord (bars 139–141). Applied.

**Problem.**
- 137–138: the cellos' motive x ends on V.
- 139: E pp in Vc, Cb and timpani.
- 140: a completely empty bar.
- 141: Hn 1, Bsn 1 and bass clarinet start again on the same E-minor chord.

Cycle 23 removed exactly this stop-and-restart at the reprise close (321–324). It survives at the first close.

**Evidence.** The reading treats a held or stepwise bass as what carries continuity over a long span (PDF 256–257). The link is a 12-bar E pedal: its pedal should begin where its tonic arrives, not after a silent bar. Dvořák's transition (harmony.csv, his bars 182–235) never stops on the tonic before turning to the trio's key.

**Fix (no length change).**
- In sC.ily, new: `sCBassCl = { R2.*34 | e,2.~\pp | e,2.~ | }`.
- In link.ily, `linkBassCl` begins `e,2. | e,2. |` (the `\pp` moves to 139).

The bass clarinet takes the tonic E with the cellos at 139 and ties it through 140 into the link's pedal. The tie across the section join engraves correctly (page 6).

### 3. The G.P. at 340 lasts one ordinary bar in the MIDI, and the final fermata is not realised. Applied.

**Problem.** In global.ily, `s2.\fermata` at 340 has no `\mt` step. The general pause between the two coda stages plays as 0.6 s of silence, and the final bar has the rit.'s last tempo. The loop judges the movement's length and the coda's hinge from this MIDI, which therefore under-measures both.

**Fix.**
- `s2.*15 \mt 96 s2.\fermata` at 340, so the G.P. lasts about two bars. The `\tempo` at 341 restores 2. = 76.
- `\mt 140 s2.*2 \mt 100 s2.-\tweak staff-padding #2 \fermata` at 376.

No printed change.
