## Findings

### 1. The horn call loses movement I's rising sixth (bars 1–7). Applied.

**Problem.** Movement I's horn call is E♭3 → C4 → B3, a rising sixth from 3̂ to 1̂ followed by the fall to 7̂. The record makes the sixth the idea's defining interval. themes-mvt1.ly (mvtOneHornCall) says: "invariant: scale degrees 3 – 1 (rising sixth) – 7, mode free". The finale keeps the sixth (E4 → C5 in the trumpets at 243–246). v10 has Hn 1 G♯4 → E4: a falling third, then a neighbour figure around E. Heard alone, G♯–E–D♯–E is a turn, not the call.

**Evidence from the reading.** A&S PDF 600–602 (Mozart K. 542) shows that cyclic and large-scale reference works only when the motive's identity survives: B–A♯–A–G♯ is recognisable because its interval pattern is kept. The scherzo itself shows this at 365–368. The horn call here keeps the scale degrees and loses the interval.

**Fix.** Keep the pivot G♯4 (the A♭4 of movement II, at the same pitch) in bars 1–2. Drop Hn 1 an octave at bar 3 and play the call in movement I's register: G♯3 → E4 (a rising minor sixth, the major-mode form, as in the finale) → D♯4 → E4. Hn 2 enters at 4 instead of 3, so it does not cross under Hn 1's G♯3 on the shared staff.

```
introHnOne = { gis'2.~\pp | gis'2. | gis2.\< | e'2.\p\> | dis'2. | e'2.~\! | e'2 r4 | R2.*5 | }
introHnTwo = { R2.*3 | b2.~\p\> | b2.~ | b2.~\! | b2 r4 | R2.*5 | }
```

**Protected status.** HISTORY protects "Bars 1–7: Hn 1 G♯4 then G♯–E–D♯–E. Reason: the pivot from movement II and into the finale." The fix keeps the pivot: G♯4 is still the first sound, alone, pp, for two bars, in the same player. Nothing the finale relies on changes.

### 2. A major → F major (72–73) has no common tone. Applied.

**Problem.** At 72 the whole orchestra stops on A major ff; at 73 the harp starts F major pp. The source comment calls this "the symphony's key relation", a major triad falling a major third. That is exactly A&S's best case for a common-tone modulation (PDF 587), and the symphony's own joins use one (A♭ = G♯ into this movement, G♯ = A♭ into the finale). In v10 the A is in the inner parts only at 72 (Hn 2 A4, Vla A4, Bsn A3), and nothing holds it into 73. The two keys simply sit side by side.

**Evidence from the reading.** *Widmung* (PDF 586): "an important common element helps to integrate the two tonal regions: the melody tone A♭/G♯, sustained". Schubert 9/iv (PDF 587): the V chord thins down to its common tone before the remote key.

**Fix.** Hn 2 holds its A4 out of the ff chord, dying away into the F-major bars, where it becomes the third of F under the harp's opening A5. The A falls out of the tutti and is picked up at the top by the harp. Hn 1 gets the same hairpin at 72, so the shared staff carries equal dynamics. The solo directive makes 73 print "2.".

```
sBHnOne = { R2.*14 | cis''2.\p\< | cis''2.\ff\> | \partCombineSoloII R2.\! | R2. | \partCombineAutomatic R2.*26 | fis'2.\p\< | ... }
sBHnTwo = { R2.*14 | a'2.\p\< | a'2.\ff\> | \partCombineSoloII a'2.~\pp | a'2 r4 | \partCombineAutomatic R2.*26 | dis'2.\p\< | ... }
```

Hn 3·4 still make their first entry at 73 with "senza sord."; Hn 2's A4 sits above their C4/F3.

### 3. The timpani label does not give the retuning, and the soft ending is played with hard sticks. Applied.

**Problem 1: the label.** BRIEF requires that "the timpani's first label gives the retuning made in the pause". v10 prints only "Timpani: E, G, B, E (retuned during the pause)". Movement II's part text names the moves ("muta A♭ in E, C in G, D♭ in B, E♭ in E"), and the finale's bar-1 label names its own moves, so the scherzo is the only link that leaves them out. New label:

```
"Timpani: E, G, B, E" "(retune in the pause: A♭ → E, C → G, D♭ → B, E♭ → E)"
```

**Problem 2: the sticks.** "hard sticks" from 246 is never cancelled. The coda's soft motive x (pp, 371 and 375) and the ppp E roll at 380–382 under the last G♯ are therefore played with hard sticks. Add at 349 (16 bars' rest):

```
codaTimp = { ... | e,4-> r4 r4 | R2. | R2.^\markup \small \italic "soft sticks" | R2.*15 | ... }
```

**Tunings.** Unchanged. They still end E, G, B, E, as the finale's label needs.

### 4. The finale's link text names a cello note the scherzo does not play. No score change; the finale thread must be told.

**Problem.** finale.md and the v30 intro.ily comment say "the scherzo's other chord tones move by step: E → E♭ (Vc E3 → E♭3) and B → C". In v10 (and v11 as built here):
- The cellos' arco E3 ends at 372. Their last note is a pizzicato E2 at 383.
- The only B near the end is the harp's B3 in the arpeggio at 383. Vln II's B3 ends at 372.

**Why it is the finale's text that should change.** The pitch link itself is correct: Hn 1 G♯4 / Vln I G♯5 → A♭4 / A♭5 in the same players. The timpani tunings match, and neither movement uses a mute. A&S's semitone moves (PDF 586: C–B♮ standing for C–C♭) are written into the finale's chord, but the scherzo's low E sounds an octave below what the finale describes. I keep the pizzicato E2: with the basses' E1 it is the resonant bottom of the last chord.

### 5. The ends of movements II and III are the same close a major third apart; the finale should take the third step. No change to the scherzo.

**What matches.**
- Movement II's coda: Cl 1 E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5 (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂ in A♭) over the A♭ pedal, IV6 – iv – I (85–89).
- The scherzo's coda: Hn 1 B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂ in E) over E – A♯°7 – A minor – E (365–368).

This is the same plagal close with mixture, transposed down a major third. It projects the symphony's equal division C – A♭ – E – C onto its cadences, as the "Appassionata" development projects A♭ → G♯ → E → C (A&S PDF 599–600). It also uses Mozart's K. 542 motive at the same pitch and in the same key (PDF 602).

**Recommendation to the finale.** Its last plagal close (v30, 273–276, inner line A–A♭–G) would complete the chain if the top voice took G–F♯–F–E over C – F♯°7 – F minor – C. Those are the scherzo's own theme-A tail pitches, G–F♯–F–E, which in C are 5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂. This would also make the finale's quotation of theme A's head at 275–276 sound at its original pitch classes. In the scherzo, 365–368 stays as it is.
