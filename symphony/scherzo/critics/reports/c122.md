## Findings

### 1. F-major episode, 76→77 and 82→83: octaves at the seams of the two sequences (applied)

**Problem.** The harp tune 73–78 is a three-statement sequence falling by step: A (73–74), G (75–76), F (77–78). Each statement ends with a two-quaver tail. At the seam between the 2nd and 3rd statements, the tail's passing G5 (76, last quaver) meets Hn 4's held G3. Both then move to F at 77: G5/G3 → F5/F3. The clarinet's continuation 81–86 is a second sequence: D (81–82), B♭ (83–84), A (85–86). At its first seam, the tail's D5 (82, last quaver, a chordal skip) meets Hn 3's held D4. Both then move to B♭ at 83: D5/D4 → B♭4/B♭3. Both are real octaves between independent voices: a horn that has held its note for a bar or more, and the solo line. They are exposed, because this is the movement's only string-free passage (pp horns under a p harp or clarinet solo). They fall at the very places where the sequence repeats.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 266: "with any repetitive patterns where all the voices participate, 5ths and octaves can become a problem. Therefore, voice-leading chords play an essential role". The tail figure recurs at every statement, so the danger recurs too. PDF 341: "unisons and octaves caused by figuration are more problematic than 5ths … you should avoid them". PDF 343: "No passing or neighboring tone can break up octaves". PDF 83: octaves are forbidden between the same pair of voices. My scan found no other such octaves in 1–376 apart from the motive-x doublings at 71/299, which are by design.

**Fix (applied, sections/sB.ily).** The inner voices move earlier, so that the solo arrives on a held note.
```
sBHnFour  76:     g2-- f4--)                 (was g2.--) )   8–7 passing motion into F at 77 (A&S PDF 118)
sBHnThree 82–83:  d'2-- bes4--~ | bes2.      (was d'2.-- | bes2.--)
sBBsnOne  82–83:  bes2 d'4~ | d'2.           (was bes2. | d'2.)
```
At 82, Hn 3 and Bsn 1 make their existing D/B♭ voice exchange on beat 3 instead of at the bar line. The chords at 83 are the same as before. At 76, beat 3 becomes ii7 on its way to I6, and the F is then held as the root of F/A. The chord in every bar is unchanged, and so are the key, the harp head, the wind colour (c047, c104) and the four real parts. That answers c080's protection of the episode, which concerns the key, the harmony and the link to the finale's IV. The scan of the scratch build reports nothing at 76–77 or 82–83.

### 2. Second idea, 52: the sequence's last chord doubles its applied leading tone just before the cadence (applied)

**Problem.** In c032's sequence, phrase 2 ends on A7/C♯ (V6/5 of V) at 52, beats 2–3, and leads straight into the cadential 6/4 in G at 53. The bass C♯, the leading tone of D, is doubled by Cl 1's C♯5, which then falls to B4. c094 removed the doubled bass of V4/3 at 51 for this reason but left 52.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267 and the summary on PDF 286 (point 2a): a doubled leading tone is acceptable in a sequence "as long as it does not appear before a goal tonic". Bar 52 is the last chord of the pattern and the one just before the goal. PDF 76: never double the leading tone of V or its inversions. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 83: never double the bass of an inverted dominant chord. The same reading closes c052's open point. The leading tones doubled in the outer voices at 49–50 (C♯) and 51–52 (F♯) lie inside the pattern, and neither is followed by its tonic, so PDF 267 allows them, and the c032 sequence needs no rewrite.

**Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily).**
```
sAClOne 51–52:  cis''2( d''4~ | d''4 e''2)      (was cis''2( d''4~ | d''4 cis''2))
```
The chord becomes C♯ (bass only), E, G and A, complete, with the 5th doubled. Cl 1 rises C♯–D–E and falls a 4th to B4 at 53, where it meets Hn 1 by contrary motion. There are no new 5ths or octaves: the scan is clean for 45–56. The melody, the bass, the hemiola and the record are unchanged.
