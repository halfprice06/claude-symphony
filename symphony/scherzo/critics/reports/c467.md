## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 349–356: the whole second-violin section doubles T an octave under one solo violin (applied)

**Problem.** In T's last coda statement the top octave (C♯6–A6) is played by one solo violin with Fl 1, both pp. The lower octave (C♯5–A5) is played by the entire Vln II section, pp, sul A. So the subordinate line has about twelve players and the lead has one string player and one flute. The ear takes the tune from the octave with the weight, and the top octave, which is the statement's point (the solo against the section), becomes a thin overtone. The three earlier cycles that worked here moved the same way without reaching a balance: c439 found the solo "too weak against the octave below" and added Fl 1; c457 took the other first violins off the lower octave (about 26 violins against one). The lower octave still outweighs the lead.

**Evidence.** PDF 138, rule 2: "the lead violin line should be carried by more players than any other line in a concerted string chord"; even 8–4 he calls "unbalanced". PDF 164: "one woodwind equals approximately sixteen violins", so one flute plus the solo balances a half section, not a full one. PDF 162–163 (Ex. 212, images checked): the solo players are set against the large section as a contrast, not doubled by it. PDF 139, rule 3: no fewer than three violins on a sustained line, so a half section (about six) is still a proper section line, not a pair in unison (c438's objection).

**Answer to the recorded reasons.** c438's chain of single solo voices (Hn 3, Ob 1, solo violin, Hn 1) is kept and made clearer. c439's Fl 1 on the top octave is kept. c457's do-not-touch ("Vln II alone carries the lower octave") is kept: Vln II still carries it alone, with fewer players. c466's "sul A" is kept. c445's rejection of "la metà" concerned 169–184, where the muted Vln II figure is the only harmony; here the violas (div.) and cellos hold the harmony, and the halved line doubles the tune.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaVlnTwo).** 349: `cis''2(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic \column { "dolce" "la metà" } -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan dis''4 | …`; 357: `gis'2.\pp^\markup \italic "tutti" | g'2. | e'2. | e'2. |`. Notes, slurs and dynamics unchanged. The inside players rest 8 bars and return at 357 on the inner line under Hn 1's motto tail. No cue is needed (the part keeps playing). MIDI is unchanged; the change is in the players' count, which the render cannot show.

### Considered and not changed

- **349–356, Fl 1 at pp beside the solo violin (PDF 164: a wind on a violin line is marked a level softer).** Sebesky's rule is for a flute on a section line. Here it is one flute with one violin, the combination Rimsky-Korsakov gives at equal levels (c439, PDF 124, Ex. 229). With the lower octave halved, a quieter flute would make the top octave thin again. Kept.
- **193–204, cellos on the basses' sustained roots (rule 7).** The PDF 139 footnote says the bass cannot sustain alone live; c367 and c267 kept them for the same reason. Kept.
- **289–296, reprise fugato with two entries only (Ex. 188's four-way conversation).** The shortening is the plan's (the reprise has been shortened since v01); 293–296 already has subject and countersubject in strings with c337's wind points. Adding a third string voice would anticipate the stretto entries at 297–299. Kept.
- **105–120 and 305–320, both violin sections on the theme at the unison (rule 6).** In a large mixed tutti PDF 168 asks for the strings voiced simply and high; the lower octave is in C.a. and Cl 2 (Ex. 213's principle). Kept.
- **329–332, homorhythmic motive x in all strings before 333 (Ex. 215).** The voices move upward together and the high reeds enter last at 333, as Ex. 215 does. Kept.
