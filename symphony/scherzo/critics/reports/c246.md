## Findings

### 1. Bars 101–116 and 301–316: Bsn 1 plays 16 bars of f–ff, the last twelve slurred, with no breath; Hn 3·4 in the same lines breathe at 104. Applied.

**Problem.** At 101–104 (and 301–304) Bsn 1·2 play the hemiola build B–A–B–A over B (f/p < ff), then at 105 (305) Bsn 1 takes the tenor countermelody of the tutti theme, slurred in a four-bar and a seven-bar phrase up to the breath mark at 116 (c086). Bsn 2 sustains the bass to its rest at 112 (c066). There is no breath between the ff a2. of 104 and the slur that starts at 105, so Bsn 1 plays 101–116 at f–ff with no planned breath: 10.9 s by the score's tempo map. Bsn 2 plays 101–112 in the low register, 8.0 s.

Hn 3·4 play the same build (b2.--( ... b2.--\ff) and the same tenor countermelody from 105 (305), and they already have `\breathe` at the end of 104 and 304. On page 4 and page 10 the comma stands on the Hn 3·4 staff and not on the bassoon staff two systems above, though the two groups play the same line.

**Evidence.** A slurred phrase is played on one breath (adler1 PDF 176), so the player needs a full breath before the slur at 105. The low register of the bassoon family takes "considerable" breath and needs periodic rests (adler1 PDF 230), and loud playing takes more breath than soft (adler1 PDF 292, written for brass but stated as a general rule). The players can take this breath by clipping 104 anyway, because 101–104 are separate tongued notes. The mark makes the clip the same length as the horns' and puts it in the same place.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-246/work).** sections/sB.ily and sections/dB.ily, last bar of `sBBsnOne`, `sBBsnTwo`, `dBBsnOne`, `dBBsnTwo`:

```
sBBsnOne: ... | b2.\f\< | a2. | b2. | a2.\ff \breathe | }
sBBsnTwo: ... | b,2.\f\< | b,2. | b,2. | b,2.\ff \breathe | }
dBBsnOne: ... | b2.\p\< | a2. | b2. | a2.\ff \breathe | }
dBBsnTwo: ... | b,2.\p\< | b,2. | b,2. | b,2.\ff \breathe | }
```

with a `% c246:` comment under each pair. The shared bassoon staff prints one comma at 104 and at 304, level with the horns' (checked at 130 dpi on pages 4 and 10). Print only: score.midi is byte-identical to v246's.

### Points checked and left unchanged

- **Hn 1's last G♯ 368–376** [written D♯5], pp→ppp, about 11.7 s with the rit. and the fermata. A held pp note in the horn's middle register takes little air; Hn 1 breathes at 366–367 while Hn 3 holds the G♯ (365–369, c130); Vln I sounds the same G♯ from 369, so the sound does not stop if the player runs short under the fermata. This is the protected pivot into the finale. No change.
- **Hn 1 C♯5 [G♯5]** at 72, 213–215, 220, 300 and **B6 at 336** in Fl 1 and Vln I: single peaks approached by step, the use footnote * on PDF 302 describes. No change (as c156).
- **Fl 1 45–56** (7.6 s, mf–f, two four-bar slurs and a three-bar slur after the rest at 44) and **Cbsn 330–339** (5.8 s, p to fff, unslurred): inside one breath for professional players. No change.
- **Harp pedal changes at 188–190** come one per bar and fall on beats where that string is silent; the A and B strings last sounded two to four bars earlier at pp. No change.
- **Terms (PDF 312).** Adler separates "via sordini" (take the mutes off) from "senza sordino" (without mute). Hn 3·4 have 72 bars of rest, and the pause, to take off movement II's mutes, so the state word "senza sord." at their entry is the right one (cyclic rule). No change.
