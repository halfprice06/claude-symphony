## Findings

### 1. Bars 53-54: two empty octaves above the bass in the forte cadential 6/4 (applied)

- **Bars:** 53-56 (sA.ily, `sABsnOne`).
- **Problem:** At the second idea's hemiola cadence in G, Bsn 1, the cellos, the contrabassoon and the basses all hold the same D2. The basses cannot go to D1, so they come up to D2, and the cellos and Bsn 1 leap a ninth down from C#3 to join them. The next sounding note above is Hn 2's D4. On the downbeats of 53 and 54 the whole third octave is empty, a gap of 24 semitones at f, under the full woodwind, horns and violins. At 55 the gap is still 19 semitones (G2 to D4). The same cadence later in the movement is spaced correctly: at 129-131 and 285-287 Bsn 1 holds B3 and then E3 an octave above the bass.
- **Evidence:** All of the appendix's forte chords (PDF 505-507) double the bass at the octave and fill the octave above it; no chord leaves the first space over the bass empty. Ch. IV, PDF 125: "The upper and lower parts of a passage can seldom be widely separated without the intermediate octaves being filled in, for this is contrary to the first principles of proper distribution of chords." The Sadko tutti in the same one-in-a-bar 3/4 (Exs. 205-206, PDF 368-369) keeps bassoons, horns and trombones in the octave above the basses. Four instruments on one D2 also add no octave and no colour.
- **Fix (applied):** `sABsnOne` 53-56 `d2.~ | d2. | g2. | g4-. r4 r4` (was `d,2.~ | d,2. | g,2. | g,4-.`). Bsn 1 now steps C#3 to D3 instead of leaping a ninth down. It holds the octave above the bass as at 129-131, rises a fourth to G3 for the cadence and ends with Hn 2 on G3. Downbeats are now D2-D3-D4 at 53-54 and G1-G2-G3-D4 at 55. The rhythm, harmony, Cl 1's 4-3 (c289), Hn 2's D4 (c282) and the hemiola bowing (c085) are unchanged. The Vc, Cb and Cbn lines are untouched.

### 2. Bars 333-338: the timpani are as loud as the tutti and louder than the brass (applied)

- **Bars:** 333 and 335-336 (coda.ily, `codaTimp`).
- **Problem:** The timpani play motive x ff at 333, level with the ff tutti, and roll fff at 336 with accents through the hemiola at 337-338. At 336-338 the trombones and tuba rest (c034), the trumpets are f and the horns ff (Dan's soft-brass rule, c200). A fff roll on B2 is therefore the loudest sound in the middle and low register. It covers Tpt 2's D#4 and the horns' F#4, the only places the third and fifth of the B-major chord sound in the brass. The movement's other tuttis already use the opposite rule: at 105 and 305 the timpani are f under ff (c056).
- **Evidence:** In the appendix the timpani are at the level of the tutti or one degree under it, never above (chord 1 ff under fff; chords 9 and 20 f under ff; PDF 505-507). In the tutti examples Rimsky-Korsakov marks the timpani one degree under the orchestra each time: Ex. 204 (PDF 367) f under ff, Ex. 206 (PDF 369, the fff restatement of a 3/4 one-in-a-bar tutti) ff under fff, Ex. 207 (PDF 370) f under ff.
- **Fix (applied):** `e,4--\f e,8 e,8 e,4 | R2. | b,4\< b,8 b,8 b,4 | b,2.:32\ff |` (was `\ff` at 333, no hairpin at 335, `\fff` at 336). The timpani's motive x at 333 is now f, as at 105 and 305. They crescendo through their motive x at 335 to a ff roll at 336, one degree under the fff strings and woodwinds and level with the horns. The movement's single fff stays in the strings and woodwinds (c200, c258). The accents at 337-338 stay. No brass dynamic changes.
