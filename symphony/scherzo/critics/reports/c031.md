## Melody overview

Theme A works: motive x, the chromatic tail, the varied bar 21 (three crotchets), and the peak C6 in bar 12 of 16. A listener will remember it, in the way Beethoven 9/ii is remembered by one rhythm. T is a singable 15-bar tune with one rhythmic cell (dotted crotchet and quaver) and a late peak (A5 in bar 11). Its second phrase is a sequence of the first, which is normal for a trio. The harp melody (73–80) and the coda's shared return of T (341–356) are settled and stay. The weak spot is the second idea. Finding 1 addresses it.

## Findings

### 1. The second idea has no rhythm of its own (45–56, 121–132, 277–288). Applied. THEME CHANGE.

**Problem.** After the first bar, the melody is ten bars of minims, in an exact two-bar sequence (D–B–G / F♯–E–D, then E–C♯–A / G–F♯–E). Nothing in it can be hummed apart from the hemiola itself, and the hemiola already belongs to the cadences (53–55, 129–131), the retransition and the coda. Every lyrical string melody Adler prints in this chapter has the same feature (reading note 9): long notes paired with short turning or dotted figures, as in Tchaikovsky 5/ii (PDF 74), Scheherazade iii and Brahms 3/iii (PDF 75), Tristan (PDF 99) and Don Quixote (PDF 102). Dvořák keeps unbroken hemiola minims for a four-bar transition and gives his lyrical tune short notes (corpus lines 22 and 31–32).

**Fix.** Each two-bar answer ends its last minim as a dotted crotchet plus a quaver that leads into the next unit. The hemiola is untouched, because the three minim attacks in each two-bar unit stay where they were. The added figure is T's own cell (dotted crotchet and quaver), which ties the two lyrical ideas of the movement together.
- G major, bar 48: `e'''4 d'''2` → `e'''4 d'''4. e'''8` (anticipates the E6 at 49). Bar 52: `fis'''4 e'''2` → `fis'''4 e'''4. d'''8` (anticipates the D6 at 53).
- E minor, bars 124/280: `cis'''4 b''2` → `cis'''4 b''4. d'''8` (a consonant leap within the G harmony, then a step down to the C6 at 125). Bars 128/284: `dis'''4 b''2` → `dis'''4 b''4. cis'''8` (upper neighbour back to B5 at 129). I used C♯ rather than D♮ at 128 so the line has no D♮ against the D♯ of the V chord that follows. I avoided a C anticipation at 124 because C over the G–E–B there would make a C-major triad.
- The same change goes in every voice that carries the melody: Vln I and Fl 1 at the written octave, Vln II and Ob 1 an octave lower (sA.ily lines 9, 11, 21, 22; sC.ily lines 9, 11, 33, 34; dA.ily lines 7, 9, 32, 33). The harmony parts do not change. The added quavers are E6 over G/B, D6 over A/C♯ (an anticipation), D6 over E minor/G, and C♯6 over E minor/G. No C-major sonority results.

### 2. T's first string statement has no string colour chosen (169–176). Applied.

**Problem.** At 169, Vln I takes T at mp espressivo from G♯5. Unmarked, players cross to the E string at once for B5–E6. Adler (PDF 75, reading note 6) says that in soft, lyrical, expressive passages composers keep the upper notes on the A string rather than crossing to the E string. His example is Brahms 3/iii, sul A, p espress. The E string's brilliance is better saved for the phrase that climbs. The loud return at 209 and the pp coda at 349 should stay on the E string: Adler names the E string strident when loud and ethereal when soft (PDF 75).

**Fix.** In tA.ily `tAVlnOne`, the markup at 169 becomes `^\markup \italic "espressivo, sul A"` (G♯5–E6 lies in the 5th–7th position on the A string, within Brahms's example). Bar 173 gets `a''2(^\markup \italic "nat." b''4`, so the second phrase (to F♯6) and the climb to A6 at 179 move onto the E string. Markup only; no notes change.

### Considered and kept

- Second idea register in G (Vln I D6–A6 at f, high on the E string). Adler (PDF 75) calls this region strident when loud. The passage is forte and doubled by Fl 1 at the unison, so brilliance is the intended effect, and Vln II already carries the lower octave (cycle 9). No change.
- T's sequential second phrase (173–176). This is the cycle 1 open point. Bars 10–11 were changed in v12 and now give the tune a late peak. A literal sequence in the second phrase is normal for a trio tune (Dvořák 9/iii's E-major tune repeats its opening bar a step away). No change.
- The coda's return of T is shared Hn 1 (A major) → Cl 1 → Vln I, with no E-major head. That came from cycle 13 and climbs well in register to the pp A6 at 351. No change.
