## What else I studied

**Schubert 9/iii.** The scherzo in C is in sonata form, 238 bars, and the A-major trio is 166 bars (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._9_(Schubert)). Its three trombones play throughout the symphony, often softly and often with melodic lines. Adler PDF 37 makes the related point from the first movement: Schubert kept a colour back and then gave it a structural moment. This score does the same in its own way. The low brass enter pp under the hemiola at 101, sound in the first tutti, then are silent from 121 to 332, so the coda's return at 333 is new again. The string/wind antiphony of Schubert's opening has its counterpart in 29–36. The movement is much shorter than Schubert's and has no repeats. That fits a scherzo placed between a long Adagio and the finale. It is protected (c083, c113, c163), and I agree with it. On the holistic question, a conductor would accept this score as a master's in its layering, its use of the timpani as a motivic voice, its recolouring of returns and its closing page. The one place that sounds like a student's habit and not like Schubert's restraint is the last loud chord, 339: there the low brass come back from rests at ff.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 339: the low brass come back at ff after three bars' rest (applied)

- **Problem.** Trombones 1·2, bass trombone and tuba rest at 336–338. At 339 they re-enter on the final E-minor chord at ff, as tenuto minims with a diminuendo over two beats at 2. = 96. In the music this is an ff attack followed at once by a decay: the "bite" Dan ruled out. The BRIEF says: "keep brass accents soft, weight rather than bite. Do not propose ff, sfz or accented attacks on brass entries; brass entries are shaped by dynamics and doubling instead (c123)." c123 recorded "no ff/sfz attacks on brass re-entries" as do-not-touch. c128 later set 339 to "low brass ff" as long as there was no accent sign, but an ff re-entry is the case the rule names. 339 is the only brass entry in the score marked ff. Every other entry, including 101, 105, 333 and the trumpets at 333, is pp or f.
- **Evidence from the reading.** Adler PDF 147: in d'Indy's unison tutti the trombones are marked ppp "so as not to dominate the entire orchestra". Adler PDF 28 (Schubert, Rosamunde, Ex. XI-12): the trombones add power by bolstering bassoons and low strings, and the diminuendo is made by taking trombones away, not by an attack-and-fade. Adler PDF 164–165: a chord that goes from loud to soft must be given to instruments that can control it. A two-beat ff-to-nothing in the tuba and bass trombone is the hardest version of that. Adler PDF 37: the brass climax comes from the colour held back (the low brass are already back at 333, f), not from extra volume at the cut-off. The chord loses no weight. Contrabassoon, Bsn 2, cellos and basses are ff on E, and horns and trumpets carry their fff level into 339.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-169/work/sections/coda.ily):**
  ```
  codaTbnOne  = { ... | g2--\f\> r4\! | ... }      % was \ff
  codaTbnTwo  = { ... | e2--\f\> r4\! | ... }      % was \ff
  codaBassTbn = { ... | e,2--\f\> r4\! | ... }     % was \ff
  codaTuba    = { ... | e,,2--\f\> r4\! | ... }    % was \ff
  ```
  Notes, tenutos, the diminuendo and the rest before the G.P. are unchanged. Trombones 1·2 share a staff and keep equal dynamics. This keeps c168's "soft brass at 339" and makes it true of the low brass as well. It undoes only the "low brass ff" part of c128, for the reason given above.
