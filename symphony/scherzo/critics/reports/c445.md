## Findings

### 1. The score drops the bow slur and the tie at 45–46 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla) and at 121–122 (Vln I) (applied)

**Problem.** These bars open the second idea, the movement's main lyrical theme in the strings. In the source each of these bars starts a two-bar bow, written as an up-bow:

```
d'''4.->(\f^\markup \italic "espress."\<\upbow c'''8 b''4~ | b''4 g'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>)
```

The engraved score shows no slur and no tie at 45–46 in Vln I, Vln II and violas, and none at 121–122 in Vln I. At 300 dpi, 45 reads D6. C6 B5 | B5 G6, which means three separate bows and a restruck B5, with nothing to show the tie over the bar line. 121 reads the same way (B5. A5 G5 | G5 E6). Vln I's accent at 121 was also missing. In every other statement (47–52, 123–128, 280–286) the slurs print normally, so the conductor sees the first bow of each phrase written differently from the rest.

The parts are correct. They do not use the score's stem-side script callback, and the Violin I part shows both slurs and both ties. So score and parts disagree. The tie is protected: "the second idea's tie over the bar line" (c411 do-not-touch).

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 37: "If no slurs are marked (non legato), each note requires a change in the direction of the bow"; "all notes under that slur are performed on one bow".
- Adler PDF 38: the only slurs in string parts are "the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow".
- Adler PDF 54: playing instructions "must appear in both the score and the parts".

As printed, the score asks for exactly the bowing the composer rejected in c006 ("Vln I two-bar bows in the second idea"). The cause is the one c018 found for the down-bow (global.ily, line 5). With the default `avoid-slur`, the up-bow script on the slur's first note runs into a dependency loop with the stem-side Y-offset callback (score.ly, c125). LilyPond then sizes the slur and the tie to a zero-width stencil without any warning: 0.15–0.22 staff space for the slurs, 0.10 for the ties. The stencil log of the whole score found exactly these four slurs and four ties and no others. The up-bows at 280 and 349 start slurs that print normally, because their first note does not start a tie.

**Fix (applied).** global.ily, next to `dnb`:

```
upb = -\tweak avoid-slur #'ignore \upbow
```

- sA.ily, bar 45 in sAVlnOne, sAVlnTwo, sAVla: `...\italic "espress."\<\upb c'''8 b''4~` (was `\upbow`).
- sC.ily, bar 121 in sCVlnOne and sCVlnTwo: `b''4.->\subP\upb\<(` (Vln II printed correctly; it is changed too so the two lines read alike).

A c445 comment is added in each file. After the fix the stencil log shows 0 degenerate slurs or ties. At 250 dpi, 45–46 and 121–122 show the slur, the tie, the V and the accent in all three parts. score.midi is byte-identical to v445's (print only). The parts build with 0 warnings.

### 2. Tested and not kept: "la metà" for Vln II at 169–184

This is the audible candidate. Adler PDF 32 gives "half" (la metà) for a passage in which only the outside players play, and c436 left the note that 169–184 is full, so later critics should thin there rather than add. I built Vln II "con sord., dolce, la metà" at 169 and "senza sord., tutti" at 193 in /tmp/critic-445/t3. The build was clean, with 13 pages.

I did not keep it, for three reasons.
- Masking is not the problem. The tune is in the cellos at mp (with Hn 3, then the violas) and lies below Vln II's pp figure. Adler's concern (PDF 38, and Ex. II-25, where "half" serves to stagger bows) is not at stake here.
- The arpeggio is the only harmony between the cellos' tune and Fl 1's answers at 169–184. There is no harp and no sustained winds above Bsn 2's bass, and c434 re-voiced the arpeggio precisely so that it carries the I6. Halving an already muted section (c409) would leave about six players on fast con sord. quavers. The I6/V harmony would thin, and single players' intonation would be exposed in the quietest texture of the trio.
- c409's mute already made the figure softer and veiled, so halving the section would only repeat that change.

Since I judge that it makes the passage weaker, the audible part of this cycle is "no change".

### 3. Checked and kept (no change)

- **Solo violin at 349–356.** The notation is octave dyads with "div.: solo / gli altri" and "tutti" at 369. Adler PDF 32 suggests writing out separate lines for a special division. Here the label names the division at its first note and "tutti" closes it, which is the instruction PDF 30 and PDF 32 require. Two voices would add stems and rests to the most crowded system on page 13. Keep.
- **"via sord." at 185** is now visible in the score as well as the part. It does not add a page, it tells the conductor why Vln II enters "senza sord." at 193, and it follows PDF 54's rule that score and parts agree. Keep.
- **div./unis. and pizz./arco.** The script found no chord without a label, no single note left under an open div., and no arco with less than four beats to take the bow (minimum: Vln II and Vla at 273). Every arco → pizz. change with one beat (Cb 205, 225) comes after an up-bow.
