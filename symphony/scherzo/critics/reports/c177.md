## Findings

### 1. Bars 201-204: four instruments on E4 at p while the top line is three detached crotchets (applied)

Problem. After the trio's forte call (197-200) the texture thins to a sustained chord: A major (201), A minor (202), B (203), B7 (204). The top line is Vln I A4–C5–B4, the ♭6–5 that c070 wrote, but each note is a single tenuto crotchet followed by two beats' rest. The sustained sound under it is packed in one octave. In bar 202, E4 is played by Cl 2, Hn 3, the violas and Vln II's crotchet; in bar 203, D♯4 is played by Cl 2, Hn 3 and the violas. After the first beat of each bar, the highest sustained note is E4 or D♯4, so the ♭6–5 is heard only as three short taps.

Evidence. Adler PDF 257: unison overlapping "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch"; it belongs to full tutti, and this passage is p with a diminuendo. PDF 255: in homophonic writing "the melody must come through ... and not be obscured by the harmony". PDF 270 (Copland, Ex. VIII-41): the weak line is the one that gets the extra doubling. Here the doubling goes to the static middle note and the moving top line gets none.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-177/work/sections/tB.ily). The violas hold the top line under Vln I's crotchets:

```
tBVla, bars 201-204:  e'2.\p | e'2. | dis'2.\> | fis'2.
                  ->  a'2.\p | c''2. | b'2.\> | fis'2.
```

The viola line moves 200 G♯4 – A4 – C5 – B4 – F♯4 – 205 F♯3 (the drop at 204-205 is the same as before). E4 at 202 is now in Cl 2 and Hn 3, with Vln II's crotchet, and D♯4 at 203 in Cl 2 and Hn 3. Violas and Cl 2 move in sixths (A/C♯, C/E, B/D♯). C5–B4 now sounds in octaves with Bsn 1's C4–B3 for the whole of each bar; c070 already doubles the line in that octave on purpose, so this is a doubling, not a voice-leading fault. Violas now lie above Vln II's two crotchets at 201-202. That is Copland's voicing (PDF 270), and Vln II is doubled there by Cl 2 and Bsn 1. Dynamics, slurs, harmony and bar 204 are unchanged. There is still no C-major triad (A–C–E at 202).

### 2. Wind counterpoint is still scarce (kept, no change)

Problem examined. Adler PDF 271-272 says wind counterpoint, "especially rhythmical", is very effective, and Ex. VIII-42 shows Mozart's oboe and bassoon imitating each other a bar apart over a running violin line and pizzicato. The movement's own model does the same thing at its first theme: Dvořák 9/iii bars 13-20 is a wind canon a bar apart. In this score the winds imitate only by taking a turn in a chain (Ob at 92, Cl 1 at 245, the codetta's Ob/Cl/Bsn at 225-236). Two winds never overlap in imitation. The only real wind counter-line is Bsn 1 at 345-348.

Why it stays. Every place where a bar-apart wind canon would fit is protected for a stated reason, and none of those reasons is answered by this chapter. Theme A's first statement 13-28 is Vln I leggiero over pp off-beats (c108). The call and response at 29-37 is protected by c007, c033 and c144, and Adler PDF 253 approves its pattern of winds alone, strings alone, then together. The fugato and stretto at 57-72 and 289-300 are for strings, with the winds entering only at the stretto (c007, c017, c097). The closing dialogue at 133-140 is cellos against timpani (c077 Finding 3). I tested one unprotected spot on paper: an echo of Bsn 1's call by Cl 1 a bar later at 270-271. It runs into Vln I's different continuation at 271 (E5–B4–B4–G4 against G5–F♯5–F5) and clouds a call and response that is clear now. The movement has enough imitation through its chained entries (principle 4), and at its climaxes the winds join a stretto. No change.

### 3. Codetta 225-236: the gesture is passed around without a summing tutti (kept)

Problem examined. The Eroica example (PDF 253-254) passes the gesture through the winds and then sums it up with a cadential tutti. Here T's head goes Ob 1 (225), Cl 1 (229), Bsn 1 (233), an octave lower each time, and then thins to Hn 1's G♯ at 237.

Why it stays. This is the trio's close over the E pedal before a pp retransition. The summing tutti for T was the trio return at 209-224. The third entry is altered as PDF 255 asks: bassoon, on iv, with the harp chords thinned (c153). Adler PDF 270-271 (Copland) shows the opposite case that fits this passage: a diminuendo scored by taking instruments out. The passage is protected by c127, c153 and c157. No change.

### 4. Tutti 108-118 and 308-318: violas above Vln II (kept)

Checked by `cross.py`. The violas' off-beat double stops lie above Vln II, which plays theme A an octave lower. This is the voicing in Copland's Ex. VIII-41 (PDF 270), and it works because Cl 2 and the cor anglais double Vln II, as Adler requires. No change.

### 5. Bars 13-28: Vln II and violas repeat the same off-beat figure for sixteen bars (kept)

Stravinsky's practice of dividing a repeated figure between players (PDF 274) does not apply to a one-in-a-bar leggiero accompaniment. Dvořák 9/iii (bars 13-20) keeps the strings on repeated crotchets for eight bars under its theme. The reprise at 253-268 alters this texture (Fl 1 heterophony, pizz. off-beats, sustained cellos), which satisfies PDF 255. No change.
