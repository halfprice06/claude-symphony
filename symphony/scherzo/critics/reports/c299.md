## What else I studied

Only the first two pages are in full score, plus the leaf of bars 10–20 found in 1967 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._8_(Schubert); https://lantern.mediahist.org/catalog/PMR_2_3_0011). The local corpus has no copy, so my comparison is from my knowledge of the score and is limited to its broad traits. Those traits are:

## Holistic comparison with Schubert 8/iii

- **Opening.** Schubert opens on his main idea at forte in the full orchestra. This score opens pp on a solo horn's G♯, then a leggiero string theme. That is a deliberate choice, required by the cyclic plan (G♯ = movement II's A♭) and modelled on Beethoven 9/ii's timpani and Dvořák 9/iii. It is not a weakness against Schubert.
- **One rhythmic idea.** Schubert's sketch has been criticised for leaning too long on one rhythm. This movement avoids that. Motive x is set against:
  - the hemiola second idea (45–56);
  - the fugato's countersubject (57–68);
  - the three-bar groups at 89–100;
  - the trio's quaver turns (185–200).
- **Trio.** Schubert's Trio and this one share the same plan: a soft, single-line tune over a sparse bed, in a key that relaxes the minor. The trio here does more with colour (Cl 1 over harp and a horn drone, then strings with a cello countermelody, then the oboe and cor anglais calls), and its key plan (E – C♯ minor – B – E) is fuller than the short Schubert sketch.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** On the evidence of the score as engraved, yes for the craft:
  - balance, doubling and voice leading have been checked at every level;
  - the dynamics are graded;
  - the closing page (357–376) is the strongest single passage.

  What still marks it as careful rather than inspired is the same as at c289 and c279: the second idea and T are well made but not yet unforgettable. Changing them now, on the last cycle before the merge, would put the finale's quotations and 50 cycles of checked voicing at risk for an uncertain gain. I make no change.

## Comparison with v289 (what changed in cycles 289–298)

1. 54–55, 130–131, 286–287: Cl 1 holds C5 as a 4–3 into G (c289). This is better: the seventh of V7 now resolves down in its own voice.
2. 53–56: Bsn 1 D3–G3 instead of D2–G2 (c294). This is better: it fills the empty octave above the bass at the cadential 6/4.
3. 333–336: timpani f, then a crescendo to a ff roll, instead of ff/fff (c294). This is better: the timpani no longer stand above the brass, and fff stays in the strings and winds only.
4. 335: Vln II, violas and C.a. climb F♯–G–A with Vln I and Fl 1 (c291). This is neutral to better. On beat 3 the A sounds in three octaves against G held in Ob 1·2, Cl 1 and Hn 2. That is an accented passing note G–A–B over the B pedal, the normal treatment of a rising line into a dominant arrival, and it resolves on the downbeat of 336. It is not worse than v289, where the A6 stood alone against G in two octaves.
5. 361–364: clarinets printed as chords so that Cl 2's B3 shows (c295). Neutral (engraving only).
6. 209, 366: rest positions (c295). Neutral.
