## Findings

### 1. The stretto at 69–72 and 297–300 is a harmonised block, not a stretto (applied)
Bars 69–72, 297–300. Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1 state the subject on A at 69; at the same moment Vln II and Cl 1 play it a fourth lower (E5–B4–B4–E5) and then in thirds (A5–G♯5–G5 under C6–B5–B♭5), and the violas move with them in crotchets. Only the cellos and Bsn 1 enter a bar later. To the ear this is the subject harmonised in parallel fourths and thirds. Bartók's stretto (PDF 44, 47) places each entry one bar after the last in a different group, so each head is heard as it arrives; Hindemith's fugato entries (PDF 42–43) are each set off by a rest. Fix (applied): Vln II and Cl 1 rest at 69 and enter at 70 with the head on E. They then rise F♯–G♯–A at 71 as before. Vc and Bsn 1 hold E at 70 and enter with the head on A at 71. This gives three entries, one bar apart: A (69, high), E (70, middle), A (71, low). The harmony keeps its plan (A minor, E over the A pedal, A major, A major ff). This does not answer c032's "keep fugato 57–72" by ignoring it: the keep was about harmony, and the chord on every downbeat is unchanged.

### 2. The brass take no part in the fugato; the stretto's last entry should be in the horns (applied)
Bars 71 and 299. In v97 Hn 1·2 hold C♯5/A4 from p at 71, as a sustained chord. Adler's whole section (PDF 41–47) shows the brass carrying the imitation, not only filling the harmony. The cyclic rules keep the trumpets to motive x, and Hn 3·4 must keep their first entry at 73. Hn 1·2 are free, though. Fix (applied): Hn 1·2 in unison play motive x on A4–E4–E4–A4 at 71 (and 299), f< into the ff chord. This doubles the cellos' entry an octave higher. Two horns in unison weigh as one trombone (PDF 44), enough to carry the last entry through the strings' crescendo. This is a middle-register unison (written E5–B4), not the high horn unison the finale owns. Hn 2 keeps A4 into 72 and 73 (the c010 common tone). Hn 1 rises A4→C♯5 for the chord and still steps C♯→C♮ into 73 (c042). Both players have equal dynamics on the shared staff.

### 3. The tenor countermelody in the tutti theme has one horn against nine brass (applied)
Bars 105–120, 305–320. The countermelody (c007; Vc espress., Bsn 1, Hn 4) runs G3–E4 under ff theme A. Above and around it are three horns (Hn 1, 2, 3) on accented ff crotchets, two trumpets on motive x, and two trombones, bass trombone and tuba on f downbeats. By Adler's measure (PDF 44, Bartók: two horns in unison = one trumpet or trombone), one horn is half the weight of each trombone it must sound against. The cello and bassoon add little at ff. Schoenberg's independent trombone line (PDF 44, 48–49) carries because it is given full weight. Fix (applied): Hn 3 joins Hn 4 in unison on the countermelody. Both mark ff and take the breath at 116/316. Hn 1·2 keep the crotchet chords on beats 1 and 3 (B4/E4, C5/E4 and so on), so c086's tutti brass layout holds. The third of the chord is still sounded by Tbn 1, the violas' off-beat double stops and the winds' theme. This partly reverses c066's choice of Hn 3 as the middle chord tone (G4/A4/F♯4). The reason is above: that middle tone is already in four other parts, but the countermelody had only one brass player. Hn 3's range here (sounding G3–E4) is comfortable, and it is not a high unison. Hn 3 still goes to B4 sub. p at 121 after two beats' rest.
