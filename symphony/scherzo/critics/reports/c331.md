## What else I studied

One rhythmic cell carries that tune: a note tied over the bar line that then moves by step. It comes back in four of its first seven bars, so the listener learns it at once. ## Findings

### F1 (applied). T's first phrase gets a prepared suspension: bars 3–4 become D♯5–F♯4–A4~ | A4–G♯4

Bars: 155–156 (Cl 1), 171–172 (Vln I 8va, Vln II), 199–200 (Cl 1, Vln II, Vla), 211–212 (Cl 1, Vln I 8va, Vln II), coda 343–344 (Hn 3, in A).

Problem: critics have flagged T's first phrase as its least memorable since c289 (c299, c301, c309, c311, c319). It is also the only phrase of T with no tie across a bar line. Phrase 3 has the A tied at 10–11 and phrase 4 the E tied at 13–14. Phrase 1 plays I – I – V7 – I with every note struck. Its one dissonance, the A on the downbeat of bar 4, is an unprepared accented passing note (c001), so the rhythm has no syncopation the ear can catch.

Evidence from the reading: A&S PDF 346 says the suspension makes rhythm, not melody, the main event and individualizes the line. PDF 357–358 (Ex. 21-23a) gives the exact progression in this bar: the 7th of V7 held over into I as a 4–3. T's bar 3 is already V7 (harp A3–B3–D♯4 over B) and bar 4 is I. Taking the 7th A on beat 3 and tying it gives a prepared 4–3 over E. Under it are the 5th B3 (Hn 3) and the doubled bass E (Hn 4, basses), which is the four-voice setting on PDF 349–350. The resolution G♯ sounds nowhere on the downbeat, as PDF 352 requires. At 200 the tied A joins Hn 1, which already holds A4 there as a 9–8 over G♯ with B♯3 (PDF 351; resolution in the bass allowed, PDF 352). Melodically, bar 3 now arpeggiates V7 downward and back (D♯5–F♯4–A4) the way bar 2 arpeggiates I (B4–E5). The phrase ends with F♯ and A on either side of G♯. Its last two bars, A tied and falling to G♯, return an octave higher as the climax of phrase 3 (A5 tied, then G♯5, bars 10–11). That gives T one tied-note cell heard three times (bars 3–4, 10–11, 13–14), as in the Dvořák tune above.

Earlier reasons, answered:
- c081 valued the 10–11 tie as "the only bar-line tie before the cadence". It is still the highest and longest note. It now repeats phrase 1's ending an octave up instead of appearing from nowhere.
- c321 described B–A–G♯ in bars 3–4 as the diatonic motto tail. B4–A4–G♯4 stays at the same pitch in bars 12–13 of every statement (C♯5–B4–A4 | G♯4), which is closer to the Hn 1 tail B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 at 357.
- c322 and c327 protect the inner resolutions under bar 3 and the accompaniment cleared under the falling sixth. The quaver F♯ is unchanged and nothing new sounds against it.

Fix, as applied:
- Tune: `dis''4. fis'8 b'4 | a'4 gis'2` becomes `dis''4. fis'8 a'4~ | a'4\> gis'2 | a'2\!( …` (octave forms likewise). In tB (199–200) the existing `\>` stays on the A. String slurs now span both bars so the tie is inside one bow.
- Coda Hn 3: `gis'4. b8 e'4 | d'4 cis'2)` becomes `gis'4. b8 d'4~ | d'4\> cis'2) | R2.\! | R2.*19`.
- So the 7th does not move in octaves with the tune:
  - harp 155 beat 3 `<a b dis'>` becomes `<fis b dis'>` (it made A3/A4 to G♯3/G♯4 with Cl 1);
  - harp 343 beat 3 `<d e gis>` becomes `<b, e gis>` (D3/D4 to C♯ with Hn 3);
  - violas 171 and 211 beat 3 `<a dis'>` become `<b dis'>`.
- Cellos 171–172 and 211–212 (and Bsn 2 at 211–212): `fis2( a4 | gis2 fis4)` becomes `fis2( b4~ | b4 gis4 fis4)`. With A3 the analyser found new outer-voice octaves F♯–A against Vln I at 171, and the cellos' G♯3 on the downbeat sounded the resolution under the suspended A in an all-string texture (PDF 352, Ex. 21-12). The cellos now hold the 5th B3 through the suspension and move to G♯3 with the violins. The countermelody still has its own motion (c051), and its tie answers the tune's.
