## Findings

### 1. Bars 365–369: the two unison horns on the closing G♯ cut off pp instead of handing over (applied)

- **Problem.** The last page is built so that Hn 3 covers Hn 1's breath (c440, c488: Hn 3 enters on G♯4 pp at 365, Hn 1 rests from beat 3 of 366 through 367 and re-enters pp at 368, Hn 3 stops on beat 3 of 369). Both releases are flat: Hn 1's G♯4 stops dead on beat 3 of 366, and Hn 3's on beat 3 of 369. Nothing else is there to hide them. After 364 the only other sound is Vln I's G♯5 ppp (and the timpani's motive x at 367). So the listener hears the level step down twice, from two horns to one, each time with the small attack-like edge of a horn release at pp. That is exactly the seam the cover was written to hide. The 361–364 chord fades since c123/c488, so these two cut-offs are the only unshaped horn releases left on this page.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 307, Ex. X-17 (Debussy, *Faune* 106–9, image): two pairs of muted horns hold overlapping notes at ppp/pp, and each held note has its own diminuendo into its release, so one horn sound gives way to the next without a cut. PDF 309, Ex. X-24 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, soft tonguing): the solo horn ends each phrase on a diminuendo hairpin. PDF 305, Ex. X-11 (Ravel): "p > pp expressif", a soft horn note ending in a diminuendo. PDF 311–312: horns held at pp inside a soft texture act like woodwinds and should shape like them; the clarinets and strings do so at 362–364.
- **Fix (applied), sections/coda.ily.** Each horn fades over the last bar before its release, while the other horn holds the pitch:
  - `codaHnOne`: `… gis'2.~ | gis'2.~\> | gis'2 \tag #'score \once \override Rest.staff-position = #0 r4\! | R2. | gis'2.~\pp …` (hairpin over 365–366; Hn 3 has entered at 365).
  - `codaHnThree`: `… R2.*19 | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~\> | gis'2 r4\! | R2.*7 | }` (hairpin over 368–369; Hn 1 re-enters at 368).
  - Shared staves: Hn 2 rests from 340 and Hn 4 from 365, so each hairpin stands alone on its staff and there are no unequal simultaneous dynamics. Hn 1's printed pp at 368 stays and restates the level after its own fade.
  - MIDI: unchanged. The horn staves share one channel and neither hairpin is followed by a new attack in its voice, so it is print only, like c488's Hn 4 fade.
- **Protected passage.** The closing page (G♯ frame, motto tail, Hn 3's cover, Hn 1's last G♯ 368–376 with its ppp) keeps every pitch, rhythm and player. Only the release shape of two held notes changes.

### 2. Bars 153–168: taper the Hn 3·4 drone into each four-bar break (built in thought, not applied)

Ex. X-17 would also suggest a diminuendo at 156, 160, 164 and 168, where the trio drone stops on beat 3. I did not apply it. Each release falls on the harp's beat-3 off-beat chord (tA.ily, harp RH), which covers it, and it coincides with the end of Cl 1's phrase, so the break is heard as the phrase's breath. c288 and c388 kept these pads flat as an accompaniment choir one level under the solo (PDF 311–312). A taper would not be audible and would add four hairpins to page 6.

### 3. Bars 141–144: taper Hn 1's G4 before the G♯ (not applied)

In the intro Vln I's G5 fades at 8, and the link is the intro reversed, so a taper here seemed to fit. I did not apply it. At 141–144 the horn, both bassoons and the bass clarinet hold one pp chord and release together on beat 3 of 144. All four then return at 145, and their second block fades at 147–148 (link.ily). The block cut sets the G-minor colour off from the G♯ that follows, much as X-19 sets the open sound against the closed one. Tapering only the horn would break up a chord the four instruments shape together.

### 4. Bars 335–336: low brass and trumpets do not grow into the fff summit (kept)

At 336 strings and woodwinds reach fff while the trumpets, trombones and tuba stay at the f they reached at 333, and the horns stay ff. PDF 311 (the horn carries over anything when loud) and X-13 (Mahler: f, ff < fff) could argue for a crescendo here. I kept the levels. Dan's listening note (brief: weight, not bite) and c488's test (trumpets f under horns ff, rows 23–24) both apply. The summit is the violins' B6, and the low brass already give it weight with the B held from 335.

### 5. Mutes and stopped horn (X-17 to X-19): still not applicable

The one stopped or muted colour that would be heard is an echo of the G→G♯ turn at 141–145 or 240–241. Ex. X-19 shows it, but the stopped sound is "nasal and sharp" (PDF 308), which is the bite Dan's rule excludes. It would also break the mirror with the open turn at 1–8. Earlier passes (c149, c316, c488) rejected it for the same reasons, and I found no stronger reading.
