## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Accented timpani strokes are the loudest low attack in the tuttis at 105, 108, 305, 308 and 333 (applied)
- **Problem.** sC/dC open with `e,4->\f` and put `b,4->` on bar 4 of the theme (108, 308). The coda's theme head opens with `e,4->\ff` (333). Since c124 every brass downbeat in the tutti theme is a tenuto, and Dan's rule (c123) asks for weight, not bite. c123 rejected an ff attack for the low brass at 333 for that reason, and c086 keeps them f there. The timpani accent therefore gives these downbeats the attack the brass were told not to make. In the MIDI the accented strokes reach velocity 104 at 105 and 108 against horns 75, trumpets 79, low brass 83 and violins 95. At 333 the stroke reaches 109 against horns 67, low brass 83 and violins 95. In each of these tuttis the timpani is the loudest instrument on the downbeat that brings back the theme.
- **Evidence.** Piston via Adler, PDF 55 and 84: a ff timpani leaves nothing else audible. PDF 141: one loud percussion stroke can cover the whole orchestra however loud the tutti is. PDF 79: in a tutti the timpani support the bass, they do not lead. c056 kept the accent at 105/305 "at the brass's level". That was before c124 changed the brass from accents to tenutos, so the brass level now has no accent.
- **Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily: `sCTimp = { e,4--\f r4 r4 | e,4 r4 r4 | e,4 r4 r4 | b,4-- b,8 b,8 b,4 | …` (dC likewise). coda.ily: `b,2.:32\< | e,4--\ff e,8 e,8 e,4 |`. The tenuto still marks the downbeat, with weight, the same way the brass mark it. MIDI after the change: 84 at 105/108 (level with the low brass, under the violins) and 89 at 333. The accents at 337–339 stay, because there the whole orchestra accents the hemiola.

### 2. One solo motive, two stick colours: 241 on soft sticks, 245–252 on hard (applied)
- **Problem.** Soft sticks are taken at 146 for the trio. "Hard sticks" is printed at 242, after the retransition's first motive x at 241, so the same pp solo figure sounds soft at 241 and hard at 245, 247 and 249–252. The player cannot change before 241, because the E roll runs to 240's crotchet. The 237–240 pp roll under the fading codetta needs a soft head, but the motive needs articulation, so moving "hard sticks" back to 225 would spoil the roll.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80: the composer chooses hard, medium or soft mallets, and mallet and spot set the tone; PDF 88 gives the medium-stick symbol for this purpose. PDF 59 and 110–111: give the player time to change sticks.
- **Fix (applied).** One mid-weight colour for the whole join, and the hard sticks back where there is time and need for them. tC.ily at 225: `R2.^\markup \small \italic \column { "muta G♯ in G" "medium sticks" }`. Medium sticks give a smooth pp roll at 237–240 and a clear motive from 241. retr.ily: the "hard sticks" mark at 242 is removed (`R2.`). dB.ily at 289: `dBTimp = { R2.^\markup \small \italic "hard sticks" | …`. That leaves twelve bars' rest before the crescendo roll at 301 and the ff motive at 304. The answers at 260/268 and the f roll at 285–286 are played on medium sticks.

### 3. The movement's last stroke has no dynamic of its own (applied)
- **Bar 375.** `e,4 r4 r4` comes after three bars' rest and inherits ppp from 371. It sits with the pizz. E (pp) and the harp harmonics (p) under the last G♯.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80: the part shows the dynamic of each note. In this score the composer restates the level after rests (c055 at 168).
- **Fix (applied).** coda.ily `| R2.*3 | e,4\ppp r4 r4 |`. The print changes; the MIDI does not.
