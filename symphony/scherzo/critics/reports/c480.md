## Findings

### 1. 199: the trio's forte peak takes the Neapolitan 6th of C♯ minor (D/F♯) before V and the A major of 201. Applied (theme change in this one statement).

**Problem.** 197–200 is the trio's loudest point before T's return. Here T (Cl 1, Vln II, violas, with Hn 1) is set against the oboe call (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I) in C♯ minor: i (197–198), iiø6/5 over F♯ (199), V (200), then the deceptive A major at 201, with A minor at 202. Bar 199 is the plain diatonic ii. The scherzo uses ♭II at each structural level (theme A, 73–88, 301–302, 331), but the trio never uses it.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 483 says the pull of ♭2̂ against its harmony "is often a compelling reason for using ♭II6, even in places where diatonic II6 would not be incorrect, and accounts for the frequent use of ♭II6 at climactic moments". Ex. 28-19 (PDF 495, image checked) is Schubert's D. 959 scherzo, also a 3/4 Allegro vivace, in these same keys. There the D-major 6/3 is ♮II6 of C♯ minor and IV6 of A major ("c♯: ♮II6 / A: IV6 V6/4–5/3"), and PDF 494 says ♭II used this way "can create a feeling of surprise that attracts attention to the key change". In the trio, C♯ minor goes to A major at 201. With D/F♯ at 199, the A major that follows the G♯ chord sounds prepared, because D/F♯ was its IV6. PDF 485 (and Ex. 28-7) say ♭II6 comes from whatever leads to II6, here the bass F♯ of iv. PDF 484 and Ex. 28-2a say to double the bass and let ♭2̂ fall a diminished third to ♯7̂. PDF 484 and Ex. 28-3d say an inner ♭2̂→♮2̂ is possible when ♭2̂ is doubled.

**Decision and fix** (sections/tB.ily, bar 199 only):
```lilypond
% Cl 1 (and Vln II same pitch)   was dis''4. fis'8 a'4\<~
d''4. fis'8 a'4\<~
% Vla                            was dis'4.( fis8 a4\<~
d'4.( fis8 a4\<~
% Cl 2                           was << cis'2. { s2 s4\< } >>
<< d'2. { s2 s4\< } >>           % D4 -> B#3 at 200: the diminished third of Ex. 28-2a
% Hn 2                           was fis'4-- dis'2--
fis'4-- d'2--                    % F#4 doubles the bass on the downbeat; D4 -> D#4 at 200 is inner (Ex. 28-3d)
```
Nothing else changes. Bass F♯ (Vc, Cb, Bsn 2), Bsn 1 F♯3, T's tied A4 4–3 into 200 (c331 protection), the call A5–B5–C♯6 (tonic-scale figuration, PDF 493 §12), and all dynamics, layering (c404, c437) and hairpins stay as they were. The outer voices are unchanged, so analysis.txt is unchanged. The chord is D–F♯–A: no F♮ (c380's trio rule), no C-major triad, no stop chord.

**Why it is the change a listener will hear.** It is one bar (0.8 s at the trio tempo), forte, in the whole string section and five winds, at the trio's climax. It is the only moment where the trio takes on the scherzo's Neapolitan colour, a fifth away from the scherzo's own ♭II (D on C♯, F on E), and it leaves the trio's ♮2̂ of E (F♯) untouched. I weighed the cost: T's bar-3 note becomes D♮ in this one statement. T is not locked (Dan, 2026-10-12), its head G♯–A–B–E (the only part movement I relies on) is unchanged, and the finale does not quote T. I kept the change.

### 2. 300: Vln I's A♯5 is spelt B♭5. Applied (print only, plus string intonation).

**Problem.** Since c473 the beat-3 chord C–E–A♯ resolves as V7 of F: A♯5 → A5, C → A, E → F. Every voice moves as a dominant seventh, and none moves as an augmented sixth. The print still shows the sharp, which points up to B.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 497 §19: raised notes ascend and lowered notes descend. PDF 498, Ex. 28-25: "G♭, not F♯", because F and its chord are the goal, and the sharp "would obscure the tonal direction". Ex. 32-9 (PDF 591, image checked), the Beethoven passage c473 cites, respells the Italian sixth's C♯ as D♭ in the bar where it becomes V7 of ♭II. The players' intonation follows the spelling: a sharp pulled high and then dropped a semitone is worse than a flat that leans into A.

**Fix** (dB.ily): `e'''2->\ff\dnb ais''4->\upbow` → `e'''2->\ff\dnb bes''4->\upbow`. The sound in the MIDI is unchanged. This does not undo c422. That protection's reason was "301 is the movement's only structural V with no chromatic approach", and since c473 bar 301 is ♭II6, not V, so the reason no longer applies to this chord. The sound c422 and c473 wanted (C–E–A♯/B♭ after A major) is kept exactly.
