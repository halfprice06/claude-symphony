I dumped the melodic lines of every section file: theme A, the counterstatement, the second idea in all three keys, the harp melody, T in every statement, the oboe call and its answer, the coda's T and the motto tail. I looked at score pages 5, 7 and 12 at 90 dpi. I wrote a scan (lyparse) that marks every beat where a brass note is the highest sounding pitch; its results are in row 5. I also listed the stretto entries 66–72 and 295–300 by instrument (row 14).

Melody, judged against the reading and the models. Theme A is the movement's tune to remember: motive x, the motto's chromatic tail G–F♯–F–E, and a single peak C6 in its twelfth bar. It is a 16-bar period, like the Dvořák 9/iii head (corpus file, bars 13–28), and has the same leggiero string presentation. The second idea's hemiola lines reach G6 and A6 and answer in two-bar units, as in the Brahms and Bruckner hemiola scherzi named in the brief. T is a 15-bar singable tune whose head is the horn call with the sixth filled in. Its three phrases rise E5 → F♯5 → A5 and it closes with a 4–3 suspension. It stands with the soft trio tunes of Dvořák 8/iii and the Eroica. This reading is about how brass presents melody, and the score's brass melodies are few and deliberate. The horn opens with the call, foreshadows T at 147–148, joins T heterophonically at 197, first states T as a full phrase at 341, and closes with the motto tail. That is the "saved colour" use of row 3. Each of these horn statements is soft, alone in its register, and over a thin accompaniment (row 5). Where brass doubles a line, it follows the book's standard pairings: horns with cellos and bassoon on a sustained tenor line (rows 10, 13), and brass voices handed group to group in the stretto (row 14).

## Findings

No finding needs a change to the score. Every principle in the table that applies to this movement is followed. The two points this reading raised on its first and second passes (c071, c161) are fixed and still hold. Below are the places I tested against the reading and decided to keep, ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. 209–224: no horn on T's lower octave in its forte return (row 10; kept)

The forte return is the trio's climax. A Romantic orchestrator would often put a horn on the lower octave of the tune here (Adler PDF 27). Here that octave is Cl 1 + Vln II at G♯4–A5, and Hn 1·2 hold thirds and sixths (B4/C♯5 over E4/G♯4). I kept it. The Hn 1·2 pair is protected (c004, c094). The tune already has three colours (Cl 1, both violins, Fl 1 from 217). A horn doubling here would also spend the colour that row 3 saves for the coda, where the horn plays T's first phrase at 341. c161 reached the same decision on different grounds (Hn 1's register against the lower octave).

### 2. 349–356: no soft horn on the cellos' arco countermelody under T's last phrases (row 10; kept)

The cello line (E3–A3, slurred, pp) is the long sustained kind of line Adler pairs with the horn. Hn 2 and Hn 4 are free at 349–352. I decided against it for two reasons. The coda's hand-over (row 18) must thin towards the strings before Hn 1's motto tail at 357, and a horn entry at 349 would anticipate Hn 1 by eight bars and blunt it. In the *Lohengrin* model the last doubling goes to the woodwinds, not back to the brass. Cl 1 already doubles the tune at 349 (c044).

### 3. 105–120 and 305–320: trumpets' repeated B4 under the head's E5–B4 (rows 2, 5; kept)

The trumpets are the instruments that "break through" a tutti (PDF 28). Tpt 1 repeats B4 in motive x rhythm in the head's own register, so I checked whether it masks the E–B–B–E contour. It does not. The tune is on top in Vln I, Vln II and Ob 1 at E5, with Cl 2 at E4, and from 109 the flutes add E6 (c124). The trumpets stay at the f they reach at 104, under the ff tutti. The B4 quavers coincide with the tune's own B4 quavers. The trumpet crotchets sit a fourth under the tune's E. Giving the trumpets the tune's pitches (E5–B4–B4–E5) would be allowed as motive x, but it would turn the trumpets into a melodic voice, which the cyclic rules hold back for the finale's trumpet solo. Kept, as in c064 and c244.

### 4. 112, 120, 312, 320 beat 3: horns' staccato crotchet after the tune's cadence (row 5; kept)

The scan marks Hn 1's B4 as the highest note on these beats, because the tune and flutes stop on beat 1. The horn note is the accompaniment's beat-3 stroke, played together with the violas' off-beats. The off-beats drive into the next phrase (113, 313), and at 120/320 they set up the subito piano (c063). Removing them would leave a hole in the accompaniment's rhythm at every phrase end. Kept (c086's tutti brass pattern).

### 5. 253–268: no horn on the reprise's sustained cello countermelody (row 10; kept)

c161 decided against this, and I agree for the same reason. The reprise is leggiero, with flute heterophony, and the horns are saved for 273 and for the second idea at 277–288. Kept.

### 6. 341–344: T's first phrase in Hn 3 rather than Hn 1 (rows 3, 9; kept)

Hn 1 owns the call and the motto tail, so I asked whether it should also own the saved horn statement of T. Hn 3 plays C♯4–A4, a good register for the low horn, at p dolce, alone over pp violas and harp chords. With Hn 3 on T, Hn 1 is fresh for its espressivo B4 at 357. A change would only swap one horn colour for another. Kept (c226).
