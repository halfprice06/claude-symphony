## Holistic comparison with Borodin 1/ii

- **Character.** Borodin's scherzo gets its speed from staccato lightness, not weight. Theme A does the same: it is leggiero spiccato over pizzicato bass (13), and its winds-and-strings call and response (29) is Rimsky-Korsakov's Ex. 182–186 kind of rising scoring. The first tutti is held back to 105.
- **The trio's key.** Borodin puts his trio a major third below, in B (enharmonically ♭VI of E♭). That is the same colour shift Dvořák 9/iii makes with its C-major trio. This score cannot do it: ♭VI of E is C major, which the cyclic rule bars. The tonic major is the right substitute, for the reasons c319 gave, and the trio's B-major cadence at 183 and its C♯-minor middle give the third-relation colour inside it.
- **The trio's tune.** Borodin's trio is folk-like. T's new bar 3 (c321) helps here. D♯–F♯–B is a falling arpeggio of the dominant with a leap of a sixth, the kind of open, plain leap a folk-style trio tune has, and it replaces a stepwise filler.
- **Form.** Borodin repeats his scherzo da capo. Here the reprise is written out and rescored: Fl 1 heterophony at 253, a shortened fugato at 289, a lighter tutti at 305, and the second idea's tenor octave at 281. That is the variety chapter IV asks for (row 19). On this point the score goes further than its model.
- **Verdict.** A conductor would believe a skilled late-Romantic master wrote this. The orchestration follows every principle of this reading that applies. The one gap that remains is the one c289–c319 named: the second idea and T are well made but less memorable than Borodin's or Dvořák's trio tunes. This reading teaches scoring, not melodic invention, so it gives no ground for rewriting them. I did not.

## Findings

None that call for a change. The points below are the ones I tested against the reading and decided to keep, ranked by how close they came to being findings.

### Considered and kept (decided, no change)

1. **205–209: Hn 1 now plays B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 at the pitch of the coda's motto tail (357–360).**
   - *The case against.* Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 116 asks whether a passage is "a climax or merely a step". A step that uses the same instrument, pitches and *p espress.* as the closing quotation could take something from 357.
   - *Why I kept it.* The two differ in harmony (over the V pedal into T's forte return, against E – A♯°7 – A minor – E), in what follows, and in role (preparation against close). Rimsky's own recurrences (Ex. 196–198) keep one colour for one figure while the setting changes, and c324's reason (Adler PDF 261) stands.
2. **T bar 3 in Cl 1 (155, 199, 211).**
   - *The concern.* On the A clarinet the sixth runs from written F♯5 down to the throat A4 on a quaver, then back over the break to D5, slurred at p.
   - *Why I kept it.* This is routine for an orchestral clarinettist. The throat note is a passing quaver, and the violins carry the same figure at 171 and 211. The leap is the point of the change.
3. **277–280 repeat the scoring of 121–124 (same key, the same sub. p, Vln I/II with Cl 1·2, Hn 2 and the strings).**
   - *The case for a change.* PDF 117–118 recommends changing the scoring when an idea recurs.
   - *Why I kept it.* From 281 the reprise does change (the tenor octave in Vc + Cl 2 + Hn 2, with Fl 1 only at 285). Borodin's own reprise is a literal da capo, and the harmony of 121–128/277–284 is protected (c302). Changing the first four bars would cost the clear recapitulation for little gain.
4. **105–120 against 305–320.**
   - *The case for a change.* They are scored nearly alike.
   - *Why I kept it.* 305 leaves out the trombones and tuba, so it is a partial tutti (row 21) under a coda that adds them back at 333. That is the change in detail Ex. 205–208 show. The layouts are protected (c304).
5. **No stopped-horn and oboe colour anywhere (row 6).** I looked for a place that wants it: 81–88, 141–148 and 241–252. 241 was already rejected in c316 (the pivot G♯→G must sound open). In the other two, the Neapolitan episode's darkness comes from the bass clarinet, bassoons and low horns. Kept.
