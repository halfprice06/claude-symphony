## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 237–240: the trio's last chord stays to the end under the poco rit., at the pivot's own level. Applied.
**Problem.** In the last four bars of the trio (poco rit.), Vln II holds B4 and the violas hold G♯4. Hn 3·4 hold B3/E3, the timpani roll on E, and the cellos and basses pluck E, all pp. Hn 1 holds G♯4, also pp. That G♯ is the pivot: at 241 Hn 1 turns it to G♮ and the movement goes back to E minor. The global comment and c023/c062/c170 make this G♯–G–F♯ the hinge of the retransition. But the chord does not thin or fade on the way to it, so the G♯ is one note of an even pp chord, and the violas sit in unison with it (G♯4) until the last beat. At the movement's end (362–364) the same G♯ is left alone because the strings and woodwinds fade into it (c123). At 240 nothing marks the change that comes at 241.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12–13 (Ex. 1): a line that adds nothing is taken out, and then the most important element can "dominate a little more noticeably". PDF 13: the ear should go to the primary element, not to the background. PDF 217–218 (Ex. 284): a change is covered by "the slight rest, the decrease in volume, and especially … the obvious change in tone color". c123 does this at 362–364 for the same note.
**Fix (applied, tC.ily).** Vln II and the violas leave the chord at the end of 239. Bar 240 is then a horn chord, E3–B3 under Hn 1's G♯4, with the timpani roll and the pizz. E. That chord is the colour the turn to G♮ happens in at 241:
```
tCVlnTwo … | b'2.~ | b'2.~ | b'2 r4 | R2. | }        % was b'2.~ | b'2.~ | b'2.~ | b'2 r4
tCVla    … | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4 | R2. | }   % was … | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4
```
I first tried a printed diminuendo to ppp in Vln II, the violas and Hn 3·4. Every printed hairpin at 238–240 moved the trio's line breaks and made 15 pages. I tested a hairpin alone, with a hidden target, with `minimum-length #0`, with `springs-and-rods ##f`, with a zero-height DynamicLineSpanner, and over three bars. So I took the colour away instead, as Ex. 1 does. The violas now have a bar's rest before their pp head at 241, which they did not have before. Hn 1, the timpani roll (c248) and the pizzicato are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 45, 209, 349: doubling parts of the lead lack the lead's character word. Applied.
**Problem.** Three statements of the main lyrical tunes print the expression word on only one of the parts that carry the line at the same dynamic:
- 45, second idea, f: Vln I has "espress.". Vln II, which plays the tune an octave lower at the same f (c009/c010), has none.
- 209, T's forte return: Vln I has "espress.". Vln II (octave below) and Cl 1 (unison with Vln II), both f with the same slurs and hairpins, have none.
- 349, T's last statement, pp: Cl 1 has "dolce". Vln I and Vln II, which carry the tune at the same pp, have none. The earlier violin statements of T (169, 209) are marked, so this one reads as unmarked, not as deliberate.
Each player reads only their own part, so these players get no instruction about the character of the line they double. At 169 both violin parts have "espress.", and at 205 both Fl 2 and Cl 2 have it, so the score itself sets the rule that these places break.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215): in the doubled sax and brass lines every part has the same accents, hairpins and marks. Ex. 2A (PDF 18): each entry has its own character word. PDF 213: give every player "as meaningful a line … as you possibly can". Dvořák 9/iii writes `più f` and `\fz` into each doubling part (strings.notesS.ily lines 75, 279).
**Fix (applied).**
```
sAVlnTwo  45:  d''2->(^\markup \italic "espress."\<\upbow b'4~ | …
tCVlnTwo 209:  gis'2(\f^\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | …
tCClOne  209:  gis'2(\f^\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | …
codaVlnOne 349: cis'''2(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic "dolce" dis'''4 | …
codaVlnTwo 349: cis''2(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic "dolce" dis''4 | …
```
I left Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 45 without a word. They double the tune at mf, a level under the violins on purpose (c067, c084), so they are a background colour and not the lead. The violas' "espress." at 197 stays only on the violas, because 197–200 is a protected layering (c167, c249).
