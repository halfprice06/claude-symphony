## Findings

### 1. Thirty swells with no target dynamic play two or three levels too loud in the MIDI (bars 23–25, 45–52, 121–124, 161–166, 177–182, 217–222, 263–264, 349–355, 357–358). Applied.

**Problem.** Cycle 38 marked messa-di-voce swells with no target, "so the swell stays within the passage's level". Cycles 58 and 98 added more of the same kind. LilyPond does not keep them within the level. Measured velocities in v138's score.midi (Vln I; printed level in brackets):
- Theme A's peak, C6 at 24: 101 [p = 53], louder than ff (91). At 264: 112 [mp = 64].
- Second idea, 46 and 50: 112 [f = 84], close to fff.
- "sub. p" at 121: up to 81 at 122, which is f.
- Trio tune, phrase 3, 178–179: 87 [mp = 63]. At 218–219: 107 [f = 84].
- Bar 52 in Fl 1/Ob 1 and bars 182–184 in the violins: after a bare `\!`, the diminuendo runs on to the next printed dynamic. The violins play 182–184 at p (53) where mp is printed. Cycle 134 found the same fault at 52 and 265 and fixed only those bars.

**Evidence.** Casanelles: velocity picks the sample layer, and the layer is the timbre, so a jump from p to f is a change of instrument, not a swell (PDF 148–149). A render with expressive crescendos or diminuendos sounds lifeless unless their size is fixed (PDF 149–150). The swell a composer wants is "a gentle swell on the note or arrival" (PDF 155). Notation does not give the curve, so the composer marks where the swell peaks and ends (PDF 152). The r07 renderer reads the printed marks from the event log, and its roll code moves one printed step on a hairpin with no target. score.midi and score.mp3 (FluidSynth) move two to three steps. The hidden dynamics are also events in the log, so they name the same one-step peak in both renders. I checked only r07's roll code, not its curves for ordinary notes.

**Fix (applied).** No printed change. Put a hidden dynamic one level above the local level where each `\>` starts, and a hidden local level where each `\!` stands, using cycle 134's form `-\tweak stencil ##f \mp`. Examples:
```
% sA.ily sAVlnOne 24-25
c'''2->\dnb-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\>( b''8 fis''8) | e''4-.-\tweak stencil ##f \p b'8-.
% sA.ily sAVlnOne 46-48 (also 50-52; Vln II likewise; Fl 1/Ob 1 with f and mf)
b''4 g'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>) | fis'''2( e'''4~ | e'''4-\tweak stencil ##f \f d'''4.
% tA.ily tAVlnOne 179-182 (Vln II likewise)
a'''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \mf\> gis'''8 fis'''4 | cis'''4 b''4 gis''4) | dis''4-\tweak stencil ##f \mp( fis''4 b''4\<~ | b''4-\tweak stencil ##f \mf\> ais''4-\tweak stencil ##f \mp cis'''4)
```
I made the same change in sC (Vln I/II 122–124, mp/p), dA (Vln I 264 mf; Fl 1 264 mp), tA (Cl 1 163–166, mp/p), tC (Fl 1 mf/f; Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II f/ff, 219–222), coda (Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II 351–355, p/pp) and coda Hn 1 358 (mp, falling to the printed pp at 361). The full list is in /tmp/critic-138/scripts/apply.py. The second players of the shared staves rest at every one of these places, so each combined staff still has equal simultaneous dynamics. After the change: Vln I 24 = 64 (+20 accent), 46 = 91, 122 = 63, 179 = 71, 219 = 89, 264 = 74 (+20 accent), 351 = 50, and 182–184 = 63 (mp, as printed). The diff shows velocity changes only in those bars, in Fl 1/Ob 1 53–56 (now mf as printed, not f), and in the harp bars of Finding 2.

### 2. The F-major episode's two melodies have no phrasing (bars 73–88). Applied.

**Problem.** The harp states theme A's head as a melody at 73–80, marked p espress. It has no slurs and no dynamic contour, and its eight bars of single plucked notes all play at one level. At 81–88 the clarinet's dolce version of the head (`d''4 a'8 a' d''4`) has no articulation at all. Every other statement of the head in the movement is staccato, so with no marks the clarinet plays the same detached figure, only softer. The "dolce" is not written into the notes.

**Evidence.** A percussive instrument cannot join its notes. A player makes a line on it by grading each note's loudness, louder into the high point and softer after it, so that the next attack is hidden in the decay (Casanelles PDF 153, the piano case). The harp therefore needs the contour written. Legato is what turns pitches into a gesture, "the arc as breath" (PDF 151–152), and the dolce transformation of a staccato motive is that change from note to line. Repeated notes inside a slur need portato dashes (Gould, *Behind Bars*), or the slur reads as a tie or as no articulation.

**Fix (applied).** No notes change. The harp melody (c121) and the episode's wind chord (c104) stay as they are.
```
% sB.ily sBHarpR 73-80: two-bar slurs; swell to C6 (74) and onto B flat 5 (79)
a''4(\p\<^\markup \italic "espress." f''4 a''4 | c'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> bes''8 a''8) | g''4(-\tweak stencil ##f \p d''4 g''4 | bes''2 a''8 g''8) | f''4(\< c''4 f''4 | a''2 g''8 f''8) | e''4( g''4 bes''4-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | a''2.)-\tweak stencil ##f \p |
% sB.ily sBClOne 81-88: phrase slurs, portato on the repeated quavers
d''4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a'8-- a'-- d''4 | f''2 e''8 d''8 | bes'4) g'8--( g'-- bes'4 | a'2.) | a'4( f'8-- f'-- a'4 | c''2\> b'8 bes'8 | a'4)\pp fis'8--( fis'-- dis'4 | e'2.) |
```
The slurs end where the old ones ended (83 beat 1, 87 beat 1). At 87 Cl 2 enters pp with Cl 1's pp, as before. The part-combiner now splits the staff one bar earlier, at 85 ("Clarinets one" track), and the print is clean (page 3).

### 3. The trio tune's first two statements end on two-bar held notes with no taper, at the hand-overs to the next voice (bars 167–168, 183–184). Applied.

**Problem.** Cl 1 holds E5 for two bars (167–168) while the violins take the tune at 169. The violins hold B5/B4 for two bars (183–184) before Ob 1's solo call at 185. Both notes are flat to their end. In v138's MIDI the clarinet even drops to pp at 166, because of the bare `\!` (Finding 1). The third statement already ends with a diminuendo (223–224), and so does the coda's Hn 1 (369–376).

**Evidence.** Casanelles: the step from one note to the next shapes the start of the next note as well (PDF 156). Long notes that change in dynamics carry more expression than static ones ("evolutions", PDF 160). At a hand-over, the held note should give way so the new voice enters without being covered.

**Fix (applied).** tA.ily: `e''2.~\> | e''2) r4\!` in `tAClOne`, `b''2.~\> | b''2 r4\!` in `tAVlnOne`, and `b'2.~\> | b'2 r4\!` in `tAVlnTwo`. Cl 2 rests, so the shared staff has one hairpin.

### Checked and left as they are

- Accents in the bass 105–120 and 305–320: accented Cb/Cbsn strokes under tenuto low brass are Casanelles' attack-over-body layering (PDF 146). They give the brass downbeats weight without a brass attack, as Dan's rule requires.
- The codetta solos 225–236 (Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1 at p, with no character word): they are a level over pp strings, which is enough (PDF 290).
- A +20 accent at p (bar 14: 73) is about one and a half levels. That is within reason, and Dan's accent rule is the renderer's business.
