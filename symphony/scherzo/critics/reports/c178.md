## Findings

### 1. The trio tune's phrase-2 peak note has no shape (bars 158, 174, 214, 346) - applied

Problem: c122 made the high note in bar 6 of the trio tune (F-sharp5 in Cl 1, F-sharp6 in the violins, B5 in Ob 1 at 346) a half note, the longest and highest note of phrase 2. c038 later gave the phrase-3 peak a swell, but this note sits flat at the surrounding level, so the line levels off right where it should lean. This happens in all four statements: Cl 1 at 158, violins at 174, Cl 1 and violins at 214, and Ob 1 at 346.

Evidence: Adler's woodwind solo examples shape exactly this kind of held peak with a hairpin pair: the Brahms 4/iv flute solo (PDF 186), with <> on each group; the Brahms 1/iv flute (188), with <f> on the long note; the Hebrides clarinet (212); and the Tchaikovsky 6 bassoon (227), with pp<> p<> mp<>. In every one of these examples, a held note at the top of the phrase gets a dynamic shape; none of them is left flat.

Fix: a crescendo into the note, then a decrescendo back to a hidden target at the current level. The printed shape changes, but the MIDI velocity does not.
```
cis''4\< fis''2-\tweak stencil ##f \p\> | e''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p dis''8 cis''4
```
I used the current level at each place: p for tAClOne (158) and codaOboeOne (346, with `fis''4\< b''2`), mp for tAVlnOne/tAVlnTwo (174, slur parentheses kept), and f for tCClOne/tCVlnOne/tCVlnTwo (214).

The first trio statement is protected by c089 so that it keeps a "plain harmony as reference". That reason concerns harmony. A hairpin changes no pitch or chord, so the reason still holds.

### 2. The codetta hand-over solos have no character word (bars 225, 229, 233) - applied

Problem: the trio tune's head passes from Ob 1 (225) to Cl 1 (229) to Bsn 1 (233). Each solo starts at p with a slur, but no character word. Every other wind statement of the tune has one: 153 dolce, 185 and 205 espress., 189 dolce, 345 and 349 dolce. So these are the only exposed solos in the trio left without character.

Evidence: Adler's solo examples always pair the first dynamic with a character word: p dolcissimo (186), légèrement et expressif (187), p cantabile (199), mf espressivo (205), cantabile (208), dolce (212), pp dolce espr. (219). On the bassoon (226) he warns that an accompanied bassoon solo is easily swallowed, and the word tells the player to sing out within p. The accompaniment at 233-236 is already marked down, so the bassoon is in good shape for this.

Fix:
```
tCOboeOne:  gis''2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a''4
tCClOne:    gis'2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a'4
tCBsnOne:   gis2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a4
```
"dolce" is a character word, not a tempo word, so no `\mt` is needed.
