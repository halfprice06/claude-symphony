## Findings

### 1. T's last cadence in the cello statement: Vln II sounds the resolution a semitone under Vln I's suspended B4. Applied.
Bars: 182 (tA.ily, tAVlnTwo).

Problem: at 181–182 T ends its second statement in B major. The cellos hold B3 and Vln I holds B4 (since c461) over the F♯ bass, as a cadential 4–3 that resolves to A♯ on beat 2. The muted Vln II arpeggio was `r8 fis'8( ais'8 cis''8 e''8 cis''8)`, so its A♯4 came on the second quaver of beat 1. That is the tone of resolution, a semitone below Vln I's held B4 in the same register and the same string colour, one quaver before the resolution.

Evidence:
- PDF 352 §12 and Ex. 21-12a–c: sounding the resolution early "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution".
- PDF 350 §8: the cadential suspension exists to stress the leading tone, and here the leading tone A♯ is exactly the note sounded early.
- Here the mute is the only contrast. The case is the same as c421's fix at 176, two phrases earlier in this statement, where the old B4 came one quaver before the cellos' A♯3 resolved. c461's Vln I doubling came after c421 and recreated the problem at 182.

Fix (applied): `r8 fis'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 ais'8)`. The pitches (F♯–C♯–E, V7 of B), the rhythm, the slur and the register are unchanged. A♯ now sounds on the last quaver, after the cellos and Vln I have resolved, as at 176.

This is the change in this reading that a listener hears most. The trio is pp–p here, and the clash was a minor second between the two violin sections at the cadence of the second statement.

### 2. The tutti theme's one dissonant suspension has its resolution tone in Hn 1. Applied.
Bars: 111 (sC.ily, sCHnOne) and 311 (dC.ily, dCHnOne).

Problem: in both tuttis the tenor countermelody (Hn 3·4, Bsn 1, Vc) holds C4 over the bar into the V chord (111.1), a 9–8 that resolves to B3 on beat 2. c112 made it; c198 called it "the movement's one dissonant suspension inside the tutti theme". Hn 1, on the same staff family, struck B4 on beat 1. That is the resolution tone a major seventh above the suspension, sounded before it resolves.

Evidence:
- PDF 352 §12. A 9–8 may have its resolution tone in the bass (Ex. 21-12d, "good"), and the bass B is fine. An upper voice in the same colour may not (Ex. 21-12a, c).
- c082 already applied this reasoning at 116/316, where Hn 1 plays F♯4 on beat 1 and B4 on beat 3 under the ♭9 C6.
- At 111 the trumpets' motive-x B4 stays. Its staccato rhythm and different colour are the "marked contrast" case of PDF 352, and in 311 the trumpets rest anyway.

Fix (applied): `fis'4-- r4 b'4-.` at 111 and 311 (was `b'4-- r4 b'4-.`).
- F♯4 is the chord's 5th and lies above Hn 2's D♯4, so the parts do not cross.
- B4 arrives on beat 3, after the resolution, exactly as at 116.
- The pattern now matches the two bars of each tutti that carry a dissonance (111 and 116).

### 3. Second idea (45–52, 121–128, 277–284): the ties stay consonant. Decided: no change.
Each tied note falls on beat 2 of the hemiola's 3/2 (PDF 362, Ex. 21-31), and each is consonant with the bass:
- B over G, E over E, C♯ over A, F♯ over D.

That is why the idea has less pull than T (principle 5). I tried three bass-only rewrites so that the melody could stay as it is:
- **D held under the E6 at 47.3:** E becomes a 9th whose resolution D6 arrives over the tritone leap D–G♯. This is not a suspension figure, and it breaks the bass sequence.
- **F♯ under 48.1:** the E6 becomes a 7th, but its resolution falls over the moving G♯. The result is 7–♭5, and the protected G♯–A rise (c432) is lost.
- **E held under the F♯6 at 51.3:** the A7 becomes a sus chord whose Cl 1 D5 must rise. This is worse voice leading than the present V/V–V–V6/5/V–V.

The ties at 45–46 and 49–50 cannot become dissonant suspensions. Each is followed by the rising sixth, so the line cannot resolve down by step (PDF 348). The reading supports c421's conclusion: the idea's memorability is a matter of its frame, not of its figuration.

### 4. 199–200: the call's G♯5 sounds over T's suspended A4. Not applied (protected; mitigated).
At 200.1, Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I land on G♯5 while Cl 1, Vln II, the violas and Hn 1 hold T's A4 over the G♯ bass. This is the same rule as Finding 1. But the two lines differ in colour and dynamics (call mp/mf, T f), which is the "much less problematic" case of PDF 352.

The cure I tested on paper was the call holding C♯6 into 200 as a 4–3 to B♯5. That would make a double suspension with T in parallel 10ths. But PDF 348 says a suspension "brings into prominence the voice in which it occurs". It would therefore push the call up against T, which c404 ("T is the principal voice at 197–200, the call stays a level under it") and c503 ("197–200 is now settled") both rule out for a clear reason. Kept.

### 5. The trio middle calls 185–188 and 189–192: a cadential 4–3 was considered and rejected.
On paper, `a'4.( b'8 cis''4~ | cis''4 bis'2)` at 187–188 would be a textbook V4–3 onto C♯ minor's leading tone (PDF 350 §8). I rejected it for three reasons:
- The call's B♯ would be followed at once by B major at 189 (harp pedal B♮, cellos' D♯3), a cross relation that the present G♯ (common to both chords) avoids.
- The answer at 191–192 would need Cl 1's A♯4 entry at 192 moved, because it would sound the resolution early (c477 protects that entry).
- c021 gave the calls their own rhythm so that the trio's middle does not repeat T's. With T's bars 3–4 and 7–8 now tied, a tied call would make every trio phrase end the same way.

### 6. T bar 12 (164, 180, 220, 352): no change.
After the anticipated A5 peak, the line drops a fourth (F♯5–C♯5) and walks down in crotchets over ii6 into the cadential 6/4. PDF 358 says suspensions suit falling lines. But I found no way to hold the F♯5 over the bar (a consonant 6th over A) that still reaches G♯4 at bar 13 without losing B–A–G♯, the echo of T's first phrase. The line follows principle 4 and is left.
