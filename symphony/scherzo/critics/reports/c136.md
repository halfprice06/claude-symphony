## Findings

### 1. Bars 72–73: the horns' ff>pp is split over two attacks, and the pp C5/A4 is a new tongued attack (applied)

**Problem.** At the stretto's ff arrival Hn 1 plays C♯5 and Hn 2 A4 (written G♯5/E5) with a diminuendo through bar 72. At 73 both attack again at pp: Hn 1 on C5, Hn 2 on the same A4. The A is not tied, although c010's comment says Hn 2 "holds A across 72–73, the common tone". So two horns that have just played ff high in the bright register have to make a fresh pp attack, unaccompanied by strings, on the first beat of the F-major episode.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 284: soft, controlled attacks in the high register are "fiendishly difficult, because of the firmness of the lips and the velocity of the breath required". Ex. IX-9 (PDF 290) makes the sff>pp decay on one sustained note, not on a new one. PDF 309: everything under one slur is played on one breath, so the slur C♯→C also takes the breath decision away from the player.

**Fix (applied).** sB.ily:
```
sBHnOne = { R2.*14 | a'4-.\f\< e'8-. e'-. a'4-. | cis''2.(\ff\> | c''2.~)\pp | c''2 r4 | ...
sBHnTwo = { R2.*14 | a'4-.\f\< e'8-. e'-. a'4-. | a'2.~\ff\> | a'2.~\pp | a'2 r4 | ...
```
Hn 2's A now really is the held common tone, and Hn 1's C♯→C, the cross relation that c042 gave to one voice, is a slurred semitone with no new attack. Dynamics on the shared staff are still equal (ff> then pp in both). The reprise (dB 300) has a rest after the ff and needs no change.

### 2. Bar 281: an accent on Hn 2 in the soft second-idea line (applied)

**Problem.** In the reprise's second idea Hn 2 carries the tune in the tenor with the cellos (c071), starting `c'2->\<(` at 281. It is the only accent on any brass note in the movement. The phrase begins sub. p at 277 under tenutos and slurs, and Hn 1 above it on the same staff has a tenuto (`c''2.--(\<`). The accent asks for a hard-tongued attack in the middle of a legato line.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 309 separates hard ("tu") from soft or legato ("da") tonguing; his example of soft tonguing is a lyrical horn line (Ex. X-24, Tchaikovsky 5/ii). PDF 312: the horn's middle register has a "velvetlike" quality. Dan's rule in BRIEF.md (c123): brass accents soft, weight not bite, with brass shaped by dynamics and doubling.

**Fix (applied).** dA.ily, `dAHnTwo`: `c'2->\<( a4~` becomes `c'2--\<( a4~`. The strings keep their accent. Notes and dynamics are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 104→105 and 304→305: Hn 3·4 go from a ff held chord straight into a 5-bar ff slur with no breath (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 3·4 crescendo from p to ff on held dotted minims at 101–104 (301–304), then begin the tenor countermelody at 105 (305) under a slur of five bars, ff, without a rest. Hn 3 then plays on with no full bar of rest to 132: 32 bars, about 22 seconds, with two beats of rest at 120. The only planned breath is c046's mark at 116. A breath at 104/105 has to be stolen from the arrival chord, and nothing tells the two players to take it at the same place.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292: brass need much more wind than woodwinds, "allow frequent intervals of rest"; "in a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath". PDF 309: a slur means one breath. The low-middle register these horns use (G3–E4 sounding) takes the most air.

**Fix (applied).** A breath mark at the end of 104 and 304 in both horns, so the shared staff shows one comma (it takes no time):
```
sB.ily  sBHnThree … | b2.\ff \breathe | }      sBHnFour … | fis2.\ff \breathe | }
dB.ily  dBHnThree … | b2.\ff \breathe | }      dBHnFour … | fis2.\ff \breathe | }
```
The horns end the arrival chord a moment early. The rest of the orchestra holds it, so nothing goes missing from the sound.

There are no further findings. The rest of the brass writing follows the reading. Ranges and registers match Adler's charts, the trumpets stay in their clearest register, the trombones and tuba move one note per bar and rest most of the movement, the tuba's low E1 is used only in slow notes, there are no stopped notes, glissandi or trills, and the horn players get rests before every exposed soft entry (141, 241, 341, 357).
