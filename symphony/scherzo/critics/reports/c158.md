## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 205–208: the motto tail anticipated in the trio sits at the level of its own pad. Applied.
**Problem.** At 205–208 Fl 2 and Cl 2 play G♯–A–B–A♯–A in octaves, reaching G♯ at 209. This is the motto's chromatic tail, anticipated here since c023 so that the close at 357 is a return. It is the only moving line in these four bars. Fl 2 is marked pp and Cl 2 p, so the octave doubling is unbalanced, and Fl 2's G♯5–B5 is the flute's soft register anyway. Around them the violas hold F♯4 after an unmarked diminuendo, the cellos hold B2 at p and the basses play pizz. at p. So the primary line is no louder than its background, and it has no expressive marking. The same line at 357 has one, since c058.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the ear must be directed "to the primary element and not diverted or confused by an overbusy background". PDF 14: the background sits a level under the soloist. PDF 210 (Ex. 277): "Even in soft, subtle background passages, your lead line should have some character." c058 made this same correction for the motto at 357–360.
**Fix (applied, tB.ily).**
```
tBFluteTwo 205: gis''2(\p^\markup \italic "espress." a''4 | ...      (was \pp)
tBClTwo    205: gis'2(\p^\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | ...
tBVla      203–205: dis'2.\> | fis'2. | fis'2.\pp | ...             (was \! with no level)
tBVc       203–205: b,2.\> | b,2. | b,2.\pp | ...
tBCb       203–205: b,,2.\> | b,,2\upbow r4 | b,,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." ...
```
The crescendo into 209 at 207 is unchanged in every part. No notes change. The Fl 1·2 and Cl 1·2 staves carry only the second players here (the firsts rest), so the equal-dynamics rule for shared staves holds.

### 2. Bars 177–184: the cello countermelody stays flat while the tune swells over it. Applied.
**Problem.** In the second trio statement the violins swell to T's climax (177–179 < mf >, 181–182 < >, 183 >). The cellos' countermelody under them (E–G♯–E–A–G♯–E…) is a flat p for all 16 bars and does not taper at 183, though the tune does (c138). Since c157 the same countermelody at 209–224 rises and falls with the tune, one level under it. So the two statements now disagree, and the quieter statement is the one without shape.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 269 (PDF 205) and Ex. 273 (PDF 208): the counter-line has its own hairpins, in step with the lead's (lead pp<mf>, violas p<mf>, cellos p<mf>). PDF 207: the counter-melody "lends the right amount of melodic interest" by carrying one bar into the next. Dvořák 9/iii marks his cello counter-line the same way (lines 667–670 above).
**Fix (applied, tA.ily `tAVc`).** The hidden targets keep the MIDI to one step (c138):
```
... | e4(\< gis2~ | gis4 e2) | a2(-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> gis4 | e2.-\tweak stencil ##f \p) | b,2( dis4 | cis2 e4) | dis2.~\> | dis2 r4\! | }
```
The swell peaks at 179 with the tune's A6, a level under the violins' mf, and the cellos fade with the tune at 183.

### 3. Bars 225–236: the codetta's three statements of T's head are flat p. Applied.
**Problem.** Ob 1 (225), Cl 1 (229) and Bsn 1 (233) each play T's head G♯–A–B–C♯–B (C♮ in the bassoon, the iv colour of c043) over held pp strings and the thinning harp (c153). Each statement rises to its third bar and falls back. None carries a hairpin, and every other statement of T in the movement has a swell (c038, c099). Played as written, the three entries are flat p, four bars each, and the twelve bars do not move from one entry to the next.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18): each wind entry has its own < > arc (flutes mp<mf>mp). Ex. 269 (PDF 205): every line rises to its peak and falls. PDF 207: "keep something going … to carry one bar into the next." c153 thinned the harp here for the same reason.
**Fix (applied, tC.ily).** Ob 1, Cl 1 and Bsn 1, the same in each:
```
gis''2(\p a''4 | b''2.\< | cis'''2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | b''2-\tweak stencil ##f \p) r4     % Ob 1, 225–228
gis'2(\p a'4 | b'2.\< | cis''2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | b'2-\tweak stencil ##f \p) r4          % Cl 1, 229–232
gis2(\p a4 | b2.\< | c'2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | b2-\tweak stencil ##f \p) r4                 % Bsn 1, 233–236
```
The accompaniment stays pp, so each entry stays one level above it. The fade into the poco rit. at 237 is still done by the harp thinning and the register falling (c153).

### 4. Bars 185–192: the pizzicato bass is at the oboe solo's level. Applied.
**Problem.** At 185 Ob 1 plays the trio call p espress., then the cor anglais and cellos answer p dolce at 189. The harp's left hand plays the bass at pp (c099). The double basses play the same notes pizz. with no new mark, so they carry the p from 169. The bass is therefore at the soloist's level, and a level above the harp it doubles. c148 set the pizz. bass under the trio's solos to pp at 153 and 341, but missed 185.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 14: the rhythm section is the tertiary level, under the soloist and the background. c099's rule: the accompaniment is a level under the tune.
**Fix (applied, tB.ily).** `tBCb = { cis,4\pp r4 r4 | ...` The arco entry at 193 keeps its printed p.
