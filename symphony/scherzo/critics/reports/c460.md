## Findings

### 1. Applied. 358–360: the cor anglais, not Bsn 1, carries the inner 6–♭6–5 line under the motto tail

**Problem.** Movements II, III and IV each close a motto-tail statement over the same inner line, 6–♭6–5 of the key:
- II 87–89: F–F♭–E♭, in the cor anglais.
- III 358–360: C♯–C–B, in the violas with Bsn 1.
- IV 273–275: A–A♭–G, in winds and brass.

The cor anglais is movement II's signature colour: theme B is the C.a.'s (II 41–44), and the finale quotes it at IV 211–218. It is also the colour of II's own close. In the scherzo, the corresponding bars give the line to a bassoon. A cyclic recall can rest on colour as well as pitch, and here the scherzo has the pitch pattern but not the colour.

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 11: "to achieve maximum effectiveness from a group of instrumental colors, the arranger must learn to distribute them judiciously throughout his score". His example keeps a colour for the moment its entrance means most.
- In this movement the C.a. has rested since 339, so an entry at 358 is fresh.
- Sebesky PDF 13–14 (focus): the C.a. enters pp under Hn 1's p espress. It stays at the secondary level, a level below the horn, as II's C.a. sits under its Cl 1.
- Hn 1's notes, the harmony E – A♯°7 – iv6/4 – E and the do-not-touch "single horn line" (c060) are unchanged.

**Fix (coda.ily).**
```
codaCorAnglais = { ... e'4-> r4 r4 | R2. | R2.*17 | cis'2.(\pp | c'2. | b2.) | R2.*16 | }
codaBsnOne: 357-360  gis2.\pp | cis'2. | c'2. | b2.  ->  R2. | R2. | R2. | R2.
```
- At 357 the chord stays complete: E3 in Bsn 2, E4 in the violas, G♯4 in Vln II, B4 in Hn 1.
- The C.a. enters at 358 on C♯4, written G♯4. That is its warm lower-middle register, well clear of the low B (Adler range).
- It stops at 360, so the wind chord at 361 keeps c377's rule of no wind–string unisons.

**Parts.** The C.a. rests 18 bars before 358, so parts-cues.json "05" gains `["cueHnOne","Hn. 1","UP",357,1]`. The cue shows Hn 1's B4 at 357; Hn 1 sounds in that bar. No other cue points at Bsn 1 in 357–360: Bsn 1's only cue use is at 269, in parts 10 and 13.

### 2. Built and rejected: cutting Bsn 1 at 357–360 outright (the literal reading of Ex. 1)

Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) looks like it fits these bars: a chromatic line in the lower-middle register under a lead. I built the cut as the first candidate and rejected it for three reasons:
- The trombone line Sebesky removed was an independent voice. Bsn 1 here doubles a chord tone the violas already hold, so cutting it lightens nothing that competes.
- Bsn 1's G♯3→C♯4 at 357–358 is the only contrary motion in a passage where every other voice descends. Ex. 1's stated gain is contrary motion made more evident, and the cut would remove it.
- It would make the scherzo the only movement whose motto close has a strings-only inner line, against II (C.a. and Hn 4) and IV (winds and brass).

Finding 1 keeps a wind on the line and changes only its colour.

### 3. Do-not-touch reason strengthened: 357–360

The recorded reasons are "plagal close harmony" (c002) and "motto close" (c400). The reading of II, III and IV together gives a stronger reason. The inner 6–♭6–5 line (C♯4–C4–B3 against B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4) is the second half of a cross-movement figure: II 87–89 has it at F–F♭–E♭ and IV 273–275 at A–A♭–G, aligned the same way (6 under ♯4, ♭6 under 4, 5 under 3). Keep this line and its alignment, together with Hn 1's notes, in any later change to 357–360. The record should list it under "What the finale relies on".

### 4. Harmonic variety in theme A: checked, no change

Theme A at 13, 105 and 253 has the same bass (E E E B E A B E A G♯ A B E G B E); 305 varies 313–314 (F♯–G). Sebesky PDF 19 asks for a new harmonisation where possible. I did not apply it here, for three reasons:
- A scherzo's da capo states the theme literally; Dvořák 9/iii is the movement's model.
- The tonic bass under bar 2 is what makes F♮ sound as E's Neapolitan, and that bar is quoted at pitch by I 79 and IV 273–275.
- 105 and 253 already vary by colour (tutti with flutes a2 since c454; Vln II pizz. with Fl 1 heterophony), which is Sebesky's other kind of variety (PDF 15).

The trio tune T already gets a new bass each time (c433).
