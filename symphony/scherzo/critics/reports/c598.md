## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 49: the slur splits the 9-8 from its resolution in Cl 1 and Hn 1 (applied)

**Bars.** 48–49, second idea in G (C). Cl 1 and Hn 1 tie B4 from 48 into 49 over the new bass A (A1/A2: basses, Bsn 1, Cbsn). B4 is a 9th over A and resolves to A4 on beat 2. Hn 2's A3 and Cl 2's C♯4 supply the 3rd and the doubled bass, as Ex. 21-10 requires.

**Problem.** Both slurs end on the tied B4: Cl 1 `b'4) a'4( e''4~` and Hn 1 `b'4) a'2--(`. So each player starts a new phrase on the resolution, mid-bar. Cl 1 tongues the A4. Hn 1 tongues it and adds a tenuto on a minim, so the resolution comes out heavier than the dissonance. The tune's phrase break is at the bar line 48|49, so this break lines up with nothing else in the texture. It is the only dissonant tie in the movement whose resolution starts a new slur in the middle of a group.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 349 (sec. 1–2): preparation, suspension and resolution form one figure of two tones.
- PDF 350 (sec. 3): the resolution "should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity".
- PDF 350 (sec. 4): "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer", and suspensions bring an inner voice into prominence (Ex. 21-4, which is all inner voices).
- Ex. 21-17 (PDF 355): Mozart's slurs run from each tied 7 to its 6.

**Fix (applied, sA.ily, with a c598 comment at the top of the file).**
```
Cl 1: gis'4 b'2~ | b'4 a'4) e''4~( | e''4 e''2)      (was b'4) a'4( e''4~)
Hn 1: gis'4-- b'2--~ | b'4 a'2--) | a'2.--( | a'2.-- | a'2.--)   (was b'4) a'2--( | a'2.-- ...)
```
Cl 1's next slur now starts on the leap to E5, the natural place to tongue. Hn 1's starts at 50, where Hn 2, Cl 2 and Bsn 1 also start their groups. Notes, dynamics, hairpins and players are unchanged, so the MIDI is identical. Dan's soft-brass rule is kept: nothing is added to the horn, and its resolution simply loses an attack. I did not add a hairpin. c198 and c298 found that hairpins on these tied notes can push the layout onto extra pages, and the slur alone gives the softer resolution.

**Check.** At 160 dpi (/tmp/critic-598/cmp.png) both slurs print clearly, Cl 1·2 and Hn 1·2 stay as separate voices on their shared staves, and nothing collides.

### 2. Bars 126/282: the ♭9 C4 over B re-bowed at its resolution (no change)

The violas (and Cl 2 at 126) hold C4 over the bass B and resolve to B3 at 127/283 under a new slur with a tenuto. This differs from 49 for two reasons. First, the resolution falls on the next two-bar hemiola group's downbeat, where the tune, the bass and Cl 2 all start their own slurs. Second, the phrase is a crescendo to f at 129, so a separately bowed, growing resolution is the phrase's shape. It is Ex. 21-32's case: a suspension that is rhythmically strong (on the bass change) though metrically weak (PDF 363). Hn 4 already slurs C4–B3 as one figure there. No change.

### 3. Codetta 225–236: tying T's C♯ into the tonic bar as a 6-5 (rejected)

This was the largest audible candidate. Ob 1 (227–228), Cl 1 (231–232) and Bsn 1 (235–236, C♮) would hold the 6th into the E bar (`cis'''2.~ | cis'''4 b''2`). The codetta's T fragments would then end with T's own tied sigh from bars 3–4, which is PDF 346's point about rhythm individualizing a line. I rejected it for two reasons from the reading:
- At 228, Vln II's B4 sounds the tone of resolution under Ob 1's held C♯6 (Ex. 21-12, PDF 352: "poor", and the contrast exception is weak at pp).
- At 236, Bsn 1's held C3 would sound against the harp's beat-1 G♯4–B4, an unprepared dissonance among the upper parts (PDF 368).

Changing it in Cl 1 alone would break the three-step relay that c593 protects.

### 4. Coda 329–332: a 7-6 chain against the outer 10ths (not applicable)

The outer voices move in parallel 10ths: the tail G–F♯–F–E–D♯ over the bass E–D–C–B. That is the frame of Ex. 21-39, where 7-6 suspensions run in the middle voice. A 7-6 at 330 needs C over E at 329, which makes a C-major 6/3 (a cyclic rule). A 4-3 at 330 sounds its F♯ early in the top voice (Ex. 21-12). The passage keeps Mozart's "cresc." shape (p< into ff) without the chain.
