## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 54–55, 130–131, 286–287: the second idea's cadential suspension has no shading (applied)

**Problem.** c441 rewrote the cadence of every second-idea statement as a hemiola with a tied 2: `b''2 a''4~ | a''4 g''2` (G major) and `g''2 fis''4~ | fis''4 e''2` (E minor). The tune's 2 is attacked on beat 3 and held over the bar line. On the downbeat the bass arrives on the tonic (G at 55, E at 131 and 287), which makes the held note a 9–8. At 55 Cl 1 and Hn 1 also have C5 as a 4–3 over it. This bar is the cadence's dissonant peak. The notes were changed and the marks were not: all three statements run at a flat f from 53 to 56, with no hairpin and no level. Every other melodic suspension in the movement is shaded: T's 4–3 and 7–8 at 155–156, 159–160, 165–166 and 199–200 swell into the dissonant downbeat and fall to the resolution (c348 row 13, c398). Hn 1's 9–8 at 216 has its diminuendo. In the MIDI the tied A at 54.3 has the same velocity (83) as the notes around it.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 51 and Ex. 3-6. A note begun on a weak beat and held through the strong one is the strongest syncopation, "because the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation". Beethoven marks the syncopated attack (*sf*) while the left hand gives the downbeat. 54–55 has exactly that layout: the tune ties over and the bass gives the downbeat.
- PDF 55, Ex. 3-16 to 3-18. A suspension comes from that syncopation, and its dissonance falls on the strong beat. The emphasis belongs there, and the resolution is the weaker event. A flat level gives the dissonant 55.1 and the consonant 55.2 the same weight.
- PDF 51–52, Ex. 3-8 and Ex. 3-7. A hemiola and a temporary metre are projected by the shaping as well as by the notes. Brahms gives each contrary group its own hairpin, and the second idea's phrases 45–52 already have one swell each. The cadence is the one group of the second idea without one.
- This does not add accents. c248 and c358 rejected accents here because the passage is legato espressivo. A swell is not an accent, and it does not change c085's bowing (separate bows for B, A~A, G). It answers c298's "hemiola 4–3s unmarked": that point was about inner voices, and the tune itself carries the suspension only since c441.

**Fix (applied).** In each voice that carries the tune, the hemiola's first minim gets a crescendo to the tied downbeat and a diminuendo to the resolution. Hidden levels close each hairpin for the MIDI (c134's rule):
- sA.ily (54–55), Vln I: `b''2\< a''4~ | a''4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\> g''2-\tweak stencil ##f \f`. Vln II likewise an octave lower. Fl 1 and Ob 1 use the same shape at their own level, `\<` … `-\tweak stencil ##f \f\>` … `-\tweak stencil ##f \mf`.
- sC.ily (130–131): Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1 and Ob 1 with the same hairpins (`fis''4-\sysLabel "1."-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>` on the wind staves).
- dA.ily (286–287): Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Vc, and Cl 1 and Cl 2 (one shared staff, so both get the same hairpins; Cl 1 has its own 4–3 G–A–G). Hn 2 doubles the cellos and gets the printed hairpins with its level kept at f (hidden `\f` at both ends). It swells inside its level and is not pushed louder (brief: brass weight, not bite).
- A `% c448` comment is at the top of sA.ily, sC.ily and dA.ily.

Result: on each page (2, 5–6, 9) the hairpins sit under bars 54–55, 130–131 and 286–287 without collisions. In the MIDI the syncopated attack rises from v83 to v88 at 54.67, 130.67 and 286.67. No notes changed.

### 2. 45–52 (and 121–128, 277–284): accents on the second idea's syncopated minims. Considered, kept

The ties at 45.3, 47.3, 49.3 and 51.3 are syncopations of the PDF 51 type. In Dvořák's E-minor scherzo every hemiola minim carries ">". But Dvořák's passage is ff marcato after a tutti, and this one is f espressivo legato. The swell already peaks on the third minim's attack (G6 at 46, A6 at 50), which is a register accent (PDF 50). The bass plays the 3/2 and the horns keep the bar, as in Ex. 3-7. c358's reason (accents would turn the line marcato) stands. No change.

### 3. 89–97: timpani answers on the third bar of each three-bar group. Kept

In Ex. 3-12 the group starts are shown by entries and pedalling. Here the head enters on each group's first bar (89, 92, 95) and the long accented minim follows on its second bar, so the three-bar period and its phase are both audible. The drum's motive x on the third bar (c327) fills the empty bar as an answer. No change.

### 4. 370: "rit." on the second bar of the group 369–372. Kept

A tempo change at a group's strong bar would follow PDF 53 more literally. But moving the rit. to 369 or 373 either crowds the "tutti, change bow freely" text (c035) or lets the last motive x at 371 (the finale's quotation) pass at tempo. The closing page is protected, and c348 kept it for the same reason. No change.

### 5. 52.3: the anticipating quaver on its own bow. Kept

PDF 56 says anticipations are unaccented. Taking the quaver on a fresh up-bow gives it a slight attack. c218 did this so that the hemiola starts down-bow at 53, and in 48 the quaver stays inside the slur. The bowing reason holds. No change.

### 6. Hypermetre scan of all printed dynamics and of the MIDI. Follows
