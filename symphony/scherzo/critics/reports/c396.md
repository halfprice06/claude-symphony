## Findings

### 1. The bass clarinet is not used in the trio, the retransition, the reprise or the closing page (bars 149-328); give it the dominant pedal at 245-252 (applied)
**Problem.** The instrument rests for 180 bars, more than two minutes. Adler's rule for the whole family is type-casting: a great orchestrator gives each instrument and register the part that sounds best on it and could not be played as well by anything else (PDF 184). For the bass clarinet that part is a soft sustained bass in the chalumeau, its "most distinctive, warmest and most effective" register, "mysterious and shadowy" (PDF 217). The clarinet family keeps pp and the niente attack in every register (PDF 175, 211), where the bassoon cannot play pp in its lowest fifth (PDF 226). The retransition (241-252) asks for exactly this sound, and it has no sustained bass. From 246 to 252 the bass is the pizzicato B on beat 1 in cellos and basses, under timpani motive x on B, while E major turns back to E minor and the dominant prepares the reprise. The bassoons' pp chord (243-245, c152) ends on B2-B3 at 245 and nothing holds the B after it.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 217 and Example VII-74 (Wagner, *Götterdämmerung* I/3: the bass clarinet enters pp under the clarinet's line and holds it). PDF 175 and 211 (clarinet pp in every register; niente). The scherzo already uses the bass clarinet this way at 73-89 and 141-148 (c036 moved the link's pedal from Bsn 2 to it for this reason). Handing over on a common note follows Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 125-126, as c213 did at 330.
**Fix (applied in retr.ily).**
```
retrBassCl = { R2.*4 | b,2.~\pp | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~\< | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,4\p r4 r4 | }
```
B2 is written C♯4 for the B♭ instrument, in the chalumeau. It enters at 245 on Bsn 2's B2, which stops there. It crescendos pp<p from 249 to 252 with Cl 2's F♯4, the timpani and the basses (c108). It lasts about 6 s at 2.=76-88, so one breath is enough. It adds no new harmony: it is the V pedal that 245-252 already implies, under the E-minor head entries (Cl 1 245, Vln I 249) and the V7 at 251-252. The B.cl. staff now shows on the system 233-252, and the page count is still 13.

### 2. Cl 2 holds 193-208 with no rest and no breath mark; add a breath mark at the end of 196 (applied)
**Problem.** At 2.=76, Cl 2 plays sixteen bars of held notes without a rest (12.6 s): F♯4 193 through the D♯4 tied over 203-208. Adler: unslurred notes are tongued, but the player still needs a place to breathe. The composer has to supply rests or relief in long sustained passages (PDF 176, 188, 192). The score marks breaths for every comparable wind line (Cl 1 160/216, Bsn 1 104/116, Bsn 2 and Cbsn 216, c216 do-not-touch), but not for this one. The natural place is the end of 196: Cl 1's slur ends there, Bsn 1 has a crotchet rest, and 197 starts the f phrase.
**Fix (applied in tB.ily).** `dis'2. \breathe | e'2.~\f` in tBClTwo. The comma prints once on the shared clarinet staff, where Cl 1's phrase also ends. After the breath, the longest stretch is 197-208, 9.5 s, falling from f to pp.
