## Findings

### 1. 277–288: the reprise is an unrelieved run of minor keys. Open the second idea in E major and turn it minor. Applied.

**Problem.** From the Tempo I at 253 to the G.P. at 340 (about 60 s) the key areas are E minor (253–288), G minor (289–296), E minor (305–339). The only major sonorities are a D7 at 273–275 and the stretto's goal A at 297–300 (4 bars). The first part had G major (37–56) and F major (73–88). The second idea at 277 repeats its E-minor form from 121 almost note for note, so the idea gets no new colour after the trio. The join 288→289 also takes G minor (III♭ of the reprise's key) straight from E minor.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 596: in minor, the major tonic is one of the two normal expanded altered areas, and composers "avoid an unrelieved succession of minor chords, the sonority of the minor triad creating more tension than that of the major."
- PDF 594 (Waldstein): a recapitulation can take a colour the exposition lacked; Beethoven's second theme "begins in A major, changes to A minor, and moves to V". That is the same shape: major, then the minor of the same root, then on.
- PDF 596 §13: a double-mixture key is best approached "in stages", first changing the major to the minor. E major → E minor → G minor is that path (G minor is ♭III♭ of E major, the relation of A major to C minor in Haydn's *Creation*, PDF 596–597). Haydn's integrating pitch, D♯ = E♭, has its counterpart here: the trio's A♯–B (T at 160, 176, 216; the motto tail at 207) becomes the fugato subject's B♭ at 290.
- PDF 585 and 600–601: the movement's motivic pair is G♯/G (1–7, 145, 241, 357–360), as F♮/F♯ is the Hammerklavier's. The new inflection G♯ (277–280) → G (283) states it once more inside the reprise.
- Dvořák 9/iii: E major at 125–175 and again at 435–485 in the da capo.

**Fix (scratch, sections/dA.ily).** Bars 277–280 change G to G♯; bar 280 becomes V6/5 of iv: the existing D♮ quaver in Fl 1 and Vln I/II is its seventh, G♯2 its leading tone in the bass only, and the inner voices move to E4/B4 so the leading tone is not doubled. 281–282 (iv, C♮) and 283 (i, Cl 1 G4) take the music back to minor; 285–288, the hemiola cadence and 289 are unchanged.

### 2. 341–376: Exercise 4 does not support a slow coda; the slow stage is kept for its own reasons. Record correction, no change.

c293 cited Exercise 4 (PDF 606) as the model for the 36-bar slow stage. The image shows that the exercise turns to the parallel major only in its last three bars and ends "in tempo", with no rit. It supports the pp E-major ending, not the change of tempo. The slow stage still has stronger reasons than that: the strong IV before I (PDF 595, Ex. 32-14), the K. 542 motive as the goal (Finding 3), the plagal close and the G♯ the finale takes over (do-not-touch). Cutting 341–348 would begin T in the middle of its tune at 349; cutting 349–356 would remove the coda's only violin statement (c264, c371). Decision: keep, and correct the recorded reason.

### 3. 205–208 and 357–360: keep the spelling A♯ in the motto tail. No change.

c152 respelt 38 as B–B♭–A–G, under A&S's rule that a descending chromatic passing tone is a lowered note (PDF 497). That rule should not spread to the motto tail. Ex. 32-22a (PDF 602) shows Mozart's own figure in E major, spelt B–A♯–A♮–G♯. Only the enlarged line of 32-22c, which passes through G minor, has B♭, and A&S describe it as the motive "with the A♯ expressed as a B♭". The A♯ also belongs to the E-major A♯°7 at 358 and to the trio's A♯–B. Decision: keep A♯ at 207 and 358; keep B♭ at 38 and 290, where the context is G major or G minor.

### 4. 331–332: ♭II is worked out by the route the reading names. Keep.

The F-major episode (73–88) is the movement's one remote area (PDF 592: its conflicts "must somehow be worked out"). The reprise leaves it out (c013), and the coda takes it up at 331: beat 1 is ♭II6/4 (Vln I F6, Vln II F5, Vla/Vc A, basses C2), beat 3 is a French sixth C–E–F♯–A♯ (Vc C3, Vln I E6, Vla F♯3, Vln II A♯4), and 332 is V. A&S PDF 593: the most natural way to lead a flat-side area to V is to turn it into an augmented sixth, and the half-step bass intensifies V. Decision: keep. This is the reason the reprise can do without the F-major episode, and it should be recorded with c013's protection.

### 5. Proportions and length: keep.

The movement lasts 278 s (4:38) between movement II and the finale. The trio (70 s) is longer than the reprise (49 s). Dvořák's reprise is as long as his scherzo, but A&S give no reason to re-lengthen the reprise, and Points for Review 5 (PDF 603) warns that added remote areas can threaten continuity. Finding 1 adds colour without adding bars. Keep the cuts made in c003 and c023, and keep the 52-bar coda.
