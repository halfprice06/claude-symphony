## Findings

No finding. Every principle in the chapter that this score uses is met, and the new material since c276 introduces no bowing, stop, pizz./arco or endurance problem. The chapter's unused techniques (harmonics, mutes, sul tasto, col legno, tremolo, glissando) would not improve this movement. Tremolo is reserved for the finale. The others are colour effects foreign to the Dvořák and Brahms models, and adding them at the soft places where they could go (the trio, the closing page) would cover protected music. The points below were tested against the reading and kept.

### Tested and kept (no change)
1. **Cellos' T, 169–184 (c344).** Tenor clef, G♯3–A4, arco after 53 beats of rest from the pizz. at 151 (PDF 54). The slurs are one or two bars (at most 1.58 s at mp–mf, PDF 39), and the hairpins sit inside single bows. No bow marks are needed (PDF 38: mark only to counteract habit).
2. **Vln II divided dyads, 169–184.** Marked div. with unis. at 193 (PDF 30). They are louré pairs on beats 2–3, with a dash per note and a slur per bow (PDF 42–43, Ex. II-30), the accompaniment use Adler names.
3. **Cellos' countermelody, 253–268 (c359).** The longest string slur in the score: 253–257, five bars, 3.41 s at pp. Adler warns against overloading the bow in slow passages and notes the shorter cello bow (PDF 39). At pp, with no hairpin, 3.4 s is well inside one cello bow, so splitting the slur gains nothing. Kept.
4. **The bow simulation gives the same list as c276.** Forced retakes: theme A's accented minims at 18/24/26/42, 110/116/118, 258/264/266/274, 310/316/318 (the preceding note is spiccato, so the bow is already off the string, PDF 43–46); 72 and 300; and the down-bow series at 333–339 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii, PDF 42, Ex. II-29). Accented downbeats on unmarked up-bows fall only at 49/125 and 103/303, all inside crescendos (PDF 38). Nothing new since c276.
5. **Pizz./arco changes.** pizz. → arco: Vc/Cb 45, 101, 253, 277 and Cb 193, 209, 358 have 5 beats (1.14–1.32 s); Vln II/Vla 273 have 4 beats (0.91 s, c175). arco → pizz. in one beat: Vc 225 and Cb 205/225 (0.26 s), each from an up-bow, which brings the hand to the frog for a quick switch (PDF 54). The basses' held B at 223–224 is the only bass under the trio's close (the harp rests there since c364), so shortening it to gain time would cost more than it saves. Adler accepts the bow-in-hand pluck when time is short (PDF 54).
6. **Breathing (PDF 25, point 6).** The longest unbroken wind phrase is Hn 1 at 205–216: the motto-tail preview (c324) runs into the trio return, about 9.5 s at p–mf. It has a slur break between 208 and 209 and a printed breath at the end of 216, which is normal horn phrase length. Cl 1's T (153–168, 12.4 s) has a printed breath at 158. Bsn 1 and Hn 3 at 101–120/301–320 have the c246 breaths at 104/304. Trumpets and trombones play at most 3 s at a time.
7. **Open strings on held notes (PDF 32–33).** Only the basses' D2 at 53 (f, cadence). No soft held note needs a vibrato substitute.
