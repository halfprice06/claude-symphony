## Findings

### 1. 101–104 and 301–304: the lower octave of the hemiola is Vln II alone, while the upper octave has Fl 1 (applied)

**Problem.** At the ritmo di quattro battute climax over the B pedal, the hemiola line B–A–F♯–D♯–B–A is played in two octaves. Vln I has the upper octave (B5–D♯6), doubled by Fl 1. Since c184 moved Ob 2 to a held D♯5, the lower octave (B4–D♯5) is Vln II alone. That octave is the register of the brass chord, which crescendos from pp to f/ff: Tpt 1 plays motive x on B4, Hn 1 holds F♯4, Hn 2 D♯4, and Tbn 1 moves F♯3–A3. Cl 1 doubles Vln II a third lower for two bars and then holds A4. So, at ff, the octave that sits inside the brass is the only one without a wind. The same layout returns at 301–304.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76: "a wind instrument progressing in unison with a stringed instrument increases the resonance of the latter and amplifies its tone".
- PDF 77 gives purpose (b): "to strengthen the resonance of the strings".
- PDF 78: when only one of two octaves is doubled, it is the lower part that the second group doubles. Here the arrangement is the reverse: the top octave has a wind and the bottom does not.
- PDF 79–80: for loud passages, Rimsky prefers all three groups in unison.
- PDF 76–77: "Violas + Ob. (Eng. horn)" is a best pair, and this line (B4–D♯5) lies where the viola and violin registers overlap.

c184's reason for taking Ob 2 off the line was the tritone A4/D♯5 between the two oboes (PDF 90). This fix does not bring that back: the oboes keep c184's F♯5/D♯5. c184 also cited PDF 52, where a wind added to a string section loses its colour. The present chapter answers that point: in a string-plus-wind unison the aim is the string's resonance, not the wind's colour (PDF 76–77, purposes b and c). The cor anglais has rested since bar 44. Its next entry is the theme's lower octave at 105 and 305, so it now arrives one phrase earlier and leads straight into that entry. c087 left this "noted" as an open point; the reading resolves it.

**Fix (applied).**
- sB.ily, new variable (parts.ily already calls `sBCorAnglais`):
```
sBCorAnglais = { R2.*44 | b'2->\f\< a'4->~ | a'4 fis'2-> | dis''2-> b'4->~ | b'4 a'2->\ff | }
```
- dB.ily:
```
dBCorAnglais = { R2.*12 | b'2->\p\< a'4->~ | a'4 fis'2-> | dis''2-> b'4->~ | b'4 a'2->\ff | }
```
- sC.ily and dC.ily: the C.a.'s first note loses its `\ff` (`e'4-. b8-. ...`). The ff is now printed at 104 and 304, and the C.a. plays on without a break, as the strings and the other woodwinds do (MOLA rule, c165).

The accents on each minim and the dynamics match the other hemiola winds (Fl 1 f< at 101, p< at 301; c148). The range is B4–D♯5, well inside the instrument. There is a breath at 108 and 308, where the theme has rests on beats 2–3.

## Noted, not changed (decided)
