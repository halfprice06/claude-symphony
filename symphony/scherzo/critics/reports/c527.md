### 1. Bars 193–196: Cl 1's answering turns lie inside Vln II's octave of the tune; Vln II rests until T at 197 (applied)

**Problem.** At 193–196 Vln I and Vln II play the rising line F♯–G♯–A–C♯–B–A–G♯–F♯ in octaves, p<. Since c477, Cl 1 answers the violas' turns (193 on A3, 195 on B3) a bar later, at 194 (C♯5–D♯5–C♯5–B4–C♯5) and 196 (B4–C♯5–B4–A4–B4). Those answers sit in exactly the octave of Vln II's line (F♯4–C♯5): at 194 a third above Vln II's held A4 and in unison with its C♯5 on beat 3; at 196 a third above G♯4–F♯4–A4, meeting its A4 on beat 3. One clarinet at p against the whole second-violin section at p, in the same register, does not come through. The answer that c477 added to make the turn pass between strings and winds is covered by a plain octave doubling. Vln II's lower octave adds nothing harmonic: Hn 1 already doubles it at 195–196 (c172), and Vln I has the line.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments." The quaver rhythm helps, but Adler says rhythmic contrast only "lessens the importance of this consideration".
- PDF 259–260 (Tchaikovsky VC, Ex. XVII-3): when the accompaniment shares the solo's range, it must not take the same register and role. Seen in the example: the solo violin's register is left to it, and the orchestral violins hold or pluck elsewhere.
- PDF 263–264 (Ex. XVII-7, 8): the colour that enters after a contrast is "very fresh". With eight bars' rest, Vln II's f entry on T at 197 becomes a new layer arriving with the forte call.
- PDF 279 (Bizet): the added octave is kept for the climax. Here the lower octave arrives at 197, where T is f, rather than being used up four bars earlier.
- Adler's own crescendo-by-entry method, as c179 used at 207: 193 Vln I alone p<, 195 Hn 1 joins on the lower octave, 197 Vln II, Cl 1 and violas on T with the call above.

### 2. Bars 61–68: the countersubject starts in octaves with each new entry (checked, kept)

The cellos' D4 at 61 and the violas' A4 at 65 double the subject's first note an octave below. A rest on beat 1 would leave each entry's first note alone. I did not build it. The countersubject's crotchets already differ in rhythm from motive x on every following beat, as PDF 259–260 asks, and it is pp against the subject's p.
