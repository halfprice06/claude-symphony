## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 29–35: the woodwind chord under the woodwind call is as loud as the call (applied)
**Problem.** The counterstatement's string-free call (Fl 1 and Ob 1 in unison, *p*, 29–30 and 33–34) carries the chromatic tail G–F♯–F–E. Under it, Cl 1·2 and Bsn 1·2 hold E-minor and A-minor chords, also at *p*. The F♮ and F♯ pass against a held E/B in the same family of colour at the same level. The reprise of the same passage (269–272) already has its held clarinets and cor anglais at *pp* under the bassoon solo, so the exposition and reprise disagree.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 132–133: the more alike the timbres of melody and basis, the harsher the passing notes sound. Between wood-wind and brass, and so all the more within the woodwind, "the harmonic basis generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design, and should be of inferior dynamic power". Here the octave is kept (G4 under G5), but the dynamic is not. The strings in this score already work this way at 13 (*pp* off-beats under the *p* theme).
**Fix.** sA.ily bar 17 (= 29): `g'2.~\pp` (Cl 1), `e'2.~\pp` (Cl 2), `b2.~\pp` (Bsn 1), `e2.~\pp` (Bsn 2). The existing `\<` at 37 now starts from *pp* and reaches the *f* at 41 with everyone. Shared staves keep equal dynamics.

### 2. 45–56: clarinets *f* inside the second idea, louder than the woodwind melody (applied)
**Problem.** At 45 the second idea has Vln I *f espress.*, Fl 1 and Ob 1 *mf* (Ob 1 on the lower octave, B4–G5), and Hn 1·2 *mf* sustaining the harmony (c084). Cl 1·2 hold the inner chord in the same octave as the oboe's melody (Cl 1 B4–E5), but they keep the *f* of bar 41. They are the loudest woodwind there, and they are a harmonic basis. c084 lowered the horns for this reason and left the clarinets, which c014 places inside the horns in the same role.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 133: a woodwind basis under a woodwind melody stays an octave away and at a lower dynamic. Here it is in the melody's octave and a level louder. The reprises already do this: 121 and 277 mark the clarinets *sub. p* with the whole group.
**Fix.** sA.ily bar 33 (= 45): `b'2(\mf d''4~` (Cl 1), `b2(\mf g'4~` (Cl 2). Notes unchanged (c014 kept).

### 3. 333–336: the coda climax steps from *ff* to *fff* with no crescendo in the held chords (applied)
**Problem.** The movement's single *fff* and its B6 (336) come after three bars of *ff* in which horns, Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2, bassoons, contrabassoon, cellos and basses hold their notes at a flat *ff*. The theme climbs E6–G6–B6 over them. Nothing in the sustained chords grows into the peak, so the arrival is a step, while the earlier climaxes at 72, 104 and 304 are all approached by crescendo.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: after the strings, the brass is "the group most facile in producing dynamic shades … glorifying crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". The horns' held chord at 333–335 is exactly that crescendo chord. Ex. 254 (PDF 418) brings every group to its *ff* with hairpins. A one-bar crescendo is a short one, made "by natural dynamic means" (PDF 130), so a hairpin is enough. No instruments are added.
**Fix.** coda.ily bar 11 (= 335): `\<` on the held note in Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Hn 1–4, Vc and Cb (e.g. Hn 1 `b'2. | b'2.\< | b'2.\fff`), and on beat 1 in the theme parts on shared or matching staves (Fl 1, Ob 1, C.a., Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II, Vla: `e'''4-.)\< b''8-. b''-. g'''4-.`), each ending at the existing `\fff` at 336. Trumpets, trombones, bass trombone, tuba and timpani are unchanged: the low brass rest at 336 and come back *fff* at 339 (c034, c086).

### 4. 249–252: the retransition's accelerando has no crescendo into Tempo I (applied)
**Problem.** At 241–252 the head enters in violas, then Vln II, then Vln I, the clarinets join at 249, and the tempo rises poco a poco. Only the timpani crescendo (to *p* at 252). Vln I enters *pp* at 249, climbs to B5 at 251 and reaches *mp leggiero* at 253 with no hairpin. The exposition's equivalent passage (89–100) builds with hairpins from 97.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: a prolonged crescendo adds instruments in the order strings, woodwind, brass. The order is here, strings then clarinets, but no part gets louder. The two need to go together.
**Fix.** retr.ily: Vln I `dis''4-.\< fis''-. a''-. | b''4-. a''-. fis''-. | dis''4-. b'-. r4\!` (250–252, into the *mp* at 253); Cl 1·2 `b'2.\pp | b'2.\< | b'2. | b'4\p r4 r4` and `fis'2.\pp | fis'2.\< | a'2. | a'4\p r4 r4`, matching the timpani's *p* at 252.

### 5. Bars 8 and 148: clarinet chords cut off at *pp* instead of fading (applied)
**Problem.** At 7–8 the clarinets (E5/G4) and Bsn 1 (B3) turn the horn call's chord to E minor and stop on beat 2 of bar 8, just before the timpani's solo. At 145–148 Cl 1·2 and bass clarinet hold the E-minor chord of the link and stop at 148, where Hn 1 already has a diminuendo and the harp enters *pp* at 149. Both chords end with a plain cut at *pp*.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 130: "Clarinets specialise in diminuendo effects and are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)". Both places are hand-overs to a new soft colour (solo timpani, then harp and pizzicato), which is where a morendo belongs. This is not c008's rejected point 8: that was 87 (now marked) and the held chord at 361–364.
**Fix.** intro.ily: `e''2.~\pp | e''2\> r4\!` (Cl 1), `g'2.~\pp | g'2\> r4\!` (Cl 2), Bsn 1 `b2\> r4\!` at 8. link.ily: Cl 1 `e'2.\> | e'2 r4\!`, Cl 2 `b2.\> | b2 r4\!`, bass clarinet `e,2.\> | e,2 r4\!` at 147–148. Notes unchanged (bars 7–8 keep no E4, c040).

Considered and not raised.
