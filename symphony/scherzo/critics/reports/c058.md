## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The second idea is 12 bars of unshaped forte, twice (45–56 and 121–132). Applied.
**Problem.** The second idea is the movement's only long legato melody in the scherzo part. From 41 to 56 the score holds a flat f for 16 bars, and 45–52 have no hairpin in any part. The tune peaks at G6 (46) and A6 (50) in Vln I/Fl 1, but nothing in the notation marks those peaks, so players will play the line terraced, at one level. The E-minor statement at 121–128 is the same. Only the reprise (277, c039) has a dynamic plan.
**Evidence.** In Sebesky Ex. 269 (PDF 205), the lead line and the counter-line each have a hairpin arc rising to the bar where the line peaks and falling after it. On PDF 207 he says such a line "still lacks enough motion to flow by itself" without help, and lists the dynamics as part of that help. Ex. 1 (PDF 12) gives every held figure its own < >. On variety, PDF 19 says not to treat a melody the same way twice. Dvořák does the same in his own E-minor scherzo: < > on each two-bar unit of a forte tune (bars 84–91 above).
**Fix (applied).** Melody parts only (Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1, Ob 1), as c038 did for the trio tune: a swell to each peak and back, ending by the downbeat of the fourth bar. "espress." goes on Vln I at 45. Horns, bass and inner parts stay at a flat f, so the hemiola against the horns' dotted minims is unchanged.
```
sAVlnOne 45: d'''2->(\f^\markup \italic "espress."\< b''4~ | b''4 g'''2\>) | fis'''2( e'''4~ | e'''4\! d'''4. e'''8) |
         49: e'''2->(\< cis'''4~ | cis'''4 a'''2\>) | g'''2( fis'''4~ | fis'''4\! e'''4. d'''8)
```
The same hairpins go in sAVlnTwo, sAFluteOne and sAOboeOne (45–52), and in sCVlnOne, sCVlnTwo, sCFluteOne and sCOboeOne (121–128, peaks E6 at 122 and F♯6 at 126). The hemiola cadences 53–55 and 129–131 are not touched. Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest in these bars, so the shared staves carry one part's hairpins.

### 2. At 357–360 the motto's chromatic tail is at the same level as its accompaniment. Applied.
**Problem.** At 357 Hn 1 plays B–A♯–A–G♯, the symphony's motto quoted at the movement's close. It is marked p. Every accompanying part is also p: Vln II, violas (unis.), Bsn 1·2, and Vc/Cb (arco at 358). All of them drop to pp at 361. The primary voice therefore has no dynamic advantage over a full string-and-bassoon chord in the same register (violas E4–B3, Vln II G♯4–E4, Hn 1 B4–G♯4). It also has no expressive marking, though it is the one long note line in the movement that carries the cyclic meaning.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13–14 says the arranger must direct the ear "to the primary element", and the chart on PDF 14 puts the background a level below the soloist. PDF 210 (Ex. 277–278) says a long-note lead line "should have some character" even in a soft passage. Ex. 284 (PDF 217–218) uses the dynamic drop of the other parts to clear the way for what matters.
**Fix (applied).** The accompaniment takes pp at its entry: Vln II and violas at 357, Bsn 1·2 at 357, Vc/Cb at 358 (`e2.\pp^\markup \italic "arco"`). The now-redundant pp at 361 is removed in Vln II, violas, Vc and Cb. Hn 1 stays p and gets `^\markup \italic "espress."`. Hn 2 keeps p because it shares Hn 1's staff (equal dynamics on a combined staff). No hairpin is added, because one would print across both horns. Hn 1's notes (do-not-touch), the harmony E – A♯°7 – iv – E and the ending from 361 are unchanged.

### 3. Solo entries carry a dynamic but no character word (73, 185, 189, 269). Applied.
**Problem.** The movement labels some solos well: Cl 1 "dolce" (81, 153), Vln I "espressivo, sul A" (169), Ob 1 and Hn 1 "dolce" (coda). But four colour entries have nothing beyond p. The first is the harp's melodic statement of the theme head at 73, which the plan calls the movement's still-unused colour. The other three are Ob 1's call in C♯ minor at 185, the cor anglais and cellos' answer in B at 189, and Bsn 1's staccato call at 269 (chosen by c041 for its humour). Without a word, players read these as plain p, and the oboe's call and the cellos' answer get the same reading.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18) gives each instrument a word at its entry ("(sweetly)", "(pretty)", "(easy)") alongside its own dynamic line. Ex. 269 (PDF 205) gives each line its own dynamic shape. PDF 204 says each line should suit its instrument's character.
**Fix (applied).**
```
sBHarpR 73:      a''4\p^\markup \italic "espress." f''4 a''4
tBOboeOne 185:   e''4(\p^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "espress." } dis''8 ...
tBCorAnglais 189, tBVc 189: dis'4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" cis'8 ...
dABsnOne 269:    e4-.\p^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "scherzando" } b,8-. ...
```
The oboe's minor-key call is "espress.", and the answer a step lower in major is the softer "dolce".
