## Findings

### 1. 137–139: end the scherzo part with plucked cellos and basses (applied)

**Bars:** 137–139 (sC.ily), with the label consequences at 149 (link.ily) and 169 (tA.ily).

**Problem:** The plan calls 133–140 "motive x in the cellos, dying away". The low strings do die away (f 133, mf 135, p 137, pp 139), but they play arco staccato the whole time. Ten bars later the basses pluck the E and B at 149 and 151, and the trio's first statement (153–168) rests on plucked basses. The close of the scherzo part therefore loses volume but keeps the same attack, while the plucked bass of the link starts on its own after nine bars of rest.

**Evidence from the reading:** Adler says pizzicato "is similar to staccato and spiccato bowing in that the sound dies away quickly", and that the bass strings ring longest (PDF 58). A plucked note dies away by itself, and that is the effect the plan asks for at 137–139. The bowed staccato has to be shortened by the player. The change of mode is safe (PDF 53–54). The cellos have three bars' rest (134–136, 2 s) to change from arco to pizz. The basses have five beats from their arco E at 135. Going from bow to fingers is the easy direction. The cellos then rest 29 bars before their arco T at 169, which is ample time. The motive x quavers at 2. = 88 (0.11 s) are well within cello pizzicato speed. The longest run, three plucked notes and a quaver pair, gives no endurance problem (PDF 57). Dvořák 9/iii does the same at the end of a dying figure (see above).

**What a listener hears:** At 133 the cellos play motive x f, bowed. At 135 the timpani answer mf. At 137–139 the cellos answer again p and then pp, plucked, together with the timpani's pp E at 139. The low strings now turn into the sound that carries the link and the trio's bass (149–168), so the bass changes from bowed to plucked at the point where the music fades into the trio, instead of at a new entry after a rest. Notes, rhythm, dynamics and length are unchanged.

A pizzicato close at 137–139 uses the chapter's main colour device where the plan already asks for a fade, and it changes no protected note.
