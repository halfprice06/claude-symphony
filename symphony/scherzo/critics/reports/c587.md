## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–43 and 273–275: the theme's lower octave is the cor anglais alone under six players; give it the violas (applied)

**Problem.** At the f climax of theme A's counterstatement (D major, V of G), the tune lies in two octaves. The upper octave (A5–D5–A5 | C6–B5–B♭5 | A5–F♯5–D5) has Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Vln I and Vln II. The lower octave has the cor anglais and nothing else. Meanwhile the violas play off-beat F♯4 crotchets between the two octaves. Those off-beats double Hn 1's held F♯4 and Cl 2's F♯4 (bar 42). The reprise has the same layout at 273–275.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 78: "It is necessary to pay more attention to cases where, of the two parts in octaves, only one is doubled. When this method is applied to a melody in the soprano register it is better to allow the wood-wind to progress in octaves, the lower part only being doubled by one of the string groups." The formulas in the image (Picc. / Fl. + Vns; Fl. / Ob. (Cl.) + Vns) put the string group on the lower octave. Here the string groups are all on the upper octave, and the lower octave has only a wind.
- PDF 76: "Violas + Ob. (Eng. horn)" is the first viola pair in "the best and most natural combinations". Rimsky lists three *Sadko*/*Snegourotchka* examples of violas + C.a. (Ex. 82, 83; [28]). The viola section matches the C.a.'s register at A4–C5.
- PDF 76: "a wind instrument progressing in unison with a stringed instrument increases the resonance of the latter … the strings will predominate." One reed against six players in the upper octave is the weakest layer of the tutti. A bowed viola section gives the lower octave the body it lacks, and that is the change a listener would hear here.
- PDF 76, "melody … over three or four octaves … difficult to achieve perfect balance". The same problem applies to two octaves when one side is so much heavier.

**Why not the textbook layout.** Rimsky's own layout would move Vln II down. c111 (from this chapter) put Vln II on the upper octave because four a2 winds on Vln I alone overpower one string group (PDF 76). That do-not-touch (wind a2 with both violin sections on the tune, c107/c111; mixed unisons at 41–43/273–275, c446) stays as it is. Using the violas meets PDF 78 without touching it.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/sA.ily `sAVla` (bars 41–43) and sections/dA.ily `dAVla` (bars 273–275), the violas take the C.a.'s notes:
```
was:  r4 fis'4-.\f fis'4-. | r4 fis'4-. fis'4-. | r4 fis'4-. fis'4-. |
new:  a'4-.\f d'8-. d'-. a'4-. | c''2->\dnb( b'8 bes'8 | a'4-.) fis'8-. fis'-. d'4-. |
```
At 273 the `^\markup \italic "arco"` stays on the first note. The violas were pizz. up to 271, and bar 272 is still a full bar's rest. Bar 44/276 is unchanged (off-beat F♯3/D♯3). F♯ stays in the chord through Hn 1 (held 41–43, 273–275) and Cl 2 (42, 274). Bass, harmony, dynamics and the two violin lines are unchanged. Beats 2 and 3 are still marked by the tune itself (motive x's quavers in eight players). I added a comment with the reasons above each variable. No cue in parts-cues.json points at the violas in these bars (the only viola cue is at 241, in the bass clarinet part). The violas have no long rest before 41.
