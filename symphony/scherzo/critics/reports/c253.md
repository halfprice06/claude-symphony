## What else I studied

I measured every section from score.midi's tempo map (/tmp/critic-253/scripts/secdur.py): intro 9.5 s, sA 30.0, sB 32.8, sC 24.5, link 8.5, trio 69.7 (tA 25.3, tB 18.9, tC 25.5), retransition 9.6, reprise 49.0 (dA 24.5, dB 10.9, dC 13.6), coda 44.2; total 278.1 s (4:38). The other movements: I 7:11, II 5:45, IV 7:02 (coherence/mvt1.md, mvt2.md, finale.md). A scherzo of 4:38 is the shortest of the four, as in most four-movement Romantic symphonies, and it sits between the Adagio and a long finale without crowding either.

I dumped bass, pitch classes and top note on every beat of all 376 bars (scripts/dump.py) and scanned them for authentic cadences (scripts/cad.py), V7→I6 (scripts/v7i6.py) and leading-tone doublings at 12 dominant bars (scripts/lt.py). I compared the cadence plan with the MCTL harmony and cadence tables for Dvořák 9/iii (reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/annotations/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt3/form/). Dvořák keeps his scherzo theme on a tonic pedal with modal v, VII, III+ and a plagal iv6/4–i, and his first authentic cadence comes only at about bar 41. Our theme A uses real V–i periods (PAC every 8 bars). That is the Classical scherzo model (Beethoven 9/ii, Eroica iii) and the theme-A period structure that Unit 7's sample melody shows (PDF 117). It is a style choice, not a fault. I read the earlier form reports on this reading (c073, c163) and the third-pass form reports (c233, c243) so that I would not repeat their work.

## Findings

No finding needs a change to the score. I checked each of the 25 principles bar by bar and the score follows every one of them. Below are the points I tested against the reading and decided to keep, ranked by how close each came to a change.

1. **F-major episode 73–88 has no dominant (principles 1, 21, 25).** A&S PDF 93 says only V can affirm a tonic, and PDF 129 says cadences without a root-position V are weak. Here F major is reached by a falling third from A (72→73). It is confirmed only by vii°7→I (79→80) and stays on F/A for 84–86. C major (V of F) is banned by the cyclic rules, so the area cannot be confirmed any other way. The weakness suits the music: the episode is a ♭II parenthesis inside a dominant approach (it ends A♯°7 → cadential 6/4 over B at 88→89), and Kuhnau's model also keeps its subordinate areas inside a larger I–V–I (PDF 95, Ex. 6-4). Kept, as c183 decided.

2. **Theme A closes with a PAC every eight bars (20, 28, 112, 120, 260, 268, 312, 320) (principles 4, 18).** The PAC is "the most final" cadence (PDF 94). Four of them inside one 16-bar theme could make the theme sound closed off twice. But 13–20 is a period (HC 16, PAC 20) and 21–28 starts on iv with the applied V6 at 22. That is the small rounded form Unit 7's sample melody describes (PDF 117), and the reprise's V6/5 and ♮VII turns keep the following bars moving. Kept.

3. **Long tonic spans 133–152, 223–241 and 357–376 (principles 12, 16).** Under PDF 102 a repeated chord takes almost no accent, so these spans have the slowest harmonic rhythm in the movement (14 s, 15 s and 21 s). Each sits on a root-position pedal (PDF 112) and closes a section. Each also carries motivic groups that change on the strong bars (codetta changes at 227, 231, 235; coda 357–360 one chord per bar), which Ex. 6-21 and 7-18 allow. All three are protected (c153, c193, c243, and the ending). Kept.

4. **Weak-to-strong repetition at 40→41 (principle 12).** D arrives on bar 40, the fourth bar of the group 37–40, and is held into the strong bar 41. The head group ends on 40 (A–F♯–D, the same arpeggio as the half cadence at 16), and 41 adds the seventh C (V8–7, PDF 101). So 41 is heard as a new chord that begins a new group, which is the exception PDF 103 gives. Kept.

5. **Dominant sevenths at the approaches 101–104, 249–252, 301–304, 336–338 (principle 8).** PDF 99 says V7 is rarely the goal of a semicadence. None of these is a semicadence: each V7 moves straight to i at the next downbeat. The half cadences that do end phrases (16, 32, 152, 160, 256, 272, 324) are triads. Kept.

6. **Proportions and length (principle 5).** Measured against the first scherzo (87.3 s), the trio is 0.80, the reprise 0.56 and the coda 0.51. Dvořák 9/iii (c243's figures) has a shorter trio (0.69) and a shorter coda (0.41). Our trio's extra length is its codetta, already thinned (c153). Our coda's extra length is stage 2 (341–376), the protected soft ending into the finale. Both are deliberate, and the total of 4:38 matches the plan's "about 5 minutes". An exposition repeat would have the Classical model behind it (Kuhnau's double bars, Ex. 6-4), but c183 rejected repeats for length and I have nothing new to answer that reason. Kept.
