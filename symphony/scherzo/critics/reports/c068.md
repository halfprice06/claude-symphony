## What else I studied
I compared the slurring of the trio tune with the movement's own winds. In the second idea (45–52, 121–128, 277–288) the flute and oboe have four-bar phrase slurs over the violins' two-bar bow slurs, and Bsn 1's statement of the tune's head at 233–236 is one slur. The trio tune in the winds instead copies the violins' bowing. I checked the balance of the coda against the trio's own scoring at 169 (tune mp, violas pp, basses p). I wrote a script that lists every entry after rests and whether it has a dynamic, and a second one that prints the bowing context of every down-bow and up-bow sign.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Coda 349–356: the pizzicato bass is louder than the tune (applied)
Problem: at 349 Vln I, Vln II and Cl 1 play the trio tune's last statement at pp ("dolce", swelling to the A6 peak). Underneath, the cellos and basses keep the p pizzicato they started at 341, where it supported the horn's p. The violas are pp and the harp is pp. The only accompaniment part marked louder than the melody is the bass, and it plucks every downbeat. MOLA's Ex. 6 (PDF 11) gives each staff its own dynamic according to its role. In the trio (169) the same texture is tune mp over pp violas and p basses: one level of difference, with the tune on top. In the coda the order is reversed.
Fix (applied): `cis4\pp` (coda.ily `codaVc`, bar 349) and `cis,4\pp` (`codaCb`, bar 349). The bass then matches the violas and harp under the pp tune. The horn and oboe phrases at 341–348 keep p over p.

### 2. Trio tune in the winds: bow-length slurs instead of phrase slurs (applied)
Problem: in Cl 1 153–168 the slurs are (153–154)(155)(156)(157–158)(159)(160)(161–162)(163)(164)(165–166), with 167–168 unslurred. These are the violins' bowings (169–184, two-bar bows sul A). On a clarinet each new slur is a tongued attack, so a dolce four-bar phrase is cut into 2 + 1 + 1, the cadence notes 155 and 156 are re-tongued, and the held E at 167–168 falls outside any slur. The movement's other wind melodies use phrase slurs: Fl 1/Ob 1 at 45–52 (four bars), Bsn 1 at 233–236 (one slur). MOLA Ex. 5 (PDF 10) slurs the flute's figures by the bar and the piccolo's line as one phrase. The same copied bowings appear in Cl 1 197–200, Hn 1 341–344, Ob 1 345–348 and Cl 1 349–356.

### 3. Expressive text: one form of "espress." (applied)
Problem: the score prints "espress." at 45, 73, 185 and 357, but "espressivo" at 169 (Vln II), 209 (Vln I) and "espressivo, sul A" at 169 (Vln I). MOLA PDF 4 asks for instructions in one conventional language. Two forms of the same word in one score is the kind of inconsistency a librarian proofreading the score (PDF 8) would query.
Fix (applied): tA.ily `"espress., sul A"` (Vln I 169), `"espress."` (Vln II 169); tC.ily `"espress."` (Vln I 209).

Points checked and left alone: down-bow signs (each one asks for a retake, so MOLA PDF 5's "specific effect" condition is met, and c018's reason stands); the tempo-mark positions on every page; the dynamics at the tuttis 105/305 (c005's single ff at 104 is kept); the final bars 368–376 (Hn 1 pp→ppp, Vln I ppp, harp harmonics p, which harmonics need to sound).
