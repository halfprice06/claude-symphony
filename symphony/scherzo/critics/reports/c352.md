## Findings (ranked)

No score change is needed. I looked for the largest improvement this reading could support and tested the candidates below. Each one either already follows the reading or would be made worse by the change.

### 1. 101–104 and 301–304: the plain V7 build. The recorded reason is wrong; the decision stands on a different reason
- **Question.** PDF 466 says the 9th "intensifies the dissonant character of the dominant" and gives the "rich sonority" of nineteenth-century music. This is the retransition's four-bar dominant before the ff theme at 105/305, so it is the obvious place for a ♭9.
- **The recorded reason does not hold.** c082, c172 and c262 kept the plain V7 "to save the ♭9 for 337". The scan finds a ♭9 C over a B bass in nine bars before 337: 24, 111, 116, 126, 264, 282, 311 and 316. Theme A's peak puts it on top in every statement. So 337 does not introduce the colour. What 337 does have is the only complete five-note V♭9, at fff, with the 9th as a 5–♭6–5 upper neighbour (Ex. 27-4a). That reason should be recorded instead of "saving".
- **The stronger reason to keep it plain.** In 101–104, every instrument that could take C sits a step from a sounding B in the same register:
  - Bsn 1·2 have B2/B3 at 101 and 103;
  - Hn 3·4 hold F♯3/B3;
  - the violas have B4 at 103;
  - the trumpets hold B4.

  A ♭9 there would sound with its tone of resolution in an inner voice, which is PDF 469–470's "unclear sonority and ineffective resolution" (PDF 352 likewise). In the top line, a C would replace the 7th A5. The hemiola carries that 7th into 105, where it is resolved by transfer (Cl 1 A5→G5, Tbn 1 A3→G3), as in Ex. 27-6a. PDF 466 would allow an unresolved ♭9 above a resolving 7th (Fl 2 C6 at 104 b2–3). But that would put a new top note over the hemiola's last minim and blur the line D♯6–B5–A5 that the build is shaped around.
- **Decision.** Keep 101–104/301–304 as written. In HISTORY.md, replace c082's "save the ♭9 for 337" with the register and transfer reason above.

### 2. 123 b3–124 b1 and 279–280: the only inverted dominant ninth (kept)
- **What is there.** Over the passing bass B2–A2–G2, Vln I/II play D♯6–C♯6–B5 (and the octave below). The result is V4/2 with C♯ on top, while Hn 3 holds B3. This is the case PDF 469–470 warns about: the 9th sounds with its resolution B3 in a lower voice, only a ninth below Vln II's C♯5. PDF 464 also says that in minor the 9th is minor.
- **Why it stays.** This C♯ is not a suspension. It is a passing tone in parallel 10ths with a passing bass (D♯/B, C♯/A, B/G), the "descending passing tone" of PDF 465, Ex. 27-4d/e. The warning in sec. 7 is about a suspension heard against its own resolution. Here the B3 is a held horn pedal of another colour (PDF 352's timbre exception), and the 10ths make the motion clear. C♮ would give the minor 9th, but it would also make an augmented second D♯–C in the second idea, which is a recorded theme. c262 judged this bar the same way for a different reason (spacing).

### 3. 155 and 160: the trio drone's E3 under V7 and V (kept)
PDF 474–475 says an unresolved 4th over V must be heard clearly on top, "or it will create a mess". Here the 4th is a held inner pedal (Hn 3·4 E3–B3 from 149, pp). It is prepared by the tonic and returns to it, so it is not an added dissonance of the V7. On a pedal, only dissonances among the upper parts must resolve (PDF 368), and those do: the 7th, Cl 1's A4, falls to G♯4 at 156. The drone stops before the cadential V7 at 166, as c262 found. Keep.

### 4. Seventh resolutions (all follow)
The scan of 7ths that leave by leap finds only transferred resolutions, which PDF 466 (Ex. 27-6a) describes:
- 104→105 and 304→305: Vln I A5→E5 (theme A's first note), resolved by Cl 1 A5→G5 and Tbn 1 A3→G3.
- 44→45: Vln II C4→D5, with Cl 2 taking B3.
- 212→213: violas D4 → A3+C♯4 double stop.
- 214→216: Bsn 1 E4→F♯4, while Hn 2 holds E4 to D♯4.

The rest are arpeggiation inside one V7 (100, 101–103, 337) or augmented sixths opening to the octave (244→245, 331). Ob 1's F5→G5 at 334→335 leaves the motto tail's chromatic passing note, while Fl 1, Cl 1, Ob 2 and the violins take it to E. That is the same transfer, inside an fff tutti. Keep.

### 6. Codetta 225–236 and close 357–360: not turned into V9 of IV (kept)
Ex. 27-13 (PDF 471) shows the tonic over a pedal turned into V9 of IV. PDF 464 also offers V9 as a second way to harmonise 5–6–5, and the codetta heads G♯–A–B–C♯–B invite it at 227/231. I did not apply either:
- The codetta's IV, IV, iv are protected (c170). Their colour is the ♭6 C♮ at 235, which is answered at 359.
- The trio already uses E9 → A at 212 (Ex. 27-13's progression).
- A dominant in the codetta would weaken the plagal close that the movement ends with.

### 7. 205–208 and 245–252: dissonances over the B pedals (follow)
- **205.** G♯5/G♯4 is a 6th above V. It sounds before any 7th and rises G♯–A–B, then the motto tail falls B–A♯–A to G♯ at 209. This is the floating upper line of Ex. 27-18, not an unresolved 13th: the 7th A at 208 resolves to G♯.
- **245–249.** The 4ths E4 and E5 over B resolve to D♯ at 246 and 249 without D♯ sounding before them (Ex. 27-16a).

### 8. Double suspensions at 55 and 131 (follow)
At 55, A5/A4 and C5 over G resolve to G and B. At 131, F♯5 and A4 over E resolve to E and G. These are 9/4 double suspensions, and their resolution tones sound only in the bass. They match PDF 472–473 (sec. 10: a double suspension sounds like a 9th chord), and they sit next to real dominant 9ths (24, 126). Keep (c139, c172, Cl 1 4–3).
