## Findings (ranked)

Under Unit 32 the large form holds, and so do its joins. Every key change in the movement is one of the types the unit describes. Each has the motivic reason the unit asks for (rows 1–4, 9–11, 17, 20–21). I found nothing in the order, the proportions or the key plan that the reading supports changing. All four points below are decided as "keep". None is applied, and the scratch copy is v203 unchanged.

### 1. 339–341: E minor ff, G.P., then A major p. Keep.

**Question.** The coda's second stage enters with no pivot chord and no held tone. *Widmung* shows how a common tone makes such a join sound integrated (PDF 586). Should E, the common tone of E minor and A major, be held across?

**Evidence.**
- The join is not a modulation inside a phrase. It is a sectional cut after the movement's only fff (336) and a G.P. That is the Schubert 9/iv model (PDF 587): the texture empties before the new area.
- A&S say of *Widmung* that the new triad "so obviously marks the beginning of a section that we hear it immediately" as a local tonic (PDF 586).
- A held tone in the G.P. would remove the silence that separates the two stages of the protected coda. Holding Hn 1's B4 (339) into 341 would not give the common tone, because A major has no B.

**Decision.** No change.

### 2. 72–73 and 141–153: are the common tones exposed? Keep.

**Question.** A&S PDF 587: "The common tone will appear in an exposed position, usually in the soprano."

**Evidence.**
- At 72–73 the A is held in Hn 2 (c010). It becomes the first note of the harp melody, A5, the highest sound at 73 (sB.ily `sBHarpR`: `a''4(\p\<`).
- At 141–153 the G♯4 is the top voice throughout: Hn 1 (145–148), the harp's top note at 149 and 151 (`<gis' b' e''>`), then Cl 1's first note of T at 153.
- Both joins also carry the inflection that the unit expects with two major triads: C♯→C (Hn 1, 72–73) and G→G♯ (Hn 1, 144–145).

**Decision.** No change. Both joins are textbook cases of PDF 586–587.

### 3. 241: a falling inflection under an accelerando. Keep.

**Question.** A&S PDF 585: a falling inflection (♯2–♮2) lowers intensity. Here the trio's G♯ falls to G at the very bar where the music starts to accelerate back to Tempo I.

**Evidence.**
- The fall is the movement's frame read backwards. Bars 1–7 go G♯ → G (Cl 2), 145 goes G → G♯, and 241 goes G♯ → G. The scherzo is the minor that the trio left.
- The intensity rises by other means. The tempo climbs every two bars (global.ily, c113: 198 → 254 → 264), the head enters Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I, and the Fr6 → V at 244–245 pushes toward the dominant.
- A&S say the inflection's effect depends on rhythm, texture and dynamics. The decrease belongs to the pitch and the increase to the tempo and the entries. That matches Pathétique Ex. 32-6 (PDF 588): the quiet °7 and fermata before an Allegro that starts p and crescendos.

**Decision.** No change. c113 already smoothed the MIDI tempo here.

### 4. The reprise without the F-major episode; ♭II worked out in one bar (331). Keep.

**Question.** A&S PDF 592: a remote area "will create tonal conflicts that the composer must somehow work out". The exposition's ♭II (73–88) returns only as the N6/4 at 331, which lasts 0.6 s at Più mosso.

**Evidence.**
- The conflict is F♮ against F♯, and every statement of theme A states it (G–F♯–F–E). The coda's N6/4 (331) → V (332) is the last direct F→F♯ confrontation before the final V. That is the Hammerklavier procedure (PDF 585), where the motivic pair carries the key relation.
- Op. 18/2 (PDF 591) shows ♭II in a fast movement as a brief delay of the tonic.
- Repeating the 16-bar episode would add about 11 s to a reprise that is now proportioned like Dvořák's (0.56 against 0.60). It would also break the protected form and length (c083, c103, c113).

**Decision.** No change.
