## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Tempo words and rehearsal letters are not repeated above the brass (applied)
**Bars:** every tempo word and letter, from 1 to 376.
**Problem:** v55 prints tempo words, rehearsal letters and "G.P." above the top staff and above the strings. On full pages (1, 4, 5, 10, 11, 12) the brass sit in the middle of a 25-staff A3 page. A conductor reading the horns and trumpets at G (105), T (305) or the coda peak (333–340) has to look up eight to ten staves to find the letter or the tempo.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 294: "It is strongly urged that tempo markings be placed at the top of the score and repeated in the space separating each choir." Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) prints "Allegretto ♩ = 100" four times, including above "4 Horns in F". MOLA PDF 4 asks for the top staff and the violins as a minimum. Adler's standard is the stricter one, and it suits a score of this height.
**Fix (score.ly):** the brass StaffGroup carries the same engravers as the strings group:
```
\new StaffGroup \with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" \consists "Staff_collecting_engraver" } <<
  \new Staff = "Horns 1·2" ...
```
**Result:** letters and tempo words now print above Hn 1·2 (or the top visible brass staff) in every system. No system begins with brass, so nothing prints twice at the top. In the three reduced systems where only one woodwind staff sits above the horns (89, 153, 241), the word appears over both groups, as Adler's example does. There are no collisions. The score is still 13 pages.

### 2. Timpani stick change at 153 comes one bar after the last stroke (applied)
**Bars:** 149, 151, 153.
**Problem:** the timpanist plays E pp at 149 and B at 151 with the hard sticks of bar 1, and "soft sticks" appears at 153 on the next stroke. That leaves one bar, 152, at the slowest point of the poco rit. (about 0.8 s), to put down one pair and pick up another. It also means the two pp strokes under the link's horn and harp are played with hard sticks.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 294: an instruction to change "should be given well ahead of time and should be written above the player's staff". App. A PDF 312 lists the stick terms ("With Soft Stick", "With Hard Sticks") as instructions of this kind. c020 put the stick labels at the joins. This change keeps that idea, because the label still sits in the link before the Trio. It only moves the label to where the player can act on it.
**Fix:** link.ily `linkTimp = { R2.*4 | R2.^\markup \small \italic "muta G in G♯" | R2.^\markup \small \italic "soft sticks" | R2.*2 | e,4\pp ...`, and tA.ily drops the markup from 153. The player now has the rests at 146–148 to change, and 149–151 sound soft.

### 3. Harp chord at 361 has no dynamic after twelve bars' rest (applied)
**Bars:** 361 (W + 4).
**Problem:** the harp's last dynamic is pp at 341. After rests at 349–360 it plays the rolled E-major chord with no dynamic, under the horns' pp at 361.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 291 item 3 says the score must contain every detail for every instrument. PDF 299 item 4 says every player must know what is happening "whether he is playing or not". A harpist re-entering after twelve bars needs the level printed.
**Fix:** coda.ily `codaHarpL = { ... | <e, b,>2.\arpeggio\pp | ...`. It prints between the harp staves, as the harp's other dynamics do.

### 4. Timpani stroke at 168 has no dynamic after fourteen bars' rest (applied)
**Bars:** 168.
**Problem:** the timpani's pp at 153 is not repeated for the stroke at 168, which closes the first trio statement.
**Evidence:** same as finding 3 (Adler PDF 291 item 3; PDF 299 item 4).
**Fix:** tA.ily `tATimp = { e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2.*3 | R2.*11 | e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2.*16 | }`.

### 5. Flute 2's first entry at 37 has a hairpin but no dynamic (applied)
**Bars:** 37.
**Problem:** Fl 2 plays for the first time in the movement at 37. Its music starts with `d''2.~\<`, with no starting level. On the shared staff the only p is at 29, under Fl 1's "1." entry. A part extracted from this source would begin with a bare hairpin.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 301, Ex. XVIII-10a/b: when two players share a staff, each part is extracted separately and carries its own dynamics. Fl 1 carries p at 29, but Fl 2 never received one.
**Fix:** sA.ily `sAFluteTwo = { R2.*16 | R2.*8 | d''2.~\p\< | ...`. The staff now prints "p<" at 37, which states the level both flutes have at that point. No duplicate dynamic and no warning.

### 6. B6 on five ledger lines at 336 (not applied; decision: keep)
**Bars:** 336, Vln I and Fl 1.
**Problem:** Adler PDF 300 item 8a says to use 8va "to avoid more than four ledger lines". B6 needs five.
**Decision:** no change. Gould PDF 47 allows up to five ledger lines for woodwinds and strings. It calls octave signs unhelpful for them, because the player links a written note to a fingering, and it asks for ledger lines rather than octave signs for isolated notes. On the flute staff an 8va is not allowed in any case, because Fl 2 sounds D♯6 under it. Breaking Vln I's peak phrase 336–338 into a different octave from the flute's would obscure the line. Gould PDF 47 says to keep a phrase at one octave. This note is the movement's only B6, and it stays as written.
