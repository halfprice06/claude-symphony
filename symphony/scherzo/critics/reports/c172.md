## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 55: the G-major cadence's appoggiatura sounds between its resolution and the 3rd (applied)
- **Problem.** At the end of the second idea in G (53 I6/4, 54 V7, 55 I), Vln II and Ob 1 play the lower octave of the tune's A→G (A4 on beat 1, G4 on beats 2–3). On the same beat, in the same octave, Cl 2 and the violas hold G4, which is the A's tone of resolution, and Cl 1 and Hn 1 hold B4, the 3rd. The MIDI beat-1 chord is G1 G2 G3 G4 A4 B4 A5: a G4–A4–B4 cluster for a full crotchet, f. c139 removed exactly this figure at 131 and 287, the E-minor statements of the same cadence. The exposition's G-major statement was missed and is now the only one left.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 469–470, Ex. 27-10: a dissonance sounding with its tone of resolution in a voice other than the bass gives "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". PDF 352 (Unit 21 sec. 12): the tone of resolution in another voice "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The only exceptions are the bass, or a top-voice dissonance against the resolution an octave lower. Here the clash is at the unison octave. PDF 465–466, Ex. 27-5: a 9th over the chord's root is a neighbour or suspension that resolves to the octave, so the octave should arrive, not already be sounding. PDF 474, Ex. 27-16a: a 4–3 sounds without its 3rd.
- **Fix (sA.ily, mirrors c139 at 131).**
  - `sAClOne` 54–55: `c''2 a'4 | b'2.)` → `c''2 a'4~ | a'4 b'2)`. Cl 1 joins the A4 as a prepared suspension and rises to B4.
  - `sAHnOne` 55: `b'2.--)` → `c''4 b'2--)`. Hn 1 has a 4–3 a third above the A–G, and the V7's 7th C5 reappears and resolves to B4 in the horn.
  - `sAClTwo` 55: `g'2.)` → `d'2.)`.
  - `sAVla` 55: `g'2.` → `d'2.`.
  - Beat 1 is now G1 G2 G3 D4 A4 C5 A5 and beat 2 is G1 G2 G3 D4 G4 B4 G5. There is no E, so no C–E–G (cmajor.py and ceg.py are unchanged). The all-pairs scan also loses the old Hn 2/Cl 2/viola octaves F♯→G at 54→55.

### 2. Bar 196: Hn 1 resolves the 6–5 on the downbeat, a tone under the suspension, and moves in fifths with the bass into 197 (applied)
- **Problem.** At 195–196 (I6/4 → V7 in E, the 7/6–5 c062 wrote), Vln I, Vln II and Bsn 1 hold G♯ over the bar line and resolve to F♯ on beat 2. Hn 1 moves G♯4→F♯4 on the downbeat instead. On beat 1 its F♯4 sounds a whole tone under Vln II's suspended G♯4, in the same octave. Then Hn 1's F♯4→G♯4 over the bass B→C♯ (196→197) makes compound parallel fifths with the cellos and basses. c112 removed exactly these fifths from Vln II by giving it G♯4–F♯4–A4.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 469 and PDF 352, as in Finding 1. PDF 474, Ex. 27-16b: the 6–5 over V7 is a single resolution, and the 5th is not sounded under the 6th. PDF 472–473, Ex. 27-15: a 9/7 (here 7/6) double suspension works because the dissonances resolve together.
- **Fix (tB.ily).** `tBHnOne` 195–196: `gis'2.(\pp\< | fis'2.)` → `gis'2.~(\pp\< | gis'4 fis'4 a'4)`. Hn 1 now doubles Vln II at the unison: it suspends with the strings, resolves on beat 2, and takes the 7th A4 to G♯4 at 197, as Cl 1 and Vln II do. The fifths with the bass are gone (all-pairs scan). Hn 2's D♯4 is unchanged.

### 3. Bars 205–208: the 6th over V sounds a tone above the 5th in the same octave (applied)
- **Problem.** At 205 (B pedal, the anticipated motto tail G♯–A–B–A♯–A in Fl 2 and Cl 2, protected since c024/c050), the G♯ is a 6th over V that rises to A and B and never resolves to F♯. Fl 2's G♯5 is clear on top. Cl 2's G♯4, however, lies a whole tone above the violas' held F♯4, the 5th it replaces, at pp. The sonority B–F♯–B–D♯–F♯–G♯ blurs the line in its middle register.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 474–475, Ex. 27-16b/d and 27-18. A 6th over V replaces the 5th, and in Chopin's Ballade the tone of resolution is left out so the 6th floats as a soprano pedal. An unresolved 6th that is not heard clearly "will create a mess". PDF 470, Ex. 27-10: wide space between the dissonant line and the accompaniment keeps the texture clear.
- **Fix (tB.ily).** `tBVla` 205–208: `fis'2.\pp | fis'2. | fis'2.\< | fis'2.` → `fis2.\pp | fis2. | fis2.\< | fis2.`. The violas go down an octave, in unison with Bsn 1's F♯3. The 5th then lies a 9th or more below both G♯s. The motto tail, its scoring and Hn 3's D♯4 are untouched. The violas leave F♯4 at 204 for F♯3 at the pp entry and reach B3/E4 at 209 by step and third.

## Considered and kept
- **200 (A4 → G♯4 under Vln I/Fl 1/Ob 1 G♯5).** This is the case PDF 352 allows: a different colour, with the tone of resolution an octave and more away. It is also c082's protected 9–8 and the 197–200 layering.
- **111/311 (inner ♭9 suspension C4 with Hn 1/Tpt 1 B4 above).** These are ff motive-x attacks a major 7th away. c112 placed this suspension to remove fifths. The clash is weaker than at 55 or 196, and any fix brings the fifths back.
- **101–104/301–304 (plain V7 for four bars).** PDF 466 says the 9th intensifies the dominant. The scherzo saves the ♭9 for the coda's last dominant (337, c082). Adding it here would spend that colour three times.
- **Tonic as V9 of IV in the codetta (PDF 471, Ex. 27-13).** c082 already declined this, and the reason holds: iv's C♮ at 235 is the codetta's colour.
