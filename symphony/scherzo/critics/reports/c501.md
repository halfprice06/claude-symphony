## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Codetta 225–236: T's head stops before its E. Applied.

**Problem.** The codetta passes T's head down through Ob 1 (225), Cl 1 (229) and Bsn 1 (233). Each statement runs `gis2 a4 | b2. | cis2. | b2` (C♮ in the bassoon, over iv). That is G♯–A–B, then two dotted minims. The head's defining fourth B–E never comes. What the listener hears three times is a generic step-and-neighbour line, not the tune. T's head is G♯4–A4–B4–E5 (153–154); movement I relies on it, and it is the trio's most hummable shape. The codetta is the last place it is heard before the retransition. Bars 2–3 of each statement, B then C♯ in dotted minims, are Sebesky's Ex. 278 case: plain long values stepping in the lead.

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 210, Ex. 277–278: even in a soft, long-value passage the lead line needs character, such as a leap. The same line in plain long notes is "much less interesting".
- PDF 206: lines may be derived from a portion of the tune itself (an intro "based solely on the initial two-note motive"). A recurring figure unifies the score.
- PDF 220, question 6: if the melody is weak somewhere, do something about it.
- Dvořák 9/iii (woods.notesS.ily 377–382) brings back the head figure of his E-major tune whole after the tune ends.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-501/work, sections/tC.ily).** In bar 2 of each statement, the dotted-minim B becomes a minim B and a crotchet E. The head is then complete (G♯–A–B–E, T's own rhythm, minim + crotchet twice). It falls a third to the 6 and resolves to 5:

```
tCOboeOne 226   b''2.\<          ->  b''2\< e'''4
tCClOne   230   b'2.\<           ->  b'2\< e''4
tCBsnOne  234   b2.\<            ->  b2\< e'4
```

Everything else stays the same:
- the relay Ob → Cl → Bsn (c157, c201) and the harmony I–I–IV–I, with iv at 235 (c042, c170);
- 6–5 at 227–228 and 231–232, and ♭6–5 at 235–236, so the ♮6–♭6–5 thread shared with 201–203, 220 and 357–360 is untouched;
- the swells (c158): the crescendo now covers B–E into the hidden mp at the 6;
- slurs, "dolce", the harp's thinning (c153), and nothing held above the solos (c423).

Every new E is a chord tone of the E-major bar it ends:
- harp `<gis b e'>` at 226/230, `<e' gis' b'>` at 234;
- it ties into nothing, then falls to C♯ (IV) or C (iv).

Register checks:
- The oboe's E6 is one crotchet at p, a third above the C♯6 it already played. It lies well below the oboe's top, and the bar crescendos into it.
- Cl 1's E5 is written F♯5, in the clarion, with no break crossing.
- Bsn 1's E4 meets the harp's E4 at 235. That E4 is in every harp chord of 233–236 anyway.

No C-major sound arises: 234 is E major and 235 is A minor (no G).

This is not a recorded theme statement (themecheck 52/52 unchanged). No other movement quotes the codetta. Under Dan's 2026-10-12 note the theme is allowed to develop, and the change is listed below so the finale and movement I threads can be told for information.
