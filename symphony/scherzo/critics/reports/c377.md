## Findings

### 1. Bars 361–364: every note of the fading E-major chord is a wind–string unison, and the space under Fl 1 is empty (applied)

**Problem.** After the plagal close, the E-major chord is held pp for four bars under Hn 1's G♯4. From the bottom it reads: Cb E2, Vc E3, Cl 2 + violas B3, Cl 1 + Hn 2 E4, Hn 1 + Vln II G♯4, Fl 1 G♯5. The two clarinets add no note of their own; each sits in unison with a string or a horn. Once the harp's rolled chord at 361 has died (it struck B4 and E5), nothing is sustained between G♯4 and Fl 1's G♯5. The flute floats an octave above a chord packed into E3–G♯4.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257: overlapping at the unison "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch"; only in a full tutti is it still common. This is a pp chord in the movement's quietest passage, where colour matters most. On the same page, Ex. VIII-24 puts the larger gaps at the bottom and voices the upper notes close. Here the widest gap above E3 is at the top, under the flute. PDF 252 (Brahms 2/iii, Ex. VIII-18) is the reading's model ending: the last chord is coloured by a wind chord in its own voicing beside the strings, not by winds sitting on the string notes. Ex. VIII-22 (PDF 256) marks flute over clarinet as "better for p or pp". The harp's own spacing at 361 (E2 B2 E4 G♯4 B4 E5) already fills the upper octave.

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily):**

```
codaClOne: e'2.~\pp | e'2.~\> | e'2.~ | e'2 r4\!
        -> e''2.~\pp | e''2.~\> | e''2.~ | e''2 r4\!
codaClTwo: \partCombineChords b2.~\pp | b2.~\> | b2.~ | b2 r4\!
        -> \partCombineChords b'2.~\pp | b'2.~\> | b'2.~ | b'2 r4\!
```

The chord now reads E2 E3 B3 E4 G♯4 in strings and horns, with B4 E5 G♯5 in clarinets and flute above. The winds form a close upper chord in their own register, and the strings and horns a separate lower one. Cl 1 ends its T statement on E5 at 356, so the clarinets come back in the register they just left. Written G5/D5 for clarinet in A is clarion, the instrument's easiest pp. Hn 2's E4 and the violas' B3 now sound alone, an octave under the clarinets. Hn 1's G♯4, the harmony, the ties (c288), the hairpins, the shared-staff chord (c295) and the fade into Hn 1 at 365 are all unchanged. This is not the last chord the finale starts from (375–376), so it is not a cyclic change.

### 2. Bars 13–28: no inner moving line under theme A (checked, kept)

Dvořák's own 21–28 and Adler's Eroica page (PDF 254: Vln II and violas in quavers under the passed gesture) both put a running inner line under a scherzo theme. That answers c177's recorded reason, which was that Dvořák keeps crotchets for eight bars: he keeps them only for 13–20. I wrote out a slurred pp quaver line for Vln II at 21–28 against this score's harmony (iv – V6 of iv – iv – V7 – i – i6 – V7 – i). Every stepwise path I tried did one of three things. It doubled the applied leading tone G♯ that c143 keeps in the bass only (22). Or it sounded D♮ against the melody's D♯5 neighbours (22). Or it struck the melody's F♯5 or E5 in octaves on the beat (21, 27). There is also a reason inside the reading itself: PDF 260 (W. Schuman) asks for a colour or figure to be held back so that its entry is new. The slurred quaver figure is the reprise's new element at 253–268 and in the tutti at 305–320 (c369). I decided to keep the plain off-beats.

### 3. Wind counterpoint is still a relay, not overlapping imitation (checked, kept)

Adler PDF 271–272 (Mozart 38, Ex. VIII-42: oboe and bassoon a bar apart over running violins) is followed only partly. The winds pass the head at 92, 245 and 225–236 and enter in stretto at 69–72 and 297–300, but two winds never overlap in canon. I checked again the spots c177 tested and two more: the Ob 1 call at 185 against the cor anglais at 189, and Bsn 1 against Cl 1 at 133–140. At 185–192 an overlapping cor anglais would enter on the oboe's held C♯5–B4 and blur the answer that the cellos double (c279 protects these hand-overs). At 133–140 the dialogue is cellos against timpani (c077). Adler PDF 253 approves the call and response at 29–36 as it stands. No change.

### 4. Bars 357–360: Bsn 1 doubles the violas' C♯4–C4–B3 at the unison (checked, kept)

PDF 257 would prefer the line in one colour. But Bsn 1 has no other good note. A3 at 359 doubles the motto's A4 in octaves, which c050 removed. E3 merges with Bsn 2 and the cellos. Taking Bsn 1 out leaves the violas alone on the line that turns the A♯°7 into iv6/4. At pp, bassoon and viola in unison blend as one colour rather than masking each other, and the motto tail stays a single horn (c060). The passage is also protected (c082, c302). No change.

### 5. Bars 229–232: Cl 1's T head is enclosed by Vln II's E5 and the violas (checked, kept)

PDF 256 warns that an enclosed instrument may lack carrying power. Here Cl 1 is p in its clarion (written B4–E5) and the frame is pp. c047 placed Vln II above it to clear the unisons that had blurred the head. The enclosure is soft and the clarinet carries. No change.

### 6. The third-time rule at 13, 17 and 25 (checked, follows)

Theme A's head comes three times in 13–28 over the same off-beats. Adler PDF 255 speaks of a scoring or sequence used three times in succession. Here the three heads are the parts of one sixteen-bar period, and the bars that come back (25–28) already differ at 27 (c323), and in dynamics after the mp climax at 24. The change Adler asks for comes at phrase level: winds alone at 29, choirs together at 37. No change.

### 7. Fugato, stretto and ostinato (checked, follow)

The countersubject (Vc 61–64, Vla 65–68) moves in steady crotchets against the subject's crotchet–quavers–crotchet. The only bare fourths in the two-voice texture are passing ones (63 beat 2, 64 beat 3 into the A pedal at 65). The ostinato at 325–328 puts each entry in a new section and register (PDF 274–277). The analyser's fifths and octaves at 326–328 are the one figure doubled by design. No change.

### 8. Scans (no action needed)

Flute lies under oboe or clarinet only in the ff chords at 72, 104, 300 and 304, where interlocking is safe (PDF 256). No wind-only chord has a gap of more than an octave above a smaller gap at the bottom. The Vln I melody meets its string accompaniment in unison only where earlier critics chose it: pizz. at 255–256 (c227), the violas' doubling at 202–203 (c177), and the tuttis.
