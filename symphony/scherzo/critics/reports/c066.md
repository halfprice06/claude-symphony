## Findings

### 1. Hn 1 sits a semitone under Adler's professional ceiling for 16 bars, twice (105–120, 305–320). Applied.

**Problem.** In the tutti theme A, Hn 1 plays accented crotchets on beats 1 and 3 on sounding E5, written B5 for horn in F. That is 28 strokes in 105–120, and the same again in 305–320, at f under ff. Adler's table puts the professional ceiling for horn at written C6 (adler2 PDF 305). The footnote on PDF 302 says the table's top notes are the ones "usually called for", which makes them limits for a few notes, not a range to sit in. The nonprofessional ceiling is written G5. In the horn chapter, Adler says it is "most taxing to play in the higher register all the time" (adler1 PDF 303). Dvořák's horns 1–2 in his E-minor scherzo stay at or below sounding A4.

Hn 1's E5 adds little. It doubles the melody's E5, which Fl 1–2, Ob 1 and Vln I already play, and it doubles Hn 2's E4 an octave higher. The trumpets' B4/E4 already give brass edge on the same beats.

**Fix (applied in sC.ily and dC.ily).** Hn 1 takes the top of the horn chord on B4/C5 (written F♯5/G5, the "bright and heroic" register in Adler's Example X-5). Hn 3 moves to the middle (G4/A4/F♯4), which is where Adler places the third horn. Hn 2 and Hn 4 are unchanged.

- Each bar spells its chord fully: i = E–G–B, V = D♯–F♯–B, iv (or iiø7 over F♯ at 313) = E–A–C.
- At 106 and 118, both horns play beat 1 only, so no G sounds against the chromatic tail F♯–F on beat 3. This keeps c011's reason.
- The horns' top note is now Tpt 1's B4.
- Hn 3 now plays lower in 105–120. This helps because Hn 3 continues straight into the sustained B4/C5 line at 121–131 (c046), so its 32 bars from 101 are easier.

### 2. Bsn 2 holds the ff bass for 24 bars with no rest, 297–320 (and 101–120). Applied.

**Problem.** In the reprise, Bsn 2 sustains A2/B2 at 297–304 and then the dotted-minim bass of the tutti at 305–319, mostly between E2 and B2, f to ff. The first rest comes at 320, about 16 s later. The exposition has the same line, 13 s from 101 to 120. Adler says loud playing uses more breath (adler1 PDF 292), and that the low reed register needs periodic rests (PDF 230). Bsn 2 is the only sustained wind bass here. A breath snatched from a clipped minim would make a hole in it at an unpredictable place.

**Fix (applied).** At bar 8 of the tutti, where the melody itself rests on beats 2–3 (Vln I `e''4-. r4 r4`), Bsn 2 plays `e,2 r4` instead of `e,2.`: sC.ily bar 112 and dC.ily bar 312. The bass trombone, tuba, contrabassoon and basses still strike the downbeat. The longest unbroken stretch drops to about 10 s.

### 3. Bsn 1 has 20 bars of held notes before the trio tune's return, 197–216. Applied.

**Problem.** Bsn 1 holds dotted minims with no rest from 197 (f) through the B pedal at 205–208 and on into 209–216, under T's forte return. That is about 16 s at the trio tempo. Each note is tongued, but a slurless line of held notes still gives no planned breath (adler1 PDF 176: a breath belongs at a phrase division).

**Fix (applied in tB.ily).** The last bar of tB (208) becomes `fis2 r4`, a breath on the beat before T's return at 209. Bsn 2 rests in that bar, so the part-combiner prints nothing new.
