## Findings

### 1. Bar 375: put the closing harmonics on beat 2, after the pizzicato and timpani E (applied)

- **Problem.** On beat 1 of 375 four sounds start at once: the harp harmonics E4–G♯4–B4 (p), Cb pizz. E1 (pp), Vc pizz. E2 (pp) and the timpani's last E2 (ppp, near the rim). The held Hn 1 G♯4 and Vln I G♯5 continue above. The harmonics are the softest attack of the four, and they speak at the same instant as three low plucked or struck attacks. The bell chord that should be the movement's last new colour therefore merges into one soft thud. The same chord at 149 and 151 is placed differently. There the pizz. bass and timpani strike beat 1 and the harmonics follow alone on beat 2 (c476). So the rhyme between the trio's door and the movement's end is incomplete in exactly the bar where it matters most.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 116, "Harmonics", point 1: "Harp harmonics are very soft and must be accompanied very lightly or played a cappella to be effectively heard." Over beat 2 of 375 only two ppp held G♯s sound, an octave apart, together with the decaying plucks, which is as close to a cappella as the orchestra gets. Ex. IV-9 (Debussy, *Nuages* 74–78) shows harmonics entering over a held texture, not under fresh attacks. The written notes E3–G♯3 (LH) and B3 (RH) lie in Adler's best range, C3–A5 written (PDF 116), and the hands stay within a fifth, as before.
- **The earlier reason answered.** c476 kept the simultaneous attack because "the plucked notes are pp and two or three octaves lower … the harmonics stay the highest new attack". Being the highest attack does not meet Adler's condition, which is about what sounds with the harmonics, not where they sit. A low attack masks upward more than downward, and a timpani stroke is broadband. c476's own design at 149/151 (bass on 1, harmonics on 2) already accepts this. With the change, 149, 151 and 375 are the same gesture.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-566/work/sections/coda.ily).** One beat of rest before the harmonics in both hands. Pitches, hands, `\harmOct`, p and "harm." are unchanged.

  ```lilypond

  r4 \harmOct { b4-\flageolet } r4 | R2. | }
  % codaHarpL, bar 375
  r4 \harmOct { <e gis>4-\flageolet\p^\markup \small \italic "harm." } r4 | R2. | }
  ```

- **Playability.** This is easier than before. Both hands have been free since 361, and the harpist places the nodes during beat 1 instead of on the downbeat while the conductor slows the rit. No pedal is involved (E, G♯ and B have been set since 333).
- **Checks.** score.midi: the harp's last notes (MIDI 64, 68, 71) now start at tick 431232 instead of 430848, one beat (384 ticks) later. All other notes are unchanged. The engraving on page 13 shows the rest and the harmonics on beat 2. analysis.txt is identical to v566's. Parts: the harp cue (Vln I, 337) is unaffected, and no other part changes.

### 2. Trio downbeats 153–168 and 225–236: wide spacing (PDF 114) built on paper and rejected

Adler's "three-note chords (per hand) spread wide are the most effective" and Ex. IV-5 suggest giving the LH an open fifth or tenth on the downbeat (E2–B2) instead of a single note that doubles the pizz. bass in unison. I rejected it. At pp under Cl 1's low T (G♯4–E5) the added B2 sits on wire-wound strings that ring for bars (PDF 112). It would thicken the space between the bass and the G♯3–E4 chords, which is exactly where Hn 3·4 hold B3/E3. The single bass with the pizz. basses is Adler's "doubling instrument" use (PDF 117) and keeps the trio light. Spreading the RH chords upward enters the solo's register, and spreading them downward enters the horns' drone (c296, c476). No change.
