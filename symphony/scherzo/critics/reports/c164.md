## Findings

### 1. 121–124 and 277–280: the double basses stay on the sub. p bass. Applied.
**Problem.** After the ff tutti (105–120) the second idea in E minor starts sub. p (c063). The upper strings, clarinets and horns drop to p. The slurred bass line E–G–B–A–G stays in three instruments: Vc and Bsn 2 at E2, and Cb at E1. c074 took the contrabassoon off these bars for this reason ("the bass is as heavy as it was at f in bar 45"), but kept the double basses. The 16-foot line is still there, and the four bars still sound like a tutti that has been asked to play quietly, not like a lighter scoring. The reprise 277–280 is the same: Cb plays pizz. at 271–275 and turns arco on the downbeat of 277 to play the same line.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 130: "When a lighter string texture is desired, the double bass was eliminated." Ex. V-6 (PDF 131, image checked) shows the method: "+Bass" for the opening, "−Bass" for the light middle, then "+Bass" again "at the dominant pedal ... and at the cadence". The text names the double bass itself, not just any 16-foot instrument. PDF 128 and 132: Haydn balances the layers by scoring, so he hardly needs dynamic marks. PDF 139 (Ex. V-16, Haydn 103): adding the cellos and basses "sets off" the next statement. PDF 137 (Ex. V-14): a crescendo is built by adding octaves. Here the crescendo starts at 125 (281). That is also where Fl 1 and Ob 1 join (c063, c044), the contrabassoon returns (c074), and the line leads to the f cadence on B at 129 (285), the dominant where Adler puts the bass back.
**Answer to c074.** c074 kept the Cb without giving a reason. With the Cb resting, the line is still in two instruments, Vc and Bsn 2, in the cello's strong low octave, so the bass is not thin. It is simply no longer a tutti bass. The Cb and the Cbsn then bring the 16-foot line back together at the same moment, on the same A, which is the step Adler's example marks "+Bass".
**Fix (applied).**

## Considered, no change (my decision)
- **Trio tune at 169 in the high octave, and 209 in the same octave** (PDF 133–135: keep the higher violin colour for the later statement). Moving 169 down would undo c011's octave doubling and put the tune on top of the violas' off-beats. That would break the trio layering protected by c107, c144 and c159. The f return at 209 is set apart by its dynamic, Fl 1 at 217 (c074) and the fuller bass, and c074 judged the three statements graded. I keep it.
- **Fugato 65 and 69**: Vln II enters on A5, and Vln I's stretto entry at 69 starts on the same A5 (PDF 144, V-21: each entry in its own register). The exposition and stretto are protected (c007, c097, c107, c157). The stretto entry is coloured by winds and lies at the top of the texture, so it still reads as new. No change.
- **Trio first statement 153–168**: the Cb plucks in unison with the harp's bass. Adler PDF 130 would allow dropping the Cb there for lightness. But c148 set this pizz. pp on purpose, and c129/c157 protect the passage. The harp plus pizz. bass is a standard blend. No change.
- **40 and 272**: the violas' off-beat D4 meets theme A's low D4 on beat 3 (PDF 135). It is one beat of a staccato line and does not cover it. No change.
- **101–104 and 301–304**: the violas' counter-line crosses above Vln II's lower octave of the hemiola melody. Vln I carries the melody above, so only a doubling is crossed (PDF 144 rule 3 concerns theme against countertheme). No change.
- **Bars 45–56**: the bass is in four instruments at f (Vc, Cb, Bsn 1, Cbsn) (PDF 151: loud close scoring is bass-heavy). The melody has four instruments in two octaves, and the bass is a moving line, not a close chord. No change.
