## Holistic comparison with Schumann 4/iii

The plans match at the points that matter.
- **Form.** Schumann's scherzo is a D-minor ternary design with a soft trio in B♭. After the second scherzo the trio comes back and does not close. It thins out and runs straight into the slow link to the finale. This scherzo does the same at full size. The coda's second stage (341–356) is the trio tune at the trio tempo. It thins to Hn 1's motto tail (357–360) and to Hn 1 and Vln I on G♯ (369–376), the note the finale takes up. Schumann's movement is not self-contained, and neither is this one. The plan holds up against the master because the trio's return is the transition, not a tune quoted after a cadence.
- **Cyclic material.** Schumann's trio takes its figure from the Romanze's violin solo. Here T grows out of the horn call of bar 3 (themes-scherzo.md) and the motto tail is quoted, not developed. Both are cyclic in the same way.
- **Scoring.** Schumann's scherzo starts forte, with the tune doubled in octaves across strings and winds, as Adler PDF 248 says of his orchestration. This scherzo starts leggiero pp with Vln I alone (13–28) and keeps the octave tutti for 41, 105, 305 and 333. That is closer to Dvořák 9/iii and Mendelssohn than to Schumann. Adler's reading supports it: the change of colour from statement to statement (PDF 139, 238) is what lets the four entries of theme A be heard as stages. Schumann's way (unbroken doubling) would cost the build at 29–41 that critics have kept since c007. I would not make the opening heavier to look like Schumann.
- **The trio.** Schumann's trio sets the violins' line over held woodwind chords. Adler PDF 241–243 describes this kind of sustained wind background behind active strings. This trio's first statement reverses it: clarinet tune, horn drone, harp off-beats. The second statement is strings only, and the third uses both choirs. Adler PDF 253 (Schumann 1/iii trio, choirs apart then together) describes this order exactly. That is a sound reason for a choice Schumann did not make.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote it?** On the scoring, yes. I checked the reading's 41 applicable principles bar by bar and found no place where the score breaks one. Spacing, doubling of inversions, the use of pedals, saving registers, the third-time change, and the colours that recur with their ideas all follow Adler's examples. What a conductor might question is beyond scoring: whether theme A and T stay in the memory as long as Schumann's scherzo theme does. Nobody in this loop can hear the music, and no principle in this reading decides that.

### Considered and not proposed
1. **330 beat 2, cellos F♯2 over the D2 pedal (Cbsn, bass clarinet).** This is a major third below C3 (Adler PDF 151, 257: wide gaps at the bottom). It is one staccato quaver of the coda ostinato under p<, on the ♮VII that c083 asked to be heard. Moving it would bend motive x's contour in the cellos (E–B–E, A–F♯–A, F–C–F). It does not justify the change.
2. **15–16 and 255–256, cellos doubling Vln I's dip to B3 (Shostakovich 6/iii, PDF 141).** Shostakovich's tune is arco with arco accompaniment. Here the theme is spiccato over pizz. downbeats (do-not-touch since c006). An arco cello doubling would thicken the leggiero, and a pizz. one would blur the downbeat pattern. I did not propose it.
3. **153–168 scored the same in antecedent and consequent (Grieg, PDF 155).** The plain first statement is the reference that the later colourings vary (c089, c224). Adler's own Schumann 1/iii example (PDF 253) supports keeping one choir per statement.
4. **Making the opening Schumann-heavy (octave tutti from bar 13).** Rejected above: it would undo the four-stage build 13–41 and go against Adler PDF 139 and 238.
