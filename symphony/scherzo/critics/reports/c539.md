## What else I studied

**Franck, Symphony in D minor, ii.** There is no Franck score in the corpus. I used my knowledge of the score and these sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_in_D_minor_(Franck)), [Music With Ease](https://musicwithease.com/franck-symphony-no-2.html), [UC Davis note](https://arts.ucdavis.edu/post/franck-symphony-d-minor), [Classical Explorer](https://classicalexburns.com/2021/08/16/cesar-franck-symphony-in-d-minor-a-fresh-style/).
- The Allegretto (B♭ minor) opens with the cor anglais over plucked strings and harp. The scherzo section that follows is a light, fast violin figure in triplet quavers, so that three of its bars make one bar of the Allegretto.
- Franck's defining move is the last stage of that scherzo: the whole cor anglais period returns *on top of* the scherzo figure. The two characters of the movement, cantabile and scherzando, are heard at once. This is the cyclic combination for which the symphony is known.

**Measured against it, v539** already has Franck's colours: wind solos over harp and pizzicato (153, 341), a light staccato main theme, and quiet superimposition at the end (timpani motive x under the horn's G♯, 363–371). What it lacks is Franck's central idea. The trio's tune T and the scherzo never share a bar: T is absent from every scherzo passage, and theme A never sounds under T. A conductor would take the movement for skilled late-Romantic work in the Dvořák line. Placed beside Franck, its two halves would look like two sets of material rather than one. Finding 1 deals with this. It uses a line that is already there.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 105, 253, 305: the tenor countermelody opens with T's head in E minor (applied)

**Problem.** The tenor countermelody under theme A (Bsn 1, Hn 3·4 and cellos at 105; cellos alone pp at 253; Bsn 1, Hn 3 and cellos at 305) opens `g2. | b2 e'4~`: G3, then B3–E4. T opens `gis2 a4 | b2 e'4`: G♯, A, B, E in the same rhythm. So the countermelody is T's head in E minor with the passing A left out. Restoring that one note makes the trio's tune sound inside the scherzo, which is Franck's combination of the two characters.
- At 105, before the trio, it is a preview in minor. The trio at 153 then sounds like the major form of something already heard.
- At 253 and 305, after the trio, it is a recall: the trio sounds under the scherzo's theme.

No note other than one crotchet per statement changes. Rhythm, register, dynamics, players and the rest of the line stay as they are.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 133–134 (Ex. 272–274, Tsar Saltan, PDF 438–440): two figures sound together clearly when their timbre and harmonic function differ. Ex. 273–274 put a held wind line over a moving cello/viola figure in the same tempo. Here a legato tenor in bowed strings and horns sits under staccato theme A and plucked or staccato off-beats.
- PDF 132–133 (principle 16): a non-harmonic note in a florid line is less harsh when its timbre differs from the fundamental notes. The new A3 is a passing note on beat 3, between G3 and B3, in arco cellos, horns and bassoon. The fundamental notes around it are the violas' off-beat B3/G4 (pizz. at 253) and the bass E.
- PDF 102 (rimsky-principles, ch. III, cited in c529): give a second to an instrument of a different colour from the note it clashes with. The A3–B3 second is horn/cello against viola.
- Franck ii: the cor anglais period over the scherzo figure (Music With Ease, above).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-539/work).** Each change is the countermelody's first bar only:

### 2. Bars 209–224: no scherzo figure under T's forte return (not applied)

Franck's combination at the cantabile's own return would mean putting a scherzo figure under T at 209. c537 rejected viola quavers there because they would sit in the clarinet's register and blur T. Rimsky PDF 132 agrees: a florid figure in the same register as the melody sounds harsh. The harp at 209–224 is protected (c004, c028, c274). Finding 1 does the combination the other way round, with T's head under theme A, and touches no protected bar. No change.
