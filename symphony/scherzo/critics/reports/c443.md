## Findings

### 1. Bar 330: the ♮VII of the coda's falling bass is blurred by Vln II's leftover E–B (applied)

- **Bars.** 329–332, the four bars of bass E–D–C–B before the ff theme head at 333.
- **Problem.**
  - Bar 330 is the only place in the movement where Unit 15's ♮VII → IV6 → V7 progression is the harmony of a full-string forte passage.
  - The cellos (A2–F♯2), violas (F♯3–D3), Vln I (A5–F♯5), the bass D and Bsn 1's A3 all sound D major.
  - Vln II repeats bar 329's tonic motive E5–B4–B4–E5. On beats 1 and 3 the strings therefore sound D–E–F♯–A. On beat 2, where Vln I, violas and cellos play their quaver pair, Vln II's B4 quavers turn the beat into B–D–F♯ over F♯2 in the cellos, a minor v6/4.
  - The listener does not hear the book's chord. Bar 330 alternates two unrelated chords, at mf< in the full string section.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 241: ♮VII's "two upper tones also belong to V7", so ♮VII "becomes absorbed into the V7 chord". PDF 242, Ex. 15-15 and 15-16, and Points for Review 9 (PDF 245) show ♮VII as a complete triad whose 5th (4̂) prepares V7's seventh. E and B are not tones of ♮VII and not tones of V7.
  - c032 recorded the E–B drone as giving way "to chord tones at 331". c083 then moved the giving-way to 330 in Vc and violas, citing these same examples, and left Vln II behind. The bar now does half of what c083 intended.
  - Dvořák 9/iii states ♮VII complete each time (What else).
- **Fix (applied).** In coda.ily, `codaVlnTwo`, bar 330 (the sixth bar of the line):
  ```
  e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-.   →   fis''4-. d''8-. d''-. fis''4-.
  ```
  - All strings now sound D–F♯–A on every beat.
  - Vln II's motive x lies a third under Vln I's A5–F♯5, as Vln I lies a tenth over the bass (Ex. 15-16).
  - F♯5 falls a semitone to F5 at 331, the root of the Neapolitan 6/4, with Cl 1's tail F♯4–F4 an octave lower.
  - Before the change, Vln II went E5→F5 while Vln I went A5→F6: similar motion into an octave. Now the two violin parts reach the octave F in contrary motion.
- **Checks.**
  - The MIDI dump at 330 now reads D–F♯–A on all six half beats; before, it read D–E–F♯–A on beats 1 and 3 and D–F♯–A–B on beat 2.
  - No other bar changed.
  - analysis.txt lists the same single outer-voice parallel (69, by design). The bass, the dynamics, every entry of the stretto, the B6/fff at 336 and the horns are unchanged.
  - Vln II still plays at 330, and no cue in parts-cues.json points at Vln II in these bars (the cues near 325–337 point at Vc and Vln I, which still sound), so no cue moves.
- **Protection answered.** c263 listed "coda build" as do-not-touch without a reason, and c403 kept the two-stage coda for its I–IV–I proportions. Neither covers Vln II's inner pitches at 330. The build's order of entries, its pp<mf<f<ff, the falling bass and the peak are untouched.

### 2. Bar 300: the cross-relation A → A♯ at the Italian sixth (checked, kept)

The bass A1 moves to C2 on beat 3, and Vln I takes A♯5. The flutes' and oboes' A5 stop on beat 3, so A and A♯ never sound together. PDF 236 calls a soprano–bass cross-relation harsh when it involves natural 7̂ against raised 7̂ (Ex. 15-8c). This one is 4̂–♯4̂ of an augmented sixth, which Units 28–29 treat separately. It was approved by c422 with a recorded reason. Kept.

### 3. Augmented III after c441's cadence change (checked, kept)

c441's new hemiola cadence puts G over B at 130 and 286 (G5–F♯5 tied over V). The scan reads G–B–D♯ there, as at 98 and 246. All four are 6/3 sonorities over the dominant bass, the kind PDF 240 says express dominant harmony. The 314 case that c353 fixed still has E on beat 2. Nothing new to fix.

### 4. 35–37 ♮VII as V of III (checked, kept)

The strings' D at 35–36 is complete, with D in the bass, F♯ and A off-beat, and motive x on D–A. III arrives at 37, is reinforced by its V7 at 41–44, and is confirmed by the cadence at 53–55, as in Ex. 15-1 and 15-3. I considered ♮VII7 (C on beat 3 of 36) and rejected it, because it would anticipate the D7 that makes 41–43 a new event.
