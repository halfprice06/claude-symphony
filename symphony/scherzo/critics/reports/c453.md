## Findings

### 1. The B area has no punctuation at its close: timpani B pp at 184 (applied)

- **Bars.** 183–184, compared with 167–168.
- **Problem.** PDF 216 asks a key area for "a punctuation in the music's flow that helps set off and emphasize the new tonic". PDF 224 says the quatrain's two A phrases cadence in different places, one on I and the other on V. The trio has both cadences, and the score already marks the first one: at 168 the timpani strike E pp under Cl 1's held E. The second A ends on B at 183–184 with nothing in the bass at 184 except Bsn 2's B2 (the basses stop at 183, the harp rests 169–184). The arrival on V is the trio's only modulation, and it ends more weakly than the I cadence before it.
- **Evidence.** PDF 216 §6; PDF 224 (quatrain); PDF 219 §10 (the area's last chord is I of V and V of I: at 184 the stroke is both). In this movement the timpani are a structural voice (Beethoven 9/ii model; the movement opens and closes with timpani motive x). With the new stroke, the timpani's trio notes outline the trio's key plan: E 168, B 184, G♯ 188 and 200 (V of vi), B roll 205–208, E 209. The stroke is pp, on the fourth drum already tuned to B, and comes in a bar where only the held B sounds. It does not add to the texture of the tune at 169–183 (c436: "thin rather than add" there).
- **Fix (applied).** tA.ily: `tATimp = { e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2.*3 | R2.*11 | e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2.*15 | b,4\pp r4 r4 | }` (was `R2.*16` at the end). parts-cues.json, part "23": add `["cueVc","Vc.","UP",181,3]`, so the timpani part shows the cellos' D♯–F♯–B | B–A♯–C♯ | B before the entry after 15 bars' rest (c435 rule). I checked the cue against the sections: the cellos sound in all of 181–183. Timpani part: still 2 pages, multi-rest 169–180, then the cue, then B pp.

### 2. Exposition repeat 13–56: the largest change the reading allows, built and rejected

- **Bars.** 13–56.
- **Issue.** PDF 224: "in quatrain-form … dance movements (especially minuets), both halves will usually repeat". Dvořák 9/iii repeats 1–60 and Beethoven 9/ii repeats its exposition. The movement runs 4:42 in the MIDI against the plan's "about 5 minutes". A listener would hear this change more than any other the unit supports, so I built it in /tmp/critic-453/rep: `\repeat volta 2` around every sA variable and the sA span of global.ily (the `\bar "||"` at 57 removed), and `\unfoldRepeats` on the MIDI score.
- **Result.** 0 warnings, 13 pages, MIDI 308 s (+30 s).
- **Why it makes the movement worse.**
  - Proportion. The first scherzo part grows from 97 s to 127 s, while the reprise stays at 49 s, 39 % of it. Dvořák's reprise is 60 % of his scherzo. The movement would sit heavily at the front and lose the "keeps moving" of the lens.
  - Saturation. PDF 224's repeat belongs to a small dance whose halves are heard only once. This scherzo's first part is already a sonata form (exposition 13–56, development 57–104, recapitulation 105–140), and its return "is effected by the events of the development section" (PDF 226). The recapitulation 105–140 is the written-out, varied repeat. With the repeat, theme A's quatrain would sound complete six times (13, 13, 105, 253, 305, plus coda 333), and the counterstatement four times.
  - Playing. At 56→13 the cellos and basses would go from arco G to pizz. E in two beats, and the parts would need repeat signs added in 30 staves.
- The stronger reason than the one on record (c183/c363 only weighed a trio repeat) is the first point: a repeat would put the proportions out of balance.
- **Decision.** Not applied.

### 3. The half cadence at 159–160 is now applied, not diatonic (keep; correct the record)

- **Bars.** 159–160 (Cl 1 E5–D♯5–A♯4~ | A♯4–B4 over harp F♯–C♯–E, then B).
- **Issue.** c363 graded T's half cadences by PDF 215: diatonic at 160, applied at 176, 4–♯4–5 at 216. Since c421 (v422), the tune's A♯ on beat 3 of 159 completes F♯7, so 160 is a V7/V → V semicadence, the "particularly effective" kind, and the harp's missing third is supplied by the clarinet.
- **Evidence.** PDF 215, Ex. 14-4. An antecedent's semicadence is meant to be a goal, and the consequent's restart at 161 supplies the continuation. The three cadences still differ in their bass (root 159, C♯ in the cellos 175, A–A♯–B 213–216), so the grading now runs through the bass line and no longer through the chord.
- **Decision.** Keep. The composer should replace c363's "160 diatonic" note with this one.

### 4. Theme A's b phrase ends on a diatonic V (keep, with the reading's own reason)

- **Bars.** 23–24 (and 115–116, 265–266, 317–318): Vln I F♯–G–A–B over iv, then C6–B–F♯ over B.
- **Issue.** The line 2–3–4–5 could become 3–♯4–5 (A♯) over vii°7 of V, the semicadence the unit praises.
- **Evidence.** PDF 215 gives the reason against it. Over the diatonic chord "the V wants to continue moving", and bar 24 must move on at once into the returning a at 25 (quatrain B "will lead back to the tonic", PDF 224). An applied goal-semicadence would stop the music at the one place in theme A that must not stop. The change would also alter theme A in four statements.
- **Decision.** Keep. Not built.

### 5. The trio's way back from B (keep)

- **Bars.** 189–208.
- **Evidence.** This passage follows the order of PDF 219–220:
  - 189–192 in B ends on its V (F♯ with A♯);
  - 193 turns A♯ into A♮ in Cl 1 (4 of E, which "neutralizes the temporary key");
  - 195–196 is B7, followed by a deceptive move to vi at 197 (PDF 219: a deceptive cadence before the real one);
  - 201–202 is IV–iv;
  - 203–208 is a V pedal whose top line, Hn 1's motto tail, ends on A♮ at 208, so V7 stands before the return at 209.
- **Decision.** Keep.

### 6. Length and proportions (keep)

- **Figures.** 97 / 88 / 49 / 44 s. The trio's B cadence comes at bar 31 of its 72 bars before the codetta, near the middle as PDF 227 asks.
- **Decision.** Keep, for the reasons in finding 2.

### 7. The trio codetta is 16 bars, not "tiny" (keep)

- **Bars.** 225–240.
- **Issue.** PDF 220 calls a coda tiny. I considered cutting the Cl 1 echo at 229–232.
- **Why not.** The cut would save 3.2 s. It would also renumber 233–376, and the finale and the records quote those bar numbers (closing timpani 363–371, motto tail 357–360). The third statement (Bsn 233–236, C♮) does the harmonic work of preparing E minor, so the first two statements are the varied repetitions of the cadence that the reading describes.
- **Decision.** Keep.
