## Holistic comparison with Elgar 2/iii

- **Climax.** Elgar's Rondo builds its climax from an ostinato that recalls earlier music, then breaks off. This movement's coda does the same in its own terms. Motive x enters as a stretto ostinato from the bottom up (325–332). It grows to the fff B6 at 336, ends on the struck chord at 339 and a general pause (340), and the trio's world returns. Here the recalled element is the motto's chromatic tail (Cl 1, now with the C.a., 329–332), which plays the part Elgar gives the "malign" recall. A conductor would recognise the design.
- **Density.** Elgar keeps his inner parts busy and full; this score is leaner throughout. The leanness is a choice of the plan (the models are Dvořák 9/iii and Beethoven 9/ii). The sparse passages (13–28, the fugato, 89–100, the trio's opening) are each protected with recorded reasons, and the reading gives none stronger.
- **Fortes.** Elgar never leaves a forte chord hollow in the middle. This score has one such place, twice: the f stretto at 70 and 298. That is the finding below.
- **Verdict.** On orchestration and form a conductor would believe a master wrote this. The open question earlier critics kept raising, the memorability of T and of the second idea, is a melodic one. This orchestration reading gives no grounds to rewrite either theme.

## Findings

### 1. 70 and 298: the f stretto chord is empty between E3 and A4 (applied)

**Problem.** In the fugato stretto (sB) and its reprise (dB), bar 70/298 sounds the following, at f:
- top line: Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I on C6–B5–B♭5;
- Vln II's entry: E5;
- violas: A4–G♯4;
- cellos and Bsn 1: E3;
- bass: the A pedal in Bsn 2, Cbsn and Cb.

Nothing lies between E3 and A4, an octave and a fourth. The chord's third, C, sounds only in the top line. The horns fill the middle from 71, but bar 70 is hollow. c419 found this "the only real empty middle in a forte chord in the whole movement". It kept the gap because "changing an entry would undo the stretto".

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages."
- PDF 96 rule 1: modern orchestrators "do not allow any void in the intermediate parts". Rule 2: wide intervals in the bass, closer ones above.
- In the forte examples of the range, the middle register is always filled under moving upper lines: Ex. 84 (Tsar Saltan, PDF 257) and Ex. 86 (Sadko, PDF 260).

**Answer to c419's reason.** The fix needs no stretto entry. Cl 2 is free at 70–71/298–299 and enters at 72/300 on A4 anyway, so it can take the middle a bar and a half early.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/sB.ily (sBClTwo, bars 70–71) and sections/dB.ily (dBClTwo, bars 298–299):

```
\partCombineApart c'2\f r4 | << cis'2. { s4 s2\< } >> | \partCombineAutomatic a'2.->\ff\>    % sB; dB ends a'2->\ff r4 as before
```

- **Beats 1–2: C4.** It adds the A-minor third a tenth above the cellos' E3, as rule 2 asks.
- **Beat 3: rest.** C4 would make an augmented fifth with the violas' G♯4 and clash with the passing B♭5.
- **Bar 71: C♯4.** Cl 2 takes the A-major third under the horns' motive x and crescendos with Cl 1, then rises to its own A4 ff. The line C–C♯ moves an octave under the violas' C5–C♯5 of 69–71, not in parallel with it.
- **Dynamics.** Cl 2's f equals Cl 1's f at 70 on the shared staff. Its hairpin starts with Cl 1's, on beat 2 of 71.
- **Engraving.** `\partCombineApart` stops the combiner printing a "1." on beat 3.
- **Low register.** Concert C4 is written E♭4 for the A clarinet, a strong chalumeau note at f.
- **Not affected.** No entry, dynamic or articulation of the stretto changes, and no C-major triad results.
- **Parts.** Cl 2 now enters after 13 bars' rest (57–69). I added a cue to parts-cues.json: `["cueVlnTwo","Vln. II","UP",65,4]` in part "07". Vln II plays its fugato entry in 65–68. At 298 the rest is 9 bars (289–297), so no cue was added.

### 3. Considered, kept: open fourths and fifths inside one colour

Rule 4 on PDF 90 asks for concords inside one colour. Four places have discords, none in the oboes, which the rule singles out:
- Cl 1·2 A4/E4 at 33–34 (pp);
- Bsn 1·2 E3/B3 at 29–30 and D3/A3 at 41–43;
- Bsn 1·2 B3/E4 at 145 and 147–148 (pp).

The bassoon fifths are the low part of the chord, where PDF 96 rule 2 puts wide intervals. Revoicing 33–34 would either lose the chord's fifth or place a fourth in the bassoons instead. 29–37 is protected (c304, c399). All are soft. No change.
