## Findings

### 1. Bar 219: the trio's ff peak bar has no fifth on its downbeat, and Bsn 1 leaves the choir to double the countermelody for one bar (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 219 is bar 11 of T at its forte return: the violins' A6 tied over the bar line at ff, harmonised as ii (F♯–A–C♯; c012). On the downbeat the orchestra sounds only F♯ and A. A6 is in Fl 1 and Vln I; A5 in Cl 1 and Vln II; A4 in Hn 1; F♯4 in Hn 2; A3 in Bsn 1, Bsn 2, Hn 4 and the cellos; F♯2 and F♯1 in the bass clarinet and basses. C♯ arrives only with the violas' off-beat at beat 2. The A is in nine parts. The choir that sustains the harmony through 209–224 (Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1, c014/c047/c088) is complete at 217 (G♯4–E4–C♯4) and 218 (G♯4–E4–B3). At 219 it collapses onto A4–F♯4–A3, because Bsn 1 drops to A3 and joins the cellos' countermelody (Vc, Bsn 2, Hn 4) for exactly this one bar. At 220 it leaves again for F♯3.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 85: "It is necessary to make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts." The "To be avoided" chords on that page (read from the image) are full-sounding chords with a harmonic note missing above the bass. That is the case at 219.1, at ff, on the loudest bar of the trio.
- PDF 90, rule 1: instruments forming chords "must be doubled or not throughout, except when one of the harmonic parts is to be made prominent." The countermelody is Vc + Bsn 2 + Hn 4 from 217 to 224. Bsn 1 adds a fourth instrument to it for one bar at its least prominent point, a held A3 under the violins' climax, and leaves again.
- PDF 97, remark 4: with the top part in the strings, the wind takes the sustained middle harmony with "a moderately full tone", and a three-part wind harmony "should form an independent whole, receiving no assistance from the bass." The choir's three notes at 219 hold only two pitch classes.

**Fix (applied, sections/tC.ily, tCBsnOne bar 11):**

```
old: cis'2.--(\< | b2.-- | a2.--\f\> | fis2.-- |
new: cis'2.--(\< | b2.-- | cis'2.--\f\> | fis2.-- |
```

The downbeat of 219 is now A6, A5, A4, F♯4, C♯4, A3, F♯2, F♯1, a complete ii with the choir alone holding A4–F♯4–C♯4. Bsn 1's line becomes C♯4–B3–C♯4–F♯3–B3–A3–G♯3, a neighbour figure and then its original descent. I checked the voice leading against every part. 218→219: Bsn 1 B3→C♯4 against the cellos E3→A3 goes from a fifth to a third; against the bass G♯→F♯ the motion is contrary; it makes parallel sixths with Hn 1 and parallel fourths with Hn 2, both inner. 219→220: Bsn 1 C♯4→F♯3 against the bass F♯2→A2 goes from a twelfth to a sixth, with no octaves or fifths. Both bassoons stay \f\> on the shared staff, so the part-combiner prints two voices at 219 and "a2" again at 220.

I considered two alternatives. Bsn 1 A3 at 220 makes consecutive octaves with the basses (A1→B1 against A3→B3 into 221). Bsn 1 holding C♯4 through 220 clashes with Hn 1's C♮5 on beat 3 (c492). Swapping the horns to C♯5/A4 at 219 makes octaves between Hn 2 and the cellos (E→A). The bassoon change is the only voicing that keeps c094's horn thirds, c492's C♮5 and the countermelody intact.

**Checks.** Build clean, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, analysis.txt unchanged (one parallel, bar 69), themecheck 52/52. Parts: Bsn 1 only changes pitch inside a bar it already played, so no rest, entry or cue in parts-cues.json is affected. Not a theme change.

### 2. Bar 221: the cadential 6/4 lacks its E on the downbeat (not applied; decision: keep)

At 221.1 the sounding notes are G♯ and B (G♯5, B4, G♯4, B3, B2, B1). E comes in the violas on beat 2 and in the tune on beat 3. PDF 85 applies here too. Each remedy breaks something stronger. ### 3. Bar 336: the oboe pair in a fourth, B5/F♯5, at fff (not applied; decision: keep)

PDF 90, rule 4 asks for concords in a same-kind pair, "specially" for the oboes. At 336 this is the only oboe discord in the movement. F♯5 in Ob 2 is the only F♯ in the upper half of the B7 chord; the other F♯s are in Hn 2·3 at F♯4 and Bsn 1 at F♯3. Moving Ob 2 to D♯5 would remove the chord's fifth from the upper parts, which breaks PDF 85. One bar at fff under two flutes and two violin sections does not expose the oboes. Keep, as c454 decided.

### 4. Bars 145–148: the bassoon pair in a fourth, E4/B3, pp (not applied; decision: keep)

Rule 4 again. The concord would be G♯3/E4 in Bsn 2/Bsn 1. That doubles Hn 1's G♯4, the pivot note the trio grows from (c051), in the bassoon's most covered register directly under it. The fourth is soft, framed by the bass clarinet's E2 below (PDF 94: a low third instrument under a pair sounds well), and c413's 5–6–5 at 146 turns it into a sixth for one bar. Keep.

### 5. Bars 177–184: the bass lies more than two octaves below the cellos' T at 178–179 (not applied; decision: keep)

PDF 85 says the bass should "rarely" lie more than an octave below the next part. At 179 it lies F♯2 under A4. PDF 86 allows such distribution in piano, and this is p. HISTORY records this statement as full and asks later critics to thin rather than add (c461, c436, line 521). The emptiness is part of the chamber colour of the trio's second statement. Keep.

### 6. Bars 29–30 and 33–34: open fifth in the bassoons and open fourth in the clarinets under the first wind call (not applied; decision: keep)

PDF 97, remark 4, first case: a wind harmony that stands alone should avoid open fourths and fifths. At 29–30 the call has Cl G4/E4 over Bsn B3/E3. At 33–34 it has Cl A4/E4 over Bsn C4/A2. Every concord voicing I tried moves the discord to the other pair, or puts Bsn 2 on E2 at pp, the insecure low register that c036 avoided. Both chords are complete and in close position, which PDF 96 remark 1 asks for first. Keep.
