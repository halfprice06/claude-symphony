## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 33–35: the second wind call repeats the first call's colour; give Ob 1 a new partner, Cl 1 (applied)

**Problem.** The counterstatement is a call and response: winds 29–30, strings 31–32, winds 33–34, strings 35–36, all together from 37. Both wind calls are Fl 1 + Ob 1 in unison, over the same pp clarinet-bassoon chord. Both string answers are Vln I + Vc. So for eight bars the listener hears two colours alternating, the same pair each time. This is the first time the woodwinds speak in the movement.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 253–254 (Eroica, Ex. VIII-20): Beethoven passes the three-note gesture through oboe, clarinet, flute and first violin, a different instrument each time, and sums it up with the tutti. PDF 271: because each woodwind has its own colour, alternation and counterpoint among winds work well. PDF 253 (Schumann): the choirs are heard apart, then together. That plan is kept, since the strings answer and everything joins at 37. PDF 261: one colour characterises a gesture. If Ob 1 stays on all three calls (29, 33, 37), the call keeps one constant colour while its partner changes. PDF 281 lists Tchaikovsky 2/iii's trio for "oboes in combination with clarinets". The oboe-clarinet unison is also the colour of the first theme of Schubert 8/i. Dvořák 9/iii, the movement's main model, also passes its first soft statement from one wind to another (oboe, then clarinet).

**Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily):**

```
sAFluteOne 33–35  old: e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | a''2->( gis''8 g''8 | fis''4-.) r4 r4
                  new: R2. | R2. | R2.
sAClOne    33–35  old: a'2.~ | a'2. | fis'4 r4 r4
                  new: e''4-.\p b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | a''2->( gis''8 g''8 | fis''4-.) r4 r4
sAClTwo    33     old: e'2.~ |        new: e'2.~\p |
```

Ob 1 is unchanged. The call's pitches, rhythm, articulation and register (E5–A5) are unchanged, and so is the harmony (A minor, the pivot at 33–34). Cl 1 plays E5–A5 (written G5–C6 for clarinet in A), in the clarion. The held chord under the call is now A2 (Bsn 2), C4 (Bsn 1), E4 (Cl 2), with the A1 pizz. at 33: a complete triad with the widest gap at the bottom (PDF 257, Ex. VIII-24). On beat 1 of 35 the D-major chord keeps D3, A3 and D4 under the call's F♯5. Both clarinets are marked p at 33 because they share a staff and enter together (Cl 2 restrikes after its rest at 32). Cl 2's E4 lies in the clarinet's soft throat register and the bassoons stay pp, so the chord does not rise above the call. The colour sequence is now Fl+Ob, strings, Ob+Cl, strings, all, and the reprise's compressed version (Bsn 1 at 269, Vln I at 271, tutti at 273) already uses a third wind colour. I did not use the cor anglais an octave below the oboe (PDF 250–251): its E4–A4 line would cross the held A4 and E4 inside the chord. I did not use Fl 1 + Cl 1 either, because the oboe would then not be the thread through all three calls. Fl 1 now rests 33–36 and re-enters at 37 with its own p<, so it needs no cue. The cues in parts-cues.json near these bars (parts 02, 04, 05, 13 at 37, all on Vln I) still point at a part that plays there.

**Why I kept it.** The protections on 29–37 (c077, c153, c302/c1023, c477) are for the call-and-response plan and the Fl/Ob unison that avoids an early A6 (c055). The plan, the unison principle and the register are all kept. Only the second call's partner instrument changes, which is the change the reading asks for. No rule is touched: there is no new note above B6, no C-major triad, no trumpet and no tremolo.
