## Findings

### 1. Bars 225–236: the trio codetta's accompaniment stays the same for three units, so the movement stands still (applied)

**Problem.** The codetta presents T's head three times over the E pedal: Ob 1 at 225, Cl 1 at 229, Bsn 1 at 233. Each unit is gis2 a4 | b2. | cis2. (C♮ at 235) | b2 r4. The foreground already changes colour and register at each entry. The background does not change: the harp plays off-beat chords on beats 2 and 3 in all twelve bars, the cellos and basses pluck E or A on every downbeat, and Vln II and the violas hold. The density profile shows it: 53 attacks and 7.0 voices in each of 225–228, 229–232 and 233–236. Then everything stops at 237 for the held chord and the poco rit. So the accompaniment runs at full speed for twelve bars and halts in one bar, and the decay is left to the tempo alone. This is the stall c133 identified. The bars were kept because a cut would renumber 237–376, which the finale cites, and because a soft da capo needs a long approach. Both reasons still hold, so the fix has to keep the length.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- In the Mozart flute concerto excerpt (PDF 267–268, Ex. XVII-10), the accompaniment figure changes with each phrase. The strings play off-beat quavers under the first solo run (59–64), the tutti lasts four bars (65–68), and the next solo phrase (69–74) has a different figure. Adler's point 1 is that the accompaniment under the solo is sparse.
- In Haydn's "The Heavens Are Telling" (PDF 288, Ex. XVII-24), the doubling changes in the second phrase. Adler: "Devices like these add great variety to accompaniments."
- Sibelius and Bartók (PDF 268–270) show that a thinner background gives the line more space.

Taken together, the background should change from unit to unit, not only the solo instrument. Here, where the music is closing, the natural change is to thin it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-153/work/sections/tC.ily, tCHarpR only).** The harp's pulse thins by unit, 3 → 2 → 1 per bar. Pitches and register are unchanged (c047's chord positions above Bsn 1; c042's C♮ at 235):

```lilypond
% 225-228 unchanged (beats 2 and 3)
r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4 | r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4 | r4 <a cis' e'>4 <a cis' e'>4 | r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4 |
% 229-232 beat 2 only (under Cl 1)
r4 <gis b e'>4 r4 | r4 <gis b e'>4 r4 | r4 <a cis' e'>4 r4 | r4 <gis b e'>4 r4 |
% 233-236 one chord with the downbeat (under Bsn 1)
<e' gis' b'>4 r4 r4 | <e' gis' b'>4_\markup \small "C♮" r4 r4 | <e' a' c''>4 r4 r4 | <e' gis' b'>4 r4 r4 |
```

**Result.**
- The attacks per unit fall from 53 to 41 to 41. The off-beat pulse is gone by 233, so the poco rit. at 237 and the held arpeggiated chord finish a slowing that has already started.
- Bsn 1, the weakest of the three solos, now has the most open texture. That fits Adler PDF 267 (a sparse accompaniment for the soloist) and c126's reason for taking Hn 3 away from it.
- Nothing protected changes: not the length, not the one-wind-per-colour rule (c127), not the register (c047), not the iv at 235 (c042), not the exit 237–252.
- The figure returns in full under Hn 1 at 341 in the coda, so the recall is unaffected.
- This does not undo any earlier fix.

### Considered and kept (decided)

- **The share of slow music (about 47% of the time).** This counts the intro, the link, the trio, the retransition, the G.P. and the Meno mosso. It is more than Dvořák's or Beethoven's share. I keep it:
  - The da capo returns softly (c133), and the slow end carries the cyclic G♯ into the finale.
  - The movement's length (4:36) is right between a 5:45 Adagio and a 7-minute finale.
  - Finding 1 makes the longest slow stretch move without cutting bars, and that was the part that stalled.
- **Two E-pedal closes (225–240 and 357–376).** The second differs: it is preceded by the motto tail over A♯°7 and iv, ends on G♯ alone, and has the timpani's motive x. The closing page is protected for cyclic reasons. Kept.
- **The link 133–152 and the retransition 241–252.** These are sparse expectant backgrounds of the kind Adler praises (PDF 268–270): held winds, harp and timpani pulses, and the head passed from viola to violins. They are protected (c103–c143). Kept.
- **Coda stages: Più mosso 9.4 s against Meno mosso 31 s.** The fast stage is short, but its peak is the movement's only fff and B6, and the theme returns before it in full twice (105, 305). Kept (c063, c123).
- **Harp chords 341–344 just under Hn 1.** The chords are a third or more below the horn and in contrary rhythm, and Adler (PDF 274) says rhythmic contrast lessens the need for distance. The horn is in its strong middle register. Kept.
- **Forte–subito piano (PDF 190).** At 120→121, 276→277 and 288→289 the loud music ends on a staccato crotchet followed by two beats of rest, so there is no held chord to overlap. 72→73 already overlaps. Not applicable.
