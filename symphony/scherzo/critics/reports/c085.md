## What else I studied

I read critic reports c006 and c018 (both on Adler ch. 2) and the v07 and v19 critique notes, so as not to repeat their points. c018 set the standard this movement already follows elsewhere: two crotchet rests is too little for pizz.→arco at this tempo, and the pizz. note before an arco entry becomes a rest when another instrument covers the bass (44, 100, 168, 280, 364). Three of my four pizz./arco places came in after that cycle (276's D♯ came with c052; 192, 208 and 272 were not checked then). ## Findings

### 1. Pizz.→arco after two crotchet rests in four places (applied)
**Bars:** Cb 192→193 and 208→209 (trio tempo, 0.53 s); Vln II and Vla 272→273, Vc 276→277 (2. = 88, 0.45 s).
**Problem:** In each place the player plucks on a beat and must play arco on the third beat after it. 193 and 209 are arco entries at p< and mf; 273 and 277 are f and sub. p arco entries.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck and then again to resume the natural way of playing … these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible. (This is especially true of the return from pizzicato to arco …)". Brahms 1/iv (Ex. II-55, PDF 54–55) puts rests around each change. This movement already follows the same rule at 44, 100, 168, 280 and 364 (c018).
**Fix (applied):** the pluck before the arco entry becomes a rest; another instrument keeps the note.
- tB.ily `tBCb` bar 192 `R2.` (harp LH F♯1 at 192 is the same pitch); bar 208 `R2.` (Vc B2 held, timpani B).
- dA.ily `dAVlnTwo`/`dAVla` bar 272 `R2.` (was `r4 b4 r4`, `r4 fis4-. r4`; Vln I's F♯–D♯–B and the bass B state the chord).
- dA.ily `dAVc` bar 276 `R2.` (was `dis4`; Bsn 2 plays the same D♯3 on that beat, Cb already rests, as Vc and Cb rest in the exposition's bar 44).
Each change now has five beats. The analyser's outer-voice list is unchanged (7).

### 2. No "unis." when the violas leave the divisi (applied)
**Bars:** 193.
**Problem:** The violas play div. thirds 169–184, rest 185–192 and enter at 193 with single notes (A3, A3, G♯3, B3). Nothing cancels the div., so the inside players have no instruction. At 225 and 357 the score prints "unis." in the same situation.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 30: "When divisi is no longer in order and the part returns to single notes, the word unisoni (or more commonly the abbreviated, unis.) should appear on the part."
**Fix (applied):** tB.ily `tBVla` bar 193 `a2.\p\<^\markup \italic "unis."`.

### 3. The hemiola cadence is one three-bar slur at f (applied)
**Bars:** 53–55, 129–131, 285–287 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla; also Vc at 285).
**Problem:** The cadence `d'''4 b'' g'' | a''2 fis''4 | a''4 g''2` is one slur: seven notes in 2.0 s at f. With the bow alternating by two-bar slurs from 45, 121 and 277, the slur starts down-bow, so the hemiola's minims (a2 at 54, g2 at 55) fall in the middle and at the end of one stroke and receive no new bow at all. The violas have three repeated G4s (E4s at 129, 285) under the slur with no dashes, so the notation does not tell them to re-articulate. c018 marked row 7 for these bars "follows", counting only notes per bow; it did not consider where the hemiola's accents fall or the viola's repeated notes.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: bowing is planned so that the accented beat gets the down-bow (Schubert 5/ii, two up-bows "in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat"; Coriolan, Ex. II-20). PDF 38–39: at f fewer notes go on one bow (Mendelssohn 4/i, six notes at f, Ex. II-22). PDF 38: "The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow." PDF 42: louré is "indicated by dashes under or over each of the noteheads, with slurs to designate the bow changes."
**Fix (applied):** the notes are unchanged (the hemiola cadences are protected for their harmony, which is not touched). Violins: `d'''4 b''4( g''4) | a''2 fis''4( | a''4) g''2` (and the same shape at 129, 285 with `b''4\f g''4( e''4) | fis''2 dis''4( | fis''4) e''2`). Counted from the two-bar slurs before, this gives D, U | D (minim), U | D (minim), then the closing staccato crotchet up. Each of the hemiola's three minim units gets its own bow, and the minims are down-bows. Violas: `g'4 g'4--( g'4--) | fis'2 fis'4 | g'2.` (129, 285: `e'4\f e'--( e'--) | dis'2 b4 | b2.`). Cellos at 285: `b4\f g4( e4) | fis2 dis4( | fis4) e2`. Files: sA.ily, sC.ily, dA.ily.

### 4. An eight-bar phrase slur in the reprise cellos (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 261–268.
**Problem:** The arco pp tenor line is slurred 253–256 (tie plus slur), 257–260, and then 261–268 in one slur of eight dotted minims (5.2 s, then a crotchet rest). That is a phrase slur. A cellist will change bow in the middle anyway, at a place nobody chose.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts. Such slurs only confuse the performer." PDF 39: even at a soft dynamic, "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow … cello and bass bows are a bit shorter."
**Fix (applied):** dA.ily `dAVc`: `e2.( | g2. | c'2. | a2.) | g2.( | b2. | a2. | g2) r4`. These are four-bar bows, the same as 257–260, with the change at the phrase join 264/265.

### 5. Viola double stops in the tutti have no "non div." (applied)
**Bars:** 105–120, 305–320.
**Problem:** The violas play off-beat double stops (B3–G4, C4–A4, B3–A4, F♯4–A4) for sixteen bars at ff. The source comment (c019) says they are meant as non-divisi double stops, as in Schumann's tutti chords. The score does not say so. Many sections divide two-note chords by default in a loud tutti, which halves the sound of each note.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 30: "Some composers wish to have each player perform double-stops. In that case, it is safer to write non div. so that no division between the two players will occur." Each interval is a sixth or seventh on adjacent strings within one position (row 1).
**Fix (applied):** sC.ily and dC.ily `…Vla` bar 105 / 305: `r4 <b g'>4-.^\markup \italic "non div." <b g'>4-.`. It prints above the first chord with no collision (checked on page 4).

### 6. A down-bow sign that only repeats the habit, and only in one part (applied)
**Bars:** Vln II 306.
**Problem:** At 306 Vln II has `g'2->\dnb(`. After `e'4 b8 b e'4` (four strokes from a down-bow), the minim falls down-bow anyway. Vln I has the same figure at 306 with no sign, and both violins at 106 have none. The other signs either counteract the habit or mark a fresh entry or a section-wide arrival; this one does neither, and it makes the two violin parts look as if they bow differently.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "without overmarking the parts, [the composer] should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players."
**Fix (applied):** dC.ily `dCVlnTwo` bar 306 `g'2->( fis'8 f'8`.
