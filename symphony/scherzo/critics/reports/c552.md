## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 118, 266, 318: theme A's closing cadence reaches its 6/4 from i6 with a leaping bass in three of its four statements. Applied.

**Problem.**
- Since c462 the first statement (25–28) runs i – i6 → ii°6 – 6/4 – V7 – i, with the bass rising G–A–B.
- The tutti statement (117–120), the reprise (265–268) and the reprise tutti (317–320) still run i – i6 – 6/4. The bass leaps G1–B1, and the theme's F♯5 (2̂) on beat 3 passes over a tonic bass.
- In the two tuttis this bass is played by tuba, bass trombone, contrabassoon and basses, so it is the most audible bass line of the theme. The book calls it the weakest way to reach V.
- Also, in v552 the tenor countermelody (Bsn 1, Hn 3·4, Vc) reaches the V7's seventh A3 on the downbeat of 119 by stepping up from G3. That is an unprepared, accented seventh.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 135: I–II–V is "more intense" than I–I6–V.
- PDF 141 §§7–8 and Ex. 9-12: I6 leads into II6 at cadences; a bass rising by step from I to V, with faster chord change, drives the cadence.
- PDF 139 Ex. 9-8a: in II6 → V7 an upper voice doubling the bass 4̂ is held, 8–7, and becomes the prepared 7th. "If the prepared 7th is metrically strong, it functions as a suspension."
- PDF 139 Ex. 9-7d: in minor, doubling the bass of II6 is "good".
- PDF 138: 6̂ falls to 5̂.
- PDF 153 Ex. 10-3b: from II6 the 4th of the 6/4 enters by step as an accented passing tone (the theme's F5→E5).

**Answer to the recorded reason.** The tenor's G3 was the only obstacle. It now moves to its goal A3 one beat early and holds it over the bar line. The A becomes the consonant doubled bass of ii°6 and then the prepared 8–7 suspension of the V7. This is exactly Ex. 9-8a, and it is better than the v552 step G3–A3 into an accented seventh. The countermelody's head (c539) and its F♯3–G3 close are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 335→336: in the coda's 6/4 the strings carry the 4th E up the scale to B6. Not applied.

PDF 153 and 160 require the 4th to resolve down. Here, though, the E is part of the run to the protected B6 peak (336). The 4th does resolve E4→D♯4 in Tpt 2, and Cl 1 and Fl 2 supply the D♯. This is the "very free or complex" texture of PDF 154. Kept.

### 3. Half cadences at 16, 32, 108, 256, 308: i → V with no intermediate harmony. Not applied.

These dominants are back-relating offshoots of the tonic (PDF 158 §13; Ex. 10-10). The theme's first phrase is i–V–i–(iv–V7–i), as in Dvořák 9/iii. A predominant there would turn the antecedent into a cadence too early. Kept.

### 4. Bars 163–164: ii7 → IV under T, the reverse of IV–II. Not applied.

T's C♯5 (6̂) on the downbeat of 164 is set better by IV than by ii6 (PDF 136; Points for review 3). The bass F♯–A–B still rises to the cadential 6/4 at 165. Kept.
