## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Every principle in the table is followed, does not apply, or is departed from for a reason already on record. One recorded reason is replaced by a stronger one (Finding 1). I rank the points by how close each came to needing a change.

### 1. The two-stage coda 325–376: kept, with a stronger reason than the recorded one

**Case against it.** The fast stage 325–339 lasts about 9.4 s. The G.P. and the slow stage 341–376 last about 35 s, so the coda is 16% of the movement.

**The recorded reason is weak.** c313 called 339 the upper 5th of IV (A at 341) in hindsight, after Ex. 16-5 (PDF 253). But Ex. 16-5's "II" is a passing chord inside a V that keeps going, with no break and no cadence. Bar 339 is different:
- It is the full-orchestra tonic of an authentic cadence: cadential 6/4 at 335, V7 at 336–338 with the B6 peak, i at 339 with E1/E2 in the bass.
- A G.P. follows it.
So 339 is a structural tonic, not a detail within IV.

**The stronger reason** comes from U16 §9 (PDF 256–257). IV carrying upper neighbours inside an expanded I is "the most frequent and important possibility". The coda is that motion at the largest scale:
- I (339)
- IV (341–348, T in A)
- I (349–356, reached through the descending-5ths chain of row 1)

The same neighbour motion appears in two smaller sizes:
- the codetta 225–236 (I–IV–I three times, iv at 235);
- the plagal close 357–360, where iv6/4 over the held E is the sustained-bass form of I–IV–I (U19 §4, PDF 309, Ex. 19-7).

So the coda's second stage repeats, in its own proportion, the movement's one recurring way of prolonging a tonic. It is not a second ending. Dvořák's post-cadence tonic (1626–1684, about 19 bars, I → i) is as long as our 357–376 (20 bars).

No change. The closing page and the coda build stay protected. The recorded reason should read "I–IV–I neighbour prolongation (U16 §9)", not "upper 5th of IV".

### 2. Three long tonic stretches, 133–152, 225–240 and 357–376 (considered, kept)

**Case against it.** Together they hold E for 56 bars, about 45 s or 16% of the movement, in textures of 1.5 to 6.5 voices (analysis.txt profile). That is a lot of standing still for an Allegro vivace.

**Why I keep them.** Each uses a different prolongation from the reading, and each leads somewhere different:
- **133–152:** a V neighbour at 138 (U16 §9, PDF 256), the mixture G→G♯ in Hn 1 (141–148) and the oscillating E–B 6/4 in the plucked basses (U19 §15, PDF 320–321). It leads into the trio on the same root.
- **225–240:** I–IV–I neighbour chords, iv at 235, then the held chord under the rit. It leads into the G♯–G–F♯ of the retransition (protected).
- **357–376:** the neighbouring iv6/4 and then G♯ alone (U19 §4, §18, PDF 309, 322–323). It leads into the finale.

Dvořák stays on i for about 18 bars before his trio and about 19 bars after his final cadence. Cutting any of the three stretches would remove a protected link.

### 3. Retransition 241–252: no III divider (checked)

The bass E–A–C–B is a stepwise passing motion to the cadential 6/4 (U16 §8, PDF 256). The French sixth over C sits at 244. The 6/4 over B is expanded (U19 §10, PDF 316), with the motto tail interpolated at 246–247 (U19 §11, PDF 317). It resolves into V7 at 249.

Ex. 16-3 (PDF 252) allows III to divide V from I before a reprise. I reject it here for three reasons:
- G major already marks 37 and 45, and G minor 57 and 289, so a G chord at 251–252 would anticipate the development's key rather than divide the cadence.
- It would sit inside the protected G♯–G–F♯ span.
- The reading offers it as an option, not a rule.

### 4. The climax cadence 335–339: expansion stays on V (checked)

U19 §10 (PDF 316) says the tension of a cadential 6/4 can be raised by expanding it. Here the 6/4 at 335 lasts one bar, and V lasts three (336–338), with the single B6/fff at 336.

Expanding the 6/4 instead would put the peak on a dissonance that then has to fall away. It would also add a bar to a protected build (333–339) and move the G.P. The present order, where the 6/4 drives the scale into the peak and V is then held, is the expansion Ex. 19-21 shows: tension raised by the bars after the 6/4 arrives at the dominant. No change.

### 5. Development and reprise key plans 57–89 and 289–301 (checked)

Both follow Ex. 16-2b (PDF 251). The rising 5ths G–D–A are heard as the step line G–A–B, with D minor as the decorative upper 5th and IV (A) as the main connecting chord. In the full development ♭II stands where Ex. 16-2 has its "I in name only". In the shortened reprise development ♭II is left out, and IV goes straight to V (297 → 301). The shorter plan suits the shorter section. No change since c313.

### 6. Cadential 6/4 voice leading after c313 (checked)

At every cadential 6/4 where the 4th is doubled, either the soprano's 4th rises, or one doubling rises while the other falls. This is the condition of U19 §9 (PDF 316: "One of these 4ths will have to move up").
- 181→182 (c372's new bass F♯1): Vln II B4→C♯5 rises; violas and cellos B3→A♯3 fall.
- 221→222: the 4th E is a melody line doubled in octaves (Fl, Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1). The inner viola E4 rises to F♯4.
- 335→336: of the many E's, only Tpt 2 (E4→D♯4) and Cl 1 by leap (G5→D♯5) end on D♯. Strings and horns rise.
- 354→355: violas E4→F♯4; Vln I E→D♯ in octaves within one line.

c383's E major at 277–280 creates no new 6/4. c397's chromatic tails at 326–328 pass over the cello's E–B motive as oscillating 6/4s on weak beats (row 29). No change.
