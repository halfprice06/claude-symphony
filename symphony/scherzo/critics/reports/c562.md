## Findings

### 1. Bars 163–164: ii7 now moves to IV, not ii6/5. Applied.

Problem. Statement 1 of the trio theme approaches its cadence as follows:
- 163 is ii7 (F♯1, E3, A3, A5).
- 164 is IV (A1, E3, A3, C♯5), because c524 removed the harp off-beats that had carried F♯.
- 165 is the 6/4.

A&S PDF 172 calls II–IV a weak rising third, and the change does not move the harmony forward. A&S PDF 178–180 call II6/5 the most important form of II7 and the normal chord before V. Statements 3 (220, Vla F♯3) and the coda (351–352) keep the ii7–ii6/5 voice exchange of Ex. 12-11 (PDF 184). Since the harp cut, statement 1 is the only statement without it. The trio's first statement is meant to be the plain reference harmony (c090), so this one should have the cleanest cadence approach.

Fix. Hn 3 at 163–164 changes from `a2.~ | a2 r4` to `a2. | fis2 r4`. Hn 3 then takes F♯3 while the bass moves F♯1→A1, which is the A&S voice exchange. Bar 164 becomes A–C♯–E–F♯ over A (ii6/5). Hn 4's drone E3 is now the 7th, prepared in 163 and held into the 6/4 at 165, as A&S PDF 177 and 182 ask. The re-tongued F♯ stays at the same soft level inside the existing drone.
- This does not touch the Hn 4 E pedal or the 3·4 drone (c469): only Hn 3's pitch at 164 changes, and the drone sustains as before.
- Cue check: parts-cues.json has no cue involving Hn 3 at 163–165.

A listener will hear this. A minor 6th now sits inside the horn chord under the clarinet's C♯–B–A, and the bar sounds like a pre-dominant instead of a repeated tonic area.

### 2. Bar 194: parallel 5ths between the clarinets. Applied.

Problem. At 194 beat 3, Cl 1 moves C♯5→B4 while Cl 2 moves F♯4→E4: parallel perfect 5ths on a shared staff, in a soft and exposed texture. A&S PDF 190 (Ex. 12-21) shows this fault: an 8–7 over II/IV entered at the same moment as the upper voice's step produces 5ths.

Fix. Cl 2 changes from `fis'2 e'4~` to `fis'4 e'2~`. The E now enters on beat 2 as a passing 8–7 over the F♯ bass (A&S PDF 177), before Cl 1 moves, so the 5ths are gone.
- The E is still held through 195 into the 6/4 at 196. This keeps the "E held from ii7 into the 6/4" protection (c472).
- c302's Cl 1 sequence and the 196 breath are untouched.
- The new scan shows no 5ths at 194.

### 3. Bars 160–161: octave over the pizzicato. No change.

### 4. Bar 221 downbeat: E is missing from the 6/4 attack. No change.

At 220, the violas move E→F♯ and the E stays in another voice. The 6/4 at 221 still has its 4th held over from the 7th (A&S PDF 182). Adding E on the downbeat in Vla would double it, and it would lose the 220 voice exchange, which matches finding 1.

### 5. Bar 23: iiø6/5 instead of ii°6. No change.

A&S PDF 179–180 prefers the 7th on II in minor. But at 23 the off-beats do not sound with the F♯, and adding E makes A–C–E–G on 23.2. That contains a C-major triad, which the cyclic rule forbids. The best candidate makes the music break a rule, so ii°6 stays.

### 6. Bars 26 and 334: ii°6 without a 7th. No change.

An E as 7th would clash a minor 9th with the tail's F (motive x's ♭2̂ colour). A&S PDF 177 requires a prepared 7th, and no voice can prepare E here without doubling the tail.

### 7. Bar 20: deceptive cadence (A&S PDF 201–204). No change.

E minor's VI is C major, which is banned. iv6 instead (A&S PDF 204) would contradict the timpani B there. The authentic cadence stays.

### 8. Bar 329: Vla/Vc parallel 5ths, F♯3→B3 over B2→E3. No change.
