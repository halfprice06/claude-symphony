## Findings

The string writing follows chapter 2 everywhere I checked. Seven passes of this reading have tidied the bowings, labels and changes, so what is left is small. Finding 1 is the one point where the score does what Adler says to avoid and the fix costs nothing. Findings 2–4 are the most audible candidates the reading suggests. I built or weighed each one and give the reason it would make the music worse.

### 1. Bar 204: the basses' arco → pizz. change at 205 (applied)

**Problem.** At 203–204 the basses hold B1 arco (`b,,2.\> | b,,2\upbow r4`) and pluck B1 pp on the downbeat of 205. That leaves one crotchet at 2. = 76, about 0.26 s, to stop bowing and pluck. c276 and c456 kept this because the up-bow is marked and Adler calls such changes "rare" but possible.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck … There are instances … when no time is provided for either maneuver, but these are rare and should be avoided if at all possible." The same page gives the up-bow as an aid and plucking with the bow in hand as the last resort. Recorded reasons say only that the change is possible. The reading says it should be avoided when it can be, and here it can be avoided for nothing. Through all of 204 the cellos hold B2 (`b,2.`) and Bsn 2 holds B2, so the bass B goes on sounding to the bar line. The basses' 16-foot B1 only stops one beat earlier, and that beat falls in the diminuendo from p to pp.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-546/work, sections/tB.ily, tBCb):**
`b,,2.\> | b,,2\upbow r4 | b,,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." …` → `b,,2.\> | b,,4\upbow r4 r4 | b,,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." …`, with a c546 comment above tBCb. The basses now have two beats (0.53 s) after an up-bow crotchet. The bowing, the hairpin, the pizz. at 205, Hn 1's preview at 205–208 and every other part are unchanged.

**Not done at 224 → 225.** The second one-beat change stays. At 223–224 the basses' E1 is the root of the trio's last f cadence, held to the end of beat 2 with the cellos on B2 (the fifth) and the violins on E. If the basses stopped a beat earlier, beat 2 would sound with B in the bass, a 6/4. The harp's left hand also plucks E on the downbeat of 225, which covers a late pluck. Dvořák writes a one-beat change (see above), and Adler's own fallback, plucking with the bow in hand, works at pp. This keeps c276's reason for 225, and only for 225.

### 2. Bars 341–348: the violas' repeated held notes under the solo horn (weighed, not applied)

**Candidate.** The violas are the only sustained string part under Hn 3's and Ob 1's T (`a2.\pp | a2. | e2. | a2. | a2.\ppp | d'2. | b2. | b2.`). Without ties each bar is a new bow (PDF 37), so the A3 is struck again at 342 and 345. Ties would give a seamless background.

**Why not.** Adler says on the same page that skilled players change bow on successive notes "without a break or any audible difference" (PDF 37). At pp the re-bow at 342 cannot be heard. At 345 the restruck A3 carries the new ppp under Ob 1's entry, c174's layering ("349 violas ppp under the pp tune, as at 345"). A tie would remove that step down. The gain is inaudible and the loss is real.

### 3. Bar 375: "l.v." on the last pizz. E (weighed, not applied)

**Candidate.** Adler PDF 58: a pizz. dies at once unless marked "let vibrate", and bass strings ring longest. The last pizz. E (Cb E1 on the open string, Vc E2) at 375 could be marked l.v. so the root rings into the final fermata.

**Why not.** It would change the last chord. The ending is protected: the last sound is G♯ in Hn 1 and Vln I (HISTORY v01, c430/c500/c501), and the finale's link starts from that sound. The timpani's E at 375 is also a single ppp stroke under the same plan. An l.v. would put an E-major root under the final G♯ and remove the bare third the finale picks up as A♭. The reading gives no reason stronger than that protection.

### 4. Bars 341–348 or 357–364: a colour device (sul tasto, mutes, harmonics) for the closing page (weighed, not applied)

**Candidate.** Chapter 2's colour devices are sul tasto ("flutey, soft, and hazy", PDF 51), con sordino ("very soft and smooth", PDF 59) and natural harmonics (PDF 62–65). Any of them would make the closing page sound different.

**Why not.** None of the models (Dvořák 9/iii, Brahms, Tchaikovsky) colours a scherzo's quiet coda this way, so the device would sound like a special effect. Sul tasto at 341–348 needs "naturale" at 349 (PDF 53), inside the settled 349–356 (c494). Mutes on the violas have only the G.P. at 340, about 2 s, and Adler warns that "chaos may result" without enough time (PDF 60). String harmonics are reserved for the harp chord at 149/151/375 (c476), and c455 and c545 rejected violin harmonics on the frame and at 224.
