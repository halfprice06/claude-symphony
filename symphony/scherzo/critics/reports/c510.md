## Findings

### 1. 237–241: at the return from the trio, the G♯ = A♭ pair now falls together, G♯4/G♯5 → G4/G5. Applied.

**Problem.** The movement turns from E major to E minor twice, at 6→7 and at 240→241. Since c470 and c499, the first turn is Vln I's G♯5 → G5 in one line, the line that movement I seeds at I 78–79 and that the finale quotes at IV 243. Since c430 and c500, the pivot itself sounds as a pair, Hn 1 G♯4 with Vln I G♯5, at 1–6 and 364–376. The second turn is the structural return of E minor, and the retransition's own header calls it "the introduction again". There it is only Hn 1's G♯4 → G4. Vln I has rested since 225, and in 240 the horns hold the chord alone (c258). No bar of the scherzo has both of the frame's voices falling together. The finale's first gesture does exactly that, ff: Hn 1 A♭4 → G4 and Vln I A♭5 → G5 at IV 1–2.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 599–600, Ex. 32-19: the Appassionata's development turns A♭ into G♯, moves by common tone to E major, turns E major to minor, and goes on in major thirds to C minor. That is this symphony's plan, II A♭ → III E → IV C minor. Its hinge is the G♯/G change in E.
- PDF 600–601, §16: "key areas project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece". The Hammerklavier (PDF 585) keeps its F♮/F♯ confrontation because that pair is the movement's motive.
- PDF 585–586, Ex. 32-3: the common tone joins the keys when it sits exposed in the top voice.
- PDF 593: the Waldstein's E major is "resolved" into E minor. 240→241 does the same, and it is the moment the listener should recognise as the opening's turn.

**What it does.** Vln I holds G♯5 ppp over Hn 1's G♯4 through the poco rit. (237–240). It slurs into G5 at 241 while Hn 1 falls G♯4 → G4, then stops after two beats, as at 7–8. The pivot now has one sound and one meaning wherever it turns E major into E minor (1–7, 237–241), and that sound stays major at the end (364–376), where the finale makes the same fall ff at IV 1–2. A listener who has heard the pp fall twice in the scherzo hears IV 1–2 as its transformation.

**Why c258 does not stand against it.** c258 removed Vln II and the violas at 239–240 so that "the horns alone hold E major with Hn 1's pp G♯ on top, the pivot". That was written when bars 1–2 were Hn 1 alone. The pivot is now the pair (c430, c499, c500), the same correction c500 made at 364–368. The cut of Vln II and the violas stays. The top voice is still the pivot G♯, and the texture still thins by colour. Vln I adds the pivot's own octave, not a chord tone. It enters with the strings' pp chord at 237, and it continues alone after they leave at 239, as at 364.

### 6. Weighed and rejected (decided: no change)

- **Vln I G5 → G♯5 at 144→145 with Hn 1, into the trio.** The rising inflection is not quoted anywhere. A G♯5 there would also pre-empt the trio's fresh first-violin entry at 169 and the harp harmonics' G♯ at 149/151 (c473 Finding 4, c036).
- **Vln I holding G5 into 242.** It would double the violas' accented G4 at the octave (c227).
- **Removing the poco rit. at 237–240 for a Hammerklavier-style hard turn (PDF 585).** The Appassionata's and *Widmung*'s turns are gentle common-tone moves (PDF 586, 599). The rit. and accelerando curve is protected (c113).
- **A D♯ on the closing page to prepare the finale's E♭, as Haydn prepares E♭ (PDF 597–598).** It would put a dominant into the protected plagal close (357–360). The E♭/D♯ thread is the finale's to close at IV 219–220.
- **♭VI or III♯ key areas in the trio (PDF 592–594).** ♭VI of E is C major, which is reserved for the finale. A III♯ (G♯ major) area would rewrite the protected trio middle (c480, c503).

### 7. For the finale's rework (no scherzo change)

Ex. 32-19 is the symphony's tonal plan, and the finale's 243 already uses E major as C's III♯ (PDF 593–594, Waldstein). With Finding 1, the scherzo gives the finale one soft model of its opening gesture in the same two players. If the finale wants a recall before its arrival, it can quote the pp form (G♯4/G♯5 → G4/G5 over E major) at pitch, as it does at 243, without a new scherzo theme.
