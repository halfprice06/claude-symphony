## Also studied

I read every text page from PDF 21 to 110 and rendered every example page from the book at 110 dpi. I checked the range charts on 21, 27, 32, 39, 60, 65 and 71–74 against each part. I built a one-staff-per-player MIDI version of the score and dumped every note to JSON. From it I scanned every part for range, for stretches with no rest or breath mark, for the longest slurs, for the number of seconds each horn plays in any 30-second window, for seconds between woodwinds, and for places where the flute is below the oboe. I compared the horn staccato quavers in motive x (71, 299) with Dvořák, Symphony 9/iii in the local corpus (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesS.ily). Dvořák writes horn quavers such as `c8 c c4 d` at a similar tempo, so Sebesky's warning on PDF 32 that horns speak late does not argue against these bars. I read c086, the first pass on this chapter, so as not to repeat its three fixes: the oboe low figure moved to C.a., the Bsn 1 breaths and rest, and the low brass at f at 333. I checked both timpani retunings. G♯ to G at 145 has 43 bars before G♯ is used at 188, and the retuning at 225 is over a long rest. Both are easy. Harp chord spans are within an octave per hand. Brass endurance is light: no horn plays more than about 22 s in any 30 s window, and the trumpets play only in tuttis and motive x.

## Findings

### 1. Cl 1 has no breath across the two halves of T, bars 160 and 216 (applied)

**Bars:** 153–168 (T, p dolce) and 209–224 (T in the trio return, f to ff).

**Problem:** Cl 1 plays the 16-bar tune with no rest. The first 8-bar slur ends on `b'2)` in bar 160, and the second slur starts on the next beat with `cis''2(`, with no gap and no breath mark. At 2. = 88 the 16 bars last 12.4 s, and at 216 they are played at f to ff with a hairpin to ff in bar 219. The player will take a breath anyway, but without a mark it will be taken at a different point by each player and will cut into the minim B. Two other parts already breathe at bar 216. Hn 1·2, Bsn 2 and Cbsn have `\breathe` there and Bsn 1 has a rest. At 160 the horn drones stop for the cadence. The clarinet is the only melody player with no marked breath.

**Evidence:** Sebesky PDF 24 says a slurred group is played in one breath. PDF 70 says that wind players need breathing spaces written into the part and should not be expected to find their own. Bar 8 of T is the half cadence of the tune (themes-scherzo.md). It is the only place where a breath does not split a slur.

**Fix:** in `sections/tA.ily` (`tAClOne`) and `sections/tC.ily` (`tCClOne`), change `ais'4 b'2) | cis''2(` to `ais'4 b'2) \breathe | cis''2(`. The note values are the same, so the bar lengths, the theme and the MIDI are unchanged. The breath comma prints above the stave in bar 160 and bar 216 without collisions.

**Applied** in /tmp/critic-176/work. The build is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty; the PDF has 13 pages; partlengths.py prints "part lengths: all match"; cmajor.py prints "C-major triads: none"; themecheck reports all 41 recorded statements unchanged; and the outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt are the same as in v176.

### Points tested and kept, ranked by how close they came to a change

2. **Horn staccato quavers in motive x, 71 and 299.** Sebesky PDF 32 and 46 say horns speak late and that fast upbeats can be left out. I kept them. The quavers are on the beat, not upbeats; they are doubled by the trumpets (PDF 32: one trumpet equals two horns, so the trumpets carry the attack); and Dvořák 9/iii writes the same figure for horns at a similar speed.
3. **Hn 1 written G♯5 ff falling to G5 pp, 72–73.** This is above Sebesky's practical top of written G5 (PDF 32). Earlier critics kept it (c136 and c156), and it is one note reached by step at the climax, held for one bar. I kept it. PDF 38 suggests a half-mute for soft high notes, but there is no time to fit one here. The pp is a held fade from ff, so it does not need a soft high attack.
4. **Ob 1 E4 staccato, 132 and 288.** This note is in the coarse low register of PDF 71. It is a short f note doubled by Vln II and Hn 2, so the coarse sound is covered and the staccato is easy (PDF 71). Kept.
5. **Hn 4 low pedal at concert A2, 305–320.** This is below the concert C3 floor that PDF 36 suggests for the bottom horn. PDF 36 is about background voicing in closed horn chords. Here the horn has a single sustained pedal at p, with a breath at 316, and earlier critics (c114 and c115) kept it. Kept.
6. **B.tbn and tuba both on the root, 333–344.** PDF 47 advises against this doubling. At the final tutti fortissimo, with low brass held to f (c086), the doubled root gives the bass the weight it needs against the full upper winds, and PDF 53 is satisfied. Kept.
7. **C.a. and Cl 2 inside the brass chord, 336.** This goes against PDF 106. It lasts one bar at ff, and both instruments double brass notes, so nothing is lost. Moving them would break their lines. Kept.
8. **Woodwind seconds, 101–104, 106 and 336.** PDF 66 and 81 warn against seconds between dissimilar woodwinds. All of these are passing notes or chord sevenths in tuttis, each lasting a crotchet. Kept.
