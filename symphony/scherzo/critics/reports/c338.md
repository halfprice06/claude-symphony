## Findings

### 1. T's second phrase starts one to two levels too soft in every full statement (bars 156–157, 172–173, 212–213). Applied.

**Problem.** c331 (v332) made T's bar 4 a 4–3 suspension (A tied over the bar line to G♯) and put a diminuendo on it: `a'4\> gis'2) | a'2\!( b'4`. The hairpin has no target, so in the MIDI the fade runs past the G♯ and carries into phrase 2 until the next dynamic, the hidden peak at bar 6 of the tune. Measured in v338's score.midi: Cl 1 plays phrase 1 at 37 (p), the G♯ at 33, and then 157 and the first note of 158 at 24, about ppp. Phrase 2's swell then rises 24→44 and falls back to 37, so it starts lower than it ends. In the violins at 169 the tune is at 63 (mp) and 173 drops to 45. In the forte return at 209 the violins are at 83 (f) and 213 drops to 62, Cl 1 from 58 to 44. Before c331 these bars played at the phrase's level (c238 measured Cl 1 at 37 throughout 157–158). The printed shape says something different: the hidden levels already in the tune bring phrase 2 back to its base after the peak (p at 159, mp at 175, f at 215). So phrase 2's base is the base level, and only the resolution G♯ is softer.

**Evidence.** Renders of expressive diminuendi sound lifeless or wrong unless the composer gives the curve its end (Casanelles PDF 149–150, 152). The step from one note to the next also shapes the start of the next note (PDF 156): here the suspension's release should fall onto the G♯ and the new phrase should start fresh. Lyrical legato swells to its arrival note (PDF 155). A phrase that enters two levels under its own peak's return level has its arrival placed wrong.

**Fix (applied).** Hidden dynamics only. The diminuendo ends on the resolution one level down, and phrase 2 restarts at the base level. The printed hairpin still runs under the G♯ (checked on pages 6–8).

The Cl 2 rests at 212–213 and the second player has no dynamic there, so the shared staff stays consistent. The coda statement (Hn 3, 343–344) ends on a rest and hands the tune to Ob 1's printed p at 345, so it needs nothing.

### 2. In the coda build the top line is the one string part that does not crescendo (Vln I, bars 329–331). Applied.

**Problem.** At 329 Vln II, violas, cellos and basses have `\mf\<` running to the ff at 333, and the woodwinds and contrabassoon crescendo from p. Vln I has mf at 329, nothing at 330–331, then f at 332. Vln I carries the line G5–A5–C6–D♯6 in tenths over the falling bass E–D–C–B. c033 wrote that line so the climb would widen the crescendo (Rimsky-Korsakov's diverging progression). In the print the leading voice holds a flat mf for three bars while every part under it swells, and then steps up. In the MIDI it is 77 on all twelve notes of 329–331, against Vln II rising from 77 to 89.

**Evidence.** A crescendo has to be marked for its whole length and in the voice that leads it. A flat stretch inside it is what makes a rendered build sound mechanical (Casanelles PDF 149–150, 152). Dynamics change timbre as well as level (PDF 148), so a terraced top line over a continuous swell below changes the chord's colour in steps. The other builds in the score (37–41, 101–104, 193–197, 249–252, 297–300) give the leading voice the hairpin.

**Fix (applied).** One printed hairpin: `codaVlnOne` 329 `g''4-.\mf\< e''8-. e''-. g''4-.`. It runs to the existing f at 332, the same step the contrabassoon makes (f at 332, ff at 333). Vln I now rises 77→87 across 329–332. The hairpin sits clear of Vln II's on page 11 (checked at 130 dpi).
