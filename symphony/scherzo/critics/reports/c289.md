## Holistic verdict: Brahms Op. 83/ii

Brahms writes a tumultuous sonata scherzo, and its trio is the loud part. This scherzo is the Beethoven 9 / Dvořák 9 type: light spiccato over pizzicato downbeats, a soft trio, a written-out varied reprise, a two-stage coda and a quiet hinge into the finale. The brief and the cyclic rules choose that model, and a conductor would accept it at this level. Its large shape, key plan, hemiola cadences, timpani writing and closing page all stand comparison.

What Brahms has throughout, and this score still lacks at three cadences, is independent inner voices that each move correctly. In Brahms an inner line's dissonance is prepared, held and released downward, and each instrument's line makes sense alone. Here the second idea's hemiola cadence has one inner voice, Cl 1, whose suspension goes the wrong way. The tune's lower octave resolves the same pitch correctly at the same moment. That is the only finding.

I do not recommend imitating Brahms's loud trio or his unbroken motion. Either would undo protected decisions (the soft trio, theme A's stops, c007/c059) and would gain nothing within this model.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Cl 1 resolves its suspension upward against the same note falling in the tune: 54–55, 130–131, 286–287 (applied)

**Bars:** 54–55 (sA), 130–131 (sC), 286–287 (dA): the hemiola cadences of the second idea.

**Problem:** At each cadence Cl 1 ties a note over the barline into the tonic as a dissonance against the new bass, then rises a step:
- 130–131 and 286–287: F♯4 over E1 (a 9th) rises to G4. In the same octave F♯4 also sounds in Vln II and Hn 2 (131), or in Vln II (287), and those parts fall to E4. One unison divides into contrary motion.
- 54–55: A4 over G1 (a 9th) rises to B4. Vln II and Ob 1 have A4 and fall to G4.

On beat 1 of the cadence bar the clarinet doubles the tune's dissonance and then contradicts its resolution. It also leaves the seventh of the preceding V7 (A at 130/286, C at 54) to resolve only in the brass (Hn 3 at 130–131, Hn 1 at 55). In a soft wind texture that is exactly the inner-voice detail a conductor hears at a cadence.

**Evidence:** A&S Unit 21 (PDF 348): "A dissonant suspension must resolve to a consonance by stepwise, downward motion… its resolution should sound like a relaxation". Upward resolution is an exception, and the exception in Ex. 21-2 is the leading tone (PDF 347). The same page says suspensions "bring into prominence the voice in which they occur", so the clarinet's wrong-way release is exposed. Unit 24 (PDF 399–400): the 7th of a seventh chord resolves down by step in its own voice. My scan (scan/vl.py `sus`) listed exactly these three places, plus the protected trio-tune anticipation at 172. After the fix only 172 remains.

**Fix (applied):** Cl 1 holds the chord seventh across the barline and resolves it down as a 4–3. This doubles in unison the horn line that already does so: Hn 3 A4–G4 at 130–131, Hn 1 C5–B4 at 55.
- sA.ily, `sAClOne`: `c''2 a'4~ | a'4 b'2)` → `c''2.~ | c''4 b'2)`
- sC.ily, `sCClOne`: `a'2 fis'4~ | fis'4 g'2)` → `a'2.~ | a'4 g'2)`
- dA.ily, `dAClOne`: the same as sC.

Both clarinets' rhythms already differed in these bars, so the shared staff engraves as before. The hemiola rhythm, the harmony (I6/4 – V7 – I), the double-neighbour tune and every dynamic are unchanged. That answers the do-not-touch entry on the hemiola cadences (cycle 2), whose reason was their rhythm and harmony. The third of the tonic is still in the clarinet on beat 2. F♯ and A are still present at 130/286 (tune, Vln II, Hn 2 and Hn 3).
