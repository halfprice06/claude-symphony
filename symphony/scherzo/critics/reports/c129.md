## What else I studied

**Beethoven 7/iii.** I worked from what I know of the score, since the corpus has only movements 1 and 2 of the Seventh. It is in F major, Presto (dotted minim = 132), and the first strain moves at once to A major. The trio is Assai meno presto (dotted minim = 84) in D major. Its tune is in the clarinets, bassoons and horns, over the violins' A sustained in octaves, and the second horn has a low A–G♯ figure underneath. The trio's second period is ff tutti, with trumpets and timpani on the pedal. The form is S–T–S–T–S, and the coda recalls the trio for four bars and ends with five ff strokes.

**How v129 compares.**
- The third-related planning has the same boldness: E minor – G – F (Neapolitan) – V here, F – A in Beethoven.
- The trio's colour rests on the same principle as Beethoven's (row 22): a wind tune over a sustained horn drone (153), not a tune doubled by its own accompaniment.
- Two of Beethoven's devices are closed to this score by the cyclic rules: a ff tutti statement of the trio tune ("the hammered tutti in major" is kept for the finale), and trumpets holding a pedal. Staying at f in strings and horns at 209 is the right answer.
- The trio's tempo drop is smaller: 88 to 76, a ratio of 0.86, against Beethoven's 0.64. I left it. T's minim–crotchet surface already halves the scherzo's quaver motion, and a slower trio would push it past its 25 % share (c123 proportions).
- The soft ending is a link to the finale, not a weakness against Beethoven's abrupt close.

A conductor reading these pages would accept the orchestration and the form as professional. What still marks the score are small register collisions between a held accompaniment note and the tune, the kind of thing c039 and c047 removed elsewhere. Findings 1–3 are the three left.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 209: Hn 2's held G♯4 doubles, then rubs against, the lower octave of T at its return
- **Problem.** At the f return of T, Vln II and Cl 1 play the tune an octave below Vln I: G♯4 (2 beats), then A4. Hn 2 holds G♯4 for the whole bar, p, slurred into 210. For two beats the horn sits in unison on the tune's first note. On beat 3 the tune's A4 sounds against the horn's G♯4 at the same pitch level, a semitone in one register.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 116: the harmonic basis must differ from the melody in fullness, intensity and colour. A held accompaniment note on the melody's own pitch does the opposite: it fuses with the tune and then clashes with its next step. The project has already treated this as a defect: at 197–200 (c039, "no A4 against a held G♯4") and at 229 (c047: Cl 1's head "began in unison with the violas' held G♯4, its A4 rubbed against it"). Bar 209 is the last statement of T where it remains (the MIDI scan finds no other). In Beethoven 7/iii the accompaniment of the trio tune lies above it (the violins' A) or below it (horns), never on it.
- **Fix (applied).** Hn 2 at 209 plays E4, so Hn 1·2 open on the fifth E–B, the drone the first statement had at 153. From 210 the thirds return (c094 kept). Bsn 1's E4 is now doubled at the unison by a horn, which is Rimsky's juxtaposition (PDF 106).
  - tC.ily: `tCHnTwo = { gis'2.--(\p | gis'2.-- | fis'2.--) |` → `tCHnTwo = { e'2.--(\p | gis'2.-- | fis'2.--) |`

### 2. Bar 193: Cl 1's held A4 under the violins' passing G♯4
- **Problem.** In the trio middle, Vln I/II play F♯–G♯ | A–C♯ (193–194) in octaves. Cl 1 holds A4 through 193, its chromatic step from A♯4 (192), so the violins' passing G♯4 on beat 3 rubs against it in the same register. c128's notes listed "Cl 1 crossing Vln II at 193–196" as left.
- **Evidence.** The same Rimsky PDF 116 principle and the same project rule as Finding 1 (c039, c047). The clarinet anticipates the violins' A by a whole bar, in their register.
- **Fix (applied).** Cl 1 keeps the chromatic A♯→A (192–193) for two beats, then moves to C♯5 on beat 3 and ties into 194, where it already held C♯5. Harmony unchanged (F♯ minor); Cl 2's F♯4 unchanged.
  - tB.ily: `ais'2.(\pp | a'2.)\p\< | cis''2. |` → `ais'2.(\pp | a'2\p\< cis''4~) | cis''2. |`

### 3. Bar 219: a semitone between the two bassoons on one staff
- **Problem.** At T's third phrase in the trio return, Bsn 2 doubles the cellos' countermelody (c087) and passes A3–G♯3 on beat 3. Bsn 1 holds A3 for the whole bar. The two bassoons meet at a minor second in the same register and colour, and the shared staff prints the clash.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 102: discords of the second are preferably given to instruments of different colour. PDF 108: when bassoons pair with horns, the discord goes to the bassoon against the horn, not between the two bassoons.
- **Fix (applied).** Bsn 2 holds A3 for the bar, in unison with Bsn 1. The cellos alone pass G♯3, so the second now sounds between cello on one side and bassoons and violas on the other. 220 is unchanged.
  - tC.ily, tCBsnTwo bar 219: `a2\f\> gis4` → `a2.\f\>`

**Considered and not changed.**

- 209–216: Hn 1·2 thirds on B4/G♯4–C♯5/A4 meet T's lower octave in unisons on long notes. Moving them would undo c010 (Vln II on T) or c094 (horn thirds). After Finding 1 the only semitone clash is gone.
