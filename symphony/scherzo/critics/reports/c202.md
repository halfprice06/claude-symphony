## What else I studied

I read every text page of A&S PDF 163-210 and rendered every example page at 110 dpi. I relied on the images rather than the OCR for chord labels. Ex. 12-21 on PDF 190 is the one this report rests on. I also read the earlier passes on the same units, c022 and c112, and HISTORY.md's protected list, which includes c080's N6/4 at 331, c198's acceptance of the Hn 1/Tpt 1 B4 above the tenor's suspended C4 at 111, and c189's A4-B4 line at 113/313. Then I built a MIDI file with one track per player from the parts. From it I made a per-beat reduction of the whole movement (bass, pitch classes and top voice) and ran three scans over every pair of voices: isolated parallel fifths, isolated parallel octaves and melodic augmented seconds. The scan found no augmented seconds and one real parallel fifth, at 110-111 and its repeat at 310-311. The remaining octave hits were melody doublings, the motive-x stretto at 326-329, which is octave canon by design, and rolled harp chords at 75/77.

## Findings

### 1. Parallel fifths Hn 1 / melody, 110-111 and 310-311 (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 110 is iv over the E pedal and moves to V at 111. On beat 3 of 110, Hn 1 plays C5 staccato and the tune has G4. On the downbeat of 111, Hn 1 plays B4 and the tune has F#4. That makes two perfect fifths in similar motion between two exposed lines. Both are forte, both are on the beat and both are in the middle register, so the fifths are audible. The tenor 9-8 that c112 added at 110-111 does not prevent them, because it is a different voice. Bar 310 repeats bar 110 exactly.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 190 says that "the threat of parallel 5ths so often posed by IV7 also looms when 3 occurs as a soprano passing tone (8-7) over a progression from IV to V (Example 12-21)", and that the strategies of Ex. 12-20 rescue it. One of those strategies, Ex. 12-20a (PDF 190, "alto skip avoids 5ths"), moves the inner voice by a skip so that it no longer runs parallel with the falling line. Here the tune's G-F# plays the 8-7 role against the iv-V motion, and Hn 1's C5-B4 is the inner voice that needs the skip. A&S PDF 177 says the same about IV7 and II7 in general: the line leading to V must be arranged so that it does not make fifths with a voice that falls by step. Hn 1 already uses the A4-B4 approach at 113/313 (c189), so the composer has solved the same spot this way once.

**Fix (applied).** Bar 110, `sCHnOne` in sections/sC.ily: `c''4-- r4 c''4-.` changed to `c''4-- r4 a'4-.`. Bar 310, `dCHnOne` in sections/dC.ily: the same change. A4 is in iv, steps up to B4 at 111 in contrary motion to the tune's G-F#, and matches 113/313. Each file has a `% c202` comment above the variable. Hn 1 keeps its downbeat C5, its staccato rhythm and its register. No dynamics change, so the part-combined horn staff is unaffected.

### 2. Bar 111 / 311: horns above the suspended tenor C4 (kept)

Hn 1 and Tpt 1 hold B4 over the tenor's C4 9-8 suspension. The resolution tone B sounds above the suspension in other voices, but it is more than an octave away (B4 against the tenor's C4-B3), so it does not cover the 9-8. c198 accepted this, and I agree. No change.

### 3. Bar 202-203: Bsn 1 / violas C-B in octaves (kept)

This is a doubled ♭6-5 line over V in C# minor, A&S PDF 166 treats ♭6-5 over V in minor as a line strong enough to bear doubling. Here the doubling is an orchestral octave of a single line, not two independent voices moving in octaves. No change.

### 4. Bar 331: Neapolitan 6/4 (kept)

The N6/4 falls outside Units 11-13, and HISTORY.md protects it under c080. My only check was that it leads into V without fifths, and it does. No change.
