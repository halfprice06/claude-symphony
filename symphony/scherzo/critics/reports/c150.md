## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 4–6: the bassoon joins the horn call at the call's own level, on its diminuendo (applied)
**Problem.** The horn call (3–6) is the scherzo's first quotation of the motto material: movement I's call 3–1–7 on one horn, left on D♯4 for the finale. Hn 1 rises pp < p onto E4 at bar 4 and then fades (p > on 4–6). At bar 4, on that same E4, Bsn 1 enters on B3 at **p** with its own diminuendo. Bassoon and horn arrive together at the call's peak dynamic, a fourth apart in the same octave. The solo colour becomes a mixed colour just as the phrase starts to die away. In the MIDI the bassoon enters at velocity 32 under the horn's 44. On paper the two are equal.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 127, "Amplification and elimination of tone qualities": contrasting two groups "transforms a simple into a complex timbre"; for a crescendo "the second group enters *piano* or *pianissimo*"; and "the transition from a complex to a simple timbre, by the suppression of one of the groups, belongs essentially to the *diminuendo*." Bar 4 does the opposite: a group is added at full level during a diminuendo. The editor's note on Rimsky's own faults (PDF 18) concerns the same problem: a supporting line marked so that it covers the line it serves. The score's own rule (c099, protected) puts the accompaniment one level under the tune. Bar 4 is the only place in the intro where that rule is broken.
**Fix (applied, intro.ily).**
`introBsnOne = { R2.*3 | b2.~\pp | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2\> r4\! | R2.*4 | }`
(was `b2.~\p\> | b2.~ | b2.~\pp | …`). The bassoon now enters pp and holds pp through the E-major/E-minor turn, so the horn is the only voice that shapes the call. It fades only at 8 with the clarinets. Notes, c090's scoring (one horn, B3 in Bsn 1) and the clarinets at 7–8 are unchanged. MIDI: Bsn 1 velocity 25 (was 32) under Hn 1's 44. The bassoon staff is not shared in these bars (Bsn 2 rests), so the shared-staff dynamics rule is not affected.
