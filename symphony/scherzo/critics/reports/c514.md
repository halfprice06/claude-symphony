## Findings

### 1. Bars 333–335: the horns stand on a static tonic chord under the coda's summing-up of theme A (Adler PDF 17; PDF 38–41; PDF 15, 44). Applied.

**Problem.** At 333–335, theme A's head returns ff in four octaves. This is the coda's version of the tutti at 105. At 105–107 the horns carry the movement's tenor countermelody (Hn 3·4 a2 with Bsn 1 and the cellos: G3 | B3–E4 | E4–B3 …). At 333–335 all four horns only hold chord tones: Hn 1 B4 (332–339), Hn 2 G4, Hn 3 E4 and Hn 4 B3 (332–339). Bsn 1 holds G3 for three bars. So the tutti's second line, its one long-note melody, is missing exactly where the coda sums up the theme. The horns are what Adler warns against on PDF 17: "stuck on the tonic and dominant notes once the initial fanfare chord is over." c424 fixed the same fault for Hn 1 in the 105–120 tutti. Nobody has checked these coda bars against it.

**Evidence.** Tchaikovsky 4/i (PDF 38, Ex. XI-21 on 39–41): Adler names the climactic tutti's added element as "a powerful new thematic element (cantus firmus)" in long brass notes against fragments heard before. In this movement, the long-note brass line that belongs to theme A's tutti is the countermelody from 105, so bringing back its opening is the scherzo's form of that technique. PDF 15 and 44 (Bartók): above mf, two horns in unison equal one trombone, so the line is a2. Smetana's rule, which c034 cited (sustained brass hold the tutti together), still holds: the line is in minims and dotted minims, and Hn 1, Hn 2, the trumpets and the low brass still hold the chord.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
- `codaHnThree`: `… f'2--(\pp\< e'4-- | dis'2.-- | e'2.--\ff) | b2( e'4~ | e'2 b4) | fis'2.--( …`
- `codaHnFour`: `… a2--(\pp\< fis4-- | b2.-- | b2.--\ff) | b2( e'4~ | e'2 b4) | b2.--( …`
- `codaBsnOne`: `… a2. | g2.\ff( | b2 e'4~ | e'2\< b4) | fis2.\fff …`

Bsn 1 now plays G3 | B3–E4 | E4–B3, which is 105–107 exactly. Hn 3·4 join a2 at 334, so Hn 3's D♯4 at 332 still resolves to E4 at 333. The bar-331 slur now closes at 333.

**What a listener hears.** Under the head's chromatic tail at 334, a rising horn fourth B3–E4 that holds over the bar line and falls back to B3 over the cadential 6/4 at 335. The ear knows this tenor line from 105, and it now carries into the fff dominant at 336. Before the change, those three bars were a held block chord under the theme.

**Checks.** The brass chord stays complete at 334: B4, G4, B3 in the horns; B4/G4 in the trumpets on motive x; G3, B2, E2 and E1 in the low brass. ### 2. Bars 336–338: low brass rest under the B6 peak (Tchaikovsky, PDF 39–41). Kept.

In Adler's Tchaikovsky example, the trombones and tuba hold long fff notes through the climax. That is the strongest case in this reading for having them sound at 336–338. I decided against it. c034's reason comes from Barber (Adler II PDF 150): leave the low brass out of the bar of the high note so it keeps its emphasis, and let their entry on the last chord give the finality. That reason fits this passage better. Tchaikovsky's climax is a long fff stretch. Here the peak is the movement's single fff bar and its single B6, and the bass of 336–338 is already carried by the basses, cellos, contrabassoon and Bsn 2. Their re-entry f> on the tonic at 339 is the cadence's weight, as Dan's brass rule asks ("weight rather than bite"). Keep.
