## Findings

### 1. Bars 361–364: Vln II doubles Hn 1's G♯4 at the unison where the motto tail has just arrived on it. Vln II ends on E4 at 360 (applied)

- **Problem.**
  - At 357–360 Hn 1 plays the motto's chromatic tail B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4, alone on its line. This is the movement's last quotation of the motto, and it lands on the G♯4 that carries the G♯ = A♭ thread from movement II into the finale.
  - At 361, with the fading E-major chord, the whole Vln II section moves E4→G♯4 and holds the horn's pitch at the unison for four bars, pp.
  - So the goal note of the motto tail stops sounding as a horn. A section of violins at pp on G♯4 outweighs one horn at pp on G♯4, and the blend reads as a string note.
  - The thread pitch is heard in the horn alone everywhere else: 1–2 (Hn 1 + Vln I an octave up), 145–149, 240, 365–376. Movement I's motto, movement II's Cl 1 tail and the finale's horn tail are each one colour.
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 188–189: at-pitch doublings "change the basic tonal quality or characteristic of the resulting sound, and this should be seriously considered". Doubling at pitch "should be reserved for very special effects". Strength comes from octave ("organ") doubling. Violin is not among the affinities Adler lists for horn (horn appears only as a partner for oboe and clarinet).
  - Adler PDF 20 ("hearing mentally"): the score shows a horn holding G♯; the ear hears violins.
  - Monteverdi via Adler PDF 20: one mood, one unchanging combination. The motto's goal note keeps the motto's colour.
  - c377 cleared every wind–string unison from this same chord, citing Adler I PDF 257 ("overlapping at the unison robs both timbres"). This horn–violin unison was the one left.
  - c430 rejected a Vln II G♯4 unison with Hn 1 at bars 1–2 because it is "hidden in the horn". The same reasoning applies here.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-440/work/sections/coda.ily).**

  ```
  codaVlnTwo = { … | gis'2.\pp | g'2. | e'2. | e'2. | R2.*16 | }
  ```

  It was `… | e'2. | e'2. | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~\> | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4\! | R2.*12 | }`. A comment citing c440 was added above the variable.
  - Vln II's E4 at 359–360 passes at the same pitch to Hn 2's E4, which enters at 361. The hand-over falls on the bar line, with no gap and no new pitch.
  - The chord at 361–364 keeps every pitch class and c377's spacing. Strings: E2 E3 B3. Horns: E4, G♯4. Clarinets and flute: B4 E5 G♯5. Only the doubling changes.
  - Both violin sections are now silent from 361 to 368. Vln I's G♯5 at 369 therefore enters as the first violin sound since 360, which brings back the pair that opened the movement (c430).
  - c050's chromatic line (Vln II G♯4–G4–E4, 357–359) and c070's protected G4–E4 at 358–359 are unchanged.
- **Parts.** No cue in parts-cues.json uses Vln II after bar 193. Vln II has no entry after 360, so it needs no new cue. partlengths: all match.

### 3. The recorded reason for the upper-octave gap at 365–368 has been false since c430. Replace it with the reason that holds now (record, no score change)

- **Problem.**
  - c140 and c340 kept the gap in the G♯5 octave between Fl 1 (361–364) and Vln I (369) because "the horn alone mirrors bars 1–2".
  - Since c430, bars 1–2 are Hn 1 G♯4 with Vln I G♯5, not the horn alone, so that reason is now false.
  - A later critic who reads it could fill the gap by bringing Vln I in at 364/365 under the flute, which XV-37 seems to support.
- **Tested.** I wrote out the alternative: Vln I enters ppp at 364 under Fl 1's fading G♯5 and holds to 376. On the reduction (Adler PDF 23) it makes the last sixteen bars one continuous fade, and it removes two things the current scoring has.
  - **The palindrome with the opening.** The movement opens with the pair (1–2), then the horn alone (3–6). It ends with the horn alone (365–368), then the pair (369–376). The order is reversed exactly, and the gap is the horn-alone half.
  - **The four-bar groups.** The close runs in groups of four: 361 chord, 365 horn alone, 369 pair, 373 fade. Each group has one timpani motive x (363, 367, 371). Its colour changes on the group's downbeat. That is the "wanted seam" XV-40 allows (Adler PDF 192), and the held G♯4 underneath is passed on with overlaps (Hn 1 → Hn 3 → Hn 1), as XV-37 asks.

  Filling the gap would blur both, so it would make the music worse. I did not apply it.
- **Fix (text, HISTORY.md do-not-touch, for the composer).** "365–368: the upper G♯5 rests between Fl 1 (to 364) and Vln I (from 369). Reason (c440, replacing c140/c340's 'horn alone mirrors bars 1–2', false since c430): the close reverses the opening, pair → horn alone (1–6) against horn alone → pair (365–376), and each four-bar group (361/365/369/373) changes colour on its downbeat around one timpani motive x."
