## Findings

No finding needs a change. I checked every principle above bar by bar. The points below are the ones the reading could argue against the score; I tested each and decided to keep it. They are ranked by how close each came to being a finding.

### 1. The soft span 225–272 (about 36 s): kept

Problem tested: from the codetta (225) through the retransition and the soft da capo up to 272, nothing is louder than p or mp. That is 48 bars, about 36 s and 13% of the movement. Adler's Mozart example (PDF 267–268) returns quickly to tutti between solo phrases, so I asked whether this span loses the movement's motion.

Evidence for keeping it:
- Its background changes on every unit. The harp thins by unit at 225, 229 and 233 (c153). At 237 the rolled chord and timpani roll begin the rit. At 241 the theme head passes Vla → Cl 1 → Vln I over pizz. B with the accelerando (PDF 192–195: colour change gives density to a sparse texture). The background crescendo in timpani and basses starts at 249 (Bartók, PDF 272). At 253 the leggiero theme returns over a held cello line, with the flute's heterophony as a new colour (PDF 273–274).
- The tempo also moves: rit. to 2.=66, then accelerando to 88.
- The first forte after it (273) arrives through a dialogue answer (Bsn 1 at 269–272, PDF 257).
- A cut would renumber 237–376, which the finale quotes (c133, c153), and the da capo is soft by design.

Decision: keep.

### 2. Second idea at 45: dense accompaniment straight after the tutti 41–44: kept

Problem tested: Adler (PDF 267, point 3) cuts the orchestra back as soon as the solo line enters after a tutti. At 45 the accompaniment is denser than the tutti before it (13.0 sounding voices against 9.6).

Evidence for keeping it: the second idea is not a solo but the exposition's forte tutti theme. Its quieter, reduced settings come later, at 121 (sub. p, Fl 1 and Ob 1 join at 125) and 277 (Ob 1 at 281, Fl 1 at 285). Those follow the Mozart and Haydn principles (PDF 267, 288) exactly, and each statement is scored differently from the one before. Thinning 45 would undo c068, c084, c088 and c147.

Decision: keep.

### 3. Trio first statement 153–168: one background figure for 16 bars: kept

Problem tested: the harp off-beats, the Hn 3·4 drone and the pizz. bass do not change across T's four phrases (PDF 267: the accompaniment changes from phrase to phrase).

Evidence for keeping it:
- This is the tune's first presentation, a musette. Adler's Verdi example (PDF 278) gives exactly this simple, unchanging figure so that the line projects.
- The drone breaks at 166 (c151).
- Every later statement changes the background: 169 strings with a cello countermelody, 209 tutti with the flute octave at 217, codetta thinning, coda 341/345/349.
- Protected by c089 and c093.

Decision: keep.

### 4. Reprise heterophony from the first bar (253), not saved for the second phrase: kept

Problem tested: Bizet keeps the octave for the climax and Haydn changes the doubling in the second phrase (PDF 279, 288). Fl 1 colours theme A from 253, not from the climb at 261–264.

Evidence for keeping it: heterophony is not plain octave doubling. Adler groups it with the Liszt flute (PDF 273–274), which accompanies the line throughout by stressing its main pitches. The flute is also what makes the da capo a new statement rather than a copy of 13–28. Protected by c107.

Decision: keep.

### 5. Coda proportions: Più mosso 9.4 s against 32 s of Meno mosso and close: kept

Problem tested: the fast stage is short against the slow one.

Evidence for keeping it:
- The fast stage is a Bartók-type build (PDF 270–272): background entries 325–328, wind and horn crescendo, timpani roll into 333. It reaches the only fff and B6, and the G.P. at 340 divides the stages cleanly.
- The Meno mosso changes colour every four bars (341 Hn 3, 345 Ob 1 + Bsn 1 counterline, 349 Cl 1 + Vln I with arco strings), as Haydn does (PDF 288).
- The closing page is protected for the cyclic join.

Decision: keep. This is unchanged since c133, c153 and c183.

Nothing else in the reading points to a change. The large form, the section lengths and the joins all follow Adler's guidelines for pacing foreground and background.
