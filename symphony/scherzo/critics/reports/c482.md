## Findings

### 1. Bars 198→199: parallel 5ths between Hn 1 and the violas/Cl 2, made by c480's Neapolitan sixth (applied)

**Problem.** In the trio's forte statement, the violas and Cl 2 carry T an octave below Cl 1/Vln II (c437). Since c480, T's bar-3 note at 199 is D, not D♯, so these two lines move E4→D4 from beat 3 of 198 to the downbeat of 199. Hn 1 holds B4 above them and falls to A4 at the same moment. The result is B4/E4 → A4/D4: consecutive perfect 5ths in similar motion, at f, between two real lines.

The fault is new. In v480 the violas moved E4→D♯4, so the same horn line gave P5 → d5, which is allowed. c480 changed the violas, Cl 1, Vln II and Cl 2 but left Hn 1 as it was. My per-player scan finds no other parallel 5ths in the movement apart from the motive-x stretto at 329, which is by design.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 267: where repeated patterns meet, 5ths and octaves "can become a problem", and voice-leading tones prevent them.
- A&S PDF 83: parallel perfect 5ths are excluded.
- PDF 85's exemption does not apply. Hn 1 is not an octave doubling of the violas. It is a separate line, T's head with its own 9-8 tie into 200 (c039).

**The protection, read as a reason.** c322 protected "Hn 1 B4→A4 at 198–199". Its reason: B4 is the 7th of C♯m7 at 198, T leaps up to E5, and the horn takes the 7th's transferred resolution down to A4 in the same register (A&S PDF 406–407, Ex. 24-11). The fix below keeps that resolution. B4 still reaches A4 in Hn 1, in the same register, on the same downbeat. It only passes through the consonant root C♯5 on beat 3, which is a decorated resolution (A&S PDF 359, Ex. 21-26).

c427 tested two other ways to change Hn 1 here and rejected both:
- holding B4 into 199 as a 4–3 suspension, which sounds against the call's A5, its own tone of resolution (A&S PDF 352);
- resting on beats 1–2 of 199, which breaks the horn's doubling of T's head.

The C♯5 avoids both objections.

**Fix (applied, sections/tB.ily, tBHnOne, bar 198).**
```
gis'2(\mf a'4 | b'2 cis''4 | a'2.~ | a'4\> gis'2)      (was ... | b'2. | a'2.~ | ...)
```
The intervals now:
- Hn 1 against the violas/Cl 2: M6 (C♯5/E4) → P5 (A4/D4). This is a hidden 5th in inner voices of a full texture, the mildest case on A&S PDF 87.
- Against T's upper octave E5→D5: m3 → P4.
- Against the call G♯5→A5: contrary motion.
- Against Hn 2 E4→F♯4: 6th → 3rd.

C♯ is already in the bass, and the beat is a plain C♯ minor triad. A4 is still tied into 200 and falls to G♯4 as the 9-8 (c039). Nothing else changes: T, the call, the dynamics, the slur and every other part are as they were. A re-scan of the scratch build shows no parallels at 198–199. On page 7 the shared Hn 1·2 staff prints the bar as two voices with no collision.

This is a note change inside a part that plays anyway. No line is silenced, moved or added, so the cues in parts-cues.json for 185–208 still point at the same sounding instruments.
