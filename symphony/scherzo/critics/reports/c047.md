## Findings (ranked)

### 1. F-major episode 73-88: the soft wind chord moves in fifths, drops to three real parts and puts the bassoon above the horns (applied)
**Bars:** 73-77, 84, 87-88.
**Problem:** This is the only string-free stretch of the movement. For 16 bars four sustained parts lie exposed under the harp's melody: Bass Cl, Hn 4, Hn 3 and Bsn 1. In v47:
- 74→75: Hn 4 F3–C4 Bsn 1 goes to G3–D4. These are parallel fifths in an inner pair, and Bsn 1 also moves in twelfths with the bass clarinet (C4/F2 → D4/G2).
- 77, 84, 87-88: two of the four players sit on the same pitch (Hn 3 = Bsn 1 on C4 at 77 and 87-88; Hn 4 = Bsn 1 on A3 at 84). At 77 the winds sound no F at all.
- 73-77: Bsn 1 lies above both horns, with the bass clarinet below them. The horns are therefore enclosed by reeds, and in every bar the horns carry the moving parts.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 108 makes four rules for this exact grouping of two horns and bassoons, soft and sustained. Crossing is recommended "for the purposes of blend, the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons". "Bassoons may also be written inside the horns, but the inverse process is not to be recommended." "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." PDF 112 adds that "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". PDF 114 says balance matters most in long sustained chords like these. Ex. 157 (Antar, PDF 330) shows woodwind and horns crossed in a moving chain of chords.

**Fix (sB.ily):**
```
sBBsnOne  (73-88): \partCombineSoloI a2.\pp | a2. | d'2. | d'2. | a2. | a2. | bes2. | a2 r4 | a2. | bes2. | d'2. | a2. | a2. | a2. | c'2. | c'2. |
sBHnThree (73-88): c'2.\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." | c'2. | bes2. | bes2. | c'2. | d'2. | des'2. | c'2 r4 | d'2. | d'2. | bes2. | c'2. | c'2. | c'2. | a2. | a2. |
sBHnFour  (73-88): f2.\pp | f2. | g2. | g2. | f2. | f2. | g2. | f2 r4 | f2. | f2. | g2. | f2. | f2. | f2. | fis2. | fis2. |
```
**Result:**
- At 73-77 the bassoon's A3 sits inside the horns' F3–C4.
- The fifths at 74-75 are gone, and Hn 4 holds F3 through 77-78 and 84-86.
- Every chord has four real parts: F at 77 and 84, and A–F♯–C–D♯ at 87 with Cl 2.
- Bsn 1 enters at 73 on A3, the A3 it played at 72.
- Hn 2's common-tone A across 72-73 (c010) is untouched.
- 78-83 are unchanged, including Hn 3's D–D♭–C (c042). I left the D♭ at 79 in the horn, which goes against row 6. Moving it to the bassoon would need a diminished-fourth leap and a crossing in both directions, and it would split the chromatic line that c042 placed in one voice.

### 2. Trio codetta 229-236: T's head is buried in held notes of its own register (applied)
**Bars:** 229-232 (Cl 1), 233-236 (Bsn 1).
**Problem:** The codetta hands T's head from Ob 1 (225) to Cl 1 (229) to Bsn 1 (233). Only the oboe is clear.
- Cl 1's G♯4–A4 | B4 | C♯5 | B4 starts in unison with the violas' held G♯4, then rubs A4 against it, then lands on Vln II's held B4 at 230 and 232. This is the fault c039 removed at 197-200.
- At 233-236 the harp's off-beat chords (G♯3–B3–E4, A3–C4–E4 at 235) are plucked at the pitches of Bsn 1's G♯3–A3–B3–C4–B3.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 112 sets out the string–woodwind layout as motion in the wind over sustained harmony in the strings. In Ex. 171 (PDF 342) the clarinet's line and the sustained divided strings lie in separate registers. In Ex. 152 (PDF 324) the held chord is the basis and the moving line is placed clear of it. The bassoon's own position inside Hn 3·4 at 233-236 is already the recommended crossing (PDF 108). Only the harp is in its way.

**Fix (tC.ily):**
- Vln II at 229-232: `e''2. | e''2. | e''2. | e''2.` (was b' b' a' b').
- Violas at 229-232: `b2. | b2. | a2. | b2.` (was gis' gis' e' gis').
- Harp RH at 233-236: `r4 <e' gis' b'>4 <e' gis' b'>4 | r4_\markup \small "C♮" <e' gis' b'>4 <e' gis' b'>4 | r4 <e' a' c''>4 <e' a' c''>4 | r4 <e' gis' b'>4 <e' gis' b'>4`.

The clarinet now runs between the violas' B3 and Vln II's E5, and the harmony is unchanged (E, E, IV, E). The harp now sits above the bassoon and doubles the held Vla G♯4 and Vln II B4. I tried the harp a full octave up first, but the analyser then reported a new outer-voice octave at 235, so I used this voicing instead.

### 3. Trio return 211 and 222: the horn takes the dominant seventh (applied)
**Bars:** 211, 222.
**Problem:** 209-224 is the two-horns-and-two-bassoons four-part texture (c014). At 211 Hn 1 plays A4, the seventh of B7, and then rises to B4 at 212. At 222 Hn 1's A4 doubles the seventh that Bsn 1 already holds (A3) at the trio's last cadence. The c014 comment says "the 7ths in the bassoon", but at these two bars the horn has it.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 108: concords to the horns, discords to the bassoons; stationary parts to the brass. Bars 214-215 already follow this (the seventh E is in Bsn 1).

**Fix (tC.ily, tCHnOne):** bar 3 (211) `a'2.` → `b'2.`, bar 14 (222) `a'2.` → `b'2.`.
- Hn 1 now holds B4 from 209 to 212 and from 221 to 222.
- The B chord at 211 is complete in the winds (B4, F♯4, D♯4, B2).
- At 222 the seventh A3 is in Bsn 1 alone, and it falls to G♯3 at 223.
- Hn 1·2 keep the thirds and sixths elsewhere (c004).

### 4. Bar 197: the horns are as loud as the woodwind they double (applied)
**Bars:** 197-200.
**Problem:** At the trio's forte, Hn 1 plays T's G♯4–A4–B4 in unison with Cl 1 (c039), and both horns are marked f, the same as the woodwind. A horn at f in unison with a clarinet at f covers the clarinet. That defeats c037's combination of T against the oboe's call.

**Evidence:** Rimsky PDF 108: "Played forte, the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind". PDF 112: when balance is difficult, mark the woodwind one degree louder than the brass. The score already does this at 101-104 (woodwind f, horns p<) and at 209 (horns p).

**Fix (tB.ily):** in tBHnOne and tBHnTwo at bar 13 (197), `\f` → `\mf`. Both players are on the shared staff, so the dynamics stay equal. The diminuendo at 200 is unchanged.
