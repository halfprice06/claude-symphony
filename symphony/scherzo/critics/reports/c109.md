## What else I studied

## Holistic comparison with Brahms 2/iii

Brahms's movement is a grazioso intermezzo. Its weight is in the woodwind choir, its bass is plucked, the brass beyond two horns is mostly silent, and every contrasting section is the opening tune in a new metre. This scherzo follows its own plan (Dvořák 9/iii, Beethoven 9/ii) and is louder and more rhythmic. Where the two do comparable things, the scherzo meets Brahms's standard:
- The quiet solos are scored the way Brahms scores his oboe tune: one woodwind with plain support. Cl 1 over harp off-beats, a horn drone and pizz. bass at 153; Ob 1 dolce over harp chords with a bassoon countermelody at 345. Rimsky's Ex. 40 (PDF 218) is the same layout.
- Returns are varied, not copied, as Brahms varies his. Theme A comes back with pizz. Vln II and flute heterophony at 253. The second idea comes back with horn and cellos in the tenor at 281. The trio tune moves to B at 180, and the coda gives it in A major in the horn.
- The movement has one tonal surprise of the kind Brahms makes with his F♯-major return: the Neapolitan F-major episode at 73, without strings and with the theme head on the harp.
- The plucked bass under the soft music is like Brahms's cellos.

Brahms's thematic economy is the one area where he goes further: his contrasting sections are metric transformations of the minuet tune. Here the trio tune comes from the horn call, not from theme A. The tune is recorded and the finale depends on the themes, so I do not propose rewriting it. A conductor would see this as a different and legitimate plan, not a weakness.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 121–128: the held horn sits on the second idea's lower octave (applied)

**Problem.** In the E-minor second idea after the tutti, the melody is in Vln I (B5–F♯6) with its lower octave in Vln II (B4–F♯5), joined by Ob 1 at 125. Hn 3 holds B4 for four bars, then C5 C5 B4 B4, at sub. p. The horn takes the first, accented note of each phrase at the unison: B4 with Vln II at 121, and C5 with Vln II and Ob 1 at 125. The lower octave then moves around the held note: G4 and A4 below it, E5, D♯5 and F♯5 above it. So a sustained horn sits in the middle of the lower octave's compass. The tutti before this ends at 120, and the strings must make the sub. p with the horn holding a note on the melody's own pitch.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 54: a melody in the middle of the orchestral range is not prominent by position and needs contrast, doubling or separation from the accompaniment.
- PDF 78: a doubled melody's lower octave is the part that gives it body.
- In all the examples with a tune in a middle octave (Ex. 1, 23, 30, 40, PDF 173, 199, 207, 218), the sustained winds and horns stay below the tune or are in another register. Ex. 30 (Sheherazade iii) is the closest case: violins in octaves, cantabile, with the horns' held notes under the lower violin line.
- The score already applied this rule at the parallel place: c084 moved Hn 1 from D5 to B4 at 45 because the held horn sat on the first note of the second idea's lower octave and covered it. At 121 the horn is at the unison, which is a stronger case than c084's.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-109/work/sections/sC.ily).** Hn 3 goes an octave lower for 121–128 only:

```
sCHnThree = { … | g4) r4 r4 | b2.--(\subP | b2.-- | b2.-- | b2.--) | c'2.--(\< | c'2.-- | b2.-- | b2.--) | b'2.--(\f | a'2.-- | g'2.--) | g'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*8 | }
```

(It was `b'2.--(\subP | b'2.-- | b'2.-- | b'2.--) | c''2.--(\< | c''2.-- | b'2.-- | b'2.--)`.) The cadence 129–132 is unchanged; Hn 3 returns to B4 at the f.

This also improves the spacing. Before the change, the next voice above Vc/Bsn 2 (E2–B2) was Cl 2/Vla at E4, more than an octave away. The horn's B3 now fills that gap, close to the violas, as in the close middle spacing of Ex. 30. B3 and C4 are in the horn's easiest soft register. Hn 4 rests here, so the Hn 3·4 staff shows Hn 3 alone; there is no notational crossing with Hn 2, which is on another staff.

**Checked.** No new outer-voice parallel (still 7). The all-pairs scan has 6 fewer octave hits at 127 (Hn 3 against Cl 2 and violas) and no new ones. Cl 2 meets the horn's B3 at 122 and 124 on held notes only.

**Not changed.** The reprise 277–284 has the same B4/C5 in Hn 1. There, from 281, Hn 2 and the cellos carry the melody in the tenor (C4–F♯4) (c071), so lowering Hn 1 would put it on that line. I left it.

### Points considered and not raised

- **Cl 1 crossing above Ob 1/Vln II in the second idea (45–52, 121–128).** This breaks the natural order (Rimsky PDF 65, 67). c009 recorded it as minor because Vln I carries the upper octave. Moving Cl 1 under the oboe would undo c014's protected "clarinets inside the horns". Rimsky does allow crossing "to attain certain special effects", and here it makes a voice exchange under the hemiola. Left.
- **37–40 Fl 1·2 a2 at p.** See above. Not a regression.
- **Flute heterophony 253–268 above Vln I** (up to A6). This is Rimsky's PDF 68 rule in reverse: a high wind line with the strings an octave below. Do-not-touch (c107).
- **Harp tune 73–80; C.a. with the cellos at 189–192; contrabassoon with the basses at 209–224.** All three match Rimsky's examples (Ex. 6/40, Ex. 15/17, Ex. 10/11).
- **Theme A over bowed off-beats at 13–28 (Ex. 1 uses pizz. off-beats).** The pizz. version is kept for the reprise at 253. That contrast is deliberate and protected (c007).
