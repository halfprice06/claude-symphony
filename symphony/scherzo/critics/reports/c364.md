## Findings

### 1. 209–224: the harp's left hand plucks the bass under bowed basses at the forte return of T, and drops out for two bars mid-phrase (applied)

**Problem.** At the trio's forte return the harp's left hand plucks the bass on each downbeat, mf: E2–G♯2–B1–E2–A1 | rest | rest | B1–C♯2–G♯1–F♯1–A1–B1–B1–E2–E2. The double basses play the same notes arco, f, sustained, and the contrabassoon doubles them. Everything else in the orchestra is sustained at f (Fl 1, Cl 1, both violin sections on T, horns, bassoons, violas, cellos). The harp rests at 214–215 only because c346 and c354 found no pedalling that lets it follow the basses' A♯1 while its low A string still rings. So the doubling stops for two bars in the middle of the phrase and returns at 216, for a mechanical reason and not a musical one. This is the only place in the movement where the harp doubles a bowed bass. At 153–168 and 341–348 it doubles pizzicato basses, which Rimsky-Korsakov calls the perfect blend.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 52: "Uniting plucked strings and percussion with bowed instruments does not produce such a satisfactory blend, both qualities being heard independently. The combination of plucked strings with percussion alone, is excellent." PDF 51: "The combined force of groups of sustained resonance easily overpowers the strings played pizz." (the harp falls in the same class: "instruments of little sustaining power"). PDF 53: plucked strings "should only be employed at reasonable intervals". PDF 81–82: "Every transition from one order of harmonic writing to another ... must coincide with the introduction of a new idea". PDF 90, rule 1: instruments "must be used continuously in the same way during a given passage, that is to say they must be doubled or not throughout". The two-bar gap breaks the last two rules. The plucked notes either disappear under the f tutti (PDF 51) or are heard as a separate click under the bowed line (PDF 52). Neither adds anything the arco basses and the contrabassoon do not already give.

The recorded reasons for keeping it do not outweigh this. c274 cited PDF 52's "wind instruments ... strengthen and clarify pizzicato strings, harp", because the contrabassoon plays the same notes. That sentence describes wind joined to plucked strings. Here bowed strings are also on the line, and the same paragraph says plucked and bowed stay apart. c033 kept "the plucked downbeat" when it made the basses arco. That gave the bass a second articulation that the cantabile forte return does not need, and it has cost three later cycles of pedal repairs (c174, c346, c354).

**Fix (applied).** In sections/tC.ily the harp's left hand rests at 209–224. The harp is then silent from 193 to 224 and comes back with both hands at 225, where the codetta's new idea begins (PDF 81). There it again doubles pizzicato basses at pp, the blend PDF 52 recommends. The pedal mark "A♮" moves from 209 to 224. With both hands resting, the score hides the harp staff in the system 193–213, so the mark printed at 209 was lost (checked on page 8: it now prints under the right hand at 224, before the entry).

### Considered and kept (my decisions)

- 45–56 and 121–128: the clarinet pair often moves in fourths and fifths under the strings' melody (D5/G4, E5/A4, D5/A4; B4/E4, F♯4/B3). This is the case PDF 97 remark 4 describes ("not open fourths and fifths"), and that is a stronger reason than PDF 91 b), which c274 used to keep it. I wrote out every beat of 45–55. Each third or sixth either puts Cl 2 in unison with the violas (undoing c277), crosses Cl 1 further above the melody's lower octave in Ob 1, or reopens the 4–3 and V–vi voice leading fixed at 47, 55 and 131 (c262, c172, c289). The clarinets are mf inside f strings, flutes and oboes. I keep it. A later melody or texture cycle that rewrites the second idea's inner parts should fix the intervals at the same time.
- 336: Ob 1 B5 over Ob 2 F♯5 is a fifth in the oboe pair (PDF 90 rule 4, "specially ... the oboe"). The only concord available is D♯5. That would leave the fifth F♯ out of the upper winds and put the leading tone in four players across three octaves (Fl 2, Cl 1, Ob 2, Tpt 2). It is one bar at fff, under Fl 1 and three string octaves. I keep it.
- 33–34: the clarinets hold A4/E4 (a fourth) in the winds-only call. The alternative (Cl 2 C4, Bsn 1 E3) puts the bassoon pair in fifths moving from 29–31's B3/E3, which is no gain. I keep it.
- 169–184: the pizzicato bass lies two octaves and more below the cellos' T, with nothing between. PDF 85 says "rarely", and PDF 86 allows wide distribution in piano. This is the strings-only colouring that c319 and c344 kept. I keep it.
