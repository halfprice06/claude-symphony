Melody, judged bar by bar. Theme A (13-28) is the movement's most memorable idea: motive x, then the chromatic tail, a peak on C6 in its twelfth bar, and the falling B7 arpeggio that ends each four-bar phrase. The second idea (45-56) has its own rhythm and a real climax on A6 at 50. T (153-168) can be sung. Its peaks rise E5, F♯5, A5, it ends with a 4-3, and it is given to the clarinet's clarino, the register Adler calls the clarinet's expressive one (PDF 210), much as Mozart's 39 trio does. The harp melody (73-80) and the coda's shared return of T (341-356) are protected and they work: in the coda the tune climbs one octave with each colour, Hn 1 C♯4, then Ob 1 D5, then Vln I C♯6, up to the G♯6 at 354. Woodwind melody writing follows Adler almost throughout. The two weak points concern how doubling woodwinds carry the themes at their phrase endings.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 39→40, 107→108, 307→308: in the doubling winds, theme A's closing arpeggio turns into a major-seventh leap upward. Applied.
**Problem.** Each four-bar phrase of theme A ends on a falling dominant arpeggio: `g'4 | fis'4 dis' b` (15-16), a step G→F♯ and then thirds down to B. The doubling instruments cannot go below their ranges or into their weak registers, so at the second of these bars they jump up an octave. They make that jump on the step, so in their part the descent G→F♯ becomes a leap up a major seventh in the middle of the cadence figure:
- 107→108 and 307→308 (tutti): Ob 1 G4→F♯5, C.a. and Cl 2 G3→F♯4. In the reprise Vln II does the same, G3→F♯4. Vln I does not. Since c010 it changes octave one beat earlier, on the head's leap (E5→B5, a fifth up in place of a fourth down), and then falls G5-F♯5-D♯5-B4. So the oboe plays Vln II's octave at 107 and Vln I's at 108, and the flutes rest at 105-108 (c124). Vln I's B5-G5 at 107 therefore has no wind doubling, and the most cutting wind line in the tutti has a kink that no string line has.
- 39→40 (Fl 1·2 a2): c036 lifted the arpeggio at 40 out of the flute's weakest octave, so the flutes now play B4→A5, a seventh up, where the theme steps B→A.

octswitch.py finds no other place in the movement where a doubling wind changes octave inside a phrase on a step. At 40→41, 43→44, 112→113 and 116→117 the change falls on a leap or at a phrase boundary, which is the right place.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 201: the oboe is a "poignant doubler" that adds "articulation and cutting edge", so "great care must be given to have it double a significant line". Here, for one bar, it doubles neither line in full. PDF 185 and 198 explain why the doublers have to move up in the first place: the flute below G4 is weak and must not be covered, and the oboe's lowest fifth is thick. The remedy is to move the octave change, not to remove it. Where the theme has a leap, the octave change can turn the leap the other way (a fourth down becomes a fifth up) without breaking the line. Vln I's c010 version at 107 does exactly this. In the flutes' own register at 39 the change is just as idiomatic: PDF 186 notes the "profound changes of register" in the Brahms 4/iv flute line, all made at leaps.
**Fix (applied).** The upper octave starts on beat 2 of the bar before, so every doubler follows Vln I's contour:
- sA.ily, sAFluteOne and sAFluteTwo bar 39: `g''4-.) d'''8-. d'''-. b''4-. | a''4-. fis''-. d''-.` (was `d''8-. d''-. b'4-.`).
- sC.ily bar 107: sCOboeOne `e''4-.) b''8-. b''-. g''4-. | fis''4-. dis''-. b'-.`; sCCorAnglais and sCClTwo `e'4-.) b'8-. b'-. g'4-. | fis'4-. dis'-. b-.`.
- dC.ily bar 307: dCOboeOne as at 107; dCCorAnglais, dCClTwo and dCVlnTwo `e'4-.) b'8-. b'-. g'4-.`.

Ob 1 now doubles Vln I in unison through 105-108, and C.a., Cl 2 (and in the reprise Vln II) double it an octave lower through those four bars. All the new notes lie in good registers: Fl D6/B5 is clear; Ob B5/G5 is in its best range; C.a. B4 sounding is written F♯5; Cl 2 B4/G4 sounding is written D5/B♭4. The analyser's parallels are unchanged. Sound and pitch classes are the same as before, so themecheck reports 41/41 (it ignores octave). The register change touches the recorded Ob 1 and Cl 2 statements at 105 and 305; see "Theme changes".

### 2. Bars 129-132 and 285-288: at the E-minor second idea's cadence the oboe drops into its "honking" fifth while the leading line is carried by a flute in its middle register. Applied.
**Problem.** In G (53-56) Ob 1 doubles the cadence `d''4 b' g' | a'2 fis'4 | a'4 g'2` at F♯4 or higher, inside its best range (from F4). In E minor the same lower-octave doubling (with Vln II) lies a third lower: `b'4 g' e' | fis'2 dis'4 | fis'4 e'2`, E4-D♯4 at f, the lowest fifth of the oboe. The lower octave is already crowded at these bars: Vln II, Cl 1·2, violas and Hn 2·3 all sit between B3 and B4. The tune itself, B5-G5-E5 | F♯5-D♯5 | F♯5-E5, is played by Vln I and Fl 1. That puts the flute in the middle register Adler calls "sweet and with little carrying power", at f under horns at f. So the theme's closing bars, the point a listener carries away from a tune, sound weakest on the line that should lead and thickest on the doubling.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 198 (Ex. VII-32): the oboe "becomes thick and tends to 'honk' in the very lowest fifth"; it is most effective "from the F in the first space to the C above the staff". PDF 185 (Ex. VII-2): in the flute's middle register the player "can only" give so much volume, so it must not be covered. PDF 201: the oboe's edge should go to "a significant line". The rule kept since c119 (at most two winds in unison with one string section, Rimsky PDF 76) still holds: Fl 1 and Ob 1 on Vln I.
**Fix (applied).** Ob 1 starts the cadence on B4 and joins the top line on beat 2. The third down (B→G) becomes a sixth up, so the octave change falls on a leap, as in finding 1. The quaver lead-in C♯5→B4 stays unbroken.
- sC.ily, sCOboeOne bars 129-131: `b'4(\f g''4 e''4 | fis''2 dis''4 | fis''4 e''2)` (was `g'4 e'4 | fis'2 dis'4 | fis'4 e'2`).
- dA.ily, dAOboeOne bars 285-287: `b'4\f( g''4 e''4 | fis''2 dis''4 | fis''4 e''2)`.

The oboe now ends on E5 with Fl 1 and Vln I. Vln II keeps the lower octave (with Hn 2 and the cellos in the tenor at 285, c071). Nothing changes at 53-56 or 125-128. The cadence harmony is unchanged, and the parallel list is unchanged.
