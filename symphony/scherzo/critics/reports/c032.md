## Findings

### 1. Second idea, 49–52: phrase 2 breaks the sequence, with parallel ninths in the outer voices (applied)

**Problem.** Phrase 2 (49–52) repeats phrase 1 (45–48) a step higher in the melody. Read in hemiola minims, phrase 1's bass is G–B–G | D–E–B, and the outer voices run 5–8–8 | 10–8–10. Phrase 2's bass is A–A–G | F♯–F♯–C♯, which gives 5–10–9 | ♭9–8–10. The melody falls A6→G6 while the bass falls G2→F♯2 (bar 50, beats 2–3 to bar 51, beats 1–2). That is a major ninth followed by a minor ninth between the outer voices. In the second of them the bass has already resolved the 7th of A7/G to F♯, while the melody takes the 7th (G) up into the top voice over its own note of resolution. At *f*, in Vln I and Fl 1 with Vln II and Ob 1 an octave lower, this is the harshest outer-voice moment in the scherzo.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 269: a sequence is defined by the outer-voice intervals of its pattern, and imperfect consonances are preferred. PDF 287: "you should preserve the sequence in your harmonization." PDF 266: chord qualities change to fit the key. The E-minor statements (121–128, 277–288) use a different and sound phrase 2 (Phrygian iv6–V), so only the G-major exposition has this fault.

**Fix (applied).** The bass of phrase 2 becomes phrase 1's bass a step higher. Bar 51 becomes A7/E → D/F♯ (V4/3 → V6 of V): the melody's G falls to F♯ as a 7th should, and Cl 1's C♯ rises to D. Cl 2 and the violas are revoiced so they make no octaves with the new bass. Bars 49–52:
```
sABsnOne, sAVc:  a,2( cis4~ | cis4 a,2) | e2( fis4~ | fis4 cis2)        (was a,2( a,4~ | a,4 g,2) | fis,2( fis,4~ | fis,4 cis2))
sACb, sACbsn:    a,,2( cis,4~ | cis,4 a,,2) | e,2( fis,4~ | fis,4 cis,2)
sAClTwo:         cis'2( a'4~ | a'4 e'2) | e'2( a'4~ | a'4 a'2)
sAVla:           cis'2( a'4~ | a'4 cis'2) | e'2( d'4~ | d'4 e'2)
```
The outer voices now run 5–8–8 | 10–8–10, matching phrase 1. The new bass and the violas make a voice exchange (A/C♯ ↔ C♯/A) at 49–50. The melody, the horns and the hemiola are unchanged, so the theme record is unchanged.

### 2. Coda 329–332: the falling bass is doubled by the leading violins, and the drone's B sounds against the iv6 (applied)

**Problem.** Over the bass E–D–C–B, Vln I's motive x falls E5–D5–C5–B4, two octaves above the bass, so the most prominent line in the crescendo is the bass in octaves (8–8–8–8). Meanwhile the E–B ostinato stays fixed in Vc, Vla and Vln II. At 331 (iv6, C–E–A) it sounds B against C in three octaves: Vc B2 against Bsn 2 C3, Vla B3 against Cl 2 C4, Vln II B4 against Vln I C5. The B is the goal tone of the Phrygian half step C→B (c022's iv6–V), so the drone sounds it before the bass gets there, and the half step loses its pull.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267: voices above a sequential bass are themselves sequential, and repeated patterns produce 5ths and octaves unless voice-leading chords intervene. PDF 269: prefer 10-10 to 8-type patterns, which "might sound empty". PDF 277–279 (Ex. 17-16a, 17-17): a falling bass scale is set with 10ths above it, and in Handel the violins move in parallel 10ths. The score already has the right line, in Cl 1: G4–F♯4–E4–D♯4.

**Fix (applied).** Vln I takes Cl 1's 10ths an octave higher, so its line G–F♯–E–D♯ leads by step into the E6 at 333 (the leading tone resolves in the top line; the leap to E6 is a 9th, shorter than the old 11th). At 331–332 the ostinato voices keep motive x's rhythm but play chord tones. The drone stays at 329–330, where it is consonant or a soft added 6th and 9th.
```
codaVlnOne 329-332: g''4-.\mf e''8-. e''-. g''4-. | fis''4-. d''8-. d''-. fis''4-. | e''4-. c''8-. c''-. e''4-. | dis''4-.\f b'8-. b'-. r4
codaVlnTwo 331-332: e''4-. a'8-. a'-. e''4-. | b'4-. fis'8-. fis'-. b'4-.
codaVla    331-332: a4-. e8-. e-. a4-. | b4-. fis8-. fis-. b4-.
codaVc     331-332: e4-. a,8-. a,-. e4-. | b,4-. fis,8-. fis,-. b,4-.
```

Bar 331 is now a clean iv6 (no G, so still no C-major triad), and bar 332 is V7 with D♯ only in the Vln I/Cl 1 octave line. The stretto entries at 325–328 are untouched; their parallel fifths are the drone effect accepted in v01.

### 3. Trio, 196–197: doubled, unresolved 7th in the deceptive cadence (applied)

**Problem.** At 196, B7 has its 7th A in both Cl 1 (A4) and the violas (A3), and the leading tone D♯ only in Cl 2. At 197 (C♯ minor, *f*), both A's leap up (Cl 1 A→E5, violas A→C♯4) and Cl 2's D♯ leaps up to G♯. None of the tendency tones resolves, so the deceptive cadence that opens the trio's forte call is weak.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: the chordal 7th "must never be doubled". PDF 104: neither the leading tone nor the 7th of V7 is doubled. PDF 202: in V7–VI the 3rd of VI is doubled so that 7 can rise to 8. A&S PDF 267/287 applies the same logic to sequences: a doubling that is harmless elsewhere is out of place just before a goal.

**Fix (applied).** The violas take B at 196 instead of A. At 197–200 the clarinet lines trade places (Cl 1 takes the old Cl 2 notes; Cl 2 takes the old Cl 1 notes an octave lower), so Cl 1 A→G♯ and Cl 2 D♯→E:
```
tBVla 193-197:     a2. | a2. | gis2. | b2. | cis'2.\f
tBClOne 197-200:   gis'2.\f | gis'2. | a'2. | gis'2.\>
tBClTwo 197-200:   e'2.\f | e'2. | cis'2. | bis2.\>
```
The chord at 197 keeps its E in Ob 1, both violins, Hn 2 and Cl 2.
