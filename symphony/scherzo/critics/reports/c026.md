## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 375: the harp's last chord rings past the final G♯. Applied.
- **Problem.** The do-not-touch list says the last sound is G♯ in Hn 1 and Vln I. At 375 the harp rolls E1 and E2 in the LH and E3–G♯3–B3 in the RH, at pp, under a ppp texture: Hn 1 and Vln I G♯, the end of a timpani ppp roll, and Vc/Cb pizz pp. The rolled chord is a crotchet followed by rests, with no damping sign, so the harpist lets it ring.
- **Evidence.** Adler (PDF 113–114) describes the lower two octaves as "somber, dark, and sonorous": the wound strings ring for many seconds. They sound through the fermata at 376 and past the conductor's release. The pizz. E in the cellos and basses dies at once, so after the release the last sound is the harp's low E, not the G♯.
- **Fix.** Use what Adler gives for exactly this context (PDF 116):
  - Octave harmonics are "very soft and must be accompanied very lightly".
  - Their best written range is C3 to A5.
  - One hand can play "two or three or more" if they lie within a perfect fifth.
  - E3–G♯3–B3 is a fifth, so the LH palm takes all three. They sound E4–G♯4–B4, around the horn's G♯4, and decay quickly.
  - The low E stays in the Cb/Vc pizz. and the timpani.

  In coda.ily:
  ```
  codaHarpR = { ... | R2.*13 | R2. | R2. | }
  codaHarpL = { ... | R2.*13 | <e gis b>4-\flageolet\p^\markup \small \italic "harm." r4 r4 | R2. | }
  ```
  I marked it p because harmonics sound well below a normal pp.

### 2. Bar 149: the pedal setting for the trio was printed after the harp's first chord. Applied.
- **Problem.** The E-major setting (D♯ C♯ B / E F♯ G♯ A) was attached to the chord at 149, so it printed under 150–151. That is after the entry. Five pedals change here (D, C, B, F, G).
- **Evidence.** Adler (PDF 112): "It is a help to the harpist to indicate how the pedals should be set". His preferred form is the letter form with the slash between the feet: D♭ C♯ B♭ / E♮ F♭ G♯ A♭. The harp rests from 87 to 148, so the setting belongs at the start of that rest.
- **Fix.** In link.ily, put the setting on bar 141, left-aligned, with the slash. Write bar 73 the same way.
  ```
  linkHarpR = { \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #LEFT
    \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.parent-alignment-X = #LEFT
    R2._\markup \small "Ped.: D♯ C♯ B / E F♯ G♯ A" | R2.*7 | r4 <gis' b' e''>4\arpeggio r4 | ... }
  ```
  In sB.ily 73: `"Ped.: D C B♭ / E F G A"`.

### 3. Bar 188: the C♮ change was marked at the chord, not where the change is made. Applied.
- **Problem.** The C♮ (B♯ of the G♯ chord) was printed under the chord on beat 2. The C♯ of 187 is struck on beat 3 of 187, so the left foot must move in the gap between the two chords.
- **Evidence.** Adler (PDF 113) describes the harpist changing "just a split second before playing the note", and his example puts "(to F♮)" before the note. The time is enough: two beats at 2. = 76, one pedal, one foot.
- **Fix.** In tB.ily: `r4_\markup \small "C♮" <gis c' dis'>4 <gis c' dis'>4`. The "C♯ A♯" mark at 190 is already in a gap: nothing on C or A sounds at 189–191, and the two changes are on different feet.

### 4. Bars 73–88: Hn 3·4 have 16 tongued bars without a rest. Applied.
- **Problem.** At their first entry (senza sord.) Hn 3·4 repeat dotted-minim chords for 16 bars with no rest. That is about 11 s at pp in the low-middle register, including the poco rit.
- **Evidence.** Adler (PDF 292): "one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath". Bar 80 is the end of the harp's eight-bar phrase. The bassoons and bass clarinet hold the harmony across it, so the beat-3 gap is not heard as a hole.
- **Fix.** In sB.ily, Hn 3 bar 80 `a2 r4` (was `a2.`) and Hn 4 bar 80 `f2 r4` (was `f2.`). The rhythm is the same in both, so the part-combined staff stays clean.
