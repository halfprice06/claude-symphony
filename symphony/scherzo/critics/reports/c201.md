## What else I studied

- Four script scans of v201 (in /tmp/critic-201/scan/), reading every part with coherence/lyparse.py. `pairs.py` lists runs of three or more onsets where two winds move in octaves, thirds or sixths in inverted order, or two of one colour move in octaves. `allocts.py` lists every wind octave run. `unison.py` lists onsets where three or more winds double one string part and no other string group has that pitch. `more.py` lists Fl/Bsn and Ob/Bsn unisons, brass+string unisons and woodwind octaves above trombones. Their only results are the known exceptions in rows 3, 21 and 25.
- Dvořák 9/iii, corpus `reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesS.ily`, the horns' ff hemiola after the opening (written C–B♭–A–A♭–G for horn in E, sounding E4–D4–C♯4–C4–B3). It is a chromatic descending brass line that works because it is loud and rhythmic, Rimsky's "vigorous phrase in the form of a fanfare" (PDF 73). The scherzo's quiet horn motto tail (357–360) is a different case. See "Considered" 3.
- I judged the tunes bar by bar: theme A 13–28, the counterstatement 29–44, the second idea 45–56/121–132/277–288, the harp melody 73–80, T 153–168/169–184/209–224, the trio call 185–200, the codetta heads 225–236 and the coda's return of T 341–356.

Verdict on the tunes. Theme A is the movement's signature: motive x (E–B–B–E), the chromatic tail G–F♯–F–E, and the ♭6 peak C6 at 24. It is four four-bar phrases with half and full cadences, and it is as easy to recall as the head of Dvořák 9/iii. The second idea has two sequential four-bar spans that climb by leaps to A6 at 50, then a three-bar hemiola cadence. T is a 16-bar hymn with a profile of its own: the held peak at bar 6 (c121), the tied A at bar 10 (c081), the 4–3 cadence (c061), and the turn to B in its second statement (c093). It is heard complete three times in three colours (clarinet solo, strings, strings and winds forte), then as heads in three solo winds in turn (Ob 225, Cl 229, Bsn 233, each an octave lower, which matches Rimsky's "all wood-wind in turn, simple timbres", PDF 70). In the coda it is passed from horn to oboe to violins. A listener leaves with theme A's head and T's opening in the ear. Every point ch. II teaches about scoring a melody is followed: the soft tunes are solos with light accompaniment set apart, the loud tunes are doubled in natural order, extreme registers have their lower octave, and no several winds stand on one string group outside the tuttis.

## Findings

No finding needs a change to the score. I checked each of ch. II's 26 principles bar by bar (table above), and the four scans found nothing beyond exceptions earlier cycles accepted with stated reasons. These are the points I tested, ranked by how much each could matter. I kept every one.

### Considered and kept

1. **The coda's horn statement of T, 341–344 (Hn 1 alone, p dolce, A major).** PDF 75 says a wind added to a brass instrument gives a finer legato, and a single horn doubled above takes two winds. Against that, PDF 72 puts the soft horn first among brass melodists. Ex. 73 (Antar, PDF 245) gives a soft line to a single horn ("Cor. I Solo") over light accompaniment, and PDF 66–67 say a solo keeps the expression a doubling takes away. The line is diatonic and sits in the horn's middle register (C♯4–A4), which suits PDF 71–72. c111 weighed the same point and kept it. I keep it too, because adding a wind would spend the oboe's entry at 345.

2. **T at 349–356 (Vln I above, Vln II + Cl 1 an octave below, pp).** This matches PDF 78: of two octave parts in the soprano, double the lower one. Moving Cl 1 to Vln I's octave would put a clarinet on G♯6 (written B6 for clarinet in A) at 354, out of the dolce range, and would leave the lower octave as bare strings. Kept.

3. **The motto tail in Hn 1, 357–360, chromatic and marked "espress."** PDF 73 says chromatic melodies suit brass less, and phrases full of passion become insipid in brass. But the four notes are movement I's motto, played p as a quotation rather than worked into a melody. The passage is protected (HISTORY v01, c060, c130). The one chromatic step to A♮ falls on a soft dynamic and a held note, where the horn keeps the simplicity PDF 73 asks for. The Dvořák comparison above shows that a chromatic brass line is fine when it is short and functional. Kept. I do not change the word "espress.", since the swell into the A♯ (c098) already gives that expression its shape.

4. **Three winds on Vln I at 113–116 and 313–316 (Fl 1·2, Ob 1).** PDF 76: several winds in unison with one string group overpower it. This is the ff tutti, though, where PDF 67 expects doubling, and Vln II carries the lower octave (c068) with the cor anglais an octave below that. The tune is meant to be wind-coloured here. c111 weighed it and kept it. Kept.

5. **Cl 1 above Ob 1 in thirds at 53–54 (and 45–52 crossings).** PDF 65 and 70 call inverted order strained. This is the voice exchange under the hemiola cadence that c014 and c109 protected, and no new argument from this reading answers their reason. Not re-raised.

6. **Codetta heads 225–236 (Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1 in turn, each an octave lower).** PDF 70 cites "Sadko [43], all wood-wind in turn, simple timbres", and Ex. 42 shows a soft solo oboe over pizz. strings. The harp thins under them (c153) and no instrument of the soloist's colour sits beside it (c127). Kept.

7. **The trio call 185–192 and its answer.** Oboe solo, then cor anglais and cellos in unison. PDF 77 lists Violas + Eng. horn and PDF 78 shows Rimsky's own Cellos + Eng. horn (Sheherazade). The cellos take the answer on their A and D strings, in the cor anglais's register (c011). Kept.

8. **Melody rhythm of T (minim + crotchet in bars 1, 2, 5, 9).** It is plain, but it is the trio's hymn character, and since c001 four theme changes (c061, c081, c093, c121) have given each phrase its own feature. Another change would cost more than it gains, and the finale thread would have to be told for nothing. Kept.
