## Findings

### 1. The second idea's cadence (54–55, 130–131, 286–287) breaks the hemiola it was written to make. APPLIED (theme change)

**Problem.** For eight bars (45–52) the second idea moves in 3/2 minims with ties over the bar line. That pattern is its identity and the cell a listener can keep (PDF 51–52, Ex. 3-7; PDF 52: rhythmic groups are part of the design). Bar 53 returns to 3/4 crotchets (D6–B5–G5), which is right: it re-establishes the bar so that a hemiola can be heard (c261). But 54–55 then go `a''2 fis''4 | a''4 g''2`. The A5 is struck again on the downbeat of 55 as an appoggiatura, so the strongest attack falls on the 3/4 downbeat. The tune is therefore heard as two bars of 3/4, not as one bar of 3/2. The only 3/2 left in the cadence is the slur. Since the clarinet's minims became a held C5, no instrument plays the cadential hemiola that c002/c085 protected. The theme also ends without its own tied cell, and the top line closes 2–7–2–1 at a pitch level an octave and a half under the A6 peak, with nothing to remember.

**Evidence.** Ex. 3-8 (PDF 51–52): Mozart's hemiola is three equal groups of two beats across two bars, without a fresh attack on the second downbeat. PDF 51 (Ex. 3-6): the tie from beat 3 over the next downbeat is what makes the counter-metre audible. PDF 55 (Ex. 3-16, 3-17a, 3-18): a tone held over the strong beat becomes a suspension that delays a chord tone. Dvořák 9/iii Ob 1 bars 50–55 (above): his cadential descent in the same key and metre is exactly minim, crotchet tied | crotchet, minim. The score's own build 101–104 (Vln I `b''2 a''4~ | a''4 …`) already uses the shape.

**Fix (in /tmp/critic-441/work).** The tune descends 3–2–1 in three minims, with the 2 tied over the bar line as a 9–8 suspension over the tonic. It joins Cl 1's (and the horns') 4–3, as in Ex. 3-18.
- G major, sA.ily, bars 54–55, all octaves (Fl 1, Vln I; Ob 1, Vln II):
  - old `a''2 fis''4 | a''4 g''2`
  - new `b''2 a''4~ | a''4 g''2`
  - Vln I/II slurs: `d'''4 b''4( g''4) | b''2 a''4~ | a''4 g''2`, one bow per minim unit (c085, c418).
- E minor, sC.ily 130–131 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II) and dA.ily 286–287 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Cl 2, Hn 2, Vc):
  - old `fis''2 dis''4 | fis''4 e''2`
  - new `g''2 fis''4~ | fis''4 e''2`

The B (G6 in E minor) on the downbeat of 54/130/286 is the cadential 6/4's sixth, held while the bass stays on V. The rule is that the note of resolution does not sound under the sixth (A&S PDF 54–55 on accented dissonance; Unit 27's added sixth replaces the fifth). So the inner voices that held the fifth or the seventh on that downbeat now enter on beat 3 with the tune's step:
- sA: Cl 1 `b'2 c''4~ | c''4 b'2` (was `c''2.~ | c''4 b'2`); Hn 1 `b'2-- a'4--` at 54 (was `a'2.--`).
- sC: Cl 1 `g'2 a'4~ | a'4 g'2`; Hn 3 `g'2-- a'4--~ | a'4 g'2--`; Hn 2 `b2-- fis'4--~ | fis'4 e'2--`; Vla `dis'2.` at 130 (was `dis'2 b4`), so that V7 keeps its third on beat 3.
- dA: Cl 1 and Hn 3 as in sC; Vla `dis'2.` at 286.

The suspensions now resolve together on beat 2 of 55/131/287: A→G with C→B, and F♯→E with A→G. The horns still restrike each downbeat they restruck before (54, 55, 130). Every chord is complete on beat 3. No C-major triad, no B–D–F–G♯, no new parallels.

**What the listener hears.** At 54 the second idea ends the way it began, with its tied cell, in three broad descending minims B–A–G that complete the 3/2 instead of breaking it. This happens in all three statements, at f. It is the change I judge most audible in this reading: it is in the tune, not in an inner voice, and it is heard three times.

### 2. Alternative tested and rejected: a consonant 5–2–1 close (`d'''2 a''4~ | a''4 g''2`)

It avoids every inner-voice change, because D6 and B5 are chord roots. I rejected it for two reasons. It repeats bar 53's D6 at once, so the line circles and does not descend. And a fall of a fourth into the suspension is less singable than the stepwise 3–2–1 of Ex. 3-16's chain and of Dvořák's line. Not applied.

### 3. Dissonant ties inside 45–52 (c421's 9–8 at 47–48). Kept

PDF 50 gives dissonance as a built-in accent, and the second idea has none before its cadence. Finding 1 now adds a suspension at the cadence, which is where Ex. 3-16 to 3-18 put one. A 9–8 at 47–48 would replace the tie over the bar line with a held F♯ across beat 3 and weaken the 3/2 that c411 protected. No change.
