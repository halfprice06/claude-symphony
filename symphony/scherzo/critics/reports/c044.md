## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Coda 349-356: the last statement of T has a soft high flute over pianissimo violins. Applied.
**Bars 349-356.** Fl 1 doubles Vln I at pitch from C♯6 up to A6 (351) and G♯6 (354). It is marked p (c036), while Vln I and Vln II are pp. The tune in its last, quietest statement is led by the instrument that cannot play softly at that height. It is also the third time T is heard in the Vln I + Fl 1 unison (169, 209, 349).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 162-163 (Brahms 3/i last chord): a high flute at piano "always sounds louder than it should". The chord "would be easier to control if the strings had the top octave doubled by the winds at the octave below". PDF 164: "the violins ... are able to play softly on any note high or low". PDF 165: doubling at pitch loses the colour of both instruments. PDF 170-171: a recurring passage gets a new colour each time. PDF 184: the principal voice is marked louder than the subsidiary voices.
**Fix (coda.ily).** Fl 1 rests 349-356 (`R2.*8 | R2.*8 | R2.*4 | gis''2.\pp`). The manual "1." at 361 is removed because the combiner now prints it. Cl 1 doubles Vln II's lower octave, so the winds sit an octave below the strings' top, as Adler advises:
`codaClOne ... | R2.*4 | R2.*4 | cis''2(\pp\<^\markup \italic "dolce" dis''4 | e''4 gis''2) | a''4.(\> gis''8 fis''4) | cis''4( b'4 a'4) | gis'4\!( b'4 e''4 | gis''2 fis''8 e''8 | dis''2 fis''4) | e''2 r4 | R2.*4 | e'2.\pp ...`
The clarinet's highest note is A5 (written C6 on the A clarinet), below the register c041 avoided. Cl 2 rests, so the shared staff prints "1." with no dynamic conflict. The coloured statements now run Cl 1 (153), Vln I + Fl 1 (169, 209), Hn 1 (341), Ob 1 (345), and violins with clarinet (349). Fl 1 rests 20 bars before its pp G♯5 at 361.

### 2. Reprise 277-288: the third statement of the second idea is scored like the first two, so its crescendo cannot add colour. Applied.
**Bars 277-288.** At 45, 121 and 277 the melody is Vln I + Fl 1 at pitch, with Vln II + Ob 1 an octave lower, from the first note. c039 changed only the dynamics at 277 (sub. p, cresc. 281, f 285). The flute and oboe double a p violin line, and the crescendo cannot open out because every doubling is already there.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 172 (*Meistersinger*): Wagner "holds back 'the highs'" and builds "the crescendo as upper octaves gradually double the violins". PDF 162 (Schumann): instruments are held back to build a heavier chord in the next phrase. PDF 170-171: a recurring passage is coloured differently each time while its texture stays recognisable.
**Fix (dA.ily).** The violins play alone in octaves at sub. p (277-280). Ob 1 joins the lower octave with the crescendo (281: `| R2.*4 | c''2\p\<->( a'4~ |`). Fl 1 joins the upper octave at the f hemiola (285: `| R2.*8 | b''4\f( g''4 e''4 |`). The notes are unchanged. Clarinets, horns, violas and bass keep the harmony as before.

### 3. Bars 72 and 300: the fugato's ff A-major chord has an empty middle. Applied.
**Bars 72, 300.** The chord is E6 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I), C♯6 (Ob 2, Cl 1, Vln II), A5 (Fl 2), C♯5 (Hn 1), A4 (Hn 2, Cl 2, violas), A3 (Bsn 1), A2 (Bsn 2, Vc) and A1. The fifth E appears only at the top. Nothing sounds between A3 and A4, and the octave below the top E6 is empty. It is the climax of the development, and it decays into the F-major episode.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 164 (Brahms 3/i): "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave". PDF 164 (Mahler 1/iv): the upper octaves are "completely filled in", and only the lowest is left bare. PDF 161 (Schumann 1/i): non-divisi double and triple stops make the strings' chord ring. c024 applied the same idea at 339.
**Fix (sB.ily bar 72; dB.ily bar 300).** `sBVlnTwo`: `<e'' cis'''>2.->\ff\dnb\>`. `sBVla`: `<e' a'>2.->\ff\dnb\>`. `sBVc`: `<a, e>2.->\ff\dnb\>`. At 300 the same notes without `\>`. All three are easy stops: the cellos use the open A, the violas the open A, and Vln II plays in 4th position. The top and bass are unchanged, so the analyser's outer voices are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 336-339: Cl 2's seventh rises at the movement's final cadence. Applied.
**Bars 336-339.** Cl 2 holds the seventh A4 through the V7 (336-338) and moves up to B4 on the E-minor chord at 339. Tpt 1 resolves the same A4 down to G4. c029 cleared the doubled seventh from the brass but missed this one.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 161-162: in Schumann's tutti "all sevenths (E♭) resolve downward" and the voice leading is "absolutely according to the book", which is what makes a big chord progression sound clean.
**Fix (coda.ily).** `codaClTwo ... | a'2.\fff | a'2. | a'2. | g'4-> r4 r4 |` (was `b'4->`). G4 is already in Hn 1, Tpt 1 and the violas, and B is still in Vln II, Hn 4 and the violas.

### Checked, no change
