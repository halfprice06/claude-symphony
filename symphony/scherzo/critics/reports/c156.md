# Critic 156: playability against Adler's Appendix A range tables, second pass (v156)

Lens 6 (playability). Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, Appendix A (adler2 PDF 302–312). This covers three things: (1) a quick reference to the ranges of the most frequently used orchestral instruments, professional and nonprofessional; (2) instrument names in four languages; and (3) frequently used orchestral terms in four languages. I read the text of all eleven pages. I rendered the range charts (PDF 302–307) as images at 110 dpi, and PDF 302 and 305 again at 200–220 dpi, then read the notes from the images. The OCR of the charts is unusable. Pitches use C4 = middle C. The score is in C, so horn pitches are given as sounding, with the written F-horn pitch in brackets.

c066 did the first pass on this appendix (v66 → v67). Ninety cycles of changes have been made since then, so I checked the current score against every row of the tables again. I also used two parts of the tables that c066 used less: the nonprofessional column, and the footnote on what the professional ceilings mean.

## What else I studied

- **Per-player MIDI.** I built one MIDI track for each of the 29 players from the v156 sources, without part-combining (/tmp/critic-156/scan/players.ly, ana.py, tess.py). For each player I listed the range, the bars of the top note, the three longest stretches without a crotchet's rest (in seconds, using the score's tempo map), and the time spent within a fourth of Adler's professional ceiling. Only two players spend any time there: Fl 1 (8.7 s, spread over single peaks) and Hn 1 (9.7 s, bars 72–73, 110–115, 213–220, 281–282, 300, 310–315, 337). Every other player stays at least a fourth below Adler's ceiling throughout.
- **String double stops** (chords.py). Every chord in Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc can be played on adjacent strings. None needs two notes on one string.
- **Pizz./arco changes** (pizz.py). Every pizz.→arco change has at least four beats. The arco→pizz. changes at 204 (Cb) and 224 (Vc, Cb) have one beat each, after an up-bow (c096). That is enough time, because putting the bow down to pluck is quicker than the reverse.
- **Harp pedals.** I traced each change against the next note on that string: the F-major setting at 73, the trio setting at 141, C♮ at 188, C♯ A♯ at 190, A♮ at 209, C♮ at 234, and the coda setting at 333. Every change falls on a rest for the string concerned, and each section uses only the pitches its setting allows.
- **Timpani.** The pitch of every stroke, bar by bar, matches the drums as tuned (see row 18).
- **Adler part 1, horn chapter** (adler1 PDF 303–304): playing high all the time is taxing, and periodic rests are needed. Horns I and III are the high horns. HISTORY.md: c046 and c109 at 121–132.

## Findings

### 1. Reprise 277–288: Hn 1 holds the high sustained line that the exposition gives to Hn 3, while Hn 3 rests for 48 bars. Applied.

**Problem.** In the exposition, c046 moved the held line of the second idea (121–132) from Hn 1 to Hn 3. Hn 1 had just played the 16-bar ff tutti, and Adler asks for periodic rests in the high register (adler1 PDF 303). c109 then put 121–128 an octave lower, B3/C4, because a held horn B4/C5 sat at the unison on the first note of the second idea's lower octave (Vln II) and covered it.

In the reprise the order is reversed: the second idea (277–288) comes before the tutti (305–320). Here the line is still in Hn 1, and still in the upper octave: B4 for four bars at sub. p, C5 for two bars with a crescendo, then B4 A4 G4 at f. That is written F♯5/G5 held soft. As a result:

- Hn 1 plays 273–288, rests ten bars, then plays 299–320 (motive x, the hemiola build and the 16-bar tutti on written F♯5/G5 ff), and 332–339 (B4 held from p to fff). Of the 9.7 s that Hn 1 spends within a fourth of Adler's professional ceiling, 277–339 accounts for most.
- Hn 3 rests from 253 to 300.
- Adler's footnote (adler2 PDF 302) says the printed top notes are the ones "usually called for", which means for single notes, not for 30 bars of the first horn's part. Written F♯5–G5 is also at or above the nonprofessional ceiling of about G5 (PDF 305).
- c109's balance fault returns: at 277, Hn 1 B4 is in unison with Vln II's accented b'2 (the melody's lower octave). At 281, Hn 1 C5 is in unison with Vln II and Ob 1 on c''2.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-156/work/sections/dA.ily).** I followed the exposition's scoring.

- Hn 1 ends after its pp swell at 273–276.
- Hn 3 takes the line. At 277–280 it holds B3 [written F♯4], which is below Hn 2's G4–F♯4 and fills E minor.
- Hn 3 rests at 281–284. It does not take C4 there, because Hn 2 starts its tenor tune on C4 at 281 (c071) and a held C4 would cover it in the same way.
- Hn 3 enters f on B4–A4–G4 at 285–288, as at 129–132.

```

dAHnThree = { R2.*24 | b2.--(\p | b2.-- | b2.-- | b2.--) | R2.*4 | b'2.--(\f | a'2.--~ | a'4 g'2--) | g'4-. r4 r4 | }
```

Hn 3 now plays 277–288, rests 289–300, and plays 301–320, which is a lighter load than in the exposition, where it plays 101–132 without a break. Hn 1 rests from 277 to 298. Its next high passage, 299–320, now follows 22 bars of rest instead of a soft high held line.

This does not undo c046, c071, c109 or c136. Hn 2's tenor tune and its tenuto at 281 are unchanged. The themecheck reports 41/41 unchanged, because the reprise's held horn is not a recorded statement.

### 2. Bars 193–209: Cl 2 plays 17 bars without a rest. Applied.

**Problem.** Cl 2 holds dotted minims from 193 (p<, f at 197) through 204 and goes straight into its slurred phrase at 205–208 and the G♯ at 209. That is about 13 s at the trio tempo with no planned breath. c066 fixed the same fault two staves lower (Bsn 1, 197–216). Adler places a woodwind breath at a phrase division (adler1 PDF 176), and bar 204 is one: the held D♯4 ends and a new slurred phrase starts at 205. Hn 3 holds the same D♯4 through 204, so a short rest in the clarinet loses nothing.

**Fix (applied in tB.ily).** `dis'2.\> | dis'2. | gis'2(\p a'4` becomes `dis'2.\> | dis'2 r4 | gis'2(\p a'4`. Cl 1 rests in this bar, so the shared staff prints nothing new.

### Points checked and left unchanged

- **Hn 1 C♯5 [G♯5] at 213–215 and 220** (trio return), and at 72/300. This is above Adler's nonprofessional top of about G5 but well under the professional C6. It is reached by step, at p in the trio, and Hn 1 rests 193–208 beforehand. For a professional first horn this is normal, and the thirds with Hn 2 are protected (c004, c094). No change.
- **Fl 1 and Vln I B6 at 336.** Both are one step under the professional ceiling, appear once, and are approached by step (c126, c016). This is exactly the use the footnote on PDF 302 describes. No change.
- **Cl 2 sounding C♯3 at 88** is written E3, the A clarinet's lowest note (PDF 304). The analyser flags it only because it assumes the B♭ clarinet's D3. No change.
- **Timpani, harp, double stops, pizz./arco.** No new fault (see above).
