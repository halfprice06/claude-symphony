## Findings

### 1. Bars 87–89: the poco rit. stops the music on vii°7/V and announces V, where the book says V needs no emphasis (applied)

- **Bars.** 87–88 "poco rit." (\mt 252, 240) and 89 "a tempo (ritmo di tre battute)".
- **Problem.**
  - The F-major episode ends on D♯°7/A (87) and A♯°7 (88). Cl 1 falls to E4, the bass clarinet rises A2–A♯2–B2, and at 89 the violas begin theme A's head on that same E4 over the B pedal.
  - The rit. slows the two active chords that lead to V. The double bar then restarts the tempo on the dominant. So the arrival of V in the bass is the most marked moment of the development, and the only internal slowing in the scherzo proper falls exactly there.
  - What follows is twelve quiet bars on V, then the f tutti hemiola at 101. The emphasis therefore comes twice: once by a tempo seam at 89, and again by dynamics and scoring at 101.
  - Between the ff at 72 and the f at 101 there are 28 soft bars (73–100). The rit. is the place where the development loses its motion.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 234 (Ex. 15-5): in III–V–I, V may appear in a weak position, because "the force of dominant harmony is so great that it does not always require special emphasis through duration or accent." Part 1's large progression is III (37–56) → V (89) → I (105).
  - A&S PDF 234–235 (Ex. 15-6, Brahms Op. 119/3): V7 is reached "by almost imperceptible degrees", so the listener is hardly aware of the bass arrival "until the sforzando and the rush of quick notes bring the V7 forcefully to our attention". The image shows bar 44 reached under p dolce with no change of tempo. Here 101 is that forceful moment, and 89 should be the imperceptible one.
  - Beethoven 9/ii, the score's own model for 89–100 (c019), starts its "Ritmo di tre battute" in tempo.
  - The recorded reason for keeping the rit. was a list item ("keep it, including the poco rit.", c013). c144 described the dark wind colour of 87–88, which this change does not touch.
- **Fix (applied).** In global.ily:
  ```
  s2.*2 \break s2.*12 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco rit." \mt 252 s2. \mt 240 s2.
  →  s2.*2 \break s2.*14
  \tempo \markup { "a tempo" \medium \italic "(ritmo di tre battute)" } \mt 264
  →  \tempo \markup \medium \italic "ritmo di tre battute" \mt 264
  ```
  I added a c533 comment. The text "ritmo di tre battute" now matches the "ritmo di quattro battute" at 101. No printed tempo word was added. Two `\mt` steps were removed, and the \mt 264 at 89 stays, which is harmless.
- **Effect.**
  - The Cl 1 E4 at 88 passes to the violas' E4 at 89 in tempo, and the bass clarinet's A–A♯–B arrives on the beat.
  - The colour change at 89 (winds out, pizz. strings pp) still marks the section, as c054 wanted.
  - sB is 0.1 s shorter. The proportions are unchanged (part 1 96.9 s).
- **Not changed.** The rits at 149–152 and 237–240 lead into real changes of tempo (Trio, accelerando), so they stay. The reprise has no rit. here and gets none.

### 2. Bar 214: VII5/3 of V for one beat (checked, kept)

The chord on beat 1 is A♯–C♯–E over A♯1, after IV at 213. PDF 243–244 prefers V6/5, but it allows VII5/3 as an incomplete V6/5 (Ex. 15-17) and prefers the third doubled (Ex. 15-18). Here C♯ is in five parts (Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1, Hn 1, Vc/Bsn), and the root F♯ enters on beat 2 in the melody and the violas. This is the book's case. It adds a new reason to c263's and c353's.

### 3. Bar 314, beat 2½ (checked, kept)

The scan finds G–B–D♯ over G for one quaver, after the violas' staccato E4. It is the theme's lower neighbour D♯ over i6, inside a rising bass F♯–G–A–B. It is not a mediant (PDF 240), and E sounds on beats 1–2 and 3 (c353). Kept.

### 4. Bars 273–277: ♮VII7 → V6/5 → I (checked, kept)

This is Ex. 15-14. Bsn 2 carries D3–D♯3 in one line. The cellos rest at 276 to change to arco (c085). Giving them D♯3 would remove that change time. Kept.

### 5. III in small contexts at 162/218/350 and 230 (tested, rejected)

### 6. Link 133–152 and the reprise without a major III (checked, kept)
