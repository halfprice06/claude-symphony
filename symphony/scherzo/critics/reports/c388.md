## Findings

### 1. Bars 73–89: in the F-major episode, the bassoon and bass clarinet re-tongue the chord the horns hold legato (applied)

- **Problem.** No strings play in the F-major episode. The harp has the theme head over a four-part wind chord: bass clarinet (bass), Hn 4, Hn 3 and Bsn 1 (top), all pp (c047, c080). Since c088 the two horns are slurred in four phrase groups (73–76, 77–80, 81–84, 85–89) with tenuto on each bar, which marks soft ("da") tonguing. The bassoon and bass clarinet hold the same chord as unmarked dotted minims. That means a plain tongued attack each bar, and on the same pitch several times: Bsn 1 A3 at 73–74, 77–78 and 84–87 (four attacks on one A3 under the poco rit.), bass clarinet F2 at 73–74 and G2 at 75–76. One soft chord is therefore notated in two articulations. The two outer voices, which carry the chord's top and bottom, pulse each bar under the harp's legato melody, while the inner voices are joined.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 321: "all slurred notes are performed on one breath, while all separate notes are tongued." PDF 309: hard and soft tonguing "should make a distinct difference" and must be told apart in the notation (Ex. X-24, soft-tongued under slurs; X-25, hard-tongued with dots). Ex. X-15 and X-16 (PDF 306–307): soft held harmony is phrased under slurs. PDF 311–312: a soft wind choir under a solo works as an "accompaniment choir" when it sounds as one body. Dvořák 9/iii ties or slurs its soft held wind chords (above). c288 Finding 3 applied the same rule to the soft wind pads at 133–136, 141–148 and 225–228. This is the same case, and it is the one where the horns sit inside the chord.
- **Fix (applied), sections/sB.ily.** These are the horns' slur groups, with tenuto, and the dynamics are unchanged. The bassoon slurs are turned down: with them above the staff, page 3 was compressed by 1.8 staff spaces.
  - `sBBsnOne … \partCombineSoloI \slurDown a2.--(\pp | a2.-- | d'2.-- -\sysLabel "1." | bes2.--) | a2.--( | a2.-- | bes2.-- | a2) r4 | a2.--( | bes2-- d'4--~ | d'2. | a2.--) | a2.--( | a2.-- | a2.-- | g2.~ | g2) r4 \slurNeutral | \partCombineAutomatic …`

### 2. Bars 3–6: the horn call, the movement's first solo, has no character word (applied)

- **Problem.** Hn 1 opens the movement alone. It plays the G♯4 pivot (1–2), then movement I's call G♯3–E4–D♯4 under one slur, pp< p> (c088). Every other exposed horn solo in the score has a word: 205 "espress.", 341 "dolce", and 357 "espress." on the motto tail, the other motto quotation in Hn 1. The call, which the cyclic plan makes the movement's identity, is the only horn solo without one. Without a word, pp reads as "quiet" only. c188 and c288 marked this row "follows" because the slur and hairpins are there, but the row's examples all have a word as well.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 304–305 and 309. Each solo-horn example joins its dynamic to a character word: X-8 Brahms "f sempre e passionato", X-9 Beethoven "p dolce", X-11 Ravel "pp" then "p > pp expressif", X-12 Strauss "ff molto espr.", X-24 Tchaikovsky "dolce con molto espr.". X-11 is the closest case: a soft horn solo made expressive by the word, not by a louder dynamic.
- **Decision.** I chose "espress." over "dolce". The call quotes the motto, as the tail at 357 does, and that statement is "espress." The word asks the player to sing through the rising sixth G♯3–E4 at pp, as Ravel's "expressif" does, without raising the dynamic. The movement keeps "dolce" for its trio material (153, 341).
- **Fix (applied), sections/intro.ily.** `introHnOne = { gis'2.~\pp | gis'2. | gis2.(\<^\markup \italic "espress." | e'2.\p\> | dis'2.~ | dis'2)\! r4 | R2.*6 | }`. It prints above the staff at bar 3, clear of the tempo mark (checked on page 1). The notes are unchanged, so movement I's thread and the finale need not be told. Bars 1–7 are protected for their notes and the G♯ pivot, and this changes neither.
