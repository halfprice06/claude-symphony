## Findings

### 1. Bars 329–332: the motto's chromatic tail is lost inside the string ostinato (applied)

**Problem.** In the coda build the four string sections play motive x in one rhythm, staccato, mf< (Vln I G5–A5–F6–D♯6 on top, over the bass E–D–C–B). Above them the long-note element of the passage is Cl 1's chromatic tail G5–F♯5–F5–E5–D♯5, p<. It is the motto's tail, the symphony's unifying line (the finale quotes the F of 331), and it leads straight into theme A's own tail at 334. At 329–330 it is a single clarinet sitting on pitches the violins strike: G5 is Vln I's note on beats 1 and 3 of 329, and F♯5 at 330 is Vln I's quaver and Vln II's beats 1 and 3. A held unison under staccato attacks on the same pitch sounds like the strings' own resonance, not like a line. Hn 3 doubles the tail only from 331. Between Cl 1 (G5) and Cl 2 (E4) the wind chord has a gap of a tenth, and no string plays there either (Vln II B4–E5, violas B3 and F♯3).

**Evidence.**
- PDF 176: "each theme must be heard at good strength"; Mozart gives each element "a strong and sympathetic color combination". Mozart's cantus firmus is the element he doubles in winds.
- PDF 175: the Jupiter cantus firmus is "a unifying element for the entire symphony". The motto tail has the same role here.
- PDF 184 (Schoenberg): the oboes and Cl 1 "greatly help to delineate the Hauptstimme, which would otherwise be made subservient" to a prominent combination. Schoenberg "assign[s] each fragment to an instrument that will be clearly heard".
- PDF 179: "doubling ... of a mixed color" for a big sound "whether the dynamic be forte or piano"; PDF 184: doublings at the octave.
- PDF 184 (Bloch): as the climax nears, add an instrument to each element. With the change, the tail goes Cl 1 + C.a. at 329, then + Hn 3 at 331.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 100 (c129's rule): wind harmony in close order with no empty spaces. The C.a.'s G4, F♯4 and F4 fill the tenth between the clarinets.

**What changes.** The cor anglais doubles Cl 1 an octave lower, G4–F♯4–F4–E4–D♯4, p<, entering with the other woodwinds at 329 (c050). It then goes on from D♯4 to its own E4 at 333, where it already plays theme A's lower octave. The tail now lies in the empty register between violas and Vln II, in two wind colours. The build keeps its order (c393, Rimsky PDF 130): strings from 325, woodwinds at 329, horns at 331, trumpets and low brass at 332, and flutes and oboes held back to 333 (PDF 172). Harmony is unchanged. The C.a. is in tenths over the bass, unison with Hn 3 at 331–332, and makes no new octaves or fifths. The French sixth on beat 3 of 331 gains an E4, which Cl 1 already has an octave up. No C–E–G sonority is formed.

**Fix (sections/coda.ily, codaCorAnglais, applied):**
```
old: codaCorAnglais = { R2.*8 | e'4-.\ff b8-. b-. e'4-. | …
new: codaCorAnglais = { R2.*4 | g'2.\p\< | fis'2. | f'2 e'4 | dis'2. | e'4-.\ff b8-. b-. e'4-. | …
```
No slur, to match Cl 1's line on the shared clarinet staff. The C.a. rests 324–328 (five bars), so it needs no cue. No cue in parts-cues.json points at the C.a., and nothing was silenced, so every cue still points at a sounding part. The C.a. part PDF needs regenerating with the next parts build.

### 2. Bars 329–330: Hn 3 on the tail instead of the cor anglais (considered, not built, rejected)

A horn would be the stronger colour in that register, and Meistersinger's rule (PDF 178) asks for a brass instrument in the mix. But a horn at 329 moves the brass entry two bars ahead of c039/c393's plan (horns at 331, the rest of the brass at 332). The shared Hn 3·4 staff would also need two different dynamics at 331, where Hn 4 enters pp under a horn already in crescendo. The cor anglais does the job inside the woodwind entry, and the horn still joins the tail at 331. Rejected.

### 3. Bars 325–328: Mozart's coloured cantus-firmus entries (checked, kept)

In mm. 372–388 (PDF 176) Mozart's entries go cellos, violas, Vln II, Vln I, the same order as this coda's motive x. Each is coloured by winds. Here the four entries are strings alone, pp. I kept them. Mozart colours the long-note element, and in this coda the long-note element is the tail, which Finding 1 now colours. A wind on each x entry would bring the oboe and flute in before 333 and break the "held-back highs" of PDF 172. It would also spoil c393's order. It would repeat c337's wind shadows of the reprise fugato as well.

### 4. Bars 253–268: the reprise countermelody has no woodwind (checked, kept)

PDF 178 asks for a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument in each element. The cellos' countermelody has Hn 3 from 261 but no woodwind. Wagner's rule describes a forte combination. Here the passage is pp, the woodwind colour is in the theme (Fl 1's heterophony), and the countermelody's growth phrase by phrase (Vc, then Vc + Hn 3) is a recorded protection. A bassoon would thicken it without a gain a listener would hear. Kept.

### 5. Bars 289–296: a third element in the reprise fugato (checked, rejected)

The Jupiter rule (PDF 172) would favour a combination here as well. I worked out, beat by beat but without building it, the second idea's head in Bsn 1 (A2–G2–F2~ | F2–D3, in place of its pointillist downbeats). With no bass under it (the basses rest), its A and F sound as the bass of 6/4 chords at 293–294. At 65–66 the cellos' quote has the basses' A pedal under it. Adding a bass pedal as well would copy 65–68 into the shortened reprise, which c417 rejected. Rejected.

### 6. Bars 95–100: violas and Vln II share crotchets under the head (checked, kept)

PDF 179's middleground is itself a staccato figure, and three critics have weighed the held-B alternative (c407, c437, c507). The reading gives no new reason. Kept.

### 7. Bars 57–72: fugato and stretto (checked, kept)

Three elements at 65–68, each with its own rhythm, articulation and level (p / pp / pp espress.), as in Ex. XV-26. The bass is independent (PDF 176). The stretto adds one colour per entry and stops on the ff chord (PDF 176, 181). The strings-only exposition also has PDF 176's "keep an established color intact" behind it. Kept.
