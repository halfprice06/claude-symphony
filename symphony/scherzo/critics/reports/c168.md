## What else I studied

I wrote a parser for the section files that lists every dynamic (printed or hidden), hairpin, articulation and text by bar and instrument. With it I checked four things: entries after a bar or more of rest without a dynamic (none outside the call-and-response gaps critic 68 already accepted), hairpins without a printed target, articulation on doubled lines, and bowing signs against stroke counts. For support I used Adler part 2 PDF 184 (Schoenberg marks the principal voice louder than the subordinate ones and prints those dynamics), Adler part 2 PDF 294 (dynamics go below the staff; tempo marks at the top and between choirs), and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 133 (the harmonic basis "should be of inferior dynamic power"). I tested each fix in /tmp/critic-168/work, and I tested four other versions of finding 1 for the page count.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Trio return 217–221: the tune's peak has no printed level, but the accompaniment's does (applied, with one exception)
Problem: T's third phrase rises to A6 at 218–219. Vln I and Vln II (f at 209) and Cl 1 (f) have a hairpin to 219 and back, and Fl 1 (mf) has the same. The peak (ff) and the return (f at 221) are hidden: c138 added them for the MIDI only. Underneath, Vla, Vc, Cb and both bassoons print `f` at 219 and `mf` at 221, Cbsn prints mf/mp, and Hn 1·2 print mf/p. So at 219 a conductor sees the accompaniment marked f and the tune marked with nothing but a hairpin. Read literally, the tune and its accompaniment meet at the same level at the movement's lyrical climax.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10) ends each hairpin at a printed level (pp < mp; mf > p). Ex. 6 (PDF 11) gives every staff its own dynamic. Adler II PDF 184 says principal voices should be marked louder than subordinate ones. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 133 says the harmonic basis should be of inferior dynamic power. This is the only place in the movement where the accompaniment prints a target and the tune does not. In the soft statements (161–163, 177–182, 349–355) nobody prints one, and an untargeted swell there is normal practice.
Fix (applied, tC.ily): print the hidden targets that are already in the MIDI:
- `tCVlnOne`: `a'''4.\ff\> gis'''8 fis'''4` (was `a'''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \ff\>`), `gis''4\f( b''4 e'''4\<~` (was hidden f)
- `tCVlnTwo`: `a''4.\ff\>`, `gis'4\f(`
- `tCFluteOne`: `a'''4.\f\>`, `gis''4\mf(` (the flute stays a level under the violins, c069)

The short swell at 222 stays hidden. A one-beat < > needs no printed target.
Exception: I also printed the targets in Cl 1 (`tCClOne` `a''4.\ff\>`, `gis'4\f(`). That build ran to 14 pages: the extra marks on the shared clarinet staff push the 213–232 system onto a page of its own. Printing the ff alone, or moving the marks above the staff, still gave 14 and 15 pages. I left Cl 1's two targets hidden. Cl 1 doubles Vln II at the unison, and Vln II now shows the level. With Vln I, Vln II and Fl 1 printed, the build is 13 pages. The MIDI does not change.

### 2. Bars 207–208: Hn 3·4 crescendo into Hn 1·2's p, with no target (applied)
Problem: Hn 3·4 hold D♯4/B3 pp under the motto-tail anticipation and crescendo through 207–208 with no end level (the hairpin was closed by `\!` on the rest at 209). At 209 Hn 1·2 take over the horn chord at p. The player cannot tell how far the crescendo goes. The MIDI plays the untargeted hairpin as a jump (c138's note), so the horn chord swells and then drops back at the hand-over.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10), pp < mp: a soft crescendo with a printed target. PDF 8: read the music through the player's eyes. Hn 3·4 cannot see Hn 1·2's p in their parts.
Fix (applied): tB.ily `tBHnThree` `dis'2.--\< | dis'2.--\p)` and `tBHnFour` `b2.--\< | b2.--\p)`. Both players have the same dynamic on the shared staff. tC.ily `tCHnThree`/`tCHnFour` start `{ R2. |` (the stray `\!` removed). The horns now hand over at p, as the strings build to mf.

### 3. Coda 329–331: the bass clarinet's crescendo has no target, and the line drops back at the hand-over (applied)
Problem: the bass rises E–D–C–B into the ff at 333. B.cl has E and D (329–330) at `\p\<` and stops at 331 with `\!` on the rest. Cbsn takes over the C at 331 at `p\<`. So the woodwind bass crescendos for two bars, then the next player of the same line starts again at p. Cb and Vc are at mf< at the same point.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): a hairpin into a printed level. Ex. 6 (PDF 11): a doubled line carries the same marks. c150's rule that an added colour enters soft (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127) does not apply here, because the contrabassoon is not added to the line: it continues it.
Fix (applied, coda.ily): `codaBassCl` `e,2.\p\< | d,2.\mp | R2.` and `codaCbsn` `c,2.\mp\< | b,,2.\f`.

### 4. Bar 128: Hn 4's crescendo target is hidden (applied)
Problem: at 125–128 Hn 4 crescendos from sub. p and stops at 129, where Hn 3 enters f on the same staff. The level Hn 4 reaches (mf) is hidden. In the Hn 4 part the hairpin runs into a rest with no level, and in the score it looks like a crescendo to Hn 3's f.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10). PDF 8 warns that parts extracted from a score do not automatically carry the right markings.
Fix (applied): sC.ily `sCHnFour` `… b2.-- | b2.--\mf)` (was `b2.--  -\tweak stencil ##f \mf)`).

### 5. Bar 334: Ob 1 lacks the accent that every other part on the tail has (applied)
Problem: at 334 Ob 1 and Ob 2 play the tail G5–F♯5–F5 in unison on the shared staff (c139). Ob 2, Fl 1, C.a., Cl 1 and all the strings have `g2->`. Ob 1 has `g''2(` with no accent. On the page the oboe staff shows an accent on one voice only.
Evidence: MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11): the sf is restated in every staff, and the parts in unison carry the same marks.
Fix (applied, coda.ily): `codaOboeOne` `g''2->( fis''8 f''8)`.
