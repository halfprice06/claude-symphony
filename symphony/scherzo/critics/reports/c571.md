### 1. 189–192: a solo cello for the answer to Ob 1's call (built, tested, not kept)

**Candidate.** The trio's middle opens with Ob 1's call, p espress., in C♯ minor (185–188). The answer comes in B (189–192): D♯4–E4–D♯4–C♯4–D♯4 | B3 F♯3 | G♯3 A3 B3 | F♯3, played by the whole cello section, dolce p, over the harp's high off-beats and pizz. basses. This is the most exposed string phrase in the trio. Adler PDF 102 offers the cello "as an occasional soloist in orchestral works" (Ex. III-63; Brahms PC 2/iii), and PDF 81 says the principals "must be of solo caliber". A single cello would answer a single oboe at equal weight. The forces would then grow in steps: one oboe, one cello, strings p< at 193, tutti f at 197. A listener would hear this change more clearly than any other this chapter supports, because one cello and ten cellos differ in tone.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-571/work, tB.ily `tBVc`: `dis'4(\p^\markup \italic "solo, dolce" …` at 189 and `fis,2.\p\<^\markup \italic "tutti"` at 193. Notes, slurs and dynamics were unchanged. The "Vc." cues at 189 in parts 07, 09, 26 and 28 (parts-cues.json) still point at a sounding cello. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, cmajor "none", the same single outer-voice parallel (69) as v571, and themecheck 52/52. Page 7 engraved correctly. The tested tB.ily and score.pdf are kept in /tmp/critic-571/candidate-solo-cello/.

**Why I did not keep it.**
- The chapter itself prefers the section here. PDF 99: "the best evidence of the singular beauty of the instrument's sound lies in … solo passages featuring the entire section". The answer lies in the A string's first position and drops to the D string for F♯3–A3, the warm part of the instrument (c211). c427 relied on that sound, the A string's "chest" quality (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 56), when it took the cor anglais off the answer so the cellos would stand alone.
- The movement's one solo string colour belongs to the coda. At 345–349 Ob 1's phrase passes to the solo violin, the same hand-off from a wind to a solo string. With a solo cello at 189 the coda would repeat an effect already used, not bring a new one. c438's plan carries T's last statement in single players (Hn 3, Ob 1, solo violin) because that colour is reserved for the close. Dvořák 9, the movement's main model, uses a solo string once in the whole symphony (the Largo) and never in its scherzo.
- The growth of forces already works with the section. One oboe, then ten cellos at p, then Vln I, violas, cellos and basses p< at 193, then tutti f at 197. A soloist would make the first step flat and the second abrupt.

Reverted. The scratch copy equals v571 and was rebuilt clean.

### 2. 169–176: the cellos' first statement of T is doubled by Hn 3 (no change)

PDF 103 lists "cello and horn" among the most used couplings. The point has been rejected four times (c513). Not re-raised.

### 3. Second idea in G, 45–52: top octave D6–A6 on the E string at f–ff (no change)

PDF 75 calls the high E string "quite forceful, even strident". Adler's own example for that string, Ex. III-8 (Don Juan, ff, F♯5–A6), uses exactly this register for a forte tune. Here the singing octave is Vln II's D5–A5, and the lowest octave has violas, cellos and cor anglais (Ex. III-39, c481). The soft statements at 121 and 281 give the tune the "clear and luminous" soft E string. Kept, as c301 and c481 decided.

### 4. Sul G (Ex. III-5): no candidate inside the string's range (no change)

On the page image, Tchaikovsky's sul G line stays within about A3–B4. I scanned every violin line for a lyrical phrase inside G3–A4 (c556's ninth). The only one is Vln II's G♯4–G4–E4–E4 at 357–360. It is an inner voice beside the cor anglais's 6–♭6–5 under Hn 1's motto tail. That passage is protected because it is shared with movements II and IV, and a string colour there would draw attention away from the horn. T at 197 and 209 goes to E5–F♯5, which rules it out as c481 and c556 found.

### 5. Theme A's B3 dips and open E5 (16, 32, 256, 272; every head) (no change)

PDF 73–74: open strings matter only in slow, expressive lines. Theme A is staccato at 2. = 88. The dips to B3 on the G string sit at the half cadences, where the G string's weight suits the low point of the phrase. Kept.

### 6. Violas' F5 in alto clef at 62 and 294 (engraving lens; not applied)

Ex. III-28 (PDF 85) shows treble clef for upper viola notes "to avoid ledger lines". The fugato's viola entry reaches F5, three ledger lines above the alto staff, for one minim. This is not a melodic fault. It belongs to the engraving lens (Gould, ledger lines), because a clef change for one bar has its own cost. Recorded only.

### Memorability verdict

- **Theme A.** Motive x and the chromatic tail make it the movement's signature, as Beethoven 9/ii's octave motive and Dvořák 9/iii's head are in theirs. A listener would hum it.
- **T.** The head G♯–A–B–E, the falling sixth, the tied A peak and the two suspensions make it singable, like the trio tunes of Dvořák 8/iii and Brahms 2/iii. Its string statements (cellos 169, Vln I 177 and 209, solo violin 349) each use a different string colour that this chapter describes.
- **The second idea.** It is still the least memorable of the three. Its scoring now matches Adler's lyrical layouts (III-8 top, III-39 bottom), and this chapter gives no reason to change its notes.
