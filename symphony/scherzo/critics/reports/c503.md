## Findings

### 1. 197–200: the forte call stands on two root-position bars of C♯ minor. A rising 6/3 pair i6–♭II6 gives the trio's middle a stepwise bass into A major. APPLIED

**Problem.** The forte call is the loudest point of the trio's middle and its turn back toward the return. In v503 the bass at 197–203 is C♯ (197), C♯ (198), F♯ (199, ♭II6 since c480), G♯ (200), A (201), A (202), B (203). Bar 198 repeats the root-position tonic under T's bar 2 and the call's C♯6–G♯5. By Unit 18 §1 a 5/3 sounds like a goal, so the bass arrives at 197 and then stands still for a bar. It then leaps a fourth down to the Neapolitan. Everything else at 197–200 is moving: T in Cl 1, Vln II and violas, the call in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I, Hn 1's C♯5 on beat 3 (c482).

**Evidence.**
- Unit 18 §1 (PDF 290–291): the 6/3's "more fluid, less stable sound" suits "passages of a transitional nature", and 5/3s sound like goals.
- §3 and Ex. 18-6f–j (PDF 293–294): 6/3 chords may also ascend.
- §2 (PDF 291): only the first and last chords of such a motion are articulated, and the chord before the cadence (Mendelssohn's II6) is stressed for its cadential role.
- Unit 28 §6 (PDF 485): "Using I6 … allows a stepwise ascent in the bass" into ♭II6. §2 (PDF 483): ♭II6 belongs at "climactic moments".
- With E under 198, the forte call has the book's progression i – i6 – ♭II6 – V. Two 6/3 chords lead from the articulated tonic at 197 to the articulated V at 200. The bass becomes C♯–E–F♯–G♯–A–A–B, a continuous rise from the call to the dominant before T's return at 209.
- Form: c463 reads the C♯ minor as an upper neighbour to the B area, not a key. A rising, passing bass fits that role better than a second root-position bar that confirms C♯ as a goal.
- §8 (PDF 299–300): E–G♯–B–C♯ on beats 1–2 of 198 is both i6/5 of C♯ minor and E with an added 6th, so the bar also points back toward E.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tB.ily, bar 198 only:
- tBBsnTwo: `cis2.\f | cis2. | fis,2.` → `cis2.\f | e,2. | fis,2.`
- tBVc: `cis2.\f | cis2. | fis,2.` → `cis2.\f | e,2. | fis,2.`

- tBHnTwo: `e'2.--(\mf | e'2.-- | fis'4--` → `e'2.--(\mf | e'2-- cis'4-- | fis'4--`

Hn 2 had to change. Held on E4, it would move E4→F♯4 in octaves with the new bass E→F♯. A first try with G♯4 made octaves with Bsn 1's G♯3→F♯3. E4–C♯4 (beat 3) avoids both. It moves in contrary motion against Hn 1's C♯5→A4 and still reaches F♯4 on 199, so c327's oblique approach to the D4 on beat 2 is kept.

### 2. 193–194: ii6/5 (bass A) under the string build. BUILT ON PAPER, NOT APPLIED

**Candidate.** The same reasoning applied at 194: bass F♯–A–B (ii – ii6/5 – cadential 6/4), with the violas taking F♯3 as a voice exchange (Ex. 18-7, PDF 294–295) to avoid octaves A3–B3 with the bass. Finding 1 starts a rising bass at 197. Together the two changes would give F♯–A–B–B–C♯–E–F♯–G♯–A from 193 to 201.

**Why not.** 193–196 is the third statement of c302's protected sequence (call 185, diatonic answer 189, then 193). Each statement begins with two bars on one bass note (C♯ C♯, B B, F♯ F♯), and that is what lets the ear hear 193 as a continuation of the sequence. A&S PDF 265 says a sequence works through "two plus the start of a third" statements. Moving the bass at 194 would cut the third statement's start to one bar. Bar 198 has no such reason: it belongs to the forte call, which is not a sequence statement. A second rising bass so close would also make the device obvious. **Decision:** no change.

### 3. 201–204: A – Am – B – B7 in root position. KEPT

A&S Ex. 18-12 would want iv6 over C at 202. But Vln I's C5→B4 (c070's ♭6–5 in its own register) would then move in octaves with a bass C→B, as c323 found. Bsn 1's chromatic C♯4–C4–B3–A3 already gives the passage the line Unit 18 asks for. **Decision:** no change.

### 4. 237–240: a 5–6–5 over the trio's last E chord, mirroring c413's 146. KEPT

A C♯ at 238 would follow the C♮ of 235 (iv in the codetta). That breaks c492's do-not-touch rule (♭6 never before ♮6, A&S PDF 388). The plain E chord here is the trio's goal, and §1 makes a 5/3 the right sound for a goal. **Decision:** no change.

### 5. Proportions, the transitions and the two-stage coda. KEPT

Scherzo 87 s, trio 70 s, reprise 49 s, coda 44 s. The sections shorten toward the end. The reprise leaves out the F-major episode, and the slow coda stage holds the cyclic close. The pedal joins 89–104, 131–152 and 225–252 are articulated only at their two ends (PDF 291). Their motion comes from a line (c233, c293) and, since c413, from the 5–6–5 at 146. Finding 1 adds motion inside the trio without changing any length. At about 4:38 the movement remains a light middle weight between the Adagio and the finale. **Decision:** no change.
