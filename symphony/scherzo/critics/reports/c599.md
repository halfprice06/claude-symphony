## Findings

### 1. Bars 141–149: the link has no high pedal. APPLIED

**Problem.**
- The link is the intro in reverse (link.ily header; c547): Hn 1 holds G4 at 141–144 and G♯4 at 145–149 over a pp wind pad of E2 (B. cl.), B3 (Bsn 2) and E4 (Bsn 1).
- Since c499, the intro has a Vln I pedal on G♯5 above the horn call. That is Fig. 6.1's layout.
- The link has nothing above Hn 1's G4. For eleven bars (from 140) the movement sounds only below G4, and every string is silent except two pizz. notes.
- This is where the movement turns from E minor to E major and moves away into the trio. Of all the bars in this score, these are where Fig. 6.1's lesson applies most directly: a static high string pedal holds while the layers below it change. Distance comes from colour, not only from level.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 272 (Fig. 6.1): strings hold the pedal pp, with the top octave in harmonics.
- PDF 272–273: distance takes off the high partials, so the far sound is a change of timbre. A natural harmonic is the string tone with the fewest upper partials.
- PDF 280: the pad is a sustained string layer.
- PDF 271–272: the pad is marked a level under the line (ppp under Hn 1's pp).
- Dvořák's own link to the trio (bars 160–175) holds pp/ppp string notes under its wind fragments. The master scherzo this movement models does the same thing.

**Fix (applied, sections/link.ily, new variable):**
```
linkVlnOne = { e'''2.~-\flageolet\ppp^\markup \italic "harm., change bow freely" | e'''2.~ | e'''2.~ | e'''2.~ | e'''2.~ | e'''2.~ | e'''2.~\> | e'''2.~ | e'''4\! r4 r4 | R2.*3 | }
```

**Design.**
- E6 is the octave harmonic of the open E string, the safest harmonic for a tutti section.
- E is the root and is common to E minor and E major. The mode change therefore stays Hn 1's G → G♯ alone (c547).
- The pad avoids G♯, so the Hn 1 + Vln I G♯ pair stays the frame's alone (c510).
- It ends on beat 1 of 149 together with Hn 1, as the harp's harmonics enter on beat 2. One flageolet colour hands over to the other with no seam (c340, c476).
- The trio frame 153–168 is untouched (harp, drone and basses alone). Vln II's con sord. entry at 168 is still the strings' first sound in the trio.

**Protections and parts.**
- No do-not-touch item covers 141–148 (c547, c413 and c498 are unchanged).
- No pitch was added to the harmony: E is already in three wind parts.
- Vln I now plays 141–149 after resting 133–140 (eight bars, so no cue is needed). It then rests until 177, and its existing Vc cue at 173 still points at the cellos' T.
- No cue in parts-cues.json points at Vln I between 133 and 176, so no cue loses its source.
- I built the parts in the scratch copy: rc 0, 0 warnings, and the Vln I part is still 3 pages.

**Why this is the best candidate.** A listener hears it: a glassy high E appears over the dark wind chord, holds through the turn to major, and dissolves into the harp harmonics. Without it, the seam is the most bottom-heavy passage in the movement. The cost is that the strings are no longer silent through all of 140–167. The colour is a harmonic at ppp, not a bowed tone, and the strings' warm sound still first returns at 168.

### 2. Bars 205–208: a high pad over the motto-tail preview. Built in thought, NOT applied

Fig. 6.1 would argue for the same pad over Hn 1's preview B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4. I reject it.
- c540 moved everything off the top of these bars so that the preview has the register and colour of the real tail at 357–360. At 357–360, too, nothing sounds above Hn 1.
- A pad here would make the preview differ from its return. Casanelles PDF 276 makes the same point: each register and combination is a different sound.
- The reading therefore supports keeping 205–208 as it is.

### 3. Comparison with v589: nothing got worse

I diffed v589 against v599 and checked each change against the reading.
- **c589, timpani f at 91 and 94.** The drum's B2 shares a band with the cellos' pizz. B2 and the basses' B1, and at pp it was masked (PDF 277–278). In its own bars the f is the level that results, not an effort marking (PDF 271). Keep.
- **c590, Ob 2 in unison with Cl 1 on the tail at 329–332.** A unison makes one ensemble timbre (PDF 277). The oboe's spectrum leads it, which separates the tail from the violins' quavers in the same octave. It is also the finale's scoring at IV 273–275. Better.
- **c592, E♯ at 180.** It is a bass line in the basses and Bsn 2 only, and does not affect masking. Neutral or better.
- **c596, damping at 3–5, 131–132, 241 and 287–288.** These remove ringing under changed harmony (attack and decay, PDF 275–276, 279). Better.
- **c598, slurs at 49.** Print and articulation only. Neutral or better.
- The analysis still shows only the known parallel at 69. The theme record is 52/52 unchanged.

### 4. Bars 1–7 and 364–376: the frame pad in harmonics. NOT applied

Fig. 6.1 tops its pedal with harmonics. A G♯5 harmonic on the violin is possible only as an artificial harmonic (stop G♯3, touch C♯4).
- The sound would be colder than the 'sul A' tone that c526 chose.
- It would put a tutti section on a 6- to 13-bar artificial harmonic, which is risky for intonation at ppp.
- It would change the last sound, and the finale relies on that Vln I G♯5 at IV 1 and IV 243.

The stopped G♯5 stays. Finding 1 places the harmonic colour in the link instead, where nothing is relied on.

### 5. Holistic verdict against Dvořák 9/iii

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Mostly, yes.
- The proportions follow Dvořák's: a scherzo with a lyrical second idea, a soft trio, a shortened reprise and a cyclic coda. So do the timpani as a motivic voice and the plan of return.
- The coda's cyclic quotation works as Dvořák's does at bars 273–278. One horn carries movement I's material while the rest of the orchestra holds (357–360, c390).
- Dvořák ends ff while this movement ends ppp on G♯. That is the cyclic plan (the pivot into the finale), not a fault.
- The one place where v599 was thinner than both Dvořák and Mahler's model was the link into the trio. Dvořák carries his link on held soft strings; this one had none. Finding 1 addresses it.
