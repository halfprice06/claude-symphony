## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 169–176: Fl 1 shadows the violins' T at their pitch (applied)
**Problem.** At T's first string statement, Fl 1 plays T's own notes at Vln I's pitch (G♯5 upward) with fill-in quavers. It meets the violins on almost every beat 1 and beat 3 and rubs a second against them on beat 2: F♯5 against G♯5 at 169, C♯6 against B5 at 170, E6 against D♯6 at 171. Vln II doubles the tune an octave below as well. So the first time the strings sing the tune, three lines carry the same melody and one of them blurs its outline.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 211, Ex. 279: violins that "shadow the melody too closely, resulting in an almost constant parallel motion which distorts the aural focus". PDF 212, Ex. 280: the good background touches the solo's notes only briefly. PDF 209: holding back the added part keeps it "for emphasis in a later bar". In Dvořák 9/iii, 68–75, the woodwind's second line moves only while the tune holds.
**Fix.** Fl 1 rests 169–176, so the first two phrases are the violins alone. It enters at 177 with its existing line for the climb to A6, where the doubling now marks the climax (c036's p is kept).
```
tAFluteOne = { R2.*16 | R2.*8 | cis'''4(\p\<^\markup \bold "1." b''8 cis''' dis'''4) | e'''4( fis'''8 e''' gis'''4) | ... (177–184 unchanged)
```

### 2. Bars 233–236: Bsn 1's T head is buried and doubled by Hn 3 (applied)
**Problem.** In the codetta, T's head moves down from Ob 1 (225) to Cl 1 (229) to Bsn 1 (233). The bassoon plays G♯3–A3–B3–C4–B3 at pp. Hn 3·4 enter on the same bar, also at pp. Hn 3 holds B3–B3–A3–B3, so the bassoon's B3 at 234 and 236 (the head's arrival notes) are in unison with a horn. At 235 the horn's A3 sits a third under the bassoon's C4. This is the softest and lowest statement of the three, and the one most covered.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 212: the background supplies "chordal tones not present in either the foreground solo line or the bass line". PDF 219: do not set the accompaniment on top of a soloist's weaker register. c047's own aim was to set the bassoon *inside* the horns (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 108, 112: the woodwind one degree louder than the brass), and that aim is not met while the horn shares its notes.
**Fix.** Hn 3 holds E4 for 233–236 (an octave above Hn 4's E3, both pp). The bassoon now moves between the horns and shares no note with them. Bsn 1 is marked p, one degree above the horns, as the oboe (225) and clarinet (229) already are. Hn 3 returns to B3 at 237.
```
tCHnThree = { R2.\! | R2.*23 | e'2.\pp | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | b2.~ | ...
tCBsnOne:  ... gis2(\p a4 | b2. | c'2. | b2) r4 ...
```

### 3. Bars 172, 176, 212, 216: the cellos' counter-melody stops where T stops (applied)
**Problem.** The cello counter-melody (c007, c037) moves against T in most bars. At the end of each two-bar half-phrase, though, it holds a dotted minim exactly where T holds its minim: 172 (T A5–G♯5, cellos G♯3), 176 (T A♯5–B5, cellos D♯3), and again at 212 and 216. On beats 2–3 of those bars no line moves, except the violas' louré off-beats in tA. Each of T's phrases ends with a long note, so this is the one place where the second line should carry the music on.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 207: "keep something going … to carry one bar into the next with as much flow as possible". When the lead is static, the counter-melody supplies the motion (Ex. 269). PDF 208, Ex. 273: the cellos change "from half note to quarter note values in order to give extra push into the next phrase".
**Fix.** A crotchet on beat 3 that steps into the next bar. At 172 and 212: G♯3–F♯3 (passing) into E3. At 176: D♯3–F♯3 into E3. At 216: D♯3–B2, because tC's arco basses move B1–F♯2 on that beat and F♯3 would make octaves with them.
```
tAVc: ... fis2( a4 | gis2 fis4) | e4( cis2~ | cis4 a,2) | ais,2( b,4 | dis2 fis4) | ...
tCVc: ... fis2( a4 | gis2 fis4) | e4( cis2~ | cis4 a,2) | ais,2( b,4 | dis2 b,4) | ...
```

### 4. Bars 147–148: the link holds G♯ but does not announce T (applied)
**Problem.** The link 141–152 consists of held notes: Hn 1 G4 then G♯4, with clarinets, bass clarinet and bassoon on E and B. Then T begins on G♯4 in Cl 1 at 153. The horn's G♯ is the right pivot (movement II's A♭), but the tune that grows from it is not heard until 153, so the trio starts cold. The opening two notes G♯–A are T's shortest recognisable cell; they recur at 197, 209, 225, 229, 233 and (in A) at 341.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 206: an introduction "based solely on the initial two-note motive of the melody". PDF 222: the orchestra's last note gives the singer the first note of their next phrase. PDF 208: inner motion as relief from long held notes.
**Fix.** Hn 1 ends the link G♯4–A4–G♯4. The A falls on beat 3 of 147, an upper neighbour over the E-major chord, the same dissonance as T's own first bar, under the existing diminuendo. The G♯ that the horn hands to Cl 1 is unchanged, and the horn call (1–7) and the frame are untouched.
```
linkHnOne = { g'2.~\pp | g'2.~ | g'2.~ | g'2 r4 | gis'2.~\< | gis'2.~ | gis'2(\> a'4 | gis'2) r4\! | R2.*4 | }
```

### Decided against
- **T's rhythm (Reading notes, row 15).** Phrases 1 and 2 share the rhythm 2–4 / 2–4 / 4.–8–4 / 4–2, the plainest Ländler pattern. It is T's weakest point next to Dvořák's turn-filled trio tune. I did not change the theme. T's contour and its peak at A5 (c011) are good, it is recorded at 153, 169, 197, 209 and 341–356, and findings 1 and 3 give its phrase ends the motion the tune itself lacks.
- **Second idea anticipations 48→49, 52→53 (row 11).** Sebesky's repeated-note warning (PDF 216–217) concerns fast inner parts. A single slurred lead-on quaver in a legato melody is a normal anticipation (c031), so I kept it.
- **Hn 1 shadowing T at 197–200.** This is related to Ex. 279, but c039 and c047 placed this horn line carefully, and the oboe's call above makes the tune clear there. Left alone.
