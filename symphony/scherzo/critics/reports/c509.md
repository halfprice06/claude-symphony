## Holistic comparison with Bizet's scherzo

**Shared methods.** Bizet makes a scherzo from a short, rhythmically sharp theme derived from his first movement, keeps a classical orchestra transparent, and sets a rustic trio (woodwind tune over a drone). This movement does the same in its own terms:
- theme A grows out of the cyclic motto's chromatic tail;
- the textures of 13–44 are as thin as Bizet's (one string section plus off-beats, then a woodwind call and a string answer);
- the trio's first statement is a woodwind tune over a sustained fifth (Cl 1 over Hn 3·4 and harp, 153).

**Where Bizet does it differently.** Bizet's drone is in the strings, this score's in the horns. Rimsky's own Ex. 40 (PDF 218) uses held pp horns under a solo reed with harp and pizz. cellos, which is exactly 153–168. The second statement (169) then moves the accompaniment to the strings, so the score already contains both colours. Bizet's tunes are shorter and squarer than T and the second idea. The second idea is still the least memorable theme here, the open point every holistic critic has noted. This reading is about scoring and gives no ground for rewriting its notes.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** For scoring, yes. Of the 25 rows, 21 follow outright. The other four are a deliberate exception (row 16), a partial match with a reason (row 6), a local crossing (row 21) and two single beats (row 23).

## Findings

### 1. 14, 18, 24, 26: violas echo motive x's quaver pair under theme A's held notes (built, rejected)

**Reading.** Ex. 1 (PDF 173), the textbook model for theme A, and Ex. 37 (PDF 215) give the violas a running staccato figure of their own while Vln II plays the off-beats. PDF 55 adds that violas are "better fitted for short characteristic phrases". In 13–28 the violas play exactly Vln II's off-beat crotchets, so one layer is spent twice. This is the change a listener would hear most that the reading supports and that no protection forbids outright (c259/c359/c459 protect the *bareness* of 13–28, meaning no winds and no cello doubling).

**Built in /tmp/critic-509/candidate-viola (sA.ily, sAVla):** beat 2 of the four held-note bars becomes motive x's quaver pair.
```
old: ... | r4 b4-. b4-. | ... | r4 c'4-. c'4-. | ... | r4 b4-. dis'4-. | r4 b4-. b4-. | r4 b4-. c'4-. |   (bars 14, 18, 24, 26)
new: ... | r4 b8-. b8-. b4-. | ... | r4 c'8-. c'8-. c'4-. | ... | r4 b8-. b8-. dis'4-. | r4 b4-. b4-. | r4 b8-. b8-. c'4-. |
```
The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triads, parallels unchanged, themes 52/52.

**Why it makes the music worse.**
- **Theme A's profile.** Theme A's identity is the contrast between motive x's moving bar and the accented held note that follows it (13→14, 17→18, 25→26). The held bar is where the motto's chromatic tail sounds on beat 3 (G–F♯–F–E). Quavers under beat 2 fill exactly the gap that gives the tail its relief, which is Rimsky's own first criterion (PDF 54).
- **The timpani's answers.** The figure is the timpani's answer rhythm (r4 x8 x8 x4) at 20 and 28. Sounding it four times in the violas first turns the timpani's motivic answer, protected since v01, into a fifth repetition.
- **Ex. 1 differs.** In Ex. 1 the tune ornaments with grace notes and turns, and the viola runs in semiquavers below it. Here the tune itself runs in quavers, so the same means doubles the rhythm instead of contrasting with it.

**Not applied;** the scratch copy is reverted to v509.

### 2. 153–168: the trio's drone in horns rather than strings (Bizet), kept

Bizet's trio sets its woodwind tune over a string drone. Here Hn 3·4 hold the fifth under Cl 1 with harp off-beats and pizz. basses. Ex. 40 (PDF 218) is this layout note for note: a reed solo over harp chords, pizz. cello bass and pp held horns. The strings take over the background at 169 (Vln II con sord. quavers, arco basses). Moving the drone to the cellos would remove the difference between the two statements, which is protected (c434, c500). Kept.

### 3. 45–56: no rhythmic figure under the second idea's octaves (Ex. 8), kept

Ex. 8 (PDF 182–183) is the closest model for a forte string cantabile in octaves, and Rimsky gives it a triplet chord figure in divided cellos. Here every voice either sings the hemiola or sustains, and only the horns' tenuto downbeats keep the bar. Supplying the figure would take the violas or cellos off the low octave and undo c419 and c481, the two reasons the statement now has three full octaves. c409 rejected a pulse figure for the same horn reason. I found no voice free to take it. Kept. The open point on the second idea's memorability remains.

### 4. 45–56, 104–105, 276–277, 304–305: Cl 1 sustains above Ob 1 (PDF 65, 67), kept

The order scan finds Cl 1 a third or a fourth above Ob 1 at those bars, against Rimsky's natural order. At 45–56 Ob 1 doubles the middle melody octave with Vln II and dips under the clarinets' held chord tones. Lowering the clarinets would put them in the low melody octave (Vla, C.a., Vc). The crossing is inside a forte string texture and is protected (c014, c110). Kept.

### 5. 45 and 50: Cl 1·2 in octaves for one beat with no string doubling (PDF 67–68), kept

Both are the clarinets' rising sixths (B3→G4, C♯4→A4) echoing the theme's sixth. Each octave lasts one beat, inside an f string texture. Changing Cl 2's first note would lose the echo, which is protected ("rising sixths", c488). Kept.

### 6. 253–268: double basses divided in octaves, pizz. (Note II, PDF 62), kept

Note II's reason is unity of tone in a bowed line on non-corresponding strings. These are single pizz. downbeats. c461 recorded this as a deliberate exception, and I agree.
