## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 341–344: under Hn 3's solo, the violas' held pad is the chord root. Hold E3 instead (applied)

**Problem.** After the G.P., T returns in A, in Hn 3 alone, p dolce. The accompaniment has three elements: the harp's off-beat chords (C♯3–E3–A3), the basses' pizz. A1, and the violas' held notes A3 | A3 | E3 | A3, pp. The violas are the only sustained string sound, and three of their four bars give the root A. That root is already in the pizz. bass, in the harp LH, and as the top note of the harp chord (A3, the same pitch). So the one held colour under the solo adds nothing to the harmony. It is exactly what c267 removed from the cellos in these bars under rule 7, but left in the violas.

**Evidence.** PDF 140, rule 7: "the least important note of a string voicing is the chordal root". Doubling it "grounds" the voicing and can "cloud" the bass line. PDF 144–145, Ex. 195 (image): with root and third elsewhere, "the addition of an E in the bottom cello at this point is unnecessary. It would have 'grounded' the voicing, making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating." PDF 146: roots in the string pad only for "temporary richness", "not to reinforce the bass". PDF 167–168, Ex. 214 (image): the lead horn plays over a light string background while the bass turns pizz., and each element "fulfils its function as a component of the overall ensemble chord".

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, codaVla, with a c567 comment).**
```
old: ... | R2. | a2.\pp | a2. | e2. | a2. | a2.\ppp | d'2. | ...
new: ... | R2. | e2.~\pp | e2.~ | e2.~ | e2. | a2.\ppp | d'2. | ...
```
The violas hold E3 across 341–344. E is the fifth of A, the root of E7 at 343 (c347's E3 there is unchanged), and the fifth of A again. They return to A3 at 345 as before. The pad now sounds the common tone of I and IV through the A-major stage, the I–IV–I prolongation of E recorded in HISTORY (c313 rewording). It no longer duplicates the bass, and E carries straight on into the E major of 349. The old pad already had E3 at 343, so this keeps both earlier fixes at that bar: c327's aim (no held B3 under the solo's falling sixth) and c347's (no G♯ shadowing the horn's leading tone). It also respects c357's reason: there is no D (the horn's seventh) in the strings. The E3 lies on the viola C string at pp, a veiled colour under the horn's C♯4–A4, with no crossing. The violas keep playing, so parts-cues.json needs no change. Page 13 is unchanged apart from the tied E3s.

### 2. Bars 125–131: the cellos double the bass while the bass already has Cb, Cbsn and Bsn 2 (built in work2, rejected)

**Problem.** In the exposition's E-minor second idea, the cellos play the bass from 121. From 125 the double basses and contrabassoon join them, with Bsn 2 also on the line. So at the crescendo into the hemiola the bass is in four instruments and two octaves, and the tune is in two violin octaves with only held notes between.

**Evidence.** PDF 140, rule 7 (habitual cello doubling of the bass). PDF 151, Ex. 202: a climax strengthened by "reinforcing the lead line an octave lower, first with the top cello".

**Tested.** In /tmp/critic-567/work2/sections/sC.ily, the cellos take the reprise's tenor line at 125–132 (`c'4.->\<( b8 a4~ | a4 fis'2) | e'2( dis'4~ | dis'4 cis'4. b8) | b4\f g!4( e4) | g2\< fis4~ | fis4 \ff\> e2 \f | e4-.`). The build is clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, lengths match, no C-major triad, no new parallels.

**Why not kept.** c071 gave this tenor octave (Hn 2 and the cellos) to the last statement, at 281–287, as the colour saved for it (Adler II PDF 20). Copying it to 125 makes the two E-minor statements the same in the strings, and the da capo loses its one new string colour. Sebesky's rule is aimed at habit doubling. Here the doubling is the plain first-statement layout, and the bass at 121–124 rests on the cellos alone (rule 3), so the cellos are not added out of habit. The music would be worse by losing its growth across the movement. Kept as it is.

### 3. Bars 13–28 and 253–268: pizz. roots in octaves on every downbeat (checked, kept)

Rule 7 and PDF 140 ("bottom-rich ... sparingly") apply to the Vc+Cb pizz. octave at 13–28 and the divided basses at 253–268. Both are recorded on other grounds: the bare first statement (c259, c359, c559) and c384's bass octave (Rimsky appendix). Sebesky's "distance ... lets the strings float" is written for a studio with a rhythm section underneath. An orchestral pizz. bass has no such foundation. Kept.

### 5. Bars 57–68: the fugato as a four-way conversation (checked, follows)

Ex. 188 (PDF 135–136) asks for separate but equal lines. At 65–68 there are four rhythmic layers: Vln II's subject in quavers and crotchets, the violas' countersubject in crotchets, the cellos' free line in minims, and the basses' pedal. Attention passes Vc → Vla → Vln II → Vln I (the stretto at 69). Sebesky's warning that full sections make quartet writing "labored" is answered by the p/pp levels and the open spacing. Kept.
