## What else I studied

I compared the v29 score with v19 page by page and diffed every section file: coda 60 lines changed, dC 68, sB 47, retr 29, tB 25. The scherzo has no local Brahms score, so I compared from knowledge of Brahms 4/iii's form and orchestration. That movement is in C major, 2/4, and is close to a sonata-rondo rather than a scherzo and trio. It is the only movement of the symphony that uses piccolo, triangle and contrabassoon. Sources: [Christopher Weeks, "4th Symphony"](https://christopher-weeks.com/10-4th-symphony/), [Classical Music Notes](https://www.classicalmusic-notes.com/brahms-symphony-4/), [classicals.de](https://www.classicals.de/brahms-symphony-no4). A script compared the sC and dC tutti bar by bar.

**Holistic verdict.** A conductor reading this score would take it for the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer. Points in its favour:
- The movement has a clear plan.
- The motivic timpani are effective.
- The F-major episode works as Brahms's soft Poco meno presto episode works: in the middle of a loud movement, the main material comes back quiet and in a remote key.
- The coda pivots on G♯.

What separates it from Brahms 4/iii is literal repetition at a structural point. Brahms keeps rescoring his main theme. Here the second ff tutti of theme A is a copy of the first. Two smaller faults are the thin upper chord at the movement's peak and a doubled seventh in the brass.

**Against v19.** Most changes are improvements: the iv6 at 331, the fff peak at 336, the coda entries held back, and the harmonics in the last harp chord. One thing got worse. The v24 cut shortened the reprise close (321–324), and it now drops from f to p in two bars without the mf step it had in v19 and still has at 133–140 (finding 4). Nothing else got worse. The 7 analyser parallels are the same ones, and the score is still 13 pages.

## Findings

**1. 305–320: the reprise tutti is a copy of the exposition tutti (applied).**
Problem: a bar-by-bar comparison of sC 105–120 with dC 305–320 finds 16 of 16 bars identical in 23 of 26 parts. Fl 1 differs in 10 bars and each trumpet in one bar. Rimsky says one idea "will be orchestrated in a different way every time it recurs" (PDF 117), and lists five ways to do it, starting with "transference into other octaves" and "extension of the whole range" (PDF 118). His examples apply them: Tsar's Bride Ex. 178–181 (PDF 348–350), Sheherazade Ex. 192–195 (PDF 358–360), Sadko Ex. 205–206 (PDF 368–369), where the second tutti adds fff and changes details. Brahms 4/iii also never repeats a tutti statement of its main theme note for note. A da capo could be literal, but this reprise is written out and already varied at 253–288, so a copied climax stands out.
Fix: Vln II plays theme A an octave lower at 305–320, with Cl 2 and the cor anglais. The strings now play the theme in octaves. With Fl 1 above (c024), the theme spans three octaves (E4–E6), where 105 has two. In dC.ily:
```
dCVlnTwo = { e'4-. b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->\dnb( fis'8 f'8 | e'4-.) b8-. b-. g4-. | fis'4-. dis'-. b-. | e'4-. b8-. b-. e'4-. | a'2->\dnb( gis'8 g'8 | fis'4-.) dis'8-. dis'-. b4-. | e'4-. r4 r4 | a'4-. g'-. fis'-. | e'4-. dis'8-. dis'-. e'4-. | fis'4-.( g'8 a'8) b'4-. | c''2->\dnb( b'8 fis'8) | e'4-. b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->\dnb( fis'8 f'8 | e'4-.) dis'8-. dis'-. fis'4-. | e'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*4 | }
```
The lowest note is G3 (bar 307). The exposition (105–120) is unchanged, so no earlier fix is undone.

**2. 333–339: the peak chord has no fifth above the trumpets (applied).**
Problem: at 336 (fff, Vln I B6) the woodwinds above Tpt 1 (B4) play B6, B5, B5, B4 and D♯5. The fifth F♯ appears only in Hn 2 and Tbn 1 (F♯4 and F♯3). Rimsky (PDF 110–111): "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets." His diagrams on PDF 111, and Ex. 166 (PDF 335) and Ex. 169 (PDF 339), show the full chord in the woodwind above the brass.
Fix: in coda.ily, Fl 2 plays `b''2.\ff | b''2. | b''2. | dis'''2.\fff | dis'''2. | dis'''2. | e'''4->` and Ob 2 plays `g''2.\ff | g''2. | g''2. | fis''2.\fff | fis''2. | fis''2. | g''4->`. At 336 the woodwind chord is now B6–D♯6–B5–F♯5–D♯5–A4, and the leading tone D♯6 resolves up to E6 at 339. Cycle 15 set these two parts as chord tones; they are still chord tones, now in a fuller spacing.

**3. 338: the seventh is doubled in the brass (applied).**
Problem: at 338 Hn 1 moves B4 → A4 in unison with Tpt 1's A4, the seventh of V7. Rimsky (PDF 102): "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour." Cycle 19 removed the same doubling at 104/300.
Fix: Hn 1 keeps B4 at 338 (`b'2.\fff | b'2. | b'2. | g'4->`). The horns now hold the B-major triad, Tpt 1 has the seventh alone among the brass, and Cl 2 has it in the woodwind.

**4. 321–324: the reprise close drops from f to p (worse than v19; applied).**
Problem: after the v24 cut, the cellos' motive x goes f (321) → p (323) → pp (325). The close at 133–140 and the v19 close go f – mf – p. Rimsky (PDF 116) says a passage must be scored as a step or as a climax according to its context. This passage is a step down from the tutti, and here it skips a grade.
Fix: in dC.ily, Vc at 323 becomes `e4-.\mf\> b,8-. b,-. g,4-. | fis,4-. dis,-. b,-.\! |`, and Cb and timpani at 323 become mf. The coda's pp at 325 is unchanged.
