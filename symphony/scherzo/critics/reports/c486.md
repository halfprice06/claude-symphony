## Findings

### 1. The trio's forte return (209-224): the octave above the bass belongs in the bass clarinet's chalumeau, not in the contrabassoon's upper register (applied)
**Problem.** At 210-220 the contrabassoon plays the octave above the basses at G♯2-E3 (c114, c384). Adler gives this register a direct verdict: playing above the lowest twelfth "takes the instrument out of its most characteristic register ... and makes it just another bassoon, a little weaker and paler than its relatives" (PDF 230). It then falls a ninth, A2 to B1, from 220 to 221, a slow-speaking downward skip at the bottom (PDF 226, 230). In the same 96 bars (149-244) the bass clarinet does not play. Its chalumeau, sounding D2 to about F3, is the register Adler calls "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective" (PDF 217), and he asks that each instrument and register get the part that sounds best on it and on nothing else (PDF 184). The line c114 wanted (A2-F♯2-C♯3-G♯2-B2 under the cellos' countermelody) lies entirely in that chalumeau: written B3-F♯4 for the B♭ instrument.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184 (type-casting), 217 (bass clarinet chalumeau), 230 (contrabassoon above the twelfth; lowest octave as foundation), Ex. VII-74 (Götterdämmerung: the bass clarinet under the clarinet's line), Ex. VII-99 (Brahms, Haydn Variations: the contrabassoon in its low octave with the basses).
**What I tried first.** I kept the contrabassoon in unison with the basses for all of 209-224 (c101) and added the bass clarinet on the octave line. That brought a fifteenth staff into the system 211-231, and LilyPond moved 232-252 onto a new page (14 pages). Forcing both systems onto page 8 gave "compressing over-full page by 4.9 staff-spaces", and a smaller staff minimum distance did not help. One added staff on page 8 does not fit.
**Fix (applied in tC.ily).** The contrabassoon keeps 209 (unison E2-C♯2, c384) and 210 (G♯2), then rests. The bass clarinet takes over at 211, so the system 211-231 swaps the Cbn. staff for the B.cl. staff, keeps its staff count, and page 8 still holds 211-252.

tCBassCl = { R2.*2 | b,2.--(\mp | e2.--) | a,2.--( | ais,2.-- | ais,2.-- | b,2-- fis,4--) \breathe | cis2.--(\< | gis,2.-- | fis,2.--\mf\> | a,2.--) | R2.\! | R2. | e,2.~\mp\> | e,2\! r4 | R2.*16 | }

Concert pitch as in the score: B2-E3 at 211-212, A2-A♯2-A♯2-B2/F♯2 at 213-216, breath (the contrabassoon's breath at 216 kept), C♯3-G♯2-F♯2-A2 at 217-220 with the same hairpins and levels as before (mp, < to mf at 219, >). It rests at 221-222 (c184's reason: a B2 would sit inside the countermelody; the basses' B1 has the countermelody's B2 as its octave) and doubles the basses' E2 at 223-224, as the contrabassoon did. The longest run is 7.9 s (211-221) with the breath at 216. The contrabassoon's ninth A2-B1 is gone, and it now plays only in its lowest twelfth except the G♯2 at 210. Parts: parts-cues.json part 08 (bass clarinet) gets `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",209,2]`. Vln I plays T at 209-210, and the bass clarinet enters at 211 after 62 bars' rest. The contrabassoon's next entry (281) keeps its cello cue at 277. The parts were not rebuilt; build-parts.sh will write the bass clarinet's 211-224 in treble clef (written C♯4-F♯4).
Why a listener hears it: this is the trio tune's one forte statement, with the bass written out in long notes. The octave above the basses now speaks in a full, warm reed tone instead of a thin contrabassoon top, which in Adler's words is weaker than a bassoon.

### 2. Bsn 2's E2 at 180 under a crescendo from pp (no change)
Adler: the bassoon's lowest fifth (B♭1-F2) is extremely hard to play pp, and the player needs a firmer attack (PDF 226). E2 at 180 is the only bassoon note in that fifth that falls inside a soft hairpin. It comes at the end of the crescendo that starts pp at 177 and reaches p at 181. It is slurred by step from F♯2, with the arco basses an octave below. That is about p, not pp, so the firmer attack Adler expects is what the crescendo asks for. Kept.

### 3. Cor anglais D♯5/F5 at 103 and 301-303 (no change)
Adler: in its upper register the cor anglais loses its personality and sounds like an oboe (PDF 205). These notes are inside the p<ff hemiola, doubling Vln II and Cl 1 (the accepted C.a. doubling of Vln II's hemiola, HISTORY). There they only have to blend, which Adler allows (PDF 206: "as agile as the oboe ... all active figures with the rest of the woodwinds"). Moving the C.a. an octave down would put it on Bsn 1 and Hn 3's C4-B3 inner line. Kept.

### 4. Flute B4 at 29/33, 256 and 276 (no change)
The flute's first octave is weak and must not be covered (PDF 185). At 29/33 the B4 is a passing quaver in unison with Ob 1, which carries it (c054, c477). At 256 and 276 it is the last note of a falling figure over a pizzicato or ending texture, p, and the violins double it. No flute line depends on these notes being heard on their own. Kept.

### 5. Cl 1 across the break at 121-124 and 277-280, sub. p (no change)
Sounding G4-B4-G4 / F♯4 is written B♭4-D5-B♭4 / A4 for the A clarinet. That crosses the break in slurred minims and sits in the pale throat register. Adler says the break troubles only inexperienced players and that the difficulty "completely disappears" with experience (PDF 210). The throat colour suits an inner line under the violins' espressivo. Kept.

### 6. Bassoon E2 sub. p at 121 and 277 (no change)
These are in the lowest fifth too (PDF 226). The cellos play the same E2 in unison, and in that coupling "the cello tone predominates" while the bassoon is absorbed (PDF 226). The marking is p, not pp. Kept (c216).

### 7. Breathing and rests, all woodwinds (no change)
No woodwind run is longer than 13.2 s (Bsn 1 101-120 with breaths at 104 and 116). Every run over 9 s carries a breath mark. The oboes' longest run is 7.6 s, with rests of 40 bars or more between playing spans (PDF 175, 198, 188). The contrabassoon's longest run is now 7.6 s (45-56). Nothing to add.
