## Findings

### 1. Coda, 332-333: the low brass re-enter f on the arrival instead of joining the crescendo (applied)

Problem: trombones, bass trombone and tuba rest from 121 to 332 (212 bars) and come back with an f attack on the downbeat of 333. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136 names this case: after a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba "should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful". PDF 127 gives the better way to bring a new group into a crescendo: "the second group enters piano or pianissimo and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense". PDF 130 orders the long crescendo strings, woodwind, brass. The coda already follows that order for the strings (325-328), the woodwind (329-330) and the horns (331-332, c039, which cites PDF 127 and 130), but c039 left the low brass out ("Low brass still enter at 333"). The same score already does it this way at 101-104: trombones and bass trombone enter pp< on the dominant and reach f.

Recorded reasons answered: c123, c169 and c303 keep the low brass at f because of Dan's rule (no ff, sfz or accented brass entries). This fix is softer than that: a pp entry with a one-bar hairpin to the f that c086 set for 333. Nothing at 333-339 changes. The reading gives a reason the record does not answer: within Dan's rule, the only option PDF 136 allows is pp, and PDF 127 explains why it makes the arrival stronger.

Fix (coda.ily), on the plain B-major chord of 332 that the horns and strings already sound:
```
codaTbnOne  = { R2.*7 | fis2.--\pp\< | g2.--(\f | g2.-- | g2.--) | ...
codaTbnTwo  = { R2.*7 | b,2.--\pp\< | b,2.--(\f | b,2.-- | e2.--) | ...
codaBassTbn = { R2.*7 | b,2.^-\pp\< | e,2.^-(\f | e,2.^- | b,2.^-) | ...
codaTuba    = { R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | b,,2.^-\pp\< | e,,2.^-(\f | e,,2.^- | b,,2.^-) | ...
```
Tbn 1 F♯3→G3 by step and Tbn 2 holds B2. Bass trombone B2→E2 and tuba B1→E1 move with the basses. Both trombones carry the same dynamic on the shared staff. There is no seventh or leading-tone doubling and no new parallels (the analyser list is unchanged).

### 2. 101-105: the tuba's first note in the movement is an f downbeat after 104 bars of silence (applied)

Problem: the tuba does not play until 105, where it starts f on the tutti downbeat, while the trombones and bass trombone have already entered pp< at 101 (PDF 136, as in Finding 1). sB.ily had no tuba variable, so the build filled 101-104 with rests.

Fix: the tuba joins the bass trombone's crescendo an octave lower, on the dominant B1 (already in the double basses and contrabassoon), and the now redundant f at 105 is removed:
```
sBTuba = { R2.*44 | b,,2.^-(\pp\< | b,,2.^- | b,,2.^- | b,,2.^-\f) | }
sCTuba = { e,,4^- r4 r4 | ...      % was e,,4^-\f
```
This is not a "full low-brass unison bass" (a finale reserve): the trombones hold F♯-A and D♯-F♯ above. The tuba still has no melody.

### 3. Length and proportions: keep (no change)

The movement lasts 4:38 in the MIDI, the shortest of the four (7:11, 5:45, 4:38, 7:02). That is the usual place for a scherzo between a long Adagio and a finale, and I decided not to lengthen it to the plan's "about five minutes". Compared with Dvořák 9/iii, the trio-tempo music here is heavier: the trio with link and retransition is 32 per cent against Dvořák's 24, and 34 s of the coda run at the trio tempo, so 41 per cent of the movement is in E major at 2. = 76. The reprise is shorter (18 against 25 per cent). I tested this against PDF 116 (a passage is a contrast, a climax or a step). The second coda stage is the cyclic close (motto tail, the G♯ pivot), not a further trio statement. The reprise moves faster than the exposition: it skips the F-major episode, the fugato is 16 bars and the tutti comes 52 bars after the return. So the ending feels like a release after the 336 peak, not a second trio. Changing section lengths would also renumber the bars that movement I and the finale quote (363-371, 357-360). Keep the proportions, as c003, c033, c083, c103, c113 and c313 did.

### 4. Varied returns, crescendo and diminuendo order, imitation by register: keep (no change)

Each return of an idea is scored differently in the ways PDF 117-118 lists. Theme A goes from strings p to winds (29), full tutti (105), mp with the flute's florid line an octave above (253, which is PDF 132's case), partial-to-full tutti with the flutes' figure (305), and full brass fff (333). The second idea goes f, then sub. p, then E major in flute and oboe. Builds and fades follow PDF 130 at 89-104, 133-140, 321-324 and 325-336. The only exception is the close, where Hn 1 outlasts the strings. Its reason is the cyclic G♯ pivot, and PDF 130 gives no stronger reason, so it stays. The codetta relay 225-236 follows the law of register (PDF 128).
