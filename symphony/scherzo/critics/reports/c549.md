## What else I studied

**Rachmaninoff 1/ii.** The score is not in the local corpus. I used [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Rachmaninoff)) and the [IMSLP page](https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.1%2C_Op.13_(Rachmaninoff%2C_Sergei)) for the facts:
- The movement is in F major.
- It opens with the symphony's turn figure (the "grupetto") and recalls the Dies irae's first notes.
- Its main theme is short and appears both in its original form and, briefly, in inversion.
- "Call signals and shudders" sit between the theme's statements.
- The central section has a solo-violin melody.
- Norris's criticism, quoted there, is that the scherzo loses drive through repeated statements of the same theme.

This movement already does what the Rachmaninoff does best:
- A cyclic motto is quoted, not developed: the chromatic tail and the horn call.
- The timpani's motive x answers sit in the theme's rests (20, 28, 260, 268).
- A solo violin carries the coda's T (349–356).
- The close is soft and thin.

It avoids Norris's fault: theme A is scored differently at 13, 29, 105, 253, 305 and 325. The one place where the growth is blurred is the start of the reprise (Finding 1). A conductor would believe a master wrote this.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 253–260: the flute doubles Vln I's theme from the first bar of the reprise (applied)

**Problem.** Since v01, Fl 1 has doubled theme A heterophonically two octaves up for all 16 bars of 253–268. As a result:
- The reprise never presents the theme as the opening did: Vln I leggiero over light strings.
- The flute's figure is the same one it plays again at 305–320 in the tutti, so the heterophony colour is heard for 32 of the 52 bars from 253 to 304.
- c449 made the cellos' countermelody grow phrase by phrase (cellos alone at 253, Hn 3 added at 261). The flute ignores that plan. The top layer is complete from bar 1, so only the bottom layer grows.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 140–141: "incessant duplication for an extended period of time should be avoided; it is only permissible in isolated phrases". Uninterrupted doubling "replaces by a mixed timbre the rare characteristic qualities" of the line.
- Ex. 289–290 (PDF 467–468): a new colla-voce doubling for each phrase.
- Ex. 303 (PDF 491–492): each echo between phrases has a new colour.
- Ex. 295 (PDF 474–475): layers enter one at a time.
- The score already applies this rule to the second idea in the same section (c044: violins alone at 277, + Ob 1 at 281, + Fl 1 at 285).

**Earlier reasons answered.** c107 listed "flute heterophony 253–268" as do-not-touch without a reason, and c278 kept it. The heterophony stays: it now starts at 261, the second phrase, together with Hn 3's entry. The same rule also governs 277–288. c069 and c134 (Vln I a level above the flute) still hold from 261. c034's lower-neighbour turn at 258 goes; the one at 261 stays.

**Result.** The reprise opens as the movement opened, in Vln I's own colour, with the cellos' countermelody as the only new element. At 261 the flute and Hn 3 enter together, and at 305 the full tutti follows. That gives three layers in three steps. The timpani answers at 260 and 268 still mark the phrase ends.

### 2. Fugato 57–72: an inverted entry, as Rachmaninoff inverts his theme (considered, not applied)

Rachmaninoff develops his theme by inversion. Here, motive x is a repeated-note rhythm, so inverting it changes almost nothing. Inverting the chromatic tail (G–G♯–A–B♭) would develop the motto, and the cyclic rule says to quote the motto, not develop it. The fugato exposition 57–68 is also settled (c107, c279). No change.

### 3. Bar 339: the final ff chord (checked, kept)

Ex. 312 (PDF 502–503, text PDF 166) asks for the final chord to be planned as the summit. At 339 all groups arrive together after the V7, with root E in five octaves (E1 to E6) and a single attack, followed by the general pause. The third G sits in seven parts, but in E minor that is normal tutti practice: Ex. 309–310 double the third as freely. No change.

### 4. Bars 349–356 and 357–376: soft climax and closing page (checked, follows)

Ex. 282 (PDF 450) and Ex. 292 (PDF 471) treat a soft peak and a distant voice with single players and no support. The coda's solo violin with Fl 1, and the horn alone with pizzicato and harp harmonics, do exactly that. These bars are protected and right.
