## Findings

### 1. Coda 352: the cellos leave a plain A-major triad on beat 3, so the return to E slips back toward A. Applied.

**Problem.** The coda states T's first half in A major (341–348, IV), pivots on E at 348, and needs four bars before its cadence confirms E (353–356). Bars 349–352 are C♯ minor, E/G♯, F♯m7 and F♯m7/A: every one of them is also diatonic in A, where they read iii – V6 – vi – I. On beat 3 of 352 the cellos move F♯3→C♯3, so the sound under the melody's A (A1, C♯3, E4, A4/A5) is a complete A-major triad, the only one in 349–356, and the melody lands on A's 1̂ there. Right after eight bars in A, that beat sounds like a return to A rather than a pre-dominant in E. The trio's own statements do not do this: at 220 the cellos hold F♯3 through the bar (ii6/5), and at 164 the harp plays A–C♯–F♯ (ii6).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 451 (§11): emphasis on IV "can upset our sense of tonality, making us hear IV as I and I as V". The coda's IV is safe because it comes late (Ex. 26-11, PDF 452), but the passage back to I is where that risk lies.
- A&S PDF 443 and PfR 2 (PDF 456): the new key is confirmed by a cadence with some form of II as well as V. With F♯ held, 351–355 is ii7 – ii6/5 – 6/4 – V7 throughout.
- c202's do-not-touch item "held E ii7–ii6/5 into the 6/4 at 219–221/351–353" describes this progression; the cellos' C♯3 had weakened its last beat in the coda.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaVc, bar 352).**
```lilypond
% was:  a2( gis4 | fis2 cis4) | b,2.~ | ...
a2( gis4 | fis2.) | b,2.~ | ...
```
F♯3→B2 into the 6/4, the cellos' move at 220–221. The violas' inner E4 (349–352) and the melody are unchanged, so the C♯ that c170 kept at 352 (the ♮6 before the ♭6 at 359) is still sounding on beat 1. No theme, register or protected bar is touched.

**Honest size.** This is a pp inner-voice change, and a listener hears it as a smoother turn into the final cadence. I looked for a bigger change that this reading supports and a listener would hear. Each candidate made the music worse:
- Bass E at 349 instead of C♯, to make the return to E a root-position tonic. The melody's C♯6 minim would then be an unprepared accented sixth held for two beats, and T's bar-9 harmony would differ from the trio's three statements (161, 177, 217).
- Harp D♯4 in the applied B7 at 347. It would double Bsn 1's D♯3, and both resolve to E (348), which gives octaves.
- T's phrase 2 over ii instead of IV at 157 (Casta diva, PDF 454). The bass would stand still for two bars and lose the IV colour, and the gain in memorability is nil.
- Cutting the link's E-major pad 149–152 (phrase modulation, PDF 452). It would renumber every bar the finale and movement I quote, and the pad is c413's Dvořák 9/iii link.

### 3. The tonal plan against Unit 26: keep. One note for the other threads.

The exposition follows the minor plan of PfR 7 with VII as the link (Ex. 26-16) and ♭II standing in for the forbidden VI (rows 11, 18, 19). The trio is Ex. 26-17 in major. The coda's IV is Ex. 26-11's phrase modulation. At the symphony scale, the scherzo is the passing area of Ex. 26-7: it begins and ends on the common tone G♯ = A♭ between movement II's A♭ and the finale's German sixth on A♭. Its F episode carries the IV of Beethoven's plan I – VI ‖ IV–V – I (PDF 454), which movement I now uses inside itself (mvt1.md, critic 440), and the finale takes it back as IV6/4 at IV 272. Movement I's critic asked that the finale's and movement I's plans stay different. The scherzo's plan (III major, then III♭ – VII – iv – ♭II – V) differs from the finale's (III, G minor → G major) and from movement I's. Keep it.
