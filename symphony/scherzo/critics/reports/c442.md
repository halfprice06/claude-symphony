## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 1 and 13: 'senza sord.' for Vln I, Vln II and violas (REQUIRED ITEM, applied)
- **Problem.** Since movement II v469, its violins and violas play the coda con sord. (II 84–92). The scherzo printed no mute instruction for any string section, so a player coming from movement II could keep the mute on for the scherzo's first G♯5 ppp and for theme A at 13.
- **Is there a documented reason to keep Vln I muted at 1–2?** I found none. The reason recorded for the bar 1–2 G♯5 (c430) is that it matches the closing pair, Hn 1 G♯4 + Vln I G♯5 at 369–376. Those bars are open: c084 kept "unmuted Vln I on the last G♯". The finale takes the pair over in the same players (IV 1, Vln I A♭5). A muted G♯5 at bar 1 would break the very match it exists for. So the opening is open.
- **Timing.** Movement II's ending to bar 1 is a pause, the same pause in which the timpani retune and the harp resets its pedals (both are labelled at bar 1). Taking off a violin mute is quicker than either. Vln II and the violas also have 12 bars' rest. Cellos and basses play II's coda open, so they need no mark. The basses already say "tutti pizz." at 3.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-442/work).** Hn 3·4's "senza sord." at 73 states the instrument's state at its entry; these marks do the same (Adler II PDF 312).
  - intro.ily: `introVlnOne = { gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "senza sord." | gis''2. | R2.*10 | }` (with a c442 comment).
  - sA.ily: `sAVlnTwo = { r4 g'4-.\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." g'4-. | …`.
  - sA.ily: `sAVla = { r4 b4-.\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord., unis." b4-. | …` (joined with the existing "unis.").
- **Parts.** The parts are built from the same variables. build-parts.sh: rc=0, 0 warnings. The Violin 1, Violin 2 and Viola parts print the text and keep 3 pages each. No line was moved or silenced, so parts-cues.json is unaffected.
- **For the composer.** coherence/scherzo.md line 12 should add that the violins and violas are marked senza sord. at their first entries (1, 13), and the movement II thread should be told it is done. This changes no notes, theme, key or tuning.

### 2. 101–104 / 301–304: ♭9 on the retransition dominant. This was the best candidate; I built and tested it and rejected it
- **Why it was the candidate.** PDF 466 says the 9th "intensifies the dissonant character of the dominant". This four-bar crescendo V7 before the ff theme at 105/305 is the dominant a listener hears most. Ex. 27-6b gives a top line for it: the 9th passing down to the 7th, which then resolves (here by transfer, Cl 1 A5→G5).
- **What I built** (/tmp/critic-442/cand, sB.ily): the hemiola's top minim at 103.3–104.1 became C6 instead of B5 in Fl 1/Vln I, and C5 in Vln II/C.a., giving D♯6–C6–A5.
- **Result.** Clean build, 13 pages, no new outer-voice parallels, no C-major triad. But the slices show C5 (Vln II, C.a.) attacked against Tpt 1·2's B4 on 104's downbeat, which the trumpets hold for the whole bar, with B3 in Hn 3·4 and Bsn 1. That puts the ♭9 a semitone above its own tone of resolution, in the same register, in a forte tutti: the "unclear sonority and ineffective resolution" of PDF 469–470 (also Unit 21, PDF 352). The C also never reaches B in its own voice.
- **The second reason.** A sustained V♭9 here would take away what makes 337 unique: it is the only complete, sustained V♭9 in the movement, at fff (c352).
- **Decision.** Not applied; c352's register-and-transfer reason is confirmed by the build. Keep "plain V7 at 101–104/301–304". Add the trumpets' held B4 at 104 to the recorded reason.

### 3. 48 and 52: no 9ths on the applied dominants of the second idea's chain (reason recorded)
- **The tempting case.** The G-major chain E7/G♯–A–A7/C♯–(G/D)–D7–G (48–55) is the one descending-5th chain of applied dominants, the situation of Ex. 27-8/27-9. PDF 467 says 9ths occur "as freely in applied dominant chords".
- **Why not.** The same unit rules it out three ways:
  - Both applied chords are inverted (bass G♯, bass C♯). A 9th would sound with its tone of resolution above the bass: E in Hn 2 E3 and the violas at 48; A in Cl 2 A4 and Hn 2 A3 at 52. That is section 7 again (PDF 469–470).
  - The minor 9th on E7 gives upper notes G♯–B–D–F, the reserved stop chord.
  - The major 9th B on A7/C♯ would sit a tone above Cl 2's A4.
- **Decision.** Keep the chain as c432 left it. Its 7th D resolves by transfer (Cl 2 D4→C♯4 while the melody rises D6→E6), as in Ex. 27-6a.

### 4. 202–208: no V♭9 by mixture before the trio's return (reason recorded)
Ex. 27-3 (pp V♭9 in major through mixture) and Exercise 3 (E major, ♮9–8–7 over B) suggest holding iv's C♮ (202) over the B pedal at 203. I did not apply it, for two reasons:
- **Clash.** The top voice already takes that C5 to B4 at 203 in its own register (c070: Vln I, violas, Bsn 1 an octave lower). A held C would sound against that B4 on the downbeat (PDF 469–470 again).
- **Repetition.** A second 9–8 would follow the V♭9 of C♯ minor at 200 three bars later and blunt it.

The trio keeps its single V♭9 at 200 and its V9 (major 9th) at 216.
