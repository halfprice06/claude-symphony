## Findings

### 1. The octave E drum is tuned for the whole movement but used once. Applied.
- **Bars.** 11 (the only E3), 333, 339.
- **Problem.** The four drums are E2, G2, B2 and E3. The 23" drum is retuned from movement II's E♭3 before bar 1, plays one note at bar 11, and then sits idle for 365 bars until the muta back to E♭ for the finale. The movement takes Beethoven 9/ii as its timpani model, but the timpani never sound their octave, which is that movement's signature. At the coda's tonic arrival (333) and its last stroke (339), the timpani play only E2, as they do at 105 and 305. Apart from the roll and the hemiola, the peak's timpani sound is the tutti's sound again.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 79: the tuning "in an octave F" in Beethoven's last two symphonies and the solo timpani in the Ninth's scherzo were "quite a shocking innovation". PDF 81, Ex. XII-50: two notes struck together are possible on a four-drum set. PDF 126 and 135: hold a percussion resource back until the climactic moment ("Brahms's restraint in using the timpani is rewarded…it is the entrance of the timpani that makes this climax work"). PDF 141: percussion used sparingly gains importance. Playability: E3 sits in the middle of the 23" drum (D3–A3, Ex. XII-46), where its tone is good. At 333 the drop from E3 to E2 takes one crotchet with alternating hands, the same move as at 11→12, which already exists. At 339 the 30" and 23" drums are the two ends of the set, one under each hand, so the double stroke is natural.
- **Fix (applied).** coda.ily, codaTimp: at 333 `e,4--\f e,8 e,8 e,4` → `e4--\f e8 e8 e,4` (motive x falls E3–E3–E3–E2, the same shape as B–B–B–E). At 339 `e,4-> r4 r4` → `<e, e>4-> r4 r4` (octave double stroke on the climax's last chord, where the low brass return f>). There is a c326 comment above the variable. Rhythm, dynamics and accents are unchanged. The protected motive x statements (9–12, 241, 249, 363–371) are untouched, and the theme record reads 51/51 unchanged.

### Checked, no change (these points stand against the reading)
- **Ranges and retunings.** Every pitch fits Ex. XII-46. The retunings in the pause at 1 (A♭→E on the 30", C→G on the 28", D♭→B on the 25", E♭→E on the 23") and at 376 (E→G, G→A♭, B→C, E→E♭) all stay inside the drums' ranges. Each muta comes at least 40 bars before the drum's next use.
- **Rolls and endings.** All match Ex. XII-47/48. The ties at 337–338 on single strokes only set the ring length, which is correct.
- **Dynamics.** No fff in the timpani. The f under the ff tutti and the ff roll under the fff at 336 follow c294 and Piston (PDF 55, 84).
- **The reprise tutti 305–320.** It has the same timpani as 105–120. I considered thinning it so that the coda's entry stands out (PDF 126, 135). I kept it, because the trumpets and timpani alternate motive x there as one tutti rhythm, and c034 already holds the low brass back from the reprise for the coda. Finding 1 now gives the coda's timpani something new without weakening the reprise.
- **Trio strokes.** The pp strokes at 153, 168 and 188 and the f at 200 are crotchets with pizz. bass, on soft sticks. They are kept (c316).
