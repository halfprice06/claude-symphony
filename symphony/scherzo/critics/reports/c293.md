## Findings (ranked)

Under Unit 32 the form, its proportions and its joins hold. Each key change is one of the types the unit describes, and each has a motivic reason. The four points below are new to this reading's passes. Each was weighed against the reading and decided as "keep". No change is applied, and the scratch copy is v293 unchanged.

### 1. 288→289: the reprise goes from E minor straight to G minor. Keep.

**Question.** In the exposition the fugato's G minor (57) comes from G major (45–56). That is A&S's "most frequent way of stabilizing" III♭: reach III, then inflect it (PDF 596, Ex. 32-15). Since c003 the reprise's second idea stays in E minor (277–288), so G minor at 289 arrives from E minor with no G-major stage. The only shared tone is G, and at 288 it lies inside the chord (Cl 1 G4; the top voice is E5). Does the join need a common tone in the soprano (PDF 587) or a staged approach (PDF 596)?

**Evidence.**
- A&S allow the direct form: "At times, however, an immediate confrontation between the two contrasting key areas becomes a compositional necessity" (PDF 596, Haydn Ex. 32-16). Haydn ties the clash to the motive: the D♯–E steps of the A-major section prepare the E♭ (PDF 597).
- Here the clash is B♮ (the fifth of the E-minor chord at 288, violas B3, after the B pedal of 285–287) against B♭, the first chromatic note of the fugato subject (290, Vc `bes2->( a8 aes8`). That subject is theme A's tail transposed. The B/B♭ pair is the movement's motive, as F♮/F♯ is the Hammerklavier's (PDF 585).
- The join is a section start after a full stop. 288 is a tutti crotchet followed by two beats of rest, and 289 is the cellos alone, p, playing G–D–D–G. A single line on the tonic and fifth states G minor by itself. In *Widmung* "the E (F♭) triad so obviously marks the beginning of a section that we hear it immediately" as a new tonic (PDF 586).
- The exposition's join 56→57 has the same rest and the same lone-cello entry. The reprise keeps the gesture and only sharpens the key relation. A held G across the rest would fill the silence that both joins share and would blur the fugato's p entry.

**Decision.** No change. The direct confrontation is the motivic kind that A&S endorse (PDF 596–597). Restoring a G-major stage would undo c003's reprise in E minor.

### 2. 133–152: twenty bars of winding down before the trio. Keep.

**Question.** From the E-minor cadence at 132 to the trio at 153, the music stays on E for 20 bars (about 14 s): a soft wind chord (133–136), the cellos' motive x dying away (137–140), Hn 1 G4 over E–B (141–144), G♯4 (145–148), then harp and pizzicato (149–152, poco rit.). Does this hold up the movement?

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 587 (Schubert 9/iv): the dominant thins to a bare octave, and a melodic tone is left alone to become the base of the new section. Here the E-minor triad thins to the fifth E–B, Hn 1's third G is left exposed, it is inflected to G♯ (145), and G♯ becomes the trio tune's first note (Cl 1, 153). The 20 bars are that one process. Each four-bar group changes one element (texture, then the bass, then the horn's G, then the inflection, then the harp).
- A&S PDF 585: a modulation by inflection "attract[s] the listener's attention to the key change". The G→G♯ needs the bare texture around it to be heard as the event.
- Dvořák 9/iii takes 34 bars (201–234) to move from the scherzo to the trio over a quiet pedal. This movement takes 20.

**Decision.** No change (also protected: c103, c153, c163).

### 3. 237–253: the trio's G♯ falls to E as the motto tail in large values. Keep, and do not add an F♮.

**Question.** A&S §16 (PDF 600–602) show key areas and long-range lines that enlarge the piece's motive. The clearest case is K. 542, in E major, where B–A♯–A–G♯ becomes B–B♭–A–(G)–G♯ across a remote area. In this movement Hn 1 holds G♯4 (237–240), falls to G4 (241, 243) and to F♯4 (244–245), and theme A returns on E at 253. That is the tail G–F♯–(F)–E across the retransition. Should the missing F♮ be supplied (for example as a ♭5 over the B pedal at 251–252) to complete it?

**Evidence.**
- In the theme itself the F♮ is the Neapolitan of E. Over the V pedal it would make B–D♯–F–A, a ♭5 dominant, which this movement uses nowhere. It would also weaken the V7 at 251–252, which A&S want plain before the return (PDF 593: the half-step bass "helps to intensify the V"; here A–C–B into the pedal).
- The F♮ already has its large-scale place: the F-major area (73–88) between G (45) and E (105). The exposition's key plan G → F → E is the tail in its largest form (PDF 600: key areas "project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece").
- The retransition already sounds the tail in full at local level: violas 241–242 E–B–B–E | G–F♯–F–E, then Cl 1 at 245–246.

**Decision.** No change. The long-range G♯–G–F♯–E line stands as it is (protected 237–245, c203). Supplying F♮ over V would add a chromatic chord at the one point where the reading asks for a plain dominant.

### 4. 325–376: a 16-bar fast stage and a 36-bar slow stage. Keep.

**Question.** The coda's fast stage (325–340, including the G.P.) lasts about 10.5 s. The slow stage (341–376) lasts about 34 s, so the movement spends its last 34 s at the trio's tempo after only about 60 s of scherzo tempo since the trio. Is the coda weighted too far toward the slow stage?

**Evidence.**
- The slow stage has its own goal: IV (A major, 341–348) → I (349–356) → the motto tail over E – °7 – iv – I (357–360) → the G♯ hand-over (361–376). That is the Brahms waltz pattern of a "strong IV" before the close (PDF 595, Ex. 32-14), with the motto tail of K. 542 (PDF 601–602) as its melodic goal.
- A&S's Exercise 4 (PDF 606), a Presto giocoso in minor, sempre staccato, ends pp "in tempo" on the parallel major. The quiet major close of a fast minor piece is a textbook shape, not a lapse in pacing.
- The fast stage peaks once (fff, B6 at 336) and stops at 339–340. Lengthening it would add a second climax under the finale's. Shortening the slow stage would cut the protected 341–376.
- Proportions: coda 16% of the time against Dvořák's 9% of the bars. Dvořák's coda is all fast. This one carries the link to the finale, which Dvořák's does not need.

**Decision.** No change.
