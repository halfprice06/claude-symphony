## Findings

### 1. Bars 86–88: Cl 2 breaks the ♮4–♯4 °7 chain (applied)

**Problem.** Bars 85–89 follow the progression of Ex. 31-14: the N6 F/A (85–86), then D♯°7 over A, which is °4/3 over ♮4 (87), then A♯°7 on ♯4 (88), then the cadential 6/4 in E minor (89). The book says that in this progression the upper voices rise by semitone into the next chord and each tone of the °7 resolves normally (A&S PDF 560–562; the consecutive-°7 spelling on PDF 558–560 makes the same point). In v342, Cl 2 played F3 (86) | D♯3 (87) | C♯3 (88). This line has three faults:

- F3 to D♯3 is a melodic diminished 3rd, which the book does not use inside this chain.
- At 87, Cl 2 doubles the leading tone D♯ under Cl 1's D♯4. Cl 1 resolves it up to E4, while Cl 2 moves down to C♯3, so one leading tone resolves and the other does not.
- At 88, C♯3 doubles a C♯ already in the strings and leaves the °7's 7th (G) unsupported in the wind choir.

analysis.txt also flags the low C♯3, although that flag is wrong: C♯3 is the lowest note of an A clarinet.

**Evidence.** The beat dump for 87 shows D♯ in two octaves on beat 3. The second D♯ goes down a tone while the first goes up a semitone. Ex. 31-14 has every voice above the bass move by a semitone or hold.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-342/work/sections/sB.ily, `sBClTwo`).** Bars 85–89 were `R2. | f2.\p\> | dis2.\pp | cis2. | R2.` and are now:

```
R2. | R2. | dis2(\pp fis4 | g2.) | R2.
```

Cl 2 rests at 86. It takes D♯3 at 87 and leaves it on beat 3, when Cl 1 resolves D♯4 to E4, so the leading tone is single. It then moves F♯3 to G3, the semitone that Ex. 31-14 shows, and G3 is the 7th of A♯°7, held on by Bsn 1 into the 6/4 at 89. The dynamics match Cl 1 (pp) on the shared staff, there is no ottava, and the slur opens at 87 and closes at 88. On the engraved staff the part combiner shows the entry cleanly. This change also answers c127's concern that Cl 2 stay registrally separate from the Cl 1 solo. Build clean; no theme is affected.

### 2. Bar 358: the common-tone °7 with G♮ is correct; keep it

**Problem checked.** The A♯°7 over E in the plagal close could be challenged for being spelled with G♮ in place of F𝄪, and for its Vln II leap G4→E4.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 544–545 (Ex. 30-21, Brahms Op. 87) shows a common-tone °7 spelled with the minor 3rd from mixture, in a trio that follows a minor-key scherzo. That is this movement's situation: the minor 3rd G recalls E minor. The parallel chromatic sixths B–A♯–A–G♯ over E–C♯–C–B are the passing chord technique of PDF 537. If Vln II held G over iv, the chord would be A–C–E–G, which contains C–E–G and breaks the cyclic rule, so the leap is required.

**Fix.** None. The reading gives a stronger reason for this passage than the "motto's tail" reason recorded in HISTORY.

### 3. Bar 331: the ♭II6/4 is prepared by association; keep it

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 536–537: a chromatic chord can be prepared by an earlier passage in which its key was established, and Ex. 30-9 (Schubert, String Quintet in E) brings back ♭II late in the movement to recall an earlier episode. The ♭II6/4 at 331 recalls the F-major Neapolitan episode at 73–88, and the French sixth on the same beat is V4/3♭5 of V (PDF 543). This is a stronger reason than the finale cross-reference recorded in HISTORY.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 325–332: the coda's lament stays in Cl 1, not in the bass

**Problem checked.** Ex. 31-2 (PDF 552–553, Bach Crucifixus, in E minor) puts the chromatic line E–D♯–D–C♯–C–B in the bass under 7–6 suspensions. The coda's bass is diatonic (E–D–C–B).

**Decision.** No change. A chromatic bass would land on C over E-minor harmony at 330, which would make C–E–G likely in the inner voices. It would also blur the ♭II6/4 at 331 and alter the bass the finale relies on. The chromatic descent is already present in Cl 1 (G–F♯–F–E–D♯, 329–332), which keeps the lament audible while the bass keeps the plan.

### 5. Bar 98: augmented triad over V is correct

**Evidence.** V with ♭6 in place of the 5th forms an augmented triad (B–D♯–G). A&S PDF 540–541 describes exactly this displacement in minor, resolving 6→5. The G resolves to F♯ on the next beat.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Common-tone °7 or augmented sixth over V or I in the scherzo: rejected

**Evidence.** Ex. 30-23 (PDF 546) embellishes V7 with a common-tone °7 whose upper voices all rise a semitone. Over B, that chord is B–D–F–G♯, which the cyclic rule forbids in every role (c072 rejected it at 101–102). A common-tone augmented sixth over E (PDF 547) is a German sixth containing C–E–G. A common-tone °7 over the trio's Hn 3·4 drone clashes with the drone's B. PDF 545 says these chords are rare on minor triads anyway.

**Fix.** None. These options are closed for this movement.
