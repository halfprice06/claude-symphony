# Critic 191: lens 1, melody and memorability (v191)

Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II "Melody", the section on strings: single sections, unison groupings, octave doublings, double octaves, three and four octaves, thirds and sixths (rimsky-principles PDF 54–63), second pass, and the examples it cites, Nos. 1–34 (PDF 173–212). I read every text page from 54 to 63 in full and rendered each one at 110 dpi to check the bracket diagrams that the OCR garbles (PDF 60, 62, 63). I rendered all forty example pages, PDF 173–212, and looked closely at Nos. 1, 3, 4, 5–6, 7, 8, 10–11, 12, 17, 19, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27–28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33–34.

On octave numbering: Rimsky's "middle of the 5th octave" (PDF 62) can only mean about F♯5 if the octaves are counted from the piano's lowest C. In German or Russian naming (where the 1st octave starts at c′) the 5th octave would start at C8 and no melody could lie above its middle. I use F♯5, as c011 did.

## Findings

No finding requires a change this cycle. I checked each statement of the five themes (theme A, the second idea, T, the harp melody, and the coda's T) against all 21 principles above, bar by bar. None is violated. The score follows the chapter's string-doubling rules in each place where a rule applies. That includes the two that matter most for a tune that must be remembered:

- **Relief (row 1).** The tune is the top voice in its own colour, and the accompaniment is a level softer and below it.
- **Octave support of high violin melody (rows 11, 17).** Every violin tune above F♯5 has its lower octave in Vln II, Ob or Cl.

The themes themselves are well shaped. Each has one high point, placed late (bar 12 of 16, bar 6 of 12, bar 10 of 15). Each has a distinct rhythmic cell. Each has a cadence that differs from its internal half cadence. I do not propose a theme change.

### Points checked and kept (ranked by how close they came to a finding)

1. **Coda T, 349–356: whole Vln I section on C♯6–A6 at pp/p (row 12).** I considered Rimsky's Ex. 24 note and Ex. 26: two desks on the upper octave, the rest of Vln I with Vln II. That would put most of the violins in C♯5–A5, the register where Cl 1 first sang these bars (161–167). Kept for two reasons:
   - Ex. 24's top line lies far above A6. The note concerns the extremity of the E string, and A6 at *p* is within normal *pp* playing.
   - The texture already has the lower octave in Vln II and Cl 1 (row 11). The one-desk upper octave that Rimsky allows is for *pp* chorus doublings, where the voice carries the lower octave. Here, thinning the top would weaken the movement's last high statement of the tune.

   c044's choice stands.
2. **Theme A's low dips at 15–16 and 31–32.** Vln I's G4 and B3 meet Vln II's off-beat G4 and B3 at the unison. Row 1 asks the accompaniment not to enter the melody's register. But the meetings are unisons on notes of the same chord in the same colour, and the melody's D♯4 on beat 2 lies above the accompaniment. Rimsky's Ex. 1 shows the same layout. c073's B3 is kept.
3. **Tutti theme, 113–116 and 313–316.** The flute leaves the upper octave just as theme A climbs to its C6 peak, so the top line at 110 (A6) is higher than at 116 (C6). The upper octave there would need B6 and C7. B6 is reserved for 336 and C7 for the finale. c171 kept this, and nothing in this reading changes that.
4. **Coda T hand-overs, 344→345 and 348→349.** The tune jumps up a ninth (Hn C♯4 → Ob D5) and a sixth (Ob E5 → Vln C♯6), where the trio statements step across their phrase joins. Rimsky's chapter on string melody does not treat hand-overs between groups. c150 kept them under his law of register (PDF 128: each new group a register higher), and c041 and c044 protect the colours. Kept.
5. **T at 209–224, the trio's only forte statement, in two octaves (Vln I; Vln II + Cl 1).** Row 19 (double octaves for forte cantabile) would add violas and cellos at G♯3. That would remove the violas' off-beats (c011) and the cello countermelody (c051, c158), which carry the trio's texture. Rimsky gives double octaves as an option, not a rule. Kept.
