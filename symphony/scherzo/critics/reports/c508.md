## Findings

**1. Applied. 69–71 and 297–299: the wood-wind A pedal of the stretto enters f together with the strings, so the build into the ff chord is a step, not a crescendo by addition.**
- Bars: 69–72 (sB.ily) and 297–300 (dB.ily).
- Problem: the fugato is a long crescendo from the cellos' p at 57 to the ff A-major chord at 72. At 69 the strings reach f. In the same bar Bsn 1·2 and the contrabassoon enter on the A pedal at f after 13–40 bars' rest (Bsn 2 and Cbsn since 45–56, Bsn 1 since 56), and they only grow f → ff. The pedal does not grow; it arrives at full weight with the strings. In the reprise (297) the same three parts jump from Bsn 1's pp crotchets (289–296) or from rest to f.
- Evidence:
  - PDF 130: a prolonged crescendo is made "by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass". At 69 the wood-wind pedal is added at the strings' level, not grown into it.
  - PDF 129 (a): when the strings arrive, the wood-wind chord begins piano under them. Ex. 256 (PDF 419) has winds and horns entering pp cresc. poco under the strings.
  - PDF 136: re-entries after a long rest at f are "less successful", and the rule "is capable of wider application".
  - The score already applies this to the same kind of bar: c408 made the wood-wind p< at 101 under the strings' f, and c050/c213 made the coda's wood-wind and contrabassoon enter p at 329–330 and "reach their crescendo more rapidly" (Rimsky PDF 127). 69 and 297 were the two places left where a sustained wind pedal enters f with the strings in a long build.
- Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-508/work):
  ```
  % sB.ily (69-72)
  sBBsnOne  ... R2. | a2.\p\< | e2. | a4-.\f\< e8-. e-. a4-. | a2->\ff\> r4 | ...
  sBBsnTwo  { R2.*12 | a,2.\p\< | a,2. | a,2.\f\< | a,2.->\ff\> | ...
  sBCbsn    { R2.*12 | a,,2.\p\< | a,,2. | a,,2.\f\< | a,,2.->\ff\> | ...
  % dB.ily (297-300)
  dBBsnOne  ... e4 r4 r4 | a2.\p\< | e2. | a4-.\f\< e8-. e-. a4-. | a2->\ff r4 | ...
  dBBsnTwo  ... R2. | a,2.\p\< | a,2. | a,2.\f\< | a,2->\ff r4 | ...
  dBCbsn    ... R2. | a,,2.\p\< | a,,2. | a,,2.\f\< | a,,2->\ff r4 | ...
  ```
  The pedal starts p and swells over two bars. It reaches f at 71/299, where Bsn 1 takes its stretto entry (motive x) with the cellos and Hn 1·2 (Hn f< there), so that entry keeps its level. The ff at 72/300 is unchanged. Bsn 1 and Bsn 2 share a staff and carry identical dynamics at every point. Pitches, rhythms, slurs and accents are unchanged. One comment line added at the top of each file.
- What a listener hears: at 69 the strings' f stretto sits on a pedal that grows from below, so the last three bars of the fugato swell into the ff chord instead of stepping up to f and staying there. The exposition and reprise strettos now build the way 101–104, 301–304 and 329–333 already do.

**2. Considered and not applied. A timpani stroke or roll at the ff chord at 72 (PDF 135).** PDF 135 asks for percussion on "disconnected sforzandi", and 72 is the only ff tutti chord without a drum. I did not apply it. There is no A drum (E, G, B, E), so the drum could only add the fifth, E. The timpani have rested since 56, and PDF 136 says a group that has been silent gains fresh interest when it returns. Their return is the solo motive x at 91 (c244), and a drum at 72 would come 18 bars before it. A drum would also give 72 the same weight as the 105 tutti, which comes after the drums' four-bar build. The reprise's stretto chord at 300 (the Italian sixth) has no drum pitch, so the two strettos would also differ. The chord already does PDF 129's sf-dim: the strings strike non div. and the horns hold into 73 pp.

**3. Kept. 273: eight wood-wind parts enter f with the strings after only two bars of string crescendo.** By PDF 130 this is a short crescendo, which the players make themselves. The winds here carry theme A in unison with both violin sections (c107, c112). They are a sudden tutti statement in a shortened reprise, not a background that should grow in. The exposition does the same at 41: Fl 2, Ob 2 and C.a. enter f cold on the tune. No change.

**4. Kept. Hn 1·2 mp at 209, Hn 4 mp< at 217, bass clarinet mp at 211.** By PDF 136, mp re-entries are "colourless". These are not entries into silence, though. They join a forte tutti as the accompanying choir one level under its bassoons and cellos (c406). pp would drop them out of that choir, and ff would break Dan's soft-brass rule (c123). No change.

**5. Kept. 207–209: Fl 1 and Hn 1 shape the motto-tail preview differently.** Hn 1 falls on the A at 208 (\>) into its mp chord note at 209. Fl 1 does not fall, and it reaches G♯5 mf in unison with Vln I's f entry of T. The flute only colours the horn line (c414), but at 209 the two lines go to different places: the horn into the accompaniment, the flute into the tune. Each mark fits where its line goes. No change.

**6. Kept. 237–240: the strings leave the trio's last chord before the horns (PDF 130 gives brass first).** c258 and c023 record a stronger reason. Hn 1's G♯ must be the last sound of the trio so that it can turn to G at 241, the protected G♯–G pivot. The reading does not outweigh that. No change.

**7. Kept. 101 strings f< and 301 strings sfp<.** Both are forms of PDF 129 (a). The difference is deliberate (c028, c393, c405, c449): 101 grows from the mp of 99–100, while 301 drops from the ff Italian sixth at 300. No change.
