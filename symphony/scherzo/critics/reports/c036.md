## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 141-148 (link): Bsn 2 E2 pp is in the bassoon's insecure lowest fifth. Applied.
**Problem.** Bsn 2 holds E2 pp for two four-bar phrases, exposed under Hn 1 and the clarinets, with nothing else in the bass until the harp and pizzicato basses enter at 149. E2 lies in the lowest perfect fifth (B♭1-F2).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 174-175: the oboe and bassoon "should not be called upon to perform pianissimo in the lower fifth of their range". PDF 226: "It is extremely difficult to play the notes of the lowest perfect fifth pianissimo. On these notes, one must expect a firmer attack." The clarinet, by contrast, "possesses a full dynamic range in all its registers from top to bottom" (PDF 175, 210), and the bass clarinet's chalumeau is "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective" register (PDF 217). The bass clarinet is idle from 89 to the end.
**Fix (sections/link.ily).** The pedal stays as cycle 12 placed it; only the instrument changes. Delete `linkBsnTwo` and add
`linkBassCl = { e,2.\pp | e,2. | e,2. | e,2 r4 | e,2.\pp | e,2. | e,2. | e,2 r4 | R2.*4 | }`
Bsn 1's B2 (above the lowest fifth) stays.

### 2. Bars 205-224 (trio, end of L into M): Fl 1 and Cl 1 have no breath before T returns forte. Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 1 plays the pp line G♯-A-B-A♯-A (205-208, the motto tail anticipated in cycle 23) and goes straight into T forte at 209, which runs to 224: 20 bars, about 15 seconds, with no rest. The 208→209 join comes from a four-bar slur, so the player has to cut the A short or come in at 209 with no air. Cl 1 has the same problem an octave lower.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: "the flute requires a great deal of breath ... it is important to give the flutist time to rest after especially difficult or sustained passages." In his Brahms 1/iv example, the second flute holds the notes "so that the first flute may breathe", and the two parts are dovetailed "to preserve the illusion of a single flute". PDF 192: multiple flutes "relieve one another in long, fast, or sustained passages". Fl 2 rests from 201 to 240. Cl 2 rests 209-224, and its D♯4 at 205-208 is doubled by Hn 3.
**Fix (sections/tB.ily).** Fl 1 rests 201-208 (`\partCombineSoloII` at 205, `\partCombineAutomatic` at 209). Fl 2 takes `gis''2(\pp a''4 | b''2.~ | b''2\< ais''4 | a''2.)` at 205-208. Cl 1 rests 201-208 (SoloII held to 208, Automatic at 209). Cl 2 takes `gis'2(\p a'4 | b'2.~ | b'2\< ais'4 | a'2.)` in place of its D♯4. The tail now passes from Fl 2/Cl 2 to Fl 1/Cl 1 on the G♯ at 209, and both first players start T after 8 bars' rest. Nothing is printed twice: the staves show "2." at 205 and "1." at 209.

### 3. Bars 105-120 (G, ff tutti): Fl 1 and Fl 2 in unison on E5/B4. Applied.
**Problem.** In the exposition tutti both flutes play theme A in unison at the octave of Ob 1 and Vln I (E5, with motive x on B4). Against full brass at ff, the second flute adds no carrying power. The reprise (305-320) already moves Fl 1 up an octave in 309-312 and 317-320. The exposition does not.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 185 (register chart, Ex. VII-2): the middle register is "sweet and with little carrying power", and from about E6 the flute is "clear and brilliant". PDF 174: "Intensity and volume vary with each instrument according to the range and particular register." PDF 185: a weak register is a warning "not to cover the instrument, for it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume".
**Fix (sections/sC.ily).** Copy the reprise layout. 108 becomes `fis''4-. dis''-. r4` (a beat for the leap, as at 308). 109-112 become `e'''4-. b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. | a'''2->( gis'''8 g'''8 | fis'''4-.) dis'''8-. dis'''-. b''4-. | e'''4-. r4 r4`. 117-120 become `e'''4-. b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. | g'''2->( fis'''8 f'''8 | e'''4-.) dis'''8-. dis'''-. fis'''4-. | e'''4-. r4 r4`. Fl 2 is unchanged. The top note is A6 at 110, as at 310 (no B6; violins untouched).

### 4. Bars 40 and 44: Fl 1 drops to D4-A4 at f. Applied.
**Problem.** At the end of each counterstatement phrase, Fl 1 doubles Vln I's falling arpeggio A4-F♯4-D4 under a crescendo (40) and at f (44), with horns, oboes and strings sounding. In 44-45 it must also leap D4→D6 within one beat. Cycle 16 gave the violins a rest for this leap but kept the D4 in Fl 1.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 185-186: the lowest register is "weak, but luscious". His examples show the composers "careful to let the flute come through by doing little with the other instruments" (Dvořák 9/i, Brahms 4/iv). Here the accompaniment is full, so the flute's D4 cannot be heard and only adds the awkward leap.
**Fix (sections/sA.ily).** In both bars, `a'4-. fis'-. d'-.` becomes `a''4-. fis''-. d''-.`. Ob 1 keeps the low octave at 44, where the oboe is "warm, prominent" (PDF 198). 44→45 is now an octave.

### 5. Bars 169, 253, 349: Fl 1 is marked pp on lines that climb to A6. Applied.
**Problem.** In the trio heterophony (169-184, A6 at 179), the reprise heterophony (253-268, A6 at 258, 261, 263, 266) and the coda's T (349-356, A6 at 351), Fl 1 is pp in its top fourth. In 169 and 253 it is also softer than the violins it decorates (mp and p).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 174: "Intonation, dynamics ... are much harder to control in extreme registers ... This is more true of the highest than of the lowest extreme pitches." PDF 186: "above the A all pitches are difficult to produce ... the highest fourth ... most problematical." PDF 185: in the extreme high register "in most cases the performer can only play extremely loud". A6 pp is the flute's least secure combination, and an octave above the violins the flute does not need pp to stay in the background.
**Fix.** Change `\pp` to `\p` at the first note of `tAFluteOne` (169), `dAFluteOne` (253) and the T entry of `codaFluteOne` (349). Fl 2 rests in all three places, so the shared-staff rule on equal dynamics is not affected.

### 6. Bars 72-88 (D→E): Bsn 1 has no breath between the ff stretto and 16 bars of pp sustained notes, and the bass clarinet has none in the episode. Applied.
**Problem.** Bsn 1 plays motive x 69-71, holds A3 ff at 72, and moves straight to the pp inner line 73-88 (16 dotted minims, about 12 seconds plus the poco rit.). That is 20 bars without a rest, with a ff→pp change of embouchure at the bar line. The bass clarinet holds 16 bars pp (73-88) without a rest. Cycle 26 gave Hn 3·4 a breath at 80, the end of the harp's eight-bar phrase. The woodwinds were left out.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188 (rests after sustained passages) and PDF 230 ("provide quite a few periodic rests so that the player can replenish his breath"). PDF 226: soft playing in the bassoon's middle register is easy, but the change from ff needs a moment to set the embouchure.
**Fix (sections/sB.ily).** Bsn 1 at 72 becomes `a2\ff\> r4` (Bsn 2 and the contrabassoon keep the A). At 80, Bsn 1 `c'2 r4` and B.cl. `f,2 r4`, together with Hn 3·4.

### 7. Score: bass clarinet labelled "in B♭ (low C)". Applied.
**Problem.** The label asks for the extended instrument. The lowest note is now D2 (written E3, the standard low E), because cycle 16 moved the old B1 at 340 to the contrabassoon. After finding 1 the part still goes no lower than D2.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 216: "writing all the way to C is still risky, for not all symphony orchestras have instruments with such extensions."
**Fix (score.ly).** `instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Bass clarinet" \small "in B♭" }`.

### Other points checked, no change
- Cl 1 sustains G4 at 7-8 pp and 17-19 p. On the A clarinet this is written B♭4, the open throat note that Adler calls "rather pale" (PDF 210). I am leaving it. A veiled colour suits the turn from E major to E minor at pp, Adler describes the throat tones as a colour, not a hazard, and the note is held, not crossed through the break at speed.
- Bsn 1 at 193-229 and Bsn 1·2 at 297-320 sustain for 20-36 bars. All of these are separate, untied dotted minims in harmony parts, so the player can breathe between notes. They do not need rests written in.
