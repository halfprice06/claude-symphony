### 1. 87–88: the end of Cl 1's dolce solo disappears into Cl 2 (applied)

**Problem.** In the F-major episode Cl 1 has the theme head *p dolce* (81–88). It ends `a'4 fis'8 fis' dis'4 | e'2.`. At 87 Cl 2 enters *pp* on D♯4 and holds it: a minor third under the solo's F♯4 quavers, then in unison with the solo's D♯4 on beat 3. At 88 Cl 2 moves to C♯4, a minor third under the solo's last note E4, in unison with Bsn 1's C♯4. So the last two bars of the solo, its leading-tone cadence D♯–E, are played inside a second clarinet at the same pitch level, and the solo's D♯4 is not heard as a separate note. c027 cleared the harp off the first half of this solo (81–84) for the same reason; these two bars were left.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 267 (Ex. XVII-10): when the solo flute enters, "the orchestra is reduced drastically", and Mozart writes no orchestral flutes "so that the solo flute will not encounter any color competition". PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible ... especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments."

**Fix (sB.ily, `sBClTwo`, bars 87–88).** The same notes an octave lower: `dis'2.\pp | cis'2.` becomes `dis2.\pp | cis2.` (sounding D♯3, C♯3; the A clarinet's lowest note is C♯3). The D♯°7 over A at 87 and the A♯°7 at 88 are unchanged; they are now voiced A2–D♯3–F♯3–A3–C4 and A♯2–C♯3–E3–G3–C♯4 under the solo, so the solo is the only clarinet above E3, and its D♯4–E4 stand a ninth and a tenth above Cl 2. Voice leading: Cl 2 falls a step, contrary to Cl 1's D♯4–E4; no new fifths or octaves.

### 2. 229–232: Cl 1's statement of T's head has its own partner a third below; Ob 1 and Bsn 1 do not (applied)

**Problem.** The codetta passes T's head from Ob 1 (225) to Cl 1 (229) to Bsn 1 (233). Each statement is meant to be a solo in a new colour. Ob 1 plays alone (Ob 2 silent), and c126 took Hn 3 off Bsn 1's statement so that it too is heard alone. But Cl 2 holds E4 from 225 to 232. At 229 Cl 1 enters on G♯4, a major third above that E4, so the clarinet statement begins as the upper note of a held clarinet third. The clarinet colour has also been sounding for four bars before Cl 1 enters, so the change of colour at 229 is weaker than at 225 and 233.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 267: no instrument of the solo's own colour beside it (Mozart, Flute Concerto), and "the sustained D in both the horns as well as the cellos and basses emphasizes the solid return to the tonic": the held tonic under a wind solo belongs to the horns and the basses. PDF 264: Mozart does not use the orchestral horns while the solo horn plays, "so as not to interfere with the solo sound". PDF 263: the solo colour is fresh because a contrasting colour precedes it. PDF 274: registral distance.

**Fix (tC.ily).** `tCClTwo`: `e'2.\p | e'2. | e'2. | e'2.` at 229–232 becomes `R2.*4` (Cl 2 keeps its E4 under the oboe at 225–228). `tCHnFour`: Hn 4 starts its E3 pedal four bars earlier, `R2.*23` becomes `R2.*19 | e2.--(\pp | e2.-- | e2.-- | e2.--) |`, followed by the existing 233–236 and 237–240 slurs. The tonic is now held by a horn an octave and a third under the solo, as in Mozart's example, and the pedal continues unbroken under Bsn 1 (233) as before. c047's frame (Vln II E5 above, violas B3–A3 below) and the harp's off-beats are unchanged. Hn 4 plays 229–240 pp with slur breaks at 232/233 and 236/237 for breathing.
