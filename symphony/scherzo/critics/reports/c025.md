## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Horns 3·4 are inverted on their shared staff throughout the tutti theme, 105–120 and 305–320 (applied)
**Problem.** `sCHnThree` and `dCHnThree` hold the low tenor countermelody (G3–B3–E4, `g2.( | b2 e'4~ | ...`). `sCHnFour` and `dCHnFour` hold the upper E4/D♯4 crotchets (`e'4-> r4 e'4-.`). The score combines `\pc hnThree hnFour`, so Hn 3 gets stems up and Hn 4 gets stems down, which puts the lower part on top. In every bar of 105–116 Hn 4's down-stem from E4 runs through Hn 3's up-stem and its ledger lines, so the E4 crotchet and the G3 dotted minim look like a single chord of two durations on one stem (page 4, bars 105, 107, 109). Hn 4's crotchet rests on beat 2 print below the staff, under Hn 3's sustained notes.
**Reading.** Gould PDF 41: ledger lines not shared by the part nearer the stave must not cut through its stem. PDF 50: upper-part rests above the middle line, lower-part rests below. PDF 51: when the notation gets this tangled, separate the parts.
**Fix (applied).** In sC.ily and dC.ily, exchange the two variables' contents: Hn 3 takes `e'4->\f r4 e'4-. | e'4-> r4 r4 | ...` and Hn 4 takes `g2.(\f | b2 e'4~ | ...`. The sounding notes are the same. The voice leading into the bar also gets better: before, Hn 3 went B3→G3 and Hn 4 F♯3→E4, crossing at 104–105. Now Hn 3 goes B3→E4 and Hn 4 F♯3→G3. The low line sits better in the low (4th) horn. Each staff now shows the upper part with stems up and its rests above, and the countermelody stems down with its slur below.

### 2. Oboes and clarinets inverted in the hemiola build, 101–104 and 301–304 (applied)

**Fix (applied).** In sB.ily and dB.ily, exchange the last four bars of `OboeOne` and `OboeTwo`, and the last two bars of `ClOne` and `ClTwo`.
