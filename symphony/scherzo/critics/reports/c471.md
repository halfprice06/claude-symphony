## Findings

### 1. Second idea 45–55: the cor anglais on the lowest octave (applied)

Problem. The second idea's only forte major-key statement is laid out in three octaves: Fl 1 + Vln I (D6), Ob 1 + Vln II (D5), and the violas alone (D4, from c419). The two upper octaves each have a reed or a flute; the lowest has none, and at 52 the violas leave the tune for the A7's seventh (G4, protected by c419), so the hemiola cadence 53–55 is in two octaves only. The listener hears a two-octave tune with a thin string shadow below it. This is the least memorable of the four ideas (open since c289), and the place where it is most exposed is its forte statement.

Evidence. PDF 78 gives the three-octave layout this passage almost copies: Sheherazade iii [M], "Vn I + Fl / Vn II + Ob / Cellos + Engl. horn"; Ex. 87 (Kashchei, PDF 261) adds C.a. and clarinets to the violas and cellos on the lowest octave. PDF 76 names "Violas + Ob. (Eng. horn)" among the best and most natural unisons, and the examples Ex. 82–83 (Sadko, PDF 256–257) show C.a. with the violas carrying a dolce line. PDF 68: Ob over C.a. in octaves "always produce a good effect". PDF 69: in three octaves the natural order holds; Fl/Ob/C.a. keeps it. The cor anglais is free (it plays 41–44, then rests 45–56), and is already printed on that system, so no staff is added. c419's reason for dropping this was the page count only; the build now stays at 13 pages.

### 2. Coda 349–356: which octave carries the wind (no change)

PDF 78 says that when only one of two octaves is doubled, the winds should take the octaves and the strings double only the lower one. At 349–356 the opposite holds: Fl 1 with the solo violin on top, Vln II la metà alone below. I considered the two ways to follow the rule: Ob 1 continuing from 348 onto Vln II's octave, or Fl 1 down to it. Either puts a wind on the lower octave, which c457 and c467 thinned precisely so that the single top violin is not outweighed; PDF 76 says the strings dominate in equal pairs, and here the lower string group is a half section. Rimsky's rule assumes full sections on both octaves. No change.

### 3. Trio T at 209–216: the top octave undoubled until 217 (no change)

PDF 66–67 places doubling in loud passages, and Ob 1 rests 209–224.
