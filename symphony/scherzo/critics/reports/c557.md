## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 95-100: the violas' repeated B3 crotchets double the pedal and blur the contrary motion of the head and the cor anglais. Applied.

**Problem.** In the retransition, Vln I states theme A's head p (95-100). Under it the cor anglais plays the V7 arpeggio in staccato crotchets (c537), the cellos and basses pluck B on each downbeat to 99, and the timpani play motive x on B (97-100). The violas also play B3 on every beat, pp, for six bars. That note is the pedal already sounding in three other parts, and the rhythm is the cor anglais's rhythm. It lies between the cor anglais (D♯4-B4) and the bass, the lower-middle register right under the tune. At 99-100 the two real lines move in contrary motion: Vln I falls F♯5-D♯5-B4, A5-F♯5-D♯5, and the cor anglais rises D♯4-F♯4-A4, F♯4-A4-C5 into c532's V♭9. The violas' static B3 crotchets fill the space between them with a third pulse. The violas also play straight on into the f hemiola at 101, so of the strings only Vln II enters fresh there.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12: every note must make "a legitimate difference to the score as a whole"; otherwise "it should be erased". PDF 12-13, Ex. 1 (image read): Sebesky cut one inner line from a soft background over a pedal. "The contrary motion of the remaining voices became more evident" and "the lower-middle register was lightened", so the focal element could "dominate a little more noticeably". The case here is the same: a soft background over a pedal, one inner part that adds no pitch and no rhythm, and two moving lines in contrary motion. PDF 13: the ear must go to the primary element (Vln I's head). PDF 11: hold a colour back so that its entry counts. The violas would then enter f with Vln II at 101 (c537's reason for Vln II, Adler PDF 213).

Answer to the Bizet point (Adler PDF 213): Bizet rhythmicises the pedal in a horn because the flutes leave beat 2 empty. Here no beat is empty. The cor anglais plays every beat, the pizz. bass marks every downbeat to 99, and the timpani play motive x on B in 97-100.

c532 described bar 100 as V♭9 "over the violas' and timpani's B". The timpani's B2 (motive x, mf) is still the lowest note at 100, so the chord stays in root position with the ninth C5 in the cor anglais, resolving to B4 at 101.

**Fix (applied, sB.ily `sBVla`).** Bars 95-100 become rests. The violas play the head at 89-91 and rest 92-100, then enter f at 101 as before.

### 2. Theme A at 13-28 and 253-268: same bass and the same off-beat harmony. Not changed.

Sebesky PDF 19 asks for a different harmonisation of a returning melody "whenever possible". The reprise is varied by counterpoint instead: the cellos' tenor countermelody (T's head in E minor, c539), Fl 1's heterophony from 261, Hn 3 from 261, and pizz. instead of arco off-beats. A new bass under theme A would change the harmony that movement I (bar 79) and the finale (273-275) quote with bar 2 at pitch, and the scherzo's da capo is meant to be heard as a return (Dvořák 9/iii repeats its scherzo harmony). The reading says "whenever possible", and here the return is the point. No change.

### 3. Bars 261-268: Fl 1's heterophony lies above Vln I's theme. Checked, kept.

On focus (PDF 13) the flute is the highest and busiest line. It is the same tune in decorated form, not new material, so it does not divide attention between two ideas. It enters only at 261, after Vln I has stated the head alone (c549), and is marked a level under the violins. Kept.

### 4. Bars 329-332: the strings play motive x in block chords. Checked, kept.

The stretto entries 325-328 rise from the cellos to Vln I. At 329 all four sections join in one rhythm under the woodwinds' long chromatic tail. Sebesky PDF 14 allows the whole ensemble to become the focus for a time. Here it does so for four bars of crescendo into 333. Kept.

### 5. Bars 105-120: the lower octave of the tutti theme. Checked, kept.

C.a. and Cl 2 play the theme at E4, the violas play off-beat B3/G4, and the tenor countermelody (Bsn 1, Hn 3·4, Vc) moves in minims. Each has its own rhythm and job, so this is not Ex. 1's redundant line. c067's "no strings on the lower octave" also holds. Kept.

### 6. Bars 169-184: Vln II's quavers con sord. over the cellos' T. Checked, kept.

The quavers are an octave or more above the tenor tune and are in a different rhythm. They are the moving fill of Ex. 2A (PDF 18), not a competing line. Kept.
