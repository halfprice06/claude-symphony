## Findings

### 1. Two f timpani strokes ring into chords they clash with: 200 → 201 and 287 → 289. Applied.
- **Bars.** 200 (G♯2 f, last stroke of the trio's middle section), 287 (E2 f after the reprise's cadence roll).
- **Problem.** Both are crotchets followed by rests, with no damping sign. A forte stroke on a 28" or 30" drum rings for two to three seconds if it is not damped. A bar lasts 0.79 s at the trio tempo and 0.68 s in the scherzo. At 200 the G♯2 therefore sounds on into 201–203. The cellos and bassoon move up to A2 there, a semitone above it in the same octave, and the chord is A major, played p. At 287 the E2 rings through the general silence of 288 (beats 2–3) and into the cellos' pp entry in G minor at 289. There E sounds against their B♭3, a tritone. In both places it is the only note left over. The exposition already handles the same spot correctly: the timpani's G2 at 56, the stroke before the strings-only fugato at 57, is written `g,4-.\f`, which tells the player to damp it. The reprise's matching stroke at 287 has no such mark.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 58 calls a timpani note that sounds against the harmony a fault: Strauss's Beethoven and Verdi cases, "neither Strauss nor any of us today would advocate this practice." Adler PDF 80 says the timpani part "must be carefully prepared to show… the exact duration of each note; for instance… how long a note may ring." Adler PDF 141 warns against percussion that obscures detail, and here the detail is the cellos' soft fugato entry. The protection on the stroke at 200 (c146, c159: "the trio's one structural arrival") is about the stroke itself, and the stroke stays. The reason recorded for its pitch (c006: V of C♯ minor going to C♯ minor at 201) no longer holds, because 201 is now A major (c070, c177). So the stroke's ring has to end at 201.
- **Fix (applied).** tB.ily, tBTimp: `gis,4\f r4 r4` → `gis,4-.\f r4 r4`. dA.ily, dATimp: `b,2.:32 | e,4 r4 r4 | R2. |` → `b,2.:32 | e,4-. r4 r4 | R2. |`. This is the same sign the score uses at 56. A staccato dot on a timpani crotchet before rests tells the player to damp. Each variable has a c416 comment. Pitches, rhythms and dynamics are unchanged. The orchestra holds both chords to the bar line, so the damped stroke's attack still marks the cadence.

### 2. The ff octave at 339 rings through the G.P., and the E at 323 rings into V. Applied.
- **Bars.** 339 (`<e, e>4->` ff, then G.P. with fermata at 340, MIDI tempo 96 crotchets, about two seconds of silence), 323 (motive x E–E–E–E mf, then the cellos' B-major arpeggio alone at 324).
- **Problem.** At 339 the strings and woodwinds play staccato crotchets and the brass hold to the bar line with f>. c228 and c328 describe the timpani here as "short", but nothing in the part says so. Played as written, the octave E2+E3 struck ff rings through the whole general pause. The pause then sounds as a decaying timpani chord rather than as silence, and Meno mosso at 341 starts while the drum is still sounding. At 323 the last E2 rings into 324, where the cellos alone outline B–F♯–D♯. E sounds against D♯ in the bar that prepares the coda's E-minor entry.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80 (how long a note may ring), PDF 58 (no out-of-harmony timpani pitch), and PDF 55/84/141 (a loud drum covers everything else, which here is the silence).
- **Fix (applied).** coda.ily: `<e, e>4-> r4 r4` → `<e, e>4-.-> r4 r4`. dC.ily: `e,4\mf e,8 e,8 e,4 | R2. |` → `e,4\mf e,8 e,8 e,4-. | R2. |`. Each has a c416 comment. The dots print clearly on page 12 (checked at 150 dpi). The accent at 339 stays.

### Largest-change test
The brief asks for the change a listener would hear most. Of the candidates this reading supports, the two semitone and tritone clashes in finding 1 are the loudest faults in the movement's timpani writing: a forte drum sounding a minor second against the bass, and a ringing E under a solo pp entry. They are also the cheapest to fix. I tested two larger ideas and rejected both:
- A *Fêtes*-style ppp timpani ostinato on E under the coda's T (341–356, PDF 126). Rejected: c414 protects strings alone at 341–356. It would also put the drum under T's A-major first half, where E is only the fifth of the chord, and it would use up the closing motive x (363–371) before its quiet return.
- Thinning the reprise tutti's timpani (305–320) so that the coda's entry stands out, as Brahms 2/iv does (PDF 135). Rejected for c326's reason, which still holds: c034 already holds the low brass back for the coda, and c326's E3 octave gives the coda's peak a timpani sound the reprise does not have.
