## Findings

### 1. Hn 4's crotchet rests at 166 sit half a space off the stave grid (applied)

- **Bars:** 166, Hn 3·4 staff (tA.ily, `tAHnFour`).
- **Problem:** Hn 4 leaves its E3 drone on beat 2 of 166 (c151) while Hn 3 holds B3. The part combiner pushes Hn 4's two crotchet rests below the stave to make room. It places them at staff-position −13, 6.5 spaces below the centre line. Every other displaced rest in the score moves by whole spaces.
- **Evidence:** Gould PDF 49: "From the centre of the stave, rests move an exact number of stave-spaces up or down, so that they remain correctly positioned in relation to the stave-lines". The "and not" example shows rests set half a space off as wrong. PDF 51: a rest outside the stave goes at the level of the surrounding pitches. Here those pitches are E3 at −11 and B3 at −7. The probe found −6.5 only at this one place, out of 1,416 rests.
- **Fix (applied):** `e4 \tag #'score \override Rest.staff-position = #-12 r4 r4 \tag #'score \revert Rest.staff-position |`. This uses the score-only tag, as at 132/301 (`sCOboeTwo`, `dCOboeTwo`). The rests now sit 6 spaces down, beside the E3 notehead (checked at 220 dpi). The change is print only, and the parts are unchanged.

### 2. Cellos' E4/F♯4 at 31 and 35–36 stay in bass clef (c459's open point closed, no change)

- **Bars:** 31, 35–36 (sA.ily, `sAVc`).
- **Point:** c459 left open whether the cellos' F♯4 should go into tenor clef. Each of these bars touches two ledger lines once, and the next notes fall to B2 (31–32) and F♯3 (36). In tenor clef those lower notes would need their own ledger lines below the stave, so the clef change would only move the ledger lines from the top to the bottom and add two clef changes per phrase.
- **Evidence:** Gould PDF 46 sets the threshold for leaving the stave's octave at three or more ledger lines. PDF 47 says players read at least four ledger lines at a glance and that isolated notes should be written with ledger lines. The PDF 46 example ("Cello: bass clef, and not treble with 8") argues for the clef that keeps the figure on the stave, and for this figure that is bass clef. Decision: keep bass clef. Record c459's open point as closed.

### 3. The change a listener would hear most under this reading: Ob 1 kept in one octave through the E-minor cadence 125–132 (considered, not kept)

- **Bars:** 128→129 and 284→285 (sC.ily and dA.ily, `OboeOne`).
- **Candidate:** Gould PDF 47–48 asks that a phrase be kept at one octave, so that its contour stays clear. The only melodic line where a doubling instrument changes octave inside a slurred phrase is Ob 1. It doubles Vln II's lower octave at 125–128, then leaps B4→B5 on the downbeat of 129 to join Fl 1 and Vln I. The alternative is to keep Ob 1 on the lower octave through 131 (`b'4( cis''8 b' a'4 | g'2 fis'4~ | fis'4 e'2)`). The listener would hear this, because the oboe colour would stay with Vln II instead of moving to the top line at the forte.
- **Why it makes the music worse:** Gould himself allows the change where it happens. PDF 47 says to change octave between phrases, or else between beats. 129 is where the second idea's cadential phrase begins (c451), and the change falls on the downbeat on the repeated B, which is the least obscuring place PDF 47 shows ("recommended"). The alternative would also take the oboe down to E4 at 132/288 at f. c214 removed that note as the oboe's coarse lowest fifth (Adler II PDF 151), and c131 put the oboe on the top line at 129–131/285–287 (kept by c261). It would also take the oboe off the hemiola's top line just when the tutti reaches f. Decision: no change.
