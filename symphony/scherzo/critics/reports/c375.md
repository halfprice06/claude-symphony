## Findings

### 1. Beamed stems in the shared staves are ¾ to 1 space shorter than their unbeamed neighbours (applied)

**Bars.** Bn 1 at 71 and 299 (`a4-. e8-. e-. a4-.`, E3 in a space, up-stems); Hn 2 at 71 and 299 (E4 on the bottom line, down-stems); Bn 2 at 105 and 305 (B2, down-stems); Cl 1 at 246 (B4 pair above Cl 2); Fl 1 at 333 (B5 pair) and Ob 1/Cl 1 at 334 (the coda's motive x in shared staves). There are nine beams in all.

**Problem.** In each of these bars the player shares a staff with a second voice, so the stems have a set direction that points away from the stave centre. LilyPond shortens such beamed stems to 2.56–2.74 spaces. Since c365, the unbeamed stems in the same staves are 3½ spaces while the open-end note is inside the stave. So bar 71 in the bassoons reads crotchet (3½), quaver pair (2.56), crotchet (3½). The beam sits on the top line, about a space below the stem ends on either side. Motive x looks broken into a short middle and two long ends (compare /tmp/critic-375/bn71-work-03.png and bn71-probe-03.png).

**Reading.** Gould PDF 32: "The stem length (including the width of the outer beam) should be as close to 3½ spaces (normal stem length) as possible", and PDF 33: 3¼ for a note on a line. PDF 35 allows a shorter stem only for an angled beam (3 inside the stave, 2½ outside). PDF 33 sets 2½ as the absolute floor ("Beams should never be closer to the notehead than ... 2½ spaces"); it is not the normal length. c105 kept these stems on the do-not-touch list because PDF 28 allows 2½ in double-stemmed writing. c365 then read PDF 28–29 more closely: stems are shortened only "as stems fall further outside the stave". The score now follows that reading for every unbeamed stem. The c105 reason therefore no longer holds, and the beamed stems are the only ones left that do not follow it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-375/work, beams.ily only).** The existing callback is renamed `c105-beam-positions`, and `gould-ledger-beam-positions` (the name score.ly, parts.ly and parts-gen.py already use) wraps it. For a group whose stems all point away from the stave centre, the wrapper gives the shortest stem at least the c365 length: 3½ for a note in a space inside the stave, 3¼ on a line, 3 for an angled beam, and outside the stave 3½ less ¼ per staff position, never under 2½. The beam only moves outward. An end that would then land inside the stave moves on to the next line attachment (PDF 31, 34). All other groups (single voices, ledger groups, stems pointing toward the centre) are returned unchanged. The core:

```scheme
#(define (c375-target p sloped)
  (let ((t (if (<= p 4) (if (even? p) 3.25 3.5) (max 2.5 (- 3.5 (* 0.25 (- p 4)))))))
    (if sloped (min t 3.0) t)))
;; in gould-ledger-beam-positions: deficit = max over stems of (target - (note - beam + 0.24));
;; if every stem points away from the centre and deficit > 0.15, move both ends outward by deficit (then to an attachment if inside the stave)
```

**Result.** The probe log shows exactly nine beams changed and no others. Bn 1 71/299 went from 2.824 to 3.76 (3½-space stems), Hn 2 71/299 and Cl 1 246 from 4.5 to 5.01 (3¼, line notes), Bn 2 105/305 from −5.82 to −6.01 and Fl 1 333 from 5.82 to 6.01 (2¾, three positions outside the stave), and Ob 1/Cl 1 334 to 5.01. Fl 1 334, five positions above the stave, keeps its 2.56 because its target there is 2½. The bassoon pair at 71 now has stems the length of its neighbours, with the beam above the stave as the crotchet stems are. System and page breaks are unchanged: the last line of every page is the same text as in v375, and the score is still 13 pages. The parts include beams.ily, rebuild with 0 warnings and keep every page count.

This finding replaces part of c105's do-not-touch item ("stem lengths in shared two-voice staves") for beamed stems only, for the reason given above. It does not change c365's callback or c285's direction pass in stems.ily.
