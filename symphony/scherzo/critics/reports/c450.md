## Findings

### 1. The largest change this reading supports: pass the closing G♯5 from Fl 1 straight to Vln I at 365 (built, rejected)

- **Candidate.** Rimsky PDF 125–126 says that in a transfer the last note of one part coincides with the first note of the next. PDF 127 says a diminuendo removes groups; it does not add them. At the close, the upper G♯5 is in Fl 1 at 361–364 and stops on 364 beat 2. It is silent at 365–368 and returns in Vln I at 369, ppp, inside the closing diminuendo. This passage carries more cyclic weight than any other: Vln I's G♯5 is the note the finale takes as A♭5 at IV 1 and recalls at IV 243. Both rules point to one change: tie Fl 1 into 365 beat 1 and bring Vln I in at 365 on the common G♯5. Then the upper octave never breaks, and nothing new enters after 365 except the timpani strokes and the chord at 375.
- **Built** in /tmp/critic-450/cand/w (coda.ily). Fl 1 `gis''2.~\pp | gis''2.~\> | gis''2.~ | gis''2.~ | gis''4\! r4 r4 | R2.*11`. Vln I `R2.*8 |` then twelve bars of `gis''2.~` from 365. Results: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, themecheck 51/52 (lastGsharpVln VlnOne 369 CHANGED).
- **Why it is worse.**
  - It removes the only four bars of the close where G♯4 sounds in the horn alone (Hn 3 365, Hn 1 from 368). The close is then no longer bars 1–6 reversed. The opening goes from the pair (Hn 1 + Vln I, 1–2) to the horn alone (3–6); the close goes from the horn alone (365–368) to the pair (369–376). That reason was recorded at c440, and I hear it as stronger than the transfer rule.
  - Rimsky's transfer is for "passages the range of which is too wide to be performed on any one instrument, or when it is desired to divide a phrase into two different timbres" (PDF 126). A held pitch with a planned four-bar rest is neither.
  - His own note on PDF 127 allows a break in the progression of parts where a contrast of timbre is intended.
  - Moving the entry to 365 stacks it on the Hn 3 → Hn 1 hand-over (365, 368). It also blurs the one fresh string entry that the finale continues in the same player.
  - It changes a recorded cyclic line, so the finale would need a message for a loss, not a gain.
- **Decision.** Not applied. The do-not-touch reason (c440) stands, and this reading does not give a stronger one.
