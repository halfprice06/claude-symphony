## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The reprise (265–340) repeats the modulation to G and never brings the second idea back in E. Applied.

**Problem.** The scherzo 13–140 is a complete sonata-shaped design: E minor, transition to G (37), second idea in G (45–56), development (57–104), theme A in E (105), second idea in E (121–132), close. The reprise is that whole design again, compressed: theme A 265–280 (= 13–28), counterstatement 281–288 ending on V7 of G (285–288 = 41–44), second idea in G (289–300 = 45–56), shortened development (301–316), theme A tutti (317–332 = 105–120), close (333–340 = 133–140). The compression cut 121–132, the one passage that resolved the second idea into the tonic, and kept 41–56, the passage that leaves it. So the listener's last hearing of the second idea is in III. A&S 220 (§11) and 225–226 tie departure and return to the form; Dvořák 9/iii's return (corpus) stays in e/E and drops its transition. The length is right: 76 bars is 0.59 of the 128-bar scherzo, the same ratio as Dvořák's 0.60. The content is the problem.

**A splice error confirms it.** At 283 the winds end their E-minor chord on beat 1 (Ob 1 e'', Cl 1 g', Cl 2 e', Bsn 1 b) while the strings enter on D (Vc and Cb d, Vln I fis''). The downbeat sounds D–E–F♯–G–B.

**Fix (applied in dA.ily, length unchanged).**
- 283–284: the strings answer in E minor and go to V, as at 31–32 (sA bars 19–20 of the strings, Vln I `e''4-.\p b'8-. b'-. g'4-. | fis'4-. dis'-. b-.`, Vc `e,4\p r4 r4 | b,4 r4 r4`). This removes the clash.
- 285–288: 41–44 a minor third lower, so it becomes V7 of E with the 7th A in place (A&S 220 §10). Every sounding part is wrapped as `\transpose d b, { a''4-.\f d''8-. d''-. a''4-. | c'''2->( b''8 bes''8 | a''4-.) fis''8-. fis''-. d''4-. | a'4-. fis'-. d'-. }`. In Fl 1 the last bar is `a''4-. r4 r4` inside the block, which avoids a B3 below the flute's range and an octave arpeggio against Vln I.
- 289–300: the second idea in E minor, copied from 121–132 (sC bars 17–28) for every part, f. Cbsn, Cl, Bsn, Vln II and Vla get `\f`. Vc and Cb get `\f^\markup \italic "arco"`.
- 301: the fugato in G minor now follows an E-minor cadence. The bare cello entry is a phrase modulation of the kind A&S 223 describes (contrast of texture instead of a pivot), so it needs no preparation.

The G-major second idea is now heard once, at 45. The do-not-touch hemiola cadence 129–131 is copied unchanged. Cycle 2's added C♯ at 293 belonged to the G-major version and is replaced by the voicing of 125, whose A-minor chord already has its third.

### 2. The trio's middle part (185–216) does not earn 32 bars; cut 189–196. Applied.

**Problem.** One 4-bar unit (half–quarter, half–quarter, dotted quarter–eighth–quarter, dotted half: the rhythm of T itself) is stated five times in 24 bars: Ob 1 at 185, the cellos' literal echo at 189, Ob 1 + cor anglais a step lower at 193, the cellos' echo at 197, and Vln I/II with Ob 1 at 205. The only new line is the rising build at 201–204. A&S 227 asks for "balance among rhythmic groups", and A&S 216 (§6) says a key area needs "a change in motivic design" and a cadence (A&S 219 §9). The B-major block 193–200 has neither: same motive, no cadence, and it ends on V of B before F♯ minor at 201. In time, the trio (96 bars at 2. = 76, about 76 s) is 0.87 of the scherzo 13–140 (128 bars at 2. = 88, about 87 s). Dvořák's trio is 0.58.

**Fix (applied in tB.ily, sections.txt `tB 24 3`, global.ily `s2.*24` after mark L).** Delete 189–196, i.e. the cellos' first echo and the Ob + CA sequence. The cellos' B-major statement (old 197–200) now answers the oboe directly, a step lower. That is a real answer with a change of key, not an echo. The cor anglais doubles it an octave up (`dis'2(\p cis'4 | b2 fis4) | gis4.( a8 b4) | fis2.`), so the CA keeps its only entry in the movement. Vc gets `\p` there. The join 188 → old 197 (G♯ major → B) is the join v03 already had at 192 → 193. The trio becomes 32 + 24 + 32 = 88 bars (about 69 s, 0.80 of the scherzo). The movement drops from 285 s to 278 s in the MIDI. That is still close to the planned 5 minutes, so I cut nothing else. This also removes the analyser's octave parallel at 194. **Every bar from 197 on moves 8 bars earlier in the scratch build:** the motto tail 373–376 becomes 365–368, B6 at 352 becomes 344, and the last G♯ 385–392 becomes 377–384. HISTORY's bar references need the same shift.

### 3. The trio's return is prepared by a plain B triad (213–216). Add the 7th. Applied.

**Problem.** B was a key area at 193–200 (with A♯). At 213–216 it returns as four bars of B–D♯–F♯ with A only as a passing note in T's head. A&S 220 (§10): after V has been a key, the way to make it sound like the dominant again is to add the minor 7th, the 4 of the main key, "the one scale degree in the tonic key that does not appear in the key of the dominant".

**Fix (applied).** In Fl 1 and Cl 1, the last bar of tB becomes `b''2 a''4)` and `b'2 a'4)` (replacing `b''2) r4` and `b'2) r4`). The 7th falls a step to G♯, T's first note at 217, so the return arrives on 3 over V7 → I.

### 4. Vln II stays pizzicato from 265 to the end. Applied.

**Problem.** dA marks Vln II "pizz." at 265 and no "arco" follows anywhere in dA, dB, dC or coda. By the notation, the legato second idea (289–300), the fugato stretto (309–312), the hemiola (313–316), the ff tutti 317 and the coda's sustained pp chords are all plucked.

**Fix (applied).** `r4 c''4-.\f^\markup \italic "arco" c''4-.` at 285 (inside the transposed block, so it sounds a'). The pizzicato off-beats at 283–284 lose their staccato dots, matching 265–280.

### 5. Hn 1 plays T's head at 241 exactly as it will at the coda (357). Applied.

**Problem.** The codetta passes T's head from Ob 1 (233) to Cl 1 (237) to Hn 1 (241: `gis'2( a'4 | b'2.~ | b'2.~ | b'2)`). At 357 the coda opens with Hn 1 dolce on the same notes, in the same key, after the same E pedal. A&S 220 describes a coda as "a varied repetition of the final cadence". The coda's main colour has already been used, so its first bars sound like a repeat of 241.

**Fix (applied in tC.ily).** Give 241–244 to Bsn 1 an octave lower (`gis2(\pp a4 | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2) r4`) and rest Hn 1. The relay now falls by octaves (Ob G♯5, Cl G♯4, Bsn G♯3), and Hn 1's T at 357 is the horn's first statement of the tune.

### 6. The reprise's tutti (317–340) and the coda's ff (349–355) stand 32 bars apart. Not changed.

I checked whether 317–340 should be cut now that the second idea returns in E at 289. I decided to keep it. Without 317 the reprise would have no tonic arrival after the fugato's V7 (313–316), which A&S 220 §10 sets up. The coda R then has a reason to start pp. The cost is two ff statements of theme A's head. That is a smaller fault than an unresolved V7.
