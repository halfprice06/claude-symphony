## Findings

### 1. Harp bass strokes on a different pitch from the basses, inside the lowest octave (151; 214–215). Applied.

**Problem.** In the trio the harp LH doubles the bass note for note, so harp and pizzicato basses sound as one attack (153–168). Two strokes break this rule. At 151, the end of the link, Vc, Cb and timpani play B, but the harp LH strikes E2, a fourth above the basses' B1. At 214–215, the forte return, the basses and contrabassoon have c157's A♯ (V6/5 of V), but the harp LH strikes C♯2, a minor third above the basses' A♯1. In both places the harp, a bass instrument here, adds a second bass note with a strong attack in the octave where intervals blur most.

**Evidence.** The harmonic series puts the octave and fifth at the bottom and thirds only from the fifth partial up (Casanelles PDF 264, 274). A minor third at A♯1–C♯2 and a fourth at B1–E2 lie far below any partial of the bass. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85 and Adler PDF 150–151 say the same for chord spacing. A struck note is mostly attack (PDF 265–266), so the harp's wrong pitch is heard at the start of each bar.

**Fix (applied).** link.ily, `linkHarpL`: `... | e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2. | b,,4 r4 r4 | R2. |` (was `e,4` at 151; B♮ is in the link's pedal setting). tC.ily, `tCHarpL`: `| a,,4 r4_\markup \small "A♯" r4 | ais,,4 r4 r4 | ais,,4 r4 r4 | b,,4 r4_\markup \small "A♮" r4 | cis,4 ...` (was `cis,4 | cis,4`). The A pedal moves after 213's stroke and returns after 216's, well before the next A♮ at 220. The RH rests throughout 209–224, so the change is free. c157's A♯ is now in the bass only, at the bass's own pitch, as it intended (c093).

### 2. The trio return's cadence chord has no fifth and a third a major third above the bass (223–224). Applied.

**Problem.** At 223 the third statement of T arrives on E. The chord is E2 (Cb, Cbsn, harp), G♯2 (Vc, Bsn 2), G♯3 (Bsn 1), E4 and G♯4 (Hn 1·2), E5 (Vln II, Cl 1) and E6 (Vln I, Fl 1). G♯ is in three octaves, one of them a major third above the bass at E2–G♯2, and B is in no part. This is the only tonic arrival in the movement spaced like this. The chord is held two bars mf with a diminuendo, so it is exposed. The first statement's cadence (167–168) has the Hn 3·4 fifth E3–B3 over the bass. The second (183–184, in B) has the cellos a tenth above the bass with the fifth in the violas.

**Evidence.** The series on E2 is E2, E3, B3, E4, G♯4: the fifth comes before the third, and the third belongs in the fourth octave (Casanelles PDF 264, 274). Under instrumental synthesis (PDF 259–260), a chord spaced on the series fuses into one sound, and this one does not. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85: harmonic notes must not be lacking.

**Fix (applied).** tC.ily. The cellos and Bsn 2 end their countermelody on B2, the fifth over E2, instead of falling to G♯2:
```
tCVc:     ... b,2(\mf cis4 | a,4 b,2) | b,2.~\upbow\> | b,2 r4 | ...        (was gis,2.~ | gis,2)
tCBsnTwo: ... b,2(\mf cis4 | a,4 b,2~ | b,2.~\> | b,2)\! r4 | ...         (was b,2 | gis,2.~ | gis,2)
```
Bsn 1 still resolves 222's seventh A3 to G♯3, and G♯ stays in Bsn 1, Hn 1 and the violas. No line moves, so no new parallels appear (analysis.txt shows the same 7). The cellos keep c157's dynamics and c096's up-bow.

### 3. Horns f inside the second idea's lower octave at its cadence (129–131, 285–287). Applied.

**Problem.** At 129 the E-minor second idea reaches f: Fl 1/Vln I B5, and the lower octave in Ob 1 and Vln II from B4 (B4–G4–E4 | F♯4–D♯4 | F♯4–E4). Hn 3 enters at 129 on B4, f, on the tune's first lower-octave note. Hn 2 crescendos to f on G4 and moves to F♯4 at 130, the tune's next lower-octave note. So for three bars two forte horns sit on and around the oboe's and second violins' line in the same band. The reprise has the same thing at 285, where Hn 3 is f on B4 (Hn 2 there carries the tenor tune itself, c071). This is exactly what c084 removed at 45 ("the oboe disappears into the horn"). Its reason for leaving 121/277 was the sub. p, and that reason does not cover 129 or 285, where the horns reach f.

**Evidence.** Mahler marks the layer behind a level lower, for the result heard (Casanelles PDF 271–272). Forte brass have the densest spectrum, and "harmonically denser instruments tend to become foregrounded" (PDF 265, 289). Same-band sounds mask each other, and a composer may keep brass out of a woodwind line's band (PDF 276–277). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 108, 112: mark the woodwind a degree louder than forte horns.

**Fix (applied).** sC.ily: `sCHnTwo ... | b4 e'2) | g'2.--(\mf | ...` (the crescendo from 125 now ends at mf); `sCHnThree ... | R2.*8 | b'2.--(\mf | a'2.--~ | ...`. dA.ily: `dAHnThree ... | R2.*4 | b'2.--(\mf | a'2.--~ | ...`. Hn 1 and Hn 4 rest at both places, so each shared staff has one dynamic. The horns still crescendo with the tune (sub. p → mf), one level under it, which is the movement's rule since c084 and c099.

### 4. Violas' held chords at the tune's own level in the coda's last statement of T (349–356). Applied.

**Problem.** At 349 T returns in the violins (pp < p) with Cl 1. The violas, div., hold E4–G♯4/B4/A4 chords pp, level with the tune and a third or fourth under Vln II's lower octave. Four bars earlier, under Ob 1's p, the score marks the same violas ppp (345). With the tune now at pp, the pad should drop with it.

**Evidence.** PDF 271–272 (mark the background for the result heard) and PDF 270 (the pad as a smooth layer beneath). The c099 rule: accompaniment one level under the tune. The coda's statements recede (PDF 272–274), and this one is the furthest.

**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, `codaVla`: `<e' gis'>2.\ppp^\markup \italic "div."` at 349 (was `\pp`). The violas return to pp at 357 under Hn 1's p espress. (c058, unchanged.)
