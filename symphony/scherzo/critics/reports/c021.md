## Findings

### 1. The trio's middle part repeats T's rhythm until T has no profile left (185–200). Applied.
- **Problem.** T's 4-bar rhythm is minim-crotchet | minim-crotchet | dotted crotchet-quaver-crotchet | crotchet-minim (or dotted minim).
  - T's first and second phrases use it at 153 and 157, and its third phrase starts with it at 161.
  - In the trio's middle part, all four 4-bar units repeat it: Ob 1 at 185 (`e''2 dis''4 | cis''2 gis'4 | a'4. b'8 cis''4 | gis'2.`), cor anglais and cellos at 189, Vln I·II at 193, and Ob 1 with Vln I·II at 197.
  - T's head returns in that rhythm at 201, and T itself at 209.
  - Out of 14 four-bar units from 153 to 208, 11 have the same rhythm. When the tune returns at 209, nothing has contrasted with it, so the return does not register as a return.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky chooses the solo wind for the character of each melody (PDF 64–65). The solo melodies he cites as typical each have a rhythmic figure of their own: the bassoon's turns in Ex. 40 (PDF 218), the cor anglais' dotted figure in Ex. 44 (PDF 221), and the horn's quaver figures over the flute in Ex. 73 (PDF 245).
  - He also says a solo line is chosen for "diversity of expression" (PDF 66).
  - Master trios use the middle part for rhythmic contrast. In Dvořák 9/iii the trio's second strain moves in quavers against the first strain's crotchets.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-021/work/sections/tB.ily).** The first bar of each call becomes a quaver turn. The second to fourth bars are unchanged, and Vln I·II at 193 keep T's rhythm as the link back to T. All passing notes lie inside C♯ minor (185, 197) and B major (189).
  ```
  tBOboeOne:   e''4(\p dis''8 cis''8 dis''8 e''8 | cis''2 gis'4) ...      % 185
               ... e''4(\f dis''8 cis''8 dis''8 e''8 | cis''2 gis'4) ...  % 197
  tBCorAnglais and tBVc, 189: dis'4(\p cis'8 b8 cis'8 dis'8 | b2 fis4) ...
  tBVlnOne, 197: e'''4(\f dis'''8 cis'''8 dis'''8 e'''8 | cis'''2 gis''4) ...
  tBVlnTwo, 197: e''4(\f dis''8 cis''8 dis''8 e''8 | cis''2 gis'4) ...
  ```
  The figure comes from Fl 1's heterophonic turns at 169–184, so it is not foreign to the trio. No recorded theme changes.

### 2. The ff tutti statements of theme A have a weak lower octave on the clarinet's throat notes (105–120, 309–324). Applied.
- **Problem.** The melody lies in one octave (E5–C6) in Fl 1, Fl 2, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II. The only lower octave is Cl 2 (E4–C5).
  - On the B♭ clarinet, the chromatic tail G4–F♯4–F4 (106, 118, 310, 322) and the held G4s and A4s are throat notes, the weakest notes on the instrument.
  - These notes play against horns on E5, trumpets, trombones and the cellos' countermelody, all forte.
  - An upper octave cannot be added: it would put the 12th bar's C6 on C7 (kept for the finale) and the run-up to it on B6 (kept for 344).
  - So this climactic return of theme A is in effect a one-octave unison, which is thinner than the coda's two-octave head at 341.
  - The cor anglais rests through both tuttis.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky says the clarinet is "weak in the middle compass" (PDF 66).
  - Melody in octaves should use pairs in natural order, and "instruments of the same branch playing in octaves, e.g. Ob. / Eng. horn ... always produce a good effect" (PDF 68).
  - The cor anglais belongs with the oboe and violas by register (PDF 76).
  - Ob 1 (upper octave) over the cor anglais (lower octave) is exactly his Ob/Eng. horn pair. In his own octave doublings of a melody below an upper line (Ex. 90, PDF 265, 2 Cl over cellos + 2 horns), the lower octave is never left to a single middle-register clarinet in a forte.
- **Fix (applied in sC.ily and dC.ily).** The cor anglais doubles Cl 2's line with the same articulation, ff, for 16 bars and then rests as before:
  ```
  sCCorAnglais = { e'4-.\ff b8-. b-. e'4-. | g'2->( fis'8 f'8 | e'4-.) b8-. b-. g4-. | fis'4-. dis'-. b-. | ...
                   (Cl 2's bars 105–120) ... | e'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*12 | e'2.\p ... }
  dCCorAnglais: the same 16 bars at 309, then the existing rests and the p at 325.
  ```
  The lowest note, G3 (107, 311), is within the cor anglais' range (E3). It has rests at 112 and 120 to breathe, and it is free from 121 until its p entry at 133.

### 3. The clarinets should be in A, for the trio tune and for an E-minor/E-major movement (staff label). Applied.
- **Problem.** T starts on G♯4 in Cl 1 (153, 209), as a minim. It returns as a minim at bar 4 of the tune (156) and as a crotchet at bar 13 (165).
  - On a B♭ clarinet, G♯4 is written A♯4, the throat B♭, the dullest note on the instrument.
  - The tune's first slur, G♯4–A4–B4, is written A♯4–B4–C♯5, across the break, at p dolce on the first notes of the trio.
  - In E major the B♭ clarinets also read in F♯ major (six sharps).
  - On a clarinet in A the same sounds are written B4–C5–D5: all in the clarion, with no break, in G major (one sharp).
- **Evidence.**
  - The weak middle compass (Rimsky PDF 66, as in finding 2). A solo for an "expressive melody" is chosen for the instrument's best qualities (PDF 64).
  - Dvořák's E-minor scherzo, this movement's named model, has "Clarinet I II in A" (corpus file above, line 40).
  - The A clarinet's lowest sounding note is C♯3, and this movement's clarinet range is G3–C♯6, so nothing falls out of range.
- **Fix (applied in score.ly; the score is in C, so no notes change).**
  ```
  instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Clarinets 1·2" \small "in A" }
  shortInstrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Cl. 1·2" \small "(A)" }
  ```
  The bass clarinet stays in B♭. The players change instruments in the pause after movement II, so no muta is needed.
