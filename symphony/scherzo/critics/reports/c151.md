## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 166: T's first cadential 4-3 does not relax, because Hn 4's drone keeps the suspended E (applied)

**Problem.** T's last phrase ends on c061's 4-3: Cl 1 holds E5 into the V7 bar and resolves to D♯5 on beat 2. In the first statement, Hn 3·4 hold the musette drone E3/B3 straight through bars 165–168. The pizzicato B1 (basses, harp) is struck only on the downbeat. The harp's A3–B3–D♯4 enters on beat 2. So on beats 2–3 of 166 the sounding notes are E3 (Hn 4), A3, B3, D♯4 (harp), B3 (Hn 3) and D♯5 (Cl 1). The lowest sustained sound is the suspended E. The clarinet resolves, but the E still sounds two octaves below, a major 7th under the harp's D♯4. The V7 never clears, and the leading tone the cadence exists to stress (PDF 350) arrives over the tonic. The other three statements of the same cadence have no E left on beat 2 (MIDI listing): 182 has C♯3, F♯3, C♯4 and A♯; 222 has B, A, F♯ and D♯; 355 has B, A3, F♯4 and D♯. Only the first, pp statement, where the texture is thinnest and the cadence is heard for the first time, loses its release.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 348, sec. 3: a dissonant suspension resolves down by step so that "its resolution should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity".
- PDF 352, sec. 12: doubling the wrong tone at a resolution "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution".
- PDF 368, sec. 28: chords may be dissonant against a pedal, but "dissonances among the upper parts themselves … must be prepared and resolved normally". Here the bass has moved to B (165–166), so the drone is no longer the bass. It is an upper part whose E needs a resolution.
- PDF 361, sec. 22 (Ex. 21-29): when the resolution tone already sounds in another register, the resolution may be left out. D♯ sounds in Cl 1 and the harp, so Hn 4 can simply stop.
- Inside the phrases (155, 160) the drone stays. There it acts as a pedal under passing dominants (PDF 367, Ex. 21-41), and the musette effect depends on it.
- Dvořák 9/iii, 75: the cellos' E figure gives way in the cadence bar and comes back at the tonic.

**Fix (applied, tA.ily, Hn 4 only).**
```
% was: ... | e2.~ | e2.~ | e2.~ | e2 r4 | R2.*16 | }
tAHnFour = { ... | e2.~ | e4 r4 r4 | e2.~ | e2 r4 | R2.*16 | }
```
Hn 4 holds E3 through the suspension on beat 1, rests on beats 2–3 while the clarinet and harp resolve, and takes up the drone again on the tonic at 167, as the pizzicato E does. Hn 3's B3 (the root of V7) continues, so the drone sound stays. No dynamic is needed: Hn 3·4 are pp from 153, and an added mark would print on the shared staff without a matching one in Hn 3. At 130 dpi the shared staff prints Hn 4 as a crotchet and two rests under Hn 3's tied B, with no label or collision.

### 2. Bars 155 and 160: the drone under V inside the phrases (considered, kept)

At 155 (B7) and 160 (B major, Cl 1 A♯4) the same E3 sounds under the dominant. I kept these. They sit inside the tonic prolongation of each phrase, where Ex. 21-41 (PDF 367) allows chords against the pedal. The drone also gives the trio its musette character, which v01's plan names. Only the final cadence needs a clear V, which is why finding 1 changes one bar.

### 3. Bar 200: Hn 1's 9-8 A4–G♯4 under the call's G♯5 (considered, kept)

The scan shows the resolution tone G♯5 sounding in Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I on the downbeat while Hn 1 holds A4. That is the "poor" spacing of Ex. 21-12a (PDF 352). It falls under that page's exception, "a texture with marked contrast in rhythm, timbre": the call in quavers on top, the tune and horn in crotchets and minims below. The layering 197–200 is protected (c039, c091, c094, c107, c117), and none of those reasons is answered by this. Kept.

### 4. Bars 110–111 and 310–311: the tenor line's ♭9 C4–B3 under Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1 B4 (considered, kept)

The tenor countermelody (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4) restrikes C4 over the V bass. Hn 1·2 and Tpt 1 hold B4, the resolution tone, above it. This is a ff tutti, and the trumpets play motive x's rhythm, so the PDF 352 exception applies. c112 made this ♭9 on purpose to remove inner fifths, which is the use PDF 365–367 describes. Kept.

### 5. Bars 187–188 and 191–192: a 4-3 at the ends of the trio's call and answer (considered, rejected)

Both two-bar endings repeat T's 4.–8–4 | 2. rhythm with no suspension. A tie (Ob 1 `cis''4~ | cis''4 bis'2`; cellos and C.a. `b4~ | b4 ais2`) would give them the cadential 4-3 of PDF 350. I rejected it for two reasons. At 192, Cl 1's held A♯4 would sound the resolution tone on the downbeat (PDF 352, "poor"). At 188, the oboe would end on B♯4, which meets the harp's B4 at 189 as a cross relation between voices (c042 removed the same kind at 192–193). The plain endings also keep the 195–196 7/6 (c061) as the trio middle's only suspension, placed just before the forte.
