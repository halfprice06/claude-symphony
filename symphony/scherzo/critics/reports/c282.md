## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–55: Horn 2 doubles the 4th of the cadential 6/4 and rises in octaves with the tune (applied)

Problem: bar 53 is the cadential 6/4 of the G-major hemiola cadence (D bass). Horn 2 holds G3, which is the 4th. The viola and clarinet 2 already have G4, and the tune reaches G5 on beat 3. At 54 Horn 2 rises G3 to A3 while flutes, oboes, violins I (G5 to A5) and violins II (G4 to A4) make the same move. The doubled dissonance therefore moves in parallel octaves with the melody into the V7.

Evidence: A&S PDF 154 and Ex. 10-5: "the bass is the best tone to double"; doubling the 4th is marked wrong (Ex. 10-5c) and repeated as review point 4 on PDF 160. PDF 153 says the 4th resolves down by step. A&S PDF 136–137 (Ex. 9-3) shows the octaves this produces.

Protection: c223 chose G3–A3–G3 so that the doubled 4th would not fall in octaves with the viola, citing Unit 19 PDF 314–316. That reason is answered here because the 4th is no longer doubled, so neither the falling nor the rising octaves can occur. The hemiola rhythm (the protected element) is unchanged.

Fix (sA.ily, sAHnTwo, bars 53–55):
`g2.--( | a2.-- | g2.--)` becomes `d'2.--( | d'2.-- | d'2.--)`
Horn 2 doubles the bass D through 6/4, V7 and I (D is the 5th of G at 55). It is approached from A3 at 52 by a 4th up, in contrary motion to the tune. Bar 56 `g4-.` is kept.

### 2. Bars 173→174 and 345→346: IV to ii with the bass falling a 3rd makes 5ths without the 5–6 (applied)

Problem: in the trio theme's bars 5–6 (A major chord on 173, F♯ minor on 174 in E major; transposed to D to B in the coda at 345), the bass leaps down a 3rd. The countermelody holds the 5th above the bass and also falls a 3rd:
- 173→174 cellos E3→C♯3 over the basses A1→F♯1
- 345→346 bassoon 1 solo A3→F♯3 over D2→B1

At one beat to the bar these downbeats are adjacent beats, so the 5ths are heard. The harp at 157–158, the first statement, avoids them.

Evidence: A&S PDF 146–147, Ex. 9-20 and 9-21: going from IV to II with a bass falling a 3rd, a 5–6 step in an upper voice breaks up the parallel 5ths. Without it, the voices move as in the "not" example of Ex. 9-3.

Fix:
- tA.ily, tAVc 173–174: `e4( cis2~ | cis4 a,2)` becomes `e4( fis2~ | fis4 a,2)`. The cello goes to the root of ii, a step up in contrary motion to the bass, and keeps its fall to A2.
- coda.ily, codaBsnOne 345–346: `a4( fis2~ | fis4 d2` becomes `a4( b2~ | b4 d2`. This is the same line a 4th lower. The rest of the solo is unchanged.

### 3. Bars 220→221: the doubled bass of ii6/5 moves in octaves with the bass into V6/4 (applied)

Problem: bar 220 is ii6/5 in E (bass A), and bar 221 is the cadential 6/4 (bass B). Bassoon 1 (A3) and the violas' lower note (A3) double the bass A and rise to B3 with the double basses (A1→B1) and the contrabassoon. The violas' A3→B3 also moves in octaves with the tune (A5→B5 in flutes and violins I, A4→B4 in violins II and clarinet).

c145 fixed the cellos and bassoon 2 here. c092 and c093 had already noted octaves at 220→221.

Evidence: A&S PDF 151, review point 5: "Be careful of parallel octaves in II6–V if the bass of II6 is doubled". PDF 136–137, Ex. 9-3: upper voices should move against the bass. PDF 139, Ex. 9-7 explains that doubling the bass of II6 is good only when the octave does not move with it.

Fix (tC.ily):
- tCBsnOne bar 220: `a2.--` becomes `fis2.--`
- tCVla bar 220: `r4 <a e'>4--( <a e'>4--)` becomes `r4 <fis e'>4--( <fis e'>4--)`

F♯, the root of ii6/5, then rises to B in contrary motion and doubles the bass B of the 6/4. The held E4 (protected, 219–221) is kept.

I rejected G♯ at 221, because it makes octaves with the tune's A5→G♯5 on beat 3. I also rejected C♯4 at 220, because it moves in octaves with horn 1's C♯5→B4.
