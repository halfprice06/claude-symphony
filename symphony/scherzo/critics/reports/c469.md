## What else I studied

- **Mendelssohn's "Italian" iii.** I compared it from my knowledge of the score. There is no copy in reference-corpus/; its README lists only Psalm 42 for Mendelssohn. The movement is a minuet in A major whose trio is in E major, the same key as this trio. In the trio, two horns and two bassoons play a soft chorale-like tune in thirds. Flutes and violins answer it, and trumpets and timpani join quietly only near the trio's climax. The coda brings the trio's horn call back pp under the last bars of the minuet.
- **v459 against v469.** I compared the two in full (`diff -r` of sections, global.ily and parts-cues.json) and viewed all 13 pages of v469. I viewed page 6 of my scratch build at 110 dpi.
- **Records.** I read HISTORY.md's do-not-touch entries and the c369 and c459 reports.

## Holistic comparison with Mendelssohn 4/iii

- **Trio colour.** Mendelssohn's E-major trio rests on soft horns that move with the harmony, with the strings held back. This trio does the same kind of thing with its own colours: Cl 1 over harp off-beats and a pp Hn 3·4 drone (153), then cellos with Hn 3 (169). The one place where the drone harms the sound is finding 1.
- **Holding back.** Mendelssohn keeps his trumpets and timpani back for one soft moment in the trio. This score keeps its low brass back from 305 to 333 and keeps B6 for 336 (Adler XI-19, XV-11). That is the same habit.
- **Recurring material.** Mendelssohn's coda mixes trio material into the minuet's close. This coda brings T back in four single colours (Hn 3, Ob 1, solo violin, Hn 1) before the motto tail. That is a more developed version of the same idea.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote it?** For the scoring, yes. The second idea is still the least memorable theme (open since c401), but no part of this reading gives a scoring reason to rewrite its notes.

## Findings

### 1. 163–164: Hn 3 should step to A3 under T's A5 peak instead of holding B3 (applied)

**Bars:** 163–164 (tA bars 11–12), Hn 3.

**Problem:** The Hn 3·4 drone E3/B3 is held for all 16 bars of T's first statement. Over F♯ minor at 163–164 (bass F♯1 then A1, harp A3–C♯4–F♯4), Hn 3's B3 falls between the harp's A3 and C♯4. This is the top of the tune: Cl 1 has A5–G♯5–F♯5. The sustained horn turns the harp chord into a pp cluster A3–B3–C♯4 in the low-middle register. The drone has the same effect at 157–159, but those bars are not the climax.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 159, rules 1–2: melody notes must stand out from the harmony, and each pitch goes where its register can sound clearly at the marked dynamic.
- PDF 157–158, Ex. XV-8 (Mahler, Kindertotenlieder): the soft pedal there is one note in the fourth horn, not a fifth that sits inside the chord.
- Mendelssohn's E-major trio has the same instruments and key, and its horns move with the harmony rather than holding against it.

**Fix (applied, sections/tA.ily):**
```
tAHnThree = { b2.~ | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2 r4 | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2 r4 | b2.~ | b2. | a2.~ | a2 r4 | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2 r4 | ...
```
- Hn 4 keeps its E3 pedal alone, so 163–164 sounds as F♯ minor 7 with no second inside the harp chord.
- The fifth drone returns at 165 for the cadence.
- Voice leading: B3→A3 lands on the harp's A3, which arrives from G♯3, so the two parts meet by contrary motion. A3→B3 at 165 meets the harp's B3, which arrives from C♯4. Hn 3 forms no parallels with the bass (G♯1→F♯1) or with Cl 1.

**Why only 163–164.** The same step at 157–158 would move Hn 3 B3→A3 in parallel unison with the harp's middle note (B3→A3 at 156→157). There is no clean alternative there, and those bars are not the peak. Moving both horns in thirds at 161–164 (I built that first) puts Hn 4's E3→F♯3 in octaves with the harp's top note E4→F♯4, so I rejected it. The drone's identity (c433: root under the drone at 153) and the colour plan (c459: 153 / 169 / 185 / 209) are unchanged.

### 2. 31–36: held arco basses under the answers (checked, kept)

This is the only v459 → v469 change that makes the texture heavier (c459). Mendelssohn's minuet keeps its bass light, which is a reason to doubt it. The other side: it is pp, it holds the answers' own root, and Adler PDF 154 treats a sustained note as what binds a quick texture together. It does not cover the staccato answers. Kept.

### 3. 209–224: Cl 1 and Fl 1 at pitch with the violins (checked, kept)

Adler PDF 165 warns that long doublings at pitch tire the ear. PDF 176 (Jupiter) also shows a flute kept on Vln I at pitch for a whole passage so that the colour stays established. The doubling here lasts one 16-bar statement, at the trio's forte climax, with affinity instruments (PDF 159, rule 3). Kept.
