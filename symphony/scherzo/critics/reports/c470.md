## Findings

### 1. Applied. Bars 7–8: Vln I G5 pp on top of the E-minor turn

**Problem.** The movement opens with Hn 1 G♯4 and Vln I G♯5 (1–2). The call follows in E major (3–6). At 7 the music turns to E minor with Cl 1 E5, Cl 2 G4, Bsn 1 B3 and Bsn 2 B2, pp. The ♭3 that makes the turn is Cl 2's G4, an inner voice on the A clarinet's throat B♭ (written), under Cl 1. The G♯ that the listener heard at the top (G♯5, 1–2) gets no answer in its own register or voice. The juxtaposition of the two forms of 3, which this opening is about, is present in the notes but is the least audible thing in the bar.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 387: two tones that represent one degree give "dramatic juxtaposition and, even, conflict". Ex. 23-1 shows them as G♯ and G♮, 3 of E, in Mendelssohn.
- Ex. 23-1a (PDF 386, image): Mendelssohn's turn to E minor (bar 6, G♮) is the violins' entry, marked "pp violins". The violins then play the minor theme. His borrowed C♮ in bar 3 is the top note of its chord.
- PDF 394, Ex. 23-12b: a chromatic change is clean when one voice makes it.
- Vln I G♯5 → G5 is that kind of change: same player, same octave, five bars apart, with only the horn call between.
- Cyclic: movement I already has G♯5 (Cl 1, I 78) over E major, turning to G5 (Vln I/Ob 1, I 79) in E minor, at this pitch (mvt1.md, "Seeds of later movements"). The finale's bars 1–2 move Vln I A♭5 → G5 and Hn 1 A♭4 → G4. The scherzo had the octave-4 form (Hn 1 G♯4 → Cl 2 G4, c270) but not the octave-5 form. Now all three movements state the same G♯/A♭5 → G5 in Vln I.
- The G5 is also the first note of theme A's tail at 14 (G5–F♯5–F5–E5, the bar I 79 and IV 273–275 quote), so the turn note hands on to the theme.

**Fix (applied).** `sections/intro.ily`, `introVlnOne`:
```
old: gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "senza sord." | gis''2. | R2.*10 | }
new: gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "senza sord." | gis''2. | R2.*4 | g''!2.~\pp | g''2\> r4\! | R2.*4 | }
```
- pp with the clarinets' hairpin, ending with them on beat 2 of 8.
- The cautionary natural is there because the player held G♯5 four bars earlier (the part shows it).
- Unchanged: Cl 2's G4 (c270), no E4 at 7–8 (c040), the unresolved D♯4 at 5–6, Hn 1 alone on the call 3–6 (c090), and the bare first statement of theme A 13–28 (c259/c359; Vln I still enters with the theme at 13).
- c430 rejected holding the violin into 3, because of the clash with D♯ at 5–6. This entry comes after the D♯ has stopped.

**Why this is the candidate.** Cycles 400–403 and most lens-10 cycles ended with documents only. This is the change in this reading that a listener hears first: the top of the bar-7 chord changes from E5 to G5, a semitone under the G♯5 the movement began with.

### 3. Candidates built in thought and rejected by the reading

a. **A minor (iv) for T's second phrase in the coda, 345–348** (Ex. 23-8; Exercise 6). The C♮ would come before the C♯ of 349–358, which is the "poor" order ♭6–♮6–5 (PDF 388, 395 pt. 3). It would also use up the plagal iv that 359 saves for the motto close (Ex. 23-5: the minor passage leads to one plagal close). Rejected.

b. **Any C♮ in 341–357** (a IIø6/5 at 355, a melodic ♭6 in T, Ex. 23-2d/g). Rejected for the same reason. This is a stronger do-not-touch reason than c403's I–IV–I alone: the coda keeps ♮6 from 341 to 358 so that 359's ♭6 goes straight to 5. HISTORY should record it.

c. **A Picardy E major at the reprise cadence 287** (Exercise 6, PDF 397). Rejected for three reasons:
- It would anticipate the trio's and the coda's E major inside the minor scherzo.
- It would join to the G-minor fugato at 289 by two chromatic shifts (G♯→G, B→B♭), where the exposition has one (B→B♭ at 56–57, c410).
- Its forte major tonic would compete with the finale's arrival in C major (Finding 4).

d. **♭6 (C) in the E-minor half of the link, 141–144**, to match 145–148's C♯ (Ex. 23-8). C over E with G is a C-major sonority, which the cyclic rule forbids (c413). Rejected.

### 4. The scherzo's place in the arc: soft mixture, loud mixture left to the finale (no change)

The reading's Picardy example (Ex. 23-7) is forte and orchestral, and A&S call it an effect of "startling power" (PDF 391). The scherzo's minor-to-major turns are both soft: the link at 145 (one horn, pp) and the coda at 341–349 (through IV, p). So the finale's C major is the symphony's only loud ♯3 arrival.

The large-scale mixture chain stands:
- The scherzo's E is III♯ of C (PDF 393, 395 pt. 5).
- Its last G♯ is 3 of E, and the finale takes it as A♭, ♭6 of C, the root of its opening German sixth. One pitch thus serves as two different degrees across the pause (PDF 387).
- The plagal closes of II, III and IV all keep ♮6–♭6–5 (F–F♭–E♭; C♯–C–B; A–A♭–G).

Keep the scherzo's major turns soft.
