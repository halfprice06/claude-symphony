## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 349-356: T's lower octave is doubled at the unison by two violin sections against one solo violin on top. Applied.

**Problem.** In the coda's last statement of T, Vln I is marked "div.: solo / gli altri". One player takes the upper octave (C♯6-A6, with Fl 1 since c439). All the other first violins play the lower octave, and Vln II plays exactly the same notes: same pitches, slurs, hairpins and dolce. So about 26 violins sound C♯5-A5 against one violin and one flute an octave above. c439 named this problem ("the line to follow is not the weakest sound against 22 violins an octave below") and fixed it by adding the flute. The unison between Vln I gli altri and Vln II stayed. It adds no pitch, colour, rhythm or part. It adds only mass, and that mass falls on the octave c438/c439 want to be secondary.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12: every note should make "a legitimate difference to the score as a whole"; if it does not, "it should be erased". PDF 12-13, Ex. 1: taking out one doubling line let the focal element "dominate a little more noticeably". PDF 13: "Always be prepared to cut something ... Sometimes 'Less Is More'". PDF 13-14: the arranger directs the ear to the primary element. Here that is the solo line on top, which c438 protects ("the coda's T is carried by single players"). Two sections in unison at one pitch and in one colour is the plainest case of a note that makes no difference.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily `codaVlnOne`).** The first violins play the solo line only. The rest of the section rests until its marked "tutti" at 369 (the closing G♯5, untouched). Vln II alone keeps the lower octave and runs on into its G♯4 at 357 as before.
```
349: cis'''2(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic \column { "dolce" "solo (gli altri tacet)" } dis'''4 | e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~ ... | e'''2 r4 |
     (was <cis'' cis'''>2( ... "div.: solo / gli altri" ... <e'' e'''>2 r4)
```
The pitches, slurs and dynamics of the upper line are unchanged. Fl 1 still doubles it, so c439 is kept. The theme record reads the same notes (52/52). The lower octave keeps about 12 violins at pp, which is plenty over violas ppp and cellos pp. No cue changes: the only cue in parts-cues.json that points at Vln I between 336 and 376 is part 25's at 337-339, before this passage, where Vln I plays, and Vln I itself never rests long, because the solo plays 349-356 and the tutti returns at 369.

### 2. Bars 13-28: theme A's first statement is a single line over off-beats; Dvořák 9/iii states its theme as a canon. Considered, not changed.

A canonic second voice would add counterpoint. But Sebesky PDF 11 (formal balance of instrumentation) argues for the present plan: strings alone first, winds held back for the call at 29. The canon in Dvořák is in the winds, and here the winds' first entry at 29 is the point of the call and response (protected since c007). PDF 13 also warns against a counter-line that competes with the primary element at a theme's first hearing. No change.

### 3. Bars 92-94: the oboe's head entry over pizzicato B alone. Considered, not changed.

I tested in my head a viola continuation (the fugato countersubject) under the oboe. Sebesky's chapter is about subtraction (PDF 12-13), and c123 and c294 kept this bareness as a light solo accompaniment. The countersubject transposed here puts C4 against the oboe's E5/G5 (93, beat 3), which risks C-E-G. No change.

### 4. Bars 169-184: T in the cellos with Hn 3, Bsn 2 on the bass, Vln II quavers above. Checked, kept.

Ex. 1 suggests lightening the lower-middle register. Bsn 2 (F♯2-E3) stays a third or more below the tune and doubles the arco basses that c436 found too weak alone (Rimsky PDF 27). Hn 3 is a colour (c431, Adler PDF 27), not a second line. Vln II's con sord. quavers lie an octave or more above the tenor tune. They are in a different rhythm and a different register, so they are the "fills" of Ex. 2A, not an overbusy counter-line. I found no note there that makes no difference.

### 5. Bars 209-216: Bsn 1 and Hn 2 share E4/F♯4 at times. Checked, kept.

The unisons at 209-211 and 214 are the four-part horn-bassoon choir that c014, c247 and c263 voiced. Bsn 1 leaves the unison where the harmony needs it (215-216). This is not Ex. 1's case of a parallel inner line.

### 6. Harmonic variety: T at 169-172 and 209-212 now share I-I6-V-I. Recorded, not changed.

c257 gave 210 an I6 to differ from 170 (Sebesky PDF 19). c433 then put I6 at 170 too, so the two first phrases are again alike in their downbeat bass. 209 differs only by the passing C♯ on beat 3. c433's do-not-touch reason is that the three statements gain bass motion in turn. That reason holds, and the return still has its own second phrase (213-216, V6/5 of V). A further change here would be less audible than finding 1. I record it for the harmony lens.

### 7. Bar 53 (129, 285): passing C5 over Cl 1's B4. Checked, kept.

c451 checked this as a normal passing dissonance (A&S Ex. 20-14). On Sebesky's terms, the clarinet's B-D-B is the chord, not a doubling of the tune. Kept.
