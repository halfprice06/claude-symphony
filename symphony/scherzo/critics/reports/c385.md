## Findings

### 1. Octave cues in the parts carry labels that mean the opposite of Gould's octave signs (applied, parts only)

**Bars.** Parts only: Horn 2 37–40 (Vln I cue), timpani 85–88 (Cl 1), harp 337–339 (Vln I), cello 165–168 (Cl 1), and oboe 1 181–184 (Vc).

**Problem.** Four cues are printed an octave below the instrument they quote and labelled "Vln. I (8vb)" or "Cl. 1 (8vb)". The oboe's cello cue is printed an octave above and labelled "Vc. (8va)". The labels describe the transposition the editor made. Gould defines the sign the other way. *Ottava bassa* ("8va bassa", "8vb") means the notes sound an octave lower than written (PDF 42), so "Vln. I (8vb)" tells the horn player that the violins sound two octaves below the climax they actually play. The labels also have no dotted line or end corner (PDF 42–45), so it is not clear which notes they cover. In the two bass-clef parts the octave displacement was not needed at all. Gould PDF 46 says to move into another clef before using an octave sign, and a timpanist or cellist reads a treble-clef cue at pitch without ledger lines.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 42 (8va raises, 8va bassa lowers the written pitch; italic 8 with a dashed line), PDF 44 (the line runs to the barline at a system break, the next system restates the 8, and the line ends with a corner right after the last notehead), PDF 46 (never use an octave sign where the instrument could change clef), PDF 47 (octave signs in parts only where needed). In the parts as published, the harp's line would also have run over the two closing rests of bar 339 to the barline. PDF 44 calls that incorrect.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-385/work).** A cue entry in parts-cues.json may now take an optional sixth field. parts-gen.py passes it through, and `make-cue-music` in parts-lib.ily acts on it. `"treble"` or `"alto"` prints the cue in its own clef (`\cueClef … \cueClefUnset`). `"8va"` or `"8vb"` quotes the instrument at its real pitch under a real octave sign (`\ottava #±1 … \ottava #0`). `":N"` sets the player's own bar rests to staff-position N for the length of the cue, so an in-stave cue does not sit on them (PDF 50–51). `"@N"` ends the octave sign after N crotchets, so the corner comes after the last cued note (PDF 44). New entries:
```
"03": ["cueVc","Vc.","UP",181,4,"8vb"]          (was cueVcHigh "Vc. (8va)")
"13": ["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",37,4,"8va"]     (was cueVlnOneLow "Vln. I (8vb)")
"23": ["cueClOne","Cl. 1","UP",85,4,"treble:-10"], ["cueVla","Vla.","UP",89,2,"treble:-12"]
"25": ["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",337,3,"8va@7"]  (was cueVlnOneLow "Vln. I (8vb)")
"29": ["cueClOne","Cl. 1","UP",165,4,"treble"]   (was cueClOneLow "Cl. 1 (8vb)")
```
I checked each at 100–130 dpi. Timpani 85–90: treble clef, Cl 1 and then the viola at pitch, bar rests hanging from a ledger line below the cue notes, bass clef back for the entry at 91. Cello 165–168: treble cue, then the tenor clef of its own entry at 169. Horn 2: 8 with a dashed line to the barline, restated "8" on the next system, corner after the last note of 40. Oboe 181–184: an 8 with a dashed line below the stave, restated after the break. Harp: corner straight after the note on 339's downbeat. The stale c165 note in parts-gen.py was brought up to date. The score is not touched. Every part keeps its page count, and the parts build has 0 warnings.
