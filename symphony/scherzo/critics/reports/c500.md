## Findings

### 1. 364–376: the closing G♯5 now runs on without a break, as the opening's does since c499 (APPLIED)

- **Problem.** Since c499, Vln I holds G♯5 ppp unbroken through bars 1–6 above the horn call and slurs into G5 at 7. The opening's high G♯ is now one held line. At the close the same pitch is broken: Fl 1 holds G♯5 at 361–364 with the clarinets' chord, the high G♯ then stops for four bars (365–368), and the tutti violins re-enter at 369. c440 protected this gap because "the close reverses the opening (pair → horn alone at 1–6, horn alone → pair at 365–376)". Since c499 there is no "horn alone" at 1–6, so that reason is void. c140 gave the gap a reason before that ("horn alone mirrors bars 1–2"), and c430 made it false. The gap has now outlived two reasons.
- **Evidence.**
  - Rimsky PDF 125–126: when a line passes between instruments, "the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one".
  - Rimsky PDF 130: a diminuendo removes the brass, then the woodwind, then the strings. In v500 the strings come back four bars after the woodwind has left (c050 and c150 noted this entry as a departure from "elimination").
  - Adler II PDF 192, Ex. XV-40: at a colour change the seams should not show unless an accent is wanted.
  - Casanelles PDF 272, Fig. 6.1, the image c499 built the opening on: the high pedal stays while the other layers come and go.
  - Adler II PDF 275: horns place pitch by ear. Hn 1 re-enters on G♯4 at 368 after its breath, and the octave above now gives it a reference.
  - With the change, the close reverses the opening exactly. The opening goes from pair (1–6) to the clarinets' chord (7). The close goes from the clarinets' chord with Fl 1 (361–364) to the pair (364–376).
- **Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, codaVlnOne):**
  ```
  ... e'''2 r4 | R2.*7 | r2 gis''4~\ppp^\markup \italic "tutti, change bow freely" | gis''2.~ (×11) | gis''2. | }
  ```
  This replaces `R2.*12 | gis''2.~\ppp^... | gis''2.~ ×6 | gis''2.`. The violins take the G♯5 on the beat where the flute and the chord release. Pitch, player, dynamic, last bar and the fermata are unchanged. A comment marked c500 records the reason.
- **Why this is not worse.**
  - The four-bar groups that c440 also gave as a reason remain. 361 has the wind chord. 365 has Hn 3 taking over Hn 1. 369 has the pair alone.
  - Vln I's tutti players have rested since 348, so the entry is still fresh (Adler II PDF 263).
  - The weak-beat entry (A&S PDF 51) is ppp and tied, without an accent. It is masked by the chord's release, which is the point of Adler's dovetail.
- **Alternatives weighed.**
  - Entry on the downbeat of 365: this leaves a one-beat seam (Adler II PDF 192).
  - Fl 1 holding to 368 and handing over at 369: an eight-bar pp G♯5 in the flute is weaker in colour than the violins, and Vln I is the pair that the finale takes up at IV 1. The flute would also need its own MIDI fade re-routed.
  - Reverting c499 to restore the old mirror: this gives back the opening's four-bar break that c499 removed for a sound reason.
- **Build and parts.** The page is unchanged (page 13, 13 pages in all), and the text clears the rit. Vln I now rests 357–364 (7½ bars), so no cue is needed. No entry in parts-cues.json quotes Vln I between 357 and 376.
- **MIDI.** Vln I shares Vln II's channel, which is at expression 0.55 in 364 (chordFadeMidi). The new beat therefore enters softer and reaches the normal level at 365, a fade-in. Print is unaffected.
