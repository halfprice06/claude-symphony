## Findings

Every wind and brass part lies within the ranges, breathing and articulation limits this chapter teaches. I found no playability fault. The findings below are the places where Sebesky's text points away from the score. For each I give the reason it stays as it is.

### 1. Ob 1·2 in unison at 41–43 and 273–275 (built and tested in /tmp/critic-446/cand; not applied)

Problem considered: at the f arrival of the counterstatement, Ob 2 plays its only three bars of the exposition, in unison with Ob 1, both flutes and both violin sections (A5–C6). Sebesky PDF 71: "I don't recommend voicing oboes in unison unless, for some reason, an especially harsh effect is required." PDF 80: unisons risk intonation more than octaves do. This is the clearest instruction in the chapter that the score does not follow. It is also the only one not tied to a cyclic rule, so it was my best candidate.

Built: `sAOboeTwo = { R2.*16 | R2.*12 | R2.*3 | R2. | R2.*12 | }`, and in dA.ily Ob 2's bars 273–275 replaced by `R2.` (21 rests before, 16 after). The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, cmajor "none", one outer-voice parallel as in v446, theme record 52/52. The part would also need its cues moved: drop Ob 2's Vln I cues at 37 and 271 (they would precede no entry), and add `["cueVc","Vc.","UP",69,4]` and `["cueVc","Vc.","UP",297,4]` for the new first entries at 72 and 300.

Why not applied: c266 (finding F2) and c356 (finding 4) kept these unisons. Their reasons were that the passages are forte, an edge is wanted, and PDF 56 fn 4 says a woodwind unison grows smoother with each added player. A closer reading supports them. PDF 71 is about voicing an oboe section on its own (thirds, open fifths, triads). Here the oboes are two of six lines in a mixed unison of flutes, oboes and violins. PDF 80 calls the flute–oboe unison "strong and useful", and fn 4 says the added reed makes it smoother, not harsher. The oboes are not exposed, so the intonation risk PDF 80 describes is covered by the flutes and violins. Without Ob 2, the line at the movement's first forte loses half its reed weight against 30 violins, and nothing is gained that a listener would hear. The reading does not give a stronger reason than the one already recorded, so I keep v446.

### 2. The flutes' upper octave stops at 113–116 (no change)

Sebesky PDF 42–43 (Ex. 49): an octave doubling should not be interrupted mid-line, and if it must be, an equal instrument should carry on. Fl 1·2 double theme A an octave above at 109–112 and 117–120, and join the tune at pitch for 113–116. The 4-bar phrase starts at 113, and Sebesky PDF 51 (Ex. 55) says that dropping an octave doubling at "the dividing line between two phrases" is "not noticeable or objectionable". The present layout does exactly that. The alternative, flutes up at 113–114 only (c404 wrote it out), breaks the doubling in the middle of the phrase, which is the case PDF 43 forbids. 115–116 cannot go up at all (B6/C7). The reading confirms v446.

### 3. Brass V chord at 336–338: horns double the fifth (no change)

PDF 50: the fifth is the least important tone, and the third and seventh belong in the lower-middle register. At 336–338 Hn 2·3 play F♯4 a2. The brass third is D♯4 in Tpt 2 only, and the seventh (337–338) is A4 in Tpt 1 and Cl 2. The only change that would satisfy PDF 50 is moving Hn 3 to D♯4, which doubles the leading tone at the fff peak (c002 removed such doublings) or adds a second seventh (c029). Sebesky himself pairs horns in unison at a climax for strength (PDF 46: "two big, beautiful unisons"), and the brass section is complete (B, D♯, F♯). No change.

### 4. Hn 1·2 a tenth or an octave apart at 45–52 (no change)

PDF 103 (Ex. 125): two horns no wider than a fifth or minor sixth, or the sound spreads and tunes badly. Hn 1·2 stand B4/G3 at 45–46 and A4/A3 at 47 and 49–52. Sebesky's rule is about a horn-and-clarinet pad in a studio texture. Here Hn 2 fills the gap between the bass (G2) and Cl 2 (B3) in a full forte texture (c004, Rimsky-Korsakov's "no empty middle"). Raising Hn 2 to D4 would leave a tenth with nothing in it above the bass. No change.

### 5. Bass trombone and tuba both on the root (101–120, 332–335, 339) (no change)

PDF 47 and PDF 88 (error list to Ex. 112): bass trombone and tuba (or baritone sax) need not double the low root, because it gives "unnecessary weight". In a lean swing chart that weight is a fault. In this movement weight is what Dan asked for ("weight rather than bite"), and the tuba plays in only 25 bars. The pair is an octave apart, not the full low-brass unison kept for the finale. c266 and c356 kept it. No change.

### 6. Horn quavers in motive x at 71 and 299 (no change)

PDF 32 and 46: the horn speaks late, so Sebesky leaves fast pick-ups out of horn parts in staccato brass figures. Hn 1·2 play motive x's quavers at 71 and 299, but with the woodwinds, not against trumpets, and on the beat at 2. = 88. c176 kept them on the Dvořák 9/iii precedent. No change.

### 7. Hn 1's last G♯ held alone at ppp (368–376) (no change)

PDF 24 (a slur is one breath), PDF 30 and 33 (Ex. 33 and 37: a breath comma before the final chord): Hn 1 holds G♯4 from 368 to the final fermata alone, about 11–12 s at the printed rit. At ppp a horn uses little air, so the note is playable. The breath is already planned: Hn 3 covers 365–369 and Hn 1 rests at 367 (c440's colour plan). No change.
