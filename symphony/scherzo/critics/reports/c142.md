## Findings

**1. Bars 220–221: inner-voice parallel octaves C♯–B (Hn 1 against Vc/Bsn 2). APPLIED.**

- Problem: at 220, Hn 1 holds C♯5 and moves to B4 at 221. Vc and Bsn 2 move F♯3 → C♯3 on beat 3 of 220, then C♯3 → B2 at 221. Both lines move C♯ → B across the same barline, which makes octaves between two real parts. The bass (Cb A1 → B1) moves in contrary motion, so the octaves are not in the outer voices. They are still audible, because this is the forte trio cadence and the horn sits above the texture.
- Evidence: A-S PDF 419 says applied and seventh chords keep normal voice leading. Ex. 24-6 (PDF 404) shows that the 7th-chord voicing exists to break up such perfect intervals. c112 removed a 5ths pair at exactly this join (Hn 1 C♯5–B4 / Vla F♯4–E4) by holding the viola's E4. These octaves are the remaining half of that problem.
- Rejected alternative: `fis2 e4` on beat 3. It doubles the ii6/5's 7th E against Vla E4, which PDF 439 forbids ("Don't double ... the 7th").
- Fix (tC.ily, tCVc and tCBsnTwo, bar 220): `fis2 cis4)` → `fis2.)`. The cello holds F♯3, the root of ii6/5, then falls a 4th to B2 under the cadential 6/4. Nothing else changes.
- The coda's 352 keeps `fis2 cis4`. There the melody moves C♯ → B on beat 2 and no horn holds C♯, so no octave forms.

**2. Bars 225–236 and the plagal close: IV with added 6th (PDF 412–413, Ex. 24-20). NOT APPLIED; kept as written.**

- The codetta's I–IV–I alternations could take an added 6th on IV. That would make an apparent II6/5, which the book gives as the typical decoration of a plagal cadence.
- I tested it on paper. In E major the added 6th on A is C♯, which sits against the C♯ melody notes already present. In the closing iv it would add C♯ to A–C–E, which is close to the forbidden C-major sound.
- The plain IV/iv is the stronger choice here. No change.

**3. Bars 81–84: falling 3rds with applied 4/3 chords (PDF 436–437). NOT APPLIED; kept as written.**

- The book's 10ths-with-applied-4/3 pattern would sharpen the sequence.
- It clashes with the held D5 in the melody, as c052 already found. The current alternating 5/3–7 voicing follows Ex. 24-6. No change.

No other bar in the score breaks a principle from Units 24–25. Every 7th resolves down by step, or is decorated, transferred, extended or delayed in one of the ways the book shows (PDF 400–410). Every applied chord (79, 87–88, 175, 215) is spelled correctly and resolves its leading tone and 7th. Leading tones are doubled only in the protected V♭9 chords and in bass octave doublings. Every cross relation either stays within one voice or is softened as on PDF 421–422.
