## Findings

### 1. Bars 73–74: Hn 1·2 hold A4/C5 an octave under the harp's first melodic notes. Applied.

**Problem.** The F-major episode is the first time the movement uses the harp as a melody (plan, sB.ily comment; c001 protects the line). The harp starts at 73 with A5–F5–A5 and reaches its phrase peak, C6, on the downbeat of 74, marked mp. Under it, Hn 1·2 hold the tail of the ff A-major chord from 72: Hn 2 A4 and Hn 1 C5, pp, to beat 2 of 74. The harp's A5 is the second partial of Hn 2's A4, and its C6 is the second partial of Hn 1's C5. Each pluck lands on a pitch the horns are already sounding as an overtone, and the pluck decays within the beat while the horns sustain. So for most of each bar the horn dyad is what is heard in that pitch class. The F-major chord does not need the horns: Hn 3·4 (F3–C4), Bn 1 (A3), the bass clarinet (F2) and the harp's left hand (F2, C3–F3–A3) already sound it complete.

**Evidence.** In Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272) Mahler stops the woodwinds' held chord in the bar where the quiet layer enters, and leaves only a pedal far from its band. A quieter sound placed on a louder sound's band only adds partials to it (PDF 277–278). The fewer instruments in a band, the clearer, and sounds in one band mask each other (PDF 286–287). A sustained sound is more present than a decaying one (PDF 290, Fig. 6.6). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: in the harp's upper register, where this melody lies, the strings are "shorter and harder in tone", so the decay is quick. c444 used the same octave argument at 233–236, where Vln II and the violas held the octave above Bsn 1, and its fix was accepted.

**Protections answered.** c010 (Hn 2's common tone A into F major), c042 (Hn 1's chromatic step C♯5 → C5 in one voice), c028 (the ff chord decays to pp, so F major arrives without a dead cut) and c136/c266 (Hn 1's slur and the soft high horn) all remain: the horns still reach F major pp on the downbeat of 73. The only change is that they release at the end of 73 instead of on beat 2 of 74. The episode's wind chord (Hn 3·4, Bn 1, bass clarinet; c105) and its spacing from 75 (c299) are unchanged.

**Fix (applied).** sections/sB.ily:

### 2. Bars 177–184: Vln II's con sord. quavers cross above Vln I's T (G♯5 over C♯5 at 177, E5 over C♯5–G♯4 at 180). No change.

The two violin sections share a band here (PDF 287). But Vln II is muted and pp, and Vln I is senza sord., p, sul A. A mute does to the string spectrum what cutting the 3 kHz area does (PDF 286), so the line with the brighter upper partials is the tune. c461 accepted this layout with Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 54 (relief by contrast of timbre). Moving Vln II down an octave would put it on the cellos' T (C♯4–A4). **Decision:** keep.

### 3. Bars 333–335: the Hn 3·4/Bn 1 countermelody (B3–E4) lies in the trumpets' band (repeated E4/B4, f). No change.

The horns play ff a2 and the trumpets play f. By PDF 289 the denser, louder horn sound comes forward. The trumpets' crotchet-and-quaver figure is a series of decaying strokes, while the countermelody is held, and by PDF 290 the held line is the more present. c514's countermelody stays. **Decision:** keep.

### 4. Bars 153–168: the harp's off-beat chords (G♯3–B3–E4) are struck inside the Hn 3·4 drone (E3/B3). No change.

The pluck's attack marks beats 2 and 3 (PDF 275–276) and decays into the pad within the beat (PDF 290). Cl 1's T (G♯4–C♯6) lies above both. c264 Finding 3 weighed the same point. **Decision:** keep.

### 5. Bars 205–208: Fl 1 an octave above Hn 1 on T's head, both p. No change.

The flute is on the horn's second partial, so the two merge into one colour (PDF 277–278). That blend is the point at the trio's retransition: a horn colour brightened on top, as in Dvořák. **Decision:** keep.

### 6. Bars 349–356: the coda's last T at A6 (PDF 273, 283–284, 287). Not re-raised.

c264, c354 and c444 weighed this against the same pages. Their reason still holds: 341–356 is one statement passed between single players, with distance built by p–p–pp and by the closing page, and a lower octave would put it in unison with Vln II. I have no new argument.

### 7. Bar 256: Fl 1's heterophony falls to B4–F♯5, the flute's weak low register (PDF 275, 277). No change.

The flute is alone in its band there, an octave above Vln I and over pizzicato strings, and the passage is one bar long. **Decision:** keep.
