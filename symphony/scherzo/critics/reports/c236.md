## Findings

### 1. The ppp close 371–375 has no beating spot (applied)
- **Bars.** 371 (motive x B–B–B–E, ppp) and 375 (last E2, ppp), under Hn 1's and Vln I's G♯.
- **Problem.** These are the movement's softest strokes, on soft sticks, on the 30" and 25" drums under a held G♯. Played at the normal spot, about six inches in, the low E2 gives a full, boomy fundamental that rings under the horn. Movement II ends its own ppp timpani roll "near the rim" for this reason, from the same page of Adler. The scherzo, the symphony's other ppp timpani close, gives no instruction.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80: "Usually, the player strikes the head about six inches in from the rim. A beautiful pianissimo can be achieved by playing even closer to the rim." PDF 80 also says the part must show how long a note may ring. Rim playing gives a shorter, lighter decay, so the last E2 supports the G♯ without sounding past it, which is the concern c186 raised when it kept "no l.v." at 375. PDF 88: the composer states the tone wanted.
- **Fix (applied).** coda.ily, codaTimp: `b,4\ppp^\markup \small \italic "near the rim" b,8 b,8 e,4`, with a c236 comment above the variable. No "ord." is needed because the movement ends; the finale's first label sets hard sticks. This is print only: score.midi is byte-identical to v236. The text prints above the timpani staff at 371 in the score and in the part, clear of the muta at 376. It changes no notes on the protected closing page.
