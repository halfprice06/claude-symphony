## Findings

None of the points below needs a change. Every principle in the table is followed, does not apply, or is departed from for a reason already recorded. I rank the points by how close each came to needing a change.

### 1. Coda: a 9-second fast stage against a 33-second slow stage (considered, kept)

**The case against it.** The fast stage 325–339 is 15 bars at 2. = 96 (about 9.4 s). The G.P. and the slow stage 341–376 then take about 35 s. Dvořák's coda is one 51-bar span, about 9% of his movement. Ours is 16% of the movement by time, and its last 20 bars (357–376) never leave E.

**Why I keep it.**
- By Unit 16 §4 (PDF 253, Ex. 16-5), 339 is in hindsight the upper 5th of IV (341, A major). The slow stage is therefore a cadence in progress, not a second ending.
- That cadence is the descending-5ths chain the reading weights at its close: vi–I6–ii7–IV6/5–cadential 6/4–V7–I (349–356; U16 §1 PDF 250, §7 PDF 255). The cadential 6/4 is expanded over two bars (U19 §10, PDF 316).
- After 356 the tonic is prolonged by the neighbouring iv6/4 at 359. This is the sustained-bass I–IV–I of Ex. 19-7 (U19 §4, PDF 309), with the motto tail on top. Then G♯ is left alone as the 3rd of I, in the manner of U19 §18 (PDF 322–323).
- Dvořák also closes his coda with mixture on the tonic (I – i, 1626–1684).
- The closing page 357–376 and the coda build are protected.

### 2. The twenty bars on E before the trio, 133–152 (considered, kept)

**The case against it.** Dvořák's transition leaves the tonic after about 18 bars and reaches the trio through III and VI6. Ours stays on E to the trio's first bar and beyond (the first change of harmony is at 155).

**Why I keep it.**
- Dvořák moves because his trio is in a new key (C, VI). Ours changes mode on the same root, so the reading's means of prolonging a tonic are the right ones:
  - V as a neighbour at 138 (U16 §9, PDF 256–257);
  - mixture G→G♯ in Hn 1 (141–148);
  - the oscillating 6/4 E–B in the pizzicato basses at 149–152 (U19 §15, PDF 320–321).
- Dvořák's own span on i before he moves (600–654) is about as long as ours.
- c223, c253, c283, c293 and c303 kept this passage.

### 3. Retransition 241–252 (checked)

The cadential 6/4 over B is reached by the stepwise bass E–A–C–B (U16 §8, PDF 256). C carries the French sixth C–E–F♯–A♯ at 244.

The 6/4 is then expanded (U19 §10, PDF 316), and the motto tail G–F♯–F–E in Cl 1 is interpolated over the B pedal (246–247; U19 §11, PDF 317). The E regained at 247–248 resolves 4–3 into the V7 at 249 (Cl 1 E5→D♯5).

A III divider before the reprise (Ex. 16-3, PDF 252) is an option, not a rule. It would add a chord that the reading does not require, inside a protected span. No change.

### 4. The first retransition: ten bars of 6/4 over B, 89–98 (checked)

The B pedal opens with i6/4 and reaches the 5/3 only at 99. This is an accented 6/4 whose job is "to delay the arrival of an expected … harmonic event" (U19 §3, PDF 307), expanded over three-bar groups as in Beethoven 9/ii's "ritmo di tre battute". The V7 hemiola at 101–104 then builds the arrival. The shortened reprise reaches B7 directly at 301 after A major. This is the plain III–IV–V step line of Ex. 16-2b (PDF 251), suited to a shortened development. No change. The 6/4 openings of the dominant pedals are on the do-not-touch list (c133).

### 5. Development key plans 57–89 and 289–301 (checked)

These are unchanged since c223 finding 3 and still follow Ex. 16-2b (row 2). The rising-5ths chain G minor – D minor – A is read as the step line G – A – B, with D minor as the decorative upper 5th. ♭II stands in place of the "I in name only". No change.

### 6. Cadential 6/4 voice leading after c282 and c289 (checked)

At every cadential 6/4 where the 4th is doubled (53, 129, 285, 335, 354), either the soprano's 4th rises or one doubling rises while the other falls. This is the condition of PDF 316 ("One of these 4ths will have to move up"). At 335 the trombones' E3/G3 stop rather than resolve; their resolution is transferred to Hn 3·4 and the violas (U19 §9, PDF 315–316). No change.

### 7. Length between the Adagio and the finale (checked)

The movement lasts 278 s, about 4:38. It keeps moving through three dominant pedals (89–104, 245–252, 301–304), four hemiola cadences and two tuttis. Nothing in Units 16 or 19 argues for a different length. The proportions (trio/scherzo 0.80, reprise/scherzo 0.56 by time) are those c203–c303 compared with Dvořák 9/iii. No change.
