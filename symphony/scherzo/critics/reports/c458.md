## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 277–280: the harmonic fill under the second idea's last statement is at the tune's own level. Applied.

**Problem.** At 277 the second idea's antecedent is in Fl 1 (B5) and Ob 1 (B4), sub. p, espress. Under it, Cl 1 (G♯4–B4), Cl 2 (E4), Hn 2 (G♯4) and the violas (E4–G♯4) hold the harmony, all at sub. p. Hn 3's B3 is pp (c208). So the tune is two single woodwinds, and the fill is two clarinets, a horn and a viola section at the same printed level, in the oboe's octave. Cl 1 even crosses above Ob 1: beat 3 of 277 and beat 1 of 278 have Cl 1 on B4 against Ob 1's G♯4. Every other soft lead in the movement has its accompaniment a level under (c264's rule: 153, 185, 225, 341–356), and the exposition's own statement at 45 has the tune f over a fill at mf. The horns at 277 already show two levels (Hn 2 sub. p, Hn 3 pp) for one fill.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the listener's ear must go to the primary element and not be diverted by the background; PDF 14: the background is the secondary level. Ex. 2A (PDF 18): the cor anglais solo is mf and the flutes' "sweetly" line mp < mf, over strings, harp, bass and drums at p. Ex. 279 (PDF 211): a background that shadows the solo too closely "distorts the aural focus". c308 kept sub. p at 121 because there the tune is in the violins and the basis in horns and clarinets, the most dissimilar timbres, while Rimsky (PDF 133) asks for the weaker basis when the timbres are close. At 277 the tune is in woodwinds and the fill is woodwind, horn and viola in the same octave, so c308's own reading of Rimsky asks for pp here. The change also removes c308's other objection (one horn split from the group): now Hn 2 and Hn 3 match.

**Fix (applied, dA.ily).**
```
dAClOne 277: gis'2\pp( b'4~ ...      (was \subP)
dAClTwo 277: e'2\pp( e'4~ ...        (was \subP)
dAHnTwo 277: gis'2.--(\pp ...         (was \subP)
dAVla   277: e'2.\pp( | gis'2.) ...    (was \subP)
281: dAClOne c''4.\p\<( ...  dAClTwo c'4.\p\<( ...  dAHnTwo c'4.--\p\<( ...
```
The bass (Bsn 2, Vc arco) stays sub. p. At 281 Cl 1·2 and Hn 2 take up the tune's notes with Ob 1, the cellos and the violins, so they restate p there and crescendo with them to f at 285 (Sebesky Ex. 281: parts on one line carry the same marks). The violas start their crescendo at 281 from pp. Shared staves get equal marks at the same moment (Cl 1·2 pp, then p; Hn 1 rests while Hn 2 plays; Hn 3·4 pp, Hn 4 rests). In the MIDI the fill drops from velocity 44/37 to 37/29 under the flute and oboe at 57; from 281 on the velocities are as before.

### 2. Bars 121–124: the same fill at sub. p under the violins' tune. Considered, kept.

Sebesky's focus rule would also give pp here. But the tune is in Vln I and II in two octaves, about 26 players, and the fill is in wind timbres unlike theirs. c308's reason (Rimsky PDF 133; one gesture of sub. p after the ff tutti) holds. I did not change it.

### 3. Bars 141–144: Hn 1's G4 held flat for four bars (Ex. 277–278). Considered, kept.

A swell on the G would give the held note the "character" Ex. 277 asks for. But the link's point is the change from G to G♯ at 145, and only the G♯ swells (145–147). A swell on the G would take that contrast away. Kept.

### 4. Bars 57–68: fugato entries all p (Ex. 269: the repeat a level higher). Considered, kept.

c158 kept this citing the strings-only protection, which is about scoring, not levels. The better reason is the model: Beethoven 9/ii's fugato keeps its entries at one soft level and lets the added voices make the crescendo. That is what happens here (hairpins 67–68, f at 69, ff at 72). Kept.

### 5. Bars 105–120: horns ff above trumpets and trombones at f. Considered, kept.

Under Sebesky's focus rule the horns' ff countermelody and off-beats could cover the spiccato theme. c124, c154 and c274 balanced four horns at ff against two trumpets at f (Rimsky PDF 51: in loud passages one trumpet equals two horns). That reason holds. Kept.

### 6. Bars 349–356: Vln II section pp against the solo violin pp. Considered, kept.

c457 has just set this balance (twelve violins on the lower octave over violas ppp). The flute doubles the solo. I did not change it a second time.
