### 1. 81–84: the harp's accompaniment chords sit on the clarinet's own notes (applied)

**Problem.** In the F-major episode Cl 1 enters *p dolce* with the theme head (81: D5–A4–A4–D5; 83: B♭4–G4–G4–B♭4; 84: A4). The harp right hand accompanies with off-beat chords <d' f' a'>, <d' f' b♭'>, <d' g' b♭'>, <c' f' a'>, whose top notes are the clarinet's pitches on the same beats: A4 on beat 2 of 81, G4 and B♭4 on beats 2 and 3 of 83, A4 on beats 2 and 3 of 84. A plucked attack on the clarinet's note at the moment the clarinet plays it removes the line's outline exactly where it dips into its softest notes.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments." PDF 267 (Mozart Flute Concerto): when a soft wind solo enters, the accompaniment is reduced and kept out of its way. The harp's own melody at 73–80 has its chords two octaves below it (LH); the clarinet should have the same room.

**Fix (sB.ily, `sBHarpR`, bars 81–84).** Same harmony, tops at least a third below the clarinet's lowest note in each bar, still in the treble staff:
```
r4 <a d' f'>4 <a d' f'>4 | r4 <bes d' f'>4 <bes d' f'>4 | r4 <g bes d'>4 <g bes d'>4 | r4 <a c' f'>4 <a c' f'>4 |
```
(was `<d' f' a'>`, `<d' f' bes'>`, `<d' g' bes'>`, `<c' f' a'>`).

### 2. 189–192: the cellos' answer is covered and crossed by the harp (applied)

**Problem.** The trio's middle part is a dialogue: Ob 1 calls in C♯ minor (185–188), the cellos with the cor anglais answer a step lower (189–192, D♯4 down to F♯3). The harp keeps the off-beat chords it had under the oboe, in the same octave: <b dis' fis'> at 189–190 and <ais cis' fis'> at 192 lie on and above the answer (B3 and D♯4 on the cellos' B and D♯ in 189, B3 on 190's B, A♯3–F♯4 entirely above the held F♯3 in 192). Under the oboe the chords were a third or more below the line; under the answer they are inside it, so the answer, which should be the new colour, is the most covered phrase of the dialogue.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257–258: dialogue works because the answer comes in "a radically different orchestral colour" and is not duplicated (Beethoven's answering strings play pizzicato "to avoid duplication of the solo sound"). PDF 274: keep the accompaniment registrally distant. The oboe is silent at 189–192, so the register above the answer is free.

**Fix (tB.ily, `tBHarpR`, bars 189–192).** Chords up an octave, pedal marks unchanged:
```
r4 <b' dis'' fis''>4 <b' dis'' fis''>4 | r4 <b' dis'' fis''>4_\markup \small "C♯ A♯" <b' dis'' fis''>4 | r4 <gis' b' e''>4 <gis' b' e''>4 | r4 <ais' cis'' fis''>4 <ais' cis'' fis''>4 |
```
The harp now frames the answer from above, as it framed the call from below, which also gives the two halves of the dialogue different spacings.

### 3. 209–224: the violas' off-beats lie above the tune's lower octave (applied)

**Problem.** At the trio's return T is forte in Vln I and Fl 1, with its lower octave in Vln II and Cl 1 (G♯4–A5). Since c009 the violas, divided, play the off-beats in E4–B4 (<e' b'>, <dis' a'>, <e' a'> …). That puts them on or above the lower octave: B4 over Vln II's G♯4 and A4 at 209, over G♯4 at 212, unisons on A4 (213, 220) and B4 (210, 216, 221). In the first statement of the doubled tune (169–184) the same off-beats are voiced B3–E4, clear below Vln II; at 209 the voicing climbed an octave and the lower octave of the melody turned into an inner part.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274 (registral distance of a simple accompanying figure); PDF 279: Bizet keeps the instruments separate from the line and adds the first violins' octave only at the climax, so the octave doubling is a strengthening of the line, not a layer that the accompaniment can cross. PDF 260: when accompaniment and melody share a range, the accompaniment must be kept to simple figuration distinct from the line; here it is distinct in rhythm but not in register.

**Fix (tC.ily, `tCVla`, bars 209–224).** The 169–184 voicing, same pitch classes, same mf, same louré slurs and hairpin:
```
r4 <b e'>4--(\mf^\markup \italic "div." <b e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a dis'>4--( <a dis'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) |
r4 <a e'>4--( <a e'>4--) | r4 <a fis'>4--( <a fis'>4--) | r4 <ais e'>4--( <ais e'>4--) | r4 <b dis'>4--( <b dis'>4--) |
r4 <gis e'>4--( <gis e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a cis'>4--( <a cis'>4--) | r4 <a fis'>4--( <a fis'>4--) |
r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a dis'>4--( <a dis'>4--) | r4 <gis e'>4--(\> <gis e'>4--) | r4 <gis e'>4--( <gis e'>4--) |
```
The violas now lie between the cellos' countermelody (B2–A3) and Vln II, as they do at 169. This does not undo c009 (Vln II keeps the tune; the violas keep the off-beats). Hn 1's held B4 at 209–210 still sits above Vln II's G♯4; it is p and sustained, so I left it.
