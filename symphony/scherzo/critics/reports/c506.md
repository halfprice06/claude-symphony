## What else I studied

I read the earlier passes on this reading (c056, c146, c236, c326, c416) and HISTORY.md's entries for the strokes at 56, 184 and 329. c416 damped 200, 287, 323 and 339 and made a rule: "timpani strokes before a rest or a change of harmony are damped (staccato) unless meant to ring." The stroke at 184 was added later (c453) and the stroke at 329 was not in c416's list. I parsed v506/score.midi with mido and printed, for every timpani onset, the pitch classes sounding with it and on the next downbeat (script /tmp/critic-506/chk.py, with c416's bars.py for beat-by-beat listings of 53–57, 129–132, 184–186, 329–331 and other spots). I compared the three second-idea cadences: G major 53–56, E minor 129–132 and 285–288.

## Findings

### 1. The G-major cadence's timpani stroke comes a bar late (55–56). Applied.
- **Bars.** 53–56. The second idea's hemiola cadence in G arrives on the bass G1 at 55 (upper voices 9–8 over it); 56 is the closing chord, cut off on beat 1 before the strings-only fugato.
- **Problem.** The timpani strike G2 at 56, not at 55. The two E-minor statements of the same cadence strike on the arrival: B roll 129–130, E at 131 with the bass E1; B roll 285–286, E at 287. In G there is no D drum, so no roll on V is possible. That makes the single tonic stroke the only timpani part of the cadence, and at present it marks the cut-off chord, not the arrival. A listener hears the bass land at 55 without the drum and then the drum arrive one bar later.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 79: the timpani's classical role is to strengthen the tonic and dominant bass notes "especially in consummating climactic cadence points". PDF 80: the part must show "how long a note may ring". c416 kept the stroke at 56 for its damping before the fugato (PDF 141: percussion must not obscure the cellos' pp entry at 57). That reason concerns where the sound stops, not where the stroke is. Moving the attack to 55 and keeping the stop at 56 meets both reasons.
- **Fix (applied).** sA.ily, sATimp: `R2.*27 | g,4-.\f r4 r4 |` → `R2.*26 | g,2.\f~ | g,4-. r4 r4 |`. One f stroke on the arrival, let ring through the held G chord, damped on beat 1 of 56 together with the orchestra (the tied note means "do not restrike", the dot means "damp", as Ex. XII-47 ends a roll on a short tied note). The fugato at 57 still starts in silence. Bar count unchanged; no part cue is affected (the timpani part's cues are at 85, 89 and 181).

### 2. The B at 184 rings into the bare C♯-minor bar at 185. Applied.
- **Bars.** 184 (B2 pp, the trio's half close in B, added by c453), 185 (C♯ minor: Cb pizz. C♯2, harp off-beat chord, Ob 1 solo; nothing else).
- **Problem.** A crotchet followed by rests, no damping sign. A timpani stroke rings well past the 0.79 s bar at the trio tempo, so the B2 sounds on under the C♯ bass in the thinnest texture of the trio, the only note there that is not in the chord.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 58 (a timpani pitch outside the harmony), PDF 80 (how long a note may ring), PDF 141 (percussion obscuring detail: here the oboe's call). c416's rule names exactly this case: a change of harmony.
- **Fix (applied).** tA.ily, tATimp: `b,4\pp r4 r4` → `b,4-.\pp r4 r4`, with a c506 comment.

### 3. The E at 329 rings a second above the basses' D at 330. Applied.
- **Bars.** 329 (E2 mf, hard sticks, the first bar of the coda's falling bass E–D–C–B), 330 (D major over D2 in Cb, Cbsn and B. cl.), 331 (F over C).
- **Problem.** The undamped E2 rings into 330, a major second above the D2 bass in the same register, and smears the step that the whole coda build rests on (c032, c033: Vln I rising against the falling bass). This is the case Strauss describes on Adler PDF 58.
- **Fix (applied).** coda.ily, codaTimp: `e,4\mf r4 r4` → `e,4-.\mf r4 r4`, with a c506 comment.

### Largest-change test
Finding 1 is the change a listener hears most: the drum now lands on the G-major cadence with the bass, as it does at the two E-minor cadences. I also weighed and rejected:
- A soft timpani ostinato under the trio tune or the coda's T (Debussy, PDF 126): rejected for c416's reasons (c414 keeps 341–356 for strings; it would use up the closing motive x).
- A second G stroke at 56 as well as 55: rejected. The E-minor cadences have one stroke, and two f strokes on the same pitch a bar apart would be heavier than any other cadence in the movement (PDF 106: "the less the better").
