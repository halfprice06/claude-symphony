## Findings

### 1. Codetta 226–227: a passing augmented fifth into the first IV (applied)

**Problem.** The codetta (225–240) states T's head three times over the E pedal, each statement an octave lower: Ob 1 at 225, Cl 1 at 229, Bsn 1 at 233. Each statement turns to the subdominant: IV at 227, IV at 231, iv at 235. The third turn has its own colour, C♯–C♮–B (6–♭6–5, c501). The second has its own bass step, G♯–A (c343). The first has nothing of its own. Bars 225–228 are I – I – IV – I, with Vln II holding B4–B4–A4–B4. The reading's most common chromatic move from I to IV is missing here. The trio's other statements do take that step at the same place, by the D♮ of V7 of IV at 172 and 212.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 537–538, Ex. 30-11 (the final bars of Carmen): the augmented 5th is "a chromatic passing tone moving up from the perfect 5th of a major triad … to the 3rd of another major chord", over I–IV, and it "makes the tonic sound like V of IV".
- PDF 538, Ex. 30-12: in a repetition, the ♯5 is heard "as a variant of the perfect 5th (which … appears in the original statement)". Here the original form is the plain I–IV at 157, and the variants are the D♮ forms at 172 and 212.
- PDF 531–532: contrasting forms of one degree work as a motive. With the change, the first statement rises B–B♯–C♯ and the third falls C♯–C♮–B. The same pitch appears as ♯5 (spelled B♯) and as ♭6 (spelled C♮), and it frames the codetta's IV–iv contrast without replacing it. c402 and c512 protect that contrast. This change keeps it and gives it a counterpart.
- PDF 559–560 and 564: a raised tone that leads upward is spelled sharp, hence B♯ and not C.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-522/work/sections/tC.ily).**
```
tCVlnTwo 225–228:  b'2.\pp | b'2. | a'2. | b'2. |
                -> b'2.\pp | b'2( bis'4 | cis''2 a'4 | b'2.) |
tCHarpR  226:      r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4
                -> r4 <gis e'>4 <gis e'>4
```
Bar 226 beat 3 is now E–G♯–B♯:
- Cl 2 E4, Vla G♯4, harp G♯3/E4, Vln II B♯4, under Ob 1's E6.
- B♯4 rises to C♯5 at 227.
- At 227.3, Vln II steps to A4, the root of IV. It returns to B4 at 228 in contrary motion to Ob 1's C♯6–B5.

The harp drops its B3 at 226. A harp B3 struck on beat 2 would ring against B♯4. The harp cannot take the B♯ itself, because its B string sounds B♮ at 226.2 and 228.2.

Voice leading:
- B♯–C♯ in Vln II moves against Ob 1's E6–C♯6 in contrary motion into the octave.
- With the bass E–A, the interval goes from an augmented 5th to a 10th.
- There are no new parallels, and analysis.txt still lists only bar 69.
- Nothing forms C–E–G or B–D–F–G♯.
- The tune, the harp's bass, Cl 2, the violas, the dynamics and the Ob 1 crescendo into 227 are unchanged.

The engraving shows B minim, ♯B crotchet, C♯ minim, A crotchet and B dotted minim under one pp slur, at 9 beats, which is an easy single bow. There is no page change. No part cue points at Vln II in 222–236. themecheck reports 52/52, because the codetta heads are not recorded statements.

### 3. Tchaikovsky's chromaticized I–V4/3–I6 (Ex. 30-24) cannot be used in the trio

This is the reading's 3/4 model, and it is in a waltz. In E, the first passing °7 is E♯–G♯–B–D, which is the excluded stop chord. The second, F𝄪–A♯–C♯–E, needs a bass F♯–G♯ into I6. T always reaches its I6 from vi, with the bass C♯–G♯, at 161–162, 177–178, 217–218 and 349–350. No change.

### 4. ♮4–♯4 °7s (Ex. 31-14) elsewhere: not applied

- **164 and 180.** An A♯ on beat 3 would clash with T's A4. The °4/3 over A (A–C–D♯–F♯) would spend the C♮ that c492 saved for 220.
- **128 and 284.** The bass F♯–A–A♯–B would copy 87–88 and touch c492's protected °7 treatment.

The chain sounds once, at 85–89, and that is its right place: it is the return from ♭II.

### 5. Coda 329–332: keep the bass diatonic

Ex. 31-2 sets the chromatic line in the bass. Here it lies in Cl 1 and the cor anglais (G–F♯–F–E–D♯) above the bass E–D–C–B, and the two lines move in 10ths at 329, 330, 331.3 and 332. PDF 564 calls 10ths "as important an organizing element in chromatic textures". A chromatic bass would also lose c443's clean ♮VII at 330 and the F on beats 1–2 of 331 that finale IV 272 relies on. No change.

### 6. Devices blocked by the cyclic rules

- The common-tone °7 on V (PDF 546) is B–D–F–G♯.
- The common-tone augmented sixth (PDF 547), the German sixth inside 6–10 exchanges (PDF 574–575) and chromatic parallel 6/3s in E minor (PDF 552) all contain C–E–G.
- V7♯5 (PDF 542) needs F𝄪 against T's F♯ at 166, 222 and 355.

None can be used, and I propose nothing for them.

### 7. The close, 357–360, re-checked

At 358 the chord is E–G–A♯–C♯ over E, spelled with ♭3 as in Ex. 30-21. The G in Vln II falls to E at 359 instead of returning to G♯. That is forced: a held G under A–C–E would make A–C–E–G, which contains the C-major triad. The ♭3 spelling carries the scherzo's minor into the major close, which is the reason Ex. 30-21 gives. Keep it.
