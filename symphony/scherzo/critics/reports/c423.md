## Findings

### 1. Bars 229–232: the held Vln II E5 sits just above Cl 1's soft solo; Vln II rests (applied)

**Problem.** The trio codetta passes T's head down through three registers: Ob 1 at 225 (G♯5–C♯6), Cl 1 at 229 (G♯4–C♯5) and Bsn 1 at 233 (G♯3–C4). Each statement is p dolce over a pp sustained accompaniment. Under the oboe and the bassoon, the held string notes lie an octave or more away. Under the clarinet, Vln II holds E5 for four bars, a sixth above its G♯4 and a minor third above its C♯5 peak. The clarinet is then an inner voice under a high violin pedal instead of the top line. The analyser's profile shows the result: the top of the texture at 229–232 is E5, not the clarinet's C♯5.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments, it is an important idea to remember." Here the accompaniment and the solo are both sustained, so rhythmic contrast does not offset the closeness.
- Adler PDF 267 (Mozart, Flute Concerto, Ex. XVII-10): when the soft wind soloist enters, "the orchestra is reduced drastically". PDF 268–269: the transparent texture "in which the entrance is couched" is what presents the soloist.
- c153's own plan for the codetta is that the accompaniment thins by four-bar units as the head falls. The harp has already gone from beats 2–3 to beat 2 at 229. The violin pedal is the one layer that rises there instead.
- c047 moved Vln II from B4 (unison with the clarinet's B4) to E5 to clear a unison. Its reason was to stop the clash, not to put a pedal over the solo. A rest clears the unison as well, and it also gives the registral distance Adler asks for.

**Fix (applied).** tC.ily, tCVlnTwo: bars 229–232 `R2.*4` (were `e''2. | e''2. | e''2. | e''2.`). Vln II comes back pp on B4 at 233 (`b'2.\pp`), an octave and more above Bsn 1's statement, as before.


Under the clarinet the harmony stays complete: violas G♯3–A3, Hn 4 E3, harp beat 2 (G♯3–B3–E4), pizz. bass E–G♯–A–E (c343). The clarinet is now the top voice for its four bars (profile: top C♯5). The codetta's three statements are now heard as one melodic descent Ob → Cl → Bsn. No theme, key or bar number changes.

### 2. Timpani motive x joining the two statements of T at 168 (Beethoven VC, PDF 273): considered, rejected

This was the most audible change the reading suggested. In Beethoven's Violin Concerto, the timpani's opening motive returns softly between the phrases of a lyrical passage. Our movement also opens with a timpani motive (9–12), and a pp `e,4 b,8 b,8 e,4` at 168 would bring it into the trio. I rejected it. The timpani's motive x is the scherzo's own signal, and its return pp on B at 241 is what announces the retransition. Sounding it inside the trio would weaken that return. This is c056's reason ("holding back the timpani pays off") applied to the form, and the reading does not outweigh it.

### 3. Trio return 209–216: save the violins' upper octave for the climax at 217 (Bizet, PDF 279): considered, rejected

Bizet doubles the voice at the higher octave only at her climax. Holding Vln I down in the G♯4 octave until 217 would open a new register at the A6 climax. But Hn 1's mp line B4–C♯5 and Bsn 1's E4–F♯4 pad lie inside that octave (c047, c406). Without Vln I on top, the tune would be tangled with them, which goes against PDF 274. A cleaner climax is not worth a less clear return. Kept.

### 4. Bar 341: the T recall starts together with its background (PDF 268–270): kept

After the G.P., Hn 3, the harp off-beats, the violas' pp A3 and the pizz. bass all start on one downbeat. The Sibelius and Bartók rule would set the background first. Doing that needs added bars before 341. That would renumber 357–376, which movement I and the finale quote. The G.P. already gives the silence for the horn to enter into. Kept.

### 5. Reprise 261–268: the flute doubles the cellos' tenor line two octaves up in the second phrase (Haydn, PDF 288): considered, rejected

The cellos' line two octaves up (C6–B5–A5…G5–E6) would cross Vln I's theme around its C6 peak at 264, which is protected at pitch. c243 also kept the heterophony for both phrases. Kept.

### 6. Proportions and tempo pacing: kept

278 s, with a scherzo of 87 s, a trio of 70 s, a reprise of 49 s and a coda of 44 s. The only slowings are the printed poco rit. bars, and the accelerando 241–252 rises in two-bar steps (Mahler's "nicht schleppen", PDF 282–283). Nothing in this reading supports a different length.

### 7. 120→121: ff off-beats into sub. p without an overlapping chord: kept

Adler's overlap technique (PDF 190) is for a held loud chord. Here the ff ends on staccato off-beats and is followed by a new phrase, sub. p, with held horns and lower strings (the held tonic of PDF 267). The cut-off is the intended effect.
