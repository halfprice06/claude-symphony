## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 79→80: the horn pair moves from a d5 into a P5 out of vii°7 of F (applied)
**Problem.** In the F-major episode, bar 79 is vii°7 of F: E2 (B. cl.), G3 (Hn 4), B♭3 (Bsn 1), D♭4 (Hn 3), with harp E–G–B♭. The two horns on one staff hold G3 and D♭4, a diminished 5th (2 and ♭6 of F). Both fall a step to F3 and C4, a perfect 5th. This is pp, under the harp's melody, in the only sustained wind voices. The harp LH's G3 makes the same fifths with Hn 3 a beat later (its F3 enters at 80.2).
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 375, §4 and Ex. 22-3c: "if the upper dissonance forms a diminished 5th, the similar motion that follows creates an obtrusive set of hidden 5ths; avoid this voice leading." Ex. 22-3a gives the correct version: 2 rises to 3 and the tonic has a doubled 3rd. PDF 393 (§6, Chopin Ex. 23-9): vii°7 in major is treated exactly as in minor.
**Fix (applied, sB.ily).**
- `sBHnFour` at 79–80: `g2.-- | a2) r4` (was `g2.-- | f2) r4`). G3–D♭4 now closes to A3–C4.
- `sBHarpL` at 79: `e,4 <e bes>2` (was `<e g bes>`).
The chord at 79 is unchanged: G is still in Hn 4 and the harp RH melody. The F chord at 80 has a doubled 3rd, as in Ex. 22-3a. No new parallels result. c042's D–D♭–C in Hn 3 and B♭–A in Bsn 1 are unchanged.

### 2. 85–88: the 7th of D♯°7 enters by a leap because Hn 3 and Bsn 1 cross (applied)
**Problem.** At 85–86 (N6 over A), Hn 3 holds C4 and Bsn 1 holds A3. At 87 (D♯°7 over A) they exchange: Hn 3 drops C4→A3 and Bsn 1 leaps A3→C4. So the chord's 7th (C) enters by a leap in a new instrument, while the instrument that held it moves away. At 88 that bassoon C rises to C♯4 and Hn 3 falls to G3. The sound of each chord is right, but the lines cross for no reason. Both common tones between the N6 and the °7, A and C, change instrument at the moment they become dissonant.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 377–378, §7 and Ex. 22-7a–c: when °7 follows IV, II6 or VI, "the 7th is prepared as a common tone". In Ex. 22-7b (II6 → °6/5), the common tone is tied in one voice. The N6 here stands in for II6 (as c072 noted, PDF 561–562). PDF 375: the 7th of a seventh chord is a line that resolves by step.
**Fix (applied, sB.ily).**
- `sBHnThree` at 85–88: `c'2.--( | c'2.-- | c'2.-- | cis'2.--)` (was `… | a2.-- | g2.--)`).
- `sBBsnOne` at 85–88: `a2. | a2. | a2. | g2.` (was `… | c'2. | cis'2.`).
Every chord has the same pitches as before. Each voice now holds or moves by step. c072's rising A–A♯ in the bass, c042's F→F♯ in Hn 4, and c127's low Cl 2 are unchanged. Hn 3 already had a breath at 80 and holds no longer than before.

### 3. 126→127: Bsn 1's F♯3 makes hidden fifths with the ♭9 C4 (applied)
**Problem.** At 126 (V♭9 after iv6, second idea in E minor), the ♭9 C4 is in Cl 2, the violas and Hn 3, with Bsn 1 F♯3 a d5 below. At 127 everything moves down a step together: C4→B3 and F♯3→E3, a d5 into a P5 against three instruments. c082 changed Bsn 1 to F♯ so that the leading tone would not be doubled. That was right, but the F♯ then fell to E.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 375, Ex. 22-3a and 22-3c. The °7 above the B pedal (D♯–F♯–A–C) is treated like vii°7 (PDF 378, §9). Its 2 (F♯) under ♭6 (C) should rise to 3.
**Fix (applied, sC.ily).** `sCBsnOne` at 127: `g2( fis4~` (was `e2( fis4~`). F♯3–C4 now closes to G3–B3. c082's complete V♭9 at 126 and its single D♯ (Hn 2) are kept. The 3rd of i is doubled (Cl 1 G4, Bsn 1 G3), as in Ex. 22-3a. Bsn 1 still joins Bsn 2 on F♯3 at beat 3.

### Considered, no change
- **282→283** (reprise of 126). Hn 1 holds the ♭9 C5 over Hn 2 and the cellos, who play the tune's lower octave (c071), F♯4→E4 under C5→B4: the same d5 into P5. Every fix I tried costs more than it saves. Resolving Hn 1 early to B4 puts the root a 7th above the ♭9 C4 in the violas. Moving it to A4 doubles the 7th (Cl 1) in unison into G4. A rest breaks c088's phrase slur. Taking C an octave lower crosses the horn pair. I keep it. It is the exposed tune against the horn line, so the effect is weaker than 126's, and c082 protects the common-tone ♭9 here.
- **266→267.** The cellos' B3→A3 against the melody's F5→E5 is d5 into P5 only on the last quaver of 266, where F is the motto's passing ♭2. No change.
- **87→88.** Cl 2's D♯3 falls to C♯3. Raising it to E3 would make octaves with Cl 1's D♯4→E4. No change.
