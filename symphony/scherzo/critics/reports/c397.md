## Findings

### 1. Coda 325–328: make the stretto a real stretto (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's build is described as a stretto. In fact it is a one-bar figure piled up in one rhythm. The cellos play motive x on E (325), the violas on B (326), Vln II on E5 (327) and Vln I on B5 (328), and every voice repeats E–B–B–E or B–F♯–F♯–B each bar. Each new entry doubles the voice below at the fifth or the octave, beat for beat. Two things follow. First, the entries are not heard as entries, only as more volume. Second, analysis.txt lists six outer-voice parallels at 326–328, which earlier cycles marked "by design". The texture has one rhythm and one harmony (a bare E–B fifth) for four bars.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 44 and 47 (Bartók, mm. 364–386): the stretto Adler calls "especially thrilling" has entries a bar apart in three trumpets and three trombones. Each voice continues the subject after it enters, so in every bar one voice holds a dotted minim while the next plays quavers. The strings join only afterwards, sustained, p cresc., and the whole orchestra takes the subject together at 386.
- PDF 42 (Hindemith): in a fugato "the subject comes through clearly every time it is played". Here a new voice cannot come through when it doubles an existing voice in the same rhythm.
- This score already has the right model. In the fugato's stretto (69–72) Vln I continues with the chromatic tail (C6–B5–B♭5) while Vln II enters with x. c297's reason for keeping the coda (a one-bar ostinato has no continuation) is answered by theme A itself. Its head is x plus the tail, the same two-bar subject the fugato uses.

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily).** Each voice enters with x as before (same bars, same entry pitches, same pp, same crescendo from 328). The voice that entered the bar before then continues with the tail, which resolves into the next downbeat. The violas take the tail on B's degrees, as the fugato's answers take it on D's.
```
codaVc     = { e4-.\pp b,8-. b,-. e4-. | g2->( fis8 f8 | e4-.) b,8-. b,-. e4-. | e4-.\< b,8-. b,-. e4-. | ...
codaVla    = { R2. | b4-.\pp b8-. b-. b4-. | d'2->( cis'8 c'8 | b4-.)\< b8-. b-. b4-. | ...
codaVlnTwo = { R2.*2 | e''4-.\pp e''8-. e''-. e''4-. | g''2->(\< fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.)\mf\< b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | ...
codaVlnOne = { R2.*3 | b''4-.\pp\< b''8-. b''-. b''4-. | ...
```
The three upper entries use motive x's repeated-note form (B3, E5, B5). The movement already uses that form: the timpani's B–B–B–B at 89–100 and 241–252, recorded in themes-scherzo.md. If the leaping form were used, the violas' F♯3 would fall under the cellos' held G3 at 326, and Vln I's F♯5 under Vln II's G5 at 328. With the repeated-note form no voice crosses. The bars now read:
- 326: G3 tail under repeated B3.
- 327: E pedal with the viola line D4–C♯4–C4 → B3. There is no G, so no C-major triad.
- 328: E minor, with Vln II's tail G5–F♯5–F5 resolving to E5 at 329, where all four strings join in x as before.

The composite rhythm now has quavers on beats 2 and 3 instead of beat 2 only, so the build moves faster into the Più mosso. The order of entry stays bottom-up, which was c279's reason. Pianissimo holds until 328 and the crescendo runs to 333 (c028). Bars 329–339 are unchanged, and so is the bass E–D–C–B. Outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt fall from 7 to 1 (only bar 69 remains). This undoes c297's decision for the reason given above. It does not undo any earlier change to the notes.

### 2. Bar 45: Hn 1·2 enter the second idea without a dynamic (applied)

**Problem.** The horns play D4/A3 on beat 1 of 44 and rest. At 45 they start the second idea's four-bar sustained chords, a new phrase, with no mark. The strings and bassoons print f. The intended level is c147's mf, set at 43, but a horn player counting rests reads the bare chord at 45 as f, like everyone else.

**Evidence.** PDF 50 and 52 (Druckman): "This score is meticulously marked, and that is exactly what one must do". Every brass entry gets its own instruction. My scan of all brass entries after a rest found this as the only new phrase without a printed dynamic.

**Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily).** `b'2.--(\mf` in Hn 1 and `g2.--(\mf` in Hn 2 at 45, so the shared staff has equal simultaneous dynamics. On page 2 of the scratch build, mf prints once under the Hn 1·2 staff and the layout does not change.

### 3. 305–320: one horn on the tenor countermelody (checked, kept)

PDF 44 says two horns balance one trumpet. At 105–120 Hn 3·4 play a2 on the tenor line. At 305–320 Hn 3 plays it alone, because Hn 4 fills the octave above the bass (c114). Since c394, Vln II doubles theme A at pitch at 309–320. This makes the melody heavier, but it does not change the tenor line's competition, which is the two trumpets' motive x on odd bars. c114's reason still holds: without trombones, Hn 4 is the only sustained brass between Bsn 2's E2 and the tenor line's G3. Kept.

### 4. 89–100: no brass punctuation of the timpani (checked, kept)

Hindemith's m. 192 (PDF 44) has the brass punctuating a theme played by the timpani. Here the timpani's motive x answers the head entries (c327), with pizzicato B below and no brass. Brass chords at 91, 94 and 97 would use up the brass entry at 101, the pp build that the reading's own order prefers (Bartók brings the horns in after the exposition, PDF 46). The tempo-of-three phrasing would also lose its quiet. Kept.

### 5. 57–68 and 289–296: fugato without brass (checked, kept)

The two brass fugatos (PDF 42–46) do not displace c297's reason. A brass entry in a p string exposition would be the loudest voice, against PDF 43's principle (row 4). A horn entry would also carry the chromatic tail, and since c390 that tail belongs to a solo horn only in the three motto quotations.

### 6. 69–72 and 297–300: stretto (checked, follows)

The entries are a bar apart (Vln I/Fl 1/Ob 1, then Vln II/Cl 1, then Vc/Bsn 1 with Hn 1·2 a2). Each voice continues after its entry, and the horns enter over sustained bassoons while the strings move in crotchets (PDF 46–47, rows 9 and 12). No change.
