## Findings

The orchestration of the melodies follows this chapter closely; four earlier passes have applied what it offers (c381 sixths at 345–348, c471 cor anglais at 45–55, c291 rise into 336). I looked for the one change this reading supports that a listener would hear most, built it, and rejected it on the evidence below. The score is unchanged.

### 1. Theme A's first wind call, 29–35: Ob 1 alone instead of Fl 1 + Ob 1 in unison (built, tested, not kept)

Problem examined. The first time the winds speak theme A (29–31, 33–35, p leggiero, strings silent) Fl 1 and Ob 1 play it in unison at E5–A5. PDF 66–67 says unison doubling "possesses the disadvantage of restricting the variety of colour and expression" and that phrases needing individual expression "should be entrusted to solo instruments of simple timbres"; doubling belongs to loud, broad passages. PDF 65 says that softly, in the low register of the pair, the flute leads. PDF 36 (ch. I, to which PDF 64 refers) gives "distinct and penetrating staccato passages" to the oboe. A solo oboe would make the call one clear colour, would make Fl 1's first entry at 37 (the "together" bars) a new colour, and would give theme A three separate solo wind colours (Ob 29, Fl 253/261, Bsn 269).

Test. In /tmp/critic-561/work I set sAFluteOne to `R2.*16 | R2.*8 | g''4-.\p\<^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "leggiero" } d''8-. ...` (bars 29–36 rest) and added a Vln I cue at 35 for 2 bars to part 01 in parts-cues.json (Fl 1 would otherwise rest 36 bars before 37). The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, cmajor "none", one outer-voice parallel (69, as in v561), theme record 52/52. Page 2 engraved correctly. The tested copy is kept at /tmp/critic-561/candidate-solo-oboe.

Why I did not keep it. The protection on this call (c054, c311, c409) has two reasons. c054's (no flute an octave higher, no early A6) does not apply to removing the flute. c311's does: the flute rounds the oboe's edge, which is the sound the composer chose. The corpus supports c311. Dvořák 9/iii is this movement's main model, and its first soft wind statement of the scherzo theme (bars 13–20) is flute II and oboe I in unison at B5, p staccato, with clarinet I answering. That is the scoring at 29–35 now. PDF 65 also lists Fl + Ob as a natural unison, "sweeter than that of the oboe". The PDF 66–67 rule is general, while the model is a scherzo theme in this same situation. A solo oboe would give a thinner, more rustic call and would make the movement less like its model. So the change is a sideways move, not an improvement, and it breaks a protection whose reason still holds. I reverted it. The scratch copy (/tmp/critic-561/work) is again identical to v561 and its rebuild is clean.

### 2. Coda 341–344: Hn 3 alone on T's first phrase (no change)

PDF 75 says a wind added to a brass instrument gives a finer legato. PDF 72 says the horn's "genial and poetic tone in piano passages" is the reason to give it the melody, and Ex. 73 (Antar, PDF 245) is such a solo horn line. The phrase is p dolce, written F♯4–E5 for the horn, in the "full, clear, ringing" middle register of PDF 71. c438 keeps the coda's T in single players. No change, as in c381 and c471.

### 3. Second idea 45–55: lowest octave in violas, cellos and cor anglais (no change)

I checked whether the lowest octave is now heavier than the top. Rimsky's model layout (PDF 78, Sheherazade iii [M]) is Vn I+Fl / Vn II+Ob / Vc+C.a. The score adds the violas to the lowest octave (c419, c481). PDF 77 names "Cellos + Violas + Eng. horn" (Snegourotchka [5]) among the best unisons, so the reading supports the present lowest octave. Taking the violas off would also undo c481's viola–cello coupling. No change.

### 4. Trio return 209–216: Vln I alone on the top octave (no change)

PDF 78 recommends that winds take both octaves and strings double only the lower one. Here Vln I is alone on top and Cl 1 doubles Vln II below until Fl 1 enters at 217. Ex. 90 (PDF 265) is a soft passage. Here the top violins are a full section at f, and Fl 1's entry at 217 is how the statement grows into the A6 (c070, c471). Ob 1 would reach F♯6 at 214. No change.

### 5. Coda 333–335: three winds with Vln II on the head (no change)

PDF 76 says several winds in unison with one string group overpower it. At 333–335 Ob 2 and Cl 1 (and Ob 1 at 334) double Vln II at E5. This is the ff tutti, the case where PDF 66–67 allows doubling, and c381 checked the Cl 1/C.a. octave here. No change.

### 6. Hn 1 motto tail 205–208, 357–360 (no change)

PDF 73 says chromatic melody suits brass less but "may sometimes be allotted" to them. It is four notes, soft, and quoted by movements II and IV. The reading gives no reason stronger than the cyclic one.
