## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 218–219: the horn pair holds open fifths under the strings' tune at the trio's climax (applied)
**Problem.** In T's forte return (209–224) the strings and Cl 1 carry the tune, the basses the bass, and Hn 1·2 with Bsn 1 hold the middle. The horn pair is in open fifths or fourths in seven of the sixteen bars: 211, 215, 216, 218, 219, 220, 222. At 218–220, the climb to A6, it is E4–B4, F♯4–C♯5, F♯4–C♯5. At 218 (I6 over G♯) no middle voice sounds the chord's third. c004 fixed this pair to thirds in v05; later voice-leading fixes (c047, c093) brought the fifths back, and c086 protected the pair while describing it as thirds and sixths.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 97, Remark 4: when the top part is in the strings, "there remains nothing for the wind except the sustained harmony in the two middle parts", which should use "no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths". PDF 90, rule 4: two instruments of one colour get concords, and fourths and fifths count as discords. PDF 85: harmonic notes must not be missing from the upper parts. The per-player scan also showed Hn 1·2 moving in parallel fifths 218→219 (C♯5/F♯4 after B4/E4), which the change removes.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily, Hn 1 bars 217–220:
`gis'2.--(\< | gis'2.-- | a'2.--\mf\> | cis''2.-- |` (was `gis'2.--(\< | b'2.-- | cis''2.--\mf\> | cis''2.-- |`). Hn 1·2 now hold E–G♯ and F♯–A. Left as they are: 211 and 222 (c047 keeps the seventh A in Bsn 1, and the horns must not sound D♯ under T's 4–3), 215–216 (c093: A♯ only in the violas), 220 (a third there makes octaves with the bass F♯→A or with the tune A→G♯). This answers c086: its stated principle (thirds and sixths) is what the change restores.

### 2. 169–184: the harp plays through the strings' statement of T, forty bars without a rest (applied)
**Problem.** The harp's left hand plays every downbeat from 153 to 192, in unison with the basses' pizzicato. At 169–184 c074 made the statement "the strings' own" (Fl 1 rests), but the harp keeps going, so the trio's first 40 bars have the same plucked bass colour throughout and the harp's chords at 185 under the oboe call do not sound new.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 53: "the quality of wind instruments soon becomes wearisome; the same may be said of plucked strings … which should only be employed at reasonable intervals", while strings can be heard "for an almost indefinite period". PDF 52: plucked strings blend with plucked strings, so the basses' pizzicato alone keeps the same bass.
**Fix (applied).** tA.ily: `tAHarpL` bars 169–184 become `R2.*16`. tB.ily: `tBHarpL = { cis,4\p r4 r4 | …` (dynamic restated after the rest). The harp now plays 153–168, rests 16 bars, and returns at 185.

### 3. 197–200: Oboe 2 in its bottom fifth, under Cl 1, in unison with Hn 2 and in seconds and fourths with Oboe 1 (applied)
**Problem.** At the trio's forte Ob 2 holds E4–G♯4–F♯4–D♯4 at f. Cl 1 (T's first phrase, G♯4–B4) is above it. Hn 2 plays the same E4, F♯4 and D♯4. Against Ob 1's call it makes a major second (F♯5/E4), a major seventh (D♯5/E4), fourths and a tritone.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 90: rule 2 (normal order; his "to be avoided" example puts the oboe under the clarinets), rule 3 (matching registers), rule 4 (concords to two instruments of one colour, "specially" the oboe "with its penetrating quality"). The notes already sound in Hn 2 and Cl 2. The scan shows Ob 2 also made octaves with Bsn 1 at 199, which goes too.
**Fix (applied).** tB.ily, `tBOboeTwo`: bars 197–201 `R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. |` (was `e'2.\f | gis'2. | fis'2. | dis'2.\> | R2.\! |`). c039's purpose (no G♯4 under the tune's A4) is kept.

### 4. 41–43 and 273–275: the clarinet pair holds a tritone for two bars at f, Cl 2 doubling Hn 1 (applied)
**Problem.** On V7 of G, Cl 1 holds C5 and Cl 2 F♯4 for two bars, f, while Hn 1 has the same F♯4.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 90, rules 1 and 4: a tone either doubled throughout or not at all, and concords to two instruments of one colour.
**Fix (applied).** sA.ily `sAClTwo` and dA.ily `dAClTwo`: `a'2.~\f | a'2. | fis'2. |` (was `fis'2.~\f | fis'2.~ | fis'2. |`). The pair moves C–A to A–F♯ in thirds. F♯ stays in Hn 1 and Fl 2. The seventh's resolution at 44/45 (c052) is untouched.

### 5. 51: the bass of V4/3 is doubled in the clarinet and violas (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 51 is A7/E → D/F♯ (V4/3 → I6 of D, c032). The bass E is doubled at E4 in Cl 2 and violas, while the root A is in Hn 1 only.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 83, with the "Bad" example on that page: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts."
**Fix (applied).** sA.ily: `sAClTwo` `| a'2.~( | a'4 a'2) |` (was `| e'2( a'4~ | a'4 a'2) |`); `sAVla` `| a'2( d'4~ | d'4 e'2) |` (was `| e'2( d'4~ …`). The root is doubled. Hn 2's E3 is a held pedal (PDF 84) and Bsn 1 is the bass line itself. Neither C♯ nor the seventh G is doubled, and there are no new parallels.

### 6. 133–136 and 321–323: the cor anglais is enclosed between the two clarinets (applied)
**Problem.** In the dying close, Cl 1 G4, C.a. E4, Cl 2 B3 and Bsn 1 E3 hold the E-minor chord. The clarinet pair is split around the cor anglais. At the same chord at 269–270 the pair lies over the cor anglais in normal order.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 91–93: overlaying in normal order is the best method for close four-part harmony. If one colour is enclosed, "it must be between two different timbres" (PDF 92).
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily: cor anglais `b2.\p | b2. | b2.\> | …`, Cl 2 `e'2.\p | e'2. | e'2.\> | …`. Cl 2 takes its E4 by common tone from 320.
