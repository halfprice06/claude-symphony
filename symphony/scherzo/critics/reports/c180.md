## Findings

### 1. 86–87: the episode's ♭2̂ never falls when the music turns back to E. Let Cl 2 double F3 and take it down the diminished third (applied)

**Bars.** At 84–86 the F-major episode becomes E minor's Neapolitan sixth (F/A, bass A doubled). At 87 it moves over the held A to D♯°7, then A♯°7 at 88 and the cadential 6/4 and V at 89. The ♭2̂, F, is carried by Hn 4 (F3, 84–86) and by the harp's F3 (85–86). At 87 Hn 4 rises F3→F♯3 (c042's one-voice chromatic step, kept by c110) and the harp is damped (c116). Cl 1's F4 at 85 is a passing quaver that returns to A4. The D♯ of 87 arrives as a fresh pp attack in Cl 2 (D♯3) and as the end of Cl 1's arpeggio (D♯4).

**Problem.** A&S PDF 483 (sec. 3): "The main point to remember is that ♭2̂ tends to move down." Review point 3 (PDF 499) says the same. The direct step ♭2̂→♮2̂ in an inner voice is "possible" only "if ♭2̂ is doubled in an inner voice" (PDF 484, Ex. 28-3d). PDF 488 repeats the condition: "If ♭2̂ is doubled in an inner voice (the most usual disposition), it may move directly to ♮2̂" (Ex. 28-12a: one ♭2̂ goes to ♮2̂ while the other falls). In v180 the condition is not met. The only continuing F goes up, so the episode's thirteen bars of F, the longest-held ♭2̂ in the movement, end without the descent that defines the chord.

The lens adds a reason. In movement I and in the finale, ♭2̂ falls at the point where the Neapolitan gives way to V (I 173–174; IV 219). In the scherzo it falls at 331 (Vln I F6→D♯6, Cl 1 and Hn 3 F4→D♯4) but not at 86–87, where the scherzo states ♭II at length. That is the place A&S Ex. 28-17 marks as the large-scale I–♭II–V point.

**Fix (applied, sections/sB.ily, `sBClTwo`).** Cl 2 enters one bar earlier on F3, in unison with Hn 4, and falls the diminished third F3→D♯3 into its existing D♯3–C♯3:

```lilypond
% old (bars 86-88)
… | R2. | dis2.\pp | cis2. | …
% new
… | f2.\p\> | dis2.\pp | cis2. | …
```

**Result.**
- At 86 ♭2̂ is doubled (Hn 4, Cl 2). One copy goes to ♮2̂ (Hn 4 F3→F♯3, unchanged, so c042's single-voice step and c110's F→F♯ inflection stay). The other falls ♭2̂–♯7̂ (Cl 2 F3→D♯3). This is exactly Ex. 28-3d / 28-12a with the diminished third of Ex. 28-2a.
- D♯3 at 87 is now approached by step instead of entering cold in the low clarinet.
- No new cross relation: each voice makes its own chromatic step.
- Shared clarinet staff: Cl 1 is at p with a diminuendo through 86 to pp at 87. Cl 2 has p> at 86 and pp at 87, so the simultaneous dynamics are equal. The A clarinet's concert F3 is a comfortable chalumeau note.
- Checks: no parallels (Cl 2 moves from a unison with Hn 4 to a third; against Cl 1, P12 to an augmented 11th), the same 7 analyser parallels, and no page added. I checked p. 3 at 110 dpi: the note, p and hairpin print cleanly under Cl 1's line.
- Not a theme change.
