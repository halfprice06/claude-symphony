## Findings

The harmony follows Units 11–13 throughout. I found nothing that breaks one of their rules and that a change could fix without undoing a protected decision. **No score change is proposed or applied.** The points below are what I checked most closely, in the order they matter.

### 1. Bar 159: the trio's first half cadence has a seventh chord without its 3rd (kept)

**What is there.** At 158–159 the first statement of T is on ii, and Cl 1 falls F♯5–E5–D♯5–C♯5. At 158 the harp has `<a cis' fis'>`; at 159 it has `<cis' e' fis'>` over F♯1. The Hn 3·4 drone adds B3 and E3. So the chord at 159 is F♯–C♯–E (+B), with no A. A&S PDF 177 says root-position II7 is "virtually always" complete, and that when a tone is left out it is the 5th, with the root or 3rd doubled (PDF 180, Ex. 12-5c–d: "omit the 5th ... double the root or 3rd"). Without the 3rd, the chord is neither the plain ii7 nor the V7/V that statements 2 and 3 use (A♯ in the violas at 175, 215; c093).

**Why I keep it.** Two reasons outweigh the textbook point:
- c089 and c093 protect the first statement's plain harmony (153–168). c093 noted this very chord and left it.
- Every fix I tried makes a new fault. The harp's A3 would ring into the downbeat of 160, where Cl 1 plays the A♯4 appoggiatura (c001), and the two would clash a minor 9th apart at pp. The voicing `<a cis' e'>` needs a lower V at 160 to avoid 5ths (A3–E4 → B3–F♯4). That voicing then makes 5ths with the bass into 161 (B1–F♯3 → C♯2–G♯3). Putting the A in Hn 3 breaks the drone, which c262 protects.

The ii here works as an extended II with a passing 8–7 in the melody (PDF 177, Ex. 12-10), and the drone's E3 supplies the 7th. No change.

### 2. Bar 243: iv7 without its 3rd (kept, the C-major rule)

iv7 in E minor is A–C–E–G, and a complete chord would contain C–E–G. The cyclic rule (no C-major triad; c042's reading: no C–E–G sound) therefore overrides A&S PDF 177's completeness rule. The 7th G is repeated from the violas' G4 at 242 and falls to F♯ at 244 (PDF 177). This is correct preparation and resolution. No change.

### 3. E minor has no deceptive cadence (recorded, no change)

Unit 13 (PDF 203–205, Ex. 13-13 to 13-15) gives the deceptive cadence as the way to extend or repeat a phrase. In minor, IV6 can replace VI there: A&S's Handel example is V–iv6. Because VI (C major) is banned, the scherzo's E-minor music never uses a deceptive cadence. Every V in E minor goes to i (104→105, 126→127, 130→131, 252→253, 304→305, 338→339). V7–iv6 (A–C–E over C) is legal under the cyclic rule and would be the idiomatic alternative. But each candidate cadence is protected: the hemiola cadences (c002, c085), theme A's cadences (quoted in the finale), the V♭9 at 126/282 (c082) and the plain V7 at 101–104 (c172). Adding one would also change section lengths. The movement does not need it: the deceptive cadences in the trio (196–197, 200–201, 216–217) give the extension Unit 13 describes. No change.

### 4. Phrygian half cadence 125–126 / 281–282 (follows)

iv6 over C doubles the 3rd above the bass (E in Bsn 1, Cl 2, Hn 1, Vla), as Ex. 11-10a–c recommend. The bass falls by half step into the V♭9 (c082, protected). The soprano leaps A5–F♯6 instead of 4̂–5̂ (A–B), which PDF 169 allows ("This is not invariable"). Cl 1 holds the A as the 7th of V, so 4̂ still moves into the dominant in an inner voice.

### 5. The trio's deceptive cadences (follow)

At 196–197, D♯ (Hn 2, Cl 2) rises to E and the 7th A (Cl 1, Hn 1) falls to G♯. vi has its 3rd E in four voices and its root only in the bass, as Ex. 13-12 requires. At 200–201 (C♯ minor, V–VI), B♯ rises to C♯ and C♯ is doubled (Vln II, Bsn 1, Cl). There is no augmented 2nd, as PDF 166 requires in minor. At 216–217, D♯4 (Hn 2) rises to E4 and E is doubled. The call that follows at 197–200 is the varied repetition PDF 204 describes after a deceptive cadence.

### 6. Supertonic sevenths into the cadential 6/4 (follow; protected exit)

At 219–221 and 351–353 the 7th E is held in the violas from 218/350, through ii7 and ii6/5, into the 6/4 (PDF 182, Ex. 12-8). The melody resolves E→D♯ at 222 and 355. The viola itself leaves upward to F♯4, which c092 chose so as not to double the leading tone. c202 protects the held E. The same doubled-4th question at 181 was weighed by c192 and c282 and kept. Unit 12 gives no reason to reopen any of these.

### 7. Passing subdominant sevenths with C–E–G inside (follow)

This is the "passing tone (8–7) within an extended II or IV" of PDF 177 and Ex. 12-10. C–E–G never sounds as a triad on its own, and cmajor.py reports none.
