## What else I studied

**Bruckner, Symphony No. 5, iii.** There is no copy in the local corpus. I used only facts I could confirm in two sources:
- [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Bruckner)): Molto vivace in D minor, in a three-theme sonata form rather than Bruckner's usual binary scherzo. The second theme comes at a much slower tempo (Jochum: "significantly slower"), and an accelerando leads to the third. The scherzo's opening bass line is the Adagio's, and its second theme returns in the finale.
- Deryck Cooke's note ([abruckner.com](https://www.abruckner.com/down/articles/articlesEnglish/cooke-deryck-bruckner-symphony-no-5/Cooke---Bruckner-5.pdf)): the Adagio's slow pizzicato becomes the scherzo's quick pizzicato under its first theme. The movement is related to Austrian peasant dances, with "abrupt contrasts" "enhanced by contrasting tempos". The trio is "a detached game with a simple rising and falling phrase, which intermittently stands on its head".

## Holistic comparison with Bruckner 5/iii

- **The link to the previous movement.** Bruckner opens his scherzo with the Adagio's pizzicato figure at speed. This movement opens with movement II's last pitch as Hn 1's G♯ and movement I's horn call, then timpani motive x. It is the same device, a scherzo that grows out of what came before. A conductor who knows Bruckner 5 would accept it.
- **The contrast inside the scherzo.** Bruckner makes his second theme a slower Ländler, so it registers as a second character. Here the second idea (45) contrasts with theme A by legato, f, the rising sixths and the hemiola, at the same tempo. A tempo change is not open to this score: one tempo per section is part of the plan, the 3/4 one-in-a-bar drive runs through the scherzo, and the second idea's hemiola already supplies the "slower" feeling. What the comparison does show is the open point c289–c409 kept naming: the second idea is the idea a listener holds least well. Bruckner gives his second theme its own tempo and space. Within this score's rules, the means at hand is scoring: the tune should be the most present line in its passage. At 45–52 it was not fully so (Finding 1).
- **The trio.** Bruckner's trio plays one phrase and its inversion with detachment. This trio's T is a lyrical tune given four colourings (153, 169, 209, 341). It is closer to Dvořák 8 than to Bruckner, which the plan intends. No change.
- **Verdict.** On orchestration and form, yes: a conductor would believe a master wrote this. All 29 principles hold except row 11, now fixed.

## Findings

### 1. 45–52: the second idea lacks its third octave (applied)

**Problem.** At its only forte major-key statement, the second idea is in two octaves: Fl 1 + Vln I (D6) and Ob 1 + Vln II (D5). The middle octave holds a third octave only in Cl 2 at 47–48 (F♯4–E4–D4), which then leaves it. The violas play harmony in the tune's rhythm, but every note they hold is already in Cl 1, Cl 2 or Hn 1. That is why c094/c102/c272 had to manage the viola's doublings note by note.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 78 gives the strong form of a melody "in three and four octaves".
- Ex. 87 (PDF 261, Kashchei, con tutta forza) puts violas and cellos with clarinets and C.a. on the lowest octave under Vln I/II + Fl + Ob.
- PDF 76–77 and Ex. 80 (PDF 253) name violas + clarinet among the "best and most natural" unisons.
- PDF 81 lets the change of texture come with the new phrase (45).
- The movement's other big statements already have three octaves: 105–120 (C.a. and Cl 2 lower octave, c067) and 333–335.
- It also answers the Bruckner comparison: within one tempo, the second idea becomes the most present line of its passage.

- **52:** the violas return to the A7's seventh G4 on beat 2 (c102: the only G in that chord), at mf.
- **53–56:** the hemiola cadence keeps its harmony, which is protected.
- **Bowing:** the up-bow at 45 gives the violas the violins' bowing, so 53 starts down-bow as c218 requires.
- **Harmony:** none is lost. 49.1's C♯ stays in Cl 2, and c094's undoubled bass E at 51 still holds.
- **Parallels:** the scan of the new line against every independent part at 45–53 shows only unisons with Cl 2 on the tune itself.
- **Not adopted:** I also tested the cor anglais on the same octave (mf). The build went to 14 pages (the tB page 173–210 split), so I dropped it. Violas with Cl 2 at 47–48 already form Rimsky's Vla + Cl pair.

### 2. Considered, kept: 70 and 298, the gap E3–A4 at f in the stretto

The only real empty middle in a forte chord in the whole movement. It lies inside a four-voice stretto entering at fixed intervals. PDF 84 grants licence to figures of a different texture, and the horns fill the space at 71, one bar later. Changing an entry would undo the stretto, so no change.

### 3. Considered, kept: Bsn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 sevenths (A3 over B2) at 102–119 and 308–319

PDF 90 rule 4 prefers concords inside one colour. These are the V7's seventh over the root, held as a tenor line. Rule 4's exception ("when discords are to be emphasised") and c319's horn reasoning apply. There is no oboe discord anywhere, and rule 4 singles out the oboe.

### 4. Considered, kept: 41–43, the six-part unison

PDF 76 warns that several winds overpower one string group. Both violin sections carry the line here (c111), and the higher octave would pass B6 (c107/c414). Kept.
