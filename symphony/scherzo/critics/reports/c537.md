## Findings

### 1. Bars 95-100: the crotchet arpeggio sits in the same colour and values as the tune; move it to the cor anglais (applied)

**Problem.** At 95-100, Vln I states the head of theme A, p, in crotchets. Under it, Vln II plays the B-major arpeggio (B4-F♯4-D♯4 / A4-F♯4-D♯4, rising to the C5 at 100), also in staccato crotchets. Both lines are violins in the same register band and the same note values. Below them sit the violas' repeated B3 and the pizz. B in the cellos and basses. Vln II has also played since bar 65, so it has no fresh entrance left for the hemiola at 101.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Ravel (PDF 217, 224): when tune and accompaniment move in the same values, "sharply contrasting colours" keep them apart. Two violin sections in the same values are the opposite case.
- Dvořák m. 33 (PDF 213): strings drop out as relief and re-enter fresh.
- Ravel's oboe-to-cor anglais hand-over (PDF 217-219) is the model for the hand-over itself. Here Ob 1 has the figure at 92-94 and the cor anglais can take it on at 95 with no overlap.
- Milhaud (PDF 236): alternate the choirs, and save the new weight for the significant entrance at 101.

**Fix.** The cor anglais plays the Vln II figure note for note, pp staccato, at 95-100. Vln II rests from 73 to 100 and enters f on the hemiola at 101. The violas, timpani, pizz. bass and Vln I are unchanged, and no brass enters before 101 (c244). The voice leading is better than before: the cor anglais's C5 at 100 now resolves in its own voice to the B4 that opens its own hemiola at 101, so the resolution c532 described is kept and stays inside one player. No pizz./arco change is involved, which answers c437's only objection.

### 2. Bars 289-304: reprise fugato basses (not applied)

Petit Poucet (PDF 221-224) asks for a return to be scored the same way but coloured differently. The reprise fugato already does this: the cello leads, the viola follows, and woodwind enters by choir switch (Ex. XVI-14, PDF 229-230). Adding basses at 293-296 would thicken an entry the reading says to mark with one section only (Ex. XVI-20, PDF 243-245). c417, c517 and c527 rejected it for the same reason. No change.

### 3. Bars 89-100: a rhythmicized horn pedal on B, after Bizet (not applied)

Bizet's Hn 3 on the pedal (PDF 213-216) would add motion inside the soft B chord. But it puts brass before 101, against c244 and against the Milhaud principle on the same pages of this reading (PDF 236). The timpani's motive x already gives the pedal its rhythm, which is what Bizet uses the horn for. No change.

### 4. Bars 95-100 alternative: give the arpeggio to Cl 1 instead of the cor anglais (rejected in favour of F1)

Cl 1 moves to A4 at 101, so the C5 at 100 would not resolve in its voice and c532's resolution would be lost. The cor anglais also continues Ob 1's double-reed colour from 92-94, which is the Ravel hand-over (PDF 217). Rejected.

### 5. Bars 41-44 and 105-112: Vln II on the lower octave (not applied)

The Schoenberg op. 25 principle (PDF 229) supports octave doubling where similar colours would swallow each other. Vln II already doubles Vln I there (c111), and no strings are on the lower octave at 105-112 (c067). The reading confirms the current layout and gives no stronger reason to change it.

### 6. Bars 209-224: a viola quaver figure under T (not applied)

Dvořák m. 9 (PDF 210) asks for a light accompaniment under a strong, soft tune. A viola quaver figure would sit in the clarinet's register and blur T. The current sustained harmonic counterpoint already matches the principle. No change.
