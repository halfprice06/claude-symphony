**Form verdict.** The first part follows Unit 15's model for a minor-key movement nearly point for point:
- I to III through ♮VII as V of III (Ex. 15-1), with III expanded into a key area (Ex. 15-3).
- III to V by a rising bass through IV and the chromatic II6, the Neapolitan F/A (Ex. 15-2b/d).
- III arriving about halfway between I and V (PDF 231).
- The 3+3+4 grouping of Exercise 2 in the retransition.

The reprise keeps the III–IV–V bass (G–A–B, 289–301) with III turned minor. It uses two VII techniques in places a listener will notice:
- ♮VII7 → V6/5 → i at 273–277 (Ex. 15-14);
- I–♮VII–(♭II)–V7 over a falling bass at 329–332 (Ex. 15-15).

The proportions (first part 97 s; trio block 88 s; reprise and coda 92 s) and the 4:36 length are right for a scherzo placed between a 5:45 Adagio and a 7-minute finale. No section stalls without a harmonic goal.

## Findings

None of the 20 principles above is violated, so I have no finding that needs a score change. I tested the following points against the reading and decided each one myself. Composer: keep all of them as they are.

### 1. Reprise without a major III (277–288): keep

A&S PDF 232 calls the major quality of III "a welcome contrast to the minor tonic", and the reprise drops it. Restoring G major at 277 would repeat 45–56 and, with it, the exposition's move to the relative key, where the reprise needs to stay in the tonic. The reprise still contains the stopover. III comes back in minor at 289 and leads by step through A (297) to V (301), the Ex. 15-2b bass. Major relief comes just before, from the 88-second E-major trio, and the A-major stretto at 297–300 supplies more. c003, c083 and c133 kept this, and the reading gives no reason to reverse them.

### 2. Join 288→289 (E minor to G in the cellos alone): keep

A&S PDF 240 warns against I→III from a weak to a strong beat. Here, however, the G at 289 does not form a III chord. It is the first note of a monophonic fugato subject, and the B♭ at 290 makes it G minor at once. The join is also marked by two beats of silence and a double bar. The new section's downbeat is heard as a new start, so the metre is not contradicted.

### 3. D5 → D♯3 at 275→276: keep

This is the only soprano–bass chromatic pair in the movement (row 10). Both voices carry the inflection themselves: Bsn 2 plays D→D♯ and Vln I plays D5…D♯5. That is the single-voice chromatic step that Ex. 15-14 shows, not the split succession that Ex. 15-8c forbids.

### 4. D♯ over the passing G at 314: keep

The beat sounds as G–B–D♯ for a moment. The D♯ is the theme's own repeated-quaver neighbour (E–D♯–D♯–E) over a passing bass F♯–G–A, so it is not a III+ chord (PDF 240). The exposition statements use a bass G♯ (114, 262; c143). c143 left 314 as it is because there the bass passes through to A under iiø7 (c057, c058).

### 5. 27 beats 2–3, a diminished triad D♯–F♯–A after the pizzicato B: keep

This is exactly Ex. 15-19 (PDF 244). The diminished chord grows out of a strong V and is heard as V.
