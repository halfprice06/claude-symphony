## Findings

The reading's techniques are already in this score, almost all of them put there by earlier passes on these same pages. A climax made by holding a colour back (1, 2, 5), forte subito piano by overlap (13), sforzandi made from a short note plus long notes (12), colouring a held note (14) and dovetailing (16) each show up at the places where they matter. I found no fault that the reading supports. Below is the strongest candidate the reading offers, which I built and then rejected, followed by the points I checked.

### 1. Bars 101–103: timpani strokes on the hemiola instead of the roll (built, rejected)

- **Candidate:** At 101–104 the hemiola minims (`a''2-> c'''4->~ | c'''4 f'''2-> | dis'''2-> b''4->~ | b''4 a''2->`) are made by accents alone, in the woodwinds and the strings. Every short-note group in those bars strikes on the bar line: the trumpets play motive x at 101 and 103, and the horns, trombones and tuba hold the V chord. The timpani holds a roll on B (`b,2.:32\p\<~ | b,2.:32~ | b,2.:32`). Adler PDF 189–190 says that "often an accent is not sufficient to emphasize sforzando" and that a short note in another group should be combined with the long notes. The obvious way to do that is the coda's own device: timpani strokes in the hemiola rhythm, as at 337–338 (`b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->`).
- **Built:** sB.ily `sBTimp` 101–103 `b,2->\p\< b,4->~ | b,4 b,2-> | b,2-> b,4~` (roll removed). Build: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad. I looked at page 4 of the build at 110 dpi.
- **Why it makes the music worse:**
  1. It goes against the first technique in this same reading (PDF 37): hold back an effect for a specific event. The timpani joins the hemiola only at 337–338, under the single fff and the B6. That is the movement's last and largest hemiola. Using the same strokes at 101 spends that effect 235 bars early. The reprise could not even match it, because the timpani rests at 301–302 (no F or A on the drums, c473), so the exposition would get a figure that the reprise does not.
  2. The build at 101–104 is a crescendo from p/pp to ff/f in every group (c028). The roll is the one layer that grows continuously under the cross-rhythm. Strokes break it into four attacks.
  3. The bar-wise pulse against the minims is the point of "ritmo di quattro battute": the trumpets' motive x on 101 and 103 and the held brass on the bar line keep the barline audible, so the hemiola is heard as a cross-rhythm. Twenty-odd parts already play the accented minims (c148), so they do not need help to be heard. A timpani in the hemiola rhythm would move the drum, which carried motive x alone through 89–100, over to the other side of that conflict.
- **Decision:** Reverted. The scratch copy is v528 again.

### 2. Bars 336–338: Tchaikovsky's fff> on held brass notes (not changed)

Ex. XI-21 (PDF 39–40) has the held brass at fff, each note with a >. This is the reading's most direct climax model, so I checked it again. The score keeps the brass under the fff: horns ff, Tpt 1·2 f, low brass resting (c200, c194, c034, c514). Dan's rule (BRIEF: weight, not bite; no ff on brass attacks) decides it. The held-brass diminuendo that Tchaikovsky uses already appears where it helps most: at 339, where the brass hold f> minims under the short tutti stroke (c128, Ex. XV-35). No change.

### 3. Bars 72→73, 300→301: forte subito piano (not changed)

At 72 the ff A-major chord has a > in every group, and Hn 1·2 tie their A into the pp F-major group at 73. A is the only common tone of the two chords, so this is the overlap of Ex. XV-36 at the one pitch where it can be made. At 300→301 the strings strike sfp and the winds and horns come in p<, which is Ex. XV-35's third case (sf > pp in the strings, a soft group with it). Both follow the reading. 120→121 and 276→277 stay as cuts after a rest (c428, c513).

### 4. Bars 332–333: the saved brass return (not changed)

Trumpets, trombones and tuba come in pp< at 332, the first time in 212 bars, and reach f at 333. This is Ex. XI-19's pp entry of the held-back colour, followed by the larger tutti (Tchaikovsky). It follows the reading as c428 left it.

### 5. Bars 149, 1–7, 364–376: coloured held notes (not changed)

Hn 1's G♯ is coloured by the harp harmonics at 149, by Vln I sul A at 1–7 and 364–376 (c526), and by Hn 3 taking over during Hn 1's breath. Everything stays at one soft level, as Ex. XV-37 asks. These bars are protected and already follow the reading.

### 6. Bars 341–349: dovetailing of T's recall (not changed)

T passes from Hn 3 to Ob 1/Cl 1 to the solo violin on phrase boundaries. Each new colour comes in on the downbeat after the previous phrase ends. At these joins the seam is meant to be heard (Ex. XV-40's exception), and c494 settled the scoring.
