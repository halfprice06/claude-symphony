## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 104 and 304: Tpt 1's held D♯5 leaves an empty sixth at the top of the brass chord (applied)
**Problem.** At the end of the build into the tutti (101–104, and 301–304 in the reprise), the brass holds V of E minor, f under the horns' ff. Through 103 the brass is in close order up to Tpt 1's B4. At 104 Tpt 1 leaps to a held D♯5, while Tpt 2 and Hn 1 stay on F♯4. The brass then reads B2–F♯3–A3–B3–D♯4–F♯4 and nothing more until D♯5: an empty major sixth directly under the highest and most penetrating brass note. That note is the leading tone, held for a whole bar by one trumpet with no horn doubling it. It is then left by a leap down to B4 at 105, so the trumpet's leading tone is not resolved in its own line.
**Evidence.** PDF 100: brass part-writing "should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". PDF 101: in the higher registers "the two upper parts are given to the trumpets", and every diagram has the trumpets close together on top. PDF 103–104: the horns are put beside the trumpets to "moderate the penetrating timbre". At 104 Tpt 1 has no horn beside it. PDF 104: a held note is given to trumpets or horns at the octave, "sometimes formed by trumpets and horns acting together" (diagram: 1 trumpet over 2 horns). PDF 110–111: the wood-wind should lie above the trumpets, but at 104 Ob 2 only doubles the trumpet's D♯5 instead of lying above it (the c014/c184 comments note this).
**Fix (applied).** sB.ily `sBTptOne` last bar `dis''2.\f` → `b'2.\f`; dB.ily `dBTptOne` last bar likewise. Tpt 1 holds B4, the note of its two motive-x bars, and keeps it into motive x at 105/305 as a common tone. The brass at 104 is now in close order: B2–F♯3–A3–B3–D♯4–F♯4–B4. Tpt 1's B4 makes the octave with Hn 3's B3 (PDF 104), and Tpt 2 stays in unison with Hn 1 on F♯4 (PDF 103). The leading tone remains in Hn 2 (D♯4) and in the wood-wind (Ob 2 D♯5), and the whole wood-wind chord D♯5–F♯5–A5–B5 now lies above the trumpets (PDF 110–111). This changes no dynamic and adds no accent or entry, so the trumpets are still only in tutti rhythm and motive x. It touches no theme. It does not undo c014 or c184: their wood-wind voicing stays, and it now sits above the trumpet as c014 intended.

### Considered and left (my decisions)
- 337: Hn 1's C5 (the ♭9) against Hn 4's B3 puts a minor ninth inside one colour (PDF 102–103). Moving Hn 4 off B3 would take the root out of the brass at the peak, and c194 and c262 kept this chord. The other discord, Tpt 1 A4 against the horns' B, is already split between colours. No change.
- 103–104: Tbn 1 A3 makes a seventh over B.tbn B2 inside the trombones. It also makes a second against Hn 3's B3 in a different colour, and it resolves to G3 at 105. c104 and c194 kept it. No change.
- 121–128/277–280: Cl 2 sits inside the horns (PDF 108). It has been kept three times (c014, c104, c194) because otherwise the clarinets cross Vln II. I found no new argument against that reason. No change.
- 105–119: above the trumpets' short strokes the wood-wind mostly doubles the theme in octaves, not a complete chord (PDF 110–111). These are detached strokes (PDF 114: resonance "a minor consideration"), and the tutti layout is protected (c254, c264). No change.
- 75–76/83: the horns sit between the bass clarinet and Bsn 1, which PDF 108 does not recommend. Rimsky's own Ex. 158 does it ("later horns within bassoons"), and the F-major episode is protected (c104, c147, c247). No change.
