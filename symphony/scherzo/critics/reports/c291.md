**Verdict on the tunes.**
- **Theme A** is the movement's signature. Motive x (E–B–B–E) and the chromatic tail G–F♯–F–E are what a listener will hum. Its bar 11 rise F♯–G–A–B into the ♭6 peak C6 is the gesture that makes the 16 bars a tune rather than a figure.
- **The second idea** is a broad hemiola line with sixth leaps to G6 and A6.
- **T** has a held bar-6 peak, a tied A and a 4–3 cadence. Its three statements in three colours and the codetta's solo heads make it stick the way the trio tune of Dvořák 8/iii does: by plain repetition in changing colours.
- **The harp melody** turns motive x's contour into a falling major-key sequence, and the clarinet answer turns it back to minor.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 335 (coda climax): the rise into the B6 peak is in the top octave only (applied)

**Problem.** At 335 Vln I and Fl 1 climb E6–F♯6–G6–A6 into the movement's only fff and its B6 at 336. c166 gave them this climb. Vln II, the violas, the cor anglais, Ob 2 and Cl 1 play motive x (E–B–B–G) at the same moment, and their next note is the B5/B4 that doubles the peak. So:
- The climb is heard in one octave only, at the top of the violin and flute compass, over five lines playing a different figure.
- On beat 3 the A6 sounds against G5 (Vln II, Ob 1–2, Cl 1) and G4 (violas, cor anglais). Those are the same instruments that double the melody a beat later.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 68: a melody in the extreme high compass is doubled an octave lower. PDF 69 and Ex. 65 (PDF 236): when a melody is spread over three or four octaves, each octave keeps the natural order and plays the same line. In Ex. 84 and 86 (PDF 257, 260), the strings doubled by winds in octaves also carry one line. The score already does this at 115 and 315: theme A's rise F♯–G–A–B–C is in three octaves there (Fl/Ob/Vln I, Vln II/C.a./Cl 2). The coda's climb is the same rise, a step lower, leading to the movement's highest note. It should be scored the same way so that the listener connects it with the theme's peak.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-291/work/sections/coda.ily, bar 335 = coda bar 11):**
```
codaVlnTwo:     e''4-.)\< b'8-. b'-. g''4-.  ->  e''4-.)\< fis''8-. g''-. a''4-.
codaVla:        e'4-.)\< b8-. b-. g'4-.      ->  e'4-.)\< fis'8-. g'-. a'4-.
codaCorAnglais: e'4-.)\< b8-. b-. g'4-.      ->  e'4-.)\< fis'8-. g'-. a'4-.
```
- **Layout.** The climb is now in three octaves: Fl 1+Vln I; Vln II; violas+C.a. (PDF 77: Violas + Eng. horn).
- **What keeps motive x.** Ob 2, Cl 1 and the trumpets still play it in the same bar. I tried Ob 2 on the climb as well and kept it on motive x: its A5 would cross above Ob 1's held G5 on the shared oboe staff, and PDF 70 calls inverted pairs "strained".
- **Rhythm, dynamics and articulation** are unchanged, and the next bar's B5/B4 is now reached by step.
- **Voice leading.** The A on beat 3 is a passing note over the E-minor 6/4 (G in the horns, Ob 1, Cl 1 and trombones), as A is at 115. The analyser shows no new outer-voice parallels.
- **Earlier fixes.** This does not undo c166, which fixed the top line's leaps. It extends c166's line to the lower octaves.

### 2. Bars 349–356: T in the coda, Vln I alone above Vln II + Cl 1 (considered, kept)

This is PDF 78's soprano rule: of two octave parts, double the lower one. Ex. 89 (PDF 263) shows the same thing: a dolce tune in violins with oboe and clarinet doubling the lower parts. Moving Cl 1 up would put it at G♯6. No change.

### 3. Bars 341–348: T's first two phrases handed from Hn 3 to Ob 1 (considered, kept)

- PDF 72 gives the soft horn the widest choice of tunes, and the line is diatonic and in the middle register (C♯4–A4).
- PDF 70 cites "Sadko [43]: all wood-wind in turn, simple timbres".
- PDF 75 says a single horn needs two winds above it if doubled, and doubling would spend the oboe's entry at 345.

No change.

### 4. Bars 197–200: Ob 1 at the lower octave on the call's first bar, then in unison at C♯6 (considered, kept)

Ob 1 leaps up a sixth where the call itself falls a third, which bends the call's contour in that one voice. But at 197 Fl 1/Ob 1 stand in the natural octave (PDF 67), and from 198 the oboe leads the Fl+Ob unison in its upper register (PDF 65). c117 and c119 placed it there so that it does not cross T in Cl 1, Vln II and the violas at G♯4–E5. The reading gives no reason that answers c117. Not re-raised.

### 5. Bars 299–301: Bsn 1·2 in octaves (scan result, considered, kept)

PDF 67–68 discourages two of one colour in octaves unless strings double both, "most satisfactory for repeated notes or sustained passages". Here the cellos play A3 and A2 and the basses A1 under an A pedal, so the exception applies. It is a bass figure, not a melody. No change.

### 6. Bars 113–116 and 313–316: Fl 1·2 and Ob 1 on Vln I (considered, kept)

PDF 76: several winds on one string group overpower it. This is the ff tutti, where PDF 66–67 expects doubling, and Vln II, the cor anglais and Cl 2 carry the lower octave in the Fl / Ob+Vln layout of PDF 78. c111 and c201 kept it. No change.

### 7. T's rhythm, minim + crotchet in bars 1, 5 and 9 (considered, kept)

The phrases share their opening rhythm. Since cycle 1, five theme changes have given each phrase its own feature (c061, c081, c093, c121, c201's review). Dvořák's 8/iii trio tune repeats its rhythm just as plainly. No change.
