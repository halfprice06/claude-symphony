## Findings (ranked)

### 1. T's cadential 4-3, 165–166, 181–182, 221–222: the suspension is not shaded (applied)

**Problem.** c061 tied the E of the cadential 6/4 over the barline so that T ends on a real V4-3. It also cited the sentence that dissonant suspensions take an accent and soften on the resolution. But no part marks this. In every statement the tune runs `gis'4\!( b'4 e''4~ | e''4 dis''4 fis''4` at one level: the hairpin of the third phrase ends on 165/181/221, and nothing follows. At 2. = 76 the tied E lasts under a second. Players read the tied crotchet as an ordinary held note, because the barline gives the cue only to a player who looks for the dissonance. The lean and release that c061 wrote the tie for is left to chance in the phrase that ends each statement of the trio tune.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348, sec. 4: "Recognizing and bringing out suspensions is of particular importance to the performer; dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer." PDF 348 (printed 350), sec. 3: because the suspension is accented, "its resolution should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity". PDF 350, sec. 8: the cadential 4-3 exists "to enhance the effect of a cadence by emphasizing the leading tone", so the D♯ should come after the lean, softer than it. You cannot put an accent on a tied note. The notation available is a swell: crescendo into the tie, diminuendo from the dissonance to the resolution. Beethoven marks a suspended V7 this way in Ex. 21-37, PDF 365 (`<>`, then `>` p). The marks go in the tune voices only, as c038 did for the third phrase's swell.

**Fix (applied).**
```
% tA Cl 1 165-166; tC Cl 1 and Vln II 221-222:
gis'4\!( b'4 e''4\<~ | e''4\> dis''4\! fis''4
% tC Fl 1 and Vln I 221-222 (an octave up):
gis''4\!( b''4 e'''4\<~ | e'''4\> dis'''4\! fis'''4
% tA Vln I 181-182 (the cadence in B; Vln II an octave lower):
dis''4\!( fis''4 b''4\<~ | b''4\> ais''4\! cis'''4)
```
The hairpins print clearly at 181–182 and 221–222 (checked at 150 dpi). Shared staves: Cl 2 and Fl 2 rest at these bars, so no dynamic is unequal.

### 2. The plagal close, 357–360: Hn 1's dissonances against the tonic pedal have no dynamic shape (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 1's motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ is marked `p` "espress." at 357 and runs flat to a written `pp` at 361. Under it the bass E is a tonic pedal (Bsn 2 from 357, basses from 358, held to 364). A♯ at 358 is a tritone above that pedal, inside the A♯°7. A at 359 is the 4th above the bass in the iv6/4, and it falls by step to G♯, the third of I, at 360. So the line moves consonance → dissonance → dissonance → resolution, the shape of a suspension's accent and release spread over four bars. The print gives no sign of it, and the drop from p to pp lands on 361 instead of on the resolution. This passage is the scherzo's last quotation of the motto, so its shape matters more than any other bar of the coda.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 368 (printed 370), sec. 28: chords dissonant against a pedal "are perfectly good if the chord succession and voice leading make sense", and tonic pedals come at endings (PDF 367). PDF 348 (printed 350), sec. 3: a dissonance resolved downward is "a relaxation, a decrease in intensity". PDF 348, sec. 4: the performer must bring out the dissonance and soften the resolution. Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365): Beethoven swells into the suspended chord and diminishes after it.

**Fix (applied).** Hn 1 only. Notes, slur and breaths are unchanged.
```
codaHnOne: b'2.(\p\<^\markup \italic "espress." | ais'2.\> | a'2. | gis'2.)~ | gis'2.~\pp
```
This touches the protected passage (HISTORY v01: Hn 1 B–A♯–A–G♯ at 357–360; c086: the closing horn page) only in dynamics. The reasons given for protecting it are its pitches as the motto quotation, its single-horn scoring and its brass playability, and none of these changes. The short `<` in bar 357 prints as "p <" because the bar is narrow. It is readable and collides with nothing.

### 3. Coda 355: the delayed 4-3 at the end of the 6-5-4 descent (applied)

**Problem.** At 353–355 the last cadence holds the 6/4 for two bars (G♯6 tenuto, c048). The upper voice falls G♯–F♯–E and ties the E into the V7 bar (c061). This is the delayed resolution of A&S PDF 359–360, sec. 21, which "builds up tension". The release at 355 is unmarked, so the tension of the delay has no softer resolution after it.

**Evidence.** PDF 359–360, sec. 21 (delayed resolution, Ex. 21-27); PDF 352, Ex. 21-11b (6/4 into 4-3 as a cadence formula); PDF 348 (resolution softer). The tenuto on G♯ already gives the lean, so only the release is needed. A `<` on the quaver E would be too short to read.

**Fix (applied).**
```
% coda Vln I 355 (Vln II and Cl 1 an octave lower, same marks):
gis'''2-- fis'''8 e'''~ | e'''4\> dis'''\! fis''')
```

### 4. Retransition 245–246 and 248–249: the dovetailed 4-3s stay at the level of the new entries (applied)

**Problem.** c037 filled the gaps between theme-head entries with the outgoing voice holding its last E as a 4-3 over the B pedal. The violas hold E4 through 245 and resolve to D♯ at 246, while Vln II starts the head on E5 at 245. Vln II then holds E5 through 248 and resolves to D♯ at 249 as Vln I enters. Both the held voice and the entering head are pp with no further marks, so the held E sounds at the head's level and against it. The resolution D♯ is covered by Vln II's accented G at 246.

**Evidence.** PDF 348: "resolutions are softer". PDF 352, sec. 12: a resolution that sounds while another line moves is clear "in a texture with marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics". Here the contrast available is dynamic. Adler (c037's own source, II PDF 209) describes this kind of background as "sustained harmonic counterpoint … without weight".

**Fix (applied).**
```
retrVla:    ... e'2.)~ | e'2.~ | e'2.(\> | dis'2)\! r4 | ...
retrVlnTwo: ... e''2.)~ | e''2.(\> | dis''2.)\! | ...
```
