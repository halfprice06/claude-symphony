## Findings (ranked)

### 1. A C-major triad at bar 66 (fugato, third entry). APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 66 Vln II (and Ob 1) has the subject's C6 for two beats, the Vla has E4, and the Vc and Cb have G2/G1. The sonority is C–E over G: a C-major 6/4 chord, the one triad the cyclic rules forbid (BRIEF: no C-major triad, kept for the finale's arrival). My scan of the whole score at semiquaver resolution found no other C-major triad.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 80: the bass "must make explicit the harmonic meaning of the soprano"; the same soprano tone can be root, 3rd or 5th, and the bass decides. Here the subject's C6 is ♭3 of A minor (the entry is in A minor), and the bass G makes it the root of C. The 6/4 is also unprepared, and its bass leaps away (G→D).
**Fix.** Keep the tonic under the entry for a second bar, so that C6 stays the 3rd of A minor. The bass becomes A–A–D–E (i–i–iv–V), the plainest form of the cadence the subject needs.
```
sB.ily  sBVc  bars 65-68:  a,2. | a,2. | d,2. | e,2.     (was a,2. | g,2. | ...)
        sBCb  bars 65-68:  a,,2.\p | a,,2. | d,2. | e,,2. (was ... | g,,2. | ...)
```
Beat 3 of 66 now has B5/B♭5 over D4 and A: chromatic passing tones over the tonic pedal. dB has no third entry, so nothing changes there.

### 2. Bass octave slip at bar 127 (second idea in E minor). APPLIED.
**Problem.** The Cb and contrabassoon go D♯2 → E3 → B1: a minor 9th up into the tonic, then an 11th down. The cellos and Bsn 2 move correctly (D♯3→E3). At the cadence of the hemiola unit (vii°7 → i), the lowest sound of the orchestra suddenly sits on E3, and the bass foundation is missing for two beats. The two lines read `e2(` where the rest of each line has two commas. It is a typing slip.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 81: leaps larger than an octave are not permitted. PDF 80: the bass regulates the succession of chords, so the leading tone D♯ in the bass must rise by step. PDF 75: lines stay in their normal register.
**Fix.**
```
sC.ily  sCCb    bar 127:  e,2( b,,4~    (was e2( b,,4~)
        sCCbsn  bar 127:  e,2( b,,4~    (was e2( b,,4~)
```

### 3. Doubled leading tone at the fugato's half-cadences (64, 68, 308). APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 64 (and 308) the D-minor entry ends on V6. The Vla's subject has C♯4 on beat 2 while the Vc's countersubject holds C♯3, so the leading tone is doubled and is also the bass. At 68 the A-minor entry ends on V. Vln II has G♯4 (beat 2) and the Vla's countersubject has G♯3 (beats 1–2). Both leading tones then move to E4 and meet in a unison, and neither rises to A.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76 and 5-4: the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V or VII or their inversions"; "triads in inversion are usually complete". These bars are a three-voice fugato, a real-voice texture. The free-texture allowance for octave doubling (PDF 84) does not cover them.
**Fix.** Change only the countersubject's penultimate note to the chord root or 5th:
```
sB.ily  sBVc   bar 64:  a,2 e4)    (was cis2 e4)   -> V in root position, C♯ only in the Vla
dB.ily  dBVc   bar 308: a,2 e4)    (was cis2 e4)
sB.ily  sBVla  bar 68:  b2 e'4)    (was gis2 e'4)  -> complete E–G♯–B, G♯ only in Vln II; A3–B3 is a step
```
The new bass at 64 (D3→A2, a falling 4th at the phrase end) follows PDF 80 on disjunct bass motion at cadences.

### 4. Parallel 5ths in the bassoons during the coda's descending bass (345–348). APPLIED.
**Problem.** Bsn 1 has B3–A3–G3–F♯3 over Bsn 2/bass clarinet/Cb E–D–C♯–B. That gives P5 → P5 (E/B → D/A), then P5 → d5, then d5 → P5 (C♯/G → B/F♯). This is the only moving line in that register while the strings repeat the ostinato pp. The bassoons are marked mf with a crescendo, so it is exposed. The analyser misses it because both bassoons share one MIDI track.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83, 5-19: parallel perfect 5ths are excluded "from the fifteenth through the nineteenth centuries". 5-20: P5→d5 is acceptable, but d5→P5 "is normally avoided since the dissonant interval does not resolve".
**Fix.** Make Bsn 1 a stepwise inner voice (PDF 80) that keeps every chord's colour (i – VII – vii° – V7):
```
coda.ily  codaBsnOne  bars 345-349:  g2.\mf\< | a2. | g2. | a2. | g2.\ff   (was b2.\mf\< | a2. | g2. | fis2. | g2.\ff)
```
Against the bass this gives m3, P5, d5, m7: no consecutive perfect intervals. The A at 348 makes V7, and its 7th resolves down to G at 349. The dynamics are unchanged, so the part-combined staff stays valid.

### 5. Vln II crosses above the motto's tail in Hn 1 (373–376). APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 373–376 Hn 1 quotes the motto's tail B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 (protected). Vln II moves G♯4 → C♯5 → C5 → B4 at the same dynamic (p). From 374 it lies a minor 3rd above the horn. The top line the listener hears is then B4 (Hn) – C♯5 – C5 – B4 (Vln II), which is not the motto, and the quotation becomes an inner voice.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 88, 5-30: "A soprano or bass line can become obscured if crossed by an inner voice; you should therefore avoid such crossings". Crossing is "least problematic when it involves only inner voices".
**Fix.** Put Vln II an octave lower from 374. The chords stay the same (E – A♯°7 – A minor – E, complete), and Hn 1 is the top voice again:
```
coda.ily  codaVlnTwo  bars 373-380:  gis'2.\p | cis'2. | c'2. | b2. | b2.~\pp | b2.~ | b2.~ | b2 r4
          (was gis'2.\p | cis''2. | c''2. | b'2. | b'2.~\pp | b'2.~ | b'2.~ | b'2 r4)
```
G♯4→C♯4 is a falling 4th followed by steps in the same direction (PDF 81, 5-11c). Vln II now crosses only the Vla (inner voices), which the reading allows. The protected horn line and the bass are unchanged.

### 6. A bare fifth on the downbeat of the second idea's second unit (49, 293). APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 49 (and 293) the tutti forte hemiola begins with A–E only: bass A, Cl 2/Vla E4, Cl 1/Vln II A4, Hn A4/E4, Vln I E6. The 3rd (C♯) arrives only on beat 3. The parallel downbeat of the first unit (45) is a complete G triad (B4 in Cl 1/Vln II).
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "Because of the empty sound and the lack of major or minor quality, the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects". There is no special effect here, since the passage is a legato chordal hemiola.
**Fix.** Cl 2 and Vla take C♯4 (a step from their D4 at 48):
```
sA.ily  sAClTwo, sAVla  bar 49:   cis'2( a'4~   (was e'2( a'4~)
dA.ily  dAClTwo, dAVla  bar 293:  cis'2( a'4~   (was e'2( a'4~)
```
The rising minor 6th C♯4→A4 is followed by a repeated A and then a fall to E4 (PDF 81: change of direction after a 6th).
