## Findings

### 1. Bars 349–356: the coda's last statement of T. The whole Vln I section plays C♯6–A6 at *pp*. Rimsky gives such a line to two solo violins and puts the rest of Vln I in the lower octave. (Applied.)

**Problem.** These bars are the movement's last full phrase of the trio tune (T bars 9–15) and the last melody before the motto tail.
- Vln I, tutti, plays it at C♯6–A6, *pp* < *p*, *dolce*. The A6 is held over the bar line (350–351) and G♯6 is held at 354.
- The lower octave, C♯5–A5, has only Vln II and Cl 1 (*ppp*).
- So the larger body of strings is on the thin top string, and the octave that carries the expression is the lighter one.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 58–59: the E string "diminishes in fulness of tone the higher it ascends from the limits of the soprano voice".
- The Note to Ex. 24, PDF 59: "One or two desks of the 1st Violins are sufficient to double the melody in the upper octave, all the other 1st Violins can play the octave below. In this way the piercing quality of the highest notes will be diminished, the melody will acquire a clearer and more pleasant sound, and the expressive tone quality of the lower octave will be strengthened."
- Ex. 26, PDF 60 and 202–203 (Snegourotchka [283]): "*Pianissimo* cantabile in two octaves … The flute and all the 1st Violins except two play in the lower octave, the two solo violins, only, in the upper. The solo desk will be sufficiently prominent owing to the general *pianissimo*."
- The image shows the two soli at D6–E6, over Vln I and the flute an octave lower, with Vln II divided in chords. That is our layout and dynamic, except that our top line goes a fourth higher (A6).

**Answering c191 (point 1), which kept the whole section on top:**
- *"Ex. 24's top line lies far above A6; the note concerns the extremity."* Ex. 26 applies the same distribution at D6–E6 in *pp*, for a *pp* cantabile in two octaves. That is lower than our A6, so the rule reaches these bars a fortiori. c191 did not weigh Ex. 26's pitches.
- *"Ex. 26 is for chorus doublings, where the voice carries the lower octave."* In Ex. 26 the lower octave is Vln I tutti plus the flute. The chorus is an extra layer, not the reason for the layout. Here Vln II and Cl 1 play the part of the flute.
- *"Thinning the top would weaken the last high statement."* Rimsky says the opposite: the top becomes "clearer and more pleasant" and stays "sufficiently prominent owing to the general pianissimo".

**Why this helps memory.** The lower octave, C♯5–A5, is the register where Cl 1 first sang these exact bars (161–167). At the farewell the listener now hears the tune strongest in the pitch where it was learned, with a clear high octave on top. The register hand-over that c150 protects (Hn C♯4 → Ob D5 → Vln C♯6) still sounds, because the soli enter on C♯6. The A6 that c264 protects is still played.

### 2. Bars 45–56 and 209–224: double octaves for the second idea and for T's forte return (considered, not applied)

**Rule.** PDF 62 (Ex. 32) offers Vln I / Vln II / violas in double octaves for "full cantabile melodies extremely tense in character, and in forte passages for choice". Both passages are forte cantabile in two octaves.

**At 45–56.** I checked the violas' notes there. Clarinets or horns double them in every bar except 52, where the viola G4 is the only seventh of A7/C♯.
- Moving the violas to the tune (D4–A4) would need Cl 2 to take that G4.
- It would also undo the inner-voice choices of c262, c272 and c277.
- The tune is already the top voice in two octaves with Fl 1 and Ob 1. The gain would be weight, not a clearer tune.

**At 209–224.** Double octaves would take the violas' off-beats and the cello countermelody (c191 point 5).

Rimsky gives double octaves as a choice, not a rule. Kept.

### 3. Bars 169–184: T in the cellos at G♯3–A4, under Vln II chords (considered, kept)

This is Rimsky's own sequence, Ex. 7–8 (PDF 56, 180–184): a cello cantabile answering the clarinet, with the violins taking the tune an octave up later (209).
- Relief comes from dynamics and colour: Vc mp espress., Vln II pp, Cb pp pizz. (PDF 54).
- The flute's answers fall only in the cellos' held notes.
- An octave higher would put the cellos at A5 and in unison with the clarinet's first statement.

Kept (c344, c349).

### 4. Leggiero theme A and the fugal heads above F♯5 without a lower octave: 14–26, 70, 96, 254–266, 298 (considered, kept)

The scan lists these bars. Each is either a *p leggiero* minim in the single-section layout of Ex. 1 (PDF 173–174), or a single-section head entry (PDF 54, row 3). Rimsky's octave rule is for "broad and expressive phrases" (PDF 59). c281 point 1 and c254 stand.

### 5. The trio tune and the second idea as melodies

I propose no change to the notes of either theme from this reading. Ch. II treats how a melody is laid out, not how it is built, and the layouts follow it everywhere except Finding 1.

Finding 1 is the largest improvement this reading supports for memorability. It makes the last hearing of T sound strongest in the register in which the tune was first heard.
