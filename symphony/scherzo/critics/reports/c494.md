## Findings

### 1. Bars 349–356: Fl 1 at A6 swells with the solo violin, so the flute leads the coda's last T (applied)

**Problem.** The last statement of T is a single solo violin on the top octave (C♯6–A6), with Fl 1 in unison (c439), over Vln II la metà an octave lower (c467). Both top instruments carry the same marks: pp, crescendo to a hidden p at T's climax A6 (350), diminuendo, hidden pp at 353. The flute is in its sixth octave, where it cannot play softly. At the climax the flute is louder than the solo violin it doubles, so the passage that c438 and c457 built as one violin's line ("solo, gli altri tacet") is heard as a flute solo with a string colour. This is also the only place in the score where a wind doubling a string tune is not marked a level under it (c405's rule: 45, 125, 179, 197, 253).

**Evidence.** PDF 162–163 (Brahms 3/i, last chord): the soft chord with flutes on top is "dangerous", "one must expect the flutes to overpower the structure because it is simply very difficult to play piano that high. This chord always sounds louder than it should." PDF 159, consideration 2: each pitch goes to an instrument that can play it at the marked dynamic. PDF 184: the principal voice is marked louder, and a subsidiary voice in a piercing register softer. PDF 165: at-pitch doubling loses the colour of both instruments, and here the flute is the stronger one.

**Answer to the recorded reasons.** c439 added Fl 1 so the top octave is not the weakest line (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 124, solo violin + flute). c467 kept the flute at the violin's level because "a quieter flute would make the top octave thin again". The flute stays, so c439's doubling and c467's lead weight stay. What changes is only the swell. Adler's point is that a high flute marked p plays louder than p, so a flute held at pp still sounds at about the violin's level, not under it. The top octave keeps its weight against the half section below, and the solo violin alone shapes the climax.

### 2. Bars 349–356: Fl 1 out, or an octave lower (built on paper, rejected)

This was the larger candidate. Adler's own remedy (PDF 162) is strings on top and winds an octave below. Fl 1 an octave lower would join Vln II la metà on C♯5–A5. That gives the lead one violin against about six violins and a flute, which is c467's imbalance made worse (the ear takes the tune from the heavier octave), and it breaks c457's "Vln II alone carries the lower octave". Taking the flute out restores c439's problem: one violin against half a section. Both make the statement worse than Finding 1, which keeps the balance and removes the flute's lead.

### 3. Bars 41–43 (and 273–275): six instruments in unison on theme A, one cor anglais an octave below (kept)

Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Vln I and Vln II play A5–C6 in unison, with only the cor anglais at A4. PDF 165: octave doublings are clearer than unisons for power. The flutes cannot go up (C7 is the finale's). Moving Vln II down an octave would give the theme a real second octave, but it answers c111's reason only partly: four winds on one string group at A5 is the imbalance c111 removed (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76). Kept.

### 4. Bar 223: no harp at the trio's main cadence (kept)

*Scheherazade* (PDF 179) uses the harp to set off phrases and to support the final cadence. A rolled E-major chord in the harp's right hand at 223 would mark the forte cadence. I kept the harp silent: c364 keeps it out of 209–224, and its entry at 225 is the codetta's change of colour after the forte (c458). A chord at 223 would take that change away.

### 5. Bars 121–122: Cl 1's B4 sounds above Vln II's G4 (kept)

On beat 3 of 121 Cl 1 rises to B4 above the tune's lower octave (Vln II G4), with Hn 2 and the violas on G4. This breaks PDF 159's first rule for one beat. The upper octave (Vln I, B5–E6) carries the tune, and c308 and c458 kept the sub. p fill here. The crossing lasts one beat and changing Cl 1's line would remove the clarinet pair's thirds. Kept.

### 6. Bars 329–332: the chromatic tail in Cl 1 at p under strings at mf (kept)

Cl 1's G5–F♯5–F5–E5–D♯5 (c478) is marked p< while the strings' motive x is mf<f. The tail is part of a crescendo by groups (c050: the second group enters p and crescendos faster; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127), and the violins already carry the rising line against the falling bass (c033). Raising the clarinet would undo that order. Kept.

### 7. Bars 217–220: no oboes at the trio's ff peak (kept)

Brahms 3/i (PDF 164) fills every chord in every choir. At the trio's peak the woodwinds are Fl 1 and Cl 1 on T, with bassoons and bass clarinet below. I kept the oboes out: Ob 1's dolce entry at 225 opens the codetta (c423), and the trio is meant to stay soft in colour even at its peak.
