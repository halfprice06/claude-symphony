**Form verdict.** Part 1 follows Unit 15's plan for a minor-key movement point by point:
- I – iv – ♮VII (as V of III) – III, with III expanded and closed by a cadence;
- III to V through IV and the chromatic II6 (the F episode's F/A);
- three-bar groups before the four-bar hemiola on V.

The reprise reverses the ♮VII's meaning (♮VII7 → V6/5 → i at 273–277). This is Ex. 15-14 on the same notes that led to III at 41–44, and it is the reprise's best formal stroke. The coda's falling bass at 329–332 is Ex. 15-15/15-16 with the Neapolitan 6/4 in place of IV6.

The movement keeps moving. Every long pedal has a goal: V at 89–104, the link's I at 133–152, the codetta's I at 225–240. The 4:38 length is right between a 5:45 Adagio and a 7-minute finale. I found nothing in the large form that the reading asks me to change.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 215: the leading-tone triad of V stands in root position with its dissonant 5th quintupled, and the root is missing on the downbeat (applied)

- **Bars.** 213–216, the trio tune's forte return. Since c157 the bass rises A–A♯–A♯–B, giving V6/5 of V (F♯–A♯–C♯–E) for two bars before the half cadence on B.
- **Problem.** At 215 the downbeat sounds:
  - A♯ in the basses, contrabassoon and harp;
  - C♯ in the cellos, Bsn 2 and Hn 1;
  - E five times: Vln I E6, Vln II and Cl 1 E5, and Bsn 1 and Hn 2 in unison on E4.
  - F♯, the root, comes only in the violas' louré off-beats on beats 2–3, under a forte tune.
  - So the bar is heard as a diminished triad A♯–C♯–E in 5/3 position, with its most dissonant tone (E, a diminished 5th above the bass) doubled the most.
  - Bar 214 has the same diminished triad for one beat only. The violins' F♯ (T's held peak) enters on beat 2 with the violas, and the cellos take F♯ on beat 3. That bar is heard as V6/5.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 243: "Diminished triads on the leading tone are seldom satisfactory in 5/3 position… V6/5 (which contains all three tones of the diminished VII) creates a far more pleasing sonority and is to be preferred." VII5/3 is the alternative only "in three-part writing", where the 6th cannot be had. This is a full tutti.
  - PDF 244 (Ex. 15-18, Bach): when the diminished triad is used, "Bach doubles the 3rd… the one tone not involved in any dissonant relationship". At 215 the doubled tone is the one inside the tritone A♯–E.
  - Ex. 15-19's excuse, a diminished chord "grow[ing] out of the strong V", does not apply. Bar 215 grows out of 214, which is the same chord.
  - Points for Review 10 (PDF 245): "a leading tone in the bass should usually support V6/5".
- **Fix (applied).** In tC.ily, `tCBsnOne`, the seventh bar of the line (215):
  ```
  e'2.-- | e'2.-- | e'2.-- | e'2.-- | fis'2) r4   →   e'2.-- | e'2.-- | e'2.-- | fis'2.-- | fis'2) r4
  ```
  - Bsn 1 now steps E4→F♯4 at 215 against the violins' F♯→E, which is contrary motion. It holds the F♯ into 216, where c247 already put it.
  - The downbeat of 215 now has all four tones, F♯–A♯–C♯–E. The unison E4 with Hn 2 is gone, and E is left in Hn 2 and the tune's octaves.
  - Hn 2 keeps the seventh E4 and its fall to D♯4 at 216 (c093, c157). Hn 1·2 keep their sixths C♯5/E4 (c094). The cellos' countermelody, the violas' louré off-beats and T itself are unchanged.
- **Answer to c247.** c247 kept the doubled E4 at 214–215 because the only horn alternative was an open fifth C♯5/F♯4 in Hn 1·2. This fix changes the bassoon instead, so c247's reason does not arise.
  - c247's other rule was "discords to the bassoons" (Rimsky PDF 108). It still holds where it matters, at 216, where Bsn 1 is inside the horns' sixth. At 215 the discord E is still carried by Hn 2 and by the tune.
- **Why not 214 as well.** Bsn 1 F♯4 at 214 would move in parallel fifths with Cl 1 and Vln II (E4/B4 → F♯4/C♯5, from beat 3 of 213 to 214).
- **Check.** An all-pairs scan of 212–217 (/tmp/critic-263/scan/pairs.py) gives the same 56 pairs before and after; all are existing doublings and none is new. The engraved bassoon staff shows the F♯4 as the upper voice at 215, under the same slur and tenuto (page 8, checked at 200 dpi).

## Points tested and kept (my decisions; composer, keep them)

1. **Reprise without a major III (253–288).** Row 6 praises III's major quality as relief from the minor tonic. The reprise does without it, but it keeps the I–III–V frame:
   - ♮VII7 at 273–276 promises G and is turned to i (row 18);
   - III returns in minor at 289 and leads by step through A (297) to V (301), the Ex. 15-2b bass.

   Restoring G major at 277 would repeat the exposition's move away from the tonic in a section whose job is to stay there. c003, c083 and c173 decided the same. The reading gives me no new reason to reverse them.
2. **Join 288→289.** I→III from a weak to a strong beat (PDF 240) does not happen here. The G at 289 is a unison fugato subject after two beats' silence and a double bar, and B♭ at 290 makes it minor at once.
3. **Coda 329–332.** Ex. 15-15 voices ♮VII as a plain triad. At 330 Vln II's E5 still sounds over the D chord as an upper 9th. c083 left it as colour, and Vln I's A5 on top gives the 4̂ that the book asks for. Kept.
4. **Bar 27 b2–3 (D♯–F♯–A after the pizzicato B) and 214 b1.** Both are diminished triads heard as part of a dominant: the first as in Ex. 15-19, the second because its root arrives on the next beat. Kept.
5. **Proportions and length.** Three blocks of 97/88/93 s, a reprise half the length of part 1, and a coda of 44 s. Kept, as c163, c183, c243 and c253 did.
