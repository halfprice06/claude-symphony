The nearest audible change it points to is at Vln II 175. It is the only bar of 169–184 whose arpeggio does not come back towards its first note: since c543 it falls a sixth, C♯5→F♯4, and that is why its stems flipped. ## Findings

### 1. Vln II 175: the one bar of 169–184 with stems up and its slur below (applied)

**Bars.** 175 (tA.ily, `tAVlnTwo`).

**Problem.** c465 set Vln II 169–184 stems down throughout, with every slur above (do-not-touch, Gould PDF 39). c543 changed 175's last quaver from A♯4 to F♯4: `r8 ais'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 fis'8)`. The group now runs from F♯4 (staff position −3) to E5 (+3), so its extremes are equally far from the middle line, and LilyPond turned the stems up. The probe log has beam +4.76 and stems up. On page 7, 175 is the only group in sixteen bars whose beam sits above the notes and whose slur sits below them (/tmp/critic-555/pages/vln2a.png).

**Reading.** PDF 38–39: when notes are balanced about the middle line, "the context may determine the direction ... notes adjacent to the group in question may take stems in one particular direction", and down-stems are the convention otherwise. PDF 39, visual considerations: "When groups within a passage together roughly span the width of the stave, visual considerations may suggest maintaining the same stem direction for the whole passage ... this allows all slurs ... to remain next to their noteheads." The passage spans F♯4–B5. Both neighbours (174, 176) and every other bar point down.

**Fix (applied).**
```lilypond
\stemDown r8 ais'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 fis'8) \stemNeutral |
```
I also added a two-line comment above `tAVlnTwo` that cites PDF 39. Result: the beam now runs −3.76 → −4.26, falling with the outer interval A♯4→F♯4 (PDF 36). Stems are 3.50 / 4.61 / 5.72 / 4.92 / 3.00, so the shorter stem meets PDF 35's 3 spaces, and the slur is above (/tmp/critic-555/pages/vln2b.png).

### 2. Vln II 182: angled beam with a 2.74-space shorter stem (applied)

**Bars.** 182 (tA.ily, `tAVlnTwo`); the rule is in beams.ily.

**Problem.** c511 rewrote the bar as `r8 fis'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 ais'8)`. The beam rises from F♯4 to A♯4 (−4.00 → −3.50), and the F♯4's stem is 2.74 spaces on a note in a space inside the stave. It is the only angled beam in the score under 2.92. c465's minimum-length rule covers horizontal groups of three or more notes. c375's covers groups whose stems all point away from the centre. A sloped five-note arch that crosses the middle line falls between the two.

**Reading.** PDF 35: "The stem that is going to be the shorter determines the starting point of the beam. This stem may be shortened to 3 stave-spaces (a seventh) when inside the stave and to 2½ stave-spaces (a sixth) when outside the stave." PDF 32: otherwise as close to 3½ as possible.

**Fix (applied).** In beams.ily (`gould-ledger-beam-positions`), c465's exemption is narrowed to pairs only, so sloped single-voice groups of three or more notes also get the 3-space minimum. A comment block above `c375-target` gives the reason.
```scheme
(if (or (and (any negative? ps) (< (length stems) 3)) (<= deficit 0.15))   ; c465: horizontal groups of 3+ also; c555: sloped groups of 3+ also
```
Result: 182's beam now runs −4.26 → −3.76, with stems 3.00 / 4.88 / 5.76 / 4.68 / 3.50 and the slope unchanged at 0.5. The probe diff of all 453 beams shows that only 175 and 182 moved. The other bars of 169–184 changed only their source line numbers. Every other group in the score is unchanged, including the parts' other groups, because beams.ily is shared.
