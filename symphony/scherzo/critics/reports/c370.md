### 2. 42 and 274: the motto tail's ♭6 sounds against its own goal at the same pitch (applied)

**Bars.** 41–43 and 273–275: theme A's head on D, f, with the motto tail C–B–B♭–A (on V of G; B♭ is ♭6 of D). Since c367 the cor anglais doubles it an octave below Vln I, at C5–B4–B♭4–A4. Clarinet 2 holds A4 through 41–42 and Cl 1 holds C5.

**Problem.** In the upper octave the tail runs over the held chord at a distance. In the cor anglais it runs inside it. Its first note is a unison with Cl 1's C5. Its B♭4 sounds against Cl 2's A4, a minor second at the same pitch, so the 5 that the ♭6 should reach is already sounding beside it, and the chromatic step of the quoted motto is covered in that octave.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 388: "♭6 is strongly active in the direction of 5. Once introduced, it tends to move to 5". Ex. 23-2f (image): the Chopin F♭ passes through the chord and no other voice holds its goal B♭. Ex. 23-12b (PDF 394): a chromatic line is clean when it is one voice's own motion. The tail is the cyclic material the brief says to quote clearly (cyclic-notes: "Quote the motto ... the chromatic tail ... every theme statement").

**Fix (applied).** sA.ily `sAClTwo`: `a'2.~\f | a'2. | fis'2.` → `a'2.\f | fis'2.~ | fis'2.`. dA.ily `dAClTwo`, the same at 273–275. Cl 2 takes the F♯4 one bar early and ties it. The V7's A stays in Bsn 1 (A3), and the A4 now arrives at 43 as the tail's resolution. The voice-leading is unchanged around it: Cl 2 already moved to F♯4 at 43 and to D4 at 44. Theme, tune doublings and dynamics are unchanged, and so is c324's unison (no flute passes B6). The print shows a tied F♯4 under Cl 1's tied C5 at 42.

### 4. Weighed and kept (do-not-touch read as reasons)

- **A ♭6 before the turn to minor at 5–6 (Mendelssohn, Ex. 23-1a, where A minor comes in bar 3).** Rejected. A C♮ under the held D♯4 would soften the direct G♯→G♮ change at 7, which is the "dramatic juxtaposition" of PDF 387. It would also add a part to the call, which movement I plays alone over a bass pedal (I 13–14; c090).
- **A mixture IIø6/5 (F♯–A–C–E) at the coda's last cadence, 355 (Ex. 23-2d/e).** Rejected. C at 355 followed by the A♯°7's C♯ at 358 and B at 360 is ♭6–♮6–5, the order PDF 388 calls poor. The current order (C♯ 341–358, C 359, B 360) is the good one.
- **Hn 3 C4–C♯4–B3 at 87–89 (c270).** The reason holds. C4 is the diatonic ♭6 of E minor held from F major, and C♯4 is the third of A♯°7 → B, not a ♮6 replacing a mixture ♭6 in major.
- **Theme A in major inside the scherzo (Ex. 23-8, Exercise 6).** Kept for the finale. Its C-major restatement at IV 275–276 is the symphony's extended-mixture answer, and a major theme A here would take it away.
- **♭VI (PDF 390, "one of the most important" mixture chords).** Not available: it would be C major.
