## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 333: the low brass return after 212 bars' rest at a plain *f* (applied)
**Problem.** Trombones, bass trombone and tuba rest from 121 to 332. c033 held them back so that the coda would be their fresh return. They come back at 333 on the coda's ff downbeat (theme head, horns ff, strings and woodwind ff), but marked `\f`. The movement's longest-prepared re-entry therefore has the attack that Rimsky calls less successful.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 136: "After a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful." PDF 136 also: a group silent for some time "gains fresh interest upon its reappearance", and the scoring should make that interest heard. PDF 116: the passage's role (here the climax) decides the scoring. In Beethoven 9/ii, bar 415, the trombones' first entry is ff with a diminuendo.
**Answer to c086.** c086 lowered these parts from ff to f because ff low brass outweighed the trumpets' f motive x and the horns (Adler PDF 53). The fix keeps c086's balance from the second bar on. Only the attack is ff, on the same beat where the four horns and the whole orchestra are ff. The hairpin brings the low brass to f by 334, under the trumpets' next motive x at 335. They still rest at 336–338 (c034) and return fff at 339, so the fff peak and the 339 chord are unchanged.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily: `codaTbnOne = { R2.*8 | g2.\ff\> | g2.\f | g2. | …`, `codaTbnTwo = { R2.*8 | e2.\ff\> | e2.\f | e2. | …` (the shared staff keeps equal dynamics), `codaBassTbn = { R2.*8 | e,2.\ff\> | e,2.\f | b,2. | …`, `codaTuba … | e,,2.\ff\> | e,,2.\f | b,,2. | …`. No notes change.

### 2. 361–364: the closing chord stops as one block instead of thinning into the horn's G♯ (applied)
**Problem.** At 361–364 the E-major chord is held pp by Fl 1 (G♯5), Cl 1·2 (E4/B3), Vln II (G♯4), Vla (B3), Vc (E3) and Cb (E2), with Hn 1·2 and the harp. On beat 3 of 364 all the woodwind and strings stop together, with no hairpin. About ten voices drop to Hn 1 alone (and Hn 3 at 365) in one step. The closing page's dynamic plan (pp at 361, Hn 1's fade to ppp over 369–372) needs a diminuendo here, but the score has a cut-off.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 127 (image): "the transition from a complex to a simple timbre, by the suppression of one of the groups, belongs essentially to the diminuendo." PDF 130: prolonged diminuendi come from removing groups combined with "natural dynamic means", and clarinets "are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)". The order brass → woodwind → strings cannot apply here, because the horn's G♯ must be the last sound (protected frame). What remains of the rule is that the other groups fade out instead of being cut off.
**Fix (applied, hairpins only).** coda.ily, a `\>` from 362 that closes on the rest of 364. Fl 1 `gis''2.\pp | gis''2.~\> | gis''2.~ | gis''2 r4\!`. Cl 1 `e'2.\pp | e'2.~\> | e'2.~ | e'2 r4\!` and Cl 2 `b2.\pp | b2.~\> | b2.~ | b2 r4\!` (equal on the shared staff). Vln II `gis'2.~ | gis'2.~\> | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4\!`. Vla `b2.~ | b2.~\> | b2.~ | b2 r4\!`. Vc `e2.~ | e2.~\> | e2.~ | e2 r4\!`. Cb `e,2.~ | e,2.~\> | e,2.~ | e,2 r4\!`. Hn 2 (E4) has no hairpin because it shares a staff with Hn 1's held G♯. It now releases alone at the end of the fade, so the elimination goes strings and woodwind, then Hn 2, then Hn 1. Notes, the motto tail 357–360 and the last G♯ are unchanged.
