## Findings

### 1. Harp LH strikes C♯2 a minor third above the basses' A♯1 at 214–215. Applied.

**Problem.** From 209 to 224 the harp LH strikes the bass note mf on every downbeat, together with the arco basses, the contrabassoon and the cellos. At 214–215 the bass is c157's A♯ (V6/5 of V): Cb A♯1, Cbsn A♯2. The harp strikes C♯2, a minor third above the basses' A♯1. It is the only plucked attack in that octave, so the clearest pitch heard on those two downbeats is C♯, not the A♯ the bass line moves through (A–A♯–A♯–B). c174 found this on the same reading and moved the harp to A♯1. c346 moved it back to C♯2 (v347): getting A♯1 meant re-pedalling the A string twice in four bars while the low A1/A♯1 was still ringing, and the harp does not suit fast chromatic changes (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46). Both reasons are sound. The two fixes have simply traded one fault for the other.

**Evidence.** The series puts only octaves, fifths and fourths at the bottom, and thirds only from the fifth partial (Casanelles PDF 274). A minor third at A♯1–C♯2 lies far below that. A plucked note is mostly attack, and the attack is part of the discourse (PDF 275–276), so the harp, not the sustained basses, decides which bass the listener hears on the downbeat. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 85 and Adler part 1 PDF 150–151 make the same spacing point. `harpbass.py` finds no other harp stroke in the movement that sits above the bass at an interval other than an octave or fifth, except the F episode's own LH chords.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tC.ily, `tCHarpL`, bars 214–215:
```
tCHarpL = { e,4\mf r4 r4 | gis,4 r4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r4 | e,4 r4 r4 | a,,4 r4 r4 | R2. | R2. | b,,4 r4 r4 | ...
```
(was `cis,4 r4 r4 | cis,4 r4 r4`). The harp drops out for the two bars where it cannot double the bass without re-pedalling a ringing string. The A pedal stays A♮ from 209 to 224 (c346), and no harp note sits above the bass (c174). The harp's next stroke, B1 at 216, then marks the dominant's arrival. The arco basses, contrabassoon, cellos and Bsn 2 carry 214–215 as before. A c354 comment is added above the line. This bar range lies inside c299's "soft trio 153–216" do-not-touch. The note being changed was put there later by c346, and both recorded reasons, c174's and c346's, are kept.

### 2. Bar 363: the first closing timpani motive x sounds inside the held E pedal. No change.

At 363 the timpani's B2–B2–B2–E2 (pp, soft sticks) enters under a held pp E-major chord. Vc E3 and Cb E2 sustain in the drum's own band until 364, and the harp's E2–B2 struck at 361 is still ringing. In Fig. 6.1 Mahler lets the distant layer in only once the woodwinds have stopped (PDF 272), and same-band sounds mask each other (PDF 287). So at 363 the figure fuses with the chord. At 367 and 371 it stands alone. I considered stopping the low strings at 362. That would leave B3 as the lowest held note under the E chord at 363–364, a 6/4 sound on the movement's last full chord. The present order also makes a perspective of its own: the figure is first heard inside the chord, then alone, then ppp near the rim (PDF 283–284). The closing page is protected, and the finale quotes 363–371. **Decision:** keep.

### 3. Coda 349–356: the last segment of T is in the violins' highest register. No change.

Casanelles says distance and nostalgia come from losing high frequencies (PDF 273, 287), and the coda's return of T, Meno mosso in tempo del Trio, is a memory. On that alone, the last segment would lie lower than A6. But 341–356 is one statement passed from player to player (Hn 3 bars 1–4, Ob 1 bars 5–8, violins and Cl 1 bars 9–16), not three statements at growing distances. The receding is done by dynamics (p, p, pp) and by the closing page that follows. The mutes that would act as an orchestral high-cut belong to movement II (PDF 286; cyclic-notes). Moving Vln I down would put it in unison with Vln II and Cl 1. c264 and c344 kept this for the same reasons. **Decision:** keep.

### 4. Trumpets enter pp under forte strings at 101/301 (PDF 271–272). No change.

Read Mahler's way, a dynamic marks what is heard, and pp trumpets under f strings are not heard until 103–104. Here the pp starts a four-bar crescendo to f, so the trumpets join the build as its last layer, without an attack. That follows Dan's rule (weight, not bite) and c264. **Decision:** keep.

### 5. Fl 1 in unison with Ob 1 at B4–A5, 29–36 (PDF 275, 277). No change.

In this register the flute's fundamental is weak, so the oboe's denser spectrum leads (PDF 289) and the flute only softens it. That is the intended blend (c054, c131), the flute's first a2 colour is kept for 37, and the call-and-response layout 29–37 is protected. **Decision:** keep.

### 6. Bsn 1 (mf) and Hn 1·2 (p) inside T's lower octave at 209–216 (PDF 286–287). No change.

At the trio's return, T's lower octave (Vln II, Cl 1, G♯4–E5) shares its band with Bsn 1's held E4/F♯4 and the horns' G♯4–C♯5. The upper octave (Vln I, G♯5–E6) is alone in its band, so the tune is heard through it, and the lower octave and the fillers make one thick middle. That is the same distribution c264 accepted for the tuttis. The horns are marked two levels under the tune, and the horn pair is protected (c087, c094). **Decision:** keep.
