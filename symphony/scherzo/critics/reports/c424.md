## Findings

### 1. Bars 105–120 and 305–320: the upper brass sound a bare E–B on every E-minor downbeat (Adler Ex. XI-1, PDF 15–16; PDF 17). Applied.
**Problem.** In both theme-A tuttis, Hn 1 plays B4 and Hn 2 plays E4 on beats 1 and 3, and the trumpets' motive x is on the same B4/E4. On the tonic bars (105, 106, 107, 109, 112, 117, 118, 120 and 305, 306, 307, 309, 312, 317, 318, 320) the brass above the trombones is a bare fourth. In the exposition the only brass third is Tbn 1's G3. In the reprise there are no trombones (c033), so the brass chord has no third at all. Every forte chord in Ex. XI-1 is complete in the brass's upper octave, with the horns placed inside or against the trumpets. Adler also names this weakness directly: once the opening chord is over, horns and trumpets "get stuck on the tonic and dominant notes" (PDF 17). Hn 1 moved only on the iv and V bars (110, 113, 115, 116). There is a voice-leading fault as well: Hn 1 leapt F♯4→B4 from the dominant at 104/304 into the tonic.
**Fix (applied in sC.ily and dC.ily, `sCHnOne` and `dCHnOne`).** Hn 1 takes G4 on the tonic bars and keeps B4 on the dominant and E/G♯ bars and C5 on the iv bars:
`sCHnOne = { g'4-- r4 g'4-. | g'4-- r4 r4 | g'4-- r4 g'4-. | b'4-- r4 b'4-. | g'4-- r4 g'4-. | c''4-- r4 r4 | b'4-- r4 b'4-. | g'4-- r4 g'4-. | c''4-- r4 a'4-. | b'4-- r4 b'4-. | c''4-- r4 c''4-. | fis'4-- r4 b'4-. | g'4-- r4 g'4-. | g'4-- r4 r4 | b'4-- r4 b'4-. | g'4-- r4 g'4-. | R2.*20 | }`, and the same first 16 bars in `dCHnOne` (then `R2.*4`). I kept the comments after the brace.
**What a listener hears.** On odd bars the brass chord is complete: Tpt 1 B4 over Hn 1 G4, with Hn 2 and Tpt 2 on E4 (enclosure, as in Ex. XI-1). On even bars Hn 1·2 sound a close third, G4 over E4, which is the horn pair's own sound in a late-Romantic tutti. Hn 1 now moves F♯4→G4 by step at 104→105 and 304→305. It alternates G4–B4–C5 with the harmony instead of holding one pitch. At 120 Hn 1's G4 passes straight to Hn 2's sub. p G4 at 121. The register is lower, so the horn is less bright, which suits Dan's "weight rather than bite".
**Checks.** Hn 2 keeps E4/D♯4, so every leading tone D♯ still resolves to E in Hn 2. I tried putting the third in Hn 2 instead and dropped it, because it would leave D♯4→G4 leaps at 104→105, 108→109, 111→112, 116→117 and 119→120. 106 and 118 still have no beat-3 note under the chromatic tail (c011). Hn 1 is now at G4 (written D5), well below c066's limit. c097's paired tenor line, c046's Hn 2 E4 and c086's layout (horns on beats 1 and 3, trumpets on motive x, single low-brass downbeats) are unchanged. No C-major triad results (cmajor.py: none). Build clean, 13 pages.

### 2. Bars 105–120: low brass on every downbeat for 16 bars (Adler PDF 14, 17). Not applied.
This was the candidate a listener would hear most. Tbn 1·2, bass trombone and tuba could play only on the odd bars, beside the trumpets' motive x, so that the tutti breathes in two-bar units. I decided against it. The bass changes chord every bar (E E E B E A B E | A G♯ A B E G B E), and the even bars hold the V at 108 and 116 and the E/G♯ at 114. Adler's second use of brass is "to strengthen and clarify the harmony" (PDF 16). Taking the brass off those bars would leave the harmony to the contrabassoon and pizz.-weight basses under a ff tutti. The strokes are short tenuto crotchets, and the reprise and coda already hold the low brass back (c033), so the "bombastic throughout" warning does not apply. Keep.

### 3. Bars 105–120: Tbn 1 could double the tenor countermelody in long notes (Tchaikovsky, PDF 38–41; melodic doubling, PDF 16). Not applied.
One trombone equals two horns (PDF 15, 44), so Tbn 1 on the line would double its brass weight in G3–E4. That would compete with theme A's lower octave (Cl 2, cor anglais, Vln II at E4–G4). It would also undo c014's reason, which Rimsky supports (PDF 104: the trombone "rarely takes part" in sustained lines). Tbn 1 already lands on the line on half its downbeats (c197). Keep.

### 4. Bars 101–104, 301–304, 333–338: single horns ff (PDF 15). Keep.
Adler's own exception covers this: the trumpets never leave E♭4–B4, their low register, where one horn at ff matches a trumpet at f. Pairing the horns would drop two chord tones. Same decision as c244 and c334.

### 5. Bars 89–100: brass could punctuate the timpani's motive x (Hindemith, PDF 42–44). Keep.
Brass here would spend the trombones' pp first entry at 101, the colour held back for that event (row 10). They would also cover the pp head entries in the violas and violins. The timpani answers at 91/94/97 (c327) already work as the punctuation.

### 6. Bars 149–152 or 133–140: soft trombones as a new colour at a formal joint (Schubert, PDF 37). Not applied.
The model fits the turn from E minor to E major. But a soft trombone chord is the sound of movement I's pp trombone chorale and is kept for the finale (cyclic-notes; c406). Hn 1's G→G♯ already marks the joint.

### 7. Bars 321–339: the coda arrival (Tchaikovsky, PDF 38–41). Follows.
The music drops to Vc/Cb at 321–324, the timpani roll is at 332, and long low-brass notes enter at 333 under theme A's head while the trumpets keep motive x in the woodwinds' rhythm. The low brass rest at 336–338 and return at 339 (c034). This is Adler's third climax technique, adjusted for the soft-brass rule. Keep.

### 8. Bars 72 and 300: two horns, single, at the ff A-major arrival (PDF 15). Keep.
At 72, Hn 3·4 cannot join: their first entry must be "senza sord." at 73. At 300, doubling Hn 1's C♯5 would make a high horn unison, which is kept for the finale. The horns here work in their woodwind capacity beside ff woodwind and strings (Franck, PDF 38), and Hn 1's C♯5→C5 into 73 is protected (c042).
