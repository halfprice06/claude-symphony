## Other study

I built a probe engraver in a scratch copy (/tmp/critic-215/probe, outside work/). It logs the x/y extents of every notehead, stem, flag, dot, accidental, rest, script, text and barline per system, and the length of every hairpin as drawn. From that log I measured the following:
- the stem-to-barline and accidental-to-barline distances (PDF 57);
- the space given to each rhythmic column, compared with the median for the same duration in the same system (PDF 54);
- every same-onset unison, second and crossing between two parts on one staff (PDF 62, 65–67);
- dot positions and dot alignment (PDF 67–68);
- chord stem directions (PDF 60–61);
- overlaps between heads, rests, dots and accidentals (PDF 55).

I viewed all 13 pages at 80 dpi and checked the suspect places at 200–900 dpi: pages 1–4, 12 and 13. I also checked v215's history for the earlier passes on this reading (c035, c125), so as not to repeat their points.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. One-beat hairpins stretch their beat to 1.4–1.7 times the system's crotchet (applied)
Bars: 111 and 311 (cello swell on the 9–8, c198), 158, 174, 214, 346 (c178 swells), 166, 182, 222 (c098 suspension swells), 355.
Problem: c155 set `Hairpin.minimum-length = #5.0` with springs-and-rods, so that short hairpins would not read as accents. Its stated aim was hairpins of "at least about three stave-spaces". In practice the shortest hairpins are drawn 4.1–4.9 spaces long. To make room, LilyPond widens the beat that carries the swell. At 111 the first crotchet takes 5.0 spaces, while the same crotchet in 107, two bars before on the same system, takes 3.1. This shows on the page as a gap in Vln I's theme A rhythm. The same happens at 158/174/214/346, where the swelled crotchet is 4.3 against 2.8–3.0, and at 166/182/222 (5.0 against 2.8–3.0).
Evidence: Gould PDF 54: "Notes of equal duration require equal spacing for an entire system, even when the speed of the note-values changes." PDF 53–54: the eye confuses rhythm "when the relative distance between notes or rests contradicts their duration".
Fix (score.ly, Staff context): `\override Hairpin.minimum-length = #3.9` (was 5.0), keeping springs-and-rods and bound-padding 0.5. In the scratch build the shortest hairpin is now drawn 3.03 spaces long and no hairpin is under 3.0, which still meets c155's aim. The stretch is gone at 111, 158, 174, 214, 346 and 355. At 166, 182, 222 and 311 it falls to about 1.35× (3.9 against 2.8), where a crescendo and a diminuendo meet on one beat. This does not undo c155: no hairpin is drawn shorter than c155 asked for.

### 2. Stems closer than one space to a barline (applied)
Bars: Fl, Ob, Cl, C.a. 334 (0.51 space); Cl, C.a., Vln II 306 (0.50), 316 and 318 (0.58), 110 and 310 (0.71–0.74), 106, 116, 118 (0.65–0.78); C.a. 189; Vla 195; Cl 246. Down-stems after a barline also stood 0.84–0.95 space from it throughout.
Problem: the beamed up-stem quavers at the end of the tutti theme's bars (`fis''8 f''8 |`, `b'8 b' |`) stand half a space from the next barline. LilyPond spaces the barline from the noteheads, not from the stems of beamed notes. At 334 the right stem and the barline form the pair of close parallel lines that Gould shows as wrong.
Evidence: Gould PDF 57: "Stems must never come closer to a barline than one space, because the close parallel lines are difficult to read" (example "acceptable in cramped conditions … but not"). PDF 56: "allow a stave-space … on either side of a barline before a notational symbol".
Fix (score.ly, Score context): `\override BarLine.extra-spacing-width = #'(-0.9 . 0) \override BarLine.space-alist.next-note = #'(semi-fixed-space . 1.1)`. After the scratch build, every stem in the movement is at least 0.91 space from a barline: the four lowest are 0.91 (Vla/Vc 296) and 0.94 (Bn/Vc 321), and all the others are at 1.0 or more. A few system breaks moved by a bar, for example 58–74 instead of 59–75, and 354–376 is now the last system. The page count stays at 13.

### 3. Clarinet 2's bar rest on Clarinet 1's quavers, bar 85 (applied)
Problem: at 85 the part combiner sets the clarinets apart, because Cl 2 enters at 86. Cl 2's whole-bar rest then hangs from the bottom line, the default for a second voice. It sits on the two F4 quavers of Cl 1's `a'4( f'8-- f'-- a'4`: the probe measured 0.06 space of overlap, and at 900 dpi the rest reads as a beam or a ledger-line block under the two heads.
Evidence: Gould PDF 55: "the distance between characters should not be less than ½ stave-space and no characters should collide."
Fix (sB.ily, sBClTwo bar 85): `\tag #'score \once \override MultiMeasureRest.staff-position = #-8 R2.` This puts the rest one space lower, on a ledger line below the staff and about 0.9 space clear of the heads. It is score only, as is Ob 2's rest at 105 (c205). I first tried `\partCombineSoloI` for 81–85, but the slur 85–87 then crossed a change of voice ("cannot end slur"), so I rejected it.

### 4. Flags 0.4 space from the next note in dotted rhythms: checked, no change
Bars: Ob 187, Vln II 195, Cl 199 and 211, Hn 284 and 343.
The flag of the up-stem quaver in `x4. y8 z4` ends 0.37–0.39 space before the next notehead. The flag tip, however, lies 0.6–2 spaces above that head (for example Cl 199: flag y 0.64–3.78, next head y −0.56–0.56), so the real distance between the two shapes is more than ½ space. PDF 55 is met, and I made no change.

### 5. Space for accidentals inside quaver pairs: checked, no change
A quaver followed by a note with an accidental (`fis''8 f''8`, `gis''8 g''8`) gets 2.9–3.4 spaces against 2.0 for a plain quaver pair in the same system. Gould PDF 57 says that one accidental "can usually be accommodated … unless notes are very close together". At this density the quavers are close together (2.0 spaces), so the extra room is the case Gould allows, and squeezing it would make the natural touch the preceding stem. No change.

### 6. Bar-length notes 370–375: not raised
c035 rejected this point and c125 agreed. The bars are still narrow (23 bars on the last system), so the reason holds.
