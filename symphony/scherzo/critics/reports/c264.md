## Findings

No finding needs a change in v264. The score already applies what this chapter teaches about orchestral balance. That work came from earlier passes over the same pages: the accompaniment is marked a level under the tune (c099, c174), the brass are held below the strings at the climaxes (c194, c199), and the bass is spaced on the series (c174). Below are the points I checked against the reading, ranked by how much each could matter, each with my decision.

### 1. Coda 349–356: the furthest statement of T is in the violins' brightest register (Principles 12–13). Not changed.
**Observation.** The coda gives T three colours, Hn 3 p (341), Ob 1 p (345) and violins pp (349). Each is softer than the one before, which is the receding perspective of PDF 283–284. The last one, though, has Vln I at C♯6–A6 with its peak A6 at 350, the register where the violin's spectrum is thinnest and brightest. Casanelles says distance takes off high frequencies first (PDF 272–273). On that alone, the furthest statement would sit an octave lower, peaking at A5.
**Evidence against changing it.** At pp, the high violins give the thin, bodiless sound the passage wants. Vln II and Cl 1 carry the octave below, so the line keeps its body without loudness. Taking Vln I down an octave would put it in unison with Cl 1 and Vln II and remove the line's only high colour before the closing page. The passage is protected (c133, c254), and themes-scherzo.ly checks 349–353. **Decision:** keep.

### 2. Coda 341–344: the harp's close triads C♯3–E3–A3 under Hn 3 (Principle 4). Not changed.
**Observation.** Struck chords this low and in close position lie inside the series' wide-spacing zone: the third C♯3 sits an octave below the A1 fundamental's fifth partial. The violas also hold the chord's top note A3.
**Evidence against changing it.** The chords are pp, and their top note is a third or more below Hn 3's lowest note (C♯4). That is the register separation the chapter asks for (PDF 277). Raising them would put them in the horn's band, which c099 removed at 345. **Decision:** keep.

### 3. Trio 153–168 and 185–188: struck harp chords under soft wind solos (Principles 6, 11). Not changed.
The harp is pp under the p solo. Its top note (E4 or F♯4) stays a third or more below the solo's lowest notes (G♯4 at 153, G♯4/A4 at 187–188), so the attack sits outside the solo's band, as PDF 275–277 asks. Hn 3·4 hold the open fifth E3–B3 underneath, in series order. No change.

### 4. Tutti 105–120: the theme's lower octave (C.a., Cl 2) inside the horns' and trumpets' band (Principle 9). Not changed.
Six parts share E4–B4 here. The upper octave (Vln I/II, Ob 1, Cl 1, then Fl 1·2 from 109) has no other part in its band, so the theme is heard through the upper octave and the lower one only thickens it. c084 and c174 judged this, and the tutti layout is protected (c214, c224, c254). No change.

### 5. Hemiola build 101–104: trumpets enter pp inside a forte tutti (Principle 11). Not changed.
Read Mahler's way, pp is the level the entrance should be heard at, and the trumpets cannot be heard at pp under f strings and woodwinds. Here, though, the marking starts a four-bar crescendo to f, so the trumpets come in as the build's last layer (c050, c128), which is Dan's rule for brass entries. No change.

### 6. Closing 363–375: distance by timbre (Principle 12). Not changed.
The last two timpani figures stay pp and then ppp "near the rim" (Adler PDF 80: a beautiful pianissimo near the rim), and the harp's last chord is harmonics. Both change the colour as well as the level, which is the distinction the Mahler passage makes (PDF 272): quieter alone is not the same sound. The page is protected. No change.

### 7. Flute register (Principle 5). Not changed.
The flutes go below D5 only at 29–40, where Fl 1 plays in unison with Ob 1 at p and then a2 on a rising line, and at 269–276. In the unison the oboe colour leads and the flute softens it, as c054 and c131 intended. Every forte flute line is at G5 or higher (c036). No change.
