## Findings

### 1. Bars 289-296: Bsn 1 doubles the cellos' line in full instead of marking downbeats (applied)

**Problem.** The reprise fugato begins with the cellos alone on the subject (G minor, p, staccato). The basses rest until 297, so the cellos are the bass. Bsn 1 plays one pp crotchet on each downbeat (c337, after Beethoven 9/ii bars 17-24), and Cl 1 does the same for the violas' answer at 293. The result is a string fugato with faint thuds on the downbeats. The subject sounds in the same colour as all three entries of the exposition fugato (57 Vc, 61 Vla, 65 Vln II). These four bars are the fourth cello statement of the subject, and they are its only return.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 241-243 (Ex. VIII-10, Figaro overture): a bassoon doubling the cello part "is certainly audible even when performed softly, since it is the only nonstring playing the part. It adds a wonderful new color". The bassoons "were frequently used to bolster the bass". The Figaro line is a soft staccato quaver bass in cellos and bassoon, the same texture as 289-292. PDF 238: Schubert "orchestrat[es] each major thematic element differently", which "enhances the clarity of the form". PDF 255: the third time, "it must be a bit different". A single bassoon is not a like-wind unison (PDF 249-250), and the range D2-D4 lies in the bassoon's best part of the instrument.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-594/work/sections/dB.ily).**
- `dBBsnOne` 289-296: `g4-.\pp r4 r4 | bes4-. r4 r4 | g4-. r4 r4 | a,4-. r4 r4 | d'4-\sysLabel "1." r4 r4 | a4 r4 r4 | f4 r4 r4 | e4 r4 r4 |` becomes `\partCombineSoloI g4-.\pp d8-. d-. g4-. | bes2->( a8 aes8 | g4-.) d8-. d-. bes,4-. | a,4-. fis,-. d,-. | d'4(-\sysLabel "1." c' bes) | a4( bes g) | f4(\< e d | e2 b,4) | \partCombineAutomatic a2.\p\<` …. These are the cellos' notes, slurs and articulation, pp under their p, with a crescendo at 295 as in the cellos.
- `dBBsnTwo` 289-296: `\partCombineSoloI R2. … R2. | \partCombineAutomatic a,2.\p\<` …, so that the shared staff does not split into two voices at the hairpin.
- `dBClOne` is unchanged. The clarinet keeps c337's downbeat marks on the violas' answer (see Finding 2).

c337's reason was that the reprise should keep the string colour and change only its hue. Adler's Figaro page gives a stronger reason for the bass line in particular. The cellos still lead, the clarinet still follows the Beethoven model, and the exposition fugato 57-68 stays strings alone (c007, c254). Audible result: at 289 the subject has the dry edge of a staccato bassoon on the cellos' bass, and in the reprise the fugato is heard as a new colour, not a repeat of 57.

### 2. Bars 293-296: Cl 1 doubling the violas' answer in full (built and reverted)

I built Cl 1 on the whole answer as well (`d''4-.\pp a'8-. a'-. d''4-. | f''2->( e''8 es''8 | …`). That puts motive x's quavers in a woodwind at 293. The woodwinds' first motive-x quavers in this section are the stretto entries at 297-299 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 at 298), and c337 keeps those new. Adler PDF 238 (Schubert does not anticipate what he is heading for) and PDF 245 (Strauss lifts the pedal so the next entry is heard) support c337 on this point. Reverted. The bassoon in Finding 1 has no quaver entry at 297, so it takes nothing away from the stretto.

### 3. Bars 105-120 / 305-320: no sustained upper pedal in the tutti theme (checked, kept)

Mozart 29 (PDF 240-241) holds oboe and horn pedals through the tutti. Here only Bsn 2's E2 is sustained, and Hn 1·2 give the pedal in crotchets. c414 tested held pedals: they clash with the iv, V and Neapolitan F under the tail, and the layout is protected (c214, c224, c304, "do not flip back"). The reading gives no stronger reason. Kept.

### 4. Bars 13-28: first statement without a wind pedal (checked, kept)

The Fledermaus page (PDF 245) supports a soft pedal under a spiccato tune. The score uses it from 29 and lifts it at 31-32 and 35-36 as Strauss does. W. Schuman's bass clarinet (PDF 260) supports keeping the winds' first entry new at 29. c324, c414 and c504 hold. Kept.

### 5. Bars 41-43: more 8-foot than 4-foot (checked, kept)

PDF 250-251 prefers octave couplings. A 4-foot octave on the theme's C6 would put flutes on C7, which is forbidden (no flute above A6 in the tuttis, and the finale owns C7). Vln II down an octave undoes c111. Kept.

### 6. Bars 209-216 and 121-124: Brahms's single bassoon on the violin tune (checked, not applied)

PDF 267: a single bassoon in octaves with Vln I brings a theme through a thick background. At 209 the lower octave already has Cl 1 with Vln II. That completes Schumann's "apart, then together" (PDF 253): T was played by winds at 153 and by strings at 169. Fl 1 waits for 217, as Schubert's flute does (PDF 238). At 121-124 the background is full, but the bars are protected as undoubled, and the tenor octave is saved for 281 (c071, c274). Not applied.

### 7. Bars 281-284: Vln I alone on the top octave (checked, kept)

Six parts carry the two lower octaves, and Vln I alone carries C6-F♯6. Berlioz's flute (PDF 266) would warm it. But c339's hand-over (the flute's last note is the upbeat at 280, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 125-126) and the flute's return on top at the f hemiola 285 follow Adler PDF 238: the flute is heard again at the cadence's top notes. Kept.

### 8. Bars 225-236: the codetta heads are a relay, not overlapping imitation (checked, kept)

Mozart 38 (PDF 271-272) overlaps its wind entries. Here Ob 1, Cl 1 and Bsn 1 enter in turn, each one octave lower. c423, c477 and c504 keep nothing over a soloist, and the third entry already changes (Bsn 1's C♮, PDF 255). Kept.
