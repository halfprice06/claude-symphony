## Findings

**1. 313–316: Hn 4 slurs a falling ninth into the low register at ff (applied).**
- Problem. At 305–320 Hn 4 holds the bass an octave above Bsn 2 (c114, c362). Its third phrase is one slur: F♯3–G3–A2–B2 (written C♯4–D4–E3–F♯3). From 314 to 315 the slur drops a ninth, from written D4 to written E3. That lands below written C4, in Adler's "dark and may be a bit unfocused" band, in the full reprise tutti at ff. A slurred ninth is a lip slur across several partials. The low note has to be found and made to speak with no new tongue stroke.
- Evidence. Adler PDF 304: the hornist must "hear" and then "find" each note, and wide leaps are hard. PDF 303: the low register is most effective sustained, not hurried. PDF 309: a slur is one breath and one legato gesture. PDF 284: the loose embouchure of low notes makes their attack the problem. adler2 PDF 3 makes the same point for the trombone's low legato. All other leaps in the brass parts are a sixth or less, and most are at p in the trio tempo.
- Notes. I tested E3 at 315 instead of A2, which would be a third down from G3 and fill the empty A2–A3 octave. It makes E3 the seventh of the ii♯ø7 under the melody's F♯5. That seventh would then have to resolve to D♯3 at 316, which doubles the viola's leading tone, or leap away to B2. F♯3 at 316 would make fifths with the bass A→B. c319 kept these notes for the same voice-leading reason, so the pitches stay.
- Fix (dC.ily): `dCHnFour = { ... | fis2.( | g2.) | a,2.( | b,2.) \once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN \breathe | ...` The A2 is now tongued as the start of a two-bar slur. Notes, the breath at 316, dynamics and the 305–312 phrasing are unchanged. Hn 4 shares the staff with Hn 3, whose slur is unchanged, so no dynamic or ottava question arises.
- Applied in /tmp/critic-496/work. No line is silenced or added, so parts-cues.json is unaffected.

**2. The candidate a listener would hear most: four soft horns under Cl 1 at 81–88 (built in thought, rejected).**
- Adler PDF 311–312 (four horns as a velvet "accompaniment choir") and Ex. X-16 (Humperdinck, four horns *sehr weich*) are the strongest audible suggestions in the reading. In the F-major episode, Hn 3·4 already hold pp chords under the harp (73–80) and Cl 1 (81–88). Hn 1·2 would make it four.
- Rejected, for three reasons:
  - Four soft horns in chords for eight bars is a horn chorale, and the brass chorale is kept for the finale (cyclic rule).
  - Hn 1 would play 71–88 and then 101–120 with only 89–100 in between. That works against Adler's "frequent intervals of rest" (PDF 292) and "periodic rests are most welcome" for the high horn (PDF 303).
  - The episode's colour is winds and harp without strings, and a second horn pair would move its weight into the brass.
- No change.

**3. 261: Hn 3 enters ppp in unison with the cellos' pp (no change).**
- Adler PDF 292 says brass "cannot be expected to play as softly as the strings". That is the reason the marking sits one level under the cellos, as at 169 (c431). In performance the horn will meet the cellos at about their level. Marked pp, it would cover them and break the "grows phrase by phrase" plan (c457).
- The entry is at written G4, in the middle register, where Adler PDF 284 sees no attack problem. Kept.

**4. Lowest notes: tuba E1 at 105–120/333–339 and Hn 4 written E3 in the tuttis (no change).**
- E1 is the bottom of X-85. Adler PDF 8 says low notes "used as slow-moving bass notes ... will be effective at any dynamic from ff to pp", and one tenuto crotchet per bar is exactly that. Moving the tuba up to E2 would put it in unison with the bass trombone. The brass would then lose the 16' weight that Dan's "weight, not bite" asks for (BRIEF).
- Hn 4's written E3–F♯3 at 305–320 are dotted minims, which is the sustained use X-4/X-5 allow (PDF 303).

**5. Stopped or muted horn (no change).**
- PDF 307–308 (Ex. X-17 to X-19) gives the colours. The places they would fit (241, 141–144, 149–168) were rejected by c406 and c488 for the soft-brass rule and the G♯ = A♭ turn. The reading gives no new reason. Not proposed again.

**6. Brass endurance and the slurs as breaths (no change).**
- Every loud brass slur is five bars or fewer. Hn 3·4 and Hn 4 have breath marks at 104, 116, 304 and 316. Hn 1 rests after each high span (121–140, 224–236, 321–331, 340–356). The tuba plays 25 bars. This follows PDF 292, 303 and adler2 PDF 8.
