## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The reading's contrapuntal principles that can apply to a scherzo whose trumpets are limited to motive x and whose brass accents must stay soft are already met (rows 1, 4–6, 8, 9, 11, 16). The rest are excluded by the cyclic rules or Dan's rule (rows 3, 10, 12, 14), or do not suit the material (rows 7, 15). I tested five candidates and kept the score as it is in each case.

### Tested and kept

1. **Bars 89–100: brass punctuation of the timpani's motive x (Hindemith m. 192, PDF 44).** Hindemith closes his fugato with the timpani stating the theme and the brass striking chords in its rests. Here the timpani's motive x (89, 92, 95, 98–100) is answered by pizz. B in the cellos and basses. Soft horn chords in the rests would copy Hindemith's device, but they would add brass attacks to a pp passage whose effect depends on the timpani being alone with the strings. The trumpets already take over the punctuating role at 101 (pp<), and the full brass punctuation follows at 105. c097 protected this passage on the same principle. Keep.

2. **Bars 333 and 335: the trumpets' motive x falls on pitches the horns are holding** (Tpt 1 B4 against Hn 1 B4, Tpt 2 E4 against Hn 3 E4); 101 and 103 are the same (Tpt 2 F♯4 against Hn 1 F♯4). By rows 4 and 8, a figure doubled at the unison by a held note in the same register might lose its outline. Bartók's horns a2 sustain under his trumpets' articulated line, though (PDF 46, mm. 342–349), and the trumpet's attack carries over a horn at the same pitch. The doubling also gives the held chord its rhythm without adding a new pitch to the sixth-heavy brass chord. Moving Tpt 2 to G4 would double Tbn 1/Hn 2's third and leave the E unarticulated. Keep.

3. **Bars 105–120: an independent trombone line (Schoenberg, PDF 44, 48–49).** Tbn 1 already meets the Hn 3·4 countermelody at the unison on eight of its sixteen downbeats (105, 109, 110, 112, 114, 115, 117, 120), so the tenor line gets trombone weight where it lands. A full trombone line would turn into the brass chorale or low-brass melody kept for the finale, and it would undo c086's layout. Keep.

4. **Bar 71/299: balance of the two-horn entry (Bartók, PDF 44).** Hn 1·2 in unison at A4 (written E5, the horn's best register) with Vc/Bsn 1 at A3, f<, sound against Vln I, Vln II, Vla and three upper winds in rising crotchets. Their weight is one trombone's, and nothing else sits in their octave on the beat (the violas are at C♯5–E5, the second violins at F♯5–A5). The entry is heard. Keep, as c097 wrote it.

5. **Bars 337–338: horns keep the barline against the hemiola.** Only the horns hold dotted minims while strings, woodwind, trumpets and timpani play the hemiola minims. This matches Adler's climactic model (row 16) and c048's choice. Holding the metre in one sustained group is the free-counterpoint layering the chapter describes (row 1). Keep.
