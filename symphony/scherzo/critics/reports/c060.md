## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 357–360: Hn 2's E4 sits at the solo's level under the motto tail. Applied.
**Problem.** The movement's cyclic close is Hn 1 B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 p "espress." The finale answers it a major third lower, and movement II's solo clarinet states it a major third higher. Hn 2 holds E4 at p directly under it on the same staff, for all four bars. Critic 58 set the accompaniment to pp but had to leave Hn 2 at p, because a shared staff needs equal dynamics (c058, F2). So the quotation sounds as the top of a horn dyad (B/E, A♯/E, A/E, G♯/E) in one colour and at one dynamic. It does not sound as a single line. The E is already present: violas E4 at 357, Vln II E4 at 359–360, Bsn 2 E3, and Vc/Cb E from 358.
**Evidence.** Economy, Sebesky PDF 12–13. Ex. 1 deletes the one line in the lower-middle register so the main element "dominates a little more noticeably". Focus, PDF 13–14. Balance, PDF 11. In Dvořák 9/iii, 263–268, the sustaining horns are kept below the horn solo. In this symphony, every other statement of the motto's tail or of the horn call is a single line: I 13–14 (Hn 1 alone), II 85–89 (Cl 1), scherzo 3–6 (Hn 1).
**Fix (applied).** `codaHnTwo`: `… R2.*16 | e'2.\p | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2.\pp | …` → `… R2.*16 | R2.*4 | e'2.\pp | …`. Hn 2 re-enters pp with the E-major chord at 361, and Hn 1 is pp there too (equal dynamics). The harmony E – A♯°7 – iv6/4 – E is unchanged, and so are Hn 1's notes. I tried `\partCombineSoloI` to print "1." over 357–360, but any switch inside Hn 1's slur (357–360) or tie (360–361) gives a slur or tie warning. I left the combiner automatic, so the staff shows Hn 2's bar rests under Hn 1. That is clear and clean.

### 2. The coherence files say the horns leave D♯4→E4 to the finale, and the score does not. Correct the documents; no notes change.
**Problem.** scherzo.md (Opening, bars 7–8) says the horn call's D♯4 is not resolved at its own pitch and that "the E♭/D♯ → E thread at E4 is left to the finale". finale.md says the finale's Hn 3 D♯4→E4 at 219–220 "also resolves the scherzo horn call's unresolved D♯4 at its own pitch". In the score, horns move D♯4→E4 at 104→105, 108→109, 111→112, 116→117, 119→120, 216→217, 304→305, 308→309, 311→312, 316→317 and 319→320 (Hn 2), and at 332→333 and 338→339 (Hn 3, ff and fff). Tpt 2 does the same at 338→339. The relation holds only on paper. What a listener does hear is local: the call stops on D♯4 over V (5–6), and bar 7 gives E only in other octaves.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 241: "Learn to trust the judgment of your ear instead of your eye. Music does not exist on paper." PDF 240: compare intention with result.
**Decision.** I am not changing any notes. Moving every horn leading tone in an E-minor movement would damage the tutti voice leading for a link nobody can hear across 25 minutes. Replace the scherzo.md sentence with: "The horn call's D♯4 (5–6) is not resolved in its own phrase: bar 7 has no E4, and E sounds only in other octaves. Later the horns use D♯4→E4 as an ordinary leading tone (Hn 2 in the tuttis, Hn 3 at 333 and 339)." Send the finale thread the same wording, so finale.md says its 219–220 resolution answers the call's phrase, not the first D♯4→E4 in the horns.

### 3. T and the second idea are the horn call in new proportions. Record this for the finale; no notes change.
**Problem.** The theme records call T "new material leaning on D♯" (cyclic-notes, scherzo v01 relay) and do not mention the second idea's source. Both come from the horn call (3–1–7):
- Second idea, 45–47: D6–B5–G6–F♯6, so B–G–F♯ is 3–1–7 in G with the rising minor sixth. 49–51 repeats it, C♯6–A6–G6.
- T, 153–155: G♯4–A4–B4–E5–D♯5. This is G♯–E–D♯, 3–1–7 in E, the scherzo's own bars 3–5 an octave higher, with the sixth filled by step. Like the call, T's head does not resolve its 7. Its first phrase ends C♯–B–A–G♯ (6–5–4–3), which is the diatonic form of the coda's motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯.
For the finale's rework, this means the scherzo's most lyrical tune is already the major horn call. The finale resolves that call (E–C–B→C, 243–246). In C, T's head is E–F–G–C–B.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 11 (vary a motive already used before bringing in a new one; Holman's growth from one germ). PDF 241–242 (older sounds in new proportions).
**Fix (documents only).** In themes-scherzo.md, add to the trioT row: "head = horn call 3–1–7 (G♯–E–D♯) with the sixth filled (A, B); first phrase ends 6–5–4–3, the diatonic form of the motto tail". Add to secondIdeaG: "B–G–F♯ (45–47) and C♯–A–G (49–51): the horn call's rising sixth and 7, in G". Send both lines to the finale thread as information. Nothing in the finale has to change.

### 4. Harmonic variety: identical harmonisations. Recorded; no change in the scherzo.
**Problem.** Theme A's bass is the same at 13, 105 and 253, and at 305 except for 313. T's bass is the same in all three trio statements (153, 169, 209: E E B E A F♯ F♯ B C♯ G♯ F♯ A B B E). Across movements, the scherzo's close 357–360 and the finale's 273–275 have the same harmony, a major third apart.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19, Ex. 3/3A: avoid harmonising a melody the same way twice, reach the same resting place.
**Decision.** No change. The literal da capo and the repeated trio period are the genre's form (Eroica iii, Dvořák 8/iii and 9/iii). Each restatement is already recoloured (row 9 of the table), and the coda reharmonises T. I tried a V6/5-of-V reharmonisation at 214 in the 209 statement. It doubles the A–A♯–B line the cellos already play at 214–215, so I dropped it. The III/IV identity is a deliberate rhyme that three threads depend on, and the scherzo's close is on the do-not-touch list. Its variety comes from the melody levels (E♭5, B4, G4) and the scoring (clarinet, solo horn, tutti), not from the chords.
