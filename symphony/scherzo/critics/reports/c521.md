## Findings

No finding changes the score. Below are the candidates I tested against the reading, ranked by how much a listener would hear each one. I built the first one.

### 1. 105–116: Tbn 1 in unison with the horns' tenor countermelody (built, rejected)

**Candidate.** In the exposition tutti, theme A is in the high strings and woodwinds, ff spiccato. Under it, Hn 3·4 (a2), Bsn 1 and the cellos play the legato tenor countermelody G3–E4. Tbn 1 plays tenuto crotchets on the downbeats. Hindemith (PDF 19–20) says the brilliance and "nobility" of a brass line come from horns and trombones in unison, not from trumpets. Tchaikovsky (PDF 28–30) carries a tune in trombones, horns, bassoons and low strings against busy counterpoint. c251 and c341 recorded the absence of trombones on this line under Dan's soft-brass rule (c123). That reason is weak, because c123 forbids accents and hard attacks, not a legato doubling. A unison doubling is exactly "weight rather than bite". So I tested the candidate.

**Build (sections/sC.ily, since reverted).**
```
sCTbnOne = { g2.(^\markup \italic "espress." | b2 e'4~ | e'2 b4 | a2 fis4 | g2.) | a2( c'4~ | c'4 b4 a4 | g2.) | c'2.( | b2. | a2 c'4 | b2 a4) \breathe | g2( e'4~ | e'2 g4 | a2 fis4 | g4--) r4 r4 | R2.*20 | }
```
The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, the same single analyser parallel (bar 69). Tbn 2, the bass trombone and the tuba kept the downbeats.

**Why it is worse.**
- PDF 23 asks which notes the composer wants to dominate. In every one of Adler's trombone examples (Hindemith, *1812*, *Les Préludes*), the trombones carry the main tune. In this tutti the main tune is theme A, so a trombone would put the most weight on the second element. That inverts the order c317 set (PDF 184 of ch. 15: the principal voice is louder). The tenor line already has two horns in unison at ff, a bassoon and the cellos. The score.ly comment at the MIDI channel setup records that Tbn 1·2 were once the loudest track in this tutti, and a sustained line would make that worse in Dan's mockup.
- The model agrees. In Dvořák 9/iii, bars 33–40, the horns alone carry the tenor-register counter-line, which is what this score does at 105. The trombones stay out.
- The low-brass downbeats would lose their upper note. Since c014 and c087 those downbeats give the tutti its one-in-a-bar pulse under the spiccato. On the page, the Tbn 1·2 staff then carries a legato line over a crotchet pulse at 105–116, which makes the tutti heavy where the scherzo needs to stay light.
- Theme A, the tune a listener should leave humming, gains nothing from the change.

Fix: none. sC.ily is back to v521.

### 2. 209–216: a horn on T's forte return (considered, kept)

This is the place where the reading most invites a horn on a tune. c406's reason against it ("would take away the horn's first statement of T at 341") no longer holds, because since c431 the horn first sings T at 169. c314's reason still stands, and the reading supports it. At pitch, T reaches A5, which is written E6, the loud high horn Schuller warns about (PDF 26). An octave lower (G♯3–F♯4), the tune would pass through Hn 2's held E4–A4 and Bsn 1's E4 and cross Hn 2 at 212–214. A tune in the same colour and register as a held chord does not separate from it. The pair's pitches are protected (c094, c406). Fix: none.

### 3. 209–216: Hn 4 on the cellos' countermelody from 209 instead of 217 (considered, kept)

PDF 27 would pair the horn with the cellos for the whole countermelody. c317 started Hn 4 at 217 on purpose, so that both elements gain a colour in the same bar, T with Fl 1 and the countermelody with the horn, as the climax nears (ch. 15 PDF 184). Starting at 209 would remove that growth. Fix: none.

### 4. 101–104 and 301–304: horns on the hemiola line, as in Dvořák 9/iii 33–40 (considered, kept)

In Dvořák the horns take the chromatic hemiola. Here the hemiola build (Vln I/II, Fl, C.a., Cl) runs over held brass, p< to f/ff, on the dominant pedal. Hn 1 on the lower octave B4–D♯5 would be written A♯5 at ff, the high loud single horn that PDF 26 warns about, and the line is accented in every part (c123). The held brass is also protected as the build's harmony (c244, c314). Fix: none.

### 5. 341–356: the coda's return of T (kept)

T moves from a horn alone, p dolce, to the oboe with clarinet sixths and bassoon, and then to the solo violin with Fl 1 at pp. That is the *Lohengrin* pattern (PDF 35–36). The horn colour that introduced T at 169 opens the recall, so the listener connects 341 with the trio. The single-player scoring is settled (c438, c494). Fix: none.

### 6. Horn unisons and soft brass (kept)

Every horn unison lies at A4 or below (row 9). Every horn melody is p or pp except the mf at 197, and none is accented. The horn call, the link's foreshadowing at 145–149, the motto-tail preview at 205–208 (Fl 1 at the octave) and the tail at 357–360 are each one horn among silent horns, as c390 set. Fix: none.
