# Critic 198: v198, lens 8 (expression)

Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations and the pedal point (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371), second pass. Cycle 98 read the same unit for the same lens, and its four shadings are still in v198: the trio's cadential 4-3s (165–166, 181–182, 221–222), the coda's delayed 4-3 at 355, Hn 1's motto tail over the tonic pedal (357–360) and the dovetailed 4-3s in the retransition (245–249). So this pass asks a narrower question. Which dissonant suspensions has the score gained since v98, and are they treated the way the unit says a performer must treat them?

I read the OCR text of all 26 pages and rendered every page at 110 dpi. I looked at every example as an image (Ex. 21-1 to 21-42, the Points for Review on PDF 369, the exercises on PDF 370–371). The figures, the "cresc. … p" in Ex. 21-17, the `<>` and "susp." labels in Ex. 21-37 and the "avoid/good" labels in Ex. 21-9, 21-12 and 21-13 come from the images, not the OCR.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The tenor's 9-8 at 110–111 and 310–311 has no accent and no release (applied)

**Problem.** In both tutti statements of theme A, the tenor countermelody (cellos "espress.", Bsn 1, Hn 3·4 at 110–111; cellos, Bsn 1, Hn 3 at 310–311) runs `a2( c'4~ | c'4 b4 a4 | g2.)`. c112 tied the C4 over the barline so that it becomes a 9-8 over the dominant (bass B), to break up 5ths with the tune. It is the only dissonant suspension anywhere in the tutti theme. Over B it is a ♭9, and on the same downbeat B4 sounds above it in Hn 1 and Tpt 1. The whole countermelody is at the tutti's ff with no internal shading. A player reads the tie as one more held note, the dissonance gets no weight, and its resolution is covered by the B4 that already sounds an octave above it.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348, sec. 4: suspensions "bring into prominence the voice in which they occur", including inner voices (Ex. 21-4 puts them in the tenor and alto). "Recognizing and bringing out suspensions is of particular importance to the performer; dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer." PDF 348, sec. 3: the resolution "should sound like a relaxation, a decrease in intensity". PDF 352, sec. 12: a tone of resolution sounding early in another voice weakens the resolution, and contrast "in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics" makes it less of a problem. A tied note cannot take an accent, so the swell is the available notation, as Beethoven uses in Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365) and Dvořák on inner viola notes in 7/iii.

**Fix (applied).** Cellos only, in sC.ily and dC.ily. The swell peaks on the tied dissonance, and the hidden dynamics take the MIDI back to ff on the resolution:
```
% sCVc 110-111 and dCVc 310-311:
a2( c'4\<~ | c'4-\tweak stencil ##f \fff\> b4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff a4) | g2.
```
Cellos only, for three reasons. In sC, Bsn 1 shares its staff with Bsn 2, which holds the bass. In dC, Hn 3 shares its staff with Hn 4, which plays the bass an octave up (c114). Dan's rule keeps brass attacks soft and their shape to dynamics and doubling, so no hairpin goes to the horns. The cellos carry the "espress." on this line. The hairpins print clearly under the cello staff at 110–111 (checked at 110 dpi). Nothing else changes: notes, slurs, bowings and the c112 voicing stay as they are.

### 2. Hn 1's 9-8 at 216 (not applied: it costs two pages)

**Problem.** At T's half cadence in the forte return, Hn 1 holds C♯5 from 213 and ties it into 216, where it is a 9th over the bass B and resolves to B4 on beat 2. Hn 2 has just resolved E4 to D♯4. The horns are p and unshaded. A diminuendo through 215–216 (Hn 1 `cis''2.~\> | cis''4 b'2)`, Hn 2 `e'2.--\> | dis'2.--)`) would follow A&S PDF 348.

**Why not applied.** I tested three versions: a diminuendo from the downbeat of 216 ending on a printed pp at 217; the same ending on a hidden pp; and a diminuendo over 215–216 ended by the existing crescendo at 217. Every version reflowed the whole score from page 1, to 15 pages, with "page 3 has been compressed" warnings. A build of the unmodified v198 with only my comment line added stayed at 13 pages, so the horn hairpins in the part-combined staff cause the reflow. The suspension is in a p accompaniment under the tune's own appoggiatura A♯–B at f, and it resolves into unison with Cl 1 and Vln II. Its shading is worth less than two pages, so I do not recommend it.

### Considered and not changed
- **195–196**: the 7/6–5 in the violins and Bsn 1, and Cl 2's 6/4 fourth E4→D♯4, resolve inside the crescendo from p (193) to f (197). Mozart runs his 7-6s under "cresc." in Ex. 21-17 (PDF 355), and the crescendo is the trio's main build (c033, c061). Kept, as c098 kept it.
- **126/282**: Hn 4's and the violas' C4 over B (the common-tone ♭9, c082) resolves at 127 inside the crescendo from sub. p to f at 129. Kept for the same reason.
- **Hemiola cadences 55, 131, 287**: the dissonances on the notated downbeat fall on the weak half of a hemiola minim, and their resolutions fall on the third minim (A&S PDF 362, Ex. 21-31: the metre shifts, so the suspension is only apparently weak). A diminuendo would undercut the cadence. No marks added. The cadences are also protected (c002, c085).
- **337–338**: Hn 1·2's C5 at fff is an upper neighbour over V7, not a suspension (c082).
- **241–244**: Hn 1's G4 becomes the 7th of iv7 only because the bass moves under it, and its slur into F♯4 already shapes it. The passage is protected (c170).
- **T's half cadences 160, 176, 216, 348**: A♯→B (D♯→E at 348) is an unprepared appoggiatura, Unit 20's subject, not this unit's.
- **Pedal points**: every dominant pedal leads to its tonic (105, 209, 253), and every dissonance over the tonic pedals moves by step (PDF 367–368).
