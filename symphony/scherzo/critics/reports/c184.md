## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 49–52: Hn 2's E3 mixes into the bass and leaves the middle of a forte chord empty (applied)
**Problem.** In the second idea's second phrase (f) Hn 2 holds E3 for four bars. The bass is A–C♯–A | E–F♯–C♯ (Vc and Bsn 1 in octave 3). At 51 the horn is in unison with the cellos' E3. At 51 beat 3 – 52 beat 1 it sits under the cellos' F♯3, a second against the bass, and an E against the D/F♯ chord. Above it nothing sounds until A4 on most beats: 51 reads E2 E3 | A4 C♯5 G5 G6, 52 beats 2–3 C♯2 C♯3 E3 | G4 A4 E5 E6, a gap of an eleventh to a fifteenth at f. The gap scan finds no other forte chord with this void outside the deliberate thin tutti at 105–120.
**Evidence.** PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages." PDF 96 remark 1: "Modern orchestrators do not allow any void in the intermediate parts ... especially in forte passages." PDF 82 note: "The bass part should never mix with the others." c094 and c119 kept the E3 as an inner pedal (PDF 84). It is not an inner pedal: it is in the bass's own octave, under the bass at 51–52, and dissonant against D/F♯. c004 had moved Hn 2 down to fill the bottom gap; A3 still fills that gap (an octave above the bass A2 at 49–50) and fills the middle as well.
**Fix (applied).** sA.ily, `sAHnTwo`: `e2.--( | e2.-- | e2.-- | e2.--) | g2.--(` → `a2.--( | a2.-- | a2.-- | a2-- g4--) | g2.--(`. A3 is a chord tone of A, A7/E, D/F♯ and A7/C♯, and sits an octave under Hn 1's A4, a concord in one colour (PDF 90 rule 4). On beat 3 of 52 it takes G3, the seventh of A7. It holds that note into the 6/4 at 53, where its old line G3–F♯3–G3 goes on unchanged. Moving the G3 to 53 instead would make octaves with Cl 2's A4–G4. The G3 against Hn 1's A4 is a ninth for one beat, a prepared seventh (PDF 97 remark 3). After the change the gap scan finds nothing at 49–52.

### 2. Bars 101–104 and 301–304: the oboes hold a tritone at f–ff, and Ob 2's doubling of Vln II adds nothing (applied)
**Problem.** In the hemiola build each woodwind pair is split, one player on a moving line and the other on a held note: Fl 1 on the line with Fl 2 on F♯5, Ob 2 on the line with Ob 1 on D♯5, Cl 1 on the line with Cl 2 on D♯4. Ob 2 doubles Vln II (B4–A4–F♯4–D♯5–B4–A4) in unison. Against Ob 1's D♯5 that gives A4/D♯5, a tritone, on four of the twelve beats, at the f→ff peak. c094 changed a two-bar clarinet tritone at 41–43 under the same rule. This four-bar oboe tritone is the case the rule names first.
**Evidence.** PDF 90 rule 4: concords, not discords, to instruments of one colour, "specially ... the oboe with its penetrating quality of tone". PDF 52: "the addition of one wind instrument to all or part of the strings in unison only thickens the resonance of the latter, the wood-wind timbre being lost". Ob 2 on Vln II is exactly that. PDF 91 (b): "one tone quality should be devoted to the stationary and another to the moving parts". PDF 95: with woodwind in pairs, mixing the doubled colours is "excellent".
**Fix (applied).** sB.ily: `sBOboeOne` 101–104 `fis''2.\f\< | fis''2. | fis''2. | fis''2.\ff`; `sBOboeTwo` 101–104 `dis''2.\f\< | dis''2. | dis''2. | dis''2.\ff` (was `b'2->\f\< a'4->~ | a'4 fis'2-> | dis''2-> b'4->~ | b'4 a'2->-\tweak stencil ##f \ff`). dB.ily 301–304 the same with `\p\<`, and `\partCombineApart` on both oboes at 301. Printed as a chord, that system pushed the score onto 14 pages; as two voices it stays at 13, and dC re-sets the combine mode at 305. The oboes now hold a third throughout. Ob 1's F♯5 is a mixed unison with Fl 2. Ob 2's D♯5 still doubles Tpt 1's D♯5 at 104, so the wood-wind still softens the trumpet (PDF 51–52). Ob 1 steps F♯5–E5 into 105. The hemiola line keeps Vln II, Cl 1's thirds and the violas' contrary line. c101's Vln II and c114's Fl 2/Ob 1 order are kept.

### 3. Bars 221–222: the contrabassoon sits inside the cello countermelody and makes seconds at the bottom (applied)
**Problem.** c114 put the contrabassoon an octave above the basses for 213–222. At 221–222 that octave is B2. The cello and Bsn 2 countermelody falls to it and below it: B2–C♯3 | A2–B2. So C♯3/B2 (221 beat 3) and A2/B2 (222 beat 1) sound as seconds in the contrabassoon's register, with the countermelody crossing under the bass's upper octave. This is the only place from 213 to 222 where the countermelody reaches the contrabassoon.
**Evidence.** PDF 85 and 96 remark 2: wide intervals (octaves, sixths) in the bass, close ones only at the top. PDF 82 note: the bass part never mixes with the others.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily, `tCCbsn` 221–222: `b,2.--\mp | b,2.--` → `b,,2.--\mp | b,,2.--` (B1 with the basses), then E2 at 223 as before (B1–E2, the basses' own motion). The octave above the bass is still sounded where it matters, by the countermelody's B2 at 221 beats 1–2 and 222 beats 2–3. c114's octave is untouched at 213–220.

### 4. Bars 251–252: the clarinet pair holds a major second (applied)
**Problem.** Over the timpani's motive x on B, Cl 1 holds B4 and Cl 2 moves F♯4→A4, so 251–252 is B4/A4, a whole tone inside one colour for five beats (p<). Of all the same-colour dissonances the scan lists, this is the only exposed second in a soft passage.
**Evidence.** PDF 90 rule 4 (seconds named first among the discords); PDF 91 (b) (stationary and moving parts).
**Fix (applied).** retr.ily: `retrClOne` 249–252 `dis''2.)\pp | b'2.\< | a'2. | a'4\p r4 r4`; `retrClTwo` `fis'2.\pp | fis'2.\< | fis'2. | fis'4\p r4 r4`. The pair now reads D♯5/F♯4, B4/F♯4, A4/F♯4. The V7's seventh moves into Cl 1, which falls D♯5–B4–A4 against Vln I's rising arpeggio, and Cl 2 is the stationary part. Dynamics are unchanged.
