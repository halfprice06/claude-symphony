## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The trio call's "turn" is not a turn (185 Ob 1; 189 C.a. and Vc; 197 Fl 1·2, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II). APPLIED.

**Problem.** c021 introduced the call's first bar as a "quaver turn": `e''4( dis''8 cis''8 dis''8 e''8 | cis''2 gis'4)`. The figure is E–D♯–C♯–D♯–E, a dip down a third and back. C♯ is a chord tone (C♯ minor), so both D♯s are plain passing notes. The figure has no upper neighbour and does not circle the main note. It also reaches C♯ a beat before bar 2's minim C♯, so bar 2's longest note is a repeat. The answer at 189 (D♯–C♯–B–C♯–D♯ into B) has the same fault. A listener hears a scale fragment, not an ornament. This is the trio's second melodic idea and it is heard three times, so its first bar should be the most recognisable thing in it.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 338–339, section 13 and Ex. 20-21b (Mahler, *Das Lied von der Erde* VI, checked on the image): the turn "combines an accented and an unaccented neighbor", and in 19th-century music it "becomes a five-note ornament consisting of main tone, upper neighbor, main tone, lower neighbor, and main tone".
- PDF 335, section 6: "The neighbor-note figure (main note, neighbor, main note) is often treated motivically". The turn is that figure on both sides of the note.
- PDF 341, section 14: the lower neighbour a half step below behaves like a leading tone. Here it is D♯–E, the same 7–1 that ends T and that the movement's opening horn call leaves unresolved (bars 5–6). The cyclic rule concerns only bars 5–8, which are untouched.
- Dvořák 9/iii trio (corpus): the ornament decorates its main note (B, upper neighbour C♯) before the line moves on.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-091/work/sections/tB.ily, comment added above tBFluteOne).** Only the first bar of each call changes. Rhythm, slurs, dynamics and bars 2–4 stay as they are.

Both neighbours are dissonant against the chord: F♯ is a 4th and D♯ a 9th over the C♯-minor harp chord at 185 and 197, and E and C♯ do the same over B major at 189. They are unaccented quavers and resolve at once, which is the normal use (PDF 335). The C♯ minim at 186 is now reached by a falling third from E, so it arrives fresh. At 197 the turn sits over Cl 1 and the violas' G♯4–A4 (T's head, c038). The two lines meet at no unison or octave, and the analyser's parallel list is unchanged. The trio middle call is not in the theme record (themecheck: 41/41 unchanged), so the finale need not be told.

## Other points checked and decided (no change)

- **Second idea, E minor, 128 and 284 (`dis'''4 b''4. cis'''8 | b''4`).** By principle 19 a falling C♯ over i6 looks like the wrong form of 6. I tested the case and decided to keep it. The C♯ completes the implied upper line F♯6–E6–D♯6–C♯6–B5 (principle 4, PDF 330) into the cadential 6/4 at 129. The G-major statement's E6–D6 lead-on does the same job.
