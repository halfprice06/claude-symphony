### 1. 237–253: the MIDI tempo jumps up on the retransition's first bar and rises in audible blocks. Applied.

**Problem.** The print has "poco rit." at 237–240, then "poco a poco accelerando" at 241 (P) up to "Tempo I" at 253. The \mt steps in global.ily do something else:
- 237–240: 216, 204, 196, 188. The bar 240 is at 2. = 63, 17.5% under the trio tempo of 228. That is the deepest slowing in the movement before the final rit. The poco rit. at 87–88 goes 9% down and the one at 149–152 14%.
- 241: back up to 204, an 8.5% jump on the downbeat where Hn 1's G♯ falls to G♮.
- Then 4-bar blocks: 204, 228 (+12% at 245), 248 (+9% at 249), 264 at 253.

c028's own comment in global.ily says "the accelerando starts from the rit.'s tempo". After the rit. was deepened to 188, it no longer does.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 585: "Rhythm, texture, and dynamics can all influence the effect of a modulation", and so can the quality of the inflection. A falling inflection (his ♯2–♮2) is the gentle, decreasing kind.
- G♯→G at 241 is that kind, pp, in one held horn. A tempo lurch on the same downbeat makes it sound like a new start rather than the turn in a single motion.
- Ex. 32-6 (PDF 588): the slow section dies to pp and a fermata, and the Allegro grows from that point. It does not jump to an intermediate tempo first.
- The print asks for "poco a poco". Four-bar plateaus with 9–12% steps are not that.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-113/work/global.ily).**

```
  s2.*12 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco rit." \mt 222 s2. \mt 214 s2. \mt 206 s2. \mt 198 s2.
  ...
  \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco a poco accelerando" \mt 198
  % c113: one tempo curve through 237-253 ...
  s2.*2 \mt 208 s2.*2 \mt 220 s2.*2 \mt 232 s2.*2 \mt 244 s2.*2 \mt 254 s2.*2
```

What the new curve does:
- The rit. ends 13% under the trio tempo, the depth of the link's rit. at 149–152.
- The accelerando starts at that tempo and rises every two bars, by at most 6%, to 254. Tempo I (264) follows at 253.
- The verified MIDI steps are 237: 222, 238: 214, 239: 206, 240: 198, 243: 208, 245: 220, 247: 232, 249: 244, 251: 254, 253: 264.
- 237–252 now lasts 13.1 s instead of 13.2 s. The total stays 276 s.
- No printed mark changes, so no new \mt is needed for a new word.

## Considered and kept

- **The reprise has no major key area (253–339).** A&S PDF 596 notes that composers avoid an unrelieved succession of minor chords. The reprise runs e – g – d – a – e, with only the one-bar A major ff at 300 and the D7 at 273–275 as major sounds. In the exposition, G major (45–56), A major (69–72) and F major (73–88) relieve the minor.
  - I keep it. The reprise is the da capo, and its tonic minor is what makes 341's turn to A and E major an arrival.
  - This is the Waldstein's mode change seen from the other side: there the close "resolves" the chromatic area into its diatonic form (PDF 593). Here the coda resolves the movement's minor/major conflict into E major.
  - Adding G major before 289 would also break c103's protection of 289. That protection rests on the D7 at 273–275 being diverted at 276 and fulfilled in the minor at 289, and I agree with it.
- **The link 133–152.** These are 20 bars on the tonic E. Bars 150 and 152 are silent in the MIDI under the poco rit.
  - A&S PDF 586's *Widmung* model is in place: G♯4 is held through the join at one pitch, Hn 1 → harp → Cl 1 at 153.
  - Bars 149–152 give the trio's accompaniment (pizz. bass on 1, harp on 2) before the tune.
  - A 4-bar cut would save 3 s and renumber 153–376, including bars the finale and the theme record cite. Keep (c103).
- **The two-stage coda.** The fast stage (325–339, 9.4 s) is the shortest span of the movement. The slow stage lasts 31 s.
  - Beethoven 9/ii and the Eroica scherzo also end with short codas.
  - The slow stage carries the protected motto tail and the G♯ pivot (357–376).
  - The build 325–332 already goes from pp to ff in eight bars to the only fff and B6 at 336. Keep.
- **The reprise tutti 305–320 repeats 105–120 nearly literally.** The flutes' octave (c024), the held-back low brass (c033) and bass F♯ at 313 are the only differences.
  - Dvořák keeps his closing tutti (a′) complete in the reprise: MCTL 1401–1527 against 471–600, two phrases each.
  - The reading gives no reason to alter it. Keep.
