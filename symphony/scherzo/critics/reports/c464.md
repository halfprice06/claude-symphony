## Findings (ranked)

This is the sixth pass over this reading, and the brass and combined-group writing follows nearly all of it. The largest improvement I found that the reading supports is at the second idea's first cadence. That is the one forte cadence where the horn pair does not do what it does at the two E-minor statements of the same cadence.

### 1. Bars 54–56: the horns' G-major cadence has a seventh inside the horn pair and a struck, unprepared fourth (applied)

**Problem.** The second idea ends with the hemiola cadence. The tune holds the 9–8 (A over G) and the inner voices hold the 4–3 (C to B). In E minor, at 130–131 and 286–287, Hn 2·3 carry the two suspensions as tied thirds: F♯4/A4 over the bar line, falling to E4/G4 with the tune. At 54–55 in G major the horns do something else. Hn 2 holds D4 for three bars. Hn 1 goes B4–A4 on 54, then strikes C5 on 55 beat 1 a seventh above Hn 2's D4. Meanwhile Cl 1 holds the same C5 tied from 54. So on the accented suspension beat:
- the dissonant seventh D4–C5 lies inside one horn colour;
- the brass leaps (A4–C5) while the wood-wind holds;
- the prepared suspension is heard in the clarinet, but the horn, which is the louder part (forte, mf horns), attacks it.

**Evidence.** PDF 102–103: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour". PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind". PDF 106: horns in unison with wood-wind "reinforce" the line and blend the two groups. PDF 114: balance matters most "in dealing with long sustained chords", and these are held minims at a cadence. The E-minor statements, at 130–131 (Hn 2 `b2-- fis'4--~ | fis'4 e'2--`, Hn 3 `g'2-- a'4--~ | a'4 g'2--`) and at 286–287, already follow all three rules. So the movement states the same cadence three times with two different horn treatments, and the first statement is the weaker one.

**Fix (applied).** sA.ily, with a `% c464:` comment above `sAHnOne`:
- `sAHnOne`, bars 53–55: `b'2.--( | b'2-- a'4-- | c''4 b'2--)` → `b'2.--( | b'2-- c''4--~ | c''4 b'2--)`. Hn 1 takes the C5 on 54 beat 3 with Cl 1 and ties it over the bar line, as Hn 3 ties its A4 at 130.
- `sAHnTwo`, bars 53–56: `d'2.--( | d'2.-- | d'2.--) | g4-. r4 r4` → `d'2.--( | d'2-- a'4--~ | a'4 g'2--) | g'4-. r4 r4`. Hn 2 keeps the doubled bass D4 of the cadential 6/4 at 53–54 (c282). On 54 beat 3 it leaps a fifth to A4, as Hn 2 leaps B3–F♯4 at 130, and ties the 9th with Ob 1/Vln II. It resolves to G4 and ends on G4 at 56 under Hn 1's B4.

Result: 54 beat 3 is V7 (D–F♯–A–C), and the horns hold its upper third, A4/C5. The seventh C lies against the bass D in bassoons, cellos and basses, a different colour. At 55 the horns' thirds A4/C5 resolve to G4/B4 on beat 2 together with the tune's 9–8 and Cl 1's 4–3. No G4 or B4 sounds on 55 beat 1, so c172's rule (no resolution tone above the suspension) holds. The harmony, the tune, the hemiola, the tied 9–8, all dynamics and slurs are unchanged, and no other part changes. Nothing is silenced or moved between instruments, so parts-cues.json needs no change. The part combiner prints the two horns as chords at 54–56 (checked on page 2 of the scratch build).

### Considered and left (my decisions)

- **337, Hn 1 C5 against Hn 4 B3 at the fff peak (PDF 102).** It is the same rule as Finding 1. But moving Hn 4 would leave the brass without the root at the movement's single fff, or with a rootless diminished seventh (c194, c262, c284, c374). The reading gives no stronger reason. No change.
- **216, 308/311/319, 108/111/119, 221–222.** These are one-beat suspensions or passing notes in running lines, or detached crotchet strokes. PDF 112 subordinates distribution to "correct progression of parts", and PDF 114 says short chords matter less. No change.
- **75–76, 83, horns between the bass clarinet and Bsn 1 (PDF 108, "not to be recommended").** Kept by five critics for the horn thirds, and Rimsky's own Ex. 158 (PDF 110: "horns within bassoons") uses the layout. The do-not-touch reason stands. No change.
- **237–240, a sixth gap in the horn chord E3–B3–G♯4 (PDF 100).** c374's reason was that the chord must stay bare so the G♯→G turn at 241 is heard. That reason is weak, since Hn 2 on E4 would not cover Hn 1's top G♯. The stronger reason is that 237–240 restates the horn chord of 149–152 note for note, framing the trio. Filling one and not the other would break the frame. No change.
- **Stopped horn with oboe or low clarinet (PDF 107, 110).** It would not break Dan's rule at pp. But the reading names no place in this score that needs the colour. The only dark passage, 87–88, is the protected Neapolitan route, and the model scherzi do not use it. No change.
- **Ob 2 / Cl 1 in unison with the trumpets' motive x at 105–119 (PDF 106, wood-wind softens trumpets).** Both rest there. An oboe in unison with a forte trumpet in a tutti would not be heard, and the trumpets are already softened by Hn 1·2 on the same pitches (PDF 103). No change.
- **45–54, 121–130, 277–280, clarinets inside the horns (PDF 108).** Moving them crosses Vln II (c014–c374). No change.
