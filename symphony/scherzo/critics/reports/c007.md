## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The tutti theme has no middleground (105–120, 309–324). Applied.
**Problem.** At the movement's two loudest statements of theme A, everything above the bass doubles the theme (Vln I and II at unison, Fl 1–2, Ob 1, Cl 1 at the octave) or repeats the chord: horns on beats 1 and 3, violas, Ob 2 and Cl 2 on the off-beats, trombones holding G/E. The texture is the opening's melody-plus-accompaniment, only louder. Adler p. 132 measures a composer's skill by how he handles all three layers. On p. 139 (Haydn 103) a restatement is "set off" by melodizing the inner harmony. The Dvořák model does this at the same point with bassoons and horns.
**Fix.** Vc, Bsn 1 and Hn 3 in unison play a legato countermelody in the tenor (G3–E4). It is in long notes against the theme's staccato quavers (Adler p. 142: its own rhythm and articulation; p. 144: separate register). Its beat-1 and beat-3 notes are chord tones, so it never clashes with the horns' repeated chord. Trombones 1·2 change from sustained dotted minims to accented crotchets on beat 1, which clears the tenor register (p. 144, "thinning the counterpoint"). The bass stays in Cb, Cbsn, Bsn 2, tuba and bass trombone. Bars 1–16 of sC and of dC:
```
sCVc     = { g2.(^\markup \italic "espress." | b2 e'4~ | e'2 b4 | a2 fis4 | g2.) | a2( c'4 | b2 a4 | g2.) | c'2.( | b2. | a2 c'4 | b2 a4 | e'2.~ | e'2 g4 | a2 fis4 | g4) r4 r4 | ...
sCBsnOne = same notes (no markup);  sCHnThree = same notes, first note g2.(\f
sCTbnOne = { g4->\f r4 r4 | g4-> r4 r4 | g4-> r4 r4 | fis4-> r4 r4 | ... (same pitches, beat 1 only)
```
The same goes in dCVc, dCBsnOne, dCHnThree, dCTbnOne and dCTbnTwo. I checked the voice leading: no parallel fifths or octaves with the theme or the bass. Every C♮ falls over an A-minor bass, so there is no C-major triad. The analyser lists no new parallels. Vln II stays on the theme (c004's balance fix is kept).

### 2. The trio's cello "countermelody" moves in T's rhythm and in parallel sixths (169–184, 209–224). Applied.
**Problem.** T moves minim–crotchet in bars 1, 2, 5, 6, 9 and 10 of its 16, and the cello line does the same (`b,2( cis4 | dis2 e4`, `e2( fis4 | gis2 e4`). At 169–170 and 177–178 the two run in parallel sixths, so the cello is a coupling rather than a second voice. Adler p. 142 (V-20): a middleground works when "each of the three parts has its own characteristic rhythm"; p. 144 rule 4: the countertheme must differ rhythmically so the two lines do not interfere.
**Fix.** Keep the contour and harmony, but have the cello move on beat 2 where T holds its minim, and hold where T moves. The tie across 177–178 adds a syncopation. tA bars 17–32 and tC bars 1–16:
```
b,4( e2 | gis2.) | fis2( a4 | gis2.) | e4( cis2~ | cis4 a,2) | ais,2( b,4 | dis2.) | e4( gis2~ | gis4 e2) | a2( fis4 | e2 cis4) | b,2( cis4 | a,4 b,2) | gis,2.~ | gis,2 r4
```
In tA the first note keeps `\p^\markup \italic "arco"`. In tC it is `\f` and keeps `gis,2.~\upbow`. Beat-2 notes are chord tones or the passing tones the old line already had. The only dissonance left is T's A over the cello's G♯ at 172/212, which was there before.

### 3. The accompaniment sits above the theme where the theme dips (16, 32, 40, 44, 260, 276, 280). Applied.
**Problem.** In theme A's fourth bar the melody falls to F♯4–D♯4–B3. Vln II keeps playing A4 on beats 2 and 3, in the same colour and the same crotchet rhythm, a sixth and a seventh above the melody. At 44 and 280 the violas also sit above it (F♯4 over D4). The theme's cadence notes are then the lowest and least audible part of the string texture. Adler p. 135: when foreground and background have similar colour, keep the background out of the melody's register and in its palest one. p. 144 rule 3: separate theme and accompaniment by register.
**Fix.** Put the off-beats under the melody in those bars only. The harmony and pulse are unchanged and the pizz./spiccato character is kept:
```
sA bar 4, 20, 28 (16, 32, 40): sAVlnTwo  r4 a4-. a4-.
sA bar 32 (44):                sAVlnTwo  r4 c'4-. c'4-.    sAVla  r4 fis4-. fis4-.
dA bar 4 (260): r4 a4 a4      dA bar 20 (276): r4 a4 r4
dA bar 24 (280), inside \transpose d b,: Vln II r4 c'4-. c'4-.   Vla r4 fis4-. fis4-.
```

### 4. The fugato countersubject copies the subject's long note (62, 66, 298). Applied.
**Problem.** In its second bar the countersubject has `a2 g4` (Vla answer at 61, `e'2 d'4` at 65). It lands its minim on the same downbeat as the subject's accented minim (`f''2->`), so both voices stop together at the one point where the subject needs the countersubject to keep moving. Adler p. 144 rule 4; p. 145: the subject comes through "because of the rhythmic differences" between subject, countersubject and background.
**Fix.** The countersubject moves in crotchets under the subject's minim: sBVc bar 6 `a4 bes g`, sBVla bar 10 `e'4 f' d'`, dBVc bar 6 `a4 bes g` (slurs unchanged). The new B♭ is an upper neighbour in D minor; the F at 66 is a passing note.

### 5. The fugato is never heard in strings alone (57–68, 293–300). Applied.
**Problem.** Every entry is doubled at the unison by a wind: Bsn 1 with the cellos (57, 293), Cl 1 with the violas (61, 297), Ob 1 with Vln II (65). The winds' arrival at the homophonic climax (69, 305) therefore adds nothing new. Adler p. 143: the exposition of a fugato is often given to the strings "before it is developed, colored, or doubled by other members of the orchestra. This has proven to be a most effective device." p. 145 and p. 147 (Bach, Weber): the homophonic climax gets its force from the contrast with the counterpoint before it.
**Fix.** sBBsnOne bars 1–4, sBClOne bars 5–8, sBOboeOne bars 9–12, dBBsnOne bars 1–4 and dBClOne bars 5–8 become `R2.`. The woodwinds now enter at 69/305 with the tutti. The Bass Cl, Hn 3·4 and harp of the F-major episode are untouched.
