## What else I studied

Bruckner 7/iii (A minor, "Sehr schnell", 3/4; MASTERS.txt lists the symphony's key, E major, but the scherzo is in A minor; trio in F major, "Etwas langsamer"). I worked from memory of the score, so I give no bar numbers. Its method has three parts. A string ostinato carries a short signal figure. Long crescendi build by adding groups, with the brass entering soft and swelling, not arriving at full strength. Each block is set off by sudden terraced drops to p. The trio is a slower, legato string song. Our score has the same outline and in places better craft: the motivic timpani, the ritmo di tre battute at 89-100, the fugato and its stretto, the trio tune's third phrase. Measured against Bruckner it falls short in two habits. Its crescendi arrive by switching groups on at the goal (333) instead of by soft entries that swell. Its reprise plays the second idea a third time at the same dynamic level (277-288), where Bruckner, even in a literal da capo, keeps the f blocks separated by pp. I also compared v39 with v29 bar by bar (diff of sections/*.ily and analysis.txt). One thing got worse: the texture at 197-200 (finding 1), where c037 added T in Cl 1 and the violas but left the held G♯4s in place.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 197-200: T's and the call's A4 grind against held G♯4s in the same octave (applied)
**Problem.** At the trio's forte, Cl 1 and the violas play T (G♯4-A4 | B4-E5 | D♯5-C♯5-B4 | A4-G♯4). At the same pitch, Hn 1 holds G♯4 through 197-198 and again at 200, and Ob 2 holds G♯4 at 197-198. So the A4 on beat 3 of 197 and on beat 1 of 200 sounds against a sustained G♯4 a semitone away, at f, in the middle register where three instruments sit within a third. At 200 the oboe call and Vln II also end on a held G♯4 while T sounds A4. This is the case Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132 describes: a line with a non-chord note against its own fundamental note in the same octave. His remedy (PDF 133) is to keep the harmonic basis "an octave removed from the melodic design" and softer, or else in a strongly different timbre. Horn and clarinet in the same octave do not differ enough. In Ex. 273-274 (PDF 439-440) the held winds lie an octave away from the moving figures. This got worse since v29: before c037 Cl 1 and the violas held G♯ and C♯, so there was no clash.
**Fix (applied, tB.ily).** The held voices move with T, so no A4 sounds against a G♯4:
```
tBHnOne  ... | gis'2\f a'4 | b'2. | a'2.~ | a'4\> gis'2 | ...   % was gis'2.\f | gis'2. | a'2. | gis'2.\>
tBOboeTwo ... | e'2.\f | gis'2. | fis'2. | dis'2.\> | ...      % 197 E4, was G sharp 4
tBOboeOne / tBVlnTwo, bar 200: a'4(\> gis'2)                   % was gis'2.\>
```
At 199-200 Hn 1 makes an A–G♯ 9-8 suspension, taken in unison with T, which is the horn's natural role in a forte wind tutti (PDF 121). The call's last bar in Ob 1 and Vln II now ends A–G♯ with T instead of anticipating the G♯. Vln I and Fl 1 keep their G♯5 an octave up, which the book allows. Hairpins on the shared staves start together.

### 2. Coda 331-333: the horns arrive at ff instead of joining the crescendo (applied)
**Problem.** At 325-332 the strings build the crescendo (pp at 325, mf at 329, f at 332), and clarinets, bassoons and contrabassoon join at 329-331. All four horns, both trumpets and the low brass then enter together at ff on 333. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127 ("amplification") says the second group "enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly". PDF 130 gives the order strings, woodwinds, brass. Ex. 258 (Mlada, PDF 421-422) shows it: piccolo trumpet, alto trumpet and tuba enter pp cresc. two bars before the arrival, under the strings' crescendo. Bruckner 7/iii builds its approaches the same way, with horns swelling from p. Our 101-104 already does this (brass p<). The coda, the movement's main climb, does not.
**Fix (applied, coda.ily).** The horns enter early on chord tones and swell. Trumpets and low brass still enter at 333, so that arrival keeps its weight.
```
codaHnOne   = { R2.*7 | b'2.\p\< | b'2.\ff ...      % 332 B4
codaHnTwo   = { R2.*7 | fis'2.\p\< | g'2.\ff ...    % 332 F sharp 4
codaHnThree = { R2.*6 | e'2.\pp\< | dis'2. | e'2.\ff ...   % 331 E4 (iv6), 332 D sharp 4
codaHnFour  = { R2.*6 | a2.\pp\< | b2. | b2.\ff ...        % 331 A3, 332 B3
```
These notes avoid the bass C at 331 (the horns play only A and E there, no G, so no C-major triad; cmajor.py: none). They take the space between Cl 1's tenths and the bass that the diverging progression (c033, PDF 131) opens, and the book says to fill that space with new parts.

### 3. Bars 277-288: the second idea's third statement is a copy of 121-132 at the same f (applied)
**Problem.** The second idea is heard at 45 (G major, f), at 121 (E minor, f) and at 277 (E minor, f). The 277 statement has the same notes, octaves, scoring and dynamics as 121, apart from the absent low brass. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 118 lists the ways to vary a recurring phrase. One of them is "variation of the general dynamic scheme, e.g. repeating a phrase piano, which has already been played forte", and he calls these operations "always successful". Bruckner's reprise blocks are set off by sudden p. Here the reprise runs f from 273 to 288 with no change of level, so the coda's pp start at 325 is the first contrast in 50 bars.
**Fix (applied, dA.ily).** At 277 every part goes from f to `\subP`, with a crescendo from 281 and f again at the hemiola 285, where the timpani roll enters f. The pitches and orchestration stay the same. This turns the third statement into a build toward the cadence instead of a second copy:
```
dAVlnOne  ... | b''2->\subP\dnb( g''4~ | ... | c'''2\<->( a''4~ | ... | b''4\f( g''4 e''4 | ...
```
The same edit applies to Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1-2, Bsn 1-2, Cbsn, Hn 1-2, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb (`\subP` at 277, `\<` at 281, `\f` at 285). This does not undo c017 or c029. 121-132 stays f.
