Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 21 "Rhythmic Figuration": suspensions, anticipations and the pedal point (aldwell-schachter PDF 346–371), third pass. c098 and c198 read this unit for the same lens. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hn 1's 9-8 at 216 is still unmarked (tested again, not applied: still 15 pages)

**Problem.** In the trio's return, Hn 1 holds C♯5 from 213 and ties it into 216, where it is a 9th over the bass B and resolves to B4 on beat 2. Hn 2 moves E4→D♯4 at the same moment. The horns are p and unshaded. c198 raised this and did not apply it because every horn hairpin it tried reflowed the score to 15 pages.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 348, sec. 4: "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer"; sec. 3: the resolution is "a relaxation, a decrease in intensity".

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-298/work216 I gave both horns of the part-combined staff a diminuendo from the downbeat of 216, ended by the existing crescendo at 217 (`cis''2.~ | cis''4\> b'2)`, `e'2.-- | dis'2.--\>)`). The build has no warnings but makes 15 pages, as in c198. The layout has not changed in a way that answers c198's reason. The suspension is an accompanying horn at p under the tune's own f appoggiatura A♯–B, so two pages are not worth it. **Decision: no change.**

### 2. c289's 4-3 with dissonant preparation at 54–55, 130–131, 286–287 needs no new mark (no change)

**Problem considered.** c289 made Cl 1 hold the V7's seventh into the tonic bar and fall a step: C5→B4 over G at 55, A4→G4 over E at 131 and 287, with Hn 3 doing the same at 131/287. This is exactly Ex. 21-23a (PDF 357–358, a 7th of V7 held as the 4 of a 4-3 over I). The suspension is unmarked, and the horns' portato tenuto falls on the resolution (`c''4 b'2--` at 55; `a'4 g'2--` at 131/287).

**Why no change.** The hemiola bowing (c085) puts the minims at 54.1, 54.3 and 55.2. So the dissonance at the downbeat of 55 is the second crotchet of the second minim, and the resolution starts the third minim. A&S PDF 362–363 (Ex. 21-31) treats a suspension like this as weak in the shifted metre. It needs no accent, and the tenuto on the third minim marks the hemiola, not the resolution. c198 reached the same verdict for the horns' F♯4/A4 at 131. The cadence is f, and in that tutti a single clarinet hairpin would not be heard. The hemiola cadences are protected (c002, c085).

### 3. Everything else follows the reading (no change)

- **Suspension shading (PDF 348).** Each marked suspension crescendos into the tie, or is held at its level, and diminuendos into the resolution: the trio cadences 165–166, 181–182 and 221–222 with hidden peaks a degree up (mp/mf/ff) and resolutions a degree down; 355; 245 and 247–249; and the cello `<>` at 110–111/310–311. These match Beethoven's `<>` … `>` p in Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365).
- **Delayed resolution (PDF 359–360).** 353–355 holds the 6/4 with a tenuto before the 4-3, and 247–249 holds Cl 1's E5 over B for two bars with `<` then p `>`. Both build and release the tension the reading describes.
- **Anticipations (PDF 363–365).** T's tied A is the peak of c038's swell in all four statements. The second idea's anticipating quaver is slurred to its preparation, and the main note is restruck with an accent, as in Ex. 21-33.
- **Pedal points (PDF 367–368).** Every dominant pedal leads to its tonic: 89–104 → 105 with the timpani's motive x growing pp → mf, then the hemiola build; 205–208 → 209 with the roll's crescendo (c248); 245–252 → 253. The closing tonic pedal is under the protected closing page (c279).
- **Anticipated resolution tones (PDF 352).** Only the bass doubles a tone of resolution, apart from 111/311 (contrasting rhythm and timbre) and 200 (protected c117).
