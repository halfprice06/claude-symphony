## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bass clarinet: the cue before the trio-return entry at 211 is unreadable (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** B. cl. 209–211 (rest 149–210, 62 bars).
- **Problem:** Since c486 the bass clarinet takes the bass line over from the contrabassoon at 211, mp, after resting through the link's poco rit., the key change, the Trio tempo and the whole middle section. Its only cue is "Vln. I" at 209–210: the violins' ff trio tune, which in the B♭ part is written four to seven ledger lines above the treble stave (parts audit: "bar 210: 7 ledger lines"). The player cannot read it at a glance, and it is the wrong register to hear from the back of the woodwinds: the line the bass clarinet continues is the cellos' and contrabassoon's bass. c487 had to tighten the page's system padding because this cue overfilled it.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5: "Cues must be audible and obvious to the musician reading the part." Adler ch. 18 (adler2 PDF 299–300): choose the cued line by register. The set's own rule (c165, c385): a cue that would sit four or more ledger lines away takes another source, its own clef or an octave sign.
- **Fix (applied):** parts-cues.json "08": `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",209,2]` → `["cueVc","Vc.","UP",209,2]`. The cellos play b,4 gis e~ | e2 gis4 at 209–210, written c♯'–a♯'–f♯' | f♯'–a♯' in the B♭ part: inside the stave, one ledger line at most, and the bass line the bass clarinet joins at 211. The part stays one page; the c487 padding override is kept (harmless, and the page now has more room).

### 2. Two cues lead to nothing: Fl 2 at 197 and C.a. at 185 (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** Fl 2 197–200 ("Fl. 1"; rest 121–272, next entry 273); C.a. 185–188 ("Ob. 1"; rest 137–268, next entry 269).
- **Problem:** A cue tells the player an entry is coming. Fl 2's cue of Fl 1's call at 197 is followed by 72 more bars of rest (201–272) across letters N, O, P and Q, the poco rit., the accelerando and Tempo I. The C.a.'s cue of the oboe solo at 185 dates from when the C.a. answered at 189; c427 silenced it there and the cue stayed, so it now leads into 80 more bars of rest (189–268). Both real entries already have their own cue just before them (Fl 2 "Vln. I" 271, C.a. "Vln. I" 265). This is the same fault c435 fixed in the trumpets at 297.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5 ("logical cues"); Ex. 4 (PDF 10) puts the cue right after the tempo change and right before the entry; MOLA PDF 8 (a score change must be proofread in the parts).
- **Fix (applied):** parts-cues.json: the entry `["cueFlOne","Fl. 1","UP",197,4]` removed from "02" and `["cueObOne","Ob. 1","UP",185,4]` from "05"; a comment in parts-gen.py records F1 and F2. Side effect: Flute 2 now fits on one page (11 systems, checked by eye: not crowded, no ledger-line clashes), so its page turn at 324/325 disappears. The C.a. part keeps two pages and its pinned turn at 288.

### 3. Shortest page turns: contrabassoon 324/325 and double bass 140/141 (no change)
- **Bars:** Cbn rest 321–329 (9 bars, turn at the Più mosso, entry 330 on the coda's D2); Cb rest 140–148 (9 bars after the pizz. at 137–139, entry 149).
- **Assessment:** MOLA PDF 7 asks for a turn in a rest long enough for the tempo and instrument. Nine bars at 2. = 96 is about 5.6 s; the contrabassoon rests on its peg and the double basses share a stand, so the inside player turns. Both are within what c435 accepted (8 bars minimum). Not worth pinning.

### Considered, no change
- **The largest audible candidate.** This reading concerns the performance material, so what a listener would hear is a missed or misplaced entry. F1 is the one with the most at stake: an exposed mp bass line after 62 bars' rest, taking over from another player in mid-phrase. F1 and F2 are that change. The only note-level way to remove the hand-over would be to give 211–224 back to the contrabassoon, which reverses c486 (a playability change made under Adler ch. 6–7); the part fix removes the risk without changing the sound, so I did not build a score change. MOLA has no rule on notes, voicing or balance that this score breaks.
- **Hn 3 at 237** (32 bars' rest, entering pp on the poco rit. under Hn 1): under the set's 40-bar rule, and the player sits beside Hn 1 and Hn 4, who play through 233–236. No cue.
- **Harp at 225** (32 bars) and **Fl 1 at 29, 101, 253** (28–29 bars): under the 40-bar rule, no tempo change in the rest.
- **Fl 1 page 2, 12 systems:** MOLA PDF 7 allows 12 if not crowded; c315 found that 10 adds a page. Kept.
- **Fl 2 cue at 271 only two bars:** Vln I rests at 269–270, so a longer Vln I cue would print empty bars. Kept.
- **Composer line on part fronts (MOLA PDF 6):** c165's set-wide reason stands.
