## Findings

### 1. 336-338: the fifth of V rests on one horn at fff while the leading tone has a trumpet and a horn (applied)

At the B6 peak the brass chord is Tpt 1 + Hn 1 B4, Tpt 2 + Hn 3 D♯4, Hn 2 F♯4 alone, Hn 4 B3 alone; trombones and tuba rest (c034). Adler PDF 15 says that above mf two horns in unison equal one trumpet or trombone, so a single horn at fff is half the weight of its neighbours; Ex. XI-1's ff chords pair the horns ("2 Hn", "4 Hn") on the notes the trumpets do not take. Here the weakest brass note is the fifth, F♯, and the strongest after the root is the leading tone, which is also in Fl 2 (D♯6) and Cl 1 (D♯5). The fix follows the pairing rule and Dvořák's 9/iii coda (Hn 3 and Hn 4 in unison at the fff chord): Hn 3 joins Hn 2 on F♯4. The D♯ stays in Tpt 2, which in this low register is weaker than a trumpet's normal weight (Adler's own exception, PDF 15), so the leading tone is no longer the heaviest note of the dominant. Hn 3 moves E4-F♯4-E4 (335-339), contrary to Hn 2 and Tpt 2, and Hn 1's ♭9 C5 at 337 now sits a tritone over F♯4 (V♭9's diminished triad) instead of over D♯4. F♯4 is written C♯5, middle register; a sustained chord tone in two horns is not the reserved "high horn unison" (Hn 3·4 are already a2 at 105-120).

### 2. 339: the last ff brass chord has its fifth only in Hn 4 (applied)

The tenuto chord at 339 (c128) is Hn 1 + Tpt 1 G4, Hn 2 + Hn 3 + Tpt 2 E4, Hn 4 B3 alone, Tbn G3/E3, B.tbn E2, tuba E1. E has six instruments, G three, B one horn. Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15-16) keeps the triad complete inside the choir at every dynamic; c104 already had to add Fl 2 B5 because the woodwinds had lost the fifth. Hn 1 holds its B4 from 338 instead of leaping down to G4: B then has Hn 1 and Hn 4 (one trumpet's worth), G keeps Tpt 1 and Tbn 1, E keeps Tpt 2, Hn 2, Hn 3, Tbn 2 and the bass. Hn 1 moves by common tone (smoother before its 341 solo after the G.P.); no new parallels (analysis unchanged: the same 7 known outer-voice parallels).
