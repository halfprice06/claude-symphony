## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The movement's peak at 336 has no dynamic of its own (applied)
- **Bars:** 336–338. Compared with 72, 104–120, 300–320 and 333.
- **Problem:** Vln I's B6 at 336 is the scherzo's highest note, held under a timpani roll. The plan calls it the peak of the movement. It is marked ff, the same level as the fugato stretto (72), the hemiola builds (104, 304), both tutti statements of theme A (105–120, 305–320) and the start of the coda tutti (333). Every climax in the movement is at the same level, so the coda's climax adds only register and tempo.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 38–40 (principle 3). Tchaikovsky's arrival is set one level above the earlier climax, at fff with the timpani roll. Adler PDF 37 (principle 1): save something for the event. Dvořák 9/iii, the model, reaches fff at its climaxes. The trumpets in this coda are already held back at f in 333 and 335, so a third level is available at 336.
- **Fix (applied):** `\fff` on the downbeat of 336 in all 25 sounding parts. Shared staves get it at the same moment in both voices. For example, Vln I `b'''2.->\fff\dnb`, timpani `b,2.:32\fff`, Tpt 1 `b'2.->\fff` (was `\ff`), Fl 2 `b''2.\fff`, Cb `b,,2.\fff`. 339 inherits fff, and the G.P. and 341 p stay as they are. fff is not on the list kept for the finale. Movements I and IV use it, and the finale's fff at its arrival (with cymbals) still stands above a three-bar fff without percussion.

### 2. Bar 72: the ff chord is held dead, and the horns' diminuendo cannot be heard (applied)
- **Bars:** 72–73.
- **Problem:** The stretto lands on A major at 72. Every wind and string part holds an accented ff dotted minim to the barline. Only Hn 1·2 have `\ff\>`, and Hn 2 is the common tone (A) that has to carry pp into the F-major episode at 73. With everyone else at full ff to the cut-off, Hn 2's diminuendo cannot be heard. The result is a full ff block that stops dead, followed by a pp chord a major third away. The composer's own intention, shown in the horns' hairpin and in c010's held A, is a chord that fades into F major, but the other parts do not carry it out.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 189–190 (principle 9): an accent alone is often not enough. Score the sforzando as a short accented note in one group against long notes in another that decay sf>pp (Ex. XV-35). Adler PDF 190 (principle 10): for a sudden piano, the soft group must already be sounding when the loud chord ends, which here is Hn 2's A. Dvořák 9/iii has the same shape at its climaxes (`\fff~ | ~ | \>~ | ~`).
- **Fix (applied, sections/sB.ily):** at 72 add `\>` after `\ff` in Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc, ending with `\!` on the rest at 73. Bsn 1 goes straight on to its `c'2.\pp`. For example, `e'''2.->\ff\dnb\> | R2.\! |`, and `R2.*16` becomes `R2.\! | R2.*15`. The double basses play the short accented note: `a,,2.->\ff\dnb` becomes `a,,4->\ff\dnb r4 r4`. Do not change bar 300 in the reprise: there the ff A major leads into the sfp< build of 301, which already follows principle 10.

### 3. Coda 325–329: the crescendo starts before the stretto entries are complete (applied)
- **Bars:** 325–333.
- **Problem:** Motive x enters from the bottom: Vc 325, Vla 326, Vln II 327, Vln I 328. Each lower entry starts `\pp\<` at once, so by 328 the cellos have been crescendoing for three bars. Vln I enters pp underneath that level and has to jump to mf at 329. The top entry, which completes the stretto, is the one least likely to be heard.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 37 (principle 1): build a climax by holding back. Here the crescendo is spent before the event it prepares. Adler PDF 192 (principle 12): a join is meant to be heard only where an accent is wanted. Adler PDF 195 (principle 13): entry dynamics are what make the entries readable.
- **Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily):** Vc, Vla and Vln II enter `\pp` with no hairpin. All three start `\<` at 328, together with Vln I's `\pp\<`, and all four strings reach `\mf\<` at 329, where the winds, the double basses and the timpani are already marked mf. The ff at 333 stays. For example, Vc: `e4-.\pp ... | e4-.\< ... (328) | e4-.\mf\< ... (329)`.

### 4. 101–104: strings and woodwinds stay at a flat f while the brass crescendo (applied)
- **Bars:** 101–104. The reprise at 301–304 is already correct.
- **Problem:** In the hemiola build onto V, horns, trumpets, trombones and timpani crescendo p/pp→ff over four bars. Strings and woodwinds hold a flat f for three bars, then jump to ff at 104. The same passage in the reprise (301–304) has strings sfp< and winds p<. In the first statement, the two thirds of the orchestra carrying the hemiola melody do not take part in the climax.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 38 (principle 2): in the Franck example the rest of the orchestra builds the climax together with the horns, with no single choir carrying it alone. Adler PDF 39–40: in Tchaikovsky's build every choir moves at once.
- **Fix (applied, sections/sB.ily):** `\f\<` at 101 in Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb, ending at the existing ff on 104. Both voices of each shared staff start the hairpin at the same moment. For example, `b''2\f\< a''4~`. The f→ff contrast with the sfp< of 301 stays: the first statement grows from f, the reprise from sfp.

### 5. The MIDI undoes the printed poco rit. at 240 (applied)
- **Bars:** 237–252.
- **Problem:** The score prints "poco rit." at 237, with no a tempo before the retransition's "poco a poco accelerando" at 241. global.ily steps 216, 204, 196, then `\mt 228` at 240, which is a MIDI-only a tempo in the last bar of the rit. The accelerando then starts at 232, faster than the end of the rit. The printed tempo words and the MIDI disagree.
- **Evidence:** The task rules require every printed tempo word to have a matching `\mt`. Adler PDF 195 (principle 13): the written markings are to be strictly observed.
- **Fix (applied, global.ily):** at 240 `\mt 228` becomes `\mt 188`. The retransition's steps become `\mt 204` (241–244), `\mt 228` (245–248) and `\mt 248` (249–252), then Tempo I `2. = 88` at 253 as before. The accelerando now starts from the slowed tempo. No printed text changes. The MIDI is 275 s (was 274 s).
