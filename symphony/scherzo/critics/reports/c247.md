## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 216: the soft horn-bassoon choir triples the leading tone D♯4 at the half cadence (applied)

**Problem.** In T's forte return (209-224), Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1 hold the harmony as the soft wind choir set up by c014 and c047. Bsn 2 is not part of it, because it doubles the cellos' countermelody. At the half cadence on B (216, beats 1-2) the sounding pitches are:
- Bsn 2 and cellos: F♯3.
- Bsn 1 and Hn 2: D♯4 in unison.
- Violas: D♯4 on beats 2-3.
- Hn 1: C♯5, then B4.

So the three-voice choir sounds only two pitches. D♯4, the leading tone of the key, is played by three players in one octave. Above the cellos there is no F♯ in the chord at all. The unison comes from 214-215, where Bsn 1 and Hn 2 share the seventh E4 (c157), and both then fall to D♯4.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 108: "Four-part harmony given to two bassoons and two horns, especially in soft passages, yields a finely-balanced tone." Here a bassoon duplicates a horn, so the parts are not four real ones.
- PDF 108, same page: "Bassoons may also be written inside the horns", and crossing is recommended "for the purposes of blend". The free bassoon can sit between the horns' sixth D♯4-B4.
- PDF 114: balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords". Bar 216 is a two-beat sustained cadence chord before the breath.
- Ex. 152 (PDF 324) and Ex. 172 (PDF 342): in each soft held chord, every horn and bassoon has its own pitch.
- c147 fixed the same fault at 201-203 (one horn duplicating a bassoon in a soft chord). Bar 216 is the remaining case in the trio return.

**Fix (applied, tC.ily, `tCBsnOne` bar 8):**
```
e'2.-- | e'2.-- | dis'2) r4 |   →   e'2.-- | e'2.-- | fis'2) r4 |
```
- At 216 the choir sounds F♯3 (Bsn 2), D♯4 (Hn 2), F♯4 (Bsn 1) and C♯5-B4 (Hn 1): a complete B chord in four distinct pitches, with the bassoon inside the horns' sixth.
- Hn 2 still resolves the seventh E4 → D♯4 (c093, c157). Bsn 1 leaves the unison E4 by contrary motion, so the doubled seventh splits instead of moving in unison.
- The Hn 1·2 sixths C♯5/E4 → B4/D♯4 (c094) are unchanged.
- Bsn 1 falls a fourth, F♯4 → C♯4, across the existing breath into 217.
- The new F♯4 makes a direct octave with Bsn 2's C♯3 → F♯3. This is between two inner voices, which Aldwell & Schachter allow. The per-player all-pairs parallel scan is identical before and after the change.
- Shared bassoon staff: same rhythm, slur and dynamics as before. Nothing else moves.

## Considered and left (my decisions)

- **214-215: seventh E4 in both Hn 2 and Bsn 1.** Row 6 asks for the discord in the bassoon only. Giving Hn 2 F♯4 instead would make Hn 1·2 an open fifth, C♯5/F♯4, under the strings' tune. That is what c094 removed, citing Rimsky PDF 97, Remark 4. The other horn options are C♯4 (an octave with Hn 1) and A♯ (the raised fourth, which c093 keeps out of everything except the bass). Finding 1 already undoes the unison at its resolution. No change.
- **211: Bsn 1 = Hn 2 on F♯4.** In this V7 the A (seventh) and D♯ are in the violas and cellos. Bsn 1 A3 would have to resolve to G♯3, in unison with Bsn 2 and the cellos at 212. Bsn 1 D♯4 would add a leading tone under the tune's own D♯5/D♯6. Both are worse than the unison. No change.
- **80: Hn 4 = Bsn 1 on A3 (doubled third of F).** c132 chose this, with the reason that vii°7 → I resolves by the rising 2 → 3 (A&S PDF 375). Row 7 is not involved. No change.
- **75-76, 82-83: Bsn 1 above Hn 3·4 with the bass clarinet below** (row 7). c104 and c147 checked the alternatives (parallel twelfths, a thick G2-D3-G3), and the F-major episode is protected. No change.
- **361-364: clarinets E4/B3 at and below Hn 2** (row 10). This is the protected closing page. The horns are the basis, and Fl 1 G♯5 sits above them. No change.
- **336-338: Hn 2 and Hn 3 both on F♯4, so the horns hold no third.** D♯ is already in Tpt 2, Cl 1 and Fl 2. Adding it to a horn would double the leading tone in the brass, which c002/c019 removed. No change.
