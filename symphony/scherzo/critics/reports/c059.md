## Verdict against the Eroica

A conductor would take this for professional late-Romantic writing. Several things match Beethoven's method closely: strings alone and soft at the start (13–28), winds answering in their own colour (29–36), one rhythmic motive carried by the timpani, a tutti statement of the theme with winds in two octaves over held bassoons (105–120), metrical play (the hemiolas and the *ritmo di tre battute* at 89–100), and a soft, differently coloured trio. Where it falls short of Beethoven is the long-range dynamic plan. The Eroica holds *pp* for 92 bars and spends its first *ff* on the theme. This score has four loud arrivals before the trio (f at 41, ff at 72, ff at 104, f at 121), so the tutti at 105 is the third. I decided not to change this. The f second idea is the exposition's lyric peak, and c039 and c058 shaped its dynamics. The ff at 72 is the homophonic close of the fugato, which Adler PDF 145 and 147 recommend. Lowering either would cost more than it gains.

## Findings

### 1. Rehearsal letters and tempo marks print twice on systems without brass (bars 29, 185, 253). Applied.

**Problem.** Since v56, c055 has given the brass StaffGroup its own `Mark_engraver` and `Metronome_mark_engraver`. On a system where no brass staff is shown, the group's marks have no staff to sit on. LilyPond then stacks them above the flutes, on top of the woodwind marks. In v59, "B" prints twice at bar 29 (page 2) and "M" prints twice at 185 (page 7). At 253 (page 9), "Q" and "Tempo I (2. = 88)" print twice, and the upper "Q" box collides with its "Tempo I". v49 printed each mark once. A conductor would read a duplicated letter as an engraving error. That is the first thing to fix before a score could be taken for a master's.

**Evidence.** Crops of pages 2 and 9 from v49 and v59 side by side. Adler PDF 137 states the principle behind all of chapter 5: orchestration and its notation must clarify the form, and a doubled letter at a formal join does the opposite.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-059/work/score.ly).** Move the two engravers from the brass StaffGroup into the Horns 1·2 staff. Each mark then belongs to that staff and disappears with it when the staff is removed:

```lilypond
    \new StaffGroup <<
      \new Staff = "Horns 1·2" \with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" instrumentName = ... }
```

(The old `\with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" \consists "Staff_collecting_engraver" }` on the brass StaffGroup is removed.) Result: one "B", one "M", and one "Q" with one "Tempo I". The brass marks stay wherever Horns 1·2 is shown (checked at A, C, G, U, V and W). The cost: on the two systems where only Horns 3·4 is shown (73–93 and 153–172), "F" and "Trio" no longer repeat above the brass. They still print above the woodwinds and the strings, as they did in v49. I tested a Scheme stencil callback that hides group marks when their staves are dead. It did not detect removed staves reliably, so the staff-level solution is the safe one.

### Examined and kept (decisions, no change)

- **Adler PDF 141 (Shostakovich): cellos doubling theme A's low dip.** At 16 and 32, Vln I drops to F♯4–D♯4–B3 on the G string. I tested Vc pizz. `fis4 dis4 b,4` at both bars, with Cb keeping the downbeat B. The build reports new outer-voice octaves at 16 and 32 (D♯→B over D♯→B), which the rules forbid. The low violins are not covered, because Vln II's A3 and the violas' F♯3 sit below. Decision: keep v59.
- **Adler PDF 152 and 257: doubled third of a 6-chord.** Bars 49 and 51 (temporary leading tones in both outer voices) stay open, as c052 left them. Removing them means rewriting c032's four-bar sequential bass, and the bass's sequence is what holds 45–52 together. At 331 (iv6) the C sits in Cb, Cbsn, Bsn 2, Cl 2 and Vln I. Vln I's C6 is the goal of its rising line G–A–C–D♯ into 333, and Adler PDF 255 puts the melody first. Decision: keep both.
- **Adler PDF 238 (do not anticipate the top register).** c054's comment says the flute's top fourth is "kept for the tutti (110)", but Vln I and Fl 1 already reach G6 at 46 and A6 at 50. The comment is inaccurate. The note itself stays: A6 is the recorded peak of the second idea, and Adler PDF 266 says a flute on the violins warms a rising line. The composer may correct the c054 comment.
- **Adler PDF 267–268 (flutes an octave above Vln I in a tutti).** At 105–108 and 113–116 the flutes sit at the violins' pitch. Raising them an octave would put B6 at 107 and C7 at 116. B6 is reserved for 336 and C7 for the finale. Keep.
