## Findings

No finding needs a change. Every principle in the table is followed or does not apply, or the score departs from it for a reason already recorded and accepted. Below are the points I tested against the reading and decided to keep, ranked by how close each came to a change.

**Considered and kept.**

1. **121: Hn 4's held B3 is sub. p, while the same note at 277 (Hn 3) is pp.** c208 made Hn 3 pp at 277 by PDF 136 (a fresh entry after 36 bars) and by PDF 133 (the basis under the melody's level). By PDF 133 the same note at 121 could be pp too. But PDF 133 sets the "inferior dynamic power" for woodwind against brass, where the timbres are close. At 121 the melody is in the violins and the basis in horns and clarinets, the pairing the same page calls the most dissimilar after voice and orchestra. At 121 Hn 4 also continues a phrase it was already playing, with Hn 2, the clarinets, Bsn 2 and the lower strings, all marked sub. p together after the ff tutti. A single horn a level under the group would split a sub. p that players read as one gesture. The violins' swell to a hidden mp at 122 already lifts the tune. Kept, as c208 judged.

2. **72: the ff A-major chord dims to nothing in one bar, and the horns carry it ff>pp into 73.** PDF 129's sf-dim method would let the strings attack a short ff chord while the winds hold. Here the strings hold a dotted minim ff>. The horns do hold the chord (A with C♯→C, protected c110). At 2. = 88 the bar is under a second, a "short" diminuendo that PDF 130 leaves to the players. c108, c208 and c288 weighed the same point and kept it. A short string chord would also take the violas' and cellos' C♯ away before the horns' C♯→C, which is the point of the join. Kept.

3. **Low brass f at 105 and 333, horns f at 71 and 299.** By PDF 136 these are "less successful" re-entries. Dan's soft-brass rule (BRIEF.md, c123) forbids ff and sfz brass attacks. A pp entry on a tutti downbeat would go against PDF 119–120 (a tutti belongs to f and ff). The f entries sit under ff strings and woodwinds, which is the basis-below-melody relation of PDF 133. Kept (c086, c123, c266, c303).

4. **Hn 1 p at 357 after 16 bars' rest.** PDF 136 prefers pp. Its accompaniment (Bsn 1, Vla, Vln II, Vc, Cb) is pp, so a pp horn would sit at the level of its own basis, which PDF 133 argues against. The pitches are the protected motto tail. Kept (c303).

5. **mf/mp re-entries of other instruments: Fl 1 and Ob 1 mf at 197, Fl 1 mf at 217, violins mp at 169, harp LH mf at 209.** PDF 136 extends the re-entry rule "more widely". Each of these sits one level under or over its neighbours in a graded layering:
   - 197 is the protected call-over-tune layering (c107, c117).
   - 217 is Fl 1's entry into T's climb, with a crescendo to f at 219 (c069).
   - 169 is one step in the trio's planned rise (p, mp, f, ff).
   - 209 doubles the arco basses under an f tutti.

   None of them is a colourless solo entry. Kept (c303, c274).

6. **193–197: strings and woodwind start the crescendo together, horns two bars later.** PDF 130's order is strings, woodwind, brass. Here the clarinets and Bsn 1 hold the harmony from the first bar of the phrase. If they entered at 195 they would come in mid-phrase on a held chord. Four bars is a medium crescendo, and the brass still enter last (pp< at 195). Kept.

7. **Harp harmonics p at 375, the last attack of the movement, among ppp and pp parts.** PDF 136 rules out mf and mp at the end, not p. Harmonics sound softer than their mark, so p is the usual dynamic for them to speak. They are protected (c116, c296). Kept.

8. **The coda's one-bar crescendo pp→mf at 328, after the strings enter pp one bar apart (325–328).** This is a short crescendo by natural means (PDF 130). The long crescendo 325–336 is made by orchestration in the order PDF 130 gives. This is do-not-touch since c108. Kept.
