## Findings

### 1. The E-minor cadence stroke at 131 rings through the silence of 132. Applied.
- **Bars.** 129–130 (B roll f), 131 (E2 f, the second idea's cadence in E minor, with the bass E1), 132 (the orchestra's last chord on beat 1, then two silent beats), 133 (pp tonic pedal, Cl/C.a./Bsn, c582's vii°7).
- **Problem.** The timpani part has `e,4 r4 r4 | R2.` with no damping sign. A forte stroke with hard sticks on a 30" drum rings for two to three seconds. The orchestra stops on beat 1 of 132, so beats 2–3 of 132 should be silent. In fact the drum still rings there, and it carries on into the pp entry at 133. The matching cadences are already written correctly. At 55–56 (G major) c506 wrote `g,2.\f~ | g,4-.`: the drum rings with the held chord and is damped with the orchestra. At 287 (the reprise) c416 damped the stroke for exactly this reason: "rings through the general silence of 288". The exposition's E-minor cadence was left out of both fixes, so in this one place of three the break is not silent.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80: the part must show "how long a note may ring". PDF 79: the timpani's role in "consummating climactic cadence points", so the stroke stays on the arrival. PDF 141: percussion must not cover detail; here the detail is the break and the soft wind chord after it.
- **Fix (applied).** sC.ily, sCTimp: `b,2.:32\f~ | b,2.:32 | e,4 r4 r4 | R2. |` → `b,2.:32\f~ | b,2.:32 | e,2.~ | e,4-. r4 r4 |`. This is c506's notation from 55–56. The tie means "do not restrike" and the dot on 132's crotchet means "damp". The stroke, its pitch and its dynamic are unchanged. Of the timpani changes this reading supports, this is the one a listener hears most: the two silent beats before the closing chord are now silent, as they are at 56 and 288.

### 2. The first stroke (bar 3) rings under the horn's D♯ over V. Applied.
- **Bars.** 3 (E2 pp, hard sticks, with the pizz. E; Hn 1 G♯3), 4 (Hn 1 E4, Bsn 2 B2), 5 (pizz. B, V; Hn 1 E4 falls to D♯4 on beat 3), 6 (D♯4 held), 7 (E minor).
- **Problem.** The stroke is written as a crotchet followed by five bars' rest, with no damping. c030 put the bass on V at 5–6, so that the tonic E arrives only with the E-minor chord at 7 and the call's D♯ is left over the dominant. A pp stroke on the low drum still sounds 1.4 s later at the downbeat of 5. In this texture (one horn, Vln I G♯5 ppp, Bsn 2 B2 pp) the drum's E2 is the only tonic note under the V chord. When the horn reaches D♯4 it sounds against that E. c100 kept the stroke itself, as the answer to movement II's last A♭2, and it stays. Only its length is wrong.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 58 (a timpani note outside the harmony is a fault), PDF 80 (how long a note may ring). This is the case covered by c416's rule ("strokes before … a change of harmony are damped unless meant to ring").
- **Fix (applied).** intro.ily, introTimp: `e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2.*5 |` → `e,2.\pp~ | e,2.~ | e,4-. r4 r4 | R2.*3 |`. The drum rings with the E-major chord through 3–4, under the horn's G♯3–E4. It is damped on the downbeat of 5, when the pizz. B brings V. The horn's D♯ then has nothing under it but the B, as c030 intended. Page 1 prints cleanly. Motive x at 9–12 and the label are unchanged.

### 3. The B at the end of motive x at 241 rings under the viola's tail alone. Applied.
- **Bars.** 241 (timpani B–B–B–B pp, medium sticks, under the violas' head E–B–B–E, pizz. E), 242 (violas alone: G4–F♯4–F4, the motto's tail), 243 (A1 bass, iv), 244 (C bass, the A–C–B approach to the Fr6, c593).
- **Problem.** The last B (beat 3 of 241) is undamped. It rings through 242, where the violas play the motto's chromatic tail alone. That puts a tritone under the F♮, which is the tail's characteristic note. It is still sounding at 243 over the new A bass. The tail is the movement's main cyclic quotation, and here it is a single line at pp.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80 and 58; c416's rule.
- **Fix (applied).** retr.ily, retrTimp: the first `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 |` → `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4-. |`. Motive x's notes, rhythm and dynamics at 241 and 247–251 are unchanged (do-not-touch for finale IV 143–145). Only the ring after the last stroke is cut. 247's B rings into the B pedal at 248 and stays as it is.

### 4. 287–288 written as 55–56 and 131–132. Applied.
- **Bars.** 285–286 (B roll f), 287 (E2, the reprise cadence), 288 (orchestra on beat 1, then silence), 289 (cellos pp, G minor).
- **Problem.** c416 damped 287 (`e,4-.`), so the drum stops on beat 2 of 287 while the orchestra holds its E-minor chord to 288. The silence at 288 and the fugato at 289 are right. But the drum drops out of its own cadence chord a bar early, and the three cadences now use three different notations.
- **Fix (applied).** dA.ily, dATimp: `b,2.:32 | e,4-. r4 r4 | R2. |` → `b,2.:32 | e,2.~ | e,4-. r4 r4 |`. The drum rings with the chord and is damped on beat 1 of 288 with the orchestra. c416's reason (no E under the G-minor entry at 289) still holds.

### Largest-change test
Finding 1 is the change a listener hears most: a forte drum ringing into a general pause is audible in any hall. I also weighed and rejected three larger ideas from this reading:
- Thinning the timpani in the reprise tutti 305–320 so that the coda's entry stands out, as Brahms 2/iv does (PDF 135). Rejected for c326/c416's reason: the low brass are already held back for the coda, the E3 octave gives the coda a timpani sound the tuttis do not have, and the strokes pair with the trumpets' motive x.
- A ppp *Fêtes*-style ostinato under the coda's T at 341–356 (PDF 126). Rejected: strings-only there (c414), and it would spend the closing motive x before 363.
- Pp strokes with the pizz. basses on each phrase of T at 153–168 (Bernstein, PDF 141). Rejected: "the less the better" (PDF 106). The strokes at 153 and 168 already mark the two phrase groups, and more strokes would weigh down the soft trio.
