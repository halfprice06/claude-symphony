## Holistic comparison with Schumann 1/iii

- **What the two movements share.** Schumann's scherzo opens forte and syncopated, comes between a slow movement and a finale it foreshadows, and ends its coda quietly on a reminiscence of trio material. This movement keeps the same frame:
  - a soft trio;
  - a coda that recalls T in single players (341–356);
  - a close that returns to the opening's colours (357–376, adler2 PDF 224).
  - Schumann's antiphonal Trio I, winds against strings, has its counterpart in the call and response at 29–36 and 185–192.
- **Where this movement differs, by design.**
  - It opens leggiero over pizzicato instead of forte. That is the Dvořák 9/iii model, and c259's protection holds.
  - It has one trio, not two. The length is protected.
  - It stays in 3/4 throughout; Schumann's Trio I changes to 2/4.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** For the scoring, yes, almost everywhere. Schumann is the classic case of a master whose high woodwinds are left holding loud notes that cannot decay. This score has corrected that habit at 223 (c479) and 72 (c079, oboes only), but bar 72 still asks the first flute's E6 and the first oboe's C♯6 for a ff-to-nothing diminuendo inside one bar. That bar is the hinge into the F-major episode. Finding 1 addresses it.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 72: Fl 1 (E6) and Ob 1 (C♯6) hold a ff dotted minim with a diminuendo to nothing (applied)

**Problem.** The fugato's stretto arrives ff on A major at 72 and decays in one bar into the F-major episode. There the horns enter pp on the common-tone pivot C♯5→C5 and A4 (Hn 1·2, 73), with Hn 3·4 and the harp. The top of the decaying chord is:
- Vln I E6, Fl 1 E6;
- Ob 1 C♯6, Cl 1 C♯6, Vln II C♯6;
- Fl 2 A5, Ob 2 A5.

Every one of these carries `2.->\ff\>`. A flute's third-octave E6 and an oboe above C6 cannot make that diminuendo in 0.7 s; they are the last tones to fade. So the chord's top stays bright while the horns' soft pivot enters beneath it.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 213 (Dvořák, Slavonic Dance 8 m. 33, score PDF 212): in the model ff-to-p diminuendo "the piccolo part … has been written an octave lower than before to enable the player to accomplish the diminuendo to piano; all other instruments are also in controllable ranges." PDF 212 shows each wind striking ff and dropping to p within the bar.
- PDF 224 (Ravel): the transcriber must notate "decay and cessation of a pitch".
- This score already applied the same reasoning to the same note at 223 (c479: Fl 1 releases T's last E6 after two beats). c079 moved the oboes at 72 down from E6 but left Fl 1 and Ob 1 holding.

**Fix (sB.ily).** Fl 1 and Ob 1 strike the arrival as an accented crotchet ff and rest for beats 2–3. The diminuendo is carried by the instruments that can make it: Vln I/II (E6, C♯6), Cl 1 (C♯6; the clarinet is the woodwind that fades best), Fl 2 and Ob 2 (A5), and the horns. The chord's top pitch and every chord tone stay in the bar, and the colour at the moment of impact is unchanged.

### 2. 13–28: a forte, syncopated opening after Schumann (considered, not proposed)

Brahms raised the orchestral dynamics above the piano's (PDF 205), and Schumann opens forte. Both would argue for a louder first statement. c259 protects the leggiero opening, and the four-stage build of theme A (13, 105, 253, 305) depends on it. The reading gives no reason stronger than c259's.

### 3. 89–100: rhythmising the dominant pedal in a horn, after Bizet (considered, not proposed)

Bizet's third horn rhythmises the pedal (PDF 213). c244, c559 and c576 rule out any sustained pedal and any brass before 101, and say not to propose it again. A rhythmised horn would also put brass in before 101.
