## What else I studied

Brahms 1/iii is not in the local corpus. I used my knowledge of the score and checked its form against https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Brahms): A♭ major, 2/4; a stepwise clarinet melody whose four-bar figure is stretched to five bars by a short string bridge; the later phrase extended to seven bars by a chromatic bridge; a trio in B major in 6/8, introduced by flute, oboe and bassoon; a shortened, varied return of A; and a coda that recalls the trio. From the score I remember the opening texture: clarinet over pizzicato cellos and a held horn note, and a quiet, transparent scoring without trumpets or timpani. I did not re-check the tacet instruments against a printed score. I looked at every page of v199's score.pdf at 80 dpi, read every sections/*.ily file and global.ily, and diffed v189 against v199 file by file with comments removed. The changed files were coda, dC, link, sC, tA, tB and score.ly. I checked each change against the HISTORY entries for cycles 189–198. I scanned score.midi for flute notes below E5.

**Holistic verdict.** Brahms 1/iii is an intermezzo, not a scherzo, so it is a model for the trio and the soft passages, not for the tuttis. On that ground the score holds up well. The trio's first statement (153–168), a dolce clarinet tune over plucked bass notes and a held horn fifth, has the same texture as Brahms's opening. As in Brahms, the accompaniment stays a level below the tune and out of its register. The return of the scherzo is shortened and varied (253–324 against 13–140), as Brahms shortens his A3–B2–A4, and the coda recalls the trio and dies away. A conductor would take the trio, the soft codetta and the closing page for professional work. Brahms is ahead in phrase rhythm. His tune breathes in five- and seven-bar phrases, while nearly all of this score moves in four-bar groups. The exceptions are the ritmo di tre battute at 89–100 and the hemiola cadences. I considered a one-bar Brahmsian extension in the trio (an echo bar after 160 or 176). I am not proposing it: it changes the length of a protected large form (c133, c163, c183) and the trio tune's record, and the three-bar groups at 89–100 already give the movement one irregular passage. The single place where this score loses the restraint that Brahms and Dan both ask of brass is the horns at the coda peak (Finding 1).

**Against v189.** I found nothing that got worse. The ten changes since v189 hold up. The tenor countermelody now resolves A3→G3 at 116→117 and 316→317 (c189). Bassoons replace the clarinets in the link pad at 145–148 (c190), and the result is the series spacing in row 6. The cadential 6/4 is in the cellos at 181 (c192). Tbn 2 has B2 at 333–334 (c194). ## Findings

### 1. Coda 335–338: the horns crescendo again and re-attack at fff on the peak (applied)

**Problem.** The four horns reach ff at 333 and hold the chord. At 335 they have a second crescendo, and at 336 they start a new slur with a tenuto re-attack marked fff on the same pitches: Hn 1 B4, Hn 2 F♯4, Hn 3 F♯4, Hn 4 B3. This is the only brass fff in the movement. In v189 the trumpets also went to fff here. c194 kept them at f, citing Dan's rule. The low brass rest at 336–338 and return f at 339 (c169). The horns alone are left with a loud re-attack. Hn 1's B4 (written F♯5) is the register where the horn turns brassy at fff.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 275, quoting Fineberg: brass instruments at a loud dynamic "tend to have dissonant upper partials as the loudest components of their spectrum". That is the "bite" Dan wants kept out (BRIEF.md; c123). Casanelles PDF 271–272: the written dynamic should state the result. The intended result at 336 is the strings' and woodwinds' B6 on top, with brass weight underneath, and for that the horns do not need fff. PDF 272: instruments differ in strength at one marking, and four horns at ff in their middle register carry weight in a fff tutti. The score already applies this rule to the trumpets at 105–120 and 335–338 and to the low brass at 333 and 339.

**Fix (applied).** In coda.ily, remove the crescendo at 335 and the fff at 336 from all four horns. The horns stay ff from 333 through 338, and the diminuendo at 339 is unchanged:
