## Findings

### 1. Bars 253-268: the tenor countermelody returns without the horn colour it was born with (applied)

**Problem.** The tutti at 105-120 introduces a tenor countermelody (G3-B3-E4, B3-A3-F♯3 …) in Hn 3·4 a2 espress. with the cellos and Bsn 1. At the reprise 253-268, c359 brings the same line back pp in the cellos alone. At 305-320 it returns ff in Hn 3, Bsn 1 and the cellos.

The middle statement is the only one without the horn, so the soft return does not sound like the line the listener heard at 105. A pp cello line in the tenor, under a mp violin theme, a p flute descant two octaves up and pizzicato off-beats, is also the weakest layer of that texture.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 113: strings and brass are seldom combined, with one named exception: "the splendid effect of horns doubled by divided violas or 'cellos". The score already uses this pairing for this exact line at 105 and 305, and for T at 169-176 (c431).
- Rimsky PDF 112: in every chord the parts in octaves (and unisons) strengthen one another.
- Rimsky PDF 108, with Ex. 152 (PDF 324) and Ex. 157 (PDF 330): sustained horn tones are the binding layer under moving upper parts. At 253 the moving upper parts are Vln I, Fl 1 and the pizz. off-beats.

**Fix (applied, dA.ily, `dAHnThree`).** Hn 3 plays the cellos' line in unison, ppp dolce, with the cellos' slurs:
```
dAHnThree = { R2.*24 | b2.--(\pp ...
→ dAHnThree = { g2.(\ppp^\markup \italic "dolce" | b2 e'4~ | e'2 b4 | a2 fis4 | g2.) | a2.( | c'4 b4 a4 | g2.) | c'2.( | b2. | a2 c'4 | b2 a4) | g2( e'4~ | e'2 g4 | a2 fis4 | g4) r4 r4 | R2.*8 | b2.--(\pp ...
```
- **Dynamic.** ppp puts the horn a level under the cellos' pp. This follows the movement's rule that a wind doubling a string line prints a level under it (c405), Hn 3 under the cellos at 169 (c431), and Rimsky PDF 112's grading of brass a degree below woodwind.
- **Register.** The horn sounds G3-E4 (written D4-B4), its middle register. It ends at 268 and still has 8 bars' rest before its pp B3 at 277.
- **Cue.** The Hn 3 part rests 241-252, so I added a Vln. I cue at 249-252 under an 8va sign to parts-cues.json: `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",249,4,"8va"]`. Vln I plays motive x there.

**Answer to c161 and c156.**
- c161 said the horns were "saved for 277-288 and the tutti at 299". That is no longer true. Hn 1 sounds at 241-244, nine bars before 253. Hn 1·2 enter at 273, before 277.
- c161 also said a horn would make the reprise "heavier". One horn at ppp in its middle register is a lighter addition than any other wind on that line would be. The theme stays staccato over pizzicato off-beats, so c238's "leggiero theme over pizz." still holds.

### Considered and kept (my decisions)

- **105 and 305, Hn 1 F♯4→G4 against Hn 4 F♯3→G3.** These are octaves inside the tutti, created by c424. Hn 4 has the countermelody's first note, and Hn 1's G4 is the brass chord's third that c424 added. Rimsky PDF 112 says octave doublings strengthen one another, and PDF 114 says arrival chords matter less. No change.
- **130, Cl 2 D♯4 inside Hn 2 B3 / Hn 3 G4** (since c442 re-timed the hemiola). This is one bar at f, and Rimsky lists clarinets within horns as a valid enclosure (Ex. 162, PDF 110). Moving it would undo c442's voice-leading. No change.
- **284, Hn 2 B3 between Bsn 2 A♯3 and Bsn 1 C♯4.** This lasts one quaver, inside Hn 2's doubling of the tune. It is a melodic passage, not a chord layout (PDF 112). No change.
- **101-103, clarinets under the trumpets' B4/F♯4.** The diagrams on PDF 111 put the woodwind harmony above the trumpets. Here the flutes, oboes and cor anglais already lie above, and Cl 1 doubles the C.a. a third lower in the moving line. Ex. 162-163 sanction clarinets within the brass, and c408 protects the build order. No change.
- **Bsn 1 also on 253-268 (PDF 112: Fag + cellos).** I rejected this as the alternative to finding 1. A bassoon in unison with the cellos blends almost without trace, so it adds little that a listener would hear, and it would spend Bn 1's fresh "scherz." entry at 269.
