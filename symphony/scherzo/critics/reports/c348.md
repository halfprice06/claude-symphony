## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 161–163, 177–179, 217–219, 349–351: the climax of the trio tune peaks on the tie, not on the attack (applied)

**Problem.** In the third phrase of T the high A is attacked on beat 3 and tied over the bar line: `e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~( | a'''4.\ff\> gis'''8 fis'''4`. This note is the trio's high point: A6 is first heard here at 218 (c344), and it is the tune's highest note in every statement. The crescendo from 217 is marked to reach ff on the downbeat of 219, the tied continuation. The diminuendo also starts on that downbeat. So the printed goal of the crescendo falls on a beat where the violins play no new note. Fl 1 (f at 219) and the hidden levels of Cl 1 at 219, Cl 1 at 163, cellos at 179 and Vln I/II/Cl 1 at 351 do the same.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 51: a note begun on a weak beat and held through the strong one is the strongest syncopation, "because the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation". The emphasis belongs to the attack.
- Ex. 3-6 (Beethoven Op. 69/iii), checked on the page image: Beethoven writes *sf* under the attack on beat 3 of the tied A, not under the tied downbeat. The left hand marks the downbeats with chords. This score has the same layout at 219: the bassoons, horns, cellos, basses and violas all attack the new chord on the downbeat, and the tune holds its A over it.
- PDF 50: dissonant and very high notes carry their own accent. At 218.3 the A is a fourth over the E chord (an anticipation, PDF 56) and the highest note of the trio. On 219.1 it becomes a consonant chord tone of the next harmony. A swell that peaks on the continuation lands on the consonant, unattacked moment and passes over both built-in accents.
- PDF 55 sets out the difference: in a suspension the dissonance falls on the strong beat, so swelling into the tied downbeat is right there. I left the suspensions at 155–156 and 165–166 as they are (Reading notes, row 13). The A at 162/218 is not a suspension, because it is dissonant at its attack.
- This does not undo c248's "keep" for the tied A. The swell from 161/217 into the tie is kept. Only its goal moves one beat earlier, to the attack, and the diminuendo starts on the next downbeat as before.

**Fix (applied).** In each statement the dynamic moves from the continuation to the attack:
- tC.ily, Vln I: `e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~\ff( | a'''4.\> gis'''8 fis'''4`; Vln II likewise an octave lower.
- tC.ily, Fl 1: `a'''4~\f | a'''4.\>`; Cl 1: `a''4~-\tweak stencil ##f \ff | a''4.\>`.
- tA.ily, Cl 1: `a''4~-\tweak stencil ##f \mp | a''4.\>`; cellos: `a'4~-\tweak stencil ##f \mf( | a'4.\>`.
- coda.ily, Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1: `a'''4~-\tweak stencil ##f \p( | a'''4.\>` (and the matching lower lines).

The printed ff and f on page 8 now stand under beat 3 of 218. The hairpins on either side engrave cleanly. Notes and rhythm are unchanged, and the theme record reads 52/52.

### 2. 104 → 105 and 304 → 305: the timpani peak on the weak bar and drop on the arrival (applied)

**Problem.** In 101–104 (and 301–304) the timpani roll p < and then play motive x on B at **ff** in 104. At 105 (and 305), the tutti return of theme A, they are marked **f** on the E. The MIDI shows the drop: v89 at 104, then v84 at 105. The trumpets and trombones cross the same bar line at one level, f (v83 at both). So the drum's loudest stroke of the build is on the fourth, weak bar of the group, and the drum gets softer on the strong first bar of the next group, where the theme returns.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 53: in a four-bar group the first and third bars are strong and the second and fourth weak. 105 begins the tutti's first hypermeasure; 104 ends the hemiola group.
- PDF 51: an attack stronger than its surroundings is an accent. A ff motive x on 104 followed by an f stroke on 105 accents the weak bar against the hypermetre that the whole build is aimed at.
- PDF 54: 104 is the half-cadence bar (V7) and 105 the goal. The goal should not be the quieter of the two.
- The score's own rules. c056 set the timpani to f in the tutti for balance (Piston: a ff timpani covers the orchestra), so f is the drum's tutti level at 105, 305 and 333. c128 brought the trumpets and trombones in 101–104 to f, not ff, "so the brass do not drop a level on the arrival". The timpani were left out of c128 and are the one part that still drops at 105. This fix keeps c056's balance at 105 and applies c128's reason to the drum.

**Fix (applied).** sB.ily and dB.ily, `sBTimp`/`dBTimp` last bar: `b,4\f b,8 b,8 b,4` (was `\ff`). The roll's hairpin from 101 now ends at f. sC.ily and dC.ily: `e,4-- r4 r4` at 105/305, without the restated `\f` (one dynamic per change, as c128/c165 did for the brass). MIDI: 104 and 105 are now both v84. A `% c348:` comment is added at the top of sB.ily.
