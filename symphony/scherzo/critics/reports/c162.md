## Findings

### 1. Bars 88–89: the A♯°7 never reaches its goal (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 88 is the end of the F-major episode. It sounds a full °7 on ♯4 in E minor: bass clarinet A♯2, horn 4 E3, bassoon 1 G3, horn 3 C♯4. This is the chord c072 asked for. At 89 all four players stop together, and only the violas' B–E remains. The chord with the strongest pull in the passage, ♯4 pulling to 5, resolves to nothing. The bar that should confirm the dominant region before the timpani's motive x is an open fourth.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 555, §14 and Ex. 30-22: the °7 on ♯4 normally goes to the cadential 6/4. The bass rises a half step to 5, the 7th of the chord (here G) stays as the common tone, and the other voices move by step to the root and fifth of the tonic. PDF 561–562 (Ex. 31-14 and 31-15): when ♮4 is followed by ♯4 (here F-major harmony, then A♯), the °7 is a passing step to V, and the V must be heard. PDF 530 makes the general point that a chromatic chord gets its meaning from where it goes. The chord dump showed bar 89 as only B and E before the fix. It now shows B1: E G B, a complete i6/4 over B.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-162/work/sections/sB.ily).**

```lilypond
% Bass clarinet: A#2 rises to B2
... a,2. | ais,2. | b,2 r4 | R2.*15 | }          % was: ais,2. | R2.*16
% Bassoon 1: holds the 7th G3 as the common tone
... g2.~ | g2 r4 | \partCombineAutomatic R2. ... % was: g2. | \partCombineAutomatic R2. (one R2. absorbed)
% Horn 3: C#4 falls to B3, slur closed on the resolution
... cis'2.-- | b2) r4 | ...                      % was: cis'2.--) | R2.
% Horn 4: holds E3, slur closed on the resolution
... e2.-- | e2) r4 | ...                         % was: e2.--) | R2.
```

All four parts play minims at pp, with no new dynamics. The shared staves keep equal dynamics, and the bassoon stays under `\partCombineSoloI` until the rest. The slurs now close on the resolution note. I added a comment to sB.ily with the citations.

**Protected passages answered.**

- c083's do-not-touch on the F-major episode exit (84–89): its stated reason was the key plan F (Neapolitan) → V. The key plan is unchanged. The Neapolitan sixth at 84–86 and the A♯°7 at 88 are untouched. Only the missing resolution is added, and it is the V region the plan calls for.
- c097 on timpani motive x with pizzicato at 89–100: the winds sound for two beats of 89 at pp, are gone by beat 3, and do not touch 90–100. The timpani and pizzicato stand alone from 90, as before.

**Cyclic rules.** The new sonority is E–G–B, so there is no C-major triad and no stop chord. It adds no trumpet, no tremolo and no new tempo word.

No other finding. Every other chromatic chord and every other progression I checked follows the reading (table rows 1–28). I make no further changes. I do not invent problems where the score already does what Units 30–31 teach.
