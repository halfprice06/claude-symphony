## Findings

There are no findings that need a score change. The two Neapolitan events and the one augmented sixth follow the reading point for point. Below are the options I considered, ranked by how close they came to being worth applying, with the reason for keeping each passage as it is.

### Considered 1: bars 125–126, N6/4 in place of iv6–V♭9 (rejected)

- **Option.** The second idea goes iv (125) to iv6 (125 beat 3) to V♭9 (126). Changing the inner E to F in Cl 2, Vla, Bsn 1 and Hn 2 on beat 3 of 125 would make C–F–A, an N6/4 with C as the upper neighbour of B. That matches A&S 489–490 (Ex. 28-15), and it reaches ♭II from iv by 5–6 as in Ex. 28-7 (PDF 485–486). F♯6 in the soprano of 126 would then sound against the ♭2̂ just heard, as in Ex. 28-6.
- **Reasons not to.**
  - A&S say ♭II is a special colour for climaxes and fermatas (PDF 483). The movement already has two: the tonicized F at 73–86 and the N6/4 at the coda climax, 331. A third at 125, and its reprise at 281–282, would take away the singularity of 331, where c080 put the chord on purpose.
  - The change would alter four parts in two places and break c017's viola line.
  - The existing iv6 to V♭9, with C held as a common tone, already gives a falling semitone C→B in the bass. That is the voice-leading point of Ex. 28-15, made with a diatonic chord.

### Considered 2: bars 297–301, augmented sixth before the reprise's V (rejected for the third time)

- **Option.** A&S PDF 516 (Ex. 29-28) shows tonicized IV, then an augmented sixth, then V. 297–300 is an A-minor-to-A-major area before B7 at 301, so the plan seems to invite one.
- **Reasons not to.**
  - Only beat 3 of 300 is free.
  - A French sixth there would pre-empt 244.
  - In 297–300, A major is heard as V of D, so the augmented sixth would follow a dominant function rather than a predominant one.
  - A&S present the augmented sixth as a marker of one structural point (PDF 503). c062 and c152 reached the same result.

### Considered 3: bars 201–203, Italian sixth in the trio (rejected)

- **Option.** 202 (A minor, iv of the trio's E) to 203 (B) is the Phrygian iv–V that A&S 503–504 derive the augmented sixth from.
- **Reasons not to.**
  - The trio is in E major, where the reading treats this colour as borrowed from minor.
  - The scherzo's return should keep the augmented-sixth sound to itself (c062).
  - The It6 F–A–D♯ would also put F against the trio tune's D♯ figure at 199.

### Considered 4: bar 199 (blocked)

- **Option.** F♯–A–C♯–D♯ could be respelled or made into a French sixth leading to 200.
- **Reason not to.** The D♯ is a note of the recorded trio tune T, and the chord's function as ii7 of E is correct as written.

### Considered 5: codetta 235 (rejected)

- **Option.** An augmented sixth or ♭II neighbour of the final tonic, as in A&S PDF 522 (Ex. 29-36).
- **Reason not to.** Under the E pedal it needs F, which makes a minor ninth against E in the bass. A&S 522 places the chord over a moving bass, not a pedal.

### Considered 6: bar 331, doubling (kept)

- **Question.** The N6/4 has F in five parts, against A&S's advice to double the bass of ♭II6 (PDF 484).
- **Reasons to keep.**
  - That advice is for ♭II6. In the 6/4 the bass C is a neighbour, and the fifth-doubling is acceptable at a climax.
  - Every F falls to D♯ or B (PDF 484, 489), and there are no parallels.
  - analysis.txt lists no outer-voice parallels at 331–332.

### Considered 7: bars 357–360, plagal close (kept)

- **Question.** E – A♯°7 – A minor – E. Should a ♭II neighbour (Ex. 28-20, PDF 495–496) or a ♭2̂ augmented sixth (Ex. 29-36) replace the A♯°7?
- **Reasons to keep.**
  - The plan fixes a plagal close with the motto's tail in Hn 1 (B–A♯–A–G♯).
  - The current chromatic lines are all spelled as A&S PDF 497–499 require: Vla E–C♯–C–B and Bsn 1 G♯–C♯–C–B falling with lowered notes, and Hn 1 descending through A♯ as a passing note to A.
  - An F would clash with the tail.
