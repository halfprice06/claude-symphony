## What else I studied

- **Beat scan (bars 1–376).** I checked the whole movement against rows 1–17 with beats.py and six3.py. There is no 6/3 run anywhere: 81 beats sound as 6/3 chords, all single and each with a function from Ex. 18-19. This is the fifth pass to find this. One reason the scherzo cannot use more 6/3 colour, which no earlier report states: in E minor the diatonic 6/3 over the tonic bass is E–G–C, a C-major triad. Any descending 6/3 run in E minor, or in F major (whose V is C), passes through E–G–C, so the cyclic rule bars 6/3 runs from every minor-key and F-major join. E major and B major are the only key areas where Unit 18's techniques are free.

## Findings

### 1. Link 141–148: the E-major pad gets one 5–6–5 neighbour at 146. APPLIED

**Problem.** From the second idea's cadence at 131 to the trio at 153 the bass is E for 22 bars. In the link, 141–148 is one wind chord held for eight bars: B. cl E2, Bsn 2 B3, Bsn 1 E4 (and Bsn 1 B3 before 145), under Hn 1's G4, then G♯4. The only change in the link is Hn 1's G→G♯ at 145. That turn is the movement's pivot and must stay the main event. But after it, 145–148 repeats the bare major triad for four bars, and the listener has heard the same chord since 133.

**Evidence.** Unit 18 §8 (PDF 299–300, Ex. 18-14): over a sustained tonic bass, a 6th can decorate the 5th, I(5–6–5). The chord keeps tonic function; Aldwell and Schachter print "not VI6!" under it. Ex. 18-19a and j/k (PDF 302) list the neighbouring 6/3 and "6 displacing 5". §1 (PDF 290–291) says the 6/3's "more fluid, less stable sound" suits transitional passages, and the link is the scherzo's transition into the trio. Dvořák colours the same kind of E-pedal link in 9/iii with VI6/5 and VI6 over E (bars 201–208, 231–234). He uses the 6/3 to modulate to C; here the 6th returns to the 5th. E major is one of the few places in this movement where a 6/3 over the tonic bass is allowed: E–G♯–C♯ is not a C-major triad.

**Fix (applied).** sections/link.ily, linkBsnTwo:
- old: `R2.*4 | b2.--(\pp | b2.-- | b2.--\> | b2--) r4\! | R2.*4 |`
- new: `R2.*4 | b2.--(\pp | cis'2.-- | b2.--\> | b2--) r4\! | R2.*4 |`

Bar 146 now sounds E2 – C♯4 – E4 – G♯4. Bars 145, 147 and 148 are as before (beats.py). The C♯ sits under Hn 1's held G♯ during its crescendo, and Hn 1's G♯–A–G♯ at 147–148 (T's first two notes, c051) still sounds over the plain triad, as T itself does at 153. The bass, Hn 1's line, the pivot G♯ and every theme are unchanged. Bsn 2 moves B3–C♯4–B3, a step each way, with no parallels against any voice. The C♯ also anticipates the trio's C♯-minor middle (185) and the IV colour of the codetta and the close (227, 231, 359). I added a comment above the variable.

Why it does not undo earlier decisions: c190 moved this pad to the bassoons to keep the clarinet colour fresh for 153; that still holds. c288's four-bar slurs and tenuto are kept. c323 kept 145–148 because "a moving series under it would take the attention from the pivot". A single neighbour inside one slur, after the pivot has sounded, is not a series and does not touch the G→G♯ at 145.

### 2. The F-major episode 81–84 as a 6/3 run (Ex. 18-3). TESTED IN THOUGHT, REJECTED

**Considered.** Bars 81–84 are vi–IV–ii–I6 in F (bass D–B♭–G–A, root position). Ex. 18-3 shows the same falling 3rds hidden in a stepwise 6/3 run that ends on a cadential 6/3, and the episode does end on a 6/3 (F6/A = ♭II6 of E, 84–86). A bass F–D–B♭–A under 6/3 chords would match the reading closely.

**Why not.** (a) The diatonic passing tone between F and D in that run is E, and its 6/3 is E–G–C, a C-major triad. (b) With the bass falling D2→B♭1 at 82→83, Cl 1's D5→B♭4 (82 last quaver → 83 downbeat) makes octaves with it. (c) c202 protects the falling-3rds harmony at 81–84 (A&S Unit 11, PDF 163–164: VI moves to IV and II by falling 3rds), and c112's common-tone dyad depends on it. The reading gives no stronger reason. **Decision:** no change.

### 3. The E-minor half of the scherzo-to-trio join (133–144). KEPT, with a reason not recorded before

**Considered.** The same 5–6 colour in the minor half, where the pad is E–B–G for 141–144 and the winds hold E minor at 133–136.

**Why not.** In E minor, 5–6 over E gives E–G–C, a C-major triad, which the cyclic rule forbids. A chromatic C♯ would give E–G–C♯, a diminished 6/3 with no function in E minor, and it would blur the G♮ that Hn 1 must sound plainly before the G♯ at 145. So the minor half stays a plain 5/3 pedal, and Finding 1 puts the only colour in the major half. **Decision:** no change. I suggest recording this reason (the C-major rule rules out Unit 18's 6/3 colour over E minor) in HISTORY so later passes do not test it again.

### 4. The coda's second stage opens on a harp 6/3 (341–344). KEPT

c323 Finding 4 still holds. The pizz. A1 alone on each downbeat after the G.P. is heard as the root (§8, PDF 299), and the phrase is the IV of an I–IV–I neighbour prolongation (c403). The more fluid 6/3 sound suits that transitional role (§1, PDF 290). **Decision:** no change.

### 5. Coda 329–332, Phrygian bass without iv6 and its 7–6. KEPT

The bass E–D–C–B is Ex. 18-12's 1–♭7–♭6–5 (PDF 298–299), but the chord over C is ♭II6/4 (c081), not iv6 with a 7–6. The book calls the 7–6 "almost invariable" only for iv6. The recorded reason, that finale IV 272 takes this F back, is a cross-movement protection, and the reading gives nothing that outweighs it. **Decision:** no change.

### 6. Proportions and length. KEPT

The scherzo takes 87 s, the trio 70 s, the reprise 49 s and the coda 44 s. Sections get shorter toward the end, the reprise leaves out the F-major episode, and the slow coda stage holds the cyclic close. The longest static stretch, 131–152 (about 16 s with the rit.), is shorter than Dvořák's 34-bar E-pedal link in 9/iii. After Finding 1 it has a harmonic event in each half: the G→G♯ turn and the 5–6–5. At 4:38 the movement is a light middle weight between the Adagio and the finale. Unit 18 says nothing about large proportions. Its rule that a transition is articulated only at its two ends (PDF 291) is met by every pedal join. **Decision:** no change.
