## Findings

### 1. Bars 301–320: the trumpets are not held back for the coda (applied)
- **Problem:** The low brass have been saved since c033: they play 101–120 and come back only at 332–339. The trumpets are not. They play 101–120, again 301–320, and again 333–339. So at the coda's arrival (333) the trumpets are the same colour the listener heard 28 bars earlier, and only the low brass are new. The reprise tutti at 305 and the coda's 333 therefore differ only by the trombones and tuba.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 37: "One of the most successful ways of building climaxes is by holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event." Schubert uses the trombones at the close of the exposition and "again to close the recapitulation and lead to the coda". Here the trumpets play the same two places, 101–120 and the coda, but also play the reprise. Adler PDF 38 (Franck) shows a climax reached "not by the brass but by the rest of the orchestra plus horns in their woodwind capacity". Without trumpets, the reprise tutti is that kind of climax: strings, woodwinds in three octaves, four horns and timpani at ff. Adler PDF 38–40 (Tchaikovsky): the larger tutti comes after the colour has been kept out. With this change, the whole brass section comes back together at 333 after 212 bars, under the movement's only fff at 336.
- **Fix (applied):** dB.ily `dBTptOne = { R2.*16 | }`, `dBTptTwo = { R2.*16 | }` (were motive x pp< at 301 and 303 and a held B4/F♯4 f at 304). dC.ily `dCTptOne = { R2.*20 | }`, `dCTptTwo = { R2.*20 | }` (were motive x f on odd bars 305–319). The coda's trumpet entry stays as it is (`b'4-.\f b'8-. b'-. b'4-.` at 333). The build into 305 keeps c408's order: strings sfp<, woodwinds p<, horns p< at 301. The horns still give the brass chord its third (Hn 1 G4 on tonic bars, c424), and Tpt 1's B4 at 304 doubled the horns and woodwinds. Motive x stays in the timpani at 305–320 and in theme A itself. Nothing quoted by another movement changes: the finale quotes the timpani, not the trumpets.
- **Why it is not worse:** The exposition tutti (105) keeps full brass. The reprise tutti (305) is one colour lighter. The coda (333–339) is the full brass again plus fff. That is the order Schubert's trombones follow. The reprise tutti loses some brightness on its odd bars, but that brightness is what makes 333 a new event, and 336 is meant to be the movement's single peak (c028).

### 2. Bar 332: the trumpets could sneak in pp< with the low brass (not applied)
- **Question:** In Ex. XI-19 the saved colour enters pp, and the low brass do that at 332 (pp<). Should the returning trumpets do the same with motive x on B?
- **Decision:** No. The low brass already enter softly at 332, so the colour is introduced the Schubert way. If the trumpets also entered one bar early, the new colour would arrive inside the crescendo and not at 333, the moment that Adler says the colour should mark (PDF 37: "adding clarity to the moment"). Their f motive x on 333's downbeat is a tutti rhythm, not an accented attack, so Dan's rule is kept.

### 3. Bars 336–338: horns held ff without Tchaikovsky's > on each held note; low brass rest (no change)
- **Question:** Tchaikovsky's held brass notes (PDF 39–40) carry fff>.
- **Decision:** Keep, for c328's reasons. A > after a loud brass attack sounds as an fp, which is the bite that Dan's rule forbids. The low brass rest under the B6 (c034) and return on 339 as the long note under the short stroke (Ex. XV-35). Finding 1 does not touch these bars.

### 4. Bar 209: Franck's "f molto sostenuto" for the climactic return of T (no change)
- **Question:** Franck marks the return of his tune *f molto sostenuto* and brings the brass in a degree lower. Should T's return at 209 change from "espress." to "sostenuto"?
- **Decision:** Keep "espress.". The levels already match Franck: strings f, Hn 1·2 mp, Cl 1 mf. The broadening that "sostenuto" asks for is already written in: c418 bowed the climax so that each swell is an up-bow and the A6 at 218 a down-bow, and the tied suspensions hold each phrase back. Players read "sostenuto" as a tempo word. It would need a MIDI step and an "a tempo" before the codetta at 225, which has its own poco rit. at 237.

### 5. Bars 120→121 and 276→277: "sub. p" without overlap (no change)
- **Question:** Ex. XV-36 says the soft group should enter under the loud chord before it ends.
- **Decision:** Keep. Adler's technique applies to a held loud chord. At 120 and 276 the tutti ends on a crotchet followed by rests, and the second idea starts on a new chord, so nothing could be overlapped. At 72→73, the one place with a held ff chord, the overlap is already written (Hn 1·2 ff> tied into the pp F-major group).
