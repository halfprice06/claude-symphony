## Findings

### 1. Coda 345–348: the harmonised fragment of T still has the half cadence that T lost in c421. Applied.

**Problem.** Since c421, T's bars 7–8 suspend the leading tone over the bar line (E–D♯–A♯ tied, A♯–B) in every statement: Cl 1 at 159–160, cellos at 175–176, Cl 1/Vln I/Vln II at 215–216. That tied leading tone and the tied A in bars 3–4 are now the tune's two expressive points, and the listener has heard both three times by the coda. In the coda, Ob 1 and Cl 1 play bars 5–8 in A major, in sixths. This is the only time T is harmonised by a wind pair, and its last appearance before the solo violin (c381). Here bar 7 still has the old form: Ob 1 `a''4. gis''8 fis''4 | dis''4 e''2`, with an unprepared D♯ on the downbeat. So the most intimate statement of the tune is the one that drops the gesture the trio taught. c421 kept it for one reason only: Bsn 1's E3 on beat 3 of 347 would sound the note of resolution under a struck D♯5. That reason can be met by changing one bassoon note.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 212–213: clarinets (and paired winds) may play "two or more completely independent parts in different registers". Ex. VII-63 (Hebrides, rendered): the clarinet pair moves in thirds and sixths, and the tied notes pass from one player to the other over the bar lines. Ex. VII-64 (Mozart 39 Trio, rendered): the trio's tune has its tied note over the bar line in every phrase, kept identical at each return. Adler PDF 198: the oboe is "basically a lyrical instrument". D♯5 and E5 are in its "warm, prominent" range, and the pair's suspension sits there and in the clarinet's clarino (written C5 A4 → B4; the F♯4 is the inner-voice throat note that c401 already accepted at 345). Aldwell & Schachter Unit 21 (PDF 352, cited by c421): do not sound the resolution in another voice before the suspension resolves.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
- `codaOboeOne`, 347–348: `a''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p gis''8 fis''4 | dis''4 e''2)` → `a''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p gis''8 dis''4~ | dis''4 e''2)`.
- `codaClOne`, 347–348: `cis''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p b'8 a'4 | fis'4 gis'2)` → `cis''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p b'8 fis'4~ | fis'4 gis'2)`. This keeps the sixth with the oboe.
- `codaBsnOne`, 347–348: `dis2-\tweak stencil ##f \p e4 | gis2 b4)` → `dis2-\tweak stencil ##f \p fis4 | e2 gis4)`.

Over the B7 of 347 the pair now prepares D♯/F♯ on beat 3. They hold it over the E bass (basses and harp E on 348) as a 7–8 and 9–10 in sixths, and resolve together on beat 2 with the harp's E-major chord. The bassoon's F♯3 on beat 3 of 347 is a chord tone. Its E3 on 348's downbeat doubles the bass, as in the trio statements, where the bass carries the resolving note under the suspension. It no longer sounds G♯3, the clarinet's resolution, under the held F♯4. The bassoon line still rises (A–B–D–D♯–F♯) and hands over to the cellos' E3 at 349. Nothing else in 345–348 changes: dynamics, slurs and the harp off-beats (F♯3–E4, c390) are the same.

### 2. T's first statement in Cl 1 (153–168). Kept.
T is in the clarino except for the bar-3 quaver F♯4 (written A4, throat; PDF 210). The accompaniment is pp and lies below the tune (harp G♯3–E4, Hn 3·4 drone), so the clarinet is not covered (PDF 185). Adler's Mozart 39 model (PDF 213) suggests a Cl 2 arpeggio under it. c401 rejected that because the harp already supplies the moving accompaniment, and I agree. With finding 1, the first and last wind statements of T now carry the same suspension.

### 3. Cl 1 dolce 81–88 on the A clarinet. Kept.
Concert G4, F4, F♯4 and E4 (83, 85, 87, 88) are throat tones on the A clarinet, and each second note crosses the break (PDF 210). I checked whether anything covers these weak notes (PDF 185). Harp RH chords at 81–84 lie a third or more below the line, and harp and horns are silent or pp at 85–88. The pale colour comes with the diminuendo to pp into vii°7/E. A change to the B♭ clarinet would need two changes of instrument in the scherzo (c401). I tested the alternatives in my head: an octave higher breaks the falling two-bar sequence D5–B♭4–A4. The oboe would be in its "thick" lowest fifth at pp, which PDF 198 forbids. The low flute is weak and adds a colour the episode does not need (PDF 186). No change.

### 4. The trio call (Ob 1, 185) and the cellos' answer (189). Kept.
The call lies in the oboe's best range at p, with rests on both sides (PDF 198). The cor anglais's richest register (PDF 205) fits the answer, but c427 removed it so that the answer is in a different family (Adler II PDF 257). c489 recorded that 185–192 stays call and answer over the harp only. This chapter gives no stronger reason than c427's.

### 5. Codetta relay 225–236 (Ob 1 G♯5–C♯6, Cl 1 an octave lower, Bsn 1 two octaves lower). Kept.
The oboe's held C♯6 at 227 lies just above Adler's "most effective" top, C6, at the edge of "thin but clear" (PDF 198). It is held for only one bar at mp, and the relay then falls by octaves through each instrument's lyrical register (PDF 210, 226). This is the chapter's personality principle used as form. No change.

### 6. Second idea (45–56, 121–132, 277–288). Kept.
Fl 1 is "clear and brilliant" at D6–A6, Ob 1 is in its best octave, and the C.a. takes the low reed octave. Every register follows PDF 185, 198 and 205. The idea is still the least memorable of the four (c401). Its trouble is rhythmic: eight bars of hemiola minims. A woodwind chapter does not support rewriting its notes, and c481 has recorded that its doubling is full.

### 7. Coda top octave of T: Fl 1 with the solo violin (349–356). Kept.
The A6 at 350 is approached by step (G♯6–A6, PDF 186, "the best approach is by scale"), at p, and doubled by a solo violin. That is the right way to place the flute's difficult top softly (PDF 185). The coda top octave is protected (one player per instrument).

### 8. Woodwind breathing and rests. Follows.
The Fl 2 relief at 205–208 (PDF 188, Brahms 1/iv), the Cl 1 breath at T's half cadence (160, 216), the Ob 1 rests in the trio, and the Cbsn rests (PDF 230) all follow the chapter. No change.
