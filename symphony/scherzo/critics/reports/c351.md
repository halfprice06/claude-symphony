## Findings

The themes follow every principle in the table that applies. I looked for the largest change the reading could support. No candidate was clearly better than the score, so I made no change. Below are the six places I tested, ranked by how close each came to a change, with the reason each stays.

### 1. T bars 3–4 after c321 and c331 (155–156, 171–172, 199–200, 211–212, coda 343–344): the new figure holds under the reading (kept)

**What I checked.** Two recent theme changes have not yet been read against this unit. Bar 3 is now `dis''4. fis'8 a'4~ | a'4 gis'2`.
- The falling sixth lands on F♯4, a quaver on the second half of beat 2, the weakest place in the bar (PDF 50). At 2. = 76 that quaver lasts about 0.13 s. I asked whether the leap c321 gave T can be heard as T's own figure.
- The A on beat 3 is attacked and tied over (PDF 51), so the strongest attack of bars 3–4 is on a weak beat. That is the same device c081 reserved for the climax at 162.

**Why it stays.**
- PDF 50 lists unusually low notes among the built-in accents. F♯4 is T's lowest note, so the register gives the quaver weight that its metric position does not. It works as a quick plunge, not as a passing note.
- I tested the alternative `dis''4 fis'4 a'4~`, which puts the sixth on beat 2 as a crotchet. It loses the dotted rhythm that rhymes bars 3, 7 and 11 (`dis''4. fis'8`, `e''4. dis''8`, `a''4. gis''8`). It also gives bar 3 three equal crotchets, the shape the reading calls least natural for a strong bar (PDF 51, Ex. 3-1: long values on the strong beats). The current version keeps the long D♯5 on the downbeat.
- The tie over the bar line now recurs at bars 3–4, 10–11 and 13–14. PDF 52 says rhythmic groups "form an important element in the design". The figure crotchet tied over the bar line, then crotchet and minim (`a'4~ | a'4 gis'2`), is the second idea's opening cell at 45–46 (`b''4~ | b''4 g'''2`). The two lyrical themes now share a rhythm, which helps a listener connect them. This answers the "no figure of its own" point that c289–c319 raised.
- The suspension is prepared on the weak beat and resolves 4–3 on beat 2 of the next bar, as in Ex. 3-16 to 3-18 (PDF 55). Nothing in the accompaniment sounds the resolution early (c327, c331).

Decision: no change.

### 2. T's climax ladder 154 → 158 → 162 (and 170/174/178, 210/214/218): each peak carries more built-in accent than the last (kept)

The three phrase peaks rise E5 → F♯5 → A5. Their accent grows by the reading's own measures (PDF 50–51):
- E5 at 154: a crotchet on beat 3.
- F♯5 at 158: a minim on beat 2.
- A5 at 162: attacked on beat 3 and held over the strong beat, the longest and highest note, and an anticipation (PDF 56).

The printed hairpins give the same hidden level at phrases 2 and 3 (mp, mf, ff in the three statements). I considered raising phrase 3's level by one step. The reading puts the climax's emphasis in duration, register and syncopation, and those are already there. c348 has just moved the dynamic onto the attack. A further level change would affect only the MIDI, because these dynamics are hidden. No change.

### 3. The second idea 45–56 (121–132, 277–288): a counter-metre, not a contour (kept)

The tune moves in equal minims in 3/2 for eight bars (PDF 51, Ex. 3-7). Its peaks G6 (46) and A6 (50) fall on the second minim of each 3/2 group: weak bars, weak beats, high notes. That is a consistent counter-accent, which PDF 51 says sets up a pattern of its own.

The memorability gap that c299–c321 recorded is real: the line works by sequence and hemiola, not by a striking rhythm. Ex. 3-7 shows the remedy the reading offers, varied values inside the counter-metre (the Brahms solo has crotchets and minims within its 5/4). The second idea already varies its fourth and eighth bars (`d'''4. e'''8`, c031). Any further variation of bars 1–3 breaks the equal minims that make the 3/2 audible against the horns' dotted minims. The cadential hemiola at 53–55 then also loses its frame, because Ex. 3-8's hemiola works against a 3/4 already established.

Finding 1's shared cell (45–46 = 155–156) is the most the reading adds here. No change.

### 4. The cellos' T and the flute's answers, 169–184 (c344, c349): the answers avoid the accented dissonances (follows)

Fl 1 enters on beat 2 of 172, 176 and 183. In each case the cellos' accented dissonance on beat 1 has just resolved:
- 172: the A3→G♯3 suspension;
- 176: the A♯3 appoggiatura;
- 183: the cadence.

So the obbligato never sounds against an unresolved accented dissonance (PDF 54–55), and it fills the tune's held bars, where the tune has no attack. The swell into B5 at 184 places an attack on the fourth, weak bar of the group, after the cadence at 183. That is a feminine echo of the cadence, and the oboe call starts the next group on the strong bar 185. Kept.

### 5. The coda's return of T, 341–356: groups, hemiola and anticipation (follows)

The return is laid out as 4 + 4 + 8 bars: Hn 3 in A, Ob 1 in A, then Vln I in E. Each hand-over falls at a hypermeasure line (PDF 53).
- The cadence keeps a hemiola at 354–355: G♯6, then F♯6–E6 tied, then D♯6–F♯6 (Ex. 3-8).
- The anticipation `fis'''8 e'''~` is held over (Ex. 3-19).
- The tonic arrives on 356, the fourth bar, as in Ex. 3-13's bar 8. The motto tail then starts the next group on a strong bar at 357.

The closing timpani x at 363, 367 and 371 always falls on the third bar of its group (PDF 53). Kept, and protected (finale IV 9).

### 6. Phrase lengths: no five- or seven-bar phrase in any theme (kept, c261's reason confirmed)

PDF 54 (Ex. 3-14) allows asymmetric phrases. The movement varies its group lengths in the retransition, 3 × 3 bars then 4 (PDF 53, Ex. 3-12; Beethoven 9/ii). The coda's T already stretches its cadence by a bar. Any extension elsewhere would add bars:
- in the trio, it moves every bar from 225 onward, including the closing page that the finale quotes by bar;
- in the exposition, it moves the whole score.

The reading treats asymmetry as an option, so there is no stronger reason to act than c261 had. No change.
