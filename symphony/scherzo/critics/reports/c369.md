## Holistic comparison with the "Rhenish" scherzo

- **Theme in the middle of the texture.** Schumann's ländler theme starts in violas, cellos and bassoons, not in the violins. This score does something close: theme A opens in the violins, but the movement's long tenor line (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4 at 105, 253, 305) gives the middle register its own voice.
- **Each return varied.** Schumann never brings his tune back the same way. It is reharmonised, figured and moved between registers. At the end the full orchestra thins until only cellos and bassoon hold the theme, and the movement closes on soft pizzicato. This score does that well in most places: T has four settings, the coda thins to Hn 1's G♯ (357–376), and dA (253–288) recolours theme A with a cello countermelody and a flute quaver heterophony. The one exception is the reprise tutti at 305–320, which repeats 105–120 almost note for note. Schumann's practice and Adler's Mahler 4 example (PDF 169–171) both point to the same fix, and that is finding 1.
- **Trio-like contrast.** Schumann's A-minor wind episode over a C pedal is a quiet, held contrast. The E-major Trio here (153–252) plays the same role, with T over held harp and pizzicato bass. It works.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote it?** On the scoring, yes, for almost every passage. The literal repeat at 305 is what a conductor would mark as the one place where the composer stopped composing and copied. With finding 1 applied, the reprise tutti brings back all three dA elements together, and that is the kind of return Schumann writes.

## Findings

### 1. Reprise tutti 305–320: the flutes should bring back the dA heterophony, not rest and then double the theme (applied)

**Bars:** 305–320, Fl 1·2.

**Problem:** At 305–320 the flutes rest for four bars and then double Vln I an octave up for twelve bars. Apart from the held-back low brass, the c362 revoicing and the bass F♯ at 313, this copies 105–120. dA (253–268) has just exposed three separate elements: the staccato theme, the legato tenor countermelody (Vc, 253) and a slurred flute quaver heterophony (Fl 1, p leggiero). The reprise tutti brings back two of them and drops the third.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 175–178 (Meistersinger prelude, XV-26): elements first heard separately are later combined, each in its own mixed colour and with its own articulation.
- PDF 169–171 (Mahler 4, XV-20 to XV-22): a recurring passage is scored differently each time while its texture keeps it recognisable.
- PDF 147–148 (d'Indy, XV-1): a slurred line against detached lines in a tutti gives tension.
- PDF 178–181 (Scheherazade, Holst): a big sound needs distinct rhythmic layers, and running figuration keeps a loud texture moving.
- PDF 165–166 (Mahler 4, XV-17): long doublings tire the ear.

At 305–320 the theme (strings, oboes, cor anglais, clarinets) is detached crotchets and quavers, and the tenor line is in minims. The flute quaver figure adds a third rhythm and a third articulation that the listener already knows from 253.

**Answering the protected reason.** HISTORY protects the 105–120/305–320 tutti layouts. It records c124's reason for the flute rests at 105–108/305–308: a flute doubling there would sit in its weak register at pitch, or the upper octave would reach B6 at 107 and Fl 1 would have to drop out at 108. The heterophony answers all three points:
- It turns down in bar 3 (e'''8 dis''' e''' b'' g''4), so it never goes above A6. B6 stays reserved for 336.
- I put bar 4 (308) an octave above its 256 form (fis'''8 e''' dis''' cis''' b''4), so the flutes stay in their upper octave throughout, as c078 asked for 109–120.
- The flutes play continuously from 305 to 320, so nobody drops out.

I leave the exposition tutti at 105–120 exactly as it is. The figure has not yet been heard there, so plain doubling is right at 105 and the difference at 305 is audible.

## Considered and not proposed

- **Pianissimo heterophony at 305:** dropped. A soft figure would be lost in an ff tutti, and cycle 36 accepted p, not pp, for high flute lines.
- **A new colour for the coda stretto at 325–328** (Berlioz XI-14): protected, and the same device already exists at 289–296.
- **Timpani rolls at 129–130 and 285–286 marked p<** (Bloch PDF 184): c269 kept them at f because they come after the tutti, not inside a build.
- **Brass diminuendo at 339** (Schubert XI-12): settled by c128/c130, and the chord lasts about 0.4 s.
- **Recolouring the tenor line at 305 as well:** one change is enough to make the return audibly new. Changing the countermelody too would weaken the link to 105.
- **Unmixed cello colour for T at 169:** Adler PDF 166 accepts pure colour, so I left it.
