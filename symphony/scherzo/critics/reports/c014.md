## Findings

### 1. Bars 109 and 313: Tpt 2 plays D♯4 in a tonic bar (applied)
**Problem.** In the ff tutti the trumpets play motive x in alternate bars on a fixed E4/B4 fifth. At 109 (sC bar 5) and 313 (dC bar 5) Tpt 2 has `dis'4-. dis'8-. dis'-. dis'4-.`. The bar is E minor: melody E5–B4–B4–E5, Hn 4 E4, Hn 1 E5, Tbn 2 E3, bass E. The trumpet's D♯4 sits a semitone under Hn 4's E4, at f, on the accented downbeat and repeated three times. The next motive-x bar (111/315) is on V and correctly has D♯; bar 109 seems to have taken that bar's note by mistake.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 100: brass harmony in close order on chord tones. PDF 102–103: a second inside a chord should at least be split between different colours. Here it is not a planned dissonance; it is a wrong note in the trumpet.
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily, `sCTptTwo`/`dCTptTwo` bar 5: `e'4-. e'8-. e'-. e'4-.`.

### 2. Bars 105–120 and 309–324: the bass trombone is the only sustained part in a detached tutti (applied)
**Problem.** Under theme A ff, the bass trombone holds dotted minims on the bass line (E2 for three bars, then B2, A2...) for 16 bars. Every other bass part (tuba, contrabassoon, Bsn 2, Cb, timpani) plays an accented downbeat crotchet, and the trombones 1·2 were cut back to crotchets in cycle 7. The one sustained brass note is therefore the lowest and heaviest one. It blurs the downbeat-and-silence pattern that makes the tutti a scherzo tutti, and it covers the legato tenor line in Vc/Bsn 1/Hn 3.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 101: when the bass needs doubling in octaves, "the too resonant trombone and tuba are seldom used, the duplication being effected by the bassoon". PDF 104: sustained notes go to trumpets or horns; "the trombone with its ponderous tone rarely takes part". PDF 114: balance matters most where chords are sustained. Dvořák 9/iii, the movement's model, has no trombones at all.
**Fix (applied).** Swap the two parts in bars 1–16 of sC and dC. `sCBassTbn = { e,4->\f r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | b,4-> r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | a,4-> r4 r4 | b,4-> r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | a,4-> r4 r4 | g,4-> r4 r4 | a,4-> r4 r4 | b,4-> r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | g,4-> r4 r4 | b,4-> r4 r4 | e,4-> r4 r4 | R2.*20 | }`, and Bsn 2 takes the old sustained line: `sCBsnTwo = { e,2. | e,2. | e,2. | b,2. | e,2. | a,2. | b,2. | e,2. | a,2. | g,2. | a,2. | b,2. | e,2. | g,2. | b,2. | e,4 r4 r4 | ...` (rest of the part unchanged; Bsn 2 then runs straight into its sustained line at 121). The same for `dCBassTbn`/`dCBsnTwo`. The bass trombone stays in octaves with the tuba (E2 over E1), as c004 asked.

### 3. Bars 104 and 308: the woodwind harmony lies under the trumpets on the ff chord (applied)
**Problem.** On the V7 chord that ends each hemiola build, Tpt 1 has D♯5 and Tpt 2 A4 at ff. Under them: Ob 2 B4, Cl 2 A4 (unison with Tpt 2 and Hn 1), Cl 1 D♯4–F♯4. Only Fl 1 (melody) and Fl 2 (D♯5, unison with Tpt 1) reach the trumpet's height. The oboe and clarinet harmony is enclosed by the brass and adds nothing that can be heard.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 110: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets"; all three diagrams on PDF 111 put the woodwind pairs above 2 Tr-be. PDF 108–109: at forte, balance against the brass by doubling the upper harmonic parts in the woodwind.
**Fix (applied).** sB.ily and dB.ily, last bar: `sBOboeTwo ... | fis''2.\ff |` (was `b'2.\ff`) and `sBClTwo ... | a''2.\ff |` (was `a'2.\ff`); the same in `dBOboeTwo` and `dBClTwo`. The woodwind chord at 104 is then D♯5 (Fl 2) – F♯5 (Ob 2) – A5 (Cl 2) under Fl 1's B5–A5, above Tpt 1. Ob 2 moves by step from its D♯5 in 103; Cl 2 moves up the octave on the downbeat, in the clarion. Both enter at 105 after a crotchet rest, so the leap back down is not heard as a leap.

### 4. Bars 209–224: both bassoons in unison on the bass, a gap of more than an octave under Hn 1·2 (applied)
**Problem.** At the trio tune's forte return the sustained harmony is Hn 1·2 (thirds and sixths, from c004) at p. Below them the next sustained part is Bsn 1·2 *a2* on the bass at E3–F♯2, then Cb pizzicato. The Vc countermelody moves through the tenor, the violas play off-beat crotchets. The sustained wind chord is therefore horns on top and two bassoons on one bass note: an empty octave and more between E3 and G♯4, and one bassoon wasted on a unison. c004 noted the unison and left it.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 108: two bassoons and two horns in soft four-part harmony give "a finely balanced tone recalling the effect of a quartet of horns, but possessing slightly greater transparence", with the discords preferably in the bassoons. PDF 100: close order, no empty spaces. PDF 108: bassoons may sit inside the horns.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily, `tCBsnOne` bars 1–16: `e'2.\p | e'2. | dis'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | dis'2 r4 | cis'2. | b2. | a2. | a2. | b2. | a2. | gis2. | gis2 r4 |` (Bsn 2 keeps the bass). Bsn 1 now fills the middle of the chord, almost all by step. It takes the sevenths of F♯m7 and F♯7 (E4 at 214–215) and of V7 (A3 at 222), and the A3 resolves to G♯3 at 223. That is the p. 108 layout: concords in the horns, discords in the bassoon. Bsn 1 lies above the cellos' countermelody (whose top is A3) and below Hn 2, so it does not double or cross the countermelody.
