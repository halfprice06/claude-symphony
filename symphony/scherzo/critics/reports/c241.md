To check rows 3, 4, 10 and 28 bar by bar across the whole score, not only the themes, I wrote /tmp/critic-241/scan/susp.py. It reads score.midi, takes every note above E3 that is held over a beat where it is dissonant with the lowest sounding or struck bass, keeps those that then move by step in the same track, and lists any other track sounding the resolution's pitch class at that beat. It found 106 candidates (output in /tmp/critic-241/scan/susp.txt). The T cadences at 166, 182, 222 and 355 have no other voice on the resolution tone. Every case with one is a 9-8 or ♭9-8 over the bass, a ff tutti with contrasted rhythms, or the 200 case that c151 kept. Changes made since c151 (c202 at 110/310, c223 at 54, c224 at 133–136, c227 at 15–16 and 242, c234 at 141–144, c237 at 99–100, c226 at 341–344) add no new cases. I compared the trio with Dvořák 9/iii, bars 84–91 (reference-corpus/pdf/DvorakA_O95__Mvt3_Scherzo.pdf, PDF p. 7, viewed at 80 dpi). There the flute and bassoon tie their notes over the bar lines while the strings keep fz/fp off-beats. T does the same at its climax (162–163) and its cadence (165–166). It keeps its other bars plain, which keeps the two ties audible.

**Judgement of the melodies.** Theme A uses motive x, the chromatic tail and the C6 peak. It is a sixteen-bar tune of four four-bar phrases with one accented dissonance at its top, and it is the movement's hook. The second idea sets up a 3/2 metre against the 3/4 metre, which is Ex. 21-31's case. Its ties are consonant, so its one real dissonance (the horns' suspended chord at 47) stands out. T now has every device this unit names for a lyrical tune: a long note off the beat (158, Ex. 21-32), a tied anticipation at the climax (162), a cadential 4-3 (166), and a delayed 4-3 in the coda (354–355, Ex. 21-27). Each appears once per phrase, not as a habit. That is how Dvořák's and Brahms's trio tunes use them. A listener will leave with T's third phrase and theme A's head. I found nothing that the reading shows to be wrong. The findings below are the points I checked and the decision on each.

## Findings (ranked; no score change)

### 1. Bars 24 and 264: theme A's peak C6 sounds over the violas' B3 on beat 2 (considered, kept)

**Problem examined.** Vln I holds C6 (accented minim) over the V bass B from the downbeat and resolves to B5 on beat 3. On beat 2 the violas' off-beat B3 sounds the resolution tone two octaves and a ninth below. At 116 and 316 the tenor line (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4) has B3 on beat 2 as well. PDF 352, sec. 12 calls the resolution tone in another voice "an unclear sound" in a homogeneous texture.

**Decision: keep.** PDF 352 gives the exceptions that apply here. First, the bass itself is B (cello and bass pizz. on the downbeat), so this is a ♭9-8 over the bass, the Ex. 21-12d "good" case; the violas' B3 doubles the bass's pitch class. Second, the texture has marked contrast: an accented legato minim at p/mp against pp staccato off-beat crotchets (at 116/316, a ff tutti against a tenor countermelody). Third, the reading says Beethoven allowed exactly this: a top-voice dissonance over the resolution tone an octave lower in an inner voice. The alternatives are worse. D♯4 on beat 2 doubles the leading tone, which c002 removed elsewhere. A rest on beat 2 breaks the off-beat pattern `r4 b dis'` in a protected theme statement. The c112/c202 tenor at 110–111 already shows the composer handling this dissonance deliberately.

### 2. Bars 45–56, 121–131: the second idea's ties (considered, kept)

The ties in the second idea (`b''4~ | b''4`, `e'''4~ | e'''4`, and so on) are consonant. Melody, bass and violas move together in minims across the bar line, so the music is in 3/2 for two bars, as in Ex. 21-31 (PDF 362). The ties are not weak-beat suspensions. They need no resolution (PDF 348: "A consonant suspension does not need to resolve"). The one dissonant suspension is the horns' D-major chord held over the bass E at 47. It resolves at 48 (Hn 1 A4–G4, a 4-3), which is Ex. 21-37's suspended chord (PDF 365) with Ex. 21-14's moving bass (PDF 353). The quaver lead-ons at 48 and 52 are restruck anticipations on the weakest part of beat 3 (PDF 363–364, Ex. 21-35). The idea is protected (hemiola cadences, c032, c121). Kept.

### 3. Bars 153–168 and returns: T's devices (considered, kept)

Scan results for T: the four cadential 4-3s resolve with no other voice sounding the resolution tone on the downbeat (at 166 the harp's D♯ enters on beat 2 with the resolution; the violas' beat-2 chords are F♯3–C♯4 at 182 and A3–F♯4 at 222 and 355, not the resolution tone). The anticipation A5 at 162 enters by step, falls on beat 3 and is tied (PDF 363–364). In bar 6 (158) the long high F♯5 on beat 2 over a change of harmony is Ex. 21-32's rhythmic accent against the metre (PDF 362–363). Bars 7 (159, 175, 215) take the E as the seventh of V7/V on the dotted crotchet and resolve it down by step on the quaver (PDF 348). Nothing to change; T and its cadence are protected (c061, c121, c151).

### 4. Bars 155–156: T bar 4's struck A instead of a prepared 4-3 (considered, rejected)

A prepared 4-3 would turn T's fourth bar into a real suspension (PDF 349–350). It needs A4 on beat 3 of 155 (`dis''4. cis''8 a'4~ | a'4 gis'2`). That replaces the line's arrival on B4, so the first phrase loses its D♯–C♯–B descent. It also makes phrase 1 end with the same tied cadence figure that c061 saved for the end of the tune. The struck accented passing tone (c001) is the dissonant-preparation case the book allows (Ex. 21-23b, PDF 360), and it keeps the tied figure for the cadence. Rejected.

### 5. Bars 193–196 and 205–209: the trio middle (considered, kept)

At 195 the strings and horns make an I6/4 over B. At 196 the 4th resolves on the downbeat (Cl 2 and Hn 2 E–D♯) and Vln I's G♯5 is held to beat 2 before F♯5. This is the 6-5 over 4-3 of Ex. 21-11 and the 7/6–5 over V7 of Ex. 21-15f (PDF 354–356). The resolution is softer than the suspension, since the crescendo continues into the f at 197. That follows PDF 348, because the line is a lead-in, not a phrase end. At 205–208 Fl 2 and Cl 2 carry B–A♯–A over the B pedal and hand the G♯ to T at 209. That is an indirect suspension of the motto tail (Ex. 21-22, PDF 357). Kept.

### 6. Bars 61–68: the fugato countersubject in plain crotchets (considered, rejected)

PDF 348, sec. 4 says suspensions make a polyphonic line independent, and the countersubject (Vc 61–64, Vla 65–68) has none. I did not apply a tie. The countersubject's falling crotchets already differ in rhythm from the subject's quavers and accented minim. The cello's `f2 e4~ | e2.` at 67–68 is a consonant syncopation into the dominant. The strings-only fugato 57–72 is protected (c032, c207, c237), and this is a texture point rather than one about the movement's tunes. Rejected.
