## Findings

### 1. The cellos join the violas and cor anglais on the second idea's lowest octave, 45–55. Applied.
**Problem.** The second idea's only forte major-key statement (45–55) is the place where critics since c289 have called the movement's least memorable theme most exposed. It is laid out in three octaves:
- Fl 1 + Vln I at D6
- Ob 1 + Vln II at D5
- violas + C.a. at D4, with the violas leaving the tune at 52 for the A7's seventh (c419)

The lowest octave is the weakest of the three: one string section against fourteen to sixteen violins in each upper octave, and through the hemiola cadence 53–55 only the cor anglais. Meanwhile the whole cello section plays the bass line in unison with Bsn 1. That line is already doubled at 16′ by the basses and contrabassoon. Bsn 2 is free (it rests 45–56).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 89 names "the very successful coupling of violas and cellos" on a tune. Ex. III-39 (PDF 91, Beethoven 5/ii) shows the two sections in unison on the main tune. PDF 99: the cello's beauty shows best in passages "featuring the entire section". The A string, which the tune's D4–A4 lies on, is "the most brilliant and piercing", so it carries in a forte tutti. PDF 105: a section of basses alone "can raise the roof", so the basses with Cbsn and two bassoons hold the bass. PDF 103 lists cello and bassoon as a standard coupling, and Bsn 2 takes over exactly the cellos' line. Dvořák 9/iii mm. 33–41 does the same in its own ff statement.

**Recorded reasons answered.**
- c419/c471 protect "the forte G-major statement in three octaves". It stays in three octaves; only the lowest gains weight.
- The violas still leave the tune at 52 (c419). The cellos carry it with the cor anglais through 53–55, which answers c471's stated problem that the hemiola cadence was in two octaves only.
- c431 kept the cellos off the second idea at 121 because there they carry the bass with Bsn 2 while the basses rest. At 45 the basses play, and Bsn 2 replaces the cellos.
- c071's exposed tenor colour (cellos + Hn 2 + Cl 2, p, 281–287) is untouched. At 45 the cellos are inside a forte tutti doubling, not heard as a solo colour, so the reprise's colour is still new.
- c389 (no bass octaves with the tune), c452 (C♯–D rising in its own register, now Bsn 1·2 C♯3–D3) and c294/c282 (6/4 doubling) are unchanged, because no pitch changes. The cellos double existing notes of the cor anglais and violas.
- c027's rejected cello counter-line was a new line. This is a doubling.

### 2. Vln II "sul A" for the soft E-minor statement 121–124. Not applied.
Ex. III-7 (PDF 75) suggests keeping a soft lower octave on the A string, as c466 did at 177 and 349. Vln II's line at 121 falls B4–A4–G4. The G4 lies below the open A, so the mark cannot hold for the phrase; c466 ended its own mark before G♯4 for the same reason. Kept.

### 3. Hn 3 doubles the cellos' T at 169–176 (c431). Kept.
Adler's cello models are all the section alone (PDF 99–100). But PDF 103 lists "cello and horn" among the most used couplings. The reading therefore supports both choices, and c431's reasons (the horn call in T's head; preparing Hn 3's T at 341) stand.

### 4. Sul G for Vln II at 193–200. Not applied.
Ex. III-5 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii) supports a rising p< line on the G string, and 193–196 is one. But at 199 the same line has T's falling sixth D5–F♯4 on a quaver. On one string that is a sixth-wide shift at f, which is Adler's first section hazard (PDF 82–83, Ex. III-22, wide skips on the same string). A mark lasting only 193–198 would end inside T's head. Kept.

### 5. Second idea's top octave on the high E string, 45–52 (c301). Kept.
PDF 75 calls the high E string strident when loud. Finding 1 adds warmth below rather than taking brilliance from the top. The E string's "tremendous carrying power" is what keeps the D6 octave above the newly weighted D4 octave, and the soft statements at 121 and 277 still give the same tune the string's luminous colour.

### 6. Climax layouts (Ex. III-38). Kept.
The trio's ff peak 197–200 has T in Vln II with the violas an octave below. The coda peak 333–338 has Vln I, Vln II and violas in octaves. Both follow Berlioz's layout.

### 7. Coda (Ex. III-20; PDF 108). Kept.
The solo violin at 349 colours T's last return after horn and oboe, as Brahms 1/ii does. The basses' held E at 358–364 lasts about 5.5 s at pp, short enough not to need "change bow at will".

### Memorability verdict
- **Theme A** is the movement's signature, recognisable from motive x alone (Beethoven 9/ii, Dvořák 9/iii).
- **T** is singable, with peaks rising E5 → F♯5 → tied A5, its falling sixth and two suspensions.
- **The second idea** is still the weakest of the three. Its forte statement now sounds with a full lower string octave, the scoring of the big string tunes in Tchaikovsky and Dvořák, and that statement is where a listener first learns it.
