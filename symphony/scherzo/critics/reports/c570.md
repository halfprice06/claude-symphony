## Findings

### 1. Bar 318: Hn 4's held F♯3 sounds against theme A's passing F♮ (applied)

**Problem.** On beat 3 of 318 the reprise tutti plays theme A's tail F♯–F (quavers) in seven parts: Fl 1·2 F6, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II F5, C.a., Cl 2 and violas F4. Since c552, Hn 4 holds F♯3 for the whole beat (`g2 fis4`). So F♮ and F♯ sound together for a quaver, ff. The other three statements of this cadence (26, 118, 266) have no held F♯ under the tail. At 118 Hn 4 is in the tenor on A3, and beat 3 is A–C with the melody's F♯–F above it. c552 chose F♯3 to avoid a G against the A. Its own report says the F5 "adds a passing Neapolitan sixth". With F♯3 held below, it does not.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a: a chromatic succession split between two voices "can sound disagreeable". Here the two tones are not even in succession: they sound at the same time.
- PDF 395, point 7: cross relations "are usually avoided".
- The tail is the motto's chromatic line on E. Movement I (I 79) and the finale (IV 273–275) quote it at pitch, so every statement should keep it clean. c011 recorded the same concern: no E on beat 3 under F♯–F–E.
- The scan of the rebuilt per-player MIDI lists 318.3.5 as the only F/F♯ overlap in the movement. It is absent from the stale v570 cc.midi, which predates c552.

**Fix (applied, dC.ily, dCHnFour).**
```
old: g2.( | g2 fis4 | b,2. | e4) r4 r4 | R2.*4 | }
new: g2.( | g2 a,4 | b,2. | e4) r4 r4 | R2.*4 | }
```
- Hn 4 moves G3–A2–B2–E3, doubling the bass A–B into 319 in unison with Bsn 2. That is the doubled bass of ii°6, as c552 asked (A&S Ex. 9-7d).
- Beat 3 is now A–C with the melody's F♯–F, as at 26, 118 and 266.
- There is still no G against the A, and no C–E–G.
- G3–A2 (a seventh down) is an easy slur inside the ff tutti; Hn 4 already plays A2 at 310.

### 2. The best mixture candidate, built and rejected: Vln II G♯4–G4–F♯4–E4 at 357–360

**Candidate.** This is the change the reading itself points to most directly. Vln II's ♭3 at 358 leaps G4–E4, which c070 recorded as the one imperfect ♭3 in the movement. Taking it by step to F♯4 at 359 makes Schubert's line ♮3–♭3–2–1 (Ex. 23-4, PDF 389). It also turns 359 into II♭6 4/2 over the tonic pedal, which is exactly Ex. 23-2e (PDF 388). I built it in /tmp/critic-570/cand (`gis'2.(\pp | g'2. | fis'2. | e'2.)`). The build is clean, 13 pages, 52/52, and analysis.txt is identical.

**Why it makes the music worse.**
- **Vln II shadows the motto tail.** It would move in thirds under Hn 1's B4–A♯4–A4 for three bars. c490 thinned Vln II to "la metà" because the two-bar shadow (G♯–G under B–A♯) already competed with the solo horn. A third bar turns the motto's single chromatic line into a pair of chromatic thirds. The cyclic rule is to quote the motto, not develop it, and c060 and c090 keep the tail a single horn line.
- **It breaks the rhyme with the finale.** The finale closes the same way at IV 273–275: I, then a common-tone °7, then iv6/4 (F–A♭–C at 274), then I, over its tonic pedal. The scherzo's 359 A–C–E is that chord. Its ♭3 also sits in one voice beside 3 (Cl 1, Bsn 1 and Hn 4: E–E♭ at 273). An added F♯ would make the two closes differ.
- **It weakens the ♭6.** The C.a.'s C4 would form a tritone with F♯4, so the ♭6 would colour a half-diminished ii instead of the minor subdominant. Ex. 23-5's point is that the brief minor passage ends on IV♭–I.

Not applied. c070's "♭3 leaps to 1" stays. The reason for keeping it is now stronger than c070's: the finale's IV 274 has the same chord.

### 3. Long-range mixture across the arc: no change

- **Two tones for one degree (PDF 387).** The scherzo states 3 of E as both G♯ and G at four joins, each time in one voice: 1–7, 141→145, 240→241 and 357→358.
- **Secondary mixture at the symphony's scale (PDF 393, 395 pts 5–6).** E is III♯ of C.
  - It is reached from movement II's A♭ through the common tone G♯ = A♭, so the ♮5–♯5 step G–G♯ never sounds across the pause.
  - It is left to the finale's German sixth on A♭, a predominant, and then to V: A♭4→G4 in Hn 1 at IV 1–2, ♭6–5 (PDF 388).
  - The scherzo's frame pitch is therefore the finale's ♭6, held through the scherzo as 3 of E.
- **The Picardy third (Ex. 23-7).** The movement's major turns stay soft (145, 341, 349), so the finale's C major is the only loud ♯3 arrival.
- **♭6 order.** Every C♮ in the E-major bars goes to B (PDF 388); the coda's IV (341) and iv (359) repeat the codetta's IV–iv (Ex. 23-8) at a larger span.
