## Findings

### 1. Trio tune T, bars 10–11 of every statement: the climax has no rhythmic accent (applied)

**Bars.** 162–163 (Cl 1), 178–179 (Vln I, Vln II), 218–219 (Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1) and the coda's 350–351 (Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1).

**Problem.** T is the movement's lyrical centre, the tune a listener should keep. Its contour is good: three phrases peak in the same place, on beat 3 of the phrase's second bar, and the peaks climb E5 (154), F♯5 (158), A5. Its rhythm does not support that climb.
- In the first 12 bars every bar begins with a new note on the downbeat. Every rhythmic group is one bar long.
- The first two peaks are short upbeat crotchets. At the third, the A5 comes on the next downbeat as a dotted crotchet, no longer than the D♯5 and E5 that held the same place in phrases 1 and 2 (bars 3 and 7).
- So the tune's highest note is not its longest or its most stressed note.
- The only syncopation before the end is the cadential tie at 165, which c061 added.

The tune therefore climbs in pitch but not in rhythm. The three phrases share the shape 2+1 | 2+1 | 4.-8-4 | …, which is the open point from cycle 1.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- PDF 50: "long notes attract accents… unusually high or low notes come across more strongly… dissonant or chromatic elements… tend to sound accented."
- PDF 51: a note "that begins on a weak beat… and is held through the next strong beat" gives the strongest rhythmic accent, because "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation."
- PDF 56, Ex. 3-19: the anticipation is a tone "shifted out of its normal rhythmic position by beginning before the strong beat", held into the consonance that follows.
- PDF 52, Ex. 3-10: rhythmic groups that run past the bar line.

All four point to the same change. Move the A onto the beat-3 peak position that phrases 1 and 2 have already set up, and hold it across the bar line. The A then becomes the highest, longest and only syncopated note before the cadence. It is a dissonant anticipation over I6 (bar 10) that turns consonant over ii (bar 11, c012's ii–ii6).

Dvořák's E-major trio tune in 9/iii (Ob 1, 68–76) is built on exactly this device. The tied notes are what make it sing above a waltz-like accompaniment.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-081/work).** The rhythm of bar 11 stays `4. 8 4` and only the A is tied into it.
- Old: `e''4 gis''2 | a''4. gis''8 fis''4`
- New: `e''4 gis''4 a''4~ | a''4. gis''8 fis''4` (each statement at its octave)

In the string parts the first slur now ends on G♯ and the A starts a new bow, so the anticipation gets a fresh attack:
- tA Vln I: `cis'''2(\< dis'''4 | e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~( | a'''4.\> gis'''8 fis'''4) |`
- The same in Vln II, tC Vln I and II, and coda Vln I and II at their octaves.

The wind parts keep one slur: tA Cl 1, tC Cl 1 and Fl 1, coda Cl 1, e.g. `e''4 gis''4 a''4~ | a''4.\> gis''8 fis''4`.

The hairpins are unchanged: the swell from bar 9 now peaks on the held A and the diminuendo starts on it.

- The cellos have E3 there and A3 on the next downbeat, as before.
