## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 225–240: the cellos pluck the tonic an octave over the basses for sixteen bars under the codetta (applied)

**Problem.** In the codetta T's head passes Ob 1 (225), Cl 1 (229), Bsn 1 (233) over Vln II and violas holding B4/G♯4, the harp's thinning chords and an E pedal. The pedal is struck on every downbeat by the basses (E2), the harp's left hand (E2) and the cellos (E3); from 229 Hn 4 also holds E3. So the root sounds in three or four instruments while the third and fifth lie in one or two. At 233–236 Bsn 1's solo (G♯3–C4) sits a third above the plucked E3. The trio's first statement, which the codetta recalls, has no cellos (153–168, c037), and c267 took the cellos out of the coda's matching passage (341–347) for this reason.

**Evidence.** PDF 140, rule 7: the root is "the least important note of a string voicing"; doubling the bass with the lowest cello by habit "can be detrimental to the overall sound, 'anchoring' or 'grounding' it", and putting distance between the cellos and the bass lets the strings "float". PDF 145 (Ex. 195): an extra root in the bottom cello "would have 'grounded' the voicing, making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating". PDF 146: the bottom cello plays roots "for temporary richness and depth ... but *not* to reinforce the bass"; at the end "the arco bass, supplying the chordal roots all by himself, is now part of the string ensemble". Ex. 204 (PDF 157, image checked) ends the same way. PDF 173: "the cellos would only be in the way ... in order to avoid clutter, I had them tacet".

**Answer to the recorded reasons.** c254 kept the pizz. downbeats because "the pizzicato E is the pedal that holds the codetta on the tonic". The pedal stays: basses and harp still pluck it every bar, Hn 4 holds it from 229, the timpani roll takes it at 237. Only the octave doubling goes. c127 and c153 protect the wind relay and the harp's thinning, which are untouched; the thinning now also reaches the bass. c267 kept the passage as protected without a reason of its own.

**Fix (tC.ily, retr.ily).** `tCVc`: `... b,2.~\upbow\> | b,2 r4\! | R2.*16 | }` (225–240 rests; the hairpin now ends at 224). `retrVc`: `e4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." r4 r4 |` at 241. The cellos also get 16 bars instead of one crotchet for the arco→pizz. change kept by c096.

### 2. Bars 209–224 and 349–356: Cl 1 doubles Vln II's lower octave of T at the violins' level (applied)

**Problem.** In both E-major statements of T for violins, Vln I has the tune, Vln II the octave below, and Cl 1 doubles Vln II at the same dynamic (209: f, hidden ff peaks; 349: pp, hidden p peak). c364 recorded these as "single winds ... used as thickening, a level softer"; the print shows they are not. With a clarinet at the strings' level the lower octave outweighs the lead.

**Evidence.** PDF 164: "one woodwind equals approximately sixteen violins"; a wind added to a violin line thickens it, and "to compensate ... I indicate a lesser volume marking" for the wind. PDF 138, rule 2: "the lead violin line should be carried by more players than any other line". The score already does this at 45, 125, 197, 217 and 253 (c067, c167).

**Fix.** tC.ily `tCClOne`: 209 `gis'2(\mf^\markup \italic "espress."`; hidden targets one level down (`\mf`→`\mp` at 212; `\f`→`\mf` at 213, 215, 221, 222; `\ff`→`\f` at 214, 218, 222). coda.ily `codaClOne`: 349 `cis''2(\ppp\<`, hidden `\pp` at 350, `\ppp` at 353 and 355. ### 3. Bars 41–43 and 273–275: the forte counterstatement has six parts on one octave and nothing below (applied)

**Problem.** At 41–43 (and 273–275) theme A's head in G is played f by Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, Vln I and Vln II, all at the same pitch (D5–C6). No instrument has the lower octave. At 44 the cor anglais suddenly plays the theme's last bar alone (A4–F♯4–D4, c086), a fragment of a line it has not played. c324 rejected moving the flutes up (they would pass B6); nobody has tried the octave below.

**Evidence.** PDF 140, rule 6: "Whenever a particularly strong ensemble sound is necessary, especially when the strings are playing a concerted melodic passage, the lead violin line should be reinforced an octave lower." PDF 151 (Ex. 202, image checked): the climax gains body by "reinforcing the lead line an octave lower". PDF 165 (Ex. 213, image checked): winds an octave under the lead violins, "nicely filling out the string chord". The tutti at 105–112 already puts the cor anglais and Cl 2 on the lower octave.
