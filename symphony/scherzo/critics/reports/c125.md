## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Oboes cross at 333–339, so the shared staff shows overlapping parts (applied)
Problem: in the coda tutti, Ob 2 holds G5 (`g''2.` ×3, then `g''4` at 339) above Ob 1's figure E5–B4–E5–G5 (`e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-.` …). At 338, Ob 1's D♯5 also lies under Ob 2's F♯5. The part combiner keeps Ob 1 up-stemmed and Ob 2 down-stemmed, which is correct, but the heads sit almost on the same x. At 333 and 335, Ob 2's white G5 sits on Ob 1's black E5, and the down-stem seems to hold both notes. This is the cross check's only hit in the movement.
Evidence: Gould PDF 65: the upper part always takes up-stems, even when the pitches overlap. PDF 66–67: when parts overlap, separate them so that "the stems do not appear to include both notes". The simplest way to meet both is to stop the parts overlapping. The oboes are a background doubling here: the theme is in the flutes and violins.
Fix (coda.ily): swap the two lines, which leaves the sound the same:
`codaOboeOne = { R2.*8 | g''2.\ff | g''2. | g''2.\< | b''2.->\fff | a''2-> fis''4->~ | fis''2. | g''4-> r4 r4 | …`
`codaOboeTwo = { R2.*8 | e''4-.\ff b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | g''2->( fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.)\< b'8-. b'-. g''4-. | fis''2.\fff | fis''2. | fis''4 dis''2-> | e''4-> r4 r4 | …`
Each pitch and attack still happens at the same moment (the F♯ re-attack at 338 is Ob 2's, as before). The dynamics are the same in both parts. The staff now reads as a held upper line over the figure. At 334 the two G5 unisons are kept apart as in PDF 65.

### 2. Tenutos under ledger-line notes look like extra ledger lines and touch dynamics (applied)
Bars: 105–120 B.tbn (E2 at 105–107, 109, 112, 117, 120) and Tuba (every downbeat, E1–B1); 201–202 Vln II (C♯4, E4).
Problem: c124 changed the low-brass downbeats from accents to tenutos in v125. On a note below the staff, LilyPond puts the tenuto under the notehead, one space below it. It is as wide as a ledger line, so under B.tbn E2 it reads as a second ledger line (C2), and under the Tuba's four ledger lines it reads as a fifth. At 800 dpi, the Tuba's tenuto at 105 is less than ½ space from the f. The Vln II tenuto under C♯4 at 201 touches the p, and the one under E4 at 202 sits where the C4 ledger line goes.
Evidence: Gould PDF 55 (at least ½ space between characters, none colliding). PDF 56 (no sign placed where it can be read as another: "An accidental must be sufficiently far from a clef or a key signature for it not to be mistaken for one").
Fix: put the tenuto at the stem end. In sC.ily, every tenuto in sCBassTbn and sCTuba becomes `^-` (for example `e,,4^-\f r4 r4 | e,,4^- r4 r4 | …`). The Tbn 1·2 staff above already prints its tenutos above, so all three low-brass staves now match. In tB.ily: `cis'4^-\p r4 r4 | e'4^- r4 r4` (Vln II). The articulation itself is unchanged, so c124's tenuto (not accent) still holds.

### 3. Lower-voice horn tenutos below ledger lines: checked, no change
Bars: Hn 2 at 45–55, Hn 4 at 73–88, 201–208 and 211–214.
These tenutos sit at the end of the lower voice's down-stem, not under the notehead. At 800 dpi they are narrower than the ledger lines and more than a space below the last one, so they do not read as ledger lines. I decided against changing them, because the only fix would be stem lengths in shared two-voice staves (protected since c105).
