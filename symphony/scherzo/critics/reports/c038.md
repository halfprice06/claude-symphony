## Findings

### 1. The trio tune's climax has no dynamic shape in any of its four statements (161–164, 177–180, 217–220, 349–352). Applied.

**Problem.** Phrase 3 of T (cis–dis | e–gis | a. gis fis | cis b a) is the tune's highest point. Cycle 11 raised its peak to A5 (A6 in the violins) so the tune would have a climax. All four statements print one flat level through it: p in Cl 1 at 161; p/mp in Fl 1, Vln I and Vln II at 177; f in Fl 1, Cl 1, Vln I and Vln II at 217; p/pp in Fl 1, Vln I and Vln II at 349. The only hairpin anywhere in T is the diminuendo on its last note (223–224).

**Evidence.** Casanelles says notation cannot show a nonlinear dynamic curve (PDF 152), so the composer has to mark the points the player needs: where the swell starts, where it peaks, where it ends. The lyrical legato that composers want is one that moves "into a gentle swell on the note or arrival" (PDF 155). Fixed dynamic levels are what make an expressive line sound "lifeless" (PDF 149–150). Dvořák marks exactly this swell in his trio-like episode (woods.notesS.ily 40–47). The accompaniment should stay a transparent legato with no swell (PDF 155), so I marked only the melody voices. Hn 3·4, the violas, the harp, Hn 1·2 and the bassoons keep their flat levels.

**Fix (applied).** In each statement, put `\<` on the first note of phrase 3, `\>` on the dotted-crotchet peak, and `\!` on the first note of phrase 4. No target dynamic, so the swell stays within the passage's level (a messa di voce). Example, tA.ily `tAClOne`:
```
cis''2(\< dis''4 | e''4 gis''2) | a''4.(\> gis''8 fis''4) | cis''4( b'4 a'4) | gis'4\!( b'4 e''4 |
```
Same pattern in `tAVlnOne`, `tAVlnTwo`, `tAFluteOne` (its heterophony starts `cis'''4(\< b''8 cis''' dis'''4)`), `tCFluteOne`, `tCClOne`, `tCVlnOne`, `tCVlnTwo`, `codaFluteOne`, `codaVlnOne` and `codaVlnTwo`. Shared staves: Fl 2 rests at 169–184, 209–224 and 349–356, and Cl 2 rests at 209–224, so each combined staff carries one set of dynamics. No notes changed.

### 2. Theme A's second phrase rises to its peak (C6 at 24, and again at 264) at the same flat p as the 15 bars around it. Applied.

**Problem.** Bars 13–28 (and 253–268) are 16 bars of repeated staccato crotchet–quaver figures at p, with no dynamic event. Phrase 2 climbs F♯–G–A–B (23) to the accented C6 with a down-bow (24), the highest note of the theme. Its only marks are the accent and the bow sign. The violas and Vln II off-beats keep the same pp through it.

**Evidence.** Casanelles' round-robin passage: the same sound repeated at one level is heard as mechanical, and players vary their attacks "motivated by the idea of the musicality of a passage" (PDF 150, 152). The score should show where that variation goes. The notation point applies again: a peak that is not marked gets played flat (PDF 152). Dvořák's E-minor scherzo theme changes level every few bars (strings.notesS.ily 3–28).

**Fix (applied).** Mark a small swell in the melody only, and leave the accompaniment flat. sA.ily `sAVlnOne`, and the same in dA.ily `dAVlnOne`:
```
fis''4-.\<( g''8 a''8) b''4-. | c'''2->\dnb\>( b''8 fis''8) | e''4-.\! b'8-. b'-. e''4-. |
```
dA.ily `dAFluteOne` (the heterophony, so Fl 1 and Vln I shape the bars together):
```
fis'''8(\< g''' a''' g''' fis'''4) | c'''8(\> b'' ais'' b'' fis''4) | e'''4-.\! b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. |
```
Fl 2 rests at 253–272. The hairpin ends where the phrase's last statement begins (25/265), so the p leggiero returns before the tail G–F♯–F–E. This does not touch the protected tail or motive x.
