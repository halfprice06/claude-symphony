## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 181: the cadential 6/4 of B turns into a root-position B tonic in the cellos. APPLIED.
**Problem.** c093 moved the second statement of T to a cadence in B: ii6/5 of B over E (180), then the cadential 6/4 and V7 over F♯ (181–182), then B (183). The F♯ is only a pizzicato F♯1 in the basses on the downbeat. The bowed bass is the cello countermelody, which plays B2 for two beats and then D♯3 (`b,2( dis4`). Once the pizzicato dies, bar 181 sounds as B major in root position, then B/D♯, under the violins' D♯–F♯–B. The cellos hold the 4th of the 6/4 and leave it by an upward leap, while the violins resolve B→A♯ at 182. So the progression heard is ii6/5 → I → I6 → V4/3 → I6, not ii6/5 → V6/4 → V7 → I.
**Evidence.** PDF 152 §1: the cadential 6/4 "does not act as an inversion of I"; play it with a different bass "and hear how different the chord sounds". PDF 153 §3 and PDF 160 pt. 2: the 4th is dissonant and "must resolve downward by step". PDF 154 §5–6: the bass is the best tone to double, and the 6/4 "resolves over a stationary bass". PDF 148, rule 3: intermediate harmonies "do not lead to I". The first statement (165, harp and basses on B1) and the return (221, Vc/Bsn 2 on B2) keep the bass on 5̂. Only 181 loses it.
**Fix.**
```
tA.ily  tAVc  bar 181:  fis2.( | cis2 e4) | dis2.~ ...      (was b,2( dis4 | cis2 e4) | dis2.~ ...)
```
The cellos double the basses' F♯ for the whole bar. Their line is now E3–F♯3–C♯3–E3–D♯3: a step into the 6/4, and the 7th E3 still resolves to D♯3 at 183. They still move at 182 while T holds its B (c051). Every beat of 181 now has F♯ as the lowest sounding note (red.py: F♯1 | F♯3 | F♯3, was F♯1 | B2 | D♯3). I kept the violas' B3/D♯4 (c092's voicing). The 4th is now in the violas and the violins only, and the violins resolve it.

### 2. Bars 191–192: in the trio middle section the harp's IV–V moves every upper voice with the bass. APPLIED.
**Problem.** The call (185–188, C♯ minor) and the answer (189–192, B major) both end on IV–V. At 187→188 the harp's chord falls (A3 C♯4 F♯4 → G♯3 B♯3 D♯4) as the bass rises F♯1→G♯1, which is the textbook voicing. At 191→192 the harp's right hand rises in parallel, G♯4 B4 E5 → A♯4 C♯5 F♯5, and its left hand leaps down a seventh, E2→F♯1. The outer voices therefore move E/E → F♯/F♯ (octaves) and B/E → C♯/F♯ (fifths) by contrary motion, at pp in an exposed accompaniment. The basses rest at 192, so the harp alone carries both outer voices.
**Evidence.** PDF 136 §3: between IV and V "all four voices must proceed to a new tone … it is dangerously easy to produce parallel 5ths and octaves; to avoid them, lead the upper voices in contrary motion to the bass, as in Example 9-3a". Ex. 9-3b–c (image) show the parallel version as "not" and "even worse". Unit 5 (PDF 86, cited by c182) treats 5ths and octaves by contrary motion as best avoided.
**Fix.**
```
tB.ily  tBHarpR  bar 192:  r4 <fis' ais' cis''>4 <fis' ais' cis''>4    (was r4 <ais' cis'' fis''>4 <ais' cis'' fis''>4)
tB.ily  tBHarpL  bar 192:  fis,4 r4 r4                               (was fis,,4 r4 r4)
```
The bass now rises a step (E2→F♯2) and the three upper voices fall (G♯→F♯, B→A♯, E→C♯), as at 187–188. The pitch classes and the A♯ pedal are unchanged. The outer voices go from an octave to a twelfth by contrary motion, and par2.py shows nothing new. Cl 1's entering A♯4 at 192 is now in unison with the harp's A♯4. The chord sits under the cor anglais and cello F♯3–G♯3 line as before.

### Considered and not changed

PDF 150 §18 lets a melodic high point accent a weak bar. The E4 is the call's peak after the rising sixth (c010) and the goal of the \< into p, so bar 4 is accented. The horn call is protected, and c030 places the pizzicato B at 5.
- Bar 221 (and 353–355): the violas double the 4th (E4) and move it up to F♯4 while the tune resolves E→D♯ (PDF 154: never double the 4th). c043 (A&S Unit 19) allows one doubled 4th to rise in a free texture, and c092 chose this voicing to avoid doubling D♯. I tested violas B3/G♯4 → A3/F♯4 (8–7, 6–5). It leaves the 4th only in the tune's third beat, so the 6/4 would lose its dissonance for two beats. I kept the current voicing.
- 76→77 and 83→84: ii → I6 in F (rule 3, PDF 148). At 84 the F/A is the Neapolitan 6 of E minor, an intermediate harmony to V at 89, so in E it follows. 77 rests on the rising bass F–G–A, which is protected (c122: the C-major rule excludes V4/3 of F, which would contain C–E–G).

- 26→27, 118→119 (theme A's fourth phrase): i6 → 6/4 with no intermediate harmony. Review pt. 7 (PDF 160) allows tonic harmony before the 6/4. Theme A's accompaniment is protected.
- 151: the harp's E-major chord over B1, then E at 153. This is a bass arpeggiation under one tonic chord (A&S Unit 19 arpeggiating 6/4), not a cadential 6/4.
- 335→336: Hn 3 E4 rises to F♯4 while Tpt 1 resolves E4→D♯4. This is c043's allowance, inside the protected coda build 333–340.
