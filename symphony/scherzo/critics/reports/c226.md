## Findings

### 1. Bars 305–312: Hn 4 plays eight ff bars in the low register under one slur (applied)

**Problem.** In the reprise tutti Hn 4 holds the bass line E3–E3–E3–B2–E3–A2–B2–E3 (written B3 down to E3) under one slur from 305 to 312, ff from the arrival at 304. That is 7⅔ bars, about 5.2 s, at ff in the horn's lowest working register, with no rest or breath mark. Hn 4 has just held 301–304 p<ff (with c136's breath at 304). Hn 3 on the same staff breaks its slur after 309. In the exposition (105–112) Hn 4 doubles Hn 3 and breaks with it (5 + 3 bars), so the reprise is the only place where the low horn has to play eight loud bars on one breath.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292: "all slurred phrases are performed on one breath. In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath … it does take more breath to play loudly than softly." PDF 309 repeats that everything under a slur is one breath. PDF 303: the low register is dark and works when sustained, which is why these notes are held, but held ff low notes use the most air.

**Fix (applied).** dC.ily, `dCHnFour`:
```
dCHnFour = { e2.( | e2. | e2. | b,2. | e2.) \once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN \breathe | a,2.( | b,2. | e2) r4 | …
```
The break falls where Hn 3 breaks (309/310), so the shared staff shows one phrase shape, and the comma sits below the staff as at 316 (c046). Pitches and dynamics unchanged.

### 2. Bars 339–344: Hn 1 goes from ff to an exposed p dolce solo in 2.4 s, then has only 12 bars before the ending (applied)

**Problem.** All four horns play ff from 333 to 339 (Hn 1 on B4–C5, written F♯5–G5, the bright register). After the G.P. (bar 340, about 1.9 s with the fermata, plus the rest on beat 3 of 339) Hn 1 alone plays the trio tune's head p dolce at 341–344. It then rests 345–356 (about 9.5 s) before the most exposed horn music in the movement: the motto tail at 357–360, held into 366, and the last G♯4 from 368 to the final fermata (about 11–12 s at ppp). Hn 3 played the same ff bars but lower (E4–F♯4, written B4–C♯5), and from 341 it rests until its cover note at 365.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292: brass need "frequent intervals of rest … so their lips can recuperate and they can catch their breath". PDF 303: it is "most taxing to play in the higher register all the time, so periodic rests are most welcome". PDF 303–304: I and III are the high horns, so Hn 3 is the right player for a first-horn solo. The phrase sits in the middle register (C♯4–A4 sounding), where any of the high horns sounds the same. c156 moved the reprise second idea from Hn 1 to Hn 3 for the same reason. c136 counted 341 among entries "with rests before them", but the rest there is the G.P., under 2.5 s.

**Fix (applied).** coda.ily: the four bars move from `codaHnOne` to `codaHnThree` with the same notes, slur, p and "dolce".
```
codaHnOne   = { … | b'2--\> r4\! | R2. | R2.*16 | b'2.(\p\< … (motto tail unchanged)
codaHnThree = { … | e'2--\> r4\! | R2. | cis'2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" d'4 | e'2 a'4 | gis'4. fis'8 e'4 | d'4 cis'2) | R2.*20 | gis'2.~\pp … (cover unchanged)
```
Hn 1 now rests from 340 to 356 (about 15 s). On the Hn 3·4 staff the part-combiner prints "3." over the solo (checked on page 12). Hn 4 rests there, so the staff has only one player. Bassoon, oboe and violin hand-overs at 345 and 349 are unchanged.

There are no further findings. The rest of the brass writing follows the reading. Ranges and registers fit Adler's charts. The trumpets stay in their clear middle register and play only motive x and tutti rhythm. Trombones and tuba move one note per bar or hold, and rest for most of the movement. Brass attacks are shaped by tenuto, slurs and dynamics with no accents. There are no mutes except the "senza sord." reminder, and no stopped notes, glissandi or trills.
