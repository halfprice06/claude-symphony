# Critic 090: v90, lens 10 (cyclic design and the arc of four movements)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 17 "The Orchestra as Accompanist" (adler2 PDF 256–290). I read every text page and looked at every example page as an image (PDF 258–289; Ex. XVII-1 to XVII-25). The chapter is about writing for a soloist: how to introduce a solo colour, how to keep the accompaniment out of its way, and how to give it pitch support. For this lens I applied it to the scherzo's solo moments that carry the cycle: Hn 1's G♯ and horn call at 1–7, the timpani's motive x, the motto tail at 357–360, and the last G♯ that the finale takes over.

## Findings

### 1. 371–375: the closing timpani roll repeats movement I's last scoring (applied)
**Problem.** The scherzo ends like this: timpani motive x at 363 and 367, an E roll ppp at 372–374, then a harp chord and a last stroke at 375, all under Vln I's held G♯5. Movement I closes the same way: two soft timpani pulses (I 179–180), a ppp roll (181–182) and a harp chord under Vln I's held C5. Movement II's thread took the roll and the harp chord out of its own ending for exactly this reason (II c050, c120: "movement II ended in the same scoring a second time"). So III now brings back the close that II deliberately avoided, and in the same order: pulse, pulse, roll.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 263: the composer chooses a colour so that it "is very fresh, even after the first movement has been heard". That is a rule about colour across movements, and it applies to how a movement ends as well as how it begins. Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13, Beethoven, Violin Concerto i): the timpani's job in that passage is to play the motive between phrases, not to sustain. The scherzo's timpani are motivic all through the movement (Beethoven 9/ii is its model). Its last soft bars should keep that identity.
**Fix (applied).** In codaTimp, the bars after the statement at 367 change from `| R2.*3 | e,2.:32\ppp~ | e,2.:32~ | e,2.:32 | e,4 r4 r4 |` to `| R2.*2 | b,4\ppp b,8 b,8 e,4 | R2.*3 | e,4 r4 r4 |`. Motive x now sounds every four bars (363, 367, 371), then the stroke at 375 with the harp harmonics and the pizzicato E. The last sound, the final tuning and everything the finale relies on stay the same. G♯ now sounds alone over 372–374 before the E at 375 confirms the key. That makes the pivot pitch even more exposed as the finale's A♭. HISTORY's "keep the closing page" entries (c015/c016 engraving, c086 horns) are not affected: the horns are untouched and the page count is still 13.

### 2. 4–7: a second horn sits under the quoted horn call (applied)
**Problem.** Hn 1 quotes movement I's horn call at 3–6 (G♯3–E4–D♯4, protected). Hn 2 holds B3 under it at 4–7, on the same staff, in the same colour and a third to a fourth below. In movement I the call is a single horn (I 13–14: Hn 2–4 rest; the viola supplies the harmony). c060 took Hn 2 out from under the motto tail at 357–360 for the same reason ("a single horn line, as movement I's horn call"). The opening quotation had not been brought into line.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 264 (Ex. XVII-9): "Mozart never uses the orchestral horns while the solo horn is playing ... so as not to interfere with the solo sound." Adler PDF 274 (Ex. XVII-15): a bassoon in its middle register against the solo gives clarity by contrast of colour.
**Fix (applied).** `introHnTwo = { R2.*12 | }` and `introBsnOne = { R2.*3 | b2.~\p\> | b2.~ | b2.~\pp | b2.~ | b2 r4 | R2.*4 | }`. Bsn 1 already held B3 at 7–8. It now holds the same B3 from bar 4, one note that is common to E major (4–6) and E minor (7–8). The harmony is unchanged, Hn 1's slur and notes are unchanged, there is still no E4 at 7–8 (c040), and the call's D♯4 stays unresolved for the finale's Hn 3 (IV 219–220).

### 3. 9–10: the timpani answer movement II's last timpani figure; record it (applied as a comment)
**Problem.** The intro comment calls motive x "a melodic timpani figure, still unused in I and II". That is wrong. Movement II's last timpani sound is a solo melodic answer: E♭3–C3–A♭2, the falling triad 5–3–1, pp (II 84–85, c040/c120). The scherzo's first figure for timpani alone (9–10) is B2–(B–B)–G2 | E2: the same 5–3–1 a major third lower, and A♭→E is the key relation of II and III. The finale's timpani then quote motive x a major third lower again, on G (IV 143–145). This is a real chain across three movements. Because nothing records it, a later critic could change B–G–E at 9–10 without knowing.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 261–262: material passes between foreground and background roles; here the timpani carry the figure from movement to movement. Adler PDF 273: the timpani as the instrument that joins phrases.
**Fix (applied).** I corrected the comment in intro.ily (it names II 84–85, the major-third step and IV 143–145, and says to keep B–G–E at 9–10). The composer should also add one line under "Themes quoted from earlier movements" in coherence/scherzo.md: "Timpani 9–10 B2–G2–E2: movement II's last timpani figure E♭3–C3–A♭2 (II 84–85) a major third lower; the finale's motive x on G (143–145) continues the chain." The note should be sent to the finale thread for information. No notes change.
