## What else I studied

**Bruckner, Symphony No. 3, iii.** The corpus has no Bruckner score, so I used my knowledge of the score and the following web sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Bruckner)), the [Gramophone introduction by Markus Poschner](https://gramophone.co.uk/articles/bruckner-s-symphony-no-3-an-introduction-by-markus-poschner), the [Audiophile Audition Thielemann review](https://www.audaud.com/?p=75719) and the [IMSLP page](https://cn.imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.3_in_D_minor_(Bruckner,_Anton)). The scherzo is in D minor, 3/4, beaten in one. It opens with a pp string ostinato over a D pedal, with the bass always plucked or held under it. A steady crescendo leads to a ff unison on D and A for the full orchestra. The trio is an A-major Ländler, a dialogue between violas and violins. The scherzo ends in D major.

Measured against it, v529 already uses the same devices:
- an ostinato build over a pedal: 89–104 on V, and the coda stretto at 325–336;
- a ff tutti statement of the head after a long soft stretch: 105;
- a Ländler-like trio with a plucked bass on the downbeat and chords on beats 2–3: harp and basses at 153;
- a dialogue in the trio: Ob 1 and cellos at 185–192;
- a close in the tonic major.

Bruckner's ff tutti also puts the theme in unison brass, and the cyclic rules forbid that (soft brass; no hammered tutti; trumpets only in motive x). Bruckner keeps a bass under his soft opening the whole time. Here, the only bars in the scherzo's exposition with no bass below the bassoons are the first wind call at 29–30. Finding 1 deals with those bars. A conductor who knew Bruckner 3 would accept this movement as the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 29–30: the first wind call has no bass (applied)

**Problem.** In the counterstatement, the winds' call at 29–30 (Fl/Ob theme, Cl E4–G4, Bsn E3–B3) has nothing below E3. The plucked bass has sounded on every downbeat for sixteen bars (13–28). At 29 it stops, and it comes back arco only at 31. The second call (33–34) has Bsn 2's A2 as a bass, so the two calls are not set up the same way. A listener hears the harmony lift off the floor at 29. That is the only point in the exposition where this happens.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 121: "Violoncellos and double basses playing more or less important pizz. notes are often added to wood-wind instruments (tutti) … this process renders the sustained notes in the wood-wind more distinct." The passage cites Tsar Saltan [14], [17], [26], which are Ex. 182–184.
- Ex. 183 (PDF 351) puts plucked strings on the beats under the wind and brass statement of the march.
- Ex. 186 (PDF 353), the softest of the five scorings of that march, keeps a plucked bass under the flute and clarinets.
- Bruckner 3/iii keeps the bass under its soft opening throughout.

**Fix (built in /tmp/critic-529/work/sections/sA.ily).** sACb bar 29: `R2.` becomes `e,,4\pp r4 r4`. The basses are still pizz. there (pizz. since 13), and they have five beats to take the bow for the arco E1 at 31, which already carries its label.

```
sACb = { ... | b,,4 r4 r4 | e,,4 r4 r4 | e,,4\pp r4 r4 | R2. | e,,2.\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | b,,2. | ...
```

The cellos stay silent, so the strings' answer at 31 still comes in as a new colour, and the call and response that was kept since c007 is unchanged above the bass. One pp pluck does not turn the call into a string phrase. No cue is affected: the basses play at 27–28 and 31, and parts-cues.json has no cue in these bars.

**Build.** 0 warnings. 13 pages. "part lengths: all match". C-major triads: none. Theme record 52/52 unchanged. analysis.txt shows the same single outer-voice parallel as v529 (bar 69, accepted since v01).

**Why this is the best candidate.** I tested the reading and the master against every section. The score follows all the other reading principles (table). Bruckner's larger devices are either already present or forbidden by the cyclic rules: the unison brass theme, and an ostinato added under 89–94 (which is protected with the timpani's motive x and pizz.). This is the one place where a reading rule is broken in a way a listener can hear. It is a small change, and I do not claim more for it.

### 2. Bars 161–164: harp off-beats removed by c524 (considered, no change)

Rimsky's operation d) (PDF 118) supports changing the scoring from one statement of an idea to the next, not inside a phrase. Here the change happens inside T's first statement. Still, under Cl 1 the horns' fifth and the plucked bass give a complete chord in each bar. The cadence phrase gets the off-beats back, and the third phrase is the period's contrasting phrase. The result is not worse than v519, so I left it.

### 3. Bars 101–104 and 333–338: horn weight in the mixed brass chord (considered, no change)

PDF 102 asks for twice as many horns as trumpets or trombones. Here each horn plays its own note. PDF 103 allows the alternative of marking the horns a degree louder. The score does this (Hn p< against Tpt/Tbn pp<, f against f). The rule is met.

### 4. Bars 29–36 versus Bruckner's continuous ostinato (considered, rejected)

Bruckner would run the strings' crotchets on through the wind calls. That would remove the call and response, which has been kept since c007, and it would make the opening's bare statement (13–28, c259/c359) sound the same as the counterstatement. Finding 1 takes the one part of Bruckner's practice that fits: the bass is present throughout.
