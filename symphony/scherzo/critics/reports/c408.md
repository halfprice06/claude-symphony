## Findings

**1. Applied. 101–104: the wood-wind enter f with the strings, so the first long build of the movement is a plateau at f, not a crescendo by addition.**
- Bars: 101–104 (sB.ily), the "ritmo di quattro battute" hemiola into the first tutti at 105.
- Problem: from 93 to 100 only strings and timpani play, pp to mp. At 101 the strings reach f, and all ten wood-wind parts (Fl 1·2, Ob 1·2, C.a., Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn) enter f at the same moment. The wood-wind's crescendo over the four bars is only f → ff. The brass are the only group that grows from below (horns p<, trumpets, trombones and tuba pp<).
- Evidence:
  - PDF 130: a prolonged crescendo is obtained "by the gradual addition of other instruments in the following order: strings, wood-wind, brass". At 101 the second group arrives at full weight with the first.
  - PDF 136: re-entries after a long rest should be pp or ff; "piano and forte re-entries are less successful", and the rule "is capable of wider application". The flutes and contrabassoon have rested since 72, the clarinets since 88, the oboes since 94.
  - PDF 129a: the strings attack while the wood-wind begin piano. Ex. 256 (PDF 419) shows the wind and horns entering pp cresc. poco under the strings.
  - The score's own reprise of the same bars (301–304, dB.ily) already does this: wood-wind p<, strings sfp<, brass p</pp<. Nothing in HISTORY.md records a reason for the exposition to differ. c028 asked only for "crescendo in all groups", and that still holds.
- Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-408/work/sections/sB.ily; one dynamic per part on the downbeat of 101, `\f\<` → `\p\<`):
  ```
  sBFluteOne   b''2->\p\< a''4->~ ...
  sBFluteTwo   fis''2.--(\p\< ...
  sBOboeOne    fis''2.--(\p\< ...
  sBOboeTwo    dis''2.--(\p\< ...
  sBCorAnglais b'2->\p\< a'4->~ ...
  sBClOne      a'2->\p\< fis'4->~ ...
  sBClTwo      dis'2.--(\p\< ...
  sBBsnOne     b2.--(\p\< ...
  sBBsnTwo     b,2.--(\p\< ...
  sBCbsn       b,,2.--(\p\< ...
  ```
  Strings stay f< and brass and timpani are unchanged. The ff at 104 in all wood-wind is unchanged. Shared staves stay equal (both players p at 101).
- What a listener hears: at 101 the violins carry the hemiola alone at f. The wood-wind colour swells in under them over four bars, then the brass, so the tutti at 105 arrives as the end of a built crescendo, as it does at 305. Winds doubling a string line now start below it, as the score does at 45, 125, 179, 197 and 253 (c405's do-not-touch). The hemiola accents stay, so the syncopation still speaks.

**2. Kept. Horns p< at 101, 301 and 332, against PDF 136's pp.** Under principle 19 these are "less successful" re-entries. c076 raised the horns a degree at 101/301 so they are heard under the f strings, and the trumpets, trombones and tuba already supply the pp layer under them. After finding 1 the wood-wind also start p, so the horns are no longer under a full f wall. Making them pp would leave them inaudible at the start of the build. No change.

**3. Kept. No grace-note glides (PDF 129–130, Ex. 254).** Rimsky's anacrusis glide is a Russian accent figure. Where this score stresses a note, the approach is already a written scale: theme A's climb to C6 at 23–24 and 263–264, the coda's quavers into B6 at 335–336. The other accented arrivals, at 72, 300 and 339, are 2–4-note non div. string chords, and PDF 130 rules out a string glide into those. A wood-wind glide at 72 would land on the ff chord that c108, c208 and c308 kept dying in one bar, and it would add a gesture foreign to the Brahms–Dvořák models. No change.

**4. Kept. 72: the ff A chord dies in one bar.** This is the sf-dim of PDF 129 done by the players, a short diminuendo (PDF 130). The horns' C♯→C join into 73 is protected (c110, c308). No change.

**5. Kept. Low brass f at 105 and 333, horns f at 71 and 299.** These sit inside a tutti. Dan's soft-brass rule forbids ff attacks (c123), and the trombones and tuba now reach f by a pp< crescendo (c393). No change.

**6. Kept. Hn 1 p at 357 (the motto tail) over a pp accompaniment.** PDF 133 puts the basis below the melody, and pp would set the horn at the level of its basis. The pitches are protected. No change.

**7. Checked. Vln II's new arpeggios at 169–184 (v408).** They are pp dolce under the cellos' mp espress. T, an ornamental basis moving on its own (PDF 133), and a level under the melody. They follow the principle. No change.
