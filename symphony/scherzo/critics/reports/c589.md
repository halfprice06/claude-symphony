## Holistic verdict

A conductor would accept most of this score as the work of a skilled composer. It has:

- a clear motivic design (motive x, the head, the G♯/G♮ third);
- a real ritmo di tre battute and ritmo di quattro battute, both modelled on Beethoven;
- a strings-only fugato;
- a trio that keeps G♯ as a common tone.

The weakest page is 89–100. The plan copies Beethoven's ritmo passage: a three-bar head, the drum answering in its own bar, pizz. B underneath. But the dynamics run pp, pp, p < mf. So the passage that should be the movement's joke is its least audible stretch. In Beethoven the drum answers f against p woodwinds, and that contrast makes the passage memorable. The A&S literature examples make the same point in their own terms: Beethoven's sudden f/p and pp/ff contrasts (Ex. 22-1, PDF 373; Ex. 27-3, PDF 464), and the Hammerklavier's abrupt inflection, "which softening would weaken" (PDF 584).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 91 and 94: the timpani answers should be f, not pp (applied)

**Problem.** c327 placed the drum's motive x in the landing bar of each head (91, 94, 97), and that placement is right. At pp, however, a B2 drum stroke over pp pizz. B1/B2 is masked by the pizz. strings. The dialogue that c327 argued for (Adler II PDF 257–260, 273) cannot be heard.

**Evidence.**
- Beethoven 9/ii bars 195–205: the drum answers f in its own bars, the woodwinds answer p, and the strings play pizz. It is the model c019 and c327 cite.
- A&S literature examples: the abrupt f/p contrast (PDF 373, 464), and inflections that should not be softened (PDF 584).
- In bars 91 and 94 the drum sounds with only the pizz. bass (the viola and oboe heads end on the downbeat of 91 and 94). Nothing else in the texture can cover it, so f does not upset the balance.

**Fix.** In `sBTimp`:
- bar 91: `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4` becomes `b,4\f b,8 b,8 b,4`;
- bar 94: `b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4` becomes `b,4\f b,8 b,8 b,4`;
- bar 97 keeps `\p`, which now reads as subito p;
- 98–100 `< mf` and the roll at 101 `p<` stay as they were.

The heads at 92 (Ob 1 pp) and 95 (Vln I p) now enter p after the drum's f, as Beethoven's woodwinds do.

**Notes on the build.**
- I first tried a written accent and a "sub." marking. Together they made page 3 over-full (build warning: "page 3 has been compressed"), so I removed both. Beethoven also writes plain f at 195.
- The build is clean.

**Protections.** The do-not-touch item (timpani motive x with pizz. at 89–100) and c327's reason (the drum's own bar, joining the phrases) are kept: placement, rhythm and instrumentation are unchanged. c327 only recorded its dynamics as "unchanged" and gave no reason for them. Nothing sustained and no brass is added before 101 (c244, c559, c576).

**Cues.** No line was moved, silenced or added. The cues at 89, 92 and 97 (Vla., Ob. 1, Timp., Vln. I) still point at sounding instruments.

### 3. Bars 45–55: the second idea starts with the bass (no change)

A&S PDF 327 says a melody that starts before the bass gives character. An anacrusis on 44.3 would apply that. I did not build it, for three reasons:
- it changes a recorded theme;
- c027 deliberately made the idea homorhythmic as a foil to theme A;
- a single upbeat crotchet in five octaves is less audible than Finding 1.

### 4. Bars 57–68: woodwind punctuation in the fugato (no change)

Beethoven punctuates his fugato with pp woodwind crotchets. The strings-only exposition is protected, and its reason holds: the fugato's first full-orchestra moment should be 69. Adding woodwinds would weaken the entry at 69.

### 5. Bars 101–104: an augmented 6th over the B pedal (rejected)

A&S PDF 503 places the augmented 6th just before a structural point, but resolving to V, not over it. Over a dominant pedal it would only be a passing chord. It would also blur the pure V7 of the protected hemiola build.

### 6. Carrying the ♭9 through 101–104 (rejected)

A&S PDF 463 shows the 9th lasting the whole dominant. But c532 resolves the ♭9 (C) at 100→101 as a neighbour (also on PDF 463). Holding it would undo that resolution and blur the protected V7 hemiola build. No gain.

### 7. Bars 336–340: the summit V, then i and the G.P. (no change)

This matches A&S PDF 464 (Op. 31/1: chords set off by silence, then the tonic) and PDF 584 (silence after the inflection). It is correct as it stands.
