## Findings

### 1. Bars 148–153: the trio's background starts on the same downbeat as its tune; set the drone first (applied)

**Problem.** The link ends with Hn 1's G♯4 at 145–148 over bassoons and bass clarinet. All of them stop on beat 2 of 148. Bars 149–152 (poco rit., about 3.5 s) then hold nothing at all: one pizzicato or timpani stroke and one rolled harp chord at 149 and again at 151, with silence between. At 153 four layers start together: Cl 1 with T, the harp's off-beat figure, the Hn 3·4 E–B drone and the steady pizzicato bass. The colour change at 148→149 is a seam, and the tune has no established background to enter into.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 192: where the colour changes, "make certain that the seams don't show". At 148 the horn sound stops, and the next sustained sound starts at 153.
- PDF 263: Dvořák and Tchaikovsky "begin with the woodwind choir before the string soloist enters. The solo color is therefore very fresh". The background colour comes first and the solo colour second.
- PDF 268–270: Sibelius sets the muted violins before the solo enters, and Bartók sets the strings, timpani and held clarinet chord before the piano. In both, "the soloist is introduced… in a manner that highlights his best characteristics because of the sparse and transparent texture in which the entrance is couched".
- PDF 190: the soft layer that holds the music together enters "unperceived" before the change.
- Corpus: Dvořák 9/iii (Mutopia score pp. 5–6) gives the trio's accompaniment four bars on its own before the tune.

**Fix (applied).** Hn 3·4 take up the drone at 149, four bars early, at the drone's own pp. Hn 3 holds B3 and Hn 4 holds E3, with the breath at the end of the four-bar group as at 156, 160 and 164. The pp at 153 is no longer restated, since the drone has sounded since 149 with only a beat's breath (c165's one-dynamic rule).

The drone enters as Hn 1 leaves, so the horn sound is continuous across 148→149. The harp's rolled E chords at 149 and 151 now sound over a held floor rather than in silence. At 153 only the tune and the harp's regular off-beats are new. This does not affect the protected harmony of the link (c253: a plain V). At 151 the drone E3/B3 sounds the same E-over-B sonority as the harp chord and the B1 bass. It also keeps c151's break at 166 and the drone under V inside the phrases (c262). Hn 3·4 have rested since 132, so the four extra bars cost nothing in endurance.

### 2. Reprise tutti 305–320: cut the second phrase (313–320)? Considered and rejected

This was the largest structural option I examined. The reprise runs ff from 297 to 320 (24 bars, about 16 s), dips for 8 bars (321–328), and reaches the coda's peak at 333, which opens with theme A's head again. Cutting 313–320 would remove 5.4 s of material already heard at 113–120. I rejected it:
- The second phrase carries theme A's peak C6 (316) and the iv colour. The coda's B6 at 336 is the movement's top note, but it quotes only the head.
- Adler's Mozart example (PDF 267–268) asks for a reduction between a tutti and the solo's entry, not a shorter tutti. The 8-bar dip at 321–328 and the ostinato build (Bartók, PDF 270–272) already give the coda's peak its preparation.
- In Dvořák 9/iii the da capo also keeps its full tutti before the coda.
- A cut would renumber 321–376, which the finale and movement I records cite (closing timpani 363–371, motto tail 357–360, last G♯).

Decision: keep.

### 3. The coda's slow stage, 341–376 (33 s, 12% of the movement): kept

It follows Adler's rules for pacing. The T recall changes colour every four bars (Hn 3, then Ob 1, then Cl 1 with violins; PDF 288). The horn solo has no other horn under it (PDF 264). The close colours one held G♯ through several instruments (PDF 191). The fast stage is short (11 s), but it ends on the only fff and B6, and the G.P. at 340 separates the stages. The ending is the protected cyclic pivot. Decision: keep.

### 4. Bars 120→121 and 276→277: sub. p without an overlapping chord: kept

Adler's forte-subito-piano technique (PDF 190, Ex. XV-36) is for a held chord. Here the ff phrase ends on a staccato crotchet and two beats' rest. The sub. p then starts a new phrase after a caesura, so the overlap does not apply. Decision: keep.

### 5. Bar 140: a full bar of silence before the link: kept

By Adler's rule on seams (PDF 192), this is a seam. It is a deliberate one. It completes the four-bar group 137–140 after the dying motive x, and it marks the double bar at J. Adler allows seams that are meant to be heard. Unlike 149–152, it does not come straight before a tune. Decision: keep.

### 6. Trio first statement: the static background is now four bars longer (149–164): checked

With finding 1 the drone sounds for 16 bars before its break at 166. The harp's figure still starts at 153, so the full musette figure is no longer than before. The drone is a pedal under a tonic prolongation, and c243 kept its 12 unchanged bars on the strength of Adler's Verdi example (PDF 278). Decision: acceptable as applied.
