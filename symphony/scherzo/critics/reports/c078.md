## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Trio tune at the forte return (209-224) and Bsn 1's countermelody (345-348): wind slurs copy the violins' bowings (applied)

**Problem.** c069 gave the winds one slur per four-bar phrase wherever they play the trio tune (153-168, 197-200, 341-356), but two places were missed. Cl 1 at 209-224 and Fl 1 at 217-224 still have the violins' bow slurs: `gis'2( a'4 | b'2 e''4) | dis''4.( cis''8 b'4) | a'4( gis'2)`. Bsn 1's countermelody at 345-348 still has the cellos' bowing from 173-176. Adler (PDF 187) says slurred notes are played legato on one breath and separate notes are tongued. Read that way, the clarinet re-tongues at 211 and 212, and the bassoon at 347, in the middle of a phrase. The violins change bow there without any audible break. The tune is the same at 197-200 in the same clarinet, where it has one slur, so the articulation is now inconsistent between two statements eight bars apart.

**Fix (tC.ily, coda.ily).** Notes, dynamics and hairpins are unchanged; only the slurs change:

### 2. Fl 2 at ff in its weak register in both tuttis (109-112, 117-120, 309-312, 317-320) (applied)

**Problem.** In these bars Fl 1 carries theme A's upper octave (E6-A6) alone. Fl 2 plays the lower octave at B4-A5, together with Vln I, Vln II, Ob 1, the cor anglais and Cl 2, under ff brass. Adler's chart (PDF 185) calls B4-E5 "sweet and with little carrying power". The same page says a weak register cannot be pushed louder: "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume out of his instrument in that register". So the ff marking produces nothing audible there. Meanwhile the brilliant upper octave, which the flute does project in, has only one player. PDF 192 lists unison doubling as a normal use of two flutes.

**Fix (sC.ily, dC.ily).** Fl 2 plays Fl 1's notes in bars 5-8 and 13-16 of each section, an octave up from its current notes. For example, bars 5-8 become `e'''4-. b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. | a'''2->( gis'''8 g'''8 | fis'''4-.) dis'''8-. dis'''-. b''4-. | e'''4-. r4 r4`. The flutes are now in unison from 105 to 120, so the part-combiner prints a single a2 line with no new labels. The top note is A6. B6 stays reserved for 336, and 105-108 (which would reach B6) are untouched. Fl 2 is not in the theme record.

### 3. Second idea 45-52: Ob 1 at f on the lower octave (applied)

**Problem.** The melody is Vln I f espress. on D6-A6, with Fl 1 doubling at mf (c067: a doubling wind sits a degree under the violins). The lower octave is Vln II and Ob 1, and Ob 1 is marked f. That puts the oboe at full strength in what its chart calls its "warm, prominent, reedy and poignant" register (PDF 198). Adler (PDF 201) warns that the oboe "can add great articulation and cutting edge to any instrument with which it is combined". Care is therefore needed "so that an instrument that needs to be featured more prominently is not overshadowed". The oboe now has the stronger doubling on the secondary octave and the flute the softer one on the melody.

**Fix (sA.ily, bar 45).** `\partCombineAutomatic d''2->(\mf\< b'4~`, replacing `\f\<`. The hairpins stay. Ob 2 rests here, so the shared staff has only one dynamic.

### 4. Coda 345-348: the violas' held chord covers Bsn 1's countermelody (applied)

**Problem.** Bsn 1's p dolce countermelody (A3-F♯3-D3-D♯3-E3-G♯3-B3) begins in unison with the violas' held A3 (345) and reaches their G♯3 at 348. It also lies inside the harp's pp chords (C♯3-B3). The violas are pp, one degree below the bassoon, in the same register and partly on the same notes. Adler (PDF 226): when accompanied, the bassoon "has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments"; for a bassoon solo, "care must be taken to mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist". At 233-236 the accompaniment is also pp, but nothing doubles the bassoon there, so I left it alone.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaVla).** Bar 345 `a2.\ppp`; bar 349 `<e' gis'>2.\pp^\markup \italic "div."`. The second mark restores c068's pp under the tune at 349. The bassoon keeps its p dolce, so Ob 1's tune (p) stays the main line.
