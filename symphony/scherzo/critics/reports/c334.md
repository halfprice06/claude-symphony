## Findings

All are checked points; none is applied.

### 1. Bars 333–338, 101–104, 301–304: single horns ff against trumpets f (Adler PDF 15). Keep.
The rule says to pair the horns above mf. c244 kept the single horns because the extra dynamic level makes up the difference. The reading gives a further reason that c244 did not use: Adler states that the two-horns-to-one-trumpet equation "does not hold true if the trumpet is in a low register". In this score the trumpets never go above B4 (concert) and at 333–338 they sit at E4–B4 and D♯4–A4, the low-middle register, where a trumpet at f is no stronger than one horn at ff. Pairing the horns at 333–335 (B4, G4, E4, B3) would remove two chord tones from the horns for no gain in balance, and a higher brass dynamic is ruled out by Dan's rule. Keep.

### 2. Bars 305–320: the reprise's tenor countermelody has one horn (Adler PDF 15, 44). Keep.
At 105–120 Hn 3·4 play the tenor line a2, as Bartók's paired horns do; at 305–320 Hn 4 holds the bass an octave up (c115) and Hn 3 is alone, with Bsn 1 and the cellos. Against the trumpets' f motive x that is half the horn weight. I checked whether the octave above the bass could move elsewhere so Hn 4 returns to the tenor line: Bsn 2 already holds E2 below it, the bass clarinet would be at the bottom of its strength at E3 under ff woodwinds, and the reprise has no trombones (c033), so Hn 4 is the only brass under the trumpets. Ex. XI-1 shows every loud brass chord with its own bass; taking Hn 4 away leaves the reprise brass chord without one. The full cello section on G3–E4 carries the line. Keep.

### 3. Bars 105–120, 305–320: horns and trumpets "stuck on tonic and dominant" (Adler PDF 17). Keep.
Hn 1 plays B4 on most beats and Hn 2 E4. I mapped them against the bass (E E E B E A B E | A G♯ A B E G B E): at 110, 113 and 115 (iv) Hn 1 moves to C5, at 111 and 119 (V) Hn 2 to D♯4 and F♯4, at 116 Hn 1 to F♯4. The horns keep common tones and move where the chord changes, which is what valve horns allow and what Adler contrasts with natural-horn writing. The trumpets' pitches were fitted the same way by c064 (A4 at 113/115, D♯4 at 111, F♯4 at 119). Keep.

### 4. Bars 333–335: the low brass hold a pedal, not a new thematic element (Tchaikovsky, Adler PDF 38–41). Keep.
Tchaikovsky's trombones carry a cantus firmus at the recapitulation's tutti. Here the trombones and tuba hold E–E–B, a pedal. A melodic line in the trombones would be a trombone melody, which c244 and the cyclic plan keep for the finale's brass chorale. The coda's new element is already present: the horns' B4–C5–B4 ♭9 neighbour at 336–338 (c083) and Vln I's B6, the movement's peak, with the timpani roll entering at 332 as in Tchaikovsky. Keep.

### 5. Bars 89–100: brass could punctuate the timpani's motive x (Hindemith, Adler PDF 42–44). Keep.
Hindemith ends his brass fugato with the timpani stating the subject and the brass striking in its rests. c327 already made the timpani answer the head entries at 91, 94 and 97. Brass strokes here would bring the trombones in before 101, where their pp entry is the Schubert-style saved colour (row 10), and would cover the pp viola and violin head entries. The punctuation the reading describes happens from 101: trumpets take motive x and the trombones enter under the timpani roll. Keep.

### 6. Bar 339: final brass chord spacing (Adler Ex. XI-1, PDF 15–16). Keep.
E1 (tuba), E2 (bass trombone), B2 (Tbn 2), G3 (Tbn 1), B3 (Hn 4), E4 (Hn 2, Hn 3, Tpt 2), G4 (Tpt 1), B4 (Hn 1). Octave and fifth at the bottom, closer spacing above, the root tripled in the middle, the third present twice. This matches the ff layouts of Ex. XI-1. The horns sit above the trumpets at the top (enclosure), which Adler lists among the usable layouts. Keep.
