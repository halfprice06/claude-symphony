## Findings

Every principle in the table is followed or does not apply. The tonal plan is the reading's own. The large plan follows Points 7: I–III–passing IV–V, with the ♭II episode standing over the IV bass. The trio is Ex. 26-17, a prolonged V with VI and IV as neighbours. The coda follows §16, I–IV–I. As the focus asks, I looked for the one change in this reading that a listener would hear most, and built it (Finding 1). It makes the music worse for a reason the reading itself gives, so this cycle ends with no score change. The other findings record why the main form decisions still hold under a sixth reading, so that later cycles need not reopen them.

### 1. Fugato 64→65: leading tone before the A-minor entry. Built and rejected (best candidate)

**Candidate.** Ex. 26-10 and §10 (PDF 450–451) show the leading tone brought in before an entry. In the reprise, c073 made 296 into V of A. In the exposition, the violas' D-minor entry instead ends on A major at 64 (E4–C♯4–A3 over the cellos' A2–E3). Vln II then enters in A minor at 65 with no G♯ before it. The candidate gives 64 the reprise's form, so the A-minor entry comes in by V–i:

```lilypond
sBVla: | e'4-. cis'-. a-. |   ->   | e'4-. b-. gis-. |
sBVc:  | a,2 e4) |             ->   | e2 b,4) |
```

**Build.** In /tmp/critic-463/work: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, no C-major triad, only the known parallel at 69. No other part sounds in 64 and no cue is affected.

**Why it is worse.** The image of Ex. 26-10 shows that Bach does not prepare the minor-v answer. The answer enters at bar 6 ("V (minor)") straight after the subject. The leading tone the text points to is at bar 12, "to prepare for the next entrance in the tonic", which is the entry that ends the tonicization.

Our fugato does the same. The entries at 61 and 65 enter by reinterpretation: each entry's closing major triad becomes the next entry's minor tonic (Ex. 26-7, PDF 448). The first G♯ is held back to 68, before the f stretto in A at 69, which is the fugato's goal. The reprise's 296 is that same pre-stretto bar, because the reprise cuts the Vln II entry. So exposition and reprise already agree.

The candidate would spend V–i in A minor four bars early. 69 would then repeat an arrival the ear had already heard at 65. It would also break the sequence of closing triads G→D→A, because 60 would stay a reinterpretation while 64 became a dominant. c373's do-not-touch on 60–61 and 64–65 stands, now with this reason from the reading.

**Fix.** None. Scratch reverted.

### 2. Exposition plan 37–105: the F episode is ♭II standing on the passing IV. Keep

§8 (PDF 449) and Ex. 26-8 say III is not a goal: tonicized IV leads from III to V. In our exposition, the stretto reaches A at 72, which is IV of E with a raised third, as Mozart's development opens on a major chord that turns out to be V of IV.

The F episode does not replace that IV. Its bass runs A (72) – F (73–80) – D–B♭–G–A (81–84) – A under F, the Neapolitan sixth (85–87) – A♯ (88) – B (89). F is a third below the IV, inside the IV's prolongation, and the IV then goes to V through A♯°7.

F itself is confirmed with a II inside the phrase (ii at 75–76, vii° 79, I 80), as Points 2 allows ("after intervening material"). The 32 soft bars from 73 to 104 do keep moving: the bass moves in every bar through 89. This answers the pacing question at its source. Keep the episode and the pedal (c253, c283).

### 3. Trio 183–209 is Ex. 26-17. The C♯-minor area is a neighbour, not a key. Keep

The B area is reached at 183. It is then extended by C♯ minor (185, VI as upper neighbour), B again (189), F♯ minor (193), A (201, IV as lower neighbour) and B7 (205–208), then E (209). That is the plan of §15 note for note.

The C♯ minor is never confirmed by an authentic cadence. 188 G♯ goes to B at 189, and the V7 at 196 resolves deceptively at 197. A&S call this VI a neighbour to V, so it needs no cadence of its own; a C♯-minor cadence would turn the neighbour into a goal. The B area itself lasts only two bars before the C♯ minor, but the B at 189–192 and 205–208 belongs to the same prolonged V. Keep (c453).

### 4. Coda 339–360: I–IV–I, with the IV as a transient key. Keep

§16 (PDF 456) names I–IV–I as the most important contrapuntal expansion in sectional pieces. The A major at 341 enters at a section start without a transition, as PDF 452 describes.

It is a transient modulation in the sense of §6 (PDF 447–448). It is never confirmed by an authentic cadence: T's first half ends on E at 348, and that E becomes I of the home key. E is then confirmed by vi–iii–ii–IV–6/4–V–I (349–356), a cadence with both II and IV (Points 2), and again by the plagal motto tail (357–360). An authentic cadence in A would make IV a goal that must then be left. Keep (c373, c403, c420).

### 5. Reprise 253–305: mixture and the third relation. Keep

Since c373, the reprise's antecedent at 277–280 turns E major for four bars (c383) and back to minor at 281. Points 1 lets a key relate to another through mixture, and this is mixture on the tonic itself, not a modulation. It also colours the third relation E → G minor at 289.

The 288→289 join is a phrase modulation at a section start. It is foreshadowed by the D7 at 273–275 (Ex. 26-7) and connects stable E minor to the V at 301 as a passing area (Points 4). The shortened development keeps the reading's order: III (by mixture), passing IV (297–300, A, with the Italian sixth at 300), V7 301, I 305. Keep (c003, c193, c373, c422).

### 6. Proportions and length. Keep

Unit 26 is about the order of key areas, not their length. Each area here lasts as long as its function needs:
- G: 20 bars, then it turns minor.
- F: 16 bars.
- B in the trio: 2 bars as a key, 10 bars as a prolonged V.
- A in the coda: 8 bars.

Against Dvořák 9/iii, our reprise is a little shorter (56 % against 60 %), the coda is the same length, and the retransition is close (12 bars against 9). At 4:38 the movement is the shortest of the four (I about 7:11, II 5:45, finale about 7:00), which suits a scherzo between an Adagio and a finale. A repeat (c453) or the F episode in the reprise would add a page and slow the reprise, whose job is to reach the coda. Keep.
