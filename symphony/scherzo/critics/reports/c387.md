## Findings

### 1. 205–209: one flute above a single horn on the motto-tail preview; make it two flutes (applied)

**Problem.** At 205–208 the trio anticipates the motto's chromatic tail. Hn 1 plays G♯4–A4–B4–A♯4–A4 at p espress. (c324), and the same line is played an octave higher by Fl 2 alone (G♯5–B5). Under them, Hn 3 holds D♯4, Hn 4 and the bassoon hold F♯3/B3, and violas and cellos sit on the B pedal. Below the flute's octave, the horn is in its strongest singing register. The flute is in its weak middle octave, at the same marking. The doubling map shows that this is the only place in the movement where a horn line is doubled at the octave by a single wind. The passage matters: it is the first time the motto's tail sounds in the trio, and 357–360 answers it on the same horn at the same pitch.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 75 (B. Combination of wind and brass in octaves): "If a single horn is used, the upper part is allotted to 2 clarinets, 2 oboes, or 2 flutes". The bracketed formula on the page image reads 2 Ob. or 2 Cl. or 2 Fl. over 1 Horn. With one wind, the upper octave does not balance the horn.
- Ex. 90 (PDF 265, *Sheherazade* iv at [U]): even at p, Rimsky puts two clarinets a2 in the octave above the horn and cello line, not one.
- PDF 75 (A): a wind added to a brass instrument gives "a finer legato effect". Here the horn line is legato under one slur.
- The do-not-touch list keeps "Fl 2 for Fl 1 205–208" as a breathing point (c036/c286). c036's stated reason was that Fl 1 "had 20 bars, 205–224, with no rest". That no longer holds: Fl 1 now plays the call at 197–200, rests 201–204, and does not re-enter until 217 (tC). Fl 1 is free, and it gets a four-bar rest before and a seven-bar rest after.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tB.ily: Fl 1 doubles Fl 2. The part-combiner prints "a2" with one "espress." (checked on page 7 of the scratch PDF).
```

  gis''2(\p^\markup \italic "espress." a''4 | b''2.~ | b''2\< ais''4 | a''2.) | }
```
(was `\partCombineSoloII R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | \partCombineAutomatic`). In sections/tC.ily, Fl 1 resolves the A5 to G♯5 with Fl 2 at 209, so the a2 line does not stop on the seventh:
```

```
(was `R2.*8 | ...`). Fl 1's entry at 217 keeps its own mf. Comments with these reasons are added above both variables. Notes, dynamics and slurs are identical to Fl 2's, so the shared staff carries equal simultaneous dynamics. No change to Hn 1, so the motto-tail preview and its resolution at 209 are unchanged. The Fl 1 part has no cue in 201–216, so no cue has to change.

### 2. 341–344: T in Hn 3 alone (checked, kept)

PDF 75 (A) and Ex. 92 (violas + horn) would favour a wind or viola unison with the horn. I keep the solo for the reason c187 and c287 gave, which the reading does not answer. This is the coda's first return of T, and its single horn colour is answered by Hn 1 alone at 357–360. A doubling here would anticipate the doubling that 345–348 introduces (Ob 1 / Cl 1 in sixths, c381). The "as a rule" in PDF 75 describes the typical blend, not a requirement for every brass solo.

### 3. 169–184: T in the cellos alone (checked, kept)

PDF 77 gives "'Cellos + Cl." and "'Cellos + Fag." as natural pairs, and PDF 79 praises horns with cellos. But c344 made this statement the trio's string colour, between the clarinet's statement at 153 and the forte return at 209, and Fl 1's answers at 172/176/183 already supply the wind colour. Adding Bsn 2 or a horn would flatten the graded plan (153 wind, 169 strings, 209 combined) that the do-not-touch list protects ("trio layering 153–184").

### 4. 41–43 and 273–275: four winds on two violin sections (checked, follows)

PDF 76 says several winds in unison overpower one string group. Here Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2 are spread over Vln I and II together (c111), which makes two winds per section. The C.a. alone takes the lower octave (c367). This is within the rule. The passage is the f arrival on V of G, where Rimsky wants the fuller resonance (PDF 77, purpose b).

### 5. 333–339: theme head in three octaves (checked, follows)

PDF 76 warns that three or four octaves are hard to balance. Rimsky's own examples on that page weight the lowest octave with a trumpet or with trumpet + C.a. In the coda each octave has one string section and one or two winds: Fl 1 + Vln I, then Ob 2 + Cl 1 + Vln II, then C.a. + violas. The trumpets cannot join the lowest octave's line, because the cyclic rule limits them to motive x. They play motive x at that pitch at 333 and 335, and B4 with Hn 1 and the C.a. at 336. This stays inside both the rule and the reading. Kept.
