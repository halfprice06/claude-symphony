## What else I studied

I read all 13 pages of v231 as images and every section file, and wrote three scans on the parsed parts (/tmp/critic-231/scan/, using coherence/lyparse.py):
- `motion.py` lists bars in which no part moves after the downbeat.
- `shadow.py` counts, for 15 melody statements, each other part's parallel 3rds/6ths on the tune's onsets and its unison or octave contacts.
- `bars.py` prints every part's notes in a range of bars.

Shadow scan results: the largest count is 5 of 22 onsets (Cl 1 against the second idea at 45–56, 121–132 and 277–288, the hemiola chorale c027 protects). Next is the tenor countermelody (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4) at 105–120 and 305–320: 4 of 52 onsets (106, 108, 113, 117). Everything else is 3 or fewer. None of these is Ex. 279's lagging echo. In the static-bar scan, nearly all the spans are intended: the intro, the link, the trio's held last chord and the closing page.

I compared the trio with Dvořák 9/iii, Poco sostenuto 68–90 (reference-corpus/pdf/DvorakA_O95__Mvt3_Scherzo.pdf, PDF p. 6). Dvořák's flute and oboe play the tune in thirds over plucked-style off-beat strings. At 76 the clarinets take over, and at 80–82 the second oboe and bassoon answer them: each new colour enters as the last one finishes. T works the same way, with one colour per statement and the cellos' fills on held notes. I also read c051 and c141 so that I would not repeat their points.

## Judgement of the melodies

- **Theme A** is the movement's hook: E–B–B–E, then the chromatic tail. Its single peak is C6 in bar 12, and it comes back in four settings.
- **T** can be hummed after one hearing. Each four-bar phrase has its dotted figure in bar 3 and its own peak: B, then the held F♯ (c121), then the tied A5 (c081). It closes with a 4–3 (c061). Its contour works the way the Ländler trios of Dvořák 8/iii and Bruckner 7/iii work.
- **The second idea** is the plainest tune, because it is an exact sequence a step higher. Its hemiola still gives it a profile.
- **The harp head 73–80** and **the coda's return of T** are both protected. In the return, three colours share T and turn it from A back to E (341–356).

v231 meets every principle in the reading. Every borderline point either has already been weighed by an earlier critic, or the change it calls for is weaker than the reason that protects the passage. I make no score change. The points below are new to this pass or rechecked against current bars, ranked by how close each came to a change, with my decision on each.

## Findings (ranked): none requires a change

### 1. 141–148: a long-note link whose top line is Hn 1 G4 for four bars, then G♯4 (Ex. 277–278, PDF 210). Not applied.
**Observation.** For eight bars nothing moves after the downbeat except Hn 1's G♯–A–G♯ at 147–148. Bass clarinet holds E2, Bsn 1 holds B2 and then E4, and Bsn 2 adds B3 at 145. On paper this is close to Ex. 278's plain lead.
**Decision.** No change. Here the lead line's single event is its point: G becomes G♯, the intro reversed and the turn from E minor to E major. The neighbour A at 147 already foreshadows T's head (c051), which is Sebesky's principle 3 (a line from part of the tune). The link is protected (c163, c190). Any extra motion would weaken the one change that matters.

### 2. 345–348: Bsn 1's counter-melody is p dolce, the same level as Ob 1's phrase of T (PDF 219). Not applied.
**Observation.** Ch. 7 says the background "should in no way compete", and c099 asks for accompaniment one level under the tune.
**Decision.** No change. Bsn 1 has a counter-melody, not an accompaniment, and Ex. 269 gives its viola counter-melody the same mf as the lead. It also moves in contrary motion throughout, which is Ex. 276's device. The oboe rises D5–E5–F♯5–B5 while the bassoon falls A3–F♯3–D3. Then the oboe falls A5–G♯5–F♯5–D♯5 while the bassoon rises D♯3–E3–G♯3–B3. It lies two octaves under the oboe in the bassoon's weaker tenor register. No unison contact (shadow scan: 0).

### 3. 356–357: Hn 1 enters on B4, pp espress., after 17 bars' rest, over a bare E octave at 356 (Ex. 285, PDF 222). Not applied.
**Observation.** Sebesky has the orchestra sound the soloist's next first note just before the entry. At 356 only E is sounding (Vln I/II, Cl 1, violas, cellos and basses).
**Decision.** No change. B is in the bass for three bars just before (Vc B2 held 353–355, Cb B1). At 357 the B4 is the fifth of a plain E-major chord (Vln II G♯4, violas E4, Bsn 1·2 G♯3/E3). Sebesky asks for the cue when "the harmonic complexity can make it difficult" to find the note, and that is not the case here. Adding a B at 356 would put a fifth into the T cadence's held tonic and blur the start of the motto tail. 357–376 is protected.

### 4. 45–56, 121–132, 277–288: the second idea is the least singable theme (question 6, PDF 220: "Is the song weak in some respect … What can I do to overcome these faults?"). Not applied.
**Decision.** No change to the tune. Sebesky's answer to a weak song is the setting, and each statement is already set differently:
- 45: f, octaves, horn pad.
- 121: sub. p, Fl 1 and Ob 1 join at 125, crescendo (c063).
- 277: p, tenor horn and cellos at 281 (c071).
The tune is protected (c027, c032, c139), and the finale is told whenever a theme changes. A rewrite would cost more than the gain.

### 5. 167–168: Cl 1 holds E5 for two bars at the end of T's first statement, with only harp off-beats and pizz. under it (Ex. 286, PDF 223: a fill takes up the slack while the voice holds). Not applied.
**Decision.** No change. The first statement is kept plain as the reference that the later statements colour (c089, c157). Sebesky's fills appear where the later statements hold notes: the cellos at 172, 176, 212 and 216. With no fill at 168, Vln I's entry at 169 is fresh, as is the cellos' entry (c037). The harp's G♯3 on beats 2–3 of 168 gives the violins their first note (principle 21).

### 6. 225–236: the codetta states T's head three times on the same five notes (PDF 212: "concentrate on every bar"). Not applied.
**Decision.** No change. Each statement has its own colour and octave: Ob 1 G♯5, Cl 1 G♯4, Bsn 1 G♯3, descending as the harp thins (c153). It is a written-out fade, the "recurrent figure" of principle 4, and it is protected (c157, c177).
