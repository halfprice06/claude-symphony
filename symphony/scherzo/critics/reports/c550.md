## Findings

**F1 (applied). Bar 364: the closing G♯5 passes from Fl 1 to Vln I with a gap, not an overlap.**
Problem: v550 has Fl 1 `gis''2 r4` and Vln I `r2 gis''4~` in 364. The flute, the clarinets' E5/B4, Hn 4's E4 and the violas, cellos and basses all stop on beat 3 of 364, and the tutti first violins start on that same beat. The one note the finale takes over (IV 1, Vln I A♭5) therefore begins with a fresh attack at the exact moment everything else drops out. In a ppp texture of two or three players this is the most exposed join in the movement.
Evidence: c500 cites PDF 125-126 ("the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one") but wrote the change as a butt join. Ex. 237 (PDF 407) always has the next part sound before the previous one lets go. PDF 127 says a diminuendo works by taking one group out of a combined timbre. When the violin is already sounding under the chord, its release at 364.3 uncovers the G♯5 instead of starting it. This also fits c500's own second reason (PDF 130: the strings should not re-enter after the winds have gone).
Fix (applied, coda.ily): `R2.*7 | gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "tutti, change bow freely" -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan | gis''2.~ | …` (was `R2.*7 | r2 gis''4~\ppp…`). The ppp violins now enter on beat 1 of 364, two beats under Fl 1's fading pp G♯5 and the pp chord that masks them. Pitch, player, ppp, sul A, the last bar and the 361/365/369/373 groups are unchanged.
I tested two alternatives on paper and rejected both. Holding Fl 1 through 364 would leave a bare flute-and-violin unison for a beat after the chord has gone, so the winds would outlast the strings, against PDF 130. Starting Vln I at 361 would change the 361 group's colour, which c440/c527 protect for a reason that still holds.

**F4 (no change). 105-120 and 305-320: the tenor counter-line (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4; T's head G3-A3-B3-E4 since c539) under the theme-A tutti.**
This is Steinberg's second faulty-passage type (PDF 18): a low-string counter-melody doubled by woodwind and lost under brass played at the marked level. Here the line is carried by two horns a2 as well as the cellos and Bsn 1. The brass above it are short notes: trumpets in motive x on alternate bars, trombones and Hn 1·2 in crotchets. So the only sustained line in that register is the counter-line itself. It follows the reading, and nothing should change.

- Coda: Hn 3 (C♯4, 341), Ob 1 with Cl 1 (345), then solo violin, Fl 1 and Vln II (349). Each imitation rises and goes to a higher instrument.
- Codetta: Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1 (225, 229, 233). Each one falls and goes to a lower instrument.
- Retransition: Vla 241, Cl 1 245, Vln I 249. Each overlaps the next on a held note, as PDF 126 asks.
Nothing to change.

**F6 (no change). Outer-voice gap at 365-374 (PDF 125).**
Between 365 and 374 the only sounds are G♯4 (Hn 1/Hn 3) and G♯5 (Vln I) over timpani strokes on B2/E2 at 367 and 371. That is the wide, unfilled spacing Rimsky-Korsakov calls strange. It is brief, ppp and percussive, and the harp harmonics E4-G♯4-B4 with pizz. E2/E1 fill it at 375. It is the intended thinning of the close (PDF 127), and nothing should be added.
