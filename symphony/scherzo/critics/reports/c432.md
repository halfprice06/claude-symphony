## Findings

### 1. Bar 48: the first phrase of the second idea ends without the leading tone that ends the second phrase (applied)

**Problem.** The forte G-major second idea (45–52) is a two-phrase sequence a step apart, G (45–48) and then A (49–52). The second phrase ends on A7 in 6/5 position over C♯ (52 beats 2–3), so the bass rises C♯–D into the hemiola at 53. The first phrase ended on a plain G/B (48 beats 2–3, bass B2) and the bass fell B–A into 49. The sequence's two halves therefore do not match where they join their next downbeats. The move from G to A, which is the theme's main harmonic step, had no pull, and that is part of why the second idea has stayed the least memorable theme.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 554 (Ex. 31-3b): in an ascending 5–6 progression, chromatic passing tones in the bass turn the 6s into applied chords, and major triads are preferred. PDF 556 (Ex. 31-6): each 5–6 can be expressed as a root-position applied dominant. PDF 534 (Ex. 30-6): VI♯ in major is secondary mixture and gives the chord emphasis. PDF 537: an altered chord is least disruptive when its altered tone belongs to a chromatic line in one voice and when it sits inside a sequence. The movement already uses this exact move elsewhere. At 22 (theme A) and at 280 (the reprise's E-major second idea, c383), E/G♯ leads to A over the bass G♯–A. Only the exposition's G-major statement did not have it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-432/work/sections/sA.ily).** Bar 48 now has E major on beat 1 and E7 in 6/5 position on beats 2–3. The chain into the cadence is now E7 – A – (A7) – D7 – G.
```
sAClOne   47–48: a'2( g'4~ | g'4 g'2)          ->  a'2( g'4 | gis'4 b'2)
sAHnOne   47–48: a'2-- g'4--~ | g'2.)          ->  a'2-- g'4-- | gis'4-- b'2--)
sAHnTwo   47–48: a2-- g4--~ | g2.)             ->  a2-- g4-- | e2.--)
sABsnOne  48:    e4 b,2)                       ->  e4 gis,2)
sAVc      48:    e4 b,2)                       ->  e4 gis,2)
sACbsn    48:    e,4 b,,2)                     ->  e,4 gis,,2)
sACb      48:    e,4 b,,2)                     ->  e,4 gis,,2)
```
Voice leading:
- c022's V–vi with the doubled third stays on 47 beat 3 (G4 in Cl 1/Hn 1, G3 in Hn 2).
- On 48 beat 1, Cl 1/Hn 1 (one line in unison) take G4–G♯4 over the held bass E. Hn 2 drops to E3, so the G♯ is not doubled.
- On beat 2 the G♯ passes to the bass, and Cl 1/Hn 1 move on to B4.
- At 49 the bass G♯2 rises to A2. Cl 2's D4, the 7th, resolves to C♯4, and Cl 1/Hn 1 fall B4–A4, against the bass.
- The tune's D6 rises to E6 before reaching C♯6 later in 49. Cl 2 takes the 7th's resolution, which is a transferred resolution of the kind A&S's exercise on PDF 583 labels.

No new parallels: analysis.txt still lists only bar 69. The change adds no C-major triad and no B–D–F–G♯. The tune, its ties, the rising sixths and the hemiola 53–55 are unchanged, so themecheck reports 52/52 unchanged. Score layout is unchanged at 13 pages.

### 2. Bar 124: the E-minor exposition statement keeps i6; no matching change

**Problem checked.** Bar 124 is the place in the E-minor statement (121–128) that matches bar 48. Its bass G2 goes to A1 at 125.

**Decision.** No change. Beat 1 of 124 is V4/2 (bass A), and its 7th has to fall to G, the 3rd of i6. G♯ there would make the 7th resolve into the major mode in the middle of a minor statement. The reprise already has the G♯–A form at 280, in the E-major antecedent c383 introduced, and A&S PDF 532 notes that mixture in a minor key sounds less special than mixture in major. Keeping 124 minor preserves the contrast c383 created between 121 (minor) and 277 (major).

### 3. The A♯°7 collection at 79, 88, 128, 284 and 358 is spelled and resolved by the book; keep it

**Checked.** Each appearance has a different function:
- 79: vii°7 of F over E, spelled E–G–B♭–D♭. Every tone resolves by step into F at 80, as PDF 559–560 requires for spelling.
- 88, 128 and 284: °7 on ♯4 leading to the cadential 6/4. PDF 545 (Ex. 30-22) shows this with the 7th held as a common tone.
- 358: common-tone °7 over the tonic pedal, spelled with G♮ from mixture (PDF 544–545, Ex. 30-21, Brahms Op. 87 after a C-minor scherzo).

Only 358 has the tonic in the bass, so the close keeps its own sound. No change.

B–D♯–G is ♭6 displacing the 5th of V in minor (PDF 537, 540–541). The G moves to F♯ on the next beat at 98→99 and within bar 246. No change.

### 5. Bars 34, 313 and 315: passing C–E–G pitch-class sets inside quaver figures

**Checked.** My scan finds C, E and G sounding together for one quaver at 34.3 (passing G over A minor), 313.1 (G over F♯–A–C–E) and 315.1. These are passing tones inside 6–10 figures. A&S PDF 563–564 and 568 call such sonorities products of voice leading with no harmonic status. cmajor.py does not count them, and c342 accepted the one at 34 on the same grounds. No change.

### 6. Common-tone °7 on V, common-tone augmented sixth on I, and V7♯5 into the trio's cadences: closed for this movement

Over B, the common-tone °7 of PDF 546 is B–D–F–G♯, the finale's stop chord. Over E, the common-tone augmented sixth of PDF 547 is C–E–G–A♯, which contains a C-major triad. V7♯5 (PDF 542) needs F𝄪 against the tune's F♯ at 166, 222 and 355. None of the three can be used, so I propose nothing here.
