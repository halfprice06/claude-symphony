## What else I studied

I counted the bow direction of every string part through the movement with Adler's rules from PDF 37–38. Each unslurred note is a new bow, a slur or a tie is one bow, a note after a rest starts by the habit (a downbeat down-bow, an upbeat up-bow), and printed ⊓/V override the habit. I then checked every hairpin in the strings against the bow it falls on. I read the earlier Adler ch. 2 reports (c006, c018, c085, c096) and the expression reports c098 and c108, so as not to repeat their points. ## Findings

### 1. 45–52 and 121–128: every swell in the second idea falls against the bow (applied)
**Bars:** Vln I and Vln II, 45–52 (f) and 121–128 (sub. p).
**Problem:** The second idea is bowed in two-bar slurs. Its swells (c058) rise through the first bar of each slur and fall through the second: `\<` at 45, `\>` at 46 to 48, `\<` at 49, `\>` at 50 to 52. They follow the same pattern at 121–128. At 121, c018's ⊓ puts the first slur on a down-bow. At 45 the habit does the same, because the bar comes after a rest. So each crescendo is drawn toward the tip and each diminuendo toward the frog, all eight bars long, in both statements. The peaks (G6 at 46, A6 at 50, E6 at 122, F♯6 at 126) all arrive near the tip. The ⊓ at 121 also only repeats the habit, since the bar comes after a rest. At 121 the dynamic is sub. p, where the tip, the light end of the bow, is wanted.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog … The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow. … indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit." PDF 41 (Ex. II-27): the point of the bow for "extreme lightness". The bow count also shows that V at 45 and 121 is already the natural continuation of the bow: 30–32 and 118–120 end on a down-bow. So no retake is needed. The mark only stops the players from starting on a down-bow after the rest.
**Fix (applied):**
- sA.ily `sAVlnOne` 45: `d'''2->(\f^\markup \italic "espress."\<\upbow b''4~`; `sAVlnTwo` 45: `d''2->(\<\upbow b'4~`.
- sC.ily `sCVlnOne` 121: `b''2->\subP\upbow\<( g''4~` (was `\dnb`); `sCVlnTwo`: `b'2->\subP\upbow\<( g'4~`.

Now 45–46 and 49–50 swell on up-bows toward the frog, and 47–48 and 51–52 fade on down-bows. The same holds at 121–128. The sub. p accent at 121 starts at the tip. This reverses c018's ⊓ at 121 only. Theme A's accented minims keep their ⊓. I left 277 (⊓, no hairpin until 281) as it is.

### 2. Trio tune, third phrase (177–182, 217–222, 349–351): the climax A6 starts at the tip after a crescendo drawn on a down-bow (applied)
**Bars:** Vln I and II, 169–184, 209–224 and 349–356.
**Problem:** From the down-bow entry at 169 (and 209), the slurs give 177–178 (`cis'''2(\< dis''' | e''' gis''')`, the crescendo, c038) a down-bow. The tied A6 that starts on beat 3 of 178, the tune's climax, then starts at the tip on an up-bow, and its diminuendo (`a'''4.\>`) is drawn toward the frog. The coda statement at 349 has the same problem, since it enters down-bow after rests. These are the movement's most expressive bars, and the bowing works against both hairpins.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38, as in finding 1. PDF 37 (Ex. II-19): a bow plan is made so that the strong note gets the strong bow. PDF 39: few notes per bow are needed only at f. A seven-beat slur (1.8 s at 2. = 76) is well within the limit.
**Fix (applied):** I joined two slurs in each statement so the crescendo falls on an up-bow and the A on a down-bow. I left the notes and hairpins alone.
- tA.ily and tC.ily, `VlnOne`/`VlnTwo`: `e'''4.( dis'''8 cis'''4 | ais''4 b''2)` (175–176 and 215–216 one bow) and `a'''4~( | a'''4.\> gis'''8 fis'''4 | cis'''4 b''4 gis''4)` (178 beat 3 to 180 one bow; `a''4)` at 220). Vln II takes the same pattern an octave lower.
- coda.ily 349: `cis'''2(\pp\<\upbow dis'''4` (Vln I) and `cis''2(\pp\<\upbow dis''4` (Vln II).

The new bowing is: 177 V with the crescendo, 178 beat 3 ⊓ (the A at the frog) with the diminuendo, and 181 V with the swell into the 4–3 (c098's shading keeps a crescendo on an up-bow). The statement ends on a down-bow. At 349–356 the 4–3 at 355 now falls on a down-bow with its diminuendo.

### 3. 333–336: the cellos' and basses' crescendo into the fff peak is a down-bow that ends at the tip (applied)
**Bars:** Vc, Cb 333–336.
**Problem:** c108 added `b,2.\<` at 335, after c096 had bowed 336–339 with ⊓ (fff), ⊓, V, ⊓, ⊓. The basses start 333 down-bow after a rest, so 335's crescendo is a down-bow toward the tip. The fff at 336 then needs a retake from the tip. By the count from 323, the cellos start 333, the ff arrival, on an up-bow.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38 (crescendo up-bow; the heaviest attack at the frog). PDF 42–43 and Ex. II-29: a series of ff down-bows with the bow lifted, the standard heavy stroke in a fast scherzo.
**Fix (applied):** coda.ily.
- `codaVc`: `e,2.\ff\dnb | e,2.\dnb | b,2.\<\upbow | b,2.\fff\dnb`.
- `codaCb`: `e,,2.\ff | e,,2.\dnb | b,,2.\<\upbow | b,,2.\fff\dnb`.

333–334 are two heavy ff down-bows, 335 is an up-bow crescendo that arrives at the frog, and 336 is a fresh down-bow. 336–339 are unchanged (c096).

### 4. 193–197: the basses reach the trio's forte arrival on an up-bow, against the cellos (applied)
**Bars:** Cb 193–204.
**Problem:** The basses take the bow at 193 after a rest, so they start down-bow. The tie at 195–196 makes 197 (f, the arrival of the call's forte phrase) an up-bow. The cellos come from their slurred answer 189–192 and start 193 on an up-bow, so they reach 197 down-bow. The two bass parts bow the same line in opposite directions, and the basses' crescendo ends at the tip. The count also leaves 203 and 204 both up-bows, which forces a hooked repeat before the pizz. at 205.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38 (crescendo up-bow; heavy stroke at the frog); PDF 54 (an up-bow just before the pizz.).
**Fix (applied):** tB.ily `tBCb`: `fis,,2.\p\<\upbow^\markup \italic "arco"`. Now the basses bow with the cellos: 197 is a down-bow, and 204 is an up-bow by the count, so the existing V needs no hook. The cellos need no mark.
