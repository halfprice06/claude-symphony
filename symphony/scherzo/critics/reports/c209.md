## What else I studied

For the holistic comparison I used Bruckner's Ninth, ii (Scherzo: Bewegt, lebhaft, D minor; Trio: Schnell, F♯ major). ## Holistic verdict

Would a conductor take this for a master's scherzo next to Bruckner 9/ii? On construction, yes. Both are sonata-shaped scherzi with a development and a shortened, varied return. Both build long crescendi over a held dominant: Bruckner's opening approach to bar 43 corresponds to the retransition at 89–104 and the coda build at 325–336, and both of those add strings, then woodwind, then brass. Both use pizzicato as a structural colour (the basses under theme A, Vln II and violas at 253–271). Both give the timpani a thematic voice.

The differences are all settled decisions, not weaknesses:
- Bruckner's climaxes hammer every beat with full brass. Here the brass give weight, not bite (Dan's rule, c123, c199), and the trumpets play only motive x.
- Bruckner's trio is faster. This one is "un poco meno mosso", after the Dvořák and Eroica models in the brief.
- The movement runs 4:36 against Bruckner's ten minutes or so, which is right between the Adagio and the finale (c203's proportions).

I do not recommend importing any of Bruckner's devices. His keyless opening chord and abrupt fff hammering would break the G♯ frame (bars 1–8) and the soft-brass rule. A second fully scored scherzo statement would undo the form protected since c083/c113.

Against the reading, the movement's melodic scoring matches Rimsky-Korsakov's practice throughout:
- High violin lines are doubled an octave lower (#13, #19).
- Soft wind solos are kept single (#22, #24).
- Doubling grows with dynamic level (#24).
- The cor anglais is absorbed into the cellos' answer, as in Ex. 15 and 17 (#10).
- Bass lines always have cello, bassoon or harp support (#7).
- No group of violas or cellos is divided in octaves (#18).

The scans found no inversion of the woodwind order that earlier cycles had not already examined and kept.

## Findings

### Considered and kept (decided, no change)
1. **24 and 264: theme A's *p* peak C6 in Vln I with no lower octave** (Rimsky Note I, PDF 62; p. 59: very high violin lines become "isolated" unless doubled). I keep it. Rimsky says "generally". His own Ex. 1 (PDF 173), cited on p. 55 for exactly this kind of *p grazioso* Vln I melody over pizz. accompaniment, leaves the melody single. The peak lasts a half-bar at *mp*, and Vln II's off-beats are protected texture (c018, c118). Doubling it would thicken the one light statement of the theme. At 264, Fl 1's heterophony already sounds above it.
2. **45–55: Cl 1's inner line crosses above Ob 1, which doubles the tune's lower octave** (PDF 70: thirds and sixths out of register order sound "strained"). I keep it. The crossing lies under the real top line (Vln I + Fl 1 an octave higher). Swapping the oboe and clarinet lines moves the crossing, it does not remove it, because the two lines cross by contour. c202 recorded it as a known exception.
3. **104/304: Cl 1 leaps to A5, above Fl 2 and the oboes in the V7 chord** (PDF 65, natural order). I keep it. Cl 1 doubles Fl 1's melody note A5 in unison, which is the Fl + Cl unison of PDF 65 ("the clarinet will predominate in the higher register"). It is a melodic doubling, not a wrongly spaced chord tone. c184 settled the oboes' F♯5/D♯5.
4. **153–168: Cb pizz. without cellos under the trio tune** (PDF 56). I keep it. The harp LH doubles every bass note an octave higher, as the harp supports the pizz. bass in Ex. 57 (PDF 229). The cellos rest so that their entry at 169 is fresh (c040).
5. **86–88: Bsn 1 A3 above Cl 2's falling F3–D♯3–C♯3** (PDF 65). I keep it. This is c180's resolution of the Neapolitan F (A&S), at *pp*, in the clarinet's chalumeau.
6. **The trio's tempo against Bruckner's faster trio.** I keep it. The brief names the soft trios of the Eroica and Dvořák 8 as models, and the proportions were confirmed by c203.
