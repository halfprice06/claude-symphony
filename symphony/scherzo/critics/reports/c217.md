## Findings

No change. Every principle the chapter teaches is already met in this score, most of them because c007, c017, c097, c107, c117, c137 and c177 applied the same chapter or its neighbours. The passages that carry the polyphony do what Adler's examples do:

- Fugato and stretto 57–72 / 289–300. The subject, countersubject and third line have three rhythms (crotchet-quaver-quaver-crotchet; even crotchets; minims), as the Jupiter elements do (PDF 175). The entries are strings alone, then at the stretto each entry takes one string section plus winds, Vln I with Fl 1 and Ob 1, Vln II with Cl 1, cellos with Bsn 1 and Hn 1·2, over a non-thematic A pedal: this is Mozart's scheme at 389–400 (PDF 176) point for point. The subject is p and the subsidiary lines pp (PDF 184; c117).
- Tutti 105–120 / 305–320. Four layers with four colours and four articulations: theme (strings in octaves with flutes, oboe, cor anglais, Cl 2, staccato), tenor countermelody (cellos, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4, legato; one string, one woodwind, one brass, as Wagner's three themes at PDF 178), rhythm (Hn 1·2 and trumpets in motive x), background (viola off-beats; tenuto bass crotchets with low brass and timpani, Holst's "heavy bass notes", PDF 181).
- Trio 169–184, 193–200, 209–224. Tune in the violins, louré viola off-beats as the "dance" middleground, cello countermelody in a complementary rhythm, plucked bass: the Scheherazade layout (PDF 178–179). At 197–200 the call and T are rhythmically separate since c117.
- Reprise 253–268. Theme (Vln I, staccato), flute heterophony an octave up (slurred), sostenuto cello line, pizzicato off-beats and bass: four elements told apart by articulation (PDF 178–179).
- Coda 341–356. T passes Hn 1 → Ob 1 → violins with Cl 1 in four-bar phrases, each over the same harp/pizzicato background, with Bsn 1's and then the cellos' countermelody in their own rhythms and registers (PDF 176, 179).

Candidates I examined and decided against:

1. Bars 193–194: Bsn 1 holds A3, the pitch the violas' turn (A3–B3–A3–G♯3–A3, c137) circles. Changing it is worse: F♯3 would be the fourth F♯ in the chord (bass, Cl 2, Vln II), and C♯4 would leap to the next note or land on the violas' B3 at 195 instead. The bassoon gives the turn's centre some body under a p line. Kept.
2. Bar 336: timpani roll fff (PDF 184 warns that a ff roll covers the themes). At 336 no themes are combined. It is the harmonic peak on B6, strings and woodwinds are fff, and the cellos and basses hold the same B. Bloch's warning is about a build where themes are being combined, as at 101–104, where the roll already starts p. Kept.
3. Bars 289–296: the reprise fugato has two voices and no bass, where the exposition had three plus a pedal. The reprise is shortened (no third entry, straight to the stretto at 297), and the two-voice texture is the compression. A bass here would only repeat 65–68. Kept (c117, c207).
4. Bars 57–68: Beethoven 9/ii gives the basses their own fugal entry. Here the basses enter at 65 with a harmonic pedal, which is also an independent part (PDF 176) and keeps the bass free for the stretto pedal at 69. Making them a fourth fugal voice would rebuild the protected fugato (c097, c157). Kept.
5. Bars 209–224: T has no brass in its colour (PDF 178). The only brass free are the horns, whose held pair is protected (c086), and trumpets may not carry melody. Kept.
