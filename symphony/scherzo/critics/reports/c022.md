## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Augmented 2nd C–D♯ from iv to V7 in the main theme (18–19, 110–111, 262–263, 314–315) — applied
Problem: in theme A's sixth bar (iv to V7) the viola's off-beats go C4 to D♯4. In the tutti (110–111, 314–315) Hn 1 also goes C5 to D♯5. That is the melodic augmented 2nd 6̂–♯7̂ that A&S forbid in minor in every unit of the reading: Ex. 11-5 (PDF 166, "not A2"), Ex. 11-6b (PDF 167, "wrong"), Ex. 11-10d (PDF 169, "poor"), Points for Review 6 (PDF 172) and Ex. 13-12 (PDF 203). The C should fall to B, and the 7th A is already held in Vln II.
Evidence: sA.ily Vla bar 19 `r4 dis'4-. dis'4-.` after `r4 c'4-. c'4-.`; sC/dC Hn 1 `c''4-> r4 c''4-.` then `dis''4-> r4 dis''4-.`. The tune keeps D♯5 in every case, so the chord stays complete.
Fix (applied):
- sA.ily Vla bar 19 and dA.ily Vla bar 263: `r4 b4-. b4-.`
- sC.ily Vla 111 and dC.ily Vla 315: `r4 <b a'>4-. <b a'>4-.` (C falls to B; the 7th A is held and falls to G at 112)
- sC/dC Hn 1 bars 111/315: `b'4-> r4 b'4-.`
- Hn 2 bars 111/315: `fis'4-> r4 fis'4-.` (the horns now sound B–F♯ over Hn 4's D♯, with no unison)

### 2. Augmented 2nd C–D♯ in the bass of the E-minor second idea (125–126, 285–286) — applied
Problem: the bass (Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Vc, Cb) rises A–C–D♯–E under iv–iv6–vii°7–i. This is A&S Ex. 11-14b exactly ("bass line 6–7–8 in minor … wrong", A2; PDF 171). The reading names IV6–V as "a most important function of IV6: preceding V in a semicadence in minor", with a stronger effect "because of the bass motion by half step, which intensifies V". It also asks for the 3rd above the bass to be doubled (PDF 168–169).
Fix (applied in sC 125–126 and dA 285–286): Bsn 2 `c4 b,2`, Cbsn `c,4 b,,2)`, Vc `c4 b,2)`, Cb `c,4 b,,2)`; Bsn 1 `a2( e4~ | e4 dis2)`. Bsn 1 doubles E, the 3rd above C, and then takes D♯ above the bass, so it moves by step and has no A2. The result is iv–iv6–V(♭9)–i. Bars 127–128 (E–F♯–G into the 6/4, cycle 12) are untouched, and so is the secondIdeaE melody.

### 3. The diminished triad C♯–E–G on the way to V at the coda's climax (339) — applied
Problem: the coda's bass falls E–D–C♯–B (337–340). Bar 339 is C♯–E–G (Cl 2, B.cl., Bsn 2, Cb, Bsn 1 G, Vln I `cis''4-. g'8 g' cis''4`). A&S give I–♯VI–V as "wrong" and call ♯VI "a very ugly diminished 5/3 chord" (Ex. 11-6c, PDF 167; also Ex. 11-14c, PDF 171). C♯ was used because C–E–G would be a C-major triad. The descending tetrachord in natural minor, E–D–C–B, ending in the Phrygian iv6–V (PDF 168), avoids both problems if the G is replaced by A: C–E–A has no G.
Fix (applied, coda.ily 339): Cl 2 `c'2.`, Bsn 2 `c2.`, Cb `c,4-.`, Bsn 1 `a2.` (held into V7's 7th at 340), Vln I `c''4-. a'8-. a'-. c''4-.`. The bass clarinet cannot reach C2 (its low limit is written E♭3, i.e. D♭2; see the c016 note), so it rests from 339 (`R2.\! | R2.`). The contrabassoon now enters one bar early: `R2.*6 | c,2.\mf\< | b,,2.\f`. The motive's interval was already off (C♯–G was a tritone); it is now a minor 3rd. cmajor.py: none.

### 4. Parallel 5ths into V in the fugato's stretto preparation (67–68) — applied
Problem: bar 67 is iv7 in A minor (Cb D, Vc F, Vla C–B–A, Vln II ending on C). The viola's A on beat 3 (over the basses' D2) moves to B over E at 68, so viola and basses move in parallel 5ths, D–A to E–B. This is the IV8–7–V case of Ex. 12-21a (PDF 190). The reading's remedy is the characteristic voice leading of IV7: "its 5th … descend[s] by step into the leading tone, thus moving to V in parallel 3rds, 6ths, or 10ths with its 7th" (PDF 189).
Fix (applied, sB.ily Vla 68): `gis2 e'4)`. The viola's 5th A falls to G♯ in 6ths with Vln II's C–B.

### 5. Parallel 5ths and octaves from iv to V at the trio's forte (199–200) — applied
Problem: iv to V in C♯ minor, root position, f. Cl 1 goes C♯5–D♯5 against Ob 2's F♯4–G♯4 (parallel 5ths), and also against the bass F♯–G♯ (compound 5ths). Ob 2 doubles the bass F♯–G♯ an octave higher (octaves). The reading warns about parallel octaves when IV5/3 goes to V (PDF 169) and about 5ths into V from IV7 (PDF 189–190). In both cases the upper voices should move against the bass.
Fix (applied, tB.ily 200): Ob 2 `dis'2.\>` (F♯ falls to D♯ with Hn 2), Cl 1 `bis'2.\>` (C♯ falls to the leading tone with Fl 2).

### 6. Parallel 5ths from iv to V before the trio's dominant pedal (202–203) — applied
Problem: the viola's E4 over the bass A (iv, 202) rises to F♯4 over B (203), in parallel 5ths with Bsn 2/Vc/Cb. This is the same IV–V fault (PDF 189–190).
Fix (applied, tB.ily Vla 203): `dis'2.\>`. E falls to D♯ and the viola takes F♯ at 204 as before.

### 7. The trio codetta's I–IV–I has no neighbour over IV (225–236) — applied
Problem: each of the three T-head statements (Ob 1 225, Cl 1 229, Bsn 1 233) ends on a B held through the plagal IV. So the IV chord (A–C♯–E) sounds with an unresolved 9th three times (Hn 3 holds B as well at 235). Bsn 1's C♯ falls in bar 226, over I, so the neighbour comes one bar before its chord. A&S tie I–IV–I to the melodic neighbour 5–6–5 or 3–4–3: "IV, which contains the upper neighbors to 3 and 5 plus 1 as a common tone, works particularly well as a neighboring chord to I" (PDF 196, Ex. 13-1, 13-2).
Fix (applied, tC.ily):
- Ob 1 225–228: `gis''2(\p a''4 | b''2. | cis'''2. | b''2) r4`
- Cl 1 229–232: `… | b'2. | cis''2. | b'2) r4`
- Bsn 1 233–236: `… | b2. | cis'2. | b2) r4`
- Bsn 1 225–228: `b2.\pp | b2. | cis'2. | b2.`
- Hn 3 235: `a2.`

These codetta heads are not in the theme record.

### 8. The 3rd of vi is not doubled in the second idea's V–vi (47–48) — applied
Problem: at 47 beat 3 (V to vi in G) the vi chord has E seven times, G only in Cl 1, and no B. The leading tone F♯ falls to E in three inner parts (Vln II, Vla, Cl 2). A&S: with V–VI "the 3rd of VI is doubled … because the doubled 3rd allows 7̂ to move to 8̂ and prevents parallel 5ths" (PDF 202, Ex. 13-12).
Fix (applied, sA.ily Vla 47–48): `fis'2( g'4~ | g'4 d'2)`. The viola's leading tone rises to G and G is doubled. Vln I/Fl/Ob (secondIdeaG) are unchanged.
