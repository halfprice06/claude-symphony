## Findings

None of the 20 principles is broken, so no finding needs a change to the score. The movement uses Unit 14's methods for form at both levels. Theme A is a 16-bar quatrain whose first two A's cadence on V and I (PDF 224). The trio is a larger quatrain whose second A modulates to V (PDF 222, 224), with a pivot on ii of B (PDF 217). That B area is cancelled by 4 of the main key (A♮ at 193; A♯→A♮ at 207–208, PDF 220). Every return to E goes through V7 (PDF 220). Every authentic cadence carries 2–1 or 7–1 in the top voice (PDF 226).

I tested the six points below against the reading and decided each one myself. Composer: keep all six as they are.

### 1. No repeats in the trio or the scherzo: keep

A&S PDF 224 says that in quatrain dance movements "both halves will usually repeat". Dvořák 9/iii writes out its trio's second half twice (d d d c' c' | d d d c' c').

This trio repeats only its first half, at 169, and that repeat already changes (it ends in B). Repeating 185–224 would add 40 bars, about 32 s at 2. = 76, bringing the movement to about 5:08. I timed that against the section table. The trio block would grow from 88 s to 120 s, against a 49 s reprise. That reverses the balance c003, c023 and c093 set, when they cut trio and retransition bars to remove literal repetition.

The 4:36 length is right between a 5:45 Adagio and a 7-minute finale. The scherzo is also the shortest movement in Dvořák 9 and Brahms 4. Decision: no repeat signs, no added bars.

### 2. The trio's B area is short (183–184, 189–192): keep

A&S PDF 213 says the line between tonicization and modulation cannot be drawn exactly, and in short forms "it scarcely matters". The B area is made convincing by three means the unit lists:
- the pivot (C♯ minor at 177, ii of B, PDF 217);
- an authentic cadence with 2–1 in the melody (182–183, PDF 219);
- a return to B after the springboard into C♯ minor (189–192, PDF 222).

It is then cancelled by A♮ (193), the tonic's 4 (PDF 220).

Dvořák gives his trio's V whole phrases. This trio's middle uses vi (C♯ minor) as its main contrast instead, and Ex. 14-12 shows a B section that only prolongs V. Making B longer would cost the C♯ minor call and response (185–192), which c091 and c137 shaped. Decision: keep.

### 3. The F-major episode has no authentic cadence (73–88): keep

A&S PDF 216 says no temporary tonic lasts long without its own dominant; PDF 219 says new keys are usually confirmed by an authentic cadence. F's dominant is C major, which the cyclic rule forbids. The episode uses vii°7 of F (79 → 80), the substitute A&S allow in its place (PDF 213: V or VII).

The episode is a phrase modulation, marked by its new colour (PDF 223). It works as ♭II, and F/A at 85–86 is "a chord that would normally lead into the V chord of the new key" (PDF 217), here V of E at 89. The reading supports the episode as it stands. Its melodies (73–80 harp, 81–88 Cl 1) are protected. Decision: keep.

### 4. The tonic runs 105–179 (about 55 s on E): keep

A&S PDF 220: without a contrasting key area "there would be no impression of departure and return". From the tutti theme at 105 to the B pivot at 177, the music stays on E for about 55 s.

Within that span:
- the change of mode at 145 sets off the large division, and the trio's new tempo and texture articulate it (PDF 220: modulation is one means of articulation, not the only one);
- c093 moved the first V area to 180, inside the trio;
- 121–132 already holds the V♭9 at 126.

Every way to shorten the span touches protected passages: the link 133–152, the trio's first statement 153–168, and the hemiola cadence 129–131. Decision: keep.

### 5. The coda's weight against the reprise (42 s against 49 s): keep

The coda is 15 % of the movement against Dvořák's 9 %. A&S PDF 220 describes the coda as "a varied repetition of the final cadence", and that describes the second stage:
- T's half cadence in A at 348 doubles as the pivot back to E (PDF 219);
- the expanded cadence 353–356 follows;
- the plagal repetition 357–360 comes next;
- then the held G♯ that the finale continues.

The first stage (325–340) is only 11 s. Shortening 341–356 would remove the A-major half of T, which c013 added so the late coda carries IV. Decision: keep (357–376 is protected for the link to the finale).

### 6. C♯ in both outer voices at 49–50: keep

My ♯4 scan (PDF 214) flags 49 beat 3 – 50 beat 1 in G: C♯6 in the tune (Fl 1, Vln I; Ob 1 and Vln II an octave lower) against C♯ in the bass (Vc, Bsn 1, Cbsn, Cb). The C♯ is the third of a prolonged A-major chord, arpeggiated inside a sequence that repeats 45–46 a step higher, and neither voice treats it as a leading tone (both move on to A).

c122 closed this point by the sequence exception (A&S PDF 267, 287), and the reading gives no reason to reopen it. Every other ♯4 that sounds twice is a single line doubled in octaves or at the unison, which is orchestral doubling of the line, not a doubled chord tone. That was the distinction c093 applied at 175/215. Decision: keep.
