## Findings

No finding needs a score change. My task was to find the largest improvement this reading supports, so I tested the strongest candidates against the reading and against the reasons recorded in HISTORY.md. They are ranked below by how close each came to a change. In each case I decided to keep the score as it is.

### 1. 209–216: a horn on T at the forte return (rows 3, 9, 10; kept)

T's forte return is the trio's climax, and a horn on the tune here is the obvious Romantic choice (PDF 27; the Eroica trio named in the brief). c251 kept the score for three reasons: the protected Hn 1·2 thirds (c004, c094), the three colours T already has, and the horn colour saved for 341. The third reason is weaker than recorded, because Hindemith saves an instrument that has not yet played the theme (PDF 20), and Hn 1 already plays T's head at 197–200. The decision still stands, for a reason the reading gives more plainly:
- The tune's octaves are G♯4–A5 (Cl 1, Vln II) and G♯5–A6 (Vln I).
- A horn on the upper of them would reach A5 sounding (written E6) at ff. That is the high loud horn writing Schuller warns against (PDF 26), and with two horns it would be the high horn unison reserved for the finale.
- The octave below (G♯3–A4) is occupied by Bsn 1's sustained E4–F♯4, the cello countermelody with Bsn 2, and from 217 Hn 4. A horn there would sit in unison with or cross those lines and blur the tenor.

c317's Hn 4 on the cello countermelody at 217–224 already supplies the horn–cello pairing of PDF 27. Kept.

### 2. 45–56: a tenor doubling for the first statement of the second idea (rows 3, 7, 10; kept)

This melody stays in two high octaves (D5–A6), and a horn and cello tenor octave would give it the weight of PDF 24 and 27. I decided against it:
- The tenor colour is the new colour of the third statement at 281 (c071, row 3), and using it at 45 would spend it.
- Hn 3·4 cannot enter before their protected "senza sord." entry at 73.
- Hn 1·2 are the only parts that keep the 3/4 barline against the hemiola (dotted minims while Cl, violas and the bass move in minims). Moving them to the melody would erase the metric conflict that defines the theme.

The memorability gap of the second idea is a question of its notes. This reading does not address that, and c321 found no better version that keeps the rising sixth and the hemiola.

### 3. 341–348: keep the horn line under Ob 1, as in *Lohengrin* (row 18; kept)

In Ex. XI-18 the brass line continues while its doubling passes from strings to woodwinds. Here Hn 3 stops at 344 and Ob 1 takes phrase 2. I tried Hn 3 an octave under Ob 1 at 345–348 (D4–B4). It fits between the oboe and Bsn 1's counterline (D3–B3), but it fills the gap that keeps the oboe's phrase a solo over a bass line, so the hand-over turns into three voices. It also keeps a horn sounding until eight bars before Hn 1's motto tail at 357, which blunts that entry (c226, c251). The present hand-over, horn → woodwind → strings with one colour at a time, reaches pp by elimination, and PDF 35 asks for nothing more. Kept.

### 4. 145–148: Hn 1 foreshadowing T's head G♯4–A4–B4 instead of the neighbour G♯–A–G♯ (row 3; kept)

A literal foreshadowing of the head, left unfinished before the clarinet completes it at 153, would make the trio tune easier to recognise. It would end the horn on B4 and give up the G♯ held into 149 (c340), the pivot that recalls bar 1 (the G♯ = A♭ frame). The neighbour already sounds T's first interval. Kept.

### 5. 225–236: the codetta relay at one dynamic level (row 18; kept)

*Lohengrin*'s hand-over leads to a diminuendo. Here Ob 1, Cl 1 and Bsn 1 each enter p dolce and swell to mp. The register falls an octave at each entry (G♯5, G♯4, G♯3), and the horns arrive pp at 237, so the passage already diminishes through register and colour. Marking Bsn 1 pp would only make it harder to hear under the pizzicato. The codetta is protected (c127). Kept.

### 6. 336–338: horns on the falling climax line (rows 4, 7; kept)

Bruckner builds the climax with brass octaves. Here the falling line B–A–F♯–D♯–E is in Fl 1/Vln I, Ob 1/Vln II and violas/C.a. (c071, c088), and the brass hold V7 with Hn 1's ♭9 C5 at 337 (c082). Putting Hn 1 on the line would put the brass on the melody at the movement's climax and remove the ♭9. Both run against the soft-brass rule and the finale's reserved brass melody. Kept (c304, c334).
