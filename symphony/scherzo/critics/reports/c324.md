## Findings

### 1. Bars 205–209: the anticipation of the motto's tail should be in the horn that sings the tail at 357 (applied)

**Problem.** At 205–208 the trio anticipates the motto's chromatic tail: G♯–A–B–A♯–A, falling to G♯ at 209. Fl 2 (G♯5) and Cl 2 (G♯4) play it in octaves. The tail returns at 357–360 as a single horn, Hn 1, B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4. That is the same pitch as Cl 2's line here, and the horn is the symphony's motto instrument (the horn call at 1–7, the G♯ frame, movement I's horn call; c060). Eight bars before, at 197–200, Hn 1 plays T's head G♯4–A4–B4–A4–G♯4. So the movement has the diatonic form of the figure in Hn 1, the chromatic form in clarinet and flute, and the motto proper in Hn 1 again. The one place where T's head turns into the motto tail is in a different colour from both of its neighbours.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 261: assigning "a motive or melody to a particular instrument or group of instruments ... can be an important device in bringing greater clarity to the form of a piece". In *Nuages* the figure is "always played by the English horn". In *Fêtes*, Debussy "chose to reiterate a certain melodic fragment with the same instrument throughout the piece. This gives great clarity and unity not only to the orchestration but to the entire form" (PDF 261, Ex. VIII-33 and 34, PDF 262). Adler PDF 255 says to change something the third time: here the horn plays the same figure three times (197, 205, 357), and the line changes each time (diatonic return, chromatic fall, the full tail on B), so the colour can stay the same. PDF 250 (the organ's 8-foot with 4-foot) supports keeping Fl 2 an octave above as the flute colour. PDF 257 says a unison of two colours "robs the timbral characteristics from both", so the horn replaces Cl 2 rather than doubling it. c158 made this line the primary voice, p espress., and that is kept. The reasons recorded for the old scoring (c036: Cl 1 and Fl 1 rest before 209; c050: the line ends on G♯ at 209) both still hold. Hn 1 is free at 201–208. It rests 201–204 (about 3 s) after 195–200, and its longest run is now 205–216 legato at p with a break between the slurs at 208/209 and the existing breath at 216 (Adler PDF 292, 303 on horn rests). The line lies at G♯4–B4, inside the horn's middle register, and is not a high horn unison. Hn 3·4's held D♯4/B3 (pp to p) lies below the line's lowest note, so the background stays out of the melody's register (Adler PDF 135).
