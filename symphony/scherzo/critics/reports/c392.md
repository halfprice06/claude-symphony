## Findings

### 1. Bar 280: parallel 5ths between the tune and Hn 2/violas, put back by c383 (applied)

**Problem.** At 279→280 the tune falls C♯6–B5 (Fl 1) and C♯5–B4 (Ob 1) on beat 2 of 280. Hn 2 and the violas move F♯4–E4 at the same moment. The result is consecutive perfect 5ths: compound 5ths with Fl 1 and simple 5ths with Ob 1, in the same register. In the exposition the same spot (124) was fixed by c112: "124 Hn 2/Vla F♯4-G4 (no 5ths with the tune's C♯6-B5)" (sC.ily comment). c383 put the reprise into E major at 277–280. To keep the G♯ single under the V6/5 of iv, it wrote the inner voice as E4, and in doing so it brought the 5ths back.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 267: in a repeated pattern "5ths and octaves can become a problem", and voice-leading tones are what prevent them. The second idea is the movement's main sequential theme (c032). A&S PDF 83: parallel perfect 5ths are excluded. Hn 2 and the violas form a real sustained line here (G♯–G♯–F♯–E), not a filler, so PDF 85's exemption does not apply. c383's reason is sound and stays met. On the last quaver of 280 (the D♮ that makes the chord V6/5 of iv), the G♯ must be in the bass only.

**Fix (applied, sections/dA.ily, bar 280).**
```
dAVla:   fis'2.~( | fis'4 gis'4 e'4)        (was fis'2.~( | fis'4 e'2))
dAHnTwo: fis'2.--~ | fis'4 gis'4-- e'4--)   (was fis'2.--~ | fis'4 e'2--))
```
On beat 2 the inner voice takes G♯4, the third of the plain I6, as c112 took G4 in minor. The tune's B5 against it is a 6th, not a 5th. On beat 3 the inner voice goes to E4, so the G♯ is single (bass G♯2) before the D♮ quaver arrives, as c383 requires. The E4 then goes to E4 (violas) and C4 (Hn 2) at 281 as before. The scan finds no parallels at 279–281 now.

### 2. Bars 127–128: Bsn 1 in octaves with Cl 1 and then with the bass (applied)

**Problem.** Under the E-minor second idea, Bsn 1 plays G3 (127) – F♯3 (127, beat 3, tied) – G3 (128, beat 2). Cl 1 plays G4–F♯4 in the same rhythm at 127, so the two make octaves G–F♯. The bass (Vc, Cb, Cbsn) plays F♯2–G2 in the same rhythm at 127–128, so Bsn 1 makes octaves F♯–G with the bass as well. Bsn 1's own line (A–E–F♯–G–F♯–G) is independent, so these are not doublings: it falls into one voice and then into another. The chord on beat 3 of 127 is a bare V6/4 (F♯, B, D♯) between i and i6.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83: parallel octaves are excluded. A&S PDF 123 and 128: V4/3 "forms a natural connection between I and I6" and is "very frequent" as a passing chord in that position. When it moves in 10ths with the bass, "4̂ ascends" (the 7th rises). A&S PDF 269: 10-10 patterns are better than patterns with 8ves. The bass here is E–F♯–G (c013), which gives exactly the 10ths G–A–B.

**Fix (applied, sections/sC.ily, sCBsnOne bars 127–128).**
```
g2( a4~ | a4 b2) | b2.~\f      (was g2( fis4~ | fis4 g2) | b2.~\f)
```
Bsn 1 now runs G3–A3–B3 in 10ths over E2–F♯2–G2. Beat 3 of 127 becomes V4/3 (its 7th, A3, appears only here). Against Cl 1's F♯4–E4 it makes 6ths. Against the tune's D♯6–B5 it moves inward to the B octave. The line arrives on the B3 that Bsn 1 already holds f at 129, the root of the cadential 6/4. The reprise (283–284) is left as it is: there Bsn 1 doubles the bass E–F♯–G for three notes, which A&S PDF 85 treats as a doubling and not as a parallel.

### Considered and not raised

- **33–35, the call's G♯5 over A minor falling to F♯5 over D (VII).** It looks like a chromatic interval kept where a diatonic sequence would change it (PDF 266). But 29–35 is not a transposed sequence. The winds and strings restate theme A's bars 13–15 and 17–19 literally, and A–G♯–F♯ is the theme's own bar 18 (`a''2-> gis''8 g''8 | fis''4`), the motto tail on iv. Left.
- **47, Hn 1/Hn 2 A–G in octaves.** From 47 on, Hn 2 doubles Hn 1 at the octave (A3/A4 through 52), so this is the start of an octave doubling (PDF 85). Left.
- **194→195, Cl 1 C♯5–B4 with Vln I C♯6–B5.** Cl 1 joins Vln II in unison for that step. It is a doubling inside the protected trio middle (c302). Left.
- **280→281 and 300→301, octaves inside the clarinets and in the cellos' double stop.** These are doublings and chord fillers (PDF 85). Left.
- **329–332, Vln I 10-5-4-10 over E–D–C–B.** A strict descending 5-6 (PDF 277–279) needs C–E–G on C, which is the forbidden C-major triad. The ♭II6/4 and the French sixth on beat 3 of 331 are the right substitute. Left (c122, c212, c302).
- **73–78, harp head repeated by stepwise descent (A, G, F).** PDF 287 says this "often indicates a sequence with descending 5ths". From F, descending 5ths reach V of F, the C-major triad. The rising bass F–G–A (protected since c122) is the answer. Left.
- **89–97, the head three times on E (Vla E4, Ob E5, Vln I E5).** This is a repetition over the V pedal, not a sequence. The rise is carried by the change of instrument and by the arpeggios at 98–100 up to the hemiola. Left (c019).
