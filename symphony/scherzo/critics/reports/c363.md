## Findings

No principle of the unit is broken. The four earlier passes reached the same verdict. Since c273 the cycles have touched the bars where key areas are made: 169–184 (c344, c349, c357), 199/211/343 (c327), 253–268 (c359), 305–320 (c362), 331 (c332) and 343 (c347). I re-checked each of them against the reading and found nothing new to fix.

The focus asks for reasons rather than locks. In four places below the reading gives a stronger reason than the one on record, and I state it so the next critic tests that reason. I decided every point myself. None needs a change to the notes.

### 1. The trio gives V nine bars, where Dvořák gives it 62 (keep: the reading supplies the stronger reason)

- **Bars.** 177–193 (B area 180–184 and 189–192), middle part 185–208.
- **Issue.** PDF 219 and Points 8b (PDF 228) name the tonicized V as the usual basis of an ABA middle section. Dvořák 9/iii's trio puts whole phrases in V. This trio touches V only twice and builds its middle on vi. This is the largest change the unit could argue for, so I tested it against the bars.
- **Evidence.** Read with Unit 14, the B area is already a complete modulation in miniature, in the unit's own order:
  - pivot at 180, the chord just before the first V of B (PDF 217): ii6/5 of B over E, the I5–6 sonority of Ex. 14-6b;
  - cadential 6/4 and V7 at 181–182, with ♯4 (A♯) in one voice only;
  - authentic cadence with 2–1 in the line at 183 (PDF 219, 226);
  - punctuation: the held B and Fl 1's F♯–G♯–A♯–B at 183–184 (PDF 216);
  - springboard into C♯ minor at 185 (PDF 222). C♯ minor is the pivot chord read backwards: ii of B, vi of E.
  - The answer at 189–192 returns to B and ends on its V. Cl 1 then turns A♯ into A♮ at 192–193, which is the cancellation of Ex. 14-13b ("cancelled by the F♮ in bar 6", PDF 222), done by 4 of the main key (PDF 220).
  - A middle section in V would repeat this whole process at greater length. It would also push out the C♯ minor call and response (c091, c137) that gives the trio its one minor-mode contrast.
  - The recorded reasons were the Jupiter trio (c273) and the shaping of 185–192 (c183). The stronger reason is that the B area already does everything PDF 216–222 asks of a modulation, so more time in B would add length and no new function.
- **Decision.** Keep. Fix: none. The c093 comment in tA.ily calls 180 "ii6/5 of B over E". The composer should add "(the I5–6 pivot of A&S Ex. 14-6b)" so the bar is not "simplified" later.

### 2. Three half cadences of T, graded by the reading's own scale (keep, with a new reason)

- **Bars.** 159–160 (harp F♯–C♯–E, no A♯), 175–176 (V7/V, A♯ in Vln II), 213–216 (V6/5 of V over the bass A–A♯–B).
- **Issue.** The harp chord at 159 has no third, and c089/c292 protect it as "the plain reference the later statements colour".
- **Evidence.** PDF 215 and Ex. 14-4 rank exactly these three forms. With the diatonic chord "the V wants to continue moving". With the applied chord the semicadence becomes a goal. With 4–♯4–5 in the bass it is stronger still.
  - At 160 the music should continue: T's consequent follows at once.
  - At 176 the semicadence is followed by the modulation.
  - At 216 the semicadence is the last V before the trio's climax.
  - The weakest form stands where continuation is wanted and the strongest where arrival is wanted. That reason is stronger than "reference".
- **Decision.** Keep all three. Fix: none.

### 3. Length 4:38 and proportions 97 / 88 / 49 / 44 s; no repeat signs (keep)

- **Bars.** Whole movement.
- **Issue.** The plan says about 5 minutes. PDF 224 says dance halves usually repeat. The tonic E holds from 105 to 179 (about 55 s).
- **Evidence.**
  - The three blocks balance (97 / 88 / 93 s).
  - The reprise is 51 % of part 1 against Dvořák's 60 %. The reprise keeps a departure, the fugato G minor → A → V at 289–304, which PDF 220 requires for a return to register.
  - A trio repeat (185–224, about 32 s) would restate T's forte return, the trio's one climax.
  - The stretch on E is articulated by mode, tempo and texture (link 145, trio 153), and PDF 220 lists modulation as one means of articulation, not the only one.
  - The coda is 14 % of the movement against Dvořák's 9 %. Its second stage is the "varied repetition of the final cadence" (PDF 220): T's half cadence in A doubles as the pivot at 348, then come 353–356 and the plagal 357–360. That stage carries the joins to the finale, so it is protected.
  - Between a 5:45 Adagio and a 7-minute finale, 4:38 is the right weight.
- **Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 4. C♯ in both outer voices at 49–50 (keep, with the unit's own reason)

- **Bars.** 49 beat 3 – 50 beat 2, G-major second idea. C♯ sounds in eight parts: Fl, Ob, Vln I, Vln II against Bsn, Cbsn, Vc, Cb.
- **Issue.** My scan flags it under PDF 214's "Don't double ♯4".
- **Evidence.** The rule's own ground is "because ♯4 functions (temporarily) as a leading tone". At 49–50 the C♯ is the third of a prolonged A chord, and both voices leave it to A, not to D. It has no leading-tone function there. Where C♯ does lead to D (52, V6/5 of V), it is in the bass line alone (c122).
  - The record cites the sequence exception of Unit 17 (PDF 267). Unit 14 itself is a stronger reason, because it does not depend on calling the passage a sequence.
- **Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 5. The coda's IV area and its return (keep)

- **Bars.** 341–349.
- **Evidence.** A major is a phrase modulation after the G.P. (PDF 223).
  - A has its own dominant at 343 and 347–348 (PDF 216).
  - It has an inner 4–♯4–5 into its V: Bsn D3–D♯3–E3 at 345–347 under B7 (PDF 215). The leading tone D♯ is never doubled.
  - The E chord at 348 is I of E and V of A at once, and it needs no preparation (PDF 219).
  - Ob 1 reaches E5 through D♯5, with the line going to 1 (PDF 219).
  - It still holds after c347's violas at 343.
- **Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 6. The low-activity span 133–152 into the soft trio (keep)

- **Bars.** 133–152 (20 bars, 15 s, 8–26 attacks per four bars), then 153–184.
- **Issue.** This is the movement's longest quiet stretch, and the lens asks whether the movement keeps moving.
- **Evidence.**
  - PDF 220: the large division needs articulation. The articulation here is Hn 1's G→G♯ at 145, a change of mode, which needs no pivot.
  - The trio's first V comes at 180, inside the time the reading allows for a first key area (rows 7 and 21).
  - The span is shorter than Dvořák's 34-bar transition into his trio (bars 201–234).
  - Every way to shorten it touches protected bars: 133–152 (c253, c293, c353) or 153–168 (c089).
- **Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.
