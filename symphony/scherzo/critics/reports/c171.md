### 1. 113–116 and 313–316: in the tutti theme the top line is lowest at the theme's climax (recorded; no change)

**Problem.**
- Theme A's climax is its twelfth bar: C6 over V, the theme's only accented dissonance (♭9), marked ">" and reached by a stepwise swell (c001, c038).
- In the ff tutti statements the flutes (a2) double the tune an octave up at 109–112 and 117–120 and in unison at 113–116.
- `top.py` gives the highest sounding notes as:
  - 110: A6
  - 111: F♯6
  - 113: A5
  - 115: B5
  - 116: C6
  - 118: G6

  The reprise at 310–318 is the same.
- So the top line sits a sixth below the A6 of six bars before, and a fifth below the G6 two bars after, at the very bar where the melody peaks.

**Evidence.** Aldwell & Schachter PDF 50: "unusually high or low notes come across more strongly". By register, the strongest top notes fall at 110 and 118, not on the climax. The register accents at 110 and 118 do coincide with theme A's intended weak-bar counter-accents (PDF 51, row 4; c081, c148), so the passage is not incoherent. It does weaken the climax.

**Why no change.** Each possible fix breaks a standing rule:
- Flutes in the upper octave at 113–116 reach C7 at 116, above the movement's single B6 at 336 (cyclic rule; c035: no B6 before the coda peak).
- Flutes in unison throughout 109–120 undo c025, c036, c078 and c124 (flutes high in the tuttis, entering high at 109/309). They would also leave the flutes in their weak middle register under ff brass.
- A held A6 (the chord seventh) at 116 only equals 110's height.

I decide to keep the present scoring. The constraint comes from reserving C7 for the finale. If a later cycle ever relaxes the B6 ceiling for winds, this is the first place to use it.
