## Verdict on the large form

The form and the length are right. Do not change the section lengths.

- **Length.** At 4:36 the scherzo is the shortest of the four movements and about 19% of the symphony's 24½ minutes. Dvořák 9's scherzo has about the same share.
- **Reprise.** The reprise is half the length of the scherzo (49 s against 97 s). It changes the order of the scherzo's events and leaves out the F-major episode, so it does not read as a copy.
- **Slow music.** The slower tempo takes about 40% of the time (trio, link and retransition rits, coda stage 2). That is a lot for a scherzo, but the coda has to end soft and slow: the cyclic G♯ ending is protected, and the finale enters ff after a pause. The fast coda stage (325–340) is short, but it is complete. It goes ostinato, Phrygian descent, head ff, B6 fff, hemiola, chord and G.P. in 15 bars, the way Beethoven 9/ii ends its fast music.
- **Pedals.** The joins around the trio rest on E pedals: 133–152 (20 bars) and 225–244 (20 bars). This is the same practice as Dvořák's pedal-based link, and the movement does not stall elsewhere.

## Findings

### 1. Retransition 243–245: there is no step between iv and the structural V, and bar 244 is a bare viola E4 (applied)

**Bars.** 241–245 (P, poco a poco accelerando).

**Problem.** The retransition's bass is pizz. E at 241, A at 243 and B at 245. Bars 242 and 244 have no bass at all. At 244 the whole orchestra plays one note, the violas' held E4 (MIDI dump: 244.1–244.3, only E4). The iv at 243 jumps straight to the dominant that the reprise depends on. This is the movement's most important arrival on V. The rest of the score prepares its dominants with more care:

- At 84–88 the exposition's retransition reaches V through the Neapolitan 6/3 over A and then vii°7 over A, held two bars.
- At 125–126 and 281–282 the second idea uses iv6–V.
- At 329–332 the coda uses the full Phrygian descent E–D–C–B.

Here the empty bar weakens the drive of the accelerando at exactly the point where the tempo is rising into Tempo I.

**Evidence.** Unit 18 gives two ways to fill this gap:

- A 6/3 can expand a 5/3, with the bass rising a 3rd under the same harmony (Ex. 18-19e, PDF 301).
- iv6 reaches V by the Phrygian half step ♭6–5 in the bass (Ex. 18-12, PDF 297–298).

Ex. 18-11 (PDF 297) also says that the chord before a tonicized V is lengthened so that V "falls on a strong beat". Here V arrives at 245, the downbeat of the second four-bar group, and the bass now moves once per bar (A, C, B) into it. The c013 reason (approach V from the subdominant) is kept, since iv6 is still the subdominant.

**C-major check.** Hn 1's G4 stops on beat 3 of 243, so 244 holds only A–C–E. cmajor.py reports none.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-053/work/sections/retr.ily).** Bar 244, cellos and basses, still pizz.:

```lilypond
retrVc = { e4\pp r4 r4 | R2. | a,4 r4 r4 | <c a>4 r4 r4 | b,4 r4 r4 | ... }
retrCb = { e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2. | a,,4 r4 r4 | c,4 r4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r4 | ... }
```

The cello double stop is C3 + A3, playable pizz. on the C and G strings. The violas' E4 (243–245) becomes the 3rd of iv6 and then the prepared 4th that resolves to D♯ at 246, so c037's dovetail is unchanged. Nothing else is changed.

## Checked and kept (decided, no change)

- **Coda 331: no 7–6 into iv6.** Unit 18 says the suspension is "almost invariably found" with the Phrygian progression (PDF 297). Adding one here would mean taking the resolving A3 out of the violas, cellos, Bsn 1 and Hn 4 on the downbeat of 331, inside a motive-x ostinato building through mf to ff. It would also go back over c032's decision that no B should sound against the bass C. A one-beat dissonance inside a fast crescendo would not be heard as a suspension. No change.
- **Link 141–152 with no 6/3 transition.** A descending 6/3 series under Hn 1's held G4 (141–144) or G♯4 (145–148) clashes with the horn in every chord. In E minor it also reaches C–E–G on the 6/3 over E, which the cyclic rule forbids. The link joins E minor to E major, two stable points on the same tonic, so a pedal is the right means. Dvořák's link (9/iii 142–175) is also built on pedals. No change.
- **Trio 201–203 (A minor → B).** This could take the same iv6 as finding 1. I left it: the C natural in the bass right after the upper voices' C♯ at 201 would make a cross relation, which c042 removed elsewhere. Also, one Phrygian approach per formal join is enough, and the structural one is the retransition.
- **G major at 37–39 in root position.** I considered I6 there, so that the second idea at 45 would be the first root-position G. I rejected it because 33–39 is a circle-of-fifths sequence (A–D–G) whose goal is properly a 5/3 (principle 1: 5/3s are goals).
- **Section lengths, the reprise order, the two-stage coda and the G.P.** Unchanged (see the verdict above).
