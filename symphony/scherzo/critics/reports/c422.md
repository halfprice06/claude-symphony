## Findings

### 1. Bar 300, beat 3: an Italian sixth before the reprise's dominant (applied)

**The problem.** In the exposition, the development reaches its dominant at 89 through the Neapolitan machinery (85–88: N6 over A, D♯°7/A, A♯°7, i6/4). The retransition reaches its V at 245 through the French sixth at 244. The coda reaches its V at 332 through the N6/4 and French sixth at 331. The reprise cuts the F-major episode (this is planned, and it stays cut). That leaves 301, the dominant that leads into the reprise's ff tutti at 305, as the one structural V with no chromatic approach. The strings hold the A-major chord through beat 3 of 300 after the winds cut off, and then step straight up to B.

**The evidence.**
- A&S PDF 526, sec. 17: "One very important possibility is a modulation that returns to I, as at the end of a development section… a motion from a tonicized IV through an augmented 6th to the home dominant" (Ex. 29-28a, PDF 527).
- PDF 503: augmented sixths "often occur just before important structural points".
- Review 6, PDF 526: their long-range role is "preparing return modulations to I".
- 297–300 is that tonicized IV. The stretto entries are in A minor over the A pedal and end on A major, and nothing in the reprise follows it as V of D.

**Answering the earlier rejections.**
- **c062: "the movement's one augmented sixth".** This lapsed when c332 added the French sixth at 331.
- **c242: "A major is heard as V of D".** That holds in the exposition, where F major follows. In the reprise, A is the goal of an A-minor stretto with no D anywhere near. A&S PDF 509 says that after a strong IV, 4̂ "will carry over as root".
- **c242: "a marker of one structural point".** PDF 503 says "points", in the plural. The book's own Ex. 29-1 uses augmented sixths twice in one retransition (bars 73 and 78).
- **c332: "a copy of the retransition's French sixth".** I use the Italian form, not the French. A&S (PDF 505) give it for "a thin, transparent sonority" that "usually moves directly to V". That is the situation here: the strings play alone for one beat after the winds stop, and the hemiola V follows at once. The three approaches now differ in chord, dynamic and scoring: French pp pizzicato at 244, Italian ff arco at 300, French at the climax at 331. Every augmented sixth to V in E minor has C in the bass and resolves to B. That is the chord, not a repetition.

**What the listener hears.** The ff strings' last beat of the development becomes a new chord. Its outer voices C2 and A♯5 open by contrary semitones to the octave B1/B5 on the sfp of 301 (Ex. 29-2). The cut from the A-minor stretto into the hemiola build now has the "intensity of the resolution to V" that PDF 510 describes, instead of a parallel step A→B.

**The fix (dB.ily, bar 300).** These are the beat-3 crotchets that were the held A-major chord. Ties removed:

```
dBVlnOne: e'''2->\ff\dnb ais''4->\upbow        % was e'''2->\ff\dnb~ e'''4\upbow
dBVlnTwo: <e'' cis'''>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." e''4->\upbow
dBVla:    <e' a'>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." e'4->\upbow
dBVc:     <a, e>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." <c e>4->\upbow
dBCb:     a,,2->\ff\dnb c,4->\upbow
```

**Voice leading of the new chord.**
- The chord is C2, C3, E3, E4, E5, A♯5. This is an Italian sixth with the third doubled, as PDF 505 says. There is no G, so no C-major triad.
- The bass moves A→C and the top voice E6→A♯5. This is A&S's chromaticized voice exchange from IV (Ex. 29-28a). The C♯6 of beats 1–2 against the C of beat 3 is the cross relation that this exchange always produces (A/A♭ in Ex. 29-28a).
- The motions into 301 are:
  - C→B in the basses
  - A♯5→B5 in Vln I
  - E4→D♯4 in the violas
  - E5→B4 in Vln II, which joins its octave line
- The outer voices move in contrary motion.

## Considered, not changed

- **86, Cl 1 C–B–B♭–A over the N6 (review 14c: ♯4̂, not ♭5̂).** B♭ belongs to the scale of F, which has been tonicized for twelve bars. It points down to A, which PDF 498 (Ex. 28-25) accepts when the flat shows tonal direction. It is also the motto tail's spelling. I kept it.
- **85–87: ♭2̂ leaves only by F→F♯ (Hn 4).** The doubled F (Hn 4 and the harp, which is damped at 87) is in inner voices, which Ex. 28-3d calls "possible". The diminished third F→D♯ that c180 gave to Cl 2 was removed by c342 for good reasons (a doubled leading tone). I kept it.
- **Trio 202–203, iv–V (Ex. 29-6).** An augmented sixth here would carry the scherzo's chromaticism into the soft trio, and the iv at 202 already gives ♭6̂. I kept c062/c332's decision.
- **357–376, ♭II or an augmented sixth on ♭2̂ inside the final tonic (Ex. 28-20, 29-36).** This passage is protected. An F would clash with the motto tail's A, and the A♯°7–iv close already does this job.
