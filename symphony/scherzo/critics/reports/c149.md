## What else I studied

Mahler, Symphony No. 1, ii, "Kräftig bewegt, doch nicht zu schnell". The local corpus has no Mahler. I used the Mahler Foundation's movement page and listening guide (https://www.mahlerfoundation.org/?p=603, https://mahlerfoundation.org/?p=24606) and a Keeping Score page on its folk sources (https://keepingscore.org/interactive/gustav-mahler/origins/folk-folkways). From these sources, the facts of the movement are:
- It is a Ländler in A with a trio in F, "Recht gemächlich".
- The main section is at minim = 66 and the trio at 54, a ratio of 0.82.
- A four-bar solo horn changes mood and tempo into the trio, and the solo horn leads back to the reprise.
- The reprise is shortened, has no repeats and is scored more heavily.
- The ending speeds up and is cut off by one grace-note stroke.

Point by point, v149 makes the same structural choices:
- The tempo ratio is 88 to 76, which is 0.86.
- Hn 1 carries the link into the trio (141–148, foreshadowing T at 147–148) and out of it (237–241, G♯ to G♮).
- The reprise is shortened (dB 16 bars, dC 20) and scored more heavily (the reprise tutti 305–320).
- The coda accelerates (Più mosso, 2. = 96) to one short chord at 339.

The difference is the ending. Mahler stops on the stroke. This score adds the trio recall and the soft G♯. That close is protected and required by the cyclic plan (G♯ = A♭ into the finale), so it is not a finding. Mahler's character comes from devices this score does not use: stopped and bells-up horns, col legno, and Ländler portamento. Adding them would cut across Dan's soft-brass rule and the plan's neo-Romantic models. I do not recommend them.

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? On the large points, yes: the plan, the key scheme, the hemiolas, the horn links, the motivic timpani and the trio layering. The orchestration at the level Rimsky-Korsakov's operatic examples teach (light accompaniment under solos, a tutti version of the trio tune, offstage-thin scoring at the close) matches what he shows in Ex. 277–312. Only one local point betrays a less experienced hand.

## Findings

### 1. Hn 1·2 re-enter mf after a long rest (41–43, 273–275): applied
**Problem.** At 41 Hn 1·2 enter on F♯4/D4 under the a2 woodwind theme. They enter mf and hold for three bars. Hn 1 has rested since bar 7 (34 bars), and Hn 2 has not played at all. At 273 the same happens after 32 bars' rest. c147 lowered these entries from f to mf so that the horns sit a degree under the woodwind's f. That balance reason is sound (Rimsky PDF 108–112). It left, though, the one entry level Rimsky-Korsakov rules out.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136: "After a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful, while re-introducing these instruments mezzo-forte or mezzo-piano produces a colourless and common-place effect."
- His examples in the assigned range bring the horns in this way. In Ex. 298 (PDF 482) Hn IV enters pp, then Hn III–IV join "poco cresc." (PDF 483). In Ex. 304 (PDF 493) the horns enter on a pp chord. In Ex. 312 (PDF 502) they enter a2 p, and later all parts crescendo together.
- Every other horn re-entry in this score already follows the rule: 73, 153, 195 (c139), 201 (c049), 225, 237, 331 enter pp; 101/301 enter p by c076's one-degree rule. So 41/273 are the only exceptions.

**Fix (applied).** The horns enter pp and swell to c147's mf by the third bar. c147's balance is kept from 43 onwards. Both players have the same dynamics, so the shared staff stays clean.
- sA.ily: `sAHnOne = { R2.*28 | fis'2.~\pp\< | fis'2.~ | fis'2.\mf | d'4 r4 r4 | ...` and `sAHnTwo ... | d'2.~\pp\< | d'2.~ | d'2.\mf | a4 r4 r4 | ...`
- dA.ily: `dAHnOne ... | fis'2.~\pp\< | fis'2.~ | fis'2.\mf | fis'4 r4 r4 | ...` and `dAHnTwo ... | d'2.~\pp\< | d'2.~ | d'2.\mf | b4 r4 r4 | ...`

The mf from 43 carries into 45 as c147 intended. This modifies c147's finding but does not undo it: the balance under the woodwinds is the same from bar 43.

No other findings. The remaining principles in the table are followed, or they concern passages whose reasons earlier critics have already answered (101/301 horns p, c076; low brass f at 333, c086/c123; closing page 357–376).
