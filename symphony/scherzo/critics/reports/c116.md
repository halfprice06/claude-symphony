## What else I studied

- Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles*, harp (PDF 45–47, page images viewed):
  - In the middle and lower octaves the resonance "is slightly prolonged, and dies away gradually. In changes of harmony the player stops the vibration of the strings with his hands"; otherwise "the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect" (PDF 46).
  - Chord notes "written close together" (PDF 46).
  - Harmonic chords "two for the left hand and one for the right" (PDF 47).
  - A single harp is not heard against a forte orchestra (PDF 47).
- MOLA guidelines PDF 5: "Harp pedaling should be left to the performer and not printed". This is the source of c075's `partCleanHarp` in parts-lib.ily.
- Movement II v201 intro.ily, where c116 of that movement puts the starting setting in the part (A♭: D♭ C B♭ / E♭ F G A♭).
- Score pages 1, 3, 7, 8 and 13. All four pages of the harp part (partsout/parts-25-harp.pdf).
- Where the trio's RH chord changes on beat 2, the RH rests on beat 1. It has that beat to stop the old strings while the LH plays the bass, so the bar-by-bar changes at 153–168, 185–192, 225–236 and 341–348 follow Rimsky's rule as written.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 1: no starting pedal setting, and the harp part shows no setting at all. Applied.
- **Problem.** The harp first plays at 73. The score gives "Ped.: D C B♭ / E F G A" only under that first note. In the part even that is gone: `partCleanHarp` drops every markup that begins "Ped." (MOLA PDF 5). So the player reads no setting anywhere.
- **What the change involves.** Movement II ends with the harp on D♭ C B♭ / E♭ F G A♭. The pause therefore has to bring D♮ (left foot) and E♮ and A♮ (right foot, one after the other).
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 112: "It is a help to the harpist to indicate how the pedals should be set at the beginning of a work", preferably in letters. The timpani already have their bar-1 tuning label (BRIEF.md). Movement II's harp has its starting setting in the part for the same reason (mvt2 v201 intro.ily, c116). MOLA's rule concerns pedalling during the music, not the opening setting.
- **Fix (intro.ily).** The text does not start with "Ped.", so the part keeps it. The score keeps its reminder at 73.
  ```
  introHarpL = { \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #LEFT \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.parent-alignment-X = #LEFT R2._\markup \small \line { \italic "set pedals in the pause:" "D C B♭ / E F G A" } | R2.*11 | }
  ```
  I checked it in both the score (page 1) and the rebuilt part (page 2).

### 2. Bars 87, 193, 241: ringing harp strings bring back cross relations that were removed from the score. Applied.
- **Bar 87.** At 86 the LH strikes A1 and then the minim C3–F3–A3. Half a second later, at 87, F♯ enters in Hn 4 (F♯3) and Cl 1 (F♯4) over the D♯°7/A. Nothing tells the harpist to stop the strings, so F3 sounds against F♯3. That is the F/F♯ cross relation c042 resolved into one voice and c110 protects ("F→F♯ exit at 86–88").
- **Bar 193.** At 192 the RH strikes A♯4–C♯5–F♯5 on beats 2 and 3. At 193, A♮ enters in Cl 1 (A4), the violas and Bsn 1 (A3). The ringing A♯4 recreates the A♯/A clash c042 also removed (192–193).
- **Bar 241.** The rolled E-major chord at 237 includes G♯3 in the middle register. At 241 Hn 1 turns G♯ into G♮ for the E-minor retransition, which is a protected pivot. About four seconds of slower bars pass first, so less is left. Still, the last G♯ should come only from the horn.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 113–114: the lower and middle registers are "sonorous", and the lower eleven strings are wound (PDF 112). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: at a change of harmony "the player stops the vibration of the strings with his hands", otherwise "the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect". In all three places a hand is free: the LH after 86 beat 2, the RH after 192, and both hands from 238.
- **Notation.** The mark is the damping word movement I already uses ("étouffez", I 182). Adler's "sons étouffés" (PDF 117) is the related technique of damping each note as it is played.
- **Fix.**
  - sB.ily, `sBHarpL`, bar 87: `\once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #LEFT \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.parent-alignment-X = #LEFT R2._\markup \small \italic "étouffez"` (was `R2.`).
  - tB.ily, `tBHarpR`, bar 193: the same markup on the first `R2.` after the A♯ chord.
  - retr.ily (new variable): `retrHarpR = { <same overrides> R2._\markup \small \italic "étouffez" | R2.*11 | }`.

  No notes change. The partCleanHarp filter keeps the word, and all three marks print in the score and the part.

## Checked and kept (decided)
- **Close-position off-beat triads in the trio and coda.** Adler (PDF 114) prefers wide three-note chords. Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 46) asks for close chords with little space between the hands. The close triads follow Rimsky, and they keep the harp below the solo (c004, c028). Kept.
- **Repeated RH chords on beats 2 and 3.** At 2. = 76 the chords come 0.26 s apart. At 81–84, at 2. = 88, they come 0.23 s apart. One hand can repeat a three-note chord at that speed, and the free beat 1 gives time to damp. Kept.
- **LH bass alone, mf, at 209–224 under the forte return of T.** Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 47) says one harp is not heard as a line against a forte orchestra. Here it only adds a pluck to the arco basses' downbeats, and it carries straight on into the pp return at 225. Kept.
- **Pedal changes at 188, 190/192, 209, 234 and 333.** The timing is adequate, no foot moves two pedals at once, and the marks sit in the gap before each change. The settings at 141 and 333 stay score-only, as MOLA asks and as movement II does. In the part, the key signatures (F major at 73, E major at 149 and 341) already give those settings, and D♮ at 343 is printed as an accidental.
- **MIDI.** The harmonics at 375 play at written pitch (E3, G♯3, B3) instead of an octave higher. The single score feeds both the layout and the MIDI, so fixing this needs a separate MIDI score. I left it. The printed part is correct.
