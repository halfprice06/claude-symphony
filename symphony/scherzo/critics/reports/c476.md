## Findings

### 1. Bars 149 and 151: make the harp's entry the movement's final chord, E4–G♯4–B4 in harmonics (applied)

- **Problem.** At 149–152 the link reaches E major. Hn 1 has carried the G→G♯ turn and lets go of G♯4 after beat 1 of 149. Under it are a pp horn drone (Hn 3·4, B3/E3), single pp timpani and pizzicato strokes, and the harp's first notes since 86: a bass on beat 1 and a rolled ordinary chord G♯4–B4–E5 on beat 2. This is the quietest, most lightly accompanied passage the harp plays. It is exactly the setting Adler gives for harmonics, yet the harp plays ordinary rolled chords there. So the movement's one harmonic chord (375, E4–G♯4–B4 with Hn 1's G♯4) has no earlier sound to answer. The G♯ pivot that frames the movement (Hn 1 at 1–2 and 360–376) reaches the trio as a plain chord.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 116: harmonics are "very typical, practical, and beautifully bell-like". They are "very soft and must be accompanied very lightly or played a cappella". Several can sound in one hand "if the compass is not more than a perfect fifth", and the best written range is C3 to the A above the treble staff. At 149/151 the accompaniment is a pp drone and single pp strokes, so the passage meets the condition. The harmonics' written notes E3–B3 lie well inside the best range. The v476 chord G♯4–B4–E5 spans a minor sixth, so in harmonics it would need both hands, and the left hand is busy with the beat-1 bass. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47 gives the practical shape: three close notes, two in the LH and one in the RH. That is the same layout as 375. Adler PDF 117 (melody, accompaniment, doubling) does not require the harp to double the bass. The pizzicato basses and the timpani already sound E and B on those downbeats.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-476/work/sections/link.ily).** The harp's beat-1 bass at 149 and 151 is dropped, so both hands are free to place the nodes. On beat 2 the harp plays the chord of 375 in harmonics: LH E3–G♯3 and RH B3, written with "o", sounding E4–G♯4–B4, p, marked "harm." at the first one. The harmonic G♯4 takes over Hn 1's G♯4 at the same pitch on the beat after Hn 1 lets go. At the trio's door and at the movement's last sound, the horn's G♯ is now answered by the same bell chord. A local function plays the harmonics an octave up and softer in the MIDI, as `\harmOct` does at 375, and then resets the harp's MIDI volume so the trio's harp is unaffected.

  ```lilypond
  harmOctLink = #(define-music-function (m) (ly:music?) #{ \set PianoStaff.midiMaximumVolume = #0.6 \transposition c'' #m \transposition c' \unset PianoStaff.midiMaximumVolume #})
  linkHarpR = { R2.*8 | r4 \harmOctLink { b4-\flageolet } r4 | R2. | r4 \harmOctLink { b4-\flageolet } r4 | R2. | }
  linkHarpL = { ...Ped. setting at 141... | R2.*7 | r4 \harmOctLink { <e gis>4-\flageolet\p^\markup \small \italic "harm." } r4 | R2. | r4 \harmOctLink { <e gis>4-\flageolet } r4 | R2. | }
  ```

- **Playability.** No pedal change is needed (E♮, G♯ and B♮ have been set since 141). Both hands have rested since 86 and are free on beat 1 of each bar. Each hand holds at most two harmonics a third apart, inside Adler's fifth. The harp's dynamics are still printed once, on the LH staff, and the trio's harp keeps its own pp at 153.
- **What was given up, and why the change is still better.** The harp no longer previews the trio's bass-plus-chord pattern at 149/151. The timpani and pizzicato basses still play the beat-1 bass, and 153 states the pattern itself two bars later. The top E5 is lost, but the drone and the harmonics still spell E major. c174's B1 at 151 was there to avoid an E2 a fourth above the basses. With no harp bass, that problem cannot occur.
- **Parts.** The harp part's cue (Hn 1, 145–148) still points at a sounding instrument. No other cue is affected.

No other findings. I traced the pedals, timings, ranges, hand spans and damping again and found no fault. The three passes since c296 confirm what c206 settled.

## Checked and kept (decided)

- **Harmonics at 375 struck with the pizz. E1/E2 and the timpani E (Adler PDF 116, "accompanied very lightly").** The plucked notes are pp and two or three octaves lower, and Hn 1 is ppp. The harmonics stay the highest new attack. The finale relies on all three harmonics. Kept.
- **Harp melody 73–80 in the light top register (PDF 113–114).** An octave lower would be warmer, but it would lie among Bsn 1, the bass clarinet and Hn 3·4 at pp, and it would leave theme A's register (A5 is theme A's own octave). The melody is p<mp over pp low winds, so it carries. Kept (do-not-touch since cycle 1).
- **Close three-note chords in the trio and coda (PDF 114, "spread wide").** c296's reason still holds: spreading up enters the solo's register, and spreading down enters the cellos' countermelody or the wound strings. I tested on paper the idea of continuing the off-beats under the coda's T at 349–356. The chords would sit on the cellos' countermelody (E3–A3) and the violas' E4–G♯4 and would undo c457's thinning. Rejected.
- **Harmonics also at 237 (end of the trio).** Not built. At the right sounding pitch they would need written G♯2–E3, below Adler's C3 limit. One rhyme (149/151 → 375) is enough.
