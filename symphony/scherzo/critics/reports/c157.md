## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 213–216: the forte return harmonises T's second phrase exactly as the first two statements do (harmonic variety). Applied.

**Problem.** T's second phrase is heard three times in the trio: 157–160 (Cl 1), 173–176 (violins) and 213–216 (violins, Cl 1, horns, bassoons, f). All three use the same chords over the same bass: A (IV) – F♯ (ii, F♯–A–C♯) – F♯ (V7/V) – B. Since c089, statements 2 and 3 share their colour at 172–173/212–213, so the third statement now differs from the second only in its doublings. Bars 209–216 are the return of the trio's tune at its loudest. They should not be a louder copy of 169–176.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19: "I usually try to avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice, whenever possible." Ex. 3/3A give one melody new chords that "still arrive at the same resting place". PDF 20: "be aware of harmonic substitutions and use them whenever you feel the need for a little lift in a musical phrase". c089 kept 153–168 plain on purpose, "as the reference the later statements colour"; this change leaves 157–160 and 173–176 as they are.

**Fix (applied, tC.ily).** In 214–215 the bass rises A–A♯–A♯–B, giving V6/5 of V (F♯7 over A♯) for two bars in place of ii – V7/V. The phrase reaches the same B at 216.
```
tCCb:     ... | a,,2. | ais,,2. | ais,,2. | b,,2 fis,,4 | ...      (was fis,,2. | fis,,2.)
tCCbsn:   ... | a,2.--( | ais,2.-- | ais,2.-- | b,2 fis,4) ...      (was fis,2.-- | fis,2.--)
tCHnTwo:  ... | a'2.-- | e'2.-- | e'2.-- | dis'2.--) \breathe ...   (was a'2.-- | fis'2.-- | fis'2.--)
tCVla:    ... | r4 <cis' fis'>4--( <cis' fis'>4--) | r4 <cis' fis'>4--( <cis' fis'>4--) | ...   (was <a fis'>, <ais e'>)
tCVc, tCBsnTwo: ... | e4( cis2~ | cis2 fis4) | cis2.( | ...       (was cis4 a,2)
tCHarpL:  ... | cis,4 r4 r4 | cis,4 r4 r4 | ...                     (was fis,,4; the harp's A♮ pedal stays)
```
- The A♯ is in the bass only (c093, A&S PDF 214: the raised fourth is not doubled), and it rises to B.
- The seventh E4 is in Bsn 1 (held since 209) and Hn 2. Both resolve to D♯4 at 216.
- Hn 1·2 now lie in sixths: C♯5/E4 at 214–215, then B4/D♯4. They were a fifth at 215 before, so c094's thirds-and-sixths rule is better kept than it was.
- The cellos move to F♯3 on beat 3, after the tune reaches F♯. My first version moved on beat 2 and made octaves with the tune's C♯–F♯; the all-pairs scan caught it.
- No A♮ sounds against the A♯.
- The per-player all-pairs parallel scan is now identical to v157's, and analysis.txt shows the same 7 outer-voice parallels.

### 2. Bars 209–224: the cello countermelody plays at the tune's own level (focus). Applied.

**Problem.** At 209 the tune (Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1) is f. The cellos' countermelody is also f, doubled by Bsn 2 at mf, and it stays f to 223 with no shaping of its own. Everything else in the passage is a degree or more under the tune: violas mf, Hn 1·2 p, bassoons mf. In the second statement (169) the same countermelody is p under the violins' mp. So the countermelody is the only background voice at primary level, in the cello's singing register under the tune's lower octave.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the ear must go to the primary element "and not [be] diverted or confused by an overbusy background or counter-line". PDF 14: the counter-line belongs to the secondary level. The score's own rule is c099's "accompaniment one dynamic level under the tune", and 169 follows it.

**Fix (applied, tC.ily `tCVc`).** `b,4(\mf e2` at 209; `e4(\< gis2~` at 217; `a2(\f\> gis4` at 219; `b,2(\mf cis4` at 221. The cellos now take the bassoons' existing mf < f > mf. That keeps the countermelody one level under the tune (f < ff > f) at every point, and it still swells with the tune's climb to A6. Both hairpins have printed targets, so the MIDI does not jump (c138).
- This answers c087's "both bassoons mf, a degree under the cellos' f". The cello–bassoon unison is one line, and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76 notes that in that pair "the strings predominate" anyway.
- The degree c087 kept between the cellos and their own doubling does more good between the countermelody and the tune.

### Considered and not raised (my decisions)
- **219–220 mixture (ii half-diminished, violas C4).** I first wrote this as the substitution for finding 1. Under the tune's C♯ at 220 the C♮ would have had to rise to C♯. Aldwell & Schachter PDF 388 say ♭6 "tends to move to 5; it will not normally be replaced by ♮6 before 5 is reached" (Ex. 23-2h, "what to avoid"). I withdrew it.
- **121–124 and 277–280: Cl 1, Hn 2 and violas on G4–F♯4–G4** (principle 6). The doubling is real, but each line comes from a different place: the violas from E4 at 121, Cl 1 from B4, Hn 2 sustained. The sub. p entry is horn-coloured by design (c046, c109). The full chord stays complete in other parts, so cutting a line would gain little. Kept.
- **197–200: Hn 1 on T's lower layer with Cl 1, Vln II and violas.** Hn 1 is the harmony note at 199 (A4 of F♯m6) as well as a doubling, and the layering is protected (c117, c139). Kept.

- **98–100: Vln I's head entry merges into Vln II's B7 arpeggio** (an octave apart at 100). This is the written build into the hemiola, and the ritmo di tre battute is protected (c019, c097). Kept.
