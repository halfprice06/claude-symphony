## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Tutti theme at 105–120 and 309–324: the horns are written in chords, play louder than the trumpets, and cover the theme
**Problem.** Each horn part has a two-note chord: Hn 1 and Hn 2 both play `<b' e''>`, and Hn 3 and Hn 4 both play `<e' g'>`. A horn plays one note, so as written the parts cannot be played. The horns are marked ff while the trumpets and trombones are marked f. Rimsky's table (PDF 51) makes a trumpet twice as strong as a horn, so this balance is upside down. The theme sits at E5 in Vln I alone, with Fl 1·2 and Ob 1 in unison. Flutes are weak at E5–G5 (rule 3, PDF 90). Vln I in forte equals two woodwinds (PDF 51). Against this stand four horns at B4–E5, which is the theme's own octave (rule 2, PDF 90), plus trumpets on B4/E4. In Dvořák 9/iii, bars 43–50, both violin sections carry the theme.
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily: one note per horn, and horns f like the trumpets:
`sCHnOne = { e''4->\f r4 e''4-. | ... dis''4-> ... | ... c''4-> ...` (Hn 2 `b'`/`a'`, Hn 3 `g'`/`fis'`/`a'`, Hn 4 `e'`/`dis'`/`e'`). The `<a' c''>` + `<e' a'>` bars stay A minor (no C-major triad).
Vln II takes the theme at the unison with Vln I for 105–120 and 309–324 (copy of `sCVlnOne`'s first 16 bars). The off-beats stay in Ob 2, Cl 2, violas and horns. Vln II gets `\f` at 121.

### 2. Harp off-beats double the bowed off-beats at 169–184 and 209–224
**Problem.** The harp RH plays the same off-beat chords `<gis b e'>` in the same rhythm as the portato off-beats of Vln II (`b'`) and violas (`e'`). Rimsky (PDF 52) says plucked and bowed strings together "do not produce a satisfactory blend, both qualities being heard independently". On PDF 53 he says plucked strings "soon become wearisome" and should be used "at reasonable intervals". With these bars the harp plays the same figure, in the same register, for about 70 bars of the trio and coda.
**Fix (applied).** tA.ily: `tAHarpR` bars 169–184 become `R2.*16`. tC.ily: `tCHarpR` bars 209–224 become `R2.*16`. The harp LH keeps its downbeats with the double-bass pizzicato (plucked with plucked, which blends). The RH off-beats stay where the harp is the only off-beat voice (153–168, 185–192) and in the codetta 225–237, where the strings hold notes. The harp's re-entry at 225 now marks the codetta.

### 3. F-major episode 73–88: the harp melody is covered, and Cl 1's dolce phrase crosses Cl 2 and the harp chords
**Problem.** At 73–80 the harp, which has little sustain, plays a single-line melody at p. Under it six winds sustain a chord: Cl 1·2, Bsn 1, B.cl., Hn 3·4. Cl 1's `a'`/`bes'` sits just below the melody. This is the case of PDF 51: sustained groups overpower plucked sound. Because Cl 1 already plays in 73–80, its dolce phrase at 81 is not a fresh entry (PDF 81: texture changes should come with a new idea). At 85–86 Cl 1's `a'4 f'8 f' a'4 | c''2 b'8 bes'8` lies inside Cl 2's held `f'` and inside the harp's `<c' f' a'>`. That gives unisons on F4 and A4, with held and moving parts in the same timbre (PDF 90 rule 2, PDF 91 b).
**Fix (applied).** sB.ily: `sBClOne` bars 73–80 rest. `sBClTwo` rests from 73 to 86 and enters `dis'2.\pp` at 87. `sBHarpR` 85–86 become `R2. | R2.`, and `sBHarpL` 85–86 become `a,,4 <c f a>2 | a,,4 <c f a>2`. The Neapolitan sixth then sounds below the melody, in the same pattern as 73–80. The harmony at 73–80 stays complete: F and C in Hn 3·4 and Bsn 1, A in the harp LH.

### 4. Second idea at 45–56: two empty octaves between the bass and the harmony, in forte
**Problem.** The bass (Vc, Bsn 1, Cbsn, Cb) is on G2–E3. The next voices up are Hn 2 and the violas/Cl 2 on G4. Nothing sounds in the tenor register. Rimsky says the bass "should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it" (PDF 85) and "nothing is worse" than wide empty intervals in forte (PDF 86; PDF 96, Remark 1). The composer's own restatements at 121–132 and 281–292 fill this gap (Bsn 1 on E3), so only 45–56 is affected.
**Fix (applied).** sA.ily: `sAHnTwo` bars 45–56 an octave lower (`g2.\f | g2. | fis2. | g2. | e2. ... | g4-. r4 r4`). Hn 2 is then a harmony voice in the tenor, an octave above the bass, and does not double the bass. This creates no parallels with the bass.

### 5. Trio return 209–224: Hn 1·2 hold open fourths and fifths under the violins' tune
**Problem.** Hn 1·2 hold `b'/e'`, `a'/dis'`, `a'/e'`, `ais'/e'` and so on. Of 16 bars, ten are fourths, fifths or tritones. Rimsky's rule 4 (PDF 90) gives two instruments of the same kind concords, not discords. Remark 4 (PDF 97) says that when the strings have the top part, the wind's middle parts should be "seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths" and avoid open fourths and fifths. The result is hollow at the trio's forte return. The bassoons also have no dynamic at 209.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily, bars 209–224:
`tCHnOne = { b'2.\p | b'2. | a'2. | b'2. | cis''2. | cis''2. | ais'2.~ | ais'4( b'2) | gis'2. | b'2. | a'2. | cis''2. | b'2. | a'2. | gis'2. | gis'2. | R2.*16 }`
`tCHnTwo = { gis'2.\p | gis'2. | fis'2. | gis'2. | a'2. | a'2. | fis'2. | dis'2. | e'2. | gis'2. | fis'2. | a'2. | gis'2. | fis'2. | e'2. | e'2. | R2.*16 }`
This gives thirds and sixths that follow the harp's harmony. `tCBsnOne` and `tCBsnTwo` begin `e2.\p`.

### 6. Reprise 257–276: Vln II pizzicato and violas arco play the same off-beats
**Problem.** At the reprise Vln II plays its `g'`/`a'` off-beats pizzicato, while the violas play the same rhythm arco staccato on `b`/`c'`/`dis'`. This is the mixture Rimsky says does not blend (PDF 52). Each half of the off-beat chord comes out with a different sound.
**Fix (applied).** dA.ily: violas pizz. with Vln II, `r4 b4-.\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." b4-.`. At 277, `r4 fis'4-.\f^\markup \italic "arco" fis'4-.`, where Vln II also takes arco. I kept the staccato dots because removing them lengthened the MIDI notes and produced a false analyser parallel.

### 7. 87–88: the bass of the diminished seventh is tripled
**Problem.** The chord over A at 87–88 is D♯°7 (D♯–F♯–A–C) with the seventh, A, in the bass. A is in the bass clarinet (A2) and also in Bsn 1 and Hn 4 (A3). Rimsky (PDF 83–84, with the "Bad/Good" examples on PDF 84) says the bass of an inversion of a seventh or diminished seventh chord is never doubled in the upper parts.
**Fix (applied).** sB.ily: `sBHnFour` 87–88 `fis2. | fis2.` and `sBBsnOne` 87–88 `c'2. | c'2.`. A stays in the bass only. F♯ and C fill the chord, and D♯ is in Cl 2 and Cl 1.

### 8. Coda 341–343: tuba in unison with the bass trombone on E2
**Problem.** `codaTuba` plays `e,2.` at the same pitch as `codaBassTbn`. Rimsky (PDF 82) doubles the bass in the octave below only. A unison of tuba and bass trombone also moves toward the low-brass unison bass that is kept for the finale. At 105 the composer already puts the tuba on E1.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily: `codaTuba` bars 341–343 `e,,2.\ff | e,,2. | e,,2.`, then B1 as before.

Not changed: Fl 2 alone on C♯5 at 197–200 (PDF 90 rules 1 and 3) and the bassoons' unison E3 at 209–224 (PDF 82 note). Both are minor. The Hn 3·4 open-fifth drone at 153–168 is a deliberate musette and is exempt from Remark 4.

None of these fixes undoes an earlier accepted fix. Protected passages are not touched: 1–7, 365–368, 377–384, the B6 at 344, Hn 3·4 "senza sord." at 73, the timpani label.
