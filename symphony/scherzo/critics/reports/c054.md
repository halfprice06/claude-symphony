## Findings

### 1. Bars 29-30 and 33-34: the wind call puts Fl 1 in its top register at p and spends A6 in bar 34 (applied)

Problem. In the counterstatement the winds answer the strings with theme A. Fl 1 plays it an octave above Ob 1 and an octave above the violins' statement at 13-28: E6-B5-E6 | G6-F♯6-F6 | E6 at 29-30 and E6-B5-E6 | A6-G♯6-G6 | F♯6 at 33-34, tongued staccato at p. A6 in bar 34 is the highest note of the movement until the tutti at 110 and the trio climax at 219, and only a semitone below the coda's peak B6 at 336.

Evidence. Adler PDF 238 rejects Schubert re-scored in octaves (Ex. VIII-4, 5) because they "anticipate the expansion of the registers to which Schubert is heading"; the flute enters undetected and "is not really heard until it plays the highest notes of the cadential chords". PDF 274: each gesture in the best register of its instrument; the top fourth of the flute at p is its hardest place for soft tonguing and intonation (the reason c036 marked the trio's high line p). PDF 236 and Ex. VIII-2 show the alternative this passage needs: flute and oboe in unison, where "the oboe ... will certainly stand out, but the flute will somewhat neutralize the nasal quality of the oboe and give the passage a rounder, richer sound". PDF 233, function 3: the winds as a contrasting colour echoing what the strings played. An echo is heard as an echo when it is at the strings' pitch. With the change the flute's line continues in register into 37-40 (G5 with Vln I), the movement's first G6 and A6 arrive in the ff tutti at 109-110, and A6 stays special for the trio climax (219).

Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily, sAFluteOne bars 29-35):
```
sAFluteOne = { R2.*16 | e''4-.\p b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | g''2->( fis''8 f''8 | e''4-.) r4 r4 | R2. | e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | a''2->( gis''8 g''8 | fis''4-.) r4 r4 | ...
```
Ob 1, the clarinets and bassoons are unchanged. The flute now doubles Ob 1 at E5-A5; the wind chord below (E3-B3-E4-G4 at 29, A2-C4-E4-A4 at 33) stays under it.

### 2. Bar 212: Hn 2 holds G♯4 under the tune's appoggiatura A4 in the same octave (applied)

Problem. At T's forte return (209-224) Hn 1·2 sustain the harmony p at B4 and G♯4, which is the register of the tune's lower octave (Cl 1 and Vln II, G♯4-C♯5). In bar 212 the tune has the appoggiatura A4 on the downbeat resolving to G♯4 on beat 2, while Hn 2 holds G♯4 from the downbeat: the resolution sounds against the dissonance in the same octave, a semitone apart, in the bar where the phrase cadences. In the other bars the horns' held notes are either chord tones the tune also takes (210, 213, 216, 221) or meet only a passing note (209 beat 3, 220), which is normal.

Evidence. Adler PDF 255: "The most important thing to remember is that the melody must come through ... and not be obscured by the harmony." PDF 243 (Figaro, Ex. VIII-10): the sustained winds are placed at a "wide distance" from the theme, which "adds great prominence to the sustained counterpoint without detracting from the major thematic idea". The same fault was removed at 197-200 by c039 (no A4 against a held G♯4); 212 was left.

Fix (applied, sections/tC.ily, tCHnTwo bar 4 of the section): Hn 2 enters on beat 2 with the tune's G♯4 instead of holding it under the A4:
```
tCHnTwo = { gis'2.\p | gis'2. | fis'2. | r4 gis'2 | a'2. | ...
```
Hn 1 keeps B4 for the bar; Bsn 1 (E4), Bsn 2 (E3) and the tune's G♯ in both octaves complete the E-major chord on beat 1. The horn re-enters on the note of resolution, so the A4 is heard against nothing in its own octave. The c047 four-part horn-bassoon voicing is otherwise unchanged.
