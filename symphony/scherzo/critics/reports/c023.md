## Findings

### 1. Trio to retransition: 20 bars on the same E-major chord before the inflection (225–244). Applied.

**Problem.**
- The trio's codetta (225–240) is 16 bars over the E pedal. It is pp, it states T's head three times at the same pitch, and a poco rit. holds the chord at 237–240.
- The retransition then opens with four more bars of that chord: 241–244 have Hn 1 G♯ over E, pp, with timpani x on B.
- Only at 245 does G♯ become G♮. The movement stands still for about 18 seconds at its slowest tempo.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 584–585: a modulation by chromatic inflection "attracts the listener's attention to the key change". Rhythm, texture and dynamics shape its effect, so the inflection itself is the event and needs no extra bars of the old chord.
- A&S PDF 586–587: in Widmung the common tone G♯ is held across a single bar of ritard. into the new key.
- Dvořák 9/iii takes about 9 bars from the end of the trio to the scherzo (MCTL 1080–1108).
- The analyser's density profile shows the plateau: 20, 13 and 13 attacks per four bars at 237–248.

**Fix (applied).**
- Cut retr 241–244. sections.txt: `retr 12 3`.
- Hn 1 takes the G♯ at the end of the trio. tC.ily, last four bars of `tCHnOne`: `R2.*12 | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4 |`.
- The G♮ now falls on 241, the retransition's first bar. The E-minor key signature moves there too. global.ily: `\bar "||" \key e \minor \mark \default % N 241`, then `\mt 232 s2.*4 \mt 244 s2.*4 \mt 256 s2.*4`.
- retr.ily keeps old 245–256. Timpani x on B moves onto the new 241 (`b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4`), with "hard sticks" at 242; the first B-pedal entry is at 245.
- "muta G♯ in G" moves to tC 225, where the timpani rest for 12 bars.
- The theme record's repeated-B form of x stays at 241.
- Bars from 241 on are 4 lower.

### 2. Reprise close to coda: the close dies to an empty bar, then the coda restarts the same cello figure (325–333). Applied.

**Problem.**
- dC 325–332 copies the scherzo's close 133–140: motive x in the cellos f, mf, p, a falling V, then E pp at 331 and an empty bar at 332.
- At 333 the coda's ostinato begins with the same motive x, in the same cellos, at the same pitch (E–B–B–E), pp.
- The movement deflates to silence and restarts with identical material. Before the trio the full stop works, because the link and a new tempo follow. Before the coda it is a false ending.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 585: rhythm, texture and dynamics decide whether a join is abrupt or smooth.
- A&S PDF 592: chromatic and structural events must keep "a sense of directed, goal-oriented progression".
- Dvořák 9/iii's coda follows the return's last phrase directly (MCTL 1527/1530).
- Profile at 329–336: 12 attacks, then 40.

### 3. The trio's retransition does not prepare the motto tail that closes the movement (205–209). Applied.

**Problem.**
- Over the B pedal, Fl 1 and Cl 1 play T's head and then hold B for two and a half bars. They fall to A only on the last beat of 208, and to G♯ at 209.
- Hn 1's close at 365–368 (now 357–360) quotes the motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ at exactly these pitches.
- The movement never sounds that line before the close. The close is therefore a local quotation, not the outcome of the movement's design.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 601–602: Mozart's K. 542 (also in E) states B–A♯–A♮–G♯ at the opening and quotes it in the second section. A large-scale B–B♭–A–G♯ then makes the G-minor passage meaningful.
- A&S PDF 603, Points 5: relate the chromaticism to the motivic design.
- A&S PDF 597–598: Haydn prepares the later E♭ by the repeated use of D♯ earlier.

**Fix (applied).**
- tB.ily, Fl 1: `gis''2(\pp a''4 | b''2.~ | b''2 ais''4 | a''2.) |`. Cl 1: `gis'2(\p a'4 | b'2.~ | b'2 ais'4 | a'2.) |`.
- A♯ is a passing tone over B–D♯–F♯ (Hn 3, Vla, Bsn 1). A is the V7's seventh for the full bar 208 and resolves to G♯ when T returns at 209.
- This is a quotation at pitch, not a development, so the cyclic rule is kept.

### 4. Timpani E sounds on the dominant bar of the close (138; v23 330). Applied.

**Problem.** In both closes the cellos play the V (F♯–D♯–B, basses B) at 138. The timpani's E pp lands on that bar, a bar before the basses' E pp at 139. A pp E against B–D♯–F♯ is an unprepared fourth, and it pre-empts the tonic arrival.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 593: the cadential V should arrive intensified, not blurred.
- The basses' pp E at 139 shows the intended placement.

**Fix (applied).**
- sC.ily, `sCTimp` ending: `R2. | R2. | R2. | e,4\pp r4 r4 | R2. |`, so the E is at 139.
- In dC this bar is removed by Finding 2.

### Checked and left alone

The trio and the coda supply the major. Putting the second idea in E major would anticipate the coda's E major. No change.
- **The F-major episode and its approach** (A&S PDF 586–587, 592, 596, 600). The episode follows the reading: a common-tone A in the top voice, ♭II as the minor mode's normal chromatic area, and a large-scale ♭II–V–i that enlarges the tail's F–E. Keep it.
- **The link 141–152.** It is a chromatic inflection on an exposed horn tone (A&S PDF 584–587), after the scherzo's full stop. Keep it.
- **Length.** 274 s is right for this place in the symphony.
