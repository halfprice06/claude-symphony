## Findings

### 1. Bars 205–209: two flutes in unison on the motto-tail preview (applied)

**Problem.** At 205–208 Hn 1 plays the anticipation of the motto's chromatic tail, G♯4–A4–B4–A♯4–A4, p espress. Since c387, Fl 1 and Fl 2 play the line an octave higher in unison and resolve together to G♯5 at 209. This is the only soft passage in the movement where two like winds share a line in unison. The scan of score.midi finds flute a2 only at 37–44, 109–120, 273–276, 305–320 and 339, all builds or ff tuttis, plus 205–209 at MIDI velocity 37–44 (p). The oboes, clarinets and horns have no soft unison lines at all. Here the movement's quietest quotation of its motto is the one place where it breaks the scoring rule it keeps everywhere else.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 249–250: two like instruments "can never be absolutely in tune with one another, the overtone balance is upset, the sound thickened, and in certain registers, muddied. The essential timbre of the solo instrument is altered. Further, a unison solo of two like winds limits and impedes the expressive quality and prohibits any slight rubato which a single player would add". He limits this to "ordinary solo passages, not tutti sections where simple volume is required". This passage is a p espress. solo line with a swell to the A♯ (c158, c324). The remedy on the same page: "Doubling wind passages in octaves has already been established as an accepted norm … the fundamental (eight-foot stop) with an octave (four-foot) … gives a transparent but forceful sound, while simply adding more fundamentals … would take away a great deal of the clarity" (PDF 250; Rossini's own version, Ex. VIII-17, PDF 251). Here the octave is already there: Hn 1 is the 8-foot and the flute is the 4-foot. A second flute adds a second 4-foot rank at the same pitch, which is the thickening Adler describes. PDF 261 (one colour for a recurring gesture) makes Hn 1 the motto's instrument (c324). The flute is the horn's octave colour, not an equal voice, so it does not need to match the horn's weight.

**Answer to c387's reason.** c387 cited Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 75: "If a single horn is used, the upper part is allotted to 2 clarinets, 2 oboes, or 2 flutes." That rule balances a melody in two equal octaves. At 205–208 the horn carries the line and the flute only colours it; the organ coupler Adler describes is meant to be lighter than the fundamental. The dynamics are low (p, Hn 1 alone, Hn 3·4 resting since c390, held Cl 2/Bsn/strings pp), so one flute at G♯5–B5 is heard. c387's second reason, Fl 1's rest, still holds: Fl 1 now plays 197–200 and 205–209 and rests 201–204 and 210–216.

- tC.ily, `tCFluteTwo`: `gis''2\mf r4 | R2.*31` becomes `R2.*32`. Fl 1 alone resolves A5 to G♯5 at 209 (`gis''2\mf r4`, unchanged). Without the change Fl 2 would enter for one minim in unison with Fl 1.
- Comments added in both files. On page 7 the shared staff prints "1." with espress. at 205 and a single voice at 209.

No note of any line changes pitch: the motto preview, its resolution and every other part are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 341–356: the coda's T statement ends with the strings alone (checked, kept)

Schumann (PDF 253) and Copland (PDF 270) bring the choirs together after hearing them apart. At 341 the horn has T, at 345 the winds, and at 349–356 the strings alone (c044 and c379 took Fl 1 and Cl 1 out). The point where the choirs come together is 357–364: Hn 1's motto tail with the bassoons over the strings, then the clarinets and flute colour the E-major chord at 361 (c377), as in Brahms 2/iii (PDF 252). The summation is placed on the close instead of on T, and adding winds at 349 would take the freshness from their 357/361 entry (PDF 260). Kept.

### 3. Bars 106–120 and 306–320: Cl 1 and Ob 2 rest through the ff tutti theme (checked, kept)

Dvořák's *Carnival* (Ex. VIII-39, PDF 267–268) fills the upper-octave harmony with oboes and clarinets, and Mozart 29 (PDF 240) holds wind pedals through the tutti. I tested where the two free players could go. The theme sits in three octaves: E4 (C.a., Cl 2), E5 (Vln I/II, Ob 1) and E6 (flutes from 109). Doubling the violas' off-beat dyads an octave up (B4–A5) puts staccato chords inside the theme's main octave, on its own beats 2–3. Holding a tonic or dominant pedal clashes with iv, V and the Neapolitan F under the tail. The horns already supply the Classical pedal in crotchets (B4/E4 on beats 1 and 3). The layout is protected (c214, c224, c304), and the reading does not give a stronger reason to change it. Kept.

### 4. Bars 13–28: no held wind pedal under the first statement of theme A (checked, kept)

The Fledermaus page (PDF 245–247) supports a soft wind pedal under a spiccato tune. The score uses exactly that from 29 (Cl 1·2 and Bsn 1·2 pp under the wind call, lifted at 31–32 and 35–36 as Strauss lifts his). Keeping 13–28 for strings alone gives the winds' first entry at 29 its novelty (PDF 260, W. Schuman). c324's reason holds. Kept.

### 5. Bars 41–43 and 273–275: six parts on one unison line (checked, kept)

PDF 250 prefers octaves to more 8-foot stops. The flutes cannot go up an octave (C7 at 42, above the B6 ceiling), and the oboes cannot go down one (D4 is the oboe's coarse bottom, removed by c086). This is a crescendo into a forte cadence, where Adler allows a2 (PDF 250, "tutti sections where simple volume is required"). Kept.
