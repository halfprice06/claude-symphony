## Findings

### 1. 197–200: the call and the trio tune merge instead of combining. Applied.
**Bars.** 197–200 (tB.ily bars 13–16).
**Problem.** At the trio's forte the oboe's call (Fl 1·2 a2 and Vln I at E6, Ob 1 an octave lower) sounds against T's first phrase (Cl 1, Vln II, violas, Hn 1 at G♯4–E5). The call's bars 2–3 come from T, so in 198–199 the two elements have the same rhythm: call `cis'''2 gis''4 | a''4. b''8 cis'''4` against T `b'2 e''4 | dis''4. cis''8 b'4`, a mirror in contrary motion. The ear hears one harmonised line, not two elements. Ob 1 makes it worse: its lower octave (`cis''2 gis'4 | a'4. b'8 cis''4 | a'4 gis'2`) lies inside T's register, crosses it at 198 beat 3 (G♯4 under T's E5) and 199 beats 2–3 (B4/C♯5 exchanged), and at 200 plays `a'4 gis'2` in unison with Cl 1, Vln II and the violas. c107 moved Vln II off this lower octave for the same reason but left Ob 1 there.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 177 (Meistersinger): the three elements are clear because they are "rhythmically very independent" and "assigned to very distinctive color combinations". PDF 179: "in order to insure clarity, the rhythms of the elements must be distinct". PDF 175: each element needs its own rhythmic make-up to be recognised. PDF 172: an oboe at pitch gives the violins "a sharper edge". adler1 PDF 198: the oboe is effective to C6, so it can join the top line from 198 (C♯6 at most) but not in 197 (F♯6).
**Fix (applied).** The call moves where T holds: 198 crotchet C♯, minim G♯ (T holds B4 and moves on beat 3); 199 minim A, then B–C♯ quavers (T moves on beats 2½ and 3). The turn at 197 is unchanged. Ob 1 keeps the turn at E5 in 197, then joins the top line at pitch from 198, so the oboe colour stays on the call and leaves T's register.

No new parallels between the call, T and the bass; the analyser's outer-voice list is unchanged. T's recorded statement (trioT ClOne/Vla 197) is untouched; the call is not a recorded theme.

### 2. 65–68: the basses' pedal is louder than the countersubject. Applied.
**Bars.** 65 (sB.ily, sBCb).
**Problem.** In the fugato's third entry the subject (Vln II) is p and the countersubject (violas) and the cellos' third line (c017) are pp, as c017 set them. The basses enter at 65 on the A–A–D–E pedal marked p, so the background bass is at the subject's level and above the two subsidiary lines.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184: Schoenberg marks the principal voice p and the subsidiary ideas pp; "principal gestures were to be played louder than the subordinate ones". PDF 179: the sustained background is the softest layer.
**Fix (applied).** `sBCb = { R2.*8 | a,,2.\pp | ...` The crescendo from 67 into the f stretto at 69 is unchanged. (The reprise fugato 289–296 has no bass, so nothing changes there.)

### Checked, no change
- Stretto 69–72/297–300: three entries a bar apart, each a string section with a wind (and Hn 1·2 on the last), over a non-thematic pedal: Jupiter's layout (PDF 176). Protected by c097, and correct.
- Coda ostinato 325–332: the entries share one rhythm, so this is one thickening element, not polyphony. Adler's Holst example (PDF 181) treats figuration of this kind as background that builds a climax, which is its role here; it is protected (c007, c017, c108).
- 89–100: Vln I's continuation at 100 doubles Vln II's arpeggio an octave higher. This is one bar inside a crescendo into the hemiola, where the layers are meant to converge (PDF 184, Bloch); left.
- 225–240, 341–348, 349–356: the head or tune, middleground off-beats and sustained background are each in their own register and rhythm (PDF 179).
