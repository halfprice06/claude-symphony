## Findings

### 1. Retransition 99-100: Vln II's accompaniment merges into the melody (applied)

**Bars:** 99-100, Vln II (sB.ily, `sBVlnTwo`).

**Problem:** From 95, Vln II plays B7 crotchet arpeggios (b'–f♯'–d♯' | a'–f♯'–d♯') under the theme head in Vln I. Both lines are staccato crotchets in the same colour. In the three-bar group 98-100 Vln I continues the head with falling arpeggios: f♯''–d♯''–b' at 99, a''–f♯''–d♯'' at 100. At 99 Vln II falls with it in the same rhythm. At 100 it plays Vln I's own notes an octave lower. The accompaniment becomes an octave doubling of the line in its last bar, one bar before the octave doubling the hemiola sets up on purpose at 101 (Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1, C.a., Cl 1). So the doubling changes in the middle of the *ritmo di tre battute* phrase, and the head's last bar is heard as part of the accompaniment.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 217 (Ravel, *Petit Poucet*): the accompaniment and the melody are "essentially an eighth-note movement; therefore, the highly contrasting colors throughout the piece are very necessary to separate the two elements for the listener." Here they share both colour and note values. The only thing that kept them apart was pitch and direction, and that is lost at 99-100.
- Adler PDF 228 (Stravinsky, *Pulcinella*): colour changes are made "never breaking up a phrase needlessly". The planned doubling at 101 starts at a group boundary; the accidental one at 100 does not.
- Adler PDF 229 (Schoenberg, Brahms op. 25): two similar colours on one line "would swallow each other up".
- The score's own precedent: c227 took Vln II's off-beats off theme A's half-cadence notes at 15-16/31-32 for the same reason. The second retransition (249-252) keeps Vln I's arpeggio free of string accompaniment.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-237/work/sections/sB.ily):**

```
% old 98-101
a'4-.\< fis'-. dis'-. | b'4-. fis'-. dis'-. | a'4-. fis'-. dis'-. | b'2->
% new 98-101
a'4-.\< fis'-. dis'-. | dis'4-. fis'-. a'-. | dis'4-. fis'-. a'-. | b'2->
```

Vln II now rises through the same B7 (D♯4–F♯4–A4) against Vln I's falling arpeggios, so the two move in contrary motion. Its A4 then steps up to the B4 of the hemiola at 101, where the octave doubling begins as planned. The harmony is unchanged: V7 over the B pedal, with Vla on B3, the timpani's motive x and the pizz. B. The crescendo from 98 is unchanged. Beat 3 of 99 is A4 under Vln I's B4, a second inside the V7; it is not a unison. Bar 100 passes through one octave F♯ on beat 2 in contrary motion. The old 100→101 parallel octaves between the violins (D♯→B in both) are gone. The protected material at 89-100 is untouched: the timpani's motive x with the pizz. punctuation (c097), the three-bar groups (c019), and the head entries Vla → Ob → Vln I.
