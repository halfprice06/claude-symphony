## What else I studied

**Beethoven 4/iii.** I compared the movement with what I know of the score, checked against the [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Beethoven)) and the program notes at [Aspen](https://www.aspenmusicfestival.com/program_notes/view/symphony-no.-4-in-b-flat-major-op.-60), [Codaweb](https://codaweb.org/?p=3298) and the [Hungarian Philharmonic](https://filharmonikusok.hu/en/?p=91353). Beethoven's movement has five parts (scherzo, trio, scherzo, trio, shortened scherzo). The cross-rhythm is in the theme's first bars. In the trio ("un poco meno allegro") the winds sing long notes and the violins answer in the gaps. In the coda the trio comes back for a few bars before the horns "blow the whole movement away" (Tovey, quoted by Wikipedia).

This movement matches Beethoven where it matters:
- a slower trio held back for wind and soft colour;
- a scherzo that develops its head motive (fugato 57–72, retransition 89–104);
- a coda that brings the trio tune back (341–356) after the scherzo's peak.

It departs from Beethoven on purpose, and I keep those choices:
- ABA instead of ABABA. A second trio would add about a minute between the Adagio and the finale (c003/c023 proportions).
- The quiet G♯ ending instead of Beethoven's horn cut-off. This is the cyclic pivot, protected.
- Hemiola only at the cadences (53–55, 101–104, 129–131), not inside theme A (c002/c085).

One gap remains against Beethoven's trio. His soft wind melody is never left alone: the strings answer each phrase end. In v349 the trio's second statement has nothing that answers the tune. That is Finding 1.

Four earlier critics protected "the coloured second statement" and "trio layering 169–184" (c017, c107, c254, c257). c344 gave no reason for dropping that layer. Only this point got worse.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 169–184: the cellos' T has no answer in its held bars. Applied.

**Problem.** Since c344 the second statement of T is the cellos (G♯3–A4, mp espress.) over Vln II div. off-beat dyads (pp) and pizz. basses. Nothing else plays for 16 bars. Three times the tune holds:
- 172: A3 tied to G♯3 for two beats;
- 176: B3 for two beats at the half cadence;
- 183–184: B3 for five beats at the cadence in B.

In those bars the off-beat dyads repeat and nothing moves. In v339 the cello countermelody moved exactly there (c051: "a counter-melody supplies the motion"). c344 moved that line to 209 and put nothing in its place. The statement that should be the more coloured of the two quiet ones (c257) is now the barer.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov, ch. V, PDF 138: the composer "should reserve his orchestral outbursts for the intervals during which the voice is silent, distributing the singer's phrases and pauses in a free and natural manner."
- The examples show this at small scale. In Ex. 277 (PDF 444) Fl 1 "dolce" plays a four-bar phrase in the voice's pause, over pp off-beat strings and pizz. bass, which is the texture of 169–184. In Ex. 280 (PDF 447–448) Fl and Cl answer each vocal phrase end with a short espressivo turn. In Ex. 303 (PDF 491–492) a flute figure bridges the tenor's phrase ends.
- PDF 142 and Ex. 284 (PDF 457): a solo wind obbligato with a cantabile line "either plays alone or as the leading melodic voice" and is "often contrapuntal".
- PDF 137: the accompanying instrument must not copy the solo's rhythmic figure. PDF 139: harmony in the solo's own register covers it. So the answer belongs in the gaps, in a rhythm T does not use, and well away from T's octave.
- Beethoven 4/iii's trio works the same way: soft wind melody, answers in the gaps.

**Earlier reasons answered.**
- c051 took Fl 1 off 169–176 because it shadowed the tune at its pitch. These answers sound only where T holds, two octaves above it, and never double a note of the tune.
- c074 and c344 kept the high colour for the forte return (Adler PDF 129, 135). The answers stay in E5–B5 and Fl 1 still first reaches C♯6–A6 at 217.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-349/work/sections/tA.ily).** Fl 1, "1." dolce p, plays T's head (a rising third and step) in quavers in each held bar:
- 172, over I/I7: G♯–A–B;
- 176, over V: D♯–E–F♯, a fifth lower;
- 183–184, into the B cadence: F♯–G♯–A♯–B, the head completed on B.

The cellos, Vln II and basses are unchanged.

## Also examined and kept (no finding)

- **Soft climax at 349–351** (row 9). Three parts reach A6/A5 at p. Ex. 282 would leave it lighter, but c251 and c314 protect this scoring and the violas and cellos are already ppp/pp. Kept.
- **Five-part form and a horn cut-off ending** (Beethoven). Rejected for the reasons above: proportions, and the protected G♯ pivot.
- **T's memorability** (open since c289). Beethoven's trio tune is no more memorable than T. What makes his trio stick is the dialogue round it. Finding 1 adds that dialogue without touching T's notes.
