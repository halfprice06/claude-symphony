## Findings

### 1. Coda, 332-333: the trumpets come back f on the arrival after 212 bars' rest (applied)

Problem: the trumpets play only 101-120 and then rest until the downbeat of 333. There they enter f with motive x, with no preparation. Every other brass group joins the coda's build before the arrival. Hn 3·4 enter pp< at 331, Hn 1·2 p< at 332, and the trombones, bass trombone and tuba pp< at 332 (c393). The trumpets are the only group that still enters f on the arrival.

Evidence: PDF 136 says that after a long rest the brass should re-enter "either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful", and that the remark "is capable of wider application". The trumpets are named in the same paragraph as the group that may be "tacet for long periods". Dan's rule rules out ff on brass entries, so the only form the book allows is pp. PDF 127 says how a pp entry serves a crescendo: "the second group enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense." The score already does this with the same instruments in the exposition. At 101-103 the trumpets play motive x pp< and reach f at 104. The coda's arrival is the movement's main climax (the single fff at 336), so it should have at least the same preparation as the exposition's. The order of the build stays as PDF 130 asks: strings 325-328, woodwind 329, horns 331, all brass 332, arrival 333.

Recorded reason answered: c428 kept the trumpets out of 289-324 so that they and the low brass "return together at the coda's 333 after 212 bars". This change keeps that. The trumpets now return in the same bar as the low brass, which c393 moved to 332, and the reprise tutti stays without trumpets. The trumpets still play only motive x in the tutti rhythm. Vln II, the violas and the cellos play the same figure on B in 332.

Fix (coda.ily):
```
codaTptOne = { R2.*7 | b'4-.\pp\< b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'4-.\f b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | ...
codaTptTwo = { R2.*7 | fis'4-.\pp\< fis'8-. fis'-. fis'4-. | e'4-.\f e'8-. e'-. e'4-. | ...
```
Both trumpets have the same dynamics on their shared staff. B4 and F♯4 are already in Hn 1, Hn 2 and Tbn 1. Tpt 2 moves F♯4 to E4 against the bass B to E, in contrary motion from a fifth to an octave. analysis.txt is unchanged. parts-cues.json: the Vln I cue in the Tpt 1 and Tpt 2 parts is shortened from 329 for 4 bars to 329 for 3 bars (329-331), so it ends before the new entry. Vln I plays motive x mf< in 329-331.

### 2. Horn entries p< at 101 and 332 and p at 357: keep

Hn 1·2 enter p< at 101 after 26 bars' rest, while the trumpets and trombones enter pp<. PDF 136 would prefer pp. But PDF 127 explicitly allows "piano or pianissimo" for a group that enters a crescendo. Since c194 the score marks the horns a degree louder than the two trumpets so that the horn pairs balance them (PDF 103). Making the horns pp< would undo that offset. At 357 the p entry is one level above a pp basis, as PDF 133 asks for wind melody against a softer basis (c303). Kept.

### 3. Length and proportions: keep

The movement is 4:38, the shortest of the four (7:11, 5:45, 4:38, 7:02). PDF 116 asks whether each passage is a climax or a step. The scherzo has three graded peaks (105 full tutti, 305 partial tutti, 333-336 full tutti with the single fff), and the trio has one forte peak at 197-200 and its return at 209. The trio-tempo share (trio 25 per cent, plus 34 s of coda) is high, but the second coda stage is the cyclic close, not another trio. Cutting it would renumber the bars the finale and movement I quote (357-360, 363-371, 375-376), which this reading gives no reason to do. Kept.

### 4. Varied returns and the partial tutti at 305: keep

The reprise tutti uses methods (d) and (e) of PDF 118 and is a partial tutti in the sense of PDF 119: horns only, with the flutes' florid line an octave above the violins, which is PDF 132's case. This keeps the full brass colour fresh for 332-339 (PDF 136). Kept.

### 5. Transfers in the coda's T, 344/345 and 348/349: keep

PDF 125-126 joins the parts of one passage on a common note. At 341-356 the relay Hn 3 → Ob 1/Cl 1 → solo violin and Fl 1 falls on T's four-bar phrase ends, each with its own cadence, and it rises in register (PDF 128). The common-note rule is for a passage too wide for one instrument, which this is not. Overlapping notes would blur the phrase ends. Kept.

### 6. Woodwind entering f at 69 and 297: keep

The winds join the stretto f with no crescendo of their own. PDF 127 says the change from simple to complex timbre can be made "suddenly, or by degrees". Here the strings have already built up through 65-68 and 293-296, and the sudden added colour marks the stretto. Kept.
