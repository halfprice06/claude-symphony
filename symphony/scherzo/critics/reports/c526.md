## Findings

### 1. Bars 1–7 and 364–376: Vln I's held G♯5 frame, mark "sul A" (applied)

- **Problem.** The movement opens and closes on a held G♯5 in the whole first-violin section at ppp, paired with Hn 1's pp G♯4 (movement II's A♭, and the pitch the finale picks up). No string is named. A section reads G♯5 as first position on the E string, Rimsky-Korsakov's brilliant string. These are the two most exposed sustained string sounds in the movement. At 1–2 and 368–376 nothing above the horn and the timpani sounds with them, and at 364–376 the violins enter from nothing at ppp under the horn.
- **Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27: "The top string on the violin (E) is brilliant in character … The A and D strings on the violin … are somewhat sweeter and weaker in tone than the others." A ppp note that is meant to merge into the horn, which Rimsky calls "soft, poetical" (PDF 42), wants the sweeter and weaker string. PDF 26 sets the limit for a high note on a lower string at "the octave note or the ninth of the open string". G♯5 is a seventh above the open A (fourth position), and the slur to G5 at 7 stays on the same string, so the frame is inside the limit. The same reasoning put sul A on T at 177–180 and 349–352 (c466). **Answer to c376**, which kept the E string because Adler calls it "luminous" when soft: luminous is exactly the quality that makes a held ppp G♯5 stand apart from the horn an octave below. Here the violins are colouring the horn's note, not carrying a line of their own, so Rimsky's sweeter, weaker A string is the better match. On the A string the bow can also stay at ppp longer without the E string's whistle, which helps "change bow freely" over 13 bars.
- **Fix (applied).** Playing words only. Pitch, player, dynamics, slurs and the last bar are unchanged.
  - intro.ily: `gis''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "senza sord." -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan | … | g''2\> r4\!\stopTextSpan |`
  - coda.ily: `r2 gis''4~\ppp^\markup \italic "tutti, change bow freely" -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan | … | gis''2.\stopTextSpan | }`
- **Why this is the change a listener would hear most.** The rest of the chapter finds nothing wrong (rows 1–5 and 7–36). The first and last sustained sounds of the movement are where the colour of one string section is heard almost alone, so this is where a timbre choice from this chapter is most audible.
- **Protections.** The frame is protected for its pitch, player and ppp (c430, c499, c500, c510): all three are unchanged. themecheck: 52/52 unchanged. The finale and movement I quote the pitch, so they need only an information note.
