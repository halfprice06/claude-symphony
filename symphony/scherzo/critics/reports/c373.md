## Findings

Every principle in the table is followed or does not apply, so no finding calls for a change of notes. None of them beats those reasons, so the findings below are decisions and record corrections, ranked by how much each matters to the form. ### 1. Coda 341–356: the return from A to E is confirmed by the cadence, as Point 2 asks. Keep

**Question.** §11 (PDF 451) warns that too much IV can make us "hear IV as I and I as V". The coda spends 341–348 in A major and ends on an E chord (348) that is V of A. T's second half then starts on C♯ minor (349), not on E. So E is never stated as a tonic on a downbeat until 356.

**Answer.** The danger in §11 is an early IV. This IV is the last area of the movement, and §16 (PDF 456) names I–IV–I as the main expansion in sectional pieces. The return is confirmed by vi–iii–ii (351)–IV (352)–6/4 (353)–V (354–355)–I (356), a cadence with both II and IV (Points 2). The motto tail repeats the plagal I–iv–I in small. Adding a B7 at 348 would turn the phrase modulation into a transition, which c283 rejected for the same reason.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. T's second phrase 157–160 follows §13. Record correction to c283. Keep

c283 marked Ex. 26-15 as not applicable ("T's second phrase is on IV, not II"). The image of Ex. 26-15 shows Bellini's opening four bars stated again one step higher, with II moving to V.

T's second phrase does the same thing melodically. The head G♯4–A4–B4–E5 (153) returns as A4–B4–C♯5–F♯5 (157). The harmony is IV at 157 (harp A–C♯–E over A1), ii at 158 (A–C♯–F♯ over F♯1), ii7 at 159 and V at 160, where A♯4 leans into B4.

Record it as "follows". The repetition a step higher is what keeps the phrase from being an exact repeat, and it is the reason T's first eight bars are not the static spot the melody critics (c289–c319) named. Do not reharmonise 157 as I, and do not make 158 a IV: either one would lose the II that leads to V.

### 3. Fugato 57–68: the answer keys come by reinterpretation, not by a prepared leading tone. Record correction to c283, no change

c283 (row 13) wrote that "the next key's leading tone comes before the next entry". That is true only in the reprise, where 296 is V of A before the A entry (c073).

In the exposition no leading tone comes before the entry:
- At 60 the cellos close on D–F♯–A (a, fis, d,), V of G minor. The violas enter on D minor at 61, so C♯ first sounds at 64, inside their entry.
- At 64 the violas close on A–C♯–E (V of D). Vln II enters on A minor at 65, and G♯ first sounds at 68 (violas).

This is Ex. 26-7's procedure, not Ex. 26-10's. The closing major triad of one entry becomes the tonic of the next, by mixture, and §2 gives the minor v (61) as the normal key area in minor. A C♯ under 60 or a G♯ at 64 would make the subject's last bar a dominant of the new key and blur the G-minor close. The entries rise G–D–A as a sequence (row 5), which already orients the ear.

**Fix.** None. Correct the row in the record.

### 4. VII as connecting link: 29–44 and 273–277. Keep

In Ex. 26-2c, G: V is e: ♮VII, the same two keys as this movement. Ex. 26-16 shows VII leading to V. The score uses both directions:
- **Out to III.** E (29–32), A minor = iv of E and ii of G (33–34, the pivot), D (35–36), G (37), D7 (41–44), G (45).
- **Back from III.** D7 (273–275) → V6/5 (276, D→D♯) → i (277).

These two places are the only ones where E minor and G meet without a cadence between them, so they carry the whole I–III relation of the reprise. Keep the winds' A–C–E at 33–34 (c283) and the D→D♯ in one voice at 275–276 (c083).

### 5. 288→289: keep, with the recorded reason narrowed

The G minor at 289 follows an E-minor cadence with no G major before it. c103 and c193 kept it partly on PDF 452, that a key "enters right at the beginning of the new section without a transition". On the page, that sentence is about tonicized IV in ABA and rondo forms. It does not cover a remote minor III inside a reprise.

The unit's real support is different:
- §6 and Ex. 26-7 (PDF 448): the D7 at 273–275 is V7 of G and foreshadows the key fourteen bars ahead.
- G minor is a transient area between the stable E minor (277–288) and the V (301), Points 4.
- The relation through mixture that §2 asks of a remote key exists at the scale of the movement: G major turns to G minor at 55–57.

c293 adds PDF 586: a key that obviously starts a section is heard at once as a new tonic. These reasons are enough. A pivot on beat 3 of 288 (a D, ♮VII of E and V of G minor) was weighed in c193 and gives a weaker section start.

**Fix.** None. The record should cite PDF 448 and 586, not 452.

Unit 26's plans are about the order of key areas, not their length, and every area here lasts only as long as its function:
- G: 20 bars, then it turns to minor (§8: III "is not a final goal").
- F: 16 bars as the ♭II episode.
- B in the trio: 2 bars, the length of a cadence.
- A in the coda: 8 bars.

Exercise 8 extends its reprise, but it is a binary minuet with repeats. A scherzo whose first part already returns to the tonic tutti at 105 does not need a longer reprise. Dvořák 9/iii's reprise is 70 % of its first scherzo, ours 56 %, and the coda lengths match.

The movement is the shortest of the four (I about 7:11, II 5:45, finale about 7:00), which is right between an Adagio and a finale. No change (c003, c083, c283, c313).

### 7. Static spans 133–152 and 225–252: keep

These are the movement's two long tonic and dominant pedals: 133–152 (13.9 s) and 225–252 (22.5 s). Neither has to make a modulation. 133–152 changes mode and tempo into the trio. 241–252 brings back the leading tone through iv7–Fr6–V7, as §8 asks before a return ("the leading tone must appear").

The codetta 225–240 stays on E. Ex. 26-11 places a transition between the middle section's IV and the reprise; here the transition is 241–252. c023 already cut four bars from these spans. A further cut would renumber every later bar that movement I and the finale quote (357–360, 363–371), for no gain the reading names.
