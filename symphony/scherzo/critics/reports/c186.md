## Findings

### 1. The struck multiple stops at 339 have no "non div." (applied)
**Bars:** 339. Vln II G3–E4–B4–G5, violas B3–G4–E5, cellos E2–B2: one accented crotchet with a down-bow at the end of the coda peak.
**Problem:** c025 added these chords as multiple stops so that every player sounds the whole chord. Elsewhere the score marks this case: "non div." at 72, 105, 300 and 305, and "div." at 169, 209, 244, 349 and 354. Here, on the loudest string chord of the movement and the only four-note chord, nothing is marked. In a loud tutti, sections often split an unmarked three- or four-note chord by stand partner (outside the upper notes, inside the lower ones). That would halve each note and change the chord the composer wrote.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 30: "Some composers wish to have each player perform double-stops. In that case, it is safer to write non div. so that no division between the two players will occur." PDF 31: "When triple- or quadruple-stops are to be divided, it is helpful to specify how this is to be done", and Ex. II-12 shows the div. / non div. markings on such chords. PDF 29: a triple stop needs f or mf and a quadruple stop is arpeggiated. At fff both work as written, so the chord itself stays.
**Fix (applied):** coda.ily, bar 339:
`codaVlnTwo`: `<g e' b' g''>4->\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." r4 r4 |`
`codaVla`: `<b g' e''>4->\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." r4 r4 |`
`codaVc`: `<e, b,>4->\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." r4 r4 |`
A `% c186:` comment heads the file. The text prints above the ⊓ signs on page 12 and collides with nothing (checked at 150 dpi). No note, dynamic or bow sign changed.
