## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 47: Hn 1's A4 sounds a tone above its own resolution G4 (applied)
- **Problem.** In the G-major second idea, the woodwinds and strings change chord on the 3/2 minims. At 47 b3 the bass moves D→E (V–vi), and Cl 1 and the violas go to G4, the 3rd of vi. Hn 1 holds A4 (the 5th of the D chord) through the bar and resolves to G4 only at 48. On 47 b3 the A4 therefore sounds for a full crotchet a whole tone above two instruments already playing its resolution at the same octave, inside an E4–G4–A4 cluster at f/mf. Hn 2 holds the leading tone F♯3 over the E bass, a step above the cellos' E3, and rises only at 48.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 469–470 (Unit 27 sec. 7, Ex. 27-10): a dissonance sounding together with its tone of resolution, in a voice other than the bass, gives "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". PDF 352: anticipating the tone of resolution "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution". The only safe cases are the bass and a top-voice suspension against its resolution an octave lower. Here the resolution is at the unison and the horn is in the middle. PDF 202 (Unit 13): in V–vi the doubled 3rd lets 7̂ move to 8̂. Hn 2's F♯ is exactly that 7̂, and it now lags a beat behind. This score removed the same figure at 55, 131 and 287 (c139, c172) and at 196 (c172). The E-minor statement of this idea already moves its horn with the 3/2 at the matching place (sC Hn 2 `fis'2.--~ | fis'4 g'2--` at 123–124), so bar 47 was the one statement left with the clash.
- **Answer to c241's reason for keeping it.** c241 read 47 as a whole-chord suspension over a moving bass (Ex. 21-37, 21-14). In Ex. 21-37 every upper voice is suspended, so no voice sounds the resolution early. At 47 two voices sound it. PDF 352's relief for "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre" covers Beethoven's top-voice suspension over an inner resolution an octave lower. It does not cover a unison second in the middle of the chord.
- **Fix (sA.ily).** `sAHnOne` 45–48: `b'2.--( | b'2.-- | a'2.-- | g'2.--)` → `b'2.--( | b'2.-- | a'2-- g'4--~ | g'2.)`. `sAHnTwo` 45–48: `g2.--( | g2.-- | fis2.-- | g2.--)` → `g2.--( | g2.-- | fis2-- g4--~ | g2.)`. Beat 3 of 47 is now E2 E3 E4 G3 G4 E5 E6, a clean vi with its 3rd doubled. Hn 1 moves A4→G4 in unison with Cl 1, and Hn 2 moves F♯3→G3 an octave under the violas, which it already doubles at 45–47. The horns keep their downbeat dotted minims everywhere else in 45–56, including the hemiola cadence 53–55, and both horns now share one rhythm (one stem in the part-combined staff). The all-pairs scan's only new entry is that Hn 2/viola octave doubling at 47 b3. No outer-voice parallel was added.

## Considered and kept
- **155 and 160 (trio drone E3–B3 under V7).** The horns' E is a held inner pedal. It is prepared by the tonic and returns to it, and it stops before the cadential V7 at 166. That is PDF 368's case, not an unresolved 4th of the PDF 474–475 kind. It is the trio's colour.
- **200 (A4 under G♯5) and 111/311 (inner ♭9 C4).** c172 kept both for reasons that still hold (different colours an octave and more apart; fifths return if 111 is changed).
- **126 (♭9 C4 below the 3rd D♯4).** The 9th and 7th move in parallel 6ths into B3 and G4, which is PDF 463's inner-voice case. It is c082's protected common-tone ♭9.
- **347 b3 (Bsn 1 D♯3–E3 under the B7).** This is a weak-beat anticipation of the next chord tone, Ex. 27-17's case. The oboe takes the leading tone D♯5 into 348.
