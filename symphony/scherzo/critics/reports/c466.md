## Findings

### 1. Vln I at 177–180: mark "sul A". Applied.

**Bars.** 177–180, Vln I, p dolce espress., T's consequent an octave above the cellos (C♯5–D♯5–E5–G♯5–A5–G♯5–F♯5–C♯5–B4), "a level under" them (c461).

**Problem.** Unmarked, a section plays this in first or third position. That puts E5, G♯5, A5, G♯5 and F♯5 on the E string, and E5 may be the open string. The line then changes colour in the middle of the slur, on the phrase's top notes, and turns brightest on the A5 that the cellos are holding as their own climax. The doubling is meant to stay under the cellos, and the E string works against that.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75: "In soft, lyrical, expressive passages, the composer will often prefer the higher pitches to be performed on the A-string rather than have the player cross to the E-string, where these upper notes take on a different, more brilliant timbre." His example is Brahms 3/iii mm. 13–24, sul A (Ex. III-7). On the page image it stays within C5–B♭5. Adler cites the same movement's mm. 1–12 as a model cello melody (PDF 100). So Brahms's movement does exactly what this trio does at 169–184: the cellos sing the tune, then the violins take it on the A string. PDF 73: name a string only for timbre, and timbre is the reason here. PDF 73–74: an open string stands out in an expressive passage, and sul A removes the open E5. The compass C♯5–A5 lies inside the octave above the open A, which is c076's limit (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26) and the limit c121 used when it removed the earlier mark at 169. In 4th position on the A string (E5 1, F♯5 2, G♯5 3, A5 4) the notes 177–179 lie under one hand.

**Fix (applied).** tA.ily, tAVlnOne:
`cis''2(\p\<_\upbow^\markup \italic "dolce espress." -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan dis''4 | … | cis''4 b'4\stopTextSpan gis'4)`.
The dashed line ends on B4 at 180 beat 2, because the G♯4 on beat 3 lies below the A string. No notes, slurs or dynamics change. A c466 comment is in tA.ily.

### 2. Vln II at 349–352: mark "sul A". Applied.

**Bars.** 349–352, Vln II (the whole section since c457), pp dolce. This is T's lower octave, the same C♯5–A5 line as finding 1, under the solo violin and Fl 1 at C♯6–A6.

**Problem.** The coda's T is built from two colours an octave apart: one violinist pp on the E string ("dolce, solo") over the Vln II section. Unmarked, the section plays E5–G♯5–A5–G♯5–F♯5 on the E string too. The two octaves then share the same bright string, and the solo loses the contrast that sets it apart from the section.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75 defines the two colours that are wanted here. The E string played softly gives "the most ethereal sounds, which are clear and luminous". That suits the solo, and c438/c457 made the line a single player for this reason. The A string keeps soft high notes less brilliant, which suits the section under it (Ex. III-7). PDF 81: the solo is the concertmaster, so the colour difference between soloist and section is the point. The same compass and position as finding 1 apply, so the two statements of T's consequent in the trio and coda are played alike.

**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, codaVlnTwo:
`cis''2(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic "dolce" -\tweak bound-details.left.text \markup \italic "sul A" \startTextSpan dis''4 | … | cis''4( b'4\stopTextSpan a'4)`.
The mark ends before the A4 at 352 beat 3, so the open A is left to the player's choice. A c466 comment is in coda.ily.
