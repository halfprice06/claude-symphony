## Findings

### 1. Bars 161-164: the harp's off-beat chords stop for T's third phrase. Applied.

**Problem.** The trio's first statement (153-168) has one background for all sixteen bars: harp off-beat chords on beats 2 and 3, pizz. bass with the harp's left hand on beat 1, and the Hn 3·4 pedal. The third phrase (161-164) is the period's contrasting phrase. It climbs to A5, has no tied suspension and ends on IV. It gets the same accompaniment as phrases 1, 2 and 4. The first critic of this series noted it (c001, "T's third phrase shares the rhythm and contour of the first two"). Later cycles changed its notes (c081, c122), but its scoring never changed.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 136-137, Ex. V-13 (Mozart, K. 525/ii, mm. 1-8): "a sudden cessation of the Alberti-type accompaniment figure changes the mood of the predictable, settled statement to a light, ethereal texture which highlights the second phrase of the theme." The page image shows the second violin's figure stopping while the bass keeps its crotchets. PDF 137: orchestration clarifies "even a phrase, by means of texture". PDF 166: orchestration "must clarify the form ... by its unique contribution, the element of color". Bars 153-160 set up the pattern, so its absence at 161 is heard. Its return at 165 marks the cadence phrase.

**Harmony without the harp's chords.** 161: C♯ (bass, tune), E3 (Hn 4), B3 (Hn 3). 162: G♯1, E3, B3, tune E5-G♯5-A5. 163: F♯1, E3, A3 (Hn 3 since c469), tune A5-F♯5. 164: A1, E3, A3, tune C♯5-A4. Each bar keeps its root, and the horns keep the pedal. Bar 166 (c493) and the "non arpegg." marking at 153 are unchanged.

**Fix (applied, sections/tA.ily, tAHarpR; comment c524 above the variable).**
`r4 <gis cis' e'>4 <gis cis' e'>4 | r4 <gis b e'>4 <gis b e'>4 | r4 <a cis' fis'>4 <a cis' fis'>4 | r4 <a cis' fis'>4 <a cis' fis'>4 |` (161-164) becomes `R2.*4 |`. tAHarpL (the downbeat bass) is unchanged. No note of T or of any other part changes. parts-cues.json needs nothing: the harp has no cue here, and the Cl 1 cues at 165 still point at a sounding Cl 1.

### Considered, built in thought or tested against the reading, not applied

- **181-184, Vln I an octave higher (D♯5-F♯5-B5 | B5-A♯5-C♯6 | B5).** This was the next most audible candidate. Vln I's doubling falls to D♯4-B4 at T's cadence in B, its weak register (PDF 133). It makes the music worse for three reasons. First, the doubling would become the top line and take the foreground from the cellos. Second, B5 would anticipate the violins' high statement at 209, which is the anticipation PDF 135 warns against ("Brahms saves the higher violin color for the more important foreground statement"). Third, Fl 1's turn into B5 at 183-184 would merge with it. As things stand, the bars follow Ex. V-19 (PDF 141): low violins with cellos an octave below.
- **169-184, violas con sord. dovetailing Vln II's downbeat rests (PDF 135).** The downbeat is already sounded by the cellos' tune and the arco bass. Adding violas would spend their re-entry at 193 (c461) for a change that is barely audible at pp.
- **15-16, cellos doubling theme A's fall (PDF 141, Ex. V-19).** Ex. V-16 (PDF 139) supports the current order: bare first statement, cellos added in the counterstatement at 31-32 (c459). Not raised again (c259, c434).
- **237-240, basses dropping out under the timpani roll (PDF 154).** The harp already thins the codetta (c153), and the change would be too small to hear. Not applied.
