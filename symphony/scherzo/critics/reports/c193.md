### 1. Three section headers describe an older score (105–120, 205–208, 245). Applied (comments only).

**Problem.** The composer and later critics locate the form and its scoring from these headers.
- sC.ily: "105-120: theme A ff, tutti: Vln I with Fl 1-2 and Ob 1 at pitch, Cl 1 and Vln II an octave lower". The score has Vln I, Vln II and Ob 1 at pitch at 105–112, Cl 2 and the cor anglais an octave lower (c015, c021), the flutes entering an octave higher only at 109 (c125), and Vln II on the lower octave from 113 (c067).
- tB.ily: "201-208: ... T's head in Cl 1 and Fl 1". Since c036, 205–208 are Fl 2 and Cl 2 (G♯–A–B–A♯–A, the motto tail anticipated before 209), so the firsts rest before T at 209.
- retr.ily: "the head of theme A in the violas (241), Vln II (245) and Vln I (249)". Since c161 the second entry is Cl 1; the header also does not name the approach to V that c013, c053 and c062 built (iv7 243, Fr6 244, V 245).

**Evidence.** PDF 450, boxed note: reductions and plans are meaningful only when checked against the score. PDF 456 §15 is the principle these bars serve (the returning V preceded by IV and an augmented sixth, with its 7th at 251–252); a header that leaves it out misleads anyone checking row 22 of the table.

## Tested and kept (no change)

- **The reprise's G minor at 289.** It follows an E-minor cadence (287–288) with no G major before it, so G minor here is reached without the intermediate III that the exposition sounds at 37–56 (PDF 443: remote keys relate only through mixture). I considered a pivot on beat 3 of 288 (a soft D, ♭VII of E and V of G minor, as Ex. 26-2c and 26-16 use it). I kept the bar as it is: 289 is the start of a new section after a double bar, and PDF 452 says a new key "frequently—and typically—enters right at the beginning of the new section without a transition"; the D7 at 273–275 has already sounded V of G inside the reprise; and an upbeat chord before a solo fugato entry would weaken the entry the exposition makes from silence at 57. c103's decision stands.
- **C♯ minor is never confirmed by its own I (185–188, 197–200).** Correct under §15 and Ex. 26-17 (PDF 456): VI is an upper neighbour of the prolonged V, not a key; the deceptive G♯7→A at 200–201 turns it into the step down in 3rds to IV (Ex. 26-14).
- **349–354 without D♯.** The return to E is made at 347–348 (B7 → E) and confirmed by ii7–ii6–6/4–V7–I at 351–356 (Points 2). No change.
- **The fades at 133–152 and 225–252.** Each sits on a mode change (G→G♯ at 141–145, G♯→G at 241) or on the subdominant approach to V (243–245). PDF 456 §16 allows the mode change into the trio without a dominant. Raised and kept by c013, c103, c133, c153, c183; the reading gives no new reason.
- **Length and the two-stage coda.** 276 s is right between the Adagio and the finale; the coda's first stage (11 s) gives the bass E–D–C–B (♭VII to V, PDF 455) and its second stage carries the late IV (PDF 451–452) and the protected close. No cut, no repeat.
