## Findings

### 1. The first retransition 89-100 has no sustained sound; the bass clarinet should keep its B2 (applied)
Bars 89-100. The F-major episode ends with the bass clarinet arriving on B2 at 89 (pp, the last note of its 73-89 pedal), and it then stops. For the next twelve bars, the whole approach to the reprise over the dominant, the B pedal is only pizzicato crotchets (Vc, Cb) and the timpani's motive x. Above them are the violas' head at 89, Ob 1's answer at 92-94, the cor anglais's staccato arpeggios at 95-100 and Vln I's head at 95. Nothing sustains the dominant. The second retransition, at 245-252, has exactly this sustain: the bass clarinet holds B2 pp under the timpani (c396, accepted). Adler casts the bass clarinet's chalumeau as "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective sound ... mysterious and shadowy and can be quite sinister" (PDF 217), and PDF 184 asks for each register to get the part that "could not be performed on any other instrument". A soft held dominant under a pizzicato and timpani approach is that part. It is not a brass entry (c559's reason), and it does not double the pizzicato rhythm the way the violas' crotchets did (c557's reason), because it is a held tone two octaves below the violins' head. The player has the B already under his fingers. The listener hears it as a low halo under the dry pizzicato, and the B then passes without a break to Bsn 2, the bass trombone and the low strings at 101. For breath (PDF 188 and 230, much breath in the low register), the line breaks at beat 3 of 94, where the timpani's second x ends and Ob 1's answer closes. It comes back pp under Vln I's head at 95 and grows with the timpani from 98 to mp at 100.

Fix (sB.ily, sBBassCl, bars 89-104; was `ais,2.-- | b,2) r4 | R2.*15 |`):
```
ais,2.-- | b,2.)~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2 r4 | b,2.~\pp | b,2.~ | b,2.~ | b,2.~\< | b,2.~ | b,2.\mp | R2.*4 |
```
Sounding B2 = written C♯4 on the B♭ bass clarinet, inside the chalumeau. The section length is unchanged (16 bars after 88). parts-cues.json needs no change: part 08's next entry is 139 with its Vc cue at 137, and no other part cues the bass clarinet. Before keeping it I checked whether this best candidate makes the music worse. The dryness of 89-100 is still there, because every attack is still pizzicato or timpani. The only change is that the dominant now has a floor, which also prepares the held B pedal of 101-104.

### 2. Contrabassoon articulation (no change)
Adler PDF 230 calls fast repeated contrabassoon staccato "treacherous and risky". In the reprise tuttis (105-120, 305-320) the Cbsn plays single tenuto crotchets with two beats' rest, not motive x's quavers. Its sustained writing stays in its lowest octave (E1-B1). Its breaths fall at 56, 72 and 104. Nothing to change.

### 3. Cor anglais register (no change)
PDF 205: the top "loses its personality", and the lowest fifth or sixth is "richly beautiful". The C.a. reaches D♯5/F5 only inside the hemiola doubling (103, 302), where it adds weight and is not meant to be heard as a colour of its own. Its solo moment, 357-360 (C♯4-C4-B3, c560), is in the rich low register. At pp, B3 (written F♯4) sits at the top edge of the lowest fifth, which is the edge Adler warns about for the oboe family (PDF 174-175, 198). The note is the end of a diminuendo line in one of the most secure registers on the instrument, so I leave it.

### 4. Flute breathing (no change)
PDF 188 (Brahms 1/iv: the second flute holds so the first can breathe). Fl 1's longest unbroken run is the second idea, 45-56: twelve bars at 2.=88, about 8 seconds, slurred in four-bar phrases. A quick breath is possible at each slur break (48-49, 52-53). Fl 2 relieves Fl 1 at 205-208 (c036). Fl 1 rests 321-332 before the B6 summit. This follows the book.

### 5. Clarinet break in the trio tune (no change)
PDF 210. On the A clarinet T's head G♯4-A4-B4 is written B4-C5-D5, just above the break, and T's long notes sit in the clarion ("bright, incisive, expressive" in Ex. VII-56). The throat tones (sounding E4-G4) come only as passing notes. Adler says the break gives a professional no trouble.

### 6. Bassoon solos accompanied (no change)
PDF 226: an accompanied bassoon "has a tendency to get swallowed up". Bsn 1's T head at 233-236 (p) is a degree above Hn 3·4 (pp). The dolce at 345-348 is over pizzicato only, with Ob 1 and Cl 1. Both are marked the way Adler asks.
