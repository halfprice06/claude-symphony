## What else I studied

**Schubert 4/iii.** There is no copy in reference-corpus/, and IMSLP's files could not be opened from here, so I worked from what I know of the score and checked it against three sources. The Menuetto is 54 bars and the Trio 32 bars, both with repeats (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Schubert)). Apple Music's note says the Menuetto "is notable for agile rhythmic syncopation and, in its trio, a blithe tune for flutes and oboes" (https://classical.music.apple.com/us/work/franz-schubert-1797-pp45). A listener's note calls the opening "getting ready to trip over its own feet" and the movement "heavy handed" with timpani (https://fugueforthought.de/2015/04/22/schubert-symphony-no-4-in-cm-d-417-tragic/). The facts I used: the Menuetto is a single short tutti period, f, with accents set against the 3/4 bar and chromatic part-writing. The Trio is a soft wind tune over plain string support. The structure is binary with repeats. Adler's two Schubert examples in the reading (Ex. VIII-1, VIII-5/6, Symphony 8; PDF 235–238) show the same composer's habit of saving registers and giving each thematic element its own colour.

## Holistic comparison with Schubert 4/iii

The two pieces are of different sizes. Schubert's is a Classical minuet: two repeated strains, one tutti texture, f almost throughout, with its interest in accents against the bar line and in chromatic inner voices. This movement is a late-Romantic scherzo of 376 bars with a written-out reprise, a fugato, a Neapolitan episode and a two-stage coda. I would not move it toward Schubert's form. The plan, the keys and the finale's quotations depend on the present shape. Three points of the comparison still apply.

- **Accents against the bar.** Schubert's main resource is the metre pushed off its downbeats. This movement does the same thing at its cadences (hemiolas at 53–55, 101–104, 129–131, 285–287, 301–304, 337–339) and at the three-bar groups of 89–100. That is as consistent a metric idea as Schubert's, used at more points. No change.
- **The trio as a wind tune over plain strings.** Schubert's trio contrasts with the Menuetto by colour, not by development. This trio does the same at 153 (Cl 1 over harp off-beats and a horn drone) and then turns the colour round at 169 and 209 (Adler PDF 253). This is at a master's level.
- **Doublings that change with the function of a phrase.** Schubert (Adler PDF 238) gives the opening theme a dark Ob+Cl unison and then reverses the clarinets' role for the second theme. This movement does that everywhere except at one place, the strings' answers in the counterstatement (Finding 1).

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? On the scoring, yes. I checked the 40 applicable principles above bar by bar. The one breach I found is that the counterstatement's string answers are scored exactly like the first statement's soft dip. Two of the reading's examples (Haydn 103, Shostakovich 6) treat exactly that moment as the place for a change.

## Findings

### 1. 31–32 and 35–36: the cellos double the strings' answers an octave lower, and the basses hold the root arco (applied)

**Problem.** In the counterstatement the winds call (Fl 1 + Ob 1 in unison over held Cl/Bsn chords, 29–30 and 33–34) and the strings answer (31–32, 35–36). The answers are Vln I alone at p, falling to B3 and F♯4 (E5–B4–B4–G4 | F♯4–D♯4–B3, then F♯5–D5–D5–A4 | D5–A4–A4–F♯4), over the violas' off-beats and the plucked bass. That is the same scoring as theme A's first statement at 13–16. So the answer has less body than the call it answers, its lowest notes are the violins' weakest, and nothing tells the ear that this is a second statement.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 141, Ex. V-19 (Shostakovich 6/iii): in a light string theme, "when they reach into their lower register, they are doubled in octaves by the cellos. This addition contributes a feeling of greater resolution to the passage and prepares the listener for the woodwind" entry. Here the next event is the woodwinds' second call (33) and then the a2 flutes (37). Adler PDF 139, Ex. V-16 (Haydn 103): "the simple addition of the cellos and double basses … sets off the first and the second statement of the theme and prepares so well for the tutti version that is to follow". Here the tutti follows at 41. Adler PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12: a held bass "ties it together harmonically, while pointing up the spiccato nature of the tune" by contrast. The basses' held E and B, D and D under the answers carry over the wind chord that has just stopped (Adler PDF 241–243). Earlier critics rejected the same doubling at 15–16 (c259, c359) for two reasons: it would thicken the leggiero opening, and Shostakovich's accompaniment is bowed while that one was plucked. Neither reason applies here. The first statement 13–28 is left as it is, and at 31 the violas' accompaniment is already bowed. The Fledermaus point (Adler PDF 245) is kept as well: the wind chord still breaks at 31 and 35, so each string answer is heard clearly.

### 2. 15–16 and 255–256: no cello doubling (considered, not proposed)

The same Adler PDF 141 principle could be applied to theme A's own dip at 15–16. I keep c259/c359's decision. There the accompaniment is plucked, the opening is the movement's lightest page, and the whole effect of Finding 1 depends on the first statement staying bare.

### 3. Schubert-style weak-beat accents in theme A (considered, not proposed)

Schubert's accents against the bar would mean sf on beat 2 or 3 in theme A. The do-not-touch list protects theme A (finale quotes at pitch), and Dan has asked for no accented brass attacks. The metric conflict this movement already uses is the hemiola at cadences, which is enough (A&S Unit 3, c441). Not proposed.

### 4. 349–356 after c457 (checked, no regression)

One solo violin and Fl 1 carry the top octave over Vln II tutti pp an octave lower. The top octave is the one the ear follows, and the flute strengthens the solo there (Adler PDF 266, Ex. VIII-37). No change.
