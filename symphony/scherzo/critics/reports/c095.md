## Findings

### 1. Middle-line notes flip against the surrounding stems, about 45 stems in 30 bars (applied)

**Problem.** LilyPond gives every note on the middle line, and every two-note chord balanced about it, a down-stem, whatever the notes around it do. In this score many accompaniment figures sit just below the middle line. A figure therefore flips on its one middle-line note:
- Violas, `r4 c'-. c'-.` at 18, 21, 23, 258, 261 and 263, among bars of `r4 b-. b-.` with up-stems (sA/dA). The viola part prints the same flip (page 1, bars 18, 21, 23).
- Vln II, B4–A4–D♯4 at 95, 97 and 99: B4 is down, the rest of the group up.
- Vln II 181: D♯4 and F♯4 are up, B4 is down and tied over to a down-stemmed B4 in 182, then A♯4 is up. Vc 181: G3 up, B3 down, A3 up.
- Vc/Cb, D3 crotchets at 35–36 down-stemmed, then G2 at 37–38 up-stemmed, in the same figure.
- Cl 1·2, G4–D5 dyads at 41–46 and 53 down-stemmed among up-stemmed chords.
- Also Vc/Cb 345, Cb/Cbn 53–54, Cb 67, Vc 216, Vln II 121 and 277, Cl 83 and 206–207, Hn 1·2 299–300, C.a. 336.

**Evidence.** v95 page 1, bar 18 (Vla). Page 2, bars 35–36 (Vc, Cb). Page 4, bars 95–99 (Vln II). Page 7, bar 181 (Vln II, Vla, Vc).

**Reading.** Gould PDF 27: "When notes are on the centre stave-line, the stems may go in either direction. The direction is determined by context. Continue the stem direction of surrounding stems that are in one direction only." Her example on that page is E–F♯–G–B–A–G–F♯ with the B stemmed up. PDF 28: "When the stem direction varies within a bar, maintain the stem direction of the notes that are part of the same beat or half-bar". The down-stem is only the convention "when there is no clear-cut case". PDF 60–61 applies the same rule to chords whose outer notes are equidistant from the middle line ("the direction may be determined by context. The aim is to achieve a consistent stem direction within a phrase, bar or half-bar"), and for chords of three or more notes the majority decides. Gould PDF 21 gives the reason behind the clef rules, which applies here too: the reader should see the contour of a figure intact.

**Fix (applied).** A Voice engraver in a new file, `stems.ily`, used by both the score and the parts. It does the following:
- It collects the voice's stems, leaving out the stems LilyPond gives rests.
- It finds each run of neutral, unbeamed stems with no direction already set. Part-combined voices keep the directions the combiner gives them.
- If the run lasts at most a bar, and the stems on either side of it, within a bar's length, both point up, or only one side has a stem and it points up, the run takes up-stems.
- Where the two sides disagree, or the run lasts more than a bar, the down-stem stays (Gould's "no clear-cut case"). An example is Vln II B4 at 37–39.
- It applies the majority rule to balanced chords of three or more notes.

Files changed:
- `stems.ily` (new): the engraver, `middleLineStemEngraver`.
- `score.ly`: `\include "stems.ily"` before `\include "global.ily"`, and `\consists #middleLineStemEngraver` in the `\Voice` context of `\layout`.
- `parts-gen.py` (the parts.ly template): `\include "stems.ily"` after `\include "parts-lib.ily"`, and the same `\consists` in its `\Voice` context, so parts.ly is regenerated with it.

The heart of it (the full file is in /tmp/critic-095/work/stems.ily):
```
(dir (cond ((not (within? (mo i) (mo last-i))) #f)          ; run longer than a bar: leave
           ((and prev next) (if (= prev next) prev #f))      ; both sides agree
           (else (or prev next))))                           ; one side only
(if (eqv? dir UP) (for-each (lambda (m) (ly:grob-set-property! (st m) 'direction UP)) members))
```
No notes, rhythms, slurs, dynamics or bar lengths change. Slurs and ties on the flipped notes move to the other side automatically (Vln II 181–182: the slur now runs above).
