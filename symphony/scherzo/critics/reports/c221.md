## Findings

### 1. Bars 269-271: the cor anglais holds B3 pp at the top of its lowest fifth, a fourth above the bassoon's solo call. Applied.

**Problem.** At the reprise's call (c041) Bsn 1 plays theme A's head, p staccato, "scherzando", on E3-B2-B2-E3 | G3-F♯3-F3 | E3. Above it Cl 1·2 hold G4/E4 pp and the cor anglais holds B3 pp. On the cor anglais, B3 is written F♯4, the top note of its lowest fifth (written B3-F♯4). Two problems follow. The marking asks for a dynamic the reading says this register cannot give. And the held note sits a fourth to a sixth above the bassoon's melody (E3, G3), in the same reed colour. That is the register where an accompanied bassoon is easiest to swallow, so the call loses definition.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 198: in the oboe's lowest fifth "one should never require a pianissimo dynamic in this register, even from the best players". PDF 205: the cor anglais's registral properties "are like its sister instrument, the oboe", and its lowest fifth or sixth has "tremendous expressive carrying power", which is not what an accompaniment needs. PDF 226: the bassoon, accompanied, "has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments", and the accompaniment's dynamics must keep it clear. c041 itself put the clarinets at pp for that reason (dA.ily comment). The reading says clarinets can play pp in every register (PDF 210), so the clarinets are not the problem; the cor anglais is.

**Fix (applied).** The cor anglais holds B4 (written F♯5), the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" register of Ex. VII-47, where pp is easy. The chord becomes E4-G4-B4 in close position above the clarinets, two octaves clear of the bassoon's E3-G3. Colour, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged, and so is c041's choice of instruments. dA.ily, `dACorAnglais`, bars 17-19 of the section:

```
b'2.~\pp | b'2. | b'4 r4 r4      % was b2.~\pp | b2. | b4 r4 r4
```

At 271 beat 1 the B4 crotchet sits under Vln I's E5 (V of E, chord tone). There is no new outer-voice motion: the top voice is a held note over the bassoon, and analysis.txt is identical to v221's.

### Memorability (lens 1)

The woodwinds carry three of the movement's tunes: T (Cl 1), the trio call (Ob 1, answered by cor anglais and cellos) and the codetta echo. Each sits in the register Adler names as the instrument's best melodic register, so each colour stays attached to its tune. Theme A and the second idea belong to the violins, with winds doubling at the unison or octave, and the reading finds nothing wrong with those doublings. Compared with Dvořák 9/iii (flute and oboe on the trio tune) and the soft trios of Mozart 39 and Beethoven's Eroica (wind solos over light accompaniment), the scoring of the tunes is idiomatic. The only point the reading contradicts is the one held accompaniment note at 269-271.
