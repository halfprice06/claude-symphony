## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The MIDI ignores the printed dynamics of the part-combined voices. Every rendering has had a false balance in the tuttis. Applied.
**Bars:** 105-120 and 305-320 especially, plus about 30 smaller places: 29-44, 51-52, 77-88, 121-124, 147-148, 205-208, 213-223.
**Problem.** `\partCombine` creates the voices "one", "two", "shared" and "solo". Each Voice has its own Dynamic_performer, so a dynamic written in a part only reaches the voice that is active at that moment. When the combiner switches voices, the new voice plays at a stale or default level. In v124's MIDI:
- The trombones play 105-120 at velocity 123, the loudest track in the tutti, at the ff of 104 plus accents. The print says f.
- Ob 1 plays the ff tutti theme at 33 (pp) and the clarinets at 41.
- The bassoons fade from 40 to 34 through the ff (105-120).
- Hn 3·4 play 77-80 and 85-88 of the pp F-major episode at 66 (mf), twice the level of the harp and the winds.
- Cl 1·2 play the link's pp (147-148) and the sub. p at 123-124 at 64.

The print is right. The MP3 that Dan listens to is not, and the trombone line is exactly the brass bite that his rule forbids.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 147-148 (d'Indy marks the trombones ppp "so as not to dominate the entire orchestra"), PDF 154-156 (the background a level under the foreground), and PDF 151 (Mozart: the octave with the most weight is heard as the theme). In this MIDI, the oboe's octave of the theme was nearly silent.
**Fix (score.ly, \midi block; the print is unchanged).** Move the performer to the staff. The shared staves already carry equal simultaneous dynamics (a score rule), so one set per staff is what the print means:
```
\context { \Staff \consists "Dynamic_performer" }
\context { \Voice \remove "Dynamic_performer" }
```
The harp's PianoStaff performer is kept, and the harp's velocities are unchanged. Two open hairpins then needed a written level, because the MIDI ran each one to a default: `link.ily` Hn 1 `gis'2(\p\> a'4` at 147 (the pp crescendo from 145 peaks at p), and `tB.ily` Bsn 1 `fis2.\pp` at 205 (was `\!`; this matches Fl 2 and Cl 2's pp there). After the change, no part-combined track stands out from the median in any bar except Hn 1·2 at 105-120/305-320. Those horns are marked ff in the print (c076).

### 2. Tutti theme A, 105-120 and 305-320: the accented brass crotchets on every downbeat give bite, not weight. Applied.
**Bars:** 105-120 (Hn 1·2, Tbn 1·2, bass trombone, tuba) and 305-320 (Hn 1·2).
**Problem.** Every downbeat of the 16-bar tutti has `b'4->` in the horns and `e,4->` in the low brass, which is 94 accents in sC and 32 in dC. The horns are also ff. With F1 applied, the horns are the loudest track in the tutti, and the accents are why.
**Evidence.** Dan's listening rule (BRIEF.md): brass accents soft, "weight rather than bite", with entries shaped by dynamics and doubling. Adler PDF 154 (Smetana): the brass give the fast tutti its "roundness", and brass on the fast figure would add "too much weight and take the lightness out". PDF 147-148: the trombones are kept under the tutti.
**Fix (sC.ily HnOne, HnTwo, TbnOne, TbnTwo, BassTbn, Tuba; dC.ily HnOne, HnTwo).** Replace each `4->` with `4--` (tenuto), for example `b'4--\ff r4 b'4-.` and `e,,4--\f r4 r4`. The notes, rhythm, dynamics (horns ff, low brass f, c076) and the c086 writing (horns on beats 1 and 3, single low-brass downbeats) are unchanged. The trumpets' staccato motive x is unchanged. The contrabassoon and basses keep their accents, because they are not brass and they define the downbeat. In the new MIDI the melody tracks (violins, cor anglais) lead the tutti, and no brass track is an outlier.

### 3. 105-108 and 305-308: two flutes in their weak register double the violins at pitch under ff brass. Applied.
**Bars:** 105-108 and 305-308 (Fl 1·2 a2, `e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-.`, B4-B5).
**Problem.** For the first phrase of the tutti, both flutes play at pitch with Vln I, Vln II and Ob 1 (and Ob 1 alone at 107). That puts five lines on E5 and two flutes on B4-G5 under four ff horns, trumpets and low brass. At 108 Fl 1 must drop the theme's last note (B4) to leap to E6 for 109. The flutes cannot take the upper octave at 105-108, because 107 would reach B6, which is kept for 336.
**Evidence.** adler1 PDF 185: the orchestrator must not "cover" a weak register, because "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume". Adler II PDF 151 (Mozart): every note must be "played effectively by every instrument that he uses", and the flute is not kept in unison where it "would have added nothing". PDF 150 (Barber): the doubling is enlarged "slightly in every measure". PDF 156: "a rest before a certain color enters" makes the entry "clearly perceived". PDF 152: the high flute octave needs no help.
**Fix (sC.ily and dC.ily, FluteOne and FluteTwo).** `R2.*4 | e'''4-.\ff b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. | a'''2->( ...` The flutes rest for the first phrase and enter a2 ff in the top octave at 109/309, as before. The first phrase is now strings, oboe, clarinets and cor anglais. Fl 1's seventh A5 at 104 is still resolved in its register by Cl 1's G5 at 105 (c092). The flutes still double the at-pitch octave at 113-116 (A5-C6, a good register), and the top octave at 117-120. Nothing else changes.
