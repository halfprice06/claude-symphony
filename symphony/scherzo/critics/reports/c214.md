## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 132 and 288: Ob 1 drops to E4, its thick lowest fifth, for the closing crotchet of the second idea. Applied.
**Problem.** In the E-minor statements of the second idea, Ob 1 doubles the tune's lower octave with Vln II (125-128, 281-284). At the hemiola cadence (129, 285) it starts on B4 with Vln II and then leaps up to G5 to join Fl 1 and Vln I for G5-E5 | F♯5-D♯5 | F♯5-E5. The composer did this so that the oboe does not follow Vln II down to G4-E4-D♯4 at f. On the closing staccato crotchet (132, 288), though, Ob 1 drops an octave from its E5 to E4 and doubles Vln II. E4 is the only oboe note below F4 in the whole movement (low.py; analysis.txt gives the oboes' range as E4-C♯6). It sits in the lowest fifth, where Adler says the oboe "becomes thick and tends to 'honk'", at f, on a light staccato cadence chord whose E4 Vln II already has.
**Evidence.** Adler II PDF 151 (Mozart 40/iv): the oboes are not given the last low note, "although they would have added a raucous quality to the low C♯"; they "fill out the space much better exactly where they are written", and "the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument". Adler II PDF 152 and 154: study why each line leaves the melody. Here the reason for leaving it (register) is dropped on the last note. Adler I PDF 198 (Ex. VII-32): thick and heavy in the lowest fifth; most effective from F4.
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily, sCOboeOne, bar 132: `| e'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*8 | }` becomes `| e''4-. r4 r4 | R2.*8 | }`. dA.ily, dAOboeOne, bar 288: `| e'4-. r4 r4 | }` becomes `| e''4-. r4 r4 | }`. Each has a c214 comment. Ob 1 now ends on E5 with Fl 1 and Vln I, in the octave it has held since 129/285. The chord at 132 keeps E2-E3-B3-E4 (Vln II)-G4-E5. The G-major statement at 56 is unchanged: there Ob 1 stays in Vln II's octave throughout (D5-B4-G4 | A4-F♯4 | A4-G4 | G4), all at or above F♯4, so it is consistent.

### Considered and kept
- **Ob 1's leap B4-G5 inside the slur at 129 and 285.** It breaks the tune's falling third, but each alternative is worse. Staying in Vln II's octave puts G4-D♯4 at f in the thick register. Taking B5 on the downbeat means a seventh leap up from C♯5 across the phrase join. Adler PDF 152 accepts deviations like this when register forces them.
- **Fl 1·2 at pitch with Vln I and Ob 1 at 113-116 and 313-316 (D♯5-C6).** This is not c124's weak register (B4-B5). Adler I PDF 185 calls the flute "clear and brilliant" from about the top of the staff, and the upper octave would reach C7, which is reserved for the finale.
- **Timpani G♯ f at 200 under the strings' diminuendo.** It is a single stroke on the bass G♯ at the moment the f is reached, before the fade. Adler PDF 150 (Barber, point 4) uses timpani on the last low note to mark the end of a phrase in the same way.
- **Bass clarinet silent through 333-339.** This is a partial tutti (PDF 147). In a fff tutti it would add nothing that Bsn 2, Cbn, Vc and Cb do not already give, and its B1 was moved to the contrabassoon by c016.
- **Ff horns held under the staccato summing-up at 333-335.** This is Smetana's adhesive (PDF 154), a level above the f trombones as in d'Indy (PDF 147-148). It also keeps Dan's rule of weight, not bite.
- **F-major episode 73-88 (Cl 2 under Cl 1's solo, B.cl. pedal).** Adler PDF 154 allows accompaniment by similar colours, and the passage is protected (c047, c104, c080).
