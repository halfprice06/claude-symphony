## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Every principle in the table is followed or does not apply. The points below were tested against the reading and kept, ranked by how close they came to needing a change.

### 1. Vln I 36→37: F♯4 to G5 (ninth) at speed. Kept.
Bar 36 ends on F♯4 (D string) and 37 starts on G5 (E string), a ninth across a silent A string, in staccato crotchets at 2. = 88. Adler PDF 82–83 lists silent string crossings among a section's skip problems, but his examples (Meistersinger, Bartók) are skips that need a change of position. Both notes are in first position (F♯4 2nd finger on D, G5 2nd finger on E), the bow is separate, and the same move occurs at 40→41, already accepted by c016/c121. No rest is needed; adding one would cut the head of the G-major phrase.

### 2. Cellos A3 on the countermelody's top minim at 179 and 219. Kept.
In first position the cellist plays A3 on the open A string (D string 4th finger only reaches G3). Adler PDF 73–74: open strings stand out in expressive passages. A cellist will choose a 4th-position D-string A3 here if the colour matters, and Adler says fingering is best left to the player (PDF 73). A printed "sul D" would put the next notes (G♯3, F♯3) in a high position for no gain. No mark.

### 3. Trio answer in the cellos at 189–192, D♯4–E4, marked dolce. Kept.
Adler PDF 99 calls the cello's A string brilliant and piercing. This line lies in the lowest position of the A string (D♯4, E4, C♯4, B3) and falls to the D string for F♯3–A3, which is the warm part of the instrument, not the piercing upper register Adler means. It is also in unison with the cor anglais (c011). Kept.

### 4. Countermelody 209–224 on the cellos' G and D strings under a forte T. Kept.
Adler PDF 99: the G string carries least. At 209–224 the cellos play mf under T at f, which is right for a background voice, and Bsn 2 doubles them (c088). An octave higher would cross the violas' off-beats (B3–E4) and Vln II's lower octave of T. Kept.

### 5. T's first-phrase rhythm (minim-crotchet, minim-crotchet, dotted figure, crotchet-minim) in Vln I at 193–196, then in Cl 1 and violas at 197–200. Kept.
This is the only place where the tune's opening rhythm is heard twice in a row outside a full statement. In 193–196 the violas have the call's turn figure (c137) under it, so the passage combines the two trio ideas before 197, where the call returns over T's head (c038, c117, protected). Lyrical string lines in the reading repeat a rhythm across a sequence in the same way (Tchaikovsky 5/ii, Ex. III-5; Brahms 3/iii, Ex. III-7). A change would reach into the protected 197–200 layering. Kept.

### 6. Melodic peaks and the E string. Kept.
Peaks: theme A C6 (24), second idea A6 (50), T in Vln I E6 then A6 (218–220), coda T G♯6 pp (354), single B6 fff (336). Loud high notes sit where Adler wants the E string's brilliance (PDF 75: second idea, 209, 336); soft high notes are the "ethereal, luminous" E string (169, 349–356, 369–376). The order of peaks rises through the movement to the one B6, under the finale's C7. Kept.

### Memorability verdict (lens 1)
Theme A is the movement's most memorable idea: a four-note head, the motto's chromatic tail, and a peak (C6) in its twelfth bar. The second idea has its own rhythm (minim across the bar, dotted lead-on since c031) and a clear climax. T is singable: peaks E5 → F♯5 (held, c121) → A5 (tied, c081), and a 4–3 cadence. The harp head at 73–80 and the coda's shared return of T are protected and work. Compared with the trio tunes Adler prints (Brahms 3/iii, Ex. III-7) and with Dvořák 9/iii's E-major trio tune, T has the same traits: long notes across the beat, stepwise contour, and one leap to each phrase peak. I found nothing that a change would improve without undoing an earlier, accepted decision.
