## Findings

### 1. Bar 339: the score loses Tbn 2's B2 in the last tutti chord. Applied.

- **Bars:** 339 (coda.ily, Trombones 1·2 staff).
- **Problem:** `codaTbnTwo` has `b,2--\f\> r4\!` at 339, and the trombone 2 part prints and plays it, but the full score shows only Tbn 1's G3 on the shared staff, and score.midi has no B2 there (the MIDI dump at 339 gives "Trombones 1·2: G3" only). The same lines at 333-334 combine correctly into B2/G3 chords. I reproduced it in a ten-bar test file: two parts that come out of shared multi-bar rests (`R2.*3`) and enter together with the same rhythm, dynamics and hairpin are merged by LilyPond 2.24's `\partCombine` into the first part's note alone. Splitting the rests into single bars does not help; `\partCombineChords` or `\partCombineApart` before the entry does. The compare script found no other dropped note on any shared staff (its only other hit, Hn 1 C5 at 110/310, comes from allplayers.midi being stale; see the note below).
- **Evidence:** the appendix chords (PDF 505-507: chords 1-4, 9, 17-19) all hold the trombones in close position over the tuba, with the chord complete inside the brass. Rimsky PDF 101 states the rule, and c194 applied it to give the trombones E2-B2-G3 over the tuba at 333-334 and 339. At 339, the final E-minor chord before the G.P., the printed brass now reads E1 (tuba), E2 (bass trombone), G3 (Tbn 1 alone), E4/G4 (trumpets), B3/E4/E4/B4 (horns). The fifth is missing from the trombones, the score disagrees with the part, and a conductor reading the score hears a B2 the score does not show.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-204/work/sections/coda.ily):**
  `codaTbnOne = { R2.*8 | g2.--(\f | g2.-- | g2.--) | R2.*3 | \partCombineChords g2--\f\> r4\! | \partCombineAutomatic R2. | R2.*36 | }`
  with a c204 comment above it. The staff now prints the B2/G3 chord with one f and one hairpin. Both players keep equal dynamics, and score.midi has B2 and G3 at 339. The parts do not change: the directives have no effect outside the part combiner, and the Tbn 2 part already had the note.

### Checked and kept (no change)

- **72/300, ff A-major chord without trumpets, trombones or timpani** (T3, T5, appendix): this is a step into the pp F-major episode, not a climax. The timpani have no A, and their silence from 57 to 88 makes the re-entry at 89 count. Hn 3·4 cannot play here before their senza sord. entry at 73.
- **336-338, no low brass under the fff peak** (appendix A1/A4 would add them): c034 answered this with Adler PDF 150 (Barber: leave trombones and tuba out of the bar of the high note, bring them in on the last chord). The horns, trumpets, timpani roll, contrabassoon, bassoons, cellos and basses carry the B-major chord. Finding 1 restores exactly that last chord's trombone spacing.
- **105-120, horns 1·2 and trumpets both on E4/B4, third only at G3 in Tbn 1 and Hn 3·4:** the brass chord is complete (A1). The G3s are the tenor countermelody, and putting G4 in Hn 2 would only thicken a third that already sounds in five parts.
- **339, Hn 1 B4 above the trumpets' E4/G4:** this is voice leading (Tpt 1 A4-G4 resolves the seventh). The brass stays in close order.
- **Timpani dynamics** (A5): f under ff at 105 and 305, ff at 333, the roll fff at 336. The appendix shows both equal and lower timpani levels, and the score's levels follow the brass rule.
