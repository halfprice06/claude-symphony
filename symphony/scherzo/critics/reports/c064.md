## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Trumpet 2 plays E4 against the dominant at 119 and 319 (applied)
Bars 119 (sC) and 319 (dC). Bass B, Hn 1 D♯5, Hn 2 D♯4, Hn 3 B4, Tbn 1·2 F♯3/D♯3, and the violas have D♯4 on beat 3. Tpt 2 plays motive x on E4, so beat 2 has E4 quavers against the melody's D♯ quavers (the same rhythm) and a semitone against Hn 2's D♯4 at f. At bar 111, the earlier V bar of the same phrase, Tpt 2 correctly has D♯4; bar 119 copies the tonic bars. Adler PDF 17 (printed 330) describes natural trumpets as limited to tonic and dominant, and his examples (Handel XI-3, PDF 18; Tchaikovsky XI-21, PDF 39–41) change the trumpet's pitch with the harmony. Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15) never puts a brass note a semitone against another brass note in a forte chord.
Fix (sC.ily `sCTptTwo` and dC.ily `dCTptTwo`, 15th bar): `dis'4-. dis'8-. dis'-. dis'4-.` replaces `e'4-. e'8-. e'-. e'4-.`.

### 2. Trumpet 1's B4 over the iv bars 113, 115, 313 and 315 (applied)
Bass A (F♯ at 313, iiø7). The violas hold C4+A4 and Hn 1 plays C5 (at 113/313) with an accent on beats 1 and 3, while Tpt 1 repeats B4 four times: a semitone between Tpt 1 and Hn 1 at f, and a ninth over the chord in the loudest instrument on the stage. The timpani already rests in these two bars, so the score drops the "tonic-dominant" instrument here but keeps the other one. This is the limitation Adler names on PDF 17: horns and trumpets "get stuck on the tonic and dominant notes". The trumpet should take a chord tone that also suits motive x.
Fix (sC.ily `sCTptOne` and dC.ily `dCTptOne`, 9th and 11th bars): `a'4-. a'8-. a'-. a'4-.` replaces `b'4-. b'8-. b'-. b'4-.`. A4 is the root of iv at 113/115 and the third of iiø7 at 313. It is in unison with Hn 3's A4 and sits a ninth under the melody's passing G5. No C-E-G sonority is added that was not already there (G is the melody's passing note in an A–C–E chord, as in v64). The trumpets still play only motive x, and the rhythm is unchanged.

### 3. Tuba in unison with the bass trombone on the last fff stroke, 339 (applied)
Coda bar 339 (codaTuba): the tuba plays E2 (`e,4`), the same note as the bass trombone. At 333–335 the tuba held E1 under the bass trombone's E2, which is the layout of every forte chord in Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16): tuba on the root an octave below the trombones' bass. The contrabassoon and basses are on E1 at 339, so the tuba moving up an octave takes weight off the low register at the climax. It also brings the low brass close to the "full low-brass unison bass" that BRIEF.md keeps for the finale.
Fix (coda.ily `codaTuba`): `e,,4->\fff r4 r4` replaces `e,4->\fff r4 r4` (the bar after `R2.*3`).
