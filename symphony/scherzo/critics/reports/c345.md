## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Four cues went stale when c344 moved the trio tune to the cellos (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** 165–193 and 181–185.
- **Problem:** c344 gave T's second statement (169–184) to the cellos, silenced Vln I from 153 to 192 and the violas from 169 to 184. The cue list was not updated. cuecheck.py finds:
  - Oboe 1, 181–184: the cue is "Vln. I", but Vln I is silent, so the part prints the label over an empty four-bar rest. Ob 1 has rested 52 bars (133–184) and enters alone, p espress., on the call at M (185).
  - Violin I and viola, 165–168: Cl 1 cue for an entry at 169 that no longer exists. Both now enter at 193 after 60 bars' rest; the cue stands 24 bars before the entry, and the entry itself has none. The c075 rule (a cue at every entry after 40 or more bars' rest) is broken twice.
  - Oboe 2, 193–196: a Vln I cue that leads to nothing; Ob 2's next entry is 273, already cued at 271.
  - Flute 2, 193–196: Vln I cue, then 8 more bars of rest before the espress. entry at 205.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest … Cues must be audible and obvious." A cue that prints nothing, or that sits 24 bars before the entry, is neither. MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7: extracted parts are not error-free by themselves; the score change did not proofread the parts. Ex. 4 places the cue in the bars just before the entry.
- **Fix (applied):** parts-cues.json: `"03"` 181 → `["cueVcHigh","Vc. (8va)","UP",181,4]`; `"26"` and `"28"` → `[["cueCA","C.a.","UP",189,4]]` (the C.a./cello answer dis'4 e'8 dis' cis' dis' | b2 fis4 …, the line just before 193; three ledger lines at most in Vln I, none in the alto clef); `"04"`: the 193 cue removed; `"02"`: 193 → `["cueFlOne","Fl. 1","UP",197,4]` (the desk partner's phrase, then 4 bars' rest). parts-gen.py: `\addQuote "cueVcHigh" { \transpose c c' \vc }` (at pitch the cellos' D♯3 would sit five ledger lines under the oboe stave). cuecheck.py now reports every cue sounding, and every gap from cue to entry is 0, except Fl 2 (4) and timp (see F3).

### 2. Tempo words at rehearsal letters print 3–7 mm above the letter (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** F 89 (a tempo), K 153 (Trio), P 241 (poco a poco accelerando), Q 253 (Tempo I), U 325 (Più mosso), V 341 (Meno mosso), in all 28 playing parts.
- **Problem:** The letter and the tempo word are two grobs, a RehearsalMark and a MetronomeMark, and the outside-staff stacking puts them at different heights. markpairs.py measured 168 letter/tempo pairs: only 12 have both on one baseline. In the rest the tempo word floats 10–22 pt above the box, often beginning over the previous rest (Ob 1 U and V; Fl 1 Q 22 pt; Cl 1 V 21 pt). This is the fault the coordinator relayed from movement II, and it is present here.
- **Evidence:** MOLA Ex. 4 (PDF 10) prints "[E] Tempo I" on one line, the box over the barline and the word to its right. MOLA PDF 8: in extracted parts "the placement of any 'anchored' markings may not remain positioned as they were … leading to collisions or floating markings", and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) shows exactly a drifted "a tempo" at box F.
- **Fix (applied):** parts-lib.ily, in `partGlobalOf`, a pass `merge-mark-tempo!`: where a `\tempo` follows a `\mark \default` with no duration between them, the tempo event is removed and its text and metronome figure are drawn inside the RehearsalMark's stencil, to the right of the box on the letter's baseline. A `self-alignment-X` callback keeps the box, not box plus text, centred on the barline, and the full width counts for spacing so the text cannot run into the margin. `outside-staff-priority` 1600 places these after the plain letters, so a letter that falls under a long tempo line (L and M under "Trio …" in Vln I) keeps its normal height and the tempo line clears it. parts-gen.py: `outside-staff-horizontal-padding = #1.5` for MetronomeMark and RehearsalMark, so a later tempo word that would end a word space after an earlier one is stacked rather than run on (harp: "a tempo (ritmo di tre battute)" then "ritmo di quattro battute" at 101).
  - Result: 168 of 168 pairs on one baseline; checked visually in Ob 1, C.a., Hn 1, Hn 3, timp, harp, Vln I, Vc, Cb.
  - The change re-spaces lines and the page-turn breaker then chose worse turns in three parts (Vln I 204–208, 5 bars, instead of the 60-bar rest 133–192; C.a. 9 bars instead of 24; Hn 3 10 instead of 13) and put Tpt 2 on two pages. Fixed in parts-gen.py: `TURNS = {"05": 288, "14": 288, "26": 152}` adds `{ \skip 2.*N \pageTurn }` to those parts, and `PAPX["17"]` uses optimal breaking (one page, no turn). The timpani keep their new turn at 236/237 (12 bars' rest 225–236; forcing 284 gave four pages). Ob 1's turn moved from 288/289 (9 bars) to 252/253 (44 bars, 229–272), Cl 1's from 8 to 12 bars, Cl 2's stays 15. Every part has the same page count as v345; every page has 12 systems or fewer (strings 26–28 and 30 at most 10).

### 3. The cellos enter at L (169) with the trio tune after the Trio's tempo change, uncued; the timpani's cue ends two bars early (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** Vc 152–169; timp 85–91.
- **Problem:** Since c344 the cellos carry T at 169, mp espress., alone in the melody. They stopped at 151, rested through the poco rit. (149–152), the key change and the new tempo (2. = 76), and come in after 16 bars of Cl 1's statement. c255 cued exactly this entry for the upper strings when they had it; the reason now applies to the cellos. In the timpani part, c327 moved the entry from 89 to 91 (answering the viola's head), so c255's Cl 1 cue at 85–88 now ends two bars before the entry, and the bars in between are the a tempo the player must count.
- **Evidence:** MOLA Ex. 4 (PDF 10): the cue comes after the tempo change and just before the entry. MOLA PDF 5: "audible and obvious". Adler PDF 300 item 7: choose the cued line by register.
- **Fix (applied):** parts-cues.json `"29": [["cueClOneLow","Cl. 1 (8vb)","UP",165,4]]` (gis b e'… two ledger lines in bass clef); `"23"` adds `["cueVla","Vla.","UP",89,2]` after the Cl 1 cue, so the timpanist sees the viola's head e'4 b8 b e'4 | g'2 fis'8 f' and answers it at 91.
