## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–54: the doubled 4th of the cadential 6/4 falls in octaves (applied)

**Problem.** The G-major hemiola cadence has its cadential 6/4 at 53 over D. The 4th, G, is held in Vla G4 and Cl 2 G4 (one line) and in Hn 2 G3, an octave lower. All three fall to F♯ at 54, so Hn 2 moves in parallel octaves with the viola/clarinet line. The V7 at 54 then doubles the leading tone F♯ of G at the octave, in a horn.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 316: "In free textures, composers will sometimes let an inner voice double a 4th that appears in the soprano … One of these 4ths will have to move up; if both resolved down, parallel octaves would result." Ex. 19-4 bar 7 shows the doubled 4th.
- The movement's other cadential 6/4s already follow this:
  - At 129 and 285, the same cadence in E minor, the 4th E4 is in Vla and Cl 2 only (one line, unison).
  - At 354–355 the violas take E4 up to F♯4 while Vln I falls.
  - At 335–336 the Vln I and flute rise while Cl 1 and Tpt 2 fall.
- Bar 53 is the only cadential 6/4 where a second, independent voice doubles the 4th and falls with it.

**Fix (sections/sA.ily, sAHnTwo, bars 53–55).**

```
g2.--( | fis2.-- | g2.--)   →   g2.--( | a2.-- | g2.--)
```

**Result.**
- Hn 2 makes an upper neighbour G3–A3–G3, the "4th that moves up".
- The 54 chord stays complete: D, F♯ (Vla, Cl 2), A, C (Cl 1). The leading tone is now in one line only.
- Hn 1·2 meet on A4/A3 at 54 by contrary motion (B4→A4 against G3→A3) and part again by contrary motion into 55 (C5 against G3).
- Rhythm, slurs, tenutos and dynamics are unchanged, so the hemiola cadence that c002, c012 and c163 protect keeps its rhythm, harmony and bowing. Only one inner horn note changes.
- Checked in the scratch build: the score prints A4/A3 on the Hn 1·2 staff at 54. No new outer-voice parallel, 13 pages, 0 warnings.

### 2. Bars 131–156: about 26 bars on the tonic before and into the trio (considered, kept)

**The case against it.** From the E-minor cadence at 131 to the trio's first change of harmony at 155, the bass is E except for V at 138 and the pizzicato B at 151. That is about 19 s with no harmonic motion. Unit 16 §2 (PDF 250) warns that harmony that does not move away cannot make its arrival a goal. The trio then starts on the same root.

**Why I keep it.**
- The span is a tonic prolonged by the means the reading lists:
  - V as a neighbour at 138 (U16 §9, PDF 256–257);
  - mixture G→G♯ in Hn 1 (141–148), which turns the minor tonic into the major one;
  - the oscillating 6/4 E–B in the pizzicato basses at 149–152 (U19 §15, PDF 320–321), which signals the dance tempo of the trio.
- The model does the same: Dvořák 9/iii's transition into its trio is about 34 bars of quiet approach (MCTL 600–703), longer than this link's 20 bars (133–152).
- c133, c153 and c183 kept these fades.
- A neighbour IV in the close (133–140) would anticipate the codetta's I–IV–I (225–236), which is the trio's own way of standing still. Keeping the scherzo close on i–V–i keeps the two closes distinct.
- No change.

### 3. Bars 57–89 and 289–301: development by ascending 5ths (considered, kept)

**The case against it.** G minor – D minor – A is a chain of rising 5ths, the motion Unit 16 §2 (PDF 250) calls "not strongly goal-directed".

**Why I keep it.**
- The reading's own exception applies. In minor, III–VII–IV–(I)–V is the useful segment, read as a rising step line III–IV–V with VII and I as upper 5ths (Ex. 16-2b, PDF 251; review point 2, PDF 260).
- Here the step line is G (III, 57) – A (IV, 65–72) – B (V, 89). D minor is the decorative upper 5th.
- In the first development ♭II (F, 73–88) takes the place of the "I in name only". The reprise drops it and goes A (297) – B7 (301), as in the Bach chorale.
- The goal V is reached through the stepwise bass A–A♯–B (84–89), the long-range passing motion of PDF 256.
- No change.

### 4. Bars 339–356: the coda's last tonic held back by IV (considered, kept)

**The case against it.** After the E-minor ff chord and G.P. (339–340), stage 2 starts in A major.

**Why I keep it.**
- By Unit 16 §4 (PDF 253, Ex. 16-5; review point 4, PDF 260), 339 becomes in hindsight the upper 5th of IV. E7 at 343 and 348 is V of A, and E becomes I only at 349. Its root position arrives at 356, after the cadential 6/4 is expanded over two bars (353–354; U19 §10, PDF 316).
- The movement's last tonic is therefore a goal reached through IV and an expanded cadence. It is not the twentieth bar of a long tonic.
- Proportion: coda 42 s against the reprise's 49 s, close to Dvořák 9/iii's coda (about 51 bars) against its 140-bar da capo.
- No change. 357–376 is protected.
