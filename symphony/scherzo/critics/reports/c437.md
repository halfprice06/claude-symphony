## Findings

### 1. 197-200: the trio's forte head is four instruments at one pitch; the violas take the lower octave (applied)

**Bars:** 197-200, violas (tB.ily, `tBVla`).

**Problem:** At the forte of the trio's middle section, T's head (G♯–A–B–E, D♯–F♯–A tied | A–G♯) sounds at one pitch, G♯4-E5, in Cl 1, Vln II, the violas and Hn 1 (mf). Nothing plays it below. Above it, the call (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I) is a level softer (c404). Vln II and the violas are two string sections of nearly the same colour on the same notes, so they add volume and lose the separate colour of each. The head therefore has less body than at 169-184, where the cellos and violas carry it alone at G♯3. The protected layering (call above, tune below, distinct rhythms: c117, c404) does not require the tune to stay in one octave.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 229: Schoenberg, "instead of doubling at pitch as Brahms did, uses octaves to provide a richer sound, for he does not have two distinct colors ...; doubling at pitch, the violins and cellos would swallow each other up." Vln II and the violas at G♯4 are that case.
- PDF 239 (op. 43 Var. IV): in the loud phrases "the flutes and clarinets, doubled in the lower octave by the second violin" exchange high and low tessitura. The tune gains its lower octave from a string section.
- PDF 213 (Bizet): the repeat is "a fuller version ... with the melody doubled". The 197 statement is the forte return of T's head after the soft statements at 153 and 169. Giving it a second octave makes it the fuller one without adding an instrument.
- Earlier passes (c179, c279) marked PDF 229 "follows" here by counting four colours. Cl 1 and Hn 1 are distinct colours, but Vln II and the violas are not, and those passes did not test a lower octave.

### 2. 95-100: the head and its background share colour and note values (checked, kept)

PDF 217 (*Petit Poucet*) asks for contrasting colours when the melody and accompaniment move in the same values. At 95-100, Vln I's head, Vln II's arpeggios and the violas' repeated B are all arco crotchets. Making the violas or Vln II pizzicato would leave them two beats to take up the bow for the f arco hemiola at 101. That breaks c086's five-beat rule for a pizz.→arco change. The head's quavers on beat 2 are the only quavers in the strings, and c237 already set Vln II in contrary motion at 99-100. Kept.

### 3. 153-168: signal the end of the clarinet's solo by doubling a middle part (Schoenberg, PDF 230) (considered, not applied)

Schoenberg leaves the middle parts undoubled until the oboes signal that the cello solo is ending. The analogous change would be an oboe pair on the harp's inner notes at 165-168. That adds a fourth colour to the plain first statement, which c090, c257 and c337 keep as the reference the later statements colour. The timpani's pp E at 168 and the harp's last off-beat already mark the seam, and the cellos' entry at 169 is fresh. Not applied.

### 4. 169-184: second statement (checked, kept)

c436 asked later critics to thin rather than add here. I checked the one at-pitch doubling (Hn 3 with the cellos at 169-176) against PDF 229. Horn and cello are distinct colours, so they do not swallow each other, and c431's reason (the horn call's sixth filled in a horn before the coda's Hn 3) still stands. Kept as is.
