## What else I studied

**Dvořák 4/iii.** The score is not in the local corpus. I used [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._4_(Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k)), the [Dvořák Society work page](https://www.antonin-dvorak.cz/en/work/symphony-no-4/) and the [IMSLP listing](https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.4,_Op.13_(Dvořák,_Antonín)), together with what I know of the piece:
- D-minor scherzo, C-major trio, 388 bars, ABA.
- The outer parts are built on one hard rhythmic figure.
- The trio is a march that "approaches from a distance and then recedes". It uses triangle, cymbals and bass drum and recalls the *Meistersinger* overture.
- The scherzo comes back da capo.

This movement does several things better than Dvořák's early scherzo:
- The reprise is varied: the shortened fugato and the Italian sixth at 300.
- The trio has the same distant-near-distant arch (pp 153 → ff 218 → pp 237) without military percussion. Cymbals and C major are kept for the finale.
- The coda brings T back instead of a plain da capo.

Dvořák's one advantage is the drive of a single feroce figure. Motive x and the timpani already supply that here, and Dan's soft-brass note rules out his brass bite. I would believe a master wrote this, with the open point noted below.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 253–268: Hn 3 doubles the cellos' countermelody for all 16 bars. Applied.

**Problem.** Since c447, Hn 3 ppp doubles the cellos' arco espressivo tenor line in unison throughout 253–268. The line has two phrases, 253–260 and 261–268. With the horn on both, the reprise has no point where the line is heard in the cellos' own colour. At 305 it returns in horn, cello and bassoon, so 253–305 adds only the bassoon, and the build from preview to tutti is mostly lost.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 140–141: "incessant duplication for an extended period of time should be avoided; it is only permissible in isolated phrases … Doubling, when limited to a few special phrases supports the voice and endows it with beauty and colour". Constant doubling "replaces by a mixed timbre the rare characteristic qualities" of the line.
- Ex. 289–290 (PDF 467–468): the cellos double the line "dolce (colla voce)" for one phrase, and the cor anglais takes the next "colla voce". Each phrase gets a new colour.
- Ex. 303 (PDF 491–492): Ob 1 + violins, then Cl 1 + violins.
- PDF 166 and Ex. 312: groups are added in turn toward the summit.

**Earlier reasons answered.**
- c447 (Rimsky PDF 113, horns doubled by cellos) is kept for the second phrase, so the horn colour is still heard before 305.
- c359's "cellos alone" preview is kept for the first phrase.
- The sequence becomes cellos alone (253), cellos + horn (261), cellos + Hn 3·4 + Bsn (305). The colour grows by one layer at each step.
