## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 104→105 and 304→305: the soprano seventh is left unresolved (a regression of c052). APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 104 the V7 has A5 on top (Fl 1, Cl 1, Vln I). At 105 every instrument at the top leaps down to the theme's E5, and nothing takes G5. c052 fixed this in v53 (Fl 1 G5 on the downbeat). c065 (v66) then put Fl 1 back on E5 so the flute staff would part-combine cleanly, and the fix was lost. The comment at sC.ily:16 and dC.ily:9 still says "Fl 1 G5".
**Evidence.** PDF 76: the seventh "forms a dissonance". Here it is the top voice and has no resolution. PDF 87–88, rules 5, 7 and 8 with Ex. 5-29b ("poor"): hidden octaves are most noticeable in the outer voices and with a leap in the soprano. Here the top leaps A5→E5 while the bass falls B1→E1 onto the octave, at the movement's main ff arrival.
**Fix.** Cl 1 is resting at 105. It takes G5 for one crotchet, so the seventh resolves by step in its own register and the flute staff stays as c065 left it:
```
sC.ily  sCClOne  bar 105:  \partCombineApart g''4-. r4 r4 | \partCombineSoloII R2.*15 |   (was \partCombineSoloII R2.*16 |)
        sCClTwo  bar 105:  \partCombineApart e'4-. b8-. b-. e'4-. | \partCombineSoloII ...      (directive moved one bar)
dC.ily  dCClOne / dCClTwo  bar 305: the same
```
I also updated both comments. Both clarinets are ff from 104, so the shared staff keeps one dynamic. Cl 1 and Bsn 1 now resolve A→G in octaves. That is the doubled tutti seventh resolving as one line, which is how c052 had it.

### 2. 67→68: parallel 12ths between the subject and the countersubject in the strings-only fugato. APPLIED.
**Problem.** Vln II plays the subject's C5 on beat 3 of 67 and then B4. The Vc's countersubject holds F3 and then moves to E3. C5/F3 → B4/E3 is a pair of perfect 12ths in similar motion. Only four strings play here (p, crescendo), so the fugato is a real-voice texture.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-19: parallel 5ths are "wrong". PDF 87, rule 1: the fewer the voices, the stronger the effect. The free-texture allowance (PDF 84) covers doublings only, not two independent fugal lines.
**Fix.** The cello anticipates its E on beat 3, so the subject falls C5→B4 over a held E3 (oblique motion):
```
sB.ily  sBVc  bars 67-68:  f2(\< e4~ | e2.)     (was f2.(\< | e2.))
```
I rejected two alternatives. D3 on beat 3 would make octaves by contrary motion with the Cb's D2→E1 (PDF 86). G3 would make a cross relation with the violas' G♯3 at 68.

### 3. 345–347: Ob 1's tune and the violas move in 5ths, then in octaves. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In the coda's soft A-major phrase, Ob 1 plays D5–E5 | F♯5–B5 | A5 over violas A3 | B3 | A3. That gives E5/A3 → F♯5/B3 (compound 5ths) and then B5/B3 → A5/A3 (compound octaves), across two bar lines in a row. Only the oboe, Bsn 1, the harp and the violas play (ppp), so both pairs are exposed. The same tune is voiced correctly at 355, where the violas take A3/F♯4 under the D♯.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18 and 5-19. PDF 80: inner voices "complete the tones of the chord".
**Fix.** The violas take other chord tones: D4 (the 3rd of ii) and then B3 held:
```
coda.ily  codaVla  bars 345-348:  a2.\ppp | d'2. | b2. | b2.     (was a2.\ppp | b2. | a2. | gis2.)
```
Against the oboe this gives P5 → M10 → m7 → P5 (by oblique motion). The violas hold the root of B7 at 347. At 348 they sit under the oboe's D♯5 appoggiatura without forming 5ths. The new D4 at 346 is reached when Bsn 1 falls to D3 (oblique motion), and the B3 at 348 is in unison with Bsn 1 on beat 3.

### 4. 182 and 222: the leading tone is doubled at the trio tune's 4–3 cadence, and the viola moves E–D♯ in octaves with the tune. 211: the same E–D♯ in Bsn 1. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 181→182 (second statement) and 221→222 (forte return), the tune resolves its suspension E→D♯ (Vln I/II, Cl 1, Fl 1). At the same moment the divided violas move from E4 to <A3 D♯4>. The leading tone of V7 is then doubled between independent voices, and E→D♯ sounds in octaves. The coda statement (355) has the violas on <A3 F♯4>, so the score is already inconsistent with itself. At 210→211, Bsn 1 holds E4 under the tune's arrival on E5, then both fall to D♯ (octaves).
**Evidence.** PDF 76 (Ex. 5-4): the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V". PDF 83: parallel octaves.
**Fix.**
```
tA.ily  tAVla     bar 182:  r4 <a fis'>4--( <a fis'>4--)    (was <a dis'>; second of the two <a dis'> bars)
tC.ily  tCVla     bar 222:  r4 <a fis'>4--( <a fis'>4--)    (was <a dis'>; second of the two)
tC.ily  tCBsnOne  bar 211:  fis'2.--                        (was dis'2.--: E–F♯–E, an upper neighbour, unison with Hn 2)
```
The viola goes E4→F♯4 against the tune's E→D♯ (contrary motion), so V7 is complete with one D♯. I did not change the first statement (166, harp only, c089's do-not-touch). Its harp D♯4 is a chord filler (PDF 85).

### 5. 194→195: parallel 5ths between Cl 1 and Cl 2. APPLIED.
**Problem.** Cl 1 C♯5→B4 over Cl 2 F♯4→E4 (ii → I6). Vln II doubles Cl 1 from beat 3 of 194, so the 5ths also sound C♯5/F♯4 → B4/E4. This is the soft middle of the trio (p<), where the clarinets are the inner voices.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-19. PDF 80: inner voices move mostly by step.
**Fix.** Cl 2 anticipates its E on beat 3 of 194 (a passing seventh of ii that becomes the root of I6):
```
tB.ily  tBClTwo  bars 193-195:  fis'2.\p\< | fis'2 e'4~ | e'2.     (was fis'2.\p\< | fis'2. | e'2.)
```
Cl 2 still carries the only E at 195 and the only D♯ at 196.

### 6. 215→216: parallel 5ths between the tune and Hn 2 at the half cadence. APPLIED.
**Problem.** The tune falls C♯5→A♯4 (an appoggiatura to B). Hn 2 falls F♯4→D♯4, so the two lines move F♯/C♯ → D♯/A♯. At 216, D♯ is also in Bsn 1, the violas, the Vc and Bsn 2.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-19. PDF 76: doubling should follow the voice leading.
**Fix.** Hn 2 keeps its F♯ as a common tone:
```
tC.ily  tCHnTwo  bar 216:  fis'2.--) \breathe     (was dis'2.--) \breathe)
```
Hn 1·2 now sound A♯4/F♯4 (M3) and then B4/F♯4. Bar 217 continues F♯4→E4 by step. c086 protected the trio horn pair for its register, its softness and the breath at 216. All three stay as they were, and one note becomes a held note.

### 7. 199–201: Bsn 1 doubles the leading tone B♯ and then leaps a minor 9th. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 200 (V of C♯ minor, with c082's 9–8), B♯ is in Cl 2 (B♯3) and Bsn 1 (B♯2). Bsn 1 reaches B♯2 by a falling diminished 5th from F♯3, then jumps to C♯4 at 201, a minor 9th.
**Evidence.** PDF 76 (Ex. 5-4): no doubled leading tone. PDF 81: "Leaps larger than an octave are not permitted". PDF 82: dissonant leaps are excluded.
**Fix.**
```
tB.ily  tBBsnOne  bar 200:  dis'2.\>     (was bis,2.\>)
```
F♯3→D♯4 is a rising major 6th that turns back by step to C♯4 (Ex. 5-11b, "good"). B♯ is now single (Cl 2), and the 9–8 is unchanged. One point is not changed: Cl 2's B♯3→E4 across the phrase seam at 200–201 is still a diminished 4th. It sits under the diminuendo and the new phrase, and C♯ is present at 201.
