## Findings

### 1. Bars 86–87: the exposition's structural Neapolitan has no ♭2̂ that falls. Applied.

**Problem.** Bars 85–86 are the only place where the long ♭II (73–86) turns to the home dominant: F/A → D♯°7/A (87) → A♯°7 (88) → i6/4 over B (89). At 86 the ♭2̂ F sounds in Hn 4 F3 and the harp's F3. At 87 the harp is damped (c116) and Hn 4 rises F3→F♯3. Cl 1 touched F4 only as a passing quaver in 85. So the chord whose identity depends on ♭2̂ leaves it upward only. The d3 ♭2̂–♯7̂ that the unit treats as this chord's sound is missing. c180 had put it there: Cl 2 F3 at 86 → D♯3 at 87. c342 removed the F3 when it rebuilt Cl 2's 87–88. c480's table still listed "86–87 Cl 2 F3–D♯3" as present, and that entry was wrong.

**Evidence.** PDF 483–484: "The main point to remember is that ♭2̂ tends to move down". Going directly to a dominant-function chord, it "skips down to the leading tone; the melodic line will contain a diminished 3rd" (Ex. 28-2a, b). A d3 "often makes for a peculiarly intense melodic line". Review point 3 (PDF 499) says the same, and point 6 names ♭II6 → VII°4/3 as the non-cadential case, which is 86→87 here. PDF 484 (Ex. 28-3d) and PDF 488 (Ex. 28-12a) allow ♭2̂→♮2̂ in an inner voice only when ♭2̂ is doubled and the other copy goes on: in 28-12a one D♭ rises and the bass D♭ falls by d3. As things stand, Hn 4's rise is the only continuation.

**Answer to c342.** c342 had three objections to c180's line F3 | D♯3 | C♯3. The second and third were the doubled leading tone under Cl 1's D♯4 at 87, which then fell to C♯3, and the doubled C♯ at 88. Both stay fixed, because I keep c342's 87–88 exactly: D♯3 on beats 1–2, F♯3, then G3. The first objection was that a d3 has no place in Unit 31's chain of °7s, where every voice rises by semitone. That chain runs 87→88, and its semitone rises are untouched. The F3→D♯3 step happens one bar earlier, at the ♭II6 → VII°4/3 junction, which Unit 28 governs. There the d3 is the normal motion. The route 85–89 protected by c422 keeps every chord.

**Fix (applied, sections/sB.ily, `sBClTwo`, bar 86).**
```
old: … R2. | R2. | dis2(\pp fis4 | g2.) | …          (85, 86, 87, 88)
new: … R2. | f2. -\tag #'score -\tweak stencil ##f \p -\tag #'part \p -\tag #'part \> | dis2(\pp fis4 | g2.) | …
```
Cl 2 doubles F3 at p, under Cl 1's p>, and both reach pp at 87. On the shared staff Cl 1's printed p> serves both players (c045), so Cl 2's p is hidden in the score and printed in the part with its hairpin. Simultaneous dynamics are equal, and the MIDI plays Cl 2 at p. A slur over 86–87 made page 3 over-full by 0.7 staff spaces, so the slur still starts at 87 as before and the F3 is tongued.

**Checks.** There are no parallels with the held A bass, Cl 1 (B♭4 → A4) or Hn 4 (F→F♯ against F→D♯, contrary). F3 is in unison with Hn 4 and the harp at pp. Cl 2's rest before the entry is 73–85, one bar shorter than before, and it never had a cue there. parts-cues.json has no entry for part 07 near 86 and none that points at Cl 2, so nothing needs changing.

**Why a listener hears it.** The texture at 86 is thin: Cl 1 solo, horns pp, bass clarinet A2. The A clarinet's chalumeau F3 falling to D♯3 is the darkest line in it and enters as the F-major colour turns to E minor's dominant. It is the exposition's one structural ♭II6 → V, and the movement's long-range Neapolitan is heard to resolve at this point.

### 3. Trio 202: iv 5–6 → ♭II6 of E before V. Considered, not applied.

Ex. 28-7 (IV 5–6 → ♭II6) and PDF 488–490 (♭II in major through mixture) fit 202 exactly: A minor, then E4→F4 in Hn 3 and Cl 2, then F→D♯ at 203. I rejected it on this unit's own evidence. The end of Ex. 28-5 (PDF 485) shows the parallel-major trio featuring ♮2̂, and c380's rule of no F♮ in 153–240 rests on it. A ♭II of E three bars after 199's ♭II of C♯ would also blunt c480's change (c442 used the same reasoning against a second V♭9).

### 4. Bars 85–86: ♭2̂ as the soprano tone. Considered, not applied.

PDF 483 puts ♭2̂ on top for intensity. Cl 1's 85–86 (A4–F4–F4–A4 | C5–B4–B♭4) carries the motto tail on A (C–B–B♭–A), the movement's own cyclic figure. Moving F to the top would break that figure or create the augmented second D♯5–C5 that c089 removed. With Finding 1 in place, the d3 is heard in Cl 2, which was c480's condition for leaving the top alone.

### 5. Bars 73–80: a lower register for the ♭II melody (Ex. 28-16). Considered, not applied.

Chopin heightens the tonicized ♭II with a sudden low melody. The harp's head on F at A5–C6 is protected (c001, c300). An octave lower it would sit on Hn 3's D4–C4 and Bsn 1's D4. The drop the example describes already happens at 81, when the melody passes to Cl 1 (D5 down to D♯4).

### 6. ♭II without its own dominant. Keep, recorded for the finale.

PDF 491 names ♭II's own dominant as the usual means of tonicization. In E minor that dominant is C major, which the cyclic rule excludes. The scherzo therefore reaches F from A major by a common tone (72–73), uses vii°7 of F at 79 (c042), and has only one beat of C–E–B♭ without G (300). The finale is the one that tonicizes its ♭II (D♭) from its full dominant, A♭7 at 205–210. The difference is right for the arc and should stay.

### 7. ♭VI–♭II at a major-mode ending (Ex. 28-14, 28-20). Kept for the finale.

The dark ♭II neighbour inside a final major tonic belongs to the finale's coda (IV 227–276). The scherzo's E-major close uses ♭6̂ (cor anglais C♯–C–B, 358–359) and the plagal A♯°7–iv (357–360), which are protected.
