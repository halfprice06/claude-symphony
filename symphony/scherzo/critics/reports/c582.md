## Findings

### 1. 133–136: the scherzo's closing wind chord moves i – iv6/4 – vii°7 – i over the tonic pedal. APPLIED.

- **Bars.** 133–136 (Cl 1, Cl 2, cor anglais; sC.ily).
- **Problem.** The reading's own use of vii°7 over a tonic pedal (row 11) does not occur anywhere in the movement. The scherzo part ends with four bars of one unchanged E-minor wind chord (133–136) under the cellos' and timpani's motive x. This is the only exposed soft wind chord in the scherzo, and the ear sits on it. The link then gives the major-mode neighbour figure: Bsn 2 B3–C♯4–B3 over E major at 145–147 (c412), and Hn 1 G♯–A–G♯. That figure has no minor-mode antecedent, so the change of mode at 145 is heard as a new event rather than as a varied repetition.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 383, §10 and Ex. 22-10: "VII°7 often appears over a tonic pedal, usually taking the place of V7 in the progression I–IV–VII°7(=V7)–I."
  - PDF 381, §7: "harmonizing 6 in the soprano progression 5–6–5 is a particularly characteristic and important function of the diminished seventh chord." Coming from IV, its 7th is "prepared as a common tone" (Ex. 22-7a).
  - PDF 391–392 and 395, point 3: ♭6 is an upper neighbour to 5 and moves on to 5.
  - PDF 392, §5: mode contrast is "particularly vivid when the change of mode varies what otherwise would be a repetition". After the change, 133–136 (5–♭6–5, B–C–B) and 145–147 (5–♮6–5, B–C♯–B) are that pair.
  - PDF 375: the d7 C5/D♯4 resolves inward to a P5 (B4/E4), and the d5 D♯4/A4 resolves inward to a 3rd (E4/G4).
- **Voice leading (scratch build).** Cl 1 B4–C5–C5–B4, C.a. G4–A4–A4–G4, Cl 2 E4–E4–D♯4–E4, Bsn 1 E3 held. The basses (E1, 133 and 135) and the timpani (E, 135) keep the pedal. 133→134 moves in parallel 3rds. 135→136: the 3rds stay parallel, the d7 goes to a P5 and the d5 to a m3, with no 5ths or octaves. The top line is the only outer voice that moves, against a fixed bass, so the analyser reports nothing new.
- **Rules.** No G sounds at 134–135, so there is no C-major triad (c413's "no 6 over E minor" concerns E–G–C; here the G moves to A). There is no B–D–F–G♯. The ♭6 reaches 5 at 136 before the next C♯ at 146 (c492's do-not-touch). The cellos' i–V–i echo at 137–140 and the cellos' and basses' dynamics are unchanged.
- **Answer to c223.** c223 kept the close on i–V–i because "a neighbour IV in the close would anticipate the codetta's I–IV–I (225–236)". The change does not repeat the codetta's progression. The close is minor, and its goal is the °7 (i–iv–vii°7–i). The codetta is major (I–IV–I twice), and it borrows iv only at its last turn (235). The codetta's minor iv at 235 now recalls this close across the trio, which is the long-range kind of mixture the reading describes on PDF 387. The pairing the reading cares about (row 24) is the one with 145–147, and that pairing is new.
- **Relation to the protected close 357–360.** The movement's last plagal close (E – A♯°7 – iv – E, motto tail in Hn 1) is untouched. It uses a different °7 (the common-tone A♯°7) and the reverse order (°7 before iv). The scherzo part now ends with the same family of tonic-pedal neighbour chords that the whole movement ends with, but in minor. I judged that a rhyme across the form, not a pre-emption: the motto tail, the major mode and the horn are reserved for 357–360.

### 2. 321–323, the reprise close: kept plain. Not applied.

Repeating the change at 321–323 would put the vii°7 at 323. It would then move to the cellos' V at 324 instead of to i, against row 1 (PDF 374: the °7's dissonances resolve into the tonic). Bar 324 is the upbeat to the coda's ostinato, so the reprise close stays i–V. The difference between the two closes also marks the second one as the turn into the coda.

### 3. 237–240 → 241: no ♭6 neighbour before the turn to minor. Not applied.

A C♮ neighbour over the held E-major chord at 239 would prepare the minor at 241 (PDF 387). But the direct G♯→G at 241 mirrors the opening 1–7 and the link 144→145, and the reading itself credits the direct change of 3 with "startling power" (PDF 391, §4). A prepared change would weaken all three. The F♮ question here was settled by c293.

### 4. 41–43 and 273–275: no vii°7 of G over the D pedal (Ex. 22-9, mixture §6). Not applied.

An E♭ (♭6 of G) would turn D7 into V♭9 of G. The tune's B5 on beat 3 of 42/274 would then clash with the E♭ (a diminished 4th, spelled against B♮). The tune's own C–B–B♭–A tail already carries the chromatic colour of these bars.

### 5. 22, 114 and 262: the G♯ bass (V6 of iv, ♯3 by mixture) stays a triad. Checked.

The vii°7 of iv here would be G♯–B–D–F, the finale's reserved stop chord (BRIEF.md: in any spelling or role). The bass G♯ under E5–D♯5–E5 is a clean applied I6 and follows PDF 391.

### 6. 101–104 and 301–304: plain V7, no ♭9 (Ex. 22-9). Kept, as c442 decided.

The reading allows a long °7 over V. The movement saves its ♭9 for 111/311, 126/282 and 337, and for the neighbour at 100 (c532). A ♭9 in the four-bar brass build would take the edge off the 126 and 337 sonorities. No new reason here answers c442.

### 7. Diminished-seventh census and ♭6 order: unchanged and correct. Checked.

The complete °7s are the C♯ family (79, 88, 128, 284, 358) and the D♯ family (87, 126, 200, 282, 337); the B–D–F–G♯ family never occurs. Every ♭6 in the major sections (202, 220, 235, 359) and the new one in minor (134–135) falls to 5 inside its own voice. No ♭6 is followed by ♮6 before 5 arrives.
