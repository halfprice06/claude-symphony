## Findings

### 1. Bar 49: the dotted crotchet is narrower than the quaver after it (applied)

Bars: 49, seven staves (Fl 1, Ob 1, C.a., Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc), the second idea's `e'''4. d'''8 cis'''4~` and its lower octaves.

Problem: the dotted crotchet E gets 3.66 spaces and the quaver D after it gets 4.05. The sharp on the following C♯, in all seven staves, widens the quaver's space. This is the only place in the score where a note in its own voice gets less space than a shorter neighbour. It falls on the forte statement of the second idea, where the dotted figure is the thing to read.

Evidence: Gould PDF 54: "Each longer value ... should have more space than its shorter neighbour (a), or at least as much (b)"; the "not acceptable" example shows exactly this. PDF 57: "note-spacing should remain unaffected by the addition of accidentals".

Fix (sA.ily, sAVlnOne, bar 49): give the quaver's column a wider left extent in one staff so that the dotted crotchet takes the space:
```
e'''4.->(\< \once \override NoteHead.extra-spacing-width = #'(-2.0 . 0) d'''8 cis'''4~
```
Result: dotted crotchet 4.47 spaces, quaver 4.04 (case (a)). Other dotted crotchets in that system get 3.62–3.64. No other bar on the system moves noticeably.

### 2. Bar 199: Hn 2's beat 2 stands almost on top of the off-beat quaver (applied, improved)

Bars: 199 (the Neapolitan bar of the forte call, c480–c482). Hn 2 has `fis'4 d'2` with a natural on the D (beat 2). Cl 1, Vln II and the violas have `d''4. fis'8` (the F♯ on beat 2½).

Problem: the natural and the dot of Hn 1's dotted minim push the horn's beat-2 column to the right. It ends up 1.04 spaces before the beat-2½ column, against a system median of 1.82 for a quaver step. In the score the horn's beat 2 lines up with the clarinets' and violins' off-beat quaver, so the eye reads two different beats as one.

Evidence: Gould PDF 54: "Notes of equal duration require equal spacing for an entire system"; PDF 57: accidentals should not distort the rhythmic spacing, and "two or more accidentals usually require extra space".

Fix (tB.ily, tBVlnTwo, bar 199):
```
d''4. \once \override NoteHead.extra-spacing-width = #'(-2.6 . 0) fis'8 a'4\<~
```
Result: beat 1→2 3.81, beat 2→2½ 1.48, beat 2½→3 2.37 spaces (was 3.81 / 1.04 / 2.37). I also tested Gould's other remedy (PDF 57: less space around the accidental, `Accidental.extra-spacing-width = #'(0.5 . 0)` and `#'(0.7 . 0)` on Hn 2's D). It moved beat 2 left by up to 0.7 space but left the 2→2½ step at 1.04–1.29. The column is held by Hn 1's dot, not by the natural, so I left that override out. A full 1.8 would need beat 1→2 at over 4 spaces, which distorts the crotchet instead.

### 3. Shared staves, unisons and seconds: checked, no change

### 5. The audible candidate: checked, not built

This reading is about how the page looks, not about the sound. The one rule in it that changes what players do is PDF 60: "the bow can sustain only two notes at once", so a chord of three or four notes is struck and only two notes ring. I looked for a string chord of three or more notes that the score asks to be held: there is none. The held chords at 72 and 300 are double stops, and the four-note chords at 339 are struck crotchets. The best candidate was to divide the 339 chords. I did not build it: it would undo c186's "non div.", which wants every player to sound every note of the fff stroke (Adler PDF 30–32), and dividing a struck crotchet would thin the loudest chord of the movement without making any note sustain longer. So no sound change is supported. Both applied findings help the reading: a dotted rhythm that reads true in seven staves at 49, and beats that line up correctly at 199.
