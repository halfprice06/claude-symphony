## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Applied: the woodwinds attack bar 301 *sfp* with the strings (MOLA Ex. 6, PDF 11; PDF 8)
- **Bars:** 301, letter S, the Neapolitan sixth after the Italian sixth of 300 (c473), where the hemiola crescendo to the reprise tutti begins.
- **Problem:** all five string parts attack 301 *sfp* (c008, kept by c108). Every woodwind attacks the same downbeat marked only *p*. Fl 1, cor anglais and Cl 1 play Vln I's line in unison or at the octave (A–C–F | D♯–B–A) with `->\p`. Fl 2, the oboes, Cl 2, the bassoons and the contrabassoon hold the chord `--\p`. The players on the line therefore read two different attacks. The strings start with a sforzando at the weight of the ff bar before, and the winds start with a soft accent at *p*. The result is a string accent with the winds under it, not one tutti attack, and the crescendo starts from two different levels.
- **Evidence:** in MOLA's Ex. 6 (PDF 11, Beethoven 9/iv, bars 852–854) the editors put "[sf]" in every wind part that attacks with the strings' *sf*, so that every player on the tutti figure reads the same mark. PDF 8 asks for the vertical line to be proofread as carefully as the horizontal, which is how this mismatch is found. My entries-after-rest scan and the c495 vertical scan both miss it, because each wind part does have a dynamic. The fault is only visible when the column is read across the staves.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-585/work/sections/dB.ily):** at 301, `\p` becomes `\sfp` in Fl 1, Fl 2, Ob 1, Ob 2, cor anglais, Cl 1, Cl 2, Bsn 1, Bsn 2 and the contrabassoon. On Fl 1, cor anglais and Cl 1 the now-redundant `->` on that note goes, to match Vln I's `a''2\sfp\<`. For example, Fl 1 `a''2->\p\< c'''4->~` becomes `a''2\sfp\< c'''4->~`, and Fl 2 `a''2.--(\p\<` becomes `a''2.--(\sfp\<`. Every shared wind staff now has equal simultaneous dynamics. The horns stay *p* (Hn 1·2, Hn 3·4), because Dan's soft-brass rule (c123) forbids a sforzando on a brass entry, and that rule is a stronger reason than Ex. 6's uniformity. The strings' *sfp* (c108 do-not-touch) is unchanged. A listener hears 301 as one orchestral *sfp* attack, minus the horns, that the hemiola then builds from to ff at 304. That is the Beethoven gesture the example prints.
- **Why this is the change the reading supports most:** MOLA has no rule about notes. Ex. 6's bracketed marks are its only principle that changes the sound, and bar 301 is the only tutti attack in the movement where the strings and the woodwinds on the same line carry different attack marks. I checked the theme-A tuttis at 105, 305 and 333, the second idea at 45 and 121, and 101–104. At 45 and 121 the winds sit a level under the string tune, which c405 settled deliberately as a balance between different lines. That is a different case from an attack on the same line.
