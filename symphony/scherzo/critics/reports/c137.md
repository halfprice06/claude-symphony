## Findings

### 1. Trio build 193-196: the call disappears under held chords (applied)

**Bars:** 193-196, Vla.

**Problem:** The trio call is a quaver turn. It goes oboe (185-188) to cor anglais and cellos (189-192), then returns in tutti at 197. In the four bars of crescendo between them (193-196), every voice under the violins' octave line holds dotted minims, so the figure drops out for four bars just before its biggest statement. The crescendo is only dynamic: no line moves inside the held harmony, and nothing points ahead to the tutti.

**Evidence:**

- Adler (PDF 229-230) describes how Schoenberg's Brahms op. 25 "emphasizes" a theme heard in fragments by switching choirs at each fragment, "two measures, one measure, two measures and a beat". The trio already has the longer fragments; the short ones that would compress the chain into the tutti are missing.
- On PDF 230-231 a countertheme is built "from the pitches of the bar" but in its own rhythm, and on PDF 201-202 a crescendo is orchestrated by adding activity, not only by a hairpin (Ex. XVI-4, the Beethoven 6 horns on "p cresc.").
- Bizet's La Toupie (PDF 213) adds motion inside a held chord for the same reason.

**Fix (applied, sections/tB.ily, tBVla):**

```
% old  193-196
a2.\p\<^\markup \italic "unis." | a2. | gis2. | b2. |
% new
a4(\p\<^\markup \italic "unis." b8 a gis a) | a2. | b4( cis'8 b ais b) | b2. |
```

The violas now play the turn on A at 193 and on B at 195. That gives Ob 4, CA+Vc 4, Vla 1 (with a bar's rest), Vla 1, then tutti, the shortening chain Adler describes.

- The written pitches are the chord tones the violas held before plus neighbour notes, so the harmony is unchanged.
- The G♯ of the cadential 6/4 at 195 is still in Bsn 1 and the violins, and no E is doubled (the c102 condition).
- The c061 7/6-5 suspension is in other voices and is unaffected.
- The call is not in the theme record, and themecheck reports 41/41 unchanged.

### 2. Violas double the trio cello countermelody at pitch, 177/179 and 217/219 (applied)

**Bars:** 177, 179 (tA) and 217, 219 (tC), Vla.

**Problem:** At these bars the cellos carry the sustained countermelody under the trio tune (G♯3 to A3). The violas' louré off-beat chords put G♯3 at 177/217 and A3 at 179/219, the same pitches in a neighbouring string colour. At p the viola attack on beats 2 and 3 masks the cello line, so the cello line sounds like a bass to the chords instead of a counterpoint.

**Evidence:**

- Adler (PDF 229) says Schoenberg uses octaves rather than at-pitch doubling where two similar colours would "swallow each other up".
- PDF 209 (Dvořák SD 8) asks for a light accompaniment with the strings' sustained "harmonic counterpoint" kept clear of it. That only works when the counterpoint line has its own pitch space.

**Fix (applied, sections/tA.ily tAVla and sections/tC.ily tCVla):**

```
% tA 177-179 old
r4 <gis e'>4--( <gis e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a cis'>4--( <a cis'>4--)
% tA 177-179 new
r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <cis' e'>4--( <cis' e'>4--)
% tC 217-219 old
r4 <gis e'>4--(\< <gis e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <a e'>4--(\f\> <a e'>4--)
% tC 217-219 new
r4 <b e'>4--(\< <b e'>4--) | r4 <b e'>4--( <b e'>4--) | r4 <cis' e'>4--(\f\> <cis' e'>4--)
```

- At 177/217 the chord becomes C♯m7 with B as a held common tone, which is prepared and resolves as before.
- At 179/219 it becomes F♯m7 with E held over, again a prepared seventh.
- The violas move by step or not at all, and the outer voices are unchanged.
- I checked the inner parallels by hand and found none new. The build analysis also still shows the same 7 outer-voice parallels (69, 326-328).
- The Bsn 1/2 A3 at 219, set by c129, stays.
