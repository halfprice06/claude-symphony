# Critic 086: v86, lens 6 (playability), reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 2 Wind Instruments (sebesky PDF 21–110)

I read every text page of sebesky PDF 21–110 in full and rendered every page of the range as an image (pairs of pages, 80 dpi; the range charts on PDF 21, 27, 29, 32, 39, 60, 64, 65, 70 and 72 again at 150 dpi) and looked at each. PDF = printed page + 9. The chapter is written for the recording studio and the big band; I took from it what concerns the instruments themselves (ranges, registers, breath, articulation, horn behaviour, blend and balance between brass and woodwinds) and left aside what is specific to saxophones, jazz phrasing and multitrack recording. Bar numbers are v86 numbers.

To check the score I built one MIDI file per player from the v86 sources (scratch file /tmp/critic-086/m/per.ly), listed each player's range, the notes at the extremes of each instrument's register, the unisons and seconds inside each pair, and the longest stretches with no rest of a crotchet or more.

## Also studied

Besides the reading I used no other source; the checks were made with per-player MIDI files built from the v86 sources. Two points from the table I checked and decided not to change. Hn 1 holds concert B4–C♯5 (written F♯5–G♯5) at p and mf through 209–224, breathing once at 216. Written G♯5 is a semitone above Sebesky's normal top (PDF 32), but the line is soft, slow, gets 16 bars of rest before it and has a breath in the middle, and the register is normal for a principal horn in late-Romantic writing. Lowering it would put Hn 1 under Hn 2 or into unison with it, and the tC comment rules out horn unisons there. Hn 1's C♯5–C5 at 72–73 (ff dim. to a pp written G5) is c042's chromatic line in one voice, so it stays. I also checked the tuba's E1. It is at the bottom of Sebesky's BB♭ chart (PDF 39), but it occurs only on single accented crotchets at f. That is ordinary for an orchestral F or CC tuba with a fourth or fifth valve, so I kept it.

## Findings

### 1. 44 and 276: the oboe's falling figure ends in its coarsest notes (applied)

**Problem.** At 44 Ob 1 plays A4–F♯4–D4 at f. On beat 3, D4 is the only melodic note: Vln I and Fl 1 rest there (c016, c076), so the D4 stands alone. At 276 the same figure, a third lower, ends on B3. That is a semitone above the oboe's lowest note.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 71 (Ex. 87): the lowest part of the oboe's range, "F down to B♭", is coarse and its notes are "the most difficult to control … it would be wise to avoid writing them". A short staccato B3 is the hardest of these. PDF 72: the English horn "does not become coarse or nasal in the bottom of its range, which makes it extremely useful as the lowest voice … in place of an oboe". The cor anglais is free at both places: it rests through 13–56 and from 272 to 288.

**Fix (applied).** Ob 1 keeps the first crotchet in unison with Ob 2 (c035's two-stem notation is unchanged), and the cor anglais plays the whole figure:
- sA.ily: `\partCombineApart a'4-. r4 r4 | \partCombineAutomatic` for Ob 1 at 44, and a new `sACorAnglais = { R2.*31 | a'4-.\f fis'-. d'-. | R2.*12 | }`.
- dA.ily: Ob 1 at 276 `\transpose d b, { \partCombineApart a'4-. r4 r4 \partCombineAutomatic }`; dACorAnglais bar 276 `fis'4-.\f dis'-. b-. |`.

The notes and register are unchanged; only the instrument changes. This replaces c076's "Ob 1 keeps its D4": the note is kept, on the instrument that can play it cleanly. Ob 1's lowest note is now F♯4.

### 2. 101–120 and 297–320: Bassoon 1 has no breath in the tutti tenor line (applied)

**Problem.** Bsn 1 plays 101–104 (p< ff) and then the tenor countermelody 105–120 at ff, with no rest for 20 bars (13 s). In the reprise it plays 297–320 at f–ff without a rest for 24 bars (16 s). Hn 4 plays the same line from 105 and was given a breath at 116 and 316 (c046). Bsn 1, its partner, has a slur from 113 straight through to 120 and no breath.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 24: a slur means the notes are played connected, "usually in one breath". PDF 70: an instrument with a large bore that needs much air must be allowed "as many breathing spaces as possible". The same rule is used for Hn 4 (c046) and Bsn 2 (c066).

**Fix (applied).** The slur is split and a breath added where Hn 4 breathes. In the reprise, Bsn 1 ends 300 on beat 2, as it already does at 72, and breathes before the sub. p at 301:
- sC.ily: `... | b2 a4) \breathe | e'2.~( | e'2 g4 | a2 fis4 | g4) r4 r4 |`
- dC.ily: the same at 316.
- dB.ily: bar 300 `a2\ff r4` (was `a2.\ff`). Bsn 2 still holds A2, so the bass is unchanged.

The longest stretch without a breath falls from 24 bars to 16.

### 3. 333–335: the low brass are louder than the horns' chord and the trumpets' motive x (applied)

**Problem.** At the start of the coda's peak, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn and Tuba hold an E-minor chord at ff. The horns hold their four-part chord at ff, and the trumpets play motive x at f. Every other tutti in the movement puts the horns a degree above the other brass (105, 305: horns ff, trumpets and trombones f, c076). Only here are the low brass equal to the horns and a degree above the trumpets.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 32 note 1 and PDF 46: a trombone at mf or louder has about twice the power of a horn, so horns must be given more weight to balance. Sebesky PDF 53: "the intensity of the lower parts should not exceed that of the higher ones". At ff, a close trombone chord G3/E3 under horns at B4/G4/E4/B3 covers the horns' lower notes. It also covers the trumpets' f motive x, the motive the coda is built on.

**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, 333: `g2.\f` (Tbn 1), `e2.\f` (Tbn 2; the shared staff keeps equal dynamics), `e,2.\f` (B.tbn), `e,,2.\f` (Tuba). The low brass still rest at 336–338 and enter fff at 339 (c034), so their entry remains the loudest point of the coda. Nothing else changes.
