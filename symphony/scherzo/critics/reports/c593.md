## Findings

### 1. 201–203: a Phrygian iv6 at 202 (Ex. 18-12). BUILT, TESTED, NOT APPLIED

**Why this candidate.** Unit 18's most audible device is the stepwise bass under 6/3 chords. The only place in a key where it is allowed (no E–G–C), with the bass free and the passage not a protected theme statement, is the trio's mixture bar 202. Ex. 18-12 (PDF 299) and §6 (PDF 298) give the progression: iv6 over ♭6, then V by the half step ♭6–5 in the bass. Here that is A (IV, 201) – C (iv6, 202) – B (V, 203). It is the only Unit 18 change I found that a listener would clearly hear.

**What I built** (sections/tB.ily, bar 202 only):
- Bass C: Vc `a,2.` → `c2.`, Cb `a,,2.` → `c,2.`, Bsn 2 `a,2.` → `c2.`.
- The upper voices leave C, so that they do not move C→B in octaves with the bass (§3, PDF 293): Bsn 1 `c'2.` → `a2.`, Vla `c''2. | b'2.` → `e''2. | dis''2.`, Vln I `c''4--` → `a'4--`.

**Result.** The build is clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, "C-major triads: none", "part lengths: all match". par2.py shows no new parallels. The outer voices move A4/A1 – E5/C2 – D♯5/B1.

**Why it is worse, and reverted:**
- **It takes away the reason for the bar.** c070's ♭6–5 is the top voice's C5→B4 (Vln I, Vla, Bsn 1's C♯4–C4–B3). It is how the listener hears A major turn to A minor. c503 listed it as do-not-touch. The Phrygian form moves ♭6–5 into the bass, and every upper voice must then leave C. The mixture becomes a bass event at p, under the violins' single crotchets.
- **It uses up the retransition's bass early.** 243–245 already has the bass A–C–B: iv7, then the Fr6 (c062, the movement's one augmented sixth), then the structural V. A&S keeps that chord for the approach to structural dominants (PDF 513, 526, cited by c062). The same bass 40 bars earlier, at a lesser cadence inside the trio, would make the retransition's approach sound like a repeat.
- **It roughens the viola line.** The viola has to leap D♯5→F♯4 into 204.

**Decision:** not applied. The scratch copy is v593.

### 2. No parallel 6/3 series anywhere. NOT APPLICABLE; THE REASON STANDS

sixths.py finds no run of three or more beats with outer-voice 6ths over a stepwise bass, in any section. This is Unit 18's central technique (§§1–4, PDF 290–295). In this movement it is ruled out for two reasons:
- **The C-major rule.** A descending 6/3 series in E minor, G major, F major or D minor passes a 6/3 on E, which is E–G–C. 80–84 shows the problem most clearly. The bass there, F–D–B♭–G–A, is the root-position form A&S prints as "similar to" Mendelssohn's series (Ex. 18-3b, PDF 292). The stepwise form I6–(VII6)–VI6–(V6)–IV6–II6 needs a V6 of F, which is C/E. This was rejected at c202 and c413 for that reason, and the reason still holds.
- **The allowed keys.** In A minor, E major, B major and C♯ minor a series is possible. The A-minor passages (65–72, 297–300) are pedal stretti, where a held A is the right device for a build. In the trio the possible places are the protected T statements, the 185–196 sequence (c302) and 197–200 (c503).

**Decision:** no change. The movement uses Unit 18's single-chord functions (rows 8–17) and keeps its 5/3 goals.

### 3. The return from the trio: 28 bars, 22.5 s, from T's cadence to Tempo I (225–252). KEPT, NOW WITH A CORPUS REASON

This is the movement's longest low-motion stretch: 20 bars on E (225–244) and 8 on the B pedal (245–252). Dvořák 9/iii, the plan's first model, takes 25 bars over the same span. His trio closes with two 8-bar restatements of its theme (345–360), then a 9-bar transition (361–369). Our codetta also restates T's head in three relays (Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1) and then holds the trio's goal chord, and our retransition is 12 bars. The proportion matches the model. Unit 18 §1 supports ending the trio on a 5/3 goal (237–240) rather than a fluid chord.

**Decision:** no change. This answers c133's "one static place" with the model's own proportions; c153's thinning stays.

### 4. The link 141–152 against Dvořák's VI6 pivot (§8, PDF 299–300). FOLLOWS

Dvořák moves into his trio with a 6/3 over the tonic bass (VI6 over E, bar 231). Our link does the E-major counterpart: c413's C♯ over the E pedal at 146 (5–6–5) is a "VI6" that stands for I. It answers c582's minor B–C–B at 134–135, and it comes after Hn 1's G→G♯. The link's 12 bars (8.5 s) are well inside Dvořák's 34-bar transition. **Decision:** no change.
