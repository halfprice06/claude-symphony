## Findings

### 1. Bar 180: the trio's turn to B reaches its cadential 6/4 by a whole step; add E♯ in the bass on beat 3 (applied in /tmp/critic-592/work)

- **Problem.** In T's second statement (cellos, with Vln I an octave higher from 177) the consequent turns to B, as c093 set it up. The harmony is ii6/5 of B over E at 180 (C♯–E–G♯ with the tune's B), the cadential 6/4 and V7 over F♯ at 181–182, and B at 183. The basses and Bsn 2 move E→F♯, a whole step. This is the one cadence in the movement that goes to a new key, and it is approached by the weakest bass the reading describes.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 424, §6: "Perhaps the most important function of chords applied to V is to emphasize dominant harmony (usually at a cadence) by means of a rising half-step progression in the bass (♯4–5) … ♯4 is most often a chromatic passing tone leading from IV, II6, or II6/5 to V, possibly embellished by 6/4." Ex. 25-12c is this progression exactly: II6/5 – V6/5 of V – V. PDF 422, §4: chords applied to V "significantly increase their drive to the dominant". In the movement's other cadences to V (52, 88, 128, 214–216) the bass already rises by a half step; 180 is the exception.
- **Reason against the record.** c412 (finding 2) kept 164, 180, 220 and 352 without ♯4 because "T's bar 12 has A on beat 3 in the tune (C♯–B–A)", and c522 repeated this. That is true at 164, 220 and 352. It is not true at 180: since c093 and v94 this statement's bar 12 is C♯–B–G♯ (the cadence moved to B), and the ♯4 of B is E♯, not A♯. On 180.3 the tune is G♯ (Vc G♯3, Vln I G♯4) and Vln II plays C♯5–G♯4. E♯ completes C♯–E♯–G♯, V6 of V, with no clash. c492's "soft first statements" reason was about saving the C♮ mixture for 220. This change uses no mixture, and 180 belongs to the second statement, not the first.
- **Fix (applied, tA.ily).** Cb: `| e,,2. |` becomes `| e,,2 eis,,4 |`. Bsn 2: `e,2.--)` becomes `e,2-- eis,4--)`. The bass is now E (ii6/5 of B, beats 1–2) – E♯ (V6 of V, beat 3) – F♯ (6/4, 181). E♯1 is F1 on the bass's E string, and the bass player keeps the same bow and dynamic. No other part changes.
- **Voice leading checked against the reading.** The chromatic step E→E♯→F♯ happens in one voice, the bass line in octaves (Ex. 25-3a). E♯ appears only in that line, so the temporary leading tone is not doubled (PDF 419, 439 point 3). Vln II's E5 on the second quaver of beat 2 sounds before E♯ arrives. It is an inner accompaniment figure (con sord. pp, under the tune's mp/mf), it doubles the bass's own E, and E♯ arrives in the bass. Those are the conditions of PDF 420 (leading tone in the bass, top voice not involved, no leaps) and of the "apparent cross relation produced by doubling" (PDF 402). No E♮ sounds at 181. The E♮ at 182 is the seventh of V7 of B, after F♯ has arrived, which is the normal order (Ex. 25-12). The voices move to 181 by step or by common tone, with no new parallels. The scan diff against v592 is empty.

### 2. Bars 164, 220, 352: the same cadence in E keeps ii6/5 → I6/4 (kept)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-12c with A♯ in the bass on beat 3 of T's bar 12.
- **Why kept.** In these three statements the tune has A on beat 3 (C♯–B–A). A♯ under it would be a clash inside one chord. That is not a cross relation A&S allow (PDF 419–420). c412's reason holds here, and T's notes are not worth changing for this. 220 already has the reading's other intensifier, ii ø6/5 with C♮ (c492), and 349–356 is settled (c494).

### 3. Codetta 227/231/235: IV with added 6th (not built)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 24-20 (PDF 412–413): add F♯ to the second plagal IV at 231 (A–C♯–E–F♯).
- **Why not.** A&S give the added 6th "especially where an ascent to 3̂ is wanted". Here the melody over each IV falls C♯–B (6̂–5̂). The series IV – IV – iv already grows by mixture at 235, and the codetta relay 225–236 is protected (c412, c501). An added F♯ would be a pp inner note with no line to carry it.
