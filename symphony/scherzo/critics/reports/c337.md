## Findings

### 1. Reprise fugato 289-296: a literal copy of 57-64; give it a different hue (applied)

**Bars:** 289-296 (dB.ily): Bsn 1, Cl 1.

**Problem:** The reprise fugato (cellos' subject in G minor at 289, violas' answer in D minor over the cellos' countersubject at 293) has the same notes, register, articulation and colour as the exposition at 57-64. Only the strings play; every wind and the harp rest. Everything else that comes back in this movement is rescored. The reprise of theme A has the flute heterophony, the arco cello line and pizz. off-beats. The tutti at 305-320 is re-voiced without low brass. T's return at 209 adds the cellos, bassoons and Hn 4. The reprise fugato is the one passage that returns unchanged, so the listener hears the development's opening a second time with nothing new.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 209: Dvořák reworks the repeat of a strain "without changing the basic color ... to give it a slightly different hue."
- PDF 213: Bizet makes "slight, but significant changes ... to give the repeat a different flavor."
- PDF 224: Ravel's return is "orchestrated in very much the same way as the beginning, but differently colored."
- PDF 230: Schoenberg's whole addition in the Brahms tutti is that he "only adds secco chords at the beginning of each measure".
- Beethoven 9/ii bars 9-24 (corpus): each string entry of the fugato has a pp wind crotchet on its downbeats, bassoons with the cellos and clarinet with the violas. The texture stays a string fugato, and the winds play no quavers.

**Answer to the recorded reasons (c007, c207).** c007 removed wind doublings at the unison, which ran with every entry. Those made the winds' arrival at the climax add nothing new (adler1 p. 143: a fugato is exposed in the strings "before it is developed, colored, or doubled"). c207 kept the reprise strings-only for three reasons: p. 143, adler1 p. 142 (limit the colours when a passage returns), and the freshness of the winds at 297. The fix below does not double the line. One wind marks each downbeat of one string line at pp, and the strings still carry all the counterpoint. The exposition at 57-68 stays strings-only, so p. 143 is still met where it applies. The reprise is a return, which p. 143 does not cover, and ch. 16 asks for exactly this kind of slight change of hue (PDF 209, 213, 224). Two winds, one note a bar, is the smallest colour change there is, so p. 142's limit holds. At 297-299 the winds still enter with the subject's quaver rhythm for the first time in this section. Cl 1 rests at 297 before its f stretto entry at 298 (PDF 228: the solo's entrance stays fresh).
