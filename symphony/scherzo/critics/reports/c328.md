## Findings

No score change. The candidates I examined are below, ranked by how much they would matter if they were wrong, with my decision on each.

### 1. Bars 336–338: low brass silent under the fff peak (no change)
- **Question:** Tchaikovsky's arrival (PDF 39–40, image) holds the trombones and tuba fff through the climax. Here the trombones, bass trombone and tuba play 333–335, rest at 336–338 and return at 339.
- **Decision:** Keep (c034, c169). The rest makes 339 work as two of the reading's own devices: the low brass re-entry is the long note under the short stroke of strings, woodwinds and timpani (PDF 189–190, Ex. XV-35), and it is a held-back colour returning for one event (PDF 37). The V harmony at 336–338 already has its bass in Vc, Cb, Bsn 2 and Cbsn at fff and the timpani roll, so nothing is missing. Adding them at 336 would put a fourth brass level under Vln I's B6, the movement's one fff, and Dan's rule allows no fff brass, so they could only enter f, under the tutti, which adds weight to the high note without adding its loudness (c034's Barber reason, adler2 PDF 150).

### 2. Bars 333–338: horns held ff for six bars without Franck's or Tchaikovsky's > on each held bar (no change)
- **Question:** Franck (PDF 38) writes *f molto sostenuto* with > on each held bar; Tchaikovsky's trombones (PDF 40) have fff>. The four horns here hold ff with tenuto from 333 to 338 and only taper at 339.
- **Decision:** Keep. A > after a loud brass attack sounds as an fp, an accent made by the decay, which is the "bite" that Dan's rule forbids (BRIEF; c123, c199). c188's tenuto slurs (333–335, 336–338) already give the horns two soft-tongued groups, so the strings' and woodwinds' hemiola minims at 337–338 are not covered by re-attacked downbeats. Franck's > marks are on strings and woodwinds at f, not on brass at ff.

### 3. Bars 155, 171, 199, 211, 343: the new falling sixth in T bar 3 (no change)
- **Question:** c321 gave T a falling sixth (D♯5 dotted crotchet – F♯4 quaver – B4). Adler PDF 188 says the dynamic and register decide how a tune is played. Does the new leap need its own mark (a tenuto, a hairpin)?
- **Decision:** Keep the marks as they are. In Cl 1 (155) the leap lies inside the four-bar dolce slur, so it is played legato at p across the break, and in the violins (171) it has its own bow, so the leap is a string crossing within one bow. Both are what the reading's Berlioz example does: one hairpin pair at the phrase's peak (Ex. XV-33), not a mark on each interval. T's peaks (bar 6 and the tied A in bar 11) already carry the swells. A tenuto on D♯ would put the weight of bar 3 on the leading tone, before the phrase's peak.

### 4. Bars 300→301: forte subito piano without overlap (no change)
- **Question:** Ex. XV-36 says the soft group should enter under the loud chord before it ends.
- **Decision:** Keep. 300 is A major and 301 is the B pedal: the chords differ, so the soft winds cannot sustain 300's chord. The strings themselves hold ff to the barline and take sfp on 301 while the winds and horns enter p, so the change happens on one attack. That is how the reading's goal (no gap in sound at the subito) is reached here.

### 5. Bars 101–104: the brass's first entry is buried in a crescendo (no change)
- **Question:** Schubert's trombones (PDF 37) enter as a new colour at a distinct moment. Here Tpt and Tbn enter pp< under strings and woodwinds already f<.
- **Decision:** Keep. The new colour arrives at the moment of the form that matters, the arrival of the tutti theme at 105 (the tuba's first note, the trombones' f strokes), and the pp< entry is the Schubert image's own way in (trombones pp, then the tutti grows). c128 and c244 settled the levels.
