## Findings

### 1. Reprise tutti 305-320 one level lower (f, not ff): the best candidate. Built and rejected

The candidate. PDF 116 asks whether a passage is "a climax or merely a step", and PDF 136 says the ear cannot take the full orchestra for long. From 297 to 339 the movement is loud for 35 of 43 bars: the stretto 297-304, the reprise tutti 305-320 at ff, and the coda tutti 333-339 (ff, fff at 336), with only 321-328 between. If the reprise tutti were the "step" and the coda the climax, the coda would have more headroom. PDF 118 (e) allows a varied dynamic for a return. Of all the changes this reading supports, this is the one a listener would hear most: sixteen bars of the full orchestra one level lower.

What I built. In /tmp/critic-573/work/sections/dC.ily: Fl 1·2 `\ff` → `\f` at 305, and `<>\f` at the start of every other sounding part (Ob 1·2, C.a., Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Hn 1-4, timp, strings). Shared staves got equal dynamics. The build was clean, with 0 warnings and 13 pages.

Why it makes the music worse:
- 301-304 is a crescendo by amplification that reaches ff at 304: strings fp, winds p<, Hn 3·4 and timpani p< to ff (PDF 127, 129). With the candidate, the arrival of the reprise at 305 becomes a subito drop on the downbeat of the theme's return. PDF 127 says the second group "attains its crescendo" to make the process "more tense". Its goal is the arrival, and the candidate takes the arrival away.
- The reprise tutti is already a step in Rimsky's own sense. It is a partial tutti (PDF 119, horns only), with no trumpets, trombones or tuba (c428). The full brass colour is kept fresh for 332-339 (PDF 136: "a group … silent for some time gains fresh interest"). The difference between 305 and 333 is already made by timbre and by the single fff at 336, which is how PDF 118 and 119 prefer to make it.
- In the corpus models the scherzo's return is the scherzo at its own dynamics. Dvořák 9/iii and Beethoven 9/ii both repeat the scherzo da capo, ff tuttis included, and their codas get their weight from new material and scoring, not from a softened reprise.

I restored dC.ily, rebuilt, and confirmed that the scratch sections are identical to v573.

### 2. Re-entries after long rests (PDF 136): the audit holds; nothing applied

I checked every entry after ten or more bars' rest.
- **Brass that rest long.** They re-enter pp: trumpets and low brass pp< at 101 and 332; Hn 1·2 pp at 273; Hn 3·4 pp at 149, 237, 277 and 331. This answers PDF 136 directly.
- **Hn 3 p at 341 and Hn 1 p at 357.** These horns enter as melodies (T in A, the motto tail) over a pp basis. PDF 133 asks for the basis to be "of inferior dynamic power" to the melodic design. pp on the melody would put T and the motto tail at the level of the harp and the pizz. basses beneath them. c303 and c483 kept these on the same grounds. The reading gives no stronger reason, so I keep them.
- **Hn 1·2 f at 299 and Hn 3·4 f at 300.** These are entries inside the ff stretto, after 10 and 39 bars respectively. Rimsky's alternative would be ff, which the brief excludes (c123), or pp<, which cannot reach ff inside a bar at 2. = 88. c505's horn punctuation f in the tuttis stands.
- **Bass clarinet mp at 211, after 63 bars.** This is the one literal "colourless" mp re-entry. It doubles the basses' B1 an octave up, inside an mf accompaniment under the violins' f return of T. It cannot be heard as an entry, so changing it would change nothing a listener notices. I did not apply it.

### 3. Crescendo and diminuendo order (PDF 130): keep

- **Crescendi.** 89-104 adds strings (pizz., then head entries), then woodwind, then brass. 325-333 adds strings at 325, woodwind at 329, horns at 331, and all brass at 332. Both are the order PDF 130 gives.
- **Diminuendi.** 133-140 and 321-324 drop the brass first, then the woodwind, and end on pizz. strings and timpani.
- **The cut at 339.** Here the brass hold a minim with a diminuendo after the tutti's accented crotchet. That is a cut-off with a decaying brass chord, not the prolonged diminuendo PDF 130 describes. PDF 130 itself calls the brass the group "most facile in producing dynamic shades", and this is also Dan's soft-brass rule (weight, not bite). Keep.

### 4. Economy of colour (PDF 136): keep

The trumpets, trombones and tuba play only in 101-120 and 332-339. The harp rests from 241 to 340. The violins rest from 133 to 168, so their con sord. entry at 169 is fresh. These long rests are the "fresh interest upon its reappearance" that PDF 136 describes. The two string-free episodes, F major at 73-88 and the link plus the trio's opening at 141-168, are the "wind tutti" and "harmonic basis" cases of PDF 116 and 121.

### 5. Varied returns (PDF 117-118): keep

Each return of theme A uses at least one of operations (a)-(e). The 105 tutti returns as 253 mp with pizz. (e and d), 305 is a partial tutti with the flutes' florid line (d, PDF 132), and 333 is the full tutti with the fff summit. T moves from Cl 1 to the cellos, to the f call at 197, to the violins at 209, and to the coda relay. The second idea moves from G major f to E minor sub. p to the reprise's E major. Nothing is repeated with the same scoring.

### 6. Proportions and length: keep

At 4:38 this is the shortest of the four movements and suits its place between the Adagio and the finale. In seconds the exposition runs 87, the trio with link and retransition 88, and the reprise with coda 93, a nearly even three-part balance. The trio-tempo coda (341-376, about 34 s) is the cyclic close, and the finale and movement I quote its bars (357-360, 363-371, 364-376). Shortening it would renumber those quotations, and this reading gives no reason to do so. The development, at 48 bars (fugato 12, stretto 4, F major 16, retransition 12, hemiola 4), keeps moving: each stage is a "step" to the 105 tutti, and that tutti is scored as a climax (PDF 116).

### 7. Transfer, imitation and echo (PDF 125-129): keep

- **Transfers.** Hn 3 and Hn 1 overlap at 361-369, B. cl. and Cbsn share D2 at 329-330, and Cl and the violas share E4 at 88-89. Each puts the common note where PDF 126 asks for it.
- **Imitation.** The head entries at 89/92/95 and the coda's T relay rise in both register and instrument (PDF 128).
- **The coda relay at 344/345 and 348/349.** These are separate four-bar phrases with their own cadences. They are not one passage too wide for a single instrument, so the common-note rule does not apply (c483).
