## Findings

### 1. 105–108: the first ff tutti arrives with nothing above the tune, and the trumpets outweigh it in its own register (applied)

**Problem.** At 105 the theme returns ff for the first time, after the hemiola build in which the flutes reach D♯6 and B5 (101–104). At this arrival the flutes stop for four bars. The tune is then Vln I + Vln II + Ob 1 in unison on E5–G5, with C.a. and Cl 2 an octave lower. Directly under it the two trumpets play motive x on B4/E4 in the tune's own rhythm, the horns hold G4/E4, and Hn 3·4 a2 play the tenor line. The top of the orchestra at the arrival is G5 in one clarinet (105, the seventh's resolution) and E5–G5 in the tune. The upper octave arrives only at 109. The reprise of the same tutti has had the flutes on top from its first bar since c369 (305).

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 51: in loud passages "Trumpet = Trombone = Tuba = 2 Horns", "Horn = 2 Clarinets = 2 Oboes = 2 Flutes", and a string department is worth two winds in forte. At 105–108 the tune has about five units (two violin sections, one oboe). The two trumpets have about eight, a fourth below, in the same rhythm. Doubling the tune in a register the brass does not reach is the remedy that the chapter's balance table points to.
- PDF 82: harmony that seems to have more than four parts "is usually only four part harmony with extra parts added", which are duplications "in the adjacent upper octave of one or more of the three upper parts". The diagram "Duplication of 1 part" doubles the top part above.
- PDF 85: "make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts". On the same page Rimsky calls "doubling the upper note in octaves" an effective method.
- PDF 90 rule 1: a part is doubled or not throughout a passage. The 16-bar tutti 105–120 has its top doubled at 109–112 and 117–120 and at pitch at 113–116, but not at 105–108, which is the arrival.

**The recorded reason answered.** c124 removed the flutes for three reasons. (a) At pitch they were in their weak register, B4–G5 under ff brass (Adler PDF 185). (b) The upper octave was impossible because 107 would reach B6, which belongs to the coda peak at 336. (c) A colour that enters after a rest is heard more clearly (Adler PDF 156). On (a): the new line is in the upper octave, E6–G6, the flute's clear register. On (b): at 107 the flutes keep the theme's own falling contour one octave above the original statement (E6–B5–B5–G5). Vln I already rises to B5 there (c009), so the flutes meet it at B5 and G5 and never reach B6. At 108 the flutes are an octave above Vln I (F♯6–D♯6–B5), and B5 steps to E6 at 109. The leap G5–F♯6 at 107–108 is the same leap the reprise flutes make at 307–308 (c369). On (c): the flutes have just played ff at 101–104, so a four-bar gap at the arrival does not make 109 a new colour. It only takes the top away at the loudest new event. Barber's point that c124 cited (PDF 150: do not let the flutes drop out before the phrase's last note) is now also met, because the flutes play 108's last note instead of resting for the leap (c036).

**Fix (applied in sections/sC.ily, both flute variables; the c036 rest at 108 beat 3 goes and the duplicate \ff at 109 is removed).**

### 2. 133–136 and 321–323: the cor anglais is enclosed between the two clarinets, which stand a fifth apart (not applied; decision: keep)

The dying E-minor chord is Cl 1 B4, C.a. G4, Cl 2 E4, Bsn 1 E3. Rimsky PDF 92 says a single enclosed colour should sit "between two different timbres". PDF 94 shows the low third instrument (C.a., bass clarinet) under the pair. Rule 4 (PDF 90) wants a concord, not the fifth E4–B4, in the clarinet pair. In close position there are only three ways to place two clarinets and the C.a. on E4–G4–B4. Each breaks one rule. Cl 1 B4 / Cl 2 G4 / C.a. E4 puts Cl 2 on concert G4, the A clarinet's written B♭4, the palest throat note (Rimsky PDF 60, c356). C.a. B4 / Cl 1 G4 / Cl 2 E4 is c184's version, which c356 rejected for the same note. The current voicing is the third. I also tried an open voicing, Cl 1 G5 / Cl 2 B4 / C.a. E4 over Bsn E3. That doubles the bass an octave above in a widely spaced chord, which PDF 82's note calls heavy, and it brightens a chord that should die away. **Decision:** keep. The throat B♭ held pp for four bars is a worse sound than an enclosed C.a. that the clarinets already match in level. This spot has changed three times (c094, c184, c356), and none of the options is free.

### 3. 45–55, 121–128, 193–196: open fourths and fifths in the clarinet pair under a string top (not applied; decision: keep)

PDF 97 remark 4 describes this texture: when the top is in the strings, the wind keeps the two middle parts and should use "no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths". The pair has fifths and fourths at 45.3, 49.3, 51.3–52, 53.2, 54 and 193–195. I worked through 49–52, where most of them fall. Cl 2's A4 forms a fourth or fifth with Cl 1 because the horns hold A4/A3 under the A7 chord. Each concord I tried either doubles the bass C♯ of A7/C♯ at 50 and 52, which PDF 83 forbids for an inverted dominant seventh, or puts a clarinet in unison with the violas' octave of the tune (c277), or lifts Cl 1 further above Ob 1's octave (c014/c109). In this passage the horns complete each chord (for example 45.3 G3–G4–B4–D5), so the middle is not thin. Remark 4 aims at a two-part wind middle with nothing else, and that is not the case here. **Decision:** keep. c364 found the same result.

### 4. 225–232 and 341–348: harp off-beats strike the violas' held pitches (not applied; decision: keep)

PDF 52 says plucked and bowed sounds on the same notes are "heard independently". At 225–232 and 341–348 the harp's off-beat chords include the violas' sustained G♯3/A3 (and E3, D4). Here that independence is the point. The harp is a rhythmic layer (beats 2 and 3), and the violas are a pp pad. Nothing is meant to blend. At 153–168 the same harp figure sits on the horns' drone, the wind-with-harp combination PDF 52 praises. **Decision:** keep.
