## Findings

### 1. Codetta 233–236: Bsn 1's statement of T's head lies under Vln II and violas held an octave above it, on its own second partial. Applied.

**Problem.** In the codetta T's head passes Ob 1 (225) → Cl 1 (229) → Bsn 1 (233), p dolce, swelling to mp. Under Ob 1 (G♯5–C♯6) the strings hold B4/G♯4 an octave below. Cl 1 (G♯4–C♯5) has nothing above it since c423. At 233–236, though, Vln II (B4–B4–A4–B4) and the violas (G♯4–G♯4–E4–G♯4) come back pp, held, directly above the bassoon's G♯3–A3–B3–C4–B3. Each bassoon note's octave is a held string note: G♯3 under the violas' G♯4, B3 under Vln II's B4, and C4 under the harp's C5 at 235. The bassoon's second partial and its formant region (about 400–500 Hz, G♯4–B4) are the strings' pitches. So the bassoon's colour merges into the string chord, and what comes through is its fundamental as a moving inner voice under a held chord. The third statement of the head, the one the codetta builds toward by thinning, is the least audible of the three.

**Evidence.** Sounds in one band mask each other and weaken each one's contribution to its own timbre; the fewer instruments in a band, the clearer they are (Casanelles PDF 286–287). A quieter instrument whose band another instrument covers only adds partials to it ("would that constitute one or two notes?", PDF 277–278). In Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272) Mahler stops the woodwind chord at the bar the soft layer enters and leaves only a pedal far from its band. Each register of an instrument has its own strength in combination (PDF 257), and the bassoon's middle register is the weakest of the three codetta voices. Adler I PDF 226 (cited by c126) says the same: the bassoon "blends and is swallowed when enclosed by instruments of its own range". c126 took Hn 3 off these bars for that reason. c423's do-not-touch says "nothing held above the codetta's wind solos at 225–236", and the current 233–236 contradicts it. c423 kept Vln II at 233 because it lies "an octave and more above Bsn 1". The reading shows why that does not help: an octave above is the bassoon's own second partial, which is the most exact way to cover it.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tC.ily, bars 233–236 rest in Vln II and violas, which re-enter pp on the closing chord at 237:

```
tCVla = { ... | gis2. | gis2. | a2. | gis2. | R2.*4 | gis'2.~\pp | gis'2.~ | gis'2 r4 | R2. | }
```
(were `b'2.\pp | b'2. | a'2. | b'2. | b'2.~ | ...` and `gis'2. | gis'2. | e'2. | gis'2. | gis'2.~ | ...`). The harmony stays complete. The harp's downbeat chords (E4–G♯4–B4, at 235 E4–A4–C5, c153), Hn 4's E3 and the pizz. E2/A1 remain. The three codetta statements now each have their band to themselves: Ob 1 above the pad, Cl 1 above the violas, Bsn 1 above the horn pedal. The strings' return at 237 (Vln II B4, violas G♯4 with Hn 1's G♯4, kept since c440) marks the arrival of the poco rit. chord, as the accompaniment thins toward it by four-bar unit (c153). A c444 comment is added above `tCVlnTwo`.

### 2. Second idea 45–50: Cl 1 exchanges D5/B4 and E5/C♯5 with the tune's middle octave. No change.

At 45 beat 3 and 49 beat 3, Cl 1 rises (B4→D5, A4→E5) while Ob 1/Vln II fall (D5→B4, E5→C♯5). For about a beat and a half the filler lies a third above that octave of the tune, in the same band (PDF 287). The tune's top octave (Vln I + Fl 1, D6–A6) has no other instrument in its band, and the ear follows the line there. The middle octave is the doubling. The clarinets' voice exchange is the inner voice of c432's chain E7–A–D7–G, and the three-octave statement is protected (c419). **Decision:** keep.

### 3. Low intervals at 330 and 215 (PDF 274). No change.

`low.py` finds two places where a third or sixth lies below C3. At 330 beat 2 the cellos' quaver F♯2 sounds over the basses' D2. At 215 beat 3, Cbsn A♯2 sounds over Cb C♯2. Both are weak-beat, short notes inside a moving figure. The downbeats are a fifth (330: D2–A2) and an octave (215), and both bars are c083/c443 and c157 harmony that is protected. **Decision:** keep.

### 4. Harp downbeat chords strike with Bsn 1's attacks at 233–236 (PDF 275–276). No change.

A pluck is mostly attack, and at 233–236 the harp chord falls on each bassoon onset. I considered moving it to beat 2, as at 229–232. I kept the downbeat. The pluck decays within the beat and the bassoon's sustained tone is heard after it (PDF 290: a sustained sound is the more present one). c153's pulse thinning 3 → 2 → 1 per bar into 237 is a designed shape. With the string chord gone (Finding 1), the harp is the only sound above the bassoon, and its chord is short.

### 5. Coda 349–356: the last segment of T in the solo violin and Fl 1 at A6 (PDF 273, 283–284, 287). No change.

On the reading alone, a remembered theme loses its highs, and here the coda's T ends in the movement's brightest register. c264, c344 and c354 weighed this. Their reasons still hold: 341–356 is one statement passed between single players, not three statements at growing distance. The distance is built by dynamics (p, p, pp) and by the closing page that follows, and moving the line down would put it in unison with Vln II. The solo violin (c438) already does what PDF 286 describes, a narrower, more focused sound in place of a section. **Decision:** keep.

### 6. F episode 73–80: harp LH chord (C3–F3–A3, beat 2) struck inside the held Hn 3·4/Bsn chord of the same pitches. No change.

This is a struck chord in the band of a held pad (PDF 287). But the harp's melody (F5–C6) lies alone two octaves higher, which is the line that matters. The LH stroke gives the pad an attack on beat 2 that marks the 3/4 in a passage with no strings (PDF 275–276). The harp melody is protected (c001), and c436 kept the LH. **Decision:** keep.

### 7. Tutti 105–120: trombone crotchets (E3–G3) on each downbeat in the band of the Hn 3·4/Vc/Bsn countermelody (G3–E4). No change.

The trombones are f, so they are harmonically dense (PDF 289), and they sound in the countermelody's band (PDF 287). They play one decaying crotchet per bar, though, and the countermelody holds through the bar in three colours. By PDF 290 the held line is the more present. The crotchets also give the bass weight in the only tutti with low brass before the coda (c424, c428). **Decision:** keep.
