## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hn 1·2 punctuate the tutti theme at ff, one level above the trumpets, trombones and timpani (applied; audible)

**Bars:** 101–120 and 301–320 (Hn 1·2 only).

**Problem:** In both tutti statements of theme A, Hn 1·2 play crotchet chords on beats 1 and 3 (G4/E4, the beat-3 chord staccato) in the bars where the theme rests or holds. They crescendo to ff at 104 and 304 and stay at ff. The trumpets (motive x), trombones, tuba and timpani in the same punctuating role are at f. So the loudest brass attack in these 32 bars is a staccato horn chord on the weak beat. Dan's note asks for brass weight, not bite. The tune is in the ff woodwinds and strings, and Hn 3·4 (ff, a2) have the only melodic brass line, the tenor countermelody with Bsn 1 and the cellos.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 297, Ex. XVIII-4a (Brahms 1/i mm. 82–87, the example Adler chose for score layout). Flutes, oboes, clarinets, bassoons, violins, violas and basses are ff. Horns, trumpets and timpani play the punctuating rhythm and are marked "cresc. … f", one level under, and the horns are at the trumpets' level, not above it. c497 (Finding 2) cited Hindemith (adler2 PDF 43) for the same point, that short accompanying figures go a level under the line, and kept the ff only because of c076.

**Answer to c076/c497:** c076 relies on Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41: a horn has about half a trumpet's power, so horns need a level more to *equal* the trumpets. That rule fits when the horns must match the trumpets. Here they should not. The trumpets carry motive x, the theme's own motive. Hn 1·2 only fill the theme's rests, and Brahms marks that job at the trumpets' printed level. Hn 3·4's tenor line keeps its ff, so the brass line c076 protected is unchanged, and it now stands out from the horn punctuation as c497's Hindemith principle asks. The build order into 105/305 (strings, woodwind, brass; c408) is unchanged.

**Fix (applied):** the crescendo target of Hn 1·2's held chord at 104 and 304, on both players (the shared staff keeps equal dynamics):
- sB.ily `sBHnOne` 104: `fis'2.--\ff)` → `fis'2.--\f)`; `sBHnTwo` 104: `dis'2.--\ff)` → `dis'2.--\f)`
- dB.ily `dBHnOne` 304: `fis'2.--\ff)` → `fis'2.--\f)`; `dBHnTwo` 304: `dis'2.--\ff)` → `dis'2.--\f)`

In the MIDI the Hn 1·2 punctuation drops from velocity 67/75 to 62/70. Hn 3·4 stay at 67. The parts for Horns 1 and 2 print f at 104 and 304. No line moves or goes silent, so no cue changes (parts-cues.json still matches).

### 2. Seven shared-staff entries begin with no player label in their system (applied; print only)

**Bars and staves:** Bn 1·2 at 173 (Bn 2 alone; its "2." is at 169 on the previous system). Hn 3·4 at 173 (Hn 3; "3." at 169). Cl 1·2 at 192 (Cl 1 enters pp after 23 bars' rest, and the combiner printed nothing because Cl 2 joins at 193). Cl 1·2 at 211 (Cl 1 on T; "1." at 209, previous system). Hn 3·4 at 277 (Hn 3 alone, pp). Bn 1·2 at 359 (Bn 2; "2." at 357). Fl 1·2 at 361 (Fl 1's G♯5, pp, after the 349 label on the previous system).

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 295: "it is imperative that all the instruments must be clearly marked in all systems appearing on a page." Ex. XVIII-10a (PDF 301) names the player on a shared staff. Mahler's reduced page (Ex. XVIII-3b, PDF 296) labels each line again in each system. At 359–361, on the page that holds the ending, the conductor sees a single E3 in the bassoons and a single G♯5 in the flutes and cannot tell which player has them, or whether both do.

**Fix (applied):** `\sysLabel` where the entry is in the system's first bar, and a score-only bold label elsewhere (as c415 did at 217):
- tA.ily `tABsnTwo` 173: `a,2.---\sysLabel "2."(`; `tAHnThree` 173: `a2-\tweak stencil ##f \p-\sysLabel "3."( b4)`
- tB.ily `tBClOne` 192: `ais'2.(\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."`
- tC.ily `tCClOne` 211: `dis''4.-\sysLabel "1." fis'8 a'4\<~`
- dA.ily `dAHnThree` 277: `b2.--(\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "3."`
- coda.ily `codaBsnTwo` 359: `e2.-\sysLabel "2."`; `codaFluteOne` 361: `gis''2.~\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."`

All seven print, and none collides with anything at 130 dpi. After the change, the label scan finds no unlabelled single-player entry at the start of any system. The remaining flags are two-voice entries, which the stems already make clear. The parts are unchanged by these labels.

### Considered, not changed
- **Coda horns at ff, 333–339, against f trumpets and trombones.** The Brahms example would argue for f here too, but the case differs. These are sustained chords, the brass's weight at the movement's one fff climax (336), not short weak-beat punctuation. c428's rule that trumpets and low brass return together is about their entry, not the horns' level. Lowering them would thin the climax. Kept.
- **Hn 3·4's tenor line at 105–120/305–320** stays ff (c076, c497). It is the brass's only line.
- **B6 at 336 without 8va** (PDF 300 item 8a). Kept, as c055/c145/c235/c325/c415 decided. It is the single peak note, and an octave sign would make it read as a lower note at speed.
- **Hn 1 at 41 (35 bars' rest) without a cue.** It is under the set's 40-bar rule (c435). Hn 2's cue at 37 is for a 40-bar rest. Kept.
- **Fl 1 slurs A6–B6 at 335–336** while the strings and C.a. play A staccato. At fff in the tutti this cannot be heard as a different attack, and the slur helps the flute reach its top note. Kept.
- **String page turns** (PDF 299 item 5): all fall at 1→2 in rests (152/153, Cb 140/141), and pages 2–3 face each other. No string passage loses half its players to a turn.
