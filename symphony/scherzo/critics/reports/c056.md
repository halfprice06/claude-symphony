## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Tutti 105–107 and 305–307: the timpani stay ff while the brass play f (applied)
- **Problem.** The roll and motive x at 101–104 and 301–304 end ff. sC and dC then start the timpani on `e,4->` with no new dynamic, so the accented E strokes at 105–107 and 305–307 are still ff. An earlier cycle set the horns, trumpets, trombones and tuba to f here so that the theme in the violins and woodwinds comes through. As written, the timpani are the loudest brass-register instrument in the tutti, with an accent on every downbeat.
- **Evidence.** Piston's warning (adler2 PDF 55 and 84): a ff on the timpani covers the rest of the orchestra. Adler PDF 141: a loud percussion stroke can cover the whole orchestra however loud the tutti is. PDF 79: in the tutti the timpani's role is to support the bass, not to lead.
- **Fix.** sC.ily `sCTimp = { e,4->\f r4 r4 | …` and dC.ily `dCTimp = { e,4->\f r4 r4 | …`. The accent stays, so the downbeat is still marked, but at the brass's level.

### 2. Multi-bar rolls are not tied (applied)
- **Bars.** 101–103, 129–130, 205–207, 237–239, 285–286, 301–303, 372–374.
- **Problem.** Every bar of these rolls is a separate `b,2.:32` (or `e,2.:32`) with no tie. Read literally, the player restrikes at each barline. In the crescendos (101–103, 301–303) that puts bumps on the downbeats. In the ppp roll at the end (372–374) it puts audible strokes under the horn's last G♯ just as it fades.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80, Ex. XII-48: "A longer roll should be notated" as tied tremolo notes. On the same page, the part must show where the roll ends. The finale's rolls are already tied.
- **Fix.** Add a tie to each bar of a roll except the last, e.g. sB `b,2.:32\<~ | b,2.:32~ | b,2.:32 |`, coda `e,2.:32\ppp~ | e,2.:32~ | e,2.:32 |`. In the trio (207→208 and 239→240) the roll is also tied into its closing crotchet: tB `b,2.:32\<~ | b,4\mf`, tC `e,2.:32~ | e,4 r4 r4`. This is Adler's Ex. XII-47, a roll ending on a tied note, so these quiet trio rolls end without a new attack. The roll at 374 is not tied into 375: the last E stays a fresh stroke with the pizzicato E (Bernstein example, PDF 141, and the coherence file's "then a last E"). Rolls that move to another drum (130→131, 286→287) cannot be tied.

### 3. ff on the last note of 104 and 304 instead of on the downbeat (applied)
- **Problem.** At 104 and 304 the timpani's ff is attached to the third crotchet (`b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4\ff`). Every other part reaches ff on the downbeat of 104 or 304. So the hairpin from 101 or 301 runs one bar longer in the timpani than in the orchestra, and motive x at 104 is printed as still growing. The timpanist cannot tell whether the motive's first stroke is ff.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 80: dynamics must be shown exactly. Ex. XII-52 (Beethoven 9/ii) marks the dynamic on the first note of the motive.
- **Fix.** sB.ily and dB.ily: `b,4\ff b,8 b,8 b,4 | }`.

### Checked and kept
- Tunings and ranges (Reading notes rows 1 and 7). G♯2 on the 28" drum is mid-range. B2 is one semitone above the bottom of the 25" drum, which is the standard place for it. Each retune has more than 20 bars of rest around it.
- The high E3 drum is used only once (bar 11). It is needed anyway for the Beethoven-style octave in motive x and for the finale's E♭. No change.
- Stick changes all have enough time (row 14).
- No timpani note falls outside the harmony (row 8).
- The hemiola at 337–338 is written with tied crotchets (`b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->`). On a drum the tie only means "do not strike", and it matches the rest of the orchestra. Kept.
