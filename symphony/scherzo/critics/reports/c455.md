## Findings

No score change. Every rule in the reading holds in v455. The points below are the ones I checked most closely and what I decided on each.

**1. Best audible candidate, built and rejected: Vln I's closing G♯5 at 369–376 as an artificial harmonic (PDF 25).** The reading's one rule that changes sound is the diamond harmonic. I looked for the change a listener would notice most. It is the movement's last violin sound, ppp under Hn 1's G♯4, turned into a glassy harmonic to match the harp harmonics at 375. No open string gives G♯5 as a natural harmonic: the partials of G3, D4, A4 and E5 contain no G♯ below G♯7. So the only playable version is an artificial harmonic on the G string: stop G♯3, touch C♯4 (a fourth higher), sounding G♯5. In LilyPond that is `<gis cis'\harmonic>2.~` ×8 with "sul G". I built it in /tmp/critic-455/cand. The build gave 0 warnings and 13 pages, and Gould's diamond printed white with the stem at the side (PDF 25).

I rejected it for four reasons:
- It breaks the frame that c430 and c440 protect: Hn 1 G♯4 plus an ordinary Vln I G♯5 at 1–2 and again at 369–376. The opening cannot also be a harmonic, because the violins come in cold at bar 1.
- The finale takes the last Vln I G♯5 as its own first sound at IV 1, so a change of timbre across the join would need the finale rewritten.
- Sixteen players holding an artificial harmonic for eight bars of ppp through a rit., changing bow freely, would beat in pitch and colour. A held ordinary tone does not.
- The score would show G♯3–C♯4 where the reader now sees the pitch that is heard (PDF 21: the clef and the notes show the contour), and the MIDI would play a C♯4–G♯3 fourth against the final chord.

The harp harmonics at 375 already supply the glassy colour at the right moment.

**2. Hn 3·4's tenor countermelody prints in treble clef at 105–120 and in bass clef at 305–320. Kept.** At 105–120 the a2 line (G3–E4, low point F♯3) sits on two or three ledger lines. That is within PDF 21's "up to at least three ledger lines", and it keeps the line's contour in one clef next to Hn 1·2. At 305–320, c115 used bass clef because Hn 4 holds the bass down to A2. That would need five ledger lines in treble, which is past the rule. The two places differ for a reason, so the score follows the rule in both.

**3. Hn 3·4 on three ledger lines for long stretches (73–89, 149–171, 229–240). Kept.** PDF 21 prefers this to frequent clef changes. The line never goes below E3 there. As c365 decided, bass clef is used only where the line really goes lower (217–224 for Hn 4, 301–320).

**4. Harp RH E3/F3 at 155 and 157 (three ledger lines below the treble stave). Kept.** These are two isolated off-beat chords between treble-clef chords. A bass clef for one beat would break PDF 22 (a clef between phrases, not for a single beat) and would hide the shape of the chords.

**5. Clef positions after rests (189, 229, 331, 358\|359). Kept.** Each one is where PDF 22–23 puts it: before the barline of the entry when the rest is shorter than a system, and at the end of the system after the player stops when the rest is longer.
