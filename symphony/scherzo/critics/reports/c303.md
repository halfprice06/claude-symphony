## Findings

No finding needs a change. Every principle in the table is followed, does not apply, or is departed from for a reason already recorded and accepted. Below are the points I checked against the reading and decided to keep.

**Considered and kept.**

1. **Hn 1 enters p at 357 after 16 bars' rest (the motto tail).** PDF 136 prefers pp or ff for a horn re-entry. But PDF 133 says that where melody and harmonic basis are wind and brass, the basis "should be of inferior dynamic power". At 357 the basis is Bsn 1, the violas, Vln II and the low strings, all pp. If the horn entered pp it would sit at the same level as its accompaniment. The p entry is one level above the basis, which is what PDF 133 asks for, and the mp peak at 358 is the top of a hairpin, not an entry. The pitches are protected (357-360). Kept.

2. **Horns enter p< at 101, 301 and 332.** These are entries into crescendos. PDF 127 ("Amplification") allows a second group to enter "piano or pianissimo" and grow faster, and these entries do that: horns p< under strings and winds f< at 101-104/301-304, and Hn 1·2 p< a bar after Hn 3·4 pp< at 331-332. They follow the reading.

3. **Low brass enter f at 105 (tuba) and 333 (trombones, bass trombone, tuba), and horns enter f with motive x at 71/299.** By PDF 136 these are "less successful" than ff. Dan's listening note rules out ff, sfz and accented brass entries (BRIEF.md; c123). The f entries sit under ff strings and woodwinds, which follows PDF 133's softer basis. The only answer to the book that the soft-brass rule allows would be a pp entry, and a pp entry at the coda's ff downbeat would contradict principle 6 (tutti belongs to f and ff). Kept, as c123 and c266 decided.

4. **The violins re-enter mp at 169 after 36 bars, and the harp LH re-enters mf at 209.** PDF 136 says the re-entry rule applies "more widely". At 169 the violins take T an octave up, one step in the trio's planned rise (p 153, mp 169, f 209, ff 217; c148, c213). At 209 the harp's single low notes double the arco basses under a full f return. The harp's mf is a balance level under f strings, not a colourless solo entry (c274). Kept.

5. **The twenty bars on E before the trio (133-152).** By principle 20 this is a diminuendo by elimination: brass and violins out at 132, then reeds, then cellos and basses, then the link's B.cl., bassoons and Hn 1 at pp. Principle 3 makes it a step, not a climax. It also prepares the trio's new colour (harp, Cl 1, Hn 3·4 drone at 153), which principle 26 says gains interest from the harp's 68 bars of silence. The pacing of the passage was confirmed by c253, c283 and c293. Kept.

6. **The reprise tutti 305-320 so close to the coda climax 333-339.** Principle 26 warns against long stretches of full orchestra. Between the two tuttis come 16 bars at lower levels: the graded close 321-324 and the string-only start of the coda at 325 (pp). The reprise tutti has no trombones or tuba, so it is a partial tutti and a step, not a climax (principles 3 and 6). The low brass return fresh at 333 after 212 bars (principle 26). Kept.

7. **Proportions and length.** The scherzo is 87 s, trio 70 s, reprise 49 s and two-stage coda 44 s, about 4:38 in all, unchanged since c283. Nothing in this reading argues for a different length. The coda's fast stage (16 bars, a strings-woodwind-brass build to the single fff) and slow stage (36 bars, elimination down to the horn and Vln I G♯) follow principles 20 and 21 in that order. Kept.
