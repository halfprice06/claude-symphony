## Findings

### 1. Bar 54: parallel 5ths at the hemiola cadence of the second idea (applied)

Problem. At 53.3 the cadential 6/4 over D has C4 in the cor anglais (the 7th of c471's line, D4–E4–D4–C4) and G4 in Cl 2 and the violas. On the downbeat of 54, the cor anglais falls to B3 and Cl 2 and the violas fall to F♯4. C–G to B–F♯ is a pair of parallel 5ths in the middle of the texture, at mf to f, in the bar where the hemiola makes the arrival heard. It also clouds the cadence: on 54.1 the melody is still on B5 (the 6th above the bass), while F♯ has already sounded below it. The 6/4's 4th has resolved before its 6th, so the cadential 6/4 does not sound as one chord.

Evidence. PDF 184–185, Ex. 12-20e: the 5ths that threaten a II7 or IV7 moving to V are avoided by a cadential 6/4 whose upper voices stay put while the bass holds, and the 6/4 then resolves as one chord. PDF 182, Ex. 12-8: the dissonant tone carried into the 6/4 is held as the 4th and falls only with the other voices on the move to V. In the hemiola, 54 groups as two minims, so the change from 6/4 to V7 belongs on 54.3, where the melody's B5–A5 and the horns already change.

Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily, violas and sAClTwo; c472 comment above sAClTwo):
```
violas:  g'4 g'4--( g'4--) | g'2 fis'4 | d'2.
sAClTwo: g'4( g'4 g'4 | g'2 fis'4 | d'2.)
```
After the fix the beats read D:D G B, D:D G B, D:C D F♯ A. The 6/4 now holds for a full minim, and all voices resolve together to V7, matching the hemiola's grouping. The cor anglais line (c471) is unchanged. The pairwise test no longer lists any 5th at 54.

### 2. Bars 176–177: octaves between the cellos' T and the bass at V–vi (applied)

Problem. In trio statement 2 the cellos carry T (A♯3–B3 | C♯4). The basses and Bsn 2 hold B1/B2 through 176 and rise to C♯ for the vi at 177. The tune and the bass both move B to C♯, so the arrival on vi is in octaves, the same fault as at a deceptive cadence where the bass doubles the leading-tone line. The ear hears the tune's arrival on C♯ as bass reinforcement instead of a new harmony. Statement 3 at 216 already avoids this with a bass B–F♯ on beat 3.

Evidence. PDF 197–199 and Ex. 13-12: when VI follows V as a substitute for I, the bass rises 5̂–6̂ and the upper voices move against it. With the 3rd doubled in VI, no voice moves with the bass in octaves. PDF 199–200 gives the same rule for the deceptive cadence.

Fix (applied, sections/tA.ily, tACb and tABsnTwo; c472 comment above tACb):
```
tACb:      ... | b,,2 fis,,4 | cis,2. | ...
tABsnTwo:  ... b,2-- fis,4--) \breathe
```
The bass now reaches C♯ from F♯ by a 4th, as at 216. The cellos and bass meet C♯ by contrary motion, and the only octaves left are Bsn 2 with the basses, which double each other throughout. The bass F♯ on beat 3 is a consonant arpeggiation of V and adds no new harmony.
