## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 197–200: Fl 1 and Ob 1 one level under Vln I on the forte call (applied)
**Problem.** At 197 the trio's middle section has two layers (c117): the call above, in Vln I with Fl 1 at the unison (E6–F♯6) and Ob 1 (E5, then at the unison on C♯6 from 198); and T below, in Vln II, violas, Cl 1 and Hn 1. Both winds are marked f, the same as the violins. In the sixth octave a flute and an oboe at f replace the violin tone of the call. Two f winds at the top also push down the tune, which is in the middle register.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 164: "one woodwind equals approximately sixteen violins". A single flute on the lead violin line "thickens the string tone considerably, sacrificing purity for extra body and intensity … To compensate somewhat for the difference in tonal weight, I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings." c067 applied this at 45 and 217 (Fl 1 mf against the violins' f), and both were accepted. 197 is the third forte place where a flute sits on a violin line, and it was missed. c034's reason for adding Fl 1 here (body on the trio's forte call) still holds at mf. The c119 rule (at most two winds on one string section) is unchanged.
**Fix** (tB.ily, bar 197; Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest, so each shared staff has one dynamic):
```
tBFluteOne: … | e'''4(\mf^\markup \bold "1." fis'''8 e'''8 dis'''8 e'''8 | cis'''4 gis''2) | …
tBOboeOne:  … | e''4(\mf^\markup \bold "1." fis''8 e''8 dis''8 e''8 | cis'''4 gis''2) | …
```
The diminuendo at 200 stays. Notes, rhythm and the 197–200 layering are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 125 and 281: woodwinds join the violins' second idea pp<, not p< (applied)
**Problem.** At 125 Fl 1 (C6) and Ob 1 (C5) join Vln I and Vln II at the unison on the second idea's second phrase, marked p< like the violins, and grow with them to f at 129 (c063). At 281 Ob 1 joins Vln II's C5 the same way. The violins have been playing alone since the sub. p at 121/277. At the soft dynamic the strings' purity matters most, so here a wind at the same marking costs the most.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 164, as in Finding 1. The rule matters most at the start of the build, where the wind's weight against the strings is greatest. c099's one-level rule (Casanelles) is the same principle, and the score already applies it at 29–35, 45 and 253 (Fl 1 p against Vln I mp).
**Fix.** sC.ily: `sCFluteOne` 125 `c'''2->(\pp\< a''4~`, `sCOboeOne` 125 `c''2->(\pp\<^\markup \bold "1."`. dA.ily: `dAOboeOne` 281 `c''2\pp\<->( a'4~`. The hairpins keep their targets (f at 129 and 285), so c063's layered growth still arrives together. MIDI velocity at 125 is now 49 for the winds against 73 for the violins (it was 57).

No further findings. Apart from these places, the textures follow the reading: the fugato is a conversation of one line per section; the lead line always has the most players; there are no high seconds in string chords; the trio's left-hand/right-hand layout has the cellos' counter line; the horns double the cellos in only three places; high reeds are saved for the coda peak; and the tutti strings are voiced simply and high.

## Considered and not changed

- **193–204, cellos doubling the basses on sustained roots** (row 11, Sebesky PDF 140, 146). This is the one long arco passage where the cellos only shadow the basses, between their counter line at 169–184, their answer at 189–192 and their counter line at 209–224. I tried two ways of freeing them. (a) Cellos in unison with the violas' turn at 193–196 (Ex. 202's unison low-string counter line): the bass is then F♯1/B1 alone, more than two octaves under the next part (Rimsky PDF 85, cited by c094). (b) Cellos on Bsn 1's mixture line C♯4–C4–B3–A3 at 201–204: the C♯ at 201 would then be in four parts. Neither is better than what is there, so I kept it.
- **195–196 into 197** (row 16, PDF 147). Every line falls or holds through the crescendo into the f deceptive cadence (B7 → C♯ minor). A rising inner line would have to take the leading tone D♯ away from E, so I did not add one.
- **105–120 tenor line with Hn 3·4** (row 31). Two horns on a cello line at ff is not "sparing". c097's reason still stands: one horn would be half the weight of each trombone around it.
