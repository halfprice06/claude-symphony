## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 201-204: the soft horn-bassoon chord has three pitches for six players and an empty tenor (applied)
**Problem.** After the trio's forte (197-200), the chord at 201-204 is held p/pp by two horns and two bassoons, with Cl 2 and the violas. Hn 4 plays C♯4-C4-B3-B3 in unison with Bsn 1. Cl 2 doubles them at 201, and then Hn 3 and the violas on E4/D♯4. The sounding pitches are:
- 201: A2 | C♯4 (Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 4, Vln II) | E4 (Hn 3, violas).
- 202: A2 | C4 ×2 | E4 ×4.
- 203: B2 | B3 ×2 | D♯4 ×3. No F♯ sounds anywhere in bar 203.

Between the bass A2 and C♯4 there is a tenth with nothing in it. My scan finds no other soft sustained wind chord in the movement with this many unisons.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 108: "Four-part harmony given to two bassoons and two horns, especially in soft passages, yields a finely-balanced tone recalling the effect of a quartet of horns." Here the four players make only three parts, because one horn duplicates a bassoon.
- PDF 108, same page: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." At 201-203 the moving chromatic line C♯-C-B is in a horn and a bassoon together.
- PDF 114: balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords".
- Ex. 152 (PDF 324): the held horn chord is the full basis, with every horn on its own pitch.

**Fix (applied, tB.ily, `tBHnFour` bars 17-20):**
```
cis'2.--(\pp | c'2.-- | b2.--\> | b2.--)   →   a2.--(\pp | a2.-- | fis2.--\> | fis2.--)
```
- Hn 4 now holds the root A3 under the A-major and A-minor chords and moves to F♯3, the missing fifth, under B and B7. It rises a fourth to its B3 at 205.
- Each chord now has four real wind parts: 201 A2-A3-C♯4-E4; 203 B2-F♯3-B3-D♯4; 204 B2-F♯3-A3-D♯4, with the seventh in Bsn 1.
- The bassoon alone carries the moving line, and the horns hold the stationary notes.
- c070's flat 6 to 5 (Vln I C5-B4) is still doubled an octave lower, in Bsn 1. c112's doubled third of VI at 201 (Cl 2 and Bsn 1 on C♯4) is kept.
- Hn 4 enters fresh at 201, so it adds no new voice-leading. The all-pairs scan shows no new parallels.
- The shared Hn 3·4 staff keeps equal pp dynamics and does not cross.

### 2. Bars 41-44 and 273-276: two forte horns hold the root and third under single clarinets on the seventh (applied)
**Problem.** In the D7 under the a2 theme (41-43, and the same in the reprise at 273-275):
- Hn 1·2 play F♯4 and D4 at f.
- The seventh C is only in Cl 1 (C5, f) at 41 and 42 beats 1-2, and in Bsn 1 (C4) at 43. Cl 2 has A4.
- The dynamics are equal: horns f, clarinets f, bassoons f.

So the defining note of the chord, its seventh, is the weakest one, under two f horns in the same octave.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 108-109: "Played forte, the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind; balance may be established by doubling the upper harmonic parts" (diagram on 109: 2 Fl + 2 Ob + 2 Cl over 4 Cor. f). PDF 112: "in difficult cases this may be secured by judicious dynamic grading, marking the wood-wind one degree louder than the brass". The score already does this at 101 (woodwind f, brass p/pp), at 197 (c047: horns mf under woodwind f) and at 45 (c084: horns mf). Bars 41-44 are the one place where the horns join a woodwind chord at the woodwind's own level. The fix also answers c059's open note about the loud arrivals before the trio: it takes some weight off the arrival at 41 without changing a note.

**Fix (applied):**
- sA.ily: `sAHnOne` bar 29 `fis'2.~\f` → `fis'2.~\mf`; `sAHnTwo` bar 29 `d'2.~\f` → `d'2.~\mf`. The now-repeated `\mf` at 45 is removed in both horns, so the level carries on.
- dA.ily: `dAHnOne` and `dAHnTwo` bar 21 (273), `\f` → `\mf`. At 277 the existing `\subP` follows.
- Both players are on the shared staff and stay equal. MIDI follows the staff dynamics (c124).

## Considered and left (my decisions)
- **79 and 87: the discord sits in Hn 3** (D♭4 of vii°7 of F at 79; C4, the seventh of D♯°7, at 87), against PDF 108's crossing rule. At 79 the D♭ is c042's chromatic line D-D♭-C in one voice. At 87 c132 prepared the seventh as a common tone from the Neapolitan. Swapping Hn 3 and Bsn 1 at 84-88 moves the problem: the seventh G3 of A♯°7 at 88 would then go to the horn, and the bassoon would sit above the horns. No change.
- **75-76 and 83: Bsn 1 D4 lies above Hn 3·4, with the bass clarinet below**, so the horns sit inside the reeds (PDF 108). I checked the alternatives on paper. Hn 3 C4→D4 makes parallel twelfths with the bass clarinet's F2→G2. Bsn 1 D3 below the horns gives a thick root-position G minor at G2-D3-G3. The F-major episode is protected (c144). No change.
- **45-56, 121-128, 277-284: clarinets inside the horns** (PDF 108). This is still protected by c014, c104 and c109, and their reason (Vln II's lower octave, the Hn 2 tenor fill) still holds.
- **105-120: Bsn 1 in unison with Hn 3·4 at ff.** It adds little ("in forte passages the horns overwhelm the bassoons", PDF 108), but the unison Horn + Bassoon is listed as a normal combination (PDF 106), and the tutti brass writing is protected (c086). No change.
- **276: F♯4 in five instruments on one crotchet.** This is a short chord, where resonance "is a minor consideration" (PDF 114). No change.
