## Findings

### 1. Theme A's authentic cadences at 20 and 28 end on a bare octave, and the V7's seventh does not resolve (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 19–20, 27–28 (sA, exposition).

**Problem:** Both PACs of theme A land on a hollow octave E. Vln I has E5 and the bass has E, with no 3rd in any part. The MIDI dump for bar 20 is `E1:E^E5 | B2:B | E2:E`. On the bar before, Vln II holds the V7's seventh A4 (19 and 27). It stops and does not resolve. So the two cadences that set out the tonic of the movement have neither a resolved seventh nor a tonic third.

**Evidence from the reading:**
- A&S PDF 98 (and Ex. 6-10): a chord may drop its 5th but needs its root and 3rd. The checklist on PDF 104 repeats "root and third present" and "7th of V7 resolves down by step".
- PDF 98–100: the 7th of V7 is a dissonance that must go down to 3̂.
- PDF 94: the PAC is the most conclusive cadence. Here it is the weakest-sounding chord in the theme.
- The score itself shows the intended version. The reprise of the same phrase resolves A→G in the cellos' tenor line (dA 259–260, 267–268), and the tutti cadences at 120 and 320 are complete.

**Answer to the earlier "special effect" reading (c002):** c002 called these unison cadences, a scherzo effect. A unison cadence makes sense only when the approach is in unison too. Here the dominant is harmonised as a full V7 and its seventh sounds right before the cadence. The open octave is therefore heard as a missing note, not an effect. That is clear because the reprise fills it in. Neither bar is on HISTORY.md's protected list.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-163/work/sections/sA.ily, `sAVlnTwo`):** bars 20 and 28 change from `R2.` to `g'4-. r4 r4`. The A4 of 19/27 now steps down to G4 on the cadence downbeat, and the cadence chord is E–G–E. The 3rd enters by step from 2̂ against a leaping bass, which A&S PDF 101 (Ex. 6-17) calls the good kind of doubled 3rd. The staccato quarter matches Vln II's existing articulation and dynamic (`pp` carries over), so no new dynamic is needed. The line now begins:

```lilypond
sAVlnTwo = { r4 g'4-.\pp g'4-. | r4 g'4-. g'4-. | r4 g'4-. g'4-. | r4 b4-. b4-. | r4 g'4-. g'4-. | r4 a'4-. a'4-. | r4 a'4-. a'4-. | g'4-. r4 r4 | r4 a'4-. a'4-. | ...
```

(Bar 28 makes the same change at index 16.)

### Points checked and left as they are (no change)

I decided all of these myself after checking them against the reading. None needs a change.

- **24→25, V7(♭9)→i with 2̂–1̂ in the top voice.** It sounds like a cadence in mid-phrase (A&S 115–116). But it falls on the same 4+4 phrase boundary as 16→17, so a cadence is right there. An I6 at 25 would create the V7–I6 hidden octaves that PDF 109 rules out. Keep it.
- **Long tonic spans 131–156, 223–242, 357–376.** Under A&S 102–103 these could count as static harmonic rhythm. But c063/c073/c133/c153 protect them, and Ex. 6-21/7-18 allow repetition across motivic groups, which these are. I have no new reason to give against the stated reasons. Keep them.
- **iv7 without its 3rd at 243.** This would breach PDF 98, but it is inside the protected retransition, where it is a voice-led passing sonority over the V pedal. Keep it.
- **288→289, E minor to G minor.** This is a direct phrase modulation (justified by c003). It falls on a phrase boundary, which PDF 117 treats as the right place for a change. Keep it.
- **Proportion and pacing.** Every section moves to a cadence within 16 bars or sits on a root-position pedal (PDF 112). The V pedal 89–104 and the retransition 241–252 are I–V–I expansions of the Kuhnau kind (PDF 93–95). The reprise is shortened, the coda has two stages and ends on a root-position PAC (PDF 129: no contrapuntal final cadence), and the length is 4:36. Nothing to cut or add.
