## What else I studied

## Holistic judgement against Sibelius 2/iii

A conductor who knows the Sibelius would take this for the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer in the Dvořák–Brahms line. It would not pass for Sibelius, and the brief does not ask it to. Points the two works share:
- a light string opening over a soft accompaniment (13–28);
- timpani used as a voice (89–100, motive x);
- held-back brass;
- a trio that sets a solo wind over soft sustained harmony (153–168).

Sibelius's strongest devices are the extreme tempo change for the trio, the attacca into the finale, and raw pp/ff blocks. I decided against importing any of them:
- **Trio tempo.** c129 rejected a slower trio for reasons of proportion, and the models (Dvořák 9/iii, Eroica) keep the dance pulse.
- **Attacca.** The scherzo has to end ppp on G♯ for the pause before the finale. That is a cyclic rule.
- **pp/ff blocks.** The score already uses the Slavonic Dance pattern of a loud tutti followed at once by a soft tune (Adler PDF 209), at 72→73 and 120→121.

Sibelius's one device that does carry over is the long crescendo built by adding layers. That is Adler's "orchestrated crescendo" (PDF 201–202, 213), and this score uses it at every approach but one (finding 1).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 205–208: the crescendo into T's forte return is left to hairpins, and Bsn 1 does not take part (applied)

- **Problem.** 209 is the trio's loudest arrival: T at f in Cl 1 and the violins, with seven parts entering. The approach at 205–208 has the same instruments throughout (Fl 2/Cl 2 on the motto-tail line, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4, violas, cellos, pizz. basses, timpani roll), and only the hairpins at 207–208 carry the crescendo. Bsn 1 holds F♯3 pp with no hairpin at all, while the violas, in unison with it since c172, crescendo on the same F♯3. The other approaches in the movement are built by entries: 89–104 (winds at 101, brass inside the crescendo), 193–197 (Hn 1·2 at 195) and 321–336 (motive x layered from the bass).
- **Evidence.**
  - Adler PDF 201, point 3: a crescendo "is made clearer when actually written out in an orchestral score".
  - Ex. XVI-5 (PDF 202): Beethoven adds horns "p cresc." and then "Bassi pp cresc." under the line before the ff.
  - PDF 213 on Bizet: "Just as he orchestrated a diminuendo in the beginning, he did the same for the crescendo, mm. 13–18" (Ex. PDF 215: the winds enter one after another).
  - The same page praises the 4th horn that "sustains the pedal" while other instruments carry the rhythm. At 205–207 the basses only pluck the B.
- **Fix** (tB.ily). Bsn 1 crescendos with the violas. Bsn 2 and the contrabassoon join the B pedal at 207 pp, crescendo, and lead into their own entries at 209 (Bsn 2 re-strikes B2, Cbsn rises to E2). No pitch of the motto-tail line, the horns or the strings changes.

  On the shared bassoon staff both players get pp< together (equal dynamics), and the hairpin ends on 209's printed mf. The contrabassoon reaches its own mp at 209. Bsn 1 still breathes at 208 beat 3 (c066). The pizzicato basses keep their rest at 208 for the change to arco (c086).

### 2. Bar 271: a crescendo hairpin on a single pizzicato note, running over a bar of rests (applied)

- **Problem.** c175 gave Vln II time for pizz.→arco by making beat 3 of 271 a rest. That left `r4 g'4\pp\< r4 | R2.`: a hairpin that starts on one plucked note, which cannot grow, and runs under the rest bar 272 to the arco f at 273. This was in neither v169 nor earlier versions.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 224, on Ravel: he is "especially careful to notate even the slightest details of articulation as well as decay and cessation of a pitch". The notation should show what the player does. The violas keep their hairpin, because they have two pizzicato notes that can grow.
- **Fix** (dA.ily, dAVlnTwo, bar 271): `r4 g'4\pp r4` (hairpin removed). Bar 273's f arco is unchanged.

**Points I considered and kept:**
- **Hn 2 at 361–364.** It holds E4 pp without the diminuendo the rest of the chord has. It shares a staff with Hn 1's continuing G♯4 (equal-dynamics rule), and the passage is protected (357–376).
- **The violas' hairpin over 272.** Two pizzicato notes can make a crescendo.
- **Open strings in the tutti chords** (Adler PDF 209). The ff chords at 72, 300 and 339 already use the open G, D, A and E strings in the inner parts.
- **Sibelius's tempo contrast, attacca and pp/ff blocks.** Not imported, for the reasons in the judgement section above.
