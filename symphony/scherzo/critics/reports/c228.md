## Findings

No score change. Each principle in the assigned reading either holds in v228 or does not apply to this style. Below are the candidates I examined and my decision on each.

### 1. Bars 276→277: forte straight into sub. p in the same strings (no change)
- **Bars:** 276–277 (reprise, before the E-minor second idea).
- **Question:** In the exposition, the tutti ends on beat 1 of 120 and two beats' rest come before the sub. p at 121. In the reprise, Vln I and the cor anglais play three f crotchets (F♯–D♯–B over V) and Vln II and the violas play the off-beats, all up to the barline. The same violins then start the second idea sub. p at 277. Adler PDF 190 (Ex. XV-36) says the most effective forte subito piano has a second group enter under the loud chord and sustain at p.
- **Decision:** Keep. The f bar is staccato, with a gap after every crotchet, so the strings have no held loud chord to cover. At 277 the soft group (Cl 1·2, Bsn 2, Hn 2, Hn 3, violas and cellos) enters together with the violins, so the p sonority is complete on the downbeat. Adler's overlap answers a loud *held* chord. Making it overlap here would mean adding notes over 276 and changing the bar's harmony, and nothing in the reading asks for that.

### 2. Bars 277–280: the second idea's first phrase has no swell, unlike every other statement (no change)
- **Bars:** 277–280 (compare 45–48, 121–124, 281–284).
- **Question:** Every other two-bar unit of the second idea swells to its leap (`\<` … `\>`). At 277, Vln I and II hold the sub. p level without a hairpin.
- **Decision:** Keep. This is c039's plan: the third statement starts sub. p and grows from 281, adding Ob 1 at 281 and Fl 1 at 285 (c044). Holding the level at 277 is what Adler PDF 190 asks of a subito piano: the soft group "simply sustains at that level". A swell at 277 would also take away the contrast with 281.

### 3. Bar 72: ff> with no printed target before the F-major pp (no change)
- **Bars:** 72–73.
- **Question:** In Adler Ex. XV-35 the long notes read sf>pp, with the target printed. At 72 the woodwinds and strings have ff> to the barline with no target, and the new group enters pp at 73.
- **Decision:** Keep. Hn 1·2 carry the decrescendo into the pp at 73 under the F-major group (c010, c028), so the target is printed on the continuing voice. The other parts stop at the barline. The double bar, the key change and the new pp group say the rest, as in Dvořák's `fz>` notation. A printed p at the end of 72 in sixteen staves would add clutter and no information.

### 4. Bars 336–338: horns and trumpets under the fff (no change)
- **Question:** In Tchaikovsky's arrival (PDF 39–40) every part is fff, the brass included.
- **Decision:** Keep the horns ff and the trumpets f (c194, c199, c200). Dan's rule in the BRIEF (soft brass, weight not bite) answers the reading here. The brass hold and re-attack long tenuto notes, which is what the image shows, one or two degrees under the rest of the orchestra.
