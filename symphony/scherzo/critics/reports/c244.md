## Findings

All four are checked points. I applied none of them, for the reasons given.

### 1. Bars 333–335 and 101–104: four horns on four pitches above mf (Adler PDF 15). No change.
Adler's rule is to double the horns above mf. At 333–335 the horns take B4, G4, E4 and B3 one each at ff, and on the even bars (334) the trumpets are silent. At 101–104 the horns again take four pitches, F♯4, D♯4, B3 and F♯3. In both places the horns are marked ff while the trumpets and trombones are marked f. One dynamic level is the usual way to make up for one horn against one trumpet. The chord is also complete and well spaced from E1 up (row 3), and each horn note is doubled at the octave by a trombone or trumpet or by a woodwind. Pairing the horns would leave two chord tones without brass, or it would need ff brass marks that Dan's rule rules out. Keep.

### 2. Bars 305–320: the reprise's tenor countermelody has one horn, where the exposition has two (Adler PDF 15, 44). No change.
At 105–120, Hn 3 and Hn 4 play the tenor line in unison, which Adler counts as one trombone's weight (Bartók, PDF 44). At 305–320, Hn 4 holds the bass an octave up instead (c115), because the reprise has no trombones or tuba (c033). That leaves the countermelody to Hn 3, Bsn 1 and the cellos (espress.). This follows the plan: the reprise tutti is meant to be lighter than the exposition's so that the coda's low brass at 333 are new (row 8). The line still has a horn, a bassoon and a full cello section in its best register. Putting Hn 4 back on the tenor line would leave the reprise tutti with no brass bass, which is what c115 corrected (Rimsky's octave above the bass in every tutti). Keep.

### 3. Bar 105: the brass chord has no B between G3 and E4 (Adler Ex. XI-1, PDF 15–16). No change.
At the downbeat of 105 the brass sound E1, E2, E3, G3 (three players), E4 and B4, which leaves a sixth with no brass note inside the chord. Adler's ff chords keep the middle close. Here the violas' double stops and the clarinets and cor anglais fill that octave, and Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1 move on the tenor line from 106, so the "chord" lasts one crotchet. Moving a horn to B3 would take it off the countermelody (c097) or off the E4 that carries the root above the trombones. Keep.

### 4. Bars 89–100: the brass could punctuate the timpani's motive x (Hindemith, PDF 42–44). No change.
Hindemith ends his fugato with the timpani stating the subject and the brass striking chords in its rests. The scherzo has the same arrangement at 89–100, with pizz. cellos and basses instead of brass. The pp timpani alone with the strings is what prepares the build at 101, where the trumpets take over motive x and the brass enter for the first time (rows 8 and 10). c197 tested this and kept it. Adding brass here would spend the trombones' first entry eight bars early. Keep.
