## Findings

### 1. Bars 37–40: the choirs join without the call's oboe, and two flutes carry a soft line in unison (applied)

**Problem.** The counterstatement is a dialogue. Fl 1 and Ob 1 in unison call theme A at 29–30 and 33–34 over a pp clarinet–bassoon chord. The strings answer at 31–32 and 35–36 (Vln I, cellos an octave lower). At 37 the plan says winds and strings go "together in G". In the score, Ob 1 drops out at 37 and Fl 2, which has not played yet, joins Fl 1 in unison, p <, with Vln I. At 39–40 the flutes leave the violins and carry the upper octave alone (G5–D6–B5 | A5–F♯5–D5). So the bars that should join the two colours already heard bring a third colour instead (two flutes), and the line that the flutes carry alone is a soft like-wind unison. At 41 Ob 1 comes back together with Ob 2, Fl 2 is already there, and the f arrival gains less than it could.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 253 (Schumann 1/iii, Ex. VIII-19): "whenever the choirs are first used separately and then together, a most satisfying sense of completion or resolution results". PDF 270 (Copland, Ex. VIII-41): "a completely restful summation of two previous phrases, one for strings alone, the other for winds alone. At this point they come together". The completion depends on hearing the same two colours: here the call colour is Fl 1 + Ob 1, which Adler PDF 236 (Ex. VIII-2) describes as the oboe "certainly" standing out while "the flute will somewhat neutralize the nasal quality of the oboe and give the passage a rounder, richer sound". PDF 249–250: two like winds "can never be absolutely in tune with one another, the overtone balance is upset, the sound thickened … a unison solo of two like winds limits and impedes the expressive quality", which he limits to "ordinary solo passages, not tutti sections where simple volume is required". 37–40 is p; volume is needed only from 41. PDF 238: Schubert's flute "enters almost undetected … doubling the oboe for the crescendo": the crescendo is made by entries. c414's MIDI scan listed 37–44 as a build; the f part (41–44) is one, but 37–40 is not.

- `sAFluteTwo`: `R2.*16 | R2.*8 | g''4-.\p\< … | a''4-. fis''-. d''-. | a''4-.\f` becomes `R2.*16 | R2.*12 | a''4-.\f`. Fl 2 enters f at 41 with Ob 2 and the cor anglais.

The oboe line lies at D5–D6, the oboe's best register, and Ob 1 has the 35–36 rest before it and the 44 rest after it, before its espress. at 45. Both shared staves have equal dynamics (Fl 1 and Ob 1 p< at 37; all four f at 41). The c107 problem (Fl 2 holding notes inside the theme) does not return; c111's balance at 41–43 (Vln II with Vln I against four winds) is unchanged, and at 37–40 the string line has two winds on it as before. Audible result: at 37 the oboe's colour from the call is heard on top of the violins, so the dialogue resolves into its two voices; 39–40 is one flute and one oboe instead of two flutes; 41 adds three winds, a step a listener hears.

### 2. A6 is spent long before the trio's peak (not applied; record correction)

Adler PDF 238 rejects scorings that "anticipate the expansion of the registers". c477 and c344 describe A6 as "first heard at 218". The scan finds A6 at 50 (second idea, Fl 1 + Vln I), 110 (tutti flutes), 218, five times in Fl 1's p heterophony at 254–266, at 306–318 and at 335–337; B6 is only at 336. The trio's A6 is therefore not a new register, but the movement's top note B6 is still held back, which is the part of Schubert's rule that matters for the form. The heterophony is protected (c108) and comes after the trio. No change; the coherence record should stop calling 218 the first A6.

### 3. Bars 41–43: six parts on A5, the cor anglais alone on A4 (considered, kept)

PDF 250–251 prefers 8-foot plus 4-foot to more 8-foot stops. The one player that could take the lower octave without range trouble is Vln II. But c111 put Vln II on the unison because four winds on Vln I alone overpower it (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76), and Adler's reason does not answer that. Kept.

### 4. Bars 13–28: no pedal under the first statement (checked, kept)

The Fledermaus page (PDF 245) recommends a soft pedal under a spiccato tune. The score uses it at 29–40 and lifts it at 31–32 and 35–36 exactly as Strauss does, then holds it through 37–40 into the f (PDF 245: "its omission later on contributes to the tension"). The bare first statement keeps the winds' entry at 29 new (PDF 260; c259, c359, c414). Kept.

### 5. Bars 169–176: Hn 3 in unison with the cellos' T (checked, kept)

PDF 257 and 265 prefer octave doublings to unison ones. c431's reason is more specific: Adler II PDF 27, "usually the horn and cello double on long sustained melodies", and it ties T to the horn call. Kept.

### 6. Tuttis 105–120, 305–320, 333–339 (checked, follow)

### 7. Bars 225–236: the codetta's heads are a relay, not overlapping imitation (checked, kept)
