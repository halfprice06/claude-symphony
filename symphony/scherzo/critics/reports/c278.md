## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 29 (and its repeat at 33): the winds' first statement of the head has no "leggiero" (applied)

**Problem.** At 29 Fl 1 and Ob 1, in unison, play theme A's head in staccato quavers at p. This is the first time the winds have the theme. The strings at 13 are marked "p leggiero", and so is Fl 1 when the theme returns at 253. The winds at 29 have only p. This matters most for the oboe. Adler (PDF 211) ranks its staccato as the most pointed of the woodwinds, and at E5-B4 it is in its "warm, prominent, reedy" register (PDF 198, Ex. VII-32), so a plain p staccato will be played full and pointed. The call and response at 29-36 then sounds heavier in the winds than in the strings it answers.

**Evidence.** Every fast, light wind staccato that Adler prints in this chapter pairs p with a character word. Mendelssohn 4/iv has "p leggiero" with accents on the metre (PDF 189, Ex. VII-11). Berlioz's two oboes in *Symphonie fantastique* v have "p leggiero" (PDF 201, Ex. VII-38). Bartók's bassoons have "p staccato" (PDF 229, Ex. VII-96). The score already follows this practice at 13 (strings), 253 (Fl 1) and 269 (Bsn 1, "scherz.").

**Fix (sections/sA.ily).** The word goes on the first note at 29 in each staff. It still holds when the same figure returns at 33 after the strings' answer, so it is not repeated there.
```
sAFluteOne = { R2.*16 | e''4-.\p^\markup \italic "leggiero" b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | ...
sAOboeOne  = { R2.*16 | e''4-.\p^\markup \italic "leggiero" b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | ...
```
It prints above "1.", as at 253. I checked page 2 and there is no collision. It is not a tempo word, so no `\mt` is needed.

### 2. Bars 185-192: the trio's two calls end flat on their held notes (applied)

**Problem.** The trio's middle part opens with a call in Ob 1, p espress. (185-188), and an answer a step lower in the cor anglais doubled by the cellos, p dolce (189-192). Each call ends with a rising A-B-C♯ (answer G♯-A-B) that falls to a held dotted minim. Neither call has any hairpin. So the oboe's G♯4 at 188 is held at full p while the cor anglais enters under it at 189, and the answer's F♯3 at 192 is held at full p into the strings' entry at 193. The third call (Fl 1/Ob 1, 197-200) already has a diminuendo on its last bar. The first two are the only exposed wind solos in the movement whose held note has no shape: c178 shaped T's held peaks, and c158 shaped the codetta heads.

**Evidence.** The cor anglais solos on Adler's pages are the closest models for this call, and every one of them shapes its held notes. Berlioz, *Roman Carnival*, has "mf espressivo" with `<` under the rising notes and `>` after (PDF 205, Ex. VII-48). Wagner, *Tristan* III, has `p< f dim.` (PDF 206, Ex. VII-49). Sibelius, *The Swan of Tuonela*, has `ff >< dim.` and `mf< ... p dim.` (PDF 206, Ex. VII-50). Adler's bassoon example from Tchaikovsky 6/i gives each two-bar group its own `<>`, rising pp, p, mp (PDF 227, Ex. VII-93). The trio's calls already rise by level (p, p, then mf at 197, c167), but without the hairpin inside each call.

**Fix (sections/tB.ily).** A crescendo through the rising third bar, then a decrescendo on the held note. The peak is a hidden mp, which is the c138/c178 convention, so the MIDI follows the printed shape. The cellos double the cor anglais in unison and get the same hairpins. Their p at 193 ends the hairpin.
```
tBOboeOne:    ... cis''2 gis'4) | a'4.(\< b'8 cis''4) | gis'2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | R2.\! | ...
tBCorAnglais: ... b2 fis4) | gis4.(\< a8 b4) | fis2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | R2.\! | ...
tBVc:         ... b2 fis4) | gis4.(\< a8 b4) | fis2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | fis,2.\p\< | ...
```
Ob 2 rests throughout, so the shared oboe staff still has one set of dynamics. The c091 turn and the c117/c167 layering at 197-200 are unchanged.

## Considered and not changed

- The hemiola at 53-55, 129-131 and 285-287. The winds that double it are slurred over the whole hemiola, while the strings re-bow each minim (c085). Adler (PDF 187) treats wind slurs as phrase and breath marks, and c078 settled that winds do not copy bowings. The minims are audible by their length, so I left it.
- The cor anglais E♭5/C5 at ff (103, 116, 303, 316). This is the upper register that "loses its personality" (PDF 205), but here the instrument doubles inside a tutti and is not a solo.
- Bsn 1 F♯4 at 211-216, in the bassoon's top fifth that "does not project" (PDF 226). It is an inner part of the soft horn-bassoon choir (c014/c247), not a line meant to be heard.
- Cl 1 G4 (written B♭4, the throat tone at the break) at 83. It is slurred and at p (PDF 210). c078 already looked at this and left it.
- No "leggiero" on the retransition head entries, Ob 1 at 92 and Cl 1 at 245. These are pp imitative entries inside a build, not a new statement, and the word at 29 already sets the winds' manner.
- Fl 1 A6 at p in 254-266. It is approached by step, and it is protected (c036, c178).
