## Findings

### 1. Flutes 1·2, 105–108, 305–308 and 316: part-combine labels in the wrong places, and a reprise bar that differs from the exposition (applied)
**Problem.** At 105 and 305 Fl 1 plays G5 and Fl 2 E5 on the downbeat only, then both are in unison. The combiner prints two noteheads on beat 1 and "a2" over beat 2. At 108 and 308 Fl 1 rests on beat 3 while Fl 2 plays B4, so "2." appears over one crotchet, and the next bar splits into octaves with no label. At 316 Fl 1 has `c'''2->( b''4)` but Fl 2 `c'''2->( b''8 fis''8)`, so one notehead carries two accents (above and below) and two slurs. At 116 both flutes play `b''8 fis''8`. The 316 difference has no comment and looks like an accident.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 9: the operator must judge the software's output. PDF 13: be consistent. PDF 65: unisons share a notehead and each part's markings stay on its side. PDF 51: parts share a rest where they can.
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily, `sCFluteOne`/`dCFluteOne` first note `e''4-.` (was `g''4-.`). `sCFluteTwo`/`dCFluteTwo` bar 4 `fis''4-. dis''-. r4` (was `b'-.`), so both flutes breathe before Fl 1's leap to E6 at 109/309. `dCFluteOne` bar 12 `c'''2->( b''8 fis''8)` (was `b''4)`), as at 116. Result: one "a2" on the downbeat of 105, 305 and 313, and no mid-bar labels. B4 on beat 3 of 108/308 is still played by Ob 1, Cl 2, cor anglais and the violins.

### 2. Flutes 1·2 and Clarinets 1·2, 209–211: a G♯ minim from the second player makes split voices and a late "1." (applied)
**Problem.** Fl 2 and Cl 2 finish their 205–208 line with `gis2 r4` at 209, the same pitch as the first player's entry with T. The staff splits into two voices for two bars: a crotchet rest under beat 3 and a whole-bar rest in 210. "1." then appears at 211, two bars after the solo began. A player reading 209 cannot tell from the label who has the tune.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 50–51: different durations need separate rests, and when this gets complicated the notation should be simplified. PDF 9: errors in layout cost rehearsal time.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily: `tCFluteTwo = { R2.*32 | }` and `tCClTwo = { R2.*16 | e'2.\pp ...` (the `gis2\f r4` bar removed). Fl 2's and Cl 2's A at 208 now resolves to G♯ at the same pitch in Fl 1 and Cl 1. "1." now prints at 209 on both staves, with no split voices. I tried `\partCombineUnisono` then `\partCombineSoloI` in mid-bar first. It broke T's opening slur (the slur cannot cross from the shared voice to the solo voice), so I rejected it.

### 3. Harp RH, 348–352: the treble clef comes back right before 16 bars of rest (applied)
**Problem.** The RH moves to bass clef at 341 for the chords. `\clef treble` follows the last chord at 348, so the new clef prints in mid-system ahead of the rests. The next RH entry is 365.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 23 ("after periods of rests"): put the change at the end of the system after the player has finished. That is less disruptive and better than a change immediately before the rests.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily, `codaHarpR`: `... r4 <e gis b>4 <e gis b>4 | R2.*3 \clef treble R2.*9 |`. The treble clef now prints at the end of the system that ends with 351 (page 12) and at the start of page 13.

### 4. Horns 3·4, 116 and 316: Hn 4's breath mark is printed on Hn 3's side (applied)
**Problem.** c046 gave Hn 4 a breath in its 16-bar legato line (lower voice). The comma prints at the top of the stave beside Hn 3's staccato crotchet, so it looks like Hn 3's breath. Hn 3 already has rests in every bar.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 39 (keep indications next to their noteheads) and PDF 65 (in double-stemmed writing each part keeps its own side).
**Fix (applied).** sC.ily `sCHnFour` and dC.ily `dCHnFour`: `b2 a4) \once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN \breathe`. The comma now sits by Hn 4's C♯/A at the bottom of the stave.
