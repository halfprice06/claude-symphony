## Judgement of the melodies

After 100 cycles the tunes are in good shape, and this reading finds no fault in their notes. Theme A's motive x and chromatic tail and T's rising head with its 4–3 cadence are both easy to sing back. The way the score presents them follows Rimsky-Korsakov's practice almost everywhere. High lines are doubled an octave below in Vln II. Each tune starts undoubled and gains woodwind doublings on its later statements. The coda peak is in double octaves with wind support. Two places remain where the reading's rules are not met: the lower octave of the melody that leads into the tutti reprise of theme A, and the bass under T's loudest statement.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 101–104 and 301–304: the hemiola melody into theme A's tutti has no string on its lower octave. APPLIED.

**Problem.** The four-bar hemiola that launches both tutti statements of theme A is the retransition's melody: B5–A5 | A5–F♯5 | D♯6–B5 | B5–A5, f< to ff (sfp< in the reprise). Vln I and Fl 1 play it in unison. Its lower octave is Ob 2 alone. Vln II moves between F♯5 and D♯5, but Fl 2 (F♯5) and Ob 1 (D♯5) already hold those two notes in the same rhythm frame. At its top the line reaches D♯6, so it is a high violin line under brass and timpani at ff. c087 noted the point and left it open: "Vln II and the violas carry the inner hemiola voices, which matter more."

**Evidence.**
- PDF 58–59: Vln I and II in octaves is "a very common process ... in particular [for] those in the very high register". High violin figures "become too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves", and the doubling "secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre".
- PDF 62, Note I: melodies "exceeding the middle of the 5th octave are generally doubled an octave below". This line runs from F♯5 to D♯6.
- Ex. 8 (PDF 182) and Ex. 32 (PDF 210): in forte cantabile the winds hold the harmony while the violin sections all take the melody in octaves. Ex. 22 (PDF 198) does the same at p.
- PDF 63: unequal numbers in the two octaves (here Vln I + Fl 1 above, Vln II + Ob 2 below) do not matter.
- Answer to c087's reason: Vln II's notes are F♯5 and D♯5, which Fl 2 and Ob 1 hold through these same bars (Ob 1 D♯5 at 101–103, Fl 2 F♯5 at 101–103, then F♯5/D♯5 at 104). The violas keep their own rising inner line D♯4–F♯4–A4–B4. No harmony note and no hemiola rhythm is lost.

**Fix (sB.ily `sBVlnTwo`, dB.ily `dBVlnTwo`, last four bars; comments added).**
```
101–104:  b'2\f\< a'4~ | a'4 fis'2 | dis''2 b'4~ | b'4 a'2\ff | }
301–304:  b'2\sfp\< a'4~ | a'4 fis'2 | dis''2 b'4~ | b'4 a'2\ff | }
```
Dynamics and hairpins are unchanged. Vln II now plays in unison with Ob 2, which Rimsky-Korsakov's string-plus-wind unisons support (PDF 57). Vln II crosses under the violas' A4 for two beats of 102, an inner crossing that does not affect the top octave. Bowing is unchanged: c085's one bow per minim unit and c096's split at 300 still apply. The analyser's outer-voice parallels are unchanged (7). An all-pairs scan finds only the new octave doubling itself, plus Vln II joining Vln I's existing E→B at 300→301. The harmony and brass at 101–104 (c093, c097) are untouched.

### 2. 209–224: the bass of T's forte return is in the double basses alone. APPLIED.

**Problem.** At 209 the cellos take their countermelody and Bsn 2 doubles them (c087). The bass line, dotted minims F♯1–E2 at mf, rising to f at 219, is then played only by the basses, arco, with the harp's left hand plucking each downbeat. This is the trio's loudest statement of its tune. Everywhere else in the movement a sustained bass is played by cellos and basses together, or by basses with Bsn 2 or the contrabassoon (45, 121, 277, 281). c094 left "bass in one octave only" open, because an upper-octave doubling would cross the cellos at 209, 216 and 222–224.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 54: the double bass is "dull in tone and of little flexibility, chiefly employed in unison or in octaves with the violoncellos".
- PDF 56: "In my own compositions there is no phrase of any importance given to the double bass without the support of 'cellos or bassoons."
- Ex. 10 and Ex. 11 (PDF 185, viewed): the basses are supported by the contrabassoon in unison. Ex. 11 is forte, the dynamic of this passage.
- PDF 61 f: the bass is normally built in cellos and basses together. Here the cellos are needed for the countermelody, so the bassoon family is the support Rimsky-Korsakov names.
- A unison doubling adds nothing above the basses, so it cannot cross the cellos. This answers c094's reason for leaving the point open.

**Fix (tC.ily, new variable `tCCbsn`; parts.ily already calls it; comment added).**
```
tCCbsn = { e,2.--(\mp | e,2.-- | b,,2.-- | e,2.-- | a,,2.-- | fis,,2.-- | fis,,2.-- | b,,2 fis,,4) \breathe | cis,2.--(\< | gis,,2.-- | fis,,2.--\mf\> | a,,2.-- | b,,2.--\mp | b,,2.-- | e,2.~\> | e,2)\! r4 | R2.*16 | }
```
The contrabassoon plays the basses' notes at sounding pitch (F♯1–E2, its strong middle register). It is a degree softer (mp, mf at 219), as c067 set for doubling winds. Its phrase slurs, tenuto and breath after 216 match the horns and bassoons on c088's pattern. It rests from 225, so the pp codetta keeps its pizzicato bass. The contrabassoon has not played since 132, so the colour is fresh. Its first entry in the trio comes at the trio's loudest point.
