## Findings

### 1. Bars 217–224: the trio's countermelody has no brass and gains nothing as the climax nears (applied)

**Problem.** At T's forte return, T is in Vln I, Vln II and Cl 1, and Fl 1 joins it at 217. The cellos' countermelody is doubled only by Bsn 2 at mf. The horn colour that the scherzo gives its tenor line at 105–120 (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4) is missing. Hn 3·4 rest from 209 to 228. At 217, where the phrase climbs to the f/ff peak at 219, the main element gets a new colour (Fl 1). The countermelody does not.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 178, on *Meistersinger* (Ex. XV-26): "The color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument." Its second theme puts the "octave-lower cellos plus Horn I doubled at pitch".
- PDF 184 (*Schelomo*): "As the climax nears, the composer does add the contrabassoon to the main theme, all the flutes to the middleground", which means an instrument is added to each element, not only to the tune.
- PDF 176: "each theme must be heard at good strength".

c314 tested a horn on T at 209 and kept T without one: at pitch it would pass written C6, and an octave lower it would cross Hn 2's held E4–A4. Neither objection applies to the countermelody. It lies at E3–A3 down to A2 (written B3–E4 down to E3), under Hn 2 and inside the fourth horn's low register (Adler PDF 303: II and IV are the low horns). c251 saves the horn colour of *T* for 341, and this line is not T. The foreground/background plan protected since c304 is unchanged: T stays the foreground, one level louder, and the countermelody stays middleground. The horn is marked one level under the cellos, so it adds weight and no attack, as Dan's soft-brass rule asks.

I start the horn at 217, not 209. Hn 4 has just held the B pedal at 201–208, and joining at 217 matches the Fl 1 entry on T in the same bar, so both elements gain a colour together, as in PDF 184.

### 2. Bars 325–332: should each ostinato entry be coloured by winds, as in Mozart's coda? (checked, kept)

Mozart's coda (PDF 176, Ex. XV-24) brings its entries in cellos → violas → Vln II → Vln I, which is this coda's order. Each entry has its own wind colour. Adler is explicit, however, that Mozart "doubles only the *cantus firmus*-type first theme, while the other tunes are brought in, played by different sections of the string choir". Here the quick figure is motive x, a one-bar ostinato (c277: "one figure doubled in four registers"). The long-note element is the falling bass E–D–C–B at 329–332, and that bass already has the winds (Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.cl.). The passage therefore follows the Mozart model. Adding winds at 325–328 would also break Rimsky-Korsakov's order for a long crescendo (strings, then woodwind, then brass; PDF 130), which the coda now follows (c308). Kept.

### 3. Bars 57–68: strings-only fugato against Mozart's coloured entries (checked, kept)

Mozart's coloured entries come in a coda that develops material already exposed (PDF 175–176). The scherzo's fugato is an exposition. c007's reason (adler1 p. 143: a fugato is exposed in the strings "before it is developed, colored, or doubled") is not answered by this reading. The colours arrive one per entry at the stretto 69–71 (Fl 1+Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1+Hn 1·2). This follows PDF 176 (each entry differently coloured) and PDF 172 (colour held back for the build). The three strands at 65–68 already differ in rhythm and articulation (PDF 175, 178), and the dynamics place the subject one level above the others (PDF 184). Kept.

### 4. Bars 349–356: no horn on the coda's countermelody (checked, kept)

After Finding 1, someone could ask for the same horn at 349. c314's reasons still hold: the coda thins towards the strings after the horn's T at 341, and Hn 1's motto tail at 357 needs the horn colour fresh. With Finding 1 applied, 349 differs from 209–224 in one more respect, which suits PDF 176 and Adler's Mahler rule. Kept.
