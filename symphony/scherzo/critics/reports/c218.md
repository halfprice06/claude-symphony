## Findings

### 1. The hemiola cadences at 53–55 and 129–131 are now bowed the reverse of c085's plan (applied)
**Bars:** Vln I, Vln II and violas, 51–56 and 127–132.
**Problem:** c085 bowed the hemiola cadences `d'''4 b''4( g''4) | a''2 fis''4( | a''4) g''2` so that the first crotchet and the two minims (54 beats 1–2, 55 beats 2–3) fall on down-bows. The record says "each hemiola minim falls on a down-bow" and protects this. Then c118 marked V at 45 and 121 so that the second idea's swells go with the bow. Each statement has four two-bar slurs, an even number of bows, so that V carries on to 53 and 129. Now 53 and 129 start on an up-bow, and both minims and the cadence minim at 55 (and 131) are up-bows. That gives the light end of the bow to the stressed units of the hemiola and to the tonic arrival. c118 checked only the bars before 45 and 121. The reprise shows the difference. There the statement starts on beat 3 of 277, with ⊓, and the hemiola at 285–288 still bows as c085 planned: 285 b''4 ⊓, 286 fis''2 ⊓, 288 e''2 ⊓. The violas are bowed the same way as the violins: at 54 and 129–131 their minims are up-bows too.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; PDF 37 (Ex. II-19, Schubert 5/ii): the bow signs are arranged so that each accented downbeat gets ⊓. PDF 38: "a performer will naturally begin an anacrusis … with an up-bow", and players take two bows in one direction to get "a down-bow on the accented first beat of the next measure". In both places the bar before the hemiola ends on an anticipating quaver (52 `e'''4. d'''8`, 128 `b''4. cis'''8`) that is a natural upbeat. Taking it as its own up-bow puts 53 and 129 on a down-bow, with no retake and no added sign.
**Fix (applied):** I changed slurs only. No notes, signs or dynamics changed.
- sA.ily `sAVlnOne` 52: `fis'''4-\tweak stencil ##f \f e'''4.) d'''8 |` (was `e'''4. d'''8)`). `sAVlnTwo`: `e''4.) d''8`. `sAVla` 51–52: `a'2( d'4~ | d'4) g'2 |` (was `d'4 g'2)`).

The simulation now gives 52 quaver V, 53 ⊓, (b g) V, 54 a''2 ⊓, (fis a) V, 55 g''2 ⊓. The same holds for 129–131, and the violas match. c118's up-bow swells at 45–52 and 121–128 are unchanged. This restores c085's protected bowing. It does not change it.

### 2. Trio cello countermelody, 175–184 and 215–224: the swell goes against the bow and against the violins (applied)
**Bars:** Vc 175–184 (p) and 215–224 (mf to f).
**Problem:** c158 shaped the cellos' countermelody: `e4(\< gis2~ | gis4 e2) | a2(\> gis4 | e2.)`, which swells into 179 (219) with the tune's climax and then fades. Two-bar slurs from 169 (209) put 177 (217) on a down-bow, so the crescendo moves toward the tip and the diminuendo from 179 (219) toward the frog. c118 rebowed the violins over the same bars for this reason: crescendo V at 177, the climax A6 ⊓ at the frog at 178 beat 3. So at the movement's most expressive bars the violins and cellos bow in opposite directions. At 219 the cellos play f against the violins' ff, so their arrival on the tip of an up-bow is the weakest part of the bow.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "the tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … a diminuendo with a down-bow". The composer should mark the bow "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". A habit is easier to put in place by slurring than by signs. PDF 37 (Ex. II-19): the bowing is planned so that the strong note gets the strong bow. PDF 54: the up-bow before the pizz. at 225 must stay.
**Fix (applied):** I changed slurs only. The countermelody's crotchet upbeat takes its own bow.
- tA.ily `tAVc` 175–176: `cis2.( | dis2) fis4 | e4(\< gis2~` (was `dis2 fis4)`). Now 176 beat 3 is ⊓, 177 V with the crescendo, 179 ⊓ with the diminuendo, 181 V, and 183 ⊓ with the last diminuendo.
- tC.ily `tCVc` 215–216: `cis2.( | fis2) b,4 | e4(\< gis2~`; 221–222: `b,2(\mf cis4 | a,4) b,2 | b,2.~\upbow\>`. Now 217 is V, 219 ⊓ with the f diminuendo, 221 V, 222 b,2 ⊓, and the marked V at 223 (c096, before the pizz. at 225) comes naturally, with no hook.

The tune, the hairpins and Bsn 2's doubling are unchanged.

### 3. Violas' repeated notes under a slur with no louré dashes, 45–46, 127–128, 283–284 (applied)
**Bars:** Vla 45–46 (`g'2( g'4~ | g'4 g'2)`), 127–128 (`b2( b4~ | b4 e'2)`), 283–284 (the same in the reprise).
**Problem:** In the second idea the violas repeat a pitch inside a slur in the hemiola rhythm minim | minim over the bar | minim. Nothing tells the player to re-articulate the repeated notes, so the line can come out as one held note and the hemiola is lost from the inner part. Everywhere else the score marks this case: violas 53, 129 and 286 (c085) and the trio violas' off-beats have dashes under the slur, and the horns' and clarinets' held chords in these bars have tenutos (c088).
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 42–43: louré is "a legato stroke, but a slight separation of the notes is effected while the bow is being drawn … indicated by dashes under or over each of the noteheads, with slurs to designate the bow changes". He calls it "very expressive … used quite often in accompaniments" (Messiah, Ex. II-30).
**Fix (applied):** sA.ily `sAVla` 45: `g'2--(\mf g'4--~ | g'4 g'2--)`. sC.ily `sCVla` 127: `b2--( b4--~ | b4) e'2` (with Finding 1). dA.ily `dAVla` 283: `b2--( b4--~ | b4 e'2)`.
