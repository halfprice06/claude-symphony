## Findings

### 1. Cl 1·2, bar 192: Cl 2's bar rest touches Cl 1's pp (applied)
Problem: at 192 Cl 1 enters alone on A♯4 (pp, "1."), and Cl 2 enters at 193. The part combiner keeps the staff in two voices, so Cl 2's bar rest is printed hanging from the bottom stave-line. At 600 dpi its left edge touches the second *p* of Cl 1's pp under the stave.
Evidence: Gould PDF 55: "the distance between characters should not be less than ½ stave-space and no characters should collide". PDF 50: in double-stemmed writing "lower-part rests [are placed] below the centre line", and a rest moves outside the stave only "should parts otherwise collide". Here nothing forced the rest outside: Cl 1's A♯4 is in the second space. c215 fixed the same kind of fault on this staff at 85.
Fix, sections/tB.ily, tBClTwo, eighth bar (score only, so the Cl 2 part keeps a centred rest):
```
old: ... R2. | R2. | R2. | fis'2.\p\< ...
new: ... R2. | R2. | \tag #'score \once \override MultiMeasureRest.staff-position = #-4 \tag #'score \once \override MultiMeasureRest.extra-offset = #'(0.4 . 0) R2. | fis'2.\p\< ...
```
-4 hangs the rest from the second line, inside the stave below the centre line. At -2 (tried first) it touched the duration dot of Cl 1's A♯4. The 0.4-space shift to the right leaves about ½ space between the dot and the rest, and the rest still reads as centred in a bar about ten spaces wide. Result: about one space from the pp and ½ space from the dot. Print only: score.midi is byte-identical, the text layer of the PDF is identical, and the Clarinet 2 part, rebuilt with build-parts.sh, is pixel-identical.

### 2. The audible candidate: strings' struck chord at 339 divided (built, rejected)
This reading governs how notes are written, not which notes sound. It has one rule with a direct consequence for sound: PDF 60, a chord may combine different durations "only in string writing ... because the bow can sustain only two notes at once". So a quadruple stop is always broken, and its lower pair sounds only as an upbeat. The one place this applies is the movement's last loud chord at 339 (2. = 96): Vln II G3–E4–B4–G5 and violas B3–G4–E5, struck crotchets marked "non div." (c186). The listener would hear most of any change here if the chord were marked "div.", so that each half of each section holds two notes and all of them sound together for the whole crotchet. I built it in /tmp/critic-515/cand (coda.ily, both "non div." → "div."). It is clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, no C-major triad. I rejected it because it makes the music worse. (a) Dividing would add only a longer G3 and B3. The cellos, the bassoons' crotchets and the brass's held tenuto minims (c128) already cover the chord's low register. It would also halve the number of players on G5 and B4, the notes that carry the chord over the timpani and the brass. (b) What a listener hears at 339 is the attack. A broken ff down-bow chord gives the crack Adler and c186 asked for (every player sounds every note, Adler PDF 30–32). A divided chord is smoother and weaker, just before the general pause. (c) PDF 60 does not say a broken chord is wrong. It explains why string chords are written the way 339 already is. The struck crotchet uses the two-note limit; it does not try to beat it. So the score keeps "non div." at 339, and the reading supports no other change that could be heard.
