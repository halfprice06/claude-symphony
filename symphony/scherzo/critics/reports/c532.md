## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 100: the retransition's last bar before the build becomes V♭9 (applied)
- **Problem.** The B pedal lasts from 89 to 104, sixteen bars of dominant. Every upper chord in 89–100 is plain V or V7, with theme-A heads as appoggiaturas. Bar 100 is the threshold. The cellos and basses rest, the timpani play motive x at mf, the violas repeat B3, Vln I falls A5–F♯5–D♯5, and Vln II rises D♯4–F♯4–A4 (c237). The same four notes then arrive as the ff V7 at 101.
- **Evidence.** PDF 466: the 9th "intensif[ies] the dissonant character of the dominant — the harmony that more than any other tends to attract dissonant formations". PDF 470–471, Ex. 27-11/27-12: the upper notes of V♭9 are vii°7, and a composer can put 5̂ under vii°7 to make it. Schumann's example is an Allegro molto vivace: a pp diminished seventh over the strings' 5̂ just before the music returns. PDF 464: in minor the 9th is minor. PDF 463, 479 point 4: the 9th resolves down by step. PDF 467: an unprepared 9th after V has begun is "rather frequent", and an inner-voice 9th that resolves in its own line is Chopin's practice (Ex. 27-7). The movement's own ♭9 is C→B, the peak of theme A (24, 116, 264, 316).
- **Fix (applied).** sB.ily, `sBVlnTwo` bar 100: `dis'4-. fis'-. a'-.` → `fis'4-. a'-. c''-.`. Bar 100 now reads B (violas, timpani) / F♯4–A4–C5 (Vln II) / A5–F♯5–D♯5 (Vln I), a complete V♭9 for one bar at mp, inside the existing crescendo. The C5 falls by step to Vln II's own B4 on the f downbeat of 101, where its hemiola minims begin.
- **Rules checked.**
  - Vln II's arpeggio still rises, against Vln I's falling one, so c237's reason holds and there are no octaves.
  - 100.3 is C5 under D♯5. At 101 the motion is contrary in all three upper strings.
  - The chord is D♯°7 (the C–E♭–F♯–A class), the one already used at 87. It is not the stop chord (B–D–F–A♭) and has no C–E–G.
  - Root position: the only B sounding is the violas' B3 a ninth below and the timpani's B2. At 100 these are the bass, because Vc/Cb rest. That is the normal case of PDF 465, not the inverted 9th of PDF 469.
- **What it does not touch.**
  - The plain V7 at 101–104 stays plain. The ♭9 now resolves into it, so the build starts from a resolution.
  - 337 stays the only sustained, complete V♭9 at fff (c352's reason).
  - The parallel place in the reprise has no B-pedal retransition, so there is nothing to match.
  - No line was silenced, moved or added, so parts-cues.json is unaffected.

### 2. Bar 100: the same 9th in Vln I (C6) instead (tested on paper, rejected)
`c''' a'' fis''` at 100 would put the 9th in the soprano, which PDF 479 point 1 prefers, and would echo theme A's C6. But the C6 would leave by a leap down a third and never reach B in its own voice (Vln I goes to B5 at 101 only by way of F♯5). That is Ex. 27-6a's unresolved embellishing 9th. ### 3. Bars 1–7: the frame G♯5 is Chopin's floating 6th (follows; record the reason)
At 4–6, Vln I's G♯5 sounds over V (pizz. B, Hn 1 E4→D♯4 since c530): an unresolved 6th over the dominant in the top voice. At 7 it turns to G5 for E minor. PDF 474–475, Ex. 27-18: Chopin leaves the 6th unresolved "to create the effect of a pedal point in the soprano", as "a wonderful dramatic preparation" for the next section, and a common tone links the two sections. The book's condition is met: the 6th lies on top, alone and clearly heard. Record this as a further reason for the frame (c430, c526), besides its link to movements II and IV.

### 4. Bar 348.1: Cl 1's F♯4 over E rises to G♯4 (kept)
- **What is there.** Cl 1 (a sixth below Ob 1) ties F♯4 from the B9 at 347 into 348. Over the E bass it is a 9th, and it rises to G♯4 while Ob 1's D♯5 rises 7–8. PDF 472 and 479 describe 9/7 double suspensions with downward resolutions.
- **Why it stays.** The pair is T's own tied leading tone (c421, c491) doubled in 6ths. The bassoon supplies E3 on the downbeat, and the harp's G♯3/B3/E4 enter on beat 2. A falling F♯4→E4 would put Cl 1 in octaves with Ob 1's E5. The upward 9–10 coupled with 7–8 is a Romantic retardation, heard on a weak hypermetric bar at p. No change.
