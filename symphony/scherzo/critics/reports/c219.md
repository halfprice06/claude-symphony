## What else I studied

**Mendelssohn, Symphony No. 3 in A minor "Scottish", ii (Vivace non troppo, F major, 2/4).** The corpus has no score of it, so I worked from my knowledge of the score and two web sources: The Classic Review's guide (https://theclassicreview.com/beginners-guides/mendelssohn-symphony-no-3-scottish-a-beginners-guide/) and a search summary of teaching notes from the Tasmanian Symphony Orchestra (the PDF itself returned 404). Its plan:
- A sonata-form scherzo with no trio. It opens with a few bars of staccato repeated notes in the strings, then the clarinet plays the pentatonic tune with the Scotch snap, which grows out of the first movement's introduction.
- The first ff tutti comes early, with the tune in the winds and horns over rushing semiquavers.
- Most of the movement stays soft and is built from two contrasting textures: restless staccato strings and short wind outbursts.
- It winds down to a pizzicato close.

## Holistic verdict

Would a conductor who knows the Scottish scherzo believe a master wrote this movement? Yes, though a master of a different school. The shared craft:
- **Wind solo over light strings.** Mendelssohn's clarinet tune over staccato strings is the model of a single wind voice above a light accompaniment. This score does the same in the trio (Cl 1 on T over harp off-beats and the Hn 3·4 drone, 153–168) and in theme A (Vln I leggiero over off-beat Vln II/violas and pizz. basses, 13–28). It keeps solos single and adds doubling only as the dynamic rises (Rimsky #10, #12).
- **Groups alternating in short units.** Mendelssohn trades phrase-length units between strings and winds. So does the counterstatement 29–36, and the coda's T passes Hn 1 → Ob 1 → Cl 1.
- **Pizzicato close.** Both movements end soft over a pizzicato. Here the last sound is G♯ in Hn 1 and Vln I over a timpani roll and pizz. E, as the cyclic frame requires.
- **Early ff tutti with a thematic bass.** Both put the first big tutti early, with the theme in the winds and a running or thematic bass.

The differences are settled decisions:
- Mendelssohn has no trio. The brief's models (Dvořák 9/iii, the Eroica, Dvořák 8) call for one.
- Mendelssohn stays mostly soft, while this movement has four loud arrivals before the trio (41, 72, 104, 121). c059 and c079 weighed this against the Dvořák/Beethoven models and kept it.
- The brass here give weight, not bite (Dan, c123).

Against the reading, the movement's doublings and chord spacing follow Rimsky-Korsakov's practice:
- Every multiple stop is ff and wind-supported.
- Three-group unisons use his "most natural" combination, Vla + Cl + Hn (197–200).
- Horn and clarinet share inner lines in unison for a soft legato (45–55).
- Wind chords keep their doubling throughout a passage.
- No forte chord has a void in its upper parts. The f/ff passages are filled at every beat sampled: 72, 101–104, 197–200, 333–339.

### Considered and kept (decided, no change)

1. **357–360: the motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ is a chromatic line in a solo horn** (PDF 73: chromatic melodies suit the brass much less). I keep it. It is a protected cyclic quotation (HISTORY v01; plagal close do-not-touch). Rimsky's own Ex. 74 (PDF 245) gives chromatic solo phrases to a stopped horn and a muted trumpet at p/mf. A four-note p descent fits that usage, not the "sickly" passionate brass melody he warns against.

2. **45–55 and 121–131: Cl 1·2 move through open fifths and fourths in the inner harmony** (45 D5/G4, 49 E5/A4, 51 D5/A4, 52 E5/A4, 53 D5/G4; 121 B4/E4, 123 F♯4/B3). Two rules bear on this: PDF 90 rule 4 (concords for same-colour pairs) and PDF 97 remark 4 (middle parts in seconds, sevenths, thirds and sixths). I keep it:
   - Each fifth lasts one or two beats and alternates with thirds (46, 50, 54).
   - Cl 1 doubles Hn 1 in unison, so the clarinets are not a self-contained two-part layer but a colouring of the horn line (PDF 74–75).
   - Every chord's third is present in Vln II/Ob 1, the violas or Hn 2.
   - Re-voicing the pair would undo c094, c122 and c172 (A7/C♯ voicing, 4–3 at 55), which were fixed for voice leading.

3. **101–104 and 301–304: Ob 2 D♯5 against C.a. A4/B4, a tritone in the oboe colour** (PDF 90 rule 4, "specially the oboe"). I keep it. It is the dominant seventh's own tritone in an ff tutti with full brass, where Rimsky allows discords that are meant to be heard. c184 settled the oboes' F♯5/D♯5 here.

4. **199–200 and 219: the tenor lies a tenth or more above the bass octave at f** (PDF 85: the bass "should rarely" be more than an octave from the part above). I keep it. These are two-beat moments in the trio's middle and return. The bass is doubled in octaves (Cb + Vc + Bsn 2), Hn 2 and Bsn 1 fill the fourth octave, and Rimsky's word is "rarely", not "never".

5. **224, downbeat: a minor thirteenth between Bsn 1 G♯3 and the E5/E6 melody octaves.** Since c208, Hn 1·2 rest here. I keep it. The violas' div. G♯3/E4 fill beats 2–3, the passage is a diminuendo out of f into the p codetta, and PDF 96 allows wider spacing in soft passages. c208's reason (Rimsky PDF 130: a long diminuendo takes the brass out first) stands.

6. **Mendelssohn's lighter dynamic plan.** I keep this movement's four loud arrivals before the trio, as c059 and c079 decided. The brief's models are Dvořák 9/iii and Beethoven 9/ii. The Scottish scherzo's model is a sonata scherzo without a trio, and importing its all-soft plan would change the protected form and proportions (c203).
