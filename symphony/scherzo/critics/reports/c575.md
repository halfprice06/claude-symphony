## Findings

### 1. 321–340: the coda's build and fff summit are on the most crowded system in the score (applied)

Bars: page 12, 321–340 (U at 325, the build from 329, the fff at 336, the struck chord at 339).

Problem: c395 fixed the system breaks from 253 so that every system had a crotchet at least 1.35 times a quaver, and measured 321–340 at exactly 1.35. Since then the coda has gained the cellos on theme A (c484), the trumpets' motive x at 334 (c497), the cor anglais's octave (c517), the held low brass (c554) and more. In v485 the system was at 1.22; in v575 a crotchet gets 2.13 spaces and a quaver 1.88, a ratio of 1.13. That is the "not acceptable" case on PDF 54, and it falls on the loudest page, where a conductor reads fastest. Page 11 (305–320) has 1.49.

Evidence: Gould PDF 53 (proportional spacing, quaver 2½ against crotchet 3½); PDF 54: "Where the density of the notation increases, the uniform space for a note-value may need to be noticeably different from that of an adjacent system", that is, fewer bars on a denser system; PDF 55: each system should hold a comparable number of symbols. c415's note on the pinned breaks says "a change that widens a system may need these moved". That has now happened.

Fix (global.ily): page 12 starts at U 325, and 305–324 is two systems of ten bars:
```
  s2.*10 \tag #'score \break s2.*10
  \bar "||" \tag #'score \pageBreak \mark \default % U 325
```
(was `s2.*16 \tag #'score \pageBreak  s2.*4` and `\bar "||" \mark \default`). Result: 325–339 crotchet 3.38 against quaver 2.12 (1.59); 305–314 and 315–324 give 3.0–3.2 spaces per quaver. The coda now starts its own page at its double bar, as Q 253, S 289 and V 341 do. I tested three layouts. One system for 305–324 gave crotchet = quaver (1.95 each), which is worse, so I rejected it. A break at 313 (the phrase join) gave 3.70 against 2.71, which is uneven. The break at 315 is the even one. With brass tacet in 305–324, two systems fit on page 11. The page count stays at 13.

The new system start at 315 needs its player labels (c235's rule, Adler PDF 295). In dC.ily, Fl `fis'''8-\sysLabel "a2"(`, Ob 1 `fis''4-\sysLabel "1."-.(`, Cl 2 `fis'4-\sysLabel "2."-.(`. The labels at 312 now fall mid-system, and c235's test suppresses them. I checked this at 150 dpi.

### 2. The trio tune's dotted figure: the off-beat quaver stands almost on beat 2 (applied)

Bars: 155 and 159 (Cl 1), 187 (Ob 1), 191 (Vc), 211, 215 and 219 (Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II): T's `dis''4. fis'8` and `e''4. dis''8` figures and the call's `a'4. b'8`, over the harp's or the violas' off-beat crotchets.

Problem: in each of these bars the quaver on beat 2½ stands only 1.25–1.29 spaces after the beat-2 chord in the accompanying staff, and 1.8–3.2 spaces before beat 3. The system's quaver median is 1.7–1.8. So the eye reads beat 2 and its off-beat as nearly one column, and the gap after the quaver as the beat. LilyPond places the quaver by the dotted note's own space and the beat-2 chord by the harp's crotchet, and the half-beat between them takes all the compression. At 155 and 211 the quaver comes after a falling sixth, and LilyPond's optical correction for stems pointing apart (PDF 55) pulls it further left. That is why a normal left extension on the quaver had no effect there; I measured this in a small test file.

Evidence: Gould PDF 54: "Notes of equal duration require equal spacing for an entire system"; PDF 53: "Musicians rely heavily on good spacing to read rhythm".

Fix: a print-only function in global.ily,
```
beatq = #(define-music-function (w m) (number? ly:music?) #{ \tweak extra-spacing-height #'(-inf.0 . +inf.0) \tweak extra-spacing-width #(cons w 0) #m #})
```
and `\beatq #0.8 fis'8` / `\beatq #0.8 dis''8` (tAClOne 155, 159), `\beatq #-0.4` on Ob 1's B4 at 187, the cellos' A3 at 191 and Vln I at 211, 215 and 219. Because the extent is full height, the quaver clears the dot of the dotted note even after a leap. A finite height (±20) had no effect in the score. Result (beat 1→2, 2→2½, 2½→3): 155 2.47/1.94/2.37, 187 2.47/1.90/2.37, 191 2.55/1.90/1.81, 211 2.57/1.99/2.37, 215 2.27/1.90/3.15 (the A♯). One staff moves the whole column, so the doubling parts need nothing. The function keeps `#'(...inf...)` out of the sections, because lyparse would read the "f" of "inf" as a note.

### 3. Bars 49 and 199: c485's partial fixes brought to the same standard (applied)

Bars: 49 (the second idea's `e'''4. d'''8 cis'''4` in seven staves) and 199 (the forte call's `d''4. fis'8` in Cl 1, Vln II and Vla, against Hn 2's beat-2 D).

Problem: c485 widened these with `NoteHead.extra-spacing-width #'(-2.0 . 0)` and `#'(-2.6 . 0)`, and recorded the half-beat at 1.46 and 1.48 spaces against 1.95 and 1.82. It said the column was held by a dot that its override could not reach. The full-height extent of finding 2 reaches it.

Fix: sA.ily sAVlnOne `\beatq #-0.4 d'''8`; tB.ily tBClOne `\beatq #-0.4 fis'8` (c485's override is in Cl 1, not Vln II as its report says). Result: 49 2.99/1.99/4.03 (the 4.03 holds the sharps of beat 3, PDF 57; the dotted crotchet now has 4.98 against the quaver's 4.03, case (a)); 199 3.81/1.99/2.37.

### 4. Bar 198: beat 3 stands a quaver's width after beat 2 (applied)

Bar 198: Fl/Ob/Vln I `cis'''4 gis''2` start a minim on beat 2, and Cl, Hn 1 (C♯5, c482), Vln II and the violas strike beat 3 after a minim. Beat 1→2 got 2.50 spaces and beat 2→3 only 1.78, which is the system's quaver width. The minim's compressed space (PDF 53–54: "longer durations are compressed") sets the beat-3 column, so two equal crotchet beats look unequal (PDF 54). Fix: tBVla `b2 \beatq #-1.0 e'4)`. Result: 2.50/2.59.

### 5. Violas at 106 and 118: crotchet a little narrower than the quaver after it (checked, not applied)

`<b g'>4-. fis'8( f'8)` at 106 and `<c' a'>4-. gis'8( g'8)` at 118 (c487). The crotchet gets 2.75 and 2.61 spaces, and the quaver after it 2.89 and 2.83, because the second quaver carries a natural, as do Fl, Ob, C.a. and Cl in the same column. This is under PDF 54's case (b) by 0.14–0.22 space, which I could not see at 200 dpi. To fix it, PDF 57's remedy (less space around the accidental) would have to act on five naturals in the column of the chromatic tail, and the tail is the thing to read there. I left it.

### 7. The audible candidate: the struck quadruple stops at 339 (not built)

The only rule in this reading that changes what players do is PDF 60: "the bow can sustain only two notes at once". The score has no held string chord of three or more notes. The chords at 339 (Vln II `<g e' b' g''>4`, Vla `<b g' e''>4`, "non div.") are struck crotchets before the G.P. Dividing them would let every note sound at once, but each pitch would have half the players, the open-string G of the Vln II chord would lose its ring, and c186's reason for "non div." (every player sounds the whole fff stroke) would be undone. Nothing needs to be sustained there, so the music would be thinner and not clearer. A build cannot show the difference, because the MIDI already sounds every note, so I did not build it. No sound change is supported by this reading, and score.midi is byte-identical to v575's.
