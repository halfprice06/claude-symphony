## What else I studied

- **Glazunov, Symphony No. 5 in B♭, Op. 55.** On IMSLP the Scherzo (Moderato) is movement ii, not iii (https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.5,_Op.55_(Glazunov,_Aleksandr)). I could not download the score from IMSLP, so I did not read it bar by bar. My comparison rests on the Chandos CHAN 9739 booklet (https://www.chandos.net/chanimages/Booklets/CH9739.pdf). The booklet describes a six-bar introduction leading into "a bustling theme for the woodwind", legato chromatic scales in the first violins set against that theme, "flecks of colour from the harp and glockenspiel", and a woodwind-led middle section marked pochissimo meno mosso. The model Glazunov offers is two layers in opposite articulation sounding together: a sustained, legato string line under or over a fast, detached figure.
- **Supporting books.** Aldwell–Schachter on the pedal point: PDF 367 sec. 28 ("top-voice or inner-voice pedals are not uncommon"; tonic pedals are frequent at beginnings), PDF 368 (dissonances against a pedal need not resolve), and PDF 415, Ex. 24-25 (Chopin, Op. 56/3, a major 3rd sustained as an upper pedal while the lower voices move). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 84 ("Upper and inner pedal notes are more effective on the orchestra … owing to the greater variety of tone colour") and PDF 128 (an echo given to muted brass gives a distant effect). Adler I PDF 307–308 (muted and stopped horn).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 3–6: the horn call has no high pedal, and the change of mode at 7 is split by four bars of silence. APPLIED

**Problem.** Since c430, Vln I holds G♯5 ppp at bars 1–2 with Hn 1 and stops at the fermata. Since c470, it re-enters on G5 at bar 7 so that "the change of mode G♯ → G natural is heard as one line". Four bars of rest separate the two notes, so the line is not heard as one: the ear has let go of the G♯5 by bar 7. Meanwhile the horn call at 3–6 has only Bsn 2's B2 and three pizzicato notes under it. The opening is the movement's most exposed passage, and from 3 to 6 it has no sound above the call.

**Evidence.**
- Fig. 6.1 (Casanelles PDF 272) is the textbook form of this moment: a call from far away over a sustained high string pedal at pp, which stays while the other layers come and go. Casanelles PDF 280 names this layer the pad.
- A&S PDF 367 and PDF 415 (Ex. 24-25) give the harmonic basis: a top-voice pedal on the major 3rd, held while the lower voices move. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 84 says upper pedals work better in the orchestra than at the piano.
- The pedal is consonant with every note under it: a 10th above G♯3 at 3, a 6th over E at 4, and a 5th above D♯4 over B2 at 5–6. Over the B bass it forms B–D♯–G♯, so no dissonance needs resolving (A&S PDF 368).
- At 7 the G♯ slides to G♮ in the same octave and the same bow, under the clarinets' E minor. This is what c470 described, now unbroken.
- It matches Glazunov's layering: a legato held string layer against a moving one.

**Fix (sections/intro.ily):**

### 2. Bars 333–335 (v489 → v499 comparison): trumpets in all three bars of the coda's theme A. KEPT, not applied

**What changed.** c497 gave Tpt 1·2 motive x at 334, where theme A holds its accented G. The coda's trumpets now play x in every bar from 332 to 335. In the exposition tutti (105–120) they play alternate bars and rest where the theme holds.

**Weighing it.** Casanelles PDF 272 (layers take turns) and PDF 275 (loud brass give their dissonant upper partials) argue for the rest. Against that, the coda is a stretto: motive x has sounded every bar since 325, and c497's brass punctuation at the hold (Adler II PDF 42, 44) is a sound reason. The trumpets are f, not accented, and are a level under the ff strings and winds.

**Decision.** Keep c497. The coda is meant to go further than the exposition, and reverting a change made two cycles ago would be churn without a stronger reason than c497's.

### 3. Bars 363–371: distance at the close. No change

The three timpani echoes (pp, pp, then ppp "near the rim") lower both the level and the colour. This is Casanelles' point (PDF 272–273) that distance is timbre as well as volume, and the bars already follow it. Hn 1 ppp and Vln I ppp on the last G♯ act as the high pad (PDF 280). The closing horn page is protected (c087, c488), and the reading gives no reason against it.

### Holistic verdict against Glazunov 5/ii

Glazunov's scherzo is lighter. Its woodwinds lead a fast detached theme, and colour comes in small touches (harp, glockenspiel). This scherzo is heavier: it has three ff/fff tuttis and its theme A is led by the strings. Its woodwind counterpoint (29–40, the fugato 289–296) and its soft trio frame (153–168) are at a master's level. I propose no change towards Glazunov's lighter weight, because the bare string statement 13–28 and the tutti brass writing are protected and the key plan is settled. A conductor would believe the opening after finding 1. Before it, bars 3–6 sounded thin compared with Mahler's model of the same gesture.
