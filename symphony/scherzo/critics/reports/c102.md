## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Trio middle section: ii7 – I6 – V7 at 193–196. Applied.
**Problem.** Bars 193–196 (Vln I/II F♯5–G♯5 | A5–C♯6 | B5–A5–G♯5 | F♯5) have the bass F♯ | F♯ | G♯ | B. That gives ii, ii7, then E/G♯ (I6) at 195, then V7 and the deceptive move to vi at 197. Rule 3 on PDF 148 says intermediate harmonies "do not lead to I or I6". Example 9-23b (PDF 149) marks a progression with II6 → I as "poor". A "I6" between II and V is not the passing "I6" of §11 (PDF 145), which stands between II and II6. The upper voices already move as a cadential 6/4: Cl 2 holds E4 from 194 and falls to D♯4 at 196, Bsn 1 G♯3 falls to F♯3, Cl 1 B4 falls to A4, and the melody descends B–A–G♯–F♯. Only the bass G♯ turns that into a tonic.
**Fix.** Make the bass B at 195, tied into 196: ii7 – V6/4 – V7 – vi. The 6/4 is approached by intermediate harmony (PDF 160, pt. 7), stands on the strong third bar of the group (PDF 155), and its 4th E enters as a common tone and resolves down (PDF 153). The melody gets the stepwise 5̂-4̂-3̂-2̂ line the 6/4 allows over II → V (PDF 156). tB.ily: `tBVc` bars 11–12 `b,2.~ | b,2.` (was `gis,2. | b,2.`); `tBCb` bars 11–12 `b,,2.~ | b,,2.`. The parallel check also shows that the old octaves G♯→B between the violas and the basses at 195→196 are gone.

### 2. Second idea in E minor: V – A major – i6 at 123–124 and 279–280. Applied.
**Problem.** The hemiola bass B | A | G under the melody D♯6–C♯6–B5 is harmonized V (123.1) – A–C♯–E (123.3, Cl 1 A4, Cl 2 E4) – i6 (124.2). An IV chord after V breaks the rule in the box on PDF 135 ("not I–V–IV–I") and rule 2 on PDF 148. The same IV then leads to i6, which breaks rule 3. The A major replaced the G-major model's vi (bar 47), because in E minor that vi would be C major. The passing A in the bass is the 7th of V, and A&S give the right chord for it: V4/2, whose bass moves down by step to I6 (rule 1, PDF 148; Ex. 9-14, PDF 142). The old voicing also has parallel octaves: Cl 1 A4→G4 against the bass A→G at 124.2 and 280.2 (allpairs_par).
**Fix.** At the second minim of 123 (and 279), Cl 2 takes D♯4, and Cl 1, Hn 2 and the violas keep F♯4 until the bass reaches G. The chord is then B–D♯–F♯–A (V4/2) with the melody's C♯ as a passing tone, and it resolves A→G, D♯→E, F♯→G/E. The melody, bass and rhythm do not change. In sC.ily and dA.ily: Cl 1 `fis'2.~( | fis'4 g'2)` (was `fis'2( a'4~ | a'4 g'2)`); Cl 2 `b2( dis'4~ | dis'4 e'2)` (was `b2( e'4~ | e'4 e'2)`); Hn 2 `fis'2.--~ | fis'4 e'2--)` (was `fis'2.-- | e'2.--)`); Vla `fis'2.~( | fis'4 e'2)` (was `fis'2.( | e'2.)`). Hn 2 and the violas join the hemiola for this one bar; the slurred tenuto horn chords of c088 stay. The C♯ that c091 protects at 128/284 is untouched.

### 3. Second idea in G: the 4th of the cadential 6/4 at 53 enters by leap. Applied.
**Problem.** At 52→53 the violas leap E4→G4 into the 4th of the cadential 6/4 (G/D). Hn 2 leaps E3→G3 at the same moment, which makes parallel octaves E→G between the two (allpairs_par, beat 156). PDF 153: "even in more complex instrumental styles, the 4th normally enters as a common tone or by stepwise motion." Only Cl 2 (A4→G4) does this. The E-minor version at 128→129 already has the violas hold the 4th as a common tone (c012).
**Fix.** sA.ily `sAVla` bar 52: `d'4 g'2` (was `d'4 e'2`). The G makes the chord at 52.2 A7/C♯ (V6/5 of V). Its 7th enters by leap, which is allowed in instrumental textures (PDF 140, Ex. 9-9). The violas then repeat G into 53 and resolve it to F♯ at 54, as before. The hemiola cadence 53–55 (protected) is unchanged. I left Hn 2's leap alone: moving it to G as well would double the 7th at 52.
