### 1. 189–192: the cellos answer Ob 1's call alone; the cor anglais rests (applied)

**Problem.** The trio's middle part is a call and response: Ob 1 calls p espress. in C♯ minor (185–188), and the answer comes a step lower in B (189–192). The answer is the cellos on the A string doubled at the unison by the cor anglais. The cor anglais is an oboe: half of the answer is the call's own reed colour, a fourth lower. This is the one dialogue in the movement where the answer repeats the caller's instrument family, and it is the most exposed one (harp pp, basses pizz. pp, nothing else). The cor anglais plays nowhere else in the trio, so these four bars are its only trio entry and do not serve a later plan.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 257: dialogue introduces the solo colour "by itself, implanting its timbre firmly in the listener's mind", and the answer comes "in a radically different orchestral color"; Beethoven's answering strings play pizzicato "to avoid duplication of the solo sound" (Ex. XVII-1, PDF 258, seen as an image: the solo violin's 4 bars, then winds and pizz. strings).
- PDF 267 (Mozart flute concerto, Ex. XVII-10): no orchestral instrument of the solo's own colour, "so that the solo flute will not encounter any color competition"; PDF 264 (Ex. XVII-9): no orchestral horns beside the solo horn.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 56 (the reference c011 gave when it moved the cellos up): cantabile melodies on the cello's A string have "a wonderfully rich 'chest' quality", and his Snegourotchka example has the cello on the A string imitating the first clarinet. A wind call answered by cellos alone is exactly that.
- c027 already moved the harp clear of this answer (Adler PDF 257, 274), so the answer has room to stand alone.

**Fix (tB.ily, `tBCorAnglais`).** Bars 189–192 become rests:
- before: `R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | dis'4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" e'8 dis'8 cis'8 dis'8 | b2 fis4) | gis4.(\< a8 b4) | fis2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | R2.\! | R2. | …`
- after: `R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | R2. | …`

The cellos' line, dynamics, "dolce" and bowing are unchanged. Comment at line 6 of tB.ily updated. The dialogue is now oboe against cello, two different families, and the cor anglais next sounds at 269. Note for the parts: parts-cues.json cues "C.a." at 189 in parts 07, 09, 26 and 28; parts-gen.py must take the cue from "Vc." now.

This reverses part of v01's scoring (the answer was cellos with cor anglais from the start; c011 only moved the cellos up to the cor anglais's octave). No earlier critic recorded a reason for the cor anglais itself.

### 2. 245: the timpani strike with Cl 1's head instead of answering it (applied)

**Problem.** In the second retransition, theme A's head passes viola (241) → Cl 1 (245) → Vln I (249) over the pizzicato B. At 245 the timpani play motive x on B in the same bar and the same rhythm as Cl 1's pp head, two octaves and a fourth below, and the drum then answers in 247 anyway. c327 removed exactly this doubling in the first retransition (89–97) and left 241–247 because the bass moves to A and C at 243–244, where a B drum cannot answer. That reason covers 243–244, not 245: at 245 the bass is back on B and the drum's answer at 247 is already written.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13, Beethoven Violin Concerto, seen as an image): "the timpani is very important because it plays the motive that joins the two phrases of the melody". At 245 it plays with the phrase, not between phrases.
- PDF 259–260: the accompaniment "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part". At 245 it duplicates the head note for note.
- PDF 268–270 (Sibelius, Bartók): a soft entry is heard best over a sparse background. The clarinet's pp head now sits over the pizzicato B and the bass clarinet's held B alone.

**Fix (retr.ily, `retrTimp`).**
- before: `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2.*2 | b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | b,4\< …`
- after: `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2.*2 | R2. | R2. | b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | b,4\< …`

The order is now: viola head with the drum (241, the turn to E minor, where the drum is the intro's signal from 9–12), the French sixth (243–244), Cl 1's head alone (245–246), the drum's answer (247, pp restated after the rests), Vln I's head and the drum's build (249–252). ### 3. 199: Hn 1's held A4 sounds T's suspension note before the tune reaches it (not applied)

**Problem.** At the trio's f statement of T (197–200), Hn 1 doubles T's head (G♯4–A4–B4) and then holds A4 from the downbeat of 199. Since c331, T's bar 3 is D♯5–F♯4–A4, with the A tied over as the suspension into 200. So the tune's A4 on beat 3 of 199 (Cl 1, Vln II, violas) lands on a pitch the horn has held for two beats, in a colour that blends with it (Adler PDF 273). uni.py finds no other case like it in the trio.

**Tested alternative and why I did not keep it.** Hn 1 holding B4 to beat 3 (`b'2 a'4~ | a'4 gis'2`) makes a 4–3 suspension over the F♯ bass that resolves with the tune. But the call (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I) holds A5 from the same downbeat, so the horn's B4 would sound against its own resolution an octave up (Aldwell & Schachter PDF 352: avoid sounding the tone of resolution in another voice against the suspension). A rest on beats 1–2 breaks c047's horn doubling of T's head. c322's protected B4→A4 at 198–199 stays. The cost now is small: the tune's A arrives in three instruments at f against one horn at mf, and the call has already sounded A5.

### 4. 209–216: the first violins' upper octave from the start of T's return (not applied)

**Problem considered.** Bizet adds the first violins an octave above the voice only at its climax (Adler PDF 279, Ex. XVII-20), and Haydn changes the doubling in the second phrase (PDF 288). At 209 Vln I already takes T's upper octave, so the climax at 217–218 adds only Fl 1's colour. Vln I could double Vln II at the unison for 209–216 and leap to the upper octave at 217.

**Why not.** With no upper octave, Hn 1's held B4 (210–212) and C♯5 (213–216, protected by c426 as the common tone of three chords) would lie above the tune's A4–G♯4 and A♯4–B4 at 211–212 and 215–216. The horn would become the top line at both half-cadences, which breaks PDF 274 (keep the accompaniment registrally distant from the tune) in a worse way than the flaw it cures. The Fl 1 entry at 217 (c069) already marks the climax, and the upper octave is first heard at 209 (c344). No change.
