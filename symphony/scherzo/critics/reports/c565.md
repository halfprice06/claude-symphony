## Findings

### 1. Fl 1·2 at 217: the player label "1." is printed twice (applied)

- **Bars:** 217, Flutes 1·2 staff (tC.ily, `tCFluteOne`).
- **Problem:** Two "1." labels are stacked over Fl 1's C♯6 at the start of T's forte return. One is the manual `-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."`. The other is the part combiner's own solo label (`soloText = "1."` in score.ly). The duplicate came in at v541. c540 removed Fl 1's G♯5 at 209, so Fl 1 now enters from rest at 217 with Fl 2 silent, and the combiner labels that entry itself. v540's page 8 shows one "1." here and v541's shows two (checked).
- **Evidence:** Gould PDF 50–51: a shared stave shows each shared indication once ("parts share a rest wherever possible"), and the two-part examples never restate a label or symbol. PDF 55 (cited by c155/c215/c305 for this score): no characters collide or crowd. The text-box scan found no other stacked label anywhere in the score.
- **Fix (applied):** `cis'''2(\mf\< dis'''4 |` in place of `cis'''2(\mf\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." dis'''4 |`, plus a c565 comment line after `tCFluteTwo`. The combiner's "1." remains (checked at 250 dpi). This is a print-only change. The markup was score-only, so the Fl 1 part does not change, and score.midi is byte-identical to v565's.

### 2. The change a listener would hear most under this reading: Cl 1 kept at one octave through the hemiola, A4 at 104 and 304 instead of A5 (built, not kept)

- **Bars:** 101–104 (sB.ily `sBClOne`) and 301–304 (dB.ily `dBClOne`).
- **Candidate:** Gould PDF 47–48 asks that a phrase keep its octave so that its contour stays clear. Outside the bass parts and the harp's left hand, Cl 1 at 103→104 and 303→304 is the only place where a melodic wind line jumps an octave on the same pitch inside a phrase: A4 at 103, A5 ff at 104. I built the alternative in /tmp/critic-565/cand (`a'2. | a'2.\ff |` in both files). It is clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, and analysis.txt is identical to v565's.
- **Why it makes the music worse:** The leap is at the place Gould PDF 47 marks "recommended": on the bar line, on a repeated pitch, at a new attack. It also falls on the ff downbeat that ends the hemiola. Kept at A4, the clarinet would leave the clarion register at the loudest point and drop under Cl 2's F♯4–D♯4 line into the thick middle of the tutti. The seventh (A) would then lose its clarinet colour where Fl 1 and Vln I state it at A5. The written contour is clear as it stands: the hemiola is 101–103, and 104 is the arrival. Decision: no change. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-565/work does not contain it.
