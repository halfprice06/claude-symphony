The count gave one new case: the five-line Hn 4 notes at 308–319, which came in with v115 (c114 moved Hn 4 to the bass an octave up). As support for the bass clef, I used Adler, *The Study of Orchestration* (adler1 PDF 304, "Old and New Notation"): in new scores the horn "always transposes a perfect fifth down, whether the part is notated in the treble or the bass clef". In this concert-pitch score, then, a bass-clef horn passage simply sounds as written.

## Findings

### 1. Horns 3·4 at 305–320 go down to five ledger lines under the treble stave (applied)

**Bars.** 305–320 (T, the reprise tutti). The approach is 301–304.

**Problem.** c114 gave Hn 4 the bass an octave above Bsn 2 (`dCHnFour`: E3, B2, A2, G3). On the shared Hn 3·4 treble staff at concert pitch, B2 sits on the fifth ledger line and A2 hangs below it, at 308, 310, 311, 315, 316 and 319. Hn 3, in the upper voice, is meanwhile on G3–A3 with two ledger lines. The two parts cover the whole space below the stave, so the slurs, the low breath mark at 316 and Hn 4's rest at 312 all print three to seven spaces under the stave. The rest at 312 sits 3.9–6.75 spaces below it (probe). On page 11 the Hn 3·4 staff takes more height than any other wind staff in that system. A horn at concert pitch has a lower clef available, so this is exactly the case the reading rules out.

**Reading.** Gould PDF 46: "Use up to at least three ledger lines before transposing pitches up or down an octave", and "Never use an octave sign when an instrument could move into a higher-pitched or lower-pitched clef". Her example puts the cello in its own clef, not in a clef with an octave sign. PDF 47: "Brass instruments and voices should never need octave signs". Here a brass instrument changes clef. PDF 51: rests are placed at the level of the surrounding pitches, and when the notation becomes this complicated it should be simplified. PDF 41: the overlapping parts share their ledger lines. Adler (adler1 PDF 304): horn parts are notated in treble or bass clef. Elsewhere the score keeps horn notes within three lines (73–88, 153–168, 233–240), so 305–320 is the single exception.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-115/work).** The bass clef goes in from the barline of 301. That is after Hn 3·4's twelve bars of rest, so the clef does not interrupt the ff entry at 305. The treble clef returns at 321, in the rests after the passage. Both clefs are tagged `'score`, which parts-lib.ily drops (`drop-for-part?`), so the Horn 3 and Horn 4 parts stay as they are (Horn 4 written E3 at most, three lines).

```
% sections/dB.ily
dBHnFour = { R2. | ... | R2. | \tag #'score \clef bass fis2.\p\< | fis2. | fis2. | fis2.\ff | }
% sections/dC.ily
dCHnFour = { e2.(\ff | ... | e4) r4 r4 | \tag #'score \clef treble R2.*4 | }
```

Result on page 10–11: Hn 4 lies on and inside the bass stave (A2 in the first space, B2 on the second line). Hn 3 reaches E4 at 317 on the second ledger line above the stave. The rest at 312 and both breath marks at 316 now sit inside the stave. No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or slur changes. This does not undo c114: Hn 4 still holds the bass an octave up.

### Checked and left

- Hn 2 D♯3 at 286 has four ledger lines in the shared Hn 1·2 staff, while Hn 1 has the tenor second idea above it. c095 left it. Gould PDF 47 says four lines can be read at a glance, and a clef change for one note on a staff whose upper part carries the tune would cost more than it saves.
- Hn 3·4 have three ledger lines at 73–88, 101–104, 153–168 and 233–240. This is within Gould PDF 46's "at least three". I kept the exposition 101–120 in treble: there the horns go no lower than three lines, so the reprise needs the bass clef only because of c114's octave.
- Cl 2's bar rests at 210–212 and Hn 2's at 357–360 and 365–366 are printed under the other player, where the rest of the score uses "1.". Gould PDF 50 gives each part its own rests in double-stemmed writing. The rests hang from the bottom line clear of the notes, and the combiner keeps two voices because a slur or tie crosses into the bars where both play. A forced `\partCombineSoloI` would break those ties, so I did not change it.
- The tuba's E1 and the harp LH's F♯1 (four lines) have no lower clef, and an 8va would be wrong for brass (PDF 47) and for isolated harp notes (PDF 47).
