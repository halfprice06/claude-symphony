## Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?

On craft, yes, though a conductor would not take it for Mahler. The differences that matter:

- **Where the weight comes from.** Mahler's scherzo gets its character from weight: stamped crotchets and accented brass. This score is a light Dvořák-type scherzo by design. The brief rules out Mahler's means here: soft brass (c123), no hammered tutti, trumpets only in motive x.
- **Cyclic material.** Both movements bring material from elsewhere in the symphony back in triple time. In this score that is the horn call at 3–6, the motto tail at 357–360, and the G♯ = A♭ frame. Mahler develops the borrowed material; this score only quotes it, as the cyclic rule requires. On that point the score is doing what it should.
- **Colour.** Mahler's colours are specific: a solo instrument named for one passage, a doubling chosen for one entry. The one place in v439 where the colour has become less specific in the last ten cycles is the top line of the coda's T. Finding 1 deals with it.
- **Metre.** Mahler's irregular trio metre is not available here, because the movement is 3/4 throughout.

## Findings

### 1. 349–356: the solo violin alone on T's top octave is too weak against the octave below (applied)

**Bars:** 349–356 (coda, T's third coda statement; Vln I "div.: solo / gli altri").

**Problem.**
- Since c438, the melody's real pitch (C♯6–A6) is carried by one violin, pp.
- The octave below has the rest of Vln I and all of Vln II, also pp: about 22 players.
- So the line the listener should follow, the top, is the thinnest sound on the page. The lower octave takes over the tune.
- c371 had two solo violins there. c438 cut them to one because two like players in unison beat against each other (Casanelles PDF 147–148). That reason is sound, but it removed half the top line's strength.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov, PDF 124, lists two remedies for a melody in the upper register: "2 Vni soli or Violin solo + Fl. (Picc.)". One violin alone is not among them.
- Same page: a solo string doubled by a woodwind in unison gains "great purity and abundance of tone, without impairing the timbre of the solo instrument (especially in the high … registers)".
- Ex. 229 (Golden Cockerel, PDF 399–400) has piccolo pp dolce in unison with Viol. solo dolce, over muted string chords and pizz. bass. That is the texture of 349–356.
- Ex. 231 (Kitesh, PDF 402) puts piccolo plus flute over two solo violins at pp.
- Fl 1 is free: it rests from 341 to 360 and next enters at 361.

**How this answers c438.**
- c438's protection is that each voice carrying T in the coda is a single player, with no two like instruments in unison. The fix keeps that: still one violinist, now joined by one flute.
- A violin and a flute in unison are Rimsky's own "solo + Fl." pair, chosen because the flute does not impair the solo's timbre (PDF 124). Two violins in unison are not that pair.
- c371's reason (the top must not be piercing; strength goes into the lower octave) also still holds: pp, one string and one flute.

**Fix (applied in coda.ily, codaFluteOne).** Bars 349–356 replace the second `R2.*8`:

```
cis'''2(\pp\<^\markup \italic "dolce, col Vl. solo" dis'''4 | e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~-\tweak stencil ##f \p( | a'''4.\> gis'''8 fis'''4) | cis'''4( b''4 a''4) | gis''4-\tweak stencil ##f \pp( b''4 e'''4 | gis'''2-- fis'''8 e'''8~ | e'''4\> dis'''4-\tweak stencil ##f \pp fis'''4) | e'''2 r4\! |
```

- The flute has the same notes, slurs and hairpins as the solo violin, with the same hidden levels.
- The part combiner prints "1.".
- The rest of 357–360 and the pp entry at 361 are unchanged.

**Effect.**
- The top octave keeps the violin's colour and gains the flute's steadiness. A6 is reached under a hairpin with a hidden p, and Fl 1 already plays to A6 in this score.
- The trio tune's last full top-octave statement gets a colour of its own, different from Cl 1 (153), the cellos (169) and the violins (209).

**Checks.**
- Page 13 keeps its layout: the flute staff appears in the first system and the score stays at 13 pages.
- The fix does not move or silence any line. Fl 1's entry follows 9 bars' rest, so no new cue is needed.

### 2. v429 → v439: one thing got worse, and it is Finding 1 (fixed). Nothing else got worse (no change)

- **c431 (Hn 3 with the cellos at 169–176) and c436 (Bsn 2 doubling the basses at 169–180)** follow Rimsky's harmonic-basis rule (PDF 132–133):
  - the melody (cellos + Hn 3, p–mp) and its background (Vln II con sord. pp, an octave and more above) differ in timbre and register;
  - the bass (Cb arco + Bsn 2, pp) sits an octave below.
- **That passage is now full.** I agree with c436's note that later cycles should thin rather than add there. I tested no thinning: no line in it breaks a rule from this reading.
- **c437's violas an octave down at 197–200** follow principle 1, rescoring a return.
- **c430's Vln I G♯5 at 1–2** is principle 8's thin frame used on purpose, and is protected.

### 3. Mahler's weight and irregular trio metre (no change)

- The largest differences from Mahler 6/iii are its stamped crotchet ostinato with accented brass and its irregular trio metre.
- Each is excluded by a recorded rule: the soft-brass rule (c123), the hammered tutti kept for the finale, and 3/4 throughout.
- The reading gives no reason stronger than those rules, so I propose neither.
- The nearest permitted equivalent, the timpani as a motivic voice (motive x at 9–12, 89–100, 241–249, 363–371), is already present and protected.
