## Holistic comparison with Mahler 5/iii

- **Form.** Mahler's scherzo is a long, multi-section movement: a D-major Ländler, a slower waltz, and a horn-led central trio. The themes come back in a development that combines them. This movement keeps a Classical scherzo–trio–scherzo frame and a two-stage coda. That fits its models (Dvořák 9/iii, Beethoven 9/ii) and its place before a finale that owns development and combination ("the combined heads" are kept for the finale). Mahler's method of combining themes would be the wrong model here, and I do not propose it.
- **One instrument as the movement's voice.** Mahler gives the obbligato horn the identity of the movement. This movement does the same on a smaller scale. Hn 1 has the opening G♯ and call (1–6), the G→G♯ turns at 141–152 and 237–241, and the motto tail and the last G♯ (357–376). Hn 3 has T's head at 341. Mahler's stopped-horn echoes and bells-up effects stay excluded (c149, soft-brass rule).
- **Recolouring a recurring passage.** Adler's Mahler 4 example (PDF 169–171) is the principle Mahler also applies in 5/iii. T is scored four ways (153, 169, 209, 341–356), and theme A goes through seven settings, each recognisable by its texture. This is the strongest Mahlerian trait in the score.
- **Chamber-like scoring inside the full orchestra** (Mahler's Kindertotenlieder, PDF 157–159). It appears at 197–200, 225–236 and the closing page, where the colour changes almost note by note. Elsewhere the score doubles in octaves the way Brahms and Dvořák do, which matches the models in the brief.
- **Would a conductor believe a master wrote it?** On the scoring, yes. Bar by bar I found 42 applicable principles of this reading followed and one small breach (finding 1). A conductor who knows Mahler 5 would find this movement compact and Classical rather than Mahlerian, and that is the brief's aim.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 332: the timpani roll into the coda's ff has no printed starting level (applied)
- **Problem.** At 329 the timpani play E mf, rest two bars, then roll on B with a crescendo into the ff at 333 (`b,2.:32\<`). No level is printed at the roll. Every other build roll in the movement prints its start: 101 and 301 p<, 205 pp, 129 and 285 f.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 184 (Bloch, Ex. XV-30) says the percussion in a build are marked at a level below the orchestra and then crescendo, "because a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra". A roll with no starting mark leaves the player to choose, and the natural choice after a rest before a ff is too loud. At 332 the strings are f (Vln I) and the basses crescendo from mf. The roll should start under them.
- **Fix (applied).** In coda.ily, `codaTimp`: `b,2.:32\<` → `b,2.:32\mf\<`. The MIDI already plays this level, so only the print changes.

No other finding. I considered three more points and did not propose them:
- **Timpani roll f at 129–130 / 285–286 (Bloch, PDF 184).** It doubles the sustained V pedal (Vc/Cb/Bsn B, tied two bars) at the orchestra's own f under a cadence, not a build, so it covers nothing.
- **Strings-only fugato 57–68 (Jupiter, PDF 176: one wind per entry).** Kept by c007, c017 and c254. Adler's own point that a colour should be kept intact long enough supports it.
- **The reprise tutti 305–320 scored like 105–120 (Mahler 4, PDF 170).** It already differs: no trombones or tuba (c033), Hn 4 on the bass (c115), Fl 1 high (c025), Vln II low (c029).
