## Holistic comparison with Dvořák 9/iii

**Plan.** This movement takes its key, metre and genre from Dvořák's scherzo, and the parallels hold at the large scale:
- an E-minor Molto vivace / Allegro vivace with one beat to the bar;
- the timpani as a melodic voice (Dvořák's E–B figure at bars 1–4; motive x at 9–12 here);
- a lyrical contrast inside the scherzo before the trio (Dvořák's Poco sostenuto at 68; the F-major episode at 73–88 here);
- a softer trio;
- a coda that quotes the symphony's earlier music and dissolves, with the opening figure coming back more and more sparsely.

**Differences.** Each one is required by the cyclic plan, not a weakness:
- Dvořák opens with a ff wind chord; this score opens with a pp solo horn on G♯ (movement II's A♭).
- Dvořák ends with ff solo strokes after ppp pizzicati; this score ends ppp on the horn's G♯ for the finale.
- Dvořák uses string tremolo, triangle and a C-major trio. All three are reserved for the finale here.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** On the page, yes for craft. Voice leading, doubling, spacing and the dynamic plan are checked at every level. The trio's colour changes and the closing page (357–376) are as good as anything in the model.

The one place where Dvořák's practice is plainly sharper than this score's is the hand-over into a lyrical woodwind solo. Before his Poco sostenuto, the accompaniment (Vln II, Va, Vc) enters p at 64 and is taken down to pp by 67. The Fl and Ob solos then enter p at 68, a clear level above their bed, and the triangle and trumpet join pp (Mutopia p. 6). That is the same rule Casanelles draws from Mahler. This score already follows it under every trio and coda solo (harp pp at 153, 185, 341; c099). In the F-major episode it does not (Finding 1).

The gap earlier critics named, that the second idea and T are well made rather than unforgettable, is still there. The assigned reading is about sound and mixing, not melody, and gives no ground for rewriting either tune on the last cycle. So I make no melodic change.

## Findings

### 1. Harp stays at its melody's level when it becomes the accompaniment at 81–86 (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 81–86 (sB.ily, sBHarpL).

**Problem.** At 73–80 the harp carries the episode's melody: p espress., with hairpins to a hidden mp and back to p. At 81 it hands the tune to Cl 1, which enters p dolce. The harp turns into the accompaniment: RH off-beat chords A3–D4–F4 / B♭3–D4, and long LH bass notes. But it has no new dynamic, so it carries on at p. The accompaniment therefore sits at the same printed level as the solo it now supports.

The v399 MIDI shows the effect. At 81.2 the harp plays at velocity 53 against Cl 1's 39, so the render plays the harp's chords louder than the tune. The horn, bassoon and bass-clarinet chord under the same bars is pp.

The score's own practice is a level lower in every comparable place:
- harp pp under Cl 1's p at 153 and under Ob 1's p at 185 (c099);
- harp pp under Hn 3's p at 341;
- harp pp under Ob 1's p at 225.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 271–272: the printed dynamic states the level to be heard, and the layer that joins or supports is marked under the one it accompanies (Mahler's ppp trumpets under pp woodwinds; Fig. 6.1, PDF 272).
- PDF 280 (Moylan): balance is set by what the music should say. Here the clarinet's answer is what the music says.
- Dvořák 9/iii, bars 64–68 (Mutopia p. 5–6): the accompaniment is taken from p to pp before the Fl/Ob solo enters p at the Poco sostenuto.
- c099's recorded do-not-touch reason ("accompaniment one dynamic level under the tune", Casanelles PDF 271–278) is the same rule. 81 is the one place it was missed.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-399/work/sections/sB.ily).** I added pp on the LH's first note at 81. The harp staff carries one set of dynamics for both hands (score.ly: Dynamic_performer on the PianoStaff), and the LH's dynamics print above it, between the staves:

```
old (sBHarpL): ... f,4 <c f a>2 | d,2. | bes,,2. | g,,2. | a,,2. | ...
new (sBHarpL): ... f,4 <c f a>2 | d,2.\pp | bes,,2. | g,,2. | a,,2. | ...
```

I also added a comment line (c399) above sBHarpR.

My first try put the pp under the RH chord. That pushed page 3 over-full ("compressing over-full page by 2.5 staff-spaces"), so I moved it to the LH. With the pp in the LH, the pp prints between the staves beside "non arpegg." on the existing system.

**Verified in the scratch build:**
- Both hands now play at velocity 41 from 81 (the same as the harp's pp at 153 and 225), under Cl 1's 37–44.
- No pitch, rhythm or layout change.
- The étouffez at 87 and the LH chords at 85–86 now sound pp.

## Considered and not changed

- **375: Vc/Cb pizz. pp and harp harmonics p over Hn 1 and Vln I ppp.** By the letter of principle 1, a layer joining a ppp texture would be ppp, and Dvořák's dissolving coda uses ppp pizzicati (Mutopia p. 27). I keep it:
  - A pizzicato and a harp harmonic are single attacks that decay at once. They do not form a sustained layer competing with the held G♯.
  - Harmonics are inherently very soft, and Adler PDF 116 (c116) asks for them to be marked up, not down.
  - c158 kept these levels.
- **205–208: flutes a2 an octave above Hn 1's motto preview.** Casanelles PDF 277–278 says an octave doubling at the same level fuses into the horn's spectrum, so this is horn plus flutes, not a solo horn. I keep it: c387 chose the combination on Rimsky-Korsakov's melody-in-combined-groups grounds, and it builds into the trio's return at 209. The cyclic rule asks for the horn to quote the motto, and it does.
- **Horns ff at 333–338 (Casanelles PDF 275: loud brass's dissonant upper partials).** The horns are already one level under the fff strings and winds, and the trumpets are at f. This answers both the reading and Dan's "weight rather than bite". c199 is kept.
- **A rewrite of the second idea or of T for memorability.** The reading gives no ground for it, and it would not be fully verifiable on the last cycle.
