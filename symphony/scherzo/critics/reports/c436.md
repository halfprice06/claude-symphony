## Findings

### 1. Bars 169–180: the double basses play the trio's moving bass alone, below the bass voice (applied)

**Problem.** In T's second statement (cellos espress., Hn 3 to 176, Vln II con sord. quavers), the bass line is E2–G♯1–B1–E2 | A1–F♯1–F♯1–B1 | C♯2–G♯1–F♯1–E2. c433 made it I–I6–V–I moving in tenths with the cellos' tune. Only the arco double basses play it, pp. Half of its notes (G♯1, A1, F♯1, B1) lie below the bass voice. At 181 Bsn 2 joins the same line an octave higher (c372), so the line has a pitch partner only for its last four bars. A soft double bass alone on G♯1 and F♯1 gives weight but little pitch, so the tenths with the tune that c433 wrote are weak in the hall.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27: "notes on the double bass below the range of the bass voice ... lose in expression and warmth", and "Except in the case of pedal notes, the double bass rarely plays an independent part, usually moving in octaves or in unison with the 'cellos, or else doubling the bassoons. The quality of the double bass tone is therefore seldom heard by itself." Here the cellos are on the tune, so the bassoon is the partner he names. PDF 42 and Table B (PDF 35): the bassoon's low register is "full", and the bassoon is one of the instruments that double the bass group. The score already does this at 181–184.

**Fix (applied, tA.ily).** Bsn 2 doubles the basses an octave higher from 169, pp, in two eight-bar slurs with a breath at 176|177. The line runs straight into its existing entry at 181. Bsn 1 rests, and the shared staff prints "2.".
```
tABsnOne = { R2.*16 | \partCombineSoloII R2.*16 | \partCombineAutomatic }
tABsnTwo = { R2.*16 | \partCombineSoloII e2.--(\pp | gis,2.-- | b,2.-- | e2.--) | a,2.--( | fis,2.~ | fis,2. | b,2.--) \breathe
             | cis2.--(\< | gis,2.-- | fis,2.-- | e2.--) | fis,2.~\p( | fis,2. | b,2.~ | b,2) r4 | \partCombineAutomatic }
```
Register: the new line (F♯2–E3) stays below the cellos and Hn 3 throughout. Its closest point is E3 under their G♯3 at 169, a plain tonic third. It does not cross the tune. Pitches added: none outside the existing harmony, since every note doubles the bass. Bsn 2 now rests 149–168 (20 bars), so it needs a cue: parts-cues.json part "10" gains `["cueClOne","Cl. 1","UP",165,4,"treble"]` (Cl 1's T cadence, which sounds at 165–167). The Bassoon 2 part renders the cue in treble clef and still has 2 pages with the same turn. c429's do-not-touch item (the two statements differ in bass: harp/pizz. 153–168, arco basses 169–183) still holds. This is not c087's case: c087 took Bsn 2 off an octave-bass line at 209 because it crossed the cellos' countermelody, and here the cellos are two octaves up on the tune.

Why this is the change a listener hears most: it is the only place in the movement where a moving bass line lies in the double basses alone for twelve bars. It sits under the movement's quietest tune statement, where nothing else in the bass register covers it.

### Checked and not changed

- **Contrabassoon 210–220 (E2–E3, an octave above the basses).** Table B and PDF 38 call this register little used and "by no means so useful". I considered putting the contrabassoon in unison with the basses and giving the octave line to Bsn 2. I did not apply it. It would undo c087's cello-plus-bassoon pairing on the countermelody (Rimsky PDF 76–77) and reopen the two-octave gap at 219 that c114 filled (Rimsky appendix). The part is soft (mp–mf), has a breath at 216, and c306 kept it. Kept.
- **Harp LH 75–80 (C3–A3 chords on beat 2, a new chord every bar at 2. = 88).** By PDF 46 these middle strings ring into the next chord, giving faint seconds at 79–80 (D/F/A under E–B♭, then E/B♭ under F). The winds (Hn 3·4, Bsn 1, B.cl.) hold the same harmonies pp, which covers most of the overlap. The cure would be crotchet chords with damping on beat 3, and that would dry out the dolce colour that is protected for 73–88 (c014, c116). Kept.
- **Coda 2 soli on the upper octave, C♯6–A6 pp (340–347).** PDF 27 says this range loses warmth. Here the thin, cool sound is the intended colour (c371), and the melody is carried by the rest of Vln I and Vln II an octave lower. Kept.
- **Hn 1's last G♯4 at 368–376**, about 12 s from pp to ppp with no relief (PDF 37, 44). It is a soft middle-register note that uses little air, and the pivot into the finale is protected. Kept, as c246, c316 and c346 kept it.
