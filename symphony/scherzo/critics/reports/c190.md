## Findings

### 1. Bars 145–148: the pad before the trio is in the soloist's own colour (applied)

Problem. Cl 1·2 hold E4/B3 pp under Hn 1's G♯4 at 145–148, Cl 1 rests four bars, and then Cl 1 opens the trio with T, dolce, at 153. The introduction to the trio's first solo is therefore played partly by the solo instrument itself, and the clarinet colour is not new when the tune arrives. This is the one place in the movement where a solo theme enters without a change of colour.

Evidence. Adler PDF 263: to introduce the soloist, composers use "a distinctly contrasting color combination", so that the solo colour is "very fresh"; the Dvořák and Tchaikovsky slow movements open with the woodwind choir before a string soloist (Ex. XVII-7, XVII-8, PDF 263–264). PDF 267, item 4: Mozart writes no orchestral flutes in the flute concerto so that "the solo flute will not encounter any color competition". PDF 257–258 (dialogue): the colour of the material around the solo is chosen "to avoid duplication of the solo sound". In this score the other solo entries already work this way: Hn 1's call at 3 comes out of silence; Ob 1's call at 185 follows the trio's string statement; the coda moves T from Hn 1 to Ob 1 to Vln I. In the link the bassoons are free (Bsn 1 B2 at 141–144, then rests; Bsn 2 rests 141–152), and the bassoons already hold B3 under the clarinets in the intro at 7–8, so the "intro in reverse" keeps its bassoon colour.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-190/work/sections/link.ily). Same pitches, rhythm, dynamics and hairpins, moved from the clarinets to the bassoons; Cl 1·2 rest 137–152, so Cl 1's dolce at 153 is the first clarinet sound for 16 bars:

E4 and B3 are in the bassoon's tenor register, where pp is secure, so c036's reason for moving the E2 pedal off the bassoons (pp in the lowest fifth) does not apply; the bass clarinet keeps the E2 pedal. The harmony, the Hn 1 line (G–G♯, the G♯–A–G♯ foreshadowing at 147–148, c051) and the link's length are unchanged, so this does not touch what c153 and c163 protected in the link (its form and proportions, not its scoring). Both bassoons have equal simultaneous dynamics on the shared staff. Not a theme change; nothing changes for the finale. In the parts, the Cl 1·2 rest becomes 16 bars (137–152); a bassoon cue in the clarinet parts is not needed, since Hn 1's G♯4 at 145–148 stays the cue for Cl 1's G♯4 at 153.
