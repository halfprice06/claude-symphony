## Findings

### 1. Bars 45–46: the I6 under the second idea is almost all third, and the violas double Cl 2 at the unison (applied)

**Problem.** The second idea opens in G over the bass G–B–G (Vc, Cb, Bsn 1, Cbsn). On beat 3 of 45, tied into 46, the bass is B, so the chord is I6. The melody is also on B (Vln I and Fl 1 B5, Vln II and Ob 1 B4), and Hn 1 holds B4. The fifth, D, is heard only in Cl 1's D5. The violas repeat G4 in unison with Cl 2's G4. In this one bar the scan counts nine instruments on B, three on G and one on D. The violas are also the only inner part that does not move with the hemiola minims here: at 47, 49 and 51 they move on each minim (A–G–D, C♯–A–C♯, A–D–G).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 257 and Ex. VIII-25 say that in a first inversion "the third (bass) should be doubled toward the bottom rather than the top. The 'open' quality of the first inversion is thus preserved." Here the melody already puts the bass's B on top, and that cannot change, so the inner parts should not add to it. The same page says a unison doubling "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch". The Cl 2/viola G4 is such a unison, and it falls in a forte legato passage, not a tutti. On the lens: an inner part that moves against the bass helps the texture (ch. 8 §6, PDF 271).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-277/work/sections/sA.ily, sAVla):**

```
g'2--(\mf g'4--~ | g'4 g'2--)   ->   g'2--(\mf d'4--~ | d'4 g'2--)
```

The violas now play G4–D4–G4 against the bass G2–B2–G2, in contrary motion. Against the bass B they supply the fifth D. They leave the unison with Cl 2 and return to G4 on beat 2 of 46, as before. The slur, the louré dashes (c218) and the dynamic are unchanged, so the bowing is unchanged. The new D4 makes an octave with Cl 1's D5 by contrary motion, inside one G-major harmony. That is a doubling of a chord tone, not a voice-leading fault. The scan now gives four B, two G and two D instruments at 45–46. No theme, bass or protected line changes. The chord at 49 already has the shape this gives 45: the inner voices hold the root and the third sounds only in the bass and the melody.

### 4. Wind counterpoint and the fugato (checked, kept)

The countersubject is real: it is the cellos' line at 61 transposed for the violas at 65, with the cellos' third line in minims. The winds imitate in chains at 92, 225–236 and 245. No change.

### 5. Bars 325–328: the ostinato sounds motive x at the fifth and octave (checked, kept)

The analyser lists 326–328 as fifths and octaves. Each string section enters a bar apart in the same rhythm, so this is one figure doubled in four registers that thickens bar by bar. It is not four independent lines. This matches Adler's demand for each gesture in its best register (PDF 274, Ex. VIII-44). The build is protected (c131 and others). It is the movement's only additive build of this kind, and the winds' sustained harmony from 329 is what PDF 252–253 describes. No change.
