## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 128→129 and 284→285: the bass leaves the applied leading tone A♯ by a falling major seventh. APPLIED.
**Problem.** The E-minor second idea reaches its hemiola 6/4 through VII°7 of V. c412's comment describes this as "A sharp-C sharp-E-G over the bass's rising half step A sharp-B". In the score, however, every bass instrument sounds the A♯ an octave above its B. At 128 the instruments are Vc A♯3→B2, Bsn 2 A♯3→B2, Cb A♯2→B1 and Cbsn A♯2→B1. At 284 Bsn 2, Cb and Cbsn do the same; there the cellos have the tenor tune. So the leading tone of V falls a major seventh. The bass then falls again, a fifth to E at 131/287: two leaps down in a row that outline no chord. The bass and the tune (C♯6→B5) also move down together into the octave on B, where contrary motion was available. These are the loudest bass notes of the passage (crescendo to f, timpani roll on B), and the bass's rising semitone is the gesture the harmony is built on.
**Evidence.** PDF 76: the leading tone has an "active tendency toward 1". PDF 82: dissonant leaps "are excluded entirely from the simplest vocal styles". PDF 81, Ex. 5-11b and 5-12: after a 6th, turn back by step, and two leaps in one direction only when they outline a chord. PDF 82: contrary motion gives the most independence. PDF 87–88, rule 7: skips emphasize hidden octaves.
**Fix** (sC.ily, dA.ily), the A♯ an octave lower:
```
sCVc     … e2( fis4~ | fis4 ais,2) | b,2.~\f …     (was ais2)
sCBsnTwo … e2 fis4~ | fis4 ais,2 | b,2.~\f …       (was ais2)
sCCbsn   … e,2( fis,4~ | fis,4 ais,,2) | b,,2.~\f … (was ais,2)
sCCb     … e,2( fis,4~ | fis,4 ais,,2) | b,,2.~\f … (was ais,2)
dABsnTwo … fis4 ais,2 | b,2.\f~ …    dACbsn/dACb … fis,4 ais,,2) | b,,2.\f~ …
```
The bass now runs E–F♯, falls a minor sixth to A♯ (a consonant leap), and rises a semitone to B (Ex. 5-11b, "good"). The tune falls C♯→B as the bass rises A♯→B, so the outer voices meet the 6/4 by contrary motion. c392's tenths stay compound tenths: Bsn 1's C♯4 over A♯2 and A♯1. The lowest sonority is still an octave (A♯1/A♯2), so nothing turns muddy (PDF 77). The hemiola, its harmony and the tune are unchanged.

### 2. 52→53: the cellos fall a major seventh C♯3→D2 into the G-major 6/4. APPLIED.
**Problem.** c432 recorded that both phrases of the G-major second idea end on a rising chromatic bass, C♯–D into 53. Bsn 1, the contrabassoon and the basses do rise a semitone. The cellos, the 8-foot bass and the most audible of the four, leap down a major seventh to D2 and sit in unison with the basses. The cadential 6/4 at 53–54 then has no cello octave above the basses.
**Evidence.** PDF 82 (dissonant leaps), PDF 76 (leading-tone tendency), PDF 80 (stepwise bass motion gives melodic quality).
**Fix** (sA.ily): `sAVc … e2( d4~ | d4 cis2) | d2.~ | d2. | g,2. | g,4-. r4 r4 |` (was `d,2.~ | d,2.`). The cellos rise C♯3→D3, in unison with Bsn 1's D3 (c294), and fall a fourth to G2 at 55. Cb and Cbsn keep D2. Against the tune's E6→D6 the cello's rise is contrary motion into the octave.

### 3. 179–181: the basses leap a seventh up and back for a lower neighbour. APPLIED.
**Problem.** Under the second trio statement's turn to B (c093), the bass is F♯ (179), E (ii6/5 of B, 180), F♯ (cadential 6/4, 181). The basses play F♯1–E2–F♯1 and Bsn 2 plays F♯2–E3–F♯2: a minor seventh up and back down, around a note that is a lower neighbour by function. The basses' E1 is available as their open string.
**Evidence.** PDF 80: stepwise motion gives the bass a melodic quality. PDF 82: dissonant leaps excluded. Ex. 5-11.
**Fix** (tA.ily): `tACb … fis,,2. | e,,2. | fis,,2.~ …` (was `e,2.`). `tABsnTwo … fis,2.-- | e,2.--) | fis,2.~\p( …` (was `e2.--)`). The bass now descends C♯2–G♯1–F♯1–E1 and turns back to F♯1 by step. Both instruments are pp and in range.

### 4. 88: the seventh of A♯°7 is doubled (Cl 2 and Bsn 1 on G3). Not changed.
Cl 2 (F♯3→G3) and Bsn 1 (A3→G3) converge on the chord's seventh. PDF 76 says the seventh "must never be doubled". The two are at the unison, pp, in one blended colour (PDF 85, unison doubling). Cl 2 stops at 89, and Bsn 1 holds G as the common tone into the 6/4, so only one G continues. The alternatives are Cl 2 E3 (a unison with Hn 4) or C♯4 (a fifth leap). Either one breaks c180's line D♯3–F♯3–G3, and neither would be heard. I decided not to change it.

### 5. 300→301: Vln II E5→B4 and the cellos' upper E3→B2 in octaves out of the Italian sixth. Not changed.
The Italian sixth (c422) triples E in the strings alone. Two of the E's fall a fourth to B in octaves (PDF 83, Ex. 5-18). At 301 the whole orchestra enters ff on V, so the number of parts changes (PDF 84, free texture), and the octave is covered by sixteen new entries. Removing the cellos' E3 would undo the doubled third c422 asked for (PDF 505). I decided not to change it.
