## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 277-280: the flute carries the tune alone on top in its weakest melodic octave, at the same level as the oboe an octave below, and with no shaping (applied)

**Problem.** In the reprise the second idea's antecedent (E major) is in Fl 1 at B5-E6, with Ob 1 an octave lower at B4-E5. Both are marked sub. p and espress.; the violins rest until the upbeat to 281, and the fill (Cl 1·2, Hn 2, Hn 3, violas) is pp since c458. Two things are wrong. First, the flute is in its second octave, where Adler says it is "sweet and with little carrying power". The oboe is in F4-C6, its "warm, prominent, reedy and poignant" range, at the same marked level. Played as written, the tune will sound an octave lower in oboe colour, with the flute as a halo above it. Second, the phrase has no shaping at all. When the violins play the same phrase at 121-124, both octaves swell from sub. p into the E6 of bar 122 (hidden mp) and fall back to a hidden p at 124 (c448's rule that each melodic suspension swells into the dissonance and falls to the resolution). At 277-280 the winds play a flat sub. p.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 185: a register called weak is a warning not to "cover" the instrument, because "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume out of his instrument in that register". PDF 185, Ex. VII-2: the flute's second octave is "sweet and with little carrying power". PDF 198, Ex. VII-32: the oboe is at its most effective from F4 to C6. PDF 201: the oboe is "a most poignant doubler"; "great care must be given to have it double a significant line so that an instrument that needs to be featured more prominently is not overshadowed". The soft flute solos Adler prints are all shaped inside the phrase: Brahms 4/iv is "mp" with `<>` on each figure (PDF 186, Ex. VII-4), Tchaikovsky's is "p dolcissimo" with a long `<` and `>` (Ex. VII-5), and Debussy's is "p <" (PDF 187, Ex. VII-6). The score already treats this phrase that way when the strings play it (121-124).

**Fix (sections/dA.ily, dAFluteOne), as built.** Fl 1 gets Vln I's swell from bar 121. Ob 1 stays level at sub. p, so the flute rises above it into E6, where the flute's third octave begins to carry:
```
b''4.->\subP\<(^\markup \italic "espress." a''8 gis''4~ | gis''4 e'''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\>) | dis'''2( cis'''4~ | cis'''4-\tweak stencil ##f \p b''4. d'''8) |
```
(was `b''4.->\subP(... | gis''4 e'''2) | dis'''2( cis'''4~ | cis'''4 b''4. d'''8) |`). The printed result is sub. p with `<` to the E6 and `>` back, and the hidden levels work only in the MIDI, as at 121. No notes, slurs, words or bar lengths change. Fl 2 rests here, so the shared flute staff has one set of dynamics. In score.midi, Fl 1's velocity now rises 37 → 40 → 44 at the E6 and returns to 37 by 280. Ob 1 is unchanged at 37.

I decided against also making the oboe softer ("sub. pp"). c458 put the fill a level under the tune because the fill lies in the oboe's octave, and Cl 1's G♯4-B4 crosses Ob 1 at 277-278. An oboe at pp would sink into that fill. Leaving the oboe level and letting the flute swell gives the flute the lead without undoing c458.

### 2. Bar 277: Ob 1 at sub. pp instead (considered, not applied)

This answers the doubling rule (PDF 201) more literally, but it costs c458's layering, as explained above. It would also need a new markup dynamic, and score.ly's MIDI map plays every markup dynamic at the p volume, so the MIDI would not change. Finding 1 gets the balance in both print and MIDI without that cost.

### 3. Bars 29-36: grade the two wind calls a level apart, after Tchaikovsky 6/i (considered, not applied)

Ex. VII-93 (PDF 227) raises each two-bar group by one level (pp, p, mp). In this score the calls at 29 and 33 are both p, and the sequence crescendos only from 37 to f at 41. I kept it. 29-36 is the plateau before the order of growth that c408 protected (strings, then woodwind, then brass, into 105). Raising 33 would take away the step that 37 makes. The low B4 of Fl 1 at 29/33 is in unison with Ob 1, so it does not need its own level (c311, c561, c577).

### 4. Bar 361: clarinets enter niente (N < pp) on the closing E-major chord (considered, not applied)

Adler names the niente attack as the clarinet's own effect (PDF 211, Ex. VII-57), and 361-364 is a held soft clarinet chord (E5/B4). But 361 is the resolution of the plagal close (iv6/4 → I under Hn 1's G♯4). It is struck together by Fl 1, Hn 4 and the strings, and a niente start in two of those parts would blur the arrival. This is the same reason c478 gave for bar 7. The closing page is protected for the motto tail, the last G♯ and timpani motive x, and this change would touch none of them, but it would make the music worse.

### 5. Bars 121, 180, 277: Bsn 2 on E2, in the bassoon's lowest fifth (checked, follows)

Adler PDF 226 says this fifth cannot be played pianissimo and needs a firmer attack. At 121 and 277 the E2 is sub. p straight after f, so a firm attack is what is wanted. At 180 the E2 is the top of a crescendo from pp, and the next printed dynamic is p. No change.
