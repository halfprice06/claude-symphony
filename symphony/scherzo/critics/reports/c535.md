## Findings

### 1. Bar 333: Vln I arrives on the coda's ff downbeat with an up-bow (applied)

**Problem.** At 333 the full orchestra reaches ff, and theme A's head returns in the strings. Vln I enters at 328 and plays four separate notes a bar through 331. Bar 332 has three notes and a rest (`dis'''4-.\f b''8-. b''-. r4`). Counted from 328, 333 therefore falls on an up-bow, and the ff E6 is attacked at the tip. The other sections count differently. Vln II and the violas reach 333 on a down-bow by count, and the cellos have a printed ⊓ there. The marked ⊓ at 334 in Vln I was written as if 333 already began down-bow: with four notes in 333, it is redundant only from a down-bow start. As printed, a first violinist who follows the count plays the loudest downbeat so far at the point, with less weight than the three sections under it.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog of the bow than toward the tip", and the composer "should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players". The habit here is the count, which gives an up-bow. Ex. II-20 (*Coriolan*) marks the bow so that "the following attack will be solid". Ex. II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii, Allegro molto vivace) marks ⊓ on each ff attack in the violins as in the lower strings. The one-beat rest at 332 lasts 0.21 s at 2. = 96. Many players would retake there, but the score should not leave the top line of the ff arrival to chance when the cellos are marked. This is the change under this reading that a listener would hear most: the first violins' E6 lands together with the rest of the strings, at full weight, on the downbeat that opens the coda's climb to B6 at 336.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily, `codaVlnOne`).**
```
dis'''4-.\f b''8-. b''-. r4 | e'''4-.\ff\downbow b''8-. b''-. e'''4-. | g'''2->\dnb( ...
```
A c535 comment is added. No sign is added to Vln II or the violas, because they already reach ⊓ by count and a sign would overmark (PDF 38). The ⊓ at 334 stays; it now agrees with the count. I checked the result at 220 dpi: ⊓ above the staccato E6 at 333 and above the accented G6 at 334, with no collision.

### 2. Bar 177: Vln I's up-bow is printed below the stave (applied, print only)

**Problem.** `cis''2(\p\<_\upbow` forces the V under the note, between the C♯5 and the *p*. Every other bow sign in the score is above the stave (in all five string staves and the parts), and the V at 349, the same bowing on the same phrase of T, is above. A sign below the stave, beside the dynamic, is easy to read as part of the hairpin or to miss.

**Evidence.** All of Adler's bowing examples on PDF 37–45 (II-18 to II-36) place ⊓ and V above the stave, and PDF 54 asks that score and part carry the same instructions. The bowing itself is right: c418 counted the V crescendo at 177 to reach the climax A5 at 178 on a down-bow.

**Fix (applied, tA.ily, `tAVlnOne`).** `cis''2(\p\<\upb^\markup \italic "dolce espress." ...`, so the V uses c445's `\upb` (avoid-slur ignore), as c445 asked for every new bow sign on a slurred note. At 220 dpi the V sits above the slur's start, under "dolce espress." and "sul A", with no collision. The Violin I part rebuilds with 0 warnings.

### 3. Rechecked and kept: the cellos' redundant ⊓ at 333 and the bow counts in the other tuttis

The cellos reach 333 on ⊓ by count, so their printed ⊓ there is redundant. I kept it. It marks the bass of the ff attack, and with finding 1 the top and bottom of the string section are now marked alike. Removing it would change nothing a listener hears. I counted the other tutti arrivals the same way. At 72 and 300 the ⊓ retakes are marked. At 45 and 121 the V is marked for the crescendo (c445). At 305, the six new notes of the hemiola after the marked ⊓ V at 300 bring every section to ⊓. At 101 and 105, Vln II (eighteen notes from 95) reaches ⊓. Vln I does too, provided it retakes in the two beats of rest at 97: its head ends there, and a new four-bar crescendo phrase starts at 98. That retake is the players' habit after a phrase ending, so no mark is needed (PDF 38). Bar 332 is different. Its single beat of rest, 0.21 s, falls inside one continuous f–ff build, and in that build the bow is moving at speed, so the habit is to keep counting. That difference is why finding 1 marks 333 and nothing is marked at 98.

### 4. Considered and rejected: senza vibrato on the frame G♯5 (PDF 33)

Adler offers *senza vibrato* for "a white, pale sound". The held ppp G♯5 at 1–7 and 364–376 is the only place where that colour could serve, as movement II's A♭ heard without warmth. I did not build it, for two reasons. The marking is a twentieth-century device that none of the models (Brahms, Dvořák, Tchaikovsky) uses. And c526 chose "sul A" precisely so that the violins' tone would merge with Hn 1's pp G♯4. A vibrato-less violin would split from the horn instead of blending with it.

### 5. Considered and rejected: ⊓ series on the hemiola 101–104 / 301–304 (Ex. II-29)

Tchaikovsky's down-bow series is the obvious model for accented minims at f. c318 kept the alternating martelé minims (c085), and Bruckner 9/ii (Ex. II-37) alternates in the same way. A retake on every minim at 2. = 88 would leave gaps in a passage built on tied minims across the bar line. The series is saved for 336–339, where it closes the scherzo. No change.
