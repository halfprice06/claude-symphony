## What else I studied

I wrote a parser for the section files (in /tmp/critic-468/scripts) that places every note, rest, printed or hidden dynamic, hairpin and accent on the bar grid. With it I listed: hairpins that do not end on a printed level; entries after rests that have no printed dynamic; and, for every pair of parts playing the same rhythm and pitch classes in a bar, every difference in hairpins and accents. Most differences are deliberate: brass print tenuto where woodwind and strings print accents (Dan's soft-brass rule, c123); single held notes coincide by chance; and the second idea's low doubling at 285 has its own notes. Three differences are not deliberate, and they are the findings below. As support I used the composer's own rule recorded by c448 (A&S PDF 51, 55), "every melodic suspension swells into the dissonance and falls to the resolution", and Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 112–113 on horns doubled by cellos as one colour (the reason c447 gave for the Hn 3/Vc pairing).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 110–111 and 310–311: the tenor countermelody swells in the cellos only, while the bassoon and horns that double it play flat (applied)

Problem: in both theme-A tuttis the tenor countermelody G3–B3–E4… is played in unison by Vc, Bsn 1 and Hn 3 (Hn 4 joins at 105). Its sixth bar is `a2( c'4~ | c'4 b4 a4`. The C4 is tied over the bar line against V (B–D♯–F♯) and resolves to B3, so it is a ♭9–8 suspension and the one peak of the line. The cellos swell into it and fall away: `c'4\<~ | c'4-\tweak stencil ##f \fff\> b4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff`. Bsn 1, Hn 3 and Hn 4 have no hairpin. In a ff tutti the cellos cannot make the swell heard on their own while three wind players hold the same notes at a steady level. The listener hears a flat line where the score asks for the countermelody's single expressive point. This is the change in this cycle that a listener will hear most. It is also the composer's own rule (c448: every melodic suspension swells into the dissonance and falls to the resolution), which so far is carried out by one staff out of four.

Evidence: MOLA PDF 8: "equal attention should be given to both the vertical and horizontal lines of music when proofreading". Ex. 6 (PDF 11): in Beethoven's tutti every staff that plays the line carries the same marks, down to Fag. and Cfg. Looking through the player's eyes (PDF 8), the Bsn 1 and Hn 3 parts show no swell at the line's peak.

Fix (applied):
- sC `sCBsnOne`, `sCHnFour`: `a2( c'4\<~ | c'4-\tweak stencil ##f \fff\> b4-\tweak stencil ##f \ff a4`; `sCHnThree` the same, with its `-\sysLabel "a2"` kept on the tied C.
- dC `dCBsnOne`, `dCHnThree`: the same.
- Shared staves: Bsn 2 (sC and dC) and Hn 4 (dC) play the bass under the countermelody. They get the same hairpin at the same moments so that the part-combined staves keep equal dynamics: `\after 2 \< a,2.` then `\after 4 -\tweak stencil ##f \ff b,2.-\tweak stencil ##f \fff\>` (Hn 4 with its slur `a,2.(`). The score prints one hairpin per staff. Each player's part shows the swell.

Brass: this is a swell inside a held line in a ff tutti, not an entry and not an accent, so it follows Dan's rule that brass is shaped by dynamics. In the MIDI the tied note is not struck again, so the mockup barely changes. The change is for the players.

### 2. Bar 281: the clarinets double the reprise second phrase without the accent that every other staff has (applied)

Problem: at 281 the second idea's E-minor consequent starts with `c4.->` in Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II and Vc (Hn 2 has tenuto by rule). Cl 1 doubles Ob 1 in unison (C5) and Cl 2 doubles Vc in unison (C4), but both print `c''4.\p\<(` / `c'4.\p\<(` with no accent. In the clarinet parts this is the only statement of the phrase without its accented first note.

Evidence: MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11): the accent is restated in every staff that plays the line. MOLA PDF 8: vertical proofreading.

Fix (applied, dA): `dAClOne` `c''4.->\p\<( b'8 a'4~`; `dAClTwo` `c'4.->\p\<( b8 a4~`. Both players on the shared staff get the same mark.

### 3. Bar 367: the timpani's second closing motive x has no dynamic after a three-bar rest (applied, print only)

Problem: the closing timpani figure B–B–B–E comes at 363 (pp), 367 and 371 (ppp, near the rim). In the timpani part, 367 follows the multi-rest "364–366" with no level. The do-not-touch record in HISTORY.md already reads "pp at 363 and 367", so the score does not yet match its own record.

Evidence: MOLA Ex. 3 (PDF 10): after the multi-rest "9–11" the entry is marked pp. c268 fixed the same case at 28/268.

Fix (applied, coda `codaTimp`): `R2.*2 | b,4\pp b,8 b,8 e,4`. The MIDI is unchanged (it was already pp). The protected figure keeps its notes, its timing and its ppp at 371.

### Checked and kept
- 105 and 305: no ff is restated at the arrival of the theme-A tutti, because every staff reaches ff on the last minim of 104/304. Ex. 6 restates ff at bar 851 because a new tempo starts there. Here the level carries straight on from the cadence a beat earlier, so a restatement would add marks without information.
- Strings at 31/35 and timpani at 92/94: these entries without a dynamic lie inside one phrase at an unchanged level (c268, c368).
- Diminuendos that end on a rest (8, 72, 135, 147, 223, 323, 339, 362–364): each one dies into silence. Ex. 3's target rule covers a hairpin between two sounding levels.
- Bowing signs (PDF 5): there are 81, all retakes or protected strokes. I removed none.
