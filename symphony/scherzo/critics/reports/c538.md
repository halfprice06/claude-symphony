## Findings

### 1. The trio's emotional peak names no note transition; Vln I gets one portamento into the second phrase's peak (bar 214). Applied.

**Problem.** The return of T at 209–224 is the trio's climax: f espress., Vln I in the upper octave, Vln II and Cl 1 an octave lower, the swells shaped by hidden ff peaks. Everything the score can say about this line is said through dynamics and slurs. What the reading calls the core of lyrical expression, the move from one note to the next, is left entirely to the players. Nowhere in the 376 bars does the score name a transition. At 214 the second phrase rises a fourth, C♯6 to F♯6, under one slur into its peak minim, and the swell peaks on that minim. A Romantic string section left to itself will play this shift cleanly. A neo-Romantic master marks the one place in a lyrical span where the shift should be heard. Tchaikovsky does this in 6/ii and Elgar does it often.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 151: "All the small changes that happen between note transitions connect the audience to the performer's ideas … listening to how the solo violinist plays the first two notes … of the main theme in Tchaikovsky's violin concerto is almost an open door to the performer's inner feelings." PDF 154: the transition depends on dynamic, vibrato and "the speed at which the transition takes place", and the same holds for "similar techniques that live in the transition from one note to another, such as portamento or glissando". PDF 152: notation "cannot encode the details of what happens in the transitory moment", so the composer can only name it. PDF 155 (Buhler): lyrical string legato "transitions … into a gentle swell on the note of arrival". At 214 the swell already peaks on the arrival F♯6, so the slide and the swell reach the same note. Corpus: Tchaikovsky 6/ii, bars 102–103, octave slide in Vln I, violas and cellos into the ff peak of a dance movement in triple-group metre. That is a named model, in the same kind of movement.

**Why Vln I alone, and why only here.** At 213–216 Vln I is the only voice in its octave. Vln II has Cl 1 in unison, and a clarinet cannot share a slide. Casanelles PDF 147–148 says two sources on one line with different transitions are heard as "two distinct entities". The same reason rules out the cellos' T at 173–174, because Hn 3 is in unison with them (c431, protected). It also rules out the second idea's rising sixths at 46/50/122/126/278/282, because Fl 1, Ob 1 or the cor anglais double the strings there, and those are scherzo statements at 2. = 88, where one bar lasts 0.68 s. The coda's solo violin at 349 is protected and has Fl 1 col Vl. solo. One marked slide keeps the effect rare. A second one at 218 would be a semitone, which carries no slide, and that bar's down-bow is protected (c418).

**Fix (applied).** Print only. Notes, slur, bowing count and hairpins are unchanged.

### Checked and left as they are

- **The best rejected alternative: a swell to theme A's C6 peak in the ff tuttis (115–116, 315–316).** The first statement swells there (23–24 to mp) and the reprise does too (263–264 to mf), so by PDF 149–152 the tuttis look flat. I did not apply it, for three reasons. At ff only a printed fff would give the swell a size, and fff is reserved for 336 (c028). The flutes a2 already put the peak on top (c448). Eight more hairpins in the densest system risk the page layout pinned in global.ily. A listener would barely hear it. The portamento is clearly audible.
- **Two-player unisons (PDF 147–148).** I found no new exactly-two unison on an exposed line since c438. c504's Ob 1 joins Fl 1 and Vln I at 37–40. c517's cor anglais is an octave below Cl 1. c514's countermelody at 333–335 has three players.
- **Round robin (PDF 150).** The trumpets' motive x in the tuttis repeats on B4/A4 at f. Varying it by accents is ruled out by the soft-brass rule (c123), and the strokes sit inside moving ff tuttis. No change.
- **Tempo words.** Every printed rit., poco rit., accelerando, Più mosso and Meno mosso has its `\mt` steps in global.ily. "port." is a playing word, not a tempo word.
