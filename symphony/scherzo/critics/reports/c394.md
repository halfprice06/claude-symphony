## Findings

### 1. Bars 309-320: theme A's own octave is under-weighted in the reprise tutti (applied)
**Problem.** From 309 the theme at E5-C6 has only Vln I and Ob 1. The octave below (E4) has Vln II, cor anglais and Cl 2. Above, Fl 1·2 a2 play a slurred ff quaver figure at E6-A6. c159 put Vln II in the lower octave from 309 because the flutes then entered at 309 to double the theme an octave higher (c124). c369 later gave the flutes their own figure, so that support is gone. The situation c159 described ("Vln I and Ob 1 alone could not carry it") now applies to 309-320. The exposition at 109-120 has Vln I, Vln II (109-112) and Ob 1 on the theme, plus Fl a2 on it at 113-120.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151 (Mozart 40/iv): the weight of each octave tells the listener which octave carries the theme. Instruments added for sheen must not cover that octave. Here the heavier octave is the one below the theme. PDF 152 (Beethoven 9/i): two flutes in the top octave cut through without doubling. Here they play a different line, so they compete with the theme instead of adding sheen. PDF 152/154: every departure from the melody needs a reason of register or strength. Vln II's octave drop at 309 had one (the flutes above it), but c369 removed it.

**Answer to the earlier reasons.** c029 wanted the reprise scored differently from 105-120. That difference is now provided by the flutes' figure (c369), the resting low brass (c033) and Hn 4's bass line, so moving Vln II is no longer needed for it. c159's own reason supports this change.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-394/work/sections/dC.ily).** Vln II 309-320 now doubles Vln I at pitch; 305-308 is unchanged:
