## Findings

### 1. Layout: horn and trumpet short names, page 1 number, bar ranges in the parts (applied)
Bars: every system after the first; score page 1; every part with multi-bar rests.
Problem: movement I v266 and movement II v261 now print the horn short names as "in F", number page 1 of the score, and print the bar range over every multi-bar rest in the parts. The scherzo printed "Hn. 1·2 (F)", "Hn. 3·4 (F)", "Tpt. 1·2 (C)", left page 1 unnumbered, and printed only the count over its rests. A player counting a 24-bar rest in the scherzo's Trio, then turning to the finale, should find the same conventions in every movement.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 9, Ex. 2 prints the short names "Hn. 1,2 in F", "Tpt. 1, 2 in C" and "Cl. 1,2 (B♭)"; MOLA PDF 7: right-hand pages are odd-numbered in the upper right corner; MOLA PDF 5: number ranges over multi-bar rests. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 21, axiom II (well-written parts) and Glazunov's first class (an orchestra that sounds well at sight). Movement I v271 prints its trumpets "in C" as well; I follow it, and MOLA's example. The clarinets keep "(A)", as MOLA's clarinets and the other movements' clarinets keep their parenthesis.
Fix (applied): score.ly, `shortInstrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Hn. 1·2" \small "in F" }`, the same for "Hn. 3·4", and `{ "Tpt. 1·2" \small "in C" }`; `print-first-page-number = ##t` in the `\paper` block; parts-gen.py, movement II's `mmr-range-stencil` with `\layout { \context { \Staff \override MultiMeasureRestNumber.stencil = #mmr-range-stencil } }` before the parts' `\layout`. The score stays 13 pages; every part keeps its page count (Vln I–Cb 3, harp 4, the rest 1–2); the short names fit the margin.

### 2. Clarinets: no instruction for the change from A to B♭ before the finale (applied)
Bars: 365–376 (Cl 1, Cl 2).
Problem: the scherzo is the only movement of the four with clarinets in A (I, II and IV use B♭). Both joins therefore need a change of instrument in the pause, but neither movement II's last bar nor the scherzo's last bar says so. The scherzo already marks the timpani's retuning for the finale in its last bar (c020); the clarinets' change is the same kind of instruction and is missing.
Evidence: MOLA PDF 5: "clear indications of when to change followed by a statement of which instrument is being played at the next entry". Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 21, axiom II and axiom III (instruments in the keys the music is actually played in).
Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily): the last 12 rest bars of each clarinet become `R2.*11 |` and a last bar `\once \override MultiMeasureRestText.parent-alignment-X = #RIGHT \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #RIGHT R2.^\markup \small \italic \right-column { "muta in B♭" "(for the Finale)" } |`, printed once on the shared score staff (Cl 1) and with `-\tag #'part` on Cl 2 so it reaches both parts. No sounding change. For movement II: please print "muta in A (for the Scherzo)" on the clarinets' last bar, as its timpani muta does.

### 3. Join from movement II into bar 1 holds (no change)
Movement II v262 ends with Vln II A♭4 pp→ppp, Vln I C5 sul D, cellos tutti and basses 4 soli on A♭, Hn 3·4 "via sord." at 91, timpani "muta A♭ in E, C in G, D♭ in B, E♭ in E", harp on D♭ C B♭ / E♭ F G A♭. The scherzo takes A♭4 as Hn 1 G♯4 at the same pitch (Rimsky PDF 125–126: the first note of the new part coincides with the last of the old), its timpani label gives the same four moves, the harp label sets D, E and A in the pause, the basses re-enter "tutti pizz." at 3 after movement II's 4 soli, and Hn 3·4 are marked "senza sord." at 73. The first G♮ (Cl 2 G4, bar 7) replaces the G♯4 in the same octave, so the turn to minor is a regular part progression across groups (Rimsky PDF 126–127). Nothing to change.

### 4. Join into the finale and the finale's quotations hold (no change)
Finale v241 bar 1: Hn 1 A♭4 and Vln I A♭5 continue the scherzo's last G♯4/G♯5 in the same players; the timpani label (low E → G, G → A♭, B → C, high E → E♭) matches the scherzo's last muta; harp harmonics E4 and B4 at 375 move by semitone to the finale's E♭4 and C5. Quotations checked note by note: IV 9 (`g,4 g,8 g,8 g,4`, motive x after the scherzo's closing 363–371), IV 143–145 (retransition motive x, scherzo 241–252 on B), IV 219–220 (the horn call's D♯4 resolved; scherzo 5–6 still unresolved), IV 273–275 (Ob 1 and Vln II G5–F♯5–F5–E5, the pitch of scherzo bar 14 and of movement I bar 79), IV 275–276 (theme A's head in C). One record point: IV 9 takes the closing figure's rhythm and instrument, not its pitches (the scherzo ends B–B–B–E, the finale plays G–G–G–G, its retransition form). The scherzo's do-not-touch line for 363–371 should therefore read "rhythm, instrument and dynamics (pp, pp, ppp near the rim)". This is an echo in Rimsky's sense (PDF 128–129): same instrument, decreasing, then taken up loud across the pause. No score change.

### 5. Transfers and imitations in the cyclic passages follow the reading (no change)
The coda's T rises by phrase Hn 3 → Ob 1 → Vln I, each a higher instrument for a higher register (PDF 128). The held G♯ is passed with overlapping notes (365–369, 368–369), as in Ex. 237 (PDF 125–126, 407). The 369 and 375 additions inside the diminuendo are the one exception to PDF 127's diminuendo-by-elimination; c150 weighed this same reading and kept them, because the G♯ must be in the finale's two players at the end and the 375 chord is the finale's pivot. I agree and do not re-raise it.
