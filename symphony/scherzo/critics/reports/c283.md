## Findings

All 21 principles are followed or do not apply, so no finding needs a note change. Since c273 last checked the form, cycles 274–282 changed 45–46, 47, 53–55, 173, 220, 281–288 and 345. I checked each of those bars against principles 3, 6 and 19, and none of them moves a key area, a pivot or a cadence. The decisions below are mine, ranked by how much each matters to the form.

### 1. The way into G at 29–37 is a descending-fifths sequence with a real pivot: keep

Ex. 26-1 (PDF 442, 445) reaches its new key by descending fifths: "it would be pointless to search for a pivot chord". The counterstatement does the same, and it also has a pivot (principle 3):
- E minor (29–31);
- V (32, D♯);
- the winds' A minor chord at 33–34 (Cl E4/A4, Bsn A3/C4 under the theme's head on E with the A–G♯–G tail);
- D (35–36);
- G (37).

The A-minor chord at 33 is iv of E and ii of G, the "chord that belongs to both keys" of PDF 443. It sits in a weak spot of the bar plan, the winds' answer, so a later edit could easily thin it out. Keep Bsn 1·2 A3/C4 and the clarinets' E/A at 33–34. Without them, 32→35 becomes B major → D major with a bare cross relation D♯→D♮ and no pivot.

### 2. The trio's tonal plan is the I–VI–IV–V–I plan of Ex. 26-13/26-14 and Exercise 8: keep

c273 read the trio middle as Ex. 26-17's prolonged V. Unit 26 allows a second, compatible reading, and it confirms the plan:
- The bass goes E (168) → [B, 183] → C♯ (185) → A (201) → B7 (205) → E (209). This is "tonicized VI leading down in 3rds from I to IV" (PDF 452–453), which Mozart uses for the C sections of his rondos (Ex. 26-14).
- The book's own Menuetto exercise (PDF 461, Exercise 8) closes its first half on V and opens the second half by tonicizing vi.

Keep the C♯ minor at 185, the A at 201 and the B7 at 205–208, unchanged and in that order.

### 3. The coda's A major (341–348) is a phrase modulation in an I–IV–I expansion: keep

PDF 452: a late IV "creates no problems of tonal balance" and in ABA forms "typically enters right at the beginning of the new section without a transition". 341 does exactly this after the G.P.

PDF 456 (§16 and footnote) names I–IV–I as the most important expansion in sectional pieces. The coda's whole second stage is that expansion, and the plagal motto tail at 357–360 repeats it in small.

The E chord at 348 is the pivot (V of A, I of E). The return to E is confirmed by ii–IV–6/4–V–I at 351–355, as Points 2 asks. Do not add a dominant of A before 341 or of E before 349: either one would turn a phrase modulation into a transition.

### 4. The two transitional spans (133–152, 13.9 s; 225–252, 22.5 s): keep

These are the only places where the movement slows its harmonic rhythm to one chord per several bars. Together they take 36 s of 278, 13 %. Neither has to make a modulation:
- the trio is in the parallel major;
- the retransition changes the mode and adds V.

So Unit 26 gives no reason to shorten them beyond what each one does:
- 133–152 liquidates the theme and changes mode and tempo (the rit. to 2. = 76 lands at the trio tempo);
- 241–252 brings back the leading tone with iv7, the French sixth and V7 (principle 11, PDF 449: "before the recapitulation … the leading tone must appear").

Dvořák 9/iii's scherzo-to-trio transition is about 34 bars; ours is 20. c023 already cut 4 bars from the retransition. Keep both (c193, c273).

### 5. The reprise's G minor at 289: not re-raised

c103 and c193 answered this from the same pages:
- the D7 at 273–275 foreshadows it (Ex. 26-7, PDF 448);
- a new section after a double bar may enter in its key without transition (PDF 452).

The reading gives me nothing new to answer those reasons with. Keep.

### 6. Length and proportions: keep

Scherzo 97 s, link–trio–retransition 88 s, reprise 49 s, coda 44 s; 4:38 in all. The proportions are near Dvořák 9/iii's. The movement is the shortest of the four (I about 7:11, II 5:45, finale about 7:00), which is normal for a scherzo placed between an Adagio and a finale.

Unit 26's plans are about the order of keys, not their length. Every key area here is shorter than the material around it, and none outstays its function:
- G lasts 12 bars before it turns to G minor (PDF 449: III "is not a final goal");
- F lasts 16 bars, as a phrase-modulation episode;
- B in the trio lasts 2 bars, the length of a cadence.

No change (c003, c083, c113, c183, c263, c273).
