## What else I studied

Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 3, iv. The corpus has no score of it, so I used my knowledge of the score and two web sources: Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._3_(Tchaikovsky)) and the DuPage Symphony listening guide (https://www.dupagesymphony.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Tchaikovsky-Symphony-No.-3-Listening-Guide.pdf). The movement is in B minor and 2/4, conducted in one. The strings are muted throughout. Upper strings and woodwind trade semiquaver questions and answers. A single trombone solo is saved for the end of the scherzo section. The trio is a march (from the 1872 Peter the Great cantata) over chattering wind figures, and those figures continue as accompaniment when the scherzo returns. The coda recalls the trio's march.

Measured against it, v429 holds up. Its call and response at 29–36 is the same idea as Tchaikovsky's wind and string exchanges. Its saved colours (trumpets and low brass rest from 289 and return at 333; one fff and B6 at 336) match his saved trombone. Its coda returns to the trio tune (341–356), as his does. The one structural device it lacks is trio material continuing under the scherzo's return. The cyclic rules keep combined themes for the finale, so I do not propose it.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 168–185: the double basses bow the bass of the cellos' T (applied)

**Problem.** The trio tune's second statement (169–184) is the cellos' melody in the tenor register. Under it there is only a pizzicato bass on each downbeat, and Bsn 2 holds a note at 181–184 (c372 added it because "the pizz. F♯1 alone left the cellos' D♯3 as the sustained bass"). The first statement (153–168) has the same plucked bass, together with the harp. So the two statements share their foundation, and from 169 the cello line floats over dry plucks.

**Evidence.**
- Both of the reading's examples of a string melody in the cello range put a held soft bass under it. In Ex. 152 (Antar, PDF 324) the cellos have the melody mf, and two horns p and the double basses p sustain under the harp and clarinet arpeggios. In Ex. 195 (Sheherazade, PDF 360) the solo cello's figure lies over double basses pp held through the phrase, while the other cellos pluck.
- PDF 117–118, operation d), alteration of details: a recurrence of the same tune should change something. At 169 the arco bass is that change, against the plucked bass at 153.
- The held basis is two octaves under the melody, as PDF 132–133 requires (basis an octave or more removed, and weaker: pp under mp).

**Fix (built in /tmp/critic-429/work/sections/tA.ily, tB.ily).**
- 168: the basses rest. The harp's E2 and the timpani's E keep the downbeat, which gives five beats for pizz.→arco, as c085 arranged at 192→193 and 208→209.
- 169–183: arco pp, one dotted minim per bar on the same bass notes, with ties where the note repeats.
- 184: the basses rest. Bsn 2 holds B2 and the cellos hold B.
- 185: the basses play pizz. again, with the label added.

**Alternatives built in thought and rejected.**
- A horn drone continued from 153–168. Its B3 would sit on the cellos' B3 at 170 (PDF 133: the basis an octave removed). It would also repeat the first statement's colour, which goes against operation d) and c407.
- Bsn 2 sustaining the bass from 169. The bassoon cannot take the low F♯1 and G♯1. An octave up, at E3–F♯2, it sits under the cellos' G♯3 and blurs the melody, again against PDF 133.

The double basses are the only voice that can hold this bass at the right distance. Nothing protected is touched. c158's pp at 185 and c372's Bsn 2 are kept.

### 2. Bars 75–76 and 82–83: bassoon above Hn 3 in the F-major episode (considered, no change)

PDF 108 says bassoons may sit inside the horns, "but the inverse process is not to be recommended". At 75–76 and 82–83, Bsn 1 D4 lies above Hn 3 B♭3 and Hn 4 G3, so the horns sit inside the B.cl–Bsn frame. Swapping the parts would give Hn 3 D4 over Hn 4 G3, a fifth. That breaks c019's thirds and sixths for Hn 3·4, and Rimsky-Korsakov's own Ex. 158 (PDF 331) uses "horns within bassoons" at pp. The chords are all concords there (G minor), so PDF 108's concord/discord rule is met. No change.
