## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 101–120 (Fl 1, Cl 2) and 301–320 (Cl 2): the tutti's level never appears in these two parts (applied)

Problem. In the hemiola build 101–104 every wind crescendos from p to ff. Fl 2 and Cl 1 print their ff on beat 1 of 104. Fl 1 and Cl 2 reach theirs on beat 2 of 104 (the last minim), and that ff is hidden (`-\tweak stencil ##f \ff`) so that the shared staff does not print a second ff in the bar. The score looks right, because the staff shows the other player's ff. But each player reads his own part. The Flute 1 part shows "p <" at 101, a hairpin ending on nothing, and then the whole theme-A tutti 105–120 with no level at all (the next printed level is pp at 125). The Clarinet 2 part shows the same at 101–120 and again at 301–320. Fl 1 plays the tutti's top line a2 (E6–G6); Cl 2 plays theme A in the lower octave with the cor anglais. In a reading, both players will be guessing their level at the loudest 16 bars of the scherzo. This is the change in this cycle that a live listener would hear most: the top octave of the exposition tutti depends on it. The MIDI already has ff, so the mockup does not change.

This also answers the reason recorded for keeping "no ff restated at 105/305" (c468): "every staff reaches ff on the last minim of 104/304". That is true of the score but not of these two parts, whose ff is invisible.

Evidence. MOLA PDF 10, Ex. 3: the hairpin ends on a printed level (mf > p, pp < mp). PDF 11, Ex. 6: at the tutti every staff carries ff. PDF 8: look through the player's eyes, and do not assume an extracted part is right because the score is.

Fix (applied).
- sC `sCFluteOne`, `sCFluteTwo`: `e'''4-.\ff b''8-. b''-. e'''4-.` at 105, as the reprise already has at 305 (both flutes, equal dynamics on the shared staff, a2).
- sB `sBClTwo` (104) and dB `dBClTwo` (304): `fis'2->-\tag #'score -\tweak stencil ##f \ff -\tag #'part \ff`. The score keeps the hidden ff beside Cl 1's printed one; the part prints ff where the crescendo ends. Checked in the rebuilt part (parts-07-clarinet-2.pdf, bar 104: "< ff").

### 2. Bar 125: Cl 1 doubles the E-minor second idea's first note without its accent (applied)

Problem. At 125 the second phrase of the second idea starts with `c4.->` in Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I and Vln II. Cl 1 plays the same C5 in unison with Ob 1 in the same rhythm (`c''4.(\< b'8 a'4~`) without the accent. c468 fixed exactly this at the reprise (281, Cl 1·2) but not here, so the exposition and reprise now differ, and the Clarinet 1 part has the phrase once with and once without its accent.

Evidence. MOLA PDF 11, Ex. 6 (marks restated in every staff playing the line); PDF 8 (vertical proofreading). c468, finding 2.

Fix (applied, sC `sCClOne`): `c''4.->(\< b'8 a'4~`. In the MIDI the accent adds to the attack, so Cl 1 now leans into the phrase with the oboe instead of entering under it.

### 3. Bar 264: Fl 1 joins Vln I on theme A's C6 appoggiatura without the accent (applied)

Problem. In the reprise, Fl 1's heterophony (from 261, c549) meets Vln I in unison on the C6 at 264, the appoggiatura that peaks the third phrase. Vln I has `c'''2->\dnb`; Fl 1 has `c'''2` with only the hairpins. Wherever else the flutes double this note (116, 316) they carry the accent.

Evidence. MOLA PDF 11, Ex. 6; PDF 8. The note is accented in all six statements of the phrase in the strings.

Fix (applied, dA `dAFluteOne`): `c'''2->-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\>( b''8 fis''8)`. Hidden levels unchanged (the flute stays one level under the violins, c067).

### 4. Best larger candidate considered: a swell to theme A's C6 in the ff tuttis (115–116, 315–316) (not applied)

I looked for the single expressive change a listener would hear most, beyond consistency. The strongest candidate is the one c538 and c548 already tested: the soft statements swell into the C6 (23–24, 263–264), the ff tuttis do not. I did not apply it. The assigned reading teaches that every part should show what the score already intends; it gives no reason to add a new shape. At ff a hairpin without a printed fff is barely audible, and a printed fff would spend the coda's single fff (336). The C6 is already accented in every staff that plays it (116, 316). This would make the tutti fussier, not stronger.

### 5. Bar 336: the bass B under the summit has no accent (kept)

The upper strings, flutes, oboes and cor anglais accent the B6 at 336; Vc, Cb, Bsn 2 and Cbsn do not. Ex. 6 would suggest the same mark everywhere. But the bass B is a pedal restruck from 335 (only the cellos arrive on it), and the held chord tones in the winds are tenuto, not accented. Accents in the bass then start with the hemiola at 337. The distinction (moving line accented, held layer not) is consistent, so I left it.
