## Findings

### 1. Bar 215: the bass clarinet doubles the raised 4th under the tune (applied)

- **Bars.** 213–216 (T's forte return, second phrase), beat 3 of 215.
- **Problem.** PDF 214: "Because ♯4 functions (temporarily) as a leading tone, it must not be doubled". PDF 228 repeats it: "Don't double ♯4!". c093 set up this bar so that A♯ sounds in one voice only. c157 then put A♯ in the bass (A–A♯–A♯–B, V6/5 of V), and c421 gave T a tied A♯ on beat 3 of 215 (the 7–8 suspension). The double basses were adjusted for this: they play `ais,,2( cis,4)`, so they leave A♯ for C♯ on the beat where the tune takes it. c486 then gave the octave above the bass to the bass clarinet, and its line holds `ais,2.` for the whole of 215. On 215.3 the result is A♯2 in the bass clarinet against A♯4/A♯5 in Cl 1, Vln II and Vln I, at f. Both voices then go to B, the bass clarinet on 216.1 and the tune on 216.2. These are octaves on the leading tone, displaced by one beat, which is what c093 removed.
- **Evidence.** PDF 214 NOTE; PDF 215 and Ex. 14-3d (4–♯4–5 in the bass, with ♯4 in the bass only); PDF 228 Points 2–3. The scan finds A♯ in the bass clarinet and in the tune on 215.3 in v543, and only in the tune after the fix.
- **Fix (applied).** tC.ily, `tCBassCl`: `a,2.--( | ais,2.-- | ais,2.-- | b,2-- fis,4--) \breathe` → `a,2.--( | ais,2.-- | ais,2-- cis4-- | b,2-- fis,4--) \breathe`. The bass clarinet now follows the double basses an octave higher (A♯2 → C♯3 on beat 3, joining the cellos' C♯3), and the bass A–A♯–B of PDF 215 is unchanged. Comment added.

### 2. Bar 175: Vln II's last quaver doubles the tune's A♯ (applied)

- **Bars.** 175–176 (second trio statement, cellos and Hn 3 on T).
- **Problem.** The muted Vln II arpeggio `r8 ais'( cis'' e'' cis'' ais')` ends on A♯4 on beat 3½. On that beat the cellos and Hn 3 have just taken T's tied A♯3 (c421). So for a quaver the V7/V's ♯4 is in two voices an octave apart. The A♯4 on beat 1½ is not a problem: there the tune has E4, and that note gives the chord its third.
- **Evidence.** PDF 214 NOTE. c093 made the ♯4 single at this bar, and c421's theme change later moved the tune onto A♯. c511 handled the same conflict at 182 by putting Vln II's A♯ after the tune's note; here a change of pitch is cleaner, because Vln II goes on to F♯4 at 176 anyway.
- **Fix (applied).** tA.ily, `tAVlnTwo` bar 175: `r8 ais'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 ais'8)` → `r8 ais'8( cis''8 e''8 cis''8 fis'8)`. Comment added.

### 3. The largest change the unit argues for (a V middle section for the trio, or repeats) stays unbuilt

- **Bars.** 153–240; 13–56.
- **Issue.** PDF 223 and Points 8b name a tonicized V as the usual harmony of an ABA middle section. PDF 224 says dance halves usually repeat. These are the changes a listener would hear most.
- **Why not.** c453 built the exposition repeat and measured it (+30 s, the reprise falls to 39 % of the first part), and c363 answered the V middle. I add one reason from the score's own key plan. B already holds the bass for long stretches on both sides of the trio: 151, 203–208 (the V pedal before T returns) and 245–252 (the retransition). A trio middle in B would put the dominant under roughly 40 of the trio's 88 bars, and it would remove C♯ minor (185–200), which is the trio's only minor-mode contrast. A trio repeat would play T four times running before the middle. The written-out pairs that the score already has (153/169 for T; 13/105/253/305 for theme A) are the varied repeats PDF 224 describes.
- **Decision.** Keep. Not built.

### 4. Bars 157–160: the antecedent's half cadence is not turned into a cadence in B (keep)

- **Issue.** PDF 220–221 (Chopin, Ex. 14-9): an antecedent may close with an authentic cadence in V "as an intensified replacement of the usual semicadence".
- **Why not.** Since c421, bar 160 is already reached by V7/V (Cl 1's A♯ at 159). The difference from the Chopin case lies in what follows. Here the consequent restarts on vi at 161 and cadences in E at 167, so B at 160 is heard as V. To make it heard as a key, B would need a pivot and time, which would anticipate the real B cadence at 183. That cadence is the arrival PDF 224 asks for (one A on I, the other on V), and anticipating it would weaken it.

### 5. Bar 182: Vln II A♯4 in unison with Vln I (keep)

- c511 placed Vln II's A♯4 on the last quaver, after the tune's resolution of the 4–3 suspension. In B, A♯ is the leading tone and both voices take it to the same B4 in unison a quaver apart. This is a single pitch, not a doubling in another register. It also lies outside PDF 214's case, because here B is the tonic and A♯ is not ♯4 of the local key.

### 6. Length and proportions (keep)

- **Figures.** 97 / 8.5 / 70 / 9.6 / 49 / 44 s, total 4:42. The trio's B cadence comes near its middle (PDF 227).
- **Assessment.** The reprise is half the first part, as c273 and c453 found. The length suits a movement placed between the Adagio and the finale. Findings 1–2 add no time and change no bar count.
