## Comparison with Mahler 4/ii

A conductor who knows Mahler 4/ii would find the same habits in this score in its own late-Romantic dialect. Each recurrence keeps its shape and changes its colour, which is Adler's point about Mahler 4/iv (PDF 170–171). Theme A is bare strings at 13, a tutti at 105, Vln I alone over pizzicato with a cello countermelody at 253 (flute heterophony and Hn 3 only from 261), and a tutti at 305 with the flute figure and without trumpets or trombones. T is heard on Cl 1 over the harp at 153, the strings at 169, Vln II with Cl 1 under Vln I at 209, with flute and cellos at 217, Hn 3 at 341, Ob 1/Cl 1/Bsn 1 at 345, and solo violin with flute at 349. The harp's off-beat chords and the pizzicato bass are the constant at 153, 225 and 341, as Adler describes the harp in Mahler 4/iv. The quiet passages are written as chamber music in the way Adler describes Mahler (PDF 158–159): the link 141–152, the F-major episode 73–88 and the closing page 357–376 have single players and one soft pedal. Mahler's score is more grotesque and uses more special colours (scordatura, mutes on and off, stopped horn). These do not belong in this neo-Romantic movement, and the cyclic rules reserve the effects that would come closest. I do not count their absence against the score.

## Findings

1. **Bars 209–216, Cl 1 doubling Vln II at pitch (best candidate, built and rejected).** Adler PDF 165 says doubling at pitch loses each instrument's colour and that long doublings tire the ear. PDF 156 says a rest before a new colour makes it clear, and PDF 37 says to hold a colour back for an event. Cl 1 doubles Vln II at pitch for all 16 bars, 209–224. This was the change a listener would hear most clearly. I built it in /tmp/critic-569/work: `tCClOne = { R2.*4 | \partCombineSoloI R2.*4 | cis''2(\mf\<^\markup \italic "espress." dis''4 | …`, so Cl 1 rests 209–216 and enters with Fl 1 at 217. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, cmajor.py "none", no new outer-voice parallels. themecheck reported trioT ClOne 209 as CHANGED (a scoring line, not a note change). I reverted it, because the reading itself argues against it:
   - With Cl 1 gone, 209–216 becomes violins in octaves over sustaining winds. That is the colour of the string statement at 169–184, so the return would repeat the previous statement's colour. This goes against principle 39 (PDF 170–171), which is the main lesson of the Mahler passages in the range.
   - Cl 1 is T's first carrier (153). At mf under strings marked f, it is a colour doubling (Adler's second purpose, PDF 165), not a power doubling. It brings the trio's first colour back at its return, much as Mahler 4/iv keeps the harp as the constant.
   - Cl 1 would rest 201–216 and need a new cue in part 06 (c435).
   No change.
