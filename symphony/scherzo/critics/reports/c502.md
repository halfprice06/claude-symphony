## Findings

### 1. Bar 300 beat 3: the temporary leading tone of V7 of F is tripled, and two of the three copies leap away (applied in /tmp/critic-502/work)

- **Problem.** At 300.3 the strings alone play C–E–B♭ (Cb C2, Vc C3+E3, Vla E4, Vln II E5, Vln I B♭5) into the Neapolitan sixth F/A at 301. Since c473 this chord is V7 of F, and c480 spelt it that way (B♭, not A♯). That makes E a temporary leading tone. It sounds in three octaves. Only the violas resolve it (E4→F4). The cellos' E3 drops to A2 and Vln II's E5 drops to A4, so the strongest leading-tone copy, the one at the top of the inner texture, never reaches F.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 419: applied chords follow "the procedures of voice leading and chord construction … for normal V and VII. In particular, remember that the temporary leading tone is not doubled." PDF 439, Points for Review 3: "Don't double the temporary leading tone or the 7th." PDF 423 (§1) on applied chords: their voice leading "is exactly the same as if the [goal] chord were really a tonic." Read as a reason rather than a lock, c422's note ("the third doubled, PDF 505") belonged to the Italian-sixth reading (C–E–A♯ to V). In that reading E is the chord's third, and doubling it is the normal choice for an Italian sixth. c473 changed the chord's goal to F, and the reason no longer applies. Vln II also stops doubling Vln I's line for this one beat. At 301–302 it plays Vln I's line an octave lower (A4–C5–F5 under A5–C6–F6), so B♭4 at 300.3 keeps it in its own role.
- **Fix (applied, dB.ily).** Vln II: `<e'' cis'''>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." bes'4->\upbow` (was `e''4`). Vc: `<a, e>2->\ff\dnb^\markup \italic "non div." c4->\upbow` (was `<c e>4`). The chord is now C2/C3 (bass), E4 (violas, rising to F4), and B♭4/B♭5 (the violins' octave line, falling to A4/A5). No G is added, so there is no C–E–G sound. No line is silenced and no cue changes.
- **Checks.** No new parallels (the analyser still reports only bar 69, as in v502). Vln II B♭4→A4 runs in octaves with Vln I B♭5→A5, the same octave doubling the violins have through 301–302. Cmajor.py: none. The sound at 300.1–2 and at 301 is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 193–195: ♯4 in the bass into the cadential 6/4 of the trio's string build (built on paper, rejected)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-12b (PDF 424), "II6/5 – VII°7 of V – V … possibly embellished by 6/4". The bass would rise F♯ (193–194) – A♯ (194.3) – B (195), with VII°7 of V (A♯–C♯–E–G) on beat 3 of 194. Bsn 1's A3 would become G3 and rise to G♯3 at 195, the E♭–E–D line of the book's example. This was the most audible candidate: a rising chromatic bass inside the p< build to the forte call.
- **Why rejected.** Vln I holds A5 on beats 1–2 of 194, so A5 → A♯ in the bass would be a cross relation between the outer voices, and the soprano leaps A5–C♯6 at that moment. PDF 420 allows the bass's applied leading tone to excuse a cross relation only if it is kept "between an inner voice and the bass—don't involve the top voice" and the upper voices make no unnecessary leaps. Both conditions fail. Cl 1's B4 neighbour on 194.3 (c477) would also sound against A♯. The melody is c302's call, which is protected, so it cannot be altered to avoid this.

### 3. Bar 17: E major as V of iv on the return of theme A's head (rejected)

- **Candidate.** PDF 439 (point 1: any triad may be preceded by its applied V). Vln II `gis'4 gis'4` at 17 would rise to A4 at 18, matching the V6 of iv that the second phrase already has at 22.
- **Why rejected.** It makes the music worse. It puts E major into the first eight bars of the E-minor theme, ahead of the trio, whose E major is meant to come fresh at 153. It also gives away the G♯ that the tail A–G♯–G–F♯ at 18 introduces as a chromatic note. The first phrase stays plain, and its second-phrase copy is the one that carries the applied chord.

### 4. Bar 26 beat 3: V6/5 of V (A♯ in the bass) at theme A's cadence (rejected)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-12 (PDF 424), ii°6 → V6/5 of V → V.
- **Why rejected.** Beat 3 carries the motto's tail F♯5–F5. The F♮ over A♯–C♯ would sound as a B♭-minor triad, and the tail is protected. c462's ii°6 stays.

### 5. Bar 215 beat 3: A♯ in the bass clarinet and in the tune at once (kept)

- **Check.** The temporary leading tone is in two real voices: B.cl. A♯2 (c486) and the tune's tied A♯4/A♯5.
- **Why kept.** The tune's A♯ is a 7–8 suspension over the B at 216, and the bass clarinet's B2 on 216.1 is that suspension's bass. The two A♯s do not resolve together, so they make no octaves. The bass clarinet's register in 211–220 is c486's playability reason.

### 6. Bars 223–224: deceptive cadence on VII°7 of V before the codetta (still rejected)

- **Candidate.** Ex. 25-16 (PDF 427). The soprano stays on 1̂ over the applied chord.
- **Why rejected.** c412's reason holds. T's held E at 224 over the restated V would be an unresolved 4th. A real restatement of V needs an added bar, which would move every protected bar number after 224.

### 7. Bars 48–49: the second idea's 7th D6 rises to E6 (kept)

- **Check.** The scan flags D→E in every doubling.
- **Why kept.** It is a delayed resolution of a soprano 7th (PDF 408–409, Ex. 24-15). The melody's E6 is the dotted crotchet from c411, its passing D6 follows, and C♯6 arrives on 49.3 in the same voice. c432's applied V7 at 48 stands.
