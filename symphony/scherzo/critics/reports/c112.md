## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 110–111 and 310–311: IV8–7–V fifths in the tutti tenor line

Problem: in both tutti statements of theme A, the tenor countermelody (Hn 3, Hn 4, Bsn 1, Vc) goes `a2( c'4 | b2 a4)`. Its C4 on beat 3 of 110 falls to B3 on the downbeat of 111. At the same moment the tune's chromatic tail falls G→F♯ in the violins, oboes and clarinets. The result is a fifth C–G to a fifth B–F♯ between the main melody and the loudest inner line, at ff, with four instruments on the inner voice.

Evidence: A&S PDF 190, Ex. 12-21, shows this exact pattern. IV with an 8–7 in the upper voice going to V makes parallel fifths when the other voice passes through 3̂. The book's remedy is to delay one voice. Ex. 12-8 (PDF 181–182) shows the same delay used as a suspension.

Fix (applied, sC.ily and dC.ily, in sCHnThree, sCHnFour, sCBsnOne, sCVc and the dC equivalents): `a2( c'4 | b2 a4` → `a2( c'4~ | c'4 b4 a4`. The C is tied over the bar as a ♭9 over V (B–D♯–F♯–A) and resolves to B on beat 2, with the slurs kept. The countermelody's contour and rhythm are otherwise unchanged (c017). Hn 1's detached C5 on beat 3 also moves to B4, but it is staccato and separated, so I left it.

### 2. Bars 219–221: the trio's last pre-dominant is a II6 that moves its inner voice into fifths

Problem: before the cadential 6/4 at 221, the violas play `<a cis'>` at 219, `<a fis'>` at 220 and `<b e'>` at 221 (II6 over the bass F♯–A). Their upper voice F♯4→E4 makes fifths with Hn 1 C♯5→B4 at 220→221. The E that 218 already has is given up and then taken again.

Evidence: A&S PDF 177 says the 7th of II6/5 is prepared as a common tone and resolves down. PDF 181–182 (Ex. 12-8) says that before the cadential 6/4 the 7th of II7 or II6/5 is held in the same voice and becomes the 4th of the 6/4. PDF 178 says II6/5 suits a full texture better than II6, and this is the tutti return at f.

Fix (applied, tC.ily tCVla): bars 219–220 become `r4 <a e'>4--(\f\> <a e'>4--) | r4 <a e'>4--( <a e'>4--)`. E4 is held from 218 through the 6/4 at 221, and bar 222 keeps `<a fis'>`. I tested D♯4 at 222 and rejected it: it doubled T's 4–3 resolution E→D♯ in octaves. This does not fix c092's open item (Hn 1 vs cellos octaves at 220→221), which is still open.

### 3. Bars 351–353: the same cadence in the coda, II6 where II6/5 belongs

Problem: the violas go `<cis' a'>2. | fis'2. | e'2.` over the bass F♯–A–B. This is ii, then ii6, then the 6/4, and the E only arrives on the 6/4.

Evidence: same as finding 2 (PDF 177; PDF 181–182, Ex. 12-8). PDF 183 (Ex. 12-9) gives I–VI–II6/5 as the standard chain, which is the shape of 349–352 (vi–I6–ii–ii6).

Fix (applied, coda.ily codaVla): `<e' a'>2. | e'2. | e'2.`. The E is held from 350 through ii7 and ii6/5 into the 6/4 at 353. The cadence and the ending after 357 are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 196–197 and 200–201: V–VI in the trio's middle section

Problem: at 196→197 (B7 → C♯ minor, V–vi in E), Vln II moves F♯4→G♯4 while the bass moves B→C♯, which gives open parallel fifths. At 200→201 (G♯ → A, V–VI in C♯ minor), Cl 2 holds the leading tone B♯3 and then leaps to E4, so the leading tone never resolves.

Evidence: A&S PDF 202–203, Ex. 13-12: at V–VI the leading tone must go to the tonic and the 3rd of VI is doubled. Otherwise you get fifths or an unresolved leading tone. PDF 177 says the 7th of V7 resolves down.

Fix (applied, tB.ily): Vln II 196 `gis'4 fis'4 a'4)`. Vln II takes the 7th A4 with Cl 1 and resolves it to G♯ at 197, with the slur kept. Cl 2 at 201 `cis'2.\p` (was `e'2.`): B♯ goes to C♯, doubling the 3rd of VI as the book asks. Bsn 1 also has F♯→G♯ at 196–197, but with a rest in between, so I left it.

### 5. Bars 82–83: the harp loses the common tones of the falling-third step

Problem: in the F-major episode the bass falls D–B♭–G–A (vi–IV–ii–iii6/I). The harp right hand moves `<bes d' f'>` → `<g bes d'>` as a block, which gives B♭3–F4 → G3–D4 parallel fifths. In this progression the common tones B♭ and D can simply be held.

Evidence: A&S PDF 163–164, Ex. 11-2: when chords move by a falling third, the upper voices keep the two common tones and only one voice moves.

Fix (applied, sB.ily sBHarpR): 82–83 `r4 <bes d'>4 <bes d'>4 | r4 <bes d'>4 <bes d'>4`. I tried a version with F3 at the bottom of 82 first, but it made fifths with the harp's own left hand at 81→82. F, the 5th of IV, is still in the tune at 82, and G is in the harp left hand and the bass clarinet at 83. Protected-passage answer: c080 protects 73–88 as the F-major link to the finale's IV. Key, harmony, the harp head 73–80 and the wind chord (c104) are unchanged. Only one accompanying dyad loses a doubled note, and the harp stays below the solo (c028).

### 6. Bars 345–346 (coda) and 157–158 (trio): harp voicing makes fifths with the tune

Problem: at 345→346 (in A, IV–ii), the harp's `<d' fis' a'>` → `<d' fis' b'>` moves A4→B4 while Ob 1 moves E5→F♯5, giving fifths. At 157→158, the harp's `<a cis' e'>` → `<a cis' fis'>` moves E4→F♯4 under Cl 1's B4→C♯5, also fifths.

Evidence: at 346, A&S PDF 177 and 180 (Ex. 12-6) prefer ii7 to the ii triad in a pre-dominant chain. The A held from 345 becomes the prepared 7th and is held into V7/V at 347, so the two dissonances interlock. At 157, the plain I–IV–I with 5–6 motion (PDF 195–197) is right as it stands, and only the spacing is wrong.

Fix (applied): coda.ily codaHarpR 346 `r4 <d' fis' a'>4 <d' fis' a'>4` (ii7, A held 345–347). tA.ily tAHarpR 157 `r4 <e a cis'>4 <e a cis'>4`, a voicing change only. The chord and the plain harmony of 153–168 (c089, c093) are unchanged.

### 7. Bars 123–124 and 279–280: Hn 2 and violas against the second idea

Problem: Hn 2 and the violas go `fis'2.~ | fis'4 e'2` while the tune falls C♯6→B5 (Vln II C♯5→B4) over A–G. The F♯–C♯ fifth moves to E–B.

Evidence: the reading warns about this in every progression it discusses (PDF 167, 190, 202–203). Inner voices must not move in fifths with the soprano when a step or a held note would avoid it. Here the bass G under the arrival chord makes G the natural continuation for the F♯.

Fix (applied, sC.ily sCHnTwo and sCVla; dA.ily dAHnTwo and dAVla): `fis'4 g'2` with the slurs and tenutos kept. G→E at 125/281 then leads into the A-minor chord.

### 8. Bars 108 and 308: violas move in fifths with the bass

Problem: the violas' lower note goes B3–F♯4–B3 across 107–109 while the bass goes E–B–E. That gives fifths both ways (i–V–i), on the off-beat chords of theme A.

Evidence: A&S PDF 189–190 (Ex. 12-20). The 5th of the chord before V must not move with the bass into V's fifth. The book treats the IV7 case, and the same rule holds for any step into V.

Fix (applied, sC.ily sCVla and dC.ily dCVla): 108/308 `r4 <b a'>4-. <b a'>4-.`. The violas keep B3 as a common tone (V7 stays complete in the other parts: B, D♯, F♯ and A all sound at 108).
