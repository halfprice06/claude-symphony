## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 47: the passing V in the G-major second idea doubles the leading tone in two inner voices. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 47.1 the bass is D (Vc D3, Bsn 1 D3, Cb D2), so the chord is V of G. The melody has F♯ (Fl 1, Ob 1, both violin sections, Cl 2 an octave below). F♯ is also in two independent inner voices, violas F♯4 and Hn 2 F♯3. The root D is only in the bass. Both inner voices reach F♯ from G at the same moment as the melody's G6→F♯6, so the f homophonic passage also has two parallel octaves into the downbeat. The E-minor statements voice the same chord with the inner voices on the 5th (violas, Hn 2 and Cl 1 F♯4 under the melody's D♯ at 123 and 279).
**Evidence.** PDF 76, Ex. 5-4: the leading tone "should never be doubled when it is part of V or VII or their inversions"; review point 3, PDF 89. PDF 83, Ex. 5-18: parallel octaves.
**Fix.**
```
sA.ily  sAVla    bar 47:  a'2( g'4~        (was fis'2( g'4~)
sA.ily  sAHnTwo  bar 47:  a2-- g4--~       (was fis2-- g4--~)
```
Both voices take A, the 5th of V, as in the E-minor statements. Each still moves on beat 3 to G (c262). The violas now join Cl 1 and Hn 1 on A4→G4 at the unison, and Hn 2 doubles that line an octave lower. That is a doubling (PDF 84–85), and it matches 123–124, where three players share F♯4→G4. From 47 to 52 Hn 2 is then an octave under Hn 1 throughout, which it already was at 48–52. The melody, the bass, the hemiola and the dotted minims are unchanged.

### 2. 110→111 and 310→311: Hn 1 moves A4→B4 in octaves with the bass A→B in the tutti theme. APPLIED. This is a side effect of c202.
**Problem.** c202 changed Hn 1's beat-3 crotchet at 110/310 from C5 to A4 to remove fifths with the tune. It gave as its reason that A4→B4 "matches 113/313". At 113 the bass goes A→G♯, but at 110 it goes A→B (Cb A1–B1, B.tbn A2–B2, Tuba, Bsn 2 A2–B2). So Hn 1 now steps A4→B4 in octaves with the bass on the ff downbeat of V at 111, in the most exposed brass voice. c202's comment calls 110 "iv over the E pedal", but the bass is A.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18: one part "duplicates the pitch and motion of the other in a different register". PDF 87, rule 5: these progressions are most noticeable in outer voices, and the bass is one.
**Fix.**
```
sC.ily  sCHnOne  bar 110:  c''4-- r4 r4     (was c''4-- r4 a'4-.)
dC.ily  dCHnOne  bar 310:  c''4-- r4 r4     (was c''4-- r4 a'4-.)
```
Hn 1 keeps its downbeat C5 and rests on beat 3, as it already does at 106, 118, 306 and 318 (c011). C5 (110.1) to B4 (111.1) moves against the rising bass. With no note on beat 3 there is no C5→B4 against the tune's G→F♯, so c202's correction holds. The trumpets enter at 111 as before.

### 3. 52: the seventh of V6/5 of V is doubled, and the horn's copy then rises. APPLIED.
**Problem.** On beat 3 of 52 the chord is A7 over C♯ (bass C♯, Hn 1 A4, Cl 2 A4, melody E, D). The seventh G is in the violas (G4, from 52.2) and also in Hn 2, which moves A3→G3 on beat 3. The violas carry their G through the cadential 6/4 at 53 and resolve it to F♯4 at 54. Hn 2's G3 instead rises to A3 at 54 (c223). The doubled seventh lasts one beat and leaves one copy unresolved.
**Evidence.** PDF 76: "The chord 7th forms a dissonance, so it must never be doubled"; review point 3, PDF 89.
**Fix.**
```
sA.ily  sAHnTwo  bar 52:  a2.--)           (was a2-- g4--))
```
Hn 2 holds A3, the root, through 52. It then steps to G3 at 53 in contrary motion to the bass C♯→D and to Hn 1's A4→B4. c223's doubled 4th of the 6/4 at 53 and A3 at 54 are unchanged. The seventh now sounds once (violas) and resolves in its own line.

### 4. 196→197: Bsn 1 F♯3→G♯3 makes fifths with the bass B→C♯ into the deceptive cadence. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In 195–196 Bsn 1 has the 6–5 over B (G♯3–F♯3). It rests one crotchet and enters f on G♯3 at 197, over the bass C♯ (Vc, Cb, Bsn 2, which is on the same staff). B–F♯ to C♯–G♯ are two perfect fifths with both voices rising by step, one beat apart. c112 removed the same fifths from Vln II, and c172's comment on Hn 1 names them; Bsn 1 still has them. The progression is V7 to vi (B7 to C♯ minor). A&S's standard voicing for it doubles the third of vi, which avoids exactly these fifths. The bassoon's crotchet rest does not hide them, because the bass is held and the bassoon re-enters louder.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-19: parallel 5ths are "wrong". PDF 83: the 5th's "resistance to forward momentum". PDF 76: doublings bend "to bring about smoother voice leading".
**Fix.**
```
tB.ily  tBBsnOne  bar 197:  \partCombineAutomatic e2.\f      (was \partCombineAutomatic gis2.\f)
```
F♯3→E3 is a step down against the rising bass. E is the third of vi, doubled with Cl 2 and Hn 2 (E4). Bsn 1 returns to G♯3 at 198, a third up, and its line from 199 on (F♯3, D♯4 …) is unchanged. The shared bassoon staff keeps one dynamic.

### 5. 259 and 264: the seventh of B7 is doubled by the pizzicato filler in the reprise. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In the reprise, theme A has the cellos' arco tenor line (c037). At 259 and 264 that line holds A3, the seventh of B7, and resolves it to G3 at 260/265. Vln II's pizzicato off-beats repeat A4 in the same bars, as they did at 19 and 24, where the cellos had the bass B2 and Vln II held the only seventh. At 259 Vln II then rests at 260, so its seventh does not resolve. At 264 it moves A4→G4 a beat after the cellos' A3→G3, which makes octaves heard one beat apart.
**Evidence.** PDF 76: the chord seventh is never doubled. PDF 85, Ex. 5-26: fillers "merely reinforce the sonority". Here the filler reinforces the one tone that must not be doubled.
**Fix.**
```
dA.ily  dAVlnTwo  bar 259:  r4 fis'4 fis'4     (was r4 a'4 a'4)
dA.ily  dAVlnTwo  bar 264:  r4 fis'4 fis'4     (was r4 a'4 a'4)
```
The filler takes the 5th, so the chord is complete with each tone once: bass B, violas B3/D♯4, Vln II F♯4, cellos A3, melody D♯/C–B. At 264–265, Vln II steps F♯4→G4. The theme, the cello line and the exposition (19, 24) are unchanged.
