## Findings

### 1. Bars 210-212: the bass stands alone in octave 1 at the trio's forte return (applied)

- **Bars:** 210-212 (tC.ily, `tCCbsn`).
- **Problem:** At T's forte return the double basses and contrabassoon play the bass in unison (c101), and the cellos and Bsn 2 carry the countermelody above it. Since c382 the bass falls E2-C#2-G#1 under the IV6. So on the downbeats of 210, 211 and 212 the bass sounds G#1, B1 and E2 in one octave only, and the next note above is the cellos' E3, F#3 and B3: gaps of 20, 19 and 19 semitones, at mf rising to f under ff violins. c294 kept 211-212 because the contrabassoon was in unison with the harp's bass. c364 has since removed the harp from 209-224, so that reason no longer applies. Bars 213-220 already have the contrabassoon an octave above the basses (c114).
- **Evidence:** In the appendix every loud chord doubles the bass at the octave and fills the octave above it (PDF 505-507). Ch. IV, PDF 125: "The upper and lower parts of a passage can seldom be widely separated without the intermediate octaves being filled in, for this is contrary to the first principles of proper distribution of chords". PDF 124-125: in octave 1 the intervals lie too close; the low register is for the contrabassoon and the low winds. In Ex. 205-206 (PDF 368-369) the contrabassoon, bassoons and trombones sit in the octave above the basses.
- **Fix (applied):** `tCCbsn = { e,2--(\mp cis,4-- | gis,2.-- | b,2.-- | e2.--) | a,2.--( ...` (was `gis,,2.-- | b,,2.-- | e,2.--`). The contrabassoon now sounds G#2, B2, E3 at 210-212, an octave above the basses, and continues into c114's A2 at 213 by a fifth down. It never crosses the cellos: their notes are E3-G#3 over G#2, F#3-B3 over B2, and B3-G#3-F#3 over E3. Bar 209 stays in unison (E2-C#2), because an E3 on its downbeat would sit above the cellos' B2. The downbeat gaps are now 12, 12 and 12 semitones. The basses' line, c382's harmony and c184's unison at 221-222 are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 253-268: the pizzicato bass loses its octave in the reprise of theme A (applied)

- **Bars:** 253-268 (dA.ily, `dACb`).
- **Problem:** In 13-28, under theme A, the pizzicato bass sounds in octaves: cellos E2, basses E1. In the reprise, c359 gave the cellos the tenor countermelody (G3-E4, arco pp), and nothing replaced the cellos' bass. The bass is now a single pizzicato line on the basses' lowest string (E1, A1, G#1, G1, B1). The next note up is the cellos' line, 24-33 semitones higher (E1-B3 at 254, G1-E4 at 266). A p pizzicato on E1 alone has little pitch. Bars 253-268 are also the only statement of theme A whose bass has no octave.
- **Evidence:** In the appendix the bass always has its octave (PDF 505-507). Ch. IV PDF 125 (T10, above). In Ex. 216 (PDF 385), under a wind tune, the pizzicato bass is cellos and basses together. Ch. IV PDF 121-122: a pizzicato tutti has only medium power, so the bass is the place where it needs its full octave.
- **Fix (applied):** the basses divide in octaves for those 16 bars: `dACb = { <e,, e,>4\p^\markup \italic "div." r4 r4 | <e,, e,>4 r4 r4 | ... | <e,, e,>4 r4 r4 | R2. | R2. | e,,4\p\<^\markup \italic "unis." ...` The upper octave sounds the cellos' old E2 of 13-28. c359's cello countermelody, the off-beat pizzicato in Vln II and violas, and the flute heterophony are unchanged. The downbeat gaps are now 20-21 semitones, from the upper bass octave to the cellos' tenor line. That is the same layout as 13-28 (bass in octaves, an inner layer above), and lighter than a filled tutti, as the leggiero character asks.
