## Findings

### 1. 72→73: the soprano at the join (best candidate; built, tested and rejected)

**Question.** PDF 587 says the common tone of a common-tone modulation "will appear in an exposed position, usually in the soprano", and *Widmung* (Ex. 32-3) sustains it in the melody. At 72 the stretto's top voice is E6 (Fl 1, Vln I) over A major; the common tone A sounds below it (Fl 2/Ob 2 A5, Hn 2 A4), and the F-major episode begins at 73 with the harp's A5. Of every change this reading could support, putting the A on top at 72 is the one a listener would hear most: it alters the peak of the exposition's only f stretto.

**Test.** In /tmp/critic-563/work I changed Fl 1 and Vln I at 72 from `e'''2.->\ff\>` to `a'''2.->\ff\>` (sB.ily), so the stretto line ends A5–B5–C♯6–A6 and the A carries over to the harp's A5. Build: 13 pages, cmajor "none", part lengths match, no new outer-voice parallels, but LilyPond warned "compressing over-full page by 2.7 staff-spaces / page 3 has been compressed".

**Why it is worse.** (a) The join already has its sustained common tone: Hn 2 holds A4 from 72 (ff>) into 73 (pp), exactly *Widmung*'s procedure, and the soprano of the new section is A5 from its first note, so PDF 587's "usually in the soprano" is met where the new key is heard. (b) The leap C♯6–A6 breaks the stretto's rising arpeggio A–B–C♯–E, the same line the reprise repeats at 297–300, where the E6 must stay because the Italian sixth (300, c422) and the ♭II6 (301) follow from it; changing only 72 would make the two strettos differ for no gain. (c) PDF 585 says such a modulation should be heard as a confrontation of keys; the E6 dying away (ff>) and the harp starting on A5 an octave and a fourth lower separates the keys by register as the reading's Hammerklavier separation does (open octaves, pedal release), without blurring them. (d) The page-3 compression breaks the 0-warning rule. Reverted; the scratch build is identical to v563.

### 2. 144→145 and 240→241: the two exposed inflections at the trio's edges. Keep.

The movement turns E minor → E major and back by single-note inflections in the same player and register: Hn 1 G4 (141–144) → G♯4 (145) pp< and Hn 1 G♯4 (237–240) → G4 (241) pp. PDF 585 asks for exactly this: a rising inflection into the trio that adds intensity, a falling one into the retransition that lowers it, both drawing attention to the key change. The crotchet rest before each new note is the "slight separation" PDF 585 says does not undo the confrontation. Nothing to change.

### 3. 288→289 and the reprise plan. Keep.

The reprise confronts E minor with G minor directly (PDF 596–598, Haydn's way rather than the staged way), with B3 (violas, 288) → B♭3 (cellos, 290) in one register and G common to both chords. c293 and c410 examined copying the exposition's horn B4–B♭4 here and rejected it; their reason (the reprise already has its one-voice step) holds under this reading, so I do not re-raise it. The reprise then relieves its minor keys with A major (297–300) and ♭II (301–302) before V, so PDF 596's warning against an unrelieved succession of minor chords does not apply.

### 4. 301–304: ♭II6 → V with a whole-step bass. Keep.

PDF 593 says the half-step bass intensifies V when ♭VI leads to it; the exposition does this at 86–89 (A–A♯–B under N6–A♯°7–V). The reprise's two-bar ♭II6 (c473) reaches V7 at 303 with bass A–B. I tested on paper a passing A♯ on beat 3 of 302 to copy 88: it would sound against the held A5 (Fl 2, Ob 1, Hn 1) as a minor second and against F6 (Fl 1, Vln I) as a diminished fourth. The N6–V bass 4–5 is the normal one; no change.

### 5. Coda 339–349: the last mode change and the strong IV. Keep.

After the G.P. the slow stage opens in A major (IV) and turns to E at 348–349, Brahms Op. 39/8's "strong IV that precedes the final V and I" (PDF 595, Ex. 32-14). The minor-to-major inflection G♮ (339, Vln II, violas) → G♯ (343) is carried in Hn 3's melody, the exposed voice there, as PDF 587 asks. c561's protection of Hn 3 alone at 341–344 stands.

### 6. Proportions and length. Keep.

278 s. Scherzo with intro 38%, trio 25%, reprise 18%, coda 16%. The reprise is half the exposition's length, as Beethoven's shortened scherzo returns are, and the reading gives no reason to re-lengthen it: every remote area of the first part (G minor, F) is recalled in it in brief, which is what PDF 591 ("delayed, and not for very long") and PDF 603 (tie remote areas to the motivic design) describe. At 4:38 the movement sits inside the plan's "about 5 minutes"; adding bars would also add a page.
