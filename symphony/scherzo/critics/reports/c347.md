## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 212-213: Bsn 1 moves in fifths with the bass from I to IV in T's forte return (applied)

**Problem.** In the soft wind choir under T's forte return (Hn 1·2 p, Bsn 1 mf), Bsn 1 holds B3 through 212 (c327) and leaps to E4 on the downbeat of 213. On the same bar line the contrabassoon goes E2 → A2 (it doubles the basses an octave higher from 213, c114) and the basses go E2 → A1. B3/E2 → E4/A2 is a twelfth to a twelfth in similar motion. Against the basses it is a fifth to a fifth by contrary motion. This is the fifth of I moving to the fifth of IV. The per-player scan finds no other consecutive fifths between independent parts anywhere outside the tuttis and the coda ostinato. The fault arose because c327 and c114 each moved one of the two voices.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 112: "in a succession of chords where the melodic position is constantly changing, distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts." The choir's layout must not cost the part-writing.
- PDF 108: "in a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." Bsn 1 is the free moving part of the choir, so it can take the E earlier, when nothing else moves.
- Ex. 152 (PDF 324) and Ex. 172 (PDF 342): the soft held parts move by step or common tone against the bass, never in fifths with it.
- c327's own reason is kept: Bsn 1 still does not rise B-E in octaves with the bass at the 211-212 bar line.

**Fix (applied, tC.ily, `tCBsnOne`, bar 212):**
```
| fis'4-- b2-- | b2.-- | e'2.-- |   →   | fis'4-- b2-- | b2-- e'4-- | e'2.-- |
```
- Bsn 1 now enters E4 on beat 3 of 212, over the held E bass. That chord is I to V7/IV (the violas' passing D4, c089), so the motion is oblique, with no parallel.
- Bsn 1 then holds E4 into 213 as a common tone over the bass's E → A.
- The shared bassoon staff already had two rhythms in this bar (Bsn 2 B3-G♯3-F♯3), so the print is unchanged apart from the note. The slur and tenutos stay.
- Scan after the change: no fifths or octaves between independent parts at 212-213.

### 2. Bars 342-343: the violas shadow Hn 3's A4-G♯4 in octaves in the coda's recall of T (applied)

**Problem.** In the coda's A-major recall (341-344), Hn 3 plays T alone, p dolce. Beneath it the violas are the only sustained string part, pp. Hn 3 steps A4 (342, beat 3) → G♯4 (343). The violas step A3 → G♯3 at the same moment, so they are in octaves with the solo, and they double its leading tone. The harp already sounds G♯3 on its off-beats. c327 chose G♯3 to keep a held B3 away from the solo's new falling sixth G♯4-B3. That reason holds, but G♯3 traded one fault for another.

**Evidence.**
- Rimsky PDF 112: correct progression of parts comes before distribution.
- PDF 113: strings and brass are "seldom employed in juxtaposition, crossing, or enclosure". A held string part that moves with the solo horn's line becomes an unintended doubling of it, not a background.
- Ex. 152 (PDF 324): the horns' sustained basis does not move with the solo line.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily, `codaVla`, bar 343):**
```
a2.\pp | a2. | gis2. | a2. |   →   a2.\pp | a2. | e2. | a2. |
```
- E3 is the root of the V7 and doubles the harp's E3. It sits below the solo's B3, so c327's aim stands, and it moves by contrary motion to the solo.
- The leading tone is kept and resolved by the harp (G♯3 → A3 at 344).
- The basses pluck only on beat 1, so they are silent when the violas move. No contrary octave with the bass sounds.
- Scan after the change: the 343 octave is gone and nothing new appears.

### Considered and kept (my decisions)

- **104→105 and 304→305:** Hn 1 F♯4 → B4 against the bass B → E, fifths by contrary motion at the tutti arrival. This is a one-beat attack chord inside a full tutti. PDF 114 says resonance matters least in short disconnected chords, and c322 protects the join. No change.
- **127:** Cl 1 and Bsn 1 G → F♯ in octaves. Bsn 1 joins the bass line in unison at that beat, so the octave is a hidden octave between Cl 1 and the bass arrival, and c132 chose this note. No change.
- **209-211:** Bsn 1 in unison with Hn 2. PDF 106 lists "2 Horns + 2 Fag." as a regular unison pairing (juxtaposition), and c247 kept 211 for reasons that still hold. No change.
- **75-76 and 82-83:** horns between bass clarinet and bassoon (row 7). Rimsky's own Ex. 158 (PDF 331) moves to "horns within bassoons", and c104/c147 tested the alternatives. No change.
- **Trio accompaniment:** off-beat chords at 153, 169, 209, 225 and 341. PDF 112 and Ex. 171 offer woodwind figuration over sustained strings as the other method. However, the trio's foreground/background plan (c304) and the four colourings of T are protected, and each statement already changes the instrument that carries the off-beats. No change.
