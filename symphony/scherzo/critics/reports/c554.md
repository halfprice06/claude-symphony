## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 335–338: the trombones and tuba stop on the cadential 6/4 and leave the fff summit chord without its brass bass (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's crescendo reaches 333–335 with the full brass. Tbn 1·2 hold G3 and B2→E3, the bass trombone E2→B2 and the tuba E1→B1, all f and tenuto under one slur. At 335 they sound the cadential 6/4 over B (Tbn 1 G3, Tbn 2 E3). At 336, the movement's only fff and the B6 peak, the 6/4 resolves to V7 everywhere except in the trombones. They stop, and stay silent through 336–338 until the tonic at 339. As a result:
- The trombones' sixth and fourth (G3, E3) are left unresolved in their own colour at the strongest downbeat of the movement. Their resolution notes F♯3/D♯3 are heard only in Bsn 1.
- The brass chord of the summit begins at Hn 4's B3. Under it are only the strings' B1/B2, Bsn 2, Cbsn and the timpani roll. Bsn 1's F♯3 alone fills the octave B2–B3. The B6 in Vln I and Fl 1 stands over a brass chord with no low register, two bars after the full brass sounded.
- In the hemiola 337–338 the strings and wood-wind move in minims across the bar. The horns and trumpets hold. The trombones, the natural sustaining layer, are silent.

**Evidence.** PDF 100: brass harmony "of the close order with no empty spaces". PDF 101: "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts are generally allotted to the two remaining trombones". Every full-brass diagram on PDF 102 and 110–111 has the tuba on the bass under the trombones. PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind". PDF 112: "the parts in octaves strengthen one another, the harmonic sounds in the lowest register coinciding with and supporting those in the highest", and "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". PDF 114: balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords". 336–338 are the longest fff chords in the movement.

**Answering c034.** c034 (cycle 34, Adler II PDF 150) took out the trombones and tuba "under the B6 peak" so that the high note is not made heavy, and kept them for the entrance at 339. I read Adler's page. Barber leaves the trombones and tuba out of one bar of an orchestral glissando in octaves, so that a single high D on the horns stays brittle. That is a unison gesture. Bar 336 is a full V7 chord at fff, harmonised in every group, with a timpani roll. For a harmonised tutti chord Rimsky's rules above apply, and Adler's own Barber example ends with the low brass under the chord. The 339 "entrance" also costs the 335→336 resolution. Once the trombones hold through, 339 still has the root motion B→E in the bass trombone and tuba, the tutti stroke, and the brass diminuendo (c128). No other protection is touched. c200's brass levels are kept (trombones f, no brass fff, no accent, no new attack, since the slur from 333 simply continues). c194's B2 and c204's \partCombineChords at 339 are kept. The do-not-touch "coda build to fff at 336" and the brass economy (trombones and tuba only at 101–120 and 332–339) are unchanged. There is no low-brass unison either: the trombones hold F♯3/D♯3 over the B.tbn/tuba octave.

### 2. Bars 105–120: unpaired horns on the tutti strokes (kept)

PDF 103 marks unpaired horns one degree louder than two trumpets. Hn 1·2 are f, like the trumpets (c505). The open sixth G3–E4 in the brass is filled by Hn 3·4/Bsn 1/Vc and the violas. These are detached crotchet strokes, for which PDF 114 says resonance is "a minor consideration". Making them ff would break Dan's soft-brass rule (BRIEF, c123) and c505's do-not-flip. No change.

### 3. Bar 337: Hn 1's ♭9 C5 over Hn 4's B3 inside the horns (kept)

PDF 102 prefers the discord in a different colour. c464 kept it because moving Hn 4 would leave the brass without a root. After Finding 1 the root is in the bass trombone and tuba, so that reason no longer holds. I tried the alternative, Hn 4 F♯3 at 336–338. It triples the fifth (Tbn 1, Hn 4, Hn 2·3) to remove a one-bar neighbour-note ninth, and PDF 104 accepts a doubled middle note when the timbres are mixed. The ♭9 is a single bar's neighbour C–B in Hn 1, and the change would make the horn chord worse for two bars to fix one. No change.

### 4. Bars 76, 79, 87: the tritone in the horn pair, not the bassoons (kept)

PDF 108 asks for concords in the horns and discords in the bassoon. c547 showed that every swap breaks the chorale's common tones and the tenths with the bass. The reading gives no stronger reason. No change.

### 5. Bars 305–320: the reprise tutti without trumpets and trombones (kept)

PDF 113 asks for all three groups for a "full, round, firm tone", and the exposition tutti 105–120 has them. The reprise holds the low brass back so that the coda's entry at 332 is new (c033, c244). After Finding 1 the coda uses the full brass for seven bars, which makes the economy pay off more clearly. No change.

### 6. Stopped horn with oboe or cor anglais (PDF 107, 110) (not applicable)

This is Rimsky's colour for p or sfp chords. The score's dark places are the F-major chorale (protected) and the closing page (protected). Adding a new brass colour near the end would compete with the G♯ frame. No change.
