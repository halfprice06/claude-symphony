## Findings

### 1. Bars 209–210: the forte return of T puts IV6 under its 4th note (applied)

**Problem.** T's head is G♯–A–B–E, scale degrees 3̂–4̂–5̂–8̂. This is the soprano pattern A&S build Unit 13 sec. 3 around. At the trio's climax, the forte return (Vln I with Vln II and Cl 1 in octaves), the A on beat 3 of 209 is a plain passing tone over a held E2. The bass then leaps up a third to G♯2 at 210. The first statement (153) keeps the plain drone on purpose (c089). The second (169) already puts IV under the cellos' A. So the most important statement has the least harmonic motion under its head.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 199: "In this very important progression, IV6 supports a passing tone ascending from 3̂ to 5̂; the bass moves from I to I6, not up a 3rd by step as is usual, but down a 6th by leap" (Ex. 13-7, Haydn 101/iv, the same 3̂–4̂–5̂ head). PDF 199 also says the device is "most valuable, especially if a leading-tone chord ... is not wanted as the support for 4̂". That is the case here, because c257 set the bass to reach I6 at 210. PDF 200 and Ex. 13-9b–d: the bass must fall from IV6 to I6. A rise C♯–G♯ would leave 6̂ "exposed and unresolved", which is why the I6 goes down to G♯1. PDF 169: the usual doubling in IV6 is the 3rd above the bass (E). The result is the bass line E2–C♯2–G♯1–B1–E2 (209–212). It keeps c257's I6 at 210 and adds a falling sixth in contrary motion to the rising tune.

**Fix (in tC.ily).**
- `tCCb`: `e,2\mf^\markup \italic "arco" cis,4 | gis,,2. |` (was `e,2. | gis,2.`)
- `tCCbsn`: `e,2--(\mp cis,4-- | gis,,2.-- |`
- `tCVc` and `tCBsnTwo`: `b,4(\mf gis4 e4~ | e2 gis4) |` (was `b,4( gis2 | e2 gis4)`). The countermelody takes E, the 3rd above the bass, and ties it into 210. It does not go G♯3–A3, which would move in octaves with the tune's G♯–A.
- `tCVla`: first bar `r4 <b e'>4--( <a e'>4--)`
- `tCHnOne`: `gis'2--(\p cis''4-- | b'2.-- |`. G♯4 still resolves the motto tail's A4 at 209 (c324). The C♯5 falls to B4 at 210.

On beat 3 of 209, every sounding part now plays A, C♯ or E (checked in the MIDI). The all-pairs scan finds no new parallels. 153–168, 169–184, 210–240 and the tune itself are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 345: Cl 1's G♯4 sounds against the 7th that the coda's phrase holds for three bars (applied)

**Problem.** Since c381, Cl 1 has played a sixth under Ob 1 at 345–348. On beat 3 of 345 it plays G♯4 (under Ob 1's passing E5). At that moment the harp strikes A4 and the violas hold A3. That A is the 7th of the chord on beats 2–3 (IV with the 5–6 B in Bsn 1, that is, ii6/5 of A). It is held through ii7 (346) and V7/V (347) and resolves to G♯ only at 348 (harp G♯4). Cl 1 sounds the resolution pitch, in the same octave as the harp's A4, three bars before the resolution.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 177: the 7th of II "enters as a common tone held over or repeated from the preceding chord" and resolves "downward and by step". PDF 180–181 and Ex. 12-6a: in II7–V7 the 7th is held over and then resolves. The coda carries out this chain in the harp. A lower neighbour sounding against it in the same register cancels the effect. The fix also uses the reading's own device: Cl 1 joins the held A, so it prepares the 7th of 346 as a tied common tone (PDF 177, Ex. 12-2: the alto moves "to prepare the suspension").

**Fix (coda.ily, `codaClOne`).** `fis'2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a'4~ | a'4\< d''2 ...` (was `gis'4 | a'4`). Cl 1 stays a sixth under Ob 1 on every other beat of 345–348. On this one beat it lies a fifth under the oboe's passing E5. Pitches, rhythm of 346–348, dynamics and Cl 1's rests from 349 are unchanged. This refines c381 and does not undo it.
