## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 45–52, 121–128, 277–284: the second idea sounds like its own reduction (APPLIED, theme change)

**Problem.** The second idea is the movement's broad lyrical contrast. It has been on record as "well made but not memorable" since c289 (also c299, c309, c319). In v361 every note on a minim is a chord tone, and the bass moves with it in 10ths and octaves: D6 over G, B5 over B, G6 over G, then F♯6/D, E6/E, D6/B. The middle minim of each hemiola, tied over the bar line (B5 at 45–46, C♯6 at 49–50, G5 at 121–122, A5 at 125–126), doubles the bass in octaves. The only figuration is the anticipation quaver at 48 and 52. So the tune is the same as its first-level reduction. A&S PDF 328 says this is the version that "removes much of the music's individual flavor".

**Evidence.**
- PDF 328, Ex. 20-1: in Schumann the reduction shows the frame, and the accented dissonances on top of it make the melody.
- PDF 332, Ex. 20-7a: a passing tone fills a third within one chord. D6–B5 at 45 is a chordal skip inside G major (I to I6), so a passing C6 fills it. E6–C♯6 at 49 is the same inside A major.
- PDF 336, Ex. 20-14/15: accented passing tones are displacements of the normal kind.
- PDF 338, Ex. 20-20: a sequence pattern decorated on its strong beats with accented dissonances. 45–48 and 49–52 are such a sequence, a step apart.
- In the hemiola the middle minim's attack (beat 3) is a strong beat. A dissonance there gets the accent the hemiola needs (PDF 336: a dissonance is emphasised).
- C6 over B is theme A's own ♭9–8 (C6–B5 at 24). The second idea now carries the movement's characteristic semitone in place of a plain octave with the bass.

The do-not-touch reasons hold. c321 kept the second idea because "every rewrite lost the rising sixth or the hemiola", and c271 because "an accented dissonance on its minims would need c032's sequential bass changed". This change keeps all three:
- The rising sixths B5→G6 (46) and C♯6→A6 (50) are intact.
- The bass and harmony are untouched.
- The hemiola stays three minims per two bars in the bass, the inner parts and the harmonic rhythm.

### 2. T's first phrase, 153–156 (considered, kept)

T's frame is G♯–A–B–E | D♯–F♯–A–G♯. Its figuration is the 4–3 at 155–156 (c331), the falling sixth (c321), the appoggiatura at 160, the tied A at 162 and the 4–3 at 165–166. Each of its four phrases has its own dissonance (PDF 336, 338). The open point is that its rhythm (minim–crotchet) has no figure of its own. I tried the reading's own remedy: a quaver neighbour or turn on beat 3 of bars 2 and 6, as in Dvořák's lines 31–38. On bar 2 it would put notes between B4 and E5, which changes the head G♯4–A4–B4–E5 that movement I relies on. On bar 6 alone it would be a single ornament with no echo. T has also been changed twice in the last 40 cycles (c321, c331). The reading supports the second idea more strongly, and that change is free of cross-movement ties. No change.

### 3. Harp head 73–80 (considered, kept)

Its diatonic quaver passing tones (PDF 332) are a plain reduction of theme A's chromatic bar 2, and every downbeat is a chord tone. Against the decorated tutti this plainness is its character, and it is protected (c080). No change.

### 4. Motto-tail chromatic order (considered, kept)

G–F♯–F–E and B–A♯–A–G♯ put the diatonic half step first, the reverse of PDF 339's usual order. This is the motto's own order (movement I, E♭–D–D♭–C), it is protected, and each spelling follows the direction rule. No change.

### 5. Coda return of T, 341–356 (checked, kept)

The A-major half (Hn 3 341, Ob 1 345) keeps T's dissonances at 344 and 348 (D♯→E, a leading-tone appoggiatura resolving up, PDF 338). In the E-major half, 354 G♯6–F♯6–E6 is a passing figure over the held 6/4 (PDF 332), and 355 is the 4–3. No change.
