## Findings

### 1. Bar 170: the new I6 doubles its bass (the third) at the top of the texture. Applied.

**Problem.** c433 put the basses on G♯1 at 170 under the cellos' T, so the bar is now I6 (E/G♯). It did not change the upper voices. Vln II's con sord. arpeggio in that bar is r8 G♯4 E5 G♯5 E5 G♯4. The bass's third is therefore doubled twice in the treble, and G♯5 is the highest note sounding. Above the bass the cellos and Hn 3 play only B3 and E4 (T's bar 2). The other notes of the inversion sit low, and its third sits on top.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 152, with Ex. V-27: in I6, IV6 and V6 the bass, which is the third, "would invariably be doubled somewhere to strengthen it. It is advisable to double it near the bottom and leave the 'open' sound so characteristic of this inversion intact." In the example, both A-major I6 chords marked "good" keep C♯ out of the treble and double it in the bass staff. Both chords marked "not good" put C♯ in the treble, and the second of them has it at the top. Bar 170 is the second "not good" case. At 210 the same I6 in T's forte return is voiced correctly: the G♯ is in the bass and in the cellos' G♯3, and only B and E sound above. This finding does not touch c344's reason for G♯4 at 170, which was to keep the third once the cellos' countermelody left. With the bass on G♯ the third is now present, at the bottom.

**Fix (applied, sections/tA.ily, tAVlnTwo, bar 170; comment c434 at the head of the file).**
`r8 gis'8( e''8 gis''8 e''8 gis'8)` becomes `r8 b'8( e''8 b''8 e''8 b'8)`
The figure keeps the up-and-down shape of the bars around it. Root and fifth sound above the bass's G♯, which gives the open sound. The top goes from G♯5 to B5, two octaves above T's B3, and moves in contrary motion with the bass at 169-170 and at 170-171. It stays pp con sord. and in the pale register that c344 chose. Movement I quotes T's head (G♯4-A4-B4-E5); that head and T's notes are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 16 and 32: violas doubling theme A's fall to B3. Tested in principle, not applied.

This was the most audible candidate. Adler (PDF 141, Ex. V-19) has the cellos join the violins when the violins go low "for a feeling of greater resolution". PDF 152, item 3b, lists violins with violas in unison. The fall F♯4-D♯4-B3 at the half cadences 16 and 32 is the lowest moment of Vln I's main theme (PDF 133: Vln I is "very weak" in its low register). A viola unison avoids the reasons c254 and c259 gave against the cellos, because nothing is thickened arco below and the bass is not given up. It still makes the music worse. The violas would have to give up their F♯3 off-beats, which keep V complete under the melody (c073, c227). At the half cadence the foreground and the background would merge in the same string colour, which PDF 135 warns against. The fall is also a weak-beat cadential descent that the light opening (c259) wants light. Theme A gets its strengthened, lower restatement at 105 and 137-138 (the cellos' close). Not applied.

### 3. Bars 121-128: a moving viola figure under the E-minor second idea. Not applied.

PDF 135 says a "repetitious and simple" background figure "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody". PDF 141 (Ex. V-18) shows violas in a contrasting divisi rhythm, and PDF 159 (Ex. V-34) shows arpeggiated figures over held harmony. The second idea moves in long tied notes, and HISTORY still lists it as the least memorable idea. The violas at 121-128 hold dotted minims (E-G-F♯-E-E-C). A quaver figure would be the most audible change to that statement. I rejected it for three reasons. Quaver arpeggios in a muted string section are the trio's background at 169-184 (c407), and using them in the scherzo would spend that colour beforehand. That is the same anticipation PDF 133 and 135 warn against. The violas' dotted minims are c017's third rhythmic layer under the hemiola (PDF 144, point 4, is already met). And at 2. = 88 the quavers would run at about nine notes a second under a sub. p espress. line, which would make it agitated rather than lyrical. Not applied.
