## Findings

### 1. T's final cadence breaks off its hemiola one beat early (166, 182, 222). APPLIED (theme change)

**Problem.** T's last cadence is `gis'4 b'4 e''4~ | e''4 dis''4 fis''4 | e''2.` (165–167; 181–183 in B; 221–223). The first five beats group G♯–B | E~E | D♯, which is the hemiola of Ex. 3-8: three groups of two beats across two bars. Then the F♯ on beat 3 of the second bar puts a fresh attack on a 3/4 beat just before the arrival, so the hemiola is never completed and the tune ends in neither metre. Two other things point the same way.
- At the forte return the cellos' bass already moves in 3/2 under it: `b,2 cis4 | a,4 b,2` at 221–222. This is the c271 double neighbour, and it is protected. Only the tune's F♯ contradicts the bass.
- T's own half-closes end with the tied cell `x4~ | x4 y2`: bars 3–4 `a'4~ | a'4 gis'2` and 7–8 `ais'4~ | ais'4 b'2`. The final cadence was the one phrase end without it.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 51–52 and Ex. 3-8: the hemiola is two bars of 3/4 heard as one bar of 3/2. In Mozart's example it comes at the cadence, and the left hand joins it.
- PDF 52 and Ex. 3-10: rhythmic groups are an element of the design.
- PDF 55 and Ex. 3-16 to 3-18: the suspension delays one tone of the chord. With the change, the resolution D♯ is held for the two beats of V7 instead of being left at once for F♯.
- PDF 49: the melody's main tones set the felt pace. Bars 13–15 now carry three, two and one notes, a written-out broadening into the tonic. This suits the trio's meno mosso.
- In this score: every scherzo cadence uses this formula (54–55, 130–131, 286–287, 101–104, 337–338). The trio now shares it in its own soft dynamic, which is Brahms's practice of carrying a scherzo's cadential hemiola into the trio.

**Fix (in /tmp/critic-531/work).** In bar 14 of T the D♯ (A♯ in the B-major statement) becomes a minim. The F♯ (C♯) is dropped. Slurs, hairpins and the hidden MIDI dynamics are unchanged, so the c448 swell into the suspension and fall to the resolution stays.

### 2. T bar 12 (164, 180, 220, 352) as a dotted sequence of bar 11. Considered, rejected

`cis''4. b'8 a'4` would answer `a''4. gis''8 fis''4` as a sequence. PDF 51 says the simplest rhythms fit the metre. The even walk-down C♯–B–A–G♯ is the relaxation before the cadence, and it keeps the B–A–G♯ echo of the first phrase (c511). Making bar 12 a dotted sequence would crowd the dotted cell into bars 11–12, just before the new cadence. No change.

### 3. Theme A bar 21 (113, 261, 313) in the tail's rhythm (`a''2 g''8 fis''8`). Considered, rejected

This would put a long note on the strong first bar of the continuation (PDF 53). But the continuation now drives in crotchets and motive x to the one long note, the C6 at 24 (PDF 50: long + high + dissonant). A second long note at 21 would weaken that drive. It would also give the motto's tail rhythm to a diatonic A–G–F♯–E, which comes close to developing the motto (cyclic rule). No change.

### 4. Phrase lengths (Ex. 3-14). Kept

All themes are in fours; 89–100 supplies the three-bar groups (Ex. 3-12). A five- or seven-bar phrase would renumber the bars that the finale and the records quote (357–376). This is c441's reason, and the reading gives none stronger. No change.

### 5. Second idea (45–56, 121–132, 277–288). Follows

c491 called its trouble "rhythmic: eight bars of hemiola minims". Ex. 3-7 shows a consistent counter-pattern kept against the written metre as a legitimate device. Here the horns' and clarinets' dotted minims keep the 3/4 against the 3/2 tune. Bar 53 (c451) restores 3/4 before the cadential hemiola, as bar 7 does in Ex. 3-8. No change.

### 6. Theme A, the harp melody, the coda's T. Follow

- **Theme A.** Motive x then the long tail on the even bars. The tail's E overlaps into the next motive x (Ex. 3-11). The ♭9 appoggiatura C6 is the theme's one long, high, dissonant peak (PDF 50).
- **Harp melody 73–80.** Long notes on even bars, falling by step in two-bar groups to the goal at 80 (Ex. 3-13). Protected.
- **Coda's T 341–356.** 4 + 4 + 8 bars on hypermeasure lines (PDF 53). The anticipated E at 354 (Ex. 3-19) and the hemiola-like 354–355 make it the varied last statement.
