## Findings

### 1. Best candidate, built and rejected: a diminished-third chord at 202, under the trio's return

**Bars:** 201–203 (tB.ily).

**What I tested.** At 200–203 the trio does the following:
- 200: V of C♯ minor.
- 201: a deceptive A major. This is VI of C♯ and IV of E.
- 202: A minor (iv, the mixture C♮ in Vla, Bsn 1 and Vln I).
- 203–208: V7 of E. The motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ is anticipated at 205–209.

This is the trio's one structural dominant that is reached without a chromatic chord. The reading supports changing that in three places:
- PDF 513: "Augmented 6ths frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI … because they form a convincing transition to V."
- Ex. 29-31b (PDF 519): in major, IV – ♯IV(♭6) – V with the bass rising 4̂–♯4̂–5̂.
- PDF 505: the thinnest form suits soft places.

I built the version that leaves every upper line alone:
- Bass A♯ at 202: Vc `ais,2.`, Cb `ais,,2.`, Bsn 2 `ais,2.`.
- Hn 4 `fis2.--` at 202, leaving the A3 that would clash with the bass.

The chord is A♯–C–E–F♯. The diminished tenth A♯2–C4 closes to the octave B2–B3, and nothing else moves. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, no new parallels. I kept that file as /tmp/critic-512/tB.candidate202.ily.

**Why it makes the music worse.**
- **It repeats the scherzo's return chord.** A♯–C–E–F♯ resolving to B is the same pitch-class set, in the same key and onto the same B, as the French sixth at 244. That chord announces the scherzo's return 42 bars later, and the coda's at 331. A&S PDF 503 gives the augmented sixth its force as the signal of "the beginning or end of a phase in the tonal movement or the form". Sounding it at the trio's internal return spends that signal before 244. This is the reason c062, c332 and c422 gave, and the reading confirms it rather than answering it.
- **It takes the novelty from the motto tail's A♯.** The tail's A♯ at 207 (Fl 1, Hn 1, B–A♯–A–G♯ falling) is the trio's chromatic event over this V7. A rising A♯ in the bass five bars earlier pre-empts it.
- **It removes a mixture the trio builds on.** The iv at 202 is the trio's first ♭6̂ (C5–B4 in Vln I and violas, c071/c177). The codetta's IV–iv–I at 227/231/235 builds on it. A&S PDF 488–489 places chromatic colour in major inside "a larger mixture with minor", and here that mixture is the iv. A diminished-third chord replaces it with a leading-tone sound.
- **It breaks a protection.** It changes the protected soft horn–bassoon voicing at 201–204 (c229).

**Result.** The scratch copy is restored to v512 at 202 and rebuilt. Do not raise this again without a reason that answers the 244 duplication.

### 2. Record correction: there is no Italian sixth at 300 any more

**Bars:** 300–302 (dB.ily). This is information only; no notes change.

HISTORY keeps "Do-not-touch: the Italian sixth at 300 (c422)" and c422's "three different augmented-sixth approaches". Since c473 (♭II6 at 301–302) and c480 (B♭5), beat 3 of 300 is a different chord:
- It is spelled C–E–B♭: Vln I B♭5, Vln II B♭4, Vla E4, Vc C3, Cb C2.
- It resolves B♭→A, E→F and C→A into F/A.
- That makes it V7 of ♭II without its fifth. It is the Italian sound, resolved as the enharmonic dominant of ♭II.

This follows the reading:
- PDF 524, Ex. 29-39: German sixth ≡ V7 of ♭II, which composers use "with a dramatic conception".
- PDF 491: ♭II tonicized "by its own dominant" does not impede ♭II–V.
- PDF 496–497: B♭ correctly shows the falling goal A.

The ear expects B and gets F, the "false clue" of PDF 533, and then V7 at 303. It is the strongest of the movement's three approaches to a structural V. Keep it.

Proposed HISTORY wording: "300.3 C–E–B♭ = V7 of ♭II (Italian-sixth sound, A&S Ex. 29-39), resolving to ♭II6 at 301; do not respell B♭ as A♯ or resolve it to B. Augmented sixths proper: French at 244 and 331 only."

### 3. Record correction: the reason c242 gave for the codetta no longer holds, but the result stands

**Bars:** 225–240 (tC.ily). This is information only.

c242 rejected a ♭II neighbour at 235 because "A&S 522 places the chord over a moving bass, not a pedal". Ex. 28-20 (PDF 495, Chopin Op. 25/4) shows "♭II" inside a final tonic over tenor and bass pedals in a coda, functioning "much like a IV used at a plagal cadence". So the pedal is not an obstacle.

The codetta should still not get one, for a different reason. The movement's own close (357–360) already does exactly this, with A♯°7–iv over the E pedal under the motto tail. An F at 235 would anticipate it in the same key, and the codetta's point is its IV–iv–I mixture (c402, A&S Ex. 23-8).
