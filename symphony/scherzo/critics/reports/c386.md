## Findings

### 1. Bar 193: the harp's damping mark is missing from the full score (applied)

- **Problem.** c116 put "étouffez" on the rest bar 193 so the F♯-major chord of 192 (F♯4–A♯4–C♯5 over the wound F♯2 string) is damped when the music turns back to E. Since c364 silenced the harp at 209–224, the system 193–212 has nothing but rests in the harp, so LilyPond removes the harp staff from that system, and the word goes with it. In v386 the full score (page 7) shows the harp's last chords at 191–192 and then no damping instruction anywhere; `pdftotext` finds no "étouffez" on page 7. The harp part still has it, so the harpist is told, but the conductor's score is not, and the score no longer matches the part.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 117: sons étouffés means "each note is dampened as soon as it is played"; the composer has to say where. PDF 112–113: the lowest eleven strings are wire-wound, and F♯2 is one of them; it rings well past the bar line. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: the harp must be damped at a change of harmony, the reason c116 gave. The two other damping marks (87, 240) are in bars where the harp staff is printed and still show.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-386/work/sections/tB.ily).** Print the word at the end of bar 192, where the harp staff is shown, and drop it from 193:

  ```lilypond
  % tBHarpR, bar 192, was:  r4 <fis' ais' cis''>4 <fis' ais' cis''>4 | \once \override ... R2._\markup \small \italic "étouffez" |
  r4 <fis' ais' cis''>4 << <fis' ais' cis''>4 { s8 s8_\markup \small \italic "étouffez" } >> | R2. |
  ```

  Result: bar 192 moves to the start of the next system, which now carries the harp staff with the mark at the end of 192 (page 7, second system). Pages 1–6 and 9–13 have the same layout as before; page 7 now holds 173–211 and page 8 212–252 (was 213–252). Still 13 pages. The harp part prints "étouffez" at 87, 192 and 240.

No other findings. The harp part follows every principle of the reading that applies to it. The faults this chapter can expose (missing pedal settings, harmonics notation, damping, the ♮ signs, the pedal time at 189) were fixed by c026, c116 and c206, and c296 found nothing new. The one fault here was not in the harp writing but was a side effect of c364's cut, which no later critic caught because the part still had the mark. I do not propose changes for their own sake.

## Checked and kept (decided)

- **Do-not-touch reasons re-read against the reading.** None of them is weaker than Adler's text. The harp melody 73–80 (A5–C6, p) is in the light top register (PDF 114), but the only other instruments are pp low winds and Hn 3·4, so it carries. The harmonics at 375 sound G♯4 in unison with Hn 1. Adler asks for light accompaniment (PDF 116), and everything else there is ppp/pp. The harmonic's attack gives a bell-like start inside a horn note that is already sounding, and the finale relies on the harmonics' E4 (c062). Kept.
- **Harp chords 345–348 now share the band D4–B4 with Cl 1's sixths (since c381).** Adler ch. 4 gives no rule against a plucked accompaniment beside a wind line. The harp's A4 at 345–347 is the prepared seventh that c382 relies on. Moving the chords down masks Bsn 1 (c099), and moving them up masks Ob 1. Kept.
- **Close three-note chords (153–168, 185–192, 225–236, 341–348) rather than Adler's wide spacing (PDF 114).** c296's reason stands: spreading them up enters the solo's register, and spreading them down enters the wound strings under the pizz. basses. Kept.
- **Pedal marks stripped from the harp part (MOLA; c075, c135).** The B♯ spelling at 188 tells the player. Kept.
- **Low wound bass notes 81–86 and the trio's beat-1 basses.** Damping the previous bass string while placing the next is normal practice when the harmony changes (c206, c296). A mark on every bar would be clutter that Adler does not ask for. Kept.
