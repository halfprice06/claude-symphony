## Findings

### 1. Bars 197–200: the call in Vln I's sixth octave is as loud as T's head below it (applied)

**Problem.** At the forte centre of the trio the two ideas of the middle section are combined: T's head G♯4–A4–B4–E5 in Vln II, violas and Cl 1 at f, Hn 1 at mf, and the call (E6–F♯6–E6–D♯6–E6, C♯6–G♯5) in Vln I at f with Fl 1 and Ob 1 at mf. c167 set the winds a level under the violins they double, so the call's weight is that of the violins at f. The call lies two octaves above T, in the register the ear follows first. With equal markings, the decorative line in the sixth octave is heard ahead of the trio tune it decorates, at the one place in the trio where T carries a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument together.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184 (Schoenberg op. 16/5, Ex. XV-31): the principal voice is marked louder than the subsidiary ones, and a subsidiary voice in a piercing register is marked softer still. PDF 159, first consideration for any orchestral texture: the melody notes must be more prominent than the others. PDF 169: two ideas are told apart by timbre and design; here that already holds (c117), so the dynamic is the remaining difference to set.

**Fix (applied).** sections/tB.ily: `tBVlnOne` bar 13 `e'''4(\f` → `e'''4(\mf`; `tBFluteOne` bar 13 `e'''4(\mf` → `e'''4(\mp`; `tBOboeOne` bar 13 `e''4(\mf` → `e''4(\mp`. c167's one-level offset between the winds and Vln I is kept. T's own markings are unchanged (Vln II, Vla, Cl 1 f with the ff swell at 200; Hn 1 mf). Vln I's crescendo from p at 193 now leads into mf at 197, so it still rises. Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest in these bars (c119, c094), so the shared staves have no conflicting dynamics. This undoes no earlier fix: the layering, notes and rhythms of c107/c117 stay as they are.

### 2. Bars 113–116: the flutes leave the sixth octave in the middle of the exposition tutti (tested; kept)

**Problem considered.** At 109–112 and 117–120 Fl 1·2 a2 play theme A an octave above Vln I (E6–A6). At 113–116 they drop to unison with Vln I and Ob 1 (A5–C6, with E5–D♯5 at 114). In those bars nothing sounds above C6, and the flutes at E5 under a ff tutti with brass add little (PDF 159, item 2; Brahms and Mahler, PDF 164: every upper octave filled).

**Why kept.** The whole phrase cannot go up: 115–116 would reach B6 and C7 (the single B6 at 336 and the finale's C7 are protected). The only legal version is the flutes up an octave at 113–114 only. I wrote it out against the parts but did not build it. That gives the top line A6–E6 at 113–114, then F♯5–C6 at 115–116, then E6 at 117. The theme's accented peak, C6 at 116, then comes after higher flute notes and loses the top of the texture just when it arrives. At present the top line at 113–116 is the theme itself rising to its C6 peak, and the flutes enter above it again at 117. Wagner's rule (PDF 172: hold back the highs and add octaves as the passage grows) supports the present layout. On paper that version makes the phrase worse, so I did not apply it. The reprise (313–316) avoids the problem because its flutes play the heterophony (c369), which turns below B6. That figure cannot move into the exposition, because it is first heard alone at 253 (PDF 177, 181).

### 3. Bars 45–56: the second idea is doubled at pitch for 12 bars (kept)

Brahms 3/i mm. 183–87 (PDF 163–164) leaves the violins' forte tune undoubled over full wind chords, with the high flutes marking the first melody note of each bar. PDF 165 warns that sustained at-pitch doublings tire the ear. Here Fl 1 doubles Vln I and Ob 1 doubles Vln II at pitch for the whole tune. I kept it for three reasons. The tune moves in hemiola minims tied across the bar line, so there is no "first note of each bar" for the flutes to mark. Taking Fl 1 off for the first half would copy the staged entries of the E-minor statement at 121–125, and the three statements (45, 121, 277) need different colours (PDF 170–171). c224 and c314 kept this doubling as a doubling for power, which PDF 165 allows. This is still the least memorable idea (open since c289). The reading gives no orchestral fix for that.

### 4. Bars 169–184: no sustained background under the cellos' statement of T (kept)

*Scheherazade* (PDF 179) has three layers: foreground, figuration and sustained harmony. At 153–168 T has all three: Cl 1, the harp's off-beats and the horn drone. At 169 the cellos have T over Vln II's louré off-beats and pizz. basses, with no sustained layer. I kept this. Mahler's rule (PDF 170–171) asks a returning passage to keep its texture and change its colours. 169 changes every colour, and the missing drone is part of that change. Adding horns or winds would make the second statement a copy of the first (c257).

### 5. Bars 205–208: two flutes in unison at p on the motto-tail preview (kept)

PDF 165–166 says a unison of like instruments weighs the tone down with a slight "flatness" (Mahler's four flutes, a deliberate effect at f). c387 doubled Fl 2 with Fl 1 on Rimsky-Korsakov's rule that two winds, not one, balance a single horn an octave below (rimsky-principles PDF 75). The doubling is there for balance against the horn's strong register, not as the melody's own colour, and it lasts four soft bars. Adler's warning does not outweigh that reason.

### 6. Bars 336–338: trombones and tuba rest under the B6 peak (kept)

The Wagner and Mahler examples (PDF 164, 172) put the lowest octave in bass trombone, tuba, cellos and basses. At 336 the lowest octave is Cbsn, Vc, Cb and timpani at fff on B1–B2, and the low brass return at 339. c034 chose this so the high note is not made heavy and the last chord gets the low-brass entry (PDF 162: holding back for the next chord). A full low-brass unison bass is also on the finale's list. Kept.
