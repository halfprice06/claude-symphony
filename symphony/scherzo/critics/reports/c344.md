## Findings

### 1. Bars 169-184: T's second statement belongs to the cellos, not to the violins in the octave of the forte return. Applied.

**Problem.** T is stated four times in the trio. Cl 1 has it at G♯4 (153). Vln I has it at G♯5 with Vln II an octave lower, mp (169), and the statement peaks on A6 at 178. Cl 1, the violas and Vln II have its head at G♯4 (197). Vln I and Vln II have it at G♯5/G♯4, f-ff, peaking on A6 at 218 (209). So the soft second statement has the same octave, the same instruments and the same peak as the forte return. The return differs only in dynamics and added winds. The violins' A6 is first heard at mp in 178. The protected plan ("four colourings of the trio tune"; c254, c304) tells the statements apart by their surroundings, but not by the colour or register of the tune itself at 169 and 209. That gap is why later critics kept calling T "well made but not memorable" (open since c289; c299, c301, c309, c311, c319).

**Evidence.** Adler's main example in this chapter (PDF 133-135, Ex. V-7 to V-11) asks exactly this question for a theme at this pitch level. Brahms 3/iii is a third movement in triple time whose theme starts around G3-C4. In Vln I the theme "is very weak and without great emotional force"; an octave higher, "the next statement would be anticlimactic and subsequent restatements anticipated" (PDF 133). Vln II on the G string is "of a much darker hue than the upper strings of the cello", and the viola "lacks intensity and opulence" (PDF 134). The choice is "the cellos in their lyrical and intense register", which "allows the composer to build up to a higher pitch level in the violin statement" (PDF 135). The background of violins and violas goes into middle, pale registers so that it does not "anticipate the fact that the violins would repeat the melody at the higher pitch level" (PDF 135). Haydn's variations make the same two points: the cello takes the theme where Vln II would be weakest (PDF 127, Ex. V-3), and the octave transposition is held back for the last statement (PDF 129). Adler gives the cello solo as one way to strengthen a string melody (PDF 152, item 5), and says orchestration "must clarify the form" (PDF 166). c074 already used PDF 129 and 135 here to keep Fl 1 off this statement. The same reason applies to Vln I.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-344/work/sections/tA.ily; comment in global.ily at L).**
- Cellos: T an octave below Cl 1's 153 statement. The notes, rhythm, slurs and hairpins are Vln II's present line moved down an octave (G♯3-A4, on the D and A strings, peak A4 at 178), mp "arco, espress.", in tenor clef.

What this changes. The trio now presents T in four registers and colours: Cl 1 at G♯4, cellos at G♯3, Cl 1 with the violas at G♯4, and the violins at G♯5 forte. A6 is first heard at the forte peak 218. The cellos' countermelody enters at 209, as one of the elements added for the return (PDF 129). The statement is still for strings alone (c074, c319). The cellos still rest at 153-168 and enter fresh at 169 (c037). The c093 modulation to B at 180-184 and c331's suspension at 171-172 are kept note for note: under the held A3 nothing doubles the A or sounds the G♯. Two earlier fixes are reversed because their reasons no longer apply. c011 put Vln II on T an octave lower; that doubling is no longer needed because the tune is now low. The c051/c158/c192/c218 shaping of the cellos' countermelody at 169-184 also goes, but it stays where the countermelody is still played (209-224). This answers the do-not-touch entries "trio layering 169-184" (c107), "four colourings" and "trio foreground/background plan 149-240" (c304). Their stated reasons (graded layering, strings alone at 169, fresh cellos, the harp's rest) all still hold, and the reading gives the stronger reason: the 169 colour should not anticipate the 209 colour.
