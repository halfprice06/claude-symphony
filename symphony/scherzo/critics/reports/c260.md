## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The theme record cannot see the four links the other movements take at pitch (records; tested, not a score change)

Bars: 1–6 (horn call), 14, 106, 254, 306 (theme A bar 2), 363, 367, 371 (closing timpani motive x), 368–376 Hn 1 and 369–376 Vln I (the last G♯).

Problem: HISTORY.md protects these bars because other movements take them at the same pitch: I 79 and IV 273–275 play G5–F♯5–F5–E5; IV 1 continues the last G♯4/G♯5 as A♭4/A♭5 in the same players; IV 9 takes up the closing timpani figure; IV 219–220 resolves the call's D♯4. themes-scherzo.ly has no line for the closing figure or the last G♯ at all. The lines it has cannot catch a register change either: `shape()` in coherence/themecheck.py compares intervals modulo 12, so a statement an octave higher or a fifth lower passes as unchanged. I tested this. In a scratch copy (/tmp/critic-260/negtest) I moved Vln I's bar 14 up an octave (G6–F♯6–F6–E6) and changed the closing figure's E2 at 363 to E3. The current tool still reports "theme record: 51/51 unchanged". This is Sebesky's point on PDF 241: what worked has to be written down so that it is applied again. Here the written record is the only thing the finale and movement I check before they rely on the scherzo, and it is blind to the property they rely on.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 241 (annotate what worked, and know why); PDF 12 (every note there for a reason; the reason for these notes is their pitch); the tool's own docstring ("transposition and octave changes are allowed").

Fix (tested in /tmp/critic-260, for the composer to copy into symphony/coherence/; I wrote nothing outside /tmp except this report):
- themecheck.py: an optional last field `abs` on a `%@` line also compares absolute pitches. The patch is /tmp/critic-260/themecheck-abs.diff (57 lines). Its core: the regex gains `(?:\s+(abs))?`, `shape(notes, absolute)` returns `(iv, ln, ioi, beat, tuple(n[1] for n in notes))` when it is set, and a failed `abs` line is reported CHANGED (never MOVED). The old tool reads the new lines and ignores the field, so the change is backward compatible (checked: 51/51 with the unpatched tool).
- themes-scherzo.ly, appended (tested copy: /tmp/critic-260/themes-scherzo-test.ly):
```
motiveXclose = { b,4 b,8 b,8 e,4 }
lastGsharpHn = { gis'2. }
lastGsharpVln = { gis''2. }
%@ motiveXclose Timp 363 abs
%@ motiveXclose Timp 367 abs
%@ motiveXclose Timp 371 abs
%@ lastGsharpHn HnOne 368 abs
%@ lastGsharpVln VlnOne 369 abs
%@ hornCall HnOne 1 abs
%@ themeA VlnOne 14 4 5 abs
%@ themeA VlnOne 106 4 5 abs
%@ themeA VlnOne 254 4 5 abs
%@ themeA VlnOne 306 4 5 abs
```
Results: v260 51/51 unchanged with the patched tool. The negative test gives "2 CHANGED" (VlnOne 14: record G5/F♯5/F5/E5, version G6/F♯6/F6/E6; Timp 363: record E2, version E3). The finale thread can use the same field for its own A♭4/A♭5 at IV 1 and G5–F♯5–F5–E5 at IV 273–275. themes-scherzo.md's motiveX row should add "the closing form B2–B2–B2–E2 at 363, 367, 371 (pp, pp, ppp near the rim), taken up by the finale at IV 9".

### 3. Note for the finale: "arco" for cellos and basses at IV bar 1 (no scherzo change)

Bars: scherzo 375 (Vc E2, Cb E1 pizz., the last string attack of the movement); finale 1.

Problem: the scherzo's cellos and basses end pizzicato. The finale's bar 1 gives them a bowed ff chord with `\dnup` but no "arco" (no "arco" in finale v250's intro.ily or pOne.ily). At our own bar 3 we print "pizz." (and "tutti pizz." for the basses) after movement II's arco ending. The finale's bar 1 needs the reverse instruction.

Evidence: Adler part 1 PDF 54: "When the player is to resume playing with the bow, the word arco must be printed." Sebesky PDF 241: the ear does not see paper, but the players read it.

### Considered and decided

- The finale does not quote T. I do not ask it to. Sebesky PDF 11 says to restrict the number of melodic ideas, and the finale already carries five scherzo quotations as well as its own F1, F2 and chorale.
- 357–360: Bsn 1 doubles the violas' C♯4–C4–B3 in unison under the motto tail. Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) could argue for thinning it. But "close with moving bassoons" is protected (c014), and the line is the chord's third and seventh, not a decoration, so economy does not require removing it. Not raised.
