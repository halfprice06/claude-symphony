I read every text page from 17 to 30 in full and viewed PDF 19–30 as images at 110 dpi, with 300 and 600 dpi crops of the stem-length and tail examples (PDF 28–30) and of the stem-direction example (PDF 27). I inspected all 13 pages of v365 at 60–170 dpi and took 260–600 dpi crops at 45–56, 111–130, 173–192, 213–224, 253–270 and 357–376.

As in c275 I measured rather than only looked. I built a probe copy (/tmp/critic-365/probe, not a proposed change) with an engraver that logs every engraved stem: staff, voice, bar, beat, clef, notehead staff positions, direction and the stem's final extent — 5,244 stems. I logged the score before and after each change and compared the two. I also measured stem and stave-line thickness in pixels, at 600 dpi, both in this score and in Gould's own engraved examples.

## Findings

**1. Every bar: stem length. 617 stems were shorter than Gould's standard; applied.** Gould PDF 28: the standard stem is an octave, 3½ spaces from the notehead's centre, and in double-stemmed writing stems are shortened only "as stems fall further outside the stave" — octave, seventh (3), sixth (2½ and never less). LilyPond instead begins shortening at the middle line, so in this score the shortening had nothing to do with how far outside the stave the note lay: in the part-combined staves an up-stem on the top line was 2.67 spaces and one a step above the stave 2.5 (its full shortening already reached, with nowhere to go for the notes four ledger lines up at 219), while 174 single-voice middle-line notes (Vla 18/21/23, Vln II 37–39 and 95–104, Vc 273–275, Cl 1 154–165, Hn 1 206–207, Fl 1 256) stood at 3.33. The effect is that the bassoons', clarinets' and horns' two-voice writing reads lighter than the single-voice staves around it, and shortened stems inside the stave crowd the tenutos and dynamics hung off them. Fix, in stems.ily, with the callback set in score.ly and parts.ly: a stem keeps 3.5 while the note at its open end is inside the stave (staff position −4…4), then loses a quarter space per position outside it (3.25, 3.0, 2.75, 2.5), never below 2.5 and never below LilyPond's own value, so ledger-line stems still reach the middle line (PDF 28) and flagged quavers keep room for the tail (PDF 29–30). The probe confirms: 617 stems lengthened, none shortened, shortest still 2.5, no stem now inside the stave below 3.5, the only flagged quaver under 3.25 spaces is Cl 2 at 284 (3.0, within the PDF 30 limit). System and page breaks are unchanged (same bars on all 13 pages) because the callback also answers the page-breaking estimate.

**2. Every bar: stem thickness. The stems are heavier than the stave-lines; applied.** Gould PDF 27: "Stems should be thinner than the stave-line, but not so thin as to reproduce too faintly." Measured at 600 dpi on page 9 of v365: stave-line 3 px, stems 5 px, stave-space 29 px — LilyPond's default stem is 1.3 times the stave-line. Measured the same way on Gould's own stem-direction example (PDF 27, stave-space 26.5 px): stave-line 3–4 px, stems 3–4 px. The score's stems are about 1.7 times as heavy relative to the stave as the book's engraving, which makes the dense tutti pages (105–120, 305–320, 333–339) look blacker than they are. Fix: `\override Stem.thickness = #0.9` in the `\Voice` context of score.ly and parts.ly. Re-measured after the build: stems 3–4 px against a 3 px stave-line, the same proportion as the book; at 300 dpi the stems print as solidly as the stave-lines, so the second half of the rule is kept.

**3. Bars 184–189, cellos: the return to the bass clef stood before four bars' rest instead of before the entry; applied.** The cellos play T in tenor clef 169–184 (c344), rest 185–188 and re-enter at 189. The `\clef bass` sat at the end of 184. Gould PDF 22: "The clef always goes before the barline, whether or not rests precede the entry", and PDF 23: placing it immediately before a group of rests "draws unwanted attention to the new clef at an arbitrary point", where it may also be overlooked by the time the entry comes. Fix: `\clef bass` moved from the end of `tAVc` to the head of the 189 bar in `tBVc`, so it now prints before the barline of 189 (verified in a 200 dpi crop). The other four clef changes in the score (Hn 3·4 at 301 and 331, harp RH at 341 and 345, Vc at 169) already stand where PDF 22–23 asks.

Checked and deliberately kept, with the reasons the reading gives:

4. Cl 2's C♯3/D3 at 87–88, 285–287 and 331 and Hn 2's D♯3 at 285–288 use four ledger lines, one more than PDF 21 asks. Both are shared staves: a clef change would carry the first player with it, and the transposed parts read three ledger lines. Keep (as c185, c275).
5. Hn 3·4 at 73–88, 153–168 and 229–240 sit on three ledger lines below the treble stave for long stretches. PDF 21 explicitly prefers this to frequent clef changes, and the bass clef is used where the line really goes low (217–224, 301–320). Keep.
6. The harp's low F1/G1 (83, 158–163, 218–219) and the tuba's E1 (105–120) use four ledger lines under the bass clef, the lowest clef there is. Keep (PDF 21 does not offer an alternative; octave signs belong to the later pages of the chapter, settled in c185/c295).
7. The harp's harmonics at 375 use the harp's circle, not Gould's diamond. PDF 25 names only the piano as the exception, but harp harmonics are not covered by this excerpt and the circle is the harp's own convention. Keep (c026, c275).
8. The low-brass tenutos at the stem end (105–120, 333–335) sit far from the E1/E2 noteheads because PDF 28 extends those stems to the middle line. That placement (s125) is deliberate so they do not read as a ledger line. The longer stems of finding 1 do not touch them (no new collision; the build is clean). Keep.
