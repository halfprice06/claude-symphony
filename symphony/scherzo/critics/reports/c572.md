## Findings

### 1. Bar 33: the descending-5ths counterstatement loses its bass register on iv (applied)

**Problem.** The counterstatement at 29–40 is a descending-5ths sequence in 4-bar units: winds' call, then strings' answer. The roots are E (29–32, with V at 32), A (33–34), D (35–36) and G (37–39), then D7 (40–44) into G at 45. In G this is vi–ii–V–I (PDF 268–269). The basses mark the bass register at the start of each unit, with E1 at 29 under the call, B1 at 32 and D2 at 35, and with G1 at 37. At 33, though, the call over iv has only Bsn 2's A2. The basses rest at 33–34, so the bass register as heard runs E1 – B1 – (A2, an octave too high and only in a bassoon at pp) – D2 – G1. The first step of the series, the one that turns the tonic into a sequence, is the only one not in the bass register.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 267: "Normally ... all the voices above a sequential bass are themselves sequential". Units 1 and 3 begin with a bass-register root (cb E1 at 29, cb/vc G1 at 37). Unit 2 does not.
- A&S PDF 268, Ex. 17-3a: the bass of a descending-5ths series forms two interleaved stepwise lines (C–B–A–G against F–E–D–C), and "one of these two lines will usually predominate, with such factors as rhythm and register throwing it into relief". Here the lines are B–A–G (32, 33, 37) and E–D (29, 35). Without a low A, the B1–A1–G1 line has a hole in it, and neither line is brought forward.
- A&S PDF 267: a scalewise bass helps the sequence "fulfill its basic function", connecting two stable points (theme A's close at 28–29 and the G major of 45).

**Fix (applied, sections/sA.ily, sACb, bar 33).**
```
... e,,2.\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | b,,2. | a,,4-. r4 r4 | R2. | d,2.~ | d,2. | g,,4-.\p\< ...
(was ... | b,,2. | R2. | R2. | d,2.~ ...)
```
This is one arco staccato A1 crotchet at the prevailing pp. The basses are already arco from 31, and 37's G1s are arco staccato crotchets, so the stroke matches the next unit, and 29's single crotchet E1 sets the pattern. The cellos stay silent, so the call keeps its wind colour except for this one low stroke, as at 29. No pitch above the bass changes. The A1 doubles Bsn 2's A2 at the octave and moves B1→A1 by step from the held B1 of 32. Nothing moves in similar motion against it: the cellos' B2 at 32 and Vln I's B3 lead into rests. The bass line is now E1 – B1 – A1 – D2 – G1: the low stepwise line B–A–G is complete, and D2 is the interpolated step, as in Ex. 17-3a.

**The protection, read as a reason.** c302 and c482 protected "the descending-5ths counterstatement 29–37/29–44" because every Unit 17 rule held there. This fix leaves their harmony, melody and voicing as they were and only puts the series' second root in the register of the other three. The composer's note at sA.ily line 29 calls 29–30 and 33–34 string-free, but 29 already carries the basses' E1, so 33 now matches 29. The reprise counterstatement (269–276) has no iv, so it has no counterpart to change.

**Parts.** The basses play 31–32 and 35–36, so the part is not a long-resting entry and needs no cue. The basses part is not in parts-cues.json for these bars, and no cue elsewhere points at the basses in 29–36.

### Considered and not raised

- **33, Fl/Ob head E5–B4–B4–E5 over A minor.** In unit 2 the head keeps theme A's pitches (bar 17), so its quaver B4 is a 9th above the bass, leapt to and left by leap against Cl 1's A4. PDF 266 says a diatonic sequence changes its intervals to fit, which would give E5–C5–C5–E5. I left it because the quaver lasts about 0.11 s at 2. = 88 on a weak beat, and the E–B–B–E head is motive x's fingerprint. A rewrite would trade the motive's identity for the removal of a passing rub. c392 kept this unit's melody for the same reason (its G♯ over iv).
- **157–160, T's second phrase to the half cadence on V.** ♯4̂ (A♯) arrives only on beat 3 of 159, not on the second step as PDF 282 describes for 5-6 series moving to V. Phrase 2 is not a chordal 5-6 series, and an F♯-major chord at 158 would put A♯3 in the harp against Hn 3's drone B3, the pp cluster c469 removed at 163–164. It would also spend the A♯ that the 7–8 suspension into 160 saves. Left.
- **40–44, five bars on D.** The series already contains its II–V–I in G (33–37, PDF 268–269). The D7 bars are the key-defining progression PDF 284 asks for after a sequence rising from III, and they carry the head on V/III (the fourth head entry, about which PDF 265 says more than three statements can be tedious unless the pattern is short). It is a 4-bar unit with a different chord and the call/answer layout, so it does not read as a fourth statement. Left.
- **81–84, the falling-3rds sequence in F.** Its bass falls by 3rds each bar (D–B♭–G) while Cl 1's head falls a 3rd every two bars, so melody and bass units differ. It is inside the protected F-major episode (finale link), and PDF 279's root-position variant allows the disjunct bass. Left.
