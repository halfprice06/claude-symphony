## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 305–318 (reprise tutti): an open fifth on the downbeat of 307, and Hn 4 in octaves with the tune. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In the reprise tutti there are no trombones (c114/c266), and Hn 4 doubles the bass an octave up. At 107 the third G3 on the downbeat comes from Tbn 1 alone. At 307 nobody takes it: sixteen players attack E and B and nothing else. The tenor countermelody (Hn 3, Bsn 1, cellos) is holding its tied E4, and Hn 4 has E3. The G does not arrive until the violas on beat 2. This is the only downbeat of the 32 tutti bars (105–120, 305–320) without a third. Hn 4's bass doubling also moves in octaves with the tune at three bar lines: F♯3→E3 against Cl 2's F♯4→E4 at 304→305; B2→E3 against the tune's B4→E5 at 311→312 (the real bass falls B→E, so only the horn's octave rises with the tune); and E3→G3 against E5→G5 at 317→318. At 308→309 the held B2 meets the tune's beat-3 B4 and rises with it to E. At 309→310 the held E3 meets the tune's E5 and moves against it to A (contrary octaves).
**Evidence.** PDF 76: "the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects"; review point 4, PDF 89. PDF 83, Ex. 5-18, parallel octaves "wrong". PDF 86, Ex. 5-27: contrary octaves "best avoided" except at the end of a phrase.
**Fix** (dC.ily):
```
dCHnFour = { g2.( | e2. | g2. | b,2) r4 | e2 r4 | a,2.( | b,2. | g2) r4 | fis2.( | g2. | a,2. | b,2.) \once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN \breathe | g2.( | g2. | b,2. | e4) r4 r4 | R2.*4 | }
(was  e2.( | e2. | e2. | b,2. | e2.) \breathe | a,2.( | b,2. | e2) r4 | … | e2.( | g2. | b,2. | e4) …)
```
Hn 4 takes G3 at 305, 307, 312 and 317. That is the tenor line's own downbeat note, and what Hn 4 plays at 105–120. At 307 it supplies the missing third. The rests on beat 3 of 308 and 309 replace the breath c226 put after 309. c114's reason ("the octave above the bass filled in every tutti") still holds, because Bsn 2 holds E2/B2 under every one of these downbeats. The only leap the change adds is B2→G3 at 311→312, which is followed by a step down to F♯3 (PDF 81, Ex. 5-11b). The engraved Hn 3·4 staff shows the unisons as two heads where the durations differ, and no new labels.

### 2. 109→110, 117→118 and 309→310, 317→318: Bsn 2 holds E under the tune's beat-3 E and moves with it to the next root. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In bars 5 and 13 of each tutti statement of theme A, motive x ends on E on beat 3, and the tune then leaps to the root of the next chord (E→A at 110/310, E→G at 118/318). The bass takes the same root. The contrabasses, contrabassoon, bass trombone and tuba play crotchets on beat 1, so they are silent when the tune's E sounds. Bsn 2, however, holds E2 for the whole bar and then moves E2→A2 or E2→G2 in octaves with the tune. Bsn 2 already stops on beat 3 of bar 8 (112/312, c066's breath) for the same musical reason.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18. PDF 87, rule 5 for hidden and direct octaves (most noticeable in the outer voices, and Bsn 2 is the bass's sustained octave). PDF 84: an octave doubling is not a parallel, but Bsn 2 doubles the bass, not the tune.
**Fix** (sC.ily, dC.ily):
```
sCBsnTwo bars 109 and 117:  e,2 r4      (was e,2.)
dCBsnTwo bars 309 and 317:  e,2 r4      (was e,2.)
```
The downbeat chord and its bass octave are unchanged. Bsn 2 gains two short breaths in 16 bars of ff. The bassoon staff prints a crotchet rest with no solo label, as at 112. After findings 1 and 2, par3.py finds no octaves between the tune and any bass-register line in 105–120 or 305–320. The only remaining hits are the contrary B→E octaves at the four-bar phrase ends 108→109, 111→112, 308→309 and 311→312 (PDF 86–87, Ex. 5-27a).

### 3. 330→331 (coda): cellos and Vln I move A→F in octaves into the Neapolitan 6/4. APPLIED.
**Problem.** In the coda's stretto the cellos play motive x on each chord. At 330 the cellos' A2 sounds with Vln I's A5. At 331 both leap up a minor sixth to F (cellos F3, Vln I F6). These are two exposed string lines at the coda's harmonic turn (♭II 6/4 over C, c242/c332), and they move in parallel octaves. Elsewhere the two lines are independent: E3/G5 at 329 and B2/D♯6 at 332.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-18: "one part duplicates the pitch and motion of the other in a different register".
**Fix** (coda.ily):
```
codaVc bar 331:  a,4-. f,8-. f,-. c4-.     (was f4-. a,8-. a,-. c4-.)
```
The cellos keep A2 as a common tone and fall a third to F2, which is the shape of their motive x at 330 (A2–F♯2). They then rise to C3 for the French sixth's bass on beat 3 (c332). F stays in Cl 1, Hn 3 and both violin sections on beats 1–2, so the IV6/4 link to finale IV 272 is untouched. The cellos stay above the basses' C2.

### 4. 353 (coda T): the cadential 6/4 has no sixth on beats 2–3. APPLIED.
**Problem.** At 353 the trio tune arpeggiates G♯–B–E over the cellos' B2, which is the start of the two-bar cadential 6/4 (themes-scherzo.md, since v44). The violas hold E4 alone. So after the tune's G♯ on beat 1 the sonority is only B and E. In the trio's own statements the sixth is sustained: harp G♯3 at 165, and Hn 2 G♯4 held through 221.
**Evidence.** PDF 76: "the best vertical sonority is achieved when … all the tones of a triad are present", and the 3rd is never omitted.
**Fix** (coda.ily):
```
codaVla bars 353–354:  <gis e'>2.~^\markup \italic "div." | <gis e'>2. |     (was e'2. | <gis e'>2.^\markup \italic "div." |)
```
The lower violas take G♯3 a bar early and tie it into 354, where they already play it. It then goes to A3 (the seventh at 355) and back to G♯3 as before. c092's protected voicing at 354–355 is unchanged.
