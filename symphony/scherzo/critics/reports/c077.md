## Findings

### 1. The retransition 89–100: all three head entries are in the strings (applied)
**Bars:** 89–97.
**Problem:** The entries go viola (89), Vln II (92), Vln I (95). All three are staccato strings over pizzicato B and the timpani. The winds have just finished a 16-bar winds-only episode (73–88) and stay silent until 101. So the development is made of single-choir blocks: strings 57–72, winds 73–88, strings 89–100. Inside the blocks nothing passes between the groups.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 253–254 (Eroica 45–57) passes a short gesture between oboe, clarinet, flute and violins before the tutti sums it up. PDF 255 says that the third time the same orchestration comes back, something must change. PDF 271–272 (Mozart 38/iii) shows winds imitating over pizzicato strings, which is this passage's texture. The model the score names, Beethoven 9/ii 177–195, gives these entries to bassoons, oboes, clarinets and flutes (see above).
**Fix (applied):** Ob 1 takes the second entry at Vln II's pitch, pp. Vln II rests 92–94 and comes back with its pp crotchets at 95. The entries now go viola → oboe → violins, so each one has a different colour and the strings lead into the hemiola at 101. Ob 1 then rests 95–100 before its dis'' at 101.
```

e''4-.\pp b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | a''2->( gis''8 g''8 | fis''4-.) r4 r4 |
% sBVlnTwo bars 36-38 (92-94) become R2. | R2. | R2. |
```
The part-combiner prints "1." by itself, so no markup is needed. The recorded statements (Vla 89, Vln I 95) are unchanged. Vln II 92 was not in the record. The reprise retransition 241–252 keeps its all-string entries, so the two retransitions now differ.

### 2. Coda 345–348: the trio tune's recall has no counter-line (applied)
**Bars:** 341–348.
**Problem:** In the trio, tune T always has a second line under it: the cellos' countermelody at 169–176, and again at 209. In the coda recall, Hn 1 (341–344) and Ob 1 (345–348) play it over harp off-beats, pizzicato bass and a viola line that only alternates A and B. The coda's statement is the only full-length one with no inner line. Under Hn 1 (C♯4–A4) there is no room for one; under Ob 1 (D5–B5) there is.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 271 says each wind's own colour makes a contrapuntal line clear. PDF 267 (Brahms 1/ii) says a single bassoon line adds warmth and makes the melody stand out. PDF 255 says a returning passage should not come back unchanged: here the counter-line returns in a new colour. The harmony of 345–348 (D, Bm, B7, E over d, b, b, e) is the trio's 157–160/173–176 a fourth higher, so the cellos' line from tA 173–176 fits note for note.
**Fix (applied):** Bsn 1, p dolce, plays the trio's cello countermelody a fourth higher. Bsn 1 rests 341–344 and 349–356, and the protected entry at 357 is unchanged.
```
% coda.ily, codaBsnOne bars 21-24 (345-348), were R2.
\partCombineSoloI a4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" fis2~ | fis4 d2) | dis2( e4 | gis2 b4) \partCombineAutomatic |
```
I checked the voice leading. There are no consecutive fifths or octaves with the bass or with Ob 1; the 5th at 346 comes after a 3rd. The D♯ at 347 completes the B7 that the harp's <fis a b> implies, and the line ends on G♯–B under Ob 1's D♯–E.

### 3. Close of the exposition 133–140: the cellos' last echo duplicates the timpani (applied)
**Bars:** 133–140.
**Problem:** Motive x dies away in the cellos at 133 (f), 135 (mf) and 137 (p). At 135 the timpani play the same rhythm on E at the same time, so the one echo that could change colour is just doubled. Elsewhere in the movement the timpani answer the strings (20, 28; the intro 9–12).
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 253–254 passes the gesture from one group to another. PDF 261 keeps one colour for one recurring gesture, and motive x is the timpani's figure.
**Fix (applied):** the cellos rest at 135 and the timpani answer alone at mf (was p). The order is now cellos f, timpani mf, cellos p, then both pp at 139.
```
% sC.ily, sCVc bar 31 (135): R2.     sCTimp bar 31: e,4\mf e,8 e,8 e,4
```
The basses keep their single pizzicato E at 135.
