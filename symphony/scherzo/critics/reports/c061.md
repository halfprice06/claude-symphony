## Findings (ranked)

### 1. T's cadence, 165–167, 181–183, 221–223 (and 354–355): no cadential suspension (applied; theme change)

**Problem.** T's last phrase runs gis'4 b' e'' | dis''2 fis''4 | e''2. over I6/4 – V7 – I. The leading tone D♯ arrives on the downbeat of the V7 bar with nothing before it. Bars 164–166 are a run of even crotchets ending in that minim: the cadence is the plainest formula available, and the tune's last phrase is the one that should stay in the ear. Across its fifteen bars T never ties a note over the barline, so its rhythm repeats phrase after phrase (cycle 1 "open"; c051 "decided against"). The cadence is the one place where a single tie changes that without touching the contour.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 350, sec. 8: "One of the most important compositional uses of the suspension is to enhance the effect of a cadence by emphasizing the leading tone. Frequently the cadential suspension takes the form of a simple 4-3 above V" (Ex. 21-8). PDF 346, sec. 1: the suspension animates and individualizes a line, and in it "rhythm rather than melody assumes paramount importance". PDF 348: "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer", which is the lean-and-release a dolce trio cadence needs. The E is prepared: it is the 4th of the cadential 6/4 on beat 3 of 165 (PDF 347, preparation metrically free; PDF 351–352, Ex. 21-11b, the 6/4 leading into the 4-3). It is metrically strong against its resolution (PDF 347) and resolves down by step (PDF 348). On the downbeat of 166, 182 and 222 no other part sounds D♯: harp and violas enter on beat 2, and cellos, bassoon and horns have A, B and F♯. So the resolution tone is not anticipated (PDF 352, sec. 12). The suspension sits above A and B, the 7/4-3 into a seventh chord of Ex. 21-15a (PDF 354). In Dvořák 9/iii, 68–76, the tune's notes cross the barline in the same way.

**Fix (applied).** In every full statement of T, tie the E into the V7 bar:
```
% tA Cl 1 165-166, Vln II 181-182; tC Cl 1, Vln II 221-222:
gis'4\!( b'4 e''4~ | e''4 dis''4 fis''4) | e''2.~ |
% tA Vln I 181-182; tC Fl 1, Vln I 221-222 (an octave up):
gis''4\!( b''4 e'''4~ | e'''4 dis'''4 fis'''4) | e'''2.~ |
% tA Fl 1 181-182: the heterophony moves its turn to beat 2 (it had D sharp 6 under the violins' E6)
gis''4\!( b'' e'''~ | e'''4 dis'''8 e''' fis'''4) | e'''2.~ |
% coda Vln I 354-355 (Vln II and Cl 1 an octave lower):
gis'''2-- fis'''8 e'''~ | e'''4 dis''' fis''') | e'''2 r4 |
```
In the coda, the quaver E at the end of c043's 6-5-4 descent is now held into the V7 bar. This is a delayed resolution (PDF 359–360, sec. 21). The movement's last cadence becomes the trio's new cadence enlarged by the two-bar 6/4. It still differs from the trio's cadences, as c043 wanted. The violas' A3–F♯4 at 355 contain no D♯. The cadence keeps its harmony, bass and bar count.

### 2. Bars 195–196: the string build repeats T's rhythm into a bare V7 (applied)

**Problem.** Before the trio's forte (197), Vln I and Vln II climb fis''2 gis''4 | a''2 cis'''4 | b''4. a''8 gis''4 | fis''2. under a crescendo. That is T's rhythm again (2–4 | 2–4 | 4.–8–4 | 2.), the pattern c021 set out to stop repeating in the trio's middle part. The phrase ends on F♯, the plain 5th of the V7 (bass B, Cl 1 A, Cl 2 D♯), on the downbeat before the deceptive arrival at 197. The crescendo peaks on a consonance.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 353–354, sec. 15: the 7/6 "occurs rather frequently, especially in nineteenth-century music", as a delay over V7, and in the Josef Strauss waltz (Ex. 21-15f) "the 7/6 forms a beautiful suspension resolving into V7". It is the Romantic form of the device, and right for this style. PDF 348: suspensions bring the voice that carries them into prominence. Here that is the top line at the height of a crescendo. The G♯ is prepared as a consonance (the 3rd of I6 at 195, bass G♯; PDF 347). It is strong against its resolution and falls by step.

**Fix (applied).** Tie G♯ over the barline in both violin parts. Bsn 1, which had F♯3 on the downbeat of 196 (the resolution tone in advance, PDF 352, sec. 12), ties with them and keeps its breath on beat 3:
```
tBVlnOne 195-196: b''4.( a''8 gis''4~ | gis''4 fis''2) |
tBVlnTwo 195-196: b'4.( a'8 gis'4~ | gis'4 fis'2) |
tBBsnOne 195-196: gis2.~ | gis4( fis4) r4 |
```
The 7th A4 stays in Cl 1 and still falls to G♯ at 197 (c032). Vln II's G♯4 against Cl 1's A4 on one downbeat is the 7/6 itself, and it resolves at once. It is not the held clash that c039 removed at 197–200. Bars 193–196 are not a recorded theme statement.

## Decided against

- **Second idea 45–52, tied notes in octaves with the bass (rows 4, 19).** Each tied note in the G statement (B, E, C♯, F♯) doubles the bass (c032's "5-8-8 | 10-8-10"). These are consonant suspensions, which need no resolution (PDF 348). The hemiola gives them their accent (PDF 362–363). The E-minor statements (121, 277) already sit in 3rds and 6ths. A new bass would undo c032's sequence and c027's "keep homorhythmic second idea".
- **T's first cadence 155–156 as a suspension with dissonant preparation (row 15, Ex. 21-23a).** dis''4. cis''8 b'8 a'8~ | a'4 gis'2 would hold the V7's 7th as a 4-3 over I. I kept the struck appoggiatura A. It is stronger than a tied A, the extra quavers would blur the 6-5-4-3 echo of the motto tail, and one tied figure in the tune (finding 1) keeps that figure distinct. With this choice, phrase 1 ends on a 4-3 appoggiatura over I, phrase 2 on a 7-8 appoggiatura to V, and phrase 4 on a prepared 4-3 over V.
- **Cellos' G♯3 under the violins' A at 172 and 212 (row 10).** The resolution tone is two octaves lower, in an inner contrapuntal line with its own rhythm: this is the exception on PDF 352. Hn 2's G♯4 in the same octave was the real problem, and c054 has already fixed it.
