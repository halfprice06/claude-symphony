## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Cor anglais, bar 101: an entry after 56 bars' rest with no cue (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** C.a. plays 41–44 (cue at 41–43, plays from 44), then rests 45–100 and enters f at 101 with the hemiola build (b'2-> a'4~ …, written f♯''2 e''4~).
- **Problem:** c075 set the rule for this set of parts: a cue at every entry after 40 or more bars' rest, and c165 completed it. This is the only real entry in the 30 parts that breaks the rule. The list of entries after more than 30 whole notes' rest shows every other one with a cue. The player counts 56 bars, across the F-major key change (73), the poco rit. (87–88), the a tempo with ritmo di tre battute (89) and the change to ritmo di quattro battute at 101 itself, and then comes in on a loud tied minim in the middle of a phrase.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest, the cues being transposed to the reading key of the instrument. Cues must be audible and obvious." Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows the cue in the bars just before the entry. Adler PDF 299 item 6 says the same. The trumpets already carry this very cue (Vln I 97–100, parts-cues "16"/"17"), so it is audible. In the C.a.'s key it reads b''–e''' (at most three ledger lines), within the set's ledger limit.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-255/work):** parts-cues.json, "05": insert `["cueVlnOne","Vln. I","UP",97,4]` between the 41 and 185 cues. Checked in the PDF: cue at 97–100, the rest before it reads "89–96 8". The C.a.'s only page turn moves from 324/325 to 288/289 and now falls in 24 bars' rest (277–300), where before it had 8 (325–332). Two pages as before.

### 2. Upper strings at 169 and timpani at 89 enter after a tempo change in a long rest with no cue (applied, parts only)
- **Bars:** Vln I, Vln II and Vla rest 133–168 (36–37 bars) and enter mp/pp at L (169) with T in octaves and the divisi off-beats. Before that come the link, the poco rit. 149–152, the key change to E major and the Trio's new tempo (2. = 76). The timpani rest 56–88 (33 bars), through the F-major episode and the poco rit. 87–88, and then enter pp alone at F (89, a tempo, ritmo di tre battute), the solo that starts the retransition.
- **Problem:** In both cases the player has not played in the new tempo, or through the rit., and enters on an exposed line. In the strings' case the entry takes over the tune from Cl 1 in the Trio's tempo, which they have not heard while counting. The timpani's case is a solo pp entry on the first beat after a rit. that the player can only count. Neither part shows what is sounding.
- **Evidence:** MOLA Ex. 4 (PDF 10) is this case: a long rest broken by a tempo change ("Etwas bewegter", "Tempo I"), with a "Viol. I" cue right after the tempo change and just before the entry. MOLA PDF 5: cues "audible and obvious". At 165–168 the audible line is Cl 1's cadence of T (gis'4 b' e''~ | e'' dis'' fis'' | e''2.~ | e''2). At 85–88 it is Cl 1's dolce solo into the poco rit. (a'4 f'8 f' a' | c''2 b'8 bes' | a'4 fis'8 fis' dis' | e'2.). At pitch that solo would sit four or five ledger lines above the timpani's bass stave, so it is quoted an octave lower, as c165 did for "cueVlnOneLow". Adler PDF 300 item 7 says to choose the cued line by its register.
- **Fix (applied):**
  - parts-gen.py, after `\addQuote "cueVlnOneLow" …`: `\addQuote "cueClOneLow" { \transpose c' c \clOne }`.
  - parts-cues.json: `"23":[["cueClOneLow","Cl. 1 (8vb)","UP",85,4]]`; `"26"`, `"27"`, `"28"`: `[["cueClOne","Cl. 1","UP",165,4]]`.
  - Checked: the cues sit on or next to the stave (the viola reads up to F♯5 on the third ledger line), and the audit adds no ledger-line warning. The strings' turn stays at 152/153 and their pages 2–3 face each other. The timpani's turn moves from 267/268 (7 bars' rest) to 284/285 (16 bars' rest, 269–284). Page counts are unchanged.

### 3. The "muta" texts at bar 376 float into the system above in the Cl 1, Cl 2 and timpani parts (applied, parts only; score unchanged)
- **Bars:** 376, the last bar, where Cl 1·2 have "muta in B♭ (for the Finale)" and the timpani have the three-line retuning for the finale.
- **Problem:** On the ragged last page the systems sit at their basic distance. The text is stacked above the fermata on the last bar's rest, so it lands nearer the system above than its own bar. In the Cl 1 part (150 dpi) "muta in B♭ / (for the Finale)" reads as hanging under bar 354, at the right end of the 339 system, beside the "rit.". Bar 354 is in the middle of Cl 1's dolce T statement, and the player still needs the A clarinet at 361–364. In the timpani part the retuning text reads as belonging to the 357–362 rest of the system above. The instruction is correct, but its position tells the player the wrong place.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 8: "simply extracting parts from the score in notation software will not lead to error-free parts … the placement of any 'anchored' markings may not remain positioned as they were in the score, leading to collisions or floating markings" (Ex. 7, PDF 12). MOLA PDF 5 says a doubling change needs "clear indications of when to change".
- **Fix (applied):** coda.ily: the last bar of codaClOne becomes `R2.-\tag #'score ^\markup \small \italic \right-column { "muta in B♭" "(for the Finale)" } -\tag #'part _\markup \small \italic \right-column { "muta in B♭" "(for the Finale)" }`. In codaClTwo, the part-only text changes from `^\markup` to `_\markup`. In codaTimp, the muta gets the same `-\tag #'score ^… -\tag #'part _…` pair. score.ly, clarinet staff: `\removeWithTag #'part \clOne` (Cl 1 had no 'part tags before, so nothing else changes). Below the last system there is nothing to collide with. Checked in all three parts: the text now hangs under the fermata bar. Score pages 1–13 are pixel-identical to v255's.
