## Findings

### 1. Bar 226: Ob 1's E6, the one soft exposed wind note at the top of Adler's nonprofessional column. Built, tested, not kept.

**The candidate.** Since c501 the codetta's oboe statement of T's head is G♯5–A5 | B5–E6 | C♯6 (p dolce, < to mp). The E6 is on the third ledger line, exactly the top of the oboe's nonprofessional column (PDF 304), and the held C♯6 follows. Every other note above or at that column is loud or doubled; this one is a soft solo over the E pedal, so it is the note where a change of instrument or register would be heard most.

**What I built.** In /tmp/critic-516/work, sections/tC.ily: Fl 1 takes 225–228 at pitch (`gis''2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a''4 | b''2\< e'''4 | cis'''2.-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | b''2-\tweak stencil ##f \p) r4`) and Ob 1 rests 209–240. Build: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, no new outer-voice parallel. No parts-cues.json entry points at Ob 1 or Fl 1 in these bars.

**Why it makes the music worse.** (a) Adler names this register as the oboe's place for "a thin, softly piercing effect … sometimes most beautifully scored" (adler1 PDF 198), which is what "dolce" over a pedal asks for; the flute instead "gets more brilliant as it goes up" (same page). (b) Fl 1 has just played the trio's climax in the same octave (217–223, c069, c479). With the flute at 225 the codetta starts in the colour that ended the tune, two beats later, and the codetta loses its first new colour. (c) The codetta's plan is three reeds, each an octave lower than the last (Ob 1 G♯5 at 225, Cl 1 G♯4 at 229, Bsn 1 G♯3 at 233; c022, c501). (d) The E6 is inside the nonprofessional column, so the reading gives no fault to correct. Moving the oboe down an octave would double Cl 1's statement at 229, and lowering only the E would break T's head G♯–A–B–E, which movement I quotes. I reverted it.

### 2. Bars 205–216: Hn 1 plays twelve bars with no marked breath. No change.

Hn 1 plays the motto-tail preview 205–208 (p espress., slur closed at 208) and then the trio's return 209–216 (mp, held C♯5 213–216) before c176's breath at 216: about 9.5 s at 2. = 76. A `\breathe` at the end of 208 would be the obvious place, but there Hn 1's A4 resolves to G♯4 on the downbeat of 209, so B–A♯–A–G♯ is the motto's chromatic tail completed in one horn (c050, c390). The span is soft and in the middle of the horn's register, well short of what Adler's ch. 10 treats as an endurance problem. Do not add a breath mark at 208.
