## Other sources

The MCTL form annotation (musicxml-converted/annotations/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt3/form/form_analysis.csv) gives these shares of the movement: scherzo 40% (0–671), trio 24% (703–1108), shortened return 25% (1107–1527), coda 9% (1530–1684). At the printed tempi, v33 divides its time as scherzo 1–140 about 35%, link 3%, trio 25%, retransition 4%, reprise 18%, coda 16%. The reprise is shorter and the coda longer than Dvořák's, but both stay inside the norm for a written-out, shortened da capo with a two-stage coda.

At 4:35 the movement sits correctly between the Adagio and the finale. Earlier form critics (cycles 3, 13, 23) already shortened the trio middle, the retransition and the reprise close. I propose no further cut and no added repeat. The pacing problems I found are in how the sections are scored and approached, not in their lengths.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The reprise uses the coda's full brass, so the coda's climax is not fresh (301–320; applied)
**Problem.** Trombones play 101–120, 301–320 and 333–339; the tuba plays 105–120, 305–320 and 333–339. The reprise tutti (301–320) is a step towards the coda, yet it uses the same full brass, with the low brass doubling the downbeats, as both the first tutti and the climax. The low brass then rest only 12 bars (321–332) before the coda's fff peak.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 136: the ear cannot bear the full orchestra for long, and "a group of instruments which has been silent for some time gains fresh interest upon its reappearance. The trombones, trumpets and tuba are occasionally tacet for long periods." PDF 116: the scoring depends on "whether it forms a climax or merely a step". PDF 119: a partial tutti (horns, or horns with trumpets) is still a tutti. Dvořák 9/iii has no trombones or tuba at all.
**Fix (applied).** dB.ily: `dBTbnOne = { R2.*16 | }`, and the same for `dBTbnTwo` and `dBBassTbn`. dC.ily: `dCTbnOne = { R2.*20 | }`, and the same for `dCTbnTwo`, `dCBassTbn` and `dCTuba`. The reprise tutti becomes horns, trumpets, timpani, woodwinds and strings. The contrabassoon and double basses keep the accented downbeats. The hemiola chord at 304 stays complete in the horns, trumpets and woodwinds. The low brass now rest from 121 to 332, 212 bars, and return ff at 333, as PDF 136 asks. The empty staves are hidden by the layout, and the score stays at 13 pages.

### 2. The crescendo into the coda falls in every part instead of opening outwards (329–332; applied)
**Problem.** Over the bass E–D–C–B (329–332), Vln I plays G5–F♯5–E5–D♯5 and Cl 1 plays G4–F♯4–E4–D♯4. During a crescendo from mf to ff, the whole texture sinks with the bass. Vln I then leaps a ninth from D♯5 to the ff E6 at 333.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 131: in a diverging progression "the three upper parts" ascend while "the bass descend[s]", "the intermediate intervals are filled up by the introduction of fresh parts as the distance widens", and such progressions "sometimes involve an increase … in tone." In this score the fresh parts already exist: Fl, Ob, horns, trumpets and trombones all enter at 333.
**Fix (applied).** coda.ily `codaVlnOne`, bars 330–332: `a''4-. fis''8-. fis''-. a''4-. | c'''4-. a''8-. a''-. c'''4-. | dis'''4-.\f b''8-. b''-. r4 |` (was `fis'' d'' / e'' c'' / dis'' b'`). Vln I now rises G5–A5–C6–D♯6 against the falling bass, mostly in contrary motion, and its D♯6 resolves by step into E6 at 333. Cl 1 keeps c032's tenths with the bass, so this keeps c032's aim (no doubling of the bass) and changes only Vln I's direction. No new parallels and no C-major triad: the iv6 at 331 is C–E–A with C on top.

### 3. The trio's main return is dropped in at forte straight after pp (205–209; applied)
**Problem.** 201–208 dies away over the B pedal: Fl 1 and Cl 1 play the motto tail pp, and the timpani roll pp. Then T returns f at 209 with no crescendo. This is the trio's main formal arrival, and it sounds like a jump cut rather than a goal.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 127 (amplification): the second group "enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense." PDF 130: short crescendi come from natural dynamics. The timpani roll on the dominant is exactly the part that should carry the crescendo.
**Fix (applied).** tB.ily: `\<` from 207 in Fl 1 (`b''2\< ais''4`), Cl 1 (`b'2\< ais'4`), Cl 2, Hn 3·4, Vla, Vc and Cb, and timpani `b,2.:32\< | b,4\mf r4 r4`. The hairpins resolve into the f, mf and p already marked at 209. Parts that rest at 209 close the hairpin on the rest: tC.ily `tCClTwo = { R2.\! | R2.*15 | …`, `tCHnThree`/`tCHnFour = { R2.\! | R2.*23 | …`. The motto tail B–A♯–A now leads straight into T's first G♯ under the crescendo. Shared staves carry equal hairpins.

### 4. The trio's forte statement repeats the soft statement's bass (209–224; applied)
**Problem.** The third statement of T (209, f espressivo) has the same harmony and bass scoring as 169 (mp): pizzicato double basses and the harp's plucked downbeat, with the cellos busy on their countermelody. The forte climax of the trio has only a plucked bass.
**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 117–118: "one idea will be orchestrated in a different way every time it recurs", by (c) extension of range or (d) alteration of details. PDF 121 notes that pizzicato basses serve to make sustained winds distinct; they are not a sustained support.
**Fix (applied).** tC.ily `tCCb`: `e,2.\mf^\markup \italic "arco" | e,2. | b,,2. | e,2. | a,,2. | fis,,2. | fis,,2. | b,,2 fis,,4 | cis,2. | gis,,2. | fis,,2. | a,,2. | b,,2. | b,,2. | e,2.\> | e,2 r4\! | e,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz." …`. The basses have two beats to take the bow at 209 and one beat to change back to pizz. at 225. The F♯ on beat 3 of 216 stops the sustained bass from moving B–C♯ in octaves with T's B5–C♯6 into 217. An arco-only version made the analyser flag octaves at 217; this version is clean. The harp keeps the plucked downbeat, so the trio's colour stays.
