## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Applied: "1." printed twice on the Hn 1·2 staff at 357 (letter W, the motto tail)
- **Bars:** 357 (page 13, top system).
- **Problem:** two "1." labels are stacked over Hn 1's B4 at the motto's chromatic tail. This is the most exposed horn entry of the movement and the one the conductor reads most closely. A label-position scan of all 13 pages finds no other doubled label.
- **Cause:** c488 (v489) moved the closing E4 at 361–364 from Hn 2 to Hn 4, so Hn 2 now rests from 340 to the end. The part combiner sees a solo from 357 and prints its own "1.". The hand-written `^\markup \bold "1."` that c060/c135 had added for the old layout is still there. The v488 page shows one label, the v489 page shows two.
- **Evidence:** MOLA Ex. 6 (PDF 11) prints one part label per entry on each shared staff. MOLA PDF 8 says that "anchored" markings shifted by the software must not collide or float, and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) shows exactly that fault. c225 removed the same kind of duplicate at 193.
- **Fix (applied in coda.ily):** in `codaHnOne`, `b'2.(\p-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."\<-\tweak outside-staff-horizontal-padding #1 ^\markup \italic "espress." |` becomes `b'2.(\p\<-\tweak outside-staff-horizontal-padding #1 ^\markup \italic "espress." |`, with a c495 comment line. One "1." now prints, from the combiner, under "espress." and box W. At 300 dpi the slur and the p< hairpin are clear of each other. The change is to the print only: score.midi is byte-identical to v495's, and the Hn 1 part is unchanged because the label was tagged `'score`.

### 2. Best audible candidate, built and rejected: Hn 3 mp with the cellos at 169–176
- **Bars:** 169–176 (letter L, T's second statement: cellos mp arco espress., Hn 3 p dolce in unison).
- **Why it is the candidate:** this reading supports no change to notes. The only principle in it that affects sound is Ex. 6's bracketed "[sf]", which gives every part playing a figure the same mark, together with PDF 8's vertical proofreading. My scan of the movement (F3) found one place where two instruments play the same line in unison, not in octaves, and the second is neither a descant nor brass held back by Dan's rule: Hn 3 doubles the cellos' T note for note at 169–176 one level under them.
- **Test:** in /tmp/critic-495/work2/sections/tA.ily, Hn 3 starts mp and its hidden hairpin levels match the cellos' (mp, mf, p, mp, mf, mp, mf). The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, cmajor "none", same parallels, themes 52/52.
- **Why it makes the music worse:** a horn at the same printed level as a cello section in the middle register covers it, as Adler's balance tables and c431's "horn melodies stay soft and middle-register" both say. The second statement would turn from a cello tune with horn colour into a horn tune, and it would anticipate the coda, where T is a horn solo at p dolce (Hn 3, 341). The level difference is a balance instruction between two different instruments. Ex. 6's brackets correct a missing mark in parts of one tutti figure, which is a different case. It also follows the same convention as c405's do-not-touch (winds doubling a string tune print a level under it), and the reading gives no stronger reason against that. Not kept: /tmp/critic-495/work does not contain it.
