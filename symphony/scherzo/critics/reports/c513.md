## Findings

### 1. Bars 169–176: Hn 3 doubles the cellos' T in unison straight out of its 20-bar drone; Hn 3 rests (applied)

**Problem.** T's first statement (153–168) is Cl 1 over the harp, the pizz. bass and the Hn 3·4 drone, which has sounded since 149. The second statement (169–184) is meant to be T in a radically different colour: the strings, which have been silent since 141 apart from the pizz. bass (c344: "T in the cellos alone"; c074: "the strings' own"). But Hn 3 leaves the drone on beat 3 of 168 and enters on 169 in unison with the cellos (c431). So the horn colour that held the background for 20 bars carries straight into the new statement, and the cellos' first T is a horn-cello blend, not a fresh string sound. This is the join a listener hears most clearly in the trio, because it is the moment the tune changes hands.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 257–258 (Beethoven Romance, Ex. XVII-1): the tune is "immediately repeated in a radically different orchestral color"; Beethoven even has the strings play pizzicato "to add greater variety and to avoid duplication of the solo sound". This "aids in a clearer exposition of the form."
- PDF 263–264 (Tchaikovsky VC ii, Dvořák Vc Concerto ii): a contrasting colour comes first, so "the solo color is therefore very fresh". At 153–168 the contrasting colour is winds, harp and horns. Carrying one of those horns into 169 undoes the contrast.
- PDF 189: at-pitch doublings "change the basic tonal quality or characteristic of the resulting sound, and this should be seriously considered". PDF 264 (Mozart Horn Concerto): a doubling does not weaken the soloist when the colours are vastly different. Horn and cello at the same pitch are the opposite: they blend (that blend was c431's reason, Adler II PDF 27).
- PDF 279 (Bizet): the added doubling is saved for the climax. With Hn 3 gone, the statement's one added doubling is Vln I's octave from 177, at the climb to A5.
- c436 recorded that 169–184 is full and that "later critics should thin rather than add there."

**Fix (applied).** tA.ily: `tAHnThree` bars 169–184 `R2.*16` (was `\partCombineSoloI gis2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" a4 | b2 e'4) | … | ais4 b2)\! | \partCombineAutomatic R2.*8`); `tAHnFour` bars 169–184 `R2.*16` (the `\partCombineSoloI`/`\partCombineAutomatic` pair is no longer needed). The cellos keep mp espress., Vln II con sord. pp, arco basses pp, Bsn 2 on the bass, Fl 1's answers at 172/176. Hn 3 now rests 169–200 and comes back pp at 201. That is a 32-bar rest, so I added a cue to its part: parts-cues.json "14" gains `["cueFlOne","Fl. 1","UP",197,4]` (the forte call, which sounds at 197–200). No other cue points at Hn 3. This also takes away 28 continuous bars of playing from Hn 3 (149–176). The analyser profile at 169–176 drops from 4.6 to 3.6 voices; top and bass are unchanged.

**Does it undo an earlier fix?** Yes, c431. Its reason was Adler's brass chapter: horn and cello double well on long melodies. That reason is true, but it describes a blend. The reading here puts the clear change of colour at the trio's second statement first, and c344/c074 planned that statement for the strings. c495's rejection (Hn 3 mp at 169–176) was about raising the level, not about having the horn there, so it does not apply. The reprise's Hn 3 with the cellos at 261–268 (c447/c449) is a different case: there the horn adds to a countermelody in the second phrase. It is not a solo colour returning, so I left it alone.

### 2. Reprise 253–268: Fl 1's heterophony in both phrases (Haydn, PDF 288): built in principle, rejected

The strongest Haydn-type option is for Fl 1 to rest at 261–268 so the second phrase changes doubling, with the flutes' a2 at 273 entering fresher (PDF 267). It would make the music worse. The second phrase is where theme A climbs to its protected C6 at 264. Since c449 the texture grows phrase by phrase there (cellos, then + Hn 3 at 261). Taking the flute out would thin the top just where the line rises, against that growth and against the Liszt model (PDF 273–274), whose heterophonic flute runs throughout. c243 and c423 reached the same verdict on different grounds. Kept.

### 3. Bar 341: T's recall starts together with its background after the G.P.: kept

Adler's Sibelius, Tchaikovsky and Bartók examples (PDF 263–272) set the background first. Here that needs an added bar before 341. Stage 1 (325–340, with the G.P.) and the recall (341–356) are each 16 bars, and c440's closing groups (361/365/369/373) are four-bar units. One extra bar would break that hypermeter at the movement's most exposed join. The G.P. already supplies the silence the horn enters into. Kept; this is a stronger reason than c423's renumbering one.

### 4. Timpani motive joining T's phrases (Beethoven VC, PDF 273): kept out of the trio

c423 and c056 hold the timpani back so that its pp motive x at 241 announces the retransition. The reading's example is a concerto opening, not a contrast section, so it does not outweigh that reason.

### 5. 120→121 and 288→289: no overlap of loud and soft chords (PDF 190): kept

Adler's overlap is for a held loud chord. Both tuttis end on a short crotchet with a two-beat caesura, and then a new phrase starts sub. p (121) or as a fugato subject (289). The cut is the effect wanted, as at 56→57.
