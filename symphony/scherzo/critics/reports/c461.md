## Findings

### 1. 177–184: Vln I should double the cellos' T an octave higher for its consequent, as in Ex. 7. (Applied.)

**Problem.** The second statement of T is in the cellos.
- From 177 its consequent climbs C♯4–D♯4–E4–G♯4 to the tied A4 (178–179), the tune's peak, and turns to B at 181–184.
- Since c391 the violas double this in unison. A viola–cello unison adds weight in the same octave, and the cello colour absorbs it (PDF 57, "the 'cello quality predominating").
- So the peak of the trio tune is heard only at A4, an octave below where the listener learned it. Cl 1 sang it at A5 at 162.

**Evidence.**
- Ex. 7, PDF 56: "A broad melody dolce ed espressivo, afterwards doubled by the first violins an octave higher." PDF 181 shows the layout: Vln I joins the cello line partway through, over Vln II and violas in broken-chord quavers.
- PDF 60–61 (e): violins and cellos in octaves are "used in very expressive passages where the 'cellos have to play on the A or D strings. This method produces a more resonant tone" than the viola–cello octave. Pan Voyevoda [134] is listed as "cantabile melody given first to 'cellos alone (cf. Ex. 7)".
- Our cellos play C♯4–A4 here on the D and A strings. That is exactly Rimsky's condition.
- PDF 54: relief "by selection and contrast of timbres". Vln I plays without mutes over the muted Vln II quavers, so it stands out by colour as well as by register.

**Answering the recorded reasons.**
- c344 moved this statement to the cellos to save "the high violin octave for 209". Vln I here plays C♯5–A5, in Cl 1's register, not the G♯5–A6 octave. The high octave is still heard first at 209.
- c391 wanted more carrying power for the climbing phrase. Vln I gives more, in a new octave. To respect c436 ("full, so thin rather than add"), the violas return to rest, so the number of sections on T is unchanged.
- c418: the first slur is up-bow, and the A at 178 beat 3 starts a new down-bow slur, as in every other statement.

**Memorability.** T's peak A is now heard twice at the same pitch, A5 at 162 and A5 at 178, before the violins carry it to A6 at 218. The tune climbs through the trio by octave: Cl 1 at A5, cellos with violins at A4 and A5, violins at A5 and A6.

### 2. 349–356: the coda's last T has Fl 1 in the upper octave and Vln II alone in the lower (considered, kept)

Ex. 26 (PDF 60, 202–203) puts the flute and all first violins except two in the lower octave, and two soli in the upper. Here one solo violin and Fl 1 are on top and Vln II alone is below. PDF 57 (Vln I + II in unison "gains in power and richness") would answer c457's economy reason for removing the other first violins. I kept the passage as it is:
- the Note to Ex. 24 allows "one or two desks";
- the lower octave already has a full section, as row 14 asks;
- c438's design carries the coda's T by single players (Hn 3, Ob 1, solo violin);
- a third reversal in 90 cycles (c371, c457) would be churn without a clear gain in sound.

### 3. 209–216: T's forte return has no woodwind on the upper octave until 217 (considered, rejected)

PDF 57 and 61 expect woodwind on a forte violin melody. I considered Fl 2 with Vln I at 209–216 (not built). It would remove the growth that Rimsky's own Snegourotchka sequence describes (PDF 61): violins and Cl 1 first, then Fl 1 joins on top for the climb to A6 at 217–218. The flute's entry at 217 is that growth. It would also give the flutes 20 bars without a rest (c036, c414). Kept.

### 4. 253–268: double basses divided in octaves, pizz. (kept)

Note II (PDF 62) advises against divided basses in octaves, because the two halves lie on strings that do not correspond. These are single pizzicato downbeats under the cellos' arco countermelody, not a bowed line, so the unity-of-tone reason hardly applies. The alternatives each have a cost:
- one octave only loses either the 16′ or the 8′ bass;
- taking a cello half (Note III) breaks c447/c449's countermelody.

Kept, and recorded as a deliberate exception.

### 5. 197–200: the oboe call in Vln I and Fl 1 at C♯6–E6 without a lower octave (kept)

The scan lists 198 and 200. Note I says high melodies are "generally" doubled below. This is a two-bar call, a level above T (c404, c437), and the flute supplies the "firmness of timbre" of PDF 59. It is protected. Kept.

### 6. 21–24 and 261–264: theme A's C6 peak in Vln I alone (kept)

PDF 58–59 warns that high figures are isolated without an octave. Theme A is a *leggiero* tune in the single-section layout of Ex. 1, and the opening stays bare as the first of four stages (c259, c359). At 264 Fl 1 joins the C6 in unison. Kept.

### 7. The second idea's memorability (no change from this reading)

All three statements already use Rimsky's strongest layouts:
- 45: double octaves, Ex. 32;
- 121: violins in octaves;
- 281: violins and cellos in octaves (row 16).

This chapter teaches how to lay out a melody, not how to build one, so it gives no ground for new notes. The point stays open for a figuration or form reading.
