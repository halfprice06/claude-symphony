## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 81-84: the clarinet's dolce solo in the F-major episode has no shape in its first phrase (applied)

**Problem.** Cl 1 has the only clarinet solo of the scherzo proper at 81-88: p dolce over the harp's off-beats, with pp horns, bassoon and bass clarinet beneath it. The line is two four-bar phrases. The first rises to its peak, F5, a minim on the downbeat of 82, and falls to A4 at 84. The second is lower, peaks on C5 at 86 and fades to pp at 87 under the existing `>` at 86. The first phrase, which is the higher one and carries the episode's melody after the harp hands it on, has no hairpin at all. So its peak F5 is played at the same flat p as the A4 it starts from. In the movement, every other exposed wind solo is shaped: the trio calls (185-192, c278), T's held peaks (c178), the codetta solos (225-236), Ob 1 at 345, and Cl 1 at 349. This phrase is the one left out.

**Evidence.** All four clarinet solos Adler prints give each phrase its own hairpin as well as a dynamic and a word. Tchaikovsky 5/i has p, più f, mf, then dim. inside one soft theme in the low clarinets (PDF 211, Ex. VII-59). The *Hebrides* clarinets have p dolce with `<>` and dim. (PDF 212, Ex. VII-63). In Wagner's *Götterdämmerung* the clarinet plays pp with `< >`, dim. and più p (PDF 217, Ex. VII-74). The harp's own statement of the head just before this (73-80) already has a hairpin. Adler also notes that the clarinet keeps its full dynamic range in every register (PDF 210), so a swell from p at D5 to F5 needs no help.

**Fix (sections/sB.ily, sBClOne).** The line crescendos to the peak and diminuendos back. The peak is a hidden mp and the return a hidden p, following the c138/c178/c278 convention, so the MIDI plays the shape and the print shows only the hairpins. The second phrase keeps its p and its `>` at 86 into pp at 87. The two phrases are therefore graded down, mp and then p, as the episode moves from F toward the diminished sevenths of 87-88.
```
d''4(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce" a'8-- a'-- d''4 | f''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> e''8 d''8 | bes'4)-\tweak stencil ##f \p g'8--( g'-- bes'4 | a'2.) | ...
```
Cl 2 rests at 81-85, so the shared staff has one set of dynamics. The harp, horns, bass clarinet and Bsn 1 stay as they are. This changes no notes and no spacing, so the protected F-major episode (harmony, colour, spacing, harp head 73-80) is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 345-347: Bsn 1's countermelody stays flat while Ob 1 swells above it (applied)

**Problem.** In the coda's second phrase of T (345-348), Ob 1 has the tune, p dolce, with a swell to a hidden mp at 346. Bsn 1 has the countermelody (c078), p dolce, A3-B3 held over the bar, D3, D♯3-E3, then G♯3-B3. It has no hairpin. When the oboe swells over the bar line 345-346 and the bassoon holds its tied B3 at p, the bassoon is covered just where its long note should carry. The two lines are meant as a duet over ppp violas and pp harp. As written, the oboe's shape makes the bassoon sound like accompaniment.

**Evidence.** Adler warns that the bassoon, "when accompanied, has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments", and that the dynamics of the instruments around it must be marked so that they do not cover it (PDF 226). His bassoon model in this range is Tchaikovsky 6/i, where each two-bar group has its own `<>` (PDF 227, Ex. VII-93). Here the simplest correct way to keep the duet balanced is to give the bassoon the same swell as the oboe, not to take the oboe's swell away.

**Fix (sections/coda.ily, codaBsnOne).** The swell follows the oboe's, peaking on the bassoon's long note at 346 and back to p at 347:
```
\partCombineSoloI a4(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce" b2~ | b4-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> d2 | dis2-\tweak stencil ##f \p e4 | gis2 b4) \partCombineAutomatic
```
Bsn 2 rests through these bars, so the shared staff carries one set of dynamics. The hairpin prints under the bassoon staff level with Ob 1's (checked at 150 dpi on page 12).
