## Findings

### 1. Bars 105–132: the second idea at 121 is a fourth loud arrival with no change of colour (applied)

**Problem.** From the brass entry at 101 to the cadence at 131–132 the music never falls below f: 101–104 build, 105–120 ff tutti theme A, then 121–132 the second idea at f. That makes 32 bars (about 22 s), the longest loud block in the movement. The statement at 121 also uses the colour and doubling of 45: Fl 1 and Ob 1 at pitch with Vln I, Vln II an octave lower, clarinets and bassoons in hemiola chords, horns sustaining, everything f from the first note. Coming straight after a tutti, the ear hears 121 as a continuation of the tutti, not as the start of a new section. This is the "four loud arrivals before the trio (41, 72, 104, 121)" that c059 recorded and left open for a later form critic. Two of those four are not plateaus: 41 builds on into 45, and 72 lasts one bar with a diminuendo out of it. The 105–132 block is the one that tires the ear.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Adler's dialogue principle (PDF 257–258): a restatement should come back "in a radically different orchestral color", and doing so "aids in a clearer exposition of the form".
- Mozart's practice (PDF 267, point 3): "as soon as the flute enters, the orchestra is reduced drastically" after the tutti.
- Haydn (PDF 288) changes the doubling in the second phrase "to add great variety".
- Forte–subito piano (PDF 190): the soft group takes over at the moment the loud sound cuts off. At 120 the tutti already stops on beat 1, so the two silent beats work as that cut-off, and the strings can come in sub. p on the downbeat.

The reprise already treats this music this way at 277: sub. p, Ob 1 from 281, Fl 1 and f from 285 (c039, c044). The exposition's E-minor statement is the only one that does not.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-063/work/sections/sC.ily).** Bar 121 is sub. p in every part. Strings, clarinets, bassoons, contrabassoon, Hn 2 and Hn 3 carry 121–124, keeping c058's swell in the violins. Fl 1 and Ob 1 rest at 121–124 and enter at 125 p with a crescendo. All parts crescendo from 125 and reach f on the hemiola cadence at 129. Pitches and rhythms are unchanged. For example:

**Result.** The ff tutti now ends cleanly, and the second idea starts as a new section in a new level and colour. The f returns only for the cadence, which then hands over to the cellos' f motive x at 133 as before. There is no longer a fourth loud plateau before the trio. The reprise keeps its own version of the same shape (sub. p at 277 and the staggered wind entries), so c039's reason for differentiating the reprise is not undone. Exposition and reprise now treat the E-minor statement alike, which is what a da capo should do. This change does not undo any earlier fix: c045's "f in the accompaniment at 121" was a fix for parts that still read ff, and every part now has its own explicit marking.

### Points checked against the reading and judged sound (no change)

- **The coda's length (325–376, 42 s; Meno mosso 31 s).** The coda takes 37% as many bars as the first scherzo section, against Dvořák's 22%. I keep it.
  - It is not a fourth literal statement of T: its first half is in A major (c013) and it is passed Hn 1 → Ob 1 → Cl 1 + Vln, a new colour for each phrase as Adler PDF 288 asks.
  - It opens into a bare texture after the G.P., as at PDF 267–269.
  - It holds the pivot G♯ that the finale needs.
  - The fast part (325–339) is short, but its peak is the movement's only fff and B6, and since c033 the reprise tutti keeps the low brass back for it.
- **Trio proportions (88 bars; T in full at 153, 169 and 209).** These follow the dialogue principle (Cl → violins → tutti f). Dvořák 9/iii uses four statements of c before the d section.
- **Link 141–152 and retransition 241–252.** Both build expectation with held wind chords, pulsing timpani and harp, as in Adler's Bartók and Sibelius examples (PDF 269–270). Both are dovetailed (bass clarinet E at 139–141; c037 suspensions).
- **72→73.** Adler's overlap is already there: Hn 1·2 tie the ff chord into the pp (PDF 190).
- **Length of the movement (4:36).** Right between the Adagio (5:45) and the finale (7:01).
- **Page count.** Unchanged at 13 pages.
