## Findings (ranked)

### 1. T bar 3 in the clarinet (Cl 1 155): candidate built, then withdrawn
- Bars: 155. The same figure appears at 171 (Vc/Hn 3), 199 and 211 (strings and Cl 1), and 343 (Hn 3, in A).
- Question: T's first statement is the movement's most exposed woodwind melody, Cl 1 p dolce over a bare background. Its whole compass lies in the clarino, which Adler calls "bright, incisive, expressive" (PDF 210, Ex. VII-56), with one exception. In bar 3 the falling sixth D♯5–F♯4 lands on the throat tone F♯4 (written A4 on the A clarinet, "rather pale"). That note is then slurred across the break to the tied A4 (written C5), and the tied A4 is the preparation of the phrase's 4–3 suspension. HISTORY lists T's first phrase as "the least memorable idea" (c289–c311), so I tested whether removing the pale note would help.
- Test: in tA.ily, `dis''4. \beatq #0.8 fis'8 a'4\<~` became `dis''4. \beatq #0.8 b'8 a'4\<~`, which keeps the whole bar in the clarino with no break crossing. The build was clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths match, no C-major triad, no new parallels. themecheck reported trioT ClOne 153 CHANGED.
- Verdict: the change makes the music worse, so I withdrew it. D♯5–B4–A4–G♯4 turns T's one distinctive interval into a plain 7–5–4–3 descent. That is the c322 form, which c321 replaced because the falling sixth inverts movement I's horn-call interval and gives phrase 1 its profile. HISTORY protects it: "T's new bar 3 and the inner resolutions under it". The throat note is a quaver, about 0.13 s at 2. = 76. Adler's own sentence on the break (PDF 210) is that it "completely disappears as the performer becomes more experienced". Changing only the clarinet statement would also make bar 3 differ between statements. The recorded reason for the falling sixth outweighs the register reason the reading gives, so bar 3 stays as written. No theme change.

### 2. The second idea is introduced forte and massed (45–56): no change
- Problem: the movement still has one open melody point (c401, c431, c481): the second idea is the least memorable of the four ideas. Dvořák 9/iii introduces its lyrical theme softly in a woodwind pair (flute and oboe, p) and repeats it in a second woodwind colour (clarinet, mp). Here the first hearing (45) is forte espress. in three octaves (Vln I/Fl 1, Ob 1/Vln II, Vla/C.a./Vc). The soft woodwind-led hearing comes only in the reprise (Fl 1 at 277).
- Reading: Adler PDF 184 values a woodwind as a presenter of melody. Nothing in ch. 7 says a forte doubled presentation is wrong. Fl 1 at D6–A6 is in the flute's "clear and brilliant" register and Ob 1 is an octave lower in its "warm, poignant" register (PDF 185, 198).
- Why no change: thinning or softening 45–56 has been rejected several times (c224, c314, c481, c684: "the 45–55 statement is now full", "forte G-major statement in three octaves"). The soft presentation Dvořák uses already exists at 121 (two violin octaves, sub. p) and 277 (Fl 1). This reading gives no stronger reason than the recorded ones.

### 3. Ob 1 E6 at 226 (codetta head, p<): follows the reading
The oboe's top is C6–F6, which Adler describes as "thin but clear… for a thin, softly piercing effect, the oboe is sometimes most beautifully scored" there (PDF 198). Ob 1 → Cl 1 → Bsn 1 falls an octave per statement (225, 229, 233). Moving the oboe down would break that relay. No change.

### 4. Cl 1 at 81–88 crosses the break eight times at p dolce: follows the reading
The motive-x figures at 83 and 85–87 (written D♭5–B♭4–B♭4–D♭5, C5–A♭4–A♭4–C5, C5–A4–A4–F♯4) cross the break. 87–88 lie in the throat. Adler PDF 210 says the break gives an experienced player no difficulty. The pale throat colour suits the phrase's fade to pp into the Neapolitan (c580's resolution at 86–87). No B♭ or A clarinet avoids the crossing at these pitches, and moving the line to the oboe would put pp F4 in the oboe's lowest fifth, which PDF 198 forbids. No change (as c131, c311, c401).

### 5. Fl 1 A6 pp with the solo violin at 350: follows the reading
Adler PDF 186: notes above A6 are hard and are best approached by scale. Here A6 is the top note, approached by step from G♯6 (bars 349–350). It is not above A6. The passage is settled (c457, c466, c494). No change.
