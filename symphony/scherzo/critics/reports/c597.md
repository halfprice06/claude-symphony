## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 105–120: Tbn 1 on the Hn 3·4 tenor countermelody. Built, build clean, rejected.

**Candidate.** In the exposition tutti the trombones play only beat-1 crotchets (G3/E3). These double Hn 1·2's chord an octave lower, and on most bars they also double the tenor line's attack. The reading gives two reasons to give Tbn 1 a line. First, the trombones' independence (PDF 44 and 48–49, Schoenberg). Second, the Bartók equation (PDF 44): two horns in unison equal one trombone. With Tbn 1 added, the tenor countermelody would weigh two "units" instead of one, and the trombone section would play counterpoint instead of a duplicated beat. This is the change in this reading that a listener would hear most: a trombone playing a legato line inside a ff tutti.

**Build.** In /tmp/critic-597/cand/sections/sC.ily I replaced `sCTbnOne` at 105–120 with the tenor line, phrased and breathed like Hn 3:
```
sCTbnOne = { g2(^\markup \italic "espress." a4 | b2 e'4~ | e'2 b4 | a2 fis4 | g2.) | a2( c'4~ | c'4 b4 a4 | g2.) | c'2.( | b2. | a2 c'4 | b2 a4) \breathe | g2( e'4~ | e'2 a4~ | a2 fis4 | g4) r4 r4 | R2.*20 | }
```
Results: build-warnings.txt empty, 13 pages, "part lengths: all match", "C-major triads: none", parallels unchanged (bar 69 only), theme record 52/52 unchanged. Page 4 engraves cleanly.

**Why it is worse.** The tenor line (F♯3–E4) lies right under theme A's lower octave, which the cor anglais and Cl 2 play at B3–A4. The trombone would be in unison with that octave on E4 at 107 and 117. Elsewhere it would mostly be within a third to a sixth of it (105 A3 against B3, 109 G3 against B3, 114 B3 against D♯4/E4). By PDF 44, one f trombone equals two horns. The two reeds that carry the theme's middle octave would be covered. c587 identified that octave as the weakest layer of the tutti, and c557/c595 kept it as it is. The change would also:
- reverse c076's balance (horns one level above trumpets and trombones);
- give the exposition tutti a heavier countermelody than the coda climax, where the c514 countermelody stays in Hn 3·4 + Bsn 1;
- touch a tutti layout that has been protected repeatedly (c05, c254, c264, c497, c595).

The reading teaches independence. It does not ask for a trombone line that masks the theme. Not applied.

### 2. 89–100: the timpani's motive x without brass punctuation (closest match to Hindemith m. 192). Kept.

PDF 42 and 44 (*) describe brass punctuating the theme in the timpani's rests. In Hindemith, though, this comes at the end of a full brass fugato. The brass have been playing for thirty bars and close the passage with it. Here the same device would be the trumpets' and trombones' first entry, before 101. That would end the quiet of the ritmo di tre battute, which c197, c397, c497 F3, c244/c559/c576 and c537 protect. The reading gives no stronger reason than the one already recorded. Not proposed.

### 3. 69–72 and 297–300: stretto and horn entry. Follows.

The entries come a bar apart: Fl 1/Ob 1/Vln I at 69, Cl 1/Vln II at 70, Hn 1·2 a2 with Bsn 1 and Vc at 71. Each voice continues after its entry (rows 4, 9 and 10). The horns are a2 on A4–E4, their best register, and weigh as one trumpet against the woodwinds and strings at f<. No change.

### 4. 57–68 and 289–296: fugato. Follows.

The subject is always in a clear register (Vc G3, Vla D5, Vln II A5). The voice that goes on into the countersubject drops a level (Vc pp under Vla p at 61; Vla pp under Vln II p at 65), as Hindemith's trombones do at mm. 158–172 (row 6). The strings-only colour stays (c254, c337, c497).

### 5. 331–339: coda brass. Follows. I checked one variant and rejected it.

The brass enter in stages: Hn 3·4 at 331 (pp<, on Cl 1's augmented tail), then Hn 1·2, the trumpets and the trombones at 332. The trumpets keep x through 335 (c497). At 336–338 the brass hold B7 in dotted minims against the woodwind and string hemiola, the climactic sustain of PDF 41. I considered re-attacking the horns in the hemiola rhythm (Hn 1 `c''2 c''4~ | c''4 b'2`, following Bartók's "entire choir" joining at m. 386). That would remove the bar-line-against-hemiola counterpoint, which is the same device as at 101–104. Rejected.

### 6. 334–335: Tbn 1 doubling the c514 countermelody. Rejected.

The countermelody B3–E4 | E4–B3 lies in the violas' octave of theme A (E4–A4) at 333–335. A trombone added there would cover the violas, for the reason given in finding 1. Not built.

### 7. 101–104 and 301–304: brass sustain under the hemiola; trumpet x on bars 1 and 3, timpani x at 104. Follows.

This is principle 1 together with Hindemith's exchange between brass and timpani (PDF 44). It is also principle 13 in reverse: the strings move and the brass sustain. c497 F5 already kept the four-bar hypermeter (no x at 102). No change.

### 8. PDF 50–54 (mutes, rips, sf>p, niente). Not applicable.

These effects are excluded by Dan's soft-brass note (c123) and the BRIEF. The meticulous-marking point is already met: brass entries carry their dynamics, tenuto slurs (c1125) and breaths.
