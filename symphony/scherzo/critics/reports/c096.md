## What else I studied

I worked out the bowing of every string part with a script. It follows Adler's rules (PDF 37–38): a new bow on every unslurred note, one bow per slur or tie, an upbeat after a rest starts up-bow, a downbeat after a rest starts down-bow, and the printed ⊓/V signs override the habit. I then read the result bar by bar wherever an accent, an sf/sfp or an ff/fff arrival landed on an up-bow, and at every arco/pizz. change. I read c006, c018 and c085, the earlier Adler ch. 2 reports, and the HISTORY entries for them, so as not to repeat their points. Finding 1 answers c085's finding 6. The corpus *Coriolan* (mutopia/BeethovenLv_O62) prints no bowings, so the evidence for finding 1 is Adler's own Ex. II-20.

## Findings

### 1. 300–306: the extra ff bar in the reprise reverses the strings' bowing (applied)
**Bars:** 300–306, all five string parts.
**Problem:** In the exposition, the ff chord at 72 is followed by sixteen bars of rest. The crescendo at 101 therefore starts on a fresh down-bow. It reaches 104 on an up-bow (the natural crescendo direction, PDF 38), and the tutti theme starts down-bow at 105, with the accented minim at 106 also down-bow. In the reprise, the ff dotted minim at 300 (marked ⊓) runs straight into the sfp at 301. Counting one bow per note or slur, the sfp at 301 now falls on an up-bow in every string part. The ff arrival at 304 falls on a down-bow, so it is approached by a diminishing stroke. The tutti downbeat at 305 (accented in the basses) falls on an up-bow, and so does the accented minim at 306 in Vln I and II. This also answers c085's finding 6. That finding removed Vln II's ⊓ at 306 because "after `e'4 b8 b e'4` (four strokes from a down-bow), the minim falls down-bow anyway". But 305 starts on an up-bow, not a down-bow, because of the chain that begins at 300.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus* 276–86): "Sometimes two vigorous articulations follow one another, and a double down-bow situation is called for … A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid." He adds that if this is done well "the bow change will hardly be noticed." PDF 38: crescendo goes naturally up-bow and the heavy stroke is at the frog; mark the bow only where the habit must be overridden. Here a single mark at 300 corrects six bars.
**Fix (applied):** dB.ily, bar 300 (the dB section's 12th bar). The dotted minim becomes a minim tied to a crotchet, with ⊓ on the minim and V on the crotchet, as in Ex. II-20:
- `dBVlnOne`: `e'''2->\ff\dnb~ e'''4\upbow`
- `dBVlnTwo`: `<e'' cis'''>2->\ff\dnb~ <e'' cis'''>4\upbow`
- `dBVla`: `<e' a'>2->\ff\dnb~ <e' a'>4\upbow`
- `dBVc`: `<a, e>2->\ff\dnb~ <a, e>4\upbow`
- `dBCb`: `a,,2->\ff\dnb~ a,,4\upbow`

Now 301 sfp is down-bow, 304 ff is reached on an up-bow crescendo, and 305 and 306 are down-bow, exactly as at 101–106. The sound does not change (it is a tie), and no sign at 306 is needed.

### 2. 333–339: in the cellos and basses the fff peak and the final chord fall on up-bows, and the violas bow the hemiola against the violins (applied)
**Bars:** 336–339 in Vc, Cb and Vla.
**Problem:** From 333 the cellos and basses play four separate ff/fff dotted minims (E, E, B, B). Each bar takes a new bow, so the fff at 336 (the movement's peak, with Vln I's only B6) lands on an up-bow, and so does the final accented chord at 339. The basses come from single bowed staccato crotchets at 329–332, each after a rest, so 333 is certainly down-bow and 336 and 339 certainly up-bow. The violins mark ⊓ at 336, 337 and 339, so they play the hemiola minims down–up–down and the last chord down-bow. The violas have no signs at 337 and 339, so they play the same hemiola up–down–up, in octaves with the violins.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: the bow is louder and heavier at the frog; the composer marks direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit." Here the habit puts the two strongest attacks of the coda on up-bows. Adler PDF 42–43 and Ex. II-29 (Tchaikovsky 6/iii, Allegro molto vivace) show successive down-bows, each with the bow lifted, as the standard heavy ff stroke in a fast movement, used by every string part together. The violins here already do this.
**Fix (applied):** coda.ily.
- `codaVc`: `b,2.\fff\dnb | b,2->\dnb b,4->~ | b,4 b,2-> | <e, b,>4->\dnb r4 r4`
- `codaCb`: `b,,2.\fff\dnb | b,,2->\dnb b,,4->~ | b,,4 b,,2-> | e,,4->\dnb r4 r4`
- `codaVla`: `a'2->\dnb fis'4->~ | fis'4 dis'2-> | <b g' e''>4->\dnb r4 r4`

All five string parts now bow 336–339 alike: ⊓ (fff), ⊓, V (tied), ⊓, ⊓. This does not touch c085's protected hemiola bowing at 53, 129 and 285.

### 3. 223–224: the basses leave the bow at the tip with one crotchet to pluck (applied)
**Bars:** Cb 223–224 → pizz. at 225.
**Problem:** The basses play separate dotted minims from 209. Counting bows from the arco entry at 209 (down-bow after a rest), bar 223 is up-bow and bar 224 (`e,2 r4`) down-bow. The player therefore ends at the tip and has a crotchet at 2. = 76 (about 0.26 s) to set the bow and pluck at 225. The cellos, which pluck at the same moment, already have `gis,2.~\upbow` tied over 223–224 (c006/c085) and arrive at the frog.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54: "it is much easier to change from arco to pizzicato, if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog. They then have enough leverage on the bow to make the switch more quickly." Cb 204 → 205 already follows this.
**Fix (applied):** tC.ily `tCCb`: `e,2.~\upbow\> | e,2 r4\!`. The E is tied as in the cellos. The diminuendo is unchanged, and the bass line loses only a soft re-attack of the same note.
