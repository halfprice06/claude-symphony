## Findings

### 1. 169–176: Hn 3 doubles the cellos' T in unison (applied)

**Problem.** T is the movement's lyrical tune. Its head G♯–A–B–E–D♯ is the horn call 3–1–7 (bars 1–6) with the sixth filled in. In the trio, the horn sings T only heterophonically at 197–200, and the first time a horn states it is the coda's four bars at 341. In the second statement at 169 the cellos play T alone in their tenor register (G♯3–A4), mp espress., with Vln II con sord. above. This is a long, sustained, slurred tenor melody. Adler names it as the line that "usually" takes a horn and cello doubling (PDF 27, Ex. XI-10). The score already uses that pairing three times: the tutti countermelody at 105/305, the second idea at 281–287 and the countermelody at 217–224. The trio tune is the one long tenor melody that never gets it.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 27: horn and cello double on long sustained melodies, and the horns add articulation and "personalities" to the cello line.
- PDF 20: a later statement takes a new doubling as a new colour. Statement 1 is Cl 1 over harp, and statement 2 is now cellos with horn: the same tune in a new tenor colour.
- PDF 35–36, Ex. XI-18: the doubling of one line passes from group to group. At 177 the violas already join the cellos (c391). The horn now hands the doubling to the violas at the half cadence, so the colour changes at the phrase join, not inside a phrase.
- PDF 23: know which notes are to dominate. The horn adds weight to the antecedent's tied A (171–172) and the suspended A♯ (175–176) without going above the line.
- Brahms 3/iii gives a cello tune to a solo horn when it returns.
- A listener now hears T sung by a horn at 169. That ties it to the horn call of bars 1–6 and prepares Hn 3's T at 341. This is the audible link that makes the tune easier to remember.

**The protection answered.** c304 and c319 kept "strings alone 169–184", for two reasons. The first was that the whole choir changes after 16 bars of clarinet and harp. The second was that a wind added there would weaken the drop to wind colours at 185 (Ob 1, C.a.). Both reasons have been overtaken. Since c349, Fl 1 answers at 172, 176 and 183–184, and since c372 Bsn 2 holds the bass at 181–184, so the passage is no longer strings alone. Hn 3 stops at 176, eight bars before 185, and the call at 185 is oboe and cor anglais, which are not horn colours. Hn 1's entry at 195 is unchanged. The change of choir after 168 still happens, because the harp and the clarinet stop. Nothing protected changes: the different bass of the two trio statements (c429), Vln II's quaver background (c407), the violas from 177 (c391) and the bowing of 173–178 (c418) all stay as they were.

### 2. 209–216: no horn on T's forte return (kept)

The trio's climax is the obvious place for a Romantic horn on the tune. c251 and c341 kept the score as it is, and the reading supports them. The tune's upper octave would put the horn on A5 sounding at ff, the loud high horn that Schuller warns about (PDF 26). The lower octave (G♯3–A4) is taken by Bsn 1, the cellos' countermelody with Bsn 2, and Hn 4 from 217. Finding 1 also gives T its horn-cello statement at the right dynamic. Kept.

### 3. 121–128: the second idea in E minor stays in the violins (kept)

The second idea is still the least memorable idea (open since c289). I considered moving its antecedent at 121–124 to Hn 1 + cellos two octaves down (B3–E4), and decided against it. The reprise already gives the second idea that exact tenor horn-cello colour at 281–287 (c071). Hindemith's practice (PDF 20) is to give each repetition a different colour, and doing it at 121 as well would use up the colour that makes 281 new. The cellos at 121 also carry the bass with Bsn 2 while the basses rest, so they cannot be spared. Kept.

### 4. 341–356: the coda hand-over of T (kept)

Hn 3 → Ob 1 (with Cl 1 a sixth under) → two solo violins over the section is the Lohengrin pattern on PDF 35–36. Each colour enters alone and the passage ends pp by elimination. With Finding 1, Hn 3's T head at 341 is now the return of a horn colour the trio introduced, so it is no longer a first use. That makes the coda's recall clearer. Kept.

### 5. Horn unisons and soft brass (kept)

c341's scan found no horn unison at A4 or above except Hn 1·2 on motive x at 71/299 (middle register, PDF 26), and the only horn line Finding 1 adds is a single horn at G♯3–F♯4. Every horn melody is p or pp, and the mf at 197 has no accent, as Dan's soft-brass rule asks. Kept.
