## Findings

No finding requires a change to the score. The chapter's principles are all met, most of them because earlier critics applied this chapter or the parallel Rimsky-Korsakov and Adler ch. 15/17 rules (c054, c074, c084, c107, c111, c124, c127). I record the points I checked closely and why each stays.

### 1. Bars 87-88: low close-position diminished sevenths under Cl 1 (kept)

Problem examined. These are the only wind-only chords where the voices are packed in thirds near the bottom and the widest gap is above them: 87 A2 (B.cl.) – E♭3 (Cl 2) – F♯3 (Hn 4) – A3 (Bsn 1) – C4 (Hn 3) under Cl 1 A4-F♯4-E♭4; 88 B♭2 – C♯3 – E3 – G3 – C♯4 under Cl 1 E4. Adler PDF 257 prefers "the greater gaps ... between the bass and the next highest voice", and Ex. VIII-24 calls a low close wind chord "difficult, and not very good".

Why it stays. PDF 258 and Ex. VIII-29 show the other side: a dark hue is made exactly by low, close clarinets, bass clarinet and bassoons at p. These two bars are the pp, poco rit. darkening at the end of the F-major episode (D♯°7/A, A♯°7), before the a tempo at 89, and the dark colour is the point. Each re-voicing I tried breaks an earlier accepted fix: moving Cl 2 back up puts it in Cl 1's register again (c127, Adler ch. 17); moving Hn 3 or Bsn 1 changes the prepared seventh (c132); the bass clarinet cannot go lower (its lowest note is D♭2; c016). The horns are pp and in their middle register, so the chord does not thicken in practice. No change.

### 2. Bars 37-44 and 273-275: flutes (and oboes from 41) a2 in unison (kept)

Problem examined. Adler PDF 249-250: two like winds in unison on an ordinary passage give a thicker, slightly out-of-tune tone and prevent rubato; a2 belongs in tuttis, or should be in octaves.

Why it stays. This is not a solo. From 37 the winds and both violin sections join on the theme and build p to f, and the unison is needed for that build. Adler PDF 238 describes the same thing in Schubert: the flute "enters almost undetected, doubling the oboe for the crescendo". An octave doubling would push Fl 2 to G6-C7 (against principle 4 and above the violins' B6 limit) or down to G4 (weak). c107 and c111 settled this passage. No change.

### 3. Bars 29-30 and 33-34: Fl 1 in unison with Ob 1 down to B4 (kept)

Problem examined. In these two bars the flute plays at the bottom of its range (B4-E5), staccato p, where it has little power.

Why it stays. Adler PDF 236 (Ex. VIII-2) recommends exactly this: the oboe, in its better register, "will certainly stand out, but the flute will somewhat neutralize the nasal quality of the oboe and give the passage a rounder, richer sound". The flute here colours the oboe; it does not need to carry. This was c054's fix, and it keeps A6 unused until 50. No change.

### 4. Bars 189-192: cor anglais with the cellos at pitch on the trio's answer (kept)

Problem examined. Adler PDF 249-250: wind-string unison at pitch can "detract from the clarity of a line".

Why it stays. PDF 266 (Berlioz, Ex. VIII-37) shows the other side: without the wind doubling the line has "neither the warmth nor richness". The call at 185 is Ob 1 alone, and the answer is a different colour (double reed plus cello), which is Adler's function 3 (PDF 233). With the call in a single colour and the answer in a mixed one, the two are clearly told apart, and at 197 both join (principle 12). c011 made this choice. No change.

### 5. Bars 101-104 and 105-120: wind pedals in the hemiola build and the tutti (kept)

Checked against Mozart 29 (PDF 240-241, Ex. VIII-8, 9). In 101-104 Ob 1 holds D♯5, Fl 2 F♯5, Hn 3·4 F♯3-B3 and Cbsn B1 for four bars under the moving hemiola in Fl 1/Vln I, which gives the stability Adler describes. In 105-120 the sustained layer is Bsn 2 E2 and Hn 3·4. The horns' short beats 1 and 3 are protected (c086), and Dan's soft-brass rule rules out a louder held brass pedal. No change.
