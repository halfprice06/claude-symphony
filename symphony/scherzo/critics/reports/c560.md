## Findings

### 1. Bars 358–359: the violas double the cor anglais's 6–♭6–5 line in unison, which covers the colour c460 put there (applied)

**Problem.** c460 moved the inner line C♯4–C4–B3 under Hn 1's motto tail from Bsn 1 to the cor anglais. The reason was cyclic: movement II closes its own motto tail over the cor anglais's F4–F♭4–E♭4 (II 87–89), and the finale has the same line at IV 273–275. But the violas (unis., the whole section, pp) play the same C♯4–C4–B3 in unison with the single cor anglais.
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 52: adding one wind instrument to all or part of a string section in unison "only thickens the resonance of the latter, the wood-wind timbre being lost in the process".
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76: in such unisons the strings predominate.

So what a listener hears at 358–360 is a viola line, not II's cor anglais. This is the case Sebesky's ch. 9 describes (PDF 240): the voicing does not sound the way it was intended. Movement II itself avoids the problem: its violas and Hn 4 take the line an octave below the cor anglais, never at its pitch.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Economy (PDF 12–13, Ex. 1): the violas' C♯4–C4 add nothing to the harmony. A♯°7 at 358 and iv6/4 at 359 are already complete without them (Hn 1 A♯4/A4, Vln II G4/E4, C.a. C♯4/C4, Bsn 2 and Vc E3, Cb E2).
- Instrumentation (PDF 11, 15): the C.a. has rested since 340, so its entry at 358 is the moment its colour should register.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily, codaVla).**
`e'2.(\pp^\markup \italic "unis." | cis'2. | c'2. | b2.) | b2.~ | …`
becomes
`e'2.~(\pp^\markup \italic "unis." | e'2.~ | e'2. | b2.) | b2.~ | …`
- The violas hold E4 through 357–359. E4 is a tone of I, of A♯°7 and of iv6/4.
- They join the line's goal, B3, at 360. The goal note is the coinciding note of the hand-over (Rimsky PDF 125–126), and they hold it into the 361 chord as before.
- The C.a. now plays C♯4–C4 alone at its pitch, as II's C.a. does.
- Unchanged: Hn 1's tail, the C.a.'s notes, the harmony, the 357 chord (E4 still fills the gap between E3 and G♯4), c050's rule that each chromatic step is made in one voice (C♯–C in the C.a., G♯–G in Vln II), c518's one bow over 357–360, and every dynamic.
- At 359–360 the violas' E4 meets Vln II's E4 in unison. Both are strings, so no colour is lost.

**Alternatives tried on paper and rejected.**
- Violas an octave lower (C♯3–C3–B2), as in II. This puts the violas under the E3 pedal of cellos and Bsn 2, a close low spacing that Rimsky PDF 125 warns about, and leaves an octave leap to their B3 at 361.
- Violas resting at 357–360. This leaves 357 with only E3, G♯4 and B4.
- Dropping the C.a. instead. This undoes c460's reason, which still holds.

**Records.** Notes, rhythms and pitches of every recorded theme are unchanged (52/52). This is not a theme change.

- **Horn call not recalled in the coda.** Sebesky PDF 11 (grow from the germ) could argue for bringing the horn call (3–6) back before the close. I rejected it for three reasons:

  - The finale relies on the call's D♯4 staying unresolved (IV 219–220).
  - The close already recalls the call's G♯ and its motive-x timpani.
