## Findings

### 1. Bars 65–66: the fugato's third line quotes the second idea's head (applied)

**Problem.** The development (57–72) works only theme A's head. The second idea, heard forte at 45–56 just before the fugato, never returns in combination with theme A anywhere in the movement. The cellos' third line at 65–68 (since c017) is a neutral minim line, E3–D3 | C3–D3 | F3–E3~ | E3, that a listener hears as filler under the subject and countersubject.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 172: in the Jupiter finale "each subject is exposed by itself and then utilized throughout the development section in various combinations with the other themes". The scherzo has exposed two themes and combines only one of them with itself.
- PDF 177: Wagner's three elements "have been exposed separately before this combination occurs. This fact … gives clarity and great excitement". The second idea ends at 56. At 65 it is eight bars old and easy to recognise.
- PDF 175: each element needs "its own characteristic rhythmic makeup". The second idea's head (dotted crotchet, quaver, crotchet tied over the bar line, then a crotchet and a minim leaping up a sixth) is a third rhythm, distinct from the subject's motive x and the countersubject's even crotchets.
- PDF 178, item 2: each element has its own articulation. The quote is legato and marked *espress.* against the staccato subject and the slurred crotchets.
- PDF 184: the principal voice is louder. The quote stays pp under Vln II's p subject.

**What changes.** In A minor the second idea's head (G major 45–46: D6–C6–B5~ | B5–G6, degrees 5–4–3 | 3 up a sixth to 1) becomes E3–D3–C3~ | C3–A3. Bars 67–68 keep c017's F3–E3~ | E3 over the D–E bass, so iv and V and the approach to the stretto are unchanged. I tried continuing the quote into the second idea's bar 3 (7–6, G♯3–F3) and rejected it: over the D bass and the subject's C5 at 67 it makes D–G♯–A–C, which breaks the passage's iv. Beat by beat at 65–66 the chords are A–E–G (passing), F/A, A minor, F/A, then the chromatic passing B/B♭ as before. There is no C–E–G and no B–D–F–G♯. Vc against Vla moves A3–D4 to F3–C4 (fourth to fifth), and there are no new parallels.

**Fix (sB.ily, sBVc, applied):**
```
old: … | d'4(\pp c' bes | a4 bes g | f4 e d | a,2 e4) | e2( d4 | c2 d4) | f2(\< e4~ | e2.) | …
new: … | d'4(\pp c' bes | a4 bes g | f4 e d | a,2 e4) | e4.(^\markup \italic "espress." d8 c4~ | c4 a2) | f2(\< e4~ | e2.) | …
```
The fugato stays for strings alone (c007/c017/c254/c269/c410). The cello line gets one quoted bar and one changed note, and the dynamics are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 129–130 and 285–286: forte timpani rolls at the hemiola cadences (checked, kept)

PDF 184 asks for percussion rolls that start p and crescendo, so that the roll does not "obliterate" themes being developed and combined. These two rolls start f on B2 under the second idea's hemiola cadence. The passage is a homophonic cadence: every part, including the bass, moves in the hemiola. No developing element lies near the drum's register, and the tune is two to three octaves above it. Adler's warning is about a passage "meant to develop the themes and combine them … not merely to build a climax", and these bars are a cadence. The rolls that do lie under developing material (101, 301, 332) already start p or mf and crescendo. c416 has just reviewed the timpani. Kept.

### 3. Bars 289–296: no second-idea quote in the reprise fugato (checked, kept)

The reprise fugato has only two string entries (Vc 289, Vla 293), and Cl 1's pp crotchets mark the beat. Vln II and Vln I rest until the stretto at 297–298, so their entries there are fresh (c097). A quote at 293 would need one of them, or a wind, and would undo both points. Hearing the combination once in the development is enough. Repeating it in a shortened reprise would weaken the shortening that the form critics protected (c083). Kept.

### 4. Bars 45–56: the homorhythmic second idea (checked, kept)

PDF 179 asks for distinct rhythms between elements. Here the melody, violas, clarinets and bass all move in the hemiola. This is one element harmonised, not three: the horns' slurred dotted minims keep the 3/4 bar against it (c027, c409, c411), and PDF 176 lets a single element carry its own harmony. Finding 1 now gives the second idea a contrapuntal life in the development, which answers the open point (c401, c409: "the second idea is still the least memorable") from the texture side without touching its exposition. Kept.

### 5. Bars 69–72 and 325–339: stretto and coda climax (checked, kept)

The stretto adds one colour per entry (Fl 1+Ob 1, Cl 1, Bsn 1+Hn 1·2) and then stops on the ff A-major chord. This follows PDF 176 (a new colour for each entry) and PDF 181 (all motion ceases on the climax chord). The coda adds instruments to each element as the peak nears (PDF 184), then turns homophonic at 333–339 and stops on the G.P. (PDF 176, 181). Kept.
