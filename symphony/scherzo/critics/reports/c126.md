## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 300→301: the winds and horns go from a full bar of ff, in their high registers, straight into the p of 301. Applied.
**Problem.** At 300 (the stretto's A-major arrival, ff), Fl 1 E6, Fl 2 A5, Ob 1 C♯6, Ob 2 A5, Cl 1 C♯6, Cl 2 A4, Bn 2 A2, Cbn A1 and Hn 1·2 C♯5/A4 all hold a dotted minim ff. On the downbeat of 301 they all enter p, on new notes, to begin the four-bar crescendo of the hemiola V7. They have no rest, so no time to breathe or to reset the embouchure. For Ob 1 this sits inside 16 bars without a rest (297-312, 10.3 s): ff C♯6, sub. p D♯5 held four bars with a crescendo to ff, then theme A ff staccato from 305. Bn 1 already ends 300 on beat 2 (c086, "as at 72") and the strings hold beat 3. The other winds do not. In the exposition the same join does not occur: the winds diminuendo at 72 and rest before the p entry at 101.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 198: on the oboe "the breath control required (holding the air in and letting it out very slowly until the next breathing point) makes frequent rest periods mandatory". PDF 175: the oboist "often has to exhale instead of inhale". PDF 188: "the flute requires a great deal of breath ... give the flutist time to rest". PDF 174: "Intonation, dynamics ... are much harder to control in extreme registers ... more true of the highest". A clean p attack on the downbeat right after a held ff C♯6 is the hardest combination in that list. Adler's sfp "envelopes" (PDF 178) work on one note and one breath. They do not help with a new note that needs a new breath.
**Fix (dB.ily, applied).** The ff note becomes a minim and a crotchet rest, as Bn 1 already has: `e'''2->\ff r4` (Fl 1), `a''2->\ff r4` (Fl 2, Ob 2), `cis'''2->\ff r4` (Ob 1, Cl 1), `a'2->\ff r4` (Cl 2), `a,2\ff r4` (Bn 2), `a,,2\ff r4` (Cbn), `cis''2\ff r4` (Hn 1), `a'2\ff r4` (Hn 2). The strings keep beat 3 (their up-bow, c096) and the sfp at 301 (c038) is unchanged. Each shared staff now has the same rhythm and dynamics in both players. I checked page 10 of the scratch build: the rests print cleanly and nothing moved.

### 2. Bars 335→336: Fl 1 leaps G6→B6 onto the fff peak. Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 1 plays motive x as E6-B5-B5-G6 at 335 and then has to start the movement's only B6, fff, at 336. That is a leap of a sixth and then a third into the flute's top fourth, at Più mosso (crotchet = 288). Vln I doubles the B6 at the unison, so if the flute's B6 cracks or sounds late, the whole peak is heard as a fault.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 186: "above the A all pitches are difficult to produce, and extreme caution must be used in approaching them. The best approach is by scale ... the highest fourth ... most problematical." Range chart PDF 184-185: B6 lies in the "a bit shrill" top segment.
**Fix (coda.ily, applied).** `e'''4-.)\< b''8-. b''-. g'''8( a''' | b'''2.->\fff)`. Fl 1 replaces the last crotchet of the motive with the quavers G6-A6, slurred into B6. A6 is a passing note into V7, of which it is the seventh (Fl 1 holds A6 at 337 anyway). Vln I, Vln II, violas and Ob 2 keep motive x at 335, so the motive is still heard. B6 still sounds only at 336 and only in Vln I and Fl 1. themecheck shows no change to any recorded statement (41/41).

### 3. Bars 233-236: Bn 1's statement of T's head is enclosed by Hn 3 and Hn 4. Applied.
**Problem.** In the codetta T's head descends through three instruments: Ob 1 (225), Cl 1 (229), Bn 1 (233), each at p. Bn 1 plays G♯3-A3-B3-C4-B3, its "sweet, more subdued" middle register (Ex. VII-89). Hn 3 holds E4 pp a third to a sixth above it, and Hn 4 holds E3 pp below. The bassoon line sits inside a horn octave, and horn and bassoon in this range are the closest blend in the orchestra. The descending statements end with the one that is hardest to hear.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 226: "when accompanied, it has a tendency to get swallowed up for it blends incredibly well with other instruments ... care must be taken to mark the dynamics of the accompanying instruments so that they do not overshadow the soloist." The score already does this at 345, where Bn 1's countermelody has violas ppp under it (c078). At 233 the accompaniment is pp, one level under p, and it lies on both sides of the line.
**Fix (tC.ily, applied).** Hn 3 rests 233-236 and enters pp at 237: `R2.*4 | b2.~\pp | ...`. Hn 4 keeps its E3 pedal below the bassoon, and its 237 attack also gets `\pp`, so the shared staff prints one dynamic. E4 is still in the violas and the harp's right hand, so the iv chord at 235 (c042) is complete. The staff shows "4." at 233.
