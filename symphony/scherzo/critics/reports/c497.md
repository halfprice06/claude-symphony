## Findings

### 1. Bar 334: the coda's motive-x ostinato stops for one bar at the climax (applied)

**Problem.** From 325 the coda is built on motive x in every bar: the string stretto 325–328, all four string sections in x at 329–332, the trumpets in x at 332 and 333. At 333 the strings and woodwind take theme A's head, and at 334 they play its tail: a minim G on beats 1–2, then F♯–F quavers. In that bar nothing in the orchestra attacks beat 2. The trumpets and timpani rest, the horns and trombones hold, the bass holds E. The x ostinato then comes back in the trumpets at 335, against theme A's bar 3, which has the same rhythm as x. So at the movement's main climax the trumpets only double the theme's rhythm (333, 335), and the bar where they would be heard as a separate part (334) is empty. Two bars before the fff peak, the drive drops for a bar.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 42 and 44 (Hindemith, m. 192, *): the brass punctuate the theme where it holds or rests, "a most common use of the brasses". The held G at 334 is the only two-beat hold in theme A's head.
- PDF 44 and 47 (Bartók mm. 363–386): in the stretto Adler calls "especially thrilling", each brass voice goes on after it enters, so in every bar one voice holds while the next moves. Here the held voice at 334 is the tail and the moving voice should be the x that has run since 325.
- Motive x has the right rhythm for this. Its two quavers fall on beat 2, where the tail holds. Its last crotchet falls on beat 3, where the tail moves in quavers. The two rhythms do not coincide.
- The tuttis at 105–120/305–320 keep the head/tail alternation (trumpets on odd bars). The coda can differ because it has had x every bar for eight bars. The trumpets carrying it on to 336 makes the coda grow out of the tutti rather than repeat it.

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily).** The trumpets play x at 334 on the pitches Hn 1·2 already hold (B4/G4). They stay f, with no accent and no new dynamic:
```
codaTptOne = { R2.*7 | b'4-.\pp\< b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'4-.\f b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'4-. b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'4-. b'8-. b'-. b'4-. | b'2.-- | ...
codaTptTwo = { R2.*7 | fis'4-.\pp\< fis'8-. fis'-. fis'4-. | e'4-.\f e'8-. e'-. e'4-. | g'4-. g'8-. g'-. g'4-. | e'4-. e'8-. e'-. e'4-. | dis'2.-- | ...
```
Tpt 2 takes G4, not E4, so no E sounds under the tail's F♯–F on beat 3 (c011's reason). The G4 is the tail's own G and Hn 2's held note. The tail itself (G5–F♯5–F5–E5, quoted at pitch by movement I and the finale) does not change. The trumpets sit at B4/G4, two octaves under the top line and inside the held horn chord, so the tail stays on top. Brass layout and dynamics are as c086/c194 set them: horns ff, trumpets f. The trumpets still return with the low brass at 332 (c428, c483) and still hold at 336–338. This stays within the cyclic rule: the trumpets play only motive x and tutti rhythm. The trumpet part loses a one-bar rest and needs no new cue.

### 2. 105–120: Hn 1·2's punctuation at ff against the Hn 3·4 tenor line (considered, not changed)

PDF 43 (Hindemith mm. 173–180) has the subject a level above the short accompanying figures. That suggests f for Hn 1·2's crotchets under the ff tenor line. I did not apply it. c076's rule (Rimsky PDF 41: horns one degree above trumpets and trombones, which are f here) applies to the punctuation as much as to the line. The tenor line is already two horns in unison plus Bsn 1 and cellos, which by PDF 44 balances a trumpet. Hn 1·2 lie above it (G4/E4 against G3–E4), and they rest on beat 2, where the line moves. The line is not covered, so c076 keeps priority.

### 3. 89–100: brass punctuation of the timpani's motive x (checked, kept)

This passage is the closest match to Hindemith m. 192, but c197 and c397's reason still holds. Any soft brass chord here would come before the brass's first entry at 101 (pp<, built by groups, c408) and would end the quiet of the ritmo di tre battute. The pizz. B answers already punctuate the timpani.

### 4. 331: horns entering with motive x to carry the stretto into the brass (built mentally, rejected)

Bartók's horns take the subject after the trumpets (PDF 46). At 331, though, all four string sections already play x in one rhythm. A horn x would double them, which is the fault c397 removed at 325–328. It would also take Hn 3 off its doubling of Cl 1's augmented tail F–E–D♯ (329–332), the one line here that is already in counterpoint with x (augmentation against the motive). Kept.

### 5. 101–104: horns answering the trumpets' x at 102 (rejected)

A horn x at 102 would make a Tpt–Hn–Tpt dialogue. It would also put x on every bar of the ritmo di quattro battute, so the x on bars 1 and 3 of the four-bar group would no longer mark the hypermeter. And it would break the held brass chord under the hemiola, which is what PDF 41 recommends at a climax. Kept.

### 6. Fugato and stretto 57–72, 289–300 (checked, follow)

The entries lie in good registers (Vc G3, Vla D5, Vln II A5). Each voice goes on into the countersubject after its entry. The countersubject moves on beat 2 when the subject holds. The stretto is a bar apart, and the horns enter at 71/299 over short string chords and sustained Bsn 2/Cbsn (rows 3, 4, 9, 12). Inversion (row 7) would invert the motto tail and is excluded by BRIEF. No change.
