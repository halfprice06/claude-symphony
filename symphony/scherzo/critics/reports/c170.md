## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 188: the harp spells III♯'s third B♯ as C♮ (applied)

**Problem.** At 185–192 the trio's middle moves through C♯ minor. Bar 188 is its dominant, G♯ major (G♯–B♯–D♯), over the harp's and basses' G♯. The harp writes `<gis c' dis'>` and prints the pedal change "C♮" under it. Two consequences follow.
- The chord's third looks like ♭6 of E. In this trio, C♮ is the mixture tone that marks the A-minor colour at 202, 235 and 359, and each time it falls to B. At 188 the "C" (C4) never falls to B3: the harp moves up to B4–D♯5–F♯5 at 189 and the bass moves G♯→B. A conductor or player reading the score sees a ♭6 that does not behave like one.
- The same bar is also III♯ of E in A&S's sense, and enharmonically the A♭-major triad, movement II's tonic. The movement spells that pivot pitch as G♯ throughout. Its leading-tone thread is spelt D♯ (BRIEF.md), so this chord's third belongs on the sharp side too. Cl 2 already spells the same chord's third correctly as B♯ at 200 (`bis2.`).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 387, "A note on terminology": a specific passage uses the accidental that occurs in its key. In the same passage, two tones that stand for one degree (G♯/G♮ as 3) are what mixture is made of, so the spelling tells the reader which degree is meant.
- PDF 393 and PDF 395, point 5: secondary mixture III♯ is the major triad on 3. Its third is ♯5 (B♯ in E), not ♭6.
- PDF 388 and PDF 395, point 3: a real ♭6 moves to 5. Bar 188's "C" moves elsewhere, so the notation contradicts the voice leading.

**Fix (applied, sections/tB.ily, `tBHarpR`).**
```
old: r4_\markup \small "C♮" <gis c' dis'>4 <gis c' dis'>4 | r4 <b' dis'' fis''>4 <b' dis'' fis''>4 | r4 <b' dis'' fis''>4_\markup \small "C♯ A♯" <b' dis'' fis''>4 |
new: r4_\markup \small "B♯" <gis bis dis'>4 <gis bis dis'>4 | r4_\markup \small "B♮" <b' dis'' fis''>4 <b' dis'' fis''>4 | r4 <b' dis'' fis''>4_\markup \small "A♯" <b' dis'' fis''>4 |
```
`bis` is B♯3, the same sounding pitch as the old `c'`, so the MIDI is unchanged (I compared it event by event with v170's: identical).

The pedal work is no harder. The B pedal goes sharp during 185–187, where no B sounds, and back to natural in the crotchet rest at the start of 189. That replaces the old C♯→C♮ change in the crotchet rest at 188. The C pedal now stays sharp, so the mark at 190 is only "A♯". I checked the page: the marks print under the harp staff at 188, 189 and 190 without collisions, and the score is still 13 pages.

### 3. Bars 349–356: the last T statement keeps ♮6 (C♯) in its pre-dominant. Considered; no change

**Question.** A&S call ♭6 colouring of IV and ii "frequent" (PDF 395, point 2a). The movement's final cadence (352: IV with C♯6 in T; 353–355: cadential 6/4 to V) could take iv or iiø6/5.

**Decision.** No change. If 352 had C♮, then 358's A♯°7 (with its C♯ in the violas and Bsn 1) would bring ♮6 back after ♭6, before 5. That is the order A&S call poor (PDF 388; PDF 395, point 3: "♭6–♮6–5 is poor"). As written, the line runs C♯ (352, 358) – C (359) – B (360), the good order, and the movement's only ♭6 after 337 is saved for the plagal close. The same reasoning keeps the codetta's first two statements in IV (225–232) before the iv at 235. I record this so later critics do not add mixture earlier in the coda.

### 4. Bars 199–200: B (♮5) in the tune, then B♯ (♯5) in Cl 2. Considered; no change

**Question.** A&S PDF 395, point 6: III♯ is often approached by II6 to avoid the direct ♮5–♯5. At 199 T's B4 (Cl 1, violas, Vln II, beat 3) is followed by Cl 2's B♯3 on 200. That is a split chromatic succession in the inner voices.

**Decision.** No change, for three reasons:
- Point 7 allows it away from the outer voices. Here the outer voices are the call (C♯6 → G♯5) and the bass (F♯ → G♯).
- The B is T's own passing note, which the theme record protects.
- The remedies are worse. II6 (bass A at 199) gives the outer voices a direct octave into 200 (C♯6/A → G♯5/G♯). Dropping B♯ would leave III♯, which is movement II's A♭ major, without its third.

c070 accepted it for the same reason.

### For the finale's rework (information; no action)

The scherzo gives the finale three mixture devices, and the finale already uses all three:
- Its opening takes the scherzo's G♯ as ♭6 of C and resolves it at once (A♭→G, bars 1–2; A&S PDF 388).
- Its 243 uses the scherzo's key as III♯ in the arpeggiation I–III♯–V (PDF 395, point 5b).
- Its 275–276 restates theme A's minor head in major, the "change of mode that varies a repetition" (PDF 392).

The keys of the four movements fall by ♭VI steps: C → A♭ → E (= F♭) → C (PDF 390). The ♮6–♭6–5 close links II (F–F♭–E♭), III (C♯–C–B) and IV (A–A♭–G). The finale does not need T: T's major-mode material would compete with the finale's own C-major themes. Nothing to send.
