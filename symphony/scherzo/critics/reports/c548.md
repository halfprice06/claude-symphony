## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 49–56: the G-major second idea falls back before its cadence (applied)

**Problem.** The second idea in G is 12 bars long: two four-bar groups (45–48, 49–52), then the cadence group (53–56) with c448's swell into the 9–8 at 54–55. In the strings each group has the same shape: f, crescendo to ff (hidden) on the long note in its second bar (G6 at 46, A6 at 50), then diminuendo to f at 48 and 52. Bar 53 restarts at f. The winds do the same one level lower. So the listener hears three identical swells in eleven bars. Nothing in the dynamics says that 49–52 leads anywhere, and the music sags at 51–53, just before the arrival in G. The other two statements are already shaped differently. At 125–129 (E minor) and 281–285 (reprise), the second group is one crescendo to a printed f at the start of the cadence phrase, and only then comes the swell into the 9–8. The G-major statement is the one exception.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 54 and Ex. 3-13: a phrase is a group of bars whose end meets a goal of tonal motion. Beethoven's 8 bars are two four-bar hypermeasures that run to the tonic. Here the tonal goal is G at 55, and the bass already moves toward it (C♯–D at 52–53, c432/c452). The dynamics should move toward it as well. They should not repeat the first group's arch.
- PDF 53: hypermeasures combine into larger units. The first group states the idea, and the second, a step higher, is its continuation. When it closes exactly like the first, the two groups sound like two separate phrases.
- PDF 50: the A6 at 50 is the idea's highest note and is already accented by register. A crescendo through it does not need a separate peak there.
- PDF 51–52 (Ex. 3-8): Mozart introduces his cadential hemiola with a rise to *f*. The new shape does the same: the crescendo arrives at 53, and the hemiola 54–55 is the top of the phrase.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-548/work/sections/sA.ily).**
- Strings (Vln I, Vln II, Vc on the tune). The hidden ff and the `\>` at 50 are removed, and so is the hidden f at 52. The `\<` from 49 now runs to a printed `\ff` at 53 (Vln I `d'''4\ff e'''8( d''' c'''4)`). The c448 swell at 54–55 stays, one level higher: `a''4-\tweak stencil ##f \fff\> g''2-\tweak stencil ##f \ff` (hidden, MIDI only).
- Violas. They follow the tune to 52.2 and then leave it. Their `\mf` at 52.2 is removed, and they reach `\ff` with the other strings at 53 (`g'4\ff g'4--( g'4--)`).
- Winds on the tune (Fl 1, Ob 1, cor anglais). The same shape one level lower: the `\<` from 49 runs to a printed `\f` at 53, and the cadence peak is hidden ff, falling to hidden f.
- Bass (Bsn 1·2 a2, Cbsn, Cb). `\<` at 49 and `\ff` at 53 (`d2.~\ff`). Both bassoons get identical marks on their shared staff.
- Clarinets, horns, trumpets and timpani are unchanged. The horns stay at mf, so the soft-brass rule is untouched. The printed fff still appears only at 336, and hidden fff already exists at 110–111/310–311.
- A `% c548` comment is at the head of sA.ily.

What the listener hears: the second phrase, from the A6 down, keeps growing into the cadence instead of falling back. G major arrives at 55 as the goal of the whole 11 bars, and the drop to the p fugato at 57 is larger. The MIDI velocities rise through 50–55 instead of dipping at 51–53. On page 2 the long hairpins run under 49–52 with ff/f at 53, with no collisions.

### 2. 4–5: the horn call's suspension is not swelled into (considered, not applied)

Since c530 the horn's E4 is lengthened over the bar line, and the pizz. B at 5 makes it a 4–3 suspension (Ex. 3-17a). The `\>` starts at the E4's attack at 4, so the dissonant downbeat of 5 is softer than the consonant attack, unlike every other suspension in the score (c448's rule). I did not apply a swell to 5.1, for two reasons. The note is tied, so the MIDI cannot render it (a velocity is fixed at the attack). And the E4's attack is where the rising sixth arrives: it is the call's longest note and its register peak (PDF 50–51: the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation). In this suspension the dissonance is made by the bass moving under the horn, not by the horn moving. The opening frame also stays as it is.

### 3. 113–116 and 313–316: swell to theme A's C6 in the ff tuttis (considered, not applied)

The soft statements swell to the C6 at 24 and 264. In the tuttis the C6 is long, high and dissonant, so it is accented three times by the music itself (PDF 50). c538's reasons stand: a listener would barely hear the swell at ff, and the pinned page layout is at risk. This is less audible than Finding 1.

### 4. 45, 47, 49, 51: accents on the second idea's syncopated minims (kept)

The minims at 45.3 and 47.3 are syncopations of the PDF 51 kind. Beethoven marks his with *sf* in Ex. 3-6, but that passage is staccato and piano. This line is legato espressivo. c358 and c448 kept it unaccented, and the new crescendo makes the 3/2 audible enough. No change.

### 5. 161–163: T's climax at the same level as its suspensions (kept)

In the first trio statement every swell peaks at mp (156, 158, 160, 162, 166). The A5 at 162 is already the tune's register peak, and c348 put its emphasis on the attack. Making it a level louder would put a dynamic accent on an anticipation, which PDF 56 says is unaccented. No change.

### 6. 89–100: ritmo di tre battute (follows)

The heads enter on each group's first bar (89, 92, 95), and the timpani answer on the third bar. That makes the three-bar hypermeasure audible in the way Ex. 3-12 does with *ten.* and pedalling. No change.
