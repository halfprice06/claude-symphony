## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Tutti theme A, 105–120 and 305–320: the woodwind off-beats crowd the theme's lower octave (economy, tonal balance). Applied.
**Problem.** On beats 2–3 of every bar, Ob 2 and Cl 1 play G4/A4, the same notes as the upper notes of the violas' double stops (`<b g'>`, `<c' a'>`). The register E4–C5 also holds the theme's lower octave (cor anglais and Cl 2, plus Vln II at 305–320), Hn 2's E4, Hn 3's B4 and Tpt 2's E4, and the tenor countermelody (Bsn 1, Hn 4, Vc, G3–E4) climbs into it. That makes eight to ten parts inside one octave. The two reed off-beats add no pitch and no rhythm that the violas and horns do not already play.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12 says every note must make a real difference to the score, or it should be erased. PDF 12–13, Ex. 1: removing the one middle-register line made the remaining voices' motion clearer and lightened the lower-middle register so the important part could dominate. Dvořák 9/iii bars 33–40 leave the woodwinds out of the ff string statement.
**Fix (applied, sC.ily and dC.ily).** Ob 2 plays only beat 2 of the first bar, so the seventh A4 it holds at 104/304 still resolves to G4 in its own voice. Then Ob 1 continues alone ("1."). Cl 1 rests, and Cl 2 continues alone ("2."):
```
sCOboeOne = { \partCombineApart e''4-. b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | \partCombineSoloI g''2->( ...      % rest unchanged
sCOboeTwo = { \partCombineApart r4 g'4-. r4 | \partCombineSoloI R2.*15 | \partCombineAutomatic R2. | R2.*11 | R2.*8 | }
sCClOne   = { \partCombineSoloII R2.*16 | \partCombineAutomatic g'2(\f b'4~ | ...           % 121 on unchanged
sCClTwo   = { \partCombineSoloII e'4-. b8-. b-. e'4-. | ...                                   % notes unchanged
```
The same change is made in dC (305–320). Cl 1's A5 at 104/304 is resolved in its register by Fl 1's G5 (c052), so Cl 1 can rest. The violas keep the off-beat, and Hn 1–3 keep beat 3.

### 2. Retransition, bar 95: Vln II's accompaniment starts above Vln I's theme entry (focus). Applied.
**Problem.** At 95 Vln I enters with theme A's head (E5–B4–B4–E5, p). In the same bar Vln II starts its B7 arpeggio on F♯5 (`fis''4 dis'' b'`), a tone above the theme's first note and at the same dynamic. The background is on top of the focal line for one bar and rubs F♯5 against E5. From 96 on, Vln II lies below (A4–D♯4).
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the arranger must see that the ear goes to the primary element and is not diverted by the background. PDF 14: the background is the secondary level.
**Fix (applied, sB.ily).** Vln II starts the figure in the octave it keeps from 96, one degree softer than the theme:
```
... | b'4-.\pp fis'-. dis'-. | a'4-. fis'-. dis'-. | b'4-. fis'-. dis'-. | a'4-.\< ...
```
(was `fis''4-.\p dis''-. b'-.`). The violas are already pp, and the crescendo from 98 is unchanged.

### 3. Reprise tutti, 313: theme A gets a different bass the fourth time (harmonic variety, the bass line). Applied.
**Problem.** Theme A's bass is note for note the same in all four full statements (13–28, 105–120, 253–268, 305–320). The reprise statement 253 adds a cello line above it, but the bass is unchanged. Bars 313–316 are the climb to the theme's C6, and they repeat iv – i6 – iv – V (A–G–A–B) a fourth time.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19, Ex. 3/3A: avoid harmonising a melody the same way twice, and reach the same resting place by a different route. PDF 20: use a substitution where the phrase needs a lift.
**Fix (applied, dC.ily, 313 only; 113 keeps the exposition's harmony).** Bass F♯ instead of A in Cb, Cbsn and Bsn 2:
```
dCCb:     ... | fis,,4-> r4 r4 | g,,4-> r4 r4 | a,,4-> r4 r4 | b,,4-> r4 r4 | ...
dCCbsn:   ... | fis,,4-> r4 r4 | g,,4-> r4 r4 | a,,4-> r4 r4 | b,,4-> r4 r4 | ...
dCBsnTwo: ... | fis,2. | g,2. | a,2. | b,2. | ...
```
The chord at 313 becomes iiø7 (F♯–A–C–E). The upper parts already supply its notes: violas C4–A4, Hn 1 C5, Hn 2 E4, Hn 3 A4, the countermelody's C4. The bass now rises F♯–G–A–B by step into V, against the theme falling A5–G5–F♯5–E5 at 313–314: contrary motion, as in Ex. 1's lesson. i6 at 314 is a passing chord between two predominants. The resting place, E minor at 317, is unchanged. The trumpets' B4 against C was already there over A, so this change adds no new clash. There is no C–E–G sonority (cmajor.py prints none), and the analyser shows no new outer-voice parallel.

### Considered and not raised
- Trio return 209–224: the cello countermelody is a note-for-note repeat of 169–184. Every alternative I tried was worse. Raising it an octave puts it into Vln II/Cl 1's lower octave of T, which would break principle 9. A canon on T's head clashes with the violas' A3 at 211. The statement already differs in colour (flute and Vln I, horns and bassoons sustaining, arco basses), which meets principle 11.
- Second idea 121–124: Hn 3 holds B4 inside the melody's lower octave. That octave is a doubling, and the primary line is Fl 1/Vln I at B5–E6, so focus is not lost. It is also inside the homorhythmic passage on the keep list.
- Flute heterophony 253–268: it is the theme itself, varied, not a competing counter-line (PDF 14: the background may take the primary level for a time).
