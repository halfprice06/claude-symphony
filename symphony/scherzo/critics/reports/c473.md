## Findings

### 1. 300–304: the reprise reaches its tutti with no trace of the ♭II area it cut. Turn the Italian sixth into V7 of F, Neapolitan sixth at 301–302, V7 at 303–304. Applied.

**Problem.** The exposition goes from the stretto's A major (72) straight to F major (73–88), the movement's one remote area, and reaches V through the Neapolitan (85–89). The reprise cuts that episode (c013) and its stretto goal A major (297–300) goes through the Italian sixth (300, c422) to four bars of V (301–304). The only later reference to ♭II is one beat of ♭II6/4 inside the coda's Più mosso (331, 0.6 s). A listener who heard the 16-bar F-major episode hears nothing of it again until the coda, and then only in passing.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 591, Ex. 32-9 (image checked): Beethoven turns an Italian sixth in G into V7 of A♭ — "a brief tonicization of ♭II in place of an expected" resolution; "the tonic is simply delayed, and not for very long". PDF 592: a remote area "will create tonal conflicts that the composer must somehow work out". PDF 593: ♭VI reaches V7 through ♭II in *Widmung*. PDF 600–601: key areas should project the piece's pitch pairs. The Italian sixth's A♯ is a true enharmonic of B♭ (PDF 588), the seventh of V7 of F; C–E–B♭ resolves to F/A with E→F, B♭→A, C held.

**What it does.** 300 (It6 ff) → 301 F/A sfp < → 303 B7 → 305 ff. In two bars the reprise repeats the exposition's A major → F move (72→73) and then the exposition's own way to V, N6 over A → V (85–89). The hemiola tune C6–A5–F5 | D♯6–B5–A5 (was B5–A5–F♯5 | D♯6–B5–A5) inflects B→C and F♯→F, the movement's own pairs; the F6 at 302 falls a diminished third to D♯6 (♭2→7), and the coda's F6 at 331 now answers it. c422's reason for the Italian sixth ("301 was the movement's only structural V with no chromatic approach") is met more fully: the sixth stays, and V is now approached by ♭II6 as in the exposition. No bars added, no theme touched.

### 2. 52–145: the horn line is Ex. 32-22c. Keep and protect.

Hn 1 B4 (52–55) → B♭4 (56, G major turned minor) → A4 (Hn 2, 72–74, under the A→F common tone) → G4 (Hn 1 on the tonic bars 105–120 and held 141–144) → G♯4 (145). This is Mozart's enlarged motive B–B♭–A–(G♮)–G♯ in K. 542 (also in E, also through G major → G minor, PDF 601–602), and the plain motto tail B–A♯–A–G♯ comes at 205–208 and 357–360. Decision: keep all five steps in the horns; record it with c410's protection of 56.

### 3. 72→73: a textbook common-tone modulation. Keep.

A major → F major, major triads a third apart with the inflection C♯→C in Hn 1 and the common tone A5 on top (Fl 2/Ob 2 at 72, the harp's first melody note at 73) as PDF 587 asks. I considered giving Hn 1 the held A4 (so the horn line would carry every step of Finding 2) and Hn 2 C♯4→C4; that doubles Hn 3's C4 at 73 and loses the top C5 of the soft chord. Rejected.

### 4. 149–152: holding Hn 1's G♯4 into the trio (Ex. 32-3). Considered, rejected.

*Widmung* sustains the melody tone across the change. Here the G♯ is already carried by the harp (G♯4 in the chords at 149 and 151), and Hn 1 held through 150–152 would pre-empt T's first note in the same register, so Cl 1's G♯4 at 153 would no longer be a new start. Keep the four-bar drone before the tune.

### 5. 87–88 poco rit.: keep.

PDF 585 says rhythm can soften an inflection, and the Hammerklavier keeps its confrontation hard. But the F→F♯ here falls at 86→87, inside the closing bars of a soft wind-and-harp episode, and the "a tempo" at 89 restarts the drive with the timpani. Dvořák 9/iii eases the tempo inside its scherzo too (Poco sostenuto 68, in tempo 99). Removing the rit. would gain nothing a listener needs.

### 6. Proportions and length: keep.

278 s (4:38). Scherzo 38%, trio 25%, reprise 18%, coda 16%. Finding 1 adds colour without bars. The fast coda stage (11 s) is short but carries the stretto, the B6 peak and the hemiola cadence; the slow stage (33 s) is the link into the finale and is protected. Nothing in Unit 32 argues for re-lengthening the reprise.
