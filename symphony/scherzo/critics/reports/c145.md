## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Brass get no tempo word or rehearsal letter where Horns 1·2 rest (applied)
**Bars:** 87 (poco rit.), 89 (F, "a tempo (ritmo di tre battute)"), 153 (K, "Trio Un poco meno mosso"), 169 (L).
**Problem:** c055 put the marks on the brass StaffGroup. c059 moved them onto the Horns 1·2 staff, so that they would vanish in systems with no brass. They now also vanish in systems where other brass play but Hn 1·2 are hidden. In the system 76–93 (page 3), Hn 3·4 hold the F-major episode's chord through the poco rit. at 87–88 and into F. In the system 153–172 (page 6), Hn 3·4 hold the trio's drone under K and L. The brass get no mark in either system, although they are the middle choir of the page.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 294: "It is strongly urged that tempo markings be placed at the top of the score and repeated in the space separating each choir." Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) prints the tempo above "4 Horns in F". PDF 299, item 4: "Chaos may result if a rehearsal number ... is omitted because it occurs while an instrument is resting." The same applies to a conductor reading a choir that is playing.
**Fix (score.ly):** the marks go back on the brass StaffGroup (c055), with a callback that does c059's job. In each system it asks whether any brass staff survives `\RemoveEmptyStaves`. If none does, the mark is dropped. If one does, LilyPond's default `move-to-extremal-staff` runs and the mark sits above the top printed brass staff:
```
#(define (c145-choir-mark grob)
  (let* ((sys (ly:grob-system grob))
         (sup (ly:grob-object grob 'side-support-elements))
         (els (if (ly:grob-array? sup) (ly:grob-array->list sup) '()))
         (live (any (lambda (e)
                 (let* ((orig (or (ly:grob-original e) e))
                        (pcs (if (ly:spanner? orig) (ly:spanner-broken-into orig) '()))
                        (pcs (if (null? pcs) (list e) pcs)))
                   (any (lambda (pc)
                          (and (eq? sys (ly:grob-system pc))
                               (let ((v (ly:grob-parent pc Y)))
                                 (and (ly:grob? v)
                                      (begin (ly:hara-kiri-group-spanner::force-hara-kiri-callback v) (grob::is-live? v))))))
                        pcs)))
                 els)))
    (if live
        (ly:side-position-interface::move-to-extremal-staff grob)
        (ly:grob-set-property! grob 'stencil #f))))
choirMarks = \with { \consists "Mark_engraver" \consists "Metronome_mark_engraver" \consists "Staff_collecting_engraver"
  \override RehearsalMark.after-line-breaking = #c145-choir-mark \override MetronomeMark.after-line-breaking = #c145-choir-mark }
...
    \new StaffGroup \with \choirMarks <<      % brass group; the two engravers are removed from the Horns 1·2 staff
```
**Result:** "poco rit." at 87, F with "a tempo (ritmo di tre battute)" at 89, K with "Trio" at 153, and L at 169 now print above Hn 3·4. Every system where Hn 1·2 are printed is unchanged. The brass-free systems at 29, 185 and 253 show no second letter above the flutes (checked at 170 dpi), so c059's result is kept and only its method changes.

### 2. Horns 1–4 and the three trombones are not joined as families (applied)
**Bars:** every system with two horn staves or with Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn. (pages 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10–13).
**Problem:** the brass bracket joins all six brass staves alike. Nothing shows that Hn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 form one section and that Tbn 1·2 and B.tbn. form another. On full pages the conductor tells the horns from the trumpets only by reading the names.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 294: "heavy brackets set off the choirs; and an additional brace clarifies instruments of a family." Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) joins Horns I–II with III–IV, and the three trombones, inside the brass bracket.
**Fix (score.ly):** a nested StaffGroup with a secondary square bracket, the modern form of Adler's brace, around each family:
```
\new StaffGroup \with { systemStartDelimiter = #'SystemStartSquare \override InstrumentName.padding = #1.2 } <<
  \new Staff = "Horns 1·2" ... \new Staff = "Horns 3·4" ... >>
...
\new StaffGroup \with { systemStartDelimiter = #'SystemStartSquare \override InstrumentName.padding = #1.2 } <<
  \new Staff = "Trombones 1·2" ... \new Staff = "Bass trombone" ... >>
```
The padding keeps "Hn. 1·2" and "Tbn. 1·2" clear of the square. A family square disappears when only one of its staves is printed (153–168), so reduced systems are unchanged. The horns now sit a little closer together, and the trumpets a little further from them. The score is still 13 pages.

### 3. Tempo words above the timpani and harp (considered, not applied)
**Bars:** all tempo words and letters.
**Problem:** Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) prints the tempo a fourth time, above "6 Timpani". This score prints it three times: top, brass, strings.
**Test:** I wrapped the timpani group and the harp in an outer StaffGroup with no bracket and with the same `\choirMarks`. It built cleanly in 13 pages. In reduced systems, though, the result was crowded: at 153 "K" and "Trio Un poco meno mosso" printed four times above five staves (Cl, Hn 3·4, Timp, and Vln I after the harp). Adler's example is a full first page. On this score's full pages the timpani sit three staves under the brass mark, and the harp sits three staves above the violins' mark.
**Decision:** no change. Three positions, at the top, above the brass and above the strings, cover the page. A fourth would crowd the reduced systems.
