### Tested and kept (decided; not a request)
1. **Vc 280→281, G2 to C4 (an eleventh, no gap).** c196's leap list missed this skip; the passage is unchanged since v197. G2 is the open G string at the end of a slur, and C4 is the second finger on the A string in first position. The hand stays in first position and the bow changes at the slur break, so this is not one of Adler's three problem cases (PDF 82–83: same string, extreme registers with a full position change, legato). Kept.
2. **Vln I 330→331, A5 to F6 staccato at Più mosso (2. = 96).** This is a shift of a sixth on the E string, from first to fifth position, after a staccato crotchet. Adler's examples of hard skips (III-22 to III-24, PDF 83) are octaves and wider, on one string or legato. A sixth after a detached note is normal section technique, and F6–C6–F6 then lies in one position (C6 on the A string). Kept.
3. **Vln I 272→273 and 276→277 (B3 to A5, B3 to B5).** Both stay in first position and cross from the G to the E string after a staccato crotchet (c196). Kept.
4. **Vla motive x quavers pp at 326–332 (B3/F♯3).** Adler PDF 86 says light bowings are harder on the viola. These are one quaver pair per bar on the G and C strings, between staccato crotchets, with the cellos playing the same figure a fifth lower. The players can bow them off the string near the frog. Kept.
5. **Cb A1 held at 213 (open A string) in the trio's return.** Adler PDF 73–74 is about violins in slow solo lines. On the bass, A1 can also be stopped on the E string, and PDF 73 leaves that choice to the player. No mark needed. Kept.
6. **Vc and Cb tied E 361–364 (pp, 2.9 s).** Adler PDF 108 asks for "change bow at will" only for a lengthy tenuto. At pp under a diminuendo this fits in one bow, and the up-bow at 358 starts the passage in the right half of the bow. Kept; Vln I's "change bow freely" at 369 already covers the movement's one long note.
