## Findings

### 1. Bars 361–364: the horn's E4 is the only voice of the closing chord that does not fade (applied)

- **Problem.** At 361–364 the E-major chord is held pp by Fl 1 (G♯5), Cl 1·2 (E4/B3), Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb. Since c123 every one of them has `\>` from 362 to the release on beat 3 of 364, and in the MIDI the chordFadeMidi steps carry it. Hn 2's E4 (the third under Hn 1's G♯4) holds a flat pp and cuts on the same beat. c123 left it out only because Hn 2 shares a staff with Hn 1, whose G♯ goes on, and a shared staff needs equal simultaneous dynamics. The result is that the fade reaches near-silence everywhere except one horn note, which then stops at pp. On the movement's most exposed page this leaves a bare horn third and then an audible cut-off, just before Hn 3 joins the G♯ at 365. c123's own description ("strings and woodwind, then Hn 2, then Hn 1") does not hold in time, because Hn 2 releases on the same beat as the others. Only its level differs.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 307, Ex. X-17 (Debussy, *Faune* 106–9, image): horns hold overlapping notes ppp/pp, and each held note carries its own diminuendo into its release, so the soft horn sound dissolves rather than stops. PDF 309, Ex. X-24: the soft-tongued horn line follows the hairpins of its phrase. PDF 311–312: horns as a soft "accompaniment choir", and the horn treated as a woodwind "some of the time". At 361–364 the horn is a member of the woodwind chord and should shape with it. PDF 305, Ex. X-11 (Ravel): "p > pp", where the soft horn ends in a diminuendo, not a flat cut.
- **Fix (applied), sections/coda.ily.** The shared-staff reason is answered by moving the E4 to Hn 4, which rests from 340 and is alone on the Hn 3·4 staff at 361–364 (Hn 3 enters at 365). It takes the same hairpin as the chord:

### 2. Stopped-horn echo on the link's G (141–144) or the retransition's G (241): built in thought and rejected

This is the reading's most audible remaining device (Ex. X-19, open then stopped). Stopping Hn 1's G4 at 141–144 and opening on G♯ at 145 would mark the trio's arrival by colour. I rejected it for three reasons. It breaks the mirror with bars 1–8, whose G♯→G turn is open, and the link is recorded as "the intro reversed". The stopped sound is "nasal and sharp" (PDF 308), which is the edge Dan's rule rules out (weight, not bite). And none of the models (Dvořák 9/iii, Brahms, Eroica trio) uses it. The earlier reasons (c149, c316) stand.

### 3. Muted horn drone for the trio (149–168): rejected on playability

Ex. X-17 suggests muted horns for a ppp drone. Hn 3 holds the drone until beat 2 of 168 and plays T with the cellos at 169, so one crotchet rest is not enough time to take the mute out. Muting Hn 4 alone would unbalance the open fifth. No change.

### 4. Hn 1·2 at the trio's peak 219 (mf under the accompaniment's f): kept

Row 20 (the horn carries when loud) could argue for f. The horns stay one level under the bassoons at 209, 219 and 221 (c049, c168), and raising 219 alone would break that plan. No change.
