## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 217–220: T's climb to its peak lacks the cellos. Applied.

**Problem.** Bars 217–220 are T's third phrase in the trio's forte return: C♯–D♯ | E–G♯–A tied | A–G♯–F♯ | C♯–B–A. This is the tune's climb to its highest note, A6 at 218, and the lyrical peak of the movement. It sounds in two octaves:
- Vln I with Fl 1 (from 217) on top;
- Vln II with Cl 1, an octave lower.

The cellos stay on their countermelody (E3–G♯3–A3–F♯3) at 217–220. Bassoon 2 and Horn 4 already double that countermelody note for note (Bsn 2 since c087; Hn 4 since c317, "Hn 4 on the cello countermelody at 217–224"). So the cellos sing T at 169–180 and are then left out of its climax, adding a third player to an inner line.

**Evidence.**
- **PDF 62, Ex. 32 (PDF 210–211, Antar [65]).** Double octaves (Vln I / Vln II / Vla + Vc) "may be used for full cantabile melodies extremely tense in character, and in forte passages for choice". The image shows Vln I, Vln II and the low strings on one line in three octaves, over wind chords and harp.
- **PDF 60–61 (e), Ex. 29–30 (PDF 206–208).** Violins with cellos in octaves are "used in very expressive passages where the 'cellos have to play on the A or D strings", and give "a more resonant tone". At 217–220 the cellos lie at C♯4–A4, on the A string.
- **PDF 58, Ex. 17 (PDF 191–194).** The same Snegourotchka cantabile comes back a third time with the cellos added, "still more powerful in resonance".
- **PDF 54.** "Crossing of parts (violoncellos above the violas …)" is one of the natural means that gives a melody relief. At 217–220 the cellos rise through the violas' off-beat dyads (B3/E4), so the inner register carries the tune rather than a third copy of the countermelody.

**Memorability.** At 177–180 the cellos played this same phrase at these same pitches (C♯4–D♯4–E4–G♯4–A4), and the listener learned the climb in that colour. At the climax the tune now sounds in the voice that first sang it, in three octaves, A4/A5/A6. After the peak the cellos return to the bass for the cadence. Rimsky's Ex. 7 and Ex. 17 describe this kind of growth from statement to statement.

**Answering the recorded reasons.**
- c191 point 5 and c371 Finding 2 rejected double octaves at 209–224 because they "would take the violas' off-beats and the cello countermelody".
  - The violas keep their off-beats.
  - The countermelody stays complete in Bsn 2 (mf<f) and Hn 4 (mp<mf) for these four bars.
  - The cellos leave it only for the one phrase where the tune climbs, and take it back at 221.
- c461 Finding 3 kept the flute's entry at 217 as the growth step. That step now adds a layer at each end at once (Fl 1 on top, cellos below), as in Snegourotchka (PDF 61).
- c436 caps T at three string sections. It is three here (Vln I, Vln II, Vc).
- c531: the cadential hemiola (221–222) and its cello minims are unchanged.
- c218, c318 and c418 (bowing):
  - 216's B2 keeps its own bow, so 217 is an up-bow crescendo.
  - The tied A4 is attacked on beat 3 of 218 with a new (down) bow, as c418 fixed for the violins.
  - 221 is up-bow, 222's B2 is down-bow, and the printed up-bow at 223 stays natural.
  - The basses' two-bar bows are unchanged.

**Dynamics.** The cellos reach f, one level under the violins' ff, because "the 'cello tone prevails" in violins with cellos (PDF 58). They diminuendo to mf at 221, where they were before.

### 4. 193–196: Vln I's T-head sequence (F♯5–C♯6, p<) without a lower octave (kept)

The oct.py scan lists 193–194. The phrase lies at the limit of the soprano range, not in the "very high register" of PDF 59. It is soft, and it is the top part, so it stands out by position (PDF 54). Vln II's rest is c527's choice, to give Cl 1's turns registral room. Kept.

### 5. 253–268: basses divided in octaves, pizz. (kept)

Note II (PDF 62) is about bowed lines on strings that do not correspond. These are single pizzicato downbeats. The cellos are on their arco countermelody, so the basses must supply the 8′ octave. c461 recorded this as a deliberate exception, and that still holds. Kept.

### 6. 21–24, 261–264 and the 197–200 call: high notes without a lower octave (kept)

Theme A's C6 peak keeps Ex. 1's single-section *leggiero* layout (PDF 173–174). At 264 Fl 1 joins it in unison. The 197–200 call is a two-bar figure with Fl 1 and Ob 1 and is protected (c480, c503). Kept.

### 7. The second idea's memorability (open since c401; no note change from this reading)

All three statements use Rimsky's fullest layouts:
- 45: three octaves with wind support (PDF 63);
- 121: violins in octaves;
- 281: violins with cellos in octaves (PDF 60–61).

This chapter teaches how to lay out a melody, not how to build one. It gives no ground for new notes. The point stays open for a figuration or form reading.

### 8. 169–176: Hn 3 in unison with the cellos' T (kept)

Antar [63] (PDF 56) gives a D-string cello cantabile "doubled by the bassoons", so a wind doubling of a low-lying cello tune is Rimsky's own practice. c513's settlement agrees with the reading. Kept.
