## Findings (ranked)

This reading has been applied four times before (c014, c104, c194, c284; c147 and c247 used part of it for texture). The brass is in close order, the horns are a degree louder than the other brass, the wood-wind lies above the trumpets in every tutti, and the bassoon–horn blends follow PDF 108. I looked for the largest improvement the reading supports and found one place where it applies: a sustained one, in the passage that anticipates the motto's tail.

### 1. Bars 207–208: Hn 1's chromatic tail grinds against a B3 in the same horn colour (applied)

**Problem.** At 205–208 Hn 1 (p espress., with Fl 2 an octave above) anticipates the motto tail: G♯4–A4 | B4 | B4–A♯4 | A4, falling to G♯4 at 209. Under it Hn 3·4 hold D♯4/B3 for four bars. At 207 beat 3 Hn 1's A♯4 forms a major seventh with Hn 4's B3. At 208 Hn 1's A4, the seventh of V7, is held for a whole bar a minor seventh above Hn 4's B3. Both dissonances lie inside one horn colour, at the point where the music is quietest and most exposed (pp<p over a timpani roll, no violins). The violas meanwhile double Bsn 1's F♯3. At the real statement of the tail, 357–360, Hn 1 is the only brass instrument and the harmony is in bassoons and strings. So in the anticipation the same line sounds against its own colour, and in the statement it does not.

**Evidence.** PDF 102–103: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour". The diagrams on PDF 103 give the seventh to one family and the root to another. PDF 114 says balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords", and 208 is a held bar. PDF 106 lists 2 Horns + 2 Bassoons in unison as a standard blend, and that is what Hn 4 joins once it moves. PDF 113 says strings and brass are seldom combined. The violas' B3 is a separate harmony note under the horn line, not a doubling of it, so the seventh now lies between strings and horn and the horns keep their own consonant notes.

**Fix (applied).** tB.ily:
- `tBHnFour`, last two bars: `b2.--\< | b2.--\p)` → `fis2.--\< | fis2.--\p)`. Hn 4 moves down a fourth to F♯3, its own note of 203–204, in unison with Bsn 1. It keeps the hairpin and the slur.
- `tBVla`, last two bars: `fis2.\< | fis2.` → `b2.\< | b2.`. The violas take the B3, and their crescendo leads to the B3/E4 off-beats at 209.

The chord at 207–208 is now B1–B2 (Cbsn, Bsn 2, Vc, timpani), F♯3 (Bsn 1 + Hn 4), B3 (violas), D♯4 (Hn 3), A♯4/A4 (Hn 1, with Fl 2 above). The horns hold F♯3–D♯4 and play the line above, with no seventh or second among them. The seventh A4 still resolves to G♯4 at 209, and the B pedal is complete. Bars 205–206 are unchanged, so c172's viola F♯3 under Hn 1's G♯4 at 205 is kept. Also kept: c324's Hn 1 line, c248's timpani roll and c248's horn crescendo to p at 208. No theme, dynamic or rhythm changes.

### Considered and left (my decisions)

- **337, Hn 1 C5 against Hn 4 B3 (♭9 inside the horns, ff).** This is the same rule as Finding 1. Moving Hn 4 to D♯4 would leave the brass at the peak with D♯–F♯–A–C, a rootless diminished seventh, and would take B out of the brass at the movement's single fff. c194, c262 and c284 kept it for that reason. The reading does not give a stronger one: PDF 103 allows a wide, chorale-like chord to leave a register undoubled, but it does not ask for the root to leave the brass. No change.
- **308, 311, 319 (Hn 4 B2 under Hn 3 A3/C4) and 216 (Hn 2 D♯4 under Hn 1's suspended C♯5).** These are one-beat or one-bar suspension and passing dissonances inside running lines. c319 kept them because Hn 4 F♯3 would make octaves with the tune. c262 and c298 kept 216 as a 9–8 suspension, and its resolution B4 follows on beat 2. PDF 114 lets moving parts take precedence ("distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts", PDF 112). No change.
- **Stopped horns (PDF 107, 110).** A pp stopped-horn chord would not break Dan's soft-brass rule, which is about accents and bite. But the reading itself gives no place in this score that needs the colour. The one dark passage, 87–88 (D♯°7 over A), is the protected F-major episode, and none of the models (Dvořák 9/iii, Beethoven 9/ii, Brahms) use it in a scherzo. The c149 and c316 reasons stand. No change.
- **201, Cl 2 C♯4 inside Hn 4 A3 / Hn 3 E4 (PDF 108).** The clarinet's C♯4 resolves its own B♯3 from 200, the leading tone of C♯ minor. Moving it would break that resolution. No change.
- **237–240, the horn chord E3–B3–G♯4 has a sixth gap (PDF 100).** Hn 2 E4 could fill it, but the chord is the trio's frame: the same horn chord as 149, with Hn 1's G♯ on top. It needs to stay bare so that the G♯→G turn at 241 is heard. No change.
- **45–56 and 121–128/277–280, clarinets inside the horns (PDF 108).** Kept since c014 because otherwise the clarinets cross Vln II. I found no new argument. No change.
