## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 119 and 319: the theme's 4–3 appoggiatura sounds against its own resolution (applied)
- **Problem.** The tail of theme A lands on E (Vln I/II, Fl, Ob, C.a., Cl 2: E4, E5, E6) on the downbeat over V7. The E is a 4th over B and resolves to D♯ on beat 2. At the same moment Hn 2 (D♯4, accented ff), Tbn 2 (D♯3) and Tpt 2 (motive x on D♯4) sound the 3rd. The downbeat chord is B–D♯–E–F♯–A: the "eleventh" with its 3rd present, and the tone of resolution sounding a minor 9th below the dissonance in four parts.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 474, Ex. 27-16a: V7 with 4–3, and no 3rd while the 4th sounds. PDF 475: such a dissonance must be clearly heard or it "will create a mess". PDF 469: the resolution tone should not be present in another voice except the bass. The soft statements (27, 271) and all four trio cadences (166, 182, 222, 355) already follow this rule. Beat 1 of 27 is B–E only; 355 has no D♯ on beat 1 (c061). The tuttis are the only exception.
- **Fix.** `sC.ily` bar 119: `sCHnTwo` `dis'4->` → `b4->` (beat 3 keeps `dis'4-.`); `sCTbnTwo` `dis4->` → `b,4->`; `sCTptTwo` `dis'4-. dis'8-. dis'-. dis'4-.` → `fis'4-. fis'8-. fis'-. fis'4-.`. `dC.ily` bar 319: the same changes to `dCHnTwo` and `dCTptTwo` (the trombones rest there). The downbeat now reads B–E–F♯–A, and D♯ arrives with the tune on beat 2 (violas D♯4). Trumpets stay in motive x on a V chord tone. This adjusts c064's pitch choice at 119/319 without undoing its aim of fitting the trumpets to the harmony.

### 2. 126: an inner 4th makes a six-note dominant (applied)
- **Problem.** On beats 2–3 of 126 (second idea in E minor, V after iv6), Hn 2 holds E4 from the iv6. The chord becomes B–C–D♯–E–F♯–A: the ♭9 C (Hn 3 C5, Vla C4, Cl 2 C4), the 7th A4 (Cl 1), and the unresolved 4th E4 placed below the 7th, with its resolution D♯3 (Bsn 1) sounding underneath. The reprise (282) does not have the E; there Hn 2 carries the tune.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 475: an unresolved 4th belongs above the 7th, in the soprano. Below it, it is a mess. PDF 463–464, Ex. 27-2a: the complete V9 in five voices, with the 9th and 7th resolving in parallel 3rds. PDF 465, Ex. 27-4c: a 9th held as a common tone from IV/iv is correct, so the C's stay.
- **Fix.** `sC.ily` bar 126: `sCHnTwo` `e'2.` → `e'4 dis'2`, so Hn 2 takes the leading tone and resolves it to E4 at 127. `sCBsnOne` `e4 dis2)` → `e4 fis2)`, so the leading tone is not doubled and there are no octaves D♯–E with Hn 2. Bar 126 is now a complete V♭9, B–D♯–F♯–A–C. At 127, C5→B4 (Hn 3) and A4→G4 (Cl 1) resolve in parallel 3rds, exactly as in Ex. 27-2.

### 3. 116 and 316: Hn 1 holds the root a semitone under the tutti ♭9 (applied)
- **Problem.** At theme A's ff peak the ♭9 C (C6 in Fl/Ob/Vln I; C5 in Vln II, C.a., Cl 2) is an accented neighbour over V. Hn 1 strikes B4, its resolution tone, accented on the same downbeat, a minor 2nd below the C5. In the soft statement (24, 264) the C6 sits two octaves above the violas' B3, which is the clear spacing the book praises.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 469–470 (Ex. 27-10): a dissonance sounding with its own resolution tone in an inner voice gives "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". Wide spacing keeps the texture clear. PDF 465–466 (Ex. 27-5): this 9th resolves to the octave inside V, so the B should arrive as the resolution, not be anticipated.
- **Fix.** `sC.ily` 116 and `dC.ily` 316: `sCHnOne`/`dCHnOne` `b'4-> r4 b'4-.` → `fis'4-> r4 b'4-.`. Hn 1 doubles Hn 3's F♯4 on beat 1 and returns to B4 on beat 3, after the tune's C→B. The tenor countermelody's B3 (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 4) stays: it is a minor 9th below the C5 and part of an independent line.

### 4. 336–338: the coda's last dominant has no 9th (applied)
- **Problem.** The movement's peak (B6 fff at 336, hemiola 337–338) prolongs a plain V7 for three bars. The motto's C–B (♭6–5, theme A's peak as ♭9–8 over V) is missing from the dominant that ends the scherzo proper.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 466: the 9th "can intensify the dissonant character of the dominant", a sonority "attractive to many composers—especially those of the nineteenth century". PDF 464, Ex. 27-4a: the 9th as upper neighbour, "a new way to harmonize 5–6–5". PDF 465–466, Ex. 27-5: 9–8 without change of chord. PDF 467–468, Ex. 27-7: a 9th may be moved into an inner voice and resolve there.
- **Fix.** `coda.ily` `codaHnOne` bar 337 `b'2.` → `c''2.`, so Hn 1 plays B4–C5–B4 (336–338) and G4 at 339. The C5 lies a minor 3rd above Tpt 1's 7th A4 and a 9th or more above the B's in the bass, so it does not crowd its resolution tone. There is no E or G in bar 337, so no C–E–G forms. This touches c014's kept coda brass (333–338) by one pitch; the scoring and balance c014 kept are unchanged.
