## Findings

No score change. Every rule in the reading holds in v545. Below are the candidate I built and the checks that came closest to a fault, with the decision on each.

**1. Best audible candidate, built and rejected: Vln I's E6 at 223–224 as a natural harmonic (PDF 25).** In this chapter only the harmonic rule changes the sound. c455 has already built and rejected the frame G♯5 as an artificial harmonic. So I looked for a held, high violin note that is a true natural harmonic (E6 is the octave node of the E string) and that comes where the music is thinning out. The best one is the end of T's forte return at 223–224: E6 held for six beats on a diminuendo into the pp at O (225). In /tmp/critic-545/cand I wrote `e'''2.~\harmonic^\markup \small \italic "harm." | e'''2\harmonic\> r4\!` in tCVlnOne. It engraved as Gould asks, with a white diamond and the stem at the side (checked at 400 dpi), 0 warnings and 13 pages. I rejected it because it makes the music worse:
- The note is the goal of the trio's loudest cadence. It starts at f, an octave above Vln II's E5, under the ff chord that c544 has just completed. A natural harmonic cannot hold f, and the top octave would drop out of the balance on the cadence's goal note.
- The glassy pure tone is reserved for the link and the close. The harp's only harmonics are at 149, 151 and 375 (c476). Using it here would weaken the colour of the ending.
- Swapping the stopped E6 for the harmonic mid-tie, only on the diminuendo, would need a "harm." change on a tied note. That is the cumbersome tie that PDF 23 warns against for clefs, and players would not do it in one bow.

**2. Bars 45–55, cellos in tenor clef (c481, new since the last pass). Kept.** The line runs D4–A4. In the bass clef A4 needs three ledger lines and stays there for eleven bars of legato melody. PDF 21 names the tenor clef as the cello's clef for high passages. The clef stands before the barline of 45 and the bass clef before the barline of 56 (PDF 22). The tie at 54–55 ends before the change (PDF 23). Stems follow PDF 27–28, including A3 on the middle line at 54.3–55.1, which takes the down-stem of the beat it is tied from.

**3. Bars 35–36, cellos' F♯4 (open since c459). Decided: stay in the bass clef.** The two bars sit on two ledger lines between bass-clef bars, so a tenor clef would mean two clef changes in four bars. PDF 21 prefers "up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently." This closes c459's open point.

**4. Bar 189, cellos' bass clef after four bars' rest. Kept.** The clef stands before the barline of 189 (600 dpi crop; c365, PDF 22). Staying in tenor clef would have read D♯4–E4 more easily, but the phrase falls to F♯2 at 193, and one clef for the whole phrase keeps the contour visible (PDF 22, "between phrases").

**5. Bars 321–331, Hn 3·4 return to the treble clef at 331 rather than at the page break after 320. Kept.** The rest lasts ten bars, half of the 321–340 system. PDF 23's "return at the end of the system" applies only to rests of more than a system, so PDF 22 governs: the clef goes before the barline of the entry (c155).

**6. New ledger-line notes: Ob 1 E6 at 226, Fl/Vln I at 302. Kept.** Both are three ledger lines in the treble clef, the highest clef these instruments use. PDF 21 accepts this, and octave signs are outside this reading (c385, c475).
