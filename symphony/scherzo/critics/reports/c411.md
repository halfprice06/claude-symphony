## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Second idea, 45, 49, 121, 125, 277, 281: the first half of each phrase is three even minims (APPLIED, theme change)

**Problem.** Bars 1–3 of each phrase are three plain chord-tone minims in the tune: D6–B5–G6 (45–46), E6–C♯6–A6 (49–50), and in E minor B5–G5–E6 and C6–A5–F♯6. In G, D6 and B5 are a skip inside one chord (I to I6), so the line opens as a bare arpeggio. Its only figure, the dotted lead-on (c031), comes at the end of each phrase (48, 52, 124, 128). Nothing at the start of a phrase can be sung back. This is the open point recorded since c289 (c401 and c409 also left it open).

**Evidence.**
- Sebesky PDF 210 (Ex. 277–278): the lead line of a long-value passage must have character. Ex. 277 gets its character from a tie over the bar line and a short figure. Ex. 278's even values are "much less interesting".
- PDF 206 (Ex. 270): a figure that recurs gives the score its shape.
- PDF 220 (question 6): if the melody is weak, find what will overcome the fault.
- PDF 212: when to move a line depends on tempo and harmonic rhythm.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-411/work).** The first minim of each phrase becomes a dotted crotchet plus a passing quaver. The quaver fills the chordal third and leads into the tied note:

```
sA.ily  45-46  sAVlnOne, sAFluteOne  d'''2 b''4~ | b''4 g'''2       ->  d'''4. c'''8 b''4~ | b''4 g'''2
               sAVlnTwo, sAOboeOne   d''2 b'4~ | b'4 g''2           ->  d''4. c''8 b'4~ | b'4 g''2
        49-50  same parts            e'''2 cis'''4~ (8vb e''2 cis''4~) ->  e'''4. d'''8 cis'''4~ (e''4. d''8 cis''4~)
sC.ily  121    sCVlnOne/Two          b''2 g''4~ (8vb)                ->  b''4. a''8 g''4~
        125    sCVlnOne/Two, sCFluteOne, sCOboeOne, sCClOne
                                     c'''2 a''4~ (and octaves)       ->  c'''4. b''8 a''4~
dA.ily  277    dAFluteOne, dAOboeOne b''2 gis''4~ (8vb)              ->  b''4. a''8 gis''4~
        281    dAVlnOne/Two, dAOboeOne, dAClOne, dAClTwo, dAHnTwo, dAVc
                                     c''2 a'4~ (all octaves)         ->  c''4. b'8 a'4~
```

All dynamics, accents, slurs, bowings and "espress." stay where they were. Each changed file has a c411 comment.

Why this keeps what the protections protect:
- **The tie over the bar line** (the composer's condition after c361) is untouched. B5 is still tied into 46, C♯6 into 50, and so on.
- **The hemiola is unchanged.** The tune still attacks on beats 1 and 3 and on beat 2 of the next bar, and the bass, inner parts and harmonic rhythm are unchanged. The only new attack is a quaver on the half-beat after beat 2, the same weak position as the existing lead-on quaver at 48 and 52. It pushes into the hemiola's second minim, so it reinforces that minim instead of striking the downbeat.
- **The rising sixths** B5→G6 and C♯6→A6, the horn-call link that c321 protected, are intact. They now come after a step, D6–C6–B5. The line goes down by step, then leaps up a sixth.
- **The dotted figure** now opens and closes every phrase (45 and 48, 49 and 52). This is the recurrent figure of Ex. 270.

Every new quaver is an unaccented passing tone between two chord tones:
- C6 over G major, D6 over A major, A5 over E minor;
- B5 over A minor (125, 281), A5 over E major (277).

At 125 and 281 every part that carries the tune at any octave moves with it, including Cl 1 and the tenor line (Vc, Cl 2, Hn 2). So no part holds the frame note against the passing quaver an octave away.

Layout: the extra quavers widened the system 37–57, bar 58 slid back onto page 2, and page 3 became over-full (2 warnings). In global.ily, `s2.*16` after mark D became `s2. \break s2.*15`. The result is 13 pages and 0 warnings. The systems are now 19–37 and 38–57 on page 2, and page 3 starts at 58 as in v411.

### 2. A counter-line under the second idea, 45–52 (considered, rejected)

This is the second candidate the reading suggests: the cellos leave the bass and move in crotchets where the tune holds (Ex. 269, 273). I rejected it on the reading's own terms.
- Sebesky gives a counter-melody to a static lead (PDF 207: "fairly static for a bar and a half"). At 2. = 88 the second idea changes note every 0.45 s.
- The horns already re-strike each downbeat (c409), so the 3/4 frame against the hemiola is there.
- Sebesky warns that the device "must not be overdone" (PDF 208). Crotchets at this speed would be a running figure under a broad forte tune.
- c027's reason (the homorhythm makes the hemiola heard) still holds.

No change.

### 4. Backgrounds shadowing a tune (Ex. 279–280)

I tested every part against the tune in each theme statement (Vln I, Cl 1, Vc). Only one kind of span has more than 70 % similar motion at a varying interval: the coda's stretto heterophony, 329–336. It is by design and protected. Under the tunes the backgrounds move mostly in oblique or contrary motion:
- the cellos' countermelody at 209–223 is 25 oblique, 5 contrary and 10 similar out of 40 moves;
- Vln II's arpeggios at 169–183 are 39 oblique, 13 contrary and 8 similar.

Both supply chord tones, as Ex. 280 asks. No change.
