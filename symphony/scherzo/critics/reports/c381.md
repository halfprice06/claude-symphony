## What else I studied

Dvořák 9/iii (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/woods.notesS.ily), the E-major trio. Fl 1 and Ob 1 first play the tune in unison, p, which is Rimsky's Fl+Ob (row 3). When the tune comes back forte, Fl 1+Ob 1 play `cs'8 e e4` over Fl 2+Ob 2 `a8 cs cs4`, the same line in thirds (trio bars 17–24; Mutopia bar numbers 84–91, barNumberCheck TwomS = 99). This is Rimsky's "Fl.+Ob. / Fl.+Ob. 3" (PDF 70). Rimsky's Ex. 68 (The Christmas Night [187], PDF 242) sets a 3/4 dance tune for Ob+Cl in thirds over harp chords and horn rhythm. That texture is close to our trio's.

I wrote two scripts over every part (in /tmp/critic-381/tools/): one finds wind octave doublings and checks them against the natural order; the other finds wind lines moving in parallel thirds or sixths. ## Findings

### 1. Coda 345–348: T's second phrase in sixths, Ob 1 over Cl 1 (applied)

Problem: T appears seven times, and every time as a single line, with octave doublings at most. The coda's return of T (341–356) passes through Hn 3, Ob 1 and the solo violins, but in substance it repeats the trio's lines. The tune is never harmonised in its own melodic rhythm. Dan asked on 2026-10-12 that the themes be allowed to develop. The coda is where T comes back for the last time, so it is the natural place for T to appear in a new form.

Evidence: Rimsky PDF 70 says a melody in sixths is better on two different instruments in normal order (Ob over Cl). Ex. 68 shows the same kind of 3/4 tune over harp in paired winds. Dvořák's own E-major trio puts its tune into thirds when it returns (see above). Ob 1's phrase at 345–348 is p dolce and Cl 1 is free there: it rests from 341 to 360, and c379 rested it at 349–356 so that its pp entry at 361 sounds fresh. The phrase is four bars long, so Cl 1 still rests for twelve bars before 361.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-381/work/sections/coda.ily, codaClOne; Ob 1 and all other parts unchanged):
```
| R2. | R2.*4 | fis'2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" gis'4 | a'4\< d''2-\tweak stencil ##f \mp\> | cis''4.-\tweak stencil ##f \p b'8 a'4 | fis'4 gis'2) | R2.*8 | R2.*4 | e''2.~\pp ...
```
(this replaces `| R2. | R2.*4 | R2.*4 | R2.*8 | R2.*4 | e''2.~\pp`). The line runs a sixth under Ob 1's D5–E5 | F♯5 B5 | A5. G♯5 F♯5 | D♯5 E5. Its rhythm, slur and hairpins match the oboe's. Checked against the harmony: F♯4 is the third of D at 345. A4 and D5 belong to Bm7 at 346. C♯5–B4–A4 are the ninth, root and seventh of B7 at 347, where Bsn 1 has D♯3. At 348, F♯4–G♯4 sits under D♯5–E5 as T's bar-8 appoggiatura figure (V of E). The only perfect interval with the countermelody is the D5/D3 at 346, reached in contrary motion. The outer voices do not change (Ob 1 on top, bass unchanged), and analysis.txt lists the same 7 parallels as v381. The concert F♯4 at 345 and 348 is written A4 for the clarinet in A, a throat note. It lasts a minim at p and moves at once, which is not the sustained throat writing c356 removed. The engraved part is clean: "1." and "dolce" on the clarinet staff, as on the oboe staff.

### 2. T in thirds or sixths inside the trio (examined, not applied)

I tested whether the reading supports harmonising T inside the trio as well, at 161–164 (Cl 1 solo) or at the forte return 209–224. I decided against both. Rimsky PDF 66–67 gives melodies that need variety of expression to a solo instrument of simple timbre. T's first statement in Cl 1 is that case, and harmonising it would take away the trio's intimate opening. At 209–224 the strings carry T in octaves at f–ff, and a pair of winds in thirds below Vln II would be covered (PDF 76: the strings dominate). It would also need a horn or more winds, which c251 and c314 rule out. Keeping the harmonised form for the coda (finding 1) makes it new when it arrives.

### 3. Second idea 45–56, 121–132, 277–288 (no change)

The scoring is Rimsky's Sheherazade disposition (PDF 78): Vln I+Fl 1 on top, Vln II+Ob 1 an octave lower, horns sustaining. The question of the idea's memorability is about its notes, and this chapter is about how a melody is distributed among instruments, not how it is composed. I found nothing in the reading that supports changing the notes, and c361's condition (keep the tie over the bar line) stands. No change.

### 4. 333–334 and 339: Cl 1 an octave above the cor anglais (no change)

The order script flags this as an inversion of PDF 67 (clarinet above oboe). It is not one in sound: Ob 2 plays Cl 1's line in unison, so the octave pair is Ob 2 over C.a., which PDF 68 calls same-branch octaves that "always produce a good effect". The clarinet adds weight inside the oboe line, which PDF 66 allows in tutti. No change.

### 5. Hn 1 motto tail 357–360 (no change)

This is the only chromatic melody given to a brass instrument, and PDF 73 says chromatic melody suits brass less. Rimsky writes that such melodies "may sometimes be allotted to the brass". Here the line is four notes, p espress., and a quotation. PDF 72 says the horn's poetic tone in piano passages allows a wider choice of melody. It is protected for cyclic reasons, and the reading gives no stronger reason against it. No change.

### 6. Coda Hn 3 341–344 alone (no change)

PDF 75 says a wind added to a brass instrument gives a finer legato. I considered putting Bsn 1 or Cl 2 in unison with Hn 3's T phrase. I decided against it: the phrase is p dolce, which is the case PDF 72 gives to the horn alone, and c090/c060 keep the movement's horn statements as single horn lines. Bsn 1's countermelody follows at 345 as a separate colour. No change.
