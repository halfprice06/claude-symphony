# Critic 105: v105, lens 5 (engraving and notation)

Assigned reading: Gould, *Behind Bars*, ch. 1, Beams and Ledger lines (PDF 31–41, printed pp. 17–27), second pass. I read every text page from 31 to 41 in full and viewed every page as an image at 110 dpi. I viewed PDF 35 again at 300 dpi to see where the beams of ledger-line groups sit.

To check every beamed group, not only the ones visible on the page, I added a logging engraver to a scratch build. It writes each beam's staff, voice, bar, end positions, stem x-positions, stem directions and notehead positions: 399 groups in v105. A script tested each group against the rules below. I looked at all 13 pages of v105 at 60 dpi, and at page 4 (110 dpi) and page 9 (200, 400 and 600 dpi), which hold the groups the script flagged.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Ledger-line beams end in the middle of a space, and a repeated figure is sloped: Fl 1 254, 258, 261, 262, 263, 266; Vln I 355 (applied)
**Problem.** c015's callback chooses the beam's start from 1.24/0.76/0.24 spaces and then adds the slope. For a ¼ slope this puts one end at 1.49 spaces, in the middle of the space between the fourth and fifth lines, with a thin white wedge on each side. At 261 the figure A6–G6–A6–G6 was also given a slope.
**Reading.** Gould PDF 34: "Both ends of a slanted beam should be attached to a stave-line ... no beam starts or finishes in the middle of a stave-space" (the "but not" example is exactly this case). PDF 31: the same rule for horizontal beams. PDF 36 (b): a repeated pattern of pitches takes a horizontal beam. PDF 35 sets the ¼ and ½ slopes for ledger groups, and the image there shows long stems with the beam on the second line from the top.
**Evidence.** Beam log v105: 254 (1.24, 1.49), 258 (1.49, 1.24), 261 (1.49, 1.24) for A6–G6–A6–G6, 262 (1.49, 1.24), 263 (1.24, 1.49), 266 (1.24, 1.49), Vln I 355 (1.49, 1.24).
**Fix (score.ly, applied).** Replace the body of `gould-ledger-beam-positions` so that both ends are picked from attached pairs: ¼ slope from `(1.0 . 1.24) (0.76 . 1.0) (0.0 . 0.24) …`, ½ slope from `(0.76 . 1.24) (0.24 . 0.76) …`, horizontal from `1.24 0.76 0.24 …`. The highest pair that leaves every stem at least 3¼ spaces is used. A group of even length with period 2 (`c105-repeated?`) counts as horizontal. Result: 254 (1.0, 1.24), 258 (1.24, 1.0), 261 (1.24, 1.24), 262 (1.24, 1.0), 263 (1.0, 1.24), 266 (1.0, 1.24), Vln I 355 (1.24, 1.0). The inner beam edge stays at or below 1.48, so the top line stays clear (PDF 33).

### 2. Too steep for closely spaced notes, and a short stem: Fl 1 256; theme A beat 1 at 25, 117, 265, 317 (applied)
**Problem.** At 256 (`fis''8( e'' dis'' cis'' b'4)`, stems 2.8 spaces apart) the beam drops 0.82 spaces and the C♯5 stem is 2.74 spaces, inside the stave. The descending scale is drawn as a steep wedge through the lower half of the stave. At 25, 117, 265 and 317 the A5–F♯5 quaver pair, 1.6–1.8 spaces apart, slopes 0.65 in six staves (Vln I, Fl, Ob; and the lower octave in C.a., Cl, Vln II at 117/317).
**Reading.** Gould PDF 34: "Notes spaced very close together horizontally (closer than three spaces) take only a slight angle (¼ or ½ space) regardless of the interval." PDF 35: the shorter stem may be shortened to 3 spaces inside the stave.
**Fix (score.ly, applied).** In the same callback, for groups not beyond the ledger lines: keep LilyPond's placement, but if the stems are less than 3 spaces apart on average and the slope is above 0.55, choose the attached pair (`k`, `k±0.24`) with a slope of 0.2–0.53 in the same direction, nearest LilyPond's ends, with every stem at least 3 spaces. Result: 256 (−1.76, −2.24), slope 0.48 with a 2.98-space shortest stem (checked at 600 dpi: the beam now hangs from the bottom line); 25/117/265/317 (−0.24, −0.76), slope 0.52.

### 3. The trio call's turn is sloped though concave: Ob 1 186; Ob 1 and Vln II 198 (applied)
**Problem.** The turn F♯5–E5–D♯5–E5 (c092's figure) has its inner D♯ nearer the beam than both outer notes, but LilyPond slopes the beam 0.18 spaces (−1.82 → −2.0). Vln I and Fl at 198 (the same figure an octave up, set by the ledger callback) are already horizontal, so the octave doubling shows two different beam shapes.
**Reading.** Gould PDF 36 (c): "The beam is horizontal when ... an inner note is closer to the beam than either of the outer notes. Such a group forms a concave shape."
**Fix (score.ly, applied).** In the callback, a concave group (also a group starting and ending on one note, or a repeated pattern) is made horizontal at the outer of LilyPond's two ends: 186 and 198 now sit at −2.0, centred on the bottom line, so the D♯5 stem is 3.24 spaces (PDF 33: 3¼ for a note on a line).

No other group in the beam log breaks the reading. The other 390 groups are unchanged.
