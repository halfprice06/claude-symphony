## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 251–253: the retransition's melodic line stops on the seventh of V7 (APPLIED)

**Problem.** From 245 the retransition's foreground is Cl 1: theme A's head, then the falling line E5 (247–248, swelled) – D♯5 (249) – B4 (250) – A4 (251–252), over the B pedal with Cl 2 on F♯4. The last thing in the woodwinds before the reprise is Cl 1's A4, the seventh of V7, held for four beats and ending p after a crescendo. At 253 the clarinets are silent. The downbeat is E1 in the basses, B3 in the cellos (a new arco line), and E5 and E6 in the tune. G4 comes only on beat 2, as a pizzicato off-beat in Vln II, which is another colour and another layer. So the line's goal G4 never arrives in the voice that leads to it.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 337, Ex. 20-18: when a figurated melody is harmonised, the reading in which "the 7th of V7 does not resolve" is the incorrect one.
- PDF 332, Ex. 20-8: a descending line can depend on a stepwise arrival at its goal tone.
- PDF 343, review point 3: passing tones are sometimes necessary for melodic continuity.
- The score already follows this rule at the same kind of join. At 20 and 28, Vln II takes G4 on the downbeat (c163). At 105 and 305, Cl 1 resolves the hemiola's seventh to G5 (c092). Bar 253 is the one V7 → i join in the movement where the seventh is held in the foreground and then dropped.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-271/work/sections/dA.ily, with a c271 comment above dAClOne).**
```
253  dAClOne  old: R2. |        new: g'4\pp r4 r4 |
253  dAClTwo  old: R2. |        new: e'4\pp r4 r4 |
```
Cl 1 goes A4→G4 (seventh to third) and Cl 2 goes F♯4→E4 (fifth to root), in the same instruments and register. The downbeat now has a full tonic, and the reprise stays leggiero, since it is one pp crotchet under the strings. pp is one level under the p that ends 252, so the resolution is softer than the dissonance (c098). Nothing else changes: c184's voicing, c268's hairpins at 247–252, Vln II's pizzicato pattern, and the light first bar of theme A at 13 are as before. The clarinets share a staff and have the same rhythm and dynamic here, so they combine as one chord with no solo label (checked on page 9). The voice leading is clean: Cl 1 moves against Vln I (B4→E5) in contrary motion, and Cl 2's F♯4→E4 against the basses' B1→E1 is a fifth into an octave by step, not a parallel. Not a theme change.

### 2. Bars 45–56, 121–132, 277–288: the second idea is close to its own reduction (considered, kept)

Every strong note of the second idea is a chord tone. Its only figuration is the lead-on quavers at 48/52 (anticipations) and 124/128 (an incomplete neighbour and a neighbour). By principle 1 (PDF 328) and the Dvořák comparison, it is the least decorated tune in the movement. I kept it, for three reasons:
- Its chord tones carry the cyclic derivation recorded in themes-scherzo.md: B–G–F♯ and C♯–A–G are the horn call's rising sixth and 7, and the finale was told so.
- An accented dissonance on its minims would need c032's sequential bass changed (c091 reached the same conclusion).
- Its role is the broad hemiola contrast to theme A's figured surface. PDF 330 supports the reading of it as a two-voice line, upper A6–G6–F♯6–E6–D6 over a lower voice, which is a kind of figuration the reading values. No change.

### 3. Bars 221–223: Vc and Bsn 2 B2–C♯3–A2–B2 under T's 4–3 (considered, kept)

The A2 on the downbeat of 222 doubles Bsn 1's seventh A3 an octave below and then rises. Read as an appoggiatura it would break PDF 338 (resolution down unless it is a leading tone). In context it is the lower half of a double-neighbour figure round B2 (B–C♯–A–B), which PDF 334 (Ex. 20-12) calls idiomatic outside chorale style. Bsn 1 still resolves the real seventh, A3→G♯3. No change.

### 4. Bars 73–80: the harp head has no accented dissonance (considered, kept)

It moves in diatonic passing quavers, a reduction of theme A's chromatic bar 2, and every downbeat is a chord tone. That plainness is its character against theme A, and it is protected as the link to the finale's IV (c080). No change.

### 5. The motto tail's chromatic order (considered, kept)

G–F♯–F–E (and A–G♯–G, C–B–B♭, B–A♯–A–G♯) puts the diatonic half step first, the reverse of PDF 339's usual order. The order is the motto's own (movement I, E♭–D–D♭–C) and is protected. The spelling of each chromatic note follows the direction rule. No change.

### 6. T and its coda return (153–168, 341–356): checked, kept

T has an accented passing tone at 156, a leading-tone appoggiatura at 160, the tied anticipation at 162 and the 4–3 at 165–166. These fall on bars 4, 8, 10 and 13–14 of the tune, so each phrase ending has its own dissonance (PDF 336, 338). The A-major coda statement keeps them at 344 and 348. The coda's G♯6–F♯6–E6 at 354 is a passing tone filling a third (PDF 332).
