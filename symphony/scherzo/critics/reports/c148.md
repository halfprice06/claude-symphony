## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 105–120 and 305–320: the basses accent all 32 tutti downbeats (applied)

**Problem.**
- In the ff tutti statements of theme A, the double basses and contrabassoon put ">" on every downbeat crotchet: `e,,4-> r4 r4` sixteen times in sC and sixteen times in dC.
- The tuba and bass trombone double the same notes at 105–120 with tenutos (c124), and the timpani have tenutos at 105 and 108 (c146).
- So the bass line has two articulations at once.
- In the reprise (305–320) the low brass rest (c033), so the accented basses are the whole bass.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 50: the strong beat after the bar line carries the metrical accent without a mark. Composers mark an emphasis that goes against the metre: syncopation, PDF 51, Beethoven's *sf* in Ex. 3-6.
- At 2. = 88 each bar is one beat, so a downbeat accent in every bar is the same as an accent on every beat.
- PDF 53: bars group into hypermeasures, with bars 1 and 3 strong and 2 and 4 weak. Sixteen equal accents make all sixteen bars equally strong and cancel that grouping.
- The weak-bar accents in the melody above are a deliberate counter-stress: theme A's ">" on the long chromatic notes at 106, 110, 116, 118. The bass accents compete with them.
- Dvořák 9/iii's basses in the ff tutti carry no accents. Accents appear only in the hemiola (see above).
- The score's own rule since c124/c146 is weight, not bite, for the downbeat strokes in this tutti.

**Fix (applied).** In sC.ily (`sCCb`, `sCCbsn`) and dC.ily (`dCCb`, `dCCbsn`), every `4-> r4 r4` in the first sixteen bars becomes `4-- r4 r4`, for example `e,,4-- r4 r4 | e,,4-- r4 r4 | ...`. Dynamics are unchanged (ff from 104/304). This matches the tuba and bass trombone tenutos. No earlier fix is undone: c124 changed only the brass, and no critic protected these accents.

### 2. 101–104 and 301–304: the hemiola's syncopated minims are unmarked under twelve restruck downbeats (applied)

**Problem.**
- In the "ritmo di quattro battute" build before each tutti, these parts play the hemiola `b''2 a''4~ | a''4 fis''2 | dis'''2 b''4~ | b''4 a''2`: Vln I, Vln II, violas, cellos, basses, Fl 1, Ob 2, and Cl 1 (101–102) / Cl 2 (103–104).
- Fl 2, Ob 1, Bsn 1·2, Cbsn, Hn 1–4, Tbn 1·2 and the bass trombone hold dotted minims. They restrike them on the downbeats of 102 and 104 while crescendoing to ff. The trumpets strike motive x on 101 and 103, and the timpani roll.
- None of the hemiola notes has a mark. The minims that start on beat 3 of 101 and 103 and are held over the bar line are exactly the syncopations whose attack must be heard as stronger than their continuation. At the bar line their continuation is covered by twelve fresh attacks.
- At 301 the strings' first minim has *sfp*, and nothing after it does.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 51: "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation". A syncopation's conflict with the metre depends on that attack. Beethoven marks it *sf* (Ex. 3-6).
- PDF 52, Ex. 3-8: Mozart marks his hemiola with a dynamic (*f* after *fp*), and the left hand plays it as well.
- Dvořák 9/iii's fff hemiola accents each minim, including the tied syncopation (`e2-> d4->~ | d cs2->`).
- This score already does this at its third structural hemiola, 337–338 (c048: every hemiola minim accented). The two approaches to the tutti theme are the only hemiolas left unmarked.

**Fix (applied).** Accent each hemiola minim, pitches and dynamics unchanged. In sB.ily (101–104):
- `sBVlnOne`: `b''2->\f\< a''4->~ | a''4 fis''2-> | dis'''2-> b''4->~ | b''4 a''2->\ff`
- Vln II, violas, cellos and basses: the same pattern (`b,2->\f\<^\markup \italic "arco" b,4->~ | b,4 b,2-> | b,2-> b,4->~ | b,4 b,2->\ff`).
- Fl 1 and Ob 2: the same, with the hidden ff kept (`a''2->-\tweak stencil ##f \ff`).
- Cl 1 `a'2->\f\< fis'4->~ | fis'4 dis'2->` and Cl 2 `fis'2-> dis'4->~ | dis'4 fis'2->`.

In dB.ily (301–304) the same, keeping the strings' `\sfp\<` on the first minim (no added accent there) and accenting the winds' first minim at `\p\<`.

Brass, timpani and the bowing are untouched (Dan's rule; c085 and c096 concern bowing only). The accents engrave cleanly on pages 4 and 10.

### 3. 153 and 341: the pizzicato bass sits at the solo's level under the soft trio tune (applied)

**Problem.**
- At 153 Cl 1 plays T *p dolce*. The harp is pp (c099), Hn 3·4 pp, timpani pp, but the double basses' pizzicato downbeats are *p*.
- At 341 Hn 1 plays T *p dolce* over harp pp and violas pp/ppp, but cellos and basses pluck their downbeats at *p*.
- In both places the only accompaniment at the solo's level is the lowest one, struck on every metrical downbeat.

**Evidence.**
- Aldwell & Schachter PDF 50: unusually low notes come across more strongly, and the downbeat already carries the metrical accent. A plucked low E or A on every bar line at the solo's dynamic stresses the bar line under a legato tune whose phrases cross it (T's slurs run four bars).
- c099's protected rule is that the accompaniment is one dynamic level under the tune (Casanelles PDF 271–278). These two bass entries were missed when c099 lowered the harp.
- The basses already restate *p* at 169, under the violins' *mp*, which gives a natural step up for the second statement.
- At 349 the coda bass is already pp.

**Fix (applied).**
- tA.ily `tACb`: first note `e,4\pp`; the `e,4\p` at 169 stays.
- coda.ily: `codaVc` and `codaCb` at 341 become `a,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz."` and `a,,4\pp^\markup \italic "pizz."`.
