## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 329-332: Cl 1 carries theme A's chromatic tail in the clarinet's palest register under the coda's crescendo (applied)

**Problem.** In the Più mosso build, the strings stack motive x from the cellos up (325-328) and crescendo from mf at 329 to ff at 333. The bass falls E-D-C-B. Above that bass, Cl 1 sustains G-F♯-F-E-D♯ in dotted minims at p<. These are theme A's own tail notes (G-F♯-F-E, the motto's chromatic tail on E's degrees), lengthened, and then the leading tone. c032 put this line in tenths with the bass. When c033 turned Vln I upward (G5-A5-F6-D♯6), Cl 1 became the only instrument playing it. It sits at G4-D♯4, which on the A clarinet is written B♭4, A4, A♭4, G4, F♯4: the whole line is in the throat register. That is the one place on the clarinet that Adler calls pale. So the movement's last statement of the tail, just before the ff arrival, is played where the clarinet is weakest, inside a string crescendo in the same octave (violas at F♯3-D4, Vln II at A4-F♯5). The line also leaps a ninth from D♯4 (332) to E5 at 333, so its leading tone does not resolve in its own voice.

**Evidence.** Adler's clarinet register chart (PDF 210, Ex. VII-56) marks written G4-B♭4 "throat tones, rather pale" and the clarino above the break "bright, incisive, expressive". He also says the clarinet can play every dynamic in every register (PDF 210), so moving the line up an octave does not require a louder marking. PDF 185 gives the reason for the register warnings: in a weak register "it is physically impossible for the player to extract more volume", so a crescendo marked there does not happen against an orchestra. PDF 184 states the governing rule: a great orchestrator gives each part to the register where it sounds best. All the clarinet melodies Adler prints lie at or above the break (Tchaikovsky 5/i, Hebrides, Tannhäuser; PDF 211-212). The throat is used for soft fill, and the score uses it that way elsewhere (29, 121, 225, 249).

**Fix (sections/coda.ily, codaClOne), as built.** Cl 1 plays the line an octave higher, with the same rhythm and the same p<:
```
codaClOne = { R2.*4 | g''2.\p\< | fis''2. | f''2 e''4 | dis''2. | e''4-.\ff b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | ...
```
(was `g'2.\p\< | fis'2. | f'2 e'4 | dis'2. |`). Written B♭5-F♯5 is the lower clarino. The tail now sounds at theme A's own pitch, G5-F♯5-F5-E5 (bar 14), drawn out over the falling bass. D♯5 resolves by step to Cl 1's E5 on the ff downbeat of 333. What earlier critics protected here is kept. The tenths with the bass (c032/c033) are still tenths, an octave wider. The F-E-D♯ that fills ♭2's diminished third (c332) is still there, and Hn 3 keeps it at F4-E4-D♯4. F stays on beats 1-2 of 331 (finale IV 272). No note leaves the chord: the line doubles Vln I's G5 at 329.1 and Vln II's F♯5-F5 on the downbeats of 330-331 as one sustained colour. It does not add a new part. Dynamics are unchanged (p<, c050). Cl 2, B.cl. and the bassoons are unchanged. The shared clarinet staff now prints the two players as two voices at 329-332, with one set of dynamics (both p<).

I checked the new line against the outer voices. Vln I stays above Cl 1 everywhere except the second and third beats of 329, where Vln I's motive x dips to E5 under the held G5. analysis.txt is identical to v478's, so there are no new outer-voice parallels. The D♯5-E5 / D♯6-E6 pair with Vln I at 332-333 replaces D♯4-E5 / D♯6-E6, which was already a compound octave to an octave, so nothing new is added there.

## Considered and not changed

- **Bar 7, clarinets dal niente.** Adler's niente attack (PDF 211, Ex. VII-57) is the clarinet's own effect, and 7-8 is a soft clarinet chord (E5/G4) after the horn's held D♯4. But Bsn 1's B3 and Vln I's G5 (c470) attack pp at the same moment, so a niente start in two of the five parts would only blur the turn to minor. That turn should be heard as an event. Cl 2's G4 at 7 stays (c040, c270).
- **233-236, Bsn 1's codetta head.** Adler's warning that an accompanied bassoon gets swallowed (PDF 226) does not apply here: the only accompaniment is a harp chord on the downbeat at E4-B4, above the bassoon's G♯3-C4 (c153), with Hn 3's E3 below.
- **349-356, Fl 1 pp in the third octave with the solo violin.** The brilliant register (PDF 185) is at the top of what can be played pp. "col Vl. solo" already puts it under the violin, and the pairing is protected (c439). No change.
- **Retransition entries 89/92/95 (pp, pp, p).** Tchaikovsky's model (PDF 227) raises each entry by a level. Here the oboe at 92 is in its most prominent register (PDF 198), so its pp already stands one level above the violas' pp. c278 and c378 agree.
- **Codetta relay 225-236 (Ob, Cl, Bsn, each p dolce `<mp>`).** The descent is made by register and by the thinning of the accompaniment from three pulses to one (c153). A graded dynamic descent would leave the bassoon too soft, the instrument PDF 226 says is easiest to swallow.
- **Throat-register pads (29, 37, 121-124, 225-228, 249-251).** These are soft held chord notes, where a pale colour is right for fill.
