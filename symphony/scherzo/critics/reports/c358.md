## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 121–124 and 277–280: the second idea's soft minor statements have no character word. Applied.
**Problem.** At 45 the second idea enters f with "espress." in Vln I and Vln II. At 121 (E minor) the same tune, in the same violins, has only "sub. p", and at 277 its leads, Fl 1 and Ob 1, also have only "sub. p". These statements change everything except the tune: minor instead of major, soft instead of loud, legato after a ff staccato tutti (105–120) or a ff stretto (273–276). The level is marked; the manner is not. A violinist who played "espress." at 45 sees nothing at 121, so the omission reads as deliberate (c258's argument for 349). A soft entry right after a ff tutti, with only "sub. p", is likely to be played timidly and without vibrato.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18): every new entry has its own level and a character word, soft entries included ("p (sweetly)" on the flutes, "(pretty)" on the guitar). Ex. 269 (PDF 205): when a unit returns at a new level, its marks are restated. PDF 13: the ear has to be directed to the primary element. Here that element is a soft lead over clarinets, horn 2 and violas at the same printed level.
**Fix (applied, sC.ily, dA.ily).**
```
sCVlnOne 121:  b''2->\subP\upbow\<(^\markup \italic "espress." g''4~ | …
sCVlnTwo 121:  b'2->\subP\upbow\<(^\markup \italic "espress." g'4~ | …
dAFluteOne 277: b''2->\subP(^\markup \italic "espress." g''4~ | …
dAOboeOne 277:  b'2->\subP(^\markup \italic "espress." g'4~ | …
```
Not added: Vln I/II at 280 (they enter on the up-beat into a crescendo whose goal, f at 285, defines the playing, as at 45–49), and Fl 1/Ob 1 at 125 (pp under the violins, a colour and not the lead; c258's reason at 45).

### 2. Bars 197–200: Cl 1 and Vln II lack the "espress." of the violas they double. Applied.
**Problem.** At the trio's forte (197) T's head is played at the same pitch and level (f, same slurs and diminuendo) by Cl 1, Vln II and the violas. Only the violas have "espress.". c258 kept it that way because 197–200 is a protected layering (c167, c249). That protection covers which instruments play which line. A playing word changes neither, so the reason does not apply. Vln I's and the winds' call above (f, mf) is a different line and stays unmarked.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215): in each doubled sax and brass line, every part has the same accents, hairpins and marks. Ex. 2A (PDF 18): the three flutes in unison have identical marks. The score follows this rule at 189 (C.a. and Vc both "dolce"), 209 (c258) and 349 (c258).
**Fix (applied, tB.ily).**
```
tBClOne  197: gis'2(\f^\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | b'2 e''4 | …
tBVlnTwo 197: gis'2(\f^\markup \italic "espress." a'4 | b'2 e''4) | …
```
The word prints above Vln II's staff, under Vln I's f, and sits clearly on Vln II's notes.

### 3. Bars 105 and 305: Bsn 1 and Hn 3·4 double the cellos' "espress." countermelody without the word. Applied.
**Problem.** In both tuttis the tenor countermelody (G3–B3–E4 …) is played by Vc, Bsn 1 and Hn 3·4 a2 (at 305, Hn 3 only), all at the ff carried from 104/304. Only the cellos have "espress.". Each player reads only their own part, so the bassoon and horns get no word about a line that the score marks as singing.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215), as in finding 2. Dvořák 9/iii marks each doubling part the same way (strings.notesS.ily lines 75, 279).
**Fix (applied, sC.ily, dC.ily).** `g2.(^\markup \italic "espress."` on sCBsnOne, sCHnThree, dCBsnOne, dCHnThree. sCHnFour gets it in the part only (`-\tag #'part`), as its "senza sord., dolce" at 73, so the shared horn staff prints the word once. No dynamic or accent is added: "espress." asks for a legato, singing tone, not a harder attack. That fits Dan's soft-brass note (weight, not bite).

### 4. Bar 183: Fl 1's last answer comes in after six bars' rest with a hairpin but no level. Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 1 answers the cellos' T at 172 (p dolce), 176 and 183–184. At 183 it starts `fis''8(\< …` after six bars' rest, so the flautist's part has a crescendo with no starting level.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18): each entry carries its own level. MOLA PDF 10 and the score's own rule (c268): an entry after a multi-bar rest restates its dynamic.
**Fix (applied, tA.ily).** `r4 fis''8(\p\< gis''8 ais''4 | b''2\>) r4\! |`. The MIDI is unchanged, because the level was already p.
