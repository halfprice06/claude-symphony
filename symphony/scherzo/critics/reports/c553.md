## Findings

### 1. Bars 92–94: the retransition's middle head entry answers on the minor dominant. Applied

**Problem.** The retransition 89–100 passes theme A's head through three entries in three-bar groups over the pizzicato B: violas at 89 (E4), Ob 1 at 92, Vln I at 95. In v553 the oboe starts on E5, the same pitch as the violins at 95. Its tail is A5–G♯5–G5–F♯5. So the three entries are all on E, and two of them are at the same height.

Harmonically the dominant pedal goes straight from a 6/4 colour (89–94) to V with D♯ (95–104). It is never heard as a minor v.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 449 (§8, Ex. 26-8, image): Mozart's goal V "appears first as a minor chord". The minor V "does not lead forcefully to I; before the recapitulation, therefore, the leading tone must appear".
- PDF 457, Points 8: "I–V♭–V♮–I" is a frequent plan in minor.
- PDF 450–451 (§10, Ex. 26-10, image): in a fugal exposition in minor, subject and answer alternate between tonic and minor dominant, and the leading tone is brought in "to prepare for the next entrance in the tonic". The answer in bar 6 is labelled "V (minor)".
- Beethoven 9/ii's ritmo di tre battute, which c019 took as the model for this passage, moves its head entries to new degrees.

**Fix (applied).** sB.ily, `sBOboeOne`, bars 92–94:

```lilypond
% old
e''4-.\pp b'8-. b'-. e''4-. | a''2-\sysLabel "1."->( gis''8 g''8 | fis''4-.) r4 r4 |
% new
b'4-.\pp fis'8-. fis'-. b'4-. | d''2-\sysLabel "1."->( cis''8 c''8 | b'4-.) r4 r4 |
```

A comment in sB.ily records the change and the reasons.

**What a listener hears.** The head now sounds three times at three heights: violas E4 (subject), oboe B4 (answer in B minor, with its tail D5–C♯5–C5–B4), violins E5 (subject). This is the order of a fugal exposition, and the series rises a fifth and then a fourth instead of stopping on one pitch. The dominant is heard first as minor v (D♮ at 93). Two bars later the cor anglais brings D♯ (95) under the violins' tonic entry, and D♯ then holds for ten bars before the return at 105. This is PDF 449's order, and Points 8 in small.

Some further effects of the change:
- The oboe ends on B4 at 94, and the cor anglais starts its arpeggio on the same B4 at 95, so c537's single reed line now hands over on a common note. Before, the oboe ended on F♯5.
- The tail's C♯–C–B over B is 6–♭6–5 in E, the pitch classes of the inner line under the motto tail at 357–360 (c460).
- The C♮ at 93 also looks ahead to the V♭9's C5 at 100 (c532).

**Checks.**
- The oboe stays in its middle register at pp (F♯4–D5).
- Nothing else sounds at 92–94 except the pizzicato B and the timpani's B at 94, so no new parallels are possible.
- Not a recorded theme statement: the record lists Vla 89 and Vln I 95, and themecheck reports 52/52 unchanged.
- parts-cues.json: the cor anglais cue "Ob. 1, 92, 3 bars" still points at a part that sounds in those bars. No line was silenced, moved or added.

### 6. Bar 335: a predominant before the coda's fff dominant. Weighed, not built

**Candidate.** PDF 456 says the returning V is "often preceded by a II or IV". At the coda's climax the bass goes E (333–334) → B (335, a cadential 6/4 under the run to B6) → V (336–338) → i (339), with no predominant. That made it the most audible candidate after finding 1.

**Why not.**
- c552, reading A&S Unit 10 a cycle ago, kept 334→335 because a cadential 6/4 may be approached from the tonic (PDF 160).
- Points 2 is already met in this cadence group by the ♭II at 331 (Neapolitan 6/4 → V 332 → i 333).
- Each version of the change makes a fault:
  - Bass A under the run gives outer-voice octaves A1/A6 → B1/B6 into 336.
  - Bass C repeats the Phrygian C→B of 331→332 four bars later.
  - Either version sets B3 against C or A in c514's protected horn line E4–B3 at 335.
- The climax would also lose the theme's own i–V shape (13–16).

Keep.
