## Findings

### 1. 331: the movement's Neapolitan never returns after bar 86. Make the coda's iv6 a ♭II 6/4 (applied)

**Bars.** In v80, 329–332 is bass E–D–C–B. Bar 331 is iv6 (C–E–A), V follows at 332, then i ff at 333, V7 fff with the B6 at 336, i at 339 and the G.P. at 340.

**Problem.** In this movement ♭2̂ (F♮) is a long-range thread:

- It is the chromatic note of theme A's tail.
- F major is the episode key at 73–88 (A&S Ex. 28-17's I–♭II–V plan: head in E at 13, on ♭II at 73, V at 89).
- It is the scherzo's answer to movement I's D♭.

The thread is dropped after bar 86. The reprise cuts the episode, and the coda reaches its last dominant through the plain Phrygian iv6.

A&S name the place where ♭II belongs (PDF 483): because ♭2̂ cannot go straight to 1̂, ♭II6 is used "at climactic moments, at fermatas, before rhetorical silences". The coda's 329–340 is exactly that: the B6 peak, the cadence, the G.P.

Cyclic evidence:

- Movement I's coda reaches its last V through the Neapolitan sixth (I 171–174, bass C–F–F–G).
- The finale colours its recapitulation with D♭ (IV 163).
- Dvořák 9/iii's coda opens in the Neapolitan region (MCTL harmony.csv, V4/2 of ♭II at the start of the Coda section).

**Fix.** The bass C at 331 and its fall to B are kept (c022, c053). So the chord is the ♭II 6/4, whose bass is "upper neighbor to V" and which is "stable … by the associations evoked by its progression to V" (PDF 490–491, Ex. 28-15, Liszt: ♭II6/4 → V7 at ff). Only E changes to F. sections/coda.ily, bar 331 (seventh bar of each variable):

### 3. For the finale's rework: the scherzo's ♭II is the finale's IV (no change to the scherzo)

A&S PDF 494–495 (Ex. 28-19): the most frequent pivot use of ♭II is "the Neapolitan 6th of a minor key becomes IV6 in the major key a 3rd lower". The symphony makes the same move on the scale of movements: E minor (scherzo) → C major (finale), a major third lower. The scherzo's F-major episode (73–88, harp head and the wind choir without strings) is therefore the only key area in the scherzo that is diatonic to the finale's C major, where it is IV.

The finale already closes plagally (I – F♯°7 – iv6/4 – I, IV 273–275), which completes the chain of closes from II and III. So I do not ask the finale to add a quotation. The decision for the scherzo: keep the episode in F major and keep its harp head, because it is the hinge between the scherzo's ♭2̂ and the finale's subdominant. A later change that moves the episode to another key would cut that link and must be reported to the finale thread.
