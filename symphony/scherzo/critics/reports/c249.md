# Critic 249: v249, lens 9 (holistic), master: Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 6 in B minor, iii (Allegro molto vivace); comparison with v239

Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, examples (rimsky-principles PDF 441–504), third pass. In this scan PDF 441 starts with Ex. 275, not Ex. 261. These pages hold Ex. 275–312 (PDF 441–503). PDF 485 and 504 are blank. Ex. 261–274 are on PDF 427–440, which c239 covered. The text layer of the example pages holds only captions, lyrics and OCR noise. I rendered all 64 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one. The examples have no prose of their own. Rimsky-Korsakov cites them in the end of ch. IV ("Artificial effects", PDF 134) and in ch. V–VI ("Combination of the human voice with orchestra", "Voices", PDF 137–168). I read PDF 133–168 in full as the key to the examples. The principles below come from that text, checked against the printed scores. Most of them are about accompanying a voice. I apply them to this score's principal lines (the trio tune T, the harp and clarinet solos, Hn 1's motto tail) and to its choral-style tutti chords.

## What else I studied

**Tchaikovsky 6/iii.** The corpus has it: reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt3.musicxml, with a form file (annotations/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt3/form/form_analysis.csv: A 1–70, B 71–138, A′ 139–228, B′ 229–315, coda 316–347, in 12/8). I parsed the MusicXML for the bars in which each part plays. The comparison, bar by bar where the two movements share a device:

- **Economy of colour (Rimsky PDF 136).** Tchaikovsky's tuba plays 61–69 and 87–88, then rests to 193. The timpani rest 70–178. The bass drum has one stroke at 69 and nothing more until 221, and the cymbals wait until 238, the march's ff statement. This score holds the trombones back until 101 and rests them from 121 to 332 (c244). The tuba plays 105–120 and 333–339, and fff is used once, at 336. The method is the same. The cyclic ban on cymbals removes Tchaikovsky's last layer, and the soft-brass rule keeps the brass weight without its bite.
- **Accumulation.** Tchaikovsky plants the march head in the winds from bar 3 and lets it take over. This score plants motive x in the timpani at 9–12, gives it to the trumpets in each tutti, and builds the coda on it (325–339). This is a smaller version of the same plan.
- **Surface motion.** Tchaikovsky's strings keep triplet quavers going almost without a break. Here theme A moves in spiccato quavers over off-beat crotchets, and the second idea and the trio are legato. The brief names Dvořák 9/iii and the Brahms and Bruckner hemiola scherzi as models, and their surface is closer to this score's. This is a choice, not a lack.
- **Soft wind tune over pizzicato.** Tchaikovsky's first march statement (B, from 71) puts the clarinets on the tune over light strings. This is Rimsky's accompaniment method (Ex. 279, 285), and this score uses it at 153 (Cl 1, harp, horn drone, pizz. basses) and at 341–348.
- **Ending.** Tchaikovsky ends with a fortissimo cut-off. This score ends ppp on G♯ for the cyclic link, which is protected.

A conductor who knows the Pathétique scherzo would find the same economy and the same soft-wind-over-pizzicato layouts here. The one thing he would miss is the perpetual motion, and that comes from the models the brief chose. I found nothing that reads as student work.

## Findings

No finding this cycle calls for a change to the score. I checked all 28 principles bar by bar. None is broken outside passages that HISTORY.md protects for reasons I cannot answer. In ranked order, these are the points I examined and decided to leave:

1. **229–232, Vln II's held E5 above Cl 1's statement of T's head (G♯4–C♯5)** (principle 5; PDF 139: harmony in the solo's own register burdens it most; Ex. 277, 279 keep the sustained support below or plucked). I considered Vln II on E4 there (`e'2. | e'2. | e'2. | e'2.` for `e''2.` ×4). I rejected it. E4 lies a third under the solo, in the clarinet's own register. c047 placed the violins above and the violas below precisely to clear a unison and a rub at the solo's pitch. At pp the E5 is the E pedal's upper octave (Rimsky's "duplicated in the higher register", PDF 139), not a moving part. Kept.
2. **197–200, Hn 1 shadowing the T head (G♯4–A4–B4–A4) at the unison with Cl 1, Vln II and the violas** (principle 5; PDF 139: horn and woodwind doubling covers a solo). Here the line is not a solo. It is the lower layer of an f tutti under the Fl 1/Ob 1 call, and Rimsky allows doubling in simpler rhythm in full passages (PDF 145, Ex. 293). The layering is protected (c118, after c039/c091/c094/c107/c117). Kept.
3. **357–363, sustained arco cellos and basses (E2/E1 pp) under Hn 1's motto tail** (principle 5; PDF 139: long double-bass notes under a voice "throb"). Rimsky holds V-c. e C-b. pp under the tenor and chorus himself in Ex. 298 (PDF 482). The passage, its plagal harmony and its scoring are protected (357–376). Kept.
4. **209, Hn 1·2 re-entering p after eight bars' rest** (principle 28, PDF 136). They played 195–200, so the colour is not new. They enter as the soft sustained basis under T's forte return (principles 6 and 11), which is what PDF 138–139 asks of an accompaniment. Kept.
