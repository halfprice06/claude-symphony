## What else I studied

I parsed every brass part with coherence/lyparse.py and listed each player's continuous playing spans (any rest shorter than a bar counted as continuous), with register and share of time sounding. The longest spans are:
- Hn 2 at 101–131: 31 bars, sounding 81% of the time. The ff part, 105–120, is crotchets with rests. The sub. p part, 121–131, is in 4-bar slurs.
- Hn 4 at 101–128: 28 bars, sounding 98%, with breath marks at 104 (c136) and 116 (c046) and slur breaks at 109/110 and 112/113.
- Hn 3·4 at 301–319, with c226's breath at 309/310.
- Hn 1 at 209–223: 15 bars at the trio tempo, p–mf, broken at 216 (c046).

I measured the last G♯ from the `\mt` steps. Hn 1 holds 368–376 for about 11.7 s at ppp under the rit. and fermata, after its breath at 367 under Hn 3's cover (365–369). That is long, but it is soft, and soft playing takes less breath (Adler PDF 292). The passage is protected, and nothing in the reading gives a stronger reason to change it.

Timpani tunings and retunings: G2 is used at 9, 56, 118, 314 and 318, and G♯2 only at 188 and 200, between "muta G in G♯" at 145 and "muta G♯ in G" at 226. Each change has at least 40 bars of rest around it.

I looked at score page 4 (93–110) for the builds at 101–104. I also looked at the Horn 4 part, page 1: senza sord. at 73, the breath at 104, the sub. p at 121, treble clef throughout. The part pages and turns were settled by c315.

One correction to the record: c226's table describes Ex. X-6 as interlocking the four horns. The example (PDF 304, viewed as an image) stacks them I, II, III, IV from the top on two staves. "III between I and II" in the text refers to three-horn scoring. This score's four-horn chords at 101–104, 301–304 and 332–338 follow the four-horn layout, so nothing changes.

## Findings

No finding. I checked the score against each principle in the table bar by bar, and none is violated.

This is the fourth pass on this reading. c046, c136 and c226 already fixed the faults it exposes:
- loud slurs that were too long for one breath (Hn 4 at 305–312);
- the high horn's lack of rest before the ending (Hn 1 at 339–344);
- breaths in the trio and tutti horn lines;
- the tied ff>pp fade at 72–73.

Every brass line now sits within Adler's ranges and in the register he names for its dynamic. Every loud slur is at most five bars. Every pp brass entry is in the middle register, where Adler says all dynamics are manageable (PDF 319). Trombones and tuba move one note per bar or hold, and they rest for all but 31 bars of the movement.

I looked for an improvement the reading supports beyond fixing faults. The two candidates were a soft unmuted low-brass chord (PDF 292) and a stopped-horn echo (PDF 307–308, Ex. X-19). I rejected both:
- A soft trombone and tuba chord in the trio or the close would read as the brass chorale that the finale keeps (cyclic rule in BRIEF.md).
- Stopped horn on Hn 1's G♯→G at 241 would change the timbre of the inflection on which the G♯ = A♭ frame turns (protected 237–245). It would also add a colour that none of the movement's models uses in this place.
