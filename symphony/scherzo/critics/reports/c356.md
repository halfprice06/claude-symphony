## Findings

### 1. Cl 1 holds its weakest throat tone for four soft bars at the end of the exposition (133–136, 321–323). Applied.

Problem: the dying E-minor chord at 133–136 is C.a. B4, Cl 1 G4, Cl 2 E4, Bsn 1 E3, p fading to pp. For A clarinet, concert G4 is written B♭4, the top of the throat register and its thinnest, least stable note. Cl 1 holds it for four bars with tenuto marks and a diminuendo, and it is the most exposed wind note in the chord. The reprise close 321–323 is the same.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 60 (Ex. 66) marks written G4–B♭4 as the throat tones, "somewhat pale, cold". The clarion just above the break is the clarinet's best soft register. PDF 72 (Ex. 90) puts G4 well inside the cor anglais's best range. PDF 106 lists the cor anglais with clarinet as a pairing for soft sustained colour. Swapping the two upper notes keeps the same pitches. Cl 1 moves to written D5 (clarion) and the cor anglais takes the G, which it plays fully. The top note (B4) and the bass do not change, so the outer voices are as before. The earlier move of the cor anglais from B3 to B4 (Adler PDF 198, 205) was about avoiding its low register, and G4 still avoids it.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-356/work):
- sC.ily `sCClOne`: `g'2.--(\p | g'2.-- | g'2.--\> | g'2.--)\!` → `b'2.--(\p | b'2.-- | b'2.--\> | b'2.--)\!`
- sC.ily `sCCorAnglais`: `b'2.--(\p | b'2.-- | b'2.--\> | b'2.--)\!` → `g'2.--(\p | g'2.-- | g'2.--\> | g'2.--)\!`
- dC.ily `dCClOne`: `g'2.--(\p | g'2.-- | g'2.--)\> | R2.\!` → `b'2.--(\p | b'2.-- | b'2--)\> r4 | R2.\!`
- dC.ily `dCCorAnglais`: `b'2.--(\p | b'2.--\> | b'2--) r4\! | R2.` → `g'2.--(\p | g'2.--\> | g'2.--)\! | R2.`

In dC I also swapped the two release points: the B now stops on beat 2 of 323 and the G holds through the bar. My first version kept each player's original rhythm, and that added an outer-voice fifth at 324 (B4–F♯ over E2–B1). With the releases swapped, the top note on beat 3 of 323 is G4 as in v356, and analysis.txt shows the same seven parallels as v356. The Cl 1·2 staff keeps equal simultaneous dynamics (p, then the hairpin from bar 323 in both).

### 2. Cl 1·2 pad in the throat register under theme A (29–39). Not applied.

Problem: under the flute and oboe theme, the clarinets hold thirds at p: G4/E4 (written B♭4/G4) at 29–31, A4/E4 at 33–34, B4/G4 at 37–39. Cl 1 sits on written B♭4 for two bars.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 60, the same throat-tone warning as finding 1.

Decision: keep it. Here the clarinets are a background pad under a melody one octave higher, and the paler tone keeps them behind the tune. Raising them an octave would put them into the flute and oboe line (E5–A5) and blur it. The PDF 60–61 rule of clarinets in thirds is followed. If it ever needs changing, the fix is `sAClOne` 29–31 `b'2.~ | b'4 ...` (Cl 1 to B4, written D5), with the cellos keeping the G. I am not applying it.

### 3. Cl 1 crosses the break in T's first statement (155). Not applied.

Problem: bar 155 is `dis''4. fis'8 a'4~ | a'4\> gis'2`. The falling sixth lands on concert F♯4 (written A4, a throat tone) and then slurs up to A4 (written C5) across the break.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 60 (throat register and break) and PDF 24 (the slur is one breath, so this crossing is legato).

Decision: keep it. The crossing goes upward, at 2. = 76, from a quaver, which every A-clarinet player handles. The falling sixth is T's bar-3 change from c321, which the finale and movement I have been told about. The breath at 160 (c176) is still in place.

### 4. Oboe unisons at f (41–43, 105, 273–275, 305, 334–338). Not applied.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 71 and 80 warn against oboe unisons because of intonation and a hard tone, except when a harsh sound is wanted. Every unison here is at f or ff, inside a tutti, where that edge is wanted. c266 kept them for the same reason, and this reading gives no stronger reason against them.

### 5. Bass trombone and tuba double the root (333–339). Not applied.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 47 (also 88) uses the tuba sparingly and does not double the bass trombone's root. The tuba part here is short and only at f/ff, and the recorded reason ("weight, not bite") fits this one climax. The tuba is used sparingly as a whole, so PDF 47's main concern does not apply.

No other playability faults: ranges, breathing, horn timing, trombone positions and brass endurance all match the reading.
