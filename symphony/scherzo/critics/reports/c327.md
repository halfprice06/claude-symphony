### 1. 89–97: the timpani strike with each head entry instead of answering it (applied)

**Problem.** In the retransition over the B pedal, theme A's head enters in a new colour every three bars: Vla at 89, Ob 1 at 92 and Vln I at 95. The timpani play motive x on B (crotchet, two quavers, crotchet) at 89, 92 and 95, which is the same bar and the same rhythm as each head. So every head is shadowed beat for beat by the drum two octaves and a fourth below. Bars 90–91, 93–94 and 96–97 are left with only the pizzicato B.

The movement's own practice is the other way round. At 20 and 28 (and 260, 268) the timpani answer the theme in its cadence bar. In the second retransition the drum answers Cl 1's head at 247.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13, Beethoven Violin Concerto): "the timpani is very important because it plays the motive that joins the two phrases of the melody".
- PDF 259–260: the accompaniment "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part". Here it duplicates them exactly.
- PDF 257: dialogue sets the solo colour in the listener's mind.
- c019's model agrees. In Beethoven 9/ii's "Ritmo di tre battute" (corpus pp. 13–14, bars 195–210), the drum's motive has a bar to itself (195, 198, 201, 204): winds and strings rest in the timpani bars, and the motive continues in the other two bars of the group. The drum is one voice of the dialogue, not a doubling.

**Fix (sB.ily, `sBTimp`).** The drum moves into the landing bar of each head:
- before: `b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2. | b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2. | b,4\p b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2. | b,4\< …` (89–98)
- after: `R2. | R2. | b,4\pp b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2. | b,4 b,8 b,8 b,4 | R2. | R2. | b,4\p b,8 b,8 b,4 | b,4\< …`

The new order is head (Vla 89–90), drum (91), head (Ob 92–93), drum (94), head (Vln I 95–96), drum (97). From 97 the drum plays every bar, so the answers close up into the build at 98–104.

Unchanged: the three-bar groups (defined by the head entries), the dynamics pp, pp, p < mf, the roll at 101 and the ff stroke at 104. Each head is now heard alone over the pizzicato B, as in Adler's Sibelius and Bartók examples (PDF 268–270).

The second retransition (241–247) is left as it is. Its bass moves to A and C at 243–244, where the B drum cannot answer, and it already answers at 247. This revises c019's placement of the drum, not its presence: c097's "timpani motive x with pizz. at 89–100" still holds. The global.ily comment is updated.

### 2. 199, 211, 343: T's new falling sixth lands on a held accompaniment note (applied)

**Problem.** c321 made bar 3 of the trio tune T D♯5–F♯4–B4, a falling sixth; in A at 343 it is G♯4–B3–E4. The accompaniments were voiced for the old bar (D♯5–C♯5–B4), which stayed above them. In three of the five statements, the sixth's goal note is now already sounding, at the same pitch, in a held inner part:
- At 199, Hn 2 holds F♯4 under Cl 1, Vln II and the violas. The tune has no upper octave there, because Vln I, Fl 1 and Ob 1 play the call.
- At 211, Hn 2 and Bsn 1 hold F♯4 under the tune's lower octave (Cl 1, Vln II). c321 noted this unison and left it.
- At 343, the violas' held B3, the only sustained accompaniment, sits under Hn 3's solo. This is the most exposed case.

In each place the leap arrives on a pitch the ear already has, in a blending colour (horn, bassoon and viola against clarinet or horn). So the theme's new interval, the one thing c321 added, is the part that disappears.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 273: instruments "have a strong tendency to blend when used in certain registers and with acoustically sympathetic orchestral combinations".
- PDF 274: "try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". Cl 1's F♯4 is in its weak throat register.
- PDF 264 (Ex. XVII-9): under a solo horn the other strings take "subsidiary harmonic roles".
- PDF 259–260: the accompaniment can move where the solo holds.

**Fix.**
- **199** (tB.ily, `tBHnTwo`). `fis'2.--` becomes `fis'4-- dis'2--`. Hn 2 moves on beat 2, while the tune holds D♯5, to the D♯4 it already held at 200. D♯ is the root of the iiø6/5 here (c322), not a leading tone. Hn 2 reaches the octave by oblique motion rather than from E4, so it makes no octaves with the tune's E5–D♯5 at 198–199. The F♯4 quaver now enters a third above the horn. I tried the alternatives, and each one either doubles the seventh C♯ or moves E–D♯ in octaves with the tune.
- **211** (tC.ily). `tCHnTwo` `fis'2.--)` becomes `fis'4-- b2--)`. `tCBsnOne` `fis'2.-- | e'2.--` becomes `fis'4-- b2-- | b2.--`. The pair steps down to the root B3 on beat 2. D♯4 is ruled out because the violas hold the leading tone (c247). Bsn 1 keeps B3 through 212 so that it does not rise B–E in octaves with the bass, and rejoins E4 at 213. Hn 2 rests on 212 beat 1 as before. The fifth F♯ stays in the cellos.
- **343** (coda.ily, `codaVla`). `b2.` becomes `gis2.`. The violas now move A3–G♯3–A3, a lower neighbour that resolves the leading tone with the harp's G♯3. Hn 3's B3 quaver is no longer in unison with them.

Unchanged: the tune, its rhythm and dynamics, the harmonies, and c322's resolutions of the leading tone and the seventh (harp at 155, violas at 171 and 211, harp at 343).
