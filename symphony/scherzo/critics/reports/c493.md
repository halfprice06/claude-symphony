## Findings

### 1. 354–355: the last authentic cadence resolves a doubled 4th in octaves (applied)

**Problem.** At the cadential 6/4 of the coda's last T statement, two voices hold the 4th, E, over the bass B:
- the violas' E4, held from 351, tied over the bar;
- the tune's E, in Vln I solo, Fl 1 and Vln II (E6/E5), entering as a quaver at the end of 354 and tied into 355.

On beat 2 of 355 both fall to D♯, so the violas move E4–D♯4 in octaves with the tune. Unit 19 §9 (PDF 316) names exactly this case: "composers will sometimes let an inner voice double a 4th that appears in the soprano … One of these 4ths will have to move up; if both resolved down, parallel octaves would result." A side effect is that beat 2 of the V7 has no fifth: it is B–A–D♯ only, with the leading tone in four parts.

**History.** c043 had the inner 4th rising, citing the same page. c403 confirmed it ("354→355: violas E4→F♯4"). c092 protected "coda cadence voicing 354–355". c462 (Units 9–10) then changed the viola to fall with the tune, citing Unit 10 (PDF 153, 160): the 4th resolves down and is not left by an upward step.

**Answer to c462.** Unit 10 states the norm for one 4th. Unit 19 §9 is the passage written for a doubled 4th in free texture, and it requires one of the two to rise. c462's real objection was that c043's viola left the E on the downbeat, before the tune had resolved. The fix keeps c462's tie and changes only beats 2–3.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily, codaVla):**
- old: `<gis e'~>2. | <a~ e'>4 <a dis'>2 | <gis e'>2.`
- new: `<gis e'~>2. | <a~ e'>4 <a~ fis'>4 <a dis'>4 | <gis e'>2.`

The violas' E4 is still tied over the bar (c462). On beat 2 it rises to F♯4 while the tune falls to D♯. On beat 3 it takes D♯4 while the tune takes F♯. That is the voice exchange over a sustained dominant of Unit 19 §7 (PDF 314, Ex. 19-15b; review point 8, PDF 324). The violas then rise D♯4–E4 at 356 against the tune's F♯–E, in contrary motion.

**Result.** Each beat of the V7 is complete (B–A–F♯–D♯, then B–A–D♯–F♯). The leading tone sounds in one voice-line on each beat, and the inner voice moves against the tune.

**Why this is the change to build.** It is the movement's last authentic cadence, in a strings-and-flute pp texture where the violas are exposed. Of the candidates the reading supports, it is the one a listener hears most that breaks no rule or protection; Findings 3–5 give the reasons against the others. The A3 (c462's 6–7) and the 353–354 two-bar 6/4 (c043) are unchanged.

### 2. 165–166: the same octaves at T's first cadence, harp against Cl 1 (applied)

**Problem.** In the first trio statement, the harp's off-beat chord at 165 holds E4 under Cl 1's E5. At 166 the harp plays A3–B3–D♯4 on beat 2, just as Cl 1's tied E5 falls to D♯5. The 4th is doubled and both voices fall in octaves (Unit 19 §9, PDF 316), and the leading tone sounds in both. c151 already took Hn 4's E3 off beat 2 of 166 for a related reason (the drone's E against the resolution).

**Fix (applied, tA.ily, tAHarpR, bar 166):** `r4 <a b dis'>4 <a b dis'>4` → `r4 <a b fis'>4 <a b fis'>4`.
- The harp's E4 rises to F♯4 while Cl 1 resolves.
- The V7 is complete with Hn 3's B3.
- F♯ is already in the harp's pedal setting.

The harp reaches E4 at 167 beat 2, after Cl 1's E5 on the downbeat, so the off-beat pattern makes no new octaves. The other cadences are already correct:
- 181–182: the 4th is only in the T line and its octave doubling.
