## Findings

### 1. Bar 200: the trio's V of C♯ minor gets its prepared seventh (Bsn 1 holds F♯3). The chord is the finale's opening sound at the same pitches. Applied.

**Problem.** The trio's forte call 197–200 runs C♯m – E – N6 (199, D over F♯) – V (200, G♯–B♯–D♯ with the A–G♯ 9–8) – VI (201, A major, p). Bsn 1 doubles the N6's bass F♯3 at 199, then leaps up a sixth to D♯4 at 200, a doubling of Hn 2's D♯4, and moves on to C♯4. So the V at 200 is a plain triad, and the deceptive cadence V–VI is weaker than it needs to be.

The cyclic point is the larger one. With F♯ added, the chord at 200 is G♯–B♯–D♯–F♯. That is the finale's first chord, the German sixth A♭–C–E♭–F♯ at IV 1, with the same bass (G♯1/G♯2) and at the same inner pitches: F♯3, C4 (B♯3), D♯4, and G♯5 on top. The finale enters ff on a sound the symphony has not yet presented. After this change, the scherzo plays it once, f, in its own key as V7 of C♯ minor, and resolves it differently (to A). At IV 1 the same notes resolve as a German sixth to G.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 447–448, Ex. 26-7: Mozart's passing modulation is foreshadowed, "even the applied dominant", by a small figure in the preceding phrase. This is the device that ties a transient area to the goal that follows. PfR 4 (PDF 456): a passing modulation connects two more stable areas. At the symphony scale the scherzo's E is that passing area between II's A♭ and IV's C (row 9). Until now its only anticipation of the finale was the common tone G♯ = A♭.
- PDF 456, Ex. 26-17 and PfR 12: the returning V "often supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly". 200 is the dominant of the trio's middle key.
- PDF 484, Ex. 28-2b (image checked): after ♭II6, "the bass (4) is the best tone to double. This doubling allows one to prepare the 7th of V7." This is Bsn 1's F♯3 at 199. It held, which is the tenor voice of Ex. 28-2b.
- Exercise 3 (PDF 458): a 7 over a bass is either a seventh chord or a suspended 7th. Here the F♯ is a true chordal seventh, prepared by the common tone and resolved down by step (F♯3 → E3, the fifth of VI at 201).

**Why the settled-passage note does not stand against it.** c503 records 197–200 as settled after c480 (N6 at 199), c482 (Hn 1 C♯5 on 198.3), c485 (spacing) and c503 (bass E at 198). None of those reasons is touched. The N6, the bass, the tune (record line trioTcall), the 9–8 at 200 (c082) and every dynamic are unchanged. Only one inner bassoon note changes per bar. The B♯–D♯–F♯–A on beat 1 is the diminished seventh C–E♭–G♭–A, not the reserved B–D–F–A♭. There is no C–E–G (cmajor.py "none"), and a German sixth on A♭ is not on the kept-for-the-finale list.

**Honest size.** The change is one sustained inner note at f. A listener hears the trio's call close on a dominant seventh, a darker and more pointed deceptive cadence into the p A major. The cyclic link only registers for someone who later hears the finale's ff chord at those pitches. I picked this as the change in this reading that a listener would hear most; the other candidates are in Finding 6.
