## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Four shared staves start a system with no player label (applied)
**Bars:** 111 (Hn 3·4, a2; first bar of page 5), 131 (Hn 1·2, Hn 2 alone; Hn 3·4, Hn 3 alone; first bar of page 6), 214 (Cl 1·2, Cl 1 alone; first bar of page 8).
**Problem:** the part combiner prints "1.", "2.", "3." or "a2" only where the state changes. At 111 Hn 3 and Hn 4 have been in unison since 105, labelled "a2" on page 4; page 5 shows one unlabelled line, in the tutti where the tenor countermelody's weight depends on both horns playing. At 131 Hn 1 has rested since 121 and Hn 4 since 129. Page 6 opens with one line on each horn staff and nothing to say it is Hn 2 and Hn 3. At 214 Cl 2 has rested since 209; the combiner's "1." stands at 213, now the last bar of page 7, because the system break moved from 213 to 214 after c235. Page 8 shows T's climb in one clarinet line without saying whose.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 295 on reduced scores: "it is imperative that all the instruments must be clearly marked in all systems appearing on a page". PDF 291, item 2: "every instrument appropriately labeled". Ex. XVIII-10a (PDF 301) marks the player ("1.") on the shared staff.
**Fix (applied):** c235's score-only `\sysLabel`, which prints only when its note opens a system, so it costs nothing if the breaks move again:
- sC.ily `sCHnThree`, bar 7: `c'4-\sysLabel "a2" b4 a4`; bar 27: `a'4-\sysLabel "3." g'2--)`.
- sC.ily `sCHnTwo`, bar 27: `fis'4-\sysLabel "2." e'2--)`.
- tC.ily `tCClOne`, bar 6: `cis''4-\sysLabel "1."\< fis''2...`.
Checked at 150 dpi: each label prints above its note and touches nothing.

### 2. Horn 3's last entry after twenty bars' rest has no player label (applied)
**Bars:** 365–369 (Hn 3·4 staff).
**Problem:** Hn 3 enters pp on G♯4 at 365 under Hn 1's held G♯ and the timpani's motive x. The Hn 3·4 staff has been empty since 344, and Hn 3's "3." stands at 341, on the previous page. The entry on the closing page is one unlabelled line, so a conductor reading the last page cannot tell whether it is Hn 3, Hn 4 or both, which matters at pp in the last bars. Hn 1's re-entry two staves above at 369 already has its "1.".
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 301, item 9 and Ex. XVIII-10a: on a shared staff the player is named at the entry. PDF 291, item 3: "the score must contain every detail for every instrument". c135 accepted the same rule for re-entries after rests (MOLA).
**Fix (applied, coda.ily `codaHnThree`):** `R2.*20 | gis'2.~\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "3." |`, the form used for Hn 1 at 357 (`codaHnOne`). This touches the protected closing page 357–376. The reason given there (c214/c215/c245: its spacing and collisions are settled) still holds: no note, system or page moves, and the label clears the staff above and the timpani muta (checked at 150 dpi).

### Considered, not changed
- B6 at 336 without 8va (PDF 300, item 8a). It is the single peak note, five ledger lines, in Fl 1 and Vln I. An 8va for one note would make the movement's top note look like a lower one to a conductor reading the page at speed. Adler's own items are about parts; the parts print it the same way. Kept, as c055, c145 and c235 decided.
- Tempo words above the timpani (Ex. XVIII-1): crowding in the reduced systems (c145's test). Kept.
- Mixed languages for terms ("hard sticks", "near the rim", "étouffez", "muta"). App. A (PDF 312) gives each term in four languages without requiring one. English stick and playing terms are standard in modern scores and the French harp term is the one harpists use. Kept.
- The parts audit's "entry without a dynamic" lines are cue notes (Tpt 1 cue at 97–100, Tbn cues before 101), not real entries. Real entries all carry a dynamic.
