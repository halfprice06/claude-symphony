## Findings (ranked)

### 1. 48→49: c541's new F♯ moves in parallel 5ths with Hn 1 and Cl 1. APPLIED.
**Problem.** c541 changed the second idea's lead-on quaver at 48 beat 3½ from E to F♯ in every octave of the tune: F♯6 (Vln I, Fl 1), F♯5 (Vln II, Ob 1), F♯4 (Vla, Vc, C.a.). It falls to E on the downbeat of 49. Over the same bar line Hn 1 and Cl 1, the sustained inner harmony at mf, fall B4→A4. F♯5–B4 to E5–A4 are real parallel 5ths between Vln II/Ob 1 and Hn 1/Cl 1, adjacent voices a fifth apart. Against Vln I and Fl 1 the same motion is a compound 5th. It happens on the strong downbeat that opens the second phrase, with the tune at f and crescendo, and it is the first chord change after the quaver. Before c541 the tune repeated E (E6→E6), so the horn's B→A was oblique and there were no 5ths. This is the only new pair of parallel perfect intervals in my scan of v542 that is not a doubling.
**Evidence.** PDF 83, Ex. 5-19: parallel perfect 5ths are "wrong". The 5th's "very strong stability and resistance to forward momentum" is why composers from the fifteenth through the nineteenth centuries excluded them. PDF 85, Ex. 5-25/5-26: 5ths are excused only when they arise from octave doubling of voices moving in parallel 4ths, or from fillers. Hn 1/Cl 1 are not a doubling of the tune, and the tune is not a filler. The pair of voices is real: the B4 has sustained since beat 2 of 48, and the A4 holds for four bars (49–52). PDF 82: oblique motion is the next most independent after contrary motion.
**Fix** (sA.ily). Hn 1 and Cl 1 tie the B4 into 49 and resolve to A4 on beat 2, a 9–8 suspension over the A bass. A&S Unit 21 covers this, and the second idea already uses its own tied 9–8 at 54–55.
```
sAHnOne … | a'2-- g'4-- | gis'4-- b'2--~ | b'4) a'2--( | a'2.-- | a'2.-- | a'2.--) | …
          (was … | gis'4-- b'2--) | a'2.--( | a'2.-- | a'2.-- | a'2.--) | …)
sAClOne … | a'2( g'4 | gis'4 b'2~ | b'4) a'4( e''4~ | e''4 e''2) | …
          (was … | gis'4 b'2) | a'2( e''4~ | e''4 e''2) | …)
```
The tune now moves F♯→E over a held B (oblique). The B resolves to A under a held E (oblique), so neither motion forms parallels. The suspended B4 is a 9th above Hn 2's A3 and a 5th above the tune's lowest octave E4. Its note of resolution sounds only below it, in the bass and Hn 2, which Unit 21 allows. From beat 2 of 49 the chord is as before, and bar 48 is unchanged. The tune, c541's F♯ and the doublings are kept. After the change par.py reports no hit at 49 and no new hit anywhere. The engraved Hn 1·2 and Cl 1·2 staves show the tie and the new slur start cleanly (page 2).

### 2. 194→195: Cl 1's answering turn ends in octaves with Vln I's resolution. Not changed.
Cl 1's turn (c477) ends C♯5 on the last quaver of 194 and falls to B4 on 195. Vln I's call falls C♯6→B5 at the same bar line, so the two lines move in octaves (PDF 83, Ex. 5-18). The only other endings are A♯4→B4 or a C♯ tied into 195. A♯4 makes a cross-relation with the A3 in Bn 1 and the violas at 194. A tied C♯ sounds against its note of resolution, B5, a 7th above. The answer meets the call on the goal note, and from 195 Cl 1 holds B4 as an octave doubling of Vln I. The number of parts changes there, which is PDF 84's free-texture case. The motion is one quaver into a downbeat at p. I decided to leave it.

### 3. 79→80: Bn 1's seventh B♭3 and the harp's B♭5 resolve to A in octaves. Not changed.
At 79 (C♯°7 over E) Bn 1 holds the seventh B♭3. The harp melody arpeggiates E5–G5–B♭5, which doubles the seventh on beat 3 (PDF 76: the seventh is never doubled). Both then go to A at 80. The harp's B♭5 is a plucked arpeggio note of the protected melody 73–80, and Bn 1 is the voice that carries the resolution. Any other Bn 1 note on beat 3 (G3, C♯4) makes unisons with Hn 4 or Hn 3 into 80. The harp has decayed by the downbeat at pp. I decided not to change it.

### 4. 115→116 and 315→316: Hn 1 leaps a tritone, C5→F♯4. Not changed.
PDF 82 excludes dissonant leaps from simple settings. Here the leap crosses a chord change (A minor → B7) and a rest on beat 2 of the punctuation figure. B4 would sound the resolution of the tune's C6 appoggiatura against it (c082, c511). A4 would put the B7's seventh in Hn 1 a beat before Bn 1 and the cellos reach it, and it would then rise. F♯4 is the best of the available notes.

### 5. 101–104, 304: the leading tone D♯ in three octaves in the tutti V7. Not changed.
D♯3 (Tbn 2), D♯4 (Cl 2, Hn 2, Vla) and D♯5 (Ob 2) sound over the B pedal. PDF 76 forbids doubling the leading tone. PDF 84 allows octave doublings for emphasis when the number of parts changes. The resolving D♯s (Ob 2, Hn 2) go to E, and Tbn 2 and Cl 2 move to F♯ before 105. c362 and c452 accepted this on the same grounds, and I agree.
