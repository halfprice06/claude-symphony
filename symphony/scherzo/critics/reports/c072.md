## Findings

### 1. 101–102 and 301–302: six bars of unchanged V7 before theme A returns (applied)

Problem. From 99 to 104 (and from 301 to 304) the harmony is one B7 chord. The strings arpeggiate it at 99–100, then the whole orchestra holds it in hemiola minims for four bars. The hemiola gives the passage rhythmic drive, but the harmony stands still at the moment that most needs pull towards the tonic.

Evidence. A&S PDF 546 (§16, Ex. 30-23a): "Common-tone diminished sevenths can embellish V. A particularly important possibility is moving to V7, with all the upper tones ascending a half step." Their examples are the Beethoven Violin Concerto and Tchaikovsky 5/iii (Ex. 30-24, a symphonic scherzo-type movement). Principle 6 (PDF 536–537) is met here too. The new chord's F♮ is the ♭2 the movement has already used: theme A's tail G–F♯–F–E and the F-major (Neapolitan) episode 73–88 just before. So the chromatic tone has been heard in a plainer use first. The chord B–D–F–G♯ contains no C–E–G.

Fix (applied in sB.ily and dB.ily; hemiola rhythm, dynamics, trumpet motive x on B and the B pedal unchanged). 101–102 become the common-tone °7 B–D–F–G♯, and every upper tone rises a semitone into the V7 at 103: D→D♯, F→F♯, G♯→A.
- Vln I, Fl 1 (Ob 2 an octave lower): `b''2\f\< gis''4~ | gis''4 d'''2 |` then the existing `dis'''2 b''4~`. The top voice now moves D6→D♯6, chromatically. I did not keep F♯→F♮ in the melody, because F5→D♯6 would be an augmented sixth leap.
- Vln II `f''2 d''4~ | d''4 b'2`; Vla `d'2 f'4~ | f'4 gis'2`.
- Fl 2 `f''2. | f''2.`; Ob 1 `d''2. | d''2.`; Cl 1 `gis'2 f'4~ | f'4 d'2`; Cl 2 `d'2. | d'2.`; Bsn 1 102 `gis2.`.
- Hn 1 `f'2.`, Hn 2 `d'2.`, Hn 4 `f2.` (Hn 3 keeps B); Tpt 2 motive x on `f'` at 101; Tbn 1 `f2.`, Tbn 2 `d2.` (101–102, sB only; the trombones rest in dB).
The same notes go in dB 301–302 with that section's dynamics (`\p`, `\sfp`). Voice leading: B is held, D–F–G♯ rise together, and no voice pair moves in perfect fifths or octaves (B–F d5 → B–F♯ P5 is oblique). At 301 the change from the A-major chord at 300 is also by step: C♯→D, E→F, A→G♯.

### 2. 87–88: the °7 over ♮4 goes straight to V; the °7 on ♯4 is missing (applied)

Problem. The F-major episode closes with N6 over A (84–86) and then D♯–F♯–A–C over A (87–88), which is an apparent °4/3 on ♮4. The bass then moves A→B at 89 with no ♯4 in between. Because 87 and 88 hold the same chord for two bars, under a poco rit., the arrival at the dominant is weaker than it could be.

Evidence. A&S PDF 561 (§8, Ex. 31-14): "An applied °7 on ♯4, one of the most important intensifications of V, is itself often preceded and intensified by another °7 on ♮4. This represents by far the most frequent and characteristic usage of consecutive rising °7s. The first °7 is in 4/3 position and usually comes from a IV or a II in first inversion." PDF 561–562, Ex. 31-15 (Schubert) shows the same °4/3 standing for II6. This passage follows that model exactly, with II6 replaced by the Neapolitan sixth, except that the ♯4 step is missing. The added chord, A♯–C♯–E–G, is the chord of the coda's close (358). This gives that chord an earlier, plain hearing as vii°7 of V, the kind of preparation PDF 536–537 recommends. (It contains C♯–E–G, not C–E–G.)

Fix (applied in sB.ily, bar 88 only; 87 unchanged apart from untying the bass clarinet). Every voice moves by step:
- B. cl. `a,2.` (87, tie removed) | `ais,2.` (88)
- Bsn 1 `cis'2.`; Cl 2 `cis'2.`; Cl 1 `e'2.` (its D♯4 on beat 3 of 87 now rises to E4, and the violas take that E4 at 89 as the first note of theme A's head)
- Hn 3 `g2.`, Hn 4 `e2.` (thirds A/F♯ → G/E)
Voicing: A♯2, E3, G3, C♯4 (twice), E4. A♯, the leading tone of V, and G, the 7th, are each single (Unit 5, PDF 76). C♯ and E are doubled.
