## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Every applicable principle in the table is followed. The six points below are the ones that came closest; I tested each against the reading and decided to keep the score as it is.

### 1. T is never sung by the cello section (153–224, 341–356). Kept.
Adler PDF 99 calls the cello's D string "the most musically captivating" and says the instrument's beauty shows best in passages "featuring the entire section", citing Tristan (Ex. III-59), Brahms 3/iii mm. 1–12 and Eroica i (PDF 100). T goes to Cl 1 (153), Vln I (169), Cl 1 + violas (197), Cl 1 + Vln I (209) and Hn 3 / Ob 1 / Vln I (341–356). A cello statement would be the most direct way, within this reading, to make T stick. But every statement is protected: the four colourings (153, 169, 209, 341–356, HISTORY do-not-touch) and the 197–200 layering (c117, c279). Under each statement the cellos already have their own tenor countermelody on the G and D strings (169, 209, 349), which uses them as the reading's "voice within the contrapuntal texture" (PDF 99). A fifth statement would need new bars, and the trio is 64 bars long and in proportion (c283, c293). Kept.

### 2. The second idea in G sits D6–A6 on the E string at f (45–52). Kept.
PDF 75: the E string is "forceful, even strident, in the higher positions". The second idea climbs B5–G6 and C♯6–A6 in sixths with Fl 1 at the unison and Vln II + Ob 1 an octave lower. This is the brilliance Adler describes, and the movement wants it here: it is the exposition's loudest lyrical idea, after the counterstatement's f on V of G. The two E-minor statements (121, 277) lie a third lower (B5–F♯6) and start subito p, which is the "clear and luminous" soft E string. So the same tune is heard in both of the string's colours. That contrast helps a listener recognise it on its return. Kept.

### 3. At 276→277 the second idea enters with no breath after the counterstatement's B3. Kept.
At 44 and 120 a rest separates the counterstatement from the second idea. At 276 Vln I runs F♯4–D♯4–B3 straight into the B5 accented subito p at 277. c286 settled the playability: both notes are in first position (PDF 82–83). On melodic grounds, the B3 is the root of the half-cadence arpeggio, and the second idea takes the same pitch class two octaves up. The accent and subito p mark the new theme, so it is heard as a new start. A rest would also undo the reprise's compression of this join, which goes back to c003. Kept.

### 4. The slur breaks before T's tied A6/A5 (178, 210, 350; Cl 1 is slurred through). Kept.
`e'''4 gis'''4) a'''4~(` gives the violins a new bow on the phrase's peak. Adler's lyrical examples change bow at the peak or the swell (Ex. III-7, III-38, III-59), and the MIDI swell on the tied A (c238) assumes a fresh bow. The clarinet does not bow, so one slur is right for it. Kept.

### 5. Theme A's E5 on the open string (13, 15, 17 and every statement). Kept.
PDF 73: open strings stand out in slow expressive passages, but "in a fast passage this may not make any difference". Theme A is leggiero spiccato at 2. = 88. Kept, no mark.

### 6. Theme A's chromatic tail at speed (14, 18, 26; coda 334). Kept.
PDF 84 (Ex. III-25/26): chromatic steps are a fingering matter, and half position avoids portamento in fast passages. The tail is G–F♯–F under one slur with the F♯–F in quavers. The player chooses the fingering. No mark.

### Memorability verdict
- **Theme A** is the movement's tune: a four-note head, the motto's chromatic tail, and a peak (C6) in bar 12. Like the main subject of Beethoven 9/ii and the head of Dvořák 9/iii, its rhythm alone identifies it, and the timpani prove that by playing only the rhythm.
- **The second idea** works by sequence (two four-bar units a step apart, then the hemiola cadence), as Brahms's and Bruckner's hemiola themes do.
- **T** has the shape of the great soft trio tunes (Eroica iii, Dvořák 8/iii, Tchaikovsky's waltz-trios): a stepwise rise, peaks rising from E5 to a held F♯5 and then a tied A5, and a 4–3 cadence. It is singable and well paced. It is less striking than theme A because its first phrase is a filled-in horn call with no leap of its own. That is the gap c299 named. I did not rewrite T. Every statement is protected, and the reading (string colour, not melodic invention) gives no basis for changing its notes.
- **The harp head 73–80** and **the coda's shared return of T** stay as they are.
