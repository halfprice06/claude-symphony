## What else I studied

Beethoven 9/ii in the corpus (reference-corpus/pdf/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt2.pdf, p. 2, the fugato): each voice that has finished its entry keeps moving in its own counterpoint while the next enters, and the winds add crotchet chords on the off-beats; the texture grows to five real lines. Mozart 41/iv as printed by Adler (PDF 173–174): the stretto entries overlap one bar apart. Against these, the v17 fugato is a two-voice canon over a bass that turns into a homophonic block. I also checked HISTORY.md: cycle 7 (same lens) made the countersubject crotchets and kept the fugato entries in the strings alone; nothing below undoes that.

## Findings

### 1. The fugato never reaches three real voices (57–68). Applied.
Bars: 65–68. Problem: at 65 Vln II has the subject (A minor), violas the countersubject, and the cellos, the first voice, drop to a,2. a,2. d,2. e,2., a doubling of the double basses an octave up. At no point do three independent lines sound together. Evidence: Adler PDF 172 defines polyphonic orchestral texture as "three or more important elements sounding simultaneously"; PDF 175 says each element needs its own rhythm to be heard. Beethoven 9/ii (corpus p. 2) keeps every finished voice in counterpoint. Fix: the cellos take a third line in minims, a rhythm of its own between motive x and the crotchet countersubject, while the basses keep the bass:
```
sBVc 65-68:  e2( d4 | c2 d4 | f2.\< | e2.) |
```
Vertical check: 65 A–E–A / D minor on beat 3; 66 A minor, F/A, B♭/A passing; 67 D minor 7; 68 E. No parallels against the subject, countersubject or bass (contrary octaves only at 66 beat 1).

### 2. The "stretto" at 69–72 and 301–304 is a homophonic block. Applied.
Bars: 69–72, 301–304. Problem: the plan and the section comment call this a stretto, but Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Cl 1, Vln II and violas play the subject in parallel fourths and thirds in one rhythm, and the cellos repeat a, a a,. No entry overlaps another, and the countersubject vanishes at the climax. Evidence: Adler PDF 175–176 (Jupiter): entries overlap, each new entry gets its own colour (bassoon + cello for the first), pedals are left to non-thematic instruments; PDF 177: elements exposed separately are then combined. Fix: keep the upper block and the A pedal (Bsn 2, Cbsn, Cb), and give the cellos with Bsn 1 (string + woodwind) motive x one bar after Vln I, on E then A, so the head answers itself in stretto in the tenor; the quavers fall on beat 2, where the top line holds, so the two layers complement each other rhythmically:
```
sBVc / dBVc 69-72:   a,4-.\f a-. a-. | e4-. b,8-. b,-. e4-. | a4-. e8-.\< e-. a4-. | a,2.->\ff |
sBBsnOne / dBBsnOne: a2.\f | e4-. b,8-. b,-. e4-. | a4-. e8-.\< e-. a4-. | a2.\ff |
```
The cellos' beat 3 of 69 is now a (A3), not a, (A2), so that A→E at 70 moves against the violas' a'→e'' by contrary motion rather than in octaves. Harmony unchanged: E under A minor, under E7 (beat 3a) and under G minor/E (ii half-diminished of D) at 70; A under F♯ minor, E under E7, A under A at 71.

### 3. The second idea in E minor has a frozen inner voice (121–126, 281–286). Applied.
Bars: 121–126 and 281–286. Problem: the violas double Cl 2 note for note (e'2 e'4~ | e'4 b2 | b2 e'4~ | e'4 e'2 ...), mostly on one pitch, in the same hemiola rhythm as the tune and the bass. Melody, inner voices and bass form one rhythmic layer; only the horns' dotted minims differ. In the G-major statement (45–52) the inner line moves (G–F♯–E–D–C♯–A); in the E-minor transposition it stopped moving. Evidence: Adler PDF 175 (each element its own rhythm), PDF 179 (rhythms of the elements must be distinct for clarity), PDF 178 (colour of each element mixes string and brass). Fix: the violas leave Cl 2 and join the horns' dotted minims with a line of their own, E–G–F♯–E–E–C, which with Hn 2 makes a string + brass layer in the bar's own metre under the hemiola:
```
sCVla 121-126:  e'2.( | g'2. | fis'2. | e'2. | e'2. | c'2.) |   (127-128 unchanged: b2( b4~ | b4 e'2))
dAVla 281-286:  e'2.\f( | g'2. | fis'2. | e'2. | e'2. | c'2.) |
```
Checked against the bass (no octaves: 124 bass a,→g, moves under a held e'; 126 C is reached by oblique motion) and against the tune.

### 4. Subject and countersubject at the same dynamic (61–68, 297–300). Applied.
Bars: 61, 65, 297. Problem: each new subject entry is p and the countersubject continues at the same p in the cellos' and violas' full middle register, so nothing places the subject in front. Evidence: Adler PDF 184: Schoenberg marks the principal voice p and the subsidiary voices pp; principal gestures are marked louder than subordinate ones. Fix: countersubject (and the new third line) pp; the subject entries keep p; the existing crescendo from 67/299 is unchanged:
```
sBVc 61:  d'4(\pp c' bes      sBVla 65:  a'4(\pp g' f'      dBVc 297:  d'4(\pp c' bes
```
