## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 339: the low brass enter with an accented fff crotchet, and the closing stroke is a single short accent in every group (applied)
- **Bars:** 339 (the E-minor stroke before the G.P.).
- **Problem:** The trombones, bass trombone and tuba rest under the B6 (336–338, c034) and re-enter on 339 with `4->\fff`. The horns and trumpets play the same accented crotchet. This is exactly what the BRIEF rules out: an accented attack on a brass entry, at the loudest level. Nothing else in the movement keeps it, since c124 turned the tutti brass accents into tenutos. The low brass are also at fff with the horns, although c086 sets them a degree under the horns (Sebesky: one trombone at mf has about twice a horn's power). The whole orchestra strikes the same short note, so the brass give bite and no weight.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 189–190 (principle 13): an accent alone is often not enough. Combine a short note in one group with long notes in another, and the long notes decay (Ex. XV-35, sf>pp). Adler PDF 39–40 (principle 6): Tchaikovsky's climactic brass hold their notes, and the trombones' held notes carry fff>. Dvořák 9/iii's fff brass are held and unaccented, with a diminuendo (brass.notesS.ily 86, 171, 278, 368, 447).
- **Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily):** strings, woodwinds and timpani keep their short accented stroke. All brass hold a tenuto minim and decay to the rest, so the stroke comes from the short notes and the weight from the long ones:
  - Hn 1–4 and Tpt 1–2: `g'4-> r4 r4` becomes `g'2--\> r4\!` (fff carries over from 336).
  - Tbn 1: `g2--\ff\> r4\!`. Tbn 2: `e2--\ff\> r4\!`. B.tbn: `e,2--\ff\> r4\!`. Tuba: `e,,2--\ff\> r4\!`.
  - The low brass entrance on the last chord, which c034 wanted for finality, stays. It is now made by weight, one degree under the horns as c086 sets it at 333.

### 2. Bars 335–338: the trumpets jump from f to an accented fff, and their hemiola is accented (applied)
- **Bars:** 335–338.
- **Problem:** Every other part crescendos through 335 into the fff at 336: Fl, Ob, Cl, Bsn, C.a., horns `\<`, strings `\<`. The trumpets play motive x at f in 335 with no hairpin and land on `b'2.->\fff`. That is a two-level jump on an accented brass attack at the movement's peak. At 337–338 the trumpets also accent the hemiola minims (`a'2-> a'4->~ | a'4 a'2->`). The hemiola is already made clear by the re-attacks and by the accents in the tune (c048), so the trumpet accents add only bite.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 39–40 (principle 6): the climactic brass are held and shaped, not struck. BRIEF (Dan): brass entries are shaped by dynamics and doubling. c124's tenuto-for-accent rule in the tutti theme.
- **Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily):** Tpt 1 at 335 becomes `b'4-.\< b'8-. b'-. b'4-.`, and 336–338 become `b'2.--\fff | a'2-- a'4--~ | a'4 a'2--`. Tpt 2 at 335 becomes `e'4-.\< …`, and 336–338 become `dis'2.--\fff | dis'2-- dis'4--~ | dis'4 dis'2--`. The accents in the strings, woodwinds and timpani at 337–338 (c048) are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 104→105 and 304→305: the trumpets and trombones drop a level on the arrival (applied)
- **Bars:** 101–105 and 301–305.
- **Problem:** In the hemiola build onto V, the trumpets and trombones (and the bass trombone at 101–104) crescendo from pp to ff at 104. On 105, the downbeat of the tutti, they are marked f (c076/c086 balance under the horns). They therefore drop from ff to f at the moment the music arrives, so the brass are louder in the preparation than in the event. The reprise is the same for the trumpets at 304→305. Holding ff for a bar on the leading tone D♯5 (Tpt 1) is also the brass bite Dan asked to avoid.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 38–40 (principle 7): the arrival is the larger event; Tchaikovsky's tutti exceeds the climax before it. Adler PDF 37 (principles 1–2): the brass colour enters soft and is saved, not spent before the event. BRIEF: brass shaped by dynamics.
- **Fix (applied):** the brass crescendo reaches f, the level they keep from 105. In sections/sB.ily at 104: Tpt 1 `dis''2.\f`, Tpt 2 `fis'2.\f`, Tbn 1 `a2.\f`, Tbn 2 `fis2.\f`, B.tbn `b,2.\f` (were `\ff`). The f that now repeats at 105 is removed in sections/sC.ily (Tpt 1·2, Tbn 1·2, B.tbn; the tuba's first f at 105 stays). In sections/dB.ily at 304: Tpt 1 `dis''2.\f`, Tpt 2 `fis'2.\f`. The repeated f at 305 is removed in sections/dC.ily. Horns, strings, woodwinds and timpani stay ff at 104/304. The timpani's ff→f is c056's playability choice and is not changed.
