## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Hairpins drawn so short that they read as accents. Applied.
**Bars:** 79 (harp RH), 165–166, 181–182, 221–222 (Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II: c099's one-beat swells on T's cadence), 339 (Tbn 1·2, B.tbn, Tuba diminuendo), 355, 357 (Hn 1 crescendo into the motto tail).

**Problem:** The diagnostic build measured these drawn lengths: bar 79, 0.85 spaces; 222, 1.11; 166, 1.24; 182, 1.33; 221, 2.24; 357, 2.28; 339, 2.33; 165, 2.39; 181, 2.43. A hairpin about one notehead long under a single crotchet looks the same as an accent (>) or its mirror image. At 357 the printed "p <" looks like a dynamic followed by a stray sign. c099's shaping (swell into the dissonance, softer resolution) does not survive if the player sees an accent.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 9: the software "will apply notation principles, but the operator must decide if these are the most apt solutions, and a trained eye is required to refine the settings". PDF 55: symbols evenly spaced and never crowded. PDF 53–54: spacing can be adjusted a little where symbols need room, as long as the rhythm still reads.

**Fix (score.ly, the Staff context that holds Dynamic_engraver):**
```
\context { \Staff \consists "Dynamic_engraver" \consists "Dynamic_align_engraver"
  \override Hairpin.minimum-length = #5.0 \override Hairpin.springs-and-rods = #ly:spanner::set-spacing-rods \override Hairpin.bound-padding = #0.5 }
```
After the change, the shortest drawn hairpin is 2.83 spaces (339). Bar 357 is 3.28 and all others are over 3.5. Testing showed `minimum-length 3` alone changed nothing, because the visible hairpin is about 1.9 spaces shorter than the rod. Every system and page break is unchanged (same bars on each of the 13 pages). Dynamics and their timing are untouched, so c099's shaping, Dan's soft-brass rule and the MIDI all stay as they were.

### 2. Bar 269: "1." printed twice on the bassoon staff. Applied.
**Problem:** `\partCombineSoloI` prints the staff's soloText "1.", and the markup `\bold "1." \italic "scherzando"` adds a second "1." directly beneath it. A PDF scan found no other stacked labels in the score.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 11 and 13: one convention used consistently; familiar notation reads fastest. PDF 55: no symbol crowded by another.

**Fix (dA.ily, dABsnOne):** `^\markup { \bold "1." \italic "scherzando" }` becomes `^\markup \italic "scherzando"`. The single remaining "1." comes from the part-combiner. A Bassoon 1 part does not need "1." anyway.

### 3. Hn 3·4: the treble clef comes right after the last bass-clef note (320), not before the entry at 331. Applied.
**Problem:** Since c115 the Hn 3·4 staff is in bass clef for 301–320. The return to treble is printed straight after the last note of 320, followed by ten bars' rest in the same system (321–330) before the entry at 331.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 22: "The clef always goes before the barline, whether or not rests precede the entry". The example shows the clef just before the entry bar, "rather than" after the barline at the start of the rests. PDF 23: returning clefs go at the end of the system or before the entry; that placing is "better than an immediate clef change before rests, which draws unwanted attention to the new clef at an arbitrary point". The rests and the entry are in one system, so the clef goes before the entry.

**Fix:** In dC.ily `dCHnFour`, `| \tag #'score \clef treble R2.*4 | }` becomes `| R2.*4 | }`. In coda.ily `codaHnFour`, `R2.*6 | \tag #'score \clef treble a2.\pp\<`. LilyPond prints the clef before the barline of 331. Only the score is affected: parts are untagged, as in c115.

### 4. Bar number 59 printed under the system separator (page 3). Applied.
**Problem:** On page 3 the two systems are close together. The slash separator between them is drawn over the bar number "59" of the upper system, so the number is unreadable. It is the only one of the six two-system pages where this happens.

**Evidence:** Gould PDF 55: "no characters should collide". MOLA (cited by c045): every bar of a full score numbered.

**Fix (score.ly \paper):** `system-separator-markup = \markup \translate #'(0 . -2) \slashSeparator`. I checked pages 2, 3, 6, 7, 8 and 9: the separator now sits below the bar numbers on each page and touches nothing.

Points checked and kept, with no finding: clef placement at 301 and 341/345; no octave signs (Gould PDF 46–47, B6 on five ledger lines); rests in the two-part horn staff at 357–365 (PDF 50–51: different rhythms need their own rests); double barlines at the section joins (PDF 53); bars per system (PDF 54); chord and dot placement (PDF 60–68).
