## What else I studied

**Dvořák 6/iii.** The corpus has no score of it. I worked from my knowledge of the score and from these sources:
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._6_(Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k): D minor scherzo; D-major trio at about bars 154–288; furiant hemiolas at phrase starts; the piccolo only in this movement, in the trio, over pizzicato strings; no trombones or tuba in the movement; return without repeats.
- https://thelistenersclub.com/?p=15806: the timpani adds a cross-metre layer in the last statement.
- https://www.bso.org/works/dvorak-symphony-no-6: the trio is soft and in the upper registers.
- IMSLP for the movement heading and scoring: https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.6,_Op.60_(Dvořák,_Antonín).

I compared bar by bar where the two movements share a device:
- **Metre.** Dvořák's identity is the 2+2+2 against 3+3 cross-rhythm at the head of nearly every phrase. Here the hemiola is saved for cadences and builds (53–55, 101–104, 129–131, 285–287, 301–304, 336–338), and theme A stays in plain triple time with motive x. Dvořák uses the hemiola everywhere; this score keeps it for arrivals, as Brahms 4/iii and Bruckner do. The brief names those models, so this is a choice, not a lack.
- **Lyrical second section in the relative major.** Dvořák's is in F. Here it is the second idea in G at 45–56, legato over held horns, then in E minor at 121–132 and 277–288.
- **Trio.** Dvořák's trio is a soft upper-register colour unique to the trio (piccolo over pizzicato), plainer in harmony, and it winds down on a long tonic. Here the trio has the clarinet over harp and the horn drone at 153, the violins an octave apart at 169, and the codetta over the E pedal at 225–240. It is equally plain harmonically (E – C♯ minor – B – E with mixture) and winds down the same way.
- **Return.** Dvořák returns almost literally. This score rescores the reprise (Vln II and violas pizz. with Fl 1 heterophony at 253, the compressed counterstatement, a shorter fugato without the F episode). That is closer to the Beethoven model the brief names, and an improvement on a literal da capo.
- **Ending.** Dvořák ends the movement loud in its own key. This score ends pp on G♯ for the cyclic link, which is protected.

A conductor who knows Dvořák 6 would recognise the hemiola cadences, the pizzicato bass under woodwind solos, the soft-timpani links and the long tonic wind-down. Nothing reads as student work. I found no place where the furiant comparison shows a fault this score could fix without undoing a decision the brief or HISTORY.md records.

The one c238 change I examined closely is the timpani falling from mf (100) to p (the roll at 101). In v229 the timpani stayed p from 95 and the roll grew from p. In v239 the strokes grow first, then the roll restarts p under the strings' f and the brass pp. This is Rimsky's amplification order (principle 12): the roll is a new sustained layer, it enters soft under the arrival and grows to ff at 105 faster than the strings. The bass still rises through 98–100 with the cellos and basses. It is not worse.

Nothing got worse between v229 and v239.

## Findings

No finding this cycle calls for a change to the score. I checked all 24 principles bar by bar; none is violated outside passages that HISTORY.md protects with a stated reason I cannot answer. In ranked order, these are the points I tested and decided to leave:

1. **329–332, Vln I alone in the top octave for two bars (F6 at 331, D♯6 at 332) with no flute, and an octave or tenth gap below it** (principles 6, 18, 19; PDF 124, 131–132, Ex. 256–263). I considered Fl 1 doubling Vln I there (`f'''4-.\mf\< c'''8-. c'''-. f'''4-. | dis'''4-. b''8-. b''-. r4`). I rejected it for two reasons. The build 325–332 is protected (c007, c109, c137, c230; c213 reset its wind bass). It also follows Rimsky's own order: strings first, then woodwind at 329 in the middle, horns at 331–332, flutes with the fff peak at 333. Ex. 263 (PDF 430) shows the same thing, a held gap he allows when the top part climbs alone. Adding flutes two bars early would spend the colour that marks 333. Not applied.
2. **98–101, timpani mf → roll p** (c238). Discussed above. Kept, per PDF 127.
3. **209–220, Hn 1·2 held inside T's lower octave (Cl 1, Vln II)** (principle 20, PDF 132–133). The remaining one-beat seconds (209 B4/A4, 212 B4/A4, 213 A4–C♯5 around B4, 220 C♯5/B4) are passing and neighbour notes against a softer basis (horns p, tune f). This is the "four colourings of T" do-not-touch (c224), and c054/c129 already removed the held unisons. Kept.
4. **45–56 and 121–132, horns' held basis in the oboes' and Vln II's octave** (principle 20). Horns are mf under f and the contacts are passing notes. The cadence 53–55 is protected (c002, c223). Kept.
