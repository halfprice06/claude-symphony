## Findings

### 1. 357–360: Vln II stays "la metà" under Hn 1's motto tail (applied)

- **Problem.** The motto's chromatic tail B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 (Hn 1, p espress.) is the scherzo's main cyclic quotation and the line the finale continues to G at IV 1–2. At 357 Vln II goes from "la metà" (349–356) to "tutti", so about twelve violins play G♯4–G4–E4–E4 pp directly under the horn. They move in the horn's own dotted minims, and for the first step they shadow it a third below (B/G♯ → A♯/G). The violas, cor anglais, cellos, basses and Bsn 2 also strike every downbeat with it. So nothing contrasts with the solo in rhythm, and the full violin section is the nearest voice to it.
- **Evidence.** Adler says rhythmic independence is "the most crucial" problem; the accompaniment "neither duplicates nor imitates" the solo (PDF 259–260). "Try to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist … rhythmic contrast lessens the importance of this consideration" (PDF 274). Here there is no rhythmic contrast, so the register rule applies in full. In Ex. XVII-9 "the rest of the strings have more subsidiary harmonic roles" (PDF 264). The transparent background lets the solo be "instantly perceived" (PDF 269). Bizet keeps the instruments rhythmically apart from the voice (PDF 279). The harmony cannot move: Vln II's G4–E4 is protected (c070), and so are the 6–♭6–5 line (c460) and the plagal close (c002, c422). So the fix that is left is weight: the half section that has just played T's lower octave carries G♯–G–E alone.
- **Fix (applied).** In `codaVlnTwo`, bar 357: `gis'2.\pp^\markup \italic "tutti"` becomes `gis'2.\pp`. Vln II stays la metà to its last note at 360 and has no later entry in the movement. The notes, dynamics and MIDI are unchanged, and no part-cue points at Vln II here. The A♯°7's G stays in about six violins at pp, and violins carry well in that register. The 6–♭6–5 line (full violas + cor anglais) and Hn 1's tail are now the two clearest lines, which is the point of c460.

### 3. 361–364: c390's second reason for the closing chord's E4 no longer holds; keep the E4 on its first reason (record)

c390 kept the horn E4 under Hn 1's held G♯4 for two reasons. First, from 361 the G♯ is the third of a held tonic chord, not a solo phrase (Adler PDF 270, Bartók model). Second, "Hn 2's E4 → the finale's Hn 2 E♭4 at IV 1" gives the same player the same register across the pause (PDF 275). c488 moved the E4 to Hn 4 so that it could fade on its own staff, and in v501 the finale's Hn 4 enters on F♯3, not E♭4. The second reason is therefore void. The first reason still holds (PDF 264's rule covers the solo line, which ends at 360). The shared-staff rule (equal simultaneous dynamics) prevents moving the E4 back to Hn 2 with a fade. **Decided:** keep Hn 4's E4. HISTORY and scherzo.md should give its reason as c390's first reason plus c488's fade, not the Hn 2 → Hn 2 link. The E4→E♭4 link across the pause rests on the harp's E4 at 375 (c070).

### 5. Weighed and rejected (decided: no change)

- **A contrasting colour for T's lower octave at 349–356 (PDF 264, 267: no orchestral instrument of the solo's colour).** Half of Vln II is in the solo violin's colour. But c379 removed Cl 1 so that its entry at 361 is fresh; c457 and c467 settled the weight; and c438's single-player relay is kept. Giving the octave to Cl 1 would spend the 361 entry and reverse three cycles. The lower octave is a level under the solo plus Fl 1 since c467, which answers PDF 264 well enough.
- **Removing Hn 4's E4 at 361–364 (PDF 264).** This would reverse c488 two cycles after it was made. It would also leave a sixth gap B3–G♯4 in the fading chord, with E sounding only at E2, E3 and E5. Finding 3 gives the reason to keep it.
- **Vln II entering on beat 2 at 357 (rhythmic independence, PDF 259).** It would blur the A♯°7's downbeat, and the harmony (c070, c460) is protected. Finding 1 gets the clarity by weight instead.
- **Pitch lead for Hn 3 at 341 after the G.P. (PDF 275).** The violas' A3 and the pizz. A1 sound with its C♯4. A professional horn places a third over a struck root without help, and a harp anticipation would break the G.P.'s silence before the new key.

### 6. Note for the finale's rework (no scherzo change, no message)

Adler PDF 263 explains why the finale's direct ff tutti works after the scherzo's last eight bars (two players on one pitch, ppp): the contrast makes the new colour fresh. The finale should keep its bar 1 a full tutti and not open with a soft solo horn on A♭. A solo horn there would duplicate the scherzo's last colour instead of answering it.
