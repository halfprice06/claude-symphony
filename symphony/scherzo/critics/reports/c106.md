## Findings

### 1. Bar 244: the cellos' pizz. C3 + A♯3 cannot be stopped cleanly as a double-stop (applied)
- **Bars:** 244 (`retrVc`, `<c ais>4`, pp, pizz.), the French sixth C–E–F♯–A♯ (c062).
- **Problem:** C3 lies on the G string; A♯3 lies on the D string only at the top of 4th position, or on the A string in half position. On the adjacent G and D strings the two notes are a minor seventh apart. Adler (PDF 97) says the cello's first position spans a third and "anything larger than sixth double-stops is difficult in the low register". In first position the notes fall on the G and A strings, with the open D string between them. A player who strums the chord sounds open D3 inside the French sixth. Plucking two non-adjacent strings with two fingers is possible but unusual, and the left hand needs an extension at the same time. Every other cello double-stop in the score is a fifth stopped with one finger across two strings (72, 300, 339). The chord is pp in an exposed bar: violas hold E4, Hn 1 has F♯4 and the harmony is otherwise thin, so a wrong D or a split pluck would be heard.
- **Evidence:** Adler PDF 97 (compass of the hand, sixths the practical limit low down); PDF 76 and 88 (what is hard for one player is "quite simple played divisi"; the viola's and cello's divisi practice is the same as the violin's); Ex. III-62 (Rossini, divided cellos) on PDF 101–102 shows the section divided by stand into single lines.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-106/work/sections/retr.ily):**
  `retrVc = { e4\pp r4 r4 | R2. | a,4 r4 r4 | <c ais>4^\markup \italic "div." r4 r4 | b,4^\markup \italic "unis." r4 r4 | ...`
  Pitches, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. The bass C stays doubled by the basses' C2, and each half of the cellos plucks one note on one string. I added a comment, signed c106, above the variables. I considered moving the A♯ to the violas, who hold E4 through 243–245. I rejected it because the violas' held E is c037's suspension and c098's diminuendo, and the cellos' A→A♯ is c062's chromatic voice exchange, which must stay in one voice.

No other string passage in the score breaks a rule of this chapter (table above). I decided against two changes I considered:
- A treble clef for the viola's F5 at 62/294. It is one note, and the clef would have to change and change back within four bars.
- "Change bow freely" for the cellos' and basses' tied E at 361–364. That is about 3.2 s at pp, inside one bass bow (PDF 108 applies to long slurs and lengthy tenuto).
