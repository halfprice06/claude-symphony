## Judgement of the melodies

- **Theme A** is the movement's hook: motive x, the chromatic tail, the lower-neighbour bar 22, the climb with two passing tones at 23, the accented ♭9 at 24 and the double incomplete neighbour at 27. A listener will hum motive x and the falling chromatic tail, as with the timpani motive in Beethoven 9/ii. No change.
- **T** uses every type of figuration in the unit. Its climax (tied A5) falls at bar 10 of 16, the third phrase runs into the fourth, and the cadence has a real 4–3. It is a tune a listener can sing back, in the line of Dvořák 8/iii's trio. No change.
- **The second idea** works by its hemiola and its implied two-voice sequence, not by figuration. It is protected. No change.
- **The harp melody 73–80** is plain on purpose, as a contrast of colour (♭II, no strings). It is protected.
- **The coda's T** (341–356) splits the tune between Hn 1, Ob 1 and Vln I, with the first half in A (c013). This is how Dvořák 9/iii's coda recalls its trio. It is protected (c133, c150).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 263, Fl 1: a dissonant F♯6 is left by a falling diminished fifth into theme A's peak (APPLIED)

**Problem.** In the reprise, Fl 1 decorates theme A an octave above Vln I (253–268, c107). At 263 it plays `fis'''8( g''' a''' g''' fis'''4)`, then C6 at 264, where it joins Vln I in unison on the peak. C7 is not available, because it is kept for the finale. The beat-3 F♯6 sounds against the cellos' and violas' C4 (a tritone over iv) and Vln I's B5. It is approached by step from G6 and left by a leap of a diminished fifth down to C6. This is the only melodic tritone leap out of a dissonance in any melodic line of the score (diss2.py). It falls in the bar that should carry the line up to the peak, so the flute's line drops by an awkward interval just as the violins arrive.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 336, section 7: "a dissonance is emphasized when it is preceded or followed by a leap". F♯6 is neither a passing tone nor a returning neighbour. It is a lower neighbour of G6 that does not return, and it is left by leap.
- PDF 333, Ex. 20-10 (Beethoven op. 59/1, checked on the image): two consecutive passing tones fill a fourth. A6–G6–F♯6–E6 is such a fourth, and E6 is a chord tone of iv.
- PDF 343, review points 3 and 6: the passing tone fills a third or a fourth by step. The incomplete neighbour decorates the preceding or the following tone.
- In the exposition (23) and the tuttis (115, 315) every melodic line climbs F♯–G–A–B by step into C6, so the reprise flute is the one line that reaches the peak from above by a tritone.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-181/work/sections/dA.ily, with a c181 comment above dAFluteOne).**
```
263  dAFluteOne  old:  fis'''8(\< g''' a''' g''' fis'''4)
                 new:  fis'''8(\< g''' a''' g''' fis''' e''')
```
The bar now runs A6–G6–F♯6–E6 by step, and E6 falls a third to C6 at 264, where the line continues as before. The flute keeps its heterophony and its upper octave, and only the last beat of one bar changes. This answers c107's protection, because the flute still varies the theme an octave up. It also keeps c049's spelling (no new false relation, since no D♮ or C♯ is added). Checks: E6→C6 moves against Vln I's B5→C6 in contrary motion and in thirds with the cellos' C4→A3. The six slurred quavers move by step at the same speed as the violins' quavers. The analyser's parallel list is unchanged. This is not a theme change (the flute heterophony is not in the record; themecheck 41/41).

## Points checked and decided (no change)

- **172 and 212: the cellos (and Bsn 2 at 212) sound G♯3 on the downbeat under T's accented passing A** (20-15). This anticipates the tone of resolution. I keep it. The cello's A3 at 171/211 is the seventh of the V7, and the G♯3 on the beat is its resolution. Every alternative breaks something else: E3 leaves the seventh unresolved, B3 doubles the violas' B3 at pitch (c137's rule), and holding A3 into beat 1 makes octaves A→G♯ with Vln II. A&S PDF 352 (Unit 21) allows the tone of resolution in an inner voice "in a texture with marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics", and this cello line is a sustained low countermelody under a violin tune. c054 removed the same-octave G♯4 (Hn 2) at 212, which was the real clash.
- **326–328: motive x in fifths and octaves (Vc, Vla, Vln II, Vln I).** By review point 8 (PDF 343), parallels made by chordal skips are faulty. Here, though, the four string groups play one figure together at the fifth and octave. That is a doubling that builds an open-fifth drone, not a set of independent voices. A&S exclude doublings in free texture (PDF 341), and the passage is protected (v01 "by design", c091, c159). I keep it.
- **115→116 and 315→316: Hn 1 C5→F♯4** (an inner tritone across iv→V in the tutti accompaniment). It then turns up to B4 on beat 3, and the voicing is c083's (F♯4 on beat 1 under the ♭9). It is an inner chord part, not a melody. I keep it.
- **Second idea lead-ons.** In G, 48 and 52 are anticipations (E6–E6, D6–D6). In E minor, 124 and 128 are neighbour notes (D6–C6, C♯6–B5). These are two figuration types, so the reprise varies the exposition (PDF 328–329). I keep them.
- **Harp melody 73–80 and Cl 1 81–88**, all downbeats chord tones (principle 1). The plain line is the episode's character and is protected. I keep them.
