## Judgement of the three main melodies

- **Theme A (13–28)** is the most memorable of the three. Motive x and the chromatic tail make a real hook, close to Dvořák 9/iii and Beethoven 9/ii in kind. Its weak point is the continuation (21–24): crotchet scales lead to a peak (B5) that is a plain chord tone, struck twice in a row (23 beat 3, 24 beat 1) and then left by arpeggio. The phrase has no high point a listener would keep.
- **The second idea (45–56)** is well built as a polyphonic line (principle 4), and its second unit carries real dissonance (A6 over G at 50, G6 over F♯ at 51). Its first unit and its cadence are the framework itself: chord tones in minims, closing on D6–B5–G5 | A5–F♯5 | G5.
- **T (153–168)** is the weakest as a tune that stays with a listener. Every downbeat is a chord tone and every non-chord tone is an unaccented passing note (principle 6 is the only device used). Its three opening phrases share one rhythm (minim–crotchet | minim–crotchet | dotted crotchet–quaver–crotchet), each a step higher, and the 4th and 8th bars are bare dotted minims. In this movement the Fl 1 heterophony at 169 is the figurated version of T; T itself is the reduction (principle 1).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. T has no accented dissonance; its phrase ends are bare (153–168, 169–184, 217–232, 357–364). APPLIED.
**Problem.** The cadential bars 156 (G♯4) and 160 (B4) are plain dotted minims of chord tones. Nothing in the tune leans, so nothing in it gives it a profile (Unit 20, PDF 328: without figuration only the framework is left).
**Evidence.** PDF 336 (20-15): accented passing and neighbour tones are displacements of the plain kind. PDF 338 (20-20): an appoggiatura resolves by step, and it resolves upward when it is a leading tone. PDF 339–340 (20-23): a chromatic lower neighbour connects better and gives the "pungent" effect of 20-1. PDF 337 (20-17): the figure B–A–G♯ is the falling second decorated by an accented passing tone. Dvořák's lyrical tune (corpus, above) has such a leaning note in nearly every bar.
**Fix.** In the 4th bar of each statement, an accented passing tone A→G♯ (from B, step down: B–A–G♯). In the 8th bar, a chromatic lower appoggiatura A♯→B, reached by a falling third from C♯ and resolving up as the leading tone of B over the B-major chord. That chord is prepared by V7/V at 159, where Vln II already has A♯. Both are semitone or step sighs, so they relate to the chromatic tail. Phrase 3 is unchanged.
```
tA: tAClOne 156  a'4( gis'2)     160  ais'4( b'2)
    tAVlnOne / tAFluteOne 172  a''4( gis''2)   176  ais''4( b''2)
tC: tCClOne 220 a'4( gis'2)  224 ais'4( b'2);  tCVlnOne, tCFluteOne 220 a''4( gis''2)  224 ais''4( b''2)
    tCHnOne 223 ais'2.~  224 ais'4( b'2)   (Hn 1 held B against the Cl's A♯; it now ties the A♯ into the resolution)
coda: codaHnOne 360 a'4( gis'2);  codaClOne 364 ais'4( b'2)
```
Side effect: the analyser's octave at 364 (C♯5→B4 over C♯→B) is gone.

### 2. Theme A's peak is a plain repeated B5 (24; also 116, 276, 328). APPLIED.
**Problem.** Bar 24 `b''4 fis'' dis''` comes straight after 23's B5. The peak of the theme is the same note struck twice and then an arpeggio.
**Evidence.** PDF 333 (principle 8): the neighbour figure works as a motive. PDF 336 (principle 10): the accented neighbour. The rhythm of bar 14 (an accented minim and two quavers) is already the theme's second-bar motive. Dvořák 9/iii places the ♭6–5 semitone at the top of his scherzo theme.
**Fix.** At 24, C6 as an accented upper neighbour to B5 over B7 (V7♭9; no C-major triad), in the rhythm of bar 14:
```
sAVlnOne 24, dAVlnOne 276, sC/dC Fl 1, Fl 2, Ob 1, Vln I (116, 328):  c'''2->( b''8 fis''8)
sCClOne 116, dCClOne 328:  c''2->( b'8 fis'8)
dAFluteOne 276 (heterophony, a turn with a chromatic lower neighbour, PDF 338–340):  c'''8( b'' ais'' b'' fis''4)
```
I first tried `c'''2->( b''4-.)`. The leap B5→E5 into 25 made outer-voice octaves with the bass B→E at 117 and 329, which is exactly the fault on PDF 331 (principle 5). Ending on F♯5 makes the arrival a step, so the final version adds no parallels and removes the old entry at 276.

### 3. The second idea's cadence is the bare framework (53–55; also 129–131, 297–299). APPLIED.
**Problem.** D6–B5–G5 | A5–F♯5 | G5 is an arpeggio and 2–1. It is the least distinctive moment of the tune and the one a listener keeps last.
**Evidence.** PDF 334–335 (20-13c, Schubert D. 894): the double incomplete neighbour, both neighbours before the main tone. PDF 338: an appoggiatura is reached by leap and resolved down by step. F♯5 (54) – A5 (55, leap of a third, on the downbeat over G) – G5 is both at once. The second idea then ends with a leaning note, as T and theme A now do.
**Fix.**
```
sA 55, dA 299: sAFluteOne/sAVlnOne/dAFluteOne/dAVlnOne  a''4 g''2)    sAOboeOne/dAOboeOne  a'4 g'2)
sC 131 (E minor): sCFluteOne/sCVlnOne  fis''4 e''2)    sCOboeOne  fis'4 e'2)
```
The slur is unchanged. On the downbeat Ob 1's A4 sits between Vla G4 and Vln II B4 for one crotchet. That is the appoggiatura sounding; I keep the oboe doubling.

### 5. Harp melody (73–80) and coda return of T (357–372). No change.
The harp line (A–F–A | C–B♭A | G–D–G | B♭–AG …) falls by step in two-bar units built from chordal skips plus a passing pair (principles 3 and 6). It is clear and idiomatic. The coda passes T from Hn 1 to Cl 1 to Fl/Vln I at its phrase joins, so the tune is heard whole. With finding 1, the horn at 360 and the clarinet at 364 now carry the two leaning notes.
