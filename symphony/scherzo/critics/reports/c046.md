## Findings

### 1. Bars 105–132 and 305–320: Hn 1 plays 28 bars high without a rest, and Hn 2 (a low horn) plays above Hn 3 (a high horn) (applied)

**Problem.** In the exposition tutti Hn 1 plays accented crotchets on E5 (written B5, the top of Adler's "brilliant and loud" register) for 16 bars at ff, then goes straight into 12 bars of sustained B4–C5 (written F♯5–G5) at f for the second idea, with no rest from 105 to 132. Its next entry is the exposed pp G4 that opens the link at 141. Hn 2 meanwhile plays B4 (written F♯5) for 28 bars, above Hn 3's E4, so the low horn has the high part and the high horn the low one. Hn 3 is free from 121 to 140.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292: "one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate". PDF 303: "it is most taxing to play in the higher register all the time, so periodic rests are most welcome", and "I and III, High Horns; II and IV, Low Horns". Ex. X-6 (PDF 304) interlocks the chord I–III–II–IV. PDF 284: soft attacks are the hardest part of brass playing, and Hn 1's pp entry at 141 comes after the heaviest stint of the movement.

**Fix (applied, pitches and balance unchanged).** In sC.ily and dC.ily, Hn 2 and Hn 3 swap their 105–120 (305–320) lines: Hn 2 takes the E4 crotchets (with the rest on beat 3 of 106/118 that c011 asked for), and Hn 3 takes the B4 crotchets. In sC.ily, Hn 3 also takes Hn 1's sustained line at 121–132 and Hn 1 rests from 121. Hn 1 now rests 20 bars before the link's G4 pp.
```
sCHnOne   = { e''4->\f r4 e''4-. | ... | e''4-> r4 e''4-. | R2.*20 | }
sCHnTwo   = { e'4->\f r4 e'4-. | e'4-> r4 r4 | ... | e'4-> r4 e'4-. | g'2.\f | g'2. | fis'2. | ... | e'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*8 | }
sCHnThree = { b'4->\f r4 b'4-. | ... | b'4-> r4 b'4-. | b'2.\f | b'2. | b'2. | b'2. | c''2. | c''2. | b'2. | b'2. | b'2. | a'2. | g'2. | g'4-. r4 r4 | R2.*8 | }
dCHnTwo   = { e'4->\f r4 e'4-. | ... (Hn 3's former line) ... | R2.*4 | }
dCHnThree = { b'4->\f r4 b'4-. | ... (Hn 2's former line) ... | R2.*4 | }
```
The Hn 1·2 staff now shows E5/E4 in the same rhythm, and Hn 3·4 shows Hn 3's crotchets against Hn 4's legato line. Both have f, so the part-combined dynamics stay equal. This keeps c044's "Hn 1's E5 at 105–120" and c011's clear beat 3 at 106/118. I did not change the coda's stacked order at 331–338: the Hn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 entries there come at different bars (332 p<, 331 pp<), so a swap would put two different dynamics on one shared staff, and those 8 bars are short enough not to be an endurance problem.

### 2. Bars 113–120 and 313–320: Hn 4's forte legato line has no place to breathe (applied)

**Problem.** Hn 4's tenor countermelody runs 105–120 at f under slurs of 5, 3 and 8 bars with no rests. The last slur (c'2.( ... g4)) covers 8 bars, about 5.5 seconds at 2. = 88, and has the tie e'2.~ e'2 inside it. A player who has already played eight slurred bars at f in the low-middle register (which takes the most air) has nowhere to breathe.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292: "all slurred phrases are performed on one breath. In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath"; PDF 309 repeats this for the horn. The musical phrase divides naturally after a4 at 116, before the held E4.

**Fix (applied).** sC.ily and dC.ily, `sCHnFour` / `dCHnFour`:
```
c'2.( | b2. | a2 c'4 | b2 a4) \breathe | e'2.~( | e'2 g4 | a2 fis4 | g4) r4 r4
```
`\breathe` takes no time, so bar lengths are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 209–224: Hn 1·2 hold 16 tongued dotted minims at p with no rest (applied)

**Problem.** At the trio tune's forte return, Hn 1·2 sustain the harmony for 16 bars without a rest (about 12.6 seconds at 2. = 76; Hn 1 is at B4–C♯5, written F♯5–G♯5). No breath is marked, so the two players may breathe at different points in the middle of a phrase.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292 (breathing and rests for brass); PDF 284 (controlled soft playing in the upper register is hard, which makes an unplanned breath more audible). The tune's phrase ends at 216 (Hn 1 ais'4( b'2)).

**Fix (applied).** tC.ily: a breath mark after bar 216 in both horns, so the shared staff shows one comma:
```
tCHnOne = { b'2.\p | ... | ais'2.~ | ais'4( b'2) \breathe | gis'2. | ...
tCHnTwo = { gis'2.\p | ... | fis'2. | dis'2. \breathe | e'2. | ...
```

No further findings. The rest of the brass writing follows the reading. Trumpets stay in their clear middle register and only play motive x and chords. Trombones and tuba move one note a bar, inside their ranges, and rest most of the movement. The mute is cancelled in time ("senza sord." at 73). Horn ranges and registers match the characters Adler gives them.
