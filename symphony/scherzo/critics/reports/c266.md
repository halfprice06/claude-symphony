## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bars 44 and 276: Ob 2 doubles a landing note that Ob 1 and the C.a. already have

**Problem.**

- At 44, Ob 1, Ob 2 and the cor anglais all play A4 together, p to mf, on beat 1. At 276 the same three play F♯4.
- This is a leftover from c086. That cycle moved the falling figure to the C.a. but left Ob 2 on the landing note under `\partCombineApart`.
- The result is three double reeds in unison in the oboe's lower register, at a soft dynamic, on a single exposed crotchet.

**Evidence.**

- Sebesky PDF 71: oboes in unison are harsh and nasal, and the bottom of the oboe is coarse.
- PDF 80: unisons risk intonation conflicts more than octaves do, and that is worst on an exposed short note after a rest.
- PDF 71: oboe and English horn combine well in unison. So Ob 1 plus the C.a. is the right pair.
- PDF 43: when a doubling stops, an instrument of equal weight should carry the line on, and the C.a. does that. The extra Ob 2 adds nothing except a tuning problem.

**Fix.** In sections/sA.ily:

- Ob 1: `| \partCombineApart a'4-. r4 r4 | \partCombineAutomatic d''2->(\mf\<` becomes `| a'4-. r4 r4 | d''2->(\mf\<`
- Ob 2: `| \partCombineApart a'4 r4 r4 | \partCombineAutomatic R2.*12 |` becomes `| R2. | R2.*12 |`

In sections/dA.ily:

- Ob 1: `\transpose d b, { \partCombineApart a'4-. r4 r4 \partCombineAutomatic }` becomes `\transpose d b, { a'4-. r4 r4 }`
- Ob 2: the same bar becomes `R2.`

**Result.** The part combiner now prints "1." over the oboe crotchet at 44 and 276 with no collision. I checked this on pages 2 and 9 of the scratch build. Ob 2 also gets one more bar of rest before its next entry.

### F2 (kept, no change). Bars 41–43, 273–275, 334–338: Ob 1·2 a2 unison

PDF 71 calls oboe unisons harsh. These passages are f tutti and the fff climax, where an edge is wanted, and PDF 56 says woodwind unisons become smoother with more players. The flutes double the line here, so the oboes are not exposed. No change.

### F3 (kept, correcting c176). Bars 71 and 299: horn motive-x quavers

PDF 32 warns against horns in fast syncopated figures combined with other brass. c176 said the trumpets double the horns here, but that is not so: the trumpets rest. The horns are the only brass, Bsn 1 doubles them in the same rhythm, and the quavers fall on the beat. The warning does not apply. Dvořák writes the same kind of figure for horns at similar speeds. No change.

### F4 (kept). Bars 72–74, 368–376: Hn 1 high and long soft notes

- **72–74.** Hn 1 plays written G♯5 to G5 at pp. PDF 33 says soft high horn needs care, but this is the top of the normal range, not beyond it, and three cycles (c136, c156, c176) have confirmed it.
- **368–376.** Hn 1's last G♯ lasts about 12 s with the rit. and fermata. Hn 3 holds the same pitch class from 367, so Hn 1 can breathe at 367 (PDF 30, breath marks in long lines). The ending is protected. No change.

### F5 (kept). Bars 305–320: Hn 4 A2 concert

This is written E3, above the pedal range of PDF 33, at f under the tutti. It is playable and supported by the B.tbn. No change.

### F6 (kept). Bars 105–120 and 333–339: B.tbn and tuba both on the root

PDF 40 describes the tuba as a spreading bottom voice and the B.tbn as the edgier one. PDF 47 asks for the root in the lowest brass at f. Both are on the root at f/ff with rests between phrases, so endurance is no problem. No change.

### F7 (kept). Bar 332: Cl 2 B3 against Bsn 1 A3

This is a sustained major second inside the woodwinds. PDF 41–44 warns against close intervals low in brass voicings. This interval is in the woodwinds and in the middle register, under a full tutti, and Cl 2's octave leap at 332–333 is protected by c256. No change.

### F8 (kept). Bars 209–216: Bsn 1 E4/F♯4 under soft horns

PDF 73 says the bassoon's high register is tense, and PDF 106 says bassoon combines well with soft brass. The tense colour is wanted here, the notes stay in bass clef (PDF 72–73, no clef changes for short passages), and the line lies within the normal range. No change.
