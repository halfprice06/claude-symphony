## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The trio never makes B (V) a key: its two A statements cadence identically on E, and 133–184 is 52 bars on the tonic. Applied.

**Bars.** 153–168 (T, Cl 1) and 169–184 (T, violins): both are 16-bar periods with a half cadence on V (160, 176) and a perfect cadence on E (167, 183). The middle (185–208) is C♯ minor; B is touched only at 189–192 and never confirmed. The trio's only perfect cadences are on E (167, 183, 223).

**Problem.** A&S 224: in an AABA quatrain "the first A and second A usually cadence in different places, one of them on I and the other on V". A&S 220: without the tonicized V "there would be no impression of departure and return; the form would lose much of its plasticity". The pacing shows it: the scherzo's close (133–140), the link (141–152) and both trio statements (153–184) all stay on E, 52 bars (about 40 s), and the written-out second statement differs from the first only in scoring. Dvořák's trio, which also cadences its first phrases on I, puts its whole middle in the dominant. Here c003 cut the B-major middle block for good reasons (static repetition), so the V has to come from somewhere else.

**Fix (applied, tA.ily, no change of length).** Make the second statement's consequent modulate to B, as Ex. 14-10 does and as Heidenröslein's second phrase does (PDF 212: it starts as a repeat, then one chromatic change turns it to V). 177–179 (C♯m – E/G♯ – F♯m, with the tied A6) stay: in B they read ii – IV6 – v and serve as the pivot area (PDF 216–217). 180 becomes ii6/5 of B over E, 181–182 the cadential 6/4 and V7 over F♯, 183 B major, and the melody is T's own cadence moved to B, with its 4–3 suspension intact:

### 2. The raised 4th (A♯) of V7/V is doubled at 175 and tripled at 215–216, and resolves in octaves. Applied.

**Bars.** 175: Vc A♯2 (beats 1–2) and Vla A♯3 (beats 2–3); the cellos then play B2 on beat 3 against the violas' held A♯3 (a major 7th). 215: Vc and Bsn 2 A♯2, Vla A♯3 and Hn 1 A♯4 together; Hn 1 holds A♯4 into 216, where Cl 1 and the violins have A♯ too, and Hn 1 and Vln I then rise A♯→B together on beat 2 of 216 (the pair scan reports an octave there).

**Problem.** A&S 214: "Because ♯4 functions (temporarily) as a leading tone, it must not be doubled." Ex. 14-3 gives every applied chord with ♯4 in one voice only. The melody's A♯ appoggiatura at 176/216 is the line itself; the inner doublings are not.

That gives a double appoggiatura (A♯ below, C♯ above) closing onto B.
