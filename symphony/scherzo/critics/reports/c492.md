## Findings

### 1. 220: half-diminished ii6/5 by mixture under the trio's forte climax. APPLIED.

- **Bars.** 220, beat 3 (Hn 1).
- **Problem.** T's bar 12 (C♯–B–A) comes four times: 164, 180, 220 and 352. Each time it is harmonised the same way, ii7 → ii6/5 → cadential 6/4. Inside T this reading's main colour is never used, although Units 22–23 present it as the most common Romantic intensifier before a cadence. 220 is the only one of the four that follows T's ff peak (A6 at 218), and the only one that can take mixture. c170 keeps 352 on C♯ so that the ♭6 after 337 is saved for the plagal close, and 164 and 180 are soft first statements.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 395, point 2a: "♭6 to color and intensify IV, II6, II7". PDF 387–388 and Ex. 23-2d/e: II6/5 with ♭6 going straight to V. PDF 388 and 395, point 3: ♭6 goes on to 5, and "♮6–♭6–5 is good". PDF 392, §5: mixture is "particularly vivid when the change of mode varies what otherwise would be a repetition". Here the change varies the fourth appearance of bar 12. PDF 394, Ex. 23-12b: a chromatic step inside one voice is unobjectionable, even when the other tone is doubled elsewhere. Hn 1's C♯5–C5–B4 is the same ♮6–♭6–5 line that Bsn 1 sings at 201–203 and that the close sings at 358–360 (c460). The trio's two forte peaks now both turn through ♭6 to 5, and the line looks ahead to the movement's last inner line.
- **Why beat 3 only.** On beat 2 the tune's B4 (Cl 1, Vln II) would rub a semitone against C5. On beat 3 the tune is on A, the chord is clean A–C–E–F♯, and no C♯ sounds anywhere. Voice leading into 221: C5→B4 makes parallel 6ths with Vln I (A5–G♯5) and parallel 3rds with Cl 1/Vln II (A4–G♯4). Against Vla F♯3→B3 the d5 closes to an octave in contrary motion (row 5). c112's E4 is unchanged, and no 5ths or octaves appear. The next C♯ comes at 225 (the codetta's IV), after the ♭6 has reached 5 at 221.
- **Fix (tC.ily, tCHnOne).** `a'2.--\mf\> | cis''2.-- | b'2.--\p` → `a'2.--\mf\> | cis''2-- c''4-- | b'2.--\p`, with a comment line (c492) above c112's. The engraving prints Hn 1's written G♮5 as a stem-up crotchet over Hn 2's dotted minim F♯. Dynamics are unchanged, so the shared staff stays clean.

### 2. 52: vii°7 of V (C♯–E–G–B♭) in place of V6/5 of V in the G-major second idea. Considered, NOT applied.

c412 made 128/284 a vii°7 of V. A&S PDF 374 and 393 would allow the same at 52 through mixture (B♭ = ♭6 of D). But the next chord, the cadential 6/4 over D at 53, has B♮ (B4 in Cl 1 and Hn 1·2). The B♭ would therefore go to B♮ before reaching A, which is ♭6–♮6–5, the order PDF 388 and 395 (point 3) call poor. In E minor at 128 the 7th G is the same G that the 6/4 over B holds, so the problem does not arise there. Keep V6/5 of V at 52. Both statements are right for their mode.

### 3. 95–100: ♭9 neighbour (C5) in Vln II's V7 arpeggio over the B pedal (Ex. 22-9). Not applied.

The Appassionata footnote (PDF 378) supports a long °7 over a dominant pedal. Here, though, the pedal's tension comes from the rising head entries and the three-bar groups (c019). The movement keeps its ♭9 for the soft peaks at 24/264 and 126/282 and the coda's 337. c442 and c352 already rejected the ♭9 at 103–104. A single passing C in an inner arpeggio would not be heard as a harmony.

### 4. 155: viiø7/vii°7 over the trio's E drone (Ex. 22-10). Not applied.

The horn drone holds B3. Either D♯–F♯–A–C♯ or D♯–F♯–A–C would sit on top of it (a minor 9th C/B, or B against C♯ in the same octave). Ex. 22-10's pedal is the tonic alone, so its °7 has no fifth to clash with. The V7 over the drone stays.

### 5. 124: vii°4/3 (A–C–D♯–F♯) in place of V4/2 before i6 (PDF 375). Not applied.

The tune's B5 (beats 2–3) is the root of V4/2. With C in the chord it would form a major 7th/minor 9th against the tune. Keep.

### 6. T's consequent in E minor (Exercise 6, PDF 397; §5, PDF 392). Not possible.

T's second half opens on vi (C♯ minor at 161, 177, 217). In E minor that chord is VI, a C-major triad, which the cyclic rule forbids. The mode change that varies a repetition already happens in the reprise's second idea, at 277–288 (c383).

### 7. 352 and the codetta. Kept, for the reasons c170 and c402 give.

Finding 1 does not touch the coda's ♭6 plan: there is still no C♮ in 341–358 (c470). Nor does it change the codetta's IV–IV–iv. The trio's ♭6s are now 202, 220 and 235, each falling to B in its own voice.
