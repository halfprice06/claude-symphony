## Findings

No finding needs a change to the score. Every principle in the table that applies to this movement is followed. Below are the places I tested against the reading, ranked by how close each came to a change.

### 1. Bars 209–224: no brass on T in its forte return (row 29; kept, with a corrected reason)

Adler's summary of the *Meistersinger* combination (PDF 178) is that each theme's colour mixes at least one string, one woodwind and one brass instrument. *Scheherazade* (PDF 179) puts horns at pitch on the main theme. At the trio's climax, T has Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1 and, from 217, Fl 1, but no brass. c217 kept this because "the only brass free are the horns, whose held pair is protected". That is not accurate. Hn 3·4 rest from 209 to 228, so a horn is free. I tested both possible octaves, and the stronger reason to keep the passage comes from the reading itself:
- At the pitch of the lower octave (Vln II and Cl 1, G♯4–A5), the line reaches A5 at 218–219. That is written E6 for horn in F, above written C6, the professional ceiling in Adler's Appendix A (adler2 PDF 305, as c066 cites it).
- An octave lower (G♯3–A4), a horn would run through Hn 2's held E4–A4 and Bsn 1's E4. It would cross Hn 2 at 212–214. A tune in the same colour and register as a held chord is what PDF 169 warns against: the ear separates two ideas only when they have opposing timbres and different designs.

c251 also saves the horn colour of T for 341. Kept. The record should say "Hn 3·4 are free but no horn octave fits" instead of "no horn is free".

### 2. Bars 349–356: the coda's last T statement uses the same colour as 209 (row 23; kept)

Mahler 4/iv (PDF 170–171) colours each return of a passage differently. At 349, T is in the violins in octaves with Cl 1 at pitch on the lower octave, the same combination as 209 without Fl 1. I considered Hn 4, which is free from 340, on the cellos' arco countermelody (E3–A3, written B3–E4). Cello and horn are the pairing Adler names (adler2 PDF 27). It would give 349 a colour T has never had. c251's reasons against it still stand: the hand-over 341 → 345 → 349 thins towards the strings, a horn at 349 would come eight bars before Hn 1's motto tail at 357 and blunt it, and in the *Lohengrin* model the last doubling goes to the woodwinds. The reading does not override this, because 349 already differs from 209 in everything except the tune's doubling. Its dynamic is pp, not f. The violas hold div. sustained notes instead of louré off-beats. The bass is pizz. instead of arco with contrabassoon and harp. Hn 1·2 are silent instead of holding thirds. Mahler's rule asks for a recognisable texture with new colours, and that is what 349 has. Kept.

### 3. Bars 105–108 and 305–308: no flute above the tutti theme for four bars (row 12; kept)

The Brahms example (PDF 164) has high flutes mark the first melody note of each bar, and the Mahler 1 chord (PDF 164) fills every upper octave. At 105–108 the top of the ff tutti is the violins and oboes at E5–G5, with nothing in the sixth octave until the flutes enter at E6 at 109. The Wagner discussion on the facing page (PDF 172) supports the present layout: hold back the highs and add upper octaves one at a time as the passage grows. c124 chose this layout on that ground. The flutes' entry at 109, on the theme's second phrase, is audible because they were held back. Kept.

### 4. Bars 45–56: no harmony between the melody's two octaves (row 12; kept)

At the forte second idea, Vln I and Fl 1 play at D6–G6 and Vln II and Ob 1 an octave lower. The harmony (Cl 1·2, Hn 1·2, violas) lies below B4, so the fifth octave holds only the tune's lower doubling. Brahms's "every chord in every octave" is advice for a brilliant ff tutti. This passage is a forte legato melody with a hemiola against the horns' dotted minims. Here the Schubert principle applies (PDF 169): melody and harmony in separate registers and colours are easier for the ear to tell apart. Filling the gap would put chord tones into the tune's own register. Kept.

### 5. Bars 197–200: T at pitch in four instruments (row 17; kept)

PDF 165 warns that a doubling at pitch loses each instrument's colour. Here Vln II, violas, Cl 1 and Hn 1 all play T's head at G♯4. This is the forte middle of the trio, where the doubling is for power. It is also the one place where T carries string, woodwind and brass together (row 29). The call above it is in a different rhythm and colour (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I; c117). Kept.

### 6. Bars 101–104 and 301–304: the hemiola build is scored almost the same both times (row 23; kept)

The reprise build differs where it matters for form: the strings play sfp at 301, and there are no trombones (row 9, held back for 333). A reprise that repeats its build in the same layout is ordinary in the classical scherzo. Mahler's rule applies to a passage that returns three times within one section. Kept.

### 7. Bars 339 and 361–376: chords that must fade (rows 10, 14, 15; kept)

At 339 everything but the brass stops on the crotchet, and the brass minims fade alone under tenuto, as in Adler's chord "under control" (PDF 164–165). At 361–364 the top note is Fl 1's G♯5, not a high flute pp (PDF 162). The harp's harmonics at 375 are the soft "ping" of PDF 165. Kept.
