## Findings

### 1. Bars 213–216: the harp's low A string is re-pedalled twice while it rings (applied)

Problem. Under T's forte return the harp LH doubles the bass, one crotchet a bar at mf: 213 A1, 214 A♯1, 215 A♯1, 216 B1, with "A♯" printed in the rest of 213 and "A♮" in the rest of 216. A1 and A♯1 are the same string. The A pedal has to move from natural to sharp half a second after A1 is struck and back two bars later after A♯1 has sounded twice. At A1 and A♯1 the string rings for several seconds, so unless the player damps it first the pedal change bends the ringing note audibly (the familiar pedal buzz), and the damp-and-pedal has to fit into two crotchets at 2. = 76.

Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: "In the middle and lower octaves the resonance of the strings is slightly prolonged, and dies away gradually ... in quick modulations this method [damping] is not feasible, and the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect." PDF 46: "The harp is essentially a diatonic instrument, since all chromatic passages depend on the manipulation of the pedals. For this reason the harp does not lend itself to rapid modulation." A chromatic step on one low string, there and back within four bars, is the case he warns against. The harp adds nothing at 214–215 that needs A♯: the basses (A♯1) and the contrabassoon (A♯2) already hold that bass.

Fix (applied). The harp takes C♯2, a chord tone already in the cellos (cis), Bn 2 and T, for the two bars; both pedal marks go, and the A pedal stays natural from 209 to 224:
```
tC.ily  tCHarpL: ... | a,,4 r4 r4 | cis,4 r4 r4 | cis,4 r4 r4 | b,,4 r4 r4 | ...   (213–216; was a,,4 r4_"A♯" r4 | ais,,4 … | ais,,4 … | b,,4 r4_"A♮" r4)
```
The harp still strikes once a bar, so its pulse under the tune is unchanged. Its C♯2 lies above the basses' A♯1 and an octave below the cellos, so the bass stays A♯. It is a harp doubling only, so the analyser's outer voices are not affected.
