## What else I studied

I read c005 and c095 (the earlier critics on this reading) and the engraving entries in HISTORY.md (c015, c065, c105, c115, c125, c155) so as not to repeat them. I wrote two LilyPond probes, kept in /tmp/critic-185/probe/ (probe.ily, probe2.ily) with the measuring script /tmp/critic-185/stemlen.py:
- A Staff engraver that logs every notehead's staff position, bar and pitch, and every clef change with its bar and position in the bar (rows 4–10).
- A Voice engraver that logs every printed stem after line breaking: its direction, its noteheads, whether it is beamed, and its drawn extent (rows 15–21).

The probes do not change the score. One result needed checking against the drawing. Twelve beamed stems in shared staves (Bn 1 71 and 299, Cl 2 105 and 305, Fl 1 333–334) first measured 2.32 spaces, under Gould's 2½. I tested a fix in the beam callback; it changed nothing. A debug print from the callback then showed why: LilyPond draws a beamed stem only to the beam's centre line, and the beam's own half-thickness (0.24) takes the visible stem to its outer edge. That makes 2.56 spaces, within Gould's minimum. I removed the test change, so this is not a finding.

### Checked and kept (decided, no change)

1. **Hn 2 alone at 285–288, E3–D♯3 in treble clef (D♯3 at 286 on four ledger lines).** c095 left this note because the staff was shared. Hn 1 now rests 277–288, so a bass clef would be possible. I keep the treble clef. Gould PDF 21 asks for "up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently", and only one note here passes three. A bass clef for four bars would add two clef changes: one at 285, between the phrases, and one back to treble at the end of the system, as PDF 23 asks. The Hn 2 part is in F, so the written note there is A♯3 with no ledger problem. Gould's priority is to keep the contour in one clef.
2. **Harp RH bass clef 341–344.** In treble clef the chords C♯3–A3 would need four to six ledger lines, so the clef is justified (PDF 21). It stands before the entry barline after 16 bars of rest (PDF 22–23), and the return to treble at 345 comes at a barline between chords. Keep.
3. **Harp RH chords G♯3–E4 below the treble stave at 153–168.** They need at most three ledger lines (PDF 21). In bass clef they would sit just as far above the stave. Keep.
4. **Hn 3·4 treble clef at 331 (c155).** The rest 321–330 lies inside one system (page 11), so Gould's rule for rests longer than a system (PDF 23) does not apply, and PDF 22 puts the clef before the entry barline, where it is. Keep.
