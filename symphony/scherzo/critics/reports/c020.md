## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The pivot from movement II lasts 1.4 seconds. Fermata on bar 2. Applied.

**Bars:** 1–3.

**Problem.** The scherzo's first sound is Hn 1 G♯4 pp, movement II's last A♭4. At 2. = 88 it lasts two bars, 6/264 of a minute = 1.36 s in the MIDI. At bar 3 it drops an octave with a crescendo into the horn call. After the pause between movements, 1.4 s at pp does not register as a held echo of the previous chord. It sounds like the first note of the call (G♯4–G♯3–E4…), an octave upbeat. The pivot and the quotation run together.

The other end of the movement is built the other way. The scherzo holds G♯ for 17 bars (368–384), and the finale's intro.ily comment relies on that length. Movement II holds its A♭4 in Vln II for six slow bars with a rit. and a fermata (87–92).

**Evidence.** A&S §2 (PDF 443): E is remote from both A♭ and C. The only tie is the common tone, so the common tone must be heard as a tone in its own right. §6 (PDF 447–448): a transient key between two stable ones is understood through what frames it. Here the frame is A♭/G♯ on both sides of the scherzo. §16 (PDF 456) reads the same way: the scherzo expands A♭ (VI) by its lower third and returns to it in the finale's German sixth. That reading depends on the G♯ being audible at both ends.

**Fix (global.ily, bar count unchanged).** Bar 1 is in tempo. Bar 2 has a fermata, which prints on every staff, as at the G.P. 348. The tempo starts with the pizzicato E at 3, so the horn call (G♯3–E4–D♯4–E4) begins as its own gesture after the held pivot.

```
  s2. \mt 88 s2.\fermata \mt 264
  s2.*10
```

This replaces the first `s2.*12`. The MIDI now holds the G♯ for about 2.7 s, and the movement lasts 280 s instead of 278 s. The protected bars 1–7 keep every note. The fermata strengthens the reason they are protected.

### 2. The timpani labels at the two joins are incomplete. Applied.

**Bars:** 1 and 384.

**Problem 1: no retuning at the end.** Movement II tells the timpanist at its last bar how to retune for the scherzo ("muta A♭ in E, … (for the Scherzo, during the pause)", II coda 92). The scherzo ends with no such line. The finale needs all four drums changed (E→G, G→A♭, B→C, E→E♭). The player meets that only on the finale's first page, which opens with a ff A♭2 on the drum that held G.

**Problem 2: no sticks at bar 1.** The scherzo's first stick marking is "soft sticks" at 153. 1–152 have none. The player arrives with movement II's last instruction ("ord." at II 79, after "near the rim"). The reprise of the same material is marked "hard sticks" (246), so the composer wants hard sticks in the scherzo proper.

**Evidence.** Adler, part 2, PDF 81: "It is advisable to mark changes in tuning." PDF 80: mallets come "in hard, medium, and soft varieties", and the tone depends on them. The cycle's own convention is set by movement II's muta at 92 and by the finale's bar-1 label.

**Fix.**
- intro.ily, timpani label: add a third line, `\italic "hard sticks"`, after "(retune in the pause: A♭ → E, C → G, D♭ → B, E♭ → E)".
- coda.ily, the last bar of codaTimp: replace `R2.` with
  `\once \override MultiMeasureRestText.parent-alignment-X = #RIGHT \once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #RIGHT R2._\markup \small \italic \right-column { "muta: low E in G, G in A♭," "B in C, high E in E♭" "(for the Finale, in the pause)" }`
  The right alignment keeps the text inside the margin. Without it, the text ran off the page.

The tunings are unchanged, and the last tuning is still E, G, B, E, which the finale relies on. I print the muta in the score as well as the part, so the conductor sees how long the pause has to be.
