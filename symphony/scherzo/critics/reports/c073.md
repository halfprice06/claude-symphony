## Findings

### 1. Bars 296–297: the cut in the reprise fugato joins V of D minor directly to the A-minor stretto (applied)

**Problem.** dB shortens the fugato. Bars 289–296 copy 57–64, and 297–300 copy 69–72, so the four bars 65–68 are gone. In the exposition, the viola entry ends on V of D minor (64: E4–C♯4–A3 over A2), and that V resolves onto D-minor harmony over the A at 65 (Vln II enters, violas A4–G4–F4). The A-minor stretto then arrives at 69 from its own dominant at 68 (E–G♯–B in Vln II and violas, bass E).

The reprise skips both steps. At 296 the violas, who are the top voice there (only violas and cellos play), outline A major with the leading tone of D, C♯4. Bar 297 then brings the whole orchestra in f on A minor, with C♮5 in the same violas. The result:

- The leading tone in the top voice goes to C♮, not to D.
- The A-major chord is left as an unresolved dominant.
- The f entry of the stretto, the reprise's first tutti arrival since 285, has no dominant before it.

So the reprise's development section arrives less firmly than the exposition's, at a point where it should match it.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 93: V–I combines "the strongest possible harmonic motion (in the bass) with the strongest possible melodic motion".
- PDF 97: a V that "creates the expectation" of its tonic raises 7̂.
- PDF 100: 7̂ in the top voice "must" move to 8̂.
- PDF 129: a root-position V before the I, with the bass leaping a 5th or 4th, "strongly articulat[es]" the arrival.

The exposition already does exactly this at 68→69.

**Fix (applied).** In dB.ily, bar 296 becomes V of A, the harmony of bar 68:

```
dBVla (296): e'4-. cis'-. a-.   ->  e'4-. b-. gis-.
dBVc  (296): a,2 e4)            ->  e2 b,4)
```

The bass now runs D3 (295) – E3 – B2 – A2 (297), and the violas' G♯3 is taken up by the A3/A4 of 297. Rhythm, dynamics and the countersubject's slur are unchanged. Counterpoint: 295→296 is contrary motion (F4→E4 over D3→E3), and 296→297 is contrary motion (G♯3→C5 over B2→A2). The viola's subject now ends on the dominant of the next entry's key, which is normal practice for a fugato entry that modulates. This is a change to a recorded statement (see Theme changes), not to theme A itself.

### 2. Bars 16, 32, 256, 272: theme A's soft half cadences are V7, and the 7th leaps up a seventh (applied)

**Problem.** Theme A's first phrase ends on V at 16, with the melody at rest on 5̂ (F♯4–D♯4–B3), and the consequent closes with a PAC at 20. That is the A&S semicadence/authentic-cadence pair. To keep the accompaniment below the melody's low dip (c007), Vln II plays A3, the 7th, at 16. At 17 it jumps up to G4. So the dissonance is never resolved in its own register, and the 7th makes the half cadence push forward instead of closing the sentence. The same happens at 32 (the strings' answer), 256 (reprise, pizz.) and 272 (the compressed counterstatement).

In the loud versions the 7th is handled correctly. At 108 and 308 the tenor line and the violas take A to G. At 24 and 264, Vln II's A4 falls to G4. Those bars are fine.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 99: "If the melodic line comes to rest on 7̂, 2̂, or 5̂, V will generally produce a better effect than V7… For this reason, V7 is rarely used in a semicadence."
- PDF 98, 102 and 104: the 7th resolves down by step, in the last voice that holds it.
- PDF 94–95: the half cadence ends "a sentence", the PAC ends the paragraph.

**Fix (applied).**

```
sAVlnTwo (16, 32): r4 a4-. a4-.  ->  r4 b4-. b4-.
dAVlnTwo (256):    r4 a4 a4      ->  r4 b4 b4
dAVlnTwo (272):    r4 a4 r4      ->  r4 b4 r4
```

B3 is the root, which A&S's doubling rule prefers (PDF 95). It stays below the melody on beat 2 and meets its B3 at the unison on beat 3. The V triad is complete: B in the basses, F♯3 in the violas, D♯4 in the melody. Bars 24 and 264 keep their V7, because there the 7th resolves in register and the C6 appoggiatura is the theme's peak.

### Points checked against the reading, no change

- **T at 155 (and 171, 211, coda 343).** I considered replacing the root-position V7 inside the opening tonic with an inversion (PDF 110, 121–123). V6/5 would put the leading tone in the bass while it is also in the melody (D♯5), which A&S call wrong (Ex. 7-5e). V4/3 would make the bass F♯ twice before the ii at 158. And PDF 119's warning is about 2̂–1̂ or 7̂–1̂ on top. Here the melody goes 5̂–4̂–3̂, so no cadence is made in mid-phrase. Kept.
- **The reprise's PAC at 287 before the fugato at 289.** A PAC is "the most final" cadence (PDF 94). But a first part that closes in the tonic, followed by a contrasting section that returns to V (301–304) and to the tonic (305), is a sectional rounded binary. The drop to a solo cello pp marks the new section, as the double bar does in Kuhnau. Kept (c003, c013).
- **Link 141–152 (tonic without a dominant before the trio).** A&S PDF 102 says a repeated chord takes little accent, and the trio's downbeat is marked by tempo, key and colour rather than by a V–I. Beethoven 9/ii and the Eroica also go straight from tonic to tonic-major trio. c063's reason for keeping the link (expectation built on a sparse texture) still holds. Kept.
- **Proportions and length (276 s).** Unchanged, as c063 found.
