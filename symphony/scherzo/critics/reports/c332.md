## Findings

### 1. Bar 331: the Neapolitan 6/4 goes straight to V7. A&S's own model for this bass is the N6/4 resolving to an augmented sixth on the same C (applied)

**Bars.** In the coda's descent, 329–332, the bass falls E–D–C–B (i, ♮VII, ♭II6/4, V7) under the motive-x ostinato, crescendo into the ff tonic at 333.
- Bar 331 is C–F–A for all three beats.
- It goes directly to B7 at 332.
- Vln I, Cl 1 and Hn 3 leap F→D♯; the inner A (Vln II A4, Cl 2, Bsn 1, Hn 4 A3) stays.

**What is wrong.** The reading names this exact bass as the classic approach to an augmented sixth. The coda, the movement's last approach to its dominant, uses only half of the procedure.
- **The bass.** PDF 510–511, sec. 10 and Ex. 29-17: in minor the bass moves stepwise down from 1̂ to ♭6̂ (here E–D–C) and the augmented sixth stands on ♭6̂. The upper voice rises 4̂–♯4̂–5̂ (Ex. 29-3a, 29-20). At 331 the 4̂, A4 in Vln II, is already there and simply waits.
- **The N6/4 and the augmented sixth together.** PDF 525, sec. 25, and review point 9 on PDF 527: "Sometimes a 'Neapolitan 6/4' decorates the German 6/5 in the manner of a cadential 6/4" on the same bass. The 6/4's 4th falls to the 3rd, as F→E does here (Ex. 29-41, Schubert).
- **Why French, not German.** The German form here is C–E–G–A♯, which the C-major rule forbids. The French, C–E–F♯–A♯, is open, and PDF 507 calls it "particularly well suited" as the chord before V.
- **The melodic line.** PDF 483–484 (Ex. 28-2c, e): the F→D♯ diminished third is usually filled by a passing 1̂. "♭2̂ might seem to move directly to 1̂ … but this '1̂' functions as a passing tone." The filled line is still "peculiarly intense".
- **Structural weight.** PDF 503: an augmented sixth opening to the octave "sounds like nothing else in tonal music" and marks important structural points. The arrival at 333 (the last tutti and the climb to the only fff and B6) is the movement's largest. At present its approach is weaker than the retransition's at 244.

**Earlier objections, answered.**
- **c062 (on the then iv6).** c062 said an A♯ would "alter the motive-x ostinato in four string parts" and rewrite c032's tenths.
  - Only beat 3's crotchet changes. Motive x's rhythm is untouched, and the ostinato's pitches already change from bar to bar (c033, c089).
  - Cl 1's tenth over the bass comes back: E4 over C, then D♯4 over B, as c032 designed it before c080 put F4 there.
- **c080 (protecting ♭II at 331).** c080's reason is that the finale reads the F as IV6/4 over C at IV 272. F stays on beats 1–2, so that link holds.
- **c242 ("one strong augmented-sixth event per approach to V").** That rule is not in A&S. PDF 503 and 526 (review 6) tie augmented sixths to structural points, and 331 is one.
