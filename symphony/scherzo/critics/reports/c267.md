## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 341–347: the cellos' pizzicato roots double the basses and the harp under T's soft return (applied)

**Problem.** After the general pause at 340, T returns "tempo del Trio" in A major: Hn 3 p dolce (341–344), then Ob 1 with Bsn 1's countermelody (345–348), over violas' held notes, the harp's off-beat chords and pizz. bass. On every downbeat the root is sounded three times at once: harp LH (A1), basses pizz. (A1) and cellos pizz. (A2), with the violas on A3 above. At 341 the chord is A1–A1–A2–A3 under the horn's C♯4; the third exists only in the horn and in the harp's off-beats. The trio's own first statement, which this passage recalls, has only the harp and the pizz. basses on the bass (153–168, 185–188). The comment of c148 at 341 says the bass is "as at 153", but no cello plays at 153. The cellos then change to arco and enter at 349 with the countermelody (c107), so their pizz. bars only prepare an entry that would have more effect from silence.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 140, rule 7: "the least important note of a string voicing is the chordal root … Many arrangers make a habit of automatically doubling the bass notes with the lowest cello … this is usually unnecessary and sometimes can be detrimental to the overall sound, 'anchoring' or 'grounding' it. It can also 'cloud' the clarity of the bass line." PDF 145 (Ex. 195): with the third supplied elsewhere, "the addition of an E in the bottom cello at this point is unnecessary. It would have 'grounded' the voicing, making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating." PDF 146: the bottom cello plays roots "for temporary richness and depth … but *not* to reinforce the bass." PDF 168 (Ex. 214, image checked): when the lead horn takes the melody "the texture becomes lighter … while the bass changes to pizzicato", the bass alone. PDF 150–151 (Ex. 201–202, images checked): the low strings come in as a unison counter line under the high violins. Ex. 204 (PDF 153–157, images checked) is the closest model: the soloist plays first over harp and bass alone, and the strings come in under the second half of the tune. The score already works this way in the trio (153 Cl 1 over harp and Cb; 169 the cellos enter with their counter line). Without the cellos, 341–356 follows the same order: horn and oboe over harp and pizz. basses, then the violins with the cellos' counter line entering at 349.

**Fix (coda.ily, `codaVc`):** bars 341–347 become rests; "arco" at 349 is removed, because the cellos last played arco at 339.
