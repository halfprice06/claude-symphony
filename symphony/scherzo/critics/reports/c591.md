## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Harp head 73–80 without motive x's quavers: built, not kept

**Candidate.** This is the change a listener would hear most, and the one the reading most directly suggests. In the string-free F-major episode the harp plays theme A's head as three even crotchets (`a''4 f'' a''`, `g'' d'' g''`, `f'' c'' f''`), without motive x's two quavers. PDF 206 asks for lines derived from the tune and for a recurrent figure that unifies the score. Restoring the quavers would make the episode's opening an exact quotation of theme A's rhythm.

**Built** in /tmp/critic-591/cand/sections/sB.ily:
```
sBHarpR 73  a''4( f''4 a''4    ->  a''4( f''8 f''8 a''4
        75  g''4( d''4 g''4    ->  g''4( d''8 d''8 g''4
        77  f''4(\< c''4 f''4  ->  f''4(\< c''8 c''8 f''4
```
The build is clean: 0 warnings, 13 pages, part lengths all match, no C-major triad, themecheck 52/52, and the same single outer-voice parallel (bar 69). Repeated strokes on one harp string are playable at this speed.

**Why it is not kept.** The episode now sharpens the motive in three stages:
- the harp sings the head legato, espress., slurred over two-bar groups (73–80);
- Cl 1 brings back the quavers as portato (`d''4( a'8-- a'-- d''4`, 81–88);
- the violas return to staccato motive x at 89 under the timpani.

The candidate removes the first stage. That gives four statements of the same figure in descending sequence (73, 75, 77, 81 …), the monotony that ch. 1's variety rule and PDF 207's "not overdone" warn against.

PDF 206 also says to let the tune lead, and PDF 204 says to write for the instrument. A legato transformation that suits the harp's plucked, decaying sound is the more idiomatic choice here. The rhythm of the tail (minim and two falling quavers) already tells the listener this is theme A.

c001's protection of 73–80 had no recorded reason. This is the reason, and it should be recorded. Candidate kept in /tmp/critic-591/cand for reference; not applied to /tmp/critic-591/work.

### 2. An upbeat into the second idea at 44.3 (PDF 207, Ex. 285): considered, not built

"Carry one bar into the next" and the cue principle both suggest an anacrusis, for example A5 to D6, before the forte entry at 45. Bar 44 now has F♯ on beat 2 and only Ob 1's D4 on beat 3, so the D4 already gives the cue pitch (PDF 222). That near-silent beat is the effect: the second idea arrives forte after a breath. An upbeat would also break three things:
- c027's homorhythmic design;
- Vln I's shift rest (c016);
- Fl 1's breath (c076).

c589 weighed the same point.

### 3. Fl 1's answers at 172 and 176 stop on the head's third note (PDF 206): no change

`gis''8 a'' b''4` and `dis'''8 e''' fis'''4` leave out the head's fourth, as the codetta did before c501. At 183, `fis''8 gis'' ais''4 | b''2` completes four notes into the cadence. Completing 172 in quavers (G♯–A–B–E) would use up 183's effect. 176 cannot complete upward: it would reach B6, which is reserved for the violins at 336.

### 4. The second idea (45–56, 121–132, 277–288), question 6 (PDF 220): no note change

The idea is a sentence: a two-bar idea, its sequence a step higher, then a cadence. That plan is normal, not a fault. Ex. 277's tests are already met:
- the passing quavers (c411);
- the ties;
- the rising sixths;
- motive x's rhythm at 53 (c451).

I checked each other contour for phrase 2 (51–52) against its chords (A7/E, D/F♯, A7/C♯; c032), and each one puts a minim on a non-chord tone. The minims cannot be broken up, because the hemiola is protected (c531).

Ex. 283 objects to repeated notes, but the D6 anticipation at 52→53 (and B5 at 128→129, 284→285) is a sung cadential anticipation, not an awkward inner-part repeat. It gives the cadence phrase its upbeat; c541 deliberately kept it at phrase 2 while phrase 1 got the leap-and-step.

The point stays open for a form reading, not this one.

### 5. Trio middle 193–196: Vln I's T-head sequence rises a third, not a fourth (PDF 206): no change

`fis''2 gis''4 | a''2 cis'''4` is the head's step–step shape, but its last interval is a third. With D6 for C♯6 at 194.3 it would end on a fourth, but D would sit against Cl 2's E4 in an F♯m7 (ii7) chord and leave out the chord's C♯. It would then fall a third to B5 at 195. The passage is a free sequence leading to the forte T at 197, and is not a statement. No change.

### 6. T bar 12 (164, 180, 220, 352): even crotchets after the peak (Ex. 273): follows

`cis''4 b' a' | gis' b' e''~` changes from T's minim figures to crotchets, which drives into the cadence as Ex. 273's cellos do. The fourth F♯5–C♯5 leaves E5 and D♯5 out of the descent, and the cadence 165–166 sounds exactly those notes. No change.

### 7. First trio statement 160→161: tune and bass in octaves B→C♯ across the breath (PDF 209): no change

The bass B1 (harp, pizz.) is struck on beat 1, the tune reaches B4 on beat 2, and both step to C♯ at 161. Statements 2 and 3 put F♯ in the bass on beat 3 (176, 216). The first statement is protected: root per bar under the drone (c089, c257, c433). The breath and the decayed pizzicato make the octave hard to hear, and Sebesky's contrary-motion advice does not outweigh that design.
