# Movement II mockup loop, series 2: brief for every critic and actor

## Goal

The mockup is movement II (Adagio, A flat major) of the symphony, played in REAPER by EastWest Hollywood Orchestra Opus Edition (Diamond) through the Opus plugin and improved by actor-critic cycles. Each cycle makes it sound more like a real orchestra playing this score well. The score is fixed. Only the rendering changes.

Series 1 (Dan, 2026-10-11: "It's ok. Use 30 actor subagent critic loops to make it better. Use all available resources.") mocked up score `symphony/mvt2/v77` in `symphony/mvt2/reaper/`: cycles c00-c26 were rendered, and c27 has critic reports on the c26 render only (no actor). Its cycle folders are linked into `cycles/` here.

Series 2 (Dan, 2026-11-06): the score is now `symphony/mvt2/v600`, the end of the composition loop. Cycle 27 is the port to v600 and its baseline render: series 1's c26 tables carried over and checked against v600, no musical changes beyond the port (`cycles/c27/`, `loop/HISTORY.md`). Cycles 28-47 are Dan's 20 improvement loops. Dan will judge the result by ear, so favour changes a listener hears; his priorities are articulations, per-track volume and blend, and long timpani rolls must not drown other parts.

What changed in the score between v77 and v600 (check whether carried-over per-onset tables still fit): 1261 of v77's 1598 notes are unchanged (onset, staff, pitch); v600 has 1628 notes, about 25% more dynamics, slurs, hairpins, breaths and scripts, and the same length. The changes concentrate in qn 117-270. The bass clarinet solo of bars 31-36 is an octave lower; the basses play bars 23-28 an octave higher; the violas are divided from bar 84 to the end and in bars 80-81; the basses (bars 65-70 and from bar 87) and celli (bars 83-86) play "4 soli"; the strings put mutes on again in the coda; the trombone quotation's first note (qn 212.5) is no longer accented and the allargando starts a bar later (bar 64). The port's decisions are in `README.md` ("Port to v600") and `cycles/c27/PORT.md`.

Read `mockup-kit/LEARNINGS.md` (from "What a listener noticed that measurement missed" on) before your first change: it records the measured lessons of movement II series 1 and movement I about this library and pipeline. Movement I's own later lessons are in `symphony/mvt1/reaper-v621/loop/LEARNINGS.md` (read only). Do not relearn them.

Dan's standing priorities for renders (from `symphony/render/BRIEF.md`, written for the earlier FluidSynth/VPO renders, still his rules):
1. Articulations must be audible and correct: slurs/legato, tenuto, accents, tremolo, pizzicato, as printed.
2. Each track's level must match its printed dynamic and its role (melody above accompaniment) at every dynamic level.
3. Blend: one ensemble in one room (common reverb, seating, matched colours, nothing sticking out because of a sample set).
4. Accents: emphasis and weight, not a hard click (about 2-4 dB, no sharp transient).
5. Timpani rolls: start at the printed dynamic, then settle under the texture. Only a printed crescendo makes a roll grow. A long roll is never the loudest stem unless the timpani lead.
6. The delivered mp3 must be comfortable at one headphone volume (Dan, 2026-10-11, after series 1 c07: "I have to turn my head phones to max volume to hear the soft parts well, which makes the loudest parts almost unbearable"): soft passages clearly audible, loud passages not painful. Targets (`loop/DYNAMICS-RESEARCH.md`): delivered LRA 14-16 LU, 16-19 dB from the 10th-percentile short-term loudness to the loudest, no reversed crescendos or diminuendos, the climax (bars 61-64) still clearly the loudest, delivery limiter at most about 3 dB. `render_cycle.py` masters the mp3 with `tools/deliver_master.py`; do part of the work in the mix (soft_trim_db, solo roles), not with CC11. pp must still sound soft through timbre and texture.
7. Memory: keep REAPER's settled footprint under about 45 GB on this 96 GB Mac (unused articulation rows are unloaded: `UNLOAD_UNUSED_ROWS`; series 1 settled at 44 GB with these 32 tracks). Do not add Opus instances without removing others. Restart REAPER before every render and quit it after (save first); record REAPER memory after the render in CHANGES.md. Another user process (the Movement I thread) may hold several GB; check `top -o mem` before rendering.

Dan's listening notes on movement II series 1 (2026-10-12, after c24; each came from a person hearing a render after the measurements had passed it):
8. **No audible sample-loop pulsing on long held notes** ("sounds like there's some kind of weird bowing effect being applied on the last note"). Hollywood Strings sustains loop every 3.95 s; one sample held for many seconds pulses in level and tone colour, and sections that start a chord together pulse in step. Series 1 c26 re-bowed the final chord by hand (`REBOW_AT`, fitted shapes in `build/rebow_fills.json`); since c27 `score_to_parts.py` also re-bows every other string note over 4.5 s (`AUTO_REBOW`; held tremolos and the listed points excepted), staggered per section (`tools/space_rebows.py`). Check every held note over about 6 s on the stems and the master: the 100-300 Hz band level and the spectral centroid folded at the loop period (autocorrelation at 3.95 s), and the level dip and step at each re-bow. Brass, winds and timpani rolls loop too.
9. **Brass accents soft: weight, not bite** ("the accented notes the trombones play during the call back to movement 1 at the climax: the accents are too harsh", Trombone 1 bars 63-64). The HO accent samples and an accent CC11 bump into the top layer put a 2-5 kHz burst 7-13 dB over the note body. Since c27 every brass, wind and string accent plays the plain sustain with a volume swell (`GENTLE_ACCENT`; Trombone 1's two accents keep their c26 per-onset values). Measure 2-5 kHz energy over the first 80 ms of each accent against the note's body and against the other sections, as well as level over the neighbours.

Dan's listening notes on movement I series 2 (2026-11-06, from hearing its c34-c35), standing checks for every lens here too:
- **S1. Repeated notes must be heard as separate notes.** Check every same-pitch repeat (winds, brass and strings, slurred or not) for an audible gap or re-attack in the stem and in the master; the library's repetition rows (`loop/MANUAL-GAPS.md` #2) have not been tried.
- **S2. The perceived centre of each attack must sit on the beat.** Measure the perceptual onset (where the attack reaches its body level, or the energy centroid of the first 80-150 ms), not the MIDI note-on, against the beat grid, per section and per articulation; the leads align note starts, not perceived attacks. In this slow movement late soft string entries and slow legato arrivals are the usual cases.
- **S3. A subito must not sound like a glitch.** A sudden drop must keep the hall's ring of the louder level decaying under the softer one. Mechanism (Movement I c36, measured): CC7 and CC11 act inside Opus, before its hall, so a drop there leaves the tail ringing; REAPER's track volume envelope (vol_db: roles, trims, hairpin dB ramps, fills) acts after the hall and cuts the tail with the note. Put sudden drops in CC11/CC7, never as a fast fall in the volume envelope. v600 prints no "sub." mark; its sudden drops are the fp at bar 47 (qn 148, Violins II, violas, basses; FP_SPIKE on CC11), the short dip into the breath before bar 59 (qn 195-196, CC11), the strings' niente in bar 66, and every role or trim row that ends with a step under a sounding chord: check those for cut tails.

## Facts already established (details and numbers in mockup-kit/LEARNINGS.md)

- HO: CC11 crossfades the dynamic layers and sets volume; CC1 crossfades a non-vibrato and a vibrato layer in the woodwind and string KS Masters (not a depth control; on the strings the Molto Vib set is recorded louder). In the Hollywood Brass KS Masters and the Clarinet KS Master CC1 crossfades the dynamic layers (`CC1_DYN_TRACKS`, not used by movement II yet). Make balance corrections in the volume envelope so CC11 keeps each printed dynamic's timbre.
- Legato patches are monophonic; transitions need overlap; legato targets start early by measured per-family leads; a same-pitch re-attack on Leg Slur MAX plays the slow first-note layer.
- KS Masters load only some rows; `build_reaper.py` loads the rows a track uses and unloads the rest, so a track whose set of rows changes is fully rebuilt.
- A silent 3 qn pre-roll carries each track's first keyswitch and CC values and the CC36 round-robin reset; `render_cycle.py` cuts it off, so the rendered files are on score time.
- The audio device is part of the render conditions: `render_cycle.py` refuses to render unless REAPER's device is 48000 Hz, 512-sample blocks, no input; REAPER closes the device by itself when it is not the frontmost app (`reaper.Audio_Init()` in a bridge job reopens it just before the render; Movement I LEARNINGS c34-c35), and a Bluetooth headset as the system input wrecked a Movement I render (24 kHz mic opened, every output shifted 256 samples). Never save with `Main_SaveProjectEx` and a path (it writes a copy elsewhere); use `reaper_ctl.py save`.

## Rules

- Only the actor drives REAPER. Critics never run `rpr.py` or `reaper_ctl.py`, never touch the bridge folder, never write into the `.rpp`, and never start renders. Critics may run their own analysis scripts on the rendered files (`~/.venvs/claude-composes-reaper/bin/python` has numpy, soundfile, mido; also ffmpeg, lilypond).
- REAPER is shared with the Movement I thread through `~/.rpr-bridge/REAPER.lock`. Before any REAPER step the actor runs `~/.venvs/claude-composes-reaper/bin/python symphony/mvt2/reaper-v600/tools/reaper_ctl.py lock "mvt2 cNN actor"` (it waits up to 6 h while the other thread holds REAPER), keeps every bridge job's `.lua` file inside `symphony/mvt2/reaper-v600/`, and after the render and any `--mix-only` write for a stem fix runs `reaper_ctl.py quit` and then `reaper_ctl.py unlock`. Never break, edit or remove the other thread's lock. Do all non-REAPER work (edits, parts.json, diffs, simulations) before taking the lock, so the lock is held only for the build, the restart, the render and the save.
- Never edit the score or anything in `symphony/mvt2/v600/`, `symphony/mvt2/reaper/` (series 1, read only; `cycles/c00`-`c26` here are links into it), `symphony/mvt1/`, `symphony/render/`, `mockup-kit/`. Never commit or push. Never delete files outside your own cycle folder and your own scratch folder.
- Do not install software (no pip, brew or downloads). No GUI or computer use; REAPER is driven only through the bridge and `reaper_ctl.py`.
- Everything that changes the result lives in scripts or config files under `symphony/mvt2/reaper-v600/` (tools, `build/score_config.py`, `build/*.json`), so the project can be rebuilt from scratch. A setting made only inside REAPER is lost when a track is rebuilt. Keep generic rules generic: a rule that would help any score goes in `tools/` with a default, its movement II values in `build/score_config.py`.
- The printed notes, dynamics, articulations and tempos are the reference. A rendering must not contradict them. Small liberties that real players take (shaping inside a hairpin, a slight swell on a long note, breath before a breath mark, a little rubato inside a printed rit.) are allowed if the score supports them.
- One render per cycle, from a freshly restarted REAPER; fix level-only problems on the stems afterwards (`tools/stem_fix.py`, mockup-kit LEARNINGS section 15) instead of rendering twice.
- Keep scratch under 2 GB per agent, in `/private/tmp/claude-501/mockup-loop/mvt2-<your label>/`, and delete your scratch folder when you finish. Before rendering, check `df -h /System/Volumes/Data`: at least 30 GB must be free. Stem fixes keep copies only of the stems they replace.
