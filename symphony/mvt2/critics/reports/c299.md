## What else I studied

I read the brief, the lenses, the plan, the end of HISTORY.md and the earlier reports on bar 76 (c073, c091, c101). I looked at all eight pages of v299's score.pdf and read every section file. I diffed v289 against v299 and dumped all-pairs parallels from both MIDI files with tools/critic-scripts/allpairs_par.py.

The corpus has no Brahms score, so the comparison with Brahms 3/ii uses two sources. The first is my knowledge of the work. The second is two sources found on the web. The [BHSO programme note](https://www.bhso.org.uk/?p=1292) gives the scoring: a folk-like tune in the clarinets, supported by the bassoons, with brief violin and cello interjections; "chamber music" scale; trombones used with restraint; trumpets and timpani silent; a quiet ending. A [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bach-to-brahms/indistinct-formal-functions-and-conflicting-temporal-processes-in-the-second-movement-of-brahmss-third-symphony/96B96597C6C26315C4EC261D94C7F64C) quotes Frisch's sonata-form reading: first theme at m. 1, second theme at m. 41, closing group at m. 63. The two Brahms slow-movement scores in the reading support the comparison: Brahms 2/iii's ending (PDF 252) and Brahms 1/ii's opening (PDF 267).

**Holistic verdict.** On the conduct of the texture, a conductor would accept this movement as the work of a master. It shares Brahms 3/ii's main habits:
- A wind choir carries the first statement (7–14).
- Short string answers follow (15–22).
- Each return is rescored (Adler PDF 255).
- Trumpets are silent and the brass is used sparingly.
- The texture thins to a soft close.

The one point where a Brahms score would not do what this score does is the register plan before the last climax (Finding 1). Brahms 3/ii keeps its top register back. Its loudest bars are short, and they gain their effect from what came before. In v299 the climax pitch, B♭6, is heard three bars before the climax. The melodic material is protected and I do not reopen it.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 74–77: Vln I reaches the climax note B♭6 at 76, three bars before the ff climax at 79 (applied in /tmp/critic-299/work)

**Problem.** In A′, Vln I plays theme A an octave above Vln II. At 75–77 that puts Vln I on F6–B♭6–A♭6 and then G6. The B♭6 at 76 b2 is the same pitch as the movement's climax at 79, where it is the ff appoggiatura approached by the grace run. As a result, the climax adds loudness and winds but no new register. The composer's own rule says the opposite. At c105, A♭6 replaced B♭6 at 58 so that "B♭6 [is] kept for 79", and "registral peak B♭6 at 79 only" stood on the do-not-touch list from c105 to c114. Bar 76 is the one exception.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 238 rejects two doublings of Schubert's 8/i theme (Ex. VIII-4, VIII-5) because they "anticipate the expansion of the registers to which Schubert is heading" at the climactic cadence. The flute "enters almost undetected … but is not really heard until it plays the highest notes of the cadential chords."
- Adler PDF 154 (Tchaikovsky Serenade): "How stunning the big chord … sounds after this pale, subdued harmony", because the violins were kept "in a nonbrilliant register."
- The B-section climax shows the same plan already working: violins and flute top out at A♭6/A6 at 58–63. The pitch B♭6 is new only at 79 if 76 does not use it.

**Answer to the earlier reasons.** c073 closed this point with A&S PDF 94, which lists the markers of a goal and does not include register. Adler PDF 238 and PDF 154 are the orchestration answer: register is how the scoring marks the goal. c073 rejected c071's fix (rest Fl 1 at 75–77) because it touched the protected A′ octaves and Fl 1's p< at 75. My fix leaves the flute alone. c101 closed "Vln I single octave 70–74" by keeping the octaves at 71–74. Those bars are unchanged. Wind doubling still arrives at 77, which matches c101's reading of Rimsky.

**Fix (applied).** In `sections/aTwo.ily`, `aTwoVlnOne`, Vln I joins Vln II at pitch from the pickup at 74 b3 to the end of 77, then takes the upper octave again on the f A♭5 of the deceptive cadence at 78:

### 3. Bar 87: the IV6 over F doubles its bass in five parts, including C.a. F4 (recorded, not applied)

Adler PDF 152 and Ex. VIII-25 (PDF 257) say that a first-inversion triad on scale degree 6 should not double its bass. When it must, the doubling goes near the bottom. At 87 the F sounds in Cb, Vc, Vla (lower), Hn 4 and C.a. F4. The root D♭ is only in Cl 1 (D♭5). The top of the chord is therefore thin on the root and heavy on the third.

The C.a.'s F is the first note of the protected F–F♭–E♭ coda line, and the bar lies in the protected coda (c263) and the "soft wind chords 87–88". I record the point and do not ask for a change. If the composer reopens the coda, the cheapest fix is the violas at 87: `<aes des'>2.` instead of `<f aes>2.`, which goes to `<fes aes>` at 88 by step. That puts the root in the middle and takes one F out.

### 4. Points checked against the reading and found consistent (no change)

- **71–78** follows Adler's foreground/middleground/background model exactly (PDF 142–143). The cello counter-melody was heard alone with the theme at 23. The viola eighth figure is new at 71 and has its own rhythm. The theme sits on top.
- **1–6 and 87–92** melt strings into winds and winds back into strings, as Brahms 2/iii does at its end (PDF 252). The last chord keeps one wind (Cl 2) inside the string chord.
- **7–30.** The choirs are heard separately (winds 7–14, strings 23–30) before they join at 71 and 79, as PDF 253 asks.
- **Same restraint as Brahms 3/ii.** As in Brahms 3/ii, the trumpets are silent, the trombones play only at 60–64, and the timpani play as colour.
- **Brahms 3/ii's coda recalls its second theme. This score's coda does not.** c263 decided this with reasons (the finale quotes theme B), so I do not raise it.

## What must not be touched

The 1–6 link, the horn solo 7–14, the plain unison statement 23–30 with its cello counter-melody, the 71–74 violin octaves, the build 53–61 with the tutti 61–62, the quotations, the V♭9 at 80–81 and the plagal close 87–92 are at a master's level. Leave them as they are.
