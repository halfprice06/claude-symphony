## Findings

The harp writing follows almost every rule in this chapter. Four passes on the same pages have already corrected the pedal plan, the range, the rolls and the harmonics. Two small playability points remain, both in the harp's melodic moment at 82–83. A third (idiom at 53–56) is recorded only.

### 1. Bar 83: say which hand plays the harmonics (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's harmonics (written A♭3, C4, E♭4) are on the RH staff, so the harpist will start reading them as RH harmonics. The RH plays the pickup E♭4–F4 at pitch on 82 b3 (c116) and has one eighth (about 0.6 s at ♩=52) to get from plucking F4 to placing the index knuckle on the node of the A♭3 string, a third lower. The LH is free: its only note, A♭1 at 82 b1, is tied into 83 to let it ring (c214). It has beats 2 and 3 of 82 (about 2.5 s) to rest its palm on the A♭3 node.

**Evidence.** Adler part 1 PDF 116 gives the two methods: LH palm on the node with the LH thumb plucking, or RH index knuckle on the node with the RH thumb plucking. Only the LH is free and already in position here. The web guides above say the RH takes one harmonic at a time and the LH is the more flexible hand. PDF 118: "The composer should strive to make his intentions very clear in the notation." Harpists choose their own hands, but at this tempo the choice has to be made before the pickup, so it is worth printing.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaHarpR, bar 83):** change only the text above the first harmonic:
```
\voiceOne aes4.(-\flageolet^\markup \small \italic "harm., L.H. (sound 8va)" c'8-\flageolet es'4-\flageolet)
```
The notes stay on the upper staff. On the LH staff they would fall under the 8 bassa line that carries the A♭1 tie through 83.

### 2. Bar 82 b3: the pickup is one level louder than the harmonics it leads into (applied)

**Problem.** The harp's dynamic at 82 is p (LH chord on b1), and nothing changes before the pickup E♭4–F4 at pitch on b3. The pickup is the start of theme A's head (E♭–F | A♭–C–E♭). Its arrival, A♭4 on the downbeat of 83, is a harmonic. Harmonics are much softer than ordinary notes in the middle register, so the head is louder on its pickup than on its goal. The orchestra is at pp by the end of 82: Vln I, Fl, Cl, B.cl., Bsn and Vc all diminuendo p>pp across the bar. So a p pickup also stands one level above the chord it sounds over.

**Evidence.** Adler part 1 PDF 116, rule 1: harmonics "are very soft and must be accompanied very lightly or played a cappella". This harp is itself the accompaniment to its own harmonics. PDF 113–114: the middle register where E♭4–F4 lie is the harp's "rich and warm", carrying octave. At pp the pickup is still heard, because it is the only attack in the bar and the orchestra is fading to pp under it. c116's reason for playing the pickup at pitch (harmonics would be covered by the held chord) still holds. Only the level changes.

**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpL, bar 82; harp dynamics print once, between the staves):**
```
\ottava #-1 << { <aes,,~ es,>2.\p\arpeggio } { s2 s4\pp } >> |
```
The MIDI follows (the pickup now plays at pp).

### 3. Bars 53–56: the harp's figure is a string figure (recorded, no change)

The harp plays the violas' 37–40 triplet zigzag (for example `aes8 des' f' des' aes des'`) in one hand within a sixth. Every second note re-strikes a string that is still ringing. On the harp the figure blurs into a sustained chord with a pulse on it, not a broken-chord line. Adler PDF 114–116 shows the harp's own accompaniment as arpeggios spread across both hands and a wide compass (Ex. IV-7, IV-8; Ex. IV-8 is a flute melody over harp arpeggios, the same layout as theme B over the harp here). A wider, two-hand version has no room in this passage, though. Below the figure are the cellos' bass (D♭3–G♭2) and Bsn 1 (D♭3–G♭3). A harp note below the cellos would become the bass and change the V6 at 53 b3 (c212). Above it are the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line (A♭3–B♭4) and the violins' theme B. c137, c142, c212 and c241 already reshaped and softened the figure to fit between them. The blur is also a fair harp colour under a melody. I recommend no change. If the composer wants a more harp-like shape without moving the register, the RH can play each beat as one rising group: bar 53 `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' f' aes des' f' }`, keeping c137's notes. This is not tested.

### Checked, no change

Pedal plan (one pedal per foot per change, at least one beat for each, letters A♮/A♭ just before the need); range (E♭1–F4 written, nothing on the hand-tuned strings); chord sizes (at most three notes per hand, no stretch past a tenth); rolls (solid with the pizzicato 37–44, rolled elsewhere, connected across the hands); harmonic range and notation; tacet in the ff tuttis. Other instruments: ranges in analysis.txt are all normal; breaths, mutes, divisi and the timpani retuning were settled by c214, c236, c246 and c256.
