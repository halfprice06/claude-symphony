## Findings

All findings are recorded with no score change. In this cycle I found no bar where a change would bring the score closer to Adler's practice without undoing a protected decision. They are ranked by how near each passage comes to breaking a principle.

### 1. Bars 23-30: the accompaniment attacks with the melody (protected; recorded with numbers)

**Problem.** Adler calls rhythmic independence "the most crucial" accompanying problem (PDF 259). The second scan gives these counts of accompaniment attack points that fall off the melody's attacks: 23-30, 5 of 27; 71-78, 24 of 50; 37-40, 30 of 45; 53-56, 34 of 52; 57-60, 63 of 75. So 23-30 is the passage where the accompaniment most often moves with the tune. At 24 and 28 the violas (`<c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4`) and the basses move in the melody's quarters.

**Evidence and reason for no change.** This is the protected plain first statement (c007, c046; c359 finding 3). The reprise at 71-78 adds the independent eighth-note viola figure, and the difference between the two statements is the design. It also follows Haydn's variety of doubling (PDF 288). Glazunov 5/iii does the same at its first string statement (c359). I do not re-raise it. If the plain statement is ever reopened, the place to start is the violas at 24 and 28 only. The motto-tail harmony there needs its E♭7 tones on beat 3, so any change must keep `<bes g'>` on 24 b3.

### 2. Bars 41-44: the cor anglais in its lowest fifth (follows; recorded)

**Problem checked.** Theme B's second phrase starts on A♭3, in the C.a.'s weakest fifth (PDF 275). Above it, Fl 1 plays a line in the same rhythm in thirds and sixths (42-43). The pizz. violas lie in the C.a.'s octave.

**Evidence.** The flute is Adler's heterophonic doubling in a contrasting colour (Ex. XVII-9, PDF 264; Liszt Ex. XVII-14, PDF 273-274). It sits more than an octave above the solo and a dynamic below it (c101; protected, "Fl 1 thirds/sixths 41-44"). Since c267 the violas no longer attack any C.a. note together with it. The only remaining near-unison is the violas' A♭3 at 41 b1⅔, plucked after the C.a.'s held A♭3 has spoken. Pizz. decays, and the rhythmic contrast (triplets against quarters) is what PDF 274 says "lessens the importance" of distance. No change.

### 3. Registral scan of all wind and solo passages (follows; recorded)

**Method.** The first scan covered Hn 1 (7-14, 67-70), Ob 1 (15-16, 19-20), solo Vln (17-22), B.cl. (31-36), Fl 1 (37-40), C.a. (41-44, 47-52), Ob 1 (45-46) and Cl 1 (85-89). Only these places put accompaniment within a whole tone of, or above, the sounding solo note:
- 9 b2½: Hn 1's B♭3 a whole tone above Bsn 1's held A♭3. 10 b3 and 13 b3½: Hn 1 reaches E♭3 and B♭3, which the harp struck a beat or more earlier. c057's rule removed every harp note attacked together with the horn's note; these harp notes are decaying, not new attacks.
- 14 b1: Bsn 1's leading-tone resolution on the horn's A♭3 (c267 finding 3), and the harp chord above the horn (c361).
- 31: B.cl.'s opening arpeggio passes Bsn 2's A♭2 pedal (c149, c220; woodwind choir protected).
- 37 b1: the C.a. ends its slur on the flute's first F5. This is the overlap join c443 recorded, and it leads the flute to its pitch (PDF 275).
- 48-52: Vln II above the C.a. This is a counter-line, as the first violins are in Tchaikovsky's Ex. XVII-3 (PDF 259-260).

**Verdict.** PDF 274 is kept everywhere except at recorded, reasoned points. No change.

### 4. Bars 15-22: dialogue and inner-voice echo (follows)

The oboe and the senza-sordino solo violin alternate two-bar phrases over muted strings, which is Adler's colour differentiation and dialogue (PDF 257-258). The violas answer the oboe's rhythm a bar later and an octave lower (oboe 15 `c''4. des''8 es''4`, violas 16 `des'4.( c'8 bes4)`; oboe 19 `c''4( des''8 c''8 bes'4)`, violas 20 `c'4( des'8 c'8 bes4)`). This is the movement's clearest imitation between groups. At 20 b1-2 Vln II rests where the oboe drops onto its A♭4, which is PDF 274 applied by hand. Protected (c127). No change.

### 5. Roles exchanged and colours rotated (follows)

PDF 262 asks that soloist and orchestra exchange foreground and background. Fl 1, the B soloist at 37-40, accompanies the C.a. at 41-44. Hn 1, the A soloist at 7-14, is the counter-line at 53-56 and part of the counter-melody at 78-81. The harp, a background instrument at 7-14 and 53-56, states theme A's head at 83, and the timpani join it to Cl 1's tail at 84-85. That is exactly Beethoven's timpani in Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273). Mozart's two horn and flute rules (PDF 264, 267) hold: no other horn sounds under Hn 1 at 7-14 or 67-70, Fl 2 rests at 37-40, the oboes rest under the C.a. at 41-52, and the clarinets rest under the B.cl. at 31-36 (c027). No change.

### 6. Second-phrase counter-melodies, compared with the Canzonetta (follows)

In Op. 35/ii, Tchaikovsky keeps the background sustained for the solo's first phrase (13-20). He adds a string counter-melody only when the phrase is restated (21-24), and it moves in eighths where the solo repeats quarters. This score uses the same order three times: cellos at 27-30 (`es2( f4) | aes4( f4 des'4) | bes2( des'4)`), cellos with Hn 2 and Bsn 1 at 75-78, and Fl 1 at 41-44. At 75-78 the counter-melody gains Hn 2 and Bsn 1 only in the second phrase. Mahler's rule (PDF 283) is kept: the line is helped, not doubled throughout. No change.

### 7. Higher octave saved for the climaxes (follows)

Bizet doubles the voice at the higher octave only at the climax (PDF 279). Theme B: violins in unison at 53-56, the octave above with Fl 1 at 57, D♭7 at 61. Theme A: unison at 23-30. At 71-74 Vln I is an octave up, and at 75-76 it drops into unison with Vln II, so the upper octave returns at 77 and B♭6 is heard first at 79. The order is deliberate and protected (c101 "violin unison/octave plan"). No change.

### 8. Transparent backgrounds at solo entries (follows)

Adler's Sibelius and Bartók examples (PDF 268-272) set a soloist into a sparse, quiet background. This score does the same at:
- 37: Vln II div. sul tasto pp, viola triplets pp, pizz. bass.
- 65-70: timpani roll near the rim ppp, the basses' E♭ held by four soli, Hn 1 alone. This is also Mozart's sustained pedal before the tonic return (PDF 267, item 3).
- 83-86: Vc soli and Cb on A♭, nothing else under the harp, the timpani and Cl 1.

No change.
