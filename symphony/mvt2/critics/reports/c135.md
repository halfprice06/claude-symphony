## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Ten players wait 24–60 bars with no cue, then enter in the 59–61 build or at 52. Applied (part-only).
**Bars:** Hn 2 is silent 1–60 and enters f at 61. Hn 3 and Hn 4 rest 31–59/60. Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba rest 1–59 and enter pp at 60. Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest 1–60 and enter ff at 61. Cbn rests 37–60. Timpani rest 7–51 (45 bars and five tempo changes) and enter pp at 52. In v135 only Fl 1 (34–36), Hn 1 (49–50) and Tbn 1 (61–62) have cues.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5 says that logical cues are expected during long periods of rest, and that cues must be audible and obvious. Example 4 (PDF 10) shows a cue ("Viol. I") placed just before the entry after a long multi-bar rest. Here every entry comes during a tempo change ("poco a poco animando", then "poco allargando"), where counting rests is least reliable.
**Fix (in /tmp/critic-135/work):** I added three quotes to genparts.py: `\addQuote "hnOneQ" { \removeWithTag #'part \hnOne }`, `\addQuote "oboeOneQ" { \removeWithTag #'(score part) \oboeOne }` and `\addQuote "vcQ" { \removeWithTag #'(score part) \vc }`. hnOne's quote keeps its score-tagged `R1*2` so its bars stay aligned. In bSec.ily I put the cues in the c075 pattern, for example `\tag #'score { R1*4 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring #"hnOneQ" #UP { <>^\markup \tiny "Hn. 1" R1*4 } }`:
- Hn 2 gets Hn 1 57–60.
- Hn 3 and Hn 4 get Hn 1 57–59.
- Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba get Vc 58–59.
- Fl 2 and Ob 2 get Ob 1 59–60.
- Cbn gets Vc 59–60.
- Timpani get Vc 50–51.

score.ly now applies `\removeWithTag #'part` to oboeTwo, tbnTwo, bassTbn, tuba, timp and cbsn, as it already did for the other parts, so the score never shows a cue. I checked the written cue pitches against Horn 1's own part: Hn 3 at 57–59 shows E♭5–D♭5 | C5 B♭4 C5 | D♭5 B♭4 C♭5, which is correct.

### 2. Hairpins squeezed to a single "‹" glyph in the extracted parts. Applied (parts layout).
**Bars:** Hn 1 and Hn 2 at 78 b3 → 79 ("mf ‹ f"), and Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba at 59–60 ("pp ‹ mf"). The part's dynamics take up nearly all the space between the two notes, so the hairpin is drawn about one space long. It reads as a bracket or accent, not as a crescendo.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 8 and Example 7 (PDF 12): an extracted part does not keep the score's placement, which leads to collisions and floating markings. Gould PDF 55 says no symbol should be placed where it can be mistaken for another.
**Fix:** in the parts layout (genparts.py), `\override Hairpin.minimum-length = #8` (was 5). The spacing rods then widen the bar. Every part still fits on one page and the harp on two.

### 3. Parts book page numbers run through the whole book. Applied.
**Problem:** Flute 2's single page is numbered 2, with the number at top left as on a verso. Horn 1 is page 12, and the harp part starts on page 26. Once the book is split into parts, half the parts begin on an even, left-hand page number.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 7: right-hand pages carry odd numbers at top right, left-hand pages even numbers at top left. PDF 6–7 treats each part as a document of its own.
**Fix:** `bookpart-level-page-numbering = ##t` in the parts \paper block (genparts.py). `first-page-number` inside a \bookpart is ignored by LilyPond 2.24, which I tested. Every part now starts on page 1, on the right. The harp is 1–2, with its turn at 70→71 after six bars of rest.

### 4. No trumpet pages in the parts book. Applied.
**Problem:** The score says "Trumpets tacet", but the parts book has no trumpet entry. A trumpet player's folder needs to say that movement II is tacet, so the player knows the movement was not left out by mistake.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5: "tacet" is acceptable only when the player takes no further part until the end of the work or of an act. That covers a whole movement, but the word has to appear in the player's part.
**Fix:** genparts.py adds one page each for "Trumpet 1/2/3 in C" with a centred **Tacet**, placed in score order between Horn 4 and Trombone 1. The parts book is now 30 pages.

### 5. The short names drop the instrument keys. Applied.
**Problem:** Page 1 has "Clarinets 1·2 in B♭" and "Horns 1·2 in F". From page 2 on the names are "Cl. 1·2" and "Hn. 1·2" with no key.
**Reading:** MOLA Example 2 (PDF 9) gives the abbreviated forms with keys: "Cl. 1, 2 (B♭)", "Bass Cl. (B♭)", "Hn. 1, 2 in F". In a C score the key is what tells the conductor how the part reads.
**Fix (score.ly):** `shortInstrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Cl. 1·2" \small "(B♭)" }`, and the same for "B.cl." (B♭), "Hn. 1·2" (F) and "Hn. 3·4" (F). The score stays at 8 pages with the same breaks.

### 6. No composer on the score or the parts. Not applied.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 6 asks for the title and the composer's name on the score's cover, and at minimum the title, composer and instrument name on the front of each part.
**Fix:** add `composer = "…"` to the \header in score.ly and to each \bookpart header in genparts.py (the title line already exists). I did not apply it because Dan's name as it should be printed is not in the project files. Movement I's score has the same gap.

### 7. Multi-bar rests have no bar-number ranges. Not applied.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5 and Example 3 ("9 - 11" under a 3-bar rest) say ranges are helpful on multi-measure rests. Hn 2's opening rests (6, 8, 8, 8, 4) and the long rests in Tbn 2, B.tbn, Tuba and the timpani would benefit most. MOLA marks this as helpful, not required, and finding 1 now gives these players cues, so I rank it last.
**Fix:** in the parts layout, give MultiMeasureRestNumber a stencil that prints the start and end bar under the rest. Use `ly:context-property` currentBarNumber from a small engraver, or simpler, add `_\markup \tiny "1–36"` by hand only to the rests longer than 20 bars (Hn 2 1–36, Cbn 37–60, Timp 7–36).

### 8. Harp starting pedal setting and string bowings. No change.
MOLA PDF 5 says harp pedalling should not be printed and string bowings should appear only for a specific effect. The harp part's single "set pedals during the rests" diagram (c116) is a starting setting, not a pedalling of the music, and the harp plan is protected. Each printed bowing (59–61, 77–80, 89–92, "change bow at will") is there for a stated effect. I list these only so later critics do not raise them again.
