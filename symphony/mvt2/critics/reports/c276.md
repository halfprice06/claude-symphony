## Findings

### 1. Bars 32–34: cor anglais marked pp in its lowest fifth (applied)

**Problem.** The cor anglais enters at 32 with `c'2.(\pp` and descends C4–C♭4–B♭3–A♭3 (written G4–G♭4–F4–E♭4). From 33 b3 it is in its lowest fifth (written B3–F♯4, concert E3–B3). The pp is left over from c111, which placed the line an octave higher (C5–A♭4) and marked it pp "under the solo". c140 lowered the line again but kept the pp. c036 had marked it p.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 174–175: "the oboe and bassoon should not be called upon to perform pianissimo in the lower fifth of their range". PDF 198 repeats it for the oboe: "one should never require a pianissimo dynamic in this register, even from the best players." PDF 205: the cor anglais' registers are "like its sister instrument, the oboe", and its lowest fifth has "tremendous expressive carrying power". PDF 169 places the cor anglais in the oboe family. A pp the player cannot give is played as p anyway, so the score should say p. The balance under the solo is kept by the dynamic plan: the bass clarinet solo is p and rises to mp at 33, Bsn 1 enters pp on F3 at 33, and the cor anglais lies a sixth above the solo, not on its pitches.

**Fix (applied, trans.ily, `transCorAnglais`).**
```
transCorAnglais = { R2. | c'2.(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" | c'4 ces'4 bes4 | aes2.) | R2. | r2 c''4(\p | }
```
Notes, slurs and "dolce" are unchanged; this is a dynamic inside the protected woodwind choir 31–36, not a change to its harmony or scoring. The MIDI renders the cor anglais one level louder at 32–34.

### 2. Viola part, bars 47–48: "poco rit." and "poco sostenuto" stacked against the system above (c275 open item; applied)

**Problem.** In the viola part, bar 47 is a whole note and is narrow, so "poco rit." (47 b3) and "poco sostenuto" (48) overlap horizontally and stack. The stack was printed directly under the hairpin of the system above (41–43), farther from bars 47–48 than from bar 43.

**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) on floating markings, cited by c165 and c275. Adler PDF 175: give the player clear instructions in the part; a tempo word read against the wrong system is a playability problem in an extracted part.

**Fix (applied).**
- genparts.py, viola bookpart only: a one-line title block ("Viola" left, "Symphony No. 1 in C minor II. Adagio" centred), which frees about one staff height. `system-system-spacing.padding` goes from 0.1 to 0.2 (0.3 and above made LilyPond compress the page).
```
pc = "\\paper { system-system-spacing.padding = #0.2 bookTitleMarkup = \\markup \\fill-line { \\fontsize #3 \\bold \\fromproperty #'header:instrument \\line { \\bold \\fromproperty #'header:title \\hspace #1 \\bold \\fromproperty #'header:subtitle } \\null } } " if v == 'vla' else ""
```
- bSec.ily, `bSecVla` bar 47, part only: an empty text 20 spaces wide widens the bar, so the two words print side by side, each over its own beat, just above the system that holds 46–49.
```
\tag #'part { \once \textLengthOn } b1\fp\>-\tag #'part _\markup \with-dimensions #'(0 . 20) #'(0 . 0) \null |
```
- bSec.ily, `bSecVla` bar 53, part only: the new line-up put "a tempo, poco a poco animando" against the tenuto marks of 49. Half a space lower it clears both those marks and the "53–56" bar range.
```
\tag #'part { \once \override Score.MetronomeMark.extra-offset = #'(0 . -0.5) } R1*4 |
```
I tested widths of 12, 16 and 20 spaces. Only 20 gives a one-line pair at 47–48. The part stays on one page with no compression warning. The score is unchanged: score page 3 is identical pixel for pixel.

### 3. Bar 63: flute 1 alone on the shared staff with no "1." (applied)

**Problem.** At 63 flute 1 plays A6 (the resolution of the A♭6 held from 61) and flute 2 has stopped. In the score, flute 2's 63 is a spacer (`\tag #'score s1`), so the part-combiner prints neither flute 2's rests nor "1.". A conductor sees one note after two bars of chords and cannot tell whether it is a2.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 181, rule 3 and Ex. VI-15: when one player of a shared staff plays alone, "one can either supply the appropriate rests in the score, or mark the part 1., 1°, or 2., 2°". The other entries in this score already do this: "1." at 37, 57 and 81.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily, `bSecFluteOne`).**
```
\once \stemDown a'''4)-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." \tweak staff-position #0 r4 \tweak staff-position #0 r2 | R1 |
```
The label prints above the A6, clear of the slur. The parts are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 89–92: clarinet 2's last E♭4 is about 19 s plus the fermata on one breath (recorded, no change)

At the MIDI steps (89 at 48, 90 at 44, 91 at 40, 92 at 36 then 20) the tied E♭4 lasts 3.8 + 4.1 + 4.5 + 6.3 s = 18.7 s, plus the fermata. That is at c126's 20-second limit. Adler PDF 188 (Ex. VII-9, Brahms 1/iv) solves a long flute note by having the second player hold while the first breathes. Here the dovetail would need clarinet 1 to re-enter on E♭4, which changes the protected 87–92 hand-over and the c226 niente entry. At ppp in the low chalumeau the clarinet uses little air, and Adler PDF 210 credits it with full control at the faintest dynamic, so a professional can hold the note. I do not propose a change. If a conductor's fermata runs long, the remedy is a part-only "(breathe in the fermata only if needed)", not a rescoring.

### 5. Flute register plan (recorded, no change)

- Theme B at 37–40 lies B♭4–G♭5, Adler's "sweet and with little carrying power" register (PDF 185). It is accompanied as his examples are (PDF 186, Ex. VII-3 to VII-5): divided second violins pp sul tasto, violas pp, pizzicato basses and harp. Flute 1 is not covered.
- The only notes in the flute's highest fourth (A6 at 63, B♭6 at 79–80) are reached by step or by a scale run (PDF 186). Both are ff, where the top is controllable.
- Flute 1's longest span is 61–63 b1, about 10 s ff, after breaths at the ends of 58 and 60. It then rests for 13 bars (PDF 188).

### 6. Oboe and cor anglais registers and rests (recorded, no change)

- Oboes 1·2 lie A♭4–B♭5, inside Adler's most effective F4–C6 (PDF 198). Nothing lies in the thick lowest fifth or the hard top.
- Oboe 1 has long rests between its four entries (PDF 198: frequent rests mandatory).
- The cor anglais' solos use the registers Adler praises: the quotation 47–51 (C♯4–D5) and theme B's lower octave 41–44 (A♭3–B♭4) lie in the deep and mellow registers (PDF 205). Its one upper-register note, F5 at 37, was shortened to an eighth by c271.
- Oboe 1 doubling the lower octave at 57–60 is protected (c259). The top octave there has flute 1 in its clear and brilliant register, so Adler's warning on PDF 201 about an oboe overshadowing the featured line does not apply.
