## Findings

### 1. Bar 33: the A♭ pedal stops in both instruments on the beat where the common-tone °7 moves on (applied)
**Problem.** The CT°7 is on 33 b2: A♭ pedal, B.cl. D3, Bsn 1 F3, C.a. C♭4. On b3 it moves to D♭–F–B♭ and then to I at 34. On that b3, Bsn 2 releases its A♭2 (`aes,2 r4`, c149). On the same beat the contrabassoon has its breath before re-striking A♭1 (`aes,,2 \breathe aes,,4~`). c149 took Bsn 2 off because "the contrabassoon keeps the A♭ pedal", but at that moment the contrabassoon is breathing. So the common tone breaks on the beat the chord resolves.
**Evidence.** In a common-tone °7 the bass is "clearly a stable tone", the sustained 1̂, and the dissonance is in the upper voices (A&S PDF 553–554, Ex. 30-19). In Ex. 30-20a–d the bass whole note is held through the chord and its resolution. A pedal that is re-attacked at the point of resolution sounds like a new bass event, not a held common tone.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-220/work/sections/trans.ily).**
`transBsnTwo = { aes,2.~\pp | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,4 r2 | R2.*2 | }`
Bsn 2 now holds A♭2 through 33 and releases on 34 b2, after the tonic arrives. The contrabassoon's breath stays where it is and Bsn 2 covers it. c149's reason still holds: Bsn 2 is silent at 35–36 under the solo's A♭2. That is about 11 s at pp for Bsn 2. No other part changes, and the solo and the CT°7 are untouched (protected since c002).

### 4. Bar 33: the common-tone °7 passes downward instead of resolving upward (recorded, no change)
The textbook resolution is ♯2→3 and ♯4→5 rising into I (PDF 554). Here D3→D♭3 (the motto's tail in the B.cl.) and C♭4→B♭3 both fall, to D♭–F–B♭ over A♭ (ii4/2 over the pedal) and then I at 34. Ex. 30-20 and Tchaikovsky's Ex. 30-24 (PDF 556) show the common-tone °7 used as a passing chord, and the reading calls its tones "of contrapuntal origin". The descent is the motto tail itself, so it is the reason for the chord. Keep it.

### 5. Bar 86: half-diminished where the scherzo and finale use a full °7 (recorded; c190's reason stands)
The reading supports both forms over a tonic pedal. A D°7 (C♭4 in the violas) would be the common-tone °7 of A♭ with the ♭3 notation of Finding 3. But D5 goes down to D♭ (tail), not up, and C♭ would put ♭3̂ under the line one bar before the C5 goal that links all three movements. c190's reason holds, and nothing in Unit 30 overrides it. Bar 86 is protected. No change.

### 6. The reading's augmented-triad techniques would need pitches the cycle reserves (recorded, not proposed)

### 7. A common-tone °7 decorating V7 at 65–70 (recorded, not proposed)
Ex. 30-23a (PDF 556), E♭–G♭–A–C rising to E♭7, would fit the six-bar dominant. But 65 is the arrival of every voice falling a semitone from the German sixth at 64 (c210). An A♮ and a G♭ at 66 would recall A minor right after the trombone quotation and blur its end. 65–70 is protected as a thin E♭7 under the horn (c003, c153). The reading is better served by keeping the single common-tone °7 at 33.
