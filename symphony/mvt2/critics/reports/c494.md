## Findings

Nothing in the score needs to change. The brass in this movement are horns throughout, three bars of low brass (60-62) and one trombone quotation (63-64), with the trumpets tacet. Three earlier passes over this reading (c224, c364, c424) have already adjusted or recorded each of these bars. I checked every rule in the table against the sounding pitches rather than the written parts. The items below are recorded so the next pass need not repeat them. One of them corrects an earlier report.

### 1. Bars 61-62: bass trombone and tuba double the low roots in octaves. Recorded, no change

**Problem.** At 61 the bass trombone plays D♭3 and the tuba D♭2. At 62 b1 they play G♭2/G♭1, and at 62 b3 F♭2/F♭1. Sebesky PDF 47 points out that in the climax of Ex. 51 (bars 7-8) "the bass trombone and tuba do not double the low roots". He uses the tuba only where it reinforces a line or a low root, so that the ensemble keeps a "pointed" edge.

**Evidence and decision.** Sebesky's band has no contrabassoon or orchestral basses, and his aim is a pointed jazz brass sound. In a late-Romantic tutti, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 101 states the opposite as the norm: "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves". At 61-62 the octave roots are also in the contrabassoon, the basses and Bsn 2/B.cl., so a different note in the bass trombone would only thin the bottom of the climax. The tuba is used sparingly, as Sebesky asks: three bars in the whole movement. c224 and c424 recorded the low octaves for the same reason. No change.

### 2. Bars 79-80 b2: the chord's F is missing from the lower-middle register. Considered, not tested, not recommended

**Problem.** At 79 b1 the sounding pitches from D3 to C4, the Ex. 53 band, are A♭3 (Hn 4) alone, with D♭3 (B.cl., Bsn 2, timpani) at the band's lower edge. F is first heard at F4 (Hn 3, Cl 2). Read as IV with an added sixth (the B♭6 appoggiatura), the chord's third does not reach the register Sebesky PDF 50 asks for.

**Why not change it.** I checked the obvious fix, Hn 4 F3 at 79 (`f2.` for `aes2.` in `aTwoHnFour`, then `aes2~ aes4` at 80 as now). On 79 b1, Hn 4's A♭3 is the only A♭ in the orchestra (MIDI scan), so the fix would turn the ii6/5 into a plain ii6 and lose the seventh. Read as ii6/5, which is how the composer and c364 read it, the chord already places its third (D♭3) and its seventh (A♭3) in the lower-middle register, as Ex. 53 asks. The fix would also put an open F3 in Hn 4 eight bars before the muted Hn 4 F3-F♭3-E♭3 at 87-89. That muted line is c150's echo of Hn 3's open F4-F♭4-E♭4 at 79-81. The 79-81 horn spacing is recorded (c374, c424) and the A′ climax is protected. No change.

### 3. Correction to c364: the register bracket in Ex. 53 (record only)

c364 gave the "lower middle register" of Ex. 53 as roughly A2 to E♭4. At 300 dpi the bracket starts on D3, with C♯3 in brackets before it, and ends on C4, with D♭4 in brackets after it. The narrower band changes no verdict. The brass thirds and sevenths at 60 b3 (G♭3, C4), 61 (F3), 62 (B♭3, A♭3), 29-30 (E♭3, D♭4 at the bracketed top) and 87-90 (F3, A♭3) all fall inside it. Later citations of Ex. 53 should use D3-C4.

### 4. Bar 60, beats 1-2: low fifth without its seventh (recorded, as c224 and c364)

Tbn 2's E♭3 over the bass trombone's A♭2 is an open fifth in the low register, which PDF 44 says reduces vibrancy. Since c364, Hn 4's C4 supplies the third from beat 1. The seventh G♭3 arrives in Tbn 2 on beat 3, which gives the trombone a melodic line (PDF 42) and places the seventh in the trombones (PDF 49). No change.
