## Findings

### 1. Bar 27, beat 2, violas: the I6 has its third in three octaves (applied)
- **Problem.** At 27 the basses move A♭–C–D♭ under the held A♭4 of the tune. On beat 2 the chord is A♭/C. The basses play C2 and C3 (c204 octaves), the cellos E♭3, the divided violas still hold C4–E♭4 from beat 1, and the violins have A♭4. The third, C, sounds in three octaves. The root, A♭, is only in the unison tune. This is the thick, closed I6 that Adler's Ex. V-27 marks "not good".
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 152: in I6 with more than four voices, "double it near the bottom and leave the 'open' sound so characteristic of this inversion intact". The basses already double the third at the bottom, so the violas' C4 is one doubling too many, in the middle of the chord. At the parallel place in the reprise, 75 beat 2, the violas' figure plays E♭4–A♭3–F4 over the bass C, so the reprise already has the open voicing. Bar 27 is the only one of the two that does not.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-207/work/sections/aOne.ily, line 85).** The lower violas move to A♭3 on beat 2 and hold it into beat 3. The upper violas tie their E♭4.
  ```
  % was:  <c' es'>2\< <aes des'>4 |
  <c' es'~>4\< <aes~ es'>4 <aes des'>4 |
  ```
  The lower violas' C4–A♭3 against the bass's A♭–C is a voice exchange in contrary motion. Beat 2 now reads C2 C3 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4: the root is doubled, the third is only at the bottom, and the fifth is in two octaves. Beat 3 (D♭ chord) is unchanged. There are no new parallels; analysis.txt is identical to v207's (0 outer-voice parallels). The rhythm, the dynamics, the c127 avoidance of the cellos' notes (25, 26, 28) and the unison-violin plan are all unchanged.

### 2. Bar 75, beat 2, harp RH (not applied; low)
On the same I6 in the reprise, the harp's after-beat chord <A♭3 C4 E♭4> puts a C4 in the middle, above the bass C and the bassoon's C3 (Adler PDF 152). It is a single, softly struck chord, p, and the violas are already open. Optional: `<aes es'>4\arpeggio` in place of `<aes c' es'>4\arpeggio` at 75 b2 (aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpR). I did not test it, and it ranks below everything else here.

### 3. Bars 63–64: the gap in the string tremolo is the trombone's space (recorded, no change)
The A-minor tremolo leaves 19 semitones between the cellos' A2 and the violas' E4. That breaks the even spacing of Ex. V-26 (PDF 151). Trombone 1's quotation (E3–C4–B3–B♭3–A3) and the bass trombone lie exactly in that gap. This is Adler's Tchaikovsky case on PDF 154: the strings stay out of the melody's register so that they "cannot interfere with it". Keep.

### 4. Bars 87–88: IV6 with the third F in five voices (recorded, no change)
At 87 the bass F is doubled by the cellos (F2), horn 4 and the lower violas (F3), and the cor anglais (F4). The root D♭ is only in clarinet 1. Ex. V-27 would call this over-doubled. But each of these F's starts the chromatic line F–F♭–E♭ (iv–I), which is the point of the plagal close. Adler PDF 150 ranks melodic considerations with spacing. The close is protected (standing list; c194, c199). Keep.

### 5. Bars 23–30 and 27–28: same doubling in antecedent and consequent, and a wide top gap (recorded, no change)
Grieg (PDF 154–155) doubles the strings differently in antecedent and consequent. The movement does this at 37–44, 53–60 and 71–82. At 23–30 both halves use the same scoring, apart from horns 3–4 at 29. At 27 b3 and 28 b2 the unison violins (F5, B♭5) stand 16–17 semitones above the violas' top note, and Ex. V-26 (PDF 151) asks for closer spacing at the top. Either remedy would put Vln II an octave lower at 27–28 or put an octave B♭4 in the violas. Both are blocked by the protected unison plan for 23 and 53 against octaves at 71 (c191, "unison up to B♭5 at 27–28 … protected contrast with 71"). The reason still holds: the reprise and the three-octave B♭ at 79 depend on the a′ phrase not having octave doubling. No change proposed.

### 6. Bars 24 b3 and 72 b3: leading tone G in two voices in V7 (recorded, no change)
At 24 the violas' G4 is the correct resolution of the seventh A♭4 of B♭7 (V7/V). The cellos' G3 belongs to their counter-melody (F3–G3–A♭3). At 25 the two G's resolve differently (G4–E♭4 inside, G3–A♭3 in the counter-melody), so there are no octaves. 72 is the same in the viola figure. The doubling comes from two correct lines, so the doubling rules behind Adler PDF 152 ("we have learned …") are not broken in substance. Keep.

### 7. Bars 37–40: Vln II div. dyads share an octave with the viola triplets (recorded, no change)
The held D♭4, B♭4 and G♭4 in the second violins are notes the viola figure touches on every beat. This is option 4 on PDF 151 (divided Vln II with violas, a "lighter but still subdued colour"): a pad with a moving figure inside it, as in Dvořák 9/ii. The B-section background plan is protected (c107). Keep.
