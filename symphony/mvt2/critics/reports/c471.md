## Findings

The score does not violate any principle in the reading. The melodic lines have not changed since c351 checked them against the same pages, apart from two cello details that the reading does not touch. I made no change in /tmp/critic-471/work. The findings below say what the reading shows, ranked by how much each point affects whether a listener will remember the tunes.

### 1. Theme B rests on the half-step active tones more than the Largo tune does (37-44, 53-60; no change)

On the strong beats (1 and 3) of theme B at 37-44, four of sixteen tones are 4̂ (G♭) or 7̂ (C), the two tones a half step from a stable tone that PDF 22 says "urgently" demand continuation. G♭-F occurs in four of the eight bars (37, 39, 42, 43). Dvořák's cor anglais tune at 7-10 has none of these on its strong beats. This is the one property that makes theme B harder to sing back than theme A. It is deliberate: theme B keeps moving and does not land on 1̂ until 61, which is the K. 545 device of PDF 16, and its neighbour-note design is protected (c211, c351, c411). I do not propose a change. The thing to watch in future revisions is that theme A keeps more statements than theme B (7, 23, 71 and the coda harp head at 82-83), so that the tune the listener carries away is the one with stable downbeats.

### 2. The F♯-minor quotation uses the two minor-key half steps, as Dvořák's C♯-minor tune does (47-50; follows)

PDF 26-27 says the half steps 2̂-3̂ and 5̂-♭6̂ give natural minor its character. The cor anglais at 47-50 is C♯ | A (held) G♯ F♯ | D (held) C♯ | C♯ B A | G♯ F♯: 3̂-2̂-1̂, then ♭6̂-5̂ on a long note. Dvořák's middle section of the Largo (flute, woods.notesL.ily lines 26-29) is E-D♯-C♯ and later G♯-A-G♯: the same 3̂-2̂-1̂ and ♭6̂-5̂ in C♯ minor. The quotation's minor colour therefore comes from the same scale steps as the corresponding passage in the model. It is a protected single statement. No change.

### 3. The minor forms are used as the reading prescribes; no augmented 2nd in any melody (19-20, 45-51, 63-64; follows)

minor.py finds no melodic line that ascends 6̂-7̂-8̂ in a minor area, so the raised 6̂ of the melodic minor (PDF 27-28, Ex. 1-20) is never needed. Descending lines use the natural ♭6̂: oboe D♭ at 19 (F minor), cor anglais D5 at 49 (F♯ minor). Raised 7̂ goes to 8̂ where it is a goal: Vln II E♮4-F4 at 20-21; oboe and cellos A♮ as lower neighbour of B♭ at 46. The viola's E♮4 at 19 b3 leaps to C4 at 20; it is a chord tone of C7 (the harmonic form for chords, PDF 28), and Vln II takes the leading tone to F. The viola hits at 63-64 are arpeggiated chord tones in sextuplets, not scale lines. scan.py finds one augmented 2nd, the basses' B1-A♭1 at 50-51, which is c023's protected respelling and sounds a minor 3rd. No change.

### 4. Chromatic spelling: two exceptions, both protected; one new diminished 3rd recorded (7-8 and the other motto statements, 32-33, 46-47, 63; no change)

PDF 25 gives the descending chromatic passing tone the letter of the tone before it (A-A♭-G). The cor anglais at 32-33 follows this (C-C♭-B♭). The motto tail E♭-D♮-D♭-C reverses the order at each statement (Hn 7-8 and 67-68, Vln I and II 23-24 and 71-72, B.cl. 31-33, Cl 1 85-87). c211 Finding 1 explains why: D♮ is ♯4̂ over V7/V and the figure is movement I's cyclic tail. The trombone quotation C-B♮-B♭ at 63 is the same pattern in A minor and is protected. scan.py also reports a diminished 3rd that c351 did not list: Bsn 2 B♭2 at 46 to G♯2 at 47, under one slur. G♯ is the fifth of the C♯7 and stands after the new F♯-minor signature at 47, so the spelling follows the harmony and the signature (PDF 23-25). Writing A♭2 would contradict the signature. Record only.

### 5. Theme A's frame and its peak follow the Brahms model (7-14, 23-30, 71-82; follows)

Downbeats of theme A are stable tones except the two phrase ends (10 G = 7̂, 13 G = 7̂). The held G at 10 gets its A♭ at 11 in the same register after the incomplete-neighbour pickup E♭-F (PDF 22, Ex. 1-11). The second phrase's accented B♭ (2̂) at 12 and the 5̂-4̂-3̂-2̂-1̂ descent at 13 match Ex. 1-10a. At the return the same 2̂ becomes the registral peak B♭6 at 79, reached by the passing run F-G-A♭ (Ex. 1-8). The listener meets the climax tone as a degree already known from the theme. No change.

### 6. Theme B's restatements begin on 5̂ over I6, which keeps root-position D♭ for 61 (41, 57-61; follows)

PDF 20 says a melody beginning on 3̂ or 5̂ "almost always" has the tonic in the bass. Theme B's first statement does (F5 over D♭ at 37). The restatements at 41 (cor anglais A♭3) and 57 (flute, oboe, violins A♭) begin over F, a first-inversion tonic. This is the exception the word "almost" leaves room for, and it serves principle 2: the bass reaches D♭ in root position under the melody's 1̂ only at 61, after the violins hold C6 (7̂) for the whole of 60 (PDF 22: a held 7̂ makes the ear demand 8̂). No change.

### 7. Mixture, Phrygian colour and the open ending stay inside major-minor (21, 47-50, 62-64, 79-81, 87-92; follows)

G♭ minor is written as F♯ minor with its own signature, as PDF 29 prescribes (Chopin Op. 28/15). The Phrygian ♭2̂ appears only inside minor (G♮ at 49, B♭ at 63; PDF 30). ♭6̂ F♭ appears in inner and horn lines (Vln I F-F♭ at 62, Hn 3 at 79-81, C.a. F-F♭-E♭ at 87-89), never in theme A itself, so theme A is always heard in the same mode. The last melodic tone is C5 (3̂), less final than 1̂ (PDF 20); this is the cyclic rule and leads into the scherzo. No change.
