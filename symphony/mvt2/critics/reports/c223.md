## Findings

### 1. Parallel fifths at the final plagal cadence, 88→89 (applied)

- **Problem.** At 88 the violas are divided F♭3/A♭3 over cellos D♭3 and basses D♭2 (iv). At 89, since c199, both viola halves go to E♭3 while the cellos fall to A♭2. The upper half and the cellos move A♭3/D♭3 → E♭3/A♭2. These are consecutive perfect fifths in the string choir, at the movement's last chord change, in the most exposed texture of the piece (pp, rit., nothing else moving). It is the textbook fault of the plagal cadence: the fifth of IV moves to the fifth of I while the root moves down a fourth. The usual voice leading keeps that fifth as the common tone, the root of I.
- **Evidence.** `allpairs_par.py` on v223's score.midi reports exactly one perfect-fifth pair in the movement: beat 292.0 = bar 89 b1, Violas/Violoncellos. The beat dump confirms the notes: 88 "Db2 Db3 E3(F♭) Ab3 …", 89 "Ab1 Ab2 Eb3 … ". Horn 3's tied A♭3 holds the common tone in the winds, but the muted horn at pp does not hide a string fifth. A&S §27 (PDF 365–366) and Ex. 21-40 (PDF 367) show a voice moved off the parallel at a IV–V cadence to break the fifths, and Review 12 (PDF 369) states the rule. c199's own reason, no new attack in the decay 90–92, does not need the fifths. It is met equally well if the upper half simply keeps its A♭3.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-223/work, coda.ily, `codaVla`).** Tie the upper half's A♭3 from 88 and keep the section divided to the end:
  `<f aes>2.\pp^\markup \italic "lower half sul C" | <fes aes~>2. | <es aes>2.~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | <es aes>2.~ | <es aes>2.~\> | <es aes>2.\ppp\fermata |`
  (was `<fes aes>2. | es2.~^\markup … { "unis.," "change bow at will" } | es2.~ | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata |`).
  The upper half has no new attack after 88, the lower half's F–F♭–E♭ line (c020/c194) and its E♭3 at 89 (c199) are unchanged, and the bass and the top line of the plagal close are untouched. The last chord now has A♭3 in the violas (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5); before, A♭3 dropped out when horn 3 left at 90. The sounding pitch classes, the top C5 and the bass A♭ are unchanged. **This is a change to movement II's ending:** the composer should tell the scherzo thread, although the scherzo's opening (Hn 1 G♯4 = this movement's last A♭, timpani retuning) is not affected.

### 2. The transition's tonic pedal ends on an open fifth, 34 b3–36 b2 (applied)

- **Problem.** The woodwind choir over the A♭ pedal loses its third after the cor anglais' A♭3 at 34. From 34 b3 to 36 b2 the sound is A♭1 (Cbn), A♭2 (B.cl.) and E♭3 (Bsn 1) with nothing above. That is about 7 seconds at the poco rit. (♩=49, then 45), and C returns only as the pickup at 36 b3. The composer's stated plan (trans.ily header, c020) is that "the A section closes on A♭ and D♭ enters at 37 by phrase modulation". A bare A♭–E♭ fifth is the weakest way to state that tonic just before the change of key.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 368 and Review 13 (PDF 369): a pedal "most often begins and ends with a statement of the chord it prolongs". The pedal begins at 30–32 with the full triad (C4 in the C.a. at 32) but ends without it. The open fifth does not come from any stated decision. c140 and c149 placed the solo on A♭2 and kept bassoon 2 off it to avoid a unison, and both concerns still hold with this fix. Dvořák's A♭ pedal at Largo 43–45 also leads straight into the new key.
- **Fix (applied, trans.ily, `transCorAnglais`).** `… | aes2.) | c'2.(\pp | c'4) r4 c''4(\p | }` (was `aes2.) | R2. | r2 c''4(\p |`). The C.a. holds C4 pp, which completes the triad in open spacing (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 C4) and stays two octaves from the solo. The rest on 36 b2 gives a breath before the C5 pickup. It also means the C.a. does not leap C4→C5 against the bass clarinet's B♭2→C3, which would be a direct octave. The woodwind choir, the solo, the pedal and the pickup into 37 are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 67: no motion over the dominant pedal (recorded, no change)

The E♭7 at 65–66 dies away, and at 67 only horn 1's E♭4 sounds over the basses' E♭2 and the timpani's E♭3: three octaves of the pedal tone and no chord for a whole bar. A&S PDF 367 defines the pedal as a tone held "through chord changes or contrapuntal activity in other voices". At 67 there is neither. The reason for keeping it is the cyclic plan, "horn 1 unsupported (67–70)", and the protected retransition 65–70. The horn's E♭ starts the motto tail that brings in the dominant seventh again at 68–69. Without the plan, I would have the bassoons enter on G3/D♭3 at 67 rather than 68. I make no proposal.

### 4. Bar 52: cor anglais F4 over the dominant pedal (recorded, no change)

Over the A♭ pedal at 52 (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 C4 E♭5), the cor anglais holds F4 for three beats and then G♭4, and the F becomes the third of D♭ at 53. It is not an anticipation by A&S's definition, because it comes on the stronger beat and is longer than the note it anticipates (PDF 363 §25). It works as an added sixth over V that the change of chord resolves, close to the whole-chord effect of Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365). It forms a seventh with Vln II's E♭5. It is approached by step (A♭–G♭–F) and resolved at 53 when the chord changes, which meets PDF 368's requirement for upper-voice dissonances. No change.

### 5. Pedal plan and its weight in the form (recorded, no change)

Pedals: tonic 1–6, 30–36, 83–86 and 89–92; dominant 51–52, 60 and 65–70. Together they take about a third of the movement's 345 s. Each one sits where A&S PDF 367 puts it: tonic at the start, at the close of A and at the end; dominant before each return (53, 61, 71). The climax at 61 gets only one bar of dominant (60), where the lesser return at 53 gets two. The 57–60 rising bass (protected) and the A♭ timpani roll at 60 make up for that, and lengthening 60 would change the bar count and the 8-page layout. The three six-bar pedal blocks (1–6, 31–36, 65–70) are the same length, but section proportions are closed (HISTORY line 128). No proposal.
