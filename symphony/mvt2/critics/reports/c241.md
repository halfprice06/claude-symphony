## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–56: the harp figure is marked louder than the theme A counter-line in the same octave (applied)

**Problem.** At 53 theme B returns in the violins (p<mp), and Hn 1 with Cl 1 adds the counter-line built from theme A's head (`aes8 bes8 | des'4. f'8 bes'2~ | bes'2 ges'2 | c'2 des'2`). It is the only place in the movement where the two themes sound together, which makes it the best chance for a listener to connect them. The counter-line starts pp and grows to mp at 55 (Cl 1 p). The harp's triplet accompaniment lies in exactly the same octave (A♭3–B♭4) and is marked p at 53 and mp at 55. So the accompaniment figure is a level above the counter-line for two bars and equal to it at 55. The figure also has continuous eighth-note attacks against the counter-line's held halves.

**Evidence.** Adler part 1 PDF 113–114: the harp's middle two octaves are its "rich and warm" register, and "the fortissimo at the top is like a mezzo forte in the middle". This octave is therefore where a harp marking counts for most. Adler part 2 PDF 184 (Schoenberg's Hauptstimme and Nebenstimme marks): principal gestures are marked louder than subordinate ones, and subsidiary ideas in a prominent register are pianissimo. The harp figure ranks below both themes here. Adler part 1 PDF 117 lists the harp as an accompanying instrument at this point. It is not the melody.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-241/work, `sections/bSec.ily`, bSecHarpR):**
- bar 53: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"^-^|^-^^" des' f' des' aes des' } ...` (was `aes8\p`)
- bar 55: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8\p des' es' des' bes des' } ...` (was `bes8\mp`)

The harp still grows (pp to p) with the build. The build's endpoints (violins p at 53, mf at 56, f at 59, ff at 61; c218) are unchanged. The score stays at 8 pages and the harp part keeps its layout.

### 2. Bars 75–77: harp p<mf against the counter-melody at mp (optional, not applied)

**Problem.** In A′ the cellos (mp) and Hn 2 (p<mp) carry the counter-melody in A♭3–D♭4. The harp grows p<mf to 77 with rolled chords whose RH reaches D♭4–F4 (76) and G3–E♭4 (77), in the same register. At 77 the harp is a level above the counter-melody.

**Evidence.** As in Finding 1: adler1 PDF 113–114 and adler2 PDF 184. c116 set this crescendo so that the harp grows with the orchestra and cited the same Adler pages. Single rolled chords decay quickly, so the overlap is shorter than at 53–56. That is why this item ranks low.

**Fix if wanted (aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpL bar 77):** `<es,, es,>2.\mp\arpeggio` (was `\mf`). Keep `\f` at 78 b3. The harp still grows with the orchestra, which answers c116, and it stays under the counter-melody. I did not test this.

### 3. Bars 82–88: the harp's melodic moment and the coda's reassembly of theme A (recorded, no change)

The harp states theme A's head at 82 b3–83: the pickup E♭4–F4 at pitch, then A♭4–C5–E♭5 in harmonics. The timpani mirror it at 84–85 (E♭–C–A♭). Clarinet 1 then begins the tail on the same E♭5 at 85 (E♭–D–D♭–C to 88). Across three colours, the coda plays theme A's first phrase once more, ending on the C5 the movement started from. That suits a theme that is meant to be remembered. Dvořák's Largo coda does the same, breaking the tune into pieces separated by rests. The harmonics follow every condition in adler1 PDF 116 (rules 14–16 above). The harp's role as a melodic instrument (PDF 117) is met, which is also the cyclic rule's "melodic moment for harp". The harp could double the harmonics an octave higher in the LH (two hands, adler1 PDF 116), but the sound is already clear over a ppp pedal (c063 closed the balance item), so I propose nothing. Protected: harp–timpani–clarinet chain 83–89.

### 4. Bars 53–56: stale pedal note in the source (applied, comment only)

The comment above bSecHarpR says the A pedal goes to A♮ "during 55 b3–4 for 56 b1–2". Since c137's revoicing, bar 56 has E♭–G♭ and B♭–F and no A♮ at all. Adler part 1 PDF 112–113 asks for a pedal plan that the harpist can trust. A composer who copied this note into the part would ask for a pedal change the music does not need. **Fix (applied):** the line now reads "No A natural in 53–56 since c137's revoicing, so no pedal change inside the figure." The printed score and parts are unchanged.

### 5. Bars 7–14: close RH dyads under the horn's theme (recorded, no change)

Adler part 1 PDF 114 says three-note chords per hand, spread wide, are the most effective on the harp. The RH at 7–13 plays two-note close chords at C3–E♭3, under the horn's A♭3–B♭4. A wider spread would put harp notes above the horn at its attacks, which c019 and c057 removed. The horn theme's first statement must stay on top, so the melody rule wins over the idiom rule. The LH octaves supply the width (A♭1–E♭2 under C3–E♭3). No change.

### 6. Bars 37–44: solid harp octaves with the pizzicato bass under theme B (recorded, no change)

The octaves are not rolled, so they attack with the pizzicato (adler1 PDF 114–115: solid unless a wavy line is written). They lie in the low "sombre, sonorous" octaves (PDF 113), three octaves under the flute's theme B at F5. The theme has no harp note in its own register. The pedal changes for A♮ at 40 are timed as PDF 112–113 asks. No change.
