## Findings

All findings are records; none needs a change in the score. They are ranked by how useful they are to the composer and to later critics.

### 1. The v362 inventory of 6/4s matches c283; each one has a function in the book's terms (record, no change)

The scan of v362 finds exactly these 6/4s:
- 5 (whole bar): neighbouring iv6/4 over the A♭ pedal, upper voices in 6ths, bass doubled (§4, PDF 308–309, Ex. 19-8 for the strong position). Protected link.
- 21 b2: passing I6/4 between IV and the French sixth, with a chromatic voice exchange F5–E♭5–D5 against D♭–E♭–F♭ and the bass tone doubled (§5, PDF 309).
- 38 b4½ and 43 b2: arpeggio 6/4s of ii in the rests of the pizz. bass (§13, PDF 320).
- 43 b3: accented cadential 6/4 (§3, §9).
- 68 b3: passing 6/4 over the dominant pedal, inside 8-7-6-5 (§6–7, PDF 312–315).
- 74 b3: one eighth, E♭–G over B♭1 inside V4/3 (§13).

33 b3 shows in the scan only because the A♭ pedal sits under ii7 (B♭–D♭–F with the pedal as its 7th, ii4/2); it is not a 6/4. Bar 14's harp change in v362 (A♭4–E♭5 over the held A♭) adds none. Nothing in the 79 cycles since c283 has added or removed a 6/4. This answers the book's closing rule (PDF 324): every 6/4 in the movement has a stated function.

### 2. Bar 43 b3: the cadential 6/4 is correct; correct the c283 wording, and keep the viola's E♭3 (record, no change)

**What sounds.** Bass A♭1/A♭2 (Cb, harp, Vc), viola pizz. E♭3–A♭3–E♭3, cor anglais D♭4 (the 4th), flute F5 (the 6th). On b4: Fl E♭5 (6-5), Ca B♭3, Vc C3 (the 3rd), viola E♭3–G♭3–A♭3 (8-7), so V7 arrives, and V at 44.

**Wording.** c283 says the 4th "leaves the cor anglais by step to B♭". D♭4 to B♭3 is a leap of a 3rd. The case is the book's Ex. 19-19 (PDF 316), where the 4th's resolution goes to another voice so the upper line can move on. Here the cellos' A♭2 → C3 carries the resolution, which c222 recorded correctly. Please record it in HISTORY as "4th D♭ leaves by leap, resolution transferred to Vc C3 (Ex. 19-19)".

**Viola E♭3.** On b3 the viola sounds E♭3, the note the flute's F5 resolves to, together with the F5, so the chord is A♭–E♭–D♭–F rather than a pure 6/4. I checked whether to remove it. The book allows freer dissonance treatment of the cadential 6/4 in instrumental style (§9, PDF 315). The E♭ is a pp pizz. note two octaves and a 2nd below the F, and it belongs to the arpeggio figure that runs through 41–44. Every replacement has already been tried and rejected: F3 makes octaves with the flute's 6-5 (c221), D♭ doubles the 4th (c192; PDF 317), C3 or C4 sounds the 3rd against the cor anglais's D♭4, and a viola rest breaks the triplet pattern one beat before the half close. Keep it.

### 3. Bar 68: the passing 6/4 over V and the delayed 7th follow Ex. 19-14 and 19-16 (record, no change)

Hn 1 goes E♭4 (67) – D4 – D♭4 – C4 (68) – B♭3 (69) over the E♭ pedal, with Bsn 1 B♭3–A♭3–G3 below it. The D♭ on 68 b2 is the 7th. The step to C4 on b3, which makes the 6/4 with Bsn 1's A♭3, does not resolve the 7th (PDF 312: the 6th of the 6/4 is a passing tone, not a goal). Bsn 2 brings the 7th back as D♭3 at 69–70 and resolves it to C3 on the downbeat of 71. That is the Mozart case in Ex. 19-14, where the 7th resolves bars later in another voice. The full line is 8-7-6-5 over 6-5-4-3 (Ex. 19-16, PDF 315). No change.

### 4. Bar 21: textbook passing I6/4 inside the protected 17–22 sequence (record, no change)

IV (21 b1) – I6/4 (b2) – French sixth on F♭ (b3) – V (22). The bass rises D♭–E♭–F♭ while Vln I falls F5–E♭5–D5. This is the voice exchange of §5 (PDF 309), made chromatic (F against F♭). The 6/4's bass tone is doubled (Cb E♭2, Vc E♭3, Vln I E♭5), which the book names as the usual result of the exchange. A♭3 (violas) is held as the stable 4th, as in the book's description of the passing V6/4 ("the 4th above the bass is a stable tone"). No change.

### 5. Where a new cadential 6/4 could go, and why none should be added (record, no change)

The accented 6/4 delays an expected arrival (§3, PDF 307). I checked every V arrival:
- 10, 26 (half closes of theme A) and 60, 81 (climax approaches): the melody holds the 3rd of V (G3, G4, C6, G6). A 6/4 there would need the melody to change. 60 and 81 are protected; the theme cadences were settled by c012 and c283.
- 22: the melody's E♭5 would allow a French sixth → I6/4 → V (with C♭ by mixture), a common 19th-century path. But 17–22 is a protected sequence, c192 already fixed the voice leading of the French sixth into 22, and it would add a second cadential 6/4 to A's 30 bars when the movement's only one is the B1 half close at 43–44. That single 6/4 is what makes 44 sound different from 10, 22 and 26.
- Coda: Ex. 19-30b (Schumann ending on I6/4) is ruled out by the cyclic plan, which needs a plain root-position A♭ chord for the finale's German sixth and the scherzo's G♯ join.

Using few 6/4s is correct for this style: Dvořák 9/ii uses weak-beat and plagal forms (c283 Finding 1), not repeated accented ones.

### 6. Bar 58 b4 is a 4–3 suspension in a seventh chord, not a 6/4 (record, no change)

On 58 b4 the melody G♭5/G♭6 (Fl 1, Ob 1, violins) is held over D♭ with C♭ (Cl 2) and D♭5 (Cl 1). It resolves to F on b4½ (Vc F3, Hn 1 F4, melody F5/F6), giving D♭7 = V7/IV into G♭ at 59. Because the chord has a 7th, it is not a 6/4 in the book's sense, and the 4th resolves by step in its own voice (§3, PDF 306–307: the resolution is down by step over a held bass). Bar 58 is protected (c119, c151, c198). No change.

### 7. The link (1–6) and the retransition (65–70) use the two 6/4 types that suit joins (record, no change)

The modulation plan joins its keys with 6/4s only twice: at the link, where the iv6/4 at 5 is a neighbour inside the new tonic after the common-tone move C → A♭ (§4), and at the retransition, where the passing 6/4 at 68 lies inside V (§6–7). Both prolong a single harmony; neither takes part in a key change. That matches the book's point that the neighbouring and passing types depend on the 5/3 they decorate (PDF 309, 312). The remote moves (D♭ → F♯ minor at 46–48, G♭ → F♭ → A minor at 62–64, E → E♭ at 64–65) use seventh chords and augmented sixths, not 6/4s, so nothing in this reading bears on them. No change.
