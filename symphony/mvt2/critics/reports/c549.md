## Findings

### 1. The bVI chords at 78 follow the loud-chord spacing (recorded, no change)
- **Bars:** 78 b1 and b3.
- **Evidence:** c529 did not check 78. At 78 b1 (F♭ major, the deceptive turn) the sounding chord is E2 E3 A♭3 B3 A♭4 A♭5: root alone in the two lowest octaves (Cb, Bsn 2), as chords 1-12 show (PDF 505-506). The melody's A♭ is doubled most; c542 recorded that the deceptive cadences double the third of VI, and here the third is the home tonic held through the turn. At 78 b3 the chord is E♭1 E♭2 E♭3 G3 C4 E♭4 G4 C5 C6, root in three octaves (harp LH, Cb, Bsn 2/timp), horn C4 inside, flute C6 on top: principles 2, 3, 4 and 11 at once. No change.

### 2. The approach chord at 60 b3 follows the brass-under-winds marking (recorded, no change)
- **Bars:** 60.
- **Evidence:** A♭1 A♭2 F♯3 C4 F♯4 A♭4 C5 C6: root A♭ alone in the lowest two octaves (Tba, Cb, B.tbn, Bsn 2), brass in close middle position (Tbn F♯3, Hn 3 C4, Hn 1 F♯4), woodwind and violins on top. The brass enter pp< under the woodwind, one degree or more below them, which is chord 2's practice (PDF 505) applied to a crescendo. The seventh (G♭) is spelt F♯ only in the MIDI dump.

### 3. The soft frame chords follow chords 13-16 (recorded, no change)
- **Bars:** 3-6, 87-92.
- **Evidence:** at 87 the chord is F1 F2 F3 A♭3 F4 A♭4 D♭5: muted horns one note each (F3, A♭3), C.a. F4, Cl 1 D♭5, strings muted and soft. At 92: A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5. Chords 14 and 16 (PDF 507) are pp woodwind-and-horn chords in close position over a single bass note; the score's soft chords use the same layout, with muted strings added as Dvořák 9/ii does at its close. Protected (plagal close 87-92).

### 4. Comparison with Tchaikovsky 1/ii (recorded, no change)
- **Shared plan.** Tchaikovsky frames the movement with a soft muted-string passage, gives the main tune first to a wind soloist (oboe) over a sparse accompaniment, passes it to the strings in the middle register, and builds to one full statement before returning to the frame. This movement does the same: a soft frame (1-6 link, 83-92 coda, both with C5 on top), theme A first in a wind soloist (horn 1, 7-14, strings silent), then in the violins with a cello counter-line (23-30), then in octaves at the A′ peak (79-81), then the frame. A conductor who knows Tchaikovsky's movement would recognise the method.
- **Difference.** Tchaikovsky's peak puts the tune in the four horns in unison ff, the family that had held back. Here the peak puts theme A in violins and flutes with horns a2 on the counter-line (79-81). That is the A′ texture plan (c067 and later, protected) and it follows principle 6 (unison wind pairs concentrate the chord, chord 5). Rimsky's tables give no reason to move the tune to the horns: the horn already owns theme A at 7-14, and repeating that colour at the peak would lose the contrast the plan relies on. I do not propose it.
- **Scoring restraint.** Tchaikovsky uses trumpets at his peak; this movement keeps them tacet (c060, cyclic). With the brass marked one degree under the woodwind and strings at 61 and 79, the score reaches a full ff without them, which the tables show is normal practice (chords 2, 9).
