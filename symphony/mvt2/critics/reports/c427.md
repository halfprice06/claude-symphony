# Critic 427: v427, lens 7 (texture and counterpoint)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 16 "The Orchestral Transcription" (adler2 PDF 197-255, printed pp. 510-568), fourth series. I read the text of every page in the range. Prose is on PDF 197-201, 203, 205, 208-209, 213, 217, 224-225, 227-228, 230-233, 236, 239, 245-248 and 255. The other pages are examples. I rendered all 59 pages at 110 dpi into /tmp/critic-427/pages and looked at each example page as an image. The examples are: XVI-1 and XVI-2 (Ravel, PDF 200), XVI-3 (Bartók, 201), XVI-4 (Mahler, 201), XVI-5 (Beethoven 6, 202), XVI-6 and XVI-11 (Bizet "La Toupie", 202, 214-216), XVI-7 (Chopin/orchestra, 203-204), XVI-8 (Mussorgsky-Ravel, 204-205), XVI-9 (Brahms Hungarian Dance 1, 206-207), XVI-10 (Dvořák Slavonic Dance 8, 209-212), XVI-12 (Ravel "Petit Poucet", 218-223), XVI-13 (Gallo/Stravinsky *Pulcinella*, 225-226), XVI-14 (Brahms-Schoenberg op. 25, 229), XVI-15 to XVI-17 (Milhaud *Suite française*, 232-235), XVI-18 to XVI-20 (Schoenberg op. 43A/B, 237-244), and XVI-21 and XVI-22 (Schumann quintet and Suppé for a school ensemble, 248-254). The OCR of the example pages is unusable, so everything I say about notation comes from the images. Three things show only in the images. In XVI-7b the pianist's left-hand arpeggio becomes three layers: a held bass, off-beat string chords, and a separate tenor line in two bassoons. In XVI-12b (PDF 218-223) Ravel hands the slurred eighth-note background from the muted violins to the violas and cellos at each new entrance of the tune. In XVI-11b (PDF 215) the "whispering" cello tritone at m. 28 is a slow alternation under a ppp held chord.

This chapter has had three texture passes before this one (c037, c137, c277) and two holistic passes that used its examples (c219, c319). c137 and c277 made the changes this reading supports most directly (harp at 53-56, violas at 57-58). I did not repeat them.

Verdict: v427 follows every principle in this chapter that applies to an original orchestral slow movement. The layers are ranked and kept apart in rhythm, register and colour. ## Findings

### 1. Bars 71-78: the viola figure plays notes that clarinet 2 is holding (recorded, no change)

**What the script shows.** The violas' eighth-note figure attacks E♭4 at 71 b1.5 and b2.5, and at 75 b1.5 and b2.5, while clarinet 2 holds E♭4 (pp, then p<). It attacks A♭4 at 72 b1.5 and 73 b2 while clarinet 2 holds A♭4, and it touches clarinet 2's G4 at 77 and A♭4 at 78. This is the same kind of meeting on one pitch that c137 (harp, 53-56) and c277 (violas, 57-58) removed.

**Why it is not a fault here.** In 53-58 the held notes belonged to a counter-line, a middleground melody. Here clarinet 2 holds harmony notes inside the background. c247 and c324 already moved it off the violins' melody notes. Adler PDF 209 describes this layout in Dvořák's Slavonic Dance 8, m. 9: sustained "harmonic counterpoint" that stands in for the pianist's pedal and is "important enough to be emphasized and sustained in order to complete the harmony without making it heavy". PDF 230 item 2 says the same of Schoenberg's Brahms: the harmony is kept in sustained chords, "the use of the sostenuto pedal". c137 recorded the same relation between Vln II and the violas at 37-40 as "a sustain of the viola arpeggio, not a doubling fault". The cello counter-melody (the middleground layer) gets no re-attacks from the figure (c127). Bars 71-82 are also protected (A′ texture).

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bars 15-22: the oboe and the solo violin alternate without overlap (recorded, no change)

**What happens.** Oboe 1 (15-16, 19-20) and the solo first violin (17-18, 21-22) answer each other in two-bar phrases. Each phrase begins on the downbeat after the other has stopped. Vln II's louré A♭4 runs through the whole passage, and the violas and divided cellos keep moving under it.

**Why it is right.** c007 left "overlapping dialogue" open and c407 closed it, saying the passage has a real middleground. This chapter supports that decision for another reason as well. In "Petit Poucet" (PDF 217; Ex. XVI-12b, PDF 219-220) Ravel hands the tune from the oboe to the English horn "without overlap and a fresh articulation", while the background "dovetails or overlaps". That is exactly what happens at 15-22: the tune changes hands cleanly and the background is continuous. Dvořák's delayed oboe entrance (PDF 213) concerns an answering counterpoint that runs against the tune. Here the two players share one melody, so an overlap would blur its phrase ends. The Vln II louré at 15-22 and the mute plan are protected (c096).

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 41, 44 and 82: plucked notes inside held notes (recorded, no change)

**What the script shows.** At 41 b1.67 the pizzicato violas play A♭3 while the cor anglais holds its first note, A♭3. At 41 b2.33 they play D♭3 inside the cellos' held D♭3, and at 44 b1 and b2.33 E♭3 inside the cellos' E♭3. At 82 b2 the harp plucks A♭3-C4 under the counter-melody's last note, C4, which the cellos, horns 1·2 and bassoon 1 hold with a diminuendo.

**Why it is right.** Adler PDF 217 cites this exact effect in "Petit Poucet" as one to imitate: "The sustained G, softly accented by the double basses' pizzicato, is held by the clarinet into the next measure, an effect comparable to that produced by the damper pedal on the piano." A plucked note decays and does not compete with a bowed or blown sustain, so it colours the sustain instead of covering it. c057 chose pizzicato at 41-44 for this reason. c267 already removed the strikes that fell on the cor anglais's own attacks (A♭3 and D♭4 on the beat). The one left at b1.67 lands inside a held note, where the chapter approves it. Bars 41-44 are also under c124's rule for 37-60.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 27-29: the crescendo to f is written only as hairpins (recorded, no change)

**What happens.** The first violin statement of theme A rises to f at 28 with strings only. Horns 3·4 enter pp only at 29.

**Reading.** Adler PDF 201-202 (Mahler XVI-4, Beethoven XVI-5) and 213 (Dvořák m. 33, Bizet mm. 13-18) say a crescendo is clearer when the orchestration writes it out. The score does this in both later builds (53-61 and 75-79, Reading notes row 3).

**Why it stays.** Bars 23-30 are protected as "the plain first statement" (c007), and the protection exists so that A′ (71-82) can add exactly what this chapter asks for. A string-only crescendo in the first statement and an orchestrated one in the return is the contrast the chapter describes on PDF 209 and 224: a repeat gets a fuller distribution. Adding winds at 27-28 would undo that plan.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bars 87-92: no added motion in the sustained close (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** Bizet at m. 28 of "La Toupie" (PDF 213; image PDF 215) drops a held chord from pp to ppp and adds slow motion inside it: the cellos' "whispering" tritone and a timpani roll.

**Score.** In 87-88 the cor anglais (F4-F♭4), clarinet 1 (D♭5-C5) and violas move inside the held chord. Vln I enters on C5 at 89 and then the chord holds to ppp. The motion the chapter asks for is there, at 87-88, before the final hold. The plagal close 87-92 is protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Script check of the middle parts under solos (recorded, no change)

Adler PDF 230 item 3: in Schoenberg's Brahms "there is no actual doubling of the middle parts" until the solo ends. I checked every solo passage (horn 7-14 and 67-70, bass clarinet 31-36, cor anglais 41-44 and 47-51) for inner parts that attack the solo's pitch while it sounds. Apart from the decaying harp and pizzicato notes in finding 3, the only hits are the harp RH meeting the bassoon chords' notes at 7-13. Those are two accompaniment layers meeting each other, not the solo, and that layout is protected (c057; harp plan 7-14). Bars 59-60 (viola sextuplets against the counter-line and horn 4) were recorded as no change by c277 and c397, and I do not reopen them.

**Fix.** None.
