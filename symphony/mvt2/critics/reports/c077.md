Two open items can be closed on Adler's evidence rather than changed. First, "Vln II chords vs viola arpeggio 37–40" and "harp RH vs viola figure 75–77". Both are the technique Adler describes on PDF 262–264: one part sustains or plucks the same pitches that another part articulates differently, and he likens the effect to divided violins playing arco and pizz. on the same notes. The unison here is that intended effect, not the "overlap" of PDF 257, which Adler describes for homophonic wind chords. Second, "horn return of theme A at 70–74". Horn 1's solo tail at 67–70 already brings back theme A's colour just before the reprise, which is Debussy's practice on PDF 261.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 15–22: the dialogue uses two colours, and the third statement is scored like the first (applied)
**Problem.** The two-bar gesture goes oboe (15–16), solo violin (17–18), oboe (19–20), solo violin (21–22), and the violas or cellos answer after each one. Bars 19–20 repeat the orchestration of 15–16 exactly, a step lower, and they belong to the sequence that 17→21 continues.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 253–254 (Ex. VIII-20, Beethoven 3/i bars 45–55) passes the gesture through oboe, clarinet, flute and violin before the tutti sums it up. That is the shape of 15–23, which ends with the tutti violins' pickup at 22, but here the gesture uses only two colours. Adler PDF 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration. This is often true of any sequential material." No woodwind besides the oboe plays a melodic phrase in bars 7–36 (the "woodwinds rarely carry themes" open item). Clarinet 1 first sounds at 53.
**Fix.** Give bars 19–20 to clarinet 1 with the same pitches, rhythm, dynamics and hairpins. Oboe 1 rests. Concert C5–G4 is written D5–A4: the bottom of the clarion register and the throat A at p, which suits a dying close.
```

aOneClOne = { R2.*12 | c''4(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce" des''8 c''8 bes'4 | aes'2\> g'4)\! | R2.*2 | R2.*8 | }
```

### 2. Bars 14→15: winds and strings meet at a barline with no overlap (applied)
**Problem.** At the end of bar 14 the horn, bassoons, bass clarinet and harp all release, and at 15 the muted strings and the oboe start together. This is the only change of choir in the A section that is not dovetailed. Since c063, 30→31 overlaps (the Cbsn pedal), 36→37 hands the bass clarinet's C4 to flute 1, and 70→71 has a pickup.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 252, Ex. VIII-18 (Brahms 2/iii, last 8 bars): "the woodwind chord dissolves into a string chord". In the example the winds hold through the strings' pp entry and fade under it. The harmony makes this easy here: bar 14 is I with Bsn 2 on C3, and bar 15 is I6 with the cellos on C3.
**Fix.** Tie both bassoons into 15 and fade them by beat 3. The hairpin is shared, so the dynamics on the shared staff stay equal.
```
aOneBsnOne: … bes2 g4 | aes2.~ | aes2\> r4\! | R2.*15 |
aOneBsnTwo: … es2. | c2.~ | c2\> r4\! | R2.*15 |
```
Bsn 2's C3 becomes the cellos' C3, and Bsn 1's A♭3 sits under Vln II's A♭4. Nothing moves, so there are no new parallels. The horn line and the 7–14 layout are not touched.

### 3. Bars 53–56: bassoon 2 doubles the cellos at pitch (applied; open item "Bsn 2 = cellos 53–56")
**Problem.** `bSecBsnTwo` at 53–56 is the cello line note for note in the same octave (`des2 c2 | bes,2 ges,2 | es2 des2 | a,2 bes,4`), with the basses an octave below.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 265–266: "'at pitch' doublings are thought to detract from the clarity of a line and thicken the sound by muddying the overtones of both instruments"; octave doublings "are more successful." The unison also weakens the staging that Adler PDF 253 recommends (choirs heard apart, then together). Without it, 53–56 is strings and harp with the horn/clarinet counter-line and bassoon 1's inner F3–G♭3, and the woodwind bass (Bsn 2 with the bass clarinet) arrives fresh at the mp< of 57.
**Fix.** `bSecBsnTwo` 53–56: `R1*4 |`. Bar 57 onward is unchanged. Bassoon 1's 55–56 now prints as "1." on the shared staff.

### 4. Two open items closed by the reading (no change)
Vln II chords against the viola triplets (37–40) and harp RH against the viola figure (75–77) are Adler's same-pitch articulation pairing (PDF 262–264), not unison overlap (PDF 257). The horn colour already returns at 67–70 before the reprise (PDF 261). I recommend the composer close all three items.
