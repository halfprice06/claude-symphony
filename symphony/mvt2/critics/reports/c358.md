## Findings

### 1. Bar 60: the timpani roll starts p< while the trombones and tuba on the same A♭ start pp< (applied)

**Problem.** At 60 the timpani's A♭2 roll (`aes,2.:32\p\<~ aes,8\f`) begins one degree above Tbn 2, the bass trombone and the tuba (`\pp\<`). Those three hold the same A♭ under the poco allargando. The drum has been silent since its damped stroke at 53. It is the only instrument to begin the brass crescendo chord at p, so it is heard as a new attack before the crescendo starts.

**Evidence.** Rimsky's crescendo is the brass and drum chord growing into the climax (PDF 130). Its starting level is set by the after-a-rest rule: pp or ff, with p "less successful" (PDF 136). c349 applied that rule to the low brass at this bar and restored pp<. In the appendix chords the timpani roll carries the low brass's mark. In chords 13 and 16 (PDF 506-507) the trombones, tuba and timpani are all pp. In the loud chords 9 and 20 the timpani are f with the brass under ff woodwind. This score already follows the loud case at 61-62 and 79-81 (timpani f) and the soft case at 52 (pp<). Bar 60 is the exception.

**Earlier decisions answered.** c134 asked to keep "the timpani p< at 60", citing Adler's Bloch example (adler part 2 PDF 184): a roll inside a crescendo starts soft and then grows, because an ff roll would cover everything. pp< meets that principle better than p<. c128 closed "poco allargando vs timpani p at 60", which was about where the tempo word sits, not about the dynamic. The tied stroke, the f arrival on 60 b4 (c056) and the f roll at 61 (c084) do not change.

**Fix** (bSec.ily, `bSecTimp`): `R1 | aes,2.:32\pp\<~ aes,8\f r8 |` (was `\p\<`). The new comment cites PDF 130, 136 and 506-507.

### 2. Bars 61-62: horn and trombone levels after c264 (checked, no change; comment out of date)

Hn 1 and Hn 2 are marked ff, Hn 3·4 a2 f, and the trombones and tuba f. This looks uneven, but it follows Rimsky. Since c264 split Hn 1·2 onto separate notes, they are single horns, and single horns are marked one degree above the trombones (PDF 41: "if the trumpets and trombones play pp, the horns should be marked p"; PDF 103). The Hn 3·4 pair counts as one trombone. The woodwinds and strings are ff (PDF 112; chords 9 and 20). The comment on `bSecTbnTwo` and `bSecTuba` still says "c218: f with the horn pairs", which stopped being true at c264. The composer should change it to "f: Hn 3·4 pair at f, Hn 1 and Hn 2 single at ff (PDF 41, 103)". This is a comment only, so I did not apply it.

### 3. Bars 63-65: woodwinds leave before the brass (checked, no change)

Rimsky's diminuendo removes brass, then woodwind, then strings (PDF 130; No. 259). At 63 every woodwind except Fl 1's single sf note stops, while Tbn 1 and the bass trombone continue to the end of 64 and the strings to 65-66. The order is broken on purpose: the quotation of movement I's first theme must sound alone in trombone 1 (c158, c303, protected). The brass still ends on p (c218), and the strings carry the last, longest fade, so the end of the order holds.

### 4. Bar 57: the basses re-enter mf< (checked, no change)

After resting at 53-56 the basses enter at mf, a level Rimsky calls colourless for re-entries after a rest (PDF 136, "capable of wider application"). They are not a fresh colour, though. They take over the bass from the cellos, which reached mf at 56 (c164). The woodwind bass doubling them (B.cl., Bsn 2, p<) is the new colour, and it enters soft. Keep.

### 5. Bars 75 and 78: Hn 2 p< and Hn 1·2 mf< re-entries (checked, no change)

Both are exceptions to PDF 136. Their reasons still hold: Hn 2 sits one level under the cellos' mp counter-melody (c217), and the mf pickup at 78 b3 must be heard inside the f tutti (c088, c218, c273; not to be re-raised). The horns join this build early and softly, as the horns do in No. 256 and No. 257.

### 6. Bar 58: the dip before the f at 59 (checked, no change)

Inside the prolonged crescendo, Fl 1, Ob 1 and the violins have a one-eighth > on 58 b4 before f at 59. The clarinets, horn, bass line and violas keep growing through the bar. Rimsky's examples No. 256-257 show unbroken crescendi. Here only the melody breathes at its phrase end (the breath mark at 58), and the orchestral crescendo, made by adding instruments, does not stop. Keep.
