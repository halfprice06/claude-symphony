## Findings

### 1. Bar 41 beats 1–2: the viola pizzicato strikes the cor anglais's first two notes in unison, on the same beats (applied)

**Problem.** Theme B's second phrase starts in the cor anglais at 41 on A♭3, at the bottom of its range, with the arpeggio A♭3–D♭4–F4. The violas' first pizz. group is `aes8 des' f' des' aes des'`. Its first note is the C.a.'s A♭3, attacked together with it. Its fourth note (beat 2) is the C.a.'s D♭4, attacked together with it. In the first two beats of a woodwind solo in its weakest register, the accompaniment plucks the solo's own pitches in the solo's own octave. No other place in the movement does this.

**Evidence.** PDF 274: with a simple accompanying figure, "keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist whenever possible … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". Rhythmic contrast (here pizz. triplets against quarters) "lessens the importance" but does not remove it. PDF 275: the lowest fifth of a range "must be lightly accompanied". PDF 267, item 3: Mozart cuts the orchestra back the moment the flute enters in its soft register. c219 found the same register problem and lowered the dynamic, saying the violas "cannot move down an octave". They do not need to move a whole octave. A lower position of the same D♭ chord fits the viola's range. c108 already lowered beats 3–4 of this bar for a related reason.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-267/work, bSecVla, bar 41 b1–2).**
`\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8\pp\<^\markup \italic "pizz." des' f' des' aes des' }` →
`\tuplet 3/2 4 { des8\pp\<^\markup \italic "pizz." f aes f des aes }`
The group lies under the solo (D♭3–A♭3) and never attacks a pitch together with it. Its D♭3 is the cellos' held arco note, so the pizz. only articulates it. The group ends on A♭3 and steps to the G♭3 of beat 3. An ending on F3 would move in octaves with the basses' F2–G♭2. Beats 3–4 (c108), the dynamics (c219) and the figure's contour stay as they are.

### 2. Bar 42 beat 4: the viola's F4 follows the cor anglais's own resolution to F4 (applied)

**Problem.** At 42 b4 the C.a. resolves G♭4 to F4 on b4½ over the D♭ bass. The violas' last group, `aes8 des' f'`, plays F4 on b4⅔, a sixth of a beat later, in the same octave. It is the last near-unison with the solo in 41–44.

**Evidence.** As finding 1 (PDF 274). Doubling the note a suspension has just resolved to also weakens the resolution.

**Fix (applied, bSecVla bar 42).** `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' f' } |` → `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' aes } |`. A♭3 steps to the G♭3 at 43 b1.

**Parts consequence.** With the lower group at 41 the viola part became two pages. In genparts.py the viola's `system-system-spacing.padding` is now 0.1 (was 0.25). The part is one page again and the book is 30 pages; I checked the page image.

### 3. Bar 14 beat 1: bassoon 1 lands on horn 1's final A♭3 (no change)

This is the only remaining unison with a solo attack, and it is required. Bsn 1 holds the leading tone G3 at 13 b3 and must resolve it to A♭3. The horn arrives on A♭3 at the same moment, as the phrase's tonic. c219 removed the other bassoon unisons at 9 and 11 because those were not voice-leading needs. Recorded so it is not raised again.

### 4. Bars 53–56: the harp figure crosses the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line at 54 b1 and 56 b1–2 (recorded, no change)

When the soloist accompanies, the figure should be idiomatic (PDF 262–263, Ex. XVII-6) and separate from the other lines (PDF 274). Here the harp copies the viola figure in A♭3–A♭4. At 54 b1 its G♭4 is above the counter-line's D♭4. At 56 b1–2 its E♭4–G♭4 lies above the counter-line's closing C4. The crossings are short. The harp is a dynamic below the counter-line (c241). Moving the harp down at 56 would double Bsn 1's G♭3, and moving it up would put it into the violins' theme. c266 recorded the idiom question. I found no voicing that improves both points, so I propose no change.

### 5. Bars 7–14: the bass moves in the horn's own quarter-note rhythm at 8 and 11 (recorded; protected)

Ex. XVII-3 teaches that the accompaniment should neither duplicate nor imitate the solo's rhythm (PDF 259–260). At 8 and 11 the bass clarinet and the harp's left hand walk in quarters while the horn moves in quarters. That is chord-per-beat writing, not figuration. In Mozart's horn concerto, the strings under the solo horn play eighth-note figures (Ex. XVII-9). The horn theme's accompaniment, its harmony and the bassoon voicing are protected (c004, c019, c117, c219, c252). Here the quarters carry the descending-fifths harmony of the motto tail, which is also protected (c181). Recorded, not proposed.

### 6. Bars 71–78: the viola figure is legato, as the melody and counter-melody are (recorded, no change)

Ex. XVII-4 (PDF 260) contrasts a smooth solo with a detached string figure. In Ex. XVII-3 the violas' and basses' figure has rests. At 71–78 the violas' eighths are slurred in threes, under a legato theme and a legato cello and horn counter-melody (c257 recorded "every strand is legato"). The strands still differ in rhythm (eighths, dotted quarters, halves) and in register, which is the chapter's main requirement. The A′ texture plan is protected. A lighter articulation, such as two-note slurs at 71–74 only, would be the smallest test. Not proposed.

### 7. Doubling changes between 23–30 and 71–78 follow Haydn's practice (no change)

Haydn moves the flute from the soprano to the tenor two octaves up in the second phrase, and Adler says this gives "great variety" (PDF 288, Ex. XVII-24). In this score the reprise changes its doublings: violins in octaves instead of unison, Hn 2 joining the cello counter-melody, and a viola figure in place of chords. Bizet's rule that the violins' higher octave is held back for the climax (PDF 279) is kept in B (unison 53–56, octaves from 57). Recorded.
