### 1. The exit from IV, measured in time against both Mozart sketches (no change)

**Reading.** Ex. 26-11 (PDF 452): IV over bars 85–100, transition 101–108, reprise 109; about 2:1 in bars. Ex. 26-14 (PDF 453): IV from 261/262 to about 296, augmented sixth at 297–298, V at 300, I at 312; about 36:15, or 2.4:1. §15 (PDF 456): the return to V goes through VI and II/IV and often carries a seventh.

**Score.** From the tempo map in global.ily: D♭ 37–61 lasts about 102 s; the exit 62–70 (♭VI, A minor, French sixth, E♭7) lasts about 35 s (62: 4.4 s, 63: 4.4 s, 64 with allargando: 5.4 s, 65–70: 20.8 s). The ratio is 2.9:1. That is a little shorter, proportionally, than either Mozart example, and longer in absolute time (Mozart's 8-bar transition in a minuet tempo is roughly 12 s). The difference is explained by the reading's own distinction: in both Mozart examples IV is a quiet area, while here IV carries the movement's crescendo and climax (57–61), and the 6 bars of dominant at Tempo I, pp, with horn 1 alone, are long enough to cancel D♭ (G♮ in the violas at 65–66 and Bsn 1 at 69–70). A longer dominant would make the retransition the longest quiet passage since 31–36. c383 compared only Ex. 26-11 by bar count; the time measure and Ex. 26-14 support the same conclusion. No change.

### 2. The middle section is itself the Ex. 26-13 plan, and the movement repeats it at the large scale (no change)

**Reading.** §12 (PDF 452–453): tonicized VI leads down by thirds from I to IV; Ex. 26-13 does this in a phrase and Ex. 26-14 across a section, where the expanded IV then reaches V through an augmented sixth.

**Score.** Inside D♭ the keys fall by thirds exactly as in Ex. 26-13: D♭ (37) – B♭ minor (45) – G♭ minor written as F♯ minor (47–50) – A♭7 (51–52) – D♭ (53). The movement then repeats the shape of Ex. 26-14 at the large scale: IV (61) – ♭VI (F♭, 62 b3) – augmented sixth on F♭ (64 b4) – V (65) – I (71). Ex. 26-14's IV moves to V "through an augmented 6th chord", and 64 b4 is the French sixth that c232 put there. The middle section's inner plan and the movement's outer plan are therefore the same kind of motion at two sizes, which is the "purposeful" succession §7 (PDF 448) asks for. No change.

### 3. F♯ minor is spelled as a remote key but functions as a mixture key; the spelling is right (no change)

**Reading.** §2 and Points 1 (PDF 443, 456): related keys have signatures the same or one accidental apart; a key related through mixture is nearer than one that is not.

**Score.** The signatures follow the classification: A♭ (4 flats) and D♭ (5 flats) are one apart; F♯ minor (3 sharps, 47–50) and A minor (none, 63–64) carry their own signatures for 4 and 2 bars. Functionally, 47–50 is G♭ minor, iv of D♭ by mixture, so it is nearer than its signature suggests, and it returns to D♭ through A♭7 at 51–52. G♭ minor would need eight flats, so the sharp spelling is the practical one, as Dvořák writes D♭ minor as C♯ minor in 9/ii (signature change at 46). The A minor at 63–64 is the one truly remote key (♭ii of A♭) and it is also the shortest (about 9.8 s). Length is graded by nearness: D♭ 102 s, G♭/F♯ minor about 16 s, A minor about 10 s. No change.

### 4. D♭ waits 16 bars for its first authentic cadence, which §3 allows (no change)

**Reading.** §3 (PDF 443): the confirming cadence "can appear after intervening material" and usually contains II or IV.

**Score.** D♭ enters at 37 by phrase modulation. Its first dominant (cadential 6/4 to V at 43–44) is deflected to B♭ minor at 45, and the first V7–I in D♭ comes at 52–53, after iv (as G♭ minor) at 47–50, so the confirming cadence has its IV-form. The second, at 59–61 (G♭–G♮–A♭–D♭), is the climax. The delay keeps D♭ open during the quotation and makes 53 the point where the key is settled, which is also where the build begins (animando from 53). This order of events fits §3, and the reading gives no reason to add an earlier D♭ cadence. No change.

### 5. The coda tonicizes only by figuration, the weakest means in §5 (no change)

**Reading.** §5 (PDF 446): the least emphatic tonicizations come from melodic figuration alone, without chords, when the top voice borrows tones from another key over slower harmony.

**Score.** At 85–88 clarinet 1's E♭–D♮–D♭–C over the A♭ pedal borrows D♮ (leading tone of E♭) and then D♭ (from IV) without any chord leaving A♭ until IV6 at 87. The coda glances at V and IV with figuration only and keeps them from becoming keys, as is proper after the middle section has already spent 102 s on IV (§11). The chordal IV6–iv–I at 87–89 is the §16 footnote's I–IV–I in miniature. Protected (plagal close 87–92). No change.

### 6. The reprise has a dominant before it, against §16's alternative (no change)

**Reading.** §16 (PDF 456): in 19th-century ABA forms the middle section may expand IV and return to I at the reprise "without an intervening dominant".

**Score.** This score takes the other option, 6 bars of E♭7 (65–70). I considered whether the IV–I return of §16 would be stronger here and rejected it: the middle section ends not on D♭ but in A minor (63–64), and a direct move from A minor to A♭ would be a chromatic slide with no functional link. The retransition is also where the motto's tail (horn 1, 67–69) must sound before the A′ (protected list: 65–70). No change.
