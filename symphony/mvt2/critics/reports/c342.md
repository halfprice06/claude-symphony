## Findings

### 1. Bar 43 b2: the bSec.ily comment calls the chord IV6; it is ii4/3 (comment only, applied in the scratch copy)

**Bars:** 43 (bSecCb comment, c239).

**Problem.** The comment says the pizzicato bass arpeggiates "43 IV-IV6-V" and that there is "no 6/4 on a weak beat". At 43 b2 the sounding notes are B♭1 (Cb), B♭2 (Vc), B♭3 and D♭4 (viola figure, D♭ at b1.67), E♭4 (cor anglais, held from b1) and G♭5 (flute, held from b1). The sustained voices alone (B♭, E♭, G♭) look like a 6/4, and my 6/4 scan flags the beat. The chord is ii7 (E♭-G♭-B♭-D♭): ii6/5 on b1 over G♭, ii4/3 on b2 over B♭, with the melody's E♭ as its root. It is not IV. The comment's claim is right, but its label is wrong.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 66-67 (§9-10, Ex. 4-12, 4-13): name chord tones from the bass, and a numeral that ignores how a chord arises misleads. PDF 69-70 (§15-16, Ex. 4-18): with the 5th in the bass, a seventh chord is a 4/3, not a 6/4. The E♭ lasts a beat and a half on the downbeat in the melody, so it is a chord tone and not an added sixth. A later critic who reads "IV6" and then hears E♭ over B♭ would report a weak-beat 6/4 that the music does not contain.

**Fix (applied, comment only).** In bSec.ily, bSecCb, the c239 comment now reads: "43 ii6/5-ii4/3-V: c342, the bass arpeggiates ii7 G flat-B flat under the cor anglais's held E flat4, the 7th D flat4 in the viola figure, so 43 b2 is a 4/3, not a 6/4 (A&S PDF 69-70, Ex. 4-18; PDF 66-67: name chords from the bass)". The music text is unchanged.

**Test.** /tmp/critic-342/work built with tools/build.sh. build-warnings.txt is empty, score.pdf has 8 pages, and analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels. tools/midi-note-diff.py finds no difference between v342's score.midi and the scratch score.midi. The parts book was regenerated (genparts.py, lilypond) with no warnings and is still 30 pages.

### 2. Sevenths: all resolve down by step except the decided cases (recorded, no change)

The scan (PDF 67-68, Ex. 4-15) found three groups of sevenths that do not fall by step in their own voice.

- **Figuration in the viola triplets (38-44, 57-60).** In these the 7th leaps inside an arpeggio of the same chord. That is the figurated texture of Ex. 4-21 (PDF 70). The resolution comes in the sustained voices.
- **Sevenths exchanged within one chord.** These are 12 b2-b3, 28 b2-b3 and 76 b2-b3: the A♭ of ii6/5 goes to D♭ in Bsn 1, Vc and Hn while the melody keeps A♭ and takes it down to G at the next bar. The 7th stays in the chord and resolves where the chord changes.
- **60-61, cellos G♭3-A♭3.** Bsn 1, Tbn 2 and Hn 1 take the A♭7's seventh down. c294 recorded this and gave the reason it stays (protected Vln II and Vc octaves at 61). I have no new book reason to reopen it.

Since c202 the changed bars (77 b1 Vln I G5, 80-81 Hn 3·4 and violas, 52 cellos, 64 cellos) all take their sevenths down by step: A♭5-G5 at 76-77, A♭3-G3 and F♭4-E♭4 at 80-82, D♭4-C4 at 74-75.

### 3. Bar 36 b3: a bare C octave is the one bass tone heard alone that is not a root (recorded, no change)

PDF 63 says an unfigured bass sounds like a root. At 36 b3 only C3 (B.cl.) and C5 (C.a. pickup) sound, after two beats of A♭ and E♭. Read strictly by the rule, a lone C is a C root, and C-major material is reserved for the finale. In context, the A♭ chord has sounded for six bars and the C moves straight to D♭-F at 37, so the ear takes it as the third of A♭ and the leading tone of D♭. c202 reached the same verdict. It lasts one pp beat inside a melodic pickup. If the composer ever wants to remove the doubt, the cheaper fix is a held A♭1 in the contrabassoon through 36 b3 (`aes,,2.~` in place of `aes,,2 r4` at transCbsn bar 6, with no tie into 37). That is untested, and I do not recommend it: it would blur the pickup's clean break into the più mosso.

### 4. Bar 49 b2: the 6 over F♯ arrives by leap, not by a 5-6 in one voice (recorded, no change)

PDF 65 (Ex. 4-11b): a 5-6 over a stationary bass is "normally" kept in one voice. At 49 b1 the F♯-minor chord has no fifth (F♯ and A only). The D that makes the 6/3 at b2 comes in the cor anglais quotation, which leaps F♯4-D5. So the 6/3 is a chord over the held bass, not a 5-6 motion. The quotation is protected and its D is the model's own interval. c262 labels the beat "VI6 (5-6 over the held F sharp)". The label is accurate as a figure but not as a voice motion, which is a minor point and does not justify another comment edit. A violas' `r8 cis'8 r8 d'8` at 49 b1-2 would make a literal 5-6, but it would double the quotation's D in octaves and undo c107's same-pitch off-beats. I do not recommend it.
