# Critic 556: playability (brass), Adler ch. 9-10

Version: v556. Lens: playability, instrument by instrument. Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 9 "Introduction to Brass Instruments" and ch. 10 "Individual Brass Instruments" (adler1 PDF 282-329, adler2 PDF 1-13), fourth pass of this reading.

I read every text page in the range in full. I rendered every page at 110 dpi and looked at all the example pages as images (adler1 284-292, 296-297, 301-311, 313-314, 316-320, 322-329; adler2 1-5, 7-12). I read the range and register charts (Ex. X-4, X-5, X-6, X-68 to X-74, X-85, X-86) and the tonguing and dynamic examples (Ex. IX-9, IX-14, X-24) from the image, not from the OCR.

Result: no change. The brass notes of v556 are the same as in v426, the last critic to take this reading (c426). Since then only cues, text and MIDI-only marks have changed: the part-only cues added by c455 and c466, the "3·4: mettere sord." label (c448), and the bass trombone's niente drawn to the barline (c485). I checked every brass bar again against the principles below. All brass writing stays inside what the chapter allows. The places that rely on an exception were recorded and protected by earlier critics, and the reading gives no new reason to reopen them.

## Findings

No finding needs a change. They are ranked by how near each comes to a real playing risk, so the composer can see what was checked and why it holds.

**1. Hn 1 endurance, 53-70 (no change).** Hn 1 plays from 53 b4 to the end of 62, about 38 s, with breaths only at bar ends after 56, 58 and 60. It ends with 8.9 s of tongued ff at written C5-E♭5 (61-62). Its next entry is the p dolce solo at 67 on written B♭4. Timed from global.ily, the rest is 63 (4.4 s) + 64 (5.4 s) + 65-66 (6.9 s) = 16.7 s. Adler asks for "frequent intervals of rest" (adler1 PDF 292) and calls constant high playing taxing (PDF 303). The high part here lasts two bars. The solo lies in the "deep and solid" to "bright" zone of Ex. X-5, and it falls from written B♭4 to F4. Recorded by c286, c336 and c426. No change.

**2. Hn 4 on the top line at 61-62 (no change, protected).** Hn 4 is a low horn by convention (Ex. X-6, PDF 303-304), yet it doubles Hn 3 up to written G♭5 at f. Before that it leaps from written G4 to E♭5 across the breath at 60-61, the widest horn leap in the movement (PDF 304). The note is inside Ex. X-4. The two bars are tongued half and whole notes at f, not a soft attack (PDF 284), and the player has a breath and a Hn 1 cue before them. The doubling has been settled since c086 for weight. The chapter gives no new reason against it.

**3. Unmuted horns at ppp, 1-6 (no change, protected link).** Adler says unmuted brass "cannot be expected to play as softly as the strings" (PDF 292). At bar 1 Hn 3 and Hn 4 are marked ppp with the strings. Hn 3 is tied over from movement I's last chord, and Hn 4 enters on beat 2 on the same written G4, so no separate attack is heard. The strings then fade, and Hn 3·4 continue alone at pp from bar 3, where the chapter's "unique and most effective" unmuted brass pianissimo applies. The passage is part of the do-not-touch link 1-6.

**4. Cold loud entries at 61 (Hn 2, Hn 3) and 63 (Tbn 1) (no change).** Hn 2 enters ff on written A♭4 after 60 bars of rest. Hn 3 enters f on written E♭5 after 30 bars. Tbn 1 enters ff on 63's second eighth after 62 bars. Adler's warning about attacks concerns soft high notes and very low notes (PDF 284). These are loud attacks in the middle register, and each part carries a cue in the bars before: Hn 1 at 57-60 in the Hn 2-4 parts, and B.tbn at 61-62 in the Tbn 1 part, which I checked in the parts book. The hear-and-find rule of PDF 304 is met.

**5. Trombone slide motion and legato at 63-64 (no change, protected quotation).** Against Ex. X-71 (PDF 329), Tbn 1 moves only between neighbouring positions (II-III-IV-I-II-III-I-II) in eighths at Largamente 54. The slurs join notes from different harmonic series, which is the soft-tongued legato of adler2 PDF 3. The "marcato" word tells the player to tongue clearly. The phrase is 9.8 s at ff to p, with slur ends at 63 b4 and 64 b1, so the "not too many notes on one breath" rule (PDF 292) is met. B.tbn D2 and E2 at 64 lie on the F attachment (Ex. X-74).

**6. Tuba and bass trombone at the bottom, 60-62 (no change).** The tuba's A♭1, D♭2, G♭1 and F♭1 lie in the "deep and heavy" zone of Ex. X-86. Adler: low notes "used as slow-moving bass notes … will be effective at any dynamic from ff to pp" (adler2 PDF 8). They are whole and half notes, pp< then f, with breaths after 60 and 61. F♭1 (E1) is within Ex. X-85 and is an easy four-valve fingering at this speed. The tuba plays only these three bars, which suits "frequent rests" (PDF 8).

**7. Mute plan, 82-92 (no change).** "Mettere sord." at 82 leaves about 17 s before "con sord." at 87. The words appear in the score and in both parts (PDF 295). The mute is non-transposing, so the concert pitches stand (PDF 293, 307). "Via sord." at 91 and the scherzo's "senza sord." meet PDF 308. The muted Hn 3·4 notes (written B♭3-E♭4) are held, as PDF 303 asks of low horn notes.

**8. Horn notation in the parts (no change).** Horn parts are in F, treble clef, without key signatures (PDF 297-298, 304). Trombone and tuba parts keep their signatures and the bass clef (top note C4, below the PDF 328 change to tenor clef). Horns and trombones each have their own sub-bracket in the score, and the tuba has its own staff (PDF 298).
