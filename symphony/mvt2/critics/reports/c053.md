## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–55: the build starts on a static bass and moves backwards (V7 → ii7). Use the reading's descending 6/3 bridge (applied)

Problem: 53–60 is the third statement of theme B and the start of the animando build. Its bass is D♭ D♭ | B♭ A♭ | E♭ D♭. Bar 53 holds D♭ for the whole bar, exactly like the first statement at 37. At 54 the A♭7 on b3 moves up a 5th to ii7 at 55, a retrogression. Since c052, bar 38 has the bass D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭ (I–IV6–ii6–ii7), so the build is now harmonically weaker than the exposition it is meant to intensify.

Evidence: A&S PDF 290–291 says a 6/3 series is the contrapuntal bridge from one stable point (here the D♭ of 53) to the next articulated chord (the ii7 at 55 and the vi at 56), and the 6/3s give it a "more fluid, less stable" motion. PDF 297 (Ex. 18-11a) says a falling-thirds descent normally starts with I moving down to VI "connected by a V6". PDF 291 (Ex. 18-3) shows Mendelssohn's I6–(VI6)–(IV6)–II6, a line falling in 3rds filled in by step, ending on the cadential II6. Dvořák does this at the same point in his form: the D♭ pedal of 9/ii m. 7 and 15 gives way to D♭–C–B♭–A♭ at m. 17, where the restated theme rises. The new bass for 53–55 is D♭–C | B♭–G♭ | E♭–D♭ (I–V6 | IV6–ii6 | ii7–I(6)), a descent of almost an octave under the rising crescendo. It then turns into the rising bass of 57–61 (F–G♭–B♭…G♭–G–A♭–D♭), so the build gets contrary motion in its outer voices over eight bars. The melody is unchanged. At 53 b3 its G♭5–F5–E♭5 sits over V6 (the G♭ is a passing 7th). The outer voices are E♭/C → D♭/B♭, two 10ths, which PDF 293–294 allows for a short stretch.

c052's objection was horn 1 and clarinet 1's A♭4 at 54 b3–4, which does not fit ii6. Change it to B♭4. The counter-line then reads A♭–B♭ | D♭. F B♭, theme A's second-phrase head (bars 11, 27, 75: A♭. C F, rising to the 6th) at the D♭ level. Before, it was the first-phrase head. It stays a quotation of theme A and resolves B♭4–G♭4 into 55 in 10ths with the bass. The leading tone C is doubled only in the harp's passing arpeggio. PDF 293 forbids that doubling only when the tonic follows, and here IV6 follows.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-053/work/sections/bSec.ily):
```
% bSecBsnTwo 53-54:  des2\p c2 | bes,2 ges,2 |
% bSecVc 53-54:      des2\p\< c2 | bes,2 ges,2\mp |
% bSecCb 53-54:      des,2\p\< c,2 | bes,,2 ges,,2\mp |
% bSecHarpR 53 b3-4: \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' c' aes es' }
% bSecHarpR 54 b3-4: \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 es' ges' es' bes es' }     (as the violas at 38)
% bSecHnOne 54:      des'4.\< f'8 bes'2 |
% bSecClOne 54:      des'4.\< f'8 bes'2 |
```
This does not undo any earlier fix. It extends c052's accepted change at 38 and closes the open item "V7→ii7 at 54".

### 2. Bars 53→54 and 55→56: the harp arpeggio ends on the bass pitch an octave up, then both move to the next bass note in octaves (applied)

Problem: this has been open since c049 ("arpeggio/bass octaves 53→54, 55→56"). In v53 the harp's last triplet eighth of 53 is D♭4 over the bass D♭3, and its next note is B♭3 over B♭2. At 55→56 it is D♭4/D♭3 → A3/A2. Those are octaves on consecutive attacks, exposed at p in the only moving accompaniment of 53–56.

Evidence: PDF 292–293 says the problem of a passage like this in four parts is parallel octaves. The remedy is to "allow one of the voices to forego parallel motion and to alternate doublings", and the series is treated as three real voices with octave doublings (PDF 292, 302 note). The harp figure is a compound line, and its last note acts as a voice against the bass.

Fix (applied): end 53 b4 on E♭4 (`aes8 c' es' c' aes es'`, finding 1). End 55 b4 on F4: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' f' des' bes f'_\markup \small "A♮" }`. The pitch set and pedals are unchanged, and F4 → A3 leads into the vii°7/vi of 56.
