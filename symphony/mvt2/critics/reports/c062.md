## Findings

### 1. Bars 64 b3–65: the return to A♭ has the augmented-sixth bass but not the augmented sixth (applied)

**Problem.** The most important structural point in the movement is the move from the A-minor quotation (63–64) to the dominant pedal (65–70). In v62 it is bare. At 64 b3 the bass D plays under the trombone's F. At b4 the bass E plays under the trombone's F→E. Then E slips to E♭. In A♭ the bass line D–E–E♭ is ♯4–♭6–5, which is the bass of an augmented-6th approach. But no upper voice ever sounds the ♯4 (D) against the ♭6 (F♭ = E). The ♭2 of the region (A = B𝄫, Vc A3 and Vln I A6 at 63–64) leaps away and does not move down.

**Evidence.** A&S 29 §1 (PDF 503): the augmented 6th "intensifies the following chord in a unique way… composers can use them to signal the beginning or end of a phase in the tonal movement or the form." §17 (PDF 516–517) says the same of return modulations. §24–25 (PDF 524–526, Ex. 29-41 Schubert) shows V7 of ♭II reinterpreted as a German 6th. Here that is literal: E7 is V7 of A minor and is also F♭–A♭–C♭–D, the augmented 6th of A♭. §4 (PDF 505): the Italian form suits a thin texture. §15 (PDF 515): it may go directly to V7, with the 6th contracting to the 7th. Unit 28 Points 3 (PDF 499): ♭2 goes down, often filled in as ♭2–1–7. The fix gives exactly that line, A3 (Vc, 64 b1–2) → G♯3 = A♭ (Va, b3–4) → G3 (Va, 65), in one register.

**Fix (applied).** Violins II and violas enter p on 64 b3 and fade into their existing 65 notes. On b3 this gives D–F–G♯, which is vii° of A minor and also vii°7/V of A♭ (A&S 29 §9, PDF 509–510). On b4 the bass E under G♯ and D gives the Italian 6th F♭–A♭–D. At 65 it resolves: D→D♭ (Vln II, the 7th), G♯→G (Va), E→E♭ (bass).

```lilypond

<e'' c'''>2:32\p d'2(\>^\markup \italic "sul G" |
% bSec.ily, bSecVla, bar 64
<c' e'>2:32\p gis2(\> |
% retr.ily, bar 65: close the slurs
retrVlnTwo = { des'2.)\pp^\markup \italic "unis." | ...
retrVla = { g2.)\pp^\markup \italic "unis., sul C" | ...
```

The trombone quotation, the bass A–D–E–E♭ and the slip into 65 are unchanged. The new notes are p into pp, enter after the quotation's last accent, and do not double it, so "Tbn 1 alone 63–64" and the F♭→E♭ thread (do-not-touch since c013) still hold. The slip now has a reason the ear knows. Voice leading: G♯3/E2 → G3/E♭2 are parallel 10ths, and D4/E2 → D♭4/E♭2 are parallel 7ths by semitone, as in §15. There are no perfect parallels. allpairs output is identical to v62's.

### 2. Bar 49: a Neapolitan 6th under the quotation's tenuto D5 (applied)

**Problem.** The cor anglais' tenuto, swelling D5 (49 b2–3, poco sostenuto) is ♭6̂ of F♯ minor. It falls to C♯5 over V at b4. That is the Neapolitan soprano ♭6–5. Underneath, the harmony is iv7 (B–D–F♯–A), so the expressive peak of the movement-I quotation has a diatonic, weak pre-dominant. Vln II holds F♯5 above the melody through it.

**Evidence.** A&S 28 §2 (PDF 483): ♭II6 is used "at climactic moments, at fermatas, before rhetorical silences", and 4̂ or ♭6̂ may be in the soprano. §6 and Ex. 28-7 (PDF 485–486): ♭II6 comes from IV/iv by a 5–6 motion. §3 and Ex. 28-2a (PDF 484): going straight to V gives the "peculiarly intense" diminished 3rd ♭2–♯7, and doubling the bass is best. §3–4 (PDF 484, 488): the ♭2/♮2 cross relation between voices "does not create a bad effect… you may use it freely". Only ♭2→♮2 in one voice is to be avoided.

**Fix (applied).** On b3, after the D5 has been struck, Vln II moves F♯5→G5 (5–6 over the B bass) and the violas move A3→B3 (doubled bass). Bar 49 then reads i – iv – ♭II6 – V7. Vln II continues G5→E♯5 (diminished 3rd), and the violas B3→G♯3.

```lilypond

fis''2 g''4 eis''4 |
% bSec.ily, bSecVla, bar 49 (was a2. gis4)
a2 b4 gis4 |
```

The quotation's pitches, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. The string move falls on b3, where the cor anglais holds. That keeps c027's aim of not moving with the quotation's rhythm. No voice goes G→G♯. Contrary motion into V: Vln II G5→E♯5 against bass B→C♯.

### 3. Bar 68: optional French-6th neighbour over the dominant pedal (not applied)

**Problem.** In the six-bar E♭ pedal, the horn's tail E♭–D–D♭–C has Bsn 1 a third below (B♭–B♭–A♭). The D♮ (♯4) is a passing tone over V and nothing more.

**Evidence.** A&S 29 §12 (PDF 513): augmented 6ths "often originate in the use of ♭6 as upper neighbour to V… the French 4/3 is particularly well suited… because it contains 2̂ as a common tone with V". Bassoon 2 could take F♭3 at 68 b1–2 → E♭3 (b3) → D♭3 (69, its existing note). That makes 68 b1 E♭(pedal)–F♭–B♭–D, a French 6th as a neighbour of the dominant, and returns the movement's F♭→E♭ thread under the motto's ♯4.

I did not apply this. Finding 1 already sounds F♭→E♭ three bars earlier. The thin 65–70 texture and the horn/bassoon layout are protected (c027, c047), so this is the composer's choice. If taken, check the part-combined slurs at 68.
