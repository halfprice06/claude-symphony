## Findings

### 1. Bars 2–3, double basses: mark "change bow at will" on the tied A♭1 (applied)

**Problem.** The basses enter at bar 2 on A♭1, pp, and hold it tied through bar 3 with a diminuendo to niente: six beats at ♩ = 52, about 7 seconds in one bow. Every other long bass note in the movement carries "change bow at will" (61, 65–70, 83–92), but this one does not. Violin I has the instruction at bar 1. The other lower strings hold the same six beats, but their bows are longer, so they can do it at pp.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." PDF 105: the section, not the single player, gives the bass its body, so staggered changes keep the pedal even while it fades. The c106 finding at 61 (4.4 s, ff) used the same page.

**Fix** (intro.ily, introCb):
```
introCb = { R2. | aes,,2.\pp~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t aes,,2.\> | R2.\! | R2.*2 | }
```
This changes no pitch, rhythm or dynamic of the protected link 1–6. It is a bowing instruction, of the same kind c019 added to Violin I at 1–3. score.midi is byte-identical.

### Checked, no change needed (the new items and the c186/c187 changes)

- **c192 cello divisi at 21 b3–22.** Each half plays one line: upper A♭3 (in unison with the violas) to G3–A♭3–B♭3–G3, lower F♭3 to E♭3. As a double stop, E3/G♯3 in first position would need the E on the G string in 4th position against a stretched finger on the D string, which is the awkward low-register stop Adler PDF 97 warns against. Divisi is the right choice (PDF 76). "div." at 21 and "unis., cantabile" at 23 print in the score and the part. Keep.
- **c194 viola divisi at 87–90.** The lower half plays F3–F♭3–E♭3 in first position on the C string, the upper half A♭3 with a low first finger on the G string. Both halves are easy at pp, and the C string gives the colour PDF 86 describes. The words "lower half sul C" are redundant, because F3, F♭3 and E♭3 lie below the open G and can only be played on the C string (PDF 73: specify a string only when there is a choice). They do no harm and confuse no one, so I leave them. The "div." from 84 stays in force until "unis." at 90 b3, so the chords cannot be read as double stops (ch. 2 PDF 30). Keep.
- **c186 bowings.** Vln II, Vla and Cb at 45 up-bow, so the crescendo at 46 is up-bow and the fp at 47 down-bow. Vln II 49 b3–4 slurred, so 52 b4 is an up-bow pickup with Vln I. Cb down-bow at 75 and second up-bow at 80 b3, so the f at 78 and the ff at 79 are down-bows. I counted all of them again and they work.
- **c187 cor anglais with the violas at 79–82.** The violas play B♭4–A♭4 on the A string, which Adler PDF 87 says "combines beautifully with woodwind instruments". Keep.
- **Solo violin 17–22.** It sits in first position on the E and A strings (A♭5–B♭5 at most) with no open E5. The concertmaster plays it (PDF 81) and has 22 b3–23 to put the mute on (closed, c186). Keep.
- **Ranges.** Vln I E♭4–B♭6, Vln II B♭3–C6, Vla C♯3–C5, Vc D2–F4, Cb E1–F♯2. All are inside Adler's practical orchestral ranges (PDF 72, 85, 96, 104–105). Clefs: cello tenor clef only at 44–47; bass parts within two ledger lines (PDF 96 Ex. III-53, PDF 104).
