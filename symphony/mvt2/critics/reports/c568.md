### 2. Static leads are shaped by dynamics and given an inner motion (checked, follows)

**Reading.** PDF 207 on Ex. 269: a lead of two repeated notes is "somewhat modified ... by the dynamics" but still needs the violas' counter-melody to flow.

**Check.** The four held leads each have both. 60: Fl 1/Vln I C7 whole note under a crescendo, with the viola sextuplets and the cellos' E♭-F-G♭ beneath. 61-62: tenuto ff with the bass moving D♭-G♭-F♭ (protected brass and tutti layout). 79-80: B♭6 held molto sostenuto while the cello counter-melody moves (protected). 89-92: Vln I's C5 at a steady pp under "rit.", with Cl 2's morendo and the moving lower chord (protected close).

**Verdict.** Follows. Keep.

### 3. Push into the next phrase, and leaps covered by rest, diminuendo and colour (checked, follows)

**Reading.** PDF 208, Ex. 273: shorter values "give extra push into the next phrase". PDF 218, Ex. 284: a big leap is acceptable after "the slight rest, the decrease in volume, and ... the obvious change in tone color".

**Check.** Pushes: violin pickups in eighths at 22 b3 and 70 b3; B.cl. quarters at 36; cellos' quarters at 60 and 78 under the crescendos into 61 and 79. Covered leaps: at 63-64 the strings go sf> tremolo to p, then Vln II/Vla re-enter sul G and sul C with a diminuendo and the cellos drop D2-B2-B♭2 inside c428's diminuendo; at 82-83 the orchestra goes p>pp, a beat's rest, then "4 soli" ppp cellos and harp harmonics. **Verdict.** Follows. Keep.

### 4. Tempo words and rubato (checked, follows)

**Check.** Eleven tempo words, each at a phrase end or a section start, each with its `\mt` step in global.ily: poco rit. 35 (49, 45); Poco più mosso 37 (60); poco rit. 47 b3 (57, 55); poco sostenuto 48 (54); a tempo, poco a poco animando 53 (60-64); poco allargando 60 (62, 58); Largamente 61 (54); allargando 64 (48, 42); Tempo I 65 (52); poco allargando 78 (50-44); a tempo 83 (52); rit. 89 (48-20) into the fermata. **Verdict.** Follows. Keep.

### 5. Bars 41-44: Fl 1 shadows the C.a. in rhythm at 42-43 (protected; mitigated; no change)

**Reading.** PDF 211, Ex. 279: violins that move with the soloist in near-constant parallel motion "distort the aural focus". Ex. 280 (PDF 212): the better background moves with the solo only briefly.

**Check.** At 42-43 the descant moves in the C.a.'s rhythm in sixths and tenths, which is Ex. 279's case for two bars. Three things separate it from Ex. 279: it enters half a bar late (41 b3), it moves against the C.a. in contrary motion at 42 b4 and in oblique motion into 44, and since c557 it is a dynamic below the C.a. (Fl pp<p against C.a. p<mp). "Fl 1 notes at 41-44" and the thirds/sixths duet are on the do-not-touch list (c090, c494, c511). An expression-only change (a later swell for the flute) would fall under the c124 rule for 37-60. I have no argument that answers those reasons.

**Verdict.** Recorded. Keep.

### 7. Every entry has a level; hairpins have defined ends (checked, follows)

**Reading.** PDF 212: "concentrate on every bar".

**Check.** I scanned every instrument variable in the seven section files for a note entering after a multi-bar rest without a dynamic in the same bar. The only hits are three harp RH entries (9, 75, 82) on a grand staff whose LH carries the level. The hairpins into rests at 37, 47 and 53 have niente circles since c558. The solo violin's p< at 21 ends at its D5 with no written goal; the E♭5 at 22 then hands over to the tutti violins' mp pickup at 22 b3, so the crescendo's goal is the tutti's mp. That is a hand-over at a matching level, not a missing end.

**Verdict.** Follows. Keep.
