## Findings

All findings are "no change". They are ranked by how much it would matter if the point were wrong.

### 1. The return from the climax takes Beethoven 9/iii's route: IV section, then the ♭II region, then V, then the reprise (61–65). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 593: "the usual goal of ♭VI is a cadential V", and in Widmung "♭VI moves to V7 through ♭II". PDF 591 (Ex. 32-9, Op. 18/2): a short tonicization of ♭II takes the place of the expected tonic, and the tonic "is simply delayed, and not for very long".

**Corpus.** Beethoven 9/iii, B♭ major, has this key plan: B♭ 1–24, D major (III♯) 25–42, B♭ 43–64, G major (VI♯) 65–82, E♭ (IV) Adagio from 83, then C♭ major from the G♭ pickup at the end of 91 to 98. C♭ is ♭VI of E♭ and the ♭II region of B♭. The cellos play C♭ and G♭ (bars 92–94), the flutes' F♭–D♭ become F–D at the end of 98, and the 12/8 variation enters at 99 over the basses' F (V). The last episode before the reprise is therefore in IV, and it reaches V through IV's own ♭VI, which is the Neapolitan region of the tonic. Bars 83–98 also show PDF 594's I–VI♯–IV arpeggio (B♭ 43, G 65, E♭ 83), which this movement does not need (PDF 593: VI♯ needs more preparation than the ♭ side).

**Score.** The middle section is in D♭ (IV). At 62 it goes to F♭ (written E), and at 63–64 to A minor, which is B𝄫 minor: ♭VI of D♭ and the ♭II region of A♭, as Beethoven's C♭ is to E♭ and B♭. The bass then moves by half step E→E♭ into V7 at 65 (PDF 593), and the six-bar dominant pedal leads to A♭ at 71. The plan matches Beethoven's. Two details differ, and both suit the Dvořák 9/ii model better. Beethoven's ♭II region is seven soft bars and his bass inflection rises (F♭–F), while here the region is two bars after the ff climax and the inflection falls (E–E♭), the gentle kind (PDF 585). The quotation at 63–64 and the 61–65 route are both protected.

**Fix.** None. This adds a corpus precedent to the protected 61–65 route.

### 2. The G♭-minor area (47–50) is theme B's 4–3 suspension G♭–F on a large scale (42, 58 → 47–53). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 600: key areas "project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece" (Chopin's D♮–C♯). PDF 602 (K. 542): without its motivic link a remote area "would lack any organic connection with the movement as a whole".

**Score.** Theme B's one suspension is G♭ held over the D♭ chord and resolving to F (42 b3–4 in the cor anglais and flute; 58 b4 in Fl 1, Vln I and Vln II; c061, c151). The B section's remote area is G♭ minor, written F♯ minor (47–50, iv of D♭ by mixture). When the area ends, its tonic becomes the seventh of A♭7 (51) and falls to F: the cor anglais plays A♭4–G♭4 | F4.–G♭4 | F4 (51–53), and both violin sections enter theme B on F5 at 53. So the G♭-minor area returns to D♭ the way the theme's suspension resolves: G♭ falls to F over a D♭-major arrival. This is the reading's Chopin procedure. It gives the area a second link to the movement (besides the quotation), and nothing in HISTORY records it.

**Fix.** None. Recorded so that the cor anglais line at 51–52 is not thinned or reharmonised without keeping G♭–F into 53.

### 3. Abrupt and gentle inflections are placed where the form wants them (1–2, 46–47, 62–63, 64–65). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 585: "Rhythm, texture, and dynamics can all influence the effect of a modulation". A falling inflection (decreasing intensity) is gentler than a rising one. An abrupt one is right when the motive needs it.

**Score.** The movement has four inflection points. 1–2: E♮4→E♭4 in the violas, ppp→pp, while C5 is held: falling and gentle, so the movement opens without a jolt. 46–47: into F♯ minor by C♯7 with "poco rit.", fp> and fewer voices: the quotation is marked but stays soft. 62–63: A♭6 rises to A6 in Fl 1 and Vln I at sf after the ff, the only abrupt, rising inflection, at the movement's turning point and on the long-range A♭6–A6–B♭6 line recorded in HISTORY.md. 64–65: E→E♭ in the bass, p→pp, Tempo I: falling and gentle, so the retransition relaxes. The one rising, abrupt inflection is at the climax, and the falling, gentle ones are at the joins. The reading's typology is used to shape the tension curve.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. The B section's key comes from the motto tail's passing D♭ (31–37). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 587 (Schubert 9/iv, Ex. 32-4a): "a seeming element of melodic figuration ... becomes the basis of a whole section".

**Score.** In theme A's tail E♭–D–D♭–C (8, 24; bass clarinet at 33 in the transition), D♭ is a chromatic passing note (4̂). In 31–36 the woodwind choir holds A♭ and the motto tail is heard again over it. D♭ then arrives at 37 as a new tonic by phrase modulation, without a V7 before it (trans.ily, c020). The arrival is diatonic (IV), so this is the reading's idea on a smaller scale. It is enough to tie the middle key to the opening motive, and it returns at the end in the plagal D♭–C (87–89).

**Fix.** None.

### 5. The chromatic areas' share and placement remain in proportion (45–51, 62–64). No change; c393's measure re-checked against Beethoven 9/iii.

**Reading.** PDF 592 and 603 (Points 5): remote areas "can threaten the continuity and unity".

**Score and corpus.** sections.txt still gives 304 quarter beats. The two remote areas take about 26 of them (9%), both in the middle third, and the climax still begins at beat 204 (67%). In Beethoven 9/iii, III♯ and VI♯ fill 36 of 157 bars, but those are variation episodes in a sectional form at a faster Andante. C♭, the area that matches ours in function, takes about 7 bars before the last variation. For a 92-bar ABA′ Adagio, two short ♭-side areas are the right amount.

**Fix.** None.
