## Findings

Since c308, the expression marking has passed the MOLA checks, and the changes made after it pass as well: the coda mutes (c468, c470, c485), the 64 diminuendi (c428), the 74 mp (c438) and the basses at 56 (c527). The one defect I found is in a part.

### 1. Viola part, 83–84: the mute instructions run together, and "con sord." touches the ppp (APPLIED, part only)

- **Bars:** 83 (part-only "mettere sord." under the whole-bar rest) and 84 b3 (ppp, with "con sord., / div., change bow at will" below it).
- **Problem:** in the extracted viola part, "mettere sord." and "con sord.," sit on one baseline less than a quarter stave-space apart. The line reads "mettere sord. con sord., div., change bow at will" as one instruction, and the top of "con sord." touches the ppp above it. The player must put the mute on at 83 and play muted at 84. Those are two separate actions and should read as two separate marks. The score is not affected, because there the words are above the staff and the bars are wider.
- **Evidence:** MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7 (PDF 12): marks that are correct in the score collide in the extracted part, so the part must be proofread on its own. MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 5 (PDF 10): the change is marked where the player stops, and the new state is named at the entry, as two distinct marks. Gould PDF 55: at least ½ stave-space between characters, and no collisions.
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-528/work/sections/coda.ily, codaVla, part tags only):**
  - `R2.-\tag #'part -\tweak extra-offset #'(-2.5 . 0) _\markup \italic "mettere sord."`
  - `-\tag #'part -\tweak padding #1.0 _\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "con sord.," "div., change bow at will" }`
  - Checked at 250 dpi: the two instructions now stand about two stave-spaces apart, "con sord." is clear of the ppp, and the viola part is still one page.

### 2. Recorded: the bass line at 56 is marked at two levels (no change)

The basses re-enter p< under the cellos' mf on the same notes an octave lower (c527). Under PDF 8's vertical proofreading this is a spread on a doubled line. It is a deliberate step: the basses come in under the cellos and reach mf with them at 57, and c527 states the reason. The same applies to Ob 1 p over Vc pp at 44–46 (c124: the oboe's octave leads). Keep both.

### 3. Recorded: Cl 1·2 re-entry at 60 without a dynamic (optional, not applied)

After the half-bar rest at 59 b3–4, the clarinets come back at 60 with only a crescendo hairpin. Ex. 3 restates the dynamic after rests, but its rest is three bars long. Here the rest is a breath inside an f phrase that started at 59, and c308 recorded it as an exception. If a later engraving pass wants it, a part-only `-\tag #'part \f` on the 60 whole note in both clarinet parts would satisfy Ex. 3 without changing the score.

### 4. Recorded: harp p – pp – p at 82–83 (no change)

The harp has the LH chord p at 82, the pickup E♭4–F4 pp, and the harmonics p at 83. On the page this looks like a jump, but c266 and c298 give the reason: harmonics sound much softer than plucked notes at the same marking, so p at 83 sounds at about the pickup's pp level. Keep it.

### 5. Recorded: tempo words and set-up changes across all parts (no change)

Every tempo word reaches every part, including parts resting through 47–49, 53–56, 60–64, 78 and 89–92. Every mute, stick and muta change is printed where the player stops and again at the entry. This includes the coda mutes added after c308: violins and violas at 82, 85 (Vla), 87 (Vln II) and 89 (Vln I), "via sord." at 92, and Hn 3·4 at 82, 87 and 91. This follows Ex. 4–5.
