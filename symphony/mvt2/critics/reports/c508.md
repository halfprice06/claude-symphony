## Findings

### 1. Cellos 60: a four-beat f→ff crescendo on one up-bow, while the basses split it (applied)

**Bars.** bSec.ily, bSecVc 59–60: `ges2(\f es4 fes4) | es4(\upbow\< f4 ges2) |`.

**Problem.** Bar 60 is two beats at ♩=62 and two at ♩=58 (global.ily `\mt 62 s2 \mt 58 s2`), 4.0 s. The cellos play it in one up-bow, crescendo from f to ff, and double horn 1's counter-line into the climax at 61. The basses play the same bar in two bows (`aes,,2~\downbow\< aes,,2\upbow`, c096) for the same reason. The cellos are the only low string still carrying the whole crescendo on one bow. c398 kept the bar as one bow because Vln I/II play four beats at f in 59. That comparison does not hold for 60: 59 is a held f at ♩=64 (3.75 s) on the longer violin bow, while 60 is longer, grows to ff, and is played on the shorter cello bow.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39: "Even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow ... Remember also that the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter." PDF 40: the Liszt passage of Ex. II-23, cellos and basses with a crescendo under long slurs, "is impossible to perform as the composer has marked while still adhering to the crescendo marks, unless it is broken up into several bows" (Ex. II-24). PDF 38: forte takes fewer notes per bow, and a crescendo is natural on the up-bow.

**Fix (applied).** Four bows in 59–60. The count is natural, so no bow marks are needed, and the old `\upbow` comes off:
```
% bSec.ily, bSecVc bars 59-60 (was: ges2(\f es4 fes4) | es4(\upbow\< f4 ges2) |)
ges2(\f es4) fes4 | es4(\< f4) ges2 |
```
Count: 58 ends with the up-bow on the tie (c398). 59 b1–3 down (the f stays at the frog with the rest of the strings), 59 b4 up, 60 b1–2 down, 60 b3–4 up (crescendo), 61 ff down. This keeps c085's up-bow crescendo into the ff down-bow and c398's down-bow f at 59, and it has no two up-bows in a row (c096). The cellos now change bow at 60 b3, where the basses do. Pitches, ties, hairpins and dynamics are unchanged. The bar is not protected: the do-not-touch item is the down-bow arrival at 61, and that is kept.

### 2. Violins 60: c'''1 one up-bow crescendo (recorded, no change)

Vln I/II hold `c'''1\upbow\<` / `c''1\upbow\<` for the same 4.0 s. c186 recorded this, and I agree. The violin bow is longer, and the crescendo runs from tip to frog, where Adler says the stroke gains weight (PDF 38). Splitting it would need a retake or a second bow in 59, which carries the f arrival with the winds. Fl 1 and Ob 1 hold the same C6 and cover the sound. No change.

### 3. Vln I coda 89–92: marked bow changes together on the last held C5 (recorded, no change)

Every other string section in 89–92 has "change bow at will", the staggered bowing of Ex. II-25 (PDF 40). Vln I is marked V ⊓ V ⊓, so the whole section changes bow together at 90, 91 and 92 on the top voice at pp–ppp. I considered asking for staggered changes here too. I do not, because the marked plan is master practice: in the Dvořák Largo the first violins change bow together on each tie of a held pp note (strings.notesL.ily 17–20). The plan also follows Adler PDF 38: the line enters up-bow at the tip and dies away on a down-bow. The bowing has been protected since c016/c018.

### 4. Tremolo at 61–64 (follows)

`:32` on half and whole notes at ♩=54 could be read as measured thirty-seconds, which is the "shorthand" of Ex. II-47 (PDF 50). The printed "trem." / "non div., trem." makes it the unmeasured tremolo of PDF 49, and sf> on the tremolo at 63 is idiomatic. The notes without slashes at 64 b3 need no "ord.", because tremolo is shown by the slashes, not by a change of playing position (PDF 53 lists contact-point and wood effects). No change.

### 5. Short crescendos on down-bows at 25, 52 and 54 (recorded, no change)

The count puts three short crescendos on down-bows: Vc 24 b3–25 b3 (`g4( | aes2\< c'4)`, mp at most), Vln II 52 b1–3 (c258, c398) and Vln I/II 54 (p→mp continuing 53's up-bow crescendo). Each is at most three or four beats at p–mp, and none ends in an arrival that needs the frog. Adler PDF 38 asks for direction marks only "where he wishes to counteract the normal habit", and here the habit is easy to counter. Changing any of them would disturb the protected bowing of 23–30 or the 52 b4 pickup shared with Vln I.

### 6. Pizz./arco timing (follows)

Cb 62→63 goes from arco to pizz. with no rest. The half note before it is marked up-bow (`fes,,2--\upbow`), which is Adler's remedy (PDF 54: an up-bow "in order to arrive at the frog"). The pizz. is a single sf note, and the return to arco at 64 has three beats at ♩=54. Vla 40 b3 has the up-bow before its pizz. at 41 and two beats' rest before arco at 45. Cb 37 starts pizz. after rests. "pizz." and "arco" print in the score and in the Viola and Double bass parts. No change.
