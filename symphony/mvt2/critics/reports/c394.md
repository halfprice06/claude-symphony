## Findings

### 1. Bars 80 b3–81 b1: flute 2 leaves at the F♭, so the harmony part to be made prominent loses its octave and the A♭5–A♭6 octave is empty at ff (applied)

**Problem.** At 79–80 b2 the inner F is doubled the same way the whole time: Fl 2 F6, Ob 2 F5, Hn 3 F4 (with Cl 2 F4 on 79 b1). F6 is the only note between the melody's two upper octaves (B♭5–B♭6, then A♭5–A♭6). At 80 b3 the line turns to F♭ (c042's 6–♭6–5; c234 gave Ob 2 the F♭5 so the woodwind choir would have it). On that beat flute 2 stops. The F♭ goes on in Ob 2 F♭5, Hn 3 F♭4 and the lower violas, but without its upper octave. Nothing sounds between Ob 1/Cl 1/Vln II A♭5 and Fl 1/Vln I A♭6, still at ff (the diminuendo starts at 81). At 81 b1 the same octave, G5–G6, is again empty under the V♭9. The doubling changes on the one beat where the passage's colour note arrives, and the change goes the wrong way: the note that should stand out loses a doubling.

**Evidence.** PDF 90 rule 1, with its "To be avoided" example: chord instruments "must be doubled or not throughout, except when one of the harmonic parts is to be made prominent". PDF 96 rem. 1: no void in the intermediate parts of close wind harmony, "a bad effect especially in forte passages". PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages". PDF 82's "duplication of 1 part" allows an octave void only when the top part alone is doubled. At 79–80 b2 an inner part was doubled as well, so 80 b3 is a change of method, not that case. The same reasoning lies behind c014 (Fl 2 and Ob 2 double the upper harmony against four horns) and c234 (F♭ in the wind choir).

**Fix (applied, sections/aTwo.ily, `aTwoFluteTwo`):**
```
aTwoFluteTwo = { R2.*8 | f'''2.~\ff | f'''2 fes'''4 \breathe | fes'''4-\tag #'part \> r2-\tag #'part \! | R2. | }
```
Before: `f'''2 r4 | R2.*2`. Flute 2 now plays exactly what oboe 2 plays, an octave higher: F6–F♭6, struck again after the shared breath, and released at 81 b2 with oboe 2. F♭6 (E6) is easy and strong on the flute. Against flute 1 it makes a major third at 80 b3 (a concord in one colour, rule 4) and sounds as a minor third at 81 b1. The F♭ resolves to E♭ in its real part (Hn 3, violas) at 81 b2, as before. Its octaves with Ob 2, Hn 3 and the violas are duplications of one part (PDF 83). The outer-voice scan is still 0. Because flute 2 now sounds at 81 b1, the part-combiner gives flute 1 up-stems, which broke the c385 beam positions (four "weird stem size" warnings). I kept flute 1's down-stems explicitly for 81 b2–3, the same way 78 b3 does it (`\temporary \override Stem.direction = #DOWN ... \revert Stem.direction`). This does not undo any earlier fix. The A′ melody doublings and c234's oboe 2 notes are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 81: the shared diminuendo on the flute and oboe staves stopped at beat 2 (applied with Finding 1; engraving)

**Problem.** On a shared staff, the second player's `\> r2\!` ended the staff's single hairpin at 81 b2. This was already the case on the oboe staff since c234: oboe 1's diminuendo from 81 to the p at 82 printed as a one-beat hairpin. Finding 1 would have done the same to the flute staff, whose hairpin ran the whole bar in v394.

**Fix (applied, `aTwoFluteTwo` and `aTwoOboeTwo`):** `fes'''4-\tag #'part \> r2-\tag #'part \!` and `fes''4-\tag #'part \> r2-\tag #'part \!`. In the score, flute 1's and oboe 1's hairpins now run the whole bar to the p at 82 (checked on page 7 at 250 dpi). The flute 2 and oboe 2 parts keep their own hairpin on the single F♭. The diminuendo starts on the same beat for both players, so the two players' dynamics still agree. No warnings. Only score printing changes; the MIDI is identical apart from Finding 1's notes. If the composer prefers not to touch oboe 2, the flute change alone also works, but then the two staves differ.
