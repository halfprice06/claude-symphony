Unit 5 covers doubling, spacing, motion, parallels, crossing and overlap. It does not teach the resolution of the leading tone and the seventh as such (that is Unit 6 onward), but it gives the reasons for both: the leading tone has an "active tendency toward 1̂" and the seventh "forms a dissonance" (PDF 76). I checked their resolution in the chordal passages on that basis.

## Findings

The music has not changed since v180, and the four-part procedures in the requested passages are sound. None of the findings below needs a change. I list them because each one looks like a breach of a Unit 5 rule in a reduction, and the composer should know why it stays.

### 1. Bars 62 b3–63 b1: the leading tone G♯ (written A♭) of A minor sounds in four octaves; only the top voice resolves. No change

**What the scan shows.** The F♭ chord at 62 b3 is E major, V of the A minor at 63. Its third sounds as A♭3 (Vla lower note), A♭4 (Ob 2, Hn 2), A♭5 (Cl 1) and A♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I upper). At 63 b1 the winds stop, Fl 1 and the upper Vln I rise G♯6→A6, and the violas' double stop A♭3/F♭4 becomes E4/C5, so their G♯3 does not go to A. A&S call the leading-tone rule "virtually absolute" (PDF 76, Ex. 5-4).

**Why it stays.** This is a ff tutti in a free texture: the inner G♯s are octave doublings of the top voice's G♯, which A&S treat as doublings for "emphasis" rather than as separate voices (PDF 84). The leading tone resolves in the soprano, the most exposed voice (PDF 80). Dvořák's Largo doubles the leading tone of its tutti dominants the same way (corpus, bars 25–26 and 94–95). The only way to give the violas a real G♯3→A3 is `<a e'>1:32` at 63, and that puts a viola A3 inside trombone 1's quotation (E3–C4–B3–B♭3–A3, in unison with its A3 at 64). That is the reason c074 moved the cellos from A3 to A2. 61–65 is protected (c180). I tested nothing here because the alternative breaks a stronger rule.

### 2. Bars 21 b3–22 b2: a 10th, then a 12th, between solo violin and Vln II. No change

**What the scan shows.** Vln I (the solo player at this point) has D5→E♭5 over Vln II B♭3, with nothing between, for five beats at the half cadence. A&S say a gap wider than an octave between soprano and alto sounds thin, "especially if continued beyond one or two chords" (PDF 77, Ex. 5-6a).

**Why it stays.** It lasts two chords (the French sixth on F♭ and V), which is the limit A&S allow for spacing driven by the lines (PDF 79, Ex. 5-9), and the top voice is a solo instrument over a muted accompaniment, the case where A&S call spacing an expressive resource (PDF 79, Ex. 5-10). Vln II has to leave F4 when the bass moves to F♭ (c170). I checked the ways to fill the gap. D4 doubles the D5, which is the leading tone of V/V (PDF 76). A♭4 makes octaves A♭→G with the violas' A♭3 and the cellos' G3 at 22. Either one is worse than the gap.

### 3. Bar 87: the IV6 of the plagal close has its third F four times and the root D♭ only in clarinet 1. No change; nothing to report to the scherzo

**What the scan shows.** At 87 the notes are F1, F2 (Cb, Vc), F3 (Vla, Hn 4), A♭3 (Hn 3), F4 (C.a.), A♭4 (Vln II), D♭5 (Cl 1). A&S prefer to double the root and treat other doublings as exceptions (PDF 76).

**Why it stays.** A&S allow the exception "to bring about smoother voice leading" (PDF 76), and that is the reason here: the three upper Fs are the protected F–F♭–E♭ line (87–89) in C.a., violas and horn 4, and the bass F goes D♭–A♭ under it. Each F leaves by step. A D♭ added in the middle would either double Cl 1's D♭5–C5 in octaves or cross horns 3 and 4 on their shared staff. The ending (83–92) is unchanged, so nothing needs to go to the scherzo thread.

### 4. Bars 64→65: cellos leap E2→B♭2 (a diminished 5th) into the E♭7. No change

**What the scan shows.** The cellos leave the basses' E2 and take the fifth of the E♭7 while the basses slip E→E♭. A&S keep dissonant leaps out of the simplest styles (PDF 82).

**Why it stays.** The same page says the dominant seventh is the chord that makes dissonant leaps "logical and attractive" (PDF 82). The leap goes to a member of V7, at pp, and the B♭ is then held for two bars. It makes the V7 at 65 complete and in open position (B♭2–G3–D♭4 over E♭2). Doubling the root on E♭2 instead would leave out the 5th, which is allowed, but the chord would be thinner. The retransition is protected (c093).

### 5. Bars 27–28 and 75–76 (outside the requested passages, same rule): a 12th between the theme and the violas under the B♭ appoggiatura. No change

The violins hold B♭5 (28) or B♭5/B♭6 (76) over the violas' F4 for one beat, then A♭ over F4 (a 10th). The gap follows from keeping the resolution tone A♭ out of the inner voices while the appoggiatura sounds (c082, c123). It lasts two chords, so it stays within PDF 79.

### 6. Bar 62 b1: the seventh F of G♭maj7 sounds in three octaves, with horn 2's G♭4 a semitone above F4. No change

All three Fs belong to lines that move together to F♭ at 62 b3 (Fl 2/Vln I, Ob 1/Cl 1/Vln II, Bsn 1/Vc). They are octave doublings of one resolving voice (PDF 84), not three sevenths. The seventh resolves down by step in every octave. Horn 2's G♭4 is the root against them, the split of the major seventh that c014 chose. 61–62 is protected.
