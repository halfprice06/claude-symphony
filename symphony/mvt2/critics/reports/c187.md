## Findings

### 1. Bars 79–82: the theme's lowest octave is the violas alone (applied)

**Problem.** At the second ff peak theme A is in three octaves. The upper octave (B♭6) is Fl 1 + Vln I and the middle octave (B♭5) is Ob 1 + Cl 1 + Vln II. The lowest octave (B♭4–A♭4–G4–E♭4) is the violas with no wind. Around it at f/ff are Hn 3 F4, Hn 4 A♭3, Cl 2 D♭5, Ob 2 F5 and the Hn 1·2 + Bsn 1 + Vc counter-melody, which rises to D♭4. One string section is the only voice of its octave among sustained horns and winds. That is the case the reading says is hard to balance. c071 added the third octave to make the peak fuller, but with the violas alone it adds little.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 76: "When it is desired to distribute the melody over three or four octaves, it is difficult to achieve perfect balance of tone". Both of his examples give every octave a combined group. PDF 78 lists *Sheherazade* iii [M] as a model three-octave melody: Vns I + Fl / Vns II + Ob / 'Cellos + Eng. horn. That is this score's layout, except that here the lowest octave has no wind. PDF 76–77: Violas + Eng. horn is one of the "best and most natural" unisons by register (examples No. 82–83, *Sadko*; *Sheherazade* iii [H], Violas + Ob + Eng. horn), and its purpose is "to strengthen the resonance of the strings". In Ex. 87 (PDF 261, *Kashchei* [105], ff), the lowest octave of the tune has the most winds (C.a., 2 Cl, Fag), not the fewest. The cor anglais is free: it has rested since 53. B♭4 (written F5) is the top of its full register, so it can play ff. Its staff is already printed in the 79–82 system's layout and the score stays at 8 pages.

**Fix (sections/aTwo.ily, new variable; the assembler had filled it with rests):**
```
aTwoCorAnglais = {
  R2.*8 |
  bes'2.~(\ff | bes'4 aes'2) \breathe | g'4(\> es'2 | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t es'2)\p\> r4\! |
}
```
It follows the violas note for note. It leaves the theme with them at 81 b2 (c074: E♭4 held, clear of the counter-melody). It releases with them on 82 b3 (c063: clear of the harp's E♭4–F4 pickup). It breathes after 80 with Ob 1, Cl 1 and Fl 1. The middle octave keeps its two winds, so c034's balance (the heaviest-scored octave heard as the theme) still holds. The cor anglais is weaker than Ob 1 + Cl 1. Nothing else changes. This does not touch the ending (83–92), the exported themes or the quotations.

**Protection.** "A′ 71–82", "double octaves 79–82" and "re-scored return 71–82" protect the octave layout, the middle octave's weight and the staged release. The fix keeps all three: it adds one wind to an existing octave, with the same notes, slurs, release and breath.

### 2. Bars 57 b3–60: horn 1's counter-line is an octave above the cellos and bassoon 1 (no change)

Ex. 90 (PDF 265, *Sheherazade* iv [U]) puts the horns in unison with the cellos and the clarinets an octave above. PDF 79 says the horn + cello blend works in unison. Here Hn 1 (G♭4–F4–E♭4…) is an octave above Vc + Bsn 1 (from 59). c087 raised the PDF 79 point and kept it. The example gives the layout but no new argument. There are two ways to get a unison. Taking horn 1 down an octave puts it at G♭3–E♭3 at f, where it would be weaker than now. Taking the cellos up an octave makes them leap from G♭4 down to A♭3 at 61, where they carry the augmented head's lowest octave. Both change the protected 53–61 build and the cello crossing at 61–62. The present octave works under the rules the reading gives: the upper octave (Hn 1) is a single brass line over a string + wind unison (Vc + Bsn 1, PDF 76–77). Recorded, not applied.

### 3. Bars 23–30 and 71–74: the cello counter-melody alone (no change)

PDF 78 says a sweet low melody should be Vc + Fag. The counter-melody at 23–30 is the protected "plain statement" (c157, c167), and 71–74 match it on purpose. Bsn 1 + Hn 2 join at 75, as the reading recommends for the louder continuation. Recorded, not applied.
