## Holistic comparison with Saint-Saëns, Poco adagio

What Saint-Saëns does (score PDF 57–72):
1. Theme (PDF 57–58): violins I and II, violas and cellos all on the tune in unison and octaves, arco pp, over pp organ chords. Basses rest. One colour for the whole first period.
2. Second strain (letter Q, PDF 58–60): strings divided on sustained chords; a soft p chorale in Cl 1, Hn 3·4 and Tbn 1·2 answers them in thirds. The brass appear only at p.
3. Reprise (S, PDF 61–63): before it, one pp wind chord with a <> swell. The theme then returns in the violins as a variation, with sixteenth-note figuration worked into the line; violas and cellos take the figuration a few bars later. Melody and accompaniment change together.
4. Middle episode (U, PDF 63–66): the basses play a pizzicato triplet walking figure, "molto tranquillo", under sparse wind chords. Pizzicato strings add motion without weight.
5. Peak (PDF 68–69): one swell to f, "sempre f", in the strings and organ only. No ff and no brass at the peak.
6. Last statement (X, PDF 70): the tune goes to the woodwinds pp (Fl 1, Cl, Bsn in octaves) over repeated-note triplets in Vln II and the violas. Bizet's "motion in a sustained chord" (Adler PDF 213) is made into a whole texture.
7. Close (PDF 72): the violas and cellos play short espressivo fragments over pp chords; Vln I and Vc make one last rising arpeggio with cresc.–dim.; the basses pluck the final chord pp, and the organ is marked "morendo".

Against that model, this movement is credible as a master's work, and the reading's techniques are applied throughout (39 of 39 principles followed or not applicable). The A section has the same kind of block design Saint-Saëns uses: one colour per eight-bar period (horn 7–14, oboe and solo violin 15–22, strings 23–30). The B section works the way his Q passage does: soft winds answer strings, and the trombones are kept for one place. The retransition 65–70 uses the device of his close (letter 7 below): a pedal, a soft roll and one solo voice. The movement departs from the model in three ways. Each departure comes from the plan or the cyclic rules, not from a weakness in craft:
- The plan's model is Dvořák 9/ii, so there are two ff peaks with brass (61, 79). Saint-Saëns has one f peak with strings and organ.
- Theme A comes back almost literally at 71 (re-scored, as Adler PDF 209/213 recommends, with the line extended at 79–81). Saint-Saëns varies the melody itself at his reprise.
- The last four bars (89–92) hold one chord without motion. Saint-Saëns, Bizet (Adler PDF 213) and Ravel (PDF 224) all keep a small motion or a new colour going inside their final soft chord.

A conductor reading this score would take the orchestration for professional work. If anything shows a less experienced hand, it is the number of colour changes in 41–52 (finding 1). None of the three departures is a fault, so I recommend no change for them.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–52: the theme B melody changes colour five times in twelve bars. Recorded, no change.
- What: Fl 1 (37–40), then CA (41–44), Ob 1 with Vc and Bsn 1 (45–46), CA again for the quotation (47–50), Vln II held E♭5 (51–52), and the violins (53). Each change falls on a phrase joint (Adler PDF 228: never break up a phrase needlessly), and each new solo enters on a lighter texture (PDF 228). Adler still warns that too many colours can confuse the form (PDF 203), and his Brahms example keeps one combination for 24 bars (PDF 208). Saint-Saëns keeps his theme in one string colour for its first sixteen bars.
- Why no change: the quotation at 47–50 must sit in a distinct colour (cyclic rule), and the theme-B colour plan 37–60 is on the do-not-touch list. The cor anglais is the one colour used twice, with an oboe bar in between. That double use is the only point I would revisit if the plan ever opens again (fallback: theme B's B♭-minor head at 45–46 in Vc and Bsn 1 alone, oboe resting, so the CA's quotation at 47 is the only double-reed entry in 45–50). I did not test this because it touches protected bars.

### 2. Bars 71–82: the reprise re-scores theme A rather than varying it. Recorded, no change.
- What: Saint-Saëns's reprise (letter S) varies the melody with figuration. Here 71–78 repeats 23–30's line note for note, and only the scoring changes: octaves, the viola eighths, horn 2 and bassoon 1 on the counter-melody. The line is extended at 79–81.
- Evidence it is right as it stands: Adler PDF 209 (Dvořák re-scores the repeat "without changing the basic colour") and PDF 213 (Bizet's repeat is "fuller ... melody doubled at pitch"). Both describe exactly what 71–82 does. Theme A's line is protected (c012, c171).

### 3. Bars 89–92: the final chord is static. Recorded, no change.
- What: after the clarinet's D♭–C (87–89), every part holds the tonic chord for four bars under a rit. to ♩=20. Only Cl 2's morendo and the hairpins change. Bizet adds a timpani roll and a cello figure to a ppp chord (Adler PDF 213, Ex. XVI-11 m. 28). Saint-Saëns closes with a rising arpeggio and a pizzicato last chord (score PDF 72).
- Why no change: the opening's held strings return (Adler PDF 224, Ravel's last five bars), which this score already does, and Dvořák 9/ii also ends on a held chord. The coda 85–92 is protected (c169, c177). If motion were ever wanted, the least intrusive version is `codaCb` bar 92 `aes,,4\ppp^\markup \italic "pizz." r2\fermata`, the Saint-Saëns ending. I did not apply it.

### 4. Bars 61–62: the largest doubling is on the head's lower octaves, with Fl 1 and Vln I holding above it. Recorded, no change.
- What: the augmented head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ is in Fl 2, Vln II, Vc, Hn 3·4 (A♭ only) and Bsn 1 (second half). Fl 1 holds A♭6 and Vln I holds D♭6 at the top (Adler PDF 209: the melodic figure gets the most doubling).
- Why no change: the held top pitches were chosen at c051 and c059 to put the registral peak on the held climax. The head is still the most doubled line in the bar. 61–65 is protected.

### 5. Bars 36–37: the cor anglais pickup joins flute 1's first note in unison. Recorded, no change.
- Adler PDF 217 passes a melody "without overlap and a fresh articulation". Here the CA's C5–F5 lands on the flute's F5. It is a one-note dovetail (PDF 213, 224), and the flute pickup was closed at c072 to keep the 8-page layout.
