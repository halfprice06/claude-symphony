## Holistic comparison with Dvořák, Cello Concerto ii

The two movements are built the same way. Each opens with a wind choir and keeps strings out at first (Dvořák mm. 1–8; here 3–14). Each has a darker middle section that quotes music from outside it once (Dvořák's song; here movement I's two themes at 47–51 and 63–64). Each brings the main theme back after a horn passage (Dvořák's horn trio; here Hn 1's tail at 67–70, then the strings at 71). Each closes softly.

In the accompaniment technique Adler takes from this repertoire, the score is at a master's level. Theme B changes colour at every statement. Pizzicato strings answer a sustained solo. Off-beat strings sit under the quotation. The texture is cut back after each tutti. The timpani link phrases in the coda. A conductor would accept this as professional work.

Dvořák's opening woodwind choir does something the score does less well. His solo enters in a colour nobody has heard yet. Here theme A's horn enters out of a horn chord (finding 3). Dvořák also accompanies his soloist mostly from below and in other registers, and the score departs from this at two small points (findings 1 and 2).

Compared with v209, nothing got worse. The changes since then are: Vc ff at 79, Hn 2 p<mp at 75–77, low brass f at 61, Tbn 1's last note p at 64, B.cl. p<mp>p at 31–33, Vln II A5 at 49, Vc B2 at 64, harp RH at 53, timpani damp at 53, harp A♭1 tied 82–83, and score-only bracket and mark changes. All hold up. The only one that comes near to undoing a texture rule is Vc ff at 79 (finding 4).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 9 and 11: bassoon 1 sounds horn 1's note in unison while the horn plays it (applied)

**Problem.** At 9 bassoon 1 holds A♭3 for the whole bar, and the harp RH strikes A♭3 on beat 2. The horn's phrase (C4 – B♭3 – A♭3) arrives on that A♭3 on beat 3, a note that is already sounding in the same octave. At 11 b2 bassoon 1 re-strikes A♭3 while the horn is still holding its A♭3 (`aes4.`), so a second attack lands inside the solo's sustained note.

**Evidence.** Beethoven puts the answering strings in pizzicato to avoid duplicating the solo sound (PDF 257). Mozart keeps the solo's colour and notes out of the accompaniment (PDF 264, 267). The simple accompanying figure should keep its distance from the soloist (PDF 274). c057 removed the harp's notes on the horn's pitches at 7–13 for the same reason, but missed the bassoons and 9 b2.

**Fix (applied; pitches only, rhythm and texture of horn over bassoons unchanged).**
- aOneBsnOne: `| aes2.) |` at 9 → `| aes2 f4) |`; 11 `es4( aes4-- aes4--` → `es2( aes4--`.
- aOneBsnTwo: 9 `c2 f4` → `c2 des4`; 11 `c4( es4 f4` → `c2( f4`.
- aOneHarpR: 9 `r4 <es aes>4\arpeggio f4` → `r4 <c es>4\arpeggio f4`.

At 9 b3 the IV chord is now D♭2 (B.cl.), D♭3, F3 (bassoons), F3 (harp) and A♭3 (horn alone). At 11 b2 the I6 is C3 (B.cl. and Bsn 2), E♭3 (Bsn 1) and A♭3 in the horn. The bassoons move in contrary motion or in thirds, and there are no new parallels. The descending-fifths chain at 8 is not touched.

### 2. Bars 41–44: viola pizzicato triplets at the cor anglais' own dynamic, inside its register (applied)

**Problem.** The cor anglais plays theme B's second phrase in its low register (A♭3–B♭4). The violas' pizzicato triplets cover A♭3–G♭4, twelve attacks a bar, and carry the same swell, p < mp > p. At 42 the violas reach mp together with the solo and strike its F4 and G♭4.

**Evidence.** Keep the accompaniment registrally distant from a woodwind soloist; rhythmic contrast only reduces this need (PDF 274). The lowest part of a range carries least and must be lightly accompanied (PDF 275). When the soloist is in a weak register, the orchestra is reduced (PDF 267, item 3). c057 already chose pizzicato for this passage. The violas cannot move down an octave (below C3), so the remaining step is dynamic. c038's reason, sharing the swell, still holds if the swell keeps its shape one level lower, as the cellos' pp < p > pp already does.

**Fix (applied, bSecVla).** 41 `aes8\p\<` → `aes8\pp\<`; 42 `aes8\mp\>` → `aes8\p\>`; 44 `ges8\p` → `ges8\pp`.

### 3. Bars 3–7: the horn solo enters out of a horn chord (no change)

**Problem.** Dvořák and Tchaikovsky open with the woodwind choir so that the soloist's colour is fresh (PDF 263–264). Here horns 3–4 hold the chord at 3–6, and Hn 1's pickup enters on 6 b3, the beat on which they release. In Dvořák's opening, by contrast, nothing of the solo's colour is heard before it enters.

**Why no change.** Mozart allows the orchestral horns in the introduction before the solo (PDF 264). Horn 3's C4 is the held common tone from movement I (link 1–6, protected for the cyclic join, c030, c140). Its C–D♭–C at 5 previews the plagal close. I record the point and do not propose a change.

### 4. Bar 79: Vc ff on the counter-melody (no change; compared with v209)

c217 raised the cellos from f to ff to match Bsn 1, which shares a staff with Bsn 2's ff bass. Under Adler's foreground and background grading (PDF 261–262) the counter-melody is background to the theme. That theme is ff in three octaves, each a string group plus a wind, and it holds for a full bar, while the counter-melody moves in quarters. The risk is that the moving line competes with the held B♭6. With horns 1–2 kept at f, the counter-melody is still one string section plus one wind against three such groups. I do not count it as worse than v209. If a later lens hears the counter-melody on top, the fix is cellos f with Bsn 1 split from Bsn 2 at 79–81 (`\partCombineApart`). That would avoid the shared-staff dynamic rule c217 met.

### 5. Bars 71–74: the theme in octaves from the start of A′ (no change)

Bizet saves the first violins' higher octave for the singer's climax (PDF 279). This score follows that rule in the B section (unison 53–56, octaves from 57), but A′ has Vln I in the upper octave from 71. The A′ texture 71–82 is on HISTORY.md's do-not-touch list ("A′ texture 71–82") as the intensified reprise, and the octave at 71 is what sets A′ apart from the plain statement at 23. Recorded, no change.

### 6. Bars 48–51: Vln II above the cor anglais quotation (no change)

The pp Vln II line (F♯5–A5–G5–E♯5–F♯5–D5) lies above the cor anglais' quotation (C♯4–D5), which goes against the usual placement of the accompaniment below the solo (PDF 274, Verdi Ex. XVII-19). It is rhythmically apart from the solo (half notes against the quotation's syncopation, PDF 279), and the quotation is p–mp over pp. c202's A5 at 49 gives the tonic its third. Recorded, no change.
