## Findings

### 1. Bar 64 beat 4: exposed fifths from the German sixth into the E♭7 (applied in the scratch copy)

**Problem.** c210 completed the German sixth at 64 b4: E2 (Cb, B.tbn), B2 (Vc), G♯3 (Vla), D4 (Vln II), which is F♭–C♭–A♭–D in A♭ and resolves straight into E♭7 at 65. The cello's C♭3 and the bass's F♭2 then fall together to B♭2 and E♭2, which is a perfect fifth to a perfect fifth. Above them there are only the violas and second violins, at pp and ♩≈42, held for two bars. My all-pairs check shows these as the only consecutive fifths between real parts in bars 57–70 ("Double basses / Violoncellos 7 → 7" at quarter-note 220). A&S PDF 505–506 (Ex. 29-9) say these fifths are unavoidable in plain stepwise writing. PDF 524–525 tolerate them "though not between outer voices" when the figuration hides them. Here they are not hidden. They are the lowest two voices of a thin chorale texture.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 507 (Ex. 29-11, Mozart, "Ach, ich fühl's"): "an anticipation of 2̂ over a German 6th can produce the momentary impression of a French 6th … a very frequent way of avoiding 5ths with the German 6th". PDF 505: in a thin sonority the Italian or French form is the better choice. Mozart 41/ii (corpus, onsets 75–79) does not take a German sixth straight to V in a plain texture; it goes through the 6/4.

**Fix** (bSec.ily, bSecVc, bar 64):
```
a,2:32\p d,4( b,8 bes,8) |
```
(was `a,2:32\p d,4( b,4) |`). The German sixth with the cello's C♭ still sounds on the beat, so c210's pun (E7 = V7 of A minor = German sixth of A♭) and its semitone C♭–B♭ are kept. On the second eighth the cello anticipates B♭2, and the chord becomes the French sixth F♭–A♭–B♭–D. The bass's F♭–E♭ then moves against a held B♭, so there are no fifths. retr.ily is unchanged (Vc `bes,2.\pp` at 65). Built in /tmp/critic-232/work: build-warnings.txt is empty, analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, the score is 8 pages, and the Cb/Vc fifths are gone from the all-pairs list with nothing new added. The parts book is 30 pages, and every part except the harp is on one page. This does not undo c210. It keeps its B2 and only adds the eighth-note anticipation.

### 2. Bars 73–74: the reprise's V4/3 semicadence (recorded, no change)

**Problem considered.** At 73 b3 → 74 the A′ statement has vi7 (F–A♭–C–E♭) → V4/3 over B♭ (c060, which made it differ from 25–26). A&S PDF 520 (§20, Ex. 29-33) note that a V4/3 semicadence is weak, and that Tchaikovsky strengthens it by resolving into it from the 4/2 form of the German sixth. In A♭ that would put C♭ in the bass with D, F♭ and A♭ above, falling to B♭.

**Why no change.** The cellos' protected counter-melody has C4 on 73 b3 (`aes2\< c'4`; the cello counter-melody 71–78 is protected), and C♮ cannot sound against a C♭ bass. Moving the counter-melody would change a protected line for one beat of colour. The weak V4/3 also suits a reprise phrase that should run on into 75, not stop. **Fix.** None.

### 3. Bar 78: augmented sixth after the ♭VI deceptive cadence (recorded, no change)

**Problem considered.** A&S PDF 513: "augmented 6ths frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI (in major, to ♭VI), because they form a convincing transition to V". Adding D♮ to the F♭ chord at 78 b2 would make a German sixth into the E♭ on 78 b3.

**Why no change.** The E♭ at 78 b3 is a passing bass (F♭–E♭–D♭) under a 6/3 (E♭–G–C) on the way to the pre-dominant at 79–80, and the real V arrives at 81. A&S PDF 503 says the resolution of an augmented sixth is heard as a dominant. A German sixth at 78 would make 78 b3 sound like V two bars early, and 79 (ii6/5) would then be a retrogression. The protected ♭VI and the 78–80 approach stay as they are. **Fix.** None.

### 4. Bar 43: German sixth from IV6 before the cadential 6/4 (recorded, no change)

**Problem considered.** 43 b1–2 is G♭–B♭–E♭ over G♭ and then over B♭ (cello B♭2 on b2), and the cadential 6/4 follows at b3. This is the setting of Ex. 29-13 (PDF 508, Beethoven Adagio cantabile: IV6, bass lowered to ♭6̂, German, V6/4), the most common preparation in the unit.

**Why no change.** Flute 1 holds theme B's G♭5 to b2.5 and then plays A♭5 (`ges''4. aes''8`). Every augmented sixth of D♭ needs G♮ (♯4̂) against B𝄫 in the bass. The melody's G♭ would clash with that G♮, and its A♭ would clash with both the G♮ and the B𝄫. **Fix.** None.

### 5. Bars 46–47: the turn to F♯ minor (recorded, no change)

**Problem considered.** A&S PDF 516 (§17): an augmented sixth before a new key's V makes that chord "sound like a V, thereby preparing the listener for the change of key". The C♯7 at 47 is reached from G♭ (F♯) major at 46 b3–4, so on arrival it can still sound like D♭ with an added seventh.

**Why no change.** The oboe and cellos hold C♯5 (theme B's head) through 46 b3–4. That note is 5̂ of the goal chord, and no augmented sixth contains the tone it resolves to. HISTORY also protects the C♯7 at 46–48 and the F♯-minor modulation 46–53. The Neapolitan sixth at 49 b2–3 then confirms the key. **Fix.** None.

### 6. Bar 80 beat 3: the final pre-dominant (recorded, no change)

80 b3 is iiø7 with ♭6̂ from mixture (B♭–D♭–F♭–A♭), and it resolves to the V♭9 at 81 (F♭ → E♭ in Hn 3 and the lower violas). That is the PDF 518 family (Ex. 29-30), one step short of the augmented sixth. The "root-position French sixth" (PDF 520 footnote: 2̂ in the bass, V7 of V with a diminished fifth) would need D♮ in place of the D♭ that Cl 2 holds at 80 and the counter-melody reaches at 81. **Fix.** None.

### 7. Where the movement uses augmented sixths (recorded, no change)

The movement has two augmented sixths. One, a French sixth from IV by chromatic voice exchange, closes the b phrase at 21–22. The other, a German sixth that is also V7 of ♭II, ends the B section at 64–65. The mid-phrase half cadences at 9–10, 25–26 and 73–74 stay diatonic, so each chromatic preparation marks the end of a phase, as PDF 503 describes. The link at 1–2 (C major to A♭) is the III♯–I relation of Ex. 29-38 (PDF 523). **Fix.** None.
