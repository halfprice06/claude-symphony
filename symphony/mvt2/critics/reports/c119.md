## What else I studied

**Master comparison.** The local corpus has no Bruckner. The IMSLP file pages for Symphony No. 7 (#624948, #26842) load only through a JavaScript redirect, so I could not download the score. I worked from the movement's published description (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._7_(Bruckner), https://www.filharmonikusok.hu/en/?p=91571, https://www.abruckner.com/down/articles/articlesEnglish/wagnerklausbruckne2/wagner_klaus_bruckner_7.pdf) and from my own knowledge of the score. Bruckner's Adagio opens with four Wagner tubas and the lower strings: a soft brass choir is the movement's home colour. The second theme (Moderato, F♯ major, 3/4) is a flowing string tune. The A material returns twice in waves that build by sequence and broken-off crescendos to a single late climax (C major, with cymbal). The coda is a soft chorale for tubas and horns.

**Verdict.** Measured against Bruckner, this score is more compact. It is about 5¾ minutes against about 20. It works in Dvořák/Brahms 8-bar periods, not in Bruckner's block sequences. Its plan matches his in outline: a soft opening colour, a contrasting theme with a faster pulse, two crescendo waves with the stronger one late (61 at 66%, 79 at 85%), and a coda that changes colour. A conductor would accept the harmony, the voicing and the climaxes as professional. What a conductor would still notice is in the soft brass and the muted strings. Bruckner's brass is never hollow when it plays softly. Rimsky-Korsakov's examples in this range never mute the double basses. Findings 1–3 address these two points.

**Against v109.** I diffed every section file. The changes in v110–v119 are: A♭6 instead of B♭6 at 58 (c110); cor anglais an octave higher at 32–34 and the pickup at 36 (c111); bar 55 rebuilt and viola D♭4 at 43 (c112); harp LH octaves at 74, 77, 78 (c114); engraving at 14, 63, 70, 78/82 (c115); harp pedal diagram, 82 pickup at pitch and harp mf/f at 77–78 (c116); Bsn 1 at 60 and harp D♭4 out at 13 (c117); bowing 61, 77–79 and cello slurs (c118). Almost all of these are improvements. The one place that got worse is bar 58 (Finding 4).

I also ran allpairs_par.py, and my own pairwise fifths scan on every voice of every track, chords and divisi included. The scan found nothing real. Its hits at 26–27, 60–61, 77–78, 78 b3 and 89 all have an intervening note in one voice or come from harp decay.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 60: the low brass entry is a hollow A♭–E♭ chord (APPLIED)

**Problem.** In the bar before the climax, the brass enter pp with a crescendo on Tuba A♭1, B.tbn A♭2, Tbn 2 E♭3 and Hn 4 A♭3. That is octaves and a fifth, with no third and no seventh. Meanwhile the strings and woodwinds play V7 of D♭ (C6 in Vln I, viola sextuplets G♭–C–E♭, the counter-line G♭–F). The brass swell sounds as an empty fifth growing under a full dominant seventh.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 97 no. 4: when the strings have the upper part, the wind middle parts should use "no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths", so that open fourths and fifths are avoided. RK PDF 100: brass part-writing "of the close order with no empty spaces". Ex. 115 (PDF 289): the brass take the chord p from the woodwind as a complete chord. Ex. 120 (PDF 294–295): each group joins the long crescendo on a full harmony. Bruckner's soft brass entries in his Adagio are always complete chords.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Tbn 2 plays G♭3, the seventh, which resolves by step to its existing F3 at 61:
```lilypond
bSecTbnTwo = { R1*23 | ges1\pp\< \breathe | f2--\mf aes2-- | bes2-- ces'2-- | R1*2 | }
```
The brass now read A♭1–A♭2–G♭3–A♭3, a seventh and a second, as no. 4 asks. The only new pair in allpairs_par.py is the doubled seventh's resolution with the violas' last sextuplet note (G♭4→F4). That seventh is already doubled through bar 60 by the violas, cellos, Bsn 1 and Hn 1. The anticipated F at 60 b4.5 (Vc/Bsn 1/Hn 1) briefly sounds against the held G♭3. That is the normal effect of an anticipation and lasts one eighth.

### 2. Bars 21–30: the double basses are muted, against every example in the reading (APPLIED)

**Problem.** From 15 to 30 all strings are con sord. (c039). From 23, the cellos leave the bass for their counter-melody, so the muted basses carry the bass alone at 16′, rising to f at 28. c109 (finding 2) noted the thin bass here. Its fix (Bsn 2) would add a staff, and it was not taken.

**Evidence.** Every muted-string example in this range leaves the basses open. In Ex. 125 (PDF 300, Kitesh), Vln II, the violas and the first cellos are "con sord." and the score marks "V-c. II e C-b. (senza sord.)". In Ex. 102 (PDF 281), all strings are con sord. except the basses, which play pizz. Ex. 110 (PDF 285) mutes the violas while the cellos and basses play pizz. Ex. 120 (PDF 292) does the same with the violins. RK PDF 28: a mute means "the volume of tone is always greatly reduced". The basses lose body under a mute, and in this passage they have no doubling to make it up.

**Fix (applied; marks only, MIDI unchanged).** aOne.ily bar 21: `\once \stemUp des,2\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." bes,,4`. The basses' "via sord." is removed at 31 (trans.ily: `transCb = { R2.*6 | }`), and so is their "senza sord." at 37 (bSec.ily: `des,4\p^"pizz." r4`). The upper strings keep their mutes. This does not undo c039. It keeps the muted colour where the book puts it.

### 3. Bar 28: muted strings at f (not applied; answers c103 and c109)

**Problem.** The first string statement of theme A peaks at f at 28 with all strings con sord. (c103 raised mf to f).

**Evidence.** RK PDF 28: muted strings "turn to a slight hiss or whistle in loud ones". In the muted examples Ex. 102, 110, 123, 125 and 126 (PDF 281, 285, 299–301), the muted strings stay between ppp and p. Ex. 120 (PDF 292–295) builds its crescendo molto mainly in the winds and brass. c103's Dvořák reason concerned the height of the peak, not the mute. c109 called it a matter of taste. The book gives a technical reason.

**Fix.** In aOne.ily bar 28, replace `\f` with `\mf` in Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb, keeping the hairpins. The ff at 79 then stands two degrees above this peak. I did not apply it, because 23–30 is protected and the decision belongs to the composer. If the f is kept, Finding 2 at least makes the bass unmuted under it.

### 4. Bar 58: the two octaves of theme B play different lines; worse than v109 (not applied)

**Problem.** Since c110, Fl 1 and Vln I hold A♭6 (`aes'''2 ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8`), while Ob 1 and Vln II an octave lower keep the turn A♭5–B♭5–A♭5. On beat 2, B♭5 sounds a seventh under the held A♭6. Theme B's characteristic turn is then heard only in the lower octave, under the line the ear follows. In v109 both octaves played the turn.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 67–69 and Ex. 61–66: octave doubling doubles the same line. Every octave pair in the examples viewed (Ex. 90, PDF 265–267; Ex. 127, PDF 302, piccolo and flute) moves note for note. c110's goal (B♭6 only at 79) is right and stays met.

**Fix.** Make the lower octave match: bSec.ily bar 58, Ob 1 `aes''2 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8` and Vln II `aes''2) ges''4(~ ges''8 f''8)`. Alternatively, leave Fl 1/Vln I and drop only the oboe's turn, so that the turn is heard in Vln II alone, as a counter-voice. I did not apply it. Theme B scoring 45–60 is protected (c111), and c110 kept the lower turn on purpose.

### 5. The horn quartet never plays a soft chord alone (observation, not applied)

**Problem.** Bruckner's home colour is the soft brass choir. Rimsky-Korsakov's equivalent is the horn quartet in four parts, "perfectly balanced in tone, without doubling the bass" (RK PDF 100). Ex. 76 (PDF 247, May Night Act III) is a pp chord for four horns con sord. with woodwind. In this movement the four horns sound together only at ff/f (61–62, 79–81). The coda's muted brass at 87–88 is Hn 3·4 with the cor anglais.

**Fix (if wanted).** In the coda, horns 1·2 con sord. could join horns 3·4 at 87–88 as a four-part chord, e.g. Hn 1 `f'2.\pp | fes'2.` doubling the cor anglais, and Hn 2 `des'2. | des'2.`. Page 8 has room for the extra staff. I record this only. The soft wind chords at 87–88 are protected (c114), and the present scoring follows Rimsky-Korsakov's own pairing of muted horns with the cor anglais (PDF 107).
