## Findings

### F1. Theme B's antecedent has no recurring rhythmic group: bar 40 (and 56) should rhyme with bar 38 (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 40 (Fl 1), 56 (Vln I, Vln II); the arpeggio at 40 (Vla) and 56 (harp RH).

**Problem.** In v81 the rhythms of 37–40 are `2 8 8 4 | 4 8 8 2 | 4 3×8 4 4 | 2 2`. All four bars differ. The theme's one characteristic group, a neighbour-note pair on beat 2 (38 C–D♭, 39 F–G♭–F, 42 B♭–A♭), stops at 40. There it gives way to two plain half notes, the slowest melodic motion in the B section. As a result the antecedent has no rhythmic rhyme, and a listener has nothing to hold on to before the consequent (41) arrives.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 52 (§7, Ex. 3-9, 3-10): rhythmic groups "form an important element in the design of a composition".
- PDF 53 (§8): bars 2 and 4 of a four-bar group are the weak, matching bars. A parallel rhythm in 38 and 40 lets the listener hear 37–40 as 2+2.
- Dvořák 9/ii does this in both of its tunes (see above).
- PDF 49 (§2): perceived tempo follows the main tones and the chord rate. Bass changes come about once every 2 s in both 7–14 and 37–44, and theme B's melody moved at 0.94 attacks/s against theme A's 0.79. "Poco più mosso" depends mostly on the melody's own motion, and the half notes at 40 slow it down. The change brings 37–44 to 1.0 attacks/s.

**Fix (applied).**
- Fl 1 at 40: `c''2\! bes'2) \breathe` → `c''4\! des''8 c''8 bes'2) \breathe`.
- Vln I and Vln II at 56: `c''2(\mf bes'2)` → `c''4(\mf des''8 c''8 bes'2)`.

The D♭5 is an unaccented upper neighbour over vii°7/vi (A&S PDF 54: short and weak, so it does not disturb c042's A°7). Beat 2 now carries the neighbour group in 38, 39, 40 and 42.

The new C5 attack at b2.5 made an existing consecutive octave more exposed: melody C5→B♭4 against the arpeggio's last triplet note C4 (b2.67)→B♭3 (b3), at 40 (violas) and 56 (harp RH). allpairs_par.py does not report this pair. So the arpeggio's last note goes to E♭4:
- Violas at 40: `a8( c' es' c' a c')` → `a8( c' es' c' a es')`.
- Harp RH at 56: `a8 c' es' c' a c'` → `a8 c' es' c' a es'`. The E♭ is already on its pedal setting, so the pedals do not change.

This does not undo any earlier fix. c031's head rhythm (37, 45, 53), c051/c071's pickups and c061's 4–3 at 42/58 are unchanged.

### F2. A′ violas, bar 72: the 3+3 slurs cut across a chord change on every beat (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 72 (Vla).

**Problem.** In 71–78 the viola eighths are slurred 3+3, which gives a 6/8 grouping. A&S PDF 51 (Ex. 3-7) describes this as a consistent counter-metric pattern, and it fits bars 71, 73–75, 77 and 78, where one chord lasts the bar or changes at mid-bar. Bar 72 is different:
- The harmony changes on every beat: vi7 – V7/V – V7 (bass F, B♭, E♭).
- The viola's pitches already fall into one pair per beat: C–A♭, B♭–A♭, B♭–G.
- The 3+3 slurs split the beat-2 pair across a slur break, so the bowing contradicts both the metre and the harmonic rhythm.
- This is the bar where the theme puts its chromatic D6 on beat 2 (PDF 50: chromatic notes are heard as accented). It is the one bar where the accompaniment should mark beat 2.

**Fix (applied).** Vla at 72: `c'( aes' bes) aes'( bes g')` → `c'( aes') bes( aes') bes( g')`. Only the slurs change; there are no new notes.

### F3. Inner-voice octaves into the reprise's bar 73: Cl 2 and harp LH, B♭→A♭ (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 72 b3 → 73 (Cl 2, harp LH).

**Problem.**
- Cl 2's B♭4 anticipation (c061, 72 b2.5) moves to A♭4 at 73.
- The harp LH chord `<es,, bes,,>` on 72 b3 moves its upper note B♭1 to A♭1 at 73.
- These are consecutive compound octaves between an inner voice and the bass's doubling fifth. allpairs_par.py does not report them; I found them with my own all-track scan. v81 already had them.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 56 (Ex. 3-19, 3-20): the anticipated tone belongs to the next chord. The anticipation is correct; the harp's fifth duplicates its motion. c049 removed the same harp fifth at bar 9 for the same kind of reason.

**Fix (applied).** Harp LH at 72: `<es,, bes,,>4\arpeggio` → `es,,4`. The harp still doubles the bass (c026 protection).

### F4. Both themes peak on B♭ in the same way (observation; no change recommended)

**Bars:** 11–12 (and 27–28, 75–76) against 41–42 (and 57–58).

**Problem.** Theme B's consequent repeats the shape of theme A's second phrase:
- Each rises from A♭ through a dotted value to a note held across a beat (theme A: F, tied over the bar line 11→12; theme B: F, a dotted quarter on 41 b3).
- Each reaches B♭ on beat 2 of the following bar.
- Each falls back to A♭.
- In theme A the B♭ is a quarter with the accent shifted onto it (PDF 51, syncopation). In theme B it is an eighth on a weak half-beat, so theme B's peak is the less accented of the two (PDF 50).

**Why no change.** The kinship works as a cyclic link and the climax plan relies on it. c051 and c073 decided that B♭6 at 58 stays a passing eighth so that the held A♭6 at 61 and the B♭6 at 79 are the peaks. Lengthening the B♭ at 42 alone would break the motivic neighbour group that F1 strengthens. I record it only so that later melody critics do not reopen it without answering c073.
