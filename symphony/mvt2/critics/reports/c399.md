### 1. Holistic comparison with Bruch 3/ii: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? (recorded)

Yes. Point by point, from the score:
- **Key plan.** Bruch puts the Adagio in G, a third away from the symphony's E, and puts the middle section in E♭, the flat submediant (p. 65, letter B, pivoting on a held B♭ in the horns). This movement is in A♭, a third away from movement I's C (♭VI, recorded in mvt2.md), with D♭ in the middle and the brief F♯-minor and A-minor colours. Both composers use third relations as the governing idea. Bruch's E♭ section is in fact the same ♭VI relation that this movement has to its symphony.
- **Frame.** Bruch frames the movement with a pp hymn for full winds, soft trombones and timpani, with fermatas (pp. 60-61, bars 1-9), and brings the hymn back at G (p. 84) before closing morendo over pizzicato basses and a timpani stroke. This movement frames itself with the common-tone link (1-6) and the coda over the A♭ pedal (83-92). It may not use a trombone chorale (reserved for the finale, cyclic rule), so it uses horns, clarinets and divided violas instead. That serves the same purpose. Rimsky's Ex. 76 and 106 (PDF 247, 283) show the same soft wind pad in an Adagio.
- **Middle-section engine.** At B Bruch sets the clarinets' melody over cellos and basses repeating eighths "sempre p ma un poco marcato" and violas in measured 32nds (pp. 65-66). Those are the "drones" the Arts Fuse essay mentions. This movement's B section (37-44) uses pizzicato basses, viola triplets and held second violins. The method is the same one, and it is the string harmonic basis under a wind solo that Rimsky describes (PDF 89, Ex. 102-104; Ex. 120 on PDF 292).
- **Return.** Bruch's return at D (p. 74) gives the theme to the first violins pp and adds a new 32nd-note figure in the second violins and violas (pp. 76-80), so the return is livelier than the first statement. This movement does the same at 71-78 (the viola eighths, protected as A′'s difference from 23-30).
- **Climax.** Bruch has one ff climax, at F (p. 81). The winds hold the chord, the upper strings play tremolo, the low strings rush up in 32nds into each bar, and the music drops at once to p "molto espress." This movement has two ff points (61 and 79), each released within two bars. Dvořák 9/ii also has two. The second climax has a later, higher peak (B♭6 at 79), which is the normal Romantic order.
- **Difference worth recording.** Bruch's surface rhythm is more varied: dotted 6/8 figures, 16th turns and 32nd-note string figures recur throughout. This movement's surface is mostly quarters and eighths, and its fastest values (viola sextuplets at 59-60, the grace runs at 78-79) are kept for the builds. At ♩ = 52-60 in 3/4 and 4/4 this is the Dvořák 9/ii manner, which is the main model in the brief. I do not recommend adding figuration: the places where Bruch uses it (the return, the approach to the climax) are already the places where this movement thickens its motion.

### 3. Inverted dominants and the doubled bass (recorded, follows PDF 83-84)

PDF 83-84: "The bass of an inversion of the dominant chord should never be doubled in any of the upper parts", and the same for other sevenths and diminished sevenths. My scan of score.midi, quarter by quarter, found 37 beats with a pure dominant-seventh or diminished-seventh sonority. The six inverted ones are at 33 b2, 40 b1, 53 b3, 56 b1, 59 b3 and 64 b3. In each, the only other notes on the bass pitch class are the bass line itself in octaves (Bn 2, B.cl. or harp left hand with the cellos and basses), which PDF 82 allows. 74 b1 does not show in the scan because passing notes make the sonority impure. It is the known case recorded by c394: the cellos' B♭3 is a step of the protected counter-melody at p. No change.

### 4. Soft brass layout at 60-62 against PDF 100-103 (recorded)

At 60 the horns, trombone 2, bass trombone and tuba enter pp under the poco allargando, and at 61 the brass is at ff/f. PDF 101-102: when horns are mixed with trombones, double the horns or mark them one degree louder. Horns 1·2 are ff against trombones at f, and horns 3·4 are a2 at f, so both conditions are met. The seventh G♭3 at 60 is in trombone 2, a different colour from the horns' chord tones (PDF 102: discords to different colours). The F3-F4 space in the brass at 61 is recorded by c259 and is protected. No change.

### 5. Coda texture against Ex. 92, Ex. 125 and PDF 88 (recorded)

Ex. 125 (*Kitesh*, Larghetto alla breve, PDF 300): muted Vln II, violas and cellos I move above a senza-sordino pedal in cellos II and basses, with clarinet, bass clarinet and bassoon II + contrabassoon held pp. Ex. 92 (PDF 269): horn with muted violas.
