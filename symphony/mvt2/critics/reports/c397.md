Overall verdict: the movement now follows nearly every principle in this section. Each combined passage has three or four strands. The strands differ in rhythm, register and usually colour. Counter-lines combine material the listener has already heard. The builds add instruments to each element in turn. Dynamics are graded by importance. The earlier passes on this reading (c017, c117, c257) fixed the places where two strands met on one pitch. Only one point in the reading is not used: a soft timpani roll leading into the second climax. I applied it in scratch. Two other points are recorded with reasons for leaving them alone.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 78 b3 into 79: the crescendo to the A′ peak has no timpani roll (applied)

**Bars.** 78 b3-79 b1.

**Problem.** The movement has two climaxes. The first is prepared exactly as Adler describes it. At 60 the timpani roll on A♭ from pp with a crescendo to f, under the brass pedal, and arrive on D♭ at 61. The second is the A′ peak at 79 (B♭6 in Vln I and Fl 1). It has a one-beat crescendo at 78 b3: the strings, Fl 1 and bassoons all have hairpins (accepted in an earlier cycle, HISTORY line 1575), Hn 1·2 enter mf<, the harp plays f and the violins run up to B♭6. The timpani are silent through 77-78 and then enter f with a roll on D♭ at 79 b1. The only instrument whose job in this chapter is to drive a crescendo starts on the peak instead of leading into it.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184: in *Schelomo* "the crescendo to m. 235 as well as the one to m. 243 are greatly aided by a timpani roll", which "the composer wisely marks ... piano and then crescendo". PDF 176: the timpani supply the "cohesive tonic-dominant, nonthematic pedal notes" of a contrapuntal texture. The bass at 78 b3 is E♭, the dominant, and the E♭ drum is tuned (A♭ C D♭ E♭), so the roll is that pedal. The movement's own bar 60 already uses this device. Against the change: Dvořák 9/ii enters ff at its climax (96) with no roll before it. His climax is a sudden tutti arrival, though, and 79 here is reached by a written crescendo, which is the case Adler describes.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-397/work/sections/aTwo.ily, `aTwoTimp` bar 78):**
```lilypond
aTwoTimp = { R2.*6 | R2.^\markup \small "medium sticks" | r2 es4:32\p\< | des2.:32\f^\markup \small \italic "ord." | ...
```
Before, bar 78 was `R2.`. The hairpin ends on the existing f at 79, so the f roll at 79 is unchanged (protected). So are the drum tuning and the E♭ roll at 81, which uses the same drum. The roll lasts one beat, about 1.3 s at the allargando's ♩=48-50. It sits on E♭3, three octaves and more below the violins' run, so it cannot cover the melody. The timpani staff now prints on the lower system of page 6; the score stays at 8 pages. This finding is the lowest-risk kind of change. If the composer prefers Dvořák's sudden arrival, the reason should be recorded as an answer to PDF 184.

### 2. Bars 59 b3-60: the viola sextuplets strike the counter-line's notes at pitch (recorded, no change)

The counter-line in Hn 1 (with Vc and Bsn 1 an octave lower) moves G♭4 | E♭4 F♭4 | E♭4 F4 G♭4. F4 at 59-60. The viola sextuplets arpeggiate the same chords and touch the counter-line's pitches in the same octave: E♭4 inside 59 b3, F♭4 on the attack of 59 b4 (`fes'16( des' bes ...)` against the horn's `fes'4`), E♭4 inside 60 b1 and G♭4 at the end of 60 b4. By PDF 184 (each fragment where it is clearly heard) this is the one place in 57-60 where middleground and counter-line meet on a pitch. I recommend no change. The counter-line is f in a three-colour mixture (PDF 178), and its rhythm (halves and quarters) is wholly different from the sixteenth-note figure (PDF 175, 179). The viola notes are passing members of an arpeggio, not held doublings. If the composer wants to remove the one attack on a beat, the only edit needed is 59 b4 `\tuplet 6/4 4 { bes16( des' fes' des' bes des') }` in `bSecVla` (F♭4 moves off the beat, same pitches). I did not apply it, because 59-60 sits inside the protected 57-61 counter-line passage.

### 3. Bars 71, 74, 78 b2: the viola figure touches Vln II's held note (recorded, no change)

The viola eighths `c'8( es' aes')` reach the lower theme octave's held pitch at 71 b2 (A♭4), 74 b2 (G4) and 78 b2 (A♭4). By PDF 184 this is a middleground voice sounding a foreground note at pitch. The point is already settled: c257 finding 3 left it open as optional, a later cycle recorded "viola figure at 71, 74, 78" with no change (HISTORY line 2100), and the viola figure 71-78 is on the do-not-touch list (lines 1016, 1701). Each contact lasts one eighth under a held note, and the rise of the figure is its shape. I add no new reason to reopen it.

### 4. Bars 87-90: the inner line F-F♭-E♭ in three colours (confirmation)

In the coda the chromatic thread F-F♭-E♭ is played by the violas (F3-F♭3-E♭3), Hn 4 con sordino at the same pitch and the cor anglais an octave higher (F4-F♭4-E♭4), under Cl 1's D♭5-C5 and the violins' and cellos' A♭ pedals. That is a string, a brass and a woodwind instrument on one element, doubled only at the unison and the octave (PDF 178, 184). It is also the last statement of the F♭-E♭ thread, and its colour differs from every earlier one (PDF 176). No change.

### 5. Bars 53-56: the theme in violins alone (confirmation of c257 finding 5)

PDF 178 asks for a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument on each element. At 53-56 theme B is in the violins alone, the counter-line in horn and clarinet, the figure in the harp. This is Bloch's choir separation (PDF 184), the other method the section teaches for clarity. It also leaves the mixed doubling to arrive at 57 (PDF 172, 184). No change.
