## Findings

### F1. Theme B's minor head loses the semitone neighbour that defines the theme; use the chromatic lower neighbour A♮ at 46 (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 46, in Ob 1, Bsn 1 and Vc, which double the head in octaves (c087).

**Problem.**
- Every neighbour in theme B's antecedent is a semitone: G♭–F at 37, C–D♭ at 38, F–G♭–F at 39, D♭–C at 40. c081 made 38 and 40 rhyme on this figure.
- When the head returns in B♭ minor at 45–46 (`des''2 es''8 des''8 c''4 | bes'4 aes'8 bes'8 des''2`), both neighbours become whole steps.
- The lower neighbour returns upward to the local tonic, B♭, over a B♭-minor chord: Vln II F4, Vla F3, Bsn 2 B♭2, Cb B♭1.
- With A♭, the melody in 45–46 uses only pitches of D♭ major (D♭ E♭ C B♭ A♭). The minor colour comes only from the bass, two bars before the C♯7 pivot.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 339–340, §14 and Ex. 20-23: "If the diatonic neighbor is a whole step away from the main tone, bringing it a half step closer makes the two tones sound more connected." Also: "Chromatic lower neighbors, which produce the melodically active effect of leading tones, occur more often than upper ones."
- PDF 340, §15 and Ex. 20-24: in minor, the form of 7̂ depends on the direction of the line and the harmonic context. Here the line turns back up to 1̂ over i.
- With A♮ the head keeps the theme's semitone neighbour, so the listener hears it as the same tune in a darker mode. The A♮–B♭ step also repeats the bass at 40 (A°7 → B♭ minor) and prepares the A♮ of the F♯-minor quotation at 48.
- Counter-evidence: Dvořák keeps the modal B♮ in his C♯-minor tune (9/ii, 48–49). The A♭ is therefore a defensible Dvořák colour. I still rank the change first because it carries theme B's motive into the minor. The composer may prefer the modal colour; this is a judgement, not a rule.

**Fix (applied).**
- Ob 1, `bSec.ily` line 36: `bes'4( aes'8 bes'8 des''2\<)` → `bes'4( a'8 bes'8 des''2\<)`.
- Bsn 1, line 87: `bes4( aes8 bes8 des'2\<)` → `bes4( a8 bes8 des'2\<)`.
- Vc, line 219: the same change.
- The A♮ is an eighth on beat 2 against the sustained F and B♭, the same metric place as the C over B♭ at 38. It creates no parallels (scan clean) and no change to the layout.

### F2. A′ bar 76→77: Cl 2 and the viola arpeggio move B♭→G in octaves; a chordal skip makes the octaves (APPLIED; answers c039)

**Bars:** 76 b3 → 77 b1. Cl 2 (shared staff with Cl 1, who rests) and Vla.

**Problem.**
- Cl 2 plays B♭4 on 76 b3 and G4 on 77.
- The viola's last eighth of 76 is the chordal skip B♭3 (b3.5), which falls to G3 on the downbeat.
- These are consecutive octaves between two inner voices. allpairs_par.py reports them at beat 256.0.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 331, Ex. 20-5 ("causes parallel octaves"): chord members that break up parallels "impose on them the obligation not to cause any". Fifths and octaves made by chordal skips count as faulty voice leading.
- PDF 340–341, §16: octaves caused by figuration are more serious than fifths, "and you should avoid them".
- The Ex. 20-6 exemption (quick extended arpeggios) does not cover this case: the viola's B♭ lands on the downbeat with the clarinet.

**Answer to c039.** c039 kept the B♭4 because A♭4, F4 and D♭5 on beat 3 were each worse. I confirmed that F4 on beat 3 alone makes fifths A♭/D♭ → F/B♭ with the bass. allpairs_par.py reports them once F4 is in.

The fix is to move Cl 2 to F4 one beat earlier:
- Cl 2 holds F4 from b2 to b3 while the bass holds D♭, then moves B♭.
- Every motion against the bass is therefore oblique, and nothing moves in parallel.
- F4 completes ii7 on beat 3 (B♭–D♭–F–A♭), which in v91 had no fifth.
- On 77 it rises to G4 against the viola's fall to G3 and Vln II's A♭–G: contrary motion in both pairs.
- The new F4 on b2 doubles the viola's F4 on the same eighth for one beat. This is harmless.

**Fix (applied).** `aTwo.ily` line 75, Cl 2 at 76: `aes'4 aes'4 bes'4` → `aes'4 f'2`.

**Result.**
- allpairs_par.py: the only difference from v91 is that the four "256.0 Clarinets 1·2:two / Violas P8" lines are gone. Nothing is added.
- My scan shows no new pairs anywhere.
- This replaces c039's "Cl 2 B♭4 at 76" protection. Its stated reason, that every alternative was worse, does not hold for a two-beat F4.
