## Findings

No finding needs a score change. Every principle in the reading that applies to a symphonic slow movement is met. The findings are ranked by how close each comes to a weakness, and each says why the text should stay.

**1. Overlap at the section joins, bars 30-31, 36-37, 52-53, 64-65, 70-71, 82-83 (Adler PDF 190, Ex. XV-36; PDF 192, Ex. XV-40).** Adler's two rules on joins are that a soft group must already be sounding before a louder one stops, and that a line passed between colours must overlap so the seam does not show. I checked each join in the source files. At 30 the contrabassoon enters pp under the strings' p > pp and holds the A♭ pedal into 31, where the bass clarinet starts; the strings stop without a gap in the sound. At 36-37 the C.a.'s slur crosses the double bar into 37. At 52-53 the C.a. holds through 53 b1-2 under the violins' pickup on 52 b4, and the timpani roll at 52 is the background before the build (as Bartók's timpani in Ex. XVII-12). At 64-65 the second violins, violas and cellos tie their pp notes from 64 into 65 (retr.ily line 7-9), and the basses take the E♭ pedal at 65. At 70-71 Hn 1 holds B♭3 to the barline while the violins' pickup starts on 70 b3 (c343). At 82-83 the basses' A♭1 and the harp's A♭1 are tied from 82 into the coda's ppp (aTwo.ily, aTwoCb and aTwoHarpL; coda.ily, codaCb). Each join therefore follows the forte-subito-piano scoring, with the quieter layer in place before the louder one ends. The movement's pacing depends on these joins, because four of its seven sections start softer than the previous one ends. No change.

**2. The staged build 53-61 and its one deliberate seam at 56-57 (Adler PDF 192, Ex. XV-40 second line; PDF 267).** The triplet figure passes from harp (53-56) to violas (57-58) with no overlap, and doubles its speed at 59 (sextuplets). Adler allows a seam "if one wishes each group ... to be accented". Here the seam coincides with the start of theme B's second phrase, where flutes, oboes, clarinets, bass clarinet, bassoons and Hn 1 all enter (57), so the change of figure marks the next stage of the build rather than a fault in a continuous line. c049 designed the order harp 53, violas 57, sextuplets 59; c413 recorded the build adding strings, woodwind and brass and releasing them in reverse. An overlap (harp continuing into 57 b1) would blur the stage and add an attack to the downbeat that c124 and c434 already judged sufficient. No change.

**3. The climax and the cut-back, bars 61-67 (Adler PDF 189-190, Ex. XV-35; PDF 267, Ex. XVII-10 at C; PDF 264-266).** The ff tutti lasts two bars (61-62, about 9 s), then 63 combines three of Adler's sforzando methods: a short woodwind sf (Fl 1 crotchet), a low brass sf > p (B.tbn), and strings sf > pp in tremolo, under which Tbn 1 plays the quotation ff. This is Mozart's procedure in Ex. XVII-10 (tutti, then a drastic reduction as the solo enters), applied to a quotation instead of a concerto soloist. The second cut-back, 64-67, follows the same example's second point: a sustained low pedal (E♭ in the basses and the timpani roll) marks the approach to the tonic while Hn 1 plays alone. Adler's Mozart rule on horns (PDF 264) also holds: Hn 1·2 and 3·4 play in the tutti at 61-62, which serves as the interlude, and only Hn 1 plays at 67-70. A longer ff plateau would either cover the quotation or move the peak past two-thirds of the duration; Dvořák's climax area starts at about 68 %, this score's at 65 %. No change.

**4. Proportions of the return and coda, bars 71-92 (Adler PDF 278-279, Ex. XVII-19; PDF 267).** The return and coda take 83 s (24 %), the same share as Dvořák 9/ii's Tempo I return and coda (101-127). Verdi's Ex. XVII-19 keeps the accompaniment to a few soft parts and brings in the full orchestra only at the final cadence. In this score the reverse order applies because the A′ climax comes first: the full orchestra peaks at 79-81, cuts back at 82-83, and the coda (83-92) uses at most nine parts over a single A♭ pedal. Adler's Mozart example (PDF 267, item 1) explains why the coda's three solo ideas (harp head 83, timpani 84-85, Cl 1 tail 85-89) each sit over one held pedal: a sparse accompaniment gives the soloist freedom. The rit. starts at 89, after the last melodic entry, as Bizet's rall. comes only on the last notes of a phrase (PDF 279-281). No change; the ending, the exported themes and the quotations are not affected.

**5. Colour rotation as a guide to the form, bars 7-82 (Adler PDF 257-258, Ex. XVII-1, XVII-2; PDF 288, Ex. XVII-24).** Adler says dialogue "clarifies the melodic material, and aids in a clearer exposition of the form", and Haydn varies which line the flute doubles in the second phrase. Theme A has three scorings (horn with winds and harp; violins in unison with the cello counter-melody; violins in octaves with Fl/Ob, viola eighths and harp). Theme B passes Fl 1 → C.a. → Ob 1 → violins → violins with Fl/Ob. A listener can tell A from A′ and each B statement from the next by colour alone. The violas switch to pizz. at 41 when the C.a. answers the flute, which is Beethoven's device in Ex. XVII-1. No change.

**6. Introduction and solo preparation, bars 1-7, 37 and 71 (Adler PDF 263-264, Ex. XVII-7, XVII-8; PDF 268-272, Ex. XVII-11, XVII-12).** Adler's two concerto slow movements begin with a woodwind choir so that the solo colour is fresh after the first movement; Dvořák's Cello Concerto ii gives the woodwinds eight bars, then drops to pp low strings under the solo. Here the strings hold movement I's chord (1-3), the winds take over (3-6), and Hn 1 enters at 6-7 over Cl 2, B.cl., bassoons and harp at pp. The shorter introduction (6 % against the Canzonetta's 10 %) suits a link that continues the previous movement's last chord rather than stating a new idea. At 37 and 71 the background and the tune start together, without Sibelius's bars of preparation; c233 and c363 recorded that 37 follows Dvořák 9/ii at 46 and that the 35-36 fifth and the section lengths are protected. At 71 the violins' pickup at 70 b3 overlaps Hn 1's held note (Finding 1), so the entry is prepared by the horn. No change.
