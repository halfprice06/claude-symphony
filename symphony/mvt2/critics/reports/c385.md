## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Flute 1, bar 78 beat 3: ledger-line beam flat while Violin I's is sloped (applied)

**Problem.** The rising figure C6 – F6–G6–A♭6 (quaver plus semiquaver triplet) lies wholly above the first ledger line. In Violin I, on the same page with the same pitches and rhythm, the beam slopes +0.36 space (−0.18 to +0.18). In Flute 1 it is horizontal (0.00 to 0.00). c195 gave both staves the same slope with `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.18 . 0.18)`. c375 then rewrote Flute 1's bar 78 to give the group down-stems, and the new line dropped c195's override. The c195 slope is on the do-not-touch list ("the score's beam slopes and stems", c255), so this is a regression, not a new choice. The Flute 1 tuplet bracket also moved to the notehead side, above the ledger lines, because the part-combiner's voice one sets brackets up. In Violins I and II the bracket is on the beam side, inside the stave.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 35: "When all the notes of a beam fall outside the first ledger line, the beam takes only a slight slope, regardless of the pitches. Intervals of a second take a slope of ¼ stave-space; all wider intervals take a slope of ½ stave-space." The outside interval here is a sixth, so the slope should be about ½ space, not 0. PDF 36: "The beam is angled in the direction of the interval for a pair of notes or the outside interval for a group of notes." The group rises, and it is not concave (no inner note is nearer the beam than both outer notes), so the beam should rise. PDF 35, beams outside the stave: "matching angles look better". Flute 1's own falling figures at 77 and 81 slope −0.36, matching Violin I.

**Fix (applied, sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoFluteOne, bar 78):**
```lilypond
aes''2)\f \breathe \temporary \override Stem.direction = #DOWN \temporary \override TupletBracket.direction = #DOWN \once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.18 . 0.18) c'''8(\< \tuplet 3/2 { f'''16 g''' aes''' } \revert Stem.direction \revert TupletBracket.direction |
```
The logger now reports Flute 1 at 78 b3 as −0.18 → +0.18, the same as Violin I. The bracket sits under the beam as in the violins. `\temporary` and `\revert` give the part-combiner's directions back for the shared chord at 79, so the rest of page 6 and all other pages are pixel-identical to v385. No pitch, rhythm or MIDI change.

### 2. Violas 37–60: down-stems on notes below the middle line (protected, no change)
The script flags these groups because the majority of their notes lie below the middle line (PDF 38, majority rule). The single down direction for the whole passage is c015's choice, kept by c105, c195 and c255. It is what Gould PDF 39 ("visual considerations ... maintaining the same stem direction for the whole passage") allows, and it keeps the slurs and the "6"/"3" numbers on one side. Leave it.

### 3. Bass clarinet, bar 31: four-quaver beam slopes 1.65 spaces (no change)
E♭2–F2–A♭2–C3 across two beats, about 6.8 spaces between notes. Gould PDF 33 says beams "usually cross no more than one stave-line", and PDF 34 limits a shorter group to one space but allows "a long group of notes" to cross "one or possibly two stave-spaces". c105, c195 and c255 recorded it under the second rule. It stays within two spaces and its spacing is wide, so it does not produce the close lattice that PDF 34 warns against. If the composer wants it flatter, `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(1.18 . 2.18)` before the E♭ in transBassCl gives 1.0 with the right-hand stem at about 2.7 spaces. Just outside the stave, that is above Gould's 2½-space minimum for a stem outside the stave (PDF 35). Not applied: it is a matter of taste and was settled three times.

### 4. Cello div., bar 18: upper-voice beam 0.68 for C4–E♭4 (no change)
c255 called this 0.18 over the ledger-line limit. On the page images, C4 is on the first ledger line, not beyond it. PDF 35's rule covers groups whose notes all "fall outside the first ledger line", so it does not apply here, and 0.68 is within the one-space limit for a short group (PDF 34). Leave it.

### 5. Two-voice shared staves (no change)
Up-stems on notes above the middle line at Bn 45–46, Cl 77, and Ob/Cl 81 are the upper voice of a split part-combined staff (Cl 2 / Ob 2 / Bn 2 have their own rhythm there). Gould's beam-direction rules (PDF 38) are for a single part. Double-stemmed writing follows ch. 2, and c205/c335 checked it.

### 6. Everything else in the range follows
No kneed beams, no divided beams, no fractional beams, no beams crossing a rest, no beam sloping against its outside interval, and every flat beam is explained by equal ends, a repeated pattern or a concave or mixed shape (rows 19–24). Ledger lines are visibly thicker than stave-lines and do not join in the B♭6 passages at 76–81 (PDF 40).
