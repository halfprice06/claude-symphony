### 2. Harmonic variety and focus at the cyclic moments: the score already does what the reading asks (8, 24, 33, 47-53, 63-64, 68, 72, 86-89; no change)

**What the reading says.** PDF 19, Ex. 3/3A: give a returning melody a new harmony where possible, but keep the resting chord. PDF 13: one element in front at a time.

**What the score does.** Across the movement the motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C gets four harmonizations, and each one lands on C. That is Ex. 3/3A's procedure, used for the symphony's motto rather than a song chorus. The D♮ changes meaning each time. It is the third of V7/V at 8, 24 and 72. At 33 it belongs to the common-tone diminished seventh (the stop chord's pitch classes). At 68 it is the third of V over the E♭ pedal, and at 86 the root of iiø4/2 over the tonic pedal. Inside theme A the three statements keep vi7-V7/V-V7. That is protected (c434, c442), and I agree with keeping it. Ex. 3A keeps the melody's notes and rhythm but changes every chord. If it did that to theme A at 72, the reprise would stop sounding like a return, and in this ABA′ form the return has to be heard. The variety the reading asks for comes at 73-74 (I-vi7 | V4/3 since c060) and 77-81, right after the tail.

Focus holds at each cyclic moment. At 47-50 the C.a. is the only line marked p/mp. Vln II above it moves only in minims, and the violas play off-beat quavers sotto voce, pp. At 63-64 Tbn 1 is the only ff line, and the strings drop sf > p under it. At 67-70 Hn 1 has only ppp timpani and pp bassoons, with Bsn 1 a third below for one bar. At 85-89 Cl 1's tail sits over a ppp pedal; the only other moving line is the F-F♭-E♭ step at 87-88, and it moves while Cl 1 holds D♭5, which is the focus shift PDF 14 describes.

### 3. Economy test on the coda's inner voices (87-90; no change)

**Test.** Sebesky's Ex. 1 cut a line that moved in the lower-middle register, sounded no pitch the chord needed, and hid the main voice. I looked for a line like that in the link (3-6), the retransition (65-70) and the coda (83-92). The only candidate is Hn 3·4 at 87-90, con sord., pp. They double the divided violas at the unison (Hn 4 F3-F♭3-E♭3 with the lower violas, Hn 3 A♭3 with the upper violas).

**Verdict.** Keep. They are not like Sebesky's trombone line. They add no pitch and no separate motion that could cover the contrary motion of the other voices, and muted at pp they lie a tenth and more below Cl 1. They give the IV6-iv-I close a horn colour. The finale quotes Hn 4's F-F♭-E♭ at pitch (finale 197-198, items 11 and 28 of its movement II list). The plagal close 87-92 is also on the standing list. Removing them would save nothing the ear needs, and it would break a recorded cross-movement link.

### 4. Instrumentation held back and the arc between movements (no change)

PDF 11's advice to hold a colour back works here at the scale of the whole symphony. The trumpets are tacet for the whole movement. The trombones and tuba play only at 60-64, and Tbn 1 plays alone for movement I's first-theme head. The finale's C-major brass therefore comes as the "second chorus" entry. Movement I ends soft on C major, this movement ends ppp on A♭ with C5 on top, and the scherzo starts pp on Hn 1's G♯4. The A♭ panel is ♭VI between C and E, and the soft frame (1-14, 83-92) brackets two peaks without sagging (ch. 9, PDF 240). Nothing to change.
