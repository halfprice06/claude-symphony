## What else I studied

**Schubert 8/ii.** No score is in the local corpus, and I did not get a score from the web. I worked from Wikipedia's description of the movement (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._8_(Schubert)) and The Listeners' Club guide (https://thelistenersclub.com/2017/04/05/schuberts-unfinished-symphony-haunting-mysterious-groundbreaking/). That gives the movement's main features:
- sonatina form (no development);
- a plaintive minor second theme, first in solo clarinet (C♯ minor), then continued by solo oboe in C♯ major;
- a full-orchestra closing theme that ends in D♭;
- a recapitulation that puts the second theme in the subdominant (A minor), with oboe and clarinet trading the roles they had in the exposition;
- a long coda that brings the first theme back in the woodwinds in A♭ major before E major returns.

I did not check bar-level details against a score, so I do not cite Schubert bar numbers.

**Would a conductor take this for a master's work?** Measured against Schubert 8/ii, the movement does what Schubert does:
- the second theme moves between solo woodwinds (Fl 1 → C.a. → Ob 1);
- a single tutti outburst stands between soft solos (61–62);
- the remote key areas are reached through single held tones (the F♭/E–E♭ thread);
- the return is rescored instead of repeated.

Rimsky treats the last point as the main resource of variety (PDF 118). The two places where Schubert does more are the coda and the reprise of the second theme. Both are cyclic decisions protected in HISTORY (c263; Finding 2). The few accompaniment problems that the reading flags are small (Findings 1 and 3).

## Findings

**1. Bars 67–70: Hn 1's motto tail over a four-bar timpani roll and a held bass E♭ (moderate, optional).**
- *Problem:* PDF 139 names two textures as the most likely to cover a soft solo: a timpani tremolo, and long held double-bass notes. Bars 67–70 have both under Hn 1 p–mp: a continuous E♭3 roll for 3½ bars (retrTimp) and the Cb E♭ held for six bars (retrCb).
- *Evidence:* Rimsky's own A♭-major Lento (Ex. 298, PDF 482) puts a pp timpani roll and a pp horn pedal under a soloist, but stops the roll after two bars and then uses a single stroke. The roll is never under the whole phrase. c240 left the same balance point open, from the MIDI side.
- *Fix (untested; the horn line, the bass and the tied final stroke stay):* `retrTimp = { R2.*2 | es2.:32\ppp~^\markup \small \italic "near the rim" | es2.:32 | R2. | r2 es8 r8 | }`. The roll then covers only E♭–D–D♭ (67–68), and B♭3 at 69–70 sounds over the basses alone, with the short stroke at 70 b3 kept as c056 wants.
- *Protection:* this changes the accompaniment of a protected passage (65–70 retransition, c003/c262). The reason for that protection is the horn solo and the E♭7 harmony, and neither changes. If the composer treats the timpani as part of the protection, leave it.
- *Not applied.*

**2. Coda 83–92 and the second theme: Schubert recalls a theme in a remote key in his coda; this score recalls only the head of theme A on the tonic (recorded, no change).**
- *Problem:* Schubert's coda brings the first theme back in the woodwinds in A♭, a key remote from his E, before E returns. Here the coda stays on the A♭ pedal (harp harmonics, timpani answer, the motto tail in Cl 1 and the C.a./Hn 4 echo of F–F♭–E♭).
- *Why no change:* c263 records that theme B is not recalled after 61, because the finale quotes it, and that the F–F♭–E♭ line at 87–89 is its trace. The F♭ at 88 is the remote colour, in the same place Schubert puts his. Under Rimsky PDF 118 (alteration of details), that line already counts as the varied recall. Do not reopen.

**3. Bars 87–88: brass doubling strings, and wind doubling brass, under the Cl 1 solo (recorded, no change).**
- *Problem:* PDF 138–139 warns against strings doubled by brass and brass doubled by wind under a solo. At 87–88:
  - Hn 3 A♭3 is in unison with the upper violas;
  - Hn 4 F3–F♭3–E♭3 is in unison with the lower violas;
  - the C.a. plays Hn 4's line an octave higher;
  - all of this sits under Cl 1's D♭5.
- *Evidence that it is acceptable:* Rimsky does the same in the fuller return of the Sadko duet (Ex. 290, PDF 467–468), with ppp horn chords and woodwind dolcissimo under the voices. Here everything is pp, the horns are muted, and Cl 1 is a sixth or more above in its clarion register, so it is not covered. The layout is protected (c259, c287).
- *Recommendation:* no change.

**4. Bars 37–52: the second-theme solos follow Schubert's model and the reading (recorded, no change).**
- Schubert hands his second theme from one solo woodwind to another, with only soft strings under it.
- Here Fl 1 (37–40) hands over to the C.a. (41–44), then Ob 1 with the cellos (45–47), then the C.a. quotation (48–50), over pizz. strings, harp and held Vln II. This is the setting Rimsky calls the most favourable for a solo (PDF 138, Ex. 277 and 285).
- The doublings are confined to single phrases (PDF 141).
- The violas keep out of the quotation's rhythm (PDF 137).
- Nothing to change.
