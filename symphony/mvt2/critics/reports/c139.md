## What else I studied

I compared the score with Brahms 4/ii from memory of the score, checked against program notes: [Cleveland Orchestra](https://www.clevelandorchestra.com/works/brahms-symphony-no-4), [Apple Classical work note](https://classical.music.apple.com/ai/work/johannes-brahms-1833-pp4), [Christopher Weeks](https://christopher-weeks.com/10-4th-symphony/). The corpus has no Brahms 4. I diffed v129 against v139 section by section. I viewed all 8 pages of score.pdf and ran allpairs_par.py; it reports only intended doublings. I also scanned double stops and paired staves by hand at 61–64 and 79–82.

**Brahms 4/ii as a measure.** Brahms opens with horns and winds in a modal call. He then gives the theme to clarinets over pizzicato strings (Rimsky principle 4). He reserves the full string body for the warm second subject, and he changes the colour of every return. This score does the same kinds of things. The horn solo over winds and harp at 7–14 is the counterpart of Brahms's clarinets. Pizzicato bass, harp and violas support the woodwind solos at 37–44 as Brahms's strings support his clarinets. The violins hold the warmth back until 23 and 53, and the A′ reprise is recoloured. A conductor would accept this as a craftsman's slow movement. Two differences remain, and both are protected earlier decisions, so I do not reopen them. First, Brahms's movement is about twice as long and develops in long paragraphs. Second, his second theme returns at the climax, while here theme B is held back for movement III (c100). Where this score falls short of Brahms is in the small joints. Brahms's colour changes overlap: no instrument hands over a melody without a common note, and the accompaniment never swells against a soloist. The findings below are all joints of this kind.

**Compared with v129.** Ten cycles of change: niente circles, Vln II and cello swells (c138), the harp revoicing at 53–56, the viola changes at 50, 52 and 59, Tbn 2 at 60, Cbsn D♭1 at 61, Bsn 1 at 78, cues and part labels. None of these weakens the score except one: the c138 Vln II swells under the solo violin (Finding 2). Everything else is neutral or better, and the layout is still 8 pages.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 36–37: theme B's first rising fourth is split between two instruments (applied)

The cor anglais plays the pickup C5 at 36 b3 and stops. Flute 1 starts theme B on F5 at 37 with no common note. At 44–45 (oboe) and 52–53 (violins), the same instrument plays both the pickup and the head. Only the first and most important statement has its identifying interval broken across two timbres with a gap in between.

Rimsky PDF 125–126: "In order to connect the phrases on each instrument in the best possible way, the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one." Ex. 237–238 show this hand-over. Brahms 4/ii hands its opening from horns to clarinets in the same way, overlapping and never across a gap.

Fix (applied; 8 pages, 0 warnings): the cor anglais slurs C5 to F5 and plays F5 as a quarter in unison with flute 1's first note.
```
% trans.ily
transCorAnglais = { ... | R2. | r2 c''4(\p | }
% bSec.ily
bSecCorAnglais = {
  f''4) r4 r2 | R1*3 |
```
This adds a cor anglais staff to the 37–40 system. Page 2 still fits. The earlier alternative was to restore the flute's own pickup (transFluteOneX, dropped at c072). That adds a flute staff to the 31–36 system and is not needed.

### 2. Bars 17–18 and 21: Vln II swells under the solo violin (applied; partly revises c138)

c138 gave the muted Vln II a hairpin in every bar from 15 to 21, copying the shape of the oboe and the solo violin. Under the oboe (15–16, 19–20) this is harmless, because the two timbres differ. Under the solo violin (17–18, 21), a full section of the same family rises together with one player. That player has the escape tones G5 at 17 and D5 at 21, which are protected (c091). This is the one change since v129 that is a step back.

Rimsky PDF 123: "The comparatively weak tone of the solo instrument necessitates light, transparent accompaniment." PDF 132–133: when the melody's non-chord tones meet the harmonic basis, the remedy is difference of timbre, and failing that the basis "should be of inferior dynamic power". Ex. 222 shows Vln I con sord. under the solo violin as an even pp, and Ex. 223 shows a solo violin over plain held horn chords. Neither accompaniment has swells.

Fix (applied): remove Vln II's hairpins at 17, 18 and 21. Keep those at 15–16 and 19–20.
```
aes'2--(\pp\< aes'4--) | aes'2--(\> g'4--\!) | aes'2--( aes'4--) | aes'2--( f'4--) |
aes'2--(\< g'4--) | aes'2--(\> e'4--\!) | f'2--( f'4--) | bes2 es'8(\mp ...
```

### 3. Bars 14–15: hard seam from the wind/harp group to the muted strings (not applied; layout)

At the end of 14, horn 1, the bassoons, the bass clarinet and the harp all stop together. The muted strings start at 15 b1, so the colour change falls exactly on the barline. At 30–31 the composer already overlaps the change (c063, Cbsn pedal), and Rimsky shows the device in Ex. 249 (PDF 415) and Ex. 247 (PDF 413): the outgoing wind chord is held to the downbeat on which the muted strings enter and then released ("mor."). The cellos' first note at 15 is C3, which is bassoon 2's note at 14, so the hand-over can be an exact unison (PDF 125–126).

Fix:
```
aOneBsnTwo: ... | es2.( | c2.~ |
  \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t c4)\> r4\! r4 | R2.*15 |
```
I tested it. It builds clean, but it adds the bassoon staff to the 15–22 system and the score goes to 9 pages. I took it out of the scratch copy. Adopt it only if the composer can find the space, for example with a bassoon cue that is not printed in the score, or with tighter staff spacing on page 2. Otherwise leave it.

### 4. No other changes

Every other principle in the table is met. The crescendo and diminuendo orders, the diverging build to 61, the sf> string tremolo under the trombone quotation, the slurred grace glide into B♭6, and the converging coda on Vln I's held C5 are textbook cases of PDF 129–132. Points I considered and set aside because they are protected:
- The register gap at 83–84 (harp harmonics over the A♭ bass until the violas enter at 84 b3; Rimsky PDF 125). This is the c124 decision.
- The cor anglais above the bass clarinet at 31–36 (recorded at c129).
- Theme B's return at the climax, the Brahms model (closed at c100).
