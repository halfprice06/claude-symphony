## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 18, 22, 29–30, 70, 85: five voices play at LilyPond's default MIDI volume, against the printed dynamic (applied)

**Problem.** When a voice has no dynamic of its own, LilyPond plays it at the default volume 0.71, which is louder than this score's ff (0.72 is ff, and 0.71 is reached before the instrument equalizer). This happens to divisi voices created with `<< { } \\ { } >>`, to part-combined voices whose dynamic is printed only in the part, and to a niente hairpin that has no start dynamic. score.midi (velocities):
- 18 and 22, cello divisi: 79 against the cellos' pp of 50 in the bars around them. In bar 18 that is louder than the oboe and as loud as the solo violin.
- 29–30, Hn 3's D♭4–C4: 66 against Hn 4's pp at 37 on the same staff. The pp is tagged part-only (c099).
- 70, Bsn 1·2 (combined to one voice at 70): 53 against their own pp of 25 at 68–69. They sound louder than horn 1's B♭3, which ends the motto tail at 44.
- 85, Cl 1's first note of the movement's last motto tail: 56. That is the loudest note in the coda (strings 31–57). It then drops to 29 at 86. The score asks for the reverse: niente growing to pp.

The printed score is correct at all five places. The rendering contradicts it. Two of the places are cyclic statements: the tail that ends the retransition (67–70) and the movement's last statement of the tail (85–89).

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 240: "Do the voicings sound the way they were intended to? If not, why not?" PDF 241: compare the score with the recorded performance and find the cause of each weak spot. PDF 13–14: the listener's ear must go to the primary element. At 85 and 70 the rendering puts an attack or an inner part above it. The cause here is mechanical, not musical.

**Answer to c154's reason.** c154 rejected a MIDI-only horn dynamic at 80–81 because the printed score is correct. These marks add no expression that is not already printed. Each one gives a voice the dynamic that is printed for it, which LilyPond drops when it splits or combines voices. The mp3 is the only performance anyone in this project hears (PDF 241).

**Fix (applied; invisible in score and parts):**
```
% aOne.ily, aOneVc, 18 and 22
<< { c'8(-\tweak stencil ##f \pp^\markup \italic "div." es'8 c'4 des'4) } \\ { c2-\tweak stencil ##f \pp bes,4 } >>
<< { g8(-\tweak stencil ##f \pp^\markup \italic "div." aes8 bes4 g4) } \\ { es2.-\tweak stencil ##f \pp } >>
% aOne.ily, aOneHnThree, 29
r2 des'4-\tag #'part \pp -\tag #'score -\tweak stencil ##f \pp |
% retr.ily, 70
retrBsnOne: ... | g2.) | g2-\tweak stencil ##f \pp r4 | }
retrBsnTwo = { R2.*4 | des2.\pp | des2-\tweak stencil ##f \pp r4 | }
% coda.ily, codaClOne, 85
\after 2 \pp es''2.(-\tweak stencil ##f \ppp \<^\markup \italic "dolce" |
```
New velocities: cellos 50 at 18 and 22, Hn 3 37 at 29–30, bassoons 25 at 70, Cl 1 22 → 29 at 85–86. The PDF is identical except at 85, where the niente circle of Cl 1's hairpin starts about 0.3 mm further right (still under the notehead). analysis.txt and the allpairs output are unchanged.

### 2. For movement III: the recall of theme B runs 2.4 times faster than theme B (relay; no change in II)

**Problem.** Movement III (v11) recalls theme B in the trumpets at 211–214, "note for note as in movement II", with the same note values: `aes'4 des''4 f''4. ges''8 | aes''4 bes''8 aes''8 ges''4. f''8 | ...`. III marks 211 "Largamente" at half note = 72, which is quarter note = 144. In II the same notes run at quarter note = 60 (41–43) and 60–64 (57–59). The four-bar recall lasts about 6.7 s, against 16 s in II. At that speed the recall is a diminution of theme B, not a return of it.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 11: when a melodic idea comes back, it should be recognisable as the idea already heard; that recognition is what gives an arrangement unity. Harmonic change is welcome (PDF 19), and III's new harmony (I | IV–V | N6) follows Ex. 3/3A. PDF 240: compare the intention ("theme B's single recall") with the result.

**Options for III's critics** (III is read-only for me):
- (a) Double the note values at 211–218 (`aes'2 des''2 | f''2. ges''4 | aes''2 bes''4 aes''4 | ges''2. f''4 | es''2. f''4 | des''1~ | des''1`). This gives quarter note ≈ 72, close to II's 60–64, but it needs four more bars before the German sixth at 215.
- (b) Keep the bars and mark 211–214 "Largamente (♩ = 72)" in four, then return to the half-note beat at 215.

Movement II needs no change.

### 3. The default instrument equalizer biases the mock-up against the winds (tested; not applied)

**Problem.** LilyPond's built-in `instrumentEqualizer` (midi-init.ly) scales flute, oboe and clarinet to 0–0.7 of the printed level, horn to 0.1–0.7, bassoon to 0–0.6 and viola to 0.1–0.7. It does not scale this score's cor anglais ("english horn"), violins ("string ensemble 1"), trombones, tuba or harp. So identical printed dynamics come out unequal:
- 15–18: the oboe's phrase (p<) peaks at 57; the solo violin's answer, with the same marks, peaks at 81.
- 37: the flute's theme B at p (37) is under the Vln II pp chords (41).
- 87–89: Cl 1's D♭5–C5, the motto's last step, is at 29 under the C.a.'s inner line and Vln II at 41, with all three printed pp.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13–14 (focus) and PDF 240–241 (does the result sound as intended?).

**Test.** In score.ly's `\midi` Score context, `instrumentEqualizer = #(lambda (s) #f)` makes every staff play its printed level. With that change the oboe/violin dialogue matches (53→81 in both), and the flute leads at 37. The PDF is unchanged and the build is clean. But it also lets hairpins without an end dynamic overshoot in the winds as they already do in the strings. For example, Hn 3's `fes'4~\<` at 80 reaches 117, above the tutti; that is c154's finding 4. I did not apply it. If the composer wants it, the open hairpins in the winds need end targets first (at least Hn 3 at 80). It should also be decided for all three movements together. Movements I and III use the same default.

### 4. Which form of theme B III recalls (recorded; no change)

III recalls bar 42's form, with the B♭ turn (`aes4 bes8 aes8 ges4~ ges8 f8`), which II plays once, in the C.a. at 41–42. The climax form at 57–58 (`aes4. ges8~ ges4~ ges8 f8`, c110 and c151) is louder and on top. Under Sebesky's focus principle (PDF 13–14) the C.a. at 41–44 is the primary voice: the flute is marked a level under it (c101). The head A♭–D♭–F is the same in every statement. The recall therefore points at a real foreground statement. Keep bar 58 as of v152.

### 5. The cor anglais plays theme B just before it quotes movement I (considered; keep)

Sebesky PDF 11 ("Time and Love") warns that a colour heard just before an important entrance loses its surprise. The C.a. plays theme B at 41–44 and then the S-head quotation at 47 b4. Three things answer this. Oboe 1 and the cellos take 45–47 between the two C.a. entries. The key turns to F♯ minor, with poco rit. and poco sostenuto. And the C.a. is movement I's colour for the motto answer (I 5–8, 171–174), so the colour itself is the memory (PDF 241–242, Rochberg). Both passages are protected (c041, cycle 130). No change.
