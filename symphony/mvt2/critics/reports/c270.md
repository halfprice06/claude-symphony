## Findings

### 1. Bar 5: the muta "G in D♭" is out of range; replace it with a chain (APPLIED in scratch)

**Problem.** Movement I's tuning is G2, A♭2, C3, E♭3. Its own retunings fix which drum is which: the A♭ drum goes to B♭2 (I 34) and to B♮2 (I 84); both are above the 30" drum's top A2, so the A♭ drum is the 28". The G2 is therefore on the 30", the C3 on the 25" and the E♭3 on the 23" (Adler Ex. XII-46, PDF 79). Our bar 5 sends the G drum (30") to D♭3, a fourth above its top note. On the 28" it would still be a semitone too high (top C3). D♭3 is a semitone below the 23" drum's lowest D3. Only the 25" can play D♭3, and that drum holds the C3 we need at 84. Casanelles states the rule the bar breaks: an orchestral instrument is a fixed framework whose player produces "the pitches within the register of the instrument" (PDF 223, also 205), and the orchestra's balance depends on keeping its established principles (PDF 235–236, 239).

**Second problem, at the scherzo join.** Under the old muta the A♭ stays on the 28". The join text at 92, "muta A♭ in E, C in G, D♭ in B, E♭ in E", and the scherzo's "retune in the pause: A♭ → E, C → G, D♭ → B, E♭ → E" then send the 28" down to E2, below its F2. The names in both labels are correct only if the A♭ is on the 30". The chain below puts it there.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-270/work/sections/intro.ily, bar 5).**

```
R2.^\markup \small \column { "muta G in A♭, A♭ in C, C in D♭" "(A♭ C D♭ E♭, low to high)" } | R2. |
```
