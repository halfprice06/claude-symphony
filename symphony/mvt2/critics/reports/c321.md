## Findings

After 320 cycles, the melodic lines meet every principle in this reading that applies to them. No finding changes a note. Findings 1–3 record what I checked, so later critics on this lens do not reopen these points without new evidence. Finding 4 is a comment-only correction, which I applied and tested.

### 1. The B♭ appoggiatura is stressed by register, doubling and dynamic at each statement. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 12, 28, 76, 79.

**Check:** Adler PDF 23 asks which melody notes are stressed, why, and how they are made to dominate. The B♭ appoggiatura to A♭ is theme A's hook. Its rise and its fall to the motto tail are what a listener will hum afterwards. Each statement gives it more weight in a planned order:
- At 12 it is the horn's highest note, B♭4 at mp, at the peak of the bar 11 crescendo. The horn is the only melodic voice, and the harp at 11–12 has no note above the horn (c019, c057).
- At 28 it is B♭5 in Vln I and Vln II at f.
- At 76 it is held back to B♭5 (c299).
- At 79 it reaches B♭6 at ff in three octaves (Fl 1 + Vln I, B♭6; Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II, B♭5; Vla, B♭4).

The counter-melody under 79 (Hn 1·2 a2 at f, with Vc and Bsn 1) moves D♭4–C4–B♭3. It falls while the tune holds, so the B♭6 stands alone at the top. This is the Elgar model (PDF 24): the peak is built from octave and double-octave doublings and is not thickened in the middle.

### 2. Schuller's warning at 61–62 still describes a harmony voice, not the melody. (Recorded, no change; protected)

**Bars:** 61–62, Hn 3·4 a2.

**Check:** Schuller (Adler PDF 26) says two horns in unison, high and loud, crack. His remedies are horn 1 or horn 3 alone, or a trumpet added to the two horns. Since c264, horns 1 and 2 are divided below, so 3·4 is the only unison pair. It plays concert A♭4–B♭4–C♭5 (written E♭5–F5–G♭5) at f in tenuto half notes. That is upper-middle register, not the high ff that Schuller describes. The line is a chord voice: theme B's augmented head is in Fl 2, Vln II and Vc in three octaves, with Fl 1 and Vln I above (Finding 4). The a2 was settled after c064, c069 and c086. The reason given then still holds: with trumpets tacet, a single horn 3 would carry the brass top line at half a trombone's weight. Schuller's trumpet remedy conflicts with the trumpets-tacet decision, which keeps the brass's first forte major chord for the finale. I do not re-raise it.

### 3. Horn with cello on the counter-melodies follows the Strauss model. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 57–60, 75–82.

**Check:** Adler PDF 27 says horn and cello usually double each other on long sustained melodies, and that staggered horn entries give the line "personality". At 57–60, Hn 1 plays the counter-line with Bsn 1 and the cellos. At 75–82, Hn 2 joins the cellos at 75 (p< mp, one level under them, c217), and Hn 1 joins at 78 b3 for the a2 at 79. The horn colour is therefore added in two stages, as Strauss adds his four horns one by one. At 23–30 the cellos carry the counter-melody alone, which keeps the first string statement plain (protected). The design is the one the reading teaches.

### Comparison with the models

Theme A follows the plan of Dvořák's Largo tune. Its head rises by leap to a held note and then falls by step, and its chromatic tail (E♭–D–D♭–C) is the part a listener keeps. The movement uses that tail as its last melodic events: horn at 67–70, clarinet at 85–89. Dvořák likewise ends his Largo on a fragment of the tune. The horn statement at 7–14 has the setting of Tchaikovsky 5/ii's horn solo: a middle-register horn line over a soft, low wind bed. Adler's Hindemith passage (PDF 20) explains why it works: the line stays dark because it never climbs into the bright register. Theme B's stepwise first phrase and its yearning rise to A♭6 at 58 were closed at c291. I found nothing in the melodic lines that this reading would change.
