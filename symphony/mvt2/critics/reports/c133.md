## Findings

### 1. Bar 61: the climax root is higher than the bass on both sides of it (applied)

**Problem.** In the build, the bass descends into the lowest octave: A♭1 at 58, G♭1–G1 at 59, A♭1 through 60. Then, on the downbeat that the whole middle section aims at, every bass instrument plays D♭2 (contrabassoon, tuba and basses; bass trombone, bass clarinet and bassoon 2 an octave higher). Bar 62 drops back to G♭1 and F♭1. So the goal chord of the movement's largest motion has a higher bass than the dominant before it and the chords after it. The low step A♭1→D♭1, the strongest descending-5th close, is never heard. The climax is marked by dynamics and by the full voicing, but not by register.

**Evidence.** A&S 16 §1 (PDF 250, Ex. 16-1, PDF 249): in a 5th chain, the chords that carry the motion are marked by "low bass register" and, at the cadential end, by "bass register, increased rhythmic activity". 16 §8 (PDF 257): a long stepwise bass is an expanded passing motion toward its goal. Here G♭1–G1–A♭1 runs in the lowest octave, and its goal, D♭, belongs in that octave as well. In Rimsky-Korsakov's tutti tables (c114 row 2), the root sits in the low octave. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 68: the double bassoon is "doubled by bassoon, clarinet or bass clarinet an octave higher", which this chord already has. PDF 38: the double bassoon's "very deep notes … are remarkably thick and dense". The basses cannot play D♭1 (their lowest note in this score is E1), and the tuba should not: Adler part 2 PDF 7, Ex. X-85, gives E1 as its lowest note. The contrabassoon reaches B♭0.

**Fix (applied).** One note, contrabassoon only:
```
% bSec.ily
bSecCbsn = {
  R1*24 |
  des,,1--\ff | ges,,2-- fes,,2-- | R1*2 |
}
```
The contrabassoon now steps from D♭1 up a 4th to G♭1 at 62, while the others fall from D♭2. Its line becomes D♭1–G♭1–F♭1 and moves in contrary motion to the bass trombone and bassoon 2, so three parallel-octave pairs at 62 b1 disappear and none are added. This does not undo c114 ("tutti chord 61–62 and brass dynamics"). No brass part, layout or dynamic changes. The voicing gains one octave below the existing D♭2, which the table c114 cites asks for, and the tutti chord gains a deeper root.

### 2. Every 6/4 in the score has a function; record the list (no change)

### 3. The prolongations at 62–65 and 78 match Unit 16; record them (no change)

- 62 b3–65: F♭/E is the upper neighbour to the structural V (E♭). A minor (63–64) is the chord between them (16 §10, PDF 258: "over longer spans … VI as upper neighbour to V"). The E→E♭ slip is therefore the neighbour's return, not a modulation, and this supports keeping the protected 61–65 route.
- 78 b3: the chord over E♭ is E♭–G–C, a 6/3 over a bass passing tone between F♭ and D♭ (16 §8, PDF 256: "6/3 or 6/4 chords are often preferable to 5/3s as passing chords"). It is not a V. Clarinet 2's A♭→G and the violins' C→B♭ at 79 are the voice leading of that passing chord. A later critic should not "correct" it to a V7.
- 44–48: the B section reaches the quotation's key by descending 3rds, V (44) – vi (45) – iv (48). This is the goal-directed I–VI–IV type (16 §3, PDF 251), so F♯ minor arrives as a goal and needs no bridge.
