- `susp.py` lists every note held into a half beat where it is dissonant against the lowest sounding pitch, with its next note and any other voice that sounds that next pitch within two octaves.
- `slice.py` prints every sounding pitch by instrument at each beat.
- `antic.py` lists every off-beat note repeated on the next beat in the same voice.

I read Dvořák 9/ii in the corpus (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/woods.notesL.ily`, the English horn tune, bars 7–14) for Finding 1.

On memorability, judged by the reading. Theme A is the tune a listener keeps. Its head is the Ex. 21-1 device: the `4. 8` arpeggio lands on a note tied across the barline, so the strong beat of the next bar is a held tone, not a new attack. At 7–8 that held tone becomes the 7th of vi7 and resolves down by step. The motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C then implies a 7th chain (Ex. 21-30). The second phrase repeats the tie on a consonant F (11–12), leaps to the B♭ and comes down the scale. Rhythm, the tie and the falling resolution make one cell, and the cell comes back four times (7, 11, 23, 27) and twice more at 71 and 75. Rhythmically, theme B is the weaker of the two. Its first phrase has no tie (closed: c131, c141, c291), and the suspensions are in the second phrase (42, 58).

## Findings

### 1. Neither theme uses the anticipation, the second device of the reading; the model's tune is built on it (recorded; no change recommended)

**Bars.** Themes A and B everywhere; cadences 13–14, 29–30, 81–82, 43–44, 59–60.

**Problem.** The anticipation is described as "the opposite of the suspension". It is placed in the soprano "at cadences or other places where the next melodic tone is highly predictable", and bringing that tone in early "can create a slight surprise and add to the listener's interest" (PDF 363). Ex. 21-33 and 21-35 show restruck and tied forms, both at cadences (PDF 364). `antic.py` finds no restruck anticipation in any melodic voice of v311. The only melodic anticipation is the tied G♭ at 58 b2½ (c151). Every cadence of theme A (14, 30, 82) and of theme B (40, 44, 56, 60) arrives on its main beat.

**Evidence.** The main model's tune uses this figure in five of its first eight bars. The English horn at Dvořák 9/ii bar 7 is written `c8.( ef16 ef4 c8. bf16 af4)`, which sounds F–A♭–A♭–F–E♭–D♭. The A♭ arrives on the last sixteenth of beat 1 and is restruck on beat 2, which is the restruck anticipation of Ex. 21-33. The same figure is in bars 9, 11 and 13. The phrase cadence at bar 10 (`bf8( c bf8. af16 af2)`, sounding E♭–F–E♭–D♭–D♭) is the cadential anticipation of PDF 363. This dotted snap is the rhythm a listener remembers from the Largo. Theme A gets its rhythmic identity from the other device in the unit, the tie across the barline (Ex. 21-1), and does not need Dvořák's figure as well.

**Why no change.** The place where an anticipation belongs by the reading is the soprano at a cadence. Those notes are protected: theme A's line and cadences (cycle 12, c061, the horn solo 7–14 in the standing list, the climax 79–82), theme B's line (c141), and the F–F♭–E♭ echo in the coda. They are also exported themes, so any change would have to be reported to the other movements. Critic 151 tested the idea at 13–14 and kept the stepwise G–E♭–D♭–C–B♭–A♭ descent, which I agree with. The only unprotected candidate is the C.a. at 43 b4 (`des'4 bes8. c'16 | c'2.`). It would anticipate a consonant C within one V harmony, as Dvořák does in his bar 10. I did not apply it, because 43 is inside theme B's protected line and the same change would have to go to 59 under the protected build. This is recorded so that a later melody cycle does not raise it again without answering c141.

### 2. Bars 41 b3–4 and 57 b3–4: the melody's F sounds against G♭ in an inner voice and then rises to G♭ (checked; acceptable)

**Bars.** 41 (C.a. F4 against violas G♭3), 57 (Fl 1/Vln I F6 and Ob 1/Vln II F5 against Hn 1 G♭4, with B.cl., Bsn 1 and Vc on G♭3).

**Problem.** `susp.py` flags these two places. The melody holds F for a beat and a half over the G♭ bass (IVmaj7). The note it moves to, G♭, already sounds in an inner voice, a major 7th below at 57 (Hn 1 G♭4 under the oboe's F5) and a 9th below at 41. Ex. 21-12a, b and c mark the tone of resolution in another voice "poor" (PDF 352).

**Evidence.** The rule in §12 is about suspensions. At 41 and 57 the F is struck together with the G♭ bass (`slice.py`: 57.3 every voice attacks at once), so it is an accented 7th of the chord, not a held tone. It rises a half step to the root of the chord it belongs to. That is the 7-8 of Ex. 21-24a, with F acting as the local leading tone of G♭ (§19, PDF 358). At 57 the inner G♭ is in the horn and low reeds, a different timbre, one octave and more below the double-reed and violin melody. That is the "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics" in which the reading calls the doubling "much less problematic" (PDF 352). The first statement at 41 has the same figure, so the two statements match. Removing Hn 1's G♭4 at 57 would break the protected horn counter-line (c160, c201). No change.

### 3. Theme A's suspensions are clean at all six statements of the tie (follows; record)

**Bars.** 7–8, 11–12, 23–24, 27–28, 71–72, 75–76.

**Evidence.** `susp.py` finds the E♭ held over the F bass at 8, 24 and 72 (a 7th of vi7) resolving to D on b2. No other voice sounds D before b2 (PDF 352, §12). The resolution falls on the weaker beat (PDF 347). At 12, 28 and 76 the tied F is a consonant 3rd over D♭, so it is free to leap (PDF 348, last line of §3). At 12 b3 the horn's A♭4 is held into the 7th of ii7 and falls to G at 13 (PDF 353–354, §15). This is the Ex. 21-1 shape, and I recommend no change.

### 4. Bar 58 keeps c151's suspension chain, and 42 keeps its double suspension (follows; record)

**Bars.** 42 b3–4, 58.

**Evidence.** At 58 the melody `aes4. ges8~ ges4~ ges8 f8` holds one G♭ through three functions: the consonant 3rd of ii7 at b2½, the prepared 7th of V7 at b3, and the 4 of a 4-3 over D♭ at b4 (PDF 347, 353–354, 357–358). Hn 1 and the cellos hold E♭ through b4 and rise to F with the resolution, a 9-10 beside the 4-3 (PDF 356–358, Ex. 21-21, 21-24). `susp.py` shows no voice sounding F before 58 b4½ and no voice sounding F at 42 b4. The c198 hairpins put the accent on the suspension (PDF 348). Nothing has changed here since v152.

### 5. Bar 81: the 7th's resolution is transferred, and the counter-line's D♭ sits under the melody's passing C (follows; record)

**Bars.** 81.

**Evidence.** Cl 2 holds D♭5, the 7th of V♭9, into 81 b1 (c292) and then leaps to G4. Hn 1·2, Bsn 1 and the cellos carry D♭4 from 81 b2 and resolve it to C4 at 82. That is the transferred resolution of Ex. 21-28 (PDF 360–361). At 81 b3 the melody's passing C5/C6 sounds above the held D♭4. This is a passing tone in the falling scale, not a resolution sounded early, and it is the theme's own cadence figure at 13 and 29. The register and timbre contrast meets the condition on PDF 352. No change.

### 6. The pedal plan matches §28 (follows; record)

**Bars.** 1–6, 30–36, 65–70, 83–92.

**Evidence.** There are tonic pedals at the start of the movement (1–6), after the A section (30–36) and at the end (83–86, 89–92). The dominant pedal 65–70 is placed "before a tonic that comes at a recapitulation" (PDF 367). Dissonances against the bass are free: the common-tone diminished seventh at 33 and the horn's D4 over E♭ at 68. Dissonances between upper parts are prepared and resolved. At 86, Cl D5 against Vla C4 comes by step from E♭5 and leads to IV6 at 87. That pedal leads into another chord, as in Ex. 21-42 (PDF 368). The pedal plan is protected (c223), and I recommend no change.
