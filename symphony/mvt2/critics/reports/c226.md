## Findings

### 1. Bar 89: clarinet 2 enters under a note that is already sounding; give it the niente attack (applied)

**Problem.** At 89 clarinet 2 enters on E♭4 (written F4) for the morendo. The cor anglais is still holding that pitch (c150: the cor anglais resolves F♭4→E♭4 at 89 and releases at 90). In the score the entry has no marking at all. The part shows a plain pp, which means a tongued attack. On a unison already sounding, a tongued pp start is the one moment in the hand-over where an attack can be heard.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 211: the niente attack, "in which the tone starts from almost complete silence without articulation", is the effect the clarinet does "better than any other woodwind instrument" (Ex. VII-57: N < p > N, a circled-tip hairpin). The score already uses it for clarinet 1 at 85 (c041, c160). PDF 210: the clarinet keeps its full dynamic control at the bottom of the chalumeau. Written F4 is the top note of that register in Ex. VII-56, so the instrument can do this. Rimsky-Korsakov's rule for a transferred phrase supports the same seam: the new part's first note coincides with the old part's last (PDF 125–126, cited by c150).

**Fix (applied, coda.ily, `codaClTwo`).**
```
codaClTwo = { R2.*6 | es'2.~-\tweak stencil ##f \ppp _\tweak circled-tip ##t \< | \after 2 \! es'2.~-\tag #'part \pp \> |
  es'2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | es'2.\ppp\fermata | }
```
The circled-tip hairpin is drawn below the staff from 89 to 90. It ends where both clarinets start their shared diminuendo at 90. The hidden ppp gives MIDI a start level, as c160 did for clarinet 1 at 85. The part keeps pp, now at the end of the hairpin. Clarinet 1 has no new dynamic at 89 and stays at pp, and the two diminuendi at 90 still coincide, so the shared staff is unchanged apart from the one hairpin. Pitches and rhythm are unchanged. This touches the ending, but not the plagal close's bass or top line, and not the last chord, so the scherzo thread does not need to be told.

### 2. The bass clarinet never crosses its break; c126's "slur over the break 35–36" no longer applies (recorded, no change)

c126 kept "bass clarinet slur over the break 35–36". It wrote that when the solo ended on concert A♭3 (written B♭4). c140 and c187 then lowered the solo an octave. Today 35–36 read A♭2–B♭2–C3, written B♭3–C4–D4, which is low chalumeau and nowhere near the break. The whole bass clarinet part runs from concert B♭1 to F♯3 (analysis.txt), written C3–G♯4. That is exactly Adler's chalumeau, "the first octave and a third … the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective sound" (PDF 217). The HISTORY "kept" entry should be closed as obsolete. That way no later critic re-raises a break crossing that no longer exists.

### 3. Bar 8: bass clarinet low C (concert B♭1) (recorded, no change)

Adler PDF 216: "writing all the way to C is still risky". The part's title says "(low C)" and it prints the ossia "(no low C: 8va)". On a low-E♭ instrument the ossia plays B♭2, a unison with bassoon 2. The harp's left hand, 8 bassa, still sounds the low B♭ on the beat, so the bass is not lost. This follows the reading as far as a part can. I do not propose a change, because bar 8's motto harmony is protected.

### 4. Clarinet break crossings at soft dynamics (recorded, not re-raised)

- Cl 1 at 53–56: concert F4→B♭4 and B♭4→G♭4, written G4→C5 and C5→A♭4, slurred, at pp<.
- Cl 2 at 57–58: B♭4–G♭4–A♭4–C♭5, tongued, mp<.
- Cl 2 at 71–79: the open throat A♭4 (written B♭4) with slurred crossings at 72 and 76.

Adler PDF 210 says the break troubles "all but the most expert player" and in the end presents "no difficulties whatsoever". c126, c144 and c178 kept all of these as playable for a professional section. The throat A♭4 is a deliberate pale inner colour. I have no new reason to reopen them.

### 5. Bassoon and contrabassoon register checks (recorded, no change)

- No bassoon note in the lowest fifth (B♭1–F2) is soft. The only such note, Bsn 2's F♭2 at 62, is ff (PDF 226).
- No bassoon line reaches the weak top fifth, and every soft bassoon line lies in the "sweet, more subdued, but expressive" register of Ex. VII-89.
- The contrabassoon stays in its lowest twelfth (B♭0–A♭1 sounding), doubles the bass an octave lower, has long rests between its three entries, and has breaths at 33 and 80 (PDF 230). Its B♭0 at 80 is a ff quarter between D♭1 and E♭1, which suits its slow speech at the bottom.
