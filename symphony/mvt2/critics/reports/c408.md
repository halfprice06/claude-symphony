## Findings

### 1. Bar 78 b3: the timpani re-enter p<; Rimsky's re-entry rule and the movement's own bar 60 call for pp< (applied)

**Bars.** 78 b3, timpani E♭3 roll into the f roll on D♭ at 79.

**Problem.** The timpani have rested since 70 (eight bars) and re-enter at 78 b3 with a one-beat roll marked p<. Everywhere else in the movement, after a rest, the timpani enter ppp, pp or f (2, 52, 60, 67, 79, 84). Bar 60 is the same device before the first climax (an A♭ roll crescendo into the tutti), and c358 changed it from p< to pp< for exactly this reason. Bar 78 was added at c397 with p, citing Adler's "piano and then crescendo", after the expression passes on this reading had already run (c388). No later expression pass has checked it.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136: after a long rest, re-entries should be pp or ff; p and f are "less successful", and "this remark is capable of wider application". PDF 130: a crescendo in the brass and drum chord turns into a sforzando climax, so it should start from the bottom to have room to rise. PDF 129 (cresc.-sf): the accent comes at the end of the swell, which here is the f roll at 79. HISTORY c349 and c358 already rank PDF 136 above the Adler reading for a crescendo start, and bar 60 now reads pp<. Adler PDF 184 asks only for a soft start and a crescendo, and pp satisfies that.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-408/work/sections/aTwo.ily, `aTwoTimp`):**
```
r2 es4:32\pp\< | des2.:32\f^\markup \small \italic "ord." | ...
```
(was `r2 es4:32\p\< |`). This changes one printed character. The roll, its hairpin, the f at 79, the drum tuning and the "medium sticks" at 77 are unchanged. The roll covers one beat (about 1.2 s at ♩=50), and pp to f in that time gives the steeper swell into 79 that Rimsky describes. It sits on E♭3, far below the violins' run, so it cannot cover the melody. The comment should cite c408 and Rimsky PDF 136 alongside c397.

### 2. Bar 47: the sf-p attack uses single string notes, not a compound chord (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 47 b1, the C♯7 goal chord before the cor anglais quotation.

**Point.** Rimsky PDF 129 (a) prefers the strings' sf attack on the woodwind's p chord to be "a compound chord for preference". Here Vln II, Vla and Cb play single notes fp> with the bassoons' p chord and Ob 1's mf. A double stop in Vln II would strengthen the attack. I do not recommend it. The book says "for preference", not as a rule. The fp is already marked and rendered (c388). A double stop would add a doubling in the same register as the oboe's C♯5 and the cellos' mp line just as the quotation (protected, 47-50) starts. The effect the book wants, a string accent that dies away under a soft wind chord, is already present.

### 3. Bars 81-83: the release follows Ex. 259 in order, though in less time (recorded, no change)

Ex. 259 (PDF 425-426) takes the winds out one by one over four bars, each marked "smorz.", while the strings stay and divide. Here the order is the book's (horns and timpani at the end of 81; Hn 1·2, oboes, cor anglais, contrabassoon, Vln II and Vla at 82 b2; flutes, clarinets, bass clarinet, bassoons, Vln I and Vc at the barline; basses and cellos continue into 83). The release takes about two bars, not four, because the coda's a tempo at 83 follows. The release is protected (c186, c218), so I leave it.
