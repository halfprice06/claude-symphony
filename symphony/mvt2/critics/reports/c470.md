## Findings

### 1. Bar 92: the violins and violas end muted, but nothing tells them to take the mutes off (applied in the scratch copy)
**Problem.** Since v469 (c468), Vln I, Vln II and violas play con sord. from 84 to the end. The horns, the only other muted instruments, get "via sord." at 91, so movement II removes their mutes itself. The clarinets ("muta in A") and the timpani (muta at 92) are also told in movement II what to change in the pause. The strings get no instruction. Their mutes would carry into the scherzo unless that score happens to mark "senza sord." at its first string entry. The scherzo now opens with Vln I G♯5 ppp at its bars 1-2 (finale.md, scherzo v431 and later). The checkout's scherzo.md does not record c468's request. Muted violins on that held G♯5 would change the scherzo's opening sound.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 60 says the mute change must be given its time, "otherwise, chaos may result". A&S PDF 494-495 (§14) shows why this join matters. Movement II's place between I and III depends on smooth joins: the common-tone link at 1 and the A♭ = G♯ hand-over at 92. A colour left over from II would blur the scherzo's contrasting start, which cyclic-notes line 183 asks for ("a contrasting attack after a pause").
**Fix (applied).** I added part-only text after the last note, in the same form as Clarinet 2's muta (c265), in sections/coda.ily (codaVlnOne, codaVlnTwo, codaVla):
```
c''2.\downbow\ppp\fermata \tag #'part { \textEndMark \markup \small \italic "via sord." } |
aes'2.\ppp\fermata \tag #'part { \textEndMark \markup \small \italic "via sord." } |
<es aes>2.\ppp\fermata \tag #'part { \textEndMark \markup \small \italic "via sord." } |
```
The score is unchanged and keeps 8 pages. A two-line version ("(for the Scherzo, in the pause)") or a version below the staff made LilyPond compress the Vln I part by 0.3 staff-spaces, so I kept one line above the staff, which fits cleanly. **Report to the scherzo thread.** This is text at the movement's ending, not a pitch change. The scherzo should still print "senza sord." at its first violin and viola entries as a reminder, as it does for Hn 3·4 at its 73.

### 3. The symphony's key plan is A&S's ♭II pivot at long range, and the score makes it audible at 87 (record, no change)
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 494 (§14) names the most frequent ♭II pivot: "the Neapolitan 6th of a minor key becomes IV6 in the major key a 3rd lower". C minor to A♭ major is exactly that pair of keys. Bar 87 states the pivot as a chord: D♭/F with the bass doubled (PDF 484), and D♭5 alone on top in Cl 1 (PDF 483), falling to C5 at 89. It is placed at the end, where its meaning is recalled, not at the link. The link at 1-2 uses the common tone instead (protected). At the next level up, PDF 492-493 (Ex. 28-17) shows a plan I – ♭II – V with the theme restated on ♭II. Movement II's D♭ section (theme B, 37-61) is ♭II of the symphony's C, and the finale tonicizes D♭ with that same theme at its 211-218 before its German sixth, V and I. The finale also leaves the D♭ from its 6/3 position, as §2 asks. The movement therefore supplies both ♭II roles that Unit 28 describes for a long span: pivot (87) and tonicized ♭II key (37-61, completed in IV).
**Fix.** None. Keep the B section in D♭ and the IV6 at 87 (already protected: key plan, c080, plagal close).

### 4. Bars 63-64: root-position ♭II has the specific reason the book requires (record, no change)
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 487 (§8) says ♭II5/3 "will usually occur for a specific compositional reason", because the bass would otherwise leap a diminished 5th to V. At 63 the A-minor chord stands in root position (Cb pizz. A, Vc and B.tbn A) because it carries movement I's first-theme head in its own key. The bass then goes A–D–E–E♭ (64-65). It fills the A–E♭ tritone by step in the way Ex. 28-11 fills its diminished 3rd with a passing tone, and it ends with the semitone E→E♭ of the movement's E/F♭→E♭ thread. ♭2̂ (A) is on top in Fl 1 at 63 (A6, sf) and is followed by silence, the "rhetorical silence" placement of PDF 483. It never moves straight to B♭ in the top voice (PDF 484). It is approached from ♭VI (F♭, 62 b3), the Verdi route (PDF 489-490). mvt2.md calls 63-64 "♭II in enharmonic notation" without its position.
**Fix.** None to the score (protected quotation). Add "root position (A&S PDF 487, §8)" to mvt2.md's line on 63-64.

### 5. Bar 49: the quotation's Neapolitan matches movement I's (record, no change)
**Evidence.** i – VI6 – N6 – V7 – i under the cor anglais, as at movement I 173. ♭2̂ G5 falls a diminished third to E♯5 (Ex. 28-2a). The bass is tripled. The cellos hold B2 as V's seventh and resolve it to A2 (Ex. 28-2b). The cross relation G/G♯ is split between voices (PDF 484). All as c452 found. The quotation therefore brings back movement I's harmony as well as its tune.
**Fix.** None.

### 6. Coda: no ♭II over the final pedal (not reopened)
**Evidence.** Ex. 28-14 (Verdi, ♭VI–♭II at the end of a major movement, "a peculiarly inward quality") and Ex. 28-20 (Chopin, "♭II" over the coda pedal) would both fit an Adagio coda. c310 and c312 rejected this for a stated reason: the finale owns the ♭II/♭VI resolution, and II's last chord stays plain for the finale's German sixth on A♭. The reading does not answer that reason. The coda already holds the Chopin figure at symphony level: Cl 1's D♭5–C5 at 87-89 is ♭2̂–1̂ of C over A♭'s plagal close and resolves movement I's last C5–D♭5–C5 at pitch.
**Fix.** None.
