## What else I studied

I wrote /tmp/critic-456/scripts/spans.py, which reads score.midi with its tempo map and lists, per MIDI track, every stretch of continuous sound longer than 12 seconds with bar numbers. Breath marks do not shorten notes in the MIDI, so each long span was then checked by hand against the printed breaths and slur breaks in sections/*.ily. ## Findings

### 1. Bass clarinet 57-60: 15.5 seconds in the low register with no breath, crescendo to f and into ff (applied)

Bars: 57-60, bass clarinet (sections/bSec.ily, `bSecBassCl`).

Problem: the bass clarinet enters at 57 p and plays F3-G♭3 | B♭2-E♭3-A♭2-D♭3 | G♭2-G2 | A♭2, with a crescendo to f at 59 and a further crescendo at 60. The only breath is at the end of 60, before the ff at 61-62. From the tempo steps in global.ily (57-58 at 62, 59 at 64, 60 at 62 then 58) this is 7.7 + 3.8 + 4.0 = 15.5 seconds, followed by 8.9 seconds at ff. Bassoon 2 has the same line with the same single breath.

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 70 asks for "as many breathing spaces as possible" for large-bore winds; the bass clarinet's low register uses the most air, and here it is growing to forte. PDF 42-43: when an octave or unison line must break, "an instrument of equal strength should continue the line at the point where the original doubling was interrupted". Bassoon 2 and the basses play the same line, so a breath in the bass clarinet alone leaves the line intact. The best place is the end of 58, where flute 1 and oboe 1 already breathe at the melody's seam (c086). c326's statement that no low-wind span over 12 seconds was left without a breath is wrong for these bars. The line has no slur, so a player could breathe anywhere, but without a mark the bass clarinet and bassoon 2 are likely to breathe together at the same spot.

On the protected "breath plan 57-60" (c176): that plan set the breaths for flute 1 (after 58), the horns and the low brass (end of 60). This adds one breath in a part the plan did not cover, at the same place as flute 1's breath, and changes no existing breath.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-456/work/sections/bSec.ily):

```
f2\p\< ges2 | bes,4 es4 aes,4 \once \stemUp des4 \breathe | ges,2\f g,2 | aes,1\< \breathe |
```

Bassoon 2 is unchanged, so the two players breathe at different times. A comment line citing c456 sits above the line. The B.cl. part shows the comma at the end of 58 and still fits on one page.

### 2. Flute 1 37-40: 16 seconds at p with only a slur break (no change)

Flute 1 plays 37-40 without a rest (16.0 s at crotchet = 60). The slur breaks at 38/39, on a half note, which gives a quick catch breath, and the breath between theme B's two halves is at the end of 40 (c036). Sebesky PDF 24 says a slur is "usually in one breath", which fits two slurs of about 8 seconds each. The passage is protected (flute over thin strings 37-44, c326 row 19). Recorded only.

### 3. Muted Hn 4 at 88 lies a third above the cellos (no change)

At 88 the cellos have D♭3 (iv) and the muted Hn 4 has F♭3, a minor third above. Sebesky PDF 36 keeps a background horn chord a fifth to two octaves above the bass so the horns "breathe" and the bass resonates on its own. At pp, muted, with Hn 3 a fifth above the bass and the chord lasting one bar, the effect is small, and the coda and plagal close 87-92 are protected (c194, c199, c223). Recorded only.

### 4. Ranges, mutes, endurance and blend: no change

Every wind and brass note lies inside the best ranges in Sebesky's charts (PDF 21, 27, 29, 32, 39, 60, 64, 65, 70, 72), checked against the images rather than the OCR. Horns 3 and 4 get about 18 seconds (82-86) to fit straight mutes before 87 (PDF 37). Horn 1 rests before its solo at 67 (PDF 44, c426). The brass at 61-62 is complete within itself, with roots low and the third in trombone 2 (PDF 42, 50). Oboes are never in unison (PDF 71), and the flute is always above the oboe (PDF 79). The clarinet with horn at 53-56 and with cor anglais at 89-90 are both doublings Sebesky recommends (PDF 63).
