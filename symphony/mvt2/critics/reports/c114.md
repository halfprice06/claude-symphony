## Findings

### 1. Bars 74, 77, 78: harp LH puts a fourth, a fifth and a sixth in the lowest octave (applied)

**Problem.** In A′ the harp's left hand plays B♭1–E♭2 (a fourth) at 74, E♭1–B♭1 (a fifth inside octave 1) at 77, and E♭1–C2 (a major sixth) at 78 b3. At 78 b3 this is the lowest sound in the orchestra, on the arpeggiated pickup chord into the ff peak, and the harp's low strings ring on (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46, cited in c076). The same moments in the first statement use octaves: E♭1–E♭2 at 10 and 13, A♭1–A♭2 at 9. c089 changed 8, 13, 72 and 73 to octaves for the same reason, but 74, 77 and 78 were left as they were.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 124: the lowest range ("octaves 1 and −1") is where "the proximity of harmonic intervals creates a bad effect". Rimsky's octave numbering puts the upper register in octaves 5 and 6 (C5–C7), so octave 1 is C1–B1. E♭1–B♭1 and the E♭1 of the sixth lie in it.
- Appendix PDF 505–507: in every f/ff chord the bottom octave carries the root only, single or doubled at the octave (Cb, C-fag., tuba). The third and fifth begin an octave or more above it.
- PDF 125: upper and lower parts are seldom far apart without the middle filled in. With E♭1–C2 under the right hand's G3 the hands were a twelfth apart; with the octave they are a tenth apart.

**Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoHarpL`):**
```
<aes,, es,>2\arpeggio f,,4 | <bes,, bes,>2.\arpeggio |
<aes,, es,>4\p\arpeggio c,4 \once \stemUp des,4 | <des, aes,>2\arpeggio bes,,4 | <es,, es,>2.\arpeggio | r2 <es,, es,>4\arpeggio |
```
No new pitch classes, so the harp pedals are unchanged. The LH still follows the bass (the c060 bass B♭ at 74 is kept), and the harp still rests at 78 b1–2. The E♭ and C left out of the harp are already played by violas, Bsn 1, Vc, horns and harp RH.

Build: 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 8 pages. allpairs_par.py adds only one line, at 74: an octave between Bsn 2 and the harp LH's upper note, both doubling the bass line B♭. Neither is an independent voice, so it is a doubling, not a parallel. Parts book regenerated (`python3 genparts.py parts-book.ly`) and built: 0 warnings.

### 2. Bars 61–62: the main tutti chord checked against the tables (no change)

At 61 b1 the sounding chord is D♭2 (Cbsn, tuba, Cb), D♭3 (B.cl., B.tbn, Bsn 2, timp.), F3 (Bsn 1, Tbn 2), A♭3 (Vc), D♭4–F4 (Vla double stop, Hn 2), A♭4 (Hn 3·4, Cl 2, Vln II), D♭5, F5 (Ob, Cl 1), A♭5 (Fl 2), D♭6 (Vln I), A♭6 (Fl 1). The brass is graded below the woodwind and strings as in chords 2–3 and Ex. 209. At 62 b3 the timpani are already damped before the F♭ chord, as the tables only ever roll the drums on a chord tone. Nothing to change.

### 3. Bars 63–65: release after the climax checked against Ex. 209 and PDF 115–116 (no change)

Ex. 209 lets the trumpets and trombones play only the arrival, holds the horns, and then thins the texture to a solo violin within three bars. Here the woodwind and harmony brass stop after 62. Tbn 1 states the quotation alone over a string tremolo marked sf>p, with no woodwind harmony. Then horn 1 plays alone over the basses' E♭ (65–70). PDF 115–116 says this is the right layout for a brass phrase over tremolo when the phrase must stand out from the harmonic basis.

### 4. Bar 79: arrival of the A′ peak checked against PDF 129–130 and Ex. 209 (no change)

In Ex. 209 the second violins and cellos add a multiple stop on the climax downbeat. I looked at doing the same at 79. I do not propose it: PDF 130 says string grace-note runs "should not lead up to chords of three or four notes, as this would be awkward for the bow", and at 79 the violins arrive from the grace run. Single notes are correct there.
