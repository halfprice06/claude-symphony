## Findings

### 1. Bar 78: the reprise's deceptive cadence should go to ♭VI (F♭ major) under the melody's held A♭, then F♭→F in the bass to vi. Applied in the scratch copy.

**Problem.** The large plan rests on one chromatic relation, F♭/E against E♭. It appears in these places:
- bar 1: violas E→E♭;
- bar 5: Cl 2 F♭→E♭;
- 62–65: the climax bass F♭ = E → E♭;
- 87–88: Cl 1 and violas F–F♭–E♭ in the plagal close.

The A′ section (71–82) does not take part. Its harmony repeats 23–30 chord for chord, and its one non-diatonic moment, the deceptive cadence at 77–78, goes to diatonic vi (F minor). So the reprise sounds unaware of the middle section, and the climax at 79 is prepared by the same progression as the plain statement at 28–30.

**Evidence from the reading.**
- A&S make three points about ♭VI. It is reached either by a common tone, as in Widmung, or by a deceptive cadence, as in Chopin (PDF 592–593). The Widmung example is this movement's own case: A♭ major moves to F♭ with the melody's A♭ held (Ex. 32-3, PDF 586–587). And ♭VI's usual goal is a cadential V.
- A closing section "resolves" a chromatic area into the diatonic context (Waldstein, PDF 593).
- In K. 542, a deceptive cadence on ♭VI blocks the close. The tension is not released quickly: ♭VI is inflected, and only then do V and a strong authentic cadence follow (PDF 601–602).
- A remote area "would make little sense" unless it is tied to the motive (PDF 602–603).

At 78 the melody already holds A♭5 for two beats over the deceptive bass. That is the Widmung common tone, so the change needs no new melody.

**Fix.** Beats 1–2: bass F♭ under V7's resolution (E♭→F♭ is the deceptive half step). The 7th D♭ falls to C♭ in Vc and Vla, and the leading tone G rises to A♭ in Cl 2. Beat 3: the bass rises F♭→F, a chromatic inflection that raises the tension (PDF 584–585), giving vi under the melody's C. Then 79–82 continue unchanged.

Parallels and part-combining needed four extra adjustments:
- Cl 1 holds A♭4. C♭→C over F♭→F would give chromatic parallel 5ths with the bass, and Cl 1 B♭4→C♭5 with the bass E♭→F♭ was a 5th pair.
- The Vc ends on A♭3 at beat 3 rather than C. A♭→C would move in octaves with the melody.
- Horn 2 ends its counter-melody slur at 77 and joins horn 1's pickup C4 at beat 3. `\partCombineUnisono` moves to bar 78 so the shared slur closes.
- The harp plays only on beat 3. An E/B pedal change from bar 77 has no time.

### 2. No other change to proportions or key plan; reasons recorded so later cycles do not churn

These points follow the reading as written, and I recommend leaving them alone:
- **The A minor bars 63–64 are short.** That fits A&S's treatment of the remote area as a "stop on the way" (K. 421, PDF 589) between ♭VI (62) and V (65). Haydn's integration conditions are met (PDF 597): E♮ is heard as a leading tone at 19–20 and in bar 1, before A minor uses it as 5̂, and the return goes by E→E♭.
- **The respellings at 47 and 51 are notational, as they should be (PDF 588).** F♯ minor stands for G♭ minor, the minor iv of D♭ reached in stages through B♭ minor and C♯7 (PDF 596). Writing it in flats would be "unkind to the performers".
- **37 needs no pivot (PDF 586).** The new metre, tempo, key signature, pizzicato bass and viola triplets make the new tonic obvious.
- **The tension curve has two ff points: 61 (brass, F6) and 79 (strings and horns, B♭6).** The reading does not rank them. Finding 1 gives 79 a harmonic reason to be the higher one, with ♭VI→vi→ii6/5→V7→I under it. No dynamic change is needed.
