## Other study

I compared v89 with v79 file by file and listed every change made by critics 080–088. allpairs_par.py misses inner voices on shared staves, so I wrote my own per-beat checker for consecutive inner voices and ran it on both versions. For the holistic lens I used the Mutopia source of the Eroica slow movement (reference-corpus/mutopia/BeethovenLv_O55) and the corpus PDF (BeethovenLv_O55__ScoreMvtII.pdf, 35 pp.). I compared four things: the theme statement and its first restatement (1–17), the maggiore (69 ff.), the fugato (about 114 ff.), and the reprise and coda (about 173–247).

## Holistic comparison with Eroica/ii

Several features hold up next to Beethoven. The coda breaks theme A into fragments separated by rests and fades out (83–89). Beethoven does the same at 238–247, sempre più p, before his final chords. The reprise (71–78) is scored differently from the exposition and has new figuration, as Beethoven re-scores his reprise from about 173 with triplets and thirty-seconds. The middle section puts a wind melody over string triplets (37 ff.). This matches the Eroica maggiore at 69, where the oboe sings over violin triplets and a horn pedal. Mixture across large spans (the F♭ of 62 recalled at 78 and 88) is handled with the same care Beethoven gives to his C minor/E♭ relations. A conductor would accept this as competent mature work.

There are two gaps a conductor would notice. Neither can be fixed by a small edit. First, Beethoven's bass is motivic: the grace-note figure of bar 1 drives the accompaniment, and the fugato makes counterpoint out of the theme. Our bass mostly supports the harmony. Only the build 53–61 develops a motive in a real contrapuntal way. Second, Beethoven gives his theme to a second colour almost at once (oboe from bar 8/9). Our theme A stays in the strings and horn. A wind restatement is still open from earlier cycles, and there is no staff room for it. Both are issues of design, and the proportions are protected in HISTORY ("do not reopen"), so I do not propose them as edits.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 58 beat 4: parallel fifths with the bass (regression since v79) — applied

**Bars:** 58 b4 → 59 b1, Hn 1 and Vc.

**Problem:** v89 changed Hn 1 F4 to A♭4 and Vc F3 to A♭3 so that the resolution tone would not sound under the G♭ suspension. Against the bass the A♭ makes A♭/E♭ → D♭/A♭, a direct parallel fifth in two orchestral voices (horn and cellos). In v79 this beat had F, which was clean. allpairs_par.py does not report it. My inner-voice checker does.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 352 says that doubling the tone of resolution against a suspension is "much less problematic" when the two are separated by timbre or dynamics. Here they are: the suspension is in a different instrument and register. Parallel fifths between consonant chord tones are a plain error (PDF 340–341). Critic 082's reason is answered: the doubling was the smaller problem, and the cure caused a worse one. The other options fail. D♭ makes octaves with the bass into 59. C♭ doubles the seventh.

**Fix (applied in sections/bSec.ily):**
```
% Hn 1
\partCombineSoloI aes'2(\mp\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1." ges'2 | f'4 es'2 f'4) \breathe | ...
% Vc
| f4) es2( f4) |
```
The Vc anticipation at 60 b4 from c082 is kept.

### 2. Harp left hand: parallel fifths between consonant chord tones in theme A — applied

**Bars:** 7→8, within 8, 13→14, 71→72, within 72, and 73.

**Problem:** The low harp chords use open fifths (for example `<f,, c,>`, `<bes,, f,>`, `<es,, bes,,>`) and move by step or by fifth. This makes parallel fifths between chord tones in the bass register, where they are most audible. The open item from c076 (c084 called it "borderline") is still open. The new checker confirms the fifths are between consonant tones on every beat.

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 341: parallels made by consonant tones and chordal skips are "more problematic … avoid them". Only fifths that come from dissonant figuration are free.

**Fix (applied in sections/aOne.ily and sections/aTwo.ily):** use octaves instead of the fifths and keep the arpeggios.
```
% aOne, bar 8
| <f,, f,>4\arpeggio <bes,, bes,>4\arpeggio <es,, es,>4\arpeggio |
% aOne, bar 13
<es,, es,>2.\arpeggio
% aTwo, bar 72
<f,, f,>4\arpeggio <bes,, bes,>4\arpeggio es,,4 |
% aTwo, bar 73
<aes,, es,>2\arpeggio f,,4 |
```
At 73 beat 3 I first wrote `<f,, f,>4`. That made new fifths with the cellos into 74 (F2/C4 → E♭2/B♭3), so I changed it to a single `f,,4`. The tenth at 73 b1–2 (A♭/E♭) is not part of a parallel. The octave doubling of the bass is already present in the cellos and basses, so the change adds no new octaves against another voice.

### Notes not applied

- **78, German sixth (open item).** A&S PDF 513 says augmented sixths often follow a deceptive cadence to ♭VI. At 78 the I6/4 on beat 3 goes to IV at 79, so a German sixth here would sit in the wrong place. Critic 062's judgement stands. I do not recommend it.
- **63, Vln I divisi.** The upper half goes E6→A6, in octaves with the bass E→A. This has been there since c059 and is a tutti doubling of the bass's leap. It is minor and should stay.
- **81, Hn 4.** A♭3→G3 moves in octaves with the melody's leading tone. It is short, inside a held chord, and in a different register. Minor; no change.
- **68, "Bn 2 French sixth" (open item).** It conflicts with the motto's D→D♭ (cyclic rule in BRIEF). Not recommended.
