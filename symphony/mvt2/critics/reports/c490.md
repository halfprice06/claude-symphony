### 2. Both joins are the III♯ relation of Ex. 30-1, and the key cycle is an augmented triad (record, no change)
**Check.** Bar 1 sounds C3 C4 E4 G4 C5 (cellos, Hn 3, violas, Vln II, Vln I). At bar 2 the bass enters on A♭1 and the violas move E4 to E♭4. That is III♯ → I, a secondary-mixture chord (PDF 534, Ex. 30-5a) whose meaning comes from the movement before, as Chopin's A♭ chord gets its meaning from E (PDF 531). At the end, Vln II holds A♭4 through 87-92. The scherzo's Hn 1 takes it as G♯4 pp (scherzo v400 intro.ily: `gis'2.~\pp`), and E follows at the scherzo's bar 3: G♯ = III♯ of E, Chopin's exact pair.
**Added point.** The movement keys C-A♭-E-C are the three notes of an augmented triad (C-E-G♯/A♭). The reading says augmented triads are usually local, but that late-nineteenth-century composers gave them "a larger role" (PDF 539). Its Wagner footnote (Ex. 30-14) describes a tonic that is avoided and reached only at the end. Here C, movement I's tonic, appears in II only as III♯ at bar 1 and as the top C5 of the last chord. C as a key is reserved for the finale (cyclic rule: no C-major material). The ending into the scherzo with no seventh and no added sixth keeps this.
**Fix.** None. Optionally add to mvt2.md's key-plan note: "The keys C-A♭-E-C outline an augmented triad; A&S PDF 539 (Ex. 30-14 and its footnote) on augmented-triad relations in a larger role and a goal tonic deferred to the end (c490)."

### 3. The three forms of III are a motive across the symphony (record, no change)
**Reading.** Chopin's piece contrasts three forms of III (III♯, ♮III, ♮III♭), and A&S say the contrast "constitutes a kind of motivic element" (PDF 531-532).
**Check.** Movement II sets III of A♭ (C) in three ways:
- C major at bar 1 (III♯): movement I's last chord.
- C minor at 78 b3 (E♭2 bass; C4, E♭4, G4, C5, C6 above): iii6, movement I's key, the last chord before the B♭6 peak at 79. It passes and goes on to D♭ at 79.
- C5 alone, on top of the plain A♭ triad at 89-92: the pitch movement I ended on.

Major, minor, then the bare pitch. The finale, in C, holds the resolution.
**Fix.** None. Keep 78 b3 as a passing iii6, not a cadence in C minor. A cadence there would turn the motive into a key, and the cyclic rules keep C as a key for the finale.

### 4. Bars 47-50 and 62-65: both quotations follow the reading's preparation rules (record, no change; adds to c360 F3-F4)
**Check, 47-48.** 47 is a full bar of D♭7, spelled C♯7 (bass D♭2, violas B3, Vln II F4). 48 is G♭ minor spelled F♯ minor (bass F♯2, cellos C♯3, C.a. A4). This is Purcell's preparation (PDF 537): a full-bar applied dominant leads the ear to expect major, and minor "appears logical in retrospect".
**Check, 62-65.** 62 b3 is E major (E1-E2 bass; E4, A♭4/G♯, B4 above), the applied V of the A minor at 63 (PDF 537: "Preceding a chromatic chord by its own V"). 63 is minor ♭II, double mixture, at the climax of the phrase, as in Schubert's Ex. 30-9. At 64 b3-4 the chord over E is first heard as V7 of A minor and becomes a French sixth into E♭7 at 65 (D4 → D♭4). That is the "double meaning" A&S describe for the chord after ♭II.
**Added point.** The reading lists association with an earlier plain use of the altered tone as preparation (PDF 536-537). The A♮ of 63 has two earlier uses: the one-beat neighbour at 46 b2 (Bsn, cellos A3; oboe A4), and the C.a.'s held A4, the long note of movement I's S head at 48. So the second quotation's tonic is the first quotation's held note. The two quotations from movement I are linked by pitch as well as by source. This is the "recollection of an earlier episode" kind of preparation (PDF 537), at the level of the symphony.
**Fix.** None. This adds a reading-based reason to keep 47-50 and 63-64 as they are (once each; c360 and the do-not-touch list).

### 5. The last chord stays plain: the finale supplies the Am Meer chord (record, no change)
**Reading.** A common-tone augmented sixth decorates a tonic over a held bass. Its augmented sixth expands to the octave on 5̂ (Schubert, *Am Meer*, Ex. 30-25, PDF 547). In C, the chord's upper tones are A♭-[C]-E♭/D♯-F♯.
**Check.** The finale opens with A♭-C-E♭-F♯ (finale.md, "Opening"). That is the upper part of the *Am Meer* chord in C, laid over movement II's last chord, A♭-C-E♭. The finale then resolves it to V of C, not to I over a held C. This confirms c210 and c480 from the side of this reading. II's last chord must not add the G♭/F♯ or a seventh, so the finale's chord sounds as a change to the chord that ended movement II.
**Fix.** None to the score. Text for mvt2.md, "Themes quoted" paragraph, after "so the finale's opening German sixth on A♭ resolves it": "(the finale's chord is the upper part of a common-tone augmented sixth in C, A&S Ex. 30-25, PDF 547; c490)".

### 6. Focal mixture chords are plain triads, and directed ones carry dissonance (record, no change)
At each focal mixture chord, MIDI shows no added dissonance:
- 78 b1-2: F♭ major. E2 E3 B3 A♭3 A♭4 A♭5.
- 88: D♭ minor. D♭2 D♭3 F♭3 A♭3 F♭4 A♭4 D♭5.
- 5: iv6/4. A♭2 F♭3 A♭3 D♭4.
- 63: A minor until the passing B♭ on b4.
- 89-92: A♭ major.

The directed mixture chords do carry dissonance:
- 80 b3: iiø7 with F♭ (B♭ bass; E/F♭ in Hn, Vla, Ob, Fl).
- 81: V7♭9.

That is §6 of the reading (PDF 537). It matters for the arc: the F♭ colour, which the scherzo (E) and the finale (F♭3→E♭3 at 197-198) take up, is always heard as a plain sonority at its focal points.
