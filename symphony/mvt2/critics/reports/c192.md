## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 21–22: the upper cellos leap an augmented 2nd, F♭3→G3, into the half cadence. Applied.
**Problem.** At 21 b3 all the cellos play F♭3, the bass of the French sixth (F♭–A♭–B♭–D). At 22 they divide: the lower half falls to E♭3, and the upper half starts its answer figure on G3, so that half moves F♭3→G3, an A2. The violas' A♭3, the voice that should fall to G, stops at 22. c152 said the cellos "take over" that A♭, but the upper half never plays A♭.
**Evidence.** A&S Ex. 9-3c (PDF 136) labels exactly this move, ♭6̂→7̂ as an A2 in the approach to V, "even worse". Ex. 9-6c (PDF 138, "not") and Ex. 9-7f (PDF 139, "poor") reject the same A2 in II6–V. PDF 136 §3: in moving to V "all four voices must proceed to a new tone", by step and against the bass.
**Fix (aOne.ily, aOneVc).** Divide the cellos one beat earlier. The upper half plays A♭3 with the violas and resolves to G3. The lower half keeps F♭3 with the basses.

The French sixth that c189 protects keeps all four pitches and its F♭ bass. The change is only to doubling: the tonic A♭ is now doubled and the tendency tone F♭ is doubled less. The near cross-relation (Vln II F4 / bass F♭) is unchanged. allpairs_par loses one cello–bass octave (F♭→E♭) and gains nothing.

### 2. Bar 43 b3: the 4th of the cadential 6/4 is doubled. Applied.
**Problem.** Over the A♭ bass the cor anglais holds D♭4 (the theme's lower octave) while the viola triplet plays A♭3–D♭4–E♭4. That puts the dissonant 4th in two parts at the same pitch, and both then resolve to C4 (viola on b4, cor anglais at 44).
**Evidence.** PDF 154 §5 and Ex. 10-5c ("4th doubled – wrong"); Points 4, PDF 160: "never double the 4th". The bass is the best tone to double (Ex. 10-5a). c102 checked this bar against rows 15–17 of this table but not against the doubling rule. It tried A♭–D♭–F and rejected it (octaves with the flute's F–E♭). Doubling the bass avoids that problem.
**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVla bar 43).** `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' es' }` → `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 es' aes' }`. The pizzicato figure keeps its shape and the 4th is left to the cor anglais. The flute's 6–5 and the soprano's 4̂–3̂–2̂ are unchanged. No new parallels.

### 3. Bars 79–82: the A′ cadence follows the reading closely. No change.
The cadence runs IV with the B♭6 peak (79), IV (80 b2), iiø7 (80 b3), V♭9–V7 (81) and I (82). 80 b2→b3 is IV–II with the bass falling a 3rd (Ex. 9-21, PDF 147; Points 7). Cl 2's D♭5–G4–A♭4 is the 4̂–7̂–1̂ diminished-fifth line of Ex. 9-10 (PDF 140). The 7th D♭ is held in Cl 2 and enters in the cellos by leap (Ex. 9-9). The melodic peak sits on the intermediate chord (§18, PDF 150). No cadential 6/4 can stand at 81 or at 13 and 29, because the theme has 7̂ on V's downbeat. A&S's Exercise 5 note (PDF 162) requires a 6/4 only for 3̂–2̂ and 1̂–7̂ figures, and the melody here is 1̂ over II and then 7̂. Dvořák's Largo 17–18 also closes without one. Keep c012's ii7–V7 cadences.

### 4. Half cadences at 10, 26, 22 and 44. No change apart from finding 1.
10 and 26 reach V from one beat of IV (IV7 at 25), with the soprano 1̂→7̂ and contrary motion (PDF 136, Ex. 9-3a). 44 has the movement's only cadential 6/4. It sits on b3 of 43 and resolves on b4 (PDF 154 §6). It comes from ii6/5 (Points 7), and the flute's G♭–F–E♭ puts 3̂ on the accented beat (§8, Ex. 10-9). After finding 1, 22's French sixth resolves in every voice by step.

### 5. Bars 12–13 and 28–29: the 7th of V7 is not prepared from ii7. No change.
D♭ is held at 12 b3 (Bsn 1) and 28 b3 (Vc), and it moves to B♭ instead of becoming the 7th (PDF 139 §6, Points 1). The 7th appears only as the melody's passing D♭ (and at 29 b3 in Vc/Hn 3). A&S allow an unprepared 7th in instrumental textures (PDF 140, Ex. 9-9). A held D♭4 at 13 would rub against the horn's own E♭4–D♭4–C4 descent (c117 removed the harp's D♭ for that reason). The cello line at 29 is the protected counter-melody.

### 6. Bars 17–18: IV–"I6"–ii–vi breaks syntax rule 3 on paper. No change.
At 17 b3 IV goes to I6 on the downbeat of 18, and 18 b3's ii goes to F minor at 19 without a dominant (PDF 148, rules 2–3). The I6 is a passing chord between two pre-dominants in a chain of 10ths: soprano F–E♭–D♭ over bass D♭–C–B♭. That is the passing "I6" of Ex. 9-18 (PDF 145), but on a downbeat, where A&S say such chords are unusual. The bars belong to the 17→21 sequence that c032 protected, and A&S treat chords inside a sequence as linear. Any repair would change the solo violin's answer or the cello division. I record the point so the next harmony critic does not reopen it without a new reason.

### 7. Bars 59–60 and 64–65: approaches to V. No change.
At 59 the chords are ii6, then V6/5 of V (59 b3), then vii°7 of V (b4), then V (60). That is a rising chromatic bass under intermediate and applied harmonies (Points 6–7, PDF 151). A 6/4 cannot stand under the held C6 (protected 57–61 descent). At 64 b4, E7 is enharmonically the German sixth of A♭ (F♭–A♭–C♭–D). It works as a chromatic intermediate chord before E♭7 at 65, even though D slips down to D♭ (the motto) instead of rising. That passage is the protected 61–65 climax.

### 8. Bar 68 and the ending. No change.
68 b3 has A♭–C over the E♭ pedal resolving across the bar line, which Ex. 10-6a ("never!") forbids for a cadential 6/4. But it is the passing 6/4 of the 8–7–6–5 over the held dominant (closed by c043, c102, c133), and this reading gives no new reason. I made no change to the ending. The closing E♭–D–D♭–C over IV6–iv–I at 85–89, the exported themes and both quotations are untouched.
