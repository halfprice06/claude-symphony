## What else I studied

My master this time is Borodin's Symphony No. 2, iii (Andante, D♭ major). It is a Russian slow movement close to Rimsky-Korsakov's own practice, and it has not been used in any series. Its outline is documented in program notes: solo clarinet and harp open and close it, the horn has the main tune, impassioned strings lead to a climax where the horn theme returns richly scored, and it runs attacca into the finale, which starts from hushed open fifths (https://imslp.org/wiki/Symphony_No.2_(Borodin,_Aleksandr); https://thelistenersclub.com/?p=16190; https://www.bhso.org.uk/work/borodin-symphony-no-2-2/). I had no local score of it, so I compare outlines only, not bar numbers. I also diffed v269 against v279 with comments stripped. The changes are:

- c271: C.a. eighth at 37.
- c272: Bsn 2 B♭2 at 9 (II6/5).
- c274: Vln II F♯–A at 50, and Vc C3 with viola E♭4 off-beats at 51.
- c275: flute stems.
- c267/c277: viola figures at 41–42 and 57–58.
- c269: C.a. taper at 53.
- c270: timpani muta chain.
- c278: ties at 32–33, 57–59 and 65–66.

I checked each in the score. None made the music worse. The only side effect is the item in Finding 2.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 28: the cellos hold the resolution note A♭3 under the violins' B♭5 appoggiatura, all in string colour (not applied)

**Problem.** At 28 b2 both violin sections play the B♭5 appoggiatura, f. The cellos' counter-melody holds A♭3 on beats 1–2, which is the note the appoggiatura resolves to. At 76 the same A♭3 is also in Bsn 1 and Hn 2, so there the clash is between different colours.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 132–133 says a non-harmonic note against its own fundamental note is harsher in the same timbre. Greater contrast of timbre makes it less discordant.

**Fix (optional).** In sections/aOne.ily, aOneVc, bar 28: `\after 4 \f \after 4 \> aes4( f4 des'4) |` (was `aes2( des'4)`). The cello moves away from A♭ when the B♭ arrives. It is in contrary motion with the violins and keeps the D♭4 peak.

**Why not applied.** The cello counter-melody 23–30 and the plain first statement are protected. The two notes are two octaves and a step apart, and B♭ over IV is a consonant 6–5. I record it so a later melody or harmony cycle can decide. Bar 76 should stay as it is.

### 2. Bars 57–58: c277's viola triplets now sit in unison with clarinet 2 (no change)

At 57 b3 the violas' top note B♭4 doubles Cl 2's held B♭4. At 58 b3 their A♭4 doubles Cl 2's A♭4. In v269 they doubled Hn 1. PDF 133 asks only that an ornamental basis move independently of the melody, and the melody is in the violins and flutes two octaves higher. A unison with a held inner clarinet note is harmless, so nothing here got worse in a way that matters. No change.

### 3. Bar 83: a two-octave gap under the harp harmonics (no change)

For one bar, harp harmonics at A♭4–C5–E♭5 sit over Vc A♭2 and Cb A♭1, with nothing between them. PDF 125 says the outer parts are seldom this far apart without the middle filled, but it allows the gap for a special colour. The violas fill C4/E♭4 from 84 b3, and the timpani play E♭3–C3–A♭2 at 84. The coda 83–92 is protected (c263). No change.

### 4. Bars 78–79: the strings' run to B♭6 is not slurred into the main note (no change)

PDF 129–130 says the grace-note anacrusis is "as a rule" slurred to the main note. Fl 1 does slur it (78 b3 to 80). The violins end their slur on A♭6 and take B♭6 on a fresh down-bow, which is the protected c246 bow plan. The run leads to a single note, not a string chord, so the rule's warning does not apply. Keep.

### 5. Holistic comparison with Borodin 2/iii (no change)

- **Frame.** Borodin brackets his Andante with clarinet and harp. This movement opens its theme with horn and harp (7) and closes with harp harmonics, timpani and the clarinet's motto tail (83–92).
- **Climax.** Borodin's climax restates the horn theme richly scored. Here 79 does the same with theme A in a partial tutti, with the horns on the counter-melody.
- **Ending.** Both movements run attacca into the next one. Borodin ends on open fifths. This movement's last A♭ chord stays a plain triad on purpose, for the finale's German sixth (c250).
- **Ornament.** Borodin's horn tune has folk-style grace-note turns. Theme A is plain, which suits the Dvořák 9/ii model, and theme B already has a triplet turn at 39 and 55.

A conductor who knows the Borodin would find nothing here that a master would not write. No change.

### 6. Bars 87–88: the muted-horn echo of the 61–62 brass F–F♭ is what the reading recommends (no change)

PDF 128 describes this exact case: muted brass repeating, softly, a phrase first heard unmuted. That is how the ff brass F–F♭ at 61–62 returns pp at 87–88 in muted Hn 4 with the C.a. Keep.
