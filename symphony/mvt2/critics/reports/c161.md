## Findings

No finding requires a change. After 160 cycles the melodic design meets every applicable principle in this reading. The items below record what I checked against the reading, so that later critics on this lens do not reopen them without new evidence.

### 1. Theme B's augmented head at 61–62 dominates by doubling. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 61–62.

**Check:** Adler PDF 23 asks which melody notes the composer wants to stress and how they are made to dominate. The c021 comment in bSec.ily still names "trumpet 1's A♭–D♭–F–F♭", but the trumpets have been tacet since c060. I traced where the head now sounds. It is in Fl 2 (A♭5–D♭6 | F6–F♭6), Vln II (A♭4–D♭5 | F5–F♭5) and Vc (A♭3–D♭4 | F4–F♭4), which is three octaves. Bsn 1 (D♭4 | F4–F♭4), Cl 2 (A♭4–D♭5) and Ob 2 (D♭5) join for parts of it. Above it, Fl 1 holds the head's first note, A♭6, and Vln I carries D♭6–F6 to A♭6/F♭6, so the head's pitches are also on top. The brass (Hn 2, Hn 3·4 a2, Tbn 2) play the moving inner harmony. That layout is a protected exception (c122 record of Rimsky PDF 108/116), and Bruckner's practice (PDF 20) allows the other choirs to carry the line while the brass add weight.

**Housekeeping only:** the comments at bSec.ily lines 24, 43 and 128 still refer to trumpets. A future editor could take them as live scoring. The composer may add "(trumpets tacet since c060)" to them. This changes no sound.

### 2. The A′ peak at 79 already uses the Elgar/Bruckner octave build. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 78–81.

**Check:** Adler PDF 24 credits the massive sound of the "W.M.B." coda to octave and double-octave doublings. PDF 20 says Bruckner builds his climax "with octaves" after one bar in quasi-unison. At 78 the line narrows to the written run C6 + F6–G6–A♭6 into B♭6 in Fl 1/Vln I and Vln II. At 79 the B♭ appoggiatura sounds in B♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I), B♭5 (Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II) and B♭4 (Vla), at ff. The counter-melody (Hn 1·2 a2, Vc, Bsn 1) stays at f, as c034 and c074 decided, so the stressed note dominates by register and by dynamic. This is the reading's model, and the c071 point (violas on the third octave) is in place.

### 3. Horn unisons checked against Schuller. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 61–62 (Hn 3·4 a2), 78–81 (Hn 1·2 a2).

**Check:** Schuller (Adler PDF 26) warns about high, loud unison horns and gives the remedies: horn 1 or 3 alone, or two horns plus a trumpet. At 78–81 the unison is at concert C4–D♭4, the middle register, so the warning does not apply. At 61–62 horns 3·4 play written E♭5–F5–G♭5 at f with tenutos, in half and whole notes. That is high enough to note but not at the cracking extreme Schuller describes, and the dynamic is f, not ff. The a2 was settled after three reversals (c064, c069, c086) and is on the standing list. The trumpet remedy conflicts with the cyclic rule that keeps the trumpets for movement III. I do not re-raise it.

### 4. Colour plan of the two themes against Hindemith's "save a colour" rule. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** whole movement.

**Check:** Hindemith (PDF 20) recolours each return and keeps the tuba for a fresh statement. Theme A here has a new colour at every appearance: horn (7), muted violins with the cello counter-melody (23), the bass clarinet's chalumeau fragment (31), violins in octaves with Cl 1 and later Fl 1 (71–82), harp harmonics on the head (83) and the clarinet tail (85–89). Theme B goes Fl, Fl/CA, Ob/Vc, violins, then the tutti, with the trombone colour held back to 63 and the tuba used only at 61–62. The return of the horn colour with theme A at 70–74 was closed at c077 (Hn 1's tail at 67–70 brings the colour back just before the reprise). Nothing to add.

### Comparison with the models

Theme A's head works as Dvořák 9/ii's Largo tune does: it rises by step and leap to a held note and then falls by step. Its chromatic tail, E♭–D–D♭–C, is the hook a listener keeps, and the movement places that tail in the horn (67–70) and the clarinet (85–89) as its last melodic events. Dvořák closes his Largo on a fragment of the tune in the same way. Theme B's rising-fourth pickup (c071) and its climb to A♭6 at 58 give the middle section the second, more yearning tune that Tchaikovsky 5/ii and Brahms 4/ii also use. I found nothing in the melodic lines that the assigned reading would change.
