## Findings

None of these findings calls for a change to the notes. The movement already uses Unit 29 the way the chapter describes: two augmented sixths, each at the end of a phase, and the rest of the family (applied 6/5, vii°7 of V, iiø7 with ♭6̂) at the other dominants. Findings 1–3 add points that earlier reports did not cover. Findings 4–5 record two details of voice leading.

### 1. Every structural dominant is prepared from the same family, and only two get the augmented sixth itself (record, no change)

**Check.** PDF 503 says augmented sixths "often occur just before important structural points". PDF 518 (Ex. 29-30) ranks the family by chromaticism: 5–6, applied 6/5, vii°7, augmented sixth. Here is how the movement prepares each dominant that ends or opens a phase:

- 10, 26, 74 (mid-phrase half cadences in theme A): diatonic ii6/5, IV, vi7. These are the least chromatic.
- 22 (end of the b phrase): French sixth.
- 44 (end of B1): IV, ii6, cadential 6/4. Diatonic, because theme B's G♭5 blocks G♮ (c232).
- 60 (V of D♭ before the climax): applied 6/5 at 59 b3, then vii°7 of V at 59 b4. The viola triplet supplies the D♭4, so the chord is complete.
- 65 (retransition V7): vii°7 of V at 64 b3, then the German sixth at 64 b4.
- 81 (final V♭9): iiø7 with F♭ at 80 b3, one note short of the augmented sixth.

The chromaticism increases toward the two places where a key area ends: 21 closes the F-minor excursion, and 64 closes the whole middle section. That matches PDF 503. A third augmented sixth at 44 or 81 would weaken the two that mark the form (c312).

**Fix.** None.

### 2. The augmented sixths' F♭→E♭ is the movement's opening figure, as in the reading's Mozart footnote (record, no change)

**Observation.** The footnote on PDF 503 says the Mozart sonata opens with a grace-note figure D♯–E. The augmented sixths and their resolutions "embody this figure", so they recall the opening and prepare the recapitulation "motivically as well as harmonically". This movement does the same with one semitone:

- 1→2: violas E4→E♭4, the common-tone link out of movement I's C major.
- 5→6: Cl 2 and the low voices F♭3→E♭3 (iv over the pedal → I).
- 21→22: Cb/Vc F♭2→E♭2 (the French sixth's bass).
- 64→65: Cb E2→E♭2 (the German sixth's bass) into the retransition, which leads to the reprise at 71.
- 88→89: Vla, C.a. F♭→E♭ (plagal iv → I).

c250 and c350 recorded the F♭ = E thread as preparation for the scherzo's key. This finding adds the reading's own reason for it: both augmented sixths resolve with the movement's first melodic motion, so the reprise at 71 is prepared by the motive as well as by the dominant.

**Fix.** None in the score. Composer: if coherence/mvt2.md is edited for other reasons, add one line under "Key plan": "Both augmented sixths (21, 64) resolve F♭→E♭ in the bass, the link's E→E♭ of 1–2 (A&S PDF 503 n.)."

### 3. Bars 50–51: the return from F♯ minor is the reading's model place for an augmented sixth, and the quotation blocks it (record, no change)

**Problem considered.** F♯ minor (47–50) is G♭ minor, the minor iv of D♭. PDF 516–517 (§17, Ex. 29-28a–c) gives the standard return plan: "a motion from a tonicized IV through an augmented 6th to the home dominant". PDF 524 adds that the German sixth of D♭ (B𝄫–D♭–F♭–G♮) sounds as A7, V7 of D. D is VI of F♯ minor, so the chord would be a pivot that the F♯-minor ear already accepts. In v372, 50 b3–4 is iv7 of F♯ minor (B1/B2 bass, Vln II D5, C.a. B4–A4, Vla D4–B3). The bass then leaps B1→A♭1 into A♭7 at 51.

**Why no change.**
1. Every augmented sixth of D♭ has B𝄫 (A) in the bass. On 50 b4 the cor anglais plays A4→A♭4 (the quotation of movement I's second theme, protected 47–53). With A in the bass as well, bass and solo would both resolve A→A♭: parallel octaves, which PDF 505 names as the result of a bad doubling.
2. The diminished-3rd form (♯IV7, PDF 518–519) would put G♮1 in the bass under the C.a.'s B4. That changes the iv harmony c312 set at 49–50, for a chord the reading calls rarer and avoided by Mozart.
3. The current route reaches D♭ through F♯ minor's own dominant: G♯7 = A♭7, and C♯ = D♭. That is a sound return, and the bass leap B→A♭ is the notational augmented second c232 accepted (PDF 514).

This answers c210's one-line note ("blocked by the cor anglais' A4–A♭4") with the voice-leading reason.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 20–22: the French sixth follows a deceptive cadence to VI, the reading's commonest approach (record, no change)

**Check.** PDF 513: "Augmented 6ths frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI … because they form a convincing transition to V." At 20 b3 the cellos' C3, Vla B♭3, Vln II E4 and Ob G4 form V7 of F minor. At 21 b1 it moves deceptively to D♭ (VI of F minor, which is also IV of A♭). A passing 6/4 on b2 then gives the French sixth on b3 (Cb F♭2, Vc E3 [F♭] and A♭3, Vla A♭3, Vln II B♭3, Vln I D5), and E♭ follows at 22. The figure combines three of the reading's approaches: VI after a deceptive cadence (PDF 513), IV with its bass lowered (PDF 508), and a chromatic voice exchange filled by a passing 6/4 (PDF 508, Ex. 29-14). The doubled tone is A♭, the third above the bass, as PDF 505 prescribes. Every voice moves by step or holds: F♭→E♭, D→E♭, A♭→G, B♭ held. The spelling F♭ is correct for A♭ major with mixture (PDF 505, Ex. 29-6).

**Fix.** None (protected since c192 and c238).

### 5. Bars 64–65: the contraction to V7 puts the seventh in the top string voice (record, no change)

**Observation.** PDF 515 (§15) allows an augmented sixth to go straight to V7 by contraction, "especially if the 7th is in an inner voice". At 65–66 the strings play E♭2 (Cb), B♭2 (Vc), G3 (Vla) and D♭4 (Vln II), so the seventh D♭4 is the top voice of the only sounding chord. Vln II then stops at 66 without resolving. Hn 1 enters on E♭4 at 67 and plays the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C at 68, so the seventh comes back in the same octave and resolves D♭4→C4 at 68 b3 over the A♭/E♭ 6/4 (Bsn 1 A♭3).

**Why no change.** The reading treats an outer-voice seventh as less common, not as wrong. Here the seventh is pp and sustained, and its resolution is moved two bars later into the motto. This is the bar the cyclic plan builds the retransition on (65–70 protected; c089 rejected a French sixth at 68 for the same motto). Putting the seventh in an inner voice would mean giving Vln II a pitch above D♭4. The only one in the chord is E♭4, which would double the horn's entry note at 67 and blunt it.

**Fix.** None.
