## Findings

### 1. 71-78: the cello counter-melody in the reprise has no suspension (applied)

**Problem.** At 71-78 the cello counter-melody is the 23-30 line note for note. Every note is attacked on a beat, mostly with the melody's own attacks: 73 b1, 74 b1 and 75 b1. In this reprise the theme is in octaves above it, and the line is kept from being heard as a parallel accompaniment only by the horn and bassoon doubling from 75.

**What the reading teaches.** A&S use Ex. 21-1 (PDF 346) for this case: a counterpoint shifted by syncopation against the main subject, so that it holds into the next harmony as a dissonance and resolves down. Section 4 (PDF 348) gives the reason: suspensions "bring into prominence the voice in which they occur" and make the parts independent.

**How Beethoven 4/ii handles it.** The movement never brings back a statement unchanged. Its first theme returns ornamented over the same accompaniment. This score varies its reprise only from 77 on (the run to B flat6). Bars 71-76 are the first statement transposed up an octave, with a new viola figure.

**Fix (reprise only; 25-26 stay plain).** The cello's C4 at 73 b3 is consonant over the F1 bass. It is held into 74 as a 9-8 suspension over the V4/3's bass B flat1 and resolves down to B flat3 on the weak beat. The passing A flat (c302) stays, as an eighth. Change bar 73's last note and bar 74 in `aTwoVc`:

```
| aes2\< c'4~)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp \> | c'4 bes8( aes8) g4\! -\tweak stencil ##f \p |
```

(was `c'4)` and `bes4( aes4) g4`)

**Checks.**
- 74 b1 sounds B flat-C-D flat-G, a 9-8 with the tenth (D flat4 in Cl 2 and viola) above it, which is the normal 9-8 layout (Ex. 21-5).
- The resolution B flat3 doubles the bass at the octave on a weak beat.
- No parallels: the analysis reports 0 outer-voice parallels. Inner parallels were checked against the viola (E flat4 to B flat3) and Vln II (G4).
- Bowing: the tie continues the bow from 73, the B flat-A flat eighths take a new bow, and G4 keeps its own bow for the up-bow crescendo at 75 (c118).
- The cello is not part of any theme record, so the theme checks are not affected.

**Protection.** This answers c032 and c301/c302, which ruled only on the passing A flat; the A flat is kept. The A′ protection (71-82 texture and doublings) covers the doublings, which are unchanged.

### 2. 71-76: the violins' return is literal where Beethoven ornaments (recorded, not applied)

Beethoven 4/ii brings the first theme back in the first violins covered in figuration. This is the A&S Unit 20 idea (PDF 328) of figuration over a fixed framework. Here 71-76 is the 23-28 line an octave higher.

I do not recommend changing it:
- Vln I and Vln II at 70-78 are an exported theme record (themes-mvt2.ly: `themeA VlnOne 70 17`).
- The main model, Dvořák 9/ii, also brings its Largo tune back without ornament.
- c319 and c391 rejected a turn at 72.

The reprise is already varied in its setting (viola eighths, octave register) and from 77 on, and finding 1 adds one more point of difference. A conductor would read the plain return as Dvořák, not as a weakness.

### 3. Accompaniment as the unifying figure (recorded)

Beethoven keeps one accompaniment rhythm and brings it back with every return of the main theme. This score gives each statement of theme A its own accompaniment:
- 7-14: bassoons and harp
- 23-30: muted divided violas
- 71-78: viola eighths

The unifying element is the motto tail E flat-D-D flat-C instead. It comes at 8, 24, 33, 42, 58, 68, 72 and 85-86, and in the timpani's E flat-C-A flat answer at 84-85. That answer is this score's counterpart to Beethoven's timpani coda solo, placed at the same point in the form. This is a different and legitimate plan, recorded by c319 ("three accompaniment types"). No change.

### 5. Harmony against Units 22-32 (recorded)

Every harmonic item in the table follows its book model: the diminished sevenths, mixture, applied chords, the dominant ninth, N6, the French sixth, flat VI, the chromatic sixths and both chromatic-inflection modulations. The modulation at 62-63 is the clearest match. It is a chromatic-inflection modulation of the *Hammerklavier* kind (Ex. 32-1, PDF 584-585), and the score's F flat-E-E flat line gives it the motivic justification that A&S say such an abrupt step needs (PDF 585). No change.

### Overall verdict against Beethoven 4/ii

Measured against Beethoven 4/ii (wind-led lyric opening, a second idea given to a single wind, a forceful outburst in the middle, a timpani moment in a quiet coda), the movement holds. A conductor would accept it as a Dvořák-school Adagio. Its weakest point in this comparison was the plain reprise surface, and finding 1 addresses the part of it that is not protected.
