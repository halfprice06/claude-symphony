## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–56: the harp figure strikes the counter-line's held notes at the unison (applied)

**Problem.** In 53–56 the harp plays the triplet figure that the violas played in 37–40, at the same pitches (c049). Since c019, horn 1 and clarinet 1 play their counter-line, theme A's head against theme B, in the same octave. The harp plucks the counter-line's held notes while they sound: D♭4 twice in 54, G♭4 twice in 55 (the counter-line's long note), C4 together with the horn's attack and again in 56, and D♭4 three times in 56. The harp's G♭3 and F3 would also land on bassoon 1's held line in 56. The figure was written for 37–40, where no middle voice existed. It now re-articulates a melodic layer at mp, so the counter-line sounds as if it pulses with the arpeggio and loses its independence.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 203: the transcriber must study "the layers of the music and determin[e] their order of importance". PDF 217 (Ravel, Petit Poucet): where accompaniment and melody move together, "highly contrasting colors … are very necessary to separate the two elements for the listener". Plucking the same pitch merges the two layers instead of separating them. PDF 230 item 3 (Schoenberg–Brahms): "no actual doubling of the middle parts" while the solo line runs. Earlier fixes in this score follow the same rule: c057 at 7–14 and c127 at 71–78.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-137/work/sections/bSec.ily, bSecHarpR).** The figure, rhythm, dynamics and pedal setting stay the same. Only pitches that duplicate a held line are replaced with other chord tones:
```lilypond
% 53 (beat 4 only; was aes8 c' es' c' aes es')
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' c' es' aes' }
% 54
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes ges' bes ges bes } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 es' ges' es' bes es' } |
% 55
\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8\mp des' es' des' bes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 c' es' c' bes c' } |
% 56
\tuplet 3/2 4 { es'8 ges' es' ges' es' ges' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 f' bes f' bes f' } |
```
The change at 53 beat 4 does two things. It takes the harp's A♭3 off the horn and clarinet's A♭3 pickup. It also avoids the A♭3–G♭3 against E♭5–D♭5 fifths with the violins into 54 that the first version of this fix created. In 56 the figure narrows to two-note oscillations before the violas take the triplets back at 57. This also removes an octave C→B♭ between the harp and the violins at 56 b2.5–3 that the all-pairs checker had flagged in v137. No new pedal changes are needed (G♭, B♭, E♭, F, C, D♭ as set at 53).

**Answer to the protection.** The c124 rule ("no further accompaniment changes in 37–60") is there to stop new figures and colour changes from obscuring the form (c124 report, Adler PDF 146). This fix adds no figure and changes no colour or rhythm. It removes duplicated pitches inside the existing figure. The Hn/Cl counter-line at 53–60 and theme B are untouched.

**Result.** With all files changed so far, the scratch build is clean: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 8 pages, outer-voice parallels 0. The all-pairs checker now reports one fewer octave pair and nothing new. My unison script finds no re-attacks on the counter-line in 54–56; v137 had 8 on each of horn 1 and clarinet 1. The parts book was regenerated (`python3 genparts.py parts-book.ly`) and builds with 0 warnings at 30 pages.

### 2. Bars 57–60: the viola triplets and sextuplets touch the counter-line's pitches (no change)

At 57–58 the viola figure strikes the horn line's G♭4 and F4 once each. At 59–60 its sextuplets land on the Vc/Bsn 1 counter-line's E♭3 and F♭3 twice in each bar, and on the trombone 2 and horn 4 notes at 60. This happens inside an orchestrated crescendo from mp to ff, and the figure doubles its speed at 59. The chapter's crescendo models do the same thing: added instruments and a faster inner figure thicken every layer (Beethoven 6, Ex. XVI-5, PDF 202; Bizet bars 13–18, PDF 213). Here the duplication adds weight at the point where weight is wanted. Leave it. Because of this the fix in finding 1 stops at 56.

### 3. Bars 37–40: Vln II's held chord duplicates the viola triplets on purpose (record; do not "fix")

The unison script flags 6–8 re-attacks per bar: the violas' A♭3–D♭4–F4 triplets sound the pitches that Vln II div. holds. This is the technique Adler describes in the Schoenberg–Brahms analysis: "the harmony in sustained chords is also retained. This technique reflects the use of the sostenuto pedal" (PDF 230 item 2). It is also Dvořák's sustained "harmonic counterpoint … to complete the harmony without making it heavy" (PDF 209). Here the held voice is the residue of the figure, not a melodic layer, so it differs from finding 1. Future critics should not treat it as faulty duplication.

### 4. The rest of the movement against the chapter (no change)

Bars 15–22 switch choirs in the oboe and solo-violin dialogue (PDF 230). The muted Vln II rhythmicises the A♭ pedal under it, like Bizet's horn 3 (PDF 213). The countertheme is passed from instrument to instrument at 23–30, 53–60 and 71–82 (PDF 230–231). The cellos make a delayed, independent entry at 41 over the pizzicato bass (PDF 205, 213). The lean texture at 79–81 makes the big chord tell (PDF 230). The diminuendo at 81–83 is orchestrated by drop-out (PDF 213, 224). The close returns to the opening colours, Vln I's C5 over held strings and horns 3·4 (PDF 224). All of these do what the chapter teaches. The B section's background changes every four bars, which is the limit set by PDF 203's warning against too much colour (c124). Finding 1 does not add to that.
