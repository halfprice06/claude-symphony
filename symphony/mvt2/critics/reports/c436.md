## Findings

The timpani part is well written after three earlier passes on this chapter. Only one point falls short of the reading, and it is small.

### 1. The last strokes at 84-85 have no stated end (applied)

Bars 84-86. The timpani answer `es4.\pp c8 aes,4~ | aes,2. |` and then rest from 86 to the end. Adler requires the part to show "how long a note may ring" (PDF 80). Elsewhere this score does that: the single D♭ strokes at 53 and 62 carry "damp", and the rolls at 3, 60 and 70 end on tied strokes (Ex. XII-47). At 84-85 the E♭ and C drums are struck and keep ringing under the A♭, and nothing tells the player when to stop the three drums. The player could let them ring into the plagal bars 87-88, or into the fermata, where the A♭ and E♭ drums have to be retuned for the scherzo. The notated value ends at 85, so the fix states that value.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-436/work/sections/coda.ily (codaTimp):

```
R2.^\markup \small "soft sticks" | es4.\pp c8 aes,4~ | aes,2. | R2.^\markup \small \italic "damp" | ...
```

The damp falls on 86 b1, while the basses still hold the A♭ pedal, so it is not heard. The drums are quiet before the muta. In the score "damp" prints over the timpani's bar 86. In the part it prints over the 86-88 multi-bar rest, which now reads 3 + 2 bars as before (no extra break). If the composer would rather let the answer ring, the alternative is `aes,2.^\markup \small \italic "l.v."` at 85, with "damp" at 89. Either way the instruction should be written.

### 2. Optional: start the scherzo retuning of the C and D♭ drums at 86 (not applied)

Bar 92 asks for four pedal changes "in the pause": A♭→E (30"), C→G (28"), D♭→B (25"), E♭→E (23"). The scherzo's timpani enter at III 3 after about 3 s of solo horn. All four targets are inside Ex. XII-46's ranges. Adler says to mark tuning changes "especially if it must be accomplished rather quickly" (PDF 81). Four changes by gauge in a short pause are the most rushed tuning task in the movement. The D♭ drum is last struck at 80 and the C drum at 84. Neither pitch is the bass of 86-92, so they can be changed in the rests (seven bars, about 25 s under the rit.). The A♭ and E♭ drums should stay for the pause, because A♭ is the held pedal and E♭ is in the last chord, and a head moved through a sounding pitch can hum.

Fix (text only, in coda.ily codaTimp): add `^\markup \small \italic "muta D♭ in B, C in G"` to the 87 rest. Then change the 92 text to `"muta A♭ in E, E♭ in E" "(for the Scherzo, in the pause)"`. Because this changes the movement's ending text and the tuning hand-over that scherzo.md quotes ("retune in the pause: A♭ → E, C → G, D♭ → B, E♭ → E"), the composer must tell the scherzo thread if it is taken. I did not apply it, since the player can do this unasked and the present text is not wrong.

### 3. Restrikes at 62 and 80 are correct (no change)

Adler's Ex. XII-48 ties a roll that continues across bars. At 61→62 the bass moves from D♭ to G♭ (bSecCb `des,1 | ges,,2 fes,,2`), and at 79→80 the basses and contrabassoon restrike D♭ (aTwoCb `des,2. | des,2 bes,,4`). A restrike follows the bass in both places, so no tie should be added. This settles c296's optional item 2.

### 4. Drum layout and the 23" E♭ (no change)

E♭3 is a semitone above the bottom of the 23" drum (D3-A3), so the ppp roll "near the rim" at 67-70 is on a slack head. Soft sticks at the rim on a slack head speak easily at ppp. No other drum can take E♭3, because D♭3 needs the 25". A♭2 and C3 sit at the top of the 30" and 28" drums and are used only at p or below, except the f strokes at 60. Keep the c270 muta chain.

### 5. Dynamics against the tutti (no change)

The timpani are f, never ff, under the two ff peaks (61-62, 79-80). This follows Piston's warning as Adler reports it (PDF 55, 84). The pp< entry at 78 b3 under the f tutti was accepted at c407 (Rimsky PDF 136) and I do not re-raise it.
