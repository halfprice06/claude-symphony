## Findings

### 1. Bars 31–36: the clarinets cover the bass-clarinet solo (applied)
**Problem.** The transition is the bass clarinet's one melodic moment (cyclic rule). Its accompaniment is two clarinets, the soloist's own family, in its own register. At 32–33 they lie a third and a sixth under it. At 33 Cl 1 moves C–B–B♭ in quarters exactly under the solo's E♭–D–D♭, so the two lines run in parallel thirds in the same rhythm. At 34 Cl 1 sits on the solo's C4. At 35–36 Cl 1·2 hold E♭4/C4 *above* the solo's held A♭3 and its rising A♭–B♭–C, and bassoon 1 doubles the A♭3 in unison. For two bars the solo is an inner voice.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 264 and 267: Mozart keeps the orchestral horns out while the solo horn plays and writes no orchestral flutes into the flute concerto, "so that the solo flute will not encounter any color competition". PDF 259–260: the accompaniment "neither duplicates nor imitates the rhythmic patterns in the solo part". PDF 274: keep the accompaniment "registrally distant … especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments". Dvořák 9/ii keeps the clarinets silent under the cor anglais (above).
**Fix (applied, `sections/trans.ily`).** Clarinets tacet 31–36. The same harmony goes to cor anglais and bassoon 1, a double-reed colour against the single-reed solo. The common-tone diminished seventh at 33 b2 is kept (A♭ pedal, F, B, D). The lower voice is tied over the bar so the two accompanying voices do not move together into 34, and nothing doubles the solo's C4. At 35–36 the solo is the top voice.
```lilypond
transCorAnglais = { R2. | c'2.(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" | c'4 b4 bes4 | aes2.) | R2.*2 | }
transBsnOne = { R2. | aes2.(\pp | aes4 f2~ | f4 es2~ | es2.~ | es2) r4 | }
% transClOne / transClTwo deleted (rests)
```
This keeps cycle 20's rule (woodwinds alone on A♭, no V7 of D♭) and the dim7 at 33. It replaces cycle 7's "clarinets below BCl 32–34" with double reeds below it. That change kept the register and fixed the crossing, but it left the colour clash and the rhythmic shadowing.

### 2. Bars 41–44: the cor anglais' lower octave is buried in the Vln II chord (applied)
**Problem.** At 41 the cor anglais takes theme B an octave below the flute (A♭3–B♭4). Vln II's divided sul tasto chord (B♭3–G♭4) lies in exactly that band and sounds the cor anglais' own notes: D♭4 at 41 b2, F4 at 42 b4, D♭4 at 43 b3, C4 for all of 44. The chord also repeats the harmony the viola triplets already give, beat by beat (41: A♭–D♭–F / B♭–D♭–G♭; 44: G♭–C–E♭). The composer used the same reason (c021) to keep the cor anglais out of 37–40, but at 41 the chord is still there.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274 (registral distance, especially under a woodwind solo); PDF 262: in Tchaikovsky's PC 1 the soft sustained wind harmony and the solo are kept in separate layers, and roles change between sections.
**Fix (applied, `sections/bSec.ily`).** `bSecVlnTwo` bars 41–44 → `R1*4`. The sustained layer of 37–40 is now carried by the cor anglais line itself; violas, pizz. bass and harp keep the harmony and pulse. Vln II's *unis., ord.* entry at 45 becomes a fresh entry. This undoes part of cycle 9 ("Vln II sustained chords 37–44"), for 41–44 only.

### 3. Bar 49: the whole string body plays in the quotation's rhythm (applied)
**Problem.** The oboe's quotation of movement I's second theme (47–52) is accompanied by whole notes, which is fine (Verdi model, PDF 278). But at 49 Vln II (F♯–F♯–E♯), violas (A–B–G♯), cellos and basses all play quarter–half–quarter, the oboe's own rhythm F♯–D–C♯, so the quotation is played in block chords. At 49 b1 Vln II also doubles the oboe's F♯4.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 279: Bizet "is very careful to keep the instruments and the voice quite separate rhythmically"; PDF 259–260 (Tchaikovsky).
**Fix (applied).** Upper strings hold through beats 2–3. Only the bass moves to B with the oboe's D5, so beat 2 becomes iv7 (B–A–D–F♯), and the upper strings move only to V on beat 4:
```lilypond
% bSecVlnTwo bar 49:  fis'2. eis'4   (was fis'4 fis'2 eis'4)
% bSecVla    bar 49:  a2. gis4       (was a4 b2 gis4)
```
The quotation itself and its bass are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 45–46, open item "viola triplets through 45–46": close it, no change
The point has been open since cycle 17. Under Adler PDF 274 it should not be done. At 45–46 the theme head is in Vln I with cellos an octave below at D♭4–A♭3 (tenor clef). The triplet figure (A♭3–F4 in 37–44) would lie on the cello line's own notes. The bars' sustained Vln II/viola/bass background contrasts in rhythm with the head, which is what PDF 259–260 asks for. Recommend closing the item.

### 5. Bars 71–78: the counter-melody is doubled at the unison by the viola figure and the harp (not applied; protected)
**Problem.** The A′ counter-melody (horn 2 + cellos, E♭3–D♭4) is the melodic counterpoint of the passage, in the role of Tchaikovsky's Vln I line in Ex. XVII-3. The viola eighth figure (G3–A♭4) and the harp RH chords (G3–F4) lie in the same octave and keep striking its notes. Viola: B♭3 at 74 (8ths 1 and 5, under the held B♭3), D♭4 at 76 b3, B♭3 and D♭4 at 77, C♭4 and A♭3 at 78. Harp: A♭3/C4 at 73 b2, B♭3 at 74 b2, B♭3/D♭4 at 77 b2. The counter-melody is surrounded by figuration in its own register, while Adler's examples keep the counterpoint and the figuration in separate bands (PDF 260, 274).
**Fix (suggested).** Keep the figure and the instruments, and re-register only the notes that double the counter-melody. For example, viola 74 `es'8( g' bes') g'( es' g')`, 77 `es'8( g' bes') g'( es' bes')`, and harp RH 73 `r4 <es' aes'>2`, 74 `r4 <es' g'>2`, 77 `r4 <es' g'>2` (the same chords without the counter-melody's pitches). I did not apply this because the A′ texture 71–82 is on the do-not-touch list (cycles 12, 17). The stated reason is that A′ should differ from 23–30. This change keeps every difference and only removes the unisons.

### 6. Bars 71–74: octave doubling from the first note of A′ (suggestion, not applied)
Adler PDF 279: Bizet takes the first violins up to double the voice at the higher octave "only when the singer reaches her dramatic climax". In A′ the violins are in octaves from the pickup at 70 (p). The flute joins at 75 and the oboes at 79, but the octave split itself is spent at the start. Option: Vln I at the lower octave 70–74 (unison with Vln II, as at 22–26), splitting into octaves at 75 with the flute entry and the *mp <*. The A′ texture still differs from 23–30 (viola figure, horn 2, harp, clarinets). This is for the composer to weigh against the do-not-touch on 75–82, which this would not alter.

### 7. Bars 7–14, 15–22, 37–40, 65–70, 83–92: checked against the reading, no change
- 7–14: no other horn plays under the solo horn (PDF 264). The harp RH touches the horn's note on beat 2 at 7, 9, 10, 11, but the colour contrast is large and Adler (PDF 264, 273–274, Mozart and Liszt) accepts heterophonic emphasis in a contrasting colour.
- 15–22: the bass and Vln II share a half–quarter rhythm that matches the dialogue's second bars, but cycle 17's viola answers (dotted and eighth figures) break it in each of those bars.
- 37–40: flute alone in its register over sul tasto chords, triplets and pizz. bass. This matches PDF 268–270 (Sibelius, Bartók) and 278 (Verdi).
- 65–70 and 83–92: thin, transparent textures under a solo, with the timpani motive linking phrases at 84–85 (PDF 273, Beethoven).
