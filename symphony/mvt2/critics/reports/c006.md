## Findings

### 1. String slurs are phrase slurs that cannot be played on one bow (applied)

**Bars:** Vln I/II 22–30, 53–62, 70–82; Vc 23–30, 61–62, 71–82; Vln I 91–96.

**Problem:** The violins' theme at 22–26 sits under one slur of four and a third bars, at ♩=52 about 15 seconds. The slur at 26–30 is the same length and contains a crescendo to mf. Bars 57–60 put four bars (about 15 seconds) under one slur at mf rising to f. Bars 78–82 put five bars under one slur at ff, including a 4-beat B♭6. The cellos have the same problem at 23–30 and 78–82. Vln I at 91–96 has a 6-bar slur at pp–ppp of about 25 seconds.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts. Such slurs only confuse the performer. The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow." PDF 38–39: how many notes fit on a bow depends on tempo and dynamic. Mendelssohn gets only six notes on a bow at forte, and slow passages need "special caution … not to overload the bow" (Ex. II-21–22). PDF 39–40: the Liszt crescendo (Ex. II-23) "is impossible to perform as the composer has marked" until it is broken into bows (Ex. II-24–25). PDF 38: crescendo goes naturally on an up-bow, diminuendo on a down-bow. Dvořák 9/ii slurs one bar or less (corpus file above, lines 10–40).

**Fix (applied):** bow-length slurs. The upbeat is up-bow, the crescendo at 27 is up-bow and the diminuendo at 28 is down-bow. aOne Vln I (Vln II the same):

### 2. Viola double stops with no *div.* (applied)

**Bars:** Va 23–30, 61–64, 71–90; Vln II 63–64.

**Problem:** Without *div.*, the violas must play every chord as a double stop. At 28–30 and 76–81, <f' aes'>, <es' g'> and <es' aes'> fall on the D string in first position: two notes on one string, which only works after a shift to 3rd or 4th position. At 79–81 they come as ff tremolo double stops in that position. <bes des'> at 62 sits on the G string, at ff tremolo. The coda asks for an 8-bar ppp double stop (83–90). The texture is clearly two lines in each case.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 29–30, Ex. II-8 (viola): pairs on the same string are marked "Not possible". PDF 30: "If the word divisi does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop … unisoni … should appear." Dvořák 9/ii writes "div." on string chords (lines 188, 403, 429).

**Fix (applied):** `<c' es'>2.\p^\markup \italic "div."` at 23; `^\markup \italic "unis."` on the viola's first triplet at 37; `<des' f'>1:32\ff^\markup \italic "div."` at 61; `"div."` on Vln II `<e'' c'''>` at 63; `"unis."` at 65 (Vln II, Va); `"div."` at 71; `"(div.)"` reminder at 83 after the page turn; `"unis."` at 91.

### 3. Double basses go from arco to pizz. with no time, 36→37 (applied)

**Bars:** Cb 31–37.

**Problem:** The basses hold A♭1 arco through the last beat of 36 (the slowest bar, ♩=45), then play D♭ pizz. on the downbeat of 37.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck … instances … when no time is provided … should be avoided if at all possible." The return at 44→45 is done correctly, with three beats' rest.

**Fix (applied):** trans.ily `transCb = { aes,,2.\pp~ | aes,,2.~ | aes,,2.~ | aes,,2.~ | aes,,2.~ | aes,,2 r4 | }`. The pedal still sounds through the bass clarinet's cadence, and the timpani hold A♭.

### 4. Wind phrases with no place to breathe (applied)

**Bars:** B.cl 31–37; Fl 1 78–82; Hn 1 79–82.

**Problem:** The bass clarinet's solo sits under one slur from 31 beat 2 to 37 beat 2. Through the poco rit. that is about 22 seconds with no rest. Flute 1 plays from the pickup at 78 to 82 at ff, including five beats of B♭6, about 17 seconds in one breath. Horn 1 (a2 with Hn 2) plays four ff bars under one slur in the allargando.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 25: strings are "tireless", while "a wind or brass player usually needs to stop from time to time to breathe". The string bowings in finding 1 change bow at the same places.

**Fix (applied):** trans.ily `… c'2 bes4) \breathe | aes2.~(\> | aes4\p\< bes4 c'4 |` (the slur still closes at 37). aTwo Fl 1: `bes'''2.~\ff | bes'''4 aes'''2) \breathe | g'''4(\> es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 | aes''2.)\p |`. aTwo Hn 1: `des'4(\ff c'4 bes4 | aes4 bes4 c'4) \breathe | bes2(\> des'4 | c'2.)\p`.

### 5. Horn 1 endurance from 53 to 82 (not applied)

**Bars:** Hn 1 53–62, 67–82.

**Problem:** Horn 1 plays the counter-melody and the ff climax (53–62), rests four bars, plays the exposed solo (67–70), rests one beat, then plays twelve more bars (71–82) ending ff a2. That is about 65 seconds with one beat's rest, and the most exposed entry is at the end. Horn 2 rests at 71–78. The counter-melody (concert E♭3–D♭4) lies in the second horn's low register.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 25 (winds and brass must stop to breathe and rest). Cycle 4's reason, a single horn alone at 71–78, is kept if horn 2 plays it.

**Fix:** move aTwo bars 71–78 from `aTwoHnOne` to `aTwoHnTwo` (`es2\p aes8( g8 | f2) g4( | … | c'2)\f c'4 |`) and give Hn 1 `R2.*8` before the a2 at 79. This leaves Hn 1 fresh for the ff peak. It changes who plays, not what is played.

### 6. Horns 3·4 hold a tied ppp note for about 20 seconds at the end (not applied)

**Bars:** Hn 3·4 93–96.

**Problem:** A♭3 and E♭3 are tied from 93 to the fermata through the rit. (♩=48→20), about 20 seconds at ppp. That cannot be done in one breath. The two horns cannot cover each other because they hold different notes.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 25. Adler PDF 40 also notes that staggering is how a long held line is kept going.

**Fix:** let the horns drop out before the final chord, where the strings already hold the sound: `codaHnThree = { \clef treble R2.*8 | aes2.\pp | aes2. | aes2.~ | aes2\> r4\! | R2. | R2.\fermata | }`, and the same for Hn 4 with `es`. Or keep the horns and stagger them: Hn 3 breaks at 94 and Hn 4 breaks at 95.

### 7. Optional: muted violins for the coda (not applied)

**Bars:** Vln I/II 91–96.

**Problem:** None as written. This is a colour option that the reading points to. The violins rest 83–90 (about 28 seconds), which is enough time to put on mutes.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 59–60: muting gives "a very soft and smooth sound", but enough time must be allowed to put mutes on. Dvořák keeps the Largo's strings *con sordino* (corpus lines 10, 116, 400).

**Fix:** `codaVlnOne = { R2.*8 | des''2.\pp\downbow^\markup \italic "con sord." | …`, and the same in Vln II. Not applied because it is a colour choice for the composer.
