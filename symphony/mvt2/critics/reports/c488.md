## Findings (ranked)

None of these changes the print. All are MIDI-only, so the render matches what is printed.

### 1. Bars 37-44 and 63: the pizzicato basses and violas play bowed in the render (applied, MIDI only)

**Problem.** score.midi has one program per staff for the whole movement. The double basses' "pizz." at 37-44 and the sf pizz. at 63, and the violas' pizz. triplets at 41-44, play on the bowed contrabass (43) and viola (41) patches. In score.mp3 the B section opens with a bowed bass under the flute, at velocity 57 for a printed p, and the violas' plucked figure under the cor anglais is a bowed legato figure. The 63 sf pizz. under the trombone quotation is a bowed accent at velocity 75.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 127: "virtual instruments tend to become articulation-specific ... a violin tremolo will become a virtual instrument different from a pizzicato violin". PDF 144: a string library "will regularly incorporate separate virtual instruments that depict legato, sustain, staccato, pizzicato, harmonics, tremolo". PDF 133-134: the mockup should be "a close but inferior version in every aspect" of the performance, and it exists to catch mistakes. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 121: pizzicato basses under winds make the winds' sustained notes more distinct, and held bowed notes do not.

**Fix (applied, `sections/bSec.ily`, `score.ly`).** Switch the staff's MIDI patch at each pizz./arco change:
```
Cb  37: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "pizzicato strings" des,4\p^\markup \italic "pizz." ...
Cb  45: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "contrabass" bes,,1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" ...
Cb  63: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "pizzicato strings" a,,4\sf^\markup \italic "pizz." r4 r2 |
Cb  64: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "contrabass" a,,2\p^\markup \italic "arco" ...
Vla 41: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "pizzicato strings" \tuplet 3/2 4 { des8\pp\< ...
Vla 45: \set Staff.midiInstrument = "viola" f1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" ...
```
and in score.ly's equalizer: `("pizzicato strings" . (0.1 . 0.7))`. Every switch falls on a note that carries a dynamic, so each level is computed with the new patch. fixmidi.py keys channels on each track's first program, so the basses stay on channel 14 and the violas on 12, and the pizzicato program changes only their own channel. Result: Cb 37-44 at velocity 44 (was 57), 63 at 62 (was 75); violas unchanged at 37-44, because their range is the same.

### 2. Bars 61-64: the viola tremolo plays as held notes in the render (applied for the violas; violins and cellos not applied)

**Problem.** LilyPond ignores stem tremolo in MIDI. The violas' "non div., molto sostenuto, trem." at 61-64 b2 therefore sounds as plain held dyads. This is the one place where the score uses Casanelles's layering (a quiet tremolo inside a sustained string sound), and the mockup removes it.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 134-135: tremolo under sustained strings gives "a more emotional and verisimilar sustained passage". PDF 127: tremolo is its own instrument. The project already handles the timpani the same way: c360 and c388 turned rolls into strokes because a held note misrepresents them (rolls-midi.ily).

**Fix (applied, `sections/bSec.ily`, `score.ly`).** `\set Staff.midiInstrument = "tremolo strings"` before `<des' f'>1:32\ff` at 61, `\set Staff.midiInstrument = "viola"` before `gis2(\>\upbow` at 64 b3, and `("tremolo strings" . (0.1 . 0.7))` in the equalizer. The range matches the viola's, so every velocity is unchanged (61: 67, 63: 62, 64: 44) and the arco G♯ at 64 b3 keeps its level. The two patches measured within 1 dB of each other. **Not applied:** the violins' tremolo at 63-64 b2 and the cellos' at 63-64 b2. Vln I and II share one channel ("string ensemble 1"), and Vln II plays arco at 64 b3 while Vln I rests, so a switch there needs care. The cellos' equalizer range (0.2 . 0.8) differs from the viola's, so their levels would change. If the composer wants these too, use `\rollStrokes #32` on a MIDI-only copy, as for the timpani.

### 3. Bars 75-78: bassoon 1 misses the part's crescendo and f in the render; c400's comment is wrong (applied, MIDI only)

**Problem.** At 75-78 Bsn 1 doubles the cello and horn counter-melody. Its part reads p< (75), mf (77 b3), f (78), and on the shared score staff Bsn 2's marks stand for both. c400 added MIDI-only mp (76) and mf (77) and wrote that "at 78 ... Bsn 2's printed f already sets Bsn 1's level". It does not: in score.midi Bsn 1 is a separate voice (track "Bassoons 1·2:one"). v488 renders Bsn 1 at 75 as three notes at velocity 32 (flat p, because the \< is part-only), and at 78 its C♭4 at 44 (mf) against Bsn 2's F♭3 at 50 (f) and the cellos' C♭4 at 75 (f). The counter-melody's wind colour drops a layer at the deceptive cadence.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 141-142, Fig. 4.5: each printed level selects its own sample layer, so equal markings must reach equal layers. PDF 127: in a shared instrument each line needs its own dynamic shape. PDF 133-134: the mockup is there to catch exactly this kind of slip.

**Fix (applied, `sections/aTwo.ily`).**
```
es2(\p-\tag #'part \< -\tag #'midi \< f4 | aes2-\tag #'midi \mp -\tag #'midi \< des'4 | bes2 des'4-\tag #'part \mf -\tag #'midi \mf) \breathe |
ces'2-\tag #'part \f -\tag #'midi \f c'4(\< |
```
The print removes 'part and 'midi (bsnOneX), and genparts.py removes 'score and 'midi, so neither the score nor the part changes. New render: Bsn 1 32/36 (75), 38/40 (76), 41/44 (77), 50 (78), the same as Bsn 2. I also added a correction to c400's comment in the file.
