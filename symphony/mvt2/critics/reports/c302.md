## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 74: cello passing A♭3 on beat 2, as at 26 (applied; closes c301's open item)

**Problem.** c301 changed the cello counter-melody at 26 to B♭3–A♭3 in quarters, then G3. At 74, the same point in A′, it is still `bes2 g4`, so the cellos stop moving for two beats while the violins hold G. c301 left 74 open as untested because the harmony there is different: V4/3 over a B♭1 bass, where 26 has root-position V.

**Evidence.** Over the B♭ bass the A♭3 is a 7th that arises as a passing tone filling the third B♭–G. That is A&S's case for a 7th "prepared ... as a passing tone" in a descending-third motion (PDF 406, Ex. 24-9). It falls on the weak beat between consonant B♭3 and G3, and it is not a chordal 7th that would need a resolution. Its neighbours are Vla G4 and E♭4 and Cl D♭4. The A♭3–G4 major 7th is the same interval c301 accepted at 26. A MIDI check after the change found no new consecutive fifths or octaves (the all-pairs scan still finds 0 fifths; the octave-run list is unchanged), and the outer-voice count is still 0.

**Fix (applied).** `sections/aTwo.ily`, `aTwoVc`, bar 74:
```
| bes4( aes4) g4\! |      % was: | bes2 g4\! |
```
The bowing stays as at 26: two bows, with G3 on its own bow (c118, c032). Horn 2 joins only at 75, so no doubling changes.

### 2. Bar 59 b4: the 7th F♭ of VII°7 of V sounds in four parts (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** On 59 b4 (G–B♭–D♭–F♭ over the ♯4̂ bass G) the 7th F♭ is in Hn 1 and Vla (F♭4) and in Bsn 1 and Vc (F♭3). A&S's rule is "Don't double the temporary leading tone or the 7th" (PDF 439 item 3; PDF 419).

**Evidence.** Three of the four parts are one line, the Hn 1/Bsn 1/Vc counter-line G♭–E♭–F♭–E♭–F–G♭ (c117, c158), doubled at the octave. It resolves F♭–E♭ in both octaves at 60. The viola's F♭4 belongs to its sextuplet arpeggio, and the arpeggio leaves F♭ for D♭. This is orchestral octave doubling of one voice, not two independent voices on the 7th. The difficulties A&S list for this progression (PDF 425, Ex. 25-14) are absent. The soprano over the chord is B♭ (6̂ of D♭), not 3̂, so the upper voices do not all fall by step and there are no fifths. C, the leading tone of the resolution chord, is doubled only by the melody's octaves. Keep it: the counter-line is protected and it carries the build into 61.

### 3. Bars 58 b4–59: the leading tone of V7/IV in the melody falls to E♭ (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** At 58 b4 the chord is D♭7 (V7/IV) with its 7th C♭ in Cl 2 and Vla. Its temporary leading tone F is in the theme (F5/F6), and the theme falls F–E♭ at 59.

**Evidence.** The leading tone does reach G♭ in Hn 1 (F4–G♭4) and the cellos (F3–G♭3), but two octaves lower. A&S prefer a transfer into the next lower voice in the same register (PDF 406–407). The arrival chord at 59 b1 is G♭–B♭–D♭–E♭, IV with an added 6th (apparent II6/5, PDF 412–413). So the melody lands on a chord tone of the arrival, and the frustrated leading tone is the theme's own contour (theme B, protected). Cl 2 C♭5–B♭4 resolves the 7th directly. The viola's C♭4–G♭3–B♭3 is a decorated resolution (PDF 400–401). No change.

### 4. Bars 52 b4–53: the leading tone C at the return of D♭ (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** At 52 b4 (A♭7) the leading tone sounds in the violins' pickup C5 and in the violas' held C4. The violins leap to F5 and the violas rest at 53.

**Evidence.** The violas' C4 is answered by the harp's D♭4 half a beat later in the same register, which is A&S's preferred kind of transferred resolution (PDF 406–407). The violins' C–F is theme B's opening fourth. The C.a.'s 7th G♭4 resolves to F4 (PDF 409–410, extended 7th G♭–F–G♭–F over 51–53). The thin I at 53 (cellos and harp, no basses) is the planned restart of the build (protected order 53–61). No change.

### 5. The modulation plan read through PDF 422 (follows; no change)

A&S distinguish an applied chord's local meaning from its long-range meaning (PDF 422, Ex. 25-9; Ex. 25-19 is Beethoven's I♭7 = V7 of IV). The plan holds together at both levels:
- **47–50.** C♯7 at 47 is D♭7, I♭7 of D♭ acting as V7 of G♭. Its F♯ minor is G♭ minor, D♭'s iv by mixture, with its Neapolitan at 49 b3.
- **50 b3–51.** iv7 → G♯7 is V7/V in F♯ minor locally and A♭7, V7 of D♭, over the long range. Each chromatic step stays in one voice (D–D♯ in Vln II, B–B♯ in the cellos, A–G♯ in the C.a.), as PDF 420, Ex. 25-3a asks.
- **64.** E7 is V of A minor locally and the German sixth of A♭ over the long range (c210). The F♭/E thread therefore ends on E♭7 by half step.

The middle section prolongs D♭ (I–vi–iv–V–I), and 61–65 prolong a motion to A♭'s V. This is Dvořák's own procedure in 9/ii, a D♭ Largo with a C♯-minor middle. No change.

### 6. Coda 83–89: V7 of IV over the tonic pedal not used (considered, not recommended)

A&S's Unit 25 (Ex. 25-19 and Exercise 1b on PDF 439: "A♭: tonic pedal using I♭7 as V of IV") offers a G♭ at 86 to turn I into V7 of IV before the plagal close. I tested the idea against the score and do not recommend it. Bar 86 already carries the apparent °4/3 decoration of IV–I (D♮ in Cl 1 over A♭–C–F, PDF 414, Ex. 24-23), and the D♮ is the ♯4̂ of the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C. A G♭ would sound a diminished third against that D♮ and blur the tail. The plagal close 85–92 is protected. Recorded only.
