## Findings

### 1. Bar 47: Ob 1's niente circle touches the cor anglais's "solo, espr." (applied)
**Bars:** 47 beat 4, Ob 1·2 staff (hairpin below) and C.a. staff (text above).
**Problem.** c558 gave Ob 1's diminuendo on C♯5 (47 b1-3) a niente circle. The hairpin ends at the rest on b4, which is exactly where the cor anglais enters with "solo, espr." above its staff. At 300 dpi the circle sits about one pixel from the "s" of "solo": the circle reads as part of the text, or the text as the end of the oboe's hairpin. MOLA PDF 8 and Example 7 (PDF 12) name this kind of error: an anchored marking placed against another so that the two collide. Example 7 shows "a tempo" laid over the letter F and dynamics run into notes; here a hairpin end runs into the next instrument's direction at the solo hand-over, the one moment where the conductor most needs both clear.
**Fix (tested).** End the oboe hairpin 1.5 staff spaces earlier in the score only, in sections/bSec.ily, Ob 1 bar 47:
```
\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t cis''2.\mf -\tag #'score -\tweak shorten-pair #'(0 . 1.5) \> -\tag #'part \> r4\! |
```
(was `cis''2.\mf\> r4\! |`). The circle now stops just after the last C♯ beat, with about ¾ space clear of "solo,". Pitches, rhythm, printed levels and the c558 niente are unchanged; the part keeps its own hairpin (parts book pixel-identical).

### 2. Recorded, no change: tempo-word placement on every page
Every tempo word and letter prints above the top staff of the system and above Vln I, or above Vln II (37, 47, 48) or Vla (83) when the first violins rest, as MOLA PDF 4 asks. At 31-36 only woodwinds play, so the C.a./B.cl. top staff carries "poco rit." alone, which is the "similarly positioned staff" case. The extra copy above the horns (1, 61, 64) is common publisher practice and does not conflict.

### 3. Recorded, no change: parts against MOLA PDF 5-7
Clefs, keys and cautionaries at line ends; ranges on multi-rests (Ex. 3); letters that match the score and stay on one line with their tempo words (Ex. 4, c225/c355/c455); cues in the reading key with the source instrument named (Ex. 4 "Viol. I"); "muta in A" at 92 (Ex. 5); horns and timpani without signatures; 7.4 mm staves, 20 mm margins, indented first line, instrument headers, odd/even page numbers. The viola part has 11 systems, inside MOLA's limit of 12 for an instrument without many ledger lines; the one-page rule for this book keeps it that way, and I found no collisions in it at 150 dpi (78-92 is the densest line, all texts separate).

### 4. Recorded, no change: text style of timpani instructions
"Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭", "muta G in A♭ ..." (4), "soft sticks", "medium sticks" print in upright small type, while "damp", "near the rim" and the bar-92 "muta A♭ in E ..." print in italic. MOLA says nothing about type style, so this is not a MOLA point; I note it for a future Gould cycle and do not propose a change.
