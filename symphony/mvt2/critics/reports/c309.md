## What else I studied

**The master.** I compared the score with Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 1, ii (Adagio cantabile ma non tanto, E♭ major, 168 bars; Wikipedia and Tchaikovsky Research). IMSLP's copy of the movement (file #00677) is behind a bot check, so I could not open the score. The facts I used come from these descriptions:
- The movement is built on one melody, taken from *The Storm*, and varied by degrees (Wikipedia, sin80).
- It opens with a string introduction.
- The main theme is an oboe solo, answered by the cellos (classicalmusic-notes.com; classicalsource.com review).

From memory, not checked against a score: the theme returns at the climax ff in the four horns, over string figuration, and the movement ends with the muted-string opening.

**v299 against v309.** I diffed every file. Most changes are MIDI-only (c300), engraving fixes (c305, c308), or the cello passing notes at 26 and 74 (c301, c302), which are improvements. The one change to the sound is c299's: Vln I drops to pitch from the pickup at 74 b3 through 77. Finding 1 deals with it.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 74 b3-77: the A′ build loses its upper octave just as the crescendo starts (got worse since v299). **Applied.**

**Problem.**
- Since c299, Vln I plays in unison with Vln II from 74 b3 to 77, then jumps a minor seventh (B♭4 to A♭5) into 78.
- So 75-76 sound at the same pitch and with the same violin unison as the plain first statement at 27-28. The top voice falls from E♭6 and C6 (71-73) to B♭5 (analysis.txt: the top for 73-76 went from B♭6 in v299 to C6; 75-76 now peak at B♭5). The pickup at 74 b3 drops a tenth, from G5 to E♭4.
- At 77, Fl 1 plays the upper octave (G6, E♭6, D♭6, C6, B♭5) alone, above Vln I + Vln II + Cl 1, which are all on the same G5 line.

**Evidence.**
- Adler describes a build that adds octaves step by step. Wagner "holds back 'the highs' ... and build[s] the crescendo as upper octaves gradually double the violins" (PDF 172). Barber "enlarges the doubling slightly in every measure" (PDF 150).
- Here the build (mp< at 75, f at 78, ff at 79) begins by taking an octave away.
- Mozart 40/iv arranges the octaves so that "the flute is not left alone in its upper reaches" and the most heavily scored octave is heard as the theme (PDF 151). At 77 the opposite happens: a single mp flute stands above three instruments in unison.
- c079 had paired the two octaves at 77 (Fl 1 + Vln I above, Cl 1 + Vln II below), and c299 undid that.
- Tchaikovsky's reprises of his theme also grow fuller at each stage; they do not thin out.

**Fix.** Keep c299's aim: B♭6 is first heard at 79, and 75-76 stay at pitch. Put only bar 77 back in the upper octave. G6 already sounds in the flute at 77, so no new top note is added. aTwo.ily, aTwoVlnOne, bar 77:

```
| g'''4(\< \once \override Beam.positions = #'(0.18 . -0.18) es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8) | aes''2\f ...
```
(was `g''4(\< es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8)`)

Vln I now moves to the upper octave on a new bow (up-bow, the crescendo of c258), with the winds' entry. 77-78 descend by step into the deceptive cadence on A♭5, as before c299. The minor-seventh leap 77→78 becomes a major-seventh leap A♭5→G6 at the slur break 76→77.

**Result.** The build is clean: 0 warnings, 8 pages with the same system breaks, 0 outer-voice parallels, analysis.txt unchanged. The parts book is 30 pages and each part fits on one page except the harp. The Violin I part was checked by eye.

**Effect on other movements.** Bars 74-78 are not among the finale's quotations (41-44, 80-81, 85-89), and nothing exported changes.

### 2. Horns and the main theme at the climax (recorded; no change)

Tchaikovsky gives his climactic statement of the theme to the horns. Here the theme opens in the horn (7-14), but at the peak (79) it is in the violins and woodwinds, with Hn 1·2 on the counter-melody at f.
- Adler's "save a colour for an event" (PDF 37) and Schuller's unison warning (PDF 26) could support a four-horn theme at 79.
- But the A′ texture 71-82 is protected, and c034/c074 keep the horns as middleground on purpose.
- Two horns a2 on the counter-melody already balance as one trumpet (PDF 44).

The scoring at 79 follows the reading as it stands. I do not recommend a change.

### 3. One theme against two themes and quotations (recorded; no change)

Tchaikovsky's Adagio grows one melody over 168 bars. This movement has two themes and two cyclic quotations in 92 bars, with the colour changing every 4-6 bars in 31-52.
- Adler warns against changing effects so often that the form suffers (PDF 146, 176).
- But each solo here keeps its colour for a full phrase.
- c300 recorded the 31-52 plan, and the proportions are closed (c128, c303).

A conductor would hear a Dvořák-type episodic Largo rather than Tchaikovsky's single arc. That is the stated model, not a flaw.

### 4. The frame (follows)

Tchaikovsky returns to his opening string sound at the end. Here 89-92 return to bar 1's sustained C5 in Vln I sul D over an A♭ chord in the strings at ppp. There are no flutes at the end, which avoids Brahms's "dangerous" chord (PDF 162-163). Nothing to change.

### Comparison with v299

Apart from finding 1, nothing is worse:
- The cello passing notes at 26 and 74 add motion.
- The MIDI fades at 63 and 66-70 and the engraving fixes leave the printed music as it was.

## Do not touch

Theme A and the horn solo 7-14, the protected A′ texture 71-82 with B♭6 first at 79, the 63-64 quotation and its sf>p control chord, and the plagal close with C5 on top at 87-92 already meet the reading and the model. Leave them, and the 8-page layout, as they are.
