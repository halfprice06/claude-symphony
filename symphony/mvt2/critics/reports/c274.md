## Findings

### 1. Bar 51: the A♭7 has no third on any beat (applied)

**Problem.** At 51 the strings hold Cb A♭1, Vc E♭3 and Vln II E♭5. The cor anglais plays A♭4 and then G♭4. The third, C, comes only in the violas' sotto voce off-beat eighths, so for a whole bar the dominant seventh of D♭ sounds on its beats as a bare A♭–E♭ with the seventh. Every chord in the appendix tables has its third, soft chords included (PDF 505–507, chords 13, 14, 16). In each table the string group alone is a complete chord. Ch. IV (PDF 116) also says the harmonic basis under a woodwind solo is a separate layer, set apart by colour and dynamics. Here that layer is the strings, so the chord has to be complete within the strings. The off-beat figure itself is right (c107, Adler V-32). But in an on/off-beat accompaniment the sustained parts carry the rest of the chord, and here the sustained parts lack the chord's most important tone. c272 left this point open.

**Fix (bSec.ily).**
```
bSecVc:  fis2 b,4 cis4 | fis2 b,2 | es1 | es1\< |    ->   fis2 b,4 cis4 | fis2 b,2 | c1 | es1\< |
bSecVla: ... r8 d'8-_ r8 b8-_ | r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ |
     ->  ... r8 d'8-_ r8 b8-_ | r8 es'8-_ r8 es'8-_ r8 es'8-_ r8 es'8-_ |
```
- The cellos hold the third, C3, a tenth above the bass. That is the register of the tenor thirds in chords 1 and 8, well above the low limit of PDF 124.
- The violas' off-beats move to E♭4. Without that change the leading tone would be doubled at the octave (c212's rule).
- At 52 the cellos go to E♭3 and the violas hold C4, so bars 51–52 form a voice exchange.
- The bass, the chord and the Bm7→A♭7 pivot (c242) do not change. The cor anglais quotation and c032's viola D4 at 50 are untouched.
- Voice leading checked: Vc B2→C3→E♭3, Vla B3→E♭4→C4, and parallel tenths between the cellos and Vln II at 50→51. There are no 5ths or octaves.

### 2. Bar 50 beats 1–2: F♯-minor tonic without its third on a beat (applied)

**Problem.** At 50 b1–2 the sounding notes are F♯2, F♯3, C♯5 (C.a.) and F♯5 (Vln II). The A is again only in the violas' off-beats. c202 fixed the same fault at 49 by giving Vln II an A5. The violins cannot take A on b1, because Vln II's E♯5 at 49 b4 has to resolve to F♯5.

**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo).**
```
fis''2 d''2 | es''1 |    ->    fis''4( a''4) d''2 | es''1 |
```
- The E♯ resolves on the beat, and the third enters on b2 while the cor anglais holds its C♯5.
- The violins therefore move against the solo's held note, not in its rhythm (c027's reason holds).
- The A5 repeats c202's A5 from 49.
- A5→D5 over F♯→B is parallel tenths with the bass, and it is a sixth to a third against the cor anglais.

This one is less important than finding 1 because the A still sounds within an eighth note. Take it or leave it with finding 1.

### 4. Bars 79–83 against Ex. 209 (Scheherazade iii, the book's one slow-tempo climax among the tutti examples) (no change)

Ex. 209 and bars 79–83 share these features:
- The melody is in octaves across the flutes, oboes and violins.
- The clarinets, bassoons and horns hold the harmony.
- The brass plays one degree under, at f.
- The climax gives way within two or three bars to pp.

Rimsky also puts the cellos on the melody, and here the cellos have the counter-melody (protected, c217). The release 80–83 follows the order of Ex. 210: brass out, then the woodwind, with the strings and timpani last. No change.
