## Findings

All findings are "no change". I rank them by how much each would matter if it were wrong.

### 1. The remote key areas are in proportion: about 9% of the movement, against about 12% in Mozart 41/ii (45–51, 62–64). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 592: remote areas are possible only "without sacrificing the underlying diatonicism". PDF 603 (Points 5): they "can threaten the continuity and unity of a composition".

**Score.** Quarter beats from sections.txt: 18 + 72 + 18 + 112 + 18 + 36 + 30 = 304. F♯ minor runs from 47 to 50 (about 16 beats with the C♯7 approach) and F♭/A minor from 62 b3 to 64 (about 10 beats), so about 26 beats, 9%. Everything else is A♭ or its diatonic area D♭ (IV).

**Corpus.** Mozart 41/ii (F major, 3/4, 303 quarter beats in the MCTL harmony file): F 58%, C (V) 17%, D minor (vi) 12%, and the chromatic areas C minor (v, mixture, 9.6%) plus a sequence through B♭ minor, F minor, C minor and G minor (beats 150–160, 3% together), so about 12.6% chromatic. The two movements use their remote areas at nearly the same rate, and both put them in the middle of the movement. Tchaikovsky 6/iv by contrast stays in B minor and D major (73% and 26% in the MCTL file), with no remote area long enough to be annotated. This movement sits between the two, which suits a Dvořák-model Adagio. c253's proportions (36/40/24%, climax at 65–67% of the beats) are unchanged in v393: bar 61 begins at beat 204 of 304 (67%), the A′ peak at 79 at beat 262 (86%).

**Fix.** None.

### 2. The A minor area is prepared the way Haydn prepares C minor in *The Creation* (1–2, 5, 20–21, 62–65). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 596–598 (Ex. 32-16, 32-17): a double-mixture area is the hardest to integrate. Haydn does it through one top-voice pitch: the C minor section's E♭ is prepared in the A major section by many D♯–E motions, and the return is made by an augmented sixth whose D♯ is that E♭ respelt.

**Score.** A minor is ♭II of A♭ with a minor third (B𝄫 minor, double mixture). Its characteristic pitch is E♮ (F♭), and the movement prepares it in the main key before the area arrives: Vla E♮4→E♭4 at 1–2, the F♭ of the iv6/4 at 5, Vln II's E♮ in the French sixth at 20–21 resolving to the E♭ octave at 22. After the area, E falls to E♭ in the bass at 64→65 (Ex. 32-10a) and F♭ returns inside the key at 78 and 80–81 and in the coda at 88. This is the reading's procedure: the remote area's pitch is heard as a semitone above the dominant note several times before and after it. coherence/mvt2.md already records the F♭/E–E♭ thread six times; the reading gives it the Haydn precedent.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. The B section does not chain minor areas without relief (45–53). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 596: composers "avoid an unrelieved succession of minor chords"; secondary-mixture minor areas are "less generally useful".

**Score.** B♭ minor (vi of D♭) at 45–46 is followed by a major-major-minor chord, D♭7 = C♯7 at 46 b4–47, then F♯ minor (= G♭ minor, iv of D♭ by mixture) at 47–50, then G♯7 = A♭7 and G♭7 at 51 and A♭7 at 52, and D♭ major for nine bars (53–61) before A minor appears at 63. Each minor area is entered and left through a major-quality dominant seventh, and the D♭ major build sits between the two minor areas. The minor areas also fall on the movement's two quotations, so each has a motivic reason (PDF 601–603).

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Do not add a chromatic sequence to the B section (53–60). Considered and not recommended.

**Reading.** Points for Review 3 (PDF 603): two key areas can be connected by a chromatic sequence. Mozart 41/ii does this at about bars 51–54 (MCTL beats 150–160: D minor, B♭ minor, F minor, C minor, G minor, back to D minor).

**Why I checked it.** This movement has no sequence (c382 recorded this). The build 53–60 intensifies by orchestration and register over D♭ harmony, not by moving through keys, so a sequence would be the obvious textbook way to add motion to the middle section.

**Why not.** (a) The sequence's job is to *connect* key areas. Here the two remote areas are already joined to D♭ and A♭ by single enharmonic dominants (Findings 1 and 3), so there is nothing left for a sequence to connect. (b) D♭ is confirmed late, at 61 (c373, protected); a sequence through other keys in 53–60 would weaken that arrival and make the climax a key arrival instead of a dynamic one. (c) Dvořák 9/ii, the main model, also builds its middle-section climax without a key sequence (c263, c373). (d) It would add a third remote passage and push the share in Finding 1 above Mozart's.

**Fix.** None. Record so the question is not reopened without an answer to (a)–(d).

### 5. The exit from the climax follows the reading's ♭VI → ♭II → V route, and 78 restates it inside the key (61–65, 78–82). No change.

PDF 593: "the usual goal of ♭VI is a cadential V"; in Widmung "♭VI moves to V7 through ♭II". Bars 62–65 are that route: F♭ (♭VI, written E) at 62, A minor (♭II area) at 63–64 with the trombone quotation, then E→E♭ to V7 at 65 (Ex. 32-10a). The retransition then holds V for six bars (65–70), which is long enough to undo the chromatic area before theme A returns at 71. In the reprise, 78 reaches F♭ by the other route the reading names, a deceptive cadence (PDF 592, Ex. 32-11), and the bass moves F♭→E♭ by semitone in the same bar before ii6/5–V at 79–81. The return section therefore repeats the climax's chromatic step inside the tonic, as the reading's "worked out" conflict requires (PDF 592). Already recorded by c250 and c263; confirmed in v393.

### 6. Both ends of the movement use the Widmung common-tone modulation (1–6, 92). No change.

PDF 586–587: no pivot chord, a common tone in an exposed voice, major triads a third apart. At 1–2 movement I's C-major chord becomes A♭ with Vln I's C5 held on top and the inflection E♮→E♭ in the violas, at ppp/pp (the gentle, falling kind, PDF 585). At the end, A♭ is held as G♯ into the scherzo in E (Hn 1 G♯4), the exact A♭→E (F♭) progression of Ex. 32-3. Nothing in this report changes the ending, the exported themes or the quotations, so nothing needs to go to the scherzo or finale threads.
