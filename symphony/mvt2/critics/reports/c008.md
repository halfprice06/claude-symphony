## Findings

### 1. A′ return (70–78): starts mf with the bass as loud as the tune, so the crescendo to 79 has nowhere to grow
- **Bars:** 70 (pickup), 71–78.
- **Problem:** Vln I·II enter at 70 mf; Vc, Bsn 2, Cb and harp are mf; Vla mp; Cl p; Hn 2 p on the same counter-melody as the cellos at mf. The only dynamic step before the ff peak is one hairpin at 75 to f at 78.
- **Evidence:** PDF 136: mf re-entries are "colourless and common-place", and the remark "is capable of wider application" (here the return of the main theme). PDF 130: a prolonged crescendo is built by adding instruments strings → woodwind → brass, which the score already does (Fl 75, Hn 1 78, Ob/BCl/Hn 3·4/timp 79), but the dynamics start too high for it to work. PDF 133: the harmonic basis should be "of inferior dynamic power" to the melody; at 71 the bass (Bsn 2, Cb, harp LH) is level with the tune.
- **Fix (applied):** retr.ily Vln I `r2 es''8(\p^\markup \italic "espr." f''8`, Vln II `es'8(\p`; aTwo.ily: Vc `es2\p` (71), `es2(\mp\<` (75); Hn 2 `es2(\mp\<` at 75; Vla `c'8(\pp` at 71, `c'(\p\<` at 75; Cl 1·2 `\pp` at 71, `\p\<` at 75; Bsn 2 and Cb `\pp` at 71, `\p\<` at 75; harp LH `\pp` at 71, `\p` at 75; Vln I·II `\mp\<` at 75; Fl 1 enters `\mp\<` at 75. Arrival f at 78 and ff at 79 unchanged.

### 2. The peak at 79 has no emphasis beyond ff
- **Bars:** 78–79, Vln I·II.
- **Problem:** The violins leap from C to B♭ (a seventh) onto the movement's peak note with no slur and no stress. Everything else at 79 is also ff, so the peak does not stand out.
- **Evidence:** PDF 129–130: to stress a note, "a chain of three or four grace notes, in the form of a scale" may be used in strings or wind, "a kind of upward glide", "connected to the main note by a slur"; not into three- or four-note string chords. Ex. 254 (PDF 418) shows this in the strings at a tutti arrival. Vln I and II are single lines at 79, so the device fits.
- **Fix (applied):** aTwo.ily bar 78, `aes''2)\f \afterGrace c'''4 { f'''16( g''' aes''' } | bes'''2.)~\ff` in Vln I and `aes'2)\f \afterGrace c''4 { f''16( g'' aes'' } | bes''2.)~\ff` in Vln II. `\afterGrace` keeps the grace notes inside bar 78 (a `\grace` at the start of 79 was engraved at the end of the previous system, across the break).

### 3. 63–64: under the ff trombone quotation, the strings play f> and the woodwind leave before the brass
- **Bars:** 63–65.
- **Problem:** At 63 the woodwind stop, the strings play an f tremolo with a hairpin that ends on a rest (no target dynamic), the bass trombone stays f to the end of 64, and 65 is pp. The diminuendo removes woodwind first and brass last, and the strings' level competes with the quoted line.
- **Evidence:** PDF 129 (a): the strings' sf attack on a chord "is also employed for a sf-dim."; PDF 133: the harmonic basis should be of inferior dynamic power; PDF 130: diminuendo removes brass, then woodwind, then strings. Dvořák uses `\fz\>` on held string chords in 9/ii (strings.notesL.ily 42, 179).
- **Fix (applied):** bSec.ily 63–64: Vln I `e'''1:32\sf\> | e'''2:32\p r2`, Vln II `<e'' c'''>1:32\sf\> | <e'' c'''>2:32\p r2`, Vla `<e' a'>1:32\sf\> | <e' a'>2:32\p r2`, Vc `a1:32\sf\> | a2:32\p r2`, Cb `a,,1\sf\> | a,,2\p d,4 e,4`; bass trombone `a,1\f | a,2\> d,4 e,4\p`. Trombone 1's quotation is unchanged (ff marcato, protected).

### 4. Horn re-entries at 53 and 57 are mp and mf
- **Bars:** 53 (Hn 1, after resting since 14), 57 (Hn 2, first entry of the movement in the B section).
- **Problem:** Both horns return at the dynamic level Rimsky-Korsakov calls the weakest choice for a brass re-entry.
- **Evidence:** PDF 136: "After a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff ... mezzo-forte or mezzo-piano produces a colourless and common-place effect." Here the horns join a crescendo, so ff is wrong; the quieter entry is the one the reading supports.
- **Fix (applied):** bSec.ily Hn 1 `aes8(\p^\markup \bold "1."^\markup \italic "espr."` at 53; Hn 1 `aes'2(\mp\<` and Hn 2 `f'2\mp\<` at 57 (equal, as the shared staff needs). They still reach f at 59.

### 5. 15–22 and 37–44: the oboe/violin dialogue and theme B have no phrase dynamics
- **Bars:** 15–22 (Ob 1, Vln I), 37–44 (Fl 1, Cl 1).
- **Problem:** Sixteen bars of melody are marked p with no hairpin. Theme B has no character word at its first statement, unlike the horn theme (6, "dolce espr.") and the cellos (23, "cantabile").
- **Evidence:** PDF 130: short crescendi and diminuendi are produced "by natural dynamic means", i.e. the players' own shading, which the score must ask for. Emphasis of a phrase's high point by hairpin is the first means listed on PDF 129.
- **Fix (applied):** aOne.ily: Ob 1 `c''4.(\p\< des''8 es''4 | f''2\> es''4)\!` (15–16), `c''4(\< ... | aes'2\> g'4)\!` (19–20); Vln I `aes''8(\p\< ... | es''2\> des''4)\!` (17–18), `f''8(\< g''8 es''4 d''4\! |` (21). bSec.ily: Fl 1 and Cl 1 at 37 `^\markup \italic "dolce cantabile"`; at 41 `\<`, at 42 `\mp\>` on the A♭ high point, `\p` at 44.

### 6. Coda 93–96: the morendo is held by horns 3·4; the clarinets are silent
- **Bars:** 93–96.
- **Problem:** Horns 3·4 hold A♭3/E♭3 tied for four bars into a ppp fermata (already open in HISTORY as a playability point). The last sound of the movement is given to brass.
- **Evidence:** PDF 130: "Clarinets specialise in diminuendo effects and are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)"; diminuendo removes brass first. Dvořák 9/ii ends the same way: clarinet 1 `bf1\p\>~bf4\!` in the last bars (woods.notesL.ily 366).
- **Fix (applied):** coda.ily: horns 3·4 `aes2.~ | aes2\> r4\! | R2. | R2.\fermata` and `es2.~ | es2\> r4\! | R2. | R2.\fermata` (they play 91–94, keeping the F–F♭–E♭ line); new `codaClOne = { R2.*10 | aes2.~\pp | aes2.~ | aes2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | aes2.\ppp\fermata | }` and `codaClTwo = { R2.*10 | es2.~\pp | es2.~ | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata | }`. The clarinets enter under the horns at 93 on the same pitches, so the hand-over is not heard as a new event.

### 7. The broadening before 61 is in the MIDI but not in the printed score
- **Bars:** 60.
- **Problem:** global.ily slows 64 → 62 → 58 in bar 60 (`\mt`) while the printed instruction is still "poco a poco animando". A conductor would not broaden before the Largamente.
- **Evidence:** PDF 130: a crescendo glorified "into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes" needs the arrival prepared; the tempo of that preparation must be written for the players. The task rules also require printed tempo words and `\mt` steps to agree.
- **Fix (applied):** global.ily `\mt 64 s1 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco allarg." \mt 62 s2 \mt 58 s2` (no change to the MIDI).

### 8. Wind doublings of the A′ tune have no phrasing slurs
- **Bars:** 75–78 (Fl 1), 79–82 (Ob 1).
- **Problem:** The violins are slurred, the flute and oboe doubling them are not, so the winds would tongue every note of a legato tune.
- **Evidence:** PDF 129–130 treats the slur as the binding of a melodic group; Ex. 254 and 256 (PDF 418–419) slur the doubling winds with the strings.
- **Fix (applied):** aTwo.ily Fl 1 `aes''4.(\mp\< c'''8 f'''4~ | ... | aes''2)\f c'''4(`; Ob 1 `bes''2.~(\ff | bes''4 aes''2) | g''4(\> es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 | aes'2.)\p`.
