## Findings

### 1. Bars 61 b3–62: the brass chord has an empty octave between trombone 2 and the horns (applied)

**Problem.** At the climax the brass layout is now tuba D♭2, bass trombone D♭3, trombone 2 F3, horn 2 F4, horns 3·4 a2 A♭4. At 62 it is G♭1, G♭2, G♭3, G♭4/B♭4 and then F♭1, F♭2, A♭3, A♭4/C♭5. Each chord has an octave inside the brass with nothing in it (F3–F4, G♭3–G♭4, A♭3–A♭4). The trombone layer is root and third or root alone, and the horn layer has no root. This happened in stages. c014 approved a layout with trumpets and trombone 1 in the middle. c060 made the trumpets tacet. c069 moved trombone 1's A♭3–B♭3–C♭4 to horn 4, and c086 then put horn 4 a2 with horn 3 an octave higher. Each step had its own reason, but together they emptied the brass middle.

**Evidence.** PDF 100: "Here, as in the wood-wind, part writing should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals." PDF 101: "the third trombone and the tuba usually form the bass in octaves, and the three upper parts are generally allotted to the two remaining trombones reinforced by a trumpet or two horns in unison". In v104 only one of those "remaining trombones" plays, and it doubles horn 2's line. The wide "double chorus" layout on PDF 103 is not a defence, because in that diagram each layer is a complete chord. PDF 114: balance "is much more important in dealing with long sustained chords", and 61–62 is held tenuto at Largamente.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).**
```
bSecTbnTwo = { R1*23 | es1\pp\< \breathe | f2--\mf aes2-- | bes2-- ces'2-- | R1*2 | }
```
Trombone 2 keeps F3 on 61 b1–2, so c099's objection (E♭3→A♭3 against the bass A♭→D♭ makes fifths) does not arise, and the closed "low third" item is not reopened. It then takes trombone 1's original middle line A♭3–B♭3–C♭4 an octave below horns 3·4. The brass now reads D♭2 D♭3 A♭3 F4 A♭4 (61 b3), G♭1 G♭2 B♭3 G♭4 B♭4 (62 b1) and F♭1 F♭2 C♭4 A♭4 C♭5 (62 b3). That is close, complete, and has a trombone under the horns as PDF 101 and 103 ask. The F♭ chord's fifth C♭, carried until now only by Cl 2 and Hn 3·4 at C♭5, gets a middle-register note. Horn 3·4 a2, trombone 1's rest before the quotation, the low brass mf and all woodwind notes are unchanged. The only new allpairs pairs are Tbn 2 doubling Hn 3·4 at the octave (a duplication, PDF 103–104). They replace the same number of Tbn 2/Hn 2 octave pairs, so the count is unchanged and there are no new fifths.

This answers the "61–62 spacing" do-not-touch from c094. That critic judged the woodwind spacing (PDF 96) and the horn pair. The brass group's own spacing (PDF 100) was last checked at c014, when trumpets and trombone 1 still filled the gap.

### 2. Bars 87–88: the muted horns are paired with a throat-register clarinet; give that line to the cor anglais (applied)

**Problem.** In the plagal close, Cl 2 plays F4–F♭4 an octave above muted horn 4 (F3–F♭3) and the violas, in the clarinet's throat register. Rimsky names two combinations for stopped or muted horns: with oboe or cor anglais, which they resemble, and with *deep* clarinet notes for a dark tone. This chord uses neither. Meanwhile the cor anglais, the movement's second solo voice (31–34, 41–44, 49–52), has been silent since 53.

**Evidence.** PDF 107: "Stopped or muted notes in trumpets and horns resemble the oboe and Eng. horn in quality; the combination of these instruments produces a magnificent tone." PDF 110 (diagram): stopped horn and 2 oboes in one chord, p. PDF 107 (diagram): the dark tone needs "middle notes in stopped horns and deep notes in the clarinet". Here the clarinet is an octave above the horns. c084 called this clarinet register "rather pale" (Adler part 1 PDF 210). Dvořák 9/ii bar 122 ends the cor anglais inside a soft horn chord.

**Fix (applied, coda.ily and score.ly).** Pitches are unchanged. The F4–F♭4 moves to the cor anglais, and Cl 2 enters on E♭4 at 89 for its existing morendo.
```
codaClTwo = { R2.*6 | es'2.~-\tag #'part \pp | es'2~\> es'4~\! | es'2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | es'2.\ppp\fermata | }
codaCorAnglais = { R2.*4 | f'2.\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" | fes'2. | R2.*4 | }
```
score.ly: `\pc \clOne \removeWithTag #'part \clTwo`, so the shared clarinet staff does not print a second pp at 89 while Cl 1 is already pp (Cl 2 had no part tags before). The cor anglais staff appears in the last system, which is alone on page 8 with room to spare, and the score stays at 8 pages. The plagal pitches, Vln I's C5, the viola doubling of horn 4 (c087) and the clarinet morendo are all kept. allpairs shows only the cor anglais taking over Cl 2's octave pairs with horn 4 and the violas at 87–88. The pair at 89 disappears, because Cl 2 now attacks E♭4 there instead of moving to it.
