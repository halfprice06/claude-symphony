## Findings

### 1. Strings' tempo mark at 37 is set in a different style from every other tempo mark (applied)
**Bars:** 37, the "Poco più mosso" printed over Vln II (Vln I rests, so the mark comes from `\strMark`).
**Problem.** At 300 dpi the winds read "**Poco più mosso** (♩ = 60)": the words bold, the parenthesis, the note and "60" in the normal weight, no space after "(". The strings read "**Poco più mosso ( ♩= 60)**": everything bold, a word space after "(", no space before "=", and a larger note (`\fontsize #-2` against LilyPond's `\smaller`). The other string tempo marks (1, 61, 65) come from the Vln I staff's own Metronome_mark_engraver and match the winds. So 37 is the only place where one tempo is printed two ways on the same page. The cause is the hand-made markup: `\strMark` wraps its tempo argument in `\bold`, and the markup was written as separate words.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 13: "Effective communication results from establishing a convention and adopting a consistent approach." PDF 9: the program applies rules, "but the operator must decide if these are the most apt solutions, and a trained eye is required to refine the settings". LilyPond's own formatter (`metronome-markup`, translation-functions.scm lines 107-143) is the convention the rest of the score uses. MOLA PDF 4 puts the string copy of the mark there so the violins read the same instruction as the top of the score.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-555/work/sections/bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo, bar 37).** Replace
```
\strMark "E" \markup { "Poco più mosso (" \fontsize #-2 \general-align #Y #DOWN \note {4} #1 "= 60)" }
```
with
```
\strMark "E" \markup { "Poco più mosso" \medium \concat { "(" \smaller \general-align #Y #DOWN \note {4} #UP " = 60)" } }
```
`\medium` cancels strMark's `\bold` for the parenthesis, as the winds print it. Checked at 300 dpi: the two marks now match in weight, spacing and note size. The string copy is tagged score-only, so the parts do not change. Nothing else on the page moved except a vertical shift of one or two pixels in the lower systems of page 2. The breaks are the same.

### 2. Winds' letter E at 37 sits under its tempo mark, unlike the other letters that coincide with a tempo word (tested, not applied)
**Bars:** 37, top of the system (Fl 1·2 staff).
**Problem.** Wherever a letter and a tempo word fall together (G 53, H 61, J 65, L 83), the boxed letter is the outermost mark, with the tempo word under it. At 37 the order is reversed: "Poco più mosso (♩ = 60)" is on top, "dolce cantabile" under it, and the E sits low on the left, level with Fl 1's "1.". The strings' E at 37 has the usual order (letter above tempo), so the two copies of the same landmark are stacked differently. Gould PDF 13 (consistency) applies.
**Tests.** (a) `\once \override Score.RehearsalMark.outside-staff-priority = #1600` before `\mark \default` at 37: no effect. At the line start, LilyPond does not treat the letter as overlapping the tempo and text above the first note. (b) `\once \override Score.RehearsalMark.outside-staff-horizontal-padding = #2`: the E moves above the tempo mark, as at the other letters. But it then sits at the height of the system above's bar numbers "31" and "32", in the gap between the two systems, where it reads as part of that number row. Gould PDF 55 asks for evenly spread symbols with no crowding, and the 8-page layout is protected, so the gap cannot grow. The present placement is clear and does not collide (more than ½ space everywhere), so I left v555 as it is.
**Option for the composer.** If the order matters more than the crowding, use (b) above in global.ily (`\bar "||" \key des \major \time 4/4 \once \override Score.RehearsalMark.outside-staff-horizontal-padding = #2 \mark \default`). This also settles c435's open note about the strings' E standing about 4 mm right of the winds' E only if the winds' E moves. I do not recommend it over the current layout.
