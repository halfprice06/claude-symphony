## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 72 b2: V7/V has no 7th on the beat in the third statement of the motto chain (applied)

**Problem.** Theme A's motto tail is harmonised vi7-V7/V-V7-I three times (c002; c302 protects the chains). The 3rd of vi7 (A♭) is held as a common tone and becomes the 7th of B♭7:
- at 8, Bsn 1 holds A♭3 through b1-2 and falls to G3;
- at 24, the violas hold A♭4 through b1-2 and fall to G4;
- at 72, clarinet 2 leaves A♭4 for F4 on b2. The violas' eighth figure has A♭4 only on the off-beats (b1.5 and b2.5).

So on 72 b2 the sounding chord is a plain B♭ triad: B♭1, B♭2, F3, B♭3, F4, D5/D6. The scan finds a dominant seventh at 8.2 and 24.2 and none at 72.2. The D in the violins, the ♯4̂ of the tail, is now supported by V/V without the dissonance the other two statements give it, and this is the louder return (p to mp).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 400 and Ex. 24-3: in descending 5ths with interlocking sevenths, "each chordal 3rd prepares the 7th of the following chord as a common tone", and a free texture can keep every chord complete. The A♭ is already in clarinet 2 on 72 b1. The only reason it moved on b2 was c061's B♭4 anticipation, which avoids parallel fifths F4-B♭4 against the bass B♭1-E♭2. With A♭4 on b2 that fifth is gone. I kept the B♭4 anticipation anyway, so c059's B♭4 on b3 (it avoids octaves with the counter-melody's G3-A♭3) and c061 both stand.

After the change, the clarinet's 7th steps up to the anticipation, and the resolution G4 comes in the violas at b3.5. The violas are the next lower voice and in the same register, which is the transferred resolution A&S prefer (PDF 406-407, Ex. 24-11). The violas' own A♭4-B♭3-G4 stays a decorated resolution (PDF 400-401, Ex. 24-4b). No new parallels:
- The A♭4 makes a 7th with the bass on b2, then an octave (b2.5) and a 5th (b3) with it.
- Against the violas it gives a 7th, a 2nd and an octave.
- The all-pairs list is identical to v442's (1121 lines) and analysis.txt still reports 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 2. Bar 42 (and 58): the 7th of vi7 reaches G♭ only over V7 (recorded, no change)

At 42 b1 the 7th A♭ of B♭m7 sounds in the C.a. (A♭4) and in the violas' triplet arpeggio (A♭3). The C.a. goes A♭4-B♭4-A♭4-G♭4, so its resolution tone arrives on b3, where G♭ is the 7th of A♭7. This is the decorated resolution of PDF 400-401 (Ex. 24-4b) combined with the descending-5ths chain of PDF 404 (Ex. 24-6, 5/3 between two sevenths). The flute supplies ii's 3rd (G♭5) on b2.

The viola's A♭3 is a broken-chord member that moves on to D♭4 and F4 inside the beat. It is not a second voice holding the 7th, so the doubling rule (PDF 439 item 3) is not broken. The parallel octaves A♭3-B♭3 (viola) against A♭4-B♭4 (C.a.) fall on triplet downbeats with two arpeggio notes between them. The all-pairs scan does not report them, and they are not heard as parallels. Bar 58 repeats the pattern a fifth lower, with a 4-3 suspension over V7/IV at b4 (c302 finding 3). Both chains are protected. No change.

### 3. Applied chords between statements of their tonic, and over stepwise basses (recorded, no change)

- **19 b3.** E-G-B♭ over G3 is VII6 of vi, passing between two F-minor chords (19 b1 and 20 b1). This is A&S's case of an applied chord connecting two statements of its own "tonic" (PDF 419, Beethoven Op. 14/2 bar 11). The violas' leading tone E4 falls to C4 while F3 arrives in the cellos. That is the usual freedom of 7̂ in an inner voice, and the bass carries the resolution (PDF 406-407). Acceptable.
- **53 b3-54.** The A♭7 over C3 resolves its 7th G♭5 to F5 inside the beat. It then passes to B♭ minor over the stepwise bass D♭-C-B♭ (c053). PDF 436-438 (Ex. 25-35) covers this case: over a diatonic stepwise bass, passing dominant chords "attract very little special attention", and their contrapuntal meaning outweighs the V-I implication. So the leading tone in the bass falling to B♭ is correct here.
- **44 b2-45.** In A♭7 the 7th G♭3 (violas) resolves to F3. The leading tone C4 (C.a.) resolves by transfer to D♭4 in bassoon 1 and the cellos, in the same register (PDF 406-407).

### 4. The modulation plan read through Unit 25 (follows; no change)

- **46-47.** The D♭7 at 47 is I♭7 of D♭ used as V7 of G♭. This is A&S's double label in Ex. 25-9c ("V7 → IV" = "I♭7 → IV", PDF 422) and Beethoven's opening in Ex. 25-19 (PDF 428). The score spells it C♯7 into F♯ minor, the same procedure as Dvořák 9/ii's C♯-minor middle section. The 7th B3 (violas) reaches A3 an eighth after the bar line; the C.a.'s A4 sounds the resolution pitch on the downbeat; E♯4 (Vln II) rises to F♯4.
- **49-50.** The Neapolitan G-B-D goes to C♯7, with G5 falling a diminished third to E♯5, and the cellos' 7th B2 resolves to A2 (c312). Correct.
- **59-60.** The progression is IV add6 (apparent 6/5, PDF 412), V6/5 of V, VII°7 of V, V, over the bass G♭-G♮-A♭. This is Ex. 25-12c exactly (PDF 424). The soprano rises B♭5-C6, so the Ex. 25-14 difficulties (3̂ in the soprano) do not arise.
- **62-64.** F♭ major is V of A minor locally, and E7 becomes the French sixth on F♭ that slips to E♭7. These are the local and long-range meanings of PDF 422.

The middle section's tonicisations are introduced by applied V and VII in all the standard positions. No change.
