## Findings

**F1. Bar 78 b1–2: clarinets 1 and 2 in unison on A♭4 at f. Applied.**
Clarinet 1 ends its doubling of the violins' lower octave on A♭4 (77 b3 C5–B♭4 → 78 A♭4). Clarinet 2 comes in on the same A♭4 at f and moves to G4 on b3. At 78 b1 the woodwinds are Fl 1 A♭5, Cl 1 A♭4, Cl 2 A♭4, Bsn 1 C♭4, Bsn 2 F♭3, so the second clarinet adds only loudness to a note already held by Fl 1, Cl 1, Vln I, Vln II and the violas. Adler PDF 249–250 says two like winds in unison thicken the tone, upset its overtone balance and are justified only "in tutti sections where simple volume is required"; for winds a 2 he recommends the octave (PDF 250, the 8′ + 4′ registration). PDF 257 says overlapping at the unison "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch", except in a full tutti. Bar 78 is not a full tutti: oboes, horns (until b3), brass and timpani are silent. The A♭ already sounds in octaves without Cl 2 (Fl 1 A♭5, Cl 1 A♭4), and the F♭ triad is complete in the winds (Bsn 2 F♭3, Bsn 1 C♭4, Cl 1 A♭4).
I tried two other notes for Cl 2 first. C♭5 put Cl 2 above Cl 1 on the shared staff, where the part-combiner prints a one-stem chord that reads with the top note as player 1. F♭4 makes octaves with the bass when it falls to E♭4 at b3, and going on to G4 makes an augmented second. Taking Cl 2 out of b1–2 has neither problem. It keeps Cl 2's G4 on b3, the only wind G in the iii6, and gives Cl 2 a breath before the ff at 79.
Fix, `aTwo.ily`, `aTwoClTwo`, bar 78: `aes'2\f g'4` → `r2\f g'4`. The f stays on the rest, so the shared staff still prints a single f and the MIDI level does not change. Pitches, harmony and the build order 71–79 are unchanged. The only change is that one doubling on a note already held by five other parts is removed, so the protection of the cadence 77–82 is not affected.

**F2. Bars 81 b2–82: oboe 1 in unison with clarinet 1 (E♭5–B♭4) and then with clarinet 2 (A♭4) during the release. No change; record as an exception.**
These are the oboe–clarinet unisons that Adler PDF 235 lists as risky (the oboe covers the clarinet; tuning). Copland's diminuendo (PDF 270) suggests taking the oboe out. Both unisons come from correct voice leading: Ob 1 resolves 2̂–1̂ (B♭4–A♭4), and Cl 2 carries the only resolving leading tone (G4–A♭4, c122). Both lines are inside the protected release 80–83. Removing the oboe would leave its line hanging on B♭4 or G5. The dynamic falls from ff to p over these two bars, so this is the last part of a tutti, not a soft solo, and Adler accepts a deliberate oboe–clarinet colour (PDF 236). Please record it so later critics do not raise it again.

**F3. Bars 71–74: clarinet 2's held A♭4 lies on the theme's lower octave. No clean fix; record.**
The Figaro example (PDF 241–243) puts the sustained winds at a "wide distance" from the string theme so that they add prominence "without detracting from the major thematic idea". Cl 2 holds A♭4 at pp. That is the pitch of Vln II at 71 b1–2 and 73 b3, so for those beats the wind background doubles the theme's lower octave instead of standing apart from it. The other registers are taken. An octave higher is Vln I's A♭5. Below are the viola figure (C4–A♭4) and the cello counter-melody (A♭3 at 71 b3). c067 also reduced the lower octave on purpose. The pp level keeps the effect small. I recommend no change, and I record the point so it is not raised again.
