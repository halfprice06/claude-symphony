## Findings

The nonprofessional column gives no reason to change anything. Every staff except Fl 1 and Vln I stays inside the amateur range or at its edge. The professional-only notes are a few top notes at the two climaxes and three low notes that earlier cycles already recorded. Adler's footnote (PDF 302) puts these top notes inside what is "usually called for in orchestral playing". The items below are ranked by how near each comes to a real playing risk. They are recorded so that later passes do not repeat the check. No item needs a change.

**1. Flute 1 A♭6 held 61–62 ff, then A6 at 63 (recorded, no change).**
A♭6 and A6 lie above the nonprofessional flute top, which is about G6 (PDF 303), and below the professional C7. The A♭6 is tied for two 4/4 bars at ff. On the flute that register speaks easily loud and is hard only soft, so ff is the right dynamic for it. Fl 1 takes a breath at the end of 60 (`c'''1)\< \breathe`), and the A♭6 is approached by step from the 57–59 line. Two bars at q = 60 to 66 is about 8 seconds, within one breath at ff. The A6 at 63 is a single sf quarter. Keep.

**2. Violins I at A♭6/A6 (57–63) and B♭6 (79) (recorded, no change).**
These notes are above the amateur violin top, about B5 or D6 (PDF 302), and far below the professional E7. At 58 the A♭6 comes in a stepwise line doubled by Fl 1 at pitch at mf. At 62–63 the top notes are in the upper half of a div. chord (`<fes''' aes'''>`, `<e''' a'''>:32`), so each half plays one note. B♭6 at 79 is reached by the c008 grace run, at ff. All of this is standard E-string playing in the 5th to 7th positions. Keep.

**3. Clarinet 1 written C6 at 79, Violins II C6 at 63–64 (recorded, no change).**
Both are exactly at the top of their nonprofessional columns (PDF 302, 304). Both come at ff/sf climaxes, and the Vln II note is a non-div. tremolo double stop E5–C6, an easy sixth in 3rd position. Keep.

**4. Low notes at the edges of the chart (recorded, closed by earlier cycles).**
B.cl. written C3 at 8 b2 is the note Adler puts in parentheses (PDF 304); the part gives the small-note octave (c156). Cbn written B♭1 at 80 b3 is the chart's bottom (PDF 304); it comes at ff inside the c184 line. Cb E1 at 62 b3 is the open string, without the † extension (PDF 302). Cl 2 written F3 at 3–6 ppp is one semitone above the bottom. Adler's nonprofessional row for the clarinet starts at the same E3 (PDF 304), so even an amateur can play it, and low chalumeau is the clarinet's easiest pianissimo register. Keep all four.

**5. Instruments Adler marks as rare in amateur orchestras (not applicable).**
The cor anglais (theme B, 37–44), bass clarinet (solo at 31–36) and contrabassoon are marked on PDF 304 as "very seldom", "usually unavailable" and "usually not available" in amateur groups. The commission is a professional symphony orchestra (BRIEF.md), so these comments do not apply. I note them only because the score depends on the C.a. and B.cl. for solos and has no cues for them in other instruments. A professional orchestra does not need such cues. Keep.

**6. Horns and low brass within the amateur column (recorded).**
Horns go up to written F♯5 (sounding B4, Hn 3 at 62 ff), under the nonprofessional top G5 (PDF 305). The 7–14 solo tops out at written F5. Trombones top at C4, the bass trombone at C♯3 and the tuba at C♯2, all well under the amateur limits (PDF 306). For an Adagio this brass writing puts no strain on endurance, which matches c426's reading of Adler ch. 10–11. Keep.

**7. Terms on PDF 312 (recorded).**
Every term the score uses appears in Adler's table, in its English or Italian form: con sord., via sord., senza sord., div., unis., solo, "1.", a2, sul tasto, ord., the "muta … in …" form, and soft sticks. Adler's Italian cell for "Change C to E" reads "Sol Muta in Mi", a misprint for "Do muta in Mi". The score writes the muta with English note names (A♭, C, D♭ …), so the misprint does not reach the score. The mixture of English and Italian has been raised before (c066, c156, c295, c306) and is not raised again. The c445 open point on the clarinets' bar-92 text is about engraving, not range, and I leave it to that lens.
