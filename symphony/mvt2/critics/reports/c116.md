## Findings

### 1. The harp part has no pedal setting before its first entry. Applied (part only).
Bars 1–7. Movement I ends with the harp on C2–G2–C3 in a C-minor/major setting and runs attacca into movement II, so the harpist resets four pedals (D, B, E, A at least) during 1–6. The score shows the A♭-major diagram at 7; the part shows nothing at any point, because c075 tagged all diagrams `'score`.
Evidence: Adler PDF 112: "It is a help to the harpist to indicate how the pedals should be set at the beginning of a work", in letters or a diagram. c075's reason was MOLA PDF 5, "Harp pedaling should be left to the performer." That sentence concerns the sequence of changes, which the harpist marks for herself; it does not cover the starting setting, which she cannot infer from the movement's key signature when she has six bars to reset from the previous movement. I keep the mid-movement diagrams and the A♮/A♭ letters score-only, which respects c075.
Fix (intro.ily, part only; score.ly already removes `'part` from `\harpL`):
```
introHarpL = { R2.*6-\tag #'part _\markup \small \line { \italic "set pedals during the rests:" \harp-pedal #"^-^|^--^" } | }
```

### 2. The part splits every two-hand roll into two rolls. Applied (genparts.py).
Bars 12, 76, 78 b3. The score's PianoStaff has `connectArpeggios = ##t`, so these chords print as one wavy line from the bottom of the LH to the top of the RH, one roll from the bass upward. The part's PianoStaff lacks it, so it prints two wavy lines, which tells the harpist to roll both hands at once.
Evidence: Adler PDF 114–115: a chord is rolled only where a wavy line stands, and without arrows it rolls from the bottom up. The line's extent is the instruction, so score and part must agree. c026 asked for the connected arpeggios; the parts book, built later (c075), dropped them.
Fix (genparts.py, harp bookpart): `\new PianoStaff \with { connectArpeggios = ##t } << ...`

### 3. The harmonic pickup at 82 b3 is covered by the held chord. Applied.
Bar 82 b3. The RH plays E♭4–F4 as harmonics, written E♭3–F3, at the moment flutes, Cl 1·2, B.cl., Bsn 1·2, Vln I, Vc and Cb still hold the cadence chord, p diminuendo to pp. The timpani were already damped on this beat for the harmonics' sake (aTwoTimp comment), but the winds and strings were not, and they are protected (cadence 77–82, staged release 81–83).
Evidence: Adler PDF 116: harp harmonics "are very soft and must be accompanied very lightly or played a cappella to be effectively heard." At 83 the condition is met (Vc/Cb ppp only); at 82 b3 it is not.
Fix: play the two pickup notes at pitch, which sound the same and carry; harmonics begin on the downbeat of K. The MIDI is unchanged (the hidden voice already played es' f').
```
aTwo.ily  aTwoHarpR bar 82:  R2.*3 | r4 <aes c'>4\arpeggio es'8 f'8 |
coda.ily  codaHarpR bar 83:  { \voiceOne aes4.(-\flageolet^\markup \small \italic "harm. (sound 8va)" c'8-\flageolet es'4-\flageolet) }
```

### 4. The harp stays p while the orchestra grows to f at 75–78. Applied.
Bars 75–78. Harp p at 75 with no further mark. Around it: Vln I/II and Vc mp, Cl/Bsn p, Hn 1·2 mp, all crescendo to mf at 77 and f at 78. The harp's rolled iii6 on 78 b3, the chord that leads into the 79 climax, is marked p under an f tutti.
Evidence: Adler PDF 113–114: the middle two octaves (where these chords lie, A♭3–F4 and E♭1–E♭2) are "very rich and warm", and the harp's real weakness is only at the top. A p roll under f strings and winds is inaudible; the harp is either part of the build or it should rest (the c076 precedent at 79–81).
Fix (aTwoHarpL):
```
<aes,, es,>4\p\<\arpeggio c,4 \once \stemUp des,4 | <des, aes,>2\arpeggio bes,,4 | <es,, es,>2.\mf\arpeggio | r2 <es,, es,>4\f\arpeggio \ottava #0 |
```

### 5. The A♭ pedal mark falls on ringing A♮ strings. Applied (score only).
Bars 40–42. "A♮" at 39 b4 is placed correctly, under a rest just before the A♮ octave. "A♭" is printed at 40 b3, the beat on which the LH leaves the A♮ half note for B♭. The A strings are still sounding there, and a pedal moved under a vibrating string bends its pitch. A♭ is not needed until the octave at 42 b3.
Evidence: Adler PDF 113: the harpist changes "just a split second before playing the note", usually "even later than it is marked." The mark belongs just before the need, after the A♮ has been damped or has decayed.
Fix (bSecHarpL): remove `-\tag #'score _\markup \small "A♭"` from `<bes,, bes,>2` at 40 and put it on the last rest of 41: `<f, f>4 r4 <ges, ges>4 r4-\tag #'score _\markup \small "A♭" |`.

### 6. The harp's own low A♭1 at 83 sits under its harmonics. Not applied (recommendation).
Bar 83. The LH strikes A♭1 (8 bassa) on the same beat as the first harmonic, with no new dynamic, so at the p of 82. A wound low string is the loudest harp sound there and doubles the Vc/Cb ppp pedal.
Evidence: Adler PDF 116 (rule 1, as in finding 3); PDF 113 (low register "sonorous").
Fix if wanted: let the 82 chord ring and drop the re-attack. This touches the edge of the protected release 81–83, so I leave it to the composer.
```

codaHarpL bar 83:  R2.*4 |   % replaces aes,,2. \ottava #0 | R2.*3 |
```
