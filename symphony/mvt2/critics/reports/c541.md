## Findings

Every finding below is a check that needs no change. v541 meets each principle of this unit, and the one new melodic detail since c331 (the cello's 9–8 at 73–74) also follows it.

### 1. Theme B's half notes fall only on beat 3 in 38–40; the downbeat is held by bar 37, the harmony and the model (recorded)
- **Bars:** 37–40 (Fl 1), 53–56 (violins).
- **Reading:** PDF 50 says long notes carry built-in accents, and PDF 51 says the simplest rhythms put long notes on strong beats. In 38–40 the only long notes are `es''2` at 38 b3 and `bes'2` at 40 b3. Beat 1 of each of these bars has a quarter followed by an eighth-note turn. The pizzicato bass also strikes beats 1 and 3 alike (`des,4 r4 des,4 r4`), and the harmony changes every half bar. Played alone, 38–40 could therefore be heard with the barline two beats later.
- **Why it holds:** Three things fix the barline. (a) Bar 37 sets it with one chord for the whole bar and a half note on b1. (b) Beat 3 is the secondary accent of 4/4 (PDF 50), so a half note there is a metrical accent, not a syncopation. (c) The phrase's goal, B♭ minor at 40 (A–B♭ in the bass), lands on b3, and the half-cadence goal at 44 (`c'2.`) lands on b1. Dvořák's Largo tune has the same layout: its two-bar groups end on half notes on beat 3 (corpus lines 208 and 210, `bf2`, `af2`), and the downbeats carry short dotted figures. c331's Exercise 3 point needs one refinement. For theme B, bar 37 and the bass establish the metre; the melody alone does not. Theme A's metre is clear from the melody alone.
- **Fix:** none. Theme B's neighbour-figure design is protected (c261, c263).

### 2. The B♭ peak on the weak beat of 12, 28 and 76 carries three of the built-in accents at once; 79 answers it on a downbeat (recorded)
- **Bars:** 11–12, 27–28, 75–76; 79.
- **Reading:** PDF 50 names three built-in accents: duration, register and dissonance. At 12 b2 the B♭ is the theme's highest note and an appoggiatura (the sixth over D♭, resolving to A♭). It comes straight after the tied F, a syncopation (PDF 51). So the strongest emphasis of bar 12 falls on beat 2, the stress of a sarabande. That is the theme's most recognisable moment. Ex. 3-15c shows appoggiaturas normally on the strong beat. A′ uses that norm only at the climax: B♭6 on the downbeat of strong bar 79, held four beats, resolving at 80 b2. The weak-beat form becomes a strong-beat form only once.
- **New since c331:** at 73–74 the cellos hold C4 over the bar into the bass B♭1 of V4/3 and resolve it to B♭ on 74 b2. This is a 9–8 made by lengthening a note, as in Ex. 3-17a. It is prepared as a consonance at 73 b3 (over F), it is accented, and the bass strikes the downbeat of 74, as point 1 of the table requires. It does not compete with the violins, whose 74 b1 G is struck.
- **Fix:** none.

- **Fix:** none. 79–82 is protected.

### 4. Theme B's main tones move about half as fast as theme A's; the quicker tempo is carried by the accompaniment, as in Dvořák (recorded)
- **Bars:** 7–10 against 37–40.
- **Reading:** PDF 49 says felt tempo depends on the beat, the chord changes and "the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody". Theme A has about one main tone per beat at ♩=52. Theme B has about one per half bar at ♩=60 (F, E♭ | D♭, E♭ | G♭, E♭ | C, B♭). The "più mosso" is heard through the half-bar harmonic rhythm, the pizzicato quarters and the viola triplets. That makes theme B the broader of the two tunes to sing, which is right for a middle-section melody in this tradition.
- **Fix:** none (c331, c238).

### 5. The phrase lengths are regular in the themes and irregular only at the joins (recorded)
- **Bars:** 1–6, 31–36, 45–47, 65–70.
- **Reading:** PDF 53–54 accepts three-bar groups (Ex. 3-12) and five- or seven-bar phrases (Ex. 3-14) next to the common fours and eights. Both themes are in fours and eights (7–14, 15–22, 23–30, 37–44, 53–60, 71–78). The irregular groups are the six-bar interludes and the three-bar B♭-minor head at 45–47, which leads into the quotation. A listener remembers the square themes and hears the odd groups as passages in between.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Memorability: theme A is the tune a listener will hum; theme B's memorable part is its second phrase (recorded)
- **Theme A:** the head `4. 8 4~` (A♭–C–E♭) is heard at 7, 11, 23, 27, 71 and 75. It is also heard in the B.cl. arpeggio at 31, in the horn counter-line at 53–54, in the harp at 83 and mirrored in the timpani at 84. The motto tail follows it at 8, 24, 33 and 72. This many returns, each with a different setting, is how Dvořák's Largo makes its tune stay with a listener: its `8. 16 4` head returns in the cor anglais at 7, 9, 15, 17 and again at the reprise (corpus lines 207–216, 226–229). The metre is clear from the melody alone (Exercise 3), which helps it stick.
- **Theme B:** the rising fourth and dotted figure of its second phrase (A♭–D♭–F, `4 4 4. 8`, 41 and 57) is the part a listener keeps, because it returns augmented at the climax (61–62). The first phrase (37–40) is mostly steps and turns. That point was raised and closed (c261, c263), and I do not reopen it.
- **Fix:** none.
