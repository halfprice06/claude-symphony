## Findings

### 1. Bar 92: a stray "via sord." prints at the top of the score, over the cor anglais, and the strings get no mute instruction near their own staves (applied)

**Problem.** At the right edge of bar 92, after the final barline, "via sord." prints above the top staff of the last system, which is the cor anglais. The cor anglais has no mute. The text comes from c470's part-only `\textEndMark` in `codaVlnTwo`. A text mark is a Score-level object, so it prints at the top of the system. Vln I and the violas are passed through `\removeWithTag #'part` in score.ly, but Violins II are not (`<< \global \vlnTwo >>`), so Vln II's copy leaks into the score. c470 said "the score is unchanged", and that has been wrong since v471. The violins and violas really are muted from 84 to 92 (c468), but the score does not say at their staves that the mutes come off. The horns' "via sord." at 91 is printed in the score (c136, for the same reason).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 291 item 3: the score "must contain every detail for every instrument, including special instructions". PDF 294: a change instruction "should be written above the player's staff". Item 1 (PDF 291): the score must be "as unproblematical as possible". A mute instruction above an instrument that has no mute fails that test.

**Fix (applied).** score.ly, Violins II:
```
<< \global \removeWithTag #'part \vlnTwo >>
```
Nothing else in `vlnTwo` is tagged `'part`, so the only change is that the stray mark disappears. In sections/coda.ily the last note of Vln I, Vln II and violas becomes, for example:
```
<< c''2.\downbow\ppp\fermata \tag #'score { s2 s4^\markup \small \italic "via sord." } >> \tag #'part { \textEndMark ... }
```
The parts keep c470's one-line `\textEndMark`; the parts book text is identical. In the score, "via sord." now prints on beat 3 of 92 above each of the three muted string staves, right of the fermatas, with no collision. Pitches, rhythms and dynamics at the ending do not change, so the scherzo thread need not be told.

### 2. Bar 37: "Poco più mosso" sits on the bar-number line of the system above (applied)

**Problem.** On page 2 the system 31-36 (woodwinds only) carries its bar numbers 7 staff spaces below the contrabassoon (`bnBelow`, staff-padding 7). The next system opens with a stack of four items: the tempo, "dolce cantabile", the letter E and the "1." label. The tempo, the highest of the four, ends up on the same baseline as the numbers "31 ... 32 ... 36". It falls horizontally inside bar 31's span, so it reads as "31 Poco più mosso". pdftotext reports the same line, `31 Poco più mosso (♩ = 60) 32 33 34 35 36`. This is the only tempo change in the movement that can be read as belonging to the wrong system. At 61, 65 and 83 the letter and tempo sit clear above their own system.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 291 item 2: the layout must let the conductor read at once what belongs together. PDF 294: tempo marks go "at the top of the score", meaning the top of the system they govern. In Ex. XVIII-3b (PDF 296) the number and tempo word sit directly over their own system ("20 Etwas gemächlicher"). MOLA PDF 4 places measure numbers below the system, where they belong to the system above.

**Fix (applied).** I tried moving the tempo, the letter and "dolce cantabile" (priority and X-offset tweaks). Each version kept a four-level stack or made a collision ("1." under the tempo). The fix that works is to bring this one system's bar numbers closer to the contrabassoon. In score.ly:
```
\new Staff = "Contrabassoon" \with \bnBelow 31 36 \with { \override BarNumber.staff-padding = #4.5 ...
```
The numbers now sit as close to the staff as on the other systems, and the tempo has about 5 mm of clear space above system 37. Page 2 keeps its four systems and the score keeps 8 pages. The c285 slash position and the tempo and letter placement (c445's do-not-touch) are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 65-70: an empty bass-trombone staff in the reduced system (applied; one MIDI velocity changes)

**Problem.** The system 65-70 (page 5, lower half) shows Bn 1·2, Hn 1·2, **B.tbn**, timpani and strings. The bass trombone rests in all six bars and does not play again in the movement. Its staff is kept alive by c445's hairpin end `d,4\> e,4 <>\!`: the `<>\!` falls at 65:0, inside the next system. Removing that event (test) removes the staff. So since v446 the reduced system has carried a staff of six whole-bar rests.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 295: a reduced page holds the systems where "only a few instruments are playing". Ex. XVIII-3b (PDF 296) shows only the players. Ex. XVIII-2 (PDF 295) shows the extreme case: a staff exists only while its instrument plays. PDF 291 item 2 asks for a page the conductor can read at once, and an all-rest staff in a six-staff system works against that.

**Fix (applied).** sections/bSec.ily, bar 64 of `bSecBassTbn`, score only (the part keeps the old form):
```
d,4-\tag #'score -\tweak shorten-pair #'(0 . -5.6) \> -\tag #'part \>
  \tag #'score << e,4 { s8 s8\! } >> \tag #'part { e,4 <>\! } |
```
The hairpin now ends inside bar 64 and is drawn to the barline. At 300 dpi the niente circle sits where it was, just before the double bar (c445 kept). I rejected two other end points. With `s16*3 s16\!` or `\after 8. \!`, LilyPond binds the end to the next column and the circle lands over the A♭ key signature. Cost: in score.midi, B.tbn's E2 on 64 b4 has velocity 33 instead of 36, because the hairpin's MIDI interpolation now ends half a beat earlier. That is the only MIDI difference. analysis.txt is unchanged.

### 4. global.ily comments name the pre-c425 letters (applied, comments only)

**Problem.** c425 inserted D at 31 and moved the later letters up one, but the comments in global.ily still read "% E 45", "% F 53", "% G 61", "% H 65", "% J 71", "% K 83". The printed letters are F, G, H, J, K and L. score.ly's `brassLetter "H"` and the `strMark` strings were updated; only these comments were not. Anyone who edits the file by letter will be misled.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 291 (opening): errors in score preparation are not forgiven in rehearsal. PDF 299 item 4 (same chapter, outside the range): letters must agree between score and parts.

**Fix (applied).** The six comments now read F 45, G 53, H 61, J 65, K 71 and L 83. The comment-stripped text of global.ily is unchanged.
