## Findings

### 1. Cello counter-melodies 23–30 and 71–77: mark "sul D" (applied in the scratch copy)

**Bars.** 23–30 (Vc unis., cantabile, p–f, under theme A in the violins) and 71–77 (the same line under the reprise). "ord." at 41 and 78.

**Problem.** The counter-melody lies between E♭3 and D♭4. With no string mark, a cellist plays it in first position and crosses to the A string for B♭3, C4 and D♭4: C4 at 25, D♭4 at 28 and 29, and the same notes at 73, 76 and 77. The line therefore changes colour inside the phrase. The A-string notes are the line's high points, and they fall where the violins carry the theme, at 28 (D♭4 f under the B♭5 appoggiatura) and 76–77. At 78 the line climbs C♭4–C4–D♭4 to the ff at 79, and that is the place where the A string's brilliance is wanted.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 99: "The D is the most musically captivating string on this instrument; it has a warm and lyrical quality. The A is the most brilliant and piercing." PDF 73 and 76: name a string when the timbre requires it, and mark sul D/A to keep a passage on one string. PDF 75, Ex. III-6, shows the D string's mellower upper positions in a soft cantabile line (Scheherazade iii). On the D string the highest note, D♭4, is a major seventh above the open string (about 5th position). That is inside the limit of an octave or ninth that c366 took from Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26, and it is the position Adler's Tristan example (Ex. III-59, PDF 99) uses for a whole cello section. The score already uses the same convention for the cellos' other cantabile line, sul D at 44–47 (c121, accepted). c281 raised this as finding 5, optional and untested, and HISTORY.md still lists it as open in the cycle 340 entry. I tested it, and I rank it above "optional" for one reason: it makes the A string at 78–81 a change of colour placed at the climax, where now the line moves between strings throughout.

In the one-page cello part, "sul D" at 44 still follows "ord." at 41, as before. This changes no theme, quotation or ending, so the other movements' threads do not need to hear about it.

### Checked, no change needed

- **Ranges.** Every string part lies inside the ranges on PDF 72, 85, 96 and 105. The violin's notes above A6 (B♭6 at 79–80) are approached by step and doubled by flute 1. Vln I A6 at 63 is a divided tremolo.
- **Double stops 61–64.** Each one is a third or sixth on adjacent strings in 1st–3rd position (Vla B♭3/D♭4 is the 4/2 third in 3rd position on C and G). This is "only the most easily accessible double-stops ... for especially sonorous passages" (PDF 76).
- **Open D3 in the cellos at 52** (half note, pp<, passing C3–D3–E♭3 over the A♭ pedal). It is short and moving, and c376 recorded it. Adler leaves such choices to the player (PDF 73), so I add no mark.
- **Viola sextuplets 59–60.** These are one-beat slurred arpeggios over three strings at ♩≈64, f. They are heavier than the violin version (PDF 86) but stay in low positions, and c035 and c256 recorded them as playable.
- **Bass bow.** Every long bass note is marked "change bow at will" (PDF 108). "4 soli" pedals have "tutti" cancellations (PDF 105, 108).
- **Solo violin 17–22.** The concertmaster plays it (PDF 81). It lies D5–B♭5 on the soft E string, the "ethereal" colour of PDF 75. Moving it sul A would put B♭5 in 7th position for no gain.
