## Findings

No finding calls for a change. Every principle in the reading that applies to this score is followed, or the exception was already raised and kept for a stated reason. I did not edit the scratch copy, so there was no build to run. The points below are what I checked, ranked by how much a reader of Rimsky-Korsakov would question them. Each says why it stands.

**1. Horns inside the bassoons at 62 b1-2 and 79-80 (PDF 108, rule 7).** At 79 Bsn 2 has C♯3 and Bsn 1 C♯4, and Hn 4's A♭3 sits between them. At 62 b1-2 Hn 1·2's C♯4 sits between Bsn 2's F♯2 and Bsn 1's F4. Rimsky calls this "not recommended", but that diagram is about a soft four-part chord where the bassoons carry the harmony. Here, at ff and f, each bassoon doubles another line (Bsn 1 the horn and cello counter-melody, Bsn 2 the bass), and the horns carry the harmony. c437 recorded this. One more piece of evidence: Rimsky's own Ex. 158 (Ivan the Terrible, Act I, PDF 331; text PDF 110: "flutes within horns; later horns within bassoons") encloses horns inside bassoons in a soft Adagio, so he did not treat the rule as absolute. Keep.

**2. Moving brass under held woodwind at 60 and 79-81 (PDF 108, rule 8).** The rule gives stationary parts to the brass. At 60, Hn 1 rises E♭4-F4-F♯4 while Fl, Ob and Cl hold C; at 79-81, Hn 1·2 a2 fall C♯4-C4-B♭3-A♭3 under held B♭ woodwind octaves. In both places the moving line is a counter-melody, not a harmony part, and Bsn 1 and the cellos double it. Ex. 143 (The Christmas Night, PDF 317, Adagio) does the same thing: the horns move chromatically in thirds while the flutes and clarinets hold. Protected (c129, c437). Keep.

**3. Bassoons take the discord, horns the concord, at 67-70 (PDF 108, rule 5).** Over the basses' E♭2, Hn 1 ends on B♭3 (the fifth of V7). Bsn 1 has G3 and Bsn 2 the seventh, D♭3, which resolves to C3 at 71. This is the layout Rimsky prints as correct ("2 Cor. above, 2 Fag." with the dissonance in the bassoons). The horn's D4 at 68 b1 is a melodic passing note in the motto tail (protected), not a chord tone that could be moved. Keep.

**4. Muted horns with cor anglais at 87-90 (PDF 107, 110, rule 3).** The C.a. plays F4-F♭4-E♭4 an octave over Hn 4's F3-F♭3-E♭3. Hn 3 holds A♭3 and the divided violas double both horns (c194, PDF 113). That combines two of Rimsky's recommended pairings, muted horn with C.a. (Ex. 164, PDF 334; "Tsar's Bride 123: Ob., Eng. horn, Horn (+)") and horns with divided violas, without crossing strings and brass any other way. The dark-tone option of rule 4 (deep clarinet under stopped horns) would mean moving Cl 2 down to E♭3 at 89. That would double the violas' E♭3 and blur the protected clarinet morendo, so I do not propose it. Keep.

**5. Woodwind one degree louder than brass at 61 (PDF 112, rule 13).** At 61 the woodwinds are ff, Hn 3·4, trombones and tuba f, and Hn 1·2 ff. Hn 1·2 play two different notes there (F4, C♯4), so each is a single horn, which c424 marked one degree up on purpose. The woodwinds also double Hn 1·2's notes in octaves (Ob 1 and Cl 1 F5, Ob 2 C♯5), which is the second balancing method on PDF 108-109. Keep.

**6. Rhythmic motion in strings rather than wind, 37-60 and 71-78 (PDF 112, rule 14).** Rimsky's Ex. 171 (Antar, Adagio, PDF 342) and Ex. 152 (PDF 324) put the florid accompaniment in clarinet 2 inside held chords. This score puts it in the violas (triplets 37-56, sextuplets 59-60, quavers 71-78) under held or melodic winds. The text lists both layouts ("rhythmic motion in the wind, sustained harmony in the strings" is one example, not a rule). The viola triplets are protected (c029, c176). A clarinet figure would also add a third colour to the theme B accompaniment, which already changes at each phrase (c567). Keep.

**7. Strings with brass at 29-30 and 63-64 (PDF 113, rule 15).** At 63-64 the strings are tremolando under Tbn 1's quotation and the bass trombone's sf. That is the first exception Rimsky names. At 29-30, Hn 3's D♭4-C4 lies inside the string chord and the cellos double its C4 at 30. Hn 4's E♭3 has no string double (c287, "as Ex. 171"). It is pp, held, and the bass of a V-I seam, so it reads as a pedal, not as a crossing. Keep.

**8. No clarinet inside the horns (PDF 108, rule 9).** I scanned bars 3-6, 53-62, 75-82 and 87-90. No clarinet sits between two horn notes. The one unison (Cl 2 F4 with Hn 3 at 79 b1) is c234's recorded doubling. Nothing to change.

Summary: no change. This passes the fourth series examples on overlaying, crossing and enclosure. The two places that break the letter of PDF 108 (rules 7 and 8) are backed by Rimsky's own Ex. 158 and Ex. 143. No change affects movement II's ending, its exported themes or its quotations.

Do not touch: the wind-brass layouts at 3-6, 60-62, 67-70, 79-81 and 87-90 (horns within bassoons at 62 and 79 as Ex. 158, moving horn counter-melodies as Ex. 143, discord in Bsn 2 at 69-70, muted horns with C.a. and divided violas at 87-90), the standing list and the 8-page layout.
