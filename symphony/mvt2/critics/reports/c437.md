## Findings

I recommend no change to the score. In this movement the wind-brass layouts follow the reading. The bassoon takes the discord when it plays with a soft horn (8, 69-70, 62). The muted horn is paired with the cor anglais (87-90). Clarinets lie under the horns in the dark low register (3-6). At forte the woodwind chord sits above the horns and its upper parts are doubled (61-62, 79-81). Strings meet brass only over tremolo, or where horns double the cellos or violas. The two places where the score departs from the letter of a rule are both forte, and each departure has a reason. I tested both alternatives on paper and rejected them. Nothing was edited in /tmp/critic-437/work: it is a byte-identical copy of v437 (diff -rq reports no differences). v437's build-warnings.txt is empty and analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, so the scratch copy builds clean as it stands.

### 1. Bars 62 b1-2 and 79 b1-81 b2: a horn sits between the two bassoons (no change; corrects c287's scan record)

**Problem.** PDF 108: "Bassoons may also be written inside the horns, but the inverse process is not to be recommended." At 62 b1-2, Hn 2 D♭4 lies between Bn 2 G♭2 and Bn 1 F4. At 79 b1-3 Hn 4 A♭3 lies between Bn 2 D♭3 and Bn 1 D♭4/C4/B♭3. The same happens at 80 b3 (A♭3 between B♭2 and B♭3) and at 81 b1-2 (G3 between E♭3 and B♭3/D♭4). c287 wrote that no horn note ever sits between two bassoon notes. That statement is wrong for v437.

**Evidence.** Rimsky gives the rule for four-part harmony shared by 2 Fag + 2 Hn, "especially in soft passages", and adds that "in forte passages the horns overwhelm the bassoons, and it is wiser to employ four horns alone" (PDF 108). At 62 and 79-81 the dynamic is ff/f, the four horns already hold a complete chord, and neither bassoon has a harmony part. Bn 1 doubles the counter-melody in unison with Hn 1·2 a2 and the cellos (79 D♭4-C4-B♭3, 80 A♭3-B♭3, 81 D♭4). Bn 2 doubles the bass with B.cl. and the basses, which PDF 108's own four-horn example allows ("the bassoon may then double the bass an octave below"). No bassoon colour is heard as a separate part between the horns.

**Fix considered.** At 79-81, give Bn 2 horn 4's line (`aTwoBsnTwo` 79 `aes2.` | 80 `aes2 aes4` | 81 `g2 es4`, concert) so that it doubles the horn rather than enclosing it. Rejected for three reasons. It takes the reed out of the bass at the ff peak. It changes the protected A′ doublings at 75-82. And it changes nothing that can be heard at ff. At 62 the layout is part of the protected 61-62 brass and woodwind block.

### 2. Bar 60: the moving parts are in the brass and the held parts in the woodwind (no change)

**Problem.** PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." At 60 Hn 1·2 rise E♭4-F4-G♭4 and Tbn 2 E♭3-G♭3. Meanwhile Fl 1, Ob 1 and Cl 1 hold C (C6/C5) and Cl 2 holds A♭4.

**Evidence.** The moving line at 60 is not a harmonic part in a chain of chords. It continues the horn counter-melody that runs from 53 (Hn 1 + Cl 1 at 53-56, Hn 1·2 with Vc and Bn 1 at 57-60), over a held V7 with an A♭ pedal. Rimsky's own Ex. 143 (Christmas Night, Adagio, PDF 317) has four horns moving chromatically under held woodwind. At 60 the line is also doubled in the woodwind (Bn 1) and the strings (Vc, Va). The strings → woodwind → brass build order at 53-61 is on the standing list.

**Fix considered.** None. The line could be taken from the horns, but that would undo the horn counter-melody (c007, c124).

### 3. Bars 85-89: the F-F♭-E♭ descent passes from the violas to C.a. and muted Hn 4 (follows; positive)

**Evidence.** The violas move F3-F♭3-E♭3 under the clarinet's tail at 85-87. At 87-89 the C.a. (F4-F♭4-E♭4) and muted Hn 4 (an octave lower) take the same descent. This is the stopped-horn and double-reed pairing of PDF 107 and 110 (Ex. 164 has the same group over a plain bass). It is also an answer between groups like Ex. 172 (PDF 342), where an oboe-bassoon figure is taken up by the horns against the strings. Hn 3 is on the violas' A♭3, which is the "horns doubled by divided violas" exception of PDF 113. The bars are protected (clarinet morendo and plagal close 87-92). No change.

### 4. Bars 3-6: the horns over a low clarinet use the layout of the "dark tone" example, but open (no change)

**Evidence.** PDF 107 recommends stopped horns in the middle register over deep clarinet notes, and says the colour is lost if bassoons replace the clarinets. At 3-6 Hn 3·4 (C4-D♭4-C4 over A♭3) lie over Cl 2 E♭3-F♭3-E♭3 and B.cl. A♭2, with no bassoons. That is the layout of the example, but the horns are open and pp. Muting Hn 3·4 here would add the colour Rimsky describes. However, bars 1-6 are protected (the link from movement I), and the muted horns are kept for the echo at 87-90, which would lose its contrast. Recorded; no change.

### 5. Bars 7-14 and 67-70: soft horn over bassoons, discords in the bassoons (follows)

**Evidence.** PDF 108: in soft passages the concords go to the horns and the discords to the bassoons. At 8 b2 the seventh of V7/V (A♭3) is in Bn 1 while the horn has the melody. At 69-70 the seventh of V7 (D♭3) is in Bn 2, Bn 1 has the third (G3) and the horn holds B♭3. The horn's chromatic D4-D♭4 at 68 is the motto tail (melody, protected), not a harmony note. No change.

### 6. Bars 61-62 and 79-81: juxtaposition and superposition at the two peaks (follows; recorded c284, c287)

**Evidence.** Every chord tone at 61-62 is in both the brass and the woodwind (PDF 106, Ex. 142). The woodwind lies above the horns and its upper parts are doubled at forte (PDF 108-109). The trombones and tuba are below the horns (PDF 110-111). Hn 1·2 at ff equal to the woodwind is covered by the doubling remedy (c284). No change.

### 7. Strings with brass (follows)

**Evidence.** PDF 113 allows strings with brass only over string tremolo, with short sf chords, or where horns double divided violas or cellos. 63-64: Tbn 1 and B.tbn over tremolo violins, violas and cellos, as in Ex. 174 (PDF 344, muted brass over strings). Horns sit on the cellos' notes at 29-30 (Hn 3), 57-60 and 75-82, and on the violas' notes at 87-89. The one exception, Hn 4 E♭3 at 29-30 with no string on its note, is recorded (c234, c287). No change.
