## Findings

### 1. Bar 14: the harp chord sits on the horn's final note in the horn's own octave (applied)

**Problem.** Horn 1 ends theme A's first statement on A♭3, p, held for the whole bar. On beat 2 the harp right hand arpeggiates A♭3–C4–E♭4 and lets it ring. The solo's cadence note becomes the bottom of a harp chord a third and a fifth above it, in the horn's weak middle-low octave. Everywhere else in 7–14 the harp keeps under the horn (c019, c057, c117).

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139: voices "suffer more ... when they come in contact with harmony in a register similar to their own". On the same page he names harmony "duplicated in the higher register" as one of the ways to keep an accompaniment off the voice. PDF 142 and Ex. 282 (PDF 450): a soft culminating point is left unsupported or held by light, transparent harmony, which in Ex. 282 lies under the soprano. c221 raised this point; it was rejected because its fix (`<c es>` in the bass clef) left the horn as the top voice into the oboe's C5 at 15, which analysis.txt reports as an outer-voice octave.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-361/work, `sections/aOne.ily`, aOneHarpR bar 14):**
```
\clef treble r4 <aes' es''>2\arpeggio |
```
(was `\clef treble r4 <aes c' es'>2\arpeggio |`). The harp now doubles the horn's A♭ an octave higher, with E♭5 above, outside the horn's register. C is left out so that no harp C5 rings into the oboe's C5 entry at 15 (the reason c097 kept the horn off the cellos' D♭4 at 29). The third is still in bassoon 2 (C3). The top line now runs E♭5→C5 over A♭→C in contrary motion, so analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels, and allpairs_par.py output is identical to v361's. The chord's register also previews the coda's harp harmonics at 83 (A♭4–C5–E♭5 sounding). Pedals, clefs and the 8-page layout are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 77: viola eighths under the melody's eighth-note descent (no change)

At 77 the melody plays G, then E♭–D♭–C–B♭ in eighths (Vln I, Vln II, Fl 1, Cl 1). The violas' running figure (`g( es' g') es'( bes g)`) also descends in eighths on beats 2–3. PDF 137 warns against accompanying figures "corresponding with those in the vocal part". Here the violas are 1½ to 2½ octaves below and in a different interval pattern (sixth, seventh, ninth, tenth, so no parallels). The figure has run in eighths since 71, so it does not copy the tune. The melody is also doubled in four instruments at mp<f. The rule is not broken in substance. Optional, untested: `g8( es' g') es'4( bes8)` would hold beat 2 and keep the descent in the melody alone. I do not recommend it; c324 settled the viola figure.

### 4. Bars 32–34: cor anglais in sixths with the bass clarinet's tail (no change)

The cor anglais moves C4–C♭4–B♭3–A♭3 in the bass clarinet's rhythm, a sixth above it, through the common-tone diminished seventh. PDF 137 (no figure in the voice's rhythm) points one way. PDF 142 ("support voices in concerted numbers by harmony and duplication") points the other. This is a duet in sixths in two colours, which is the second case. c330 also noted that these are movement I's first two motto instruments. The woodwind choir 31–36 is on the standing list. No change.

### 5. Theme B's first phrase is less memorable than its second (no change)

Bars 37–40 (and 53–56) move by step between F5 and B♭4, with a lower-neighbour figure in each bar. The phrase's only leap is the minor third E♭5–G♭5 at 38–39. The memorable gesture is the second phrase's A♭–D♭–F rise (41, 57). That phrase gets the louder colours on its second hearing (Fl 1 + Vln I in octaves with Ob 1 + Vln II at 57), and its head is the climax at 61. The plain-then-arpeggiated order follows PDF 141 (doubling kept for the broad phrase). Theme B's line is protected (c031, c151, c201). No change.
