### F1. The two themes are told apart by their type of figuration, which helps memorability (recorded, keep)
- **Theme A (7-14), bar by bar:**
  - 7: chordal skips A♭-C-E♭ (Ex. 20-2d).
  - 8: tied E♭ with D♮ (3rd of V7/V) and D♭ (7th of V7).
  - 9: passing B♭ (Ex. 20-7a).
  - 10 b3.5: F, an unaccented incomplete neighbour left by leap to A♭, in the soprano (PDF 336).
  - 11: chordal skips to F.
  - 12: B♭ appoggiatura, leap of a 4th, resolved down (PDF 338).
  - 13: chordal skip G-E♭ then a passing line D♭-C-B♭ to A♭ (Ex. 20-10, PDF 333).
- **Theme B (37-40):** the neighbour figure in every bar (F-G♭-F, D♭-C-D♭, F-G♭-F, C-D♭-C), which is the motivic use of PDF 333-334.
- **Result:** theme A is built from skips, passing tones and one appoggiatura. Theme B is built from neighbours. Each theme can be recognised from a single bar.
- **Comparison:** Dvořák's Largo tune (sounding F-A♭-A♭-F-E♭-D♭ | E♭-F-A♭-F-E♭) works the same way: one figuration type and one rhythmic cell (dotted eighth-sixteenth) in seven of its first eight bars. Theme A uses its dotted cell (4. 8) in bars 7, 9 and 11, and its tied long notes at 7-8 and 11-12 act as a second cell. That is less repetition than Dvořák but enough to recognise.
- **Fix:** none.

### F2. Theme A's second phrase is a polyphonic melody as in Ex. 20-3 (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 11-13 (Hn 1), 27-29 (violins), 75-77.
- **What happens:** the held F4 at 11-12 is the inner voice. The B♭4 appoggiatura begins an upper line B♭-A♭-G. At 13 b2 the leap G4-E♭4 brings in the inner voice's resolution F→E♭, and the descent to A♭ follows.
- **Reading:** this is Ex. 20-3's point (PDF 330). Tones that are not adjacent connect, and the melody suggests two lines.
- **At 77:** Vln I takes the E♭ an octave higher (G5 then E♭6, c341). That is a register transfer of the same inner-voice tone. Fl 1 and Vln II keep it in the original octave. Settled register plan; recorded only.
- **Fix:** none.

### F3. No parallels made by figuration (recorded)

### F4. Complementary rhythm between the theme and the cello counter-melody (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 23-30, 71-78.
- **What happens:** the cellos attack at 24 b1 and 28 b1, the two downbeats where the tune is tied over the barline. They move in quarters at 26 b2 while the tune holds G4. The two rhythms combine into continuous motion at the places where the tune stops (Ex. 20-16, PDF 336).
- **Where they coincide:** on b3 in most bars, which is where the tune's pickups and resolutions fall. The book warns only against overdoing parallel 3rds and 6ths, and the cello line moves mostly in contrary or oblique motion to the tune.
- **Fix:** none.

### F5. No 19th-century turn in the themes (recorded; no change recommended)
- **Bars:** 39/55 and 72.
- **Theme B:** the figure G♭-F-G♭-F-E♭ at 39/55 has the right number of notes, but it is not Ex. 20-21b's turn (main, upper, main, lower, main). It is a neighbour figure followed by a passing tone.
- **Theme A:** a real turn could be added at the reprise, on the held E♭ at 72. That would vary the surface as Exercise 4 (PDF 345) asks.
- **Why no change:** c319 tested and declined this, and 72 carries a motto-tail statement (cyclic, each statement scored differently, c330). A turn on its first note would change a cyclic element that the other movements quote. The reading gives no reason strong enough to reopen it.
- **Fix:** none.
