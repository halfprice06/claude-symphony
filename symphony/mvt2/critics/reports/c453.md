## Findings (ranked; none applied)

### 1. The I–IV–V–I span plan and its proportions. Keep

**Point from the reading.** Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95) shows Kuhnau's 33 bars as expanded I (1–14, 42%), semicadence, expanded V (17–27, 33%), closing I (28–33, 18%). The expanded V "helps to shape the form". PDF 112: an expansion that forms a whole section needs a root-position chord.

**Score.** The large spans are I 1–36 (36%), IV 37–64 (34%), V 65–70 (6%), I 71–92 (24%). Each rests on a root-position pedal or bass: A♭ 31–36, D♭ at 37, 53 and 61, E♭ 65–70, A♭ 83–92.

**Verdict.** IV takes the place of Kuhnau's long V, as a ternary slow movement's contrasting key does. The structural V is short (20.8 s). Mozart 41/ii's dominant spans run 6–8 beats; Dvořák 9/ii's return has no dominant span at all (c313). This movement's 18 beats of V fall between those models. The closing tonic, 24%, is close to Kuhnau's 18%. No change. The 65–70 span and the proportions are protected (c073, c143, c253).

### 2. Bar 44 is V7 moving to vi, not a plain-V semicadence. Correct c313's record; keep the music

**Point from the reading.** PDF 99 §10 and PDF 103 point 8: "V7 is rarely used in a semicadence"; if the line comes to rest on 7̂, 2̂ or 5̂, plain V is better, because V7 "conveys an inappropriate feeling of activity". PDF 101 §14: V8–7 extends V "frequently in an authentic cadence". PDF 98 §9: the seventh must resolve down by step.

**Score.** c313 listed 44 among the triad half cadences (its row 12). That is wrong. The violas (bSec.ily line 215–216) play A♭3 at 43 b4, G♭3 on 44 b1 and b2 (`es8\pp ges aes ges es ges`), as c221 wrote it (8–7 in the violas, 6–5 in flute 1, 4–3 in the cor anglais). So 43 b4–44 b2 sounds A♭7, with the flute and cor anglais holding E♭5 and C4. At 45 b1 F3 and F4 sound (Vln II holds F4), so the seventh goes down by step, and the bass moves A♭→B♭ (vi).

**Verdict.** The reading's objection is to V7 as the goal of a semicadence, where the activity has nowhere to go. Here it does go somewhere: V7 resolves deceptively to vi at 45 (letter F), and the seventh resolves normally. The effect is an evaded close, which fits c313's Finding 2 (the middle section should stay open; no PAC in D♭ before 61). The melody still halts on 2̂ with a half note, so the period is clear, and the activity of the seventh carries it into 45. That is the use of V7 the reading allows. No change. For the record, the plain-V version would be violas 44 `\tuplet 3/2 4 { es8\pp aes es aes es aes } r2` (no G♭, no C4 unison with the cor anglais). I do not recommend it: it removes the 8–7 and makes 45 sound like a new start after a full stop.

### 3. Quick chord change before each arrival; broadening at the end. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 95: "the quicker successions of chords at the cadences mark important points of arrival". PDF 94: many cadences broaden.

**Score.** One chord per beat at 12–13 (IV–ii7–V–V7), 28–29, 58 b1–60 b4 and 77–81, and one chord per bar or two in the theme statements and pedals. The final arrival broadens instead: 87 IV6, 88 iv, 89 I, one chord per bar under rit. and morendo.

**Verdict.** Follows. The closing plagal motion is the "closing tonic" of Ex. 6-4, after the authentic close at 82, and is in root position (PDF 129 §11). No change.

### 4. Harmony and metre across every barline. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 102 §16 (Ex. 6-20, 6-21) and PDF 115 §13 (Ex. 7-18): avoid repeating a chord, or moving between two positions of it, from a weak beat to a strong one; strong–(weak)–strong is good; an initial tonic may be repeated weak to strong.

**Score.** The scan of 7–92 found five places where a chord continues over a barline. 30→31: I from a downbeat to the next (the PAC into the A♭ pedal). 43 b3→44: the cadential 6/4 on beat 3 resolves to V on beat 4 and V continues into 44; the dominant starts on a semi-strong beat, so this is strong–(weak)–strong. 48→49: the quotation's initial tonic in F♯ minor. 63→64: A minor from a downbeat to a downbeat. 82→83: the PAC's tonic into the coda pedal. All other barlines bring a new chord.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 5. The retransition dominant 65–70. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 102 §15 (6-19): a transferred seventh resolves in the last voice that has it. PDF 129–130 (8-14, 8-15): "I" can be a passing chord inside V. PDF 98 §8 (6-10): when a triad is expanded, do not omit the third.

**Score.** 65–66 E♭7 (B♭2, G3, D♭4) in the strings; 67 the bare octave E♭2/E♭4 while horn 1 starts the motto tail; 68 horn D4–D♭4–C4 over B♭3, with A♭3/C4 on beat 3 as a passing 6/4; 69–70 E♭7 again; 71 the seventh goes to C3/C4.

**Verdict.** The seventh is dropped at 67, picked up by the horn, passed through D♭4→C4 inside V at 68, and returned at 69 to resolve at 71: the reading's transferred seventh. The passing 6/4 sits on a weak beat and cannot be heard as a goal (PDF 130, Ex. 8-15). The third is missing only in 67, a single bar where one horn plays over a pedal, which is the plan's unsupported horn moment (cyclic rule) and not a four-part texture. No change.

### 6. Phrase starts, contrapuntal close and the shortened reprise. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 109 §5: 6/3 chords give a "more flowing effect" but will not do "in most beginnings and virtually all endings". PDF 121 (Schubert D. 935, A♭): V7 inversions move an opening tonic forward. PDF 129 §11: a stepwise-bass close may end a group inside a phrase.

**Score.** All sections start in root position; I6 begins only inner phrases (15, 41, 57). In A′ the bars 73 b3–75 run vi7–V4/3–I with the bass B♭1→A♭1, so A′'s first group closes contrapuntally and goes on; A′ lasts 0.52 of A.

**Verdict.** Follows, as c313 found. No change.

### 7. Mid-phrase V7–I. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 115 §12 and the box on PDF 119 (point 5): do not make an authentic-cadence effect inside a phrase; set the melody on 3̂ or 5̂, or use V6/VII6/I6.

**Score.** Root-position V7–I falls inside theme A at 8→9, 24→25 and 72→73, each with 3̂ (C) on top at the arrival and the line moving on at once.

**Verdict.** Follows; this is the book's own first remedy. Theme A's harmony and the root-position V7/V–V7 inside 8, 24 and 72 are protected (cycle 2, c442). No change.
