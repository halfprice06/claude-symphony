## Findings

### 1. 78 b3 is a passing 6/3 between basses a 3rd apart, not "III6 representing V" (correct c293's label; no note change)

**Bars:** 78–79.

**Problem.** HISTORY gives three labels for the same chord. The aTwo.ily comment (line 9, c070/c202) calls it "passing 6/3 chord E flat-G-C between flat VI and ii6/5". c293 (Finding 4) relabelled it "V with 6 for 5 ('III6' representing V), PDF 300". c323 recorded it as "iii6 in the falling bass F♭–E♭–D♭". A later critic who sees the c293 label may "correct" the comment or the voicing.

**Evidence.** The voicing at 78 b3 is E♭1/E♭2 (harp), E♭2 (basses), E♭3 (Bsn 2, timpani), G3/G4 (harp, Cl), C4–C6 (Bsn 1, Hn 1, Vla, Vc, violins, Fl 1). It follows F♭ major (♭VI) at 78 b1–2 and leads to ii6/5 over D♭ at 79. PDF 297 covers this case: "Sometimes a passing 6/3 will connect two chords whose bass tones lie a 3rd apart". F♭ and D♭ lie a 3rd apart, and Ex. 18-19b/c (PDF 301) shows the shape. The "III6 = V" examples on PDF 299–300 are different. Ex. 18-16 (Bach) sits inside an expansion of V that ends on I. Ex. 18-17 (Haydn) uses the 6th so the top voice can repeat "without contradicting the impression of a V–I cadence". At 78 b3 no V–I follows: the chord moves to the pre-dominant at 79, and V comes only at 81. The timpani and the harp's E♭1 do give 5̂ the weight PDF 300 describes, but that weight makes the chord a strong passing chord; it does not make it a dominant. For the form, the bar is a stepwise bass descent F♭–E♭–D♭ that carries the deceptive move at 78 into the second peak at 79. The full cadence waits until 80–82.

**Fix.** None in the music. HISTORY should replace c293's Finding 4 label with this one and keep the aTwo.ily comment as it is. If the composer wants the reason in the file, add after line 9: `% c433: 78 b3 confirmed passing 6/3 between basses a 3rd apart (F flat - E flat - D flat), A&S PDF 297, Ex. 18-19b/c; not "III6 = V" (c293), since no V-I follows`.

### 2. The 6/3 bridge 53–56 has two outer 10ths and no hidden 5ths (record)

**Bars:** 53 b4 → 54 b1.

**Evidence.** The outer voices are C3/E♭5 → B♭2/D♭5, which is two 10ths, the "danger" case of Ex. 18-5 (PDF 292; PDF 294: "even in a succession of only two chords"). The inner voices are Cl 1/Hn 1 A♭3 → D♭4 and harp E♭4 → G♭3. The A♭3–E♭5 5th goes to D♭4–D♭5, an octave, so there are no parallel 5ths. The series is one passing motion from D♭ (53 b1, root position, p) to B♭ minor (56 b3, mf). Only the first and last chords are articulated, as on PDF 291, and the cresc. p–mp–mf (53, 54, 56) carries the line between them. The texture is three real voices (violins in unison, Cl 1/Hn 1, cellos) plus harp fills, which matches the note on PDF 302.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. The joins use pedals, not 6/3 series, as Dvořák's does (record)

**Bars:** 31–36, 65–70, 83–86.

**Evidence.** PDF 290 calls a 6/3 series one way to make a transition, not the only way. c143 recorded the pedal joins. The corpus supports them. In Dvořák 9/ii, bars 36–45 hold D♭ (pedal D♭2 under I and IV), and bar 45 thins to a single A♭4, which becomes the common tone into C♯ minor at 46. There is no 6/3 series in that join. This score's 31–36 (A♭ pedal, bass clarinet) and 65–70 (E♭ pedal, horn) work the same way. The 6/3 chords are kept for the music that moves, the build at 53–56, which follows PDF 290–291's contrast between fluid 6/3s and 5/3 goals.

**Fix.** None. The joins are protected (c143, c243).

### 4. The I6 → IV half-step neighbour starts six phrases, and Dvořák uses it at his return (record; extends c293 Finding 3)

**Bars:** 11, 15–16, 27, 41, 57, 75.

**Evidence.** At 11 b2, 27 b2 and 75 b2 the bass is C under A♭–C–E♭, then D♭ for IV on b3. At 15–16 the bass is C3–D♭3, and at 41 and 57 it is F–G♭. PDF 296 says "a neighbor in the bass works best when the melodic progression is a half step. Thus, I6 as N to IV is usually better in major". In Dvořák 9/ii at the return, bar 103 b3 has F2 under D♭–F–A (I6 with raised 5th), and 104 b1 goes to G♭2 (IV). It is the same half-step neighbour. Each of this score's statements of theme A (11, 27, 75) uses it in the same place, so the reprise at 75 sounds like the earlier ones without being a literal repeat.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. The applied chord at 59 is lengthened so V lands on 60 b1, and its doublings are correct (record)

**Bars:** 59 b3 – 60 b1.

**Evidence.** At 59 b3 the chord is E♭7 over G (V6/5 of V); at 59 b4 it is G–B♭–F♭ (vii°7 of V, incomplete); at 60 b1 it is A♭. This is the lengthened applied chord of Ex. 18-11b, "to permit the goal V to fall on a strong beat" (PDF 297). The leading tone G sounds only in the bass line's octaves (basses G1, bass clarinet and Bsn 2 G2), which is an orchestral bass doubling, not a doubled upper voice (PDF 292). The F♭ (E3/E4) is in Bsn 1, cellos, Hn 1 and violas, and every one of them falls to E♭ at 60. The build therefore ends on the strong beat, and the climax at 61 follows a full bar of V.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. The coda's 6/3 chords stand for 5/3s on the way to the plagal close (record)

**Bars:** 85–89.

**Evidence.** At 85 and 86 the A♭ pedal carries C–E♭, then C–F with D in Cl 1. The upper voices form a "VI6" (A♭–C–F) that is I with the 6th displacing the 5th (Ex. 18-14, PDF 298–299), with the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C on top. At 87 the bass moves to F under D♭–F–A♭ (IV6, F in basses, cellos, Hn 3, Vla and C.a.), then to D♭ for iv at 88 and A♭ at 89. This is Ex. 18-19l, "from 5–6 (first chord substitutes for 5/3)" (PDF 301). Doubling the 3rd of IV6 is one of the patterns in Ex. 18-6 (doublings 3–6–3–6, PDF 293), and F is not a tendency tone. In terms of form, the coda stays on I for four bars and then closes plagally with no new key area, as c263 decided.

**Fix.** None. The coda 83–92 is protected.
