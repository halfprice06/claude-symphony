## Findings (ranked)

None of the findings needs a change. The only place where the reading points at a real departure (F1) lies inside the protected horn solo and bassoon layout at 7–14, and the departure there is softened by the harp. I record the evidence so later critics do not need to re-scan.

**F1. Bars 7, 9, 11 b1–2, 14: the sustained low winds form a close triad at the bottom. No change; record.**
Adler PDF 257 and Ex. VIII-25: "the greater gaps exist between the bass and the next highest voice. This spacing is preferred, and it is usual to voice the upper woodwinds or the upper notes of the chord in close position". Ex. VIII-25 marks a close chord of bass clarinet and bassoons in the low register as "bad". The grid gives B.cl. A♭2, Bsn 2 C3, Bsn 1 E♭3 and the horn A♭3 at 7 b1. That is a close triad whose lowest interval is a major third at A♭2–C3. The same layout comes back at 9 b1–2, 11 b1 and 14. Three things soften it, and together they answer the rule. (a) The harp LH strikes A♭1–E♭2 under every one of these chords (7, 9, 11, 14), so the real bass sits an octave and a fifth below the bass clarinet and the gap Adler asks for is present at each attack. (b) Everything is pp and con legato, with the horn alone on top. (c) The layout is Rimsky-Korsakov's "2 Fag. over Cl. basso" close four-part harmony (c004), and it is protected ("horn theme and bassoon layout 7–14", c077 onwards). Any fix would move Bsn 2 off C3, and that would either cross Bsn 1 or leave the third out of the chord. No change. If this point is ever reopened, the place to change is the harp LH, not the bassoons.

**F2. Bars 53–56: Cl 1 doubles Hn 1's counter-line in unison. No change; record against Adler PDF 257.**
Adler PDF 257: "doubling instruments at the unison ... robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch. In full-orchestra tutti this practice is still common." The counter-line at 53 b4–56 (A♭3–D♭4–B♭4–G♭4–C4–D♭4) is Cl 1 and Hn 1 at the same pitch, pp to mp, outside a tutti. This is the one soft unison between two winds in the movement. It was a stated choice: c038 put the clarinet "one level under horn 1, so the unison is one colour", and c078 protected it. The reading supports that choice in another passage. In Copland's Ex. VIII-41 (PDF 270), Adler praises a clarinet and a muted horn doubling a weak inner part ("the second violins therefore need the heavy doubling of the second clarinet and the muted horn"). A blended horn and clarinet colour for a counter-line is the effect Adler admires there. The line has to sound through the harp triplets and the violins in unison, and a single horn at pp in the A♭3–B♭4 range would be weaker. Record and keep.

**F3. Bars 82–92: "melting" and a scored diminuendo. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 252 (Brahms 2/iii, Ex. VIII-18): "the woodwind chord dissolves into a string chord; then, at the very end, the final chord is colored by a wind chord". Adler PDF 270 (Copland): a diminuendo is scored "by cutting out" an instrument. In the score, the strings' pedal enters at 83 under the harp before the winds of 82 have died away. At 87–90 the C.a., Cl 1 and muted Hn 3·4 colour the IV6–I strings. At 90 b3 the C.a., Hn 3·4 and Cl 1 leave together, and Cl 2's E♭4 alone colours the last string chord through 92. That is Brahms's layout, and the diminuendo is done by removing instruments as Copland does.

**F4. Choirs separately, then together. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 253 (Schumann 1/iii): "whenever the choirs are first used separately and then together, a most satisfying sense of completion or resolution results." The plan is: winds alone 3–14; strings with one oboe 15–30; winds alone 31–36; mixed from 37; the full tutti only at 61 and 79. The grid confirms that no string sounds from 4 to 14 or from 31 to 36. It also confirms that no wind except Ob 1 sounds from 15 to 28, apart from Hn 3·4 and the contrabassoon taking over at 29–30.

**F5. The third use of an orchestration. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration." Theme B's accompaniment changes at every entry. At 37–40 the violas play arco triplets under Vln II's held chord, with the basses pizz. At 41–44 the violas play pizz triplets and the cellos hold. At 53–56 the harp takes the triplets and the violas rest. At 57–60 the violas return with triplets that turn into sextuplets at 59. Theme A has three colours: horn (7), violins in unison (23), violins in octaves with the viola figure (71). The motto tail keeps its own colours as in PDF 261: horn at 8 and 67–69, clarinet at 85–86.

**F6. Bassoon 2 takes the 8-foot bass while the cellos play the counter-melody, 71–76. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 267–268 (Ex. VIII-39): in normal doubling the bassoons go with the cellos. At 71–76 the cellos leave the bass for the counter-melody. The grid shows Bsn 2 an octave above the basses on every bass change (F2/F3 at 72, B♭1/B♭2, E♭2/E♭3, A♭1/A♭2 at 73, B♭1/B♭2 at 74). So the bassoon keeps the bass's 8-foot octave, which the cellos would normally give. The one exception, C3 over A♭1 at 71 b1, is an inner tenor note. This is the role Adler gives the bassoons, moved to the line that needs it.

**F7. First-inversion doubling scan. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 257: "the third (bass) should be doubled toward the bottom rather than the top." inv.py found 43 beats whose lowest note is a triad's third. In every case except two, the doublings of that third lie in the lower half of the chord: bass clarinet, bassoons, cellos, violas, or the horns below C5. The two exceptions are both melody notes. One is Ob 1's C5 at 15 b1–2, the head of the oboe's dialogue phrase. The other is Fl 1's held G♭5 at 43 b1, a note of the flute's counter-line over C.a. theme B. Adler's rule covers chordal filling, not melody notes. Nothing to change.

**F8. Counterpoint and imitation. Follows in counter-melody form; record.**
Adler PDF 271: "since each of the woodwinds has such a unique color, contrapuntal writing, especially rhythmical, is very effective". Mozart's Ex. VIII-42 has the oboes answered by the bassoons over pizzicato strings. The movement's contrapuntal centre is 53–56: theme A's head (pickup, then dotted quarter and eighth) in Cl 1/Hn 1 against theme B in the violins and the harp triplets. Two themes in two colours and three rhythmic layers make the rhythmic counterpoint Adler means. Critic 177 (F4) recorded that the movement has no strict imitation by design, as in Dvořák 9/ii, and that the only free places are closed by the c124 rule (37–60) or protected (71–82). I found no reason to reopen that.
