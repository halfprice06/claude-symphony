## Findings

### 1. Bars 52 b4–60: the theme in the violins has no character word, while its counter-line has two (applied)
- **Problem.** Theme B's first statement in the strings starts on the violins' C5 pickup at 52 b4 with "senza sord., sul A" (Vln I) and "sul A" (Vln II), then "ord." at 57. These are all technical words. The counter-line in horn 1 has "espr." at 53, and the cellos that double it get "espr." again at 57. So in the build to the climax the only expressive words are on the secondary line.
- **Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: "It is the arranger's job to see that the listener's ear is directed to the primary element and not diverted or confused by an overbusy background or counter-line." In Ex. 2A (PDF 18) every colour that takes over the lead is given a word for how to play it ("SWEETLY" on the flutes, "PRETTY", "EASY"). Every other lead entry in this score has one (horn 6, B.cl. 31, Fl 37, C.a. 41, Ob 44, C.a. 47, Hn 67, Vln I 70, Cl 85). c058 removed "espr." from the violas at 71 for the opposite case (a word on the background). The counter-line's "espr." is right (Ex. 269, PDF 205–207, gives the counter-melody its own character). The theme needs at least an equal word.
- **Fix (bSec.ily).** Words only; no dynamic, slur or pitch changes. Vln I: `r2 r4 c''4(\p^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "senza sord., sul A," "cantabile" } |`. Vln II: `es''2.\< c''4(\p^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "sul A," "cantabile" } |`. Two lines, as the "tutti ..., espr., sul D" text at 22, so nothing runs past the right margin at the end of the system.

### 2. Bars 15 and 17: the oboe and the solo violin start the b phrase with no character word (applied)
- **Problem.** The dialogue at 15–22 is the first time the melody leaves the horn. Oboe 1 enters at 15 with `\p\<` and the label "1." only. The solo violin at 17 has "solo, senza sord." only. These are the only two solo melodic entries in the movement without an expressive word. The violins that accompany them have "con sord." and are the only parts on the page with text.
- **Evidence.** Ex. 2A (PDF 17–18): when the melody passes to a new colour, that colour gets its own dynamic and a word. The flutes' "SWEETLY" comes with the same mp<mf>mp hairpins that the oboe and solo violin have here. Sebesky PDF 14: focus moves from player to player, and the arranger must "manipulate his ensemble accordingly". The word tells each new lead player that the line is theirs and how to colour it. The horn had "dolce espr." at 6, and the oboe gets "dolce espr." at 44, so "dolce" here belongs to the same family and leaves "espr." for the later, more intense entries.
- **Fix (aOne.ily).** `aOneOboeOne`: `c''4.(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce" des''8 es''4 |`. `aOneVlnOne` at 17: `^\markup \italic "solo, senza sord., dolce"`. Dynamics and hairpins are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 59: the counter-line is slurred in horn 1 but articulated in bassoon 1 and the cellos (applied)
- **Problem.** From 59 horn 1, bassoon 1 and the cellos play the same line in unison/octaves at f: G♭ (half), E♭, F♭ | E♭, F, G♭., F. Bassoon 1 (`ges2\f es4( fes4 | es4\< f4 ges4. f8)`) and the cellos (`ges2\f es4( fes4) | es4(\upbow\< ...`) both detach the G♭ and start a new slur on the E♭ at beat 3. Horn 1 alone slurs G♭ into E♭ (`ges'2(\f es'4 fes'4 | ...`). The figure therefore starts with two different attacks on 59 b3.
- **Evidence.** Ex. 281 (PDF 214–215, viewed as images): every part that plays a figure has the same articulation, in every section. Sebesky PDF 213 says well-written "charts play themselves": players get the feel of the notes at once. Two of the three parts, including the strings, whose bowing c096 fixed, already articulate the E♭. The horn should match them, not the other way round.
- **Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecHnOne`).** `ges'2\f es'4( fes'4 | es'4\< f'4 ges'4. f'8) |`. Pitches, dynamics, the breath after 58, and the c151/c082 rhythm at 58 are unchanged. This touches articulation only, not the dynamics or scoring of the protected build 53–61.
