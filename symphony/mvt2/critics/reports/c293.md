## Findings

I found no place where the score breaks a principle of Unit 18. None of the findings below is a defect that stands between this score and a master's work. They are records, ranked by how likely a later critic is to raise them again, so the composer can add them to HISTORY. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-293/work is an unchanged copy of v293.

### 1. Theme B's falling-3rds bass is filled by a passing V6 only in its third statement, at the start of the build (record, no change)

**Bars:** 37–38 and 53–54.

**Check.** Both statements stand on the same bass, D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A–B♭ (37–40, 53–56). This is the hidden descent in 3rds of Ex. 18-3b, "similar to I–(VI)–(IV)–II–V–I" (PDF 291). At 37 b3 the bass stays on D♭ and the G♭5 on top is a 6 over the tonic bass (Ex. 18-14, PDF 298–299). At 53 b3–4 the bass falls to C under A♭–G♭ and A♭–E♭ (V6/5, then V6), and 54 b1 is IV6 over B♭. That is the passing V6 that "connects I with VI, the usual start of a falling-thirds descent" (PDF 297, Ex. 18-11a). Dvořák 9/ii bar 17 makes the same motion (D♭–C–B♭ under I–V6–vi). For form this means the first, solo statement (flute over pizzicato and harp, 37) keeps a plain bass, and the third statement (violins, 53), where the build 53–61 starts, gets the more fluid passing-6/3 bass. PDF 290 says this is where a 6/3 fill belongs: in a passage moving towards a goal.

**Fix.** None. Do not "regularise" 37 b3 to a passing C, and do not take the C out of 53 b3–4.

### 2. The 6/3 chords cluster in the B section's build and are rare in the reprise, as the reading's account of their "less stable" sound predicts (record, no change)

**Bars:** whole movement.

**Check.** My script lists first-inversion triads lasting at least an eighth note at: A section 11, 15, 18, 19, 20, 27; transition 34; B section 38, 39, 41, 43, 44, 49, 53, 54, 57, 59; A′ 74 (passing, inside V4/3), 75, 78; coda 87. Ten of the 21 places fall in the B section (37–64), and four of those ten fall in the build 53–59. The A′ reprise has three, the coda one, and all three joins (31–36, 65–70, 83–86) stand on pedals with no 6/3 series. PDF 290–291 calls the 6/3 "more fluid, less stable" than the 5/3 and suited to transitional passages, with 5/3s as goals. The score puts the fluid chords where the music is moving (the middle section and its build) and the root-position goals where it arrives (37, 53, 61, 71, 82). This supports the existing tension curve and the c143 record that the joins use pedals, as Dvořák's do.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. The neighbour 6/3 I6 → IV with a half-step bass marks the start of three phrases (record, no change)

**Bars:** 15–16, 41, 57.

**Check.** The b phrase (15), theme B's second phrase (41) and the tutti statement of that phrase (57) all open on I6 and move to IV with the bass rising a half step (C–D♭ at 15–16; F–G♭ at 41 b1 → b3 and at 57 b1 → b3). PDF 296: "a neighbor in the bass works best when the melodic progression is a half step. Thus, I6 as N to IV is usually better in major than in minor." Dvořák 9/ii 9–10 does the same (F2 → G♭2 under I6 → IV). For form, these openings start each continuation off the tonic root, so 15, 41 and 57 read as continuations, not new beginnings. The bass also rises where theme B's own head rises.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. The cadence-delaying "6 for 5" at 78 b3 is "III6 representing V", not a free passing chord (record; comment label only)

**Bars:** 78–79.

**Check.** At 78 b3 the bass is E♭ (E♭1–E♭3) under G and C (C minor 6/3). The aTwo.ily comment (c133) calls it "a passing 6/3". By PDF 300 and Ex. 18-16 and 18-17 it is the dominant with the 6th displacing the 5th ("not III6"): the bass E♭ is V, and the top C lets the line lead to 79's B♭6 appoggiatura without a full V–I. The progression then goes to IV (D♭, 79) instead of I. That keeps the cadence open for the second peak 79–82, which closes with ii–V♭9–I at 80–82. In form terms the deceptive move at 78 (F♭, ♭VI) and this evaded V at 78 b3 extend the reprise by four bars (79–82) before the full close. A 59–60 parallel is also correct: the V6/5 of V and incomplete vii°7 of V (G–B♭–F♭) fill 59 b3–4 so that V falls on 60 b1, as Ex. 18-11b's lengthened applied chord does (PDF 297).

**Fix.** No note change. Optional comment correction in sections/aTwo.ily next to c133's note: `% c293: 78 b3 C minor 6/3 over the E♭ bass = V with 6 for 5 ("III6" representing V, A&S PDF 300, Ex. 18-16/17), evaded to IV at 79`. I did not apply it because comments do not change the score.

### 5. "VI6" over the tonic bass keeps the transition, the quotation bar and the coda on their tonic (record, no change)

**Bars:** 34, 49, 85–86.

**Check.**
- 34 b1: over the A♭ pedal the upper voices C3–F3–A♭3 go to C3–E♭3–A♭3 on b2. This is the 5–6–5 decoration of Ex. 18-14 (Chopin), "not VI6", so the bass-clarinet transition stays on A♭ until the A♭7 pivot.
- 49 b2: D/F♯ over the held F♯ is i with 6 for 5 (c262's 5–6). Per PDF 298–299 it is still the tonic, so the cor anglais quotation's bar keeps one harmony for two beats and changes with the bass on b3 (N6, G/B), then V on b4.
- 85–86: over the A♭ pedal, C–E♭ becomes C–F (6 displacing 5), then the bass moves to F for IV6 at 87 and to D♭ for iv at 88. This is Ex. 18-19l, in which the first chord stands for a 5/3 on the way to the predominant (PDF 301). The coda therefore prolongs I for four bars before the plagal close, and does not introduce a new harmonic area. That fits the c263 decision that the coda recalls no theme in a remote key.

**Fix.** None. All three passages are protected (woodwind choir 31–36, quotation 47–53, close 85–92).
