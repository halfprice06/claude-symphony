## Findings

No violation of the assigned reading remains in v353. The findings below are records, ranked by how much they bear on the form. I made no change, so I did not build the scratch copy. /tmp/critic-353/work is an unmodified copy of v353.

### 1. The double pedal at the two ends frames the movement (records a strength)

- **Bars:** 1-3 and 89-92.
- **What the score does.** At 2-3 the bass holds A♭1/A♭2, Vln II holds A♭4 and Vln I holds C5. At 87-92 Vln II holds A♭4 from 87, Vln I takes C5 at 89, and the bass returns to A♭1 at 89. This is a double pedal (A♭ in the bass and an inner voice), and the top-voice pedal on C5 makes it a triple pedal at the end. A&S PDF 367 names top-voice, inner-voice and double or triple pedals as standard. Here the inner A♭4 pedal holds through IV6 (87) and iv (88), the two chords that move against it. The beat dump confirms the same three pitches (A♭1, A♭4, C5) at 2 and at 89-92.
- **Verdict:** follows. This ties the first and last bars together more strongly than the plagal close's top line alone. Nothing to change.

### 2. 65-67: three bars with nothing moving over the dominant pedal (recorded; no change)

- **Bars:** 65-67.
- **What the score does.** At 65-66 the E♭7 (E♭2 B♭2 G3 D♭4) is held without a new note and fades to niente. At 67 only E♭2 (basses), E♭3 (timpani) and E♭4 (horn 1's entry) sound. That is 9 beats, about 10 s at ♩=52, with no chord change and no figuration. A&S PDF 367 defines the pedal as the static element against moving voices, and Ex. 21-41 keeps the upper voices moving all through its pedals. c223 recorded bar 67 for the same reason.
- **Why I do not propose a change.** The cut-back at 65-67 is protected (c253: "cut-backs at 65-67 and 83"; plan: "horn 1 unsupported"), and Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365) supports the effect. A V7 held past the point where motion is expected sounds like a whole-chord suspension and makes the listener wait for the tonic. In this score that tonic arrives at 71 with the return of theme A. Dvořák's bass part does the same thing on a larger scale: before its reprise it holds C♯ for three bars and then rests for three more. If the composer ever reopens 65-70, the step the book supports is bassoons 1·2 entering at 67 instead of 68 (c223's suggestion). It would not change the bar count or the horn line.

### 3. The pedal plan matches A&S PDF 367 and has good proportions (recorded)

- **Placement.** Tonic pedals come at the beginning (2-6), at the close of A (30-36) and at the end (83-86, 89-92). Dominant pedals come before each return: 51-52 before theme B's return at 53, 60 before the climax at 61, and 65-70 before A′ at 71. That is A&S PDF 367 point for point, including "before a tonic that comes at a recapitulation of the opening material".
- **Durations (from score.midi):** 1-6 20.8 s; 30-36 25.0 s; 51-52 8.9 s; 60 4.0 s; 65-70 20.8 s; 83-92 39.4 s (of which 87-88 are off the pedal). That totals about 119 s of 345 s, a third of the movement, with about 76 s on I and 34 s on V. The pedals sit only between thematic statements (link, transition, retransition, coda). Theme A at 7-30 and 71-82 has a moving bass, as the plan asks ("melodic bass counterline"). Dvořák instead puts a tonic pedal under the theme's second statement. This score's choice gives the A sections more harmonic motion and keeps the pedals as section markers. Both are within the book's description ("tonic pedals occur frequently at beginnings and endings").
- **The one short dominant.** The climax at 61 has only one bar of dominant pedal (60), against two bars before the lesser return at 53. c223 recorded this. The chromatic bass rise G♭-G-A♭ at 59-60 and the A♭ timpani roll at 60 carry the preparation, and the bass ascent 57-60 and the 8-page layout are protected. I add no proposal.

### 4. Every upper-voice dissonance over a pedal is prepared and resolved (A&S PDF 368)

My script lists every dissonant interval above the pedal tone. All of them are handled as the book requires:
- **33 b2:** F3-C♭4 (°7 over A♭): C♭ goes to B♭ and D to D♭ at 33 b3, by step.
- **51 b3-4:** G♭4 (seventh of A♭7) against C3 resolves to F4/F5 at 52 b1. The G♭4 at 52 b4 resolves to F at 53.
- **52 b1-2:** D3 against C4 and F4. The D is the cellos' passing ♯4 (C3-D3-E♭3, accepted in c322), and it moves on by step at 52 b3.
- **52 b3:** C.a. F4 against E♭3 and E♭5 is an added sixth over V. It moves by step to G♭4 and returns to F at 53, where the chord change makes it the third of D♭ (c223, finding 4).
- **60 b2-4:** passing F, then G♭ as the seventh of V7, which resolves to F at 61.
- **65-66 and 69-70:** D♭ (seventh of E♭7) resolves to C at 71.
- **86:** D5 against C4 is the clarinet's chromatic passing tone in the motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C (protected). It moves on by step to D♭5 at 87.

No leap from a dissonance occurs between the upper voices. The leaps against the pedal (33, 52) are the kind the book allows ("leaps involving dissonances against the bass are also good").

### 5. The joins out of each pedal follow Ex. 21-42 or Review 13 (recorded)

- **36→37:** the A♭ pedal ends on its own chord and D♭ enters by phrase modulation. The plan (c020) avoids V7 of D♭, and the bare fifth at 35-36 is protected (c184, c213, c223).
- **50→51 and 64→65:** the bass moves by step or semitone into the new pedal (B→A♭ under the protected Bm7→A♭7 pivot; E→E♭ slip, protected). The pedal starts on its full chord in both places (A♭7 at 51 b3, E♭7 at 65).
- **86→87:** the tonic pedal leads into a chord that moves away (D-F-A♭-C → IV6). This is the exception the book shows in Ex. 21-42, and here it serves the plagal close.

All follow. No change.

### 6. Whole-chord suspension at 47 (recorded)

At 47 the C♯7 (D♭2 A♭2 B3 D♭4 F4 D♭5) is held for a full bar under "poco rit." before F♯ minor at 48, which makes the listener wait for the quotation's key. This is the effect of Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365) and Dvořák 9/ii 53-54 (cited in global.ily, c048). F♯ minor at 48 then sits on its open fifth G♭2/D♭3 for a bar, a short tonic pedal under the quotation's start. Follows.
