## What else I studied

I compared v109 with v99 bar by bar from the diff. The changes since then are: strings f at 28 (c103); the IV7 voicing at 25 (c102); iii6 at 78 b3 (c102); Fl 1 dynamics at 41–44 (c101); Bsn 1 D♭3 at 54–56 (c107); Tbn 2 at 61–62 (c104); timpani at 62 (c099); fp at 47 (c108); viola off-beats at 48–51 (c107); cor anglais at 87–88 (c104); Vla/Vln II at 38–39 (c102); Hn 1 pp at 52 (c108). None of them made the score worse. The bass at 25 and the third at 78 are cleaner than in v99, and the B section's background is now settled.

The master movement is Scheherazade III (Andantino quasi allegretto, "The Young Prince and the Princess"). It has not been used in an earlier holistic cycle, and the assigned examples give its scoring directly: Ex. 12 (PDF 186) shows the opening theme in Vln I+II unison on the D and A strings over sustained violas and divided cellos, pp, poco cresc to mf and back. Ex. 30 (PDF 207–208) shows the reprise with violins and cellos in octaves, clarinet and flute arabesques around it, and a soft timpani and pizzicato pulse. Ex. 58 and 66 (PDF 230, 237–238) show the middle section with its snare-drum and pizzicato rhythm.

Holistic verdict. A conductor would accept this score as competent and idiomatic. The voicing is correct, every solo is chosen by register, and the climax is doubled the way the book teaches. Rimsky-Korsakov keeps one theme alive by changing its colour and its decoration at each return (strings, then clarinet arabesques, then cello-and-violin octaves). This movement keeps its theme alive with counter-melodies, in the Brahms/Dvořák manner. That is a legitimate choice, and it is protected (c023, c060), so I do not ask for Rimsky-style arabesques. Where this score falls short of the master is in small practical markings, not in design. My one applied finding is of that kind.

## Findings

### 1. Retransition 65–70: the double-bass E♭ pedal should be marked "4 soli" (APPLIED)

**Problem.** For six bars the only string sound is the full double-bass section on E♭2 (sounding E♭1 written as `es,`) at pp, under Hn 1 alone and a timpani roll. A whole section of basses on a pp pedal under one unsupported horn is thicker than the texture needs. It also makes the timpani roll and the bass pedal hard to tell apart.

**Evidence.** The book says the double bass on its own, without cellos or bassoons, is a special effect (PDF 56 d). In both examples where Rimsky-Korsakov leaves the basses alone under a soloist he thins them: Ex. 40 (PDF 218, Scheherazade II) has a bassoon solo over "4 C-bassi soli con sord.", and Ex. 5 (PDF 179) marks "C-b. 4 soli". Scheherazade III's opening (Ex. 12, PDF 186) also keeps its string bass light under the melody. Here the section has no melody to support. It holds a pedal, which is exactly the case these examples cover.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-109/work).**
- sections/retr.ily, retrCb: `es,2.\pp~^\markup \italic "4 soli, change bow at will"` (was `"change bow at will"`).
- sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoCb, bar 71: `aes,,2.\pp^\markup \italic "tutti"`.

No notes, dynamics or MIDI changed, and no tempo word was added. "change bow at will" stays, because four players still have to sustain six bars.

### 2. Bars 23–28: the double basses carry the bass alone at 16' (not applied)

**Problem.** Under theme A in the violins the cellos play the counter-melody, so the basses are left alone on the bass line, sounding an octave below the written pitch. The book says to avoid this (PDF 56 d, PDF 61 f).

**Evidence and verdict.** The bass here is slow and harmonic, with no melody of its own, and only the basses carry it: the bassoons and harp rest from 15 to 30, and the contrabassoon enters only at 30. The cellos' counter-melody is protected (c060, the plan's "melodic bass counterline"). In Scheherazade III's reprise (Ex. 30, PDF 207–208) the cellos also leave the bass to join the melody, but the bass is reinforced there. If the composer wants the book's practice, the fix is to give Bsn 2 the bass line an octave above the basses at 23–26, pp: `aes,2. | f4 bes,4 es4 | aes,2 des4 | es2. |` in aOneBsnTwo. A bassoon entering in a system where it was silent can add a staff, so the page count would need checking. I do not recommend it now. The 23–30 string sound is meant to be pure strings after the string-free 7–14, and the bar-25 voicing (c102) was balanced against the present bass.

### 3. Bar 28: strings f against an mf model (not applied)

**Problem.** Theme A's first string statement peaks at f in bar 28 (c103). Scheherazade III's first string statement rises only to mf (Ex. 12, PDF 186), and keeps f for the reprise.

**Evidence and verdict.** c103 based the f on Dvořák 9/ii. Both models are valid, and the f leaves room above it for the ff at 79. A change would be `\mf` in place of `\f` at 28 in all string parts. I am not applying it. The difference is a matter of taste, and reverting a change made six cycles ago without a stronger reason would only cause churn.

### 4. Bar 62: Cl 1 above the oboes in the ff tutti (not applied)

**Problem.** The book's natural order puts the oboes above the clarinets when the woodwinds play harmony (PDF 65, 67).

**Evidence and verdict.** At ff in a full tutti the crossing is not audible as a fault, and the book allows crossing for melodic reasons. c084 placed Cl 1 there to keep its line continuous. No change.

### 5. Bars 53–56: Hn 1 + Cl 1 pp unison (not applied)

**Problem.** The book says a unison doubling of two different instruments costs expressiveness, and intimate phrases are best left to one solo (PDF 66–67).

**Evidence and verdict.** This passage is the B3 counter-line from theme A's head. c019 and c107 protect it, and the Hn 1 pp marking was settled in c108. The mixed colour also serves as a blend under the violins rather than as a solo, which PDF 65–66 approves. No change.

### 6. Proportions: the movement is short beside the model (not applied)

At 92 bars and about 5:46 it is shorter than Scheherazade III (about 10 minutes) or most of the models used before. The 8-bar periods are regular throughout. Rimsky-Korsakov's movement also uses square phrases, so this is not a fault by the reading's standard, and c023 protects the proportions. Recorded only as an observation.
