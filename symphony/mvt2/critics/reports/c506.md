## Findings

This is the sixth pass of Rimsky-Korsakov ch. I under the playability lens. The score meets every principle in PDF 24-43 that applies to it. I found no defect that needs a change. The findings record the places where the reading gives a reason to look twice, ranked by how much each could matter. Each one gives the fix the composer could use. I applied none of them.

### 1. The bass clarinet solo at 31-36 lies outside its scope of greatest expression, and its peak swells to mf in the "dull" middle register. Recorded, no change.

The written range is F3-F4 (sounding E♭2-E♭3). Table B (PDF 35, image) gives the bass clarinet Low E3-C4 "full, dark" and Middle D4-C5 "dull", and it underlines only the high register (D5-A5) as the scope of greatest expression. PDF 32 says that outside the scope a wind instrument "is more notable for richness of colour than for expression". The held E♭3 at 32-33 (written F4) is in the middle register and is marked p < mf >.

The use still follows the reading. PDF 38 values the bass clarinet for its "darker colour in the low register" and says it "lacks the silvery quality in the upper notes". PDF 39 says these instruments "are often used in the orchestra, as solo instruments, clearly exposed" for their distinctive low timbre. The passage is a colour solo over an A♭ pedal, written for the bass clarinet's low voice (cyclic-notes: melodic moments for bass clarinet). An octave transposition (written F4-F5) would put the peak at written F5, still below the scope, and would break the protected woodwind choir at 31-36. Fix only if wanted: in `transBassCl` change `\mf \<` / `es4\mf\>` to `\mp` so that the swell does not ask for more than the middle register gives. I do not advise it, because the mf is reached for only one beat.

### 2. Violin I plays theme A above C6 at mp at 71-73. Recorded, no change.

PDF 27 (image): violin notes above the soprano's top C6 "lose in expression and warmth of tone". At 71-73 Vln I has E♭6-D6-D♭6-C6 at mp<>. Vln II carries the same line an octave lower sul D (PDF 27: the D string is "somewhat sweeter"), and the cellos have the counter-melody. The expressive weight is therefore in the voice range, and Vln I adds the octave above, which is Rimsky's own way of using the top register. Fix only if wanted: `aTwoVlnOne` bars 71-73 an octave lower, in unison with Vln II. That would undo the protected violin octave plan (c009, "Vln I single octave 70-74" closed), so I do not advise it.

### 3. Covered strings at pp at 64-66 (Vln II D♭4 sul G, Vla G3 sul C). Recorded, no change.

PDF 27: the covered strings (violin G, viola and cello G and C) "are rather harsh". Both inner parts sustain the 65-66 chord on covered strings as they fade to pp. The violin has no choice, since D♭4 exists only on the G string. For the viola, the reading itself gives the reason for the choice: "open strings are clearer and more powerful but less expressive than stopped strings" (PDF 27), and an open G could not taper evenly to nothing. A stopped G3 on the C string (fourth finger in first position, well inside the fourth-position limit of row 6) is the right choice for a diminuendo. The string colour plan is protected (c186 list).

### 4. Horns above the Table C scope: Hn 1 written F5 at 12 and 54-55; Hn 3·4 written G♭5 at 62. Recorded, no change.

Table C (PDF 43, image) gives the horn's scope of greatest expression as partials 4-10 (written C4-E5) and marks partials 12-16 (written G5 upward) as "little used". Hn 1's B♭4 (written F5) at 12 b2 (mp) and at 54-55 (pp<mp, held for 1½ bars in unison with Cl 1) lies between partials 10 and 12. That is above the scope but below the "little used" range. Hn 3·4's C♭5 at 62 (written G♭5, f, a2) is a semitone under partial 12. PDF 41 says brass "played pp the resonance is sweet", and the clarinet unison at 54-55 makes the soft entry safe. At 62 the note is at f and doubled, which is the safest way to place a high note on a low horn. The Hn 3·4 a2 is on the standing list (c064, c069, c086), and c436/c466 recorded the horn ranges. If the composer ever reopens 61-62, the fix is to give Hn 4 the lower octave `aes1 | bes2 ces'2` (sounding A♭3-C♭4) and leave Hn 3 alone on top. c069 tried that and c086 reversed it, so I leave it.

### 5. Single-player and "soli" divisions are marked as PDF 25 asks. Recorded.

PDF 25: "It is always as well to mark how the passage is to be divided in the score." Each reduced division here has its own marking: the Vln I solo player at 17-22, with the part text telling the soloist when to put on the mute and rejoin at 25; the Cb "4 soli" at 65-70 and 87; and the Vc "4 soli" at 83-86 with "tutti" at 87. The change of personnel at 87 comes on a new note in both sections, so no player drops out under a held note. Nothing to fix.
