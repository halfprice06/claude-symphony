## What else I studied

As support I used Adler ch. 2, PDF 37–38. It says each unslurred note means a bow change, which skilled players make "hardly noticed". It says the up-bow tends to grow louder and the down-bow to fade. It also says a composer should mark bow direction only to counteract that habit. I read the string parts of Dvořák 9/ii in the local corpus (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily`). There the strings play con sordino almost throughout. Solo strings are used ("2 violins con sordini", l. 116, 240; "2 violas", l. 370). The basses hold tied pedals with no bowing text, which leaves the bow changes to the players. Finding 1 below is therefore a part-preparation point, not a compositional fault. I rendered score pages 2–8 of v16 and checked every string variable in every section file. I also checked the timpani tuning (A♭2, C3, D♭3, E♭3: each drum is within its normal compass and nothing needs retuning). The harp pedal setting at 7 (D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭) fits every harp note in 7–14, 71–82 and 83–92, and the harp has no part in 15–70, so it never changes a pedal. I found no new problem in brass endurance: horn 1 has four slow bars of rest (63–66) between the ff at 61–62 and the p solo at 67.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Long double-bass pedals have no bow-change instruction (31–36, 65–70). Applied.
**Problem.** The double basses hold A♭1 tied for 5½ bars at 31–36 (♩=52 slowing to 45, about 22 s). They hold E♭2 tied for six bars at 65–70 (about 21 s). Both pedals are pp, and in both passages the basses are the only strings playing (31–36) or nearly so. No bow change is shown.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." Adler PDF 37: with this instruction the section's bow changes are "hardly … noticed". A tie across six bars, with no instruction, tells each player to sustain one note, and a bass bow cannot hold one stroke for 20 s at pp.
**Fix.**
- trans.ily: `transCb = { aes,,2.\pp~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | …`
- retr.ily: `retrCb = { es,2.\pp~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | …`

### 2. The coda's lower-string pedals need the same instruction (83–86, 89–92). Applied.
**Problem.** The cellos and basses hold A♭ tied for four bars at 83–86. At 89–92 every string section except Vln I holds a tied note for four bars under a rit. that slows to ♩=36 and then to a fermata (about 20 s, ppp). Vln I already has bow marks. The other sections have none.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 108 (as in finding 1), and PDF 37–38 on unnoticed bow changes. A staggered change at ppp is inaudible; one bow held for 20 s is not playable.
**Fix.** coda.ily:
- `codaVla`: `<c' es'>2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "(div.) change bow at will"`
- `codaVc`: `aes,2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`
- `codaCb`: `aes,,2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`
- `codaVlnTwo`, bar 89: `aes'2.~^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`

### 3. Vln I coda bowing ends the morendo on an up-bow (85–92). Applied.
**Problem.** Vln I has written bowings: down at 85, then alternating, so the last bar (92, ppp, fermata, ♩=20 in the MIDI) is an up-bow. An up-bow moves toward the frog, where the bow is heaviest. The pp entry at 85 is a down-bow from the frog, the heaviest place to start a soft note.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog… The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural… there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow." The same page says to mark bow direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". As written, the marks work against the habit at both ends of the line.
**Fix.** Move the pattern by one bow, so the line enters up-bow at the tip and dies away down-bow toward the tip:
```
codaVlnOne = {
  R2.*2 | es''2.\pp\upbow | d''2.\downbow |
  des''2.\upbow | des''2.\downbow | c''2.~\upbow | c''2.~\downbow | c''2.~\upbow\> | c''2.\downbow\ppp\fermata |
}
```
The hairpin at 91 now starts on an up-bow. At this dynamic that is a small cost, and the final fermata is the more important stroke.

### 4. The violins' p unison statement of theme B at 53–56 should be "sul A". Applied.
**Problem.** At 53–56 both violin sections play theme B in unison, p rising to mf, on F5–G♭5–E♭5–D♭5–C5. Most violinists would play these notes in first position on the E string, which is the bright colour. Four bars later the violins climb to B♭6 at f, and those bars need the E string. As written, the soft statement and the climb have the same colour, so the climb has less to grow from.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75: "In soft, lyrical, expressive passages, the composer will often prefer the higher pitches to be performed on the A-string rather than have the player cross to the E-string, where these upper notes take on a different, more brilliant timbre." His Ex. III-7 (Brahms 3/iii, mm. 13–24) is the same case: a p espressivo violin melody of the same height, marked sul A. On the A string, F5–G♭5 lie in 3rd–4th position, which is easy. The score already uses this device at 22–26 (sul D … ord.).
**Fix.** bSec.ily, Vln I and Vln II bar 53: `f''4.(\p\<^\markup \italic "sul A" ges''8 …`. Bar 57: Vln I `aes''4(\<^\markup \italic "ord." des'''4) …` and Vln II `aes'4(\<^\markup \italic "ord." des''4) …`.

### 5. The violas' open G3 is held and exposed at 65–66. Applied.
**Problem.** At the start of the retransition the only strings playing are Vln II D♭4, Vla G3 and Vc B♭2, pp diminuendo, for two slow bars. G3 is the viola's open G string. An open string cannot be vibrated and, in Adler's word, "stands out" against the stopped D♭4 and B♭2.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 73–74: open strings "stand out peculiarly" in "slow, expressive passages", and "the desired timbre should be the only justification for putting fingerings in the score". This passage is a case of that justification. G3 can be stopped with the 4th finger in first position on the C string (fingering chart, PDF 86).
**Fix.** retr.ily: `retrVla = { g2.\pp^\markup \italic "unis., sul C" | g2.\> | R2.*4\! | }`

### 6. Points checked and not raised
- Vln I B♭6 at 76 and 79–80 (Adler PDF 72) is above the old 7th-position limit but within the practical range. It is reached by step or by a fourth and is doubled by Fl 1. I see no playability problem. The ledger-line notation is the open engraving point for cycle 25.
- The cellos' cantabile line at 23–30 and 71–78 lies naturally on the D string, the colour Adler (PDF 99) prefers. Writing "sul D" would add nothing.
- The cellos' F4 at 62 in bass clef has two ledger lines. That is below Adler's three-ledger threshold (PDF 104), so no clef change is needed.
