## Findings

### 1. Ties on one note of a two-note chord are drawn on the wrong side (Vln II 38-39, Vla 80-81, Vla 88-89). Applied in the scratch copy.

**Bars and problem.** Where only one note of a chord is tied, LilyPond gives that single tie the direction of a tie on a lone note, opposite the stem. In four places this sends the tie into or past the other note of the chord, so it appears to join two different pitches:

- Vln II 38 b1-b3, 38 b3-39 b1 (`<des' bes'~>2 <ges' bes'~>2`): stems up, the tied B♭4 is the upper note, but both ties curve down and run through the space next to G♭4, ending at the G♭4 of the next chord. It reads as D♭4 or G♭4 being held, not B♭4.
- Vln II 39 b3-4 (`<c' ges'~>4 <es' ges'>4`, the c472 change): stems up, the tied G♭4 is the upper note, the tie curves down between the noteheads and points from C4 towards E♭4.
- Vla 80 b3-81 b1 (`<fes'~ aes'>4) | <fes' g'>4`): stems down, the tied F♭4 is the lower note, but the tie curves up from the top of the chord and lands on G4 of the adjacent-note chord at 81. It reads as A♭4 tied to G4. This is the F-F♭-E♭ line protected since c042/c227, so it matters that the player sees F♭ held.
- Vla 88-89 (`<fes aes~>2. | <es aes>2.~`): stems up, the tied A♭3 is the upper note, but the tie curves down under F♭3 and ends at E♭3 at 89. It reads as F♭3 tied to E♭3 and A♭3 re-bowed, the opposite of what c223 wrote (A♭3 held as the common tone, F♭3 moving to E♭3).

The other partial ties are already correct by chance (Vla 27, where stems are down and the tied E♭4 is on top, then the tied A♭3 is below; Vla 80 b2-3, where the tied A♭4 is on top with the stem down; harp 82-83, where the tied A♭1 is the lower note).

**Evidence.** The chapter's contents (PDF 59) give "Tie direction on single-stemmed chords" its own section (book p. 65): in a single-stemmed chord the outer ties curve away from the chord, so in a two-note chord the upper note ties upward and the lower note downward, whatever the stem direction. When only one note is tied, its tie keeps that direction. The pages in the excerpt make the same point about identification. Each head must be "immediately identified as part of the chord" (PDF 64). In double stems, parts a second apart are displaced because otherwise "it is hard to tell which note belongs to which part" (PDF 62). A tie that starts at one head and ends at a different pitch is the tie version of that ambiguity. The violas at 80-81 and 88-89 and Vln II at 39 are exactly the cases where the next chord has a different note next to the tied one, so a misplaced tie reads as a wrong tie.

**Fix (applied).** A direction on the tie inside the chord:

```
% bSec.ily line 188 (Vln II 38-39)
<des' bes'^~>2 <ges' bes'^~>2 | <des' bes'>2 <c' ges'^~>4 <es' ges'>4 |
% aTwo.ily line 62 (Vla 80)
... <f' aes'~>4 ... <fes'_~ aes'>4) | <fes' g'>4( ...
% coda.ily line 46 (Vla 88)
<f aes>2.\pp | <fes aes^~>2. | <es aes>2.~ ...
```

Checked at 300 dpi after the build: the B♭4 and G♭4 ties at 38-39 now arch over the chord, the F♭4 tie at 80-81 runs under the chord to F♭4, and the A♭3 tie at 88-89 runs over the chord to A♭3. Notation only: no pitch, rhythm or MIDI change, and the parts take the same source, so the Vln II and viola parts are corrected too. A source comment for the composer: "c475: tie direction on partial chord ties (Gould PDF 59, book p. 65; PDF 62, 64)".

### 2. Dots, chord stems and adjacent-note chords in bars changed since c415 (no change)

The bars changed since the last pass follow the reading. Vln II 39 (c472): both chords are stem-up, with the outer note furthest from the middle line deciding (PDF 60). Cellos 73-74 (c469): a single line, and the tied C4 crosses the barline with the tie on the side away from the stem, which is correct for a single note (PDF 59, "Tie direction for single notes", book p. 64). Violas 43 (c422): triplets, no chord. Cl 2 72 (c442): separate stems from Cl 1 (PDF 60). Dotted chords at 87-92 (Vla, Hn 3·4) have one dot per head, aligned, each in its own space (PDF 68). The part-only cues at 73-74, 76-78 are single lines in small notes and raise no chord or dot question.

### 3. Single-stemmed chords with unequal durations in the strings only (no change)

PDF 60 allows different durations on one stem only in string writing. The score never does this in the winds. Bassoons at 7-14 and 33-34, Cl 1·2 at 89-92 and Hn 3·4 at 87-90 use separate stems where the durations differ (c205). The strings do not use it either, which is fine. No change.
