## Findings

### 1. Bar 21 b3: the half cadence of the b phrase should be approached by a French sixth from IV by voice exchange (applied)

**Problem.** Bars 15–22 go from A♭ to F minor and close on V of A♭ at 22. At 20 b3 C7 (V7 of F minor) goes to D♭ at 21 (VI of F minor, IV of A♭): a deceptive cadence. At 21 b3 the bass falls D♭→B♭ for V7/V while the solo violin, the top voice, reaches D♮5. That gives the outer-voice cross relation D♭ (bass) → D♮ (soprano) that c132 recorded as an exception. c132 looked only for ways to put D♭→D♮ into one voice and found none.

**Evidence.** The reading offers the other remedy. A&S 29 §8 (PDF 508–509, Ex. 29-13, 29-14): the most frequent preparation of an augmented sixth is IV, and it often comes "from a chromaticized voice exchange": the bass rises from 4̂ to ♭6̂ while an upper voice moves to ♯4̂. Here IV has D♭ in the bass and F on top. If the bass goes D♭→F♭ while the violin goes F→(G, E♭)→D, the D♭/D♮ succession becomes that exchange, which A&S draw with crossing lines in Ex. 29-28a and 29-29a (PDF 517). 29 §11 (PDF 512–513): augmented sixths "frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI … because they form a convincing transition to V", which is exactly 20–21. 29 §6 and §12 (PDF 506–507, 513): the French form contains 2̂ as a common tone with V, so it goes to V without parallels. Here 2̂ is B♭, which Vln II already plays on 22 b1. 29 §1 (PDF 503): the augmented sixth marks the end of a phase of the form, and 22 ends the A section's middle phrase. The bass F♭→E♭ also brings the movement's F♭–E♭ thread (5–6, 62–65, 78, 88–89) into the first A section, the way A&S's footnote on PDF 503 says Mozart's augmented sixths embody the opening D♯–E figure.

**Fix (applied).** At 21 b3 the cellos and basses play F♭ instead of B♭, and Vln II plays B♭3 instead of F4, tied into its existing B♭3 at 22. The chord is F♭–A♭–B♭–D (French 4/3). It resolves bass F♭→E♭, solo violin D5→E♭5 (the augmented sixth opens to the octave), Vln II B♭ held, and the violas' A♭3 is taken over by the cellos' G3 in the same register at 22. The solo violin line, the viola answer, the Vln II louré rhythm and all dynamics are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 33: cor anglais B♮3 should be written C♭4 (applied, notation only)

**Problem.** At 32–34 the cor anglais descends C4–B♮3–B♭3–A♭3 and the bass clarinet E♭3–D3–D♭3–C3 over the A♭ pedal. Both are descending chromatic passing tones, not neighbours (c062 called them neighbours). The D♮ is right, but the B♮ is not.

**Evidence.** A&S 28 §19 (PDF 497, Ex. 28-23): passing tones are written as a minor 2nd to their goal, "lowered ones descending", so C–C♭–B♭. 28 §20 (PDF 497–498): in major only ♯4̂ and ♭7̂ are used in both directions, so the bass clarinet's D♮ is correct. Accidentals that suggest remote keys are avoided. B♮ points to C major/A minor (and this movement may not use C-major material), while C♭ is ♭3̂, the mixture tone of A♭. The vertical then reads A♭ | D–F–A♭–C♭, vii°7 of V over the tonic pedal, which is what the two falling lines do.

**Fix (applied).** The sound and MIDI are identical, and the common-tone chord kept since c002 sounds the same.
