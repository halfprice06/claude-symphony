## Findings

All findings are recorded with no change. None is a reason to edit the score.

### 1. Holistic comparison with Svendsen 2/ii: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? (recorded)

Yes, on the evidence available. Point by point:
- **Key relation and length.** Svendsen puts his slow movement in E♭, the subdominant of B♭. This movement is in A♭, ♭VI of C (recorded in mvt2.md); both are standard Romantic choices. Svendsen takes 6-7 minutes at ♩ = 66. This movement takes 345 s (MIDI) at ♩ = 52-60, so the proportions match.
- **Several melodies, combined at the climax.** This is the one structural feature the sources describe, and the score has it twice. In the build (53-56), theme A's head (Cl 1 + Hn 1, A♭3-B♭3 | D♭4. F4 B♭4) sounds under theme B in the unison violins. At the main climax (79-81), theme A's peak (Vln I B♭6, Vln II B♭5) sounds over the cello counter-melody that began at 71, doubled by Hn 1·2 a2 (D♭4-C4-B♭3). The difference is that Svendsen, by the booklet's account, combines his separate melodies at the climax itself, while here theme B does not come back at 79-81. Bringing it back would change the protected 79-81 wind and brass layout (c218, c287) and would add a third idea to a bar Rimsky's PDF 54 wants clear. I do not recommend it.
- **Restraint.** Svendsen is described as using "restrained dynamic effects". This movement has two ff points (61-62 and 79-81), each prepared over four bars and each cut back at once (63, 82). The trumpets are tacet and the trombones play only at 60-64. That is the same restraint. Svendsen's orchestra has no cor anglais, bass clarinet, contrabassoon, tuba or harp, so his colour comes from the standard pairs. This score uses its extra colours one at a time as solos (C.a. 41-52, B.cl. 31-36, harp 82), which Rimsky PDF 64-66 recommends for expressive melody.
- **What a conductor would notice.** The quotations (47-50, 63-64) and the common-tone link have no counterpart in Svendsen. They belong to the cyclic plan, not to his model. Neither would read as a student's gesture: each is one statement, prepared harmonically and scored for one solo colour.

### 3. Same-instrument octaves in the winds (recorded, follows PDF 67-68)

The scan finds bassoons 1·2 in octaves at 46, 59, 61-62 and 79-80, flutes 1·2 at 61 (A♭5/A♭6 held), and clarinets 1·2 at 77 b1 (G4/G5). PDF 67-68 discourages two instruments of one kind in octaves "unless stringed instruments ... double" them. In every bassoon case the cellos double the upper note and the bass clarinet or bass trombone doubles the lower one. The flute octave at 61 is a held chord in a ff tutti, not a melodic progression. The clarinet octave at 77 lasts one beat and is a held chord tone under Cl 1, which doubles both violin sections. No change.

### 4. Bass line without cellos at 23-28, 41-43, 48-52 (recorded)

At these bars the basses play the harmonic bass while the cellos carry the counter-melody (23-28) or a tenor line (41-43, 48-52). PDF 56 forbids giving the double basses a *phrase of importance* without cellos or bassoons. These are bass lines, not phrases. Rimsky's own Ex. 5 (PDF 179) has four solo basses pizzicato alone under cellos divided a 4, and Ex. 40 (PDF 218) has four solo basses holding a pedal under a bassoon solo. c249 tested a bassoon doubling at 23-28; it added a ninth page and a wind to the protected plain statement. No change.

### 5. Woodwind order at 61-62 and 79-82 (recorded)

The scan flags Cl 1 above Ob 2 at 61-62 and 79-82. In each case Cl 1 is at or below Ob 1 (unison F5 at 61, unison B♭5 at 79-81, c379), so the clarinets interlock with the oboes rather than invert the order PDF 65 describes. Ch. III (PDF 106-108) allows interlocking. No change.

### 6. The D♭ climax at 61 against Ex. 32 (recorded)

*Antar*'s Adagio in D♭ (Ex. 32, PDF 210-211) is the closest Rimsky example to bar 61: a ff melody in double octaves in the strings, with winds repeating the chord in pulsing pairs and the harp in arpeggios. Bar 61 puts theme B's head A♭-D♭ in three octaves (Fl 2 A♭5; Cl 2 and Vln II A♭4; Vc A♭3) under the held Vln I D♭6 and Fl 1 A♭6, with the violas in tremolo. The tremolo plays the same part as Rimsky's repeated wind chords: it gives motion inside a held ff. The harp rests, which keeps it fresh for 82. The layout follows PDF 62. The 61-62 brass layout is protected. No change.
