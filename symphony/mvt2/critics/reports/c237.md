## Findings

### 1. Bar 21: make the IV → French-sixth approach a real contrary-motion exchange (applied in scratch)

**Bars:** 21. Vln II, Vc, Cb.

**Problem.** In v237, bar 21 b1–2 is IV (D♭ in the bass, F in Vln I) and b3 is the French sixth (F♭ in the bass, D in Vln I), which goes to V at 22. The melody moves F5–(G5)–E♭5–D5 by step. The bass jumps D♭2 → F♭2 on b3, and the cellos jump D♭3 → F♭3/A♭3 with it. Vln II holds F4 and Vla holds A♭3. Only the melody moves, and the bass answers it with a leap.

**Evidence.** A&S present this exact pair of sonorities as a voice exchange:
- The bass and the soprano trade the 10th of IV (D♭–F) for the augmented 6th (F♭–D) in contrary motion (PDF 565, Ex. 31-20b: "starting on the 10th permits an elaboration of the IV/German 6/5 voice exchange in major").
- A chromatic step may be left out (PDF 565).
- At the point where the exchange produces the octave, a passing 6/4 usually appears (boxed note, PDF 566).
- In Ex. 31-17 (PDF 563), Beethoven expands IV this way: IV6 – German 6/5 – passing 6/4 – IV. The authors say the German-to-6/4 succession must be read as passing inside IV, not as a cadence (PDF 564).

In this bar the melody's E♭5 on b2 is already the octave point. The bass only has to step there.

**Fix (scratch, aOne.ily):**
```
% Vln II bar 21 (was f'2--( bes4--~))
f'4--( c'4-- bes4--~) |
% Vc bar 21 (was \once \stemUp des2 <fes aes>4^\markup \italic "div.")
\once \stemUp des4 es4 <fes aes>4^\markup \italic "div." |
% Cb bar 21 (was \once \stemUp des,2\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." fes,4)
\once \stemUp des,4\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." es,4 fes,4 |
```

**Result.**
- The bass now rises D♭–E♭–F♭ against the melody's F–E♭–D, giving M10 → P8 → A6.
- 21 b2 is a passing I6/4: E♭ in Cb, Vc and Vln I, with A♭3 in Vla and C4 in Vln II. The bass is doubled, as a 6/4 needs.
- Vln II's F4–C4–B♭3 gives the inner part its first motion in the bar. It moves in parallel 10ths with the melody on b2–3 (C4/E♭5 → B♭3/D5).

**What is kept.** The French sixth stays on b3 with the same notes (c189), and the cello divisi stays at b3 (c192). The melody and the 17→21 sequence are untouched (c181), and so is the dialogue of 15–22 (c177).

**Checks.** I found no parallels by hand, and analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. The build is clean.

### 2. Contrary chromatic inner motion at 46–47, 59–60 and 77–79 already follows the reading (no change)

These three junctures already work by contrary chromatic motion, as described on PDF 563–565:
- **46 → 47:** Vla B♭3 → B3 rises while Vln II G♭4 → E♯4 falls, into C♯7.
- **59 → 60:** the counter-line (Vc, Hn 1, Bsn 1) falls F♭ → E♭ while the bass rises G♮ → A♭. This is ♮4–♯4 with ♭6–5 (PDF 561–562, Ex. 31-15).
- **77–79:** the cello/Bsn 1 counter-melody rises C♭4–C4–D♭4 while the bass falls F♭–E♭–D♭.

Each one sits at a structural joint: the pivot into F♯ minor, the dominant before the climax, and the deceptive cadence. I recommend no change.

### 3. Bars 51–52: an eight-beat dominant with only the cor anglais moving (recorded; no change recommended)

**Problem.** At 51–52 the inner parts stand still:
- Vc holds E♭3, Vla holds C4 and Vln II holds E♭5 for eight beats, over the A♭ bass and the timpani roll.
- Only the C.a. moves (A♭4–G♭4 | F4–G♭4).

By Exercise 4 (PDF 583) the texture lacks a complementary middle voice. A&S's normal way to expand V7 is the m6–M10 exchange between V6/5 and V7, with the inner voices held (PDF 564–565, Ex. 31-19b; review point 6, PDF 580).

**Why I do not apply it.**
- The exchange needs C in the bass at 51 (V6/5 of D♭). That changes the inversion of the pivot chord at 50–51, which is protected (c457).
- I also tested an inner line over the pedal on paper. A falling Vc E♭3–D3–D♭3–C3 would state the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C a seventh time, and c211 protects the six statements.
- A rising Vc E♭3–E♮3–F3 makes a cross-relation with Vln II's held E♭5.

The pedal with its crescendo is a legitimate standing-on-the-dominant. I record the point so that a later cycle does not repeat this test.

### 4. 64 b4 → 65 and 88 → 89: parallel chromatic slides (no change)

- **64 → 65:** E–G♯–B–D slides to E♭–G–B♭–D♭ in all four string voices. Because of c232's B♭ anticipation, it has no open fifths. This is the single-step form of the augmented-sixth slide (PDF 557–558).
- **88 → 89:** F♭4/D♭5 → E♭4/C5 are chromatic parallel 6ths, the sonority A&S prefer for chromatic parallel motion (PDF 552).

Both bars are protected (62–65 thread; plagal close 87–92), and both follow the reading.

### 5. Devices from the unit that the movement does not use (no change)

The movement uses none of these:
- chromatic 7–6 chains (PDF 552–553);
- descending chromatic 5–6 (PDF 556);
- augmented-triad chains (PDF 562);
- m7–M10 exchanges (PDF 566);
- extended exchange chains (PDF 567–571).

A&S call these transitional or sequential devices (PDF 571–572: equal divisions are "one relatively minor aspect"). The movement's transitions (31–36, 45–47, 62–65) are pedals and pivots, and they are protected. Adding a chain would mean a new passage, not a texture fix, so I do not propose one.
