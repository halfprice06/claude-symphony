### 4. Recorded, no change: both builds follow the staged crescendo of Figs. 5.7–5.8

Fig. 5.7 marks pp – p – mf – ff on a held chord, and Fig. 5.8 draws a curve that rises slowly at first and steeply in the last third (PDF 216–217). The build at 53–61 has the same shape. It moves from p at 53 to mp at 54 and mf at 56 in the violins, where the line rises slowly. Woodwinds join p/mp at 57, and the f at 59 follows hairpins in every part. The steep last third is 60: Tbn 2, bass trombone, tuba and timpani enter pp< under "poco allargando" and arrive ff at 61. The second build (75–79) is shorter: mp at 75, f at 78, the timpani roll pp< from 78 b3 into ff at 79. Neither peak ends in a cut, which PDF 231–232 and 242 say an acoustic orchestra cannot do. 61–62 hand over to the sf> tremolo and the trombone at 63. 79–81 thin out to p at 82, and the timpani roll fades to niente at 81. Nothing to change.

### 5. Recorded, no change: reserved sounds and how II sits between I and III

Casanelles's *Annihilation*, *Gravity* and *Man of Steel* examples (PDF 227, 238, 243, 245, 247–248) say that a sound kept for one place carries the most weight there. II keeps to this at symphony level:
- The trumpets are tacet, and the finale restores the trumpet-and-timpani pairing at its 199–204.
- The trombones play only at 60–64, with no chorale.
- String tremolo is used only at 61–64.
- Muted brass appear only at 87–90.
- There is no percussion besides timpani, and no C-major material after bar 1.

The horn carries theme A's first statement (7–14), which fits PDF 227's point that horns are the warm, inward voice. The theme's last appearance is its most intimate: the head in harp harmonics at 82 b3–83, answered by the timpani's E♭3–C3–A♭2 at 84–85 (PDF 227–228, the upright-piano example). That timpani figure is also the source of the scherzo's motive x, a major third lower (scherzo.md line 10). The bar-92 muta (A♭→E, C→G, D♭→B, E♭→E) matches the scherzo's bar-1 label (scherzo.md line 11), and Vln II's A♭4 under the fermata is the scherzo Hn 1's G♯4. The minor iv at 5 and 88 sounds nostalgic rather than tragic because of its colour: soft clarinets and horns at 5; muted horns, cor anglais and divided violas at 88. This is PDF 239's point that the same chord can carry a changed meaning. The last chord stays a plain triad for the finale's German sixth. Nothing to change.
