### 1. Each section is scored for its role, as PDF 116 asks (no change)

PDF 116 says the scoring of a passage depends on "whether a given passage is a complement to or a contrast with what goes before and comes after, whether it forms a climax or merely a step". Checked section by section:
- 1-6 (link) and 31-36 (transition) are steps. Each keeps one colour group with no strings: horns and woodwind at 3-6, a woodwind choir at 31-36. They contrast with the string statements on either side.
- 7-14 and 23-30 complement each other. They are the same theme, first string-free in the horn, then in the violins with a cello counter-melody.
- 37-52 contrasts with A in metre (4/4), key, tempo and colour (Fl 1, C.a., viola triplets, pizz. bass).
- 53-60 is a step toward the climax, and 61-62 is the climax. Only here do the low brass play together with the full woodwind.
- 63-64 contrasts again: one trombone over a string tremolo, the reading's fanfare-over-tremolo case (PDF 115).
- 65-70 is a step. It uses the horn solo and timpani over the E♭ pedal, the colours of the opening.
- 79-81 is the second climax and is scored differently from 61-62: horns only, with no trombones or tuba.
- The coda thins step by step to strings and Cl 2.

No two neighbouring sections share a scoring. The two climaxes are distinguished by both register and brass weight.

Fix: none.

### 2. Every recurrence of a theme uses one of the five re-scoring operations of PDF 118 (no change)

PDF 118 lists five operations: (a) transfer to another octave, (b) another key, (c) extension by added octaves, (d) altered details, (e) a new dynamic plan. Theme A: 7 (Hn 1, low, p) → 23 (violins, an octave up: a) → 71 (violins with the viola figure and the cello 9-8 at 73-74: d) → 79 (third octave added at the peak, ff: c, e) → 83 (head only, harp harmonics, p: a, e). Theme B: 37 (Fl 1) → 41 (C.a., an octave down: a) → 45 (head in B♭ minor: b) → 53 (violins, p: d) → 57 (an octave up with the woodwind: c) → 61 (ff tutti: e). The chapter adds (PDF 117) that "one idea will be orchestrated in a different way every time it recurs". Here no statement repeats an earlier scoring. This also matches the corpus models, where Dvořák 9/ii's return gives the Largo theme to muted strings, not to the cor anglais again.

Fix: none.

### 3. Climax placement and the two builds follow the crescendo rules (no change)

PDF 130 says long crescendi add strings, then woodwind, then brass. PDF 127 says the second group should enter p or pp and crescendo faster. PDF 131 describes a diverging progression: upper parts rise, the bass falls, and new parts fill the widening space.

At 53-61 the strings start p. Hn 1 and Cl 1 enter pp at 53 b4 and Bsn 1 pp< at 54, the full woodwind at 57, and Tbn 2, B.tbn, tuba and timpani pp< at 60. The top rises from the violins' D♭6 to A♭6 at 61, and the bass falls to D♭1. The basses re-entering at 56 (A1-B♭1, c527, the one musical change since c518 covered this chapter) is part of this descent and enters p<, as the amplification rule asks.

At 71-79 the order is strings, then Cl 2, then Bsn and Hn 2 (75), Fl 1 (77), then Hn 1 and the timpani (78 b3), with the top rising to B♭6 and the bass falling to B♭0.

The main climax falls at 0.65 of the length, and the shorter, higher second peak at 0.84. That is the shape c233, c343 and c433 recorded for Dvořák 9/ii and the other corpus Adagios.

Fix: none.

### 4. The releases after both climaxes remove groups, as PDF 127 and 130 require (no change)

Elimination "belongs essentially to the diminuendo" (PDF 127), in the order brass, woodwind, strings (PDF 130).
- 81-83: Hn 3·4 and the timpani leave in 81, Hn 1·2 at 82 b2, then the woodwind and violins at the end of 82. The low strings carry the A♭ into the coda.
- 87-92: Hn 3·4 and C.a. leave at 90. Cl 2 continues "morendo" to the end. PDF 130 names the clarinet as the instrument for a morendo, and here it is the last wind.
- 63-66: the woodwind goes before the brass because the trombone carries the quotation. c518 recorded this as the one exception, and the reading's own note on contrast (PDF 127) covers it.

At 82 the whole orchestra makes a one-bar diminuendo together before the groups drop out. c218 and c518 recorded this. Under my lens it is right: the coda has to start ppp at 83 after a ff peak only two bars earlier, and the A′ is too short (12 bars) to stretch the release further without moving the coda.

Fix: none.

### 5. Brass re-entries and PDF 136 (no change; the exceptions are already recorded)

PDF 136 says brass re-entries after a long rest should be pp or ff, and that mp or mf is "colourless". Four entries do not follow this. All four are recorded, and each has a stated reason:
- Hn 3·4 f at 61 and 79: one degree under the ff tutti, as the horns' share of the chord (c044, c218).
- Hn 2 p at 75: one level under the cellos it doubles (c217).
- Hn 1 mf< at 78 b3: it joins Hn 2, who is already playing, so the brass colour is not returning after a silence (c088, c218).

The re-entries that mark form points do follow the rule: low brass pp at 60, Tbn 1 ff at 63, Hn 3·4 pp at 87.

Fix: none.

### 6. Proportions of A′ and coda (no change)

A′ lasts 43 s and the coda 39 s, so the reprise is only a little longer than the coda. Taken alone, that would look short. But the coda is mostly held pedal, with the harp/timpani exchange and the last motto tail. The reprise carries the movement's second peak. PDF 136 warns that the ear "cannot stand, for long, the full resources of the orchestra", and this A′ has a full partial tutti in 3 of its 12 bars. A longer reprise would mean either a second tutti or a longer quiet stretch before the coda's own quiet stretch. Both would weaken the contrast the reading asks for in Finding 1. c323, c433 and c523 reached the same verdict on other grounds.

Fix: none.
