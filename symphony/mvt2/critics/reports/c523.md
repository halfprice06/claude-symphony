# Critic 523: form, pacing and proportion (lens 3)

Version reviewed: v523. Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 32 "Chromaticism in Larger Contexts", PDF 584–606 (fourth series). Result: no score change. Earlier Unit 32 cycles (c010, c023, c110, c113, c250, c263, c390, c393, c403, c410) have already recorded the key route, the joins and the share of remote keys. This report adds one new point, on how the movement handles the pitch A♮, the pitch that belongs to both remote areas. A♮ is introduced in stages (40 → 46 → 47–50 → 62–64), and it is never heard again after bar 64. This is the reading's way of fitting a double-mixture area into a piece (PDF 596–598, 593, 601–602). The report also checks the proportions in seconds against Dvořák 9/ii.

## Findings

All findings are "no change". They are ranked by how much it would matter if they were wrong. Findings 1 and 2 are new and should be recorded, because they limit what later revisions can do.

### 1. A♮, the pitch of both remote areas, is brought in stages (40, 46, 47–50, 62–64). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 596 says double-mixture areas are "among the most difficult triads to expand convincingly into key areas", and that "one way of dealing with this difficulty might be to approach the altered chord in stages". PDF 597–598 (Ex. 32-17) shows Haydn preparing his remote area's main top-voice pitch earlier in the piece. PDF 585 and 601 describe Op. 106's recurring F♮–F♯, a semitone clash that runs through the whole movement and decides where its key changes fall.

**Score.** A minor at 63–64 is B𝄫 minor, the ♭ii region of A♭, and that makes it double mixture. Its tonic pitch is A♮. My search of the source finds no A♮ anywhere in bars 1–39. After that the pitch gains weight in four steps:
- 40: a chromatic passing note in the bass. Double basses (pizz.) and harp L.H. play A1–B♭1 (bSecCb, bSecHarpL), the applied leading note of vi.
- 46: a melodic neighbour note. Ob 1 plays B♭4–A4–B♭4 (c181).
- 47–50: a chord tone of the first remote area. A is the third of F♯ minor. The cor anglais quotation holds A4 at 48 and closes B4–A4 at 50, and Vln II has the string top voice A5 at 49 and 50 b2 (analysis.txt profile, 49–52: top A5).
- 62–64: a tonic. Fl 1 and Vln I go A♭6 → A6 sf at 63 (c113's long-range line), and trombone 1's quotation turns on A3 with the neighbour G♯3–A3 (64).

Both quotations therefore contain the motion G♯–A or A–G♯, and G♯ is the movement's tonic note A♭. The cor anglais has A4–G♯4 at 48, and trombone 1 has G♯3–A3 at 64. The one abrupt inflection in the movement, A♭6 → A6 at 62–63, is this same semitone moved to the top of the climax. So the A-minor area is not a surprise from nowhere. Its main pitch is a passing note at 40, a neighbour at 46, and the top voice of the F♯-minor area at 49–50, an octave below the A6 of 63. This is the reading's staged approach, and Haydn's top-voice preparation, applied to the second quotation. No earlier report links the two quotations through A♮.

**Fix.** None. Keep the bass A1–B♭1 at 40, the Ob 1 A4 at 46, Vln II A5 at 49–50 and the cor anglais A4 at 48–50. If 37–44 is ever reharmonised, the A1 at 40 is the first A♮ in the movement and should stay.

### 2. After 64, A♮ never sounds again; the reprise turns ♭2̂ back into diatonic 2̂ (65–92). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 592: a remote area "will create tonal conflicts that the composer must somehow work out". PDF 593 (Ex. 32-12): the Waldstein exposition "resolves" its chromatic key area into its diatonic counterpart. PDF 602 (K. 542): B–B♭ only makes sense through the movement's motive.

**Score.** A search of retr.ily, aTwo.ily and coda.ily finds no A♮ in 65–92. The bass E→E♭ at 64–65 (Ex. 32-10a) brings back E♭7, and after that the second scale degree is always B♭. ii7 comes before V7 at 76 and 80, the A′ peak is B♭6 at 79 (one step above the A6 of 63), and the coda closes on A♭ with D♭–C on top. The scherzo's first note is G♯4 (Hn 1), which is A♭ respelled, not A♮. The second half of the movement therefore takes the climax's ♭2̂ back to 2̂ and the climax's A back to A♭. This is how the conflict the reading describes gets worked out. The F♭/E thread is handled differently: it stays in the reprise and coda (78, 80–81, 88) as ordinary ♭6̂ mixture, which c263 and c393 recorded. So of the two remote pitches, ♭6̂ is kept as colour and ♭2̂ is removed. That suits their different weight. ♭6̂ is plain mixture, while ♭2̂ with a minor third is double mixture (PDF 596).

**Fix.** None. Record the rule: no A♮ (B𝄫) anywhere in 65–92. A chromatic lower neighbour to B♭6 at 79–80, an A-based applied chord in the coda, or a sharpened viola passing note in 71–78 would bring the climax's conflict back after it has been settled.

### 3. Proportions in seconds; the remote climax is followed by enough diatonic music (whole movement). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 592 and 603 (Points 5): remote areas threaten unity unless the rest of the piece absorbs them. How a composer deals with this "depends largely on ... length, rhythm, tempo, form".

**Score.** From the `\mt` map in global.ily: 1–36 take 125 s (36%), the B section 37–64 takes 116 s (34%), the retransition and A′ 65–82 take 64 s (19%), and the coda takes about 40 s (11%), for about 345 s in total (as c413 found). Inside the B section, 48–52 at ♩=54 take 22 s, the build 53–60 takes 35 s, and 61–64 take 19 s. The climax begins at 223 s (65%), the A-minor area ends at 242 s (70%), and the A′ peak at 79 falls at 290 s (84%). In the analysis.txt density profile the two peaks have about the same average voice count (61–64: 15.8; 77–80: 15.9). They differ in register (A6 against B♭6) and in brass (trombones at 61–64; no trumpets or trombones at 79, by the recorded choice of c024).

**Corpus.** In Dvořák 9/ii the middle section in C♯ minor starts at bar 46 (key change in Dvorak_Sym9_mvt2.ly), the ff with the movement-I quotations is at 96–100, and the D♭ reprise is at 101 out of about 127 bars. That puts the climax at about 76% of the bars, with a quiet reprise after it. Dvořák's middle key is the parallel minor written enharmonically (PDF 588, notational), and it ends in C♯ major before D♭ returns. This movement puts its remote climax earlier (65%) and gives the reprise its own diatonic peak. In Unit 32 terms that choice is the stronger one here, because the A-minor area is double mixture and needs more diatonic music after it than Dvořák's simple mixture does. 122 s of A♭ follow it (35% of the movement), against Dvořák's roughly 21% of bars. Bar 61 is protected as the climax (c013, c513), and the A′ dynamic plan is protected (c008).

**Fix.** None.

### 4. The joins keep the reading's balance of gentle and abrupt inflection (1–2, 46–47, 62–63, 64–65, 92). No change; confirms c403 Finding 3 on v523.

**Reading.** PDF 585, as in Reading notes 2 and 3.

**Score.** Nothing has changed since v403. The falling inflections at 1–2 (Vla E♮4→E♭4, ppp→pp) and 64–65 (bass E→E♭, p→pp, Tempo I) are gentle. The way into F♯ minor at 46–47 keeps D♭5 = C♯5 on top in Ob 1, a notational common tone (PDF 587–588). The one rising, abrupt inflection is A♭6→A6 at 62–63, and Finding 1 now gives the motivic reason the reading asks for. The exit at 92 is the Widmung common tone A♭ = G♯.

**Fix.** None.
