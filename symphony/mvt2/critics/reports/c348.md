## Findings

### 1. Bars 65–71: the basses' "sul A" is never cancelled (applied)

**Problem.** c344 marked the four solo basses' E♭2 pedal at 65–70 "4 soli, sul A,". At 71 the section returns with "tutti", and the part never says "ord.". Every other string name in the movement is ended by "ord." or by the next string name: Vln I sul D 22→27; Vln II sul D 22→27, sul tasto 37→45, sul A 52→57, sul G 64→sul D 70→ord. 75; Vla sul C 64→ord. 71; Vc sul D 44→ord. 48. The basses are the only exception. "Tutti" cancels "4 soli", not the string. The A♭1 at 71 cannot be played on the A string, so the first note forces the change. But 72–78 (F2, B♭1, E♭2, D♭2, E♭2, F♭2) all lie where a player still following "sul A" would shift up the A string or cross back and forth. That is not the colour of a plain bass line under the reprise.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 64: the string "must be carefully marked", because pitches are duplicated on other strings. An instruction to change strings needs a matching instruction to go back. The score already does this for the violas at 71 and Vln II at 75, in the same system. Adler PDF 70: give the instruction in words in both score and parts.

**Fix (sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoCb, applied in /tmp/critic-348/work).**
```lilypond
\set Staff.midiExpression = #1.0 aes,,2.\pp^\markup \italic "tutti, ord." | f,4 bes,,4 es,4 | ...
```
This is text only, with a c348 comment line above it. score.midi is byte-identical to v348's, and the layout is unchanged (8 pages). The text sits clear above the bass staff at 71.

### Recorded (no change)

2. **Pizzicato note lengths (37–44, 63).** The basses' pizz. half notes at 40 and the sf A1 at 63 have no "l.v." (PDF 58). At 40 the harp's octaves carry the bass. At 63 the bass trombone's held A2 sf and the cellos' tremolo sustain the root, so the pluck is meant as an attack, as in Brahms Ex. II-55. Keep (c208 item 3).
3. **No string harmonics.** I checked Adler's partial tables (PDF 63–66) against the held notes of the ending. The violas' C string gives C5 as its 4th partial, so a viola harmonic could double Vln I's last C5. I do not propose it. It would add a new timbre in the morendo (c050, c120 rule), change the protected close and its top line, and duplicate the silvery colour the harp harmonics already give at 83 (PDF 66, Ex. II-77).
4. **Muted strings at f, 28, and the mute plan.** Adler PDF 59 names the loud muted sound as a colour of its own, and the unmuted basses at mf balance it. Mute times all pass PDF 60. The plan is protected (c208).
5. **Sul tasto 37 and no sul ponticello.** Sul tasto under the flute is the "hazy" background Adler describes (PDF 51). Ponticello tremolo at 61–64 would be the "eerie, glassy" sound (PDF 52), the wrong colour for a Largamente climax. Keep.
6. **Pizz./arco dynamics.** Every change has its own dynamic (37 p, 41 pp, 45 pp, 63 sf, 64 p), and the viola pizz. at 41–44 has hairpins as in Ex. II-61. It follows the reading and needs nothing.

I found nothing else in this range that the score breaks. After c208 and the cycles since, the string colours in this score follow Adler's chapter. No change affects the ending, the exported themes or the quotations, so the other movements are not affected.
