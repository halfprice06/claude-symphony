## Findings

The brass writing follows the reading on every point that applies to an Adagio. I found nothing that needs changing; the points below are recorded so the composer can see each principle checked. Ranked by importance.

**1. Holding the brass back (bars 1-59, 60-64; Adler PDF 14, 17, 37). Follows; no change.**
Trumpets are tacet for the whole movement. Trombones and tuba are silent until 60, enter pp<, sustain the D♭ climax chord at 61-62, and Tbn 1's first and only notes are the quotation at 63-64. This is Adler's Schubert principle exactly: a colour used only to sustain is then given "an entirely new sound color... adding clarity to the moment and to the entire form" (PDF 37). It also follows PDF 17: brass used throughout "robs the climactic passages of their full power". Keep the trumpets out; adding them anywhere in this movement would undo the strongest brass point in the score.

**2. Horn doubling at 61-62 and 79-81 (Adler PDF 15, 44). Partly follows; not reopened.**
Adler's rule: above mf, horns in unison pairs, two horns equal one trombone. At 61 b1 the brass chord is Hn 1 F5 ff, Hn 3·4 a2 A♭4 f, Hn 2 D♭4 ff, Tbn 2 F3 f, B.tbn D♭3 f, tuba D♭2 f. Hn 1 and Hn 2 are single above mf. The score answers the rule with dynamics instead of doubling: the single horns are marked one level louder (ff) than the doubled pair and the trombones (f). Adler's own ff chord in Ex. XI-1 (PDF 16, transposed score) also gives the four horns four separate notes, with two trumpets on top, so the rule is advice, not law. c194 applied the rule (Hn 1·2 a2); c264 reversed it to close the gap between A♭4 and F3. That reason still holds: with Hn 1·2 a2 on F5, the chord has an octave and a sixth gap A♭4-F3 in the middle of the brass. Hn 1's F5, G♭5, A♭5 are also doubled by Ob 1 and Cl 1 (F5 at 61, F5/A♭5 at 62), so the top line is not a lone horn. At 79-81, Hn 3 F4 and Hn 4 A♭3 are single at f but they are inner notes, doubled by Cl 2 and violas, under a ff tutti whose top is strings and woodwinds; this is the Franck model (finding 4), where horns are filler "in their woodwind capacity". No change.

**3. The climax and the quotation, 61-64 (Adler PDF 38-41, Ex. XI-21; Dvořák 9/ii). Follows; no change.**
Tchaikovsky thins the texture to cellos and basses, then brings a tutti with a new brass theme in long notes. This score reverses the order: a full tutti at 61-62, then the tutti breaks off at 63 to sf> string tremolo (dying to p within the bar) and pizzicato basses, and Tbn 1 states movement I's first-theme head ff marcato over the bass trombone's A2. The result is the same thing Adler praises: the brass theme enters on a cleared texture and is heard as new material. Dvořák 9/ii gives the corresponding quotation to all three trombones; Tchaikovsky gives his to trombones and tuba in octaves. One trombone ff in its best register (E3-C4, PDF 42), over a tremolo that falls to p, carries without help. Doubling it (Tbn 2 in unison, `bSecTbnTwo` bars 63-64) would match Dvořák but lose the solo colour that c029 and c128 protect. Not recommended.

**4. The A′ climax at 79 without trombones (Adler PDF 38, Ex. XI-20 Franck). Follows; no change.**
At 79 the ff peak (B♭6 in Vln I) is carried by strings and woodwinds, with the four horns at f on the counter-melody (Hn 1·2 a2 with Bsn 1 and cellos) and the inner harmony. This is Franck's method in Ex. XI-20: the climax comes "not by the brass but by the rest of the orchestra plus horns in their woodwind capacity". Because the movement's one brass climax is at 61-64, the second climax gets a different colour, which is what the reading recommends. The do-not-touch list already has "no Tpt/Tbn at 79".

**5. Low brass spacing at 61-62 (Adler PDF 15-16, Ex. XI-1). Follows; no change.**
Tuba an octave under the bass trombone (D♭2/D♭3; G♭1/G♭2; F♭1/F♭2), Tbn 2 a sixth or tenth above, then the horns in close order: this matches the bottom of most chords in Ex. XI-1. The tuba's G♭1 and F♭1 at f are low, but they double the contrabassoon and basses on the bass line only, and the chord above them is in open root position, so the low octave does not blur the harmony. The ff third doubled at 61 (Hn 1 F5, Tbn 2 F3) was recorded by c194 and is a normal Ex. XI-1 doubling.

**6. Soft and muted horn chords (3-6, 29-30, 87-90; Adler PDF 15-16, 50-51). Follows; no change.**
At mf and below the score gives each horn its own pitch, as Adler allows. The low pp horn chords at 3-6 and the muted Hn 3 A♭3 over Hn 4 F3-F♭3-E♭3 at 87-90 are Adler's "very dark" chord type (Ex. XI-1, PDF 16). The mute instructions at 82, 87 and 91 are as complete as PDF 50 asks. Gershwin's sf>p muted chord (Ex. XI-27) introduces a solo; at 87 the soft, plain entry is right for a coda and should stay.

**7. Brass as independent and contrapuntal voices (53-60, 61-62, 63-64; Adler PDF 41-44). Follows; no change.**
Hn 1's counter-line from theme A's head at 53-60 and Tbn 2's moving line inside the chord at 61-62 are Adler's use 6, written in the instruments' best registers (PDF 42). The B.tbn's A2-D2-E2 at 64 hands the bass to the retransition's basses and timpani without a gap. Brass punctuation (Hindemith) and jazz effects (PDF 42-44, 50-52) do not apply to this movement.
