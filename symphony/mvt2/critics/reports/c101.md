## Findings

### 1. Bars 41-44: the first statement of theme B's second phrase is covered by the flute above it (applied)

**Problem.** At 41-44 the cor anglais plays theme B's second phrase (A♭3-D♭4-F4 rising, peak B♭4 at 42). This is its first hearing. Since c090, flute 1 moves above it in tenths and sixths, A♭5-B♭5, at the same dynamic (both p< to mp at 42, > p at 44). The flute enters at 41 b3, after the rising fourth, so its line does not contain the theme's head interval. The top voice of a pair in parallel tenths and sixths is the one the ear follows. A listener therefore hears A♭5-B♭5-F5-G♭5 as the tune and the actual theme as an inner voice. The viola pizzicato triplets at 41-43 also cross the cor anglais's register (A♭3-G♭4) at mp.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 54: melody "in the middle of the orchestral range ... is not so prominent", and the means of relief are named: "strongly accentuated dynamic shades", choice of timbre, doubling. The same page says they apply "also to two part melody (in thirds and sixths)". The cor anglais at A♭3-B♭4 is in that middle range. Timbre contrast is already present (cor anglais against flute), and doubling was withdrawn by c090. That leaves dynamic shading as the means, and it is not used: both parts are mp. In Rimsky's Ex. 17 (PDF 191-194), the cor anglais is part of the melody group and the accompanying wind figures are a level below. In Ex. 1 (PDF 173), the clarinets and bassoons under the violin tune are pp to the melody's p.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-101/work, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecFluteOne` bar 41-44).** The flute keeps c090's pitches (on the do-not-touch list) and plays one level under the cor anglais:

```
% was: r2 aes''4.(\< bes''8 | f''4\mp ges''8 f''8 es''4~ es''8\> f''8 | ges''4. aes''8 f''4 es''4 | es''2.)\p r4 |
r2 aes''4.(\pp\< bes''8 | f''4\p ges''8 f''8 es''4~ es''8\> f''8 | ges''4. aes''8 f''4 es''4 | es''2.)\pp r4 |
```

The cor anglais stays p< mp > p. Flute 2 rests in these bars, so the shared staff has no dynamic conflict. Optional, not applied: the violas' pizzicato could stay p at 42 rather than mp. c038 accepted the accompaniment swell 41-44, so I leave that to the composer.

### 2. Open item "Vln I single octave 70-74": close it, no change

**Problem as raised (c031, c067).** In 71-74 the upper octave of theme A is carried by Vln I alone, and the lower by Vln II, with no wind doubling. The concern was that the upper octave has too few players.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 58-59 calls Vln I and Vln II in octaves "a very common process used for all kinds of melodic figures, in particular those in the very high register". The process he warns against on PDF 59 is different: each section divided in octaves, which halves the players. Ex. 25 (Ivan the Terrible, Act III, PDF 201) is the closest model in the book. It is in A♭ major, "dolce e cantabile", with Vln I and Vln II in octaves and no wind on the tune, over harp arpeggios, a divided viola figure and a sustained bass. That matches 71-74 almost layer by layer: violins in octaves, the viola eighth-note figure, harp chords, and the bass. Wind doubling is added only for the crescendo from 77, which is what Rimsky's examples do when the dynamic rises (Snegourotchka [9]/[65], PDF 61: "twice pianissimo, not doubled, then twice ... doubled in the wood-wind"). The score already follows that order.

**Recommendation.** Close the item. Do not add winds at 71-74.

### 3. Theme B's climax at 61 against Rimsky's Ex. 32: no change

**Observation.** Rimsky's Ex. 32 (Antar, Adagio, D♭ major, PDF 210-211) places the climax tune in double octaves (Vln I / Vln II / Vla+Vc, mf, cresc.) over harp arpeggios. At 61 the tune is not stated in full. Vln I holds D♭6, and the augmented head of theme B's second phrase (A♭-D♭-F-F♭) sounds in Vln II and Vc in octaves, with the winds. This is Rimsky's PDF 60-61 violins-and-cellos octave, used for a thematic line. It is consistent with the reading, and 61-62 and the build 53-61 are on the do-not-touch list. I record the comparison so later critics do not reopen it on this ground.

### 4. Bar 18: divided cellos touch an octave (C3/C4): no change

Rimsky PDF 62, note II, warns against *progression* in octaves within one divided section, because the parts are on strings that do not correspond. At 18 the upper cellos start on C4 over the lower cellos' C3 for one beat, then move away (E♭4, C4, D♭4 over C3-B♭2). There is no parallel octave motion, so the rule is not broken. I note it so the bar is recorded as checked against this rule.
