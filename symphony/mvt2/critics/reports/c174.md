## Findings

None of the findings below calls for a change. Two passes of this chapter's principles over the score found no violation that a fix could remove without touching a protected passage or reversing a recent, reasoned decision. I list them so that later critics do not raise them as new.

### 1. Bars 65–70 and 82–86 follow the layout of Mahler's Fig. 6.1 (no change)

**What the reading shows.** In Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272, viewed) the woodwinds hold a pp chord in bars 17–21 and stop. The distant trumpets enter at 22 over a static string pedal spread across many octaves. Casanelles (PDF 271–273) says the soft, filtered, distant sound works because of where it is placed and how it is filtered, not only how loudly it is marked.

**The score.** The retransition uses the same order:
- 65–66: Vln II, violas and cellos hold pp and fade to niente.
- 67: horn 1 enters p, alone in the middle register, over the 4 solo basses' E♭2 and a ppp timpani roll near the rim.
- 68: bassoon 1 enters pp below the horn.

The coda does the same. The tutti releases on 82 b3 (c063, c116), and the harp's harmonics then sound over the ppp A♭ pedal alone. The violas wait until 84 b3 (c124), and the clarinet's tail grows out of the pedal niente at 85.

**Fix.** None. Keep both passages as they are.

### 2. Theme A's last appearances move further away, as in *The Social Network* (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 283–284: the same theme recorded further away at each return, until it sounds like a memory. PDF 286–287: cutting the highs gives a nostalgic, distant sound. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44 and 128 say the same for muted brass.

**Score.** Theme A's colours move from near to far:
- 7: open horn.
- 23: muted violins.
- 71–81: open strings and winds, up to ff.
- 83: harp harmonics.
- 85–89: the clarinet's niente tail.
- 87–90: the muted horns echo horn 3's open F4–F♭4–E♭4 of 79–81 an octave lower (Hn 4 F3–F♭3–E♭3, with the violas sul C).

This is Rimsky's echo rule (PDF 128) carried out in the instrument that played the original.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Frequency allotment: no brass under an undoubled woodwind line (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 287: "the composer might decide not to use brass in a passage where the flute plays a line to prevent the brass overtones from masking the flute sound."

**Score.** The woodwind solos have no brass under them: oboe 15–22 and 45–47, flute 37–44, cor anglais 41–44 and 48–52, bass clarinet 31–36. The brass sound under the flute only at 57–62 and 77–81:
- At those points the flute doubles Vln I at the unison, so the line does not depend on the flute's spectrum alone.
- The horns lie an octave or more below: Hn 1 at A♭4–E♭4 against Fl 1 at A♭5–A♭6 (57–59); Hn 1·2 at D♭4–B♭3 against B♭6 (79–81).

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Harmonic density and dynamic grading at the two peaks (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 289: forte brass are "essentially equivalent to distortion in terms of harmonic density", and "harmonically denser instruments tend to become foregrounded". PDF 290: volume can be rebalanced so that a softer source sits above louder ones.

**Score.**
- 61–62: the brass are marked under the theme (horns f, trombones and tuba mf; c054, c076). Their denser spectrum would otherwise pull the harmony in front of the ff theme in the winds and strings.
- 63: the reverse. Trombone 1 ff marcato is the only dense layer, over strings and bass trombone sf>p (c154), so the quotation comes forward without help.
- 79–81: horns 1·2 are f on the counter-melody (c034). That leaves it in the middle ground while the dense brass attack still makes it audible under the ff theme.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Unison doublings ("one note or two?"): all are deliberate (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 277–278: an instrument whose lower spectrum is masked by a stronger unison partner adds only upper content.

**Score.** My unison scan of the whole movement found only these cross-instrument unisons:
- Bassoons with harp RH, and B.cl. with harp LH, under the horn solo 7–14. The harp gives the attack, the winds the sustain (PDF 275–276).
- Basses with harp LH, 37–44.
- Viola triplets with the Vln II chord, 37–40 (recorded at c137).
- Cl 1 one level under Hn 1 at 53–56 (c038, Casanelles' own colour unison).
- Fl/Ob/Cl on the violins' octaves at 57–62 and 77–82.
- The Vc/Hn/Bsn counter-melody, 75–82.
- Hn 4 con sord. with the violas sul C, 87–88.
- The Cl 1–Vln I handover on C5 at 89.

Each is a colour doubling the history records as intended. The one doubling that adds almost nothing is Cl 2's throat A♭4 (written B♭4) under Hn 3·4 a2 and Vln II at 60–61. c126 considered it and kept it; I do not reopen it.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Spectral envelope of the timpani answer at 84–85 (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 276: each register and dynamic has its own envelope. PDF 290: a sustained ("compressed") sound is more present, and a single hit decays.

**Score.** The timpani answer E♭3–C3–A♭2 at 84–85 is in single pp strokes, and the A♭2 is tied into 85 and decays under the clarinet's niente E♭5. A roll would keep the A♭ present and compete with the clarinet's entry. The decay leaves the field clear for the tail, which is point 8 of the reading notes again.

**Fix.** None. Do not change the strokes to a roll.

### 7. One low third, bar 54 (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** PDF 274: the harmonic series gives octaves, fifths and fourths at the bottom and thirds only higher up.

**Score.** Across the movement, the only onset where the two lowest pitches are a third apart below C3 is bar 54: cellos B♭2 and bassoon 1 D♭3, pp, for two beats. The basses rest there by design (c164), and 53–56 is the protected 6/3 bridge (c143).

**Fix.** None. If 53–56 is ever rescored, bassoon 1 could hold F3 (`f1~` in place of `des1~`) in bar 54 only. That breaks c107's held D♭3, so I do not recommend it now.

The retransition 65–70 and the coda release 82–86 (Fig. 6.1 layout), the near-to-far order of theme A's colours ending in the muted-horn echo 87–90, the brass grading at 61–64 and 79–81, and the 8-page layout.
