## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Two Neapolitan links to movement I are in the notes but missing from the coherence files (record; no score change)

**Bars 87–89.** IV6 at 87 (D♭ major over F; Vc F2, Cb F1, lower Vla F3, Hn 4 F3, C.a. F4; Cl 1 D♭5 on top) is the Neapolitan sixth of C minor. It is voiced the way PDF 484 recommends: the bass is the doubled tone and ♭2̂ of C (D♭5) is alone on top. coda.ily records this under c080, citing PDF 494. Two things make it a cyclic event:

- A&S §14 (PDF 494, Ex. 28-19) names the most frequent pivot use of ♭II: the N6 of a minor key becomes IV6 in the major key a third lower. C minor to A♭ major is exactly that key relation, so the symphony's I→II relation is heard as one chord in II's last phrase.
- Movement I's last motto (Vln I, I 177–182) ends C5–D♭5–C5. There D♭ is a neighbour inside the final tonic, the "♭II" of §15 (PDF 495). In II, Cl 1 holds the same D♭5 over IV6–iv (87–88) and falls to the same C5 at 89, where Vln I takes it over. Movement I's ♭2̂ becomes II's 4̂ and resolves straight to C. In movement I it could only pass to C over V (PDF 483: "this '1̂' functions as a passing tone").

**Bar 49.** Movement I's coda puts its minor S head over i–iv–N6–cadential 6/4–V (I 171–174; themes-mvt1.ly, mvtOneThemeSCodaMinor). II's quotation of the minor S head also passes through ♭II: G major over B under the C.a.'s D5 at 49 b3, then V7 and i (c062, c312). Within this symphony, both minor S-head statements outside movement I's exposition lean on the Neapolitan.

**Problem.** coherence/mvt2.md does not name the N6 at 87 or the N6 at 49 in its quotation tables. finale.md line 19, the "Neapolitan (♭II) thread across the four movements", lists only "A minor at 63–64" for movement II. That thread decides how the finale treats ♭2̂ in its coda ("never ♭2̂→♮2̂ in the top line"), so the finale thread should know that II already resolves movement I's D♭5 to C5 at pitch.

**Fix.** No score change. Composer, add to mvt2.md under "Themes quoted from movement I": `| Neapolitan sixth of C minor (I 7, 20–22, 140–143, 173) | 87 | Vc F2, Cb F1, Vla/Hn 4 F3, C.a. F4, Cl 1 D♭5 | D♭/F heard as IV6 of A♭ (A&S PDF 494, N6 of a minor key = IV6 of the major key a third lower); Cl 1 D♭5 → C5 at 89 answers movement I's last C5–D♭5–C5 (I 178–182) at pitch |`. Also add to the 47–53 row: "harmonized i – VI6 (5–6) – N6 (49 b3) – V7 – i, as movement I's coda S head runs over N6 (I 173)". Then tell the finale thread so its line 19 can list 49 and 87.

### 2. 61–65: the ♭II region is A minor, not the Phrygian major triad, and that is right here (record, no change)

A&S define ♭II as a major triad (PDF 483). In A♭ that is B𝄫–D♭–F♭ = A major. The score has A minor (A–C–E) at 63–64. This is not a slip. The quotation is movement I's minor P head and must keep its mode. The A-minor triad also keeps C♮, which is 3̂ of A♭ and the symphony's common tone: movement I's last note, II's first and last top note. In the quotation, C is the held, syncopated note (Tbn 1 C4 at 63 b2–b3), and Vln II C6 and Vla C5 hold it in the tremolo. With A major, C♯ (= D♭) would replace it, and the one C the climax carries would be lost. Everything else in 61–65 follows the unit closely:

- ♭VI (F♭ major at 62 b3) acts as the dominant of the ♭II region (Ex. 28-14).
- The bass goes A → D (♯4̂ under vii°7 of V) → E (F♭) → E♭, the chromatic route of Ex. 28-1.
- The spelling switches at the key change, as PDF 490 allows in a four-flat key.
- B♭ appears over the ♭II region as 2̂ of the tonic scale (PDF 493).

This is protected (c312 do-not-touch: the 61–65 return).

### 3. ♭2̂ at the climax answered by ♮2̂ as the soprano of V (63 → 69–70; record, no change)

At 63 ♭2̂ (A6 = B𝄫6) is on top in Fl 1 (sf) and Vln I (div., tremolo). This is the most intense placement, at the climax (PDF 483). No voice moves A→B♭ (PDF 484, Ex. 28-3). The step to ♮2̂ comes in other voices: Tbn 1 B♭3 at 63 b4 and Vc B♭2 at 64 b4½. The cross relation between voices is allowed (PDF 484). The Mendelssohn device of Ex. 28-6 (PDF 485) then closes the span. ♮2̂ is the top voice of V at the end of the retransition: Hn 1's tail E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 lands on B♭3 at 69–70 over the E♭ pedal, with nothing above it. So the retransition's horn tail (protected) is also the ♭2̂→♮2̂ resolution of the climax, six bars later and about three octaves lower. Keep.

### 4. Theme B in D♭ is the symphony's ♭II theme (record; protects 41–44 at pitch)

Ex. 28-17 (PDF 492–493) restates a theme on ♭II, and §11 (PDF 491) tonicizes ♭II at length. mvt1.md (line 177) says movement I never makes D♭ a key: its ♭II is always a chord. Movement II gives D♭ its first key area (37–60) and its own theme. The finale then tonicizes ♭II of C at length (A♭7 at 205–210, D♭ at 211–218) with that theme: II's C.a. phrase at 41–44, quoted at IV 211–218, with Hn 1·2 at II's own pitch at 217–218 (finale.md item 15). It leaves through the N6 and German sixth to I at 223, in its own scale as PDF 493 asks for a long tonicization. In the symphony's arc, movement II supplies the melody for the finale's ♭II region. Theme B's second phrase and its D♭ level must stay as they are. This adds a reason to the existing protection.

### 5. Chromatic spelling of the cyclic tails follows Ex. 28-24 (record, no change)

Every motto tail in II is written 5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂ with D♮, not E𝄫: Hn 1 at 8, violins at 24 and 72, B.cl. at 32–34, Hn 1 at 67–69, Cl 1 at 85–89. PDF 497–498 (Ex. 28-24a, C major G–F♯–F–E) prescribes this: in major, ♯4̂ is used descending as well as ascending. The scherzo's B–A♯–A–G♯ and the finale's G–F♯–F–E follow the same rule, so the three closes falling by major thirds are spelled alike. The quotation of movement I's P head writes ♭2̂ of A minor as B♭ (63 b4), the minor chromatic scale's descending form (Ex. 28-27, PDF 499). F♭ is always written as a lowered passing tone falling to E♭ (Ex. 28-23): 5, 80–81, 87–89. Nothing to change.

### 6. A ♭II colour in the coda (Ex. 28-20, Ex. 28-14): still not recommended

PDF 495 (Chopin Op. 25/4) and PDF 489–490 (Verdi Kyrie: ♭VI–♭II–V7–I, ppp morendo, at the end of a major movement) both colour a major ending with ♭II. II's coda (83–92) uses the plagal form of §15 instead: an A♭ pedal, then IV6–iv–I with F–F♭–E♭. c312 and c310 rejected B𝄫 or ♭VI here. Their reasons still hold under this lens. D♭–C on top is a cyclic rule. The finale quotes 87–89 at pitch (F3–F♭3–E♭3 at IV 197–198) and 85–89 (Ob 1 at IV 95–98). The finale's coda (C major) already keeps ♭2̂ only as a neighbour over the tonic, which is the Chopin model, so the symphony uses it once, at its end. A second use here would pre-empt it. Recorded only.

### 8. Emotional arc through the Neapolitan (summary, no change)

Movement I keeps ♭II as a chord that never resolves to the tonic: its coda ends N6–6/4–V and then C major. Movement II tonicizes it twice. The first is softly, in a borrowed key (F♯ minor's N6 under the S-head quotation, 49). The second is ff, as the region of movement I's own P head (63–64), reached from ♭VI and left through V. Its coda then turns movement I's D♭ into a plain IV and lets D♭5 fall to C5 (87–89). The scherzo touches ♭II of E once before its last dominant, and the finale tonicizes ♭II of C with II's own theme B before C major arrives. II thus moves ♭II from tragic chord (I) to lyrical key and quiet resolution, which is the role of a slow movement between a C-minor first movement and a scherzo. I find nothing in the 92 bars that works against this arc.
