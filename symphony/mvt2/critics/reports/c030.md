## Findings

### 1. Bars 1–4: horn 3 holds one C4 for more than 30 seconds (applied)
**Problem.** In movement I v156, Hn 3 holds C4 ppp from bar 180 to 182. That is two bars of Tempo I at ♩=50 plus a fermata bar at the MIDI step ♩=30, about 18 seconds. In movement II, Hn 3 picks this note up with `\repeatTie` and ties it through bars 1–4 (12 beats at ♩=52, about 14 seconds). The note has no break and no other player takes it over. No horn player can hold it.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 207–208: synthesized sound can begin and sustain without the limits of a physical body, while acoustic notes follow an envelope. PDF 210–212: once the mock-up is part of the working process, it can sound right while being unplayable. The FluidSynth render holds this note without trouble, which is why the problem has not shown up. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44: "the remarks on breathing … apply with equal force to the brass".
**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** Horn 4 enters under the tie, horn 3 breathes in bar 2 and takes the C again at bar 3. The sounding pitches do not change, and horn 3 stays on top at 3–6 (c010).
```
introHnThree = { c'2.\repeatTie\ppp | R2. | c'2.~\pp | c'2. | des'2. | c'2 r4 | }
introHnFour = { r4 c'2~\ppp | c'2.\< | aes2.\pp | aes2. | aes2. | aes2 r4 | }
```
Bar 1 is still movement I's chord: the same five tied parts at the same pitches, with Hn 4 doubling the C4. For the coordinator: movement I's own 18-second C4 is also long. Movement I's Hn 4, which plays G3 at 181–182, could take the C4 at 181 under Hn 3.

### 2. Bars 85–90: the last tail statement repeats movement I's ending (applied)
**Problem.** Movement I v156 ends with Vln I playing G4–E♭5–D5–C5–D♭5–C5 at pp–ppp (bars 177–182). Movement II follows it attacca and ends with Vln I playing E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5 at pp–ppp (85–92), in the same instrument, register and dynamic, about six minutes later. It is also the sixth statement of E♭–D–D♭–C at this pitch level in movement II (8, 24, 32, 68, 72, 85). This point has been open since cycle 23.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 201: structure and meaning can be carried by timbre instead of pitch. PDF 204: a change of timbre is a musical process "comparable to changes in harmony, melody". PDF 227–228: the decisive statement of Zimmer's theme is set apart by its instrument. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Clarinets specialise in diminuendo effects and are capable of decreasing their tone to a breath (morendo)", and in a diminuendo the instruments leave in the order brass, woodwind, strings. The clarinet also answers movement I's own coda clarinet (E♭4–C5–B4, 175–177).
**Answer to the protection (cycle 10, "tail in Vln I").** The reasons given were the full tail at movement I's pitch, the D♮ that the timpani lack, and C5 on top at the end. All three are kept. Only the instrument for E♭–D–D♭ changes. Vln I takes the C5 over at 89 and ends the movement on it, so the movement still begins and ends on Vln I's C5. The bowing keeps an up-bow entry and a down-bow ending (c016/c018). Cl 2 takes over the F4–F♭4–E♭4 octave that Cl 1 played over the violas' sul C line (c020): the pitches are the same and only the player changes.
**Fix (applied, coda.ily).**
```
codaClOne = { R2.*2 | es''2.(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" | d''2.) \breathe | des''2.(\pp | des''2. | c''2.~ | c''2) r4 | R2. | R2.\fermata | }
codaClTwo = { R2.*4 | f'2.\pp | fes'2. \breathe | es'2.~ | es'2.~ | es'2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | es'2.\ppp\fermata | }
codaVlnOne = { R2.*6 | c''2.~\pp\upbow | c''2.~\downbow | c''2.~\upbow\> | c''2.\downbow\ppp\fermata | }
```
I considered horn and rejected it. Concert E♭5 is written B♭5 for horn in F, which is too high to sustain at pp. An octave lower, the line sits inside the violas' C4/E♭4 and repeats the horn's tail at 67–68.

### 3. Bars 2–4: timpani tuning at the attacca (applied)
**Problem.** Movement I ends with the timpani tuned G, A♭, C, E♭ (intro.ily label and the dev/trans "muta" returns). Bar 2 of movement II labels the drums "A♭, C, D♭, E♭" as if the player had time to retune. At an attacca they have none: they arrive with G on one drum. A♭ at bar 2 is already available. D♭ is first needed at 61.
**Evidence.** Adler part 2 PDF 81: "It is advisable to mark changes in tuning". PDF 79: pedal drums can be changed quickly, but only when the part says so. Casanelles PDF 212: material made by separate writers stays one piece only if they share the same template. The two movements are made in separate loops, and this is a joint between them.
**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** `^\markup \small "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭ (as in movement I)"` at bar 2, and `R2.^\markup \small "muta G in D♭: A♭, C, D♭, E♭"` at bar 5, at the start of the rests that run to bar 51. The tuning in the movement is unchanged (cycle 6). This also settles the open "timpani label before bar 1" point.

### 4. Bars 87–88: one muted-brass colour, at the close (applied)
**Problem.** Movement I uses no muted or stopped brass (critic 129), and neither does movement II. The coda's F→F♭→E♭ (minor iv, the movement's E/F♭→E♭ thread) is where a distant colour carries the most meaning.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44: muted brass "produce an effect of distance", and the muted horn is "tender and dull in piano". PDF 53: muted horn approaches the oboe and cor anglais. Adler part 1 PDF 307: con sordino uses a non-transposing mute. Casanelles PDF 223–224: a technique is chosen for one specific sound and meaning. PDF 245/247–248: Annihilation keeps its synthetic sound for "the only time" at the end. PDF 236: instrumental synthesis, here muted horn with violas sul C on the same F3–F♭3–E♭3. Stopped horn (Adler PDF 308, nasal, sf>p) does not suit a ppp Adagio close. I also rejected muting Hn 1 at 67–70: that solo is the open horn's warm statement of the tail (Casanelles PDF 227) and is protected.
**Fix (applied).** `codaHnThree = { R2.*4 | aes2.\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." | ...` Hn 3·4 rest from 82, which leaves about 15 seconds to insert the mute. They do not play again, so no "senza sord." is needed. The cycle-6 open point on a con sordino coda is answered in this form.

### 5. A&S Unit 31 devices: keep them out of movement II (no change)
**Judgment.** Descending diminished-seventh chains (A&S PDF 559–560) are the most unstable chromatic device. They would bring movement I's storm into a diatonic Dvořák-style Largo, and they belong in movement III's development. The rising chromatic 5–6 with augmented triads (PDF 554) is a device for building, and movement II's build (53–60) already has its bass 4̂–♯4̂–5̂. Movement III's climb to the C-major chorale needs it more. A&S give the lament bass with 7–6 suspensions (PDF 552) the "affect of grief". Casanelles PDF 239 says a device's meaning depends on its setting. In this consoling movement the descent is already present as the motto tail (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂) and as the F–F♭–E♭ inner line (62–65, 87–89), which is the last three steps of a lament. I tried a full A♭–G–G♭–F–F♭–E♭ bass under the half cadence at 19–22. It works only if the oboe's G4 at 20 b3 is changed, and that rewrites the protected dialogue. **Proposal:** record in cyclic-notes that movement II uses the lament only as these fragments, and that movement III should use the full 1̂–5̂ lament with 7–6s in C minor before its turn to major.

### 6. The attacca joint itself is correct (no change; note for cyclic-notes)
v156 does not tie out of bar 182, as cyclic-notes ("Changes from cycles 111–120 / cycle 130") says. It ends the five parts with `\fermata\laissezVibrer`, and Cb, Hn 4, harp and timpani release. Movement II's `\repeatTie` in Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3 and Hn 3 C4 matches those parts and pitches exactly, at ppp, and "attacca" is printed in movement I (`\textEndMark`). The cyclic-notes line should say "laissez-vibrer ties with fermata" so the next critic does not reopen it. This follows Casanelles PDF 200: the link works because the listener has just heard the chord.
