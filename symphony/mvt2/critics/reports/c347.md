## Findings

Since c207, 140 cycles have changed the strings at 26, 28, 52, 71-78 and 73-75. I checked each place against the reading again. Nothing in v347 breaks a rule from this reading that is not already protected or explained. I found no change that improves the score by this reading's standards. The findings are ranked by how close each comes to a real weakness.

### 1. Bars 75-76: the unison violins stand 15-17 semitones above the next voice (recorded, no change)
- **Observation.** On 75 b3 the full orchestra sounds D♭2 D♭3 F3 A♭3 D♭4 | F5. On 76 b2 it is D♭2 A♭2 D♭3 A♭3 F4 | B♭5, and on 76 b3 B♭1 B♭2 D♭4 F4 | A♭5. The violins in unison are the only voice above F4. Ex. V-26 (PDF 151) keeps the upper gaps smaller than the lower ones. 71-74, with Vln II an octave below, does not have this gap.
- **Why it stays.** This is the register plan settled by c299, c309 and c341 (unison from 74 b3, upper octave from 77 b2, B♭6 first at 79). HISTORY allows it to be reopened only with a new book reason. Adler's spacing rule is not new: c207 raised it for the same unison at 27-28 and it was set aside for the same plan. Two things already reduce the gap. The violas' eighths reach A♭4 on 76 b1 (the beat-onset reduction does not show that), and the harp strikes D♭4-F4 there. The obvious filler would be the violas' 76 b2-3 group moved up an octave (B♭4-F4-B♭4). That doubles the violins' B♭5 appoggiatura in an inner voice over the IV chord, which A&S's doubling rules and Ex. V-27's logic both reject. Tchaikovsky's mm. 9-16 (PDF 154) show that a thin accompaniment well apart from the tune keeps the tune clear. Keep as is.

### 2. Bar 61 b1-2: Vln II plays A♭4, not an octave under Vln I's D♭6 (recorded, follows)
- **Observation.** Grieg (PDF 155) has the second violins double the first an octave lower at the strongest bars instead of adding harmony. At the climax downbeat, Vln II plays A♭4 under Vln I's D♭6.
- **Why it follows.** Vln II's A♭4-D♭5-F5-F♭5 is theme B's augmented head. It sounds in three octaves with Fl 2 above and the cellos below (c021, c161, c321). So Vln II is doubling the main line at the octave, as Grieg does, but the doubled line is the head, not Vln I's held D♭6. From b3, D♭ sounds in every octave from D♭1 (Cbn) to D♭6 (Fl, Vln I). Keep.

### 3. Bars 21-22 and 27-28: wide gap under the top line (recorded, no change)
At 21-22 the solo violin (senza sord.) sits 15-17 semitones above the muted violas and cellos. That is the solo-over-thin-accompaniment case of PDF 154 and of Rimsky's Ex. 222 (c039), so it follows. At 27 b3 and 28 b2 the unison violins (F5, B♭5) are 16-17 semitones above the violas. This is c207's finding 5, blocked by the protected unison-versus-octaves plan for 23 against 71 (c191). The reason still holds: Grieg's rule (PDF 155) is met across the movement because 23-30 is plain and 71-81 is doubled. Keep.

### 4. Bars 83-92: the bass reaches the tonic and stays (recorded, follows in substance)
Tchaikovsky's diminuendo (PDF 154, mm. 29-36) drops the basses each time they reach the tonic. Here basses and cellos hold the A♭ pedal through 83-86 and again from 89 to the end. The thinning is done in another way. The cellos are 4 soli at 83-86, the basses are 4 soli from 87, everything is ppp, and the violas move into the lower half sul C. This is option 2 on PDF 151, divided low strings for "a very mellow, darkish sound". Removing the basses at 89 would take away the 16-foot A♭ under the plagal close (protected) and the A♭ that the scherzo's G♯ picks up. Keep.

### 5. Bars 87-88: IV6 with the third F in five voices (recorded, no change)
inv.py flags 87 b1-3: F1 (Cb), F2 (Vc), F3 (Vla, Hn 4), F4 (C.a.), with the root D♭ only in Cl 1. Ex. V-27 would call the upper F's "not good". But each F begins the chromatic F-F♭-E♭ line of the iv-I close, which is protected (c194, c199). Adler PDF 150 ranks melodic considerations alongside spacing. This is c207's finding 4, and nothing has changed since. Keep.

### 6. Foreground, middleground and background (PDF 155): checked and clear (no change)
- 15-22: foreground oboe and solo violin; middleground violas and upper cellos answering in the bar's rhythm (dovetailing, PDF 154); background Vln II's louré A♭4.
- 37-44: foreground Fl 1 and then C.a. with Fl 1 in tenths; background Vln II div. sul tasto, viola triplets (arco, then pizz.) and pizzicato basses. The cellos enter at 41 as a quiet middle part.
- 53-56: foreground Vln I+II in unison; middleground Hn 1 with Cl 1 an octave above; background harp triplets taking the viola figure, Bsn 1, and cellos alone on the bass (c327).
- 71-78: foreground violins; middleground the cello counter-melody, joined by Bsn 1 and Hn 2 at 75 and Hn 1 at 78; background the viola eighths, harp, and basses with Bsn 2.
Each group keeps one job for the whole passage, as in the Tchaikovsky and Grieg examples. No change.
