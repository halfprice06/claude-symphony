## What else I studied

The full score of Dvořák's Symphony No. 6, second movement (Simrock 1880, IMSLP #26977, PDF pages 54-64; 2/4, Adagio, ♩ = 42, about 216 bars, about 10 minutes). No Dvořák 6 is in the local corpus. Bar numbers below are approximate, read from the printed tens. Its plan: four bars of woodwinds intimate the theme (fp>pp); Vln I takes the theme at 5 over off-beat repeated chords in Vln II and violas, and an oboe answers between phrases; a Poco più animato with horns in repeated semiquavers and a ff pesante string cadence come at about 27-36; an episode follows (B, about 70) with a viola espressivo line; the theme returns at C (about 90) over the same repeated chords with pizzicato bass; a second build (Poco più animato) leads to a ff Tempo I climax with trumpets, horns and a timpani trill (about 104), after which the music drops at once to p with Vln I alone (D); after a further crescendo the theme returns again pp at E (about 138) with Vln I and II in octaves over off-beat horn chords; the coda (F, about 170) has pulsing viola semiquavers, a short ff outburst (about 182) and a final ff chord (about 205), and ends pp in woodwinds and horns with the strings silent. The Adagio uses no trombones or tuba. Program notes: hollywoodbowl.com/musicdb/pieces/4030/symphony-no-6 (theme first intimated in an atmospheric introduction, then given to the violins), thelistenersclub.com/2021/09/01/dvoraks-sixth-symphony-sunny-bohemian-pastures/ (a slow rondo; the opening woodwind lines recall Beethoven 9/iii). I also diffed v309 against v319 (sources, analysis.txt, build files) and compared pages 6-7 of both.

## Findings

Holistic verdict: at the level of the bar, a conductor would take this for a master's work. Every scoring device that Adler's transcription chapter credits to Brahms, Dvořák, Bizet, Ravel, Stravinsky and Schoenberg appears where it belongs: dropouts that make the diminuendi, late doublings, the counter-theme, the return to the opening colour, brass held back. The movement differs from Dvořák 6/ii in scale and in the surface of the theme, not in craft. I have no finding strong enough to change a protected passage, and almost every passage is protected. I applied no change. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-319/work is v319 unchanged, rebuilt to confirm it is clean.

### 1. Scale: theme A has three statements, Dvořák's theme has four, and the movement is half as long (record, no change)
- Bars: whole movement; theme A at 7, 23, 71.
- Problem: Dvořák 6/ii runs about 216 bars of 2/4 (about 10 minutes) and scores its theme four times (5, about 90, about 138, coda). This movement runs 345 s (analysis.txt). A conductor coming from Dvořák 6/ii, 7/ii or 9/ii (10-12 minutes) will hear a shorter, more concentrated Adagio.
- Evidence: Adler PDF 209 and 213 value a repeat that keeps the basic colour but changes its hue. The three statements here do exactly that (horn with winds and harp; violins in unison with the cello counter-melody; violins in octaves with the counter-melody and viola eighths). The form critics settled the proportions against Dvořák 9/ii (HISTORY cycle 253: 36/40/24 percent against 9/ii's 38/39/23, "coda not long"; c303: timings 36/34/30 percent).
- Fix: none recommended in this cycle. Adding a fourth statement would be a new section, not a revision, and the proportions are protected. If Dan ever wants a longer movement, the place to add one is after 82, before the coda: theme A's first four bars in the woodwinds over pulsing violas, as in Dvořák's coda at F. That would reopen the protected 83-92.

### 2. The orchestrated diminuendi at 63-65 and 81-83 match Dvořák and the reading (record)
- Bars: 63-65, 80-83.
- Evidence: after his ff climax, Dvořák cuts the winds and leaves Vln I alone, p, at D (Simrock p. 59). At 63 this score cuts the woodwinds and horns, and the strings go sf>p under Tbn 1. At 80-82, Fl 2 leaves at 80 b3, Ob 2 at 81, harp and timpani before 82, and most winds at 82 b3. The flute line itself comes down from G6 to A♭5 for the p (Adler PDF 213, Slavonic Dance m. 33: the high part is lowered so the diminuendo can be played; PDF 224: a diminuendo made as the instruments come down and drop out).
- Fix: none. The release plan at 80-83 is protected (c278).

### 3. Theme A returns at 71-78 with no ornamental variant except the grace run at 78 (record for the melody lens)
- Bars: 71-78 against 23-30.
- Problem: Dvořák varies the surface of his theme at each return: turns and dotted demisemiquavers in Vln I at C and E, and new woodwind filigree. Adler PDF 224 (Ravel) and 228 (Stravinsky) both note that the orchestral version varies and extends ornaments. Here the reprise has the same notes as 23-30, an octave higher. The only new ornament is the grace run into B♭6 at 78.
- Evidence: aTwoVlnOne 71-77 equals aOneVlnOne 23-29 up an octave (except the octave changes at 74 b3 and 77).
- Fix: not recommended now, because theme A's line is protected (standing list, c-entries "theme A line with B♭ appoggiatura 7-14, 22-26, 71-82"). If a melody critic reopens it, the least intrusive variant is a turn on the held E♭6 at 72 in Vln I only, for example `es'''4\> d'''4( des'''4)` → `es'''8.( f'''32 es''' d'''4) des'''4`, with Vln II keeping the plain line so the theme stays recognisable. Untested; it would need the slur and hairpin checked against c118's bowing.

### 4. Accompaniment types: this score follows Dvořák 9/ii at 23-30 and Dvořák's Slavonic Dance at 37-44 (record)
- Bars: 23-30, 37-44, 71-78.
- Evidence: Dvořák 6/ii keeps one accompaniment type (off-beat repeated chords) under the theme throughout. Adler PDF 209 calls the Slavonic Dance version "a light accompaniment and sustained harmonic counterpoint". This score uses that model under theme B at 37-44 (viola triplets, held Vln II chord, pizzicato bass and harp), sustained strings under theme A at 23-30 (the 9/ii model, which is this movement's main model), and flowing viola eighths at 71-78. Three types for three kinds of statement is more varied than Dvořák 6/ii, not less.
- Fix: none.

### 5. Ending colour: Dvořák ends in winds and horns, this score ends in strings (record)
- Bars: 87-92.
- Evidence: Dvořák's last seven bars have the strings silent and the woodwinds and horns holding pp. This score returns to the string colour and Vln I C5 of bar 1. That follows Adler PDF 224 ("the return of the exact opening colours for the last five measures") and the cyclic rule (C5 on top, D♭-C plagal close). The held chord at 89-92 has no internal motion (Adler PDF 213, Bizet: motion added to a ppp chord). The motion comes just before, at 83-88 (harp harmonics, timpani, Hn 3·4 and C.a. swell), as in Dvořák 9/ii's last bars.
- Fix: none. The ending is protected and is the join to the scherzo.

### 6. Brass economy matches Dvořák 6/ii (record)
- Bars: 60-64.
- Evidence: Dvořák's Adagio leaves out trombones and tuba and uses horns and two trumpets fully only at the climax and in the coda outburst. This score uses trombones and tuba only at 60-64, and trumpets not at all (Adler PDF 236: Milhaud saves the brass for the significant entrance).
- Fix: none.
