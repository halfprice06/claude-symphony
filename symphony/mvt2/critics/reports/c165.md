## What else I studied

Gould, *Behind Bars* PDF 50–51 (rests in double-stemmed writing: "a rest moves further from the centre of the stave or to outside the stave should parts otherwise collide"; semibreve rests go on a ledger line outside the other part) supports finding 3. Gould PDF 19 points to the rhythmic cue line as the way to show a cue whose pitch does not matter, and PDF 42–48 (octave signs, ledger lines) supports finding 4's octave transposition of a cue instead of an 8va sign. I read the earlier MOLA and parts reports c045, c075 and c135 and the c145 parts notes in HISTORY.md, so I do not repeat the composer name, rest-number ranges or cue set. I wrote small LilyPond test files to find why multi-bar rests split. Every one of the following starts a new multi-bar rest: a skip boundary in the global voice (even in a separate Voice), a boundary between two R events, a bar check, and a property set such as the MIDI-only `\mt` step. A printed letter or tempo word splits it too, which is correct.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Multi-bar rests are broken where nothing is printed. Applied (parts only).
**Bars:** all parts. Examples from v165: Flute 1 shows 53–56 as "2 | 2", 71–76 as "4 | 2", 83–88 as "4 | 2" and 89–92 as four single-bar rests. The tuba's 65–92 is "6 | 4 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1": thirteen rest blocks where the player can see only five landmarks (H, J, poco allargando at 78, K, rit. at 89). The same splits occur in Fl 2, Ob 2, Cl 1·2, B.cl., Cbn, all horns, trombones, timpani and harp (harp 84–92 "3 | 2 | 2 | 2").
**Cause:** the `\mt` MIDI tempo steps (55, 57, 59, 79–82, 90–92), the skip boundaries left by the score's `\break`s (41, 75, 87), the bar checks and section joins in the instrument variables (31, 37, 71), and joins such as Hn 2 "R2.*5 | R2." at 70.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5 and Example 4 (PDF 10). The model part runs each rest as one block ("16", "36", "17", "20") and breaks it only at a letter, a tempo word or a key change. A split with nothing printed over it makes the player wonder what was missed, and the player has to add the pieces up while counting. In this movement the rests most broken up are in the allargando and rit. bars, where counting is hardest.
**Fix (genparts.py):** a Scheme function `partMerge` flattens the part's sequential music, drops the `\mt` property sets and bar checks, and joins adjacent plain skips (global) and adjacent plain whole-bar rests (instrument). A rest or skip that carries a mark (markup, fermata) is never joined to the one before it, so every printed item still splits the rest. It is applied as `partGlobalPlain = \partMerge \noBreaks \global` and as `\partMerge \removeWithTag #'score \<var>` for every part and both harp hands. The tuba now reads "6 | 7 | 5 | 6 | 4" for 65–92, Flute 1 reads "11" for 23–33, and the harp reads "5 | 4" for 84–92. One split remains at 59: the c035 spacing section (`\newSpacingSection` plus the SpacingSpanner override) also breaks rests, and the viola part needs it to stay on one page. Shorter rests also let LilyPond pack the harp's 71–92 onto one crowded line, so genparts.py now adds a `\break` before 78, where the line broke before.

### 2. Letters and tempo words float nearer the system above than their own. Applied (parts only).
**Bars and parts:** Bass clarinet: "poco sostenuto" (48) is printed directly under the solo at 32–33, just below "mf > p". A player reads it as a sostenuto in the solo. "J" (71) is printed under bar 57 of the line above. Horn 1: "poco sostenuto" (48) sits under the end of the horn solo at 13–14. Contrabassoon: "E" (45) and "G" (61) hang under the line above. Cor anglais: "poco sostenuto" sits under bar 37 of the line above. Oboe 1, Fl 2: "poco allargando" (78) hangs under bar 59 of the line above.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 8 and Example 7 (PDF 12): in extracted parts, anchored marks do not keep their score placement, which produces collisions and floating marks. Each of these marks is a stack two or three deep (letter over tempo word over italic word). With LilyPond's default 1-staff-space padding between systems, the top of the stack ends up 2–4 staff spaces below the system above and 8–10 above its own staff.
**Fix (genparts.py \paper):** `system-system-spacing = #'((basic-distance . 12) (minimum-distance . 8) (padding . 4) (stretchability . 60))`. Most parts fill half a page, so there is room. The viola part fills its page, has no floating stack, and keeps the default padding through `\paper { system-system-spacing.padding = #1 }` in its bookpart, so it stays one page with no turn. I checked every part afterwards: each stack now sits clearly over its own staff.

### 3. Whole-bar rests printed across the cue notes in four parts. Applied (part-only tags).
**Bars:** Cbn 59–60, Tbn 2 58–59, B.tbn 58–59, Tuba 58–59 (the c135 "Vc." cues). These cues are `#DOWN`, so the player's own rest goes up to the fourth line, which is exactly where the cellos' F3–G♭3 cue notes sit. In the contrabassoon the rest is drawn across the eighth notes of 60, and in the tuba across the stems of 59.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5 says a cue must be "audible and obvious", and Example 7 (PDF 12) shows collisions as an extraction fault. Gould PDF 50–51 says a rest moves outside the other part so that the parts do not collide.
**Fix (sections/bSec.ily, inside `\tag #'part`):** `\cueDuring #"vcQ" #UP` for cbsn, tbnTwo, bassTbn and tuba. The cue stems go up and the part's own rest moves to the bottom of the staff, clear of the cue. The timpani cue at 50–51 does not collide and is unchanged. The score is not affected (`\tag #'part`).

### 4. Flute 1's bass clarinet cue sits three to five ledger lines under the staff. Applied.
**Bars:** Fl 1 34–36. Since c140 the bass clarinet plays the motto at movement I's pitch (C3–E♭3 range), and the flute cue reproduced it at concert pitch far below the treble staff. That is hard to read and gives no useful pitch information to a flautist.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 5: cues must be "obvious to the musician reading the part". It also says to avoid octave signs in parts, so the cue should be transposed, not marked 8va. Gould PDF 19 and PDF 42–48: when a cue gives the rhythm and contour, the register is free.
**Fix (sections/trans.ily, part-only):** `\transposedCueDuring #"bassClQ" #DOWN c'' { <>^\markup \tiny "B.cl." R2.*3 }` puts the cue an octave higher, on or just below the first ledger line. I tested `c` (wrong way: an octave lower) and `c''` (an octave higher).

### 5. The instrument name is printed three times on each part's first page. Applied.
**Problem:** it appears in the running header ("Flute 1"), in the title block (bold "Flute 1") and again as the piece label at top left above the first system.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 6: the instrument name must be on the front of the part. PDF 7: the instrument name goes in a header "on each subsequent page".
**Fix (genparts.py):** the odd-page header prints `\unless \on-first-page-of-part \fromproperty #'header:instrument`, and the `piece` field is removed from each bookpart header. The name now appears once on page 1 (the title block) and as the header from page 2 on (the harp's page 2 shows "2 … Harp").

### 6. The harp part is filed after the double bass. Applied.
**Reading:** MOLA PDF 4 and Example 1: the order is woodwind, brass, timpani/percussion/harp, strings. A librarian sorting the folders by the book would put the harp last.
**Fix (genparts.py):** the harp bookpart is generated by `harp()` just before Violin I. The order is now Timp, Harp, Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb. The harp is still pages 1–2 with its turn at 70→71.

### 7. Stacked "poco rit." (47) and "poco sostenuto" (48) in every rest part. Not applied; for the record.
In parts that rest at 47–48, the two words are stacked (c145's padding keeps them from running together), with "poco sostenuto" on top. With finding 2 both now sit by their own staff, so the reading is clear. A further change, such as right-aligning "poco rit." at 47 b3 in the parts only, would put the two words on one level. It is cosmetic and touches the c048 tempo plan in global.ily, so I leave it.
