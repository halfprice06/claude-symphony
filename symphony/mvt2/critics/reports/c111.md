## Findings

### 1. Bars 32–34: the cor anglais doubles the bass clarinet's tail a third below, the order the reading calls forced (applied)

**Problem.** The bass clarinet solo states the motto tail E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 at 32–34. The cor anglais runs under it in parallel thirds, C4–B3–B♭3–A♭3. This puts a clarinet-family instrument above an oboe-family one. It also puts the bass instrument high in its range (E♭4 is written F5, the bass clarinet's upper register) over the alto double reed low in its range (A♭3 is written E♭4, four semitones above its lowest note). Rimsky-Korsakov says thirds and sixths need either one colour or different colours "in the normal order of register" (Fl/Ob, Fl/Cl, Ob/Cl, Cl/Bsn, Ob/Bsn). When that order is inverted, "a strained and forced resonance is created" (PDF 70). On octaves he gives the reason: "confusion of registers, the instrument of lower compass playing in its high register and vice versa" (PDF 67), and on the general order, "placing bassoons above clarinets and oboes … creates a far-fetched, unnatural tone" (PDF 65). The tail is the movement's most-heard melodic cell. Here it is coloured by exactly the pairing the reading warns against.

**Evidence.** trans.ily line 18 (`c'2. | c'4 b4 bes4 | aes2.`) under transBassCl (`es'2.~ | es'4 d'4 des'4 | c'2`). c027 introduced the cor anglais to avoid the solo's own clarinet family. It noted the line holds "the same harmony" but did not test the order against PDF 70. c110 relayed these bars to movement III as a second instance of melody in thirds.

**Fix (applied).** Move the cor anglais up an octave so it doubles the tail a sixth above, at pp under the solo's mp–mf:

```lilypond
transCorAnglais = { R2. | c''2.(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" | c''4 b'4 bes'4 | aes'2.) | R2. | r2 c''4\p | }
```

After the change, an oboe-family instrument sits above a clarinet-family one (normal order). The interval is a sixth between different colours, which PDF 70 calls "more suitable" for sixths. The cor anglais (C5–A♭4, written G5–E♭5) is in its own middle register. The pitch classes and the common-tone diminished seventh at 33 do not change, and the cor anglais stays above bassoon 1 as before. Every interval to bassoon 1, bassoon 2 and the contrabassoon keeps its class (no new fifths or octaves; checked by hand and with allpairs_par.py). The layout repeats 41–44 in miniature: the lead is the lower voice and the upper partner in sixths/tenths is one level softer (c101). This prepares theme B's scoring. The woodwind choir of 31–36 is kept as it is (winds alone, same instruments, same harmony). Only the cor anglais's octave changes, so the protected passage keeps what c020/c027 protected.

Alternative if the composer wants the solo kept as the top voice: bassoon 1 plays C4–B3–B♭3–A♭3 (Cl over Bsn is normal order). The cor anglais then rests, and bassoon 2 takes A♭3–F3–E♭3 while the contrabassoon holds the A♭ pedal. This changes more parts and loses the 8′ pedal, so I did not test it.

For the coordinator: if adopted, the c110 relay should read "parallel sixths at 32–34" in place of "thirds".

### 2. Bar 36: theme B's first statement has no rising-fourth pickup; 44 and 52 have one (applied)

**Problem.** Theme B begins on the downbeat F5 at 37 with no anacrusis. Its two later statements have the rising-fourth pickup: A♭4–D♭5 into the oboe at 44 b4, and C5–F5 into both violin sections at 52 b4. So the version the listener hears first is the only one without the gesture that the plan says the theme "opens with". When a hummed tune is heard three times, the first hearing fixes how it starts. c071 added the flute pickup C5 at 36. c072 dropped it because the flute staff entering in the 31–36 system made the score 9 pages, and c073 closed the item. I tested the flute pickup again: 9 pages.

**Answer to the layout reason.** Give the pickup to the cor anglais. Its staff is already in the 31–36 system, so no staff is added. With finding 1 the cor anglais is already in this register (A♭4 at 34). This keeps c071's idea ("the solo hands its anacrusis to the next colour"; the C5 is the bass clarinet's C4 an octave higher). The cor anglais then carries theme B's consequent at 41, so the instrument that introduces the tune also continues it. This is the reading's case of one expressive line passed between solo instruments of simple timbre (PDF 66–67). The flute's F5 at 37 keeps its own p, slur and "dolce cantabile". Built: 8 pages.

**Fix (applied).** Included in the line above: `| R2. | r2 c''4\p |` at 35–36. The cor anglais part shows written G5 on 36 b3 (checked in the parts book).
