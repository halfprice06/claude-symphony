## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The theme's cadential 6/4 falls on beat 3 and resolves across the bar line (bars 12, 28, 76, 80). Applied.
**Problem.** Every cadence of theme A follows the same pattern. Beat 3 carries E♭ in the bass under A♭ and C (bar 12: bass clarinet E♭3, bassoons C4/A♭3, horn A♭4), and this V6/4 resolves to V7 on the next downbeat. The bass E♭ is repeated from the weak beat to the strong one. This is A&S Ex. 10-6a, marked "never!" (PDF 155). The rule says "the cadential 6/4 must appear in a metrically strong position; no weak-to-strong bass repetitions are permitted" (PDF 154 §6; Points for Review 3, PDF 160). The stepwise 2̂–1̂–7̂ soprano that the 6/4 allows needs 1̂ on an accented beat (PDF 156 §8), and here A♭ sits on beat 3. The effect is a cadential arrival one beat early, followed by a static downbeat. These are the most-heard cadences of the movement, and the same metrical fault occurs four times. At 76 it is worse: violas play B♭–E♭–B♭ (the 5/3 of V) against the C of the cellos, horn 2 and clarinet 1. The 6/4 and its resolution therefore sound together.
**Fix (book: IV–II7–V7, PDF 146–147, Ex. 9-20/9-21; II leads to V7 with prepared 4̂, PDF 139).** On beat 3 the bass leaps down a third, D♭→B♭, so the beat is ii7 (B♭–D♭–F–A♭). The melody's A♭ becomes the 7th of ii7 and resolves to G over V7, so all upper voices fall against the bass's falling 5th. The melody, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged, and so is the B♭ appoggiatura from cycle 1, which still resolves to A♭.
- 12: `aOneBassCl` `des2 es4` → `des2 bes,4`; `aOneBsnOne` `aes2 c'4` → `aes2 des'4`; `aOneBsnTwo` `f2 aes4` → `f2 f4`; `aOneHarpL` `<des, aes,>2\arpeggio es,4` → `... bes,,4`.
- 28: `aOneCb` `des,2\mf\> es,4` → `des,2\mf\> bes,,4`; `aOneVla` 3rd chord `<es' aes'>4` → `<f' aes'>4`; `aOneVc` `aes2(\mf\> c'4)` → `aes2(\mf\> des'4)`.
- 76: Cb `des,2 es,4` → `des,2 bes,,4`; Bsn 2 `des2 es4` → `des2 bes,4`; harp LH `es,4` → `bes,,4`; Vla `bes( es' bes)` → `bes( des' bes)`; Vc and Hn 2 `aes2 c'4` → `aes2 des'4`; Cl 1 `des''4 des''4 c''4` → `des''4 des''4 des''4`.
- 80: Cb, Bsn 2, B.cl. `des2 es4` → `des2 bes,4` (Cb `bes,,4`); harp LH likewise; Timp `des2:32 es4` → `des2.:32` (the drums have no B♭); Vla 3rd chord `<es' aes'>4:32` → `<f' aes'>4:32`; Hn 4 `des4 des4 c4` → `des4 des4 des4`; Cl 1 → `des''4 des''4 des''4`; counter-melody (Vc, Hn 1, Hn 2, Bsn 1) `aes4 bes4 c'4` → `aes4 bes4 des'4` (C against the B♭ bass would be an unprepared 9th).
This touches the "plain first statement" accompaniment 23–30 (cycle 7) and the A′ climax 79–82 (cycle 11) by one bass note per phrase. The texture, doubling and octave layout those entries protect are unchanged. Horn theme 7–14 is unchanged.

### 2. The half cadence at bar 10 is reached from I6, without an intermediate harmony. Applied.
**Problem.** Bar 9 is I – I6 (bass A♭–C), and bar 10 is V. A&S calls I–I6–V weaker than I–IV–V or I–II–V, because "its bass arpeggiates the stable tones 1̂, 3̂ and 5̂" (PDF 135 §1). They also name the soprano that the horn already plays: "At cadences 4̂ (moving to 2̂) and 1̂ (moving to 7̂) are the most usable" over IV–V (PDF 136 §3). The horn's A♭→G is exactly 1̂→7̂. Because the I6 drops C3 straight to E♭2, the antecedent (7–10) ends with less pull than the consequent (11–14).
**Fix.** Make beat 3 of bar 9 IV: `aOneBassCl` `aes,2 c4` → `aes,2 des,4`; `aOneBsnTwo` `c2 es4` → `c2 f4`; `aOneHarpL` `c,4` → `des,,4`; `aOneHarpR` bar 9 `r4 <aes c' es'>2\arpeggio` → `r4 <aes c' es'>4\arpeggio <aes des' f'>4\arpeggio` (D♭ and F♮ are on the pedals). Bass D♭2→E♭2 rises while bassoons and horn fall (F3→B♭2, A♭3→E♭3, A♭4→G4): contrary motion as in Ex. 9-3a. I did not copy this to 25 and 73. There the cello counter-melody's C4 on beat 3 would form a minor 2nd with the violas' D♭, and the I6 can stay as variation.

### 3. Chord IV is repeated from weak beat to strong beat at bars 15–16. Applied.
**Problem.** The cellos and basses move C2→D♭ on beat 3 of 15 and hold D♭ into the downbeat of 16, and the violas move to F on beat 3, both under the same IV. A&S: avoid "a weak-strong progression within the same chord" and weak-strong bass repetition (PDF 149 §15, Ex. 9-24a "poor"; PDF 151 point 9). The oboe's answer phrase loses its downbeat. Its E♭ on beat 3 also sits as an unexplained 9th over D♭.
**Fix.** Hold I6 through bar 15 and move to IV on the downbeat of 16 (I6→IV by the step 3̂–4̂, PDF 141 §7): `aOneVc` `c2\pp des4` → `c2.\pp`; `aOneCb` `c,2\pp des,4` → `c,2.\pp`; `aOneVla` `es'2\pp f'4` → `es'2.\pp`. The oboe's E♭ becomes a chord tone, and no octaves appear (bass C→D♭ against oboe E♭→F, violas E♭→D♭).

### 4. The 7th of C♯7 rises at the move into F♯ minor (bars 47–48). Applied.
**Problem.** In bar 47 the violas hold B3, the 7th of C♯7 (V7 of F♯ minor), and move up to C♯4 at 48. A, the tone of resolution, appears only in the oboe (A4) and in the cellos two octaves down (A2). The cellos' A2 makes a close minor 3rd with the basses' F♯2 under the quotation, which is the "low third" problem cycles 4 and 11 left open at 61. A&S treats the 7th of V7 as a dissonance that is prepared and resolved (PDF 139 §6). It must resolve down by step, in the last voice that holds it (Unit 6, PDF 102 §15 and PDF 104).
**Fix.** `bSecVla` bar 48 `cis'1` → `a1`; `bSecVc` bar 48 `\clef bass a,1\pp` → `\clef bass cis1\pp`. The chord becomes F♯2–C♯3–A3–F♯4–A4, with the 7th resolved and the bottom spaced in a 5th. The oboe's quotation (protected) is unchanged.

### 5. The suspended 4th over A♭ in bar 43 resolves on the next downbeat. Applied.
**Problem.** Beats 3–4 of 43 are A♭–D♭–E♭ (viola triplets, Vln II D♭–E♭ dyad). The 4th D♭ enters correctly by step from 2̂ (PDF 153 §3) but resolves to C only on the downbeat of 44, so the resolution is stronger than the dissonance (PDF 154 §6; 160 point 3). Bar 59 is not affected: its G♮ bass makes it V6/5 of V.
**Fix.** Resolve on beat 4: `bSecVla` bar 43 second tuplet group → `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( des' es') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( c' es') }`; `bSecVlnTwo` `<des' es'>2` → `<des' es'>4 <c' es'>4`. The flute's B♭ on beat 4 becomes a 9th over V that rises to C.

### 6. Open point "half cadence at 14": close it without a change.
A&S's period (PDF 158–159 §14; 160 point 9) is already present inside 7–14. The phrase 7–10 ends on V (antecedent) and 11–14 on I (consequent), with equal lengths. The b phrase ends with its own half cadence at 22. A half cadence at 14 would give three half cadences in sixteen bars. After finding 2 the antecedent's V is approached by IV, which strengthens this period.

### 7. Not applied (for the composer to consider): an incomplete progression for theme B2.
A&S notes that beginning a phrase on II6 or IV gives an upbeat effect characteristic of Brahms and Schumann (PDF 150 §17, Ex. 9-26). Bar 45 (B♭ minor, after the A♭7 of 44) could open on E♭-minor 6/3 (ii6 of D♭ = iv6 of B♭ minor) on beat 1 and reach i on beat 3. I did not test this, because the cellos' octave doubling at 45–47 (cycle 11) would need re-voicing.
