## Findings

**1. Bars 87–90: the muted Hn 3·4 line has no slur, while the cor anglais it doubles is slurred.** (applied)
Problem: At 87–90 Hn 4 plays F3–F♭3–E♭3 con sord. pp, the octave below the cor anglais's slurred F4–F♭4–E♭4 "dolce" and in unison with the violas. Hn 3 holds A♭3, re-struck at 88 as the bass moves F→D♭. With no slur, each horn note is tongued, so the muted echo gets three separate attacks under a legato cor anglais. This is the softest and most exposed brass moment of the movement and the last sound of the horns before the scherzo.
Evidence: Adler PDF 309: "All phrases under one slur will automatically be performed on one breath", and a lyrical line is soft-tongued, as in Tchaikovsky 5/ii (X-24, slurred, "dolce con molto espr."). Every soft muted brass example in the chapter is slurred: Debussy's muted horns at ppp/pp (PDF 307, X-17), Mahler's muted trumpets into pp (PDF 320, X-48b), Debussy's "con sord." trumpets at pp (PDF 320, X-49). The passage lasts about 13 s at the rit., which is easy on one breath at pp (PDF 303: low notes are best sustained).
Fix (coda.ily): Hn 3 `R2.*4 | \partCombineChords aes2.(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." | aes2. | aes2.~ | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t aes2)\> r4\! \partCombineAutomatic |`; Hn 4 `f2.(\pp-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "con sord." | fes2. | es2.~ | … es2)\> r4\! |`. The `\partCombineChords` is needed: with the slur alone the part-combiner printed Hn 4 only and dropped Hn 3's A♭3 from the score and the MIDI (I tested this). With it, the staff shows one slurred chord line, as at 3–6.
**Ending change (83–92):** articulation only. Pitches, dynamics, mute texts, "via sord." at 91 and the last chord are unchanged; score.midi is identical. Nothing in the handover to the scherzo changes.

**2. Bars 80–81: Hn 3·4's suspension line is tongued note by note.** (applied)
Problem: Hn 3's F♭4 (80 b3, tied) resolving to E♭4 (81 b2) is the 9–8 suspension of c082. Hn 4 moves A♭3 (tied)–G3–E♭3 below it. Neither has a slur, so the resolution is re-tongued. Around it the counter-melody (Hn 1·2, Vc, Bsn 1) and the theme are all slurred. This is also the line that the muted Hn 4 echoes at 87–90 (coherence/mvt2.md), so the open statement and the echo should share an articulation.
Evidence: Adler PDF 309 (slur = one breath, soft tonguing for lyrical lines); PDF 304–305 (in Brahms X-8 and Beethoven X-9 the horn's stepwise falling figures are slurred). Adler PDF 311: the horn carries at f, so the slur costs no presence.
Fix (aTwo.ily): Hn 3 `f'2 fes'4~(\< | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t fes'4\> es'2) |`; Hn 4 `aes2~ aes4(\< | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t g2\> es4) |`. Pitches, dynamics and hairpins stay as they are, so the c147 four-horn chord and the c174 brass dynamics at 79–81 are untouched. The staff already splits into two voices at 81, so each slur prints on its own voice.

**3. Bars 79–81: horns f inside the ff tutti.** (recorded, no change)
Adler PDF 311 says the horn "does possess enough brilliance and carrying power in loud passages to be heard over almost any combination", which supports f horns being heard under ff strings and woodwinds. The grading (c034, c044) is protected by c174 and the reading gives no new reason to change it.

**4. Bars 79–82: the new cor anglais (c187) and contrabassoon (c184) dynamics.** (recorded, no change)
Cor anglais: ff at 79, breath after 80, diminuendo at 81, p with a niente tip released at 82 b3. This matches the violas it doubles and oboe 1 exactly. Contrabassoon: ff at 79, breath after 80, diminuendo at 81, p with a niente tip released at 82 b3. This matches the double basses and Bsn 2 through 81. At 82 it leaves with the upper winds, while Bsn 2, the bass clarinet and the basses hold to pp. That fits the staged release (c024, protected). Both entries agree with the tutti.

**5. Mute instructions and the scherzo handover.** (recorded, no change)
"mettere sord." at 82 gives five bars (about 17 s) to fit the mute. "con sord." stands at 87 in both parts, and "via sord." at 91 in both parts and the score. This follows Adler PDF 307–308 and 321 and the appendix term "Via Sordini" for taking mutes off (adler2 PDF 312). The scherzo (v13) marks "senza sord." at Hn 3·4's first entry. The mute is the right tool for a sustained soft echo; stopped horn would be the wrong colour (PDF 307–308).

**6. Horn solo markings.** (recorded, no change)
Bar 7 "1. solo, dolce espr." p, bar 53 "espr." pp (with Cl 1), bar 67 "1. solo, dolce" p. Each pairs a soft level with a character word, as in Adler's X-9, X-11 and X-24 (PDF 305, 309). Phrase swells end on stated levels where the line ends (14 p, 70 pp).
