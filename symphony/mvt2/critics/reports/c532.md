## Findings

No finding needs a change. In every place where the scans touch a rule of the reading, the score either follows it or breaks it for a reason HISTORY already gives. Below, I rank the places where the reading bears most directly. Finding 1 is new. Findings 2-6 add evidence to decisions already recorded.

### 1. Bar 9 b1: a tonic with a doubled third and no fifth in the horn solo. Follows; no change.

**Problem.** On 9 b1 the sounding notes are A♭1, A♭2 (harp LH, B.cl.), C3 (Bsn 2), A♭3 (Bsn 1) and C4 (horn melody). The root sounds four times, the third twice, and the fifth not at all, until the harp's E♭3 arrives on b2. PDF 76 says that when the fifth is omitted, "most often ... the root is tripled and the chord 3rd not doubled". Bar 7 (the same tonic) and the restatements at 25 and 73 are complete.

**Evidence.** PDF 76 also says exceptions must have "a reason—usually to bring about smoother voice leading". The reason holds here. Bassoon 2 comes from B♭2, the fifth of the E♭7 at 8 b3. The only other chord tone it could take is E♭3, but B♭2→E♭3 against the bass clarinet's and the harp's E♭2→A♭2 makes parallel fifths (Ex. 5-19). A♭2 would double the bass clarinet in unison. The other possibility is bassoon 1 G3→E♭3, which gives a complete chord, but then the leading tone in the solo's only upper accompanying line falls instead of rising. The fifth is missing for a single beat at pp, and the ear supplies it over a root-position bass (PDF 76: "the ear assumes a 5th above the bass"). I record this so a later critic does not try the E♭3.

**Fix.** None. If 7-14 is ever reopened, the one clean alternative is `aOneBsnOne` bar 9 `es2( f4)` (was `aes2 f4`). Bsn 1 G3 then falls to E♭3 and steps to F3. I did not apply it, because the horn solo and its accompaniment are on the standing list.

### 2. Bars 24 b3, 72 b3 and 52 b4: doubled leading tone in a thin texture. Recorded (c163, c282); I confirm no change.

**Problem.** These are the only places outside the tutti where PDF 76's "virtually absolute" rule (Ex. 5-4) is broken:
- At 24 b3 and 72 b3 (E♭7 before the goal I), G sounds in the cellos (G3→A♭3, the protected counter-melody) and in the violas (G4→E♭4).
- At 52 b4 (A♭7 before D♭), C sounds in the violins' pickup (C5) and the violas (C4).

**Evidence.** I re-tested c282's alternatives. Viola G4→A♭4 at 25 makes octaves with the cellos' G3→A♭3 (PDF 83, Ex. 5-18). A viola E♭4 on b3 would leave the A♭4 seventh of V7/V at b2 unresolved (PDF 76: the seventh must resolve). The doubling comes from two independent lines, not from a chord voicing, and the falling viola G is an inner voice. Dvořák does the same in non-tutti textures. My scan of Dvořák 9/ii finds 25 of 55 dominant-seventh onsets (six or more notes) with the leading tone in more than one octave, including six- to eight-note sonorities at bars 35, 71-72, 87-88 and 104, not only at the climax (94-95). The same scan gives this movement 29 of 46.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 58-60 and 81: leading tone and seventh in several octaves in the tutti. Follows (PDF 84-86).

**Problem.** At 60 the V7 of D♭ has C in six octaves (Fl, Ob, Cl 1, Hn 4, Vln I-II, Vla), and only the top line resolves to D♭6. The seventh G♭ sounds at G♭3 and G♭4. Bassoon 1, trombone 2, horn 1 and the violas resolve it to F, but the cellos rise to A♭3 (c294). At 81, G is in eight octaves and D♭ in four.

**Evidence.** PDF 84 ("Doubling in free textures") says octave doublings in instrumental pieces "are not parallel octaves" and serve "variety of sound or ... emphasis". The rule against doubling is written for a fixed number of voices (PDF 85: "Where the number of voices must remain the same ... such doublings should be strictly avoided"). The reduction has a single resolving leading tone (Fl 1/Vln I) and a resolving seventh (Bsn 1/Hn 1). Dvořák 9/ii at 94-95 (13-16 notes) has the leading tone and the seventh in several octaves on every V7 onset. Hn 4's C4→A♭4 at 61 stays as c392 recorded it.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 50-51: double basses B♮1→A♭1, a written augmented second. Recorded (c432); no change.

**Problem.** PDF 82: "The augmented 2nd ... should not be used." In the bass part (`fis,2 b,,2 | aes,,1`), B♮1 is the root of iv in F♯ minor and A♭1 begins the A♭7 pedal that returns to D♭. It is the only augmented second in the movement. The Bsn/Hn/Vla three-semitone steps at 58-59, 62 and 64 are all spelled as minor thirds (A♭-C♭, D♭-F♭, B-D).

**Evidence.** The step sounds as a minor third. It is the enharmonic seam of the protected pivot 50-51 (c432: "B1-A flat1 at 50-51 keeps its spelling"). Respelling B as C♭ would misname the B-minor chord above it (PDF 67, Unit 4: only seven letter groups form triads). PDF 82 states the rule for chorale settings. In a bass at a key change, the spelling that names each chord correctly is the better choice.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Leaps beyond the octave at 59, 63, 64 b3, 74, 79 and 83. Follows; 64 b3 added to c392's list.

**Problem.** PDF 81: "Leaps larger than an octave are not permitted" (Ex. 5-11b, "forbidden"). The leap scan finds:
- Vla A♭4→G♭3 at 59 and G3→B♭4 at 79;
- Vc E4→A2 at 63 and C4→A♭2 at 83;
- Vln I G5→E♭4 at 74 b3;
- Vln II C6→B3 and Vla C5→G♯3 at 64 b3.

The overlap scan finds the same 64 b3 notes, because both lines drop below each other's former positions (PDF 88-89, Ex. 5-31a).

**Evidence.** Each of these leaps either sits inside an arpeggiated figure (59, 79: PDF 81-82, Ex. 5-12, arpeggiation) or starts a new phrase after a held or tremolo note (63, 64, 74 b3 pickup, 83). In those cases the two notes are not one line in the chorale sense. At 64 b3 the strings leave the p tremolo E5/C6 and E4/C5 and start the "sul G" line B3-D4 and G♯3-G3 that leads into the retransition. c392 listed 41, 59, 63, 74, 79 and 83. I add 64 b3 so a later scan does not report it as new.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Parallel and hidden fifths and octaves: only doublings and recorded seams. Follows.

**Evidence.** Before the doubling-run filter, the all-pairs scan of 27 separate players finds 2,309 coincidences of perfect intervals. After it, 221 remain, and each one is one of the following:
- a bass doubling in a different rhythm: harp LH, Cb, Vc, B.cl. or Bsn 2 at 11 b3, 13, 21, 43, 49, 57, 74-77;
- the protected horn-cello unison into 30;
- the viola or harp figuration touching a melody note: 38, 40 b3, 42, 54 b1 (the harp RH's A♭4→G♭3 is a chord filler, PDF 85, Ex. 5-26);
- tutti doublings at 61-62 and 79-81.

These are the cases PDF 84-86 exempts. The new basses at 56 (c527) double the cellos' A2→B♭2 at the octave. Their A1 is the leading tone of the diminished seventh, and both octaves resolve to B♭ (PDF 84; Ex. 5-21 for the octave pair).

- 36 b3, a phrase seam with two players;
- 44 b4, where the top and bottom instruments change (Fl/C.a. stop, Ob/Vc start);
- 64 b1, a hidden fifth after the quotation, recorded by c392.

Ex. 5-29 and point 8 on PDF 88 ("use your ear in doubtful cases") cover all five. analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels.

**Fix.** None.
