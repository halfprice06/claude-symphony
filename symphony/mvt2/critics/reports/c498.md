## Findings

I found nothing that the reading shows to be wrong and that a change would clearly improve. Below are the checks that are new in this pass, ranked by how much they bear on expression. All are recorded with no change.

### 1. The cello 9-8 suspension added at 73–74 (c469) is shaped as the reading describes (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 55 and Ex. 3-16 and 3-17a: a suspension arises from syncopation or from lengthening a tone, and it is accented. PDF 51 and Ex. 3-6: the attack of a syncopated note is heard as stronger than its continuation, and Beethoven puts the *sf* on that attack.
- **Score.** `aes2\< c'4~)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp \> | c'4 bes8( aes8) g4\! -\tweak stencil ##f \p`:
  - the crescendo peaks on the attack of the tied C4 at 73 b3, the consonant preparation over F;
  - the diminuendo runs through the dissonance at 74 b1 to the resolution on B♭3 at b2;
  - the hidden p comes on G3 at b3.
- **Comparison with the plain statement.** At 25–26 the same contour has no suspension and the same hairpin shape. Only the dissonance is new, which is what c469 intended.
- **Verdict.** The suspension gets its built-in accent (PDF 50: dissonant elements sound accented), the resolution is softer, and no printed accent is needed on a held note. c288 found the same shape in the upper parts at 7–8, 23–24, 58 and 71–72. This is the first check of the cello line since c469 added the suspension.
- **Fix:** none. The suspension and its voicing are protected (c469, c479, c482).

### 2. The quotation's swell at 49 follows movement I's marking, not a generic hairpin rule (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 51: a note held from a weak beat through a strong beat conflicts with the metre. Ex. 3-15 and PDF 50: a dissonant neighbour carries a built-in accent.
- **Score.** C.a. 49 is `fis'4 d''2--\< cis''4\!`. The D5 enters on b2 as an incomplete upper neighbour and is held through b3, so it is a syncopation. The crescendo ends on the C♯5 of the resolution, not on the D, which goes against c288's pattern of peaking on the syncopated dissonance.
- **Why it stays.** Movement I marks every statement of the S head exactly this way: tenuto on the syncopated half note, with a crescendo into the next quarter. Examples are v421 tTwo.ily 36 `f'2--\< es'4\!`, dev.ily 20, 49 and 382, and recTwo.ily 128 and 241. A quotation should keep the shape it has in its source, and c048 copied that shape on purpose. The tenuto already gives the D its weight. Pitches, rhythm and dynamics at 47–53 are protected.
- **Fix:** none. Any change would have to be made in movement I too, and that needs a reason from the movement I thread.

### 3. The coda keeps its hypermeter under the new mute plan (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 53: in a four-bar group, bars 1 and 3 are strong. PDF 54: a phrase ends on a tonal goal.
- **Score (since c468, with violins and violas con sord.).** The coda groups as 83–86, 87–90 and 91–92.
  - **86 to 87.** The ppp→pp crescendo in the violas, cellos and basses reaches pp at 87 b1. That is the strong first bar of the second group, where the plagal iv enters and the cellos go "tutti" (one level up, PDF 53).
  - **89.** The tonic arrives on bar 3 of the group, a strong bar. Vln I enters pp con sord. on C6 with "rit." (`\mt 48`).
  - **91–92.** The two-bar extension carries only the morendo, ending ppp with the fermata.
  - **Clarinets.** Cl 1's D♭5–C5 on top (87–89) is unaccented, and Cl 2's pp at 90 (c478) sits on a weak bar.
- **Verdict.** Every dynamic step and the rit. fall on strong bars, and the weak bars only sustain. The plagal close is protected.
- **Fix:** none.

### 4. Bassoon 1's pp< p at 54–55 (c478) arrives on a strong bar under the violins' weak-bar steps (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 53: in a four-bar group, bars 1 and 3 are strong.
- **Score.** In 53–56 the violins and cellos reach mp at 54 b3 and mf at 56 b1. c378 accepted these weak-bar arrivals because they climb in steps toward 57. Bsn 1 (`des1~(\pp\< | des2 c2\p`) reaches its p at 55 b1, bar 3 of the group.

- **Fix:** none.

### 5. Theme B's dynamic peaks sit on chromatic harmony, and the 37–44 tempo now matches its chord rhythm (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 50: chromatic elements sound accented. PDF 49: felt tempo comes from how often the chords change and how fast the main tones move.
- **Score at 39 (Fl 1).** The hairpin pair is `ges''4(\< triplet es''4\> des''4`, so it peaks on b3. There the harp and basses move to C under A♮, the chromatic °7. The registral peak G♭5 at b1 starts the crescendo, and the harmonic accent at b3 crowns it. The pair spans one group, as in Ex. 3-7.
- **Score at 55.** The same bar in the violins crescendos through to mf at 56. The return builds, which is the reason for the difference.
- **c238's open point.** c238 said the Poco più mosso at 37 changed chords at theme A's rate. That no longer holds. The chords now change every half bar at 38–40 and 41–43 (harp and pizz. basses, Cb 41–42 in quarters), about 2 s each at ♩=60, against about 3.5 s per bar in theme A at ♩=52. The faster tempo mark is supported by the harmonic rhythm, as PDF 49 requires.
- **Fix:** none. c238's open item can be closed.
