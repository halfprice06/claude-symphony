## Findings

### 1. Theme B's first phrase has no head interval. Give it a rising-fourth pickup at 36, 44 and 52. (Applied)

**Bars:** 37 (Fl 1), 45 (Ob 1 + Vc), 53 (Vln I + II).

**Problem:** Each statement of theme B opens on a held F5 (D♭5 at 45), followed by the G♭–F–E♭ neighbour. Its first four bars stay inside C5–G♭5, and the G♭–F semitone comes back in 37, 39 (twice) and 42. Theme B's one memorable gesture, the rising fourth A♭–D♭ that the plan names ("Opens with a rising fourth"), arrives only in the fifth bar (41, 57). A listener therefore meets the weakest part of the tune first. This point has been open since c051 ("theme B's opening weaker than its second half") with no concrete fix offered.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 23 says to know "the melody notes the composer wishes to stress" and to make them dominate. The note theme B stresses is the downbeat F, and in this phrase nothing leads into it. Adler PDF 30–32 (Berlioz) shows a line passing from one colour to the next. At 36 the bass clarinet already rises A♭–B♭–C into the D♭ section, so a flute C5 on 36 beat 3 takes over that C an octave higher and leaps a fourth to F5.

**Fix:** With the pickup, both phrases of theme B open with a rising fourth: C–F in phrase 1 and A♭–D♭ in phrase 2, a third lower, which then climbs further to B♭5. The rhythm of the head (2 8 8 4, c031) is unchanged. Nothing uses a rising sixth or the S-head rhythm, and every pickup falls on a beat that was empty or held.
- trans.ily: new `transFluteOne = { R2.*5 | r2 c''4(\p^\markup \italic "dolce cantabile" | }`. In bSecFluteOne, 37 becomes `f''2 ges''8 f''8 es''4 |` (the slur, p and text move to the pickup).
- bSecOboeOne: `R1*7 | r2 r4 aes'4(\p^\markup \bold "1."^\markup \italic "dolce espr." | des''2 es''8 des''8 c''4) |`. The oboe plays A♭4 alone on the empty fourth beat of 44 over V7, then D♭5 over B♭ minor, so the deceptive cadence still works.
- bSecVc 44: `es2) r4 \clef tenor aes4(\p |`, and at 45 `des'2 es'8 des'8 c'4) |`. The cellos keep their octave with the oboe.
- bSecVlnOne: `R1*4 | R1*3 | r2 r4 c''4(\p^\markup \italic "senza sord., sul A" |`, and at 53 `f''2\< ges''8 f''8 es''4) |`. bSecVlnTwo 52: `es''2.\< c''4(\p^\markup \italic "sul A" |`, and at 53 `f''2\< …`.

**Layout:** The flute pickup puts the Fl staff into the 31–36 system. Bars 37–40 then no longer fit on page 2, and the score grows from 8 to 9 pages, which touches the 8-page layout listed in HISTORY. If the composer will not accept a ninth page, keep the pickups at 44 and 52 and drop the one at 36: I tested that version (/tmp/critic-071/t8) and it builds at 8 pages with no warnings. Forcing `page-count = 8`, or a page break after 36 plus another after 52, does not fit.

### 2. The A′ peak has the theme in two octaves only. Put the violas on its third octave at 79–82. (Applied)

**Bars:** 79–82, violas.

**Problem:** At the movement's climax (ff, 79), theme A's B♭ is in Vln I/Fl 1 (B♭6) and in Vln II/Ob 1/Cl 1 (B♭5). Below B♭5 the melody has no octave. Between B♭3 and B♭5 the texture is the counter-melody (Hn 1·2, Vc, Bsn 1 on D♭4–C4–B♭3), the held horns (F4/A♭3) and a viola tremolo on F4/A♭4 that only duplicates the horns. This point has been open since c063 ("violas on the theme at 79–81, three octaves").

**Evidence:** In Elgar's "W.M.B." coda, Adler (PDF 24) tells the reader to "notice the octave and double-octave doublings so very effective in obtaining a massive sound." On the Bruckner example (PDF 20) he says the climax is built "with octaves." His PDF 23 principle, making the stressed melody notes dominate, applies most at the peak. All the notes the violas give up are still sounding: F in Fl 2, Ob 2 and Hn 3; A♭ in Hn 4, Cl 2 and the harp; F♭ at 80 beat 3 in Hn 3.

**Fix (aTwoVla, 79–82):** `bes'2.~\ff^\markup \italic "unis." | bes'4(\upbow aes'2) | g'4(\> es'8 des'8 c'8 bes8) | aes2\p\> r4\! |`. The viola bowing matches the violins'. The release on 82 beat 3 (c063) is kept.

**Check:** allpairs_par.py shows only new octave doublings of the melody (viola with Vln I/II, Fl, Ob, Cl), and no new fifths or octaves against the bass or the counter-melody. The viola passes under the counter-melody's D♭4 only on the last two eighths of 81 and ends on A♭3 under C4.

**Protections:** This touches the "A′ climax 79–81" protection only in the violas. The melody, the octaves above, the horns, the counter-melody and the dynamic plan are unchanged.

### 3. The registral peak B♭6 is used up before 79. (Not applied)

**Bars:** 58 (Fl 1 and Vln I, passing eighth, f), 76 (Fl 1 and Vln I, the theme's appoggiatura, mp<), 79 (ff).

**Problem:** The listener hears B♭6 twice before the climax, and has held A♭6–A6 ff at 61–63. At 79 the movement therefore gets louder but no higher, and nothing in the register is new.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 20 (Bruckner): the fullest spread is saved for the arrival. PDF 20 (Hindemith): a colour is kept back for the later statement.

**Fix:** The cheapest change is to rest Fl 1 at 75–77 so that its first note in A′ is the 78 grace run into B♭6 (aTwoFluteOne: `R2.*4 | R2.*3 | r2 \afterGrace c'''4(\f { f'''16 g''' aes''' } | bes'''2.~\ff | …`). The violins keep the theme's own B♭ at 76. This partly undoes c033's "Fl 1 p< at 75", and 58 falls inside the protected build 53–61, so I leave it to the composer.

### 4. Theme A never comes back in its own colour. (Not applied)

**Bars:** 70 beat 3 to 74.

**Problem:** Theme A is first heard in the horn (7–14). Its recurrences keep only the horn's tail (67–70) and a counter-melody (78–82), so the instrument the listener links to the tune never plays the tune again.

**Evidence:** In Dvořák 9/ii the reprise brings back the original solo colour: the cor anglais returns with the theme at bar 101 (woods.notesL.ily). Adler PDF 27 (horns doubling a string line add "personality"), and PDF 20 (later statements recoloured).

**Fix:** Horn 3 rests from 63 to 78, so it can double Vln II's lower octave p dolce from the pickup: retrHnThree `R2.*5 | r2 es'8(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" f'8 |` and aTwoHnThree `aes'4. c''8 es''4~ | es''4 d''4 des''4 | c''4. bes'8 aes'4 | g'2) r4 |` before its existing 79–81. Horn 1 cannot do it, because it finishes the tail on 70 beat 2 with no time to breathe. This reverses c067's strings-only 71–74, so it is the composer's choice.
