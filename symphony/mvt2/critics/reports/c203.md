## Findings

### 1. Bar 36: no seventh on the tonic pedal before D♭ arrives (applied in the scratch copy)
Problem. Bars 30–36 are seven bars of A♭ harmony (I at 30, then the A♭ pedal 31–36 under "poco rit."), and D♭ (IV) arrives at 37 without anything that marks the A♭ chord as its dominant. The bass clarinet's rising A♭–B♭–C and the cor anglais' C5 pickup imply V of D♭, but no voice states the seventh. In pacing terms the transition stays static at the tonic and the change of key, tempo and metre at 37 all fall on one downbeat.
Evidence. A&S say any triad can be preceded by its applied V7 within the phrase (PDF 419, 439 item 1). Over a tonic pedal the standard move is I♭7 = V7 of IV (Ex. 25-19, PDF 428), and Exercise 1b (PDF 439) asks for exactly this case in this key: "A♭: tonic pedal using I♭7 as V of IV". This answers c020's reason. c020 kept 31–36 on A♭ with "no V7/D♭ bridge" so that D♭ enters by phrase modulation (A&S PDF 452). One passing seventh on the last beat is not a bridge or a pivot passage. It is an applied chord inside the phrase. The section stays on A♭, stays woodwinds only, and the common-tone diminished seventh at 33 does not change.
Fix (trans.ily, transBsnOne, bar 36):
`es2.~ | es2 r4 | }` → `es2.~ | es2 ges4 | }`
Bassoon 1's G♭3 on 36 b3 goes down by step to its F3 at 37 (already in bSec.ily). Against the bass clarinet's C3 (lowest voice on that beat, since the contrabassoon has stopped) it is a diminished fifth C–G♭ that contracts to the third D♭–F at 37 (A&S PDF 433). No doubled leading tone: C is in the bass clarinet only, and the cor anglais' C5 is the same pitch class in the pickup to the flute. Test: build clean, 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 8 pages; the G♭ prints in the Bsn 1·2 staff as "1." continuing. Rank: moderate. It changes one note in a passage where c020 made a deliberate choice. The composer should weigh the plain fresh entry of D♭ against the reading's tonic-pedal idiom.

### 2. Cadence hierarchy (no change)
A&S PDF 424 says the main use of chords applied to V is to stress the dominant "usually at a cadence" with ♯4̂–5̂ in the bass. The score keeps this device for the B-section build: 59 has G♭–G♮–A♭ under the melody's D♭6–B♭5, which is Ex. 25-12a exactly. The first statement of the same phrase at 43–44 approaches V diatonically (IV–V6/4–5/3). The A sections follow the same plan: 12–13 and 28–29 have diatonic IV–ii–V, and the final cadence 80–81 has an altered ii (iiø7 with F♭, PDF 422 §3). Each statement gets more intense than the last, as in Dvořák 9/ii. Adding ♯4̂ at 9, 12 or 25 would flatten this grading, and 23–30 and 75–82 are protected. Record only.

### 3. Deceptive cadence at 77–78 and the restated cadence 79–82 (no change)
PDF 427 says a deceptive cadence delays the resolution, the cadence is then restated, and the authentic cadence is stronger for the delay. At 78 the soprano reaches 1̂ (A♭5) over ♭VI (F♭), and the restated IV–ii–V7–I at 79–82 is the movement's peak (ff, B♭6). The model uses an applied chord to V in place of ♭VI, but the formal function is the same, and ♭VI carries the F♭–E♭ thread (protected, c023). Record only.

### 4. Coda pedal 83–86 (no change)
The fragment of §9 (PDF 428 margin) gives IV–V7 (or VII°7)–I over a tonic pedal. At 86 the D♮ chord (D–F–A♭–C over A♭) is a VIIø7 of V that does not go to V. It goes to IV6 at 87, so it is an apparent applied chord in the sense of PDF 430 §15: a chromatic passing chord in the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C. Making 86 a real I♭7 (G♭) would remove the tail's D♮, and the tail, bar 86 and the plagal close are protected and are relied on by the scherzo and finale (c190). Record only. This confirms that finding 1 should go at 36, not in the coda: the movement then has one I♭7-to-IV gesture over a pedal, at the formal point where IV becomes a key.

### 5. Tonicized area inside the b phrase 15–22 (no change)
19 b3 (VII°6 of F minor) – 20 (F minor) – 20 b3 (C7) – 21 (D♭) gives F minor as a real but brief tonicization, then deflects it to IV. Per PDF 430 §15 this is a varied I–(III♯)–IV, with vi as the "tonic", and the French sixth at 21 then drives to the half cadence at 22. The b phrase is the A section's only point of harmonic motion away from A♭, which is the right proportion for an 8-bar contrasting phrase. Record only.
