## Findings

Within this lens and this reading, nothing in the score keeps it from a master's level. Every place where Unit 31's techniques apply is either correct or protected. Every technique the score leaves out is one the reading treats as optional or as a threat to the tonic (PDF 572). The five findings below are records, and none asks for a change.

### 1. Bars 39-40 and 55-56: the minor-3rd chain of sevenths is correct; the bass register at 56 differs from 40 (record, no change)

**Check.** Both passages descend E♭m7 (b1) → Cø7 (b3) → A°7 (b1) → B♭ minor (b3). The bass falls by minor 3rds and stops one step before the full cycle, then turns to vi. This is the minor-3rd extension of seventh chords in Ex. 31-30 (PDF 575-576), and it stays tonal because it ends on a diatonic goal. Each seventh resolves:
- the D♭ of E♭m7 goes to C: Vln II D♭4 → C4 at 39 b3, and Bsn 1 D♭3 → C3 at 55 b3 (c257);
- the G♭ of A°7 goes to F: Vln II G♭4 → F4 at 40 b3, and Bsn 1 G♭3 → F3 at 56 b3;
- the leading tone A rises to B♭ in the bass.

The soprano runs in 10ths with the bass on each harmony (G♭/E♭, E♭/C, C/A, B♭/B♭), as PDF 554 recommends.

**The difference.** At 39-40 the basses play E♭2-C2-A1-B♭1, a stepwise descending chain in one register. At 55-56 the cellos carry the bass alone (basses tacet 53-56, c164), and their lowest string is C2. Their line is E♭2-C2-A2-B♭2, so the last step of the descent turns into a rising sixth. The 10ths survive as pitch classes: C2/E♭5 becomes A2/C5, which is two octaves plus a third instead of three. The bass, though, no longer descends through the chain as it did at 39-40.

**Why no change.** Every available fix breaks a recorded decision:
- the basses' entry at 57 belongs to the protected build order 53-61 (c164, c493);
- cello C3 at 55 b3 was c257's "before", rejected because it merged with Bsn 1's C3;
- Bsn 2 cannot reach A1.

The rising A2 is the leading tone, so it still resolves up by step. The only option is for the composer to reopen 53-56 for another reason. In that case the reading supports one change, Cb `R1*3 | a,,2\pp( bes,,2) |` in place of `R1*4 |` at 53-56, which restores the line of 40. I have not applied or tested it.

### 2. Bars 73-74: the cello 9-8 added since c462 is a suspension over V, not a chord root (record, no change)

**Check.** A&S PDF 553 says the dissonance in a suspension chain decorates the chord under it, and that "the lower tone ... does not function as a root". At 74 b1 the cellos hold C4 from 73 b3 over B♭1 in the basses and harp, with E♭ (Cl 2 E♭4, viola triplet E♭3-G3-E♭4) and G (violins). The sonority is V6/4 → V4/3 of A♭ with C as a suspended 9th above the bass, and C resolves to B♭3 on beat 2. It is not a ninth chord on B♭ or a ii chord.
- Approach: the C4 is approached as a consonance. At 73 b3 the chord is vi7 on F1, and C4 is its fifth.
- Outer voices: Vln I goes A♭5 → G5 against C4 held, which is m13 → P12. They meet in oblique motion, so there are no parallels.
- Cl 2: E♭4 → D♭4 on beat 2 (c479) supplies the 7th of V4/3 after the 9th has resolved, so the two dissonances do not sound together on the downbeat (c479's rule).

This is the Bach and Haydn practice of Ex. 31-2 applied to a single suspension.

**Fix:** none (74 downbeat protected, c469, c479, c502).
