## Findings (ranked; none applied)

### 1. Large plan and proportions match Kuhnau's model and Dvořák 9/ii. Keep

**Reading.** Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95): expanded opening I (Kuhnau 1–14, 42%), semicadence, expanded V (17–27, 33%) that "helps to shape the form", closing I (28–33, 18%). PDF 112: a section-long expansion needs a root-position chord.

**Score.** From the MIDI tempo map (c453's figures, which my bar ranges confirm): 1–36 (intro, A, transition) 125.4 s, 36%; 37–70 (B and retransition) 137.2 s, 40%; 71–92 (A′ and coda) 82.7 s, 24%. Each span rests on a root-position bass: A♭ at 7, 23, 31–36; D♭ at 37, 53, 61; E♭ 65–70; A♭ 71, 82–92.

**Corpus.** Dvořák 9/ii's conductor track gives A 1–45 (Largo ♩=52, 178 beats, 205 s), B 46–100 (Un poco più mosso 68, Poco meno mosso 64, Poco più mosso 68, Meno 60; 207 s) and return plus coda 101–127 (Tempo I 52, 108 beats, 125 s): 38%, 39%, 23% at the marked tempos. This movement's 36/40/24 is within two points of each. c253 made the same comparison with the same figures; I re-derived Dvořák's from the source and they hold. Dvořák has no dominant span before his return (c313); this movement's 20.8 s on V is the one place it differs, and it is protected (c073, c143, c253).

**Verdict.** Follows the reading and the main model. No change. Proportions stay closed (c023).

### 2. Cadence plan and the V7 goals at 44 and 47. Keep; correct c163's label for 47

**Reading.** PDF 99 §10 and PDF 103 pt 8: "V7 is rarely used in a semicadence", because at a melodic halt it "conveys an inappropriate feeling of activity". PDF 98 §9: the seventh must resolve. PDF 117: find the cadences first.

**Score.** Phrase map from the beat dump: 7–10 HC (plain V, G on top) | 11–14 PAC | 15–18 | 19–22 HC (plain V; E♭5–D5–E♭5 on top, so not the inactive 6-3g type) | 23–26 HC | 27–30 PAC | 31–36 tonic pedal | 37–40 | 41–44 V7→vi at 45 | 45–47 V7 of F♯ minor | 48–50 quotation | 51–52 V7 of D♭ | 53–60 build | 61 IAC | 61–64 | 65–70 V7 pedal | 71–74 | 75–78 V7→♭VI | 79–82 PAC | 83–92 closing tonic, plagal 88→89.

c163 recorded 47 as a "C♯7 half cadence" exception to PDF 99. The dump shows it is not a halt on V7: the whole bar sounds C♯–E♯–G♯–B, and at 48 the bass moves to F♯ with A in the cor anglais and violas. The violas' seventh B3 (bSec.ily, viola bar 47 `b1\fp\>`) goes to A3 after an eighth rest at 48 (`r8 a8-_`), in its own register, so the seventh resolves as PDF 98 requires. The "poco rit." and "poco sostenuto" mark the join, but the harmony is V7–i, the authentic motion PDF 98 assigns to V7. Bar 44 is the same case (c453): V7 moving to vi. So no semicadence in the movement uses V7. That is stricter than the reading needs.

**Verdict.** Follows. Record correction only: 47→48 is V7–i with a delayed but direct resolution of the 7th, not a V7 semicadence.

### 4. Where the bass gains 6/3 and V7-inversion motion. Keep

**Reading.** PDF 106–107 (7-1): a bass of only I and V is "rather primitive"; 6/3 chords give "a distinction between goals of motion and intermediate steps". PDF 121–123 (8-1, Schubert D. 935, also A♭ and 3/4): inversions of V7 move an opening tonic forward.

**Score.** Theme A's bass is mostly root position (A♭–F–B♭–E♭–A♭–D♭–E♭–A♭ at 7–10), with I6 only at 11 b2, 27 b2, 75 b2. That is the descending-fifths chain of theme A, protected with its harmony (c032, c580). Theme B's bass is the stepwise 6/3 line the reading praises (D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A–B♭ at 37–40 and 53–56). The one V7 inversion of the Schubert kind is the V4/3 at 74 b2, which joins vi7 at 73 to I at 75 and is how the reprise is shortened (c313). So the two themes differ in bass style, which helps the contrast between A and B. The reprise gets a touch of Schubert's method, not a change of the theme.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 5. Voice leading into the tonic at 70→71. Keep; correct c453's description

**Reading.** PDF 101 §13 (6-17) and PDF 127 §9 (8-9): avoid similar motion from 5̂ to a doubled 3̂; 2̂–3̂ against 4̂–3̂ is good.

**Score.** c453 (row 14) described 70→71 as "B♭→C rises against the falling bass and D♭→C". The per-track dump shows otherwise: the horn's B♭3 ends at 70 and does not move to C; the C at 71 comes from Bsn 2 D♭3→C3 (the seventh resolving) and from the violas entering on C4 at 71. The violins' last eighth is F5 (the pickup E♭5–F5) leaping to A♭5. No voice moves 5̂–3̂ in similar motion with the seventh, so the verdict stands. At 81→82, the real final cadence, Cl 1 B♭4→C5 rises against Bsn 1, Hn and Vc D♭4→C4, the 6-17a voice leading the book calls good.

**Verdict.** Follows. Record correction only.

### 6. The leading tone in the top voice at 52→53. Keep (recorded exception)

**Reading.** PDF 100 §12: 7̂ "tends strongly to move to 8̂, and if it appears in the soprano voice (an exposed position), it must do so."

**Score.** At 52 b4 the sound is V7 of D♭ (A♭1, C, E♭, G♭). The violins' pickup C5 is the top note and goes up a fourth to F5 at 53. D♭ arrives only in the cellos (E♭3→D♭3), timpani and harp. The cor anglais' G♭4→F4 resolves the seventh.

**Verdict.** The rule is written for a cadence in four voices. Here 53 is not a cadence goal; it is the start of the build, and the C5 is the upbeat of a melodic figure that changes direction after the leap (F–G♭–F–E♭, PDF 131 §13). The same pickup opens theme B at 36→37 (c071), and c302 recorded 52–53 as no change. A literal fix would be violins `des''4(\p` instead of `c''4(\p` at 52 b4 (bSec.ily, Vln I and Vln II), but it puts D♭5 on the weak beat before the D♭ chord, which is the weak-to-strong repetition PDF 102 warns against. It also removes the rising fourth that links 53 to 37. I do not recommend it.
