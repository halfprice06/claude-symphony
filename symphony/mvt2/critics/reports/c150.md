## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 87–89: the cor anglais leaves its F♭4 unresolved, and the resolution is passed to clarinet 2 with no common note (applied)

**Problem.** The E/F♭→E♭ thread runs from the attacca (movement I's E♮ falling to E♭ at bar 2) through 62–65 and 79–81 to the coda, and cyclic-notes lists "every F♭ resolves to E♭" as a convention. In the coda the line F–F♭–E♭ sounds in two octaves. The lower one (Vla sul C and muted Hn 4, F3–F♭3–E♭3) resolves in place. The upper one does not: the C.a. plays F4–F♭4 at 87–88 and stops, and clarinet 2 enters on E♭4 at 89 with no overlap. This is the only F♭ in the movement that does not reach E♭ in the instrument that plays it. The upper octave is also the pitch of horn 3's open F4–F♭4–E♭4 at the A′ climax (79–81), so the coda's soft restatement of that line breaks off on its dissonant note.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 125–126: when a phrase passes from one instrument to another, "the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one"; Ex. 237 (PDF 407) shows each new string part entering on the previous part's last note. PDF 126–127: across a change of group the progression of parts must stay "as regular as if there were no leap", "specially to chromatic passages". PDF 128: an echo in muted brass of a phrase first heard unmuted gives the distant effect, with a second instrument of affinity; c104 chose the C.a. for its affinity with the muted horns. Here the chromatic step F♭→E♭ is the seam itself, and the two instruments share no note. c140's table listed 88–89 as a clean dovetail. That entry looked at Cl 1 → Vln I on C5 at 89, which is correct; the C.a. → Cl 2 junction one octave lower is not.

**Fix (applied; sections/coda.ily).** The C.a. resolves to E♭4 under the same slur, holds it while clarinet 2 enters on that note at 89, and fades out at 90 with the muted horns it pairs with. Clarinet 2, the violas and the strings keep the morendo to 92 unchanged.
```
codaCorAnglais = { R2.*4 | f'2.(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" | fes'2. | es'2.~ | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t es'2)\> r4\! | R2. | R2.\fermata | }
```
The C.a. staff is already in the 87–92 system, so the layout is unchanged (8 pages). The allpairs checker now lists P8 C.a./Hn 4 and C.a./Vla at 89 as well as at 88. That is the same octave-doubled ♭6̂–5̂ line that c142 recorded and kept (finding 4 there). Before this change, clarinet 2 supplied the same E♭4 at 89. The chord at 89 is unchanged. An alternative closer to PDF 128 would give the F4–F♭4–E♭4 to muted horn 3 itself, the player who had it open at 79–81, with the C.a. on A♭3. I do not recommend it because it changes the c104 layout and c147's protected muted-horn close.

### 2. End of the timpani part: no retuning note for movement III (applied, part only)

**Problem.** Movement II ends with the drums on A♭, C, D♭, E♭. Movement III's first label says to retune D♭ → G "during the pause" (mvt3 v04 intro.ily). Movement II's part does not mention this. The player learns of it only after turning to movement III, which shortens the time for a quiet retune between movements. Movement II already marks its own retune at 5 ("muta in A♭, C, D♭, E♭"), and the Hn 3·4 "via sord." at 91 prepares their "senza sord." in III. The timpani are the only hand-over left unmarked.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 21, axiom II: "a composer's work stands the best chance when the parts are well written". Glazounov's first class in the same note: the orchestra sounds well at sight. MOLA PDF 5 (c075's source): a change is marked at the end of the previous passage, not only at the next entry. Retuning during 86–92 would be audible under the ppp close, so the note belongs on the last bar.

**Fix (applied; codaTimp in sections/coda.ily, part only):**
```
R2.\fermata -\tag #'part _\markup \small \italic \right-column { "muta D♭ in G" "(for III, during the pause)" } |
```
The score and MIDI are unchanged. Page 23 of the parts book shows the text inside the margin.
