# Critic 491: v491, lens 1 (melody and memorability)

Assigned reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 5 "Voices" (sebesky PDF 197-203, printed pp. 188-194), fourth series. Earlier passes on this chapter: c231 and c371, both with no change. I read all seven text pages in full and rendered all seven pages at 110 dpi (Ex. 259-268). I read the images, not the OCR. The OCR of the examples is unusable. The chapter is about choral and commercial vocal-group writing. I apply it to instrumental melody through four questions. Does each line move as a singer's line would? Does each theme stay in one voice's normal range? Does each line breathe where a singer would? How are lines doubled, held and inflected?

### 1. Both themes fit one voice's normal range; the notes outside it are the peaks (recorded, no change)

Ex. 260 (PDF 198) gives each voice a normal span of about a thirteenth and brackets the notes beyond it. Ex. 262 (PDF 200) narrows the span for small groups. Measured at concert pitch in v491:

- Theme A spans a twelfth in each statement: Hn 1 E♭3-B♭4 at 6-14 (analysis.txt: "Horns 1·2:solo Eb3-Bb4"), and violins E♭4-B♭5 at 22-30.
- Theme B's first phrase is B♭4-G♭5 (Fl 1, 37-40), a minor sixth. Its second phrase is A♭3-B♭4 (C.a., 41-44). Together they span an octave and a step.
- The notes outside a theme's sung compass are A♭6 at 58 and 61, A6 at 63 and B♭6 at 79. These are the two climaxes, so they act as the reading's bracketed exceptions.

A listener can hum either theme in one voice, as with Dvořák's tune, which also spans a twelfth. No change.

### 2. Breaths fall at phrase ends and before pickups (recorded, no change)

Ex. 261 (PDF 199) puts the comma after the held note and before the pickup. Ex. 259 (PDF 197) ends all voices together on a rest. Ex. 267 (PDF 203) breathes before a triplet pickup. In v491:

- Hn 1 `g2) \breathe es8( f8` at 10, with Bsn 1 resting under it (`es2 r4`).
- Fl 1 breathes after `bes'2)` at 40, at the ends of 58 and 60, and at 78 b2 before the triplet run. The run at 78 is Ex. 267's shape.
- C.a. breathes after `a'4)` at the end of 50, before the quotation's second half.
- Ob 1, Fl 1 and C.a. breathe after `aes2)` at 80.

The longest unbroken wind phrase is Fl 1's A♭6-A6 at 61-63 b1. That is nine beats at ♩=54, about 10 s at ff, which is playable. Cl 1 breathes after D5 at 86, which splits the motto tail E♭-D | D♭-C into two slurred pairs. That breath is protected (c226) and was recorded by c231 and c371. The reading gives no reason to move it: in 85-90 the clarinet holds 18 beats at a slowing tempo and must breathe once, and the break falls between two slur groups. No change.

### 3. Something moves under each held melody note (recorded, no change)

Ex. 261 (PDF 199) moves the alto under the soprano's whole note. Ex. 266 (PDF 202) moves the harmony pair where the melody pair holds. In v491:

- Bsn 1 (`aes4-- aes4-- g4`) moves under Hn 1's tied E♭ at 8, and the bass clarinet moves under its tied F at 12.
- The cellos take a new note on the violins' tied beat at 24 and 28 (`f2`, `des'4`) and at 72 and 76.
- At 60, Hn 1 and the cellos move `es4 f4 ges2` under the melody's whole-note C6.
- At 79-80, the cellos, Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1 move `des'4 c'4 bes4 | aes2 bes4` under the held B♭6.

At 54 b3-4 and 56 b3-4 the melody and the Hn 1 + Cl 1 counter-line hold together. c201 protected the 54 tie because it carries theme A's head rhythm. The reading adds nothing that answers that reason. No change.

### 4. Doubling grows toward the peaks in the order the reading implies (recorded, no change)

PDF 200 and 203 say that more singers on a line, or an instrument in unison with it, add warmth and body. Ex. 263 (PDF 201) doubles the lead an octave lower for a section sound. v491 keeps every soft first statement to one player: Hn 1 at 7-14, Fl 1 at 37-40, C.a. at 41-44 and 47-53, Cl 1 at 85-88. It doubles in unison at the second statements (violins at 23-30 and 53-56). It doubles in octaves only at the peaks, as Ex. 263 does: Fl 1 + Vln I over Ob 1 + Vln II at 57-60, and three octaves at 79-81. The harmony pair at 53-56 enters after the melody has started, as in Ex. 266, so the tune is heard alone before the counter-line joins. No change.

### 5. Memorability: the head cells return often enough, and theme A is the tune a listener keeps (recorded, no change)

Ex. 268 (PDF 203) gets individuality from rhythmic inflection of a plain line. The Dvořák tune repeats its head cell six times in twelve bars. Here is how often each head returns:

- Theme A's head (`es8 f8 | aes4. c8 es4~`) is heard at 7, 11, 23, 27, 71 and 75, and in the harp at 83.
- Its tail, the motto E♭-D-D♭-C, ends every A statement. It also sounds in the B.cl. at 32-34, Hn 1 at 67-68 and Cl 1 at 85-89.
- Theme B's triplet turn returns at 39 and 55.
- Its rising-fourth head A♭-D♭-F returns at 41, at 57 and in augmentation at 61-62.

Theme A has three things that make a tune stick. Its head rhythm sits on the same beat in three of its eight bars. Its ties across the barline give it the syncopated inflection Ex. 268 describes. Its B♭ appoggiatura peak at 12 recurs at 28 and at B♭6 at 79. Theme B's first phrase is narrow and mostly stepwise, the weaker half to hum. It is protected (c031, c151, c201, c361 finding 5). Its range fits Ex. 260 well, so the reading gives no reason to widen it. No change.

### 6. One register drop is the only place a sung line could not follow (recorded, no change)

At 74 b3, Vln I drops from G5 to the E♭4 pickup (`g''2) es'8( f'8`), an octave and a sixth, so the violins can climb to B♭6 at 79 in unison with Vln II. No single voice in Ex. 260 would make this jump. But the pitch-class line is unbroken, and Vln II has the same pickup. c299, c309 and c341 settled this register plan ("reopen only with a new book reason"). A choral range table is not a reason that outweighs the build to 79. No change.

### 7. Inner and bass lines sing (recorded, no change)

Ex. 259 (PDF 197) makes every voice a line, and its bass moves in slurred pairs. I read each accompanying line of 7-14, 23-30, 53-60 and 71-82 with comments stripped. I also ran a script (/tmp/critic-491/tools/leaps.py) over every section file to list leaps wider than an octave between consecutive notes. Apart from leaps across rests and false hits from text, it found only one: Vln I's drop at 74 b3 (finding 6). The widest inner-voice leap is Vln II's octave `fis'2 fis''2` at 48. That bar is inside the protected quotation frame 47-51 (c369), and it is a register shift between half notes. The cello counter-melodies at 23-30 and 71-82 are the clearest case of the Ex. 259 bass: they move by step and third and answer the tune's held notes. No change.
