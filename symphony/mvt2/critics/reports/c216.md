## Findings

### 1. Bass trombone part, bars 61–64: notes squeezed into the end of a line (applied)
- Problem: in parts-book.pdf page 21 the bass trombone's only notes in the movement (61–64) share one line with the multi-bar rests of 65–92. Bar 62's G♭2 and F♭2 half notes sit against the naturals of the A-minor key change; the mf of 61 sits under the 62 notes; bar 64's D2 and E2 quarters, both on ledger lines, are pressed against the double bar and the 3/4 change. These are the bass trombone's lowest notes, which are played in positions that Adler says must be known exactly (PDF 329, Ex. X-70: each position a semitone, E2 in seventh position; D2 below the tenor range of Ex. X-68, PDF 328, so on the F attachment). A player coming in after 59 bars' rest has to read them at a glance. MOLA PDF 7: "avoid over-crowding to provide clarity within each measure."
- Fix (part only; the score is unchanged): end bSecBassTbn with a part-tagged break:
  `a,1\sf\> | a,2\p d,4 e,4 | \tag #'part \break }`
- Result in /tmp/critic-216/work: the part now has 58–64 on one evenly spaced line and 65–92 on the next; still one page, no turn. Score build: 0 warnings, 8 pages with the same breaks, 0 outer-voice parallels. Parts book: 30 pages, no warnings; only the bass trombone page changes (parts-book.ly source is identical, since genparts.py takes the music from the sections).

### 2. Trombone 1, bars 63–64: no breath mark (recorded, no change)
- The quotation runs about 9 seconds at ff marcato (bar 63 at ♩=54, bar 64 at ♩=48 then 42) with no rest. Adler PDF 321: slurred notes are one breath; the separately tongued B3–B♭3–A3 at the end of 63 is where a player would breathe if needed. A professional trombonist can play it in one breath in the very strong register (PDF 329), and a printed breath would cut the line B–B♭–A | A that leads into the turn. Leave it.

### 3. Low brass entry at 60, pp (recorded, no change)
- Adler PDF 319 (trumpet, but the trombone is "a big trumpet", PDF 328): pp is hard to control in the low register. The entries at 60 are Tbn 2 E♭3 and B.tbn A♭2 (both in the "very strong" register of Ex. X-69) and tuba A♭1, all with a crescendo to mf at 61 under the full woodwinds and strings, and each part has a cue (c135). The risk is small and the soft entry is what the build needs. Leave it.

### 4. Trumpets tacet (not re-raised)
- Adler PDF 313 describes the Romantic trumpet's job as pedals and tutti chords, which the peaks at 61–62 and 79–81 do without. The tacet is protected (c060) for a cyclic reason: the scherzo's trumpets are their first entry since movement I. The reading does not answer that reason, so I do not propose trumpets.

No other playability problem in the brass. Every trombone and tuba note is in range (Ex. X-68), in the bass clef below G4 (PDF 328), reachable without fast long slide moves (Ex. X-70, X-71), and correctly phrased for breath and tongue (PDF 321). This change does not touch the movement's ending, exported themes or quotations, so the other threads need not be told.

Do not touch: trombone 1 held back to the single ff quotation at 63–64, the tenuto low-brass chord at 60–62 with Hn 3·4 a2, trumpets tacet, and the standing list including the 8-page layout.
