## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 49: chord changes over a bass held from a weak beat into a strong beat. Applied.

**Problem.** In v262 the cellos and basses play `fis4 b,2 cis4`. The bass B arrives on weak beat 2 under iv7 in F♯ minor (B–D–F♯ with the violin II's A5 as a 7th) and is held through strong beat 3. There the harmony changes to the Neapolitan sixth (B–D–G). The new chord on the strong beat has no new bass, and the bass B first sounds under a dissonant chord on the weak beat.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 149, §15 and Ex. 9-24: a bass repeated or held from a weak to a strong beat while the harmony changes "contradicts the metre". IV–II6 in that form is labelled poor. The pattern is good only when the strong-beat chord holds a suspension, and here it does not, because the G on beat 3 is approached, not suspended. Points for Review 9 (PDF 151) repeats the rule. Ex. 9-20 (PDF 146) shows the accepted way to join IV and II6: the 5–6 motion over a held bass from strong to weak.

**Fix (applied).**
```
% Vc, sections/bSec.ily, bar 49
| fis4 b,2 cis4 |   →   | fis2 b,4 cis4 |
% Cb
| fis,4 b,,2 cis,4 |   →   | fis,2 b,,4 cis,4 |
```
Beat 1 is now i. Beat 2 is VI6 by 5–6 over the held F♯, a strong-to-weak hold, which the book allows. Beat 3 is N6 with the bass B arriving on the strong beat. Beat 4 is V. The violin II line `a''2 g''4( eis''4)` is unchanged. c202's A5 still sounds on beats 1–2, so the third falls on the downbeat, and c027's rule that the upper strings do not move in the quotation's rhythm still holds. The Neapolitan, which is protected, is still there and arrives more strongly. The violas' off-beat A3–A3–B3–G♯3 fits the new bass. No stated reason in HISTORY.md protects the bass rhythm 4-2-4.

### 2. Bar 43, beat 3: the cadential 6/4 sounds the 5th of V against its 6th. Partly applied.

**Problem.** 43.3 is the movement's only cadential 6/4: A♭ bass with D♭ and F above, resolving to E♭7 on beat 4. In v262 the cellos play E♭3 on beat 3. That is the 5th of V, which the 6th (flute F5) is meant to delay, so the delay is not heard as one.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153, §2: the 6/4 is two non-chord tones, 6 and 4, over the bass of V, delaying its 5th and 3rd. Ex. 10-2 shows the 6/4 with neither 5 nor 3 present. PDF 154, Ex. 10-5: the normal doubling is the bass, the 6th is possible, and the 4th is never doubled.

**Fix (applied for the cellos).**
```
% Vc, bar 43
bes,2(\pp es4 c4 |   →   bes,2(\pp aes,4 c4 |
```
The cellos now double the bass A♭ (A♭1 harp left hand and basses, A♭2 cellos). c222's C3 on beat 4 is kept. The violas still have E♭3 in their triplet. c221 rejected the obvious alternative, viola F3, because it forms octaves with the flute. The remaining E♭3 is one note of a moving triplet on a weak part of the beat and does not sound with the 6/4 for the full beat. **Further option (not applied):** take the violas' third triplet note to D♭3 (the 4th, in a different octave from the English horn's D♭4). That breaks the doubling rule for the 4th, so I recommend leaving the viola as it is.

### 3. Bar 68, beat 3: a 6/4 on a weak beat. Not applied; keep.

**Problem.** Over the E♭ pedal, the horn D♭–C–B♭ and the bassoon B♭–A♭–G form an A♭ 6/4 on weak beat 3, which resolves into strong 69. Read as a cadential 6/4, this is the Ex. 10-6a pattern marked "never!" (PDF 154).

**Evidence.** Both voices pass by step, and the sonority sits over a pedal. That makes it a passing or pedal 6/4, not a cadential one, and the rhythmic rule on PDF 154–155 applies only to the cadential type. 67–70 is protected, including the horn tail (c043, c133).

**Fix.** None. If the passage is ever reopened, move the bassoon's A♭ to an eighth after the beat (`bes4 r8 aes8 g4`) so the 6/4 never sounds as a complete chord.

### 4. Bars 53→54: V6 moves to IV6. Not applied; keep.

**Problem.** Syntax rule 2 (PDF 148) says intermediate harmonies go to V and do not follow it. 53.3 V6 to 54 IV6 reverses this.

**Evidence.** The bass D♭–C–B♭ falls by step through both chords, and IV6 then moves on to V. The V6 is a passing chord between I and IV6, which is the kind of exception the book allows for chords on a stepwise bass (PDF 141, Ex. 9-12). c212 recorded it the same way.

**Fix.** None needed. If a stricter reading is wanted, label 53.3 a passing chord in analysis.txt and leave the notes as they are.

### 5. Bar 21, beat 2: passing 6/4 over the dominant bass. Follows.

The 6/4 at 21.2 comes from a voice exchange between the divided cellos and the violas, and resolves on the French sixth. A&S PDF 157 treats 6/4s that do not cadence as acceptable when their voice leading is stepwise. 21 is protected (c237). No change.

### 6. Only one cadential 6/4 in the movement. Recorded; not a defect.

**Problem.** A&S PDF 158–159 (Ex. 10-14) shows the cadential 6/4 giving weight to the consequent's cadence. Dvořák's Largo, the model for this movement, uses it at almost every main cadence. This movement uses it once (43).

**Evidence.** The main cadences at 10, 13, 26, 29, 60, 77 and 81 all put 7̂ in the melody on the downbeat of V. A 6/4 there would need 1̂ or 8̂ on that beat (PDF 156, Ex. 10-9), which would change protected melodies. At 51–52 the chord before V is V7, and Points 7 (PDF 160) forbids approaching the 6/4 from dominant harmony.

**Fix.** None in the protected cadences. If the composer wants one more, the best place is the last cadence of the C section before the return (around 63–64): put `aes,2` under `des'' f''` on the strong beat before E♭7. Test it against the 64→65 back-relating dominant first.

### 7. Bars 13, 29, 77, 81: the 7th of V7 is not prepared. Follows.

At these bars the A♭/D♭ 7th of V7 does not sound in the previous chord in the same voice. It re-enters by leap from above, which Ex. 9-9 (PDF 140) allows, and it resolves down by step. No change.
