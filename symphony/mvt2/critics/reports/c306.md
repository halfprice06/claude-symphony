## Findings

No change is needed from this reading. The items below are recorded so the next pass does not redo them.

**1. Contrabassoon B♭0 at 80 b3 is the lowest note of Adler's chart (recorded, no change).**
Written B♭1 is the bottom of the contrabassoon row on PDF 304. It sits in an ff descending line D♭1–B♭0–E♭1 (c184), one quarter long, followed by a breath. At ff the lowest note speaks; every professional contrabassoon has it (many reach A0). An octave-up fallback is not needed and would undo c184's octave below the basses. Keep.

**2. Bass clarinet low C at 8 b2 (recorded; closed by c156).**
Adler prints the low C in parentheses (PDF 304). The part already gives the small-note upper octave and the title says "(low C)". Nothing more to do.

**3. Timpani drum layout and both mutas (recorded).**
Against PDF 307: A♭2 on the largest drum (38" C2–A2, or c270's 30" D2–A2), C3 on 28" (F2–C3, top note), D♭3 on 25" (B♭2–F3), E♭3 on 23" (D3–A3). For the scherzo at 92: E2 largest, G2 28", B2 25", E3 23", all inside. The first muta has rests from 4 to the next stroke at 52 (A♭2 roll); the second is during the pause between movements. Adler's term "Muta in Mi" (PDF 312) is the form used. Keep.

**4. Strings at their open-string bottoms (recorded).**
Cb E1 at 62 b3 (open E, no extension, PDF 302 †), cellos C2 at 55 b3 (open C), violas C♯3 at 41 and 46 one semitone above the open C. All are standard and need no change.

**5. Soft low clarinet at 3–6 (recorded).**
Cl 2 sounding E♭3 (written F3) ppp, held in the c280 ties: one semitone above written E3, the bottom of the PDF 304 row. The low chalumeau is the clarinet's easiest pianissimo register. Keep.

**6. Top notes (recorded).**
Fl 1 B♭6 and Vln I B♭6 only at 79 ff; Fl 1 A♭6 at 61–63. Both are below Adler's "usually called for" tops (C7 flute, E7 violin; PDF 302–303) and arrive at ff. Keep.

**7. Language of instructions (not re-raised).**
English "soft sticks", "damp", "near the rim", "change bow at will" alongside Italian terms. Adler's own table (PDF 312) gives English first, so English is acceptable by this reading; c066, c156 and c295 left it as optional. No new reason to change it.
