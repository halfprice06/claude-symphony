## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Clarinet 1, bars 87–88: up-stems on a solo line above the middle line (applied)
**Problem.** Clarinet 1 plays the last motto tail alone from 85: E♭5 (85) and D5 (86) have down-stems, but D♭5 at 87 and 88 has up-stems. Clarinet 2 does not enter until 89, so there is no second part on the staff. The part-combiner switched to two-voice layout two bars early, because clarinet 2's entry at 89 is tied on. Bar 87 also starts page 8, so the reader sees an upper voice with nothing under it.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: notes above the middle line take down-stems. The two-voice layout (PDF 28, "double-stemmed writing") is for two parts that both sound. The same player's line changes stem direction between 86 and 87 for no musical reason.
**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, `codaClOne`):**
```
\tweak Stem.direction #DOWN des''2.(\pp | \tweak Stem.direction #DOWN des''2.-- |
```
A per-note tweak is needed because the combiner's voice setting overrides `\stemDown` (as c095 found at Bn 1 68). Bars 89–92 keep two voices.

### 2. Double basses, bar 42 b4: middle-line D♭ against the surrounding up-stems (applied)
**Problem.** The pizzicato line B♭1–E♭2–A♭1–D♭2 is written an octave up, so D♭ falls on the middle line. LilyPond gives it a down-stem, but the note before it in the same half-bar (A♭) and the next note (G♭ at 43) have up-stems. The same bass line at 58 already has `\once \stemUp des,4` (c095), so 42 and 58 look different although they are the same figure. The part shows the same thing.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: on the middle line, "continue the stem direction of surrounding stems that are in one direction only". PDF 28: within a bar, keep the direction of the notes in the same beat or half-bar.
**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecCb`):** `bes,,4 es,4 aes,,4 \once \stemUp des,4 |`

### 3. Cor anglais, bar 50 b3: B4 down-stem in a half-bar that goes up (applied)
**Problem.** In `cis''2( b'4 a'4)` the middle-line B4 is stemmed down and the A4 beside it in the same half-bar is stemmed up, and the line goes on up-stemmed through 51–52.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28, with the 4/2 example on that page: the middle-line note takes the direction of the other notes in its beat or half-bar.
**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecCorAnglais`):** `cis''2(\mp \once \stemUp b'4 a'4) \breathe`. This is inside the protected quotation 47–53, but only the stem changes. Pitch, rhythm, dynamics and slur are unchanged.

### 4. Bassoon 1, bar 55 b3: C3 forced down below the middle line (applied)
**Problem.** `des2 \once \stemDown c2` forces a down-stem on C3, which is below the bass-clef middle line. Bassoon 1 is the only bassoon playing (53–56). No comment in the file or in HISTORY explains why.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: notes below the middle line take up-stems. The rule about following the context applies only to notes on the middle line (PDF 27–28). The D♭3 on beat 1 is on the middle line with no clear context, so by PDF 28 it keeps the conventional down-stem.
**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecBsnOne`):** `des2 c2 | ges2 f2) |` (the `\once \stemDown` is removed). If the composer added the override on purpose, they should say so in a comment. Otherwise it should go.

### 5. Contrabassoon 79–81 after c184 (checked, no change)
The written pitches are D♭2–B♭1–E♭2. With the score header's "sound an octave lower than written" and `\transposition c`, these sound D♭1–B♭0–E♭1, which is what c184 intended. The MIDI range in analysis.txt is B♭0–A♭1. The notes need one or two ledger lines below the bass stave, inside Gould's "at least three" (PDF 21), so the stave does not need an octave sign or a clef change. The up-stems reach the middle line (PDF 28: notes on more than one ledger line). The part (parts-book p. 11) prints the same notes with the breath at the end of 80 and p at 82, and has no collisions. Contrabassoon parts are conventionally written an octave above sounding pitch without a printed note, so the part needs no text.

### 6. Clefs (checked, no change)
The score has four clef changes: harp RH to bass at 7 (warning clef at the end of 6), back to treble before the barline of 14, and cello tenor clef at 44 b4 and bass clef at 48. All four follow PDF 21–23: warning clef at the system end, clef before the barline, change after the rest and between phrases, and the usual clef restored before the next entry. Horns 3·4 go down to E♭3 at 29–30, 81 and 89–90, and clarinet 2 to E♭3 at 3–6. That is three ledger lines below the treble stave, which PDF 21 allows without a bass clef. c005's Hn 3·4 bass clef at 79–82 is no longer in the score. That follows PDF 21 better than the earlier version did (keep one clef where at most three ledger lines suffice), so do not bring it back.
