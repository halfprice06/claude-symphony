## Findings

### 1. Bar 20: Vln II's re-struck A♭4 lands on the oboe's resolution note (applied)

**Problem.** In 15–20 Vln II plays a soft inner pedal on A♭4 (con sord., pp, louré), struck again at the start of each bar. At 20 b1 the oboe ends its phrase on the same A♭4 (B♭4–A♭4, with a diminuendo), and Vln II strikes A♭4 together with it and holds it for two beats. This is the only bar of 15–22 where the pedal coincides with the melody's own note. At 16, 18 and 22 the pedal lies below the melody. The violas answer the oboe in this bar (C4–D♭4–C4–B♭3), so three things happen at 20 b1: the oboe's arrival, the muted violins' unison on that note, and the viola answer.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13: the arranger must see "that the listener's ear is directed to the primary element and not diverted or confused by an overbusy background". Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) shows the cure: take out the one line that adds nothing, and the remaining voices become clearer. Here the pedal's A♭ is already present in the oboe, so Vln II adds no harmony on 20 b1–2. It only thickens the soloist's cadence note with a section sound. My unison scan found no other new case in the movement (the others are recorded exceptions, see the table row 9).

**Fix (applied, sections/aOne.ily, aOneVlnTwo).** Was `aes'2--(\> e'4--\!)`. Now:
```
aes'2--(\< g'4--) | r2\! e'4--\pp | f'2--( bes4--~) |
```
Vln II stops after the swell at 19 and comes back on 20 b3 with the leading tone E♮4 of C7, which resolves to F4 at 21 as before. At 20 b1–2 the chord is F–C–A♭ (Vc F3, viola C4, oboe A♭4), still complete. The oboe's A♭4 is now the only A♭ sounding, over the viola answer. I tested F4 at 20 b1 as the alternative, and rejected it. Vc G3–F3 and Vln II G4–F4 from 19 b3 to 20 b1 make parallel octaves (A&S PDF 83). The rest also leaves the swell under the oboe at 19 (c139) as it was. The louré bowing elsewhere in 15–22 is unchanged.

### 2. Theme A's second phrase has the same harmony at 11–13, 27–29 and 75–77 (no change; recorded)

**Problem.** Sebesky PDF 19 asks that a melody not be harmonised the same way twice. Ex. 3/3A does this by giving the same tune a new bass line, which still reaches the same resting place. In this movement the first phrase of theme A keeps its bass A♭ | F B♭ E♭ | A♭ at 7, 23 and 71, and the second phrase keeps A♭ C D♭ | D♭ B♭ | E♭ at 11, 27 and 75.

**Evidence and verdict.** The movement already applies the principle where it matters most. 73–74 has a new harmony (vi7–V4/3, c060). 78 turns to ♭VI instead of the tonic (c023), so the A′ reaches its resting place by a different road, as Ex. 3A does. 79–82 is a new cadence, with the counter-melody against the B♭6. c057 proposed I6 at 71 and that was rejected; the A′ is protected (71–82, rising bass at 11–12). A vi chord at 75 is not a good substitute, because vi7 is already heard at 73. Do not raise this again.

### 3. Bars 57–58: bassoon 2 doubles the bass clarinet in unison, and both double the cellos' F3–G♭3 (suggestion, not applied)

**Problem.** From 57 to 62 Bsn 2 plays exactly the bass clarinet's notes at the unison (F3–G♭3 | B♭2 E♭3 A♭2 D♭3 | G♭2 G2 | A♭2 | D♭3 | G♭2 F♭2). At 57 the cellos begin their counter-line on the same F3–G♭3, so for two beats one pitch is in three instruments at p, above the basses' F2. c057 found four instruments on F3 here and took Bsn 1 out. From 59 (f) to 62 (ff) the unison gives weight to the crescendo and is justified.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12 (economy) and PDF 13 (Ex. 1: the lower-middle register lightened). Neither the colour nor the harmony at 57–58 changes when Bsn 2 is taken out.

**Fix (suggestion).** In bSecBsnTwo, have Bsn 2 rest for 57–58 and enter at 59 with the f:
```
\partCombineApart R1*2 | ges,2\f g,2 | aes,1\< \breathe |
```
I did not apply it, because of the c124 rule (no further background changes in 37–60). That rule's stated reason is that the background already changes colour every four bars. This change adds no colour and no new change point. It only removes a unison until the f. If the composer keeps the rule as absolute, record this item instead. Check the shared bassoon staff for dynamics: Bsn 1 also rests at 57–58, so the f at 59 is the only dynamic on the staff. Bsn 1's score-only spacers at 57–58 (`\tag #'score { s1*2 }`) must then become rests, or the staff prints empty bars. Move the "2." label to 59.
