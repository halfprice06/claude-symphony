## Findings (ranked)

None of these findings needs a score change. Where this chapter applies, the texture follows it. The two places that depart from it (F1, F2) are departures the record already gives a reason for, and the reason still stands. I applied nothing in /tmp/critic-597/work.

**F1. 37-60: counterpoint between groups is counter-melody, not imitation. Follows; no change.**
Adler PDF 271 says that because "each of the woodwinds has such a unique color, contrapuntal writing, especially rhythmical, is very effective". In Ex. VIII-42 (Mozart 38/iii, mm. 120-138, read at full size), oboes and bassoons answer each other one bar apart in plain quarter-and-eighth rhythm over pizzicato strings. The score uses the other kind of counterpoint the chapter names, independent lines in contrasting rhythm and colour. At 41-44 the C.a. runs A♭3-D♭4-F4-G♭4-A♭4-B♭4 in quarters under Fl 1's held notes and moves where the flute holds (41 b3-4, 43 b2-4). At 53-56, Cl 1 + Hn 1 play theme A's head in 3/4 phrasing against theme B in the violins, with the harp in triplet eighths. At 57-60 the horn/Bsn 1/Vc counter-line moves in halves and quarters against the theme's dotted rhythm and the violas' sextuplets. These are three rhythmic layers in three colours, which is what PDF 271 asks for. Strict imitation is absent by an earlier decision (c124 rule for 37-60; c457 F1 corrected the reason: Dvořák 9/ii does imitate at its 90-92). I do not re-raise it. The only place outside 37-60 where an answer would fit is the coda clarinet line at 85-89, and that is protected (c431: the motto tail in augmentation). Fix: none.

**F2. 57-58: B.cl. and Bsn 2 double the bass at the unison, with the basses an octave below. Follows by stated choice; record.**
Adler PDF 257: overlapping two winds at the unison "robs the timbral characteristics from both and usually only results in a stronger pitch. In full-orchestra tutti this practice is still common." PDF 265-266 says the same of at-pitch doubling. At 57-58 (p to f, not yet tutti) the B.cl. and Bsn 2 play the same F3-G♭3 | B♭2-E♭3-A♭2-D♭3, and the basses play it an octave lower (score page 4). Two things make the unison acceptable here. First, the line is the bass, where blending matters more than separate colour. Second, the two winds breathe at different places, Bsn 2 at the end of 57 and B.cl. at the end of 58 (c456, c536), so the line never breaks. Adler PDF 188 and Sebesky's rule cited there call for exactly that. One could put the B.cl. an octave down with the basses (F2-G♭2 | B♭1...), but then the line has nothing at 8' pitch while Bsn 2 breathes. The alternative is therefore worse. Fix: none.

**F3. 65-66: two bars of held V7 with no moving inner voice. Follows (pivot); record.**
PDF 255-256 says that when the progression is the point, it is scored by shade, dynamic and inversion. The grid gives Cb E♭2 / Vc B♭2 / Va G3 / Vln II D♭4, held for two bars. Its widest gap is at the bottom (a fifth, then a sixth and a diminished fourth), which is Ex. VIII-25's "good" spacing. No line moves. This is the hinge between the climax's release (63-64) and the horn's motto tail (67-70), and the dark, close upper voicing over an open bass is the "dark" case of Ex. VIII-29. Adding movement here would anticipate the horn. Fix: none.

**F4. 89-92: the final chord's third is in Vln I alone. Follows the protected link; record.**
In Copland's quiet doubling (Ex. VIII-41, PDF 270-271) the muted horn leaves its doubling to "emphasize the third of the chord" at the end. In this score the muted Hn 3·4 double the violas (F3/A♭3, E3/A♭3, E♭3/A♭3, 87-90) and leave at 90 b3. The final chord's third, C5, is only in Vln I, and Cl 2's E♭4 is the single wind colouring the string chord. That is Brahms's "melting" with a wind tint at the very end (PDF 252, Ex. VIII-18). Giving C to Hn 3 would weaken the exposed C5 that links to movement I and the scherzo (standing list). Fix: none.

**F5. 15-22: dialogue and the third-time rule. Follows; record.**
PDF 253-255 describes a gesture thrown between choirs and then summed up, with something altered the third time. At 15-22 Ob 1 and solo Vln I exchange two-bar units over the muted strings. In the third unit (19-20) Vln II drops out at 20 b1-2 and the violas take the oboe's rhythm. In the fourth (21-22) the basses enter and the other violins join on the pickup, summing up into the unison statement at 23 (score page 2). That is Beethoven's pattern in Ex. VIII-20 on a smaller scale. Fix: none.

**F6. Spacing and first inversions. Follows except the protected close triad at 7-14.**
Ex. VIII-25 (PDF 257) wants the widest gap at the bottom and the bass third of a first inversion doubled low. On the grid, the first inversions at 11 b2 (harp C2, B.cl. C3, harp RH C3), 41 b1 (F2 / F3 / F3) and 75 b2 (C2 / C2 / C3) double the third at the bottom only. The closing chord 89-92 is A♭1 / A♭2 / E♭3 / A♭3 / E♭4 / A♭4 / C5, with an octave at the bottom and close at the top. The only close low triad under held winds is 7-14 (B.cl. A♭2, Bsn 2 C3, Bsn 1 E♭3), which the harp's A♭1-E♭2 sits under. c317 and c457 recorded it, and the horn theme and bassoon layout at 7-14 are on the standing list. Fix: none.
