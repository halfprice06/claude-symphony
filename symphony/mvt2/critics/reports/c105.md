## Findings

### 1. Viola triplet figure 37–44 and 57–58, harp RH 53–56: beams too steep for three-note groups — applied
**Bars:** Vla 37 b1, b3; 38–42 (b1 and b3 of each bar, plus 42 b2, b4); 43 b1, b3, b4; 44 b1; 57 b1, b3; 58 b1–4. Harp RH 54 b1, b3; 55 b1; 56 b1. 22 groups in all.
**Problem:** Each rising triplet (for example A♭3–D♭4–F4) is one beat wide (about 9 staff spaces) and its beam climbs 1.18–1.65 spaces. Gould PDF 34: "To avoid creating a steep beam angle the beam of shorter groups should not cross more than one stave-space"; her example marks a 2-space slope "not" and a 1-space slope as correct. PDF 33: angles "usually cross no more than one stave-line". These figures run for 12 bars in the violas and 4 in the harp, so the effect is the lattice of slanted beams through the stave lines that PDF 34 warns against. The arch groups in the same bars (D♭4–A♭3–D♭4) are horizontal, so each bar alternates steep and flat beams.
**Fix (applied):** bSec.ily, bSecVla: `\override Beam.damping = #2` after the c015 `\stemDown` (line 176) and before the triplets at 57 (line 197); `\revert Beam.damping` with each `\stemNeutral` (45, 61). bSecHarpR: the same override at 53 and revert after 56. All 22 slopes are now 0.35–0.82 space. Both ends still sit on or hang from lines, because LilyPond's quanting is unchanged. Notation only.

### 2. Viola 60 b4: closely spaced sextuplet with a 1.7-space slope — applied
**Bars:** 60 b4 (G♭3–C4–E♭4–G♭4–E♭4–G♭4, semiquaver sextuplet).
**Problem:** The bar has a tighter spacing section (global.ily, c035), so these notes are 2.0–2.1 spaces apart. Gould PDF 34: "Notes spaced very close together horizontally (closer than three spaces) take only a slight angle (¼ or ½ space) regardless of the interval." The other seven sextuplet groups in 59–60 are horizontal. This one climbs 1.68 spaces with two beams, which is the steepest beam in the score, at the end of the build into the climax.
**Fix (applied):** bSec.ily line 203: `… ges16( c' es' c' ges c') \once \override Beam.damping = #6 ges( c' es' ges' es' ges') }`. The slope is now 0.32 space.

### 3. Flute 1, 78 b3: grace notes with down-stems, beam among the ledger lines — applied
**Bars:** 78 b3, Fl 1 grace run F6–G6–A6 into the B♭6 at 79.
**Problem:** The part-combiner prints the grace group with stems down at grace size, so the semiquaver beams lie across the first three ledger lines above the stave. Gould PDF 33 (Notes on ledger lines; image): "There must be one clear stave-line between the innermost beam and the first ledger line". Her "and not" example is exactly this: high notes whose beams touch their own ledger lines. It also differs from the same run in Vln I and Vln II on the same beat (up-stems). The player cannot count ledger lines through a beam.
**Fix (applied):** aTwo.ily, aTwoFluteOne: `\afterGrace c'''4( { \once \override Beam.damping = #4 \tweak Stem.direction #UP f'''16 \tweak Stem.direction #UP g''' \tweak Stem.direction #UP aes''' }`. `\stemUp` was not used because it would cancel the combiner's voice setting (see c095 finding 2).

### 4. Vln I and Fl 1, 78 b3: ledger-line grace run sloped 0.63 space — applied
**Bars:** 78 b3 (F6–G6–A6 in both).
**Problem:** Gould PDF 35 (image): "When all the notes of a beam fall outside the first ledger line, the beam takes only a slight slope, regardless of the pitches. Intervals of a second take a slope of ¼ stave-space". Both runs are seconds above the first ledger line and sloped 0.63. PDF 35 also asks for matching angles outside the stave.
**Fix (applied):** aTwoVlnOne `\afterGrace c'''4 { \once \override Beam.damping = #4 f'''16( g''' aes''' }`, and the same override in Fl 1 (finding 3). The slope is now 0.13 space at grace size. Vln II (F5–A5, inside the stave) is unchanged.

### 5. Viola stem direction 42–44, 58–60 against the majority rule — no change
Nine groups (for example 43 b2 D♭3–B♭2–D♭3, or 59 b1) have most of their notes below the middle line, so PDF 38 would give up-stems. They point down because of c015's single `\stemDown` for the figure. PDF 39 ("visual considerations") says that when a passage's groups span the stave, one stem direction may be kept so that slurs stay next to the noteheads. Here the slurs stay above the notes from 37 to 60. This is correct; keep it.

### 6. Bass clarinet 31: four-note group sloped 1.35 spaces — no change
E♭3–F3–A♭3–C4 is a long group (four notes over two beats), and PDF 34 allows "one or possibly two stave-spaces". It also matches the solo's rising contour.
