## Findings

### 1. Bar 10 b3: the half-cadence dominant has no third for a beat. Applied

**Problem.** At 10 the horn holds G3, the leading tone and V's third, for two beats, then plays its E♭3–F3 pickup into 11. On the same beat bassoon 1 and the bass clarinet rest for their breath (c036). The harp's G3 was removed at 10 (c057), so its b2 dyad is B♭2–E♭3. On 10 b3 the sounding pitches are E♭1 and E♭2 (harp LH), B♭2 (Bsn 2, harp RH), E♭3 (harp RH) and the horn's E♭3–F3: an empty fifth on the dominant of the first half cadence. No other V in the movement loses its third. At 22 b2 the cello's G→A♭→B♭ is passing motion inside V, and 26 b3 drops the fifth, not the third.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "Because of the empty sound and the lack of major or minor quality, the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects." The same page says the leading tone has an "active tendency toward 1̂". Here the empty beat is not a chosen effect. Three separate decisions caused it: the horn's pickup, c036's breath and c057's harp cut. The texture is five pp players with no strings, which is where PDF 87 (point 1) says thin spots show most.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-392/work).** `sections/aOne.ily`, `aOneBassCl`, bars 10–11:

```
  aes,2.\pp | f,4( ... es,4) | aes,2( des,4) | es,2 \breathe g,4( |
  aes,4 c4 \once \stemUp des4) | \once \stemUp des2( bes,4) | es,2. | aes,2. |
```
The old version was `es,2 r4 |` and `aes,4( c4 ...`. The bass clarinet takes G2 on 10 b3 and is slurred into the A♭2 it already plays at 11 b1, so the leading tone resolves by step to 1̂. The breath moves one beat earlier, to the horn's own breath after 10 b2. Bassoon 1 still breathes at 10 b3 and bassoon 2 at 12 b3, so the staggering stays. The horn line, the harp and both bassoons are unchanged. The all-pairs scan finds no new parallels. G2→A♭2 against the horn's F3→A♭3 is a hidden octave in a five-voice texture with the lower voice stepping, which is the mild case of PDF 87 (points 2, 5 and 7). The B.cl. already sounded A♭2 under the horn's A♭3 at 11 b1. G2 is written A3, well inside the instrument's range. The bass clarinet part still fits on one page.

### 2. Bars 60–61: horn 4's C4 (c364) doubles the leading tone and leaps a minor sixth into the a2. No change recommended

**Problem.** c364 replaced A♭3 with C4 at 60, so that V of D♭ has its third in the middle register. At 61 horn 4 leaps C4→A♭4 to join horn 3's protected a2 (A♭4–B♭4–C♭5).
- A&S Ex. 5-11b (PDF 81) marks a sixth followed by a step in the same direction "poor".
- On 61 b1 horn 4 also meets Vln II (C5→A♭4) by contrary motion from octave to unison, which PDF 86 asks you to avoid ("the same applies to motion between a unison and an octave").
- C sounds in six instruments at 60. Only Vln I resolves it to D♭6. Horn 2 and the violas supply D♭4 (PDF 76).

**Why no change.** I tested the alternatives against the reading:
- E♭4 at 60 b3–4 would give a smaller leap (a fourth then steps, Ex. 5-11c "good"), but it makes compound fifths E♭4→A♭4 against the tuba and basses' A♭1→D♭2. That is exactly why c046 took horn 3 off E♭4.
- D♭4 at 61 resolves the leading tone but breaks the protected horn 3·4 a2 (c064, c069, c086).
- A♭3 brings back the octave leap that c364 replaced, and the third is lost again.

This is a full tutti, so PDF 84–86 (free-texture doubling) covers the doubled leading tone. The contrary octave–unison lies between two inner instruments inside a 22-note chord. I record the trade-off so later critics do not reopen it without an answer to c046.

### 3. Bars 55–56: cello bass C2–A2–B♭2, a sixth followed by a step in the same direction. Follows in context

Ex. 5-11b marks this shape "poor" in a vocal line. Here it is a bass line, which PDF 80 says is "often quite disjunct". The A2 is the leading tone of the diminished seventh and has to resolve up to B♭2. The basses rest at 53–56, so A1 (the shape of 39–40) is below the cello's range. c257 gave a reason for C2 at 55 b3: it keeps the bass apart from bassoon 1's C3. No change.

### 4. Bar 47: a tenth between bassoon 2's A♭2 and the violas' B3 for the whole bar. Follows

PDF 77 limits adjacent upper voices to an octave "especially if continued beyond one or two chords". At 47 (C♯7 = D♭7, fp, the goal of 45–47) bassoon 2's G♯/A♭2 sits a fifth above the bass and acts as part of the bass sonority. The gap above it follows the harmonic series, as c334 recorded. PDF 77 itself allows the tenor up to two octaves from the bass, and PDF 79 (Ex. 5-10) treats wide instrumental spacing as expressive. Recorded only.

### 5. Bar 76 b1–2: clarinet 2 and the viola figure both move A♭4→F4. Follows

Clarinet 2 holds A♭4 on b1 and falls to F4 on b2. The violas' eighth-note figure reaches A♭4 on the off-beat and F4 on b2, so the two parts meet in unison for half a beat. The viola figure is accompaniment figuration, and PDF 85 (Ex. 5-26) says chord fillers "do not count as forbidden parallels". PDF 85 (Ex. 5-24) also says unison doublings "occur frequently in chamber and orchestral music". No change. This keeps c252's clarinet line.
