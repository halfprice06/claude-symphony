## Findings

### 1. Bar 44 b2–b4: the sounding chord is VII6 of D♭, not a root-position V7. Correctly voiced; record it (new)

**Situation.** The basses and harp strike A♭1/A♭2 only on 44 b1 (pizzicato, then a rest to change to arco for 45). From b2 the lowest sounding note is the cellos' E♭3, with the violas' G♭3, the cor anglais's C4 and flute 1's E♭5 above it. What sounds is C–E♭–G♭ over E♭: VII6 of D♭. c352's list counted 44 among the root-position cadential dominants, and c453 called it V7. Both are true of b1 only.

**Evidence.** VII6 "contains the three upper tones of V7" and keeps its function (PDF 112 §9), so the half cadence still reads as dominant. The doubling is the one §10 recommends: the bass tone 2̂ (E♭) is doubled by the cellos and flute 1, and the leading tone C appears once, in the cor anglais. The tritone G♭3–C4 is an augmented 4th. At 45 the violas take G♭3→F3 (4̂–3̂), and the leading tone's resolution D♭4 comes in its own register in bassoon 1 and the cellos (Ex. 7-14, PDF 113). The goal is vi, not I, so this is the deceptive turn c453 described. The voice leading follows the summary box (PDF 115), and nothing is doubled that should not be.

**Fix.** None. A sustained bass A♭ would need arco at 44 and would break the protected pizz./arco timing at 40–45. The note corrects the label only, so later critics do not count 44 as a root-position V.

### 2. Bar 59 b3–b4: V6/5 of V and vii°7/V over the bass G. Record only

At 59 b3 the chord is E♭7 over G (V6/5 of A♭, the V of D♭). At b4, F♭ (written fes in the violas, horn 1, bassoon 1 and cellos) replaces E♭ and makes vii°7/V over the same G. The leading tone G is in the contrabasses, bass clarinet and bassoon 2, which are octave doublings of the one bass line. No upper voice holds G, so PDF 108 §4 is kept. F♭ sits in four parts in two octaves, and every one falls to E♭ at 60. Unit 7 does not cover the diminished seventh, and "diminished seventh at 59" is on the do-not-touch list. No change.

### 3. I6 openings at 41 and 57 follow vi, not a held tonic. Correction to c352; keep

c352 wrote that the 6/3 openings at 15, 41 and 57 "continue a harmony already sounding". That is true at 15 (I at 14) but not at 41 and 57. Both follow B♭ minor (vi) at 40 b3–4 and 56 b3–4. At 57 b1 only F and A♭ sound, and D♭ arrives with the melody on b2. The placement still follows PDF 109 §5: each period opens on a root-position tonic (37 and 53, D♭ in the bass), and the I6 marks the second phrase as an intermediate step, not a new beginning (PDF 106–107 §1). This is the same plan as Ex. 7-6, where Schumann's I6 opens an inner phrase. No change.

### 4. Bars 14–16 and 11, 27, 75: I–I6–IV–V arpeggiation. Matches the book and Dvořák

The bass A♭ (14) – C (15) – D♭ – E♭ (16) arpeggiates the tonic and continues by step to V, as in Ex. 7-8a and b (PDF 110 §6). In Dvořák 9/ii the lower cellos play D♭–F | G♭–A♭–D♭ at bars 9–10 (strings.notesL.ily, `cellomL`). That is the same I–I6 | IV–V–I with the same bass rhythm, in the same key area as this movement's middle section. Bars 11, 27 and 75 use I–I6–IV in one bar. 75 has the voice exchange (Ex. 7-4b), and 11 and 27 have the held 8–6 top (Ex. 7-4g). No change.

### 5. Weak-to-strong scan (Ex. 7-18): two cases to add to c352's list, both acceptable

Besides 11→12, 27→28, 75→76 (IV tied over with the theme's syncopation, re-struck on the downbeat: Ex. 7-18c), 79→80 (hemiola) and 14→15 (the tonic arrives on 14 b1), the scan finds 38 b3–4 → 39 b1 and 54 b3–4 → 55 b1. In both, ii6 over G♭ becomes ii7 over E♭. They are not Ex. 7-18a. The ii6 enters on the half-bar strong beat of 4/4, so no new chord sits on a weak beat. The bass moves (G♭→E♭, part of the protected falling-third bass), and the melody takes a new note on the downbeat (G♭5 at 39 and 55), which supplies the emphasis Ex. 7-18c allows. No change.

### 6. Hidden octaves on theme A's pickup at 10→11 and 22→23. Keep

The scan of outer voices finds two hidden octaves with a leaping top. Both are the pickup F→A♭ over E♭→A♭: horn 1 over the harp and bass clarinet at 10→11, and the violins over the basses at 22→23. Both fall on the seam into a new phrase, and PDF 118 allows perfect consonances "at points of articulation". The reading's remedy for octaves in a I–V–I frame is V6 (Ex. 7-9c, PDF 111). It does not apply here, because 10 and 22 are half cadences, which need a root-position V (PDF 110 §7), and 7–14 is the protected horn solo. At 26→27 and 70→71 the bass falls while the melody rises, so those are clean. No change.

### 7. VII6 at 19 and 74, V6/5 at 53: unchanged since c352

I rechecked all three against the images of Ex. 7-13a, 7-15 and 7-10b. At 19 b3, G is doubled and the single E4 leaps to C4 to complete the F-minor chord (§11 exception). At 74, B♭ is doubled, D♭4→E♭4 against G→A♭ (A4 to P4), and the violas' G4 eighth is recorded by c352. At 53 b3, C is in the cellos only, and the 7th G♭5 resolves on 53 b3.5. No change.
