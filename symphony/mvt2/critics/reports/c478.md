## Findings

### 1. Bars 54-56: bassoon 1 holds pp through the build while every other part grows (applied)
Problem: at 53-56 ("poco a poco animando") the violins and cellos go p< mp (54) < mf (56), clarinet 1 pp< p (55 b3), horn 1 pp< mp, the harp pp to p at 55. Bassoon 1, which enters at 54 with the sustained harmonic basis (D♭3-C3-G♭3-F3, c107), has a single pp and no crescendo. Under strings at mf it stops being heard at 55-56, and the build has one static part in it.
Evidence: Rimsky says prolonged crescendi are obtained by the players' own crescendo combined with added instruments (PDF 130). In the full-score crescendos he prints as examples, the held parts carry the cresc. at the same point as the moving ones: Ex. 256 (PDF 419, Fag. pp, then "cresc. poco" with the rest), Ex. 261 (PDF 428, every held wind and brass part "cresc." over the stationary chord). The basis should stay one level below the melody (PDF 133), so the bassoon should rise only to p, matching clarinet 1, which it sits under.
Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecBsnOne`, bar 54-56): `R1 | des1~(\pp\< | des2 c2\p | ges2 f2) |`. Bassoon 2 rests 53-56, so the shared staff has one dynamic line.

### 2. Bars 89-90: clarinet 2's niente swell has no printed goal in the score (applied)
Problem: clarinet 2 enters at 89 from niente (`circled-tip` hairpin) and starts its diminuendo at 90. The pp at 90 is tagged part-only (`-\tag #'part \pp`), so the conductor's score shows a crescendo from nothing to an unstated level. Clarinet 1 above it is at pp from 87, and the part says pp, so score and part disagree.
Evidence: the clarinet's morendo is the device Rimsky names for the end of a diminuendo (PDF 130); in his examples each cresc. reaches a printed level (Ex. 256: "p cresc." to "mf"; Ex. 263: "cresc." to sf). The score is what the conductor balances from.
Fix (coda.ily, `codaClTwo`, bar 90): `\after 2 \! es'2.~\pp \>` (drop `-\tag #'part`). It prints under the lower voice at 90 b1 without collision.

### 3. Bars 53-61: order of addition in the long crescendo (checked, no change)
Strings and harp at 53, clarinet, horn and bassoon 1 at 53-54, the full woodwind and the violas and basses at 57, brass and timpani at 60, all to ff at 61. This is Rimsky's strings-woodwind-brass order (PDF 130) and the cresc.-sf layout into 61 (Ex. 255). The flute and oboe entering p under violins at mf at 57 is the practice of Ex. 256-257, where new parts enter a level lower and catch up. No change.

### 4. Bars 79-83: the release (checked, no change)
Hn 3·4 stop at the end of 81, then woodwinds and Hn 1·2 fade at 82 with the upper strings, and only the low strings and harp remain at 83. Brass first, strings last, as PDF 130 asks, and the converging rule of PDF 131 (keep the stationary low group) holds. Protected since c218.

### 5. Bars 61-64: woodwinds leave before the trombone (recorded, no change)
At 63 the woodwinds and horns stop at once while Tbn 1 enters ff and the strings play sf> tremolo. That is the reverse of Rimsky's diminuendo order (brass before woodwind). It is a subito change of colour for the quotation, not a diminuendo, and the quotation and its scoring are protected (c153, c213, c248 Finding 3). No change.

### 6. Bars 87-92: coda morendo (checked, no change)
Clarinet 2 carries the last diminuendo to ppp over the strings' pp>ppp (PDF 130: clarinets "decreasing their tone to a breath"). The C.a. and muted Hn 3·4 drop out at 90, before the clarinet and strings, which is the converging order of PDF 131. No change.
