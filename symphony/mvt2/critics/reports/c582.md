## Findings (ranked)

None of these needs a change. The harmony already does what this reading teaches, so the findings record what I checked and why it stands.

### 1. Bar 24 b3: the interlocking chain leaves the leading tone doubled (recorded, no change)

At 24 b3 the voicing is E♭3 (Cb), G3 (Vc), B♭3 (Vla lower), G4 (Vla upper), D♭5 (violins). The upper viola's G4 is the correct resolution of the 7th A♭4 of B♭7 (Ex. 24-3, PDF 400). The cellos' G3 is the counter-melody's own note. So the leading tone is doubled, and the viola's G4 then falls to E♭4. Moving the G4 up to A♭4 would make octaves G-A♭ with the cellos.

Ex. 24-3 says a free texture can keep every chord of an interlocking chain complete. It does not lift the rule against doubling the leading tone (Points 3, PDF 439), so this is a real exception. c352 and c532 recorded it, the passage is protected (the plain first statement 23-30 and the chains at 8/24/72), and the only fix would alter the cello counter-melody. At 72 the cellos alone have the leading tone, and at 8 Bsn 1 alone. No change.

### 2. Bars 41 and 57, b3-4: F over G♭ is an upward-resolving dissonance, not an unresolved 7th of IV7 (recorded)

At both places the melody holds F (C.a. F4 at 41; Fl, Ob and violins F5/F6 at 57) over the bass G♭ for a dotted quarter, then steps up to G♭ at b4.5 before the leap to A♭. The F does not leave by leap. It resolves up by step to the root, as the passing G does in A&S's Tristan example (§13, PDF 411-412, Ex. 24-19). There a sonority that looks like a seventh chord continues a triad and its dissonance rises. So this is an apparent seventh. c112 rejected "IV-vi7 at 41 and 57", and this unit supports that rejection.

### 3. Bar 60 b4 to 61: the cellos leave the 7th G♭3 upward (recorded)

At 60 b3-4 the V7 of D♭ has its 7th G♭3 in Bsn 1, Tbn 1, Vla and Vc. Bsn 1 and Tbn 1 resolve it to F3 at 61, and the horns resolve G♭4 to F4. The cellos go G♭3-A♭3 to begin their ff arpeggio A♭3-D♭4-F4-F♭4. This is A&S's first case of a transferred resolution: a voice moves up to reach the start of a theme, and "the tone of resolution appears in the same register" in another voice (§9, PDF 406-407, Ex. 24-11). Here the resolution is at the same pitch, in the bassoon and trombone. Follows.

### 4. Bar 74: changes since c442 follow Unit 24 (recorded)

c469 added the cellos' C4 9-8 suspension, c479 the clarinet 2 E♭4-D♭4, and c502 the viola G3-E♭4. Together they make 74 a V6/4 with a 9-8 suspension, then V4/3, then V6/4 into I at 75:
- The suspension is prepared at 73 b3 and resolves on b2.
- The 7th D♭4 enters in clarinet 2 as a passing tone from E♭4 (§7, PDF 406).
- Clarinet 2 rests at b3. The violas take the D♭4 at b3.5 and resolve it to C4 at 75.

This is the transferred 7th of Ex. 24-14b (PDF 408), not a dropped dissonance. c562's bassoon 2 at 11 (C3-E♭3-D♭3), c472's Vln II E♭4 at 39 b4 and c527's Cb A1-B♭1 at 56 also follow. The A1 doubles the cellos' bass leading tone an octave lower, and both resolve to B♭.

### 5. Bars 37-40: falling-third bass with a 7th resolved into the bass (recorded)

The bass falls D♭-B♭-G♭-E♭-C-A, then rises to B♭. This is the descending-3rds pattern of Ex. 24-9 (PDF 406) and Ex. 25-36b (PDF 438):
- Vln II's D♭4 is the 7th of E♭m7 at 39 b1 and is held through b2 to C4 at b3.
- At 39 b3-4 (Cø7), the viola figure's B♭3 goes to C4 at 40, while the bass takes the resolution A1. This is the transfer into the bass of Ex. 24-12 (PDF 406-407). The A then rises to B♭ at 40 b3.
- A&S warn that root-position applied chords over a disjunct bass sound choppy "without figuration" (PDF 438). The viola triplets and Vln II's held chords supply that figuration, as c442 also found.

Follows.

### 6. Bars 77 b3 to 78: the deceptive cadence and its cross relation (recorded)

The soprano C6 at 77 b3 is followed by C♭4 in Bsn 1 and the cellos at 78. That is a cross relation between the top voice and an inner voice, which Ex. 25-4 asks us to avoid. But the soprano moves by step, C6-B♭5-A♭5 (the eighths at 77 b3-3.5). That is exception (c) in Points 4 (PDF 439) and Ex. 25-3c (PDF 420). The C♭-C♮ that follows (78 b1 to b3) stays inside the cello line, as Ex. 25-3a asks. The progression ♭VI, passing 6/3, ii6/5, iiø7, V♭9, I is the pattern of §8 (PDF 427, Ex. 25-16): a deceptive cadence followed by a restated V and a stronger authentic close. Follows.

### 7. Bar 58 b3-4: C to C♭ is split between the clarinets (recorded, no change)

In the chain A♭7-D♭7-G♭ (Ex. 25-27 to 25-29, PDF 431-433), A♭7's 3rd C5 should become D♭7's 7th C♭5 chromatically. The score splits this between two voices: Cl 1 goes C5-D♭5 and Cl 2 goes A♭4-C♭5. That is a cross relation, but between adjacent upper voices in the same register, which A&S call mild and frequent (PDF 421, Ex. 25-5; Points 4b, PDF 439). The C♭ resolves to B♭4 at 59, and the clarinets' 57-59 octave is protected (c087, build 53-61). Keeping the chromatic step in Cl 1 would need Cl 2 to cross above it. No change.
