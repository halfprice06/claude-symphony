## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 43, beat 3: the viola's E♭3 sounds the 5th of V on the beat of the movement's only cadential 6/4. Applied.

**Problem.** 43.3 is A♭ bass with D♭4 (cor anglais) and F5 (flute 1), resolving to A♭7 on beat 4. In v422 the violas pluck `es8 aes es` on that beat, so the first triplet is E♭3. The sonority on the beat is A♭–E♭–D♭–F, a dominant with an added 6th and 4th, not a 6/4. The flute's F5–E♭5 is meant to be heard as a delay of the 5th, but the 5th is already sounding two octaves below it.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 153 §2 and Ex. 10-2b derive the cadential 6/4 by "delay[ing] the 5th of the V chord by first using a sixth". The chord is defined by the absence of the 5th and 3rd. Every 6/4 in Ex. 10-2, 10-3, 10-4 and 10-5 (PDF 153–154) has bass, 6th and 4th only, and Ex. 10-5a names the bass as the best tone to double. PDF 154 §6 says that when the 6/4 resolves within a divided beat, "the 6/4 will appear on the strong part of the beat and its resolution on a weaker part". An E♭ on the first triplet reverses that order. History: c192 had A♭3 on this beat ("the bass is the doubled tone"). c221 changed the figure to E♭3–A♭3–E♭3 to keep it under the solo and so moved the E♭ onto the beat. c262 left it because it believed the E♭3 fell "on a weak part of the beat". In the current source it is the first triplet of the beat, so that reason does not hold.

**Fix (applied, `sections/bSec.ily`, bSecVla bar 43):**
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { es8 aes es }   →   \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 es aes }
```
Beat 3 now opens on A♭3. The bass is doubled in four places (Cb A♭1, harp A♭1/A♭2, Vc A♭2, Va A♭3), D♭ sounds only in the cor anglais, and E♭3 moves to the middle, weak triplet. This answers all the earlier reasons. The top note is still A♭3, under the cor anglais (c221). There is still no D♭ in the violas (c192) and no F3 to move in octaves with the flute (c221). The 8–7 A♭–G♭ that c221 wanted still runs A♭3 (43.3, last triplet) to G♭3 (43.4, middle triplet). Pitches, rhythm, pizz. and dynamics are otherwise unchanged. One side effect: the viola's last note of beat 2 (B♭3) now steps to A♭3 while the cellos go B♭2–A♭2. That is a one-triplet octave doubling of the bass step, not two independent voices in octaves. allpairs_par.py output is identical to v422's. If the composer wants to remove it, end beat 2 on D♭4 instead (`bes8 ges des'`). That D♭4 is under the cor anglais's F4 and gives the 4th a common-tone preparation in the texture (PDF 153 §3).

### 2. Bars 11.3–12.1, 27.3–28.1, 75.3–76.1: IV arrives on the weak third beat and is still sounding at the next downbeat. Recorded; no change.

**Problem.** In the consequent of theme A, IV (D♭–F–A♭) arrives under the melody's tied F on beat 3 and is restruck unchanged on the next downbeat. PDF 149 §15, citing Unit 6, says that repeating a chord from a weak to a strong beat "can neutralize the metric accent".

**Evidence that it is acceptable here.** Bars 12, 28 and 76 are the second, weak bar of a two-bar group. A&S PDF 150 §18 treats that bar as normally weak and Ex. 9-1 places its II6 there. Each bar still changes harmony inside itself, exactly as Ex. 9-20/9-21 (PDF 146–147) prescribe: a 5–6 to II6 on beat 2 and the bass falling a third to II7 on beat 3. The antecedent (bar 8) puts its chord change under the tied note. The consequent anticipates IV instead, which speeds the motion toward V as PDF 141 §8 describes. The horn line and the 23–30 accompaniment are protected. No change.

### 3. No cadential 6/4 at the authentic cadences 13–14, 29–30, 60–61 and 81–82. Recorded; no change.

A&S PDF 158–159 (Ex. 10-14) says cadential 6/4s give weight to the cadences of a period, and Dvořák's Largo uses them often. This movement has one, at 43. At 13, 29 and 81 the melody has 7̂ on V's downbeat after 1̂ on ii7 (c012, c262 finding 6). A 6/4 would need 1̂ on that beat (PDF 156 §8), which changes theme A. At 60 the violins' and flutes' C6 is the leading tone in octaves into 61 (c282, protected). A 6/4 there would need D♭6 on 60's downbeat. I add one point to c262's: the movement does not lack weight at these cadences. It gets it from the prepared 7th (79–81, row 9) and the borrowed ii°7 (80.3, row 17), which are the stronger devices that Units 9–10 offer when the melody fixes 7̂ on the downbeat.

### 4. Bar 53.3–54.1: V6/5 to IV6. Recorded (c212, c262); not reopened.

This breaks syntax rules 1 and 2 on PDF 148 in name. The bass D♭–C–B♭ is a stepwise passing line, and IV6 goes on to V (PDF 141 §8). No new reason to change it.
