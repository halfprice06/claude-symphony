## Findings

### 1. Hn 3 bar 88: repeated A♭3 under a slur reads as a tie (applied)

Bars: 87-89, Hn 3 (con sord.), section coda.

Problem: `aes2.(\pp ... | aes2.~ |` places a second A♭3 inside the slur at 88 with no articulation. score.midi re-strikes the note, so the intent is a re-articulation, but in the part the slur over two equal notes is easily read as a tie and the player will hold it. Clarinet 1 has the same situation in the same bar and already carries a tenuto, which is this score's convention (c126: slur plus tenuto means soft re-articulation).

Evidence: Sebesky PDF 23-24 (items 1 and 11) lists articulation as part of the written line and shows the tenuto as the mark for a held but separated note. Adler part 1 PDF 177, Ex. VI-7, gives slurred repeated notes with dashes as soft tonguing on one breath. The Cl 1 tenuto at 88 gives the internal precedent.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-326/work/sections/coda.ily, `codaHnThree`):

```
aes2.(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." | aes2.--~ | aes2.~ | ...
```

A comment line citing c326 was added above `codaHnFour`. Nothing else changes; the tie into 89 is kept, so the sound is the same as score.midi already plays.

### 2. Breathing in long wind lines: no change needed

Every wind and brass span over 12 s is broken by a printed breath (10, 34, 58, 60, 62 b3 Cbn, 77, 79, 80), a slur break, or a rest. The longest unbroken soft span is the C.a. solo at 41-44 (about 15 s at p), which suits a double reed (Sebesky PDF 72). Low brass and Cbn spans are shorter than the woodwind spans, which matches Sebesky PDF 70 on large-bore instruments.

### 3. Ranges: no change needed

All parts lie inside Sebesky's chart (PDF 97). The B.cl. B♭1 at 8 already has an ossia (PDF 64). The B. Tbn D2/E2 at 64 are trigger notes (PDF 29). Fl 1 reaches B♭6 only at ff (PDF 65).

### 4. Horn spacing, entrances and mutes: no change needed

Pairs stay within a sixth (Sebesky PDF 36, Ex. 41; PDF 103), the bottom horn stays at or above concert C3 and a fifth or more above the bass at 29-30 and 87-90, entries join sounding lines (PDF 46), and Hn 3·4 have about 17 s to fit straight mutes before 87 (PDF 37).

### 5. Woodwind blend: no change needed

No seconds between unlike woodwinds in the close voicings at 7-14, 31-36, 61-62, 79 and 87-90 (Sebesky PDF 81); the flute sits above the oboe (PDF 79); the unison doublings at 53-56 and 89-90 and the clarinet thirds at 57-60 are the combinations Sebesky recommends (PDF 63, 80).

### 6. Brass endurance: no change needed

Hn 1 plays ff at 61-62 and then rests 16 s before 67, as recorded at c316; this is the rest for the lead player that Sebesky PDF 35 asks for. The trombones play slow values at close positions throughout 47-64 (PDF 28).
