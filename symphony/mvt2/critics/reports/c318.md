## Findings

### 1. Bars 31–34: the bass clarinet solo is no louder than the cor anglais above it (applied)

**Problem.** The transition's solo is the bass clarinet's motto tail E♭3–D3–D♭3–C3 (33–34), marked "solo, espr." p<mp>p. Above it the cor anglais moves in the same rhythm at 33 (C4–C♭4–B♭3 over E♭3–D3–D♭3) and holds A♭3 at 34, marked p. The two lines are at the same dynamic at 32 and 34, and one degree apart only on 33 b1.

**Evidence.** c218 lowered the solo to p<mp>p because the cor anglais and bassoon were pp: "one degree over the pp cor anglais and bassoon is enough". c276 then raised the cor anglais to p, correctly, because 33 b3–34 lies in its lowest fifth, where the oboe family cannot be asked for pp (PDF 198, 205). That removed the degree c218 relied on, and no cycle restored it. The reading makes the imbalance worse than the marks suggest: the cor anglais's lowest fifth or sixth has "tremendous expressive carrying power" (PDF 205), while the bass clarinet's chalumeau is "mysterious and shadowy" and "speaks a bit less incisively" (PDF 217). The cor anglais is also the upper voice. Adler's warning on PDF 185 applies: do not cover a register that cannot produce more volume. The motto tail is the cyclic material here; it should be the line heard.

**Fix (applied).** trans.ily, `transBassCl`, bar 33 peak and bar 34 arrival one degree up; the re-entry at 31 stays p, so c218's point about colourless mp/mf re-entries (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136) still holds:
```
r4 es,8(\p^\markup \italic "solo, espr." f,8 aes,8 c8 | es2.~\< | es4\mf\> d4 des4 | c2\mp bes,4) \breathe |
```
(was `es4\mp\> ... c2\p`). The solo now stays one or two degrees over the cor anglais's p until the cor anglais stops at 34; the diminuendo at 35 to pp at 36 is unchanged. Cor anglais, bassoons and the choir's harmony are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 61–62: the contrabassoon holds ff for about 9 s in its lowest octave with no breath (applied)

**Problem.** `des,,1--\ff | ges,,2-- fes,,2--`: D♭1–G♭1–F♭1, eight crotchets at Largamente ♩ = 54, all ff. The tuba and bass trombone breathe after 61 (c286); the contrabassoon, which takes the most air of the woodwinds, has no breath. At 30–36 (breath at 33) and 79–82 (breath after 80) it has one.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 230: "the amount of breath it takes to produce these notes is considerable. Therefore, it is advisable to provide quite a few periodic rests". The notes are tongued, not slurred (PDF 187), so a breath costs no legato. Brahms 1/iv (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188) shows the method: stagger the breaths of instruments on the same line "so that the first flute may breathe without diminishing the intensity". If the contrabassoon breathes with the tuba after 61, the 16-foot and 32-foot bass drop out together.

**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecCbsn`: breathe at 62 b3, between G♭1 and F♭1, where the tuba is still sounding:
```
des,,1--\ff | ges,,2-- \breathe fes,,2-- | R1*2 |
```
The comma prints above the staff, as the other breath marks in this system do.

### 3. Bar 37: the bass clarinet's last note stays at p under the flute's first note (applied)

**Problem.** The solo ends with `des2)\p r2` at 37: D♭3 at p for two beats, under Fl 1's entry on F5 at p, "dolce cantabile", in the flute's middle register, which has "little carrying power" (Ex. VII-2, PDF 185). The bass clarinet arrives on this note with a crescendo from pp (36) and simply stops.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 186, on the flute's weak registers: the composers "let the flute come through by doing little with the other instruments" (Dvořák 9/i, Brahms 4/iv). The cor anglais solos he quotes end their held notes with a diminuendo (Ex. VII-48, VII-50, PDF 205–206). A release diminuendo hands the D♭ chord to the harp and pizzicato basses and leaves the flute alone on its first bar. The bass clarinet register is far below the flute, so this is a small point.

**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecBassCl`: `des2)\p\> r2\! | R1*7 |` (was `des2)\p r2`).

### 4. Bars 87–90: no swell on the F♭ of the closing mixture line (checked, not recommended)

The C.a., Hn 4 (con sord.) and lower violas play F–F♭–E♭ at pp with no hairpin, and Cl 1 has D♭5 (tenuto) to C5. Adler's cor anglais examples put a small swell on expressive held notes (Ex. VII-48, VII-50). But the closing pp is planned as an echo with every timbre fading (c150, c290), the plagal close 87–92 is protected, and Cl 1's tenuto on D♭5 at 88 already marks the bar. I would not add a hairpin.

### 5. Bars 3–6: the wind chord stops without a diminuendo under horn 1's pickup (checked, not recommended)

Cl 2, B.cl. and Hn 3·4 hold their pp chord to 6 b2 and stop as horn 1 enters p at 6 b3. A diminuendo on 6 would follow the hairpin practice of the examples, but the link 1–6 is protected and the release at the horn's entry is the hand-over the plan describes. Recorded only.

### 6. Bar 53: Cl 1 doubles horn 1's "espr." counter-line without the word (checked, not recommended)

Horn 1 has "espr." at 53 b4 and Cl 1 has the same notes with only pp. Every other wind solo carries a word (row 6). Here c038 deliberately sets the clarinet one level under the horn so the unison is one colour, so giving the clarinet its own "espr." would work against that. No change.
