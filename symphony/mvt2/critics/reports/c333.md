## Findings (ranked)

There are no changes to the notes in this cycle. All eight findings are records. The reading's techniques are already used in this score, each at one formal point, and the places where the score departs from the textbook idiom (31–36, 77–78, 86) are protected decisions. The reason for each is stated below and I do not answer it with anything new.

### 1. Where the tonicized areas fall follows the tension curve (follows; record)

**What the reading teaches.** Applied chords make tonicized areas inside the phrase (PDF 419, 439 item 1). Chords applied to V "significantly increase their drive to the dominant" (PDF 422 §4), and ♯4̂ in the bass is used "usually at a cadence" (PDF 424).

**The score.** Each new goal that is reached through an applied chord, with its share of the running time:
- A section (7–30): one, F minor at 19 b3–20 (VII°6 of vi), at 18 %. The theme's own V7/V at 8/24 is a chord inside the A♭ cadence, not a new area.
- B1 (37–44): one, B♭ minor at 40 (VII°7/vi), at 40 %.
- B2 (45–52): B♭ minor at 45–46, F♯ minor at 47–50 (C♯7), and the return to D♭ through A♭7 at 51–52 (45–55 %).
- B3 and the climax (53–64): B♭ minor at 56, IV at 58 b4–59 (V7/IV), V at 59 b3–60 (VII°7/V over ♯4̂ G♮ in the bass), and A minor at 62 b3–63 (F♭ = E as its V). That is four goals in nine bars, at 59–70 %.
- Retransition, A′ and coda (65–92): no new goals. There is the long E♭7 (65–70), the motto's V7/V at 72, and the apparent chord at 86.

So the rate of tonicization rises through the middle section and peaks at the climax (density 13.2 and 15.8 voices in analysis.txt at 57–64). Then it stops, and the reprise is harmonically the plainest part of the movement. The one ♯4̂-in-the-bass cadence is at 59, the B section's main cadence, as PDF 424 describes. The other cadences (12–13, 28–29, 43–44, 80–81) stay diatonic or use mixture. c203 found the same grading. The new evidence here is the timing table, and it shows the plan works in time as well as in bar count. Keep.

### 2. The two remote areas each have a local and a long-range meaning (follows; record)

PDF 422 (Ex. 25-9) says one chord can be read as an applied chord locally and as an altered chord of the main key over the long range. The two remote areas use this at their edges. At 47, C♯7 is V7 of F♯ minor and also D♭7 (I♭7 of D♭). At 62 b3 the chord is spelled F♭–A♭–C♭ (♭III of D♭, ♭VI of A♭) and resolves as E major, V of A minor. At 64 b4, E7 is V of A minor and the German sixth of A♭, and it falls to E♭7 at 65. In form terms, F♯ minor (four bars, 5 % of the time) and A minor (two bars) are entered by one applied dominant each, so they are heard as events inside the D♭–A♭ plan and not as new tonics. That suits a 92-bar Adagio, where a full modulation would need more time than the middle section has. The signature changes at 47 and 63 are for reading only and were settled (HISTORY "respellings 47/51 ... do not reopen"). Keep.

### 3. Deceptive cadences lead to restated, stronger cadences (follows; record)

PDF 427: a deceptive cadence delays the resolution, the cadential V is restated, and the authentic cadence is "made stronger by the immediately preceding tension". The score does this twice at large scale. The half cadence at 44 goes to B♭ minor at 45, and the authentic D♭ cadence is held back to 60–61, 16 bars later, as the ff Largamente arrival. The ♭VI at 78 (soprano on 1̂, A♭5) is followed by IV–iiø7–V♭9–I at 79–82, the movement's ff peak with B♭6. The two ff points of the movement are exactly the two restated cadences. The reading's model puts an applied chord to V at the deceptive point (bass down to ♯4̂ or up to 6̂). The score's ♭VI keeps the F♭–E♭ thread, which is protected (c023, c124), and its formal function is the same. Keep.

### 4. F♯ minor is framed by applied chords that connect statements of its tonic (follows; record)

PDF 419: an applied chord can connect two statements of its own "tonic". At 46 b3–48 the G♭ (= F♯) major chord goes through C♯7 to F♯ minor, and at 49 b3–50 the Neapolitan goes through C♯7 back to i. So the area is confirmed twice within four bars before the iv7 → A♭7 pivot at 50 b3–51 leaves it. The static F♯ minor of 48 (c274) is the stable centre of the quotation, and the applied chords sit at its edges. In terms of pacing, the quotation is the calmest moment of the middle section, which is right for a recalled theme held back by "poco rit." and "poco sostenuto". Keep.

### 5. Apparent applied chords mark both returns to D♭ harmony (follows; record)

PDF 430–431 (§15, Ex. 25-25, 25-26): a chord that looks like an applied dominant but does not go to its "tonic" is a varied diatonic progression, and III♯ is not an applied dominant. Three places use this:
- Bars 1–2: C major, coming from movement I, is III♯ of A♭ and goes to I, not to F minor. The listener reinterprets the opening chord, as in Ex. 25-19, where Beethoven 1 opens on a chord whose key becomes clear only later. Protected link.
- 20 b3–21: C7 → D♭. F minor is real at 19–20 (VII°6 → i), so here the C7 is a true V7 of vi turned to IV. This is the one point in the A section where a tonicized area is entered and then deflected, which suits the contrasting b phrase.
- 52 and 86: the same sonority, C–D♮–F–A♭ over a held A♭, leads both times to a D♭ chord (D♭ at 53, IV6 at 87). At 52 it brings back theme B for the build. At 86 it turns the coda's tonic pedal toward the plagal close. The same chord therefore prepares the two late arrivals of D♭ harmony. It also carries the motto tail's D♮ in the coda. This is a formal rhyme worth keeping; nothing to change.

### 6. Tonic pedal with I♭7 as V of IV (31–36, 83–86): not used, by decision (record; not re-raised)

Ex. 25-19 and Exercise 1b (PDF 428, 439) give the textbook way to turn from a tonic pedal toward IV: add the minor seventh to I. The score has two A♭ pedals that move to IV: 31–36 → D♭ at 37, and 83–86 → IV6 at 87. Neither uses I♭7. c203 proposed G♭ at 36 b3 and it was rejected (the A♭7-into-D♭ join was removed at c020; D♭ enters by phrase modulation). c302 tested G♭ at 86 and did not recommend it (diminished third against the motto's D♮). The reading adds nothing that answers either reason. For pacing, the fresh D♭ at 37 together with the new metre and tempo makes 37 the clearest section boundary in the movement, which is a valid use of the unprepared entry. Not re-raised.

### 7. Sequences: falling thirds are figured; the build does not use a rising 5–6 (follows; record)

PDF 437–438 (Ex. 25-36b): falling thirds in root position sound choppy without figuration. At 37–40 and 53–56 the bass falls D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A under continuous viola triplets and harp, so the figuration is there, and the A♮ VII°7/vi is the only applied chord in the sequence (no augmented second). PDF 435 offers rising 5–6 sequences with applied chords and chromatic bass lines as a way to build intensity. The build 53–60 does not use one. It rises through the theme's register (the octave leap at 57), the doubling of the viola figure's speed at 59–60, and the brass entries. Its only rising chromatic bass is G♭–G♮–A♭ at 59–60, the cadential ♯4̂–5̂ of finding 1. A 5–6 sequence would mean rewriting theme B's bass at 53–58, which c143 and c293 settled. The single chromatic rise placed at the cadence is the stronger use of the device, so I do not recommend adding more.

### 8. Doubling and cross relations at the applied chords (follows; record)

On the beat-by-beat list, every temporary leading tone listed in row 2 of the table sounds in one line only, or as an octave doubling of one line. The one 7th in several parts, F♭ at 59 b4, is the protected Hn 1/Bsn 1/Vc counter-line in octaves (c302 finding 2). Cross relations follow PDF 420–422: the A♮ at 40 and 56 is in the bass, and the chromatic steps at 1–2, 50–51 and 77–78 stay in one voice. analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels. Nothing to change.
