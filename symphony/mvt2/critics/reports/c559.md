### 1. Comparison with "Nimrod": would a conductor believe a master wrote this? (recorded, no change)

Bars 7-30 and 71-92. "Nimrod" is one tune heard three times, each time with more players. It opens with strings alone at pp. Woodwind and horns are added at the second statement. The full orchestra with brass plays the last statement ff, and a short diminuendo leads to a soft held E♭ chord. Its effect comes from three things: one unbroken build, a bass that moves by step under the tune, and holding the brass back until the last statement. This movement uses the same method for theme A. The first statement is horn over woodwind (7-14), the next is violins in unison over a cello counter-melody (23-30), then violins in octaves (71-78), then three octaves with the winds ff at 79. That is Rimsky's list of operations for a return (PDF 118: other octaves, octaves added, altered details, a new dynamic plan), the same list Elgar's plan follows. Two differences are by design and are not faults. First, this movement puts the D♭ middle section and its own tutti (61-62, with the trombone quotation at 63-64) between the statements, where "Nimrod" has one span. Second, the A′ peak at 79 uses horns without trombones, so the brass weight of the movement falls on the B climax, where Nimrod puts it on the last statement. The plan (Dvořák 9/ii model) and HISTORY (A′ texture plan 71-82, c067 and later) fix both choices, and PDF 116 supports keeping the two climaxes in different colours. A conductor would recognise Elgar's method of growing one tune, and would find the scoring of every statement idiomatic. The remaining distance from a master is in the tunes, not the orchestration, and earlier melody critics have recorded that. Fix: none.

### 2. Brass harmony at 60-64 checked against PDF 100-105 and 113 (recorded, no change)

At 60 the brass enter under the moving woodwind and strings. Hn 4 plays C4 at p, while Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba play pp. That is Rimsky's rule for a horn that cannot go in pairs: mark it one degree louder (PDF 103). At 61 the chord is close order from D♭2 to A♭4 with no gap in the middle (PDF 100). The bass sits in B.tbn and tuba octaves with the horns above (PDF 101). The woodwind is marked ff over trombones at f (PDF 112), and the held brass sits under moving woodwind (PDF 108). At 63-64 Tbn 1's quotation sounds over string tremolo, the one string-brass combination Rimsky recommends (PDF 113; cf. Ex. 131, PDF 306-307). Fix: none. This confirms c554 from a different part of the book.

### 3. Horn and bassoons at 67-70 against PDF 108 (recorded, no change)

From 68 to 70 Hn 1 has E♭4-D4-D♭4-C4-B♭3, Bsn 1 has B♭3-A♭3-G3, and Bsn 2 has D♭3 under E♭7. On the held chord at 69-70 the horn takes the concord (B♭3), and the bassoons take G3 and the seventh D♭3, which is what Rimsky asks for: "the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons" (PDF 108). The bassoons sit below the horn, not inside it, so the rule that bassoons may go inside horns but not the reverse does not come up. The passage is p, where PDF 108 says the horn-bassoon mixture balances, and not forte, where four horns would be needed. Fix: none.

### 4. The coda fade and Ex. 143 (recorded, no change)

In Ex. 143 (*Christmas Night*, PDF 317) Rimsky fades by reducing the number of violinists (tutti, then 8, 4, 2) under "dim. poco a poco" and "morendo". The coda thins the same way: 4 cellos on the basses' A♭ pedal at 83-86 and 4 solo basses from 87. One move runs the other way: the cellos go from 4 soli to tutti at 87 as the music fades. c259 gave the reason. The section takes the plagal bass F-D♭-A♭ at 87-89 while the basses thin to four, so the full cello section carries the bass line and the overall number of low players goes down. That is the same principle applied to the whole low register, and it matches PDF 88, where Rimsky says to reduce the players under divided upper strings. The muted Hn 3·4 doubled by the divided violas at 87-90, with C.a. and Cl 1, is the colour Rimsky describes on PDF 107 and 113 (cf. Ex. 149-150, PDF 321-322). The "dim. e mor." into a long held chord at 89-92 matches Ex. 158 (PDF 331). The fade order is on the do-not-touch list (c558), and I see no reason in the reading to change it. Fix: none.

### 5. Re-scoring of returning material against PDF 116-118 (recorded, no change)

Theme A has four settings (7, 23, 71, 79) and theme B three (37, 45, 53). No two use the same melody colour or the same accompaniment. Each follows PDF 116's rule that the harmony differs from the tune in colour. The horn tune at 7 has woodwind and harp harmony with the strings out. The flute tune at 37 has string harmony. The violin tune at 53 has harp triplets and a Cl 1 / Hn 1 counter-line. The tutti appears only at 61-62 and 79-81, both f or ff (PDF 119-120). Fix: none.
