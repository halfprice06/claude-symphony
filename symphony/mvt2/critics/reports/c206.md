## Findings

No horn passage in v206 goes against the assigned reading. I first suspected the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line slur (finding 1) and tested a breath in the scratch copy. On recounting the bars, the line starts at 53 beat 4, not 52, so the slur is about 13 s and no breath is needed. I reverted the test. The findings below are therefore checks that need no change, ranked by how much risk each passage carries for the players. I list them so the composer can record them.

### 1. Slur lengths (PDF 309; PDF 292). No change.
Each horn slur is one breath. The durations from the tempo map are:
- Hn 1 solo: 6 b3–10 b2 is 13.8 s and 10 b3–14 is 15.0 s, both p to mp.
- Counter-line 53 b4–56: 12.9 s, pp to mp, with Cl 1 in unison.
- 57–58: 7.3 s. 59 b3–60: 5.9 s at f, which is the loudest slur, and it is short.
- 67–70 b2: 12.7 s.
- Hn 2: 75–77 is 10.4 s.
- Hn 1·2: 78 b3–79 is 5.0 s. 80–82 b2 is 10.4 s, f falling to p in the allargando.
- Hn 3·4 muted: 87–90 b2 is 13.4 s.
- Hn 4: 3–6 b2 (unslurred) is 12.7 s.

The long slurs are all soft. The loud slurs are under 11 s and shrink as the tempo slows. This is what PDF 292 asks for.

### 2. Endurance and rests (PDF 303). No change.
Hn 1 has three stretches of playing:
- 6–14, then 38 bars of rest.
- 53 b4–62, ten bars rising to f (written up to F5), with breaths after 56, 58 and 60.
- 67–70, then 78 b3–82.

The shortest rest is 63–66, about 16.7 s at Largamente, allargando and Tempo I, before the p solo at 67 on written B♭4. That is the middle of the "bright" register, not a high entry. c194 accepted this rest when it gave Hn 1 the a2 at 61–62, citing Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 102. I agree. Hn 3·4 never play more than four bars without a long rest.

### 3. Ranges and registers (Ex. X-2, X-4, X-5). No change.
- The highest horn note is C♭5, written G♭5. Hn 3 and Hn 4 play it a2 at f on 62.
- Hn 4 is a low horn, and Ex. X-2 puts the cor basso's top at written G5, so G♭5 is inside the limit.
- In Ex. X-5 this note is the top of the "bright and heroic" register, which suits f in a tutti.
- The a2 is protected (c064/c069/c086; c169 "acceptable"). The reading gives no reason to reopen it.
- Theme A (written E♭4–F5, p) sits where Adler's Tchaikovsky solo sits (Ex. X-24, written D4–A5, dolce).
- No horn goes below written B♭3, so the "dark, unfocused" register and the B♭-side pedals (PDF 303) are never asked for.
- The muted pp chord at 87–90 lies in the "deep and solid" band (written B♭3–E♭4). A mute speaks reliably there.

### 4. Entries: "hear, then find" (PDF 304). No change.
Every exposed entry starts on a pitch that is already sounding or is doubled at the moment of entry:
- Hn 1 at 6 b3 on E♭3, which Cl 2 holds.
- Hn 3 at 29 b3 on D♭4, with the cellos' D♭4 (c097).
- Hn 1 at 53 b4 on A♭3, the fifth of the D♭ chord, with Cl 1 in unison.
- Hn 1 at 67 on E♭4, over the E♭ pedal.
- Hn 1·2 at 78 b3 on C4, inside the passing E♭–G–C chord.
- Hn 4 at 61 leaps an octave from its own A♭3. c136 cleared this: an octave on one fingering.

The only entry on a pitch that is not sounding just before is Hn 2's F4 (written C5) f at 61, after 60 bars of rest. It falls on the tutti downbeat, where F is the third of the D♭ chord in many instruments. Hn 2's part shows a cue of Hn 1's G♭4 at 57–60 (c135), so the player hears the G♭ that resolves to F. No leap in any horn part is wider than a fourth inside a phrase, apart from those octaves.

### 5. Mutes (PDF 307–308). No change.
- "mettere sord." comes at 82, with 82–86 for the change (about 17.9 s).
- "con sord." is at 87, in the score and both parts.
- "via sord." is at 91, in the score and both parts.

The muted passage is four sustained bars, which is the case Adler gives for a mute rather than the hand. Movement II does not run attacca into the scherzo (coherence/mvt2.md), and the scherzo marks "senza sord." at Hn 3·4's first entry. Adler asks for exactly this pair of words.

### 6. Notation and parts (PDF 302–304). No change.
- The score is in C, and the horn staves are labelled "in F" (Ex. X-6 layout).
- The parts are "Horn n in F", in treble clef, without key signatures.
- Their lowest written note is B♭3, so the old-versus-new bass-clef problem of Ex. X-7 cannot arise.
- The head of the score says "Full score in C". That is the concert-score form of the warning Adler recommends on PDF 304.

### 7. Effects the reading lists but the score does not use (PDF 308–311). No change.
The score uses none of stopped notes, cuivré, bells up, glissando, multiple tonguing or trills. Adler presents them as colour for loud, comic or sardonic passages, or (stopped) for single soft notes. Nothing in this Adagio calls for them, and adding one would only be for its own sake.
