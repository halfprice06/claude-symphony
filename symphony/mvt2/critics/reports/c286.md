## Findings

**1. Tuba and bass trombone, bars 61–62: 8.9 s of f in the low register on one breath. Applied in the scratch copy.**
Problem: since c218 the low brass are f at 61–62 (they were mf). The tuba plays D♭2 (whole note) then G♭1 and F♭1 (half notes), the bass trombone D♭3 then G♭2 and F♭2, all tenuto, with no breath between the breath after 60 and the one before 63 (B.tbn) or the release at 63 (tuba). At Largamente ♩ = 54 that is 8 beats, 8.9 s.
Evidence: Adler PDF 292: "In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath in a slow tempo, for it does take more breath to play loudly than softly." adler2 PDF 8: the tuba "takes a great deal of breath ... special care must be exercised not to tire out the performer." PDF 287 and adler2 PDF 1: the wide bore and large mouthpiece of the tuba and bass trombone are what favour the low notes, and they cost the most air. The notes are tongued, not slurred (PDF 292: unslurred notes are tongued), so the barline 61→62 is a place to breathe, but a tenuto whole note in a Largamente invites the player to hold it full; the score should say where. The gap is covered: contrabassoon, double basses (ff, "change bow at will"), bass clarinet and Bsn 2 hold D♭ through the barline, and everybody re-attacks G♭ at 62.
Fix (bSec.ily): `bSecBassTbn = { ... aes,1\pp\< \breathe | des1--\f \breathe | ges,2-- fes,2-- \breathe | ...` and `bSecTuba = { ... aes,,1\pp\< \breathe | des,1--\f \breathe | ...`. Tbn 2 (small bore, middle register, half notes) and the horns are left as they are.

**2. Horns 61–62: horn 4 is above horns 1 and 2. No change recommended.**
Problem: Adler PDF 303 divides the horns into high (I, III) and low (II, IV), and Ex. X-6 (PDF 304) lays the chord out that way. At 61–62 horn 4 doubles horn 3 on the top line (written E♭5, F5–G♭5, f) while horn 1 plays written C5, D♭5–E♭5 and horn 2 written A♭4, A♭4–C♭5.
Why not change it: the only voicing that obeys the division puts horn 1 on the top line a2 with horn 3. That is c046's original problem: horn 1 would end 53–62 on written G♭5 and then have about 16.7 s before the exposed p solo at 67, against PDF 292 and 303 (rests for the lips). Written G♭5 at f is inside a low horn's working range in Ex. X-4, and the pair is protected (c086, c264: Hn 3·4 a2, close-order chord). Endurance outranks the seating convention here.

**3. Horn 1 endurance 53–62 into the solo at 67. No change.**
Horn 1 plays 53 b4 to 62 (about 37 s) with breaths after 56, 58 and 60, all in the middle register (written E♭4–E♭5, Ex. X-5 "deep and solid" to "bright and heroic"), then rests 63–66 (4.4 + 5.4 + 6.9 = 16.7 s) before the p dolce solo on written B♭4. Adler asks for periodic rests after high playing (PDF 303); this is middle-register playing with a rest before the next exposed entry. The phrases themselves are short: 6 b3–10 b2 is 13.8 s at p, 10 b3–14 is 15.0 s at p–mp, 53 b4–56 is 12.9 s at pp–mp, 67–70 b2 is 12.7 s at p–mp, each one slur and one breath (PDF 309).

**4. Trombone 1 quotation, 63–64, on one breath. No change.**
From 63 b1½ to the end of 64 is 9.3 s (63 at ♩ = 54, 64 at 48 then 42), but only about 6.4 s is ff; the diminuendo starts at 64 b3. The slurs E3–C4, B3–B♭3–A3 and G♯3–A3 move between near positions (II, III/VI, IV, I, II, III), so they are soft-tongued legato as adler2 PDF 3 describes, and no far position alternation occurs (Ex. X-71). If a player needs air, the place is after the tenuto A3 on 64 b1, which ends a slur. Do not print it: the quotation keeps movement I's articulation (c240).

**5. Horns 1·2 at 80–82 b2: one slur at f, 10.4 s. No change.**
PDF 292 warns against long loud slurs in slow tempi. Here the f lasts only through 80 and 81 b1 before the diminuendo, the horns are a2, so they can stagger, and the slur matches the cellos' hemiola bowing (c118). Within the reading's limits.

**6. Horn 4 ppp at bar 1. No change.**
PDF 292 says brass cannot play as softly as strings. Horn 4's ppp C4 (written G4) enters on beat 2 in unison with horn 3's C4, which is already sounding and tied over from movement I, so the attack is covered; it then crescendos only to pp. This is the relief the attacca needs (protected link 1–6).
