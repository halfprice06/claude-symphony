## Findings

### 1. Violins 27–30 and 75–78: the B♭ appoggiatura, the f arrival and both diminuendos fall against the bow (applied)

**Bars:** Vln I and Vln II, 27 b3–30 and 75 b3–78.

**Problem.** Counting from the up-bow pickup at 26 b3: 27 b3 `f''4~` is a down-bow and its tie runs into 28 b1. Then `bes''4(\f\> aes''4)` is an up-bow: the f and the stressed B♭ appoggiatura start at the tip, and the diminuendo runs toward the frog. 29 is a down-bow and 30 `aes'2.\p\>` (p to pp) is an up-bow. All three expressive events of the phrase's end go against Adler's natural direction. The reprise has the same pattern, one bar later in the bow, and it reaches into the build. 76 b2–3 (the B♭6 appoggiatura that c228 shaped as the peak of the swell) is an up-bow from the tip. 77's crescendo `g'''4(\<` is a down-bow. 78 `aes''2\f` is an up-bow, and the run had to be hooked as a second up-bow (c246) so that 79 could start down-bow. The cellos under them were rebowed by c118 so that their f at 28 and 78 is a down-bow. The violins, which carry the tune, are the ones left against the bow.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; "the tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … Conversely, there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow." Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus, PDF 38) marks "a down-bow and up-bow … over the long note so that the following attack will be solid", and "if this passage is executed well, the bow change will hardly be noticed." Dvořák's Largo violins do the same on tied notes (lines 17–20, 40 above). c198 and c228 established that the appoggiatura at 28, 58 and 76 carries the stress (A&S PDF 348). A stress at the tip on an up-bow works against that.

**Answer to the protection.** c018 protected "up-bow crescendo pickups, down-bow arrivals". This fix keeps every pickup on an up-bow (22, 26, 70, 74) and puts the arrivals on down-bows: the f at 28 b2, the f at 78 and the ff at 79. c118 left 28 and 30 alone because it thought the fix would need `\downbow` retakes. It needs only one mark per bar, on a note already tied, which is Adler's own device. The c118/c246 plan for 77–80 keeps its purpose: 79 still starts ff on a fresh down-bow, and 80–81 are unchanged. The difference is that the run at 78 b3 is now a natural up-bow after a down-bow A♭, not a hooked second up-bow. Pitches, slurs, hairpins and dynamics are unchanged. score.midi is byte-identical.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-258/work).**
```

f''4\upbow bes''4(\f\> aes''4) |
% aTwo.ily, aTwoVlnOne, bar 76 (Vln II the same an octave lower: f''4\upbow bes''4( ...)
f'''4\upbow bes'''4(-\tweak stencil ##f \mf \> aes'''4)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp |
```
Bow plan after the fix. A: 26 b3 pickup U, 27 b3 D, tie change U, 28 b2 B♭ f D (diminuendo on the down-bow), 29 U, 30 D (p>pp). A′: 74 b3 U, 75 b3 D, tie change U, 76 b2 B♭6 D, 77 U (crescendo), 78 A♭ f D, run U, 79 ff D, 80 U, 81 D (diminuendo). The existing `\upbow` on the run and `\downbow` at 79 now confirm the natural direction. I kept them because they mark the protected c246 arrival, but the composer may drop them under Adler's "without overmarking". The marks print cleanly over the tied note in score and parts (score pages 2 and 6).

### 2. Violins II 15–16: the louré swells under the oboe run against the bow (applied)

**Bars:** Vln II, 15–22 (louré, protected since c018 for its stroke).

**Problem.** Vln II enters at 15 after fourteen bars' rest, so it takes the first bar down-bow. Each bar is one louré bow (`aes'2--( aes'4--)`). So 15's crescendo (c138: the oboe's swell one level down) is a down-bow and 16's diminuendo an up-bow, the reverse of the habit. The oboe it shadows has no bow. The swell at 19 falls within one bar, so it is neutral either way.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38 (crescendo on the up-bow, diminuendo on the down-bow; mark only to counter habit). Ex. II-30 (Handel, PDF 43): the louré accompaniment is printed with its ⊓/V pattern because the bow direction is part of the expression. c186 used the same up-bow entry at pp for Vln II, Vla and Cb at 45.

**Fix (applied).** Up-bow entry at 15, and a second up-bow on the E♮4 at 20 b3 after the half-bar rest (Ex. II-19, obs. 1–2). Without it, 21 and the pickup at 22 b3 would turn over, and the pickup has to be an up-bow with Vln I into 23 (protected c018 logic).
```
% aOne.ily, aOneVlnTwo
aes'2--(\pp\<\upbow^\markup \italic "con sord." aes'4--) | aes'2--(\> g'4--\!) | ...
aes'2--(\< g'4--\>) | r2\! e'4--\pp\upbow | f'4--( c'4-- bes4--~) |
```
Count: 15 U (crescendo), 16 D (diminuendo), 17 U, 18 D, 19 U, 20 b3 U, 21 D, 22 b3 pickup U. The louré, the slurs and the dynamics are unchanged.

### 3. Vln II 52: crescendo on a down-bow (recorded, no change)

From the c186 up-bow at 45, 51 `es''1` is an up-bow and 52 `es''2.\<` a down-bow, so the crescendo into the theme's pickup goes toward the tip. A fix would need a hook at 51 or a change to c186's 49 slur, which exists to keep 52 b4 an up-bow with Vln I. The crescendo is three beats from pp in an inner part, and the habit is easy to counter there. Not worth a new mark (PDF 38, "without overmarking").

### 4. Violins 82: p>pp on an up-bow (recorded, no change)

After 81's down-bow diminuendo, 82 `aes''2.\p\>` is an up-bow. Changing it would need a hook at 81 inside c246's protected 79–81 plan. The diminuendo starts at the tip, where the stroke is already lightest, so the effect is small.

### 5. Swells inside one stroke at 23 b3–24 and 71 b3–72 (recorded, no change)

The tied E♭ carries `\<` then `\>` on one down-bow. A swell within one stroke is the normal way to shape a single held note, and it needs no bow change. The Dvořák lines above use one bow per hairpin only where the hairpins span whole bars.

### 6. Coda Vln I 89–92 (recorded, not re-raised)

Adler's staggered bowing (Ex. II-25, PDF 40) would give the closing C5 a seamless line, as "change bow at will" does for Vln II, Vla, Vc and Cb. The marked U/D in every bar is protected (c016/c018; c186 also declined to re-raise it), and its reason still holds: an up-bow entry and a down-bow ppp fermata. Each change is on a tie, which Ex. II-20 says a good section hides.

### 7. Viola off-beats 48–51 (recorded, follows)

`r8 a8-_` on separate bows is correct. Adler's louré (PDF 42) needs slurs only when several notes share one bow. With rests between the off-beats, separate soft strokes are the sotto voce effect wanted under the quotation.
