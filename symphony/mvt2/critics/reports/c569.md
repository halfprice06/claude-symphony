## What else I studied

**Model work: Dvořák, Violin Concerto in A minor, Op. 53, ii, Adagio ma non troppo (F major).** No lens-9 critic has used it; I searched the report headers c009-c559. No score of it is in the corpus, so I worked from memory and two descriptions, and I make no bar-level claims about it. The descriptions confirm three things. The movement follows the first movement without a pause, by way of an introspective bridge. It is in F major. The soloist introduces the flowing principal melody, and a contrasting episode follows (https://fwsymphony.org/program-notes/dvořák-antonín-violin-concerto-in-a-minor-opus-53; https://wikiwand.com/en/articles/Violin_Concerto_(Dvořák); IMSLP https://imslp.org/wiki/Violin_Concerto,_Op.53_(Dvořák,_Antonín)). Two further points are from memory and not confirmed: the opening theme has quiet woodwind support, and late in the movement the theme returns in the horns. I compared v569 with v559 file by file using `diff`. I viewed all eight score pages at 60 dpi, and page 1 (bars 7-14) at 150 dpi.

## Findings (ranked)

**F1. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Compared with Dvořák Op. 53/ii (recorded, no change).**
The design points match Dvořák's own practice, and each follows the reading. Like Dvořák's Adagio, the movement grows out of the previous movement without a break: bars 1-6 hold the C5 and hand the string chord to horns, clarinet and bass clarinet in the same position, an octave lower (principle 10, PDF 127). The first theme is set for one wind colour over a soft wind basis, in a different group from the melody and about an octave below it (7-14; principle 17, PDF 133). Each return of a theme is rescored by octave transfer, extension and new detail (principle 1, PDF 118). The two big builds add strings, then winds, then brass, each new group entering softer and crescendoing faster (53-61, 71-79; principles 11 and 15, PDF 127, 130). The releases remove the same groups in reverse order (81-92, Ex. 259 on PDF 424-426). A conductor would take all this as professional work in the Dvořák tradition.

The distance from Dvořák lies in one plan choice. Dvořák's contrasting episode works against the main melody, while this movement's middle section (37-60) is a second song, and its one dramatic stroke comes late (61-64). That plan is fixed by the key plan and quotations on the do-not-touch list, and I do not reopen it. It also has a reason: the scherzo follows this movement, so it needs no internal storm. If I remember correctly that Dvořák brings his theme back in the horns, the parallel here is A′ (71-82), where the horns join the cellos' counter-line at 78-81 rather than taking the theme. A′ is protected and the horn solo at 7-14 already gives theme A to the horn, so I make no proposal.

**F2. Comparison with v559: nothing got worse (recorded).**
Three changes were made since v559. (1) c562's Bsn 2 at 11 (`c4( es4 des4`). It removes the C3-D♭3 unison motion with the bass clarinet. The new line meets Bsn 1's held E♭3 by oblique motion on beat 2, so the harp's E♭3 is in unison with both bassoons for one beat at pp, and it reaches the bass clarinet's D♭3 by contrary motion on beat 3. Neither is a fault. The engraving at 150 dpi is clear: two voices with separate stems on the shared staff. (2) c566's timpani "damp" at 4 is text only. (3) c565 shortened Ob 1's hairpin at 47 in the score only. The score is still 8 pages and analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

**F3. Bar 83: one bar with two empty octaves between the harp's harmonics and the bass (recorded, no change).**
Principle 8 (PDF 125) says upper and lower parts are seldom widely separated without the middle being filled, and that the gap "serves for strange and grotesque effects" (Ex. 235, PDF 405). At 83 the harp's harmonics sound A♭4-C5-E♭5 over cellos (4 soli) A♭2, basses and harp L.H. A♭1, ppp/p, with nothing between A♭2 and A♭4. The gap lasts one bar. The timpani fill it at 84 (E♭3-C3-A♭2), and the violas at 84 beat 3 (C4/E♭4). A conductor would hear a bare harmonic over a pedal rather than a fault, and the coda hand-over is protected (c050, c521). The only cheap filler would be Bsn 2 E♭3 pp held 83-84 (`codaBsnTwo = { es2.~\pp | es2 r4 | R2.*7 | R2.\fermata | }`). I did not apply it, because it would blur the harmonics' colour, which is the point of the bar.

**F4. Bar 63: the sf-dim uses Rimsky's "less effective" method (recorded, no change).**
PDF 129 prefers strings attacking sf on a soft woodwind chord, and calls sustained strings tremolando with short wind chords "not so effective, and therefore less frequent". Bar 63 is the second kind: strings tremolo sf>, with Fl 1, pizzicato basses and the bass trombone sounding sf on the downbeat. It is the right choice here. The woodwinds must be out of the trombone's register for the quotation, and trombone over string tremolo is protected (c008, c507, c554). Recorded so a later critic does not raise it from this page.

**F5. Hand-overs follow the transference rule (recorded, no change).**
PDF 125-126 asks that the last note of one part coincide with the first note of the next (Ex. 237-238, PDF 407). This holds at every hand-over I checked: 36-37 (C.a. and Fl 1 share C5-F5), 47 (Ob 1's C♯5 to the C.a.'s C♯4 at beat 4, lower register to the lower instrument, PDF 128), 52-53 (the C.a.'s F4 and the violins' F5 together), 70 (Hn 1's B♭3 held under the violins' pickup) and 89 (Cl 1 and Vln I meet on C5).

**F6. Amplification at 60-61 and 78-79 matches Ex. 244 and 248 (recorded, no change).**
In Ex. 244 (PDF 410) the brass enter p and crescendo into the strings' sf. In Ex. 248 (PDF 413) the winds enter pp cresc. over string sextuplets already sounding. Bar 60 does both: Tbn 2, B.tbn and tuba pp<, Hn 4 p< under winds and strings already f, into ff at 61. At 78, timpani pp< and Hn 1·2 mf< enter one level below the strings' f. The horns are the basis (f) under the ff melody at 61 and 79, as PDF 133 asks.

**F7. Theme recurrences are rescored as PDF 118 describes (recorded, no change).**
Theme A: horn with low winds (7), violin unison with cello counter-line (23), violins in octaves with the viola figure and harp (71), winds in three octaves (79). Theme B: flute (37), cor anglais an octave lower with flute descant (41), violins sul A with theme A's head against it (53, principle 18), winds and violins in octaves (57). Each return uses operations (a), (c) or (d) of PDF 118, as Ex. 213/214 do with the Kitezh theme.
