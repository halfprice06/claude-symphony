## What else I studied

I made my own MIDI reduction of the whole score (per beat and per eighth note). It reads mixed metre from the time-signature events and leaves out the timpani. With it I wrote out the Roman numerals for every bar and checked every pair of voices for parallel 5ths and octaves. The scan found no parallel 5ths, and every octave it found was an intended doubling. I compared the score with Dvořák 9/ii in the local corpus (MusicXML, my own parser). His bar 10 approaches the cadence with IV add-6, which is II6/5, then V7–I. That supports a II6/5 in the same place in this theme. It also supports keeping the authentic cadences at 14 and 30, so I dropped the idea of a deceptive cadence at 14. I read c112 (the previous critic on these units), c012 (which added the IV at 9 b3 because the horn's A♭→G is 1̂→7̂) and c219 (which put D♭3 in bassoon 2 there). I checked that my change answers both of their stated reasons.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 9 beat 3: IV becomes II6/5, the passing pre-dominant the theme lacks (applied)

**Bars 9–10 (and 25, 73 by comparison).**

**Problem:** Beat 3 of bar 9 is a plain IV in root position: D♭2 (bass clarinet, harp), D♭3 (bassoon 2), F3 (bassoon 1, harp), A♭3 (horn). A&S say II6/5 is "richer" than IV or II6 over the same bass (PDF 177–178, Ex. 12-3a). They also show it on a weak beat, made from a passing tone, leading to an accented V (Ex. 12-7, Schubert, PDF 181). Bar 9 is exactly that situation. Bassoon 2 already moves C3 at the downbeat toward B♭2 at bar 10, so the passing B♭ is in the line already. The doubled D♭ just delays it by one beat.

**Evidence:**
- With B♭2 on beat 3, the chord is D♭–F–A♭–B♭, which is II6/5.
- The horn's A♭3 becomes the dissonant 1̂ of II6/5. It resolves down to the leading tone G3 at bar 10, as A&S require (PDF 177–178).
- B♭2 is held into the V at bar 10 as a common tone (PDF 180–181).
- Each statement of the pre-dominant now differs by one tone, as PDF 183 describes: II6/5 at 9, IV7 at 25 and vi7 at 73. Before the change, 9 was the plainest of the three.
- The reasons recorded by earlier critics still hold:
  - c012's reason (the horn's 1̂→7̂ over a pre-dominant) is kept, and the A♭ is now an active dissonance.
  - c219's reason (the horn lands on A♭3 without a doubling) is kept, because bassoon 2 moves to B♭ rather than taking the A♭.
- Voice leading checked: the bass goes D♭→E♭, a step. B♭ is held. F3 and A♭3 move to E♭3 and G3, both by step, and that pair moves in parallel 3rds. There are no parallels.

**Fix (aOne.ily, aOneBsnTwo):**
```
c2 des4 | bes,2.) |      →      c2 bes,4~ | bes,2.) |
```
The slur already covers bars 9–10 and the tie stays inside it. Only one part changes, and the horn line is untouched.

### 2. Bar 74: keep V4/3; root position would create 5ths (not applied, recorded)

**Bars 73–74.** I considered putting V7 in root position under the vi7 at 73, which would make a stronger cadence approach. I rejected it.
- With E♭ in the bass at 74, the protected cello line C4→B♭3 moves against the bass A♭/C→E♭ in parallel 5ths. A&S warn about this when VI goes to V (PDF 167, parallel 10ths with the 3rd doubled).
- G in the bass is not possible either, because the melody is on G. A bass 7̂ cannot take soprano 7̂ (PDF 172–173 tables).
- The existing V4/3 (bass B♭, 2̂) is the inversion the tables allow under soprano 7̂.

No change.

### 3. Bars 50–51: the chord thirds are only in the violas' off-beat eighths (not applied, observation)

**Bars 50–51.**
- At 50 b1–2 the F♯-minor tonic sounds G♭2/G♭3/D♭5/G♭5 on the beats. Its third (A3) comes only from the violas' sotto voce off-beat eighths (`r8 a8-_`).
- At bar 51 the A♭7 has its C4 only in the same off-beat pattern.
- A&S allow the 5th to be left out of a seventh chord (PDF 177) but never the third.

The third does sound on every half beat, so the harmony is clear. The off-beat figure is a texture decision that c027/c202 recorded. Only the A&S rule on what may be omitted from a chord touches it, so I make no change.

If a later critic wants the third on the beat, the smallest fix is to have the harp's left hand at 50 b1 and 51 b1 take a single A3 and C4. An alternative is to change the violas' bar 51 to `c'4-_ c'4-_ c'4-_ c'4-_`. I did not test either.
