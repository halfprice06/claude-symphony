## Findings

All are records. None calls for a change to the score. They are ranked by how much each matters to the form.

### 1. The return (65–71) follows Ex. 19-14 exactly, and the 7th's real resolution is in bassoon 2 (record)

**Bars:** 65–71.

A&S say that over a held dominant the step to a passing 6/4 "does not resolve the 7th of V7, despite the downward motion"; the resolution comes later, "transferred to another voice" (19 §6, PDF 312–313, Ex. 19-14). The score does this. The horn's D♭4 at 68 b2 steps to C4 at 68 b3, but that C is the 6th of the passing tonic 6/4 over E♭ and not a resolution. The V7 returns at 69. Bassoon 2 holds D♭3 from 69 and moves to C3 at 71, under the A♭ of the return (retr.ily c377; aTwo.ily c052). Bsn 1 a third under the horn (B♭–A♭–G) makes the 8-7-6-5 over 6-5-4-3 of Ex. 19-15/19-16 (PDF 314–315).

For the form this matters because it is the only point where V7 goes to the reprise's I. The return depends on that one D♭3–C3. If 69–70 are re-scored later (breaths, thinning), bassoon 2's tied D♭3 into C3 at 71 must stay or pass to another voice. I add this to the do-not-touch line below.

### 2. Dvořák 9/ii closes each theme with a passing 6/4 into a plagal IV–I. This score closes its themes with ii7–V7–I and keeps the plagal close for the coda. Both are sound (record, no re-raise)

The sonority scan of the corpus Largo finds the same cadence three times, at 17–18, 38–39 and 112–113. The bass descends D♭–C–B♭–A♭–G♭–D♭ (I–V6–vi–I6/4–IV–I), with the 6/4 on beat 4 as a passing chord in a stepwise bass. That is the passing 6/4 over a moving bass of 19 §5 (PDF 310) inside an expanded passing motion (16 §8, PDF 257). At the last return (116–117), Dvořák holds a D♭/A♭ 6/4 for two beats, and the expected V never arrives: the theme breaks off unaccompanied (117–119) and D♭ returns at 120. That is the elided-V case of Ex. 19-30a (19 §18, PDF 323).

This score's theme cadences (12–14, 28–30, 80–82) use II6/5 or ii7 to V7 to I without a 6/4. Its plagal motion is saved for one place, the coda's IV6–iv–I (87–89). c283 settled this ("do not bring back cycle 12's beat-3 6/4s"), and I do not re-raise it. The comparison shows that the two movements use the same ingredients in a different order: Dvořák goes plagal at every phrase end, and this score only at the end. The score's choice gives the coda's plagal close more weight. A passing 6/4 between I6 and IV at 11 b2–b3 or 27 b2–b3 is the one Dvořák-like detail that would not touch a protected cadence. I tested it on paper and rejected it: the bass already rises C–D♭ there (I6–IV), so a 6/4 would need a falling bass and would reverse the rising bass at 11–12 that c102 protects.

### 4. 77–82: the cadenza principle applied to V (record, protected)

19 §11 (PDF 318–319, Ex. 19-23, 19-24) puts material between a cadential 6/4 and its V to build tension; the cadenza is the extreme case. At 77–81 the expected V–I is put off in the same way, without a 6/4. V7 (77) goes to ♭VI (78, the upper-neighbour idiom of 16 §10, PDF 258). A short passing 6/3 follows (78 b3, the form 16 §8 prefers for short passing chords), then II6/5 for nearly two bars at ff (79–80), and V♭9 only at 81. The delay gives the movement its second peak at 84 percent of the time. The first ♭VI neighbour (62–65) goes to A minor and the second (78) goes back to V, so the two uses are kept apart. This is on the standing list; no change.

### 5. 15–17: root-position IV and V on passing bass notes are correct here (record)

My passing-chord scan flagged one place: the bass C3–D♭3–E♭3–F3 at 15–17 has root-position IV (16) and V (16 b3) on its middle notes. 16 §8 (PDF 256) prefers 6/3 or 6/4 chords over bass passing tones in short spans. Its second paragraph (PDF 257) covers this case, though: a stepwise bass over a longer span is an expanded passing motion in which 5/3s are normal passing chords. Here the span is I6 (all of 15) to vi (17), more than two bars, and IV and V are real harmonies (c012: "I6 held through 15, IV on 16"). analysis.txt shows no parallels.

### 6. Proportions and the three pedal frames (record)

Section durations are unchanged since c423: total 345 s, climax at 61 (65 percent), second peak at 79 (84 percent). The three six-bar frames each prolong one chord by a device from this reading. 1–6 is I with the neighbouring iv6/4 (19 §4). 31–36 is I heard afterwards as the upper 5th of IV (16 §4), ending with the ascending step line A♭–B♭–C into D♭ at 36 b3 (16 §2). 65–70 is V7 with a passing 6/4 and 8-7-6-5 (19 §6–7). The climax chain at 58–61 places the reading's marks of the main chord (16 §1, PDF 250) on 61, which has the lowest bass of the movement (D♭1) and the fullest voicing. It does not place them on 53, where the chain starts. That fits 16 §8 (PDF 257): 53–60 is one passing motion with subordinate chords, and 58 b4's D♭7 is an applied dominant inside it (16 §1, chromatic alteration).

### 7. Not applicable in this score

Minor v (16 §11–14): every minor-key area is reached through major V, and no passage needs ♭7̂ over 5̂. The oscillating 6/4 (19 §15) is the waltz or march bass; Dvořák's C♯-minor middle section has 6/4s over a G♯ bass under C♯ minor (54, 58, 63 in the corpus file), but this score's B-section pizzicato bass moves by 3rds and 5ths and does not need it. The augmented-4th 6/4 (19 §17) does not occur.
