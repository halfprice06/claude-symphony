## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Oboe 1 part, 61–62 and 79–82: every stem points up (applied)
**Problem.** bSec.ily line 45 sets `\stemUp` for Oboe 1 at 61 (c014, so oboe 1 sits above oboe 2 on the shared score staff) and never reverts it. In the score the part-combiner covers this. In the Oboe 1 part the override runs on to the end: the F5 halves at 62, the B♭5–A♭5 at 79–80, the G5 and the quaver group E♭5–D♭5–C5–B♭4 at 81, and the A♭4 at 82 all have up-stems, although every note but the last is above the middle line. These bars are the climax and the theme's close. At 81 the beamed group has up-stems of 2.55 spaces inside the stave, and the beam slopes 0.81 at 2.9-space spacing.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 38: the majority of stems go in the "correct" direction. PDF 35: the shorter stem may be cut to 3 spaces inside the stave, no further. PDF 34: notes closer than three spaces take ¼–½ space.
**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, bSecOboeOne):** `\tag #'score \stemUp f''1--~\ff | f''2 fes''2-- \tag #'score \stemNeutral | R1*2 |`. The score is unchanged (pixel-identical pages). In the part, 79–82 now have normal down-stems, and the 81 group has stems of 3.24 and a slope of 0.19.

### 2. Transposed parts keep concert-pitch middle-line stems (applied)
**Problem.** Five `\once \stemUp` and `\tweak Stem.direction #UP` commands were written for a concert B♭4/B4 on the middle line of the score staff. That is a context choice, and the score is right to make it. The parts transpose the note off the middle line, but they keep the up-stem: Horn 1 12 b2 (written F5) and 54–55 (F5 tied), Clarinet 1 54–55 (C5 tied), Clarinet 2 72 b3 (C5), cor anglais 50 b2 (F♯5). Each is a single up-stem above the middle line among down-stems.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 38–39: the context rule applies only to notes on, or equidistant from, the middle line. A note off the line takes its normal direction.
**Fix (applied):** make each command score-only, as the file already does for labels: `\tag #'score \once \stemUp` in aOne.ily (aOneHnOne, bar 12), bSec.ily (bSecCorAnglais 50; bSecClOne and bSecHnOne 54 and 55, where the 55 `\tweak Stem.direction #UP bes'2` becomes `\tag #'score \once \stemUp bes'2`), and aTwo.ily (aTwoClTwo 72). The score is unchanged and the parts now have normal stems (checked on pp. 5, 6, 7, 12).

### 3. Parts: beams too steep for their spacing (applied, genparts.py only)
**Problem.** At part spacing, LilyPond keeps its usual 0.6–1.0 space slopes for stepwise quaver groups. Thirteen groups in the parts are steeper than ½ space with notes 1.7–2.9 spaces apart. Before → after:
- Clarinet 1 77: 0.81 → 0.38; Clarinet 1 81: 1.00 → 0.38. The 81 beam ran a whole space through the stave.
- Violins I and II 29: 0.81 → 0.19.
- Violin II 77 and 81: 0.81 → 0.38.
- Horn 1 13: 0.81 → 0.19.
- Clarinet 2 72: 0.81 → 0.38.
- Harp 53 b3, 54 b1 and b3: 0.62 → 0.38/0.19.
- Viola 71: 0.69 → 0.31.
- Cello 18: 0.69 → 0.31.
- Oboe 1 81: see finding 1.

The score's spacing is four spaces or more, so the score has none of these.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 34 (image): "Notes spaced very close together horizontally (closer than three spaces) take only a slight angle (¼ or ½ space) regardless of the interval". The example shows the same figure flat-ish when compressed and sloped when widely spaced. PDF 34 also says to avoid steep angles through stave-lines (lattice). PDF 20 and 22: ends stay on lines and slope with the outside interval.
**Fix (applied, genparts.py header, after the parts \layout):** a Beam.positions callback. It takes LilyPond's quanted result, and if the notes average under three spaces apart and the slope is over ½ space, it doubles the beam's damping and quants again. That way both ends still sit on lines and the slope keeps its direction and does not go flat:
```
#(define (gould-close-beam grob)
   (let* ((p (beam::place-broken-parts-individually grob))
          (n (length (ly:grob-array->list (ly:grob-object grob 'stems))))
          (xp (ly:grob-property grob 'X-positions)))
     (if (or (< n 2) (not (pair? xp)) (not (pair? p))) p
         (let ((sp (/ (- (cdr xp) (car xp)) (- n 1))))
           (let loop ((p p) (d (ly:grob-property grob 'damping 1)))
             (if (or (>= sp 3.0) (<= (abs (- (cdr p) (car p))) 0.55) (> d 40)) p
                 (begin (ly:grob-set-property! grob 'damping (* 2 d))
                        (loop (beam::place-broken-parts-individually grob) (* 2 d)))))))))
\layout { \context { \Voice \override Beam.positions = #gould-close-beam } }
```
Explicit `Beam.positions` overrides in the music (c195's Fl 1/Vln I 77, 78, 81) still take precedence. Every slope after the change is between 0.19 and 0.38, in the right direction. The parts book stays 30 pages, one page per part and two for the harp. Only pp. 3, 5, 6, 7, 12, 24, 26–29 differ from v255's.
