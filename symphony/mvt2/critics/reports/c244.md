## Findings

### 1. The harp's MIDI dynamics ignore c228's harp range (applied; MIDI only)
**Bars:** all harp entries.
**Problem:** c228 gave the harp the range ("orchestral harp" . (0 . 0.7)). The harp's dynamics are performed in the PianoStaff (`\consists "Dynamic_performer"`), and that context has no `midiInstrument`, so the equalizer never finds an entry. The harp plays at LilyPond's raw volume: pp at v41, p at v53, mf at v73, f at v83. At 7–14 the harp's pp accompaniment (v41) is almost level with the horn's p theme (v44). At 77 the harp's mf (v73) equals the cellos and is louder than Hn 2 (v50) and Bsn 1 (v32), who carry the counter-melody.
**Evidence:** Casanelles PDF 290: in a mix, volume is set per instrument so that the intended voice leads. PDF 287: fewer sounds in one range means a clearer line. c228 meant to do this (Casanelles PDF 133–134, 141–142) and the fix did not take effect.
**Fix (score.ly):** `\new PianoStaff \with { connectArpeggios = ##t midiInstrument = "orchestral harp" ... }`. Result: pp v29, p v37, mp v44, f v58. At 7 the harp is v29 under the horn's v44. The harp's p harmonic head at 83 (v37) is now level with the timpani's pp answer at 84 (v37). Before, both were about 53–54.

### 2. The ppp timpani roll at 67–69 is louder than Hn 1's p tail in the render (applied; MIDI only; closes c240's open item)
**Bars:** 67–69, and every timpani note.
**Problem:** In print the horn leads (Hn 1 p<mp>pp, roll ppp "near the rim"). In score.midi the roll is one held stroke at v47. Hn 1 enters at v44 on the same beat. The reason is LilyPond's default timpani range (0.2 . 0.9): its floor puts ppp (0.375) above the horn's p (0.352). Every soft timpani note is raised the same way. The pp answer at 84 was v54, above the harp's p head.
**Evidence:** Casanelles PDF 290: a sound with a constant dynamic "becomes perceptually more present". A roll is that kind of sound, so in a real hall it sits under a moving solo only if it is clearly softer. The score already marks it two levels softer, and the render should agree. Adler part 1 PDF 21: the timpani belong with the brass, so the horn's range is the right one to use.
**Fix (score.ly, MIDI block):** `instrumentEqualizer = #(lambda (s) (or (assoc-get s '(("timpani" . (0.1 . 0.7)))) (assoc-get s instrument-equalizer-alist) (assoc-get s '(...existing list...))))`. The timpani entry is listed first because the default list has its own timpani entry. Result: 67 roll v31 against horn v44; 61 and 79 f v62 against horns f v62–63; 84 pp v37. I tried a `\set Staff.midiExpression = #0.7` at 67 first (c240's suggestion). I dropped it, because it fixed only one passage and left the floor problem everywhere else.

### 3. The harp's crescendo at 75–77 ends at mf, above the counter-melody's mp (applied; printed change; closes c241's open item)
**Bars:** 75–77 (harp LH dynamic at 77 b1).
**Problem:** At 77 only the harp is marked mf. Vc, Vln I/II are mp<, Hn 2 reaches mp, and Bsn 1 is p<. The harp RH G3–E♭4 at 77 b2 encloses the counter-melody's B♭3 (Vc, Hn 2, Bsn 1), and the LH E♭1/E♭2 adds a fresh attack under it. A plucked chord at a louder level than the line in its own register puts the line behind it.
**Evidence:** Casanelles PDF 287: "sounds in the same frequency range mask each other". The EQ goal of giving each instrument its own range (PDF 285–286) can only be reached here through dynamics, because the voicing is protected (c127). Adler part 1 PDF 113–114 (c116's source) says the middle register carries well, which is a reason not to mark it louder.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpL):** `<es,, es,>2.\mp\arpeggio` (was `\mf`). The f at 78 b3 with the orchestra stays. The harp still grows with the orchestra (p<mp, then f), as Hn 2 does (p<mp, then mf< at 78 b3).

### 4. 79–81: four horns at f inside the strings' ff (recorded, no change)
By PDF 289, f brass is spectrally dense and comes forward, so the horns marked f could cover the ff theme. The theme, however, is in three octaves with winds (B♭6/B♭5/B♭4 at c034, c187), and the horns sit at C4–F4, an octave or more below the lowest theme octave. The cellos and Bsn 1 already carry the counter-melody at ff (c217). Lowering the horns to mf would leave the counter-melody without brass weight. Keep it.

### 5. Timpani rolls render as single strokes (recorded, untested)
Every `:32` roll in score.midi (2–3, 60, 67–70, 79–81) is one note-on that decays. A real roll holds a steady level (PDF 290, Fig. 6.6, right-hand envelope). In the render the roll at 67 is an attack at 67 b1 that is gone by 69. At 79–81 the f roll is the same. The print is correct and nobody here can hear the render, so I did not change it. If wanted, the rolls could be unfolded for MIDI only, using a tagged `\repeat unfold` voice inside a separate `\score { ... \midi {} }`. That is a larger change to score.ly and would need a MIDI note-count check (c240's note).

### 6. 63–64: Tbn 1 ff quotation over sf string tremolo (recorded, follows)
The quotation is the only ff line, and it is brass, the densest spectrum in the bar (PDF 289). The strings fall from sf to p under it, and the winds are silent. This follows the reading. Keep it.
