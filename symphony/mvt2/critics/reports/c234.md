## Findings

### 1. Bars 80 b3–81 b1: the woodwind choir has no F♭ in the half-diminished chord and the minor ninth (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 80 b3 the harmony becomes iiø7 (B♭–D♭–F♭–A♭), and at 81 b1 it is V♭9 (the "V♭9 at 80–81" on the standing list). F♭ is the note that defines both chords. In the whole orchestra it sounds only in horn 3 at F♭4 and, since c227, in the lower half of the violas at the same pitch. Fifteen woodwind players are holding notes there, and none plays F♭. They play Fl 1 A♭6, Ob 1/Cl 1 A♭5, Cl 2 D♭5, C.a. A♭4, Bsn 1 B♭3, B.cl/Bsn 2 B♭2 and Cbn B♭0, which is B♭–D♭–A♭. Heard by itself the woodwind choir gives a B♭-minor sound with no fifth instead of the half-diminished chord. Flute 2 and oboe 2 stop on 80 b3 (c054) at exactly the moment the new chord tone arrives.

**Evidence.** Sebesky, PDF 102: "Whenever possible, voice each separate body of instruments as a complete-sounding entity within the total ensemble sound." The same point is made on PDF 86 ("enable it to sound complete within itself while still contributing to the overall ensemble sound") and PDF 98. PDF 110, Ex. 136, names "the harsh, incomplete sound of the woodwind voicing considered as a separate entity" as something to use only on purpose. PDF 88: a chord tone given to one colour inside a larger texture "will be lost in the overall sound". Here the only F♭ is at F♭4, among horns 1·2 and 4, the cellos and bassoon 1 on the counter-melody. PDF 81: unlike woodwinds want at least a third between neighbours. An oboe on F♭5 has A♭5 (Ob 1/Cl 1) a third above and D♭5 (Cl 2) a third below. c227 already found that the F♭ was too thin and doubled it in the violas at the same octave. This fix gives the woodwinds their own F♭ an octave higher, in the register where they are playing.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-234/work/sections/aTwo.ily).** Oboe 2 takes F♭5 at 80 b3 and again at 81 b1. It breathes at the barline with oboe 1 and drops out on 81 b2, where oboe 1 sings the resolution E♭5 in the same octave:

```lilypond
aTwoOboeTwo = { R2.*8 | f''2.~\ff | f''2 fes''4 \breathe | fes''4\> r2\! | R2. | }
```

(was `f''2.~\ff | f''2 r4 | R2.*2 |`). This keeps c054's reason: oboe 2 does not double G4–A♭4 in the diminuendo and has left by 81 b2. I did not tie the F♭ over the bar. On the shared oboe staff a tie would run through the breath comma that oboe 1 takes at the end of 80, and a re-struck F♭ after a shared breath is plain to read. On 81 b1 the two oboes have the same rhythm and the same `\>`, so the part-combiner prints one chord (F♭5–G5, a minor third in sound). Nothing else changes: harmony, outer voices, horns, violas, ending. The F♭5 doubles horn 3's F♭4 an octave higher, which is a doubling and not a voice-leading change.

### Items checked against the reading and left unchanged

- **29–30, horns 3·4 a seventh then a sixth apart** (E♭3 under D♭4–C4). Sebesky allows wider spacing "for reasons dictated by logical voice leading" (PDF 103). B♭3→A♭3 in horn 4 would make octaves with Vln II's B♭4→A♭4 into 30, and G3 was ruled out by c052. No change.
- **60–62, B.cl doubling B.tbn on the root.** Sebesky allows this in the lower-middle register (PDF 90 B(1)). At 62 it is low (G♭2–F♭2), where he would use one instrument only. But at 62 the cellos have the theme, so B.tbn and B.cl are the only 8′ bass, and without the B.cl the 8′ bass would be one trombone against the ff tutti. No change.
- **79 b1, Cl 2 F4** (written G4, a weak open tone) in unison with horn 3 at ff, before the clarinet leaps to D♭5. It adds nothing audible (PDF 78, 106) and does no harm. Changing it would mean re-writing the 78 b3 pickup. No change.
