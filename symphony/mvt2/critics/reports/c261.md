On the lens question. Theme A is the tune a listener keeps. It is a parallel period: phrase 2 repeats phrase 1's rhythm (dotted quarter, eighth, tied quarter, then three quarters), so the second phrase is recognised as it starts, and its peak (B♭ appoggiatura at 12, 28, 76; B♭6 at 79) is reached by the same gesture each time. It is heard complete three times, an octave higher and more heavily doubled each time (horn A♭3, violin unison A♭4, violins in octaves A♭5/A♭4). That is the Snegourotchka plan on PDF 61 and the same economy as Dvořák's Largo. Theme B's memorable part is the consequent with the rising fourth A♭-D♭ (C.a. 41, violins and Fl/Ob 57). The string doubling of both themes matches the reading. The candidate changes the chapter suggests (Findings 1-4) would each break a protected passage or blur the order of statements, so I recommend none of them. Finding 5 is a note for later critics.

## Findings

### 1. The cello A string is never given a cantabile (no change recommended)

**Bars.** 23-30, 44-47, 57-60, 71-82.

**What the reading says.** PDF 56: cellos are "more often entrusted with tense passionate cantabile melody", "usually laid out for the top string (A) which possesses a wonderfully rich 'chest' quality". Ex. 7 (Pan Voyevoda, PDF 180) gives a broad melody to the cellos alone, later doubled by Vln I an octave higher (PDF 61). This is a standard Romantic slow-movement colour (the cello tune in Brahms's Piano Concerto No. 2/iii, for instance).

**The score.** The cellos sing in four places, none on the A string: the counter-melody 23-30 and 71-78 (E♭3-D♭4, G and D strings), theme B's head in B♭ minor at 45-46 (A♭3-E♭4, marked sul D), and the counter-line 58-60 (E♭3-G♭3).

**Would it help?** I tested the only practical placement on paper: the A′ counter-melody 71-78 an octave higher (E♭4-D♭5, A string). It crosses above Vln II's lower octave of theme A at 73 b3 (Vc C5 over A♭4), through 74 (B♭4 and G4 over G4 and E♭4-F4), at 77 b3 (D♭5 over C5-B♭4) and at 78 b1-2 (C♭5 over A♭4). PDF 54 allows crossing, but here it would cloud the theme's lower octave, which PDF 59 says gives the high line its "fulness of tone and firmness". The A′ texture plan 71-82 and the cello counter-melody are protected (c247, c257) for the same reason. At 45-46 the D string is the Antar [63] layout (PDF 56), and the darker string suits the minor key. A cello A-string tune would need new material, and the movement has no free bars for it.

**Fix.** None. Recorded so later critics do not reopen it on PDF 56 alone.

### 2. Vln I / Vln II + Vla for the A′ theme (no change recommended)

**Bars.** 71-78.

**What the reading says.** PDF 60 (c): with violins and violas in octaves, "Vn I / Vn II + Violas" gives "the lower part a fuller and more cantabile quality". Rimsky also writes that the violins' octave doubling "secures expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre" (PDF 59).

**The score.** Vln I on the upper octave (A♭5-F6), Vln II on the lower (A♭4-F5), violas on the eighth-note figure (cycle 7; A′ texture plan protected by c247), cellos on the counter-melody.

**Would it help?** Putting the violas on Vln II's octave would remove the moving figure, the main texture change that keeps 71-74 from repeating 23-26 (an open point once, settled as "theme A reprise varied by setting", c251). The lower octave already has its fullness: Vln II plays it from 70 b3, sul D, and Cl 1 and Ob 1 join it from 77-79. PDF 60 offers the option for a fuller lower line; it does not require it.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Crossing of parts as a means of relief (not applicable)

**Bars.** 23-30, 71-78.

**What the reading says.** PDF 54 lists crossing ("violoncellos above the violas and violins") among the natural ways to make a melody stand out. Ex. 35 (Capriccio espagnol, PDF 213) puts cellos above the violins in sixths, which PDF 64 calls "distinctly original".

**The score.** Crossing would only matter where the theme is in the middle of the texture. In this score theme A always lies on top when the strings carry it, so it already stands out by position (PDF 54). The one middle-register tune with an instrument above it is the C.a. at 41-44, under Fl 1's line in tenths and sixths, a dynamic softer (protected in HISTORY.md as "Fl/CA 41–44" and "Fl 1 a dynamic below the cor anglais"). That is the dynamic-shading method of PDF 54, and the woodwind chapter (PDF 64-80) covers it, not this one.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bass at 23-28 and 71-78 while the cellos are on the counter-melody (follows)

**Bars.** 23-28 (Cb unison, 8-foot octave, A♭2-E♭3), 71-78 (Cb A♭1 octave with harp LH).

**What the reading says.** PDF 61 (f): the bass is "usually constructed" from cellos and basses in octaves. PDF 61 (g): violas and basses in octaves are used "only when the 'cellos are otherwise employed". PDF 56: no "phrase of any importance" for the double bass without cellos or bassoons.

**The score.** Here the cellos are "otherwise employed". The bass is a harmonic line in half and quarter notes, not a phrase, so PDF 56 does not apply. On the strong beats the cellos meet it at the octave (F at 24 b1, A♭ at 25 b1 and 27 b1). Giving the violas the bass, as layout (g) allows, would take them off the divided chords at 23-30 and the figure at 71-78. Ex. 12 (PDF 186), the model for 23-30, also has the basses alone under divided cellos and violas. c249's 8-foot unison follows PDF 62 Note II.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. For later critics: what this chapter has settled

Four readings (c011, c101, c191, c261) have now checked the string melody layouts against PDF 54-63, and the score follows every rule. In the third series, more points will come from the melodic substance than from the doubling. Two open questions for the melody lens, outside this reading, that a later critic could take up with a different book:
- Theme B's antecedent (37-40, Fl 1) is all steps and neighbour turns, one turn figure per bar (c251: 16 of 17 intervals are steps). The consequent with the rising fourth is the part a listener keeps. c251 kept the design; an A&S Unit 20 or Adler melodic-writing reading would be the place to test it again.
- After 61, theme B returns only through the motto tail. Dvořák 9/ii also leaves its middle theme behind, so this is a choice, not a fault.

**Fix.** None.
