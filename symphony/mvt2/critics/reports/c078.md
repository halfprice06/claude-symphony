## Findings

### 1. Flute 1's leap to B♭6 at 76 sounds early and loud. Re-raised with c073's reason answered. (Applied in scratch.)
**Bars:** 75-77, flute 1.
**Problem.** Flute 1 enters at 75 on A♭5 at p< and doubles Vln I. On 76 beat 2 it leaps a fourth from F6 to B♭6, which is the climax pitch held ff at 79. c073 closed the point "B♭6 at 76 before the climax" because the 76 note is "one beat long, at mp". The reading answers that reason directly. A6 and above is the flute's "a bit shrill" register, where the player "can only play extremely loud" (PDF 185). Pitches above A6 must be approached "with extreme caution… the best approach is by scale" (PDF 186). So the mp that c073 relies on cannot be played. A B♭6 attacked by a leap comes out at about f, three bars before the ff B♭6. At 79 the composer applied this same rule: c036 approaches that B♭6 by a grace-note scale and cites PDF 186. Bar 76 is the only B♭6 in the movement taken by leap. The flute also plays 75-78 without a break and then holds the ff B♭6 for five beats, where PDF 188 asks for rest after sustained passages.
**Fix (applied).** Flute 1 rests at 75-76 and enters on G6 at 77, at the dynamic the violins have reached by then. Its first B♭6 is then the scale run into 79:
```
aTwoFluteOne = {
  R2.*4 |
  R2.*2 | g'''4(\mf\< es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 | aes''2)\f \breathe \afterGrace c'''4( { f'''16 g''' aes''' } |
  bes'''2.~\ff | ...   (unchanged from here)
```
**What stays the same.** The violins still carry both octaves at 75-76, and Cl 1 joins the lower octave at 75 as before. The flute's B♭6 at 79-81 and the grace run are unchanged. HISTORY protects "A′ octaves 75-81" and "B♭6 at pitch". This fix keeps the octaves in the strings and changes only which bar the flute joins in. It undoes c033's Fl 1 p< at 75. The composer has to decide whether the reading answers c073. I think it does.

### 2. The first statement of theme B, flute 1 at 37-40, has no dynamic shaping. (Applied.)
**Bars:** 37-40.
**Problem.** The flute plays `f''2(\p ... c''2 bes'2)` for four bars with no hairpin, in its "sweet, with little carrying power" register (F5-G♭5; PDF 185). Every soft middle-register flute solo Adler prints is shaped inside the phrase. Ex. VII-4 (Brahms 4/iv) puts < > on each figure. Ex. VII-5 (Tchaikovsky) gives "p dolcissimo" with a swell to the high note. Ex. VII-10 (Piston) has mp< ... mf (PDF 186-188). The score shapes the same theme everywhere else: the cor anglais at 41-44 (p< mp>), the violins at 45-48 and 53-56, the flute at 57-60. The unshaped bars are the theme's first and most exposed statement, which is also where the thin accompaniment (Vln II pp sul tasto, Vla pp, pizz. p) leaves the most room.
**Fix (applied).** Swell to the head's held F5/G♭5 and to the G♭5 peak of bar 39, staying inside p. No new dynamic letters:
```
f''2(\p\< ges''8 f''8 es''4\! | des''4\> c''8 des''8 es''2)\! | ges''4(\< \tuplet 3/2 4 { f''8 ges'' f'' } es''4\> des''4 | c''2\! bes'2) \breathe |
```

### 3. At the ff climax, 61-62, clarinet 2 lies entirely in the throat register. (Not applied.)
**Bars:** 61-62, clarinets.
**Problem.** Clarinet 2 plays F4, G♭4, A♭4 concert (written G4, A♭4, B♭4). Clarinet 1's A♭4 at 61 beats 1-2 is written B♭4. Adler calls this register "throat tones, rather pale" (Ex. VII-56, PDF 210). It is the weakest part of the instrument, at the movement's loudest moment. c014 put the clarinets in unison with the trumpets there. c060 then removed the trumpets, so the clarinets now add a pale colour that the horns already cover in the same octave (Hn 3 A♭4-B♭4-C♭5, Hn 2 F4-G♭4-A♭4). Their ff adds almost nothing.
**Fix (suggested, untested).** Clarinet 2 an octave higher, in the clarino ("bright, incisive"), filling the space between the oboes and the flutes:
```
bSecClTwo ... \partCombineAutomatic f''1--\ff | ges''2-- aes''2-- | R1*2 |
```
This doubles Ob 1's F5 at 61. Check 62 against Ob 1/2 and Fl 2 for exposed octaves before adopting it. The 61-62 layout is protected (c077 build order). This changes register only, not pitch classes or the order of entries.

### 4. Slur the bass clarinet's moving bass under the horn solo, 8-9 and 11-12. (Applied.)
**Bars:** 8, 9, 11, 12, bass clarinet.
**Problem.** The horn's dolce espressivo theme rests on a pp wind chorale. The bass clarinet's moving quarter notes (8: F2-B♭1-E♭2; 9: A♭2-D♭2; 11: A♭2-C3-D♭3; 12: D♭3-B♭2) are written without slurs, so each one is tongued (PDF 187: "all separately notated pitches are tongued, while slurred ones are played legato"). Each beat is then a fresh attack in the low register at pp, under a legato horn. The bassoons' repeated notes have to be re-tongued, but the bass clarinet's changing pitches do not.
**Fix (applied).**
```
aes,2.\pp | f,4( bes,,4 es,4) | aes,2( des,4) | es,2 r4 |
aes,4( c4 des4) | des2( bes,4) | es,2. | aes,2. |
```
Pitches, dynamics and the c036 breath at 10 are unchanged. The horn line and the layout of 7-14 are untouched.

### 5. Bassoon 2's F2 at pp, bar 73. (Applied.)
**Bars:** 73 beat 3.
**Problem.** `aes,2 f,4` under pp. F2 is the top note of the bassoon's lowest perfect fifth, which Adler says is "extremely difficult to play… pianissimo… one must expect a firmer attack" (PDF 226). That happens on the soft first bar of the reprise. The note doubles the basses' and harp's F an octave above, so the F is already in the bass. At the parallel bar 9 the composer already wrote F3 for bassoon 2.
**Fix (applied).** `c2.\pp | f4 bes,4 es4 | aes,2 f4 | bes,2 r4 |` (F3, as at 9). allpairs_par.py shows no new pair. Against the cellos it moves A♭–A♭ octave to C–F fifth, with no parallel.

### 6. Recorded, no change: clarinet 2's E♭4 hold, 89-92.
About 19 s at ppp plus the fermata. Adler gives the clarinet the full dynamic range down to a niente (PDF 210-211), so the dynamic is playable. The breath is the open question, and c076 already recorded a fallback for that. PDF 188's Brahms dovetail would need clarinet 1 to take the E♭4 over at 91. That changes the protected morendo, so I am only noting it.
