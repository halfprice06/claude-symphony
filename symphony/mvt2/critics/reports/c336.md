## Findings

1. **Bars 61-62: horn 4, a low horn, plays the top line a2 with horn 3 (written E♭5-F5-G♭5, f).** This is the one place where the score departs from the reading. Adler PDF 303 makes 1 and 3 the high horns and 2 and 4 the low horns. Here horn 4 is above horns 1 and 2. I do not re-raise it, for three reasons:
   - c086 settled the a2 after c064/c069, and it is on the do-not-touch list.
   - Its reason still holds. With the trumpets tacet, two horns in unison are needed on the brass top line to match a trombone at f.
   - Ex. X-4 places written G♭5 well inside the normal range, and Adler warns only about pedal notes and constant high playing.

   The only swap that would follow PDF 303 strictly is horn 1 a2 with horn 3, with horn 4 taking horn 1's middle line. That costs horn 1's lips 25 s before its exposed p solo at 67, which c046 avoided on PDF 303's own advice about rests. No change recommended.

2. **Horn 1's endurance over the whole movement (PDF 303: regular rests).** Horn 1's playing and rest spans are:

   | 6 b3-14 | solo | about 33 s, breath at 10 |
   | 15-52 | rest | about 2½ min |

   Written F5 occurs only at 12 (mp) and 54-55 (pp<mp). Horn 1 plays written G♭5 only at 62 (ff); every other bar stays in the deep-and-solid or bright register. This follows the reading, and no change is needed. The 17 s rest before the 67 solo is short, but the 61-62 notes are written C5-E♭5, in the middle register, as c194 recorded.

4. **Low notes (PDF 303: low notes sustained or slow).** Every note at or below written C4 is a held note at p/pp:
   - Hn 2, written B♭3-C4 at 75 (minims);
   - Hn 4, written B♭3 tied at 29-30;

   - Hn 1's pickup, written B♭3-C4 at 6.

   None is in a fast figure, and none uses the B♭-side pedal notes. Follows.

5. **Mute timing and marking (PDF 307-308).**
   - "mettere sord." is printed at 82, five bars (about 17 s) before "con sord." at 87.
   - "via sord." is printed at 91, in the score and in both parts.
   - The scherzo's horns 3·4 first play at its bar 73, marked "senza sord." (coherence/scherzo.md line 12), so they have ample time.

   A straight non-transposing mute is assumed; no "stopped" mark is used, so the player will not confuse the two. Follows.

6. **Leaps (PDF 304).**
   - The only leap wider than a fourth in any horn part is horn 4's octave, written E♭4 at 60 (p<) to E♭5 at 61 (f), across a printed breath. That is an easy interval to find after a breath, on a doubled note, with horn 3 entering on the same pitch.
   - Horn 1's fourths, written C5-F5 at 12 and 54, sit inside the theme's arpeggio, so the player hears them in advance.

   Follows.

7. **Notation (PDF 304).**
   - The score is headed "Full score in C".
   - The horn parts are transposed to F in treble clef. The lowest written note, B♭3, needs only one ledger line, so no bass clef and no old-notation note are needed.
   - Each part's title says "in F", and "1. solo" / "solo, dolce espr." appear where the reading's solo examples (X-9, X-11, X-24) print them.

   Follows.

8. **Stopped horn, cuivré, glissando, trills, multiple tonguing (PDF 308-311).** None of these is used. That suits an Adagio whose horn writing is lyrical and choral (PDF 311-312: "accompaniment choir" in the middle register). I do not suggest adding any; c328 already recorded "no stopped horn".
