### 1. Release title block (score) and part titles. Applied.

**Problem.** The score's subsubtitle reads "Full score in C — draft v200". The parts print "Symphony No. 1 — II. Adagio" as one title line. Movement I's parts print "Symphony No. 1 in C minor" with the movement as subtitle.

**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 6: print the full title as in a concert programme, and add a print or revision date. Movement I's release (its c200) dropped "draft" and kept the version and date: "Full score in C — v221, 12 October 2026". Adler part 2 PDF 291 item 2: the set must be organised and labelled consistently.

**Fix (score.ly).** Change the first line of the subsubtitle and leave the other three lines as they are:
```
subsubtitle = \markup \center-column { "Full score in C — v201, 13 October 2026" \small "Contrabassoon and double basses sound an octave lower than written" \small "Trumpets tacet" \small "Follows movement I attacca: bar 1 continues its last chord" }
```

### 2. The viola part spills onto a second page, with a turn at bar 83 (side effect of c199; breaks c165's "viola part one page"). Applied.

**Problem.** v200's parts book has 31 pages, against 30 in every version from v166 to v199. The viola part's page 2 holds one system (84–92). On one line, c199's text "unis., change bow at will" at 89 widened the last system, and the system no longer fits on page 1. The violas must turn between 82 b3 and 84 b3, which is bar 83: the harp harmonics play there alone over the ppp pedal. Earlier critics cleared that bar so the harmonics would be heard alone (c063, c124).

**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 7: put the turn in a rest or an unexposed passage, best with a multi-bar rest at the end of the page. Adler part 2 PDF 299 item 5: even in string parts, consider the effect of half the section turning. c165 protected the one-page viola part, and genparts.py keeps special viola spacing for exactly this reason.

**Fix (coda.ily, codaVla, bar 89).** Set the same words on two lines, in the c065 style used at bar 22:
```
es2.~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "unis.," "change bow at will" } | es2.~ | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata |
```
The viola part returns to one page with the v199 line breaks. The score's page 8 is unchanged apart from the two-line text, and score.midi is byte-identical.

### 3. The timpani muta for the scherzo at 92 is cut off in the part. Applied.

**Problem.** In the timpani part, the part-only text at 92 is centred on the last bar. It runs past the right margin and prints as "muta A♭ in E, C in G, D♭ in B, E♭ in" and "(for the Scherzo, during the paus". The player loses the drum E♭ goes to.

Both lines now print in full under 86–92, ending at the final barline. The score does not show this text, because timp is tagged 'part there.

### 6. Cyclic placement between movements I, III and IV: no change

- The join II→III is a common-tone modulation of the *Widmung* type (A&S PDF 586–587). A♭ major falls a major third to E (= F♭, ♭VI), and the common tone A♭/G♯ is exposed: Vln II A♭4 at 89–92, taken by the scherzo's Hn 1 G♯4. The E♭ in the last chord becomes D♯. The join I→II (C→A♭, C5 held) is the same device a major third higher. The three keys C–A♭–E and the three closes (A♭, E, C) form the major-third cycle that A&S PDF 600–601 describes as a key plan enlarging a motive. Keep the last chord as it is.
- The plagal tail at 85–89 (♮6–♭6–5 under 5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂) is the model that the scherzo (365–368) and the finale (273–275) answer. A&S PDF 395 marks ♮6–♭6–5 as good usage. It must stay.
