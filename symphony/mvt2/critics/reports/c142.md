## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 53 b4 → 54 b1: harp figure and violins in consecutive fifths (applied)

**Problem.** c137 rewrote the harp's 53 b4 figure as C4–E♭4–A♭4, so that it would avoid the horn and clarinet's A♭3 pickup and the A♭3–G♭3 / E♭5–D♭5 fifths that its first draft had created. The figure now ends on A♭4, a fifth under the violins' E♭5. It then starts 54 on G♭3, a twelfth under their D♭5. Both parts fall, so the fifths are back an octave higher on the same pitch classes. The A♭4 is the figure's top note and the last attack before the barline, so it is the most audible note of the group. My scans and allpairs_par.py both report it, and it is the only parallel fifth in the movement.

**Evidence.** A&S use the 7th chords of a 5ths chain to "break up the 5ths that occur on the first beat of each measure" (PDF 404, Ex. 24-6). Applied chords in a stepwise progression are there to "prevent the 5ths and octaves that would otherwise occur between the main chords" (PDF 434, Ex. 25-31; PDF 435, Ex. 25-32). Fifths into a downbeat are the case the reading guards against. A harp figure at p is not exempt, because it is the only moving part between the bass and the theme in 53–56.

**Fix (applied).** `sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecHarpR`, bar 53 beats 3–4:
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' c' es' c' } |   % was: aes8 c' es' c' es' aes'
```
Beat 4 now rocks C4–E♭4–C4. This is the two-note motion the figure uses in 56, so no new figure is added. The figure's top note at 53 b4 is E♭4, an octave under the melody, and the next attack is G♭3. There are no fifths into 54. c137's goals still hold: no A♭3 under the counter-line's pickup, and no A♭3–G♭3 fifths. Pedals are unchanged (C and E♭ are set). This changes pitches only, so it satisfies the c124 rule as c137 did.

### 2. Bars 39 b3–4: the 7th of the half-diminished seventh is doubled, and the sustained voice leaves it by leap (applied)

**Problem.** The chord at 39 b3 is the half-diminished seventh on C (C–E♭–G♭–B♭) over the bass C. c141's Vln II re-voicing holds B♭4 from 38 b3 through 39 b4 and puts G♭4 under it. The viola figure already has B♭3 on b3 and again on b4. The 7th is therefore in two parts, and neither resolves down by step. Vln II leaps B♭4 → G♭4 at 40, and the viola rises B♭3 → C4. The 7th's only resolution is the bass's chromatic A♮ at 40 (PDF 406–407 allow a transfer into the bass). The doubling is the fault. v141 did not have it, because its Vln II had E♭4–G♭4 at 39 b3.

**Evidence.** "Rule of doubling: Don't double the temporary leading tone or the 7th" (PDF 439 item 3). The 7th must be able to resolve down by step (PDF 399). A transfer is convincing only when one voice carries the dissonance (PDF 406–408, Ex. 24-11, 24-14).

**Fix (applied).** `sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecVlnTwo`, bar 39:
```
<des' bes'>2 ges'2 |   % was: <des' bes'~>2 <ges' bes'>2
```
Both halves of the divisi play G♭4 on b3–4 and split again at 40 (`<es' ges'>2 <des' f'>2`, unchanged). The 7th B♭ is now only in the viola figure and passes to the bass's A♮. c141's aims still hold. The upper line is still A♭ | B♭ | B♭ G♭ | G♭ F, with G♭ arriving half a bar earlier. The section still avoids the flute's E♭5 at 39 b3, and the B♭4 tie from 38 is kept. At 40 the 7th of vii°7/vi (G♭) is in both Vln II and the viola figure, as it was in v141. Both parts resolve it correctly to F at 40 b3, so I left it.

### 3. Bar 60 b4½: the anticipated F sounds against the held 7th G♭ in the same octave (no change; recorded)

Hn 1, Bsn 1 and Vc anticipate F3/F4 on the last eighth of 60 (c082). Meanwhile Tbn 2 holds G♭3 (c132) and the violas hold G♭3/G♭4, which is the 7th of V7. That makes a minor second F3–G♭3 inside the trombone and cello register for one eighth. The reading treats anticipation as a normal way to bring in a resolution early (PDF 425, Ex. 25-14f, Bach's "Ant. prevents hidden 5ths"). The 7th still resolves in its own voices at 61 (Tbn 2 G♭3–F3; violas to D♭4–F4), so this is a delayed resolution, not an abandoned 7th (PDF 409–410). Bars 59–60 are protected ("approach to V at 59–60"). The clash is short and at the allargando. I record it as heard and correct, not as a fault.
