The assigned reading was enough, so I did not use the corpus or the web.

Verdict: v567 follows every principle in the chapter that applies to an original slow movement. The texture is layered and ranked. Answering voices enter late, as in Dvořák. The background changes colour at each entry of theme B, as in Ravel. Counter-lines are built from the harmony and passed between instruments, as in Schoenberg. None of the points below separates this score from a master's work, so I recommend no change and applied none.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 41-44, 53, 57, 75: delayed entrances in an answering voice (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** Adler PDF 213 on Dvořák's Slavonic Dance 8: "The delay of the oboe entrance clarifies the tune much better, and makes the last four measures sound truly polyphonic." The image (PDF 211, mm. 25-32) shows the tune in piccolo, flute and Vln I from m. 25. The oboes enter a2 at m. 28 with their own line, doubled by Vln II.

**Score.** The same device appears four times. At 41 b3, two beats into the C.a.'s statement of theme B, Fl 1 enters pp above it. It runs mostly in sixths (42: F5/A♭4, G♭5/B♭4, E♭5/G♭4) and joins the C.a.'s rhythm. At 53 b4 Cl 1 and Hn 1 bring in theme A's head a bar after the violins start theme B. At 57 b3 the cellos leave the bass (they double it at 57 b1) for an espressivo line. At 75 Hn 2 and Bsn 1 join the cello counter-melody four bars late. In each case the second voice comes in while the first is already established, and the tune stays on top. This answers c547's "no imitation": the chapter's polyphony in a homophonic piece is this kind of late answer, not canon.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bars 57-60: one counter-line in three colours under the tune (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** PDF 203: study "the layers of the music" and determine "their order of importance". PDF 230-231: Schoenberg adds "a nice counterpoint made up of the pitches found in the measure, but having its own rhythm", which passes "first in the second horn and the bassoons, then in the violas".

**Score.** In 57-60 the tune is in Fl 1, Ob 1 and both violin sections. Under it is one counter-line: Hn 1 (A♭4 G♭4 | F4 E♭4 ...) with the cellos an octave lower from 57 b3 (G♭3 | F3 E♭3 ... G♭3 E♭3 F♭3) and Bsn 1 joining at 59. Then come the clarinets' held thirds, the viola triplets (sextuplets at 59-60) and the bass (B.cl., Bsn 2, Cb). layers.py finds four to five rhythmic layers in each of 57-59, each in its own colour. The counter-line uses chord tones and passing notes and does not change the harmony. Only one secondary line runs against the tune, which keeps the build from becoming "too colourful" (PDF 203). Build order 53-61 is protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 61-62: homorhythmic climax (recorded, no change)

**Score.** layers.py finds two layers at 61 and three at 62. Almost every instrument moves in half notes, with the violas' tremolo as the only motion inside the chord.

**Reading.** PDF 230 (Schoenberg's Brahms, mm. 75-77): "The leanness of the texture (essentially two-part) makes the color and the big chords very successful." The two bars carry theme B's peak over a stepwise bass (D♭-G♭-F♭). The trombone quotation then enters alone over tremolo at 63. A counter-line at 61-62 would crowd the quotation's entry. The 61-62 brass layout and Tbn 1 alone at 63-64 are protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Bars 45-47 and 87-92: sustained "harmonic counterpoint" (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** PDF 209 (Dvořák m. 9): "a strong, but soft color for the melody, a light accompaniment, and sustained 'harmonic counterpoint' in the strings", which completes "the harmony without making it heavy".

**Score.** At 45-46 Ob 1 has theme B's head, doubled an octave lower by Bsn 1 and the cellos. Vln II, violas and basses hold the harmony: two layers. At 47 everything is held under Ob 1's last note and the C.a.'s pickup. At 87-92 the strings hold the plagal close while C.a., Hn 4 and the violas move F-F♭-E♭ inside it at 87-88. This is the Bizet device (PDF 213, 215 m. 28: motion added "to enliven the sustained chord"). Both passages are the model's soft-tune texture. The plagal close is protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bars 37-60: the background changes at each entry of theme B (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** PDF 224 on "Petit Poucet": Ravel "changes both fore- and background color with each new entrance". The images (PDF 219-223) show the eighth-note background in the muted violins (m. 1), then violas and cellos (m. 12), back to the violins (m. 22), and violas under the bassoon solo (m. 50).

**Score.** Theme B's foreground goes Fl 1 (37), C.a. (41), Ob 1 with Bsn 1/Vc (45), the C.a. quotation (47), violins (53), then tutti (57). The background changes with it: arco viola triplets with held Vln II (37), pizzicato violas with the cellos' tenor line (41), held strings (45), off-beat violas (48), harp triplets (53), viola triplets again (57), sextuplets (59). No two entries share both colours. Viola triplets 37-60 and c124's rule are protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Bar 36-37: the C.a. pickup lands on Fl 1's first note (recorded, no change)

**Reading.** PDF 217: the melody passes "from the oboe by the darker-hued English horn without overlap and a fresh articulation". In the image (PDF 220, mm. 11-12) the oboe's line ends before the English horn starts.

**Score.** The C.a. plays C5 at 36 b3 and lands on F5 for an eighth at 37 b1, where Fl 1 enters on the same F5. That overlaps on one note. It is deliberate: c071 gave theme B's rising-fourth pickup to the C.a. at its first statement, as at 44 and 52, after c072 dropped the flute pickup for the 8-page layout. The shared note is an eighth long, at pitch, and covered by the flute's p entry. A clean hand-over would need the pickup in Fl 1, which c072 rejected for the layout. The other hand-overs follow Ravel: 15-22 (c407, c427), 44-45 (C.a. ends at 44 b3, Ob 1 starts at b4), and 52-53, where the overlap joins two different lines (the end of the quotation and the start of theme B), which PDF 224 allows. Layout protected.

**Fix.** None.

### 7. No bar-by-bar motive dialogue as in Schoenberg op. 43B (recorded, not a fault)

**Reading.** XVI-19b (image PDF 238) passes a short motive bar by bar among Ob, E.h., Cl, B.cl., Bsn and four solo violins, each marked as the principal voice, over muted strings. This belongs to a variation texture.

**Score.** This movement has no such dialogue. The nearest thing is 15-22, where Ob 1 and the solo violin alternate in two-bar phrases (row 25). The models in the brief do not use the device either: Dvořák 9/ii is built from long lines handed between solo winds, which is what this score does. Adding motive exchanges would cut up theme A and theme B, which PDF 228 warns against: Stravinsky makes his colour changes "never breaking up a phrase needlessly, and preserving the shape of the piece". I record it so that a later critic does not raise it as missing.

**Fix.** None.

### 8. Bars 15-20 and 7-14: bass line without double basses (recorded, no change)

**Score.** At 15-20 the cellos (partly divided) are the lowest voice and the basses rest until 21. At 7-14 the bass is the bass clarinet with the harp's left hand.

**Reading.** In "Petit Poucet" (image PDF 219-220) the muted violins carry the background with no bass for eleven bars. The basses come in only at m. 12, with one pizzicato note. PDF 213 calls the strings' absence "a great relief" and a bridge colour. The later entry of the basses (21 senza sord., then arco for 23-30) is the structural point, and it comes where theme A returns in the violins. The bowing at 23-30, the mute plan 15-37 and horn theme 7-14 are protected.

**Fix.** None.
