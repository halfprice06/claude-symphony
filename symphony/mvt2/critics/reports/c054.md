## Findings (ranked)

**F1. Bars 48–52: Vln II sits on the cor anglais quotation. Applied.**
The quotation of movement I's S head (C.a., p) runs A4–G♯4 | F♯4 D5 C♯5 | C♯5 B4 A4 | A♭4 G♭4 | F4 G♭4. Under it, Vln II holds F♯4 | F♯4 E♯4 | F♯4 D4 | E♭4 | E♭4.
- At 49 b1 the quotation lands on F♯4, which Vln II is already holding in unison.
- At 48 and 51–52 the pad is a second or third below the solo.

Adler PDF 241–243 says the sustained part should be far from the line it accompanies. The same reason led c027 to take Vln II out under the cor anglais at 41–44. This is the movement's one cyclic quotation, and its arrival note disappears into the string pad.

Fix: the same pitches one octave higher, pp, which puts a sixth or more between them and the solo.
```
eis'1\mp\> | fis''1\pp^\markup \italic "poco sostenuto" | fis''2. eis''4 |
fis''2 d''2 | es''1 | es''1\< |
```
- Vln II's E♭5 at 52 now steps to its F5 at 53 (before, it leapt E♭4–F5).
- My scan shows no new fifths or octaves.
- c027's "hold at 49" rhythm is unchanged.

**F2. Bars 61–62: the brass cover the theme's upper octave. Applied (dynamics only).**
Theme B's augmented head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ is scored as follows:
- Upper octave: two flutes (A♭5–F♭6) and the lower half of Vln I. That half only joins from D♭6.
- Lower octave: Tpt 1, Cl 1 and Vln II.
- Against them, Hn 2–4, Tpt 1–3, Tbn 1·2, bass trombone and tuba are all marked ff.

Adler PDF 268 (Dvořák, *Carnival*): "the trick is to keep the volume of the brass down enough to let the strings and winds through." Part 2 PDF 184: the principal voice is marked louder than the subsidiary ones. As written, the head is heard at the trumpet's octave, and the flutes and violins only colour it.

Fix: the brass that hold harmony are marked f, and the trumpet staff, which carries the theme, stays ff (Tpt 1·2 share a staff, so both keep ff):
- Hn 2: `f'1\f`
- Hn 3·4: `aes'1\f` and `aes1\f`
- Tpt 3: `des'1\f`
- Tbn 1·2: `aes1\f` and `f1\f`
- Bass trombone: `des1\f`
- Tuba: `des,1\f`

Tbn 1 keeps its own ff on the quotation at 63. This follows c044's grading at 79 (Hn 3·4 f). It changes the level that c024 and c039 set (ff), but it is not an mp/mf colourless re-entry, which was Rimsky's objection (PDF 136).

I also tried Cl 1 an octave up on the head (A♭5–F♭6, Adler PDF 250's 8′ + 4′ principle). That pushed bars 65–70 onto a ninth page, so I reverted it.

**F3. Bars 81–82: both oboes stay into the pp, in unison. Applied.**
- At 81, Ob 2's G4 doubles Cl 2 and the violas in unison.
- At 82, Ob 2 and Ob 1 both end on A♭4 at p>pp, together with Cl 2, Vln II and the violas.

Adler PDF 249–250 says two like winds in unison are never in tune and the timbre thickens. Adler PDF 270–271 notes that Copland makes a diminuendo partly by cutting an oboe.

Fix: `aTwoOboeTwo = { R2.*8 | f''2.~\ff | f''2 r4 | R2.*2 | }`
- Ob 1 finishes the line alone.
- The B♭ at 80 b3 is still in Vc, Hn 1·2, Bn, B.cl. and Cb.
- This gives up c024's "B♭4 leads to G4", whose G4 already sits in Cl 2 and the violas.
- The staged release 81–83 stays in its order (brass, then winds, then harp). One double reed now leaves earlier, as part of the diminuendo, which is how Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 127) treats removing a timbre.

**F4. Bars 41–44: the cor anglais plays inside the viola triplets. Not applied (protected).**
The cor anglais' lower octave of theme B (A♭3–B♭4) lies in the same register as the viola triplets (A♭3–G♭4), with the same pitch classes on the same beats: A♭3 at 41 b1, D♭4 at 41 b2, C4 at 44 b2. This is the problem c027 removed Vln II for (Adler PDF 241–243, 274).

Suggestion, if the composer reopens "viola triplets 37–60": at 41–44, violas play the figure's first triplet of each half bar and rest on the second, e.g. `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( des' f') } r4`. The cor anglais then gets every other beat to itself. The cello tenor line from c037 already supplies the sustained harmony.

**F5. Bars 3–6: Cl 1 and Hn 4 in unison on A♭3 at pp. Not applied (link 1–6 protected).**
This is overlapped voicing outside a tutti (Adler PDF 257d). Adler PDF 234 treats horns as the wind section's pedal holders, so the horn pair C4/A♭3 can stand alone over Cl 2 E♭3 (F♭3 at 5) and B.cl. A♭2. Option: `introClOne = { R2.*6 | }`, which leaves the A♭ chord and the iv6/4 at 5 complete.

**F6. Bars 7–14: the harp RH plucks the horn's own note.** It happens at 7 b2 (A♭3), 9 b2–3 (C4, A♭3), 10 b2–3 (G3, E♭3), 11 b2 (A♭3), 12 b1 (F4) and 14 b2 (A♭3). These are unisons of unlike timbres (Adler PDF 256–257), and they put a plucked attack on the solo horn's sustained line. c019's rule was "at or below the horn". Taking "below" strictly, drop the top note where it duplicates the horn: 7 `<c es>2`, 11 `<c es>2`, 14 `<c es>2`, and 9 b2 `<es aes>4`. The B.cl. A♭2 + Bn 2 C3 third at 7, 9, 11 and 14 is also close for the bottom of a wind chord (PDF 257, Ex. VIII-25). The harp LH octave keeps that acceptable, so I do not propose touching the protected horn–bassoon layout.

**F7. Bar 22 b3: the viola's B♭3 moves under the violins' upbeat E♭4–F4 into 23. Minor, not applied (viola answers protected).** Adler PDF 245 (*Fledermaus*): suspend the accompaniment so that the upbeat to the restatement is heard. Option: `g8( aes8 g4) r4`. The answer keeps the 8–8–4 head it imitates from 21.
