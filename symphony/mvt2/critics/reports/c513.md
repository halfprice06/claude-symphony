## What else I studied

- Corpus: Mozart, Symphony No. 41, ii (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/ly/Mozart_Sym41_mvt2.ly`, bass part from line 3338). This is the corpus slow movement that actually uses V as its key area, the case Unit 14 describes. The bass reaches G (V of C) at 27-28, cadences in C at 44 (end of the exposition, 44 of 101 bars, before repeats), and the development ends on C (V7 of F) at 59 before the recapitulation at 60. That is Ex. 14-13's model at full size: new-key cadence near the middle, then the new tonic turned back into V of I. This score cadences in its contrast key at 61 of 92 bars (66%), because the ABA form spends that cadence on the climax rather than on a midway point. Dvořák 9/ii (c373's comparison) works the same way as this score: a sectional change of key and tempo, and the climax late in the middle section.

## Findings

I found nothing in Unit 14 that the score breaks and that a change would improve. The findings below record what I checked, ranked by how much each matters to the form. No change is proposed, so nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-513/work. That copy is identical to v513 (diff -rq clean); its build-warnings.txt is empty, analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, the score is 8 pages and the parts book 30 pages. The parts book was not regenerated because nothing changed.

**1. The way into D♭ at 36-37 does all three things PDF 216 asks of a lasting modulation: no change.** PDF 216 says the applied dominant alone cannot make a key; a long modulation also has "a melodic line that gravitates to the new goal as if it were 1̂, a change in motivic design, and a punctuation". At 36 b3 the texture thins to the bare octave C3 (B.cl.) / C5 (C.a.), the third of the A♭ chord. At 37 the bass goes to D♭ (Cb, harp, B.cl.), so the lowest line moves 7̂-1̂ of the new key while the A♭ chord above it is reinterpreted as V (the "A♭ to A♭7 pivot", protected). The motive changes (theme B's rising fourth), and the punctuation is the double bar, metre change, poco rit. into Poco più mosso. Ex. 14-11 (PDF 223-225) is exactly this: the B section starts at once in the new key with new rhythm and texture. This detail (the C-D♭ bass step under the phrase modulation) was not listed by earlier passes; it supports the protected joint.

**2. The D♭ cadence comes at 61, not midway: no change.** Ex. 14-13 (PDF 226-227) asks for the new-key cadence "midway through the exercise", and Mozart 41/ii puts its V cadence at 44 of 101. Here the D♭ PAC, with the bass G♭-G♮-A♭ (4̂-♯4̂-5̂, PDF 215) and C6-D♭6 in Vln I, is held to 61. PDF 219 allows the cadence to come well after the key change, through a deceptive cadence first (Ex. 14-1); this score does that at 44-45. The late cadence is what gives 53-61 its goal. In seconds (c413), the climax falls at 65% of 345 s, close to Dvořák 9/ii. Protected since c93 ("D♭ cadence held back to 59-61").

**3. Leaving the foreign keys (62-71): follows, no change.** The reading's return rules (PDF 219-220) are met. 64 b4 is the double-meaning chord (E7♭9 in A minor, German sixth of A♭; c243). The dominant takes its seventh D♭, the one degree of A♭ that is foreign to E♭ (PDF 220): Vln II D♭4 at 65-66, Hn 1's passing D♭4 at 68, Bsn 2 D♭3 at 69-70. Bar 67 is bare E♭ octaves (Cb, timpani, Hn 1). Because 65-66 already sounded E♭7, one bare bar does not let E♭ settle as a tonic, and the empty bar sets off the horn's motto tail. Root-position A♭ with theme A comes back at 71, "in a place of some prominence" (PDF 219). I checked this again because c373 rested on v373; v513 has the same pitches.

**4. Leading-tone doubling at the applied chords: follows, with one recorded exception.** PDF 214 forbids doubling ♯4̂ because it acts as a leading tone. At every applied dominant of V (8 b2, 24 b2, 59 b3-4, 72 b2) the raised degree is in one line only, the melody or the bass line in orchestral octaves. The other applied chords also keep their leading tones single: E♮4 at 20 b3 (Vln II), A♮ at 40 b1 (bass line) and 56 b1 (Vc only), E♯ (F4) at 47 b1 (Vln II only). The one place where a leading tone is spread across many voices is 62 b3-4: the F♭ (= E) chord before A minor has its third A♭ (= G♯, the leading tone of A minor) in Tbn 1, Vla, Hn, Ob, Cl, Fl and Vln I, from A♭3 to A♭6. Only Fl 1 and Vln I take it up to A at 63. I recommend no change. The A♭ there is spelled and heard as the movement's own tonic held through the climax (the F♭-E-E♭ thread, protected), not as a part-writing leading tone, and the ff tutti doubles every chord tone, which the reading's four-voice rule does not address. The A minor at 63 is then entered by the thinned sf texture, so the unresolved A♭s are not heard as a fault. I record it so a later critic does not raise it as a Unit 14 violation.

**5. Graded half cadences at 10, 22, 26, 44: no change.** PDF 215 (Ex. 14-4): ♯4̂ in a rising top line makes a semicadence sound final; the diatonic version leaves V wanting to move on. The score uses the strong kind once in A, at 21-22 (French sixth, D♮5-E♭5 over E♭), where the b phrase ends and a′ needs a firm upbeat. At 10 and 26 (snapshots: E♭-B♭-G with G4 or E♭4 on top) and 44 (A♭ with E♭5 on top) the next phrase follows at once, so the open kind is right. Same verdict as c184 and c373.

**6. Two A sections closing on I (14 and 30), against the quatrain model: no change.** Ex. 14-12 (PDF 224-225) has its two A phrases cadence in different places (I and V). Here a (7-14) and a′ (23-30) both close with a PAC. This is a period structure, not a quatrain: b (15-22) ends on V with the strong semicadence, which provides the open cadence the quatrain puts at the end of its first A. The point was closed in cycle 12 and the cadence plan (HC 10/22/26, PAC 14/30/82) is protected. The reading gives no reason to reopen it.
