## Findings

### 1. IV is never tonicized outside the middle section (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 451 §11: too much IV "can upset our sense of tonality, making us hear IV as I and I as V"; Points 9 (PDF 457): "Beware of modulating to IV early in the piece, especially in major". The same section also says brief tonicizations of IV are common even at the start.

**Score.** I searched the source of intro, aOne, trans, aTwo and coda with comments stripped: none of them contains a single G♭. Bars 1–36 and 71–92 therefore never sound V7 of D♭. The only subdominant chords there are the iv neighbour at 4–5 (Cl 2 E♭–F♭–E♭ under horn 3's C–D♭–C) and IV6–iv at 87–88. Both are plagal neighbours over a tonic bass, not keys. As a result, the D♭ of 37 arrives as a new key and not as a dominant resolving, and after the middle section the A♭ of 71 is not weakened. This is stricter than §11 requires.

**Considered and rejected.** A Romantic coda often colours the plagal close with I7 (A♭7) going to IV, and the §16 footnote names I–IV–I as the most important plan. In this score bar 86 already holds the motto tail's D♮ (Cl 1, 85–88) over the A♭ pedal. A G♭ there would form a diminished third or augmented sixth against it, and the plagal close 87–92 is on the protected list. No change.

### 2. The link and Ex. 26-4 cover the same two keys by different routes (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 445–446 §4, Ex. 26-4: from C to A♭, C is reinterpreted as III of A♭ and moves through II6 or VI–II to a cadential V. "Establishing a temporary key area requires a strong dominant in the new key."

**Score.** Bars 1–6 do not use that diatonic route. They use a chromatic common-tone move from movement I's C major (C5 held, E♮ to E♭, C to A♭), and the reading's own Point 1 (PDF 456) puts that relationship outside this unit. What the unit does require is met: A♭ is the movement's tonic, not a temporary key, and it receives its strong dominant soon after: V at 8 b3, the half cadence at 10, and V7–I at 13–14 with the horn reaching A♭3 (1̂) on the downbeat of 14. The six-bar link with no dominant is proportionate. The A section (7–30) then has three dominants and two full closes before anything leaves A♭. Protected (link 1–6). No change.

### 3. The middle section follows the textbook IV plan; Dvořák's follows the mixture plan (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 451–452 §11 and Ex. 26-11: IV in the middle of ABA, entering by phrase modulation and moving to V of the home key. PDF 443 §2: degrees of remoteness, with mixture as the nearer kind.

**Corpus.** In Dvořák 9/ii (strings.notesL.ily) the key signature changes from D♭ major to C♯ minor at 46 and back to D♭ major later. His middle section is the tonic's parallel minor, a key related by mixture, not IV. This score uses the related key (IV) for its long middle section. It keeps the mixture keys, F♯ minor = G♭ minor (iv of D♭) and A minor, for 4 and 2 bars. Each departure is therefore graded by length as §2 and Points 1 describe: the nearest key lasts longest, the remoter ones are brief. c253 measured the proportions against Dvořák (36/40/24% against 38/39/23%). The reading suggests no change to either the keys or the lengths.

### 4. The way out of IV matches §15 and Ex. 26-17, scaled to a slow movement (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 456 §15: returning to V after a stay away, the music gravitates to VI as V's upper neighbour, and V "is often preceded by a II or IV and often supports a 7th in order to lead convincingly to I". Ex. 26-11 has IV for 16 bars (85–100) and a transition to V of 8 bars (101–108), about 2:1.

**Score.** IV runs 37–61 (25 bars). The exit then takes 9 bars: ♭VI (F♭) at 62 b3, A minor (♭ii, standing as the upper neighbour on E) at 63–64, the French sixth on F♭ at 64 b4, and V7 with D♭ in bassoon 2 held through 69–70 into C3 at 71. The ratio is about 2.8:1 against Mozart's 2:1. The difference suits an Adagio whose middle section carries the climax, and the dominant (6 bars at Tempo I, about 21 s) is long enough to cancel D♭: the bass sits on E♭ throughout, and G♮ removes G♭ (violas 65–66, bassoon 1 69–70). The A♭ of 71 arrives on 1̂ (Vln I A♭5) with the bassoons' contrary semitones G–A♭ and D♭–C (c377). Protected (61–65, 65–70). No change.

### 5. The two transient keys inside IV have the foreshadowing that Ex. 26-7 describes (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 447–448 §6, Ex. 26-7: in a passing modulation the apparent key becomes a springboard. Mozart foreshadows it twice with V6/5–VI, and the B♮–C of the previous phrase foreshadows its applied dominant.

**Score.** B♭ minor (45–46) is the springboard to F♯ minor (47–50). It is foreshadowed by the same applied chord: the A°7 at 40 (bass A♮, after the half-diminished chord on C at 39), heard again at 56. The F♯-minor goal is foreshadowed by the D♭7 that ends the B♭-minor phrase (47), the movement's only dominant seventh on the local tonic. A&S's Exercise 1f (PDF 457), D♭ to B♭ minor with V of D♭ as the pivot, is the 44–45 move exactly. These passing keys never get a cadence of their own except F♯ minor's iv–N6–V–i, which is right for a quotation that must sound settled. No change.

## What must not be touched

The key plan I–IV–(♭VI–♭ii)–V7–I, with the phrase modulation at 37 out of an A♭ that never takes a seventh. The exit 61–70, the plagal close 87–92 with no G♭, and the proportions are at a master's level and should stay as they are, with the standing list and the 8-page layout.
