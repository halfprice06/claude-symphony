## Findings

I found nothing in v587 that breaks a principle of this reading and has not already been settled with a stated reason. I recommend no change to the score. The findings below record what I checked; the first three are new checks on this range, the rest confirm earlier records. Nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-587/work. It is an unchanged copy of v587. I rebuilt it to confirm the baseline (result at the end). The parts book was not regenerated because no file changed.

**1. Bars 15-22: Sebesky's quartet conversation (Ex. 188) is kept to soloists. Follows; no change.**
PDF 134-136: in a quartet "the center of attention flows naturally from one player to another", but the same constant motion given to a full section "would sound labored and overbearing". In Ex. 188 every part moves in turn and the lead changes each bar. At 15-22 the score passes the lead between two soloists only: Ob 1 at 15-16 and 19-20, solo Vln I (marked "solo, senza sord.") at 17-18 and 21-22. The muted sections underneath do not join the conversation. Vln II holds louré half notes (A♭4, then G4-E4-F4-C4-B♭3), the violas move once or twice per bar, and the cellos divide only at 18 and 22 for one bar each. That is Sebesky's distinction made correctly: chamber motion for single players, sustained lines for sections. Ex. 212 (PDF 162-163) uses the same contrast, solo players against the section, which the score repeats with the 4 solo cellos at 65-66 and 83-86.

**2. Rule 7 and footnote 2 (PDF 139-140): cellos doubling the basses at 49-50 and 83-92. Recorded; no change.**
Sebesky says the lowest cello need not double the bass, because "the resonance of a recorded string bass" makes it "usually unnecessary". His own footnote 2 (PDF 139) limits this: the bass can sustain a line alone "only in a recording studio, never in a live performance". So rule 7 applies with less force to a concert score. I checked every bar. The cellos have their own line over the basses at 23-30, 41-44 and 71-82, and the basses rest at 53-56 (theme with two string lines, c447 #1). The octave doublings are short or deliberate. At 49 and 50 b3-4 the cellos join the basses at the octave (F♯3/F♯2, B2/B1) under the cor anglais quotation, between independent cello notes at 48 (C♯3 over F♯2) and 50 b1-2 (A2 over F♯2). At pp sotto voce in F♯ minor this gives the basses pitch definition, which is footnote 2's point. At 83-92 the cellos and basses hold the tonic in octaves: Sebesky's "organ" bass (Ex. 204, PDF 157) and his "temporary richness and depth" (PDF 146). 47-53 and 87-92 are protected. No change.

**3. Rule 3 (PDF 139): every thin string line is a marked solo or is doubled. Follows.**
Sebesky's minimums (two violas, three violins per sustained line; only the cello may carry a line alone) apply to ensemble lines. In this score the only lines with fewer than a full section are: solo Vln I at 17-22 (a soloist, so the minimum does not apply); 4 solo cellos at 65-66 (one line, sul A, pp, with the full basses below) and 83-86 (A♭2 ppp with the basses); and 4 solo basses at 87-92, which sit under tutti cellos an octave above, so the bass line is never carried by a small group alone. The viola divisi at 23-30, 80 and 85-92 put half the section on each line, well over two players. Vln II's divisi at 37-40 has six or seven on each line. No change.

**4. Rules 2, 4 and 6 at the peaks 61-63 and 79-81. Follows (confirms c447 #4, c507 #4).**
The theme is in both violin sections at every string statement: unison at 23-30, 53-56 and 71-76, octaves at 57-62 and 77-81, three string octaves at 79-81 (B♭6, B♭5, B♭4). The only place the lead sits on half a section is Vln I's divisi at 62 b3 (F♭6/A♭6) and 63 (E6/A6 trem.). Rule 4 allows thirds and fourths in the high register, and Fl 1 doubles the top note. At 80-81 the violas divide, but the upper half keeps the theme's lower octave (A♭4 under Vln II's A♭5, G4 under G5) through 81 b1, so the octave doubling stays in one instrument as Ex. 203 recommends (PDF 153).

**5. Rule 1 and Ex. 200 (PDF 138, 148-149): economy and internal resonance at 23-30 and 71-74. Follows.**
In Ex. 200 Sebesky voices the violins as one unit and the violas and cellos as another, each complete. At 23-30 the violins are one unison line and the lower group is complete on its own: violas divided in two (C4/E♭4, C4/A♭4 and so on), the cello counter-melody, and the basses on the bass from 21. At 71-74 the violas' eighth-note figure, the cellos and the basses again make a complete lower group under the unison violins, with Cl 2 and the bassoons doubling inside it. No section divides into more than two lines anywhere in the movement, which is rule 1.

**6. Combining strings with winds (PDF 164-173). Confirms c507; no change.**
I rechecked c507's table against v587: the score has had no pitch change in these bars since then. The flute is one level under the violins below f (57) and equal at f and ff (59, 78, 79-81), settled at c167, c217, c307 and c357. The horn joins the cello line only at the builds (57-60, 75-82; PDF 166 "sparingly"). Horns and strings are each complete at 61 and 79, with the bass clarinet on the root (PDF 168). The strings are high and simple at the tutti peaks (Ex. 215), and drop out of the trombone's octave at 63 (Ex. 216). The doubling of horns and violas at 87-90 remains as c507 recorded it (protected since c150).
