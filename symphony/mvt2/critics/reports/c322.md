## Findings

### 1. Bars 51-53: bar 52 holds one chord with no chromatic motion before the A♭7 (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 51 is the protected A♭7 pivot in third inversion, and the A♭ pedal continues into 52. For three beats of bar 52 the strings hold A♭-C-E♭-F, and only the cor anglais moves. The cello sits on E♭3 for the whole bar, and Vln II holds E♭5. This is the approach to the return of A♭ in the movement's main structural dominant area. It is the one place in the movement where the reading's main technique would apply directly, and no chromatic line is used.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 544 (Ex. 30-20d) shows ♯4 passing between 3 and 5 over a held tonic, marked "P". PDF 546 (Ex. 30-23, and the Tchaikovsky in Ex. 30-24) shows a common-tone chord embellishing the dominant seventh, with its upper tones moving by half step into the V7. PDF 536-537 says a chromatic chord can be prepared by association with an earlier normal use. The sonority A♭-C-D-F already occurs at bar 86, where D falls within the motto. At 52, D rises, so the same chord returns with its line going the other way. A&S PDF 553 (Ex. 30-19) describes 6 as a diatonic neighbour of 5, which covers F5 over E♭5 in Vln II.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-322/work/sections/bSec.ily):**

```
Vc   (line 255): | c1 | es1\< |            ->  | c1 | d2\< es2 |
Vln II (line 194): | es''1 | es''2.\< c''4(\p  ->  | es''1 | f''2\< es''4 c''4(\p
```

Bar 52 now reads: beats 1-2 A♭1, D3, C4, F4 (cor anglais), F5, that is A♭-C-D-F; beat 3 V; beat 4 A♭7 (cor anglais G♭4, violin pickup C5). The cello line C3-D3-E♭3 moves against Vln II E♭5-F5-E♭5 in contrary motion. There are no parallels and no B/C♭, E♮ or A♮. The A♭ pedal and the inversion at 51 are kept.

**Protected material and cyclic rules.**
- The new cello line D-E♭ is not the motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C: the notes come in a different order and the bass does not rise in it.
- The stop chord and the rise B→C are untouched.
- c124's rule ("no further accompaniment changes in 37-60") has already been set aside for pitch changes by c262, c274 and c312, which were accepted. The c124 dynamic plan (pp < mp under oboe 1) is kept, because the hairpin only moves to beat 1.

**Build.** The build is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty, analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, and score.pdf is 8 pages. I checked the engraving at bar 52 on page 3. The parts book was regenerated: 30 pages, 0 warnings, the harp on 2 pages and every other part on 1.

### 2. Bars 62-65: the ♭II-minor climax follows Schubert's model (no change)

62 is a mixture chord with no 7th. F♭ (= E) acts as V of A minor, that is ♭II made minor. At 64 beat 3 the chord is both vii°7 of A and a German sixth of A♭, then E♭7 follows. This is the procedure of Ex. 30-9 (A&S PDF 535-536): the climax, its own V, and a chord with a double meaning returning to V. Nothing here should change.

### 3. Bar 21: the French sixth is reached by a correct voice exchange (no change)

The outer voices go M10 → P8 (passing 6/4) → A6, and the French sixth at 21 is V7♭5 in 4/3 position (A&S PDF 543). This matches Ex. 31-20b and the note on PDF 566, where the passing 6/4 at the octave is the good form and the note-against-note M6-m10 version is called poor (PDF 565). No change.

### 4. Bar 33: the common-tone °7 is spelled and led correctly (no change)

The common-tone °7 on I uses C♭ in place of B♮, as in the Brahms example (Ex. 30-21, A&S PDF 544-545). The upper voices resolve by step to the tonic triad. The spelling also keeps B♮ out of the movement, as the cyclic reservation requires. No change.

### 5. Augmented triads and V7♯5 are not used, and should not be added (no change)

The reading's augmented-triad techniques (I+ → IV, 5-♯5-6; PDF 537-541) would be possible at 75. They are blocked there:
- The protected counter-melody (c104: cello, horn 2 and bassoon 1) holds E♭3 through beat 2, and the harp and clarinet 2 double E♭4. A ♯5 (E♮) would clash with all of these.
- An added E♮ would also reopen the viola's E♭/E♮ thread, which closes in the finale.

V7♯5 at the cadences (PDF 542) needs ♯2 = B♮ rising to C, which is the finale's reserved rise B→C. Leaving both techniques out is correct.

### 6. Correction to c220: E♮ also occurs at bar 20

c220 states that E♮ occurs only at bar 1 and bars 63-64. Vln II also has E4 at 20 beat 3, as the third of V7/vi. This is a correct applied dominant and needs no change, but HISTORY.md should not repeat c220's claim when later critics check the E♭/E♮ thread.

### 7. Bars 39-40, 47-50, 55-56, 59, 80, 88-89: other chromatic chords checked (no change)

- 39-40 and 55-56: C∅7 → A°7 over the bass E♭-C-A is part of a minor-3rd cycle (PDF 575-577), stopped before it can weaken the tonic (PDF 572).
- 47: C♯7 is respelled at the key change (PDF 559-560), with E♯→F♯ and B→A resolved.
- 49: the Neapolitan sixth leads G→E♯ correctly.
