## Findings

### 1. Bar 49: the quotation's syncopated D5 has lost movement I's tenuto and swell (applied)
- **Problem.** The S head in movement I is `x2.~ x8 y8 | z4 w2-- v4`. In every statement there, the syncopated half note on beat 2 carries a tenuto, and in most of them also a crescendo. The statements are: tTwo 32 `aes4\! f'2--\< es'4\!`, dev 18/39/61 (`ges'2--`), recTwo 79 `a'2--\<`, and coda 69 `aes'2--\<`. In movement II, the cor anglais at 49 plays `fis'4 d''2 cis''4` with no mark.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 51 says a note that begins on a weak beat and is held through a strong beat "conflicts, sometimes very strongly, with the meter". Ex. 3-6 marks *sf* on exactly such notes. The syncopation is the gesture that makes this a quotation, and movement I always marks it. Without the mark, the cor anglais gives the D5 the same weight as its neighbours. The one-statement rule is unaffected.
- **Fix** (bSec.ily, `bSecCorAnglais`):
  `| a'2.~ a'8 gis'8 | fis'4 d''2--\< cis''4\! ) | cis''2(\mp b'4 a'4) |`
  The swell leads into the mp that already stands at 50.

### 2. Bars 47–53: the quotation enters with no tempo give (applied)
- **Problem.** The middle section has "Poco più mosso" at 37 and "poco a poco animando" at 53. Between them, the 3-bar group 45–47 reaches its goal, the C♯7 at 47, and movement I's second theme enters in a foreign key. The tempo does not move at all.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 53–54: a phrase is a group of measures that ends on a goal of tonal motion. The 3-bar hypermeasure 45–47 is the irregular unit of Ex. 3-12.
  - A&S PDF 47 (footnote): deviation from strict time is normal in performance. A master marks it at structural seams, not inside phrases.
  - Dvořák 9/ii does this at the same point of its middle section: "poco ritard." at 53, "Poco meno mosso" at 54.
  - Movement I treats this theme the same way: "poco rit." into "Poco meno mosso" (♩=112 against 126).
  - In v48 the cor anglais' pickup at 47 b4 lands in strict time right after the oboe's mf→pp diminuendo. Nothing tells the conductor that a different movement is being remembered.
- **Fix.**
  - global.ily bar 47:
    `s2 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco rit." \mt 57 s4 \mt 55 s4`
  - global.ily bar 48:
    `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco sostenuto" \mt 54 s1`
  - global.ily bar 53, replacing the current text:
    `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "a tempo, poco a poco animando" \mt 60 s1*2`
  - Violins I rest here, so I put the words above Vln II (bSec.ily) as well, following c045's practice:
    `\after 2 ^\markup \italic "poco rit." eis'1\mp\> | fis'1\pp^\markup \italic "poco sostenuto" |`
  - The tempo steps are 60 → 57/55 → 54 (close to Tempo I, 52) → 60 at 53.

### 3. Bars 80–81: no hemiola at any 3/4 cadence; add one under the A′ peak (applied)
- **Problem.** The movement is in 3/4 for 65 of its 92 bars, but it never regroups the metre. The A′ cadence 79–82 is broadened only by the "poco allargando" at 78. The counter-melody (Vc, Hn 1·2, Bsn 1) moves in plain quarters, `aes4 bes4 des'4 | bes2 des'4`, which is the same beat pattern as every other bar.
- **Evidence.**
  - A&S PDF 51, Ex. 3-8: two bars of 3/4 regrouped as 2+2+2 sound "like a larger bar of 3/2" just before the cadence. This is Brahms's standard way to write the broadening into the notes instead of leaving it all to the conductor.
  - Here the counter-melody becomes `aes2 bes4~ | bes4 des'2 | c'2.`. The pitches are unchanged and each note fits its chord: A♭ over IV, B♭ as the root of iiø7 at 80 b3 and the fifth of V7 at 81 b1, D♭ as the seventh of V7 for two beats, resolving to C at 82.
  - The B♭ tied across the bar is a syncopation at the V7 arrival (PDF 51).
  - The melody keeps its own 1+2 / 1+½+… grouping. The hemiola is therefore a second layer against the held B♭6–A♭6, and c037's held inner parts are untouched.
  - The only new interval is a cello B♭3 an octave above the bass B♭1 at 80 b3. It is approached by contrary motion and is not parallel.
  - allpairs output is unchanged. My hand scan of the shared staves (Bsn 1·2: Bsn 1 A♭3→B♭3 against Bsn 2 D♭3→B♭2, contrary) and of the inner voices against Vla, Hn 3·4, Ob 2 and Cl 2 found no parallels.
- **Fix** (aTwo.ily):
  - `aTwoVc`: `des'4\ff c'4 bes4 | aes2( bes4~ | bes4)\> des'2 | \after 2 \pp c'2.\p\> |`
  - `aTwoHnOne`: `des'4\f c'4 bes4) \breathe | aes2( bes4~ | bes4\> des'2 | c'2)\p\> r4\! \partCombineAutomatic |`
  - `aTwoHnTwo`: same as Hn 1, without the combiner commands.
  - `aTwoBsnOne`: `r2 c'4( | des'4\ff c'4 bes4) \breathe | aes2( bes4~ | bes4\> des'2 | \after 2 \pp c'2.)\p\> |`
  - The horns' and Bsn 1's breath moves from the end of 80 to the end of 79, so the hemiola is one breath (about 9 s at ♩=48). Pitches of the four-horn chord 79–81 (c047) are unchanged. Dynamics on the shared staves still match: Bsn 1 and Bsn 2 both start the diminuendo at 81 b1.

### 4. Bars 67–68: the horn's accented passing D has no shaping (not applied; protected passage)
- **Problem.** In the retransition the horn plays the tail E♭–D–D♭–C–B♭ over the E♭ pedal. The D on 68 b1 is a major seventh above the bass. It is an accented passing tone on the strong beat, and c043 describes it as the "7" of the 8-7-6-5. The part reads `es'2.(\p | d'4 des'4 c'4 | bes2.~\>`, so it stays flat p until 69.
- **Evidence.** A&S PDF 54–55 (Ex. 3-15) places accented passing tones and appoggiaturas on strong beats, and PDF 50 says dissonant and chromatic notes are heard as accented. The swells elsewhere in this score already follow that logic: theme A swells to its chromatic D at 8, 24 and 72. The retransition statement of the same tail is the only one without a swell.
- **Fix** (retr.ily `retrHnOne`, if the composer accepts touching the "Hn 1 solo 67–70" entry; pitches, rhythm and the solo label unchanged):
  `es'2.(\p\< | d'4\> des'4 c'4\! | bes2.~\> | bes2)\pp r4 |`
