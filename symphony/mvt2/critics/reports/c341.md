## Findings

### 1. Bars 76 b3 → 77 b1: Vln I leaves the dissonant 7th A♭5 by a rising major 7th (applied)

Problem. At 76 b3 the bass is B♭1 (ii7). The theme's A♭5, in Vln I and Vln II in unison, is the chord's 7th: the resolution of the B♭ appoggiatura, which must itself fall to G. Since c309, Vln I jumps from that A♭5 up to G6 at 77 b1, a major 7th, while Vln II steps down to G5. The first violins lead the theme here (75-76 at pitch, c299), and in their own line the 7th now leaves by a dissonant leap. This is the only top-voice dissonance in the movement that is left by a leap larger than a 3rd (dissleap.py; leaps.py shows it is new since v202). At 12→13 and 28→29 the same A♭ falls by step to G.

Evidence. A&S PDF 39 §9, Ex. 2-8: dissonance is "approach[ed] and le[ft] ... by step", which "channels the tension"; isolated dissonances "lead to no goals". PDF 38 §6: all 7ths are dissonant. PDF 35-36 §3, Ex. 2-5: the rising M7 is the inversion of the falling m2, so the resolution exists only by octave displacement. c309 recorded the trade itself: its fix turned a m7 leap at 77→78 into "a major-seventh leap A♭5→G6 at the slur break 76→77". That trade moved the leap onto a dissonance.

Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoVlnOne, bar 77): Vln I takes G5 on b1 with Vln II, so its own line resolves the 7th by step. It rises a minor 6th into the upper octave on b2, from G to E♭, two tones of V7 over the E♭ bass. That is a consonant leap inside an arpeggiated chord (Ex. 2-7, 2-12), and it is the one expressive m6 of Ex. 2-1.

```
        | g''4(\<  \once \override Beam.positions = #'(0.18 . -0.18) es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8) |
```
Same slur, bow, crescendo and beaming. The top line is unchanged, because Fl 1 still plays G6-E♭6-D♭6-C6-B♭5. B♭6 is still first heard at 79 (c110, c299).

Relation to c309. This partly undoes c309, at 77 b1 only. c309's reason (Adler part 2 PDF 151: the flute should not be left alone in the upper octave) now applies for one beat instead of a whole bar. Fl 1 + Vln I pair in the upper octave from b2, and the octave grows in stages as c309's own Adler PDF 171-172 point asks. Theme record: themecheck.py reports 17/17 items unchanged (the themeA VlnOne check at 70 compares 17 notes, which end before 77 b1).

### 2. Theme A and theme B intervals against Ex. 2-1 and the Largo (no change)

Theme A's melodic intervals are m2, M2, m3, M3 and P4 only (7-14, 23-30, 71-78). The run at 78 and the M3 drops G-E♭ at 10, 13, 29 and 81 are consonant leaps between chord tones. Theme B is mostly steps (c251 recorded 16 of 17), plus the rising-4th pickup and the 5̂-1̂-3̂ arpeggio of its second phrase. Both match the interval vocabulary of the Largo tune cited above, and both keep their leaps consonant (PDF 38). The memorable point of each theme is a stabilised active tone: B♭ (2̂) over IV at 12, 28, 76 and 79, and B♭4 (6̂) closing theme B's antecedent at 40 as the root of vi (PDF 40, Ex. 2-10). A leap variant for theme B was recorded and rejected in c251. I do not reopen it.

### 3. Short échappées left by leap at 43 b2.5 and 59 b2.5 (no change)

dissleap.py also flags two eighth-note dissonances in theme B's consequent figure `es4. f8 des4`:
- 43 b2.5: Fl 1 A♭5 is a m7 over B♭1 and falls a m3 to F5.
- 59 b2.5: Vln I and Fl 1 F6 is a M7 over G♭1 and falls a M3 to D♭6.

Both are approached by step, sit on a weak half-beat and last an eighth. Both are escape tones of the kind already protected at 17 and 21. The strong beats on either side are consonant: E♭/G♭ M6 and D♭/G♮, a d5 that resolves at 60. PDF 39 states the stepwise norm, but these notes do not form the "isolated" dissonance it warns against. The bar 43 flute figure is protected (Fl 1 sixths 41-44, c090; c311 left a C.a. option unrecommended).

### 4. Outer voices and tritones (no change)

The outer voices are perfect at the articulations: P8 at 7, 14, 22, 30, 44, 56 and 82; P5 at 61 and 63; P8 at 51. Between articulations they are mostly 3rds, 6ths and passing dissonances (outer.py), which is §8 (PDF 39). At 53-54 the theme and the cellos move in 10ths (F/D♭, E♭/C, D♭/B♭). The neighbour G♭5 over the passing C3 at 53 b3 makes a tritone that does not resolve inward. It is a by-product of the neighbour figure (Ex. 2-21), and the structural 10ths carry the line. The key-defining d5s resolve as Ex. 2-15 requires: the bass G1 with D♭6 at 59 b3 goes to A♭1/C6 at 60, and D♭ falls to C over A♭ at 8→9, 13→14 and 81→82.

### 5. Fourths over the bass (no change)

There are four places where the 4th sits over the bass:
- 5: Hn 3's D♭4 over A♭2 is the neighbour 4th of Ex. 2-11 and resolves to C4.
- 37 b3 and 53 b3.5: theme B's G♭5 (neighbour) and F5 (passing) are the "P N" 4ths of Ex. 2-11.
- 74 b3: the pickup E♭4 over B♭1 is the root of V4/3, an inverted 5th, and is consonant by Ex. 2-12.

All follow PDF 40-41.

### 6. Minor-mode passages (no change)

Each minor episode has its raised 7̂ in the tune:
- 45-47: oboe A♮4, lower neighbour, in B♭ minor.
- 47-50: C.a. E♯, the V at 49, in F♯ minor.
- 63-64: Tbn 1 G♯, in A minor.

So each key is defined by the 4̂/♯7̂ tritone (Ex. 2-17), not left to drift to its relative major (Ex. 2-16). The B♭-minor head is the one place where that drift is used on purpose, back to D♭. The quotations are protected.

### 7. Pickups F→A♭ at 10, 22, 26 and 70 b3.5 (no change)

The pickup F over the dominant's E♭ bass is a M2 (9th). It leaps a m3 to A♭ over A♭ at the next downbeat. The bass changes with it, so the dissonance belongs to V's last half-beat and moves straight to a perfect consonance at the articulation (PDF 39 §8). It is theme A's own upbeat (c331: theme A always enters from an upbeat). Keep.

### 8. c201's changes intact (no change)

Bar 55: Hn 1/Cl 1 B♭4 tied, G♭4 on b3 (6ths with the theme). Cellos E♭2 under bassoon 1's D♭3. Both are as c201 left them, and the theme and counter-line at 53-56 are still mostly imperfect consonances.
