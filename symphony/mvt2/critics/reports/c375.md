## Findings

### 1. Flute 1, bar 78 beat 3: the run C6–F6–G6–A♭6 is stemmed up (applied)
**Problem.** Every note in this group lies above the stave, between one and five ledger lines. The stems point up and the beam floats above the fifth ledger line. On the same page, Violins I play the same pitches in the same rhythm with down-stems, as do Violins II an octave lower. Flute 1's own figures at 77 and 81 (G6–E♭6–D♭6–C6–B♭5) are also down-stemmed. In the score and in the Flute 1 part this is the only single-voice beamed group in the movement stemmed towards the centre line while lying entirely on one side of it.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: "Notes above the centre stave-line take down-stems." PDF 28: "Stems for notes on more than one ledger line extend to the middle stave-line." With down-stems the beam therefore lies inside the stave, so PDF 33 ("one clear stave-line between the innermost beam and the first ledger line") is met. That answers c105's reason for up-stems ("down-stems put the beam among the ledger lines; as in the violins"). The violins no longer have up-stems here (c246), and with stems drawn to the middle line the beam does not reach the ledger lines. c275's note shows the up-stem now comes from the part-combiner: flute 2 enters at 79, so 78 b3 is set in voice "one".

**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoFluteOne, bar 78):**
```lilypond
aes''2)\f \breathe \temporary \override Stem.direction = #DOWN c'''8(\< \tuplet 3/2 { f'''16 g''' aes''' } \revert Stem.direction |
```
`\temporary` makes the revert restore the combiner's direction, so the shared Fl 1·2 chord at 79 is unchanged. The Flute 1 part gets the same down-stems. No pitch, rhythm or MIDI change.

### 2. Double basses, bar 21 beat 1: centre-line D♭3 stemmed up between down-stems (applied)
**Problem.** The basses rest from 15 to 20 and enter on D♭3 (displayed pitch), which sits on the centre line, with `\once \stemUp`. Every stem after it points down: E♭3 and F♭3 at 21 and E♭3 at 22. So the bar reads up–down–down with nothing before it to justify the up-stem.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: "Continue the stem direction of surrounding stems that are in one direction only." PDF 28: "When there is no clear-cut case for either direction, the convention is to use a down-stem." Both rules give a down-stem here. c245 kept 21 b1 as "mixed context". That is true for the cellos, whose C3 at 20 b3 is up-stemmed, but not for the basses, which have no preceding stem. The cellos' up-stem stays.

**Fix (aOne.ily, aOneCb, bar 21):** drop the override:
```lilypond
des,4\pp^\markup \italic "senza sord." es,4 fes,4 | es,2. |
```
This changes notation only.

### 3. Cor anglais staff name without "in F" (judged; no change recommended for movement II alone)
The coordinator raised this point after I had submitted. p375.txt does not contain it. **Facts.** In score.ly (line 124) the cor anglais staff is named "Cor anglais" / "C.a.", while clarinets, bass clarinet and horns carry "in B♭" / "in F". The premise that movement I added "Cor anglais in F" is not borne out. mvt1/v221/score.ly (line 111), scherzo/v201 (line 140) and mvt3/v201 (line 121) all name it "Cor anglais" with no key. The movement II parts book already heads the part "Cor anglais in F" (page 5).
**Against the reading.** Gould ch. 1 (PDF 17–30) does not cover instrument names. MOLA PDF 6 asks for "keys of all transposing instruments" in the instrumentation list at the front of the work. It asks for "instrument name (and instrument key where applicable)" on the front of each part. The part already does this. The front-matter list belongs to the symphony as a whole, not to this movement's staff labels. The keys on the clarinet and horn staves (c055, c135, c260) carry information that can change: the clarinets change to A for the scherzo, and horn keys are traditionally named. The cor anglais is only ever in F.
**Judgement.** Adding "in F" would make the clarinet, horn and cor anglais labels match within this score. It is not required by MOLA, and done in movement II alone it would make the four movements inconsistent. If the composer wants it, it should be done in all four movements at once, as c345 proposed for "4 Timpani". The change for each movement would be `instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Cor anglais" \small "in F" } shortInstrumentName = \markup \center-column { "C.a." \small "in F" }`. This touches the shared cross-movement convention, so the other threads must be told. Not applied in /tmp/critic-375/work.
