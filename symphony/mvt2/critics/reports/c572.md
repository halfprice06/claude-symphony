## Findings (ranked)

None of the findings calls for a change. The score's leading-tone sevenths and its mixture have been checked against this unit four times before, and the few pitch changes since v432 follow it. I list what I checked so the composer does not reopen settled points.

### 1. Hidden fifths from tritones (Ex. 22-3c): clean in every voice pair (no change)

A&S say a d5 between upper voices that goes in similar motion to a P5 makes "an obtrusive set of hidden 5ths; avoid this voice leading" (PDF 375). The all-pairs scan finds two hits. Both are pairing errors in the scan, not real voice leading. At 40 b3 the Vln II divisi `<es' ges'>2 <des' f'>2` moves E♭→D♭ and G♭→F, so the d5 between the bass A1 and the E♭4 closes to a 3rd as the rule for tritones with the bass requires. At 61 b1 the viola's `<des' f'>1` takes the G♭4 of its 60 figure to F4, the regular resolution. The only real case left is the viola's arpeggio at 40, recorded by c432 and protected with the viola triplets (c153). The parallel-fifth scan (Ex. 22-15) finds no parallel fifths between any two voices in the movement.

### 2. Bars 39–40: the viiø7's 7th resolves by transfer (no change; new label)

At 39 b3 the chord is viiø7 of D♭ (bass C2, Vla B♭3, Vln II C4/G♭4, Fl E♭5). Its 7th, B♭3, should fall to A. In the viola it leaps to C4 instead, and the A arrives two octaves lower in the basses at 40. Because the pizzicato bass rests on 39 b4 for the harp's A♮ pedal, the viola's B♭3 is briefly the lowest note, so the B♭→A succession is split between two voices, and A&S warn against that (Ex. 23-12a, PDF 394). The passage is a transferred resolution of the Ex. 22-5 kind (PDF 376), in which "the downward resolution of the first violin's 6̂ is taken over by" another voice. Here the bass takes it, and the bass's own line, C2–A1–B♭1, keeps the outer-voice motion intact. At pp, inside a pizzicato texture, this is a resolution, not a cross relation. At 56 the cellos hold C through b4, so the same progression there has the B♭ inside. Leave both. (Earlier passes listed 40 only under the °7's resolution; this records its approach.)

### 3. Bar 64: °4/3 to V, then the semitone slide to E♭7 (no change)

The vii°7 of A minor stands over D (D2, Tbn 1 F3, Vla G♯3, Vln II B3) and goes to E with B, F, G♯, D above it. By §6 (PDF 377) a °4/3 goes to I6. Here it goes to V, which is the case of §9 (PDF 378–379): the bass D is a neighbour to E, and the functional chord is E7♭9. A&S's enharmonic point (Ex. 22-13, PDF 381) explains what follows. The same four pitches spelled D–F–A♭–C♭ are vii°7/V of A♭, so the E→E♭ bass at 64–65 completes in sound what the °7 implied in the home key. This is protected (F♭→E♭ thread, c046 and later), and it matches the reading.

### 4. Bars 59–61: tendency tones in the build and tutti (no change)

At 59 b4 the vii°7/V of D♭ (G, B♭, D♭, F♭) resolves in the voices that carry it. Bsn 1 and the cellos take F♭3→E♭3 (d7 G–F♭ to P5 A♭–E♭, Ex. 22-2), Hn 1 takes F♭4→E♭4, and the A4 F♭4–B♭4 opens to the M6 E♭4–C5. At 60→61 Vln II drops from the leading tone C5 to A♭4. That is theme B's last note going to the next phrase, and Vln I resolves the same C6→D♭6 on top. A doubled leading tone in an inner part may move freely, so this follows. The Cl 1/Cl 2 C–C♭ at 58 b3–4 is covered by the viola's own C4–C♭4 (Ex. 23-12b, "unobjectionable", PDF 394). Bar 58 has been protected since c151.

### 5. Mixture plan: every kind listed on PDF 395, each once in its place (no change)

The movement uses the four frequent kinds in Points 2a–d. ♭6̂ colours IV and II at 5, 80 and 88. ♭6̂ makes VII°7 at 59 and 81. ♭3̂ and ♭6̂ together make ♭VI at 78 (deceptive, Ex. 23-6). The Brahms pairing of ♭VI with the plagal IV♭–I (Ex. 23-5) spans 78 to 87–89. The F♭ follow-up scan confirms Points 3 at every F♭: none is followed by F before E♭. Theme A's statements at 7–14, 23–30 and 71–77 stay diatonic, which keeps ♭6̂ for the second climax and the coda. That is Unit 23 §5's point that the change of mode is most vivid when it varies a repetition (Ex. 23-8, PDF 392–393): 79–81 repeats the cadence of 75–77 with iiø7 and V♭9.

### 6. The link from movement I read through Unit 23 (no change)

Bar 1 is movement I's last chord, a C-major close after C minor, the Picardy third of §4 (Ex. 23-7, PDF 391–392). Bar 2 reinterprets it. The violas' E♮ falls to E♭, the bass falls C→A♭, and C5 holds, so the new key is ♭VI of C, the mixture chord of Ex. 23-5 and 23-6 (PDF 390). Unit 23 §1 (PDF 387) says using "two different tones to represent the same scale degree" gives "the potential for dramatic juxtaposition", and the movement carries that duality on: E♮/E♭ at 1–2, F♭/E against E♭ at 62–64, 78, 80–81 and 88, then into the scherzo's E. The link is protected and needs nothing.

### 8. Density of °7s compared with Mozart 41/ii (no change)

Mozart 41/ii has 6 °7s in about 180 annotated chords, almost all applied. This score has 6 full °7s, all applied or embellishing V, and no °7 is used for colour alone at a theme's opening. Adding the tonic vii°7 of A♭ under theme A's 5̂–6̂–5̂ (Ex. 22-6a, PDF 377) would be the textbook use. Theme A has no 5̂–6̂–5̂ on a beat, though, and its harmonisation under the motto tail is protected (c002, c283), so there is no place to add one.
