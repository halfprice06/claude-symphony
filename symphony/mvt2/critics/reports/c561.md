I wrote two scans of score.midi in /tmp/critic-561/scr/: oct.py lists every violin note above F♯5 and the tracks that sound its lower octave at the onset, and div.py finds octaves inside one viola, cello or bass track. For a model I used the Dvořák 9/ii string parts in the corpus (`reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily`, lines 110-125). At the Largo tune's last return Dvořák thins the players to "4 violins", then "2 violins con sordini", then "1 violin", and only then goes back to tutti. This is Rimsky's Ex. 26 principle: a few players carry a pp line by position alone. I did not use the web.

On the lens question. Theme A is the tune a listener will hum afterwards. It is a parallel period, and both phrases open with the same rhythm (dotted quarter, eighth, tied quarter), so the second phrase is heard coming. Its contour rises by arpeggio to a held note and falls through the chromatic tail E♭-D-D♭-C. The appoggiatura peak B♭ comes at 12, 28 and 76, and the registral peak B♭6 at 79. This is the shape of the Dvořák Largo and Elgar Nimrod tunes: few full statements, each one varied by scoring rather than by changing the notes, which is what makes them stick. In theme B, the part a listener keeps is the consequent with the rising fourth A♭-D♭-F (41, 57), not the neighbour-note antecedent (37, 53). c261 and c401 ranked the two halves the same way. The reading's string layouts support the memorability of both themes, and I found nothing in the chapter that calls for a change.

### 1. Both themes follow the Snegourotchka statement order (follows; no change)

**Bars.** Theme A: 7-14, 23-30, 71-82. Theme B: 37-44, 45-47, 53-62.

**Reading.** PDF 61 (h): the same melody "twice *pianissimo*, not doubled, then twice (*mezzo-forte* and *forte*), doubled in the wood-wind". PDF 57 (a): a violin unison gains "power and richness", and wind doubling usually comes later. PDF 58-59: octaves give "expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre".

**Score.** Theme A goes from one horn (mp) to the violins in unison (mp to f, no wind), then to the violins in octaves (mp), then adds Fl 1 and Cl 1 at 77 and reaches ff with B♭6 at 79. Theme B goes from flute alone (p) to C.a. with a flute descant (p-mp), to the head in Ob 1 + Vc (B♭ minor), to the violins in unison (p-mf), and to octaves with Fl/Ob at 57 (f). Each statement is one step louder and doubled one step more than the one before, so the listener knows the tune before it is fully scored. Dvořák 9/ii does the same, from the cor anglais alone to the strings.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. The violin unisons at 23-30 and 53-56 match Ex. 12 (follows; no change)

**Reading.** Ex. 12 (PDF 186) has the Scheherazade iii cantabile in Vln I + II unison, sul D, with only held divided violas, cellos and basses, p, and no wind. PDF 57: in this layout "the tone quality remains that of the string quartet".

**Score.** At 23-30 the violins are in unison sul D under "espr.", with divided violas, the cellos' counter-melody and the basses, and no wind. At 53-56 the unison is sul A, over harp triplets and the Hn 1 + Cl 1 counter-line at pp. The first string statement of each theme therefore has one colour. The winds are kept for 57 and 77, where the reading puts them.

**Fix.** None. This is the c191 doubling plan, which is protected.

### 3. Note I scan: every violin line above F♯5 has its lower octave, with recorded exceptions (follows; no change)

**Reading.** PDF 62, Note I: melodies "exceeding the middle of the 5th octave, are generally doubled an octave below". PDF 59: high lines "become too isolated" unless doubled in octaves.

**Scan (oct.py).** The scan found lower octaves at 57-62, 71-74 and 77-82 in Vln II, Ob 1 or Cl 1. Only five places have no lower octave:
- 17 b1.5-2 and 21 b1.5 (B♭5, G5): the solo violin's two-beat phrases at p over a pp Vln II pedal. Ex. 26 (PDF 202) and its text (PDF 60) say a solo desk "will be sufficiently prominent owing to the general pianissimo".
- 28 b2-3 and 76 b2-3 (B♭5-A♭5): two beats of the violin unison at the appoggiatura. These are recorded and protected (c191 item 5, contrast with 71; c299, B♭6 kept back for 79).
- 63 b1 (A6): the top of the Vln I tremolo chord, in unison with Fl 1. It is the chromatic resolution G♯6-A6 (c070), not a melodic line.

B♭5 is only a minor third above Rimsky's limit, and none of these places is longer than two beats.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Note II scan: no octaves inside a divided low-string section (follows; no change)

**Reading.** PDF 62, Note II: octaves between "Violas I / Violas II", "'Cellos I / II" or "D. basses I / II" are "generally to be avoided", because "unity of tone is impaired".

**Scan (div.py).** Violas, cellos (both MIDI tracks) and basses: 0 simultaneous octaves in all 92 bars. The violas' div. at 25-29 and 80-81 and the cellos' div. at 18, 21-22 use thirds, sixths and fourths.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Middle-register tunes keep their relief by dynamic level (follows; no change)

**Bars.** 41-44 and 48-52.

**Reading.** PDF 54: a melody in the middle register "is not so prominent", so "the methods referred to above come into operation", and dynamic shading comes first among them.

**Score.** At 41-44 theme B's consequent is in the C.a. (A♭3-B♭4) with Fl 1's descant above it. The descant is one level softer (pp<p against p<mp), the violas' pizz. triplets are pp, and Vln II rests (c027). At 48-52 the movement I quotation in the C.a. (C♯4-D5, p to mp) has the Vln II line (c293) above it at pp. That line moves in halves, with quarters only where the quotation holds (49 b3-4, 50 b1-2), and the off-beat violas are sotto voce. Ex. 23 (PDF 199) uses the same kind of layout: the melody in octave violins marked "dolce, espress. e cantabile", with the harp and divided low strings at pp. Re-raising the flute descant at 41-44 would mean answering the c511 rejection: the descant is the passage's only independent line. Nothing in this reading answers that reason, so I do not re-raise it.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. The climax at 79-81 matches Ex. 32 (follows; no change)

**Reading.** PDF 62 (a): Vln I / Vln II / Violas in double octaves "may be used for full *cantabile* melodies extremely tense in character, and in *forte* passages for choice". Ex. 32 (Antar, Adagio in D♭, PDF 210): the tune is in string octaves and the winds and harp only accompany. PDF 63: an unequal number of players across the octaves "is not of any great importance".

**Score.** At 79-81 the tune is at ff in three string octaves: Vln I B♭6, Vln II B♭5, Vla B♭4. Each string octave is paired with a wind (Fl 1, Ob 1 + Cl 1, C.a.). The horns, cellos and bassoon play the counter-melody in a hemiola, and Fl 2 and Ob 2 hold the harmony. The layout is the one the reading recommends for the most tense moment of a slow movement. The tune comes back with its notes unchanged, only higher and in more octaves, which helps a listener remember it.

**Fix.** None. This is the A′ texture plan and the B♭6 at 79, both protected.
