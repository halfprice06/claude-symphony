## Findings

I found no fault that the reading supports changing, and I applied nothing. The findings below are the points where ch. 8 bears on this score that c444 did not examine, with the bar evidence. Each is "follows; no change".

**F1. Bars 15-22: the oboe/violin exchange and the third-time rule. Follows PDF 233 (function 3), 253-255. No change.**
Adler gives "contrasting color repeating or echoing a passage previously played by the strings" as a basic wind function (PDF 233), cites Beethoven's three-note gesture passed between winds and strings and then summed up in tutti (PDF 253, Ex. VIII-20), and says the masters "alter something ... the third time they use the same orchestration ... especially with sequential material" (PDF 255). Bars 15-22 are four two-bar units: Ob 1 (15-16), solo Vln I (17-18), Ob 1 (19-20), solo Vln I (21-22). The third unit, Ob 1 at 19-20, is altered: the line falls (C5-D♭5-B♭4-A♭4-G4, against the rise to F5 at 15-16), Vln II drops out at 20 b1-2 so the oboe sits alone over violas and cellos, and the violas move chromatically F4-E♮4 at 19 b3. The fourth unit adds a new colour, the basses' first entry (senza sord., 21). The summation is the violins' tutti statement at 23. Nothing to change.

**F2. Pedals at the pickups 22 b3, 36 b3 and 52 b4. Follows PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12. No change.**
Strauss lifts the pedal so the first violins' anacrusis to the restatement is heard and the repeat is set apart; keeping the pedal later "contributes to the tension" (PDF 245). c444 checked 10, 26, 44, 70 and 74. The other three pickups do one or the other on purpose. At 36 b3 the contrabassoon and Bsn 1 stop and only the bass clarinet's C3 sounds under the cor anglais's C5 pickup, which hands to Fl 1 on 37 b1: the lift, as in Ex. VIII-12, so theme B begins on a clear pickup. At 22 b3 the cellos' E♭3 and the basses' E♭2 hold under the violins' E♭4-F4 pickup: the half cadence's dominant is kept, and the violins (mp) are two levels above it (pp). At 52 b4 the timpani's A♭ roll (pp<) and the basses' A♭1 hold under the violins' C5 pickup to the build at 53: tension kept where the music is meant to push on. Each choice fits the place.

**F3. No flute is interlocked under a reed. Follows PDF 256, Ex. VIII-22. No change.**
Ex. VIII-22 marks a low flute inside an oboe pair "bad, especially if the dynamic is pp", and a flute under a clarinet "weaker". scan.py checked every half beat of score.midi in bars 1-92. No flute note is ever lower than a sounding oboe, clarinet or cor anglais note. In the soft wind solos (37-44, 77) the flute is always the top wind voice: Fl 1 a sixth to a third above the C.a. at 41-44, and above Cl 1 at 77. This confirms c444's result on v584.

**F4. Joins between the choirs. Follows PDF 252, 259-260. No change.**
Adler's "melting" of a wind chord into a string chord (Brahms 2/iii, PDF 252, Ex. VIII-18) and W. Schuman's holding the strings back so their return is fresh (PDF 260) are both used at every join. At 3-6 the strings fade out under the held wind chord (Hn 3·4, Cl 2, B.cl), so the horn's pickup at 6 b3 is alone. At 14→15 the harp and winds end on A♭ and the muted strings enter pp with Ob 1, a new colour after eight string-free bars. At 30 the contrabassoon's A♭1 enters under the strings' last chord and continues into the wind choir at 31 (with Bsn 2). At 36→37 the cor anglais's C5-F5 overlaps the strings' return. At 82→83 the harp's harmonics take over from the tutti. The movement's last chord (91-92) is strings plus Cl 2 alone, a wind colour on the string chord as at the end of the Brahms example.

**F5. The two tutti chords at 61 b1 and 79 b1. Follows PDF 257, 265-268, Ex. VIII-24, 27, 37, 39. No change.**
Adler wants the widest gaps at the bottom and the upper winds in close position (PDF 257). At 79 b1, from the bass up: D♭1 (Cbsn), D♭2 (Cb), D♭3 (B.cl, Bsn 2), A♭3 (Hn 4), D♭4 (Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 1·2), F4 (Hn 3, Cl 2), B♭4 (Vla, C.a.), F5 (Ob 2), B♭5 (Vln II, Ob 1, Cl 1), F6 (Fl 2), B♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I): two octaves at the bottom, then fifths and fourths. The A♭ appears only up to A♭3 because B♭6 is an appoggiatura to A♭ (80 b2), and doubling its resolution note higher up would weaken it. The flute doubles Vln I at pitch on B♭6. Adler says octave doubling is usually clearer (PDF 265-266), but the octave above B♭6 is outside the flute's practical range at ff. His Berlioz example also shows that an at-pitch flute adds warmth "especially as the line goes up" (Ex. VIII-37). Oboes and clarinets fill the upper harmony and the bassoon joins the cellos, which is the *Carnival* layout (Ex. VIII-39). At 61 b1 the layout is the same: D♭1, D♭2, D♭3, then F3, A♭3, D♭4, F4, A♭4, D♭5, F5, A♭5, D♭6, A♭6, with the wide gaps at the bottom.
