## Findings

### 1. Bars 25, 26, 28 (violas) and 39 (Vln II): single notes inside a divisi passage, with no *unis.* and one stem. **Applied.**
**Problem.** The violas are *div.* from 23 and stay in double notes to 30, except F4 at 25 b3, E♭4 at 26 b1-2 and F4 at 28 b3 (c127, c257 thinned these to one note). Vln II is *div.* at 37-40 and has a single G♭4 at 39 b3 after the upper half's tied B♭4. Each is printed as a plain single note on one stem. A player on the outside of the stand cannot tell whether to play the unison or rest; the upper half at 39 may hold B♭ or move.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: "When divisi is no longer in order and the part returns to single notes, the word unisoni (… unis.) should appear on the part." Printing *unis.* and *div.* on alternate beats would clutter four bars, so the fix uses the other standard sign of a unison inside a divided part: one notehead with an up stem and a down stem, i.e. both halves play it (as Ex. II-12 shows each half's notes on its own side of the chord).
**Fix (applied).** aOne.ily, Vla: `<c' es'>2 << { f'4 } \\ { f'4 } >> | << { es'2 } \\ { es'2 } >> r4 |` and `<des' f'>4\f\> << { f'4 } \\ { f'4 } >> |`. bSec.ily, Vln II 39: `<des' bes'>2 << { ges'2 } \\ { ges'2 } >> |`. No pitch changes. The MIDI now plays those four notes as two unison voices (analysis.txt lists "Violas:2" and "Violins II:2" tracks with 3 and 1 notes); the sound is the same pitch. If the composer prefers words, the alternative is `^\markup \italic "unis."` on each single note and `^\markup \italic "div."` on the next chord.

### 2. Bars 45-47 (violas, basses): the arco re-entry after pizzicato is marked up-bow. **Applied.**
**Problem.** The violas play pizz. to 44 b2 and the basses to 44 b1; at 45 both take the bow again with `\upbow` on a pp whole note (c186), so that the crescendo at 46 b3 is an up-bow and the fp at 47 a down-bow. After pizzicato the bow is in the palm at the frog; an up-bow start makes the player carry the bow to the tip in the two or three silent beats before a pp entry.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: the return from pizzicato to arco "is a bit more awkward … because the bow must be rearranged into playing position"; the up-bow before pizz. exists to bring the hand to the frog. PDF 38: mark bow direction only to counteract the players' habit, without overmarking; crescendo is natural on the up-bow. An entry after rests is taken down-bow by habit.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Vla: `f1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | des2( bes2)\< |`; Cb: `bes,,1\pp^\markup \italic "arco" | bes,,2( ges,,2)\< |` (V removed, 46 slurred). Bowing is now 45 down from the frog, 46 one up-bow with the crescendo, 47 fp on a fresh down-bow at the frog. This answers c186's do-not-touch: its two aims (crescendo on the up-bow, fp at 47 down-bow) are kept, and one bow sign fewer is printed in each part. Vln II, which was not pizz., keeps c186's V at 45; it reaches 47 down-bow as well. Four beats at p on one up-bow at ♩=60 is within Adler's limits (PDF 39).
