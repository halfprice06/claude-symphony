## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 59–60: the bass G♮ is followed by G♭ in two other voices at the V7 arrival (applied)

**Problem.** At 59 b3–4 the bass (Cb G1, B.cl and Bsn 2 G2) has G♮, the root of vii°7/V (G–B♭–D♭–F♭). At 60 b1 the bass rises to A♭, which is correct, but G♭ enters at once in two other voices: the violas' sextuplets (G♭3 on every group) and trombone 2 (G♭3, added at c119). So the chromatic step G→G♭ is split between the bass and inner voices on the strongest beat of the crescendo into the climax. This is the case A&S tell you to avoid: "a chromatic succession can sound disagreeable if it is split between two voices, especially when the two outer voices are involved" (PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a; Points 7, PDF 395). Here the bass takes part.

**Evidence that the bar already contains the remedy.** Bassoon 1, horn 1 and the cellos play E♭3–F3–G♭3–F3 in bar 60, so the 7th of V7 enters on b3 as a passing tone. That is one of the three approaches to the 7th that A&S recommend (PDF 377, Ex. 22-7d–f). The early G♭ in the violas and trombone 2 adds nothing to the harmony and creates the cross relation.

**Fix (bSec.ily), applied:**
- `bSecVla` bar 60, first sextuplet group: `ges16(\< c' es' c' ges c') es'( c' ges c' es' c')` → `aes16(\< c' es' c' aes c') es'( c' aes c' es' c')`. Beats 3–4 are unchanged, so c029's final G♭ still resolves to the F of 61.
- `bSecTbnTwo`: `ges1\pp\< \breathe` → `es2\pp\< ges2 \breathe`.

**On c119's reason (open fifth in the low brass).** For two beats at pp the trombones and tuba hold A♭–E♭–A♭. Meanwhile seven parts (flutes, oboe, clarinets, violins, violas) hold the C, so the chord is complete in the orchestra. On b3 trombone 2 takes the 7th together with the counter-line, which keeps c119's G♭3 → F3 resolution into 61. c104's fifth problem (E♭3–A♭3 into 61) does not return, because trombone 2 is on G♭3 when the bar ends. The viola change is a pitch correction, not a change of texture, so c124's rule (no further accompaniment changes in 37–60) is kept in spirit.

### 2. Bars 50–51: B♮ in the bass becomes B♯ (written C) in the violas at the pivot back to D♭ (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 50 b3–4 is iv of F♯ minor (B–D–F♯, bass B1/B2). Bar 51 is G♯7 respelled as A♭7, the V7 of D♭. Two tones are inflected by a semitone. D→D♯ (written E♭) moves directly in Vln II, which is good. B→B♯ (written C) is split: B in the bass, then C4 in the violas' off-beat eighths at 51. By ear this is the same chromatic step written two ways, and the bass takes part (PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a). The respelling is right for the key signature, but it does not change what is heard.

**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bar 50), applied:** `r8 a8-_ r8 a8-_ r8 d'8-_ r8 d'8-_` → `r8 a8-_ r8 a8-_ r8 d'8-_ r8 b8-_`.

The violas keep D4 on b3, so iv still has its 3rd. On b4 they move to B3, which then rises to C4 at 51: the chromatic step is now in one voice, and the bass B becomes a doubling, which A&S call "unobjectionable" (PDF 394, Ex. 23-12b). B3 → C4 moves in contrary motion to the bass (B → A♭) and in parallel 10ths with Vln II (D5 → E♭5). The cor anglais quotation (47–53) and the off-beat rhythm (c107) are unchanged.

### 3. Bar 21: outer-voice cross relation D♭ (bass) → D♮ (solo violin). No change; recorded

At 21 b1–2 the basses and cellos hold D♭ (VI of F minor after the deceptive V7 at 20). At b3 they fall to B♭, and the solo violin, the top voice, reaches D5 for V7/V. No voice moves D♭→D. This is Ex. 23-12a between the outer voices. I tested every way to put the step into one voice. A cello D3 under the violin's D5 makes D–E♭ octaves into 22, and so does any D in the bass. An inner D4 in Vln II doubles the leading tone of V/V. The melody is protected (solo violin 17–22). The effect is also weaker than in A&S's example: the violin reaches D5 from E♭5 a beat after the bass's D♭ is struck, not straight after a D♭ in the same register. Record it as a known exception and do not raise it again.

### 4. Bars 24 b3 and 72 b3: doubled leading tone in V7. No change; recorded

At 24 b3 (and at 72) the violas' upper A♭4, the 7th of V7/V, falls to G4, as A&S require (PDF 375). The cellos' counter-melody also reaches G3, so G is doubled in V7, and at 25 the violas' G4 leaps to E♭4. Both moves are required by stronger rules: the 7th must fall, and the counter-line rises G3–A♭3. The doubled G resolves once (cellos) and leaves once in an inner voice. 23–30 and 71–82 are protected. Not worth changing.

- Every ♭6̂ goes to 5̂ before any ♮6̂ returns (PDF 388, 395): 5–6, 59–60, 78, 80–81, 88–89. The coda's F–F♭–E♭ is A&S's "good" order, ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂.
- 77–78 is the ♭VI deceptive cadence of Ex. 23-6, with the 3rd of ♭VI doubled and the 7th D♭ falling to C♭. 78 b3 then returns to major, with C♭→C in one voice (Ex. 23-4 type ♭3̂–♮3̂).
- 81's V♭9 is a °7 embellishing V7 by suspension (PDF 378, Ex. 22-9), and it is the "VII°7 in major through ♭6̂" of the Chopin mazurka in this same key (PDF 393, Ex. 23-9).
