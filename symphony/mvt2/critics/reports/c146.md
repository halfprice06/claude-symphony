## Findings (ranked)

**F1. Bars 50–81: soft sticks are the only beaters named, but the part rolls f in the tutti at 60–62 and 79–81. Applied.**
Problem: "soft sticks" at bar 1 was chosen for the ppp attacca roll (c056). Nothing later changes it, so it also covers the f roll at 60 into the Largamente downbeat at 61, the f D♭ roll at 61–62 under ff brass, and the f rolls at 79–81 at the A′ climax. With lambswool sticks these rolls lose definition, and the 61 downbeat loses its attack. That downbeat is the cadence the timpani are meant to complete.
Evidence: Adler PDF 61 and 80 ask the composer to name hard, medium or soft. PDF 88 asks for beater instructions in score and part. PDF 79 gives the classical role as "consummating climactic cadence points". Ex. XII-2's footnote (PDF 61) requires time for a stick change. Movement I does the same thing: soft for its opening roll, medium for its loud music, soft for its coda.
Fix (applied): bSec timpani, in bar 50, which falls inside 13+2 bars of rest (about a minute):
`\tag #'score { R1^\markup \small "medium sticks" R1 } \tag #'part { <>^\markup \small "medium sticks" \cueDuring #"vcQ" #DOWN { <>^\markup \tiny "Vc." R1*2 } }`.
I added no change back to soft sticks. The ppp roll at 67 is handled by the beating spot (F3), and the pp solo answer at 84 gains pitch clarity on medium sticks. This keeps the part to one change.

**F2. Bar 62 b3: the D♭ is not damped under the F♭ chord or into A minor at 63. Applied.**
Problem: `des4.:32~ des8 r2`. The f D♭ roll ends on a stroke at b2½, and then the harmony moves to F♭ major (E–G♯–B) at b3 and to A minor, with C♮5/C♮6 on top, at 63. A struck f D♭3 (C♯) keeps ringing for several seconds unless the player damps it. Two beats at the allargando tempo are about 2.5 s, so the C♯ is still sounding against the C♮ at 63. The rest tells the player when to stop playing, not when the sound stops. c056 fixed the same problem for the A♭ in this bar by shortening the roll, but it did not mark the damping.
Evidence: Adler PDF 58 (Strauss: no out-of-the-harmony timpani notes with modern tuning); PDF 80 (the part must show "how long a note may ring"); PDF 63 and 71 (l.v. and its exact length must be stated, so a note that must not ring needs the opposite instruction).
Fix (applied): `des4.:32~ des8 r2^\markup \small \italic "damp" |`.

**F3. Bars 67–70: ppp roll under the horn solo, with no beating spot. Applied (closes c056 F7).**
Problem: four bars (about 14 s) of ppp E♭3 roll under Horn 1's tail, over a held dominant, with a thin texture (3.8 voices on average at 65–68). F1 puts medium sticks in the player's hands here. E♭3 also sits near the bottom of the 23" drum (Ex. XII-46), where the head is slack and the tone is less focused.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80: "A beautiful pianissimo can be achieved by playing even closer to the rim."
Fix (applied): retr bar 67 `es2.:32\ppp~^\markup \small \italic "near the rim"`, and aTwo bar 79 `des2.:32\f^\markup \small \italic "ord."` so that the normal spot returns for the f roll.

**F4. Bar 5: the retuning is placed in the exposed attacca link. Not applied, optional.**
Problem: "muta in A♭, C, D♭, E♭" stands at bar 5, during the soft wind chords of the link. Three drums must move: by Ex. XII-46 the 30" drum's G cannot reach D♭3, so A♭ moves to the 30" drum, C to the 28" and D♭ to the 25". The player then rests until 52, and the new D♭ is not needed until 61. In bars 4–6 no D♭ is sounding to check the new pitch against. From bar 37 the orchestra plays in D♭ major with D♭ in the bass.
Evidence: Adler PDF 81, Ex. XII-51: the tuning change is marked over the rests before the new pitch is needed. PDF 79: pedals make the change quick, so there is no reason to do it early in the most exposed bars.
Fix: move the text into the long rest. intro bar 5 becomes `R2. |`, and the first bar of bSec becomes `R1^\markup \small "muta in A♭, C, D♭, E♭" | R1*12 |`. The score shows separate rests, so the layout is unaffected. The part's 13-bar multi-rest would split 1+12. I did not apply this, because the tuning at 5 is playable and was settled at c056.

**F5. Roll notation at ♩=52–60 (c056 F6, open since cycle 56). No change. Close it.**
The three-stroke slash on every roll is the notation Adler prefers to "tr" for drum rolls (PDF 83, after Ex. XII-58). Ex. XII-47 and XII-48 (PDF 80) show it as the standard timpani roll. No player reads three slashes on a half note as measured 32nds. Adler asks for a measured/unmeasured mark only for tremolos between two drums (Ex. XII-49), and there are none here.
