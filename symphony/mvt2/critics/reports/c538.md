## What else I studied

I read every woodwind variable in sections/intro, aOne, trans, bSec, retr, aTwo and coda, and the global tempo plan, and looked at all eight score pages at 90 dpi. For each wind note marked p or softer I checked its pitch against the danger bands above (oboe B♭3-F4, bassoon B♭1-F2, contrabassoon sounding B♭0-F1). For each wind solo I compared its printed character word and hairpin shape with the matching Adler example. I read c536 (playability, the same chapter, one cycle ago) so as not to repeat its breathing and register points, and c528, c518, c508, c498 and c537 for the open expression items. For the A′ climax I checked Dvořák 9/ii against the reading's own reference (Adler cites Dvořák 9/ii 7-18 for the cor anglais, PDF 206): the Largo's wind solos carry one character word at the entry and shape the phrase with hairpins, with no tempo words inside the solo, which is what this score does at 41, 47 and 67.

## Findings

This reading is about dynamics, articulation and character by register in the individual woodwinds. The score follows it at every point I could test. Five earlier passes over this chapter, and c536 a cycle ago, fixed the larger problems. I found nothing that needs a change to the score. Each finding below records a check so that the reasons are on file, and finding 5 is an open option I did not apply.

### 1. Soft dynamics by register: every pp or ppp sits outside the bands Adler forbids (recorded, no change)

**Check.** Adler forbids pp in the oboe's lowest fifth (PDF 198) and the bassoon's lowest fifth (PDF 226). He says the clarinet family can play the faintest pp anywhere (PDF 210). He also warns that a "shrill" top register can only be played loud (PDF 185). In this score the oboes never go below A♭4. The cor anglais's softest low notes (A♭3-C4 at 32-34 and 41) are marked p, not pp. Adler calls that register "richly beautiful, with tremendous expressive carrying power" (PDF 205), so p is the right level, and there is no ban on soft playing there. The bassoons' pp notes are all at B♭2 or higher (7-14, 31-34, 45-47, 68-74). The contrabassoon's pp is on sounding A♭1 (written A♭2), above its lowest fifth. The lowest soft wind notes are the B.cl. B♭1 at 8 (pp) and Cl 2 E♭3 at 3-6 (pp). Both are clarinets, which Adler allows at any dynamic. The flute's highest notes (A♭6 at 58, 61; A6 at 63; B♭6 at 79-80) are all at mf or louder.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 2. Wind solos: character words and hairpins match Adler's models (recorded, no change)

**Check.** Every one of Adler's expressive solo examples gives the solo a character word at the entry and shapes the phrase with hairpins whose peak falls on the expressive note: Ex. VII-5 "p dolcissimo", VII-6 "p< légèrement et expressif", VII-34 "p cantabile" with fp, VII-48 "mf espressivo" with sf>, VII-77 pp<>pp (PDF 186-218). In this score:
- Hn 1 at 6: "dolce espr." p, with swells peaking on the tied E♭4 at 7-8 and on B♭4 at 12 b2.
- Ob 1 at 15 and 19: "dolce" p<>, peaking on F5 at 16 b1 and on the appoggiatura A♭4 at 20 b1.
- B.cl. at 31: "solo, espr." p<mf>mp, peaking on the held E♭3 before the chromatic descent.
- Fl 1 at 37: "dolce cantabile" p<> each bar.
- C.a. at 41: "solo, dolce cantabile" p<mp>p.
- Ob 1 at 44: "dolce espr." p<mf>.
- C.a. at 47: "solo, espr." (the quotation, protected).
- Hn 1 at 53: "espr." pp<mp.
- Hn 1 at 67: "dolce" p<mp>pp.
- Cl 1 at 85 and C.a. at 87: "dolce" niente<pp and pp.

Every peak lands on a suspension, an appoggiatura or the highest note, as in VII-4 and VII-34. No solo carries more than one word, and no hairpin peaks on a resolution.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 3. Bars 56 and 81: the two closest cases to the bassoon and contrabassoon low-register rule (recorded, no change)

**Bar 56.** Bassoon 1 ends its 54-56 line G♭2-F2 at p. F2 is the top note of the bassoon's lowest fifth, where Adler says pp is "extremely difficult" and "one must expect a firmer attack" (PDF 226). The level here is p, not pp. The F2 is slurred from G♭2, so it has no attack of its own. It sits under the cellos at mf and the basses' A1-B♭1 (c527). A firmer F2 would not stand out.

**Bar 81.** The contrabassoon holds sounding E♭1 (written E♭2), which is inside its lowest fifth, under a diminuendo from the ff at 79. It reaches p only on the A♭1 at 82, above that fifth, before its niente release, which the basses cover (c418, c536). The rule is not broken: the low note is never asked for pp.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 4. Doubling levels: the doubler is marked under the line it supports (recorded, no change)

**Check.** Adler says the oboe "can add great articulation and cutting edge", so it must not overshadow the instrument that is featured (PDF 201). He also says a bassoon doubling the cellos is "absorbed in the whole" (PDF 226). The score sets the doubling instrument a level below, or equal to, the featured line:
- 41-44: Fl 1 pp→p is the octave above the C.a. solo at p→mp.
- 45-47: Ob 1 p→mf over Vc pp→mp and Bsn 1 pp→p.
- 53-56: Cl 1 pp→p doubles Hn 1 "espr." pp→mp in unison.
- 75-78: Bsn 1 and Hn 2 at p doubling the cello counter-melody at mp.
- 57-60: Ob 1 doubles Fl 1 at the same level, but there both double the violins' theme B head, and none of the three is a solo, so equal levels are correct.

c537 recorded 45-46 as protected and named an Ob 1 p at 45 as the only test to make. Ob 1 already enters at p on 44 b4 and holds that level into 45, one level over the cellos and bassoon. So that test is already met in print.

**Verdict.** Follows. No change.

### 5. Bar 60: clarinets 1·2 re-enter after a half-bar rest with only a crescendo (open option from c528, not applied)

**Problem.** In the clarinet parts, 59 ends with "f r2", and 60 is a whole note with "<" and no restated level. In the score the f at 59 b1 on the shared staff covers it.

**Evidence.** Adler's clarinet examples restate the level at each new phrase entry after a rest (Tchaikovsky 5, Ex. VII-59, PDF 211: p, più f, mf, p; Mendelssohn, Ex. VII-63, PDF 212). c528 raised the same point from MOLA and left it optional (HISTORY, cycle 528: "Open (optional): part-only f for Cl 1·2 at 60").

**Fix (optional, not applied).** In bSec.ily, bSecClOne `c''1\< \breathe` → `c''1-\tag #'part \f \< \breathe`, and in bSecClTwo `aes'1\< \breathe` → `aes'1-\tag #'part \f \< \breathe`. The score and MIDI do not change. I did not apply it. It concerns the parts only, the player already has f two beats earlier, and my reading gives it only weak support.
