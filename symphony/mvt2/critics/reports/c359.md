## Findings

None of these needs a score change. The movement passes every applicable principle in the reading. Findings 1 and 2 are the only bars where a principle is met only through its stated exception. I give them first so the composer can record them; the fixes I tried on paper are worse than the current text.

### 1. Bars 53-56: harp triplets share the counter-line's register (recorded, no change)
- Problem: the harp RH triplet figure (A♭3-A♭4; `bSecHarpR`) lies in exactly the register of the Cl 1/Hn 1 counter-line (A♭3-B♭4, from 53 b4). Adler PDF 274 says to keep the accompanying forces registrally distant from the soloist, "especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments".
- Evidence against changing it: Adler's sentence goes on, "rhythmic contrast lessens the importance of this consideration". The counter-line moves in halves and dotted quarters against continuous triplet eighths. The harp's plucked notes also decay under the held wind tones, which is the composer's own reason for the pizz. violas under the cor anglais at 41-44 (c057). Glazunov does the same at [3] and [10]: the harp arpeggios run through the register of the clarinets' and flute's solo line.
- Fixes tried on paper: an octave up puts the harp's F5 on the violins' melody note at 53 b1. An octave down puts A♭2 under the cellos' D♭3 at 53 (a 6/4 on the downbeat) and crosses the B♭2-G♭2 bass at 54. Neither is better. Keep as written.

### 2. Bars 45-46: Vln II F4 inside the octave of theme B's head (recorded, no change)
- Problem: Ob 1 (D♭5) and Bsn 1/cellos (D♭4) play the head in octaves. Vln II holds F4-G♭4 between them, a third above the lower line (`bSecVlnTwo`, `f'1 | f'2 ges'2`). Adler PDF 274 applies, as in Finding 1.
- Evidence against changing it: the held whole note against the moving line is the rhythmic contrast Adler allows. The F4 is the only third of B♭ minor above the bass in 45, since the violas' F3 doubles it an octave lower. Vln II also carries straight into the E♯4 fp of 47, the leading note of the F♯-minor quotation (c042), which works as Adler's "lead to the first pitch" (PDF 275) for the cor anglais. If Vln II rested at 45-46 (`R1*2`), its entry at 47 would be fp with nothing before it. Keep.

### 3. Bars 23-30: violas move in the theme's quarter-note rhythm (protected; Glazunov agrees)
- Adler PDF 260 asks for accompaniment rhythm that "neither duplicates nor imitates" the solo. At 24 and 28 the violas (`<c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4`) and basses move in the melody's quarters. This is the protected plain first statement (c007, c046). Glazunov gives the same plainness to his first violin statement at [1]: the divided violas and cellos move in the melody's dotted-quarter rhythm, and the decorative accompaniment comes only at [3]. This score likewise saves the eighth-note figure for A′ at 71. No change.

### 4. Bars 71-82: the return keeps the theme in the violins (settled; Glazunov differs, not a reason to change)
- At "Tempo I" Glazunov gives his theme first to the violins in a new setting, then at [10] to a solo flute over harp semiquaver arpeggios and pizzicato strings. That is a colour change for the soloist (Adler PDF 263-264, 267). This score keeps theme A in the violins at 71 and changes the setting: viola eighths, Hn 2 on the cello counter-melody, Fl 1 from 77, B♭6 at 79. c319 settled this ("the return changes setting, not melody"), and c339 and c349 confirmed it. The horn statement at 7 and the violin statements at 23 and 71 already give theme A two colours, as Glazunov has two. No change.

### 5. Bars 79-81: no harp at the climax (settled; c076, c079 stand)
- At his "allarg. appass." climax Glazunov writes the harp ff in arpeggios, with cellos and clarinets in semiquaver arpeggios under the sustained tune. That is Adler PDF 260's rhythmic independence at full strength. In this score everything at 79-81 sustains or moves with the melody. The harp was taken out of 79-81 by c076 and c079: one harp cannot be heard against a full forte (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47), and its re-entry inside the diminuendo was an attack against the drop-out. Glazunov's tutti at that point is marked f, not ff, and his trumpets and trombones fall at once from f to mf. That leaves his harp room that this score's ff tutti, with four horns, does not. c079's reason stands. No change.

### 6. Holistic comparison with Glazunov 5/iii (no change)
Points where the score matches Glazunov's practice:
- A wind-and-brass opening before the strings take the tune. Glazunov's first ten bars alternate a wind chorale with divided strings; here 3-14 are winds, horn and harp. This is Adler PDF 263's colour contrast.
- One clearly placed climax per half, prepared by a build in which a moving inner figure speeds up. Glazunov uses semiquaver triplets at [7]; here triplets at 53 become sextuplets at 59.
- A contrasting middle section in a third-related key with a new accompaniment type. Glazunov goes to E major with string triplet semiquavers and off-beat horn chords; here D♭, F♯ minor and A minor, with triplets, off-beats and tremolo.
- A soft close that thins by instruments. Glazunov's last page drops the strings to pizzicato under winds and harp; this score drops to strings and one clarinet.
- Where Glazunov is richer: his accompaniments are busier (harp arpeggios through most of the reprise). This movement is a Dvořák-model Largo, and its sparer surface is deliberate (c303, c319, c339).
- A conductor would accept this score as professional late-Romantic writing. It sits nearer to Dvořák's restraint than to Glazunov's decorative fullness, which is the brief.
