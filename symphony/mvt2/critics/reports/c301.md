## Findings

### 1. Bar 26: the strings stop moving at the half cadence, and the cello counter-melody has the same rhythm in six bars running (applied)

**Bars:** 23-26, in particular 26 b2.

**Problem:** From 24 to 29 the cello counter-melody has the rhythm half + quarter in every bar (`f2 g4 | aes2 c'4 | bes2 g4 | es2 f4 | aes2 des'4 | bes2 des'4`). At 26 the violins hold the theme's half-cadence G4 for two beats. The violas hold E♭4 and rest on b3 (c257), the cellos hold B♭3 and the basses hold E♭2. So beat 2 of bar 26 is the only beat in 23-30 where no string part attacks or moves. In the horn statement the harp arpeggiates on that beat (bar 10). In the reprise the violas' eighths move (bar 74). Only the first string statement stalls there.

**Evidence:**
- Sebesky PDF 207: "keep something going ... projecting enough character to carry one bar into the next".
- PDF 208, Ex. 273 against Ex. 274: in bars 3-4 the cellos change from half notes to quarter notes "to give extra push into the next phrase". Bar 26 is bar 4 of the phrase 23-26.
- PDF 212, Ex. 280: the background moves where the soloist holds.
- Dvořák 9/ii does the same thing at cello bar 17 (`df'4 c bf af`).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-301/work/sections/aOne.ily, line 96):**
```
was:  ... aes2\< c'4)\> | bes2 g4\! |
now:  ... aes2\< c'4)\> | bes4( aes4) g4\! |
```
- The A♭3 is a passing seventh of V7. It falls by step to G3, and G3 stays on b3, so c032's reason holds: no fifths with the bass into 27.
- It is still two bows (B♭-A♭ slurred, G separate), so c118's bow plan is unchanged: crescendo up-bow at 27, diminuendo down-bows at 28 and 30.
- No other part plays A♭ on that beat, and no unison or octave is formed.

**Protection:** "23-30 plain first statement" and "cello counter-melody 23-30" are on the do-not-touch list. The stated reason is that the first string statement stays plain, with violins in unison and the cello counter-melody below. One passing quarter inside the existing counter-melody keeps the statement plain and keeps the counter-melody's contour and goal notes.

I left 74 unchanged, because the violas already move there. If the composer wants the two statements identical, the same edit fits `aTwo.ily` line 54 (`bes4( aes4) g4\!`). It is untested there and would sound against the violas' B♭3 on 74 b2½.

### 2. Bars 41-44: flute 1 moves note for note with the cor anglais (recorded, no change)

**Problem:**
- In 42 and 43 the flute (F5-G♭5-F5-E♭5~E♭5-F5 | G♭5.-A♭5-F5-E♭5) has exactly the cor anglais' rhythm, a sixth above or a tenth above (A♭4-B♭4-A♭4-G♭4~G♭4-F4 | E♭4.-F4-D♭4-B♭3).
- In bar 42 the two parts meet on an octave F on b4½.
- This is the texture of Sebesky's Ex. 279 (PDF 211): "almost constant parallel motion which distorts the aural focus". The higher line tends to take the ear from the theme below it.

**Why no change:**
- c090 made this the movement's single melody-in-sixths and reported it to the coordinator as a cyclic device. It is protected ("Fl 1 thirds/sixths at 41-44 (the one instance)"). c101 placed the flute one dynamic level under the cor anglais.
- Sebesky's example is a background trying to echo a soloist. Here the flute is a duet partner, which Romantic slow movements use often.

**If it is reopened:** keep 41-42 in sixths, which preserves the one instance, and follow Ex. 280 in 43: the flute holds while the cor anglais moves. Replace `ges''4. aes''8 f''4 es''4` with `ges''2 f''4 es''4`. This is untested, and I do not recommend it without the coordinator.

### 3. Theme A and its climax: memorable as it stands (recorded)

**The theme (7-14, 23-30, 71-82):**
- It has a clear rhythmic cell: 4. 8 4 at 7, 9, 11, and the same cell at 23/71.
- It has a recurring chromatic tail (E♭-D-D♭-C).
- It peaks once, on the B♭ appoggiatura in bar 6 of 8 (12, 28, 76), and its range is a ninth.
- Like Dvořák's Largo tune it is mostly stepwise, with one arpeggio, and it can be sung after one hearing.

**The climax:** 79-82 restates 76 b2-78 an octave higher (B♭6-A♭6-G6-E♭6-D♭6-C6-B♭5-A♭5), so the climax brings no new melodic material. Sebesky PDF 223 supports where it is placed: keep the colour for the long held note at the end of the long section, as with "prayer" in Ex. 286. The held B♭6 at 79 is that note, and the run at 78 b3 fills the held A♭ as the flute run fills "summer's day" in Ex. 286 (PDF 222). It is protected (c105, c299). No change.

### 6. Bars 11, 27 and 75: lead and bass rise together (recorded, protected)

**Problem:**
- In each statement the bar before the peak has the melody A♭-C-F and the bass A♭-C-D♭ rising at the same time.
- At 27 the bass's C3 on b2 anticipates the melody's C5 on b2½ by an eighth.

**Why no change:** the bass at 11 is protected (c201, c252), and analysis.txt reports no outer-voice parallels. A contrary bass (A♭-G♭-F, IV6 instead of the rising I6-IV) would change the harmony of all three statements. It belongs to a harmony cycle. I recorded it only.
