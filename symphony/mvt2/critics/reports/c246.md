## Findings

### 1. Violins 78 b3–79: the ff peak arrives in the middle of a five-second down-bow (applied)

**Bars:** Vln I and Vln II, 78 b3 to 79 (the movement's second ff peak, B♭6 in Vln I).

**Problem.** c118 set 77 as one bow, the A♭ at 78 up-bow, and "C6 + grace run + B♭6 one down-bow". The slur still runs `c'''8( \tuplet 3/2 { f'''16 g''' aes''' } | bes'''2.)~\ff`. At the printed poco allargando the MIDI steps are ♩=50 at 78 and ♩=48 at 79, so this one down-bow lasts about 1.2 s + 3.75 s ≈ 5 s, at f rising to ff, with the B♭6 attack coming after a quarter of the bow is already used. It is the longest loud bow in the movement.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38–40: the number of notes on one bow is limited by tempo and dynamic, and "even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow" (the Liszt passage, Ex. II-23, is impossible as slurred). At ff the limit is much shorter. PDF 38: playing is "louder and heavier toward the frog"; Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus) puts the bow change before the strong attack "so that the following attack will be solid". Ex. II-19 and its note (PDF 38, observation 2) show the device for it: two up-bows in succession, with a short stop, "in order to have a down-bow on the accented first beat of the next measure". The score already does this at the first peak: 60 `c'''1\upbow\<`, 61 `des'''2~--\ff\downbow`. At 79 the violas (16 bow strokes from 71 land on a down-bow at 79), cellos and basses (c186) already attack the ff on down-bows; only the violins arrive mid-stroke. The Grieg example above bows its ff notes separately.

**Answer to the protection.** "Bowing 77–80 as of v119" (c118, c196) was set to cure an overloaded bow (77–78 b2 in one down-bow, about 6 s). The present slur still leaves about 5 s at f–ff. The fix keeps c118's 77 and 78 b1–2 bows, the run, the pitches, the tie and the up-bow at 80; it only moves where the down-bow starts.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-246/work/sections/aTwo.ily).** The run becomes a second up-bow, hooked after the A♭; the slur closes on the run's last note, and the B♭6 starts a fresh down-bow at the frog. The tie 79→80 and the \upbow at 80 stay.

```
% Vln I, 78 b3 – 79
aes''2\f \once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.18 . 0.18) c'''8(\upbow \tuplet 3/2 { f'''16 g''' aes''') } |
bes'''2.~\ff\downbow | bes'''4(\upbow aes'''2) | ...
% Vln II, same
aes'2\f \once \override Beam.damping = #5 c''8(\upbow \tuplet 3/2 { f''16 g'' aes'') } |
bes''2.~\ff\downbow | bes''4(\upbow aes''2) | ...
```

Bow plan after the fix: 77 down (mp<f), 78 A♭ up, run second up-bow (crescendo toward the frog, PDF 38), 79 ff down from the frog (3 beats, about 3.75 s), 80 up, 81 down with the diminuendo (PDF 38). Fl 1 is unchanged (its own breath plan, c041). This changes notation only: score.midi is byte-identical.

6. **Open strings and vibrato (PDF 32–33).** No long soft note sits on an open string: the violas take G3 at 65–66 sul C, the second violins take D4 and D♭4 at 64–66 sul G. Keep.
