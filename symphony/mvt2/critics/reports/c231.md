# Critic 231: v231, lens 1 (melody and memorability)

Assigned reading: Sebesky, *The Contemporary Arranger*, ch. 5 "Voices" (PDF 197–203, printed pp. 188–194). I read all seven text pages and rendered all seven pages as images at 110 dpi (Ex. 259–268). The chapter is about choral and commercial vocal-group writing. I applied it to instrumental melody through four questions: does each line move as a singer's line would, does it stay inside one voice's normal range, does it breathe where a singer would, and how are lines doubled and held?

### 1. Theme ranges match one voice's normal range, and the extreme notes are kept for the climaxes (recorded, no change)

Bars 6–14, 22–30, 36–44, 53–60, 70–82.

Ex. 260 (PDF 198) gives each voice a normal span of about a twelfth and brackets the notes beyond it as unusual. The themes of this score measure as follows:

- **Theme A:** from the E♭ pickup to the B♭ appoggiatura it spans a twelfth in every statement. Horn: E♭3–B♭4 at 6–14. Violins: E♭4–B♭5 at 22–30, and E♭5–B♭6 at 70–82. The body of the tune, from A♭ to B♭, is a ninth.
- **Theme B:** the two halves together span A♭ to B♭, an octave and a step. C.a.: A♭3–A♭4 at 41–44. Fl 1: B♭4–B♭5 at 37–44.

So each theme can be sung or hummed by one untrained voice. The same holds for Dvořák's cor anglais tune in 9/ii, which spans a twelfth: written F4–C6, sounding B♭3–F5 (woods.notesL.ily, lines 206–217).

The notes outside a theme's sung compass are the peaks: A♭6 at 58 and 61, A6 at 63, and B♭6 at 76 and 79. This is how Sebesky treats the bracketed notes: they stay outside the ordinary line. No change.

### 3. Something moves under the melody's held notes (Ex. 261), with one place where only the harp moves (recorded, no change)

- **Bars 23–24, 27–28, 71–72, 75–76:** the cello counter-melody moves in eighths or changes note under theme A's tied E♭ and F. This is the alto triplet under "Days" in Ex. 261 (PDF 199), and it has been in place since c001.
- **Bars 38 and 40:** Vln II changes chord on beat 3 under the flute's held E♭5 and B♭4.
- **Bar 54, beats 3–4:** the violins hold E♭5 while the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line attacks B♭4 on the same beat and ties it into 55, so both lines hold together. Only the harp's eighths move.

The counter-line at 54 uses theme A's head rhythm (`des'4. f'8 bes'2`, after `aes4. c'8 es'4`). That reference to theme A is why the line was given to the horn (plan, B3). c201 protected the tie for the sixths at 55. A moving note on 54 b3–4 would blur the head rhythm, so I do not propose one.

### 4. Repeated cells make both themes memorable (recorded, no change)

Ex. 268 (PDF 203) makes plain triads individual through rhythmic inflection of the tune. Both themes have such inflections:

- **Theme A:** a tied syncopation (`4. 8 4~ | 4`) at bars 1–2 and 5–6 of each statement.
- **Theme B:** the triplet turn at 39 and 55, and the tied G♭ at 58.

Dvořák's tune repeats its opening cell (`c8. ef16 ef4`) four times in twelve bars. Here, theme A's head cell comes twice in each eight-bar statement, three statements in all. Theme B is heard twice as the same eight-bar pair: the falling half (37–40, 53–56) followed by the rising-fourth half (41–44 in the C.a., 57–60 in the tutti). A listener can follow theme B through its second statement. No change.

### 5. The doubling plan follows Ex. 263 and PDF 200 and 203 (recorded, no change)

The tune gains singers as the movement goes on:

- one horn (7–14)
- muted unison violins (23–30)
- open unison violins with a unison horn and clarinet counter-line (53–56)
- octaves with flute and oboe (57–60)
- violins in octaves (71–78)
- three octaves at 79–82, each octave doubled by strings and wind: Fl/Vln I B♭6, Ob/Vln II B♭5, C.a./Vla B♭4

This is the reading's rule that more voices per line give body (PDF 200). It also matches the instrument doublings of voices on PDF 203, and the lead doubled an octave lower in Ex. 263. No change.

### 6. Inner lines are singable (recorded, no change)

Ex. 259 (PDF 197–198) asks for every line to be singable.

- **Coda chorale, 87–92:** every line moves by step or holds. C.a. F4–F♭4–E♭4; Vla F3–F♭3–E♭3 and A♭3; Cl 1 D♭5–D♭5–C5; Vc and Cb F–D♭–A♭.
- **Wind choir, 31–36:** the same holds.
- **Bassoons under the horn, 7–14:** they move in quarters by step or by a third.

No line in these passages has a leap a singer would find awkward. No change.
