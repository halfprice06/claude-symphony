## Findings

There is no change to the score. Every woodwind melodic point I checked follows Adler ch. 7. The items are ranked by how much they bear on the melody. The first one corrects the reason on record, not the music.

### 1. Bars 36-40: theme B's first phrase; the record cites Ex. VII-5 for the wrong reason (record correction, no score change)

**Problem.** c291 (finding 1) closed the question of theme B's narrow first phrase partly by calling Adler's Ex. VII-5 (Tchaikovsky PC 1/ii, PDF 186) "a narrow, mostly stepwise tune". The image says otherwise. The flute opens A♭5-E♭5-A♭5, falls to A♭4 by the fourth bar, and climbs back by a rising sequence (A♭4-B♭-D♭-E♭, D♭-E♭-F-G♭, F-G♭-A♭-B♭♭) to B♭♭5 in bar 7. It spans an octave in four bars. Theme B's first phrase (F5-G♭5-F5-E♭5 | D♭5-C5-D♭5-E♭5 | G♭5-F5-E♭5-D♭5 | C5-D♭5-C5-B♭4) stays within B♭4-G♭5 and moves by neighbour notes.

**Evidence that the protection still stands.** Adler's point on PDF 186 is about the accompaniment: the melody "is perceived with a great deal of delicacy" "because of the care the composer has given the accompaniment". Bars 37-40 follow that point (row 5). The movement's model, Dvořák's Largo, has a first phrase a fifth wide (corpus, woods.notesL.ily 207-210). The leap comes in theme B's second phrase (41-44), in the cor anglais's best band (PDF 205), and that is the phrase the finale quotes. So the reasons from c141 and c291 still hold, without Ex. VII-5's range as one of them. Theme B's line is protected (c141), its second phrase is quoted in the finale, and nothing in the reading answers the reason for keeping it.

**Fix.** None in the score. In HISTORY.md, record that Ex. VII-5 supports 37-40 for its thin accompaniment, not for a narrow range.

### 3. Bars 57-63 and 77-79: flute 1's top notes at the two climaxes (recorded, keep)

A♭6 at 58 comes by step from G♭6. A6 at 63 comes by a semitone. B♭6, the movement's single registral peak, comes at 79 by the scale run C6-F6-G6-A♭6 (PDF 186: "the best approach is by scale"). A♭6 at 61 is a sixth up from C6 after a breath, below A6 and at ff, where the top is meant to be loud (PDF 185). Fl 1 rests 64-76 after the first climax (PDF 188). Fl 2 carries theme B's head an octave below at 61-62, so Brahms's relief (Ex. VII-9) is not available and is not needed. Keep.

### 4. Bars 15-22: oboe 1's answers in the A section's middle phrase (recorded, keep)
