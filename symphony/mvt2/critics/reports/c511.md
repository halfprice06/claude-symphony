## Findings

**1. Bars 41-42: the flute's descant moves against the head of theme B. Applied in the scratch copy.**
Problem. Theme B's second phrase is the version the finale quotes. It opens in the cor anglais with its one distinctive gesture: the rising fourth and arpeggio A♭3-D♭4-F4, then G♭4-A♭4. Flute 1 enters on 41 b3 and is the top voice, two octaves up. It plays A♭5-B♭5 and then falls a fourth to F5 on 42 b1, exactly where the tune rises to A♭4. At the tune's arrival the top line goes the opposite way and lands on a different pitch. The listener follows the highest, brightest line, so the line heard on top at 42 b1 is not the theme. From 42 b2 the flute moves in true parallel sixths and tenths with the cor anglais, which is fine.
Evidence. Rimsky PDF 70 describes "melodic progression in thirds and sixths", meaning two lines moving together. The three flute notes at 41 b3-42 b1 do not progress with the tune. Ex. 57 (PDF 229), which c401 cited as the model for this passage, actually has flute and cor anglais in octaves on the same tune, "p dolce e legato assai", not a descant. PDF 67 lists Fl over a double reed as a natural octave pair, and PDF 69 allows mixed timbres in octaves. Dvořák 9/ii's middle section does the same: flute 1 and oboe 1 double the new tune in octaves (woods.notesL.ily). The melody lens matters most here. At 57-58 flute 1 climbs F6-G♭6-A♭6 on this same head. If the flute has already played F5-G♭5-A♭5 at 41-42, the climax repeats a figure the listener has heard in that instrument, and theme B's head becomes the thing people remember.
Answer to the protections. c090 introduced the tenths and sixths as "the symphony's first melody in parallel thirds/sixths". c101 protected "Fl 1 notes at 41-44". The fix keeps the sixths for 42 b2-44, so the one instance remains. It changes only the three notes that were not parallel. It partly restores c051's octave for the head. c101's dynamic (flute a level under the cor anglais) is kept.
Fix (bSec.ily, bSecFluteOne, bars 41-42):

becomes

The top voice over the G♭ bass at 41 b3 is now F5, the same appoggiatura the flute sounds at 57 b3 over the same G♭. Built: 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 8 pages, page breaks unchanged.

**2. Theme B's neighbour-note cell is used in nearly every bar of its statements. Recorded, no change.**
The cell is `2 8 8 4` or `4 8 8 2` with an upper or lower neighbour (G♭-F, C-D♭). It is at 37, 38, 40, 42, 45, 46, 53, 54 and 56. That is nine of the twenty bars of theme B before the climax. Rimsky leaves this to "the orchestrator's own personal taste" (PDF 64). In memorability terms the repetition helps: Elgar's Nimrod and the Dvořák Largo tune both build on one repeated rhythmic cell. Theme A's cell is `4. 8 4`, which keeps the two themes apart. The risk is that 53-56 repeats 37-40 note for note. That is offset by the new colour (violins with Hn 1 + Cl 1 counter-line, PDF 74-75) and by 57 starting the climax on the arpeggio head. Leave it.

**3. Brass melody: the horn tail at 8 and 68, the trombone quotation at 63-64, horn F♭ at 59. Recorded, no change.**
PDF 73 says chromatic melody suits brass less. The book qualifies this twice. The horn in piano allows "greater scope" (PDF 72), as in Ex. 76's pp horns. "Vigorous phrases … introducing chromatic notes sound singularly beautiful on the brass", and Ex. 74 shows exactly a solo trombone phrase with chromatic steps, con forza. Tbn 1 at 63-64 is ff marcato, in minor ("dark and gloomy", PDF 72), and undoubled above (PDF 75). Hn 1's F♭4 at 59 is doubled by the cellos, which blend "beautifully" with the horn (PDF 79). All three follow.

**4. Theme A: contour and memorability. Recorded, no change.**
Theme A rises by arpeggio to a held E♭ and then falls through the motto tail. The second phrase reaches higher (F, then the B♭ appoggiatura) before a stepwise fall. Its range is a ninth. The rhythm `4. 8 4~` comes three times in eight bars. This is the shape of the Dvořák Largo tune and of Tchaikovsky 5/ii's horn melody: one held peak per half and a later, higher second peak. The tune is heard in horn, violins, violins in octaves with the B♭6 peak, and the harp head in the coda. Each colour suits its register (PDF 64, 72). Theme A will be remembered and should stay.

**5. 45-47 and 48-51: oboe head in B♭ minor and the cor anglais quotation. Recorded, no change.**
45-47 is Ob 1 over Bsn 1 + Vc in octaves, the lower part doubled by strings (PDF 67, 78). The quotation is a cor anglais solo marked espr. over sotto voce off-beat violas, the texture of Ex. 44 (PDF 221). Both follow the book.
