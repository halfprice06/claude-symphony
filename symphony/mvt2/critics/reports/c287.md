## Findings

I recommend no change. Everything this reading teaches is already in the score: the bassoon sits inside the horns and takes the discord (62 b1), muted horn is paired with C.a. (87–90), the woodwind chord is superposed above the horns and doubled at forte (61–62, 79–81), the woodwind is marked a degree above the brass (60, 79), and strings meet brass only over tremolo or where horns double cellos or violas (63–64, 29–30, 75–82, 87–89). The five points below are the only places where the score departs from the letter of a rule. Each has a reason, and I checked a change for each and rejected it. I edited nothing in /tmp/critic-287/work. It is identical to v287, and v287's build-warnings.txt is empty. Because no bars changed, I made no MIDI diff.

### 1. Bars 61–62: horns 1·2 at ff, level with the woodwind (no change; recorded c284)

**Problem.** Rimsky PDF 112 says that in difficult cases the woodwind should be marked one degree louder than the brass. At 61–62 the woodwind and Hn 1·2 are both ff. Hn 3·4, the trombones and the tuba are f.

**Evidence.** PDF 108–109 gives a second remedy for forte horns: double the woodwind's upper parts. At 61 the woodwind has F in Ob 1 and Cl 1 (F5), A♭ in two octaves (Fl 1 A♭6, Fl 2 A♭5, with Cl 2 A♭4 in b1–2) and D♭ in Ob 2 (D♭5), joined at b3 by Cl 2 (D♭5) and Fl 2 (D♭6). Horns 1 and 2 each play one note alone (Hn 1 F4, Hn 2 D♭4) against Hn 3·4 a2 on A♭4. c284 gave this as the reason for ff in horns 1·2. The woodwind doubling covers the balance rule.

**Fix considered.** `bSecHnOne`/`bSecHnTwo` bar 61 `f'1--\f`, `des'1--\f`. Not recommended: it would weaken the two single horns under the a2 pair.

### 2. Bar 79 b1: clarinet 2 F4 in unison with horn 3 at the top of the horn chord (no change; recorded c234)

**Problem.** Rimsky PDF 108 says a soft four-horn chord is completed by clarinets "in the upper register" above it. At 79 b1 Cl 2 plays F4 (written G4, throat register) on horn 3's F4 for one crotchet before it rises to D♭5.

**Evidence.** The clarinet is not inside the horns (the scan finds no such case), and at ff it adds nothing audible. c123 moved it off A♭4, where it clashed with the violas' B♭4. Any other note at b1 either leaps a tritone from the G4 pickup (straight to D♭5), adds a seventh B♭ to an appoggiatura that is already doubled in four octaves, or brings back the c123 clash.

**Fix considered.** `aTwoClTwo` bar 78 b3 `c''4` and bar 79 `des''2.`. Rejected: Cl 2 would drop the 78 b3 G4, which is the clarinets' only G. It would also add a fourth C to the chord at 78 b3, where the C is already in Vln II, Fl 1 and Vln I.

### 3. Bars 80 b3–81 b1: the discord F♭ is in horn 3 (no change; protected V♭9)

**Problem.** The crossing rule on PDF 108 gives the concords to the horns and the discords to the bassoons. At 80 b3–81 b1, horn 3's F♭4 carries the defining note of iiø7 and V♭9.

**Evidence.** Rimsky states the crossing rule for soft passages with two horns and two bassoons. Here the passage is forte with four horns, the case where he says to "employ four horns alone". The woodwind already has its own F♭ an octave above, in Ob 2 F♭5 (c234). The V♭9 at 80–81 is on the standing list.

### 4. Bars 29–30: horn 4's E♭3 is the only brass note inside the muted string chord without a string on the same note (no change; recorded c234, c094)

**Problem.** PDF 113 says strings and brass are seldom combined by juxtaposition or enclosure. The exception it names is horns doubled by divided violas or cellos. Horn 3 meets that exception (its D♭4–C4 is the cellos' D♭4–C4). Horn 4's E♭3 lies between the basses' E♭2/A♭1 and the cellos' B♭3/C4 with no string on the same note.

**Evidence.** Rimsky's own Ex. 171 (Antar [57], PDF 342), which he cites on PDF 112, has a horn holding inside divided second violins and violas at pp. In his classification a held soft horn counts as a wind voice. The note is pp and is held for two bars as the common tone of V7 and I. I tested the alternative on paper: lower violas on E♭3 at 29–30, so that divided violas double horn 4 as PDF 113 recommends. It moves the violas off E♭4 at 29 and leaves 29 b1 with no E♭ between E♭3 and the violins. That is a worse spacing for no gain in sound.

### 5. Bars 87–89: the moving line F–F♭–E♭ is in a horn, not the woodwind (no change; protected)

**Problem.** PDF 108 says that in a chain of chords the brass should hold the stationary parts and the woodwind the moving ones. At 87–89 muted horn 4 moves F3–F♭3–E♭3 while horn 3 holds A♭3.

**Evidence.** The moving line is the echo of 61–62's F–F♭ (c279, standing list "plagal close 87–92"). It is doubled an octave higher by the C.a. and in unison by the lower violas. That uses two more of the reading's recommended pairings at once: muted horn with C.a. (PDF 107, 110), and horns doubled by divided violas (PDF 113). The held A♭3 is in horn 3 and the upper violas, so the stationary part is also in brass.
