### F1. Theme B's antecedent is almost all steps; Dvořák's model mixes in thirds (recorded; optional, not recommended)
- **Bars:** 37–40 (Fl 1), 53–56 (Vln I/II); head at 45–46 (Ob 1/Vc).
- **What I counted:** 37–40 has 17 melodic intervals: 16 steps and one third (E♭–G♭ into 39). The range is B♭4–G♭5, a minor sixth. Every bar uses the same neighbour figure (F–G♭–F, D♭–C–D♭, F–G♭–F, C–D♭–C).
- **Reading:** PDF 333–334 says the neighbour figure is "often treated motivically", so a neighbour in every bar follows the book. PDF 329 (Ex. 20-2d) says chordal skips "improve melodic lines by varying a prevailing stepwise movement".
- **Dvořák comparison:** Dvořák's middle-section tune (flute 1, bars 46–49) has the same range (G♯4–E5, a minor sixth) and the same triplet-neighbour idea. But it has about 14 steps and 11 thirds: C♯–E at each bar join, and B–G♯–B and C♯–A–C♯ at 48–49. His tune is held together by the neighbour figure and given its shape by the skips. Ours is held together by the neighbour figure only. The consequent (41/57 A♭–D♭–F, 43/59 F–D♭–B♭) supplies the skips.
- **Why no change:**
  - The steps-then-arpeggio contrast between the two phrases is a real design.
  - The neighbour cells are linked across the movement. Bar 40's C–D♭–C–B♭ is the oboe's line at 19 (F5). Bar 38's lower neighbour has its chromatic counterpart at 46 (A♮, c091).
  - The head F–G♭–F–E♭ was accepted in cycle 1.
  - A skip at 38 or 40 would break these links.
- **Optional fix, if the composer wants one variant only in the third statement:** at 55 (Vln I, Vln II), `ges''4( \tuplet 3/2 4 { f''8 ges'' f'' } es''4 des''4)` → `ges''4( \tuplet 3/2 4 { f''8 es'' ges'' } es''4 des''4)`. The triplet now steps down to E♭ and skips back up to the chord tone G♭ over E♭m7. That gives two thirds (G♭–E♭–G♭–E♭), close to Dvořák's C♯–E–D♯–C♯, and keeps the downbeats, the harmony and the descent to C at 56. Untested. I do not recommend it without hearing it.

### F2. Bar 76 b2.5: the viola arpeggio sounds B♭3 while the B♭6 appoggiatura is still sounding (recorded; considered, no change)
- **Bars:** 76 (Vla `f'( aes' f') bes( f' bes)`; Vln I/II B♭6/B♭5 on b2; bass D♭ to b3, B♭ on b3).
- **Reading:**
  - PDF 338: an appoggiatura is a nonchord tone.
  - PDF 331: arpeggio tones are chord members.
  - At 12 and 28 nothing else sounds B♭ while the appoggiatura sounds, so it is a pure lean against A♭3. At 76 the viola's second 3-note group starts on B♭3 half a beat early. For the second half of the beat the dissonance is doubled two octaves down, as an anticipation of the bass B♭ on b3.
  - My chord-membership audit of all accompaniment figuration found this one case. The other cases are viola triplets 37–44 and 57–60, harp 53–56, viola sextuplets 59–60, and viola eighths 71–78. Each tone in them is a member of the chord sounding with it, or (43 b3) the 5th of the dominant under the cadential 6/4 that c222 settled.
- **Why no change:**
  - c081 recorded the B♭3 as a correct anticipation (A&S Unit 3).
  - c127 chose `bes( f' bes)` in place of `bes( des' bes)` so that the figure does not strike the counter-melody's D♭4 (Vc, Hn 2 at b3).
  - D♭4 at b2.5 would pre-sound that note. A♭3 would re-strike the cellos' held A♭3.
  - The appoggiatura's attack on b2 is clean, which is what the ear takes.
- **Fix:** none.

### F5. The figures that make the tunes memorable recur across the movement (recorded, keep)
PDF 333–334 calls neighbour figures motivic, and PDF 329 says arpeggios connect registers. The movement does both:
- Theme A's head arpeggio returns in five forms:
  - in eighths in the B.cl. at 31;
  - on D♭ in the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line at 53–55 (D♭4. F8 B♭: chordal skips subdividing a sixth, Ex. 20-2e, in theme A's dotted rhythm against theme B);
  - as theme B's consequent opening (41/57 A♭–D♭–F);
  - in the harp harmonics at 83;
  - in the motto tail of Cl 1 at 85–90.
- Theme B's C–D♭–C–B♭ cell is heard first in the oboe at 19, eighteen bars before theme B.
- Vln II at 49 decorates F♯ with G♮ and E♯, the double incomplete neighbour of Ex. 20-13c (PDF 335).

A listener leaves with two cells (the rising arpeggio to a held note, and the semitone neighbour) and the motto tail. That is how Dvořák 9/ii works too.
- **Fix:** none.

### F6. Theme A is varied by its setting, not by melodic figuration; quicker motion is kept for the climax (recorded, keep)
- **The three statements:** theme A appears at 7, 23 and 71 with the same pitches and rhythm through bar 6 of each statement. A′ is varied by figuration in the accompaniment: the viola eighths at 71–78 are chordal skips that "intensify rhythmic activity" (Ex. 20-2a). The tune itself is not varied.
- **Quicker motion:** the tune's shortest value is the eighth until 78. There the grace run (16th triplet, C–F–G–A♭) takes the line to B♭6. The book's "quicker motion" (PDF 328) is therefore saved for the one climax.
- **Comparison:** Dvořák 9/ii's reprise is also literal. Exercise 4's variation technique (PDF 345) would fit a theme that comes back more often than this one.
- **Fix:** none. The A′ texture plan (c247) and the climax are protected.
