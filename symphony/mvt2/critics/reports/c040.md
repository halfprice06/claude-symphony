## Cyclic checks that pass (no change)

- **Link 1–6 against movement I v164.** Movement I ends with a fermata and \laissezVibrer on Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4 (morendo), Vc C3 and Hn 3 C4, all ppp, with "attacca". Movement II bar 1 has \repeatTie and ppp on the same five pitches in the same five parts. The voice leading into bar 2 is chromatic and common-tone: G4→A♭4, E4→E♭4, C3→A♭2, with C5 held. Hn 4 enters on beat 2 under Hn 3, and Hn 3 drops out on beat 3. This is the dovetail of Ex. XV-37 and XV-40, and the seam does not show. The tempos join closely (Lento ♩=50 into Adagio ♩=52). The basses, Hn 4's G3 and the timpani do not carry over, as cyclic-notes (cycle 130) says. One exception is the harp; see F3.
- **Theme A and movement I.** Theme A bar 2 (E♭–D–D♭–C), followed by C–B♭–A♭–G, is movement I's first-theme head (G–E♭–D–D♭–C–C–B–C–A♭–G) with its opening sixth replaced by a rising arpeggio and its C–B–C turn made diatonic. The kinship is audible, and the theme obeys the no-rising-sixth rule.
- **Theme B.** Its head F–G♭–F is 3̂–4̂–3̂ in D♭, the same neighbour figure as Hn 3's C–D♭–C at 3–6 and the final D♭–C. The middle section is therefore built from the motto's last step, as critic 60 asked.
- **Quotations.** 47–49 is movement I's S head in minor with its pickup, transposed exactly (C♯–A–G♯–F♯–D–C♯ = G–E♭–D–C–A♭–G). 63–64 is the P head in A minor, transposed exactly (E–C–B–B♭–A | A–G♯–A–F–E). Each is stated once. The trombone statement follows the Dvořák 9/ii 96–98 model and Adler's sforzando recipe 2 (PDF 189).
- **Reserved for movement III.** Movement II has no C-major material beyond the inherited chord of bar 1, no trombone chorale, no trumpet B→C, and no tam-tam. The S-head rhythm appears only in the quotation.

## Findings

### F1 (applied). Bars 47–53: give movement I's second-theme quotation to the cor anglais, not oboe 1

**Problem.** The quotation's pickup is C♯4 at p in oboe 1. That is the oboe's lowest fifth, where Adler says the instrument "becomes thick and tends to 'honk'" and should never be asked to play softly. He gives the effective range as F4 to C6 (adler1 PDF 198). Cycle 36 already had to soften the line's end (F4–G♭4 at 51–52) for the same reason. The note in question is the rising sixth C♯4–A4, the S head's identifying interval. Adler's rule for a solo tune (adler2 PDF 188) is to fix its register and dynamic first and then choose the instrument whose character suits both. For a p tune between C♯4 and D5, the cor anglais is the choice.

**Cyclic evidence.** In movement I the cor anglais plays the motto with its pickup at 5–8 (`d'4 | bes'2.~ bes'8 a'8`) and at 171–174 (`g'4 | es''2.~ es''8 d''8`). cyclic-notes §1 points out that this first bar is the same as the first bar of the minor S head with its pickup. In the cor anglais, then, the quotation comes back in the colour movement I used for that bar (Adler PDF 19: timbre clarifies content). The cor anglais is free from 45 to 56.

**Fix (applied).** I moved the line unchanged to `bSecCorAnglais`: same pitches, slurs, p/mp/p dynamics, and "solo, espr.". Oboe 1 rests 45–56 and re-enters at 57 as before.
```
bSecCorAnglais = { R1*4 | <41–44 unchanged> |
  R1*2 | r2 r4 cis'4(\p^\markup \italic "solo, espr." | a'2.~ a'8 gis'8 | fis'4 d''2 cis''4) | cis''2(\mp b'4 a'4) |
  aes'2(\> ges'2 | f'2. ges'4)\p | f'2 r2 | R1*3 | R1*8 | }
bSecOboeOne: bars 45–56 → R1*12
```
This keeps the do-not-touch condition, which is a single statement of the S head in minor with its pickup in a solo woodwind (cyclic-notes §3). Only the instrument changes. If the composer wants the cor anglais kept for theme B alone (41–44), clarinet 1 in the chalumeau and throat register is the second choice. The oboe is the weakest of the three for this register at p.

Cycles 11 and 30 removed the tail from theme B (42/58) and from Vln I's last bars. After those changes each formal unit has one statement, except the coda, which has two in six bars. The timpani's version is incomplete (they have no D♮), and it comes immediately before clarinet 1's complete one. The tail has already been heard at 63, 68 and 72, so the third and fourth statements in 22 bars weaken the one that should close the movement.

**Fix (applied).** The timpani answer the harp's rising head (A♭–C–E♭ in the rhythm 4. 8 4, harmonics, 82–83) with its mirror image in the same rhythm. This keeps the timpani's melodic moment (BRIEF, critic 74) and the call and response between harp and timpani:
```
codaTimp = { R2. | es4.\pp c8 aes,4~ | aes,2. | R2. | R2.*2 | aes,2.:32\ppp~ | ... }
```
The coda now has one tail, complete with D♮, in clarinet 1 (85–89), and it lands on the plagal D♭–C. This partly reverses c013/c019 (the timpani's tail) but not their reason, which was "no two statements at once". The timpani still play alone, at 84.

### F3 (applied). Bar 2: movement I's harp chord rings into the A♭ chord

**Problem.** Movement I's last harp chord, C2–G2–C3 ppp, is marked \laissezVibrer, and the harp has no part in movement II until 7. Low harp strings ring for many seconds: the fermata plus bar 1 at ♩=52 is about 8 s. They are still sounding when the cellos, basses and timpani move to A♭ at bar 2, so G2 rubs against A♭1 and A♭2. Adler's rule for any colour change is that the seam must not show (adler2 PDF 192). Here an undamped residue from the previous movement shows through instead.

**Fix (applied).** I gave the harp a damping instruction at the moment the C-major chord gives way:
```
introHarpL = { R2. | R2.^\markup \small \italic "étouffez (movement I's chord)" | R2.*4 | }
```
A better solution may lie in movement I; see the coordinator list.

### Not changed, with reasons

- **The three tails at 63, 68 and 72.** The chain runs from the P head in A minor (C–B–B♭–A), through the horn's E♭–D–D♭–C over E♭7, to theme A′. The tail rises a minor third and becomes the reprise. Hn 1 at 67–70 is protected, and the chain does the work of a retransition, so I recommend no change.
- **The BCl tail at 33.** It is at the same pitch as Hn 1 at 8 and 68. Its D♮ belongs to the protected common-tone diminished seventh, and it is the transition's single statement. It stays.
- **Cl 1 → Vln I hand-over at 89–90.** Clarinet 1 stops at 90 b3 without a diminuendo, under Vln I's C5. A fade would follow Ex. XV-37/XV-40. But clarinet 2 shares the staff and holds E♭4 at full pp until the morendo at 91, so a hairpin on clarinet 1 alone breaks the rule that a shared staff must have equal simultaneous dynamics. I tested it and reverted it. I leave it to the composer's judgement.

## For the coordinator (movements I and III; not edited)

1. **Movement I, bar 182, harp.** Remove the \laissezVibrer from the harp's C2–G2–C3, or mark it "secco/étouffez at the fermata's release". Then movement II's damping note (F3) becomes unnecessary. Only the five tied parts should cross the attacca.
2. **Movement III's opening.** The I→II joint is a held common tone with attacca. II→III should not repeat that device. Movement II ends with Vln I C5 ppp under a fermata, which is the same final sound as movement I. If III starts quietly from C, the symphony would begin each movement out of the same sound three times. Adler PDF 20 (a new mood needs a new combination) supports a pause and a contrasting full attack for III. A&S's German-sixth route (II's last chord + F♯ → V of C) can instead appear inside III's introduction.
3. **Pitch thread.** Movement I's last viola note is E4; movement II's last viola note is E♭3 sul C. III's turn to C major can make the E♭→E♮ change in the violas, which closes the thread across all three movements.
4. **Items still owed by movement III.** These are the soft trumpet melody (critic 74; movement II's trumpets play only at 61–62 ff), the trumpets' B→C, the falling major motto in full brass, and the violins' C-major S head (§2 items 1–3). Movement II used muted brass once (Hn 3·4, 87–88), so a muted trumpet in III would be new.
5. **Proportions** (MIDI length): movement I v164 about 7:11, movement II v40 about 5:44. Movement III at 6 minutes or more (critic 30) keeps the cycle balanced.
