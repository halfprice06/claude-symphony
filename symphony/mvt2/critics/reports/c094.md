## Findings

### 1. Bars 63–64: violas' tremolo sits inside the trombone quotation, and the chord has an empty octave E4–E5 at the sf (applied)

**Problem.** Trombone 1 plays the movement I quotation in E3–C4. The violas hold C4–E4 tremolo sf>p, so the trombone's C4 on 63 b1.5 is a unison with the violas, and its B3, B♭3, A3 and G♯3 lie a semitone or tone under the violas' C4. Above the violas the next sound is Vln II E5–C6, so the A-minor sf chord has nothing between E4 and E5. This was open since c084 ("viola tremolo inside the trombone quotation 63–64").

**Evidence.** PDF 86: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages." PDF 85 and the example there: wide intervals at the bottom and close ones at the top. PDF 89: in an sfp chord with held upper notes "the balance of tone must still be maintained". PDF 88: "In the case of sustained chords or forte tremolando on two strings ... the intervals chosen being those which are the easiest to play."

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily, bVla):** `<e' c''>1:32\sf\> | <e' c''>2:32\p gis2(\> |` (was `<c' e'>`). The D string keeps F♭4 = E4 from 62 b3, and the G string's C♭4 rises to C5 on the A string. The strings now space as A1, A2, E4–C5, E5–C6, E6–A6, and the trombone has E3–C4 to itself. allpairs_par.py reports one new octave pair (Vla and Vln II top notes into 63 b1). That pair is the two double stops doubling each other an octave apart (E4–C5 / E5–C6), which PDF 82 allows as duplication. There are no new fifths. This does not touch the protected trombone line or c032's fix: the bass-E/A fifths came from an A4 in the violas, and there is no A4 here.

### 2. Bars 75–77: horn 2 and the cellos play the A′ counter-melody in unison with no woodwind binding them (applied)

**Problem.** From 75 the counter-melody E♭3–F3–A♭3–D♭4–B♭3–D♭4 is horn 2 + cellos only. Bassoon 1 joins at 78 b3. HISTORY's do-not-touch line (c087) already describes 75–82 as "Vc+Hn+Bsn counter-melody", but for the first three bars it is two families, brass and strings.

**Evidence.** PDF 51–52: the wood-wind "thickens the strings and softens the brass. ### 3. Bar 29: horns 3·4 hold an open fifth under the strings' melody (applied)

**Problem.** At 29 (V7 under the violins' G5–E♭5–D♭5–C5–B♭4) horn 4 holds E♭3 and horn 3 holds B♭3. That is an open fifth in the middle, and B♭3 is also the cellos' own note in unison. The 7th D♭ only passes through in the violins and arrives in the cellos on beat 3.

**Evidence.** PDF 97, remark 4: if the upper part goes to the strings, "there remains nothing for the wind except the sustained harmony in the two middle parts ... it is desirable to provide the intermediate parts with a moderately full tone, choosing no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths." PDF 85: "make sure that the harmonic notes are not lacking in the upper parts."

**Fix (applied, aOne.ily):** `aOneHnThree = { R2.*22 | des'2.\pp | c'2. | }`. Horn 3 holds the 7th D♭4 over horn 4's E♭3 (a seventh) and resolves it to C4 at 30 (a sixth over E♭3). The chord is now complete: E♭2, E♭3, B♭3 (Vc), D♭4, E♭4–G4 (Vla), melody. Horn 3 moves in sixths with the violins' B♭4–A♭4, the doubled leading tone that c052 removed does not come back, and the check finds no new parallels.
