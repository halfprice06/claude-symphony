## Findings

### 1. Bars 89–90: clarinet 1 leaves its C5 at pp with no diminuendo while Vln I takes it over (applied)

**Problem.** Clarinet 1's last tail lands on C5 at 89. Vln I enters on the same C5 pp at 89, and clarinet 1 stops dead at 90 beat 3 (`c''2) r4`). No hairpin is marked in either part.

**Evidence.** The hand-over itself is Rimsky's transfer (PDF 125–126): the last note of one part coincides with the first note of the next. But suppressing one timbre of a combined sound "belongs essentially to the diminuendo" (PDF 127). Without a hairpin, the clarinet's release is an audible cut in the quietest place of the movement, at the arrival of the plagal resolution. This item has been open since cycle 40 ("Cl 1 fade at 89–90, needs matching Cl 2"). Critic 040 tested a hairpin on clarinet 1 alone and reverted it because of the shared staff. The fix below gives both players the same hairpin over the same span, so the shared-staff rule holds.

**Fix (sections/coda.ily).**
```
codaClOne = { R2.*2 | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t \after 2 \pp es''2.(\<^\markup \italic "dolce" | d''2.) \breathe | des''2.(\pp | des''2. | c''2.~ | c''2)\> r4\! | R2. | R2.\fermata | }
codaClTwo = { R2.*4 | f'2.\pp | fes'2. \breathe | es'2.~ | es'2~\> es'4~\! | es'2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | es'2.\ppp\fermata | }
```
Both hairpins start at 90 beat 1 and end at beat 3, and one hairpin is printed. Clarinet 2 then continues its existing morendo to ppp. Pitches, the tail and the protected clarinet morendo are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 91: the harp's ppp chord enters during the closing diminuendo and repeats movement I's final scoring (applied)

**Problem.** At 91 every part has a hairpin to ppp, and the harp then attacks an arpeggiated chord, A♭1–E♭2–A♭2 | C4–E♭4–A♭4–C5. Apart from C4 and E♭2, every note is already sustained: Vln I C5, Vln II A♭4, Cl 2 E♭4, Vc A♭2, Cb A♭1. Movement I ends the same way: Vln I C5 ppp under a fermata, a timpani roll ppp, and a ppp harp chord in its last bar.

**Evidence.** Rimsky ties the arrival of a new group to the crescendo and the departure of one to the diminuendo (PDF 127). Here a new timbre arrives in the diminuendo. In the preface he lists using the harp "in every possible chord" as a beginner's phase (PDF 22). He also asks that repeated phrases of different character be scored to suit each (PDF 128). Movement I's ending is a C-minor tragedy turned to major; movement II's is a plagal consolation, and the two should not share their last scoring. The harp's own melodic moment, the harmonics head at 82–83 answered by the timpani at 84, stays, and the harp ends there.

**Fix (sections/coda.ily).** In `codaHarpR` and `codaHarpL`, the last two bars become `R2.*2 |`; this also removes the LH ppp and its direction overrides. The final page now shows clarinets, muted horns, timpani and strings only. The bass and the top line of the protected plagal close (87–92) are unchanged, and the middle register stays filled (PDF 125).

### 3. Bars 1–9: the harp's D♭1 scordatura has no instruction before the attacca (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 7 prints "(lowest D string tuned to D♭)" for the D♭1 at 9 b3. Movement I ends attacca into bar 1, so the harpist has no pause between movements and learns of the retuning only at the bar where it must already be done. Bars 1–6 (about 21 s at ♩=52) are the only time available.

**Evidence.** Axiom II: "orchestral writing should be easy to play"; and Glazunov's first class of scoring is music that sounds well at sight (PDF 21). A retuning that the part reveals too late fails that test at the first reading. Cycle 26 made the scordatura do-not-touch, so I keep it and move the instruction.

**Fix (sections/intro.ily).**
```
introHarpL = { R2.^\markup \small \italic "tune lowest D string to D♭ (for bar 9)" | R2.*5 | }
```
I also replaced the stale c040 comment above it, which still described a damping at bar 2 that no longer exists, so the composer does not restore the étouffez. Movement I's coordinator item is listed below.

## Not changed, with reasons

- **47, oboe/cello C♯ → cor anglais C♯4.** This is not a transfer in Rimsky's sense, because the quotation is a new phrase. Critic 044 separated the cello deliberately so that the quotation's first note is heard as an entry. Left.
- **33, cor anglais in thirds under the BCl tail.** It moves in the solo's rhythm, which weakens the detachment of the melody that PDF 19–20 asks for. But the cor anglais' B3 is the protected common-tone °7, and any other rhythm gives D♭4 against B3. Left.
- **62→63, A♭ (winds) → A♮ (bass).** This would be a false relation under PDF 126–127. The footnote on PDF 127 allows it when "extreme contrast of timbre between two adjacent chords is intended", and that is exactly the effect of the tutti collapsing to the trombone quotation. Left.
