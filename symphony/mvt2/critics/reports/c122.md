## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 43: the bass jumps an octave up out of the descending-5ths chain's lower stepwise line. Applied.
- **Problem.** Bar 42 is the chain vi7–ii7–V7–I (bass B♭1–E♭2–A♭1–D♭2), and it continues by another fifth to G♭ at 43, as bar 58 does into 59. The lower strand B♭1–A♭1 should go on to G♭1. In the score the double basses and the harp LH take G♭2 and A♭2 at 43, a seventh above the A♭1 they just played, and then drop back to A♭1 at 44. So the bass rises a fourth twice in a row (A♭1–D♭2–G♭2). The stepwise descent B♭–A♭–G♭ under theme B's peak (G♭5, 43) is lost in the register change. At the parallel spot (58–59) the composer keeps it low: B♭1–A♭1–G♭1–G1–A♭1, the line critic 032 singled out.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267, §6: the descending-5ths bass "forms two stepwise lines ... One of these two lines will usually predominate, with such factors as rhythm and register throwing it into relief". PDF 267, §4: the scalewise component "helps the sequence fulfill its basic function". Here register works against the predominant strand.
- **Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecCb`, bar 43: `ges,4 r4 aes,4 r4` → `ges,,4 r4 aes,,4 r4`. `bSecHarpL`, bar 43: `<ges, ges>4 r4 <aes, aes>4 r4` → `<ges,, ges,>4 r4 <aes,, aes,>4 r4`. The cadential 6/4 (43 b3) and V7 (44) now share A♭1, and the bass is B♭1–A♭1–G♭1–A♭1–A♭1, as at 58–60. Pitch classes, instruments, pizzicato and pedals are unchanged; G♭1 lies inside the harp's and the basses' ranges. Protection: the "pizz./harp support 37–44" and "B-section background" entries protect scoring, which is unchanged.
- **Note for the composer's check.** allpairs_par.py now reports P8 at quarter 132 (43 b1) between the basses or harp and the violas. This is a sampling artefact. Its eighth-note grid catches the middle note (D♭4) of the violas' last triplet in 42, but the violas' last note before 43 is F4, and their beat notes are A♭3 (42 b4) and G♭3 (43 b1), a fifth and then an octave above the bass. My onset scan finds nothing there.

### 2. Bar 81 b3: leading tone doubled on the beat before the goal tonic, and horn 4 leaves it unresolved. Applied.
- **Problem.** On 81 b3 (V7 before the full close at 82) the leading tone G sounds in clarinet 2 (G4) and in horn 4 (G3). Both are harmony voices, not melody doublings: the melody has already moved on to E♭–D♭–C. Clarinet 2 resolves G4→A♭4. Horn 4 holds G3 to the barline and stops, so one of the two leading tones of the movement's main cadence goes nowhere. Horn 4's G3 itself is right on b1, because it resolves the A♭3 seventh of the iiø7 at 80 b3. Only the hold through b3 is the problem.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267, §5 and the summary on PDF 286: a doubled leading tone is acceptable "if not just before a goal tonic". PDF 268: the last three chords of a fifths series (here B♭–E♭–A♭, 80 b3–82) form the cadence, the place where the voice leading is most exposed.
- **Fix (applied).** aTwo.ily, `aTwoHnFour`, bar 81: `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t g2.\>` → `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t g2\> es4`. G3 falls to the root E♭3 inside V7, and horns 3–4 still leave together at 82 for the mute. The niente hairpin, the V♭9 F♭4–E♭4 in horn 3 and the staged release 81–83 are unchanged. E♭3 arrives against horn 3's held E♭4 by oblique motion. No new parallels.

### 3. Bars 52→53: the violas hold the leading tone C4 through the dominant bar and stop at the D♭ arrival. Not applied.
- **Problem.** At 52 (A♭7, V of the returning D♭) the violas hold C4 for the whole bar with a crescendo and then rest from 53. The violins' C5 on 52 b4 is theme B's pickup and leaps to F5, which is thematic, as at 36. So the leading tone is doubled on the beat before the goal tonic (53, the start of B3), and neither C resolves in its own line. The harp's D♭4 on 53 b1.5 takes over the resolution in the same octave, a triplet eighth late. That is why this ranks below the other two.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267 and 286, as in Finding 2.
- **Fix (tested, not applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bar 53: `R1*4\! |` → `des'2\p r2 | R1*3 |`. This is clean (0 warnings, no parallels), but it puts the viola staff into the 53–56 system and the score grows to 9 pages, so I removed it. That anticipates the tonic, which A&S PDF 366 accepts, though the D♭4 sounds for a beat against the violins' C5. Otherwise leave it and rely on the harp.
