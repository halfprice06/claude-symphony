## Findings

This is the fifth pass of this reading under the playability lens. The score meets every principle that applies. I found no new defect that needs a change. The findings below record checks that a conductor or player might query, ranked by how much each matters, each with the fix the composer can use if he wants it. None was applied.

### 1. Timpani retune all four drums for the scherzo in the pause after 92, though they are free from 86. Recorded, no change (needs the scherzo thread).

Bars 84-92. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 48 takes for granted only that a timpanist with three drums "will be able to tune at least one of them during a pause of some length". At 92 the part asks for four changes at once: A♭→E (down a major third), C→G (down a fourth), D♭→B, E♭→E. The last strokes are at 84-85 (E♭3, C3, A♭2), and the drums are damped at 86. From 86 to 92 is about 27 s at the coda tempi plus the fermata. Two of the changes (C→G, D♭→B) involve drums not needed again in this movement, so they could be made during 86-91 while the strings hold ppp, which leaves only two drums for the pause. Fix, if the scherzo thread agrees about how long the pause is: in `codaTimp`, put `R2.^\markup \small \italic "muta C in G, D♭ in B"` at bar 87 (replacing the plain `R2.` of the `R2.*3` group) and shorten the 92 text to "muta A♭ in E, E♭ in E". This is the open point from c436, and the bar-92 muta text is protected, so I leave it to the composer.

### 2. Horn 2's first note is ff at 61 after 60 bars tacet. Recorded, no change.

Bar 61. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41: in forte "two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone", so a single horn at ff against trombones at f is right for balance (row 21). The playability question is the cold entry on D♭4 ff. The part has a Hn 1 cue at 57-60 (c466 style), and c024 and c426 recorded the entry. A horn player warms up quietly off stage before a movement where they wait this long, so this is normal practice. No change.

### 3. Bassoon 2 at 57-60 has no printed breath (15.5 s, p to f) before 8.9 s ff. Recorded, no change.

PDF 37: "care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time". c456 added the bass clarinet's breath at the end of 58 and left bassoon 2 without one on purpose, so the two do not breathe together (Sebesky PDF 42-43). The line has no slur, and a bassoon uses less air than a bass clarinet in this register. That reason holds. Fl 1 at 37-40 (16 s, slur break at 38) was recorded by c456 for the same reason and is protected.

### 4. Harp left-hand octaves change once a beat in the low register at 8, 11-12, 71-72 and 75-76. Recorded, no change.

PDF 46: in the low and middle octaves "the resonance of the strings is slightly prolonged", and the player damps by hand at changes of harmony, which fails only "in quick modulations". Here the bass changes at most once a beat at ♩=52 (1.15 s), within one key, and each new octave comes a step or a fifth from the last. That is time for the left hand to damp. The gap between the hands at 75-78 (E♭2 to A♭3) is left-hand octave doubling of the bass, which PDF 46 allows for the extreme notes.

### 5. Double basses "4 soli" at 65-70 and 87. Recorded, no change.

PDF 24 gives the medium orchestra 4-6 basses. In a four-bass section "4 soli" means the whole section, so the marking thins the sound only in larger orchestras. The 65-70 pedal is E♭2 pp on the A string, under a solo horn, and the timpani double it from 67. It balances even with all the basses. The scherzo has already been told about these soli ("Cb tutti pizz." at its bar 3). No change.

### 6. Upper limits: Fl 1 A♭6 held ff 61-62 and the violins' B♭6 at 79. Recorded, no change.

Table B (PDF 34, image) puts A♭6 in the flute's "clear, whistling" very-high register, which is usable. It is held 8.9 s after the breath at 60 and reached by step. PDF 26 allows a violin note above A6 only when it is long and approached by scale, never by skip. The B♭6 at 79 is 3.75 s, ff, after the run at 78 b3. Both points were closed earlier (c025, c446) and still meet the reading.
