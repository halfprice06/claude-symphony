## Findings

### 1. Bar 62 b3: give trombone 2 the third (A♭3), not the fifth (C♭4). APPLIED in /tmp/critic-224/work
**Problem.** On the F♭-major chord at 62 b3, the trombone section plus tuba sounds F♭1 (tuba), F♭2 (bass trombone) and C♭4 (trombone 2). That is root and fifth with no third. The only brass third is the horn pair's A♭4. Trombone 2 also moves B♭3→C♭4 in octaves with Hn 3·4's B♭4→C♭5, so the chord's fifth is doubled in the brass and its third is not.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 50 and Ex. 53 say thirds and dominant sevenths go in the lower-middle register "where they are most resonant, thereby exerting their influence over the entire ensemble voicing". PDF 50 and Ex. 54 call the fifth "the least important tone" that "can actually weaken the overall resonance" and mark a trombone fifth as a "weak tone". PDF 42 says each section should be complete in itself, and PDF 44 says low fifths reduce vibrancy. The F♭ chord is also the E-major chord that leads into A minor at 63. A♭3 = G♯3 is the leading tone of that move, and the violas already hold A♭3 under it (`<aes fes'>2:32`). c104's reason for the present line was Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 100, no empty space between the low brass and the horns. That reason still holds at the beat in question: the gap A♭3–A♭4 is filled by F♭4 in Vc, Bsn 1 and the violas, and the brass alone keep F♭2–A♭3–A♭4–C♭5, which has no gap larger than the low root octave. Rhythm, dynamics and instruments are unchanged, so the cycle 14/24 "61–62 brass layout" is kept; only one pitch moves.
**Fix** (bSec.ily, bSecTbnTwo):
```
  f2--\f aes2-- | bes2-- aes2-- | R1*2 | }
```
(was `bes2-- ces'2--`). Trombone 2 moves B♭3→A♭3 by step, in tenths with the bass trombone's G♭2→F♭2 and in contrary motion to both horn pairs.
**Test.** Build clean: build-warnings.txt empty, 8 pages, outer-voice parallels 0, analysis.txt otherwise identical to v224. allpairs_par.py: the only difference from v224 is that four P8 entries at 62 (Hn 3·4 against Tbn 2, B♭→C♭ octaves) are gone; nothing new. Parts book regenerated: 30 pages, each part on one page except the harp. Tbn 1 rests at 62, so the shared staff has no combining issue.

### 2. Bar 60 b1–2: the low brass enter on root and fifth only. Recorded, no change
Tuba A♭1, bass trombone A♭2, trombone 2 E♭3 (pp<), with Hn 4 A♭3. Sebesky PDF 44 (Ex. 50, last chords) warns against fifths in the low register. However, the seventh G♭3 enters on b3 in the lower-middle register (PDF 50), the passage is pp, and c132/c099 placed E♭3 on b1–2 for voice-leading reasons (no G♮–G♭ cross-relation after 59; no fifths into 61). The book does not outweigh those reasons. Leave it.

### 3. Bars 61–62: bass trombone and tuba double the low root in octaves. Recorded, no change
Sebesky PDF 47 points out that in Ex. 51 bars 7–8 "the bass trombone and tuba do not double the low roots", and PDF 52 leaves the root to the bass. In this score the bass trombone (D♭3, G♭2, F♭2) and tuba (D♭2, G♭1, F♭1) double each other at the octave. That is the standard late-Romantic layout (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 101: third trombone and tuba in octaves on the bass), and it gives the bass trombone its melodic line D♭–G♭–F♭ (principle 3). Sebesky's advice comes from a studio big band with an amplified bass. I would not change it. If the composer wants Sebesky's lighter version, the alternative is bass trombone `ges,2-- aes,2--` at 62, which puts the third in the bass trombone and gives G♯2→A2 into 63. But it makes octaves with Hn 1·2's G♭4→A♭4, so Finding 1 is the better way to get the third.

### 4. Horn pairs at 61–62. Follows; no change
The horns are two to a line against the trombones (PDF 46). The pairs sit above the trombones and overlap nothing (PDF 46, "maintaining their identity as a separate section"), and they rise while the bass falls (PDF 43). This confirms c194.

### 5. Soft horn doublings (53–56, 75–77, 87–90). Follows; no change
All three are unisons with one other line (Cl 1, Vc, Vla), never octave reinforcement, which PDF 42 calls "overpowering" in ballad writing. The horn entries come in on held notes and are slurred into the line, never on quick upbeats (PDF 46, horns "speak" late).

### 6. Trombone quotation 63–64 over the bass trombone's A2 sf>p. Follows; no change
The lower part decays from its first attack while trombone 1 enters ff after an eighth rest. The lower part does not exceed the upper (PDF 53). After the cluster-like f chord, a single line gives the contrast PDF 44 asks for.
