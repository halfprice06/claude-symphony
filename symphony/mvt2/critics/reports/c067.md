## Findings

### 1. Bars 15–22: cellos lock to the basses for the whole dialogue (applied)

Problem. In the b-phrase the oboe and solo violin alternate, the violas answer in each even bar (c017), and the cellos double the basses an octave higher in every bar. This is the passage where the score aims for chamber-like conversation, yet one of its four string voices only reinforces the root.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 134–136 (Ex. 188): good quartet writing is a "four-way conversation", and "the center of attention flows naturally from one player to another"; in the example the viola takes the line and then the cello does. PDF 140, rule 7: "Many arrangers make a habit of automatically doubling the bass notes with the lowest cello ... It can also 'cloud' the clarity of the bass line." PDF 146: the bottom cello plays roots "for temporary richness ... but not to reinforce the bass". The other side of the rule matters too: PDF 139, rule 3 and its footnote, the basses alone cannot sustain a line in live performance, and PDF 144 has the cellos divided except for an important counter line. This addresses c007's open item "varied lower-string rhythm in 15–22".

Fix (applied). At 18 and 22 the cellos divide: the upper half plays the viola's answer, the lower half stays on the bass with the basses, and the violas rest those two bars. The cellos' answer at 22 leads straight into their counter-melody at 23 ("unis., cantabile").

### 2. Bars 71–74: the lead octave is lighter than the octave below it (applied; reopens c059 for these four bars)

Problem. In the A′ reprise Vln I plays the theme alone in the upper octave. Vln II plays the lower octave on the G string, and since c059 clarinet 1 doubles it. The subordinate octave therefore has a string section plus a woodwind, and the lead has a string section only. Flute 1 joins the upper octave at 75, so from 75 the two octaves are matched.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 138, rule 2: "the lead violin line should be carried by more players than any other line." PDF 164: "one woodwind equals approximately sixteen violins". PDF 139, rule 4: the higher the line, the more the number of players shows. This is the long-open item "Vln I single octave 70–74". c059 gave Cl 1 the lower octave so that the reprise would be re-scored, after Brahms 1/ii. The reprise is already re-scored without it: Vln I is an octave higher, the violas have the eighth-note figure, and the horn and cello counter-melody is new. Cl 1 can keep c059's idea from 75 on, entering together with the flute's upper octave. Then both octaves gain a wind at the crescendo, and 71–74 keep the pure string sound Sebesky values above all (PDF 164, first paragraph).

Fix (applied). `aTwoClOne`: 71–74 `R2.*4 |`, and 75 becomes `aes'4.(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce" c''8 f''4~ | ...` (rest of the part unchanged). Clarinet 2's inner A♭4–G4 stays. Both clarinets are p at 75, so the shared staff has one dynamic.

### 3. Bars 71–74: horn 2 doubles the cello counter-melody from its first note (applied)

Problem. Horn 2 doubles the cellos' counter-melody at the unison from 71 at p. The horn+cello sound then returns at 79–82 (Hn 1·2, Vc, Bsn 1, f), and it was also used at 57–60. Because it is present from the first soft bar of A′, it is no longer a step up when the crescendo comes.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 166: "A French horn adds considerable body and thickness to a unison cello line ... It generates a good deal of emotional intensity and should be used sparingly." PDF 151 (Ex. 202): the passage "gains momentum with each bar" because colour is added step by step. At 23–26 the cellos carry the same counter-melody alone and are heard.

Fix (applied). `aTwoHnTwo`: 71–74 `R2.*4 |`, entering at 75 `es2(\mp\< f4 | ...` with the cellos' mp crescendo. The plan's "counter-melody in cellos and horns" still holds from 75. With findings 2 and 3, A′ is strings plus Cl 2 at 71–74, both winds and the horn arrive at 75, and the ff comes at 79.

### 4. Bars 37–40: the pizzicato bass is played three times (not applied)

Problem. Harp LH plays D♭2–D♭3 octaves (c026), the basses play D♭2 pizz. and the cellos play D♭3 pizz. Every cello note is already sounding in the harp at the same pitch and attack.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 140 (rule 7, grounding and clouding the bass); PDF 138 (economy); PDF 173: "in order to avoid clutter I had them tacet". PDF 133: the harp's job is to support string entrances. When the cellos enter arco at 41 (c037), that entrance would also be fresher after four bars of rest.

Fix. `bSecVc` 37–40: `R1*4 |`, moving "senza sord." to the arco entry at 41: `des1(\pp\<^"arco"^\markup \italic "senza sord." | ...`. The basses and harp keep the bass.

### 5. Bars 53–56: bassoon 2 doubles the cellos in unison over the basses (not applied)

Problem. The bass D♭–C | B♭–G♭ | E♭–D♭ | A–B♭ is played by Bsn 2, the cellos (same octave) and the basses. Above it there are only the harp figure, the horn/clarinet counter-line and the violins in unison. The B.cl. and Bsn 2 bass is useful at 57, the next stage of the build, but at 53 it comes too early.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 138 (economy), PDF 140 (bottom-rich sound used sparingly), PDF 151 (momentum added bar by bar). c049 already staged this build as harp at 53, violas at 57 and sextuplets at 59.

Fix. `bSecBsnTwo` 53–56: `R1*4 |`, with Bsn 2 entering at 57 with B.cl. as written (`\partCombineApart f2\mp\< ges2 | ...`). Bsn 1's p entry at 55 then stands alone on the staff.

### 6. Bars 63–64: cello tremolo inside the trombone quotation's register (not applied; see do-not-touch)

Problem. While trombone 1 plays the quotation E3–C4–B3–A3 | A3–G♯3–A3–F3–E3 at ff, the cellos tremolo on A3 and the violas on C4–E4, which are the quotation's own notes and register.

Evidence. Sebesky PDF 173: when the brass come to the foreground, the cellos "would only be in the way ... so, in order to avoid clutter, I had them tacet"; PDF 168: in a mixed tutti the strings should be voiced simply and high.

Fix. Keep the protected sf> tremolo (c058) and the violas' C4–E4 (c032). Move only the cellos down an octave at 63–64 b2: `a,1:32\sf\> | a,2:32\p d4( e4) |`. This is octaves with the basses (doubling, not parallels), and it clears the tenor register for the trombone. It is a small gain at sf>p and should be done only if the composer agrees the quotation is masked.
