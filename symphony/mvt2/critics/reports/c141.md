## Verdict on the tunes

Theme A is the tune a listener will hum. It has a rising arpeggio to a held fifth, the chromatic tail, a parallel period and a fourth-leap appoggiatura. It is heard three times in rising register and has one held B♭6 climax. Theme B is the weaker of the two, but its plan is the plan of Dvořák's middle tune (finding 2). The scan finds only one place where the background takes the melody's pitches for more than a passing beat: Vln II at 37–39.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 37–40: the second violins' held chord doubles theme B's antecedent an octave below (applied)

**Problem.** At 37–40 the flute plays theme B's first phrase p, C5–G♭5, which is the flute's weak register (c131). Vln II, div. sul tasto, holds two-note chords whose upper voice is F4 | G♭4 G♭4 | G♭4 G♭4 | G♭4 F4. On three of the phrase's four main notes it sounds the flute's pitch an octave lower for two beats: F at 37 b1, G♭ at 39 b1 (the phrase's peak) and E♭ at 39 b3. The scan reports exactly these, plus D♭ at 38 b1. A sustained string section on the solo's own notes, one octave down, blends the weak-register flute into the chord. In addition, the background's top line stays on one note for six half notes.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 212 (Ex. 280): the good background "touches down briefly on the solo melody notes", and "for the major part of the passage" it supplies "chordal tones not present in either the foreground solo line or the bass line". PDF 211 (Ex. 279): a background that shadows the soloist "distorts the aural focus". PDF 219: make up for the soloist's weak register; do not cover it. PDF 210 (Ex. 277/278): even in a soft background "your lead line should have some character"; Ex. 278 is the static version. PDF 207–208 (Ex. 271): a slow inner melody within the chord relieves held notes.

**Fix (applied), `sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecVlnTwo` 37–40:**
```
<des' aes'>1\pp\strMark "D" ... | <des' bes'~>2 <ges' bes'~>2 | <des' bes'~>2 <ges' bes'>2 | <es' ges'>2 <des' f'>2 |
```
The upper half now plays A♭4, then B♭4 held through 38–39, then G♭4–F4. These are chord tones of D♭, IV6/ii6, E♭m7 and Cø7 that the melody does not have on those beats. The lower half moves D♭4–G♭4 twice under the held B♭, which is Ex. 271's inner motion within the chord. The only doubling left is D♭ at 38 b1, where the melody moves on after one beat. Rhythm, dynamics, sul tasto and div. are unchanged. All pitches come from the viola arpeggio's chords.

**Answer to the c124 rule** ("no further accompaniment changes in 37–60"). The rule exists so that new figures and colour changes do not obscure the form. This change adds no figure, colour or rhythm. Like c137, it only moves pitches within the existing chord. c137's note that the chord is "a sustain of the viola arpeggio" still holds. It now sustains the arpeggio's A♭ and B♭ an octave up instead of its F and G♭ at the unison.

**Checks.** My first version had G♭4 on top at 38. That made parallel fifths with the flute (E♭5/A♭4 to D♭5/G♭4, 37 b4 to 38 b1), so I replaced it. I then checked by hand the final version against the flute, the viola figure, the bass and harp LH, and the cor anglais' F5 at 37. There are no parallel perfect intervals except one. allpairs_par.py newly reports Vln II–viola P8 at 38 b1. This is the held A♭4→B♭4 against the lowest notes of the viola triplets, A♭3 (37) and B♭3 (38), with D♭4 between them. It is the same sustain-of-the-arpeggio relation that v141 had at the unison (Vln II F4→G♭4 with the figure's F4→G♭4). I judge it not a voice-leading fault.

### 2. Theme B's narrow first phrase (37–40, 53–56): no change; the Dvořák model has the same design (recorded)

**Problem raised before.** c131 recorded that theme B's antecedent is narrow (C5–G♭5, a neighbour figure in every bar) and allowed a later attempt starting at bar 39. I tested a peak B♭5 at 39 (`bes''2 ges''4 des''4`) on paper. It gives the phrase a high point, but it anticipates the consequent's rise. It also makes octaves with the viola figure if the line passes E♭5→C5.

**Evidence for leaving it.** Dvořák 9/ii, flute 1, bars 46–53 (corpus, `woods.notesL.ily`): for six bars the tune turns inside a fourth with triplet neighbour figures (46–51). It breaks out only in bar 52, with a leap to G♯ and an accented C♯ an octave higher at 53. Theme B follows the same plan. The antecedent turns in triplet and eighth-note neighbours. The consequent rises A♭–D♭–F–G♭–A♭–B♭, an octave plus a step, at 41–42. At 57–58 the violins carry that rise to A♭6, and the ff arrival at 61 follows. Sebesky's question 6 (PDF 220) asks whether a tune is weak and how to make up for it. Here the weakness is the flute's register, not the line. Finding 1 deals with the register, and c051 had already given the consequent its best register and the ff arrival its colour. PDF 223: the weight belongs on the held arrival, not on a note that goes by. In this tune the arrival is 61, not 39.

**Recommendation.** Close c131's "later attempt starts at 39" note. The narrow, turning antecedent is the model's design, not a defect.

### 3. Bars 54–56: the horn/clarinet counter-line touches the violins' downbeat notes (checked, no change)

The scan reports Hn 1 and Cl 1 an octave below the theme on the downbeats of 54 (D♭), 55 (G♭) and 56 (C), one beat each. Each time the melody moves off the note within the beat (C–D♭, F–G♭–F, D♭–C), while the counter-line holds or rises against it. This is the brief touch-down of Ex. 280 (PDF 212), not the shadowing of Ex. 279. The alternatives I tried bring back the parallel sixths that c051 removed at 55–56, or put the counter-line in the same rhythm as the tune. Keep.

### 4. Bar 11: bassoon 1 re-strikes A♭3 inside the horn's held A♭3 (noted, no clean fix)

Bsn 1 plays `es4( aes4-- aes4-- | aes2--`. Its A♭3 on 11 b2 falls inside the horn's dotted-quarter A♭3. This is the kind of re-articulation c057 removed from the harp, and with the next beats it gives four soft-tongued A♭s in a row (Sebesky PDF 216–217, Ex. 282–283). I tested both ways out and neither works. If Bsn 1 holds E♭3 through b2, it doubles Bsn 2's E♭3. If Bsn 2 holds C3 instead, horn C4→F4 and Bsn 2 C3→F3 make parallel octaves on 11 b2.5–b3. The A♭ is a common tone held by a protected section at pp, so I leave it. Recorded so later critics need not retry it.

### 5. Theme A and the climax plan: at master level (no change)
