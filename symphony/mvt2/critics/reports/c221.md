## What else I studied

I compared the Largo of Dvořák 9 in the local corpus (`reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily` and `woods.notesL.ily`, bars 7–10). There the muted Vln I hold F4, the cor anglais's own home note, and the tune dips to E♭4 and D♭4 under it. So a master does let a held, muted common tone sit a step above a low cor anglais. What he does not do is put new plucked attacks a fourth or fifth above it, which is what bar 43 does (finding 1). I also checked HISTORY.md for every earlier ruling on bars 14, 41–44, 48–51, 53–56 and 61 (c019, c057, c108, c117, c192, c201, c202, c219) and kept to them.

On the lens question: both tunes are memorable in the way the models are. Theme A's held E♭ followed by the chromatic tail (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂) is a single, singable hook, as is the Largo's rising third and falling step. Theme B withholds its tonic until 61, so its last statement has a goal. No melodic line needs to change. The findings below are about whether the accompaniment lets each line be heard, which is what the reading is about.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 43 b3–44: the viola pizzicato rises above the cor anglais solo (applied)

**Problem.** In the second half of theme B's first statement the cor anglais plays in its low register, p, with flute 1 a tenth or more above it. From 43 b3 the viola triplets plucked A♭3–E♭4–A♭4 over the cor anglais's D♭4, then A♭3–C4–E♭4 over its B♭3, then G♭3–C4–E♭4 over its C4. For six triplets the accompaniment's top note was a third to a fifth above the solo. At 43 b4 the violas also sounded C4 a step above the cor anglais's B♭3, before the solo resolves to that C4 at 44.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139: sustained harmony is safe below the voice, while voices "suffer more ... when they come in contact with harmony in a register similar to their own". PDF 137 says the accompaniment must leave the voice free. c108 already applied the same rule to this figure at 41 b3–4. The rest of 41–44 now keeps the violas under the solo, so bar 43 was the one place left. Ex. 277 and Ex. 284 (PDF 444, 457) keep every accompanying string attack below the cantabile line. In Dvořák 9/ii (above) the string note above the cor anglais is a held common tone, not a new pluck.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-221/work, `sections/bSec.ily`, bSecVla bars 43–44):**
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes des' bes ges bes } \tuplet 3/2 4 { es8 aes es } \tuplet 3/2 4 { es8 ges aes } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { es8\pp ges aes ges es ges } r2 |
```
The cadential 6/4 now goes to V7 by the book's motions. The 8–7 (A♭–G♭) is in the violas, the 6–5 (F5–E♭5) in flute 1 alone, and the 4–3 (D♭–B♭–C) in the cor anglais. The violas still have no D♭ at 43 b3 (c192). The top viola note is A♭3, under the solo throughout. My first attempt used F3 at 43 b3. allpairs_par.py showed that F3–E♭3 moved in octaves with the flute's F5–E♭5, so I removed it. Rhythm, pizz., dynamics and the background plan (c107) are unchanged. All-pairs output is identical to v221's.

### 2. Bar 14: the harp chord covers the horn's final A♭3 (applied)

**Problem.** The horn solo ends on A♭3, held through the bar at p. On beat 2 the harp RH arpeggiates A♭3–C4–E♭4 and lets it ring. The solo's cadence note becomes an inner voice under a third and a fifth in the horn's own octave. c019 and c057 set the rule that no harp note sounds above the horn at its attack. That rule kept the harp under the horn at 7–13, and bar 14 only escaped it because the chord comes a beat after the attack.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139: harmony in the soloist's register covers it, and harmony below it does not. PDF 142: a soft culminating point is "better left unsupported" or held by light, transparent harmony (Ex. 282, PDF 450, where the harmony lies under the soprano). The horn is in its weak middle-low octave here, so this is the place where covering matters most.

**Fix (applied, `sections/aOne.ily` aOneHarpR bar 14, and `sections/bSec.ily` bSecHarpR):** `r4 <c es>2\arpeggio |` (bass clef, as the chords at 7, 9 and 11), and `\clef treble` moved to just before the harp's next RH entry at 53 (`R1*16 | \clef treble ...`). The chord is complete with the bassoons and bass clarinet. The treble clef now prints at the start of the 53 system, with no clef change inside a bar. The 8-page layout is unchanged.

**Analyzer note.** analysis.txt now lists one outer-voice "octave" at 14→15: A♭3→C5 over A♭1→C3. This is the horn's last note, which ends at the barline, followed by the oboe's entry 1½ octaves higher in a new phrase. It is not one voice moving, and allpairs_par.py, which compares real parts, reports nothing new. Before this change the ringing harp E♭4 hid the horn from the analyzer's skyline. If the composer treats any analyzer line as binding, keep finding 1 and drop this one.

### 3. Bar 41 b1–2: the viola pluck on the cor anglais's first two notes (no change)

The violas begin on A♭3 and D♭4 at the same moments the cor anglais attacks those pitches. I tried a lower voicing on paper (D♭3–F3–A♭3). It doubles the cello's D♭3 and sits a third above the bass F2 at pp, which is muddier than a short unison. Rimsky PDF 140–141 lists violas among the natural unison doublings of an upper voice, and a pluck that matches the solo's attack reinforces it rather than covering it. Leave it.

### 4. Bars 48–51: violins II above the quotation (no change)

Vln II sustains F♯5–A5–G5–E♯5–F♯5–D5–E♭5 a third to a sixth above the cor anglais's quotation of movement I. That is a sustained line (PDF 138, principle 6) close to the solo. But c054 moved this line up an octave, out of the cor anglais's own octave, which is what PDF 139 asks for. It is pp in half notes against an espressivo solo in quarters. The A5 at 49 is protected (c202). The off-beat violas below it match Ex. 284 exactly. No change.

### 5. Bars 53–56: horn 1 and clarinet 1 in unison under the violins' theme (no change)

PDF 138–139 names woodwind doubled with horns as a combination that covers a voice. Here, though, it is a melodic counter-line under strings that carry the theme in unison sul A, one dynamic level above it. That is the case PDF 139 allows: one group gives the melodic design and another the sustained support, each with its own part. The counter-line's rhythm was revised at c201 and is protected. No change.

### 6. Bars 65–70: double-bass pedal and timpani roll under horn 1's tail (no change)

These are two of the accompaniments PDF 138–139 warns against: a long double-bass note ("throbbing") and a timpani tremolo. Both are ppp or pp, near the rim, and the bass E♭1 is four octaves under the horn's E♭4. The passage is protected (retransition 65–70, c056 timpani), and c219 checked it against Adler's Ex. XVII-12. No change.

### 7. Bar 61: the theme's D♭6 arrives under flute 1's A♭6 (no change)

PDF 141 asks for a lyrical climax to be supported in the melody's register. At 61 b1 the long-withheld tonic D♭6 is in Vln I alone, with flute 1 a fifth above on A♭6. This follows the reading in the other respects: the trombones and tuba enter at the peak (PDF 141), and the theme's A♭–D♭ head returns in Fl 2, Cl 2 and Vln II, bringing D♭ into three octaves on beat 3. The A♭6–A6–B♭6 line (58, 61–63, 79) is protected (c181). No change.
