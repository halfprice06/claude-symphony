## Findings

All four findings are recorded only. I did not change the scratch copy, so there was no build to run.

### 1. Breathing: every long wind span has a breath (no change)

The span list gives eleven wind and brass stretches over 15 s, and each has a breath: Hn 1 6-14 (comma at 10), Hn 1 53-60 (56, 58, 60), B.cl. 7-14 (10), 31-37 (34), 57-62 (58, 60), Bsn 1 75-82 (77, 79), Bsn 2 7-12 (10), C.a. 47-53 (50), B.tbn 60-64 (60, 61, 62). These are MIDI spans, which ignore commas. The longest single slur is Hn 1 10 b3-14, 15.0 s at p-mp. Sebesky PDF 24 says a slur is "usually in one breath", and 15 s at mp is within a horn player's breath. The passage is protected (horn solo 7-14). The only held note without a breath is Cl 2's E♭4 at 89-92 into the fermata, at ppp, closed in c086. Nothing to add since c456.

### 2. 60-62: the low root in five instruments (no change)

At 60-62 the bass trombone and tuba double every root at the octave (A♭2/A♭1, D♭3/D♭2, G♭2/G♭1, F♭2/F♭1). Bassoon 2 and the bass clarinet join the bass trombone in unison, and the contrabassoon joins the tuba, with cellos and basses below. Sebesky asks for the opposite in his brass examples. PDF 47 says "the bass trombone and tuba do not double the low roots". PDF 88 (Ex. 112, note L) says "One or the other can handle it easily—not both". He wants a lean sound in which the arco bass carries the bottom (PDF 52). He is describing jazz brass, though, and the octave-doubled bass of a late-Romantic ff tutti is normal practice; Rimsky's tutti tables, cited by c529 and c564, support it. Here it is the single loud arrival of the movement. For playability, every player has a breath at the end of 60 and 61 and a cued entry, so the passage is safe. Recorded only. If 61-62 is reopened for balance, the cleanest change is the bass clarinet tacet at 61-62 (`R1*2` in `bSecBassCl` after bar 60). That would also free it from the brass at the dynamic where Sebesky (PDF 106) says the bass clarinet stands up to middle-register brass only "if not too loud". 61-62's brass layout is protected, so I did not test this.

### 3. 79-81: bassoon 1 in unison with horns 1·2 at ff (no change)

Bassoon 1 plays des'4 c'4 bes4 | aes2 bes4~ | bes4 des'2 in unison with horns 1·2 a2. Sebesky's chart (PDF 106) gives the bassoon "very limited projection—no edge—not recommended for use in combination with open brass". Next to two open horns at ff it adds almost nothing audible, and it sits between the brass rather than above or below them. Even so, it does no harm to playability. It gives the counter-melody a reed edge in the parts if the horns are covered, and "Vc+Hn+Bsn counter-melody 75-82" is protected (c087). Recorded only.

### 4. Contrabassoon B♭0 at 80 b3 (no change)

Since c184 the contrabassoon plays D♭1-B♭0-E♭1 at 79-81. B♭0 is the bottom note of the standard instrument. Sebesky's chapter gives no contrabassoon range (PDF 74 only places it an octave below the bassoon). At ff it is a quarter note approached by step from D♭1, and the player breathes after it. The written part (displayed an octave up) shows B♭1. It is playable on every modern contrabassoon. Recorded only.

### Ranges, mutes, endurance, part notation

Every wind and brass note lies inside the normal ranges of Sebesky's charts, checked against the images: flute B♭4-B♭6, oboe G4-B♭5, cor anglais A♭3-F5, clarinet E♭3-B♭5, bass clarinet B♭1-F♯3, bassoon E2-F4, horn E♭3-C♭5 concert, trombone E♭3-C4, bass trombone D2-D♭3, tuba E1-D♭2. Horns 3·4 have about 18 s (83-86) to fit straight mutes, and horn 1 rests before each solo. The horn parts are in treble clef a fifth up, the bass clarinet part is in treble clef in B♭ with the low-C ossia, and both bassoon parts stay in bass clef. This is Sebesky's notation (PDF 32, 64, 73). Timpani tuning and retuning (muta at 5 and 92) and harp pedalling are protected and outside this chapter.
