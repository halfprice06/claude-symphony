## Findings

### 1. The symphony's augmented sixths stand on the next movement's tonic (record; supports the existing plan)

**Observation.** A&S PDF 524 says the German-sixth/V7 equivalence can be "the most striking element" of a work's design, as Chopin's C♯/D♭ is in Op. 28/22. In this symphony each movement's augmented sixth stands on its own ♭6̂. Because the key plan falls by major thirds (C → A♭ → E → C), that ♭6̂ is always the next movement's tonic:

- Movement I: German sixth on A♭ at its bar 12 (Hn 3·4 F♯3, violas E♭4). A♭ is the key of movement II.
- Movement II: French sixth on F♭ at 21 b3, German sixth on E/F♭ at 64 b4. E is the key of the scherzo. The same E sounds as a major chord at 62 b3 and as E7 at 64 b4.
- Scherzo: French sixth C–E–F♯–A♯ at its 331, after the Neapolitan 6/4 (scherzo c332). C is the finale's key, and the scherzo's progression is the one in Ex. 29-41 (PDF 525).
- Finale: German sixth on A♭ at bars 1 and 261. That brings back movement I's chord and movement II's key.

Every join also follows Ex. 29-38 (PDF 523), with the new tonic heard as III♯ of the old one: C major is III♯ of A♭ (I → II, bar 1), A♭ is III♯ of E (II → III), and E is III♯ of C (III → IV).

**Consequence for this score.** The two augmented sixths at 21 and 64 should stay on F♭/E, and the movement should not gain a third one. This agrees with c312, who found that more would weaken the two that mark the form. The F♭ = E thread (5, 21, 62–65, 78, 80, 88) is now justified twice: within the movement, and as the preview of the scherzo's key.

**Fix.** None in the score. Composer: add one line to coherence/mvt2.md, "Key plan" notes: "II's augmented sixths stand on F♭ = E (21, 64), the scherzo's tonic, as movement I's stands on A♭ (I 12) and the scherzo's on C (III 331); keep them on F♭." Tell the scherzo and finale threads that this is a record only.

### 3. 62–65: the quotation's dominant becomes the German sixth, as in Schubert's "Einsamkeit" (record, no change)

**Observation.** A&S PDF 525–526 (Ex. 29-41): Schubert first writes the chord as V7, "as if the piece were going to gravitate to C major". Later the notation shows "that the C major is an impossible hope", and the music sinks to its real key. Bars 62–65 tell the same story. The E-major chord at 62 b3 leads to the trombone's A minor at 63, which is in an A-minor key signature (PDF 524–525, Note: an augmented sixth written as a dominant seventh for motivic reasons). E7 at 64 b4 promises A minor again, but by contraction (PDF 526, Points 1: D4 → D♭4 is the seventh) it sinks into E♭7 at 65, and the horn's motto tail follows over the E♭ pedal. Movement I's theme is shown once in a foreign key and then cannot stay there. That matches the arc in cyclic-notes: movement I's material is unresolved until the finale.

**Fix.** None. Keep the A-minor spelling at 63–64 (E, G♯, B in the key signature) and do not respell the chord as F♭–A♭–C♭–D. The E7 spelling is the "false hope" that the reading describes, and c312 found the spelling correct.

The A♭3 that falls to G3 is ♭6̂ of the chord's upper part, and nothing else is doubled. **Fix.** None.
