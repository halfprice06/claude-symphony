## Findings

All points are record-only. I propose no change to the score, so I did not build a scratch copy.

### 1. Bars 62 b1–2 and 79–81: horn notes inside the bassoon span (correction to c287; no change)

c287 recorded that "no horn note ever sits between two bassoon notes". My scan of v517 finds 16 half-beats where one does: 62 b1–2 (Bsn 2 G♭2, Bsn 1 F4 around Hn 2 D♭4 and Hn 1 G♭4), 79 b1–3½ (Bsn 2 D♭3, Bsn 1 D♭4–C4–B♭3 around Hn 4 A♭3), 80 b3–81 b2½ (around Hn 4 A♭3–G3). Rimsky's warning against horns inside bassoons (PDF 108) is about soft four-part writing, where the two colours must blend. He adds that in forte "the horns overwhelm the bassoons". All these places are f or ff, and Bsn 1 is in unison with a horn or cello line at each one (Hn 1·2 at 79–81, the F of Hn 1 an octave below Ob 1/Cl 1 at 62). The soft passages, 3–14, 67–70 and 87–90, are clean: no horn inside the bassoons, no bassoon above the top horn, no clarinet inside the horns. The layout follows the book. Only c287's wording should be corrected in the record.

### 2. Bars 61–62: horns 1·2 at ff, level with the woodwind (no change; recorded c284, c287)

Rimsky PDF 112 gives dynamic grading with the woodwind one degree above the brass, and the Dvořák chorale applies it at pp/ppp. At 61, Hn 1·2 are ff with the woodwind, while Hn 3·4, Tbn 2, B.tbn and tuba are f. c287 answered this with PDF 108–109: every horn pitch at 61 is doubled above by two woodwinds, which is the book's first remedy. Grading is offered "in difficult cases" only. 61 is the movement's climax and the trombone quotation follows at 63, so the stronger horns are deliberate. I do not reopen it.

### 3. Bars 3–6: the wind–horn chord is evenly marked pp (no change)

Hn 3·4 (C4/A♭3), Cl 2 (E♭3–F♭3–E♭3) and B.cl. (A♭2) are all pp. Under the Dvořák/Rimsky grading the clarinets would be p. That would push the moving F♭ forward, and c010 and c060 lightened this chord on purpose so that horn 3's C–D♭–C (the plagal preview, protected) stays on top. The chord already follows PDF 108's "stationary parts to the brass, moving parts to the woodwind" for every part except that protected neighbour. Keep it.

### 4. Bars 67–70: horn and bassoons share out concords and discords as Rimsky prescribes (no change)

68 b3: Hn 1 C4 (concord), Bsn 1 A♭3 (the dissonant 4th of the passing 6/4). 69: Hn 1 B♭3 (5th of V), Bsn 2 D♭3 (the 7th). This is the PDF 108 crossing rule ("concords to the horns, discords to the bassoons"). The only discords in the horn are the D♮–D♭ at 68 b1–2, which are the motto tail, the melody of the passage and protected.

### 5. Bars 57–58 and 79: string–woodwind doubling matches PDF 112 (no change)

At 57 Fl 1 doubles Vln I, Ob 1 doubles Vln II, and B.cl. and Bsn 2 double the cellos. At 79 Fl 1 doubles Vln I, Ob 1 and Cl 1 double Vln II, and the C.a. doubles the violas' B♭4. Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1 join the cello counter-melody (PDF 113's "horns doubled by ... 'cellos"). This is Rimsky's "Fl./Ob. + Vns div.; Clar./Fag. + 'Cellos + Violas" layout, and all three groups sit side by side for the full, round tone of PDF 113. No change.

### 6. Bars 87–90: Ex. 174 texture at pp (no change)

Muted horns 3·4, C.a. and Cl 1 over held strings, with the moving F–F♭–E♭ tripled (Hn 4, the violas' lower part, the C.a. an octave above). This is Rimsky's No. 174 (Christmas Night: "wind + brass c. sord. + strings") and the PDF 110 pairing of a covered horn with the C.a. It is protected (c447, c507) and correct.

Do not touch the brass and woodwind layouts at 3–6, 60–62, 67–70, 79–81 and 87–90, the cello/horn/bassoon counter-melody at 75–82, or the standing list and the 8-page layout.
