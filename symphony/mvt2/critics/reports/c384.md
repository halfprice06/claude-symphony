## Findings

### 1. Bar 28: violas f in the cello counter-melody's register (applied)

- **Problem.** At 28 b2 the divided violas reach f on D♭4-F4. The cellos' counter-melody is at f in the same octave (A♭3-D♭4: `aes2( des'4)`), and the violins have the tune at f two octaves up. All strings are con sord. here (c344), so the mute does nothing to separate the layers. With pitch and colour the same, only the dynamic can separate them. The basses are already mf. The violas are pure harmony at the same level as both melodic lines, and they play the cellos' D♭4 a beat before the cellos do.
- **Evidence.** "The fewer the instruments in a frequency range, the clearer and more present they become"; "sounds in the same frequency range mask each other" (Casanelles PDF 287). The louder, denser layer comes to the foreground (PDF 289). Sebesky PDF 164: an accompanying part sits one level under the line it supports. The score already does this with the violas under the cor anglais at 41-44 (c219).
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-384/work/sections/aOne.ily, aOneVla):**
  `<des' f'>4 <des' f'>4\f\> \unisHead f'4` becomes `<des' f'>4 <des' f'>4\mf\> \unisHead f'4`, with a comment. No pitch, slur or bowing changes, and the 23-30 accompaniment (protected, cycle 7) keeps its notes. The violas still crescendo from 27 b1 and arrive one level under the cellos, level with the basses.

### 2. Soft solos are cleared acoustically (recorded, no change)

Casanelles PDF 290 describes the one thing a live orchestra cannot do: raise a soft instrument above a louder one. The score clears the space for every soft solo:
- Hn 1 at 7-14: bassoons, bass clarinet and harp are pp and lie below it.
- B.cl. at 31-34: only the pedal and the cor anglais above it.
- C.a. at 41-44: the violas play pizz. a level lower.
- C.a. quotation at 47-52: the violas play off-beat sotto voce eighths.
- Harp harmonics at 83: cellos and basses at ppp.
- Timpani at 84-85: nothing else attacks.

On the compression point (PDF 290): the timpani rolls under the horn solos (1-3, 67-70, ppp near the rim) keep a constant level that a listener hears as present, so the score keeps them at ppp. At the coda's answer (84-85) the timpani play single strokes, the decaying, less present form, which suits a closing echo. Keep.

### 3. Brass density ranks the layers correctly at 61-64 and 79-81 (recorded, no change)

PDF 289 says forte brass is the densest sound in the orchestra and will come forward. The score uses this deliberately:
- At 63-64, Tbn 1's quotation is the only ff. The strings fall from sf to p under it. The violas' tremolo (E4/C5) sits above the trombone's E3-C4, and the cellos/B.tbn A2 below it. That is the "assign each instrument its range" approach of PDF 286-287 done with pitch.
- At 61-62 the brass chord is in close position under the ff winds and strings, with the horns graded ff/f (c264).
- At 79-81 horns 1·2 are f, not ff, on the counter-melody (c034). This keeps the densest instruments one level under the B♭ octaves.

On the flute-and-brass masking example (PDF 287): at 57-60 and 77-78 the flute's line is three octaves above the horns, and Vln I doubles it, so the horns' overtones do not reach it. No change. This confirms c174 and c334.

### 4. Theme A's colours change at each return (recorded, no change)

The *Ex Machina* example (PDF 289) changes a theme's timbre on each statement so that its meaning changes. Theme A goes:
1. solo horn with dark winds (7);
2. muted violins with the cello counter-melody (23);
3. open violins in octaves with flute, oboe and clarinet at the peak (71-82);
4. high harp harmonics (83);
5. muted horns and cor anglais echoing 61-62's F-F♭ (87-90).

Each step either moves further away or toward the full sound, and none repeats an earlier colour. Keep this order (do-not-touch from c174).
