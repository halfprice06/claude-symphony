## Findings

### 1. Harp 78 b3: the f is printed on the arpeggio line (applied)

- **Bars:** 78, harp (score page 6, lower system).
- **Problem:** score.ly sets `connectArpeggios = ##t` on the harp's PianoStaff and prints the harp's single set of dynamics above the LH staff, between the two staves (`DynamicText.direction = #UP`). At 78 b3 both hands strike a chord, so the arpeggio is one wavy line from the RH chord down to the LH chord, and it passes through the space where the f sits. At 300 dpi the wavy line runs through the left half of the f. At every other harp dynamic (7, 71, 75, 77, 82) the RH has a rest on that beat, so the arpeggio covers only the LH and nothing collides; 78 b3 is the only place in the movement where it happens.
- **Evidence:** Gould says an octave sign "must never cut through other symbols, as this is visually confusing" (PDF 43), and states the general rule at PDF 55: "no characters should collide". This is the harp's f at the second climax's approach, the one dynamic of the bar, so it must be readable at a glance. The 8 bassa line below the LH has to stay outside the dynamics (PDF 43), so the f cannot move under the staff.
- **Fix, sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpL (bar 78); score only, the part keeps its f below the staff, where nothing collides:**
  ```
  \tag #'part \ottava #0 r2 \tag #'part \ottava #-1 <es,, es,>4-\tag #'score -\tweak extra-offset #'(2.9 . 0) \f -\tag #'part \f \arpeggio \ottava #0 |
  ```
  A shift of 1.2 spaces moves the f off the arpeggio and onto the LH stem, so 2.9 is needed to clear both. The f then sits right of the stem, between the staves, at the same height as the other harp dynamics.
- **Result in /tmp/critic-525/work:** at 60 dpi, pages 1-5, 7 and 8 are pixel-identical to v525. Page 6 differs only in a 16 x 9 pixel box around the f. The harp part page is pixel-identical to v525's, and analysis.txt is byte-identical (the MIDI does not change).
