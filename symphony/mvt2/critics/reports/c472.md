## Findings

### 1. Bar 39 b4: Violins II hold C4 under flute 1's passing D♭5, the same fault c332 removed at 55 (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 39 is the first statement of theme B. Over the C ø7 at 39 b3–4 the flute plays E♭5–D♭5–C5 (D♭5 on b4, C5 at 40 b1): an accented passing minor 9th above the chord root, resolving to C. Since c402 the lower half of Violins II holds C4 for both beats (`<c' ges'>2`), so the 9th's own tone of resolution sounds a minor 9th below it, in a voice that is not the bass, for the whole beat. A&S PDF 469–470 (and Unit 21 §12): a suspension or passing dissonance works best when its tone of resolution is not sounding in another voice above the bass; with the two together "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". Ex. 27-10 shows that only wide spacing saves such a voicing, and C4–D♭5 is not wide. The texture here is thin: pp, strings sul tasto, solo flute. That makes the clash easier to hear than at 55, which is fuller. Also, at 39 b4 the pizzicato bass C2 has stopped (Cb and harp play quarter notes on b3 only), so the held C4 is the lowest sustained note and the 9th is heard against it directly.

c332 (Finding 1) moved the harp at 55 b4 for exactly this reason. That report noted that 39 b4 was then clean ("the violas play E♭4–B♭3–E♭4 with no inner C"). c402 brought the C4 back at 39 so that the lower half's D♭4 (the 7th of ii7 at 39 b1–2) resolves down to C. That reason is good, but the resolution needs only beat 3.

**Evidence.** Reduction of v472 at 39 b4: C4 (Vln II, held from b3), E♭4 (Vla), G♭4 (Vln II), D♭5 (Fl 1). The scan finds no other place in 37–60 where a melodic passing 9th is accompanied for a full beat by its resolution tone in an inner voice. 55 b4 is now clean, and the brief triplet C4 at 40 b2⅓ is covered in Finding 3.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, Vln II bar 39):**
```
| <des' bes'>2 <c' ges'~>4 <es' ges'>4 | <es' ges'>2 <des' f'>2 |
```
(was `<des' bes'>2 <c' ges'>2 |`). The lower half plays D♭4–C4–E♭4. C4 still resolves c402's 7th on b3. On b4 the lower half moves to E♭4, the chord's 3rd, which it holds anyway at 40 b1. The upper half's G♭4 is tied, so it is not re-struck. Result at 39 b4: E♭4, E♭4 (Vla), G♭4, D♭5. The flute's passing 9th goes to C5 at 40 as a new pitch, the same voicing c332 made at 55. B♭ still sounds once (viola), as c142 required. This changes pitches only, using notes from the viola arpeggio's chord (c124 rule).

### 2. Bar 74 b1: the cellos' new 9–8 suspension sits a semitone under the 7th (record, follows)

c469's suspension holds C4 over the V4/3's bass B♭1 and resolves to B♭3 on b2. Relative to the bass it is a 9–8 inside V, which is Ex. 27-5's case (PDF 465–466: think of it as an ordinary suspension). Relative to the root E♭ it is a 6th that resolves to the 5th (Ex. 27-16b). This is a *resolved* 6th, so the "above the 7th" rule on PDF 475 does not apply; that rule is for unresolved 4ths and 6ths. Its tone of resolution B♭ sounds only in the bass doublings (Cb B♭1, Bsn 2/harp B♭2) and not in any upper voice, so the PDF 469 condition holds. The minor 2nd C4–D♭4 against Cl 2 and violas is the ordinary sound of a suspension under a 7th. Keep it. The difference between 26 and 74 is deliberate (c469).

### 3. Bar 40 b2⅓ and 56 b2: inner C4 under the neighbour D♭5 (record)

At 40 b2 the violas' triplet E♭4–C4 puts C4 against the flute's neighbour D♭5 for one triplet eighth (a sixth of a beat). The C4 is unaccented and comes after the D♭ is already sounding, so the neighbour does not sound together with its resolution. At 56 b2 Cl 1/Hn 1 hold C4 under the violins' D♭5 eighth (c051), which c332 recorded with the same reasoning (Ex. 27-5: a neighbour 9th against its resolution tone in a lower register). Neither has the weight of 39 b4: in both the dissonance lasts an eighth or less, and at 56 it is covered by a tutti. No change.

### 4. Bar 81: the mixture ♭9 (record, follows)

At 81 b1 F♭ sounds in four octaves (Hn 3·4/Vla E4, Ob E5, Fl E6) over E♭7. It is held as a common tone from ii°7 at 80 b3 (Ex. 27-4c) and falls to E♭ in every voice on b2 (Ex. 27-5, 9–8 inside V). The 7th D♭5 (Cl 2) resolves at 82. No E♭ sounds above the bass at b1, so the ♭9 is heard clearly (PDF 469). This is the textbook case of Ex. 27-3. Protected.

### 5. Bar 72 b2: Cl A♭4 (7th of V7/V) leaves upward (record, follows)

c442's held A♭4 becomes the 7th of B♭7 and then goes up to B♭4. PDF 466 says unresolved 7ths are "most infrequent", but Ex. 27-6a shows a 7th leaving its line and resolving in another part. Here the violas' A♭4 falls to G4 at 72 b3½, in the same register. That satisfies the book. No change.

### 6. Pedals at 31–36, 65–70, 83–92 (record; Ex. 27-13 option not recommended)

Ex. 27-13 colours a tonic over a pedal as V⁹ of IV. Earlier critics rejected a seventh on the A♭ chord at 31–36 three times (c020, c203, c233), and the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C over the coda pedal (85–89) is protected. The E♭ pedal at 65–70 carries a full V7 whose 7th passes from violins to horn (Ex. 27-6a). The coda's F♭ at 88 answers the 81 ♭9 with a stepwise F–F♭–E♭ descent (C.a. 87–89), which is the compensation PDF 467 describes. No change.
