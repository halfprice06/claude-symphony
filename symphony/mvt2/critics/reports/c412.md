## Findings (ranked)

None of these needs a change. They are ranked by how close each one comes to a fault under the reading. The two earlier Unit 17 reports in this series (c122, c282) fixed what Unit 17 could fix. This pass re-checked the parts of the reading those critics did not test.

### 1. Bars 26–29 and 75–78: the cello counter-melody makes fifths with the bass on four successive downbeats. Checked, follows, no change.
- **What the scan shows.** Downbeats, counter-melody over bass: 26 B♭3/E♭, 27 E♭3/A♭, 28 A♭3/D♭, 29 B♭3/E♭. In A′: 75 E♭3/A♭1, 76 A♭3/D♭2, 77 B♭3/E♭2, 78 C♭4/F♭2. Horn 2 and bassoon 1 double the cellos, and bassoon 2 doubles the bass (aTwo.ily), so in A′ each fifth sounds in two pairs.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267, §5: with repeated patterns "5ths and octaves can become a problem. Therefore, voice-leading chords play an essential role"; in Ex. 17-1, sequence A, "the chord on the fourth eighth note prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". Ex. 17-1 (PDF 263) shows the strong beats half a bar apart, with one intervening chord.
- **Why it follows.** Each pair of downbeats has a beat-3 tone in the cello line that is not a fifth over the bass: 26 b3 G3 over E♭ (c032's fix), 27/75 b3 F3 over D♭ (a third), 28/76 b3 D♭4 over B♭ (a third), 77 b3 D♭4 over E♭ (a seventh). From 77 b3 into 78 the two voices move in contrary motion (D♭4→C♭4 against E♭2→F♭2). The downbeats are a full Adagio bar apart (about 3.5 s at ♩=52). The counter-melody's shape, a long note a fifth over the bass and a moving quarter on beat 3, is the voice-leading tone the reading asks for. I record this so later critics do not have to test it again. No fix.

### 2. Bars 53–56 against 37–40: two forms of the falling-thirds sequence, and the break-off into the leading tone A2 at 56. Follows, no change.
- **Observation.** At 37–40 every chord is in root position (D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A–B♭), the root-position variant (A&S PDF 279, Ex. 17-18; Ex. 17-19 for Bach's neighbour-note decoration, which the flute's D♭–C–D♭ at 38 matches). At 53 the cellos put C3 under the second chord (V6/5 at 53 b3), which is the basic stepwise 5-6 form of Ex. 17-2d and 17-16 (PDF 266–267, 277). So the second statement of theme B is a varied setting of the first, within the same sequence type. That is the variation PDF 264 allows ("sometimes with slight variations").
- **Register.** In 37–40 the basses fall C2→A1→B♭1. In 53–56 the cellos carry the bass alone (basses tacet, c164) and cannot go below C2, so the line rises C2→A2 at 55 b3–56 b1. PDF 268: register "throws one line into relief". Here the rising sixth into the applied leading tone A2 separates the goal pair A–B♭ from the falling chain, so the break-off at 56 is audible. That is the right place for it: the third unit is where the reading expects the sequence to stop (PDF 265). No fix. Moving the bass back to A1 would need the basses at 56, which c164's staged build keeps out until 57.

### 3. Bars 17→21: a melodic sequence over a non-sequential bass. Follows, no change.
- **Observation.** The violin's A♭5–B♭5–G5–F5 | E♭5–D♭5 (17–18, over vi–IV | I6–ii) returns a third lower as F5–G5–E♭5–D5 | E♭5 (21–22). The harmony of the second statement is IV–I6/4–Fr+6 | V, not the model's chords a third lower.
- **Reading.** PDF 265: a Fortspinnung ends in a cadential progression. PDF 281–282: in a sequence leading to a tonicized V, ♯4 appears early. That is the D♮ at 21 b3. PDF 279: the break-off of a pattern need not coincide with a change of rhythm. Here the melody keeps its rhythm while the harmony turns cadential. Exercise note PDF 287 ("preserve the sequence in your harmonization") concerns a sequence the given line implies through its whole course. The 21 statement is the phrase's last two bars, where the reading puts the cadential progression. I checked 21 beat by beat (D♭–E♭–F♭–E♭ bass, upper voices F–E♭–D–E♭, A♭–A♭–A♭–G, F–C–B♭–B♭): no 5ths or 8ves, the French sixth resolves by step. No fix.

### 4. Bar 58: a descending-fifths chain decorated by suspensions, continuing past I to IV. Follows, no change.
- **Observation.** B♭ (vi7) – E♭ (ii7) – A♭ (V7) – D♭ (I7 = V7/IV) → G♭ (59). Over it the theme's A♭6 is a 7th over B♭, a 4–3 suspension on ii7 (A♭→G♭ at b2.5), then G♭ is the 7th of V7 and a 4–3 suspension over D♭ (G♭→F at b4.5). At 58 b3 the leading tone C is in clarinet 1 (C5→D♭5) and the violas (C4→A♭3).
- **Reading.** PDF 270: "Suspensions frequently decorate sequences". PDF 267 and 286 (2a): a doubled leading tone in a sequence is acceptable unless a goal tonic follows. The D♭ at 58 b4 is not the goal: it carries the seventh C♭ (Cl 2, Vla) and leads on to G♭ at 59, as Mozart 41/ii does at bars 32–33 and 35–36. The goal tonic is 61, after IV–vii°7/V–V. No fix.

### 5. Bar 60→61: the leading tone C in five parts before the goal tonic of the B section. Recorded by c282, not re-raised, no change.
At 60 the theme's last note C sounds in flute 1 and Vln I (C6), oboe 1, clarinet 1 and Vln II (C5) and horn 4 (C4). Only Vln I goes to D♭6 at 61; flute 1 rises to A♭6, the B-section ceiling that resolves to A6 at 63 (c070, c110). PDF 267's doubled leading tone comes from keeping chord voices sequential. Here the doubling is the melody in octaves, the normal orchestral practice c282 accepted ("60 melody octaves only"). The climax layout 61–62 is protected (c404). I tested no change.

### 6. Minor-key sequence rules (PDF 283–286). Not applicable.
B♭ minor (45–46), F♯ minor (48–50) and A minor (63–64) are reached by single applied or enharmonic dominants, not by sequences, so the warnings about II° and the approach to the leading tone in rising minor sequences do not arise. c393's reasons for keeping sequences out of 53–60 still hold, and nothing in Unit 17 answers them: the reading presents sequences as connective tissue between stable areas (PDF 265), and D♭ is confirmed at 61 on purpose.
