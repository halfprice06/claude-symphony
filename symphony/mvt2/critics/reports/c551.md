## Findings

Every finding is a check that needs no change. v551 meets each principle of the chapter that applies to it.

### 1. Theme A's three statements follow the chapter's string colours; the reprise is set as Tchaikovsky's model allows (recorded)
- **Bars:** 7-14 (horn), 22 b3-30 (violins in unison), 70 b3-82 (violins in octaves).
- **Reading:** Ex. III-5 (PDF 74) is the movement whose plan this one shares: Tchaikovsky 5/ii gives its tune first to the horn, then to the violins, and the passionate return is sul G, mf with sf. Adler uses it to show the G string's intensity in high positions.
- **Score:** The first string statement is sul D at p-mf: the warm, subdued colour of Ex. III-6 (PDF 75). It goes "ord." at 27 for the f phrase. At the reprise, Vln I has the theme on the E string at p ("clear and luminous", PDF 75), with Vln II an octave lower sul D. A sul G reprise is not available here. Vln II's E♭5 at 71 would lie twenty semitones above the open G, past the 7th position that PDF 72 gives as the limit of secure section playing. Vln I lies an octave higher still. The intensity Tchaikovsky gets from the G string comes here from the register climb instead: A♭3 (horn), A♭4 (violins), then A♭5 with the B♭6 peak.
- **Memorability:** Theme A has a rising arpeggio to a syncopated held note, the chromatic tail E♭-D-D♭-C, and a B♭ appoggiatura on a weak beat. Each statement is in a new register and colour, and the head returns in the B.cl. (31), the horn counter-line (53-54) and the harp (83). This is the tune a listener will carry away.
- **Fix:** none.

### 2. The register drop at 74 and the climb 75-79 are not hard skips in Adler's sense (recorded)
- **Bars:** 74 b1 (Vln I G5) → 74 b3 (E♭4 pickup); 75-79 (A♭4 to B♭6).
- **Reading:** PDF 82-83 lists three hard skips for a section: fast skips on one string, skips between extreme registers, and wide legato skips.
- **Score:** Vln I falls from G5 to E♭4 between the two phrases of the reprise, and joins Vln II in unison for 75-77 b1. The skip is at ♩=52, with a new slur and a change of string, so it is a phrase start in a lower register, not a legato skip. The second phrase then climbs about two octaves in four bars: A♭4-C5-F5 (75), B♭5 (76), G5-E♭6 (77, a minor sixth inside a slur on the E string, mp<), the run C6-F6-G6-A♭6 (78 b3) and B♭6 (79). Every large step in the climb is slow and soft. The one note above the 7th position, B♭6, is approached by step and doubled by flute 1 (PDF 72). The drop is what gives the climb its length. The register plan is protected (c299, c309, c341) and c547's doubling at 75-76 was declined.
- **Fix:** none.

### 3. Theme B's string statement uses the chapter's own model for a soft A-string tune (recorded, briefly)
- **Bars:** 52 b4-56 (violins sul A, cantabile), 57-60 ("ord.").
- **Reading:** Ex. III-7 (PDF 75): Brahms 3/iii, sul A, p espress., a narrow tune built on a repeated one-bar cell with a hairpin in nearly every bar.
- **Score:** Theme B's antecedent (C5-G♭5) stays on the A string with swells at 53 and 55, as in the Brahms. At 57 the E string ("ord.") takes the consequent up to A♭6 at f. The colour change comes where the tune opens its range. c411 recorded this in detail and nothing has changed since.
- **Fix:** none.

### 4. The solo violin at 17-22 and the solo strings of the Dvořák Largo (recorded)
- **Bars:** 17-18, 21-22 (solo violin, senza sord., dolce, p).
- **Reading:** PDF 81: one solo violin is the concertmaster. Ex. III-20 (Brahms 1/ii) gives the solo violin a long line above the oboe and horn near the end of a slow movement.
- **Corpus:** In the reprise of Dvořák's Largo (strings.notesL.ily lines 110-131), the first violins thin from "4 violins" to "2 violins con sordini" and then to "1 violin". The single player carries the head of the main tune (`f8. af16 af4 f8. ef16 df4`, line 121) before the tutti returns. This score uses its solo violin earlier, in theme A's contrasting b phrase. There it answers oboe 1 in two-bar units (A♭5-B♭5-G5-F5 | E♭5-D♭5; F5-G5-E♭5-D5 | E♭5), and the section takes the theme at 22 b3. In the coda, the head goes to the harp's harmonics (83). That is a protected cyclic rule (melodic moments for the harp), and the coda is protected as well (clarinet morendo and plagal close 87-92). A Dvořák-style solo-violin statement of the head in the coda would break both, for a gain in colour, not in memorability. The short answers at 17-22 keep theme A as the only long tune of the A section, which helps memorability.
- **Fix:** none. Marking the solo "sul A" (Ex. III-7) is possible, but PDF 73 leaves the string to the player unless timbre requires it, and a concertmaster chooses this himself.

### 5. Cellos, violas and open strings (recorded)
- **Cellos (PDF 99, 103):** Adler names two settings where the cello's beauty shows best: section solos and a voice in the counterpoint. The score uses the second throughout. The counter-melody at 23-30 and 71-77 is sul D, and moves to the A string only for the climb into 79 (c386). The cellos double oboe 1 on theme B's B♭-minor head at 44-47, sul D. They are coupled with horn 1 at 57-60 and with bassoon and horns at 78-81, two of the couplings on PDF 103. They never play theme A as a section solo. c074 closed that point (a fourth statement would be needed and the counter-melody would be lost), and nothing in this chapter answers that reason.
- **Violas (PDF 87, 89-91):** The violas' figures lie on the G and D strings, the "accompaniment strings". They use the C string for 64-65 and 87-92. At 79-81 they take theme A's third octave with the cor anglais, which is Ex. III-38's octave coupling. The viola-cello coupling of Ex. III-39 is not used for a theme. Using it at 23-30 would change the protected first-statement accompaniment.
- **Open strings (PDF 73-74):** The open-string scan of v551 finds every held open-string pitch covered by a sul mark, in a tutti, or in the basses' pizzicato or soli pedals. The c411 fix at 51-52 (Vc sul G) is still in place. The short hits are viola G3 eighths in the accompaniment figure at 74 b1.5, 77 and 78 b3.5, viola D4 at 50 and cello A3 at 46. These are inner accompaniment notes, not melody, so per PDF 73 the fingering is the player's choice.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Basses (recorded)
- **Reading:** PDF 105-108.
- **Score:** The "4 soli" basses play under the horn's motto tail (65-70) and in the coda (83-92), where the thin, distant sound Adler describes is the one wanted. No fast passages double the cellos, there are no double stops, the lowest note is E1 (no five-string notes), and every long note carries "change bow at will".
- **Fix:** none.
