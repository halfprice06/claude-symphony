## Findings

### 1. The build at 53–60 repeats B1's harmony, and bar 58 reaches the tonic too early (applied)

**Bars:** 53–60, mainly 58.

**Problem.** B3 is B1 (37–44) an octave higher and louder. The bass is identical bar for bar (des des | bes aes | es des | a bes | f ges | bes es aes des | ges … | aes) except for G♮ at 59. Dynamics, register and doublings do all the intensifying. On beat 4 of bar 58 the harmony is a root-position D♭ triad with F (3̂) on top in Vln I. That is a full tonic arrival 2½ bars before the cadence at 60–61, the only authentic cadence in D♭ in the whole middle section. In B1 (bar 42) the same beat does no harm, because the phrase goes on to a half close. In the build it releases tension just before the goal.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 444–445: "applied chords can help to intensify a modulation" (Ex. 26-3, an applied °7 before the pivot), and sequences of applied V's appear "frequently as modulatory transitions". A&S PDF 445–446: "for a strong sense of arrival", the melody should reach the new tonic with rhythmic resolution (Ex. 26-4). A tonic on a weak beat just before that point weakens it. A&S PDF 443: the cadence that confirms the key is built on II or IV. Making 58 b4 an applied V7 of IV pushes into the IV of bar 59 and so into the cadence.

**Fix (applied).** Add C♭ on beat 4 of bar 58, so D♭ becomes D♭7 (V7/IV). The C♭ resolves to B♭ in bar 59 in both voices.

```
% bSec.ily, bSecClTwo, bar 58
des'2\mp\< des'2 | aes4 ges4 aes4 ces'4 | bes2\f des'2 | c'1\< |
% bSec.ily, bSecVla, bar 58 (second statement only; bar 42 unchanged)
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( des' f') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8( c' es') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( ces' f') } |
```

Side effect: allpairs_par.py no longer reports v13's parallel fifth between Cl 2 and Hn 2 across 58→59 (A♭→B♭ against D♭→E♭).

**Further option (untested).** For a stronger build, the composer could also make 57 a short descending-3rds chain of applied chords (A&S Ex. 26-6, PDF 447), keeping the melody. I have not tested that.

### 2. The coda is longer than the reprise, and 83–89 is a static tonic triad (applied)

**Bars:** 83–96 (v13).

**Problem.** A′ lasts 12 bars (71–82, about 45 s). The coda lasts 14 bars (about 50 s with the rit. and fermata). In 83–89 the strings hold one A♭ triad unchanged (Vla C/E♭, Vc/Cb A♭) for seven bars. Over it the harp-head/timpani-tail exchange is stated twice: 83–86 and 87–90. The timpani part (E♭–D♭–C | A♭) is identical both times; the harp's second turn is only an arpeggio up to C6. After that, Vln I plays the motto tail into the plagal close at 89–93. So the coda states the tail three times (85, 89 timpani; 89–92 violins). Earlier critics had already flagged overuse of the tail (cycles 1 and 11, still open).

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 448–449 (§7): the large-scale plan must show "purposeful motion over large as well as small musical spans". A&S PDF 456 (§16 and footnote): this movement's large-scale plan is I–IV–V–I with IV as the middle section. The coda's job is the small-scale I–IV–I (91–96), and seven bars of unchanged I before it add length but no motion. Corpus: Dvořák 9/ii closes in about 7 bars after a reprise of about 20 (see above). In v13 the coda is longer than the reprise it follows.

**Fix (applied).** Cut v13 bars 85–88: the first timpani answer and the harp's second arpeggio. One exchange is left: the harp head at 83–84, then at 85–86 the timpani tail together with Vln I's E♭–D, which run straight into the plagal close. The coda becomes 10 bars, 83–92, and the movement 92 bars (MIDI 344 s instead of 357 s). Everything protected stays as written: the harp and timpani melodic moments, the tail in Vln I, the iv–I close with D♭–C on top, and C5 at the end. **Renumbering:** Vln I's tail is now 85–89 and the plagal close 87–92 (v13's 89–93 and 91–96).

### 3. Bar 31: the transition restates theme A a third time instead of foreshadowing B (suggestion, not applied)

**Bars:** 31 (bass clarinet).

**Problem.** Bars 23–30 have just stated the theme's head and tail twice. At 31–34 the bass clarinet plays E♭–F–A♭–C–E♭, then E♭–D–D♭–C–B♭: the head and tail a third time, at the same pitch classes. Before D♭ arrives at 37, nothing points toward the B section.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 452: in ABA forms the tonicized IV "typically" begins with the new section and has no transition. A transition is justified when it prepares the new key. A&S PDF 448 (Ex. 26-7): Mozart's twice-stated V6/5–VI "foreshadows the larger tonicization that follows". The harmony of 32–36 is protected (the A♭→A♭7 pivot, and the common-tone diminished seventh at 33, which needs the bass clarinet's D at 33). Bar 31 is not part of that chord.

**Fix (suggestion).** Bar 31 only, same rhythm. Use theme B's opening, the rising fourth and third (A♭–D♭–F), over the A♭ pedal. It sounds as a IV6/4 neighbour that hints at D♭ before the A♭7 of 32:

```
% trans.ily, transBassCl bar 31
r4 aes8(\mp^\markup \italic "solo, espr." des'8 f'8 aes'8 | es'2.~ | es'4 d'4 des'4 | c'2 bes4) \breathe |
```

I have not built this one. It changes no durations.
