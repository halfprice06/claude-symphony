## Findings

### 1. Flutes 79–80: Fl 1's quarter and Fl 2's minim on one stem (APPLIED)

- **Bars:** 79–80 (also 63 and 78 b3 as side effects of the cause).
- **Problem:** At 80 b1 Fl 1's tied black B♭6 (the end of its tie) and Fl 2's tied minim F6 hang on one down-stem. At 79 both flutes also have down-stems. Gould PDF 60 says notes of different duration share a stem only in string writing. In all other cases they take separate stems, upper part up and lower part down (PDF 65: "The upper part always takes up-stems, the lower part down-stems"; minims and black noteheads kept separate). The oboes and clarinets in the same bars are correct, with Ob 1 up and Ob 2 down. A flautist reading 80 sees a two-note quarter chord and cannot tell that the F6 lasts two beats.
- **Cause:** In bSec.ily, `bSecFluteOne` has `\stemDown` at 57 (c015) and `\stemNeutral` at 61. In the score, flute 1 is in the part-combiner's voice "one" at 61, and `\stemNeutral` reverts the combiner's own up-stem setting in that voice for the rest of the movement. I tested a minimal file: the same notes without the earlier `\stemNeutral` combine correctly, with Fl 1 up and Fl 2 down. `\partCombineApart` at 79 does not help, because the voice is already "apart".
- **Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-275/work):**
  - sections/bSec.ily, bar 61: `\stemNeutral aes'''1--~\ff` becomes `\tag #'score \stemUp \tag #'part \stemNeutral aes'''1--~\ff`. This restores the combiner's direction in the score; the Flute 1 part is unchanged.
  - bar 63: `a'''4)` becomes `\once \stemDown a'''4)`, so the A6, alone on the staff with both flutes' rests, keeps its down-stem (PDF 60: a note above the middle line takes a down-stem).
  - sections/aTwo.ily, bar 78 b3: removed `\once \override Beam.positions = #'(-0.18 . 0.18)` before `c'''8(\<`. The fixed positions had been set for the accidental down-stems. With the up-stems c105 asked for ("Fl 1 grace stems up at 78"), they gave four "weird stem size" warnings. The group now beams above the notes, clear of the ledger lines (c105, Gould PDF 33).
- **Result:** 79–80 now show Fl 1 up and Fl 2 down, matching the oboes. Nothing else on the flute staff moved; I checked 57–64 and 75–82 at 200 dpi. This completes c105's intended up-stems at 78 and does not undo any earlier fix. The same `\tag #'score \stemUp … \tag #'score \stemNeutral` pattern is in `bSecOboeOne` at 61–62. It shows no error now (Ob 1 is up at 79–81), but a later edit could expose it. Use `\once` overrides there in future.

### 2. Viola part: tempo words and letters print nearer the system above (open item b; NOT APPLIED, tested)

- **Bars:**
  - 48 "poco sostenuto" is stacked over "poco rit." (47 b3) and sits under the dynamics of 43.
  - 53 "a tempo, poco a poco animando" is stacked over F and over the "53–56 / 4" rest count. It prints under 48–49, about 1/2 space below the tenuto-staccatos of 49 (Gould PDF 55: at least 1/2 space, no collisions).
  - 78 "poco allargando" is stacked over the slur of 78–79 and prints under 65–66.
  - The same applies to the letters D (37) under 22, E (45) under 41–42, and H and J (65, 71) under 60–61.
- **Evidence (measured):** All of these are outside-staff stacks over a single staff, and the system above is placed at the skyline padding (0.1). The page has no vertical slack:
  - With `page-count = 1` forced, padding 1.0 fits and 1.5 overflows by 1.2 staff spaces. Nine gaps share less than about one staff space each.
  - Any horizontal change sends the part to two pages under LilyPond's page-breaker estimate: widening bar 47 so that "poco rit." and "poco sostenuto" sit side by side (MetronomeMark extra-spacing-width), `\markLengthOn` at 47–48, moving the breaks to 41–43 | 44–47 | 48–56, or `system-count = 10`.
  - Margins of 15 mm or staff size 20 would make room but break MOLA PDF 7 (20 mm margin; strings at no less than 7.0–7.5 mm because they share a stand).
  - Breaking the system before 77 does not help: the 78 text is high because of the slur over E♭5–G5 at 78–79, not because of a stack.
- **What works, at a cost:** In the viola bookpart in genparts.py (the `pc` string, line 154), use `\paper { system-system-spacing.padding = #0.1 page-count = 1 }` and add to the viola music a parallel `{ s2.*36 s1*10 s2 \once \override Score.MetronomeMark.extra-spacing-width = #'(0 . 0.5) \once \override Score.MetronomeMark.extra-spacing-height = #'(-inf.0 . +inf.0) s2 s1*17 s2.*28 }`. "poco rit." and "poco sostenuto" then sit on one line, and the page really fits with no compression. However, LilyPond prints "too few pages" warnings (2–3) in the parts log. The parallel voice must run to the end; one ending inside 53–56 splits that multi-bar rest and adds a system. This fixes only bar 48; 53 and 78 stay where they are.
- **Recommendation:** Leave the layout until the composer decides to spend the vertical space. The only clean room left is the viola title block: the three-line title, subtitle and instrument name (about 3 staff spaces) could be compacted on this one part. If the composer does that, raise the padding to 1.0 and apply the bar-47 widening above.
