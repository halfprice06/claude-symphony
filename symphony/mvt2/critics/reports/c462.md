## Findings

All five findings below are records with no change to the score. Each checks a place where the reading gives a rule against the bars, and each either adds a book reason that earlier critics did not give or closes a question. ### 1. Every chromatic tone is approached and left as the reading requires (record, no change)

**Check.** I listed every non-diatonic note in bars 1-92, with the note before and after it in its own part.
- Mixture tones: every F♭ (5, 59 b4, 62 b3, 64 b4, 78, 80 b3-81 b1, 88) reaches E♭ in its own register, as A&S PDF 572-573 requires ("♭6 also occurs in an upper part ... consequently ... is not left unresolved"). Three of these resolutions pass to another instrument at the same pitch:
  - At 81, F♭6 in Fl 2 and F♭5 in Ob 2 go to rest, and Fl 1 and Ob 1 take E♭6 and E♭5 on beat 2.
  - At 78, the viola's F♭4 leaps away, and the harp RH has E♭4 on beat 3.
  - At 62 b3 the winds stop on F♭, while the strings keep it as E, the common tone into A minor.
- Leading tones: every leading tone of an applied chord rises, except the viola's E4 at 19 b3. That E4 goes to C4 in the viola's answer figure, and its resolution is in the cellos' F3 (recorded by c352).
- The two ♯4 sonorities A♭-C-D-F at 52 and 86 move in opposite directions. At 52 the D rises to E♭ (Ex. 30-20d, "P"). At 86 it falls to D♭ inside the motto tail, which is a chromatic linear progression (PDF 537: such a chord "will create a less disruptive effect").

**Fix:** none.

### 2. Bars 46-48 and 62-63: the two remote mixture chords are prepared in both ways the reading names, and the spelling switch at 62/63 has a book reason (record, no change)

**Check.** A&S PDF 536-537 says drastic mixture chords need preparation, either by their own V or by "a prior statement of the same scale degree used in a simpler, more normal fashion". Purcell's G minor follows a full bar of its own dominant, after which "the minor chord, though unexpected, appears logical in retrospect" (Ex. 30-3, PDF 533, 537).

- **46 b3-48.** G♭ major (IV of D♭) comes at 46 b3, then C♯7 (= D♭7, its V7) at 47, then G♭ minor written as F♯ minor at 48, which is iv by simple mixture. This is the Purcell procedure: the same degree is first heard major, then its V is heard, and then the minor form arrives.
- **62 b3-63.** F♭ major is V of A minor, and A minor is ♭II made minor. This is the Schubert procedure (Ex. 30-9, PDF 535-536).

**The spelling at 62/63.** c072 opened the question of writing 62 b3 as E-G♯-B, and c083 closed it ("keep F♭") without giving a reason. The reading gives one. In Ex. 30-9 (image, PDF 536) Schubert's chord before the ♭II♭ is labelled and written as ♭VI of the home key (C major in E), not as V of F minor. The score does the same: F♭ is written as ♭VI of A♭ at 62 b3, and the switch to E comes only at 63, where A minor stands for B♭♭ minor and needs a readable notation (PDF 559 and 573: "for ease of reading ... other notations will sometimes occur"). This also matches the major-third rule that ♭6 is respelled ♯5 at the point where its function changes (PDF 573, Ex. 31-27a).

**Fix:** none. The composer may add this reason to the c083 closure in HISTORY.md.

### 3. Bar 43: a °7 on ♯4 into the cadential 6/4 was considered and rejected (record)

**Check.** A&S PDF 545 (Ex. 30-22) and PDF 561 (Ex. 31-14) give the °7 on ♯4 between IV and the cadential 6/4 or V as the standard chromatic intensification. Bar 43 (G♭ at b1-2, D♭/A♭ 6/4 at b3, V at b4) is the one cadence in the B section where it could go: G♮ in the basses at 43 b2, under B♭-D♭-F♭.

**Why not.** The score uses the ♮4-♯4 approach once, at 59 (G♭ → G → A♭, with the °7 written with F♭ as applied VII of V, the notation of Ex. 30-22a). That approach leads to the climax at 61. Using it at 43 as well would take away its effect at 59. Bar 43's bass motion inside the subdominant is also recorded by c423, and its viola and cello voicings by c112, c192, c222 and c422.

**Fix:** none. Keep the device for 59 alone.

### 4. Bar 33 is a chromatic passing chord in parallel sixths, not a neighbouring common-tone °7. Correct the label only (record, no change)

**Check.** A&S PDF 543 defines the common-tone °7 by its neighbour motion: ♯2→3, ♯4→5, 6→5 over the held root. At 33 b2 the cor anglais goes C4-C♭4-B♭3 and the bass clarinet goes E♭3-D3-D♭3 (the motto tail). Both lines descend, into B♭m7 over A♭ at 33 b3. The C♭ spelling follows Ex. 30-21. The motion itself is the chromatic-passing type of Unit 31 (PDF 552, Ex. 31-1: "chromatic passing tones" over a diatonic frame): two voices a sixth apart (M6, d7, M6) moving down by semitone. c162 already found that the chord does not behave as a common-tone °7. However, the comment at transBsnTwo (c220) and the standing do-not-touch list still call it a "common-tone diminished seventh" and cite PDF 543-544 for the bass as "the stable, sustained tone of the embellishing chord". The held A♭ is correct under either reading.

**Fix:** none to the notes. The composer may relabel the item in the standing list as "chromatic passing °7 at 33 (A&S PDF 552)".

### 5. Bars 21, 50-51, 59, 64-65: the chromatic voice-leading frames match the book's models exactly (record, no change)

- **21.** The outer voices go D♭2/F5 (M10), E♭2/E♭5 (P8, the passing 6/4), F♭2/D5 (A6), then V. This is Ex. 31-20b, "starting on the 10th permits an elaboration of the IV/German 6/5 voice exchange". The passing 6/4 falls where the exchange reaches the octave (boxed note, PDF 566). The A6 chord is V7♭5 of V in 4/3 position, which is the French sixth of PDF 543.
- **59.** The bass goes G♭-G-A♭ under ii6/5, then V6/5 of V and vii°7/V, then V. This is Ex. 31-14's ♮4-♯4 frame with an applied V6/5 in place of the °4/3. F♭ falls to E♭ in the cellos, horn 1 and bassoon 1.
- **50 b4-51.** Bm7 (= C♭m7) to A♭7, roots a minor 3rd apart, moves in contrary semitones (D-E♭ in Vln II, A-A♭ in the cor anglais, B-C in the cellos). This is the m7-M10 type of PDF 566 (Ex. 31-22), though not a strict exchange. The cellos' B2-C3 is an inner chromatic step in D♭, not the finale's reserved B→C leading-tone rise to a C tonic (cyclic-notes.md lines 143 and 327).
- **64-65.** The bass reaches E♭ from D and from F♭, and 64 b3 has the double meaning of Ex. 30-9 (recorded by c382; protected).

**Fix:** none.
