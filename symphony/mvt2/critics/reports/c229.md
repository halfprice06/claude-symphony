## Holistic verdict

Set beside Beethoven 7/ii, the movement does the things Adler draws from that score. Theme A rises in register by statement (horn A♭3–F4 at 7; violins A♭4–B♭5 at 23; violins in octaves to B♭6 at 71–82), as Beethoven's tune climbs from violas to Vln II to Vln I to tutti. The A′ statement at 71–78 has three layers with three rhythms (row 7). The D♭ episode is built like Beethoven's A-major episode: a wind tune over string triplets with a pizzicato bass. The climaxes are spaced like the overtone series. A conductor reading the string pages would not find the student habits ch. 5 warns against: no Vln I divisi on the tune, no undoubled tune in a weak register, no accompaniment in the tune's own rhythm. The one place where the string section's balance goes against the reading is the f peak of the muted first statement (finding 1).

Compared with v219 nothing got worse. The changes since then (bassoons and harp at 9 and 11, viola pizz. at 41–44, Vc C3 at 43 b4, Bsn 2 pedal through 33, Tbn 2 A♭3 at 62, violas divided at 80–81 and 88–92, Cl 2 niente at 89, MIDI only at 76, 91 and in score.ly) each keep or improve the spacing and doubling rules above. The last chord now has A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–E♭4–A♭4–C5, which is overtone spacing (row 13).

## Findings

### 1. Bars 27–28: unmuted, divided basses at f under muted strings (applied)

**Problem.** At the f peak of theme A's first string statement (28 b2) the violins, violas and cellos are con sord. (c039), and the basses are senza sord. (c119) and divided in octaves (c204, D♭2 and D♭3). All four groups have the same f. The basses are therefore the only unmuted strings, and they are doubled at the octave below.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151, no. 1: the plain setting "would prove very bass-heavy if the dynamic were loud, since the violas, cellos, and basses are in a much better register than the violins"; no. 3: "adding the bass an octave lower would greatly darken the line". Here the violins are also muted, which Rimsky-Korsakov (PDF 28, cited by c119) says reduces volume greatly. One level under the muted groups balances the unmuted section. c204's octaves and c119's senza sord. stay.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-229/work, sections/aOne.ily, aOneCb).**
```
<aes,, aes,>4\< <c, c>4 <des, des>4 | \after 4 \mf \after 4 \> <des, des>2 <bes,, bes,>4 | es,2.^\markup \italic "unis." | ...
```
(was `\after 4 \f`). The score prints mf under the basses at 28 b2; the hairpin and the p at 30 are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 53–56: basses tacet under the first string statement of theme B (no change)

Beethoven keeps the basses in every statement and lightens them by articulation (bars 27–50: `r4 a,8 a, gis, r gis r`). A pizzicato copy of 37–40's bass (`des,4 r4 c,4 r4 | bes,,4 r4 ges,,4 r4 | es,4 r4 c,4 r4 | a,,4 r4 bes,,4 r4`, arco at 57) would keep the low D♭ at the 52→53 arrival. But c164's model is in the assigned reading: Haydn's first statement (Ex. V-16, PDF 139) has no cellos or basses at all, and they enter for the second. The cellos' falling line reaches E♭2 by 55, and the timpani's damped D♭3 marks 53. Both models are master practice; c164's choice follows the assigned page. Leave it.

### 3. Bars 48–51: Vln II above the cor anglais quotation (no change)

Adler (PDF 144) puts dynamics last among the means of separating a theme from a counter-theme, so "pp" alone would not justify the Vln II line at A5–D5 above the C.a.'s F♯4–D5. The other three criteria are met. The quotation is in the cor anglais' best register (C♯4–D5). The counterpoint is thin (violas off-beat, basses, Vc). The lines are separated by register: Vln II is above, and Adler allows "one high, one low; or vice versa". The rhythm also differs: at 49 the C.a. has 4 2 4 with the syncopated D5 and Vln II has 2 4 4, and at 50 the C.a. has 2 4 4 and Vln II 2 2. c054, c202 and c221 are kept.

### 4. Bar 87: IV6 with the third in five places (no change)

At 87 the chord is D♭/F. F sounds in Cb F1, Vc F2, Vla F3, Hn 3 F3 and C.a. F4, and the root D♭ only in Cl 1 (D♭5). Ex. V-27 (PDF 152) asks for the doubled third near the bottom. The Cb/Vc octave is that doubling. The F3 and F4 are the start of the F–F♭–E♭ line (C.a., lower violas, Hn 3), which is protected (c194, c223), and the chord lasts one bar before iv at 88. The D♭ is the top voice, so it is heard. Recorded only.

### 5. Bars 71–78: A′ against Ex. V-20 (no change)

This is the closest point to Beethoven. The tune is in Vln I+II octaves and the counter-melody in Vc (with Hn 2 and Bsn 1 from 75), so they are separated by register and rhythm (PDF 144 nos. 3–4). The viola's slurred eighths are the background, in their own rhythm and articulation (PDF 142, point 1). Beethoven's background figure begins with an eighth rest, which leaves the downbeat to the tune. Here the viola attacks with the tune on every downbeat, but on the chord third or root (C4 at 71, 73, 75), not on the tune's pitch, and the protected viola figure (c071, c177) already pulses under Vln II's held notes. No change.

### 6. Bars 61–64: climax and quotation against Ex. V-29 and V-37 (no change)

The ff arrival at 61 keeps Vln I undivided on D♭6 (c059, PDF 152). The big chord follows pale harmony (row 18). At 63–64 the upper-string tremolo under Tbn 1's quotation is Bizet's device for an ominous low melody (Ex. V-37, PDF 161). The violins are in their brilliant register there, but the sf > p hairpin brings them down to p by bar 64, while the trombone stays marcato until its own diminuendo. This passage is protected (61–65), and the reading supports it as it is.
