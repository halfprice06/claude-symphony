### 2. The muted horns at 87–90 render as open horns, as loud as the violas they double (applied, MIDI only)
**Problem.** Hn 3·4 con sord. at 87–90 echo Hn 3's open F4–F♭4–E♭4 of 79–81 an octave lower, as a sound of distance (c030, c104, c150). In score.midi they have velocity 37 on channel 5 (general-MIDI French horn), the same velocity as the divided violas at pp, and nothing tells the synthesizer that they are muted. The render therefore plays the echo as the same open horn at pp, and its one distinguishing colour is lost.
**Evidence.** A mock-up aims at the dramatic effect of the real result, and one example is muted and unmuted string sounds combined to suggest it (Casanelles PDF 18; PDF 32: the aim is to minimise surprises after the recording). Muted brass "produce an effect of distance", and the tone becomes "tender and dull in piano" with "resonance greatly reduced" (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44). Casanelles separates volume from dynamic (PDF 30), and a lower CC11 changes amplitude, not spectrum. So this only approximates the mute. General MIDI has no muted horn, and "muted trumpet" (program 60) would be the wrong instrument. A cut in volume is the nearest thing the score's MIDI can give.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-230/work/sections/coda.ily).**
`codaHnThree = { R2.*4 | \set Staff.midiExpression = #0.7 \partCombineChords aes2.(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." | …`
CC11 is 89 from 87 on channel 5. Horns 1·2 share that channel and rest from 83 to the end. The global fade at 91 resets CC11 to 127 → 97 after the horns have stopped. The printed score is unchanged (pdftotext output identical), and analysis.txt is identical apart from its timestamp line.

### 4. The harmonic content of the link and the last chord (recorded; follows)
Movement I's last chord is C2 C3 G3 C4 E4 G4 C5. That is partials 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 of C2, so its E4 is the fifth partial. At II's bar 1 the C2 drops out, so for one bar (about 3.5 s) the chord stands on C3 and E4 is no longer a partial of the bass. At bar 2, A♭1 A♭2 C4 E♭4 A♭4 C5 is partials 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 10 of A♭1. The common tone C5 becomes a partial (the 10th) of the new bass, and the last chord, A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5, is partials 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 (Casanelles PDF 30–31). Both movements end on chords laid out as the harmonic series of their bass, and bar 2 states the new series. The thin bar 1 is the gap that lets A♭1 enter as a new fundamental and not as a bass step from C2. I do not propose holding the basses' C2 into bar 1. That would change movement I's ending (its c150) and the protected link 1–2.

### 6. Loudness against register at the cyclic statements (recorded; follows)
Low frequencies need more amplitude to be heard at the same loudness (Casanelles PDF 31). Each low-register cyclic statement sits at least one level above its accompaniment:
- B.cl. tail E♭3–B♭2 p < mp > p over the C.a. pp at C4 (31–34).
- Hn 1 tail p < mp over the pp E♭ pedal (67–69).
- Tbn 1 ff over strings sf > p (63–64).
- Timpani pp over the ppp pedal (84–85).

In score.midi the cellos' ppp C3 at bar 1 has velocity 44, against 31 for the violins. This comes from LilyPond's instrument equalizer, and it suits this principle, so it should stay.
