## Findings

Ranked by how much each would stand out to a string section reading the parts.

### 1. Bars 59-61: the violins, cellos and basses arrive at the ff of 61 on an up-bow, after a whole-note crescendo on a down-bow. **Applied.**
**Problem.** Counting the bows as written from the up-bow pickup at 52 b4 (Adler PDF 38: an anacrusis is up-bow), Vln I and Vln II take 57 up, 57 b3-58 b2 down, 58 b3-4 up, 59 b1-2 down, 59 b3-4 up, 60 (`c'''1\<`) down, 61 (`des'''1--\ff`) up. The cellos (from 53, two bows a bar, then `f2 | ges2(…f4) | es2(…aes4) | 59 | 60`) and basses (`ges,2 g,2 | aes,,1\<`) come out the same way: the crescendo bar is a down-bow and the ff arrival an up-bow.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "The tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural … Conversely, there is a tendency to perform a diminuendo with a down-bow … [the composer] should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players." Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*) marks ⊓ V so that each ff attack is a down-bow; Ex. II-19 (Schubert) uses two up-bows in a row for the same purpose. Bar 61 is the movement's main arrival ("Largamente", ff, molto sostenuto); every section should attack it from the frog.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Vln I `es'''4.(\f f'''8 des'''4 bes''4) | c'''1\upbow\< |` (59 in one bow, about four seconds at f; 60 then falls up-bow without a mark, the V confirms it); Vln II the same an octave lower. Vc `es4(\upbow\< f4 ges4. f8)` at 60 (a retake after 59, as in Ex. II-19). Cb `ges,2(\f g,2) | aes,,1\upbow\< |`. The violas' tremolo at 61 needs no sign.

### 2. Bars 59-60: violas slur twelve sixteenths to a bow at f, crescendo to ff. **Applied.**
**Problem.** `\tuplet 6/4 4 { ges16(\f … bes) }`: each slur covers two beats (about 1.9 s at ♩ = 62-64), twelve notes, at f growing to ff. The bow cannot carry that weight of tone, so players will divide it anyway, each in a different place.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39, Ex. II-22: Mendelssohn's violas have "only six notes on a bow, since the dynamic is forte" (in a fast tempo); "even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow". The do-not-touch rule from c018 is "viola triplets 37-60 (six per bow)"; the sextuplets added in c029 broke it.
**Fix (applied).** One sextuplet (one beat) per bow: `\tuplet 6/4 4 { ges16(\f bes es' bes ges bes) es'( bes ges bes es' bes) } \tuplet 6/4 4 { bes16( des' es' des' bes des') fes'( des' bes des' fes' des') }`, and likewise at 60. With four bows in each bar the violas also reach 61 on a down-bow.

### 3. Bars 89-92: violas, cellos and basses hold one note for four bars under a rit. and a fermata with no bow-change instruction. **Applied.**
**Problem.** Vla `es2.~` and Vc/Cb `aes,2.~` are tied from 89 to the end: about 20 s at the written rit. (♩ 48-36, last beat 20) plus the fermata. Their "change bow at will" at 83-84 lapsed when 87 and 88 were played as separate bows. Vln II has the instruction at 89; Vln I has its bowings marked. The lower strings, with the shorter bows (Adler PDF 39), have nothing.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39 (slow passages; cello and bass bows shorter) and PDF 40 (Ex. II-24/25: a long legato line is made by staggering the bow changes among the players).
**Fix (applied, coda.ily).** `^\markup \italic "change bow at will"` on the first tied note at 89 in Vla, Vc and Cb. It is printed at the same height as Vln II's instruction; the page count is unchanged.

### 4. Bars 41-42: the cellos have a phrase slur over eight beats. **Applied.**
**Problem.** `des1(\pp\< | des4\p\> bes,4 c4 des4)`: one slur over a crescendo whole note and four diminuendo quarters, about 8 s at ♩ = 60. It is a phrase mark; as a bowing it asks for a crescendo and a diminuendo in one cello bow.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts. Such slurs only confuse the performer. The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow." PDF 39: cello bows are shorter.
**Fix (applied).** `des1\pp\< | des4(\p\> bes,4 c4 des4)`: the whole note takes its own bow (crescendo), and the four quarters one bow (diminuendo). This is the same shape as the cellos' line at 43-44.

### 5. Bar 84: the violas' divisi is printed in parentheses, "(div.) change bow at will". **Applied.**
**Problem.** The violas played a single E♭4 at 82 and enter at 84 on C4/E♭4. This is a real division, not a reminder. Parentheses make it look cautionary or optional, so some desks will play the double stop.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: "If the word divisi does not appear in the parts, the player would be correct in performing the chord as a double-stop." The intention (two players, ppp, held for three bars) needs the plain word.
**Fix (applied).** `^\markup \italic "div., change bow at will"`. The "unis., sul C" at 87 already cancels it.

### 6. Bars 71 and 79: "unis." is printed twice in the violas with no divisi in between. **Applied.**
**Problem.** The violas' last divided passage ends at 64 (the "non div." double stops, followed by unis. at 65). They print "unis., ord." again at 71 and "unis." again at 79 over single notes. A cancelling word with nothing to cancel makes the player look for a divisi that is not there.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30: unis. appears "when divisi is no longer in order and the part returns to single notes". PDF 38 warns against overmarking the parts.
**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily).** 71: `^\markup \italic "ord."` (still needed after sul C at 65, Adler PDF 53); 79: no text. Optional and not applied: Vln II and Vla return to single notes at 64 b3 (`b4(` and `gis2(`), a bar before the "unis." printed at 65. After "non div." no unis. is strictly needed, so the 65 marks could simply be left out.

### 7. Bars 61-64: three-stroke tremolo at a slow tempo could be read as measured 32nds. **Not applied (suggestion).**
**Problem.** Adler PDF 49-50 separates the unmeasured tremolo ("as often as possible", Ex. II-46) from the measured tremolo, a shorthand for repeated détaché notes (Ex. II-47). Here `:32` on whole and half notes at ♩ = 54, slowing to 40 in 63-64, means 32nds at about 5-7 per second, a rate a section can count. Players will usually read three strokes as unmeasured, but at this tempo it is not certain.
**Fix.** Add `^\markup \italic "trem."` to the first tremolo note of each section (Vla 61; Vln I, Vln II, Vc 63), or merge it into the existing text ("non div., molto sostenuto, trem."). Check the page count; page 5 is full.
