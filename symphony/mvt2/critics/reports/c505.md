## Findings

### 1. Flute 1 and oboe 1 at 59: up-stems on a single line above the staff (applied)

**Bars:** 59 (Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2 staves, score only).

**Problem.** In 57-58 Fl 1 and Ob 1 have down-stems (c015, protected as "Fl 1/Ob 1 down-stems 57-60"). At 59 both lines turn to up-stems: Fl 1 E♭6 dotted quarter, D♭6 eighth, D♭6 and B♭5 quarters; Ob 1 C5, D♭5, B♭4, G4. The flute notes lie two to three ledger lines above the stave, and their up-stems are about 2.8 spaces long (probe: length 5.68 half-spaces), so they never reach the middle line. The D♭6 eighth carries an up-tail on that short stem.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: notes above the middle line take down-stems. PDF 28: stems of notes on more than one ledger line reach the middle line. PDF 30: with a stem shorter than 3 spaces the tail must not overshoot the notehead, so lengthen the stem or avoid the case. The cause is c155's score spacer (`\tag #'score { s1*2 }` in bSecFluteTwo and bSecOboeTwo at 59-60). The spacer makes the part-combiner switch player 1 from its solo voice into voice "one" at 59, and that voice carries `\voiceOne` (up-stems), so the `\stemDown` given at 57 no longer applies. c155 kept the shared rest out of the way but turned the stems over; this restores c015's down-stems without bringing back the rest.

**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, bSecFluteOne and bSecOboeOne, bar 59: restate the direction in the new voice.
```
... \breathe | \stemDown es'''4.(\f f'''8 des'''4 bes''4 | c'''1)\< \breathe |   % Fl 1
... \breathe | \stemDown es''4.(\f f''8 des''4 bes'4 | c''1)\< \breathe |         % Ob 1
```
The parts are single-voice and already had down-stems here, so they do not change. At 61 the score-tagged `\stemUp` for the climax chord takes over as before.

### 2. Flute 1 at 82: A♭5 dotted half with an up-stem (applied)

**Bars:** 82, Fl 1·2 staff.

**Problem.** Fl 1's last note of A′, A♭5 on the first ledger line, has a short up-stem, and the slur from 80 ends on the stem side. The same thing as finding 1 causes it: c395's score spacer `\tag #'score s2.` in aTwoFluteTwo at 82 puts flute 1 in voice "one". At 81 the composer already forces down-stems with `\temporary \override Stem.direction = #DOWN` (c394), then reverts before 82.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27 (above the middle line: down-stem); PDF 28 (no clear case: down). Neighbouring stems at 81 are down.

**Fix (applied).** aTwo.ily, aTwoFluteOne, bar 82:
```
| \once \stemDown \after 2 \pp aes''2.)\p\> |
```
(The `\once \stemDown` must come before `\after`. Placed after it, it took `\after`'s music argument and the build gave bar-check warnings.)

### 3. Clarinet 2 at 72: the tied B♭4 changes stem direction (applied)

**Bars:** 72, Cl 1·2 staff (score only).

**Problem.** Cl 2 plays A♭4 dotted quarter, then B♭4 eighth tied to B♭4 quarter. c095 gave the middle-line B♭4 quarter an up-stem (`\tag #'score \once \stemUp bes'4`) to match the up-stems around it, but not the tied eighth before it. The bar now reads up, down, up, and the tie joins a down-stemmed eighth with a flag to an up-stemmed quarter.

**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28: when the direction varies within a bar, keep the direction of the notes in the same beat. The B♭4 eighth belongs to beat 2 with the up-stemmed A♭4. PDF 27: middle-line notes follow the surrounding stems, which here are all up (71 E♭4, 72 A♭4, 73 A♭4 and E♭4).

**Fix (applied).** aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo:
```
aes'4.-- \tag #'score \once \stemUp bes'8~ \tag #'score \once \stemUp bes'4 |
```
Score only, as c255 required for transposing parts. In the B♭ part the note is written C5, above the middle line, and keeps its default down-stem.

### 4. Oboe 1 at 46 b1: B♭4 down-stem in a half-bar of up-stems (applied; re-raises c255 item 24)

**Bars:** 46, Ob 1·2 staff (and the Ob 1 part).

**Problem.** B♭4 (middle line) quarter has a down-stem, and the A♮4-B♭4 eighths that complete the half-bar have up-stems. Bsn 1 plays the same line an octave lower on the staff below, with up-stems throughout.

**Evidence and answer to c255.** c255 ruled that this follows the "continue the surrounding stems" rule (PDF 27), because 45 is all down-stems. Bar 45 is not one-directional around this note: the following group goes up and the half note D♭5 goes down. So PDF 27 does not decide it, and PDF 28 is the more specific rule: "When the stem direction varies within a bar, maintain the stem direction of the notes that are part of the same beat or half-bar." In 4/4 the first half-bar is B♭4 + A♮4-B♭4, so B♭4 goes up. This is the lowest-ranked finding. If the composer prefers c255's reading, revert it.

**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, bSecOboeOne, bar 46: `| \once \stemUp bes'4( a'8 bes'8 des''2\<) |`. The oboe is in C, so the part takes the same direction and the edit is not tagged.
