## Holistic judgement against Dvořák 8/ii

Three techniques carry Dvořák's Adagio: an opening heard in the wrong key that turns to the real one; tonic minor against tonic major as the movement's main contrast; and many short contrasting episodes. This movement does the first in its own way. Bar 1 is movement I's C major chord, and the held C turns it to A♭, which is the *Widmung* model on PDF 586. It does the second on a smaller scale: ♭6 and ♭III as neighbours and colours (21, 62, 78, 87–88), plus the F♭/E/E♭ thread across the whole movement, which matches A&S's account of the Hammerklavier's F/F♯ conflict (PDF 585). It does not do the third. ABA′ with one middle section is the plan, and Dvořák 9/ii, the main model, is ABA′ too. A conductor who knows Dvořák 8 would notice fewer changes of mood and a more uniform surface. They would not notice amateur harmony or voice leading. Every chromatic chord I checked against Units 22–32 is spelled, doubled and resolved as the book teaches. The two places where Dvořák's sound would differ most are the climax without trumpets and the A′ that is re-scored rather than varied. Both come from the cyclic plan, and both are protected.

Compared with v179, nothing got worse. Details are in finding 4.

**1. Mixture is used locally, not as a structural contrast (whole movement). No change.**
Problem: Mendelssohn's overture (Ex. 23-1, PDF 386–387) and Dvořák 8/ii set whole thematic statements in the parallel mode. Here the A theme never appears with minor inflection. Its three statements (7, 23, 71) differ in scoring only.
Evidence: A&S PDF 387: mixture "enables a composer to focus on this duality within a single piece or passage", and using two forms of one scale degree gives "the potential for dramatic juxtaposition".
Decision: I considered setting the A′ phrase 2 (75–76) over ♭6 (F♭ for F in the bass at 75 b3). The test the book sets is that ♭6 then goes to 5 and not back to ♮6 (PDF 388). Bar 76 needs F as the 3rd of ii6/5 under the B♭ appoggiatura, so ♭6 would be replaced by ♮6. That is the motion Ex. 23-2h tells you to avoid. The protected A′ texture and octaves (75–82) also stand in the way. The movement already has its mode contrast in the F♭/E/E♭ thread and the ♭VI deceptive cadence at 78. Keep as is.

**2. Bar 21: the French sixth before the half cadence is a master's touch. No change.**
Bar 21 b3 sounds F♭ (Vc, Cb), A♭3 (Vla), B♭3 (Vln II) and D5 (solo violin). That is a complete French sixth on ♭6 of A♭. It opens outward to the octave E♭ (solo violin E♭5, bass E♭) at 22, the A section's half cadence.
Evidence: PDF 503: the augmented sixth's bass lies a half step above 5, an upper voice a half step below, and it resolves to an octave on 5; "augmented 6th chords often occur just before important structural points." It is the only augmented sixth in the movement, so it keeps its force. Record it so that no later critic "fixes" the recorded Vln II F4/bass F♭ near-cross-relation at 21 by changing a note of this chord.

**3. Bar 42: Fl 1's tied E♭5 resolves upward. Kept as an exception.**
Problem: over the D♭ bass on 42 b4, Fl 1 holds E♭5 (a 9th) and rises to F5 while the cor anglais' G♭4 resolves 4–3 to F4. The two voices converge on an octave F4–F5. A&S PDF 348 says a dissonant suspension resolves down, and double suspensions move in parallel 3rds or 6ths (PDF 347).
Fix considered: `es''4~ es''8\> des''8` (9–8, parallel sixths with the cor anglais). With that change Fl 1's D♭5 → G♭5 into 43 runs against the bass's D♭2 → G♭1 as octaves by contrary motion in the outer voices, and the rising line E♭–F–G♭ is lost.
Decision: keep. An upward resolution in a voice that is moving up through a scale is a recognised exception (PDF 348). The present version keeps the outer voices clean.

**5. 61–65: the climax follows Units 28–32 in order. No change.**
D♭ (61) → G♭ (62) → ♭III F♭ (62 b3, PDF 532) → A minor (63) with A♭6 inflected to A♮6 (PDF 585) → E7 → E♭7 by parallel half steps (PDF 552) → V7 of A♭ at 65. This passage is closer to a master's chromatic practice than any other part of the movement.
