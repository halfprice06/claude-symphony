### 2. The last cyclic solo (Cl 1, 85-89) is introduced the way Adler's Mozart and Sibelius examples prescribe (record, no change)

**Bars:** 79-89.

**Evidence:**
- Mozart Flute Concerto, item 3 (PDF 267, Ex. XVII-10): the soloist follows a tutti, the orchestra is cut back at once, and a sustained tonic in the low instruments marks the return. Here the 79-81 tutti (ff, B♭6) clears at 82. At 83-86 only Vc A♭2 and Cb A♭1 (ppp) remain, under the harp's harmonics (83) and the timpani (84-85).
- Sibelius (PDF 269, Ex. XVII-11): the soloist enters over muted divided violins at pp, so the background "does not intrude at all". Here the violas enter at 84 b3 "con sord., div." ppp on C4-E♭4, one bar before Cl 1's E♭5. The interval of a sixth and the octave below the solo meet the distance rule at PDF 274.
- Beethoven VC (PDF 273, Ex. XVII-13): the timpani play the motive that joins two phrases. At 84-85 the timpani's E♭3-C3-A♭2 joins the harp's theme head (83) to Cl 1 (85). It is the link of the timpani chain I 179-180 → II 84-85 → III 9 → IV 9 (mvt1.md, scherzo intro.ily c090).
- Mozart item 4 (PDF 267): there is no colour competition. Cl 2 rests from 82 until it enters on E♭4 at 89, after Cl 1 has reached C5.
- PDF 275 (pitch reference): Cl 1's first note E♭5 sounds over the violas' E♭4 and after the timpani's E♭3.
- Liszt (PDF 273-274): a second colour picks out the solo's important pitch. Vln I takes Cl 1's C5 at 89, the pitch movement I ended on.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep 83-89 as they are. The scherzo's timpani and the finale's Ob 1 tail at IV 95-98 (finale.md item 12) depend on these bars.

### 3. The S-head quotation (C.a., 47-53): the solo replaces a wind in its group; one registral exception stays settled (record, no change)

**Bars:** 45-53.

**Evidence:**
- Beethoven VC (PDF 273): the soloist takes the flute's place in the woodwind group. At 45-47 Ob 1, Bsn 1 and the cellos play theme B's head in B♭ minor. Ob 1 stops on C♯5 at 47 b3, and the C.a. enters on C♯4 at 47 b4. The quotation takes over the oboe's place in the double-reed colour, and no oboe plays during it, which is Mozart's no-competition rule (PDF 267).
- Pitch lead (PDF 275): Bsn 1 and the cellos hold C♯4 through 47 b3, the C.a.'s first pitch at the same octave.
- Rhythmic independence (PDF 259-260): the violas play off-beat quavers at 48-50 under the C.a.'s held A4 and D5.
- Registral distance (PDF 274): Vln II is above the C.a. at 48 b3-50 (F♯5, A5, G5, F5, F♯5, A5, D5) at pp against the C.a.'s p. c294, c324 and c330 recorded this and kept it, and c202/c263 protect the A5 at 49 b1-2. Adler himself says rhythmic contrast lowers the risk, and the violas supply it. I have no new reason to reopen it.
- Colour freshness (PDF 263): the C.a. was the soloist three bars earlier (41-44, theme B's second phrase), so its colour is not fresh at 47. The dialogue principle (PDF 257) explains why this is right here. The listener has just had the C.a.'s timbre fixed, and movement I's last statement of the minor S head was its cello and C.a. duet (I 171-174, mvt1.md). The quotation is heard as that colour remembered, not as a new solo. The quotation's instrument is protected (c221).

**Recommendation:** none.

### 4. The P-head quotation (Tbn 1, 63-64) is an accompanied-recitative interruption in its strongest range (record, no change)

**Bars:** 61-65.

**Evidence:**
- PDF 276 (Ex. XVII-17, Mendelssohn): orchestral interruptions heighten the drama. After the ff D♭ climax (61) and the F♭ chord (62), the strings strike sf and fall to p on tremolo A minor at 63, and Tbn 1 enters ff "marcato" on the off-beat (63 b1.5). The quotation cuts across the close, as in the recitative.
- Orff (PDF 288): put the foreground in its best range, with an accompaniment that does not hide it. Tbn 1 plays E3-C4 between Vc A2 and the violas' E4-C5 tremolo, and Tbn 2 rests.
- Pitch lead (PDF 275): the E3 pickup follows the F♭ (= E) chord of 62.
- The bass trombone's A2 sf > p is the one same-family sound. It is an octave and more below the line and struck on the beat before Tbn 1 enters. c467 recorded it and c485 fixed its notation, and "Tbn 1 alone 63-64" is protected.

**Recommendation:** none.

### 5. The hand-over to the scherzo follows the dialogue and pitch-reference rules (record, no change)

**Bars:** 82-92; scherzo 1-12 (v400 intro.ily).

**Evidence:**
- Dialogue (PDF 257): the scherzo opens with its soloist alone, Hn 1 G♯4 pp with a fermata, so the timbre is fixed before anything else enters.
- Movement II prepares this:
  - Hn 1's last note is C4 at 82 b1-2, so the horn rests through the coda and the pause (PDF 275 on vocal fatigue applies to a horn as well).
  - The only horn sound in between is muted (Hn 3·4, 87-90, "via sord." at 91).
  - Vln II's A♭4 from 87 to the fermata gives the horn its pitch (PDF 275).
- The scherzo's timpani B-B-B-G | E at 9-10 is II's E♭-C-A♭ a major third lower, on the drums the muta at 92 retunes.
- scherzo.md (2026-11-01) says scherzo v431 adds Vln I G♯5 ppp at its bars 1-2. Movement II's upper strings have "via sord." printed at 92 b3, so they reach that entry open. Nothing in II needs to change for it.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep Vln II A♭4 to the end, Hn 1 out of 83-92, the muted Hn 3·4 at 87-90 and the muta text at 92.

### 6. The upper octave comes once, and the strings' mutes follow the Carmen model (record, no change)

**Bars:** 15-31, 79-80, 82-92.

**Evidence:**
- Bizet (PDF 279, Ex. XVII-20): the violins double the voice an octave higher only at the climax. On the rendered PDF 281 the strings are marked "senza sordini" for that climax.
- In II:
  - The strings are muted for the dialogue at 15-30 and open from 31.
  - B♭6 sounds only at 79 (Vln I and Fl 1).
  - The upper strings are muted again for the coda (from 83-84, c468).
  - Vln I ends on movement I's C5, sul D, as at bar 1.
- Across the symphony the violins' peaks rise A♭6 (I), B♭6 (II), B6 (III), C7 (IV) (mvt1.md line 224). Movement II's single B♭6 and its return to C5 place it correctly in that series.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep B♭6 only at 79 and the muted C5 ending.
