## Findings

### 1. Bars 79–81: at the A′ peak the counter-melody outweighs the theme's middle octave (applied)

**Problem.** At 79 the theme is in Fl 1 + Vln I (B♭6) and Ob 1 + Vln II (B♭5). Against it, the counter-melody D♭4–C4–B♭3 is in horns 1·2 a2 **ff**, Bsn 1 ff and the cellos ff, and horns 3·4 hold F4/A♭3 ff. That is four horns at ff in the octave of the counter-melody. The B♭5 octave of the theme has one oboe and the second violins. By weight of scoring, the strongest single line is the horns' counter-melody, not the theme.

**Evidence.** In Mozart 40/iv (Adler PDF 151, Ex. XV-3) the octave with the heaviest scoring is the one the listener takes as the theme, and the others are "simply for sheen". D'Indy (PDF 147–148, Ex. XV-1) marks the trombones ppp inside a f tutti "so as not to dominate the entire orchestra". In this texture the theme is the foreground and the counter-melody is a second, distinct idea under it (PDF 154). The scoring should keep that order.

**Fix (applied).** In `aTwo.ily`:
- Clarinet 1 doubles the theme at B♭5 with Ob 1 and Vln II for 79–81: `bes''2.~(\ff | bes''4 aes''2) | g''4(\> es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8) | \after 2 \pp c''2.\p\> |`
- Clarinet 2 takes clarinet 1's D♭5 and keeps c024's fill between D♭5 and B♭5: `aes'4\ff des''2 | des''4 des''4 des''4 | g'2.\> | \after 2 \pp aes'2.\p\> |`. The A♭4 held on 79 b1 removes an A♭–D♭ octave with the cellos at 78→79. That octave was already in v34, in clarinet 1, but the checker missed it because the two clarinets were in unison on A♭4 at 78. G4 at 81, not B♭4, avoids D♭–B♭ octaves with the cellos, horns and Bsn 1 at 80→81.
- Horns 1·2 at 79 are marked `\f` instead of `\ff`. Both players get the same mark, as the shared staff needs. Their p< pickup at 78 now leads to f.

Clarinet 1 reaches B♭5 from A♭4 at the ff downbeat. That is a ninth, but it comes after a held note and is easy at ff. Horns stay with cellos and violas (do-not-touch from cycle 14). All the differences between A′ and 23–30 remain. Trumpets and trombones stay out (the recorded choice from c024).

### 2. Bar 61: the top octave of the augmented head is in the flutes alone for its first two notes (applied)

**Problem.** At 61 the head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ is in octaves: cellos A♭3, Ob 2 + Tpt 1 + Vln II A♭4, flutes a2 A♭5. Vln I holds F5/D♭6 for the whole of bar 61 and joins the theme's top octave only at 62 (F6–F♭6). So the A♭5–D♭6 rise is in two flutes, in their middle register, against three trumpets, four horns, three trombones and tuba ff. Then the violins take over the same octave halfway through the gesture.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151 (Mozart): "the composer wants every note in this passage played effectively by every instrument that he uses". The octave tutti works when each octave keeps the same instruments through the gesture. PDF 152 (Beethoven 9) says only the highest flute octave is piercing enough to need no doubling, and A♭5–D♭6 is not that octave.

**Fix (applied).** In `bSec.ily`, Vln I bar 61 is `<f'' aes''>2\ff^\markup \italic "non div., molto sostenuto" <f'' des'''>2 |`. The top note now follows the theme with the flutes, and c024's F5–D♭6 double stop stays on beat 3. The minor-third double stop F5/A♭5 lies on the A and E strings. This does not undo c024: the upper chord is still filled on beat 3.

### 3. Bars 71–74: the harp RH chords are the viola figure's own notes (applied); 75–78 left

**Problem.** At 71–74 the harp RH plays `<aes c' es'>` and `<g bes es'>` on beat 2. At the same time the violas play `c' es' aes' es' c' aes` and similar eighths in the same octave. The harp adds an attack on notes that are already sounding and does nothing the viola figure does not. Its colour is also used up before the crescendo at 75, which is where its re-entry could be heard.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 158 (Mahler, Ex. XV-8): "There are no two instruments with identical parts for more than a few notes." PDF 146, Maroges: the most brilliance from the least colour. PDF 156: "a rest before a certain color enters" is the way to make it heard. c027 (finding 5) raised the same unisons, and HISTORY lists them as open.

**Fix (applied).** In `aTwo.ily`, harp RH 71–74 becomes `R2.*4`. The LH keeps the bass under 8 bassa. The RH comes back at 75 with the p< crescendo. For 75–78, where the same unisons continue, I suggest c027's re-registration without the counter-melody's pitches, which I did not apply: harp RH 77 `r4 <es' g'>2`, viola 77 `es'8( g' bes') g'( es' bes')`.
