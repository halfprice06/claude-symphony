## Findings

The second pass over this reading finds no violation. Every wind melody lies in the register Adler names for its character, and every weak register is left uncovered. The points below are a melodic verdict and three recorded observations. None of them is applied, because each touches a protected passage and the reading gives no reason strong enough to reopen it.

### 1. Theme A: at a master's level (no change)

The arpeggio to a held note, then the chromatic descent E♭–D–D♭–C, is the kind of tune a listener hums afterwards. Its second phrase peaks on the B♭ appoggiatura in bar 6 of 8 (12, 28, 76), the standard place, and the A′ version saves B♭6 ff for 79. Its three statements change colour (horn, violins, violins with flute) in the way Adler's PDF 184 asks for: the horn, not a woodwind, takes the first statement, and that is the cyclic plan.

### 2. Theme B: the first phrase is four neighbour-note cells inside a diminished fifth (observation, not applied)

**Bars.** 37–40 (Fl 1) and 53–56 (violins).

**Problem.** Each bar of the first phrase turns around one note: F–G♭–F (37), D♭–C–D♭ (38), F–G♭–F (39, triplet), C–D♭–C (40). The range is C5–G♭5 and there is no leap after the pickup. The tune's identity rests on its rhythm (2 8 8 4 | 4 8 8 2), not its contour. The second phrase (41–44, 57–60), with the rising A♭–D♭–F arpeggio, is the part a listener will remember. In the flute's middle register, which Adler calls "sweet and with little carrying power" (PDF 185) and "the most nondescript part of the flute's range" (PDF 186), a contour this narrow is the hardest kind of line to hear clearly. Adler's own example in that register, Tchaikovsky PC1/ii (Ex. VII-5), opens with a rising fourth and sixth before it turns.

**Why not applied.** Theme B 37–60 is protected (c121), its accompaniment is frozen (c124), and 53–56 repeats the phrase under the protected horn counter-line. The accompaniment is thin enough (pp strings sul tasto, pizzicato, harp p; Adler PDF 186), so the phrase is audible. If the composer ever reopens theme B, the place to start is bar 39: a leap (for example G♭5 approached from B♭4) instead of a third turn would give the phrase one contour point. I do not propose it at cycle 131.

### 3. No woodwind other than the cor anglais plays a whole theme (observation, recorded again)

Adler presents the oboe and clarinet as lyric soloists (PDF 198–199, 210–211), and his cor anglais list includes Dvořák 9/ii mm. 7–18 (PDF 206). In this movement the woodwinds have phrases: Fl 1 four bars of B, the cor anglais four bars of B and the quotation, Ob 1 two-bar answers and B's three-bar minor head, and Cl 1 the coda tail. c041 raised this in the first pass. Since then every passage where a woodwind could take a full theme (15–22, 23–30, 71–82, 83–92) has been protected for other reasons, and the colour plan works as it stands. I record it only so the point is not raised as new.

### 4. Cor anglais at 32–36: register checked (no change)

At 32–34 the cor anglais holds written G5 (concert C5) at pp. In Ex. VII-47 that note is the top of the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band; "thin, pinched" starts above it. The line is not in the register where Adler says the cor anglais "loses its personality" (PDF 205). c111's placement stands.
