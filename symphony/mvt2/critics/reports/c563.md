### 1. Dvořák 9/ii also has a fluid stepwise transition, but this score's joins should stay pedals (record; adds to c143 and c433 Finding 3)

**Bars:** 31–36, 65–70, 83–86; for comparison, 53–56 and 61–65.

**Evidence.** c433 justified the pedal joins with Dvořák 9/ii 36–45, a held D♭. The same movement has the other technique as well. In the MCTL edition, bars 72–76 are scored for strings alone in three real voices: Vln I E5–E♭5–D5 | E♭5–D♭5 | D♭5–C5–A4 | A4–A♭4–G4; Vln II C5–A4 | B4–G4–B♭4 | G♭4–A4–E4 | E♭4; violas A4–G♭4 | G4–G♭4–E4 | E♭4–D♭4 | C4. This line descends chromatically from the woodwind peak at 71 to the A♭1 pedal at 77, and the cellos enter on C3 only at 76. That is the Unit 18 transition: a fluid descent with no harmonic goals between two stable points (PDF 290–291), set in three voices (PDF 302 note). Dvořák places it inside the middle section and keeps his pedals for the outer joins. This score does the same thing in the same places. Its fluid stepwise passage is the build at 53–56, and its three joins hold pedals (A♭ at 31–36, E♭ at 65–70, A♭ at 83–86). After the climax, 61–65 moves by a different means: the F♭/E–E♭ thread over a bass of D♭–G♭–E–A–D–E–E♭. Applying Unit 18 to the joins as well would remove the contrast between motion and arrival that PDF 290 describes. The 65–70 join also carries the horn's motto tail, which is a cyclic rule.

**Fix.** None. The new corpus evidence supports c143 and c433.

### 2. The basses' A1–B♭1 at 56 (c527) strengthens the articulated end of the 53–56 run (record)

**Bars:** 53–56.

**Evidence.** PDF 291 (Ex. 18-3a) says only the first and last chords of a 6/3 run are articulated. Since c527 the basses play A1–B♭1 at 56, under the cellos' A2–B♭2. The run therefore starts on the cellos' D♭3 alone at 53 b1 (p) and ends on B♭ minor at 56 b3 (mf), with the bass doubled at the lower octave and the cresc. reaching its top level. The chords in between keep a single bass line (Vc C3, B♭2, G♭2, E♭2, C2). The run is still three real voices with harp fills (PDF 302). Two leading tones occur: C3 at 53 b3 (V6/5) and A1/A2 at 56 b1 (vii°7 of ii). Each is in the bass line only and moves up by half step (PDF 292). The inner voices at 56 move G♭3–F3, C4–D♭4 and C5–B♭4.

**Fix.** None. The c527 change agrees with the reading.

### 3. Where the 6/3 chords fall matches the tension curve (record; refines c293's count)

**Bars:** whole movement.

**Evidence.** Counting beats with a complete first-inversion triad in score.midi gives: intro 0; aOne 11 (11, 15, 18, 19, 20, 27, 28); transition 1 (34 b1, which is the "VI6" = I case); B 11 (38, 39, 41, 43, 44, 49, 53, 54, 57, 59); retransition 0; A′ 2 (75, 78); coda 3 (87). The fluid chords therefore sit in the b-phrase of A (15–22, the move to F minor and the half cadence) and in the B section up to the climax. None sits on the pedal stretches, and A′ keeps only the I6–IV neighbour at 75 and the passing chord at 78 b3. PDF 290–291 asks for exactly this split: 6/3s where the music moves, 5/3s where it arrives. The b-phrase opens on a whole bar of I6 (15), which expands the tonic as the opening I6 of the Mendelssohn does (PDF 291: "the I6 that begins the series is important as an expansion of the initial tonic").

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Doublings in the 6/3 chords changed since c433 are correct (record)

**Bars:** 11, 39 b4, 72, 74.

**Evidence.** At 11 b2 (I6 as a neighbour to IV, PDF 296), the bass C is doubled (harp C2/C3, B.cl C3) and bassoons 1·2 share E♭3. C is not a tendency tone in I6, so the doubling is allowed under PDF 292. Since c562, bassoon 2 moves E♭3–D♭3 against the rising bass instead of moving in unison with the bass clarinet. At 39 b4 (c472, Vln II E♭4) the pizzicato bass is silent, and D♭–E♭–G♭ sounds as an incomplete ii7 on a weak beat; no 6/3 rule applies. The changes at 72 (Cl 2 A♭4) and 74 (the cellos' 9–8 over B♭1, Cl 2's tied E♭4, the viola triplet) concern a 7th chord and a 6/4 chord, which belong to Units 19 and 21. None of them doubles a leading tone or creates parallels with the bass, and analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Mozart 41/ii uses a chromatic passing-6/3 bass where this score uses a chromatic rise (record; comparison only)

**Bars:** 19–22.

**Evidence.** In Mozart_Sym41_mvt2 bars 15–16 the bass falls D3–C♯3–B2–B♭2 under B♭/D, an incomplete C♯ diminished chord, G/B and a diminished chord over B♭. These are passing 6/3s, altered to applied leading-tone chords on degrees other than the tonic, as PDF 297 and Ex. 18-10 describe. The bass leads to V of C at 17–18 and C minor at 19. At the corresponding point, the move to the half cadence, this score goes the other way. At 19–20 it expands F minor with 5–6 over F (VI6 at 19 b2 and 20 b2, Ex. 18-19d/e) and a neighbouring vii°6 at 19 b3 (Ex. 18-9; G is doubled, the 2nd degree, which is correct). The bass then rises D♭2–E♭2–E2–E♭2 at 21–22. Both approaches follow the reading. The rising chromatic bass at 21 is the chromatic voice exchange that c557 protected, and it gives the b-phrase the upward pull that a slow movement's first half cadence needs. Swapping in a falling 6/3 bass would duplicate the descent at 53–56.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. 28 b2 is the only pure ii6 among the three statements of theme A (record; protected)

**Bars:** 12 b2, 28 b2, 76 b2.

**Evidence.** At 12 b2 and 76 b2 the chord is D♭–F–A♭–B♭: IV with the B♭ appoggiatura sounding against a held A♭. At 28 b2 the A♭ is missing (D♭3/B♭5: D♭–F–B♭), so the chord is a plain ii6 6/3. The B♭ then sounds as a chord tone, and the dissonance arrives only at 28 b3 (ii7, A♭5 over B♭2). Under Ex. 18-19f/g this is a 6/3 standing in for the 5/3 on IV. It is milder than the other two statements, and the reprise at 76 restores the sharper clash. This matches "23–30 plain first statement" on the do-not-touch list (c007 and later).

**Fix.** None. I record it so that a later critic does not take it for an accidental omission.
