## Findings

No change is applied. I made no edits in /tmp/critic-337/work and did not run a build. Every place where the reading points to a possible change is inside a protected passage, and in each case I give the protected reason below.

**1. The quotation at 63-64 is a single brass line over a held chord. Its model sets it against a second theme in the brass. (Recorded, no change.)**
Bars 63-64. Tbn 1 plays movement I's first-theme head ff over a string tremolo A-minor chord that falls sf>p, with B.tbn on A2. Hn 1 rests from 61 to 66, Hn 3·4 rest at 63-64 and the trumpets are tacet. The plan names Dvořák 9/ii as the model. In that movement, at 96-98, the trombones play movement I's theme (`cs8. e16 a cs8. e4.`) twice, at bar 96 b1 and bar 97 b3. Against them the trumpets play the Largo's own head (`cs8. e16 e4 | cs8. b16 a4`) and the horns a third figure (brass.notesL.ily, trumpetImL and tromboneImL after `\barNumberCheck#FourmL`). This is the reading's "freer contrapuntal writing" (PDF 41) and its independent trombones against trumpets (PDF 44, Ex. XI-25), placed at the point this score copies. Keeping the quotation alone is still sound. c158 protects "a single ff statement in A minor", c303 sets the solo off by reducing forces after the tutti (Adler PDF 267), the trumpets' silence is a cyclic decision, and each quotation is heard once. A counter-line would keep all of those, but it would compete with the quotation, and c300's MIDI fades were made to keep the quotation clear. I recommend no change. The difference from the model is recorded so that the next holistic critic does not have to work it out again.
Optional, untested, not recommended: a soft counter-line in the only free brass, Hn 3·4 a2 (two horns = one trombone, PDF 44). It enters after the trombone's head and avoids doubling the E7 seventh that Vln II holds at 64 b4:
`bSecHnThree`/`bSecHnFour`, bars 63-64: `r2 r4 e'4~(\p | e'4 c'4 d'4) r4` (E4 over i, C4 over i, D4 on the iiø6/5 bass D).

**2. The climax at 61-62 uses sustained brass. The reading and Dvořák 7/ii use brass punctuation. (Recorded, no change.)**
Bars 61-62. All brass play tenuto halves and wholes. Adler calls punctuation of a theme carried by another instrument "a most common use of the brasses" (PDF 42; the * in Ex. XI-23, PDF 44). Dvořák uses it in a Romantic slow movement: at 7/ii, bars 39-42, horns play repeated sixteenth pairs (`ees16-> ees-> r8 r8 ees`) between the flutes' accented melody notes (Corno_I_II.ly after Mark B). Here the climax is a held sonority with viola tremolo, and the tenuto brass, the Hn 3·4 a2 and the close-order brass chord are protected (c264, c216 list). The sustained layout suits ♩=54 Largamente better than sixteenth punctuation would. Dvořák's punctuation comes in a passage that moves forward, and this one broadens. No change.

**3. Horn weights match the "two horns = one trombone" rule. (Follows.)**
At 61-62 the single horns 1 and 2 are marked ff, the Hn 3·4 a2 f and Tbn 2 and B.tbn f. The unison pair is given a trombone's weight and each single horn is marked one level up. At 79-81 Hn 1·2 play the counter-melody a2 at f inside an ff tutti with no trombones (no trumpets or trombones at 79 is protected, c029's list). By the PDF 44 equation the pair stands in for the missing trombone and doubles Bsn 1 and the cellos. The f marking was settled at c088/c218 and should not be re-raised.

**4. There is freer two-strand counterpoint inside the brass, in small amounts. (Follows.)**
PDF 41 limits contrapuntal brass to imitation and "freer contrapuntal writing". The score has the second kind in these places:
- 3-6 and 29-30: Hn 3 moves C–D♭–C and D♭–C over Hn 4's held A♭3 and E♭3 (oblique motion).
- 61-62: Tbn 2 has its own line, F–A♭ | B♭–A♭, under the horns' F–G♭–A♭.
- 64: Tbn 1's tail A–G♯–A–F–E against B.tbn's A2–D2–E2. The two meet in contrary motion at b4 and end on an octave E.
- 80-81: Hn 1·2 rise A♭–B♭–D♭ against Hn 3's chromatic F–F♭–E♭, in contrary motion at 81 b2.
There is no imitation. c177's reason ("dialogue and counter-melody instead of imitation, as Dvořák 9/ii") still holds, because every imitative example in the reading is a fast fugato (Hindemith, Bartók). The Hn 1 counter-melody at 53-56 partly fills the violins' held notes: at 55 the horn holds B♭4 while the violins move, though both hold at 54 b3-4. That is the dialogue c177 describes. No change.

**5. The tuba and contrabassoon have almost nothing to play. Dvořák's Largo does the same. (Recorded, no change.)**
Adler praises Schoenberg for giving "very significant parts to previously neglected members of the orchestra, such as the tuba, double bass, contrabassoon" (PDF 44). In this score the tuba plays four notes, all doubling the bass at 60-62, and the contrabassoon ten. The basses are well used, with pizzicato and arco lines at 37-46 and 64 and the pedal at 65-70. Dvořák 9/ii gives its tuba only bars 1-4 and the returning chorale near the end (tubamL), so the score follows its model. The tuba also cannot be given melodic weight without breaking the protected brass economy (trombones only at 60-64). No change.

**6. Every brass line sits in a good register. (Follows.)**
These are the reading's conditions for a line to "come through clearly every time it is played" (PDF 42):
- Hn 1's theme at 7-14 and the motto tails at 8 and 67-68: concert A♭3–B♭4, written E♭4–F5.
- The counter-melody at 53-60: A♭3–B♭4.
- Hn 2 at 75-77: E♭3–D♭4, at p and doubled by Bsn 1 and the cellos.
- Hn 1·2 at 79-81: A♭3–D♭4.
- Tbn 1's quotation: E3–C4, the trombone's strong register (c328).
- The lowest brass note is tuba E1 at 62, at f.
No line has to be carried in a weak register. No change.

**7. The sf>p introduction and the muted-horn backgrounds work as in the Gershwin example. (Follows.)**
Ex. XI-27 (PDF 51) introduces a solo with muted horn chords sf>p, then puts clarinets and bass clarinet in counterpoint over a held cello note. Three places in the score match:
- At 63 the strings' and B.tbn's sf> clears the space for Tbn 1. The device is the same, though the brass is open.
- At 31-36 B.cl., C.a. and Bsn move over the A♭ pedal.
- At 87-90 C.a. and violas move over muted Hn 3·4.
The mute instructions (82 "mettere sord.", 87 "con sord.", 91 "via sord.") and the niente hairpins at 3, 82 and 90-92 are marked as fully as PDF 50-52 asks. No change.
