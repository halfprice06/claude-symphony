## What else I studied

**Mahler, Adagietto.** The local corpus has nothing after 1895 (reference-corpus README). I did not download the IMSLP score. I worked from my knowledge of the score and from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._5_(Mahler) and https://www.h2g2.com/edited_entry/A14611394 (scoring for strings and harp; "Sehr langsam"; bar 2 molto rit., bar 3 a tempo). The Adagietto is about 103 bars in F major, ABA′. The harp plays in the outer sections and is silent in the more fluid middle section, so its return marks the reprise. The melody grows from long appoggiaturas over slow harmony. The highest and loudest point comes late in the reprise and falls away quickly to a soft close.

**Verdict.** In plan, this movement is close to the Adagietto, and in several places it is closer than to the Dvořák model. Both reprises carry the theme in the violins in octaves with a cantabile cello counter-melody, and in both the strongest climax comes late in the reprise (here 79, about 85% of the way through). The peak is an appoggiatura (B♭6 to A♭6), released in three bars (79–82). Both codas thin to a tonic pedal with the harp heard alone. Where Mahler keeps one colour family throughout and saves his one change (the harp), this score changes colour every few bars: horn, oboe/solo violin, muted violins, bass clarinet, flute, cor anglais, oboe, violins, tutti. RK PDF 116 and 118–119 give the reason for that, and it is right for a movement in a full-orchestra symphony. A conductor would take the voicing, balance, doubling and releases as professional. Every voicing I checked against the reading's rules passes. What still separates it from Mahler is not scoring but pace. It runs about 5¾ minutes, against about 9–11 for the Adagietto, and the phrases are regular 4+4 groups (except 45–47), where Mahler stretches his. Proportions were confirmed at c023 and c093/c103, so I make no proposal.

**Against v119.** I diffed every file. The v120–v129 changes are: timpani roll removed at 89–92 (c120); cellos sul D 44–47 (c121); basses/harp G♭1 at 43 and Hn 4 E♭3 at 81 (c122); Cl 2 F4 at 79, timpani rest at 82, Fl 1 p< at 77 (c123); violas at 84 b3, cellos pp–mp 44–47, clarinets mp< at 77 (c124); engraving (c125); wind slurs (c126); violas and harp off the counter-melody at 25–28 and 74–78 (c127); markings (c128). I checked each for harm. The only thinning is the harp RH at 76–78, now two-note rolls, and it is still harmonically complete with the violas (78 b3 is iii6, so the G3–E♭4 fits). **Nothing got worse.**

**Voice-leading scan.** allpairs_par.py reports 0 fifths. Its octaves are all doublings. I also ran my own scan of every rank of every track, chords and divisi included, keeping only isolated parallel motions (/tmp/critic-129/tools/scan.py). It found no fifths. Every octave it found is an intended doubling (bass with harp LH or Cbsn, Vln I/II pickups, Fl/Ob/Cl octaves on the theme). The only non-doubling pair, Vln II/Vla F♭→C at 62–63, is a common-tone chord change (F♭ = E stays), not a moving parallel.

## Findings

### 1. No new music change is needed; the score passes the reading (no change)

**Problem.** None found. The 25 principles above were checked bar by bar. The only partial verdicts are protected passages (31–36, 61–62) with reasons on record.

**Evidence.** See the table. In particular, the brass rules of PDF 100–104 hold at 60–62 since c119, and the woodwind-above-brass layering of PDF 110–111 holds at 61–62. The release order of Ex. 153 and Ex. 168 (woodwind first, horns and low strings last) holds at 81–83, and the Ex. 209 model of an ff slow-movement climax followed by a solo a tempo holds at 79–85.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Theme A's reprise and the Adagietto's: keep the string-led A′ (confirmation)

**Problem.** A conductor who knows the Adagietto would compare 71–82 with Mahler's reprise. The match is strong: the theme in violin octaves, cello cantabile counter-melody, the harp back on the bass, the build by adding groups (Cl 2/Bsn 2 from 71, Fl 1/Cl 1 at 77, Hn 1·2 at 78, tutti at 79).

**Evidence.** RK PDF 119 and Ex. 196–198 (PDF 361–363, *Kitesh*): the same theme heard three times, each time with more instruments. RK Ex. 171 (PDF 342): the Antar Adagio melody on Vln I sul A over held con sord. strings and a harp arpeggio, the nearest example in the reading to Mahler's texture.

**Fix.** None. This is a reason to keep the c107/c127 protections on 71–82.

### 3. Bars 61–62: the brass carry moving inner parts (record only)

**Problem.** In the climax chord the brass parts move every half note: Hn 3·4 a2 A♭4–B♭4–C♭5, Hn 2 F4–G♭4–A♭4, Tbn 2 F3–A♭3–B♭3–C♭4. Meanwhile Ob 1 and Ob 2 hold F5/D♭5 for most of 61.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 108: "In a chain of consecutive chords it is advisable to entrust the stationary parts to the brass, the moving parts to the wood-wind." Ex. 209 (PDF 375) holds the horns on sustained harmony while the woodwind carry the moving line.

**Fix (not applied).** The literal fix would be to swap the moving inner line (Hn 3·4) with the oboes' held notes. The Hn 3·4 a2 line at 61–62 was settled after c064, c069 and c086, and the passage sounds as a block of half notes at Largamente, not as a fast chain. The point is recorded so it is not raised again. Do not change.

### 4. Bars 48–52: Vln II held above the cor anglais quotation (confirmation)

**Problem.** A critic might object that Vln II at F♯5–G5 sits above a soft middle-register solo (cor anglais C♯4–D5). It also leaves a two-octave space between the bass (B1–B2) and the solo, filled only by the violas' off-beat eighths.

**Evidence.** RK Ex. 175a (PDF 345) does the same: pp strings hold their chord above a soprano recitative in the low and middle register, and PDF 117–118 explains that brass chords there would cover the voice and pizz. or bassoon basses would sound comic. PDF 125 allows wide spacing when the effect is soft and deliberate. The c054 placement follows the reading.

**Fix.** None. 37–60 stays under c124's rule.

### 5. Bars 87–88: muted horns with the cor anglais (confirmation)

**Evidence.** RK PDF 107 and 110, Ex. 149–151 (PDF 321–323: *Tsar Saltan*, muted trumpets with oboes and cor anglais; *Antar*, horns stopped with oboes, cor anglais and bassoons) and Ex. 164 (PDF 334). This is the colour combination Rimsky-Korsakov singles out. The violas sul C doubling horn 4 is his "splendid effect of horns doubled by divided violas" (PDF 113).

**Fix.** None.
