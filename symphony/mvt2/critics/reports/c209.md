## Findings

The appendix confirms the scoring; I found no chord that departs from its rules. I ranked the findings by what most separates the score from Brahms's movement, but none of them calls for a change in the notes.

### 1. Bars 61, 62, 79–81: the tutti chords checked group by group (no change)

MIDI check at 61 b1: woodwind D♭1 (Cbsn), D♭3 (B.cl., Bsn 2), F3 (Bsn 1), A♭4 (Cl 2), D♭5 (Ob 2), F5 (Ob 1, Cl 1), A♭5 (Fl 2), A♭6 (Fl 1); brass D♭2 (tuba), D♭3 (B.tbn), F3 (Tbn 2), F4 (Hn 1·2), A♭4 (Hn 3·4); strings D♭2 (Cb), A♭3 (Vc), D♭4/F4 (Vla), A♭4 (Vln II), D♭6 (Vln I). Each group is a complete D♭ triad, the bottom octave is root only, and the brass is closed in the middle with its own root at the bottom. This is the layout of appendix chords 1–4 (PDF 505). At 62 b3 (F♭ = E major) the three groups are again complete, and the timpani are silent because no drum is on E, as in the tables, where the drums only roll on chord tones. At 79 b1 the strings hold only D♭ and B♭ because all three upper string parts carry the melody (with the violas, C.a.) and the cellos the counter-melody. The chord's A♭ comes only from Hn 3·4 (A♭3), which is correct for a 6–5 appoggiatura (B♭ to A♭ at 80, c181): the note of resolution is not doubled above. The appendix shows single chords; ch. IV (PDF 116) says to judge them in context, and Ex. 209's slow-movement climax (c114 row 10) uses the same melody-in-strings layout.

### 2. Every beat: spacing scan (no change)

`scan.py` finds no close interval below C3 and no hole above C4 inside a harmony. The low thirds it lists are at C3–E♭3/D♭3–F3 (7–14 bassoons and B.cl. under the horn solo, 27, 37, 61 trombone/bassoon F3 over D♭3, 71, 75, 88 muted horns). Appendix chords 1, 8 and 13 put trombone or bassoon thirds in exactly this octave, so these are within the rule (the "bad effect" register of PDF 124 is one octave lower, as c114 found). The gaps over an octave (27–28, 41–44, 49, 56, 76) are between a melody and its accompaniment, not inside a chord; c182 recorded 27–28 and 75–76.

### 3. Holistic comparison with Brahms 2/ii (no change)

Points where this movement matches Brahms's practice:
- Melody with a counter-line in another family from the start: Brahms's cellos with a bassoon counter-melody (bars 1–12); here the horn with bassoons and bass clarinet at 7–14 and the violins with the cello counter-melody at 23–30.
- The horn as a leading voice after the first theme (Brahms's horn-led bridge); here the horn carries theme A at 7, the counter-line at 53–56 and the motto tail at 67–70.
- A contrasting middle section marked by a new metre and key (Brahms bar 33, a new metre, F♯ major; here 4/4 Poco più mosso in D♭ at 37).
- A reprise that is re-scored and re-shaped rather than repeated (Brahms's highly modified reprise; here new viola figure, cello counter-melody, Cl/Bsn/horn entries and a new peak at 79).
- A coda that returns to the main theme's head (Brahms's coda; here the harp's theme head at 83 and the motto tail).

Where Brahms is ahead, the difference is in the material, not the scoring. Brahms derives his themes from each other ("developing variation"), so the second theme grows out of the first. Here theme A and theme B are separate tunes; the motto tail at 42 and 58 and the horn counter-line from theme A's head at 53–56 are the only links. Brahms's middle and later sections also break up the main theme into motifs, but this movement does not take its themes apart, which is closer to Dvořák 9/ii (the model in BRIEF.md). That is a design choice that comes from the model, and the 92-bar, 5¾-minute proportions could not hold a Brahms-type development without a new section. I do not recommend a change. A conductor would find nothing in the scoring that gives the composer away; the tutti and soft chords are laid out as in Rimsky-Korsakov's tables.

### 4. Bars 79–81: the second peak is a partial tutti (no change; recorded choice c024, c114)

Appendix chords 1–12 and 17–20 all include trombones and tuba at f or above. The A′ peak at 79 has no trombones, tuba or trumpets, and the horns play f. c024 recorded this as a choice and c114 classed it as a partial tutti (PDF 119). Ch. IV (PDF 136) supports it: brass that stays silent gains force when it returns, and the trombones' one return (60–64) is the movement's main climax and the quotation. Keeping 79 without trombones keeps 61 the larger peak. I agree; nothing to change.

### 5. Bars 3–6, 31–36, 65–70, 87–88: soft wind chords against chords 13, 14, 16 (no change)

Chords 13 (Mlada), 14 (The Tsar's Bride) and 16 (Christmas Night) are pp chords for woodwind and brass with no strings, or with only the basses (16). Bars 5–6 (B.cl. A♭2, Cl 2 E♭3, Hn 3·4 A♭3/C4, pp), 31–36 (woodwind choir with Cbsn), 67–70 (horn over the basses' E♭ and the timpani roll) and 87–88 (C.a., Cl 1 and muted Hn 3·4 with violas) follow this: close spacing, a reed or bass clarinet on the bass of the wind group, and the timpani roll pp on the root when they play. Chord 16's pp trombones and tuba show that a soft low-brass chord is possible, but the cyclic rules forbid a trombone chorale, and the movement's single low-brass entry at 60–64 would lose its weight. No change.
