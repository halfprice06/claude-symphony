## Findings

### 1. No rehearsal letter in bars 23-36; parts carry a 12-bar rest with no landmark (applied)

**Bars:** 31 (C at 23, D at 37).

**Problem.** The letters fall every 6-8 bars except at 23-36, where 14 bars pass with no letter. Bar 31 is a section start (the transition, `trans` in sections.txt). The strings stop there, and the bass-clarinet solo over the A♭ pedal begins on a new system. Because no letter marks it, the parts show long unbroken rests across it: for example, Oboe 1 reads "23-34 12" and then "35-36 2". At the Adagio's ♩ = 52 a 12-bar rest lasts about 42 seconds. A player who loses count has no reference until 37. In rehearsal the conductor can only say "bar 31", and most part lines do not show that number.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 299, item 4: "Be sure to include rehearsal numbers or letters in both score and parts... Make certain that every player has detailed information about what is happening every moment in the performance, whether he is playing or not." Ex. XVIII-5 shows the intended spacing: numbers every ten bars, so a sixteen-bar rest is read as 9 + 7, never as one block. Ex. XVIII-6 breaks the rest again at each metre change. This score follows Ex. XVIII-6 at every metre and key change. Bars 23-36 are the one place where it does not follow Ex. XVIII-5. Ex. XVIII-3b (PDF 296) shows Mahler placing a number at a texture change inside a reduced page ("20" at the "Etwas gemächlicher" system).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-425/work).** global.ily, before the 31-36 spacer:
```
  % 31-36: bass clarinet over an A flat pedal, to D flat
  \mark \default % D 31 (c425: Adler part 2 PDF 299 item 4, Ex. XVIII-5)
  s2.*4 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco rit." ...
```
The letters after it move up one: D 37 becomes E, E 45 → F, F 53 → G, G 61 → H, H 65 → J (I is skipped), J 71 → K, K 83 → L. Four hard-coded letter strings follow:
- sections/bSec.ily: `strMark "D"` → `strMark "E"` (37), `strMark "E"` → `strMark "F"` (45);
- sections/coda.ily: `strMark "K"` → `strMark "L"` (83);
- score.ly: `brassLetter "G"` → `brassLetter "H"` (61).

Bar 31 has no strings, so the letter prints only at the top, as A at 7 does. Result: the score stays 8 pages and no break moves (page 2 checked at 150 dpi; the letter at 31 fits above the C.a. staff). In the parts, Oboe 1 now reads 21-22 | C 23-30 | D 31-34 | 35-36, and all parts stay one page each.

**Cost.** The letters change from 37 onward. Nothing in HISTORY's do-not-touch lists or in coherence/*.md refers to movement II by letter: they all use bar numbers. The other movements do not quote movement II's letters, so no other thread needs to be told. HISTORY entries before c425 that name letters (for example "G/Largamente" in c225) refer to the old lettering. If the composer prefers not to reletter, the only alternative that matches the reading is to leave the score as it is and record that bar numbers on every bar make up for the missing letter.

### 2. Score set-up and parts against ch. 18 (recorded, no change)

Score order, choir spacing, brackets, barlines broken between choirs, key and time signatures, abbreviated names, reduced-score slashes and player labels at every system start all match PDF 292-296 and Ex. XVIII-1/3. The muta and mute instructions sit above the player's staff, with time to act: timp. at 5 and 92, Cl at 92, and Hn 3·4 "mettere sord." at 82 for con sord. at 87, four bars away. The parts follow PDF 299-301: rests are broken at letters and metre changes, there are no page turns except the harp's at a rest, the cues are small and named, and each shared staff becomes one part per player. The one exception is the letter gap in Finding 1.

### 3. Shared-staff dynamics (recorded, no change)

PDF 294 and Ex. XVIII-10a call for two sets of dynamics on a shared staff only when they differ. I checked every shared staff at 150-200 dpi, including Hn 3·4 at 80-81, where both horns have the same `\<` and `\>`. Each dynamic prints once, below the staff. Hn 3·4 at 1 shows two ppp because the horns enter on different beats (Hn 3 tied over from movement I, Hn 4 on beat 2). That follows the rule.

### 4. Appendix A ranges and terms (recorded, no change)

All notes lie inside the professional written ranges on PDF 302-307. c306 already confirmed this with a MIDI scan, and the score has not changed since. Bar 92's "muta" wording and "con sord."/"via sord." follow the PDF 312 table. Italian and English are mixed in "mettere sord." and "change bow at will", which earlier critics recorded as optional (c066, c156, c295). I do not raise it again.
