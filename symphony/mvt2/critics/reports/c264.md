## Findings

### 1. Bars 61–62: the brass chord at the climax has an empty octave in the middle. Divide horns 1·2 so the brass is in close order (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** These are the sounding brass pitches at the Largamente climax:

- 61 b1–2: D♭2 (tuba), D♭3 (B.tbn), F3 (Tbn 2), F4 (Hn 1·2 a2), A♭4 (Hn 3·4 a2).
- 62 b1–2: G♭1, G♭2, B♭3, G♭4, B♭4.
- 62 b3–4: F♭1, F♭2, A♭3, A♭4, C♭5.

The bass octave is correct. Above it, the three upper parts leave an empty octave (F3–F4), then a sixth (B♭3–G♭4), then another octave (A♭3–A♭4). Horns 1·2 double the third F a single octave above trombone 2, and the brass carries no D♭4 or C♭4. This is the loudest sustained brass chord in the movement, and these are the chords where PDF 114 says balance matters most.

**Evidence.** PDF 100 gives the first rule of brass harmony: "part writing should be of the close order with no empty spaces in the intervals". Every diagram on PDF 101–102 has the bass in octaves and the upper parts in close position. c194 made horn 1 play a2 with horn 2 so that every horn part is a pair, citing PDF 102 ("when the whole group is used the number of horns should be doubled"). The same reading qualifies that rule twice. PDF 103: "When chords of widely-divided harmony are distributed throughout several harmonic registers, the register occupied by the horns need not be doubled". The chord at 61 runs from D♭1 (Cbn) to A♭6 (Fl 1), so it is such a chord. PDF 103 also gives the remedy when the horns are not paired: mark them one degree louder ("The same method should be followed whenever the use of horns in pairs fails to produce satisfactory tone"). Earlier critics recorded the gap as filled by strings and Bsn 1 (c254, c259). By PDF 113, strings do not blend with brass except in tremolo, and of the strings only the violas are tremolo here. By PDF 108, a bassoon at ff is overwhelmed by horns.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-264/work/sections/bSec.ily, `bSecHnOne` line 119 and `bSecHnTwo` line 126).** Horn 1 keeps its line, F4 | G♭4 A♭4, including the G♭4→F4 resolution c194 wanted and c167's G♭–F seventh against Vc/Bsn 1. Horn 2 takes the missing middle notes. Both are marked ff, one degree above the single trombone, as on PDF 103:

```
% bSecHnOne, 61-62 (was \partCombineUnisono f'1--\f | ges'2-- aes'2-- | \partCombineAutomatic R1*2 |)
  \partCombineAutomatic f'1--\ff | ges'2-- aes'2-- | R1*2 |
% bSecHnTwo, 61-62 (was f'1--\f | ges'2-- aes'2-- | R1*2 |)
  des'1--\ff | des'2-- fes'2-- | R1*2 |
```

The brass now reads:

- 61 b1–2: D♭2 D♭3 F3 D♭4 F4 A♭4.
- 61 b3–4: A♭3 D♭4 F4 A♭4, fully close.
- 62 b1–2: B♭3 D♭4 G♭4 B♭4, fully close. Horn 2 holds D♭4 as the stationary part (PDF 108).
- 62 b3–4: A♭3 F♭4 A♭4 C♭5, with the top three in close position.

Horn 2 avoids C♭4 at 62 b3, which would make fifths with the bass G♭→F♭. It goes up to F♭4 instead, in contrary motion to the bass. That F♭4 joins Vc/Bsn 1 in unison, and its D♭4 joins the violas' tremolo D♭4, which is PDF 113's "horns doubled by divided violas" case. These stay as they were: horns 3·4 a2 on top (standing list), Tbn 2's F3–A♭3 | B♭3–A♭3 including c224's A♭3 at 62 b3, the tenuti, Tbn 1's silence before the quotation, and every other part. The Hn 1·2 staff prints the two horns as chords with equal ff. The c194 comment is kept and marked as superseded. This reverses c194's a2 for horns 1·2 only, and I answer its reason with PDF 103 above. No ending, exported theme or quotation is affected.

### 2. Bars 79 b3, 80 b3–81 b1, 81 b2–3: seconds inside the horn group (record, no change)

PDF 102–103: "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour." Three times at the A′ peak, the dissonant pair sits inside the horns. At 79 b3 and 80 b3–81 b1, horn 4's A♭3 (the seventh of ii-half-diminished) is a second below horns 1·2's B♭3. At 81 b2–3, horns 1·2's D♭4 (the seventh of V7) is a second below horn 3's E♭4. Each note belongs to a line that has to stay. Horn 4's A♭3 resolves to G3 at 81. Horn 3 has the F–F♭–E♭ line (c227's viola doubling). Horns 1·2 have the counter-melody with Bsn 1 and Vc. Every one of these seconds is also present between colours: B♭3 is in Bsn 1 and Vc, and E♭4 in C.a. and violas. Rimsky's word is "preferably", and none of the alternatives I tried keeps the three lines. Record.

### 3. Bar 60: brass entry (record)

At pp< the brass holds A♭1, A♭2, E♭3→G♭3 and A♭3. That is the root in three octaves, with no third. The bass octave in tuba and bass trombone follows PDF 101. Horn 4 at p over trombones at pp follows PDF 103. The seventh G♭3 (Tbn 2) against horn 4's A♭3 is a second split between colours, which follows PDF 102–103. The C is in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln I and Vln II, and the bar crescendos into finding 1's chord. c254 recorded the same layout, and this bar is part of the protected 53–61 build. No change.

### 4. Bars 63–64 and 87–90 follow the reading (record)

At 63–64, trombone 1's quotation sounds over tremolo strings with the bass trombone on the bass. This is PDF 113's first exception, and PDF 104's remark on the trombone's "ponderous tone" is respected because the trombone does not join a sustained octave. At 87–90, muted horns 3·4 with the cor anglais and divided violas match PDF 107 and 110 (muted horn and cor anglais in one chord, p) and PDF 113 (horns doubled by divided violas). Both passages are protected. Nothing to change.

### 5. Trumpets tacet (record)

Most of the brass diagrams on PDF 101–104 and 110–111 put trumpets at the top of the chord. Here the trumpets are held back on purpose for the symphony (c060, cyclic notes: their first entry since movement I comes in the scherzo). PDF 110–111 also let the oboes, clarinets and flutes take the harmony above the trombones and horns, and that is what 61–62 does. I do not propose bringing them in.
