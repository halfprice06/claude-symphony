## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 15–22: the sustained accompaniment does not share the dialogue's swells (applied)
**Problem.** The oboe (15–16, 19–20) and the solo violin (17–18, 21) each swell `p\<` to `\>` in their two-bar answers. Under them, Vln II (louré), the violas and the cellos hold pp for eight bars without moving. Only the solo line breathes. c038 fixed the same problem at 41–44, but this passage was not touched.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 149–150: without the expressive crescendo and diminuendo the sound is "lifeless", and that applies to the whole sound, not just the tune. PDF 155: a sustained accompaniment is a transparent legato, so the swell it takes should be smaller than the melody's. Dvořák 9/ii bars 13–14: the muted violins' held note takes the cor anglais' `< >`.
**Fix (applied, `aOne.ily`, Vln II only; pitches, louré and slurs unchanged).**
```
aes'2--(\pp\< aes'4--) | aes'2--(\> g'4--\!) | aes'2--(\< aes'4--) | aes'2--(\> f'4--\!) |
aes'2--(\< g'4--) | aes'2--(\> e'4--\!) | f'2--(\< f'4--\!) | bes2 es'8(\mp ...
```
Each hairpin starts and ends with the solo's hairpin, inside pp. The violas and cellos keep their answers flat, so the dialogue's middle voice stays in the background.

### 2. Bars 25–26 and 73–74: the cello counter-melody has no swell at its own arrival (applied)
**Problem.** The "cantabile" counter-melody rises A♭3–C4 at 25 (and at 73), while the violins relax from the motto peak at 24 (72). In both places the cellos have no hairpin from 23 to 26. The counter-melody's arrival on C4 is not shaped, so the two lines never trade the foreground.
**Evidence.** PDF 155: the lyrical legato "transitions ... into a gentle swell on the note of arrival". PDF 149–150: the shaping belongs to every expressive layer. The counter-melody is the melodic bass counterline that the plan asks for (movement-2-plan.md, "A, a′").
**Fix (applied, `aOne.ily` bar 25 and `aTwo.ily` bar 73; slurs and the c118 bowing unchanged).**
`... g4( | aes2\< c'4)\> | bes2 g4\! |`
The swell starts as the violins' diminuendo ends (25 b1) and closes before the crescendo at 27 (75), which is unchanged.

### 3. Hairpins that run into rests with no end level (applied)
**Problem.** Several diminuendos stop at a rest and have no target: strings at 3 (Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb) and timpani at 3; Vln II, Vla and Vc at 66; timpani at 81; Vln II, Vla, Ob 1, Hn 1·2 and Cbn at 82. Elsewhere the score marks the same gesture as niente with a circled tip (47, 81 Hn 3·4, 85, 90), so the notation is inconsistent. Separately, the violas' crescendo at 52 runs into four bars of rest.
**Evidence.** PDF 148–149: a hairpin is a crossfade between two named levels, and without an end it is undefined. Here the end is silence, so it should be written as niente. At 52 the crescendo needs an arrival before the rest.
**Fix (applied).** `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t` before each of those hairpins (`intro.ily`, `retr.ily`, `aTwo.ily`). It has to be a Staff override because this score engraves dynamics at Staff level (c038 note). At 52, Vla `\after 2. \p c'1\<` with the `\!` on `R1*4` removed (`bSec.ily`), so the violas arrive at p with the cellos and timpani. The coda Cl 1 at 88 is left alone because clarinet 2 holds on the same staff.

### 4. Bars 48–52: the quotation swells over a flat accompaniment (not applied, for the composer to decide)
**Problem.** The cor anglais quotation swells `d''2--\<` at 49 to mp at 50, then `\>` at 51 to p at 52. Vln II (held F♯, D), the violas' off-beat pulse and Vc/Cb all stay pp from 48 to 51. Fourteen identical soft off-beat attacks in the violas with no direction are the mechanical repetition PDF 150 and 152 describe.
**Evidence.** PDF 149–150, PDF 152, and Dvořák 9/ii 13–14 as in finding 1.
**Suggested fix.** Vla `r8 a8-_\< ...` at 49 b1 and `r8 a8-_\> ...` at 50 b3, ending `\!` on 51 b1; Vln II `fis''2\< g''4 eis''4\> | fis''2\! d''2`. These are dynamics only. I did not apply it, because c124 set the rule "no further accompaniment changes in 37–60". If that rule covers only scoring, the change fits it.

### 5. What already follows the reading (no change)
- Layered attack and sustain (PDF 146): `fp>` at 47 and `sf>` tremolo at 63 under Tbn 1.
- Written balance (PDF 290): every solo entry sits one level above its accompaniment (7, 31, 37, 41, 45, 47, 67). The trombone quotation stays ff while the strings fall to p at 64.
- Compression (PDF 290–291): the timpani rolls are marked one level lower than the music around them (ppp at 67–70 under a p horn, f at 79 under ff), which allows for the extra presence of a sustained roll.
- Words for what notation cannot hold (PDF 151–152): the expressive text is sparing and specific. The tempo plan (poco rit. / poco sostenuto 47–52, animando 53–59, poco allargando 60, Largamente 61, allargando 63–64, Tempo I 65, poco allargando 78, a tempo 83, rit. 89) has an `\mt` step for every printed word.
