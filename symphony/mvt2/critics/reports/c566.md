## Findings

The timpani part has had four passes on this chapter, and all but one of the 21 principles are followed or do not apply. There is one small finding, which I applied, and one open item to close.

### 1. Bar 4: damp the A♭ drum before it is pedalled to C at 5 (applied)

Bars 2-5. The 28" drum rolls A♭2 at 2-3 (`aes,2.:32\ppp~ | aes,2:32\>~ aes,8\! r8`). One bar later the muta at 5 asks "A♭ in C" of the same drum. Nothing tells the player to stop the head first. The cellos hold A♭2 to the end of 3, and the bass clarinet holds the same A♭2 pp from 3 to 6 (`introBassCl = { R2.*2 | aes,2.~\pp | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2 r4 | }`). A drum tuned to a sounding pitch keeps vibrating in sympathy with it, so when the pedal moves at 5 the head still sounds and slides up from A♭ to C. This happens in the string-free bars 4-6, where only the clarinets, bass clarinet and horns 3·4 play, at pp.

Adler says the part must show "how long a note may ring" (PDF 80), and that tuning changes are written over the rests (PDF 81, Ex. XII-51). The score already does this after every other last stroke that is followed by silence or a retuning: "damp" at 53, 62 b3 and 86 (c214, c146, c436). Bar 3 is the only place where a drum is retuned soon after it has been struck, and the only one without a damp.

Fix, applied in /tmp/critic-566/work/sections/intro.ily (introTimp):

```
... aes,2:32\>~ aes,8\! r8 | R2.^\markup \small \italic "damp" | R2.^\markup \small \column { "muta G in A♭, A♭ in C, C in D♭" "(A♭ C D♭ E♭, low to high)" } | R2. |
```

This is text only. The notes, the muta wording and its bar stay as they are, so c146's rejected move of the retuning does not come back. In the score "damp" prints over the timpani's bar 4 (page 1), level with the strings' "mettere sord.". In the part it prints over bar 4, which was already its own rest bar before the 5-6 multi-bar rest, so the part's line breaks do not change.

### 2. The c436 open item (muta of C and D♭ drums during 87-91): close it, keep the muta in the pause (no change)

c436 suggested retuning the C and D♭ drums in the rests before the end, leaving only A♭ and E♭ for the pause. Since c436 the item has been carried as "open, needs the scherzo thread" (c476, c484, c546). I checked the bars in question. At 87-88 the basses play F and then D♭ (`codaCb`: `f,,2.\pp "4 soli" | des,2.`), and Vln I's D♭-C is on top, so the D♭ drum would be pedalled off a pitch that is sounding. At 89-92 the D♭→B pedal passes through C, which is the top note of the last chord (Vln I C5), and the C→G pedal passes through A♭, which is the bass pedal. Both would happen in the quietest bars of the movement (ppp, rit.). Adler asks only that a change be marked when it has to be done quickly (PDF 81), and the 92 text does mark it. Four pedal changes in the pause between movements is ordinary work for a timpanist. The scherzo's first stroke is E2 on the 30" drum at III 3, which is one change; the other three drums are not needed until III 9. My recommendation is to close the item: keep the muta at 92. This also leaves the movement's ending text and the scherzo's quoted tuning ("retune in the pause: A♭ → E, C → G, D♭ → B, E♭ → E") unchanged.
