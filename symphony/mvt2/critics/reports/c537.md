## Findings

No finding is strong enough to change the score. Each item below is what the reading and the scans flagged, with the reason it stays. I list them so the next critic need not re-run the scans.

### 1. Economy: every duplicated line is a recorded doubling (no change)

**Scan result.** Duplications at the unison or octave occur only at: 23-29 Vln I = Vln II; 45-46 Ob 1 / Bsn 1 = Vc an octave lower; 53-56 Cl 1 = Hn 1 and Vln I = Vln II; 56 Cb an octave under Vc; 57-60 B.cl. = Bsn 2 (with Vc at 57), Fl/Ob/violins in octaves, Hn 1 / Bsn 1 / Vc on the counter-line; 61-62 tutti; 72-73 Bsn 2 an octave over Cb; 75-80 Bsn 1 = Hn 2 = Vc; 77 and 81 the theme in octaves with winds; 87 Hn 3·4 = Vla.

**Reading.** Sebesky PDF 12 asks that every note make "a legitimate difference". A&S PDF 84-85 separates doubling for weight or colour from a surplus part. Every item above is either weight on a theme (23, 53, 57, 71-82), the 16' bass (56, 72-73), the tutti (61-62), or a colour decision already argued and protected (c104 three-colour counter-melody; c157/c297 for 57; c287 for 87). Compared with c297's scan, the only new item is the Cb octave at 56 (c527), which is the bass line, not an added voice.

**Verdict.** Follows. Keep.

### 2. Ex. 1: chromatic inner lines in the lower-middle register (no change)

**Reading.** In Ex. 1 (PDF 12-13) Sebesky cut a chromatic trombone line because it thickened the lower-middle register and hid the contrary motion of the other voices.

**Check.** The movement's chromatic inner lines are listed in row 7. Each is motivic, not filler: the motto tail (8, 68), the F♭-E♭ thread (59-60, 80-81, protected by c042 and the key-plan entries), or the B.cl. bass of a passing chord (11). None lies in the primary line's register while that line sounds: at 8 the Bsn 1 A♭3-G3 sits a fifth and more below the horn's D4-D♭4, and at 59-60 the cellos and Bsn 2 move against the held melody, which is the contrary motion Ex. 1 wanted to expose.

**Verdict.** Follows. Keep.

### 3. Focus: the primary line is clear in every passage (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 13-14: one element is primary; the background must not be overbusy; levels may shift.

**Check.** I counted the layers sounding together. In soft passages there are at most four, except 41-44, which has five: C.a. melody, Fl 1 descant, viola pizz. triplets, cello line, and harp LH with pizz. basses. The descant enters on the third beat of 41 after the C.a. has begun, stays at pp to p a sixth to an octave above it, and the triplets and harp are tertiary (pizzicato, pp). Fl 1 at 41-44 is on the do-not-touch list (c090, c101; c511's change there was declined), and it is the one moving line over theme B in that passage. At 79-80 the counter-melody (Vc, Hn 1·2, Bsn 1) rises to the primary level under the held B♭6, which is the shift PDF 14 describes, and c037 made it the only quarter-note motion there.

**Verdict.** Follows. Recorded only.

### 4. Bars 45-46: the lower octave of theme B's minor head is heavier than the upper one (observation, no change)

**Scan.** Ob 1 plays the head at C♯5-E♭5; Bsn 1 and the cello section play it an octave lower, at pp. By player count the lower octave outweighs the lead. c067 removed Cl 1 from the lower octave at 71-74 on exactly this ground (Sebesky PDF 138, rule 2).

**Why it stays.** Here the octave is a two-colour duet (oboe over cellos and bassoon), named in the plan, with the cellos sul D and pp, and the oboe's reed tone in its strongest register. It is protected three ways: the 44-47 doubling plan (c104 list, line 1129), c124's "cellos pp-mp under Ob 1 at 44-47", and the c124 rule against accompaniment and dynamic changes in 37-60. I have no new argument against c124. If 37-60 is ever reopened, the only test I would make is Ob 1 marked p (one level above the cellos' pp), not a change of instruments.

### 5. Variety: colour and harmony (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 15-16 (timbre variety), PDF 19-20 (harmonic variety, Ex. 3/3A).

**Check.** No theme statement repeats the colour of the one before it (row 11). Harmonically, theme A's first phrase keeps its harmony at 7-9, 23-25 and 71-72; 73-74 and 78-81 differ. Ex. 3/3A reharmonise a melody over a new bass, and the motto tail does this (c450). Keeping theme A's harmony was decided at c057, c157 and c434 so that the return is recognised, and its colour changes each time instead.

**Verdict.** Partly follows Ex. 3; the kept harmony is a recorded decision. Keep.
