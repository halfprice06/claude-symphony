## Findings

### 1. Bar 74 b2: the accented 6/4 on 2̂ resolves for an eighth to a diminished VII6, and the viola strikes the melody's G4 (applied in scratch)

**Bars.** 74, violas (aTwo.ily, `aTwoVla`).

**What sounds in v502.** On 74 b1: B♭1/B♭2 (Cb, harp, Bsn), Vla E♭3, Vc C4 (the 9-8 suspension from 73, c469), Cl 2 E♭4 (tied from 73, c479), Vln II G4, Vln I G5. Over the bass this is a 6/4 with a 9th: the 4th (E♭) is doubled and the 6th is G. On b2 the expected motion follows in two voices: Cl 2 E♭4 → D♭4 (4-3) and Vc C4 → B♭3 (9-8). But the violas' figure goes E♭3–E♭4–G4, so on b2 the violas are on G4 and nobody holds E♭. The b2 sonority is B♭–D♭–G (Cb/Bsn/harp B♭, Vc B♭3, Cl 2 D♭4, Vla G4, Vln II G4, Vln I G5): VII6 with the G tripled. E♭ comes back only on b2½ in the violas.

**Why it is wrong by the reading.** c482 described b1 as "the V4/3 before its seventh enters"; in Unit 19's terms it is an accented 6/4 on 2̂: metrically strong, over a stationary bass, with its 4th resolving down by step (§3, PDF 306, Ex. 19-4 bar 2 shows the same thing on 6̂). The book's rule for accented 6/4s on degrees other than 5̂ is to avoid a "resolution" to a diminished 5/3 except in three-part texture, and its remedy on 7̂ is to keep a tone of the 6/4 so that the resolution is the dominant 6/5, not VII6 (PDF 306, Ex. 19-5a). The same applies here: the resolution should be V4/3 (B♭–D♭–E♭–G), not VII6 (B♭–D♭–G). The texture is seven voices, not three; the book says an incomplete dominant 6/4 shape belongs to three-part writing and a continuo player would fill it in (§17, PDF 321–322). The doubled 4th is also handled as the book requires only if one E♭ moves up or stays (PDF 316); in v502 the violas leave it by leaping to G4, so the 4th is resolved in one voice and abandoned in the other. Separately, the violas' G4 on b2 is the pitch Vln II is holding in the melody, in the same string colour. c324 removed exactly this fault at 75 b2 (viola A♭4 → A♭3) for the same reason.

**Fix (applied).**
```
% aTwoVla, bar 74 (was: es( es' g') es'( bes des'))
es( g es') es'( bes des') |
```
The violas now play E♭3–G3–E♭4 | E♭4–B♭3–D♭4. On b2 they hold the root E♭4 against Cl 2's D♭4, so the chord is a complete V4/3 from b2 (Vln II/Vln I G, Vla E♭4, Cl 2 D♭4, Vc B♭3, bass B♭). Of the two E♭s on b1, Cl 2's falls to D♭ and the violas' goes up and stays (PDF 316). The G3 on b1½ is the 6th of the 6/4 an octave below Vln II, not on its pitch. I rejected B♭3 for b1½ because it would sound the resolution tone of the cellos' suspension a 2nd below the C4 while the suspension is still held. The violas' b3–b3½ (B♭3–D♭4, the 7th that falls to C4 at 75, c060/c073) is unchanged.

**Protected-passage check.** HISTORY protects "the 74 b1 voicing around the cello 9-8 (no D♭ above the C4 suspension on the downbeat)" (c479) and "74's V4/3 voicing" (c482, which relied on Cl 2's 8-7 E♭4–D♭4 as the seventh's origin, A&S Ex. 4-15a). The fix keeps both: b1 is unchanged (Vla E♭3, Cl 2 E♭4, no D♭ on the downbeat), and Cl 2's 8-7 is untouched. It changes only the violas' b1½ and b2. The cello 9-8 (c469) and the reprise/first-statement difference at 26/74 are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 67–70: the retransition's passing 6/4 and the late resolution of the 7th match Ex. 19-14 (record, no change)

Hn 1 E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4–B♭3 over the E♭ pedal with Bsn 1 B♭3–A♭3–G3 below. The C4 with A♭3 on 68 b3 forms the passing 6/4; it does not resolve the 7th D♭ (PDF 312: the 6th of this 6/4 is a passing tone, not a goal). Bsn 2 restates D♭3 at 69–70 and resolves it to C3 at 71, the same pattern as the Mozart example where the 7th resolves bars later in another voice. Unchanged since c362.

### 5. Places where Unit 19 techniques could be added, and why none should be (record, no change)

- A beat-3 cadential 6/4 at the theme cadences (12, 28, 76, 80): rejected since c012; the melody holds the 3rd of V at 10, 26, 60 and 81, and the cadences are protected.
- An expanded cadential 6/4 (§10, Ex. 19-21/22) or an interpolation (§11) at the climax 79–81: the bar is a ♭VI neighbour to V with a hemiola (c048, c423), protected; adding a 6/4 would duplicate the delay the hemiola already gives.
- Schumann's I6/4 ending (Ex. 19-30b): ruled out by the cyclic plan, which needs a root-position A♭ chord with C5 on top for the scherzo join and the finale.
- Oscillating 6/4s (§15): wrong style for this Adagio.
