**Problem.** The other three movement threads read coherence/mvt2.md as the live description of this movement. Since v426 (c425) the rehearsal letters are A–L without I, with D at 31 and L at 83. Three statements in mvt2.md were not updated:

- Key-plan section, line 107: "There is no rehearsal mark at 31 or 47. The marks are A–K without I." There is now a mark at 31 (D), and the marks run A–L.

The section table in the same file already gives the new letters (D at 31 ... L at 83), so the file now contradicts itself.

**Evidence.** Adler ch. 1 (adler1 PDF 22) says that once every note, chord, timbre and nuance is part of the composition, the practice has to be written down so that others can use it. Here the written description is what the scherzo and finale threads use, and it should match the score. None of the other coherence files refers to movement II by letter (I searched scherzo.md, mvt1.md and finale.md), so no other thread is misled yet. This is the reason for leaving it to the end-of-series update rather than treating it as urgent.

### 2. Bar 89, the last C5 of the motto tail: hand-over kept (considered, no change)

**What I checked.** In Adler's Ex. XV-37 (PDF 191) each instrument enters on the held C while the previous one still sounds it. At 89 Cl 1 arrives on C5 (slurred from D♭5) at the same moment as Vln I attacks C5 up-bow pp sul D. So the two sounds start together and do not overlap in Adler's sense. I asked whether Vln I should enter one beat later, so that the clarinet's arrival is heard alone first.

**Why I keep it.** (a) The clarinet does not hold the C5 before the violin comes in. The C5 is the tail's goal note, and it arrives on the downbeat together with the tonic bass (I at 89). A later violin entry would fall on a weak beat inside the rit. and would be an audible new attack. The pp up-bow on the downbeat is hidden under the clarinet's slurred arrival and the bass change. Ex. XV-40 (PDF 192) asks that the seam not show, and here it does not. (b) Cl 1 holds the C5 into 90 b2 under the violin, so the two colours do overlap for four beats. That is Ex. XV-37's mechanism, timed from the arrival instead of before it. (c) The Vln I coda entry and bowing at 89–92 is protected (c019, c085, c256) and answers bar 1 at the same pitch on the same string. No change.

### 3. Cyclic and emotional arc: holds (no change)

- **Link from movement I.** Bar 1 continues I's last chord in five parts. The Hn 3/Hn 4 C4 hand-over overlaps by one beat (Hn 3 `c'2`, Hn 4 `r4 c'2~`), which is Ex. XV-37's method. The strings fade to niente at 3 while the wind chord enters pp (Ex. XV-36, PDF 190).
- **Motto tail.** It appears six times, each in a new colour and register (Hn 1, violins, B.cl. at movement I's pitch, Hn 1 alone, violins, Cl 1). This follows Adler's rule that the register sets the instrument (PDF 188).
- **The two quotations.** The C.a. in its middle register at p (47–50) and Tbn 1 ff marcato in the trombone's strong middle octave (63–64) are each heard once. Each enters after its instrument's rest, and the trombone's first sound in the movement is the quotation.
- **Placement between I and III.** The coda ends on a plain A♭ triad with C5 on top, which is movement I's last pitch. Vln II's A♭4 becomes the scherzo's G♯4, and the timpani muta prepares E G B E. The finale's German sixth on A♭ resolves the plain triad.
- **Reserved sounds.** The rules from cyclic-notes.md hold: no tam-tam, no trombone chorale, no C-major material after bar 1, and no trumpets (Adler PDF 21's trumpet–timpani pair is left to the finale).
