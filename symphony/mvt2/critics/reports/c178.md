## Findings

### 1. Bar 77: flute 1 enters on G6 at p, the first note of Adler's "a bit shrill" register (applied)

**Problem.** In the A′ build, Fl 1 enters at 77 with `g'''4(\p\<` and doubles Vln I. The clarinets enter in the same bar at mp< (c124), and the violins have been crescendoing from mp since 75. Flute 1 is the only entering wind marked p.

**Evidence.** In Ex. VII-2 (PDF 185, read at 300 dpi), the "a bit shrill" bracket begins on G6. PDF 185 says of such registers that "in most cases the performer can only play extremely loud" and that "too often these registral peculiarities are ignored by inexperienced orchestrators". c123 chose p because the flute G6 "speaks easily at p", and it cited Rimsky PDF 127: a new group enters softer and crescendos faster. Adler's chart answers the first point: a G6 entry marked p is a dynamic the register does not reliably give. Rimsky's rule still holds at mp, because the violins are past mp by 77 (mp< at 75, f at 78). With mp, all three wind entries in the bar carry the same printed level, and the flute still enters under the strings.

**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily, `aTwoFluteOne`).** `R2.*2 | g'''4(\mp\< es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 | aes''2)\f \breathe ...`. Flute 2 rests at 77, so the shared staff has one dynamic. This revises c123's dynamic but keeps its principle.

### 2. Bar 3: the clarinet's niente attack for the wind takeover (suggestion, not applied; link protected)

**Problem.** At 3 the strings start their niente release. Cl 2 (E♭3) and the bass clarinet (A♭2) enter on the downbeat at pp and take over the chord.

**Evidence.** PDF 211: the clarinet does the niente attack "better than any other woodwind… the tone starts from almost complete silence without articulation" (Ex. VII-57). The score already uses it at 85.

**Suggestion.** `introClTwo = { R2.*2 | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t es2.\< | es2.\pp | fes2. | es2 r4 | }`, and the same for `introBassCl`. This is not the ties rejected at c126, and pitches are unchanged. Horn 3 still re-attacks its C4 at 3, so the seam is only softened. Bars 1–6 are on the standing list, so this is the composer's call. I would accept the rejection.

### 3. Bars 32–34: cor anglais in its lowest sixth, a sixth above the bass clarinet solo (recorded; text option only)

The CA's C4–A♭3 is the register Adler credits with "tremendous expressive carrying power" (PDF 205). It sits above a bass clarinet that "speaks a bit less incisively" (PDF 217). The pp keeps two levels between them (solo mp<mf). If anything changes here, it should only be the CA's word: "dolce" is the character word of a melodic voice. `-\markup \italic "sotto voce"` would ask for the accompaniment's role without touching the protected choir (31–36, c140). I do not insist.

### 4. Bassoon and contrabassoon lowest-fifth check (recorded, no change)

No bassoon note at or below F2 has a soft dynamic. The contrabassoon's lowest fifth occurs only at ff (61–62). The soft contrabassoon pedals (A♭1 at 30–36 and 82) lie a sixth above it. PDF 226 and 230 are satisfied throughout.

### 5. Bar 54: clarinet 1 crosses the break under a slur at pp< in unison with horn 1 (recorded)

Concert F4→B♭4 (written G4→C5). PDF 210 says the break troubles "all but the most expert player" and in the end "presents no difficulties whatsoever". This is a professional part, so it follows. I record it so that it is not raised as new.
