## Holistic comparison with Dvořák 7/ii

Dvořák opens with a clarinet tune over pizzicato strings in which the cellos alone carry the plucked bass (bars 1–8; the basses rest until the arco crescendo at 9–10). He then changes colour at each rehearsal letter: strings with figuration at A (11), a horn solo at B (32), clarinet, flute and bassoon passing one motive at C (44–56), and several short ff outbursts (bars 27, 38–43, 63–66) instead of one long build. The movement ends with a pp horn echo of its motive over a timpani roll dying from ffz to pp across four bars.

Against that model the v79 movement is credible: the string-free horn solo at 7–14 matches Dvořák's wind opening, each theme B statement has its own colour, the trumpets are held back, and the close brings back the 3–6 group (Hn 3·4, Cl 2, timp roll), as Ravel does (PDF 224). It is narrower than Dvořák in two ways I cannot fix with small edits: theme A never goes to a woodwind soloist (open since cycle 54), and the accompaniment rhythms are almost all held notes, quarter chords and even arpeggios (Dvořák uses off-beat sixteenths, dotted figures and pizzicato/arco alternation). A conductor would accept the craft; the three findings below are where the texture still does something a master's would not.

Compared with v69, two changes since then made the score worse (findings 1 and 3). Both are side effects of fixes that were right in themselves.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 75–76: the lower octave of the theme outweighs the lead again (worse since v69). Applied.
- Problem: c078 took flute 1 out of 75–76 (correctly, to avoid a B♭6 leap at mp), but clarinet 1 still enters at 75 on the lower octave with Vln II. So at 75–76 the lower octave has Vln II + Cl 1 and the upper octave Vln I alone. That is the imbalance c067 removed from 71–74, and in v69 the flute balanced the clarinet from 75.
- Evidence: Adler part 2 PDF 209 (Dvořák, Slavonic Dance 8: the melodic figure gets the largest doubling); PDF 213 (Bizet 13–18: a crescendo orchestrated by adding instruments); c067's reasoning (Sebesky PDF 138 rule 2).
- Fix (aTwo.ily): clarinet 1 rests 75–76 and enters with flute 1 at 77, so the two octaves each gain a wind on the same beat as the crescendo builds.
  `aTwoClOne`: `R2.*4 | R2.*2 | g''4(\mf\< es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 | aes'2)\f r4 | ...`
  `aTwoClTwo` bar 77: `g'2\mf\< r4` (shared staff, one dynamic).

### 2. Bars 37–40: the pizzicato bass is in three parts on two pitches. Applied.
- Problem: Vc D♭3 doubles the harp LH's upper note and Cb doubles its lower note, in the same rhythm, for four bars. Then at 41 the cellos change to an arco tenor line, so their first sound in section D is not their real entrance.
- Evidence: Adler part 2 PDF 205 (Brahms, Hungarian Dance 1: "the steady pizzicato bass and the independent cello part"); PDF 228 (Stravinsky rests a colour so its entrance is fresh); PDF 213 (a delayed entrance clarifies). Dvořák 7/ii bars 1–8 has only one plucked bass part (cellos, basses tacet). This answers the open item "Vc pizz. 37–40".
- Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVc`): `R1*4 |` at 37–40; at 41 `des1(\pp\<^\markup \italic "senza sord." | ...` (no "arco", since the cellos no longer play pizz.). The basses and harp keep the pizzicato bass. The cello staff drops out of the 37–40 system.

### 3. Bar 81: the harp re-enters mf inside the closing diminuendo (worse since v69). Applied.
- Problem: c076 rightly silenced the harp under the ff of 79–80, but left its entry on 81 (LH E♭1–B♭1 mf>, RH V7 chord on b2). The harp now makes a fresh attack in the middle of the bar where everyone else is dropping out. In v69 the harp played 79–81 and its 81 was a continuation.
- Evidence: Adler part 2 PDF 213 (Bizet orchestrates the diminuendo instead of leaving it to the players) and PDF 224 (Ravel: instruments "descend and drop out" to make the diminuendo); PDF 205 (no sudden thickening).
- Fix (aTwo.ily): `aTwoHarpL` `R2.*3 |` for 79–81, then `<aes,, es,>2.\p\arpeggio` at 82 as before; `aTwoHarpR` `R2.*3 | r4 <aes c'>4\arpeggio ...` (the 82 harmonics pickup is unchanged). The harp's next sound is the p tonic at the cadence.

### Not applied, noted
- 83–92: Adler PDF 245 (Schoenberg Op. 43B: muted lower strings for a peaceful close) would suggest con sord. for violas, cellos and basses in the coda, as the strings were muted in 15–30. The cellos and basses tie from 82 into 83, so there is no time to put mutes on, and the close is protected. I do not recommend a change.
- Open item "woodwinds rarely carry theme A": Dvořák 7/ii passes its themes through clarinet, horn, flute and bassoon. Within the 8-page limit I see no place to add a wind statement of theme A without a new staff.
