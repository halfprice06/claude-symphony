## Findings (ranked)

None of the findings below calls for a change. Three earlier passes on these pages have already brought the score in line with them, and I found no new fault large enough to justify touching protected material. I list what I checked so that the composer does not reopen settled points.

### 1. Bar 40: the °7's upper d5 in the viola figure (no change)

At 40 b1–2 the viola triplets arpeggiate the vii°7/vi, `c'8( es' ges' es' c' es')`, and b3 continues `bes8( des' f')`. Read as a compound line, the lower strand C4 (b2.33) goes to B♭3 (b3) and the upper strand G♭4 (b1.67) goes to F4 (b3.67). That is the d5 → P5 in similar motion that A&S mark "avoid" in Ex. 22-3c (PDF 375). In real voices the chord resolves correctly: the bass A1 rises to B♭1, Vln II G♭4 falls to F4 and E♭4 to D♭4, and the flute's A4 over G♭4 goes to P4 as in Ex. 22-3b. The viola's two strands are a sixth of a beat and two-thirds of a beat away from the B♭ triad's notes, at pp, inside a broken chord, so no hidden fifth can be heard. A rewrite (`des'8( f' des')` on b3) would resolve the d5 inward. It would also change the figure's contour just before the pizz. change, and the viola triplets 37–40 are on the do-not-touch list (c153). Record only. 56 does not have this pattern: c137 already rewrote the harp's figure to E♭4/G♭4 → B♭3/F4.

### 2. Every diminished seventh resolves as Unit 22 prescribes (no change)

The sonority scan finds full °7s only at 33 b2, 40, 56, 59 b4, 64 b3–4 and 81 b1. All of them follow rows 1–4, 6, 8 and 10. Two have not been described this way before:

- 59 b4: on the beat, the vii°7/V of D♭ sounds without its D♭ (G, B♭, F♭ only). The viola's D♭4 arrives on the half-beat inside its sextuplet. The chord's 5th is the least needed tone (A&S PDF 375 lists G–D♭ as one of the two d5s, and it is supplied by the figure), and F♭ resolves in Bsn 1, Vc and Hn 1 (transferred resolution, Ex. 22-5). Leave it.
- 81 b1 is the complete V♭9 since c292 (Cl 2 D♭5 tied over). It sounds as Ex. 23-9's G–B♭–D♭–F♭ over the E♭ root, the functional V7 of Ex. 22-9.

### 3. Cross relations (no change)

The scan of split chromatic successions finds only the recorded case at 21. Every other F/F♭ and C/C♭ pair follows the good order of PDF 395, ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂: 59 (F6 at b2.5, inner F♭ at b4), 77–78 (tune C6 falls to B♭5 before the inner C♭), and 78 b3.5 (the flute and violins' F6 grace run into 79 comes after the bass's F♭–E♭ has resolved). The doubled chromatic lines at 87–88 (F → F♭ in C.a., Hn 3·4 and the violas, each in its own line) are Ex. 23-12b, "unobjectionable". c132's fixes at 50 and 60 are intact.

### 4. Bar 50–51: the bass B1 → A♭1 (no change)

The only melodic A2 in the score is the basses' `b,,2 | aes,,1` across the double barline where F♯ minor (key signature) turns back to D♭. A&S accept a melodic A2 only inside °7 contexts (PDF 380, §11). Here it is a spelling effect of the key change. The sound is C♭–A♭, a falling third from iv of F♯ minor (= C♭ minor) to V7 of D♭. Respelling the bass as `ces,,2` in a sharp-key bar would be harder to read than the switch at the barline. Keep.

### 5. ♭6̂ treatment (no change)

The ♭6̂ checker confirms PDF 388 and Points 3 (PDF 395) at every F♭: 5 (from I, upper neighbour, Ex. 23-2a), 21 (bass D♭–E♭–F♭–E♭ under the French sixth, spelled `fes,4`), 59 and 62 (inflections of F, Ex. 23-2f), 78 (deceptive ♭VI, Ex. 23-6), 80–81 (iiø7 → V♭9, Ex. 23-2e and 23-9), and 88 (F–F♭–E♭ over IV6–iv–I). Bar 88 matches the Chopin Nocturne of Ex. 23-2f: the F♭ is a passing tone and also the third of a minor IV. No F♭ is followed by F before E♭.

### 6. Half-diminished sevenths and the Ex. 22-15 fifths (no change)

viiø7 of A♭ occurs only as an extension of V, at 70 b3.5 and 74 b3.5 (the theme's pickup F over the dominant; Ex. 22-17's use). The violins' F4 leaps to A♭4, so no parallel 5ths form into I (Ex. 22-15a). viiø7 of D♭ at 39 b3 and 55 b3 holds its G♭ as the common tone that becomes the 7th of the next °7 (Ex. 22-7, rows 8 and 15). The viiø7/V at 86 stands over the tonic pedal and its D♮ falls to D♭ in the same clarinet line (the motto tail, protected). That is a chromatic inflection in one voice, not a cross relation.

### 7. Mixture plan as a whole (no change; confirms c292)

The movement uses every frequent kind of mixture listed in the Points for Review (PDF 395, items 2a–d): ♭6̂ colouring IV and II (5, 80, 88), ♭6̂ making VII°7 (59, 81), ♭3̂ with ♭6̂ making ♭VI (78), and the ♭VI–plagal pairing of Ex. 23-5 (78 → 87–89). The statements of theme A at 7–14 and 23–30 stay diatonic, so the F♭ thread is saved for the transition, the second climax and the coda, where it prepares the scherzo's E. Unit 23 §5 (Ex. 23-8) is met at 79–81 against 75–77. Adding the tonic °7 to theme A's statements would break c002's V7/V under the motto tails and c283's cadences (protected). I found nothing to add.
