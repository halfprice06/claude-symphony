## Findings

### 1. Bar 43 b2: the bSecCb comment says the viola's D♭4 makes b2 a 4/3. The D♭ is gone by then (comment only, applied)

**Problem.** c342 rewrote the c239 comment in bSecCb to read "the 7th D flat4 in the viola figure, so 43 b2 is a 4/3, not a 6/4", and it adds "no 6/4 on a weak beat". The viola line at 43 is `\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes des' bes ges bes }`, which plays D♭4 only on the last triplet of b1. On b2 the sounding notes are B♭1 (Cb), B♭2 (Vc), B♭3-G♭3-B♭3 (Vla), E♭4 (C.a., held) and G♭5 (Fl 1, held). That is B♭ with E♭ and G♭ above it, a 6/4. c423 reached the same conclusion and HISTORY records it ("c283's ii4/3 reading is wrong (the viola D flat stops on the beat)"), but the file still carries c342's wording. A later critic who reads the comment and then checks the sound will report a contradiction.

**Evidence.** PDF 66-67 (§9-10, Ex. 4-12, 4-13) says to name what sounds from the bass. A label that ignores how a chord arises misleads. PDF 64-65 (Ex. 4-10b, 4-11b) says a 6/4 can come from motion against held tones. Here the bass arpeggiates G♭-B♭ under the held E♭ and G♭. PDF 70 (Ex. 4-21) says a seventh given only in the figuration still belongs to the chord, so "ii7 on b1, its bass arpeggiated on b2" is the accurate description. The music needs no change.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-482/work, comment only).** In sections/bSec.ily, bSecCb, the c239 comment now reads: "43 ii6/5-(ii4/3)-V: the bass arpeggiates ii7 G flat-B flat under the cor anglais's held E flat4; c482 (corrects c342, agrees with c423): the viola's 7th D flat4 sounds only on the last triplet of b1 (bar 43 viola: G flat3-B flat3-D flat4 | B flat3-G flat3-B flat3), so what sounds on b2 is B flat-E flat-G flat, a 6/4 inside the ii7 of b1, made by the bass's arpeggiation under held voices (A&S PDF 64-65, Ex. 4-11 ...; PDF 70, Ex. 4-21 ...; PDF 66-67 ...)), no other 6/4 on a weak beat ...". The notes are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 74 (changed by c469 and c479 since the last Unit 4 cycle) is the 8-7 of Ex. 4-15a (recorded, no change)

The reprise's V4/3 at 74 now forms its seventh exactly as the book's model does. Clarinet 2 holds E♭4, the root, tied from 73 b3, and steps down to the seventh D♭4 on b2. The violas carry D♭4 to C4 at 75 b1, in the same register. Ex. 4-15a (PDF 68) shows the seventh as a passing tone from the octave down to the 3rd of the next chord, and §12 calls this the seventh's origin. The downbeat sonority B♭-C-E♭-G (6/4 shape with the cellos' C4 9-8 suspension) is the V4/3 before its seventh enters. The 4th above the bass is the chord's root, as in every 4/3 (Ex. 4-18, PDF 69). In the first statement at 26, V stands in root position, so the reprise's inverted dominant differs from it on purpose. Keep c479's voicing.

### 3. Bar 58: each seventh in the fifths chain resolves down by step on the half beat (recorded, no change)

At 58 the bass falls in fifths every beat (B♭-E♭-A♭-D♭-G♭). The melody's A♭6 (the 7th of B♭7) is held over E♭ as a 4th and falls to G♭6 at b2½. Its G♭6 (the 7th of A♭7) is held over D♭ and falls to F6 at b4½. Horn 1 and the cellos take E♭-F on the same half beat. Each seventh descends by step as PDF 67-68 requires, delayed by half a beat. A beat-sampled scan sees G♭6 at 58 b4 and E♭6 at 59 b1 and wrongly reports an unresolved seventh, so later scans should sample in eighths. This matches c151, c412 and the protected chains.

### 4. Bar 36 b3: a lone C is still the one bare non-root bass (recorded, no change)

PDF 63: "a bass tone, heard alone, tends to sound like a root." At 36 b3 only C3 (B.cl.) and C5 (C.a. pickup) sound. I add one point to c202 and c342. Since 34 b2 the A♭ chord has had no third: 35 to 36 b2 is the open fifth A♭1-A♭2-E♭3. So the C does not continue a sounding C, and the ear has less help in hearing it as A♭'s third or D♭'s leading tone. It is still one pp beat inside a melodic pickup that moves straight to D♭-F, and both earlier critics' reasons for leaving it stand. Any cure would change a pitch in the protected woodwind choir 31-36, so I do not recommend one.

### 5. Every key area is confirmed by its own dominant (recorded, no change)

PDF 71 (§19-20) says the 5th relation organises large spans and V7 precedes the final tonic. Here, C♯7 to F♯ minor at 47-48, A♭7 to D♭ at 60-61, E (F♭) to A minor at 62-63, and E♭7 (65-70, then 81) to A♭ at 71 and 82 all follow that rule. B♭ minor (45) is reached only by the deceptive cadence of 44-45. That suits a passing area, and the deceptive cadence is protected. The major-mode tonics keep their thirds in their final chords (PDF 61), and so does the last chord with C5 on top.

### 6. Bars 60 and 68 b3: 5-6 and 6/4 motions in one voice or one pair (recorded, no change)

At 60 over the A♭ pedal, the E♭-F-G♭ (5-6-7) line is carried in one voice group (Bsn 1, Vc, Hn 1). Trombone 2 holds E♭3 under the passing F for a beat and then leaps to G♭3 (c132's reason: no G♭ against the bass G of 59). At 68 b3 the passing 6/4 over the E♭ pedal is formed and undone in one pair: Bsn 1 B♭3-A♭3-G3 and Hn 1 D♭4-C4-B♭3. That is Ex. 4-11b's 5/3-6/4-5/3 "in the same pair of voices" (PDF 65). Bar 20 (violas C4-D♭4-C over F) does the same for 5-6-5.
