## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 20–21: augmented 2nd in the bass, leading tone not resolved (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 20 beat 3 is V6/5 of F minor with E♮ in the cellos and basses (`f2 e4`). Bar 21 jumps to D♭ (`des2`). The bass moves E→D♭, a melodic augmented 2nd, and the leading tone in the bass does not go to F. The violas also leave the leading tone by a leap: bar 19 beat 3 E4 → bar 20 A♭3, an augmented 5th downwards.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 82: "The augmented 2nd … should not be used." PDF 76: the leading tone has "its active tendency toward 1̂". PDF 81–82: dissonant leaps are excluded from simple vocal-style writing. The intended harmony is a deceptive move in F minor. The standard voicing is V7 in root position going to VI, with the bass rising a step.
**Fix (applied).** Root-position C7 at 20/3, then D♭ (VI, third doubled) at 21:
```
aOneVc:  … | f2 c4 | des2 bes,4 | …          (bar 20)
aOneCb:  … | f,2 c,4 | des,2 bes,,4 | …
aOneVla: … | c'2 des'4 | f'2 e'4 | f'2 e'4 | f'2 f'4 | bes2. |   (18–22)
aOneVlnTwo: … | aes'2 g'4 | c'2 bes4 | aes2 aes4 | g2 es'8(\mp f'8 |   (19–22)
```
The violas' E now rises to F. The Vln II B♭3 is the 7th and falls to A♭3, which in turn becomes the 7th of B♭7 at 21/3 and falls to G3 at 22.

### 2. Bars 77–78: the deceptive cadence of the reprise has parallel 5ths and octaves (applied)
**Problem.** V7→vi at the climax of the return: Cl 1 goes B♭4→C5 over the bass E♭→F, which gives parallel 5ths. The violas' lower note goes E♭4→F4 in parallel octaves with the bass E♭2→F2.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83 (Ex. 5-19): parallel perfect 5ths and octaves "forbidden"; these are real chord voices, not doublings of a line (PDF 84). In V–vi the upper voices should move against the bass, and the third of vi is doubled.
**Fix (applied).** `aTwoVla` bar 78 `<c' aes'>2\f <c' aes'>4`. `aTwoClOne` bar 78 `f'2\f f'4`. Cl 1 crosses under Cl 2 for one bar. A&S PDF 88 calls inner-voice crossing the least problematic kind. The harp's E♭–B♭ → F–C left hand is a filler and can stay (PDF 85–86, Ex. 5-26).

### 3. Bar 62: the climax chords move in parallel (applied)
**Problem.** G♭ → F♭ (half bar each): almost the whole wind and brass chord slides down a step with the bass. Fl 2, Cl 1, Hn 3 and Tpt 3 go D♭→C♭ over G♭→F♭, which gives parallel 5ths. Hn 2 and Tbn 2 go G♭→F♭ in octaves with the bass. This is planing, not Dvořák or Brahms voice leading. At ff in the brass, these are the most audible parallels in the score.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83: parallel 5ths "excluded" by composers "from the fifteenth through the nineteenth centuries". PDF 82: contrary motion gives each voice its independence. Doubling the bass (B.cl., Bsn 2, Cbsn, B.tbn, Tuba, Cb) is fine (PDF 84). The chord voices are not doublings.
**Fix (applied).** Upper voices move up while the bass falls:
```
Fl 2  des'''2 fes'''2    Cl 1  des''2 fes''2
Hn 1  bes'2 ces''2       Hn 2  ges'2 aes'2
Hn 3  des'2 fes'2        Hn 4  bes2 ces'2
Tpt 3 des'2 fes'2        Tbn 1 bes2 ces'2     Tbn 2 ges2 aes2
```
F♭ major stays complete (F♭, A♭, C♭ all present). The melody F→F♭ over G♭→F♭ is a hidden octave with the soprano moving by step, which A&S allows (PDF 88, Ex. 5-29a).

### 4. Inner voices that drop into octaves with the bass (applied)
**Problem.** Several times a chord voice briefly moves with the bass in octaves:
- 11 (and 75): Cl 1 `c''2 des''4` against B.cl./Bsn 2/Cb C→D♭.
- 18–19: Vla C4–B♭3–F4 in octaves with the cellos C3–B♭2–F3 (two consecutive pairs).
- 27 (and 75): Vla lower note C4→D♭4 with the basses C→D♭.
- 57: Cl 1 F4→G♭4 and Bsn 1 F3→G♭3 with the bass F→G♭.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83, Ex. 5-18. PDF 84 separates a consistent octave doubling of a line, which is acceptable, from an independent voice making octaves, which is forbidden. Each case here is a chord voice that shadows the bass for one or two moves and then leaves it.
**Fix (applied).** 11 and 75: Cl 1 `c''2.` (held C over IV, rising to D♭: a prepared retardation; a first try, `c''2 aes'4`, made outer-voice octaves with the harp A♭1→D♭2, so I dropped it). 18: Vla `c'2 des'4`. 27 and 75: Vla `<c' es'>2 <aes f'>4`. 57: Cl 1 `f'2 des'2` (now the same as Hn 2), Bsn 1 `f2 bes2`.

### 5. The motto tail doubles the leading tone of V/V (applied at 24, 42, 58, 72; bar 8 left)
**Problem.** Each time, the motto tail 5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂ is harmonised with ♯4̂ in the bass as well (bar 8: B.cl. D; 24 and 72: Cb/Bsn 2 D; 42 and 58: Vc/Cb/Bsn 2 G, with Vla G3 as well). The chromatic leading tone of V/V is then in the melody and the bass at once. The two copies resolve in opposite directions (melody down to ♮4̂, bass up to 5̂).
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "One rule must be regarded as virtually absolute: The leading tone … should never be doubled when it is part of V or VII." Example 5-4 shows exactly this fault. PDF 84 (Brahms Op. 76/7, Ex. 5-23) shows a master changing the harmonisation when a theme comes back. That supports varying the harmony at 24/72 while bar 8 keeps its own.
**Fix (applied).** Root-position V7/V. The bass now runs vi7–V7/V–V7–I by fifths and leaves ♯4̂ to the melody alone:
```
aOneCb bar 24 / aTwoCb bar 72:   f,4 bes,,4 es,4
aTwoBsnTwo bar 72:               f4 bes,4 es4
bSecVc 42, 58 / bSecBsnTwo 58:   bes,4 es4 aes,4 des4
bSecCb 42, 58:                   bes,,4 es,4 aes,,4 des,4
bSecVla 42, 58 beat 2:           \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' es') }
```
Bar 8 is on the do-not-touch list (cycle 1, no reason stated), so I did not change it. I recommend the same bass (`aOneBassCl` `f,4 bes,,4 es,4`, `aOneBsnTwo` `c4 bes,4 bes,4` stays). Do it only if the composer agrees the protection was about the horn line, not the bass.

### 6. Bars 63–65: the bass shadows the trombone quotation and the E→E♭ "slip" is lost (applied)
**Problem.** In bar 64 the bass trombone and basses play `a,2 f,4 e,4` under Tbn 1's `f4. e8`. The quotation and the bass move F→E in octaves (the trombone half an eighth late). Then the basses go from E1 (64) up to E♭2 (65), a diminished-octave leap. The plan's "E slips to E♭" (movement-2-plan, bars 65–70) is therefore not heard in the bass.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 83 (octaves); PDF 81: "Leaps larger than an octave are not permitted", and dissonant leaps are excluded (PDF 82).
**Fix (applied, quotation untouched).** `bSecBassTbn` `a,1\f | a,2 d,4 e,4`. `bSecCb` `a,,1\f\> | a,,2 d,4 e,4\!`. iv (D under the trombone's F) goes to V. Then the basses' E2 falls a semitone to E♭2 at 65.

### 7. F♯-minor episode 47–50: missing thirds and a bad cello leap (applied)
**Problem.** (a) The downbeats of 49 and 50, under the oboe quotation, have no A: F♯–C♯–F♯–F♯(–C♯) is an empty fifth. (b) The cellos leap B♭2→G♯3 at 46–47 (a minor 7th, spelled as an augmented 6th) and keep rising to A3.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 76: "the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects". PDF 81: after a large leap, change direction.
**Fix (applied; oboe quotation untouched).** `bSecVla` 49–50 `a4 b2 gis4 | a2 d'2`. `bSecVc` 47–48 `gis,1 | a,1`: the cellos move by step (A♯→G♯→A). Not applied: the enharmonic changes at 46/47 (G♭→C♯7) and 50/51 (B minor → A♭7) are written so that every voice crosses the barline with an augmented or diminished leap (Cb `ges,,`→`cis,`, Vln II `des'`→`eis'`, Cb `b,,`→`aes,,`). Consider spelling bar 51 in sharps (G♯7) and moving `\key des \major` to bar 52, so the pivot is notated where it is heard.

### 8. Bars 7–14: the wind accompaniment under the horn (not applied)
**Problem.** (a) Bsn 2 sits a perfect 5th above the bass clarinet almost throughout, which gives parallel 5ths at 7→8, 8→9, 10→11 and 13→14. (b) The harp's left hand (F1–C2 at 8) rings below the bass clarinet's D2 and E♭2 on beats 2–3. (c) Cl 1 (C5–D♭5) lies above the horn's melody throughout, so the "soprano" of the texture is the accompaniment.
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 85–86: 5ths are tolerated when they come from fillers. These bassoon 5ths come from a sustained solo wind in a five-voice texture, which A&S treats as real voices (PDF 83). PDF 77: low, close inner voices sound muddy. PDF 88: an inner voice crossing above the melody obscures it.
**Fix (suggestion).** Give Bsn 2 the third or root instead of the fifth on alternate chords (e.g. bar 9 `c2.`, bar 14 `aes,2.` doubling B.cl.). Re-voice the harp left hand at 8 as `<f, c>` (above the bass clarinet). Move the Cl 1 notes below the horn (cycle 1's open orchestration point).
