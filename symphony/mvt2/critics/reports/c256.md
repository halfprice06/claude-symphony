## Findings

### 1. Bars 89–92, Violin I: mark the closing C5 "sul D", as at bar 1 (applied)

**Problem.** The movement begins with Vln I's C5 tied in from movement I and marked "(sul D)" (bars 1–3), because movement I plays its last motto on the D string. The movement ends on the same C5 (89–92, pp to ppp, under a rit. and a fermata; c030 chose it so that the last sound is the first sound). With no mark, a section plays C5 at pp in first position with the third finger on the A string. The pitch is the same, but the string, and so the timbre, is not, so the rhyme between first and last sound is incomplete. The A string is also the louder colour: Adler calls it "quite strong in first position".

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 75: the D string "is possibly the least powerful of the four ... it has a warm and rather subdued quality ... It mellows even more in its higher positions as its vibrating length is shortened". C5 on the D string lies in 5th position, which is that mellow higher position, and suits a morendo to ppp. PDF 73: fingerings or string names belong in a part when "the desired timbre" calls for them. Here the timbre is the reason. PDF 76: a passage meant for one string is marked "sul D". Vln II's A♭4 under it lies on the D string or G string anyway, so the two violin sections close in the same register of warm, covered strings.

**Fix** (coda.ily, codaVlnOne; text only):
```
codaVlnOne = {
  R2.*6 | c''2.~\pp\upbow^\markup \italic "sul D" | c''2.~\downbow | c''2.~\upbow\> | c''2.\downbow\ppp\fermata |
}
```
The bowing (c016), pitches, dynamics and fermata are unchanged. The marking sits under the "rit." over bar 89 and does not change the layout of page 8 or the one-page Violin I part. **Ending note for the other threads:** this changes only the string colour of movement II's last Vln I note. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes, so the join into the scherzo (Hn 1 G♯4, timpani retuning) is not affected.

### 2. Bars 79–80, double basses: "change bow at will" on the ff D♭2 (applied)

**Problem.** At 79 the basses hold D♭2 ff for a dotted half at ♩ = 48 (poco allargando), about 3.75 s on a down-bow. They restrike it as an up-bow half note at 80, and c186's hooked up-bow B♭1 at 80 b3 takes that up-bow to about 3.9 s, all at ff. These are two full-length ff bows in a row on the shortest bow in the orchestra. The cellos split the same bars into quarters and halves. The violins and violas change bow on the tie at 80 (c118, c246). The same kind of bass note at 61 (ff, about 4.4 s) already carries "change bow at will" (c106).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." PDF 105: the bass's power comes from the section, so staggered changes keep the ff whole while each player renews the bow. This is the same reasoning c106 applied at 61 and c196 at 2.

**Fix** (aTwo.ily, aTwoCb, bar 79–80; the explicit up-bow at 80 b3 is dropped because it conflicts with free bowing):
```
  des,2.\ff^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | des,2 bes,,4 | es,2.\> | aes,,2.~\p\> |
```
This partly reverses c186's hooked up-bow at 80 b3. Its stated purpose, that the f at 78 and the ff at 79 start on down-bows, still holds: the section attacks 79 down-bow from the existing bowings at 75–78 and only then staggers. The text fits above the bass staff on page 7. In the Double bass part, bars 69–92 re-break over two lines and the part stays on one page.
