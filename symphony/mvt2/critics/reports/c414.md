## Findings

None of the findings below calls for a change. The reading's principles are already applied throughout the score, mostly by earlier cycles that cited the same pages (c034 and c067/c079 on PDF 151, c034 on PDF 158, c079 on PDF 156). I tested no edit, so /tmp/critic-414/work is an unchanged copy of v414. ### 1. The two climaxes give each octave a weight, as in Mozart 40/iv (no change)

**Bars:** 61-62 and 79-81.

**Check against the reading:** Adler PDF 151 says the listener takes the most heavily scored octave as the theme, and the flute must not be left alone high up when it adds nothing.
- At 79-81 the theme is in three octaves: Fl 1 + Vln I (B♭6), Ob 1 + Cl 1 + Vln II (B♭5) and Vla + C.a. (B♭4). The middle octave is the heaviest, which is what c034 intended so that it outweighs the counter-melody in Hn 1·2, Vc and Bsn 1.
- At 61-62 the augmented theme-B head (A♭-D♭-F-F♭) moves in Fl 2 (A♭5), Vln II (A♭4) and Vc (A♭3), with Bsn 1 from 61 b3. Vln I takes it over from 61 b3 at D♭6, and Ob 1 joins it at F5-F♭5. The flute's held A♭6 is the only instrument above the theme. PDF 152 (Beethoven 9/i) allows that: "this high octave in the flute is so piercing it need not be doubled."
- Brass are marked one level under the winds (f under ff), as d'Indy marks his trombones (PDF 147).
- The low-brass and timpani entry on D♭ at 61 is Barber's "finality" device (PDF 150, detail 4).

**Verdict:** follows. A change here would undo c034, c184 or c404.

### 2. The colour changes in 37-52 follow the phrases, so they are not the "constant flux" Adler warns against (no change)

**Bars:** 37-52.

**Check against the reading:** PDF 146 warns that too many different effects can put "the structure of the work ... in jeopardy". Theme B passes through four solo colours in 16 bars: Fl 1 at 37, C.a. at 41, Ob 1 at 44 b4 and C.a. at 47. Each change falls on a phrase boundary. The background stays the same (pizz. bass, harp, viola triplets 37-44, then sustained strings with viola off-beats 45-52), and the quotation at 47 is set apart by the key and the poco rit. This is the Tchaikovsky pattern on PDF 154-156: the melody keeps its colour for a phrase, and the change comes with a new phrase and a new background. Dvořák's Largo also moves its middle-section melody between winds by phrase.

**Verdict:** follows.

### 3. New colours enter after a rest or an anacrusis (no change)

**Bars:** 6-7, 15, 37, 52-53, 67, 70-71, 89.

**Check against the reading:** PDF 156: "An anacrusis or a rest before a certain color enters are two excellent ways of introducing new elements." PDF 156-157 (Weber): when the accompaniment begins first, the solo entry "sounds very fresh".
- Hn 1 enters after four bars of soft wind chords, with a pickup at 6 b3.
- Fl 1 at 37 takes the cor anglais's C5-F5 anacrusis (trans 36 b3 into 37 b1) on the shared F5.
- The violins enter theme B with a pickup at 52 b4, after the C.a. phrase.
- Hn 1 at 67 comes after two bars of fading strings.
- The violins' A′ pickup at 70 b3 follows the horn solo.
- Vln I's D♭-C at 89 follows two bars without it.

Bar 37 is the only entry where the background (Vln II, Vla, Cb pizz., harp) starts on the same beat as the solo. The anacrusis is there, in the C.a., which is enough under row 23.

**Verdict:** follows.

### 4. Long like-pitch doublings in 53-60 are Romantic "fat" scoring, which Adler accepts (no change)

**Bars:** 53-56 (Cl 1 = Hn 1 for 3½ bars), 57-60 (B.cl = Bsn 2, with Cb an octave below).

**Check against the reading:** The Mahler example (PDF 158) avoids any shared line longer than a few notes. Adler then says plainly that this is not a value judgement, and that the "fat" combinations of the earlier masters are equally valid (PDF 158-159). The Cl 1 + Hn 1 unison is a soft blended counter-line, recorded by c361 and c367. The 57-60 bass doubling is the crescendo built by adding instruments (PDF 149-150, Barber). Splitting these lines in the Mahler manner would go against the brief's late-Romantic models.

**Verdict:** follows for this style.

### 5. String-free passages use contrasting colours under the solo (no change)

**Bars:** 7-14, 31-36.

**Check against the reading:** PDF 154 says the accompanying colour usually contrasts with the solo.
- At 7-14 the horn has bassoons and bass clarinet sustaining under it and the harp as the contrasting plucked colour. The bassoon's blend with the horn is the point (protected).
- At 31-36 the bass clarinet solo has the C.a. in sixths over the Bsn/Cbn pedal. Of the three groups, this is the one passage that is all woodwind.

Both passages are protected, and both match Tchaikovsky's solo clarinet introduced "all by itself" (PDF 156), which Adler calls extreme but effective.

**Verdict:** follows.

## Must not be touched

The 61-62 and 79-81 octave layouts, the brass one level under the ff winds and strings, the horn theme over bassoons and harp at 7-14, the C.a. anacrusis into Fl 1 at 36-37, and the standing do-not-touch list.
