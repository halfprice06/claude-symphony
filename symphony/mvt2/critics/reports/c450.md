### 3. Harmonic variety: the motto tail is reharmonized at each appearance, theme A's three statements are not (8-9, 24-25, 67-69, 72-73, 85-89; no change)

**What the reading says.** Sebesky PDF 19, Ex. 3/3A: harmonize a melody differently on its return when possible, but arrive at the same resting chord. I checked Ex. 3A in the image: the same eight-bar tune moves from Am-Am(maj7)-Am7-Am6 to Am-Em/G-Fmaj11-F13, and both end on Cmaj9.

**What the score does.** At the cyclic level it follows this closely. The motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C has four harmonizations: vi7-V7/V-V7-I inside theme A (8-9, 24-25, 72-73); over the E♭ pedal as V, stopping on B♭3 so the resolution is left to 71 (Hn 1, 67-69); and over the tonic pedal as I - iiø4/2 - D♭/F (IV6 turning to iv with F♭ at 88) - I in the coda (Cl 1, 85-89). Each reaches C, 3̂ of A♭, the pitch movement I ended on. That is Ex. 3/3A's procedure applied across the movement, and it is how the tail becomes the movement's germ (PDF 11, row 2).

Inside theme A the three statements keep vi7-V7/V-V7-I. This is protected ("seventh-chord chains at 8/24/72", c442) and c434 recorded the reason: the returns keep their harmony and change colour. Sebesky's own wording is "whenever possible"; in an ABA′ slow movement on the Dvořák 9/ii model the return has to be recognized, and Dvořák's Largo also brings its tune back over the same chords. The variety Sebesky asks for is supplied by colour (Hn 1 over harp; violins with cello counter-melody; violins in octaves with violas' figure and Cl 1) and by the new harmony at 77-81 after the third statement. I do not reopen it.
