## Findings

### 1. Bars 63–64: the allargando starts on the first note of the trombone quotation (applied)

**Problem.** The broadening runs without a break across five bars: "poco allargando" at 60 (♩ 64→58), "Largamente" at 61 (54), then "allargando" printed at 63 with MIDI 50 for all of 63 and 46→40 in 64. So the trombone states movement I's first-theme head, ff marcato, while the tempo is already dropping, and the head is the slowest it has been since 37. The quotation is the one place where movement I comes back in this movement, and its head is what makes it recognisable. Slowing from its first note makes it sound like a fade-out rather than a statement, and the "Largamente" tempo of the climax lasts only two bars before it gives way.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 267 (Mozart, XVII-10, point 3): after a tutti, the solo entry is set off by cutting back the forces. The score already does this at 63, with strings sf> p and winds silent, so a slower tempo is not also needed to separate the solo. Adler PDF 279–281 (Bizet, XVII-20, read at 200 dpi): after the climax the orchestra plays "colla voce", the rall. ("dim. p pochiss. rall.") falls on the last notes of the phrase, and "a tempo" follows. Dvořák 9/ii, the stated model for this quotation (cyclic-notes, critic 100 note), plays its trombone quotation at 96–98 in the running "Meno" tempo, with no rit. anywhere in 90–100. It relaxes only into "Meno mosso, Tempo I" at 101.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-153/work/global.ily).** Bar 63 stays in the Largamente tempo. "allargando" moves to 64, the quotation's tail (A–G♯–A–F–E), where the bass trombone already diminuendos and the strings are at p.

```
% was:  \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" \mt 50 s1 \mt 46 s2 \mt 40 s2 \break
  s1 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" \mt 48 s2 \mt 42 s2 \break
```

No notes change, and the printed word stays in its own bar of the same system. In the build, "allargando" prints over the flutes and over Vln I at 64 ("allargando / unis."). The movement is 1 s shorter (345 s).

### 2. The tempo plan as a whole: keep the other five slowings (no change)

The movement slows six times: 35–36, 47, 60, 63–64, 78–82 and 89–92. Dvořák 9/ii slows three times in 126 bars. After Finding 1, each of the others falls at the end of a phrase or section, as on Adler PDF 281:
- 35–36 relaxes the bass-clarinet solo into the B section.
- 47 holds back the quotation (c048).
- 60 broadens into the cadence of the build.
- 78–82 covers the cadence and release.
- 89–92 is the close.

None of them falls inside a statement of a theme. I recommend no further cuts. 60 and 78–82 are protected build/cadence areas, and 47 belongs to the protected quotation.

### 3. Bar 36 b3: flute pickup into theme B at the rit. tempo (record, no change)

The MIDI plays the flute's (and cor anglais's) C5 pickup at ♩=45, the end of the poco rit. Theme B's downbeat at 37 then jumps to 60. A conductor will take the pickup in the new tempo by ear, and moving "Poco più mosso" to 36 b3 would crowd the system break. The only cost is in the MIDI timing, so I leave it.

### 4. Foreground and background pacing follows ch. 17 throughout (record)

- Every solo entry has a background established before it, or a held note under it: 7, 31, 47 b4, 63, 67, 83, 85 (PDF 268–272).
- After each tutti the forces are cut back: 63–64, 82–83 (PDF 267).
- The solo's own family stays out of the accompaniment: 7–14 and 67–70 horns, 31–36 clarinets, 37–40 Fl 2 (PDF 264, 267).
- The background rhythm speeds up through the build: triplets, harp triplets, sextuplets, then tremolo at 37–64. It slows to long notes at each peak (79–81) (PDF 259–260, 271–272).

I found nothing to change here. These areas are protected, and the c124 rule covers 37–60.

### 5. Two ff peaks (61, 79) against Dvořák's single central climax (record, no change)

Dvořák keeps his reprise quiet. This movement puts its highest note and second ff at 79, which is closer to Tchaikovsky's or Brahms's slow-movement reprises. The tension curve still rises: the analysis profile has 15.4 voices at 61–64 and 15.2 at 77–80, and the top note goes from A6 to B♭6. The B♭6 appears only at 79. The arrangement is protected (cycles 1, 123) and it works, so I do not raise it.
