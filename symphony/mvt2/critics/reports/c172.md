## Findings

### 1. Bar 60 b4½: the F anticipation sounds a semitone under the 7th G♭ in two octaves (applied)

**Problem.** Over the A♭ pedal (V7 of D♭) the counter-line E♭–F–G♭–F in Bsn 1, Hn 1 and the cellos ends on an F eighth at 60 b4½. Since c132, trombone 2 holds G♭3, the 7th, from b3 to the barline, and the violas' sextuplets end on G♭4. The last eighth therefore has F3 (Vc, Bsn 1) against G♭3 (Tbn 2), and F4 (Hn 1) against G♭4 (Vla). These are minor seconds in the same octaves, crescendo into the ff at 61. The 7th then resolves G♭3→F3 in Tbn 2 at 61, onto a pitch that Bsn 1 has already been sounding. A&S PDF 469: a dissonance that sounds together with its own tone of resolution in a voice other than the bass gives "an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution". PDF 475: a 6th left over V7 must lie "in a prominent part, almost always the soprano … above rather than below the 7th". Otherwise "it will create a mess rather than the expressive effect the composer intended". Here the F lies in the tenor and alto, under the G♭ in each octave. c082 had already called this a fault and shortened the F from a quarter to an eighth. Its "fuller remedy" (anticipation in the top voice) was impossible because the top holds C6. Trombone 2's G♭3 came in later (c132), and since then the F3 and G♭3 clash in the same octave.

**Evidence.** MIDI at 60 b4½ (v172): A♭1–A♭2, F3 (Vc, Bsn 1), G♭3 (Tbn 2), A♭3 (Hn 3·4), F4 (Hn 1), G♭4 (Vla), A♭4–C5 (Cl), C5/C6 (Ob, Vln, Fl). No other bar of the movement has a 7th sounding against its resolution like this.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, three lines):** remove the anticipation and hold G♭ for beats 3–4.
```
% Bsn 1 (line 95)
ges2\f es4( fes4 | es4\< f4 ges2) \breathe |
% Hn 1 (line 115)
ges'2\f es'4( fes'4 | es'4\< f'4 ges'2) |
% Vc (line 247)
ges2\f es4( fes4) | es4(\upbow\< f4 ges2) |
```
Result: at 60 b3–4 the whole counter-line sits on the 7th, doubled in octaves with Tbn 2 and the violas. At 61 the 7th resolves as A&S Ex. 27-6a describes: Tbn 2 and Bsn 1 G♭3→F3, Hn 1's G♭4→F4 is taken by Hn 2 ("2." at 61) and the violas' F4. The cellos go G♭3→A♭3, so their line now rises E♭–F–G♭–A♭–D♭ into the ff instead of turning back to F. The resolution tone sounds in their own octave (Tbn 2, Bsn 1), so the cello does not leave an unresolved 7th. The harmonic route 59–60, the bass, the dynamics, the c132 trombone line and the viola figure are unchanged. This takes back c082's eighth-note F, for the reason given above.

**Checks.** Scratch build: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt. The minor-second scan now finds nothing at 60. In allpairs_par.py the only change is that octave pairs move from 60 b4½ to the 61 downbeat (Bsn 1/Hn/Vla/Tbn 2 G♭→F). These are the same octave doublings of one line that v172 already had (Tbn 2/Vla G♭→F at 61 is listed in v172 too), not a new independent parallel. Parts book regenerated and built: 30 pages, no warnings.

### 2. Bar 58 b4: II7 over the dominant, not a 9th that resolves upward (record, no change)

At 58 b4 the sonority is D♭ bass with E♭3/E♭4 (Vc, Hn 1), A♭, C♭ and the melody's G♭. There is no F. The E♭ moves up to F at b4½ while the G♭ resolves down. Read as "V7/IV with a 9th", the 9th would resolve the wrong way. A&S explain it differently on PDF 471–472 (Ex. 27-14) and PDF 478 (Ex. 27-21): without the leading tone, the 5th, 7th and 9th above V "seem to form a II chord". A♭–C♭–E♭–G♭ is ii7 of G♭ over its dominant D♭, and it moves to D♭7 half a beat later. That is Schumann's Op. 17 blur, where "for a moment the two chords are blurred together". The same reading covers 42 b4 (E♭5/G♭4 over D♭, a ⁹₄ double suspension as in Ex. 27-15). Keep both. A later critic should not "resolve" the E♭ down.

### 3. Bars 59 b3–60 and 81: the ♭9s are prepared and resolved as the unit prescribes (record)

At 59 b4, E♭ in Bsn 1, Vc and Hn 1 rises to F♭ over the G bass. This makes vii°7 of A♭, which is the rootless V♭9 of Ex. 27-11/27-12, and it falls back to E♭ at 60: an upper neighbour, as in Ex. 27-4a. At 81 the F♭4 of ii°7 (Hn 3) is held into V as a common-tone ♭9 (Ex. 27-4c) and resolves to E♭ inside V (Ex. 27-5). At 64 b4, Tbn 1's F3 over E (E7♭9 in A minor) resolves F→E inside V. Nothing to change.

### 4. Bars 65–68: the held 7th of V is handed to the horn and resolved there (record)

Vln II holds D♭4, the 7th of V7, through 65–66 and drops out at 67. Hn 1 then plays the motto tail D–D♭–C at 68, so the D♭4 comes back in the same register and falls to C4 at 68 b3. A&S PDF 466 (Ex. 27-6a): when the 7th does not resolve in its own line, it is "transferred into the accompaniment where it resolves". So there is no unresolved 7th at the retransition.

### 5. Theme A's F pickup and the coda's F–F♭–E♭ (record)

The pickup E♭–F over V at 10, 26 and 70 is an unprepared soprano 9th of the frequent kind (PDF 467 §4). It goes up to A♭ and does not resolve. A&S PDF 467 call this an implied resolution because 5̂ is common to V and I. They add that an emphasised unresolved 9th may be "compensated" by a later stepwise descent from the same pitch, as in Beethoven Op. 24. The movement does this: the F6→E♭6 at 79–81 and the cor anglais F4–F♭4–E♭4 at 87–89 are the descents the pickup F never makes. Keep them.

### 6. Bars 61–63: A♭6 as a soprano pedal into A minor (record)

Flute 1 keeps A♭6 over D♭ (61), G♭maj7 (62 b1) and F♭ (62 b3), where it is spelled as G♯ and rises to A6 at 63. A&S PDF 474–475 (Ex. 27-18) describe the Chopin Ballade technique: a soprano tone held over changing harmony "seems to float above" it and becomes "a common element that connects these two contrasting sections". At 62 the A♭6 is an unresolved 9th whose 7th (F) does resolve, which PDF 466 permits. Keep it.
