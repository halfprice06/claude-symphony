## Findings

### 1. Bar 57 b1–2: the clarinets lie under horn 1 and double the viola triplets (applied)

**Problem.** At 57 horn 1 leaps to A♭4 to begin the second half of its counter-line. On beats 1–2 clarinet 1 holds F4 and clarinet 2 D♭4, below the horn. Those are the same two pitches the viola triplets play (A♭3–D♭4–F4) in the same octave, and the cellos, bassoon 2 and bass clarinet hold F3 just below. On beat 3 both clarinets leap a sixth (F4–D♭5, D♭4–B♭4) to the place above the horn that c047 gave them for 57 b3–59. The first two beats therefore keep the layout c047 removed from the rest of the passage: wind harmony packed under the horn in the register of the accompaniment figure.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 108: "Clarinets, on account of their tone quality should rarely be set inside the horns". A horn chord, piano, "may be completed by clarinets" in the upper register and the higher harmonic parts, with the bassoon on the bass (diagram on PDF 108). In Ex. 152 (Antar, Adagio, PDF 324) the woodwind harmony sits above the horn basis and only the florid figure moves inside it. PDF 112: octave doublings support each other. The F and D♭ of the chord are already in the violas, cellos, bassoon 2 and bass clarinet, so the two clarinet notes add weight in the crowded A♭3–F4 octave and nothing above the horn.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-147/work/sections/bSec.ily).** Both clarinets rest for 57 b1–2 and enter together on b3 above the horn, with the same mp< they had:
```
bSecClOne: \partCombineApart r2 des''2\mp\< | des''2 c''4 des''4 | es''2\f r2 | c''1\< \breathe |
bSecClTwo: \partCombineApart r2 bes'2~\mp\< | bes'4 ges'4 aes'4 ces''4 | bes'2\f r2 | aes'1\< \breathe |
```
Results: (a) from their first note the clarinets complete the harmony in the D♭5–B♭4 space between the theme's octaves, as c047 intended; (b) clarinet 1 no longer leaps a sixth, and after playing the counter-line at 54–56 it has two beats to breathe before the crescendo; (c) the crescendo is now built by adding instruments on 57 b3, which is the c079/c124 practice (a timbre enters in the crescendo instead of re-attacking). No new figure or colour enters 37–60, so c124's rule holds. Bars 58–60 and c049's rests at 59 b3–4 are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 79 b1: clarinet 2's F4 is a unison with horn 3 inside the horn chord (not applied; recorded)

At 79 b1 clarinet 2 plays F4 for one beat, in unison with Hn 3's F4, before it moves to D♭5 above the horns. By PDF 108 this is a clarinet inside the horns. c123 chose F4 because A♭4 would sound a second under the violas' B♭4 appoggiatura. The other choices are a diminished-fifth leap G4–D♭5 or a seventh G4–F5. Both are worse inner-voice motion, and the beat is covered by the ff tutti. Keep it.

### 3. Bars 29–30: horn 4's E♭3 is the one brass note inside the string chord (not applied; recorded)

PDF 113 says strings and brass are seldom laid out by enclosure. The exception is horns doubled by divided violas or cellos. Horn 3's D♭4–C4 doubles the cellos (the exception). Horn 4's E♭3 has no string partner and fills the space between the basses' E♭2/A♭1 and the cellos' B♭3/C4. It is pp, lasts two bars and leads the colour into the woodwind choir at 31 (c063's overlap). Doubling it in the lower cellos would halve the protected cello counter-melody at its close. Keep it. This repeats c047's verdict, so it is closed.

- 63–64: Tbn 1 over string tremolo, the first exception on PDF 113.
- 69–70: horn on the concord, bassoon 2 on the seventh (PDF 108 crossing rule).
- 75–82: horn 2 (then horns 1·2) doubled by the cellos and bassoon 1, the "splendid effect" of PDF 113. At 79–80 Fl 2 and Ob 2 double the horns' F an octave and two octaves up, as in PDF 109's forte diagram.
- 87–88: muted horns with the cor anglais (PDF 107, 110) and Hn 4 with the violas (PDF 113).
- 89–92: clarinet 2's E♭4 is a throat note, not the deep clarinet of PDF 107. Moving it to E♭3 would remove E♭4 from the last chord and put three instruments in unison on E♭3. The morendo handover is protected. Keep it.
