## Findings

### 1. Bars 7–14: the accompaniment lies above the horn theme (applied)
Problem. Horn 1 plays theme A at p between A♭3 and B♭4. Clarinet 1 holds C5 and clarinet 2 A♭4 above it throughout, at pp. Rimsky-Korsakov says that at piano all winds are of roughly equal strength (p. 51), so the horn gets no dynamic advantage, and the top line the ear follows is clarinet 1's static C5–B♭4. This breaks the normal order of register (p. 90, rule 2). It is also the case p. 97 (remark 4) describes: when the top part is the melody, the winds hold the middle parts. The texture mixes four or five timbres in close harmony, which p. 92 tells you to avoid. Dvořák's model (above) keeps everything under the solo.
Evidence. aOne.ily, aOneClOne/aOneClTwo bars 7–14; the open item from critics 1 and 2 in HISTORY.md.
Fix (applied). The clarinets are tacet in 7–14. Bassoons 1–2 take the two middle parts under the horn, with the bass clarinet as bass. This is the "pair over a low instrument of the family" grouping of p. 94. The bassoon is the instrument p. 52 names as the link to the horn. Horn, bass clarinet and harp RH are unchanged.
```
aOneBsnOne = { es2.\pp | aes4 aes4 g4 | aes2. | es2. | es4 aes4 aes4 | aes2 c'4 | bes2 g4 | aes2. | R2.*16 | }
aOneBsnTwo = { c2.\pp | c4 bes,4 bes,4 | c2 es4 | bes,2. | c4 es4 f4 | f2 aes4 | es2. | c2. | R2.*16 | }
```
(Delete `aOneClOne` and `aOneClTwo`; the `\sec` fallback prints rests.) The horn is now the top voice in every bar. Bassoon 1 meets it in unison only on A♭3 at 9 (beat 3), 11 (beat 2) and 14. Rimsky-Korsakov allows such brief unisons (p. 83 note; p. 97 remark 3). The voices in 7–14 now go: bar 7 A♭2–C3–E♭3 under A♭3; bar 8 vi7 – V7/V (root position kept, as critic 2 required) – V7 with A♭3→G3 resolving the seventh; bar 12 cadential 6/4 E♭3–A♭3–C4 under A♭4. At 12→13 bassoon 2 goes A♭3→E♭3, not to G3, because the horn moves A♭4→G4 and G3 would make octaves with the melody (p. 83).

### 2. Bars 7–14: bassoon 2 and bass clarinet move in parallel fifths (applied with 1)
Problem. Bassoon 2 doubles the bass clarinet a fifth higher in bars 7, 8 (beat 1), 9, 10, 13 and 14: A♭2/E♭3 → F2/C3 (7→8), A♭2/E♭3 → E♭2/B♭2 (9→10), E♭2/B♭2 → A♭2/E♭3 (13→14). These are real consecutive fifths in the bass pair, in one reed colour. The harp LH doubles the same fifth an octave lower.
Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov p. 81: "no perfection in resonance can accrue from faulty progression of parts". p. 97, remark 4: wind harmony written as an independent whole so that "intervals of open fourths and fifths will be obviated". p. 90, rule 4: concords, not fifths, between instruments of one kind.
Fix. The new bassoon 2 line in finding 1 moves C3 (held) – B♭2 – C3/E♭3 – B♭2 – C3 E♭3 F3 – F3 A♭3 – E♭3 – C3, mostly in thirds and sixths with the bass clarinet. I checked it note by note against the bass clarinet, bassoon 1 and the horn: no consecutive fifths or octaves.

### 3. Harp LH holds the old bass under a new bass (applied)
Problem. The harp LH plays one dyad per bar while the real bass moves on the beat. In bar 8 it rings F1–C2 under the bass clarinet's B♭1 (beat 2) and E♭2 (beat 3), so V7/V sounds over F and V7 sounds with C2 against the horn's D♭4. In bar 11 A♭1–E♭2 rings under D♭3 (beat 3). In bar 12 D♭2–A♭2 rings under the cadential E♭3. The same pattern appears with the double basses at 24, 27, 28 and in A′ at 72, 75, 76, 80. The harp is the lowest sound, so the bass the listener hears is the wrong one. This is the "harp's left hand below the bass" item in HISTORY.
Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov p. 82 (note): the bass is doubled only in the lower octave and "should never mix with the others". p. 84: the harmony rules for sustained notes apply in full in the orchestra, so a held note under a changed harmony must work as a pedal, which these do not.
Fix (applied in aOne.ily and aTwo.ily). The harp LH doubles the bass an octave lower and changes with it:
```
bar 8:  <f,, c,>4\arpeggio <bes,, f,>4\arpeggio <es,, bes,,>4\arpeggio
bar 9:  <aes,, es,>2\arpeggio c,4        bar 10: <es,, es,>2.\arpeggio
bar 11: <aes,, es,>4\arpeggio c,4 des,4  bar 12: <des, aes,>2\arpeggio es,4
```
The same applies at 23–30 and 71–76; at 80 use `<des, aes,>2\arpeggio es,4`. In bar 10 (and 26, 74) the octave E♭1–E♭2 replaces E♭1–B♭1, because a fifth on top would move C2→B♭1 in octaves with the cello counter-melody (C4→B♭3). The new harp/bass octaves in the checker output are this intended doubling.

### 4. Bars 71–78: the counter-melody outweighs the returning theme (applied)
Problem. In A′ the theme is in Vln I and Vln II an octave apart at mf. The counter-melody is in the cellos plus horns 1 and 2 in unison, also mf. On Rimsky-Korsakov's scale (p. 51) one string section counts as one or two winds and a horn as two woodwinds at f. So the counter-melody (two horns plus a section) is heavier than the theme (two sections), and the horns are in the cellos' register, where they cover them.
Evidence. aTwo.ily, aTwoHnOne/aTwoHnTwo bars 71–78; RK p. 51.
Fix (applied). Horn 1 alone at p from 71, crescendo as written to f at 78. Horn 2 is tacet 71–78 and rejoins at 79 ff:
```
aTwoHnOne = { es2\p aes8( g8 | ... }   % rest unchanged
aTwoHnTwo = { R2.*8 | des'4\ff c'4 bes4 | aes4 bes4 c'4 | bes2\> des'4 | c'2.\p | }
```
Engraving note: at 79–82 the part-combiner now prints horns 1·2 as two voices in unison (horn 1 carries a slur from 78), not "a2". The composer may prefer `\partCombineUnisono` there or a matching slur in horn 2.

### 5. Bars 63–64: an empty middle register in forte (applied)
Problem. Under the trombone quotation the strings hold A minor as an f tremolo: Vln I E6, Vln II C6, Vla A4, Vc A3, Cb A1. Between A4 and C6 there is nothing. Rimsky-Korsakov: "Nothing is worse than writing chords, the upper and lower parts of which are separated by wide, empty intervals, especially in forte passages" (p. 86; p. 96 remark 1). His remedy for strings is double stops in violins or violas (p. 88). Which notes fill the gap matters. My first test gave the violas `<a' c''>`, and the checker flagged consecutive octaves F♭→C between the viola top (F♭4→C5) and Vln II (F♭5→C6) at 62→63 (p. 83). The version below avoids that.
Fix (applied in bSec.ily; the quotation itself is untouched):
```
bSecVlnTwo: ... | <e'' c'''>1:32\f\> | <e'' c'''>2:32 r2\! |
bSecVla:    ... | <e' a'>1:32\f\>    | <e' a'>2:32 r2\! |
```
The chord now reads E6 / C6–E5 / A4–E4 / A3 / A1, with the trombone (E3–C4) below the violas. Both double stops are on adjacent strings.

### 6. Bars 20–22: second violins below the violas (not applied)
Problem. Vln II falls to C4–B♭3, A♭3, G3 while the violas hold F4–E4, F4, B♭3. For three bars the second violins sit under the violas on the G string. Rimsky-Korsakov's string harmony assumes "the parts being written according to the usual order of register" (p. 88; p. 90 rule 2).
Why not applied. Swapping the two lines (Vln II F4 E4 | F4 F4 | B♭3; Vla C4 B♭3 | A♭3 A♭3 | G3) is the natural fix. But the swap makes Vln II G4→F4 move in octaves with the bass G3→F3 at 19→20. The checker found this in my first test, so I reverted it.
Fix to test. Make the swap above, and also change Vln II at bar 19, beat 3, from `g'4` to `bes'4` (one beat in unison with the oboe's B♭4). B♭4→F4 against G3→F3 is then only a hidden octave in an inner voice. Or leave the crossing: it is minor.

### 7. Bars 71–78: clarinets inside the theme's octave (not applied)
Problem. aTwoClOne/ClTwo repeat the v04 clarinet chords of 7–14 (C5/A♭4) under Vln I at the upper octave. They sit across Vln II's line (A♭4–E♭5), so for most of the phrase the clarinets lie inside the melodic octave. Rimsky-Korsakov's note on widely spaced harmony (p. 82) allows octave doubling of the top parts, with the harmony below them. Here the doubling encloses wind harmony in the string melody's register, and the violas already double the same notes (C4–E♭4–A♭4).
Fix. Either rest the clarinets 71–76 and bring them in at 77 with the crescendo, or move them under Vln II (Cl 1 `es'2.` / Cl 2 `c'2.` …, following the viola notes an octave up). Lower priority than 1–5.

### 8. Bar 61: a low third in the ff tutti (not applied)
Problem. In the D♭ tutti the lowest layer is D♭2 (tuba, Cbn, Cb), then D♭3 (B.tbn, B.cl., Bsn 2), then F3 (Tbn 2, Bsn 1). A major third at D♭3–F3 at ff lies below where the harmonic-series layout puts it (p. 85; p. 96–97 remark 2: octaves and sixths at the bottom, fifths and fourths in the middle, thirds above).
Fix. Tbn 2 `f1` → `aes1` (doubling Tbn 1 in unison), Bsn 1 `f1` → `aes1`; the third stays in horns 2, Tpt 2, Ob 2 and the violas from F4 up.
