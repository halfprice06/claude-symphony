## Findings

The score follows the assigned reading. I found no harmonic or voice-leading fault that this reading asks to change, so I applied nothing. The points below are ranked. Only the first touches the notes, and I do not recommend changing them.

### 1. Bar 33: the "common-tone diminished seventh" does not behave as one (no change; relabel only)

**Bars:** 33, beats 2–3.

**What the reading says:** A&S define the common-tone °7 by its upper tones. These are neighbours that return to the chord tones they decorate: ♯2→3, ♯4→5, 6→5 (PDF 543–544, Ex. 30-20; review point 6, PDF 549).

**What the score does:**
- The chord at 33 b2 is A♭–C♭–D–F over the A♭ pedal.
- C♭ (cor anglais) goes to B♭ and D (bass clarinet) goes to D♭. The result is ii4/2 over the pedal (B♭–D♭–F/A♭).
- So the two altered tones are chromatic passing tones in parallel sixths, C–C♭–B♭ over E♭–D–D♭. This is the Unit 31 procedure (PDF 552: a diatonic frame, I – ii4/2, filled with chromatic passing tones), not the Unit 30 chord.

**Why I keep it:**
- The bass clarinet's E♭–D–D♭–C is the motto at movement I's pitch (c140), so the D must fall.
- The C♭ spelling (c152) is the one A&S give when mixture matters (PDF 544–545, Ex. 30-21). With A♭ in the bass, the chord is also the vii°7/V notation of Ex. 30-22a (PDF 545).
- The passage is correct as written and protected.

**Fix:** none to the music. In trans.ily (comment lines 6, 19 and 24) and in HISTORY's standing list, the chord could be called "passing °7 (vii°7/V spelling) over the tonic pedal". That would keep later critics from asking it to resolve as a neighbour chord, as c072 Note B did. This changes comments only, and the build is unaffected.

**Fix:** none. Add "62–65 = A&S Ex. 30-9 model" to the do-not-touch reasons, so the thread is defended by the reading as well as by the cyclic notes.

### 3. Bar 59: the ♮4–♯4 approach to V is complete (record; no change)

At 59 the bass rises G♭–G–A♭: ii6 on ♮4, then V6/5/V and vii°7/V (G–B♭–D♭–F♭) on ♯4, then V at 60. This is the progression A&S call "by far the most frequent and characteristic" use of rising diminished sevenths (PDF 561, Ex. 31-14).

The fully chromatic form would put a °4/3 (G♭–A–C–E♭) on beat 1. That would replace the climax build's ii6 with a second diminished chord before the vii°7/V. It would also need changes to the clarinets, horn and viola figure, and the c124 rule closes 37–60 to accompaniment changes. The present version is the diatonic kernel that A&S say the chromatic form decorates, so it is correct.

**Fix:** none.

### 4. Bars 36→37: a passing ♯5 into D♭ was considered and rejected (record)

Bassoon 1 holds E♭3 through 36 b2 and enters on F3 at 37. A&S's commonest augmented triad, the upper-voice ♯5 in I–IV (PDF 537–538, Bizet Ex. 30-11), would fill this with E♮. But A&S give the effect as making "the tonic sound like V of IV". That is the bridge that cyclic-notes (changes 111–120) and c020 removed so that D♭ enters by phrase modulation (A&S PDF 451–452).

**Fix:** none. Recorded so it is not proposed again.

### 5. Bars 85–88: the coda's passing chord (record; no change)

At 86 the clarinet's motto D5 and the violas' C4/F4 form A♭–C–D–F over the pedal. This is again a passing chord, not a neighbour chord. D falls to D♭ at 87 over the IV6 bass F, and the frame is I – IV6 – iv – I (A&S PDF 552). The coda line is protected (c150, c151), and the motto fixes the D→D♭.

**Fix:** none.

### Note B (relay to movement III, not to this score): the major-third cycle across the symphony

Movement I ends in C (with C5 on top), and this movement is in A♭ with its ♭VI, F♭ = E, as the recurring colour (62, 78, 88). Together they form the first three terms of A&S's "most important equal division of the octave", C – A♭ – E – C (PDF 572–573). Two points from A&S apply:

- The division usually descends and uses major triads.
- ♭6 is respelled ♯5 where the diminished 4th falls. Measured from C, the d4 lies between A♭ and E, so the middle term should be written E.

If movement III wants to close the cycle, it can move from A♭ through E major (spelled E, not F♭) to C. This is A&S Ex. 31-27a. It also matches c100's relayed advice to "close E♭/E♮ with an augmented sixth". Nothing in movement II changes.
