## Findings

All 25 applicable principles hold. These four instruments do not break the assigned reading anywhere. I made no change. Each finding below is recorded with no change, ranked by how much it could matter in rehearsal. Findings 1 and 2 are new checks. Findings 3–5 bring earlier records up to date.

### 1. Contrabassoon E♭1 at 81 lies in its lowest perfect fifth during the diminuendo (new check, no change)

**Bars:** 81–82, `aTwoCbsn`: `es,,2.\> | aes,,2\p\> r4\!` (circled tip).

**Problem checked:** Adler says the notes of the bassoon's lowest perfect fifth cannot be played pianissimo and need "a firmer attack" (PDF 226). He also says that all bassoon technique applies to the contrabassoon, which is "more stubborn" at the bottom (PDF 230). On the contrabassoon that fifth is B♭0–F1 sounding. E♭1 at 81 is inside it. c356 checked only the B♭0 at 80.

**Evidence:** The E♭1 is attacked straight after the breath at the end of 80. It starts the diminuendo at the ff level of 79–80, so the firm attack Adler asks for is what is written. The note reaches p only at 82 on A♭1, which is above the lowest fifth. The basses double the E♭ an octave higher, and the bass clarinet and Bsn 2 double it two octaves higher, so a firm start is covered. The fade to niente on A♭1 is also covered, by the bass clarinet's and Bsn 2's A♭2 and the basses' A♭1.

**Fix:** None.

### 2. Bassoon downward skips (new full scan, no change)

**Bars:** every bassoon part.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 226 says "some downward skips … are very difficult". I listed every downward bassoon interval of a fifth or more, slurred or not. All of them are in Bassoon 2:
- 57–58, G♭3→B♭2 (a minor sixth): tongued, at p<.
- 58, E♭3→A♭2: tongued quarters.
- 61–62, D♭3→G♭2: tongued halves at ff.
- 72 and again 73–74, F3→B♭2: slurred at pp, in the "sweet, more subdued" middle register (Ex. VII-89).
- 81–82, E♭3→A♭2: tongued, in a diminuendo.

Only the two F3→B♭2 fifths are slurred, and they stay inside one register. The minor sixth at 57–58 is re-tongued and doubled by the bass clarinet and the cellos. None of these is the acoustic problem Adler describes. Bassoon 1 never falls more than a fourth.

**Fix:** None.

### 3. Clarinet 2 holds E♭4 at 89–92 on one breath (recorded, protected)

**Bars:** 89–92, `codaClTwo`.

**Evidence:** This is about 19 s plus the fermata, at ppp, on written F4, the top of the chalumeau. Adler says a good clarinettist commands "the faintest pianissimo", starting from the lowest register (PDF 210). He says the niente is the clarinet's speciality (PDF 211, Ex. VII-57). c316 and c356 found that a breath would leave a hole, because nobody else holds E♭4 after the cor anglais releases at 90.

**Fix:** None. If the fermata is long, the player can take a silent breath and re-enter niente. That needs no new marking.

### 4. Bass clarinet notation and the low C at 8 (recorded)

**Bars:** 8, `aOneBassCl`; the whole bass-clarinet part.

**Evidence:**
- The part follows the French treble-clef method that Adler recommends (PDF 216–217). Adler's own printed examples are in German bass clef, which is a reason to keep the treble clef, not to change it.
- Concert B♭1 needs a low-C instrument, which Adler calls "still risky" (PDF 216).
- The part names the instrument "(low C)" in its title and prints the ossia "(no low C: small note)". The harp's B♭ still sounds on the beat.

**Fix:** None. This is the protected motto bass of 7–14 (c126, c156, c226, c356).

### 5. The change to A clarinets for the scherzo (recorded)

**Bars:** 91–92.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 215, item 1: give the player time to change instruments and "a moment of adjustment".
- Cl 1 stops at 90. Cl 2 changes in the pause, as its part says.
- The scherzo's clarinets first play at its bar 7, after the timpani have retuned all four drums. That pause and six bars of music give the player time to warm the A clarinet before playing.
- The clarinets rest through both sharp-key passages of this movement, so no B♭ part reads five or six sharps. This follows the flat-key/sharp-key rule (PDF 210–211).

**Fix:** None.
