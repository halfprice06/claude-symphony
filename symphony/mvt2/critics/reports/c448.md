## Findings

**1. Bar 82: "mettere sord." for horns 3·4 reads as an instruction to horns 1·2.** (applied)
Problem: The text is attached above the Hn 3·4 rest at 82. Horns 3·4 have no note in that bar (their staff shows only a whole rest), while horns 1·2 end the phrase there with C4 p > niente. The italic text therefore sits directly under the Hn 1·2 hairpin and nearer to that staff's last note than to the rest it belongs to (score page 7, checked at 200 dpi). A conductor reading the page sees horns 1·2 told to put mutes on as they finish; nothing on the page says otherwise until "con sord." appears on the Hn 3·4 staff at 87. The individual parts are correct, since each horn has its own part.
Evidence: Adler PDF 307: "The nomenclature for muting is the same as for strings", and on the string side the score names who changes (this score already does so at 4: "mettere sord. (not the solo player)"; at 22: "gli altri con sord."). PDF 307-308 treat the mute as a change of colour (muted, stopped and open sound different), so the score has to show which players change it. Adler's muted examples print the instruction on the staff of the players who use it (X-17 "1. 2. (sourdines)", "3. 4. (sourdines)"; X-18 "gest." in each pair).
Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHnThree and aTwoHnFour, last bar), score-only text, parts unchanged:
`R2.\! -\tag #'score ^\markup \small \italic "3·4: mettere sord." -\tag #'part ^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | }`
The two horns carry identical text, so the combined rest prints it once. score.midi and analysis.txt are byte-identical to v448.

**2. Bars 60-62: brass dynamics and entries.** (recorded, no change)
Hn 4 p< against the low brass pp< at 60, Hn 1·2 ff against Hn 3·4 f at 61-62, and the trombones and tuba f were checked against PDF 303, 311 and 319. c218, c264, c349 and c358 gave reasons that this chapter does not contradict: the middle-register horn may enter a degree above low brass (PDF 311), and PDF 319's warning about low pianissimo is written about the trumpet. No change.

**3. Bars 63-64: Tbn 1 quotation "marcato" ff, accents and portato, diminuendo to p at 64 b3-4.** (recorded, no change)
X-12's "ff molto espr." is for a cantabile horn melody, and the quotation is a motif. It lies in the trombone's very strong register (X-69, PDF 329), and its slurred steps use neighbouring slide positions (PDF 329, X-70/71). B.tbn sf> at 63 is the brass attack that clears space for it. This is consistent with c158, c188 and c328.

**4. Bars 79-81: Hn 3·4 swell on the F flat 9-8 suspension while the tutti diminishes from 81.** (recorded, no change)
This chapter has no rule against an inner horn pair swelling on a suspension inside a loud tutti; X-8 (Brahms, <f> swells) supports it. The passage is protected (c188: "brass dynamics 79-81").

**5. Mute plan 82-91 and the muted echo 87-90.** (recorded, no change)
"mettere sord." at 82 gives about 17 s before 87, and "via sord." at 91 comes in a rest, followed by "senza sord." in the scherzo (PDF 308, 321). The muted pair at pp, slurred, with > niente matches X-17 and X-49. The muted echo of the open 3-6 chord is the same device as X-19 (open then stopped) and X-48 (open then con sordino): one passage given twice in two colours. Only the placement of the text at 82 changes (finding 1).

**6. Horn solos 6-14, 53-60, 67-70.** (recorded, no change)
Each solo has a character word ("dolce espr.", "espr.", "dolce") with its dynamic, and short swells on each phrase, as X-9, X-11 and X-24. Every slur fits one breath at p or pp in the middle register (PDF 309). Stopped horn (PDF 308) would add a nasal colour that none of these lyrical lines needs.
