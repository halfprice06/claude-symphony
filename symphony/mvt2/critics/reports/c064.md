## Findings

### 1. Bars 61–62: the brass top line is a single horn at f against two trombones (applied)

Problem. At the climax the brass play f under ff winds and strings. Sounding pitches at 61: tuba D♭2, bass trombone D♭3, Tbn 2 F3, Tbn 1 A♭3, Hn 4 A♭3, Hn 2 F4, Hn 3 A♭4. Hn 1 rests (cycle 46). The only brass notes above the trombones are two single horns. Hn 4 doubles trombone 1 in unison on A♭3, then B♭3 and C♭4, where a trombone already gives that note full weight.

Evidence. Adler PDF 15: "If the dynamic is louder than mf, it is advisable to double the horns, meaning that two horns in unison equal the power of one trumpet or one trombone." In Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) every ff and f chord labels its horn notes "2 Hn." or "4 Hn.": the horns are paired on one pitch above trombones that each hold one note. Adler PDF 44 makes the same point about Bartók (Ex. XI-24, PDF 46–47: "Hns. I, III a2" against single trumpets). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41 says the same. The protected 61–62 layout dates from cycles 14 and 24, when three trumpets carried the brass top line. Since cycle 60 the trumpets are tacet. Hn 3 now carries that line (A♭4–B♭4–C♭5, the chromatic rise into the F♭ pivot) alone, at about half a trombone's weight. This answers the protection's basis rather than reversing it. Pitches, rhythm, the trombones and Hn 1's rest are unchanged.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-064/work/sections/bSec.ily). Horn 4 doubles horn 3 a2 at 61–62, and the shared staff is set to unisono so it prints "a2":

```
bSecHnThree = {
  R1*23 | \partCombineSoloII R1 | \partCombineUnisono
  aes'1--\f | bes'2-- ces''2-- | \partCombineAutomatic R1*2 |
}
bSecHnFour = {
  R1*23 | aes1\p\< |
  aes'1--\f | bes'2-- ces''2-- | R1*2 |
}
```

Horn 4 reaches written E♭5–F5–G♭5, which is normal for a low horn at f. The octave leap A♭3→A♭4 into 61 has no parallel problem (a static pitch class). The C♭ of the F♭ chord at 62 b3 stays in the brass (C♭5 a2) and in the clarinets and violas. allpairs_par.py lists two new octave pairs at 62 b1 (Hn 3·4 a2 over Tbn 1, A♭→B♭) and swaps one octave pair at 62 b3. These are octave doublings of a line the tutti already doubles in four octaves, like the Hn 2/Tbn 2 pair the checker already lists at the same beat. They are not independent voices.

### 2. Bars 82–87: no advance mute warning for horns 3·4 (applied)

Problem. Horns 3·4 play f open at 79–81, rest 82–86, and enter pp "con sord." at 87. Neither the score nor the parts tell the players when to put the mutes in. This was left open at cycle 55 as "advance mute warnings at 31 and 83".

Evidence. Adler PDF 50: muted brass must be marked meticulously, "especially if the players have a great many instructions with which to cope". Ex. XI-27 (PDF 51, Gershwin) and Ex. XI-29 (PDF 53–54, Druckman) mark every mute change on its own staff at the point where it happens. Five bars at about ♩=46–52 (about 17 s) is enough time, but the player has to be told.

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-064/work/sections/aTwo.ily). Bar 82 in both horn parts. Both voices carry the markup because the part-combiner drops a markup that sits on one player's rest only:

```
aTwoHnThree = { R2.*8 | f'2.\f | f'2 fes'4 | es'2.\> | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | }
aTwoHnFour  = { R2.*8 | aes2.\f | aes2. | g2.\> | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | }
```

It prints once, under the Hn 3·4 staff at 82. Bar 83 would not work, because the staff is hidden at 83–86. The "senza sord." for movement III remains the coordinator item from cycle 46.

### 3. Bars 61–62: horn 2 is the one remaining single horn at f (not applied; optional)

Problem. After Finding 1, Hn 2 (F4 | G♭4 A♭4) is still alone at f. By Adler PDF 15 it has half the weight of the trombones below.

Evidence and judgement. The note is the chord's third (F at 61, A♭ at 62 b3). Clarinet 2 and the violas double it at pitch, so it needs less brass weight than the top line. The only way to pair it is Hn 1, which cycle 46 rests from 61 to 66 before its exposed p solo at 67 (Adler part 1 PDF 292, 303). I would keep the rest. If the composer wants full pairing, `bSecHnOne` at 61–62 would be `f'1--\f | ges'2-- aes'2--` with "a2" on the Hn 1·2 staff, which leaves Hn 1 about 25 s (63–66) before 67. I do not recommend it.

### 4. Bar 60: horn 4 in unison with trombone 1 at pp (not applied)

Problem. At 60 the five brass hold only A♭ and E♭: tuba A♭1, B.tbn A♭2, Tbn 2 E♭3, Tbn 1 and Hn 4 both A♭3. Adler PDF 15 says that at mf or less each brass player can take a separate pitch, and the dominant seventh's C and G♭ are missing from the brass.

Why I did not apply it. I tried the obvious alternatives. Hn 4 on C4 at 60 makes parallel double octaves with Fl 1 (C6→A♭6) into 61. Tbn 1 on C4 doubles the C→A♭ line of Cl 1 and Vln II in octaves. Tbn 2 on G♭3 clashes with the G♭→F resolution of Hn 1 and the cellos on beat 4 (cycle 22). The unison is soft and short, and the dynamics already follow Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41 (horn p< against trombones pp<). Leave it.

### 5. Things the reading confirms and that should stay

- Holding the trombones and tuba back until 60, then giving trombone 1 the quotation at 63, is Adler's first climax technique (PDF 37, Schubert): sustain first, then a new colour for the form's key moment. Do not add trombones elsewhere.
- The A′ peak at 79–81 without heavy brass, with Hn 1·2 a2 inside the wind-string tutti, is the Franck model (PDF 38). The recorded choice "no trumpets/trombones at 79" (cycle 24) is supported by the reading.
- The low soft horn chords at 1–6 and the muted Hn 3·4 at 87–90 match Ex. XI-1's p and "dark chord" voicings (PDF 15–16).
- Tbn 1 at 63–64 lies in its best register (E3–C4) over strings that fall to p, as in the Hindemith example (PDF 42).
