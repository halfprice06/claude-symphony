### 1. Pitch changes since v412, checked against Unit 17. All follow, no change.
- **Cb 56: A1–B♭1 (c527).** Before c527, the bass of the falling-thirds break-off was A1–B♭1 at 40 but only the cellos' A2–B♭2 at 56, after a rising sixth C2→A2 (c412 Finding 2). Now both statements land on the same low A1–B♭1, so the lowest line falls a third, from the cellos' C2 (55 b3) to the basses' A1. PDF 268: register "throws one line into relief", and here it puts the goal pair A–B♭ in the same register both times. PDF 264: keeping one idea joins the end of the passage to its start. The scan shows only cello/bass octave doubling at 56–57.
- **Vln II div. 39 b3–b4: C4–G♭4 then E♭4–G♭4.** Over the Cø7 (C2 in the bass) the C4 moves to E♭4 on b4, so the octave C2/C4 does not run into 40. The A°7 at 40 has E♭4–G♭4 above A1, and the chord qualities change with the key as PDF 266 requires.
- **Vla 43 b3: A♭3–E♭3–A♭3 (c422).** This doubles the bass of the cadential 6/4. The strong-beat scan lists viola/bass octaves G♭→A♭ at 43 b1→b3. They are the viola figure's lowest notes doubling the bass line (b2 has B♭ under B♭1), not independent voices, and the 6/4's bass is the right tone to double (A&S PDF 153–154, cited by c422).
- **Vc 28: A♭3–F3–D♭4 (was A♭3 half note).** The new F3 on b2 is a third over the D♭ bass. The downbeat 5ths 27→28→29 (E♭/A♭, A♭/D♭, B♭/E♭) are still each split by a beat-3 third, F3/D♭ and D♭4/B♭. This is the voice-leading tone of Ex. 17-1 (PDF 267), as c412 found.
- **Vc 73–74: C4 tied as a 9-8 suspension (c469), with Cl 2 E♭4 tied and D♭4 on 74 b2 (c479).** The v412 scan had bassoon 2/cello and cello/bass octaves A♭→B♭ on the 73→74 downbeats. The suspension removes them. 74 b1 is now C4 over B♭1 (a 9th), and it resolves on the weak beat. PDF 270–271: suspensions decorate sequential and cadential motion. Fewer strong-beat octaves than in v412.

### 2. Bar 11: bass clarinet and bassoon 1 make 5ths on beats 1 and 3 (A♭2/E♭3 → D♭3/A♭3) over I–I6–IV. Recorded, no change.
- **Observation.** Bassoon 1 holds E♭3 through beats 1–2 and leaps to A♭3 on beat 3. The bass clarinet passes A♭2–C3–D♭3. The D♭/A♭ fifth is then held to the 12 downbeat, so the downbeats 11→12 are fifths in the same two voices.
- **Reading.** PDF 267 and Ex. 17-1: a voice-leading chord between strong beats prevents 5ths. Here the I6 on beat 2 (C3 under E♭3, a minor third) is that chord. Beat 3 of 3/4 is a weak beat, the motion C3/E♭3 → D♭3/A♭3 is a hidden 5th between inner voices, and the passage is pp. At v552's MIDI tempo a beat lasts about 1.15 s. The strong-beat scan finds no other case like this in 7–14.
- **Why no fix.** The horn passage over the bassoons at 7–14 is protected (c004, c136, c153). The bassoon voicing at 11 has already been settled twice: c219 held E♭3 on b2, and c252 put D♭3 in bassoon 2 at b3. Bassoon 1 on F3 at 11 b3 would remove the 5th but would double the horn's F4 an octave below, which c252 removed for good reason. I record the case so later critics do not have to test it again.

### 3. Tchaikovsky 6/iv builds its climax with a rising sequence; this movement builds with none. A difference of model, not a fault. No change.
- **Corpus.** In the MCTL file, Tchaikovsky reaches the D-major peak through V6/5/V – vi6/V – V6/5/vi – vi – VI6/vi – V6/5/♭VII – VI6/♭VII (beats 200–207, about bars 67–70 in 3/4). This is a rising stepwise sequence of applied dominants, the "variant using applied dominants" of the ascending 5-6 that PDF 276 mentions. He also uses a descending chain of seventh chords, ii4/3 – v7 – iv7 – iii7 – ii7 – V4/3, at beats 390–399, twice.
- **This score.** The build at 53–60 stays on D♭ (falling thirds, then the fifths chain at 58, IV – vii°7/V – V). The build at 75–78 rises through the bass A♭–C–D♭ | D♭–B♭ | E♭ | F♭ with no sequence.
- **Why no change.** Unit 17 presents sequences as transitions between stable areas (PDF 265, 267). In this movement D♭ is confirmed late, at 61, on purpose (c253, c373, c393). Those critics recorded the same reasons against a key sequence at 53–60, and the main model, Dvořák 9/ii, builds its middle climax the same way, without one. A diatonic rising 5-6 under 57–60 would not change key, but it would replace the fifths chain at 58, which c032, c122 and c412 protected. A♭/D♭ chord colour, not sequential motion, carries this Adagio's builds. I record the Tchaikovsky comparison as new corpus evidence for that choice.
