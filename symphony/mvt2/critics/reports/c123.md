## Findings

### 1. Bar 79 b1: clarinet 2 sounds the resolution A♭4 a second below the violas' B♭4 at the movement's peak (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 79 is the registral and dynamic peak of A′ (B♭6, ff). The B♭ is theme A's appoggiatura to A♭ (resolves at 80 b2) and is doubled in four octaves: Fl 1/Vln I B♭6, Vln II/Ob 1/Cl 1 B♭5, violas B♭4. On the downbeat clarinet 2 plays `aes'4\ff`, so A♭4, the note the appoggiatura resolves to, sounds a major second under the violas' B♭4, in the same octave, at ff, under a poco allargando (♩≈48). The clash is on the downbeat the whole A′ build aims at.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 132 (example and text): a melodic design with notes foreign to the harmony "does not permit" the fundamental notes to proceed in the same register; moving them an octave away diminishes "the discordant effect produced by the contact of appogiaturas and fundamental notes", and the slower the passage, the harsher. PDF 133: the harmonic basis "generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design". Every other statement of the appoggiatura already follows this: at 12 the bassoons' A♭3 is a ninth under the horn's B♭4, at 28 and 76 the A♭ is only in the cellos (and at 76 horn 2 and bassoon 1) at A♭3, two octaves and more under the B♭. At 79 horn 4's A♭3 already supplies the chord's A♭ an octave and a timbre away. c034's comment (A♭4 held "so it does not leap A♭–D♭ in octaves with the cellos") no longer applies: since then bar 78 b3 is G4, so the A♭4 is a fresh attack, not a held note.

**Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoClTwo`, bar 79):** `f'4\ff des''2 |` (was `aes'4\ff des''2 |`). G4→F4→D♭5 against cellos/Hn 1·2/Bsn 1 C4→D♭4→C4 and bass E♭→D♭: no new fifths or octaves (allpairs output loses nothing and gains nothing here; I also checked every pair on 78 b3 → 79 b2 by hand). The A♭ of the chord stays in horn 4 (A♭3).

### 2. Bar 82: the timpani strike A♭2 two bars before their coda answer ends on the same A♭2 (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's timpani moment (84–85, E♭–C–A♭ answering the harp) is one of the movement's cyclic "melodic moments for the timpani". c120 removed the timpani roll at 89–92 so this answer would be the timpani's last word. But the timpani also roll 79–81 (D♭, D♭, E♭) and strike A♭2 p at 82, the PAC. The answer at 84–85 therefore follows its own last note after one bar's rest and lands on the A♭2 just heard. Its E♭ is a fresh pitch; its goal is not.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 136: "A group of instruments which has been silent for some time gains fresh interest upon its reappearance." PDF 133–134 (stationary basis) and PDF 125–126 (a phrase handed on through a common note) both favour letting the E♭ roll stand unresolved in the timpani until their own E♭–C–A♭ resolves it at 84–85: the drums' V–I is then the melodic answer, not a repeated stroke. The A♭ is fully present at 82 without the timpani (Cb, Cbsn and harp A♭1, Bsn 2 and BCl A♭2).

**Protection.** Bars 81–83 are on the do-not-touch list as the "staged release" (c108), whose reason is PDF 130's order of elimination (brass, then woodwind, then strings). PDF 119 says timpani do not count in that grouping, and nothing in the brass–woodwind–string order changes. c116 refused to remove the harp's A♭1 at 83 because it is the coda's floor; the timpani A♭2 is not the floor.

**Fix (aTwo.ily):** `aTwoTimp = { R2.*8 | des2.:32\f | des2.:32 | es2.:32\> | R2.\! | }` (was `... es2.:32\> | aes,2\p r4 | }`).

### 3. Bar 77: flute 1 enters at the violins' level instead of entering under them and crescendoing faster (applied)

**Problem.** In the A′ build the flute joins Vln I on G6 at 77 at `\mf\<`, the level the violins have reached (they started mp< at 75), and both arrive f at 78. The new colour therefore enters at full parity and the crescendo of 77 is one even swell.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 127 ("Amplification"): "While the first group effects the crescendo gradually, the second group enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes." The score already does this at 57 (Fl/Ob/BCl p< under mp strings, c033) and 60 (low brass pp<). c078 chose mf at 77 to match the violins; that is the opposite of the amplification method, and the flute G6 speaks easily at p. Flute 2 rests in 77, so the shared staff has one dynamic.

**Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoFluteOne`, bar 77):** `g'''4(\p\< es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 |` (was `\mf\<`). f at 78 unchanged. I left clarinet 1 alone because it shares a staff with clarinet 2, which is mid-crescendo at mf.

### 4. Bar 31: the bass-clarinet solo returns at mp (suggestion, not applied)

**Problem.** The bass clarinet rests from 15 to 30 and opens the transition at `mp`, then swells to mf at 33. **Evidence.** PDF 136: re-entries after a long rest work best pp or ff; mp is "colourless", and "this remark is capable of wider application"; also not good to "commence" mf or mp, and 31 begins a new section. **Fix:** `transBassCl` bar 31 `r4 es8(\p^\markup \italic "solo, espr." ...`; the swell to mf at 33 then has a full degree more range. I did not apply it: the rule is stated for brass, the solo must carry over the bassoon/contrabassoon pedal, and 31–36 is protected; the composer should judge by the balance he wants.

Keep.
