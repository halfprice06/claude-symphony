Overall verdict: the texture follows nearly everything this chapter teaches. Each section has separate foreground, middleground and background strands. The strands differ in rhythm and usually in colour. Counter-lines combine material heard earlier. The builds add instruments element by element, and the dynamics are graded by importance. What remains are two small places where two elements meet on one pitch. I fixed both in scratch.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 55 b3–4: the cello bass leaps into unison with bassoon 1's background line (applied)

**Bars.** 54–56. Basses tacet (c164), cellos alone on the bass, Bsn 1 a sustained background line.

**Problem.** Bassoon 1 holds D♭3 from 54, the seventh of ii7, and resolves it to C3 at 55 b3 (c107). On the same beat the cellos, who are the whole bass line here, leap E♭2 → C3 and land on that note. For two beats the background voice and the bass are one pitch, in one register, both legato at p. The resolution of the seventh disappears into the bass. The background strand also stops being a separate element just as the phrase reaches its vii°7 (56). In 53–56 nothing else carries the bass: no basses, no harp left hand. The merge is therefore audible as a thinning to three strands (theme, counter-line, one low line plus the harp).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 176: in Mozart's contrapuntal coda the bass is "a completely independent member of the ensemble". Each element keeps its own colour from m. 389 to m. 400, and the double basses never take another element's notes. PDF 184: each fragment goes to an instrument where it "will be clearly heard in the complex texture". The same progression at 39 has the bass E♭2–C2 (Cb pizz.), a third down, not a sixth up. In Dvořák 9/ii (`cellomL`/`bassmL`, second statement) the cello figure and the bass pedal never share a pitch.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-257/work/sections/bSec.ily, `bSecVc` bar 55):**
```
\once \stemUp des2\p\< c2 | bes,2 ges,2\mp | es,2\< c,2 | a,2\mf bes,2 |
```
The only change is `c2` → `c,2` (C3 → C2, the open C string). The bassoon's C3 now resolves an octave above the bass. The bass descends D♭3–C3–B♭2–G♭2–E♭2–C2 through 53–55, as the bass of 37–39 does. It then rises a sixth to the leading tone A2, which resolves to B♭2 as before (c042's d7 → P5 with Bsn 1 is unchanged). c201's E♭2 at 55 b1–2 is kept. allpairs_par.py output is identical to v257's, so the change adds no new parallels. analysis.txt still shows 0 outer-voice parallels; only the lowest note of the 53–56 profile changes (E♭2 → C2).

### 2. Bar 26 b3: the violins' pickup starts on the violas' held E♭4 (applied)

**Bars.** 26–27, the join between the two halves of the muted string statement.

**Problem.** The violins (both sections in unison) take the pickup `es'8( f'8` into 27 on beat 3. The violas hold `es'2.` through the whole bar, so the pickup's first note is attacked on a pitch already sounding in the same register and the same muted colour. The pickup is the only moving voice on that beat. At the parallel joins the violas rest: at 22 b3 (R2.) and at 70 b3 (rest), so there the pickup is alone. At 26 the pickup is partly covered.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 184: each fragment is placed so it is heard clearly, and "Study the doublings very carefully and separate the elements". Here a background note doubles the foreground at pitch. PDF 176: the listener should not have to hunt for the main idea. This is the same reason c157 gave for the rest at 20, and c247 for clarinet 2 at 71/75.

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-257/work/sections/aOne.ily, `aOneVla` bar 26):**
```
<c' es'>2.\p^\markup \italic "div." | <c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4 | <c' es'>2 f'4 | es'2 r4 |
```
The violas now breathe with the phrase and re-enter with their chord on 27 b1, as they already do at 23. The harmony on 26 b3 is still complete: E♭ in the basses and the violins, G3 in the cellos. This adds no figure, so c247's "plain 23–30" is kept. The viola part stays on one page.

### 3. Bar 71 b2 (also 75 b2): the viola figure strikes the theme's held A♭4 (not applied, optional)

The viola figure `c'8( es' aes') es'( c' aes)` reaches A♭4 on beat 2. At 71 and 75 that is in unison with Vln II's dotted-quarter A♭4, the lower octave of the theme's first note. c247 noticed this ("the viola figure also touches A♭4") and fixed only clarinet 2. By PDF 184 the middleground should not double the foreground at pitch. At 71, the first bar of the reprise, the clean option is `c'8( es' c') es'( c' aes)`: beat 2 becomes C4, a sixth under the theme, and beats 3–3½ are unchanged. At 75 b2 every alternative puts a C4 over the I6's bass C, the doubling c207 and c247 removed. I recommend leaving 75 as it is. The unison lasts one eighth under a held note, and the figure's rise to A♭ is its shape (c060, c127), so I rank this low and did not apply it.

### 4. Bars 71–78: every strand is legato (recorded, no change)

Adler PDF 178–179 lists varied articulation as the third way to separate elements. In 71–78 the theme, the cello counter-melody, the viola eighths and the bass are all slurred. I recommend no change. The strands already differ in rhythm (dotted quarters, halves, eighths, quarters) and register, which are Adler's first two means. Dvořák 9/ii slurs its cello eighth figure under the legato tune in the same situation. A detached middleground (Scheherazade's spiccato) belongs to a dance figure, not to an Adagio reprise.

### 5. Bars 23–30 and 53–56: themes in one colour family (recorded, no change)

PDF 179 asks for mixed-colour doubling "whether the dynamic be forte or piano", but only when the composer wants the big nineteenth-century sound. 23–30 is the muted, intimate first string statement (protected "plain 23–30"). In 53–56 the theme (violins), counter-line (Hn 1 + Cl 1) and figure (harp) each stay in their own choir. That is Bloch's choir separation (PDF 184), the other method this chapter teaches for clarity. Both passages follow the reading as they stand.
