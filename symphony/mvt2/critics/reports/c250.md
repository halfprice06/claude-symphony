## Findings

### 1. The coherence records misstate the stop chord at bar 64 (record fix; no score change)

**Bars:** 33, 64.

**Problem.** Two records say bar 64 does not contain movement I's stop chord:
- coherence/mvt2.md, quotation table and "Differences" item 4: "three of the four pitch classes (no F)";
- themes-mvt1.md, row mvtOneStopChord: "since movement II v211 its bar 64 is a full E7, not the stop chord".

Both are wrong for v250. On beat 3 of 64, Tbn 1's dotted crotchet F3 (`f4.->`) sounds with violas G♯3, Vln II B3, and D2 in the cellos, basses and bass trombone. score.midi has exactly D2 F3 G♯3 B3 at beat 218. That is the complete stop chord B–D–F–A♭, and it has the same bass, D, as movement I's bar 138. Only beat 4 is E7(♭9).

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 588–589 and Ex. 32-7: the same °7 is read here as vii°7 of A minor (G♯ spelling) and at 33 as a common-tone °7 over A♭ (C♭ spelling).
- In both places the B/C♭ falls a semitone: C.a. C♭4→B♭3 at 33; Vln II B3→D4→D♭4 and Vc B2→B♭2 at 64–65.
- So movement II never lets B rise to C. The B→C resolution stays with the finale (222→223), as cyclic-notes requires.

**Fix (text only).**
- In mvt2.md, replace the bar-64 sentence with: "Bar 64 beat 3 has the complete stop chord, bass D as in movement I 138 (D2 Vc/Cb/B.tbn, F3 Tbn 1, G♯3 Vla, B3 Vln II). Beat 4 is E7 = German sixth of A♭. In II the chord's B/C♭ always falls (33, 64→65)."
- Delete "Differences" item 4.
- Ask the movement I thread to correct its themes-mvt1.md row.

### 2. Bars 61–65 follow Widmung's own route ♭VI → ♭II → V7 in A♭ (record; no change)

**Bars:** 62–65; the A♮ preparation at 40, 46, 48–50 and 56; the top-voice returns at 72 and 80.

**Point.** PDF 593: "In the Schumann song, ♭VI moves to V7 through ♭II". Widmung is in A♭, and so is this movement. At 62 the climax turns to F♭ (♭VI), at 63–64 it moves to A minor (B𝄫 minor, ♭II in its minor form, a double-mixture area), and at 65 it reaches V7. That is the book's route at the same pitch level. So the trombone quotation's key is not arbitrary.

The way the area is joined to the rest also follows Haydn's method (PDF 597–598):
- A♮ is stressed beforehand: bass A at 40, the B♭-minor lower neighbour at 46, the third of F♯ minor at 48–50, bass A→B♭ at 56→57.
- The area's top E6 (63–64) comes back as E♭6 in the same register at 72, where Vln I begins the motto tail.
- The A6 of 63 comes back as A♭6 at 78–80.

The A minor area is also the cycle's ♭II region: movement I has its Neapolitan D♭ (7, 173), movement II this A minor, the scherzo its F-major harp episode. mvt1.md and scherzo.md each note their own; mvt2.md does not.

**Fix.** No note change. Add to mvt2.md, key plan: "63–64 A minor is ♭II of A♭ after ♭VI (Widmung's route, A&S PDF 593), the movement's Neapolitan region, matching I's D♭ and III's F. Keep the quotation in A minor."

### 3. The II→III join is Widmung's common-tone modulation in the same keys (record; no change)

**Bars:** 87–92; the planted F♭ at 62, 78, 80–81 and 88.

**Point.** Widmung (Ex. 32-3, PDF 586) goes from A♭ to E, written for F♭, with A♭/G♯ held in the voice. Movement II ends on A♭, Vln II holding A♭4 from 87 to the end. The scherzo starts with Hn 1 alone on G♯4, then E. The inner C–B♮ (C–C♭) of the example has its counterpart in II's top C5 and the scherzo's B (Bsn 2 at its bar 4, timpani at its bar 9).

PDF 595 (Brahms Op. 39/8): the new key's pitches are planted beforehand. II does this:
- F♭ major at 62;
- ♭VI at 78;
- F♭ as the ♭9 of V at 80–81;
- the iv chord at 88 (D♭–F♭–A♭), which holds two of E major's three pitches.

The one difference from Widmung is the pause between the movements. The scherzo's lone G♯4 makes up for it, because it restates the common tone in the same octave before anything else sounds.

**Fix.** None. Keep Vln II's A♭4 unbroken from 87 to 92, at the same level as Vln I's C5, and keep the iv at 88 (both are protected already). Tell the scherzo thread that its bars 1–2 are the Widmung common tone. Nothing should come before the G♯4.

### 4. At symphony level, II is ♭VI of C and the finale treats it the textbook way (record; no change)

**Bars:** 89–92.

**Point.** PDF 592–593: ♭VI is the most important expanded chromatic area. Its usual goal is a cadential V, and the most natural way there is to turn ♭VI into an augmented sixth. In the Chopin example, ♭VI comes back as the augmented sixth "after a fantastic digression".
- Movement II is ♭VI of C.
- The scherzo is the digression.
- The finale's bar 1 is A♭–C–E♭–F♯, the German sixth on movement II's tonic, going to V of C. Its last cadence uses the same chord.

Movement II's part in this is a plain A♭ triad at the end, with no sixth or seventh added. The last chord (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5) is that.

**Fix.** None. Add one line to mvt2.md, "What the finale relies on": "II's final A♭ triad (A♭4 under C5) is the chord the finale's bar-1 German sixth recalls; do not add a seventh or sixth to it."

### 5. The E♭–F♭–E♭ motive projects the cycle, but F♭ seldom reaches the top voice (record; no change)

**Bars:** 5, 21, 29, 62, 80, 88.

**Point.** PDF 600–601: in the Appassionata the motive 5–♭6–5 appears as E♭–F♭–E♭, and the development's chain in major thirds, A♭–E–C–A♭ (Ex. 32-19), turns it into E♭–E♮–E♭. These are this movement's pitches, and the symphony's key cycle C–A♭–E–C. The plan follows the reading closely.

Where the reading asks for more: Haydn "took great pains" to put D♯–E in the top voice (PDF 597), and Beethoven keeps F♮–F♯ in plain view (PDF 585). In II, F♭ is on top only at 62 (Vln I F♭6, under Fl 1's A♭6). Everywhere else it is in an inner voice or the bass: Cl 2 at 5, bass at 21, Vc div. at 29, Ob 2 at 80, C.a./Hn 4/violas at 88.

**Fix.** No change proposed. Every one of those places is protected for a stated reason that still holds:
- 1–6: the link and Hn 3's exposed plagal neighbour (c010, c060);
- 21: the voice exchange (c237);
- 23–30: the plain statement;
- 79–81 and 87–92: the A′ plan and the plagal close.

The thread is carried by colour instead (cor anglais and muted horn at 88). I record this so that any later rewrite of 15–22 or the coda puts F♭ on top rather than further inside.

### 7. Bars 64→65: the V7–German sixth reinterpretation follows Ex. 32-10a (record)

PDF 591 says the change from V7 to augmented sixth "occurs most simply in a modulation down a half step". 64 b4 is E7, V7 of A minor, and becomes the German sixth of A♭: a half step down. Cycle 232's cello B2–B♭2 eighths are the "indirect anticipation" of Ex. 32-8 (PDF 589), which removes the fifths. The upper note D falls to D♭ instead of rising; c110 accepted this as a Romantic variant, and I agree. No change.

### 8. The respellings are notational and placed at key signatures (record)

PDF 588 allows respelling for easy reading. All four changes come at a barline with a new key signature, and none of them hides a true enharmonic:
- 47: D♭7 → C♯7;
- 51: back to D♭;
- 62→63: F♭ → E;
- 64→65: E → F♭ as the augmented sixth.

The two remote areas are written in keys a player can read (F♯ minor rather than G♭ minor, A minor rather than B𝄫 minor). No change.
