## Findings

The orchestration matches nearly every principle in this chapter. The findings below are recorded points with the reasons for leaving them; I applied no score change.

### 1. Bar 79 b1: the upper three octaves hold only B♭ and F (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 79 b1 (A′ peak, ff).

**Problem.** In the MIDI dump, every part above D♭4 at 79 b1 plays B♭ or F: Fl 1 + Vln I B♭6, Fl 2 F6, Ob 1 + Cl 1 + Vln II B♭5, Ob 2 F5, C.a. + Vla B♭4, Hn 3 + Cl 2 F4. D♭ appears only at D♭4 and below. A♭ appears only in Hn 4 at A♭3. Adler's Brahms example (PDF 164, Ex. XV-14) shows "each chord fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave", and Mahler's (Ex. XV-15) fills every upper octave.

**Why it stays.** The B♭ is the theme's appoggiatura, resolving to A♭ at 80 b2. Putting A♭ in the upper octaves would sound the resolution against its own appoggiatura (c123 moved Cl 2 off A♭4 for this reason). D♭ arrives in Cl 2 at D♭5 on b2. The bass doubles D♭ in three octaves and the counter-melody starts on D♭4 in Vc, Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1, so the chord is clear. The flutes and violins on B♭6 do what Adler praises in Ex. XV-14: high flutes on the first melody note of the bar. c234 and c254 recorded the Cl 2 F4 and the B♭/F fourths, and the 79-81 tutti is protected. Do not change.

### 2. Bars 60-61: the cellos take the A♭7's seventh G♭3 up to A♭3 (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 60 b3-61 b1.

**Problem.** Bsn 1, the cellos and Tbn 2 hold G♭3 and Hn 1 holds G♭4, the seventh of A♭7. At 61 Bsn 1 and Tbn 2 go to F3 and Hn 1 to F4. The cellos move G♭3 to A♭3 at ff, because they switch from the counter-line to the augmented theme head an octave under Vln II. Adler's Schumann example (PDF 161-162, Ex. XV-11/12) is praised because "all sevenths resolve downward".

**Why it stays.** The seventh is resolved at the same pitch by Bsn 1 and Tbn 2, and by Hn 1 an octave up, so the ear hears the resolution. Every cleaner version breaks something protected. If the cellos reach F early at 60 b4, F sounds against G♭ in three instruments. If they start 61 on F3, the protected Vln II + Vc octaves on the head at 61-62 are lost (c261). This is a normal tutti licence. Record only.

### 3. Bars 47-50: violin II line above the cor anglais quotation (recorded, no change)

The C.a. quotes movement I alone at p in its C♯4-D5 band. Vln II moves above it in half notes at pp (F♯4, then F♯5-A5-G5-E♯5-D5). Adler PDF 184 asks for exactly this: Schoenberg marks the principal voice p and any subsidiary in a bright register pp. PDF 169 asks for a different timbre and a different design: bowed half notes against the reed's dotted rhythm. PDF 164 notes that violins can play softly on any note. c271's rule that Vln II does not double the quotation stands. No change.

### 4. Bars 63-64: the A minor chord is "under control" (recorded, no change)

The sf-to-p chord under the trombone quotation puts its top A6 and E6 in Vln I tremolo, which can diminuendo, as Debussy does in Ex. XV-16 (PDF 164-165). Fl 1's A6 is a single sf crotchet, the "ping" Adler gives the harp in that example. The low A goes to B.tbn and cellos with sf>, and the basses play a pizzicato sf. Do not give the flute a sustained A6 here. It could not follow the diminuendo and would cover Tbn 1.

### 5. Bars 89-92: the last chord avoids Brahms's "dangerous" spacing (recorded, no change)

Adler warns (PDF 162, Ex. XV-13) that a soft final chord with flutes on top always sounds louder than written. Here the last chord is strings only, plus Cl 2's E♭4 morendo. C5 is in Vln I sul D, then A♭4 Vln II, A♭3/E♭3 violas, A♭2 cellos, A♭1 basses. Every instrument can reach ppp. Dvořák 9/ii ends the same way (first violins on top at ppp, winds below at pp). Keep it.

### 6. Timpani rolls follow the Bloch rule (recorded, no change)

PDF 184: a roll marked ff would cover the orchestra, so Bloch marks it p and then crescendo. The rolls here are pp< at 52, p<f into 61, ppp near the rim at 67-70 with a diminuendo, and f under the ff tutti at 79. No roll starts louder than the music around it.

### 7. Theme A rescored at each return, with the harp as the constant (recorded, no change)

PDF 170-171 (Mahler 4, Ex. XV-20-22) describes a recurring phrase given new colours each time while the texture and the harp stay the same. Theme A is horn with reeds and harp at 7-14. It is muted strings at 23-30, the plain statement (protected), so the harp rests there. At 71-82 it is violins in octaves, and the harp's left hand plays the same bass as at 7-14. Two of the three statements share the harp and all three keep the harmony. No change. A harp at 23-30 would undo the protected plain statement.

### 8. The two builds add upper octaves gradually and hold the trombones back (recorded, no change)

PDF 172 (Wagner, Ex. XV-23) holds back the highs and adds upper octaves to the violins one stage at a time. PDF 162 (Schumann) keeps trombones and timpani for a heavier later chord. The 53-61 build does both. Violins in unison at C5-B♭5 (53-56) split to octaves, with Fl 1 and Ob 1 at pitch (57). Brass enters pp/p at 60, and the top octave (Fl 1 A♭6) and full brass arrive only at 61. The 71-79 build adds Fl 1 and Cl 1 at 77, then the full woodwinds and horns at 79 without trombones. The two peaks differ in weight, as c273 recorded. No change.
