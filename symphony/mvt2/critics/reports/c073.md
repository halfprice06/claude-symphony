## Findings

### 1. Bar 74: the V4/3 at the midpoint of A′ is incomplete as heard (applied)

**Problem.** Cycle 60 reharmonised 73–74 as I–vi7 | V4/3. At 74 the melody halts on G (7̂, a half note) over the B♭ bass. The sustained voices are Vln I G5, Vln II G4, Cl 2 G4 (a unison with Vln II), Vc B♭3, Bsn 2 B♭2 and Cb B♭1. These give only B♭ and G. The chord 7th D♭ appears only on the violas' off-beat eighths (b1½, b3½). The root E♭ appears only in the harp's plucked LH and on one viola eighth. The beat dump shows "B♭: E♭ G B♭" on beats 1 and 3, so the bar sounds like a hollow V6/4, not the V4/3 that c060 wanted to carry the phrase into the crescendo.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 127 §7: inversions of V7 "almost always appear as complete chords unless there are fewer than four parts; incomplete chords are seldom necessary or desirable". The one exception the book allows is Schubert's bar 6 (8-1), where a sustained E♭ in both hands leaves no room for the missing G. PDF 114 and 124 (8-4, 8-5): V4/3 passes between I and I6, and its 7th must resolve down. PDF 99 explains why V7 is rare at a semicadence: it "conveys an inappropriate feeling of activity". That forward motion is the point of c060's change, so the 7th must actually sound. Mozart 41/ii bar 2 has the same passing V4/3 with every chord tone sustained.

**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo bar 74).** Cl 2 plays D♭4 instead of the G4 that doubled Vln II:
```
aTwoClTwo = {
  aes'2.\pp | aes'4 f'8 bes'8~ bes'4 | aes'2. | des'2 r4 |   % was g'2 r4
```
The 7th now sustains through beats 1–2. It passes to the violas' D♭4 on 74 b3½, which resolves to C4 at 75. That viola note is the last occurrence of the 7th, so the chord follows PDF 102 §15 / PDF 117 §6. Sustained B♭–D♭–G with E♭ in the harp and violas is the V4/3/VII6 sonority that A&S treat as near-interchangeable (PDF 114). Voice leading by hand: Cl 2 A♭4→D♭4 forms 3rds/10ths with the bass (F1→B♭1), the harp (F→B♭) and the cello (C4→B♭3). There are no new perfect intervals. allpairs_par.py loses one entry, the Cl 2/Vln I octave A♭→G at 73→74, and gains none. Clarinet 1 rests at 71–74, so the shared staff has only one dynamic. The pp is unchanged.

### 2. Open item "B♭6 at 58 and 76 before the climax": close it, no change

**Problem as raised (c071).** The registral peak B♭6 is heard at 58 and 76 before the ff at 79.

**Answer from the reading.** A&S PDF 94 lists what makes a goal audible: the change of harmony, slower rhythm and a stepwise descent into the cadence. Register is not on the list.

The 79 B♭6 meets every criterion that 58 and 76 lack:
- It follows the deceptive ♭VI at 78, a harmonic change.
- It falls on a downbeat and is held for five beats. The B♭6 at 76 is one beat long, at mp. The one at 58 is a passing eighth.
- The grace run approaches it.
- Faster chord changes at 80 (ii6/5–IV–iiø7, PDF 95: quicker successions "mark important points of arrival") and the hemiola broaden into the PAC.
- The line descends by step, B♭–A♭–G–E♭–D♭–C–B♭–A♭, to 1̂ at 82, which is the shape of the sample soprano on PDF 133.

The B♭6 at 76 is the theme's own appoggiatura (12, 28) an octave up. The deceptive cadence at 78 exists to state that cadential phrase again, louder. c071's cheapest fix rests flute 1 at 75–77. That changes the protected A′ octaves 75–81 and undoes c033's Fl 1 p< at 75. The 58 note lies inside the protected build 53–61. I recommend closing the item.

### 3. Open item "flute pickup at 36": close it, no change

**Problem.** c071 proposed a flute C5 on 36 b3 leaping to F5 at 37. The composer kept it open for lack of layout room.

**Answer from the reading.** There is no V7 of D♭ before 37 (protected phrase modulation). The bass clarinet's C4 on 36 b3 is therefore the only leading tone into the new key, and its step to D♭4 at 37 is the "strongest possible melodic motion" that makes 37 sound like an arrival (PDF 93, 6-2). A flute C5 would double that leading tone (PDF 95, 103 point 5: "never double the leading tone"). It would also leave it by leap in the top voice. PDF 100 says that in the soprano 7̂ "must" go to 8̂.

At 52 b4 the same pickup is acceptable, because two bars of complete A♭7 and the bass's A♭→D♭ already define the arrival. Theme B now gets its rising fourth at 44 and 52 (c071), and the 8-page layout stays.

### 4. Bars 13→14: hidden octave into the doubled 3rd under the horn (not applied)

**Problem.** In the string-free accompaniment, Bsn 2 goes E♭3→C3 at 14 b1 (5̂–3̂). The harp RH goes D♭4→C4 at 14 b2 (4̂–3̂). Both descend into C, an octave apart. At 13 b3 the harp's ringing D♭4 also sits a semitone above the horn's C4, because the horn has already resolved the 7th.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 101 (6-17b, "poor") and PDF 127 §9 (8-9a–c): similar motion into a doubled 3̂ is poor, "at least in the simple and transparent textures". 7–14 is the most transparent texture in the movement. The two notes are a beat apart, so the effect is mild.

**Fix (for the composer).** In aOneHarpR bar 13, use `r4 <g bes>2\arpeggio` (drop D♭4). The horn already carries the 7th D♭4→C4 itself (PDF 102 §15). The C4 at 14 b2 then enters fresh, and the 13 b3 semitone clash goes. c019's rule (no harp note above the horn at its attack) still holds. I did not apply this, because the passage 7–14 is protected and this is a harmony point rather than a form point.
