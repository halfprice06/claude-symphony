## Findings (ranked)

I found nothing in this reading that the score violates. Each finding below records a cyclic point I checked against the reading and why I recommend no change. Nothing is applied in /tmp/critic-560/work, so no build was run there. The copy is an unchanged copy of v560.

### 1. Bars 1-6, the link from movement I (follows; no change)

Adler's colouring of a held note (Ex. XV-37, PDF 191) is the technique the link uses. Movement I's C4 is held by Hn 3 (repeatTie, to 1 b2). Hn 4 takes it at 1 b2 and holds it through 2, and Hn 3 takes it again at 3. Each hand-over overlaps by at least a beat, so the seam does not show (PDF 192). The C5 stays in Vln I sul D, on the string movement I used for its last motto, so the colour of the common tone is fixed by string as well as by instrument (PDF 191). The strings fade to niente across bar 3 while Cl 2, B.cl. and Hn 3·4 come in at pp. This is the overlap that PDF 190 requires when one choir replaces another. Adler's first chapter says timbre makes form audible (PDF 19). Here the change from strings to a soft wind chord (PDF 187) at bar 3 is the point where movement I's sound ends and movement II's begins. The link is on the standing do-not-touch list and holds.

### 2. Bars 47-50 and 63-64, the two quotations: choice of instrument (follows; no change)

PDF 188 asks for the solo instrument to suit the register and the "emotional as well as the tonal intent". The S head in F♯ minor (C♯4-D5, p, "solo, espr.") is in the cor anglais. In movement I the minor S head at I 171-174 is also given to the cor anglais (with one solo cello), so the quotation brings back movement I's colour along with its notes. The P head in A minor at 63-64 lies E3-C4 in Tbn 1, ff marcato. That is below the top notes where Adler says the trombone "would sound most pinched", and the brass are kept back until 60 so the trombone is a new colour at that point (PDF 19, 22). The cor anglais also plays theme B at 41-44. Ob 1 plays 45-46 between the two cor anglais passages, and the quotation enters on the new key signature after a poco rit. (47). The change of key and tempo marks the quotation; a second new colour is not needed. Both quotations are single statements, as BRIEF and cyclic-notes require.

### 3. The motto's tail in six statements, each with its own register and colour (follows; no change)

PDF 188 says a tune in another register needs a different instrument, and PDF 22 says every timbre is part of the composition. The tail E♭-D-D♭-C appears in six places:

- Hn 1 E♭4 (8);
- Vln I and II E♭5 (24);
- B.cl. E♭3 (32-34, movement I's pitch and instrument);
- Hn 1 E♭4 over the Cb soli (67-69, the first statement's colour brought back just before A′);
- Vln I E♭6 with Vln II E♭5 (72, octave "organ" doubling, PDF 189);
- Cl 1 E♭5 ppp<pp (85-89).

No two statements share both player and register, except Hn 1 at 8 and 67, and that pair is the recall that prepares the return. The last statement is in the clarinet's soft clarion register. That suits Adler's dynamic criterion (PDF 188) better than movement I's ending, which gave the same tail to Vln I. The scherzo (B-A♯-A-G♯, Hn 1, 357-360) and the finale (G-F♯-F-E) answer this close a major third lower each time. coherence/mvt2.md records that chain, and it is unchanged.

### 4. Bars 61-65, climax, quotation and release (follows; no change)

The 63 b1 sforzando is built as PDF 189-190 recommends: Cb pizz. sf and a short Fl 1 sf against string tremolo sf > p, with Tbn 1 entering ff. The ff tutti at 62 overlaps the tremolo at 63, as Ex. XV-36 requires for forte subito piano. Tbn 1 tapers to p at 64 so that the pp strings at 65 take over without an exposed seam (PDF 192). For the emotional arc, this is the one place where movement I's conflict returns in full. It ends with the E→E♭ slip at 64-65 (protected) and not with any C-major sound, so C major stays reserved for the finale. c548 checked the same pages for expression; I confirm its reading for the cyclic lens.

### 5. Bars 83-92, the coda and the hand-over to the scherzo (follows; no change)

The coda passes theme A's head through three colours in turn: harp harmonics p at 83, timpani pp at 84-85, Cl 1 from niente at 85. Each entry has its own printed dynamic, which is the Klangfarben dynamics rule (PDF 195) applied to a Romantic texture. The timpani answer has no trumpets. Adler's first chapter notes that pairing timpani with trumpets is the Classical habit (PDF 21), and its use as a separate melodic voice is the later practice this style draws on. The C5 passes from Cl 1 to Vln I at 89, which is the colouring of a held note (PDF 191); the 88-89 cadence with Vln I's C5 is protected (c440). The last chord, Vln II's A♭4 (the scherzo's Hn 1 G♯4), Cl 2's E♭4 (the scherzo's D♯4), the timpani muta A♭→E, C→G, D♭→B, E♭→E, and "via sord." at 92 all match scherzo.md. themecheck gives 19/19.

### 6. Piano reduction of the joins (follows; no change)

Following PDF 23, I reduced 1-6, 61-65 and 83-92 to four staves. All three read clearly. At 1-6 the reduction leaves C major, then A♭ major, iv6/4 and I, with C on top. At 61-65 it leaves D♭, G♭ and F♭ ff, then A minor with the P head in the tenor, then V7 of A♭ pp. At 83-92 it leaves the A♭ pedal, theme A's head, the falling arpeggio, the tail over ii∅7/A♭, IV6, iv and I with C5 on top. The reductions show no doubling or spacing that blurs a cyclic event. The heavy F doubling in the IV6 at 87 (F1, F2, F3 ×2, F4) is the N6 link to movement I (coherence/mvt2.md) and was settled in c532.

### 7. Where this movement sits between movement I and the scherzo (follows; no change)

Adler's Monteverdi quotation (PDF 20) links one mood to one unchanging scoring. This movement keeps its contrast in the colour as well as in the notes. Movement I ends with a trombone chorale, tam-tam and C major. Movement II has no tam-tam and no chorale, and C major appears only in its first bar. Trumpets are tacet throughout, and trombones play only at 60-64. Movement II ends muted at ppp on a plain A♭ chord, which the finale's opening German sixth on A♭ resolves. The scherzo opens with a single horn on the same A♭. The movement therefore receives movement I's last sound and passes its own last sound on, and the colours it keeps back (trumpets, trombone chorale, C major) stay available for the movements after it.

### 8. Techniques in the reading that the score does not use (not applicable; no change)

Heterophonic doubling (Ex. XV-34), each note coloured by a different instrument (XV-38), alternate fingerings (XV-39) and pointillism (XV-41, 42) are not part of this style. Adding any of them would change the protected A′ texture plan (71-82) or the coda (83-92). I recommend none.
