## Findings

### 1. Bar 78, beats 1–2: the deceptive-cadence chord has no fifth in the winds, and the counter-melody loses its wind colour for two beats (applied)

**Problem.** On 78 b1–2 the harmony is F♭ major (♭VI). Its pitches are spread as follows. A♭ (the doubled third, correct in a deceptive cadence) is in six instruments: Vln I, Fl 1, Vln II, Cl 1, Cl 2 and the violas. F♭ is in three: basses, bassoon 2 and violas. C♭ is in the cellos alone. The wind choir therefore sounds only an open F♭–A♭, with no C♭. C♭4→C4 (♭3̂ to 3̂) is the most expressive move in the counter-melody, and it is played by one section. The counter-melody has been in Vc + Bsn 1 + Hn 2 since 75. It returns to Vc + Bsn 1 + Hn 1·2 at 78 b3. For the two beats between, at f on the cadence, it is in the cellos alone. Bassoon 1 rests for those two beats with no stated reason: its rest goes back to v100 at least, and no history entry discusses it.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 162 (Schumann, Ex. XV-11/12): in a well-scored orchestral progression "all pitches are doubled at least once". PDF 164 (Brahms 3/i): "each chord is fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave". PDF 176 (Mozart 41/iv): "keep an established color intact long enough to carry out its assignment". PDF 178 (Wagner, Ex. XV-26): each theme's colour combines a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument. Here the brass rests are part of the protected build (Hn 1 enters on 78 b3), so the woodwind is the part to restore.

**Fix (applied).** `aTwo.ily`, `aTwoBsnOne`, bar 78: `ces'2-\tag #'part \f c'4(-\tag #'part \<` (was `r2 c'4(-\tag #'part \mf -\tag #'part \<`). Bassoon 1 plays the cellos' C♭4–C4 at pitch and runs straight into the counter-melody at 79. The shared bassoon staff already shows f from bassoon 2 (F♭3), so the dynamics on the staff stay equal. The harmony, the build order 75–79 (c123/c124), the harp rest at 78 b1–2 and the brass entries are unchanged. Breathing: the bassoon still has the slur break at the end of 77 and the breath at the end of 79. 75–79 lasts about 18 s at the allargando tempo, inside the 20 s limit set in c126.

### 2. Bar 40: flute 1's cadence falls under the pp string chords in the flute's weakest register (applied)

**Problem.** Theme B's first phrase ends on C5–D♭5–C5–B♭4. That is the low register of the flute, where it is weakest. Just below it lie Vln II div. (E♭4/G♭4, then D♭4/F4) and the viola triplets (C4–E♭4–G♭4), both pp. The flute's diminuendo on 39 b3 is closed with a bare `\!` at 40 b1, with no target level. Players read that as "keep getting softer", and the MIDI drops the flute to its lowest level of the movement (velocity 29, below the strings' pp). The melody then ends at or below the level of its own accompaniment.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 159, rules 1 and 2: melody notes more prominent than harmony notes, and each pitch in a register where the required dynamic is possible. PDF 184 (Schoenberg): the principal voice is marked louder than the subsidiary voices, and "the flutes can stand the help of the clarinets in their lower register". A clarinet doubling would break the c124 rule (no new accompaniment or colour in 37–60) and would add a clarinet staff to the 37–40 system, which risks a ninth page. c131 allowed later work on this phrase from bar 39. A dynamic target is the smallest fix.

**Fix (applied).** `bSec.ily`, `bSecFluteOne`, bar 40: `c''4\p des''8 c''8 bes'2)` (was `c''4\!`). The swell in 39 now returns to p, one level above the pp strings, and the phrase still ends softly. No notes or other parts change.

### 3. Bars 41–44: the pizzicato viola arpeggio uses the cor anglais's own pitches (no change)

The cor anglais opens theme B on A♭3–D♭4–F4, and the violas' pizzicato triplets on the same beat are A♭3–D♭4–F4. Adler's Schubert principle (PDF 169) asks for "opposing timbres and differing designs". Both conditions are met: the timbres are pizzicato against reed, and the designs are a triplet arpeggio against a legato quarter-note melody. Plucked notes also decay at once. c057's reason (the pizzicato leaves the solo's held notes alone) answers the remaining overlap of register. Keep.

### 4. Bar 61, beats 1–2: the head's first note is weighted below the held bass (no change)

By Debussy's rule (PDF 165), only the melody note and the top note are doubled at pitch. At 61, D♭3 is in four instruments, and the head's A♭ is in three octaves (Fl 2 A♭5; Cl 2, Hn 3·4 and Vln II A♭4; Vc A♭3). By that rule this is a reversal. But the head is in three octaves with five instruments, in its strongest octave A♭4, and at 61 b3 it moves to D♭ in five instruments. c124 (finding 5) reached the same verdict from the Mozart test. The chord is protected (c114). Keep.

### 5. Bars 82 and 89–92: soft final chords (follows; master level)

Adler calls Brahms's p final chord with flutes on top "dangerous" (PDF 162–163). This score avoids that. Its last chord is stacked from A♭1 to C5 with every instrument in its mellow register (Vln I C5, Vln II A♭4, Cl 2 E♭4, Vla E♭3 sul C, Vc A♭2, Cb A♭1). No wind sits above the violins. At 82 the only flute note is A♭5 pp, in the flute's middle register. This matches the chord Adler prefers (top octave of the strings, winds below), and nothing should change.

### 6. Bars 53–56: theme B in the violins alone while the counter-line is a mixture (no change)

The *Scheherazade* passage (PDF 179) says the "big" Romantic sound needs mixed doublings "whether the dynamic be forte or piano". At 53–56, theme B is in both violin sections in unison, with no wind, while the counter-line (Hn 1 + Cl 1) is mixed. This is a deliberate step in the Wagner order (PDF 172): the upper winds join at 57, the flute's top octave at 61. Schoenberg's dynamic hierarchy is kept (violins p–mf, Hn 1 pp–mp, Cl 1 pp). Adler presents the chamber choice as equally valid (PDF 159). Keep. A wind doubling here would also undo the staged build that c049 and c124 protect.
