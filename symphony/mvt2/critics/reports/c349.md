## Findings

### 1. Bar 60: low brass re-enter p< since v347; restore pp< (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** Tbn 2 (E♭3), bass trombone (A♭2) and tuba (A♭1) have rested since bar 1 and enter at 60 under the f orchestra. In v339 they entered `\pp\<`; c346 changed them to `\p\<`. This is the one change between v339 and v349 that I judge worse.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 136: "After a long rest the re-entry of the horns, trombones and tuba should coincide with some characteristic intensity of tone, either pp or ff; piano and forte re-entries are less successful." The appendix shows that he writes low trombones and tuba pp in practice: chord 13 (Mlada, PDF 506) and chord 16 (Christmas Night, PDF 507) have Tbn III and tuba pp on their lowest notes. c218 put "pp/ff brass entries at 60 and 63" on the do-not-touch list. c346 answered it only with PDF 130 (a crescendo may start at p) and did not address PDF 136, which is the rule that protected it.

**Answer to c346's reason.** c346 cited Adler part 1 PDF 319 (a pp is hard to control in the low register) and the chart on PDF 329 (A♭2 below the "very strong" band). Adler's warning concerns holding a pp; here the pp is only the start of a one-bar crescendo to f, so the soft attack lasts a beat and is covered by the f strings and woodwind. If the attack is insecure, nobody hears it; if it is secure, the brass grows out of the texture, which is what PDF 130 means by "glorifying crescendo chords". A p start under an f orchestra is neither the inaudible start nor the strong one. Rimsky, who wrote the rule, uses pp low brass in his own tutti chords. Hn 4 stays p at 60 (recorded with reasons in c273).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-349/work/sections/bSec.ily, lines 156, 160, 162).**
```
  es2\pp\< ges2 \breathe |        % Tbn 2, bar 60
  aes,1\pp\< \breathe | ...       % B.tbn, bar 60
  aes,,1\pp\< \breathe | ...      % Tuba, bar 60
```
This undoes c346 and restores v339's marks. Trombone 1 is silent at 60, so the shared Tbn 1·2 staff has no unequal dynamics.

### 2. Holistic comparison with Kalinnikov 1/ii (no change)

Where this movement matches Kalinnikov's:
- Song form ABA with one clearly placed climax, at about the same scale (here 92 bars, about 6 minutes).
- The cor anglais as a colour that marks the whole movement: here theme B's second phrase (41-44), the quotation of movement I in F♯ minor (47-50) and the motto tail in the coda (87-90). Kalinnikov's cor anglais solo has the same role.
- The oboe follows as the second solo voice and leads into the build: here oboe 1 answers the violins at 15-22, and Ob 1 doubles the top at 57-61.
- The harp as a recurring sound: its figure under the horn at 7, the same figure at 71, the triplet arpeggios at 53-56, and the harmonics with theme A's head at 83. Kalinnikov's harp ostinato is the closest point of contact between the two movements.
- The return keeps the theme and changes the setting (here 71-82, c319), as in Kalinnikov's plain reprise.

Where Kalinnikov differs: his harp ostinato is a single figure that runs under most of the movement and binds it. Here the accompaniment changes with each section (three types, recorded by c319). That is the Dvořák 9/ii model named in BRIEF.md, and a running ostinato would replace the protected horn passage over bassoons at 7-14 and the A′ texture at 71-82. I do not recommend it. Kalinnikov's material is folk-like and repeats short cells; theme A here is longer-breathed and closer to Dvořák. A conductor would find nothing in the scoring of the tutti chords that marks it as a student work: every loud chord here is laid out as in Rimsky's tables (Reading notes 1-5).

### 3. Bars 61-63: the climax chords against the appendix (no change)

At 61-62 the violins and cellos sustain theme B's augmented head while the violas play tremolo double stops; chord 10 (Sadko, PDF 506) mixes held and tremolo strings in the same way. The brass is one degree under the ff woodwind and strings (chord 2, PDF 505) and the timpani roll is f under ff (chords 9 and 20). At 63 the upper strings drop to a tremolo chord, the basses play one pizz. sf, and Tbn 1 states the quotation alone; chord 8 (Ivan the Terrible, PDF 506) shows the same separation of a short string stroke from sustained winds. Nothing to change.

### 4. Bars 78-79: horn entries checked against PDF 136 (no change)

Horns 1·2 enter mf< at 78 b3 and horns 3·4 f at 79 after a rest. Both are outside the pp/ff rule of PDF 136. c218 and c273 kept them with reasons that I accept: at 78 a p entry is lost inside the f tutti and eight bars is not a long rest (horn 2 has played to 77); at 79 the f is the one-degree grading of the appendix (chord 2). c273 asks that 78 not be re-raised. I mention it only to show the rule was applied to every brass entry, not only to 60.

### 5. Soft chords 3-6, 31-36, 65-70, 87-88 against chords 13-16 (no change)

These are wind chords in close position with no strings, or with the basses only, and with a bass clarinet, bassoon or contrabassoon at the bottom of the wind group, as in chords 13 (Mlada), 14 (Tsar's Bride) and 16 (Christmas Night). The movement begins ppp and ends ppp, never mf or mp (PDF 136). Nothing to change.
