## Findings

The findings below are ranked by how hard the reading presses on each place. **1. 15-22: the oboe/solo-violin dialogue has a real middleground. Recorded; this closes cycle 7's open item ("overlapping dialogue and varied lower-string rhythm in 15-22").**
In each bar where the leading voice holds, a lower voice answers in the rhythm of the bar just heard. At 16, while the oboe holds F5, the violas play D♭4-C4-B♭3 in the oboe's 15 rhythm (dotted quarter, eighth, quarter). At 18 the upper cellos answer the solo violin's 17 rhythm (eighth, eighth, quarter, quarter) on C4-E♭4-C4-D♭4. The violas answer again at 20 and the cellos at 22, and the cellos' answer at 22 leads into their counter-melody at 23. Under this, Vln II holds a louré A♭4 pedal and the cellos keep the bass. Adler's Brahms analysis asks for exactly this: the background "serves to add intensity to the longer notes of the melody, leaving the figure undisturbed" (PDF 135). The answers sound only on held notes, so the rule that each layer has its own rhythm (PDF 142, 144 no. 4) holds in practice: the two rhythms never sound together. The Vln II rest at 20 b1-2 is Mozart's "sudden cessation" of the accompaniment under the second phrase (PDF 136-137, V-13). The basses rest 15-20 and enter at 21 for the approach to the half cadence. No change.

**2. 45-46: the third statement of theme B over a sustained background. Recorded.**
The oboe carries the theme with Bsn 1 and the cellos an octave below (D♭5/D♭4). Vln II holds F4, the violas F3 then D♭3-B♭2, the basses B♭1-G♭1, all pp and sustained. Two points of the reading touch this. First, the background stops being a figure after eight bars of triplets (37-44). That is a change of texture for a new statement (PDF 136, V-12), and it clears the ground for the quotation at 47. Second, Vln II's F4 sits inside the octave of the doubled theme. Adler says the register warning matters most when foreground and background share a colour (PDF 135). Here the held string note lies between an oboe and a cello line, which differ in colour from it and from each other, it is a whole note against moving eighths, and it is pp under p. c044 already moved it off the cellos' D♭4. No change.

**3. 48-51: on-and-off-beat accompaniment under the quotation. Recorded.**
Mendelssohn's "It is enough" (V-32, PDF 158) puts the off-beat chords in the upper strings over a bass that plays on the beat. Bars 48-51 follow it: violas play off-beat tenuto eighths, sotto voce. The cellos and basses play half notes on the beat (F♯2 | F♯2-B1-C♯2 | F♯2-B1), and Vln II holds the chord (c274 put the third on a beat at 50). So the off-beats always have an on-beat partner, and the cor anglais quotation is the only line moving in longer slurred values. The quotation and its accompaniment are protected. No change.

**4. 37-44: the bass line follows Brahms's pizzicato model and gains motion in the second phrase. Recorded.**
At 37-40 the basses play pizzicato on beats 1 and 3 with the harp's left hand, as in V-7 ("pizzicato in the double basses", PDF 135). At 41-43 they pluck every beat, and the cellos come in arco under the cor anglais. This is Adler's "addition of the cellos" to set off a second statement (PDF 139, V-16). The bass is an independent rhythmic layer under the triplets and the tune, so all three layers differ in rhythm (PDF 142-144). No change.

**5. 7-14 against 23 and 71: the order of colours for theme A follows Brahms. Recorded.**
Adler's argument in rescoring V-7 is that a first statement placed too high makes the later ones anticlimactic (PDF 133). Here the first statement is horn 1 at A♭3-B♭4 with winds and harp only. The second is the violins in unison at A♭4, with the cellos' counter-melody and divided violas (23). The third is the violins in octaves A♭4/A♭5 (71), rising to B♭6 at 79. The high violin colour is saved for the return, as Brahms saves it (PDF 135). The doubled violins at 23 in a middle register match Schumann's V-18 (PDF 141). No change; 7-14 and 23-30 are protected.

**6. 63-70: the Carmen and Fauré backgrounds in sequence. Recorded.**
At 63-64 the Tbn 1 quotation is accompanied by tremolo upper strings (Vln I E6-A6, Vln II E5-C6, violas E4-C5) and cellos on A2, with one sf pizzicato A1 in the basses. This is Bizet's "cliché device" for an ominous colour (PDF 161, V-37). At 65-66 the strings' E♭7 chord fades to niente. From 67 the basses alone hold E♭2 (four soli, sul A) under horn 1, with the timpani roll and the bassoons. That is the lower-string sombre background of V-38 (PDF 162), thinned to one section. The Carmen-to-Fauré order makes the texture thinner as the music calms, which is the scoring diminuendo of V-29 (PDF 154). The quotation and the solo-horn retransition are protected. No change.

**7. 83-92: the coda thins by scoring; the bass stays. Recorded.**
At 83-86 only the lower strings play: four solo cellos and basses on the A♭ pedal, with violas entering divided ppp at 84 b3, under the harp harmonics and the timpani answer. This is Fauré's dark string background (PDF 162). Vln II returns on A♭4 at 87 and Vln I on C5 at 89, while the cor anglais, horns 3·4 and clarinet 1 stop at 90 and only clarinet 2 holds to the end. That is Tchaikovsky's diminuendo made by scoring, with every instrument in a register where pp is easy (PDF 154). Tchaikovsky's other device, dropping the bass each time it reaches the tonic, is not used. The A♭ pedal under the plagal close is protected, and its 16-foot root is what makes the close sound complete. No change.

**8. 23, 61, 79, 87: spacing and first inversions. Recorded (c207, c347, c387).**
At 23 the strings are spaced A♭2-E♭3-C4-E♭4-A♭4, with intervals narrowing upward as in V-26 (PDF 151). At 61 and 79 the bottom is fifths and octaves with the theme in three octaves on top. At 15 (I6) the bass C3 is not doubled in the strings. The third of IV6 at 87 is doubled only at the bottom (V-27, PDF 152). No change.
