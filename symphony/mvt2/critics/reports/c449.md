## Holistic judgement

**Against Brahms 3/ii.**

- **Opening.** Brahms opens with a soft wind chorale and the strings held back. This movement does the same in a darker colour: solo horn over bassoons, bass clarinet and harp at 7-14, with no strings. Both movements keep the first theme in the winds and save the string sound for a later return (here bar 23, then the four-layer A′ at 71).
- **Cyclic second idea.** Brahms's clarinet-and-bassoon second theme is the one the finale brings back. The structural job is the same here: the cor anglais quotation at 47-53 and the trombone quotation at 63-64 import movement I's themes once each, and the last bars hand G♯4/A♭ to the scherzo's horn.
- **Coda.** Brahms closes by recalling second-theme fragments over a quiet string bed. The coda at 83-92 recalls theme A's head (harp harmonics), answers it with the timpani, and ends on the motto tail (clarinet) over a plagal string close. Its weight and length are about the same as Brahms's.
- **Dynamic ceiling.** Brahms's Andante has a lower ceiling than this movement. This movement has two ff peaks (61-62, 79-81). That fits its Dvořák 9/ii model better, and Adler PDF 120-type staging and PDF 37 (brass held back) support it.

A conductor reading this score would find nothing that points to a student hand: every return is newly scored, every tutti is complete and balanced, and soft endings avoid Adler's "dangerous" chord.

## Findings

No finding stands between this score and a master's work. I checked each of the 36 principles above against the bars listed, and none is violated. Two items were close to findings, and I record them so later critics do not reopen them without a new reason:

1. **61-62, Hn 3·4 a2 up to written G♭5 (Adler PDF 26, Schuller).** This is the passage Schuller warns about. It is marked f, not ff, and Cl 2 doubles it at pitch, so it does not meet his condition of "high passage at a loud dynamic". c086's decision and its Sebesky reasons stand. If a performance shows that the note cracks, the fallback is the one recorded at c169: Hn 4 `f'1--\f | ges'2-- aes'2--` (a third below Hn 3). No change proposed.
2. **79-81, theme A at the peak has no brass colour (Adler PDF 178, rule 1).** Meistersinger gives every theme a string, a woodwind and a brass instrument. Here only the counter-melody has all three. Trumpets are tacet in this movement by design (c024, c060), and horns on the theme would take them off the counter-melody. No change proposed.
