## Findings

### 1. Bars 61-62: at the ff climax both clarinets sit in the throat register, and clarinet 2 only doubles horn 2. **Applied.**
**Problem.** Clarinet 2 plays F4 | G♭4-A♭4 concert (written G4 | A♭4-B♭4). All three are throat tones. They are also the exact pitches of horn 2 (`f'1 | ges'2 aes'2`, f) and an octave above trombone 2. Clarinet 1's A♭4 on 61 beats 1-2 is also a throat note.

The F3-A♭4 band at 61 already holds trombone 2, bassoon 1, cellos, horn 4, horn 2, clarinet 2, clarinet 1, horn 3, violas and Vln II. The band F5-A♭5 holds only oboe 1, oboe 2 and Vln II.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 257 says every segment of the range has its own "timbre, strength, and texture", and he uses the clarinet as his example of an instrument whose timbre changes with pitch. PDF 277-278: an instrument in unison whose spectrum is masked by a stronger one adds only upper content. A pale throat tone under a horn at f adds nothing a listener can hear. PDF 287: the fewer instruments in a range, the clearer and more present each one is. Adler part 1, PDF 210, calls the throat "rather pale" and the clarino "bright, incisive". c078 raised this point. The composer kept it open pending an octave check (v79 critique, item 3). That check is done below.

**Fix (tested).** Swap the lines. Clarinet 1 follows oboe 1 from C5 to F5 and takes horn 2's line two octaves up, in the clarino. Clarinet 2 takes clarinet 1's old line: A♭4 held over from bar 60, then D♭5, then B♭4-C♭5 with horn 3. The parts do not cross on the shared staff, and both stay ff.
```
bSecClOne, bar 61:  \partCombineAutomatic f''1--\ff | ges''2-- aes''2-- | R1*2 |
bSecClTwo, bar 61:  \partCombineAutomatic aes'2--\ff des''2-- | bes'2-- ces''2-- | R1*2 |
```
**Octave check.** allpairs_par.py gives the same number of octave pairs at 61 b3 and 62 b3 before and after. They have only moved between clarinet tracks. All of them are continuous doublings of lines that were already doubled: the theme head with flute 2 and the cellos, and F-G♭-A♭ with horn 2 and trombone 2.

By hand I checked these moves:
- Clarinet 1's C5→F5 moves with oboe 1 in unison and is a third over the bass both times.
- G♭5→A♭5 against the bass G♭→F♭ goes octave → third.
- Against horn 3 and clarinet 2 (B♭→C♭) it moves in parallel sixths.

No exposed fifths or octaves appear. Pitch classes, the order of entries and the dynamics are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 61-62: the timpani roll is ff under brass marked f and mf. **Applied.**
**Problem.** c054 and c076 set the brass deliberately at f for the horns and mf for the trombones and tuba, so that the theme in the winds and strings (ff) stays on top. The D♭ roll under them is ff for two bars. That makes it the loudest sustained low sound in the tutti.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 290 and Fig. 6.6: a sound held at a constant level, like a compressed timpani note, "becomes perceptually more present" and "thicker". A roll is exactly that kind of sound. Adler part 2, PDF 184 (Bloch): "a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra." c069 finding 4 made the same point. No reason for keeping ff was recorded. The A′ peak at 79 already has the timpani at f under the horns at f, so this change also makes the two climaxes consistent.

**Fix (tested).** `bSecTimp` bar 61: `des1:32\f | des2:32 aes,4:32~ aes,8 r8 | R1*2 |`. The p< f roll at 60 now arrives at f and holds it.

### 3. Bars 63-64: the violas' C4-E4 tremolo sits inside trombone 1's quotation. **Not applied (low priority).**
**Problem.** The quotation runs E3-C4-B3-B♭3-A3 | A3-G♯3-A3-F3-E3. The violas' double stop C4-E4 starts sf on the same bar and holds C4, the trombone's own pitch on beat 2, for a bar and a half. c074 moved the cello tremolo down to A2 for this reason and left the violas where they were.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 287: sounds in the same range mask each other, and an orchestrator may keep other instruments out of a solo line's range. The problem is limited for two reasons: the violas make a diminuendo from sf to p across bar 63, and the trombone is ff marcato, which is the denser spectrum and comes forward (PDF 289).

**Possible fix.** `bSecVla` bars 63-64: `e'1:32\sf\> | e'2:32\p gis2(\> |`. This keeps the F♭-E thread in the violas. It loses the C♭4-C4 inner step, and leaves the third, C, to Vln II's C6 and the trombone. The gain is small and there is a cost, so I leave the decision to the composer.
