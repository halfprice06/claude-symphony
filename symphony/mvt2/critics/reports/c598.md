### 1. Timpani, bars 67-79: "ord." comes one stroke too late (applied)

**Problem.** The timpani play "near the rim" ppp at 67-70, then rest. At 77 they take medium sticks, and at 78 b3 they play an E♭ roll pp< into the f D♭ roll at 79. The "ord." that cancels "near the rim" is printed at 79. So the 78 b3 roll still falls under "near the rim". c397 added that roll after c056 had put "ord." at 79, and the mark was not moved. A timpanist who keeps the playing spot until told otherwise would start the climax crescendo at the rim, with a thinner and less sustained sound, exactly where c397 wanted a roll that drives the crescendo (Adler part 2 PDF 184). Nothing in the score says that rim-to-centre is intended.

**Evidence.** Adler part 1 PDF 53: "Every time a special effect ... [is] used, one must insert the indication naturale or in modo ordinario in the score at the point where the player should resume his normal playing attitude." PDF 70 applies the same rule to every device: give the exact technique in the score so the player does not have to guess. The strings already do this everywhere, for example the violins' "ord." at 27, 57 and 75, the cellos' at 41, 48 and 78, and Vln II's at 45 after the sul tasto.

**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoTimp; applied in /tmp/critic-598/work).** Move the word from 79 to the roll at 78 b3:

```lilypond
% was: | r2 es4:32\pp\< | des2.:32\f^\markup \small \italic "ord." | des2.:32 |
| r2 es4:32\pp\<^\markup \small \italic "ord." | des2.:32\f | des2.:32 |
```

This changes text only, with no pitch, rhythm, dynamic or MIDI change. In the score, "ord." prints above the 78 b3 roll on page 6, clear of "medium sticks" at 77. The layout is unchanged. In the timpani part it prints under "poco allargando" at 78. The part stays one page.

### 2. Double basses, bar 40: pizzicato half notes (optional, not applied)

**Problem.** In 37-44 the plucked bass is written in quarters with rests. The exception is bar 40, which has two half notes (A1, B♭1) doubled by the harp. A plucked note does not last a half note at ♩=60, so the notation suggests a held sound that the players cannot give.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 58: pizzicato "is similar to staccato and spiccato bowing in that the sound dies away quickly". When a composer wants a plucked note to ring, the note is written with a tie into nothing or "l.v.". The same page notes that the double bass sustains longest, and c208 accepted bar 40 on that basis. c585 raised the same point as optional.

**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecCb, bar 40).** Either `a,,4 r4 bes,,4 r4 |` (matching 37-39), or keep the halves and add a tie into nothing on each, `a,,2\laissezVibrer bes,,2\laissezVibrer |`, to say that they ring. Performance would barely change. I did not apply it, because the harp's ringing half notes already carry the length and c208 judged the bar acceptable. I list it only so the composer can close c585's open item one way or the other.
