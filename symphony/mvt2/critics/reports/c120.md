## Findings

### 1. Bars 89–92: movement II still ends in movement I's ending scoring (applied)

**Problem.** Movement I ends (181–182) with a ppp C timpani roll under Vln I's C5 and the tonic in the low strings. Movement II ends (89–92) with a ppp A♭ timpani roll under Vln I's C5 and the tonic in the low strings. Cycle 50 removed movement II's closing harp because it "repeats movement I's last scoring (harp ppp + timp roll under Vln I C5)" (coda.ily comment). The timpani half of that scoring is still there. The two endings are different chords, but they are the same sound, about six minutes apart. The relay to movement III already lists it as a fact III has to work around ("both end on Vln I C5 ppp over a soft tonic timpani roll").

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 448–449: a large-scale plan "involves more than key areas"; the succession of large events should be "no more haphazard than ... chords in a single well-composed phrase". Two consecutive movements ending on one gesture make the second sound like a repeat of the first, not the next step. Dvořák's Largo, the model, takes its timpani out before the last bars (corpus file above). The timpani already have their melodic moment in this coda (84–85, E♭–C–A♭ in theme A's rhythm), which is the role the cyclic plan gives them. The roll after it only doubles the basses' A♭.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-120/work, sections/coda.ily).**
```
codaTimp = {
  R2. | es4.\pp c8 aes,4~ | aes,2. | R2. |
  R2.*2 | R2.*3 | R2.\fermata |   % was R2.*2 | aes,2.:32\ppp~ | aes,2.:32~ | aes,2.:32~ | aes,2.:32\fermata |
}
```
The harmony, the D♭–C top voice and the Cl 2 morendo of the protected plagal close 87–92 do not change. Only a doubling of the bass goes, so the reason the passage is protected still holds. The timpani staff drops out of the last system, which stays on page 8. The movement now ends in strings and a clarinet, with no percussion. Movement III no longer inherits two soft-roll endings in a row.

### 2. The II→III join: two incompatible proposals in cyclic-notes (no score change; decide for III)

**Problem.** cyclic-notes.md says both "Movement III could start from movement II's last chord, add F♯ to make a German sixth, and go to V of C" (relay c010) and "open with a contrasting attack after a pause, not quietly from C" (relay c050). The first implies attacca, the second a pause. Movement II v120 ends with a final barline and fermatas, which fits the pause.

**Evidence and proposal.** PDF 443: a pivot is a chord that belongs to both keys. PDF 449 (Ex. 26-8): Mozart opens his development with the main idea in C major, and later events show that the chord is V of IV, not a tonic. A chord is reinterpreted after it has been heard. Both proposals can be kept: pause after II, then open III f or ff on II's sonority made into a German sixth (A♭ bass, C, E♭, F♯), resolving to V of C. III's first sound recalls where II stopped and changes its meaning at once. That gives the "contrasting attack", and III does not open from C. Record this in cyclic-notes and drop the attacca reading of the c010 item.

### 3. What movement III must do to complete the key plan (no score change)

Movement II carries out its part of the plan: A♭ is VI of C minor (PDF 443), its middle section is IV by phrase modulation (PDF 452), its internal A♭–F minor–D♭ is the descending-thirds plan of Ex. 26-13 (PDF 453), and nothing in it resolves B♮ to C. At symphony level the plan so far is I (C minor) – VI (A♭). The reading's large plans show what must follow:
- **VI must lead on.** A♭ is either the upper neighbour of V (PDF 456, Ex. 26-17; review point 12) or the first third of I–VI–IV (PDF 452–454, Ex. 26-14; Beethoven's I–VI–IV–V–I). III should pass through F minor (IV) and then hold G with B♮ in the bass before its reprise (PDF 449, 451: the leading tone returns before the recapitulation). This confirms critic 110's "leave A♭ by way of G" and the mvt I c120 note, and grounds both in Unit 26.
- **Only one C-major tonic.** If III states its main idea in C major before the end, follow K. 457 (Ex. 26-8): make it V of F minor, so the final C major is the first C-major tonic since movement I's last chord.
- **Owed items that II does not take:** the trumpets' B→C (II's trumpets are tacet; II has no rising B♮); C7 as the violins' peak (I: A♭6; II: A6 at 63, B♭6 at 76 and 79); "senza sord." for Hn 3·4; the viola's D♯→E augmented sixth closing the E♭/E♮ thread; the full motto with its rising sixth (II states only the tail, so III's major G–E sixth arrives fresh); no soft opening roll or held C.

### 4. Bars 33 and 64: movement I's stop chord, unresolved (no change; c111 confirmed)

Movement I's stop chord at 26 (and 138) is B–D–F–A♭ with B on top (MIDI: B1, B2, B4, B5, D, F, A♭): vii°7 of C. Movement II has the same pitch classes twice: 33 b2 (CA B4, B.cl D4, Bsn 1 F3 over the A♭ pedal, pp) and 64 b3 (D2, F3, A♭3, B3, as vii°7/V of A♭). Each time B falls (to B♭ at 33; to E7/E♭7 after 64). Neither resolves to C. That is Ex. 26-7's foreshadowing used across movements (PDF 448): the chord that will lead to C in III is heard in II without its resolution. Since c111, the cor anglais puts B4 on top at 33, as movement I puts B5 on top at 26, which makes the recall clearer. Keep c111. The common-tone diminished seventh at 33 is protected anyway, and moving the cor anglais back down would bury the B. For III: resolve B–D–F–A♭ to C once, with the trumpets' B→C.
