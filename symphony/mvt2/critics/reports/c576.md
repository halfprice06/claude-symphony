- C.a.: after 50 (bSec.ily, c066).

The longest stretch between two breaths is under 20 s at the printed tempos. That is the standing breathing plan in HISTORY. Appendix A gives ranges, not breath lengths, but it puts every one of these notes in the middle of its instrument (PDF 304-306), where breath support is easiest. No change.

### 2. Notes outside Adler's amateur column (recorded, no change)

The professional column applies here (BRIEF.md: a professional orchestra). The amateur column still shows where the hard notes are. Only these fall outside it, all at the two ff climaxes, approached by step or held:
- Fl 1 A♭6 (61-62) and A6 (63 sf), above the amateur top of about G6 (PDF 303). Fl 1 breathes at the end of 60. B♭6 at 79 has a breath before it at 78.

- Cl 1 written C6 at 79 and Vln II C6 at 63-64, exactly at their amateur tops.

Flute and violin both speak easily at the top when loud, and these notes are loud. B♭6 at pitch is protected (c075). No change.

### 3. Timpani: A♭2→C3 on one drum at 5-6 (recorded, no change)

The chained muta at 5-6 (G→A♭, A♭→C, C→D♭) keeps the drums in order from low to high. It puts C3 on the drum that held A♭2. On a 28" drum that is the top of the chart's range (F2-C3, PDF 307), where the head is tight and the tone thin. If the 25" drum takes the C3 instead (B♭2-F3), the C3 is easy. The player decides this. The C3 sounds only once, as the eighth note in the pp figure E♭3-C3-A♭2 at 84. The wording also tells the player which drums change, which Adler's "Sol muta in Mi" model asks for (PDF 312). The tuning and the muta at 4-6 are protected (HISTORY c041, c092, c566). No change.
