## Findings

### 1. Theme B's first phrase, 37-40 and 53-56, is all steps (recorded, no change)

- **Problem considered.** `f''2 ges''8 f'' es''4 | des''4 c''8 des'' es''2 | ges''4 \tuplet 3/2 {f''8 ges'' f''} es''4 des''4 | c''4 des''8 c'' bes'2`. The range is a minor sixth, B♭4-G♭5, and there is no leap. Sebesky asks whether the tune "is weak in some respect" (PDF 221). He also says that "even in soft, subtle background passages, your lead line should have some character" (PDF 210).
- **Why it passes.**
  - The phrase is not Ex. 278. Ex. 278 is undifferentiated whole notes. This phrase has a fixed cell, the neighbour note in `2 8 8 4` and `4 8 8 2`, and one triplet turn at the third bar. That is what makes it recognisable on its return at 53 and as the B♭-minor head at 44-46.
  - The leaps are in the consequent, `aes4 des f4. ges8 | aes4.`. Its rising arpeggio also links theme B to theme A's opening. This is Sebesky's recurrent offsetting figure (PDF 206) working between the themes.
  - c291 closed the point, citing the Largo's fifth-wide middle tune, and theme B's line is on the do-not-touch list (c141). I have no new argument that answers either.
- **Optional fix, if 37-40 is ever reopened (not recommended).** One leap at the third bar, `ges''4( \tuplet 3/2 4 { f''8 ges'' f'' } es''4 bes'4) | c''4 des''8 c'' bes'2`, keeps the cell and the cadence pitch. It must change at 53-56 as well, or the return no longer matches.

### 2. A′, 77-82: the cadence descent E♭6-D♭6-C6-B♭5-A♭5 is heard twice at the same pitch (recorded, no change)

- **Problem considered.** In Vln I and Fl 1, 77 is `g''4 es'''8 des''' c''' bes''`. After the B♭6 at 79, 81 is `g'''4 es'''8 des''' c''' bes''`. Both land on A♭5 (78, 82). A reader could take this as a missed chance to vary the climax's continuation.
- **Why it passes.**
  - The first descent ends on the deceptive ♭VI at 78, with the bass on F♭. The second ends on the tonic. The repeat after a deceptive cadence is the cadence's second attempt, so the same pitches end differently. The Canzonetta does the same with its two-bar cadence figure.
  - Sebesky's colour point (PDF 223, "prayer") is met between the two descents. The colour goes on the long note, the B♭6 held for a bar and a third, and on the run into it at 78 b3. It does not go on the passing eighths.
  - "Cadence 77-82" and "B♭6 at 79 only" are on the standing list (c423 records 77-81 as a ♭VI neighbour with an interpolation).

### 3. Theme A is stated twice and breaks out the second time (follows)

- **What I checked.** Horn 7-14: `aes4. c'8 es'4~ | es'4 d' des' | c'4. bes8 aes4 | g2 es8 f | aes4. c'8 f'4~ | f'4 bes'4 aes'4 | g'4 es'8 des' c' bes | aes2.`
  - The range is a ninth, A♭3-B♭4. Leaps are no larger than a fourth.
  - The line uses one rhythmic cell (`4. 8 4~`). It peaks once, on the B♭ appoggiatura at bar 6 of 8, reached by a rising fourth.
  - Two more peaks come in order later in the movement: A6 at 63 (sf) and B♭6 at 79 (ff). Each is reached once.
- **Comparison.** This is the Canzonetta's method on a two-statement plan. Tchaikovsky uses three statements and breaks out on the third. In A′ the break-out is enlarged: the octave leap at 77, the run at 78, and the B♭6 at 79. I can sing theme A after reading it once. That is the test this lens asks for.
- **Verdict.** No change.
