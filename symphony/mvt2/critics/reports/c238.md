## Findings

These are not the earlier passes' points again. They are this pass's checks on hypermeter, felt tempo and the placement of the two climaxes. I found nothing that the reading shows to be wrong and that a change would clearly improve, so every finding is recorded without a change. The first one names a place where the composer could act if he wants more contrast.

### 1. Bars 37–44: the "Poco più mosso" changes the beat, not the felt pace (recorded; optional fix, not applied)
- **What the reading says.** A&S PDF 49: tempo is felt from "the pace of the beats", and that pace "relates to tonal movement, to the frequency with which chords change, or to the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody". It is not felt from the quickest notes. PDF 47 explains why the quickest values do not set the tempo.
- **Measurement.**
  - Bars 7–14 (B.cl. bass at ♩=52): 14 chord changes in 27.7 s, one every 1.98 s.
  - Bars 37–44 (Cb bass at ♩=60): 16 changes in 32 s, one every 2.0 s.
  - Theme B's main tones are halves and dotted quarters (`f''2 … es''2`), about as slow as theme A's `4. 8 4~`.
  - The pizzicato bass and the harp sound on beats 1 and 3 in 37–39, 41 and 43–44, so the bass pulse is the half note (30 a minute).
  - So by the reading's three criteria, B has the same felt pace as A. Only the 15% faster quarter and the violas' triplet eighths (180 a minute) carry the "più mosso", and the reading says the quickest notes do not decide tempo.
- **Verdict.** It still follows, because the marking is "poco" and the triplets do add motion. Dvořák's più mosso gets its motion from the bass, which walks in pizzicato eighths. c171 said B "is felt as quicker than A". The numbers show that the difference comes from surface motion, not from the pace of the chords.
- **Fix (not applied; texture more than expression).** Do not raise the metronome mark. A faster ♩ would also speed up the 47–53 quotation, which is protected. If the composer wants the section to feel more mosso, change the bass rhythm in the bars that now pluck only on 1 and 3. For example, at 38 `bes,,4 des,4 ges,,4 bes,,4` replaces `bes,,4 r4 ges,,4 r4` (arpeggiated chord tones, harp LH unchanged). I did not test this. It would change the pizzicato bass pattern that has been in place since v01, so it needs a texture critic's judgement. It would leave c006's protected pizz.→arco change at 44–45 untouched.

### 2. Bars 53–61 and 75–79: both ff climaxes are on hypermetric downbeats, with the broadening in the weak bar before them (recorded)
- **What the reading says.** PDF 53: in a four-bar group bars 1 and 3 are strong. PDF 54: a phrase ends on a goal of tonal motion. PDF 51: the most natural rhythm puts long notes on strong beats.
- **Score at 61.**
  - The build 53–60 is an 8-bar phrase (53–56, 57–60).
  - Its tonal goal, the first root-position D♭ tonic of theme B (held back since 37, c211), comes in bar 61. Bar 61 is therefore the end of one phrase and the strong first bar of the next group (61–64).
  - This is the hypermetric form of the overlapping groups in Ex. 3-11.
  - The ff and "Largamente" fall on that downbeat, with long held notes: Fl 1 A♭6 and Vln I D♭6.
  - The slowing ("poco allargando", 60 b1) is in the weak fourth bar before it.
- **Score at 79.**
  - The group 75–78 ends on the deceptive cadence at 78, so its goal is evaded.
  - The ff B♭6 dotted half arrives on the downbeat of the third four-bar group (79).
  - "Poco allargando" is again in the weak bar before it (78).
- **Intermediate peaks.** The f at 59 is on bar 3 of 57–60. The trombone quotation at 63 is on bar 3 of 61–64. The local theme-A peaks (12 mp, 28 f, 76 hidden mf) fall on the weak sixth bar of each phrase, where the theme puts its B♭. c171 recorded that this is intended, so that the downbeat B♭6 at 79 keeps its effect.
- **Fix:** none.

### 5. Bars 15–22: four two-bar swells at p, with the high point on hypermetric bar 3 (recorded)
- **What the reading says.** PDF 53: two-bar groups pair into a four-bar hypermeasure, and two hypermeasures make the 8-bar phrase.
- **Score.**
  - Oboe and solo violin alternate in two-bar units: 15–16, 17–18, 19–20, 21–22. Each is marked p with a crescendo and diminuendo, and each peaks on the long note at the start of its weak second bar.
  - The phrase's highest notes (A♭5–B♭5) are at 17, the strong third bar of the first hypermeasure.
  - From there the line falls by sequence to the half cadence at 22.
  - The bass's c237 rise D♭–E♭–F♭ under the French sixth at 21 gives the goal its chromatic accent (PDF 50) without a dynamic mark.
- **Considered and rejected.** I considered raising the second hypermeasure one level, for example the solo violin mp at 21. I reject it. The descending sequence wants to fall, not rise. c139 deliberately keeps everything under the solo violin at 17–18 and 21 free of swells. And the a′ statement at 23 needs b to stay the quietest of the three A phrases (horn p–mp, b p, violins mp–f).
- **Fix:** none.
