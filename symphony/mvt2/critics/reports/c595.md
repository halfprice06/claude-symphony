## Findings

### 1. Bars 72-74, bassoons: bassoon 2 alone, printed under bassoon 1's whole-bar rests with forced down-stems (applied)
**Problem.** Bassoon 1 rests 72-74 (`R2.*3`). The part-combiner stays in apart mode, so the staff prints three whole-bar rests at the top for bassoon 1 and bassoon 2's quarters and halves with every stem down, B♭2 and A♭2 included, with no "2." label. The clarinet staff directly above, in the same bars, shows clarinet 2 alone the normal way ("2.", natural stems), so the two shared staves now read differently in one system. This is the same case c205 fixed at 57-58.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 65: the up/down rule is for double-stemmed writing, two parts on one stave. With one part playing, PDF 60's rule applies: "The notehead furthest away from the centre of the stave determines the stem direction"; A♭2 (73) and B♭2 (74) are below the middle line and take up-stems. PDF 61: keep "a consistent stem direction within a phrase, bar or half-bar", which decides bar 72 (F3, B♭2, E♭3) in favour of down-stems.
**Fix.** aTwo.ily, aTwoBsnTwo:
```
  c2.\pp | \partCombineSoloII f4( \tweak Stem.direction #DOWN bes,4 es4 | aes,2 f4 | bes,2) r4 |
  \partCombineAutomatic aes,4(\p\< c4 des4 | ...
```
The slur has to start at 72, not 71: a slur that opens in the combiner's "two" voice cannot close in its "solo" voice (my first test gave "cannot end slur" and "already have slur"). So C3 at 71 is now a single note and F3 at 72 is tongued. The basses change bow at 72 on the same F, so the extra tongued attack lines up with them. If the composer wants the 71-72 legato kept, the alternative is to leave apart mode and accept the forced stems. Applied and checked: "2." at 72, the rests are gone, the B♭2 half at 74 takes an up-stem, and the Bsn 2 part shows the slur from 72.

### 2. Bars 3-6, horns 3·4: the slur runs just under horn 4's A♭3 ties (applied)
**Problem.** In chord mode (c125), c328's slur on horn 3's C4–D♭4–C4 is placed below the chord, on the notehead side. It runs one to two points under the A♭3 ties for four bars, so at 3-4 the slur and tie look like one doubled curve, and the slur seems to belong to horn 4's held pedal rather than to horn 3's moving line.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 60 allows the one-stem chord only because both players have the same durations. That makes the chord a single object, so any slur on it has to be clearly separate from the ties to read correctly. The rules for slur and tie placement are in chapters outside this excerpt, so this finding rests on the 600 dpi crop and not on a quoted rule.
**Fix.** intro.ily, introHnThree: `\partCombineChords c'2.~^(\pp`. With the slur above, it sits on the stem side over horn 3's line. The ties stay where they were: C4 up, A♭3 down. Applied and checked. The slur stays clear of the horns 1·2 staff, and the "1. solo" label at 6 does not move.

### 4. Bar 61 bassoons and bar 62 flutes: a semibreve and a minim a third apart, heads touching (recorded, no change)
At Bsn 61 b1, the minim F3 (bassoon 1, stem up) sits on top of bassoon 2's semibreve D♭3. At Fl 62 b1, flute 1's tied semibreve sits above flute 2's minim B♭6 (stem down). PDF 62 says heads a third apart stay aligned; only seconds and unisons are offset. The stems start at the minims and do not reach the semibreves, so PDF 66's warning that semibreves must not "appear to be joined to stems" is met. The heads touch only as much as they would in any chord of a third. No change.
