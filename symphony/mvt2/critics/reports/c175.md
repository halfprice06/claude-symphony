## Findings

Ranked by how much each would mislead a player or conductor reading the page.

### 1. Bars 79-80: the ties on B♭6/B♭5/B♭4 in Vln I, Vln II and Vla are not printed in the score. **Applied.**
**Problem.** The source has `bes'''2.)~\ff | bes'''4(\upbow aes'''2)` (Vln I; Vln II and Vla the same in their octaves). The score at 79-80 shows the slur ending on 79 and nothing joining 79 to 80, so bar 80 reads as a re-attacked B♭ on an up-bow at ff. The winds' ties in the same bars print, and the ties print in the parts book. In a diagnostic build these three ties had all four control points identical (zero length); they are the only degenerate ties or slurs among the 327 in the score.
**Cause.** c125's `\override Script.Y-offset = #stem-side-y-offset` is a bare procedure, so LilyPond also runs it in the pure phase before line breaking. Its side-position call there formatted the ties next to the up-bow signs at 80 before the note columns had positions. In the unfixed build the tie stencils are produced during "Preprocessing graphical objects". Without the override, or with the fix below, the ties print normally.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*): ⊓ V is marked over one sustained note so that the bow changes inside the sound and the next attack is solid; "the bow change will hardly be noticed". c018 and c118 set up exactly this at 79-80. Adler PDF 37: with no slur or tie, each note is a new stroke. Without the tie the V asks for an audible restrike of the peak note.
**Fix (applied, score.ly line 64).**
`\override Script.Y-offset = #(ly:make-unpure-pure-container stem-side-y-offset ly:side-position-interface::pure-y-aligned-side)`
The callback still decides the final position (pages 1, 4, 6 and 8 are pixel-identical to v175, so c125's tenuto spacing is unchanged). It now uses LilyPond's own estimate during the pure phase.

### 2. Bars 22-24: "tutti" is printed at 22 b3, but the leader rejoins at 24. **Applied.**
**Problem.** The score says "tutti (gli altri con sord.), espr., sul D" at 22 b3. The part tells the solo player "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 24)" (c106), so for 22 b3-23 the section plays without its leader, and the full section first plays at 24. The conductor's score shows tutti at the wrong place and nothing at 24.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 32: when part of the section plays, "When all are to play again, the word 'all' (or tutti …) must appear on the score."
**Fix (applied, aOne.ily).** 22 b3, score and part: `"gli altri con sord.,"` / `"espr., sul D"` (was "tutti (gli altri con sord.),"). 24 b1, Vln I: `es''4\>^\markup \italic "tutti" d''4( des''4)`. The part's "(solo player …)" note is unchanged. Page 2 keeps its layout.

### 3. Bars 62 b3-63: the Vln I divisi is engraved as two voices; Adler writes div. as one chord. **Applied.**
**Problem.** `<< { aes'''2--^"div." } \\ { fes'''2-- } >>` and `<< { a'''1:32 } \\ { e'''1:32 } >>` give the lower half a down-stem with its tenuto inside the staff, separated from both notes. Both halves have the same rhythm, so two voices add nothing, and the lower tenuto reads as a stray mark.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 31, Ex. II-12: the divided double stop is one chord on one stem with "div." above it, next to the same chord marked "non div."; the word decides the performance. Ex. II-11 (Debussy) uses separate stems only where the halves move in different rhythms (as this score does correctly for the cellos at 18 and 22).
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** 62: `f'''2-- <fes''' aes'''>2--^\markup \italic "div." |`; 63: `<e''' a'''>1:32\sf\>^\markup \italic "trem." | e'''2:32\p^\markup \italic "unis." r2 |`. The pitches are unchanged. Because the halves now share one MIDI track, allpairs_par.py lists the lower half's F♭6-E6 at 62 b3-63 in octaves with Vln II, the cellos, oboe 1 and bassoon 1. That octave doubling of the line is the same sound as before and was recorded by c159; nothing new is heard.

### 4. Bars 37, 41, 45: "pizz." and "arco" are the only string instructions in upright type. **Applied.**
**Problem.** `^"pizz."` and `^"arco"` (Cb 37, 45; Vla 41, 45) print in roman. Every other string instruction in the score and parts (con sord., div., unis., sul D, ord., trem., change bow at will) is `\markup \italic`. On page 3, the violas' upright "arco" stands next to Vln II's italic "unis., ord." in the same bar.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: pizz. and arco "must appear in both the score and the parts". They are technique words of the same class as the others. Gould, *Behind Bars* PDF 13: a convention, once set, should be used consistently.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** `^\markup \italic "pizz."` and `^\markup \italic "arco"` in the four places. The width is unchanged and the layout is unchanged.
