## What else I studied

Aldwell & Schachter Unit 21, PDF 352: anticipating the tone of resolution. In a homogeneous texture, the note a suspension resolves to should not sound in another voice before the resolution. The one harmless case is a 9–8 where that note is in the bass. With contrast of timbre the effect is milder, as with Beethoven's top-voice suspension against its resolution note an octave lower. I also used A&S Unit 27, PDF 465–469: a ninth over V resolves to a tone "already present in the bass … in a lower register", and when the dissonance sounds against its own resolution note above the bass, "the result is usually an unclear sonority". I ran allpairs_par.py and my own all-voice scan. The scan pairs every note of every track, including double stops, divisi and part-combined staves, and maps each chord's voices by nearest pitch. It found no parallel fifths. Its only P5 hit, Vla/B.tbn at 63 b1, is a mapping artefact: the violas keep E4 on the D string, as the c094 comment says. Every P8 it found is a deliberate doubling. Finally I reread the history for the horn lines at 29–30 (c052, c092, c094), 53–56 (c019, c038, c051) and 63–64 (c074, c094).

## Findings

### 1. Bar 64: the cellos sound trombone 1's resolution note before it resolves (applied)

Problem: The quotation ends `f4.-> e8` (64 b3–b4.5). On b3 the F3 is consonant over the bass D. On b4 the bass moves to E, so the F becomes a held ninth over E2 (V♭9 of A minor), and it resolves to E3 half a beat later. The cellos play `d4( e4)` and reach E3 on b4, a semitone under the trombone's F3 and half a beat before the trombone gets there. The quotation's last step, F–E, which is the motto's fall, is pre-empted in the same octave by a string line moving the other way.

Evidence: This is the case A&S PDF 352 calls poor: the resolution note anticipated in a voice other than the bass. It is not the harmless 9–8 case, because the bass is E2 (bass trombone and basses), not the cellos' E3. A&S PDF 469 describes the sound that results: a dissonance heard with its own resolution note in an inner voice gives an unclear sonority. Adler's point on PDF 42 (Hindemith: "the subject comes through clearly every time it is played") and on PDF 44 (Schoenberg's trombones independent against the rest) both require the quoted line's last step to be heard on its own. c074 already moved the cello tremolo at 63–64 from A3 to A2 to keep it out of the quotation's E3–C4 range, and b3–b4 is the one place where the cellos went back into it.

Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVc, bar 64): `a,2:32\p d4( e4) |` → `a,2:32\p d,4( e,4) |`. The cellos then double the bass trombone and basses on D2–E2. The trombone's F3 becomes a minor ninth over a bass that holds its resolution note, which is the normal 9–8, and the trombone resolves alone in its own octave. Bar 65's B♭2 follows E2 by a tritone, the same interval as before. Bass line, F♭/E–E♭ thread and trombone part are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 29: horn 3 pre-sounds the cello counter-melody's last D♭4 (applied)

Problem: Since c094, horn 3 holds D♭4 for the whole of bar 29, from beat 1, and falls to C4 at 30. The cellos' counter-melody, which is protected, goes `bes2( des'4) | c'2.`: it climbs back to D♭4 on 29 b3 and falls to C4. So the horn takes the cello's note two beats early and then moves with it in unison into the cadence. The counter-melody's last rise lands on a pitch that is already sounding in another colour, and the arrival is lost. c094 moved horn 3 off B♭3 because it was "the cellos' own note", and D♭4 is the cellos' next note.

Evidence: Adler PDF 42–44: in contrapuntal writing each line is placed so that it is heard as a line, and new entries are clear (row 4). PDF 44: horns joined to another line in unison take that line's weight and do not replace its attack. A&S PDF 352 makes the same point for a single tone: a note that is about to arrive should not already be sounding in another voice. c094's goal is kept: no open E♭3–B♭3 fifth in the horns, and the seventh of V7 is present and resolves to C4.

Fix (aOne.ily): `aOneHnThree = { R2.*22 | des'2.\pp | c'2. | }` → `aOneHnThree = { R2.*22 | r2 des'4\pp | c'2. | }`. Horn 3 enters with the cellos on 29 b3. On beats 1–2, horn 4's E♭3 doubles the bass root. Horn 4 and the strings are unchanged. The shared staff now prints pp at 29 b1 (horn 4) and again at b3 (horn 3). If the composer prefers one marking, `-\tag #'part` on horn 3's pp keeps the score to one.
