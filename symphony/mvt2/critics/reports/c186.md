## Findings

### 1. Double basses 75–81: the ff at the second climax falls on an up-bow (applied)
**Bars:** aTwo.ily, Cb 75–81.
**Problem:** Counting bows from 71 (tutti entry, down-bow): 72 U D U, 73 D U, 74 D, 75 U D U, 76 D U, 77 D, 78 U D, so 79 D♭2 ff is an up-bow, and so is the f at 78. In the same bars c118 arranged the violins, violas and cellos so that the ff and f are down-bows and the diminuendo at 81 is a down-bow. The basses, which carry the bass of the second peak with the contrabassoon, were left with the weakest stroke at the loudest moment.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog"; a crescendo is natural on an up-bow and a diminuendo on a down-bow; mark bow direction "only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". Ex. II-19 (Schubert 5/ii) uses two up-bows so that the accented downbeat is a down-bow. Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus) sets up the bowing so that the ff attack is solid. c096 applied the same rule to the basses at 60–61.
**Fix (applied):** a down-bow retaken at the start of the second phrase (75, after the half cadence), so that 77's crescendo is an up-bow and 78 f and 79 ff are down-bows. A second up-bow at 80 b3, where the winds breathe, so that 81's diminuendo is a down-bow:
```
aes,,4\p\<\downbow c,4 des,4 | des,2 bes,,4 | es,2. | fes,2\f es,4 |
des,2.\ff | des,2 bes,,4\upbow | es,2.\> | aes,,2.~\p\> |
```

### 2. Violins II, violas and basses 45–47: crescendo on a down-bow and the fp on an up-bow (applied)
**Bars:** bSec.ily, Vln II, Vla, Cb 45–49.
**Problem:** All three enter at 45 on a whole note: Vln II after a rest, Vla and Cb coming back to arco after pizz. Counted from a down-bow, 46 b3's crescendo (ges'2\<, bes2\<, ges,,2\<) is a down-bow and 47's fp> (the C♯7 goal of the 3-bar group) is an up-bow at the tip. The cellos in the same bars were bowed by c118 (46 up-bow crescendo, 47 down-bow diminuendo). The other three lines were not.
**Evidence:** Adler PDF 38 as above. An fp is an attack at the frog, and Adler PDF 54 recommends planning the bowing around a change of technique (here pizz. to arco).
**Fix (applied):** `\upbow` on the 45 whole note in Vln II (`f'1\pp\upbow`), Vla (`f1\pp\upbow`) and Cb (`bes,,1\pp\upbow`). An up-bow entry at pp is the natural one. In Vln II this would turn the 52 b4 pickup into a down-bow, against Vln I in unison at 53, so 49 b3–4 goes on one bow (`fis''2 g''4( eis''4)`). With that, 52 b4 is again an up-bow and 53–56 is bowed exactly as before. In the violas, 48–51 are separated off-beats with rests, so the bowing at 52 stays free.

### 3. Contrabassoon 79–81 (c184), D♭1–B♭0–E♭1 at ff: playable, no change
B♭0 is the lowest note in Adler's range (PDF 230, Ex. VII-97: written B♭1, sounding B♭0). It arrives on 80 b3 as a quarter at ♩≈48, about 1.25 s. The breath after it takes about 0.3 s, which leaves enough time for a bottom note to speak at ff (Adler: "speaks a bit more slowly at the bottom"). The player has rested since 62, which meets Adler's request for "periodic rests" in the low register. Each half of the phrase takes about 7 s at ff, with the breath shared with B.cl. and Bsn 2 (c176). The part reads D♭2–B♭1–E♭2 with 2–3 ledger lines. One correction to the record: v185's critique says B♭0 is "reached by step". It is reached by a minor third from D♭1 and left by a fourth. That is harmless here: these are long notes at slow speed.
