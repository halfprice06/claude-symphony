## Findings

### 1. Bar 46 b1–2: the B♭-minor chord has no third (applied)

**Problem.** At 46 the theme B head in minor (Ob 1, Vc, Bsn 1) moves B♭–A♮–B♭ before rising to D♭ on beat 3. Under it, Vln II holds F4, the violas F3, Bsn 2 B♭2 and the basses B♭1. For two beats the sounding pitches are only B♭ and F, in five octaves (B♭1 to B♭4). The melody supplies the third at 45 (D♭) and again at 46 b3, but not on 46 b1–2. In bars 37–64 this is the only held chord that lacks its third.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 144: the illusion of chordal fullness "is made possible by the presence of the chordal third somewhere in the ensemble". PDF 145: when the cellos give up the third, the vocal line "now supplies the chordal third". Here the melody is on the root at that moment. A&S PDF 76: "Because of the empty sound and the lack of major or minor quality, the 3rd of the triad is never omitted except for special effects." Nothing in 45–47 makes a hollow sound part of the design. The hollow sound comes from the A♮ neighbour falling on the bare fifth.

**Fix (applied, `bSecVla`, bar 46).**
```lilypond
f1\pp^"arco" | des2 \once \override Staff.DynamicLineSpanner.staff-padding = #3 bes2\< | b1\fp\> |
```
The only change is the viola's F3 → D♭3 on 46 b1–2. The F stays in Vln II. The violas then move D♭3→B♭3 while the melody moves B♭3→D♭4, which is contrary motion with no parallels. I rejected D♭4 in Vln II as an alternative: it would sound the melody's D♭4 two beats before the melody arrives on it, which is the objection c097 made at 29. This is a pitch-only change, so it keeps the c124 rule (no new figure, colour, rhythm or dynamic). The resulting B♭2–D♭3 third lies above an octave gap to the bass B♭1 and is marked pp.

### 2. Bar 62 b1–2: clarinet 1's G♭5 makes a minor second over F5 in the upper register (applied)

**Problem.** In the G♭maj7 chord at 62 b1, F5 (Ob 1, Vln II) lies a semitone under Cl 1's G♭5. My scan of every sixteenth finds no other adjacent second above E5 anywhere in the movement. The same G♭/F clash already sounds in the lower-middle register, as Hn 2 G♭4 against Vc/Bsn 1 F4.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 139, rule 5: "As the pitch of a concerted string chord rises, adjacent seconds ... tend to become more strident ... the use of seconds should generally be limited to the lower-middle register" (Ex. 190). Ex. 191→192 revoices a chord so that its second moves to the cellos. In this score that revoicing already exists in Hn 2 over Vc and Bsn 1, so the upper copy only adds stridency. Earlier history: c086 left this as a "minor exception" to Adler's spacing rule for dissimilar woodwinds. c084 chose Cl 1 F5–G♭5–A♭5 for its clarion register and to double horn 2 two octaves higher. The fix keeps the clarion register and leaves horn 2's line intact. Only the octave doubling of one half-bar is lost.

**Fix (applied, `bSecClOne`, 61–62).**
```lilypond
\partCombineAutomatic f''1--\ff~ | f''2 aes''2-- | R1*2 |
```
Cl 1 holds F5 into 62 and moves to A♭5 at b3. Pitch classes and harmony are unchanged. The per-player scan loses two Cl 1/Hn 2 octave doublings and gains one Cl 1/Vln I octave doubling (F→A♭ at 62 b3). The new one reinforces the lead an octave lower, which is Sebesky's rule 6. There are no new fifths. The new allpairs pair, Cl 2/Vla at 62 b1, is a relabelling: those notes are unchanged, and my per-player scan lists the pair in v167 too.

### 3. Coda 83–92: the cellos double the basses for ten bars (not applied; record)

**Problem.** In the 56 bars where both instruments play, the cellos and basses share the bass in 20. Ten of those 20 are the coda: an A♭2/A♭1 pedal at 83–86, then F–D♭–A♭ in octaves at 87–92. Without the coda the share is 10 of 46 (22%), the Dvořák 9/ii figure c067 measured. Dvořák keeps the two apart in his own close (corpus bars 120–127): the bass line goes to bassoon 2, the cellos end on D♭5, and the last two bars are divided basses alone.

**Evidence.** Two passages in the reading conflict here. Rule 7 (PDF 140) and PDF 145–146 say that a cello on the root "grounds" the voicing, "making it obvious and a bit coarse instead of soft and floating". Against that, footnote 2 to rule 3 (PDF 139) says the bass cannot sustain a line alone "in a live performance", and Ex. 204 (PDF 157) has the bass with the bottom cello in octaves for an organ-like close. The plagal close 87–92 is that kind of hymn ending. In 83–86 the cellos' A♭2 also supports the timpani's E♭–C–A♭ landing. Each other cello pitch I tried would sound one of the timpani's three notes (E♭3, C3, A♭2) and cover it.

**Option, for the record only.** At 87–88 the cellos could keep A♭2 (tied from 86, re-struck pp at 87) while the basses take F–D♭ alone. The cellos would then hold a tonic pedal under IV6–iv. This leaves the root of iv at 88 only in the basses and Cl 1 at pp, which footnote 2 warns against. I recommend no change. Record the coda doubling as a reasoned exception to rule 7 (rule 3 footnote, Ex. 204) so that it is not raised again.
