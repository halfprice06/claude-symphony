## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–56: the basses double the cellos under the first string statement of theme B (applied)
**Problem.** At 53 the violins take theme B in unison, p, sul A, over harp triplets, a held bassoon line and the cellos' half-note bass (D♭3–C3 | B♭2–G♭2 | E♭3–C3 | A2–B♭2). The basses double every cello note an octave lower. At 57 the cellos leave the bass for their counter-line, the basses carry the bass alone, and winds and octaves are added. So the strings' 16-foot bass does not change at 57: it has been there since 45. The bottom of the texture is as heavy in the light statement as in the build.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 130–131, Ex. V-6: the bass doubles the cello in tutti, is dropped "when a lighter string texture is desired", and is brought back for the dominant and the cadence. PDF 139, Ex. V-16 (Haydn 103): "the simple addition of the cellos and double basses … sets off the first and the second statement of the theme and prepares so well for the tutti version that is to follow." PDF 137, Ex. V-14: crescendo by adding octaves. Dvořák 9/ii (`bassmL`) rests the basses at the start of new tunes and brings them in later.
**Fix (applied).** In bSec.ily, `bSecCb`, bars 52–57:
```
... | aes,,1 | aes,,1 |
R1*4 |
f,2\mf\< ges,2 | ...
```
This replaces `aes,,1\< | \once \stemUp des,2\p\< c,2 | bes,,2 ges,,2\mp | es,2\< c,2 | a,,2\mf bes,,2 | f,2\< ges,2`. The basses hold the A♭ pedal pp at 52 and drop out. The cellos keep the swell into 53. The basses come back mf at 57, at the cellos' level, on the beat where the cellos move to their counter-line. In score.ly the cello staff gets `\with \bnBelow 53 56`, because bar numbers are engraved on the bass staff, which is now hidden in that system (the c045 method).

Effect on protections: no pitch, rhythm or colour changes in any other part. The build order 53–61 is kept and gains one step at 57. Neither the "violins alone 53–56" theme scoring (c134) nor the harp figure is touched. This removes a doubling in the background and adds no figure, so it keeps the c124 rule. The diff of allpairs_par.py output shows only the removed Vc/Cb octave pairs at 53–56. The bass of 53–56 is the cellos' line, so the harmony is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 45–46: Vln II's F4 sits between the octaves of theme B's minor head (no change)
**Problem checked.** Oboe 1 (D♭5…) and the cellos with bassoon 1 (D♭4…) play the head an octave apart. Vln II's held F4 (then G♭4) lies between them, a third above the cellos. The cellos touch E♭4 under it on 45 b2.5.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135 says to keep the background out of the melody's register "in some instances, especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color", and lists the conditions that make it acceptable: the background is in a pale register and moves in a different rhythm. Here Vln II is pp, sustained and never in unison with the line. Placing a held inner voice between octave doublings is the Tchaikovsky practice on PDF 154 (Ex. V-29). The c124 dynamics (cellos pp–mp) already put the oboe in front.
**Recommendation.** Keep it.

### 3. Bar 64 b3–4: cellos, basses and bass trombone in unison on D2–E2 (no change; optional thinning)
**Problem checked.** After the A2 tremolo, the cellos fall to D2–E2 in unison with the basses and the bass trombone, at p, as the tutti releases. Adler PDF 151, item 5: "all cellos with the double basses doubling the bass at pitch would … be very intense", which suits a release less well than a lighter bass.
**Why no change.** c097 put the cellos there because a cello E3 on 64 b4 sounds trombone 1's resolution note a semitone under its held F3, inside the quotation's register (Adler PDF 144, point 3). The octave below is out of the basses' range. That reason holds.
**Optional fix** (thinning, Adler PDF 154): cellos `a,2:32\p r2` at 64 and re-enter on B♭2 at 65 as written. I did not apply it because 61–65 is protected and the gain is small.

### 4. Bars 89–92: string spacing of the last chord (no change)
The strings alone are A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–A♭4–C5, with an eleventh between violas and Vln II and a third at the top. This is close to Adler's distribution (PDF 151: larger spaces below, smaller above) only because clarinet 2's E♭4 fills the gap to the fermata. The clarinet's morendo to ppp at 92 is therefore part of the voicing as well as the colour. Do not cut it.

### 5. Bars 61–62: cellos above the violas (no change)
At 61 b3 to 62 the cellos (D♭4, F4, E4) rise above the violas' tremolo double stops and meet them on E4. This is Haydn's crossing for intensity (PDF 127–128, Ex. V-3, V-4), and it puts the theme's augmented head on the cellos' A string at ff. It follows the reading.
