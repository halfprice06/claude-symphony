## What else I studied

I studied the Dvořák 9/ii opening in the local corpus (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesL.ily and woods.notesL.ily, bars 1-4). There, horns, three trombones and tuba play ppp in close position, and the bassoons double Tbn 2 and the bass trombone/tuba in unison. This is Rimsky's PDF 106 juxtaposition of wind and brass. This movement's soft wind-and-horn chords at 3-6 use the other method on the same page, overlaying with clarinets below the horns, and they keep trombones out until 60. That fits the cyclic rule against a trombone chorale. I also re-read the HISTORY entries that used this reading before (c014, c104, c194, c227, c264, c287, c367) so that I would not repeat a point already settled.

## Findings

All eight findings are recordings with no change. I rank them by how much of the movement's orchestral quality depends on the passage.

**1. Climax chord, 61-62: follows PDF 100-103 and 108-112. No change.**
The brass is in three layers. B.tbn and tuba hold the bass in octaves (PDF 101). Tbn 2 is the low choir's top voice. The horns are in close order above it: D♭4, F4 and A♭4 at 61; D♭4, G♭4 and B♭4, then F♭4, A♭4 and C♭5 at 62. The single horns 1 and 2 are ff against f elsewhere in the brass (PDF 103 diagram). The woodwinds are doubled above the horns and marked one degree louder (PDF 108-109, 110-111, 112), and the bassoons and bass clarinet double the bass below. This is the arrangement in Rimsky's diagram on PDF 108 for a forte horn chord. The minor-sixth gap between Tbn 2 and Hn 2 lies between two choirs, which PDF 103 allows for a widely spread chord. Closing it would mean moving Tbn 2 back to C♭4 at 62 b3, which undoes c224, or putting Hn 4 on A♭3, which undoes c086. Neither is worth it.

**2. Brass entry, 60: follows PDF 103 and 136. No change.**
Hn 4 enters alone on C4 marked p<, against Tbn 2, B.tbn and tuba at pp<. That is PDF 103's rule for a single horn among trombones, one degree louder. The E♭3 to C4 sixth on b1-2 closes when Tbn 2 takes G♭3 on b3. Hn 4's C4 is protected (c364).

**3. Trombone quotation over strings, 63-64: follows PDF 113. No change.**
Rimsky's only allowed juxtapositions of strings and brass are brass sustaining over string tremolo and brass with short sforzando string chords. Bar 63 has both: the strings play sf> tremolo, the basses a pizz. sf, and B.tbn holds its sf>, with Tbn 1 alone on the quotation. The passage is protected.

**4. Muted horns with the cor anglais and divided violas, 87-90: follows PDF 107, 110 and 113. No change.**
The horns are muted, the cor anglais doubles Hn 4's F-F♭-E♭ an octave higher, and the violas are divided on both horns. Together this joins three of the reading's combinations: muted horn with cor anglais (PDF 107, 110), the dark mixed colour of muted horn and clarinet (PDF 107), and horns doubled by divided violas (PDF 113). It is the best orchestration in the movement for this reading.

**5. Horn and bassoon crossing, 67-70: follows PDF 108. No change.**
At 69-70 the concords go to the horn (B♭3) and the discord goes to bassoon 2 (the seventh D♭3), which is Rimsky's "concords to the horns, discords to the bassoons". At 68, the horn's D♭4 is the motto tail itself and cannot move. The bassoons stay under the horn. Protected since c014.

**6. Second peak, 79-81: follows PDF 100-101, 108-109 and 112. Known exception to PDF 108 on moving parts. No change.**
The four horns play three parts, with the bass in the woodwind and strings. The woodwinds are ff and doubled above horns at f. Hn 3's F♭4, the minor ninth, is doubled by Ob 2 and Fl 2, so the discord also sounds in another colour (PDF 102). The horns 1·2 a2 counter-melody moves while the woodwind holds, which reverses PDF 108's advice on chains of chords. The moving line is a melody doubled by Bsn 1 and the cellos, and c129 records this as a protected exception.

**7. Horns 3 and 4, 29 b3-30: minor. No change recommended.**
The seventh of V7 lies between two horns: Hn 4 holds E♭3 and Hn 3 enters on D♭4 and resolves to C4. PDF 102 prefers sevenths "entrusted to instruments of different tone colour". The cellos sound D♭4 on the same beat (c097), so the seventh is already heard in two colours. c094 chose D♭4 on PDF 97's advice to put sevenths and sixths, not open fifths, in middle wind parts. The only change that would satisfy PDF 102 more strictly is to give Hn 4's E♭3 to a bassoon (aOneBsnTwo `R2.*22 | es2.\pp~ | es2. |` with aOneHnFour resting). That would take the horn pair's soft colour off the cadence that closes A1 and add a bassoon entry for two bars. I do not recommend it.

**8. Opening and horn solo, 3-14: follows PDF 107-108. No change.**
At 3-6 the low clarinet (Cl 2 E♭3-F♭3) and the bass clarinet (A♭2) lie under horns 3 and 4. The clarinets are below the horns, not inside them, as PDF 108 asks, and the low-clarinet-and-horn colour is the one PDF 107 praises. At 7-14 the horn solo lies over two bassoons and the bass clarinet in close harmony, the soft bassoon-and-horn blend of PDF 108. Both passages are protected (link 1-6, horn solo 7-14).
