## Findings

This is the fourth series' pass on this chapter and the fifth with this lens. Every principle in the range that applies to this movement is followed or is an exception already recorded with a reason. I tested nothing that changes the score, because I found no violation that answers an earlier stated reason. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-413/work is v413 unchanged. Its rebuild is clean: build-warnings.txt is empty, 0 outer-voice parallels, 8 pages. I did not regenerate the parts book, because nothing changed. The findings below are checks with the reading, ranked by how much they bear on the form.

### 1. The two builds add groups in the reading's order, and the peak release removes them in reverse (53-61, 75-83; no change)

PDF 130: a long crescendo is made by adding strings, then woodwind, then brass, and a diminuendo by removing them in reverse. PDF 127 (image): the second group enters p or pp and makes its crescendo faster.
- 53-61: violins at 52 b3-53 (p), clarinet 1, bassoon 1 and horn 1 pp at 53-54, flutes and oboes p at 57, brass and timpani pp< at 60, full tutti ff at 61. This is the book's order and its "amplification" at 60.
- 75-79: strings mp at 75, woodwind at 77, horns 1·2 and the timpani pp< at 78 b3, ff at 79. Same order. At 75 horn 2 enters p< on the cello line. That is "p or pianissimo" under PDF 127, and c217 keeps it one level under the cellos.
- 81-83: horns 3·4, flute 2, oboe 2 and the timpani leave in 81. Horns 1·2 leave at 82 b3, the other woodwind at the end of 82, and the violas, cellos and basses carry the A♭ into the coda. That is brass, then woodwind, then strings.

Both builds take about 14-30 s, which is long enough to need the added groups and not only the players' own crescendo (PDF 130, first sentence). Fix: none.

### 2. Each return is rescored, and each scoring fits its place in the form (theme A 7/23/71-75, theme B 37/52/57/61; no change)

PDF 116 asks whether a passage complements or contrasts with its neighbours and whether it is a climax or "merely a step". PDF 117-118 says a recurring idea is scored differently each time, by octave transfer, a new key, added octaves, changed details or a new dynamic plan.
- Theme A is a horn solo, string-free, at 7 (contrast with the string link 1-6). At 23 it is in unison violins with the cello counter-melody (a step: f only at 28). At 71 it moves to the violins in two parts with a busier viola figure (operation d). At 75 it is in octaves with flute, oboe and clarinet added (operations a and c), and it rises to the second peak (operation e).
- Theme B is a flute solo over pizzicato and harp at 37, a flute-cor anglais pair at 41, violins sul A at 52, octaves with flutes and oboes at 57, and the full-tutti head at 61.

No two statements share a scoring, and only the climax statements are full. This is what the reading asks for. Fix: none.

### 3. The peaks are tutti of different kinds, and the brass are held back (61-64, 79-81; no change)

PDF 119-121 separates full tutti (all three groups) from partial tutti (part of the brass), and PDF 121 says woodwind with horns has little force in forte while brass alone is very loud. PDF 136 says a group silent for some time gains interest when it returns, and trombones, trumpets and tuba may rest for long periods.

61-62 is the only full tutti, with trombones and tuba, and it is the main climax. 79-81 is a partial tutti with horns only and gets its force from the strings and the register (B♭6). Trumpets are tacet throughout. Trombones and tuba play only at 60-64, entering pp< at 60 and with Tbn 1 ff at 63. These are the two re-entry levels PDF 136 allows. In seconds, the ff tutti last about 9 s (61-62) and 12 s (79-81) in a 345 s movement, which meets PDF 136's "cannot stand, for long". The second peak at 82 percent follows the first at 65 percent with a quiet trough between: horn solo at 67-70, analysis.txt 3.8 voices at 65-68. Fix: none.

### 4. The joins pass phrases on common notes (22-23, 36-37, 70-71, 82-83, 88-89; no change)

PDF 125-126: in a transferred phrase the last note of one part coincides with the first of the next. At 70 horn 1's B♭3 is held through b3 under the violins' pickup (c343). At 88-89 clarinet 1 holds C5 while Vln I takes it. At 82-83 the harp's plucked pickup leads into its own harmonics over the basses' tied A♭. At 36 b3 flute 1 and the cor anglais take the pickup together and carry it into 37. The cor anglais' quotation ends at 51 b2 and the violins enter at 52 b4. That is a phrase break between two ideas, not a transferred phrase, and the held strings bridge it. None of the transfers has a gap or a stop, so the sections follow one another without a break, as a slow movement in one tempo family needs. Fix: none.

### 5. The coda's first bar has empty middle octaves, which PDF 125 allows as a deliberate effect (83; recorded only)

PDF 125: upper and lower parts are seldom widely separated without the middle octaves filled, except for "strange" or "fanciful" effects. At 83 the harp harmonics (sounding A♭4-C5-E♭5) sit over cellos A♭2 and basses A♭1, ppp. There is nothing between A♭2 and A♭4 until the violas enter at 84 b3. This is the only such gap in the movement. It lasts about 3.5 s, and it is deliberate: the harmonics are heard alone over the pedal (c063, c124, citing Adler part 2 PDF 156, 267, 269). PDF 125 allows the effect, and the next bar fills the space, so it reads as the coda's one moment of distance. A possible fix, which I do not recommend because it would undo c063/c124: `codaVla = { R2. | r2 <c' es'>4~\ppp ...` becomes `<c' es'>2.~\ppp | <c' es'>2.~ | ...`, with the violas entering at 83 b1.

### 6. The proportions after the climax (61-92) were checked in seconds as well as bars (no change)

Earlier form critics compared bar counts with Dvořák 9/ii (c253; c373). In seconds, from the `\mt` map, everything after the main climax takes 122 s (35 percent): climax and quotation 19 s, retransition 21 s, A′ 43 s, coda 40 s. The coda is almost as long as the A′ it closes. In bars it is not (10 against 12), but the coda's rit. (89-92 at crotchet 48-20) stretches it. The reading does not limit coda length. Its relevant rule is the diminuendo by elimination (PDF 130), and the coda follows it: harp, then timpani, then clarinet 1, then the muted horns and cor anglais leave, so the strings are left alone at the end. The coda is also bound by the cyclic rules (harp and timpani moments, plagal D♭-C close) and is protected ("proportions; plagal coda", c253). I record the 40 s figure for the next form critic and do not ask for a change.
