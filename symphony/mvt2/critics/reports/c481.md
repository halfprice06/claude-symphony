# Critic 481: v481, lens 1 (melody and memorability)

Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. V, "Combination of the human voice with orchestra. The stage band" (rimsky-principles PDF 137–149, printed pp. 119–131). This is the fourth pass on this chapter. c221 and c361 used it for lens 1, and c289 used its examples. I read every text page in full. I also rendered all thirteen pages as images, because the OCR leaves out part of PDF 143 (the "Recitative and declamation" section) and part of PDF 146 ("Solo voice with chorus"). None of the thirteen pages contains music. The chapter's examples are in the second volume, so I viewed three of them as images: Ex. 277 (*Snegourotchka*, Adagio: soprano over muted strings, with cor anglais holding notes, PDF 444), Ex. 282 (*The Tsar's Bride*: a soft climax carried by woodwind, harp and pizzicato, PDF 450) and Ex. 284 (*The Tsar's Bride*: solo oboe doubling the soprano, a solo cello obbligato and off-beat muted strings, PDF 457).

In this report "the voice" means the instrument that carries the melody: horn 1 at 7–14, the violins at 23–30, flute 1 or the cor anglais at 37–44, and so on.

## Findings

On the lens question first. Both tunes would be remembered for the reasons the reading and the models give. Theme A is a parallel period: 7–8 and 11–12 share the rhythm `4. 8 4~ | 4 4 4`, and the rising-arpeggio head and the held 5̂ resolving by the chromatic tail make one singable hook. Its second phrase rises to the B♭ appoggiatura, which grows from mp (12) to f (28) to ff on B♭6 (79). Theme B uses one rhythmic cell in nearly every bar: an eighth-note neighbour pair inside `2 8 8 4` or `4 8 8 2`. It withholds its tonic until 61, so its last statement has a goal. Theme A states its cell less literally than Dvořák's, but Dvořák's model differs from this one (cor anglais return; c461 kept theme A's opening, which is exported). I found nothing in the melodic lines that the reading asks to change. Every finding below is ranked, but none requires a change.

### 1. Doubling plan of the three theme-A statements and two theme-B statements (no change)

**Bars:** 7–14, 23–30, 71–82; 37–44, 53–61.

**Check.** PDF 140–141 asks for doubling "limited to a few special phrases" and says incessant doubling "replaces by a mixed timbre the rare characteristic qualities" of the voice. Both themes follow that order. Each is heard first in one undoubled colour (horn 1; flute 1, then cor anglais). The next statement is in one family in unison (violins 23–30 and 53–56). Mixed octave doubling comes only at the broad phrase (theme A from 77 b1, when flute 1 and clarinet 1 join; theme B from 57). This is the order Rimsky gives. It also matches PDF 141's rule that a climax is supported in the voice's register, while the quiet statements stay single.

**Fix:** none.

### 2. Register of accompaniment attacks against every solo (no change)

**Bars:** 14, 42–43, 48–52, 52 b4, 70 b3.

**Check.** PDF 139 says harmony in the voice's own register covers it more than harmony below. My scan reports 21 attacks above the melody or within a whole tone below it, and every one is in a place already ruled on:
- 14 b2 harp A♭4–E♭5 above the horn's last A♭3 (c361 put it there, an octave above, per PDF 139's "duplicated in the higher register").
- 42 b2.67, 43 b1.67, 43 b4.67 viola pizz. a step under the cor anglais (c108, c192, c221).
- 48–52 Vln II's held line a third to a sixth above the quotation (c054, c202, c221).
- 52 b4 and 70 b3 violins' pickups over the end of a solo (hand-overs, PDF 141).

The changes since c361 (c469 cello 9–8 at 73–74, c472 Vln II 39, c478 Bsn 1 54–55, c479 Cl 2 73–74) put no attack above or next to a melody note.

**Fix:** none.

### 3. Placement of the climaxes (no change)

**Bars:** 12, 28, 61, 79.

**Check.** PDF 141 says a loud lyrical climax is supported in the voice's register, with a brass entry or "a rush of strings". PDF 142 says a soft one is better left unsupported. All four climaxes fit:
- 12 (horn mp): the harp chord sits on the tied F, and the B♭ appoggiatura sounds alone.
- 28 b2: the strings rise to f together.
- 61: trombone 2, bass trombone and tuba enter on the arrival (from 60 pp<).
- 79: the flute grace run and the violins' pickup make the "rush", horns 1·2 a2 and 3·4 enter f, and the trumpets stay tacet by design (c024, c454).

**Fix:** none.

### 4. Bars 41–44: flute 1 in tenths and sixths over the cor anglais (no change)

**Check.** PDF 137 forbids accompanying figures in the voice's rhythm, and at 42–43 the flute's rhythm is close to the cor anglais's. But PDF 137 means florid singing against a different figure. PDF 142 ("support voices ... by harmony and duplication") describes a duet in parallel intervals. The scan shows the flute is the only attack above the cor anglais there, and it is a melody partner, not harmony filler. c090, c101, c401 and c441 have ruled on it; the flute stays one level under (p< against mp).

**Fix:** none.

### 5. Held double bass and timpani rolls under solos (no change)

**Bars:** 65–70, 83–92.

**Check.** PDF 139 warns against long held double-bass notes and kettledrum tremolo under a voice. At 65–70 the bass E♭ and the timpani roll are ppp/pp, four octaves under horn 1's tail. At 83–92 the A♭ pedal is ppp under the clarinet's tail and Vln I's C5, which lie three octaves higher. Both passages are protected: the retransition, and the coda as c194, c199 and c223 designed it. c221 and c289 have already recorded them. I found no reason to reopen them.

**Fix:** none.

### 6. Paired instruments in unison on one part (no change)

**Bars:** 61–62 (Fl a2, Hn 3·4 a2), 78 b3–81 (Hn 1·2 a2 with Bsn 1 and cellos on the counter-line).

**Check.** PDF 139 lists two like instruments in unison as a combination that covers a voice. PDF 140 also asks for "fuller orchestral tone in broad phrases and climaxes", and these are the movement's two ff peaks. In neither does the unison stand against a softer solo. Hn 3·4 a2 at 61–62 is on the standing list.

**Fix:** none.

### 7. Bar 77: viola eighths beside the melody's eighth-note descent (no change)

**Check.** PDF 137 again. The violas' figure has run in eighths since 71, lies 1½ to 2½ octaves below, and has its own interval pattern. c361 and c324 have settled this.

**Fix:** none.
