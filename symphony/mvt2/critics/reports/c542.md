### 1. Bar 68 b3 is a tonic 6/4 over the dominant pedal, not IV6 (record correction, no change)

**Bars 67-69.** Over the basses' E flat2:
- Horn 1 plays E flat4-D4-D flat4-C4-B flat3.
- Bassoon 1 plays B flat3-A flat3-G3.
- At 68 b3 the sounding notes are C4 and A flat3 over E flat: a tonic 6/4.
- At 69 it resolves 6-5 (horn C4-B flat3) and 4-3 (bassoon A flat3-G3) into V7.

**Why record it:** c402's table cited this beat under A&S's "IV6 as a passing chord within V" (PDF 200-201, Ex. 13-10). That progression is V-IV6-V6/5 over a rising bass 5̂-6̂-7̂, and the score does not use it. The bar is a neighbour 6/4 inside the dominant prolongation, which is how HISTORY's 6/4 inventory lists it ("passing 6/4 at 68").
- The voice leading is correct: both dissonances resolve down by step in their own voices.
- The motto tail reaches 3̂ (C4) over the dominant, so the 6/4 is the right harmony for it.

**Fix:** none. Later critics should cite this bar under the 6/4 rules, not Ex. 13-10.

### 2. Bar 53 b3: V6/5 under the theme's neighbour note (no change)

**Bar 53, Vln I and II, cellos, harp.** In theme B's third statement the bass moves to C3 on b3. Under the violins' G flat5-F5 neighbour this makes V6/5 of D flat, with the 7th G flat on top. At 53 b3½ the F5 stands a 4th over C, and both resolve into V6 at b4 (E flat5).

**Evidence:**
- A&S's scale-degree table allows 4̂ on top over bass 7̂ only with V6/5 (PDF 172-173). That is this case.
- The table forbids 7̂ on top over V6. That does not occur: the harp's doubled C4 was removed by c212.
- The 7th resolves down by step (G flat5-F5). The 4th then falls to E flat5, a 4-3 over the same bass.
- At 37 the same neighbour note sits over a held tonic. The changed bass at 53 is the varied repeat that A&S describe after a deceptive cadence (PDF 204, Ex. 13-14).

**Fix:** none. Recorded so that the G flat5 over C3 is not reported as an unresolved 7th.

### 3. VI after V: the 3rd is doubled at all four deceptive arrivals (no change)

**Bars 17, 21, 45, 78.** A&S say that V(7)-VI should double the 3rd of VI so that 7̂ can rise to 8̂ without 5ths or (in minor) an augmented 2nd (PDF 202-203, Ex. 13-12).

| Bar | Arrival | Doubling | Voice leading |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17 b1 | vi of A flat | A flat4, A flat5 (3rd) and F3, F4 (root); no 5th | Vln II G4-A flat4 |
| 21 b1 | VI of F minor | F4, F5 (3rd) | Vln II E4-F4; violas' 7th B flat3-A flat3; no augmented 2nd |
| 45 b1 | vi of D flat | D flat4, D flat5 (3rd) | the violas' G flat3 resolves to F3 |
| 78 b1 | flat VI (F flat major) | A flat in four octaves (3rd) | the 7th D flat4 (bassoon 1, cellos) falls to C flat |

**Top voice:** A&S say the deceptive cadence is strongest with 2̂-1̂ or 7̂-1̂ on top (PDF 203, Review 5 PDF 209). In A′ the theme's last eighth B flat5 (77 b3½) goes to A flat5 at 78. That is 2̂-1̂, so the deception is as strong as the authentic cadence it imitates at 82.

**Fix:** none.

### 4. Bar 54 b2-3: harp figuration makes momentary 5ths with the clarinet/horn line (no change, optional)

**Bar 54, harp RH and Cl 1/Hn 1.**
- Cl 1/Hn 1 move F4 (b2½) to B flat4 (b3).
- The harp RH's last triplet note of b2, B flat3, goes to E flat4 on b3.
- This gives F4/B flat3 to B flat4/E flat4: 5ths in similar motion, with the two first notes attacked a triplet-sixth apart.

It is the same class of event as the figuration octaves c402 recorded at 37-42 and 76: the inner note of an arpeggio meeting a melodic voice, at p. A&S Units 11-13 do not address it.

**Fix (smallest, not recommended, not tested):** in bSecHarpR bar 54 b2, `bes ges bes` → `ges bes ges`. This gives the same pitches and no string struck twice in a row (c406), and the harp moves G flat3-E flat4 against F4-B flat4. It changes a passage that c137, c212 and c406 have tuned for harp technique, for a gain no listener will hear. I record it only so a later scan does not report it as new.

### 5. Bars 31-37: no seventh on the A flat chord before D flat (no change)

A&S say that I-IV does not define a key the way I-V does, and that a IV-I relation is "distinctly subordinate" (PDF 196). Read that way, theme B at 37 begins on D flat heard as IV of A flat:
- No G flat sounds in 31-36.
- D flat is confirmed as a key only by the V7 at 42 b3 and the cadential 6/4 at 43-44.

c020 and c203 chose this "phrase modulation" on purpose. Nothing in Units 11-13 answers their reason (the pedal is a goal, and the woodwind choir is protected). Unit 13 supports the design: the subordinate I-IV relation keeps the middle section from sounding like a new tonic too early, and the late cadence at 59-61 confirms D flat (c533). I do not re-raise c203's rejected B flat-G flat at 36 b3.

**Fix:** none.
