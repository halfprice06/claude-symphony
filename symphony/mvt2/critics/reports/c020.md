## Findings

### 1. Bars 31–36: a six-bar bridge on V7 of D♭ where the reading and cyclic-notes ask for a phrase modulation (applied)
Problem. The A section closes with a PAC at 30. Then Cl 2 adds G♭ at 32, and 34–36 sit on A♭7 (Cl 1 G♭, Cl 2 E♭, B.cl. C; then D♭/B♭/G♭ over the A♭ pedal with a timpani crescendo). The new key is announced for five bars before it arrives. Cyclic-notes (changes 111–120) asks movement II to "start the middle section directly in D♭ with no bridge".
Evidence. A&S PDF 452: when a tonicized IV occurs in ABA forms, "it frequently—and typically—enters right at the beginning of the new section without a transition". Ex. 26-11 shows I at bar 76 and IV from 77, with no bridge. PDF 451: too much emphasis on IV makes "us hear IV as I and I as V". In v20, A♭ turns into a dominant only four bars after its first full cadence. The first D♭ bar does not need the bridge. At 37 the metre, tempo and signature change, and the B.cl. still rises A♭–B♭–C to D♭ (PDF 445: the line gravitates to the new 1̂). The violas sound A♭7 at 38 b3–4 and confirm D♭ inside the new section.
Do-not-touch answered. Cycle 3 protected "A♭→A♭7 pivot 31–36" under the form lens. The cyclic note from movement I's cycle 120 is newer and asks for the opposite, and A&S 452 supports it. Cycle 2's common-tone diminished seventh at 33 is kept note for note (B–D–F over A♭). The B.cl. solo line is unchanged.
Fix (trans.ily, applied):
```
transClOne = { R2. | c'2.\pp | c'4 b4 bes4 | c'2. | es'2. | es'2. | }
transClTwo = { R2. | aes2.\pp | aes4 f4 f4 | aes2. | c'2. | c'2. | }
transBsnOne = { R2.*4 | aes2.\pp | aes2. | }
```
Bars 31–36 are now I – I – (I, vii°7, ii4/2 over the pedal) – I – I – I, and D♭ enters at 37 by phrase modulation. Option if the composer wants one hint of D♭: put G♭ in Cl 2 on 36 beat 3 only.

### 2. No "attacca": the protected common-tone link cannot be heard (not applied: movement I file)
Problem. The movement's first sound is movement I's last chord, held. Neither v147 nor v20 prints "attacca". Without it, orchestras pause and retune between movements, so bar 1 is heard as a fresh C-major chord. The link at 1–2, the reason for the movement's opening, is then lost.
Evidence. A&S PDF 445–446, Ex. 26-4: the C chord becomes a pivot (III of A♭) only when it is heard as continuing the previous key. A pause turns it into two unrelated tonics. Cyclic-notes ("Changes since cycle 90", item 5) says the opening "follows a chord that has soft horns in it" and calls a held continuation "the closest continuation", which assumes no break.
Fix. For the coordinator, in movement I's coda.ily: after the last bar's `\fermata` in Vln I, add `_\markup \italic "attacca"` (and the same in global.ily as `\mark \markup \italic "attacca"` before `\bar "|."`). Nothing needs to change in movement II.

### 3. No passage for the woodwind choir alone (applied, same bars as finding 1)
Problem. Critic 119's note asks for a passage for woodwinds alone. In v20 every wind passage includes horns (3–6, 7–14), strings or timpani (31–36: Cb pedal, timpani 35–36).
Evidence. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 89–90 treats woodwind harmony as its own texture. Like instruments take concords, and the normal order of register is kept. In the new 31–36 the clarinets are a third apart in every chord (C4/A♭3, then E♭4/C4). The bassoons are an octave apart (A♭3/A♭2). Order from the top: Cl 1, Cl 2, B.cl. (or Cl, B.cl. on top at 32–33 as the solo), Bsn 1, Bsn 2, Cbsn.
Fix (trans.ily, applied): remove `transCb` and `transTimp`. Add
```
transBsnTwo = { aes,2.~\pp | aes,2.~ | aes,2 \breathe aes,4~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2 r4 | }
transCbsn = { aes,,2.~\pp | aes,,2.~ | aes,,2 r4 | R2.*3 | }
```
The Cb's A♭1 from 30 passes to the contrabassoon and thins out by 33. The bassoon breathes at 33 b3. The Cb now has bars 31–36 rest before its pizz. at 37.

### 4. Viola C string: only held notes (applied in the coda)
Problem. Cyclic-notes (critic 126) asks for a passage on the viola's C string. The violas' lowest notes in v20 are held F3/G♭3 at 45–46 and G3 at 65–66.
Evidence. Adler part 1, PDF 86: the C string is "the most characteristic of the four… somber, austere". The best place for it is the thread this movement carries, E/F♭→E♭ (1–2, 5, 62–65, 88–89), which also ends the plagal close.
Fix (coda.ily, applied). Violas unis. sul C an octave lower at 87–92 (F3–F♭3–E♭3). This doubles horn 4 at 87–90, then the violas continue alone. Cl 1 takes the upper octave the violas leave, and Cl 2 moves to A♭3:
```
codaVla (87-92): f2.\pp^\markup \italic "unis., sul C" | fes2. | es2.~ | es2.~ | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata |
codaClOne = { R2.*4 | f'2.\pp | fes'2. \breathe | es'2.~ | es'2.~ | es'2.~\>^\markup \italic "morendo" | es'2.\ppp\fermata | }
codaClTwo = { R2.*6 | aes2.~\pp | aes2.~ | aes2.~\> | aes2.\ppp\fermata | }
```
The final chord keeps its pitches (A♭1, A♭2, E♭3, A♭3, E♭4, A♭4, C5); only the E♭3 and E♭4 change instruments. The plagal close's bass D♭→A♭ and Vln I's D♭5–C5 are untouched, which is the reason that passage is protected. Movement III's German sixth (add F♯ to this chord) still has its E♭.
