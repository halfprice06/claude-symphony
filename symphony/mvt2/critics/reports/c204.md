## Findings

### 1. Bars 23–28: the bass sounds only in the double basses' lowest octave, on their two dullest strings (applied)

**Problem.** At 23 the cellos leave the bass for their counter-melody (protected), and the double basses carry the bass alone for six bars: A♭1, F2–B♭1–E♭2, A♭1–D♭2, E♭2, A♭1–C2–D♭2, D♭2–B♭1. No cello, bassoon or harp plays the bass line in its normal octave. The next part above is the cello line, a twelfth to two octaves higher (A♭1 against E♭3 at 23, A♭1 against A♭3 at 25, D♭2 against A♭3 at 28). Every other non-pedal passage has the bass in octaves: harp LH with the pizzicato basses at 37–44, Bsn 2 at 45–47, cellos at 48–52, and Bsn 2 again at 71–78, where the same theme returns with the same cello counter-melody. So the first string statement of theme A, which is the movement's main tune, has the thinnest bass of the piece.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27 note: except on pedal notes the double bass "rarely plays an independent part, usually moving in octaves or in unison with the 'cellos, or else doubling the bassoons. The quality of the double bass tone is therefore seldom heard by itself". PDF 27: the bass is "slightly duller on the two lower strings (E and A)", and every note here lies on those two strings. PDF 30: the cellos and basses "in the majority of cases form the bass in octaves". Ch. III PDF 82: added parts are upper duplications, "the bass being doubled in the lower octave only", which means that the 16-foot line is a duplication of a bass that should also sound in its own octave. Here the duplication has no original. PDF 85: the bass should rarely lie more than an octave below the next part. c184 recorded the gap at 28 and rejected doubling the bass upward or adding a wind to the plain string statement. This fix does neither. It keeps the passage strings only, and the upper half is the bass in its normal octave, with the lower half as the lower-octave duplication PDF 82 allows. PDF 25 lists "D. basses div." among the standard divisions.

**Fix (applied, sections/aOne.ily, aOneCb):**
```
<aes,, aes,>2.\p^\markup \italic "div." | <f, f>4 <bes,, bes,>4 <es, es>4 | <aes,, aes,>2 <des, des>4 | <es, es>2. |
<aes,, aes,>4\< <c, c>4 <des, des>4 | \after 4 \f \after 4 \> <des, des>2 <bes,, bes,>4 | es,2.^\markup \italic "unis." | \after 2 \pp aes,,2.\p\> |
```
The pitches, rhythm and dynamics of the bass are unchanged. The basses join again at 29, where horn 4's E♭3 fills the tenor (c094) and the upper half would only double it. The upper half touches the cellos' F3 on 24 b1 for one beat. This is an octave doubling of the existing bass, so it adds no new voice-leading. allpairs_par.py reports the same pairs as v204. The fix does not touch the protected parts of 23–30: the violin unison, the viola divisi, the cello counter-melody, and the absence of winds before 29. Build: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. The asymmetry with 71–78 (Bsn 2 on the bass there) is deliberate in colour, but under this fix it stays a string colour. Only the octave layout of the bass changes.

### 2. Bar 40: double-bass pizzicato A♮1 on the open string, held a half note (recommendation, not applied)

**Problem.** In the pizzicato bass at 37–44 every note is stopped except the chromatic passing A♮1 at 40 b1–2. This is the open A string, plucked and left to ring for a half note, and the harp doubles it in octaves. It is the one passing note in the line, and it will sound louder and longer than the stopped D♭2, B♭1 and E♭2 around it.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 45: pizzicato "on open strings it is resonant and heavy, on stopped strings shorter and duller". PDF 27: open strings are "clearer and more powerful but less expressive than stopped strings".

**Fix.** Either stop it on the E string (`a,,2-\tag #'score ^\markup \small "IV"` in bSecCb, or the text "sul E"), or shorten it to a quarter with a rest, as in the other bars (`a,,4 r4 bes,,2`). The second is simpler for the players. It is low priority. The harp's A♮ pedal change at 39 b4 is not affected.
