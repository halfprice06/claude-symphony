## What else I studied

## Findings (ranked)

### F1. Theme B's second half copies theme A's peak gesture (bars 42, 58). Applied.
**Problem.** Theme A, bars 7–8, rises by arpeggio to a held E♭ and then falls chromatically 5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂. Theme B, bars 41–42, does the same thing in D♭: A♭–D♭–F to a held A♭, then A♭–G–G♭–F. The listener comes away with one shape for two themes. The chromatic tail also sounds at 8, 24, 33, 42, 58, 67–68, 72 and 89–92, so eight times in 96 bars. Rimsky's first rule (PDF 54) is that a melody must stand out in relief, and a second theme cannot do that when its peak bar is the first theme transposed. Dvořák's middle tune (corpus, bar 46) shares no gesture with his Largo theme. Cycle 1 raised this point and it remains open. The reason given for the tail is cyclic, but the tail keeps that role at 8, 24, 72 and 89–92 without bars 42 and 58.
**Fix (applied).** Bar 42 (Fl 1, C.a.) and bar 58 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II): replace `aes4 g4 ges4 f4` with an upper-neighbour turn at the same octave, e.g. Vln I 58: `aes'''4 bes'''8 aes'''8) ges'''4( f'''4)`. The G♮ of V7/V was only in the melody, so beat 2 becomes ii7 (E♭m7) in root position over the same E♭ bass. Violas, beat 2 of 42 and 58: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges') }`. Cl 2 at 58 changes `aes4 bes4` to `aes4 ges4`, which removes the octaves A♭→B♭ with the melody that the all-pairs check found. This changes cycle 2's "V7/V under every motto tail" at 42 and 58 only, because the tail is no longer there.

### F2. The cellos never sing a theme, and violins with cellos in octaves are never used (bars 45–47). Applied.
**Problem.** Rimsky calls passionate cantabile on the A string the cello's characteristic use (PDF 56; Ex. 7, Pan Voyevoda [134]: "a broad melody dolce ed espressivo"). For "very expressive passages" he recommends violins and cellos in octaves with the cellos on the A or D string, a sound "more resonant" than Vla+Vc (PDF 60–61; Ex. 29 and 30, viewed at PDF 206–207). In this score the cellos only play bass or the counter-melody on the C and G strings (E♭3–D♭4, 23–30, 71–82). The B2 entry at 45–46, theme B's head in Vln I alone at p, is the natural place for this sound. It also gives B2 a colour of its own, between B1 (Fl/C.a.) and B3 (violins).
**Fix (applied).** Vc 45–48: `\clef tenor des'4.(\p^"arco" es'8 des'4 c'4 | bes4 aes8 bes8 des'2) | cis'1\> | \clef bass a,1\pp |`. These notes lie on the D and A strings, and the cellos now cross above the violas' F3, which is Rimsky's "crossing" method (PDF 54). Rimsky's bass rule (PDF 56) means the basses should not hold the bass alone, so Bsn 2 takes the cellos' old line: `bes,1\pp | bes,2 bes,2 | gis,1\> | R1\! |`. The oboe quotation (47–50) is unchanged.

### F3. Theme B shares theme A's rhythm (bars 37/39/41/43 against 7/9/11). Applied in part.
**Problem.** Every odd bar of both themes uses the same `4. 8` cell, so B has no rhythmic figure of its own to remember. Dvořák's B tune is identified by its triplet-plus-tie figure (corpus bar 46). Rimsky's cited melodies are identified by a figure as much as by their line: Ex. 9 is a "melodic phrase with embellishments" and Ex. 23 a "melody with reiterated notes" (PDF 56, 59).
**Fix (applied).** Bar 39 (Fl 1, C.a.) and 55 (Vln I, Vln II): `ges''4( \tuplet 3/2 4 { f''8 ges'' f'' } es''4 des''4`. This is a triplet turn that takes up the violas' triplet accompaniment. Bars 37, 41 and 43 keep `4. 8`, so the head is still recognisable at 45 and 53.

### F4. The A′ counter-melody is in the middle register at ff (bars 78–82). Applied.
**Problem.** At 79–81 the counter-melody D♭4–C4–B♭3 is in Vc and Hn 1·2 inside a full tutti: Cl 1 D♭5, Vla div. trem. F4/A♭4, Hn 3·4, harp. Rimsky (PDF 54) says a middle-register line needs doubling to stand out, and his Antar [63] doubles the cello melody with bassoons (PDF 56). Bsn 1 is free in A′.
**Fix (applied).** New `aTwoBsnOne = { R2.*7 | r2 c'4( | des'4\ff c'4 bes4 | aes4 bes4 c'4) \breathe | bes2(\> des'4 | c'2.)\p | }`. Its dynamics match Bsn 2 on the shared staff. The C→B♭ octave between the counter-melody and Cl 1 / Hn 3·4 at 80–81 was already there. The bassoon only joins that doubling.

### F5. "sul D" for the first violin statement of theme A (bars 22–26). Applied.
**Problem.** This was left open in cycle 9. Rimsky's Ex. 12 (PDF 57; image at PDF 186) is the closest case in the reading: a Vn I+II unison cantabile, p, in the same register, marked "sul D", then on the A. Bars 23–26 stay between E♭4 and E♭5, so the whole phrase fits on the D string. The cycle-9 protection covers the unison and the cello counter-melody, and this marking changes neither.
**Fix (applied).** Vln I 22: `es'8(\mp^\markup \italic "espr., sul D" f'8`. Vln II 22: `es'8(\mp^\markup \italic "sul D" f'8`. Both at 27: `aes'4.\<^\markup \italic "ord." c''8)`. At 27–28 the line climbs to F5 and B♭5 and needs the A string.
