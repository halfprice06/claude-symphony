## Findings

### 1. Bar 55: ii7 moves to vi6 and its 7th is never resolved (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 55 b1–2 is ii7 in D♭ (E♭ bass, G♭–B♭–D♭ above; bassoon 1 holds the 7th D♭3 from bar 54). At b3 the bass falls to D♭ and the harp plays B♭–D♭–F, so the harmony is vi6. Two things go wrong:
- **Syntax.** II→VI is the weak backward progression A&S warn against: intermediate harmonies should move by descending 5ths or 3rds, and "II–VI" and "IV–VI" are listed as weak (PDF 172).
- **Voice leading.** Bassoon 1 leaps from the 7th D♭3 up to F3, so the 7th never resolves. A&S: the 7th of II7 "resolves down by step", and it does not enter or leave by leap except in free textures (PDF 177; Points 1–2, PDF 186).

Here the 7th is a held bassoon note in a sustained chorale, which is not a free texture.

**Comparison with bar 39.** Bar 39 (the same theme, a fourth lower in context) was fixed by c102: ii7 → viiø7/vi over C → vii°7/vi over A → vi over B♭. Bar 55 still has the old harmony.

**Fix (applied).** Bar 55 now does what 39 does.
- Bass E♭→C at b3:
  - Vc `es2\< c2`
  - Cb `es,2\< c,2`
- Harp RH b3–4 now spells the viiø7 (B♭–E♭–G♭): `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 es' ges' es' bes es' }`
- Bassoon 1 resolves the 7th D♭3→C3 in the same voice. It then takes c042's G♭3–F3 neighbour at 56 unchanged: `R1 | des1~(\pp | des2 \once \stemDown c2 | ges2 f2) |`
  - The `\once \stemDown` is for layout only. With the stem up, the slur clears it and the 53–56 system grows enough to push the score to 9 pages.

The bass now reads D♭–C–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A–B♭. In the melody, E♭5 at 55 b3 becomes a chord tone and D♭5 a passing tone, as at 39.

**Protected counter-line.** The Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line (53–60, c107/c109) is protected, and c102 left 55 alone because its F4 falls on b3. The stated reason is answered by the smallest change:
- The counter-line keeps every note except one. Its G♭4 is held as a whole note instead of G♭–F:
  - Cl 1 `ges'1\p`
  - Hn 1 `ges'1\mp`
- The dynamics are unchanged.
- F4 cannot sound against C–E♭–G♭–B♭: it would be a 9th over the bass and a minor 2nd against the harp's G♭4.

This is the change c102 itself proposed. The line's shape, register and scoring are untouched, and the line still goes G♭–C–D♭ into 56.

### 2. Bar 43 b1–2: plain ii6 where A&S call for II6/5 into the cadential 6/4 (applied)

**Problem.** At 43 b1–2, before the cadential 6/4 on b3, the strings and harp give ii6 (G♭ bass, B♭, E♭). The violas double the cor anglais' E♭4 in unison.

A&S say three things about this spot:
- II6/5 is the richer form of the pre-dominant (PDF 177–178).
- The normal approach to the cadential 6/4 keeps the 7th of II in the same voice as the 4th of the 6/4 (Ex. 12-8, PDF 182–183; Points 6, PDF 186).
- The 7th is best prepared as a common tone (PDF 177).

The viola has D♭4 at 42 b4 already, so the preparation is there and unused.

**Fix (applied).** Viola, 43 b1–2: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes des' bes ges bes }` (was `es'`).
- The chord becomes II6/5: G♭–B♭–D♭, with E♭ in the cor anglais.
- The viola D♭4 is prepared at 42 b4. It is held as the 4th of the cadential 6/4 at 43 b3 (the viola already has D♭4 there) and resolves to C4 at 43 b4.

This is the A&S model note for note. It also removes the viola/cor anglais unison on E♭4 that c057 objected to.

### 3. Bars 41 b3 and 57 b3: IV→vi7 (not applied)

IV moving to vi7 is a rising third, which A&S call weak (PDF 172). I am not changing it, for two reasons:
- **Bar 41.** It is protected (Fl/CA 41–44). A D♭ bass at b3 would also make outer fifths with flute 1 (A♭5/D♭ → F5/B♭).
- **Bar 57.** The protected counter-line has G♭4 there.

The progression is mild, the vi7 goes on correctly to ii7–V7–I, and the risk of a fix is higher than the gain.

### 4. Bar 25 b3: the 7th of IV7 enters by leap (no change)

The cello counter-melody (protected) leaps to C4, the 7th of IV7. The 7th is still prepared in register by violin C5, and it resolves down to B♭3 over V. A&S allow this in free textures (PDF 186). No change.

### 5. Bar 80: doubled 7th, Hn 4 in octaves with the melody (no change)

Hn 4 moves A♭3→G3 in octaves with the melody's A♭–G. This doubles the resolving 7th, which A&S avoid in part-writing (PDF 177). It was raised at c022 with this same reading and kept as an ff orchestral doubling inside the protected V♭9 (79–82). I am not raising it again.
