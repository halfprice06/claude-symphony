## Findings

### 1. Bar 49: the Neapolitan under the quotation is a model of A&S 28 §2-4 (no change)
**Evidence.** At 49 b3 the chord is G/B: B1 (Cb), B2 (Vc), B3 (Vla, off-beat), G5 (Vln II), D5 (C.a.). The bass is tripled (Points 4, PDF 508). ♭2̂ is the top voice and falls a diminished third to E♯5 on V7 (PDF 483-484, Ex. 28-2a). The cellos hold B2 as V's seventh and resolve it to A2 at 50, exactly the use of the bass doubling shown in Ex. 28-2b. V7 has no fifth, as the book says it will be incomplete. G♮ and G♯ are in different voices (Vln II, Vla), the cross relation the book allows (PDF 484). It is approached from VI6 by 5-6 over F♯ (PDF 485-486). **Fix.** None.

### 2. Bar 21: the French sixth is approached and resolved by the book (no change)
**Evidence.** IV7 on b1 (D♭–F–A♭ with Vla C4) passes through an E♭ 6/4 to F♭–A♭–B♭–D on b3: F♭2 Cb, F♭3/A♭3 Vc div., B♭3 Vla and Vln II, D5 Vln I solo. It follows the deceptive move C7 → D♭ at 20-21, which A&S PDF 513 names as a common place for augmented sixths. D♮ is spelled as a rising chromatic tone and resolves to E♭5 (PDF 497). F♭ falls to E♭, A♭ to G (Vc upper voice), and B♭ is held as the common 2̂ (PDF 507). The bass E♭–F♭–E♭ makes the sixth an upper neighbour of V (PDF 513, §12). The half cadence it reaches is a phase end before the reprise at 23 (PDF 503). **Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 61-65: the return from the D♭ section follows Ex. 28-14 and Ex. 29-28 together (no change; protected)
**Evidence.** D♭ is IV of A♭, so the B section is a long tonicized subdominant. The way back is IV (61) – G♭ – F♭ (♭VI, 62 b3) acting as dominant of A minor = B𝄫 (♭II, 63-64) as in Verdi's Kyrie (PDF 489-490). The ♭VI root F♭ then carries the augmented sixth at 64 b4 into V7 at 65 (PDF 512, §11; PDF 516-517, Ex. 29-28: tonicized IV through an augmented sixth to the home dominant). The tonicized ♭II takes its own leading tone G♯ (PDF 493, §12). This is protected (c312, c350, c372). I record a second book reason to keep it. **Fix.** None.

### 4. Bar 64 b4 → 65: the sixth contracts to the seventh in the top string voice (recorded, no change)
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 515 (§15) allows an augmented sixth to go straight to V7, "especially if the 7th is in an inner voice". At 65 the seventh D♭4 (Vln II, from D4) is the highest string note of a pp chord, not an inner voice. That is the less usual case. It is the *Einsamkeit* model (PDF 525-526), where Schubert also lets the chord's upper note fall a semitone, and the passage is protected (c211, c233, c350). The semitone descent in all four strings (E–E♭, B/B♭–B♭, G♯–G, D–D♭) is the movement's E/F♭→E♭ thread. **Fix.** None. I record it so a later critic does not raise it as a fault.

### 5. Bar 78: no augmented sixth after the ♭VI deceptive cadence (c232 confirmed)
**Evidence.** PDF 513 says augmented sixths often follow deceptive cadences to ♭VI. c232 answered this: the E♭ at 78 b3 is a passing bass under a 6/3 (E♭–G–C) on the way to the pre-dominant at 79-80, and a sixth would make it sound like V two bars early (PDF 503: the sixth's resolution is heard as a dominant). Units 28-29 give no reason against that answer. coherence/mvt2.md also limits the movement to its two sixths on F♭ (21, 64). **Fix.** None.

### 6. Chromatic spelling throughout follows A&S 28 §17-21 (no change)
**Evidence.** I listed every non-diatonic pitch in the section files. Rising chromatics are raised: D♮→E♭ (21, motto tails), E♮→F (19-20), A♮→B♭ (46), E♯→F♯ (47-49). Falling ones are lowered: C–C♭–B♭ (C.a. 33), F–F♭–E♭ (Vla, Hn 4, 87-89). The upper neighbour F♭ above E♭ (Vla 59 b4) is written as a minor 2nd (PDF 496, Ex. 28-22). The motto tail E♭–D♮–D♭–C uses ♯4̂ going down, exactly Ex. 28-24a (PDF 497: C major 5̂–F♯–F–3̂). In D♭ major the vii°7/V at 59 is spelled G–B♭–D♭–F♭, not with E♮. The changes of signature at 47, 51, 63 and 65 follow PDF 490 (spelling by sound in four-flat keys). **Fix.** None.

### 7. Coda: no ♭II colour over the final pedal (not reopened)
**Evidence.** A&S PDF 495 (§15) and PDF 522 (Unit 29 §22) show "♭II" or an augmented sixth on ♭2̂ as a neighbour of a final major tonic. The coda does the same job plagally: the A♭ pedal, A♭–C–D♮–F at 86 (♯4̂ neighbour), IV6 (= N6 of movement I's C minor, PDF 494-495), iv, I. c310 and c312 decided against a Neapolitan in the coda, and the coda is protected. A B𝄫 here would also take away from the finale the ♭II/♭VI resolution that coherence/mvt2.md reserves for it. **Fix.** None.
