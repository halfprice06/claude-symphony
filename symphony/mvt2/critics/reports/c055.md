## What else I studied

Adler's ch. 18 is a checklist, so I compared v55 against it and against movement I's latest score (mvt1/v180/score.ly), which is the other half of the same set of parts and should follow the same conventions. I scanned all eight pages of v55 at 160–250 dpi. I also extracted every dynamic event per player for the eight part-combined pairs with a LilyPond listener engraver. Wherever one player of a pair has a dynamic the other lacks, the other player is resting (Fl 37–44, 57–60, 75–82; Ob 15–20, 45–47; Cl 53–55, 85, 91–92; Bn 31–32, 45–47, 53–55, 68–78; Hn 6–14, 53–61, 67–78; Hn 3·4 1–2, 60; Tbn 63–64). So the "equal simultaneous dynamics" rule holds and Adler's two-set exception (PDF 294, Ex. XVIII-10a) never arises. Gould (*Behind Bars* PDF 54) supports the B♭6 point: it sits on the fifth ledger line, which I counted on the page image of bar 58.

## Findings

### 1. Horns and trumpets carry no key in the score (applied)
Bars: first system and every page. Adler PDF 292 says the keys of the horns and the trumpets "must be given at the beginning of the score". v55 names the clarinet keys ("in B♭", "in B♭ (low C)") but gives none for the horns or trumpets. Its horn and trumpet staves also have no key signatures (c046), which signals transposing parts, so the reader still cannot tell which transposition the parts use. Movement I v180 labels them "Horns 1·2 in F", "Horns 3·4 in F", "Trumpets 1·2 in C", "Trumpet 3 in C". Adler PDF 301 asks for proofreading for consistency, and the two movements share one set of players.
Fix (score.ly):
```
instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Horns 1·2" \small "in F" }
instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Horns 3·4" \small "in F" }
instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Trumpets 1·2" \small "in C" }
instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Trumpet 3" \small "in C" }
```
The `\small` second line matches this score's clarinet labels.

### 2. Horn 4's lone entry at 60 has no player label (applied)
Bar 60, Hn 3·4 staff. Horn 4 plays A♭3 p< alone while horn 3 rests (c046). The part-combiner prints a bare note with no "4.", because horn 3's multi-bar rest R1*24 keeps it from detecting a solo. The page image (p. 4, last bar) shows an unlabelled whole note on a two-player staff. A conductor cannot tell whether one player or both are meant. Adler PDF 294 (Ex. XVIII-2) says each instrument is "labeled every time it enters", and App. A PDF 312 lists "1., 2." as the standard player indications. Every other single-player entry in this score is labelled ("3."/"4." at 1, "2." at 61, 71, "1." at 6, 53, 67).
Fix (sections/bSec.ily, bSecHnThree):
```
R1*23 | \partCombineSoloII R1 | \partCombineAutomatic
```
This prints "4." (the staff's soloIIText) above the note at 60. Horn 3's part is unchanged, because it is a part-combine directive only.

### 3. The timpani staff has a key signature; movement I's does not (applied)
Bars 1–92 (key changes at 37, 47, 51, 63, 65). Movement I v180 removes the Key_engraver from the timpani, which is the usual orchestral practice, and writes each pitch with its accidental. v55 gives the timpani the full key signature and its cancellations: five-flat to three-sharp to five-flat naturals at 47, 51 and 63, on a staff that plays only A♭, C, D♭ and E♭. The "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭" label at 2 then disagrees in spelling with what the staff shows. This is the proofreading consistency Adler asks for (PDF 301). Adler PDF 294 also discourages shortcuts that leave key signatures out; that warns against dropping them partway through. Here the staff would have none from the start, as in movement I.
Fix (score.ly): `\new Staff = "Timpani" \with { \remove "Key_engraver" instrumentName = "Timpani" … }`. In the test build the timpani notes now carry their own flats (e.g. A♭ at 2, D♭ at 60) and the key-change clutter at 47/51/63 is gone from that staff.

### 4. Tempo words are never repeated over the brass (recommended, not applied)
Bars 1, 37, 47–48, 53, 60, 61, 63, 65, 78, 83, 89. Adler PDF 294 "strongly urges" that tempo markings be placed at the top of the score and "repeated in the space separating each choir". His Ex. XVIII-1 (PDF 293) prints "Allegretto ♩ = 100" over the woodwinds, the brass, the percussion and the strings. v55 prints each tempo word at the top and over the strings only (the MOLA minimum, c045). On the full pages (1–6, 57–64, 79–82) the conductor's eye has to travel from the brass to the top of the page to find "Largamente", "allargando" or "poco allargando". Do this only on full-score systems, because adding the engravers to the horn staff would also print a third copy in reduced systems (7–14, 53–56, 65–74). LilyPond fix, for the full-page systems only: attach the words as tagged markups to the first horn note of those bars. For example, in bSec.ily bSecHnTwo bar 61, `f'1\f-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold { "Largamente" }`, and the same for "allargando" on bSecHnThree's rest bar 63 via `R1-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "allargando"` (`\removeWithTag #'score` in the parts). Use the same pattern for "Adagio" at bar 1 (Hn 3). I did not apply it because it changes the c045 design, and the composer should judge whether the clutter is worth it.

### 5. Fourteen bars without a rehearsal letter, 23–36 (recommended, not applied)
Letters fall at 7, 15, 23, 37, 45, 53, 61, 65, 71, 83, which is every 8 bars except 23→37. Bar 31 starts a distinct passage: the woodwind choir alone, bass clarinet solo, "poco rit." at 35, and it is a protected section. Adler PDF 299 (Ex. XVIII-5) asks that every player know where they are "every moment in the performance"; his example places numbers every ten bars. A rehearsal from the bass clarinet solo has no letter to start from. Fix: `\mark \default` before bar 31 in global.ily. Letters D–K then become E–L, so the strMark calls must change to "E" (bSec.ily 147), "F" (152) and "L" (coda.ily 24). Not applied, because it renames every later letter that HISTORY.md and the critique notes refer to.

### 6. Mute changes appear only at the entry (recommended, not applied)
Bars 15 (Vln II, Vla, Vc, Cb con sord.), 37 (same players senza sord.), 87 (Hn 3·4 con sord.). Adler PDF 294 asks for change instructions "well ahead of time … above the player's staff", and App. A PDF 312 gives "via sordini" for "take off mutes". The strings rest from 31 to 36, but "senza sord." first appears on the 37 downbeat. Horn 3·4's mute instruction appears on the note at 87 after a rest from 82. There is enough time in both places, but the warning is missing. In the score the staves are hidden while they rest, so this belongs in the parts: add `-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "via sord."` on the first rest bar of 31 for the four string parts, and `^\markup \italic "muta: con sord."` at 83 in the horn 3 and 4 parts. Hn 4 already uses `\tag #'part` for its own "con sord." at 87, so this follows the existing convention.

### 7. B♭6 on the fifth ledger line in the violin I and flute parts (parts only)
Bars 58 (Vln I, Fl 1), 76, 79–80. Adler PDF 300, rule 8a: in parts, use 8va "as much as possible to avoid more than four ledger lines". B♭6 sits on the fifth ledger line. The score keeps it at pitch (c025, do-not-touch), and Adler allows that because his rule is for parts. When parts are extracted, Vln I 57–59 and 76–81 and Fl 1 57–59 and 75–80 need `\ottava #1` per phrase. No change to the score.

### 8. Shared-staff dynamics: no problem found (check only)
Adler PDF 294 and Ex. XVIII-10a (PDF 301) allow two sets of dynamics on one staff only when the two players differ. My per-player scan found no simultaneous conflict on any of the eight shared staves, so the single-set Staff-level engraving (c038 note) is correct. Recorded so later critics need not repeat the scan.
