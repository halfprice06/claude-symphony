## Findings

### 1. Timpani rolls play as single strokes in score.midi (applied, MIDI only)

Bars 2–3, 52, 60, 61–62, 67–70, 79–81. Note_performer ignores stem tremolo, so each roll, or each chain of tied rolls, sounded as one stroke that dies away in about a second. In v388's MIDI the timpani track has 13 notes in the whole movement. As a result:
- The pp< roll at 60 has no crescendo.
- The f rolls at 79–80 are one stroke per bar.
- The ppp roll at 67–70 under the horn solo is one stroke at 67 followed by four bars of nothing.

Evidence: Rimsky treats the brass crescendo chord as the way into a climax (PDF 130). c358 tied the timpani roll at 60 to the low brass on that ground (PDF 130, 506–507). PDF 136 times the timpani's re-entries on the effect of a group returning after silence. None of these marks can be heard while a roll is one stroke.

Fix: I adopted the coordinator's rolls-midi.ily. Changes in the scratch copy:
- rolls-midi.ily copied into /tmp/critic-388/work.
- In score.ly the staff music becomes a function of the timpani part, `scoreMusic = #(define-music-function (tp) (ly:music?) #{ << ... #tp ... >> #})`, followed by `\score { \scoreMusic \removeWithTag #'part \timp \layout { } }` and `\score { \scoreMusic \rollStrokes #64 \removeWithTag #'part \timp \midi { ...unchanged... } }`.
- `\rollStrokes` acts on the timpani only. The string tremolos at 61–63 have their own CC11 ramps (c300, c368) and are unchanged.

Stroke rate: 64th notes give 13.9 strokes per second at ♩=52, 14.4–16 at ♩=54–60, and 11.7 at the ♩=44 MIDI step at 81. This is within the 12–18 range, apart from the slowest step at 81. 32nd notes would give only 7 strokes per second at 52.

Checks:
- Timpani track: 1240 events (was 50).
- Bar 3 fades from velocity 31 to 20 and ends on the tied eighth.
- Bar 52 rises 37→44 into the p stroke at 53. Bar 60 rises 37→62 into the f eighth.
- Bars 69–70 fade 31→20 with the horn. Bar 81 falls 62→38.
- An event-by-event comparison of every other track shows them identical to v388's score.midi. analysis.txt differs only in the timpani note count and the density column.
- score.pdf is pixel-identical on all 8 pages. The regenerated parts book is pixel-identical on all 30 pages.

This is symphony-wide (c380's open point). The other movements can use the same pattern. The ending is not affected: the last timpani note in movement II is still the single stroke at 84–85.

### 2. Bar 47: the strings' fp plays as a plain p in score.midi (applied, MIDI only)

At 47, Bsn 1·2 begin a p chord and Vln II, the violas and the basses attack fp> on the same downbeat, under Ob 1's mf and the cellos' mp. This is the device of PDF 129 (a): a woodwind p chord with the strings attacking sf at its start (compare Ex. 253, PDF 418, strings "sfp" under a held woodwind chord). It is protected in print (c246).

The render did not play it. score.ly maps "fp" to 0.42, the same as p. Velocity is one value per note, so the whole note came out at p from its first moment: Vln II velocity 53, violas 44, basses 57, below the cellos' mp at 63. The accent that marks the arrival on C♯7 before the quotation was missing.

Fix: "fp" now gets the f velocity, and a CC11 ramp in each of the three parts takes the note back to p after an eighth and to pp by b4. The ramp uses the same pattern as the existing sf> ramps at 63.

```
score.ly:   ("sf" . 0.66) ("fp" . 0.66)            % was ("fp" . 0.42)
bSec.ily (Vln II 193, Vla 219, Cb 268), e.g. Cb:
  << { \after 2. \pp cis,1\fp\> } \tag #'score { s8 \set Staff.midiExpression = #0.64 s8 s4
     \set Staff.midiExpression = #0.58 s4 \set Staff.midiExpression = #0.5 s4 \set Staff.midiExpression = #1.0 } >> |
```

Result:
- The attack velocities are now 83, 62 and 75. CC11 goes 81 → 74 → 64 and back to 127 on 48 b1.
- Vln II shares a MIDI channel with Vln I, which rests from 31 to 51 and enters at 52, after the reset.
- "fp" occurs nowhere else in the movement.
- Nothing else changed in those three tracks. The print and the parts are pixel-identical.

### 3. Bar 63: sf-dim with the strings sustaining (checked, no change)

Rimsky prefers (a), a woodwind p chord under a short string sf. Here the woodwinds leave before 63 and the strings sustain the chord sf>p themselves, which is his case (b). He says case (b) is less effective and gives its remedy: the held chord is played tremolando (PDF 129). The score does exactly that ("trem.", "non div., trem."), and the basses add the pizz. sf as the short chord. Adding a soft woodwind chord would undo the staged removal of groups protected since c213, and would cover Tbn 1's quotation. No change.

### 4. Bars 78–79: anacrusis runs into the second peak (checked, no change)

The runs in Fl 1, Vln I and Vln II are three- and four-note upward runs, slurred to their target, as PDF 129–130 and Ex. 254 describe. Both violin targets at 79 are single notes (B♭6 and B♭5), so no run leads the bow into a three- or four-note chord (PDF 130). No change.

### 5. Re-entries and section ends (checked, no change)

All brass and timpani entries after long rests are pp/ppp or ff, apart from the exceptions recorded in the table (row 26). Each exception has a stated reason that still holds: c088 and c218 for 78, c218 for 79, c246 for 77, c349 and c358 for 60. No section begins or ends on mf or mp (PDF 136). No change.
