## Findings

All five findings record that the score follows the reading. They are ordered by how much each passage depends on the reading.

### 1. The two soft entries after a release follow Mahler's Fig. 6.1 (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 65–67 and 82–85.
**What the reading shows:** in Fig. 6.1 the woodwinds' pp stops before the distant trumpets enter. The strings hold a still pedal, and the trumpets are marked ppp as the result heard in the hall (Casanelles PDF 271–272). Adler part 2 PDF 267 describes the same thinning around a soft solo.
**Score:** at 66 b1 only Vln II D♭4, viola G3, cello B♭2 and the solo basses' E♭2 still sound, all fading. At 67 b1 the snapshot holds only Hn 1 E♭4, the timpani's E♭3 roll ppp near the rim and the basses' E♭2. At 82 b3 the tutti releases. At 83 b1 only the harp harmonic A♭4 sounds over the cellos' and basses' A♭2/A♭1. The violas enter on 84 b3 and Cl 1 on 85. Each new colour enters over a pedal with nothing else moving, as in the Mahler figure.
**Fix:** none.

### 2. Theme A moves further away with each return (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 7, 23, 71–81, 83, 85–92.
**What the reading shows:** each return of *The Social Network* theme is recorded from further away (PDF 283, Fig. 6.2). Distance takes away the highs and keeps the mids (PDF 273). PDF 286 names the orchestral forms of EQ: a solo player, mutes, sul tasto.
**Score:** the theme goes from the open horn (7) to the muted violins (23), then to the open violins in three octaves at ff (79). After that it moves away: harp harmonics (83), the clarinet's niente tail (85–88), the muted-horn echo of horn 3's F–F♭–E♭ (87–90; Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44), and Vln I con sord. sul D on the last C5. The coda mutes added at c468 make the last stage more distant. They follow PDF 286, and I would not undo them.
**Fix:** none.

### 3. Low spacing follows the harmonic series, the last chord included (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 7, 61, 62 b3, 79, 89–92.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 274: the series gives octave, fifth and fourth first, and the major third first appears at the fifth partial.
**Score:** at 61 the D♭ chord is D♭1–D♭2–D♭3 with F3 as the lowest third (fifth partial). At 62 b3 it is E1–E2 under A♭3 (G♯3, fifth partial). At 79 it is D♭1–D♭2–D♭3–A♭3–D♭4–F4. The last chord at 89 is A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–E♭4–A♭4–C5, which is partials 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 of A♭1. Its only third, C5 in Vln I and Cl 1, is the tenth partial. c174 already recorded the one low third at 54 (pp).
**Fix:** none.

### 4. Frequency allotment around the lead lines (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** all lead passages, and 61–62 in particular.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 287: "The fewer the instruments in a frequency range, the clearer and more present they become." Sounds in one range mask each other. PDF 289: harmonically denser instruments, such as forte brass, come forward.
**Score:** the band scan finds at most three or four other instruments in the octave below any lead note. The densest places are:
- 29–30: Hn 3·4, violas and cellos under the muted violins at C5–A♭4.
- 53–56: Cl 1 and Hn 1's counter-melody with the harp under the violins.
- 77 b3: bassoon, harp, Hn 2 and cellos under the violins' C5–B♭4.

All of these are soft accompaniment or the planned counter-melody, and none crosses above the lead. Together with c334's scan, this means no lead line in the movement is masked.

The one place where this reading could argue for a change is 61–62. There the augmented head of theme B (A♭–D♭–F–F♭) sounds in Fl 2, Vln II and cellos, one instrument or section per octave. Hn 1·2 (ff) and Hn 3·4 a2 (f) sit in Vln II's octave (A♭4–E4 against A♭4–F♭5). By PDF 289 the horns come forward there. But the head is also heard in Fl 2 and the cellos, and Vln I and Fl 1 hold the top. The brass layout is protected (c024's ff re-entry rule, c086, c264). This reading gives no reason strong enough to answer those decisions, so I record it and do not propose a change.
**Fix:** none.

### 5. Brass density is graded at the two peaks (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 60–64, 79–81.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 289: forte brass are "essentially equivalent to distortion" in harmonic density. PDF 275: loud brass have dissonant upper partials as their loudest components.
**Score:** at 60 the low brass enter pp. At 61–62 they play f/ff under the ff theme. At 63 the only ff brass line is Tbn 1's quotation, which is meant to come forward over the strings' sf>p. At 79–81 there are no trombones or tuba (c024's recorded choice). The horns are f on the two counter-lines: Hn 1·2 a2 D♭4–C4–B♭3–A♭3 with the cellos, and Hn 3's F4–E4–E♭4, which the coda echoes muted. The densest brass sound is placed at the B-section climax and the quotation. The A′ peak is carried by strings and winds in three octaves. That matches the reading's point that density decides which layer leads.
**Fix:** none.
