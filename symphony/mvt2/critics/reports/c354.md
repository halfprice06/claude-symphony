## Findings

**1. The harp has no melodic line beyond five notes at 82-83 (bars 7-83). Recorded, no change.**
Problem: BRIEF.md still lists "melodic moments for horn, bass clarinet, harp and timpani" among the rules that bind this movement. The horn (7-14, 67-70), bass clarinet (31-34) and timpani (84-85) have theirs. The harp's only melodic notes are the pickup E♭4-F4 at 82 b3 and the harmonics A♭-C-E♭ at 83, which is theme A's head. Everything else is accompaniment or bass doubling, and coherence/mvt2.md says so.
Evidence: Adler PDF 117 names three uses: melodic, accompanying, doubling. Ex. IV-8 (Debussy, PDF 116) shows the harp's broken chords sharing a phrase with the flute. PDF 116 says harmonics must be accompanied very lightly, which is why the melody here can only be short.
Why no change: cyclic-notes.md (lines 337, 393, 443) moved the full harp melody to the scherzo, bars 73-80, and the coda bars that could carry a longer harp line here (83-89) are protected for the harp-timpani-clarinet chain and the plagal close. A longer harmonic line at 83-84 would sit on top of the protected timpani answer at 84 and break the "a cappella" condition of PDF 116. The five-note harmonic statement meets the movement's rule in its smallest form. I suggest the composer notes in coherence/mvt2.md that the harp-melody item of the BRIEF is met by the scherzo, so later critics do not reopen it.

**2. Timpani 84-85: how long the E♭ and C ring. Recorded, no change.**
Problem: `es4.\pp c8 aes,4~ | aes,2. | R2.` gives written values to strokes that will ring at pp with soft sticks. Adler PDF 80 asks that the part show how long each note may ring.
Evidence and answer: the score has a clear convention. It prints "damp" where a stroke must stop (53, 62) and lets every other stroke ring. Here the three drums sound the tonic triad over the strings' A♭ pedal, and the violas enter on C4-E♭4 at 84 b3, so ringing strokes add only notes of the chord. The A♭ is tied to the end of 85 and the next harmony change (87, IV6) comes about 7 s after the last stroke, when a pp stroke on soft sticks has died away. The part is complete under the score's own convention. A "l.v." would make the A♭'s end at 85 ambiguous.

**3. Timpani at the A′ peak 79-82: IV and V struck, no stroke on the tonic arrival. Recorded, no change.**
Problem: Adler PDF 79 gives the classical role as strengthening the tonic and dominant bass notes and completing climactic cadences. At 79-80 the timpani roll D♭ (IV), at 81 E♭ (V) with a diminuendo to nothing, and at 82 (I) they rest.
Answer: 82 is a soft arrival (winds and strings p, falling to pp); the tonic is struck by the timpani two bars later in the solo answer at 84-85, so the drum's A♭ arrives as melody rather than as a cadence stroke. The rest at 82 was an earlier decision (HISTORY line 684), and c296 recorded that a tied stroke into 82 would bring back what c123 removed. At the first peak 61 the drums do play tonic and dominant (A♭ at 60, D♭ at 61-62), which meets PDF 79. The D♭ roll at 79 under IV is also later practice (four drums, PDF 79-80) and doubles the bass.

**4. Harp registers: only the dark and warm octaves are used (whole movement). Recorded, no change.**
Problem: Adler PDF 113-114 describes three colour zones. The harp's sounding range here is E♭1 to E♭5. All its LH octaves (7-14, 37-44, 71-78) lie on the wire-wound strings (the lowest eleven, PDF 112), and nothing lies in the light top two and a half octaves.
Answer: the choice suits the movement. The low octaves under the horn solo at 7-14 match PDF 113's "sombre, dark, and sonorous" and keep the harp below the horn (c057). The triplets at 53-56 sit in the "rich and warm" middle under theme B in the strings. The top register, which PDF 114 says has little carrying power, would not be heard in the two places the texture rises (57-62, 77-81), and the harp rests there. The bell colour of the top is supplied by the harmonics at 83. The ringing of successive low octaves at 8 and 72 was raised by c214 (F3) and rejected; I have no new book reason to reopen it.
