### 1. The stated intentions are out of date in three places (Sebesky PDF 240: compare intentions with results)
- **Bars 60, 84, 85; mvt2.md and coda.ily text only.**
- mvt2.md, "Timpani tunings" table, row 60-62: it reads "A♭2 roll p < f (60)". Since v359 (c358) the score has `aes,2.:32\pp\<~ aes,8\f` (bSec.ily, bSecTimp), which is pp<. The finale and scherzo threads read this table, so it should be corrected to "A♭2 roll pp < f (60, with the low brass)".
- mvt2.md, "Ending" section: it reads "84–85: timpani alone answer". The violas enter ppp on 84 beat 3 under the answer's held A♭2, by design (c124). It would be clearer as "timpani answer over the A♭ pedal; violas enter on 84 b3".
- coda.ily, codaTimp, the c019 comment: it said "Vln I's tail E flat-D-D flat-C follows at 85". The tail at 85-89 is in Clarinet 1, and Vln I takes only the C5 at 89. **Applied in scratch:** the comment now reads "Cl 1's tail E flat-D-D flat-C follows at 85-89, Vln I taking its C5 at 89 (… c380: comment corrected, was 'Vln I's tail')". This is comment only, so score, MIDI and parts are unchanged.
- I checked the other claims in mvt2.md and they match v380. These include: the 1-6 link parts; Bsn 1 a third below Hn 1 at 68; the last chord A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5; Cl 2's E♭4 at 89; Hn 3·4 "via sord." at 91; the muta at 92; theme B and both quotations.

### 2. Printed rolls are still single strokes in the render (Sebesky PDF 241-242: music does not exist on paper; compare the written notes with the aural result)
- **Bars 2-3, 52, 60-62, 67-70, 79-81.**
- The A♭2 roll at 2-3 is the drum's part in the common-tone link. It replaces movement I's C3 roll at its bars 181-182. The ppp E♭ roll at 67-70 sits under Hn 1's motto tail. In score.midi and score.mp3 each roll is one decaying stroke. This has been open since c244, and c334 proposed hidden MIDI voices.
- New evidence: articulate.ly in LilyPond 2.24.3 does expand stem tremolos (`es2.:32` gives 24 strokes). In a probe, however, it dropped the strokes of a roll tied into the next bar (`es2.:32~ | es2.:32` gave 24 notes, not 48). Every long roll in this movement is tied, so `\articulate` is not a drop-in fix. Movements I and III also write rolls as `:32` and render them the same way.
- **Recommendation:** do not change II alone. If the threads agree on a symphony-wide MIDI-only score block, use `\articulate` on the timpani staff only, with untied roll segments (for example `es2.:32 | es2.:32` without `~`) in that MIDI copy. That keeps the printed ties. **Not applied.**

### 3. Each cyclic statement has its own instrument for a reason (Adler PDF 22; Sebesky PDF 241, know why)
- The motto tail appears six times: Hn 1 at 8 (movement I's S-head colour, Hn 1 over harp); violins at 24 and 72; B.cl. with C.a. at 32-34 (movement I's first two motto instruments, at the B.cl.'s pitch); Hn 1 alone at 67-69; Cl 1 at 85-89.
- The C5 goes back to Vln I at 89. Vln I is movement I's last motto instrument, at its pitch and on the D string.
- No statement is in a colour chosen at random, and none of them repeats an earlier statement's colour and register together. The scherzo answers in Hn 1 and the finale answers in Ob 1 (IV 95-98, at II's closing pitches), so the closing colours of the three later movements do not repeat either. **Record, no change.**

### 4. The quotations are old sounds in new proportions (Sebesky PDF 242; Rochberg, PDF 241)
- **Bars 47-50 and 63-64.**
- The S head keeps movement I's rhythm and intervals but changes key (F♯ minor), colour (cor anglais), dynamic (p) and harmony (i–VI6–N6–V7–i). The P head keeps its notes in A minor but is moved to one trombone, ff, once.
- That matches Sebesky's definition of a new sound: the old material is recognisable, with every other parameter changed. The S-head rhythm occurs nowhere else in the movement (checked), so the once-only rule holds. **Record, no change.**

### 5. Colour changes mark the form (Adler PDF 19-20)
- A new combination enters at each formal joint, and each one coincides with a change of mood (Adler's Monteverdi rule read in reverse). The joints are: 7 (wind choir with Hn 1, strings out), 15 (muted strings with oboe), 31 (woodwind choir alone), 37 (flute over pizzicato and harp), 47 (cor anglais in the remote key), 63 (one trombone over tremolo), 65 (strings fade, horn over basses and roll), 83 (harp harmonics, timpani, solo clarinet).
- Inside sections the combination stays stable, for example the violins and cellos at 23-30 and 71-78. **Follows.**

### 6. Timpani apart from trumpets in II, rejoined in IV (Adler PDF 21)
- Adler notes that the Classical timpani went with the trumpets. Movement II has no trumpets, so its timpani pair with the low brass (60-62), the horns (67-70, 79-81), and at 84-85 play a melodic answer of their own (E♭–C–A♭). That answer leads to the scherzo's motive x (III 9) and to the finale's pulse.
- The finale brings the Classical pairing back at its hammered quotation 199-204 (trumpets with timpani `c4 r8 c8`, finale.md item 1). Across the symphony the drums leave the trumpets in the slow movement and return to them at the arrival.
- **Record only.** One sentence in mvt2.md under "Scoring facts" would let the finale thread cite it.

### 7. The reduction of the two ends shows one gesture (Adler PDF 23)
- Reduced to two staves, 1-6 is C major → A♭ with Hn 3's C4–D♭4–C4 over iv6/4. 87-92 is IV6 – iv – I with Cl 1's D♭5 → Vln I's C5 on top and the F–F♭–E♭ line inside.
- Both ends turn on the same D♭–C neighbour over the same subdominant colour, an octave apart. The reduction is clear, with no doubled leading notes or crowded spacing. **Follows; protected.**

### 8. The arc between I and III does not sag (Sebesky PDF 240)
- Movement I ends ppp with a tam-tam and chorale. II starts on that chord, builds over 60 bars to the one ff peak with brass (61-64), and has a second string-and-wind peak (79-81) that drops at once. It then thins one instrument at a time to a plain A♭ chord with no seventh, as the finale requires. The scherzo then starts with one horn on G♯4.
- The two places where the movement nearly stands still (65-70 and 83-86) both introduce a cyclic statement over a pedal. They are where movement I's material is heard most clearly. **No change.**
