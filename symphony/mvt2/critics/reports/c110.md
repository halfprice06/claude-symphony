## Findings

### 1. Bar 58: the B section reaches B♭6, the A′ peak, three bars before its own climax (applied)

**Problem.** In theme B's second phrase at 58, Fl 1 and Vln I play `aes'''4 bes'''8 aes'''8`. That B♭6 is an eighth-note neighbour, marked p< in the flute and inside the violins' mf→f crescendo. It is the highest note of the B section. It sits above the section's climax (A♭6 at 61–62, A6 at 63), and it is the same pitch as the movement's peak at 79 (`bes'''2.` ff), which is theme A's B♭–A♭ appoggiatura enlarged.

The movement's own register plan says otherwise. The cycle 51–60 relay in cyclic-notes.md reads: "B-section peak A6 at 63 … below the B♭6 at 79." The plan stopped being true in cycle 61, when the 4–3 suspension at 42/58 brought in the B♭ turn. The B♭ is harmless at 42, two octaves lower.

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 601–602 (Ex. 32-22c): in the K. 542 reduction the high points form a line, and that line is the motive. Here the line is A♭6 (B climax) → A6 (P-head quotation) → B♭6–A♭6 (A′ peak). It works only if B♭6 is new at the A′ climax.
- A&S PDF 600: the plan should "project over a long span of time those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece". A passing B♭6 at 58 uses up that pitch before its point.
- Across the cycle: movement I's violins touch A♭6 only at its four climaxes and never go higher. Movement II's B♭6 is its first step above that ceiling.
- Dvořák 9/ii (corpus, above): the middle section stays at D6 or below, and the top register is kept for the climax.

**Fix (applied).** Hold A♭6 in the top octave only. Ob 1 and Vln II keep the B♭5 turn an octave below, and the G♭–F 4–3 suspension (protected since c098) is untouched.
```

aes'''2 ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8) \breathe |
% bSec.ily, bSecVlnOne bar 58
aes'''2) ges'''4(~ ges'''8 f'''8) |
```
The bow is unchanged: 57 b3–58 b2 is still one slur. The analysis density line for 57–60 now tops at A♭6 instead of B♭6. After this change, B♭6 sounds only at 76 (the A′ theme's appoggiatura, protected) and 79–80.

This touches the protected "build 53–61" only in one top-octave figure. It answers the composer's own register plan as relayed to cyclic-notes.

### 2. Bar 1: movement II's parts and score never say the movement follows attacca (applied)

**Problem.** Since v220, every movement I part prints "attacca" at bar 182. Movement II's side of the join carries no matching instruction:
- the parts book (26 parts) has no "attacca" text;
- Vln I, Vln II, Vla, Vc and Hn 3 open with a tie arc that comes from nowhere;
- Hn 4 must enter on beat 2 under a held C4;
- every other player must be ready at once, because the A♭ arrives at bar 2, about 3½ seconds after movement I's fermata;
- the score's title block says nothing, while movement I's score says "Movement I ends attacca into movement II".

**Evidence.**
- A&S PDF 586: the two keys hold together only because "an important common element" is sustained across the change. Here that element is C5.
- A&S PDF 585: rhythm and texture "influence the effect of a modulation". A pause between the movements would separate C major from A♭ major and remove the common-tone modulation itself.
- MOLA PDF 5 (cited in mvt I c200): performance instructions belong "on all parts".

**Fix (applied).**
- In genparts.py, as movement I's parts-gen.py does:
  ```
  partGlobalPlain = \noBreaks \global
  partGlobal = << \partGlobalPlain { <>_\markup \small \italic "attacca from movement I" } >>
  ```
  The harp's left-hand staff uses `\partGlobalPlain`, so the harp prints it once.
- In score.ly, the subsubtitle gains `\small "Follows movement I attacca: bar 1 continues its last chord"`.
- Result: the parts book builds with rc=0 and 0 warnings, still 27 pages; the text appears once in each of the 26 parts. The score stays 8 pages.

## For movement III

1. **Leave ♭VI by way of V (A&S PDF 592–593, 600).**
   - Movement II is an expanded ♭VI of C, entered by a common tone exactly as in Widmung.
   - The reading names the usual continuation: ♭VI becomes an augmented sixth and goes to a cadential V, with the bass falling a half step.
   - Chopin's ♭VI–V (D–C♯) enlarges a motive from his opening. Our matching motive is A♭–G, which is in the S head (`c'4 aes'2 g'4`) and in movement I's A♭ climaxes.
   - Proposal: make A♭→G the first structural bass motion of movement III. Movement II's last chord plus F♯ is the German sixth (c010 relay).
2. **Close the E♭/E♮ thread the Haydn way (A&S PDF 597–598, Ex. 32-17).**
   - Movement II's main top-voice tone is E♭: theme A's held note, which came from movement I's viola E♮.
   - At movement III's major arrival, spell the augmented sixth A♭–C–D♯–F♯ with D♯ on top, rising to E in the cadential 6/4. E♭ then returns to E♮ through its enharmonic, in the top voice.
