## Findings

### 1. Theme B after bar 61: its absence is right; do not add a recall (bars 61–62, 83–92). No change.

**Question.** Theme B's last full statement is the build at 53–60. Its last appearance of any kind is the augmented head at 61–62: Fl 1·2 `aes''2 des'''2 | f'''2 fes'''2` and Vln II `aes'2 des''2 | f''2 fes''2`. Would a brief recall of the head in the coda (83–92) make the form stronger?

**Evidence from the reading.**
- Ex. 32-21/22 (PDF 601–602) and Points for Review 5 (PDF 603) say a key area or chromatic line is held into the whole by its link to a motive. That link already exists here. At the climax, theme B's head does not finish as a theme: it bends from F to F♭ (61–62). The F♭ then carries the music through E (62–64) to the E♭7 retransition (65). The coda's mixture line F–F♭–E♭ (C.a., Hn 4 con sord., lower violas sul C, 87–89) is the same F–F♭, now finished on E♭ over the plagal iv–I. In K.542, Mozart's chromatic passage is the opening motive enlarged. Here the coda's chromatic line is the end of theme B's last head statement, brought to its resolution. The coda therefore already recalls the shape theme B was left in at the climax. A literal restatement would repeat this link, not supply a missing one.
- PDF 592 and 603 describe conflicts that need working out because the area is *chromatic* (remote mixture). D♭ is IV, a diatonic area. It needs no motivic rescue. Harmonically it returns in the plagal close: IV6 at 87, iv at 88 and I at 89, with D♭5–C5 on top. This is the D♭ section folded into one cadence, the same correlation of plan and detail as Chopin's D♮–C♯ (PDF 600).
- Theme B shares material with the coda. Bar 42 and Cl 1's tail at 85–89 both end D♭–C (4̂–3̂).

**Evidence from the score: no clean place for the head.**
- At 86, Cl 1 holds D♮5, the third note of the motto tail. A head A♭–D♭–F leading into 87 would put D♭4 against D♮5, an augmented octave, during the movement's last tail statement.
- At 87–88, the cyclic rule in BRIEF.md (plagal close with D♭–C on top) rules out the upper octave: F5 would sit above Cl 1's D♭5. At 88, F♮ would clash with the F♭ of iv.
- The only form that works is an inner-voice head A♭3–D♭4–F4 over IV6 at 87, in the cor anglais (theme B's own instrument at 41–44). It would lead straight into its F♭4–E♭4. I tested this in /tmp/critic-263/variant (`codaCorAnglais = { R2.*4 | aes4(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" des'4 f'4 | fes'2. | ...`). The build has 0 warnings, 8 pages and 0 outer-voice parallels, and the parts book stays at 30 pages. It still does not convince. In quarter notes, at pp, inside a held IV6 chord with the same pitches in Hn 3, the violas and Vln II, the head does not have theme B's rhythm (2 8 8 4 at 53; 4 4 4. 8 at 57). A listener would hear it as an arpeggio of the chord. It also adds three attacks to a texture that c199 thinned to let it decay.

**Evidence from the models and the symphony.**
- Dvořák 9/ii, the main model, drops its middle theme after the return (corpus woods.notesL.ily, see above). Its coda recalls only the opening chords and the Largo theme.
- The finale's D♭ episode quotes theme B (finale 211–218). Its horns answer with theme B's opening at movement II's own pitch, A♭3–D♭4–F4–G♭4 at 217–218 (coherence/finale.md item 15). c100 closed this point by leaving the recall to a later movement, and the finale now does it. If movement II recalls the head after 61, the finale's quotation is no longer the theme's first return after its climax. It also changes movement II's ending, which the finale and scherzo threads would then have to recheck.

**Fix.** None. Record the item as closed with these reasons, so later critics do not reopen it without answering them. If the composer wants a recall anyway, the cor anglais head at 87 above is the only setting I found that keeps every protected element and builds clean. Because it touches the ending, it would have to be reported to the finale thread.

### 3. The two remote areas stay within the reading's limits (45–51, 62–64). No change.

PDF 592 warns that expanded remote areas threaten "directed, goal-oriented progression". Both of this movement's remote areas are short: F♯ minor lasts 4½ bars (47–51) and F♭/A minor about 2½ bars (62 b3–64), together about 9% of the movement's quarter beats. Each holds one movement I quotation, so each has a motivic reason (PDF 603). Each leaves by semitone voice leading: G♯7→A♭7 into D♭ at 51–52, and E→E♭ into V7 at 64–65 (PDF 593, Ex. 32-10a). Nothing to change. This agrees with c253's proportions (36/40/24%, climax at 65% of the duration).

### 4. Bar 78's ♭VI follows Ex. 32-11 (77–79). No change.

The deceptive cadence onto F♭ under the melody's held A♭ (78 b1–2) combines two of the reading's ways into ♭VI: deceptive cadence (PDF 592–593) and a common tone in the soprano (PDF 586–587). The bass F♭→E♭ (c070) and the ii6/5–V at 79–81 then take the expected route to V (PDF 593). The return section therefore restates the climax's F♭ inside the tonic key before the coda takes it up a third time. In form terms this is the right sequence: chromatic area (62), inflection inside the key (78), resolution in the coda (87–89).
