## Findings

All findings are "no change". I rank them by how much it would matter if the point were wrong. Findings 1, 2 and 5 are new observations and should be recorded.

### 1. Movement I's stop chord at 33 is the reading's common-tone diminished seventh, a true enharmonic rereading of movement I's leading-tone chord (bar 33, also 64). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 589 and Ex. 32-7: a °7 "can act as a common-tone embellishment rather than as a leading-tone chord". PDF 588: true enharmonics "would be heard even if they were not expressed in the notation". PDF 597–598 (Ex. 32-17): Haydn ties two areas together through one pitch that is heard first as D♯ and later as E♭.

**Score.** In movement I the stop chord B–D–F–A♭ is VII°7 of C minor, and its B rises to C. At 33 b2 movement II spells it D–F–A♭–C♭ (B.cl. D3, Bsn 1 F3, C.a. C♭4) over Bsn 2's held A♭2 (trans.ily line 25, tied through 33 into 34 b1) and the contrabassoon's A♭1. The C.a. takes C♭4 to B♭3 at 34. The same four pitches therefore become an embellishment of A♭, and the old leading tone falls instead of rising. This is a real change of function, which is the "true" kind of enharmonic in the reading's sense, not a change of spelling for convenience. The same happens at 64→65, where C♭/B again falls a semitone. The rise B→C is saved for the finale (Tpt 1 B4→C5 at IV 222–223, finale.md item 3). So the symphony follows Ex. 32-17 on the largest scale: one pitch is reread in the middle movements and restored to its first meaning at the end.

**Fix.** None. Keep C♭ falling to B♭ at 33–34 and the semitone fall at 64–65. Do not add a B→C resolution anywhere in II.

### 2. Each statement of the motto tail gives its D♮ a different harmonic meaning, as the Pathétique gives its returning °7 a new one (8, 24, 33, 68, 72, 86). No change.

**Reading.** Ex. 32-6 (PDF 588): material from the introduction comes back inside the Allegro, and its diminished seventh is reread (E♭ becomes D♯) so that it leads to E minor instead of G minor. The material returns unchanged, but its meaning changes. PDF 600–601: large-scale plans project "those pitch relationships that are most characteristic of the piece".

**Score.** The tail E♭–D–D♭–C appears seven times, and its chromatic D♮ is reharmonised in a new way at each stage of the movement:
- 8, 24, 72 (theme A): the third of V7/V, between vi7 and V7.
- 33 (B.cl. solo, transition): a member of the common-tone °7 over the A♭ pedal (Finding 1).
- 68 (Hn 1 alone, retransition): a chromatic passing tone over the E♭ pedal, which is the leading tone of the dominant (retr.ily, Hn 1 line 18 with Bsn 1 B♭3–A♭3 under it).
- 86 (Cl 1, coda): D5 over the A♭ pedal with violas C4–F4 (coda.ily line 31), a half-diminished D–F–A♭–C, which then moves to IV6 – iv – I at 87–89.

The pitch never changes and its context always does. This is the reason seven statements do not sound like repetition, and the reason the scherzo (B–A♯–A–G♯ over an A♯°7 at 357–360) and the finale (G–F♯–F–E) can give it yet another harmony a major third lower each time. The tail statements were reduced in earlier cycles (c011 took the tail out of theme B at 42/58). This finding shows why the remaining ones are needed.

**Fix.** None. A change to the harmony under any tail statement should keep the D♮ in a different chord from the statements next to it.

### 3. Movement II prepares the scherzo's III♯ in stages through F♭ = E (5, 21, 62, 64, 78, 88). No change.

**Reading.** PDF 593: III♯ "often requires a more elaborate preparation than does one to ♭VI", because it is further from the tonic in sound. PDF 596: a remote area can be approached "in stages". Ex. 32-14 (PDF 595): D♭s placed in the right hand before the double bar "help prepare the listener" for D♭.

**Score.** At symphony level, II (A♭) is ♭VI of C, which is easy to reach, and the scherzo (E) is III♯ of C, which is hard to reach. Movement II does the preparation for III♯ that the reading asks for, inside its own diatonic frame. F♭ sounds as ♭6̂ at 5 (Cl 2, iv6/4). The French sixth stands on F♭ at 21. The climax reaches an F♭-major triad at 62, which is the scherzo's tonic chord at full orchestra. 64 stands on E. The A′ deceptive cadence makes F♭ major the chord on the downbeat of 78. The coda's iv at 88 has F♭ in the lower violas, Hn 4 and C.a. Every one of these F♭s falls to E♭ inside II, so A♭ stays the key. Even so, the listener has heard the E-major triad twice at the movement's two loudest points before the scherzo makes it a key, as Brahms's D♭s prepare his ♭III. Then the common tone A♭ = G♯ (Vln II A♭4 at 89–92 → scherzo Hn 1 G♯4) completes the Widmung connection (Ex. 32-3). c243 and c460 record the F♭ thread as a mixture line. This finding adds its function at symphony level.

**Fix.** None. Keep the F♭ triads at 62 and 78 and the F♭ in the iv at 88.

### 4. Both joins and both quotations still hold at pitch (1–2, 47–50, 63–64, 85–92). No change; confirms c410 and c530 on v540.

**Reading.** PDF 587 (common tone exposed, chromatic inflection between major triads). PDF 602 (the remote area's motivic link).

**Score.** intro.ily: Vln I `c''2.\repeatTie` ppp held 1–3, Vln II G4→A♭4, Vla E4→E♭4, Vc C3→A♭2, Hn 3 C4 \repeatTie. These are movement I's last five notes, with C5 on top (Widmung's sustained top note). The coda's last chord is A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5, ppp, with the muta printed at 92. The record checks lastAflat, lastC, lastEflat, timpAnswer and both quotations, and all pass (19/19, 11/11). Each quotation sits in a remote area, is heard once, and carries the G♯/A semitone (c523). Nothing has touched the joins since c530.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Movement II's soft ending is one of three soft endings in a row (I, II, III). This is consistent with the arc and has a precedent. No change.

**Reading.** PDF 585: dynamics, texture and rhythm decide whether a key change is gentle or abrupt.

**Score.** Movement I ends on a ppp C-major chord, II ends ppp on A♭ with muted upper strings, and the scherzo ends on Hn 1 G♯4 fading to ppp after its fff at 336. The two common-tone joins work because both are gentle. A loud close at the end of II would make the A♭ = G♯ link abrupt and would take away the scherzo's opening, where Hn 1 plays alone, pp. The whole symphony's single fff close is kept for the finale. Brahms's Third Symphony ends all four movements softly, so three soft endings in a row is not a weakness. The movement-II side of the arc is right: two ff peaks (61–62, 79–80), no fff, and a ppp close.

**Fix.** None in II. If anyone ever wants the arc to vary more, the place to change it is the scherzo's epilogue, not II's coda. I note this for the coordinator only and make no request.
