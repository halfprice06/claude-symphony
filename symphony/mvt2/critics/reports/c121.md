## What else I studied

HISTORY.md, especially the melody-lens cycles (1, 31, 101, 111) and c031, the first pass of this chapter. c031 drew solo violin 17–22, Vln II on the G string at 70 (later sul D, c076) and the theme B head rhythm from it, so I did not repeat those. I read every section file and listed, for each theme statement, which string group plays it, in which register, and with which string marks. I also checked the engraved pages for 15–52 and 71–92. For context I looked at Dvořák 9/ii in the corpus (`mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily`). Its only string-colour devices in the reprise are the reductions to "4 violins", "2 violins con sordini" and "1 violin" (lines 111, 116, 122). It has no string marks, so this score's sul markings are a later Romantic practice of the kind Adler's examples show (Tchaikovsky, Brahms 3, Strauss).

Melodic verdict: theme A works. It rises by arpeggio to a held note, then falls chromatically (E♭–D–D♭–C), and its two phrases share the rhythm `4. 8 4~ | 4 4 4`. You hear the head five times (7, 23, 31, 71, 82) and the tail six times, so a listener leaves able to hum it. This is the kind of recurrence that makes Dvořák's Largo tune stick. Theme B is the more ornamental tune: five of its first six bars carry an eighth-note neighbour. Its second phrase, with the rising fourth and the A♭–B♭–A♭–G♭ peak, gives it a profile. Its three statements (37 flute, 45 minor head, 53 violins) are each presented in a colour the reading endorses. The reading has very little to correct in how the strings carry the tunes. The one point is below.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 44 b4–47: mark the cellos' minor statement of theme B "sul D", with "ord." at 48 (applied)

**Problem.** At 44 b4–47 the cellos carry the head of theme B in B♭ minor: A♭3–D♭4–E♭4–D♭4–C4–B♭3–A3–B♭3–D♭4–C♯4, p, slurred, with Ob 1 an octave above and Bsn 1 in unison. Nothing tells the players which string to use. In first position the line crosses from the D string to the A string at D♭4. The chromatic neighbour A3 in bar 46 then falls on the open A string, as an eighth between two B♭3s, in the slowest and most exposed part of the phrase. Adler PDF 73–74: in "slow, expressive passages, one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture"; the open string "stands out peculiarly". He also names the cello's D string as "the most musically captivating string… warm and lyrical" and the A string as "the most brilliant and piercing" (PDF 99). This is the only theme statement the cellos lead, and a minor-key statement, p, is where you want the warm string rather than the piercing one. Adler PDF 73 also says a string mark is justified exactly when the timbre is what you want.

**Evidence.** bSec.ily, bSecVc bars 44–47 (`r4 \clef tenor aes4(\p | des'2 es'8 des'8 c'4) | bes4( a8 bes8) des'2\< | cis'2.\mf\>`). The whole line lies between A♭3 and E♭4, which is a perfect fifth to a minor ninth above the open D3. That is the same compass the score already gives sul D to the violins at 22–26 and 70–74 (E♭4–E♭5 over open D4), and c076 accepted it there as within the reading's limit. The A♮ is a chromatic note that c091 added on purpose ("A♮ neighbour at 46"). Stopped on the D string it keeps the vibrato of the notes around it. On the open A it would be the only unvibrated note in the phrase.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-121/work, sections/bSec.ily, markings only):**

```lilypond
% bSecVc, bar 44
... | es2) r4 \clef tenor aes4(\p^\markup \italic "sul D" |
% bar 48
... | \clef bass cis1\pp^\markup \italic "ord." | ...
```

No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or scoring change, so the protected "theme B scoring 45–60" and the cello bowing from c118 are untouched. MIDI is byte-identical to v121.

### 2. Bookkeeping: the do-not-touch phrase "B♭6 at 79 only" does not match the score (no music change)

**Problem.** Since c110 the standing list has said "registral peak B♭6 at 79 only". In v121, Vln I also plays B♭6 at 76 b2 (`f'''4 bes'''4( aes'''4)`, mp–f). This note is theme A's own B♭ appoggiatura, which is protected as part of the A′ octaves 75–82. c120 relayed the fact ("B♭6 occurs at 76 (mp) and 79"), but HISTORY still records the old wording. A later critic reading "79 only" could treat 76 as an error and propose dropping it, or read the climax as unprepared.

**Evidence and verdict.** Adler PDF 72 and 75: the top of the E string is hard to control and strident when forced. At 76 the B♭6 is a quarter-note appoggiatura at mp, reached from F6 by a fourth and released at once. That is the soft, "clear and luminous" use the reading allows. At 79 the same pitch comes back held at ff after the grace run, so the climax is an augmentation of a note already heard in passing. For the melody this is a strength: the listener hears the peak inside the tune first, and the climax confirms it. I recommend no change to the notes.

**Fix.** In HISTORY's do-not-touch list, replace "registral peak B♭6 at 79 only" with "B♭6 only as theme A's appoggiatura: in passing at 76 (Vln I, mp) and held at 79 (ff); nowhere in the B section".

### Checked, no change

- **Solo violin 17–22 (PDF 75, 81).** With first-position fingering the solo cell A♭5–B♭5–G5–F5–E♭5–D♭5 crosses from the E string to the A string. Brahms 3/iii (Ex. III-7) would keep a p espress. line like this on the A string. Against the oboe, though, the solo needs the carrying E-string tone to read as a separate voice over muted strings. The soloist can choose, so I give no instruction.
- **Viola D4 at 50 (PDF 73–74).** It is an open-string pitch, but here it is a short portato off-beat eighth under "sotto voce", so a player will finger it. No mark is needed.
- **Viola G3 at 65 and Vln II D4 at 64.** Both are open-string pitches, and both are already covered by "sul C" and "sul G". The reading's point is already met.
- **Double basses 4 soli 65–70 (PDF 105).** This is a held pedal under the horn, not a bass solo line, so the warning about a thin solo does not apply.
- **Motto tail on the violins at 24 and 72 (PDF 84).** The one-finger chromatic slide gives a portamento, which is idiomatic at ♩=52.
