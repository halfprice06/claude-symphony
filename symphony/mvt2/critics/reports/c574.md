## Findings

The score follows the chapter. Every principle that it only partly meets has a recorded reason and lies in protected bars. I found nothing that would bring the score closer to a master's work, so I applied no edit. Findings 1, 3 and 4 apply readings that c434 did not use (the "ping" at 63, the Schubert unison-to-harmony passage, the harp as the constant). The others are re-checks against the current MIDI.

### 1. Bars 63-64: the A-minor chord after the peak is a chord "under control" (follows; no change)

PDF 164-165 (Debussy, Ex. XV-16): a chord struck loud that must die away puts its top notes in instruments that can diminuendo. The violins can play softly on any note, and a harp or piccolo gives only a "ping". At 63 b1 the top A6 and E6 are in Vln I as a non-divisi double-stop tremolo, sf>p. Vln II (C6/E5) and Vla (C5/E4) are inside the chord. Fl 1 doubles the A6 for one beat at f and then rests. That is the "ping" in Debussy's sense: an attack at the top that the flute never has to hold at p. Tbn 1's ff marcato entry at 63 b3 is the only undoubled line, as with Debussy's trumpets. Keep it.

### 2. Bars 57-60: three layers with mixed colours and a clear dynamic order (follows; no change)

PDF 178-179 (Scheherazade, Ex. XV-27) asks for a foreground, a middleground figuration and a sustained background, each with its own rhythm, and for doubling in mixed colours. The MIDI dump at 57 shows four layers. The foreground is theme B in Vln I + Fl 1 and Vln II + Ob 1 (string plus woodwind in each octave). The middleground line is Hn 1's counter-line, with the cellos and Bsn 1 joining it (string, woodwind and brass, as on PDF 178). The figuration is the viola triplets, which become sextuplets at 59. The background is Cl, B.cl., Bsn 2 and Cb. The violins come into 57 at mf from the crescendo at 56. The winds enter p< under them, the violas mp< and Hn 1 mp, so the principal voice stays the loudest, as Schoenberg's marking does on PDF 184. Hn 1's first beat in unison with the oboe and Vln II's A♭4 is recorded (c160, c303). Keep it.

### 3. Bars 65-70: unison into harmony over a held pedal, as in Schubert 8 (follows; no change)

In Ex. XV-19 (PDF 168-169) a unison passes into harmony, and the held pedals in the brass and timpani carry the change. At 65-66 the strings hold E♭ in octaves at pp. At 67 the four solo basses (sul A) and the timpani near the rim hold E♭. Hn 1 and then the bassoons build the harmony over that pedal, and the violins enter only on the pickup at 70. The strings are slow to break into harmony, which is the feature Adler points to. This passage is on the do-not-touch list (c003 and later). Keep it.

### 4. Theme A's three settings and the harp as the constant (follows; no change)

Mahler 4/iv (PDF 169-171, Ex. XV-20 to XV-22) rescores each return but keeps the texture and chord structure, and in the last version the harp is the constant. Here theme A is scored three ways: horn over bassoons and B.cl. with the harp's bass at 7-14; muted strings alone in the plain statement at 23-30 (harp out, c007); violins in octaves with the harp's bass back at 71-82. The coda then uses harp harmonics at 83 as the constant colour under timpani and clarinet, the same move as Mahler's last version. The harmony and accompaniment pattern hold across all three. Keep them.

### 5. Theme B's colour changes fall on phrase boundaries (follows; no change)

PDF 176 (Mozart, Jupiter finale) warns against shifting colours that bury the ideas. Theme B's colours change at 37 (Fl 1), 41 (C.a.), 45 (Ob 1 with Vc and Bsn 1), 47 (C.a. quotation), 53 (violins) and 57 (violins with Fl 1 and Ob 1). Each change comes at a four-bar or two-bar phrase start, usually after a rest or a held note. No colour changes inside a phrase. Mozart's rule is aimed at four- and five-voice counterpoint. In this homophonic setting the four-bar unit is the "assignment" each colour carries out. c414 recorded the same result from PDF 146. Keep it.

### 6. Climax chords: power doubling at 61, upper octaves at 79, a controlled release at 81-82 (re-checked; no change)

At 61 b1 every chord tone appears in at least two choirs. The bass is D♭1-D♭2-D♭3 (Cbn, Tuba + Cb, B.cl. + Bsn 2 + B.tbn + timpani). The upper octaves are full. The trombones are marked f inside the ff, and the horns are ff. This follows Tchaikovsky's power doubling (PDF 166-167) and the Mahler rule that the bottom octave may stay bare (PDF 164). At 79 b1 the upper octaves hold B♭ and F only, and D♭ and A♭ sit in the middle (Hn 1·2, Vc, Bsn 1, Hn 3·4). c294 recorded this: B♭ is the appoggiatura, and Cl 2 brings D♭5 on b2. At 81 b2-82 the top falls G6-E♭6-C6-A♭5 in Vln I with Fl 1 at pitch, so the flute never holds a high note alone at pp. This avoids the "dangerous" chord of PDF 162. The last chord at 92 (Vln I C5, Vln II A♭4, Cl 2 E♭4, Vla A♭3/E♭3, Vc A♭2, Cb A♭1) is stacked in mellow registers with strings on top. That is the spacing PDF 162-163 says is easiest to control. Keep all three.
