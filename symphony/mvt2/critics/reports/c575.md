## Findings

### 1. Ob 1 and C.a. enter ff at 79 with no cue, after 16 and 25 bars' rest (applied)

Bars: Ob 1 rests 63-78, C.a. 54-78; both enter ff on the B♭ at 79.

Problem: inside these rests come Largamente (61, C.a. only), allargando (64), Tempo I and the change to 3/4 (65), letter K (71) and poco allargando (78). Six other players with the same entry (Fl 2, Ob 2, B.cl., Cbn, Hn 3, Hn 4) got cues at 77-78 in c455 for this reason. Ob 2 shares Ob 1's score staff and has a cue, Ob 1 does not. The C.a. has the longest rest of any wind player in the movement and no cue at all.

Evidence: MOLA PDF 5: "Logical cues are expected during long periods of rest ... Cues must be audible and obvious to the musician reading the part. Tempo and meter changes must be indicated..." Ex. 4 (PDF 10) shows the "Viol. I" cue just before the entry after a long rest. c455 already answered c315's "cannot miss the climax" reason (five tempo and metre events inside the rest, loud exposed entry).

Fix (sections/aTwo.ily, part-only; the score and MIDI are unchanged). In aTwoOboeOne `R2.*8 |` becomes
```
R2.*6 | \tag #'score { R2.*2 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring #"vlnOneQ" #UP { <>^\markup \tiny "Vln. I" R2.*2 } } |
```
(as Ob 2). In aTwoCorAnglais the same, with `\transposedCueDuring #"vlnOneQ" #UP c` instead of `\cueDuring #"vlnOneQ" #UP`. That puts the cue an octave down: at pitch the transposed violin line stood on four ledger lines (written up to E♭7), and MOLA PDF 5 asks for cues that are obvious to read. The C.a. cue now starts on written D5.

### 2. Fl 1 and Cl 1 enter at 77 with no cue after 12 and 14 bars' rest (applied)

Bars: Fl 1 rests 65-76, Cl 1 63-76; both enter mp< at 77 with the winds' entry into the climax.

Problem: the same tempo and metre events (Tempo I, 3/4, K) fall inside the rest. The entry is soft and in the middle of a crescendo, so the player has to place it by ear.

Evidence: MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 4 (PDF 10), as in finding 1.

Fix (sections/aTwo.ily, part-only): aTwoFluteOne `R2.*2 | g'''4(` becomes `\tag #'score { R2.*2 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring #"vlnOneQ" #UP { <>^\markup \tiny "Vln. I" R2.*2 } } | g'''4(`. In aTwoClOne the `R2.*2` after `\partCombineSoloII` gets the same tagged pair. The cue shows Vln I's A♭4-C5-F5-B♭5-A♭5 at 75-76, the line both instruments join at 77.

### 3. Letter A at 7 lifted above "Adagio" in nine parts (applied; c455 finding 4)

Bars: 7 in Fl 1, Ob 1, C.a., Bn 1, Bn 2, Cbn, Tbn 2, B.tbn, Tuba. In Fl 1, letters A, B, C and D sat on three different heights.

Problem: "Adagio (♩ = 52)" is aligned on the time signature, so it begins left of the 1-6 rest by the width of the time signature. c455's rest widening (`mmr-fit ... 8`) does not cover that, so the word runs past barline 7. LilyPond then lifts A one level, over the word, away from its barline and above the rest number.

Evidence: MOLA Ex. 4 (PDF 10): "C" at the barline, "Etwas bewegter" beside it, "16 D 36" on one level. MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7 (PDF 12): floating marks in extracted parts are the error to proofread for.

Fix (genparts.py, `beside-letter`): the loop carries a `first-tempo` flag, and only the first tempo word (the one with no letter before it) gets extra 12 instead of 8:
```
((and (fit-on) (eq? n 'TempoChangeEvent))
 (let ((wid (mmr-fit (tempo-markup m) (if first-tempo 12 8)))) ...
```
c455 dropped this change because Fl 1's B.cl. cue at 34-36 then broke across two systems with a tie. I kept that cue together with `\noBreak` inside its part-only branch in sections/trans.ily (`R2. \noBreak R2. \noBreak R2.`). I did the same for Fl 1's Vln I cue at 55-56 in sections/bSec.ily (`R1 \noBreak R1`), which the reflow had also split. Result: A sits at barline 7 beside "Adagio" in all nine parts. Fl 1 A-D are on one level. Its first system is now rests only (1-33), and the B.cl. cue opens line 2.

### 4. Viola part: letter G at 53 still floats under bar 49 (not applied; c495 finding 3, open)

Bars: 53, Vla part, line 7 (50-56).

Problem: "[G] a tempo, poco a poco animando" stands over the "53-56 / 4" rest number. Its box is about 9 mm above its own staff and 2.5 mm below bar 49 of line 6. It reads as a mark for bar 49, which is in poco sostenuto. This is the floating marking of MOLA Ex. 7 (PDF 12).

Tests (all reverted):
- (a) The part-only "sotto voce" at 48 moved above the staff, as c495 suggested: "compressing over-full page by 0.9 staff-spaces".
- (b) (a) plus a 2.5-space spacer under bar 49: compressed by 2.5 spaces.

The systems are at minimum spacing, so G always ends up at padding distance under line 6. Any real fix needs page room.

Suggestion: the only cheap source of room is the viola's last system, which carries two rows of text below it at 84-92 ("con sord., div., change bow at will" and "change bow at will"). Putting the 84 text on one line, e.g. `"con sord., div.,"` with "change bow at will" moved to 89 where it is already repeated, may free the 2-3 spaces needed for (b). If not, record G at 53 as a known limit of the one-page viola part (c165, c200, c495).

### 5. Fl 2 and Ob 2 diminuendo at 81 ends on a rest with no target (not applied, minor)

Bars: 81, Fl 2 and Ob 2 parts. `fes4\>` runs into `r2\!`. Neither part shows the target, which the shared score staff gets from player 1's p at 82. MOLA PDF 5 (parts carry what the player needs) and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) favour a complete mark. c558 settled a similar case with niente circles: `\once \override Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t` before the `\>` in the part-only branch would show the release as a fade to nothing. I leave this as optional because c394 placed this hairpin deliberately.
