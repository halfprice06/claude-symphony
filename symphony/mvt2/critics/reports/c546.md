## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The harp part has been checked against this chapter eight times since cycle 26, and every rule in it now holds. The points below record what I checked and why it stands, so the composer can close them.

### 1. Pedal plan, bars 1-83 (follows; no change)

Every harp note fits the setting in force: D♭ C B♭ / E♭ F G A♭ at 7-14 and 71-83; D♭ C B♭ / E♭ F G♭ A♭ at 37-56, with A♮ only for the octave at 40. There are five changes in the movement and none puts two pedals on one foot (PDF 112): D and E (one per foot) in 1-6 from movement I's setting; G♭ in 15-36; A♮ at 39 b4; A♭ at 41 b4; G♮ in 57-70. Each is under a rest of at least one beat at ♩=60 or slower (PDF 113). The A♭ change at 41 b4 comes six beats after the A♮ octave was struck, so the A strings are no longer sounding loudly; the harpist can damp them with the free LH on 41 b2. The part shows the starting setting under 1-6 (PDF 112), and the D♭-major key signature at 37 and A♭-major at 71 show the later settings. After 83 the harp has eight bars of rest plus the pause to set the scherzo's pedals. No change.

### 2. Bars 53-56: one-hand triplet figure (follows; no change)

Ex. IV-8 (PDF 116) spreads the arpeggio between both hands over two octaves; here the RH plays alone inside A♭3-A♭4 at pp-p. The narrower one-hand figure is deliberate: a wider one strikes the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line's pitches (c137, c521). At triplet quavers near ♩=60 (about three notes a second) the RH figure is easy. After c396 and c406 no string is struck twice in succession, which is the case PDF 118 covers (same-string repetition by alternating hands or enharmonic strings). Bar 56's alternations E♭4-G♭4 and B♭3-F4 re-strike each string after one intervening note, about 0.7 seconds later; that is the one-hand oscillation of Ex. IV-15's second example, and it is playable. The harmonic fifths at 54 b2-3 were recorded by c542. No change.

### 3. Bars 82-83: pickup and harmonics (follows; no change)

All four conditions of PDF 116 hold: octave harmonics by L.H.; written A♭3-E♭4, inside C3-A5 and inside a fifth; circles with "(sound 8va)"; accompaniment limited to the Vc/Cb ppp and the harp's own tied A♭1, which is not re-struck (c214). The free RH damps the pickup strings at 83 b1 ("R.H. étouffez", c406), which is the hand-damping of sons étouffés (PDF 117) applied once, at a tempo slow enough for it. The A♭1 tie is in the LH while the LH also plays the harmonics; the tied low string keeps ringing without the hand, so the LH is free. No change.

### 4. Chord sizes and rolls, bars 7-14, 37-44, 71-78 (follows; no change)

PDF 114 allows up to four notes per hand and prefers wide three-note chords. Here no hand plays more than two notes. The LH octaves (A♭1-E♭2, F1-F2, B♭1-B♭2, E♭1-E♭2) give the dark low register of PDF 113 under the horn, and the RH dyads stay off the soloist's notes (protected: c127, c137, c361). Rolls are marked with a wavy line wherever wanted and the 37-44 octaves are plucked solid with the pizzicato basses, as PDF 114-115 describes. No change.

### 5. Timpani tuning, outside the reading but in the lens (follows; no change)

Timpani start in G, A♭, C, E♭ (movement I's tuning), roll A♭2 at 2-3, retune at 5 to A♭ C D♭ E♭ with 46 bars of rest before the next stroke, and play only E♭, D♭, C and A♭ after that.
