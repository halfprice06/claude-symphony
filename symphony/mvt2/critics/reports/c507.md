## Findings

I found nothing in v507 that breaks a principle of this reading and has not already been settled with a reason. I recommend no change to the score. Findings 1-5 record what I checked bar by bar and correct one earlier description. Nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-507/work. It is an unchanged copy of v507; its rebuild is clean (0 warnings, build-warnings.txt empty; analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels). The parts book was not regenerated because no file changed.

**1. Bars 63-64: the strings under the trombone quotation. Follows Ex. 216; no change.**
Sebesky drops the string chords and rests the cellos "while the trombones play their loose jazz phrasing", to avoid clutter (PDF 173). At 63 Tbn 1 plays E3-C4-B3-B♭3-A3 ff. The only string notes are well away from that octave: Vc A2 tremolo, Vla E4/C5, Vln II E5/C6, Vln I E6/A6, Cb A1 pizz. Nothing sits in the trombone's register, and the string chord is marked sf> p so it fades out of the way. This matches the rule. At 64 b3-4, as the quotation closes on F3-E3 p, Vln II (B3-D4, sul G) and Vla (G♯3, sul C) move into the same octave. That is the hand-over to the retransition. c217 recorded it and offered an optional viola rest; 61-65 is protected. No change.

**2. Bars 87-90: muted horns double the divided violas instead of alternating with them. Recorded; correction to c447.**
PDF 166 says a section of horns in lower-middle chords "can be effectively alternated with violas and cellos to provide a welcome change of tone color". At 87-90 the score does not alternate. Hn 3 holds A♭3 with the upper violas, and Hn 4 plays F3-F♭3-E♭3 with the lower violas. The cor anglais doubles the same line an octave higher (F4-F♭4-E♭4). c447 described this as "each group complete within itself" (the Ex. 214 principle). That is not accurate: the horns and violas sound the same two pitches, so it is a doubling, not two complete groups. By Sebesky's weight rule (PDF 164) the two muted horns will cover the four or five muted violas on each note, so the colour heard is horn and cor anglais, with the violas adding little. An alternation is possible within the bars (horns 87-88, violas alone 89-90 after the horns' diminuendo; the horns already end at 90 b2). But the doubling was accepted at c150 on Rimsky-Korsakov's horns-doubled-by-divided-violas model (PDF 113), and "viola divisi with the horns 87-90" is on the do-not-touch list. Sebesky's wording ("can be") is an option, not a rule, so it does not answer that reason. No change; I record the corrected description only.

**3. Bars 61-62 and 79-81: internal resonance in the mixed tutti. Follows.**
Ex. 214's principle (PDF 168) is that horns and strings are each voiced to sound complete within themselves. At 79 the horns alone hold a complete D♭ triad (Hn 1-2 a2 D♭4 on the counter-melody, Hn 3 F4, Hn 4 A♭3), and the strings alone hold theme, counter-melody and bass (B♭6, B♭5, B♭4, D♭4, D♭2). At 61 the brass chord is complete (Tbn 2 A♭3/F3, Hn 2 D♭4, Hn 3-4 F4/A♭3 a2 per c264's layout) and so is the string chord (Vln I D♭6, Vln II A♭4, Vla D♭4/F4, Vc A♭2, Cb D♭2). The bass clarinet is on the chordal root in both places (D♭2 at 61 and 79), as Sebesky suggests for bottom-rich voicings (PDF 168).

**4. Bars 57-61 and 75-79: the builds and the strings' register at the peak. Follows.**
Ex. 215 (PDF 169-170) builds "with each succeeding bar": strings rise into the high register, high reeds come in last. At 57-61 the violins move into octaves (57), the viola triplets become sextuplets (59), the horns and trombones enter at 60, and the violins reach D♭6 at 61 with the flutes on A♭6. At 75-79 the violins stay at pitch until 77, Fl 1 and Cl 1 enter at 77, and the strings reach B♭6/B♭5/B♭4 at 79. In both peaks the top string line is in its high register, single-voiced or in octaves, which is PDF 168's "voiced as simply as possible in the high register". The only divided string chords at the peaks are Vln I's thirds at 62 b3-63 and the violas at 80-81, both settled (c447 #3-4).

**5. No second-violin counter-line under the theme (Ex. 214 bars 11-12). Observation only.**
In Ex. 214 the second violins leave the melody for a descending counter-line, which then trades places with the lead horn. In this score Vln II always doubles Vln I when the violins have a theme (unison at 23-30 and 53-56, octaves at 57-62 and 71-82). The counter-melody is always in the cellos, with horn or bassoon added at the builds. This is the plan's design (the cello counterline movement I lacked), and 23-30, 53-56 and 71-82 are protected. A Vln II counter-line would thin the melody at the one place in each section where the strings carry it. I do not propose it.
