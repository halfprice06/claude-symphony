## Findings

### 1. Bars 45–47: oboe 1 over the cellos at the octave, with nothing doubling either part (applied)

**Problem.** Theme B's head in B♭ minor is in oboe 1 (D♭5–E♭5–D♭5–C5 | B♭4–A♭4–B♭4–D♭5 | C♯5). The cellos play the same line an octave below on the A and D strings. No other instrument doubles either octave. This was c011's cello octave under Vln I. When c041 moved the upper octave to the oboe, the octave became one wind against one string group.

**Evidence.** The editor's note on RK PDF 78 says this exact pairing is "not to be recommended, as the tone quality of the two groups is so widely different", and it names Ob over Vc. The rule on the same page gives the remedy. When only one octave part is doubled, the woodwind takes both octaves and a string group doubles only the lower one. RK PDF 76 uses "a bassoon with the 'cellos" as his model of an equal-power unison in which the strings still predominate. PDF 77 repeats 'Cellos + Fag among the best register matches. In Ex. 91 (PDF 268) Vc + Fag and Vla + Fag carry the line together. Bassoon 1 is free here, and the bassoon staff is already printed in this system because bassoon 2 plays B♭2. The change therefore adds no staff.

**Fix (sections/bSec.ily, bSecBsnOne, the second `R1*8` that covers 45–52):**
```
des'2(\pp es'8 des'8 c'4) | bes4( aes8 bes8 des'2\<) | cis'2.\mp\> r4\! | R1*5 |
```
The dynamics match bassoon 2 on the shared staff: pp at 45, < at 46 beat 3, mp> at 47. There is no pickup at 44, because the bassoon staff is hidden in system 41–44 and a note there would add a staff. The cello and oboe pickups at 44 still lead in. The oboe line, the cellos and the quotation that follows are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 61–62: the lowest octave of the augmented head is the cellos alone, inside the brass (applied)

**Problem.** At G the augmented head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ is in three octaves. Flute 2 (with Vln I from 61 b3) plays A♭5–F6. Vln II, joined by Cl 2 at 61 and Ob 1 at 62, plays A♭4–F5. The cellos play A♭3–F♭4 with nothing doubling them. That octave sits among Hn 2 (F4), Hn 3·4 (A♭4), Tbn 2 (F3) and the bass trombone, all at f/mf, with the timpani rolling f. Meanwhile bassoon 1 plays F3–G♭3–A♭3, a unison with trombone 2 that is also doubled by horn 2 and two octaves up by clarinet 1. So bassoon 1 adds a fourth copy of a harmony line while the theme's lowest octave stands alone.

**Evidence.** On PDF 76 Rimsky-Korsakov says a melody over three or four octaves is hard to balance. Both of his examples give every octave a combined group. PDF 79: strings and brass "can never yield such a perfect blend"; "each can be heard separately". A single string line among sustained brass of the same register is the case that line describes. PDF 76–77: 'Cellos + Fag is a natural unison that strengthens the strings' resonance. The trombone loses nothing it needs: Trombone + Fag (PDF 75) is a softening device, and trombone 2 is already marked one level below the horns.

**Fix (bSecBsnOne, bar 61–62):**
```
\partCombineAutomatic f2--\ff des'2-- | f'2-- fes'2-- | R1*2 |
```
Bassoon 1 keeps F3 on 61 beats 1–2 and joins the cellos from 61 beat 3 (D♭4, then F4–F♭4). My first try put A♭3 on the downbeat, but E♭3 (bar 60) to A♭3 over bassoon 2's A♭2 to D♭3 made parallel fifths with the bass, which the checker reported, so I kept the F. The harmony at 61–62 is unchanged: F3, G♭3 and A♭3 remain in trombone 2. Bassoon 2, the contrabassoon and all brass are untouched, and so is the Hn 3·4 a2 (protected after c086).

### 3. Bars 79–81: the theme's middle octave has two winds on one string group (no change; recorded)

**Problem.** Ob 1 + Cl 1 + Vln II play B♭5 in unison, and RK PDF 76 says the strings "will be overpowered" in such a group. c034 made this octave the heaviest on purpose, so that the theme outweighs the f counter-melody (Adler part 2 PDF 151), and "A′ octaves and release 79–83" is protected. I do not answer that reason here, so I propose nothing. I record it only so a later critic does not raise it again without the c034 context.

### 4. Bars 57–58: horn 1's counter-line has the cellos an octave below it (no change)

Rimsky-Korsakov's horn + cello blend (PDF 79) is a unison. At 57 beat 3 to 58 the cellos are an octave below horn 1, so by PDF 79 each will be heard separately. From 59 bassoon 1 joins the cellos, and that already gives the lower octave a mixed colour. Adding bassoon 1 at 57–58 would reverse c057, which removed it there for economy, and I see no stronger case than c057's. No change.
