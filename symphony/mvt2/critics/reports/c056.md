## What else I studied

I read the whole score (8 pages) at 90 dpi and compared every timpani attack with the harmony sounding at that moment, using a script over score.midi (it lists the pitch classes sounding on each beat while the timpani sound). Every attack is a chord tone. I also checked movement I v180 for the timpani tuning carried into this movement. It uses G2 and A♭2 side by side (recTwo: G–G–A♭ fff), and that matters for F5. I checked the ranges in analysis.txt: every instrument is within range. I re-ran allpairs_par.py: the output is identical before and after my changes, and the changes touch only the timpani part.

## Findings (ranked)

**F1. Bar 82: a single struck note with a diminuendo. Applied.**
Problem: `\after 2 \pp aes,2.\p\>` is one stroke (there is no tremolo) carrying a p→pp hairpin. A struck drum cannot diminish; it only decays.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80 says the part must show "the exact duration of each note … how long a note may ring." Ex. XII-47/48 attach hairpins only to rolls. At 82 b3 everyone except the harp goes to pp, and the harp's harmonics enter alone (the staged release, c019/c028).
Fix (applied): `aes,2\p r4`. One cadential stroke on the tonic, damped on beat 3, where the harmonics begin. A roll (`aes,2.:32\p\>`) would also be playable, but it would cover the harmonics.

**F2. Bars 62–63: the A♭ roll rings into the A minor quotation. Applied.**
Problem: `des2:32 aes,2:32 | R1*2`. The A♭ roll on F♭ major (62 b3–4) runs to the barline with nothing to show when it ends. A timpanist lets the last stroke ring unless told otherwise. Here A♭ = G♯, the leading tone of A minor, sounding under the ff A in trombone 1 at 63 b1.
Evidence: Adler PDF 58 (Strauss on timpani notes left out of the harmony); PDF 80 (show how long a note may ring).
Fix (applied): `des2:32 aes,4:32~ aes,8 r8 | R1*2 |`. The roll ends on a tied stroke at beat 4 and is damped on the rest before 63.

**F3. Roll endings at 3, 60 and 70. Applied.**
Problem: three rolls stop at a barline or rest with no final tied note.
- Bar 3, `aes,2.:32\> | R2.\!`: the diminuendo has no end.
- Bar 60, `aes,2.:32\p\< r4\!`: the crescendo runs into a rest, with no target dynamic, one beat before the ff D♭ at 61.
- Bar 70, `es2:32 r4`: the end is unclear.

Evidence: Adler PDF 80, Ex. XII-47 and XII-48. A roll is written tied into a short final note and then a rest. Bar 52 already does this (`aes,1:32\pp\< | aes,4\p`). This cycle makes the other rolls match it.
Fix (applied):
- intro bar 3: `aes,2:32\>~ aes,8\! r8`
- bSec bar 60: `aes,2.:32\p\<~ aes,8\f r8`. The dominant stroke lands with the poco allargando, and the hand is then free for the D♭ drum.
- retr bar 70: `es2:32~ es8 r8`. The roll ends with the horn and bassoons on beat 3.

**F4. Tuning label and sticks before the first note. Applied.**
Problem: "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭" was printed over the first note in bar 2, and the part never names sticks. The part opens with a ppp roll during the attacca link, where the stick choice decides the sound.
Evidence: Adler PDF 59 and 88 (explain the notation at the start of score and part); PDF 61 and 80 (name the sticks as hard, medium or soft).
Fix (applied, intro bar 1): `<< R2. { s2.^\markup \small \column { "Timp. in G, A♭, C, E♭ (as in movement I)" "soft sticks" } } >> |`. Soft sticks suit the ppp rolls at 2–3, 67–70 and 89–92 and the pp solo at 84. The ff roll at 61 and the f roll at 79 still speak on soft sticks, and there is time to change sticks in the rests at 7–51 if the player prefers.

**F5. "muta G in D♭" assumes a drum layout. Not applied.**
Problem: by Ex. XII-46 the final set A♭2, C3, D♭3, E♭3 fits four drums only one way: A♭2 on the 30" (at the top of its range), C3 and D♭3 on the 28" and 25", and E♭3 on the 23". The instruction "muta G in D♭" works only if movement I put G2 on the 28" and A♭2 on the 30". Movement I's fff G–A♭ alternation is more naturally played with G2 on the 30" and A♭2 on the 28". In that layout, G cannot reach D♭3 on the 30" drum (its top is A2), and two drums must change.
Evidence: Adler PDF 79 (the ranges); PDF 60 (the composer gives the requirement and leaves the equipment to the player).
Fix: in intro bar 5, `R2.^\markup \small "muta in A♭, C, D♭, E♭"`. This states the resulting tuning and lets the timpanist choose the drums. There are three bars of rest, about 10 s, which is enough for two pedal changes (PDF 79).

**F6. Three-stroke tremolo at ♩=52 can be read as measured 32nds. Not applied, optional.**
Problem: at Adagio ♩=52, `:32` written literally is about 7 notes per second, which is slower than a roll. The part does not say the strokes mean an unmeasured roll.
Evidence: Adler PDF 80 gives "tr~~~" as the alternative notation (Ex. XII-47/48) and asks for measured and unmeasured to be distinguished (Ex. XII-49).
Fix: either add `^\markup \small \italic "(roll)"` at the first roll (bar 2), or write the long rolls as `aes,2.\startTrillSpan … \stopTrillSpan`. The first option changes nothing in the layout.

**F7. Coda rolls at ppp: playing spot. Not applied, optional.**
At 67–70 and 89–92, the timpani roll ppp under a horn solo and under the final chord. Adler PDF 80 says the best pianissimo is played nearer the rim.
Fix: `^\markup \small \italic "near the rim"` at retr bar 3 (67) and coda bar 7 (89). This is optional; the soft sticks from F4 already help.
