## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Cellos 44 b4–47: tenor clef for a line that needs two ledger lines at most (applied)
**Problem.** The cellos change to tenor clef on the last beat of 44 and back to bass clef at 48: two clef changes in three and a quarter bars. The line is A♭3–D♭4–E♭4–D♭4–C4–B♭3–A3–B♭3–D♭4–C♯4. Its highest note, E♭4, sits on the second ledger line above the bass stave. The movement is not consistent about this. At 79–80 the cellos play the same E♭4 and D♭4, ff, in bass clef. The parts make it worse: in the cello part the tenor clef comes back at the start of the line at 47 for one dotted half, and the bass clef returns on the next beat.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21: "For performance material, stay in one clef for as long as is practicable, using up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef frequently. This shows the contour of the pitches, which a change of clef would obscure." The example on that page sets a line in one clef with ledger lines ("or") against the same line with two clef changes ("but not"). The 44–47 line is also the octave below Vln I (c011), and in bass clef its contour is visible next to the other strings.
**HISTORY.** "Cello clef changes 44–47" is in the c065 do-not-touch list with no reason given, and c185 recorded only that the changes are well placed (rows 10–12), which is still true. This finding does not dispute the placement. It says no change is needed.
**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecVc`):** remove `\clef tenor` before `aes4(\pp^\markup \italic "sul D"` (44) and `\clef bass` before `cis1\pp` (48). Score and parts keep their layout: 8 pages, cello part on one page.

### 2. Violin I, bar 1: mark "(sul D)" over the C5 tied from movement I (applied)
**Question from movement I's thread.** Its v252 plays the last motto sul D at I 177–182, so the C5 that ties into our bar 1 is on the D string.
**Judgement: mark it, with a dashed line that ends at the end of the note.** A note tied across a barline cannot change string, and a part that starts unmarked after a movement break reads as ordinary playing. Gould PDF 21 says every stave begins by restating what governs it ("Never omit the clef"). PDF 23 says nothing should change during a tied note ("Avoid changing a clef during a tied note"; at a break the information is restated at the new system). Here the "new system" is a new movement and a new part page. Brackets mark it as a reminder, not a new instruction. The dashed line with an end hook shows exactly how far it applies. That matters because the next Vln I entry, the solo at 17 (A5–B♭5), is plainly not sul D, and the section's own "sul D" at 22 is marked separately. An unbounded "sul D" at 1 would have needed an "ord." at 17. This is the same spanner style movement I uses for "sul A" (v251 tTwo.ily).
**Fix (applied, sections/intro.ily, `introVlnOne`):**
```
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "(sul D)"
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.right.text = \markup \draw-line #'(0 . -1)
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.right.padding = #5
c''2.\repeatTie~\ppp^\markup \italic "change bow at will" \startTextSpan | c''2.~ | ... c''2.\> |
R2.\!\stopTextSpan^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord. (not the solo player)" | ...
```
The line ends at the barline of 4. In the Vln I part it stacks under "change bow at will" with no collision. Bars 1–6 are the protected link. No pitch, dynamic or rhythm changes, only this text. **Tell movement I's thread** that movement II now prints "(sul D)" at bar 1, so its v252 marking and ours stay together. If movement I drops sul D, remove this text.

### 3. Bass clarinet 11 b3–12 b1 and double basses 75 b3–76 b1: middle-line D♭3 stemmed down inside an up-stem figure (applied)
**Problem.** Both play the bass figure A♭2–C3–D♭3 | D♭3 (half)–B♭2 at concert pitch on a bass stave, so D♭3 lies on the middle line. The notes around it are stemmed up. The two D♭s get LilyPond's default down-stem, so the figure reads up–up–down | down–up. The harp LH plays the same figure in the same bars (11–12, 75–76), and there the D♭s are up-stemmed. The bass line at 42 b4 and 58 b4 already has `\once \stemUp des` (c095, c185) for the same reason.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27: middle-line notes "continue the stem direction of surrounding stems that are in one direction only". PDF 28: when the direction varies in a bar, keep the direction of the notes in the same beat or half-bar.
**Fix (applied):** aOne.ily `aOneBassCl`: `aes,4( c4 \once \stemUp des4) | \once \stemUp des2( bes,4) |`. aTwo.ily `aTwoCb`: `aes,,4\p\<\downbow c,4 \once \stemUp des,4 | \once \stemUp des,2 bes,,4 |`.

### 4. Horn 1 and clarinet 1, bar 55 b1: the tied B♭4 changes stem direction across the barline (applied)
**Problem.** Since c201 the counter-line holds B♭4 from 54 b3 into 55. In 54 the note has `\once \stemUp`. In 55 the tied continuation takes the default down-stem, and the G♭4 that follows is up-stemmed again. So the one held note shows an up-stem, then a down-stem, then the line goes back up. That happens on both staves (Hn 1·2 and Cl 1·2, solo mode).
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 27 (middle-line note between stems that go one way) and PDF 28. A held note that changes stem side across a tie also looks like two attacks.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily `bSecHnOne` and `bSecClOne`):** `\tweak Stem.direction #UP bes'2 ges'2`. I used a tweak, not `\stemUp`, because the part-combiner can override voice-level stem settings (c095, c185). The c201 rhythm and pitches are unchanged.

### 5. Bassoon 2, bar 58 b4: D♭3 down beside A♭2 up in the same half-bar (applied)
**Problem.** In c205's solo-mode bar (`bes,4 es4 aes,4 des4`) beats 3–4 read A♭2 up, D♭3 down. B.cl. and Cb play the same line in this bar with the D♭ up (the Cb since c095).
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28: keep one direction within a beat or half-bar. In 4/4, beats 3–4 are one half-bar.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily `bSecBsnTwo`):** `bes,4 es4 aes,4 \tweak Stem.direction #UP des4 |`.

### 6. Viola part, bars 47–48: "poco sostenuto" still floats under bar 43 (open item from c235; tested, not applied)
**Problem.** In the part, "poco rit." (47 b3) and "poco sostenuto" (48) overlap horizontally, so they stack above "sotto voce". The top word ends up just under the 41–43 hairpin of the line above. MOLA PDF 8 and Ex. 7 (PDF 12) cover floating markings. Gould PDF 21–23 make the general point that every mark must be read with the music it belongs to.
**Tests in a separate scratch copy (all reverted):**
(a) `-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "sotto voce"` at 48, so the part loses one layer. Result: one page, 30-page book. "poco sostenuto" sits directly on "poco rit." over bars 47–48, about a stave-space lower than now, but still about halfway between the systems.
(b) Widening bar 47 in the part only with `\tag #'part { \once \override Score.BarLine.extra-spacing-width = #'(-N . 0) }`. At N = 7–10 the two words still overlap, because the mark at 48 is aligned on the barline. At N = 12 the viola part goes to two pages.
(c) A part-only `\break` before 48 makes it worse: "poco sostenuto" then hangs under bar 43 at the head of the next line.
**Recommendation.** Option (a) is the only one that keeps one page and improves the placement. It does drop a word that the score keeps, though the pp and the portato already ask for the same thing. I did not apply it. A complete fix needs more vertical room in the viola part, which the one-page rule (c165, c221) does not allow. The composer should record which of the two (one page, or a clean 47–48) wins.
