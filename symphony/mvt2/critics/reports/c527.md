## Findings

### 1. Bar 56: the bass line rises a sixth where the theme B model descends; bring the basses in one bar early (applied)

- **Problem.** Theme B's bass falls in thirds. At 37-40 the basses play D♭2-B♭1 | G♭1, E♭2 | E♭2-C2 | A1-B♭1. They take their one upward leap early (G♭1-E♭2 at 38-39, where E1 is their floor), and the chain ends falling a minor third C2-A1 into the leading-tone resolution A1-B♭1. At 53-56 the basses rest (c164), and the cellos carry the bass alone: D♭3-C3 | B♭2-G♭2 | E♭2-C2 | A2-B♭2. C2 is the cellos' lowest note, so the leap moves to the last link: a rising major sixth C2-A2 on the leading tone, in the bar that leads into the 57 tutti build. c522 recorded this and named the fix, but did not apply it because the basses' return at 57 belonged to c164's build order.
- **Evidence from the reading.** Ex. V-16 (Haydn 103, PDF 139): "the simple addition of the cellos and double basses ... prepares so well for the tutti version that is to follow". Adler places the added basses before the tutti, and c164 cited this same example. PDF 166: orchestration "must clarify the form and support the tonal flow". Here the missing 16-foot octave changes the tonal flow, because the leading tone A comes in a different register from the model at 40. The score already has this pattern at 21: the basses rest under the dialogue at 15-20 and come in for the approach to the half cadence (c074, Adler PDF 130-131, Ex. V-6).
- **Answer to c164's reason.** c164 removed the basses because at 53-56 "the bottom of the texture is as heavy in the light statement as in the build". With this fix, 53-55 stay without basses, so three of the four bars keep c164's light bottom. The basses come in p< under the cellos' mf for two half notes, on the leading-tone chord (vii°7/vi) and its resolution. The step at 57 is still there: the basses reach mf, the octaves are added and the winds come in. Pitch classes and harmony are unchanged; this is a doubling an octave below the cellos.
