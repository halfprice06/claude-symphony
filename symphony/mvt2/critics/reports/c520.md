## Findings

The reading supports no change to notes, dynamics or scoring. Findings 1-3 are new evidence for the cyclic design, which I record so later critics need not repeat the checks. Finding 4 confirms the symphony-level plan on v520. Finding 5 is an optional text update. Nothing is applied to the score.

### 1. Both quotations of movement I are in the movement's only remote keys, and what follows each one explains its key (no change)

**Reading.**
- PDF 443 §2: keys are related or remote by degree, and long-lasting keys are related ones.
- PDF 449, Ex. 26-8: Mozart quotes his opening idea in a key that surprises. "Subsequent events clarify the meaning" of that key: it is a step toward V, not a tonic.

**Score.** Movement II has two areas that are remote from A♭ (more than one accidental from it and not reached by simple mixture): F♯ minor at 47-50 and A minor at 63-64. Movement I's material is quoted only in these two places, and each key is explained by what comes next:
- **F♯ minor (S head, C.a., 47 b4-50).** F♯ minor is G♭ minor, the iv of D♭ by mixture. It moves to the A♭ pedal at 51-52 (score.midi at 51 b1: A♭1 C3 A♭4 E♭5), which is V of D♭, and then to D♭ at 53. The key acts as a predominant before a dominant.
- **A minor (P head, Tbn 1, 63-64).** A minor is ♭ii of A♭. It goes to movement I's stop chord over E at 64 b4 (E2 B2 F3 A♭3 D4) and then to E♭7 at 65 (E♭2 B♭2 G3 D♭4). This is IV-side harmony reaching V through an augmented sixth, the K. 488 route of Ex. 26-14 (PDF 453).

So each quotation sounds "foreign" for a reason the listener can hear (the key is remote), and is then absorbed into the movement's own I–IV–V–I as a predominant. This also matches movement I, whose coda S head runs over N6 → V (I 173; mvt2.md). In both movements the S quotation stands on the subdominant side just before a dominant. The single statements and their keys are protected (standing list). No change.

### 2. C major after bar 1 is heard only as V of F minor, the meaning Ex. 26-8 gives it (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 449: Mozart's C major "does not function as a tonic, but rather as V of IV (F minor)".

**Score.**
- Bar 1 is movement I's C-major tonic chord. It is not resolved as a tonic: at bar 2 it becomes III♯ of A♭ by common tone. PDF 443 explains why that relation is near, through mixture.
- After bar 1, the only C-major sonority with any weight is at 20 b3: C3 B♭3 E4 G4 (score.midi), V7 of F minor, which moves deceptively to D♭ at 21. This is exactly Mozart's "V of F minor".
- C major is never a key or a resting chord in movement II. That keeps the cyclic rule that C-major material is reserved for the finale (BRIEF; cyclic-notes §3). The reading gives a textbook reason for the rule: a C major that keeps acting as a dominant leaves the symphony's real C-major tonic to the finale.
- Do not add a C-major resting chord anywhere in II. No change.

### 3. The symphony's leading tone B→C is kept for the finale (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 449-451 §8, §10 (Ex. 26-8, 26-10): before the return of the tonic, "the leading tone must appear". Mozart places it "prominently in the bass" just before the recapitulation. Where the leading tone appears is a large-scale decision.

**Score.** I listed every semitone rise from B (or C♭) to C in score.midi:
- **50→51, cellos B2→C3.** B is 4̂ of F♯ minor, and C3 is the third of the A♭ chord (V of D♭), so C here is 7̂ of D♭.
- **78 b3, Bsn 1 and cellos C♭4→C4.** This undoes the mixture of the F♭ chord over the E♭ bass.

Neither is a leading tone moving to a C tonic. Every B♮/C♭ that belongs to movement I's stop chord falls (33, 64→65), as mvt2.md records. Movement II therefore never uses up the B→C resolution that the finale makes at its arrivals (finale.md, 219-223). This is Ex. 26-8's principle applied across four movements. Keep it. No change.

### 4. The symphony's key plan and both joins hold on v520 (no change)

- **Key plan.** C–A♭ is a step to a related key (PDF 443). A♭–E (= F♭) is remote, and movement II foreshadows it from inside with F♭→E♭ at 5, 21, 62-65, 78, 80 and 88, as Ex. 26-7 foreshadows its C minor (PDF 448; record c243, c260). II's last chord is a plain A♭ triad, which the finale turns into the German sixth that leads to V of C. That is §15's VI as upper neighbour to V (PDF 456), stretched over two movements.
- **Join from movement I.** The five tied parts at bar 1 (Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3, Hn 3 C4, ppp) match mvt1.md's ending, which is unchanged since v421.
