# Critic 242: harmony and voice leading (v242)

Lens 2 (harmony and voice leading), third series. Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 31 "Chromatic Voice-Leading Techniques", PDF 551–583.

I read every text page from PDF 551 to 583 in full. I rendered all 33 pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one, two pages per image, because the OCR of notation and figures is unreliable. All examples below were checked against the images. Printed page = PDF + 10 up to PDF 573 (printed 583). From PDF 574 it is PDF + 11, because the scan skips printed 584.

Critic 237 used this unit for texture. It covered contrary chromatic motion, the voice exchange into the French sixth at 21, and the slides at 64→65 and 88→89. I do not repeat those points. As the composer asked, this report covers the unit's other techniques: chromatic passing chords, enharmonic and common-tone connections, chromatic sequences and equal divisions.

## Findings

Every place where the unit's other techniques could apply already does what the reading teaches, or is protected for a reason the reading does not answer. I recommend no change and applied nothing in a scratch copy. The findings below record the checks, so a later cycle does not repeat them. They are ranked by how much each bears on the movement's harmonic quality.

### 1. The enharmonic hinges are placed and resolved as the reading teaches (no change)

**Bars:** 46→47, 50→51, 62→63, 64→65.

**Check.** A&S give two rules:
- The respelling should show where each tone goes (PDF 559–560: Beethoven writes D♯ for E♭ "to point to the coming dominant").
- In keys with many flats, the notation may favour ease of reading (PDF 573, Ex. 31-27b).

The score follows both.

- **46→47 and 50→51.** The switch sits at the key-signature change, as c023 confirmed. Respelling the G♭ chord at 46 b3 as F♯ major would put F♯, A♯ and C♯ into a five-flat signature. That is the case Ex. 31-27b allows to stay as it is.
- **62→63.** F♭ major (♭VI of A♭) stays in flats in the D♭ signature. It is respelled as E only when it acts as V of A minor at 63. This is the ♭6 = ♯5 respelling of PDF 572–573, notated for ease of reading.
- **64 b3.** The chord is G♯–B–D–F over D (4̂ of A minor). It is Ex. 31-15 exactly: a °4/3 on 4̂ standing for iiø6/5, with the 5th displaced by its lower neighbour (PDF 561–562). The c083 change (Vln II B3) completed it.
- **64 b4.** E7 is reinterpreted as F♭–A♭–C♭–D, the German sixth of A♭, and moves to E♭7. This is the move PDF 576 describes: "if the final seventh chord is reinterpreted as an augmented sixth, the passage can lead to a new … key area". It is also the single-step form of Ex. 31-9, where the first chord "is heard as" a triad and then becomes the augmented sixth (PDF 557).

**Fix:** none.

### 2. 50 b3–4 → 51: the movement's best chromatic connection; keep it as it is (no change)

**Bars:** 50–51 (pivot protected, c083 and c193).

**What the bars do.** The chord at 50 b4 is Bm7 (B–D–F♯–A). It reaches A♭7 at 51 as follows:
- one enharmonic common tone, F♯ = G♭ (C.a. and Vln II);
- two voices rise a semitone: Vla B3–C4 and Vln II D5–E♭5;
- one voice falls a semitone: C.a. A4–A♭4.

In D♭, Bm7 is C♭ minor 7, a minor ♭vii7. The succession is the ♭VII7–V7 relation that Ex. 31-22 elaborates with an m7–M10 exchange (PDF 566).

**Why not add the exchange.** The exchange would need these changes:
- the bass filled B–B♭–A–A♭;
- the Vln II D5 changed to D♯ (B7), so that the inner voices hold E♭ and G♭ as A&S require (PDF 564, "the inner voices remain stationary").

That would turn the minor iv7 into a major chord, remove the plaintive minor colour of the quotation's cadence, and change a protected pivot. The direct move already gives three semitone steps and one common tone, which is as close as voice leading gets.

**Fix:** none.

### 3. 39 b1–40 and 55–56: minor-third bass with shared-tone seventh chords (no change)

**What the bars do.** The bass falls E♭–C–A and then rises to B♭. Over it:
- E♭m7 (ii7) → Cø7 → A°7 → B♭ minor (vi).

The three seventh chords share C, E♭ and G♭ (except the first, which lacks C). Only B♭→A and the bass move. This is §17 (PDF 575–576): a minor-third division "works more effectively within the expansion of a seventh chord", and the "fleeting °7s … form the connective threads". The A°7 resolves normally into B♭ minor:
- Vla C4–D♭4;
- Vln II E♭4–D♭4 and G♭4–F4.

**One detail.** The 7th of Cø7 (Vla B♭3) moves up to C4 while the bass takes the A, so the resolution is transferred to another voice. Exercise 3 labels exactly this move "transferred resolution" (PDF 582).

At 39 b3 the figure in the violas (alto staff) is too light for anyone to hear a rising 7th. It also protects the protected vii°7/vi at 40.

**Fix:** none.

### 4. The motto tail is never set against a contrary chromatic bass (no change; a rule for later cycles)

**Bars:** 8, 24, 33, 68, 72, 85–88.

**Check.** If the tail E♭–D–D♭–C were set note against note over a rising chromatic bass, it would form the M6–m10 exchange. A&S call that "scarcely possible, because of the poor sonorities produced". It needs "some oblique motion and, sometimes, omitting a chromatic step" (PDF 565, Ex. 31-21a–b).

The movement sets the tail in four ways, and none of them is that one:
- **8, 24, 72:** descending fifths, vi7 – V7/V – V7.
- **33:** oblique over the A♭ pedal, with the common-tone °7 D–F–A♭–C♭. C.a. C–C♭–B♭ is spelled in its direction of motion (PDF 560).
- **68:** oblique over the E♭ pedal, with a passing 6/4.
- **85–88:** oblique over the A♭ pedal (Dø7 at 86), then inside an expanded IV: IV6 → iv at 87–88, Ex. 31-21b's "motion within an expanded IV".

The settings at 33 and 86 differ on purpose: C♭ at 33 (°7), C♮ at 86 (ø7, keeping Vln I's closing C5).

**Recommendation.** A later cycle should not set the tail over a contrary chromatic bass.

**Fix:** none.

### 5. Chromatic sequences and equal division: absent inside the movement, present across the symphony (no change; for the coherence file)

**Inside the movement.** There are no chromatic sequences: no 7–6 chains, no 5–6 chains, no augmented-sixth chains, no °7 or augmented-triad chains. A&S call equal-division passages "one relatively minor aspect", and say that their predominance "would weaken the gravitational attraction of the tonic" (PDF 572). The movement's chromaticism is instead:
- mixture (♭6, iv);
- one Neapolitan (49 b3);
- one ♮4–♯4 (59);
- augmented sixths at the two phrase-ends (21, 64);
- one semitone slide (64→65).

This keeps A♭ strong for the plagal close.

**Across the symphony.** The cycle of keys is an equal division into major thirds:
- C (movement I's last chord) – A♭ (II) – E (III) – C (IV).

It descends and uses major tonics. A&S call this "the most important equal division" (PDF 572–573), the one most closely related to diatonic procedure.

**Inside this movement,** the division shows only as ♭6 (F♭). A&S say ♭6 is the only foreign bass tone, and that it usually also appears as an upper neighbour of 5 and resolves to it (PDF 572). The score does this six times, each time resolving to E♭:
- 5 (Cl 2 E♭–F♭–E♭);
- 21 (French-sixth bass);
- 62–65 (F♭ = E → E♭);
- 78 (♭VI);
- 80 (iiø7);
- 88 (iv).

The scherzo's opening G♯ (= this movement's last A♭) and its key of E continue the same cycle.

**Fix:** none. The composer may want to record the major-third cycle in coherence/mvt2.md as a stated reason for the protected F♭/E thread. Nothing at the ending, in the themes or in the quotations changes.

### 6. Bar 48: four beats of F♯ minor under the held A4 (recorded; no change recommended)

**What the bar does.** Under the C.a.'s held A4 (the quotation), only Vln II moves (F♯4 → F♯5) and the violas repeat A3. Exercise 4 asks for a middle voice in complementary rhythm (PDF 583).

**What I tested on paper, and why each fails:**
- **A 5–6 neighbour in the cellos (C♯3–D3), giving VI6 at 48 b3.** It sounds the D a bar before the C.a.'s expressive D5 appoggiatura at 49 and weakens it.
- **A common-tone °7 over F♯ (B♯–D♯–F♯–A).** It needs two new moving voices under a pp quotation, and a D♯ that clashes with the D at 49.
- **A chromatic descent in Vln II from F♯5.** It runs into the N6's G5 at 49 b3.

The bar is the quotation's held note. Stillness there is the same choice c237 made for 51–52.

**Fix:** none.

### 7. ♮4–♯4 is used once, at the build (59); keep the plain cadences at 12, 28, 43 and 76 (no change)

**Check.** A&S call the °7 on ♯4 after IV on ♮4 "one of the most important intensifications of V" (PDF 561, Ex. 31-14). The movement uses it once:
- **59:** G♭ (IV) → E♭7/G → G–B♭–D♭–F♭ (vii°7/V) → V. The F♭ in Vc and Bsn 1 falls to E♭.

Every other cadence approach is diatonic:
- 12, 28 and 76: ii7 → V7;
- 43: IV – IV6 – V.

I checked whether a D♮ passing bass at 12 b3 (A♭ major: IV – vii°7/V – V) would help. It would clash with Bsn 1's D♭4, need a harp pedal change inside the protected 7–14, and use up the effect that 59 saves for the climax.

Keeping ♯4 for one place matches the reading's view of it as an intensification.

**Fix:** none.
