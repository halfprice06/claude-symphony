## Findings

### 1. Violins II "sul G" at 70-74 goes far past the fourth position (applied)
Bars 70 b3-74. Vln II plays the theme's lower octave sul G: E♭4-F4 | A♭4-C5-E♭5 | E♭5-D5-D♭5 | C5-B♭4-A♭4 | G4. On the G string C5 is an 11th and E♭5 a 13th above the open string, around the seventh position. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26 sets the limit on the three lower strings at "the one in the fourth position, either the octave note or the ninth of the open string". The same line at the same pitch is marked "sul D" at 22 (Vln I and II). On the D string E♭5 is a minor ninth, inside the limit.
Answering c031: Adler part 1 PDF 74 says the high G string "becomes very intense and stringent", and his Ex. III-5 (Tchaikovsky 5/ii) is f and sf. Here the reprise begins p under Vln I, and the crescendo only starts at 75, so that colour is wrong for the dynamic. It also makes the A′ lower octave sound different from the identical line at 22-26 for no stated reason.
Fix (retr.ily): `r2 es'8(\p^\markup \italic "sul D" f'8 |`. The "ord." at 75 stays.

### 2. Harp D♭1 at bar 9: a hand retune for one extreme note (applied)
Bar 9 LH plays `des,,4` (D♭1), the second-lowest harp string. The two lowest strings have no pedals, so the harpist must retune that string during 1-6 (text at bars 1 and 7). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46 limits the harp to its central octaves: "the extreme notes in both compasses may be employed in special circumstances, and for doubling in octaves". This is a single pp quarter, not an octave doubling, and it is the only D♭1 in the movement. The bass clarinet already has D♭2 on that beat. Playing D♭2 keeps the harmony and the harp's bass motion (A♭ octave to D♭, as in the bass clarinet). It removes the scordatura and both retuning texts, which closes the open item "harp retuning text at 1 and 7".
Fix (aOne.ily): `<aes,, aes,>2\arpeggio des,4 |`; at 7 keep only the pedal diagram (`-\tag #'score ^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"^-^|^--^"`) and drop the part-only text; intro.ily `introHarpL = { R2.*6 | }`. This revises the cycle-26 "D♭1 scordatura note". c049's reason (no compound fifths with the horn's B♭3-A♭3) still holds: A♭2 to D♭2 against B♭3 to A♭3 moves from a 9th to a 12th, not fifth to fifth.

### 3. Harp f under the ff tutti at 79-80 cannot be heard (applied)
Bars 79-80. The harp plays arpeggiated D♭ chords, f, under Fl, Ob, Cl, B.cl., Bsn, Cbsn and strings ff, plus four horns f. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47: "At least three, if not four harps in unison are necessary, if they are to be heard against a full orchestra playing forte", and the harp "is never a very powerful instrument, and the orchestrator should treat it with respect". The chords only double the strings' and horns' notes. Resting for two bars lets the harp come back as the tutti thins out, and gives the harpist time before the harmonics at 82.
Fix (aTwo.ily): RH `R2.*2 | r4 <g bes des'>2\arpeggio | ...`; LH `R2.*2 | <es,, bes,,>2.\mf\>\arpeggio | <aes,, es,>2.\p\arpeggio |`. The 8 bassa line continues unchanged. No harmony changes, so the protected A′ climax keeps its pitches.

### 4. Brass balance at 61-62: horns f against trombones and tuba f (applied)
Bars 61-62. Horns 2, 3 and 4 are f (c054: brass harmony f under the ff theme). Trombone 2, bass trombone and tuba are also f. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41: "the marks of expression in the horns should be one degree stronger than in the rest of the brass … to obtain a proper balance in forte passages, two horns are needed to one trumpet or one trombone." At equal markings the low brass will cover the horns' top line (Hn 3 A♭4-B♭4-C♭5) and Hn 2's F4-G♭4-A♭4, which lies an octave above trombone 2. Bar 60 already follows the rule (Hn 4 p< over low brass pp<). Keeping horns at f and lowering the low brass one degree respects c054 and Rimsky together. The bass is already ff in Cb, Vc, Cbsn, Bsn 2 and B.cl.
Fix (bSec.ily): Tbn 2 `f1--\mf`, bass trombone `des1--\mf`, tuba `des,1--\mf`. The bass trombone's f at 63 under the quotation stays. Trombone 1 rests 61-62, so the shared staff has one dynamic.

### 5. Horn 1 solo 6-14 has no marked breath (applied)
Bars 6 b3-14: 25 beats, about 29 s at ♩=52. The only break is the slur break at 10 b3 (`g2) es8(`), which has no time in it. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37: "Wood-wind players cannot manage extremely long sustained passages … care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest", and PDF 44: the remarks on breathing "apply with equal force to the brass". Bassoon 1 and the bass clarinet already breathe at 10 b3 (c036). Marking the horn's breath there puts the whole accompaniment and the solo in one breathing place, and it prints in the horn part.
Fix (aOne.ily, Hn 1): `c'4.\! bes8 aes4 | g2) \breathe es8( f8 |`. Pitches and rhythm of the protected horn theme are unchanged.

### 6. Vln I leaps F6 to B♭6 at 76 (not applied: protected)
Bar 76 b2. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 26 allows notes above about A6 only in long values, tremolo, slow melody or scales, and says "skips should always be avoided". At 58 and 79 B♭6 is reached by step or by the grace-note scale, but at 76 it comes by a fourth from a tied F6, in a quarter note at mp<. "B♭6 at pitch 75-81" is on the do-not-touch list, so I suggest only a bowing that makes the shift safer: the shift happens inside one bow, as an expressive portamento, instead of on a bow change. Fix (aTwo.ily, Vln I): `aes''4. c'''8) f'''4~ | f'''4( bes'''4 aes'''4) |`.

### 7. Harp LH low dyads change every beat at 8 and 72 (not applied)
Bars 8 and 72. LH plays F1-C2, B♭1-F2, E♭1-B♭1 on successive beats, 8 bassa, pp. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46: "In the middle and lower octaves the resonance of the strings is slightly prolonged … in quick modulations this method [damping] is not feasible, and the mixture of one chord with another produces a discordant effect." F1 and C2 keep ringing under the E♭ dominant. A lighter version keeps the bass motion and leaves fewer low strings ringing: `<f,, c,>4\arpeggio bes,,4 es,,4 |` at 8 and at 72. The bass clarinet, bassoons and basses carry the fifths.

### 8. Clarinet 2's last hold, 89-92 (no change proposed)
Bars 89-92. E♭4 runs for about 19 s at the rit. tempi, plus the fermata, ppp morendo, with the breath after 88. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 36 notes that the clarinet "can reduce volume of tone to a mere breath", and PDF 37 asks for rests. At ppp this is within a professional's reach, and the close 87-92 is protected, so I am only recording it. If a player reports trouble, Cl 2 can end at 91 b3 with `es'2~\> es'4\!` and leave 92 to the violas' E♭3.
