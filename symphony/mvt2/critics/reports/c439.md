## What else I studied

Score: v439 sections/*.ily, global.ily, score.pdf (all 8 pages at 75 dpi), analysis.txt, critique.md. History: HISTORY.md (all entries from 380 on, plus searches for every item I considered). For the 10-cycle comparison I diffed v429 against v439 (global.ily, score.ly and every section file) and compared their analysis.txt files.

Master work: Brahms, Piano Concerto No. 2, iii Andante (B flat). There is no local score, so I worked from memory of the published score and checked only the large-scale points on the web. The movement opens with an eight-bar cello solo, and the phrase then passes to the violins. Late in the movement the cello comes back in F sharp major, "in the wrong key", and the music is steered back to B flat (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Concerto_No._2_(Brahms); https://johnstone-music.com/orchestral-principal-cello-solos-01-brahms-piano-concerto-no-2-in-bb-major-op-83-3rd-mt/?lang=en). I could not check the details I remember, such as the Più adagio for two clarinets in F sharp. No finding rests on them.

The comparison, point by point:

- Opening. Brahms gives the tune to one warm solo voice, low and soft, with no violins. This score does the same with horn 1 over bassoons, bass clarinet and harp at 7-14.
- Hand-over. In Brahms the violins take the phrase over. Here the oboe and solo violin answer at 15-22, and both violin sections take the theme at 23-30. This matches Rimsky's Ex. 284 (PDF 457): a solo string senza sord. over muted strings.
- Remote key. Brahms's quietest, most remote place is an enharmonic area (F sharp against B flat), given to soft solo winds. Here the remote area is F sharp minor at 47-52 with the cor anglais quotation over held strings. This is the same idea in the same colour family.
- Return of the opening colour. Brahms brings the opening solo colour back near the end. Here the horn returns alone at 67-70 and the harp brings back the head at 83, so the opening colours come back.
- Climaxes. Brahms keeps his climax within the concerto's limits. This symphony has two ff peaks (61-62, 79-81). Each is prepared by the book's order of entry (row 10, row 20) and released in reverse.

A conductor reading the score as designed would accept it as the work of a skilled late-Romantic composer. The main tune does not reach Brahms's long-breathed melody: theme A works in two-bar units where Brahms's cello theme spans eight bars without a break. That is a known, accepted limit of theme A (c401). It is not something the reading lets me fix.

In the score as printed in v436-v439, however, a conductor would stop the rehearsal at bar 84 (Finding 1).

Changes since v429: Vln II text at 22 and 70; violins' mp moved to the 74 b3 pickup; C.a. slur shape at 36; string-line ends at 51-52 and 62; Vc 28 A flat3-F3-D flat4; timpani "damp" at 86; letter L moved at 83. Each one is fine on its own merits except the last, which caused Finding 1. Nothing else got worse.

## Findings

### 1. Violas 84-92: two bars lost inside a comment since v436, so the part runs two bars early (worse than v429). Applied.

**Bars:** 84-92, violas (and the viola page of the parts book).

**What is wrong:** c435 moved letter L in coda.ily line 35 and wrote its explanation before the rest of the line's music. The text `r2 <c' es'>4~\ppp^\markup \italic "div., change bow at will" | <c' es'>2. |` now sits inside the `% c435:` comment. The violas therefore lose their entry at 84 b3-85, and everything after it plays two bars early:
- 84: the violas sound `<c' f'>` (meant for 86) under the timpani's E flat-C-A flat answer.
- 85-86: `<f aes>` and `<fes aes>` (meant for 87-88) sound over the A flat pedal. At 86, clarinet 1's D5, the sharp-4 of the motto tail, sounds against the violas' F flat3. That is a diminished third, D against F flat, which nobody wrote.
- 87: the violas hold E flat3 while horn 4 and the bass have F, a second in one octave.
- 88: E flat3 against horn 4's F flat3, a minor second.
- 90: the violas end with their ppp fermata, and 91-92 are empty in the viola staff.

This breaks c194's design (violas doubling the muted horns, Rimsky PDF 113) and c199/c223's held E flat3/A flat3 to the end. The build cannot catch it: the coda is the last section, so a short part gives no bar-check warning.

**Evidence:**
- v429 has the line correct; v439 does not (diff of sections/coda.ily).
- analysis.txt: violas 296 notes in v429, 294 in v439; average voices sounding at 89-92 fall from 9.0 to 8.0. v439/score.pdf page 8 shows the viola fermata over bar 90.
- Reading: Rimsky PDF 139-140 (thin the harmony gradually, with the remaining parts held, as the line grows softer) and PDF 168 (an upper part over a low bass without stray intervals). The planned coda follows both; the printed one does neither.
- Rimsky PDF 132-133: a non-harmonic note against a basis that sounds a clashing note in the same register is the worst case, and at 86-88 that is exactly what results.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-439/work/sections/coda.ily, line 35):**
```
  <>\strMark "L" \markup \concat { \hspace #1 "a tempo" } R2. | r2 <c' es'>4~\ppp^\markup \italic "div., change bow at will" | <c' es'>2. |  % c439: ... restored  % c435: ...  % c124: ...
```
The music goes back before the comments. Letter L stays where c435 put it, because the `<>\strMark` on the first column is unchanged. In the rebuilt score.pdf, page 7 shows the violas entering ppp at 84 b3, and page 8 shows them holding to the fermata at 92.

For the other threads: this restores movement II's ending to what coherence/mvt2.md already describes (violas E flat3/A flat3 to the end). It does not change the ending. Only the v436-v439 printouts were wrong.

### 2. Guard the last section's length with a bar-number check. Applied for the violas.

**Bars:** 92 (end of every coda staff).

**What is wrong:** a part that is short in the last section produces no warning, and Finding 1 lasted four cycles because of that.

**Evidence:** a test file (`{ \time 3/4 c2. | c2. | c2. | \barNumberCheck #6 }`) gives "warning: Bar number is 4; expected 6", which build.sh writes to build-warnings.txt.

**Fix:** end each coda variable with `\barNumberCheck #93`. For example, codaVla now ends with `<es aes>2.\ppp\fermata | \barNumberCheck #93`. I added it only to the violas, and the score and parts book build without warnings. The composer may add it to the other coda staves.
