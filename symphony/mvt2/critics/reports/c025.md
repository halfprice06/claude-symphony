## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Horns 1·2, bars 57–60: horn 2's rests printed under horn 1's line (applied)
**Problem.** Horn 2 is tacet 57–60 (cycle 24 moved its first entry to 61). The part-combiner did not switch to solo here and printed horn 2's whole-bar rests on a ledger line below the stave in all four bars. They sit under horn 1's half notes, so it looks like a divisi with one part resting. There is also no "1." label on the new page.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 51: "Except in strict contrapuntal writing, parts share a rest wherever possible." Rests outside the stave are only for a second part that is actually notated (PDF 50–51). Here the second player has nothing, so the staff should read as one solo line.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily, bSecHnOne):**
```
\partCombineSoloI aes'2(\mp\<^\markup \bold "1." ges'2 | f'4 es'2 f'4 | ges'2\f es'2 | es'4\< f'4 ges'4 f'4) |
\partCombineAutomatic aes'1\ff | bes'2 ces''2 | R1*2 |
```
After rebuilding, 57–60 show only horn 1, labelled "1.", and 61 combines normally. MIDI pitches are unchanged; allpairs_par.py only renames horn 1's track label from "one" to "solo".

### 2. Harp LH, bars 7–14 and 71–82: 3–5 ledger lines below the bass staff (applied; closes the item open since cycle 5)
**Problem.** The left hand sits entirely below the staff: A♭1 (3 ledger lines), F1 and E♭1 (4), D♭1 at 9 (5). Each bar has a rolled chord with its notes stacked on ledger lines. This is the hardest notation on page 1 to read, and it spreads the harp system.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 47: "Keyboard and harp music may make more extensive use of octave signs than other instruments." PDF 46: use "at least three ledger lines before" an octave sign, and here the line goes past three. PDF 47–48: keep a phrase at one octave and change between phrases, so one sign covers each 8-bar (then 12-bar) phrase. PDF 42: the plain italic "8" is the recommended bassa form. LilyPond's default "8vb" is not among Gould's forms.
**Fix (applied):** aOne.ily, start of `aOneHarpL`:
```
\set Staff.ottavationMarkups = #ottavation-numbers \ottava #-1
<aes,, es,>2.\pp\arpeggio^\markup ...   ... <aes,, es,>2.\arpeggio \ottava #0 |
```
aTwo.ily, `aTwoHarpL`: the same `\set ... \ottava #-1` before bar 71, and `\ottava #0` after the A♭ chord at 82. The written notes now sit in or just under the staff (at most one ledger line). The line continues across the half rest at 78 (PDF 45), restates "8" at the system breaks at 75 and 79 (PDF 44), and runs below the staff with the dynamics kept above (PDF 43). The harp is not a shared staff, so the part-combine/ottava rule does not apply. The LilyPond bracket ends at the barline of 14 and 82, not right after the notehead as PDF 44 asks, because no other staff strikes after beat 1 in those bars. I accept this.

### 3. Violins I and Flute 1, B♭6 at 58, 76, 79 (and grace run at 78): no change; close the item
**Problem raised earlier (cycles 5, 15–24):** should B♭6 be put under 8va?
**Evidence.** B♭6 sits on the fifth ledger line. Gould PDF 47: "Music for woodwind and strings can use up to five ledger lines before moving into an octave transposition. Octave transpositions are unhelpful, as a player identifies a written pitch with a given fingering." The same page says a full score not played from may use 8va to save space, but "these octave signs should not be transferred to a playing part". PDF 47–48 asks that a phrase stay at one octave. The A′ line 75–81 climbs through F6–A♭6–B♭6 within one slur, and an 8va over only the peak would split the line in the middle (the "incorrect" example on PDF 47). The page layout has room for the ledger lines (page 7 is not crowded above Vln I).
**Fix:** none. Keep loco. Please record this as closed so later cycles do not reopen it.

### 4. Harp LH bar 83 after the 8 bassa line (not applied, optional)
**Problem.** Bar 82 ends the 8 bassa line on written A♭2. Bar 83 has the loco A♭1 (3 ledger lines) under the harp's theme head. A player reading the part could carry the octave on.
**Evidence.** PDF 48: loco is used "when the same written (or a neighbouring) pitch is immediately repeated after the octave transposition" and is "unnecessary" when the pitch difference is significant. Here the written pitch drops an octave, so the rule does not require loco. However, the sign ends on a note an octave above the next one.
**Fix if wanted:** coda.ily, `codaHarpL`: `aes,,2.^\markup \italic "(loco)" |`. Alternatively, extend the line through 83 by moving `\ottava #0` from aTwo bar 82 to after `aes,,2.` in coda bar 83. I did not apply either; the current notation is correct as it stands.

### 5. Timpani tuning label printed at bar 2, not before the first bar (not applied)
**Problem.** "Timp. in A♭, C, D♭, E♭" sits above bar 2, where the roll starts. Tuning indications belong at the very start of the part, before the first bar, so the player tunes before the movement begins. This movement is attacca from movement I, so that matters here.
**Evidence.** This is outside the assigned reading. It follows ordinary MOLA part-preparation practice; the relevant Gould chapters are not in this excerpt. Gould PDF 43's rule that the instruction stays outside other notation is met.
**Fix:** intro.ily, `introTimp`: move the markup onto the first bar's rest, e.g. `R2.^\markup \small "Timp. in A♭, C, D♭, E♭"` (or `\once \override MultiMeasureRestText.self-alignment-X = #LEFT`), and remove it from bar 2.

### 6. Hn 1·2 bar 67: "solo" and "1." both printed (not applied)
**Problem.** Bar 67 shows italic "solo" and bold "1." stacked. In a shared staff "1." already says which player. Elsewhere this score uses "1." alone (53, 57 after fix) or "solo, dolce espr." (6).
**Fix:** retr.ily, `retrHnOne`: drop the "solo" markup, or change it to `^\markup \italic "dolce"` if an expression word is wanted. Only the cosmetic label changes.

### 7. Vln I bar 47: decrescendo into a rest with no goal (not applied, minor)
**Problem.** Vln I's whole note at 47 has a closing hairpin that ends at the barline, with no dynamic, and bar 48 rests. Every other string part reaches pp at 48.
**Fix:** bSec.ily, Vln I bar 47: end the hairpin with `\!`, or give the destination: `cis''1\>` … `R1\!`, or keep the hairpin under the note and add `\pp` on a spacer `<>\pp` at the end of the bar.
