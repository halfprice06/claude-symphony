## Findings

All six findings record that the score follows the reading. I did not find anything that this reading shows to be wrong and that is not already protected or decided. The order is by how much each passage depends on the reading.

### 1. The two soft entries after a release follow Mahler's Fig. 6.1 (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 65–67 and 82–85.
**What the reading shows:** in Fig. 6.1 the woodwinds' pp stops one bar before the distant trumpets enter. The strings hold a static pedal across several octaves, and the trumpets come in at ppp as the result heard in the hall (PDF 271–272).
**Score:** at 65–66 the upper strings fade to niente, and Hn 1 enters at 67 over the four solo basses' E♭2 and the ppp roll near the rim. At 82 b3 the tutti releases. The harp harmonics enter at 83 over the A♭1/A♭2 pedal alone, the timpani answer at 84, the violas enter on 84 b3, and Cl 1 enters niente at 85. Each new sound enters over a pedal with nothing else moving, as in the Mahler figure.
**Fix:** none.

### 2. Theme A moves "further away" with each return (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 7, 23, 71–81, 83, 85–89.
**What the reading shows:** Casanelles PDF 282–284 describes the piano theme of *The Social Network*, recorded from further away at each return. PDF 273 and 287 say that distance and a high cut give nostalgia while the sound stays close.
**Score:** the theme goes from the horn (7) to the muted violins (23), then to the open violins at ff (79). After that it moves away: harp harmonics (83), the clarinet's niente tail (85–89), the muted horns' echo of F–F♭–E♭ (87–90, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44), and Vln I sul D for the last C5. The acoustic means are mutes, harmonics, sul D/sul C and niente attacks. Casanelles PDF 286 names these as the orchestral versions of EQ.
**Fix:** none.

### 3. Low spacing follows the harmonic series in every tutti (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 7, 57, 59–62, 75–82.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 274: the series gives octave, fifth and fourth first, and the major third only at the fifth partial.
**Score:** my scan of the sounding pitches found the third at or above the fifth partial of the bass at 61 (F3 over D♭1), 62 (B♭3 over G♭1), 62 b3 (A♭3 over E1), 79 (F4 over D♭1) and 7 (C3 over A♭1). Thirds sit lower only at pp (65 b1 G3 over E♭2; 87 A♭3 over F1). c174 already recorded the one borderline low third at 54.
**Fix:** none.

### 4. Frequency allotment around the lead lines (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** all solo and theme passages.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 287: "The fewer the instruments in a frequency range, the clearer and more present they become", and sounds in one range mask each other.
**Score:** my masking scan found only three places where another part sounds above the lead. Each was decided earlier:
- Fl 1 pp above the C.a. at 41–44, graded one level under it (c174).
- Vln II pp above the C.a. quotation at 47–52 (c294, c324, c338).
- The string tremolo sf>p above Tbn 1's ff quotation at 63–64 (c084, c324). Here the trombone is the harmonically densest sound and comes forward (PDF 289).

The only second against a lead at an equal level is passing: the violas at 42 b2.67 and 43 b1.67, inside the triplet figure, at pp. I see no reason to reopen any of them.
**Fix:** none.

### 5. Brass density is graded at the two peaks (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 61–64, 79–81.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 289: forte brass are "essentially equivalent to distortion" in harmonic density, and "harmonically denser instruments tend to become foregrounded". PDF 275: loud brass have dissonant upper partials as their loudest components.
**Score:** the brass chord at 61–62 lies at D♭3–A♭4, under the ff theme at D♭6/A♭6. Hn 1·2 are ff for the pp-or-ff re-entry rule (c024) and Hn 3·4 a2 are f (c086). This is protected, and the open-order spacing keeps the horns' upper partials below the theme's octave. At 63 the one ff brass line is the quotation, which is meant to come forward. At 79–81 the horns are f at C4–F4, more than an octave under the lowest theme octave. Reopening the Hn 1·2 ff would need an answer to c024 and c264, and this reading does not give one.
**Fix:** none.

### 6. Timpani rolls still render as single decaying strokes (open item from c244, recorded, not applied)
**Bars:** 2–3, 60–62, 67–70, 79–81.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 290 and Fig. 6.6: a sound held at a constant level is heard as more present, and compression makes a timpani hit last longer. A roll is the acoustic form of that sustained envelope.
**Score:** the print is right. In score.midi each `:32` roll is one note-on that decays, so at 67–69 and 79–81 the render's roll is gone before the printed roll ends. Nobody here can hear the render, and the fix needs a MIDI-only voice per roll, either a hidden voice without `Note_performer` swapped the other way round, or a separate `\midi` score. Either one would change the note count that analysis.txt reports. I do not recommend it now. If it is ever done, the minimal form per roll is a hidden MIDI voice beside the printed one, for example at 67:
`<< \new Voice \with { \remove "Note_performer" } { es2.:32\ppp~ ... } \new Voice \with { \remove "Note_heads_engraver" \remove "Stem_engraver" ... } { \repeat unfold 18 es16\ppp ... } >>`
Check the result against score.midi's note count, as c240 asked.
