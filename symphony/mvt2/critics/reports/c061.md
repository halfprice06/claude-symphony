## What else I studied

The full score PDF of v61 (pages 1–8), every section file, HISTORY.md to cycle 60, the plan and cyclic rules in BRIEF.md. In the corpus I compared the two themes with Dvořák 9/ii (Mutopia `DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/woods.notesL.ily`): the cor anglais Largo theme (bars 7–10) is built from one repeated rhythmic cell, 8. 16 4 | 8. 16 4, and the più mosso oboe theme (bars 54–58) is built on suspensions: `a1~ | a4 gs2~ gs8 a`, an A tied over the bar line and a G♯ syncopated across beat 3. I ran `allpairs_par.py` on v61 and on my scratch build and scanned by hand the inner voices around every note I changed.

Assessment of the themes. Theme A (7–14, 23–30, 71–82) is the stronger and more hummable tune: a rising arpeggio to a long note tied across the bar line, the chromatic motto tail falling from it, and a rhythmic cell (4. 8 4~ | 4 4 4) that comes back at 11. Its two tied notes (7–8, 11–12) are the places a listener remembers, and they are correct suspensions in Unit 21's sense. Theme B (37–44, 53–60) is the weaker one, as cycle 51 already noted. Its long notes are all struck afresh on the beat, its two halves have different rhythms, and nothing in it is held against a change of harmony. In Dvořák's middle-section theme the tie and the syncopation are the memorable features. Finding 1 gives theme B one such feature at its peak without changing its pitches.

## Findings

### 1. Theme B has no suspension; hold the 7th of A♭7 into the D♭ chord at 42 and 58 (applied)

Bars 42 (Fl 1, C.a.) and 58 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II).

Problem. Bar 42 is a chain of fifths, one chord per beat: B♭m7 – E♭m7 – A♭7 – D♭ (bass B♭–E♭–A♭–D♭, violas and harp). The melody A♭–B♭A♭–G♭–F gives the 7ths on beats 1 and 3, but each is struck on the beat and leaves on the next beat. In the whole of theme B no note is held across a beat into a new harmony. A&S say suspension is the figuration in which "rhythm rather than melody assumes paramount importance" (PDF 346) and that it "bring[s] into prominence the voice in which [it] occur[s]" (PDF 348). They also say "the 7ths of seventh chords often result from suspensions" (PDF 362) and that "the 7th of V7 is frequently suspended to form a 4-3 over I" (PDF 359, Ex. 21-23a, which I checked on the page image). Bar 42 b3–b4 is exactly that progression: A♭7 to D♭. Bar 58 has the same melody over A♭7 to D♭7 (V7/IV), where the held G♭ is again a 4th above the bass.

Evidence that it fits. Metric rule (PDF 347): the suspension falls on b4 and the resolution on the weaker b4½. Downward step resolution (PDF 348): G♭ to F. Tone of resolution (PDF 352): the violas' arpeggio at 42 b4 and 58 b4 reaches F only on the third triplet eighth (b4 + 2/3), after the melody has resolved. The only F sounding with the held G♭ at 58 b4 is the Hn 1/Vc counter-line F4/F3, two octaves below, in another timbre and rhythm, which is the case PDF 352 allows ("Beethoven … would sometimes allow a suspension in the top voice to sound against the tone of resolution an octave lower"). The bass moves A♭–D♭ under the held note, so the motion is oblique and no parallels result. The peak B♭ and all pitches stay; only beat 4's rhythm changes (4 → 4~ 8 8), and the dotted figure of bar 43 (E♭4. F8) now answers it. The descending-fifths harmony protected since cycle 32 is unchanged.

Fix (in /tmp/critic-061/work/sections/bSec.ily):
```

aes''4\mp\> bes''8 aes''8 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8 |
% C.a., bar 42
aes'4\mp\> bes'8 aes'8 ges'4~ ges'8 f'8 |
% Fl 1, bar 58
aes'''4 bes'''8 aes'''8 ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8 |
% Ob 1, bar 58
aes''4 bes''8 aes''8 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8 |
% Vln I, bar 58
aes'''4 bes'''8 aes'''8) ges'''4(~ ges'''8 f'''8) |
% Vln II, bar 58
aes''4 bes''8 aes''8) ges''4(~ ges''8 f''8) |
```
Bar 38 and 54 (first half of the theme) stay as they are, so the new figure belongs to the second half, which climbs to the peak.

### 2. Parallel 5ths between clarinet 2 and the bass at 72 b2–b3; anticipate the B♭ (applied)

Bar 72. Cl 2 F4 → B♭4 while the basses (Cb, Bsn 2, harp LH) move B♭1 → E♭2: a perfect 5th (compound) to a perfect 5th in similar motion, the 5th of V7/V moving to the 5th of V7. `allpairs_par.py` reports it on v61 (beat 243 = bar 72 b3: "Clarinets 1·2:two – Double basses P5"). Cycle 59 put the B♭4 there to avoid G–A♭ octaves with the counter-melody, so going back to G4 is not an option.

Unit 21 gives the remedy: "Suspensions and anticipations can be very effective in breaking up parallel 5ths … they displace the underlying voice leading and convert parallel into oblique motion" (PDF 366; Ex. 21-40, PDF 367, is the same remedy in a IV7–V progression). An anticipation comes on the weaker part of the beat and may be tied to its main note (PDF 363–364, Ex. 21-35). Taking B♭4 on b2½ over the sounding B♭ bass, and holding it while the bass moves to E♭, turns both motions oblique.

Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo, bar 72):
```
aes'2.\pp | aes'4 f'8 bes'8~ bes'4 | aes'2. | g'2 r4 |
```
Checked by hand at b2½: the violas are on A♭4 (B♭7's 7th, a 2nd below), Cl 1 holds D5, the counter-melody holds F3. There are no new parallels, and the P5 entry disappears from `allpairs_par.py`.

### Checks with no change (reading-based)

- **Theme A's suspensions, 8 / 24 / 72.** E♭ is prepared over I, becomes the 7th over vi7 and resolves down to D over V7/V while the bass moves (PDF 353, Ex. 21-14). No voice sounds D during the suspension at b1 (PDF 352). Correct. Do not touch.
- **Half cadence 43–44.** The cadential 4-3 is in the violas (A♭–D♭–E♭ → A♭–C–E♭ on b4, PDF 350). The melody's D♭ resolves through B♭ to C, which is a transferred and decorated resolution (PDF 361–363). Correct as it is.
- **Cadences 13–14, 29–30, 81–82.** A V4-3 is not possible here: the melody is on the leading tone G at b1 of V7, so a suspended A♭ in any inner voice would sound against its own resolution (PDF 352, "poor"). Leave them.
- **Coda 87–89.** I tested whether Cl 1's D♭5 (87–88) could be held into 89 as a 4-3 over the returning A♭. It cannot, because Vln I's protected C5 enters on 89 b1 in unison with the resolution tone, the "poor" case of Ex. 21-12. The simultaneous D♭–C over iv–I is right. Do not touch.
- **Pedals.** 1–6 and 31–36 are tonic pedals, 65–70 is a dominant pedal before the reprise and 83–92 is a closing tonic pedal. That is textbook use (PDF 367). The upper-voice dissonances over them are prepared and resolved (PDF 368): Bsn 1 F–E♭ 6-5 at 33–34, Bsn 2 D♭ at 69 → C at 71, Cl 1 D passing at 86.
- **Open point "Vln II tied A♭4 at 15–22".** This is a restruck inner-voice pedal on the tonic. A&S name inner and top-voice pedals as normal (PDF 367), and Ex. 21-2 shows restruck rather than tied notes as a surface difference only (PDF 347). Every chord in 15–22 either contains A♭ or leaves it at the bar's end (G at 16, F at 18, E at 20). I recommend closing the point with no change.
