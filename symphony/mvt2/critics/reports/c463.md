## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The reading's principles are met. The points below are new evidence, a correction to the earlier scans, and one warning against a change the reading might seem to invite. They are ranked by how much they bear on the form.

### 1. The return to A♭ uses the correct kind of dominant for a IV stopover (no change; do not replace with an inverted V)

**Check.** Section 4 of the unit (PDF 235–236) says a key area on III usually returns to I through an inverted V, with no root-position V as a goal. A reader could take that as an argument for shortening 65–70 to an inverted dominant. That would be a mistake here. The reading gives a different route for the other stopovers: from IV or II to V the bass passes by step to a root-position V, with 5̂ not held as a common tone (15-2, PDF 231–232; Review 3–4, PDF 244). This movement's stopover is IV (D♭, 37–61), and its bass runs D♭1 (61) – G♭1, E1 (62) – A1 (63–64 b2) – D2 (64 b3) – E2 (64 b4) – E♭2 (65), with no E♭ sounded between 61 and 65. That is the 15-2 route, so the root-position V7 at 65–70 is right.

The reading's inverted-V return appears where it belongs, at the small scale: in A′ the bar that was a half cadence in A (10, 26) becomes V4/3 over B♭1 at 74, stepping to I at 75 (PDF 235: "not a goal of motion"). So both of the unit's returns are used, each in its own context.

**Corpus.** Tchaikovsky 6/iv is the reading's case: B minor, with its second theme on III (D major, harmony.csv onset 108). After a long ♭VII pedal (C major, onsets 216–245) it returns to the tonic through V6/5 (onset 246), then i at 249: an inverted dominant, exactly 15-7. Dvořák 9/ii, which has a IV-type contrast like this movement, returns through a root-position dominant (Tempo I at 101). The difference between the two corpus movements matches the difference the reading draws, and this movement sides with the model it should.

**Fix.** None. Keep 65–70 as a root-position E♭7 (protected retransition).

### 2. Correction to c173 and c323: a fourth rising third into a downbeat at 41→42 (no change)

**Check.** c173 scanned downbeats for rising-third root motion (PDF 231, 240) and found 40→41, 56→57 and 57→58; c323 confirmed these three and said they were the only ones. There is a fourth: 41 b3–4 → 42 b1. At 41 b3–4 the chord is G♭ (bass F♯2/F♯1 = G♭, harp G♭2–G♭3) with B♭3 in the violas, D♭3 in the cellos, F4–G♭4 in the C.a. and A♭5 in Fl 1: IV with major seventh and ninth. At 42 b1 it is B♭m7 (bass B♭1, viola A♭3, C.a. A♭4, flute F5). It is the same move as 57→58, which c173 did record. IV7 contains every tone of vi, so the upper voices D♭, F and A♭ carry over the barline, which is the "motion within the same chord" the reading warns about.

**Verdict.** The metre is not contradicted. The downbeat of 42 is articulated by the bass leap G♭→B♭, by the C.a.'s arrival on A♭4 (the first note of the motto tail at the D♭ level) and by Fl 1 moving A♭5→F5. The vi7 also starts a chain of fifths (vi7–ii–V7–V7/IV, 15-9), and the reading says VI in that chain is subordinate. A change (for example ii6/5 on 41 b3–4) would alter theme B's harmony and the exported motto bar 42. Record the fourth instance with the same reasons as c173's three.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bar 78 b3: III is the reading's preferred chord here, not just an allowed one (no change)

**Check.** c323 recorded 78 b3 as iii6 between ♭VI and IV in a falling bass (15-10c/d). The 15-12 text (PDF 239) adds the reason it is the better choice. I6 is usually preferable, "but III can be preferable when the stepwise descent 8-7-6 in the soprano or in an inner voice is wanted". The per-instrument dump shows that line: Cl 2 A♭4 (78 b1–2, over F♭) – G4 (78 b3, over E♭) – F4 (79 b1, over D♭), with Vln II A♭4 at 78 b1 doubling the 8̂. An I6 at 78 b3 (bass C) would break the falling bass E–E♭–D♭ and the 7̂. This gives c103 and c133's ruling (do not correct the passing 6/3) a second book reason. Record it.

**Fix.** None.

Tchaikovsky 6/iv's III section runs from 21 % to 48 % of its beats; that is a III plan in minor, where III is reached early (PDF 232). ### 5. The six-bar V7 at 65–70 is long for a reason other than function (record)

**Check.** PDF 234 says the dominant's force is so great that it "does not always require special emphasis through duration or accent". The reading therefore gives no harmonic reason for six bars (20.8 s) of V7. The length comes from the form and the cyclic plan: an unaccompanied horn statement of the motto tail (cyclic rule: horn melodic moment), and the release after the 61–64 climax, as Dvořák 9/ii releases before its Tempo I. The four one-chord frames (1–6, 31–36, 65–70, 83–88) each last about 21 s (c423). That is a deliberate symmetry, not a pacing fault. I note it so that a later critic does not cite PDF 234 to shorten 65–70. Shortening would also break the 3/4 bar count of the protected retransition.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Bar 1 → 2 is not the III → I that PDF 240 warns against (record)

**Check.** Section 9 (PDF 240) says III "does not tend to move directly to I". Bar 1 is movement I's C-major chord and bar 2 is the A♭ chord with C5 kept on top, so on paper III♯ moves straight to I. But the A♭ tonic is not yet established at bar 2 (it is confirmed by the horn theme from 7). What the listener hears is movement I's tonic moving to its ♭VI by a falling third with a common tone, the long-range mixture that c460 recorded. The reading's warning is about a diatonic III inside an established key, so it does not apply. The link is protected.

**Fix.** None.
