## What else I studied

I read the whole v151 score PDF (8 pages), every section file, HISTORY.md, BRIEF.md, the plan, and the earlier Unit 21 reports c061 and c098, plus c082 and c089 on bar 58. I wrote three scripts and ran them on the MIDI. One lists every held note that becomes dissonant against a new bass, with its resolution and any other voice that sounds the resolution pitch while it is held. One prints every sounding pitch per half beat. One checks all pairs of voices for parallel 5ths, inner voices included. I ran allpairs_par.py on v151 and on the scratch build. In the corpus I compared the Canzonetta of Tchaikovsky's Violin Concerto (`mutopia/TchaikovskyPI_O35/.../mvmt2/solo.ily`, bars 19–20: `c16 bes a8~ a4 …| fis16 es d8~ d4`). There the main tones arrive an eighth early and are tied over the beat, which is A&S's tied anticipation (PDF 364, Ex. 21-35). I also used c061's reading of Dvořák 9/ii (oboe, più mosso: `a1~ | a4 gs2~ gs8 a`).

On memorability: by the reading's own test, theme A is the well-made tune. It has an arpeggio rising to a note tied across the bar, a 7th that resolves down by step, a consonant tied note in the second phrase, and an implied 7-chain in the motto tail. That is the Ex. 21-1 and 21-30 kind of line, and a listener will keep it. Theme B is weaker rhythmically. Every note of its first phrase (37–40, 53–56) falls on the beat. That point is closed (c131, c141) and I do not reopen it. Its second phrase, which is the one that builds (57–60), is where a master would put the syncopated suspension. At 58 that is exactly where v151 has two faults by Unit 21's rules.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 58 b4: horn 1 and the cellos sound the resolution F under theme B's G♭–F suspension (applied)

**Problem.** At 58 b4 the melody holds G♭ over the D♭ bass, in four instruments and two octaves (Fl 1/Vln I G♭6, Ob 1/Vln II G♭5). This is the 4-3 that critic 061 added. It resolves to F on b4½. Horn 1 (F4) and the cellos (F3) play F on b4 itself, half a beat before the melody resolves. A&S mark this "poor": sounding the tone of resolution in another voice "produces an unclear sound and weakens the effect of the resolution" (PDF 352, Ex. 21-12a). Critic 082 removed the F with an A♭. Critic 089 restored it because the A♭ made fifths with the bass (E♭/A♭ at b3 → A♭/D♭ at b4). The first statement at 42 has no F on b4, so this fault is only in the repeat.

**Evidence.** The MIDI at 58 b4 in v151 has D♭2, F3 (Vc), A♭3, F4 (Hn 1), C♭5, D♭5, G♭5, G♭6. At 42 b4 it has D♭2, D♭3, A♭3, G♭4 (C.a.), E♭5 (Fl), with F only on b4½. A&S give the cure for c089's objection: suspensions "displace the underlying voice leading and convert parallel into oblique motion" (PDF 365–366, Ex. 21-39). If Hn 1 and the cellos hold their E♭ through b4, the fifth E♭/A♭ at b3 becomes a 9th E♭/D♭ at b4 by oblique motion. That is not a parallel. The E♭ then rises to F on b4½ with the melody's resolution. This is a 9-10 next to a 4-3, the same pair that 42 already has in the flute and cor anglais (PDF 356–357, Ex. 21-21: 2-3/9-10 suspensions give a rising line; PDF 358–359: 2̂ over I rising to 3̂). E♭ and G♭ held over D♭ are two upper tones of the A♭7 at b3 carried into the next chord (PDF 357–358, Ex. 21-23a; PDF 358, Ex. 21-24b). The other options c089 rejected (D♭, which makes octaves with the bass, and C♭, which doubles the 7th) are not needed.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily).**
```
% bSecHnOne, bar 58 (was  f'4 es'2 f'4) \breathe )
\partCombineSoloI aes'2(\mp\< ges'2 | f'4 es'2~ es'8 f'8) \breathe | ...
% bSecVc, bar 58 (was  f4) es2( f4) )
f2\< ges2(^\markup \italic "espr." | f4) es2(~ es8 f8) | ges2\f ...
```
After the fix: no voice has F at 58 b4, F sounds in four octaves at b4½, my checker finds no P5 anywhere, and allpairs_par.py shows only the same doublings moved by half a beat. This re-raises c082 and answers c089's stated reason. The counter-line keeps its pitches and contour. Only the F moves half a beat later.

### 2. Bar 58 b2: the melody's A♭ is a dissonant 4th on the weak beat that resolves on beat 3 (applied)

**Problem.** At 58 the melody is `aes'''2 ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8`. On b2 the bass moves to E♭ (ii7: E♭ G♭ B♭ D♭ in the Vc, B.cl., Bsn, violas, Cl 2 and Hn 1). The A♭ held from b1 is then a 4th above the bass and resolves to G♭ only on b3, where the bass is already A♭. A dissonance on beat 2 that resolves on beat 3 of a 4/4 bar breaks the basic metric rule: the suspension "appears in a metrically strong position relative to the release or resolution" (PDF 347; review 2b, PDF 369). The exception in §24 does not apply. In Ex. 21-32 the weak-beat 4th is made rhythmically strong by a new harmony together with a long high note on that beat (PDF 363). At 58 the A♭ was struck on b1, so nothing is new on b2 except the bass. This came from c110, which replaced the original `aes4 bes8 aes8` (B♭ consonant on b2, A♭ passing on b2½, as the cor anglais still plays at 42) with a half note, to keep B♭6 out of the B section. While the A♭ is held, clarinet 2 and the violas sound its resolution G♭4 (PDF 352).

**Evidence.** The scripts list 58.2 Fl/Vln I A♭6 and Ob/Vln II A♭5 as 4ths over E♭2, resolving at 58.3. Compare the Canzonetta, bars 19–20: the melody reaches its main tone an eighth early and ties it over the beat. That is A&S's tied anticipation, placed on a weaker part of the beat than its main tone (PDF 363–364, Ex. 21-35).

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily).** Move the G♭ half a beat earlier and tie it over beat 3:
```
% bSecFluteOne 58 (was  aes'''2 ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8) \breathe )
aes'''4. ges'''8~ ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8) \breathe
% bSecOboeOne 58 (was  aes''2 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8 )
aes''4. ges''8~ ges''4~ ges''8 f''8
% bSecVlnOne 58 (was  aes'''2) ges'''4(~ ges'''8 f'''8) )
aes'''4.) ges'''8(~ ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8)
% bSecVlnTwo 58 (was  aes''2) ges''4(~ ges''8 f''8) )
aes''4.) ges''8(~ ges''4~ ges''8 f''8)
```
The A♭ over E♭ now resolves on the weaker half of b2. G♭ enters as a consonant 3rd of ii7 (the preparation). It is tied into b3 as the prepared 7th of V7 (PDF 353–354, 362: 7ths arising from suspensions). It is held again into b4 as the 4-3 over D♭ (PDF 357–358). One tied note now carries three functions: 3rd, 7th, 4th, then resolution. This is the A&S chain, and it is the rhythm that makes a phrase memorable. The bar now opens with the movement's own `4. 8` cell, as 59 does (`es'''4. f'''8`). That gives the two-bar climb into the climax a rhyme. Nothing else changes: A♭6 stays the B-section ceiling (c110), both octaves still match (c119), there is no B♭6, the bowing is the same (two slurs in 58), and the S-head rhythm `2.~ 8 8 | 4 2 4` is not formed.

### 3. Bar 58 b2: clarinet 2's G♭4 still sounds for half a beat under the A♭ (not applied; acceptable)

**Problem.** After Finding 2, Cl 2 (`bes'4 ges'4 aes'4 ces''4`) still strikes G♭4 on b2 under the A♭5/A♭6 4th for one eighth.

**Evidence.** A&S allow this when the texture has "marked contrast in rhythm, timbre, or dynamics", and when the top voice sounds against the resolution "an octave lower in one of the inner voices" (PDF 352). Here the gap is one and two octaves, and the clarinet's timbre differs from the flute/violin melody. I recommend no change. If the composer wants the bar completely clean: Cl 2 `bes'4. ges'8 aes'4 ces''4`. That touches the c124 accompaniment rule, so I did not apply it.

### Considered and left alone

- **81, top-voice 4-3 at the last cadence.** The textbook way to strengthen the final cadence would be to hold the melody's A♭6 into 81 over E♭ against Hn 3's F♭ 9-8. That suspends the whole iiø7 over V (PDF 350, 365). It would rewrite theme A's tail rhythm inside the protected cadence 77–82, where the B♭6 is already a four-beat delay (PDF 360, delayed resolution). Two delays in succession would overstate it. No change.
- **Theme A's cadences 13–14, 29–30, 81–82.** There is room for an anticipation in the top voice (PDF 363). It would replace the B♭ of the stepwise G–E♭–D♭–C–B♭–A♭ descent. The line is better as it is.
- **Pedals.** All four follow §28 (table rows 22–23).
