## Other study

I compared the three statements of theme A's second phrase (11–12, 27–28, 75–76) for how the B♭ appoggiatura is shaped, and read the earlier expression reports on this book (c038, c138) to avoid repeating their points. For the mockup findings I measured the FluidR3 patches the build uses: one held note per General MIDI program, rendered with the same fluidsynth settings as build.sh, at velocities 40, 64 and 91. All programs except timpani lie within about 4 dB of each other at equal velocity (for example at velocity 64: oboe −29.8 dBFS, english horn −33.8, horn −31.5, trombone −30.7, string ensemble −33.8), so the balance of score.mp3 is decided by velocity. I then read LilyPond 2.24's `midi-init.ly`: its `instrument-equalizer-alist` scales flute, oboe, clarinet, bassoon, horn, trumpet, timpani, violin, viola, cello and contrabass, and has no entry for english horn, trombone, tuba, orchestral harp or string ensemble 1, which therefore play over the full 0–1 range.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 76: the A′ crescendo runs straight through theme A's appoggiatura (applied)
**Problem.** In both earlier statements the B♭ appoggiatura of the second phrase is the dynamic peak of the bar and its resolution is softer: Hn 1 at 12 (`bes'4\mp\> aes'4`), Vln I/II at 28 (`bes''4(\f\> aes''4)`). At 76 the same B♭6–A♭6 lies inside one undivided hairpin from mp at 75 to f at 78, so the resolution A♭6 is played louder than the dissonance, and the strings arrive at 77 already near mf where Fl 1 and Cl 1 enter mp<.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 130 and Fig. 4.4: a real crescendo rises, eases and rises again; PDF 123 and 129: a written "crescendo" is ambiguous, so a shape the composer wants must be written. PDF 127: each line keeps its own shape; the cello/horn/bassoon counter-melody should keep rising under the theme. A&S PDF 348 (cited by c198 for bar 58): dissonant suspensions and appoggiaturas take the accent, resolutions are softer. The build is protected (c213, c218); this keeps its endpoints (mp 75, f 78, ff 79), its added instruments and the counter-melody's continuous crescendo, and adds only the inflection c198 added inside the protected 53–61 build at 58.
**Fix (applied, `aTwo.ily`, Vln I and Vln II only).**
```
Vln I:  aes''4.\mp\< c'''8) f'''4~ | f'''4 bes'''4(-\tweak stencil ##f \mf \> aes'''4)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp | g'''4(\< ... | aes''2\f
Vln II: aes'4.\mp\< c''8) f''4~ | f''4 bes''4(-\tweak stencil ##f \mf \> aes''4)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp | g''4(\< ... | aes'2\f
```
Printed: crescendo 75 b1 to 76 b2, short diminuendo on the resolution, crescendo again from 77 b1 to f at 78. The mf/mp are hidden, for score.midi only. In the MIDI the B♭6 now sounds at velocity 73, the A♭6 at 63, and 77 rises from 63 to 80, with the winds' mp at 77 (44–56).

### 2. score.midi: five instruments sit outside LilyPond's equalizer, so the mockup's balance contradicts the score at 61, 63 and 79 (applied)
**Problem.** english horn, trombone, tuba and harp have no entry in LilyPond's equalizer table and play over the full range, while their neighbours are scaled to 0–0.7. Measured in v228's score.midi: at 79 (ff) the cor anglais, which only doubles the violas' lowest octave, plays at velocity 91 against flute 1's B♭6 and oboe 1 at 64, so it is the loudest wind at the movement's peak; at 61 the trombones and tuba, marked f with the horns, play at 83 against the horns' 62 (about 6 dB louder, by the patch measurements above); the harp's f at 78 is 83, equal to the violins'. These are mockup errors, not score errors, but score.mp3 is the only version anyone hears.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 133–134: the mockup is meant to be a close version of the performance, used to hear the result and catch mistakes; a mockup that reverses the written balance hides real mistakes and invents false ones. PDF 141–142 and Fig. 4.5: the dynamic markings map to fixed sample layers, so equal markings must reach equal layers.
**Fix (applied, `score.ly`, `\midi` Score context).**
```
instrumentEqualizer = #(lambda (s) (or (assoc-get s instrument-equalizer-alist)
  (assoc-get s '(("english horn" . (0 . 0.7)) ("trombone" . (0.1 . 0.7)) ("tuba" . (0.1 . 0.7)) ("orchestral harp" . (0 . 0.7))))))
```
Each gets its family neighbour's range (oboe; horn). Result: C.a. 64 at 79 (equal to the oboes), trombones/tuba 62 at 61 (equal to the horns), Tbn 1's ff marcato quotation at 63 still the loudest note (87). I left "string ensemble 1" (Vln I/II) at full range: they carry the theme at every peak and the violas/cellos already sit under them as the score intends.

### 3. score.midi: no diminuendo on a held note is heard, including the closing morendo (applied for 91–92)
**Problem.** LilyPond writes dynamics only as note-on velocity. score.midi contains no controller messages, so every hairpin on a tied or held note is silent in score.mp3: the swells at 8, 24 and 67, the niente fades, and the whole closing morendo. At 89–92 all strings, Cl 2 and the bass hold tied notes; in the mockup they stay at the pp attack level through the rit. and stop.
**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 124: a note is only "on" and "off" with one velocity; PDF 142 and Fig. 4.5: changing the dynamic of a sounding note needs a continuous controller (CC11). PDF 127: a controller acts on the whole channel, and fixmidi.py assigns one channel per program (Vln I and II share one), so per-staff fades are unsafe where partners differ; bars 91–92 are the one place where every sounding part fades together.
**Fix (applied, `global.ily`, MIDI only; nothing prints).** In bar 91, `\set Staff.midiExpression` steps 1.0, 0.96, 0.92, 0.88, 0.84, 0.80 by eighths, and 0.76 at 92 b1 (about the pp→ppp step). Because `\global` is in every staff, each staff sends the same CC11 ramp, which the shared channels tolerate. The same method could later shape the string niente at 3 and 66 per staff (Vln I/II together), which I did not do.
