## Findings

### 1. Bars 61-62: the full tutti, checked again chord by chord (no change)

Sounding notes at 61 b1:
- Woodwind: D♭1 Cbn; D♭3 B.cl., Bsn 2; F3 Bsn 1; A♭4 Cl 2; D♭5 Ob 2; F5 Ob 1, Cl 1; A♭5 Fl 2; A♭6 Fl 1.
- Brass: D♭2 tuba; D♭3 B.tbn; F3 Tbn 2; D♭4 and F4 Hn 1·2; A♭4 Hn 3·4.
- Strings: D♭2 Cb; A♭3 Vc; D♭4 and F4 Vla; A♭4 Vln II; D♭6 Vln I.

Each group holds a complete D♭ triad. The bass octave has only the root. The tuba sits an octave under the bass trombone, and the trombones and horns are in close position above it. At 300 dpi, chords 1-5 on PDF 505 put the brass third no lower than about E3 over a C root. F3 over D♭ is the same position, so the "low third at 61" item from c004-c024 is answered and can be closed. The brass is one degree under the woodwind and strings, as in chords 2-3. Inside the brass, the ff for Hn 1·2 and f for Hn 3·4 a2 follow PDF 116 on balancing by dynamic marks. The spacing scan finds no gap wider than an octave above C3 at 61 or 62.

### 2. Bars 63-64: brass and strings without woodwind, as chords 6-8 (no change)

Chords 6 and 7 on PDF 505 are f and ff tutti chords with no woodwind except a contrabassoon. Chord 8 on PDF 506 has the upper strings play one sff stroke while the winds hold. At 63 the woodwind drop out after Fl 1's sf stroke on the downbeat. Tbn 1 and B.tbn carry on over the string tremolo sf>, and the Cb play one pizz. sf stroke under the held B.tbn A2. The tables show both devices in loud chords, so the empty woodwind at 63-64 is a scoring Rimsky used, not a hole. Protected in any case (Tbn 1 alone 63-64, c074).

### 3. Bars 79-82: the second peak against Ex. 209 (no change)

In Ex. 209 (PDF 375), the cellos double the melody in octaves with the violins. Here the cellos carry the counter-line instead (D♭4-C4-B♭3-A♭3 at 79-80), and the melody B♭ sounds in Fl 1 and Vln I (B♭6), Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II (B♭5), and C.a. and Vla (B♭4). That is three octaves with a string and a wind in each, so the melody does not need the cellos. The cello counter-line is in the plan (71-82), and the A′ texture at 71-82 is protected (c017). The other features of Ex. 209 are here: harmony held by the clarinets, bassoons and horns at f under the ff melody, the timpani roll f, and a quick release to the harp at 82 (p). The contrabassoon's sounding B♭0 at 80 b3 is the instrument's lowest standard note. It lasts one beat at ff, doubles the bass an octave under the Cb, and is playable. Recorded only.

### 4. String-free passages 3-14 and 31-36 against chord 13, Ex. 210 and Ex. 213 (no change)

Chord 13 (Mlada, pp, PDF 506) is a complete wind and brass chord with no strings. Ex. 213 (PDF 381) is a soft Andante wind texture over harps alone. PDF 121 says pizzicato low strings or harp under sustained winds make the winds clearer. At 3-6 the close wind chord in Cl 2, B.cl. and Hn 3·4 matches chord 13 in layout. At 7-14 the harp LH doubles the bass under the bassoons and bass clarinet, which is the job PDF 121 gives the harp. At 31-36 the woodwind choir sits on a Cbn/Bsn pedal, which PDF 121 says leaves a wind tutti's character unchanged. No low brass is used in these passages. That follows from the protected brass economy, and chord 13 shows low brass at pp only as an option. Protected (link 1-6, horn solo 7-14, woodwind choir 31-36).

### 5. Spacing scan of every downbeat (no change)

On every downbeat from 1 to 92, I looked for a gap wider than an octave above C3 in chords of five or more pitches. The scan finds only three: 22 (B♭3-E♭5, solo violin over Vln II, recorded c182 with Rimsky PDF 79), 43 (E♭4-F♯5) and 44 (C4-E♭5). At 43-44 the flute stands over thin strings (protected c034/c036). None of these is in a tutti, so the tables' rule against holes in a loud chord is not touched.
