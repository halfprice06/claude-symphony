## Findings

The score follows nearly every principle in this chapter. Most points the chapter raises were already examined by c294 and later critics, and the passages involved are protected. I found nothing that justifies a change, and I applied no edit. The findings below are new or re-checked observations, with the reason each one stays as it is.

### 1. Bar 57 b1: the downbeat of the build sounds only F and A♭ in the sustained parts (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 57 b1.

**Problem.** Ten voices attack at 57 b1: Vln I + Fl 1 A♭5, Vln II + Ob 1 + Hn 1 A♭4, Vla A♭3, Vc + Bsn 2 + B.cl. F3, Cb F2. The root D♭ of the I6 chord is absent from every sustained part until the melody reaches D♭ on b2. Adler's Brahms example (PDF 164, Ex. XV-14) has "each chord fully represented in all the choirs and in every octave", and his Schumann chords (PDF 162) have "all pitches doubled at least once".

**Why it stays.** The violas' triplet figure (A♭3-D♭4-F4) sounds D♭4 on the second triplet of the beat, and the melody is an arpeggio of the same chord (A♭-D♭-F-A♭), so the ear hears D♭ major within the beat. A sustained D♭ would have to come from the accompaniment, which the c124 rule freezes for 37-60, or from Hn 1. Hn 1's A♭4 is the first note of its protected counter-line (c160, recorded by c303). Do not change.

### 2. Bars 80-82: clarinet 2's seventh D♭5 moves to G4 before C5 (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 81 b1-82 b1.

**Problem.** Cl 2 holds D♭5 from 79 b2 through 81 b1, the seventh of the dominant, then moves D♭5-G4 (81 b2) and G4-C5 (82). Adler praises Schumann's chords (PDF 162) because "all sevenths resolve downward" in every doubling.

**Why it stays.** The same seventh is taken over at 81 b2 by Vc, Hn 1 and Bsn 1 on D♭4, which all resolve to C4 at 82. Clarinet 2's own D♭5 reaches C5 one beat late, by way of a chord tone, which is an ornamented resolution. The 79-81 tutti and Cl 2's D♭5 are protected (c234, c254, c394). Record only.

### 3. Bars 60-61: the cellos take the A♭7's seventh G♭3 up to A♭3 (re-checked; c294's record stands)

The MIDI dump confirms c294's description. Bsn 1, Tbn 2 and Vla resolve G♭3 to F3 or F4, and Hn 1 resolves G♭4 to F4 at 61. The cellos leave the seventh upward to start the augmented head at A♭3. Changing it would break the protected Vln II + Vc octaves on the head at 61-62. No change.

### 4. Bars 61-62: the theme B head is in woodwind and strings only, and the climax doubles mainly for power (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** Adler (PDF 178) observes that in Wagner's combination each theme's colour has at least one string, one woodwind and one brass. At 61-62 the augmented head A♭-D♭-F-F♭ is in Fl 2, Vln II and Vc in three octaves. The brass play harmony: Hn 3·4 a2 A♭4-B♭4-C♭5, Hn 1·2, the trombones and the tuba. At 61 b1 almost every chord tone is doubled at pitch: F5 in Ob 1 + Cl 1, F4 and D♭4 in Vla + Hn 1·2, F3 in Tbn 2 + Bsn 1, D♭3 in Bsn 2 + B.tbn + B.cl., D♭2 in Tuba + Cb. Debussy's chord (PDF 165, Ex. XV-16) doubles only the melody and the top note at pitch.

**Why it stays.** This is the movement's ff peak. Adler (PDF 166-167, Tchaikovsky Ex. XV-18) names this whole-chord doubling for power as the correct choice when "the power of the whole chord is the issue, not its tone color". The melody is still on top in colour: the violins and flutes hold D♭6/A♭6 above, and the head lies in three octaves. If Hn 3·4 played the head, the brass layout at 61-62 would change; that layout is on the do-not-touch list (c082, c224, c264, c424). Keep it.

### 5. Bars 81-82 and 89-92: both soft endings put the top note where it can be controlled (re-checked, follows)

At 81-82 the top line falls from G6 to A♭5 as it diminuendos. Vln I carries it, and Fl 1 doubles it at pitch, so the flute never holds a high pp note alone. That avoids the "dangerous" Brahms chord on PDF 162. The last chord at 92 is strings plus Cl 2, with Vln I sul D on C5 at ppp. This is the arrangement Adler says would make Brahms's chord "easier to control" (PDF 162). Keep both.

### 6. Timpani rolls follow the Bloch rule (re-checked, follows)

PDF 184 asks for rolls marked p and then crescendo. The rolls here run pp< into 53 at 52, pp<f at 60, ppp near the rim at 67-70, and pp< at 78 into an f roll under the ff of 79. None of them starts louder than the orchestra around it. No change.

### 7. Theme statements are rescored while keeping their texture (re-checked, follows)

Adler's Mahler 4 passage (PDF 170-171) keeps the texture and chord structure while the colours change. Theme A appears as horn with reeds and harp (7-14), muted strings in the plain statement (23-30), and violins in octaves with the harp's bass again (71-82). Theme B appears as flute over viola triplets and harp (37), C.a. over pizzicato (41), violins over harp figuration (53), and violins with Fl 1 and Ob 1 over viola triplets (57). Every statement differs in colour and keeps its harmony and accompaniment pattern. No change.

### 8. Dynamic hierarchy follows Schoenberg's rule throughout (re-checked, follows)

Principal voices are one level above subsidiary lines in every combined passage (PDF 184). Examples: C.a. p<mp over Fl 1 pp<p at 41-44; C.a. p over Vln II pp at 47-50; violins p over Cl 1 + Hn 1 pp at 53-56; violins mp over Bsn 1 and Hn 2 p at 75; Cl 1 pp over violas ppp at 85-86. No change.
