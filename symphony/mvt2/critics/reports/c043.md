## Findings

### 1. Tension curve: the middle section has no wave before the build (bars 45–48; applied)

**Problem.** Until bar 57, no bar goes above mf. The A section peaks at mf at 28. B1 and B2 (37–52, about 1 min 10 s) stay between pp and mp. The first f comes at 59, which is 64 % of the way through the movement. All the tension of the middle section therefore sits in one 8-bar ramp (53–60). The most remote harmony before the climax, C♯7 = V7 of the F♯ minor quotation at 47, is marked as a diminuendo to pp.

**Evidence.** A&S 16 §1 (PDF 249–250): descending 5ths are the strongest goal-directed progression. Chromatically altered, the chain "can produce applied dominants and can help to effect modulations". In a chain the end of the motion is the emphasised chord. At 46–48 the bass G♭–C♯–F♯ is exactly such a chain and lands in the movement's most distant key. Dvořák 9/ii marks f or fz at 18, 24–25 (ff tutti), 38–39, 48, 53, 66 and 69–71 (ff) before the main climax at 93–98 (corpus MusicXML). Its middle section rises and falls several times. This score's middle section rises once.

**Fix (applied).** A swell into the C♯7 downbeat that has fallen back to pp by the quotation's bar 48. The cor anglais pickup (47 b4, p) is unchanged.
```
% bSec.ily
bSecOboeOne: ... | bes'4( aes'8 bes'8 des''2\<) | cis''2.\mf\> r4\! |
bSecVc:      ... | bes4( aes8 bes8 des'2\<) | cis'1\mf\> | \clef bass cis1\pp | ...
bSecVlnTwo:  des'1\pp | des'2 des'2\< | eis'1\mp\> | fis'1\pp | ...
bSecVla:     f1\pp | f2 bes2\< | b1\mp\> | a1\pp | ...
bSecCb:      bes,,1\pp | bes,,2 ges,,2\< | cis,1\mp\> | fis,1\pp | ...
bSecBsnTwo:  bes,1\pp | bes,2 bes,2\< | gis,1\mp\> | R1\! | ...
```
The melody pair (Ob 1, Vc in octaves) reaches mf and the accompaniment reaches mp, so the oboe stays on top. No pitches change. This does not undo cycle 41: the oboe still carries the head and still fades before the cor anglais enters.

### 2. Retransition: the motto tail over the dominant pedal lacks the lower voice of 8-7-6-5 / 6-5-4-3 (bars 67–69; applied)

**Problem.** At 67–68, horn 1 plays E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 over only the double basses' and timpani's E♭. The strings' V7 has died away by 67, and bassoons 1–2 enter at 69. For two of the six dominant bars, the harmony that should build expectation of the reprise is a single line over a bare bass. The C4 at 68 b3 is meant as the 6 of a 6/4 (A♭–C over E♭) moving to the 5/3 at 69, but nothing sounds it as a chord. The bar does not prepare A♭ any more strongly than bar 67.

**Evidence.** A&S 19 §7 (PDF 313–314, Ex. 19-16 Mozart K. 525): over a held bass, two voices descending in parallel 3rds or 6ths give 8-7-6-5 over 6-5-4-3. The horn's line is the upper voice of this pattern; its lower partner is missing. A&S 19 §6 (PDF 311–313, Ex. 19-14): a passing 6/4 over a sustained dominant is metrically weak and does not resolve the 7th, which "will follow the dominant". Here the 7th D♭ returns in bassoon 2 at 69 and resolves to C at 71, so the bar fits A&S's model once the 6/4 is actually present. A&S 16 §9 (PDF 257–258, Ex. 16-13): tonic-derived chords as neighbours within a prolonged V.

**Fix (applied).** Bassoon 1 enters one bar earlier and moves in 3rds under the horn into its existing G3:
```
% retr.ily
retrBsnOne = { R2.*3 | bes2(\pp^\markup \bold "1." aes4 | g2.) | g2 r4 | }
```
68: D4/B♭3, D♭4/B♭3, then C4/A♭3, which is a passing 6/4 on beat 3. 69: B♭3/G3 = V. The horn line, its dynamics and its "1. solo" label are unchanged. Bars 65–70 stay thin (at most four sounds at once). This touches the do-not-touch items "E♭7 retransition with solo horn 65–70" (c003), "thin textures 65–70" (c027) and "Hn 1 solo 67–70" (c030). It keeps what they protect: the horn still has the tail, the harmony is still E♭7, and the texture is still thin. The only change is that the one bassoon already present from 69 enters at pp a bar earlier. No new parallels: horn and bassoon move only in 3rds, and against the E♭ pedal they go 4th to 3rd at 68–69.
