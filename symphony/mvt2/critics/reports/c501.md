## Findings

All eight findings are recorded checks. The reading supports no change to the melodies of v501, and none of the possible changes I considered (a turn on a held note, a leap into the B♭6 climax at 79, a leap in theme B's first half) can be made without reopening a protected passage on grounds the reading does not give. v502 can equal v501.

### F1. Theme A: the reduction and the Canzonetta comparison (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 7-14 (Hn 1), 23-30, 71-82.
- **Reduction (Ex. 20-1, PDF 327-328):**
  - Antecedent: 5̂ E♭ held at 7-8, chromatic tail to 3̂ C at 9, passing B♭ and A♭, ending on G over V at 10.
  - Consequent: 6̂ F held at 11-12, the B♭ appoggiatura to A♭ over IV, then 7̂ G at 13 with the lower line E♭-D♭-C-B♭ under it. Both reach 1̂ at 14.
  - The consequent rises a 4th above the antecedent's peak (B♭4 against E♭4). This is the usual intensified answer in a Romantic period.
- **Figuration types:** skips (7, 11), passing tones (9, 13), one appoggiatura (12), one suspension (8 b1, E♭ over V7/V; Unit 21). The theme's ornament is harmonic rather than figural. Its held note is decorated by a chord change and the chromatic tail, not by a turn or a trill.
- **Comparison:** Tchaikovsky's Canzonetta (solo.ily, from bar 13) opens with the same rhythm, G4. A8 B♭ C | D, also a rise to a held 5̂. He decorates the held D with a trill and a five-note turn D-E♭-D-C♯-D, which is Ex. 20-21b's figure (PDF 339). Over 16 bars he states the head four times.
  - Theme A states its head twice in 8 bars. The movement then repeats the whole theme twice more and recalls the head again at 83 (harp) and 84 (timpani, E♭-C-A♭).
  - The repetition count is comparable. The hook in theme A is the chromatic tail at 8 and the descent at 13, not bar 7.
- **Why no change:** a turn on the held E♭ at 72 was declined in c319 (motto-tail statement, cyclic). A turn on the held F at 12/28/76 would change the protected horn theme 7-14 and the A′ texture 71-82. The reading only describes the turn as an available ornament (PDF 338-339). It does not require one, so it gives no reason to reopen either passage.
- **Fix:** none.

### F2. Theme B: neighbour motive plus skips in the second half (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 37-44, 53-60.
- **What happens:** every bar of 37-40 carries a complete neighbour figure (F-G♭-F, D♭-C-D♭, G♭-F-G♭-F, C-D♭-C), the motivic use of PDF 333-334 (Ex. 20-11). At 41/57 the second half opens with chordal skips A♭-D♭-F, which is Ex. 20-2d (PDF 329): skips that vary a prevailing stepwise movement.
- **Chromatic neighbours (PDF 339-340):** the book raises a whole-step neighbour to a half step. Theme B's neighbours already sit a half step from their main tones in D♭ major, so none needs altering. The B♭-minor head at 46 uses A♮ as a lower neighbour, as Ex. 20-23 recommends.
- **Fix:** none (c251's leap variant at 55 is still not recommended).

### F3. The b-phrase 15-22 is a figurated descending line (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 15-22 (Ob 1, solo Vln I).
- **Reduction:** F5 (16), E♭5, D♭5 (18), C5 (19), B♭, A♭, G (20), then F-G-E♭-D-E♭ (21-22) into the half cadence. Each two-bar unit is one step of the line, decorated differently:
  - 15: passing tones C-D♭-E♭ (Ex. 20-7).
  - 17: B♭ escape tone, then G as an accented passing tone (Ex. 20-17, PDF 337).
  - 19: the neighbour C-D♭-C, theme B's cell heard before theme B (PDF 333-334).
  - 21: G escape tone to E♭, then D as a chord tone of the French sixth.
- **Memorability:** this phrase is meant to contrast with theme A, not to be hummed. It does not compete with the theme, which is right for an ABA′ Largo (Dvořák 9/ii's middle phrases do the same).
- **Fix:** none.

### F4. No parallels made by figuration (recorded)

- **Isolated octaves:** with doubling runs and the bass group removed, there are four: 38 b1 (Vla/Vln II), 40 b3 (Fl 1/Vla), 42 b2 (Vla/Vc) and 43 b1 (Cb/Vla). All four are weak tones of the viola triplet arpeggio, the case of Ex. 20-6 (PDF 331). They are the same four c391 found; nothing new since.

- **Fix:** none.

### F5. Chromatic spelling: the motto's D♮ is the only exception, and it is correct (recorded)
- **Scan:** every three-note chromatic line in every part. Eight cases spell the diatonic half step first: E♭-D♮-D♭ at 8 (Hn 1), 24 and 72 (Vln I/II), 33 (B.cl.), 68 (Hn 1). Every other case follows Ex. 20-22 (PDF 339). The C.a.'s C-C♭-B♭ at 33-34 and Tbn 1's B-B♭-A at 63 follow it too.
- **8, 24, 72:** D♮ is the 3rd of V7/V, so it is a chord tone and not a passing tone (c181, c391).
- **33 (not checked before):** D♮ is a chord tone of the common-tone diminished seventh D-F-A♭-C♭ over the A♭ pedal (Bsn 1 F, C.a. C♭).
- **68 (not checked before):** only E♭ (bass, timpani), B♭ (Bsn 1) and the horn sound. Here D♮ is a true chromatic passing tone, and the book's default spelling would be E♭♭.
  - It is kept as D♮ because this is the motto tail. Its spelling 5̂-♯4̂-4̂-3̂ is fixed across the movements (movement I: G-F♯-F-E).
  - Spelled D♮, it also reads as the leading tone of E♭ over the retransition's dominant pedal.
  - The book says "usually" (PDF 339). This is a motivic exception, not an error.
- **Fix:** none.

### F6. Minor-mode figuration in the quotations follows Ex. 20-24 (recorded)
- **C.a. 47-50 (F♯ minor):** G♯ at 48 is a lower neighbour over V and falls with the line. D♮ at 49 is the lowered 6̂ in the descent D-C♯. This is the "V chord motivates raised 7̂ in a descending line" case of PDF 340.
- **Tbn 1 63-64 (A minor):** G♯ is a lower neighbour of A, and B-B♭-A is a descending chromatic line spelled flat.
- Both quotations are protected. The check is recorded only.
- **Fix:** none.

### F7. The coda keeps the two cells that make theme A memorable (recorded)
- **Bars:** 83-90.
- **What happens:**
  - The harp plays the theme's head in harmonics at 83 (A♭-C-E♭), and the timpani answer with its skips reversed at 84.
  - Cl 1 plays the tail E♭-D-D♭-C at one note per bar (85-89). That is the enlargement of a figure across bars that Ex. 20-11b shows (PDF 334).
  - The listener's last melodic memory is the head and the tail, theme A's two identifying cells, in augmentation, over a plain plagal close.
- **Fix:** none.

### F8. The reprise climax 78-80: the B♭6 is an accented neighbour, not an appoggiatura (recorded; terminology only)
- **Bars:** 78-80 (Vln I, Fl 1, Vln II).
- **What happens:** C6, then F6-G6-A♭6 in sixteenth triplets, to B♭6 on the downbeat of 79. The run is an extended passing motion that includes chord tones (Ex. 20-10, PDF 333). B♭6 is approached by step, so by the book's definition (PDF 338) it is an accented upper neighbour (Ex. 20-14, 20-15, PDF 336), as c181 already said. The term "appoggiatura" in c001's record does not fit it.
- **The one difference from the theme:** in the theme the dissonant B♭ (12, 28, 76) is reached by the leap F-B♭. At the climax the same F-B♭ 4th (78 b3 to 79 b1) is filled in by passing tones.
  - A leap straight to B♭6 would echo bar 12 more exactly, but would lose the run that prepares the ff.
  - The run to B♭6 at 78 is protected (c251).
- **Fix:** none. Correct the record's wording only.
