## Findings

### 1. 41–44: the movement's one melody in parallel thirds and sixths (applied)

Problem. Cyclic-notes (movement I cycle 187) records that movement I never presents a melody in parallel thirds or sixths, so the colour is free for movements II and III. Movement II does not use it either: every doubled melody is in unison or octaves (theme B 41–44 Fl 1 + C.a. in octaves; 45–47 Ob 1/Bsn 1/Vc octaves; 53–56 violins in unison with Hn 1 = Cl 1; 57–60 and 75–81 octaves). The only sixths are the harmonic close of the clarinet tail at 87–89. Theme B's second phrase is also a weak case of Adler's dialogue principle (PDF 257–258): the flute that sang phrase 1 alone at 37–40 sits on top of phrase 2 in octaves, so phrase 2 sounds like phrase 1 with the cor anglais added under it, not like a repeat "in a radically different colour".

Evidence. Adler PDF 273 (Ex. XVII-13): Beethoven gives the melody to paired woodwinds moving in thirds, the clarinets of Op. 61/i bars 2–5 (`<a' c''>2 <bes' d''> | <g' bes'>2. <f' a'>4`). Dvořák puts his thirds in the middle section of 9/ii: clarinets 1·2 in thirds and sixths at 54–58 (parallel thirds at 55–56: Cl 1 `b4 a2 c4~ | c4 b4~ b8 a8~ a8 g8` over Cl 2 `g4 f2 a4~ | a4 g4~ g8 fs8~ fs8 e8`, written for A clarinet). Adler PDF 288 (Haydn): giving the doubling instrument a different line from the tune is a standard way to vary a restatement. Theme B is in D♭, the key the movement keeps for its middle section, so the new colour falls in the movement's warmest passage and stays clear of the C-major material reserved for III.

Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecFluteOne`, bar 41 b3 to 44). The cor anglais keeps the tune unchanged. Flute 1 moves from the octave to tenths and sixths above it:

```
% was: r2 f''4.(\< ges''8 | aes''4\mp\> bes''8 aes''8 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8 | es''4. f''8 des''4 bes'4 | c''2.)\p r4 |
r2 aes''4.(\< bes''8 | f''4\mp\> ges''8 f''8 es''4~ es''8 f''8 | ges''4. aes''8 f''4 es''4 | es''2.)\p r4 |
```

Intervals to the cor anglais: 41 tenths (A♭/F → B♭/G♭, a double appoggiatura over IV); 42 sixths (F/A♭, G♭/B♭, F/A♭, E♭/G♭ over vi7–ii7–V7, the 4–3 of c061 now doubled by 9–10 into an F octave on the last eighth); 43 tenths (G♭/E♭, A♭/F, F/D♭: 6–5 and 4–3 of a cadential 6/4); 44 E♭5 over C4. Fl 1 resolves E♭5 up to F5 at 42 b4.5, not to D♭5, because D♭5→G♭5 against the bass D♭2→G♭2 at 42→43 would be parallel octaves between the outer voices. The flute still enters at 41 b3, after the cor anglais has begun alone in its low register (c051's reason), and still adds weight above it. This revises c021/c051's octave doubling for these four bars only; 37–44 is not on the current do-not-touch list. The colour is used once; I considered oboes 1·2 in thirds at 19–20 (`aes'4( bes'8 aes'8 g'4 | f'2 e'4)` under Ob 1) and rejected it so the effect stays a single event.

### 3. Open item "bass rarely motivic" (critic 89): close it in movement II

Adler PDF 283 (Fidelio) and PDF 288 (Haydn) show the way a master makes a lower line heard: double it in another octave or colour. Movement II already does this for its one motivic low line, the cello counter-melody (23–30 alone, 71–78 with Hn 2 from 75, 79–82 with Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1, the hemiola at 80–81). The plan's own requirement (movement-2-plan, "a melodic bass counterline, cellos 23–30, 71–82") and cyclic-notes §3 (movement III: "one melodic bass counterline") are met. Making the double basses motivic would need a new passage, and the proportions are protected. Recommendation: close the item and leave a motivic bass, for example a bass statement of the motto tail, to movement III.

### 4. Open item "theme A never in the winds" (critic 89): close it

Adler's dialogue rule (PDF 257–258) asks that a tune, once stated by a soloist, be answered in a radically different colour. Theme A does this: solo horn with bassoons and bass clarinet (7–14), then the violins with the cellos' counter-line (23–30), then violins in octaves with woodwinds added for the peak (77–81). Adler's examples do not require each colour family to state the tune. A new woodwind statement would need staves in systems where the woodwinds are hidden (c089 found no room; HISTORY c077 records two such additions that pushed the score to 9 pages). Finding 1 gives the woodwinds a melodic moment of their own in the middle section. Recommendation: close the item.

### 5. 41–44: viola pizzicato inside the cor anglais's register (no change)

Adler PDF 274: keep the accompaniment registrally distant from a woodwind soloist; rhythmic contrast lessens the need. The violas' pizzicato triplets (A♭3–G♭4) lie in the cor anglais's octave (A♭3–B♭4), often on its pitches (42 b1: A♭3–D♭4–F4 under the tune's A♭4). The pizzicato and the triplet rhythm are the contrast Adler allows (c057 made this choice), and with finding 1 the tune now has a voice above it. Leave as is unless the composer hears the tune covered at 42–43. In that case drop the top note of each triplet at 42–43 (`aes8 des' r`-type figures), not the register.

### 6. Horns 3·4 end muted: relay to movement III (no change in movement II)

Hn 3·4 put mutes on at 82 ("mettere sord."), play con sord. at 87–90, and take nothing off before the end. Adler's examples mark where mutes come off as well as on (Bizet, PDF 280–281: "senza sordini"; Mahler, PDF 282: "con sord."). Movement III's first horn 3·4 entry must say "senza sord.".
