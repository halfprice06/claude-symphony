## What else I studied

Lens: I set themes A and B against the Dvořák 9/ii Largo tune in the local corpus (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/woods.notesL.ily, oboeIImL, lines 200-216; the solo Adler cites on PDF 206). Its first phrase spans a fifth, sounding D♭4-A♭4 (written A♭4-E♭5). It reaches its peak, D♭5, in the fifth of its eight bars. Its rhythmic cell, a dotted eighth, a sixteenth and a quarter, opens each of the first four bars.

Theme A (horn 7-14): the first phrase spans a fifth, A♭3-E♭4. The second phrase rises to B♭4 at bar 12, the sixth of eight bars, and then falls by step to A♭3. Over the whole movement it climbs in register: horn, violins an octave up at 23-30, violins two octaves up with the B♭6 peak at 79.

Theme B (Fl 1 37-40): the first phrase spans a minor sixth, B♭4-G♭5. The neighbour figure (half note or quarter, then two eighths) recurs in every bar, as Dvořák's cell does. The second phrase opens with the rising arpeggio A♭-D♭-F-G♭ (C.a. 41, then Fl 1 and violins at 57). That arpeggio is the shape a listener is most likely to keep, and it comes back at the climax.

Theme A is the tune a listener will hum, and theme B works as the middle tune. Earlier melody passes reached the same verdict (c291, c401, c421, c431), and I found nothing in Adler ch. 7 that changes it. I also looked at score pages 3-4 at 60-90 dpi, analysis.txt (ranges), c291, c356 and c416 (earlier passes over this chapter), and HISTORY entries c036, c101, c139, c271 and c276.

## Findings

There is no change to the score. The woodwind melodic writing follows Adler ch. 7 at every point I checked. The items below are recorded so the composer does not need to re-check them. They are ranked by how much they bear on the melody.

### 1. Bars 41-44: theme B's second phrase in the cor anglais under the flute counter-line (recorded, keep)

**Check.** The cor anglais plays the phrase listeners are most likely to remember, at sounding A♭3-B♭4, p<mp. Flute 1 plays above it in compound sixths, at pp<p, in its "clear and brilliant" register (PDF 185). Adler gives the cor anglais's lowest fifth or sixth "tremendous expressive carrying power" (PDF 205). The phrase starts in that band: A♭3 and D♭4 are written E♭4 and A♭4. The rest of the phrase lies lower than Dvořák's Largo solo (written A♭4-A♭5), which Adler cites as a model (PDF 206). So the tune sits where the instrument carries best. The flute is one dynamic level under it (c101). It is not an octave doubling, so Adler's warning on PDF 201 about one colour overshadowing another does not apply.

**Verdict.** Keep. A louder cor anglais is not needed. A softer flute (ppp) would drop the counter-line into the flute's weaker band without gain (PDF 185).

### 2. Bars 37-40: theme B's first statement in the flute's middle register (recorded, keep)

**Check.** Adler's Ex. VII-5 (PDF 186) is a D♭-major tune for flute in its "nondescript" middle register, p dolcissimo. It works "because of the care the composer has given the accompaniment". Bars 37-40 are the same: the same key, flute 1 p from F5 down to B♭4, and pp viola triplets, pp cellos, pizzicato basses and harp underneath. The C.a. eighth on F5 at 37 b1 keeps the rising fourth C5-F5 in one breath without colouring the flute's first note (c139, c271).

**Verdict.** Keep.

### 3. Bars 57-63 and 78-79: flute 1's top notes in the theme B climax and the A′ peak (recorded, keep)

**Check.** Adler says above A6 the notes are hard and are best approached by scale, and the top fourth is "most problematical" (PDF 186). B♭6 at 79 comes by the scale run C6-F6-G6-A♭6 at 78. A♭6 at 58 comes by step from G♭6. A6 at 63 comes by a semitone. A♭6 at 61 is a sixth up from C6, after a breath and at ff. That note is below A6, and the shrill top is meant to be played loud (PDF 185). The movement's registral peak, B♭6, comes once, at 79, as the plan intends (HISTORY line 609). Flute 1 rests for 64-76 after the first climax (PDF 188). c416 measured the 61-63 hold at about 10 s.

**Verdict.** Keep. If the composer ever wants Brahms's relief (Ex. VII-9), flute 2 is not free at 61-62, because it carries theme B's head an octave below. So the answer remains no change.

### 4. Bars 15-22: the oboe answers in the A section's middle phrase (recorded, keep)

**Check.** Oboe 1's two-bar answers (C5-D♭5-E♭5-F5-E♭5 at 15-16; C5-D♭5-C5-B♭4-A♭4-G4 at 19-20) lie in the "warm, prominent, reedy and poignant" band F4-C6 (PDF 198). Each is followed by two bars' rest, which the reed needs (PDF 198). They are lyrical in the manner of Schumann 2/iii (Ex. VII-34): p, a swell to the top note, then down.

**Verdict.** Keep. These answers are not meant to be the memorable part. They prepare the full violin statement of theme A at 23.

### 5. Theme A is never stated whole by a woodwind (recorded, no change)

**Check.** Adler opens the chapter by calling each woodwind "invaluable as a presenter of melodic material" (PDF 184). In this movement, theme A belongs to horn 1 (7-14) and the violins (23-30, 71-82). The woodwinds have it only as doublings (Fl 1, Cl 1 at 77-78; Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1 at 79-82), as the head in Cl 1 with Hn 1 at 53-56, and as the motto tail in augmentation, Cl 1 at 85-90. The Dvořák model gives its main tune to the cor anglais. Here the cor anglais is kept for theme B's second phrase and the movement I quotation, and that separation helps each tune keep its own colour.

**Verdict.** No change. A woodwind statement of theme A would need bars that the protected proportions (c143, c413) do not have.

### 6. Bars 31-36 and 85-92: bass clarinet solo and clarinet coda line (recorded, keep)

**Check.** The B.cl. solo (theme A's arpeggio and chromatic tail) lies in the chalumeau, sounding E♭2-E♭3. Adler names that register the instrument's most distinctive and warmest (PDF 217), and the solo is shaped p<mf>mp>pp, as in Ex. VII-74 and VII-75. The low B♭1 at 8 has its small-note ossia (PDF 216). In the coda, the clarinets' motto-tail line starts with the niente attack Adler gives to the clarinet (PDF 211). It stays above the break (written F5-D5). At 89, Cl 1 hands its C5 to Vln I, and Cl 2 enters niente on a held E♭4 (PDF 212; c430).

**Verdict.** Keep (protected: c431, c416).

### 7. Bassoon doublings of melodic lines at 45-46 and 78-82 (recorded, keep)

**Check.** Bassoon 1 doubles the cellos on theme B's B♭-minor head at 45-46 and the A′ counter-melody at 78-82. Adler says the cellos dominate in this doubling while the bassoon adds body (PDF 226). There is no exposed accompanied bassoon solo high in the register that could be swallowed.

**Verdict.** Keep.

No scratch copy was built, because no finding calls for a change. Nothing here touches the movement's ending, its exported themes or its quotations.

Do not touch: theme A's line, its three rising statements and the single B♭6 peak at 79; theme B's neighbour-note first phrase in Fl 1 and its arpeggio second phrase in the C.a. under the flute's sixths; the C.a. quotation 47-53; the clarinet niente hand-over 85-92; the standing list; the 8-page layout.
