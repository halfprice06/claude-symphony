## Findings

Unit 1 is the first unit of the book: it teaches scale degrees, stable and active tones, neighbours and passing tones, chromatic spelling, and the three forms of minor. Themes A and B follow it closely, and I found no violation that calls for a change. Findings 1-6 record why the points where the score departs from the textbook norm are deliberate. Finding 7 is the comparison with Dvořák. Finding 8 is an edit I tried and then rejected. Nothing was applied.

### 1. The motto tail E♭-D♮-D♭-C reverses A&S's normal chromatic order (8, 24, 33, 68, 72, 85-88; no change)

PDF 25 says a descending chromatic passing tone keeps the letter of the note before it, and the chromatic half step comes first: A-A♭-G. Read that way, the tail would be E♭-E♭♭-D♭. The score writes the diatonic half step first (E♭-D♮), then the chromatic one (D♮-D♭). PDF 22 calls ♯4̂ an active tone a half step below 5̂, and here it turns down instead of rising. Every statement has D♮ as a chord tone, not as a passing tone: the third of V6/5 of V at 8, 24, 72 (c002); the common-tone diminished seventh at 33 (protected); viiø7/V at 86 (c190, c203). So it is spelled for its harmony, which is what A&S do with the C♯s of Ex. 1-1, bar 9 (PDF 25: "not every chromatic tone produces a chromatic half step"). The tendency tone that turns away is what makes the tail recognisable. The tail now appears six times in six colours (c190). Do not add a seventh statement, and do not respell it.

### 2. Theme A's 7̂ resolves an octave lower at the cadence (13-14, 29-30, 81-82; no change)

The downbeat G (7̂) over V7 drops a third and the line ends on A♭ an octave below the G's own step: G4→A♭3 in the horn, G5→A♭4, G6→A♭5. PDF 22 says 7̂ is "very strongly attracted" to 8̂. But PDF 17 makes the two A♭s equivalent, and the 5̂-4̂-3̂-2̂-1̂ eighths that follow are the second segment of Ex. 1-10a (Brahms Op. 117/1, PDF 21). At 81 the upper A♭6 has already sounded on 80 b2 (B♭6-A♭6), so the top register is not left open. This is the protected cadence of theme A (c001, c012).

### 3. Minor-key lines follow the forms of minor; no augmented seconds (45-51, 15-22, 63-64; no change)

- B♭ minor, 45-47: the oboe/cello head D♭5-E♭5-D♭5-C5 | B♭4-A♮4-B♭4. Raised 7̂ is used as the lower neighbour of 1̂, because it moves to 8̂ (PDF 27; c091).
- F minor, 15-22: the oboe at 19-20 descends C-D♭-C-B♭-A♭-G with natural ♭6̂ (PDF 28, descending form), and Vln II's E♮4 at 20 rises to F4 at 21 (PDF 27).
- F♯ minor, 47-50: the C.a. quotation uses D♮→C♯ (6̂→5̂, the natural-minor half step of PDF 26-27).
- A minor, 63-64: Tbn 1 G♯ is a lower neighbour of A, and F→E is 6̂→5̂.

The scan found no melodic augmented 2nd in any part (PDF 27, Ex. 1-19). The one hit, bass B1-A♭1 at 50-51, sounds a minor 3rd (B-G♯) and is the protected respelling (c023). No theme ascends 6̂-7̂-8̂ in minor, so the ascending melodic form has no case to test.

### 4. Modal colour: two Phrygian ♭2̂ moments, both inside minor (49, 63; no change)

PDF 30 says that in this repertoire the other modes, "especially Phrygian", appear inside a larger major or minor context. The score does this twice. Vln II at 49 b3-4 plays G♮5-E♯5 around F♯5: ♭2̂ over B (the Neapolitan 6th of F♯ minor), then raised 7̂. Together the two notes form the double-neighbour group of Ex. 1-12 (PDF 23) around 1̂. Tbn 1 at 63 plays B-B♭-A, a ♭2̂ that is also a correctly spelled chromatic passing tone (same letter first, PDF 25). The themes themselves stay diatonic apart from the motto, and the mixture (F♭) lives in the inner voices and the bass. That matches PDF 25: chromatic elements "embellish a basically diatonic substructure". It is one reason both tunes are easy to sing back.

### 5. Chromatic spelling in every line (no change)

The chromatic passing tones follow PDF 25: C.a. C4-C♭4-B♭3 at 33, Vln I F6-F♭6 at 62, and Fl 1 A♭6-A♮6 at 62-63 (a chromatic half step with the same letter). One line has both ♭3̂ and ♮3̂ of D♭ within three beats: Hn 1/Vc at 59 b3-60, E♭-F♭ | E♭-F-G♭. This is the F♭-E♭ thread (protected; c173, c210), and F♭ returns to E♭ before F♮ arrives, so the mixture is a neighbour and not a cross-relation between voices. Keep.

### 6. Theme B holds back its tonic until the climax (37-61; protect)

This is the reading's central melodic idea (PDF 15-16), and the strongest feature of theme B. The first phrase ends on 6̂ over B♭ minor (40). The second ends on a half cadence (44) and turns deceptively to B♭ minor (45). The restatement reaches 7̂ C6 at 60 and holds it for a whole bar under a crescendo, which is the long leading tone of PDF 22, Ex. 1-1 ("notice how urgently the ear demands a continuation"). 1̂ arrives only at 61, ff. Theme A gives the opposite pattern on purpose: it closes on 1̂ at 14, 30 and 82. Its last close is delayed by the deceptive cadence at 78 and the B♭6 at 79. Keep both patterns. HISTORY already lists "theme B's consequent holding the leading tone to 61" (c091).

### 7. Comparison with Dvořák 9/ii (no change)

Dvořák's cor anglais tune (written for E.H. in F, sounding D♭) uses only stable tones 1̂-3̂-5̂ with passing 2̂ (PDF 20). It has one rhythm, a dotted eighth and sixteenth, in every bar, spans a fifth in bars 7-10, and ends bars 7 and 10 on 1̂ (woods.notesL.ily, lines 207-210). Theme A uses the same materials: a triad head (PDF 19) and one rhythmic cell (dotted quarter and eighth) at 7, 9 and 11. It adds a chromatic tail and a wider range, a ninth (A♭3-B♭4 in the horn). Theme B is built from neighbour notes (Ex. 1-9) on a descending line, like Ex. 1-10a. A listener can hum both. Neither needs simplifying.

### 8. Considered and rejected: Fl 1 D♭5 instead of E♭5 at 43 b4

The flute's tenths above the C.a. (41-44) break once: E♭5 over B♭3 on 43 b4 (an 11th) anticipates the E♭5 of 44. `des''4` would keep the tenths and make D♭ an incomplete lower neighbour of E♭ (PDF 22, Ex. 1-11). I do not recommend it. On 43 b4 the chord is V (violas A♭3-C4-E♭4, bass A♭ on b3). D♭5 would be a 4th over the bass that rises instead of falling to C, against the violas' C4 and E♭4. The present E♭ is consonant, and only the C.a.'s B♭ passes. The bars are also protected (c090: Fl 1 thirds/sixths 41-44). Not applied and not tested. The finale quotes only the C.a. line (finale 211-218), so neither version affects it.
