## Findings

Every finding below is a check with no change. This unit's principles have been applied to the melodies over five earlier passes, and v331 still meets each one. I found nothing in themes A or B that this reading shows to be wrong and that a change would improve.

### 1. Theme A's syncopations stay in the metre because the bass strikes every tied-over downbeat (recorded)
- **Bars:** 7–8 and 11–12 (horn), 23–24 and 27–28 (violins), 71–72 and 75–76.
- **Reading:** On PDF 51, a syncopation is heard as a conflict only against a metre that is still present. On PDF 47–48 (Ex. 3-1), the beat "persists as a background", and in the Mozart the left hand strikes each beat under the tied right-hand notes. In this score each theme A tie is answered by a bass attack on the downbeat: B.cl. F2 at 8 and D♭3 at 12, the cellos' F3 at 24 and 72, and the cellos and basses at 28 and 76. So the listener hears the tied E♭ and F as pushing against beat 1, not as moving the barline. The melody's hook (`4. 8 4~ | 4 4 4`) can be recognised only because of this.
- **Fix:** none. If the accompaniment of 7–14 is ever revoiced (open harp point, c317), keep a struck bass on beats 1 of 8 and 12.

### 2. Theme B's main tones move more slowly than theme A's under "Poco più mosso"; the motion comes from the bass and violas, as in Dvořák (recorded)
- **Bars:** 37–40 against 7–10.
- **Reading:** PDF 49 says felt tempo depends partly on "the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody". Theme A has about one main tone per beat at ♩=52 (about 1.15 s each). Theme B's first phrase has about one per half bar at ♩=60: F (37 b1–2), E♭ (b4), D♭–E♭ (38), G♭–E♭ (39), C–B♭ (40), about 2 s each. Taken alone, the melody would sound slower than theme A. The quicker tempo is carried by the pizzicato basses and harp in quarters (Cb `des,4 r4 des,4 r4`, 37–40) and by the viola triplets.
- **Comparison:** Dvořák's Largo tune has the same proportions. Each two-bar group closes on a half note on beat 3 (corpus lines 208, 210: `bf2`, `af2`), as theme B's bars 38 and 40 do (`es''2`, `bes'2` on beat 3). Dvořák's poco più mosso middle section also gets its motion from the accompaniment, not from the tune. c238 recorded the harmonic-rhythm side of this point and left a bass-rhythm option open. For the melody, nothing is needed: a broad tune over a quicker accompaniment is how the model works, and it makes theme B easy to sing.
- **Fix:** none.

### 3. The 15–22 dialogue is four separate two-bar groups; the viola answers join them (recorded)
- **Bars:** 15–16 (Ob 1 `c''4. des''8 es''4 | f''2 es''4`), 17–18 (solo Vln), 19–20 (Ob 1), 21–22 (solo Vln).
- **Reading:** On PDF 52–53, rhythmic groups may extend over more than a bar (Ex. 3-10) or overlap (Ex. 3-11, Mendelssohn Op. 85/4: the last note of one group is the first of the next). Each of the four melody groups ends with a half note on the downbeat of a weak bar (16, 18, 20, 22), and the next group starts on the following strong bar. On their own the four groups only touch end to end. The viola answers at 16, 18, 20 and 22 fill each half-note ending, so the parts overlap across the voices (c017). The b phrase therefore reads as one eight-bar unit (two four-bar hypermeasures, PDF 53–54) and not as four fragments.
- **Comparison:** Dvořák 9/ii bars 11–14 (corpus line 211–212) is also built from repeated two-bar units, each ending on a half note. The short groups are the model's own. They also set off theme A's longer upbeat groups at 22–30.
- **Fix:** none. 15–22 is on the do-not-touch lists (for example c127).

### 4. The joins between groups give each theme entry a clear start (recorded)
- **Bars:** 14→15, 22→23, 30→31, 36→37, 52→53, 70→71.
- **Reading:** PDF 52 (Ex. 3-9a/b) describes groups that start on the downbeat and groups that start on an upbeat. Theme A always enters from an upbeat (`es8 f8` at 6, 22, 70), and the passages between always start on a downbeat (oboe at 15) or after beat 1 (B.cl. at 31 b2). Theme B starts on a downbeat at 37 and 41 and from a pickup at 52 b4. Because the two themes start their groups differently, a listener can tell them apart from rhythm alone. The upbeat into theme A is one of its most recognisable features.
- **Fix:** none.

### 5. Both themes pass the Exercise 3 test (recorded)
- **Reading:** PDF 57, Exercise 3: from a bare melody, the reader should be able to supply the time signature and barlines.
- **Theme A** (Vln I 22 b3–30, no accompaniment): long values fall on 23 b1 (dotted quarter), 25 b1 (dotted quarter) and 26 b1 (half). The tied notes begin on beat 3, and the cadence `aes'2.` comes at 30. Only 3/4 with the barlines as written fits these.
- **Theme B** (Fl 1 37–40, C.a. 41–44): the half notes fall on 37 b1, 38 b3 and 40 b3, and the closing `c'2.` is at 44 b1. Only 4/4 with the barlines as written fits. A tune whose metre can be heard from the melody alone is one a listener can sing back. Both themes meet the standard of Ex. 3-13 (Beethoven), where the metre is clear from the line.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Hypermetric placement of the climaxes is correct (recorded)
- **Bars:** 57–61 (A♭6 at 58 b1 on a weak bar, then the strong-bar arrival at 61) and 75–79 (B♭ on beat 2 of weak bar 76, then the downbeat B♭6 on strong bar 79).
- **Reading:** On PDF 53, bars 1 and 3 of a four-bar group are strong. Ex. 3-13 puts the phrase's high point inside the second hypermeasure, on its strong bar. Here both peaks fall on the first bar of a new four-bar group (61, 79), after a first touch of the high note on a weak bar or beat. This is c171's finding 1, still true in v331.
- **Fix:** none.
