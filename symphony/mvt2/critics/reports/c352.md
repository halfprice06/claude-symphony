## Findings

### 1. Bars 19 b3–20 b1: vii°6 of F minor as a neighbour chord. Keep; the reason is now in Unit 7's own terms

**Situation.** Over the cellos' F3–G3–F3, bar 19 b3 has E4 (violas), G4 (Vln II) and B♭4 (Ob 1): vii°6 of F minor, a neighbour chord to i (Ex. 7-13a, PDF 113). It has a d5, E4–B♭4. At 20 b1 the oboe resolves B♭4→A♭4, but the violas' leading tone E4 leaps to C4 instead of rising to F4.

**Evidence.** The summary box (PDF 115) says that when VII6 goes to I5/3 a diminished 5th "must resolve" and "7 will normally move to 8". §11 (PDF 113) qualifies this: "Very frequently, the tendency of the tritone to resolve regularly (7 to 8 and 4 to 3) is offset by other considerations such as achieving a complete tonic chord or stepwise voice leading." Here F4 would leave the F-minor chord at 20 without its fifth (the only other voices are F3 and A♭4). It would also break the violas' C4–D♭4–C4–B♭3, which answers the oboe (c017). c292 kept this using Unit 22. Unit 7 itself names the reason, a complete tonic chord, so the case is settled under both units. The doubling also follows the reading: the bass G (2̂) is doubled by Vln II, and the leading tone is single (PDF 112 §10).

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bar 74: VII6 → V4/3 → I. Keep, including the viola's G4 at 74 b2

**Situation.** On 74 b1 the sounding chord is B♭–D♭–G (Cb, harp, Bsn and Vc on B♭; Cl 2 and violas on D♭4; violins G4/G5): VII6 of A♭. The violas' E♭4 at b1.5 completes V4/3 (c060, c073), and the bar goes to root-position I at 75.

**Evidence.** Doubling: 2̂ is doubled, as PDF 112 §10 recommends. Resolution: D♭4 lies below G4, so the tritone is an augmented 4th. Clarinet 2 takes D♭4→E♭4 against the melody's G→A♭, which is the A4→P4 motion of Ex. 7-15 (PDF 114) and the summary box (PDF 115). The violas take D♭4→C4, the normal 4̂–3̂. The melody's G leaves by E♭4–F4 (theme A's pickup) and reaches A♭4 at 75 b1, a resolution delayed by an arpeggio. The violas' figure `des'( es' g') es'( bes des')` touches G4 on b2 for an eighth, in unison with Vln II. Strictly read, PDF 112 §10 forbids that doubling. I tried the alternatives against the score. B♭3 on b2 rubs a second against the cellos' passing A♭3 and restores the viola–cello coincidence c127 removed. B♭4 crosses above Vln II's melody. D♭4 doubles the seventh. The G4 is an arpeggiated chord tone in an accompaniment figure, not a held voice, and it lies under two octaves of the same pitch in the melody. c324's rule for this figure was that it must not strike the only sounding octave of the melody, and here the melody sounds in two octaves.

**Fix.** None. Recorded so that the reading's §10 is not applied to it again.

### 3. Bar 53 b3–54 b1: V6/5 of D♭ to IV6. Keep (unchanged since c212)

Ex. 7-10b (PDF 111) calls a V6 whose leading tone "can't resolve" "less good". At 53 b3 the cellos' C3 falls to B♭2 under IV6, as part of theme B's falling bass D♭–C–B♭–G♭ (the protected falling-third sequence at 37–40 and 53–56). The scan confirms that c212's harp fix holds: C sounds only in the bass from 53 b3 to 54 b1, so there is no doubled leading tone (PDF 108 §4), and no upper voice is left holding an unresolved 7̂. The seventh G♭5 in the violins resolves to F5 on 53 b3.5. In a falling stepwise bass, 7̂ is a passing note, not a tendency tone. No change.

### 4. Bars 8→9, 24→25, 72→73: no V7–I6. Keep the root-position tonic

The scan finds no V7 followed by I6 anywhere in the movement. After each motto tail the seventh D♭ falls to C over a bass E♭→A♭: root-position V7–I with 3̂ on top. Ex. 7-7 (PDF 109) rules out the obvious "softer" alternative, a C in the bass on these downbeats, because 4̂–3̂ against 5̂–3̂ makes hidden octaves "particularly if I6 is in a rhythmically strong position". The score already uses the reading's other way to avoid a cadential effect in mid-phrase, a soprano on 3̂ instead of 1̂ (PDF 115 §12, PDF 117). The same applies to the mid-phrase dominants at 42 b3→b4 (top 2̂ with the C.a.'s 4–3) and 52 b4→53 (top 3̂). No change.

### 5. Barline changes of position and carried chords: all deliberate (Ex. 7-18)

PDF 115 §13 warns that moving between two positions of one chord from a weak to a strong beat, or repeating it across the barline, makes the weak beat sound stronger. The barline scan finds six cases.
- 14 b3 → 15 b1 (I → I6). The tonic arrives on 14 b1, so no new chord sits on a weak beat. 15 opens the b-phrase with I6, which keeps the tonic going without a second root-position beginning: the Schumann case of Ex. 7-6 (PDF 109).
- 11→12, 27→28, 75→76 (IV from b3 across the barline). The melody's F is tied over the barline, so the syncopation belongs to the theme. Both the accompaniment and the bass re-strike on the downbeat (harp, bassoons, and the cellos at 28 and 76), which supplies the emphasis Ex. 7-18c asks for. This was already recorded by c157, c163 and c212.
- 79→80 (ii6/5 held). This is the hemiola (c048).
- 87 → 88 (IV6 → iv). The mode changes (F→F♭), so the downbeat brings a new chord colour. The passage is the protected plagal close.
No change.

### 6. Bars 24 b3 and 72 b3: leading tone in two voices on root-position V7 (outside Unit 7; record only)

Scan (a) also shows the cellos' G3 (counter-melody) and the violas' G4 (figure) together on root-position V7 at 24 b3 and 72 b3.5. Unit 7 forbids doubling 7̂ only in V6 and VII6 (PDF 108 §4, PDF 112 §10, Points 3 and 5). The cellos' G3 rises to A♭3 at 25 and 73, so the leading tone resolves in the voice that carries the line. The violas' G4 is the top of an arpeggio that the c007 figure needs. Bar 72's sevenths are on the standing list. No change.
