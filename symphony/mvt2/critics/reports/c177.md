## Findings (ranked)

None of the findings needs a change. Every bar of v177 now falls under a protection in HISTORY.md. I checked each one against the reading and found no reason strong enough to answer the stated reasons. The findings below close two old rejections with new evidence and record four points so later critics do not raise them again.

**F1. Bars 19–20: the third gesture of the dialogue. No change; close c077 F1 for good.**
Adler PDF 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration. This is often true of any sequential material." Bar 19 is the third two-bar gesture (Ob 15, solo Vln 17, Ob 19). Critic 077 gave 19–20 to clarinet 1, and that was rejected because the score went to 9 pages. I repeated the test on v177 (`aOneOboeOne` rests at 19–20; new `aOneClOne = { R2.*12 | c''4(\p\< des''8 c''8 bes'4 | aes'2\> g'4)\! | R2.*10 | }`). The build has 0 warnings, but the score is again 9 pages, so the reason for rejecting it still holds. The principle is already met another way. Compared with 15–16, the accompaniment at 19–20 changes in three places: Vln II drops its pedal at 20 b1–2 and re-enters on E♮4 (c157); the viola answer moves from `des'4.( c'8 bes4)` to `c'4( des'8 c'8 bes4)`; and the cellos move F–G | F–C instead of C | D♭–E♭. That is Adler's "a bit different" with the same voice. I recommend the history close this item.

**F2. Bars 14→15: winds into strings without an overlap. No change; close c077 F2.**
Adler PDF 252, Ex. VIII-18 (Brahms 2/iii): "the woodwind chord dissolves into a string chord". I re-tested tying both bassoons into 15 (`aes2.)~ | aes2\> r4\!` and `c2.)~ | c2\> r4\!`): 0 warnings, 9 pages. That rejection reason also stands. The overlap is already there in the sound. The harp's undamped chord at 14 b2 (A♭3–C4–E♭4 over A♭1–E♭2, with no étouffez) still rings at the barline. The strings enter at 15 on the same chord, A♭4 / E♭4 / C3 (I6), so the harp's resonance does what Brahms's held wind chord does, at no cost in layout. Record and close.

**F3. Bars 71–78: the viola figure strikes Vln II's held note. No change; record.**
On 71 b2, 74 b2, 75 b2 and 78 b2 the top note of the viola eighth-note figure (A♭4, G4, A♭4, A♭4) is the pitch that Vln II is holding in the theme's lower octave. At 71 and 75 clarinet 2's A♭4 is there too. Adler PDF 265–266 says at-pitch doublings can thicken a line. But Adler PDF 262–264 (Ex. VIII-35, *La Mer*) describes this exact effect, one instrument pulsing pitches that another holds, as a way to animate a sustained line. Critic 077 closed the same question for 37–40 and 75–77 on that page. The figure is pp/p and the theme's held notes last a dotted quarter or more, so the result is a light pulse on the lower octave. It does not double the line. Critic 144 already found no clean register for Cl 2 (F3), and the figure is protected (c107). Record this as Adler's pulsation, not a doubling fault.

**F4. Wind counterpoint: no imitation anywhere. No change; record.**
Adler PDF 271: "since each of the woodwinds has such a unique color, contrapuntal writing, especially rhythmical, is very effective". Ex. VIII-42 (Mozart 38/iii) has the oboe answered by the bassoons. This movement has no strict imitation. Its counterpoint is of three kinds: counter-melodies (cellos 23–30 and 71–82; Hn 1/Cl 1 then Hn 1/Vc/Bsn 1 at 53–60), dialogue (15–22) and a chain of answers (83–89: harp head, timpani mirror, Cl 1 tail, Vln I). The reading's own phrase "individual winds play imitatively with strings or other wind instruments" fits 15–22 and 83–89. The main model does the same: Dvořák 9/ii has no fugal writing, and its one exchange between winds (oboe and flute, bars 90–95) is a dialogue of the same kind as 15–22. A new imitative entry could only go in 37–60, which is closed by the c124 rule, or in 71–82, which is protected. Either would also add a staff to a full system. Record: the slow movement's counterpoint is of the dialogue and counter-melody kind by design.

**F5. Winds doubling strings at pitch: full inventory. No change; record.**
Adler PDF 265–266 prefers octave doublings, but keeps the flute on Vln I where "the line goes up" (Berlioz, Ex. VIII-37). PDF 267 (Brahms 1/ii) approves a single bassoon on the theme. PDF 270 (Copland) approves a muted horn doubling a string part. My unison script lists every wind–string unison outside the ff tutti bars (61–62, 79–81). Each one fits one of these cases:
- the rising theme, Fl 1 = Vln I and Ob 1 = Vln II, at 57–60 and 77–78 (Berlioz);
- Bsn 1 with the cellos under Ob 1 at 45–47 (Brahms and Schumann, PDF 259, 267);
- Bsn 1 + Hn 2 + Vc on the counter-melody at 75–78 (c094's three-colour unison);
- muted Hn 4 with the violas at 87–88 (Copland);
- Cl 2's held A♭4 at 71–75 (recorded by c144).

No soft wind–string unison sits on a falling or static line outside these cases.

**F6. Spacing and inversion of the wind-coloured chords. No change; record.**
Adler PDF 257 (Ex. VIII-24, 25): the widest gap at the bottom, the top close, and a first inversion's bass doubled low.
- 3–6: A♭2–E♭3–A♭3–C4 (gaps of a 5th, a 4th and a 3rd, widest at the bottom).
- 89–92 (final chord): A♭1–A♭2–E♭3–E♭4–A♭4–C5.
- 87, IV6: F is doubled only in the lower half (F1, F2, F3 in the violas and Hn 4, F4 in the cor anglais), and D♭5 stands alone on top. This is the "open" first inversion Adler asks for.

All three follow the rule. With the cor anglais and muted horns this is a dark, soft shade for a chord progression that is the point of the passage (PDF 255–256, 258).

**F7. All-voice parallel scan. Clean; record.**
The scan covers the middle notes of chords that allpairs_par.py does not check. It found no parallel fifths. It found one octave pair: the viola's last eighth G3 at 78 b3½ leaps to B♭4 at 79 while flute 1 and the violins go G6 to B♭6. This is not a real parallel. On the real attack times the run is F6–G6–A♭6 in triplet sixteenths, so the flute and violins are on A♭6 when the viola's G3 sounds. The viola reaches the theme's third octave by a leap from below, as c071 planned. There is nothing to fix.
