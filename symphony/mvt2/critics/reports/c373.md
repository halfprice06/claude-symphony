## Findings

I found nothing in this reading that the score violates and that a change would fix. I list what I checked, ranked by how much each matters to the form, so the composer can record it. No change is proposed, so nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-373/work. That copy is identical to v373, whose build-warnings.txt is empty and whose analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels. The parts book was not regenerated because nothing changed.

**1. Confirming D♭ (37-61): no change.** The reading allows a new tonic that "sets in without such preparation, and it is confirmed later by its dominant" (PDF 216), and lets the cadence come well after the key change (PDF 219, Ex. 14-1 and 14-5). D♭ arrives by phrase modulation at 37 (PDF 223, Ex. 14-11). Its first V7-I is on weak beats at 42 b3-4 (A♭-C-G♭-E♭ to D♭). Bar 44 is a half cadence on A♭. The full cadence, with the top voice going 7̂-1̂ (C6 to D♭6 in Vln I) and the bass 4̂-♯4̂-5̂ before it (59-60, PDF 215), is held back to 61. Ex. 14-13 asks for the cadence in the new key "midway" (PDF 227). Here it falls at 61 of 92 bars, which is late on purpose, so that the 53-61 build has a goal. c93 and c243 protected "D♭ cadence held back to 59-61", and I agree with them. An earlier PAC in D♭ at 44 would take that goal away.

**2. Half cadences at 10, 26 and 44 are diatonic: no change.** PDF 215 (Ex. 14-4) says an applied chord with ♯4̂ makes a much stronger semicadence than IV6 or ii, which leave V "wanting to continue". The score uses the strong kind only where it wants a strong one: 21-22, through the augmented sixth with D♮5 to E♭5 on top, ending the b phrase, and 59-60 for the climax. Within the periods (10, 26) and at the end of B1 (44), the next phrase follows at once, so a weaker semicadence that "wants to continue moving" is the right one. This agrees with c184 item 7.

**3. Leaving the foreign area (63-71): follows, no change.** This is the passage the reading's return rules address most directly (PDF 219-220). The last foreign chord, 64 b4, has two meanings (E7 in A minor, German sixth of A♭). The dominant then gets its seventh (D♭, 4̂ of A♭) so that it stops sounding like a goal. In 65-70 the D♭ is in Vln II at 65-66, the horn's tail at 68 and Bsn 2 at 69-70, so the seventh never drops out for more than a bar. The return at 71 puts root-position I "in a place of some prominence" (PDF 219). Already protected (c243).

**4. ♯4̂ doubling: follows.** PDF 214 forbids doubling ♯4̂. At every applied dominant of V (8 b2, 24 b2, 59 b3, 72 b2), the raised degree is in only one voice: the melody (with its orchestral octave at 24 and 72) or the bass line (Cb, B.cl. and Bsn 2 in octaves at 59). No inner part takes it as a second, independent voice.

**5. Proportion against Dvořák 9/ii: follows.** Unit 14 says the contrast of a second key gives the "impression of departure and return" (PDF 220). The departure here takes 37% of the beats (39% of the clock time). Dvořák's takes about 44% of the bars, and his return is also shortened. A′ at 71-82 is half the length of A (7-30), and the coda (83-92) varies the final cadence plagally. That matches the "tiny coda ... with a varied repetition of the final cadence" in Ex. 14-1 (PDF 220). c363 recorded the same proportions from Adler.

**6. Phrase modulation at 37 rather than a pivot: follows.** PDF 223 says that when a B section starts directly in the new key with new rhythm and texture, "the notion of a pivot chord is not very helpful". At 36-37 the score changes metre (3/4 to 4/4), tempo (poco rit., then Poco più mosso), theme and scoring together. The A♭ chord of 31-36 still works as a long-range pivot (I of A♭ = V of D♭, PDF 217, 218), so the join is both sectional and smooth. Protected ("A♭ to A♭7 pivot 31-36").
