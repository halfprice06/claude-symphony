## Findings

I found nothing that the reading shows to be wrong and that a change would clearly improve. The findings below are new checks from this pass, ranked by how much each matters to the score's expression. All are recorded with no change.

### 1. Bars 53–64: the tempo words match the written-in harmonic rhythm (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 49: the pace of the beats "relates to tonal movement, to the frequency with which chords change, or to the rate of motion of the main tones of the melody".
- **Score.** Bass changes per bar, from the bass line actually sounding:
  - 53–56 (Vc): 2, 2, 2, 2 (halves). Marked "a tempo, poco a poco animando", MIDI 60–61.
  - 57 (Cb): 2. 58: 4 (`bes,,4 es,4 aes,,4 des,4`, one chord per beat). MIDI 62.
  - 59: 2 (`ges,,2 g,,2`), f, MIDI 64. 60: 1 (A♭ held), "poco allargando", MIDI 62–58.
  - 61: 1, 62: 2. "Largamente" ♩=54, tenutos.
  - 63: 1, 64: 3 (A, then D–E). "allargando" at 64.
- **Verdict.** The chords speed up through 57–58, the fastest harmonic rhythm in the movement, inside the animando. They broaden at 59–60 under the allargando and are slowest at 61–62 under the Largamente. The written-in tempo and the printed tempo move together, which is the reading's point that felt tempo follows chord change. The pedal passages work the same way. Over the A♭ and E♭ pedals at 31–36, 65–70 and 83–86 the felt pace is already slow, and the only slowing words there (35, 89) come where the chord stops changing. Dvořák 9/ii also puts its tempo words only at seams.
- **Fix:** none.

### 2. Syncopations and suspensions: the dynamic peak sits on the syncopated dissonance, and the diminuendo on its resolution (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 51: a note begun weak and held through a strong beat "conflicts—sometimes very strongly—with the meter"; Ex. 3-6 marks *sf* on that note. PDF 55: a suspension "originated as a consequence of syncopation" and almost always falls on an accented beat (Ex. 3-16, 3-17a).
- **Score.**
  - 58, Fl 1, Ob 1, Vln I·II: G♭ enters on beat 2½ and is held through beat 3 and beat 4 (`aes'''4. ges'''8~ ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8`). Over A♭ (beat 3) it is the seventh; over D♭ (beat 4) it is a 4–3 suspension that resolves to F on 4½. The crescendo from 57 runs to beat 4 (hidden mf in Fl 1, hidden f in Vln I), and the one-eighth diminuendo falls on the F. So the loudest point is the suspension and the release is the resolution, as Ex. 3-17a's "lengthened" tone would be played.
  - 7–8, 23–24, 71–72: the tied E♭ (theme A) crescendos into the barline, and the diminuendo starts on beat 1. Over the F bass of vi7 at 8/24/72 the E♭ is the held seventh, a prepared dissonance across the barline.
  - 11–12, 27–28: the tied F crescendos into 12/28, and the printed peak (mp, f) is on beat 2, where the B♭ enters.
  - 80–81, Hn 3 F♭: `fes'4~(\<` into the 9–8 at 81, diminuendo to E♭ (c238 recorded; protected V♭9).
- **Verdict.** Every syncopated dissonance in the score is shaped toward its strong-beat dissonance and away from its resolution. No sf is needed on top of this: these are legato lines in an Adagio, and the hairpins give the same emphasis that Ex. 3-6's sf gives in an Allegro molto.
- **Fix:** none.

### 3. Bars 37–64: the 4/4 hierarchy (beat 1 strong, beat 3 weaker) is supported in every layer except where it is cancelled on purpose (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 50: quadruple metre "has a primary emphasis on the first beat and a weaker emphasis on the third beat"; "the inner organization of a divided beat mirrors in miniature the metrical organization of a measure".
- **Score.**
  - 37–39: pizzicato basses and harp LH on beats 1 and 3 only. Viola triplets slurred by half bar (six notes per slur). Vln II holds halves. Theme B puts its long notes on beat 1 (F5 at 37) and beat 3 (E♭5 at 38, B♭4 at 40).
  - 59–60: viola sextuplets slurred by beat, 3+3 inside each beat. This is the divided beat that mirrors a triple bar.
  - 61–62: a tenuto on every half note in winds, brass and strings ("Largamente", "molto sostenuto"). Beats 1 and 3 become equal, so the bar is felt as a broad 2/2. That is the purpose of the marking at the climax, and it gives up the 4/4 hierarchy for only two bars.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Metre changes: only two, both at double bars with a tempo word (recorded; no change)
- **Reading.** PDF 50: changing metres occur much less often in nineteenth-century music.
- **Score.** 3/4 → 4/4 at 37 ("Poco più mosso") and back at 65 ("Tempo I"). Each change marks a section, as Dvořák 9/ii's change of tempo marks its sections. The bar lengths are 3.46 s (3/4 at 52), 4.0 s (4/4 at 60) and 4.44 s (4/4 at 54). So the four-bar hypermeasure of the middle section is longer in seconds than that of A, while its beat is faster.
