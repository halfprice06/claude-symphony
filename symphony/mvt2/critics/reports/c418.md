## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 79: the second peak has no sustaining direction in the strings (applied)

**Bars.** 79-80, all five string sections.

**Problem.** At 61 every string part reads "molto sostenuto" (c028, from this reading). At 79, the movement's registral peak (B♭6 in Vln I, three octaves with Vln II and violas, cellos and basses ff underneath), the strings have only ff. The violins hold B♭6 for four beats at poco allargando (crotchet about 48), down-bow then up-bow on the tie; the violas hold B♭4 the same length; the cellos play three separate ff quarters. Without a word, a long ff down-bow high on the E string tends to be played with a strong attack and a decay, and the separate cello quarters tend to be detached. The peak then fades before the resolution to A♭6 at 80 b2, earlier than the written diminuendo at 81. The two climaxes are different in kind (c238), but both need the same instruction to keep full weight through the long notes.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38, Ex. XI-20 (image): at Franck's climactic restatement each string part reads "f molto sosten.", and this climax too is made "not by the brass but by the rest of the orchestra plus horns in their woodwind capacity", which is exactly how 79 is scored (no trombones, horns f with the counter-melody). Tchaikovsky's tutti (PDF 39-40) also holds every long note at the printed top level until a hairpin begins. The score already follows Franck at 61; 79 is the one ff string arrival without it.

**Fix (applied, sections/aTwo.ily, bar 79):**

- `aTwoVlnTwo`: `bes''2.~\ff\downbow^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto" |`
- `aTwoVla`: `bes'2.~\ff-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto" -\tag #'part _\markup \italic "molto sosten." |`

Nothing else changes: no notes, hairpins, bowings or tempo. No tempo word is added, so global.ily needs no `\mt`.

### 2. Bar 82: Ob 1 and C.a. fade to niente on b3 while flutes, clarinets and Vln I hold pp (no change; closes c416's optional item)

**Bars.** 82.

**Point.** c416 left open whether Ob 1 and the cor anglais should hold pp to the barline like the flutes, clarinets, bassoons, Vln I and lower strings. I recommend leaving them as they are. The harp's harmonic pickup enters at 82 b3 at p. Adler's dovetail rule (PDF 192, Ex. XV-40) asks that the outgoing colour recede as the new one enters, and the subito-piano rule (PDF 190) asks only that a soft layer already be in place, which the pp flutes, clarinets and strings provide. The double reeds have the most penetrating tone in the held chord (PDF 188: dynamic and register decide the choice). Ending them, with Vln II, the violas and horns 1·2, exactly where the harmonics start is the staged release protected since c168. Changing them to pp would undo part of c028's finding 2, which was made so the harp is not covered.

### 3. Bars 61-64: the main climax and the quotation (follows; no change)

61 b1 carries the top dynamic in every staff (Tchaikovsky, PDF 39-40); the brass are saved for 60-64 and the trumpets for later movements (Schubert, PDF 37); the strings are "molto sostenuto" with tenuti (Franck, PDF 38); the timpani roll is damped before the A-minor bar. At 63 the tutti breaks off and Tbn 1 brings in the quotation ff marcato with accents on its long notes, over a sforzando made as PDF 189-190 recommends (Cb pizz. sf and a short Fl 1 sf against sf > p string tremolo), then diminuendo to p at 64 so the pp at 65 is not an exposed seam (PDF 190). Horns 1·2 ff against horns 3·4 and low brass f is a protected balance (c218, c264).

### 4. Solos, register and dynamic (follows; no change)

Every solo is printed with a dynamic and a word, and each dynamic fits its register (PDF 188): Hn 1 p "dolce espr." on A♭3-B♭4 at 7; B.cl. p "solo, espr." in its chalumeau at 31; Fl 1 p "dolce cantabile" at 37; C.a. p "solo, espr." on C♯4 at 47; Hn 1 p "dolce" on E♭4 at 67; Vln I p "espr." at 70; Cl 1 from niente to pp "dolce" on E♭5 at 85; harp harmonics p over ppp strings at 83. The high points carry < > (37-40, 47-50, 67-68), as in Ex. XV-33.

### 5. Held notes and joins (follows; no change)

The C at 1-3 passes from Vln I to horn 3; the four solo basses' E♭ at 65-70 is joined at 67 by the timpani roll near the rim; the C5 at 89 from Cl 1 to Vln I on the same downbeat, with the clarinet fading under the violins (Ex. XV-37). New colours enter below the current level and grow: winds p < under the violins' mf at 57 and mp < at 77, brass pp < at 60, Cbsn pp under the strings' p > pp at 30 (PDF 187, 190).
