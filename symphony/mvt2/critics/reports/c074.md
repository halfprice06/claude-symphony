## Findings

### 1. Bars 63–64: cello tremolo sits inside trombone 1's quotation (applied)
Problem: the quotation (Tbn 1, E3–C4: E3 C4 B3 B♭3 A3 | A3 G♯3 A3 F3 E3) is the foreground. The cellos' A3 tremolo is in unison with its A3 on 63 b4 and 64 b1–2 and a semitone from its B♭3 and G♯3. The violas' C4–E4 lie at the top of the line, but the cellos are inside it. This answers the open item "Vc tremolo register 63–64" (c067).
Evidence: Adler PDF 144, point 3: theme and countertheme separated registrally; dynamics (here sf>p against ff) are "not always as effective". PDF 161, Ex. V-37 (Bizet): the tremolo is placed away from the cello tune's register. PDF 135: the background is kept out of the melody's register.
Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVc):
```
... | f'2-- fes'2-- | a,1:32\sf\> | a,2:32\p d4( e4) |
```
(was `a1:32\sf\> | a2:32\p d4( e4)`). The cellos double the basses' A an octave up, the bass trombone's A2 at unison. The tremolo, the sf>, Tbn 1 and the violas are unchanged, so the c047/c058 protections ("Tbn 1 over tremolo", "sf> tremolo") hold.

### 2. Bars 81–82: the violas' octave of the theme runs into and under the counter-melody (applied)
Problem: since c071 the violas take the theme's third octave at 79–82. At 81 they descend G4 E♭4 D♭4 C4 B♭3 to A♭3 at 82, while the counter-melody (Vc, Hn 1·2, Bsn 1) holds D♭4 and falls to C4. The violas meet it in unison on D♭4 (81 b2.5) and end a third below it (A♭3 under C4 at 82). The theme's lowest octave is buried in, and then crosses under, the middleground in the same string colour.
Evidence: Adler PDF 144, point 3 (separate theme and countertheme registrally) and point 2 (thin the counterpoint so the main theme breaks through); PDF 142 (the three layers heard in order of priority). The theme continues in two octaves (Vln I/Fl 1, Vln II/Ob 1/Cl 1), so the violas' third octave is not needed for the descent.
Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoVla):
```
bes'2.~\ff^\markup \italic "unis." | bes'4(\upbow aes'2) | g'4(\> es'2) | es'2\p\> r4\! |
```
(was `g'4(\> es'8 des'8 c'8 bes8) | aes2\p\> r4\!`). E♭4 is the root of V7 and the fifth of I, it is held, and it releases on 82 b3 as c063 requires. The release timing (protected "release 81–83") is unchanged. Only the violas' pitches on 81 b2 to 82 change, which answers c071's reason (stress the peak in three octaves at 79–80).

### 3. Bars 15–20: the double basses double every bass note of the chamber dialogue (applied)
Problem: at 15–22 the texture is oboe, solo violin, muted violas and cellos at pp, a light conversation. The basses double the cellos at 16' in every bar from 15, so the lightest string passage of the A section has the full string bass.
Evidence: Adler PDF 130–131, Ex. V-6: in a lighter texture the bass is omitted and brought back at the dominant pedal and the cadence. The model movement does the same: Dvořák 9/ii, basses rest under the cor anglais tune (bars 7–14) and return for the cadence (15–18). The cellos' division at 18 already keeps half the section on the bass (c067).
Fix (aOne.ily, aOneCb, bars 15–22):
```
R2.*6 |
des,2\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." bes,,4 | es,2. |
```
The basses enter at 21 for the approach to the half cadence at 22 and stay for 23–30, so the 23–30 plain statement (protected) is unchanged.

### 4. Bar 79: cellos ff on the counter-melody, horns f (applied)
Problem: at the A′ peak the counter-melody is in Vc, Hn 1·2 and Bsn 1. c034 set the horns to f so that it "stays middleground under the theme", but the cellos are still marked ff, and their line (D♭4–C4–B♭3) is on the A string, the cello's most intense register. The middleground is marked at the same level as the theme.
Evidence: Adler PDF 135: the foreground is in the best register and the other layers are kept below it; the cello in that register is the colour Brahms chose for a foreground theme (PDF 133–135). PDF 144: graded dynamics as a supporting means. Shared-staff rule: bassoon 1 stays ff because bassoon 2 is ff on the same staff.
Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoVc): `des'4\f c'4 bes4 | ...` (was `des'4\ff`). The theme stays ff in every part that carries it, so the A′ dynamic plan (protected) is unchanged for the foreground.

### 5. Open item "cellos never play theme A": close with no change
Adler's case for a cello exposition (PDF 133–135, Brahms 3/iii) has two parts: the theme lies in the cello's lyrical register, and the cello statement saves the violins' octave for later. This score already does both with other instruments. Horn 1 (A♭3–B♭4 at 7–14) gives the first statement in a mid register. The violins give the second in unison an octave higher (23). The third is the upper octave (71), and the extremes are saved for 79. At 23–30 the cellos carry the independent counter-melody the plan asks for (protected since c051). Moving the theme to them would cost that line or add a fourth statement. Recommend closing the item.

### 6. Bars 37–40: Vln II's held dyads sit on the viola arpeggio's pitches (not applied, optional)
Problem: the Vln II div. dyads (D♭4/F4, D♭4/G♭4, B♭3/G♭4, E♭4/G♭4) lie inside the violas' triplet span (A♭3–G♭4). The violas strike each of those pitches in the same octave on every beat, so the held notes add thickness but no separate layer.
Evidence: Adler PDF 159, Ex. V-34 (Mendelssohn): the sustained notes and the arpeggiated figure are in different registers. PDF 135: background figures in the palest register, dovetailed without duplication.
Fix if the composer wants it: hold Vln II above the figure, still well below the flute (F5–G♭5): `<f' aes'>1 | <ges' bes'>2 <ges' bes'>2 | <ges' bes'>2 <f' bes'>2 | <ges' c''>2 <f' bes'>2 |` with "div., sul tasto" kept. I did not apply it because "flute over thin strings 37–44" is protected; the change keeps the strings thin and leaves the flute alone, so it does not undo that point.
