## Findings

This is the eighth pass on these chapters. The harp and timpani parts follow every applicable principle above. I found nothing that falls short of the reading and is not already decided, so I propose no score change. I list the points I checked most closely so the next pass on this reading does not repeat them.

### 1. Harmonic passage at 82-83: no change

Adler (PDF 116) gives three conditions for harmonics: written range C3-A5, an octave-harmonic notation the player can read, and very light accompaniment. The passage meets all three. It is written A♭3-C4-E♭4 (sounding A♭4-C5-E♭5). It is marked with circles plus "(sound 8va)". It sounds over the ppp A♭ pedal in four solo cellos and basses only, and the violas wait until 84 b3 (c063). The pickup at 82 b3 (E♭4-F4 at pitch, pp) joins the sounding A♭4 of 83 in the right register, so the head reads E♭-F-A♭-C-E♭ without an octave break. "R.H. étouffez" (c406) uses the sons étouffés technique of PDF 117 for its proper purpose: it damps the fundamentals plucked at 82 so the softer harmonics are not covered. The left hand plays the harmonics, which matches the first method on PDF 116, while its A♭1 rings from 82 and needs no action.

### 2. Pedalling: no change

Adler's rule on PDF 112-113 is one pedal per foot at a time, with time to change. The movement needs three pedal moves. G♮→G♭ falls in the 22-bar rest 15-36, and G♭→G♮ in the rest 57-70. A♮ at 39 b4 and A♭ at 41 b4 each get a full beat at ♩=60 before the note. All of them are right-foot moves, and no two coincide. The two printed diagrams are correct for A♭ and D♭ major (rendered as D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭ and D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G♭ A♭). The part gives the setting during 1-6 as PDF 112 asks.

### 3. Two notes per hand at 7-14 and 71-78: no change

Adler (PDF 114, Ex. IV-5) calls "three-note chords (per hand) spread wide" the most effective harp chords. The harp here never plays more than two notes in one hand: low octaves in the left hand and close dyads in the right. Read alone, that is thinner than the book's model. The thinning is deliberate, and each removed note has a stated reason: c057 and c117 at 7-14 (no attacks on the horn's held or attacked pitches), c127 and c247 at 75-78 (no attacks on the counter-melody's notes and no doubled third over I6), and c361 at 14. Adler's own later chapters, cited in those entries, put the soloist's line first. Adding the third note back would undo those fixes. The rolled attacks (Principle 11) widen each dyad into a short arpeggio, which helps the sound. Keep as written.

### 4. Timpani functions and drum layout: no change

Every stroke is a bass note or chord tone (PDF 79). The layout A♭2/C3/D♭3/E♭3 on 30"/28"/25"/23" is the only one inside Ex. XII-46's ranges. Rolls end on tied strokes (Ex. XII-47). The near-the-rim ppp at 67 follows PDF 80. The f level under both ff peaks follows Piston's warning (PDF 84). The solo answer at 84-85 is the Beethoven solo use of PDF 79-81, scaled down to pp. c436's optional earlier retuning of the C and D♭ drums during 87-91 is still open. It changes the muta text the scherzo thread quotes, so I leave it to that thread and do not raise it again.

### 5. Harp silence at the climaxes and in 84-92: no change

Adler says the upper harp has little carrying power (PDF 114). Rimsky (PDF 47, cited by c076) says one harp cannot be heard against a full forte orchestra. The harp is therefore tacet at 57-62 and 79-81, and its only f is a single low E♭ octave at 78 b3 inside the crescendo (c244, kept). It drops out after 83 so that the coda does not repeat movement I's last scoring of harp plus timpani roll under Vln I's C5 (c050, c120). These are orchestration choices for balance and for the cyclic plan, and the reading supports them.
