## Findings

The tonal plan, the joins and the proportions agree with the reading at every point I checked. I found no change that this reading supports and that a master's score would need. Findings 1–3 add evidence that earlier passes did not have. Finding 5 lists the changes the reading could suggest, and why I reject each one. I applied nothing.

### 1. Every arrival reaches 1̂ or 3̂ on a strong beat, and the bass leads in (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 445–446, Ex. 26-4: "for a strong sense of arrival, you will want to approach the new tonic with a melodic line that gravitates to its 1̂ (or possibly 3̂) and reaches it with a sense of rhythmic resolution". Ex. 26-4 itself is a modulation from C to A♭, the same keys as this movement's link.

**Score (from score.midi, top and bass at each downbeat):**

The home key also gets the "strong dominant" the reading asks for (PDF 445). The link (1–6) uses no dominant, but V arrives at 8 b3, the half cadence at 10 and V7–I at 13–14. Bar 36 b3 is the one beat in 31–36 with no A♭ in the bass: only C3 and C5 sound, so the leading tone of D♭ sits alone in the bass before 37. That gives the phrase modulation at 37 the melodic pull Ex. 26-4 describes without adding a V7 of D♭ (c020, protected).

### 2. 50–53: D♭ is heard as I, not as V of F♯ minor (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 451 §11: a key area on IV can "upset our sense of tonality, making us hear IV as I and I as V". The same risk applies in reverse when a section returns from its own subdominant.

**Score.** On paper, 50 b3 to 53 is a half cadence in F♯ minor: B minor (iv) at 50 b3, G♯7 (V7 of V, spelled A♭7) at 51–52, C♯ (spelled D♭) at 53. c193 named the risk. The beat dump shows how the score meets it:
- G♭4 (= F♯, the departing tonic) enters at 51 b3 as the chord's seventh, so F♯ is heard as a dissonance before 53.
- The A♭7 lasts two full bars under "poco sostenuto". That is long enough to be taken as V7 of D♭.
- Theme B's head returns at 53 on F5 (3̂ of D♭) with its own rising-fourth pickup, so the melody names D♭ as the tonic.
- Dvořák 9/ii makes the same enharmonic return from C♯ minor to D♭ (Tempo I at 101).

The reading's condition for a modulation is met (Points 2, PDF 456): a dominant seventh, and later a cadence with IV (59–61). No change.

### 3. The proportions match Dvořák 9/ii, the main model (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 448 §7: the succession of key areas should be "no more haphazard than … the succession of chords in a single well-composed phrase". Ex. 26-11 shows the time a three-part form spends in each area.

**Measured** (v253 score.midi, 345 s; Dvořák from his tempo marks, ♩=52 / 68 / 64 / 68 / 60 / 52, about 537 s):

The score keeps Dvořák's division within two percentage points in each part. Its middle section reaches its climax a little earlier and leaves a 6-bar dominant (65–70) where Dvořák has none. That dominant is exactly what PDF 456 §15 asks for: V with its seventh after a stay away from the tonic. The coda (83–92, 39 s) is no longer than Dvořák's (rit. at 119, Molto Adagio 126–127, about 45 s). c013 cut the coda from 14 bars to 10; this comparison gives no reason to cut it further.

### 4. The D♭ weight in the reprise and coda is within the reading's limits (no change)

**Bars:** 79–80 (ff peak over a D♭ bass, ii6/5 with B♭6 on top), 87–88 (IV6–iv).

**Reading.** PDF 451–452: the danger of IV belongs to early modulations, and IV "creates no problems of tonal balance" later. The footnote on PDF 456 calls I–IV–I "the most important" plan of its kind. In the reprise, 79–80 last six beats over the D♭ bass and lead to V♭9 at 80 b3–81. The bass dump shows E♭ or A♭ on 17 of the 36 beats of 71–82, and D♭ on 8. Every phrase end in the reprise is on dominant or tonic harmony: V4/3 at 74, V at 77 and V–I at 81–82. The tonic is not in doubt, and the coda's plagal close is a cyclic rule (D♭–C on top). This adds the beat count to c193's Finding 5. No change.

### 5. Changes the reading could suggest, tested on paper and rejected

None of these is recommended. They are recorded so a later critic need not reopen them without new grounds.

- **Foreshadow the middle key in the A section (Ex. 26-7).** K. 498 announces its C minor with a twice-stated V6/5–VI. The parallel here would be an A♭7→D♭ inside 7–30, for example a G♭ passing seventh in Bsn 1 at 9 b2. Rejected for three reasons. PDF 451 warns against IV emphasis early in a major-key piece. The A section already points to D♭ more gently: through D♭ as a chord at 9, 11–12, 16, 21 and 25, and through the motto tail's D♭3 over the A♭ pedal at 33 b3. And 7–14 and 23–30 are on the standing list (horn solo with its bassoon layout; the plain string statement).
- **A sequence of tonicizations in the build, 53–58 (Ex. 26-6, PDF 447; Points 6).** The reading presents such sequences as modulatory transitions (PDF 445: "sequences using applied V's and VII's … appear frequently as modulatory transitions"). Bars 53–60 do not modulate: they restate D♭ and confirm it at 59–61. A sequence there would weaken that confirmation. The build order 53–61 is also protected (c033 onward).
- **Return without a dominant (PDF 456 §16).** The reading allows it for nineteenth-century ABA forms, but it does not prefer it. The protected retransition 65–70 follows §15 instead, and Finding 3 shows its length fits the model.
- **More time on the remote keys.** PDF 443 says keys that persist are usually related ones. A minor (2 bars) and F♯ minor (4 bars) are already short in proportion to how remote they are. Longer stays would go against the reading.
