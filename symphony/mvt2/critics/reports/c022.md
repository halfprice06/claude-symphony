## Findings

### 1. Bars 17–18: four consecutive parallel 5ths between Vln II and the bass (applied)
Problem: Vln II C5–A♭4 | G4–F4 moves over Vc/Cb F–D♭ | C–B♭, so every beat change is a 5th to a 5th: F/C → D♭/A♭ → C/G → B♭/F (beats 50, 51 and 53 in allpairs_par output; analysis.txt misses it because Vln II is an inner voice). The passage is a chain of root-position triads a step or third apart (vi, IV, iii, ii), the situation where A&S say 5ths threaten and where VI or the 5–6 exchange is used to break them up (PDF 165, Unit 11 §2; PDF 168, "the 5–6 technique"; Ex. 11-13b marked "avoid", PDF 171). At pp in a five-part string texture the 5ths are exposed.
Fix (A&S PDF 164: hold the common tone A♭ through VI and IV; PDF 197, Ex. 13-4: IV–I6 with 10ths between outer voices). Vln II holds A♭ through 17 and the first half of 18, so bar 18 b1 becomes I6 (C–A♭–E♭) instead of iii, and the 5ths become 3rd → 5th → 6th → 5th:
```
% aOne.ily, aOneVlnTwo, bars 17-18
aes'2--( aes'4--) | aes'2--( f'4--) |      % was c''2--( aes'4--) | g'2--( f'4--) |
```
The F-minor chord on 17 b1 has no 5th (F–A♭–F–A♭); that is allowed and the melody supplies motion. (I tried Vla C4 on 17 b1 to supply the 5th; it makes new 5ths with the cellos from 16 b3, so the viola stays as it is.)

### 2. Bars 19–21: the F-minor tonicization has no leading tone (applied)
Problem: bar 19 reaches F minor plagally (B♭ minor on 18 b3 → F minor), and the only dominant, 20 b3, is C–G–G–B♭ (Cb/Vc, Vln II, Ob 1, Vla): no E♮ and no third. A&S PDF 196: I–IV–I "does not express the key nearly as strongly as I–V–I"; the key needs a real dominant. The next chord, 21 b1, is D♭ (VI of F minor), a deceptive cadence; A&S PDF 202–203 (Ex. 13-12) and the Points on PDF 209 say V(7)–VI works through 7̂ moving to 8̂ while the 3rd of VI is doubled. The 3rd (F) is already doubled here, but 7̂ is missing, so there is no deception, only a vague C chord.
Fix: Vln II takes E♮4 on 20 b3 and resolves it to F4 at 21; Vla B♭3 → A♭3 already resolves the 7th:
```
% aOne.ily, aOneVlnTwo, bar 20
aes'2--( e'4--) |                           % was aes'2--( g'4--)
```
The oboe keeps G4, so the V7 is complete (C–E–G–B♭).

### 3. Bars 27–29: violas shadow the cello counter-melody and double the 7th of ii7 (applied)
Problem: the upper viola note moves E♭4–F4–A♭4 with the cellos' E♭3–F3–A♭3 (octaves 27 b2 → 28 b1, allpairs beats 80–81), so the counter-melody that cycle 2 freed from the bass is doubled again by the chord voice. On 28 b3 (ii7, B♭ in the bass) the violas hold A♭4, doubling the 7th that the violins carry as melody (A♭5), and both resolve A♭ → G at 29: parallel octaves on a dissonance (beat 84). A&S Unit 12: the 7th of II7 resolves down by step (PDF 177); when it is in the soprano (1̂ on top, PDF 179) the inner voices take the other chord tones; the complete chord is the norm (PDF 177, "virtually always complete chords"). In bar 12, the horn version of the same harmony, only one voice holds A♭, which is correct.
Fix: violas take D♭–F under the melody, which completes ii7 (B♭–D♭–F–A♭) with the 7th only on top:
```
% aOne.ily, aOneVla, bars 27-28
<c' es'>2\< <aes des'>4 | <des' f'>4\mf\> <des' f'>4 <des' f'>4 |
% was <c' es'>2\< <aes f'>4 | <f' aes'>4\mf\> <f' aes'>4 <f' aes'>4 |
```
29 is unchanged: D♭4 → E♭4 and F4 → G4.

### 4. Bar 76: clarinet 2 doubles the 7th of ii7 (applied)
Problem: same harmony as 28. Cl 2 holds A♭4 under the melody's A♭ on 76 b3 and resolves with it to G (octaves with Vln II A♭5 → G5). The melody is already in octaves in the violins; the clarinet is a chord voice and should take a chord tone (A&S PDF 177, 180).
Fix: Cl 2 moves to B♭4 on b3 and falls a third to G4. (F4 makes 5ths with the bass D♭ → B♭; I tested it.)
```
% aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo, bar 76
aes'4 aes'4 bes'4 |                         % was aes'4 aes'4 aes'4
```
Bar 80 has the same doubling (Hn 4, Vla, Cl 2), but at ff in a tutti whose melody is in three octaves. That is orchestral reinforcement, not part-writing, so I leave it.

### 5. Bar 60 → 61: the 7th of V7 rises at the climax (not applied)
Problem: on 60 b3 horn 1 and the cellos (in unison an octave apart) reach G♭, the 7th of A♭7, and both rise to A♭ at 61. The only G♭ → F is the violas' G♭3 → F4, an octave higher in a triplet figure. A&S: the 7th resolves "downward and by step" (PDF 177; Points PDF 191, no. 2); Ex. 12-3c (PDF 178) marks as "wrong" a voicing where the resolution is "transferred into the wrong voice". At the movement's climax the most audible counter-line breaks this rule.
Fix: let the 7th resolve inside bar 60, then leap to the downbeat. F on b4 over A♭7 is an anticipation of the tonic's 3rd:
```
% bSec.ily, bSecHnOne bar 60:  es'4\< f'4 ges'4 f'4) |   % was es'4\< f'4 ges'2)
% bSec.ily, bSecVc bar 60:     es4(\< f4 ges4 f4) |      % was es4(\< f4 ges2)
```
Bars 61–64 (brass layout) are unchanged. I did not apply this because it changes the counter-line's rhythm, which is the composer's choice.

### 6. Bars 38–39 and 54–55: V7 → ii7 across the bar line (not applied)
Problem: theme B's first phrase is I | IV6–V7 | ii7–vi6 | vii°7/vi–vi. The V7 on 38 b3 is followed by ii7 on the strong downbeat. A&S PDF 172: intermediate harmonies run in descending 5ths and 3rds toward V (VI–IV–II–V), and a rising root progression "sounds more like an offshoot" than a step forward. V → ii reverses this. The V7's 7th G♭ is held into ii7 as its 3rd and resolves only on 39 b3. IV6 → V7 with the 6th preparing the 7th is correct (PDF 170, Ex. 11-12d), but here the dominant is cancelled at once.
Fix (A&S PDF 183–187: II7 expanding supertonic harmony; II4/3 near the start of a phrase): keep B♭ in the bass through 38, so IV6 becomes ii4/3 under the melody's E♭ and passes to ii7 at 39. The phrase still aims at vi through its own vii°7.
```
% bSecVc / bSecCb bar 38:  bes,4 r4 bes,4 r4 | / bes,,4 r4 bes,,4 r4 |
% bSecVlnTwo bar 38:       <des' ges'>2 <des' es'>2 |
% bSecVla bar 38 (2nd half): \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' es' des' bes des') }
```
Same at 54 for the reprise. Not applied: this changes the harmony of theme B, which critics have accepted since cycle 1, and it removes the phrase's only V7. I offer it as a syntax point.

### 7. What the reading confirms (no change)
The cadential formula vi7–V7/V–V7–I at 8/24/72 and IV–ii6/5–ii7–V7–I at 12–13/28–29/76–77/80–81 follow Unit 12 (7th prepared as a common tone, II6/5 → II7 with the bass falling to the root, 1̂ dissonant on top). The deceptive cadences at 44–45 (C–D♭ on top, D♭ tripled) and 77–78, with the varied repeat 79–82, follow PDF 203–204 exactly. The intro's I–iv6/4–I with horn 3's C–D♭–C is A&S's I–IV–I with a 3̂–4̂–3̂ neighbour (PDF 196). The coda's IV–iv–I after the PAC at 82 is the plagal extension of PDF 198. The IV7 at 62 with 3̂ on top (F, prepared in Ob 1) is the use A&S call most important (PDF 188).
