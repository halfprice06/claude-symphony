## Findings

### 1. Theme B's recall belongs in movement III, not in this coda (critic 99, observation b). No change in movement II.

**Problem raised by c099.** Theme B is heard from 37 to 61 and never again.

**Recommendation and reasons.**
- The model does the same. In Dvořák 9/ii the middle theme does not come back in the Largo; the reprise and coda use only the main theme and the opening chords. The return of material happens in the finale, which states the earlier movements' themes and then combines them (sources above). This symphony already copies the Largo's other cyclic device (one low-brass quotation of the first movement's head before the reprise, 63–64).
- A&S PDF 451–452 and 456: in an ABA form with a tonicized IV, the middle section's job ends when IV moves to V of the home key; the reprise restores A. Nothing in the form asks for B again.
- Every possible place is protected or full. The coda 83–92 is protected (plagal close, clarinet tail, muted horns; c013, c030, c050, c063). In 83–86 only the harp head and the timpani answer play, and c019/c063 cleared those bars so each is heard alone. A new instrument in 83–86 adds a staff to the last system of page 7, which has no room (c099 already said so); 87–92 is the plagal close itself. Placing a recall in 65–66 would break the "horn alone" retransition (c027, c063).
- The strongest reason is in movement III. Theme B is in D♭, and movement III already has a planned forte D♭ episode that must turn to C (cyclic-notes, c110 and c180). Theme B's consequent ends `es'''4. f'''8 des'''4 bes''4 | c'''1` (Vln I 59–60): it closes on C, held as the leading tone of D♭ until 61. In movement III the same line can end with B♮ instead of B♭: D♭–B is the Neapolitan's diminished third down to the leading tone, which A&S PDF 484 calls "peculiarly intense ... especially in the soprano", and C becomes the tonic. The planned D♭ → V → I with the trumpets' B→C (cyclic-notes c180, c110 finding 5) then comes from a theme the listener heard in movement II. This is A&S PDF 600–601's point about a key area enlarging the work's motive (D♭–C).

**Concrete proposal for movement III** (concert pitch; a sketch for the movement III composer, not for this score):
```
% III, D-flat episode (f, violins + horns), theme B's second half, then the turn to C
aes'4 des''4 f''4. ges''8 | aes''4 bes''8 aes''8 ges''4~ ges''8 f''8 |
es''4. f''8 des''4 b'4 | c''1 |
% bass, e.g.:  des2 bes,2 | ges,2 aes,2 | f,4. aes,8 g,2 | c1   (D-flat: IV6 of the old key = N6 of C, then V, then I)
```
One statement only, as the two quotations in movement II are single statements.

### 2. Bars 71–74 against 23–26 (critic 99, observation a): close with no change; c099's description was wrong in part.

**Correction.** C099 wrote that 71–74 repeat 23–26 "note for note in melody, cello counter-melody and bass". The bass does not repeat: 25–26 are `aes,,2. | es,2.` (Cb) and 73–74 are `aes,,2 f,,4 | bes,,2.` (I–vi7–V4/3 since c060). The melody and the cello counter-melody are identical.

**Evidence that the passage is varied enough.**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 118 gives five ways to re-score a repeated idea. Four are used here: (a) and (c) the theme moves from unison violins (23) to violins in octaves (71, Vln I up an octave); (d) altered details: viola eighth-note figure instead of held divisi chords, Cl 2 and Bsn 2 inner parts, harp bass; (e) the dynamic: the reprise starts p (Vln pickup 70) instead of mp (22).
- Sebesky PDF 19: avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice. 73–74 do this. 71–72 cannot without undoing two protected items: the root-position A♭ at 71 after the dominant pedal (c057 rejected I6 there) and the chain of sevenths under the motto tail at 72 (c032, c052).
- A reprise that states its first phrase as it was is the point of the A′ section (A&S PDF 452, Ex. 26-11: "reprise"). In Dvořák 9/ii the cor anglais theme comes back at 101 as it was and then breaks off. Here 75–82 depart from 27–30 (deceptive cadence on ♭VI at 78, B♭6 peak at 79, hemiola 80–81).
- The obvious way to vary 71–74 further is to bring theme B into the counterpoint, as Rachmaninoff does. That would replace the cello counter-melody (protected since c051). It is also the movement's one melodic bass counterline, which the cyclic rules require (cyclic-notes, critic 62). Finding 1 keeps theme B for movement III.

**Fix.** None. Close the observation.
