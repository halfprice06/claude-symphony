## What else I studied

The Goldmark score could not be fetched: IMSLP's full score (IMSLP26978, Schott) and parts (IMSLP355820) sit behind a bot check, and the mirrors returned HTML. So the comparison rests on secondary sources: the BIS-1842 booklet (Singapore SO/Lan Shui; https://eclassical.textalk.se/shop/17115/art85/4750185-2489d9-BIS-1842_booklet.pdf) and the Wikipedia article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rustic_Wedding_Symphony). They give these facts: the movement is an Andante of about 9½ minutes; it is a love duet in operatic style, with cellos and horns on the tenor line and violins and upper winds on the soprano; it shows Wagner's influence; it quotes the fourth variation of the first movement; the middle section turns to E♭ minor; and the garden music comes back in the finale's slow episode. I did not rely on any bar-level detail I could not check. I also read c369's report, and diffed v369 against v379 across every section file, global.ily and score.ly. I looked at all 8 score pages at 80 dpi and checked v379's build-warnings.txt (empty) and analysis.txt (0 outer-voice parallels).

## Holistic comparison with Goldmark's "Im Garten"

On the points the sources confirm, the plan matches Goldmark's:
- **Duet texture.** Goldmark's slow movement is a dialogue between a soprano voice (violins, upper winds) and a tenor voice (cellos, horns). This movement has the same pairing: the cello counter-melody against the violins at 23-30, horn 2, bassoon 1 and the cellos against the violins at 75-81, and the oboe/solo-violin dialogue at 15-22.
- **Contrasting middle section.** Goldmark moves to the minor mode of a remote key. This movement goes D♭ → F♯ minor → A minor.
- **Quotation of earlier movements.** Goldmark quotes variation 4. This movement quotes movement I twice.
- **A return that is not a repeat.** The violin theme comes back in octaves at 71 with a new viola figure.

Goldmark's Andante is longer (about 9½ minutes against 5¾) and more openly passionate. A model with a more operatic climax does not argue for lengthening this movement: proportion here follows the Dvořák 9/ii plan, and form critics (c363, c373) confirmed it. A conductor would accept the orchestration as that of a careful late-Romantic composer. The wind writing in particular follows every applicable rule of Adler's ch. 8. Its weakness, if any, is the reverse of Goldmark's: it is more restrained and less ardent. That is a matter of character, decided long ago (Dvořák model), not a fault to fix bar by bar.

## Findings

No change is proposed. Nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-379/work (the copy is identical to v379, whose build is clean: 0 warnings, 0 outer-voice parallels, 8 pages; parts book 30 pages, unchanged).

**1. Bar 70 b3 to 71: the pickup to the return under the new bassoon holds (v369 → v379 change; Adler PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12).** Strauss lets the pedal drop just before the first violins' anacrusis to the second statement, so the pickup is heard and the statements are separated. In v369 the bassoons stopped at 70 b2. Since v378 (c377) they hold G3/D♭3 through 70 b3 and resolve to A♭3/C3 at 71, under the violins' E♭-F pickup. Strauss's held chord sits in the clarinets' middle register, close to the tune. Here the bassoons are pp and two octaves below the Vln II pickup and three below Vln I. The horn's B♭3 ends at the barline and the timpani roll stops at 70 b2, so the drop-out Strauss uses still happens in the instruments nearest the pickup. Releasing the bassoons again would cost c377's contrary-semitone resolution into 71. Releasing the basses' E♭ instead would leave D♭3 in the bass for one beat, a V4/2 resolving to root position. Verdict: not worse than v369; no change. If a later critic wants more air before 71, the only safe option is `\breathe` marks in Bsn 1·2 after 70 b2 with a repeated `g4( | aes2)` and `des4( | c2.)`. I do not recommend it.

**2. Bars 79-81: Ob 1 and Cl 1 in unison on B♭5 (Adler PDF 235, 257).** Adler warns against oboe and clarinet in unison on a melody, but he accepts overlapping in a full tutti (PDF 257). Here the unison is inside the ff tutti and doubled by Vln II and the cor anglais an octave lower, and c144 recorded it. No change.

**3. Bar 87: IV6 with the third at the bottom of the strings (Adler PDF 257, Ex. VIII-25).** This follows Adler's rule to double the third of a first inversion low. It is protected (plagal close) and recorded by c304. No change.

**4. Changes since v369 (lens requirement).** The diff covers six changes:
- **21:** the basses' D♭3 loses its stem override (engraving).
- **28:** the basses slur `des2( bes,4)`.
- **52:** Vln II slurs `f''2( es''4)` (bowing).
- **70-71:** the bassoons hold and resolve (finding 1).
- **78 b3:** Fl 1 down-stems.
- **score.ly:** only the version label.

None of these makes anything worse. The 52 and 28 slurs restore down-bows at the diminuendo and the ff (Adler part 1 PDF 38). The bassoon resolution meets Adler PDF 267 (a single bassoon doubling the violins' first note in the octave below). Clarinet 2 still keeps off the theme's A♭4 at 71 (c247).
