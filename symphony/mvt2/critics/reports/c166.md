## Findings

The score now has few playability problems. Earlier cycles closed most of what this chapter teaches, so the findings below are small.

### 1. Oboe 1 plays 57-60 without a breath (applied)
Bars 57-60. Oboe 1 plays theme B's lower octave from 57 b1 to the end of 60, 16 beats at ♩=60-64 (about 15.5 s). It is one slur, crescendo p to f, and ends at A♭5 and C5. The breath at the end of 60 is its first. Flute 1 (upper octave of the same line) and horn 1 breathe after 58, and the clarinets rest at 59 b3-4. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37: "Wood-wind players cannot manage extremely long sustained passages, as they are compelled to take breath; care must be taken therefore to give them a little rest from time to time." Oboists also have to get rid of stale air. A 15-second crescendo into the climax with no release is the hardest stretch in the oboe part. The violins' slur breaks in the same place (58 ends `ges''8 f''8)`).
Fix (bSec.ily, bSecOboeOne): `aes''4. ges''8~ ges''4~ ges''8 f''8) \breathe | es''4.(\f f''8 des''4 bes'4 | c''1)\< \breathe |`. Pitches, rhythm and dynamics are unchanged. The breath matches flute 1's at the same place.

### 2. Horn 4 holds 1 b2-6 b2 in one breath (applied)
Bars 1-6. Horn 4 enters on 1 b2 under horn 3's C4, carries it through bar 2 while horn 3 breathes (c030), and then plays A♭3 at 3-6 with no gap: 16 beats at ♩=52, about 18.5 s at ppp-pp. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44: "The remarks on breathing, in the section devoted to the wood-wind, apply with equal force to the brass." The change of note at the barline 2-3 is a natural place to breathe. Horn 3 retakes C4 on 3, and Vln I still holds C5 through 3, so the common tone is not broken.
Fix (intro.ily, introHnFour): `c'2.\< \breathe | \once \override Tie.stencil = #hnFourNoTie aes2.\pp | ...`. The comma prints on the shared Hn 3·4 staff at the end of bar 2, where horn 3 rests. The sound is unchanged apart from a breath-length gap in horn 4.

### 3. Harp f chord at 78 b3 inside the f tutti (not applied; for the composer's judgement)
Bar 78 b3. The harp plays LH E♭1-E♭2 (8 bassa) f and RH G3-E♭4 under flute, clarinets, bassoons and all strings at f, with horns mf<. Every note is already in the bass (Cb, Bsn 2, Vc line) or the inner parts. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47: one harp "is never a very powerful instrument" and "at least three, if not four harps in unison are necessary, if they are to be heard against a full orchestra playing forte". c116 raised the harp to f here because the middle register carries (Adler part 1 PDF 113-114). That reason covers the RH chord, but the LH octave is in the lowest octave, where Rimsky gives no such carrying power. The attack does no harm and adds little. If the composer wants the harp clearly heard before its rest at 79-81, `\mf` on the 78 b3 chord would show that it is colour, not support. Otherwise leave it.

### 4. Single low harp notes at 12, 72 and 73 (recorded; no change)
Bars 12 b3 (B♭1), 72 b3 (E♭1), 73 b3 (F1), harp LH. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46 limits the harp to its central octaves: "the extreme notes in both compasses may be employed in special circumstances, and for doubling in octaves". These three are single notes in the lowest octave, not octaves, at pp-p. c089 chose them deliberately: the octaves made parallel fifths at 72-74. Under c076's ruling the lowest octave is acceptable as the bass of an 8 bassa line, and these notes are short, so I record the exception and propose nothing.

### 5. Muted strings at f at 28 (recorded; protected)
Bars 27-28. Upper strings are con sord. 15-30, and the first string statement of theme A rises to f at 28. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 28: under a mute the tone "turns to a slight hiss or whistle in loud ones, and the volume of tone is always greatly reduced". The f at 28 is protected (c103, c119), and there is no rest in which to take mutes off before it. I record this as the one passage that departs from PDF 28 by design. Do not change it.
