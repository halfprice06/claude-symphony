## Findings

### 1. Holistic comparison with Grieg Op. 16/ii: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? (recorded)

Yes, in its structure and its scoring. Point by point, from the score:
- **Opening colour.** Grieg begins with all five string parts "con sordini" pp, each bar breathing with its own < >, and puts the solo horn in as an answer at bar 9 over a single bassoon (p. 34). This movement holds its muted strings back to 15-22 and gives the opening to the solo horn over bassoons and harp (7-14). Both composers make the horn the first wind voice heard. This is Rimsky's Ex. 73 principle (PDF 72, 245).
- **Second strophe and first peak.** Grieg's second strophe moves the tune to the violins sul G, adds a horn line over the bassoons and builds "cresc. ed accel." to a ff peak, then "dim. e rit." and "a tempo" (p. 35). This movement's 23-30 is the same kind of strophe (violins sul D, cello counter-melody, f at 28), without the tempo push.
- **Flat-submediant turn.** Grieg drops from D♭ to E major "tranquillo", pp, with tremolo and pizz. strings (pp. 36-38). This movement's F♯ minor at 47 and A minor at 63-64 do the same job by third relation. That is the Dvořák 9/ii plan the brief asks for.
- **Climax.** At B (p. 39) Grieg's theme returns ff "pesante". The upper strings play fp tremolo, the basses pizz., and the cellos add a new "cantabile" mf counter-line, arco, doubled by the bassoon. At 79-81 this movement adds the cello counter-line doubled by horns and bassoons under the theme in octaves. The device is the same one, scored more heavily, which suits a symphony.
- **Close and link.** Grieg ends with a horn solo carrying a turn, "rit.", strings ppp, a Lento fermata, and an attacca into the finale (p. 41). This movement ends with the clarinet morendo and the plagal D♭-C close, and goes attacca into the scherzo, whose horn 1 picks up on G♯4.
- **One difference worth naming.** Grieg shapes tempo inside his phrases: "accel.", "poco stretto", "rit.", "a tempo", all within 28 bars. This movement shapes tempo only at section joins and in the B build. Finding 2 deals with the one place where that matters.

### 2. Bars 75-78: the A′ build only broadens; press on a little first (applied)

Problem: the two climaxes are approached differently. The B build has "a tempo, poco a poco animando" at 53 (MIDI 60-61-62-64), then "poco allargando" at 60 and "Largamente" at 61. The A′ build, 75-78 (theme A's second phrase, crescendo p to f, with the bass rising A♭-C-D♭), has no tempo word until "poco allargando" at 78. So the movement's main climax at 79 arrives with no forward pull, only a broadening, and the allargando at 78 slows from a tempo that never moved.

Evidence: Grieg presses before each ff and broadens after it: "cresc. ed accel." into the ff at p. 35, and "poco stretto" into the ff "ten." at p. 41, each followed by "rit." Rimsky's slow examples in the assigned range mark the same thing: Ex. 128 (*Golden Cockerel*, Larghetto, "animando pochissimo", PDF 303), Ex. 91 (*Saltan*, "animando poco a poco" into a ff, PDF 268) and Ex. 87 (*Kashchei*, "poco rubato", then "calmando", PDF 261). This movement already does the same at 53-60, so the change makes the two builds consistent. It does not touch the protected tempo points (35, 47-48, 53, 60, 64-65, 78, 89) or the metronome marks.

Fix (global.ily, line 69), replacing `s2.*4 \break s2.*3 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco allargando" \mt 50 s2.` with:

```
s2.*4 \break \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco animando" \mt 53 s2. \mt 54 s2. \mt 55 s2. \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco allargando" \mt 50 s2.
```

The rest of the line (`\break \mt 48 s2.*2 \mt 46 s2. \mt 44 s2. \break`) stays as it is. The change is small (52 to 55, about 6 percent) and the drop to 50 at 78 now makes the allargando audible. The new word prints at the head of the 75 system over the woodwinds and over Vln I, where "poco allargando" already prints. Page 6 is otherwise unchanged. Tested: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, parts book 30 pages.

### 4. Spacing at p against PDF 85-86 (recorded)

PDF 85: "The bass should rarely lie at a greater distance than an octave from the part directly above it." The scan finds gaps of more than an octave above the bass at 2, 15, 30, 34-36, 41-46, 51 and 83-86. All are p or pp, where PDF 86 allows wide spacing ("in piano passages such distribution may be possible"). Most are deliberate pedal or open-bass colours (1-2 link, 31-36 A♭ pedal, 83-86 the two-octave gap recorded by c279). The two forte cases, 62 b3-4 (E2 to A♭3) and 63 b1 (A2 to E4, filled by Tbn 1's E3 half a beat later), are protected ("62 b3 spacing"; c074). No change.

### 5. Bassoon tritone at 69-70 against PDF 90 rule 4 (recorded, optional)

PDF 90, rule 4: concords go to instruments of one colour and discords to different colours, unless the discord is to be stressed. At 69-70 Bsn 1 G3 and Bsn 2 D♭3 hold the tritone of E♭7 for two bars, pp, under the Hn 1 solo. Nothing here is meant to be stressed. The voicing has been on the do-not-touch list since cycle 14 ("horn-bassoon voicing 69-70"), with no reason given, and c517 judged 67-70 against Rimsky ch. III as following. Untested option, if the composer wants to follow the rule literally: give the D♭3 to the bass clarinet, which rests from 63 to 78, and keep Bsn 1 on G3. I do not rank this above a protected item and did not apply it.

### 6. Woodwind and string unisons at the climax (recorded)

At 79 the second violins' B♭5 is doubled by Ob 1 and Cl 1. PDF 76 says that several winds in unison with one string group overpower it. At ff, in the protected A′ octaves (75-82), this is the intended mixed tutti colour, and Rimsky's own Ex. 79 and 87 (PDF 252, 261) double strings this heavily at a climax. No change.
