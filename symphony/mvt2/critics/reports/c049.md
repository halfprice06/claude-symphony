## What else I studied

Tchaikovsky 6/iv in the corpus (musicxml-converted/xml/Tchaikovsky_Sym6_mvt4.musicxml), bars 37–50 (the D-major theme) and 126–147. Over bars 37–46 one figure carries the whole paragraph: horns pp in syncopated octave E's, bassoons in sixths, violas doubling Vln I an octave lower. Tchaikovsky changes the figure only at the next paragraph: at 47, horns 3·4 and the Vln II octave. After the climax he holds a ten-bar F♯ pedal (126–136) under descending sequences, and at 147 the bass changes from held notes to a pulse.

Judged against that, v49 reads as the work of a skilled composer. Each paragraph has one accompaniment figure (bassoon chords 7–14, louré Vln II 15–22, divisi violas 23–30, viola triplets 37–44, viola eighths 71–78). The climax is prepared, the A′ peak is approached by step and by a run, and there is a post-climax pedal (65–70). A conductor would object most to one place: B3 (53–56) repeats B1's accompaniment unchanged, so the middle section's build starts by restating its opening, and Tchaikovsky never does that. The other problems are local.

I also compared every section file of v49 with v39 (diff in /tmp/critic-049). Most of the 40–48 changes are improvements: oboe on theme B's head at 45, cor anglais quotation, hemiola 80–81, staggered winds at 57, horn breath at 58. One change made things worse: c047's clarinet octave at 57–59 (finding 2). I re-ran allpairs_par.py and my own scanner, which covers every sounding note of every track including double stops and part-combined staves.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 53–56: the viola triplets repeat 37–40 note for note, inside the horn counter-line's register (open since c037; applied)

Problem: at F (53) the violins take theme B in unison, and the accompaniment is exactly B1's viola figure (A♭3–G♭4, slurred triplets, same pitches and bowing). From 53 b4 the horn 1 + Cl 1 counter-line (A♭3–A♭4) lies inside that bowed figure. Cycle 19 doubled the horn with Cl 1 for exactly this reason: the triplets cover it.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov says to give complicated accompaniment figures "partially ... to pizz. strings and harp, as this combination has little chance of overpowering" (PDF 140), and that bowed, sustained sound covers a melody more than short detached notes (PDF 138). Ex. 282 (PDF 450) and Ex. 280 (PDF 447) put exactly this kind of triplet figure in the harp under a soft lyrical climax. Ex. 289–291 (PDF 144, 467–470) and PDF 118 show a returning idea rescored by "alteration of details". Ex. 298 (PDF 482–483) builds by adding activity in stages. In Tchaikovsky 6/iv (37–47) the figure changes when the paragraph changes. The harp has been silent since 44 and is one of the instruments the cyclic plan gives melodic moments.

Fix (applied): the harp RH plays the violas' figure at 53–56 with the same pitches, p, mp at 55, under the pedal diagram of 37. The violas rest 53–56 and re-enter at 57 mf< with the triplets, then go to sextuplets at 59. The build now runs harp (53), violas (57), doubled speed (59). The harp pedals work: A♭→A♮ during 55 b3–4 for 56 b1–2; A♮→A♭ and G♭→G♮ during 57–70, before the diagram at 71.
```
% bSec.ily, Vla 52-57
... | c'1 | c'1 |  R1*4 |
\undo \omit TupletNumber
\stemDown \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8(\mf\< des' f' des' aes des') } \omit TupletNumber \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges' des' bes des') } |
% new bSecHarpR
bSecHarpR = { R1*16 |
  \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8\p^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"^-^|^-^^" des' f' des' aes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' f' des' aes des' } |
  \omit TupletNumber
  \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' ges' des' bes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { c'8 es' ges' es' c' es' } |
  \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8\mp des' ges' des' bes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' f' des' bes des'_\markup \small "A♮" } |
  \tuplet 3/2 4 { a8 c' es' c' a c' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' f' des' bes des' } |
  \undo \omit TupletNumber R1*4 | R1*4 | }
```
The do-not-touch entry "viola triplets 37–60 (six per bow)" from c018 is about bowing. The bowing of 37–44 and 57–60 is unchanged, and the open item (c037, c047, c048) asks for exactly this rescoring.

### 2. Bar 59 b3–4: both clarinets lie above the theme's lower octave (a regression from v39; applied)

Problem: since c047 moved the clarinets up an octave, Cl 1 holds E♭5 and Cl 2 rises to D♭5 at 59 b3. On that beat the theme's lower octave (Ob 1, Vln II) is D♭5, and on b4 it drops to B♭4. For a beat and a half, two clarinets at f sit on and above the line they should support. In v39 the clarinets were E♭4/B♭3–D♭4, below it.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132–133: in the woodwind group the harmonic basis "generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design, and should be of inferior dynamic power". PDF 157: crossing parts is rare and only "with the intention of emphasising the melody in the ascending voices", whereas here the melody descends. My scan also showed that every substitute pitch on those beats either crosses the melody, doubles the leading tone G of V7/V, or makes octaves with the viola figure.

Fix (applied): both clarinets rest at 59 b3–4 and come back on the V7 swell at 60, which also gives them a breath.
```
% bSec.ily
bSecClOne: ... | es''2\f r2 | c''1\< \breathe |
bSecClTwo: ... | bes'2\f r2 | aes'1\< \breathe |
```
c047's purpose, keeping the clarinets out of A♭3–F4 at 57–58, is kept.

### 3. Bar 9: compound fifths between the horn melody and the harp LH (applied)

Problem: the horn's passing B♭3 (9 b2½) sounds against the harp's E♭2 (top of the A♭1–E♭2 fifth, struck on b1), and both move down to A♭3 / D♭1 on b3. The result is parallel fifths (a twelfth, then a fifth plus two octaves) between the melody and the harp's upper note. They are exposed because only bassoons, bass clarinet and harp accompany the solo horn. analysis.txt does not catch them because the harp is not the lowest part.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132–133: when the melodic design has passing notes, the basis should be reduced to fundamental notes and kept apart in octave or timbre. Here the harp's fifth adds nothing that the bassoons (A♭3, C3) do not already provide.

Fix (applied): the harp LH plays A♭ in octaves on 9 b1. The LH stays on the bass, as the c026 do-not-touch requires, and the horn line is untouched.
```
% aOne.ily, aOneHarpL bar 9
<aes,, aes,>2\arpeggio des,,4 |
```

### 4. Bars 41–44: the cor anglais doubling lies inside the viola triplets (not applied; suggestion)

From 41 the cor anglais (A♭3–B♭4) doubles the flute's theme B an octave lower, inside the violas' A♭3–G♭4 triplets. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139 says a line suffers most when the accompaniment shares its register. The flute still carries the tune, so this matters less than finding 1. If the composer wants the cor anglais heard as more than a shadow, the violas could play 41–43 pizzicato on beats 1 and 3 (`<aes des' f'>4\pizz r4 <bes des' ges'>4 r4`), returning arco at 44. This follows PDF 138 and 140 and Ex. 280 (PDF 447: pizzicato cello figure under the voice). I did not apply it, because c038's shared swell at 41–44 would need to be redone.

### 5. Bars 53→54, 55→56: octaves between the figure's last note and the bass (minor; not applied)

The last eighth of the triplet figure (D♭4) moves to B♭3 (54) or A3 (56) while the bass (Vc, Cb, Bsn) moves D♭→B♭ and D♭→A. That is a weak-to-strong octave by arpeggiation, which my scan flags. These octaves were in the violas in v49 and are now in the harp. If the composer wants them gone, end each of the two half-bars on F: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' f' des' aes f }` (53) and `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' f' des' bes f }` (55).
