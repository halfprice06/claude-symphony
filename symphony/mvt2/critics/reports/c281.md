## Findings

### 1. Bar 62, Vln II: the F♭ that bends theme B's augmented head falls on the open E string (applied)

**Bars.** 61–62. Vln II and cellos carry theme B's augmented head ff "molto sostenuto": A♭4–D♭5 | F5–F♭5. The F→F♭ is the turn from D♭ toward A minor that c263 protects (the F/F♭→E♭ thread of the whole movement).

**Problem.** F♭5 sounds E5, the open E string. In first position a violinist takes F5 with the first finger on the E string, and the semitone below it is then the open string. The scan found this as the only open-string pitch in any string melody that is not covered by a sul mark (the others: Vc A3 at 46 under sul D, Vln II D4 at 64 under sul G, Vla G3 at 65 under sul C).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 73–74: the open string "has a characteristic sound which ... stands out peculiarly", and "in slow, expressive passages, one usually wants all the tones to be of equal and controlled texture". PDF 73: a fingering or string indication belongs in the score when the composer wants a specific timbre. PDF 75–76: in lyrical passages keep the line on the A string and mark it "sul A". The G string (PDF 74, Ex. III-5) is not an option here: F5 on the G string is about 10th position, past the 7th-position limit for section control (PDF 72). On the A string, F5 and F♭5 lie in 3rd position (fingers 3 and 2), the bend becomes a stopped semitone with vibrato, and 63's E5/C6 sixth follows in the same position.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-281/work/sections/bSec.ily, Vln II line).** A dashed "sul A" spanner over the two notes of bar 62, in the same style as Vln I's "(sul D)" at 1–4:

```
aes'2--\ff^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto" des''2-- |
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.left.text = \markup \italic "sul A"
\once \override TextSpanner.bound-details.right.text = \markup \draw-line #'(0 . -1)
f''2--\startTextSpan fes''2-- |
<e'' c'''>1:32\sf\>\stopTextSpan^\markup \italic "non div., trem." | ...
```

No pitch, rhythm, dynamic or doubling changes. The spanner ends with a hook at 63, so no "ord." is needed.

### 2. String colour of both themes follows the chapter (recorded, no change)

Theme A's first string statement (22 b3–26) is sul D, p→mp, the warm, subdued colour of Ex. III-6. It goes to ord. at 27 for the f phrase. Theme B in the violins (52 b4–56) is sul A, p cantabile, with hairpins bar by bar, as in Brahms 3/iii (Ex. III-7). The violins move to the E string (ord.) at 57 for the build to 61. In the reprise, Vln I has theme A p on the E string at 71–74 ("clear and luminous when one plays softly", PDF 75) over Vln II sul D, and reaches the E string's full brilliance at the ff peak 79. The cellos' B♭-minor head at 44–47 is sul D, the string Adler calls the cello's most lyrical (PDF 99). The violas use the sombre C string only at 64–65 and 87–92 (PDF 86). c256's rejection of sul A at 70–74 still holds: E♭6 on the A string is above the 7th position (PDF 72).

### 3. Theme B's stepwise antecedent: the reading supports c141's closure (recorded, no change)

This is the melody point that c251, c261 and c271 left open. Ex. III-7 (PDF 75) is the reading's model for a soft violin cantabile on one string. Brahms's line also moves mostly by step and turns within a narrow range, and each bar is shaped by its own hairpin. The tune is memorable because of its rhythm and the expressive weight of each bar, not because of leaps. Theme B at 53–56 gets the same treatment (sul A, p, hairpins on 53 and 55, steady rise p–mp–mf). Its consequent then gives it the leap and the range (A♭–D♭ fourth at 57, A♭6 at 58). The chapter gives no reason to change the contour. On the lens question: theme A is the tune a listener will hum (arpeggio to a held fifth, the chromatic motto tail, the B♭ appoggiatura, three statements in rising register). Theme B is remembered by its consequent and the augmented ff head at 61–62, which finding 1 now keeps in a stopped, singing colour.

### 4. Skips and shifts in the string melodies (recorded, no change)

Following PDF 82–83, I listed the widest skips each section has to play. Vln I B♭4→A♭5 at 56→57 starts a new slur under "ord." and a crescendo, an easy move to the E string. Vln I F6→B♭6 at 76 is a fourth at the top, mp. It is protected (B♭6 only at 76/79; c121, c181). Vln II C6→B3 at 64 b2→b3 drops to first position after a tremolo, at p, with no legato across the gap. The run into B♭6 at 78 uses the c246 bow plan. None of these is the "wide skips legato" case of Ex. III-24.

### 5. Cello counter-melodies 23–30 and 71–78: optional "sul D" (not applied, low rank)

The counter-melody lies E♭3–D♭4. Played normally, it crosses to the A string for B♭3–D♭4 at 25, 28–29 (and 76–77), and the A string is the cello's "brilliant and piercing" string (PDF 99). At 28 the cellos reach D♭4 f under the violins' B♭5 appoggiatura, the high point of theme A. Marking `^\markup \italic "unis., cantabile, sul D"` at 23 and `"cantabile, sul D"` at 71 (positions up to the 4th) would keep the line on the warm string under the tune. This is a text mark only and is untested. Most cellists would take these notes on the D string anyway, so I do not press it. It does not answer c279's separate open point about the cello A♭3 under the B♭ appoggiatura at 28.

### 6. Solo violin, basses and bowing (recorded, no change)

The solo violin at 17–22 (concertmaster, senza sord., while the section is muted) follows Brahms 1/ii's use of a solo violin over winds in a slow movement (Ex. III-20, PDF 81). The basses never have double stops (PDF 107). Their soli pedals at 65–70 and 87 use the thin solo sound on purpose (PDF 105), and every long tenuto has "change bow at will" (PDF 108).
