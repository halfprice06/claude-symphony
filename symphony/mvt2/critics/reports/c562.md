## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 11 b2–b3: bassoon 2 doubles the I6 bass and moves with it into IV. Applied.

**Problem.** In the horn statement's consequent the wind choir is four real parts (horn, Bsn 1, Bsn 2, B.cl.). At 11.1 bassoon 2 holds C3 as the third of I. On beat 2 the bass clarinet rises A♭2–C3 onto bassoon 2's note, and on beat 3 both move C3–D♭3 together into IV. That is the doubled third of I6 moving in parallel unison with the bass into IV, and the harp LH's C2–D♭2 makes the same move an octave lower.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 141 §7: "Be careful about parallel octaves when moving from I6 to IV!", with Ex. 9-11a marked "wrong": the upper voice that doubles the I6 bass moves with it to IV. Points for Review (PDF 151, no. 2): in IV–V and I–II lead the upper voices against the bass. The same progression at 15–16, 27 and 75 is clean: at 75 bassoon 2 doubles the bass from the start of the bar, which is a doubled line, not a merging voice. c252 accepted D♭3 as "a unison doubling of the bass line for one note" (A&S PDF 85, Ex. 5-24). That does not describe 11: bassoon 2 enters the bar as its own voice on C3, is joined by the bass on beat 2, and leaves with it, which is exactly the Unit 9 example. c252's real goal, no C3–F3 octaves with the horn's C4–F4 (PDF 83), is kept.

**Fix (applied, `sections/aOne.ily`, aOneBsnTwo bar 11):**
```
c2( des4 | f2--) r4 | ...   →   c4( es4 des4 | f2--) r4 | ...
```
Bassoon 2 now goes C3–E♭3–D♭3. On beat 2 it joins bassoon 1's held E♭3 (oblique, no attack inside the horn's A♭3, so c219 holds). On beat 3 it falls E♭3–D♭3 against the bass's C3–D♭3, contrary motion into the root. IV at 11.3 is unchanged (D♭3, D♭3, A♭3, F4). The horn, bass clarinet, harp and bassoon 1 are untouched (c201, c219, c141 not reopened). ### 2. Bar 68 b3: a 6/4 on the weak beat resolving to V on 69's downbeat. Recorded; no change.

68.3 is E♭–A♭–C over the E♭ pedal, resolving to E♭7 at 69.1. Read as a cadential 6/4 it breaks PDF 154 §6 and Ex. 10-6a ("never!"), because the 6/4 is weak and its resolution strong. It is not cadential. The bass is a six-bar pedal, the 6/4 comes from the horn's motto tail D–D♭–C and the inner A♭, and it is approached from V7 at 68.2. PDF 160 (Points 7) says a cadential 6/4 is not approached by dominant harmony, so this one is a neighbour 6/4 inside a dominant prolongation, as c542 and c553 classified it. The rhythm rule therefore does not apply. No change.

### 3. Bar 81 b2: the prepared 7th leaves clarinet 2 by leap. Recorded; no change.

Clarinet 2 holds D♭5 from 79 through IV and ii°7 into V7 at 81. That is the prepared 7th of PDF 139 §6 and Ex. 9-8. At 81.2 it leaps to G4 and does not resolve to C5. The 7th continues in cellos, bassoon 1 and horns 1·2 on D♭4, which resolve to C4 at 82.1, and clarinet 1 has the C5 at 82. In an instrumental tutti the 7th can pass to another voice (PDF 140, Ex. 9-9, the instrumental licence), so the dissonance does resolve where it is heard. The 79–83 release is protected. No change.

### 4. No cadential 6/4 at the authentic cadences 13–14, 29–30, 60–61, 77/81–82, or at the half cadences 10, 22, 26. Recorded; not re-raised.

A&S PDF 155 §7 and PDF 158–159 (Ex. 10-7, 10-14) treat the cadential 6/4 as the normal way to weight a period's cadences. In theme A, 7̂ already falls on V's downbeat after 1̂ on ii7 (13, 29, 77, 81), and at 10 and 26 the melody's G arrives on the downbeat of V. A 6/4 would need 1̂ or 8̂ on those beats (PDF 156 §8, Ex. 10-7a), which changes the protected theme. At 60 the C6 leading tone in octaves is protected (c282). c262 and c422 reached the same result. Nothing in this fifth reading answers their reasons. The movement's one cadential 6/4 at 43, in the D♭ key area, makes the arrival of the B section's own key the most strongly prepared cadence, and that ordering is sound.

### 5. Bar 26 b2 and bars 13.3, 29.3, 77.3, 81.3: dissonances over V. Recorded; no change.

At 26.2 the cellos' A♭3 is a passing 4th (B♭3–A♭3–G3) over the half-cadence V on a weak beat. It is not a 6/4: there is no C, and it is approached from V (PDF 153 §3, PDF 160 no. 7). At the theme's final V the melody's C is an anticipation of 3̂ over V on the last beat (G–E♭–C over E♭), followed by I. PDF 162 says the first tone of 3̂–2̂–1̂ "may require" a 6/4. Here the C follows 7̂ on V, so the rule does not call for one. Both follow the reading.
