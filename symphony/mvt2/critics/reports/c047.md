## Findings

### 1. Bars 57–59: the clarinets are packed under horn 1 and leave the F4–A♭5 space empty in the forte build (applied)

**Problem.** In 57–59 clarinets 1·2 (F4/D♭4, then D♭4–C4 over A♭3–C♭4, then E♭4/B♭3–D♭4) lie below horn 1's counter-line (A♭4–G♭4, F4–E♭4–F4, G♭4–E♭4). They share A♭3–F4 with bassoon 1, the viola triplets and sextuplets, and the cellos doubling the horn. Above horn 1 there is nothing but the theme's lower octave in oboe 1 and Vln II, an octave or more higher (A♭5 at 58). At 58 b1 the chord has no note between F4 (horn) and A♭5 (oboe, Vln II), a gap of a tenth, while six parts crowd the octave below. The build reaches f at 59. Clarinet 1 also has to leap a sixth, E♭4 to C5, into 60.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 108: clarinets "should rarely be set inside the horns", and a soft horn chord "may be completed by clarinets" in the upper register, above it (diagram: 4 horns, 3 clarinets above). PDF 108–109: forte, "the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind; balance may be established by doubling the upper harmonic parts". No. 152 and No. 166 put the clarinets above or around the horn basis, never under it. PDF 112: octave doublings support the chord. This is the clarinets-inside-the-horn layout the reading warns against. The same register had already been cleared for clarinet 1 to double the horn at 55–56 (c038), so from 57 the clarinets are free to move up.

**Fix (applied in bSec.ily).** From 57 beat 3 to the end of 59, both clarinets go up an octave. Clarinet 2 ties B♭4 into 58, so it does not move B♭–A♭ in octaves with the bass of the viola arpeggio.
```
bSecClOne: \partCombineApart f'2\mp\< des''2 | des''4 des''4 c''4 des''4 | es''2\f es''2 | c''1\< \breathe |
bSecClTwo: \partCombineApart des'2\mp\< bes'2~ | bes'4 ges'4 aes'4 ces''4 | bes'2\f des''2 | aes'1\< \breathe |
```
The clarinets now complete the harmony in the empty octave above the horn (clarion register, f at 59). Clarinet 1 steps E♭5–C5 into 60, and clarinet 2's C♭5 (seventh of V7/IV) still falls to B♭4 at 59. Bar 60 and c032's A♭4 are unchanged. Horn 1, bassoon 1, the violas and the cellos keep the lower middle register to themselves, so the horn counter-line stands out more clearly against the theme. allpairs_par.py finds no new pair at 57–60.

### 2. Bar 61: no woodwind harmony above trumpet 1; A♭4 is five-fold while the A♭4–F5 sixth is empty (applied)

**Problem.** At the arrival of the climax (61 b1–2), Tpt 1 plays A♭4 and clarinet 1, oboe 2, horn 3 and Vln II play the same A♭4. Above it the only woodwind harmony is oboe 1's F5. The flutes carry the theme at A♭5. The woodwinds therefore double the trumpets instead of building the chord over them.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 110: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets", with the arrangements shown on PDF 111 (2 Ob + 2 Fl, 3 Cl, Fl + Ob stacked over trumpets in octaves). Example No. 166 (Snegourotchka, PDF 335) and No. 169 (Sadko, PDF 339) lay out ff tutti chords this way. c014 put the clarinets in unison with the trumpets by PDF 106 (unison juxtaposition), and I keep that. Only oboe 2 moves.

**Fix (applied in bSec.ily).** Oboe 2 holds D♭5 through 61 instead of doubling Tpt 1's A♭4–D♭5. Its bar 62 is unchanged.
```
bSecOboeTwo: R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*8 | des''1--\ff | des''2-- fes''2-- | R1*2 |
```
The double reeds now spell F5–D♭5 over the trumpets' A♭4/F4/D♭4, the flutes add A♭5, and the woodwind order stays natural (oboes above clarinets). The theme's lower octave keeps Tpt 1, Cl 1, Hn 3 and Vln II. The brass layout of 61–62 (do-not-touch) is unchanged. allpairs_par.py only loses pairs here (the oboe 2–flute and oboe 2–cello octave doublings on A♭–D♭) and gains none.

### 3. Note on the open list: "violas sustained 53–56 then triplets" no longer describes the score

In v47 the violas play the triplet figure from 53, and 53–58 repeats 37–42 pitch for pitch. The open item from c037 should be closed or restated as "B3 repeats B1's viola accompaniment literally". I make no change. The repeat is now offset by the violins' octave theme, horn 1's counter-line, clarinet 1 and bassoon 1 at 55–56, and the sextuplets at 59. In the reading's Adagio examples (Nos. 152, 171), florid accompaniment layers over sustained harmony in exactly this way.
