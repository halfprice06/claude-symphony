## Findings

None of these findings calls for a printed change. Six earlier passes over this reading have already adjusted or recorded every wind combination in the movement. The scan confirms what they found. No oboe or clarinet ever sits above the flutes. The B.cl, not a bassoon, is the lowest wind under the horns at 3-14. Every tutti chord is complete in the woodwinds alone. The only seconds between unlike woodwinds are the two already recorded (59, 81). The points below are new or correct an earlier record, so the next pass need not repeat them.

### 1. Bar 62 b1-2: bassoon 1's F4 inside the horn chord. Recorded, no change. Corrects c374

**Problem.** At 62 b1-2 the brass sound G♭1, G♭2, B♭3, D♭4, G♭4, B♭4. Bassoon 1 plays F4 between Hn 2's D♭4 and Hn 1's G♭4, on a pitch class no brass instrument has. Sebesky PDF 88 says a reed "wedged in between" the brass on a note of its own "will be lost in the overall sound". PDF 106 wants woodwinds above or below the brass, and says the bassoon is "no match for open brass". c374's table (row 12) said "every reed inside the brass range doubles a brass note" at 61-62. That is not true at this beat.

**Evidence and decision.** The F4 is not alone in the orchestra. Bassoon 1 at 61-62 (F3-D♭4 | F4-F♭4) shadows the cellos' A♭3-D♭4 | F4-F♭4, which double theme B's melody (Vln I, Fl 2, Ob 1, Cl 1 on F) two octaves down. PDF 88 objects to a note that is lost and also breaks a doubling "for no good reason". Here the bassoon keeps its doubling of the cellos, and the cellos carry the F. If the bassoon is covered at ff, nothing is lost. The fix that would put it in line with PDF 106, `ges'2--` for `f'2--` at 62 b1 in `bSecBsnOne` (doubling Hn 1), would break the bassoon's line for no audible gain. No change. Later citations should read "every reed inside the brass range doubles a brass note or the cellos' melody line".

### 2. Bars 14-15: winds hand over to muted strings with no overlap. Recorded, no change

**Problem.** At the end of 14 the horn solo, both bassoons, the B.cl and the harp all stop together. At 15 Vln II, violas and cellos enter con sord. pp. Sebesky's Ex. 131 (PDF 108) shows entries overlapping so that the connections are "barely discernible".

**Evidence and decision.** Ex. 131 is one continuous phrase passed from colour to colour. Bar 15 begins the b-phrase in a new colour (oboe 1 and the solo violin answering each other over muted strings), and the change falls at a phrase boundary. The common tones carry across the join: Hn 1 A♭3 goes to Vln II A♭4, the bassoons' C3 and A♭3 go to Vc C3 and Vla E♭4. Within phrases the score already chains its entries as Ex. 131 asks: 30-31 (Cb into Cbn), 36-37 (C.a. into Fl 1 on the pickup C5), 64-67, and the coda chain harp → timpani → Cl 1 → C.a./horns → Cl 2 at 83-92. An overlap at 14-15 (for example Bsn 2 holding C3 into 15 b1) would blur the a/b boundary, and the horn theme 7-14 is protected. No change.

### 3. Bar 77 b1-2: clarinets 1 and 2 an octave apart. Recorded, no change

**Problem.** Cl 1 doubles the theme (G5-E♭5-D♭5-C5-B♭4, with Fl 1 an octave above). Cl 2 holds G4 under it for two beats. PDF 104 asks for "the consonant interval of a third (or a tenth) between any two clarinets as much as possible".

**Evidence and decision.** Sebesky's rule is for clarinets voiced as a chordal section (Ex. 125). Here Cl 1 is a melodic doubling and Cl 2 is a held chord tone that passes into the 78 deceptive cadence. They are not a two-part clarinet chord. Cl 2 moves to the minor sixth (E♭5 over G4) on beat 2. The octave lasts one beat at mp<. No change.

### 4. Bars 87-88: the C.a. alone over muted horns, joined by clarinet 2 only at 89. Recorded, no change

**Problem.** PDF 106 says the C.a. has "limited projection when used in mixed combinations" and is "best used in unison with a clarinet as a lead line over soft brass". Ex. 130 shows that sound. At 87-88 the C.a. plays F4-F♭4 an octave above Hn 4's muted F3-F♭3, with Cl 1's D♭5 above it. Cl 2 joins the C.a. in unison on E♭4 only at 89.

**Evidence and decision.** The chart is about projection in a mixed ensemble at volume. At 87-88 the texture is pp: two muted horns, muted divided violas, Vln II con sord. on A♭4, and cellos and basses. The C.a. has nothing to compete with. From 89 the score uses exactly the Ex. 130 pairing. The staggered entry (C.a. at 87, Cl 2 at 89) is the last link of the coda chain. Starting Cl 2 at 87 would remove that link and would double the C.a.'s F♭, which is c150's echo of Hn 3 at 79-81. The C.a. with muted horns at 87-90 and the clarinet morendo are both protected. No change.
