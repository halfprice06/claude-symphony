## Findings

Every point where Unit 31 applies to this score already follows it. I found nothing that needs a change. The findings below are ranked by how much each place matters to the texture. Each one records what the reading confirms, so that a later cycle does not reopen it.

### 1. Bars 51-52: the eight-beat dominant now has the contrary pair c237 found missing (follows; no change)

**Bars:** 51-52. Vc, C.a., Vla, Vln II over the Cb's A♭1.

**What c237 found.** In v237 only the cor anglais moved here. Under Exercise 4 (PDF 583) there was no complementary middle voice. The usual fix, the m6-M10 exchange between V6/5 and V7 (PDF 564-565, Ex. 31-19b), needs C in the bass at 51, and the pivot at 50-51 is protected.

**Now.** Since c322 the cellos rise C3-D3-E♭3 (sul G since c411) while the cor anglais falls A♭4-G♭4-F4 and then turns to G♭4. The intervals between the two lines are:
- m13 (C3/A♭4) at 51 b1;
- d12 against the held C3 at 51 b3 (oblique);
- m10 (D3/F4) at 52 b1;
- M9 (E♭3/F4) at 52 b3, where the cello moves and the cor anglais holds;
- m10 (E♭3/G♭4) at 52 b4.

Ex. 31-19 is a m6-M10 exchange. This one goes from a minor 6th to a minor 10th. A&S say note-against-note motion is poor in that kind of exchange and recommend oblique motion and an omitted step (PDF 565, Ex. 31-21b). The passage does exactly that: there are two oblique steps. The cello's D♮3 is a chromatic passing tone in D♭. It is spelled as a raised note in a rising line, and it resolves upward to E♭3. The violas' detached eighths at 51 and their held C4 at 52 keep still, which matches A&S's remark that in such exchanges "the inner voices remain stationary" (PDF 564). The rhythm is complementary, as Exercise 4 asks:
- the violas move in eighths while the cello holds (51);
- the cello moves in halves while the violas hold (52).

**Fix:** none. Recording this closes c237 finding 3.

### 2. Bar 56 against bar 40: the second statement of theme B adds a contrary chromatic inner pair (follows; no change)

**Bars:** 40 (B1) and 56 (B3).

**Evidence.** At 40 the °7 on A resolves with the inner voices moving in parallel: Vln II E♭4-G♭4 goes to D♭4-F4, under flute C5-D♭5-C5-B♭4.

At 56 the same °7 resolves with two inner voices moving in contrary semitones:
- Bsn 1 G♭3-F3, falling;
- the counter-line (Cl 1, Hn 1) C4-D♭4, rising.

The interval between them widens from A4 (G♭3-C4) to m6 (F3-D♭4), which is the correct outward resolution of the augmented 4th. At the same time the counter-line moves in minor 10ths with the bass A2-B♭2. A&S name parallel 10ths "as important an organizing element in chromatic textures as in diatonic ones" (PDF 554, Ex. 31-5). They give contrary chromatic lines as the second organising method (PDF 563, PDF 580 point 6). B3 therefore varies B1 through its inner voices, without touching melody or bass. That is the kind of difference between statements that a master's re-scoring shows.

One detail: on the downbeat of 56 the counter-line's C4 sits an octave under the tune's C5. The two lines then separate in contrary motion, from the octave to the major 6th D♭4/B♭4 at b3. The same happens at 54 b1 (D♭4/D♭5). This is inner-voice doubling of a chord tone in a full texture, not an outer-voice fault. **Fix:** none.

### 3. Bars 46-47, 59-60, 77-79 and 80-81: inner lines converge on or leave each goal by contrary semitones (follows; no change)

c237 recorded the first three; the A′ climax adds a fourth case.
- **46-47:** Vla B♭2-B2 rises while Vln II G♭4-E♯4 falls, from compound m6 to compound A4, into C♯7.
- **59-60:** the counter-line (Hn 1, Vc, Bsn 1) falls F♭4-E♭4 while the bass rises G2-A♭2. This is A&S's ♮4-♯4 °7 resolving to V (PDF 561, Ex. 31-14).
- **77-79:** Vc and Bsn 1 rise C♭4-C4-D♭4 while the bass falls F♭-E♭-D♭.
- **80-81:** the inner F4-F♭4-E♭4 (Hn 3, Vla, Ob 2, Fl 2) falls by semitone through iiø7 with F♭ and V♭9 to V7. Against it the cellos and Hn 1·2 rise A♭3-B♭3-D♭4 and stop on the 7th, which resolves to C4 at 82. The melody falls A♭6-G6. The ♭9 F♭ resolves in its own register, as A&S require for ♭6 (PDF 572: "the foreign tone, ♭6, is not left unresolved").

The rising and falling lines meet on the chord they define, the "larger direction" A&S ask the reader to hear (PDF 564). **Fix:** none.

### 4. The motto tail is never set against a chromatically rising bass (follows; no change)

**Bars:** 8, 24, 72, 33, 68, 85-89.

**Evidence.** Ex. 31-21a shows that a descending chromatic line against a rising chromatic line, note against note, produces "poor sonorities" (PDF 565). The tail E♭-D-D♭-C is set three ways in this score:
- **8, 24, 72:** over a descending-fifths bass F-B♭-E♭-A♭, with the inner voice A♭-A♭-G making chromatic parallel tritones with the tail. That is parallel chromatic motion of the PDF 552 kind.
- **33, 68:** over a held pedal. The motion is oblique.
- **85-89:** over a held pedal and then the falling plagal bass F-D♭-A♭. At 85-86 the violas' E♭4-F4 rises against the clarinet's E♭5-D5 (octave to M6), and from 86 the clarinet's D♭5 holds over the inner F4-F♭4 (oblique). This is Ex. 31-21b's remedy: oblique motion inside an expanded IV.

The rule c242 drew from this example therefore still holds at every statement. **Fix:** none.

### 5. Bar 21: the A6-M10 exchange with its passing 6/4 is intact (follows; no change)

The basses play D♭2-E♭2-F♭2 against the solo violin's F5-(G5)-E♭5-D5. The outer voices go from M10 (IV), through the octave E♭/E♭ with A♭ and C above (passing 6/4), to A6 (French sixth on F♭). This matches Ex. 31-20b (PDF 565) and the boxed note on PDF 566. Vln II falls F4-C4-B♭3 against the rising bass. The upper cellos take the sixth's A♭3 to G3, not F♭3-G3 (c192), so no augmented 2nd appears. **Fix:** none (c237's change; standing list).

### 6. Devices the movement correctly does not use (not applicable; recorded)

The movement has no 7-6 chain over a chromatic bass, no descending chromatic 5-6, no chain of °7s or augmented triads, no extended chain of voice exchanges, and no octatonic or whole-tone passage. Unit 31 places these in transitional and often grief-laden or virtuoso contexts (PDF 552, 556, 558, 562, 567, 578-580). The Dvořák 9/ii model gets its chromaticism from single passing chords, and this score does the same. Equal division appears only in the key plan: C (end of movement I), then A♭, then F♭/E (62-63 and the scherzo's E). A♭ stays the tonal centre, as A&S require when they warn that a predominance of equal division "would weaken the gravitational attraction of the tonic" (PDF 572). **Fix:** none. Adding any of these devices would move the movement away from its model.

### 7. Bar 47, bassoon 2: written diminished 3rd B♭2-G♯2 across the key change (follows; no change)

The bassoon's held line B♭2 (45-46) goes to G♯2 at 47, where F♯ minor begins. The sound is a whole step (B♭-A♭). The written d3 follows the reading's notation rule: respell to point to the coming goal, as Beethoven writes D♯ and G♯ in bar 16 of Ex. 31-10 (PDF 560). The rule also requires the enharmonic change at the point of transformation (PDF 564: "enharmonic transformation is required"; PDF 573). G♯ is the fifth of the C♯7 that the new key signature governs. **Fix:** none.
