## What else I studied

I read MOLA *Guidelines for Music Preparation* PDF 4 and 7. Page 4 covers tempo marks above the top staff and above Vln I, bar numbers and rehearsal letters. Page 7 covers score staff size of at least 4 mm, continuous barlines within families, "avoid over-crowding to provide clarity within each measure", and a consistent amount of music per system. I counted ledger lines for every note from score.midi with a script (staff, clef and bar), and listed every bar where the two halves of a part-combined staff sound the same pitch at the same time as separate voices. Only Hn 1·2 79–82 is a real case; the flutes at 62 have a single shared note inside a chord, which is normal. I looked at all 5 pages of v05 at 60–300 dpi and all 8 pages of the fixed copy.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. System and page layout: 19–22 bars per system crush the climax (all bars; worst 1–19, 20–37, 75–96). Applied.
**Problem.** With automatic layout, page 1 holds bars 1–19 in one system, page 2 holds 20–37, and page 5 holds 75–96. The climax 75–82 is squeezed into about 40% of the width while the empty coda bars 83–90 are spread out. Page 3 has a 7-stave system (38–46) followed by half a page of white space. Phrase lengths and system lengths have nothing to do with each other.
**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 7 says to avoid over-crowding and to keep the amount of music per system consistent. Gould treats rhythmic and symbol spacing as ground rules (PDF 18 contents). At this density, ledger lines and accidentals in Fl/Vln I 76–81 and Hn 3·4 79–82 are packed into a few millimetres, which defeats the legibility that Gould's stave-space proportions are meant to protect (PDF 19, 24).
**Fix (global.ily).** Put `\break` at phrase and rehearsal boundaries, giving systems of 1–6, 7–14, 15–22, 23–30, 31–36, 37–40, 41–44, 45–48, 49–52, 53–56, 57–60, 61–64, 65–70, 71–74, 75–78, 79–82, 83–90 and 91–96. The 4/4 bars and the climax get 4 bars per system; the 3/4 phrases get 6–8. For example:
```
  s1*4 \break s1*4 \break            % 37-44
  s2.*4 \break s2.*3 \tempo ... \mt 50 s2. \break \mt 48 s2.*2 \mt 46 s2. \mt 44 s2. \break   % 71-82
```
Result: 8 pages. LilyPond puts two small systems on a page where they fit (15–22 with 23–30, the 45–52 pair, and so on), and the tutti 61–64 and 79–82 each get a full-width system.

### 2. Horns 3·4, 79–82: 4–5 ledger lines at ff in treble clef. Applied.
**Problem.** Hn 3·4 play Ab3/Db3, Ab3/Db3–C3, G3/Bb2 and Ab3/C3 at ff in treble clef. Db3 and C3 sit on 4 ledger lines and Bb2 on 5.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21: stay in one clef "up to at least three ledger lines" and change after that. PDF 22–23: put the change at a barline after rests, and return to the usual clef after the passage at the end of the system where the player stops.
**Fix.** In aTwo.ily, use `aTwoHnThree = { R2.*8 | \clef bass aes2.\ff ...` (and the same in aTwoHnFour). In coda.ily, use `codaHnThree = { \clef treble R2.*8 | ...` (and the same in codaHnFour). With the break before 83 from finding 1, the warning treble clef prints at the end of the 79–82 system, as Gould shows on PDF 23. Horn bass clef at concert pitch in a C score is unambiguous.

### 3. Horns 1·2, 79–82: unison printed as two voices instead of a2 (known side effect from cycle 4). Applied.
**Problem.** Hn 1's slur starts at 78 beat 3 and Hn 2 enters at 79, so the part-combiner keeps them apart. Every note is printed with both an up-stem and a down-stem and there is no "a2".
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28–29: double-stemmed writing is for two parts on one stave, so two stems tell the reader that two different lines are present. Here there is one line.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHnOne).**
```
  ... bes2 des'4 | c'2)\f c'4 |
  \partCombineUnisono des'4(\ff c'4 bes4 | aes4 bes4 c'4 | bes2\> des'4 | c'2.)\p \partCombineAutomatic |
```
The slur now starts at 79, because a slur cannot cross the switch between voices (keeping it from 78 gave "unterminated slur" warnings). The pickup C4 at 78 beat 3 stays unslurred. The composer can restore the pickup slur another way: give Hn 2 the pickup as well (`r2 c'4(\f` at 78) and start `\partCombineUnisono` there. Result: one stem per note and "a2" at 79.

### 4. Missing "1." labels on solo re-entries at 47 (Ob 1, the S-head quotation), 53 (Hn 1) and 57 (Fl 1). Applied.
**Problem.** The part-combiner prints "1." only when the state changes. Ob 1 last played alone at 15–22, Hn 1 at 7–14 and Fl 1 at 37–44, so their next solo entries, several systems later, carry no label. At 47 the player cannot tell who has the cyclic quotation.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 28: one stave holding two players has to make clear which part sounds. MOLA PDF 4–5 expects the score to show unambiguously who plays.
**Fix (bSec.ily).** Use `cis'4(\p^\markup \bold "1."` at 47, `aes8(\mp^\markup \bold "1."^\markup \italic "espr."` at 53 and `aes''4(\mf\<^\markup \bold "1."` at 57.

### 5. Common-time symbol at 37 in a mixed 3/4–4/4 movement. Applied.
**Problem.** The metre change at 37 prints C, while the 3/4 bars print numerals. Using one style makes the 3/4 → 4/4 → 3/4 changes at 37 and 65 easier to read at a glance. Gould's time-signature chapter is outside the excerpt, so this is a minor house-style point.
**Fix (global.ily).** Add `\numericTimeSignature` after `\accidentalStyle Score.modern`.

### 6. Ledger lines above 3 at the octave-sign threshold (harp LH Eb1/F1 at 8, 10, 13, 24, 26, 29, 72–81; Fl 1/Vln I Bb6 at 76 and 79). Not applied.
These are past Gould's three-line guideline (PDF 21), but no clef change is available, so octave signs are the only option. That is the subject of PDF 42–48 (cycle 25). Harpists and violinists read these routinely, so I recommend no change in this cycle and flag them for critic 025.
