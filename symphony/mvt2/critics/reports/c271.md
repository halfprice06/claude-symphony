## Findings

This is the third pass on this chapter, and the score already follows it nearly everywhere. c021 and c111 applied its two main points: the flute alone for the antecedent at 37–40, and the cor anglais a sixth above the bass clarinet at 32–34. Every first statement of a tune is a solo of simple timbre. Doubling starts only where the music is loud and broad (57, 61, 77–79), and each doubling uses a pair the book names. I found one small point the reading supports and tested it (finding 1). The other findings record checks, so later critics do not need to reopen them.

### 1. Bar 37 b1: the cor anglais holds theme B's first note for a full beat in its upper register (applied, optional)

**Bars.** 36 b3–37 b1. The C.a. plays the pickup C5 and lands on F5 at 37 b1, in unison with flute 1's first note of theme B. It then rests. Flute 1 holds the F5 for two beats.

**Problem.** The tune's first held note starts as a cor anglais + flute unison and turns into flute alone on beat 2. The C.a.'s F5 is written C6, in its upper register. The flute's F5 is in its lower-middle register. Rimsky-Korsakov describes the flute + oboe unison this way: "played softly, the flute will predominate in the low, the oboe in the upper register" (PDF 65, a). So for the first beat the theme's opening note sounds mostly like the double reed. The colour then changes in the middle of the note. The flute is meant to be the simple timbre of the tune's first statement (PDF 66–67; c021).

**Evidence.** bSec.ily `bSecCorAnglais = { f''4) r4 r2 | ...`. c139 made the C.a. continue to F5 for a reason: in a phrase passed between instruments, the last note of one coincides with the first note of the next (Rimsky PDF 125–126). c179 recorded the dovetail without changing it.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-271/work).**
```lilypond
bSecCorAnglais = {
  f''8) r8 r4 r2 | R1*3 |
```
The C.a. still attacks with the flute, so c139's rule is kept and the rising fourth stays in one instrument. The double-reed colour lasts half a beat instead of a whole beat, and the flute holds the note alone sooner. Nothing else changes: no pitches, no harmony, and no MIDI parallels (allpairs_par.py output identical to v271's). This is a small change. If the composer prefers the full quarter, the cost is small.

### 2. Bars 61–62 and 79–80: melody over three octaves against brass (recorded, no change)

Rimsky-Korsakov warns that a melody over three or four octaves is hard to balance (PDF 76), and that the brass always predominates (PDF 74). At 61–62 the augmented head A♭–D♭ | F–F♭ is carried by Fl 2 + Vln I (from 61 b3), Vln II + Cl 2 (61) + Ob 1 (62 b3), and Vc + Bsn 1 (62). Hn 3·4 (A♭4–B♭4–C♭5) and Hn 1·2 (F4–A♭4) hold chord tones in the melody's middle octave at f and ff. The top octave is clear. It is the violins' C6→D♭6→F6→F♭6 line, with flute 1's A♭6–A6 above it. That is the line a listener follows. The brass layout is protected with reasons (c264 close order, PDF 100/103; c218; c181 A♭6–A6–B♭6; c261 Vln II + Vc octaves). The reading does not answer any of those reasons. At 79–80 the distribution Fl 1 + Vln I / Ob 1 + Cl 1 + Vln II / C.a. + Vla is the natural three-octave order of PDF 69. No change.

### 3. Bars 41–44: flute in sixths and tenths above the cor anglais (recorded, no change)

Ex. 57 (PDF 229) puts a soft cantilena in flute + cor anglais octaves over the same accompaniment type as this score: viola triplets, harp and pizzicato bass. That might suggest doubling the C.a.'s consequent at the octave instead of the flute's descant. But in Ex. 57 the cor anglais lies above the violas. Here the C.a. (A♭3–B♭4) lies among the viola pizzicato chords (D♭3–G♭4). A flute an octave above it would start on a weak A♭4. The current pairing is the "sixths with instruments of different kinds" that PDF 70 prefers, in natural order. The lead (C.a., mp) is one level above the descant (flute, p). Protected since c111. No change.

### 4. Bars 48–50: violins II above the quotation (checked; do not re-raise)

I checked whether Vln II doubles the cor anglais quotation in unison, which would go against PDF 66–67 and PDF 78's note. It does not. Vln II stays above the C.a. and never meets it in unison: an octave A5/A4 at 49 b1 (protected, c202), a fifth and a fourth at 49 b2–3, a third at 49 b4, a fourth at 50 b1. At 50 b3–4 its D5 is a third and a fourth above the C.a.'s B4–A4. I tested one variant, F♯5 held to b3 and E5 at b4. It makes fifths with the C.a. (F♯5/B4 → E5/A4) and was reverted. c221's description stands.

### 5. Bars 53–56: horn 1 + clarinet 1 in unison on the counter-line (recorded, no change)

PDF 74–75 says the brass predominates in a wind + brass unison, and the added wind "yields a finer legato effect than when the latter instrument plays alone". Horn + clarinet is one of the pairs it names. Under the violins' theme at p–mf, the counter-line keeps the horn colour, and the clarinet helps the slurred leaps. Protected (c201, c257). No change.

### 6. Brass melody: horn tail at 8 and 68, trombone quotation at 63–64 (recorded, no change)

PDF 73 says chromatic melody suits brass less. It makes two exceptions: the horn in piano passages (PDF 72) and "vigorous phrases" with chromatic notes. Both horn tails are p and dolce, the first exception. The trombone quotation (ff marcato, chromatic, alone over string tremolo, then p) has the layout of Ex. 74 (Sheherazade ii, PDF 245), the second. PDF 75 rules out a woodwind octave above the trombone, and the score has none. No change.

### 7. Bars 87–89: muted horns with divided violas under the cor anglais (recorded, keep)

Hn 3·4 con sord. play in unison with the divided violas (A♭3; F3–F♭3–E♭3), and the C.a. plays F4–F♭4–E♭4 an octave above. This is Rimsky-Korsakov's "Viola + Horn" pairing (PDF 79) and the setting of Ex. 92 (Golden Cockerel, violas con sord. + horn, PDF 269). Protected (c259, c269). Keep.

### 8. Theme B's antecedent (37–40, 53–56) is still nearly all steps (context only)

From the melody lens, the largest remaining point is still the contour c251 (F1) and c261 left open. This chapter is about how a melody is distributed among instruments, not about contour, so it gives no reason to change the tune. Every statement of theme B is scored as the chapter asks. I make no new proposal. c251's optional variant at 55 is unchanged.
