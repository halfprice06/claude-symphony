## What else I studied

- **Master movement: Rachmaninoff, Symphony No. 2, iii (Adagio, A major).** It is not in the local corpus. I used my knowledge of the score and program notes. The movement has three melodic elements: a rising violin phrase, a long clarinet solo that "circles around a few notes in a narrow compass, never repeating itself exactly", and the first movement's motto, extended. The clarinet's close is delayed by two false cadences, and the violins finally take the tonic. In the reprise the two principal melodies are interwoven in counterpoint (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._2_(Rachmaninoff); https://bloomingtonsymphony.org/2014/02/13/the-passion-of-rachmaninoff-concert-preview-no-4/; https://www.hollywoodbowl.com/musicdb/pieces/3935/symphony-no-2-in-e-minor-op-27).
- **v89 → v99 comparison.** I diffed all section files and read critiques v90–v99.
- **Own scans of v99 and my scratch build.** I wrote a pairwise parallel scan over every voice of every track (each chord member ranked from the bottom, so double stops, divisi and the shared staves are covered) and a low-interval-limit scan of every sounding sonority. Both scripts are in /tmp/critic-099/tools.
- **Aldwell & Schachter** on parallel fifths (Unit 5), used as support.

**Holistic judgement.** A conductor reading v99 would take most of it for professional work. The string-free horn solo, the woodwind choir over the tonic pedal, the quotation set apart by tempo, the dovetailed colour changes (30–31, 82–83) and the staged release at 81–83 are all handled as a master would handle them. The deceptive cadence at 78 delaying the close plays the same part as Rachmaninoff's two false cadences.

Rachmaninoff does two things this movement does not:
- His long melody never repeats itself exactly. Here, 71–74 repeat 23–26 note for note in melody, cello counter-melody and bass; only the viola eighths and Cl 2 are new.
- His reprise combines two themes. Theme B does not appear after bar 61.

Both points touch protected material (cello counter-melody 71–78, c051; reprise bass A♭ at 71, c057), so I raise them only as observations for the composer. They are not findings with a fix. A theme-B reminiscence in the coda (83–84, where only harp and timpani play) would add a staff and is likely to cost the 8-page layout.

**Against v89, one thing got worse:** the parallel fifths at 62→63 (finding 1). Everything else from v90–v99 is an improvement. The c089/c092 fixes removed every pairwise fifth that v89 still had (harp LH at 8, 14, 72–73; the bass/horn/cello fifths at 58 b4). My all-voice scan of v99 finds only the one pair below.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 62→63: parallel fifths between the violas and the bass, a regression from v95 (applied)

**Problem.** At 62 b3 the violas play `<ces' fes'>` (sounding B3/E4) over the bass F♭ (E1/E2). At 63 they play `<e' c''>` over A. The lower viola note B3→E4 moves in parallel perfect fifths with the bass E→A (double basses E1→A1, bass trombone E2→A2).

**Evidence.**
- My all-voice scan of v99 flags exactly these two pairs (Vla/B.tbn and Vla/Cb at 63 b1) and nothing else in the movement. allpairs_par.py does not see them, because the viola's lower note is not the top of its staff at the moment the line moves.
- c032 had removed the same fault at the same bar ("was E4-A4: fifths and octaves with the bass E-A"). c094 (v95) brought it back by moving the tremolo to E4–C5 without changing 62 b3.
- Casanelles PDF 274 and 277–278: the fifth is the lowest non-octave partial of the bass. A viola fifth that moves with the bass fuses with it as reinforced partials of the same fundamental, so at the arrival of the A-minor quotation the inner voice is heard as part of the bass rather than as a voice of its own.
- Aldwell & Schachter Unit 5 is the ordinary rule.

**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bar 62):**
```
<bes des'>2:32 <ces' fes'>2:32   →   <bes des'>2:32 <aes fes'>2:32
```
The upper note F♭4 is held into 63 as E4, the common tone that c094 wanted kept on the D string. The lower note moves B♭3→A♭3 by step, and G♯3 (= A♭3) over E is a third, so 62→63 is third→fifth with no parallel. The fifth of the E chord stays in Cl 2 and Hn 3·4 (B4). The new double stop is a minor sixth on the G and D strings, the same layout as the double stop before it.

### 2. Bar 62 b3: the timpani's A♭2 roll is a low third under the F♭ chord (applied)

**Problem.** On 62 b3 the timpani roll A♭2 (as G♯, the leading tone of A minor) f. Below them the tuba, contrabassoon and basses hold F♭1, and the bass trombone, bassoon 2 and bass clarinet hold F♭2. The result is a major third F♭2–A♭2 at the bottom of a forte tutti, the only one in the movement. My low-interval scan flags it at 62.3–62.4, and nowhere else in a tutti.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 274: the series puts octave and fifth at the bottom, and the third enters only at partials 4–5, two octaves above the fundamental. Here it sits one octave above.
- PDF 275–276: the drum's spectrum is partly inharmonic and its strokes put noise at the onset, in the same band as the brass fundamentals. A rolled third there thickens the bass instead of colouring the chord.
- The G♯ already sounds in a clear register in Tbn 2 (A♭3), Hn 1·2 and Ob 2 (A♭4), Cl 1 (A♭5) and Fl/Vln I (A♭6). The drum adds no new pitch.
- c056's aim (the roll damped before the A-minor downbeat) is kept.

**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecTimp`, bar 62):**
```
des2:32 aes,4:32~ aes,8 r8   →   des4.:32~ des8 r2
```
The roll on D♭ ends with a tied stroke at b2½ and the drums are silent through the F♭ chord. The low brass and basses keep the root alone, as Mahler's pedal does in Fig. 6.1.

### 3. Bar 29: duplicate pp on the Horns 3·4 staff (applied; closes the open item)

**Problem.** Horn 4 enters pp on 29 b1, and horn 3 enters on b3 with its own pp, so the shared staff prints pp twice in one bar.

**Evidence.**
- Casanelles PDF 271–272: a dynamic names the resulting level of the line. On a shared staff the second pp states nothing new.
- The task rules require equal, single dynamics on part-combined staves.
- HISTORY lists this as open since c097.

**Fix.**
- aOne.ily: `r2 des'4\pp` → `r2 des'4-\tag #'part \pp`.
- score.ly line 93: `\pc \hnThree \removeWithTag #'part \hnFour` → `\pc \removeWithTag #'part \hnThree \removeWithTag #'part \hnFour`. Without this, horn 3's part-only tags are not removed in the score. Horn 3 had no other `'part` tags, so nothing else changes.
- The horn 3 part keeps its pp.

### 4. Bar 61: the "low third" open item should be closed (no change)

**Problem.** The open list has carried "low third at 61" since c004: D♭3 (B.tbn, B.cl., Bsn 2, timpani) with F3 (Tbn 2, Bsn 1) over D♭2.

**Evidence.**
- D♭3–F3 lies above the usual lower limit for a major third (B♭2), and my scan does not flag it.
- Spaced D♭2–D♭3–F3–A♭3–D♭4, the chord follows the series layout of Casanelles PDF 274: octave, then third and fifth.
- The obvious repair (Tbn 2 or Bsn 1 to A♭3) moves E♭3→A♭3 against the bass A♭→D♭, which makes new parallel fifths with the bass.

**Fix.** None. Mark the item closed in HISTORY.md.
