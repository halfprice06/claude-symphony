## Findings

### 1. Violas 25 b3, 26 b1-2, 28 b3 and Vln II 39 b3-4: single notes in divided passages (applied)
**Problem.** Inside a divided passage a single stemmed note reads as "one half only" or as an unmarked return to unison. At these four places both halves play the same pitch (c127 and c142 reduced the chords to one pitch on purpose). This is the c325 open point.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 63, "Chords with single-stemmed unison notes: Perfect unisons. Place one notehead each side of the stem" (example: half, quarter and ledger-line unisons on one stem). That notation says "both halves, same note" without extra words and without a "unis."/"div." pair every bar. PDF 60 allows string chords on one stem. The composer declined two-stem unisons at c325 because they double notes in score.midi; this notation adds no note.
**Fix.** A music function in global.ily that draws the second head on the far side of the stem and keeps the head's own extent (so the stem attaches normally), with extra spacing width:
```
unisHead = #(define-music-function (note) (ly:music?)
  #{ \tweak NoteHead.stencil
       #(lambda (grob)
          (let* ((s (ly:note-head::print grob))
                 (stem (ly:grob-object (ly:grob-parent grob X) 'stem))
                 (dir (if (ly:grob? stem) (ly:grob-property stem 'direction) UP))
                 (w (interval-length (ly:stencil-extent s X))))
            (ly:make-stencil (ly:stencil-expr (ly:stencil-add s (ly:stencil-translate-axis s (* dir (- w 0.13)) X)))
                             (ly:stencil-extent s X) (ly:stencil-extent s Y))))
     \tweak NoteHead.extra-spacing-width #'(-1.6 . 1.6)
     #note #})
```
aOne.ily, aOneVla: `<c' es'>2 \unisHead f'4 | \unisHead es'2 r4 |` and `<des' f'>4 <des' f'>4\f\> \unisHead f'4 |`. bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo: `<des' bes'>2 \unisHead ges'2 |`. Checked in the scratch PDF and in the viola part: one head left of the stem, one right, as Gould's example; score.midi is byte-identical to v335. Use it only on notes without an accidental and only where the passage is divided (it would collide with an accidental on a down-stem note).

### 2. Harp left hand 8 and 72: stems up-down-up across one bar (applied)
**Problem.** `<f,, f,>4 <bes,, bes,>4 <es,, es,>4` (written an octave up under 8 bassa) takes stems up, down, up: the B♭ octave's top note is the further one, so the default turns it down. The bar is one gesture of three rolled octaves.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 61: "the direction may be determined by context. The aim is to achieve a consistent stem direction within a phrase, bar or half-bar", with a four-chord "rather than" example of exactly this flip. The composer already applies this at 11 and 75 (`\once \stemUp des,4`, c095).
**Fix.** aOne.ily and aTwo.ily: `<f,, f,>4\arpeggio \once \stemUp <bes,, bes,>4\arpeggio ...`. Checked: three up-stems at 8 and 72; nothing else moves.

### 3. Bar 81, oboes: the flat of the displaced F♭5 sits in front of the G5 (recorded, no change)
Ob 1 G5 (up) and Ob 2 F♭5 (down) are a second apart; LilyPond displaces the lower part to the right as Gould PDF 62 asks, and puts the flat in the accidental column to the left of both heads, where it stands next to the G5. PDF 64 says an accidental is "always best placed beside the notehead to which it applies". The rule for accidentals in double-stemmed seconds is on book p. 91, outside the excerpt, and the usual practice is the one printed here; the flat's vertical position (on the F line) identifies its note, and Ob 2 had F♭5 on 80 b3. No change proposed.

### 4. Equal values on separate stems (recorded, no change)
Bn 1·2 at 8 (quarters) and 14 (dotted halves), Hn 3·4 at 80 (half + quarter in both), plus c205's 30, 69, 82. PDF 60 asks for one stem, but each part carries its own slur or tie into a bar where the rhythms part again; c205 tested chord mode and got unterminated-tie and slur warnings. PDF 65 covers these as double-stemmed independent parts. Bassoons 7-14 are also under the horn-solo protection.

### 5. Dots, unisons and adjacent-note chords (recorded, all follow)
Dotted chords on ledger lines (Fl 79, Hn 3·4 87-89) give each dot its own space; Vla 88 and Hn 3·4 88 dot correctly; unlike-value unisons in shared staves print as offset heads (PDF 65); adjacent-note string chords put the lower note left (PDF 61-62).
