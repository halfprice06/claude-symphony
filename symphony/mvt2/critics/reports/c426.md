# Critic 426: playability (brass), Adler ch. 9-10

Version: v426. Lens: playability, instrument by instrument. Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 9 "Introduction to Brass Instruments" and ch. 10 "Individual Brass Instruments" (adler1 PDF 282-329, adler2 PDF 1-13). Every text page in the range was read in full, and every page in the range was rendered at 110 dpi. I looked at the example pages directly (PDF 284-292, 296-297, 301-311, 317-318, 328-329; adler2 1-4, 7-11). The range and register charts (Ex. X-4, X-5, X-68, X-69, X-72, X-73, X-85, X-86) were re-rendered at 250 dpi and read from the image, not the OCR.

Result: no change. Every brass note and breath in v426 is inside what the chapter allows. The few places that lean on an exception (Hn 4 above its usual register at 61-62, Hn 1's 17-second rest before the 67 solo) were recorded by c046, c086, c286 and c336, and the reading gives no new reason to reopen them. I did not edit the scratch copy, so no new build was needed. The v426 copy in /tmp/critic-426/work has an empty build-warnings.txt and 0 outer-voice parallels, and its parts book is 30 pages.

## Findings

None of the points below needs a change. They are ranked by how close each comes to a real playing risk, so the composer can see what was checked and why it holds.

**1. Hn 1 endurance, 53-67 (no change).** Hn 1 plays from 53 b4 to the end of 62 without a rest bar, about 37 seconds at 60-64 and then 54 to the quarter. It takes only bar-end breaths, after 56, 58 and 60. The passage ends with 8.9 s of ff at written C5-E♭5. Its next entry is the p dolce solo at 67 on written B♭4. Timed from global.ily, the rest is 63 (4.4 s) + 64 (5.4 s) + 65-66 (6.9 s) = 16.7 s. Adler asks for "frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate" (adler1 PDF 292), and says the high register is taxing "all the time, so periodic rests are most welcome" (PDF 303). Here the high part lasts only 61-62, and the solo at 67 sits in the middle register (written B♭4 down to F4), the "deep and solid" to "bright" zone of Ex. X-5. It starts softly but is not exposed: Bsn 1 enters at 68 and there is a timpani roll. c286 and c336 recorded the same reading. No change.

**2. Hn 3·4 on the top line at 61-62 (no change, protected).** Hn 4 is a "low horn" (adler1 PDF 303, Ex. X-6), yet it doubles Hn 3 up to written G♭5 at f, above Hn 1 and Hn 2. Adler's division into high and low horns is a convention, not a range limit. Written G♭5 is inside the range of Ex. X-4, and the two bars are tongued half and whole notes at f after a breath, not a sustained high line. The doubling was settled at c086 for weight, and c336 recorded it against this same chapter. I found no new reason to reopen it.

**3. Cold entries at 61 (Hn 2, Hn 3) and 63 (Tbn 1) (no change).** Hn 2 has no notes before 61 and enters ff on written A♭4. Hn 3 last played at 30 and enters f on written E♭5. Tbn 1 enters ff "marcato" on 63's second eighth after 62 bars of rest. Adler's only caution about attacks concerns soft high and very low notes (adler1 PDF 284). These are loud attacks in the middle register, and each part has a cue in the bars before (Hn 1 cue in the Hn 2-4 parts at 57-60, B.tbn cue in the Tbn 1 part at 61-62). Rimsky-Korsakov's rule that brass re-enter pp or ff after long rests (PDF 136, used at c349) is met. No change.

**4. Trombone slide motion at 63-64 (no change).** Ex. X-71 (adler1 PDF 329) warns against fast alternation between positions I and VII. Tbn 1's quotation uses E3 (II), C4 (III), B3 (IV), B♭3 (I), A3 (II), G♯3 (III), F3 (I) and E3 (II): adjacent positions, in eighths at Largamente 54. B.tbn D2 and E2 at 64 lie on the F attachment (adler2 PDF 1-2, Ex. X-74) as quarter notes at 42-48. The slurs over notes from different harmonic series are the soft-tongued legato Adler describes (adler2 PDF 3). Playable.

**5. Tuba and bass trombone in the lowest octave at 60-62 (no change).** The tuba plays only A♭1, D♭2, G♭1 and F♭1, all in the "deep and heavy" zone of Ex. X-86. Adler says such notes, "used as slow-moving bass notes, … can be very well controlled, and they will be effective at any dynamic from ff to pp" (adler2 PDF 8). These are whole and half notes, pp< then f, with the c286 breath after 61. F♭1 (E1) on a CC tuba is a fourth-valve fingering, easy at this speed.

**6. Mute plan, 82-92 (no change).** "Mettere sord." at 82 leaves 82-86, about 17 s, before "con sord." at 87. The words appear in the score and in both the Hn 3 and Hn 4 parts, as Adler requires (adler1 PDF 295). "Via sord." at 91 and the scherzo's "senza sord." meet his rule that the open sound must be marked (PDF 308). A non-transposing mute is assumed, so the concert pitches stand (PDF 293, 307).

**7. Horn ranges and registers (no change).** Horns sound E♭3-C♭5 (written B♭3-G♭5), against Ex. X-4's written C2-C6. All soft horn writing lies between written B♭3 and E♭5. Written F5 occurs softly only at 54-55, reached by a slur. Muted Hn 3·4 at 87-90 sit at written B♭3-E♭4 on held notes, which suits PDF 303's advice that low notes be sustained.
