## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 81 b1: the V♭9 lacks its 7th; clarinet 2 leaves the D♭ a beat early (applied)

**Problem.** 80 b3 is iiø7 with ♭6̂ (B♭–D♭–F♭–A♭), 81 is V7 with the F♭ held over the E♭ root, the protected V♭9 of the hemiola. A&S describe this sound twice: a °7 "results from a neighbor or suspension resolving to the root of V7 … V7 is the functional chord" (PDF 378–379, Ex. 22-9), and the °7 of A♭ through ♭6̂ is G–B♭–D♭–F♭, the chord of Chopin's A♭ mazurka (PDF 393, Ex. 23-9). In v292 the chord on 81 b1 is E♭–G–B♭–F♭ with no D♭. D♭ arrives only on b2, with the counter-line (Bsn 1, Hn 1·2, Vc) and as an eighth in the tune. The one instrument holding D♭ through 80, clarinet 2 (D♭5), breathes after 80 and leaps a diminished fifth down to G4. G is already in five other parts on that beat (G3 Hn 4, G4 C.a./Vla, G5 Ob 1/Cl 1/Vln II, G6 Fl/Vln I).

**Reading.** When VII°7 follows II, its tone shared with II is prepared as a common tone (PDF 377, Ex. 22-7b). D♭ is the 3rd of iiø7 and the 7th of V7, so it should be held over the bar. The held D♭5 then resolves to C5 in the tune at 81 b3 (Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II D♭5 → C5), one register and one beat away. That is the transferred resolution of Ex. 22-5 (PDF 376).

**Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoClTwo`, bars 80–81), applied:**
`des''2. \breathe | g'2.\> |` → `des''2.~ | des''4\> \tag #'part \breathe g'2 |`

The tie gives 81 b1 the complete V7♭9 (E♭–G–B♭–D♭–F♭). The protected F♭s in Hn 3, Ob 2 and Vla are unchanged. The breath moves to after 81 b1 and prints only in the part: on the shared staff, a comma before clarinet 1's slurred eighths would read as clarinet 1 breathing. The D♭5 is held about 7.6 s at ff (79 b2–81 b1, crotchet 48–46), within the clarinet's limits. The leap D♭5–G4 now comes after the tie and returns inside the interval to A♭4 at 82, as before. The hairpin still starts at 81 b1 with clarinet 1's (equal dynamics on the shared staff).

**On the protections.** "V♭9 at 80–81" is protected for the F♭ resolution and the hemiola. Both are untouched; the change only adds the 7th of the chord. c034 chose G4 at 81 to avoid D♭–B♭ octaves with B♭4. G4 is kept from 81 b2, so that reason still holds.

### 3. Bars 19 b3–20 b1: vii°6 of F minor, upper d5 resolves outward (no change recommended)

At 19 b3 Vla E4 and Ob 1 B♭4 form a d5 over G3. At 20 they go to C4 and A♭4, a sixth, instead of inward to F–A♭ (PDF 375: between upper voices "diminished 5ths also tend to resolve normally"). The leading tone falls in an inner voice so that the F-minor chord has its 5th, and the viola's C4–D♭4–C4–B♭3 is the exact answer to the oboe's bar 19 (c017). Viola F4 at 20 b1 would resolve the d5 but break the imitation and leave the oboe's arrival without a 5th. The irregular resolution is in an inner voice at pp under the oboe, so I record it and recommend no change.

### 4. Bars 78 and 81: ♭6̂ resolved in another voice (no change)

The viola's F♭4 at 78 b2 leaves to C4, and E♭ arrives in the bass (Bsn 2/Cb F♭ → E♭). At 81 oboe 2's F♭5 stops on b1, and oboe 1 takes E♭5 on the same staff on b2. ### 5. Bar 64: the enharmonic diminished seventh (no change)

### 6. Mixture plan as a whole (no change)

The movement uses each kind of mixture A&S list on PDF 395: ♭6̂ colouring IV and II (5, 80, 88), ♭6̂ in VII°7 (59, 81), ♭VI as a deceptive goal (78, Ex. 23-6), and ♭VI followed by the plagal iv–I with ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ (78, 87–89, Ex. 23-5 Brahms 3/ii). The statements of theme A in A♭ major (7–14, 23–30) stay diatonic, so the F♭ thread is saved for the transition at 21, the climax 78–81 and the coda, where it prepares the scherzo's E. I found nothing to add. A IV♭ inside theme A's cadences would clash with the tune's F (♮6̂) at 12, 28 and 76, and c283 protects those cadences.
