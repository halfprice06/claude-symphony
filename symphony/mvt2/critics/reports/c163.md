## Findings

### 1. Bar 24 b3: doubled leading tone G3/G4 (Vc/Vla). Keep, and record as a justified exception

**What the scan shows.** At 24 b3 the V7 of A♭ has its leading tone in two octaves: the cellos' G3 (counter-melody F3→G3) and the violas' upper G4 (double stop `<bes g'>`). In the other two statements of the phrase the leading tone is single: Bsn 2 at 8 b3 and Vc at 72 b3. PDF 95 §5 and PDF 104 ("Make sure that neither the leading tone nor the 7th of V7 is doubled") forbid the doubling.

**Why it should stay.** The violas' G4 is the resolution of the seventh of the applied V7 on 24 b2 (B♭1–F3–A♭4–D5). PDF 98–99 §9 says that seventh "must resolve" down by step, and the viola has the only A♭ in the chord. Every way of removing the doubling breaks a rule the reading treats as stricter:
- `<bes es'>` leaves A♭4 unresolved by a leap of a fourth.
- Resolving the G4 up to A♭4 at 25 makes parallel octaves G3/G4→A♭3/A♭4 with the cellos.
- Changing the cello's G3 alters the protected counter-melody.

As written, the two leading tones are approached in contrary motion (F3↑, A♭4↓) and leave in different directions. G3 goes up to A♭3. G4 leaps to 5̂, E♭4, which is the inner-voice leap PDF 100 §12 (Ex. 6-15c) allows. No hidden octave results.

**Action.** No change. Record: "24 b3 doubled leading tone G3/G4: the viola G4 resolves the applied V7's seventh A♭4; keep."

### 2. Bar 47: V7 (C♯7) held as the goal of the group 45–47. Keep (principled exception to PDF 99)

**Problem as the reading puts it.** PDF 99 §10: "If the melodic line comes to rest on 7̂, 2̂, or 5̂, V will generally produce a better effect than V7 … V7 is rarely used in a semicadence." Bar 47 ends a three-bar group (c048) under a poco rit. and holds D♭7 = C♯7 for the whole bar. That is a half cadence on a seventh chord.

**Why it stays.** The seventh (violas' B3 = C♭) is what turns the D♭ triad into the dominant of F♯ minor, so it drives the enharmonic modulation 47→48. Without it the bar is a plain D♭ triad and the quotation has no harmonic ground. The "feeling of activity" A&S warn against is the effect intended here: the bar must lead on into the quotation, not stop. The seventh resolves correctly (B3→A3 on 48 b1½, PDF 98). The other half cadences (10, 22, 26) use triads, as the reading prefers. No change.

### 3. Bars 11→12, 27→28, 75→76: IV repeated from beat 3 across the barline. Keep (already recorded at c157)

**Problem as the reading puts it.** PDF 102 §16 and PDF 115 §13 (Ex. 6-20a, 7-18a): repeating a chord from a weak to a strong beat "contradicts the meter". In theme A's second phrase the D♭ chord arrives on beat 3 (bass A♭–C–D♭) and is struck again on the next downbeat, while the melody's F is tied across the bar. In the first phrase (7→8) the chord changes on the downbeat under the tied E♭.

**Why it stays.** The melody is syncopated (f4~ | f4), and the harmony moves with it. This is the "conflict between rhythm and meter" that PDF 102 calls "a very important compositional resource" once beginner exercises are left behind. The change of chord moves to beat 2 of the next bar (the B♭ appoggiatura at 12, 28, 76 over ii), so the bar still has a harmonic accent. The contrast with phrase 1 gives the second phrase its forward lean, which the A′ climax needs. c157 already decided that the three statements should stay alike. No change.

### 4. Bar 52 b4: doubled leading tone C4/C5 under theme B's pickup. Same case as 36→37 (c143)

The violas hold C4 through 52 (pp < p, c138) and stop at 53. The violins' pickup C5 leaps to F5. At 52 b4 the leading tone therefore sounds in two octaves, and neither voice goes to D♭. This is the pickup pattern of 36→37, which c143 recorded as an exception: theme B's rising fourth starts on 7̂. The only fix that would resolve the violas' C4 adds a viola note at 53. That changes the protected build 53–60 (c049's staged entries) and would add a staff to the 53–56 system, putting the 8-page layout at risk. No change. Record that c143's exception covers 52→53.

### 5. Large-scale cadence and pedal plan: follows the reading; nothing to change

Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95) and PDF 112 ask for these points, all checked against the score:
- The large spans are carried by root-position chords: A♭ 7–30, the A♭ pedal as V of D♭ 31–36, D♭ 37–64, the E♭ pedal 65–70, A♭ 71–92.
- Faster chord changes mark the arrivals: 58–60, with one chord per half bar and then per beat, into 61; the hemiola 80–81 into 82.
- The PACs sit where a closing function is needed (14 and 30 close the A section; 82 is the last).
- The B climax ends open: an IAC at 61, then the slide to A minor at 63–64.
- The cadence of the piece is not contrapuntal. It is a root-position PAC (82), followed by a plagal close over the pedal.

This matches the reading's model and also Dvořák 9/ii, the brief's model. There the return of the Largo theme is shortened, as here (A′ 43 s against A 83 s), and the trombone quotation is set in a foreign key just before the retransition. The large form is complete, and further form changes would only move sections around.
