## Findings

### 1. Bars 79–81: the second ff peak has no bass in the lower octave; the contrabassoon doubles the basses in unison (applied)

**Problem.** At the first peak (61) the bass is laid out in three octaves: contrabassoon D♭1; double basses and tuba D♭2; bass trombone, bass clarinet and bassoon 2 D♭3 (c133). At the A′ peak (79), the movement's registral summit (B♭6 in Fl 1 and Vln I), the contrabassoon plays D♭2–B♭1–E♭2 at sounding pitch. That is the same pitch as the double basses, so the lowest sound is D♭2 and the bass covers only two octaves (D♭2, D♭3). The comment above `aTwoCbsn` (c024) says the aim was to double "the bass in the low octave with C-fag.", but as written the contrabassoon only thickens the basses' own octave. The two peaks share the D♭ root and the ff dynamic, but the second one, which should not sound smaller than the first, has the shallower bass.

**Evidence.** PDF 82: extra parts are upper-octave duplications, "the bass being doubled in the lower octave only". PDF 85 (diagram): chords follow the harmonic series, with the widest intervals at the bottom. That applies most to a chord of the full tutti span (D♭1–A♭6 at 61, D♭2–B♭6 at 79). PDF 52: adding a single wind to strings in unison "only thickens the resonance of the latter", and that is what the contrabassoon does now. Adler part 1, PDF 230: the contrabassoon's "lowest octave is its most effective range … it acts like a 32-foot organ pipe … especially when it doubles cellos, basses, and bassoons at the octave". On balance (PDF 51: in forte one string section equals two winds), the basses can hold D♭2 by themselves against the ff woodwind and the f horns, as they already do in the cellos' octave at 61.

**Fix (applied, sections/aTwo.ily):**
```

```
The pitches were `des,2. | des,2 bes,,4 | es,2.`. Now D♭1–B♭0–E♭1, rising a fourth to A♭1 at 82 as before. B♭0 is the instrument's lowest note (c133: "The contrabassoon reaches B♭0"), reached by step at ff and followed by the existing breath (c176). The basses keep E1 as their lowest note, so nothing else could take this octave. The dynamics, breath and rhythm are unchanged, and only one staff is touched.
