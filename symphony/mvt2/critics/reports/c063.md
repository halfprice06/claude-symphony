## Findings

### 1. Bars 30→31: the A section ends with a hard cut from strings to winds (applied)

**Problem.** At 30 every string part and horns 3·4 hold the final A♭ chord at p and stop at the barline. Bassoon 2 and the contrabassoon start the A♭ pedal of the woodwind choir on 31 b1, and the bass clarinet enters on 31 b2. Nothing crosses the barline. The harmony does not change, since 31–36 stay on A♭, so the listener hears the same chord switch from one colour to another with a gap between them.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 190: when one group gives way to another, the new group "enters unperceived" while the old chord is still sounding, and "it is essential that the two chords overlap". PDF 192 says the same about colour changes: make sure "the seams don't show". Dvořák 9/ii overlaps at each join of this kind (bars 4–5, 45–46, 53–54). The open item "Hn 3·4 into 31–32" (cycles 33–62) is the same problem. It was never applied because 31–36 are protected as woodwinds alone, so the fix must come from the woodwinds.

**Fix (applied).** The contrabassoon enters pp at 30, in unison with the double basses' A♭1, and ties into its existing pedal at 31. The five string parts fade p→pp across 30. Bassoon 2 still enters at 31: an A♭2 at 30 would form a low third with horn 4's C3. The only change inside 23–30 is a 16′ doubling of the bass in the last bar plus the hairpins, so the "plain first statement" stays plain.

### 2. Bar 82 b3: the harp's harmonic pickup into the coda sits on the violas' held E♭4 (applied)

**Problem.** The harp's pickup E♭–F is in harmonics, written E♭3–F3 and sounding E♭4–F4. On beat 3 of 82 the violas hold E♭4, the same pitch, and Vln II, Ob 1, Cl 2 and the violas hold A♭4 just above it. The harp's own beat-2 chord also includes the E♭4 string, which is still ringing. Cycle 28 set everything to pp by beat 3 "so the harp harmonics are heard", but dynamics cannot separate a harmonic from a sustained unison. The coda's head therefore starts without its pickup. The double basses also stop at the end of 82 and re-attack A♭1 at 83 for the coda pedal.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 274: keep the accompanying forces "registrally distant" from the soloist. PDF 267: when the soloist plays in the softest part of its range, the orchestra "is reduced drastically". Harmonics are the harp's softest sound. PDF 192 also applies to the bass re-attack.

**Fix (applied).** Vln II, Ob 1, Cl 2 and the violas end on 82 b2 (`aes'2\p\> r4\!`; violas `<es' aes'>2\p\> r4\!`; Cl 2 `aes'2\p\> r4`, with Cl 1's pp closing the shared hairpin). The harp's beat-2 chord becomes `<aes c'>4` without the E♭4. The double basses tie A♭1 from 82 into 83 (`aes,,2.~\p\>`, which ends at the coda's ppp), so the pedal is already sounding under the pickup. Fl 1, Vln I (A♭5), Cl 1 (C5), Vc and Bsn 1 (C4) and the low A♭s still sustain the tonic, so the cadence chord stays complete. This modifies the protected "release 81–83". The order of the release is unchanged and the inner voices still leave first. The cycle 28 reason, that the harmonics must be heard, is what this change secures.

### 3. Bar 83: the violas re-enter on the harp's first harmonic (applied)

**Problem.** At 83 the violas enter div. on C4–E♭4 ppp on the same beat as the harp's harmonic head (A♭4–C5–E♭5 sounding), an octave below its C and E♭. This is the open item "harp harmonics balance 82–86".

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 269–270: Sibelius and Bartók create "an atmosphere of expectation" with a background that "does not intrude at all". PDF 267: reduce the orchestra drastically for a soft solo.

**Fix (applied).** The violas rest at 83 and enter at 84 under the timpani answer: `R2.\strMark "K" \markup "a tempo" | <c' es'>2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "(div.) change bow at will" | <c' es'>2. | <c' f'>2.\< |`. At 83 the harmonics sound over the cellos' and basses' ppp A♭ alone. The rehearsal mark stays on the viola staff. This continues the protected "thin textures 83–92" and does not undo it.

### 4. Bars 79–81: the movement's main climax has less melodic doubling than the B climax (tested, not applied)

**Problem.** At 61 the augmented head of theme B is in three octaves (Fl 2 A♭5; Vln II and Cl 1 A♭4; Vc A♭3). At 79–81, the higher and later peak, theme A's B♭–A♭–G–E♭ line is in two octaves only (Fl 1 and Vln I B♭6; Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II B♭5). The tutti is larger at 79, but the tune itself is thinner than at 61. Bizet (PDF 279) and Adler (PDF 189) both tie the widest doubling to the main climax.

**Fix (tested in /tmp/critic-063/work2 only).** Give the violas the theme's third octave instead of the tremolo chord. Horns 3·4 still hold F4/A♭3 and the F♭4 at 80 b3, so the harmony is unchanged:
`bes'2.~\ff^\markup \italic "unis." | bes'4( aes'2) | g'4(\> es'8 des'8 c'8 bes8 | aes2)\p\> r4\! |`
It builds with 0 warnings. The only new pairs allpairs_par.py reports are the intended octave doublings of the melody. This would undo cycle 37's held inner parts at 80 and touches the protected A′ doublings 75–82, so I leave the decision to the composer.

### 5. Bar 44 b4: keep the silence (no change; closes "Cl 1 answer at 44")

The whole orchestra rests on 44 b4 before the deceptive move to B♭ minor at 45 (protected, cycle 22). Adler PDF 192 allows a join to be heard when articulation is intended. The beat of silence separates B1 from B2, which have different colours (flute + C.a., then oboe), as the dialogue principle on PDF 257 asks. A filler would weaken the deceptive cadence. I recommend closing the open item.
