## Findings

This reading has been applied twice before (c076, c166), and the score already meets almost all of it. I found one new point, which came in with c268's change at 63 after c166. The other findings record checks and need no change.

### 1. Double basses go from a ff arco F♭ straight to an sf pizzicato on the next downbeat (62-63). Applied.

Bars 62-63. At 61 the basses play D♭2 ff, "molto sostenuto, change bow at will". They then play G♭1 and F♭1 (E1) as tenuto halves at 62, and the next note is A1 sf pizz. on 63 b1, with no rest between. No bow direction is marked at 62. Because the players change bow at will through 61, each player arrives at the F♭ on whatever bow he happens to be on. Many will end the bar at the point of the bow, with no time to move the hand before plucking.

Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 45: "The fingers of the right hand playing pizz. are far less agile than the bow ... on the double basses, for instance, it must always be much slower than on the violins." Adler part 1 PDF 54: "some short time must be allowed for the player to prepare himself to pluck", and "it is much easier to change from arco to pizzicato, if one figures out the bowing so that the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato in order to arrive at the frog". The score already does this for the violas at 40 b3 (c066: up-bow to the frog, then pizz. at 41). c268 counted on "the usual quick change played with the bow in hand" but did not secure the bow position.

Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, bSecCb, bar 62):
```
des,1--\ff^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto, change bow at will" | ges,,2--\downbow fes,,2--\upbow | a,,4\sf^\markup \italic "pizz." r4 r2 |
```
Notes, durations and dynamics are unchanged, so the sound and the MIDI are the same. The players come back together on one bow at 62 b1 after the free bowing of 61, and they end the F♭ at the frog, ready to pluck. If the composer wants more safety, the F♭ could be `fes,,4.--\upbow r8`. The F♭ is also held by the contrabassoon, tuba, bass trombone, bassoon 2 and bass clarinet, so the gap would not be heard. I did not apply that, because c268 wanted the bass to sound right up to the pizzicato.

### 2. Bass clarinet and bassoon 2 at 57-60: 16 beats, p to f, with no printed breath. Recorded, no change.

The line `f2 ges2 | bes,4 es4 aes,4 des4 | ges,2 g,2 | aes,1\< \breathe` lasts about 15.5 s at ♩=60-64, and the first printed breath comes after 60. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 37 asks for "a little rest from time to time". c166 added a breath for oboe 1 over the same span. c176 left these two parts alone because the notes are unslurred, so the players can snatch air between them, and the basses double the line an octave lower. That reason holds. If the composer wants the breath printed, put it at the same place as horn 1, flute 1 and oboe 1: `\once \stemUp des4 \breathe` (B.cl.) and `\tweak Stem.direction #UP des4 \breathe` (Bsn 2) at 58 b4. The basses carry the bass through it.

### 3. Horn 1 goes from ff at 61-62 to its exposed p solo at 67. Recorded, no change.

Horn 1 plays ff at 61-62 (written C5 to E♭5), rests 63-66 (about 16 s at the MIDI tempi), and enters p dolce on the motto tail at 67. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 44 applies the breathing rule to the brass, and PDF 42 notes how slowly the horn speaks. Sixteen seconds is enough for a principal horn to recover. The solo and the 61-62 layout are both protected (c264, horn solo 67-70). Nothing to change.

### 4. Clarinet 2 holds E♭4 for 89-92 under the rit. and the fermata. Recorded, protected.

About 19 s at ppp morendo, plus the fermata. No other instrument plays E♭4 there, so a breath would leave a hole. The clarinet can thin its tone "to a mere breath" (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 36), so the passage is playable. It belongs to the protected plagal close and was recorded by c236 and c291. Nothing to change.

### 5. Viola pizzicato triplets at 41-44. Follows.

Six plucks a second at ♩=60, over four bars at pp-p, crossing C, G and D strings. This is within Rimsky-Korsakov's PDF 45 rule (pizz. slower than arco) for a viola section. The change to pizz. at 41 comes after an up-bow to the frog and a beat's rest at 40 (as in Adler PDF 54), and arco returns at 45 after two beats' rest.

### 6. Harp triplets 53-56 in the middle register. Follows (c166).

Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 46 puts fast figures in the upper register, because the middle strings ring into the next chord. Here each chord lasts two beats (about 2 s), so the harpist has time to damp, and moving the figure up an octave would put it on the violins' theme B. No change.

### 7. Solo violinist's mute at 22-24. Follows.

The leader plays the solo at 17-22 b2 without a mute and rejoins con sord. at 24. That leaves about 4.6 s (22 b3 to 24 b1). Adler part 1 PDF 60 asks for "some time". Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 28 explains why the solo stays unmuted. The part already says "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 24)". No change.
