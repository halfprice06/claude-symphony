## Findings

All findings are records. None recommends a change.

### 1. Theme A's tie over the barline is the tune's memorable cell, and it is clean at all six statements (follows; record)

**Bars.** 7–8, 11–12, 23–24, 27–28, 71–72, 75–76.

**Evidence.** The head `aes4. c8 es4~ | es4 d4 des4` is the device of Ex. 21-1: the third note lands on beat 3 and is carried over the barline, so the downbeat of the next bar is a held tone (PDF 346). `susp.py` finds the E♭ held over the F bass at 8.1, 24.1 and 72.1. It resolves down by step to D on beat 2, the weaker beat (PDF 347–348), and no other voice sounds D before it (PDF 352). In the second phrase the tied F at 12, 28 and 76 is a consonant 3rd over D♭, so it is free to leap to the B♭ appoggiatura (PDF 348). The A♭ that follows is held as the 7th of ii7 and falls to G at 13, 29 and 77 (PDF 353–354).

Compared with the masters: Tchaikovsky's Canzonetta gives its tune the same displacement, but inside the bar. At bars 19–20 the solo plays `c16 bes a8~ a4` and `fis16 es d8~ d4`: the main note arrives half a beat early and is tied into beat 2, the tied anticipation of Ex. 21-35 (PDF 364). Dvořák's Largo tune uses the restruck anticipation (c311 F1). Theme A uses the third Unit 21 device, the suspension over the barline. Each of the three tunes is built on one rhythmic displacement used again and again, and theme A has its own. A listener keeps the rising arpeggio, the held E♭ and the chromatic fall E♭–D–D♭–C. That cell returns at 7, 11, 23, 27, 71 and 75, so it is heard six times. I agree with every earlier melody critic that theme A is the tune of the movement. No change.

### 2. No restruck anticipation in either theme; the scan finds none new (recorded c311 F1; not re-raised)

**Bars.** Whole movement.

**Evidence.** `antic.py` finds four off-beat notes that are restruck on the next beat. They are the trombone quotation at 63 b4½→64 (movement I's first-theme head, protected) and three notes of viola figuration at 42, 59 and 75. Neither theme's line has one. The only melodic anticipation is the tied G♭ at 58 b2½ (c151). This is c311's Finding 1, and its reasons still hold. The cadence notes where §25 places an anticipation (PDF 363) are protected and belong to exported themes. Critic 151 tested 13–14 and kept the stepwise descent. I do not raise it again.

### 3. Bars 55–56: the horn and clarinet counter-line leaves its G♭4 by a tritone leap, and the bassoon carries the G♭ to F (follows; record)

**Bars.** 55 b3 to 56 b3 (Hn 1 and Cl 1 in unison; Bsn 1).

**Problem checked.** `susp.py` flags Hn 1/Cl 1 G♭4 held from 55 b3 through b4 over C2, a tritone above the bass, which then leaps down to C4 at 56. A dissonance that leaves by leap looks like a breach of §3 (PDF 348).

**Evidence.** `slice.py` shows the chords: 55 b3 is C–E♭–G♭–B♭ (Cø7), 56 b1 is A–C–E♭–G♭ (A°7, vii°7 of B♭ minor), and 56 b3 is B♭ minor. G♭ is common to both seventh chords. At 56 b1 bassoon 1 takes it as G♭3 and resolves it to F3 at 56 b3. So the G♭ does resolve down by step, in another voice and register. That is the transferred resolution of §22 and Ex. 21-28 (PDF 360–361). The horn's leap is a skip within the chord, and in this free instrumental texture an indirect suspension like this is normal (§17, PDF 357). The counter-line is protected (Hn/Cl counter-line 53–60; c160, c201). No change.

### 4. The climax at 79–81 is a delayed resolution with the right voice-leading (follows; record)

**Bars.** 79–82.

**Evidence.** B♭6 is held for four beats (79 b1 to 80 b1). A♭ follows at 80 b2–3 as the 7th of ii7 over B♭0, and all its octaves fall together to G at 81 b1. `susp.py` finds no voice sounding G at 80 b3, so the resolution is not anticipated (PDF 352). Holding back the expected note is the delayed resolution of §21 (PDF 359–360, Ex. 21-27). The hemiola at 80–81 shifts the metre, and with it the accent, as in Ex. 21-31 (PDF 362). At 81 b1 the violas and Hn 3·4 hold F♭4 over the E♭1 bass and resolve it to E♭4 on b2 while the bass sounds E♭. That is a ♭9-8 against the bass, the case Ex. 21-12d marks "good" (PDF 352). Clarinet 2's D♭5 is resolved in another voice, by Hn 1·2, Bsn 1 and the cellos, D♭4→C4 at 82 (Ex. 21-28). The peak lands on a strong bar, is approached by scale and released by step, as in the Dvořák and Tchaikovsky models. No change.

### 5. Changes since c311 that bear on the reading are all correct (follows; record)

**Bars.** 56, 73–74, 77.

**Evidence.**
- 73–74 (c469): the cellos' C4 is a consonant 5th over F at 73 b3. It is tied over B♭1 at 74 b1 as a 9th and falls to B♭3 on b2. The resolution's pitch is in the bass (basses and harp B♭1, Bsn B♭2), which Ex. 21-12d marks "good" for the 9-8 (PDF 352). The suspension falls on the stronger beat (PDF 347).

- 77 b1 (c341): Vln I's A♭5, the 7th of ii7 at 76 b3, now resolves to G5 in its own line, as §15 and the review list require (PDF 353–354, 369).
- 56 (c527): the basses' A1–B♭1 doubles the cellos' bass and does not touch any held melodic note.

### 6. Pedal plan follows §28 (follows; record)

**Bars.** 1–6, 30–36, 65–70, 83–92.

**Evidence.** There are tonic pedals at the beginning (1–6), at the end of the A section (30–36) and at the end of the movement (83–86, 89–92). The dominant pedal 65–70 comes before the tonic of the reprise at 71 (PDF 367). Vln I's C5 at 1–3 and 89–92 is a top-voice pedal, which the reading allows. Dissonances against the bass are free (33, 68). Dissonances between upper voices are prepared and resolved (86 Cl D5 against Vla C4, by step into IV6 at 87; PDF 368). The pedal plan and the plagal close are protected. No change.

### 7. Memorability verdict

Theme A meets the brief's standard. It has one rhythmic idea (the suspension over the barline), one melodic idea (the chromatic fall of the motto's tail), and a peak saved for the reprise (B♭6 at 79), and it is stated six times in three colours. Like the Largo and the Canzonetta, it is a tune a listener could hum afterwards. Theme B is the plainer middle tune, with its 4-3s at 42 and 58 and its narrow first phrase. As c131, c141 and c291 ruled, that suits its function and register, and it is not raised here.
