## Findings

### 1. Low D♭1 at bar 10 needs a tuning instruction (applied)
- Bars: 10 (harp LH `des,,4`, sounding D♭1).
- Problem: D♭1 is played on the second-lowest string. Adler (PDF 111, footnote) states that the two lowest strings are not affected by the pedals and are tuned by hand. Harpists tune these strings to suit the piece; the part does not say which pitch is needed, so the harpist learns it at the first rehearsal or plays D♮.
- Fix (applied in aOne.ily, bar 7):
  `<aes,, es,>2.\pp\arpeggio^\markup \small \column { \harp-pedal #"^-^|^--^" \italic "(lowest D string tuned to D♭)" }`
  Alternative: `des,4` (D♭2), but that doubles the bass clarinet in unison and loses the low octave.

### 2. Simultaneous rolls in both hands: connect the wavy line (applied)
- Bars: 12, 76, 78 b3, 80, 91.
- Problem: in these bars both hands attack together and each staff has its own `\arpeggio`. Adler (PDF 114–115) says a rolled chord is played bottom up and a direction must be shown when another is meant. Two separate wavy lines tell the harpist to roll both hands at the same time, so the bass and the middle of the chord start together. In an Adagio, and on the ppp final chord at 91, one continuous roll from the bass up is the sound intended.
- Fix (applied in score.ly): `\new PianoStaff \with { connectArpeggios = ##t instrumentName = "Harp" ... }`. LilyPond connects only arpeggios that fall on the same moment, so the single-hand rolls elsewhere are not changed.

### 3. The harp's melodic head at 82–83 in harmonics (applied)
- Bars: 82 b3–83 (E♭4 F4 | A♭4 C5 E♭5).
- Problem: this is the harp's one melodic moment (cyclic rule: melodic moments for harp), and it is played in the same plucked tone as the chords in 71–82. Adler (PDF 116) describes harmonics as "very typical, practical and beautifully bell-like", and says they must be accompanied very lightly. Here the only accompaniment is ppp held strings (violas, cellos, basses), and the written pitches E♭3–E♭4 lie inside the practical range he gives (C3 to the A above the staff). One harmonic per eighth note at ♩=44–52 is easy. The left-hand method (thumb with the palm on the node) leaves the right hand free after its chord on 82 b2.
- Fix (applied in aTwo.ily bar 82 and coda.ily bar 83): written an octave below, with a circle, as Adler prescribes. A hidden voice carries the sounding pitch to the MIDI so the mp3 and analysis stay correct:
  ```
  % 82
  r4 <aes c' es'>4\arpeggio << \new Voice \with { \remove "Note_performer" } { \voiceOne es8-\flageolet^\markup \small \italic "harm. (sound 8va)" f8-\flageolet } \new Voice { \voiceTwo \hideNotes es'8 f'8 \unHideNotes } >> |
  % 83
  << \new Voice \with { \remove "Note_performer" } { \voiceOne aes4.(-\flageolet c'8-\flageolet es'4-\flageolet) } \new Voice { \voiceTwo \hideNotes aes'4. c''8 es''4 \unHideNotes } >> | R2.*3 |
  ```
  The slur that ran from 82 to 83 now covers 83 only. Harp slurs show phrasing only.
- Consequence: the head is softer. If the composer wants it louder, raise the LH dynamic at 82 to mp. Do not add instruments under it.

### 4. The harp never doubles and is silent for 56 bars: double the pizzicato bass in B1 (applied, optional)
- Bars: 37–44.
- Problem: Adler (PDF 117) names three roles for the harp: melodic, accompanying and doubling. This harp only accompanies (7–14, 71–82) and has five melodic notes (82–83). It never doubles, and it rests from 15 to 70, which covers the whole D♭ section. B1 (37–44) has a pizzicato bass under the viola triplets. A harp doubling of that pizzicato, in the low octaves Adler calls "dark and sonorous" (PDF 113–114), gives the plucked bass a longer ring without making it louder. The key is a flat key, which Adler (PDF 114) says resonates best. The harp leaves before the B♭ minor / F♯ minor modulation at 45, so it needs no awkward pedalling.
- Pedalling, checked against PDF 112–113: G→G♭ during the 22 bars of rest 15–36 (diagram at 37). Bar 40's A♮ (`a,,2`): right foot A♭→A♮ during the rest on 39 b4, back to A♭ during 40 b3–42 b2. G♭ is already set at that point, so only one pedal moves at a time. G♭→G♮ during 45–70, and a diagram at 71 confirms it.
- Fix (applied: bSec.ily new variable, aTwo.ily diagram at 71):
  ```
  bSecHarpL = {
    <des, des>4\p^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"^-^|^-^^" r4 <des, des>4 r4 | <bes,, bes,>4 r4 <aes,, aes,>4 r4 | <es, es>4 r4 <des, des>4 r4_\markup \small "A♮" | <a,, a,>2 <bes,, bes,>2_\markup \small "A♭" |
    <f, f>4 r4 <ges, ges>4 r4 | <bes,, bes,>4 <es, es>4 <aes,, aes,>4 <des, des>4 | <ges, ges>4 r4 <aes, aes>4 r4 | <aes,, aes,>4 r4 r2 |
    R1*20 |
  }
  ```
  aTwo LH bar 71: `<aes,, es,>2.\pp\arpeggio^\markup \small \harp-pedal #"^-^|^--^"`.
  The doubling copies the cello and bass pitches exactly, so it adds no new voice-leading. allpairs_par.py reports only the octave doublings already present between cellos, basses and violas. This is a colour choice. If the composer keeps the harp out of the middle section, as Dvořák does in 9/ii, drop the new variable and the diagram at 71.

### 6. Bass clarinet low B♭1 at bar 8 (not applied)
- Bar: 8 (`bes,,4`, written low C).
- Adler (PDF 216): "writing all the way to C is still risky, for not all symphony orchestras have instruments with such extensions." The staff is already labelled "(low C)", so the requirement is declared. If the composer wants the part playable on a low-E♭ instrument, use `f,4 bes,4 es,4`. The harp LH B♭1 (`<bes,, f,>`) keeps the low root sounding.

### Checked and left as they are
- RH close-position triads (7, 11, 13, 71, 73, 75) go against Adler's "three-note chords spread wide are most effective" (PDF 114). A wide spread would put harp notes above the horn at its attack, which c019 removed on purpose. A low wide spread (for example `<aes, es aes>`) loses the third. I recommend no change.
- Bar 78: the harp rests through the F♭ chord and enters on the F minor of beat 3. That avoids the E/B pedal changes the tempo would not allow (PDF 113), and is correct.
- Timpani A♭ C D♭ E♭ with no retuning fits the standard four drums.
