## Findings

### 1. Fl 1·2, bar 81 b2-3: upper part down-stemmed over the lower part's printed rest (applied)
**Problem.** On the shared flute staff, flute 1's quavers G6-E♭6-D♭6-C6-B♭5 have down-stems, with the beam inside the stave. Flute 2's half rest is printed under that beam. Because both parts appear on the staff, it reads as two-part writing, and in two-part writing the upper part has its stems the wrong way. One stave above, on the oboe staff in the same bar, oboe 1 has up-stems over oboe 2's rest, so the two staves are notated differently.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 65: "The upper part always takes up-stems, the lower part down-stems, even when pitches overlap." Flute 1's down-stems are deliberate: c375 and c394 chose them under Gould PDF 27-28 (in a single part, ledger-line notes take down-stems so the beam sits inside the stave). That rule is for a single part. The printed flute 2 rest is what makes the staff two-part. At bar 82, c395 solved the same problem for flute 2 by putting a spacer in the score instead of its rest.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-535/work/sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoFluteTwo).** The score gets a spacer and the part keeps its rest and hairpin end:
```
fes'''4-\tag #'part \> \tag #'score s2 \tag #'part { r2\! } | \tag #'score s2. \tag #'part R2. |
```
After this change the flute staff at 81 b2-3 shows only flute 1, still down-stemmed, so PDF 27-28 (one part) and PDF 65 both hold. Flute 2's stop is clear from its single F♭6 crotchet tied to nothing, and the same thing is done at 82. The oboe staff is unchanged. This keeps c394's down-stems and c395's method.
