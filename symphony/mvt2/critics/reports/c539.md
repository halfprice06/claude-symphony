## Findings

No finding needs a change. The ranked points below are what I checked and why each needs no change.

1. **Rachmaninoff 3/ii comparison, 7-30.** The opening follows the same order of voices. Rachmaninoff goes from solo horn over harp, to solo violin, to full violins. This score goes from Hn 1 over harp and bassoons (7-14), to Ob 1 answered by a solo violin (15-21), to Vln I+II in unison with the cello counter-melody (23-30). That is also Rimsky's growing-doubling scheme for a returning tune (Ex. 9, 15, 17, PDF 57-58). Theme A then grows further: octaves at 71 and three octaves with winds at 79 (Ex. 32, PDF 62-63). A conductor would recognise the method. Fix: none.

2. **Where this movement is weaker than the model.** Rachmaninoff's middle section breaks the slow tempo completely. Here the B section moves only from ♩=52 to ♩=60-64 and comes back through Largamente. The c533 form review and the protected section proportions already settled this. It suits a Dvořák-9/ii-type Largo, which is the brief's main model, and it suits Rachmaninoff less. I record it and do not reopen it.

3. **18, divided cellos.** Rimsky PDF 62, Note II, warns against divided cellos moving in octaves. At 18 the upper cellos touch C4 twice over the lower cellos' held C3. That is two octave coincidences, not an octave progression, so it does not break the rule. Fix: none.

4. **23-30, basses alone on the bass line.** Rimsky's rule (PDF 56) that basses carry nothing important without cellos or bassoons applies to melodic phrases. His own soft examples leave the basses alone on a bass line or pedal (Ex. 5 "C-b. 4 soli pizz.", Ex. 17, Ex. 23). The 23-30 accompaniment is also protected as the plain first statement. Fix: none.

5. **45-46, Ob 1 over Bsn 1 and the cellos.** Oboe over bassoon is a natural octave pair (PDF 67). The melody is on top, so it stands out by position (PDF 54). c537 recorded the weight of the lower octave and named an Ob 1 p test if this is reopened. I add nothing new.

6. **Same-instrument octaves (Bsn 1·2 at 46, 59, 61, 79-80; Cl 1·2 at 77; Fl 1·2 at 61).** Strings double every one of them, or it is part of a tutti chord. That is the exception Rimsky allows (PDF 67-68). Fix: none.

7. **75-76, melody in the middle register.** This is the one place where Rimsky's PDF 54 test is close. The theme falls to A♭4-B♭5 in unison violins over Cl 2 (E♭4), Bsn 1, Hn 2, the viola figure and the cello counter-melody. Unison violins at mp< over p accompaniment are enough relief, and c299, c309 and c341 protect the register plan. I do not reopen it.

8. **Compared with v529.** Nothing got worse. The two edits since v529 (c535, c536) are notational and practical, and both are correct.

What must not be touched: the standing do-not-touch list, especially the horn-over-harp opening 7-14, the oboe and solo-violin dialogue 15-21, the theme A doubling ladder 23 → 71 → 79-81, the 74-78 register plan, and the 8-page layout.
