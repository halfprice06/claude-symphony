## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The long-range mixture plan still follows Unit 23 in v310 (no change)

I traced every ♭6̂ (F♭, written E in the sharp keys) in A♭ contexts in score.midi. Each one reaches E♭ before any F♮ returns, as PDF 388 and point 3 on PDF 395 require:
- 5→6: Cl 2 F♭3→E♭3 under Hn 3's C4–D♭4–C4.
- 21→22: bass E2/E3 → E♭.
- 59 b4→60: Bsn, Vc E3 and Hn, Vla E4 → E♭.
- 78: bass F♭2→E♭2 on beat 3.
- 80 b3→81: Hn 3 E4→E♭4 and Vla E4→E♭4 on beat 2.
- 88→89: C.a. F♭4, lower violas and Hn 4 F♭3 → E♭.

In the climax, F♭ major at 62 b3 is ♭VI of the home key (PDF 390). It turns into A minor, and its bass E slips to E♭ at 65. Inside the quotation the trombone's F3–E3 at 64 is ♭6̂–5̂ of A minor, the local key, so it is not the "poor" ♭6̂–♮6̂–5̂ order in A♭. Changes since c292 (v293–v310) affect none of these notes. The c299/c309 octave changes at 74–77 contain no altered degree. Keep everything.

### 2. The S quotation at 47–50 is a mode-recast repetition reached and left by mixture (new reading note, no change)

In movement I the S head is an A♭-major theme. Here it comes back in F♯ minor, which is G♭ minor, the minor iv of D♭. Its A♮ is ♭6̂ of D♭ (B♭♭). This is section 5's "phrase cast in the parallel minor" used to vary a repetition (PDF 392, Ex. 23-8), and it uses ♭6̂ "to color IV" (point 2a, PDF 395). The way out follows the voice-leading rules:
- the cor anglais's A4 (50 b4) falls to A♭4 at 51, so ♭6̂ goes to 5̂ in the voice that carries it (PDF 388);
- the B-minor chord at 50 b3–4 moves to A♭7 at 51 with every chromatic step inside one voice: cellos B2→C3, Vln II D5→E♭5, C.a. A4→A♭4 (PDF 394, Ex. 23-12b). c132's viola fix at 50–51 is still in place.

The cyclic meaning: movement I's lyrical theme comes back darkened by the parallel mode. A&S say this gives "the potential for dramatic juxtaposition" (PDF 387). Protected; keep.

### 3. At symphony level, the key plan is a mixture plan, and II plays its part in it (no change)

The four keys C – A♭ – E – C are I – ♭VI – III♯ – I. ♭VI is "one of the most important and frequently used chords created by mixture" (PDF 390). III♯ is A&S's standard example of secondary mixture, "an E major triad as III in C major" (PDF 393; point 5, PDF 395). The plan also follows the Mendelssohn principle on PDF 386–387: the major is kept back and "only much later … does the major reassert itself". In this symphony C major comes back for good only at the finale's 223. That is why II's rule "no C-major material" matters, and II keeps it.

II does three things for this plan:
- It opens by undoing movement I's Picardy third (PDF 391). The violas' E4 falls to E♭4 at bar 2 in the same voice.
- It ends on a plain A♭ triad. The finale makes that ♭VI into a German sixth by adding F♯, which is the move of Ex. 23-8 (PDF 392).
- Its last A♭4 (Vln II) is taken over as G♯4 by the scherzo's Horn 1. That turns ♭6̂ of C into the third of III♯ through an enharmonic common tone, not through a chromatic step split between voices (PDF 394).

Nothing to change.

### 4. The opening frame completes a ♭6̂ that movement I leaves open (no change)

mvt1.md records that I 73–76 and II 3–6 are the same chord over an A♭ pedal (Hn 4 A♭3). In movement I v321, clos.ily ends the flute motto on F♭5 and holds Vln II's F♭4 through 76 into the development. II restates the chord and resolves the F♭ to E♭ at 5→6 (Cl 2). So the ♭6̂ that movement I leaves open moves to 5̂ in the next movement (PDF 388). The coda's F–F♭–E♭ over IV6–iv–I (87–89) then answers bar 5 with the Chopin inflection ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ (Ex. 23-2f, PDF 387–388). The frame is like Mendelssohn's (Ex. 23-1): soft wind chords with the borrowed iv at the start, and the same plagal neighbour at the end. Protected (link 1–6, plagal close 87–92); keep.

### 7. ♭6̂ in theme A's melody, or ♭VI/♭3̂ in the coda: not recommended

Schumann (Ex. 23-2g, PDF 388) puts ♭6̂ into the voice by a leap to stress the contrast. Brahms 3/ii (Ex. 23-5, PDF 390) colours a plagal close with ♭VI. Each would add colour to II. Neither suits this score:
- An F♭ in theme A would change an exported, protected melody that the scherzo and finale track.
- ♭VI before 87 would put F♭ before the F of the IV6 (♭6̂–♮6̂, PDF 395 point 3).
- C♭ (♭3̂) would cloud the C5 that joins I, II and the scherzo.

The score already does what Exercise 3 asks (PDF 396): it keeps ♭6̂ in the inner voices. Its most audible statements are in the open Hn 3 at the ff climax (80–81) and in the C.a. at 87–89. This agrees with c070 and c170 ("no ♭VI in the coda"). Recorded only.
