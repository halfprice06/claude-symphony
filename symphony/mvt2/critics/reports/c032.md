## What else I studied

I made a beat-by-beat chord reduction of v32/score.midi for all 92 bars. I listed every part per beat in the passages that carry the reading's devices (8, 15–22, 37–44, 45–53, 57–64, 69–82). I also ran a pairwise parallel-5th/8ve scan that takes the top and bottom note of each staff, so it covers double stops and part-combined pairs that allpairs_par.py misses, and then removed doubling runs by hand. The scan found four real cases (Findings 1–3). allpairs_par.py and analysis.txt report none of them, because none involves the outer voices. I checked each fix against HISTORY.md's do-not-touch lists. I read sections/*.ily in full.

Overall verdict: the harmony applies Unit 17 well where it counts. The theme's chromatic tail rides a descending-5ths chain with every seventh resolving. The B theme's peak (42/58) is a descending-5ths sequence whose violas are exactly sequential at 58. The b-phrase has a real lower-third sequence (17 → 21). The B-section key scheme is the falling-thirds progression of Ex. 17-2d enlarged. The problems are local: unbroken fifths and octaves at four joins, where A&S's rule on voice-leading tones (PDF 267, 271) applies.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 26→27 and 74→75: parallel fifths between the cello counter-melody and the bass. Applied.
- **Problem.** At the end of each theme-A antecedent the cellos hold B♭3 over the basses' E♭2. On the downbeat of 27 (and of 75) the cellos go to E♭3 while the bass goes to A♭1, which makes compound parallel fifths in the two lowest, most exposed lines. At 74→75 horn 2 doubles the cellos and bassoon 2 doubles the bass (E♭3→A♭2 against B♭3→E♭3), so the fifths appear twice. The horn statement at 10→11 does not have them, because there bassoon 2 goes B♭2→C3.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267 (§5): with repeated patterns "5ths and octaves can become a problem. Therefore, voice-leading chords play an essential role", as in Ex. 17-1, where "the chord on the fourth eighth note prevents 5ths between successive strong beats". PDF 271 (§8, Ex. 17-6): the intermediate V and VI "prevent parallel 5ths and octaves". This V–I join comes back three times (10/26/74), so it is exactly the repeated pattern the reading warns about.
- **Fix (applied).** Add a voice-leading tone on beat 3. G3 under the violins' E♭–F pickup gives 10th → 5th into the downbeat, and the cello line becomes a falling B♭–G–E♭ arpeggio.
  - aOne.ily, `aOneVc`, bar 26: `bes2.` → `bes2( g4)`
  - aTwo.ily, `aTwoVc`, bar 74: `bes2.` → `bes2( g4)`
  - aTwo.ily, `aTwoHnTwo`, bar 74: `bes2.)` → `bes2 g4)`
- **Protected passages.** The 23–30 "plain first statement / violin unison with the cello counter-melody" and the "A′ texture 71–82" rules are about scoring. This is one note in the existing cello and horn line. Instrumentation, doublings and bowing are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 62→63: viola double stop in parallel 5ths and 8ves with the bass into A minor. Applied.
- **Problem.** At 62 b3 (F♭ = E major) the violas play C♭4–F♭4 (B3–E4). At 63 they move to E4–A4 while the bass goes E→A. The lower note makes parallel fifths with the bass (and with the bass trombone, E2→A2). The upper note makes parallel octaves with the bass. This is the V→i arrival of the A-minor quotation, the one place in the movement where a fifth-progression should sound clean.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267 §5 (5ths and 8ves in repeated patterns; voice-leading tones break them) and PDF 269 (in a fifth-progression the root motion carries the progression and the upper voices move by step). The normal step from the fifth of V is B→C.
- **Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bars 63–64: `<e' a'>1:32\sf\> | <e' a'>2:32\p r2` → `<c' e'>1:32\sf\> | <c' e'>2:32\p r2`. B3 rises to C4 and E4 is held. The chord stays complete (A in cellos, basses and bass trombone). The brass layout of 61–62 and the trombone quotation are untouched.

### 3. Bars 50→51: Vln II parallel fifths with the bass, approached by an augmented 2nd. Applied.
- **Problem.** At 50 b3 Vln II holds F♯4 over the bass B1 (iv). At 51 it moves to E♭4 over A♭1 (A♭7 = G♯7), which makes parallel fifths with the bass. The written interval F♯→E♭ is a melodic augmented 2nd. Everything else at this pp join is sustained, so the two moving lines are clearly heard.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 268 (§5): the augmented 4th may be used in descending-5ths patterns, "however, the melodic augmented 2nd should be avoided". PDF 267: unbroken 5ths in repeated or sequential motion are to be broken by a voice-leading tone. PDF 283 (§19) also notes that minor-mode passages are where leading-tone and augmented-interval problems arise.
- **Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecVlnTwo`, bar 50: `fis'2 fis'2` → `fis'2 d'2`. D4 joins the violas' D4 on the B-minor chord and rises a half step to E♭4 (= D♯) at 51. The intervals with the bass become 10th → 5th, and the violas go D4→C4 in contrary motion. This does not touch the quotation (oboe 47–49) or the 49 hold (c027).

### 4. Bar 60: leading tone doubled in a non-melodic part before the climax tonic. Applied.
- **Problem.** On the V7 bar before the D♭ arrival at 61 (the main goal tonic of the movement), C is in Fl, Ob 1, Vln I, Vln II, Cl 1 (all melody octaves), the violas' figure, and also in clarinet 2 (C4), which is not a melody part. Cl 2 then leaps C4→F4 and leaves the leading tone unresolved. With the melody already in three octaves, the extra C makes the V7 bar mostly leading tone.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267 and the summary on PDF 286: a doubled leading tone is acceptable "if not just before a goal tonic".
- **Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecClTwo`, bar 60: `c'1\<` → `aes'1\<`. A♭4 is the root, it falls a third to the F4 that Cl 2 already plays at 61, and it exchanges A♭/F with horn 1 (F4→A♭4). I first tried E♭4. It made E♭→F octaves with bassoon 1 (E♭3→F3), so I rejected it.
