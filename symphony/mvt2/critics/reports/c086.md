## Findings

### 1. Flute 1 has a 15½-second slur rising into its third octave with no breath (bars 57–60). Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 1 plays theme B's upper octave from 57 to 60 under one slur, from A♭5 up to B♭6, with p< at 57, f at 59 and a further crescendo at 60. Its first breath is the comma at the end of 60. At the printed MIDI tempi (57–58 ♩=62, 59 ♩=64, 60 ♩=62→58 poco allargando) that is about 15.5 s. Loud playing in the flute's high register uses the most air of any woodwind, and right after this phrase the player has to hold A♭6 ff for two Largamente bars (61–62). Horn 1 already breathes after 58 (c046). The flute has no breath here, although its 37–40 statement is split into two slurs at 38.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 24 item 11: a slur is played "usually in one breath". PDF 70: a large air demand means "allow as many breathing spaces as possible". PDF 65 (Ex. 74): B♭6 is at the top of the "brilliant" range, just below the "very difficult" notes. Oboe 1 keeps its slur. A double reed uses little air, so it can carry the lower octave through the gap together with both violin sections (staggered breathing, PDF 44).
**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecFluteOne):** `... ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8) \breathe | es'''4.(\f f'''8 des'''4 bes''4 | c'''1)\< \breathe |`

### 2. The low brass and horn 4 have no breath between 60 and 61, but every woodwind does (bars 60–62). Applied.
**Problem.** Tuba (A♭1 → D♭2 → G♭1–F♭1), bass trombone, trombone 2 and horn 4 enter pp< at 60, tie straight into the tenuto mf/f bars 61–62, and are cut off at 63. That is 12.9 s in one breath, crescendo, in the tuba's and bass trombone's lowest octave. Flutes, oboes, clarinets, bass clarinet and bassoons all have a comma at the end of 60. The brass have none, so the brass arrive at the Largamente downbeat with the least air.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 39 and 40: the tuba's "large bore" and "spreading" sound. PDF 70: a large bore needs breathing spaces. PDF 30, Ex. 33: in Sebesky's own trombone-section example a comma before the last bar "indicates a breath is to be taken". PDF 24 item 11 again. The breath falls on the barline the whole woodwind section already uses, so it adds no new gap.
**Fix (bSec.ily):** `bSecTbnTwo = { R1*23 | es1\pp\< \breathe | ...`; `bSecBassTbn = { R1*23 | aes,1\pp\< \breathe | ...`; `bSecTuba = { R1*23 | aes,,1\pp\< \breathe | ...`; `bSecHnFour = { R1*23 | aes1\p\< \breathe | ...`

### 3. Horns 3 and 4 are an octave apart at the climax, so the top brass line has one horn (bars 61–62). Applied. This answers c069.
**Problem.** With trumpets tacet, the brass top line A♭4–B♭4–C♭5 (written E♭5–F5–G♭5) at 61–62 is horn 3 alone at f. Horn 4 plays the same line an octave lower, A♭3–B♭3–C♭4. At 61–62 the violas (B♭3, C♭4 double stops) and the cellos and bassoon 1 (A♭3) already sound those lower notes.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 32 fn. 1 and PDF 46: trombones at mf or louder have twice a horn's power, so at the climax he puts "two horns on each line". PDF 33: horns in unison "f or ff in the high register" are "very effective and dramatic". In Ex. 51 (PDF 36–37) the top pair is a2 on concert D5, written A5. PDF 103: two horns should be no more than a fifth or minor sixth apart, because a wider interval "tends to diffuse the horn sound and possibly create intonation conflicts".
**Answer to c069.** c069 split the horns citing Schuller (Adler part 2 PDF 26): two horns in unison, high and loud, crack. This line peaks at written G♭5, a third below Sebesky's a2 D5/A5, and it is marked f, not ff. A doubled player also covers a missed attack on an exposed cold entry: horn 3 has rested since bar 30. Under these conditions the reading supports the unison. No pitch class leaves the chord, and allpairs shows no new parallels (the Hn 3/Hn 4 octave runs disappear).
**Fix (bSec.ily):** in bSecHnThree, `\partCombineApart` → `\partCombineUnisono` before 61, so the score prints "a2". In bSecHnFour: `aes'1--\f | bes'2-- ces''2-- | R1*2 |`.

### 4. Both oboes play F♭5 in unison at 62 beat 3. Applied.
**Problem.** Ob 1 goes F5 → F♭5 and Ob 2 goes D♭5 → F♭5, so the pair meets on a unison at ff for two beats. F♭5 is also in Ob 1, Fl 2 (an octave up) and the violins.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 71: "I don't recommend voicing oboes in unison unless, for some reason, an especially harsh effect is required". PDF 80: octaves carry "less chance of an intonation conflict than in unison doublings".
**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecOboeTwo):** `des''1--\ff | des''2-- aes'2-- | R1*2 |`. This gives the F♭ chord's A♭ in the oboe's good lower-middle register. C♭5 is not an option: it makes fifths D♭/G♭ → C♭/F♭ with the bass. A♭5 is not either: it crosses above Ob 1. I checked all voices by hand. The only new pair allpairs reports is an octave D♭–A♭ with the timpani roll (D♭3–A♭2), which is a drum doubling the harmony's roots, not a voice-leading fault.

### 5. Two open playability items can be closed with no change: contrabassoon 30–32 and clarinet 2 89–92.
- **Cbsn 30–32** (open since c066). The A♭1 is held 8 beats at ♩=52 and pp, about 9.2 s, and is released at 32 beat 3. That is well within one breath. PDF 74 (sparing use) is respected.
- **Cl 2 89–92** (open since c076). The E♭4 (written F4, top of the chalumeau, not a throat tone, Ex. 66) lasts about 18.7 s plus the fermata, ppp morendo, after the comma at the end of 88. At ppp the clarinet uses the least air of any wind, and this is a single held note with no slur pressure. It is long but within professional endurance. Relaying it to Cl 1 would break the clarinet morendo that HISTORY protects.

### 6. Note only: clarinet 2's held A♭4 at 71–78 is the throat B♭4.
Sebesky PDF 60 calls written G4–B♭4 "somewhat pale, cold". At 71–78 this is a pp inner voice blending with the violas' figure, which is where a pale tone helps. No change.
