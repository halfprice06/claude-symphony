## Findings

### 1. Bars 61–62: trumpets tacet in movement II. Applied.

**Problem.** The trumpets play only at 61–62, where they hold a sustained D♭-major chord (Tpt 1·2 ff, Tpt 3 f) with horns, trombones and tuba. That is a sustained forte major chord in trumpets and full brass. cyclic-notes ("Movement I cycle 160") reserves that sound for the arrival of movement III's chorale and keeps the forte D♭ triad for movement III's episode. Inside movement II the trumpets add no pitch:

- 61: Tpt 1 A♭4–D♭5 = Cl 1 and Vln II; Tpt 2 F4 = Cl 2 and Hn 2; Tpt 3 D♭4 = violas.
- 62: Tpt 1 F5–F♭5 = Ob 1 and Vln II; Tpt 2 B♭4–C♭5 = Hn 3 and Cl 1; Tpt 3 D♭4–F♭4 = violas.

allpairs_par.py lists 20 octave-doubling pairs at 61–62 that involve a trumpet.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 11: hold a colour back so its entrance has an effect, and "distribute them judiciously throughout his score". The score here is the whole symphony. PDF 12: a note that makes no "legitimate difference" should be erased. Brahms 1/ii also leaves the trumpets out of the slow movement.

**Fix (applied).**
- In bSec.ily: `bSecTptOne = { R1*28 | }`, and the same for `bSecTptTwo` and `bSecTptThree`. The v60 notes are kept in a comment.
- In score.ly: both trumpet staves removed, and "Trumpets tacet" added to the header.

The climax keeps ff in woodwinds and strings, horns 2–4 f, trombones and tuba f, and the timpani roll. This undoes the trumpet parts of the protected 61–62 brass layout (c014, c024, c054: "Tpt 1·2 ff"). Those protections were about voicing, and every pitch they placed is still sounding. The reason they did not cover is the cyclic reservation from movement I's cycle 160, which came later. If the composer wants trumpet brilliance at 61, the alternative is to change the rule in cyclic-notes, not to keep both.

### 2. Brass key signatures (point a): one convention for both scores. Applied for the horns.

**Problem.** Movement I's concert score gives horns and trumpets key signatures. Movement II removed them at c046 (`\remove "Key_engraver"`). In the score, the horns read A♭ major, D♭ major, F♯ minor and A minor with every flat written out, while every staff around them has a signature.

**Evidence.** The reason given at c046 was Adler part 1 PDF 297–298, "most performers prefer this". That sentence is about what the players read, which is their parts. For the score, Adler part 2 PDF 294 asks for key signatures right after the clef on each staff and calls leaving them out a short cut that "should be strongly discouraged". MOLA PDF 5 puts the horn rule in its section on parts. A concert-pitch score is read by the conductor, who reads harmony vertically.

**Recommendation.**
- Scores of I and II: key signatures on all pitched staves except timpani, which is movement I's practice.
- Parts: horns and trumpets without signatures (Adler 297–298, MOLA PDF 5).

**Fix (applied).** In score.ly, `\remove "Key_engraver"` is deleted from the Hn 1·2 and Hn 3·4 staves. The trumpet staves are gone after finding 1; if they come back, give them signatures too. Pages 5–6 of the test build show the signatures and the A minor cancellation at 63 printing correctly. This supersedes c046 F4 in the score only.

### 3. Bars 3–6: clarinet 1 doubles horn 4 in unison for four bars. Applied.

**Problem.** Clarinet 1 holds A♭3 at 3–6, the same pitch as horn 4. Horn 4 took that note when c030 added it to relieve horn 3. This is the open c054 item.

The primary element here is horn 3's C4–D♭4–C4 over the minor iv at bar 5. It previews the plagal close (D♭–C on top at 87–89) and carries the common tone from movement I. The unison doubling thickens the A♭3 just below it. Cl 2 E♭3 (F♭3 at 5), B.cl. A♭2, Hn 4 A♭3 and Hn 3 C4 already make a complete chord.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12–13, Ex. 1 (seen on the PDF 12 image): taking out one doubling line "lightened" the lower-middle register so the important element could "dominate a little more noticeably". PDF 13–14, focus.

**Fix (applied).** In intro.ily: `introClOne = { R2.*6 | }`. The part combiner prints "2." on Cl 2. The link (1–2), the common tone, horn 3 on top (c010) and every sounding pitch are unchanged, so the reasons for the link's protection still hold.

### 4. Bars 73–74: the reprise is harmonised exactly as at 25–26. Applied.

**Problem.** Bars 71–74 repeat 23–26 chord for chord: I | vi7–V7/V–V7 | I (A♭ held) | V. The reprise starts to differ only at 75. That is the third time this harmony is heard, and bar 9 already varied it once with IV on beat 3.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19: "I usually try to avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice", with Ex. 3/3A as the model (same melody, new chords, same resting place). PDF 20: substitutions for "a little lift in a musical phrase".

**Fix (applied).** In aTwo.ily, bar 73 goes I–vi7 (bass F on beat 3) and bar 74 becomes V4/3 (bass B♭, with D♭4 in the violas resolving to C4 at 75):
```
aTwoCb:    ... | aes,,2 f,,4 | bes,,2. |
aTwoBsnTwo:... | aes,2 f,4 | bes,2 r4 |
aTwoHarpL: ... | <aes,, es,>2\arpeggio <f,, c,>4\arpeggio | <bes,, es,>2.\arpeggio |
aTwoVla:   ... | bes( des' g') es'( bes des') |
```
Melody, counter-melody, bar 71's root-position bass (c057) and the descending-fifths chain at 72 are unchanged. I checked every pair by hand at 73–74. The only new allpairs entries are the bassoon and harp doubling the bass in octaves. The melody/bass pairs are A♭/F, G/B♭ and A♭/A♭; the last arrives by contrary motion. The softer V4/3 into 75 carries the phrase into the crescendo instead of closing it.
