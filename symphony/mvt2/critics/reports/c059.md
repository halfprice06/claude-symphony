## What else I studied

The master movement is Brahms, Symphony No. 1, ii (Andante sostenuto, E major, 3/4, ternary), not used by earlier lens-9 critics. Adler prints its first six bars (VIII-38, PDF 267): Vln I with bassoon 1 an octave lower over full string harmony and a stopped-horn chord. For the larger form I used the Wikipedia article (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_No._1_(Brahms)): oboe solo in the A section, a C♯-minor oboe solo "over delicate string support" in B, and a "quasi-recapitulation" that "parallels the opening A section without echoing it" — winds bring the theme back, then the principal violin plays the oboe theme softly accompanied by horns. I compared v59 with v49 file by file (`diff` of sections with comments stripped), ran allpairs_par.py, and ran my own MIDI scan of inner voices on shared staves.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** For most pages, yes. The A section's three colours (horn over reeds, muted strings, both together), the D♭ middle section with its staged build, and the coda are idiomatic and balanced. What still reads as a student next to Brahms is (a) the reprise is scored as the 23–30 statement again, an octave wider, and (b) about a third of the bars (1–6, 31–36, 47–52, 65–70, 83–92) are a solo over held chords, where Brahms keeps the inner parts moving. Most of (b) is protected, so I raise only the one unprotected case.

**Compared with v49:** most changes since v49 are improvements: harp takes the triplets at 53–56, violas pizz. at 41–44, bass descent at 53–55, tenuti at 61–62, timpani damping. Two changes made things worse: the Vln I divisi at 61–62 (c051; Finding 2) and the ninth leap in Vln II at 47–48 that came with c054's octave shift (Finding 3).

## Findings

### 1. The reprise (71–78) repeats the 23–30 colour; no wind carries the theme until 75 — applied
- **Problem.** At 23 the theme is in both violin sections with the cello counter-melody. At 71 it is again violins only (now in octaves), with the same counter-melody (cellos + horn 2, same notes) and the same bass (Cb 71–74 = 23–26). Brahms's reprise brings his theme back in new colours; Adler says Schubert "by orchestrating each major thematic element differently … enhances the clarity of the form" (PDF 238) and that masters alter something the third time (PDF 255). Brahms's own opening adds one wind on the string theme in octaves "to bring out the melody even more clearly" (PDF 267). In v59 clarinet 1 holds chord tones (C5, B♭4) inside the theme's octave instead.
- **Fix (applied).** Clarinet 1 plays the theme's lower octave with Vln II from 71. Flute 1 then joins the upper octave at 75 (already there), so the reprise adds one wind per octave before the tutti at 79. Clarinet 1 is already on the theme at 79–81, so the line now runs on unbroken. Breaths at 74 b3 and 78 b3 are kept. The counter-melody, viola figure, bass, harp and Cl 2 (including the protected B♭4 at 76) are unchanged.
```
aTwoClOne (71-78):
  aes'4.(\pp^\markup \italic "dolce" c''8 es''4~ | es''4 d''4 des''4 | c''4. bes'8 aes'4 | g'2) r4 |
  aes'4.(\p\< c''8 f''4~ | f''4 bes''4 aes''4 | g''4 es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 | aes'2)\f r4 |
```
- **Inner-voice fault found on the way (applied).** In v59, Cl 2 G4→A♭4 at 72 b3→73 moves in octaves with the horn 2/cello counter-melody G3→A♭3. allpairs_par.py does not report it because Cl 2 shares a staff. Fix: `aTwoClTwo` bar 72 `aes'4 f'4 bes'4` (B♭4, the fifth of V7, falls to A♭4).
- **History.** This touches c017's "A′ texture 71–82" and the closed point "clarinets inside the theme's octave 71–78" (c014). It answers both: the line, counter-melody and accompaniment figure stay; clarinet 1 leaves the held chord tones inside the octave and joins the theme itself.

### 2. Vln I divisi at the B-section climax (61–62) weakens the line — applied
- **Problem.** Since c051, half the firsts hold A♭6 and only the other half carries C6→D♭6 and F6–F♭6 against ff brass. Adler on the Tchaikovsky Serenade: "usually the second violins, violas, and cellos are divided, while divisi would weaken the first violin line" (PDF 152). v49 had the firsts non div. This is a regression.
- **Fix (applied).** Vln I non div. on the line the lower half had: `des'''1--\ff^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto" | f'''2-- fes'''2--`. Flute 1 holds the A♭6 (`aes'''1--~\ff | aes'''1`), which is strong in the flute's top register and above the trumpets (Adler PDF 255–257: melody and top note in good registers). Flute 2 keeps the augmented head `aes''2--( des'''2-- | f'''2-- fes'''2--)`, still doubled by the full first violins an octave higher, so c021's reason (two instruments on the upper octave) still holds. "div." moves to 63, where the tremolo is accompaniment. The A♭6 peak and the A6 at 63 stay.

### 3. Vln II leading tone leaps a ninth into the quotation (47–48) — applied
- **Problem.** Since c054 moved Vln II up an octave for 48–52, the E♯4 of 47 (the leading tone of C♯7) goes to F♯5 on the quotation's first downbeat. The leading tone does not resolve in its own line, and the leap is exposed at pp. In v49 it resolved E♯4→F♯4.
- **Fix (applied).** `bSecVlnTwo` bar 48: `fis'2^\markup \italic "poco sostenuto" fis''2`. The E♯ resolves in place, and the octave shift happens at 48 b3 while the cor anglais holds A4. The shift still comes before 49, so c054's unison F♯4 with the cor anglais does not return.

### 4. The bass pedal runs under the violins' pickup to the reprise (70 b3) — not applied
- **Problem.** At 70 the horn, bassoons and timpani stop on beat 2, but the basses' E♭ continues under the violins' pickup E♭–F. Adler's Strauss example (PDF 245): suspend the pedal "so that the anacrusis to the second statement in the first violins is clearly heard; thus this repetition is separated from the initial exposition. This device also provides a natural breathing space."
- **Fix.** `retrCb`: last bar `es,2 r4`. I tested it: 0 warnings, but analyze.py then reads the Vln II pickup as the bass and reports "bar 70: octaves E♭5→F5 over E♭3→F4". That is Vln I/II doubling one line, not a voice-leading fault. I left it out of the scratch copy so analysis.txt stays at 0. If the composer applies it, they should note the false positive.

### 5. 47–52: six bars of held whole notes under the quotation — suggestion, not applied
- **Problem.** Under the cor anglais quotation, every string part holds whole notes. Adler's accompaniment patterns for a solo — on–off beat; sustained harmony plus a moving figure (PDF 158–159) — keep the background alive without crossing the solo's rhythm, which is Adler's condition at PDF 144 (countertheme rhythmically different). Brahms's C♯-minor oboe solo has "delicate string support", not bare held chords.
- **Fix.** Violas only, off-beat eighths on their current pitches, pp sotto voce, 48–51, e.g. bar 48 `r8 a8-.( a8-. a8-.) r8 a8-.( a8-. a8-.)`. This answers c027's reason (no movement in the quotation's rhythm): off-beat eighths are not the quotation's rhythm. The tempo plan 47–53 is unchanged.
