## Findings

Unit 1 teaches scale degrees, stable and active tones, passing and neighbour tones, half steps as intensifiers, chromatic spelling, the three forms of minor, mixture and modes. c211 checked v211 against the same pages and found no violation. Since then the melodic lines changed only in details (c299, c309 and c341 on the Vln I register at 74-78; c301 cello passing tone at 26). I checked v351 again bar by bar and still find no violation of the reading. I made no change in /tmp/critic-351/work. The findings below record what the reading shows about memorability, ranked by how much each bears on the lens.

### 1. Theme B leans on the half-step active tones; Dvořák's Largo tune avoids them (37-44; no change)

Strong-beat tones of theme B (beats 1 and 3, D♭ major): 37 F (3̂), E♭ (2̂); 38 D♭ (1̂), E♭ (2̂); 39 G♭ (4̂), E♭ (2̂); 40 C (7̂), B♭ (6̂); 41 A♭ (5̂), F (3̂); 42 A♭ (5̂), G♭ tied (4̂); 43 E♭ (2̂), D♭ (1̂); 44 C (7̂, held). Six of sixteen are stable tones (PDF 20, Ex. 1-8); four are 4̂ or 7̂, the tones that lie a half step from a stable tone and "urgently" demand continuation (PDF 22). Dvořák's cor anglais tune at 7-10 has eight strong-beat tones: F, F, E♭, E♭, F, F, E♭, D♭ (3̂ 3̂ 2̂ 2̂ 3̂ 3̂ 2̂ 1̂). It has no 4̂ or 7̂ at all in its first phrase, so every strong beat is a stable tone or the mild whole-step 2̂. That is a large part of why the Largo tune is easy to sing back.

Theme B is built the other way on purpose: it keeps moving and withholds 1̂ until the climax at 61, which A&S PDF 16 describes as the way to make the final arrival stronger (K. 545 bars 1-12). c211 protected this ("theme B's tonic withheld until 61"), and the HISTORY standing list protects theme B's line at 37-60. I do not propose a change. The consequence for the lens is that theme A, not theme B, is the tune a listener will carry away, and the score is already set up that way: theme A is heard four times (7, 23, 71, and its head in the coda harp at 83), theme B three times plus the climax. That ratio should not be reduced in future revisions.

### 2. Theme A's frame is stable tones on every downbeat but two, and its 7̂ resolves in the same register (7-14, 23-30, 71-82; follows)

Downbeats of theme A: 7 A♭ (1̂), 8 E♭ tied (5̂), 9 C (3̂), 10 G (7̂), 11 A♭ (1̂), 12 F tied (6̂), 13 G (7̂), 14 A♭ (1̂). The first bar arpeggiates the tonic triad (PDF 19, broken chord), so the key is fixed at once. The two active downbeats are the phrase ends, which is where A&S puts suspense (PDF 16). At 10 the G (7̂) is held a half note and the line goes E♭-F to A♭ at 11 in the same octave as the G. PDF 22 asks the reader to lengthen the B in K. 545 bar 2 and hear how strongly the ear wants C: here the lengthened G gets its A♭ one beat later, after an incomplete-neighbour pickup (Ex. 1-11). This is the main reason the theme hangs together across its half-close. It matches the pattern of the Dvořák tune, whose phrase also returns to its opening 3̂-5̂ figure after a held 2̂ (bars 8-9).

### 3. The second phrase's peak is an accented 2̂, then a complete 5̂-1̂ scale (12-14, 28-30, 76-82; follows)

Bar 12 reaches B♭ (2̂) on beat 2 over V7/V or ii, then A♭ and G, and bar 13 descends E♭-D♭-C-B♭ to A♭: 5̂-4̂-3̂-2̂-1̂. This is the second segment of Ex. 1-10a (Brahms Op. 117/1, PDF 21), where 4̂ and 2̂ carry the rhythmic weight and the dissonance pulls them to 3̂ and 1̂. At the return the same 2̂ becomes the movement's registral peak, B♭6 at 79, approached by the passing run F-G-A♭ (one passing tone per step, Ex. 1-8). The climax tone is therefore the same scale degree the listener already knows as the theme's high point. No change.

### 4. The b phrase 15-22 follows the Handel model; its fragments are short but end on a clear half cadence (15-22; no change)

Oboe 15-16: C-D♭-E♭-F-E♭ (3̂-4̂-5̂-6̂-5̂), a rise to 5̂ with upper neighbour 6̂, as in Ex. 1-10b (PDF 21-22). The solo violin answers downward (17-18), the oboe goes into F minor with natural ♭6̂ D♭ (19, descending form, PDF 28) and the leading tone E♮ appears in Vln II at 20 (raised 7̂ moving to 8̂, PDF 27). The violin's F-G-E♭-D♮-E♭ at 21-22 uses D♮ as a chromatic intensifier of 5̂ (PDF 24, Ex. 1-15) to make the half cadence. The two-bar fragments are less singable than theme A, but this is the contrasting middle of a small ternary, and the half step at 21-22 gives the return of theme A at 23 a clear goal. Protected (c091, standing list: Ob/solo Vln dialogue 15-22).

### 5. Minor-key lines use the three forms correctly; no augmented seconds (19-20, 45-51, 63-64; follows)

B♭ minor at 45-46: D♭-E♭-D♭-C | B♭-A♮-B♭-D♭; A♮ is raised 7̂ used as the lower neighbour of 1̂, so it moves to 8̂ (PDF 27). F♯ minor quotation at 47-50: C♯ pickup, A-G♯-F♯, D-C♯ (♭6̂-5̂ half step, PDF 26-27), then C♯-B-A and the enharmonic turn back to D♭ at 51 (A♭-G♭-F, PDF 25). The scan finds no melodic augmented second anywhere (Ex. 1-19 warning, PDF 27). The quotation and the respelling are protected. No change.

### 6. The motto tail still reverses A&S's chromatic order (8, 24, 32-34, 68, 72, 86-89; no change)

PDF 25 spells a descending chromatic passing tone with the letter of the tone before it (A-A♭-G); the motto writes E♭-D♮-D♭-C, diatonic half step first. c211 Finding 1 explains why: D♮ is ♯4̂, the leading tone of 5̂ (V7/V harmony), and the motto is movement I's cyclic figure. The scan shows the motto statements are the only chromatic lines in v351 that use this order, so the exception stays confined to the motto. No change.

### 7. Mixture and modal colour stay inside major-minor (21, 47-50, 62-64, 78-81, 87-89; follows)

A&S PDF 29 calls raised 6̂ and 7̂ in minor one kind of mixture and says the minor of D♭ or G♭ is written in sharps. The score's G♭ minor (iv of D♭) is written as F♯ minor at 47 with its own signature, as PDF 29 prescribes for Chopin Op. 28/15. The ♭6̂ F♭ appears in the melody only in the inner and horn lines (Vln I F-F♭ at 62, horn 3 F-F♭-E♭ at 79-81, cor anglais F-F♭-E♭ at 87-89); theme A itself is never put into minor at the return, which keeps its identity fixed for the listener. The two Phrygian ♭2̂ colours (G♮ in F♯ minor at 49, B♭ in A minor at 63) sit inside minor, as PDF 30 says other modes appear in this repertoire. No change.

### 8. The last melodic tone is 3̂ (C5), which is less final than 1̂ (89-92; no change)

PDF 20: motion to 3̂ or 5̂ "will not have the same finality as motion to 1̂". The movement ends with Vln I on C5 over the A♭ chord. This is the cyclic rule (C5 on top at start and end) and leads attacca into the scherzo, whose horn begins on G♯4 = A♭. The open ending is the reading's own device used on purpose. Protected (link, plagal close, Vln I C5). No change.
