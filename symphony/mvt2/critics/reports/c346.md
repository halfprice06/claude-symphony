## Findings

Every trombone note in the movement is playable as written. Nothing here stands between this score and a master's. Only Finding 1 proposes a change, and it is optional.

### 1. Bar 60: bass trombone A♭2 and tuba A♭1 enter pp in their low register (optional; not applied)

**Problem.** At 60 the low brass enter `\pp\<`: Tbn 2 on E♭3, B.tbn on A♭2, Tuba on A♭1. Every other part is marked higher. The strings, woodwinds, Hn 1 and Bsn are f from 59 and crescendo, Hn 4 is p, and the timpani roll is p (score page 4). So the low brass start two to three degrees under everyone else.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 319: "In the low register a pianissimo is also hard to control, and extremely loud notes, though not easy, are safer than soft ones." At 300 dpi, Ex. X-69 (PDF 329) starts the "very strong" register on A2, in the first space of the bass staff. A♭2 is below that, at the top of the "dark and rather nondescript" band E2–G2. c216's table put A♭2 in the "very strong" register, which is a misreading of the chart. Under a forte orchestra a p attack is no easier to hear than a pp one. The only effect of pp here is a harder, less secure soft attack on the lowest notes, for players who have rested since bar 1.

**Answer to the protected reason.** c218 keeps the "pp/ff brass entries at 60 and 63" on the do-not-touch list, citing Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: the trombones' crescendo from p or pp reaches the woodwinds' mark. A start at p meets that rule just as well. It also matches Hn 4's p and the timpani's p, which enter on the same bar. Tbn 2's E♭3 lies in the "very strong" register, where pp is safe, so it can stay pp. Changing the bass trombone and tuba would put two different dynamics in the low brass on one entry, so changing all three is cleaner.

**Fix (optional).** In sections/bSec.ily:
```
bSecTbnTwo:   es2\p\< ges2 \breathe |
bSecBassTbn:  aes,1\p\< \breathe | ...
bSecTuba:     aes,,1\p\< \breathe | ...
```
I did not apply this. It touches a c218 protected item, and the gain is a safer attack, not a different sound. The composer should decide.

### 2. Bars 63–64: trombone 1's quotation; slide positions and breath (recorded, no change)

From Ex. X-70, the positions are: E3 II, C4 III, B3 IV, B♭3 I, A3 II, A3 II, G♯3 III, A3 II, F3 I (or VI), E3 II. The largest move is IV→I under the slur B3–B♭3. That is a falling pitch with the slide moving in, so the slur is natural. There is no I–VII alternation of the kind Ex. X-71 warns against. The whole line lies in the "very strong" A2–F4 register (PDF 329), which is the right place for ff. The phrase lasts about 9.5 s at ff (4.4 s at ♩=54, then 2.5 + 2.9 s) with no rest. Adler PDF 321 says one slur is one breath. Here the slurs are short, and the separately tongued `a4--` on 64 b1 gives a natural place to catch a breath if a player needs one. c216 reached the same view. A printed breath would cut the turn G♯–A, so leave it.

### 3. Bar 63: trombone 1's first note after 62 bars' rest (recorded, no change)

Tbn 1 comes in ff, solo, on E3 on the half-beat of 63. The strings' sf chord on 63 b1 contains E in three octaves (Vln I E6, Vln II E5, Vla E4). The part's cue at 61–62 (B.tbn, small notes) ends on F♭2, which is E2. So the player hears and sees the pitch before playing. Adler PDF 329 says tuning is set with the main slide. A player who has sat out 62 bars will set it during the rests, and nothing in the part prevents that. Follows.

### 4. Bar 64: bass trombone D2 below the tenor trombone's range (recorded, no change)

Ex. X-68 (PDF 328) sets the tenor trombone's bottom at E2. The bass trombone's D2 at 64 needs the F attachment, which is standard on the instrument (PDF 327 shows bass trombones with F, and with F and E, attachments). D2 (F attachment, about fourth position) to E2 (F attachment second, or seventh on the open side) in quarters at ♩=42 is easy. The marking is p, in the "dark" register, which suits the fading A-minor chord. The `a,2\p d,4 e,4` stays.

### 5. Trumpets: tacet pages written in C (recorded, no change)

The score heading says "Trumpets and percussion tacet". The parts book gives each trumpet a tacet page titled "Trumpet n in C". This follows Strauss's advice as Adler reports it (PDF 317): notate the trumpets in C and let the player choose the instrument. It agrees with movement I. Nothing to change.

### 6. Bars 61–62: trombone 2 has no breath mark after 61, unlike the bass trombone and tuba (recorded, no change)

c286 added breaths after 61 for the tuba and bass trombone, because of the air they use at f in the low register. Tbn 2 holds F3–A♭3 | B♭3–A♭3 in tenuto halves for 8.9 s at f. The line lies in the "very strong" register and comes after a breath at the end of 60. A tenor trombone uses much less air than a bass trombone or tuba (PDF 328: the larger bore and bell of the bass instrument), and the halves are tongued (PDF 321), so the player can breathe between notes if needed. Adding a mark only to match the other parts is not needed.
