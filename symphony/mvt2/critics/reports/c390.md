## Findings

The movement follows every applicable principle of the unit. The ending, the exported themes and the quotations are unchanged. Only Finding 1 reports a defect, and it is in the shared theme-record tool, not in the score.

### 1. Since v389 the theme record misplaces Vln II by one bar after bar 47 (tool fix; no score change)

**Bars:** 47; reported at 53 and 57.

**Problem.** The record check on v389 and v390 prints:
- `MOVED themeBone VlnTwo: expected at 53, found intact at [52]`
- `MOVED themeBtwoBuild VlnTwo: expected at 57, found intact at [56]`

It still reports 11/11. v380 and v388 give no MOVED lines. The score is correct: page 4 shows Vln II's F5 at 53 with Vln I.

**Cause.** c388 (v389) put the MIDI fade on the fp at 47 into the parallel construction `<< { \after 2 … eis'1\fp\> } \tag #'score { s8 \set Staff.midiExpression … } >>`. The same construction is used in the violas and basses. lyparse.py keeps only the first token of a `<<` block. Here that token is `{`, so the whole bar is dropped and every later onset in Vln II reads one bar early. The violas and basses lose the bar in the same way, but nothing in the record checks them after 47.

**Why it matters for the cycle.** The other threads use this record to see whether movement II changed its exported themes or quotations. A false MOVED line, or a real change hidden by the one-bar shift, could send them a wrong signal.

**Fix (tested).** This goes in lyparse.py, which belongs to the finale thread, after `first = inner[:1]` in `events()`:

```python
            if first == ['{']:
                d2, k = 1, 1
                while d2:
                    d2 += {'{': 1, '}': -1}.get(inner[k], 0); k += 1
                first = inner[1:k - 1]
```

- The patch is at /tmp/critic-390/lyparse-c390.patch.
- With it, v389 and v390 give 18/18 and 11/11 with no MOVED lines.
- With it, movement I v371 stays 35/35 (--beats 4), scherzo v362 stays 52/52, and finale v326 stays 41/41 (--beats 4). Those are the same results as without the patch.
- If the finale thread does not want the patch, the alternative is in mvt2.md: note that Vln II, Vla and Cb read one bar early after 47 in themecheck output.

### 2. The link from movement I is a textbook common-tone modulation, and it still matches movement I v371 (verified; no change)

**Bars:** 1–6.

- PDF 587 and Review 2 name C major → A♭ major as the standard case: major triads a third apart, the common tone exposed on top, and a chromatic inflection.
- Bar 1 holds movement I's C5 in Vln I, ppp, on the D string. The violas' E4 falls to E♭4 at 2. That is a falling, decreasing-intensity inflection (PDF 585), the gentle kind, and it suits an attacca at ppp.
- Movement I v371 coda.ily still ends with `\laissezVibrer` on Vln I C5, Vln II G4, Vla E4, Vc C3 and Hn 3 C4. II takes up exactly these five with `\repeatTie`.
- Hn 3's C4–D♭4–C4 over iv6/4 at 3–6 plants the plagal neighbour and the D♭ key. This is the Brahms Op. 39/8 practice of planting the new tonic beforehand (PDF 595).

**Fix:** none.

### 3. The join into the scherzo carries both of Widmung's pitch links, and the E♭→E inflection is in the same octave (record; no change)

**Bars:** 87–92; scherzo v362 bars 1–6.

PDF 587 says that when both triads are major, the common-tone modulation also contains a chromatic inflection. Ex. 32-3 has the held A♭/G♯ and the inner C–B (C–C♭). The join has both, and a third:
- **Common tone.** Vln II holds A♭4 at 87–92; the scherzo's Hn 1 enters alone on G♯4 at its bar 1, at the same pitch.
- **Inflection E♭→E.** Cl 2's E♭4 at 89–92 is followed by the scherzo's Hn 1 E4 at its bar 4. That is the E♭→E♮ inflection at the same pitch. The horn then falls to D♯4 at 5–6, the same E♭ respelled, as the Appassionata turns E♭–F♭–E♭ into E♭–E♮–E♭ (PDF 600–601).
- **C–C♭.** It lies between II's top C5 and the scherzo's Bsn 2 B2 at bar 4, in a lower octave. That is acceptable, because the C5 must stay on top (cyclic rule).

The pause between movements differs from Widmung, which has none. The lone G♯4 makes up for it, because it restates the common tone before anything else sounds.

**Fix:** none. Keep Cl 2's E♭4 at 89–92 at that pitch; the record holds it as lastEflat.

### 4. Both remote areas are entered and left the way Haydn integrates C minor into A major (record; no change)

**Bars:** 37–53, 62–65.

PDF 597–598 sets out Haydn's method: the remote key's main top-voice pitch is stressed in the main key beforehand under its other name, and the return goes through that pitch respelled.

**F♯ minor, 47–50.**
- F♯ is G♭, the upper neighbour that opens theme B: F5–G♭5–F5 at 37 and 53, and G♭5 again at 39.
- The quotation's continuation in the cor anglais leaves the area by two inflections in its own voice. A4 falls to A♭4 at 51 (falling, gentle, PDF 585), and F♯ comes back as G♭4 in F4–G♭4–F4 at 52–53.
- That neighbour is theme B's own head, which the violins then take up at 53 an octave higher.
- So the remote area is heard as the theme's neighbour note expanded into a key. This is also what K. 542 does with its chromatic motive (PDF 602).

**A minor, 63–64.**
- A♮ is planted at 40, 46, 48–50 and 56 (c250).
- The quotation's own line, C4–B3–B♭3–A3, is the motto tail. Hn 1 answers it at its own pitch, E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4, at 67–68, ending on the C4 where the trombone's descent began.
- E6 returns as E♭6 at 72.

**Record correction for mvt2.md.** In the v211 entry, "every voice falls a semitone into E♭7 at 65" is not exact:
- Tbn 1 (E3) and the bass trombone (E2) stop at 64.
- F♭ = E reaches E♭ only in the double basses (E2→E♭2) and, two bars later, in Hn 1's E♭4.

Suggested wording: "every voice that continues into 65 falls a semitone (Vln II D4–D♭4, Vla G♯3–G3, Vc B2–B♭2, Cb E2–E♭2); Tbn 1's E3 and the bass trombone's E2 stop on 64." This is text only.

### 5. The middle section and the climax project the movement's two motives (record; no change)

**Bars:** 1–6, 37–62, 62–65, 87–89.

**Fix:** none.

### 6. At symphony level, II is ♭VI of C, and E (III♯ of C) is reached through it (record; no change)

**Bars:** 89–92.

PDF 593–594: III♯ needs more preparation than ♭VI, and Beethoven gives it one by an augmented sixth. The symphony gets there by equal division (PDF 599–600):
- C (I) → A♭ (II, ♭VI of C) → E (III, ♭VI of A♭ and III♯ of C) → C (IV).
- So each step is a ♭VI common-tone modulation, the easier kind.
- The finale's bar 1 then turns II's A♭ chord into the German sixth to V of C. That is the Chopin route of PDF 593: ♭VI returns as an augmented sixth "after a fantastic digression", and here the digression is the scherzo.

Movement II's part in this is a plain A♭ triad at 89–92, with no added sixth or seventh. That is still true in v390 (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5).

**Fix:** none.
