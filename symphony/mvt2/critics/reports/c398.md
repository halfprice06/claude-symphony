## Findings

### 1. Vln II and violas 64 b3–66: about ten seconds on one bow, fading to niente (applied)

**Bars.** Vln II `b4(\> d'4 | des'2.)~\pp | des'2.\>` and Vla `gis2(\> | g2.)~\pp | g2.\>` (bSec.ily 64, retr.ily 65–66).

**Problem.** The slur starts at 64 b3 and the tie carries it to the end of 66. That is eight beats on one bow: two beats at the allargando's crotchet 42 (2.9 s) and six at Tempo I crotchet 52 (6.9 s), about 9.8 s in all. The section fades to niente alone, under horn 1's entry at 67 and over the four solo basses. No bow change is marked or allowed. Every other long soft note in the movement has one: Vln I and Cb 1–3, Cb 61 and 65–70, the coda 83–92 ("change bow at will"), Vln I 28, 61 and 76 (marked changes on ties). This note is the one exception. The tremolo before it also leaves the direction of 64 b3 open.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39: "Even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow." Ex. II-23 to II-25 (PDF 39–40) show the fix: more bows. Ex. II-20 (PDF 38) marks the down-bow and up-bow over the long note, and "if this passage is executed well, the bow change will hardly be noticed." PDF 38 obs. 1: an upbeat is naturally an up-bow. PDF 38: the diminuendo is natural on the down-bow. The Dvořák Largo lines above do the same on a held pp note.

**Fix (applied).** The 64 b3 pickup is an up-bow, and the bow changes on the tied note at 66, so the fade to niente is a fresh down-bow. There is one mark at each end. Pitches, slurs, hairpins and dynamics are unchanged.
```
% bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo bar 64:   b4(\>\upbow^\markup \italic "sul G" d'4 |
% bSec.ily, bSecVla bar 64:      gis2(\>\upbow^\markup \italic "sul C" |
% retr.ily, retrVlnTwo:          des'2.)~\pp | ... des'2.\>\downbow |
% retr.ily, retrVla:             g2.)~\pp | ... g2.\>\downbow |
```
The cellos are not changed. Their B♭2 is restruck at 65 (64 ends with a separate eighth), so they hold only six beats (6.9 s) at pp.

### 2. Cellos 59: the f arrival is the only string attack on an up-bow (applied)

**Bars.** Vc 57–60 (bSec.ily), doubling horn 1's counter-line.

**Problem.** Counting from 48 (each half note a bow): 57 `f2` is a down-bow, `ges2( | f4)` up, `es2(~ es8 f8)` down, and then 59 `ges2\f` is an up-bow, starting at the tip. On 59 b1 Vln I/II (`es'''4.(\f`, one bow), the violas' first sextuplet and the basses (`ges,,2\f`) all start down-bow. The cellos alone take the f at the light end of the bow. c096 split 59 into two bows (U, D) so that 60 would not be a second four-beat up-bow. That avoided two long up-bows, but it left the f on the up-bow.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog of the bow than toward the tip." Ex. II-20 (PDF 38): change bow on a held note, "so that the following attack will be solid." PDF 38–39: a slur is one bow, and at forte fewer notes go on a bow. Four beats at about crotchet 64 (3.75 s) is the length Vln I/II already play at f in the same bar.

**Fix (applied).** Change bow on the tied E♭ at 58 b4 and take 59 in one bow. Then 59 is a down-bow at the frog, 60 is still a plain up-bow crescendo (its existing `\upbow` now confirms the natural direction), and 61 ff is a down-bow, as c085 and c096 intended. There are no two up-bows in succession, which answers c096's reason.
```
% bSec.ily, bSecVc bars 57-60
f2\< ges2(^\markup \italic "espr." | f4) es2~ es8(\upbow f8) | ges2(\f es4 fes4) | es4(\upbow\< f4 ges2) |
```
Count after the fix: 57 D, U; 58 (U cont.), D, U on the tie; 59 D (f); 60 U (crescendo); 61 D (ff). The pitches, ties and the anticipation F at 58 b4.5 (c082) are unchanged. In the part, the trombones' cue from the cellos (vcQ) now shows the new slurs, which is correct.

### 3. Vln II 52: crescendo on a down-bow (recorded, no change)

After c376 the bar reads `f''2(\< es''4) c''4(\p`, and the count from c186's up-bow at 45 still makes 52 b1–3 a down-bow. c258 recorded this. c376's slur does not change the count. A fix needs a hook at 51 or a change to c186's 49 slur, which keeps the 52 b4 pickup an up-bow with Vln I. The crescendo is three beats in an inner part at p, and the habit is easy to counter there (PDF 38, "without overmarking").

### 4. Intro 2–3: Vln II, violas and cellos hold six beats without "change bow at will" (recorded, no change)

Vln I (nine beats) and the basses (shorter bow, PDF 39) carry "change bow at will". The inner strings hold `aes'2.~`, `es'2.~`, `aes,2.~` for six beats at crotchet 52, about 6.9 s, pp to niente. That is within one bow at pp for violins and violas. It is at the cellos' limit but still normal practice, and the 65–66 cello hold is the same length. Adding the text would crowd the attacca system for little gain. If the composer wants the coda's consistency, add `^\markup \italic "change bow at will"` to the cellos' A♭2 at 2.

### 5. Violins 58 b3–4 and 82: diminuendos on up-bows (recorded, no change)

At 58 the slur `ges'''8(~ ges'''4~ ges'''8 f'''8)` is an up-bow under a printed diminuendo. It is a release before the f down-bow at 59, and the up-bow sets up that down-bow, so the order is right. At 82, `aes''2.\p\>` is an up-bow (c258 recorded it). Both fades start near the tip, where the stroke is already light, and changing either would disturb protected arrival plans (c246 at 79–81).

### 6. Arpeggiando and notes per bow in the violas (follows)

The viola broken chords at 37–40, 53–58 and 71–78 are the on-the-string arpeggiando of Ex. II-43 (PDF 48): slurred across adjacent strings at moderate speed. Six notes per bow at pp/mp and six sixteenths per bow at f in 59–60 match Mendelssohn's counts in Ex. II-21 and II-22 (PDF 39). Nothing to change.
