## Findings

1. **Bars 75 and 78: two horn entries after long rests have no cue in the part, where every other horn entry does.** (applied)
   - Horn 2 rests from 63 to 74, about 44 s across "allargando" and "Tempo I". It then enters p on written B♭3 (concert E♭3), the lowest note in its part. That register is "dark and may be a bit unfocused" (Ex. X-5, PDF 303).
   - Horn 1 rests from 71 to 78 b2, about 27 s. It then enters mf on written G4, a2 with horn 2.
   - Adler PDF 304: the player "must hear and then find each note in his mind and in his embouchure". Neither part gives the player anything to hear before the entry.
   - The other horn entries after long rests already carry cues: Hn 2, 3 and 4 before 60-61 (c135); Hn 3·4 before 79 (c455); Hn 1 before 53 (C.a., c075).
   - The cellos supply the pitch in both places. At 71 they start the counter-line on E♭3, and Hn 2 joins that line at 75. At 78 b3 they play C4, Hn 1's entry note.

   Fix (sections/aTwo.ily; part-only, so the score does not change):
   ```
   aTwoHnTwo = {
     R2.*2 | \tag #'score { R2.*2 } \tag #'part { \cueDuring #"vcQ" #UP { <>^\markup \tiny "Vc." R2.*2 } } |

   ```
   I used the existing `vcQ` quote, which the Cbsn and B.cl. cues at 77-78 already use, so the cue lines up. In the parts, the Hn 2 cue shows the cellos' 73-74 (written E♭4-F4-G4 | F4-E♭4-D4), leading to its B♭3. The Hn 1 cue shows the cellos' 76-77, the same notes Hn 2 plays there. Both horn parts stay on one page.

2. **Bars 61-62: horn 4, a low horn, plays the top line a2 with horn 3 (written E♭5-F5-G♭5, f).** (not applied) This departs from PDF 303 (I and III high, II and IV low). It is protected (c064, c069, c086), and its reason still holds: with the trumpets tacet, two horns are needed to equal one trombone at f. Written G♭5 is well inside Ex. X-4's range. I do not re-raise it.

3. **Bars 87-90: horn 4 muted at pp on written C4-C♭4-B♭3.** (not applied, no change) This is the low end of the "deep and solid" register, which Ex. X-5 marks as starting to darken. Under a straight mute it is the least secure part of the range. PDF 303 says low notes work best "sustained or played in slow-moving passages". Here they are dotted minims under one slur at crotchet = 52 to 44, with the cor anglais doubling the F4-F♭4-E♭4 line an octave higher (c104, c150). The writing follows the reading, and the protected close is not reopened.

4. **Bar 1: horn 3's held C4 from movement I.** (not applied, no change) PDF 309 says one slur means one breath; the same applies to a tie. Movement I's horn 3 holds C4 ppp for its last two 4/4 bars, the second with a fermata. The note then carries into movement II's bar 1, beats 1-2. c140 measured the hold at about 14 s before horn 4 relieves it on beat 2. That is within the soft-slur lengths above, so it follows.
