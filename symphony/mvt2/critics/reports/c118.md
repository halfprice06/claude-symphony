## Findings (ranked)

All four findings change only slurs and bow marks. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes, and the scratch MIDI is byte-identical to v118's.

### 1. Vln I 61–63: the ff whole note is one bow, so the sf at 63 is an up-bow while Vln II and cellos attack it down-bow (applied)
- **Problem.** At Largamente ♩=54, Vln I plays `des'''1--\ff` "molto sostenuto". That is 4.4 s of ff on one bow at the climax of the B section. Then 62 has two half notes (U, D), so the sf tremolo at 63 starts on an up-bow. Vln II (`aes'2-- des''2--`) and cellos (`aes2-- des'2--`) change bow at the half bar in 61, so they reach 63 on a down-bow. The first violins are the only section that runs out of bow on the held ff, and the only one that attacks 63 from the tip.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus): "A down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid." PDF 39: "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow." The PDF 38 point about frog and tip ("one is able to play louder and heavier toward the frog") also applies to the 63 sf. c096 used the same remedy for the basses at 60.
- **Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVlnOne` 61).** `des'''2~--\ff\downbow^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto" des'''2\upbow |`. 61 still starts down-bow (c085/c096's settled point), the tie means no new attack, 62 is D U, and 63 is D with the other strings. MIDI unchanged.

### 2. Vln I/II 77–78: a five-beat down-bow carries the crescendo into the f, and the slur runs over the barline (applied)
- **Problem.** `g'''4( es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 | aes''2)\f` is one bow from 77 b1 to 78 b2: five beats, at ♩=52 and then the poco allargando ♩=50, about 5.9 s, while the hairpin from 75 grows mf to f. The bow is a down-bow, so the crescendo runs toward the tip. In theme A's first statement the same figure ends its bow at the barline (29: `g''4( … bes'8) | aes'2.`). Here the slur has become a phrase slur over the deceptive cadence. Then `c'''4` takes a separate bow before the grace run.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: "Never mark long phrase slurs in string parts … The only slurs that should be used are the ones that designate the notes to be performed on one bow". The same page: "the tendency to play a crescendo with an up-bow is quite natural". PDF 39: do not overload the bow in a slow passage.
- **Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoVlnOne`/`aTwoVlnTwo` 77–78).** Vln I: `g'''4( es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8) | aes''2\f \afterGrace c'''4( { … f'''16 g''' aes''' } |` (slur from the C6 through the grace run to the B♭6, as flute 1 already has it). Vln II the same an octave lower. Bow count: 77 D, 78 A♭ U (crescendo into the f on the up-bow), C + grace run + B♭6 at 79 one down-bow. 79 is still the down-bow ff attack, and the c018/c085 `\upbow` on the tie at 80 is unchanged. This answers the c018 do-not-touch on 71–78's bowing: its stated reason ("up-bow crescendo pickups, down-bow arrivals") is kept and extended to 78.

### 3. Cellos 23–30 and 71–82: the counter-melody's crescendos fall on down-bows and its diminuendos on up-bows (applied)
- **Problem.** Count from 23: `es2` D, `aes8( g8 | f2)` U, `g4( | aes2 c'4)` D, `bes2( g4)` U, so 27 `es2(\< f4)` (the crescendo to f) is a down-bow, 28 (f, then diminuendo) is an up-bow, and 30 `c'2.\p\>` (p to pp) is an up-bow. Every swell in the counter-melody goes against the bow. At 71–82 the cellos have the same slurs: 75's crescendo is a down-bow and 79 b1 (the cellos' f under the B♭6 peak) is an up-bow. The violins above them are bowed with the natural direction (pickup up-bow crescendos).
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: crescendo natural on the up-bow, diminuendo on the down-bow. The composer "should indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit". Here nothing is meant to counteract it, so the slurs alone should give the natural direction. Dvořák 9/ii Largo strings, lines 17–20 (`df\ub\<~ | df\db\>`).
- **Fix (aOne.ily `aOneVc` 26; aTwo.ily `aTwoVc` 74, 80–81).** 26: `bes2 g4 |` (was `bes2( g4)`). Then 27 is U (crescendo), 28 D (f, diminuendo), 29 U, 30 D (diminuendo). 74: `bes2 g4 |`, and 80–81: `aes2 bes4~ | bes4\> des'2 |` (was `aes2( bes4~ | bes4)\> des'2`). Then 75 is U (crescendo), 78 `ces'2\f` D, 79 b1 D, and the hemiola A♭2 | B♭2 | D♭2 gets one bow per half note (U, D, U), which marks the 3/2 grouping more clearly, with 82's diminuendo on a down-bow. The pitches and the protected counter-melody are unchanged. The horn slur at 80–81 stays.

### 4. Cellos 41–47: the swells under the cor anglais and theme B's head run against the bow (applied)
- **Problem.** The cellos re-enter at 41 after four bars' rest, so `des1\pp\<` starts down-bow by default. The crescendo is then a down-bow and 42's `des4(\p\> … des4)` diminuendo an up-bow, the reverse of the c038 swell they share with the cor anglais and flute. At 45–47 the cellos carry theme B's head. In 46, `bes4( a8 bes8 des'2\<)` is one bow, a down-bow after the up-bow pickup at 44 b4, so the crescendo to mf runs out at the tip. Then 47's `cis'2.\mf\>` diminuendo is an up-bow, on the C♯7 pivot under "poco rit.".
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 38 (crescendo/diminuendo tendencies; mark direction to counter habit). PDF 39: "the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter". Brahms 1/iv, Ex. II-55 (PDF 54): each hairpin in the low strings has a bow of its own.
- **Fix (bSec.ily `bSecVc`).** 41: `des1\pp\<\upbow`. 46: `bes4( a8 bes8) des'2\< |`. Count: 41 U crescendo, 42 D diminuendo, 43–44 b2 U (six beats pp), quarter rest (retake), pickup and 45 U, 46 D then U (crescendo), 47 D (mf, diminuendo), 48 U.

### 5. Not changed: violins 28 and 30 take their diminuendos on up-bows
At 27–30 the violins' f arrives on `bes''4(\f\> aes''4)` after the tied F, which is an up-bow from the tip, and 30's p>pp is also an up-bow (Adler PDF 38). I did not change it: the bowing of 23–30 is protected (c018), and the violins' crescendo pickups are correctly on up-bows. A fix would mean `\downbow` retakes, the overmarking Adler warns against. I report it so the composer knows it remains. With finding 3 the cellos now give the natural direction underneath.
