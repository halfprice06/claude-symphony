## Findings

### 1. Theme A, second phrase (11–14, 27–30, 75–78): a step-higher copy of the first phrase, and its chromatic tone moves the wrong way. **Applied.**

Problem. The second phrase repeats the first phrase's shape a step higher: arpeggio up to a held note, then a chromatic fall (E♭–D–D♭–C in bar 8, F–E–E♭–D♭–C in bar 12). The composer's own note in critique.md flags this. The phrase's high point (F) is only a step above phrase 1's (E♭), and both arrive on beat 3 of the phrase's first bar. A listener hears the same gesture twice and no climax.

Evidence. A&S 339: a descending chromatic passing tone normally flattens the note before it (F–F♭–E♭). In bar 12 the E♮ is harmonised as a leading tone (G–B♭–D♭–E, vii°7 of F minor) and then falls to E♭ over A♭/E♭. The raised note is left against its tendency, and the diminished seventh does not resolve. A&S 333 says a longer passing motion can fill a larger interval, and A&S 338 says expressive weight comes from dissonance reached by leap. Theme A's second phrase has neither. Dvořák's tune (above) gets its effect by holding back the peak.

Fix (applied). Phrase 2 leaps a 4th from F to B♭, a 6–5 appoggiatura over IV that falls to A♭. The line then descends by step through a full octave to A♭, so the D♭ (the 7th of V7) resolves to C (A&S 337). The bass and the bar-8 motto are unchanged. The phrase now peaks in bar 6 of 8, a 4th above phrase 1.
- Horn 1 (aOne.ily): `aes4. c'8 f'4~\< | f'4 bes'4\> aes'4 | g'4 es'8 des'8 c'8 bes8 | aes2.)\! |`
- Vln I and Vln II, 27–29: `aes'4.\< c''8 f''4~ | f''4\mf\> bes''4 aes''4 | g''4 es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 |`
- Vln I 75–77 (aTwo.ily): `f'''4 bes'''4 aes'''4 | g'''4 es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 |`. Vln II and Fl 1 take the same notes at their own octaves.
- Accompaniment on beat 2 of bars 12, 28 and 76 becomes plain IV in place of the diminished seventh: Cl 2 `aes'4 aes'4 aes'4`, Bn 2 `aes4 f4 aes4`, Vla `<f' aes'>4 <f' aes'>4 <es' aes'>4`.

This also removes four of the movement's chromatic descents (see Finding 4).

### 2. Climax (78–82): the peak note is consonant, and it repeats the F–E–E♭ fall a fourth time. **Applied.**

Problem. The ff peak in bar 79 is F6, the 3rd of IV. It is reached by a 4th from C6, held, and then slides down through F–E–E♭–D♭ again. The loudest moment has no dissonance and no new contour.

Evidence. A&S 338: an appoggiatura is reached by leap and resolved down by step. A&S 336: a dissonance is "emphasized when it is preceded or followed by a leap," which is why incomplete neighbours belong in the top voice.

Fix (applied). The pickup C6 leaps a 7th to B♭6, which is held over IV for a bar, resolves to A♭ in bar 80, and then takes the new cadence of Finding 1. The peak becomes an appoggiatura reached by the largest leap in the movement.
- Vln I / Fl 1, 79–82: `bes'''2.~\ff | bes'''4 aes'''2 | g'''4\> es'''8 des'''8 c'''8 bes''8 | aes''2.)\p |`
- Vln II and Ob 1: the same an octave lower.
- Bar 80 inner parts: Vla `<f' aes'>4:32 <f' aes'>4:32 <es' aes'>4:32`, Cl 2 `aes'4 aes'4 aes'4`, Hn 3 `aes4 aes4 aes4`.

If the composer wants a sharper dissonance, an option is G♮ over IV (♯4̂) resolving up to A♭. The leading tone resolves upward (A&S 338).

### 3. Theme B's head (37, 45, 53) copies theme A's opening gesture. **Applied.**

Problem. Theme B opens A♭–D♭–F (dotted)–E♭: a rising tonic arpeggio to a dotted long note. Theme A opens A♭–C–E♭ (held) the same way, and both continue with the motto's chromatic tail in bar 6 of the phrase (bars 8 and 42). Theme B's second phrase (41) also starts with the same three notes as its first. The two themes are hard to tell apart, and theme B's own cell, the lower neighbour D♭–C–D♭ in bar 38, is heard once and then dropped.

Evidence. A&S 333: "The neighbor-note figure … is often treated motivically." Ex. 20-11 shows a neighbour figure carried through a whole passage and enlarged over four bars. Ex. 20-12 (Mozart) builds a theme from neighbour figures.

Fix (applied). Theme B now opens with an upper neighbour, F–G♭–F, followed by an unaccented passing E♭. Bar 38 answers with the lower neighbour D♭–C–D♭, so bars 37–38 form a double neighbour spread over two bars (A&S 334). Theme B now starts high and turns downward, while A starts low and rises. The rising arpeggio is kept for phrase 2 (41), so B rises to its peak only in its second half. The S-head rhythm is not used, and nothing opens with a rising sixth.
- Fl 1, 37: `f''4.(\p ges''8 f''4 es''4 | des''4 c''8 des''8 es''2)`. Cl 1 plays the same an octave lower.
- Vln I, 45 (B♭ minor): `des''4.(\p es''8 des''4 c''4 | bes'4 aes'8 bes'8 des''2)`
- Vln I / Vln II, 53: `f'''4.(\mp ges'''8 f'''4 es'''4 |` and `f''4.(\mp ges''8 f''4 es''4 |`

All the new notes are chord tones, unaccented neighbours or passing tones over the unchanged viola triplets and bass.

### 4. The motto's chromatic tail is overused. Not applied: this is a plan decision.

Problem. In v01, E♭–D–D♭–C or its transpositions occurs in bars 8, 12, 24, 28, 33–34, 42, 58, 68, 72, 76, 80, 84 and 88, about thirteen statements in six and a half minutes. After Findings 1–2 it still occurs about nine times. A&S 339 treats chromatic passing tones as colour on a diatonic line. Here they have become the main material, which wears out the motto before its last statements in the closing (88) and coda.

Fix (proposed). Keep the tail in theme A (8, 24, 72), the transition (33), the retransition (68) and the horn's last statement (88). In theme B, bars 42 and 58, use a diatonic line over the same chords: `aes''4 bes''8 aes''8 ges''4 f''4` (B♭ is a chord tone of the V7/V, A♭ a passing tone, and G♭ is the 7th of V7 resolving to F). In bar 58 the violins play this an octave higher and Fl/Ob at their octaves. The plan calls bar 42 "the motto's tail at the D♭ level", so the composer should decide.

### 5. The cello counter-melody (23–24, 71–72) stops whenever the theme stops. **Applied.**

Problem. While theme A holds its E♭ across the bar line (beat 3 of bar 23 into bar 24), the cello also holds E♭, so for a beat and a half nothing moves in any voice. Elsewhere the cello moves when the melody moves.

Evidence. A&S 336, Ex. 20-16: accented and unaccented figuration in different voices should combine into continuous motion (complementary rhythms).

Fix (applied). The cello moves under the held note with a passing tone that fills the 3rd A♭–F (A&S 332). It lands on F, the root of vi7, in place of E♭.
- Vc, 23–24: `es2\p^\markup \italic "cantabile" aes8( g8 | f2 g4 |`
- Vc, Hn 1 and Hn 2 in 71–72 (they double the cello there): `es2\mf aes8( g8 | f2 g4 |`

### 6. The b-section answers (15–22) are four fragments in the same rhythm. **Applied (bars 17 and 21).**

Problem. Oboe and violins alternate four 2-bar fragments, all in the rhythm `4. 8 4 | 2 4`, which is also the rhythm of theme A's bars 7, 9 and 11. The compound line is well made (A&S 330, a polyphonic melody between the two instruments), but the violin answers add no new figure.

Evidence. A&S 337, Ex. 20-17: an incomplete neighbour followed by an accented passing tone is "an important idiom" for decorating a falling second.

Fix (applied). Vln I 17: `aes''8(\p bes''8 g''4 f''4 | es''2 des''4)`. The B♭ is an escape tone, and the G on beat 2 is an accented passing tone over F minor. Vln I 21: `f''8( g''8 es''4 d''4 | es''2)`, the same idiom over D♭. The oboe keeps the original rhythm, so the dialogue now pairs two different figures.

### 7. In bars 7–14 theme A is not the top line. Not applied.

Problem. The horn plays the theme at A♭3–F4 (B♭4 after Finding 1), while clarinets 1·2 hold C5–D♭5 and A♭4–B♭4 above it. In Dvořák 9/ii (bars 7–18) the cor anglais tune is the highest voice. A&S 330 notes that polyphonically conceived melodies "usually occur in the soprano", so the listener follows the top line.

Fix (proposed). Thin the clarinet chord above the horn. Keep Cl 2 (A♭4–B♭4) as the only voice above the tune, and move Cl 1's notes into Bn 1 an octave lower: `c'2. | c'4 bes4 bes4 | c'2. | bes2. | c'2 des'4 | des'4 des'4 c'4 | bes2. | c'2.`, with Cl 1 resting. The passage stays string-free. Another option is a solo horn marking of *mf espr.* against *pp* clarinets.
