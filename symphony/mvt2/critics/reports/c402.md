## Findings

### 1. Bar 39: the 7th of ii7 does not resolve (applied)

**Bars 39-40, Vln II and violas.**

**Problem:** At 39 b1-2 the chord is ii7 of D flat: E flat bass, G flat5 in the flute, B flat in Vln II and the violas, and the 7th D flat4 in the lower half of Vln II and in the viola triplets. On b3 the bass falls to C and the chord becomes the half-diminished seventh on C. A&S say the 7th of II7 resolves "downward and by step" (PDF 177, Points for Review 2 on PDF 186). Its normal goal here is C, the leading tone, which is the root of the next chord. In v402 neither D flat4 goes there:
- Vln II's lower half leaps a 4th up to G flat4, in unison with the upper half.
- The viola figure steps up to E flat4.
- Only the bass reaches C, and it gets there by leap from E flat, not from D flat.

**Evidence:**
- The same progression in the second statement, 54-55 (ii7 at 55 b1, the half-diminished chord on C at 55 b3), resolves the 7th: bassoon 1 goes D flat3 to C3 (c107, c257). Bar 39 is the only one of the movement's II7/IV7 chords whose 7th leaves upward in every voice and is not a melodic appoggiatura. The others are 9, 12, 25, 28, 55, 58 and 76.
- This is the first, exposed, pp statement of theme B, with only a string chord under the flute, so the inner voices can be heard.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-402/work/sections/bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo, bar 39):**
```
<des' bes'>2 \unisHead ges'2 |      ->      <des' bes'>2 <c' ges'>2 |
```
Effects of the change:
- The lower half now moves D flat4-C4 by step. The upper half still falls B flat4-G flat4.
- B flat still sounds only in the viola, which keeps c142's reason for the unison G flat4: B flat4 was doubling the viola's 7th.
- At 40 the lower half goes C4 to E flat4 while the violas go E flat4 to C4. That is a voice exchange, and the flute's E flat5-D flat5-C5 makes a second one against it.
- New doubling: C now sounds in the bass and in Vln II. It does not need to resolve to D flat, because the next chord is the diminished seventh on A and then B flat minor. The C stays in the violas at 40 b1.

**Checks:**
- The build has 0 warnings and the score is 8 pages.
- analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels.
- My all-pairs scan finds the same six figuration octaves as v402 and nothing new.
- The parts book is 30 pages, and every part except the harp is on one page.
- The engraved chord at 39 b3 is clean (page 2).

### 2. Bars 41 b3 and 57 b3: the F over G flat is an appoggiatura, not a IV7 seventh (no change)

**Bars 41 and 57.**
- The F on beat 3 of 41 (cor anglais F4) and of 57 (F5/F6 in the theme) sounds over a G flat bass and rises to G flat on b4.5.
- A&S's IV7 resolves its 7th downward (PDF 177, 188). This F is therefore a rising major-7th appoggiatura (7-8) over IV, not the seventh of a IV7. It enters by leap (D flat-F) because it is the head of theme B.
- This is a Romantic usage, and it is not a fault under these units.
- c112 rejected changing the IV-vi7 at 41/57, and these bars are protected.

I record the reading only, so that a later critic does not cite PDF 177 against it.

### 3. Bars 12-13, 28-29 and 76-77: ii7 to V7 does not hold 4̂ for the V7's 7th (no change, recorded)

**Bars 12-13, 28-29, 76-77.**

A&S prefer that 4̂, the 3rd of II7, is held over or doubled in an inner voice so that it becomes the 7th of V7 (PDF 180-181, Ex. 12-5d, 12-6a).
- At 12 b3, bassoon 1's D flat4 leaps to B flat3 at 13. The V7's D flat arrives only as the horn's passing eighth at 13 b2.5.
- At 28 b3 and 76 b3, the cellos' D flat4 leaps to B flat3. The 7th of V7 then re-enters by an upward leap at 29 b3 (cellos and horn 3) and at 77 b3.

I made no change, for two reasons:
- At 13, c117 took a held harp D flat4 out of exactly this spot because it sat inside the horn's own E flat4-D flat4-C4 descent. A held bassoon D flat4 would bring the same unison back.
- At 29 and 77 the D flat is part of the protected cello counter-melody, and PDF 177 allows the 7th of V7 to enter by leap.

### 4. Bars 37-38 and 53-54: IV6 stands in for VI in the arpeggio to ii6 (no change)

**Bars 37-38 and 53-54.**
- The chord at 38 b1-2 is G flat/B flat (IV6), not B flat minor (vi). The viola triplet's third note is G flat4. It leads to ii6 at 38 b3.
- This is A&S Ex. 11-12c, "(IV6) II6 instead of (VI)" (PDF 170). Units 11 and 13 treat VI and IV6 as interchangeable here (PDF 168, 204-205).

Recorded so that the B flat bass is not taken for vi and "completed" with an F.

### 5. Bars 87-88: the bass 6̂ of IV6 leaps up a sixth (no change)

**Bars 87-88.**
- Cb F1-D flat2 and Vc F2-D flat3 rise a sixth from IV6 to iv.
- A&S say a 6̂ that leaps up "is left exposed and unresolved" unless it resolves to 5̂ in an inner voice, "either actually present or implied" (PDF 200, Ex. 13-9).
- Here it does resolve in an inner voice: the violas' lower half, horn 4 and the cor anglais play F-F flat-E flat.
- The plagal close also follows the authentic cadence at 82, as in Ex. 13-6 (PDF 197-198).

This follows the reading, and the coda is protected.
