## Findings

### 1. Bars 82 b3-83: the harp's theme head in harmonics has no dynamic or character word of its own (applied)
- **Problem.** At 82 b3 the harp plays E♭4-F4 at pp, and at 83 the head A♭-C-E♭ continues in octave harmonics (the plan's melodic moment for harp). Bar 83 starts a new system. The harmonics carry no dynamic and no word, so the player reads the pickup's pp. Every other solo lead entry in the movement has a character word.
- **Evidence.** Adler PDF 116, rule 1: "Harp harmonics are very soft and must be accompanied very lightly". At the same marking a harmonic sounds well below a plucked note, so pp harmonics after a pp plucked pickup drop the line's level just where the tune starts. Sebesky Ex. 284 (PDF 217, image): when the strings fall back, the harp solo is marked one level above them (mf over mp). Here the strings at 83 are ppp. In Ex. 2A (PDF 18, image) the harp's flourish gets its own word "(EASY)". c266 set the pickup to pp as "the level of the harmonics it leads into". This fix keeps the pickup at pp and marks the harmonics p, so they sound at about that level. It does not undo c266.
- **Fix (coda.ily, `codaHarpR`).** `aes4.(\p-\flageolet^\markup \line { \italic "dolce," \small \italic "harm., L.H. (sound 8va)" } c'8-\flageolet es'4-\flageolet)`. No pitch, rhythm or other dynamic changes. The MIDI harmonics go from velocity 29 to 37 (the pickup's plucked level), so score.midi still matches the printed marks. A hidden `\p` in the MIDI voice is not needed: when I tried one, it caused a duplicate-dynamic warning.

### 2. Bar 79: the counter-melody D♭-C-B♭ is slurred in bassoon 1 and horns 1-2, but bowed separately in the cellos (recorded, no change)
Ex. 281 (PDF 214-215) asks that parts playing one figure share an articulation. Here the difference is a bowing, not a different articulation: separate bows at ff on the cellos' A string give weight to a legato line. c118 chose one bow per note there, and c246 and c258 protect the 78-81 bow plan. The tenuti at 80-81 already match in all three parts. Keep it.

### 3. Bar 47: fp in Vln II, violas and basses under the oboe's mf arrival (recorded, follows the reading)
The C♯7 pivot is attacked fp and fades to pp before the cor anglais quotation enters p on beat 4. This is Ex. 284's device for a sudden harmonic lift: a strong attack, then a fall in volume (PDF 217-218). Keep it.

### 4. Bars 21-22: the solo violin's last answer has a crescendo and no diminuendo (recorded, no change)
The first three answers (15-16, 17-18, 19-20) each end with a diminuendo. The fourth rises into the half cadence and hands over to the tutti pickup at mp, which is the arrival the dialogue leads to (focus shifting to the full section, PDF 14). Keep it.

### 5. Bar 47: the oboe's diminuendo runs to a rest with no closing dynamic (recorded, no change)
Bassoon 1 in the same gesture closes p>pp. The oboe's hairpin ends in silence before the cor anglais's p entry, so a closing dynamic would add nothing (economy, PDF 12).
