## What else I studied

Holistic model for this lens: Tchaikovsky's Canzonetta (corpus `reference-corpus/pdf/TchaikovskyPI_O35__tchai_op35.pdf` PDF 64–70; source `mutopia/TchaikovskyPI_O35/.../mvmt2/`). It is close to this movement in plan. A wind chorale opens it (12 bars, clarinets, bassoons and horns, no strings). The lyrical theme follows over sustained strings marked "pp con sordini" from bar 13 (violino1.ily line 10, violino2, viola). Flute and clarinet take the theme's turn between the soloist's phrases (bars 34–37). The wind chorale returns at the end and the movement runs attacca into the finale. Movement II already has the wind-only link, the solo-and-dialogue middle (15–22), the plagal wind close and the attacca. What it does not have is the Canzonetta's muted string colour, which Dvořák's Largo also uses (`DvorakA_O95/.../strings.notesL.ily` line 10, "con sordino"). I also compared v29 with v39 file by file (diff of every section, of analysis.txt and of the PDFs) and ran my own scan of inner voices: each simultaneous note inside each MIDI track counted as its own voice, which covers chords and shared staves, with doubling runs filtered out.

**Version check, v29 → v39.** Almost everything in these ten cycles has helped: the theme B head rhythm, staggered breaths, held inner parts at 80, the Hn 3·4 crescendo into 61, the cello tenor line at 41–44 and clean al niente endings. One thing got worse. At the B-section arrival (61), v29's Vln I held F5/D♭6, so the leading tone C6 (Vln I and Fl 1 at 60) resolved upward in the top voice. c034 changed beat 1 to F5/A♭5, so the top line now falls a major sixth, from C6 to A♭5, on the downbeat of the ff climax (finding 1).

**Inner-voice scan of v39.** No parallel fifths. The parallel octaves all involve the viola arpeggio's low notes: with the bass at 40 b3 and 56 b3, with Fl 1 at 40→41, and with Cl 2 at 76→77 (B♭4–G4 against the viola's last eighth B♭3–G3). For Cl 2 at 76 b3 I tried A♭4, F4 and D♭5. Each was worse: octaves with the theme's A♭–G, fifths with the bass, or octaves with the counter-melody. c022's B♭4 should stay.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** For most of it, yes. The horn theme over bassoons, the woodwind-choir transition, the order of the crescendo at 53–61 and the staged release at 81–83 are what a professional would write. Next to the Canzonetta, the A section's strings sound like a reading rehearsal: unmuted pp strings under a solo violin and a theme in the violins. The arrival at 61 also has no top voice, and the heavy brass start the climax by switching on rather than growing into it. The findings below deal with these three points.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 60–61: the top voice drops at the climax (a regression since v29). Applied.
**Problem.** At 60 the top voice is C6 (Fl 1 `c'''1`, Vln I `c'''1`, the leading tone over V7). At 61 b1 the highest note in the orchestra is A♭5 (Fl a2 `aes''2`, Vln I `<f'' aes''>2`). The leading tone does not resolve in the top register, and the ff arrival is quieter at the top than the bar before it. D♭6 comes only on beat 3.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 131 (diverging progressions): the upper parts keep rising while the gap fills in. In Ex. 256–257 (PDF 419–420) and Ex. 261 (PDF 428) the top line climbs into the arrival chord. In the tutti plates (Ex. 210 PDF 377, Ex. 212 PDF 380, Ex. 245 PDF 411) the violins and flutes hold the top of the chord. PDF 126–127: when a chord passes between groups, the progression of parts must stay "as regular as if there were no leap from octave to octave". v29 had `<f'' des'''>1`. c034's aim (keep A♭5 with the flutes so the theme's upper octave is in the same instruments) is kept by putting A♭5 under the D♭6.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVlnI`, bar 61):**
`<aes'' des'''>2\ff^\markup \italic "non div., molto sostenuto" <f'' des'''>2 |`
This is a perfect fourth on the A and E strings. Vln I's top line is now C6–D♭6 (60–61) and F6–F♭6 (62). The flutes keep the augmented head A♭5–D♭6–F6–F♭6. The check scripts find no new parallels; the octaves C–A♭ between Vln I and the clarinets/Vln II at 60→61 disappear.

### 2. Bars 60–61: trombones and tuba enter at ff with no preparation. Applied.
**Problem.** The crescendo 53–61 adds strings, then woodwinds, then horns 3·4 (p< at 60, c029). Tbn 1·2, bass trombone and tuba then come in from silence on the ff downbeat of 61. The crescendo jumps a step at its last moment instead of being brought to its peak by the brass.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: the brass are "the group most facile in producing dynamic shades of expression, glorifying crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes", and long crescendos add the brass last. Ex. 248 (PDF 413, Russian Easter Festival): horns, trumpets, three trombones and tuba enter pp cresc. and reach f/ff with the string climax. Ex. 244 (PDF 410): trombones and tuba p cresc. molto into sf. Ex. 257 (PDF 420): trombones p cresc. into the f tutti. Ex. 261 (PDF 428): all brass pp, cresc. Ex. 215 (PDF 385): trombones enter pp. c024's rule (brass enter pp or ff, never mp/mf) is kept. c033's order (strings → woodwinds → brass) is kept, because the heavy brass are still the last to enter.
**Fix (bSec.ily), one bar on the V7 (A♭–C–E♭–G♭ already sounds in Cl/Vln/Hn):**
`bSecTbnOne`: `R1*23 | aes1\pp\< | aes1\ff | bes2 ces'2 | …`
`bSecTbnTwo`: `R1*23 | es1\pp\< | f1\ff | …`
`bSecBassTbn`: `R1*23 | aes,1\pp\< | des1\ff | …`
`bSecTuba`: `R1*23 | aes,,1\pp\< | des,1\ff | …`
I first gave the bass trombone C3. That doubles the leading tone and moves C3→D♭3 in octaves with Vln I's new C6→D♭6, so A♭2 it is. The only new pairs the checker reports at 60→61 are the bass line itself (B.tbn/Tuba with B.cl./Bsn 2/Cb, A♭→D♭), which is doubling. Tbn 1 adds four pp beats before 61–64, which does not tire the player.

### 3. Bars 15–30: muted strings for the first string entry; senza sord. from 37. Applied. (Answers the open point "con sordino 23–30 / A sections", open since cycle 16.)
**Problem.** The strings first enter at 15, pp, under the oboe and a solo violin, and at 23 play theme A sul D. None of them is muted. As a result, the solo violin at 17–22 does not stand out in colour from the section that joins it at 22. The A′ return (71) is also unmuted, so the strings' colour stays the same from 15 to the climax at 79 and only gets louder.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 123: a solo string is used "to obtain that singular difference in colour which exists between a solo stringed instrument and strings in unison". Ex. 222 (PDF 391, Snegourotchka, Lento) shows how: "Violino solo senza sord." over "Viol. I con sord.". Muted strings are the background of the slow examples: Ex. 228 (PDF 396–398, Andante, every string part con sord., solo quartet senza sord.), Ex. 247 (PDF 413, Adagio, violins div. con sord. pp), Ex. 249 (PDF 415, strings con sord. pp after the winds die away), Ex. 256 (PDF 419, Molto andante, all strings con sord. pp, cresc. poco), Ex. 260 (PDF 427, violas and cellos con sord. pp). The two closest models do the same. In the Canzonetta the strings enter "pp con sordini" when the solo begins, after the wind chorale (bar 13). In Dvořák 9/ii the strings are con sordino.
**Scope.** Only 15–30. Bars 1–3 are movement I's held chord (do-not-touch link and attacca), so they stay as they are. Mutes go on in the rest 7–14 (8 bars, about 28 s). Mutes come off in 31–36, where all strings rest (6 bars, about 8 s with the poco rit.). The B section and A′ are senza sord., so the reprise opens up the colour the first statement kept veiled, which is the Canzonetta and Largo plan applied to this form. Dynamics stay as they are. Rimsky's PDF 130 rule that strings lead a crescendo is not affected, because 53–61 are unmuted.
**Fix (aOne.ily, bSec.ily), text only:**
- 15: `aOneVlnTwo` `aes'2--(\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." …`; `aOneVla` `es'2.\pp^\markup \italic "con sord."`; `aOneVc` `c2.\pp^\markup \italic "con sord."`; `aOneCb` `c,2.\pp^\markup \italic "con sord."`.
- 17: `aOneVlnOne` `^\markup \italic "solo, senza sord."`. 22 b3: `^\markup \italic "tutti (gli altri con sord.), espr., sul D"` (the leader stays unmuted, as in Ex. 222).
- 37: `bSecVlnTwo` `"senza sord., div., sul tasto"`; `bSecVla` `"senza sord., unis."`; `bSecVc` and `bSecCb` add `^\markup \italic "senza sord."` to the pizz. at 37; `bSecVlnOne` `des''2(\p^\markup \italic "senza sord." …` at 45.
The labels fit without collisions (checked on page 2–3 of the scratch PDF), and the layout stays at 8 pages.
