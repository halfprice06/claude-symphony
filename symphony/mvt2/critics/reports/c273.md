## Findings

This is the third pass on this chapter. The form, the two builds and the releases follow the reading almost everywhere. I tested one change, found that an earlier critic's reason against it still holds, and withdrew it. So this report recommends no change to the notes or marks.

### 1. Bar 78 b3: horns 1·2 re-enter mf< on the pickup to the A′ peak (tested, withdrawn; no change)

**Question.** Horn 1 has rested since 70 and rejoins horn 2 a2 at `\mf\<` on the beat before the peak at 79. PDF 136 calls mf re-entries "colourless and common-place". PDF 127 has the new group enter "piano or pianissimo" and crescendo faster. PDF 130 has the brass "glorifying crescendo chords" into the climax. The B build does exactly this at 60, where the low brass enter pp< under f strings and woodwind. Dvořák 9/ii horn 1 at 23–25 (`r2 cs2\p\< | d\fz\< b | e->\ff`) does the same.

**Test.** In a scratch copy I set both horns to `r2 c'4(\p\<` at 78 b3 (aTwoHnOne, aTwoHnTwo). The build was clean: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels, parts book 30 pages.

**Why I withdrew it.** This exact entry was p< from c033 to c088. c088 raised it to mf because the C4 is the first note of the horns' counter-melody (`c'4 | des'4 c'4 bes4`), and a p attack two degrees under the f texture leaves the counter-melody with no audible upbeat. c218 checked it against this same page of Rimsky and kept mf for the same reason, and because eight bars is not the long rest PDF 136 means. The low brass at 60 are a different case: they hold harmony for a full bar and have no melodic pickup. The reading's amplification method assumes a crescendo long enough to be heard as a change of timbre. Here it lasts one beat (about 1.3 s). I cannot answer c088's reason, so the entry stays mf<. Recorded so that the next reader of PDF 127/136 does not raise it again.

### 2. Bars 80–83: the release from the A′ peak (checked, no change)

At 81 every group makes the same ff→p diminuendo within one bar (about 4 s at ♩≈46). PDF 130 says short diminuendi are made "by natural dynamic means", so the even fade is right. After that the order of elimination is the reading's: Hn 3·4 leave at the end of 81, Hn 1·2 at 82 b3 (c033), the woodwinds and violins at 83, and the violas, cellos and basses carry on into the coda. The p chord at 82 is the tail of that diminuendo, not a p tutti (PDF 120). Protected (c123, c213, c228). No change.

### 3. The two peaks are scored by their role in the form (61–62, 79–80; no change)

PDF 116 asks whether a passage is a climax or a step, and PDF 119–121 separates full from partial tutti. 61–62 is the movement's only full tutti, with trombones and tuba. The reading says brass alone gives "an extraordinary volume of tone", so this is the main climax. 79–80 is a partial tutti with horns only. PDF 121 says woodwind and horns give little power in forte, so this peak relies on register (B♭6), the grace-note glide (PDF 130) and the strings. The horns' one-beat mf< at 78 b3 (Finding 1) is the brass crescendo into it, without adding brass (c024). This agrees with c123 and c238 and with Dvořák's single brass climax at 90–100. No change.

### 4. Other entries after long rests (PDF 136; checked, no change)

The script found these entries outside the pp/ff rule:
- Hn 4 p< at 60, against the pp< of the trombones and tuba. c029 kept it: a single horn is marked one degree above the trombones (Rimsky PDF 41, 103), and it sits inside the 60 crescendo.
- Hn 3·4 f at 79 inside the ff tutti (c054: brass harmony f, theme ff).
- Timpani f at 79 (c084: a sustained roll sounds more present than its mark).
Each is a balance mark inside a tutti, not an exposed entry, and each has a stated reason. The exposed solos enter p or pp: Ob 1 at 15, C.a. at 41 and 47, B.cl. at 31 (c218), Hn 1 pp at 53 and p at 67. c248 recorded that a melodic solo is not one of the "colourless" entries the rule means. No change.

### 5. Returns are scored differently each time (PDF 117–118; no change)

Theme A has three settings: horn with woodwind basis (7), violins in unison with the cello counter-melody (23), and violins in octaves with the viola figure, reaching ff (71). That is operations a, c, d and e. Theme B moves from Fl, to C.a., to Ob/Vc, to violins, to Fl/Ob/Vln an octave up. The four pedal interludes each have their own solo colour: Hn 3, B.cl., Hn 1, and harp/timpani/Cl 1. The motto tail appears in Hn, B.cl., Hn and Cl, in different registers, ending on Vln I's C5. This is what the reading recommends. No change.

### 6. Section joins and transfers (PDF 125–127; no change)

Every join hands over on a sounding note or a held pedal. 22 (solo E♭5 to tutti pickup), 30–31 (strings to Cbn/Bsn pedal), 36–37 (C.a. and Fl 1 on F5, c139/c271), 47 b4 (Ob to C.a.), 52–53 (C.a. F4 under the violins' entry, c269), 64–65 (German sixth to E♭7, Cb E→E♭), 70–71 (Hn 1 pp under the violins' pickup), 82–83 (harp pickup over the held A♭) and 88–89 (Cl 1 C5 to Vln I C5). The one deliberate break in part-writing, 62→63, is the contrast case that PDF 127's note allows. No change.

### 7. Imitation and echo in the coda (82–88; no change)

The harp's harmonics at 82 b3–83 restate theme A's head after the tutti, an octave up and much softer. That is PDF 129's octave imitation "with a decrease in resonance", which works as an echo. The timpani answer at 84–85 goes lower and to a lower instrument, as PDF 128's law of register asks. Cl 1's last tail at 85–88 follows Hn 1's open statement at 67–68 an octave higher and pp. Woodwind after brass is a related pair, not the woodwind-for-strings echo PDF 128–129 rules out. No change.
