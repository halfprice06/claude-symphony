## What else I studied

I used Dvořák, Symphony No. 7, ii (Poco adagio) as the master comparison, from the corpus Mutopia source `reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/.../DvorakSYMPH7M2/` (the compiled PDF drops the wind notes in bars 1–8, so I read the .ly files). It opens with a solo clarinet melody (`Clarinetto_I_II.ly`, bars 1–8). Pizzicato violins II, violas and cellos accompany it, all below the melody (`Violino_II.ly`: F4–A4 under a melody around D5). The horns enter only at bar 9, f dim., to close the phrase. Its last bars have one line at a time and die away ppp on a single held note. I also compared v19 with v09 by diffing every section file and the density profiles in the two analysis.txt files. Most changes since v09 are improvements: bass line at 9/12, horns 3·4 placed in the middle at 79–82, Bsn 1 on the A′ counter-melody, the viola answers at 15–22. Two places got worse; they are in Findings 2 and 4. I ran allpairs_par.py on v19 and on my scratch build.

Would a conductor take this for a master's work? Mostly yes in harmony, form and the A′ climax. The doubts come from small orchestration slips of the kind Rimsky-Korsakov's examples never show. An accompaniment instrument sits above the solo it accompanies. A counter-line is buried in its own register. The coda's two solo statements of the motive collide. These are the findings below.

## Findings

### 1. Horn 1's counter-line at 53–56 cannot be heard (applied)

**Problem.** At 53 b4–56, horn 1 plays the theme-A head A♭3–B♭3–D♭4–F4–A♭4–G♭4–F4–E♭4–D♭4 at p, with no hairpin. The violas play continuous slurred triplets over the same span (A♭3–F4/G♭4), p < mp. The violins, sul A, play theme B at p < mf. So the counter-line lies in exactly the register the viola triplets fill, and it is the only line that does not grow with the crescendo. At 57 the horn is suddenly marked mp and doubled by the cellos. The line therefore becomes audible only after its first four bars are over.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 74–75 says a woodwind added to a horn in unison "yields a finer legato effect" and "softens and rarefies" it, and names Horn + Cl. RK PDF 79–80 (Ex. 93–94, PDF 269–270): in Snegourotchka, Vln I+II + Cl + Horn carry a line together across a pizzicato texture. Dvořák 7/ii does the same: its first horn entry (bar 9, `Corno_I_II.ly`) is marked f and comes in the gap between phrases, not under a moving figure.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Give Hn 1 a crescendo to mp with the strings, and double it with Cl 1 in unison. Cl 1 rests at 53–56 and plays at 57 anyway, so its line becomes continuous.
```lilypond
% bSecHnOne, bar 53-56
r2 r4 aes8(\p^\markup \bold "1."^\markup \italic "espr." bes8 | des'4.\< f'8 aes'2 | ges'2\mp f'2 | es'2 des'2) |
% bSecClOne, bars 53-56 (replaces the fourth R1*4)
r2 r4 aes8(\p bes8 | des'4.\< f'8 aes'2 | ges'2\mp f'2 | es'2 des'2) |
```

### 2. The harp's chords sound above the solo horn in 7–14 (applied)

**Problem.** The harp RH chords in 7–14 reach E♭4 and F4. The horn's melody at those moments is A♭3, C4 or G3. Bar 7 b2: E♭4 over horn A♭3. Bar 9 b2–3: E♭4 and F4 over horn C4 and A♭3. Bar 10: E♭4 over horn G3. Bar 11: E♭4 over A♭3. In each case the plucked attack is the highest pitch, so the ear hears it as the top line. The F4 in bar 9 is new since v09 (v09 had `r4 <aes c' es'>2`), so this got worse in the last ten cycles. Cycle 1 moved the clarinets below the horn for the same reason, but the harp was left above it.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 97 no. 4: when one part carries the melody, the harmonic basis takes the parts under it. Ex. 119 (PDF 291, Snegourotchka [318]): the harp's off-beat chords stay in the middle register, below the voice and flute. Ex. 111 (PDF 286) is the same. RK PDF 90 rule 2 asks for normal register order. Dvořák 7/ii bars 1–8: every accompanying note lies below the clarinet solo.

**Fix (applied, aOne.ily).** Voice the RH chords in the bass clef so that the top note is at or below the horn's note. Bar 14 keeps its old voicing: if the A♭3 were on top there, the A♭3→C5 (oboe at 15) step would make outer octaves with the bass A♭1→C2 (analysis.txt flagged this when I tried it).
```lilypond
aOneHarpR = {
  \clef bass r4 <c es aes>2\arpeggio | R2. | r4 <es aes c'>4\arpeggio <f aes>4\arpeggio | r4 <bes, es g>2\arpeggio |
  r4 <c es aes>2\arpeggio | <aes des' f'>4\arpeggio r2 | r4 <g bes des'>2\arpeggio | \clef treble r4 <aes c' es'>2\arpeggio |
  R2.*16 |
}
```
Bars 12–13 already lie below the horn (F4/A♭4 and G4) and are unchanged.

### 3. 61–64: sustained tremolo chords split by divisi in some sections only (applied)

**Problem.** At 61–62 the violas are marked "div." on the tremolo dyads D♭4/F4, B♭3/D♭4 and C♭4/F♭4. At 63–64 the Vln II are "div." on E5/C6 and the violas on E4/A4, while Vln I and the cellos are unis. A divided section puts half its players on each note, so these notes have half the weight of the undivided lines next to them. This happens at the sf of the A-minor chord, where the trombone quotation needs a full, even string chord to stand on. All these dyads are easy double stops (thirds, a fourth, a sixth).

**Evidence.** RK PDF 88: to fill the middle register of sustained or tremolo chords, "doubled notes on the violins or violas should be used… The method of dividing strings… should be avoided in such cases, as certain parts of the chord will be divided and others will not." RK PDF 89: in forte tremolo on two strings, choose the intervals that are easiest to play. Ex. 97–98 (PDF 274–276) use double stops in tutti. Cycle 16 listed this divisi under Adler's rule "use divisi where a stop is hard". That reason does not apply here, because these stops are easy, so this does not reverse a protected item.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Text only; the notes are unchanged: `^\markup \italic "non div."` in place of `"div."` on Vln II at 63 and on the violas at 61. The violas' existing "unis., sul C" at 65 and Vln II's "unis." at 65 still apply.

### 4. Coda 83–86: the timpani answer and the Vln I tail sound together; worse than v09 (applied)

**Problem.** In v19 the harp plays the theme head at 82 b3–84. In bar 85 the timpani play E♭–D♭–C, and Vln I enters on the same downbeat with its tail E♭5 (85)–D5 (86)–D♭5–C5. The motive is heard twice at once at two speeds, and the timpani's D♭3 (85 b2) is followed a bar later by Vln I's D♮5. In v09 the timpani answered alone at 85, and Vln I entered only after them. The cycle-13 cut (which was correct on proportion) moved the two together. The harp also stays at f through 81–82, while the whole orchestra makes a diminuendo to p.

**Evidence.** RK PDF 66–67: "Phrases or melodies demanding diversity of expression alone should be entrusted to solo instruments of simple timbres"; an instrument "enjoys greater independence… when used as a solo". The plan (movement-2-plan.md) treats the harp and the timpani as solo "melodic moments" that each need to be heard alone. Dvořák 7/ii's final bars (`Violino_I.ly`, last 8 bars) state one line at a time.

**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily and coda.ily).** Start the harp head with its pickup in bar 82, and move the timpani answer one bar earlier (84–85), so that Vln I's tail at 85 follows it. The harp makes a diminuendo with the orchestra at 81–82. The coda keeps its length (10 bars). Vln I's tail at 85–89, the plagal close at 87–92 and C5 at the end are untouched.
```lilypond
% aTwoHarpR bar 82:  r4 <aes c' es'>4\arpeggio es'8( f'8 |
% aTwoHarpL bars 81-82: <es,, bes,,>2.\>\arpeggio | <aes,, es,>2.\p\arpeggio |
codaHarpR = { aes'4. c''8 es''4) | R2.*3 | R2.*4 | <c' es' aes' c''>2.\arpeggio | R2. | }
codaHarpL = { aes,,2. | R2.*3 | R2.*4 | <aes,, es, aes,>2.\ppp\arpeggio | R2. | }
codaTimp  = { R2. | es4\pp des4 c4 | aes,2. | R2. | R2.*2 | aes,2.:32\ppp~ | aes,2.:32~ | aes,2.:32~ | aes,2.:32\fermata | }
```

### 5. Con sordino strings at 23–30 (not applied; colour choice, still open)

The examples give three cases where a melody or voice is carried over pp con sord. strings: Ex. 102 (PDF 281), Ex. 110 (PDF 285) and Ex. 120 (PDF 292). Dvořák 9/ii, the main model, does the same. This supports the open point from cycles 16–18. If the composer wants it, the place for it is the cello counter-melody accompaniment at 23–30 (Vla div., Cb), with "senza sord." in the 3/4 bar before 37 (the strings are out at 31–36, so there is time to change). I leave it unapplied because the violin theme at 23–30 is protected and marked sul D. A mute changes the colour of that protected statement.
