## Findings

Every principle in the reading that applies to a symphonic slow movement is met. None of the six findings below needs a score change; they are ranked by how close each comes to a weakness, and each says why the text should stay.

**1. The tension curve: one long soft span, 29-58 (Adler PDF 257-258, 267; Dvořák 9/ii).** After the strings' f at 28 nothing in the score is louder than mf until the violins' and cellos' f at 59: about 118 s, 34 % of the movement, in which four solo colours follow each other (B.cl. 31, Fl 1 37, C.a. 41, Ob 1 45, C.a. 47) over light backgrounds. Adler's concerto examples alternate solo and tutti more often (Mozart Ex. XVII-10: tutti 55-57, 65-67, 81-83, 92 between the flute's solo passages), so I checked whether this span is too long for a symphonic movement. Dvořák's own soft span is longer in time: from the brass ff at 25 to the f at 66 there are about 165 s (bars 26-65 at the printed metronome marks), roughly 31 % of his movement, also carried by solo woodwinds over pp strings. The difference is where the middle wave falls. Dvořák puts a short f-ff wave (66-69) in the middle of the middle section and then a pp passage (78-89) before the climax; this score has no middle wave and climbs in one staged build (53-61). The single build is what the movement's protected plan asks for (strings, then woodwind, then brass at 53-61; hidden peak 57-59, c558), and the colour changes in 37-52 do the job Adler gives dialogue: they mark each phrase of the form. Adding a swell at 45-46 would cut across the quotation's soft entry at 47 and the protected octaves at 45-46. No change.

**2. Tempo in the reprise: c519's open "poco animando" at 75 (Adler PDF 279-283, Ex. XVII-20, XVII-21).** c519 proposed MIDI steps 53-54-55 at 75-77 before the poco allargando at 78, and the actor left it open for a tempo pass. My reading is the only place in this series where it can be checked against the books, and the books do not support it. The forward marks in the range are Mahler's "Nicht schleppen" and "Vorwärts" (PDF 282), and they sit in the orchestral interludes and the voice's rising phrase that lead to his climax, followed by "Poco rit." In this score that role is filled by "a tempo, poco a poco animando" at 53, the passage that leads to the main climax. Bizet (PDF 279-281) slows only on the last notes of phrases and never presses. Dvořák's reprise (101-118) keeps Tempo I and only slows. The A′ in this score is a reprise of 8 bars plus a 4-bar peak; giving it a second animando would make the reprise move like the build, and the two climaxes would then be approached the same way, which c303 and c333 recorded as a strength that they are not. I recommend closing c519's option rather than keeping it open. No change.

**3. The coda's length against the reprise (Adler PDF 267, 278-279; Dvořák 9/ii 101-127).** The coda (83-92, 39.4 s) is 0.91 of the A′ (71-82, 43.3 s). In Dvořák the closing bars 119-127 are about half of his reprise 101-118. The ratio is high here because the reprise is short (0.52 of the first A by time, c313), not because the coda is padded. Each coda bar carries one event over a single pedal: harp head in harmonics (83), timpani answer (84-85), Cl 1's last motto tail (85-89), the IV6-iv-I plagal close with D♭-C on top (87-92). That is Adler's sparse accompaniment "to give the performer freedom" (PDF 267, item 1). Return and coda together are 24 % of the movement, the same share as Dvořák's (c443). Every one of these events is protected (coda 83-92, timpani link 84-85, plagal close). No change.

**4. Seams and overlaps at every join (Adler PDF 190, Ex. XV-36; PDF 192, Ex. XV-40).** c443 checked 30-31, 36-37, 52-53, 64-65, 70-71 and 82-83. I checked the rest. 46-47: Ob 1 fades on C♯5 to 47 b3 and the C.a. enters on C♯4 at 47 b4 while Vln II, violas and basses hold their fp chord, so the strings carry the join and the C.a. is given its first pitch an octave above (PDF 275). 86-87: Cl 1 breathes after its D5 (protected breath plan, c226) and continues on D♭5 at 87 as the C.a., muted horns, violas and Vln II enter, so the tail runs across the join. Three seams remain, each at a cadence. At 14-15 the bassoons, bass clarinet and harp stop on the full close and the muted strings enter with Ob 1 at 15. That marks the end of the string-free horn period at a perfect cadence, which is the accent Adler allows ("unless ... one wishes each group ... to be accented"), and the string-free 3-14 is protected. At 44-45 every part ends on 44 b3, the half cadence in D♭, and Ob 1 with the cellos in octaves starts the B♭-minor head on the pickup 44 b4: a one-beat gap that marks the turn to the minor (key plan and pivots 44-47 protected). At 56-57 the triplets pass from harp to violas at the second phrase of theme B, where all the winds enter (c443, c049). No change.

**5. Rhythmic independence and its exception (Adler PDF 259-260, 274).** The accompaniment figures are independent of the tunes everywhere: viola triplets under Fl 1 and the C.a. (37-44), viola off-beat eighths under the quotation (48-51), harp triplets under Cl 1 and Hn 1 (53-56), viola eighths under theme A in the reprise (71-78). The one place where the background moves in the solo's rhythm is the motto tail, bars 8, 24 and 72, where each tail note has its own chord (vi7 - V7/V - V7), so bassoons, bass clarinet and harp move in crotchets with the horn at 8. Adler allows "a simple accompanying figure ... providing harmony to a melody" (PDF 274), and the harmony of the tail is the protected centre of the theme (c001, c012). No change.

**6. Foreground and background in the frame sections (Adler PDF 261-264, 268-273).** Each frame passage gives a different instrument the foreground over a held pedal: Hn 3 over the intro (3-6), B.cl. over the transition (31-36), Hn 1 over the retransition (67-70), harp, timpani and Cl 1 in turn in the coda (83-89). This is Adler's exchange of roles: the harp and timpani, background instruments elsewhere (7-14, 52, 53-56, 65-70), take the foreground only in the coda, and the timpani's E♭-C-A♭ joins two phrases there as in Beethoven's Ex. XVII-13. Each solo is prepared by a thinner texture (strings fade at 65-66 before Hn 1, the orchestra drops to two players at 83), which is the Sibelius and Bartók principle of a background set before the entry (PDF 268-272). The four pedal passages are of similar length (about 21 s each, the coda 39 s), but they are scored for different soloists and sit at the four section joins, so they read as the cut-backs after each tutti that Mozart's Ex. XVII-10 shows (c233). No change.
