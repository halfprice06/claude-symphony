## Findings

No finding is strong enough to change the score. Each item below is what the scans flagged; each is either protected with a stated reason or already decided. I list them so the next critic does not need to re-run the scans.

### 1. Bars 23-29, 53-56: Vln I and Vln II in unison (no change; protected)

**Scan.** Vln II's notes equal Vln I's in every bar of 23-29 and 53-56.

**Reading.** Sebesky PDF 12 asks that every note make "a legitimate difference". A unison of two sections is a weight and colour decision, not a surplus voice: in Ex. 2A (PDF 18) the "12 violins" also play the line together and divide only at the cadence.

**Verdict.** 23-30 is the protected plain first statement (c007, c009). 53-56 is the start of the protected build (c108, c160); the violins split into octaves at 57, which is the change Sebesky's variety principle (PDF 15) asks for. Keep.

### 2. Bar 57 b1-2: F3-G♭3 in B.cl, Bsn 2 and cellos at once (no change; rejected at c157)

**Scan.** At 57 the cellos begin their counter-line on F3-G♭3, and the bass clarinet and Bsn 2 play the same two notes at the same moments (p), above the basses' F2-G♭2. It is the only three-way unison outside a tutti in the movement.

**Reading.** Ex. 1 (PDF 12-13): a line that duplicates the lower-middle register can be cut so that the register clears.

**Verdict.** c157 proposed resting Bsn 2 at 57-58; the composer rejected it under the c124 rule (no background changes in 37-60). I have no new argument against that reason, so I do not re-raise it. From 58 the cellos leave the bass clarinet, so the unison lasts two beats.

### 3. Bar 57 b1: Hn 1's counter-line starts on Vln II's melody note (no change; protected)

**Scan.** The focus scan found one new contact: Hn 1 enters at 57 b1 on A♭4 (half note, mp), the same pitch Vln II plays as the first note of theme B in the lower octave (A♭4, quarter, mf). The horn holds A♭4 while the melody moves on to D♭5.

**Reading.** PDF 13: the background must not divert the ear from the primary element. Here the contact lasts one beat at a softer dynamic, and the horn then moves against the melody (A♭4-G♭4 against D♭5-F5), which is what makes it a counter-line.

**Verdict.** Not a fault. The Hn/Cl counter-line 53-60 is protected (c160), and a change of its first note would break the theme A head it is built from (row 3). Recorded only.

### 4. Bars 75-80: Bsn 1, Hn 2 and cellos on the counter-melody in unison (no change; protected)

**Scan.** Bsn 1, the horn and the cellos play the same notes in 75-77 and 79-80.

**Reading.** This is the reverse of the Ex. 1 case. Sebesky cuts a line that adds nothing; here three colours make one line strong enough to stand against the violins' octaves at mp-f, as the reed and brass combinations in Montenegro's list (PDF 16) do.

**Verdict.** Protected as the "three-colour counter-melody 75-82" (c104 do-not-touch list). Keep.
