## Findings

No finding below changes a note. The movement's mixture was settled by c292 and c432 inside the movement; my lens adds the cross-movement reading. I list it so that the other threads do not undo it, and so that the composer can record the long-range ♭6̂–5̂ resolutions next to the joins they already guard.

### 1. The symphony's key plan is mixture at long range, and both ♭6̂s resolve in the book's order (record; no change)

**What the reading says.** ♭VI is formed from ♭3̂ and ♭6̂ (PDF 390). Once ♭6̂ is introduced it goes to 5̂, and it should not be replaced by ♮6̂ on the way: ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ is good, ♭6̂–♮6̂–5̂ poor (PDF 388; Points 3, PDF 395). E major as III of C is the book's own example of secondary mixture (PDF 393; Points 5, PDF 395).

**What the four movements do.** Movement I ends on the Picardy C major (PDF 391–392, Ex. 23-7). Bar 2 of movement II takes the E♮ back to E♭ in the violas alone (Ex. 23-12b, no cross relation) and sets A♭ under it, so the opening chord of II is C minor's ♭3̂ (E♭) and ♭6̂ (A♭) as a triad: ♭VI of C. Inside II, ♭6̂ of A♭ (F♭) is introduced at 5, worked at 59–64 and 78–81, and last sounds at 88, where it resolves to E♭ (C.a. F♭4–E♭4, Hn 4 and lower violas F♭3–E♭3). The scherzo then takes F♭ as E, its tonic, and II's A♭ as G♯ (Hn 1, scherzo 1–2). The finale resolves both at pitch: its opening E♭4 (violas, Vln II, Cl 2, Hn 2) answers the scherzo's last E4 (♭6̂ → 5̂ of A♭, the F♭–E♭ of II's 88 in the same register), and its Hn 1 A♭4–G4 at IV 1–2 resolves the A♭ that II made a tonic (♭6̂ → 5̂ of C). Between II's F♭ and the finale's E♭ no F♮ is set up as a goal, so the order is ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ at the scale of the whole work. The E of the scherzo is reached from A♭ by the common tone G♯ = A♭, not by a chromatic step G–G♯, which is the avoidance PDF 395 (Points 6) recommends for III♯.

**Why it matters.** This is what gives the emotional arc its logic: II is the symphony's ♭VI, a turn into the "minor-side" major, and the work does not let that turn go until the finale's first two bars. Movement II's part of it is three things: the E4–E♭4 at 1–2, the F♭–E♭ at 88, and the A♭ held to the last chord (Vln II A♭4, which the scherzo's Hn 1 takes as G♯4).

**Fix.** None in the score. In coherence/mvt2.md, under "Ending", add one line: "Long-range mixture (A&S PDF 388, 390, 395): II is ♭VI of C. Its last ♭6̂ (F♭→E♭ at 88) becomes the scherzo's E and resolves to E♭4 at finale bar 1; its tonic A♭ becomes the scherzo's G♯ and resolves to G at finale 1–2 (Hn 1). Keep 1–2 (violas E4–E♭4), 88 (F♭4/F♭3 → E♭) and the A♭4 in the last chord." Nothing for the other threads to do.

### 2. The coda is the Brahms 3/ii pattern of Ex. 23-5, and it closes the movement's minor colour (no change)

**Reading.** In Ex. 23-5 (Brahms 3/ii, PDF 390; checked in the image) ♭VI starts a short passage in minor that ends with a plagal IV♭–I. A&S call it "a very characteristic use of mixture".

**Score.** 77–78: V7 → ♭VI (bass F♭2, Bsn 2 F♭3, Vc and Bsn 1 C♭4), the deceptive cadence of Ex. 23-6. The bass F♭ goes to E♭ on 78 b3; the cellos' C♭4 rises to C4 in the same line, so Vln II's C5 on that beat is a doubling, not a cross relation (Ex. 23-12b). After the second peak (79–82) the coda resolves the colour as Brahms does: IV6 (87) – iv (88, F♭) – I (89), with ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ (F–F♭–E♭) in the C.a., Hn 4 and lower violas, the order PDF 395 calls good. The finale quotes this line at pitch at IV 197–198 (finale.md item 166). The minor iv is the last chord before the tonic, so the movement's final colour is the one that points to the scherzo's E.

**Fix.** None. Keep 78 and 87–89 as they are; both are on the standing list.

### 3. No whole phrase in A♭ minor (Ex. 23-8, Exercise 6); Dvořák 9/ii has one (no change, with the reason)

**Reading.** PDF 392–393 (§5, Ex. 23-8): a phrase or a group of phrases may be cast in the parallel mode, and the contrast is strongest when the mode change varies a repetition. Exercise 6 (PDF 397) asks for exactly that: a major antecedent and a minor consequent with the same figuration.

**Score and model.** Movement II never sets a whole phrase in A♭ minor. Theme A appears three times (7–14, 23–30, 71–78), always in major; the parallel minor appears only as the one-bar iv at 5 and 88. Dvořák's Largo, the main model, does the opposite at section level: its middle section is in C♯ minor, the enharmonic parallel minor of D♭ (`\key cs\minor` at bar 46 in the corpus file).

**Why I do not ask for it.** Three reasons, each from the cyclic plan. (a) The key plan (A♭ – D♭ – B♭ minor – F♯ minor – A minor – V – A♭) is protected (c130, c442), and its minor regions already supply the contrast Dvořák gets from C♯ minor. (b) A parallel-minor phrase inside II would sound A♭ minor's C♭ = B as a tonal goal, and B is the dominant of the scherzo's E; it would take away part of the scherzo's arrival. (c) The one-bar A♭-minor iv at 5 is the chord of movement I's 73–76 (mvt1.md, line 242: "the same chord as movement II's 3–6"), and 88 repeats it at the end. That is the A&S §5 idea used as a frame (the same minor colour at the start and the end) rather than as a varied repetition. The composer should not add an A♭-minor phrase in a later cycle without answering (a)–(c).

**Fix.** None.

### 4. ♭3̂ is used only as a passing tone and inside ♭VI (no change)

**Reading.** ♭3̂ in major is often a chromatic passing tone (Schubert, Ex. 23-4, PDF 389, image checked: F♮ inside D major, falling to E). Most of the text on ♭3̂ is on printed p. 393, missing from the scan; Points 2c–d (PDF 395) list ♭3̂ only for ♭VI and the minor tonic.

**Score.** C♭ appears at 33 (C.a. C–C♭–B♭, falling, as in Ex. 23-4) and at 78 (Vc and Bsn 1 C♭4, the fifth of ♭VI, then C4 in the same line). There is no minor tonic in A♭ anywhere, and the last chord keeps C5 on top (Vln I) as movement I's last note. This matters cyclically: C is the pitch that joins I and II, so a minor tonic in II would contradict the link. The score is right not to use it.

**Fix.** None.
