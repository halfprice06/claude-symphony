Lens 1 (melody and memorability). Assigned reading: Aldwell & Schachter, *Harmony and Voice Leading*, Unit 2 "Intervals" (file "harmony and voice leading.pdf", PDF 33-46), fourth series. I read the OCR text of every page from PDF 33 to 46 in full. I also rendered all fourteen pages at 110 dpi and looked at each one, because the OCR loses every example (Ex. 2-1 to 2-22) and garbles labels (for example "MZ2m3 PS" on PDF 33 and "A6and a3" on PDF 45). This report therefore checks the whole score again against the unit and records the result. It does not repeat c341's findings except where a verdict needed confirming.

## What else I studied

For a fresh model I compared the clarinet tune that opens Dvořák 7/ii, in the local corpus: reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O70/DvorakSYMPH7/DvorakSYMPH7-lys/DvorakSYMPH7M2/DvorakSYMPH7M2_musique-Clarinetto_I_II.ly, bars 1-5. At concert pitch it runs D5-D-C-A | G-A-C-A | B♭-A-G-F | E-F-A-F. Its antecedent is steps and 3rds only. It starts on 6̂ over the tonic, an active tone with consonant support (PDF 40, Ex. 2-10). Its consequent opens with a rising m6 (A4-F5) and then descends by step. That is the Verdi gesture of Ex. 2-1a.

On the lens: theme A is the movement's tune to hum, and at the level of this unit it is clean. Every dissonance in it is approached and left by step. Every tritone resolves inward. The outer voices are perfect at each cadence and mostly imperfect between cadences. Theme B is equally clean. I found nothing in the melodic writing that breaks this unit's rules and that is not already recorded and protected. I therefore propose no change.

## Findings

### 1. Theme A against Ex. 2-1 and Ex. 2-6 (no change)

Theme A's first phrase is the major triad of the overtone series (A♭-C-E♭, partials 4-5-6, PDF 36-37). It peaks on the stable 5̂, which is a P5 over the bass at 7 b3, and then descends by semitones through the motto tail. The second phrase rises A♭-C-F, then makes the theme's only rising 4th to B♭ (12, 28, 76), the active 2̂ over IV as a M6 (PDF 40, Ex. 2-10). It then falls by step to 1̂.

mel.py lists the melodic intervals in each of the first two statements (7-14 and 23-30, counting the pickup into 11 and 27):
- m2 / M2: 13
- m3 / M3: 6
- P4: 2

That is the vocabulary of Verdi's line in Ex. 2-1a and of Dvořák 7/ii's antecedent. Dvořák's consequent opens with a leap of a 6th. Theme A has its own 6th only in the reprise (Vln I G5-E♭6 at 77 b1-2, c341), where the melody moves into the upper octave. So the expressive leap is held back for the return, and the earlier statements keep their plain shape. That is a valid choice for a theme heard three times (c411: "three rising statements"). Keep.

### 3. Outer voices beat by beat (no change)

outer.py shows a perfect consonance on the downbeat of every cadence bar: 7, 14, 22, 30, 34-35, 40 b3, 44, 47, 51, 56 b3, 61, 63, 67, 69-70, 82, 88. Between them it shows mainly 3rds, 6ths and dissonances that pass or are prepared. That is §8 (PDF 39). Two places look like exceptions but are not.

At 62 b1-2 the top voice is Fl 1's A♭6, held from 61, over the bass G♭1, a M2. The 9th is prepared as a P5 over D♭ at 61. It becomes a M3 when the bass moves to F♭ at 62 b3. The dissonance therefore comes from motion in one part, the bass, against a held tone, which is the definition in §9. This is the protected climax route at 61-65.

At 86 the outer voices are A♭1 and Cl 1's D5, a tritone, for a whole bar. D5 is the chromatic passing tone of the augmented motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C (85-89) over the tonic pedal, so it is a by-product of the kind shown in Ex. 2-21 (PDF 45). The coda is protected (c194, c199, c223, c431).

### 4. Dissonances approached or left by leap (no change)

mel.py flags these places. All are recorded or protected:
- **10/26/70 b3.5:** the pickup F over the E♭ bass. It is the upbeat of the dominant, leaping a m3 to A♭ over the bass's own move to A♭ (c341, Finding 7).
- **17 b2 and 21 b1.5:** the solo violin's escape tones (protected since c201).
- **41 b3 and 57 b3:** the melody A♭5 and F6 over G♭ (c311 recorded "melody F against inner G-flat at 41 and 57 b3-4 acceptable"). At 41 the A♭ enters after a rest and rises by step to B♭. At 57 the F6 ends an arpeggio of the D♭ chord and rises by step to G♭6 when the bass moves.
- **43 b2.5 and 59 b2.5:** échappées of an eighth (c341 Finding 3; Fl 1 at 41-44 is protected).
- **59 b3:** D♭6 over G1, the d5 that resolves in to C6/A♭1 at 60 (Ex. 2-15).
- **62 b1:** Vln I F6 over G♭1. Vln I is not the top voice here (Fl 1's A♭6 is). F is the 7th of the G♭ chord, and it is approached in the strings by the D♭6-F6 arpeggio. It is inside the protected climax.
- **78 b3.5:** the grace run C6-F6-G6-A♭6 into B♭6 (c008, protected).
- **25 b3:** the cellos' C4, the 7th of IV7, entered by a 3rd. It was accepted as IV7 in the history list and falls by step to B♭3.
- **77 b3 and 81 b2:** the counter-melody's D♭4, the 7th of V7 reached by leap from another tone of the same chord, B♭3. It falls to C4.

None of these is an "isolated" dissonance in the sense of PDF 39. Each one connects by step to the consonance that follows, or it is a member of the chord it sits in.

### 5. Fourths over the bass (no change)

The 4ths fall into three groups, and each is covered by Ex. 2-11 or Ex. 2-12 (PDF 40-41):
- **Neighbour or passing 4ths (Ex. 2-11):** Hn 3 D♭4 over A♭2 at 5. Theme B's G♭5 and F5 at 37 b3 and 53 b3.5. Ob 1's E♭5 at 45 b3. The B.cl.'s D♭3 at 33 b3, where the motto tail descends over the pedal.
- **Suspended 4th:** at 58 b4 Fl 1 and Vln I hold G♭6 over D♭2, prepared at 58 b2.5 and resolving to F6 (c198). Bar 58 b2 is A♭6 over the passing bass E♭2.
- **Consonant 4ths (Ex. 2-12):** 74 b3 is E♭4 over B♭1, the root of V4/3, an inverted 5th. 78 b3.83 is the grace note A♭6 inside the run.

### 6. Minor passages and the quotations (no change)

The B♭ minor head (Ob 1, 45-47) has A♮4 as a lower neighbour on 46 b2. That supplies the raised 7̂ of Ex. 2-17. The phrase still turns to D♭, the relative major, at 47, which is Ex. 2-16 used on purpose. The F♯ minor quotation in the C.a. (47-53) has E♯ at the V. The trombone's A minor quotation (63-64) has G♯. Each minor key is defined by its own 4̂/♯7̂ tritone. Both quotations are protected and unchanged.

### 7. Where theme B reaches 1̂ (recorded, no change)

By §10 (PDF 39-40, Ex. 2-10), an active tone with consonant support "does not sound at rest until it arrives at the final 1̂". Theme B's phrases end as follows:
- 40: on 6̂ over vi
- 44: on 2̂ over V7
- 56: on 6̂ again

Its 1̂ in the top voice arrives only at 61, the climax, as D♭6 over D♭1 (P8). So the middle section's tune is kept open until the climax by this unit's own means. This is the long-range reason the B section sounds like one rising span and not four closed phrases. It agrees with c453's record that "bar 44 is V7 resolving deceptively to vi at 45, which keeps the middle section open". I record it so a later critic does not add an earlier D♭ cadence to theme B on melodic grounds.

### 8. Comparison with Dvořák 7/ii (recorded, no change)

Dvořák's tune starts on an active tone, 6̂ over I (PDF 40). Theme A starts on 1̂ and keeps its first active peak for bar 12. The difference is deliberate: theme A's head is the overtone triad, and its identity is the chromatic motto tail (c171, c450). Moving theme A's first note to an active degree would change the exported theme record and the cyclic link, which the movement I and finale threads depend on. I do not recommend it, and I list it only to show that the comparison was made.
