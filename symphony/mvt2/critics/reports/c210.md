## Findings

### 1. Bar 64 beat 4: complete the German sixth with C♭ in the cellos (applied in the scratch copy)

**Problem.** The retransition turns the trombone's A minor (♭II of A♭) into V7 of A♭: on 64 b4 the bass E2 (= F♭, ♭6̂) carries Vla G♯3 (= A♭) and Vln II D4 (♯4̂), and at 65 every voice drops a semitone into E♭7. This is the enharmonic of A&S PDF 524: V7 of ♭II heard as the German sixth. But the chord has no fifth: E2 is tripled at the unison (Cb, Vc, B.tbn) plus Tbn 1's E3, and the C♭ that makes it a dominant seventh of A minor (and so the pun) is missing. A&S PDF 526 (Points 2a) doubles the tonic, not ♭6̂, in the Italian form. The cellos then leap a tritone, E2 → B♭2, into 65.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 524 §24 (German 6/5 = V7 of ♭II; the enharmonic link "connects two contrasting sections"); PDF 518 Ex. 29-30c–d (the applied °7 on 64 b3 and the augmented sixth after it are one family); PDF 526 Points 2b (the German sixth's fifths with the bass are tolerable when not between outer voices; here the outer voices are E2–D4 → E♭2–D♭4, a seventh to a seventh). c097 moved the cellos to D2–E2 so that they did not sound E3 under the trombone's F3; B2 does not do that either.

**Fix** (bSec.ily, bSecVc, bar 64):
```
a,2:32\p d,4( b,4) |
```
(was `a,2:32\p d,4( e,4) |`). retr.ily stays `bes,2.\pp`, so C♭3 → B♭2 is a semitone. Now 64 b4 sounds E2–B2–G♯3–D4 (F♭–C♭–A♭–D) and every voice moves by semitone into 65 (E→E♭ in Cb, C♭→B♭ in Vc, A♭→G in Vla, D→D♭ in Vln II). Cb and bass trombone keep the bass E2. Built in /tmp/critic-210/work: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt empty), 0 outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt, 8 pages; the parts log has the same three "decrescendo too small" warnings as v210. This answers c097's reason and keeps the protected F♭–E–E♭ bass and the quotation unchanged. Movement II's ending, themes and quotations are unchanged; only the description of bar 64 in coherence/mvt2.md and finale.md's "Neapolitan thread" note change (finding 3).

### 4. The augmented-sixth pun on A♭ runs through the symphony (recorded, no change)

Movement I's introduction has a German sixth on A♭ at its bar 12 (A♭–C–E♭–F♯ → V of C). Movement II's middle key D♭ is the Neapolitan of C, and its dominant A♭7 at 51–52 and 60 is, enharmonically, that same German sixth of C (A&S PDF 524: German 6/5 = V7 of ♭II). The finale states it as a German 4/3 at its 219 (D♯ in Hn 3 and violas, rising to E in a C-major 6/4), the form A&S PDF 526 Points 2d gives for a major goal. Movement II's own augmented sixths sit on F♭ = E (21, 64), and E is the scherzo's key; heard from E, II's last A♭ chord is III♯ (A&S PDF 523: an augmented sixth or III♯ that misleads, then the tonic). This is a coherent long-range design and the reason not to add a seventh to the A♭ chord at 31–36 (c020, c203): A♭7 belongs to the D♭ section and to the finale's return to C. **Fix.** None; one line in mvt2.md's key-plan notes.
