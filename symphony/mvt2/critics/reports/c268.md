## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 63, beat 1: the sforzando that opens the quotation is made of long notes only (applied)
**Problem.** At 63 the ff tutti has just broken off. The A-minor bar is attacked by long notes only: tremolo sf > p in Vln I, Vln II, Vla and Vc, and sustained sf > p in B.tbn and Cb. Nothing gives a short, hard attack. All of these notes start to fade at once, so the downbeat does not mark the turn to A minor clearly. That turn is the most remote harmonic step in the movement and the moment the quotation begins.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 189–190: "Often an accent is not sufficient to emphasize sforzando... string pizzicato combined with a longer note in the winds" (method 1), and "pizzicato note in the violas with the long notes in the violins" (method 4). Ex. XV-35 (image) pairs each short sf with long notes marked "sf > pp". Tchaikovsky's tutti (Ex. XI-21, PDF 39–40, images) also attacks every re-entry with short accented chords under the long notes. **Answer to c029's reason:** c028 suggested a short woodwind chord, and c029 rejected it because it would blur the trombone. This fix adds no instrument and puts nothing in the trombone's register (E3–C4). It only changes how the basses, who already play, attack the bar. With pizzicato, the bass line at 63 also stops sustaining A1 under the trombone, which leaves the solo clearer. The B.tbn (A2) and the cello tremolo (A2) still hold the bass. The sf > p tremolo under Tbn 1 (protected since c058 and c108) is unchanged.
**Fix (applied; bSec.ily, `bSecCb`, bars 63–64):**
```
was:  ... | a,,1\sf\> | a,,2\p d,4 e,4 |
now:  ... | a,,4\sf^\markup \italic "pizz." r4 r2 | a,,2\p^\markup \italic "arco" d,4 e,4 |
```
Pitches are unchanged and the 64 bass A–D–E (cycle 2) stays arco. The switch from the bowed F♭ at 62 to pizz. on the next downbeat is the usual quick change played with the bow in hand. There are three beats of rest (about 3 s at ♩ = 54–48) before "arco" at 64. Checked at 150 dpi: "pizz." and "arco" sit clear on the bass staff, and the system and page layout are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 53: the cor anglais's last note does not give way to the violins' entry (not applied; optional)
**Problem.** The quotation's tail ends on F4 (53 b1–2) at the p it reached at 52. Vln I and II enter at 52 b4, also p and "cantabile", and take F5 at 53 b1. For two beats the outgoing solo and the incoming theme are at the same level and an octave apart. Then the cor anglais simply stops.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 192, Ex. XV-40: when a line passes from one colour to another the seams should not show. The smooth version overlaps, with the outgoing part receding. This score already does the same elsewhere: Ob 1's mf C♯5 at 47 has a diminuendo under the cor anglais's entry, and the strings fade to niente at 3 and 66 under the next colour.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecCorAnglais`, bar 53):** `f'2\> r2\!`. This is a hairpin on the last note only. The quotation's notes, its single statement and the flat pp accompaniment under it (c124, c138) are unchanged. Low priority; I did not apply it.
