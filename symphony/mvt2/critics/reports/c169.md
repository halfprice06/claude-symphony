## Holistic judgement

A conductor who knows Elgar's Adagio would accept this movement as the work of a master in the same tradition. The two movements share five design points, and here each one is carried out with the same economy Adler asks for:

- **Entry from the previous movement.** Elgar's scherzo dissolves into the Adagio, and the slow theme is the scherzo theme slowed down. Here bars 1–6 continue movement I's last chord: C5 is held, E♮ falls to E♭, the timpani's soft roll fades and the wind chord takes over. This is the "accompaniment first, then the fresh horn" order of Weber's *Freischütz* (PDF 156–157).
- **Two-stranded second idea.** Elgar's second theme is "double-stranded". Here theme B at 53–60 runs with Hn 1's counter-line from theme A's head and becomes a three-layer texture at 57: melody, Hn/Vc counter-line, viola sextuplets. That is Adler's foreground/middleground/background (PDF 178–179).
- **Restraint with brass.** Elgar keeps his trombones for the last bars. This score keeps trombone 1 silent until the quotation at 63, keeps the trumpets tacet, and leaves trombones out of the A′ peak at 79. This is Adler's "holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event" (PDF 37) and Schumann's held-back trombones (PDF 162).
- **Hushed coda that recalls the motto.** Elgar's coda tune is "related to the motto theme" and ends with a muted-brass colour and a three-note clarinet farewell. Here Cl 1 at 85–88 plays the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C in augmentation over a tonic pedal. The muted horns 3·4 colour 87–90, and Cl 2 alone holds E♭4 under the strings to the end. The cyclic rule against a trombone chorale (reserved for movement III) rightly stops a closer imitation of Elgar's trombones.
- **Detailed marking.** Elgar's score is marked note by note. This score marks every entry, every hairpin, the bowings at the peaks, and the mutes and harmonics.

**Where it differs from the model.** Elgar's Adagio is about twice as long and changes tempo almost bar by bar. This movement has 92 bars (about 5¾ minutes) and a fixed tempo plan. Both points are design choices that are already closed (section proportions "confirmed, do not reopen" at cycle 23; tempo plan final at c153). I do not reopen them. The shorter span suits a symphony whose main slow-movement model is Dvořák 9/ii.

## Findings

No finding needs a change to the music. The four below record what I checked where the reading could have pointed to a fault, so later critics need not repeat the check.

### 1. Bars 61–64: the climax and its release follow ch. 11's model (no change)

The reduction of 61 b1 (Adler PDF 23: "make a piano reduction … to ascertain which pitches are the essential ones") shows the following layers. Bass D♭1 (Cbn), D♭2 (Cb, tuba) and D♭3 (B.tbn, B.cl., Bsn 2). Theme B's augmented head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ in Fl 2, Vln II, Bsn 1/Vc and Cl 2. Inner harmony in the horns and Tbn 2. One A♭6 in flute 1 above everything, which matches Beethoven's lone high flute octave (PDF 151–152). At 63 every wind stops except the bass trombone, and the strings hold an sf> tremolo under trombone 1. That is the *Lohengrin* model (PDF 35: doubling handed on, then a diminuendo to pp) and Schubert's scored diminuendo (PDF 28). Trombone 1 enters after a rest, as PDF 156 asks for a new colour. Keep as is.

### 2. Bars 61–62: horns 3·4 a2 against Schuller's warning (no change)

Schuller (PDF 26) warns that "high passage[s] at a loud dynamic" in unison horns crack, and advises horns 1 and 3 alone or one trumpet. Horns 3·4 play A♭4–B♭4–C♭5 (written E♭5–F5–G♭5) a2 at f for two bars. That is the middle-upper register at f, not the high ff register Schuller describes. The line moves by step from a held note and is doubled by Vln II/Vla above and Tbn 2 an octave below. Bartók's "two horns in unison equal one trombone" (PDF 44) also explains why the pair is needed against Tbn 2's mf. The brass layout at 61–62 is protected (c129, c154). No change. If a performance shows a problem, the fix is `\partCombineSoloI` for Hn 3 alone at 61–62 with Hn 4 resting, which keeps the page layout. Do not add a trumpet.

### 3. Bar 57: melody doublings at p beside harmony at mp (recorded, no change)

At 57 Fl 1 and Ob 1 join the theme at p<, while Cl 1·2 (harmony) and Hn 1 (counter-line) are mp and the violins are mf. Adler's first rule for full-orchestra chords is "the melody notes should be more prominent than the harmony notes" (PDF 159), and Schoenberg marks the principal voice a level above the subsidiary ones (PDF 184). Here, though, the violins already carry the melody at mf. The flute and oboe are doublings that enter "almost undetected" and grow with the crescendo, which c159 recorded from Adler part 1 PDF 238. Fl 1 at A♭5–A♭6 needs no extra level to be heard. The dynamics of the 53–61 build are protected (c158). No change.

### 4. Bars 79–81: the A′ peak against ch. 15's tutti-chord rules (no change)

The reduction of 79 b1 has D♭2 (Cb, Cbn), then D♭3 (B.cl., Bsn 2) and A♭3 (Hn 4). Above that: D♭4 (Vc, Hn 1·2, Bsn 1), F4 (Hn 3, Cl 2), B♭4 (Vla), F5 (Ob 2), B♭5 (Vln II, Ob 1, Cl 1), F6 (Fl 2) and B♭6 (Vln I, Fl 1). Only the lowest octave is open, which is the Mahler 1 and Brahms 3 layout (PDF 163–164). The melody is in three string octaves plus three winds. The counter-line mixes string, wood and brass with its own articulation (tenuto quarters), as in *Meistersinger* (PDF 178). The holding horns and woodwinds are the "adhesive" (PDF 154). The timpani roll f, one level under the tutti's ff, plays only the roots D♭ and E♭ (PDF 184). allpairs_par.py reports octaves at 80–81 between Hn 4 (A♭3→G3) and the melody. These are a brief doubling of the melody's A♭→G over V♭9 (c122, protected 80–81), not two independent voices. Keep as is.
