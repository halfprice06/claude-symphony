## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 42 b3–43 b1: the viola pizzicato doubles the cellos' tenor at the octave, so the leading tone is doubled and resolves in octaves. Applied.
- **Problem.** Bar 42 is the chain vi7–ii7–V7–I. The cellos' arco tenor line is D♭3–B♭2–C3–D♭3 | B♭2 (43). The violas' triplet middle notes are D♭4–D♭4–C4–D♭4 | B♭3. From 42 b3 to 43 b1 the two lines move C–D♭–B♭ together an octave apart. On the V7 (42 b3) the leading tone C is therefore in two parts (Vc C3, Vla C4), and both resolve to D♭ in octaves on the I at 42 b4. At 58 b3, the parallel place, the C is also in two parts (Cl 1 C5, Vla C4), but the violas move C4→C♭4 (the 7th of V7/IV) and only clarinet 1 goes to D♭, so no octaves arise there.
- **Reading.** A&S PDF 267 and the summary PDF 286 (2a): a doubled leading tone in a sequence is acceptable "as long as it does not appear before a goal tonic". PDF 267: "with any repetitive patterns where all the voices participate, 5ths and octaves can become a problem". PDF 266–267: in function 2 the sequence contains its goal, and here the I at 42 b4 is that goal.
- **Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bar 42 b3: `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' }` → `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 es' aes }`. Beat 3 now has the same A♭–x–A♭ shape as beat 4 (`aes8 des' aes`, c267). The cellos keep the only C. The V7 stays complete (A♭ in the basses and violas, C in the cellos, E♭ in the violas and Fl 1, G♭ in the cor anglais). The viola stays below the cor anglais's G♭4 (c221, c267), and no F sounds against the cor anglais's 4–3 suspension at b4. The middle strand E♭4→D♭4 moves against the cellos' C3→D♭3 in contrary motion. There are no new 5ths with the bass: the viola's lowest note stays on A♭3 while the bass rises A♭1→D♭2. I rejected E♭3–A♭3–E♭4 because it makes 5ths with the bass (E♭3/A♭1 → A♭3/D♭2). I also rejected F3 at b4, because it sounds the resolution note against the cor anglais's suspended G♭4.

### 2. Bars 24 b3 and 72 b3: leading tone doubled on the V7 that ends the motto-tail chain. Recorded, no change.
- **Problem.** At 24 b3 and 72 b3 (V7, the last chord before the chain's goal I at 25 and 73), G is in the cellos' counter-melody (G3→A♭3) and in the violas' upper voice (A♭4→G4→E♭4). The violas resolve the 7th of V7/V correctly (A♭→G), and then their G leaves downward. At bar 8, the model statement, the G is single (Bsn 1). This is the case PDF 267/286 (2a) excludes.
- **Why no change.** I checked every remedy and each costs more than it gains. Viola G4→A♭4 makes octaves with the cellos' G3→A♭3. Viola `<bes es'>` at b3 leaves the A♭ 7th unresolved. Dropping the 7th at b2 undoes c002's V7/V under every motto tail. Changing the cello line breaks the protected counter-melody (23–30, 71–82). In an inner voice, 7̂ may fall to 5̂ in V–I (normal practice), and the doubling comes from an independent melodic line, not from a chord spacing. That makes it milder than the four-voice case the reading describes. If the composer ever revises the viola at 24/72 for another reason, the clean option is `<bes f'>` at b2 → `<bes es'>` at b3, but only by giving up the 7th of V7/V.

### 3. Bars 36 b3 and 52 b4: leading tone in two parts before the D♭ arrivals at 37 and 53. Recorded, no change.
- **Problem.** At 36 b3 the only notes are C3 (B.cl., which resolves to D♭3) and C5 (cor anglais, theme B's pickup, which leaps to F5). At 52 b4 the violas hold C4 and the violins have the C5 pickup.
- **Why no change.** In both places one C is thematic (theme B's rising-fourth pickup, c071), and the other C resolves (36: B.cl.; 53: the harp's D♭4, the reason c122's Finding 3 was rejected). A&S PDF 267's concern is a chordal doubling that weakens the goal. A melodic anacrusis that leaps on into the theme does not do that. I tested nothing here.
