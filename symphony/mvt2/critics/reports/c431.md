## Findings

No finding below calls for a change to the score. The movement meets this reading's standard, and the remaining points are either protected for stated reasons I cannot answer or are not weaknesses once the bars are checked. They are ranked by how much they would matter if they were wrong.

### 1. Bars 32-34: the cor anglais moves a sixth above the bass clarinet's tail in the same rhythm (recorded, no change)

- **Problem.** At 33 the bass clarinet plays the motto tail E♭3-D3-D♭3 in quarters. Above it the cor anglais plays C4-C♭4-B♭3 in the same quarters: a major sixth, a diminished seventh (sounding as a sixth) and a major sixth. At 34 b1 it lands on A♭3 against C3, a minor sixth. That is four attacks in parallel. Sebesky's Ex. 279 (PDF 211) shows this texture: a background line that follows the soloist note for note "distorts the aural focus". The cor anglais is also the upper part, and the ear goes to the upper part first.
- **Mitigating evidence.**
  - Ex. 280's remedy (PDF 212) is to move where the soloist holds and hold where the soloist moves. The cor anglais already does this at 34, where it holds A♭3 while the solo moves C3-B♭2.
  - At 32 both voices hold.
  - The parallel motion lasts three beats, not a phrase.
  - The cor anglais is marked p dolce against the solo's mf, so the dynamic marks it as background (Sebesky PDF 219).
  - Dvořák answers his own Largo tune with clarinets a third apart in the same rhythm (corpus `clarinetImL`).
- **Why no fix.**
  - The harmony fixes the cor anglais' pitch on every beat. 33 b1 is I, 33 b2 is the common-tone diminished seventh D-F-A♭-C♭ (protected since c002), and 33 b3 is D♭-F-A♭-B♭.
  - The only way to break the rhythm without changing that harmony is to delay the line by one beat, as a suspension (`c'4 ces'2 | bes4 aes2`). I worked this out on paper and did not build it. It gives a minor seventh D♭3/C♭4 at 33 b3, then a minor seventh C3/B♭3 at 34 b1, so it replaces parallel sixths with parallel sevenths. That is worse.
  - Leaving the cor anglais out on 33 b3 leaves the C♭ unresolved.
  - The woodwind choir 31-36 is on the standing list (c111, c140).
- **For the record.** c090 told the coordinator that melody in thirds/sixths is "used once (41-44)". That is still correct. At 32-34 the cor anglais is an accompanying voice, not a melody in sixths, so the cyclic note needs no change.

### 2. Bars 85-90: the clarinet's coda line is in dotted halves moving by semitone, the shape of Sebesky's weak Ex. 278 (recorded, follows)

- **Problem considered.** Cl 1 plays E♭5 (85), D5 (86), D♭5 (87-88) and C5 (89-90), one note per bar, at pp. On paper this is the line Sebesky calls "much less interesting" (PDF 210, Ex. 278).
- **Why it passes.**
  - The line is the motto tail in augmentation. Sebesky's own reasons apply: a recurring figure unifies the score (PDF 206), and an idea from the end of a phrase can become the ending (PDF 207). The listener has heard this figure at 8, 24, 33, 68 and 72, so the long values give it a new meaning rather than taking its character away.
  - The motion PDF 207-208 asks for is in the inner voices: C.a. F4-F♭4-E♭4 at 87-89 and violas F3/A♭3-F♭3/A♭3 at 87-88 move under the held notes (Ex. 271).
  - The passage is protected (plagal close 87-92, clarinet hand-over at 85-92, c416, c430).

### 4. Theme A and theme B as tunes (recorded)

- **Theme A (7-14, 23-30, 71-82).** It has one rhythmic cell (4. 8 4~), a recurring chromatic tail, a single dissonant peak in bar 6 of 8, and stepwise motion apart from the opening arpeggio. This is the profile of the Dvořák Largo tune (corpus `oboeIImL`) and of the Tchaikovsky and Brahms slow-movement tunes the brief names: a short repeated cell, a narrow range and one high point. A listener can sing it after one hearing. The A′ climax restates 76-78 an octave higher with the B♭6 held for a bar and a half. That is Sebesky's colour on the long held note at the end of the long section (PDF 223, "prayer" in Ex. 286).
- **Theme B (37-44, 53-60, 61-62).** It is built on its own neighbour-note cell (F-G♭-F-E♭) and its second phrase rises by a fourth. That keeps it distinct from theme A (PDF 206: a figure that "offsets" the tune). It is weaker as a tune than theme A, which is right for a middle section on the Dvořák 9/ii model (c401). Its augmentation at 61-62 is the B-section climax. No change; c131, c291 and c401 closed this.
