## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Cellos 57–60: one 14-beat phrase slur with a crescendo to ff (applied)
- **Problem.** Since cycle 17 the cellos double horn 1's counter-line and copy its phrase slur: `f2\< ges2( | f4 es2 f4 | ges2\f es2 | es4\< f4 ges2)`. That is 14 beats in one bow at ♩≈61–64 (about 13 seconds), rising from mf to f and then crescendo into ff at 61. Adler forbids phrase slurs in string parts (PDF 38). He also warns that the cello bow is shorter (PDF 39). His Liszt example (PDF 39–40, Ex. II-23) is this exact case, a crescendo under a long slur, which he calls "impossible to perform as the composer has marked". The players will rebow it anyway, each in a different place. The slur also hides the bowing that gives the crescendo its direction.
- **Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVc`).** `f2\< ges2(^\markup \italic "espr." | f4) es2( f4) | ges2\f es2 | es4(\< f4 ges2) |`. Counting bows: f D, ges–f U, es–f D, ges U, es D, then es–f–ges U with the crescendo (an up-bow crescendo, PDF 38), so the ff A♭ at 61 starts on a down-bow (PDF 38, observation 2). The horn keeps its phrase slur.

### 2. Violins 79–80: the ff peak is held on one bow for over four beats (applied)
- **Problem.** The grace run is slurred into `bes'''2.)~\ff | bes'''4 aes'''2`. Grace notes, B♭6 for three beats and the tied quarter all fall on one down-bow at ♩=48, about 5 seconds at ff on the E string. Vln II has the same at B♭5. The tone will thin out at the peak of the movement. Adler (PDF 38, Ex. II-20) says to mark "a down-bow and up-bow … over the long note so that the following attack will be solid". Dvořák marks his tied whole notes the same way.
- **Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoVlnOne`, `aTwoVlnTwo`).** `bes'''2.)~\ff | bes'''4(\upbow aes'''2) |` (and an octave lower for Vln II). The bow changes on the tie at 80, and the A♭ is slurred onto that up-bow. The diminuendo at 81 then falls on a down-bow (PDF 38), and the p A♭ at 82 is an up-bow ending. The pitches and the slur from the grace run are unchanged.

### 3. Vln I and cellos 45–46: theme B head slurred over two slow bars (applied)
- **Problem.** `des''4.(\p es''8 des''4 c''4 | bes'4 aes'8 bes'8 des''2)` puts 8 beats at ♩=60 into one bow, and the cellos double it in octaves. Adler, PDF 39: "Even if the dynamic is soft, special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow… the cello and bass bows are a bit shorter". The same head at 53–54 is already bowed one bar per bow (`f''4.( ges''8 f''4 es''4) | des''4( c''8 des''8 es''2)`). So the score bows the same idea two ways eight bars apart. Dvořák's Largo violin theme is slurred one bar per bow.
- **Fix (bSec.ily).** Vln I: `des''4.(\p es''8 des''4 c''4) | bes'4( aes'8 bes'8 des''2) |`. Cellos: `des'4.(\p^"arco" es'8 des'4 c'4) | bes4( aes8 bes8 des'2) |`.

### 4. Violas 71: no "ord." after "sul C" (applied)
- **Problem.** At 65 the violas are marked "unis., sul C", and at 71 the eighth-note figure reaches A♭4 with no instruction to leave the C string. Adler, PDF 53: "Every time a special effect … is used, one must insert the indication naturale or in modo ordinario … where the player should resume". Every other string colour change in this score (sul D at 22, sul tasto at 37, sul A at 53) is cancelled with "ord.".
- **Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoVla`).** `^\markup \italic "unis., ord., espr."`.

### 5. Vln I 1–3: nine beats of held C5 at ppp on one bow (applied)
- **Problem.** `c''2.~\ppp | c''2.~ | c''2.\>`: about 10.4 seconds at ♩=52, with a diminuendo, under a tie with no bowing instruction. A whole section of first violins cannot all hold this quietly and evenly in one bow. Adler's remedy is to divide the section and stagger the bowing (PDF 40, Ex. II-25). This score already prints that instruction, "change bow at will", on its other long holds (31–36, 65–70, 83–92), but not on its very first note.
- **Fix (intro.ily, `introVlnOne`).** `c''2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "change bow at will"`. This adds a text only. The notes, dynamics and common-tone link of bars 1–2 are unchanged.

### 6. Vln II 15–22: repeated chord tones on separate bows; louré is the idiomatic stroke (applied, optional)
- **Problem.** `aes'2\pp aes'4 | aes'2 g'4 | …` takes every note on its own bow under the oboe–violin dialogue. That is correct (PDF 37), but the re-attacks on beat 3 are neutral. Adler presents louré as "a very expressive bowing used quite often in accompaniments" (PDF 42; Handel, Ex. II-30, PDF 43). Dvořák uses it in the Largo's accompanying strings (`f8(-- ef)--`, cellos `df8--`).
- **Fix (aOne.ily, `aOneVlnTwo`).** `aes'2--(\pp aes'4--) | aes'2--( g'4--) | c''2--( aes'4--) | g'2--( f'4--) | aes'2--( g'4--) | aes'2--( g'4--) | f'2--( f'4--) | bes2 …`. That is one bow per bar, with a soft pulse on beat 3 that stays below the dialogue. If the composer wants the plainest possible background here, this one can be dropped.
