## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 59: make the ♯4̂ chord a vii°7/V by mixture (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 59, beats 3–4, into 60.

**Problem:** In bar 59 the bass sits on ♯4̂ and carries a plain V6/5/V. Then bar 60 reaches V of A♭.

- A&S call vii°7 over ♯4 "one of the most important intensifications of V" (PDF 571).
- Mixture sevenths "intensify motion toward a goal" (PDF 537, §6).
- The movement's next big event is the double-mixture move to A minor through F♭ = E at 62. A&S say a remote mixture chord is best prepared by "association with an earlier plain use of the altered scale degree" (PDF 537).

At present, F♭ first sounds in this section at 62, unprepared.

**Evidence:** At 59 beat 3 the beat dump gives G–B♭–D♭–E♭. The neighbour figure E♭–F♭–E♭ is missing.

**Fix:** On beat 4 the E♭ moves to F♭ as a neighbour. That makes G–B♭–D♭–F♭, a vii°7/V by mixture, and the F♭ resolves to E♭ at 60. The edits in `sections/bSec.ily`:

```
Bn1: ges2\f es2 | es1\< \breathe |      ->  ges2\f es4 fes4 | es1\< \breathe |
Hn1: ges'2(\f es'2 | es'4\< f'4 ...      ->  ges'2(\f es'4 fes'4 | es'4\< f'4 ...
Vc:  ges2\f es2 | es4(\< f4 ...          ->  ges2(\f es4 fes4) | es4(\< f4 ...
Va (2nd sextuplet group, bar 59):
  bes16( des' es' des' bes des' es' des' bes des' es' des')
  -> bes16( des' es' des' bes des' fes' des' bes des' fes' des')
```

Why the change lands on beat 4 rather than beat 3: the flute line has F6 as an eighth at 59, beat 2½. Waiting until beat 4 keeps the F/F♭ cross relation away from the top voice (A&S PDF 420).

What stays intact:

- the protected ♯4̂ bass;
- the dynamics and hairpins;
- the slur pairing. In the cello, the new slur is opened and closed inside bar 59.

The new F♭ is in inner voices only, so the outer voices are unchanged. This does not undo any earlier fix. It keeps the existing V6/5/V on beat 3.

### 2. Bars 22→23: parallel fifths between the upper cellos and the basses (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 22, beat 3 → 23, beat 1.

**Problem:** The divisi upper cellos move B♭3→E♭3 while the basses move E♭2→A♭1. That is a P5 to a P5 in similar motion (A&S Unit 5). It is clearly audible, because the upper cellos are the exposed answering line.

**Evidence:** My all-ranks scan flagged 23.1. allpairs_par.py does not see it, because both lines share a stave.

**Fix:** In `sections/aOne.ily`, bar 22, upper cellos:

```
<< { g8(^\markup \italic "div." aes8 g4 bes4) } \\ { es2. } >>
-> << { g8(^\markup \italic "div." aes8 bes4 g4) } \\ { es2. } >>
```

The line now ends on G3 and falls a 3rd to E♭3 against the bass fifth. After the change the scan is clean at 23.1. This changes the contour of the cello answer at 22, which c067 added. The rhythm, the slur and the motive's first three notes are kept, so the answer is still recognisable.

### 3. Harp right hand at bars 11 and 75: held chord over the new bass (APPLIED)

**Bars:** 11, beats 2–3; 75, beats 2–3.

**Problem:** The harp RH plays a half-note `<c es>` (at 75, `<aes c' es'>`) from beat 2. The bass moves to D♭ on beat 3. The held C then sounds as an unprepared major 7th against D♭, on a weak beat. At 11 it also rubs against the BCl D♭3. The held-dissonance scanner flags both places. Neither note comes from a suspension figure, so it is a leftover sonority, not a dissonance with a treatment.

**Fix:** In `sections/aOne.ily` bar 11, `r4 <c es>2\arpeggio` → `r4 <c es>4\arpeggio r4`. In `sections/aTwo.ily` bar 75, `r4 <aes c' es'>2\arpeggio` → `r4 <aes c' es'>4\arpeggio r4`.

The arpeggio stays and its length is unchanged. This touches the harp voicing that c019 and c057 settled, but only the note length; the pitches and registers are unchanged.

### Note A (not applied): spelling at bar 62

A&S prefer spelling chromatic chords for their function at the point of arrival. Examples are D♭ → C♯ as a leading tone (PDF 564) and °7s spelled toward their goal (PDF 570). In major-third cycles, ♭6 is respelled as ♯5 (PDF 583). By that logic, bar 62, beat 3 might read E–G♯–B instead of F♭–A♭–C♭.

I am not changing it, for three reasons:

- The F♭/E–E♭ thread at 62–65 is protected.
- The flute holds A♭6 across the change.
- A&S Ex. 31-27b allows spellings that help the reader.

I note it for the record only.

### Note B (not applied): the common-tone °7 at bar 33

In the common-tone °7 at bar 33, B♮ and D fall to B♭ and D♭. They do not rise as neighbours (♯2→3, ♯4→5; A&S PDF 543–544). The chord still works as a chromatic passing chord into ii4/2 under the motto, and HISTORY protects it. I propose no change.
