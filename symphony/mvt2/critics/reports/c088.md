## Findings

**1. Bar 67: the Hn 1 solo has no character word.** (applied)
Problem: The retransition solo, the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C–B♭ over the timpani's ppp E♭, is the only exposed horn solo in the movement with no expressive text. Bar 7 has "dolce espr." and bar 53 has "espr.". At 67 the player sees only "1. solo" and `p<mp>pp`.
Evidence: All of Adler's soft horn solos pair the dynamic with a character word: Beethoven 9/iii "p dolce" (PDF 305, Ex. X-9), Ravel "pp ... expressif" (PDF 305, Ex. X-11), Humperdinck "sehr weich" (PDF 306, Ex. X-16), Tchaikovsky 5/ii "dolce con molto espr." (PDF 309, Ex. X-24). The Beethoven case, a slow-movement horn solo marked p, is the closest model.
Fix (retr.ily, retrHnOne): `es'2.(\p\<^\markup \bold "1. solo"^\markup \italic "dolce"`. The word appears in the part too. The layout is unchanged.

**2. Bar 78 b3: Hn 1·2 (and Bsn 1 in its part) enter p inside an f tutti.** (applied)
Problem: At 78 b1 every other part reaches f with the poco allargando: strings, flute, clarinets, cellos, basses and Bsn 2. Hn 1·2 a2 then enter on beat 3 at p< to reach f one beat later (1.2 s at ♩=50). A p entry two levels below the texture will not be heard, so the counter-melody seems to start at 79 with no upbeat. Bsn 1 has the same p in its part.
Evidence: In Adler's horn examples the dynamic of an entry matches the passage around it (Weber, PDF 306, Ex. X-15, mf with mf; Strauss *Till*, PDF 311, Ex. X-29, f cresc. in all four horns). PDF 311 also notes that the horn has enough carrying power in loud passages to be heard over almost any combination, so it needs no p to stay in the background. It is the low marking that hides it.
Fix (aTwo.ily): in aTwoHnOne and aTwoHnTwo, `r2 c'4(\mf\< | des'4\f`. In aTwoBsnOne (part tag), `r2 c'4(-\tag #'part \mf -\tag #'part \< |`. Hn 2's crescendo to mf at 77 (c035) is unchanged. Only the p of the new entry changes.

**3. Bars 81 and 90: Hn 3·4 diminuendos end on rests with no level.** (applied)
Problem: At 81 (Hn 3 F♭4→E♭4, Hn 4 G3) and at 90 (muted A♭3/E♭3) the hairpins run into rests, so their end level is unstated. The intent, horns leaving first and dying away (c024, c008), is to fade to nothing. The clarinet's niente start at 85 already uses a circled tip.
Evidence: Adler PDF 307 (Mahler 4, X-18, `ff>p`), 309 (Tchaikovsky, `>p`) and 320 (Mahler 1, muted trumpets `>pp`): every hairpin ends on a stated level.
Fix (aTwo.ily, coda.ily): put `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t` before `fes'4\>` and `g2.\>` (81) and before `aes2\>` and `es2\>` (90). The shared staff has one equal hairpin, and the build shows the niente circles.

**4. Movement III: horns 3·4 finish movement II muted.** (not applied; for the coordinator)
Problem: Hn 3·4 go con sord. at 87 and never remove the mutes, which is correct inside movement II. Movement III's horn 3·4 parts must open with "senza sord." (or "open"), all the more if the movements run attacca.
Evidence: Adler PDF 308: "As soon as the mute is to be removed, either the term senza sordino or open must appear". PDF 321 says the same for trumpets.
Fix: in movement III, put `^\markup \italic "senza sord."` on the first Hn 3 and Hn 4 entries, and add a "muta: senza sord." note to the last bar of movement II's horn parts if movement III follows attacca.

**5. Bar 61: Hn 2's first note of the movement is a cold f entry at written C5.** (not applied; parts only)
Problem: Hn 2, a low horn, rests from bar 1 to 60 and then enters f on F4 (written C5) and rises to A♭4 (written E♭5) at the Largamente. Its part has no cue, so the player must find a written C5 at f from silence.
Evidence: Adler PDF 304: the horn player "must 'hear' and then 'find' each note in his mind and in his embouchure"; PDF 301 and 303: the second horn is the low horn. The pitches stay as they are because the 61–62 layout is protected.
Fix: put a part-only cue in bSecHnTwo for 59–60 from Hn 1 (G♭4–F4, which sits right at Hn 2's entry pitch), using the existing pattern `\tag #'part { \cueDuring #"hornOneQ" #UP { <>^\markup \tiny "Hn. 1" R1*2 } }`, with an `\addQuote` for horn 1. This also covers part of the open "more cues" item.

**6. Bars 59–61: the shared horn staff reads f < f.** (not applied; recorded)
Hn 1 is f at 59 and crescendos through 60. Its hairpin ends on Hn 2's "2." f at 61, so the page shows a crescendo from f to f. This is a side effect of the part-combine. A conductor reads it as Hn 1 into the tutti ff and Hn 2 joining at f, which is the intended effect. I recommend no change unless a later engraving pass separates the dynamics.
