## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The movement meets every principle in the unit that applies to a major-key slow movement with a IV stopover. Below is new evidence for what the reading teaches, ranked by how much it bears on the form, and one warning.

### 1. The stopover plan and its timing (no change)

**Check.** The reading's large-scale model is I – stopover – V – I, with the stopover "almost midway" and confirmed by a strong cadence before it moves to V (PDF 231–233; Review 1, 3). My time table from score.midi (total 345.3 s) gives: theme A at 6.0 %, transition 30.1 %, B 36.3 %, F♯-minor point 49.1 %, D♭ return 55.5 %, climax 64.6 %, V7 at 65 = 70.0 %, A′ 76.0 %, A′ peak 84.1 %, coda 88.6 %. These match c323, c433 and c463 to the decimal, so nothing since v463 has moved the proportions. The D♭ area is confirmed twice, quietly at 52→53 (V7–I, bass A♭1→D♭3) and fully at 60→61 (V–I ff, bass A♭1→D♭1), and only then leaves through the 15-2 route to a root-position V7 at 65. This is the reading's order: arrive, confirm, move on by step to V.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bars 50–51 are the reading's "natural VII → V7", with the inflection in one voice (no change; new detail)

**Check.** c463 named 50→51 as natural VII → V7 (15-14 to 15-16). The per-instrument dump adds why it sounds clean. The C♭ (written B) and the C♮ that replaces it are in the same voice: cellos B2 (50 b3) → C3 (51 b1). The basses fall B1 → A♭1, and the top voice (Vln II) moves D5 → E♭5. So there is no cross-relation at all, neither the harsh bass–soprano kind of 15-8c nor the acceptable inner-voice kind of 15-8d (PDF 236). The reading says the inflection of 7̂ "can produce a particularly expressive effect" (PDF 241), and here it marks the return from the F♯-minor quotation to the dominant of D♭. The cor anglais's G♭4 at 51 b3, the seventh, enters by step from A♭4. That is 15-15's "4̂ prepares the seventh of V7" (PDF 242) at the D♭ level.

**Fix.** None. Do not respell 50 b3–4 as B minor in sharps in the cellos: the C♭–C line is what the reading describes.

### 3. Corpus support for the passing iii6 at 78 b3 (no change)

**Check.** c323 and c463 justified 78 b3 (iii6, bass E♭1, between ♭VI on E2 and IV on D♭1) by 15-10c/d and 15-12. The Mozart 41/ii annotation has the same device in the same metre: bar 15 (onsets 42.5–48) runs IV6 – III6/iii6 – II6 – I6 – vii°6 in a falling bass, with iii6 on a weak half-beat as a passing 6/3. Its only other III (onset 109, in C) is a passing chord too, approached by its own dominant (v/iii) and left at once for IV. In both places Mozart uses III exactly as PDF 237 says it is mostly used, "less like an independent chord than like an offshoot", inside a falling line. This score does the same: the dump of bar 78 shows Cl 2 A♭4 (b1) – G4 (b3) and F4 at 79, the 8–7–6 that PDF 239 gives as the reason to prefer III over I6.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Rising thirds into strong beats: no new instance (record)

**Check.** I scanned every downbeat in the 3/4 sections and every beat 1 and 3 in 37–64 for root motion up a third (PDF 231, 240: such motion "tends to contradict the meter" from weak to strong). The 3/4 sections have none. In the B section the scan finds 40→41 and 57→58 again; c463 found 41→42 by hand; 56→57 is in c173's list. My other hits (52 b2→b3, 55→56, 59→60, 60 b2→b3) are false: each one is a non-chord tone over the A♭ or A pedal, or the vii°6 of a C7 read as E♭ diminished. So the list stays at the four recorded instances, each with the reasons c173 and c463 gave (bass leap and melodic arrival on the downbeat, and the start of a chain of fifths, 15-9).

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Phrase grouping against Exercise 2 (record)

**Check.** Exercise 2 (PDF 245–246) asks for phrases "whose inner groupings are not all in twos and fours" and gives a 3+3+4 sample whose middle group ends on tonicized III. The score has one asymmetric group, and it is in the right place: B2 (45–52) runs 3+3+2. The 3-bar group 45–47 reaches the C♯7, the next 3-bar group 48–50 holds the quotation in F♯ minor, and the 2-bar group 51–52 is the V7 of D♭. All other sections are in fours. One irregular group at the remote point of the middle section is what the reading's sample does with its tonicized III: it irregularises the group that leaves the tonic.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Warning: do not use PDF 231 to "fill in" the static frames (record)

**Check.** PDF 231 says that when I–III–V frames a section, "intervening chords usually lead from I to III and from III to V". A later critic could read that as a case against the four single-chord frames (1–6, 31–36, 65–70, 83–92, about 21 s each, 92 s in all, 27 % of the time). It is not. The sentence is about the framework chords of a progression. The frames here are prolongations: the reading itself treats a motion that keeps the common tones as "an expansion" of one harmony (PDF 231, 237). Each frame also has a cyclic job (the link from movement I, the bass-clarinet solo, the unaccompanied horn tail, the plagal close). c423 and c463 settled their length.

**Fix.** None.
