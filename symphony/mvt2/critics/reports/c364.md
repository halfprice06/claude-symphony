## Findings

### 1. Bar 60, beats 1-2: the dominant has no third below C5. Give horn 4 C4 instead of A♭3 (APPLIED)

**Problem.** Bar 60 is the V of D♭ that leads into the climax at 61. The low brass enter pp< and horn 4 enters p<. On beats 1-2, every instrument below C5 plays A♭ or E♭: Cb and tuba A♭1, B.cl., Bsn 2, B.tbn and timpani A♭2, Bsn 1, Vc and Tbn 2 E♭3, violas and horn 4 A♭3, horn 1 E♭4, Cl 2 A♭4. The third C sounds only at C5 (Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II) and C6 (Fl 1, Vln I). The brass enter on a bare A♭-E♭ sonority with four A♭s, and the seventh G♭3 only arrives on beat 3.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 50 and Ex. 53 say that thirds and dominant sevenths belong in the lower-middle register, "where they are most resonant, thereby exerting their influence over the entire ensemble voicing". PDF 42 says each section should be complete in itself. PDF 44 says low fifths without a third reduce vibrancy. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 289 (Ex. 115) says the brass enter p with a complete chord; c119 cited this when it added the seventh on beat 3, but the third was never supplied. Horn 4 is the one brass part that can take the third without upsetting the protected decisions: c132's E♭3 in trombone 2, the c349 pp< low-brass dynamic and the Hn 3·4 a2 at 61-62 all stay.

**Fix** (bSec.ily, `bSecHnFour`, bar 60):
```
  c'1\p\< \breathe |
```
(was `aes1\p\< \breathe |`). With this, the brass on beat 3 sound A♭1, A♭2, G♭3, C4 and E♭4/G♭4. The third and the seventh form a tritone in the lower-middle register, as Ex. 53 shows. Horn 4 then rises C4→A♭4 into the a2 at 61. That is a minor sixth, smaller than the octave A♭3→A♭4 that c326 called the only wide leap in the horns. The leading tone moves on to D♭4 in horn 2 and the violas at 61, in the same octave. C4 is written G4 for horn in F, so the note is in a comfortable range. Horn 3 rests at 60, so the shared staff has one dynamic. Vln II moves C5→A♭4 at the same moment, which makes an octave going to a unison, but in contrary motion. Horns 3·4 already double Vln II's A♭4 at 61, so the move adds no new doubling.

The only change in analysis.txt is horn 3·4's range line. The allpairs_par.py output is identical to v364's. The parts book was regenerated at 30 pages, with each part except the harp on one page.

### 2. Bar 80: three horns on A♭3 and no D♭ in the horns. Recorded, no change

At 80 b1-2, Hn 1·2 (counter-melody) and Hn 4 all sound A♭3, and Hn 3 sounds F4. The ii7's third D♭ is in Cl 2 and the strings but not in the horns. Taken alone, principle 6 (PDF 42) argues against this. However, Sebesky allows a momentary unison when voice leading needs it (PDF 44, Ex. 50, trumpets 1 and 2 at bar 3). Hn 4's tied A♭3 is the prepared seventh of the iiø7 at 80 b3 and resolves to G3 in the protected V♭9 at 81. At 81 the horns sound G3-B♭3-D♭4-F♭4, a complete section voicing. Leave it.

### 4. Bar 62: tuba G♭1 and F♭1 (E1) at the bottom of the range. Recorded, no change

Sebesky's Ex. 44 (PDF 39) puts these notes below the F tuba's normal compass and at the lowest normal note of the BB♭ tuba. No marking is needed.
