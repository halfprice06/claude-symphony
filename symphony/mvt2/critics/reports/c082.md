## Findings

### 1. Bar 81 b1: the climax cadence has no dominant ninth, and the F♭ leaves one beat early (applied)

**Problem.** At the A′ peak the progression is IV(add 6) 79–80 → ii°7 by mixture at 80 b3 (B♭–D♭–F♭–A♭; Hn 3 F♭4) → V7 at 81 → I at 82. Hn 3 moves F♭4→E♭4 on the barline, so V arrives as a plain triad and the 7th D♭ comes in on b2. No dominant in the movement carries a 9th. A&S say the dominant "more than any other tends to attract dissonant formations", and that the added 9th "helps to create a rich sonority … attractive to many composers—especially those of the nineteenth century" (PDF 466). V⁹♭ "occurs readily in major" through mixture (PDF 464, Ex. 27-3). When the chord before V already holds 6̂, as II does here, the 9th can enter as a common tone, a suspension when accented (PDF 465, Ex. 27-4c), and resolve to the octave inside V (PDF 465–466, Ex. 27-5). Here 6̂ is F♭, the pitch of the movement's F♭ thread.

**Evidence.** MIDI at 80 b3: Bb1–Bb2–Bb3, Hn 3 E4 (= F♭4), A♭3–A♭6. At 81 b1: Eb2–Eb3, G3, B♭3, Hn 3 E♭4, G4–G6. Dvořák 9/ii bars 10 and 104 put the 9th on the theme's own cadence.

**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily):**
```
aTwoHnThree = { R2.*8 | f'2.\f | f'2 fes'4~ | fes'4\> es'2 | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | }
```
At 81 b1 the chord is E♭–G–B♭ + F♭ (V♭9). G3 (Hn 4) and F♭4 (Hn 3) form the diminished 7th at the core of the chord (PDF 470, Ex. 27-11). F♭ resolves to E♭ on b2, at the moment the 7th D♭ enters in Bsn 1, Hn 1·2 and Vc. That is the 9–8-inside-V of Ex. 27-5 followed by the 7th. The F♭ still resolves to E♭, which keeps the c070 rule. Top line, octaves, dynamics, hemiola and release are unchanged, so the protected climax and staged release 79–83 are kept. Two side effects: the 9th lies below the 3rd (Vla/Cl 2 G4), which is allowed for an inner-voice 9th that resolves (PDF 463, 467–468); and it resolves onto the violas' E♭4 (G4→E♭4), so the inner voices reach a unison by step. allpairs_par.py now reports Hn 4 A♭3–G3 against the theme's A♭–G at 80→81. That octave doubling was there before. The part-combiner used to merge horns 3 and 4 at 81, which hid it from the checker.

### 2. Bar 58 b4: the counter-line sounds the resolution under theme B's 4–3 suspension (applied)

**Problem.** The 4–3 suspension in theme B (c061: G♭ held over D♭7, resolving to F on b4.5) is clean at 42: at 42 b4 no other voice has F. At 58 b4, Hn 1 (F4) and the cellos (F3) play F on the beat while the flute, oboe and violins still hold G♭5/G♭6. A&S: suspensions work best "if the tone of resolution is not anticipated in a voice other than the bass". With an added dissonance that sounds against its own resolution, "the result is usually an unclear sonority and an ineffective resolution" (PDF 469–470, citing Unit 21 §12). At 58 the theme is in octaves at mf–f, so the clash is heard.

**Evidence.** MIDI at 58 b4: Db2, F3 (Vc), A♭3, F4 (Hn 1), C♭/B4, D♭5, G♭5, G♭6. The F5/F6 resolution comes half a beat later.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily):** in Hn 1 and Vc, A♭ on 58 b4 instead of F (`f'4 es'2 aes'4)` and `f4) es2( aes4)`). A♭ is the 5th of D♭7 and steps down to the G♭ of 59 against the melody's F–E♭ (6ths), in contrary motion to the bass D♭–G♭. The line now starts 59 with the A♭–G♭ that opens bar 57.

### 3. Bar 60 b4: the 6th over V7 sits in an inner voice under the 7th (applied, reduced)

**Problem.** Over the A♭ pedal (V7 of D♭), the horn and cello counter-line E♭–F–G♭–F ends on F on b4, a full beat. Meanwhile the violas' figure keeps G♭3/G♭4, the 7th, which resolves to F only at 61. The F does not continue into 61 (Vc leaps to A♭3; Hn 1 rests), so it is an unresolved 6th ("13th") over V7, in an inner voice and below the 7th. A&S: such a tone has to be in a prominent part, "almost always the soprano … above rather than below the 7th"; otherwise "it will create a mess rather than the expressive effect the composer intended" (PDF 475). Left unresolved, a 4th or 6th sounds like an anticipation of the next chord tone (PDF 474, Ex. 27-17). This F works only as an anticipation, so it should be short.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily):** `es'4\< f'4 ges'4. f'8)` in Hn 1 and `es4(\< f4 ges4. f8)` in Vc. The 7th now resolves early as an eighth-note anticipation of D♭'s 3rd, and the clash with the violas' G♭ lasts an eighth, not a beat. The fuller remedy is to give the anticipation to a top voice, but the top here is the held C6 of the theme.
