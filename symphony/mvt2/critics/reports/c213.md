## Findings

I found nothing in v213 that the reading says is wrong. Every principle that applies is followed, and the three places that depart from a rule (35–36, 83, 62→63) are the exceptions that the reading itself allows, or are protected with reasons that still hold. I recommend no change. I have not changed the scratch copy, so there is no build to report. The points below are what I checked, ranked by how close each came to a recommendation.

### 1. Bars 35–36: the texture sits wholly in the low register (checked, protected, no change)

At 35 and 36 b1–2 the only sounds are Cbn A♭1, B.cl. A♭2 and Bsn 1 E♭3, a bare fifth that lies entirely below E♭3. At 36 b3 the C.a. pickup C5 sounds over B.cl. C3 with nothing in between. PDF 124–125 says a texture centred in the lowest octaves is rare and "dark and gloomy", and that widely separated outer parts need filling. This adds a reason from range to c184's spacing argument. But the case is local: it lasts about 7 s under the poco rit., and Rimsky's rule concerns the "entire orchestral conception", not a two-bar join. c184's stated reason also still holds. A C in the cor anglais would sound the pickup C before the pickup, and theme B's first C–F would lose its fresh start. Any other added C would be a new voice in the protected woodwind choir (c020, c203). The dark two bars also work in the form: they make the D♭ entry at 37 (flute, violas, harp, strings sul tasto) the brightest point so far in the movement, which is the "simple to complex timbre, suddenly" of PDF 127. Leave it.

### 2. Bar 83: two empty octaves between the harp harmonics and the pedal (checked, protected, no change)

gaps.py finds the movement's only wide register gap here: harp harmonics A♭4–C5–E♭5 over Vc A♭2 and Cb A♭1, with nothing between A♭2 and A♭4 for three beats. PDF 125 allows this "unusual resonance" for a special colour. Rimsky's own example (Ex. 235, PDF 405) leaves four octaves open and partly bridges them with two flutes. Here the gap is two octaves, ppp, and the violas fill it at 84 b3 (C4–E♭4), right after the timpani's answer, which c124 wanted heard alone. c150 protected it. No change.

### 3. Bars 62 → 63: a false relation across groups (checked, no change)

At 62 b3–4 clarinet 2 holds C♭5 (with Tbn 2 C♭4 and Hn 3·4). At 63 Vln II plays C6 in tremolo. The chromatic C♭–C moves both octave and group, which PDF 126–127 warns against for chromatic progressions. The note on PDF 127 lifts the rule "when extreme contrast of timbre between two adjacent chords is intended", and 62 → 63 is the movement's sharpest change of colour: the full wind and brass chord stops, and the quotation in the solo trombone enters over a string tremolo. The trombone's own E3–C4 also states the C at once in the brass, and the other chromatic voice, G♯ (A♭6) → A6, stays in Vln I (c052, c070). No change.

### 4. Dvořák 9/ii's soli reprise against this A′ (comparison, not proposed)

Dvořák uses the solo-string technique of PDF 123–124 to break his reprise into diminishing soli (4 → 2 → 1, then tutti; strings.notesL.ily lines 111–124). This movement's reprise does the opposite: it re-scores theme A in octaves and builds to the second ff at 79. It uses soli elsewhere instead: the solo violin at 17–22 and the four solo basses at 65–70. Both are good models. Changing to Dvořák's plan would reopen the A′ proportions, which were closed at c023 ("section proportions ... do not reopen") and recorded again at c173 and c193, and would weaken the two-peak plan. I record the comparison only.

### 5. Bar 43 b1: a plucked third in the bass register (checked, no change)

low.py finds one close interval below C3 that is not part of the harmonic series. The harp's upper G♭2 (LH octave G♭1–G♭2) sits a major third under the cellos' B♭2. It lasts one plucked beat at pp under the C.a. phrase, and the octave gives the harp's bass the body it needs. PDF 124's warning about close harmonic intervals in the lowest octaves concerns sustained chords. No change.

### Comment on the form

The form follows the reading's plan for intensity closely. The register rises across the statements of theme A (horn A♭3 at 7 → violins A♭4 at 23 → violins A♭5/A♭4 at 71 → B♭6 at 79). Both builds add new groups at a lower dynamic that then catch up (57–61, 75–79; PDF 127). Both releases remove groups (63–67, 80–83; PDF 127). Every hand-over of a phrase between instruments has a common note (PDF 125–126). The only wide register gap is a deliberate colour (83). In proportion (intro 6, A 24, transition 6, B 28, retransition 6, A′ 12, coda 10 bars; about 5¾ minutes), the movement matches Dvořák 9/ii's short reprise after a climax with a quotation. I have no reason to reopen it.

## Do not touch

The phrase joins at 22, 36–37, 47, 52–53 and 89; the amplified builds 57–61 and 75–79; the staged releases 63–67 and 80–83; the bar 83 harmonic colour; and the bare fifth at 35–36 are already at a master's level and should not be touched.
