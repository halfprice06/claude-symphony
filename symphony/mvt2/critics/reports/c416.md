## Findings

The woodwind writing follows these two chapters almost throughout. Four earlier passes have already fixed the larger points. What remains is small.

### 1. Bars 69-74: bassoon 2 plays 19.6 s at pp with no breath marked (applied)

**Problem.** Bassoon 2 enters at 69 on D♭3 pp. It re-strikes D♭3 at 70 and goes straight into A′ on C3 at 71. It then plays to 74 b2 with no rest and no comma, which is about 19.6 s at crotchet = 52. The other 19.6 s stretch, at 7-12, has a slur break at 10/11 where the player can snatch air (c126). The only rest in this one is at 74 b3.

**Evidence.** Adler says the flute and other winds need time to breathe, and the oboe is the one wind that can play longest (PDF 175, 188). The comma is the woodwind's printed breathing point (PDF 188, Ex. VII-9: Brahms staggers the two flutes so one breathes while the other holds). At 70/71, bassoon 1's tied G3-A♭3 runs across the bar line, and the basses hold E♭2 through 70. So a breath in bassoon 2 at the bar line leaves no hole, and it is staggered like bassoon 1's breath at 34/35 against bassoon 2's at 33 (c066).

**Fix (applied).** In sections/retr.ily, retrBsnTwo:

```
des2.\pp | des2.-\tweak stencil ##f \pp \breathe | }
```

The comma prints at the end of 70 on the shared bassoon staff and in the Bassoon 2 part. score.midi is unchanged.

### 2. Bars 81-82: oboe 1 and cor anglais fade al niente while flutes and clarinets stop at pp (recorded, optional)

At 82, oboe 1 (A♭4) and the cor anglais (E♭4, written B♭4) carry circled-tip hairpins to niente. Flute 1, both clarinets, the bass clarinet and the bassoons end p>pp. Adler names the niente as the clarinet's speciality, done "better than any other woodwind" (PDF 211). He also says control is hardest at the soft end for the double reeds (PDF 174-175, 198). Both notes lie above the lowest fifth, though, and a professional oboist can taper on A♭4. The strings and harp take over at 82-83, so nothing is left exposed. I do not recommend a change. If the composer wants the wind release to match, change both `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t` lines at 82 (aTwoOboeOne, aTwoCorAnglais) to plain `\>` ending on `\pp`, as flute 1.

### 3. Bars 60-63: flute 1's held A♭6 ff (recorded, no change)

After a snatched breath at the bar line 60/61, flute 1 holds A♭6 ff for two bars of Largamente and slurs to the sf A6 at 63. That is about 10 s in the top fourth of the flute, which Adler calls "most problematical" (PDF 186). The flute needs a lot of breath there (PDF 188). Brahms's remedy (Ex. VII-9) would give the held note to flute 2, but flute 2 carries theme B's head an octave lower (c059, c021, protected). Ten seconds is within a professional's range at this tempo. The flute then rests 64-76 (PDF 188: rest after a sustained passage). This needs no change.

### 4. Bars 89-92 and 7-12 (recorded earlier, no change)

Cl 2's last E♭4 (about 19 s plus the fermata, ppp) and Bsn 2 at 7-12 (19.6 s with a slur break) stay within the breathing plan (c126, c276). I list them only so the measured figures are on record.
