## Findings

### 1. Bars 25, 26, 28: the violas' harmony notes sit on the cello counter-melody (applied)
**Problem.** At 23–30 the cellos have the movement's first melodic counterpoint against theme A in the violins, and the violas supply the harmony. In three places the lower violas play the counter-melody's own pitch: A♭3 for two beats at 25, B♭3 for two beats at 26, and D♭4 at 28 beat 3, which is the counter-melody's peak and its f. At those moments the cello line becomes a chord note in the middle of the viola sound, and it is not heard as a separate voice.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 259–260 (Ex. XVII-3): Tchaikovsky gives one group the melodic counterpoint and the others harmonic figuration that "neither duplicates nor imitates" it. PDF 283 (Mahler): the line is "helped in pitch, yet not doubled throughout". The same pages were the basis for c027's accepted fixes at 31–36 and 49.
**Fix (applied, `sections/aOne.ily`, aOneVla).** Same rhythm, same harmony, and no other part changes:
```
<c' es'>2.\p^\markup \italic "div." | <c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4 | <c' es'>2 f'4 | es'2. |
<c' es'>2\< <aes des'>4 | <des' f'>4 <des' f'>4\f\> f'4 | <es' g'>2. | \after 2 \pp <es' aes'>2.\p\> |
```
The chords are still complete: at 25 b1 A♭–C–E♭ with C doubled, at 26 E♭–B♭–E♭–G, and at 28 b3 ii7 B♭–D♭–F–A♭ with no doubled tone. This is an inner re-voicing inside the protected "plain first statement" 23–30, of the same kind as c022's change to the violas at 27–28. The texture stays plain.

### 2. Bars 74, 76–78: the A′ viola figure and harp strike the counter-melody's notes (applied)
**Problem.** In A′ the viola eighth figure and the harp chords lie in the counter-melody's octave. They attack its pitches while it holds them. The violas play B♭3 at 74 b1 (cello B♭3), D♭4 at 76 b3.5 (cello D♭4), B♭3 at 77 b1.5 and D♭4 at 77 b3.5 (cello and horn 2). The harp plays A♭3 at 76 b1, B♭3 and D♭4 at 77 b2, and C4 at 78 b3. These are exactly the bars where horn 2 and bassoon 1 join the cellos (75–78), so the counter-melody is getting fuller while the figure keeps cutting into it.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 259–260 and 288–289 (Orff): the figure gives "pitch and rhythmic assistance" without obscuring the line. PDF 274: keep the accompaniment registrally distant from the line it accompanies. c027 made this point and it was never answered. **The protection (c107) is respected in substance.** The figure keeps its rhythm, slurs, register band, dynamics and harmony, and the counter-melody is not touched. Only single eighths that coincided with the counter-melody move.
**Fix (applied, `sections/aTwo.ily`).**
```
aTwoVla 74: des'( es' g') es'( bes des')        % was bes( des' g') ...
        76: f'( aes' f') bes( f' bes)            % was ... bes( des' bes)
        77: g( es' g') es'( bes g)               % was g( bes es') g'( es' des')
        78: unchanged (E♭4 at b3 would run F♭–E♭ in octaves with the bass)
aTwoHarpR 76: <des' f'>4  77: r4 <g es'>2  78: r2 <g es'>4   % were <aes des' f'>, <g bes des'>, <g c' es'>
```
The 78 b3 harp chord is still iii6 (c103) without its C. The only unison left is the violas' C4 on the counter-melody's pickup at 78 b3. It lasts one eighth, and moving it would create octaves with the bass.

### 3. Bar 14: the harp's closing chord lies above and on the horn's last note (tested, not applied)
**Problem.** c019's rule for 7–13 is that no harp note sounds above the horn. At 14 the harp RH plays A♭3–C4–E♭4 on beat 2, over the horn's final A♭3 and in unison with it. Adler PDF 274 and Bizet (PDF 279) both keep the accompaniment below and apart from the solo.
**Test.** I tried `r4 <c es>2\arpeggio` with the treble clef moved to the following rests. The page count stayed at 8. However, analysis.txt then reports a new outer-voice octave from 14 to 15: the horn's A♭3 becomes the top voice, then the oboe enters on C5 over the cellos' C3. These are different instruments, but under the rules of this loop it counts as a new parallel, so I reverted the change. If the composer wants it, `r4 <es c'>2` removes the unison and lowers the top note from E♭4 to C4. It still sits a third above the horn. The scratch copy does not include this change.

### 4. Bars 8, 24, 72: the motto tail is harmonised note against note (no change)
Every note of the tail (8 b2–3, 24 b2–3, 72 b2–3) is struck together with the whole accompaniment, because the descending-fifths chain changes harmony on every beat. Adler PDF 259–260 would favour some held voices. In bar 7 the accompaniment already moves while the solo holds, which is the independence he describes. The chains at 8/24/72 and the bassoon layout of 7–14 are protected (c032, c077, c126). The tail is meant to be heard as a harmonised chromatic cell. No change.

### 5. Bar 33: the cor anglais moves in sixths with the bass clarinet's tail in the same rhythm (no change)
At 33 the cor anglais's C5–B4–B♭4 runs a sixth above the solo's E♭4–D4–D♭4 in quarters. Adler PDF 259–260 argues against this. However, c027, applying this same chapter, accepted a parallel line in a different family (double reed against single reed). The B4 is needed for the protected common-tone diminished seventh, and the woodwind choir 31–36 is protected as of v112. I do not reopen it.

### 6. Bars 15–22, 37–52, 83–89: checked against the reading, no change
In 15–22 the oboe and the solo violin answer each other with different rhythms. The solo violin is senza sord. over muted strings, as in Ex. XVII-7. In 37–52 every statement of theme B gets a new colour, and the accompaniment is a figure, pizzicato or held notes, never the theme's own rhythm (PDF 257–260, 278). In 83–89 the coda follows Ex. XVII-10 and XVII-13: harmonics over the pedal alone, the timpani motive joining the phrases, and the clarinet tail over a three-part background.
