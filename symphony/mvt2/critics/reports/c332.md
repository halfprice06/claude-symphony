## Findings

### 1. Bar 55 b4: the harp strikes C4 together with the theme's passing D♭5 (applied)

**Problem.** In theme B's third statement the violins play E♭5–D♭5–C5 over the C ø7 at 55 b3–56 b1. Their D♭5 on b4 is an accented passing tone, a minor 9th above the chord root, and it resolves to C5 at 56. On that same beat the harp's triplet starts on C4 and returns to C4 on the last triplet (`bes8 c' es' c' bes c'`). The tone of resolution is therefore struck in an inner voice at the moment the dissonance arrives. A&S PDF 469–470 (and Unit 21 §12): a dissonance is clearest when its tone of resolution is not sounding in a voice other than the bass; otherwise the sonority is "unclear" and the resolution "ineffective". The bass C2/C3 is exempt, but the harp's C4 is not. The first statement has the same melody and bass at 39 b4, and there the violas play E♭4–B♭3–E♭4 (`bes8 es' ges' es' bes es'`) with no inner C. The harp lost that shape when c137 had to avoid Hn 1/Cl 1's held G♭4 at 55. It took C4 in its place, which is fine on b3 under E♭5 but not on b4 under D♭5.

**Evidence.** Eighth-beat reduction of v332 at 55 b4: C2 (Vc), C3 (Bsn 2), **C4 (harp)**, G♭4 (Cl 1, Hn 1), **D♭5 (Vln I, Vln II)**. The scan finds no other passing tone in 37–60 with its resolution struck in an inner part.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, harp RH bar 55, beat 4 only):**
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8\p des' es' des' bes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 c' es' es' bes es' } |
```
This was `... { bes8 c' es' c' bes c' }`. Beat 3 is unchanged. On beat 4 the harp plays E♭4–B♭3–E♭4, the pitches of the violas at 39 b4, and it leads straight into 56's `es'8 ges' es'`. The harp still does not strike Hn 1/Cl 1's held G♭4, so c137's rule holds, and it stays below the counter-line (c241). This is a pitch-only change, within the c124 rule as c173 read it. Result at 55 b4: C2, C3, E♭4, G♭4, D♭5. That is C ø7 with the melody's passing 9th over its 7th, and C5 now arrives at 56 as a new pitch.

### 2. Bar 56 b2: Cl 1/Hn 1 hold C4 under the violins' neighbour D♭5 (record, no change)

At 56 b1–2 the harmony is A–C–E♭–G♭, the rootless V♭9 of B♭ minor (Ex. 27-11/27-12). The violins' D♭5 eighth on b2 is its 13th, a 6–5 upper neighbour to C5 (Ex. 27-16b). The book's model sounds the 6th without the 5th. Bar 40 matches it: the violas move C4 to E♭4 on b2. At 56, Hn 1 and Cl 1 hold C4 (c051) a minor 9th below the D♭5. I tested other pitches on paper for b2. E♭4 would be re-struck by the harp's `es'8 ges' es'` (against c137). G♭4 is the harp's and G♭3 is Bassoon 1's. A3 would double the leading tone over the A2 bass. Going back to E♭4–D♭4 would undo c051's contrary motion against C5–B♭4. The dissonance is an unaccented eighth, two octaves apart, at pp–mp. A&S PDF 465–466 (Ex. 27-5) treats a neighbour 9th sounding against its resolution tone in a lower register as an ordinary neighbour. Keep it.

### 3. Bar 13 b3: the horn's passing C4 over V with the harp's B♭3 ringing (record)

At 13, horn 1 runs E♭4–D♭4–C4–B♭3–A♭3 over the E♭ bass, and the harp's G3–B♭3 from b2 is still ringing. The C4 on b3 is a passing 6th that falls to the 5th, B♭3, in the horn's own line half a beat later (Ex. 27-16b). It does not replace the 5th as an unresolved 6th would (PDF 475). The c117 comment in aOne.ily ("the horn carries the 7th E flat4-D flat4-C4 itself") is correct: the horn has the D♭4 eighth. Protected horn solo; nothing to change.

### 4. Unresolved soprano 6ths over V7 at 29, 77 and 81 b3 (record)

At 40 b2 and 56 b2 the D♭5 over the rootless V♭9 of B♭ minor is likewise in the soprano above the 7th E♭4.

### 6. Bar 41 b3: a non-dominant 9th in the flute (record)

Flute 1 enters on A♭5 over G♭maj7 (G♭ bass, the C.a.'s F4 as 7th). PDF 466: "Nondominant ninths occur infrequently except in sequences". This one is unprepared, but the A♭ is held into B♭m7 at 42 by the C.a. and violas as a common tone. The flute's A♭5–F5–G♭5 reaches the resolution tone G♭5 one beat late, through an inserted lower third. It comes from the line, not from stacking (PDF 475–476), and c090's tenths with the C.a. depend on it. Keep it.

### 7. Bar 58: 4ths over the seventh chords in the chain (record)

At 58 b4 the G♭ over D♭7 is Ex. 27-17's unresolved 4th: it anticipates the G♭ bass at 59. This is the same place c172 read as II over V (Ex. 27-14, 27-21), and both readings support the voicing.
