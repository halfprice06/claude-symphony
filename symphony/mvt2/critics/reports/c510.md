## Findings

I found no change to notes, dynamics or scoring that the reading supports. The reading asks a composer to check results against intentions, to find where a piece sags, and to make each colour change mean something formally. I made those checks and the score passes them. The findings below record the checks so later critics need not repeat them. Finding 8 is an optional text correction to the coherence file.

### 1. The stated cyclic intentions and the notes agree (Sebesky PDF 240; Adler PDF 20)

- **Check:** Sebesky's first question is whether the result matches the intention. The intentions are written in coherence/mvt2.md, and the theme records test them by machine.
- **Evidence:** themecheck.py gives 19/19 and 11/11 on v510. Both quotations occur once (C.a. 47 b4-50, Tbn 1 63-64). Movement I v421's last chord still crosses into bar 1 at the same pitches. The last A♭4 (Vln II) and the timpani muta at 92 are what the scherzo's notes in mvt2.md rely on. The finale's links (theme B 41-44; F–F♭–E♭ at 79-81 and 87-89; Cl 1 tail 85-89) are present at pitch.
- **Fix:** none.

### 2. The arc does not sag (Sebesky PDF 240)

- **Check:** "where does it sag?" The density profile in analysis.txt (voices sounding per four bars) is 6.6 at 1-4, 3.8 at 33-36, 7.4 at 37-40, 4.5 at 49-52, 13.2 at 57-60, 15.8 at 61-64, 3.8 at 65-68, 15.9 at 77-80, 7.2 at 85-88.
- **Reading:** the three low points (31-36, 49-52, 65-68) are each a cyclic event heard alone: the B.cl. tail at movement I's pitch, the S quotation, and Hn 1's tail over the E♭ pedal. They are not gaps. Each is followed by a rise (B1, the build 53-60, A′). The two ff peaks (61-64, 79-81) differ in kind and were recorded as such (c303, c333, c413). Nothing in the movement reaches fff, so it stays below movement I's and the finale's climaxes, which suits its middle position before a pp scherzo opening.
- **Fix:** none. Proportions are protected (c253, c303, c319).

### 3. The cyclic lines are not covered: red-pencil test (Sebesky PDF 241; PDF 242)

- **63-64 (Tbn 1, first-theme head, ff):** this is the place most at risk of "too busy". In the print, the full chord of 61-62 is cut to sf > p on 63 b1, and Tbn 1 enters on the second eighth, after that attack. The upper winds and horns rest through 63-64. Only the string tremolo (decaying to p at 64) and the held A in the low strings and B.tbn remain. score.midi gives Tbn 1 velocity 67-87 against strings 83 falling to 53. The quotation is the loudest new line once the sf has passed.
- **47-50 (C.a., S head, p):** the texture is 4.5 voices on average. In score.midi the C.a. is at 59-66 and the accompaniment at 37-50. Not muddy.
- **72 (Vln I/II tail):** the tail is in the top voice in octaves. The cello counter-melody is below it, so there is enough counter-melody without covering the tail.
- **85-89 (Cl 1, last tail):** score.midi gives Cl 1 47-54 against the C.a. at 29 and the muted horns at 37, and Vln I takes the C5 at 41 at 89. This matches the printed intent (c160, c240).
- **Fix:** none.

### 4. The join technique is reused at both ends (Sebesky PDF 241)

- **Observation:** Sebesky says to note what worked and use it again at the next fitting place. The movement does this at its two joins. At 1-2, Vln II's G4 (movement I) rises a semitone to A♭4. At 89-92, Vln II holds A♭4, which the scherzo's Hn 1 takes as G♯4. The voice that crosses each join moves or is reinterpreted by a semitone. Vln I's C5 is the common tone at both ends.
- **Record:** this is a strength of the cyclic design. Keep Vln II on A♭4 at 89-92 and Vln I on C5. Do not add a note above C5 at the end.

### 5. Each cyclic event has its own choir (Adler PDF 19, 20)

- **Adler PDF 19:** timbre and texture clarify the form. **PDF 20 (Monteverdi):** one mood, one combination. So a change of combination should mark a change of mood.
- **In the score:**
  - At 3 the strings leave and the winds take over movement I's chord. Movement I's world changes into II's.
  - At 31-36 the woodwind choir is alone for the B.cl. tail at movement I's pitch and instrument.
  - At 47-50 the C.a. plays over thin strings, with poco sostenuto.
  - At 63-64 Tbn 1 plays alone, the trombone's only bars in the movement.
  - At 65-70 Hn 1 plays alone over the timpani's E♭ near the rim.
  - At 84-92 the strings are muted, with muted Hn 3·4 and C.a. at 87-90, ending on strings and Cl 2.
- No two adjacent cyclic events share a combination. This also answers c500's colour-rotation record from a different book.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Timpani without trumpets is a deliberate exception (Adler PDF 21)

- **Reading:** Adler explains that in the Classical orchestra the timpani belonged with the brass because they "invariably played together with the trumpets".
- **In the score:** trumpets are tacet in II, so the timpani pair with the low brass (60-62), with Hn 1 (67-70), or play alone (52-53, 84-85). mvt2.md records (c380) that the finale brings back the trumpet-and-timpani pairing at its 199-204. Holding the Classical pairing back for the finale fits the cyclic plan, because II and the scherzo stay away from the trumpets' C-major associations.
- **Fix:** none. Keep the trumpets tacet.

### 7. Piano reductions of the joins are clear (Adler PDF 23)

- **1-6:** C3 C4 E4 G4 C5 → A♭ (E♮→E♭, G→A♭, C→A♭) → iv6/4 → I. This is three common-tone and semitone moves, playable as a four-voice piano reduction with no hidden voice.
- **63-64:** this reduces to the P head over a held A with D–E in the bass at 64 b3-4, and movement I's stop chord at 64 b3.
- **87-92:** this reduces to IV6 – iv – I with top D♭5–C5 and inner F–F♭–E♭.
- All three keep their sense at the piano. The orchestration adds colour, not missing content (Adler's test of "clarity and coloration").
- **Fix:** none.
