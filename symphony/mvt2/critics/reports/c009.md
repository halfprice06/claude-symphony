## What else I studied

I used the main model, Dvořák 9/ii, in the corpus (`reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/`). In the C♯-minor middle section (bar 46 = `TwomL` in header.ily; strings.notesL.ily and woods.notesL.ily after `\barNumberCheck#TwomL`), Dvořák puts the flute/oboe melody over three layers. The violins and violas sustain `gs2:32 fs:32` tremolo chords pp, the clarinets play sixteenth-note arpeggios, and the cellos and basses play pizzicato. The Largo's main theme is given to the cor anglais. I compared v09 with v01 bar by bar (sections/*.ily, both PDFs) and read all eight pages of the v09 PDF. In my scratch copy I ran allpairs_par.py before and after my changes and compared the two outputs.

**Would a conductor believe a master wrote this?** In most passages, yes. The horn theme over low winds (7–14), the violin unison with the cello counter-melody (23–30) and the A′ octaves with flute and oboe (75–82) are laid out the way Rimsky-Korsakov's examples are. Four details would mark it as a student score. The cor anglais staff is empty for the whole movement. The B-section melody loses its lower string octave just as it rises to its highest point. The B1 theme has no sustained harmony under it. The clarinets sit above the oboes in the climax chord.

**Against v01:** one thing got worse. In v01, bars 57–60 had Vln II an octave below Vln I, which is Rimsky-Korsakov's layout (Ex. 8). Cycle 7 changed Vln II to triplets that copy the violas an octave higher. Nothing else is worse than in v01. The revisions to the theme, the A′ plan and the 7–14 accompaniment are improvements.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 57–60: the melody lacks its lower string octave (worse than v01). APPLIED
**Problem.** Vln I plays the theme at A♭5–A♭6 mf–f, doubled in unison by Fl 1. The octave below is in Ob 1 alone. Vln II plays the same triplets as the violas, an octave higher.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov says a melody this high is "too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves" (PDF 59). When only one octave part is doubled, the lower part should go to a string group (PDF 78). Ex. 8 (PDF 182) is almost this texture: Vn I and II in octaves, *f cantabile ed espressivo*, with the triplet harmony below in the cellos. v01 had this layout.
**Answer to c007's reason.** C007 wanted the inner parts to move. The violas keep their triplets, so that motion stays. Vln II's triplets only doubled the viola figure at the octave and added no new line.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVlnTwo`, bars 57–60):**
```
aes'4(\< des''4) f''4.( ges''8 | aes''4 g''4) ges''4( f''4) | es''4.(\f f''8) des''4( bes'4) | c''1\< |
```

### 2. The cor anglais never plays. APPLIED at 37–44
**Problem.** The cor anglais is in the orchestra, and its staff is printed on every full page, but it has no notes in v01 or v09. The model is Dvořák's Largo, whose theme is on the cor anglais. A conductor will notice an instrument that is listed and never used.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov uses the cor anglais as a solo voice (Ex. 44, 45, PDF 221). He also blends it into string melodies (Ex. 15, 17, PDF 57–58, 191). Flute and cor anglais in octaves is his Ex. 57 (PDF 229, listed on PDF 67–68). Theme B's lower octave (A♭3–A♭4) lies in the cor anglais's best register. Cl 1, the present doubling, then becomes free.
**Fix (bSec.ily):** new variable `bSecCorAnglais` with Cl 1's line for 37–44 and the text "solo, dolce cantabile", followed by `R1*20`. `bSecClOne` 37–44 becomes `R1*8`. parts.ily already reads `bSecCorAnglais`.

### 3. Bars 37–44: no sustained harmony under theme B. APPLIED
**Problem.** After the downbeat of 37 the only accompaniment is the viola triplets and the pizzicato bass. No part holds a note, so the theme's long notes (bars 38, 40, 44) have nothing sustained beneath them.
**Evidence.** In Ex. 1 (PDF 173–174), the closest example (soft melody, moving viola figure, pizz. bass), Rimsky-Korsakov adds clarinets, bassoons and horn III holding chords pp. Dvořák's middle section (bar 46) has sustained tremolo chords pp in the violins and violas under the flute and oboe.
**Fix (`bSecVlnTwo` 37–44).** The two upper notes of each viola triplet chord are sustained pp in Vln II, so the pad adds no new pitches. Three chords are revoiced so that the pad does not double the melody's motion in octaves.
```
<des' f'>1\pp^\markup \italic "div., sul tasto" | <des' ges'>2 <c' ges'>2 | <des' ges'>2 <des' f'>2 | <es' ges'>2 <des' f'>2 |
<des' f'>2 <des' ges'>2 | <des' f'>4 <des' es'>4 <c' es'>4 <des' f'>4 | <bes ges'>2 <des' es'>2 | <c' es'>1 |
des'1\pp^\markup \italic "unis., ord." | ...
```
The violins' entry at 45 still sounds new, because Vln I has been silent since 30 and Vln II moves from a pad to its own line.

### 4. Bars 61–62: clarinets above the oboes in the climax chord. APPLIED
**Problem.** At 61 Cl 1 holds D♭5 above Ob 1's A♭4. Cl 2 (A♭4, then B♭4) lies above Ob 2 (F4, G♭4) for both bars.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov gives Fl–Ob–Cl–Bsn as the natural order and calls the reverse "far-fetched, unnatural" (PDF 67). This chord is the climax of the B section.
**Fix (`bSecClOne`/`bSecClTwo` 61–62):** Cl 1 `des'1\ff | des'2 fes'2`, Cl 2 `f1\ff | bes2 aes2`. The clarinets now lie under the oboes and above the bassoons. I used F3 for Cl 2, not A♭3, because A♭3 made Cl 2 move C→A♭ in octaves with the melody.

### 5. Bar 64: the bass line is in the double basses with no cellos. APPLIED
**Problem.** The A–D–E bass that leads into the E→E♭ slip (accepted in cycle 2) is played only by the basses and the bass trombone. The cellos stop on beat 3.
**Evidence.** "No phrase of any importance given to the double bass without the support of 'cellos or bassoons" (PDF 56; see Ex. 10, PDF 185). Strings and brass in unison "can never yield such a perfect blend" (PDF 79).
**Fix (`bSecVc` 64):** `a2:32\p d4( e4)`. The cellos continue into their B♭2 at 65 (a tritone, inside the V7 chord).

### 6. Bars 23–26: no string colour is specified for the first violin statement. Not applied (optional)
**Problem.** The violins first take theme A at 23 in unison, at E♭4–E♭5, with no string named.
**Evidence.** Ex. 12 (PDF 186), the model for this layout, marks Vn I+II "sul D" for the cantabile and "sul G" later. Ex. 52 (PDF 226) also uses "sul G". Playing 23–26 on the D string gives the darker, more covered sound that Dvořák gets from muted strings.
**Fix (`aOneVlnOne`/`aOneVlnTwo`):** add `^\markup \italic "sul D"` to the `es'8` pickup in 22, and `^\markup \italic "nat."` to the `f''4` in 27.
