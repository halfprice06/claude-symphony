# Critic 554: orchestration (lens 4). Version v554

Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. III Harmony: "Harmony in the brass" (four-part, three-part and several-part writing, discords, duplication, sustained octaves) and "Harmony in combined groups" (wind and brass in unison; overlaying, crossing, enclosure; stopped horns with oboe or cor anglais; wind above trumpets and trombones; strings and wind; strings and brass; the three groups), rimsky-principles PDF 100-114 (printed pp. 82-96). This is the fourth pass over this range (c194, c424 on this lens; c287 on texture).

I read every text page in the range in full. I viewed PDF 100-104, 106-112 as images at 110 dpi. The OCR of every brass diagram on PDF 101-104, 108-111 is unusable (chord labels such as "3Tr-bni" and "4 Corni" are scrambled and the noteheads are lost), so the principles below are taken from the images. Verdict: the score follows this reading. No change is applied. The scratch copy /tmp/critic-554/work is an unchanged copy of v554; it rebuilds with 0 warnings, 8 pages and 0 outer-voice parallels, and the parts book is 30 pages.

## What else I studied

I re-read the HISTORY entries that used this reading (c194, c287, c424) and c524's note on the horn second at 81, so as not to repeat settled points. In the local corpus I compared the horn writing of Dvořák 9/ii (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesL.ily). Dvořák opens with ppp horns over trombones and tuba, uses horns 1-2 muted in thirds as a soft two-part colour in the middle section, and saves the full brass for one ff passage, with trumpets used for a few bars only. This score follows the same economy: four horns, trombones and tuba sound together only at 60-62, and the muted horn pair returns only in the coda (87-90). The difference is that trumpets never play here (c060, kept for the finale), so every PDF 101-104 diagram that puts trumpets on the top parts is not applicable.

## Findings

All findings are records with no change. I found no bar where a change based on these pages would improve the score without undoing a protected decision. They are ranked by how much of the movement's orchestral quality depends on the passage.

**1. Climax chord, 61-62: follows PDF 100-103, 108-112. No change.**
The brass is laid out as Rimsky's chorale for several choirs (PDF 103): B.tbn and tuba hold the bass in octaves (PDF 101), Tbn 2 sits on top of the low choir (F3; B♭3-A♭3 at 62), and the horns form a close middle choir (D♭4-F4-A♭4 at 61; D♭4-G♭4-B♭4, then F♭4-A♭4-C♭5 at 62). The single horns 1 and 2 are ff against f in the rest of the brass (PDF 103 diagram); woodwinds are doubled above and marked ff (PDF 108-109, 110-112). Bsn 1 F4 sits between Hn 2 and Hn 1 at 62 b1 (bassoon inside the horns, allowed, PDF 108) and makes the F/G♭ second against Hn 1 in a different colour (PDF 102). The largest gap, a tenth G♭2-B♭3 at 62 b1-2, is between the bass octave and the next choir, the same spacing as in Rimsky's PDF 101 diagram with the tuba and third trombone below an upper three-part group. Closing the sixths would undo c224 (Tbn 2 A♭3 at 62 b3) or c086 (Hn 3·4 a2). Protected.

**2. Brass entry, 60: follows PDF 103. No change.**
Hn 4 enters alone on C4 marked p< against pp< in Tbn 2, B.tbn and tuba, which is PDF 103's one-degree-louder rule for a single horn among trombones. The seventh G♭ at b3 is in Tbn 2 (G♭3), Bsn 1 (G♭3), Hn 1 (G♭4) and the violas, so the discord against the A♭ bass is spread over four colours (PDF 102). Protected (c364, c119, c132).

**3. Trombone quotation over strings, 63-64: follows PDF 106 and 113. No change.**
Bar 63 has both of Rimsky's allowed string-brass combinations: brass sustaining over string tremolo (B.tbn A2 sf>, strings sf> tremolo) and brass with a short sforzando string attack (Cb pizz. sf). Tbn 1 is not doubled by any woodwind; PDF 106 says woodwind in unison "softens [the brass] and reduces its characteristic qualities", so the undoubled quotation keeps the trombone colour it needs for its one statement. Protected.

**4. Muted horns with cor anglais and divided violas, 87-90: follows PDF 107, 110, 113. No change.**
Hn 3·4 con sord. (A♭3 over F3-F♭3-E♭3), the cor anglais an octave above Hn 4 (F4-F♭4-E♭4), and the muted violas divided on both horns. This joins the PDF 107/110 muted-horn and cor anglais pairing with the PDF 113 exception for horns doubled by divided violas. The violas have been muted here since an accepted change after c424; muted violas with muted horns keep the same affinity, so the reading's verdict is unchanged. Protected.

**5. Horn and bassoon crossing, 67-70: follows PDF 108. No change.**
At 69-70 the horn has the concord (B♭3, the fifth of V7), Bsn 1 the third (G3) and Bsn 2 the seventh (D♭3): "the concords being given to the horns, the discords to the bassoons". Both bassoons stay under the horn, so there is no horn enclosed by bassoons. Protected since c014.

**6. Seconds inside the horn group, 80 b3 and 81 b2-3: acceptable. No change.**
PDF 102 prefers sevenths and seconds in different colours. At 80 b3 Hn 4 holds A♭3 (the seventh of ii ø7) while Hn 1·2 move from the shared A♭3 to B♭3; at 81 b2-3 Hn 1·2's D♭4 (the seventh of V7) lies a second under Hn 3's E♭4. In both places the moving note is also in Bsn 1 and the cellos, so the second sounds in more than one colour, and both dissonances are held-note against counter-melody, the case where PDF 112 says "distribution is subordinate to correct progression of parts". The 81 second was recorded by c524. The only way to take the 80 b3 second out of the horns is to rest Hn 4 on b3 (aTwoHnFour `aes2 r4 | g2 es4` for `aes2~ aes4( | g2 es4)`), which loses the seventh's resolution A♭3-G3 in its own line (c122). I do not recommend it.

**7. Horns over low clarinets, 3-6, and the horn solo over bassoons, 7-14: follows PDF 107-108. No change.**
At 3-6 Cl 2 (E♭3-F♭3) and the bass clarinet (A♭2) lie under Hn 3·4; the clarinets are below the horns, not inside them (PDF 108), and the low-clarinet-and-horn colour is the one PDF 107 praises. At 7-14 the solo horn lies over two bassoons and the bass clarinet in close harmony, with no bassoon above the horn (c287's scan). Both are on the standing list.

**8. Second peak, 79-81: follows PDF 100-101, 108-109, 112; known exception to PDF 108 on moving parts. No change.**
Four horns in three parts with the bass left to the woodwind and strings (PDF 100-101); woodwinds ff and doubled above horns at f (PDF 108-109, 112); Hn 3's F♭4 minor ninth also in Ob 2 and Fl 2 (PDF 102). The moving horn line is a melody doubled by Bsn 1 and the cellos (c129 exception). Protected.
