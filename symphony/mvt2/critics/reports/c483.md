## Findings

All findings are records; none needs a change. I applied nothing in /tmp/critic-483/work. It is an unmodified copy of v483, whose build-warnings.txt is empty and whose analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels; I did not rebuild it because nothing changed.

### 1. Register curve and proportions follow the "limits of range" rule (no change)

Times from the tempo map in score.midi: intro 0-20.8 s, A 20.8-103.8, transition to 125.4, B to 241.8, retransition to 262.6, A′ to 305.9, coda to 345.3 s. These are the same figures to the tenth of a second as c343 and c413, so the proportions are unchanged since v343: climax at 61 = 0.65 of the length, second peak at 79 = 0.84, A′ 43 s, coda 39 s.

The top-register scan (notes at G6 or above) finds them only at 58, 61-63, 77 and 79-81, always in Fl 1·2 together with Vln I. PDF 124-125 says the orchestra is seldom centred in the 5th-6th octaves, and when it is the flutes go with the top violins (Ex. 233: two flutes, oboe and Vln I in that register; Ex. 234: piccolos with divided violins). This score uses that register for the two peaks and nowhere else. It is the right place on the tension curve: the register rises with the build (53-60), reaches its top as the dynamic does, and is then given up at once (63 drops to the trombone's middle register). The second peak reaches B♭6, one step above the first peak's A♭6, and is shorter. This matches the shape of Dvořák 9/ii and the other corpus Adagios recorded by c233 and c343: the peak in the reprise is higher and shorter than the main climax.

Fix: none.

### 2. The lowest register is never crowded (no change)

PDF 124-125 and Ex. 232 (PDF 404) warn that close intervals in octaves 1 and below sound bad. Ex. 232 itself keeps the contrabassoon and low bassoons on open intervals and puts the close chords higher, in the muted trombones and violins. My scan finds one pair below G2 closer than a fifth: 31 b2.5, the bass clarinet's passing F2 over the contrabassoon's A♭1 (a major sixth, one eighth long, inside the solo arpeggio). It is a passing tone, and c343 recorded it. The ff tutti at 61-62 and 79-80 keep the low brass, contrabassoon and basses on octaves and fifths. The coda's A♭1-A♭2 pedal is an octave.

Fix: none.

### 3. Wide gaps between parts, including one new spot at 73 b3 (no change)

PDF 125 (Ex. 235-236): the top and bottom parts may be far apart only if the middle is filled, or for a strange effect. Compared with c343's list (36, 48-51, 53, 70, 83), this version has gaps at 36, 38, 48-51, 53, 73 and 83. The gap at 70 is gone because of c343's held horn B♭3. Two spots are new to the record:
- 38 b3.5: harp/basses F♯2 (G♭) to violas' E♭4, 21 semitones, for one half beat. The pizzicato and the harp decay. Above the gap, violas, Vln II and Fl 1 fill a full octave. The gap is between the bass and the middle, not between the outer parts.
- 73 b3-b3.5: Cb and harp F1 against bassoon F3, 24 semitones, with C4-A♭5 filled by cellos, clarinet, violas and violins above. Like 38, this is a wide interval at the bottom of the chord. It is not the empty middle that Rimsky warns against, and wide spacing at the bottom of a chord is the normal distribution (Rimsky ch. III). The harp has F1 alone there on purpose (c089: an F1-F2 dyad made fifths with the cellos into 74). The c469/c479 changes at 73-74 created no new gap between the outer parts.

The other spots (36 protected woodwind choir; 48-51 filled by the violas' off-beats; 53 b3 one half beat; 83 the harp-harmonics colour that c150, c213 and c413 protected) are unchanged.

Fix: none.

### 4. Phrase transfers coincide on a shared note at every hand-off of one line (no change)

PDF 125-126: "the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one." Ex. 237 shows this: each string group enters on the note the previous group is holding. Checked in the source:
- 30-31: Cbn A♭1 and Hn 3·4 enter under the strings' last chord.
- 34-37: C.a. ends on A♭3 over the B.cl.'s A♭2. The C.a. F5 eighth sounds with Fl 1's first F5 at 37 (c139, c271).
- 44-45: Ob 1's pickup at 44 b4 enters under the C.a.'s held C4.
- 52-53: the C.a. holds into 53 under the violins' entry.
- 56-57: Hn 1 carries the counter-line across while Cl 1 leaves.
- 64-65: Cb E→E♭ under the strings' E♭7.
- 66-67: Cb E♭ pedal held while the upper strings hand to Hn 1 and the timpani.
- 70-71: Hn 1 B♭3 held under the violins' pickup (c343).
- 82-83: harp A♭1 tied over.
- 84-85: timpani A♭ tied into 85 under Cl 1's E♭5.
- 88-89: Cl 1 C5 held under Vln I's C5.

Two joins are adjacent rather than overlapping, and both fall outside the rule:
- 22 b3: the solo violin's E♭5 ends as the section's E♭4 pickup begins. This is the end of the dialogue and the start of a new statement, not one line passed on. c213 protected it, and holding the solo's E♭5 through b3 would cut the mute time c468 secured for the leader (about 8 s to 7 s).
- 40-41: Fl 1 ends theme B's first half on B♭4, and the C.a. starts the second half an octave lower. This is the repetition case on PDF 128 (lower phrase, lower instrument), not a transfer.

Fix: none. If the composer wants the literal Ex. 237 overlap at 22, the solo would need a second voice on the Vln I staff (`<< { es''2.) } \\ { s2 es'8( f'8 } >>` with the slur carried on in 23). I do not recommend it, for the mute-time reason above.

### 5. Chords passed between groups keep regular part-writing (no change)

PDF 126-127 and Ex. 239-243 show a chord leaving one group and arriving in another with each voice moving by step or holding, so that no voice appears to leap. The main group changes in this score are 14→15 (horn, bassoons, harp to muted strings and oboe: I to I6, diatonic, no false relation), 30→31, 36→37, 64→65 (E in the basses to E♭) and 66→67. All are regular. 62→63 (tutti F♭ chord cut to the trombone's A-minor quotation over tremolo) is the break that PDF 127's note allows "when extreme contrast of timbre between two adjacent chords is intended". It is protected.

Fix: none.

### 6. Amplification and elimination shape both builds and both releases (no change)

PDF 127 (amplification) asks that a second group enter p or pp and crescendo faster than the first. In 53-61 the strings start p and the winds enter one at a time below them: Hn 1 and Cl 1 at pp (53 b4), Bsn 1 pp< (54, c478), Fl/Ob p (57), Hn 4, Tbn and Tuba pp at 60, and all reach ff at 61. Ex. 248 (PDF 413) is the model: violin tremolo first, then winds and brass at pp cresc. to f. In 75-79 Fl 1 enters mp< at 77, and the timpani enter pp< at 78. Elimination (PDF 127-128) runs the other way: 62→63 drops the winds and keeps the trombone and strings, 64→65 leaves only the strings, 81-83 removes winds and violins and keeps the low strings, and 89-92 leaves only the strings with Cl 2. The release follows the reverse of the entry order, as c413 recorded. The c478 bassoon entry at 54-55 fits the reading: the new group enters soft and one level below the melody.

Fix: none.

### 7. Imitation and echo obey the law of register (no change)

PDF 128: higher imitation goes to a higher instrument. At 15-22 the oboe states each idea, and the solo violin answers it higher (A♭5-B♭5 at 17, F5-G5 at 21). At 37-44 theme B's second half moves down an octave to the C.a. In the coda the harp harmonics (83) are answered lower by the timpani (84) and then by Cl 1 (85). At 87-90 the muted Hn 3·4 and the C.a. echo, at pp, the open ff F-F♭ of 61-62. PDF 128-129: "an echo given to muted brass following the same phrase not muted produces this distant effect". No woodwind echoes a string phrase or the reverse.

Fix: none.

### Comment on the form

After 482 cycles the form matches what this chapter asks of a slow movement. The extreme registers are kept for the two peaks and the coda pedal. Every hand-off of a single line overlaps on a shared note. Each build adds groups from soft to loud, and each release removes them in the reverse order. I found no place where the reading shows a fault. The two spots the scan added to the gap record (38, 73) are bass spacing, not empty middles.
