## Findings

I found nothing in the brass expression that the reading shows to be wrong. Each item below is recorded, with no change, and ranked by how much it matters.

**1. Horn solos 6-14, 53-60, 67-70: dynamics, words and swells.** (recorded, no change)
At 6-14 the score prints p "dolce espr.", < > at 7-8, < to mp at 12 and > to p at 14. That is the phrase-by-phrase swell of Tchaikovsky 5/ii (X-24, PDF 309) and the "p dolce" of Beethoven 6/iii (X-9, PDF 305). At 53 the solo starts pp "espr." and reaches mp, as Ravel's pp ... expressif (X-11). At 67 the horn plays "dolce" p < mp > pp alone over the dominant. The parts print the same words: "solo, dolce espr." at 6 and "solo / dolce" at 67. In the score, the hidden MIDI levels at 7-9 (mp, mp, p) fall inside the printed hairpins and do not contradict them.

**2. Bar 59: Hn 1's G♭4 is tongued, and the slur starts on E♭4.** (recorded, no change)
After the breath at the end of 58, the counter-line restarts at f on a tongued minim and then slurs E♭-F♭-E♭-F-G♭ into 61. PDF 309 and 321 say separate notes are tongued and slurred notes share a breath. A fresh f attack after a breath is the "tu" articulation this new phrase needs, and the cellos' slurs at 59-60 were aligned with this line in c509. A slur back to the G♭ would hide the breath.

**3. Bars 60-62: brass entries and levels.** (recorded, no change)
Hn 4 enters p < and the low brass pp <, all reaching f at 61. Hn 1·2 are ff and Hn 3·4 a2 are f in the bright and heroic register (X-5, PDF 303). The middle-register horn can enter a degree above the low brass (PDF 311), and PDF 319's warning about low pianissimo is about the trumpet. The tenutos on every brass note at 61-62 are protected (c088). c218, c264, c328 and c448 settled all of this, and the reading gives no new reason.

**4. Bars 63-64: Tbn 1 quotation "marcato" ff, accents and portato, > p at 64.** (recorded, no change)
The quotation is a motif, not a cantabile horn melody, so X-12's "molto espr." does not apply. It lies in the trombone's very strong register (X-69, PDF 329), and its slurred steps use neighbouring slide positions (X-70, X-71). The B.tbn sf > p at 63 is the brass attack that clears the field. PDF 308 offers stopped horn for sf>p, but adding a horn to a protected bar for a colour the movement does not need was rejected at c328 and c448. I have no new reason.

**5. Bars 79-82: horns in the ff tutti.** (recorded, no change)
Hn 1·2 a2 play f (mf < from 78) and Hn 3·4 play f with a < > swell on the F♭ suspension, ending al niente at 81 (c088). PDF 311 says the horn carries over almost any combination, so f in the middle register is enough. Brahms's < f > swells (X-8, PDF 304) support the inner swell. These dynamics are protected (c188).

**6. Mute plan 82-91 and the muted echo 87-90.** (recorded, no change)
"3·4: mettere sord." at 82 (c448) gives about 17 s before "con sord." at 87. "via sord." at 91 falls in a rest, and the scherzo prints "senza sord." (PDF 308, 321). The pair is pp, slurred, and fades to niente, as Debussy's muted horns (X-17, PDF 307). The echo of the open 3-6 chord in muted colour is the device of X-19 and X-48 (one passage in two colours). The parts agree: Horn 3 and Horn 4 print "mettere sord.", "con sord." and "via sord." at the same bars as the score.

**7. Part expression for the low brass.** (recorded, no change)
The Trombone 2, Bass trombone and Tuba parts print the same pp <, f, tenutos and breaths as the score at 60-62. The Bass trombone part also prints the sf > and the closing > niente. Tbn 1 enters at 63 after a B.tbn cue at 61-62 and carries "solo, marcato" ff. Nothing on these pages contradicts the score or this chapter.
