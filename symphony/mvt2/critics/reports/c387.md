## Findings

I found nothing in v387 that goes against the assigned reading and is not already settled with a stated reason. The items below are the places where the reading presses hardest, ranked by how close each comes to a real weakness. I recommend no change to the score. Nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-387/work, so there is no new build. v387's own build-warnings.txt is empty and its analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

**1. 41-44: Fl 1 moves in the same rhythm as the cor anglais's theme. It is a coupling, not a counter-melody. No change.**
At 42 both lines play quarter, two eighths, tied quarter, eighth (C.a. A♭4-B♭4-A♭4-G♭4-F4; Fl 1 F5-G♭5-F5-E♭5-F5). At 43 both play dotted quarter, eighth, quarter, quarter, and at 44 both hold a dotted half. If the flute were read as middleground, it would break Adler's fourth clarity rule, that the counter-theme must differ in rhythm (PDF 144), and his Beethoven 7/ii point that each layer has its own rhythm (PDF 142). Adler also names this exact device, "coupling", two instruments at a parallel interval, as a strong change of texture (PDF 136). The two lines are a tenth to a thirteenth apart, so they do not mask each other, which satisfies his register rule (PDF 144, no. 3). The doubling is protected as the movement's one melody in sixths and tenths (c090; HISTORY "Fl 1 thirds/sixths 41-44"). I record that it is coupling by design. A later critic should not call it a weak counter-melody.

**2. 47-51: Vln II above the quotation is separated mainly by dynamics. Settled, no change.**
Adler calls dynamics the least effective way to separate lines (PDF 144). c369 tested the alternative, Vln II below the C.a., on paper: it lands among the violas' off-beat A3-E♭4. I agree. The violas play the on-and-off-beat accompaniment of Ex. V-32 (PDF 158), so rhythm separates the layers as well as dynamics. The quotation and its flat pp accompaniment are protected.

**3. 53-56: the layering follows all four of Adler's clarity rules. Recorded.**
The foreground is the violins in unison, sul A, C5-G♭5. The middleground is Cl 1 and Hn 1 in unison, A♭3-B♭4, in dotted quarters and halves; it does not move with the violins' eighths. The background is harp triplets, A♭3-A♭4, the cellos' bass in halves (D♭3 down to A2), and Bsn 1's held D♭3-F3. The harp shares the counter-line's register, but plucked against wind is a change of colour, which is the case Adler exempts (PDF 135). The violas and basses rest, which thins the counterpoint (PDF 144, no. 2). Their silence also makes the octave added at 57 count (PDF 137).

**4. 71-78: the A′ background changes between antecedent and consequent, as in Ex. V-30. Recorded.**
At 71-74 the violins play in octaves (A♭5 over A♭4) over the viola eighth figure, Cl 2 and Bsn 2. At 75-77 they drop to unison A♭4, and Hn 2 and Bsn 1 join the cellos' counter-melody (E♭3-D♭4). This is Grieg's "different doublings in antecedent and consequent" (PDF 154-155) and Tchaikovsky's changing melody doubling (PDF 152-154). The viola figure runs without a gap from 71 to 78, dovetailed as in Ex. V-7 (PDF 135). At 77 the theme's eighths coincide with the viola eighths, but the two are more than an octave apart (Vln I G5-E♭6 against violas G3-G4), so they do not blur (PDF 144, no. 3). The A′ texture plan is protected (c247, standing list).

**5. Background types: four different figures, one per section. Recorded.**
Sustained chords with arpeggios at 37-40 (Vln II div. sul tasto, viola triplets, pizz. basses, as in Ex. V-7 and V-34). On-and-off-beat chords at 48-51 (Ex. V-32). Sustained winds with agitated viola sextuplets at 59-60 (Ex. V-33). Arpeggiated viola eighths under a cantabile cello line at 71-78. No section reuses another's background, which is what Adler means by orchestration serving the form (PDF 166). The B-section background plan is protected (c107: "no further accompaniment changes in 37-60").

**6. 37-44 and 57-58: the bass line and the cello's double role. Recorded.**
At 37-40 the bass is pizzicato only (Cb with the harp's left hand), as in Brahms's Ex. V-7 (PDF 135: "pizzicato in the double basses"). The cellos come in arco at 41 under the C.a., Adler's "addition of the cellos" for a second statement (PDF 139). At 57 the cellos double the bass F3-G♭3 with the bass clarinet and Bsn 2 for two beats. They leave it at 57 b3 to take Hn 1's counter-line an octave lower (G♭3-F3-E♭3). That is a brief overlap of middleground and bass, not a fault: the line becomes audible as the theme rises to A♭6. No change.

**7. Spacing and first inversions at 23, 61, 79 and 87. Recorded.**
Spacing at 23 from the bottom: A♭2-E♭3-C4-E♭4-A♭4, a fifth, a sixth, a third and a fourth, so the intervals narrow upward (PDF 151, Ex. V-26). At 61 and 79 the low brass and basses keep fifths and octaves at the bottom, with the theme in three octaves on top. At 87 the third of IV6 is tripled only at the bottom (F1, F2, F3), as Ex. V-27 asks (PDF 152). c207 and c347 recorded the same results.

**8. 83-92: the diminuendo is made by scoring. Recorded.**
The coda thins by solos: four cellos at 83, four basses at 87, divided violas at ppp, horns 3·4 muted. Tchaikovsky's dynamic-by-scoring is followed (PDF 154). His other device, dropping the bass at each tonic, is not used, because the A♭ pedal under the plagal close is protected. Lower strings alone at 83-86 give Fauré's dark background (PDF 162) under the harp harmonics and the timpani answer.
