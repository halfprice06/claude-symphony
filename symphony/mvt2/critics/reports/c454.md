## Findings

I found nothing in the assigned reading that this score breaks. The points below are what I checked, in order of weight, and why each one stays as it is.

1. **61-62, horn weights in the climax chord (Adler PDF 15, 44).** Adler's rule is that above mf the horns should be paired, two horns making one trombone. Here Hn 3·4 are paired at f on the top brass line (A♭4, B♭4-C♭5), while Hn 1 (F4, G♭4-A♭4) and Hn 2 (D♭4, D♭4-F♭4) are single and marked ff. Rimsky-Korsakov's fallback when pairs are impossible (PDF 103) is exactly that one-degree-louder mark, and c264 chose the separate pitches to close the gaps in the chord. Against the trombones at f, a single horn at ff and a pair at f are about equal, which is what the chord needs. No change.

2. **79-81, the A′ peak has no trombones or trumpets (Adler PDF 38, Franck Ex. XI-20).** Adler gives this as a model in its own right: the climax built by the rest of the orchestra with the horns working as woodwinds. That is how 79-81 is scored: woodwinds and strings ff, Hn 1·2 a2 f doubling the cellos' counter-melody, Hn 3 and Hn 4 holding the V♭9 harmony. It also keeps 61 as the movement's only brass climax, which Adler PDF 17 asks for. No change. The protected V♭9 at 80-81 stays.

3. **63-64, the trombone quotation as a held-back colour (Adler PDF 37, Schubert Ex. XI-19).** Tbn 1 does not play before 63, so the quotation brings a new colour at a structural point, as Schubert's trombones do. Its register (E3-C4) is where the trombone is clearest (PDF 42). The Tchaikovsky model (PDF 38-41) puts the trombone theme inside the tutti, with the strings first dying away to the basses. This score does the opposite: the tutti comes first and then thins to sf>p tremolo under the solo trombone. Both separate the trombone theme from what surrounds it. Doubling the quotation or moving it into the tutti would break the once-only rule and the protected 63-64 layout. No change.

4. **Low brass spacing at 60-62 (Adler PDF 15-16, Ex. XI-1).** The tuba sits an octave or more below the bass trombone, and the trombones and horns are in close position above. The tenth between B.tbn F♭2 and Tbn 2 A♭3 at 62 b3 is filled by bassoons and cellos and also appears in Adler's own low chords. The contrabassoon and basses hold the bass across the tuba's breath after 61 (c286). No change.

5. **Soft horn chords at 3-6, 29-30 and 87-90 (Adler PDF 15-16).** These are one horn per pitch at pp, as Adler allows at mf or less. The open horns at 3-6 and the muted, low, dark horn chord at 87-90 are two separate colours, and the darker one is kept for the coda. No change.

6. **Trumpets tacet (Adler PDF 14, 17).** Adler warns against overusing the brass and praises Beethoven for saving the trombones for the finale. Keeping the trumpets for movement IV (c060, cyclic-notes) follows that warning at the scale of the symphony. Dvořák 9/ii uses trumpets at its climax, so this is a recorded choice and not a copy of the model. No change.
