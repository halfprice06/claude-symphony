# Critic 417: v417, lens 7 (texture and counterpoint)

Assigned reading: Adler, *The Study of Orchestration*, ch. 15 "Scoring for Orchestra", section "Polyphonic texture, or foreground-middleground-background distribution" (adler2 PDF 172-186, printed pp. 485-499), second pass, fourth series. I read the full text of every page in the range. PDF 172, 175, 176, 177, 178, 179, 181 and 184 carry prose. PDF 173-174, 180, 182-183 and 185-186 are full-score pages with no prose. I also read PDF 187, where the Schoenberg paragraph that starts on 184 ends. I rendered every page from 172 to 186 at 110 dpi into /tmp/critic-417 and looked at each example page: XV-24 (Mozart 41/iv mm. 368-424, PDF 173-175), XV-25 (the five Jupiter elements, 175), XV-26 (*Meistersinger* Prelude mm. 158-161, 177), XV-27 (*Scheherazade* iv mm. 482-493, 178-179), XV-28 (Holst "Jupiter" mm. 140-156, 180), XV-29 and XV-30 (Bloch *Schelomo*, 181-183) and XV-31 (Schoenberg op. 16/5, 185-186). The OCR of these pages is unusable, so everything I say about the notation comes from the images. Two details are visible only in the images. In XV-26 Wagner gives each strand its own expressive word: "ausdrucksvoll" on the tune, "scherzando" and staccato on the arpeggio, "sehr markiert" on the bass. In XV-27 the violas play an accented off-beat figure that is separate from both the staccato flute/violin dance figure and the sustained wind background.

This reading has had four earlier passes: c017, c117, c257 and c397. c397's report covers the same principles, and its one applied change (the timpani roll at 78 b3) is now in the score. I checked every principle again against v417 bar by bar. I did not repeat findings that are already settled.

Overall verdict: v417 follows every principle in this section of Adler. Each combined passage has three or four strands. The strands differ in rhythm, register and colour. Counter-lines use material the listener has already heard. The builds add instruments to each element in turn, and dynamics are graded by importance. The four earlier passes fixed the places where strands met on one pitch. The points below are confirmations or very small optional points. None of them is a fault that stands between this texture and a master's work.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 77 b1: Fl 1's G6 is alone in the upper octave for one beat (recorded, no change)

**Bars.** 77 b1.

**Observation.** At 77, Fl 1 enters on G6 (`g'''4(\mp\<`). On the same beat, Vln I, Vln II and Cl 1 all play G5. Vln I joins the upper octave only on b2 (E♭6). For one beat the theme's upper octave is a solo flute with no string under it at that pitch.

**Reading.** Adler PDF 176: in Mozart's coda, each statement of the theme is coloured differently "and supported by one member of the string choir". PDF 172: Wagner adds upper octaves to the violins, not above them.

**Why no change.** c341 placed Vln I's G5 there so that the A♭5 at 76 b3 (the 7th of ii7) resolves by step in Vln I's own line. That is a voice-leading reason, and it outranks one beat of colour. The flute enters on a pitch the orchestra already holds an octave lower, at mp under a crescendo, and the violins arrive in its octave half a second later. This is the kind of entry Adler describes at PDF 187, where an instrument comes in first and the continuity instrument then joins it. Recorded so that no later critic reopens it without answering c341.

### 2. Bars 71-78: four layers on the Scheherazade model (confirmation)

The XV-27 image shows the theme in a mixed doubling (cello, horns, clarinets), a staccato "dance figure" middleground in flutes and violins, a sustained background in oboes, bassoons, horns 3-4, violas and basses, and harp strokes at the phrase starts (PDF 179). v417's A′ has the same four layers:

- Theme: violins in octaves, with Fl 1 and Cl 1 joining at 77.
- Counter-melody: cellos "cantabile, sul D", with Hn 2 and Bsn 1 joining at 75.
- Figure: viola eighths.
- Background and bass: Cl 2, Bsn 2, basses and harp LH.

The harp RH rests at 71-74 and enters at 75, 76, 77 and 78 b3 (c034, c127). Each layer has its own rhythm (dotted quarters, halves, eighths, a quarter-note bass), as PDF 175 and 179 require. No change.

### 3. Bars 57-61: build and arrival as in Holst and Bloch (confirmation)

PDF 181: during the build, figuration gives buoyancy and heavy bass notes give stability, and at the arrival "all motion ceases". PDF 184: near the climax the composer adds instruments to every element. In v417:

- The theme gains Fl 1 and Ob 1 at 57.
- The counter-line gains Bsn 1 at 59.
- The figure doubles its speed at 59 (triplets to sextuplets).
- The bass gains B.cl. and Bsn 2 at 57, then low brass and the timpani roll at 60.
- At 61 every part changes to half notes or tremolo.

This is the sequence the reading describes. The counter-line's colour (Hn 1 + Vc, then Bsn 1) stays the same until 61, as Mozart keeps each colour through mm. 389-400 (PDF 176). No change.

### 4. Bars 37-44: separation by choir and by attack (confirmation)

The layers are separated by choir and by attack:

- Theme B in Fl 1, then the C.a.
- A bowed background: Vln II chords at 37-40, the cellos' tenor line at 41-44.
- A plucked layer: violas (arco triplets at 37-40, pizz. at 41-44), basses and harp.

This is Bloch's choir separation (PDF 184) with Adler's "varied articulations" (PDF 179). The C.a.'s straight eighths at 42 are set against the violas' pizz. triplets, so foreground and middleground never share a rhythm (PDF 175). No change.

### 5. Bars 45-47: two elements only (recorded, no change)

At 45-47 there are two elements: theme B's head in Ob 1 over Bsn 1 and the cellos, and a held background in Vln II, the violas and the basses. By the measure of this section (PDF 172: three or more important elements) this is homophonic, not polyphonic. I recommend no change. The passage is a four-bar link from the D♭ statements to the C.a. quotation at 47. A thin texture there leaves the quotation's entry clear, which is the point Adler makes about holding colours back (PDF 172). c407 recorded the same passage as "figured to sustained background".
