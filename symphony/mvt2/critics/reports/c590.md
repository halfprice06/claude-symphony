## Findings

No finding asks for a change in the notes. The movement's use of mixture already does what this unit teaches, at both the local and the symphony level. The points below record the checks, so a later cycle does not reopen them without an answer, plus one stale record.

### 1. Every ♭6̂ reaches 5̂ before any ♮6̂, including the two places where that is easy to miss (no change)

**Reading.** PDF 388 and Points 3 (PDF 395): ♭6̂ "is strongly active in the direction of 5̂ … it will not normally be replaced by ♮6̂ before 5̂ is reached"; ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ is good, ♭6̂–♮6̂–5̂ is poor.

**Score, bar by bar.**
- 5–6: Cl 2 E♭3–F♭3–E♭3. Neighbour from tonic harmony (Ex. 23-2a).
- 59–60: Vc G♭3–E♭3–F♭3 (59 b4, the 7th of vii°7/V) to E♭3 (60 b1), and only then F3 (60 b2). 5̂ comes before ♮6̂, so this is allowed.
- 78: bass F♭ (Cb, Bsn 2) on beats 1–2 falls to E♭ on beat 3. Vln I's F6 is a passing sixteenth after that (78 b3.5, C6–F6–G6–A♭6 to B♭6). The viola's F♭4 (78 b2.5) leaps to C4. 5̂ is in the bass before the F♮, so this is the c070 solution and it holds.
- 79–81: Fl 2 F6 (79–80) to F♭6 (80 b3, 81 b1) to the E♭6 in Vln I at 81 b2. Ob 2, Hn 3 (F4–F♭4–E♭4) and violas (F4–F♭4–E♭4) do the same. All ♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂.
- 62–65: F♭ (bass and Vln I at 62 b3) is respelled E at 63 for the A-minor quotation. Tbn 1's F3–E3 at 64 b3–4 is A minor's own ♭6̂–5̂ over the bass D2–E2. The bass E2 then falls to E♭2 at 65. In A♭ terms the F♭/E reaches E♭ in the bass without an F♮ in that line, and the trombone's F belongs to the quoted key.
- 87–89: C.a. F4–F♭4–E♭4, Hn 4 and lower violas F3–F♭3–E♭3. This is Ex. 23-2f's Chopin progression in the same key.

**Fix.** None. The c432/c462 script result ("every F♭ reaches E♭") still holds in v590. Since v432 no pitch change has touched 59–60 or 78–81.

### 2. The opening and the coda frame the movement with mixture, and Dvořák's Largo does the same thing (no change)

**Reading.** Ex. 23-1 (PDF 386–387, image 386): Mendelssohn sets the minor colour at the start and lets the major "reassert itself" much later. Ex. 23-5 (PDF 390): ♭6̂ colours the plagal close. Ex. 23-7 (PDF 391–392): the Picardy third has "startling power".

**Score.** Movement I ends with E♭5 in its last motto (Vln I, I 178) and then a C-major chord with the violas on E4 (I 182). That is the Picardy third, Ex. 23-7. Movement II undoes it in one voice: violas E4–E♭4 at 1–2, which is no cross relation (Ex. 23-12b). In A♭ that E♮ sounds like F♭, so the movement's first chord change is the ♭6̂–5̂ motion of Ex. 23-2. Bar 5 states it again (iv6/4, Cl 2 F♭3–E♭3). The coda's last chromatic motion is the same step at the same E♭ (C.a. F♭4–E♭4 and violas F♭3–E♭3 at 88–89). The arc therefore runs from I's major-for-minor at its end, to II's minor-colour-in-major at its start and end.

**Model check.** Dvořák's Largo chorale opens with E major (Tbn 1 G♯4, Tbn 2 B3, bar 1) and reaches V of D♭ through a mixture chord with F♭4 and B♭♭4 (bar 3; Tbn 1 B♭♭4–A♭4 at 3–4 is ♭6̂–5̂ in the top voice). When the chorale returns in the coda (bars 120–123: Tbn 1 D♭4–D4–D♭4–D4–D♭4–E♭4–F4), the remote E-major chord is gone. Only the chromatic neighbour is kept. Movement II likewise does not bring back its remote opening chord (C major = III♯ of A♭) at the end. It keeps only the ♭6̂ colour at 88. This gives the standing decisions (no C-major material, a plain last chord, c210/c480) support from the main model as well as from the cyclic rules.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. The symphony's key plan uses the two mixture chords of this unit, and the III♯ approach follows Points 6 (no change)

**Reading.** §3 (PDF 390): ♭VI is "one of the most important and frequently used chords created by mixture". §7 (PDF 393) and Points 5–6 (PDF 395): III♯ is the usual secondary mixture, often approached so as to avoid the direct ♮5̂–♯5̂.

**Score.** C (I) – A♭ (II, ♭VI) – E (III, III♯) – C (IV) uses exactly these two chords in the home key. At II's end the scherzo's G♯ is approached as A♭ held over, not by G to G♯ (coherence/mvt2.md, "What the scherzo relies on"; Hn 1 G♯4 = the last A♭). That is the Points 6 advice at symphony level. Inside II, bar 1 is III♯ of A♭ going to I at bar 2, the V–III♯–I idea (Points 5c) shortened to III♯–I, with C held on top as the common tone. Earlier cycles recorded this (c460 finding 1, c490). I checked it again against v590's bars 1–2 and 89–92: unchanged.

**Fix.** None. Any change to II's last chord or to the A♭ in it must be reported to the scherzo thread (BRIEF).

### 4. No whole phrase in A♭ minor (Ex. 23-8, Exercise 6): c460's reasons still stand

**Reading.** §5 (PDF 392–393, image 393): a phrase may be cast in the parallel mode, most vividly when it varies a repetition. Exercise 6 (PDF 397) asks for this directly.

**Score.** Theme A is always in major (7–14, 23–30, 71–78). c460 declined an A♭-minor phrase for three reasons: (a) the protected key plan already supplies minor regions (B♭ minor, F♯ minor, A minor); (b) A♭ minor's C♭ = B is the dominant of the scherzo's E and would anticipate its arrival; (c) the one-bar iv at 5 and 88 already uses the same minor colour as a frame (finding 2). The Dvořák check in finding 2 adds a fourth reason: the model does not reprise its remote mixture at the end either. I do not re-raise the point.

**Fix.** None.
