## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Soft accompanying winds have no slurs, so every note is tongued separately: Bsn 1·2 7–14, Bsn 2 45–47, Cl 2 and Bsn 2 71–78 (applied)

**Problem.** Under the legato horn solo at 7–14, the bass clarinet has slurs (added in c078). Bassoons 1 and 2 have none, so the chord's middle voices are articulated note by note while its bass is legato. The repeated notes make this audible. Bsn 1 plays A♭3–A♭3 at 8 and 11–12, and Bsn 2 plays C3–C3 at 7–8 and B♭2–B♭2 at 8. Each one is re-tongued at pp under a dolce solo. The same thing happens in the pp accompaniment at 71–78: Clarinet 2 re-tongues A♭4 at 71–72 and 75–76, and Bassoon 2 doubles the basses note by note. At 45–46 Bassoon 2 strikes B♭2 three times under the oboe's slurred theme. In each of these passages the strings around the winds are slurred to the bow.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 176: "When there are no slurs marked in the music, the notes are tongued or articulated separately. When slurs are present, the player performs all the pitches under or within the slur, on one breath". Ex. VI-5 shows how much this changes the sound. PDF 177, Ex. VI-7: a slur over repeated notes with dashes is "soft tonguing", "similar to slurred staccato and louré on strings", on one breath. This is the right notation for the repeated pitches here. Dvořák 9/ii slurs bassoon I's pp half notes under the cor anglais (bars 19, 22–23).

**Fix (applied; pitches, rhythm, voicing, dynamics and the c036 breaths unchanged):**
- aOne.ily, aOneBsnOne: `es2.(\pp | aes4-- aes4-- g4 | aes2.) | es2 r4 | es4( aes4-- aes4-- | aes2-- des'4 | bes2 g4 | aes2.) |`
- aOne.ily, aOneBsnTwo: `c2.(\pp | c4-- bes,4-- bes,4-- | c2 f4 | bes,2.) | c4( es4 f4 | f2--) r4 | es2.( | c2.) |`
- bSec.ily, bSecBsnTwo bar 45: `bes,1(\pp | bes,2-- bes,2--\< | \after 2. \pp gis,1)\p\> |`
- aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo: `aes'2.(\pp | aes'4-- f'8 bes'8~ \once \stemUp bes'4 | aes'2. | des'2) r4 |` and `aes'2.(\p\< | aes'4-- f'2) | g'2\mp\< r4 | aes'2\f g'4 |`. There is no slur at 77–78: clarinet 1 re-enters there on the shared staff. A slur that crossed the part-combiner's change from solo to automatic would not close (LilyPond warns).
- aTwo.ily, aTwoBsnTwo: `c2.(\pp | f4 bes,4 es4 | aes,2 f4 | bes,2) r4 |` and `aes,4(\p\< c4 des4 | des2-- bes,4 | es2) r4 | fes2(\f es4) |`

On protected material: 7–14 is protected for the horn line and the bassoon layout. Neither changes. c078 added slurs to the bass clarinet in the same passage, and this matches the bassoons to it.

### 2. The bass clarinet part does not warn that the instrument needs the low-C extension (applied)

**Problem.** In the score the staff name reads "Bass clarinet in B♭ (low C)" (c036). In the parts book the title is only "Bass clarinet in B♭". Bar 8's written C3 then appears without warning on page 8 of the parts book. The player is the one who has to bring the right instrument, and the part is where they find out.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 216: "one can reasonably expect every bass clarinet to have a low E♭, but writing all the way to C is still risky, for not all symphony orchestras have instruments with such extensions." PDF 171: the score and the individual parts must state what the player needs.

**Fix (applied).** genparts.py: `("bassCl","Bass clarinet in B♭ (low C)","treble","bes,",False)`. Parts book regenerated and built with 0 warnings. The title now reads "Bass clarinet in B♭ (low C)".

### 3. Clarinet 2 sits on the open throat B♭ (written B♭4 = concert A♭4): held at 60 into the ff of 61, and the main held note of 71–78 and 82 (no change proposed)

**Problem.** c084 moved clarinet 1 off the throat tones at 60–62 and gave its old line to clarinet 2. Clarinet 2 now holds written B♭4 for a whole bar with a crescendo into the ff of 61, and starts 61 on the same note. At 71–78 the same note returns at 71, 72, 73, 75, 76 and 78, and again at 82.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 210, Ex. VII-56: the throat tones are "rather pale". Written B♭ is the note played "with the entire tube open", the last note before the break.

**Why no change.** At both places the note is an inner doubling. At 61 horns 3·4 play A♭4 f; at 71–78 the violas' figure contains A♭4. Adler describes the register as pale, not as unusable, and a professional player will use a resonance fingering. A pitch change at 60–61 would rewrite the protected tutti chord at 61–62 for little audible gain. If the composer ever revises 71–74 for another reason, concert E♭4 (written F4, chalumeau) is the stronger held note under the theme.

### 4. Bars 3–6: clarinet 2 and the bass clarinet re-tongue their pedal notes at every barline (not applied; protected link)

**Problem.** Cl 2 plays `es2. | es2. | fes2. | es2` and the B.cl. plays `aes,2.` four times, with no ties or slurs. That is six tongued attacks at pp in a held wind chord that takes over from the strings. The horns hold their 3–4 tie and then re-strike at 5 and 6, so the pulse is at least consistent between the groups.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 176, as in finding 1.

**Suggestion (the composer's choice; 1–6 is on the standing list).** If the chord is meant to sound as one held sonority, use B.cl. `aes,2.~\pp | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2 r4` (13.8 s, well within one breath) and Cl 2 `es2.~\pp | es2( | fes2. | es2) r4`. Pitches and the horn layout stay as they are.

### 5. Bass clarinet solo 35–36: held throat A♭3, then slurred across the break at pp< (no change)

The solo ends on concert A♭3 (written B♭4, the open throat note), diminuendo to pp. It then rises B♭3–C4 (written B♭4–C5–D5) under one slur, crescendo from pp. That crosses the break exactly. Adler PDF 210 says the break "presents problems to all but the most expert player" and "in the end ... no difficulties whatsoever" for the experienced one. c036 raised the held note and left it, and 31–36 is protected. I agree with leaving it: an orchestral bass clarinettist plays this. I record the break crossing only so that it is not raised again as new.
