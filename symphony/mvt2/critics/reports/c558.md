## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 37, 47, 53: three solo hand-overs end their diminuendo on a rest with no end level (applied)

**Problem.** In three places a solo line hands the music to a new colour and fades as it does, but the hairpin stops on a rest with no dynamic and no niente circle:
- 37, bass clarinet: `des2)\p\> r2\!`. The last note of its 31-37 solo, under Fl 1's p entry of theme B.
- 47, oboe 1: `cis''2.\mf\> r4\!`. The oboe's C♯5 under the poco rit., one beat before the cor anglais enters p with the quotation. Every other staff sounding in 47 has a written end (Bsn "p > pp", Vln II, violas and basses "fp > pp", cellos to pp at 48).
- 53, cor anglais: `f'2\> ... r2\!`. The last note of the movement I quotation, under the violins' p entry of theme B at "a tempo, poco a poco animando".

A player reading any of these parts cannot tell whether to fade out or stop at some level. In all three cases the next line enters at p, so stopping at about p would put two equal colours side by side for a moment instead of one fading under the other.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 148-149: a dynamic change is a crossfade between a small number of defined levels, so a hairpin needs both its levels. PDF 149-150: the change should run continuously through the sound. PDF 153 and 155: the outgoing note overlaps the incoming one so the seam is hidden. That works only if the outgoing note fades to nothing, not to an unknown level. Adler part 2 PDF 50: mark the niente. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: clarinets and the other woodwinds can thin their tone "to a breath". The score already marks fades into rests with a circled tip (Vln I 3, strings 66, Hn 3·4 81 and 90, Hn 1·2 82, C.a. 90, B.tbn 64; c138 and c445). These three are the only remaining solo endings without it. c445 found the oboe case and left it open as optional (HISTORY, cycle 445).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-558/work/sections/bSec.ily).** No pitch, rhythm, MIDI or layout change.
- bSecBassCl, bar 37: `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t des2)\p\> r2\! | R1*7 |`
- bSecOboeOne, bar 47: `| \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t cis''2.\mf\> r4\! |`
- bSecCorAnglais, bar 53: `\once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t f'2\> \unset Staff.midiMinimumVolume \unset Staff.midiMaximumVolume r2\! |`

Oboe 2 rests at 47, so the shared Ob 1·2 staff still has equal simultaneous dynamics. The cor anglais change is a dynamic mark on the last note of the movement I quotation (47 b4-53). Its pitches, rhythm and printed levels are unchanged, but because it touches the quotation the composer should tell the other threads. This closes c445's open item. I checked pages 2-4 at 130 dpi: each circle prints at the end of its hairpin. The oboe's circle sits just left of the cor anglais' "solo, espr." without touching it.

### 2. Bars 53-56: clarinet 1 and horn 1 in unison on the counter-line (checked, no change)

This is the score's only unison of two solo players, which is the case Casanelles PDF 147-148 warns against. c038 put the clarinet one level under the horn (Cl pp<p, Hn pp<mp) so that the horn sound leads and the clarinet only smooths its legato (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 74-75, cited in the source). At 57 the line passes to horn 1, bassoon 1 and the cellos, which is three or more. The unison lasts four bars in a crescendo under the violins, in the same register as the viola triplets. Keep it.

### 3. Bars 57-59: crescendo with a hidden peak, short taper, then f (checked, no change)

The printed shape is p< (57) ... > (58 b4) then f (59), with the mf/mp peak hidden for MIDI (c198). PDF 148-149 asks for defined end levels. Here the start (p) and the goal (f) are printed, and the taper is the suspension's resolution before the breath (A&S PDF 348, cited at c198). c488 protects the printed dynamics at 53-61. No change.

### 4. Fade order at 63-66, 81-83, 89-92 (checked, follows)

### 5. Repeated attacks (checked, follows)

PDF 150-152 describes the "machine gun" effect of an identical attack repeated with nothing changed. The repeated notes in this score are all varied by something: bassoon 1's tenuto repeats at 8 and 10 are soft-tongued under slurs (c126); the violas' sotto voce afterbeats at 48-51 change pitch at 50 and 51; the pizzicato basses at 41 and 43 change pitch on each beat; the viola triplets at 38-39 sit over a changing bass (c428 finding 3).

### 7. Sustained versus decaying sounds (checked, follows)

PDF 290-291 and Fig. 6.6 say a sound held at a steady level is more present than a struck one that decays. The score uses each kind on purpose. Under the two ff tuttis the timpani roll (61, 79-80), so they last as long as the orchestra holds. The coda's motto echo is single strokes left to ring, then damped at 86 (c436). The harp's f arpeggio at 78 decays into the ff at 79 (protected, c244/c344). The last C5 is held at a steady pp by Vln I with marked bow changes until the morendo at 91.
