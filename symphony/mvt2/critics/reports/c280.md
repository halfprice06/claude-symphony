## Findings

### 1. Bars 3–6: hold the wind chord as movement I holds it (applied)

**Problem.** c278 left this open. At 3 the winds take the chord over from the strings: B.cl. A♭2, Cl 2 E♭3, Hn 4 A♭3, Hn 3 C4. Only Hn 3 (C4–D♭4–C4) and Cl 2 (E♭3–F♭3–E♭3) move. Hn 3's C4 is tied 3→4, but in v280 Cl 2 re-strikes its E♭3 at 4, and B.cl. and Hn 4 re-strike their A♭ at 4, 5 and 6. The MIDI does not match either. In the part-combined chord the combiner carries Hn 3's tie onto Hn 4's A♭3, so the MIDI holds A♭3 through 3–4 and re-strikes it at 5 and 6. The c125 `hnFourNoTie` stencil hides that tie in the score, and the Hn 4 part has no tie. Score, part and MIDI therefore give three different versions of bar 4.

**Evidence.**
- Movement I holds every tone of this chord and of its last chord with ties, as described above. Bar 1 continues its last chord by `\repeatTie`.
- Adler ch. 15 "Colouring a note" (PDF 191, Ex. XV-37, "Held C colored") hands the held note from one instrument to the next. Each instrument holds the note, and none re-strikes it.
- PDF 192 says seams should show only where an accent is wanted. Nothing changes at 4, and the re-strikes at 5 and 6 come with the neighbour notes, so they are pulses with no accent intended.
- Ch. 1 (PDF 20) quotes Monteverdi's "unchanging combination" for one mood. It also says (PDF 20, 22) that you must hear what you write and that every nuance is part of the composition, and the three versions of bar 4 fail that test.
- A piano reduction (PDF 23) shows one held A♭ chord with two neighbour notes, so the decision is ties, not a re-pulse.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-280/work/sections/intro.ily):**
```
introHnFour = { r4 c'2~\ppp-\tag #'score _\markup \bold "4." |
  c'2.\< \breathe | aes2.~\pp | aes2.~ | aes2.~ | aes2 r4 | }
introClTwo = { R2.*2 | es2.~\pp | es2. | fes2. | es2 r4 | }
introBassCl = { R2.*2 | aes,2.~\pp | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2 r4 | }
```
The `\once \override Tie.stencil = #hnFourNoTie` is removed from Hn 4. Its definition stays, unused, with a comment. B.cl. and Hn 4 now each hold one note for 11 beats (about 12.7 s at crotchet = 52, pp). Hn 4 breathes at the end of 2 as before. The MIDI now holds Cl 2 E♭3 through 3–4, and B.cl. and Hn 4 A♭ through 3 to 6 beat 2. That is the same as the score and the parts. On the score page the ties print correctly in the chord-mode Hn 3·4 staff (both notes tied 3→4, A♭ alone tied 4→5 and 5→6).

**Kept.** Hn 3's C4 at 3 is a new attack, after Hn 4 has held the C through bar 2. I left it, because it is part of the wind choir's entry under the strings' fade (Ex. XV-36, PDF 190: the second choir enters at p while the first sounds). A tie would need Hn 3 to enter at bar 2 beat 3 while Hn 4 is in its ppp< hairpin, and the shared staff would then carry unequal dynamics. This is inside the protected link (1–6). No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The protection exists for the common-tone pitches, and those are untouched.

### 2. Bars 88–89: Hn 3's A♭3 tied as the violas that double it (applied, optional)

**Problem.** At 87–90 the upper violas double Hn 3's A♭3 (c194). The violas hold A♭3 from 88 to the end with no new attack, following c199's rule of no new attack in 89–92. Under its slur, Hn 3 re-tongues A♭3 at 88 and at 89. At 89 one A♭3 player is therefore struck and its unison partner is held.
