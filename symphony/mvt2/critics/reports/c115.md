## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Harp LH, bars 78–82: the 8 bassa line runs through three full bars of rest and restarts over a rest (applied)
**Problem.** The 8 bassa line from 71 continues without a break to 83. The harp is silent from 78 b3 to 82: bar 78 b3 has its last chord, then 79, 80 and 81 are whole-bar rests (c076/c079 took the harp out under the ff tutti). The line therefore runs over three empty bars. Bar 79 starts a new system, so LilyPond restates the "8" at the left edge of that system, under a whole-bar rest. The first note the sign refers to does not come until 82, a full system later.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 45: continue the line across *short* rests, but "when rests are more extensive, the notes between them take separate octave signs". PDF 44: at the start of a system place the 8 "just before or flush with the first note". PDF 42: the numeral goes "just left of the first note to which it applies". Here the restated sign applies to no note.
**Fix (applied, sections/aTwo.ily, `aTwoHarpL`):** end the line after the 78 chord and start a new one at 82:
```
... <es,, es,>2.\arpeggio | r2 <es,, es,>4\arpeggio \ottava #0 |
R2.*3 |
\ottava #-1 <aes,, es,>2.\p\arpeggio |
```
The written pitches are unchanged and coda.ily's `\ottava #0` still closes the 82–83 line, which now ends with its corner just after the A♭ at 83. The page image shows no line over 79–81, and a new "8" under the 82 chord.

### 2. Harp LH, bar 14: the octave line runs to the barline, not to the notehead (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 14 is one dotted-half chord on beat 1. The 7–14 line goes on to the end of the bar and puts its corner at the barline of 15. That is the end of the duration, not of the notehead. The 83 ending is already correct. Bar 78 is also correct after finding 1.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 44: "Terminate the octave transposition with a corner … immediately after the last notehead(s), including duration dots." The example marked "incorrect" ends the line at the end of the duration, which is what bar 14 does.
**Fix (applied, sections/aOne.ily, `aOneHarpL`):**
```
<es,, es,>2.\arpeggio | << { <aes,, es,>2.\arpeggio } { s4 \ottava #0 s2 } >> |
```
The `\ottava #0` on beat 2 ends the bracket at the next note column. The corner now sits just after the dot of the chord. Pitch and MIDI are unchanged.

### 3. Flutes 1·2, bar 63: three rests where one player rests (applied)
**Problem.** At 63 flute 1 plays its A6 on beat 1, then `r4 r2`. Flute 2 has a whole-bar rest (`R1`). The combined staff prints flute 1's crotchet rest and minim rest high in the stave, and flute 2's semibreve rest low. The silence on beats 2–4 is shown twice, and a reader sees three rests in a bar where only one player has a note.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 51: "Except in strict contrapuntal writing, parts share a rest wherever possible." PDF 50: two sets of rests are only for "two parts on a stave with different durations". PDF 49: when one part sounds, its rests stay centred.
**Fix (applied):** sections/bSec.ily: `bSecFluteTwo` bar 63 becomes `\tag #'score s1 \tag #'part R1`, and `bSecFluteOne` bar 63 becomes `a'''4) \tweak staff-position #0 r4 \tweak staff-position #0 r2`. score.ly: flute 2 is filtered like flute 1, `\pc \removeWithTag #'part \fluteOne \removeWithTag #'part \fluteTwo`, so the flute 2 part keeps its `R1`. I first tried `\partCombineSoloI`. Placed before the A6 it broke the slur from 62 (build warnings). Placed after the A6 it had no effect, so I used the spacer.

### 4. Horns 1·2, bar 70: horn 1's crotchet rest above the stave over a low note (applied)
**Problem.** Horn 2 is tacet 65–74 (`retrHnTwo` is not defined, so it gets full-bar rests). At 70 horn 1 ends its solo on B♭3 below the stave. Its crotchet rest on beat 3 is printed above the top line, as an upper-voice rest, while the only sounding note is on a ledger line below. The rest is on the side away from the music.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 49: rests "remain centred within the stave regardless of the pitches of surrounding notes". PDF 51: a displaced rest belongs "on the same level as surrounding pitches", which here means below the stave, not above it. There is no second sounding part to justify moving it.
**Fix (applied):** sections/retr.ily adds `retrHnTwo = { R2.*5 | \tag #'score s2. \tag #'part R2. | }`, and `retrHnOne` bar 70 becomes `bes2)\pp \tweak staff-position #0 r4 |`. score.ly: `\removeWithTag #'part \hnTwo`. The rest now sits in the middle of the stave and the horn 2 part is unchanged.

### 5. Bassoons 1·2, 57–58: bassoon 1's whole-bar rests above the stave (no change)
These rests are on a ledger line above, and bassoon 2 plays F3–A♭3 at the top of the stave. That is what Gould PDF 51 prescribes: a rest outside the stave is placed "far enough away from the stave to be outside the other part". The rest also tells the player that bassoon 1 waits inside a deliberate `\partCombineApart` (c057). It follows the reading, so leave it.

### 6. Ledger lines throughout (no change)
I checked every staff's extremes in score.midi. Woodwinds and strings stay within Gould's five ledger lines (PDF 47). The most is B♭6 at 79 in Fl 1 and Vln I, on the fifth line, and the item is closed (c025). Brass go to four lines at most (tuba E1), and Gould says brass "should never need octave signs" (PDF 47). The cellos use tenor clef at 44–47 rather than an octave sign (PDF 46). Nothing in this range needs a new octave sign.
