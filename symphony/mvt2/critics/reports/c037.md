## Findings

### 1. Bars 41-44: the cellos double the pizzicato bass while the sustained layer is missing (applied)

**Problem.** At 37-40 the texture is the Dvořák model: flute melody, light viola triplets, pizzicato bass, and a sustained Vln II chord. At 41, when the cor anglais joins, Vln II leaves (c027), and nothing sustains. The cellos spend all eight bars 37-44 in pizzicato octaves with the basses, and the harp LH plays the same notes, so three instruments play one quarter-note bass and no string holds the harmony under the theme's second half.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 209 (Dvořák, Slavonic Dance 8, m. 9): soft melody, light accompaniment and "sustained 'harmonic counterpoint' in the strings". PDF 205 (Brahms, Hungarian Dance 1): "the steady pizzicato bass and the independent cello part". PDF 230 (Schoenberg-Brahms, item 2): sustained chords keep what the sostenuto pedal would hold under an arpeggiated figure. PDF 217/224 (Ravel): the background changes colour with each new melodic entrance. At 41 the melody gains the cor anglais but the background loses a layer and does not change colour.

**Fix (applied, bSec.ily, `bSecVc`).** Cellos arco from 41 with a slow tenor line below the cor anglais (A♭3-B♭4) and inside the register that c027 cleared; basses and harp keep the pizzicato bass:

```
des1(\pp^"arco" | des4 bes,4 c4 des4) | bes,2( es2~ | es2) r2 |
```

and `^"arco"` removed from the cellos' 45 (they are already arco). The bar-42 quarters move in contrary motion to the cor anglais and flute on every beat. They avoid both an E♭3 at beat 2, which made fifths with the cor anglais A♭4-B♭4 (the checker caught this in my first try), and a held D♭ that doubled the violas' seventh. 43 has B♭2 (third of G♭) then E♭3 (fifth of A♭7) through 44. The only new checker line is 42 b1-b2 viola/cello "P8". It comes from sampling the viola arpeggio on an eighth grid: the viola's real succession is F4 to B♭3, so these are not real octaves. This closes the open item "sustained harmony under theme B at 41-44" with strings, not horns, as PDF 209 recommends.

### 2. Bar 80: inner voices repeat quarters in the counter-melody's rhythm (applied)

**Problem.** At the A′ peak the melody holds B♭6 and moves only at 80 b2 (B♭-A♭). The counter-melody (cellos, horns 1·2, bassoon 1: A♭-B♭-D♭) is the one line in quarter notes. In bar 80, though, horns 3·4 (F4/A♭3), the violas' tremolo (F4/A♭4), clarinet 2 (D♭5) and oboe 2 (F5-F5) all re-attack every quarter, so every beat of the bar is struck by the whole middle of the orchestra. The counter-melody's rhythm stops being its own, which is the effect of bar 79 (inner parts held, counter-melody moving).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 230: in Schoenberg's big tutti (mm. 75-77) "the leanness of the texture (essentially two-part) makes the color and the big chords very successful". PDF 217: when accompaniment and melody share the same note values, something must separate them. PDF 231: an added counter-theme has "its own rhythm". The held pitches F, A♭ and D♭ are all in the B♭m7 on beat 3 (bass B♭), so holding them changes no harmony.

**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily).** Bar 80 only:
- `aTwoHnThree`: `f'4 f'4 f'4` to `f'2.`
- `aTwoHnFour`: `aes4 aes4 aes4` to `aes2.`
- `aTwoVla`: `<f' aes'>4:32` ×3 to `<f' aes'>2.:32`
- `aTwoClTwo`: `des''4 des''4 des''4 \breathe` to `des''2. \breathe`
- `aTwoOboeTwo`: `f''4 f''4 bes'4` to `f''2 bes'4` (c024's B♭4 at 80 b3 kept)

Dynamics are unchanged and Hn 3·4 share equal dynamics. This does not reverse c014, c024 or c034: the same pitches are kept, held instead of re-attacked.

### 3. Bars 53-58: the B3 accompaniment repeats B1 note for note and covers the horn counter-line (not applied: protected passage)

**Problem.** The viola triplets at 53-58 have the same pitches as at 37-42, and the cellos at 53-56 play B1's bass in half notes. Adler's models rescore a returning strain (PDF 209: Dvořák's repeat "more open in the distribution"; PDF 213: Bizet's repeat with "slight, but significant changes"). In B3 the horn counter-line (A♭3-A♭4, from 53 b4) lies inside the triplets' register (G♭3-G♭4). c019 noted this and added clarinet 1 to the horn rather than clearing the register.

**Evidence and proposal.** PDF 230 (item 5): Schoenberg gives the arpeggiated triplets "as solid chords, then for one measure ... arpeggiated in order to lead smoothly" into the next passage. PDF 202 (Beethoven 6): the new rhythm enters with the crescendo. For 53-56, violas div. in sustained chords using the triplets' own pitches, for example 53 `<aes des' f'>1\p\<`, 54 `<bes des' ges'>2 <c' es' ges'>2`, 55 `<bes des' ges'>2 <bes des' f'>2`, 56 `<a c' es'>2 <bes des' f'>2`. Then the triplets resume at 57 and become sextuplets at 59. The accompaniment speeds up in three stages through the animando, the horn line is heard over held notes, and B3 no longer sounds like a repeat of B1 underneath. HISTORY lists "viola triplets 37-60 (six per bow)" (cycles 18, 31) as do-not-touch, so I did not apply this. That entry protects the bowing, and the proposal changes 53-56 only.
