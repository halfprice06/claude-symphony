## Findings

None of these needs a change in the score. They are ranked by how useful the record is to the composer and to later critics.

### 1. Theme cadences against Dvořák 9/ii: keep II6/5–ii7–V7–I; do not bring back the beat-3 6/4 (no change)

**Bars:** 12–14, 28–30, 76–78, 80–82.

**Question.** Cycle 12 replaced beat-3 cadential 6/4s at 12/28/76/80 with ii7→V7. A&S 19 §12 (PDF 319–320, Ex. 19-25) describes a metrically weak cadential 6/4 that comes from the bass reaching V early. Should a third-pass form critic ask for it again?

**Evidence.** The main model uses exactly that figure. In Dvořák 9/ii the bass at 17 is D♭–C–B♭–A♭, one note per beat, and beat 4 is A♭–D♭–F, a cadential 6/4 on the weak beat. It goes to V7 at 18 and I at 18 b3. At 38–39 and in the reprise at 112–113 the same descent leads to G♭ (IV with an added 6th) and then D♭. The V is elided and the close is plagal, as in Chopin's Ex. 19-30a (A&S 19 §18, PDF 323). Mozart 41/ii has ten accented cadential 6/4s in 101 bars, at bars 8, 10, 27, 34, 38, 75, 82, 87, 96 and 98 (harmony.csv). In this score the theme cadences are a chain of descending 5ths, II6/5 (D♭ bass) to ii7 (B♭) to V7 (E♭) to I. A&S 16 §1 (PDF 249–250, Ex. 16-1) calls this the most goal-directed progression, and the bass falls to A♭1 at 82.

**Judgement.** Dvořák's figure softens each close and makes two of the three plagal. This movement keeps its plagal close for the end (87–89, a cyclic rule), so its theme cadences can stay authentic. Then the coda's IV6–iv–I is the only plagal close, and it sounds like an ending, not like a repeated phrase ending. The beat-3 6/4 would also break the D♭–B♭–E♭ fifth chain that the hemiola at 80–81 is built on (protected). Keep the cadences as they are. Record in HISTORY: "beat-3 / weak-beat cadential 6/4 at 12/28/76/80 checked against Dvořák 9/ii 17, 38, 112 (c283); do not re-raise."

### 2. The v283 list of 6/4s replaces c133's (record, no change)

A&S's closing rule (PDF 324) is to know what each 6/4 does. My score.midi scan of v283 finds these and no others:
- 5: neighbouring 6/4 = iv over the A♭, bass doubled (19 §4).
- 21 b2: passing I6/4 between IV and the French sixth, with a voice exchange (bass D♭–E♭–F♭ against the solo violin's F–E♭–D) and the bass tone doubled (19 §5, PDF 309). It was added after c133 and follows the book exactly.
- 38 b4 and 43 b2: arpeggio 6/4s of ii where the pizzicato bass rests (19 §13).
- 43 b3: accented cadential 6/4, resolution moved to the cellos (19 §3, §9).
- 68 b3: passing 6/4 over the dominant pedal (19 §6).
- 74 b3: for one eighth, E♭–G over the B♭1 of V4/3. Clarinet 2 breathes on b3 (c036) and the violas' D♭4 enters half a beat later. This is an arpeggiation inside V (19 §13). The 7th is present before and after, so no change.

Since c133, 33 b3 has become ii4/2 over the pedal (the cor anglais's passing B♭ comes with the bass clarinet's D♭ and bassoon 1's F), and 41 b4 is no longer a 6/4 (c267's viola chord). Both changes are fine. Record this list in HISTORY as the current inventory. A new 6/4 should come with a stated function.

### 3. The large-scale plan is a prolongation in the book's terms (record, no change)

The bass plan is A♭ (1–36) – D♭ (37–61) – F♭/E with A minor (62–64) – E♭ (65–70) – A♭ (71–92), that is I – IV – ♭VI – V – I.
- 31–36: I becomes the upper 5th of IV (16 §4, PDF 253), so D♭ at 37 arrives by a descending 5th and needs no V7 of D♭ (c020).
- 37–61: inside IV, each eight-bar phrase falls by 3rds (16 §3, PDF 251). The build 57–60 is the book's long-range passing motion iii–IV–V (16 §8, PDF 257, Ex. 16-2b), F–G♭ … G♭–G–A♭, with the 42/58 fifth chain inside it. The goal D♭ at 61 is the B section's only full close in D♭, and the score reaches it with its loudest and lowest sound (contrabassoon D♭1, c133).
- 62 b3–65: ♭VI is the upper neighbour of V, the main long-span neighbour case (16 §10, PDF 258). The E→E♭ slip is the neighbour's return, not a new key.
- 77–78 and 81: the same neighbour in short form, V–♭VI (deceptive) and back to V7.

This is why the remote keys do not weaken the form: each is a neighbour or a passing chord of a structural one. Keep 61–65 as protected.

### 4. The retransition 65–70 is the book's dominant prolongation (record, no change)

65–66: V7 in the strings, pp to niente. 67–69: horn 1 E♭–D–D♭–C–B♭ over the E♭ pedal with bassoon 1 a third below. Together they make the 8-7-6-5 / 6-5-4-3 progression above a held bass (19 §7, PDF 314–315, Ex. 19-16). The C/A♭ on 68 b3 is a passing 6/4 that does not resolve the 7th (19 §6, PDF 312–313, Ex. 19-14). As in the Mozart example, the 7th comes back (Bsn 2 D♭3 at 69–70) and resolves in its own line to C3 at 71. Six bars (about 21 s) of V after a remote key is in proportion: the V lasts as long as the ♭VI and A minor before it (62–64, about 15 s) plus a bar. No change.

### 5. Coda bass by 3rds against Dvořák's stepwise descent (record, no change)

Dvořák closes with D♭–C–B♭–A♭ | G♭–D♭, with a 6/4 standing for an elided V (19 §18). This coda holds the A♭ pedal for 83–86 and then moves A♭–F–D♭–A♭: IV6 (a 6/3 over the short-span bass neighbour, 16 §10) and iv (5/3) to I. The bass arpeggiates the goal IV before returning (16 §3, PDF 251). Over the pedal, 84–86 are neighbour chords inside the expanded I (16 §9, PDF 257). Both endings are in the reading. This one keeps the D♭–C top line and the F–F♭–E♭ inner line the cyclic rules require. No change.

### 6. Points checked and not raised

- 43 b2–b3: two 6/4s in a row (arpeggio ii6/4, then cadential I6/4). A&S lists only the incomplete-neighbour pair (19 §8, PDF 315). On b1.67 the viola triplet has already sounded D♭, so the b2 chord is heard as ii4/3 going to the cadential 6/4, not as a second 6/4. No change.
- Bar 5's iv6/4 fills a whole bar in the third position of the 3–6 group; Ex. 19-8 allows a neighbouring 6/4 on a strong beat. The link is protected.
- 60 and 81: as in c043 and c133, the protected melody notes C6 and G6 rule out a cadential 6/4.
- Proportions and tension curve (A 83 s, B 117 s, A′ plus coda about 82 s; peaks at 61 and 79) were settled at c013, c023, c103, c133 and c253. Nothing in v283 changes them.
