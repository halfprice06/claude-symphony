## Findings

### F1. Horn 1 has no recovery before its exposed solo at 67 (bars 53–67). Applied.

**Problem.** Horn 1 carries the counter-line from 53 b4 to 60, crescendoing to f. It then takes the top of the ff brass chord at 61–62 (A♭4 | B♭4 C♭5 concert, written E♭5 | F5 G♭5). After 17 s of rest it must start the movement's second exposed solo, p on a held E♭4 with "1. solo" at 67.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 292: "one should allow frequent intervals of rest for the brass players so their lips can recuperate."
- Adler PDF 303: "it is most taxing to play in the higher register all the time, so periodic rests are most welcome."
- Adler PDF 284: controlled soft attacks after the firm, fast-air playing of a high ff are the hard case.
- Adler PDF 303 also gives the remedy: "I and III, High Horns". Horn 3 has rested since bar 6.

**Fix.** Horn 3 takes horn 1's two climax bars; horn 1 rests 61–66, which gives it 26 s instead of 17 s before 67.
- Horn 3's original D♭4/F♭4 is exactly Tpt 3's line, so no pitch leaves the chord.
- Horn 3 rests at 60. Moving its held E♭4 up to A♭4 would make parallel fifths with the tuba's A♭1→D♭2, which I found on the first test build. Its E♭4 at 60 doubled horn 1 and the violas. Horn 4 keeps the p crescendo into 61 (c029).

```
% bSec.ily
bSecHnOne:   ... es'4\< f'4 ges'4 f'4) |
             \partCombineAutomatic R1*4 |
bSecHnThree: R1*24 | aes'1\ff | bes'2 ces''2 | R1*2 |
% retr.ily (the part-combiner stops printing the label after horn 2's "2." at 61)
retrHnOne = { R2.*2 | \set Staff.soloText = "1. solo" es'2.(\p^\markup \bold "1. solo" | ... }
```

This touches the do-not-touch "61–62 brass layout" (c014, c024, c044). That protection exists to keep the pitch layout: woodwinds with or above the trumpets, horn 2's first entry ff at 61. Every sounding pitch and register stays the same; only which horn plays the top line changes. Horn 2 still enters ff at 61.

### F2. Horn 1's 57–60 slur is too long for a loud phrase in a slow tempo (bars 57–60). Applied.

**Problem.** One slur covers 16 beats (15.5 s) that grow from mp to f and on toward the ff. The only breath is at the end.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 292: "In a loud passage one should not phrase too many notes on one breath in a slow tempo, for it does take more breath to play loudly than softly."
- Adler PDF 309: "All phrases under one slur will automatically be performed on one breath."

The cellos that double this line already change bow after 58 (`es2( f4)`), so a breath there matches the doubling.

**Fix.**
```
\partCombineSoloI aes'2(\mp\<^\markup \bold "1." ges'2 | f'4 es'2 f'4) \breathe | ges'2(\f es'2 | es'4\< f'4 ges'4 f'4) |
```

### F3. Trombone 1 and the bass trombone have no breath before the cyclic quotation (bars 60–63). Applied.

**Problem.** Trombone 1 plays 60 (pp<), 61 and 62 at ff, then starts the movement-I quotation ff marcato after one eighth rest. At allargando ♩=50 that rest is 0.6 s. This is the trombone's one important moment.

The bass trombone plays 60–64 (about 23 s) with no rest. It goes straight from the ff F♭2 at 62 to the f A2 that supports the quotation's first beat.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 292 on breath in loud slow playing (as in F2). Adler adler2 PDF 3: the trombone's large mouthpiece makes fast tongue strokes a little harder. Trombone 1's C♭4 at 62 b3 is doubled by horn 4 (`ces'2`), so it can go.

**Fix.**
```
bSecTbnOne:  ... aes1\ff | bes2 r2 |        % then r8 e8-> ... as before
bSecBassTbn: ... des1\ff | ges,2 fes,2 \breathe | a,1\f | ...
```

The quotation itself (63–64) is untouched.

### F4. Horns and trumpets carry key signatures (whole movement). Applied.

**Problem.** The four horn and trumpet staves print A♭ major, D♭ major, F♯ minor and A minor signatures. Transposed parts would inherit four signature changes.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 297–298: "Usually, horns and trumpets are written without key signatures... all the accidentals marked as they occur... Most performers prefer this manner of notation. Trombone and tuba players are used to reading key signatures." In LilyPond, removing Key_engraver also stops the key from cancelling accidentals, so every accidental prints. I checked pages 4, 5 and 7 of the test build: all flats print, and the 3/4 changes at 65 show the time signature only.

**Fix** (score.ly, on the Hn 1·2, Hn 3·4, Tpt 1·2 and Tpt 3 staves):
```
\new Staff = "Horns 1·2" \with { \remove "Key_engraver" instrumentName = ... }
```

The do-not-touch "cautionary signatures at 36, 64" (c045) still holds on every other staff. Brass staves simply have no signature to cancel.

### F5. The mute instruction is missing from horn 4's part, and the mute has no exit (bars 87–92, and movement III). Partly applied.

**Problem.** "con sord." at 87 is attached to horn 3 only. On the shared staff it prints once, but `codaHnFour` does not contain it, so horn 4's extracted part would not have it.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 295: "when muting devices are called for, the instructions to the performer must be explicitly notated in both the parts and the score". PDF 293 makes the same point.

**Fix.** Tag the instruction for the part and remove the tag in the score, the same way the cor anglais part already does:
```
% coda.ily
codaHnFour = { R2.*4 | f2.\pp-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "con sord." | ...
% score.ly
<< \global \pc \hnThree \removeWithTag #'part \hnFour >>
```

Horns 3·4 end the movement muted. Adler PDF 308: "As soon as the mute is to be removed, either the term senza sordino or open must appear." That marking belongs at horns 3·4's first entry in movement III (for the coordinator).
