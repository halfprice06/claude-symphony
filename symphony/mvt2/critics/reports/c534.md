## Findings

The reading is already built into the score, mostly by earlier cycles that cited the same pages (c034 and c414 on PDF 151, c079 on PDF 156, c284 on PDF 147-148). The findings below record what I checked and why I propose no change. None of them overturns a recorded decision. I tested no edit, so /tmp/critic-534/work is an unchanged copy of v534. ### 1. Octave weights at the A′ peak 79-81 follow Mozart 40/iv (PDF 151). No change.

At 79 the theme is in three octaves. B♭6 has Fl 1 and Vln I. B♭5 has Ob 1, Cl 1 and Vln II. B♭4 has the C.a. and violas. The counter-line D♭4-C4-B♭3 is in Hn 1·2 a2, Bsn 1 and Vc. Adler says the listener hears the most heavily scored octave as the tune and the others as "sheen" (PDF 151). Here the middle octave carries the most weight and sits in the strongest register of oboe, clarinet and violin. The B♭6 octave gives brilliance, with no flute left alone, because Vln I doubles it, as PDF 151 asks. At 81 Fl 2 and Ob 2 both leave after F♭ (Barber detail 2, PDF 150), and the instruments still playing all reach the A♭ at 82 in their good registers (PDF 151, "every note ... played effectively by every instrument"). The B♭6 at pitch and the A′ texture are protected, and the reading supports them.

### 2. The 61-62 tutti follows d'Indy, Barber and Smetana together (PDF 147-154). No change.

The brass are marked f under ff woodwinds and strings (row 6), and only Hn 1 and Hn 2 have ff. The low brass move A♭-D♭-G♭-F♭ by fourth, fifth and step, with no octave leaps (row 10, Barber detail 1). Low brass and timpani arrive together on D♭ at 61 b1 (row 13, Barber detail 4). The brass, Fl 1, Ob 1 and Ob 2 hold notes, and the theme-B head A♭-D♭-F-F♭ moves inside them in Fl 2, Vln II, Vc and Bsn 1. Smetana uses held brass in the same way, as glue that adds roundness without weight (PDF 154, Ex. XV-5). The articulation also contrasts as in d'Indy (PDF 148): tenuto half notes in winds and brass, molto sostenuto bowing, and a viola tremolo undercurrent. Fl 1 holds A♭6 alone for six beats before Vln I joins it at 62 b3. Beethoven's high flute octave also stands alone (PDF 152). The brass layout at 61-62 is protected (c264, c454, c517).

### 3. Colour changes come at phrase boundaries, after an anacrusis or a rest (PDF 146, 156). No change.

The middle section changes solo colour often: Fl 1 at 37, C.a. at 41, Ob 1 at 44 b4, C.a. at 47 b4, violins at 52 b4. That could be the "constant flux of color" that Adler warns against on PDF 146. I checked each entry against PDF 156. Every new colour enters on an anacrusis (Ob 1 A♭4 at 44 b4, C.a. C♯4 at 47 b4, violins C5 at 52 b4) or after a rest (Fl 1 at 37 follows the C.a. anacrusis C5-F5 at 36). Each change also falls on a phrase boundary of theme B or on a key change (B♭ minor at 45, F♯ minor at 47). The background stays the same while the solos change: harp and pizzicato basses 37-44, sustained strings 45-52. So the changes read as phrase-by-phrase answers over one background, not as flux. c414 recorded the same result; I confirm it from the score.

### 4. Doubled lines in the chamber passages are blends, not Mahler-style spreading (PDF 158). No change.

PDF 158 describes Mahler's Kindertotenlieder scoring, where no two instruments share a line for more than a few notes. Three places in this score keep a doubling longer than that. Cl 1 and Hn 1 play the counter-line in unison at 53-56. B.cl. and Bsn 2 share the bass at 57-58, with the Cb an octave lower. At 87-90 the C.a. plays F4-F♭4-E♭4 above Hn 4 and the lower violas on F3-F♭3-E♭3. Adler offers the Mahler scoring as one valid option, and on PDF 158-159 he accepts the "fat" late-Romantic sound as well. All three doublings make deliberate blends. At 53-56 the clarinet softens the horn's attack under the strings: Hn 1 reaches mp at 55 and Cl 1 only p, so the horn is the main colour, as row 15 asks. At 57-58 the doubled bass supports a crescendo. At 87-90 muted horn and muted viola blend in the Dvořák manner. 53-56 and 87-90 are protected (c527; muted Hn 3·4 at 87-90). Spreading them out would bring a twentieth-century practice into a Romantic score, so I propose no change.

### 5. The string-free passages and the low entries follow Weber and Tchaikovsky (PDF 154-157). No change.

In 3-14 the clarinet, bass clarinet and horns 3·4 begin before horn 1 enters at 6 b3 (Weber, Ex. XV-7). In 31-36 the bassoon pedal comes before the bass clarinet at 31 b2. At 65-70 the Cb pedal sounds alone for two bars before Hn 1 at 67, and the timpani roll enters with the horn, "near the rim". At 37-44 the woodwind solos sit over pizzicato bass and harp, as in Tchaikovsky's Ex. XV-6, where pizzicato "makes the clarinet sound even warmer". At 52-53 the solo hands over to the violins over sustained low strings, a held bassoon and timpani, which is Tchaikovsky's own next step at m. 340. Bars 7-14, 31-36 and 65-70 are protected.
