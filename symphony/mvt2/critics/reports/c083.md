Each foreign area is entered and left through a chord with two meanings: C♯7 into F♯ minor, G♯7 = A♭7 out of it, F♭ = E at 62. ## Findings

### 1. Bar 64, beat 3: the pivot out of the B section is a root-position diminished triad (applied)

**Problem.** The last chord before the semitone slip into the retransition is D–F–G♯ for one beat at "allargando" (♩ = 40). The pitches are Cb D1, B.tbn D2, Vc D3, Tbn 1 F3 (the quotation's last long note), Vla G♯3 and Vln II D4, which is the top voice there. D appears four times, F once and G♯ once. Heard in A minor, it is vii°7 without its 3rd B. Heard in A♭, it is vii° of V in 5/3 position. The beat dump confirms it is the only root-position diminished triad in the movement.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 243 (§12): diminished triads in 5/3 position "are seldom satisfactory … V6/5 (which contains all three tones of the diminished VII) creates a far more pleasing sonority and is to be preferred". In 15-17 (Corelli), the continuo player "would supply the missing 6th". PDF 243–244 (15-18): if the triad stays, double its 3rd, "the one tone not involved in any dissonant relationship". Here the root is quadrupled. There is a form point too. PDF 231 (15-1): one chord can belong to the old key and also point to the new goal. The complete chord G♯–B–D–F is vii°7 of A minor, and spelled A♭–C♭–D–F it is vii°7 of V in A♭. Completing it gives the end of the B section a real pivot into the E♭ pedal, which is the large-scale chromatic passing tone D between IV and V (Reading note 4).

### 2. Open item "spelling E–G♯–B at 62": close it, no change

**Problem as raised (c072).** Bar 62, beats 3–4, is spelled F♭–A♭–C♭. Since it goes to A minor, c072 asked whether E–G♯–B would be better.

**Answer from the reading.** PDF 231: the G triad in 15-1 "has two meanings", ♮VII of A minor and V of III, and A&S label it in the home key with an arrow to its goal. PDF 242 (15-16, Handel): ♮VII is "at first" heard as V of the tonicised III, and "this initial impression is altered" by what follows. A chord is spelled for the meaning it has when it sounds, and the next key signature carries the reinterpretation. At 62 the chord sounds as ♭III of D♭: G♭ falls to F♭ in the bass, and the winds and brass hold their D♭-major voicing. The A minor signature at 63 then turns it into V. The F♭ spelling also carries the protected F♭→E♭ thread into 65. Keep it.

### 3. Bar 21: D♭ in the bass, then D♮ in the solo violin (no change recommended)

**Problem.** At the approach to the half cadence of the b phrase, the bass holds D♭ for beats 1–2 (IV), then moves to B♭ (V7/V) as Vln I plays D5 on beat 3. The chromatic step D♭→D is split between the outer voices. That is the case 15-8c marks "avoid!" (PDF 236).

**Why I do not propose a change.** Each remedy I tried is worse than the cross-relation:
- Bass D♮ on beat 3 (vii°/V or V6/5 of V) doubles the leading tone with the melody's D5, and both rise to E♭: octaves.
- Bass F for beats 1–2 (vi7) makes 19, 20 and 21 all F minor and turns 20→21 into a full cadence in F minor.
- Bass B♭ for the whole bar turns the C7 of 20 into a V→iv retrogression in F minor.

The D♭ at 21 is the VI of F minor after its V at 20 b3, the move that leads the phrase back to A♭ (c002). The D♭ lies three to four octaves below the D5, in a pp con-sordino texture. Record it as a known exception so later cycles do not churn on it.

### 4. Bars 45–53: B2's phrase rhythm and its return to D♭ (confirm, no change)

**Check.** Exercise 2 (PDF 245–246) is a 3 + 3 + 4 phrase: a tonic expansion, a tonicised second key, then a cadence group led through IV or II6. B2 is that shape, compressed: B♭ minor 45–47, F♯-minor quotation 48–50, and two bars of A♭7 (51–52) into D♭ at 53. The return uses the ♭vii → V7 move of PDF 241 (15-14). B minor at 50 b3 is C♭ minor, ♭vii of D♭. The inflection C♭→C is in the viola, an inner voice, against the bass B, which is the "good" cross-relation of 15-8d. A→A♭ (cor anglais) and D→E♭ (Vln II) move within their own voices. PDF 242 would put a passing IV6 between ♭VII and V7 (bass C♭–B♭–A♭). The bass here falls directly B→A♭, which is enough at "poco sostenuto". The 4̂ that A&S use to prepare the 7th (PDF 242) is supplied by the cor anglais' passing G♭ at 51 b3. Adding it earlier as a held F♯ brings back the fifths that c032 removed (F♯/B → E♭/A♭). The group is short (2 bars, not 4), but its sostenuto tempo gives it about the weight of the 3-bar groups before it. No change.
