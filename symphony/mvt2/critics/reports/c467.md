## Findings

The reading's rules are kept in v467. I found no violation that would justify a change, so I made no edit and recommend none. The points below record what this pass checked against the two new examples and the one rule (the editor's note) that earlier passes did not apply to the bass line.

### 1. Bars 57-60: counter-line as Ex. 90 (follows; no change)

Ex. 90 (PDF 265) is the model for a line doubled in only one octave by a string: the winds take the upper octave and a string doubles the lower (PDF 78). At 57-60 the counter-line is Hn 1 above, Vc + Bsn 1 below. In Ex. 90 the horns sit with the cellos and the clarinets are above. Here the horn is above and the bassoon with the cellos. Both layouts give each octave a wind, and PDF 76 allows mixed wood-and-brass octaves. c087, c187 and c327 kept Hn 1 an octave up because horn and cellos in unison would weaken the horn's top in this register at f. Ex. 90 does not change that. Recorded.

### 2. Bars 57-58 and 72-73: Bsn 2 an octave above the double basses (follows; no change)

The editor's note on PDF 78 says that doubling strings and woodwinds in separate octaves is not to be recommended. At 57-58 (F3-G♭3 | B♭2-E♭3-A♭2-D♭3 over Cb F2-G♭2 | B♭1-E♭2-A♭1-D♭2) and at 72-73 (F3-B♭2-E♭3 | A♭2-F3 over Cb F2-B♭1-E♭2 | A♭1-F1) the bass is Bsn 2 (with the B.cl. in unison at 57-58) over Cb at the octave. The cellos share that octave only in 57 and leave it from 58. The note is about a melody split between Fl over Vn or Ob over Vc. Rimsky's own text a few lines above it gives Vc + Fag / Cb as the right layout for a soft low line. Here the cellos have left the bass for the counter-melody (c164 at 57-58; the cello line at 71), and the winds take the cellos' octave. This is Rimsky's formula with the cello part removed, not the editor's string-over-wind octave. A rest for Bsn 2 at 57-58 was rejected at c157, and c060's Bsn 2 at 72 is protected. Recorded.

### 3. Bars 75-77: three-group counter-melody at p-mp (follows; no change)

PDF 79-80 say a unison of string, wood-wind and brass is preferred for loud passages or a heavy piano. At 75-77 the counter-melody is Vc (mp) + Bsn 1 (p) + Hn 2 (p, then mp) under theme A in Vln I-II octaves with no wind. That is the heavy-piano case, and it gives the counter-melody more mass than the theme for two bars. c217 settled the balance by marking Hn 2 a level under the cellos, and the theme gains Fl 1 and Cl 1 at 77 (row 11). Ex. 96 uses a three-group low line under strings that thin to p, which is the same layering. Recorded.

### 4. Bar 64: Vc + Cb + B.tbn at p (follows; no change)

PDF 79 pairs cellos and basses with trombones "for heavy massive effects" and says the string and the brass stay separately audible. At 64 the bass trombone (A2, then D2-E2) moves with the basses (A1, then D2-E2 in unison) while Tbn 1 finishes the quotation. It is marked p with a diminuendo to niente (c428, c445), so the trombone is heard as a colour at the end of the trombone passage rather than as a massive effect. The quotation and its release are protected. Recorded.

### 5. Bars 79-81: bass in three octaves (follows; no change)

The bass at 79-81 is B.cl. + Bsn 2 on D♭3, Cb on D♭2 and Cbn on D♭1 at ff. Ex. 91 (PDF 268) gives a wind to each upper octave and leaves the basses alone, and Ex. 96 puts the contrabassoon and tuba with the basses. The layout here sits between the two and follows PDF 76-77 (Cb + C-fag., Cb + B.cl., Cb + Fag are all best unisons). Recorded.
