## Findings

### 1. Bar 4: the strings get no advance mute instruction for con sord. at 15 (applied)

**Problem.** Violins I and II, violas and cellos play unmuted in bars 1–3, because movement I's last chord has no mutes. They rest from bar 4 and enter con sord.: Vln II, Vla and Vc at 15, and Vln I (all but the soloist) at 22 b3. Nothing tells them, at the point where they stop, to put the mutes on. The only instruction is the "con sord." printed at the entry. A player counting eleven bars of rest finds it on the bar of the entry, or reads ahead to it. Every other mute change in the movement is marked where the player stops: "(tutti) via sord." at 31 (c075), and Hn 3·4 "mettere sord." at 82 for 87 (c064). The rest at 4–14 is what gives the strings time for the mute. Without the word at 4, that time depends on the player happening to read ahead.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 60: "Enough time must be allowed to put on or take off the mutes… otherwise, chaos may result". Older mutes "need to be placed on the bridge, removed from it, and put away". PDF 59–60 (Ex. II-63, Weber): the mute instruction is printed in the part at the change. MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 5 (PDF 10): a change is marked where the player stops, and the new state is marked at the entry. That is the rule c075 applied at 31 and c064 at 82. HISTORY's earlier mute checks (c106, c168, c186) counted the bars of rest before 15 but never looked for the instruction that starts them.

**Fix (intro.ily, applied in /tmp/critic-208/work).** Text on the bar-4 rest of each muted section. The basses stay senza sord. (c119), so they get nothing:

```lilypond
introVlnOne = { … c''2.\> | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord. (not the solo player)" | R2.*2 | }
introVlnTwo = { … aes'2.\> | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | R2.*2 | }
introVla    = { … es'2.\>  | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | R2.*2 | }
introVc     = { … aes,2.\> | R2.\!^\markup \small \italic "mettere sord." | R2.*2 | }
```

The Vln I wording keeps the leader unmuted for the solo at 17–22. The leader's own cue, "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 24)", is unchanged. In the score the text sits in empty space over the bar-4 rests on page 1, and the layout is unchanged.

### Recorded (no change)

1. **Muted strings at f, bar 28.** Adler PDF 59 names the loud muted passage as a colour of its own ("restraint… constricted, more tense"). The a′ statement's f at 28 under mutes, with the basses unmuted (c119), is that effect. It also keeps the open-string ff for 61 and 79. Keep.
2. **No string harmonics.** I looked for a place where Adler's natural harmonics (PDF 62–66) would help. The final held notes do not work. Vln I C5 is not a violin natural harmonic. Vln II A♭4, Vc A♭2 and Cb A♭1 are not partials of any open string. The silvery colour Adler describes (PDF 66; Ex. II-77) is already in the coda, from the harp harmonics at 83. A string harmonic on the last C5 would also change the protected ending and the C5 link to movement I. Not proposed.
3. **Pizzicato 37–44.** The basses and harp carry the plucked bass with rests every other beat (PDF 57). The viola pizzicato at 41–44 lasts four bars and has hairpins, as in Tchaikovsky 4/iii (Ex. II-61). The changes between arco and pizz. leave a beat at 40 and two beats at 44 (protected, c096 "pizz./arco timing 40–45"). No l.v. is needed: the double bass sustains longest (PDF 58) and the harp rings with it.
4. **String names.** Every sul D/A/G/C is placed where the passage on that string starts and is cancelled at the next change (PDF 64). The "ord." at 27 (violins) follows a text that gave both "con sord." and "sul D" at 22. Players read "ord." as cancelling the string, not the mute, and the mute comes off only at "via sord." 31, so I leave it.
5. **Soloist's mute time 22 b3–23.** About 4.6 s. This was closed at c186 and c196, and I have no new reason to reopen it.

All earlier changes stand. My change adds text only: no pitches, rhythms, dynamics or MIDI change, and nothing at the ending, in the exported themes or in the quotations, so the other movements are not affected.
