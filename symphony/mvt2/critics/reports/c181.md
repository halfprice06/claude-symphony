## Findings

All findings are recorded checks. Following the focus I was given, I checked how each theme prolongs its structural tones, and whether the two climaxes rest on prolonged structural pitches. Both climaxes do. Neither theme has a figuration fault that the reading names and that a change would fix. I recommend no change (v182 = v181).

### F1. Climax 79–81: A♭6 is the structural pitch, prolonged by an enlarged, consonant upper neighbour B♭6 (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 78 b3 – 82. Fl 1, Vln I (B♭6), Ob 1, Cl 1, Vln II (B♭5), Vla (B♭4); bass D♭ (79–80 b2), B♭ (80 b3), E♭ (81).
- **Reading.** The protected grace run ends on A♭6 (78, last 16th), so B♭6 at 79 is reached by step, not by leap. It is therefore not the appoggiatura of 12/28/76 (A&S PDF 338, approached by leap). It is an accented upper neighbour, A♭6–B♭6–A♭6, a displacement of the normal unaccented neighbour (PDF 336, Ex. 20-15), stretched over 1⅓ bars. This is the kind of enlargement A&S show in Ex. 20-11b (PDF 334). The main tone A♭6 returns at 80 b2. At 80 b3 the bass moves to B♭ (Hn 3 F♭), which makes A♭6 a prepared seventh, and it resolves down to G6 over V at 81. The lower line of the theme then completes 5̂–4̂–3̂–2̂–1̂ in eighths to A♭5 at 82.
- **Support.** At 79, B♭ sounds in six parts and A♭ only in Hn 4 (A♭3). The neighbour is heard as a consonant 6th over IV, i.e. as the root of ii6/5, and A&S PDF 341 say a consonant figuration tone "forms a more stable element of the texture than a dissonant one". At the theme's loudest moment the peak is therefore supported as a chord tone, and it moves 6–5 into the A♭ that the earlier statements stated as the resolution. At 12/28/76 the B♭ is a true dissonance (A♭3 in Bsn 1 at 12, in the cellos at 28 and 76). The listener hears the same pitch first as a lean against the harmony and finally as harmony. That is a real gain in the reprise, not a fault.
- **Fix:** none. Do not add A♭ to the upper winds or strings at 79: it would turn the B♭6 back into a dissonance at the cost of the protected V♭9/four-horn layout (c107, c148).

### F2. Climax 61–64: theme B's consequent is a complete 5̂–1̂ descent that lands on D♭6 at 61, with A♭6 re-struck above (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 57–62. Vln I and Fl 1 (unison), Vln II and Ob 1 (octave below).
- **Framework.**
  - The head A♭5–D♭6–F6 arpeggiates I (chordal skips, PDF 329–330).
  - A♭6 at 58 is the head tone, 5̂.
  - The line falls G♭6 (58), F6 (58), E♭6 (59), D♭6 (59 b3).
  - B♭5 at 59 b4 is an incomplete neighbour to the following C6 (PDF 334). It is approached by leap, left by step, and unaccented.
  - C6 (60) is 7̂, held a whole bar, and resolves to D♭6 at 61.
- **Reading.** G♭ is the obligatory passing 4̂ of Ex. 20-8 (PDF 332). F at 59 b2½ is an escape tone decorating the descending second E♭–D♭ (PDF 334–335, Ex. 20-13a). Flute 1 leaves the melody at 61 and re-sounds A♭6 above the arrival, which is polyphonic melody (PDF 330): the head tone stays in the ear while the line reaches 1̂. At the same time Fl 2, Vln II and the cellos state the consequent head in half notes, A♭–D♭–F–F♭. This is an enlargement of the theme's own first bar (PDF 334, Ex. 20-11b), with F♭ in place of G♭ starting the protected F♭→E♭ thread. So the climax rests on D♭6 (1̂), approached through a full descent, with 5̂ held above it for two bars.
- **Fix:** none. The 61–65 route is protected (c113, c180).

### F3. A long-range chromatic ascent A♭6 (58, 61–62) – A♮6 (63) – B♭6 (79) joins the two climaxes (recorded, keep)
- **Bars:** 58 and 61–62 (Fl 1 A♭6; Vln I div. A♭6 at 62 b3), 63 (Fl 1 and Vln I A6, one beat and tremolo), 79 (B♭6).
- **Reading.** PDF 339, Ex. 20-22: a rising chromatic passing tone sharpens the preceding note (A♭–A♮–B♭) and is written as a minor second plus a chromatic step, which is how 62–63 are spelled. PDF 330: a polyphonic line can leave its continuation implied when the context supplies it. In the register above E6 the score sounds only A♭6, then A6, then B♭6, sixteen bars apart, and B♭6 nowhere else (c121). The B section's peak therefore passes on its chromatic step to theme A's peak.
- **Consequence for the composer.** This is a structural reason for the B♭6 reservation, in addition to the dynamic reason c106 and c121 gave. Keep Fl 1's A6 at 63 and do not add an A6 or B♭6 anywhere else.
- **Fix:** none.

### F4. Theme A's figuration is the textbook kind; nothing to change (recorded)
- **Head (7–10, 23–26, 71–74).** A♭–C–E♭ is a chordal skip subdividing the fifth (Ex. 20-2e). The tail E♭–D–D♭–C is not figuration. Each tone is a chord tone of vi7 – V7/V – V7 – I (the prepared 7th E♭, the 3rd D, the 7th D♭, the 3rd C), so the chromatic step is structural. This is why the descending D♮ is not a misspelled chromatic passing tone under PDF 339.
- **Phrase 2 (11–14, 27–30, 75–78).** This is a two-voice melody (PDF 330). The upper voice is B♭–A♭–G, with an appoggiatura that is leap-approached, accented, dissonant and resolved down (PDF 338). The lower voice is F (6̂, upper neighbour of the head's E♭), E♭–D♭–C–B♭–A♭. G's step to A♭ is supplied an octave lower at 14/30/78/82, the situation of Schubert's implied B♭ in Ex. 20-3b.
- **Comparison.** The appoggiatura is the theme's most memorable figure. It is the same device as the "Nimrod" and Tchaikovsky 5/ii horn-theme leans, and it appears four times, in four different settings.
- **Fix:** none.

### F5. Bar 58 drops theme B's neighbour figure; restoring it was considered and rejected (recorded)
- **Bars:** 42 (CA A♭4–B♭4–A♭4–G♭4) against 58 (Vln I/Fl 1 A♭6, dotted, G♭6 anticipated and tied).
- **Problem considered.** PDF 333–334 says the neighbour figure is "often treated motivically". Theme B uses a neighbour in every bar of its antecedent and at 42. At 58, the climax version of the consequent, the figure is replaced by the anticipation c151 accepted.
- **Why no change.**
  - In Vln I and Fl 1, restoring the figure would mean B♭6, which F3 and c106 reserve for 79.
  - In Vln II and Ob 1 only (B♭5), it would sound B♭5 against A♭6 for an eighth, a heterophonic clash at f.
  - Bar 58 as of v152 is protected.
- **Fix:** none.
