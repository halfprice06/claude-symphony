## Findings

The reading's principles are all met or are already settled with a stated reason. I found no new fault that would justify a change, so I applied nothing. The points below are the places where the reading bears most directly on this score, with the evidence that they hold. I list them so that later passes on this chapter can skip them.

**F1. Bars 44 b4-46: Ob 1 over Bsn 1 and cellos an octave lower. Follows Ex. VIII-4 and VIII-38. No change.**
Ob 1 plays theme B's B♭-minor head (D♭5-E♭5-C5-B♭4-A4-D♭5), p, "dolce espr.". Bsn 1 and the cellos (sul D) play the same line an octave lower at pp. Adler gives oboe and bassoon in octaves as one of the full-sounding pairings (PDF 237, Ex. VIII-4), and Brahms 1/ii puts one bassoon an octave under the violin tune to warm it and bring it out over the harmony (PDF 267, Ex. VIII-38). The cello at pitch with the bassoon is the Figaro colour (PDF 241, Ex. VIII-10). The upper octave is one degree louder than the lower, so the oboe leads. The sustained strings (Vln II F4, violas F3-D♭3, Cb B♭1) sit between and below the two octaves and do not cover either.

**F2. Bars 57-60: the flute enters under the violins, and the woodwinds double the strings at pitch. Follows PDF 238 and Ex. VIII-37. Recorded.**
At 57, Fl 1 doubles Vln I at pitch and Ob 1 doubles Vln II, both at p under the violins' mf. This is Schubert's flute that "enters almost undetected ... doubling the oboe for the crescendo" (PDF 238) and Berlioz's flute at pitch with Vln I, which adds warmth "especially as the line goes up" (PDF 266). The line does rise, from A♭5 to A♭6 at 58, and the winds reach f at 59 with the strings. The register is held back: the flute's B♭6 is saved for 79. The one point that partly misses the reading is the Cl 1·2 thirds (D♭5/B♭4) at 57 b3-59 b2. They lie inside the violins' octave, not at the "wide distance" Adler praises in Figaro (PDF 243). c144 and c254 recorded this and protected it: the clarinets hold the harmony that the moving violins leave out, and moving them up an octave would put them above Vln II's line. I agree and do not reopen it.

**F3. Pedals and pickups at 70 b3 and 74 b3. Follows PDF 245, Ex. VIII-12. Recorded.**
Strauss lifts the pedal so the first violins' anacrusis to the restatement is heard. At 74 b3 Cl 2 and the bassoons stop; only the harp's B♭ and the basses' B♭1 hold, under the moving violas and cellos, and the violins' E♭4 pickup is clear. At 70 b3 the bassoons hold G3/D♭3 under the violins' E♭5/E♭4 pickup. They are pp, two and three octaves below the pickup; the timpani roll has stopped at 70 b2 and the horn's B♭3 ends at the barline. Adler also notes that keeping the pedal later "contributes to the tension"; here the held bassoons resolve by step into 71 (c377). c379 examined this join and kept it. I found nothing new.

**F4. Bars 33 and 87-88: chords with a different colour on almost every note. Follows PDF 257, Ex. VIII-24 and VIII-41. No change.**
Adler says a chord with a different timbre on each note is hard to balance unless it is widely spaced, with the largest gaps at the bottom (PDF 257). At 33 b3 the chord is A♭1 (Cbsn), A♭2 (Bsn 2), D♭3 (B.cl), F3 (Bsn 1), B♭3 (C.a.): an octave, then a fourth, a third and a fourth. The widest gap is at the bottom and the five colours belong to one family of dark reeds (Ex. VIII-29, "dark"). At 87-88 the chord is F1, F2 (Cb, Vc), F3/A♭3 (violas with muted Hn 3·4), F4 (C.a.), A♭4 (Vln II), D♭5 (Cl 1). The muted horns doubling the violas is Copland's "quiet doubling" (PDF 270). Copland gives his second violins a clarinet and a muted horn because the violas sit above them; here Vln II is the top string voice with nothing above it except Cl 1, so it needs no doubling. The coda is protected; I record that it agrees with this chapter.

**F5. Bars 61-62 and 79-81: woodwind pair voicings in the two tuttis. Follows Ex. VIII-21 to 23. No change.**
At 61 the pairs are juxtaposed from the top: Fl A♭6/A♭5, Ob F5/D♭5, Cl F5/A♭4, Bsn F3/D♭3, with B.cl D♭3 and Cbsn D♭1 below, so the gaps widen toward the bass (Ex. VIII-24, VIII-27). The one overlap is Ob 1 = Cl 1 on F5, which Adler accepts in a full tutti (PDF 257). At 79 b1-81 b1 Ob 2 (F5, then E5) sits between Cl 1 (B♭5-G5) and Cl 2 (F4, then D♭5). This is the enclosed oboe of Ex. VIII-23, and the clarinets on both sides are in registers where they carry. The melody note B♭6 is in Fl 1 and Vln I, a good register for both (PDF 256). No flute is interlocked below another wind anywhere in the movement, so Ex. VIII-22's weak case never arises.

**F6. Bars 37-44: theme B's first statements in single winds over muted, thin strings. Recorded, not reopened.**
Adler warns that two like winds in unison on a soft solo thicken the tone and block rubato (PDF 249-250), and that a colour kept for one idea clarifies the form (PDF 261). Theme B is a single flute at 37-40 and a single cor anglais, with the flute a sixth above, at 41-44. The flute's lowest notes (C5-B♭4 at 40) lie a third to a fifth above Vln II's sustained B♭4/F4 and D♭4. The strings are pp, sul tasto at 37-40 and pizzicato or held at 41-44, so the flute at p carries. c304 F2 considered Dvořák's flute-and-oboe unison for this tune and did not recommend it; this pass on the same pages gives no reason to change that.
