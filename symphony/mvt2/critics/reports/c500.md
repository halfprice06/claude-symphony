I read every text page from PDF 15 to 36 in full. The chapter has no music examples. `pdfimages -list` finds one image in the range, the "check for updates" badge on PDF 15. I rendered PDF 15, 28 and 31 at 110 dpi to confirm that the text layer is complete and that no notation is missing. The book is about screen music and digital production. Its principles reach a concert score in three ways: through timbre as a carrier of meaning, through the acoustics it defines (harmonic content, loudness, dynamic against volume), and through what it says about mock-ups and multi-team work, which applies to score.midi, score.mp3 and the coherence files.

### 2. Record: the motto tail never returns in the same colour twice in a row (PDF 23, 33, 35)

- **The statements:** Hn 1 (8), Vln I+II (24), B.cl. at movement I's own pitch and instrument (32-34), Hn 1 alone (67-69), Vln I+II in octaves (72), Cl 1 (85-89). That is six statements in five settings. The two repeated colours, Hn 1 and the violins, return only after another colour has come between.
- **Why it matters:** the glossary's round-robin entry (PDF 33) says that the identical sound repeated "over and over" produces "the perception of artificiality". The idea recurs often by design, since it is bar 2 of theme A. The changing colour is what keeps it from sounding mechanical, and the last statement goes to the clarinet, the colour Rimsky gives to a morendo.
- **Keep:** keep this rotation. Do not double the coda tail (85-89) with a violin. That would repeat the colour of 24 and 72 and blur the hand-over of C5 at 89 (c030).

### 3. Record: the two quotations change key and mode but keep what makes them recognisable (PDF 19-20, 27, 35)

- **S head (C.a. 47-50):** movement I's second-theme head in F♯ minor, with its pickup and the rhythm `4 | 2.~ 8 8 | 4 2 4`.
- **P head (Tbn 1, 63-64):** movement I's first-theme head in A minor, with the same rhythm and intervals.
- **Why this works:** PDF 35 (time stretching) says fidelity is higher "when the new pitch is closer to the original". Here the transformation is in pitch level and mode only, and rhythm and contour are kept whole. PDF 19-20 says storytelling reshapes its material so that the audience can imagine the story, and PDF 27 says the model depends on audience comprehension. Each quotation is heard once, as a solo, in one colour.
- **Check:** `--find` finds no second occurrence of either quotation. The finale relies on both being single (cyclic rules, BRIEF).
- **Keep:** do not add a second statement or an imitation of either one.

### 4. Record: both ends are voiced as harmonic series, and the pitches heard on top are the shared ones (PDF 28-31)

- **Bar 1:** C3 C4 E4 G4 C5, the glossary's major chord from the first harmonics.
- **The last chord:** partials 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10 of A♭1, with no added sixth or ninth (c210, c480).
- **What is heard on top:** PDF 31 says low frequencies need more amplitude to sound as loud. At ppp the A♭1/A♭2 basses therefore recede, and C5 (the pitch shared with movement I) and A♭4 (the scherzo's first G♯4) are the notes heard at the top.
- **Keep:** the plain triad, Vln II on A♭4 and Vln I on C5 sul D.

### 5. Record: the render is a draft and cannot judge the joins; dynamics in the print are correct (PDF 18, 30, 32)

- **What the render does:** score.midi re-strikes the `\repeatTie` notes at bar 1. The CC11 fades at 3, 66 and 91 change volume, not dynamic (PDF 30). score.mp3 lasts about 5:50 and ends in silence, not in the scherzo's horn call.
- **The print:** it gives what PDF 30 calls dynamic, the spectral change, through ppp, con sord. and "sul D", which a live orchestra will produce.
- **What follows:** none of this calls for a printed change. A future critic should not judge the softness of either join from the mp3. This repeats c370's note 5 for the fourth series, because the render has not changed.
