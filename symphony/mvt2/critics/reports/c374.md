## Findings

Nothing in this range calls for a printed change. The score already uses most of what Sebesky teaches about mixing winds. The reeds sit below the horn solo at 7–14 and above or in unison with the horns at both tutti. The B.cl, not a bassoon, holds the bottom of the soft horn chord at 3–6. Each choir is complete at 60, 61–62 and 80–81. The C.a. meets clarinet 2 in unison over muted horns at 89, which is the use Sebesky recommends for the instrument (PDF 106, Ex. 130). The three points below are the only places where the score departs from the letter of the reading. In each one the departure has a voice-leading or protection reason. I give a fix for each so the composer can see the cost, but I recommend none of them.

### 1. Bar 81 b2–3: the cor anglais and bassoon 1 stand a second apart (recorded, not applied)

**Problem.** As the ff chord falls away at 81, the C.a. holds E♭4 on beats 2–3, the root of V7. Bassoon 1 holds D♭4, the seventh, a major second below it. Sebesky says unlike woodwinds a second apart "underline and amplify the differences in tonal texture" (PDF 81) and wants at least a third between neighbours.

**Evidence.** Neither note sounds alone. The C.a. E♭4 doubles the violas and horn 3 on theme A's lowest octave. That doubling is protected (c190/c191: "C.a. + Vla at 79–82"). Bassoon 1's D♭4 doubles horns 1·2 and the cellos on the counter-melody, which is also protected (c217, three-group counter-melody 75–82). Sebesky's own way out is to let moving lines hide the seam (PDF 83, Ex. 110), and here both notes belong to moving lines and resolve: D♭4–C4 and E♭4–E♭4 at 82. At p, with string doubling on both pitches, the mismatch in reed colour is masked.

**Fix (not recommended).** Hold the C.a. on G4, in unison with clarinet 2. This is Sebesky's preferred C.a.-plus-clarinet pairing (PDF 106), and it leaves a tritone with bassoon 1. It would break the protected C.a.-viola doubling:

### 2. Bar 58 b4: clarinets 1·2 a second apart (recorded, not applied)

**Problem.** Clarinets 1·2 run in thirds from 57 b3 (D♭5/B♭4, C5/A♭4). At 58 b4 they meet as D♭5 over C♭5 for one beat. Sebesky asks for a third or a tenth "between any two clarinets as much as possible" (PDF 104).

**Evidence.** The C♭5 is the seventh of D♭7 and goes down to B♭4 at 59. Clarinet 1's D♭5 is a common tone tied into 59. Sebesky allows exceptions "for reasons dictated by logical voice leading" (PDF 103), and his "as much as possible" is not absolute. One beat under a crescendo, with the flute, oboe and violins an octave or more above, does not spoil the section sound.

**Fix (not recommended).** Clarinet 2 could hold A♭4 through beat 4 (`aes'2. ces''4` becomes `aes'2.~ aes'4`). The winds would then lose the seventh and its resolution, which the violas carry alone. That is the wrong trade.

### 3. Bars 79–81: horn 3 and horn 4 a major sixth apart, then an octave (recorded, not applied)

**Problem.** On the shared horn 3·4 staff, horn 3 holds F4 over horn 4's A♭3 (a major sixth) at 79–80, then E♭4 over E♭3 (an octave) at 81 b3. Sebesky keeps two horns within a fifth or a minor sixth (PDF 103). On 79 b1 the four horns together sound A♭3–D♭4–F4, a D♭ triad, while the woodwinds and strings sound B♭ minor over D♭. The brass by itself is not the full ii6/5 (PDF 102).

**Evidence.** Sebesky's spacing rule is for a pair of horns alone. Here horns 1·2 sit on D♭4 between horns 3 and 4, so no gap inside the section is wider than a fourth. Horn 4's A♭3–G3 is the seventh resolving at 81. Horn 1·2's line D♭4–C4–B♭3 brings the root B♭ into the horns on beat 3, so the horn section's harmony is completed by its moving line. That is the Ex. 113 note A case, where Sebesky suspends completeness for smooth voice leading (PDF 90). The horns are also marked f under the woodwinds' and strings' ff (recorded as protected at c188), so they are not heard as a separate unit.

**Fix (not recommended).** Horn 4 an octave up (A♭4–G4) would cross horn 3 and double the C.a. and violas. Leave it.
