## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The long-range mixture plan carries the cyclic E thread correctly (no change)

II takes movement I's last bright note, the Picardy E♮ of the C-major chord, and makes it ♭6̂ of A♭. That is the use of two forms of one degree for "juxtaposition and conflict" that PDF 387 describes. Every return of it moves to 5̂ as PDF 388 and point 3 (PDF 395) require:
- bar 2: E♮4→E♭4 in the viola. III♯ goes to I, with the chromatic step in one voice (PDF 393–394).
- bar 5: iv6/4 straight from the tonic, F♭3 as upper neighbour to E♭3 (Ex. 23-2a–b).
- bar 21: French sixth, bass F♭→E♭ at 22.
- 62–65: F♭ major (♭VI of A♭) at 62 b3, respelled E in A minor, then E→E♭ in the basses at 65. This is ♭6̂–5̂ spread over the whole climax and quotation.
- 78: deceptive ♭VI (Madamina, Ex. 23-6), bass F♭–E♭.
- 80–81: F–F♭–E♭ in Hn 3 (II7♭ → V♭9 → V7).
- 87–89: F–F♭–E♭ over IV6–iv–I. This is Ex. 23-2f in the same key.

Bars 3–6 and 87–89 frame the movement with the same plagal ♭6̂ neighbour and the same D♭–C top step. That is the framing device of Ex. 23-1. Movement III then turns E♭ back to E♮ (D♯→E at its 219–220), so the thread runs E♭→E in I, E(F♭)→E♭ in II and E♭→E in III. Keep all of this.

### 2. The handover to movement III, and III's augmented recall (no change in II)

- **Join.** II ends on A♭ major (A♭1, A♭2, E♭3, E♭4, A♭4, C5). III bar 1 keeps A♭–C–E♭ and adds F♯, so II's tonic, heard as ♭VI of C, becomes a German sixth. This is the move of Ex. 23-8, where ♯4̂ added to ♭VI makes an augmented sixth (PDF 392). II's last top note C5 becomes III's C6→B5, and the B rises to C only at III 223. The chain matches the Don Giovanni Picardy model (PDF 391–392, Ex. 23-7): ♭VI, then a borrowed predominant, V and the major tonic.
- **III's chorale answers II's key as mixture.** At III 231–234 the top line runs A–G–A♭–G (♮6̂–5̂–♭6̂–5̂). That is the order PDF 395 point 3 approves. Movement II's tonic note comes back in III's C major as ♭6̂ falling to 5̂.
- **Theme B recall (III cycle 13).** III 211–218 now doubles II's note values at half note = 72. In quarter notes that is 72 against II's 60, about 13.3 s against 16 s for the C.a.'s 41–44. The rhythm matches the C.a. at 41–42 note for note. (II's `ges'4~ ges'8 f'8` and III's `ges''2. f''4` are the same rhythm at double value.) The ending is changed on purpose to D♭–C–B–C. This settles the c160 relay. No action is needed in II.

### 3. Relay to movement III (asked by c162 for cycle 170): close the major-third cycle with secondary mixture

Movements I and II outline C (I) – A♭ (II) – F♭/E (II 62–64). In C major, E major is III♯, the "frequent example of secondary mixture" (PDF 393; PDF 395 point 5). It is used in the arpeggiation I–III♯–V or V–III♯–I and is approached by II6 so that G does not move straight to G♯ (point 6). One I–III♯–V (or V–III♯–I) in III's C-major pages after 223 would close the cycle C–A♭–E–C inside the key. The E-major triad's G♯ and E♮ would also restate the thread in its brightest form. III v19 has no E-major chord: its only G♯ is a chromatic passing note in the counter-line (sHead, Cl 1 and violas, A–G♯–G). This is a suggestion for III's critics only. It must not touch III's protected 223, chorale top lines or 261–263.

### 4. Correction for cyclic-notes: the viola's F♭ at 78 resolves through the bass (no score change)

The c150 relay says "every F♭ in movement II now resolves in its own instrument". That is true at 5, 21, 80–81 and 88–89, but not at 78. There the viola figure `aes(\f fes' aes') fes'( c' g)` leaves F♭4 for C4. 5̂ comes in Bsn 2 and Cb (F♭–E♭) and in harp RH E♭4 on beat 3. The present layout is the correct one under A&S. ♭6̂ does reach 5̂ (PDF 388), and a viola F♭4–E♭4 against the bass F♭–E♭ makes parallel octaves (c070 and c127 tried it and rejected it). Only the relay wording needs to change: "every F♭ moves to E♭; at 78 the bass carries the resolution of the viola's figuration."

### 5. Bar 21: F (Vln II) to F♭ (bass) between adjacent chords (recorded; keep)

At 21 b1–2, Vln II holds F4 in the D♭ chord. On b3 the bass enters on F♭ (E2/E3) for the French sixth while Vln II leaves for B♭3. This is a chromatic succession split between two voices (PDF 394, Ex. 23-12a). It is not between the outer voices: the top is Vln I D5, and the outer-voice pair is what A&S warn about most. There is no clean fix. Vln II F4–F♭4 would double ♭6̂ against the bass and give F♭–E♭ octaves into 22. Moving the bass's F♭ to an upper voice would undo c152's French sixth. Keep it as an accepted inner-voice exception.
