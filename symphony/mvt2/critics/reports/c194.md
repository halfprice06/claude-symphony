## Findings

### 1. Bars 61–62: horn 1 rests at the climax, so one horn part is single; pair it with horn 2 (applied)

**Problem.** The brass chord at the B-section climax uses three horns: Hn 3·4 a2 on A♭4–B♭4–C♭5 and Hn 2 alone on F4–G♭4–A♭4. Horn 1, which has just played the counter-line at f through 60, rests from 61 to 66. Its last note, G♭4 at 60 b3–4, is the seventh of A♭7. Horn 1 stops on it, and its resolution F4 at 61 is played by horn 2 on a fresh attack. The single Hn 2 part carries the brass's only third of the F♭ chord at 62 b3 (A♭4) and the G♭4 that forms the seventh/second against the theme's F4 at 62 b1. Each of those notes has half the horn weight of the Hn 3·4 line above it.

**Evidence.** PDF 102: "If the instruments are mixed the number of horns should be doubled", and under "Writing in several parts": "When the whole group is used the number of horns should be doubled". Every diagram on that page shows "4 Corni" or "2 Cor." on each horn part. PDF 103 gives the one-degree-louder marking for orchestras where "it is impossible for the horns to proceed in pairs". That is not the case here, because four horns are available. PDF 104: duplication with horns "intensifies the tone, and moderates the penetrating timbre" of the trombones beside them. The reason for the rest (c046; Adler part 1 PDF 292, 303) was to rest the lips before the p solo at 67. Adler PDF 303 asks for periodic rests after high-register playing. When c046 was written, horn 1 had the top line (written up to G♭5). The notes it takes now are concert F4–A♭4 (written C5–E♭5), two bars at f. That still leaves 63–66 for rest, about 16.7 s at the printed tempi (63 at ♩=54, 64 allargando 48–42, 65–66 at ♩=52), before a p entry on concert E♭4.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, bSecHnOne).** Add a breath after 60 (as Hn 4, the clarinets and the bass clarinet have) and play 61–62 a2 with horn 2:
```
... | es'4\< f'4 ges'2) \breathe |
\partCombineUnisono f'1--\f | ges'2-- aes'2-- | \partCombineAutomatic R1*2 |
```
This replaces `\partCombineAutomatic R1*4-\tag #'part \! |`. The part-only `\!` is no longer needed because the `\f` ends the crescendo. `\partCombineUnisono` is needed: under the automatic combiner the two F4 whole notes at 61 printed as two separate noteheads. Now the staff prints "a2" with one f, and both players have the same dynamic. The pitches of the chord are unchanged, as are the trombone and Hn 3·4 layout, the dynamics and the 63–64 quotation. This answers c046's reason and does not touch the counter-line 53–60.

### 2. Bars 87–90: the whole viola section doubles muted horn 4 in unison and nothing doubles horn 3; divide the violas over both horns (applied; ending scoring changed, pitches unchanged)

**Problem.** At 87–90 the muted Hn 3·4 hold A♭3 over F3–F♭3–E♭3, the echo of their open 79–81 line (c150). The violas play F3–F♭3–E♭3 unis. sul C, so the full section sits in unison on horn 4's note, pp, and the muted horn's colour is lost inside it. Horn 3's A♭3 has no string partner at all. At 86 the violas are already divided (C4/F4), and at 87 they close into unison only to double one horn.

**Evidence.** PDF 113: "the combination of strings with brass is seldom employed in juxtaposition, crossing, or enclosure of parts", with one named exception: "the splendid effect of horns doubled by divided violas or 'cellos" (Sadko before [34]: Horn + Violas div., Trombones + 'Cellos div.). The point of the divisi is one string half per horn. PDF 107 and 110 support the cor anglais/muted horn pairing, which stays. PDF 51 (ch. I, c184 row 2): in soft passages one string section is roughly equal to one wind. A muted horn is weaker than that, so the full section outweighs it.

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, codaVla):**
```
<f aes>2.\pp^\markup \italic "lower half sul C" | <fes aes>2. | <es aes>2.~^\markup \italic "change bow at will" | <es~ aes>2 es4~^\markup \italic "unis." | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata |
```
This replaces `f2.\pp^\markup \italic "unis., sul C" | fes2. | es2.~... | es2.~ | es2.~\> | es2.\ppp\fermata |`. The lower half keeps c020's F–F♭–E♭ thread on the C string with Hn 4 and the cor anglais (c182's protected line). The upper half doubles Hn 3's A♭3 and joins the lower half on E♭3 at 90 b3, as the horns release. So the last chord is as before: all violas on E♭3. No pitch is added to any chord, because A♭3 is already in Hn 3. Chord notation follows the score's divisi convention (c175, Adler part 1 PDF 31). This answers the protections on 87–92 (c177 spacing, c182 voicing at 87, c188 horn slurs): the spacing, the voicing and the slurs are unchanged, and only the string doubling changes.
