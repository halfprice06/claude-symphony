## Findings

None of these needs a score change. I made no edits, so I did not build a scratch copy. The ranking is by how much each point matters, and each one records a decision.

### 1. Bars 61–62: c194's Hn 1 a2 with Hn 2 (checked; no change)

What changed: horn 1 leaves its counter-line after a breath at the end of 60 and doubles horn 2 on F4 | G♭4–A♭4, f, tenuto.

Against the reading:
- PDF 44: the pair now weighs one trombone, the same as Hn 3·4 a2. The upper brass are two balanced trombone-weights at f over Tbn 2, B.tbn and Tuba at mf.
- PDF 42: F4–A♭4 is the horn's best middle register.
- The line runs on. Horn 1's G♭4 at 60 b3–4, the 7th of V7, resolves to F4 at 61 in the same player, then rises G♭–A♭ against the bass's G♭–F♭ (contrary motion).
- Horn 1 still rests 63–66 before its p solo at 67.

The change follows the reading.

Side effect: at 62 b1–2 the pair's G♭4 lies a semitone above Vc + Bsn 1 F4. The F4 is the arrival note of the augmented theme head A♭–D♭–F–F♭. c167 recorded this semitone with one horn. Doubling the horn doubles its weight, which is a new reason to look at it again under PDF 42 ("the subject comes through clearly").

I tested one alternative on paper: Hn 1·2 `f'1-- | des'2-- aes'2--`, a voice exchange F4/D♭4 with the cellos (A&S's device). I reject it for three reasons:
- It removes the only upper G♭, so the chord at 62 b1 becomes B♭ minor over G♭ and loses the IV-major-7th colour that c014 built into the brass.
- It puts a third D♭4 into the violas' tremolo register.
- The theme's F is also present an octave higher in Vln II, Ob 1 and Cl 1 F5, so the subject is not lost.

The semitone is the chord's own 7th against its root, in the lower-middle register where c167 (Sebesky PDF 139) allows seconds. No change.

### 2. Bars 63–64: Tbn 1's quotation as an independent voice (checked; no change)

The trombone is the only moving line. The strings sustain a non-divided tremolo, sf>p, as in Bartók m. 380 (PDF 47). The bass trombone's sf> is Gershwin's attack that introduces a solo (PDF 50–51, c154). c074 moved the cello tremolo off A3 and c097 moved the cellos' D–E off the F3–E3 resolution, so nothing below or inside the line's E3–C4 span moves with it until 64 b3. At 64 b3–4, Vla G♯3 (sul C) and Vln II B3–D4 (sul G) enter at p with diminuendo. They complete the diminished seventh and V♭9 under the trombone's mf F3–E3 (c083, c096), and the F still resolves 9–8 over the bass E2. The viola takes G♯3 one beat after the trombone's G♯3 neighbour, so it follows the trombone rather than leading it. That does not pre-sound the trombone's note, which A&S PDF 352 forbids. The sul C and sul G at 64–65 are protected. No change.

### 3. Bars 53–56: Cl 1/Hn 1 counter-line (checked; no change)

The line enters pp below a p theme and rises to mp, in its best register (PDF 42–43). The octaves with the theme on the downbeats of 54–56 were closed in c097. At 56 the harp arpeggio (E♭4–G♭4, then F4) crosses above the counter-line's C4–D♭4. The harp is plucked and its notes decay, while the counter-line is sustained and slurred in two instruments, so the line still carries. No change.

### 4. Bar 57: Hn 1 continues on the theme's first note (checked; no change)

At 57 b1 horn 1's A♭4 sounds in unison with the A♭4 attack of Ob 1 and Vln II. On b3, horn 1 and the cellos take G♭, the bass's own note. So for two beats the counter-line is hidden in the theme and the bass, and it becomes independent only from 58. By PDF 42–43 a line should be clear from its entry. Here, though, the line is not entering: it continues from 56 (D♭4 to A♭4), and the bar is inside the protected 53–61 build and the Hn 1 octave at 57–60 (c187). No change.

### 5. Bars 79–81: the a2 counter-melody against the F–F♭–E♭ line (checked; no change)

This bar follows Tchaikovsky's layout (PDF 41) and Bartók's weighting (PDF 44):
- Hn 1·2 a2 with Vc and Bsn 1 carry the moving line.
- Hn 3 (F4–F♭4–E♭4) and Hn 4 (A♭3–G3–E♭3) sustain.
- Hn 3's F♭ entry at 80 b3 carries its own crescendo (PDF 43: the entering note marked above the rest).

Hn 3's F♭4 is the only F♭ in the texture, so the mixture tone is heard in one colour.

The counter-melody reaches B♭3 at 80 b2, one beat before the bass moves to B♭. This is the same rhythm as at 27–30 and 75–78, and it is consonant over D♭. The A♭3 unison with Hn 4 at 80 b1 was closed in c097. Brass dynamics at 79–81 are protected. No change.

### 6. Bars 64–68: dialogue between the trombone and the horn (checked; no change)

The trombone's last step F3–E3 (64) is taken over by the bass's slip E–E♭ at 65. Horn 1 then continues the descent by semitones, E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4 (67–68), over the held E♭. This is the overlapping dialogue of XI-26 (PDF 50), with the gap filled by the strings' pedal. No change.

### 7. Bars 87–90: the muted echo (checked; ending unchanged)

The echo of Hn 3's open F4–F♭4–E♭4 (79–81) is muted Hn 4 an octave lower (F3–F♭3–E♭3), with the cor anglais at the original pitch and the lower violas sul C (c194). Muted horns with a reed is Gershwin's XI-27 colour (PDF 51). The mute changes are all marked (82, 87, 91) and the release ends al niente (90), as PDF 50 and 53–54 require. The closing tail 85–89 over IV6–iv–I stays. I made no change to the ending, its themes or its quotations.
