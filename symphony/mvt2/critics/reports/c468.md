## Findings

### 1. Coda 83–92: the violins and violas end unmuted; mute them (applied)

**Problem.** The strings take their mutes off at 31 and never put them on again. The coda (83–92) is ppp/pp with muted horns 3·4 and the clarinet morendo, but the violins and violas hold their last chord in full tone. The comment above aOneVlnOne (c039) cites Dvořák 9/ii and Tchaikovsky Op. 35/ii for "muted strings in the A section", and c459 called the 15–30 mute plan "the Dvořák 9/ii plan". Both models keep the strings muted to the last bar.

**Evidence.**
- Dvořák 9/ii, corpus MusicXML: "con sord." in all five string parts at bar 4, no "senza" anywhere in the strings, "2 Violini con sordini" and "2 Viole con sordini" at 105, "Tutti [con sord.]" at 112. The strings play ff at 96, under the trombone quotation of movement I, while still muted. The horns, by contrast, are marked "[senza sord.]" at 96. The Mutopia edition agrees: "con sordino" at lines 10, 143, 268, 400, 519, "con sordini" at 116, 240, 370, and no "senza" in strings.notesL.ily.
- Tchaikovsky Op. 35/ii: violins and violas "pp con sordini" at the start (violino1.ily l. 10, violino2.ily l. 12, viola.ily l. 9). The only "senza sordini" is in the finale.
- Adler PDF 59: muting gives "a very soft and smooth sound", and the tone is "radically altered". That is the sound of this ending. PDF 60: allow time for the mutes. The violas have about 7 s (82 b3 to 84 b3), Vln II about 15 s, Vln I about 21 s.

I do not propose muting the whole movement as Dvořák does. The unmuted B section (sul tasto at 37) and the full-toned climaxes at 61 and 79 are this movement's contrast, and the mute plan 15–37 is protected. The coda is where the models agree and where this score is quietest.

**Earlier reasons answered.** c006 left the point open as a colour choice. c030 then closed it with the muted horns, not with the strings. c079's objection was that the cellos and basses are tied from 82 into 83, so they have no time to mute. In this fix they stay unmuted, as at 21–30, where the score already sets unmuted basses under muted upper strings (Rimsky-Korsakov Ex. 125, cited at aOneCb). c459 (Ravel, Adler part 2 PDF 224) praised the ending for returning to the opening string sound. The strings' first statement of the A material (15–30) is muted, so a muted coda returns to that colour. Bar 1 is movement I's chord, and its pitch and string (Vln I C5 sul D) are unchanged. c194/c199/c223 set the coda's pitches and viola divisi, and those are unchanged.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-468/work, text only).**
- sections/coda.ily, codaVlnOne: `R2.^\markup \italic "mettere sord." | R2.*5 | c''2.~\pp\upbow^\markup \italic "con sord., sul D" | …`
- codaVlnTwo: `R2.^\markup \italic "mettere sord." | R2.*3 | aes'2.\pp^\markup \italic "con sord." | …`
- codaVla, 84 b3: `\ppp^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "con sord.," "div., change bow at will" }`. Part-only warning on the 83 rest: `R2.-\tag #'part _\markup \italic "mettere sord."`.
- sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoVla bar 82: `r4\!-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "mettere sord."`. It is score only, because in the part a warning at 82 pushed the viola part to two pages.

The Vln I and Vln II warnings at 83 print only in the parts, because those staves are hidden at 83–86 in the score, like "via sord." at 31. MIDI is unchanged.

**Report to the other threads.** This changes the colour of movement II's ending, but not its pitches. If it is accepted, the scherzo's violins and violas need "senza sord." at their first entry. They have the pause before movement III to take the mutes off.

### 2. Bars 22–25: the soloist rejoins inside a slur, on a tied note (applied)

**Problem.** The part tells the leader "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 24)" (c106), and the score prints "tutti" at 24 b1 (c175). But 24 b1 is the tied continuation of the E♭5 from 23 b3, inside the slur `es''4~( | es''4 d''4)`. The first new bow that a player can join on is 24 b3 (D♭5) or 25 b1, where the slurred group `c''4.( bes'8 aes'4` starts. The leader also has only four beats (22 b3 to 24 b1, about 4.6 s) to fit a mute.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 60: "Enough time must be allowed to put on or take off the mutes … otherwise, chaos may result." Joining at 25 gives seven beats (about 8 s). One violin missing from a unison section for one more bar cannot be heard (c106's own reasoning).

**Fix (applied, sections/aOne.ily, aOneVlnOne).** The part text becomes "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 25)". "tutti" moves from 24 b1 to 25 b1: `… d''4) des''4 | c''4.(-\tweak stencil ##f \mp ^\markup \italic "tutti" bes'8 aes'4 | …`. Text only.

### Recorded (no change)

3. **Muted f at 28 (PDF 59).** Adler names the loud muted sound as a colour of its own ("restraint", "tense"). The unmuted basses at mf balance it (c229). Keep.
4. **Pizzicato (PDF 55–58).** Every pizz./arco change has its own dynamic. The viola pizz. at 41–44 has hairpins, as in Ex. II-61. The bass pizz. at 37–44 is quarters at ♩=60 and does not tire the player. The half notes at 40 and the sf at 63 need no "l.v." because the harp, B.tbn and cellos sustain the pitch (c348). Keep.
5. **String names (PDF 64).** Every "sul" mark is cancelled by "ord." or by the next string name. The cellos' sul D 23–30 runs to the "ord." at 41. That is long, but correct, because they rest from 31. Keep.
6. **No sul ponticello at 61–64 (PDF 52).** The "eerie, glassy" sound is wrong for a Largamente climax, and the ordinario tremolo is right. Keep.
7. **No string harmonics (PDF 62–68).** The harp harmonics at 83 already give the "silvery" colour. A string harmonic would add a second new timbre in the morendo (c348). Keep.
8. **"via sord." at 31 does not print in the score.** The string staves are hidden at 31–36, so the end of the muted passage is not marked where Weber marks it (PDF 59–60). The conductor sees "senza sord." at each re-entry (37, 41, 44), which is enough. Keep.
