## Findings

### 1. Bar 43 b3–b4: the cadential 6/4 has lost its resolution of the 4th (applied)

**Problem.** Bar 43 b3 is the cadential 6/4 of B1's half close in D♭: A♭ in the pizzicato bass and harp, D♭4 (the 4th) in the cor anglais, F5 (the 6th) in flute 1, and E♭3 in the cellos and violas. On b4 the flute's 6th resolves F5→E♭5 correctly. The 4th does not. The cor anglais plays the theme's own line D♭4–B♭3–C4, so the C reaches it only on 44 b1. Until c221 the violas played C4 on 43 b4 and took the resolution. c221 lowered the viola triplets to E♭3–G♭3–A♭3 to keep them under the solo, which was a good change for the register. But now no voice has C on b4. The chord of resolution is A♭–E♭–G♭ with the cor anglais's B♭3, so the dominant has no 3rd. The 4–3 that the accented 6/4 exists to deliver arrives a beat late, on a stronger beat than the 6/4 itself.

**Evidence.**
- A&S 19 §3, PDF 306: in an accented 6/4 "the 6th and 4th, metrically accented, move down by step to the 5th and 3rd of the chord of resolution". The chord resolves "from a stronger to a weaker beat". Here the 3rd comes from b4 to the next downbeat, the wrong metric direction.
- 19 §9, PDF 315 (Ex. 19-19, Beethoven Op. 119/11): when the voice that has the 4th moves elsewhere, its "resolution [is] transferred into the alto". That is the situation here, because the cor anglais has to keep the theme's B♭3.
- 19 §9, PDF 316 (Ex. 19-20): transfer to a lower voice is normal, and a V6/5 colour is acceptable where no strong root-position V–I is needed. Bar 44 is a half cadence, and A♭1 returns on 44 b1.
- c221's reason for dropping the viola C4 was that it sounded "the solo's next note a beat early" in the solo's register (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139). The fix below puts the C an octave and a 9th below the solo's B♭3, where Rimsky-Korsakov says harmony does not cover the voice. c221's viola line stays as it is.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-222/work, `sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecVc` bars 43–44):**
```
% was:  bes,2(\pp es2~ | es2) r4 \clef tenor aes4( ...
bes,2(\pp es4 c4 | es2) r4 \clef tenor aes4( ...
```
The cellos' E♭3 on b3 (the 5th of the 6/4) moves to C3 on b4 and back to E♭3 at 44. Beat 4 is now A♭–C–E♭–G♭ with the cor anglais's B♭3 as a passing 9th. Under the same slur the cellos' line becomes B♭2–E♭3–C3–E♭3. The dynamics, the slur, the c108/c192/c221 viola voicings and the no-D♭ rule at 43 b3 are unchanged. Checks: no new parallels (allpairs_par.py output is identical to v222's), 0 outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt, and no doubled leading tone (C is only in the cellos at 43 b4 and only in the cor anglais at 44 b1).

### 2. Every 6/4 has a function: inventory for v222 (record, no change)

A&S's closing note (PDF 323) asks for the function of every 6/4. The scan of v222 finds these, the same as c133's list plus one transient:
- **5**: neighbouring 6/4 (iv6/4 over the A♭ pedal; 3-4-3 and 5-♭6-5 in 6ths; bass doubled). §4, Ex. 19-7. Protected link.
- **33 b3**: B♭–D♭–F over the A♭ pedal after the common-tone °7. This is a sustained-bass passing chord (§6) that leads back to I at 34. Protected.
- **38 b4, 43 b2**: arpeggio 6/4s of ii, where the pizzicato bass rests and the cellos or violas arpeggiate (§13). The 41 b4 slice is the same artefact: the pizzicato G♭1 dies away under the cellos' D♭3, so the harmony is IV in root position.
- **43 b3**: accented cadential 6/4 (§3, §9). Finding 1.
- **68 b3**: passing 6/4 over the dominant pedal. The 7th resolves later in bassoon 2 (69–71), as in Ex. 19-14.
- **74 b3, first eighth**: B♭–E♭–G inside V4/3, where the viola arpeggio leaves out D♭ for one eighth. It is consonant and dependent on V (§13). Do not "fix" it.

No weak-beat cadential 6/4, no doubled 4th and no augmented-4th 6/4 appear anywhere. I recommend recording this list in HISTORY so that any new 6/4 has to arrive with a stated function.
