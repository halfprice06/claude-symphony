## Findings

The third pass over this reading finds no violation. Every wind melody lies in the register Adler names for its character, no weak register is covered, and every high flute note is approached by step or by run. The items below are ranked by how much they bear on the melodic quality of the movement. None is applied, because each concerns a protected passage and the reading gives no reason to reopen it. Two of them close points that earlier cycles left as observations.

### 1. Theme B's narrow first phrase is the right kind of theme for its register (closes c131 item 2 and c261's open point)

**Bars.** 36–40 (Fl 1), 52–56 (violins).

**Point.** c131 and c261 recorded that theme B's first phrase (pickup C5; F5–G♭5–F5–E♭5 | D♭5–C5–D♭5–E♭5 | G♭5–F5–E♭5–D♭5 | C5–D♭5–C5–B♭4) turns around neighbour notes within a minor sixth and lies in the flute's "sweet, little carrying power" band. Read with the examples, this is not a weakness. Adler's own example of a melody in this register (Tchaikovsky PC1/ii, Ex. VII-5, PDF 186) is a narrow, mostly stepwise tune, and it works because the accompaniment is pizzicato only. Here 37–40 has divided Vln II sul tasto pp, slurred viola triplets pp, and pizzicato basses with harp p on the beat, which is the same kind of plan: nothing in the accompaniment sits above the flute or moves in its register. Adler's clarinet example (Tchaikovsky 5/i, Ex. VII-59, PDF 211) is a theme a listener remembers for its rhythm and its colour, not its range, and theme B's 2 8 8 4 | 4 8 8 2 head works the same way. The movement's model confirms it: Dvořák's Largo theme in the corpus (woods.notesL.ily, bars 7–10, written A♭4–E♭5, concert D♭4–A♭4) spans a fifth in the cor anglais's mellow register and moves by step and third. Theme B's antecedent is as wide as Dvořák's.

**Fix.** None. I recommend the composer record this point as closed and not raise it again.

### 2. Theme B's consequent: the cor anglais's low register is why it is the memorable half (no change)

**Bars.** 41–44 (C.a.), 57–60 (Fl 1, Vln I, Ob 1).

**Point.** The rising A♭–D♭–F arpeggio to G♭ and A♭ starts on written E♭4, inside the cor anglais's "deep, rich, intense" band, and rises into its "mellow, reedy, sonorous" band (Ex. VII-47, PDF 205). Adler says this lowest sixth has "tremendous expressive carrying power". That is the register Dvořák uses for the model theme (PDF 206 lists Dvořák 9/ii mm. 7–18). The flute above at 41–44 is a dynamic level lower (c101) and protected (c090). At 57–60 the same phrase is moved two octaves up into the flute's "clear and brilliant" band (PDF 185) for the build to 61, so the two statements show the two registers in turn, as PDF 184 asks.

**Fix.** None.

### 3. The cor anglais quotation's peak D5 at 49 sits at the top of the mellow band (record only)

**Bars.** 48–49 (C.a., written A5 at 49).

**Point.** The peak of the F♯-minor quotation, D5 (written A5), marked tenuto with a crescendo, is the highest sustained cor anglais note in the movement. In Ex. VII-47 the "mellow, reedy, sonorous" bracket ends at about written A5 and "thin, pinched" begins just above it (PDF 205: "it gets thinner as it gets higher"). The note is therefore at the edge of the band but not past it. Moving the quotation down would change its quoted pitch level, which is a cyclic do-not-touch item (c001, c240), and moving it to the oboe would lose the colour Adler links to the Dvořák model.

**Fix.** None. If the composer ever needs the peak warmer, the only change consistent with the reading is a dynamic one: `d''2--\<` at 49 could become `d''2--\mp\<` so the player does not push the top of the band. I do not recommend it now.

### 4. Flute climaxes: every high note is approached by scale; the A♭6 at 61–63 is long but playable (no change)

**Bars.** 57–63, 77–80 (Fl 1).

**Point.** Adler warns that pitches above A6 are hard and "the best approach is by scale" (PDF 186). A♭6 at 58 is reached by the arpeggio A♭5–D♭6–F6–G♭6; A6 at 63 by a semitone from A♭6; B♭6 at 79 by the run C6–F6–G6–A♭6. The A♭6 at 61–62, slurred into A6 at 63, is nine crotchets at ♩ = 54, about 10 s ff on one breath. Adler's Brahms example (Ex. VII-9, PDF 188) solves a similar strain by dovetailing flute 2, but here flute 2 plays other chord notes (A♭5–D♭6 | F6–F♭6), so a dovetail would take a note out of the chord. Fl 1 breathes at the end of 60 and rests from 63 b2 to 77, which is the rest PDF 188 asks for after a sustained passage.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bass clarinet solo 31–36: the whole solo is in the chalumeau (no change)

**Bars.** 31–36 (B.cl., concert E♭2–E♭3, written F3–F4).

**Point.** The solo carries theme A's arpeggio and the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C. All of it lies in the "first octave and a third" that Adler calls "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective" register (PDF 217), and none of it reaches the thin notes above written G5. The written low C at 8 has a small-note ossia for instruments without the extension, which answers Adler's warning that low C is "still risky" (PDF 216).

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Oboe as doubler at 57–60 and 79–82 (record only; protected)

**Bars.** 57–60, 79–82 (Ob 1).

**Point.** Adler warns that the oboe's "cutting edge" can overshadow a line that should lead (PDF 201). At 57–60 Ob 1 doubles theme B an octave below the flute and violins, in the same octave as the horn 1 and clarinet 1 counter-line (A♭4–G♭5). The two lines meet in unison on A♭4 at 57 b1, D♭5 at 57 b2 and C5 throughout 60. The counter-line is horn plus clarinet at mp< to f, a stronger pair than the oboe at p< to f, and the oboe doubles the theme, which is the significant line. This follows PDF 201. The octave doublings at 57–60 are protected (c259). At 79–82 Ob 1 doubles theme A on B♭5, inside its most effective range (PDF 198).

**Fix.** None.

### 7. Clarinet tail 85–90: breath and niente follow the reading (record only; protected)

**Bars.** 85–92 (Cl 1, Cl 2).

**Point.** Cl 1 takes the motto tail E♭5–D5 | D♭5–C5 in the clarion (written F5–D5), with a niente attack at 85 (Ex. VII-57, PDF 211), and Cl 2 enters niente on written F4 at 89. The breath after 86 divides the tail into two pairs. Adler's rule that a slur is one breath (PDF 187) supports keeping it: 85–90 lasts about 22 s with the rit., more than the c126 breathing plan allows on one breath. The re-attacked D♭5 at 88 inside the slur matches the cor anglais echo F–F♭ (c263, protected).

**Fix.** None.

### 8. Bassoons and contrabassoon keep to Adler's dynamic limits (record only)

**Bars.** 7–14, 31–36, 61–62, 67–70, 75–82.

**Point.** No bassoon note at p or pp lies in the lowest fifth (lowest note E2, ff at 62; PDF 226). The contrabassoon stays in its lowest octave (B♭0–A♭1) and breathes at 33 inside its 30–36 pedal (PDF 230). Bsn 1 under horn 1 at 67–69 (B♭3–G3, pp) is middle register and does not cover the solo.

**Fix.** None.
