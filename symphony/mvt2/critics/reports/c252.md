## Findings

### 1. Bar 11 b2½–b3: bassoon 2 moves in octaves with the horn solo (C3–F3 under C4–F4). Applied

**Problem.** In the horn's second phrase the theme rises A♭3–C4–F4 (11 b1, b2½, b3). Bassoon 2 holds C3 from b1 and leaps to F3 on b3. At b2½ the horn's C4 sits an octave above it, and on b3 both leap a fourth to F, so the accompanying voice moves in parallel octaves with the melody. Bassoon 2 has the third of IV on b3, and the horn has it too.

**Evidence.** A&S forbid parallel octaves between one pair of voices: one part "duplicates the pitch and motion of the other in a different register; this provides some contrast but not enough to give the feeling of two individual voices" (PDF 83, Ex. 5-18, both marked "wrong"). The free-texture exception on PDF 84 covers lines that are doubled for "variety of sound or for emphasis". Bassoon 2 is an independent inner voice that meets the solo for one leap, so the exception does not apply. The texture is thin: five players, string-free, pp under a p solo. PDF 87 point 1 says the fewer the voices, the more any such motion shows, and point 7 says skips in both voices make it worse. Doubling also goes against PDF 76. On IV the root is the stable tone to double, and doubling the third needs a reason. Here the doubled third is what makes the octaves. None of the earlier critics who worked on 7–14 (c004, c019, c057, c141, c219) recorded this. c141's note is about bassoon 1's A♭3, not bassoon 2.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-252/work).** `sections/aOne.ily`, `aOneBsnTwo`, bar 11:

```
  c2( des4 | f2--) r4 | es2.( | c2.) |
```
(was `c2( f4 | f2--) r4 | ...`). On 11 b3 the chord is D♭2 (harp), D♭3 (B.cl., Bsn 2), A♭3 (Bsn 1), F4 (horn). It is complete, with the root doubled and the third only in the melody, as PDF 76 recommends. Bassoon 2 rises D♭3–F3 into bar 12 under the horn's held F4 (oblique motion), so bar 12 is unchanged. On b3 bassoon 2 now joins the bass clarinet's C3–D♭3 step. That is a unison doubling of the bass line for one note, which PDF 85 calls frequent in orchestral music (Ex. 5-24). It is not two independent voices moving together. The scan finds no new fifths or octaves. The horn line, the harp and bassoon 1 are unchanged, so c141's "do not retry" item on bassoon 1 is not reopened.

### 2. Bar 75 b3: clarinet 2's held E♭4 becomes an unresolved ninth over IV and leaves by leap. Applied

**Problem.** c247 changed clarinet 2's first note at 71 and 75 from A♭4 to E♭4, so that it no longer doubles Vln II's A♭4 at the attack. At 71 the E♭4 is held over the I chord for the whole bar, which is fine. At 75 the harmony moves to IV on b3: D♭2/D♭3 bass, F3 in the cellos, bassoon 1 and horn 2, A♭3 in the violas, F5/F6 in the violins. Clarinet 2 still holds E♭4, a ninth above the bass, and then leaps a fourth up to A♭4 on 76 b1. A consonant held tone turns into a dissonance and never resolves. Meanwhile the chord's root D♭ is missing above the bass, and F sounds in five places.

**Evidence.** A&S treat a dissonance as a tone that must be handled differently from chord tones. The seventh "forms a dissonance, so it must never be doubled" (PDF 76). Dissonant leaps "are excluded entirely from the simplest vocal styles" and need a progression that makes them logical (PDF 82). Here no progression justifies the leap: the next chord is IV again. A&S also give the doubling norm for this chord: double "the more stable parts of the triad", usually the root (PDF 76). At 75 b3 the clarinet can supply the root by step. The v247 version (A♭4 held) had no dissonance here; this is a side effect of c247's change, not something the composer chose.

**Fix (applied).** `sections/aTwo.ily`, `aTwoClTwo`, bar 75:

```
  es'2(\p\< des'4 | aes'4-- f'2) | g'2\mp\< r4 | ...
```
(was `es'2.(\p\< | aes'4-- f'2) |`). E♭4 falls by step to D♭4, the root of IV, on 75 b3, then rises to A♭4 at 76. It keeps c247's reason: no A♭4 under Vln II's attack at 75 b1. Against the moving voices on b3 (B.cl./Bsn 2/Cb C→D♭, cellos and horn 2 E♭→F, violins A♭→F) the clarinet moves in contrary motion. The one new coincidence the scan reports is D♭4→A♭4 against the harp LH's arpeggiated D♭2/A♭2 at 76 b1. The A♭2 there is a struck chord filler above the bass D♭2, which A&S say is not a voice (PDF 85, Ex. 5-26). The slur and dynamics are unchanged. Clarinet 1 rests at 75–76, so the shared staff shows the new note as clarinet 2's.

### 3. Bars 11 b1 and 23 b1: hidden octave in the outer voices on theme A's pickup. No change

The pickup E♭–F–A♭ rises a third into the downbeat while the bass moves E♭→A♭ (11: horn F3→A♭3 over harp E♭1→A♭1; 23: violins F4→A♭4 over E♭2→A♭2). A&S say Bach avoids hidden outer octaves "except where the soprano moves by step" (PDF 88, Ex. 5-29). Their other criteria make this case mild. A third is the smallest leap (PDF 81). The texture has five to nine pitches (PDF 87 point 2). There is a common tone, E♭, between V and I (point 6). Avoiding it would mean either changing the theme's pickup or moving the bass at 11/27/75 off the root, and c201 rejected that bass change (`aes,2 des4`, an established accompaniment pattern). Recorded only.

### 4. Bars 41–44 and 49–51: wide spacing above and below the solos. No change

At 41–44 flute 1 lies a tenth (sometimes a twelfth) above the cor anglais for three bars, which is longer than the "one or two chords" PDF 77 allows between soprano and alto. The two lines run mostly in parallel tenths and sixths, which PDF 82 names among "the most useful types of voice leading". The flute is a descant over the cor anglais' tune, so the gap is the space between two melodic lines, not a hole in a chord (c090). At 49–51 there is up to an eleventh between the violas' sotto-voce A3/B3 and the cor anglais' D5–C♯5. That is a soloist over a light accompaniment, the instrumental case where spacing is expressive (PDF 79, Ex. 5-10). Both stay.

### 5. Bars 55–56 and 81: dissonant leaps in inner voices. No change

Bassoon 1 leaps C3→G♭3 (an augmented fourth) at 55→56, and horn 1/clarinet 1 fall G♭4→C4 at the same point. Both leaps go into the diminished seventh A–C–E♭–G♭, and each continues by step (G♭3–F3, C4–D♭4). Clarinet 2's D♭5→G4 at 81 stays inside the V7. PDF 82 says the dominant-seventh family makes such leaps "logical and attractive". They follow the reading.

### 6. Leading tones and sevenths in the passages c182 did not scan. Follows

In 7–14 the leading tone G is single in every V7 (bassoon 1 at 8 b3 and 10 b1, the horn at 13). In 37–44 and 53–56 the leading tone C appears only at 42 b3, in the cellos with a viola triplet note, which is filler (PDF 85). The doubled leading tones at 24 b3 and 72 b3 keep the reason c132 and c163 gave: the violas' G4 is where the seventh A♭4 of V7/V resolves. I do not re-raise them.
