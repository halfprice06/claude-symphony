## Findings

The movement follows this chapter almost everywhere. Of the 40 principles, one departure is new and practical (Finding 1, applied). The other three findings are recorded points. They touch protected passages or recently settled decisions, and I do not ask for changes there.

### 1. Bars 65-70: the basses' E♭2 pedal under horn 1's solo should be played on the A string. Applied.

**Problem.** At 65-70 four solo basses hold E♭2 pp for six bars (about 21 s) under horn 1's p motto tail (67-70) and the timpani's ppp roll. The part does not name a string, so the players will take E♭2 in half position on the D string, which is the normal fingering. That is the brighter of the bass's two pairs of strings. c300 found that this pedal was the loudest line under the solo in score.midi and corrected it with CC11, but the printed part still gives the players nothing to darken or soften the tone.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27: the double bass is "slightly duller on the two lower strings (E and A), and more penetrating on the upper ones (D and G)". The same page notes that the bass is rarely heard by itself except on pedal notes, so this is one of the few places where its string colour can be heard. PDF 26 limits high notes on the lower strings to the octave or ninth of the open string. E♭2 is a tritone above the open A (third position), well inside that limit. PDF 41-42 make the horn the soft, poetic voice that should be on top here. The movement already chooses strings for colour elsewhere (sul D 22 and 70, sul A 52, sul C 64 and 87).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-344/work/sections/retr.ily, retrCb):**
```
retrCb = { es,2.\pp~^\markup \italic \override #'(baseline-skip . 2.4) \column { "4 soli, sul A," "change bow at will" } | ...
```
The text is set as two lines. On one line, "change bow at will" crossed the barline at 66. Nothing needs to cancel it: the next bass note, A♭1 at 71, lies below the A string. Only the text changes. score.midi is byte-identical to v344, and c300's CC11 steps are kept.

### 2. Bar 78 b3: the single harp's f stroke in the tutti cannot be heard. Recorded, not applied (protected by c244).

**Problem.** At 78 b3 the harp plays E♭1-E♭2 (LH) and G3-E♭4 (RH) at f, with the whole orchestra at f and crescendo into the ff at 79. The RH G3 and E♭4 lie among the violas' eighths, the horns' C4, Bsn 1, the cellos and Bsn 2's E♭3.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 47: the harp "is never a very powerful instrument ... At least three, if not four harps in unison are necessary, if they are to be heard against a full orchestra playing forte". The score already applies this at 79-81, where the harp rests. c244 kept the 78 b3 stroke on Casanelles masking grounds. Rimsky gives a separate reason: the stroke is too weak, whatever it masks.

**Optional fix (not applied):** in aTwoHarpR, bar 78, change `r2 <g es'>4\arpeggio` to `R2.`. Keep the LH octave, because its E♭1 is the lowest note on that beat (the contrabassoon rests until 79). The result sounds no different, and the harpist's right hand is free for the 82 pickup. Take it only if the composer reopens c244.

### 3. Bars 35-36: the contrabassoon's pp A♭1 lies an octave under the bass clarinet's last long A♭2. Recorded (woodwind choir 31-36 protected).

**Problem.** The bass-clarinet solo ends on A♭2, diminuendo mp to pp, for four beats, exactly an octave above the contrabassoon's A♭1 pedal at pp.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 38: the double bassoon's very deep notes are "remarkably thick and dense in quality, very powerful in piano passages". Table B (PDF 35) labels its low register "full, rough". An octave below a fading note, the solo's A♭2 will merge into the pedal's upper partial. c149 chose the octave on purpose (to avoid a unison with Bsn 2), and the merging may be the intended dissolve before D♭ arrives at 37.

**Optional fix (not applied):** in transCbsn, bar 35, `aes,,2.~\>` and in 36 `aes,,2\ppp r4`, so the pedal recedes with the solo and the B.cl.'s rising B♭2-C3 at 36 is heard over it. Dynamics only.

### 4. Bar 77 b1: flute 1 alone on G6 at mp. Follows; recorded.

Table B (PDF 33-34) uses G as the flute's register boundary, so G6 is where the very high register begins. PDF 32 says that outside the scope of greatest expression a wind is "more notable for richness of colour than for expression". After c341, flute 1 is alone in the upper octave for one beat, mp<, and then joins Vln I on E♭6. A flute can hold G6 at mp, the beat is short, and the line is going up toward the f at 78. Rimsky gives no reason to reopen c341's settled Vln I register plan, so there is no change.
