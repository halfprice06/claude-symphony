# Critic 421: movement II, v421

Lens 1, melody and memorability. Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration*, ch. II "Melody", the section on melody in the strings with unison and octave doublings (file "principles of form.pdf", PDF 54-63, plus the top of PDF 64, where the paragraph ends), and the examples it cites (Ex. 1-35, PDF 173-214). This is the fifth reading of this chapter in the loop (c011, c101, c191, c261). c261 tested the layouts the chapter offers but the score does not use: cello A-string cantabile, Vln I / Vln II + Vla, and crossing of parts. It rejected each one, with reasons, and I do not reopen them. This time I checked every principle bar by bar against v421 again, including two mechanical scans that earlier reports did by eye. I also asked again whether either theme would be easier to remember with a different string layout.

For a model I checked the string-part markings of Dvořák 9/ii in the corpus (`reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily`: con sordino at the start, "2 violins con sordini" and "2 violas con sordini" for the late return of the Largo tune, lines 116, 240, 370). I did not use the web.

On the lens question: theme A is the tune a listener will hum afterwards. It is a parallel period. Both phrases open with the same rhythm (dotted quarter, eighth, tied quarter), so the listener hears the second phrase coming. The peak of each statement is the B♭ appoggiatura at 12, 28 and 76, and the B♭6 at 79. Like Dvořák's Largo tune, it is heard complete only a few times and is changed by scoring rather than by variation. Theme B works as a contrasting middle tune. The part a listener keeps is its consequent with the rising fourth A♭-D♭ (41, 57), not the turn-figure antecedent. This is the same ranking c261 and c401 reached. The reading supports the string layouts of both themes. Nothing in it calls for a change that would make either theme easier to remember, so I recommend no change.

## Findings

All findings below are confirmations or closed questions. No change is recommended, so none is applied.

### 1. The statement order of both themes matches the Snegourotchka plan (follows; no change)

**Bars.** Theme A: 7-14, 23-30, 71-82. Theme B: 37-44, 45-47, 53-62.

**What the reading says.** PDF 61 (h) describes a melody played "twice *pianissimo*, not doubled, then twice (*mezzo-forte* and *forte*), doubled in the wood-wind". PDF 57 (a): the violins in unison gain "power and richness", and woodwind is usually added later. PDF 58-59: violins in octaves give "expression, fulness of tone and firmness of timbre".

**The score.** Theme A goes from one horn (mp, A♭3) to the violins in unison (mp-f, A♭4, no wind), to the violins in octaves (mp, A♭5/A♭4), then adds Fl 1 and Cl 1 at 77 and reaches ff with B♭6 at 79. Theme B goes from flute alone (p) to C.a. with the flute (p-mp), to Ob 1 + Vc on the head (B♭ minor), to the violins in unison sul A (p-mf, 53-56), and to octaves with Fl/Ob at 57 (f). Each statement is one step louder and doubled one step more than the last. Because of this, a listener knows the tune before it is heavily scored, which helps it stick. Dvořák's Largo does the same, from cor anglais alone to muted strings.

**Fix.** None.

### 2. The violin unisons at 23-30 and 53-56 match Ex. 12 (follows; no change)

**Bars.** 23-30 (sul D, con sord.), 53-56 (sul A, senza sord.).

**What the reading says.** Ex. 12 (Scheherazade iii, PDF 186; I checked the image) gives Vln I + II in unison sul D at p, then moves to the A string, over divided violas and cellos and pp basses, with no wind. PDF 57 notes that some woodwind doubling is usual but not required.

**The score.** Both unison statements have no wind, which is the Ex. 12 layout. The string choice also follows the example: the darker D string for the first, muted statement of theme A, and the A string for theme B's first string statement, which leads into the build. c191 and c261 confirmed this, and the string colour plan is protected (c411).

**Fix.** None.

### 3. Every string line above F♯5 is doubled an octave lower or is a short solo (follows; no change)

**Bars.** 17-18, 21 (solo Vln, A♭5-B♭5); 27-28 (unison, B♭5 for two beats); 57-62 (A♭6 at 58 and 61); 71-74 (theme A at A♭5-F6); 75-76 (unison, B♭5); 77 b2-82 (B♭6 at 79).

**What the reading says.** PDF 62, Note I: melodies "exceeding the middle of the 5th octave are generally doubled an octave below". PDF 58-59: the E string loses fulness "the higher it ascends from the limits of the soprano voice", and a high line is "too isolated" unless doubled.

**The score.** Every sustained line above F♯5 is in octaves: Vln I over Vln II at 57-62, 71-74 and 77-82, with Fl 1 on the top octave at 57-60 and 77-81. The undoubled notes above F♯5 are the solo violin's A♭5-B♭5 at 17-18 and 21 (a soloist over muted strings, protected) and the B♭5 appoggiaturas at 28 and 76 (two beats each). B♭5 is at the top of the soprano range, the limit PDF 58-59 names, not above it. c299 deliberately left 75-76 at pitch so that B♭6 is first heard at 79, and the reading does not override that.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. The cello cantabile uses the D string and bassoons, as in Antar [63] (follows; question closed)

**Bars.** 23-30, 44 b4-47, 57-60, 71-80.

**What the reading says.** PDF 56: cello cantabile is "usually" on the A string. Antar [63] gives "the same melody in D♭ maj. on the D string (doubled by the bassoons)". Ex. 7 (PDF 180) gives a broad melody to the cellos alone, dolce, later doubled by Vln I.

**The score.** The theme B head at 45-47 is Vc sul D + Bsn 1 under Ob 1, which is the Antar [63] layout in a key close to Antar's D♭. The A′ counter-melody at 75-80 is Vc + Bsn 1 + Hn 2. Both follow the book's second option. c261 (Finding 1) showed that moving the A′ counter-melody up to the A string would cross Vln II's lower octave of theme A at 73-78, and asked that it not be reopened on PDF 56 alone. I found nothing new to answer that.

**Fix.** None. Do not reopen.

### 5. Viola phrases, double-bass phrases and divided octaves (follows; no change)

**Bars.** Vla 15-21 and 87-88; Cb 63-64, 65-70, 87-88; all divided Vla, Vc and Cb passages.

**What the reading says.** PDF 55: the viola suits "short characteristic phrases", and its melodies are generally doubled. PDF 56: the double bass has no "phrase of any importance" without cellos or bassoons. PDF 61 (f): the bass part may be simpler than the cello part. PDF 62, Note II: no octaves inside one divided section of violas, cellos or basses.

**The score.** The violas at 15-21 answer in one-bar phrases. Their one melodic line in the coda, F-F♭-E♭ at 87-88, is doubled by Hn 4 con sord. and C.a. The basses never have a phrase alone: 65-70 and 83-92 are pedals, 87-88 is F-D♭ in octaves with the cellos and Bsn 2, and at 63-64 the bass is a simplified version of the cellos' line, as PDF 61 (f) allows. A scan of every chord in every Vla, Vc and Cb variable in the seven section files found no octaves.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Ex. 26 and Dvořák's solo strings for the last statement of the tune (not applicable; recorded)

**Bars.** 83-92.

**What the reading says.** PDF 60 and Ex. 26: at pianissimo, two solo violins on the upper octave are "sufficiently prominent", with the rest of Vln I and a flute below. Dvořák 9/ii returns its tune near the end in solo muted strings (corpus strings.notesL.ily, lines 116, 240, 370).

**The score.** The coda does not restate theme A. It gives the harp the head (83), the timpani's mirror (84-85), and Cl 1 the motto tail (85-89). Vln I takes over the C5 at 89 and holds it, so the movement ends on the pitch it began with. A solo-string statement here would add a fourth full appearance of theme A after the second peak. It would also change the protected coda, which c263 said must not be reopened. A whole statement does not fit in 83-92 either. I record this only so a later critic does not raise Ex. 26 for the coda.

**Fix.** None.
