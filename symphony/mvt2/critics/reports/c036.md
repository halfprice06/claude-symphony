## What else I studied

I read the full v36 score files and HISTORY.md. I wrote a MIDI test file with one staff per wind player (no part-combining) and measured every unbroken stretch of sound against the tempo map. The figures below are wall-clock seconds at the printed tempi. Existing `\breathe` marks are counted as breaks. I checked each wind part's range against Adler's charts by pitch. ## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Sustained wind accompaniment with no breathing places: 7–14, 53–62, 71–82
**Problem.** In three passages the accompanying winds have no rest, no slur break and no breath mark for 28–43 seconds. In 7–14 bassoons 1, 2 and the bass clarinet carry the whole accompaniment under the horn for 27.7 s at ♩=52. The strings are silent and all three move in the same rhythm, so any breath leaves a hole in the chord. In 53–62, Bsn 2 plays for 40.3 s and Cl 1 for 37.3 s without a rest, crescendo into ff. In 71–82, Cl 1, Cl 2 and Bsn 2 each play 43.3 s without a rest (pp chords, then the ff peak).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 176 says slurs and rests are the breath plan, and the composer writes them. PDF 175 says only the oboe can play long stretches. PDF 188 says the flute and the other winds need time to rest, and Brahms dovetails long notes so a player can breathe. PDF 230 asks for periodic rests in the low winds. In this score the flute, first bassoon and horns already breathe at 80, which is the right practice; it is just missing elsewhere.
**Fix (applied).** I staggered the breaths so the harmony never empties, and I used beats where another instrument holds the same pitch:
- aOne.ily: Bsn 1 bar 10 `es2 r4`; B.cl. bar 10 `es,2 r4` (Bsn 2's B♭ and the harp hold the chord under the horn's pickup); Bsn 2 bar 12 `f2 r4` (the repeated F is dropped). The horn line and the voicing are unchanged (do-not-touch 7–14 respected).
- bSec.ily: Bsn 2 bar 56 `a,2 bes,4 r4` (cellos and basses keep the B♭); `\breathe` after 56 in Cl 1 and Hn 1 at their slur end.
- aTwo.ily: Cl 1 `bes'2 r4` at 74 and `aes'2\f r4` at 78. Cl 2 `g'2 r4` at 74 and 77. Bsn 2 `es2 r4` at 74 and 77, with the double basses holding the bass. `\breathe` after 80 in Cl 1, Cl 2, Bsn 2, B.cl., Ob 1 and Ob 2, matching the commas already in Fl 1, Bsn 1 and Hn 1·2. The A′ doublings and pitches are unchanged.

### 2. Flute 1 and the upper winds have no breath before the climax at 61, and Fl 1 has none at 40/41
**Problem.** Fl 1 plays 37–44 straight through (31 s). The slur ends on `bes'2` at 40 and the next starts on the downbeat of 41, with no rest. At 57–62, Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1·2, B.cl., Bsn 1·2 and Hn 1 cross from the end of 60 (`c'''1\<` and so on) into the ff downbeat of 61 without a breath (about 24 s for Fl 1 and Ob 1). For the flute this is the top register, crescendo to ff.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: "the flute requires a great deal of breath ... give the flutist time to rest". PDF 176: one slur is one breath. PDF 192: a second flute can relieve the first in sustained passages, and here Fl 2 is idle until 61.
**Fix (applied).** `\breathe` after bar 40 in Fl 1 (bSecFluteOne, where the theme's two halves meet). `\breathe` at the end of 60 in Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1, Cl 2, B.cl., Bsn 1, Bsn 2 and Hn 1. The strings sustain through it, so the music does not stop. An alternative, not applied, is the Brahms dovetail: Fl 2 enters on Fl 1's C6 at 60 (`c'''1\<`) and Fl 1 drops out on beat 3. That needs matched dynamics on the shared staff, which is why I did not choose it.

### 3. Flute 1 leaps C6 to B♭6 at the A′ peak (78–79) while the violins approach by scale
**Problem.** `aes''2)\f c'''4( | bes'''2.~\ff`. The flute leaps a minor seventh to B♭6 at ff. Vln I and II, playing the same line, get the grace run F–G–A♭ into B♭ (`\afterGrace c'''4 { f'''16 g''' aes''' }`).
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 186: above A6 "all pitches are difficult to produce, and extreme caution must be used in approaching them. The best approach is by scale." The same score already does this at 58, where A♭6–B♭6 is approached by step.
**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily).** `aes''2)\f \afterGrace c'''4( { f'''16 g''' aes''' } | bes'''2.~\ff`. This is the violins' run, so the flute is in unison with them.

### 4. pp in the double reeds' lowest fifth: Ob 1 at 52–53, cor anglais at 32–34
**Problem.** Ob 1 ends the passage after the quotation with `aes'2(\> ges'2 | f'2. ges'4)\pp | f'2`, which is pp on F4/G♭4. The cor anglais accompaniment at 32–34, `c'2.(\pp | c'4 b4 bes4 | aes2.)`, sounds C4–A♭3 (written G4–E♭4), mostly in its lowest fifth, at pp.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 198: in the oboe's lowest fifth "one should never require a pianissimo dynamic in this register, even from the best players". PDF 205: the cor anglais has the same register properties as the oboe.
**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily: Ob 1 `cis''2(\mp b'4 a'4)` at 50, so the existing diminuendo at 51 lands on `\p` at 52, not pp. The quotation 47–49 is untouched. trans.ily: cor anglais `c'2.(\p` at 32. It stays below the mp bass clarinet solo; bassoon 1 stays pp.

### 5. Clarinet transpositions are not named in the score
**Problem.** The staves read "Clarinets 1·2" and "Bass clarinet (low C)". A C score still has to name the instrument's key so the parts are prepared correctly.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 171: for the clarinets "the interval of transposition must be clearly marked in the score and in the individual parts". PDF 174 and 210: flat keys call for B♭ clarinets. PDF 216–217: bass clarinet in B♭, French (treble) notation for parts.
**Fix (applied, score.ly).** `instrumentName = \markup \center-column { "Clarinets 1·2" \small "in B♭" }` and `\small "in B♭ (low C)"` under "Bass clarinet". The layout stays at 8 pages.

### 6. One low-C-extension note in the bass clarinet (bar 8) — open point "BCl low B♭1 at 8"
**Problem.** The bass clarinet's B♭1 on 8 b2 (written C3) is its only note below the standard low E♭ (concert D♭2). That single quarter note at pp forces the orchestra to supply a low-C instrument.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 216: "one can reasonably expect every bass clarinet to have a low E♭, but writing all the way to C is still risky, for not all symphony orchestras have instruments with such extensions." The staff label shows the composer knows the note needs the extension. Adler's point is that one note should not require it.
**Fix (not applied; the composer's choice).** Keep the root-position V7/V (cycle 2) and take the bass clarinet up the octave: aOneBassCl bar 8 `f,4 bes,4 es,4`. The harp LH (8 bassa) still sounds the low B♭. B.cl. and Bsn 2 then share B♭2 on beat 2, which is a root doubling. If the low octave matters more than ease of hiring, keep the note and leave the label.

### 7. Smaller points, no change proposed
- The bass clarinet solo ends 35–36 on a held concert A♭3, which is written B♭4, the open throat note. Adler's chart (PDF 210) calls this note "rather pale", and it is the least resonant held note on the instrument. It is playable for a professional (PDF 210: the break "presents no difficulties whatsoever" for an experienced player), and the line needs that register into 37, so I leave it.
- Clarinet 2's E♭4 held 89–92 into the fermata is about 22 s on one breath. At ppp morendo this is within a clarinettist's control (PDF 211, niente), and the coda is protected.
- Outside the reading, for the composer's note: horn 1 plays 53 b4–62 without a rest (37 s). The comma at 56 (finding 1) and the one at 60 (finding 2) give it two breaths.
