## Findings

Every Unit 29 principle that applies to this score is already followed, and most are handled the way the textbook does it. The places where an augmented sixth could be added (43, 50-51, 59, 73-74, 78, 80, the coda) were each tested by earlier critics and blocked for stated reasons that the reading does not overturn. I found no change that would bring the score closer to a master's work. The findings below are checks recorded against the book. I applied none of them in the scratch copy. With no file changed, I did not rebuild the score or the parts book.

### 1. Bars 20-22: the French sixth is the reading's model (record, no change)
**Bars.** 20 b3 C7 → 21 b1 D♭ (IV7 of A♭, deceptive VI of F minor) → 21 b2 E♭ 6/4 → 21 b3 French sixth → 22 V.
**Evidence.** At 21 b3 the notes are F♭2 (Cb), F♭3 and A♭3 (Vc div.), A♭3 (Vla), B♭3 (Vln II) and D5 (Vln I). The French sixth contains 2̂ (B♭), and Vln II holds B♭3 into 22. As PDF 507 says, this leaves no parallels to worry about. The sixth F♭2–D5 opens to the octave E♭2–E♭5 (PDF 503, Ex. 29-2). A♭3 in the cellos falls to G3. The approach comes after a deceptive move to VI (PDF 513: "augmented 6ths frequently follow deceptive cadences to VI"). The bass E♭–F♭–E♭ makes the chord a neighbour of V (PDF 513, §12). It marks the end of the b phrase just before the reprise at 23 (PDF 503: "just before important structural points"). The viola's A♭3 doubles the cellos' A♭3 and then drops out at 22. This is a free orchestral doubling, not a voice-leading fault.
**Fix.** None.

### 2. Bar 64 b4 → 65: the German sixth uses the anticipation of Ex. 29-11 and contracts to the 7th (record, protected)
**Evidence.** 64 b4 has E2 (Cb, B.tbn), B2 (Vc), F3 then E3 (Tbn), A♭3 (Vla) and D4 (Vln II). On 4.5 the cellos anticipate B♭2, so the last eighth sounds E–A♭–B♭–D: the momentary French sixth the book gives as "a very frequent way of avoiding 5ths with the German 6th" (PDF 507). Without it, Cb E2 and Vc B2 would move in fifths to E♭2–B♭2. At 65 the sixth E2–D4 goes to E♭2–D♭4, a seventh rather than an octave. PDF 515 (§15) allows this and prefers the 7th in an inner voice. Here D♭4 is the highest string note of the pp chord. That is the less usual case, but it is the *Einsamkeit* pattern (PDF 525-526) and the movement's E/F♭→E♭ semitone thread, protected since c211/c233. The trombone's E3 doubles the bass F♭ on 4.5 and then stops, so the doubled ♭6̂ never moves in octaves with the bass.
**Fix.** None.

### 3. Bars 50-51: the return from F♯ minor (record; c210 and c372 answered, nothing new)
**Evidence.** PDF 516-517 (§17, Ex. 29-28) makes the return from a tonicized subdominant through an augmented sixth the standard plan, and F♯ minor is G♭ minor, iv of D♭. Every augmented sixth of D♭ needs B𝄫 (A) in the bass. The cor anglais' protected quotation already moves A4→A♭4 on 50 b4–51, so an A in the bass would give parallel octaves (PDF 505: wrong doublings lead to octaves). I also tested the ♯IV7 form (§19, Ex. 29-31b) on paper: Cb and Vc G♮ on 50 b4, Vln II D♭5, violas F♭4 on 4.5, C.a. A4. It resolves cleanly (G→A♭, B𝄫→A♭, F♭→E♭, D♭→E♭). But it replaces c312's iv7, puts a leap of a major 3rd down plus a chromatic rise into the bass under the quotation's last bar, and uses a chord the reading calls rarer (PDF 519). c372 gave the same reasons, and the reading adds nothing against them. The B1–A♭1 bass with the C.a.'s A→A♭ already carries ♭6̂–5̂ of D♭.
**Fix.** None. This keeps the 50-51 entry on the do-not-touch list.

### 4. Bar 59: vii°7 of V over ♯4̂ is the reading's "close relative" of ♯IV7 (record, no change)
**Evidence.** 59 b3 V6/5 of V (G E♭ B♭ D♭) → b4 G B♭ F♭ → 60 cadential 6/4 → V7 → 61 D♭. The bass G♭–G♮–A♭ is 4̂–♯4̂–5̂. PDF 519 says of ♯IV7: "note the similarity to VII°7 of V". The only difference is B♭ against B𝄫, and B♭5 is the protected first-violin line at 59 b4. This is the applied-chord route of Ex. 29-16 without the ♭6̂ added. It is the right weight for the D♭ climax, which then has its sixth at 64 on the larger return.
**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bars 79-81: the A′ cadence uses iiø7 with F♭ rather than a sixth from II (record, no change)
**Evidence.** At 80 b3 the chord is B♭ D♭ F♭ A♭ over B♭0/B♭1 (Cbn, Cb). F♭ is in Fl 2, Ob 2, Hn 3 and the violas, and is held over the barline as an appoggiatura on 81 b1, resolving to E♭ on b2. Ex. 29-15 (Haydn Op. 76/1, PDF 509) shows II carried into a sixth on ♭6̂. Doing that here would need the bass on F♭ and D♮ against Cl 2's D♭5. That would remove the bass fifth B♭→E♭ that anchors the movement's loudest cadence, and the upper F♭→E♭ appoggiaturas would then sound in octaves with the bass. The present chord keeps ♭6̂ as an upper-voice tone resolving down, which PDF 503 lists as the defining motion of the family. c232 kept 80 for the same reason.
**Fix.** None.

### 6. Bars 43 and 78: the two other candidate places stay as recorded (no change)
**Evidence.** 43: the cadential 6/4 at b3 could be preceded by a German sixth on B𝄫 (Ex. 29-13). The ♯4̂ (G♮) would clash with Fl 1's G♭5 on b1-b2, and Fl 1's notes at 41-44 are protected (c090, c101, c511). 78: augmented sixths often follow a deceptive ♭VI (PDF 513). But a sixth at 78 b3 would make the E♭ there sound like V two bars before the real cadence (PDF 503: the sixth's goal is heard as a dominant). c232's answer stands.
**Fix.** None.

### 7. Coda: no augmented sixth on ♭2̂ (record, by decision)
**Evidence.** PDF 522 (§22) shows a sixth on ♭2̂ as a neighbour of a final major tonic. The coda does this job plagally: ♯4̂ D♮ over the pedal at 86, IV6 at 87, iv (F♭) at 88, I. c310/c312 chose this, the plagal close is protected, and coherence/mvt2.md keeps the last A♭ chord plain for the finale's German sixth.
**Fix.** None.
