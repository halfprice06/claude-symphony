## Findings

### 1. Bar 84: the violas attack the timpani's own pitches at the moment the timpani answer (applied)

**Problem.** The coda's timpani answer E♭3–C3–A♭2 (84–85) is one of the movement's planned melodic moments for the timpani. On the same downbeat the violas enter div. on C4/E♭4, ppp. So the timpani's first two notes are attacked together with a sustained chord made of the same two pitch classes an octave higher. The answer is no longer "heard alone", which is what c019 and c040 intended when they moved it, and the viola chord blurs a three-note motif into a held E♭–C sonority.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 156: "An anacrusis or a rest before a certain color enters are two excellent ways of introducing new elements and making sure they are clearly perceived." PDF 158 (Mahler): no two instruments have the same part for more than a few notes; here the timpani's E♭ and C coincide with the viola notes. PDF 154: the background should be distinct from the foreground. c063 moved the violas from 83 to 84 so that the harp harmonics at 83 would sound over the low A♭ alone. That reason applies equally to the timpani at 84.

**Fix (applied).** `coda.ily`, `codaVla`, bar 84: `r2 <c' es'>4~\ppp^\markup \italic "div., change bow at will" | <c' es'>2. |`. The violas enter on beat 3, as the timpani reach A♭2. E♭3 and C3 now sound over the ppp A♭ pedal alone. The violas' E♭4 is still sounding under the clarinet's niente E♭5 at 85, so c041's entry is unchanged, and c063's reason (harmonics alone at 83) is kept.

### 2. Bars 44–47: the cello octave outweighs oboe 1 in theme B's minor head (applied)

**Problem.** c041 gave theme B's B♭-minor head to oboe 1 so that this statement has the oboe's colour. The lower octave is the whole cello section, on the D string from 44 b4, plus bassoon 1. The cellos have the same marks as the oboe (p, crescendo to mf at 47). One oboe at p against a cello section at p in its intense tenor register, reinforced by a bassoon, makes the lower octave the heavier one. The listener hears a cello statement with oboe colour on top.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 150–151 (Mozart 40/iv, Ex. XV-3): the octave that has the heaviest scoring is the one the listener takes to be the theme, and the other octaves are "simply for sheen and brilliance". Mozart chose his distribution so that the gesture "sound[s] as written". The cellos are still needed for the octave (c011, c087), but they should be scored one level lower.

**Fix (applied).** `bSec.ily`, `bSecVc`: 44 b4 `aes4(\pp^\markup \italic "sul D"` (was `\p`), and 47 `cis'2.\mp\>` (was `\mf`). Oboe 1 and bassoon 1 are unchanged. c043's swell to the C♯7 is kept: the oboe still reaches mf and the cellos mp.

### 3. Bar 77: the lower octave enters louder than the upper one (applied)

**Problem.** At 77 flute 1 joins Vln I on the theme's upper octave and clarinet 1 joins Vln II on the lower octave. c079 timed the two entries together so that each octave gains one wind. c123 then lowered flute 1's entry from mf< to p<, but clarinet 1 still enters mf<. It has to match clarinet 2's mf, because they share a staff. The lower octave (Vln II plus clarinet in the clarion register at mf) now enters louder than the upper octave (Vln I plus flute at p), which is the imbalance c067 and c079 removed.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 151: the most heavily scored octave is heard as the theme. PDF 150 (Barber): the doubling grows a little at a time. c123's reason for p (a new timbre enters softly inside a crescendo, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127) applies to the clarinet as much as to the flute.

**Fix (applied).** `aTwo.ily`: clarinet 1 at 77 `g''4(\mp\<` and clarinet 2 at 77 `g'2\mp\<` (both were mf; the shared staff keeps one mark). Clarinet 2's crescendo from 75 p now reaches mp at 77 and f at 78, as before. Flute 1 stays p<.

### 4. Bars 37–60: the B-section background changes every four bars (not applied; a limit, not a change)

**Problem.** In 24 bars the accompaniment takes seven forms: arco viola triplets under Vln II chords (37), pizzicato triplets (41), sustained strings (45), off-beat violas (48), harp triplets (53), viola triplets again (57) and sextuplets (59). Each change has an accepted reason (c049, c057, c107 and others), and the section is protected (c117). Taken together, though, the background changes at the same rate as the foreground (flute, cor anglais, oboe, cor anglais, violins, tutti).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 146 warns against "such a great variety of different effects that the structure of the work is put in jeopardy by the constant flux of color and texture". In the model movement, Dvořák keeps one background for a whole phrase. In 9/ii's middle section the violas play tremolo for about eight bars, rest, and then play tremolo for another eight. The basses play a walking pizzicato for ten bars (`strings.notesL.ily`, `violamL` and `bassmL`, the C♯-minor section).

**Recommendation.** No change now, because each change answers a register or balance problem. But no further accompaniment changes should be added to 37–60. If one of these backgrounds is ever revised, the revision should merge two four-bar units rather than create a new figure.

### 5. Bar 61, beats 1–2: the head's top octave is in flute 2 alone (no change)

At 61 flute 1 holds A♭6 (c059, c070) and Vln I holds D♭6 (the leading tone's resolution, c039). So the first note of the augmented head, A♭5, is in flute 2 alone, in its weak middle register, under the ff tutti. c034's fix (Vln I following the head) was later replaced for those reasons. By Adler's Mozart test (PDF 151) the head at 61 is carried by the middle octave (Vln II, Cl 2, with horns 3·4 on A♭4) and the cello/bassoon octave, and at 62 it is in three octaves with six instruments. The listener therefore hears the head, and flute 2's A♭5 is harmless sheen. The tutti chord is protected (c114), and I propose no change.
