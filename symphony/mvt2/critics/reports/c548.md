## What else I studied

This is the fifth expression pass on this reading (c028, c128, c268, c418). I read c418's report and the HISTORY entries from cycle 419 to 547 so as not to repeat them, and checked each expression-related change made since v419 (cellos, basses and B.tbn diminuendo at 64 into pp at 65; violins' mp on the 74 b3 pickup; Bsn 1 pp < p at 54-55; Cl 2 pp at 90; the coda mutes; Cb A1-B♭1 at 56). I looked at all eight score pages at 75 dpi and pages 2, 4, 5 and 7 at 150 dpi, and listed by eye every entry after a rest: each carries its own dynamic, or continues a phrase that already has one (Bsn 1 at 35, Vln I/II at 53 and 71, Cb at 41). For support I checked the local Dvořák 9/ii Largo (reference-corpus/mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/brass.notesL.ily lines 68, 103, 155, 200; strings.notesL.ily lines 196, 326). Dvořák's peak is ff, never fff. He holds the ff for a bar and then diminuendos to p or pp across the next bar, and string tremolo goes ff > to mf. This score does the same at 63-64 (sf > p) and 81-82 (ff > p).

## Findings (ranked)

I found nothing in this reading that the score violates. The points below record what I checked and why I recommend no change. I applied nothing in /tmp/critic-548/work and so did not run a build. v548's own build-warnings.txt is empty and its analysis.txt reports 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 1. 61-64, main climax and quotation (follows; no change)

61 b1 prints the top dynamic in every staff (Ex. XI-21). The trumpets are held back for the whole movement, and the trombones are held back until 60 (Ex. XI-19). The strings play "molto sostenuto" with tenuti, and the winds carry only their dynamic, as in Franck (Ex. XI-20). At 63 the tutti breaks off. Tbn 1 brings in the quotation ff marcato, with accents on its long notes, over a sforzando built as PDF 189-190 recommends: Cb pizz. sf and Fl 1 short sf against string tremolo sf > p. The quotation then tapers with > to p at 64, so the pp at 65 does not leave an exposed seam (PDF 190, Ex. XV-36). Low brass, timpani and horns 3·4 at f under ff are a closed balance (c218, c264, line 210 "timpani at 79 stay f"). Tchaikovsky prints fff for everyone, but I do not reopen that balance. This movement's top level is ff, as in Dvořák 9/ii.

### 2. 79-82, second peak and release (follows; no change)

c418's "molto sostenuto" in all five string parts at 79 is in place. Hn 3·4 swell on the ♭9 at 80 b3-81 with a hidden ff target, and are printed as f < > niente. Ex. XV-36 prints its ff trumpet chord the same way, with a swell and no intermediate level, so the visible notation is standard. The release at 81 is a one-bar diminuendo from ff to p in every part. The double reeds, Vln II, violas and horns go to niente at 82, while the flutes, clarinets, bassoons, Vln I and cellos reach pp under the harp's p entry at 82 b2 (staged release protected since c168; Ex. XV-40 dovetail).

### 3. Solos, dynamics and words (follows; no change)

Every solo has a dynamic and a word chosen for its register (PDF 188): Hn 1 p "dolce espr." at 6; B.cl. p "solo, espr." at 31; C.a. p "dolce" at 32; Fl 1 p "dolce cantabile" at 37; C.a. p "solo, dolce cantabile" at 41; Ob 1 p "dolce espr." at 44; C.a. p "solo, espr." at 47; Hn 1 pp "espr." at 53; Hn 1 p "dolce, 1. solo" at 67; Vln I p "espr." at 70; Cl 1 niente < pp "dolce" at 85. Each phrase high point has its own < > (8-9, 37-39, 41-42, 47-50, 53-55, 67-68), as in the Berlioz and Franck examples.

### 4. Held notes, overlaps and new colours (follows; no change)

The C at 1-3, the E♭ at 65-70 and the C5 at 89 are passed on with overlaps at pp (Ex. XV-37). Each new colour enters below the current level and grows: Cbn and Hn 3·4 pp under the strings' p > pp at 29-30; Cb p < under the cellos' mf at 56 (c527, new since c418); woodwinds p and mp < under the violins at 57 and 77; brass pp < at 60; Cl 1 from niente at 85. This is Schoenberg's "sneaking in" (PDF 187) and Adler's overlap rule (PDF 190, 192).

### 5. Tempo words (follows; no change)

Each tempo change is printed with its MIDI step in global.ily: poco rit. 35, Poco più mosso 37, poco rit. / poco sostenuto 47-48, a tempo poco a poco animando 53, poco allargando 60, Largamente 61, allargando 64, Tempo I 65, poco allargando 78, a tempo 83, rit. 89. The broadening comes after the quotation's head rather than before it (c153), as in the Tchaikovsky example, where the tutti and the new element arrive in tempo. The reading gives no reason to add a tempo word. c269's "no new tempo words" also still stands.
