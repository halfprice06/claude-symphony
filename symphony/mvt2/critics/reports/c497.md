### 1. Bars 57-58: strings alone leave a 19-24-semitone hole between the violas and Vln II (recorded, follows in the full score)
- Observation. gap.py on the strings alone: 57 b3 G♭2 G♭3 B♭3 | F5 F6 (19 semitones), 58 b1 B♭1 F3 A♭3 | A♭5 A♭6 (24), 58 b3-4 A♭1/D♭2 E♭3 A♭3 | G♭5 G♭6 (22). Ex. V-26 (PDF 151) keeps the upper gaps smaller than the lower ones.
- Why it stays. Adler's chords on PDF 150-151 are for strings alone; here the hole is filled by the winds. In the full score, 57 b3 is G♭2 G♭3 B♭3 G♭4 B♭4 D♭5 F5 F6 and 58 b1 is B♭1 B♭2 F3 A♭3 F4 B♭4 D♭5 A♭5 A♭6: Cl 1·2 (B♭4-D♭5), Hn 1 (A♭4-E♭4) and the bassoons fill the middle, and from 59 the violas' sextuplets reach E♭4-G♭4. This is Adler's point 9 at work: the theme sits in violin octaves with flute and oboe, the middle is the horn's counter-line, and the background is figuration. gap.py on the full orchestra does not flag 57-60. Keep.

### 2. Bars 48-51: on the beat, nothing sounds between the bass and the cor anglais (recorded, protected)
- Observation. 49 b3 is B1 B2 | D5 G5 on the beat (27 semitones), 50 b1 G♭2 A2 | D♭5 G♭5 (28). The middle register is filled only by the violas' off-beat portato eighths, sotto voce.
- Why it stays. This is the protected quotation (47-53). The thin, low-dynamic accompaniment is the case on PDF 154 (Tchaikovsky mm. 9-16): a thin texture "cannot interfere" with the tune. Vln II's line above the cor anglais at 48-50 was recorded by c488. Keep.

### 3. Bars 30 and 89-92: the basses stay on the tonic instead of dropping out (recorded, follows)
- Observation. Tchaikovsky's diminuendo (PDF 154, mm. 29-36) drops the basses each time they reach the tonic. At 30 the basses go down to A♭1 under p>pp, and at 89-92 they hold A♭1 to the end.
- Why it stays. At 30 the basses' A♭1 is the hand-over to the contrabassoon pedal that carries the woodwind choir at 31 (c063, Adler part 2 PDF 190). At 89-92 the A♭ is the plagal close's bass and the pitch the scherzo's G♯ takes up (protected). The full Grieg piece that Adler cites as Ex. V-30 supports the same choice. In aasesdeath.ly (contrabassi.ly) the basses rest for eight bars (25-32) after the ff climax and come back pp for the close. They end on the tonic B held "morendo" (fis1 to b2 ppp, then b1 with a fermata), while the cellos hold F♯. This score does the same: basses rest under the dialogues at 15-20 and 53-56 and hold the tonic at the close. Keep.

### 5. Section order: where a lower section sounds above a higher one (recorded, follows)
cross.py finds four places. (a) 45-47: the cellos' B♭-minor head (D♭4-E♭4) above the violas' F3-B3. This is the bass-register melody of PDF 154, with the violas as the thin background. (b) 62: cellos on theme B's head (F4-E4) at the climax above the violas' tremolo. Adler's point 12 (high cellos, tense) applies. (c) 73-78: the cello counter-melody (C4, D♭4, B3) crosses the violas' broken-chord eighths for one or two beats at a time. The figuration stays below on average, and the counter-melody is the middleground line (PDF 155). (d) 39 b3: the violas' triplet (B♭3-E♭4-G♭4) passes above Vln II's lower half (C4) inside a pp sul tasto pad, and both hold the same chord tones. PDF 150 puts melodic considerations next to spacing, and none of these crossings hides a line. Keep.

### 7. Final chord 89-92: spaced as the overtone series (recorded, do not touch)

The gaps are 12, 7, 5, 7, 5, 4 semitones, narrowing upward, and the third appears only at the top. This is the spacing Adler derives on PDF 150-151 (Ex. V-25, V-26), and it keeps the C5 that links to movement I. Keep.
