## Findings

### 1. Bar 78 b3: no crescendo from the f of 78 into the ff of 79 in the strings and woodwinds (applied)
Problem: the deceptive cadence on F♭ arrives f on 78 b1 and stays there. On beat 3 the horns (mf<) and the harp (f arpeggio) push towards 79, but the violins, cellos, basses, flute 1 and bassoons carry no mark across that beat. They go from f to ff at the barline as a terrace step. Bassoon 1 already has the crescendo, but only in its part (`-\tag #'part \<`), so the score and the part disagree. The climax at 61 does have a full-bar crescendo in every group (60).
Evidence: Rimsky's cresc.-sf (PDF 129) puts the strings' accent at the end of a wind chord that is crescendoing. In Ex. 255 (PDF 419) every wind part is marked p< up to the sf of the last chord, and that sf coincides with the strings' attack. His anacrusis notes "form a kind of upward glide" into the stressed note (PDF 130). Brass and strings carry crescendo chords into the climax (PDF 130). At 78 b3 the run is the glide and B♭6 is the stressed note, but only the horns and harp are marked to grow. The players will probably grow anyway, but the score should say so as it does at 60, and the bassoon part and score should match.

### 2. Bar 61: the B-section climax has no anacrusis or inserted chord (checked, no change)
Rimsky lists grace-note chains and inserted string chords as ways to stress a note (PDF 129–130). At 61 the melody steps C6–D♭6 out of a full bar of crescendo on a fresh down-bow. A grace chain there would be a second use of the device that marks 79 (c238 recorded the two climaxes as different in kind), and inserted string chords would break the "molto sostenuto" tenuto halves. The arrival already works by the cresc.-sf layout (PDF 129): the winds and strings crescendo through 60, and the timpani roll on A♭ ends with a stroke at 60 b4½ and re-attacks D♭ f at 61. No change.

### 3. Bar 63: the strings' sf> tremolo under the trombone quotation (checked, no change)
This is Rimsky's case (b) (PDF 129): the strings hold the tenuto chord tremolando. His preferred artificial sf-p (a) would need a soft wind chord entering under the strings' sf. At 63 the winds are tacet on purpose, to set the quotation apart by reducing the forces (c153, c213). The releases 63–67 are protected, and the sf>p tremolo with the bass trombone's sf>p (c154) already gives the sf-dim. No change.

### 4. Bar 78 b3: the run is written out at full size and the violins break the slur into B♭6 (checked, no change)
Rimsky prints such anacruses small and slurs them to the main note (PDF 130; Ex. 254, PDF 418). Flute 1 does slur into B♭6. The violins hook the run on a second up-bow and take B♭6 on a fresh down-bow (c246, protected: Adler PDF 38–40, ff attack at the frog). The run is measured because of the poco allargando, and it leads into a single note, not a three- or four-note chord, so it meets Rimsky's string condition. No change.

### 5. Solo entries at p after long rests (checked, no change)
The re-entry rule has "wider application" (PDF 136). Ob 1 at 15 and 44 b4, the C.a. at 41 and 47 b4, and Hn 1 at 67 all enter p after rests of 4–30 bars. Rimsky's own example on PDF 418 (No. 253) has Cl II and Ob I solos entering p. The rule is about a group coming back in as colour (brass chords and accompaniment), and this score obeys it there (Tbn/Tuba pp 60, Tbn 1 ff 63, Hn 3·4 pp 29 and 87, winds ff 79). Fl 1 mp< at 77 is protected (c178).

### 6. Bar 47: the C♯7 pivot sf-p (checked, no change)
This is Rimsky's method (a) as it stands: the bassoons arrive on the p chord while Vln II, the violas and basses play fp, and then the strings fall to pp (c108). Oboe 1 (mf>) and the cellos (mp) finish the theme-head swell as melody, one level above the basis (PDF 133). No change.
