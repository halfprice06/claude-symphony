## Findings

### 1. Bars 75-76: the theme has only two string sections at one pitch while the counter-melody has three colours (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** From 74 b3 to 76, Vln I and Vln II play the theme in unison (A♭4-C5-F5-B♭5-A♭5, mp<). Under it, the cello counter-melody gains Bsn 1 and horn 2 at 75 (p<), so the subsidiary line has string, woodwind and brass while the principal line has strings only. Cl 1 rests at 75-76 and then comes in cold on G5 at 77, with the theme's descent.

**Reading.** Adler PDF 176: in the Jupiter coda each theme "must be heard at good strength", with "a strong and sympathetic color combination". PDF 178: in the Meistersinger passage "the color of each theme is made up of a combination of at least one string, one woodwind, and one brass instrument". PDF 179: for the "big" sound at forte or piano "it is essential that the doubling be of a mixed color". At 75-76 the counter-melody follows this and the theme does not.

**Why the earlier reason no longer applies.** c067 took Cl 1 off this passage (the comment in aTwo.ily keeps the old notes: `aes'4.(\p\< dolce c''8 f''4~ | f''4 bes''4 aes''4 |`) because at that time Vln II and Cl 1 made a lower octave that outweighed Vln I alone above it. Since c299/c309 both violin sections play at this pitch from 74 b3 to 76, so Cl 1 at the same pitch now adds weight to the lead itself and creates no competing octave. This does not undo c067's balance point; it applies it to the current voicing.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-547/work/sections/aTwo.ily).**
In `aTwoClOne`, replace
```
\partCombineSoloII R2.*2 | \partCombineAutomatic g''4(\mp\< es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 | aes'2)\f r4 |
```
with
```
aes'4.(\p\< c''8 f''4~ | f''4 bes''4 aes''4) | g''4(\mp\< es''8 des''8 c''8 bes'8 | aes'2)\f r4 |
```
and in `aTwoClTwo` at 75 drop the now-wrong score label: `es'2(\p\<-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "2." des'4 |` becomes `es'2(\p\< des'4 |`.
Cl 1 and Cl 2 have equal dynamics on the shared staff (p< at 75, mp< at 77). Cl 1 now plays one line from 75 to 78 b2 (about 13 s at ♩=52, with a slur break after 76) instead of entering on 77 b1, which also follows PDF 176 (keep a colour on an element long enough). Range is B♭5 at most. The layout is unchanged: 8 pages, same breaks.

### 2. Bar 78 b3: the viola figure doubles the counter-melody's pickup C4 (recorded, no change)

The violas' `fes'( c' g)` puts C4 on the same beat as the pickup C4 of the a2 horns, cellos and Bsn 1. Adler PDF 179 asks for distinct rhythm and articulation per element; here the middleground touches the counter-melody's note for one eighth. I do not propose a change: C is the root of the passing 6/3 on that beat, the alternative E♭4 would double the bass E♭, and the doubling lasts one eighth under a crescendo into ff. Recorded so that no later critic reopens it without this reason.

### 3. Dynamic hierarchy between principal and subsidiary voices (confirmation)

Every polyphonic passage marks the subsidiary voice softer than the principal one, and softer still when it lies high, which is exactly Schoenberg's practice described on PDF 184: Vla answers pp under Ob 1 p (15-22); Fl 1 counter-line pp above the C.a. theme at p (41-44); Vln II pp above the C.a. quotation (49-51); Hn 1 with Cl 1 pp-mp under the violins p-mf (53-56); violas pp and cellos p under the theme at mp (71-74). No change.

### 4. Bars 71-78: all three elements are legato (recorded, no change)

Scheherazade (Ex. XV-27, PDF 178-179) separates its middleground by staccato and spiccato. Here the theme, the cello counter-melody and the viola figure are all slurred. The rhythms are clearly different (dotted quarters; halves and quarters; continuous eighths), which is the first of Adler's three means (PDF 179), and detached playing would be wrong in an Adagio at p. The A′ texture is protected. No change.

### 5. Bars 53-56: theme A's head as counter-line to theme B (confirmation)

The Hn 1/Cl 1 line (A♭3-B♭3-D♭4-F4-B♭4) is theme A's opening shape at the D♭ level, heard alone at 7 and 23, and moves in long values where theme B moves in eighths. This is the Meistersinger and Jupiter method: elements exposed separately, then combined with distinct rhythm and colour (PDF 176-177). No change.

### 6. Imitation (observation, no change)

The movement has dialogue (15-22, 83-86) and simultaneous counter-melodies, but no imitative entries. Adler's chapter on polyphonic orchestral texture is about combining simultaneous elements, not imitation, and says fugal writing "is becoming less and less useful" (PDF 172). The one place where a canonic answer could fit (Vc a bar behind Ob 1 at 45-46) is protected (c417, c537). Not raised as a fault.
