### 2. The three closes fall by major thirds as the finale asked; bar 86 differs from the scherzo and finale and stays (recorded; no change)

**Bars.** II 85–89 (Cl 1 E♭5–D5–D♭5–C5 over I – [D–F–A♭–C over A♭] – IV6 – iv – I); III 365–368 (Hn 1 B4–A♯4–A4–G♯4 over E – A♯°7 – A minor 6/4 – E); IV 273–275 (Hn 1·2 G4–F♯4–F4–E4 over C – F♯°7 – F minor 6/4 – C).

**Evidence.** All three carry the same mixture line, ♮6–♭6–5: II F (Vla 86–87) – F♭ (88) – E♭ (89); III C♯4–C4–B3 (Vln II 366–368); IV A–A♭–G (violas 273–275). A&S PDF 395, point 3: "♮6–♭6–5 is good". The tails start on E♭5, B4 and G4, so the chain also falls by major thirds in register. Each close has a new colour (Cl 1, Hn 1, horns with Vln II, Ob 2 and Tpt 3), which is Adler's principle of repeating a tune at once in a radically different colour (PDF 257–258).

The one difference is the chord under ♯4̂. III and IV use a full diminished seventh on ♯4̂ (the seventh is ♭3̂: G♮ in E, E♭ in C). II's bar 86 has C♮ in the violas, so it is half-diminished. Making it D°7 would need C♭4 in the violas at 86. That puts ♭3̂ under the D5 one bar before the C5 goal, and the movement's link with movement I and the scherzo depends on that C5 (c170 Finding 8 reached the same result). It would also change 83–86, which c179 protected, and the close the finale asked this movement to keep. The scherzo and the finale vary the model, and the original does not have to copy them. Keep.

For the finale thread (information only): the c032 comment in mvt3/v35/sections/finale.ily says "voicing as the scherzo's coda 373–376". Since scherzo v04 those bars are 365–368.

### 3. Cor anglais at 79–82 (c187) and its coda entry at 87 (recorded; no change)

**Problem.** Up to v187 the C.a. was silent from 54 to 86. It came back at 87 ("dolce", pp, with the muted horns) as the instrument that had quoted movement I's second theme at 47–53. Adler gives the reason for such a rest twice: the solo colour stays fresh when the orchestra does not use it (PDF 263–264), and Mozart keeps the orchestral horns and flutes out while the horn or flute soloist plays (PDF 264, 267). c187 put the C.a. into the ff tutti at 79–82, so it now plays four bars before its coda entry.

**Decision: keep c187.** At 79–82 the C.a. is one of three winds inside an ff tutti, in unison with the violas. It is not heard there as a colour of its own. So at 87 it still sounds like a return after 33 bars of silence as a solo voice. c187's balance point (each octave of the peak has a string + wind group, Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 76–78) also stands. Reverting it would undo that fix for a small gain.

### 4. The coda follows the chapter's rules for introducing a soloist (no change)

83: after the 79–82 tutti the orchestra is "reduced drastically" (PDF 267) to the ppp A♭ pedal in cellos and basses, the "sustained tonic" Adler points out in Mozart's flute concerto. The harp's head in harmonics enters over it alone (c063 and c124 already moved the violas off its beat). 84–85: the timpani's E♭–C–A♭ is the motive that joins the harp's phrase to the clarinet's, as in Beethoven's Violin Concerto (PDF 273). 85–89: the clarinet tail enters niente over sustained strings an octave or more below it (PDF 274). 89: Vln I takes over the clarinet's C5, a point of pitch reference rather than a doubling (PDF 275, 283). Nothing to change; the coda is protected (c179).

### 5. The cyclic material returns in a new colour each time (no change)

The motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C sounds six times: Hn 1 (8), Vln I+II unison (24), B.cl. with C.a. a sixth above at movement I's pitch (32–34), Hn 1 with Bsn 1 below (67–69), violins in octaves (72), Cl 1 alone (85–89). No colour repeats, and the closing statement is the thinnest. This is the varied doubling Adler praises in Haydn (PDF 288) and the dialogue principle (PDF 257). Across the symphony the tail moves on in the same way: B.cl. and cellos in movement I, Hn 1 in the scherzo, horns and strings in the finale. Theme A also gets three colours: horn, violins in unison, violins in octaves widening to three octaves only at the climax (Bizet, PDF 279).

### 6. The horn solos (7–14, 67–70) against PDF 259–260, 264, 270 (recorded; no change)

No other horn plays during either solo (Mozart's rule, PDF 264). At 65–70 the background is Adler's Bartók texture (PDF 270): a held E♭ pedal (4 soli basses), a ppp timpani roll near the rim, bassoons below. One point departs from PDF 259–260. At 8 the bassoons and bass clarinet move in crotchets with the horn's E♭–D–D♭, one chord per tail note (vi7 – V7/V – V7). This harmonisation has been protected since cycle 1 ("bar 8 motto statement and its harmony"), and in Dvořák 9/ii the cor anglais tune is also accompanied chord by chord. Recorded.

### 7. The quotation at 47–50 is accompanied as the chapter recommends (no change)

C.a. solo (p, "solo, espr.") over offbeat viola quavers (sotto voce, pp), long notes in Vln II and half-note bass. The rhythm is independent (PDF 259–260). Vln II sits a sixth to an octave above the soloist, not on its pitches (c054 moved it up from the unison F♯4 at 49; PDF 274). Its G♮5–E♯5 at 49 supplies the Neapolitan colour without touching the quotation. The quotation stays one statement. Keep.
