## Findings

No finding requires a change to the score. They are ranked by how close each passage comes to falling short of the master work or of the reading.

### 1. Against Schumann 4/ii: the movement holds up on the points that make the Romanze convincing (recorded)

- **Doubling of the main line by contrasting colours.** Schumann gives the Romanze tune to oboe and solo cello in unison over plain string support. Adler's Mozart example says the same thing: a heterophonic doubling in a very different colour does not weaken the soloist (PDF 264). Here theme A changes colour each time instead (horn 7, violins 23, violins in octaves 71, three octaves at 79), and theme B passes through four colours in its first sixteen bars (Fl 37, C.a. 41, Ob 1 with Bsn 1 and Vc 45, violins 53). This is Haydn's "variety" of doubling (PDF 288). It is a different solution from Schumann's, and a legitimate one.
- **Cyclic material placed as a section, not as a reference.** Schumann sets the first movement's introduction inside the Romanze as its own paragraph. Both quotations here (C.a. 47-53, Tbn 1 63-64) are prepared by a key change, a tempo change and a thinner texture. That is Adler's "distinct colour contrast to feature soloist" (PDF 263): the woodwind/strings change at 47 and the brass entry at 63 make each quotation new.
- **The attacca ending.** Schumann ends the Romanze on a held chord that leads to the scherzo. Here the coda thins to a held A♭ chord with C5 on top, and the mute and retuning instructions sit in the pause (92). The texture at 83-92 (harp, then timpani, then clarinet, then cor anglais and muted horns, then muted strings) is Adler's sparse wind-solo setting (PDF 267, item 3) applied to a whole coda.
A conductor would not find an amateur's mark in the accompanying textures. The test that this movement passes less clearly is in finding 2.

### 2. Bars 23-30: the only solo statement where the accompaniment moves with the tune (protected; recorded)

**Problem.** Schumann keeps his accompaniment rhythmically apart from the oboe and cello throughout (plain held or plucked support). Adler calls rhythmic independence "perhaps the most crucial" problem (PDF 259) and shows the Mendelssohn figure in off-beat eighths under a legato solo (PDF 260, Ex. XVII-4). At 24 and 28 the violas (`<c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4`) and basses attack on the melody's quarters. c477 counted 5 off-melody attacks out of 27 here, against 24 of 50 at 71-78.

**Why no change.** It is the protected plain first statement (c007, c046, c359, c477): the reprise at 71-78 adds the eighth-note viola figure, and the difference is the design. I have no answer to that reason. If it is ever reopened, c477's limit stands: change the violas at 24 and 28 only, and keep `<bes g'>` on 24 b3 for the motto-tail harmony.

### 3. Bars 17-22: the solo violin is a dialogue partner, never a decorator (recorded; no change recommended)

**Observation.** Schumann's most memorable use of the solo violin is ornamental figuration over a melody in the orchestra. That is Adler's Ex. XVII-15 (PDF 274) with the roles swapped, and Adler's PDF 262 point about exchanging foreground and background. Here the solo violin at 17-22 answers the oboe phrase for phrase in the same note values (`aes''8( bes''8 g''4 f''4 | es''2 des''4)`).

**Why no change.** The 15-22 dialogue, with its viola echoes, is protected (c127, c477 finding 4), and Adler's first guideline, dialogue (PDF 257-258), is exactly what it does. An ornamental solo violin over theme A would be a new idea in a movement that already has several solo colours, not a correction. I record the comparison only.

### 4. Bars 41-44 and 47-52: wind soloists over close accompaniment (follows)

The C.a. starts theme B on A♭3, in its weakest fifth (PDF 275). Since c267 the pizz. violas never attack a C.a. note together with it, and the triplet-against-quarter contrast is the condition under which Adler says distance matters less (PDF 274). At 47 Ob 1 ends its C♯5 on b3 and the C.a. enters on C♯4 on b4. The previous colour stops before the new one starts, as in Casanelles' Fig. 6.1 (c479), and the soloist is led to its pitch an octave away (PDF 275). The violas' sotto-voce off-beats with held basses at 48-51 are Verdi's aria setting (Ex. XVII-19, PDF 278: off-beat pizz. strings, sustained chords). No change.

### 5. Bars 85-92: dovetailed hand-offs in the coda (follows)

Cl 1's tail E♭5-D5-D♭5-C5 (85-89) hands its C5 to muted Vln I at 89 on the same pitch. The C.a. and muted horns 3-4 enter at 87 under it. This is Adler's pitch-reference rule (PDF 275) and Mozart's rule against colour competition (PDF 267, item 4): no other clarinet sounds with Cl 1 until Cl 2's niente entry at 89. It matches Schumann's quiet linking chord before the scherzo. Protected (tail in Vln I, clarinet morendo). No change.

### 6. Comparison with v479: nothing got worse (recorded)

- 74 b1 (c479): the cellos' 9-8 no longer sits under a held D♭4. That is an improvement and is now protected.
- Bsn 2 breath at 10 (c486): it falls where the rest of the accompaniment breathes or rests. The comma on the shared bassoon staff is clear on page 1.
- Score "via sord." at 92 (c485): it is right-aligned near the final barline above Vln I, Vln II and Vla, after the fermata note has been reached, so it does not read as a mid-note instruction. It is needed because the scherzo follows attacca.
