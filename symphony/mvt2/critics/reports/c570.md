## Findings

I found nothing in v570 that the reading marks as wrong and that the earlier passes have not already settled. The findings below record what the reading confirms. Findings 1 and 2 add checks that the earlier passes did not make. I made no change to the score.

**1. Bars 2–3: the link from movement I is Rimsky's method 2 exactly (record, no change).**
- *Reading:* PDF 127, method 2. The same chord passes from one group to another, "in the same position and distribution, either in the same octave or in another", and the dynamics need not match.
- *Score:* at bar 2 the strings hold A♭ with the distribution root–fifth–root–third (Vc A♭2, Vla E♭4, Vln II A♭4, Vln I C5). At bar 3 the winds take it pp in the same order an octave lower (B.cl. A♭2, Cl 2 E♭3, Hn 4 A♭3, Hn 3 C4), while the strings fade to niente. Vln I's C5, the pitch movement I ended on, is the one note held across the change. Hn 4's C4 and the cellos' A♭2 give the two groups common tones, so the hand-over has no seam.
- *Cyclic point:* the chord that comes over from movement I leaves the strings at once, and the movement's first sound of its own is wind. This is the string-free colour cyclic-notes asks for, and it is reached by a rule from the book, not by a cut.
- *Do not change:* the link 1–6 is protected, and this check is one more reason to keep it.

**2. Bars 80–83 and 89–92: the diminuendos work by taking groups away, in stages (record, no change).**
- *Reading:* PDF 127. Taking a group away "belongs essentially to the diminuendo." Ex. 250 (PDF 416) shows brass and woodwind dropping out under a held texture.
- *Score, from the MIDI note list:* at 82 the woodwind, the horns, the harp and the strings all play the tonic arrival p> (the trombones, tuba and timpani have already stopped). Then the release comes in two stages inside the bar.
  - On beat 3, seven parts stop: Ob 1·2, C.a., Cl 2, Cbn, Hn 1·2, Vla and Vln II. That clears the middle register for the harp's pickup E♭4–F4.
  - Fl 1, Cl 1, B.cl., the bassoons, Vln I and the cellos hold to the barline.
  - At 83 only the harp and the low A♭ pedal are left.
- The coda repeats the pattern. At 90 b2, Cl 1, the C.a. and Hn 3·4 stop, and from 91 the strings and Cl 2 hold alone, morendo.
- *Cyclic point:* these two stages are what take the movement's last ff (79–80) down to the ppp chord the scherzo grows out of. This movement ends quieter than movement I and has fewer instruments playing. That fits the plan, in which the scherzo (in E) has to start from almost nothing: Hn 1's G♯4 is our Vln II A♭4.

**3. Bars 6 and 47: two hand-overs by adjacent attack (record; c290 settled them, I confirm).**
- *Reading:* PDF 125–126. When a phrase passes between instruments, the last note of one part coincides with the first note of the next.
- *Score:*
  - Bar 6: Cl 2's E♭3 ends on beat 2, and Hn 1's E♭3 pickup comes on beat 3.
  - Bar 47: Ob 1 holds C♯5 to the end of beat 3 with a niente hairpin, and the C.a. enters on C♯4 on beat 4.
- *Verdict:* the rule is for one phrase divided between two timbres. In both bars a new phrase begins: theme A in the first case, movement I's second theme in the second. Each enters alone on purpose. Bar 6 repeats movement I's own entry into its second theme (I 45–47, recorded in mvt1.md). Bar 47 lets the quotation enter after the poco rit. (c048). An overlap would blur a cyclic entry, so neither should change.

**5. The echo at 87–89 (record; c290 settled it, I confirm on the image of PDF 128).**
- *Score:* Hn 3 plays F4–F♭4–E♭4 open, with the lower violas, at 79–81. Hn 4 plays it con sord. an octave lower at 87–89, with the same lower violas and the C.a.
- *Verdict:* this meets each condition on PDF 128–129. The answer is weaker. It is muted brass after the same phrase played open. It is at the octave with less resonance. Its colours are related, because the violas take part in both statements. Strings do not answer woodwind, and woodwind do not answer strings. The finale uses the same line at pitch (its 197–198), so this is a cross-movement fact. Do not change it.
