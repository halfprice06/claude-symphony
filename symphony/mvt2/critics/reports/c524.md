# Critic 524: v524, lens 4 (orchestration)

Assigned reading: Rimsky-Korsakov, *Principles of Orchestration* ("principles of form.pdf"), fourth series. Ch. I, "Comparison of resonance in orchestral groups and combination of different tone qualities" (PDF 51–53), and ch. III, "Harmony": general observations, number of parts and duplication, distribution of notes in chords, string harmony, wood-wind harmony, with the example lists (PDF 81–99). I read every text page from 51 to 53 and from 81 to 99 in full. I rendered the diagram pages (82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 89, 90, 91, 92, 93, 94, 95, 96, 97) as images and read the diagrams from the images. The enclosure diagrams on 91–92 needed a 160 dpi crop to read. Earlier passes on this range: c004, c094, c184, c254, c394.

Method: I scanned score.midi (v524) at every beat with four scripts in /tmp/critic-524/s/. (1) dump.py lists every sounding pitch by instrument for bars 1–92. (2) gaps.py finds empty intervals wider than an octave above the bass, and a bass more than a tenth below the next part, at loud velocities (PDF 85–86, 96). (3) order.py checks woodwind register order: Fl > Ob/C.a./Cl > Bsn > B.cl./Cbsn (PDF 90 rule 2, 91–95). (4) samecol.py finds fourths, fifths, seconds, sevenths and tritones inside one colour (PDF 90 rule 4). inv.py checks the bass of inverted dominant sevenths and diminished sevenths for doubling in the upper parts (PDF 83–84). I compared every hit with HISTORY.md and with the "checked and left" lists in c254 and c394.

### 1. Bar 81 b2–3: a major second D♭4–E♭4 inside the horns at ff> (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** PDF 90, rule 4 (checked against the image), says seconds, sevenths, fourths and fifths belong to instruments of different colour "except when discords are to be emphasised". At 81 b2 Hn 1·2 a2 move B♭3→D♭4, the seventh of V7, while Hn 3 resolves its F♭4 9–8 suspension to E♭4 (c082). Two horn parts then sound D♭4–E♭4, a major second in one colour, for two beats under a diminuendo from ff. Hn 4 (G3) adds a tritone G3–D♭4 below. Elsewhere in the movement the samecol.py scan finds no second inside one colour except the trombones' passing notes at 63–64, which belong to the quotation.

**Evidence.** In the MIDI at 81 b2 the horns sound G3, D♭4, D♭4, E♭4. The same scan finds the clarinets' tritone D♭5–G5 at 81 b1, but that is the first beat of the release and the clarinets leave the D♭ at b2.

**Why no change.** The second is the dominant seventh against its root at the moment the 9–8 resolves. That is the discord the bar exists to stress, which is Rimsky's own exception. The obvious fix would give Hn 3 a different resolution tone (B♭3 instead of E♭4), but that breaks the protected F–F♭–E♭ line, which the muted horns and cor anglais echo at 87–89 (c150). The other fix, taking Hn 1·2 off D♭4, changes the protected hemiola counter-melody (c048). Horns at ff> in the middle register blend more than oboes would (PDF 90 names the oboe as the case to avoid). Left as it is.

### 2. Bars 28 b2, 47 b1 and 62: the only wide gaps at loud dynamics (recorded, no change)

**Problem.** PDF 86 (image) and PDF 96 remark 1 say empty middle spaces sound bad "especially in forte passages". gaps.py lists only three loud places. (a) 28 b2: violins in unison on the B♭5 appoggiatura at f, violas D♭4/F4. That leaves a 17-semitone gap for one beat. (b) 47 b1: A♭2 (Bsn 2) to B3 (Vla). (c) 62: G♭2/E2 to B♭3/A♭3 at ff.

**Why no change.** (a) is a single melodic line rising above its accompaniment, not a sustained chord, and 23–30 is protected as the plain first statement. (b) is the fp attack of a chord that drops to pp at once, so PDF 86's allowance for piano applies. (c) was recorded by c254: the timpani's D♭3 covers b1–2, and any reed filling it at b3 would make fifths with the bass G♭–F♭. This report adds 62 b1–2 to c254's 62 b3–4, for the same reasons.
