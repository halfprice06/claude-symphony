## Findings

### 1. A′ (71–78) repeats the background of 23–30 note for note; the violas give the held theme notes no motion (applied)
**Problem.** In 71–78 the violas play the same divided block chords as in 23–30 (`<c' es'>2.` | `<c' aes'>4 <bes aes'>4 <bes g'>4` …). The cellos play the same counter-melody, now doubled by horn 2, and the harp plays the same `r4 <aes c' es'>2` pattern it already played in 7–14. The theme's long notes (es'''4~es'''4 at 71–72, f'''4~ at 75–76, g''2 at 74) sit over held chords. Adler 135 says the background should "add intensity to the longer notes of the melody" with a "repetitious and simple" figure that is "dovetailed … without any pauses". Adler 136–139 (Mozart, Haydn V-16) and 166 ("orchestration must … clarify the form") call for a change of texture when a theme returns. Dvořák moves his violas at the string statement of his tune (line 292). Here the climactic return sounds like the second statement with octaves added.
**Fix.** Keep 23–30 as it is. At 71–78, replace the viola block chords with a slurred eighth-note figure in the violas' middle register (C4–A♭4), unison, below Vln II's line. It is dovetailed across the beats and moves most where the melody holds. In `aTwo.ily`:
```
aTwoVla = {
  c'8(\mp^\markup \italic "unis., espr." es' aes') es'( c' aes) | c'( aes' bes) aes'( bes g') | c'( es' aes') c'( es' aes) | bes( es' g') es'( bes es') |
  c'(\< es' aes') es'( aes f') | f'( aes' f') bes( es' bes) | g( bes es') g'( es' des') | c'(\f f' aes') f'( c' aes) |
  <f' aes'>2.:32\ff^\markup \italic "div." | ...  (79–82 unchanged)
```
The pitches follow the v07 chord of each beat. I chose the figure's turning notes so that they make no octaves or fifths with the harp LH, basses, clarinets or Vln I (the first attempts did; allpairs_par.py caught them). It does not double the 76→77 A♭–G resolution.

### 2. 53–60: the counter-melody disappears at the climax, and the background repeats 37–44 (applied)
**Problem.** Horn 1's counter-melody (theme A's head, 54–56) is the only middleground in section B. At 57 it turns into a chord tone. At 58 it moves `f'4 es'4 es'4 f'4` in the melody's own quarter-note rhythm, together with Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, horn 2 and cellos. At 60 it holds `es'1` under the melody's held `c'''1`. Adler 144 rule 4 asks for a countertheme rhythmically different from the theme, and Adler 142 asks for each part to have "its own characteristic rhythm". Adler 135 asks for movement under the melody's long notes. At 60 nothing moves except the viola triplets. The violas repeat their 37–44 triplets note for note, at the same octave. Meanwhile Vln II doubles oboe 1 in unison an octave below the melody. Adler 137 (crescendo by adding octaves) and 139 (Haydn's second statement "melodizing" the harmony in Vln II) suggest the background should also grow.
**Fix.** (a) Horn 1 at 57–60, one slur: `aes'2(\mf\< ges'2 | f'4 es'2 f'4 | ges'2\f es'2 | es'4\< f'4 ges'2) |`. It holds through the melody's quarters at 58 and rises E♭–F–G♭ under the held C at 60 into its A♭ at 61. (b) Vln II at 57–60 leaves the oboe's octave and doubles the viola triplets an octave higher, so the background climbs with the crescendo:
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes'8(\< des'' f'' des'' aes' des'') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes'8( des'' ges'' des'' bes' des'') } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes'8( des'' f'') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes'8( des'' es'') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { ges'8( c'' es'') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes'8( des'' f'') } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges'8(\f bes' es'' bes' ges' bes') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes'8( des'' es'' des'' bes' des'') } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges'8(\< c'' es'' c'' ges' c'') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { ges'8( c'' es'' c'' ges' c'') } |
```
The melody keeps Fl 1, Ob 1 and Vln I in three octaves. A further step the composer could take: give the cellos (now a third copy of the bass with Cb and Bsn 2) a tenor answer to horn 1's head at 55–56 (Adler 144 rule 3, theme high and countertheme low).

### 3. 32–34: the clarinet chord encloses the bass-clarinet solo (applied)
**Problem.** In 32–34 clarinet 1 holds G♭4 above the bass clarinet's E♭4, and clarinet 2 moves C4–B3–B♭3 in thirds below it. The solo is a middle voice inside a chord of its own colour. Adler 135: keep the background out of the melody's register "especially when foreground and background are performed by instruments of similar color". Adler 144 rule 3: separate them by register.
**Fix.** Move both clarinets below the solo. The harmony stays the same, including the protected common-tone diminished seventh at 33. Cl 1 takes the old Cl 2 line and Cl 2 takes the old Cl 1 line an octave lower. `trans.ily`:
`transClOne = { R2. | c'2.\pp | c'4 b4 bes4 | ges2. | des'2. | c'2. | }`
`transClTwo = { R2. | ges2.\pp | ges4 f4 f4 | es2. | bes2. | ges2. | }`
The solo is now the top voice throughout 31–34. Lowest note: concert E♭3, inside the B♭ clarinet's range. This also removes the B.cl–Cl fifths the checker flagged at 34–35.

### 4. 20–22: Vln II drops below the violas onto the G string (open since cycle 4; applied)
**Problem.** Vln II `c'2 bes4 | aes2 aes4 | g2` lies a fourth to a sixth below the violas' `f'2 e'4 | f'2 f'4 | bes2.`. At 22 it reaches G3, two notes above the cellos' E♭3. This breaks the "traditional place in the distribution" (Adler 151). It also puts a background voice in the violin's darkest register, the opposite of Adler 135's pale-register rule.
**Fix.** Exchange the two parts at 21–22 and re-voice 20. A straight exchange at 20 gives octaves G–F between Vln II and cellos/basses (19→20). `aOne.ily`:
Vln II `aes'2 g'4 | aes'2 g'4 | f'2 f'4 | bes2 es'8(\mp f'8 |` (bars 19–22)
Vla `f'2 e'4 | c'2 bes4 | aes2 aes4 | g2. |` (bars 19–22)
In bar 20 Vln II doubles the oboe's last two notes, which supports the end of its phrase.

### 5. Harp in 23–30 doubles the violas' chords; its colour is spent before the reprise (applied)
**Problem.** In 23–30 the harp repeats its 7–14 part exactly, and its right hand doubles the violas' divided chords (both `<c' es'>` against `<aes c' es'>`). It adds nothing that the strings do not already sound. It also means the harp's entrance at 71 is the third identical statement. Adler 135: Brahms "saves the higher violin color for the more important foreground statement". Adler 142 asks the composer to "limit his options, especially if a passage is to be repeated more than once".
**Fix.** Harp tacet 23–30: `aOneHarpR` and `aOneHarpL` end with `R2.*16 |` after the 7–14 bars. The harp returns at 71 after 56 bars' rest, together with the new viola figure. This does not touch the cycle-4 rule that the harp LH follows the bass.

### 6. Bars 28 and 76: the counter-melody moves with the theme and doubles its appoggiatura (applied)
**Problem.** At 28 (and 76) the theme plays `f''4 bes''4( aes''4)`, where B♭ is the appoggiatura accepted in cycle 1. The cello counter-melody plays `aes4 bes4 c'4`, moving in the same quarters (Adler 144 rule 4) and sounding the B♭ two octaves below the appoggiatura on the same beat. That weakens the appoggiatura (Adler 152: take care over which tone is doubled). It is the only bar where the counter-melody falls into the theme's rhythm.
**Fix.** Cello 28: `aes2(\mf\> c'4)`. Cello 76 and horn 2 at 76: `aes2 c'4`. The counter-melody holds A♭ under the appoggiatura and moves to C on beat 3.

### 7. 15–22: the dialogue does not overlap, and the four lower strings move in one rhythm (not applied)
**Problem.** Oboe (15–16, 19–20) and Vln I (17–18, 21–22) answer each other strictly in turn. Each phrase ends before the next begins. Under them Vln II, violas, cellos and basses play `2 4` in every bar for eight bars, and the cellos double the basses throughout. Adler 142–143 (Beethoven 7/ii: each part its own rhythm) and 135 (dovetailing "without any pauses") point to overlap and some independent motion.
**Fix (proposal).** Let each answer enter on beat 3 of the previous bar (Vln I `r2 aes''8( bes''8 |` at 16 as a pickup), or give the cellos an eighth-note link on beat 3 of 16, 18 and 20 (e.g. 16: `des2 es8( f8)`). This changes the theme-B dialogue rhythm, so I leave the choice to the composer.

### 8. Check, not a fault: octaves between the B-section triplets and the melody
The triplet figure starts each beat on a chord tone that often matches the melody an octave or two down: in 37 and 53 the violas, and in my 57–60 version also Vln II, have A♭ under A♭ and D♭ under D♭. These are arpeggiated chord tones, as in Brahms 3/iii (Adler 132–133), not voice-leading octaves. Watch them if the figure is ever sustained.
