## What else I studied

I compared the score with Beethoven's Ninth Symphony, third movement, in the local corpus (reference-corpus/pdf/Beethoven_Sym9_mvt3.pdf; bars 6–9 inspected as an image). Two things carry over. First, Beethoven's soft wind cadence: at bars 7–8 the strings' phrase is answered p by 2 Cl, 2 Bsn and all four horns. That is the soft 4-horn chord with woodwind that Rimsky describes on PDF 108 and shows in Ex. 141 and 143. Second, Beethoven's tempo plan. The movement has six tempo words (Adagio molto e cantabile, Andante moderato, Tempo I, Andante, Adagio, Lo stesso tempo), all at section changes, and no rit. or allargando. I also diffed v259 against v269 with comments stripped. The changes are Hn 1·2 divided at 61–62 (c264), the viola pizz. figure at 41–42 (c267), Vc A♭2 at 43 b3 and the bass rhythm at 49 (c262), Cb pizz. sf at 63 (c268), the Vc "4 soli" at 83–86 (c259), the harp text, Ob 2's hidden rest and the clarinets' muta text. None of these made anything worse. The 61 brass chord now meets row 1 better than v259 did.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 53: the cor anglais's held F4 should taper under the violins' entry (applied)

**Problem.** The cor anglais quotation ends on F4 (f'2) on 53 b1–2, with no dynamic after the p at 52. On the same downbeat both violin sections start theme B, p, sul A, an octave and a sixth above it. The F4 is the resolution of the G♭ at 52, so it belongs to the old phrase. Held at an unchanged p, it equals the new melody in level. The open item from c268 asks exactly this.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 114: balance matters most in sustained notes. PDF 115–117: the accompaniment must differ from the melody in intensity. In Ex. 172 (PDF 342), the wind chord that ends a phrase is marked dim. while the next part enters.

**Fix (applied).** In sections/bSec.ily, bSecCorAnglais, bar 53: `f'2\> \unset Staff.midiMinimumVolume \unset Staff.midiMaximumVolume r2\! | R1*3 |`

The notes are unchanged. This is a dynamic change at the last note of the protected C.a. quotation span 47–53; the quotation's notes and its p are untouched.

### 2. Bar 82: the soft cadence chord without Hn 3·4 (recorded, not applied)

**Problem.** Rimsky's standard soft cadence (PDF 108; Ex. 141, 143) and Beethoven's bars 7–8 both put a soft horn chord under the woodwind. At 82 the tonic chord has Hn 1·2 on C4 a2 and no Hn 3·4. Hn 3 E♭4 and Hn 4 A♭3, p> on beats 1–2, would complete it.

**Why not applied.** HISTORY protects "Hn 3·4 out at 82" and the "staged release 81–83" (c133, c186). Hn 3·4 must also put mutes on for 87, and they have their rest from 82 onward for that. The reading gives a preference, not a fault, so the stated reason stands.

### 3. Bars 87–89: the moving line is in a horn (recorded, protected)

**Problem.** PDF 108 gives stationary notes to brass and moving lines to woodwind. At 87–89 Hn 4 moves F3–F♭3–E♭3 while Hn 3 holds A♭3.

**Why no change.** The moving line is doubled by the C.a. and the lower violas. PDF 106–107 (Ex. 148–151) recommend this exact combination of muted horn and C.a., and PDF 113 approves horns doubled by divided violas. The passage is protected (muted horns with divided violas 87–90, c259). No change.

### 4. Viola part: three tempo texts read as belonging to the system above (not applied; engraving)

**Problem.** In parts-book.pdf, viola page, some tempo texts land closer to the system above than to their own bar. "poco sostenuto" (48) and "a tempo, poco a poco animando" (53) sit just under the system above. "poco allargando" (78) prints at the left end of the space below the 66–77 system, under bar 66. A violist would read it as an allargando at 66. This is the check c267 asked for: system padding is 0.1 so that the part fits one page.

**Tested.** A smaller staff (`#(layout-set-staff-size 19)`) with padding 1.2 keeps the part on one page, but the texts stay in the same places, because their height comes from the high notes and bow marks at 79 and from stacked text at 47–48. Test files: /tmp/critic-269/work/genparts-test.py and parts-test.pdf.

**Proposed fix for the next engraving cycle.** In genparts.py, for the viola part only, force the system break one bar earlier (before 77), so that "poco allargando" falls mid-line above bar 78. Alternatively, print these three marks as part-only `\tag #'part` text on the viola notes at 48, 53 and 78, and give the MetronomeMark `\tweak stencil ##f` in that part. Check the result by eye.

### 5. Tempo words: more than the master uses (recorded)

v269 prints 13 tempo indications in 92 bars: Adagio, poco rit. (35), Poco più mosso, poco rit. (46), poco sostenuto, a tempo poco a poco animando, poco allargando (60), Largamente, allargando (64), Tempo I, poco allargando (78), a tempo, rit. (89). Beethoven 9/iii has six in about 157 bars, all at section changes. Every one of the 13 here was accepted with a reason (c048 for 46–47, c049 for 60). The MIDI steps are matched, so this is not a fault in itself. A conductor will shape 46–47, 60 and 78 without the words. Do not add more. If one is ever cut, "poco rit." at 46 is the weakest, since "poco sostenuto" follows one beat later.

### 6. Bar 71 b2: viola A♭4 against Vln II's held A♭4 (recorded; recommend closing the open item)

The viola eighth touches the melody's pitch for one eighth, at pp, inside an arpeggio. PDF 112 treats unison and octave doublings as reinforcement, not as a fault, and theme A's line stays clear in Vln I an octave higher. Keep it, and close the c257 open item.

## Master comparison in brief

Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? In orchestration, mostly yes. The brass chord at 61, the trombone quotation over string tremolo, the muted horns with C.a. and divided violas in the coda, and the varied returns of both themes all match what PDF 100–118 teaches, and the score does them cleanly. Compared with Beethoven, two things are weaker. The tempo surface is fussier (Finding 5). The soft wind-and-horn cadence choir, Beethoven's main colour in that movement, appears here only in the link (1–6) and the coda (87–90), not at the internal cadences. Both have been decided before for stated reasons, and I do not reopen them.
