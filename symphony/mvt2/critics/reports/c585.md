## Findings

The string notation follows the reading everywhere in the table. What remains are two small faults in what the score prints for the strings.

### 1. Bars 64 and 89, Vln I: the tempo word sits on the string instruction below it. Applied.
**Problem.** The copy of the tempo mark printed above Vln I touches the italic technique text under it. At 64 "allargando" ends 0.0 pt above "unis." (font boxes touching); at 89 "rit." overlaps "con sord., sul D" by 1.9 pt, about 0.1 mm of white at 400 dpi. Both are medium italic of the same size, so they read as one instruction ("rit. con sord., sul D"). The other tempo words over string text (37 "Poco più mosso", 61 "Largamente") are bold and further off, so they stay distinct.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 30 and 53: *unis.* and the playing-condition words must be read at their note; PDF 54: the same for pizz./arco, which the player must find at once. MOLA PDF 4 places the tempo above the first violins as a separate line; Gould PDF 55: no characters collide.
**Fix (applied, global.ily).** Before each `\tempo`, `\once \override Staff.MetronomeMark.outside-staff-padding = #1.5`:
`s1 \once \override Staff.MetronomeMark.outside-staff-padding = #1.5 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "allargando" \mt 48 ...` (64) and the same before `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "rit."` (89). The gaps are now 3.6 pt (64) and 1.8 pt (89, visibly separate). No system or page break moves. The parts set their tempo words separately and are unchanged by this (the viola part's 64 already has a gap).

### 2. Bars 82-83, Vln I and Vln II: the score never says to put the mutes on. Applied.
**Problem.** The violins play con sord. from 87 (Vln II) and 89 (Vln I). Their "mettere sord." sits on bar 83's rest, which prints in the parts but not in the score, because both violin staves are hidden in the coda system 83-86. The violas' warning is printed at 82 b3 in the score (score-only tag). So the conductor sees the violas' mute change but not the violins'.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54: playing-condition changes appear "in both the score and the parts", with time to make them; c485 applied the same rule to "via sord." at 92 and the violas already follow it at 82.
**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily, score only).** Vln II: `aes'2\p\> r4\!-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "mettere sord." |`. Vln I: `<< { \after 2 \pp aes''2.\p\> } \tag #'score { s2 s4^\markup \italic "mettere sord." } >> |`. The three warnings now line up at 82 b3 above Vln I, Vln II and Vla. The parts are unchanged (each violin part still has one "mettere sord." at 83).

### 3. Bar 40, Cb pizz.: half notes in a pizzicato line. Not applied (optional).
**Problem.** In 37-44 the pizz. basses are written as quarters with rests, except 40, which has two half notes (A1, B♭1). A pizz. note does not sustain; the half notes suggest a held sound and differ from the bars around them.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 54 and Ex. II-55 (Brahms 1/iv): the pizz. notes are written short with rests; bar 44 here does the same.
**Fix (optional, bSec.ily).** `a,,4 r4 bes,,4 r4 |` at 40. No sound change in a real performance; the MIDI note lengths would shorten. The harp's half notes at 40 stay (the harp rings).
