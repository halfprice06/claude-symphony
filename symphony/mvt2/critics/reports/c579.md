## Findings (ranked)

**F1. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Compared with Tchaikovsky 5/ii (recorded, no change).**
The two movements share their main shape. Both open with a short dark chordal link that moves by common tone into the major key (Tchaikovsky: B minor to D; here bars 1-6: movement I's C-major chord to A♭). Both give the first theme to a solo horn. Both hand the theme on to the oboe. Both have a contrasting middle section that gives the clarinet and the double-reed group their own melodies. Both have a cyclic brass intrusion at the peak (here Tbn 1's movement I quotation at 63-64 after the ff at 61-62). Both bring theme A back in the strings, rescored, and close with clarinet and soft strings.

The scoring at each of these points follows the reading. The horn theme has a softer harmonic basis an octave below (principle 2, PDF 133). The middle section's accompaniment is pizzicato and harp (principle 9, PDF 138). The solo violin plays senza sord. over muted strings, exactly as in Ex. 284 (PDF 457). The climaxes are supported in register, with the brass entering at the peak (principle 14, Ex. 280-281). The soft ending is held by light winds (principle 15, Ex. 282). A conductor would read this as professional work in Tchaikovsky's and Dvořák's line.

The remaining distance is one of scale, not of craft. Tchaikovsky's horn melody runs well past eight bars, and its climaxes are long, repeated waves. Here theme A is an eight-bar period (7-14) and the tutti peak is two bars (61-62). Both are on the do-not-touch list: the horn theme 7-14 and the "lean climax 61-62" (c567, c574). The brief also binds the quotations to one appearance each, so the double motto intrusion of the model is not available. I do not reopen any of them.

**F3. Theme B as accompaniment and as tutti, 37-44 against 57-62 (recorded, no change).**
Ex. 289-291 (PDF 467-470; text PDF 144) show the same Sadko music scored twice. First it is an accompaniment: violas in running sixteenths, cellos colla voce, clarinet and cor anglais doubling one phrase each. Then it is a tutti (Ex. 291): the melody in Fl/Ob/Cl/Vln I ff in octaves, horns and trombones holding the harmony, violas and cellos keeping the running figure.

This score matches that pattern. The 37-44 viola triplets move to the harp at 53-56. They come back in the violas as sextuplets at 59-60 while Fl 1, Ob 1 and both violin sections carry theme B in octaves, and the low brass enter pp< at 60. At 61-62 the figure becomes "molto sostenuto, trem." where Rimsky keeps it running. Tremolo is the ff form of the same motion and holds the chord the quotation breaks into. The point is covered by the protected lean climax and the long-standing "trem." open item (cycles 46-51). I record it as consistent with Ex. 291 and propose no change.

**F4. Brass re-entries against PDF 136 (recorded, no change).**
Rimsky asks horns, trombones and tuba to re-enter pp or ff after long rests. Every first entry follows that except four: Hn 4 p at 60 (c364), Hn 3·4 f at 61 and 79 (c044, c069), and Hn 2 p at 75. The first three already have recorded reasons (HISTORY, cycle 179). Hn 2 at 75 has not been listed. It comes in after twelve bars' rest on the cellos' counter-line, which is already moving at mp, and c067 and c217 placed it one level under the cellos on purpose (Sebesky PDF 164, 166). Here the horn is joining a line, not making a dramatic return, so the rule's reason does not apply. Changing it to pp would make the horn inaudible under the cellos. I record the exception and propose no change.

**F5. Held basses under Hn 1 at 65-70 (recorded, no change).**
PDF 139 warns that long held double-bass notes under a voice give "a peculiar throbbing effect". At 65-70 the basses hold E♭1 for six bars under the horn's motto tail. The pedal is on the do-not-touch list ("E♭7 retransition with solo horn 65-70"). It is marked "4 soli, sul A" at pp with CC11 fades, and the timpani roll is ppp near the rim. The horn sounds an octave and more above it (E♭4-B♭3). The warning is about a voice in the bass's beat range, not a horn three octaves up. No change.
