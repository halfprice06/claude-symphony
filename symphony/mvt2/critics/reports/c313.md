## Findings (ranked; none applied)

### 1. The dominant span 65–70 is 6% of the movement. Keep

**Point from the reading.** Ex. 6-4 (PDF 95) shows the large progression made of expanded chords. In Kuhnau the expanded V (17–27) is a third of the piece and "helps to shape the form".

**Score.** The structural V here is six bars (20.8 s): E♭7 at 65–66, the bare E♭ at 67, the horn tail with the passing 6/4 at 68, and E♭7 at 69–70. IV holds the contrast (37–64, 34%).

**Why it stays.** In a ternary slow movement the contrasting key is the middle section, not the dominant. Dvořák 9/ii, the brief's model, has no dominant span at its return at all (bassmL, above). This movement's six-bar dominant pedal sits between the Kuhnau model and the Dvořák one. It also prepares the return more clearly than Dvořák does, which suits a return that is shortened. The span is protected (65–70 dominant expansion, c073; E♭7 retransition, cycle 5), and proportions are closed (c143, c153, c253). No change.

### 2. The middle section never closes in D♭ with a PAC. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 94–95: a PAC is "the most final sounding", "used mostly at the end"; Kuhnau's V section ends without its own close and leads straight back to I.

**Score.** D♭ is confirmed by the HC at 44, which goes on deceptively to vi at 45. The quotation closes with a PAC in F♯ minor at 50. A V7–I under theme B's return at 52→53 begins a phrase, with the melody on 3̂. The climax at 61 is an IAC with A♭6 (5̂) on top, and the bass leaves at once (G♭ at 62, F♭ at 62 b3).

**Why it stays.** A middle section that must lead back should stay open, and the reading's grading puts the one PAC in D♭ that would close it out of place here. The IAC at 61 is the right weight for an arrival that slides on to A minor (key plan and 61–65 route protected). No change.

### 3. A′ is shortened by a contrapuntal close at 74→75. Keep

**Point from the reading.** Schubert D. 935 (8-1, PDF 121) is in A♭, the key of this movement. It prolongs the opening tonic for eight bars with V7 inversions and closes a group with a stepwise bass (bars 7–8). PDF 129 §11 allows such a contrapuntal cadence "to end a group of measures within a larger phrase".

**Score.** In A at 10 and 26 the first four bars end on a half cadence. In A′ the bars 73 b3–75 run vi7 – V4/3 (B♭1 bass; D♭4, E♭4, G complete) – I, so the bass moves 2̂–1̂ and the phrase continues without a stop. That is how A′ fits in 0.52 of A's time, which is close to Kuhnau's short closing tonic (6 of 33 bars, PDF 95) and to Dvořák's shortened Largo reprise.

**Why it stays.** This follows the reading, and the A′ texture 71–82 is protected. No change.

### 4. Inner phrase starts on I6 at 15, 41 and 57. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 109 §5: a 6/3 chord "attracts less accent" and gives "a more flowing effect". Where stability is needed, "as in most beginnings and virtually all endings", a 6/3 will not do.

**Score.** All section starts are root position (row 20). Three phrases inside sections start on I6: 15 (oboe answer, straight after the PAC at 14), 41 (theme B's consequent, after vi at 40) and 57 (second half of the build, where the full winds enter).

**Why it stays.** None of the three is a beginning in the book's sense. Each continues a section that has already set its tonic, and each comes right after a stable arrival. The I6 keeps the music moving past those arrivals, which is the use PDF 109 gives it, and it saves the root-position weight for 53 and 61. No change.

### 5. Half cadences and passing sevenths at 22 and 26. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 99 §10 and point 8 (PDF 103): "V7 is not normally the goal of a semicadence"; 6-3g (5̂ held) is used less "because of lack of melodic activity in the soprano".

**Score.** At 26 the cellos' counter-melody runs B♭3–A♭3–G3 across the V bar, so A♭ sounds on beat 2 against G4 and E♭4 with no B♭ held. At 22 the cellos run G3–A♭3–B♭3 within beats 1–2. Bar 22's soprano holds E♭5 (5̂), but reaches it from D♮5 at 21 b3.

**Why it stays.** Both A♭s are passing tones in an inner voice, not a chord seventh held as the goal. The goal V is struck as a triad on the downbeat. The approach to 22 from ♯4̂ supplies the melodic activity that 6-3g lacks. No change.

### 6. Rhythm and metre at the two climaxes. Keep

**Point from the reading.** PDF 102 §16, PDF 103 (6-21) and PDF 115 §13 (7-18) describe the weak-to-strong rule and its exceptions.

**Score and verdict.**
- 48→49: F♯ minor is repeated over the barline. This is the initial tonic of the quotation, which "emphasizes the tonality and is therefore justifiable".
- 63→64: A minor runs from 63 b1 to 64 b2, a strong–weak–strong repetition, which the book calls good.
- 79→80: D♭ (IV) is held from a downbeat to the next downbeat, which is also good.
- 80–81: horns 1·2, bassoons and cellos move in half notes (80 b1, 80 b3, 81 b2), a hemiola, while the double basses, contrabassoon and timpani keep 3/4 (B♭ at 80 b3, E♭ at 81 b1). This is the "conflict between rhythm and meter" that PDF 102 calls "a very important compositional resource". The bass still marks the barline, so the arrival at 82 stays clear. The hemiola is protected (c171).

No change.

### 7. Bar 44: the half cadence's root sounds only on beat 1. Record only

**Point from the reading.** PDF 94 §3: at a cadence, "the arrival of V as a goal" comes with slower note values.

**Score.** At 44 the pizzicato basses and the harp's left hand strike A♭1/A♭2 as quarter notes. The cellos hold E♭3 for two beats and rest on beat 3. On beats 2–3 the lowest written note is E♭3, under the flute's held E♭5.

**Why no change.** The harp's low A♭ octave is not damped and rings through a bar at ♩=60, so the root is still heard. The c124 rule bars accompaniment changes in 37–60, and the pizz.→arco change at 44–45 is protected. The bassoon staff is hidden in 41–44 (bSec.ily line 93), so a held bassoon A♭2 would add a staff to that system and risk the 8-page layout. If the rule is ever lifted, the option is Bsn 2 `aes,2.\pp r4` at 44, moving to its B♭1 at 45. I did not test it, and I do not recommend it now.
