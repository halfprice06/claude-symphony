## Findings

### 1. Vln II "sul A" line runs into the tremolo chord at 63 (applied)
Bars 62-63. The "sul A" dashed line starts on F5 at 62 b1 and is closed by `\stopTextSpan` on the E5-C6 tremolo chord at 63. In the PDF the line crosses the double bar and the key change and ends with its hook over that chord. That says the 63 chord is also on the A string, which cannot be true: C6 has to be on the E string. Gould PDF 44: end the extension line just after the last notehead it governs, "avoid terminating the line at the end of the duration"; PDF 30 example (end of system) ends the line no later than the barline. Fix (bSec.ily, Vln II, bar 62):
```
f''2--\startTextSpan << fes''2-- { s4 s8. s16\stopTextSpan } >> | << <e'' c'''>1:32\sf\> ...
```
(the `\stopTextSpan` is removed from the 63 chord). The hook now falls just before the 62/63 barline.

### 2. Horn 3's repeat tie collides with its ppp at bar 1 (applied)
Bar 1, Hn 3-4 staff. Horn 3 (the upper part, stem up) has `c'2\repeatTie\ppp`; the tie from movement I is drawn below the notehead and runs into the ppp. Gould PDF 55: "no characters should collide"; PDF 65: in two-part writing the upper part is the up-stem part, so its tie curves up, away from horn 4. Fix (intro.ily): `c'2^\repeatTie\ppp`. The tie now sits above the notehead, clear of horn 4 and the dynamic.

### 3. Viola repeat tie touches the natural at bar 1 (applied)
Bar 1, violas, E♮4. The repeat tie's right end lands on the top of the natural sign (seen at 400 dpi). This is the bar that carries the E♮-E♭ link from movement I, so the natural must read clearly. Gould PDF 55 (no collisions, half a space minimum), PDF 64 (an accidental must be clearly beside its note). Fix (intro.ily): `e'2.-\tweak extra-offset #'(0 . 0.9) \repeatTie\ppp`. Tested in a probe against a left shift, which hit the barline instead; the raised tie is clear of both.

### 4. Horn 3's half rest at 29 sits on top of the stave (applied)
Bar 29, Hn 3-4. Horn 3 has `r2 des'4`; the part-combiner pushed its half rest above the top line. Horn 4 is two ledger lines below the stave, so there is nothing for the rest to avoid. Gould PDF 49: rests stay in the stave; PDF 50: upper-part rests go just above the centre line, and when the other part is on ledger lines the rest moves back towards the centre; PDF 51: a rest goes outside the stave only to avoid the other part. Fix (aOne.ily): `\once \override Rest.staff-position = #2 r2 des'4`. The rest now sits on the fourth line, inside the stave, at the level of horn 3's next note.

### 5. Adjacent seconds with one accidental at 58 b4 and 81 b1 (recorded, no change)
Cl 1-2 at 58 b4 (D♭5 over C♭5) and Ob 1-2 at 81 b1 (G5 over F♭5). In both, the lower part is moved right (Gould PDF 62: lower part displaced when a tone or semitone separates the parts) and the one flat is printed in front of the chord at the height of the lower note. I checked this at 300 dpi and in a probe: the flat is on the C♭/F♭ line, so the notation is correct and standard. A reminder accidental on the upper note (`des''!`, `g''!`) is possible but adds clutter. Not recommended.

### 6. Vln I "(sul D)" line at 1-3 ends at the barline of 4 (recorded, no change)
Bars 1-4. The line ends at the end of the held C5's duration, which Gould PDF 44 advises against for octave lines. c245 made this choice on purpose: the string indication covers the whole held note, as in movement I's own spanners. A string-position bracket governs the sound for its full length, so the octave-line rule does not have to apply. Kept.

### 7. Barlines, metre and clefs (recorded, no change)
The double barlines at 36/37, 46/47, 50/51 and 64/65 each mark a key change or section, not a bare metre change (PDF 53). Warning clefs and mid-bar clefs in the harp (6/7, 13/14) follow PDF 21-22. Ledger-line counts are within the limits on PDF 21 and 46-47. The harp 8 bassa lines end just after the last notehead and are restated at system breaks (PDF 42-44).
