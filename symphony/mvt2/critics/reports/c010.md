## Findings

### 1. Coda 89–93: the closing top line should be the full motto tail (applied)

**Problem.** Theme A is built on the motto's tail E♭–D–D♭–C (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂). In the coda the tail appears only in the timpani, as E♭–D♭–C at 85 and 89, because the drums are tuned A♭, C, D♭, E♭ and have no D♮. After that the violins play only D♭–C (91–93). The last top line of the movement is therefore the tail without its chromatic step. That step is the D that turns the motto into the movement's own idea. A&S PDF 600–602 shows the masters closing a movement with the motive enlarged into a slow chromatic line (K. 542: B–B♭–A–G♯ spread over the passage) and says that without this link a chromatic area "would make little sense". PDF 603 (Points 5) gives the general rule. Movement I ends with its violins playing the full motto down to C5 (177–182). Movement II ends on the same C5 but does not give the full form of its own idea.

**Evidence.** In v10 the top line at 89–96 is: rests (89–90), then D♭5 (91–92) and C5 (93–96).

**Fix (applied, coda.ily).** Vln I enters two bars earlier, one note per bar: E♭5 (89), D5 (90), D♭5 (91–92), C5 (93–96). This is E♭–D–D♭–C at movement I's pitch level, ending on C5. The violas move E♭4 → F4 at 90, so bar 90 is A♭–C–D–F over the tonic pedal (the theme's V7/V colour of bar 8, without its root), and D→D♭ is a chromatic passing step into IV at 91. The viola line becomes E♭ (83–89), F (90–91), F♭ (92), E♭ (93): the same pitches as movement I's Vln II at bars 6–8 (E♭4–F4–E♮4), with the E respelled as F♭ and turned back to E♭ (A&S PDF 597–598, 601).
```
codaVlnOne = { R2.*6 | es''2.\pp\downbow | d''2.\upbow |
  des''2.\downbow | des''2.\upbow | c''2.~\downbow | c''2.~\upbow | c''2.~\downbow\> | c''2.\upbow\ppp\fermata | }
codaVla: bar 89 <c' es'>2. (tie ends), bar 90 <c' f'>2.
```
The plagal close 91–96 keeps its pitches; only the pp moves from 91 to 89. The timpani statement at 89 is unchanged and now sounds under the violin's E♭. Cycle 1 left open that the tail appears about nine times. This is not a tenth surface statement. It is the tail augmented to one note per bar, which replaces the incomplete timpani version as the last word.

### 2. Bars 3–6: the common tone C and its neighbour D♭ fall into an inner voice (applied)

**Problem.** A&S PDF 587 says the common tone of a common-tone modulation sits "in an exposed position, usually in the soprano". Bars 1–3 do this with C5 in Vln I. From bar 4 the violins have stopped, and the clarinets' A♭4 and E♭4 lie above horn 3's C4–D♭4–C4. This is the plagal neighbour that the coda answers (D♭–C on top at 91–93), and here it is an inner voice. The movement's first statement of C–D♭–C, the motto's last step, is covered by a doubled tonic.

**Answer to the protection (1–6).** HISTORY protects the link as "Vln I C5 held; Vla E♮→E♭; Vc C→A♭" (reason: movement I critic 110, A&S PDF 585–587). The fix keeps all three, keeps horn 3's held C4 (which continues movement I's Hn 3 C4), and applies the same page 587 more consistently.

**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** Clarinets an octave lower at 3–6, in the chalumeau: Cl 1 A♭3, Cl 2 E♭3–E♭3–F♭3–E♭3. The chord is then A♭2 (B.cl.), E♭3/F♭3, A♭3 (Cl 1 + Hn 4), C4/D♭4 (Hn 3), with the horn on top.
```
introClOne = { R2.*2 | aes2.\pp | aes2. | aes2. | aes2 r4 | }
introClTwo = { R2.*2 | es2.\pp | es2. | fes2. | es2 r4 | }
```

### 3. Bars 1–2: dynamics of the link (applied)

**Problem.** Movement I ends ppp with a fermata. Movement II restates that chord at pp, so the link begins louder than the sound it continues. A&S PDF 585 says dynamics shape the effect of a modulation. In Widmung (Ex. 32-3, PDF 586) the voice holds the common tone while the new chord gets its own dynamic.

**Fix (applied, intro.ily).** Bar 1 at ppp in all strings and Hn 3. At bar 2 the voices that move (Vln II G→A♭, Vla E♮→E♭, Vc C→A♭) and the entering Cb are marked pp, while Vln I holds C5 at ppp. Hn 3 makes a small crescendo across bar 2 to pp at 3, so it matches Hn 4's pp entry on the shared staff. Pitches are unchanged.
