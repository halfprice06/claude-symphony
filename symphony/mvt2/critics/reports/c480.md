### 2. Bar 60 and the last chord: the German-sixth pun of §24 is kept for the finale (record, no change)
**Observation.** A&S PDF 524 says the German 6/5 is enharmonically V7 of ♭II, and that Chopin uses that one sonority to join contrasting sections. In this symphony the sonority A♭-C-E♭-G♭/F♯ is the hinge. It is movement I's German sixth at its bar 12 and the finale's opening German sixth on A♭. In movement II it appears once, at 60 b3-4, as V7 of D♭ (Bsn 2, Tbn 2, violas, cellos G♭3 over the A♭ pedal; spelled `ges` in bSec.ily), at the top of the build into the ff at 61. The movement's last chord is plain A♭ major (A♭1 A♭2 E♭3 A♭3 E♭4 A♭4 C5, bars 89-92), without the G♭. The finale adds the F♯ and turns it into the German sixth of C. The pun is spread across the symphony in the way §24 describes for a single piece: dominant seventh in II, augmented sixth in I and IV.

**Fix.** None. Keep the G♭ spelling at 60. Here the chord is a dominant, and §24's Note (PDF 524-525) asks for notation that follows the function. Keep the last chord without a seventh or an added sixth (c210 "no seventh on the A♭ chord at 31-36"; mvt2.md "Themes quoted": the last chord stays plain so the finale's opening resolves it).

### 3. Bars 21 and 62-65: both augmented sixths still resolve as the book requires at v480 (record, no change)
**Check, bar 21 (MIDI at the eighth).** At b1-b3 the bass rises D♭2-E♭2-E2 (F♭) against Vln I F5-G5-E♭5-D5. This is the chromatic voice exchange from IV of Points 3b and Exercise 2b (PDF 526-527), the same motion as Ex. 29-30d (PDF 518). At b3 the chord is F♭2 A♭3 A♭3 B♭3 D5, a French sixth on ♭6̂ of A♭. At 22 the augmented sixth E2-D5 opens to the octave E♭2-E♭5 (Points 1). The A♭3 is doubled (violas and upper cellos), which is the third above the bass, the doubling Points 2a gives for the Italian type. The upper cellos take it down to G3 at 22, and the violas rest at 22, so the doubled note does not move in octaves.
**Check, bars 62-65.** E major at 62 b3, the trombone's A minor at 63-64 in an A-minor signature, then E7 material at 64 b4 (first eighth E2 B2 F3 A♭3 D4, movement I's stop chord over E; second eighth E2 B♭2 E3 A♭3 D4, a French sixth). D4 contracts to D♭4, the seventh of E♭7 at 65 (Points 1). Every voice moves by semitone. Bar 64 b4.5 has no Cb/Vc fifths into 65 (c232). This is §25's "Einsamkeit" pattern (PDF 525-526): a dominant of a hoped-for key that sinks a semitone into the real one. In cyclic terms, movement I's first theme appears once in a foreign key and cannot stay there. That fits the arc in cyclic-notes, where movement I's material is resolved only in the finale.
**Fix.** None. These bars match c350's description. analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels.

### 4. Coda: no augmented sixth on ♭2̂ before the last tonic (not reopened)
**Reading.** §22 and Points 8 (PDF 522, 527) allow an augmented sixth on ♭2̂ that neighbours a final major tonic, "often at the end of the piece". Ex. 29-36 adds that the chord has a Neapolitan flavour.
**Why it stays out.** c210 and c312 rejected this with a stated reason: the finale owns the resolution of ♭II and ♭VI, and II's coda is the plagal IV6-iv-I with D♭-C on top (cyclic rule). The reading only says "sometimes", so it does not answer that reason. The coda already holds the Neapolitan colour through IV6 at 87 (c470: D♭/F = N6 of C minor) and the F♭ of iv at 88 (MIDI: Hn 4, violas E3, C.a. E4 to E♭ at 89). A third chromatic neighbour would weaken the close that the scherzo (B-A♯-A-G♯) and the finale (G-F♯-F-E) imitate a major third lower each time.
**Fix.** None.

### 5. Bar 73-74: the V4/3 semicadence still cannot take Tchaikovsky's 4/2 German sixth (record; c232 confirmed with the new voicing)
**Reading.** Ex. 29-33 (PDF 520) strengthens a weak V4/3 at a semicadence by approaching it from the 4/2 form of the German sixth.
**Check.** At 74 b1 the bass is B♭1 under V4/3, approached from IV/ii at 73. A 4/2 German sixth on 73 b3 would need C♭ against the cello counter-melody's C4. Since v470 that C4 is held into 74 as a 9-8 suspension, and since v480 Cl 2 ties E♭4 over the bar to keep D♭ off the downbeat. A C♭ on 73 b3 would sound against the held C4 in the same register and would undo both decisions.
**Fix.** None. c232's reason still holds, and it is stronger now.
