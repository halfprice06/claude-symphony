## Findings

I found nothing in the assigned reading that this score breaks. The points below are what I tested, ranked by weight, with the reason each one stays.

1. **61-62, horn weights in the climax chord (Adler PDF 15, 44).** Adler's rule above mf is horn pairs, two horns equal to one trombone; Bartók's Ex. XI-24 shows it in practice. Here Hn 3·4 are paired at f on the top brass line (A♭4, then B♭4-C♭5), and Hn 1 (F4, G♭4-A♭4) and Hn 2 (D♭4, D♭4-F♭4) are single and marked ff, against trombones and tuba at f. Pairing all four horns would leave either the third (F4) or the leading line G♭4-F4 out of the horns and reopen the gaps c264 closed. The one-degree-louder mark is the standard fallback (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 103). No change.

2. **79-81, the A′ peak without trombones (Adler PDF 38, Franck Ex. XI-20).** In the Franck page the horns play a2 f inside woodwinds and strings marked "f molto sostenuto", and the climax is made by the whole orchestra. 79-81 is scored the same way: woodwinds and strings ff "molto sostenuto", Hn 1·2 a2 f on the cellos' counter-melody, Hn 3 and Hn 4 holding the V♭9 harmony (F4, A♭3, then the F♭4-E♭4 suspension). Under PDF 15 the two single horns would be weak as lines, but they are inner harmony covered by four woodwind pairs, so their weight is not needed. It also keeps 61 as the movement's only brass climax (PDF 17). The V♭9 at 80-81 is protected. No change.

3. **60-64, the build and the trombone quotation (Adler PDF 37, Schubert Ex. XI-19; PDF 38-41, Tchaikovsky Ex. XI-21).** The brass are silent from 31 to 52, enter softly at 53 (Hn 1 with Cl 1), and the low brass come in only at 60, pp< with the timpani roll, which is Adler's "holding back" technique. Tbn 1's first sound is the movement-I quotation at 63, a new colour at a structural point, as in Schubert. Tchaikovsky puts his trombone cantus firmus inside the tutti in octaves; this score puts the tutti first and then thins to sf>p tremolo under one trombone. Both make the trombone theme stand clear. Doubling the quotation in octaves (Tbn 2 or B.tbn) as in Ex. XI-21 would weaken the once-only, single-voice quotation and change protected bars 63-64. No change.

4. **A second trombone appearance at the close (Adler PDF 37).** Schubert brings his trombone colour back "to close the recapitulation and lead to the coda". I considered a pp trombone-and-tuba A♭ chord at 82-83, Adler's "very dark" chord of Ex. XI-1 (PDF 16). I do not propose it: the timpani play their melody pp at 84-86 and a low brass chord in the same register would cover it; the coda's dark colour is already the muted horns with C.a. at 87-90; and the release at 81-83 and the coda are on the protected list. Recorded so the next critic need not test it again.

5. **Low brass spacing at 60-62 (Adler PDF 15-16, Ex. XI-1).** The tuba is an octave below the bass trombone, and Tbn 2 and the horns are in close position above. At 62 b3 the tenth between B.tbn F♭2 and Tbn 2 A♭3 is filled by bassoons and cellos and matches the low chords of Ex. XI-1. No change.

6. **Soft horn chords 3-6, 29-30, 87-90 (Adler PDF 15-16).** These are one horn per pitch at ppp-pp, which Adler allows at mf or less. The open horns at 3-6 and 29-30 and the muted horns at 87-90 are separate colours, and the darker one is kept for the coda. No change.

7. **Markings (Adler PDF 50, 52-54, Druckman Ex. XI-29; Bartók Ex. XI-24).** Mutes are announced five bars ahead (82), put on (87) and taken off (91) in score and parts. Niente ends are circled, and the quotation carries "marcato" and accents. The Hn 3·4 crescendo at 80 b3-81 b1 peaks at a hidden ff (MIDI only), and the printed parts read f < > niente. That is a normal hairpin shape and needs no printed target. No change.

8. **Trumpets tacet (Adler PDF 14, 17).** Adler's warning against overuse and his praise for Beethoven saving the trombones for the finale support keeping the trumpets for the later movements, as cyclic-notes.md requires. Dvořák 9/ii uses trumpets at its climax, so this is a recorded cyclic choice, not a copy of the model. No change.
