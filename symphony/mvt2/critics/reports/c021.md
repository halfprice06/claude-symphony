## What else I studied

I compared theme B's first statement with Dvořák 9/ii, "Un poco più mosso" (reference-corpus/pdf/DvorakA_O95__Mvt2_Largo.pdf, PDF p. 5, bars 43–48; source woods.notesL.ily, fluteImL/oboeImL): Dvořák gives his middle-section tune to Flute and Oboe I in unison at pp, and the strings' tremolo chords lie below it, never on the melody's notes. I also checked every complete statement of themes A and B (7, 23, 71; 37, 53) for contour, peak and doubling, and the climax 57–64 note by note in all parts. The tune shapes are sound: theme A's period with its B♭ peak at 12 and theme B's narrow antecedent (B♭4–G♭5) widening to a B♭5 peak at 42 are both clear and singable. Most of what stands between them and a listener's memory is how they are scored at their key moments, which is what this chapter covers.

## Findings

### 1. Climax 61–62: the theme's head has only one flute in its upper octave (applied)
Bars 61–62. At the ff arrival the melodic content is theme B's head in augmentation, A♭–D♭ | F–F♭ (from bar 41's A♭–D♭–F). Tpt 1 plays it at A♭4–F♭5 with Cl 1, Ob 2 and Vln II. The upper octave (A♭5–F♭6) is Fl 1 alone; Vln I holds D♭6 for the whole of bar 61, and Fl 2 sits on F5 then D♭6. Against four horns, three trumpets, trombones and tuba ff, one flute in its middle register does not carry, so the head is heard only at trumpet pitch and the top of the sound is a held D♭6.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 75: "To double a trumpet in the upper octave three or four wind instruments are required, but in the top register two flutes will suffice." PDF 76: Vln + Fl is the most natural unison.
Fix (applied): flute 2 joins flute 1 in unison; flute 1 marked a2.
```
bSecFluteTwo: ... R1*8 | aes''2(\ff des'''2 | f'''2 fes'''2) | R1*2 |
bSecFluteOne (bar 61): \stemNeutral \partCombineUnisono aes''2(\ff des'''2 | f'''2 fes'''2) \partCombineAutomatic | R1*2 |
```
F5 is still held by Ob 1, D♭ by Hn 3, Tpt 3, Ob 2 and violas. The brass layout of 61–62 and the F♭→E link to 63 are unchanged. If the composer wants more, Vln I could also take the head at 61 (`aes''2( des'''2`), but that moves the C6→D♭6 resolution from the downbeat to beat 3, so I did not apply it.

### 2. Theme B's first statement 37–40: the doubling hides the tune instead of colouring it (applied)
Bars 37–40. Theme B enters at p, "dolce cantabile", in Fl 1 with the cor anglais an octave below. The cor anglais's line lies inside the Vln II div. chords and the viola triplets, and it meets them in unison on the main notes: bar 37 b1 and b3 F4 (Vln II `<des' f'>`), bar 38 b1 D♭4 and b3 under the chord, bar 39 b1 G♭4 and b4 D♭4, bar 40 C4–B♭3 below the chord `<es' ges'>`. The lower octave becomes part of the accompaniment, and the first hearing of the tune is a mixed timbre where the reading asks for a single one.
Evidence: Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 66–67: melodies "demanding diversity of expression alone should be entrusted to solo instruments of simple timbres"; doubling is "more frequent in loud passages than in soft ones, also where expression and colour is broad rather than individual or intimate". Dvořák 9/ii bars 43–48 put the middle-section tune above the string chords, not among them.
Fix (applied): flute 1 alone for the antecedent; the cor anglais joins an octave below at 41, where the crescendo starts and the line rises (A♭–D♭–F–G♭–A♭–B♭).
```
bSecCorAnglais = {
  R1*4 |
  aes4(\p\<^\markup \italic "solo, dolce cantabile" des'4 f'4. ges'8 | aes'4\mp\> bes'8 aes'8 ges'4 f'4 | es'4. f'8 des'4 bes4 | c'2.)\p r4 |
  R1*20 |
}
```
This keeps cycle 9's cor anglais octave for the consequent, so it only partly undoes that fix; I did not give the cor anglais more music at 45–47 because that would put it next to the oboe's quotation at 47, which needs a fresh colour. A remaining small point: at 41 the cor anglais still meets Vln II on D♭4, F4 and G♭4. If the composer wants it clear, Vln II could rest at 41 b1–2 and enter at b3 with `<des' ges'>2`.

### 3. Close the open item "bar 31 B.cl. previewing theme B" with no change (not applied)
Bars 31–36. From the melody lens the bass clarinet's restatement of theme A's head and tail at the horn's pitch, straight after the full close at 30, is the fifth hearing of that head in 25 bars, and a theme-B preview would be welcome. But the tail note D4 on 33 beat 2 is the bass clarinet's part of the protected common-tone diminished seventh (D–F–A♭–B with Cl 1·2), and cycle 20 kept 31–36 on A♭ without V7 of D♭ on purpose. Theme B's head needs G♭ (bar 37) or the A♭–D♭ fourth leaning into D♭, which would bring back that V7. The B.cl. solo also matches Rimsky-Korsakov's example of the bass clarinet as a solo voice (No. 47, PDF 222). Recommend closing the item.

### 4. Horn solos carry the chromatic tail (no change; for the record)
Bars 8 and 68. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 73 says chromatic melody suits brass less, and that brass "lack the capacity to express passion". The horn's E♭–D–D♭–C is exactly that kind of line. PDF 72 allows the horn more freedom "in piano passages", and both statements are p and unsupported, which is the case he allows. The horn theme 7–14 and the retransition are protected; I do not ask for a change.
