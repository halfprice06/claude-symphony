## Other study

I read BRIEF, LENSES, HISTORY (all of it, including the open list) and the plan. The engraving in v35 is clean. I found no collisions, no missing dots, no stems that point the wrong way, and no case where one stem carries mixed durations. What remains is mostly about spacing and about which marks belong to which part.

Two tests I did not adopt:
- I rebroke the coda as 83–87 / 88–92, following principle 7. That squeezed the bar-87 entries (con sord. horns, Vln II, violas) to the end of a system and did not save the near-empty page 8. Keep the break at 87.
- In a first try at Finding 1 I set `common-shortest-duration` to 1/24. It changed the bar widths by less than 3%.

All fixes below are pure notation. No pitch or rhythm changed, and the allpairs output differs only in voice labels.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 57–60: the quarter beat is twice as wide in 59–60 as in 57–58 (applied)
**Problem.** In the climb to the climax, the viola figure changes from triplet eighths (57–58) to sextuplet sixteenths (59–60). LilyPond widens each sextuplet column, so the melody's quarters and halves in Fl 1, Ob 1 and the violins spread out in 59–60. Measured at 130 dpi: a quarter in Fl 1 bar 58 is about 65 px; in bar 59 it is 100–128 px. The top line looks as if it slows down on the page just as the music gathers ("poco a poco animando").
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 54: "Notes of equal duration require equal spacing for an entire system, even when the speed of the note-values changes." Her example moves from minims to quavers to triplets with the quarter unchanged. PDF 53–54: the spacing ratios are relative, so only the spacing of the shorter values should tighten.
**Fix (global.ily, bar 59):**
```lilypond
\mt 62 s1*2
\newSpacingSection \override Score.SpacingSpanner.shortest-duration-space = #1.0
\mt 64 s1 \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco allarg." \mt 62 s2 \mt 58 s2 \break
\newSpacingSection \revert Score.SpacingSpanner.shortest-duration-space
```
**Result.** Bar widths are now 316 / 340 / 325 / 327 px, and a quarter is about 80 px in all four bars. The sextuplets keep more than ½ space between noteheads (PDF 55) and do not collide.

### 2. Viola 37–60: about 90 tuplet numbers on a figure that never changes (applied; closes the open point "tuplet numbers")
**Problem.** Each `\tuplet 3/2 4 {…}` prints a "3" under every three-note group, four per bar for 20 bars. The slurs already show the grouping. The numbers add clutter in the busiest staff of the B section.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 55, "Spacing symbols": "The essence of good layout is evenly spaced symbols … each system should contain a comparable number of symbols." The numbers roughly double the symbol count in that staff compared with its neighbours. (Gould's tuplet chapter falls outside this excerpt. Printing the number only until the pattern is established is common engraving practice. I did not check this against a source for this report.)
**Fix (bSec.ily):** add `\omit TupletNumber` after the first bar of each figure, and `\undo \omit TupletNumber` before bar 53, before bar 59 (where the figure turns into sextuplets) and before bar 61. Numbers now print in bars 37, 53 and 59 only.

### 3. Horn 2, bars 75–77: the crescendo hairpin ends on the *p* of the next entry (applied)
**Problem.** `es2(\mp\< … bes2 des'4) | r2 c'4(\p\<`. The hairpin starts at 75 and has no target, so on the shared Hn 1·2 staff it runs through the rest and ends on the a2 entry's **p** at 78 beat 3. On the page this reads "crescendo from mp to p".
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 65 (double-stemmed writing) and PDF 62 make the same point: each sign on a shared staff must clearly belong to a part and to a note. Here the hairpin and the following dynamic belong to two different phrases. The other parts of the system (Fl, Cl, Bsn, strings) have their crescendos end on **f** at 78.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHnTwo):** `bes2 des'4)\mf | r2 c'4(\p\< |`. The hairpin now ends on **mf** at 77 beat 3.

### 4. Bar 33, Bassoons 1·2: Bsn 2's breath mark sits inside Bsn 1's tied F (applied)
**Problem.** `transBsnTwo` has `aes,2 \breathe aes,4~`. On the combined staff the comma prints above the stave, in the middle of Bsn 1's tie and slur (F3 tied into 34). It looks like a breath for Bsn 1 in the middle of a tied note.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 62: parts must be laid out so that it is clear "which note belongs to which part". PDF 65: the lower part's material goes with the down stems.
**Fix (trans.ily):** `aes,2 \once \override BreathingSign.direction = #DOWN \breathe aes,4~`. The comma now prints below the staff, beside Bsn 2's re-attacked A♭.

### 5. Bars 6–7 and 67: "1." and "solo" printed as two labels (applied; closes the open point "Hn 67 duplicate solo/1.")
**Problem.** The part-combiner prints "1." while the markup prints "solo" (67) or "solo, dolce espr." (6). That gives two stacked labels on one note.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 55: keep the number of symbols economical and avoid stacking characters. A single "1. solo" label is the usual form in published scores.
**Fix:** `\set Staff.soloText = "1. solo"` before the entry in `introHnOne` and `retrHnOne`. Reset to "1." at the start of `aOneHnOne` and at the end of `retrHnOne`. Remove "solo" from the markups and keep "dolce espr." at 6.

### 6. Bar-length notes in narrow bars (not applied; minor)
Bars 47–48, 51, 85–86 and 88–92 contain only bar-length notes. Gould PDF 55 puts such a note "just left of the centre of the bar". LilyPond places it at the start. The bars are already narrow and the effect is small. Fixing it would need per-bar `NoteColumn` offsets across 15 staves, so I do not recommend it.
