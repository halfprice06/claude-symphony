## Findings

I found nothing in v447 that breaks a principle of this chapter and has not already been settled with a stated reason. I recommend no change. The findings are ranked by how closely the reading bears on each place. Nothing was applied in /tmp/critic-447/work, which is an unchanged copy of v447. I rebuilt it to confirm the baseline: 0 warnings (build-warnings.txt is empty), 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels in analysis.txt. The parts book was not regenerated because no file changed.

**1. Bars 53-56: the strings have two lines only. No change.**
The violins play theme B in unison, the cellos play the bass in half notes, and the violas and basses rest. The harp's triplets and the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line (pp< to mp) fill the middle. Read on its own, Sebesky's internal-resonance rule (PDF 149: "each of the two units projects a satisfying sound, independent of the other") would ask for a third string voice. But that rule is for a string section heard alone or recorded on separate tracks. For a mixed texture his rule is PDF 144: fullness comes from "the presence of the chordal third somewhere in the ensemble", and in Ex. 194 the voice line fills the cello chord. Here the harp and the horn/clarinet line supply the third on every beat. The thin string texture is also the start of a staged build (harp 53, violas 57, sextuplets 59, basses 57), which is how Ex. 202 (PDF 151) and Ex. 215 (PDF 169) add strength bar by bar. This is c049's and c164's design. No change.

**2. Rule 7 (PDF 140, 145-146): the lowest cello and the bass. Recorded.**
Sebesky keeps the bottom cello off the bass root. Otherwise it "grounds" the voicing and makes it "obvious and a bit coarse" (Ex. 195). The score follows this where it matters most. At 41-44 the cellos have their own arco tenor line (D♭3-E♭3) over the pizzicato bass. At 23-30 and 71-82 they play a counter-melody while the basses carry the bass alone. At 53-56 the basses rest. The one long doubling is 83-86, where four solo cellos hold A♭2 over the basses' A♭1. c167 and c307 recorded this as an exception: it is the "organ pedal" of Ex. 204 (PDF 157), and Sebesky himself uses the bottom root "for temporary richness and depth" (PDF 146). No change.

**3. Rule 5 (PDF 139, Ex. 190-192): no high seconds in the strings. Follows.**
The scan found no two string notes a major or minor second apart at or above C5 anywhere in the movement. The high divisi chords are thirds, fourths and sixths: Vln I F♭6/A♭6 at 62 b3, E6/A6 at 63, Vln II E5/C6 at 63-64. Every second in the strings lies below C5, for example the violas' F4/F♭4-A♭4 at 80-81 and the coda violas, as Ex. 192 recommends.

**4. Rules 1-4 (PDF 138-139): divisi. Follows; one alternative considered and rejected.**
No section is ever divided into more than two lines. Where the line is high (62-63) the divided Vln I play thirds and fourths, as rule 4 permits. At 37-40 Vln II is divided into two sustained lines while Vln I rests. Rule 1 by itself would give the two lines to Vln I and Vln II undivided. I do not propose that. Vln I rests 37-52 so that the theme's first string statement at 53 is a fresh colour (c041). Sebesky does the same in Ex. 216 (PDF 173), where he drops a string group and brings it back. With a full second section each half still has six or seven players, more than rule 3's minimum of three.

**5. Ex. 200 (PDF 149): cello crossover to avoid an awkward line. Follows.**
Sebesky crosses his two cellos for one bar "to avoid an awkward line (E♭, D, E♮)" and switches them back as soon as the line allows. At 21 b3 the cellos divide. The upper half takes the French sixth's A♭3 and resolves it to G3, and the lower half keeps F♭ with the basses. A unison F♭3-G3 would be an augmented second into V (c192). The cellos are unison again at 23. This is the same device for the same reason.

**6. Rule 6, Ex. 203 and Ex. 215 (PDF 140, 152-153, 169): octave reinforcement. Follows.**
At 45-46 the cellos and Bsn 1 play the oboe's theme head an octave lower. At 57-60 Vln II is an octave under Vln I. At 61-62 the cellos play Vln II's line an octave lower, which is Ex. 215's "cellos voiced exactly like the violins, an octave lower". At 79-81 the theme is in three string octaves (B♭6, B♭5, B♭4). The unisons at 53-56 and 75-76 are the quiet first stages of their builds (c299, c307 #4), and rule 6 applies only to "a particularly strong ensemble sound". No change.

**7. Ex. 198 (PDF 147): in a build, at least one voice always rises. Follows.**
Bar by bar: at 57-58 the theme and the viola triplets rise. At 59-60, while Fl 1 and Vln I hold C6, the cellos and Hn 1 rise E♭3-F3-G♭3 and the violas' sextuplets climb to G♭4. At 75-77 the cello counter-melody rises E♭3-A♭3-D♭4. At 78 the cellos go C♭4-C4 and the violins' run rises into 79. No bar of either build has all lines falling or holding.

**8. PDF 166-168: horns with strings. Follows.**
The horn joins the cello line in three places only, each at a build or cadence: 29-30 (Hn 3-4), 57-60 (Hn 1) and 75-82 (Hn 2, then Hn 1). That meets "should be used sparingly". The exchanges between the groups follow Ex. 214. The Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line at 53-56 passes to Hn 1 with the cellos at 57. At 87-90 the muted horns are doubled by divided violas, each group complete within itself. A further horn doubling at 23-28 would lose the strings-only colour of the first string statement, which is protected.

**Checked and left (settled by earlier critics):** 63-64 string tremolo under Tbn 1 (Ex. 216, PDF 173; c217's optional viola rest stands, 61-65 protected); wind levels on string lines at 57 and 77 (PDF 164; c178, c357 #1); 87 IV6 in the strings without the root (c357 #6).
