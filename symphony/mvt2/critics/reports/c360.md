### 1. Bar 86: re-tested against Ex. 30-21. Keep the half-diminished chord (no change)
**Point.** At 86 the chord over the A♭ pedal is D5 (Cl 1, motto tail) with violas C4–F4: A♭–C–D–F. The scherzo (A♯°7 over E, its 357-360) and the finale use a full diminished seventh at the matching point (mvt2.md, "What the finale relies on"). Violas `<ces' f'>` would make D–F–A♭–C♭. That is the reading's common-tone °7 in its ♭3 spelling (PDF 544, §13). It is also movement I's stop chord, a third time over A♭.

**c190 and c220's reason** was that C♭ would put ♭3̂ under the line one bar before the C5 goal. The 250-dpi image of Ex. 30-21 (PDF 545) partly answers this. Brahms's strings play C–E♮–F♯–A, and the piano holds E♭ with A under the F♯, on the beat right after the melody's E♮. So a master does put ♭3 under ♯4 straight after ♮3. That alone is no reason to avoid it.

**Why it should still not change.** The reading says the upper tones of the chord are neighbours that resolve: ♯2→3, or ♭3 kept as the minor colour and then left by step (PDF 543-544, Ex. 30-20). Brahms's E♭ moves straight on to E♮. In this score the next chord is IV6 at 87 (F–A♭–D♭). It has neither C nor B♭. A C♭4 at 86 could only leap to A♭3, or force a B♭ into the protected IV6. The C♮ at 86 does move by step: it is the common tone with I, and it leads to the IV6's F and A♭ through the violas' divisi. Keep 86 as it is. Its difference from III and IV is a difference in voice leading, not a missing link. The motto tail's D♮ already gets the full common-tone °7 at 33.

### 2. The symphony's key cycle is the III♯ relation of Ex. 30-1. Add this to coherence/mvt2.md (text only)
- Chopin's Op. 28/9 (PDF 530-531) treats A♭ major as G♯ major, III♯ of E. That is exactly the join from this movement to the scherzo, where Hn 1 takes the last A♭4 as G♯4 (scherzo.md, opening).
- Movement II's own bar 1 is the same relation one step earlier: C major is III♯ of A♭, a secondary-mixture chord (PDF 534, Ex. 30-5a).
- The reading says III♯ "enter[s] into larger tonal relationships" (PDF 531) and is not a local chord. That is how both joins work. Each previous tonic becomes III♯ of the next, by common tone (C5 at 1; A♭ = G♯ at the end).
- The reading also explains why bar 1 is so short and needs no V. A&S separate local from large-scale functions, and here the large-scale meaning comes from the movement before.

**Fix (coherence/mvt2.md, key-plan table, after the augmented-sixth note).** "Each join is a III♯ relation (A&S PDF 531, 534, Ex. 30-1, 30-5): movement I's C is III♯ of A♭ at II bar 1; II's A♭ = G♯ is III♯ of E at the scherzo's bar 1, the pair Chopin uses in Op. 28/9. Record only." No score change.

### 3. Bars 46-48: the minor quotation is prepared as the reading prescribes (no change)
- 46 b3-4 is G♭ major (IV of D♭; bass G♭1, Vln II G♭4, Ob 1 D♭5).
- 47 is a full bar of D♭7 spelled C♯7 (V7 of IV).
- 48 is G♭ minor, written as F♯ minor (bass G♭2, C.a. A4).

A&S PDF 537 describe exactly this with Purcell's Ex. 30-3. An applied dominant lasting a full bar makes the listener expect the major chord. The minor form shares that dominant, so it "appears logical in retrospect." The major and minor forms of IV sit side by side, as Chopin's forms of III do (PDF 531-532). So movement I's S head in minor, the quotation, comes in on a chord the passage has already prepared. Keep it.

### 4. Bars 61-65: the minor ♭II climax and its recollection role (no change; adds to c322 and c350)
- 62 b3 is F♭ major spelled E–G♯–B (E1 in the bass; E4, A♭4, B4, E6, A♭6 above). It is the applied V of the A minor at 63. That is the single strongest preparation the reading names (PDF 537: "Preceding a chromatic chord by its own V").
- 63 is ♭II minor (double mixture), and 64 b4 is first heard as V♭9 of A minor and then becomes the augmented sixth into E♭7 at 65. This is the Schubert Ex. 30-9 pattern that c322 and c350 recorded.
- The reading adds one point not recorded before. Schubert's remote chord is also prepared because it "is a recollection of [an] earlier episode" (PDF 537). In this score the A minor is prepared the same way, at the level of the symphony: it carries movement I's P head (Tbn 1). Here the cyclic quotation does what the reading asks of a drastic alteration. That is a further reason to keep 63-64 as a single, protected statement.

### 5. Focal mixture chords stay triads, and no augmented chord is needed (no change)
- **Plain triads.** These mixture chords are focal points and have no added dissonance, as A&S PDF 537 §6 requires:

  - 62 b3 (F♭ major)
  - 63 (A minor, until the passing B♭ on b4)
  - 78 b1-2 (F♭ major, the deceptive arrival)

- **Seventh chords used for drive.** The directed mixture chords do carry sevenths: iiø7 with F♭ at 80 b3, then V♭9 at 81. That is what the same paragraph prescribes for motion toward a goal.
- **The last chord.** It stays a plain A♭ triad. mvt2.md says why: the finale's opening German sixth on A♭ resolves it.
- **Augmented chords.** The reading's augmented triads and V7♯5 (PDF 537-542) need E♮ over A♭, or B♮ → C. The cycle reserves both (c220 Finding 6). The reading itself calls these chords "momentary inflections ... a purely local function" (PDF 539). Leaving them out costs nothing at the level of the symphony.
