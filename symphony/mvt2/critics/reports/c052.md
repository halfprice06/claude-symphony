## Findings

### 1. Bars 62–63: the leading tone in the top voice skips down a diminished 4th (applied)
Problem: c051 put the upper half of Vln I on A♭6 at 61–62. At 62 b3–4 the chord is F♭ major (= E major), the V of the A minor that arrives at 63. A♭6 is now G♯6, the leading tone, and it is the top note of the orchestra. At 63 ("unis.") it falls to E6. The A at 63 is only in Vc A3, B.tbn A2 and Cb A1. The same G♯ is doubled in five octaves at 62 b3 (Timp A♭2, Tbn 2/Bsn 1 A♭3, Hn 1·2/Cl 2 A♭4, Vln I A♭6). The inner ones stop at 63, which is acceptable at a tutti cut-off. The top one moves and is heard.
Evidence: applied chords follow normal V voice leading and their leading tone is not doubled (PDF 419, 439 items 2–3). A resolution can be transferred, but only into the next lower voice in the same register or into the bass (PDF 406–407, Ex. 24-11, 24-12). The next voice down is Vln I's own lower half on E6, which is the 5th of A minor, not the resolution. A leading tone that skips down a diminished 4th in the soprano is not a transfer.
Fix (applied):
```
% bSec.ily, bSecVlnOne bar 63-64 (was: e'''1:32\sf\>^\markup \italic "unis." | e'''2:32\p r2 |)
  << { a'''1:32\sf\> } \\ { e'''1:32 } >> | e'''2:32\p^\markup \italic "unis." r2 |
```
Consequence: A6 becomes the highest note of the B section, sounding sf > p on the A-minor arrival under the trombone quotation. If the composer wants A♭6 to remain the peak (c051's aim), the second-best option is to have the upper half rest at 63. The leading tone is then abandoned rather than resolved, but it no longer skips. I did not test that option.

### 2. Bars 38–39: V7 → ii7 retrogression (applied at 38; re-raising c022 finding 6)
Problem: theme B's first phrase runs I | IV6–V7 | ii7–vi6 | vii°7/vi–vi. The A♭7 at 38 b3 moves up a 5th to ii7 at 39. Its 7th G♭ is held into a chord that is not its resolution, and it reaches F only at 39 b3.
Evidence and answer to the earlier rejection: the 7th must be able to resolve down by step, by root motion down a 5th or up a 2nd (PDF 399). c042 called this a "delayed resolution". The reading's own definition contradicts that: in a delayed resolution "the chord of resolution is extended" (PDF 409 box, Ex. 24-17a). Here the next chord is ii7, not a resolution chord. The composer's reasons were that the harmony had been accepted since cycle 1 and that the change removes the phrase's only V7. That V7 does not act as a dominant, because it goes straight to a predominant. The phrase's dominant function is the vii°7/vi at 40, which the change keeps. Changing the bass to G♭ gives a descending-3rds bass D♭–B♭–G♭–E♭–D♭ (PDF 406, Ex. 24-9; PDF 436). The 7th of ii7 at 39 then enters as a passing 8–7 from E♭ (Vla E♭4–D♭4; PDF 406 "the 7th may also come about as a passing tone", PDF 428–429 Ex. 25-21b). The melody is unchanged, and its E♭5 becomes the root of ii6.
Fix (applied, bar 38 only):
```
% bSecVc 38:    bes,4 r4 ges,4 r4 |      bSecCb 38: bes,,4 r4 ges,,4 r4 |
% bSecHarpL 38: <bes,, bes,>4 r4 <ges,, ges,>4 r4 |   (G♭ pedal already set)
% bSecVlnTwo 38: <des' ges'>2 <bes ges'>2 |
% bSecVla 38 b3-4: \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( es' ges' es' bes es') }
```
At 54 the horn/clarinet counter-line holds A♭4 on b3–4 (the top of theme A's head), and that note does not fit ii6. Either leave 54 as V7, now a varied repeat, or change the counter-line to B♭4 (`des'4.\< f'8 bes'2`) and the bass/harp figure as at 38. I did not apply 54.

### 3. Bars 29–30: doubled leading tone resolving in octaves (applied)
Problem: at the A section's full close, the leading tone G is in Vla G4 and Hn 3 G3 (plus the melody's G5 on beat 1). Both inner G's rise to A♭ at 30, so they make parallel octaves (horns pp inside the violas).
Evidence: PDF 425 Ex. 25-14c marks the doubled leading tone at a resolution "wrong", and PDF 439 items 3 and 5 say the same. Bach's solutions on the same page move one voice by skip (Ex. 25-14e: "the skip prevents doubled leading tone").
Fix (applied): horn 3 takes the inner-voice skip 7̂–5̂ and horn 4 falls to the 3rd.
```
% aOne.ily: aOneHnThree = { R2.*22 | g2.\pp | es2. | }
%          aOneHnFour  = { R2.*22 | es2.\pp | c2. | }
```

### 4. Bars 25 and 73: the cello/horn counter-melody moves in octaves with the bass (applied, counter-melody untouched)
Problem: the counter-melody A♭3–C4 (Vc at 25; Vc and Hn 2 at 73) moves in double octaves with the bass A♭1–C2 (Cb; at 73 also Bsn 2 and harp LH). It is a bass-line octave inside a line that is meant to be independent. Bar 9, the first statement, has bass A♭–D♭ and no such octave.
Evidence: the reading uses its dissonant chords to "break up the 5ths and octaves that would otherwise occur between the main chords" (PDF 404, PDF 434–435, Ex. 25-31, 25-32). Here the simplest correct solution is to let the bass hold the tonic and leave the I6 to the cellos.
Fix (applied): the bass holds A♭ for the whole bar. The cello counter-melody (protected by c051) is not changed.
```
% aOne.ily aOneCb 25: aes,,2. |   aTwo.ily aTwoCb 73: aes,,2. |
% aTwo.ily aTwoBsnTwo 73: aes,2. |   aTwoHarpL 73: <aes,, es,>2.\arpeggio |
```

### 5. Bars 69–71: the dominant 7th in bassoon 2 is abandoned (applied)
Problem: Bsn 2's D♭3 (the 7th of V7 under the horn tail, 69–70) stops at 70 b3. Bsn 2 re-enters at 71 on A♭2, so the 7th falls a 4th to the bass note. The resolution C appears only in Vla C4 and Cl 1 C5, more than an octave higher.
Evidence: a transferred resolution should be in the next lower voice in the same register, or in the bass (PDF 406–407). Neither is the case here.
Fix (applied): Bsn 2 enters at 71 on C3, the 7th's own resolution, and joins the bass at 72 as before.
```
% aTwo.ily aTwoBsnTwo 71: c2.\pp | f4 bes,4 es4 | aes,2. | es2 r4 |
```

### Noted, no change

- 60: the leading tone C appears in four octaves, but these are the melody's own octaves (Vln I, Vln II, Ob 1, Cl 1). The lower ones resolve to A♭4 as theme B's head begins. This is melodic doubling, not a chordal doubling.
