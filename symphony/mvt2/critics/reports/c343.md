## Findings

### 1. Bar 70 b3: the horn stops at the moment the violins enter (applied)

**Problem.** The retransition ends with Hn 1's motto tail E♭4-D4-D♭4-C4-B♭3 (67-70). The B♭3 is `bes2.~ | bes2)\pp r4`. Bassoons 1·2 (G3, D♭3) and the timpani roll also stop on 70 b3. At that same beat the violins start theme A's pickup E♭-F (Vln I E♭5, Vln II E♭4). So the horn's phrase ends exactly where the violins' begins, and for the pickup only the four solo basses' E♭2 sound under the violins. My gap scan finds the bars 65-70 texture at its widest there: Cb E♭2 against F4/F5 at 70 b3.5. The other section joins in the movement overlap: 30-31, 36-37, 52-53, 82-83, 88-89. c273 listed 70-71 as "Hn 1 pp under the violins' pickup", but the source does not support that. The horn rests on b3.

**Evidence.** Rimsky PDF 125-126: "In order to connect the phrases on each instrument in the best possible way, the last note of each part is made to coincide with the first note of the following one". Ex. 237 (PDF 407) shows this: the violins' figure reaches the cellos on a shared note. Dvořák 9/ii at bar 101 (above) holds the brass into the new string entry. The join into the reprise is the most important one in the form, and it is the only main join without an overlap.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-343/work/sections/retr.ily, `retrHnOne`).**
```
was:  bes2.~ | bes2)\pp \tweak staff-position #0 r4 |
now:  bes2.~ | bes2.)\pp |
```
The horn now holds B♭3, the fifth of the E♭7, under the violins' pickup and stops on the downbeat of 71, where the A♭ chord arrives. It does not sound into the tonic. The slur 67-70 lasts about 10 s at ♩=52, well under the 20 s breathing limit. The theme record `mottoTailHn` (`es'2. | d'4 des'4 c'4 | bes2.`) is unchanged. The bassoons and timpani keep their b3 stops, so the dominant thins out but does not go silent. The change touches the protected retransition 65-70 and its "held-note handovers 1-3, 65-70, 89-92". It completes that hand-over plan rather than undoing it: notes, dynamics and the horn/bassoon crossing are the same. MIDI changes in one note (Hn 1 B♭3 at 69, 5 beats to 6). It does not touch the ending, the exported themes or the quotations, so the other threads need not be told.

### 2. Proportions and the register curve (no change)

Times from the MIDI tempo map: A 20.8-103.8 s, transition to 125.4, B to 241.8, with its climax at 223.1 s (0.65 of 345.3 s). Retransition to 262.6, A′ to 305.9, with its peak at 290.4 s (0.84), then the coda. These are the shares c233 and c273 recorded, and Dvořák's are close to them. The reading adds a register test. PDF 124-125 says the whole orchestra is seldom placed at the top, and only with flutes and top violins. In this score the 5th-6th-octave register (A♭6, B♭6) appears only at 57-62 and 77-81, in Fl 1 with Vln I in both places. The lowest register (Cbn B♭0-A♭1, tuba from E1) is mostly used together only at the climax and its release, 60-64. So the register curve rises and falls with the tension curve. No change. The proportions are protected (c253, c303, c319).

### 3. Section joins follow the coinciding-note rule (no change)

I checked each join in the source against PDF 125-126.
- 22: the solo violin's E♭5 runs into the tutti pickup.
- 30-31: Cbn A♭1 and Hn 3·4 enter under the strings' last chord.
- 34-36: the C.a. ends on A♭3 over the B.cl.'s A♭2.
- 36-37: C.a. F5 as an eighth on Fl 1's first F5 (c139, c271).
- 52-53: C.a. F4 under the violins' entry.
- 64-65: Cb E→E♭ under the strings' E♭7.
- 82-83: the harp's A♭1 tied over (c214).
- 88-89: Cl 1 holds C5 through 89-90 under Vln I's C5. This is the closest copy of the rule in the score.

The 47 b4 join is a change of phrase (Ob 1's B-head ends, the quotation's pickup starts in the C.a.), not a phrase handed on, and it is protected. Bar 70 was the one exception (Finding 1).

### 4. Chromatic chords across groups keep their part-writing (no change)

PDF 126-127 warns that chords moving between groups must keep regular part-writing in chromatic passages, so that no false relation appears. The chromatic steps in this score stay inside one instrument:
- 45-47: Bsn 1 and Vc share the B-head with its B♮.
- 50-51: C.a. A4→A♭4 and Vln II D5→E♭5 lead from F♯ minor back to D♭.
- 64-65: Cb E→E♭.

No chromatic note moves in one group while the other group holds its old form. The one break, 62→63, is the tutti cut to the trombone quotation over string tremolo. The page-127 note allows that case ("when extreme contrast of timbre between two adjacent chords is intended"), and it is protected. No change.

### 5. The F♯-minor quotation 48-51: wide spacing checked (no change)

The gap scan puts 20-24 semitones between the bass (Vc/Cb F♯2-C3) and the C.a. melody (A4-D5) on the beats of 48-51. PDF 125 warns against outer parts far apart with the middle empty. Here the violas' off-beat eighths (A3, B3, D4, E♭4) sound inside the gap on every half beat. The distance is two octaves, not the four of Ex. 235, and Vln II sits a third to a sixth above the C.a., which makes the melody a two-voice line, not a bare outer part. The sparse middle suits the "sotto voce" quotation that c339 compared with Mahler 4/iii. Earlier critics checked these bars (c294, c324, c334). No change.

### 6. Imitation and echo keep the law of register (no change)

At 15-22 the oboe states each two-bar idea and the solo violin answers it higher (A♭5-B♭5 at 17, F5-G5 at 21), so the higher answer has the higher instrument (PDF 128). At 41 theme B's second half goes down an octave to the C.a. In the coda the harp's harmonics are answered lower by the timpani (83-85). At 87-90 the muted Hn 3·4 and C.a. echo the open brass F-F♭ of 61-62 at pp. This is PDF 128's "echo given to muted brass following the same phrase not muted". Woodwinds never echo strings, or the reverse. No change.
