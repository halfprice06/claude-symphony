## Verdict on the tunes

Theme A is the movement's memorable tune. It has a parallel period, a rising arpeggio to a held fifth, the chromatic motto tail and an appoggiatura peak. Three statements in rising register (horn, violins sul D, violins in octaves) let it settle. Theme B is weaker, as c031 said: its first half circles C5–G♭5 in neighbour notes, and its profile comes from the second half (41–42) and the augmented head at 61. Earlier cycles (c001, c011, c031) have reshaped theme B several times. I do not rewrite it again, but the findings below make its best moments clearer. Against Sebesky's checklist the backgrounds are strong. The violas fill the holes in the b-phrase, the counter-melodies touch the tune and then fill in chord tones, quotations appear once, and every solo entry is cued. The faults are local.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 58 and 61–62: the B section's highest note is a passing eighth; the ff arrival lies lower (applied)

**Problem.** The theme B build reaches B♭6 and A♭6 at 57–58 (Vln I, Fl 1, mf crescendo, eighths and quarters). The ff Largamente arrival at 61 has D♭6 on top, then F6–F♭6. When the music reaches its loudest point it has already left its highest register, and the tune's high point goes by unstressed.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 223: "carousel" is passed over because "the word goes by too fast", and the colour goes on "prayer", "held for six beats" at the end of the section. Here the arranging weight belongs on the held two-bar arrival at 61–62, not on the eighth at 58. PDF 210 (Ex. 277/278): in a long-note passage the top line still needs character. At 61–62 every part has half and whole notes. Dvořák 9/ii 96–97 (corpus) puts A6 ff on the arrival. This is c031's open point (Vln I divisi at 61–62).

**Fix (applied), `sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecVlnOne` 61–63:**
```
<< { aes'''1~^\markup \italic "div., molto sostenuto" | aes'''1 } \\ { des'''1\ff | f'''2 fes'''2 } >> |
e'''1:32\sf\>^\markup \italic "unis." | e'''2:32\p r2 |
```
The upper half holds A♭6. It is the fifth of D♭, the ninth of G♭maj7 and the third of F♭, and it moves to E6 at 63. I first tried c031's B♭6–A♭6 at 62. It doubled Hn 3's and Tbn 1's A♭→B♭ in octaves and took away the B♭6–A♭6 of 79–80, so I hold A♭6 instead and the A′ climax keeps the movement's only held B♭6. The lower half keeps D♭6 on the downbeat, so the leading tone C6 of bar 60 still resolves in its own line. That was c039's reason for "D♭6 on top", and the c039 resolution stays, now in the lower half. Flute 1 already leaves its C6 for A♭5. The A♭5 and F5 that dropped out of the old double stop are still in Fl/Ob/Cl/Vln II.

### 2. Bar 41: flute 1 starts theme B's second phrase in its weakest octave, on top of the cor anglais' strongest (applied)

**Problem.** At 41 the flute plays A♭4–D♭5, an octave above the cor anglais' A♭3–D♭4. The flute's low octave is weak. The cor anglais' low sixth is its fullest register. For two beats the lower octave leads, so the start of the theme's most memorable phrase sounds upside down.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 219: the arrangement must make "compensations ... for his or her shortcomings", and must not let other instruments cover a lead in a "low, weak ... register". PDF 204: the line should suit the instrument. Adler part 1 PDF 185 and 205 give the same registers (c041 finding 4, the open point "Fl 1 rest at 41 b1–2").

**Fix (applied), `bSecFluteOne` bar 41:** `r2 f''4.(\< ges''8 | aes''4\mp\> ...`. The cor anglais begins alone in its best register, and the flute joins on F5, where it is clear. Both instruments keep their entries, so c036's "flute over thin strings 37–44 (Eh from 41)" is respected. allpairs_par.py: the flute–cor anglais and flute–viola octaves at 41 b1–2 are gone, and nothing new appears.

### 3. Bars 55–56: horn 1 and clarinet 1 follow the theme's descent in sixths (applied)

**Problem.** Under theme B at 55–56 (G♭5 F–G♭–F E♭5 D♭5 | C5 B♭4), the counter-line plays G♭4–F4 | E♭4–D♭4 in half notes. It doubles the melody's G♭ on the downbeat of 55, then runs a sixth below it through 56, in the same half-note rhythm as the tune in that bar.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 211 (Ex. 279): violins that "shadow the melody too closely, resulting in an almost constant parallel motion" blur the focus. PDF 212 (Ex. 280): touch the melody's notes briefly and otherwise supply chord tones the melody and bass do not have. PDF 209: contrary motion gives a line character.

**Fix (applied), `bSecHnOne` and `bSecClOne` bar 56:** `c'2 des'2)` instead of `es'2 des'2)`. The counter-line now rises C4–D♭4 against the tune's C5–B♭4. C4 is a chord tone of A°7, and the E♭ stays in the harp. The horn's theme-A head at 53–54 and the G♭–F at 55 are unchanged. Bassoon 1's F–G♭–F (c042) is untouched. There are no new parallel pairs. The F4→C4 under D♭5→C5 is a direct octave with stepwise upper motion, which is allowed.

### 4. Bars 15–22: the top line of the b-phrase background is a re-struck A♭4 (option, not applied)

**Problem.** Vln II, the background voice right under the oboe and solo violin, plays A♭4 on beat 1 of seven bars out of eight and re-strikes it louré. The violas carry the motion, so the passage does not stand still, but the line Vln II plays would not hold a player's interest if sung.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 210 (Ex. 277/278): the lead of a soft long-note background "should have some character". PDF 216–217: sing each line, because repeated notes feel stiff. PDF 207–208 (Ex. 271): a slow inner melody within the chord, used sparingly.

**Fix (option):** tie the A♭ where the harmony keeps it (`aes'2.~ | aes'2 g'4` at 15–16 and 17–18), so that Vln II articulates only when it moves. I did not apply it, because c022 asked for the held A♭ at 17–18 and c018 set the louré. This keeps their pitches and only removes the re-strikes. The composer should decide.

### 5. Theme B's first half (37–40): no change, recorded for the overall view

Sebesky's question 6 (PDF 220) asks whether the tune is weak in some respect. Theme B's antecedent stays inside C5–G♭5 in turns. Its consequent (41) shares neither contour nor rhythm with it, so the listener carries away bars 41–42 and 61, not 37. c001, c011 and c031 reshaped this head on purpose to keep it apart from theme A. A further rewrite would undo their reasons and the derivation of 57 and 61. Findings 1–2 give the part of theme B a listener will remember its best register and its loudest moment instead.
