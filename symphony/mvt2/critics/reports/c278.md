## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 65–66: the strings strike their held chord again at the start of its release to niente (applied)

**Problem.** At 65, Vln II, violas and cellos arrive on D♭4, G3 and B♭2 over the basses' tied E♭ pedal. The chord does not change in 66. Still, all three sections play a new dotted half at 66, and that new note carries the niente diminuendo. The movement's other string release, at 1–3, is tied: `aes'2.~\pp | aes'2.\>`. So are the basses at 65–70 under it, which are marked "change bow at will".

As written, the release starts with a fresh pp attack on the downbeat of 66. This comes one bar before horn 1's solo, while the timpani are silent. It is the only event in the bar.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 149–150: the diminuendo is the continuous part of the sound that mechanical playing loses. Here the score itself breaks it with an attack.
- PDF 150 and 152: an identical attack repeated with nothing changed is heard as mechanical.
- PDF 153 and 155: a sustained accompaniment should hide its joins.
- Dvořák 9/ii, 13–14: `df\ub\<~ | df\db\>` is the same two-bar shape on one pitch, tied, with the bow change on the tie.
- Mockup note: LEARNINGS §6 says a same-pitch re-attack at pp takes 300 ms or more to reach level and leaves a 7–15 dB hole. The tie removes that hole from the render too.

**Fix (applied, `retr.ily`).**
```
retrVlnTwo = { des'2.)~\pp | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t des'2.\> | ...
retrVla = { g2.)~\pp | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t g2.\> | ...
retrVc = { bes,2.~\pp | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t bes,2.\> | ...
```
The bow change happens silently on the tie, as at 28 and 76 (c258). Pitches, the 64→65 slides (c237), the cut-back at 65–67 (c233) and the cellos' B♭ anticipation at 64 (c232) are unchanged.

### 2. Bars 57–59: the clarinets are the only unslurred sustained part in the build, and clarinet 1 re-tongues its common tone (applied)

**Problem.** Clarinets 1 and 2 enter at 57 b3 in thirds (D♭5/B♭4) and crescendo mp to f into 59. Clarinet 2 ties its B♭4 across the bar to 58. Clarinet 1 re-tongues D♭5 on the downbeat of 58, although D♭ is a common tone of the G♭ chord (57 b3) and the B♭ minor seventh (58 b1). Neither part has a slur. Around them, every melodic and sustained part in 57–60 is slurred: flutes, oboes, horn 1, cellos, bassoon 1 and the viola triplets. So the clarinets alone tongue each note in a cantabile crescendo, and the pair moving in thirds is articulated two different ways.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 151: the slur is the breath of the line.
- PDF 150: a repeated attack with nothing changed sounds mechanical.
- PDF 155: plain legato for sustained parts.
- Adler part 1 PDF 176–177 is the basis of c126's legato slurs in the woodwinds at 7–14.

**Fix (applied, `bSec.ily`).**
```
Cl 1: \partCombineApart r2 des''2~(\mp\< | des''2 c''4 des''4~ | des''2)\f r2 | c''1\< \breathe |
Cl 2: \partCombineApart r2 bes'2~(\mp\< | bes'2 aes'4 ces''4 | bes'2)\f r2 | aes'1\< \breathe |
```
Pitches are unchanged, including c049's rests at 59 b3–4, c047's octave and c254's thirds. The breath before 60 stays.

### 3. Bars 32–33: the cor anglais re-attacks under the bass clarinet's tied E♭ (applied)

**Problem.** The cor anglais shadows the solo a sixth above: C4 against E♭3, then C♭–B♭ against D–D♭ (c111, c140). The bass clarinet ties its E♭ from 32 into 33 and begins its diminuendo there (`es2.~\< | es4\mp\>`). The cor anglais strikes its C4 again on the same downbeat, inside its slur, with no tenuto.

So the accompaniment makes the attack at the moment the solo is sustaining. The shadow's rhythm also differs from the line it shadows only by that one attack.

**Evidence.**
- PDF 153 and 155: overlap in the accompaniment hides its attacks under a solo melody.
- PDF 150: a repeated attack with nothing changed sounds mechanical.
- A slur over a repeated pitch leaves the player to guess between a tie and a legato re-tongue. A tie makes the intent clear.

**Fix (applied, `trans.ily`).**
```
transCorAnglais = { R2. | c'2.~(\p^\markup \italic "dolce" | c'4 ces'4 bes4 | aes2.) | R2. | r2 c''4(\p | }
```
c276's p is unchanged.

### 4. Bars 3–4: the winds re-attack an unchanged chord under horn 3's tied C, and horn 4's print and MIDI disagree (not applied: inside the protected link)

**Problem.** At 3–4 the A♭ chord does not change. Horn 3 ties its C4, but clarinet 2 (E♭3), the bass clarinet (A♭2) and horn 4 (A♭3) are struck again on the downbeat of 4. On the shared horn staff, c125's `hnFourNoTie` hides the A♭ tie that `\partCombineChords` creates. So the score and horn 4's part show a re-attack, while score.midi holds horn 4's A♭3 from 3 to 5. The re-attacks at 5 and 6 fall on chord changes (iv6/4 and I) and are right.

**Evidence.** PDF 150 and 153, as in findings 1 and 3.

**Suggested fix (for the composer).** Tie the three notes from 3 into 4 and remove the `\once \override Tie.stencil = #hnFourNoTie` in `introHnFour`:
- `introClTwo = { R2.*2 | es2.~\pp | es2. | fes2. | es2 r4 | }`
- `introBassCl = { R2.*2 | aes,2.~\pp | aes,2. | aes,2. | aes,2 r4 | }`
- horn 4 `aes2.~\pp | aes2. | ...`

The protected link's reason is the common-tone pitches (A&S PDF 585–587), and that reason is not touched by this change. I did not apply it, because a soft re-pulse at 4, where the strings' niente ends, may be intended. If the composer keeps the re-attack, horn 4's MIDI should match the part.
