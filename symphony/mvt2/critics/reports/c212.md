## Findings

### 1. Bar 53 b3–4: doubled leading tone in the movement's only V6. Applied

**Problem.** At 53 b3 the cellos step to C3 under the violins' F5–G♭5–F5–E♭5, and the harp's triplets play A♭3–C4–E♭4 | C4–E♭4–C4. Over C3 the chord is A♭/C, V6 of D♭. Its leading tone sounds in two octaves (C3 and C4) for two beats. Neither C goes to D♭: the cellos continue C3–B♭2 and the harp C4–G♭3 into IV6 at 54.

**Evidence.** PDF 108 §4: "In V6, the leading tone must never be doubled. Both the remaining tones of V6 (2̂ and 5̂) are possible choices." Ex. 7-5e (PDF 109, image checked) marks the doubled leading tone in V6 "wrong"; Points for Review 3 (PDF 116) repeats it. Here the bass itself cannot resolve the leading tone (Finding 3), which makes the second, unresolved C in the harp the more noticeable.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-212/work/sections/bSec.ily, bSecHarpR, bar 53):**
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 es' aes' es' aes' es' } |
```
(was `\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' c' es' c' } |`). The V6 keeps its 5̂ doubled (E♭4 with the violins' E♭5) and its root in the harp, the doubling the reading prefers. It respects both earlier reasons for this figure: no A♭3 on beat 4, where horn 1/clarinet 1 enter on A♭3 (c137), and the figure ends on E♭4, not A♭4, so there are no fifths A♭4/E♭5 → G♭4/D♭5 into 54 (c142). The A♭4 in the middle of beat 4 is a fifth below the violins' E♭5, followed by E♭4, so no consecutive fifths result. No new harp pedal (A♭ and E♭ are in the existing diagram). The engraving on page 4 is unchanged in layout.

### 2. Bars 8→9, 24→25, 72→73: keep the root-position tonic after the motto tail; do not substitute I6

Unit 7 recommends I6 instead of I to soften a mid-phrase arrival (PDF 115 §12), and the obvious application here would be a C in the bass at 9, 25 and 73. The reading itself rules it out: at each of these bars the soprano resolves the seventh D♭ to C (4̂–3̂) while the bass moves E♭→A♭. With a C in the bass the progression becomes V7–I6, which PDF 109 (Ex. 7-7) says to avoid because 4̂–3̂ against 5̂–3̂ makes hidden octaves, "particularly if I6 is in a rhythmically strong position", as these downbeats are. The score already uses the other remedy the reading gives, a soprano on 3̂ rather than 1̂ (PDF 117), so the arrival does not sound like a cadence. No change; recorded so a later critic does not try the I6.

### 3. Bar 53: V6 moving to IV6 (bass D♭–C–B♭). Keep

Ex. 7-10b (PDF 111) grades a V6 whose leading tone "can't resolve" as "less good", and PDF 111 says V6 "normally continues on to I". At 53→54 the bass C3 descends to B♭2. Here C is a passing note in the stepwise descent D♭–C–B♭–G♭–E♭–C–A–B♭, the B3 statement of the bass that descends in thirds at 37–40 (protected, c107). A&S's objection is to a V6 that should resolve and does not; in a descending line the 7̂ is not heard as a leading tone that has to rise. With Finding 1 applied, no upper voice carries the C, so nothing is left unresolved. No change.

### 4. Bar 42 b3: viola pizzicato C4 with the cellos' C3 on V7 of D♭. Record

The scan finds a second doubled leading tone: the violas' pizzicato triplet at 42 b3 (A♭3–C4–E♭4) sounds C4 over the cellos' C3 in the V7 before 42 b4. Unlike bar 53, both Cs go to D♭ (cellos C3–D♭3; violas' next D♭4 on b4's second triplet), and the viola's C is one plucked note inside an arpeggio. The alternatives are worse: A♭4 on the top of the figure crosses above the cor anglais's held G♭4, the register c057 and c108 kept clear for the solo, and G♭4 doubles the seventh the cor anglais carries. No change; record it as a known arpeggio doubling. The same applies to 58 b3 (violas' C4 inside the figure, clarinet C5 → C♭5 for V7/IV, a chromatic inflection under the f tutti, c013).

### 5. Large-scale use of 6/3 and 5/3: follows the reading. No change

PDF 109 and PDF 112 ask for root position at beginnings, endings and section-long expansions, and for 6/3 chords to carry the intermediate steps. Checked bar by bar: every cadence chord and every new section is root position; each pedal (31–36, 65–70, 83–92) is root position; the 6/3 chords are on intermediate steps (11 b2, 15, 18, 41, 57, 75 b2) and on the plagal IV6 at 87, whose tonic at 89 is root position. 15 opens the b-phrase on I6 directly after the PAC, the Schumann case of Ex. 7-6 (PDF 109): the I6 continues the tonic so that 15 does not sound like a second beginning. No change.
