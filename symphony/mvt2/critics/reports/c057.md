## Findings

### 1. Bars 41–44: the viola triplets lie on the cor anglais' own notes (applied)
**Problem.** At 41 the cor anglais takes theme B's lower octave, A♭3–B♭4. The violas' slurred triplets cover A♭3–G♭4 in the same octave, and they attack the solo's pitches on the solo's beats: A♭3 on 41 b1, D♭4 on 41 b2, E♭4 and D♭4 at 43, and C4 under the cor anglais' held C4 at 44. At 41 b3, the violas' G♭4 rubs a semitone against the cor anglais' F4. This is the movement's quietest theme statement, and the solo sits inside a sustained bowed figure that has the same notes.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13 says the arranger must see "that the listener's ear is directed to the primary element and not diverted or confused by an overbusy background". Ex. 1 (PDF 12–13) solves the same problem by taking the accompaniment out of the soloist's way. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 140 recommends pizzicato strings for such figures. c054 F4 raised this and left it as protected. The protection covers the six-per-bow bowing (c018) and the triplets under the flute at 37–40 (c027). This fix leaves 37–40 and 53–60 as they are and changes only the four bars where the cor anglais plays.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily, bSecVla).** Same pitches, plucked:
```
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8\p\<^"pizz." des' f' des' aes des' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' ges' des' bes des' } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8\mp\> des' f' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8 des' ges' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 c' es' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' f' } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes es' bes ges bes } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 des' es' } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8 c' es' } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8\p c' es' c' ges c' } r2 |
\stemNeutral f1\pp^"arco" | ...
```
The rest at 44 b3–4 gives time to change to arco. In 41–44 the cello's arco tenor line (c037) is now the only sustained string part under the solo, and the whole accompaniment is plucked (Vla, Cb, harp LH). This is a new colour for the second phrase (Sebesky PDF 15). The plucked figure also prepares the harp's version at 53.

### 2. Bars 7–12: harp RH chord tops strike the note the horn is holding (applied)
**Problem.** c019 kept harp notes from sounding above the horn when the horn attacks. But the harp's beat-2 chord still lands on the horn's own held pitch: A♭3 at 7 and 11, C4 at 9 b2, G3 at 10, and F4 at 12 b1 (the horn's tied F4). At 9 b3 the harp's A♭3 attacks together with the horn's A♭3. Each of these re-articulates the solo note with a pluck, which blurs the horn's sustained tone. This is the movement's first statement of the theme.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 13 (focus) and PDF 12–13 Ex. 1 (remove the inner note so the solo dominates). The open item "harp RH tops on the horn's pitch 7–14" has been listed since c054.
**Fix (applied, aOne.ily, aOneHarpR).** Take off the top note of each chord. Bar 9 b3 becomes a single F3, because E♭3–D♭3 under the horn's B♭3–A♭3 made fifths:
```
\clef bass r4 <c es>2\arpeggio | R2. | r4 <es aes>4\arpeggio f4 | r4 <bes, es>2\arpeggio |
r4 <c es>2\arpeggio | <aes des'>4\arpeggio r2 | ...
```
The harmony is still complete, because the bassoons and bass clarinet hold the same chords. Bars 13–14 are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 57–58: the lower-middle register is crowded and two wind parts add nothing (applied)
**Problem.** At 57 b1, F3 sounds in B.cl., Bsn 1, Bsn 2 and Vc, with F2 below in the basses. At 58, Bsn 1 plays F3–E♭3–E♭3–F3 against the counter-line's F3–E♭3(tied)–F3 in Vc and Hn 1. It doubles that line but re-strikes the tied E♭, so the doubling is ragged. Also at 58, Cl 1 strikes D♭5 three times in quarters. That makes six layers moving in quarters under the theme's eighths, plus the viola triplets.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12: "every note … should be there for a specific reason, making a legitimate difference … If it does not … it should be erased." Ex. 1's cut lightened exactly this "lower-middle register" (PDF 13).
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Bsn 1: `\partCombineApart r1 | r1 | ges2\f es2 | es1\< \breathe |`. Bassoon 1 joins the counter-line at the f of 59, together with Bsn 2's f, so the shared staff has one dynamic. Cl 1 at 58: `des''2 c''4 des''4`. The B♭ that Bsn 1 supplied at 57 b3 is still present in Cl 2 and the violas.

### 4. Bar 71: the third statement of theme A has the same bass as the first two (applied)
**Problem.** At 7, 23 and 71 the theme's first phrase has the same bass (A♭ | F B♭ E♭ | A♭ | E♭). Above it the texture changes (horn, then violins and cello counter-melody, then octaves and viola eighths), but the bass never does. The A′ is where the reharmonisation would mean most.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 19: "I usually try to avoid harmonizing a melody the same way twice." In Ex. 3/3A the tune is reharmonised and still reaches "the same resting place".
**Fix (applied, aTwo.ily).** Basses `c,2.\pp` at 71 (was `aes,,2.`). Harp LH `<c, c>2.` (was `<aes,, es,>2.`), so it stays on the bass (c026). Bassoon 2's C3 is unchanged. The six-bar E♭ pedal now resolves V7→I6, which softens the joint at the reprise. The bass C–F–B♭–E♭–A♭ adds one link to the protected fifths chain at 72. Bars 73–82, the cadences and the counter-melody are unchanged. My scan finds no new parallels apart from Bsn 2 doubling the bass in octaves at 71–72. If the composer wants a root-position downbeat for the reprise, this is the one finding to drop.

### 5. Bars 75–78: harp RH chords duplicate the viola figure's register (not applied)
**Problem.** c034 took the harp RH out at 71–74 because it repeated the viola eighths. At 75–78 it comes back in the same register, A♭3–F4, on the violas' own notes: `<aes c' es'>` at 75 b2 against the violas' C4–E♭4–A♭4, and `<aes des' f'>` at 76 b1 against F4–A♭4. This is the open item "harp RH/viola overlap 75–78".
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 12 (economy) and PDF 15 (each colour used for a purpose).
**Fix (suggestion).** Harp RH rests at 75–77 and enters at 78 b3 with `<aes c' f'>4\arpeggio` on the vi chord after the ♭VI. That keeps the harp's entry for the turn to the climax. In aTwoHarpR: `R2.*7 | r2 <aes c' f'>4\arpeggio |`. Not applied, because c034's crescendo entry at 75 was a deliberate choice.

### 6. B section: no group answers another (suggestion, not tested)
**Problem.** The A section has the overlapping oboe–violin–viola dialogue (15–22). From 37 to 52, theme B moves from instrument to instrument but no other group answers it. In the phrase-end bars (44, 52), where the melody holds, nothing happens.
**Evidence.** Sebesky Ex. 2A (PDF 18): the cor anglais holds and the flutes, then the bass clarinet, fill the held notes in another colour. PDF 11: take the fill from a motive already used.
**Fix (suggestion).** At 44, while flute and cor anglais hold C, Cl 1 plays theme B's neighbour figure (Bsn 1's F–G♭–F of 55–56) as a fill: `r4 f'8(\pp ges'8 f'2)`. Check it against the A♭7 harmony and the cellos' E♭3 before adopting it.
