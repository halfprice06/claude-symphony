I read the harp, theme and counter-line source in aOne.ily, bSec.ily, retr.ily, aTwo.ily and coda.ily, and looked at score pages 1 and 7 at 85 dpi (bars 7–14 and 79–86). I also checked Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139 (harmony in a melody's own register covers it). The reference corpus (README.md) has no slow movement with harp. Dvořák 9/ii and 7/ii and Tchaikovsky 6/iv and the Canzonetta write their tunes without harp, so a light harp part that carries the tune only once is normal for this style.

On the lens question itself. Theme A is the tune a listener will take away: a rising A♭–C–E♭ head in a dotted rhythm, a held E♭, the chromatic fall E♭–D–D♭–C, and an eight-bar period with its peak on the B♭ appoggiatura (12, 28, 76). It stays inside a ninth in the horn, as Dvořák's Largo tune stays inside a narrow pentatonic span, and it is heard complete three times in three colours (horn 7, violins 23, violins and winds 71 with the B♭6 climax at 79). Theme B is more ornamental (lower-neighbour turns); its second phrase with the rising A♭–D♭–F (41–44, 57–58) is the part that sticks, and the 61 climax augments it. The coda then breaks theme A into its parts across three colours, which is how Dvořák ends the Largo. The reading does not call for a change to either theme.

## Findings

No finding needs a score change. All five record that the score follows the reading, or explain why a departure from one of its models is right for the melody.

### 1. Bars 82–88: the harp–timpani–clarinet chain passes theme A on at one pitch (follows; no change)

**What I checked.** At 82 b3 the harp plucks the pickup E♭4–F4 pp in the middle register. At 83 the LH plays A♭3–C4–E♭4 as harmonics, sounding A♭4–C5–E♭5, in the theme's own rhythm (4. 8 4). The timpani answer with the mirror E♭–C–A♭ at 84, and clarinet 1 starts the chromatic tail on the same E♭5 at 85 (E♭5–D5–D♭5, 85–88).

**Evidence.** Ex. IV-9 (Debussy, *Nuages*, PDF 116) has a flute phrase continued by harp harmonics, which is the same handover between colours. Every condition on PDF 116 holds: written range inside C3–A5, three harmonics within a perfect fifth in one hand, "o" notation with "sound 8va", and only a ppp cello and bass pedal underneath. The pickup is plucked, not a harmonic, so it lies in the "rich and warm" middle register (PDF 113–114) and is not lost under the held p>pp chord. The harmonics end on E♭5 and the clarinet takes the tail from that pitch, so the theme's held E♭ and its fall are heard as one line across three instruments. That is why the head stays in a listener's memory at the end. No change.

### 2. Bars 84–92: the harp does not close the movement (follows the brief; no change)

**What I checked.** In Ex. IV-7 (Brahms, Requiem, PDF 115) harp arpeggios end the movement on the last chord. Here the harp is silent after 83.

**Evidence.** c050 removed a harp chord at 91 because a new attack inside the closing diminuendo repeated movement I's last scoring. The plagal close 87–92 with C5 on top is on the standing list. The harp's last sound is theme A's head, and that serves the tune better than a closing chord would. No change.

### 3. Bars 7–14, 53–56, 75–78: close dyads and a narrow triplet figure instead of wide chords (departure justified; no change)

**What I checked.** PDF 114 calls wide three-note chords per hand the most effective. Ex. IV-8 (PDF 116) spreads the arpeggio over two octaves. Here the RH uses close dyads under the horn's theme (7–14) and around the counter-melody (75–78), and the triplets at 53–56 stay inside A♭3–A♭4.

**Evidence.** In each place the wider version would strike the melody's own pitches or the counter-line's: the horn's A♭3–B♭4 at 7–14, the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line built from theme A's head at 53–56, and the cello/horn counter-melody at 75–78 (c019, c127, c137, c361). Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 139 says harmony in a melody's own register covers it. The wide spacing that PDF 114 asks for is already in the LH octaves (A♭1–E♭2 and similar). For this lens the tune comes before the harp idiom. No change.

### 4. Bars 37–44: no flute–harp dialogue under theme B's first statement (not applicable; no change)

**What I checked.** Ex. IV-4 (Mozart, PDF 114) has flute and harp trading the figure. At 37–44 the harp plays solid low octaves with the pizzicato bass, three octaves below the flute.

**Evidence.** The solid octaves follow PDF 114–115 (solid unless a wavy line is written) and Ex. IV-6. A harp answer between the flute's phrases at 40 and 44 would collide with the cor anglais's entry at 41 and with Fl 1's protected descant at 41–44 (c090, c101, c511). Both pedal changes get a full beat (PDF 113). No change.

### 5. Pedal plan and spelling (follows; no change)

**What I checked.** The diagrams read D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G A♭ at 1, 7 and 71 (A♭ major) and D♭ C B♭ | E♭ F G♭ A♭ at 37 and 53 (D♭ major). The A pedal moves only at 39 b4 (to A♮ for 40) and 41 b4 (back to A♭). The G pedal moves only in long rests (15–36, 57–70). No bar needs two forms of one letter, so none of the enharmonic spellings of PDF 113 are needed, and no foot changes two pedals at once (PDF 112). No change.
