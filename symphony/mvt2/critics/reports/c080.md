## Findings

### 1. Bar 87: voice the plagal close's D♭ chord over F (cyclic-notes point (a)). Applied.

**Bars.** 86–88 (coda), Vc and Cb.

**Problem.** Movement I always puts F in the bass under its structural D♭ chords (7, 21, 141, 173; c180 finding 3), which makes them the Neapolitan sixth of C minor. Movement II is in A♭, a third below C, and states its only A-section D♭ triad at 87–88, both bars over a root-position D♭ (Vc D♭3, Cb D♭2). Nowhere does the symphony let the listener hear the C-minor Neapolitan become A♭'s IV.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 494, §14: "the most frequent use of ♭II as pivot: the Neapolitan 6th of a minor key becomes IV6 in the major key a 3rd lower". The Schubert example (Ex. 28-19, PDF 495) gets its effect from a 6/3 chord changing meaning. With F in the bass, bar 87 becomes the textbook N6 of C: ♭2̂ (D♭5, Cl 1) alone on top, which A&S call the most intense arrangement (PDF 483), and 4̂ (F) doubled in the bass, "the best tone to double" (PDF 484). It is approached from A♭ in the bass, which is VI → N6 in C, Mozart's approach in Ex. 28-1 (PDF 482, 485), and I → IV6 in A♭. Bar 88 then reaches the root D♭ with the F♭ mixture. A&S describe this kind of change of position within one harmony in Ex. 28-13 (PDF 489), where a chord that begins in one position "moves immediately" into another.

**Protection.** The c020 protection covers the plagal close's bass and top line. The cadence itself is unchanged: 88→89 is still iv–I over D♭–A♭, Cl 1 still plays D♭5–C5 and Vln I still holds C5. Only the first of the two subdominant bars moves to first inversion. The D♭ triad still sounds only in the plagal close (c060 protection). The F♭→E♭ thread in Vla, Hn 4 and Cl 2 is untouched.

**Voice leading.** 86→87 has a hidden octave between the bass (A♭→F) and the violas (F4→F3), in an inner voice, which is acceptable. 87→88: the bass goes F→D♭ while Vla, Hn 4 and Cl 2 go F→F♭, so the octaves resolve by contrary and oblique motion. 88→89 is as in v80. The all-pairs diff against v80 shows only the Vc/Cb octave doubling moving F→D♭, the same kind of entry as the existing D♭→A♭ entry at 292. analysis.txt has 0 outer-voice parallels. I also checked the inner voices at 86–89 by hand: there are no new fifths or octaves.

**Fix (sections/coda.ily).**
```
codaVc: ... aes,2.\< |  f,2.\pp | des2. | aes,2.~ | ...      (was des2.\pp | des2.)
codaCb: ... aes,,2.\< | f,,2.\pp | des,2. | aes,,2.~ | ...   (was des,2.\pp | des,2.)
```
The Cb's F1 lies within its range (lowest E1). Page 8 is unchanged in layout.

### 2. For movement I: the harp retuning cue at movement I bar 176 is obsolete. No change in movement II.

Movement I v201, `codaHarpL`, prints "Retune lowest D string (D1) to D♭ for movement II" (cycle 156, from movement II's c050 relay). Since c076 (v77), movement II has had no scordatura: bar 9's D♭ is D♭2. The lowest harp note anywhere in v80's score.midi is E♭1 (MIDI 27), played on the E string. The cue asks the harpist to retune a string movement II never uses, and the harpist has to undo it before movement III. Remove the markup from movement I's bar 176 (`R1_\markup ...` → `R1`). The comment in movement II's intro.ily (`% c050: retune during 1-6`) is also stale and could go.

### 3. Brass colours reserved for the finale (point (b)): none appear in movement II. No change.

- **Tuba melody:** the tuba plays only 60–62, a held bass (A♭1, D♭2, G♭1–F♭1).
- **High four-horn unison:** none. At 61–62 Hn 3 has the top line and Hn 4 plays an octave lower (c069), with Hn 1 resting. At 79–81 Hn 1·2 play the counter-melody at f while Hn 3·4 hold harmony (F4 and A♭3), so the four horns are never in unison.
- **Full low-brass bass unison** (tuba, trombones, bassoons, cellos and basses; Adler PDF 33–34 in mvt I c181): at 61–62 the bass D♭–G♭–F♭ is in B.tbn, tuba, Cbsn, Bsn 2, B.cl. and Cb. Tbn 1 rests, Tbn 2 holds harmony, the cellos play the tenor line, and the low brass are mf. It is a held bass doubling of the kind movement I uses at 158–161, not the melodic unison. Keep it, and do not add Tbn 1·2 or the cellos to that line in later cycles.

### 4. Key plan A♭ → D♭ → C (point (c)) and the long-range ♭II. Confirmed. For movement III.

In C, movement II is ♭VI (A♭) and its middle section is ♭II (D♭, 37–61). The section is prepared by its own dominant, the A♭ pedal at 31–36, which is A&S's tonicized ♭II (PDF 491, Ex. 28-16). The symphony therefore runs I–♭II–(V)–I across three movements, Beethoven's Op. 57 plan (Ex. 28-17, PDF 491–492). Inside movement II, the A-minor quotation at 63–64 is a tonicized ♭II of A♭ (B♭♭ minor). It reaches V7 at 65 through a bass A–D–E–E♭ that fills the tritone A&S warn about for root-position ♭II → V (PDF 487), and the filling is the movement's E/F♭→E♭ thread. This is master-level long-range planning; keep it. With Finding 1, the sonority movement II's close plants is exactly C's ♭II6. A&S stress that ♭II's intensity comes from keeping ♭2̂ from reaching 1̂ until V moves to I (PDF 483). Movement III should therefore resolve its D♭ episode ♭II6 → cadential 6/4 → V7 → I (c180's plan), not D♭ straight to C. That straight move would be the coda-neighbour use of Ex. 28-20, which movements I and II have both used already (mvt I 178–180; mvt II 3–6).

The Verdi Kyrie ending (♭VI–♭II–V7–I, ppp morendo, PDF 489–490) is the obvious model for an "inward" close in major. Movement II should not borrow it: the c070 protection keeps ♭VI out of the coda, and ♭II of A♭ is A major, the key of the trombone quotation's minor. I record it only so later critics do not raise it again.

### 5. Bar 49: the Neapolitan under the cor anglais quotation is textbook. Do not touch.

Beat 2 is iv7 (B–D–F♯–A). The violins' F♯5→G5 over B turns it into the N6 by a 5–6 motion (Ex. 28-7, PDF 486). The bass B is tripled (Vc, Cb, Vla B3; PDF 484). ♭2̂ (Vln II G5) is on top and drops a diminished 3rd to E♯5 over V on beat 4 (Ex. 28-2a, PDF 484). The G/G♯ cross relation is between voices (Vla G♯3), which A&S allow (PDF 484). The quotation's D5 (cor anglais) sits inside the chord as its third. Everything here agrees with Unit 28.

### 6. Bars 62–63: Fl 1 A♭6→A6 is written as an augmented unison. Keep.

A&S §18–19 (PDF 496–497) want a chromatic step written as a minor 2nd (G♯–A). Here the A♭6 is the third of the F♭ chord, which every other part spells with flats. That spelling is the protected F♭/E→E♭ thread (cycle 10). PDF 490 allows enharmonic notation in many-flat keys, "guided by the sound". If the composer ever respells, LilyPond 2.24 accepts an enharmonic tie (I tested `aes'''2~ gis'''2 | a'''4` with no warning). I do not recommend it while the F♭ spelling is protected.

The timpani tuning (G, A♭, C, E♭) matches. The viola's E♮→E♭ falls inside the same instrument. Nothing to change.
