## Findings

### 1. Bar 78: F♭ goes to F♮ before E♭ (applied)
**Problem.** The deceptive cadence V7–♭VI at 78 is right (Madamina model, PDF 390–391). On beat 3, though, the bass (Bsn 2, Cb; harp LH F1–C2) moves F♭→F♮ to vi, and at 79 F♮ sounds in the viola, Hn 3, Fl 2 and Ob 2. ♭6̂ is replaced by ♮6̂ before 5̂ is reached. A&S name this exact succession as poor: "♮6̂–♭6̂–5̂ is good; ♭6̂–♮6̂–5̂ is poor" (PDF 395, point 3; PDF 388: ♭6̂ "will not normally be replaced by ♮6̂ before 5̂ is reached"). Movement I's critic 170 asks for the same thing in cyclic-notes ("F♭ should resolve to E♭, not to F♮ first"). Bar 78 is the only one of movement II's six F♭ places that breaks the rule. The other five are fine: bar 5 (E♭–F♭–E♭); 62 (F♭=E → E♭ in the bass at 65); the It+6 at 64 (E → E♭); 80 (F–F♭–E♭); 88 (F–F♭–E♭). The cyclic point: F♭/E♮ is the pitch movement I ends on as its Picardy third. In movement II it is ♭6̂, and each time it falls to E♭ the movement takes movement I's last bright note back down. At 78 that note slides up to F instead.

**Answer to the protection.** Cycle 23 added "F♭ major (♭VI)… bass F♭→F to vi on beat 3" and cited "chromatic inflection, PDF 584–585". Cycle 43 protects "78–80 approach". The fix keeps ♭VI on beats 1–2, the melody, the grace run and the counter-melody. Only beat 3's harmony changes. Unit 23 gives the stated reason for the F♮ (chromatic inflection) only in the order ♮6̂→♭6̂ (Ex. 23-2f), not ♭6̂→♮6̂.

**Fix (in /tmp/critic-070/work/sections/aTwo.ily).** The bass falls F♭–E♭–D♭ (♭6̂–5̂–4̂), so beat 3 is a passing I6/4 between ♭VI and the ii6/5 of 79. The melody's C6 on beat 3 is a chord tone; the viola's C4/A♭3, the cellos' A♭3 and Hn 2/Bsn 1's C4 already fit.
```
aTwoBsnTwo  bar 78: fes2\f es4      (was fes2\f f4)
aTwoCb      bar 78: fes,2\f es,4    (was fes,2\f f,4)
aTwoHarpR   bar 78: r2 <aes c' es'>4\arpeggio   (was <aes c' f'>)
aTwoHarpL   bar 78: r2 <es,, c,>4\arpeggio     (was <f,, c,>)
```
Rejected variants. Viola `fes'( es' c')` made parallel octaves F♭–E♭ with the bass. Harp LH `<es,, es,>` made E♭–A♭ octaves with the violas into 79. Viola `fes'( c' es')` made parallel fifths E♭4/A♭3 → A♭4/D♭4 with the cellos. The viola therefore stays as written (`fes'( c' aes)`), and its F♭ resolves through the bass. A stronger alternative follows Madamina closely: keep ♭6̂ through to a borrowed iiø6/5 at 79 (D♭–F♭–A♭ under the B♭6). That would change the protected climax chord, so I only mention it.

### 2. Bars 62–63: the top voice's G♯6 is split between flute and violins (applied)
**Problem.** Since c059, Fl 1 holds A♭6 (= G♯6) at 61–62 and then stops. The A6 of the A-minor bar enters only in Vln I, whose own line leaps F♭6→A6. Cycle 52 meant the leading tone to rise to A6 in one voice. Now the step A♭6→A6 is split between two instruments at ff, at the top of the texture. A&S Ex. 23-12a/b (PDF 394): a chromatic succession split between voices is the cross relation to avoid, especially in an outer voice. Kept inside one voice it is "unobjectionable".

**Fix (bSec.ily, Fl 1).** `\stemNeutral aes'''1--~\ff | aes'''1( | a'''4) r4 r2 | R1 |` (was `aes'''1--~\ff | aes'''1 | R1*2 |`). The flute resolves its own note on the downbeat, in unison with Vln I's A6, and is gone before the trombone's first accent (63 b2). Trombone 1 stays the only wind after that, so the protected "Tbn 1 alone/over tremolo 63–64" is unchanged. Flute 2 rests, so the shared staff carries no second dynamic.
