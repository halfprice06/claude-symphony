## Findings

### 1. Bar 79: the cello counter-melody is marked f inside the ff tutti, below the bassoon that doubles it (applied)

**Problem.** At 79 every string section except the cellos goes from f to ff: Vln I/II and violas on the theme in three octaves, basses on D♭. The cellos carry the counter-melody D♭4–C4–B♭3 and the hemiola that follows, and they stay at the f printed at 78. Bassoon 1 plays the same line ff. It must, because it shares a staff with bassoon 2, which is ff on the bass. Horns 1·2 play it at f. So the weakest instrument on the line has the lowest marking, and a single woodwind is marked a level above the cello section it doubles.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 168: "In any large mixed ensemble in which strings, reeds and brass are combined, the strings are acoustically the weakest element." PDF 164: one woodwind equals about sixteen violins, and when a wind doubles strings, "I indicate a lesser volume marking for the flute than I do for the strings". The marking here runs the other way. c074 set f so that the line would stay "middleground under the ff theme". That grading is still in place: the horns are f (c034, c174, protected), and the theme has three string octaves plus Fl, Ob, Cl, C.a. With the cellos at f, the middleground is only horn and bassoon colour. The line the movement has protected since cycle 1 (cello counter-melody 23–30, 71–82) loses its string sound exactly where it reaches its top.

**Fix (applied, `aTwoVc`, bar 79).**
```lilypond
  des'4\ff c'4 bes4 | aes2 bes4--~ | bes4\> des'2-- |
```
This changes one dynamic. The cellos now step f→ff at 79 together with the other strings, which also have no hairpin into 79. It reverses c074 for the cellos only, for the reasons above. Horns 1·2 stay f. In the MIDI, the cello velocity at 79–81 goes from 75 to 80. No pitch changes.

### 2. Bars 75–77: one horn on the cello counter-melody at the cellos' own level (applied)

**Problem.** At 75 the cello counter-melody (E♭3–F3 | A♭3–D♭4 | B♭3–D♭4) gains horn 2 in unison at mp<, the same marking as the cellos. Bassoon 1 also joins (p<, part only). The theme above has only the two violin sections in octaves until 77. For three bars a horn at full cello level sits on the subordinate line. Then from 78 b3 to 82 horns 1·2 a2 double the same line. Counting 57–60, a horn doubles the cello line for twelve of the last twenty-six bars of music before the coda.

**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 166: a horn on a unison cello line "generates a good deal of emotional intensity and should be used sparingly." PDF 164: the doubling wind is marked a level below the strings. c067 moved horn 2's entry from 71 to 75. That entry and the build order stay. Only its level changes, so that the build reaches full horn weight at 78 b3, where both horns join, and not at 75.

**Fix (applied, `aTwoHnTwo`, 75–77).**
```lilypond
  es2(\p\< f4 | aes2 des'4 | bes2 des'4)\mp | r2 c'4(\mf\< |
```
Horn 1 rests 75–77, so the shared staff has one set of dynamics. The a2 entry at 78 b3 stays mf<, so it now marks the arrival of the second horn and is no longer a level the music has already reached. MIDI horn velocities at 75–77 go from 50–53 to 44–46.

### 3. Bars 79–80: the string choir sounds an incomplete chord at the A′ peak (no change; recorded)

At 79 the strings sound only B♭ (theme, three octaves) and D♭ (Vc, Cb). F and A♭ are only in the winds and horns. At 80 b3 the strings have only B♭ and A♭ (the ii ø7 has D♭5 in Cl 2 and F♭4 in Hn 3). This goes against Ex. 214's internal resonance (PDF 168: each element "complete within itself"). Row 17, from the same page, pulls the other way: in a big tutti the strings should be voiced simply and high, and the theme in three octaves is the simplest voicing. The only string that could take the F is the violas, which double the C.a. on the theme's lowest octave (c187, c191, protected). I recommend no change and record 79–80 as an exception to row 12 that row 17 justifies.

### 4. Bars 63–64: the violas' tremolo sits just above the trombone quotation (no change; option)

During Tbn 1's ff quotation (E3–C4) the strings hold an A-minor tremolo, sf> to p. Its middle note is the violas' E4/C5, a step above the trombone's top C4. Sebesky PDF 173 says that when the brass come forward, string chords become "superfluous": the violins keep a sustained high note, and the cellos are tacet "to avoid clutter" until the brass stop. The score does most of this. The strings attack before the trombone's pickup and fade to p by 64, the violins are high (E5–A6), and the cellos sit a fifth or more below the quotation. The option, if the composer wants the Sebesky texture complete, is for the violas to rest 63 b2 to 64 b2 and re-enter sul C on G♯3 at 64 b3 as now. c074 and c182 kept the violas out of A3 inside the trombone's register. This option keeps that reason and goes further. I have not applied it: the sf tremolo is the colour of the A-minor arrival, and 61–65 is protected (c180).

### 5. Bars 87–90: muted horns doubled by the violas, not alternated with them (no change)

PDF 166 suggests alternating three horns in the lower-middle register with violas and cellos for a change of colour. Ex. 214's ending moves them in contrary motion. Here (c194) the divided violas double Hn 3·4's F3–F♭3–E♭3 and A♭3. Part of the alternation is already there: the horns fade out at 90 b3 and the violas continue alone on E♭3 to the end, so the colour changes from horns to strings without a seam. c194's reason (Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 113, support for muted horns at pp) holds. No change.

### 6. Bars 53–56: the strings are only melody and bass (no change)

The violins (unison) have theme B and the cellos the bass. The violas rest. The inner parts are the harp triplets, Bsn 1 and the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line. This is not a mixed chordal voicing in the sense of PDF 168. It is a layered texture whose middle register is complete in the harp. It matches Ex. 214, bars 7–10, a lighter texture with the horn obbligato. Adding sustained violas would be a new accompaniment unit in 37–60, which the c124 rule excludes (Maroges: background changes every four bars is the limit). No change.

### 7. Bar 82: flute 1 doubles Vln I through the release at p>pp (no change)

PDF 164 gives the flute on the lead violin as an emphasis at mf or louder. At 82 it holds A♭5 with Vln I from p to pp for the whole bar, while the oboe, C.a., Cl 2 and horns release at b3. Ending Fl 1 at 82 b3 with the oboe (`aes''2)\p\> r4\!`) would leave the last beat of A′ to the strings alone. This is minor, the release order at 80–83 is protected (c213), and the doubled release is ordinary orchestral practice. Recorded only.
