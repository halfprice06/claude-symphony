## What else I studied

**The master model is Tchaikovsky, Symphony No. 5, II (Andante cantabile, con alcuna licenza).** It is not in the local corpus, so I worked from the score as I know it, without bar numbers. In that movement:
- A horn solo carries the main tune over a soft accompaniment that began first.
- Woodwinds answer the horn.
- The tune returns in the strings with more weight.
- Movement I's motto twice cuts into the lyrical climaxes in the brass at full forte. Sources disagree on the scoring: [Codaweb](https://codaweb.org/?p=3201) says "the brass section at its fullest forte", while [Audience Access](https://audienceaccess.co/bio/ASY-76554) describes a solo trumpet over timpani.
- The coda dies away with woodwind echoes over the strings.

This movement has the same main features: a horn tune over an accompaniment that starts first (1–14), woodwind and violin answers (15–22), the tune in the strings (23, 71), one brass quotation of movement I breaking into the B-section climax (63–64), and a clarinet tail over the strings at the end (85–92). It is about half the length of the Tchaikovsky. Proportions are a settled point (c023), so I do not reopen them.

A conductor would believe a master shaped the material: the key plan, the staged build at 53–61 and the release at 81–83. What would not convince a conductor sits in the brass at the B-section climax. Tchaikovsky's motto has force because the brass colour arrives as an event. Here, trombone 1 has already been sustaining chord tones for three bars before it quotes. In addition, the two low horns are pushed into a high unison that Adler's own source warns will crack. Findings 1 and 2 deal with this.

**Comparison with v59:**
- I diffed the section files and compared the PDFs page by page. Layout is still 8 pages, and the build is still clean.
- The changes since v59 are: trumpets tacet; key signatures back on the horn staves; Cl 1 rests at 3–6; new I–vi7–V4/3 harmony at 73–74; 4–3 suspensions at 42 and 58; Neapolitan sixth at 49; Italian sixth at 64; Cbn overlap at 30; releases at 82 and 84; divided cellos at 18 and 22; Cl 1 and Hn 2 enter at 75; Hn 4 a2 at 61–62.
- Only the last of these is worse by the reading. It is finding 1.
- Removing the trumpets at 61–62 thinned the brass, but it agrees with Adler PDF 37 and 171–172 (hold a colour back for its own event), so I do not reopen it.
- The new harmony at 73–74 and the sixth chords at 49 and 64 are improvements.

The all-pairs check of v69 against my scratch build shows no new parallel fifths. The only new pairs are intended octave doublings (horn 3 with horn 4, horn 2 with trombone 2).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 61–62: horns 3·4 in high unison at f. **Applied.**
**Problem:** Since c064, horn 4 doubles horn 3 a2 on A♭4–B♭4–C♭5 at f. That is written E♭5–F5–G♭5 for horns in F, for the two low-horn players.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 26 quotes Schuller: "When more than one player attempts a high passage at a loud dynamic level… it becomes virtually impossible for any one of the players to sustain his note… advisable to orchestrate such passages… for first and third horn alone." Horn 1 must rest (protected, 61–66), so horn 3, the high horn, should carry this line alone.
- c064 argued from PDF 15 (two horns equal one trombone) that horn 3 alone was too light. But this line is a harmony voice, not the melody. The augmented head is in Fl 2, Vln I, Vln II and Vc. PDF 159 rule 1 makes melody notes more prominent than harmony, and PDF 171–172 shows Wagner giving the brass a secondary role so that it does not disturb.
- Horn 4 an octave lower keeps the weight in the horns' strongest register.

**Fix** (`bSec.ily`; undoes c064 for horn 4 only):
```
bSecHnThree: \partCombineSoloII R1 | \partCombineApart  aes'1--\f | bes'2-- ces''2-- | \partCombineAutomatic R1*2 |
bSecHnFour:  R1*23 | aes1\p\< | aes1--\f | bes2-- ces'2-- | R1*2 |
```

### 2. Bars 60–63: trombone 1 sustains chords for three bars before its quotation. **Applied.**
**Problem:** Trombone 1 enters pp at 60, holds A♭3 and B♭3 at f through 61–62, and then quotes movement I ff at 63. The quoting colour has already been heard, mainly as a doubling.

**Evidence:**
- Adler PDF 37: "One of the most successful ways of building climaxes is by holding back in order to save a certain color for a specific event… Up to this truly mysterious moment the task of the trombones has been to double lines or to sustain chords. Here they offer an entirely new sound color."
- PDF 156: "An anacrusis or a rest before a certain color enters" makes sure it is perceived.
- Tchaikovsky's motto entries work the same way.
- Every pitch trombone 1 played is covered elsewhere: A♭3 by horn 4 (also at 60), Vc and Vla; B♭3 by horn 4 and Vla.
- This also closes the c065 open point about the horn 4 / trombone 1 unison A♭3 at 60.
- Trombone 2, bass trombone and tuba keep the c039 pp< entry and the f chord, so "trombones/tuba held back to 60–64" (c064) still holds.

**Protection:** The protected "rest of the 61–62 brass layout" (c014) was about balance against the woodwinds. With horn 4 on these notes, the brass pitch content is unchanged.

**Fix** (`bSec.ily`):
```
bSecTbnOne = { R1*23 | R1 | R1 | R1 | r8 e8->\ff^\markup \italic "marcato" c'4.-> b8 bes8 a8 | a4 gis8 a8 f4.->\> e8\mf | }
```
The engraver now prints "2." at 60 on the trombone staff.

### 3. Bar 57: the viola figure enters louder than the theme's new colours. **Applied.**
**Problem:** The violas re-enter at mf<, while flute 1 and oboe 1 join the theme at p< and the violins are at mf. In this register the triplet figure equals or exceeds the theme for the first two beats.

**Evidence:** PDF 159 rule 1 ("the melody notes should be more prominent than the harmony notes"); PDF 184 (Schoenberg marks the subsidiary voice a level below the principal). Everyone reaches f at 59, so the crescendo still arrives.

**Fix:**
```
\stemDown \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8(\mp\< des' f' des' aes des') }
```

### 4. Bars 61–62: timpani roll ff under brass marked f. **Not applied** (suggestion).
**Problem:** The D♭ roll is ff for two bars, while the brass harmony was deliberately set at f (c054) and the woodwinds and strings carry the line ff.

**Evidence:** PDF 184 (Bloch): "a fortissimo roll would obliterate the rest of the orchestra." With trumpets tacet, the roll is now the loudest sustained low sound.

**Fix:** `des1:32\f | des2:32 aes,4:32~ aes8 r8 |`. Keep the p< f roll at 60. Leave this as ff if the conductor wants the roll to lead the climax.
