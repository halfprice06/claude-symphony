## Findings

Holistic verdict: a conductor who knows Tchaikovsky's Andante elegiaco would recognise the same type of movement. Both are 3/4 slow movements that open with the winds, put a contrasting key and running triplets in the middle, and end very softly. The harmony here meets every standard in the thirteen literature examples. Each technique they show (figuration, suspension chains, mixture, the Neapolitan at the climax, augmented sixths before structural points, abrupt and common-tone modulation) is in this score, at a point where it does structural work. The differences from Tchaikovsky are in scale and in where the remote keys come, and both are settled decisions. I found nothing that needs a change. I applied no change.

### 1. Where the remote keys come: Tchaikovsky puts them in the reprise, this score puts them in the middle (record, no change)
- Bars: 47-52 (F♯ minor), 61-64 (A minor), 65-82 (A♭ return).
- Problem: Tchaikovsky's main reprise avoids the tonic-dominant return and comes back in a distant key (D major after the B♭ theme). Here the remote keys are confined to the middle section, and theme A returns in the tonic at 71 after a six-bar dominant.
- Evidence: A&S PDF 584-585 describe large-scale chromaticism as a tendency of the period and show that it can work at the level of key areas. This score uses it there too, just earlier: D♭ → F♯ minor (49.1 percent of the running time, c323) → A minor at the climax. Its return to the tonic follows the movement's main model, Dvořák 9/ii, and the form critics settled it (c253, c303, c313).
- Fix: none. A reprise in a remote key would rewrite 65-82, which is protected (A′ texture, V♭9 at 80-81, the 65-70 retransition).

### 2. Scale: 92 bars against Tchaikovsky's 182 (record, no change)
- Bars: whole movement (345 s by analysis.txt).
- Problem: the Andante elegiaco is about twice as long, which is typical of Tchaikovsky's slow movements.
- Evidence: c319 made the same point against Dvořák 6/ii, and the proportions are protected (c253, c303). A&S PDF 328 notes that figuration varies with "the character of an individual piece". This piece is concentrated, and its figurations change every 8 bars (Reading notes 1).
- Fix: none. This repeats c319 Finding 1 and adds nothing that would reopen it.

### 3. The join into the scherzo is the *Widmung* common-tone modulation (record; supports the cyclic plan)
- Bars: 89-92 and the scherzo's first bar.
- Evidence: A&S PDF 586, Ex. 32-3: Schumann moves from A♭ to F♭/E with no pivot chord, held together by A♭/G♯ in the melody. "The lack of a pivot chord makes this modulation look abrupt on paper. Yet it does not sound abrupt." This movement ends on A♭ and the scherzo's Hn 1 enters on G♯4 in E. That is the same keys and the same common tone, across a movement break.
- Fix: none. Reported only so that the composer can cite it in coherence/mvt2.md if wanted. No change to the ending, the exported themes or the quotations.

### 4. The climax at 61-65 follows Mozart K. 397 and Beethoven Op. 106 (record)
- Bars: 61-65.
- Evidence: A&S PDF 482-483: ♭II in a sudden forte at the climax, followed by a drop, suits "climactic moments ... rhetorical silences". At 63 the A minor region (♭II of A♭) arrives sf and the strings fall to p under the trombone quotation. A&S PDF 585: Beethoven keeps his modulation abrupt because the F-F♯ inflection is motivic. Here the abrupt F♭ = E step at 62-63 and the E→E♭ step at 64-65 belong to the F♭/E/E♭ thread that runs from bar 5 to bar 88.
- Fix: none (protected: F♭→E♭ thread, quotation 63-64).

### 5. Middle-section figuration is close to Tchaikovsky's (record)
- Bars: 37-44, 53-60.
- Evidence: Tchaikovsky's middle section runs on triplet figures. This score's viola triplets arpeggiate the held D♭ harmonies at 37-44, break into sextuplets at 59-60 to drive into 61, and the harp takes the triplets at 53-56. In Schumann's terms (A&S PDF 328) this is figuration that "animates a linear-harmonic substructure by means of quicker motion", and each section has its own figure.
- Fix: none.

### 6. Coda colour differs from Tchaikovsky's (record)
- Bars: 83-92.
- Evidence: Tchaikovsky's coda has string tremolos with wind solos over pizzicato. This coda has harp harmonics, a timpani figure and Cl 1's motto tail over held strings and muted horns. It ends with the plagal IV6-iv-I, F to F♭ to E♭, which is the two-forms-at-the-cadence device of Chopin's Prelude (A&S PDF 530-531). Tremolo or pizzicato here would break the return to bar 1's sustained string colour (c319, Adler PDF 224) and the protected plagal close.
- Fix: none.

### 7. V♭9 at 80-81 resolves 9-8 inside the dominant (record)
- Bars: 80-81.
- Evidence: Ex. 27-1 (PDF 463) shows the 9th held for the whole dominant and falling to 5 of I. Here F♭ (Hn 3, Ob 2) falls to E♭ on 81 b2 over the same E♭ bass. A&S PDF 464 §2 treats the 9th as a figuration tone, most often a neighbour, so this is a legitimate variant, and c311 already recorded the resolution in another voice.
- Fix: none (V♭9 at 80-81 is on the standing list).
