## Findings

**F1. Bar 53: the timpani D♭3 stroke rings into the cellos' C3 (V6). Applied.**
Problem: at the return of theme B the timpani strike D♭3 p on beat 1 (c173) and then rest. On beat 3 the cellos move D♭3→C3 and the harmony becomes V6 of D♭ (c212). The part does not say when the D♭ stops. A timpani stroke at p rings for several seconds, and at ♩=60 beat 3 comes two seconds after the stroke, so the drum's D♭3 sounds a minor second above the cellos' C3 in the same octave, low in the texture, under the first string statement of theme B. The score itself writes "damp" at 62, so a player reading this part will take an undamped quarter as a note that may ring.
Evidence: Adler part 2 PDF 80: "The timpani part must be carefully prepared to show dynamics and the exact duration of each note; for instance, where a roll or trill is to end, how long a note may ring." PDF 79: the drum's job here is to strengthen the tonic bass note, which it does on beat 1 only.
Fix (bSec.ily, bSecTimp, bar 53): `des4\p r4^\markup \small \italic "damp" r2 |` (was `des4\p r4 r2 |`).

**F2. Bar 83: the harp re-strikes its low A♭1 under its own harmonics. Applied.**
Problem: at 82 the harp LH plays A♭1–E♭2 p; at 83 it plucks A♭1 again on the same beat as the first harmonic (A♭4 sounding). The 82 strings are still ringing (the low wound strings are the harp's most sonorous, PDF 113), and the cellos and basses hold A♭ ppp, tied over from 82. The new attack adds no pitch. It is the loudest event in the bar (p on the lowest strings, against strings at ppp), and it lands on the harmonic's attack. A low A♭1 string also carries A♭, C and E♭ in its upper partials, the same pitches as the three harmonics.
Evidence: Adler part 1 PDF 116: "Harp harmonics are very soft and must be accompanied very lightly or played a cappella to be effectively heard." PDF 113–114: the low two octaves are "sombre, dark, and sonorous", and the top of the harp has little carrying power.
Answer to c116's reason ("protected release 81–83"): the release is the order in which the orchestra stops at 81–82 and the harp's p at 82. Nothing in that order changes. The harp still sounds A♭1 through 83, because it is tied and left to ring. Only the second pluck goes. The protected harmonics at 83 (c208) are unchanged and become easier to hear.
Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoHarpL, bar 82): `\ottava #-1 <aes,,~ es,>2.\p\arpeggio |`. The coda's `aes,,2. \ottava #0` stays as the tied note, so the 8 bassa line still closes at 83. The tie prints across the system break.

**F3. Bars 8, 72 (also 11–13, 75–76): low harp octaves on every beat ring into one another. Not applied, optional.**
Problem: in the descending-fifths chains (protected, c032/c181) the harp LH plucks F1–F2, B♭1–B♭2 and E♭1–E♭2 on three successive beats, each rolled, in the instrument's most resonant octaves. Undamped, all six strings sound together by beat 3 over the bass clarinet's single line.
Evidence: Adler part 1 PDF 113–114 (low register sonorous); part 2 PDF 80 (state how long a note may ring; the same rule applies to any undamped instrument).
Fix if the composer wants it: at 7 and 71, part and score, `_\markup \small \italic "damp the bass at each change"` under the first LH chord. Harpists often damp low strings at a change of harmony without being told, so this is low priority. I did not test it because the text could push the 8-page layout.

**F4. Bar 5: the retuning instruction lists the drums but does not say which one changes. Applied.**
Problem: "muta in A♭, C, D♭, E♭" leaves the player to work out that only G goes to D♭. The movement's own part-only instruction at 92 names each drum's change ("muta A♭ in E, C in G, …").
Evidence: Adler part 2 PDF 81, Ex. XII-51: the change is marked at the point and names the drum's old and new pitch ("B♭–B♮"). PDF 80: the part shows the player exactly what to do.
Fix (intro.ily, introTimp bar 5): `R2.^\markup \small "muta G in D♭ (A♭, C, D♭, E♭)"`. Text only. The score stays on 8 pages.
