## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The four earlier passes over this unit already applied its two usable techniques (the voice exchange at 21, c237; the contrary semitones at 52 and 70-71, c322 and c377). What remains in the reading either already matches the score or would mean writing new passages in protected bars. The findings below are ranked by how much each bears on the movement's harmonic standing, and they record checks so later cycles do not repeat them.

### 1. Bars 64 b3-65: the bass approaches the dominant from both semitones, and the last chord before it is the same French sixth as bar 21 (record, no change)

**What the bars do.** The bass is D2 (64 b3), E2 (64 b4), E♭2 (65). In A♭, D is ♯4̂ and E is F♭ = ♭6̂, so E♭ is reached from both sides. The reading says ♯4 has a "strong tendency ... to resolve into 5" (PDF 574) and that a line with two leading tones inside one whole step needs an enharmonic, which "would still be inherent in the progression, though not expressed in its notation" (PDF 554). That is the case here: the score writes E, not F♭, because at 64 b4 the chord is still heard as E7, V of A minor, after the °4/3 on D at b3 (Ex. 31-15, PDF 561-562; c242).

On the second eighth of 64 b4 the cellos move B2→B♭2 (c232's anticipation). The sounding chord becomes E2, G♯3, B♭2, D4 (+ Tbn 1 E3). Those are the pitch classes F♭-A♭-B♭-D, with the bass on the same E2 = F♭2, as the French sixth at 21 b3 (Cb F♭2, Vc F♭3/A♭3, Vla A♭3, Vln II B♭3, Vln I D5). Both resolve F♭→E♭ in the bass and D→E♭ or D♭ above. So the retransition's last chord before the dominant is the A section's half-cadence chord, at the same bass pitch. This is the reading's description of Ex. 31-9 (PDF 557): a chord "heard as" a triad prepared by its V, which then "becomes an augmented 6th", with "the final resolution ... to a dominant". c372 listed 64 b4 as "German, then French on the second eighth"; it did not note that the French form repeats bar 21 exactly.

**Fix:** none. Later cycles should not respell the bass as F♭ (it would hide the A-minor meaning that 63-64 b3 sets up), and should not remove the cellos' eighth-note B♭2: it carries the rhyme with 21 and also removes the fifths E-B / E♭-B♭ against the bass.

### 2. Bars 39-40 and 55-56: the outer voices run in parallel minor 10ths through the minor-third bass (record, no change)

**What the bars do.** At 39 b1-40 b1 the bass falls E♭2-C2-A1 (harp LH and basses) and the theme falls G♭5-E♭5-C5 (Fl 1; Vln I at 55-56). Each pair is a minor 10th: E♭/G♭, C/E♭, A/C. Both lines arpeggiate the same °7 (A-C-E♭-G♭), a minor third apart, and the passage resolves into B♭ minor with an octave B♭1/B♭4 at 40 b3. The same layout returns at 55-56.

**Evidence.** The reading calls parallel 10ths "as important an organizing element in chromatic textures as in diatonic ones" (PDF 554, Ex. 31-5) and says a minor-third division works best "within the expansion of a seventh chord", where "fleeting °7s form the connective threads" (PDF 575, Ex. 31-30d). c242 recorded the bass and the shared-tone sevenths at 39-40; the outer-voice 10ths were not recorded. A beat census of the outer voices gives 44% thirds/10ths in 53-60 against 33% in the diatonic 7-14 and 23-30, so the chromatic bars are the ones most held together by 10ths, as the reading expects. The same holds at 59 b4-60 (G1/B♭5 → A♭1/C6, the ♮4-♯4 approach in D♭).

**Fix:** none. If a later cycle revoices the flute or Vln I at 39 or 55, it should keep the 10ths with the bass.

### 3. The modulation plan reads as mixture of D♭, not as a chain of foreign keys (record, no change)

**Check.** The reading's first rule is to find the diatonic progression under a chromatic passage (PDF 552, Ex. 31-1b). In D♭ (the middle section's key):
- 45-46: B♭ minor (vi), emphasised by the oboe and cello head;
- 46 b3-47: G♭ (IV), then C♯7 = D♭7, V7 of G♭ minor;
- 48-50: "F♯ minor" = G♭ minor, iv of D♭ by mixture, with its Neapolitan at 49;
- 50 b3-51: Bm7 = C♭ minor 7 → A♭7 (V7) → D♭ at 53;
- 61-63: I-IV-♭III (F♭), F♭ = E as V of A minor;
- 64-65: the French sixth of Finding 1 → V7 of A♭.

F♯ minor and A minor are therefore not new tonics but mixture chords of D♭ and A♭ that receive emphasis. The reading describes exactly this in the Wegweiser (PDF 571): emphasised passing chords make "the listener ... mistakenly believe that a new key area is being prepared". The sharp spellings at 47-50 and 63-64 follow the rule that a passage in remote keys may be notated "for ease of reading" (PDF 573, Ex. 31-27b), and they switch at the key-signature changes (c242).

**Fix:** none.

### 4. The joint into the scherzo uses the notation of Ex. 31-27a (record, no change; for the coherence file)

**Check.** c242 recorded that the symphony's keys C-A♭-E-C divide the octave into major thirds, the division the reading calls "the most important" (PDF 572). The reading adds a notation rule: ♭6 "will probably be transformed to ♯5 at the point where the diminished 4th occurs in the bass" (PDF 573, Ex. 31-27a, where C-A♭-[G♯]-E-C is notated exactly this way). In the symphony the d4 is A♭→E, from this movement's tonic to the scherzo's, and that is where the respelling happens: the scherzo's horn 1 enters on G♯4, this movement's last A♭ (BRIEF.md; coherence/mvt2.md). Movement II itself stays in flats to its last bar. This is correct and should stay so. It needs no change; the composer may add one line to coherence/mvt2.md saying that the A♭→G♯ respelling sits at the d4 of the major-third cycle (A&S Ex. 31-27a).

### 5. Bar 62: the G♭ chord's major seventh moves to the octave with the bass; keep it (record, no change)

At 62 b1 six parts hold F over G♭ (a 7th prepared at 61). At b3 they move to F♭ while the bass moves G♭→F♭, so 7 goes to 8 by similar motion. c377 corrected c242's label (it is not a 7-6 chain). I checked it against the 7-6 rule of PDF 552-553. In a 7-6 chain the bass holds or moves chromatically under the resolution. Here the bass moves a whole step, so the line F→F♭ is the ♭6 thread of the F♭→E♭ approach (62-65, protected), not a suspension. The top voice (A♭6) is not involved, so the octave is not between outer voices. The passage is protected and the reading gives no reason to change it.

### 6. No descending chromatic bass and no 7-6 lament; a rule if one is ever added (no change)

The reading recommends 7-6 suspensions, backed by 5ths in full texture, as "a most effective way of harmonizing a descending chromatic bass" (PDF 552-553, Ex. 31-2a). The movement has no such bass. Its only chromatic bass steps are single semitones (21, 59, 64-65) and the lament affect is not this movement's. If a later cycle adds one (the likeliest place would be the closing bars 83-88, which are protected), it should use the 7-6 chain with 5ths, and not parallel 6/3s under the motto tail. The second would form the M6-m10 note-against-note motion the reading rejects (PDF 565, Ex. 31-21a; c242's rule).

### 7. Sequential devices of the unit stay unused (no change)

The unit's remaining devices are chromatic 5-6 sequences (PDF 554-557), augmented-sixth sequences (PDF 557), °7 and augmented-triad chains (PDF 558-563), extended voice-exchange chains (PDF 567-571), minor-third and tritone cycles of tonics (PDF 574-575), and octatonic and whole-tone passages (PDF 578-580). None is used. The reading treats them as transitional and warns that their predominance "would weaken the gravitational attraction of the tonic" (PDF 572). The movement's transitions are pedals and pivots and are protected. Adding a chain would be new composition, not a correction.
