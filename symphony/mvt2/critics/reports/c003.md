## Findings

### 1. The build 53–60 starts at its ceiling, so the crescendo has only three bars (applied)
**Bars:** 53–60. **Problem:** At 53 the Vln I begin the B theme at F6–G♭6, at mp, so the registral climb is already done. The crescendo and the printed *poco a poco animando* do not start until 57. They reach f at 59, and bar 60 has no hairpin, so it holds f under a whole note before the ff. In practice the build is 57–59: three bars, about 12 s. Dvořák's build to the same kind of climax (9/ii, 90–96) rises over six bars. A&S PDF 216 (§6) says a key area's goal has to be set off. Here the music reaches the D♭ goal without the preparation having grown. **Verdict on the composer's v01 question:** the build is not too short in bars. 53–64 lasts 51 s, as long as Dvořák's 90–100. It is too short in *pacing*, because the first half stands still. Adding bars is not needed. **Fix (applied):** start the build at 53. Vln I play 53–56 in unison with Vln II (`f''4.(\p\< ges''8 f''4 es''4 | des''4 c''8 des''8 es''2)\mp | ges''4.(\< f''8 es''4 des''4 | c''2\mf bes'2) |`) and only leap to the upper octave at 57, where the woodwinds enter. Vla/Vc/Cb get `\p\<` at 53, `\mp` at 54/55 and `\mf` at 56/57. Bar 60 gets `\<` in every sustaining part (Fl 1, Ob 1, Cl 1·2, Bsn 1·2, Hn 1·2, strings) into the ff at 61. In `global.ily` the *poco a poco animando* moves to 53: `\tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco a poco animando" s1*2 \mt 61 s1*2 \mt 62 s1*2 \mt 64 s1 \mt 62 s2 \mt 58 s2`. The horn counter-melody at 52 goes from mf to mp (it would otherwise cover p strings), and Hn 1 at 57 and Bsn 2 at 57 get the same dynamic as their partners on the shared staff (`\mf\<`, `\mp\<`). The timpani stroke at 53 goes from mf to p.

### 2. At the climax the leading tone does not resolve and D♭ never gets 1̂ on top (applied)
**Bars:** 60–61. **Problem:** The first violins hold C6 (7̂ of D♭) through 60 and then drop a third to A♭5 on the ff downbeat of 61. The top voice of the arrival is 5̂, and it is lower than the note before it. In 28 bars of D♭ the melody never lands on D♭ at a cadence: B1 ends on V (44), the cadence at 52–53 is elided under 3̂ at pp, and 61 has 5̂. **Evidence:** A&S PDF 219 §9 and the review list on PDF 228: "The new key area is often confirmed by an authentic cadence and a melodic motion to 1̂ of the new key". PDF 226 §12: strong cadences support 2̂–1̂, not a leap away from the leading tone. **Fix (applied):** Vln I `c'''1)\< | des'''1(\ff | f'''2 fes'''2) |`. The augmented theme head A♭–D♭–F–F♭ stays in Vln II, Fl, Ob, Tpt 1 and Vc, so nothing of the motive is lost. C6–D♭6 over A♭–D♭ is a hidden octave with the top voice moving by step, which is allowed.

### 3. The dominant before the climax has no 4̂–♯4̂–5̂ bass (applied)
**Bars:** 59–60. **Problem:** The bass goes G♭–A♭ (IV–V) with a 4–3 suspension above. This is the movement's main cadence, and it uses the plainest approach. **Evidence:** A&S PDF 215: "Among the most typical usages involving chords applied to V are those that emphasize dominant harmony (often at a cadence) by means of a rising chromatic progression, 4̂–♯4̂–5̂, in the bass" (Mendelssohn, Ex. 14-2). The existing melody on beats 3–4 of 59 is D♭6–B♭5, which is the 7th and 5th of E♭7, so V6/5 of V fits it with no change. **Fix (applied):** Vc `ges2\f g2 | aes1\< |`, Cb `ges,2\f g,2 | aes,,1\< |`, Bsn 2 `ges2\f g2 | aes,1\< |`. On beats 3–4 of the Vla `\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' es' des' bes des') }` replaces the A♭ (A♭ against G♮ would clash). Timp bar 59 `R1`, bar 60 `aes,2.:32\p\< r4\! |`. The A♭ roll now starts on the V itself, and the rest on beat 4 avoids a new timp–horn fifth into 61. The all-pairs checker shows no new parallels in 53–64; the removed lines are only the old Vln I/II octave runs at 53–56.

### 4. The closing 83–90 is a second postlude; cut it (applied)
**Bars:** 83–90 (v03). **Problem:** After the full close at 82 the movement stays on an A♭ pedal for 16 bars (83–98) before the plagal cadence. 83–104 lasts 81 s, 21% of the movement. Bars 83–90 repeat material heard in the last two minutes: Cl 1 has the theme head (83–85), and Hn 1 has the motto tail E♭–D–D♭–C (87–88), which is note for note the horn's 67–68. The coda then states the head (harp 91–92, 95–96) and the tail (timp 93–94, 97–98) twice more. Counting 1–6, 31–36, 65–70 and 83–98, 34 of 104 bars sit on one held bass note. **Evidence:** A&S PDF 220 §11: the coda "rounds off the ending with a varied repetition of the final cadence". It is a short echo of the cadence, not a new section. Dvořák 9/ii's coda is 7 of 126 bars. **Verdict on the composer's v01 question:** confirmed, the close is too long. The cause is 83–90, not the coda, which carries the required harp and timpani moments and the protected plagal close. **Fix (applied):** delete A′ bars 83–90 (`sections.txt`: `aTwo 12 3`). In `aTwo.ily`, drop the final `R2.*8` or pedal line of every variable, Cl 1's head, Hn 1's tail, and the Vla/Vc/Cb pedal bars. In `global.ily`: `\mark \default % K 83` `\tempo "a tempo" \mt 52` `s2.*10 \tempo ... "rit." ...`, and the old mark L goes. The movement becomes 96 bars and 357 s, and the post-cadential part is 53 s (15%). The horn keeps its unsupported moments at 7–14 and 67–70, so the cyclic rule still holds. This undoes no accepted fix. It also removes two of the motto-tail statements that critic 1 counted (left open in HISTORY).

### 5. The A section closes twice on the same perfect cadence (not applied)
**Bars:** 13–14 and 29–30. **Problem:** a (7–14) and a′ (23–30) end with the same melody and the same V7–I. Only b (15–22) ends on V. The listener hears the identical full close at 14, 30 and (varied) 82. This adds to the static impression the composer noticed. **Evidence:** A&S PDF 220–222 (antecedent–consequent, Ex. 14-9 and 14-10) and PDF 224 (quatrain, Ex. 14-12): the first and second A phrases "usually cadence in different places, one of them on I and the other on V". **Fix (suggested):** make 7–14 an antecedent ending on V at 14. Bass clarinet/harp bass `des,2 d,4 | es,2.` (IV – VII6/5 of V – V, the 4̂–♯4̂–5̂ of row 5), with the horn ending on B♭3 or E♭4 at 14. Leave 29–30 as the full close. I did not apply this because 11–14 is critic 1's accepted second phrase, and the horn line is the composer's choice. Bar 8 (do-not-touch) is not affected.

### 6. Remaining proportions are sound (no change)
The large tonal plan matches A&S's phrase-group model (PDF 222–223): I – pivot (A♭ = V of D♭, 30–31, PDF 216–217) – new key – new tonic turned back into V7 with 4̂ added (65–70, PDF 220 §10) – return. The transition and the retransition are both 6 bars at about 21 s and balance each other. A′ (71–82, 43 s) is half the length of A (83 s), the same ratio as Dvořák 9/ii (return 101–119 against 7–45). The B climax at 61 (full brass) and the A′ peak at 79 (B♭6, strings and woodwinds) come 18 bars apart with different colours. After findings 1–3 the B climax is the louder arrival and 79 the higher one. I would not flatten either of them.
