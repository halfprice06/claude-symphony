## Findings

### 1. Bars 57–58: the viola triplets strike the horn counter-line's held notes (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 57 the violas take the triplet figure back from the harp (the staged build: harp 53, violas 57, sextuplets 59). Horn 1 starts its counter-line at 57 at mp: A♭4 | G♭4 | F4 | E♭4 | F4 (concert pitch). The cellos join it an octave lower at 57 b3. The figure lies between A♭3 and G♭4, the octave between the horn and the cellos. Its upper notes strike the horn's held pitch four times:
- 57 b3–4: `bes8( des' ges' …)`, G♭4 at b3.67, under the horn's G♭4.
- 58 b1: `aes8( des' f')`, F4 at b1.67, under the horn's F4.
- 58 b3: `aes8( c' es')`, E♭4 at b3.67, under the horn's held E♭4.
- 58 b4: `aes8( ces' f')`, F4 at b4.67, just after the horn's F4 at b4.5.

In this passage the horn plays alone at mp, with only the cellos an octave lower. Each of these unisons puts a pulse on the horn's held note in the same register.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 203: rank the layers by importance, so that the background does not blur the line ranked above it.
- PDF 217 (Ravel): layers that share a register and similar note values need clear separation.
- PDF 230 item 3 (Schoenberg): no doubling of middle parts while a solo line runs.
- c137 used the same reasons to revoice the harp's copy of this figure at 53 b4–56, off the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line. Bars 57–58 have the same figure against the same horn, and nobody revoiced them.
- At 59–60 the figure becomes sextuplets at f, and Hn 1, Vc and Bsn 1 carry the counter-line together. There a unison matters much less (finding 2).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-277/work/sections/bSec.ily, line 228–229).** Only four upper notes change. The rhythm, the slurs (six per bow at 57, three at 58), the dynamics and the build order stay as they are.
```
% 57 (second half) and 58, was:
\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges' des' bes des') } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( des' f') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( c' es') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( ces' f') } |
% now:
\tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' bes' des' bes des') } |
\tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( bes des') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { bes8( des' ges') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( c' aes') } \tuplet 3/2 4 { aes8( ces' aes') } |
```
- The new notes are chord tones: B♭ over the G♭ chord at 57; A♭, B♭, D♭ over B♭m7 at 58 b1; A♭ over A♭7 at 58 b3; A♭ over D♭7 at 58 b4.
- No new note doubles the seventh G♭ at 58 b3.
- No new note lies on F3, E♭3 or G♭3, where the cellos are.
- At 57 b3.67 and 58 b3.67 the violas now meet clarinet 2's held B♭4 and A♭4. Those are inner harmony notes, not the counter-line.
- For those two eighths the violas sit a third above the horn. That is the same compromise c137 accepted for the harp.

**Build.**
- Score: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels.
- The attack scan shows no viola note on a horn or cello note at 57–58.
- Parts book: 30 pages, every part on one page except the harp, and the viola part still fits on one page.
- The theme B background plan protected by c257 is kept: the same instruments play the figure in the same bars, and only four pitches change.

### 2. Bars 59–60: the sextuplets also touch the counter-line (recorded, no change)

The sextuplets strike E♭4 and F♭4 at 59 b3–4 against the horn's E♭4 and F♭4. They strike G♭3 against the cellos' and bassoon 1's G♭3 at 59 b1–2 and 60 b3–4. Here the counter-line has three players at f, and the figure works as a measured tremolo under the crescendo into ff. This matches Schoenberg's handling of the tutti (PDF 230: lean texture, the big sound carried by the main lines). Moving these notes off the line would mean leaving out chord tones of each half-bar's harmony (G♭, E♭7, F♭, A♭). I would not change them.

### 3. Bar 72: bassoon 2 doubles the bass and touches the cello counter-melody (recorded, no change)

At 72 b1 bassoon 2 doubles the basses an octave higher on F3, the note the cellos' counter-melody holds for two beats. Two colours sound one pitch, which Adler PDF 229 says can cover each other. It lasts one beat. Bassoon 2 joined the basses on purpose (c060). I tried two alternatives: C3 tied from 71 makes octaves with the violas' C4–B♭3 on beats 1–2, and A♭2 makes a low third with the F2 bass. I recommend no change.

### 4. Colour changes at phrase seams, 14–15 and 44–45 (recorded, no change)

At 15 and at 45 a new background starts at the barline. Adler accepts this: Ravel sometimes overlaps the background and sometimes gives "a new articulation to the phrasing" (PDF 217, 224). The harp's undamped chord at 14 carries over into 15. At 44–45 the oboe and cello pickup on 44 b4 links the phrases across a deceptive cadence. Nothing needs changing.

### 5. Bars 37–40: pizzicato bass with no wind holding it (recorded, no change)

Ravel's "damper pedal" (PDF 217) is a held wind note after a pizzicato bass. Here the harp's left-hand octaves under the pizzicato basses ring through each half-bar, and Vln II holds the upper harmony (PDF 209, "harmonic counterpoint"). The bass therefore already sounds as if held. Adding a wind would go against c021's reason (flute alone over thin strings, 37–40).

### 6. Bars 48–50: Vln II above the cor anglais quotation (recorded, no change)

Vln II's line (F♯5–A5–G5–E♯5–F♯5–A5–D5) lies above the cor anglais's A4–D5. PDF 203 ranks the quotation first. What keeps the two apart is colour (string against reed), rhythm (half notes against the quotation's quarters and eighths) and dynamics (pp against p–mp), which is Ravel's method (PDF 217). The pitches were set by c202, c262 and c274, and c271 recorded the fifths problem. No change.
