## Findings

### 1. Bar 53: theme B's return to D♭ sounds over the timpani's A♭ (applied)

**Problem.** The B2 excursion (B♭ minor → F♯ minor) returns to D♭ through two bars of A♭7, 51–52. The timpani roll on A♭2 through 52 (pp<), and the part then strikes a new A♭2 at p on 53 b1 (`aes,4\p`). At that moment the double basses rest (c164), so the cellos' D♭3 is the real bass. The timpani A♭2 lies a fourth below it. In score.midi the timpani stroke is the loudest attack on that downbeat (velocity 62; cellos 57; violins, cor anglais and harp 53). For one beat, the arrival of rehearsal F sounds as D♭ over A♭, a 6/4 that does not resolve. Then the cellos move to C3 (V6/5) at b3.

**Evidence.** A&S PDF 235–236 (15-7, Mozart K. 511): a key area leads back to the tonic, and the inverted dominants on the way "do not normally constitute goals of motion"; the goal is I in root position. PDF 231 (15-1) makes the same point at phrase level: the consequent "adds a final tonic". Bar 53 is the formal goal of the whole B2 detour. Adler part 2, PDF 79: the timpani's role is "to strengthen the tonic and dominant bass notes", so a timpani note is heard as a bass. Every other roll in this movement ends on its own harmony's bass: 3 (A♭), 60 (A♭ into D♭ at 61, where the timpani change to D♭), 70 (E♭), 81 (E♭, with a rest at 82, c123). Bar 53 is the only place where a dominant stroke falls on a tonic downbeat.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, bSecTimp, bar 53).**
```
  aes,1:32\pp\< |
  des4\p r4 r2 | R1*5 |   % was aes,4\p
```
The roll on A♭2 now ends with its stroke on D♭3, in unison with the cellos, so the timpani play V–I into the return. D♭ is already one of the four drums (A♭, C, D♭, E♭; used at 61), so there is no retuning, and the c146/c056 tuning plan stays as it is. The dynamics, rhythm, roll and the 53–61 build order are unchanged. Only the pitch of one stroke changes, inside the B background that c157 protected. The reason it may change is that the old stroke contradicted the protected harmony.

### 2. Rising thirds into downbeats at 40→41, 56→57 and 57→58: no change

**Check.** PDF 231 and 240 advise against rising-third root motion from a weak to a strong beat, because "it tends to contradict the meter". My root scan finds three cases. At 40→41 and 56→57, vi (B♭ minor) goes to I6 (D♭/F). At 57 b3–4 → 58 b1, G♭(maj7) goes to B♭m7.

**Why it stands.** At 41 and 57 a new entry marks the downbeat: the cor anglais at 41; at 57 the winds, violas and basses. At 58 the bass leaps down a sixth (G♭2 → B♭1) under the melody's A♭6 ceiling. The vi7 there starts the vi–ii–V chain of fifths that A&S show in 15-9 (PDF 236–237). Corpus: the MCTL harmony annotation of Tchaikovsky 6/iv has vi → I6 on downbeats in its D-major theme (onset-derived bars 48 and 50). That is the same move as 40→41 and 56→57. Bar 58 is also protected as of v152 (c151). Record these three cases so later cycles do not raise them again.

### 3. Outer-voice cross-relations at 21 and 77–78: no change

**Check.** 15-8c (PDF 236) marks a cross-relation between soprano and bass as "avoid!". The scan finds two near cases. At 21 the solo violin has F5 on b1 and the bass has F♭ on b3 (the c152 French sixth). From 77 b2 to 78 b1 the top has E♭6 and the bass then has F♭ (the deceptive ♭VI). In both, the top voice has already moved on by step before the inflected note sounds (E♭5 at 21 b2; C6 at 77 b3). So the chromatic pair is never struck in direct succession between the outer voices, and the F♭ resolves to E♭ in the bass in both places. That is the harmless kind. Both bars are already recorded (c152, c170). This is a second confirmation, not a new exception.

### 4. Proportion and phrase grouping: confirm, no change

The reading's one form exercise (PDF 245–246) is a 3 + 3 + 4 phrase: a tonic, a tonicised second key, then a cadence group led through IV or II6. B2 (45–52) follows it in compressed form, 3 + 3 + 2. Its two-bar A♭7 group runs at *poco sostenuto* (♩ = 54) and lasts about as long as the three-bar groups before it, so the compression is not felt as a cut. A′ (71–82) is 4 + 4 + 4, with its deceptive ♭VI at 78 and its IV–ii6/5–V–I at 79–82, which is 15-2's III→IV/II6→V model applied to the tonic key. About 30% of the running time sits on pedals (1–6, 31–36, 65–70, 83–92). These pedals are the movement's framing device, as in Dvořák 9/ii, and each one has its own colour (link, woodwind choir, solo horn, harp and timpani). The tension curve has two ff peaks (61, 79), with the B climax dying away before the dominant. This is unchanged since c083 and c153, and I do not reopen it.
