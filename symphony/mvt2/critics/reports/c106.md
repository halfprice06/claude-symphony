## Findings (ranked)

### 1. The double basses' ff whole note at 61 needs a bow change (bar 61). Applied.
**Problem.** At the climax the basses hold D♭1 for a whole bar, ff, with a tenuto and "molto sostenuto", at Largamente ♩=54. That is 4.4 seconds at full volume in one bass bow. c016 added "change bow at will" to the soft pedals (31–36, 65–70, 83–92), but this loud one has nothing. A bass bow cannot sustain ff for 4.4 s. The tone thins out in the second half of the bar, which is exactly the part where the wind chord (Cbsn, B.cl., Bsn 2, Tuba) needs the strings under it.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 108: the bass bow is "thicker, heavier, and quite a bit shorter than the violin or even the cello bow. This fact is important when one slurs long passages or asks for a lengthy tenuto. The way to do this is to ask the players to change bow at will." A tenuto whole note marked "molto sostenuto" is a lengthy tenuto. PDF 105 adds that the section, not the single player, is what "can raise the roof". Staggered bow changes keep the whole section at full sound.
**Fix (applied).** bSec.ily, bSecCb bar 61:
`des,1--\ff^\markup \italic "molto sostenuto, change bow at will" | ges,,2-- fes,,2-- | …`
This does not undo the settled bowing at 61 (c085, c096). The attack is still a fresh down-bow on the ff downbeat, and only the continuation is staggered. The same reasoning applies, less strongly, to Cb D♭1 at 79 (dotted half, ff, ♩=48, 3.75 s, doubled by the contrabassoon) and to Vln I D♭6 at 61 (whole note, ff, with a longer bow). I left both unchanged. The composer may add the same words at 79 if wanted.

### 2. The concertmaster cannot mute between the solo and the muted tutti (bars 22–24). Applied (in the part only).
**Problem.** Bars 17–22 are "solo, senza sord.", and the solo ends on E♭5 at 22 b2. On 22 b3 the whole first-violin section enters on the theme: "tutti (gli altri con sord.)". The soloist is the concertmaster (Adler PDF 81). As written, the concertmaster has no time to put on a mute, so the leader plays all of 23–30 unmuted inside a muted section, in unison with it, from the front desk. The score's own plan is muted strings at 15–30 (c039). The text "gli altri" accepts that the leader is the exception, but it gives the leader no time to fix it.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 59: the mute alters the tone "radically". PDF 60: "Enough time must be allowed to put on or take off the mutes… otherwise, chaos may result." PDF 81: a single solo violin is played by the concertmaster. Dvořák 9/ii avoids the problem by muting his soloists ("2 violins con sordini", corpus l. 116, 240), so his "tutti" (l. 124, 247) needs no change. Here the solo must stay senza sord. because that is its colour against the muted strings (Rimsky-Korsakov Ex. 222, c039). The leader therefore needs a short rest instead.
**Fix (applied, part only, so the score layout is unchanged).** aOne.ily, aOneVlnOne bar 22, on the tutti pickup:
`es'8(\mp-\tag #'part _\markup \italic "(solo player: mute on, join at bar 24)" -\tweak X-offset #-9 ^\markup …`
The leader drops the pickup and bar 23, which is four beats (about 4.6 s at ♩=52, enough to clip on a mute), and rejoins at 24. One violin missing from a unison section for one bar cannot be heard. I regenerated the parts book, and the text prints under bar 22 of the Violin I part (parts-book page 21). If the composer would rather keep the leader playing, the alternative is to print "solo: senza sord. throughout 23–30" so the choice is explicit.

### 3. Vln I F6→B♭6 at 76 is playable: close the open item (bar 76). No change.
**Problem checked.** HISTORY has listed "Vln I F6–B♭6 at 76" as open since cycle 78. That item was opened when the flute's leap to B♭6 at 76 was removed. The violins now play that leap alone, at mp<, in the upper octave of theme A.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 72 gives the practical orchestral range as up to E7. He warns about control above A6, but B♭6 is a fourth step inside that range. PDF 82–83 lists the problematic skips: "wide skips on the same string", "wide skips between extreme ranges", and "wide skips legato". A fourth from F6 to B♭6 on the E string, separated by a tie and a new bow, is none of these. F6 and B♭6 lie under one hand position (7th to 8th position), so no large shift is needed. F6 takes the 1st finger and B♭6 the 4th. Vln II plays the same B♭ an octave lower at the same moment, and it is the appoggiatura the first violins already played at 28, an octave lower.
**Recommendation.** Close the open item with no change. The protected "B♭6 at pitch 75–81" and the octave plan stay as they are.

### 4. Points checked and not raised
- **Viola D4 at 50 b3 (half note, p, under the cor anglais quotation)** is the viola's open D string. Adler (PDF 73–74) says open strings stand out in slow passages. He also says fingering "is best left to the performer". In this context a violist will stop the D4 with the 4th finger on the G string without being told. A printed "4" would only clutter the part.
- **Viola double-stop tremolos at 61–64** (D♭4/F4, B♭3/D♭4, A♭3/F♭4, E4/C5) are all on adjacent strings in 1st–3rd position. They are in Adler's tables (PDF 88–89) and are used for a "sonorous passage" (PDF 76). They are playable non div. B♭3/D♭4 needs 3rd position (B♭3 4th finger on the C string, D♭4 2nd finger on the G string). That is easy at ff tremolo.
- **Vln II E5–C6 tremolo at 63–64** is a minor sixth on the A and E strings in 2nd position (PDF 76 table). It is easy.
- **Cellos F4 in bass clef at 62** sits on two ledger lines, which is below Adler's three-line threshold (PDF 104). Tenor clef at 44–47 is protected (c095 clef placements).
- **Open-string pedal in the basses at 63–64** (A1 sf, then A1–D2 p in unison with the cellos' D2–E2) is fine. These notes are either loud or doubled, and the basses cannot go lower than E1 without the C extension (PDF 108).
- **Mute timing elsewhere.** All strings have at least 11 bars of rest before "con sord." at 15/21, five bars for "via sord." at 31, and six bars before "senza sord." at 37/41. Hn 3·4 have "mettere sord." at 82 and four bars before 87. All of these meet PDF 60.
- **Harp pedals, timpani tuning (A♭–C–D♭–E♭, no retuning) and brass rests** are unchanged since c096 and still correct. Horn 1 rests 61–66 before its p solo, and trumpets are tacet.
