## Findings

### 1. Bar 41 b3–4: the violas sound the cor anglais's resolution note in its own octave before it resolves (applied)
**Problem.** Bar 41 is the start of theme B's second phrase: cor anglais `f'4. ges'8` (F4 for a beat and a half, G♭4 on b4½) over G♭ harmony (Cb and harp G♭ on b3). The F is an appoggiatura. The violas' pizz. triplets on b3–4 were `bes8 des' ges' des' bes des'`, so G♭4, the resolution, sounds on b3's third triplet, a minor second above the cor anglais's F4 in the same octave, inside the p< swell the violas share with the solo (c038).
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 132: a melodic design with non-harmonic notes "does not permit" the fundamental notes to proceed in the same register; moving one part an octave away diminishes "the discordant effect produced by the contact of appogiaturas and fundamental notes", and the slower the passage, the harsher the effect. PDF 133: the basis "generally remains an octave removed from the melodic design". The pizz. timbre helps (PDF 132–133), but at ♩=60 the clash lasts almost a beat. The same figure is clean everywhere else: at 37–40 and 57 the melody is an octave above the triplets, and c092 already cleared the resolution F4 out of the suspension at 42.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecVla`, bar 41, second group):** `\tuplet 3/2 4 { ges8 bes des' ges bes des' }` (was `bes8 des' ges' des' bes des'`). Root-position G♭ below the F4, rising like the triplets of bar 42; the last D♭4 moves to A♭3 at 42. Dynamics and the c038 swell are unchanged. allpairs_par.py shows no new pair (a first try, `ges bes des' bes ges bes`, made broken octaves G♭–A♭ with the cor anglais into 42, so I rejected it).

### 2. Bar 47: the C♯7 pivot has no attack; Rimsky's sf-p layout fits it exactly (applied, composer's judgement)
**Problem.** Bar 47 is the goal of the 45–47 group and the enharmonic door (D♭7 = C♯7) into the quotation. The winds begin the chord there (Bsn 2 G♯2 new, Bsn 1 and oboe arrive on C♯), and the strings (Vln II E♯4, Vla B3, Cb C♯2) all crescendo through 46 and arrive mp>. Everything arrives at the same moderate level, so the most important harmonic event of B2 is marked like any other bar. The whole movement uses one sf (63), and none in the soft sections.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 129: "At the moment when the wood-wind begins a piano chord, the strings attack it sforzando, a compound chord for preference"; the same method gives sf-dim, and the cresc.-sf version puts the string attack at the end of a crescendo, which is what 46 already is. Ex. 253 (PDF 418) shows the strings attacking while the winds hold. Dvořák 9/ii does this in the same kind of moment: the strings attack fz> at 39 as the cor anglais's phrase peaks and fp> on the chord at 73.
**Fix (bSec.ily):** Vln II `\after 2. \pp eis'1\fp\>`, Vla `b1\fp\>`, Cb `\after 2. \pp cis,1\fp\>` (all were `\mp\>`); Bsn 1 `cis'2.\p\>` and Bsn 2 `\after 2. \pp gis,1\p\>` (were mp; equal on the shared staff). Oboe 1 and cellos keep mf> on the melody; the poco rit. and the cor anglais's p entry at 47 b4 are unchanged. If the composer finds fp too strong for a dolce context, `\sfp` is Rimsky's literal marking and fp is Dvořák's; I chose the milder one.

### 3. Bar 52 b4: horn 1 returns after 38 bars at p, a level above its unison clarinet (applied; answers the open item "Hn 1 pp at 53")
**Problem.** Horn 1 re-enters on the counter-line at p while Cl 1, in unison on the same notes, enters pp. The pair has two dynamics, and the horn, the stronger instrument, has the higher one.
**Evidence.** PDF 136: after a long rest, horns should come in "pp or ff"; p is "less successful". The composer's reason for keeping p (c033) was that pp "weakens an inner line under two sections of violins". The line's audibility does not depend on the entry: it rises to B♭4 at 54 b3, in the horn's strong register, and crescendos to mp at 55 (c019), which is when it needs to be heard. Entering pp with the clarinet makes the crescendo longer and steeper, which is the amplification method of PDF 127 (a new colour enters softer and gains faster). The violins are p at 53, so a pp horn is one level under them, the normal level for a middleground line.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecHnOne`):** `r2 r4 aes8(\pp-\tag #'score ^\markup \bold "1."^\markup \italic "espr." bes8 |`. Crescendo and mp at 55 unchanged.

### 4. Bar 57: bassoon 2 and bass clarinet play the same bass line at different dynamics (applied)
**Problem.** At 57 bassoon 2 and the bass clarinet enter in unison on `f2 ges2 | bes,4 es4 aes,4 des4 | ges,2 g,2`. The bass clarinet is p< (c033), bassoon 2 is mp<.
**Evidence.** c033 (same chapter) moved the bass clarinet to p by PDF 127 and 136 and left bassoon 2 at mp only because bassoon 1 shared its staff at mp. Since c107 bassoon 1 rests at 57–58 (`\partCombineApart r1 | r1`), so that reason no longer holds. PDF 136: an mp re-entry (bassoon 2 has rested since 47) is "colourless".
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecBsnTwo`):** `\partCombineApart f2\p\< ges2 | ...`. The f at 59 is unchanged.

### 5. Bar 79: the cellos repeat the f they already have from 78 (applied)
**Problem.** `aTwoVc` has `ces'2\f c'4 |` at 78 and `des'4\f c'4 bes4 |` at 79. Nothing changes the level between them, so the score prints f twice in two bars. c074 wrote the second one to say "stays f" when it lowered ff to f.
**Evidence.** A repeated dynamic reads as a new instruction, here as a possible accent on the counter-melody's D♭4 just as the tutti goes ff; PDF 133 asks that the line under the theme stay "of inferior dynamic power", which the single f at 78 already states.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, `aTwoVc`):** `des'4 c'4 bes4 | ...` (drop `\f` at 79).

### 6. Bar 75: horn 2 rejoins the cellos at mp (suggestion, not applied)
Horn 2 has rested since 62 and enters the counter-melody at mp< with the cellos (c008 matched it to them). By PDF 136 this is the "colourless" re-entry, and by PDF 127 the new colour should enter lower and crescendo faster. Fix if wanted: `aTwoHnTwo` bar 75 `es2(\p\<` (mf at 77 unchanged; horn 1 rests, so the shared staff has one dynamic). I did not apply it because c008 chose the unison level on purpose and the gain is small.
