### 1. Holistic comparison with Beethoven Op. 61/ii: would a conductor believe a master wrote this? (recorded)

Yes. Beethoven's Larghetto is the classical model for three things this movement does, and it does them the same way:
- **A soft, single-colour opening, then the tune handed to winds.** Beethoven bars 1-10: muted strings pp, nothing else. At bar 11 the clarinet takes the theme p dolce over the horns' sustained chord, and at bar 20 the bassoon follows, with the soloist decorating. Here, 1-6 is a held string chord that thins to winds. At 7-14 the horn takes theme A over bass clarinet, bassoons and harp, with no strings. Each statement of the tune changes colour without changing the line: strings at 23, violins at 53 and 71, harp harmonics at 83. Rimsky PDF 66-67 asks for this (intimate phrases to single solo winds), and Beethoven's bars 11-20 show it.
- **Restraint.** Beethoven's movement stays between pp and p until the final tutti (corpus bars 82-83, f-ff), which is a link, not a climax. This movement has two ff points (61-62 and 79-81), and both are cut back at once (63, 82). Dvořák 9/ii is the stated model and it has a larger arch, so two peaks are right here. The second peak is scored differently from the first (c303, c333).
- **A slow movement that leads straight into the next.** Beethoven ends on a dominant-preparing tutti, a cadenza and "attacca subito il Rondo". This movement closes in its own key with the plagal D♭-C over A♭ and moves into the scherzo by retuning in the pause. That is the Romantic version of the same idea: the finish is a hand-off, not a full stop.

There is one difference, and it is not a fault. Beethoven's middle-register wind solos have pizzicato or silent strings under them (corpus bars 19-24: strings pizz. p on the off-beats). This score does the same under the cor anglais at 41-44 (viola pizz. triplets, Cb pizz.), but under the flute at 37-40 the viola triplets are arco. Rimsky's Ex. 1 and Ex. 12 (173, 186) use arco moving inner strings under a solo, so both choices follow the reading. The change from arco to pizz. at 41 also gives the second phrase its own colour. No change.

### 3. Bass pedal at 65-70 against Ex. 40 (recorded, follows)

Ex. 40 (Scheherazade ii, PDF 218) has four solo double basses, muted, holding a pedal under the solo bassoon. Bars 65-70 have "4 soli, sul A" basses holding E♭ under the solo horn, with the timpani's soft E♭ roll. This is the same layout, with the horn in place of the bassoon. PDF 56's ban on basses carrying a phrase alone does not apply to a pedal. No change.

### 4. The D♭ climax at 61-62 against Ex. 32 (recorded, follows)

*Antar*'s Adagio in D♭ (PDF 210-211) puts a ff melody in double octaves over winds repeating the chord and harp arpeggios. At 61-62, theme B's head is in three octaves under Vln I D♭6 and Fl 1 A♭6, and the viola tremolo supplies the inner motion that Rimsky's repeated wind chords supply. The harp rests and is kept for 82-83. The layout is protected (c404). No change.

### 5. Theme A's return in the violins over a figured viola part (recorded, follows)

Ex. 31 (Kitezh, PDF 209) and Ex. 25 (Ivan the Terrible, PDF 201) put a cantabile over divided violas in moving eighths, with the cellos holding a separate line. Bars 71-78 do the same: the violin tune, viola eighths, the cello counter-melody sul D, and plain basses. One figure sits in each layer, as in the examples. No change.

### 6. Coda strings 83-92 against Ex. 5 and Ex. 26 (recorded, follows)

At 83, "4 soli" cellos and the basses hold the A♭ pedal ppp. The divided violas (85) and then the whole string choir (87) come in, and Vln I enters alone on C5 at 89. Ex. 5 (179) uses "C-b. 4 soli" under divided cellos at p. Ex. 26 (202) leaves a soft top line to a few players. This movement keeps the solo players at the bottom and adds the top last, which suits a fade. The plagal close is protected. No change.

### 7. Wind octaves at 79-81 (recorded, follows PDF 67-68)

Flutes, oboes and clarinets each play in octaves at 79-81. PDF 67-68 allows same-instrument octaves when strings double them, and Vln I/II double the same pitches (B♭6/B♭5 at 79). This is a ff tutti, which PDF 67 names as the place for doubling. Protected layout (c218, c287). No change.
