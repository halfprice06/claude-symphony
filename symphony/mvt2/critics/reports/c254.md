## Findings

### 1. Bars 58–59: the clarinets' middle parts make an open fifth and an open fourth under the string melody, and Cl 2 sounds the resolution of the melody's suspension early (applied)

**Problem.** In 57 b3–59 b2 clarinets 1·2 are the wind's two sustained middle parts under theme B. The theme is in Fl 1 + Vln I with Ob 1 + Vln II an octave lower, and the bass is separate. Most of their intervals are thirds and seconds: D♭5/B♭4 at 57 b3–58 b1, C5/A♭4 at 58 b3, D♭5/C♭5 at 58 b4. Two are open. At 58 b2 Cl 1 D♭5 sits over Cl 2 G♭4, a fifth. At 59 b1–2, marked f, Cl 1 E♭5 sits over Cl 2 B♭4, a fourth. On that beat Cl 1 is also in unison with the melody's lower octave (Ob 1 and Vln II E♭5), so it stops being a middle part and only thickens the theme. There is a harmonic fault at 58 b2 as well. Over the E♭ bass the melody holds A♭ (A♭6/A♭5) as a 4–3 suspension that resolves to G♭ on the second half of the beat. Cl 2's G♭4 states the note of resolution under the suspension, so the suspension is weakened. In the score as it stands, G♭ appears in the winds at 58 b2 only because of this anticipation.

**Evidence.** PDF 97 rem. 4: when the upper part is given to the strings, "there remains nothing for the wind except the sustained harmony in the two middle parts … choosing no other intervals except seconds, sevenths, thirds or sixths". PDF 90 rule 4 (and its image): fourths and fifths count as discords that should not go to two instruments of one colour unless they are to be emphasised. PDF 84: "The rules of harmony concerning sustained and pedal passages apply with equal force to orchestral writing." PDF 52: one wind added to strings in unison "only thickens the resonance of the latter".

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, `bSecClOne` and `bSecClTwo`):**
```
bSecClOne:  r2 des''2\mp\< | des''2 c''4 des''4~ | des''2\f r2 | c''1\< \breathe |
bSecClTwo:  r2 bes'2~\mp\< | bes'2 aes'4 ces''4 | bes'2\f r2 | aes'1\< \breathe |
```
These replace `des''2 c''4 des''4 | es''2\f r2` and `bes'4 ges'4 aes'4 ces''4`. Cl 2 holds B♭4 through 58 b1–2, so the pair is a third (D♭5/B♭4) on ii7 and the suspension resolves cleanly in the melody. Cl 1 ties its D♭5 from 58 b4 into 59. The pair at 59 b1–2 becomes a third (D♭5/B♭4), and the chord becomes a complete ii6/5 (G♭–B♭–D♭–E♭). The D♭ is prepared at 58 b4 and held as the seventh. At 59 b3 the melody itself takes D♭5 (Ob 1, Vln II) as the seventh of E♭7/G, exactly where Cl 1 stops, and resolves it to C at 60. c049's rests at 59 b3–4, the breath before 60, c047's register above horn 1, Cl 2's C♭5 → B♭4 and the shared f at 59 are all kept. Equal dynamics remain on the shared staff. The clarinets' MIDI note count drops by one each.

### 2. Bars 87–92: the double basses play tutti under divided violas at the close; reduce them to four players (applied; this is a change to the ending, report it)

**Problem.** From 84 b3 the violas are divided (C4/E♭4, then F3/A♭3, F♭3/A♭3, E♭3/A♭3, with the lower half sul C). Above them the violins each hold one note, and the muted horns and cor anglais leave at 90. Under this the whole bass section doubles the cellos' bass an octave lower: F1, D♭2, then A♭1 from 89 to the fermata. Each divided viola note has about half a section. The root A♭ at 89–92 is in the full cellos, the full basses and the full second violins. The fifth E♭ is only in half the violas and Cl 2, and the third C only in Vln I. The basses are already ppp, so no softer dynamic is left to mark. c240 had to lower the basses' MIDI level at 83–86 for the same reason: they sounded above the harp harmonics.

**Evidence.** PDF 88: "If the harmony in the three upper parts, thus strengthened, is written for divided strings, the 'cellos and basses, playing non divisi will prove a trifle heavy; their tone must therefore be eased, either by marking the parts down or reducing the number of players." PDF 51: in piano one string section equals one wind. So the tutti bass weighs as much as the cor anglais or a muted horn pair, and more than half a viola section. PDF 81: a change in the forces should come with a new idea. Bar 87 starts the plagal close (new bass note F1, violas divide lower, muted horns and cor anglais enter). The score already uses "4 soli" for the basses at 65–70 (c109). Dvořák 9/ii, this movement's model, reduces the basses to "2 basses" and the cellos to "2 cellos" and "1 cello" in the soft broken phrases at 105–111 (strings.notesL.ily).

**Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, `codaCb`):**
```

```
Only text is added at 87, the first new bass note after the tie from 82. No pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The cellos stay tutti because they carry the moving bass F2–D♭3–A♭2 of the plagal close. The scherzo's double-bass part should begin "tutti"; the composer should pass this to the scherzo thread. MIDI is unchanged.
