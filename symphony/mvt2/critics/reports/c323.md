## Findings

No finding needs a score change. The reading's principles are all met, most of them already recorded at c083 and c173. The points below are new evidence or new scans, ranked by how much they bear on form.

### 1. The large plan puts the remote point at the time midpoint (no change)

**Check.** PDF 231 says the stopover "forms a resting place almost midway between I and V", and the Points for Review (PDF 244) say the stopover leads from I to V with chords leading into and out of it. Here the stopover is IV (D♭, 37–61), not III; III in major needs accidentals and is rare (PDF 233–234), so IV is the normal choice for a major-key slow movement. From the MIDI tempo map: theme B enters at 36.3 % of the running time, the F♯-minor quotation starts at bar 48 at 49.1 %, D♭ returns at 53 (55.5 %), the climax is at 61 (64.6 %), V7 arrives at 65 (70.0 %), A′ at 71 (76.0 %), the coda at 83 (88.6 %).

**Corpus.** Mozart 41/ii (101 bars, MCTL annotation) reaches its most remote keys, B♭ minor and F minor, at bars 51–52, which is 49.5–50.5 % of its length, and returns to F at bar 60 (58.4 %). This movement's farthest point (F♯ minor = G♭ minor, the minor iv of D♭) falls at the same proportion. The midpoint holds the quotation of movement I, so the cyclic event and the formal centre coincide. Record; c253 and c303 settled the section proportions.

### 2. Bar 78 b3 is the reading's VI–(III)–IV progression (no change)

**Check.** At 78 b1–2 the deceptive cadence goes to ♭VI (F♭–A♭–C♭). On b3 the bass falls to E♭ under C and G (C4 in horns 1·2 and cellos, C6 in Vln I and Fl 1, G in Cl 2 and the harp), and on 79 to D♭ (IV with added 6th). This is iii6 as a passing chord in a falling bass between VI and IV, the pattern of 15-10c and d (PDF 237–238): III takes the 7̂ (Cl 2 G4), which falls to 6̂ (Cl 2 F4 at 79). Mixture on the VI does not change the function. The rule against rising thirds from weak to strong (PDF 240) is not involved: the roots move C → D♭.

**Corpus.** Mozart 41/ii bar 15 has IV6–III6–iii6–II6–I6 in a falling 6/3 chain (harmony.csv onsets 42.5–48), the same use of III6 as a passing chord.

This confirms c103 (iii6 accepted) and c133 ("passing 6/3, do not correct"). Record it with the 15-10 reference.

### 3. A′ replaces the antecedent's half cadence with an inverted dominant (no change)

**Check.** In A (10, 26) the first phrase ends on root-position V, the semicadence of 15-1 (PDF 230–231). In A′ the same place (74) has V4/3 over B♭ on b1–2 and V6/4 on b3, and the bass steps B♭ → A♭ into 75. PDF 235–236: inverted dominants "do not normally constitute goals of motion"; the stepwise connection to I gives "a decidedly melodic, contrapuntal character". So A′ loses its internal half cadence and runs from 71 to the deceptive ♭VI at 78 and the full close at 82 as one span. This is why a 12-bar reprise does the work of the first 24 bars, and it matches the reading. c313 recorded the stepwise close (Schubert); this adds the A&S reason.

### 4. IV → V at 61–65 follows 15-2's directed bass (no change)

**Check.** PDF 231: from the stopover to V the bass should be directed and 5̂ should not be kept as a common tone. Bass: D♭1 (61) – G♭1, E1 (62) – A1/A2 (63–64 b2) – D2 (64 b3) – E2 (64 b4) – E♭2 (65). No E♭ is sounded between 61 and 65, so V arrives without a common tone, as in 15-2. The A-minor region is a chromatic interpolation (♭II), not a stopover. Protected since c083 (chromatic bass D♭–D–E♭); recorded only.

### 5. Two dominants, two treatments (no change)

**Check.** PDF 234–235 (Brahms 119/3): V is reached "by almost imperceptible degrees" and then the sforzando and quick notes push it forward. At 59–60 the approach to V of D♭ does this: bass G♭–G–A♭, viola sextuplets at 59, timpani p<f at 60, poco allargando. The movement's main dominant at 65 is treated in reverse: it arrives pp after the climax has thinned out (63–64), and the six bars of root-position V7 (65–70) carry Hn 1's motto tail, not a build. A slow movement that returns softly after its climax needs this, and Dvořák 9/ii returns the same way. Both are protected (59–61, 65–70). Record.

### 6. New scan: half-bar beats in 4/4 and phrase grouping (no change)

**Check.** c173 scanned downbeats for rising-third root motion (PDF 240). The 4/4 bars (37–64) also have a strong third beat, so I scanned beat 2 → beat 3 in every 4/4 bar. Result: no rising third into a beat 3. The root moves into beat 3 are by fifth or fourth (38, 41, 42, 43, 53, 54, 57, 58), by step (39, 40, 56, 62), on the same root (37, 45, 59, 60, 61, 63) or by falling third (46 B♭ → G♭, 55 E♭ → C). Falling thirds are not the reading's concern. The only rising thirds in the movement are still the three downbeats c173 recorded (40→41, 56→57, 57→58).

Phrase grouping (exercise 2, PDF 245–246): every section is in fours except B2 (3 + 3 + 2). The exercise suggests trying irregular groups; it is not a rule against regular ones. Dvořák 9/ii is also mostly in fours. No change.

### 7. 31–36: the reading's "two meanings" chord stays without a seventh (not re-raised)

PDF 231 shows ♮VII as V of III making III "sound like a goal", which would support adding G♭ to the A♭ chord at 36 so that D♭ at 37 sounds like a goal. This was rejected at c020, c203 and c233, with a stated reason: phrase modulation at 37 (A&S PDF 452, Ex. 26-11), the A♭ chord held as a goal, the bare fifth 35–36 protected. The reading gives no new argument against that reason. D♭ is still clearly reached through the new key signature, the tempo change, the harp and pizzicato entry and the flute's C5–F5 pickup. I do not re-raise it.
