## Findings

### 1. Vln II 52–60: the theme B pickup falls on a down-bow, and 60's \upbow becomes a second full up-bow (applied)

**Bars:** Vln II 52 (bSec bar 16) to 61. Vln I is in unison with it from 52 b4.

**Problem.** c186 set the Vln II count from 45 on: an up-bow at 45, 49 b3–4 slurred, and 52 one bow (`es''2.\<`), "to keep 52 b4 an up-bow with Vln I" (HISTORY c186; c258 finding 3 repeats the count). c322 changed 52 to `f''2\< es''4` on harmonic grounds and added a bow without adjusting anything else. The count is now 45 U, 46 D U, 47 D, 48 U D, 49 U D, 50 U D, 51 U, 52 D U, and the pickup `c''4(` at 52 b4 is a down-bow. Vln I enters on the same C5 after rests and takes it up-bow by habit. From 53 to 59 the two sections play the same notes under the same slurs, so they bow in opposite directions throughout theme B's last statement. At 59 Vln II ends on an up-bow (`es''4.( f''8 des''4 bes'4)`, four beats at f). Then the printed `c''1\upbow\<` at 60 asks for another four-beat up-bow, which cannot be hooked. The players must either retake in the air before the climax crescendo or play 60 down-bow. If they play it down-bow, the crescendo runs toward the tip and they reach the ff at 61 on an up-bow. That undoes the protected plan in which both violin sections reach 61 on a fresh down-bow (c186, c246).

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38, obs. 1: "A performer will naturally begin an anacrusis, or upbeat, with an up-bow". This is the Vln I pickup, so Vln II has to match it. PDF 38, obs. 2 and Ex. II-19 (Schubert 5/ii): two successive up-bows are a short hooked device with "the dash under the note" indicating the stop, used to land a down-bow on an accented downbeat. Two whole bars in one direction are not that device. PDF 38: the crescendo belongs on the up-bow and the ff at the frog. Ex. II-20 (Coriolanus): the bowing is arranged so that "the following attack will be solid". PDF 37: notes without a slur each take a new bow, so the second note c322 added is a bow change, not a fingering detail.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-376/work/sections/bSec.ily, bSecVlnTwo, line 194).** Slur the new neighbour note into the E♭. This keeps c322's F5 and D3 harmony, restores c186's bow count, and leaves the crescendo on one down-bow, as c258 accepted for the old `es''2.\<`:

```
% bSecVlnTwo, 50-52
fis''4( a''4) d''2 | es''1 | f''2(\< es''4) c''4(\p^\markup ... "sul A," "cantabile" |
```

The count is now 51 U, 52 D, 52 b4 U (with Vln I), 59 D, 60 U, and 61 D (ff on a fresh down-bow with Vln I). No notes, dynamics or MIDI change.

### 2. Double basses 28–30: the p>pp close is an up-bow while the cellos close on a down-bow (applied)

**Bars:** Cb 28–30 (aOne). The same applies to Vc 28–30 for comparison.

**Problem.** The basses enter at 21 after twenty bars' rest, so they enter down-bow, and every note after that takes its own bow: 21 D U D, 22 U, 23 D, 24 U D U, 25 D U, 26 D, 27 U D U, 28 D U, 29 D, 30 U. The diminuendo to pp at 30, where the strings fade as the bassoon and contrabassoon pedal takes over (c063), therefore moves toward the frog in the basses. In the cellos it is a down-bow, because c118 slurred 28 `aes2( des'4)` for that reason. The two bass parts end the phrase in opposite directions, and the basses' end against the habit.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 38: the composer should "indicate bow direction only where he wishes to counteract the normal habit of the players", and the habit is "a diminuendo with a down-bow". Ex. II-55 (Brahms 1/iv, PDF 54–55) puts each low-string diminuendo on its own bow from the frog. Ex. II-19 (Schubert) bows cellos and basses together.

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-376/work/sections/aOne.ily, aOneCb).** Slur 28 as the cellos do. The mf>, which arrives on beat 2 inside the bow as in the cellos' f>, is unchanged:

```
% aOneCb, 27-30
aes,4\< c4 des4 | \after 4 \mf \after 4 \> des2( bes,4) | es,2. | \after 2 \pp aes,,2.\p\> |
```

The count is now 28 D, 29 U, 30 D, the same as the cellos. The reprise at 79–82 has no matching problem because the basses have "change bow at will" from 79.

### 3. Recorded, no change

- **Violins and violas 82, p>pp on an up-bow.** c258 recorded this, and changing it would need a hook inside c246's protected 79–81 plan. It is not re-raised.
- **Vln II and violas 64 b3–66 (c278 tie).** The slur ends on the first tied note at 65, so the bow changes on the tie at 66. The first bow is about 6 s at p>pp (two beats at allargando, then 65 at ♩=52), which is within Adler's limit for a soft slow bow (PDF 38–39). It follows.
- **Basses 62→63, arco to pizz. with no rest.** Adler (PDF 54) prefers some time. The up-bow at 62 b3 is his own remedy, so that "the players will have an up-bow stroke just before the pizzicato" (c316). It follows.
- **Divisi, mutes, pizz./arco, tremolo, sul tasto and string names** all follow (rows 5–7, 20–26, 29).
- **No string harmonics are used.** The ppp ending is held stopped notes at 89–92, sul D and sul C, which is the warmer choice for a plagal close. Adler's natural harmonics (PDF 62–66) would change that colour for no gain in playability.
