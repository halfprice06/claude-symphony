## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bar 37: theme B's first statement lost its expressive word in v72 (applied)
**Problem.** Flute 1 plays the first statement of theme B, the movement's second theme, with "1." and p only. Every other solo in the movement has a character word: "dolce espr." (Hn 1, 6), "solo, espr." (B.cl., 31), "dolce" (C.a., 32), "solo, dolce cantabile" (C.a., 41, the same theme four bars later), "dolce espr." (Ob 1, 44), "solo, espr." (C.a., 47), "espr." (Hn 1, 52), "dolce" (Hn 1, 67), "espr." (Vln I, 70). Up to v71 the flute had `f''2(\p^\markup \italic "dolce cantabile"`. In v72 the flute pickup was dropped for the 8-page layout (c072), and the word went with it into the unused `transFluteOneX`. Nobody restored it.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: the orchestrator chooses the instrument, register and dynamic for the tune's "emotional as well as the tonal intent", and Berlioz's solo is printed "mf espress." (Ex. XV-33). Franck (PDF 38) prints "molto espress." at each choir's first melodic entry.
**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecFluteOne`, bar 37):** `f''2(\p\<^\markup \italic "dolce cantabile" ges''8 f''8 es''4\! | ...`. In the score the word sits between the tempo mark and "1."; the system and page layout are unchanged.

### 2. Bar 60: "poco allargando" over Vln I sits under the timpani's p and reads as a timpani direction (applied; closes the open item from c068)
**Problem.** At 60 the Vln I copy of the tempo word is printed directly below the timpani's `p` and its crescendo hairpin, about a quarter of a space away, so it reads as "p poco allargando" for the timpani. This is the open item "poco allargando vs timpani p at 60". c068 did not fix it because more space between the staves risks a ninth page.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 55: no less than ½ stave-space between characters and no collisions. Adler PDF 195: dynamics "must be strictly observed", which needs each dynamic and tempo word to belong clearly to its staff.
**Fix (global.ily, bar 60):** move the Vln I copy right and down beside the up-bow sign instead of adding vertical space:
`\mt 64 s1 \once \override Staff.MetronomeMark.extra-offset = #'(1.8 . -0.7) \tempo \markup \medium \italic "poco allargando" ...`
The `Staff.` override does not move the copy above the flutes. Checked at 300 dpi: the text now begins after the V and has more than ½ space below the timpani p. Still 8 pages.

### 3. Bar 91: the horn 3 and 4 parts have no "via sord." (applied in the parts only; closes the open item from c090)
**Problem.** Horns 3·4 play "con sord." at 87–90 (mutes taken at 82, "mettere sord.") and rest at 91–92. Their parts never tell them to remove the mutes. The next movement follows, and c088 relayed that Hn 3·4 need "senza sord." at their first entry in movement III. A "via sord." in the rest at 91 is the normal warning. Adler's colour examples mark every muted entry explicitly ("Hn. I muted", "Tr. I muted", Ex. XV-38, PDF 191), and MOLA PDF 5 and Ex. 5 (PDF 10), cited by c075 for the strings' mutes at 31, ask for the change in the rest before it is needed.
**Fix (coda.ily, `codaHnThree` and `codaHnFour`, bar 91):** `R2.-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "via sord." | R2.\fermata |`. Part-only, as for the strings' "via sord." at 31; the full score is unchanged at 91.
