## Findings (ranked)

None of the findings needs a score change. The movement's texture follows this chapter in every place where the chapter applies. The two departures (F1, F2) lie in passages locked by the c124 rule (no accompaniment changes in 37–60) and the c277 protection of the viola sextuplets. F1 corrects a reason in the record.

**F1. The record's reason for having no wind imitation is wrong about the model. No change to the score; correct the reason.**
Adler PDF 271: "since each of the woodwinds has such a unique color, contrapuntal writing, especially rhythmical, is very effective"; Ex. VIII-42 (Mozart 38/iii) has oboes and bassoons answering each other one bar apart over pizzicato strings. c177 (item 4) and c317 (F8) recorded "dialogue and counter-melody instead of imitation (as Dvořák 9/ii)". The corpus shows that Dvořák 9/ii does have wind imitation, at exactly the matching place in the form. At 90–92 of the Largo, just before the ff climax at 96, oboe 1 plays the sextuplet figure (90) while flute 1 waits. Flute 1 then plays the trill figure (91) while oboe 1 holds G♯. At 92 they swap: flute 1 takes the sextuplets and oboe 1 the trill figure. Clarinet 1 has both figures in the same bars, and all three join the tremolo build at 93–95 (woods.notesL.ily, flute lines after `\barNumberCheck#FourmL`, oboe and clarinet the same). That is Adler's "throwing the ball around" (PDF 253) in Dvořák's slow movement. In this score the matching place is 57–60, the build into the climax at 61. Those bars are locked by the c124 rule, and the viola sextuplets 59–60 are protected (c277). So the choice not to use imitation still stands, but its reason is the c124 rule, not the Dvořák model. The score already has its contrapuntal centre at 53–56: theme A's head (Cl 1/Hn 1, dotted rhythm) against theme B (violins, half notes and quarters) over the harp triplets. That is two themes, two colours and three rhythmic layers, which is the rhythmic counterpoint PDF 271 describes. Fix: none. HISTORY should record "no wind imitation by choice (c124 rule for 37–60); Dvořák 9/ii 90–92 does imitate", so a later critic does not cite the model for the opposite point.

**F2. Bars 59–60: the viola sextuplets cross the horn's counter-line register. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 274 (*Sacre*, Ex. VIII-44): "Even though there are so many diverse parts, the excerpt is very clear, because Stravinsky has orchestrated each gesture in the best register of each instrument", with "the mixing of rhythms three against two, four against six". At 59 b3–60 the counter-line is Hn 1 at E♭4–F♭4–E♭4–F4–G♭4, with Bsn 1 and the cellos an octave below. The violas' sextuplets run G♭3–F♭4 and A♭3–G♭4 through the horn's octave. The grid shows the viola figure striking the horn's own pitch: F♭4 on the attack at 59 b4 (Hn 1's F♭4 and the first note of the second half of the sextuplet group), E♭4 inside 59 b3, and G♭4 twice inside 60 b4 under Hn 1's held G♭4. Adler's other two conditions hold. The rhythms differ (sixteenth sextuplets against quarters and halves), and the colours differ (horn and bassoon against divided strings). The line is also tripled at f against a figure, which makes it the stronger layer. The crossing lasts six beats inside a crescendo to ff. Fix: none (locked: c124, c277). If 59–60 is ever reopened, change the viola figure, not the horn: on 59 b4 start the group on its lower note, `des'16( bes des' fes' des' bes)` for `fes'( des' bes des' fes' des')`, so the horn's F♭4 attack is not struck with it.

**F3. Pulsation under the C.a. quotation (48–51) and the viola figure on Cl 2 (71–74). Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 262 (*La Mer*, Ex. VIII-35): the oboe "pulsates" sustained pitches of the flute's tune. In the echo, the English horn "pulsates on afterbeats a little like a second violin or viola part". This score has both uses. At 48–51 the violas play sotto voce off-beat eighths (A3 at 48, A3/B3/G♯3 at 49, A3/D4/B3 at 50, E♭4 at 51) under the cor anglais's quotation, while Vln II takes a counter-line above it (F♯5 at 48, A5–G5–E♯5 at 49, F♯5–A5–D5 at 50). The quotation is therefore framed by a pulse below and a line above and is doubled by neither. At 71–74 the viola figure re-strikes Cl 2's held inner notes: E♭4 at 71 b1.5 and b2.5, A♭4 at 72 b1.5 and 73 b1.5, D♭4 at 74 b1. Clarinet 2 sustains and the violas articulate, which is the *La Mer* relation between one colour that holds and one that articulates. c247 and c324 already took Cl 2 off the melody's pitches, and what remains is this inner-voice pulsation. Fix: none.

**F4. Theme A's three colours and the third-time rule. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 255: "most of the great masters try to alter something, no matter how slightly, the third time they use the same orchestration." Theme A appears in three colours: horn over bassoons and bass clarinet (7), violins in unison over a plain string background (23), and violins in octaves with the viola figure and Cl 2 (71). From 75 the cello, Bsn 1 and horn counter-melody joins. Within 15–22 the third exchange (19–20) changes the accompaniment from 15–16 (Vln II rests at 20 b1–2, and the violas answer in the oboe's rhythm), and the fourth (21–22) brings the basses in. Copland's third statement, joining "two previous phrases, one for strings alone, the other for winds alone" (PDF 270), is the plan of 7 (winds), 23 (strings) and 71 (both). Fix: none.

**F5. Spacing scan: largest gap at the bottom. Follows except the protected 7–14. Record.**
Adler PDF 257, Ex. VIII-25: "the greater gaps exist between the bass and the next highest voice", and the upper notes lie close. From the grid: 65 E♭2 / B♭2 / G3 / D♭4 (fifth, sixth, diminished fourth); 89–92 A♭1 / A♭2 / E♭3 / A♭3 / E♭4 / A♭4 / C5 (the octave at the bottom, then a fifth, a fourth, a fifth, a fourth and a third above); 45 B♭1 / B♭2 / F3 / D♭4 / F4 / D♭5. The only close low chord under sustained winds is 7–14 (B.cl. A♭2, Bsn 2 C3, Bsn 1 E♭3), which Ex. VIII-25 marks "bad". The harp LH's A♭1–E♭2 strikes under every one of those chords, and the passage is protected (c004, c077, c317 F1). Fix: none.

**F6. Final chord and the Copland horn. Follows; record.**
Adler PDF 271: in Ex. VIII-41 the muted horn, after doubling the second violins, "switches at the end to emphasize the third of the chord". In the score the muted Hn 3·4 double the violas at 87–90 (F3/A♭3, E3/A♭3, E♭3/A♭3) and leave at 90 b3. The final chord's third, C, then sounds only in Vln I's C5. That is the protected link to movement I (C5 on top at start and end) and the plagal close with D♭–C on top. Giving the third to a second voice would weaken the exposed C5 that the cyclic plan depends on. Fix: none.
