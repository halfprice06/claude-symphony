## Findings

### 1. 48–51: four bars of held whole notes under the quotation (applied; closes the open item "off-beat violas 48–51")
**Problem.** Under the cor anglais quotation, every string part holds: Vla `a1 | a2 b4 gis4 | a2 d'2 | c'1`, with Vln II, Vc and Cb in halves and wholes. The background has no rhythm of its own. The quotation also holds long notes: the A4 tied over 48, the syncopated D5 at 49 and C♯5 at 50. Under them nothing moves.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135: the accompaniment should differ "radically" in rhythm from the foreground, be "repetitious and simple", and "add intensity to the longer notes of the melody". PDF 158, Ex. V-32 (Mendelssohn, *Elijah*, an Adagio): the standard soft support for a solo line is the on–off beat pattern. PDF 160–161, Ex. V-36: the strings carry the rhythm so that the soloist "can execute her part without orchestral interference". Dvořák 9/ii (above) keeps the inner strings moving under the woodwind tune. c027 rejected movement here because it would follow the quotation's rhythm. Off-beat eighths do not follow it: they never fall on the quotation's attacks.
**Fix (applied).** In `bSecVla`, the violas only, on their existing pitches. The pp moves from 47 b4 onto the first off-beat, because the hairpin left at 47 caused a new "decrescendo too small" warning in the parts. Beat 4 of 48 rests under the cor anglais' G♯4.
```
\stemNeutral \revert Beam.damping f1\pp^"arco" | f2 bes2\< | b1\mp\> |
r8 a8-_\pp-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "sotto voce" r8 a8-_ r8 a8-_ r4 | r8 a8-_ r8 a8-_ r8 b8-_ r8 gis8-_ |
r8 a8-_ r8 a8-_ r8 d'8-_ r8 d'8-_ | r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ r8 c'8-_ | c'1\< |
```
The notes, the harmony, the tempo plan for 47–53 and the cor anglais line are unchanged. The violas still hold C4 through 52 under the timpani roll, so the pulse stops one bar before the harp triplets start at 53. The background now runs: triplets (37–44), held notes (45–47, Adler's "sudden cessation", PDF 136), off-beats (48–51), held notes (52), harp triplets (53), viola triplets (57), sextuplets (59). The "sotto voce" tag prints in the score as well, because the viola staff does not remove part tags. The score stays at 8 pages.

### 2. 53–55: the harp arpeggio has no sustained support (applied)
**Problem.** At 53–56 the violas rest, so that they re-enter fresh at 57 (c049). Bassoon 2 rests because it doubled the cellos (c077). Between the cello/bass line (D♭3/D♭2) and the violins' theme (F5), the only sound from 53 b1 to 55 b2 is the harp's plucked triplets. Horn 1's counter-line enters only at 53 b4. Bassoon 1 already holds a support line from 55 b3 (F3, G♭3–F3), but it starts after the harmony has passed through IV6, ii6 and ii7.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 136, on Brahms 3/iii: the flutes and bassoons "clarify and support the arpeggiated harmony… another stabilizing and enriching element". PDF 143, Ex. V-34: the arpeggiated figure stands over sustained harmony notes. PDF 163–164, Ex. V-39: held notes stand in for the sustaining pedal under a moving figure. Here the support exists only for the last six beats.
**Fix (applied).** In `bSecBsnOne`, start the existing line at 54:
```
R1 | des1~(\pp | des2 f2 | ges2 f2) |
```
D♭3 is the fifth of IV6 at 54, the seventh of ii6/5 and ii7 at 54 b3 and 55 b1, and moves to F3 at 55 b3 as before. I tried entering at 53 b3 on E♭3, but that moves E♭→D♭ in double octaves with the theme (53 b4 → 54 b1), so the bassoon enters at 54. It is not a doubling of the strings, so c077's reason stands, and bassoon 2 and the bass clarinet still enter fresh at 57. A hairpin from pp to p at 55 made the system taller and pushed the score to 9 pages, so the entry is a single pp.

### Note for the composer
The header of `bSec.ily` says that at 53–60 the theme is in "both violin sections (an octave apart)". At 53–56 the two sections play in unison, and only from 57 are they an octave apart. That unison-then-octaves step is right by Adler PDF 137 (crescendo by adding octaves). Please correct the comment so that a later critic does not treat the unison as an error.
