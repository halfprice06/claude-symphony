## Findings

The melodic lines meet every principle in this reading that applies to them. I found nothing that this reading would change, and I made no edits to the scratch copy. The findings below record what I checked so later critics on this lens do not reopen these points without new evidence.

### 1. The notes the themes stress are made to dominate in a planned order. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 12, 28, 76, 79 (theme A); 58, 61-63 (theme B).

**Check against PDF 23** ("be aware of the melody notes the composer wishes to stress, and why, as well as how these are made to dominate"):
- Theme A's hook is the B♭ appoggiatura to A♭ and the fall through the motto tail. At 12 it is the horn's highest note (B♭4, mp at the end of the 11 crescendo), with nothing above it in the harp. At 28 it is B♭5 in both violin sections at f. At 76 it is held at B♭5 again (mf). At 79 it is B♭6 ff in three octaves, the movement's highest note. The counter-melody under 79 (Hn 1·2 a2, Vc, Bsn 1: D♭4-C4-B♭3) falls while the tune holds, so nothing in the middle competes with the top.
- Theme B's top note is A♭6 at 58 (Fl 1, Vln I) and stays the B section's ceiling at 61-62 (Fl 1 held). The A♭ moves to A6 at 63 (sf) for the A-minor quotation, and B♭6 is first heard at 79. The register line A♭6 → A6 → B♭6 is the stress plan across the whole movement (c110, c378, c606). The B-section climax does not exceed bar 58's top note on purpose; it gains by doubling and dynamic, as Bruckner's climax does (PDF 21: quasi-unison, then octaves), and leaves the new pitch for the A′ peak.

### 2. Schuller's warning at 61-62 still applies to a harmony voice, not a melody. (Recorded, no change; protected)

**Bars:** 61-62, Hn 3·4 a2, concert A♭4-B♭4-C♭5 (written E♭5-F5-G♭5) at f.

**Check against PDF 26:** Schuller's case is a high, loud unison melody. This is a tenuto harmony line in the upper-middle register at f, with the melody (theme B's augmented head) in Fl 2, Vln II and Vc in three octaves and Fl 1/Vln I above. His trumpet remedy conflicts with the trumpets-tacet decision (c060), and the a2 was settled at c064, c069 and c086 and recorded again at c321 and c449. I do not re-raise it.

### 3. Horn with cello on the counter-melodies follows the Strauss model. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 57-60, 75-82.

**Check against PDF 27 (Ex. XI-10):** at 57-60 Hn 1 plays the counter-line with the cellos; at 75-82 Hn 2 joins the cellos at 75 and Hn 1 at 78 b3 for the a2 at 79. The horn colour is added in stages, which is what Adler says gives the line "personality". At 23-30 the cellos carry the counter-melody alone (protected plain first statement), so the horn doubling is a new colour at the return.

### 4. Colour contrast carries the imitations and answers. (Recorded, no change)

**Bars:** 15-22, 37-44.

**Check against PDF 30-32 (Ex. XI-14):** Adler says the Berlioz canon works "more [through] the color contrasts than ... any other musical parameter". At 15-22 the b-phrase is answered oboe → solo violin → oboe → solo violin, each over muted violas and cellos; the answers differ in colour and are also varied in pitch (escape tones at 17 and 21). At 37-44 the flute's first phrase is followed by the cor anglais in its strong low register with Fl 1 in thirds and sixths above. Each melodic hand-over changes colour, as the reading asks.

### 5. The two releases follow the Lohengrin and Schubert models. (Recorded, no change; protected)

**Bars:** 62-65, 81-83.

**Check against PDF 28-29 and 35-36:** after 62 the trombones and tuba drop out and the bass trombone alone holds under Tbn 1's quotation (Schubert's dropped-trombone diminuendo); the strings' sf>p tremolo at 63-64 leads into the pp horn tail at 67. At 81-82 the winds and strings diminuendo together and the harp harmonics enter p at 83 with the theme's head, which is the Lohengrin order (full doubling, diminuendo, then a soft new colour on top). Both passages are protected (staged release 81-83; F♭→E♭ thread 62-65).

### Comparison with the models

Theme A is built the way Dvořák builds his Largo tune. Dvořák's cor anglais (corpus woods.notesL.ily, bars 7-14) repeats one two-bar cell three times with a changed ending, in a range of a sixth. Theme A repeats its head rhythm (dotted quarter, eighth, tied quarter) at bars 1, 3 and 5 of the theme, rises by a fourth, a fifth and a sixth to its held notes, and ends each half on the chromatic tail E♭-D-D♭-C, which the movement then uses as its last melodic events (horn 67-70, clarinet 85-89). The repetition is what makes it singable after one hearing; the tail is the part a listener keeps. Theme B is a stepwise neighbour-note tune, weaker than theme A by design as a middle-section tune (c401); its opening was closed at c384 and c794, and I do not reopen it. Against Tchaikovsky 5/ii the horn solo at 7-14 has the same setting (middle-register horn over a soft low-wind bed), and Adler's Hindemith passage (PDF 20) explains why it sounds dark rather than heroic: it never enters the horn's bright register.
