On the lens question. Theme A is the tune people will hum. Its head is the major triad (Ex. 2-6, partials 4-5-6). Its second phrase replaces the stable 5̂ peak (E♭, bar 8) with the active 6̂ and 2̂ (F, B♭, bars 11-12). That is the stable-to-active rise of PDF 40, and it is why the second phrase sounds like a continuation. Theme B's second phrase (41, 57) is its memorable part. At the interval level both themes are clean: every dissonance is left by step, every melodic tritone resolves inward, and the outer voices are perfect at the cadences and imperfect between them. One passage does not follow the reading: the counter-line at 53-56. Findings 1 and 2 are the two changes I recommend. The rest record what follows the reading, so later critics do not reopen it on this unit's grounds.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 55: the horn/clarinet counter-line doubles theme B's peak at the octave for a whole bar (applied)

Problem. From 53 b4 to 56 the violins have theme B and horn 1 + clarinet 1 have the counter-line built from theme A's head (A♭ B♭ | D♭. F B♭ | G♭ | C D♭). Between these two prominent lines I count 16 attack points. Three are imperfect consonances, nine are perfect (five of them octaves) and four are dissonances. At 55 the counter-line holds G♭4 for four beats under the theme's G♭5-F5-G♭5-F5, which is the highest note of the phrase and its neighbour. The two lines alternate P8, M7, P8, M7, so the counter-line sounds as an octave doubling of the peak, not as a second voice. By the same count the other duets in the movement follow the reading: Vln I/Vc at 23-30 has 13 of 24 imperfect, Fl/C.a. at 41-44 has 10 of 12, and Verdi's duet in Ex. 2-1b is mostly 3rds and 6ths.

Evidence. A&S PDF 39 §8: perfect consonances belong at points of articulation, and imperfect consonances "normally predominate in places where the music moves from one point of articulation to another"; in textures of more than two parts they lie between the most prominent lines. PDF 33, Ex. 2-1b (image): the two voices of the Recordare move almost entirely in 3rds, 6ths and passing dissonances.

Fix (bSec.ily, bSecHnOne and bSecClOne): hold B♭4 over the barline and take G♭4 on beat 3.
```
% Hn 1, was: des'4.\< f'8 \once \stemUp bes'2 | ges'1\mp | c'2 des'2) \breathe |
des'4.\< f'8 \once \stemUp bes'2~ | bes'2 ges'2\mp | c'2 des'2) \breathe |
% Cl 1, was: ... bes'2 | ges'1\p | ...
des'4.\< f'8 \once \stemUp bes'2~ | bes'2 ges'2\p | c'2 des'2) \breathe |
```
The theme now has a m6 over the counter-line at 55 b1 and a M6 at b3. Its G♭-F oscillation moves between m6 and P5 instead of P8 and M7. Octaves fall from five to three, and the remaining ones are at 54 b1, where the counter-line starts (an articulation point), and at 56, which is discussed in Finding 3. B♭ is a chord tone of ii7 (55 b1-2) and G♭ is a chord tone of the half-diminished chord on C (b3-4). The G♭4→C4 tritone into 56 and its inward resolution to D♭ are unchanged. The tie also gives the counter-line theme A's rhythm: a goal note held over the barline (`aes4. c'8 es'4~ | es'4`), which c171 protects as theme A's identity. Breath, slur, crescendo and range are unchanged, and only the arrival of the mp moves, from 55 b1 to 55 b3.

Protection. The Hn/Cl counter-line 53-56 is on the do-not-touch list (c105, c176, c197). The reasons given there were that it works as texture and is playable. This change keeps every note of 53-54 and 56, the breath plan and the doubling, so those reasons still hold. It does not touch c051's 56 (C4-D♭4).

### 2. Bar 55 b1-2: the cellos' E♭3 lies a second above bassoon 1's D♭3, so the 7th is the lowest note (applied)

Problem. In 53-56 the cellos alone carry the bass (basses tacet, c164). At 55 they jump G♭2→E♭3, above bassoon 1's held D♭3. For two beats the lowest sounding note is the 7th D♭3, a whole step under the root. The theme's G♭5 then stands a 4th above the lowest part on the downbeat, and the bottom of the texture is a low second. At 39 the root is underneath (basses E♭2).

Evidence. A&S PDF 40-41 §11, Ex. 2-11: the 4th is dissonant against the lowest part unless it is an inverted 5th or part of an arpeggio; neither applies here. PDF 39 §9: the dissonance (D♭3 against E♭3) arises from a leap in the bass, not from stepwise motion.

Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVc): `| es2\< c2 |` → `| es,2\< c2 |` (E♭2). The cellos now move G♭2-E♭2 (down a third) instead of up a sixth. The bassoon's D♭3 becomes a prepared 7th above the root, resolving to C3 at b3 as before. Harmony, dynamics and the descending-bass plan ("6/3 bridge 53-56", c143) are unchanged. The note is one ledger line below the bass staff.

### 3. Bar 56 b1: octave C5/C4 between theme and counter-line (no change)

This is the only octave left in the counter-line. Every tone of the A-C-E♭-G♭ chord is already taken: A in the bass, G♭ in bassoon 1 (its 7th, c042), E♭ in the harp, and C in the theme. C4→D♭4 is the correct resolution of the chord's third (PDF 42-43), and it rises against the theme as c051 intended. E♭4-D♭4 would double the harp and bring back c051's parallel sixths. G♭4-F4 would move in octaves with bassoon 1. The bar also leads to the phrase end at 56 b3, where PDF 39 allows a perfect consonance. Keep.

### 4. Melodic tritones and the key-defining resolutions (no change)

Every melodic tritone resolves as the reading requires. They are G4-D♭4 inside theme A's descent at 13, 29, 77 and 81, D♭5→G4→A♭4 in clarinet 2 at 80-82, G♭4→C4→D♭4 in the counter-line at 55-56, and C3→G♭3→F3 in bassoon 1 at 55-56. In each the next note lies inside the interval (PDF 42, Ex. 2-15, with inversion from PDF 36). Bar 8 b3→9 is Ex. 2-15 note for note: bassoon 1 G3 and the horn's D♭4 (d5) resolve to A♭3 and C4 (M3). D♭ gets its key-defining V7-I in theme B's sixth bar (42 b3-4, 58), with the 7th G♭ resolving to F in the melody and C to D♭ in the violas. The chromatic tritone A♭-D at 86 is a passing by-product (Ex. 2-21; c183, c190).

### 5. Theme B's pickup C5-F5 (36-37, 52-53): 7̂ leaps to 3̂ (no change)

The leading tone in the top voice leaps up a fourth instead of resolving to D♭ (PDF 39-40, 7̂ active). It is the plan's "opens with a rising fourth" (c071, c139). At 36 the C is the third of a plain A♭ triad, not of V7, and the violas or harp supply D♭4 under the F at 37 and 53. Keep.

### 6. Bars 11, 27, 75: the bass moves A♭-C half a beat before theme A does (optional, not applied)

At 11 b2 the bass (B.cl., harp LH; Cb at 27 and 75) goes A♭→C while the melody still holds A♭ (m6). Half a beat later the melody reaches C, an octave over the bass. This is an octave by anticipation inside the phrase, the same kind c052 removed at 25 and 73. It is softened by the m6 between and lies on a weak half-beat. If the composer wants the outer voices imperfect here (PDF 39 §8), the fix is `aes,2 des4` in the bass at 11 (B.cl., harp LH `<aes,, es,>2\arpeggio des,4`), 27 (Cb `aes,,2\< des,4`) and 75 (Cb, Bsn 2, harp LH), giving C/A♭ (M3) at b2.5. That loses the bass's stepwise rise C-D♭. I rank it low and did not test it.

### 7. Cello upper half D♭3→C4 at 17-18, a major-seventh leap (no change)

All 7ths are dissonant melodically as well as vertically (PDF 38). Here, though, the upper half of a divisi enters as a new voice (c067), and the lower half carries D♭3→C3 by step. Keep.

### 8. Phrase endings and spelling (no change)

The outer voices are perfect at 7, 14, 22, 30, 40, 44, 56, 61 and 82, and imperfect between them (PDF 39). The A6 at 21 resolves to the octave as Ex. 2-20b does. The enharmonic changes D♭5=C♯5 (46-47) and A♭6→A6 (62-63) keep the spelling of the key the chord comes from (PDF 45, Ex. 2-22b; c070, c180).
