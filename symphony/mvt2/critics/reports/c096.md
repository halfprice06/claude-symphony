## Findings

Ranked by how likely each is to cause trouble at the first rehearsal.

### 1. Bars 59-60, cellos: two four-beat up-bows in a row at f. **Applied.**
**Problem.** Counting the cellos' bows from the fresh start at 48 (after the rest at 47 b4): 48 D; 49 U D U; 50 D U; 51 D; 52 U; 53-56 D U each bar; 57 D, then 57 b3-58 b1 U; 58 `es2( f4)` D; 59 `ges2(\f es4 fes4)` U. Bar 60 is then marked `\upbow`, which makes a second up-bow of four beats at f, crescendo to ff. c085 called this "a retake after 59, as in Ex. II-19", but Ex. II-19 is a different case. There the second up-bow is a short eighth note that continues the same stroke after a slight stop. Here the player would need eight beats of up-bow at f-ff (about 8 s at ♩ = 64-58), which is impossible. Otherwise the player must lift and carry the bow back to the tip with no rest, and the cellos would then attack the ff at 61 out of step with the basses and violins.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 37: "all notes under that slur are performed on one bow". PDF 38 and Ex. II-19: two up-bows in a row are a device for a short note before an accent. PDF 39: "special caution must be taken in slow passages not to overload the bow… cello and bass bows are a bit shorter".
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** `ges2\f es4( fes4) | es4(\upbow\< f4 ges4. f8) |`. Bar 59 now has two bows (U, D), so 60 is an up-bow without a retake (the existing `\upbow` only confirms it), and 61 is a down-bow, as c085 intended. No pitch changed.

### 2. Bar 60, double basses: a whole-note crescendo f to ff on one bass bow under poco allargando. **Applied.**
**Problem.** `aes,,1\upbow\<` lasts about 4.3 s (♩ = 62 then 58) on the shortest bow in the section and must grow from f to ff. Bar 61 is then another 4.4 s whole note at ff. Players will change bow somewhere in 60 anyway, each in a different place, and the crescendo will lose its top.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 39-40, Ex. II-23 (Liszt, *Les Préludes*): crescendo slurs in the cellos and basses are "impossible to perform as the composer has marked while still adhering to the crescendo marks, unless it is broken up into several bows" (Ex. II-24). PDF 38, Ex. II-20 (*Coriolanus*): "a down-bow and up-bow should be marked over the long note so that the following attack will be solid".
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** `ges,,2\f g,,2 | aes,,2~\downbow\< aes,,2\upbow |`. Bar 59 takes two bows, 60 changes bow on the tie (⊓ V, as in Coriolanus), and 61 is still a down-bow. The A♭1 is not re-attacked (tie), and the MIDI and analysis are unchanged.

### 3. Bars 64-65, violas: "sul C" is printed on the second note of the slur. "unis." after non div. at 65 (violas, Vln II). **Applied. Closes the open item "unis. marks at 65".**
**Problem.** The violas' `gis2(\>` at 64 b3 is slurred to `g2.)` at 65, and "unis., sul C" stands at 65. A player who starts the G♯3 on the G string, its natural place one semitone up, finds "sul C" in the middle of the slur and has to cross strings and shift under it. The intended slide from G♯ to G♮ on one string (the E/F♭–E♭ thread on the C string, c020) is then lost. Bar 64 is also non div., so "unis." at 65 cancels nothing. Vln II has the same redundant "unis." at 65, while its "sul G" is already printed correctly at 64 b3, where its slur begins.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 64: the string "must be carefully marked", because the same pitch exists on more than one string. PDF 30: *unis.* appears "when divisi is no longer in order"; non div. means each player plays both notes, so there is no division to cancel.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily and retr.ily).** Viola 64: `gis2(\>^\markup \italic "sul C" |`. Viola 65: `g2.)\pp |`. Vln II 65: `des'2.)\pp |`. Page 5 is unchanged in layout.

### 4. Bars 61-64: tremolo written with three strokes at ♩ = 54 slowing to 40 is ambiguous. **Applied. Closes the open item "trem." at 61-64.**
**Problem.** Three beams on whole and half notes mean 32nd notes. At ♩ = 54 that is 7 notes a second, and at the ♩ = 40 of 64 b3-4 it is 5.3 a second. Both are slow enough to count, so a section could play them as measured 32nds. The intended effect, under the trombone's quotation, is the unmeasured tremolo.
**Evidence.** Adler PDF 49-50 separates the "bowed unmeasured" tremolo, "repeating a single pitch as often as possible" (Ex. II-46, *La Mer*), from the "bowed measured" tremolo, "a convenient shorthand for writing a series of repeated détaché notes" (Ex. II-47, II-48, *Holländer*), and both use the same strokes. At a slow tempo only a word separates them. The composer left this open at v86 because page 5 was full. Merging the word into the existing texts avoids adding a line.
**Fix (applied, bSec.ily).** Viola 61: "non div., molto sostenuto, trem."; Vln II 63: "non div., trem."; Vln I 63 (upper voice): `a'''1:32\sf\>^\markup \italic "trem."`; Vc 63: `a,1:32\sf\>^\markup \italic "trem."`. The basses have no tremolo. Page 5 still holds both systems (61-64, 65-70), and the score is still 8 pages.
