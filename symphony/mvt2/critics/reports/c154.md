## Findings

### 1. Bar 63: the bass trombone stays f under the quotation while everything else falls to p (applied)

Problem. At 63 the whole accompaniment to trombone 1's ff quotation is the string tremolo, marked sf> to p by 64, plus the bass trombone's A2. The bass trombone was marked f for the whole bar and only began its diminuendo at 64. After the first two beats the loudest sustained sound under the solo was a second trombone, a fifth to a tenth below the soloist's E3–C4, louder than the A-minor chord it was supporting. In the MIDI the bass trombone holds velocity 83 through 63 while the strings decay.

Evidence. Adler PDF 42 (Hindemith, Ex. XI-23): in contrapuntal brass writing every instrument is placed so "the subject comes through clearly every time it is played". Same-colour support sitting one dynamic below a solo in the same register works against that. PDF 50–51 (Gershwin, Ex. XI-27): the "sf > p muted effect that introduces the solo", where the brass chord is attacked and then gets out of the way. The strings at 63 already do this (c108 protected "sf> tremolo under Tbn 1 at 63–64"). The bass trombone was the one part that did not follow it. The change keeps the protected release 61–65 (c124) and takes it further. It changes no pitches, and Tbn 1 stays alone and ff (c158).

Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-154/work/sections/bSec.ily):

```
bSecBassTbn = { ... ges,2-- fes,2-- \breathe | a,1\sf\> | a,2\p d,4 e,4 | }
```

(was `a,1\f | a,2\> d,4 e,4\p`). The bass trombone now has the same sf> to p shape as the strings in the same bar and is p at 64, level with the cellos and basses on the A–D–E bass.

### 2. Bars 61–62: horn 2 is the only single horn at f (no change; principle checked)

Adler PDF 15 asks for paired horns above mf. At 61–62 horns 3·4 are a2 on the top line (c064/c086). Horn 2 (F4, G♭4, A♭4) is single. The trombones and tuba below it are marked mf, though, and at mf or less "each brass instrument may be assigned a separate pitch" (same page). Rimsky PDF 41 puts horns one degree above trombones. So a single horn at f is about level with one trombone at mf, and Hn 2's notes are also in Cl 2 and the violas. The only player who could pair it is horn 1, which rests from 61 to 66 before its exposed solo at 67 (protected, c046). Keep as is.

### 3. Bar 62 b1: the brass G♭ chord has no fifth (no change)

The brass at 62 b1 sound only G♭ and B♭ (Tba G♭1, B.tbn G♭2, Tbn 2 B♭3, Hn 2 G♭4, Hn 3·4 B♭4). Every chord in Ex. XI-1 (PDF 15–16) is complete within the brass. The obvious fix, Tbn 2 D♭4 instead of B♭3, makes compound fifths with the bass trombone (A♭3/D♭3 at 61 b3 to D♭4/G♭2). D♭ already sounds in the violas, oboe 2 and the timpani roll, and the brass are not playing alone here. The chord's colour note is the melody's F (G♭maj7). Recorded; no clean fix.

### 4. Bars 80–81: MIDI renders horn 4 at pp and horn 3's E♭4 above the tutti (score correct; not applied)

In score.midi the part-combiner's split voices lose the shared f of bar 79. Horn 4's A♭3 at 80 plays at velocity 37 (pp), and horn 3's E♭4 at 81 b2 plays at 83, louder than the strings (66) during the diminuendo. The printed score is right. This only affects the mp3 balance of the four-horn chord at the A′ peak (protected c147). A fix would need a MIDI-only dynamic on the "two" voice. The score's printed dynamics must not change, so I leave it to the composer's MIDI tooling.

### 5. What the reading confirms

- Holding trombones and tuba back to 60–64, with sustaining first and the new colour at 63, is Adler's first climax technique (PDF 37, Schubert).
- The A′ peak 79–81 without heavy brass, with four horns in the tutti, is the Franck model (PDF 38).
- The low close horn chords at 1–6 and the muted Hn 3·4 at 87–90 match Ex. XI-1's p and "dark chord" voicings (PDF 15–16). The mute changes at 82, 87 and 91 are marked where they happen (PDF 50, XI-29).
- Brass economy (PDF 14, 17): no change recommended anywhere else.
