## Findings

The assigned reading supports the score's form at every point where it applies. I found nothing that justifies a change. The findings below record what I checked against each principle, ranked by how much each matters to the form. None is applied. The scratch copy is v303 unchanged.

### 1. Solo statement, then tutti answer: the pattern behind the whole form (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 7-30, 37-60, 65-82.

**Evidence:**
- Adler's first guideline is dialogue (PDF 257): a solo states the tune by itself, "implanting its timbre firmly in the listener's mind", and the tune is then repeated at once in a very different colour.
- The movement does this three times:
  - Horn 1 alone at 7-14, then both violin sections with the cello counter-melody at 23-30.
  - Flute 1 (with the C.a. from 41) at 37-44, then violins at 53-60, building to the tutti.
  - Horn 1's motto tail alone at 67-70, then violins in octaves at 71-82.
- Each answer is fuller and more blended than the solo before it, as in Ex. XVII-1, where Beethoven's tutti answer is the whole orchestra.
- Solo layers carry the foreground in 54 of the 92 bars: 7-22, 31-52, 65-70 and 83-92.
- This proportion and order match the Canzonetta's solo-and-choir plan (see above).

**Recommendation:** none.

### 2. Cut-backs after each tutti (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 64-67, 82-83.

**Evidence:**
- Mozart's practice (PDF 267, Ex. XVII-10, bars 58-60 and 69-70 of the example): when the soloist enters after a tutti, the orchestra is cut back sharply, and a held note in horns and basses carries the return to the tonic.
- After 61-64 (15.8 voices on average), 65-66 holds a pp E♭7 in the strings. From 67, only the basses' E♭ pedal, the timpani roll and two bassoons sound under Hn 1 (3.8 voices on average).
- After 79-82, 83 drops to harp harmonics over a ppp A♭ in cellos and basses.
- Both cut-backs keep a pedal, as Mozart's sustained D does.
- The third quiet passage (31-36) does not follow a tutti. It follows the f peak at 28 and the diminuendo to pp at 30, and the A♭ pedal in Bsn 2 and Cbn carries it.

**Recommendation:** none. These are the reductions c233 recorded under PDF 267. The reading confirms them.

### 3. Background laid before the solo enters, except at 37 (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 3-6, 37, 65-67, 82-83.

**Evidence:**
- Ex. XVII-11 (Sibelius) and Ex. XVII-12 (Bartók) set up the accompaniment before the soloist enters (Bartók: two bars of string figuration and a timpani pulse, PDF 270). Adler says the result is that the solo "is instantly perceived by the listener, since the background does not intrude" (PDF 269).
- The score does this before Hn 1 at 6 (the wind chord from 3), before Hn 1 at 67 (the string chord at 65-66) and before the harp at 83 (the A♭ in the basses from 82).
- At 37, theme B and its viola triplets start on the same downbeat.
- c233 kept this, following Dvořák 9/ii at 46, with the bare fifth at 35-36 and the proportions protected.
- The reading gives no reason that overrides c233. At 35-36 the B.cl and C.a. already sit on a held A♭/D♭ fifth, so the key is set even though the figure is not. The 3/4-to-4/4 change at a double bar makes a fresh start there right for the form.

**Recommendation:** none.

### 4. No subito piano in the score; the sf>p at 63 is the right device (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 62-64.

**Evidence:**
- Adler's subito-piano method (PDF 190, Ex. XV-36) needs two groups: a ff group that stops, and a p group that has already entered under it. The two chords overlap.
- At 63 the score asks for something else: an accent on a new chord (A minor), then a soft background for the trombone quotation.
- That matches the sforzando list on PDF 189-190 (Ex. XV-35), not the subito piano. The score combines:
  - a pizzicato (Cb a,, sf)
  - a short high wind note (Fl 1 A6 sf)
  - long string notes (sf> tremolo to p)
- c300 already made the strings fall away under the trombone in the MIDI.
- An Ex. XV-36 rescoring (for example, horns held ff into 63 b1 while the strings enter p) would change the protected tremolo at 63-64 (c268) and the sf-p chord (c294) for no clear gain.

**Recommendation:** none.

### 5. Every colour change dovetails (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 30-31, 44-45, 47, 52-53, 70-71, 82-83, 89-90.

**Evidence:** PDF 192 says to make sure "the seams don't show". Each join either overlaps or passes the line through a shared note or a pickup:
- **30→31:** Cbn and Bsn 2 hold A♭ across the bar line as the strings fade to pp.
- **44→45:** Ob 1 and the cellos take the pickup on 44 b4 while Fl 1 and the C.a. hold.
- **47:** Ob 1 C♯5 ends on b3; the C.a. enters on C♯4 at b4.
- **52→53:** the C.a. holds F4 through 53 b2 under the violins' pickup at 52 b4.
- **70→71:** the violins' E♭-F pickup sits on b3 of the horn's last bar.
- **82→83:** the cellos and basses tie A♭ into the coda.
- **89-90:** Cl 1 and Vln I share C5, as in Ex. XV-37.

**Recommendation:** none.

### 6. Pitch reference at each solo entry (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 6, 31, 47, 63, 83.

**Evidence:**
- Adler's rule for singers (PDF 275) is to "lead a singer to his first pitch" and give "important points of pitch reference". He compares this with horn players.
- In this score every exposed entry starts on a pitch that is sounding just before it, or is part of the chord it lands on:
  - Hn 1's E♭3 at 6 (Cl 2 E♭3 to 6 b2)
  - B.cl's E♭ at 31 (Hn 4 E♭3 at 29-30)
  - C.a.'s C♯4 at 47 b4 (the strings' fp C♯7 on 47 b1)
  - Tbn 1's A3 at 63 (Vc A2 and Vla in the sf chord)
  - the harp's A♭ at 83 (the basses' A♭)
- This matches c301's note on solo entries.

**Recommendation:** none.

### 7. Top register held back for the climaxes (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 57-63, 71-79.

**Evidence:**
- Bizet adds the upper-octave violins only at the singer's climax (PDF 279).
- In A′, the B♭6 sounds only at 79 (c299), and the A♭6 is saved for 80-81. This matches Ex. XVII-20.
- In the B section, Vln I and Fl 1 reach A♭6 at 58 at f, three bars before the ff at 61. This could look like an early peak.
- But the long-range line A♭6 (58, 61-62), A6 (63), B♭6 (79) was accepted in c110 and recorded in c181. Each step adds a new top pitch, so the climax register still grows.

**Recommendation:** none. Do not move the A♭6 at 58.

### 8. Timpani motive joining the coda's phrases (checked, no change)

**Bars:** 83-86.

**Evidence:**
- In Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273), "the timpani is very important because it plays the motive that joins the two phrases of the melody".
- The coda does the same: the harp's head of theme A (83), the timpani's E♭-C-A♭ (84), then Cl 1's E♭-D (85).
- This also exchanges roles (row 13 of the table): the harp and timpani, background for 82 bars, carry the foreground for three.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep the timpani link at 84-85 (protected since c233).
