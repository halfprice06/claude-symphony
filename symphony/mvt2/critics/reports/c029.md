## Other sources

The comparison score is Beethoven 9/iii (corpus `Beethoven_Sym9_mvt3.ly` and `.pdf`). Its form is a double variation: an Adagio in B♭ (4/4) alternates with an Andante in D and then G (3/4). Each return of the Adagio theme is given to the first violins in faster figuration, first sixteenths and then triplet sixteenths in 12/8. The ff interruptions near the end use trumpets, horns and timpani with no trombones and no build-up, and quiet falls straight after them.

Our score shares its two-tempo, two-theme plan and its outburst-then-calm before the return. Its weakness against Beethoven is in one place. Beethoven's surface rhythm rises a step every time the music comes back or intensifies. Ours stays the same at 53–60, the passage that is supposed to drive forward. That is finding 1.

Against v19, the movement has gained in most places: the ♭VI at 78, contrabassoon weight at 79–81, the pp collapse at 82, the harmonic head at 83 and "molto sostenuto" at 61. One passage is worse. Since c027 removed the Vln II chord, 41–44 has no sustained harmony at all (finding 3).

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 59–60: the animando's accompaniment never speeds up — APPLIED
**Problem.** The viola triplet eighths at 53–60 are note-for-note the figure of 37–44. Over "poco a poco animando" with a crescendo to ff, the whole texture moves in the same note values it had at the quiet first statement. The flutes, oboes and violins grow louder and higher, but the motor does not change.

**Evidence.** Ex. 153 (R-K PDF 325–328): in the accelerando the accompanying figure moves to faster note values. Ex. 168 (PDF 337–338): the violas break into sixteenths at the f. R-K PDF 117–118: a returning idea must have its "details" changed. Beethoven 9/iii raises the figuration on every return of its theme, from eighths to sixteenths to triplet sixteenths.

**Fix** (`sections/bSec.ily`, `bSecVla`, bars 59–60). Same pitches, doubled speed. The last note becomes G♭, which resolves to the F of the 61 double stop instead of doubling Vln I's C–D♭ in octaves:
```
\tuplet 6/4 4 { ges16(\f bes es' bes ges bes es' bes ges bes es' bes) } \tuplet 6/4 4 { bes16( des' es' des' bes des' es' des' bes des' es' des') } |
\tuplet 6/4 4 { ges16(\< c' es' c' ges c' es' c' ges c' es' c') } \tuplet 6/4 4 { ges16( c' es' c' ges c' ges c' es' ges' es' ges') } |
```

### 2. Bars 60–61: the brass enters in one block on the ff — APPLIED
**Problem.** Every brass player except Hn 1 rests until 61 and enters ff on the downbeat. The crescendo of 57–60 is carried by woodwind and strings only, so the brass at 61 sounds added on, not grown into.

**Evidence.** Ex. 168 (R-K PDF 337–338): horns enter p cresc. a bar before the f. R-K PDF 108: stationary parts go to brass, moving parts to woodwind. Bar 60 is a whole-bar A♭7, a stationary chord.

c024 cited PDF 136 ("brass re-entries pp or ff; mp/mf colourless"). A p entry with a hairpin satisfies that, since the horns sound no mp or mf level on its own. The trumpets and trombones keep their ff attack at 61.

**Fix** (`bSecHnThree` / `bSecHnFour`). Both lines get the same dynamics, as the part-combined staff requires. Hn 3 holds E♭4 and moves down to D♭4. It does not double Vln I's C–D♭ leading tone.
```
bSecHnThree = { R1*23 | es'1\p\< | des'1\ff | ...
bSecHnFour  = { R1*23 | aes1\p\<  | aes1\ff  | ...
```

### 3. Bars 41–44: a woodwind melody with no sustained harmony (worse than v19)
**Problem.** The flute and cor anglais play in octaves over viola triplets, pizzicato basses and the harp's LH bass. Nothing holds a note. In v19 the Vln II chord did this. c027 removed it, correctly, because it sat on the cor anglais' own notes. Nothing replaced it, and 41–44 is now thinner than 37–40, where a single flute has the same support. The cor anglais' entry should thicken the passage, not thin it.

**Evidence.** R-K PDF 116: when woodwind has the melody, the harmony goes to horns, in a different colour. Ex. 152 (*Antar*, PDF 324): flute melody pp, figuration, pp horns sustained beneath.

**Fix (not applied; test by ear).** Hn 3·4 pp, `\pc`, sustained under the cor anglais in the viola's lower notes. Use horns, not strings, so that the colour differs from the melody, which is the point of PDF 116:
```
bSecHnThree: R1*4 | des'2\pp des'2 | des'2 es'2 | des'2 des'2 | c'2 des'2 | R1*15 | es'1\p\< | ...
bSecHnFour:  R1*4 | aes2\pp aes2  | bes2 c'2   | bes2 bes2   | a2 bes2   | R1*15 | aes1\p\< | ...
```
This touches c027's unison objection: the horns sit at the cor anglais' floor. If it masks the line, drop Hn 3 and keep Hn 4 alone.

### 4. Bar 63: woodwind sf (open since c028) — recommend closing with no change
R-K PDF 113 allows strings and brass alone when the strings play tremolo or sf, and 63 has both. Ex. 168 has the woodwind drop out with the brass in the diminuendo. A woodwind sf at 63 b1 would blur the trombone's A-minor quotation, which must stand as a single statement (cycle 28). Leave it as it is.

### 5. Bars 61–64 against Beethoven's fanfares — no change; confirms the design
Beethoven's interruptions are two bars long, brass and timpani only, and fall into p at once. Our 61–62 tutti and 63–64 trombone answer have the same length and the same sudden collapse. With fixes 1 and 2, our outburst grows out of the build where Beethoven's comes from nowhere. That difference fits a movement whose quotations are prepared. No action.

### 6. Bars 83–86: harp head in harmonics — watch the balance
v19 had the coda's harp head in ordinary notes; v29 has it in harmonics. Harmonics are about half as loud. Any string sustain louder than pp at 83–84 will cover the theme's last statement. In the MIDI render it was audible, but MIDI cannot judge this. If a reading shows it is lost, mark the strings at 83–84 `\ppp` rather than reverting the harmonics.
