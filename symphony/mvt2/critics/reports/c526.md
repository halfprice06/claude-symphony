## Findings

None of the points below needs a change. The string writing of v526 meets every applicable principle in Adler ch. 3. The three points that come closest to the chapter's limits are recorded here so that the next critic on this reading does not have to work them out again.

### Recorded 1. Viola double stop B♭3/D♭4 at 62 b1–2 (no change)

**Bars.** 61–63, violas "non div., molto sostenuto, trem.", ff to sf.

**What I found.** Three of the four tremolo stops lie in first or half position: D♭4/F4 at 61 (C♯4 third finger on G, F4 second finger on D), A♭3/F♭4 at 62 b3 (half position, first and second fingers), E4/C5 at 63 (D and A strings). B♭3/D♭4 at 62 b1 cannot be played there. D♭4 is below the open D, so it has to be C♯4 on the G string, and B♭3 then has to go on the C string at the 3rd–4th position (third finger on C, low first finger on G). The violas therefore shift up and back inside bar 62.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 76 says that in the orchestra only the most easily accessible double stops are used, and that what is hard for one player is simple divisi. The C-and-G-string table in Ex. III-33 (PDF 88) is a first-position table, and its lower note never goes above the fourth finger, G3.

**Why no change.** This is a minor-third shape (3 on the lower string, 1 on the upper) at a common position. It falls under an ff tremolo, each half note lasts about 2.2 s at ♩=54, and the whole tutti covers it. Any professional section plays it cleanly. "Divisi and double stops 61–64" is on the do-not-touch list (c106, c256). If 61–64 is ever reopened, the only clean alternative is a divided chord for that one beat-pair, `<bes des'>2:32^\markup \small \italic "div."` and then `<aes fes'>2:32^\markup \small \italic "non div."`. The extra words would cost more on the page than the shift costs the players, so I do not recommend it.

### Recorded 2. Vln I B♭6 at 79–80 (no change)

B♭6 is a tone above the 7th-position A6 that Adler (PDF 72) gives as the Classical limit, where "left-hand control is more and more tenuous". In this score it is reached by a slurred stepwise run from C6 at 78 b3 (Ex. III-22 to III-24 concern wide skips, and this is a run). It is ff, molto sostenuto, on a fresh down-bow, and doubled by Fl 1. It is the movement's single peak, and c525 protects it at pitch. Both 19th-century practice and the chapter's range chart (E7 orchestral) allow it.

### Recorded 3. Bass soli and bow length (no change)

Adler PDF 105 says one bass is thin and a section is powerful. PDF 108 says the short bass bow needs "change bow at will" on long notes. Both are applied exactly. The 4 soli are used only on soft held pedals (65–70, 87–92), where a thin sound is wanted and the cellos double at the octave in the coda. Every long Cb note at 2, 61, 65–70, 79 and 82–92 carries the change-bow instruction in the score and the part (parts book PDF 30).

No scratch changes were made, so /tmp/critic-526/work is v526 unchanged. Its build-warnings.txt is empty, analysis.txt shows 0 outer-voice parallels, and the parts book is unchanged at 30 pages.

Already at a master's level and not to be touched: the string colour plan (sul D / sul A / sul G / sul C / Cb sul A with their cancellations), the divisi and double-stop layout at 61–64 and 80–81, and the "4 soli" / "change bow at will" bass and cello pedals at 65–70 and 83–92.
