## Findings

### 1. Bars 83–86: tutti cellos double the tutti basses' A♭ pedal under divided violas at ppp (applied)

**Problem.** At K the cellos re-enter tutti on A♭2 ppp and hold it four bars. The basses hold A♭1 tutti, tied from 82. Above them there are only the harp harmonics (83), the timpani answer (84–85), Cl 1 (85–86) and the divided violas (C4/E♭4, then C4/F4). So the whole cello section and the whole bass section sit under half-sections of violas. c240 already had to lower the basses' MIDI level here because the pedal sounded above the harp harmonics. c254 reduced the basses to 4 soli from 87, but 83–86 was left. The timpani's A♭2 at 84 b3–85 also lands on the cellos' A♭2.

**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 88: "If the harmony in the three upper parts, thus strengthened, is written for divided strings, the 'cellos and basses, playing non divisi will prove a trifle heavy; their tone must therefore be eased, either by marking the parts down or reducing the number of players." The basses are already ppp, so the only way left is fewer players. PDF 81: a change in forces should coincide with a new idea. Bar 87 (the plagal close, new bass F) is that idea, and the cellos must be tutti there because they carry the moving bass F2–D♭3–A♭2 (c254's reason). Ex. 103 and 125 (PDF 282, 300) show Rimsky's own soft Larghetto bass: a few cellos and basses under divided violas, not the full sections. Dvořák 7/ii ends with a single held horn note over a thinned string body (Corno_I_II, last bars).

**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-259/work/sections/coda.ily, `codaVc`):**
```
\set Staff.midiExpression = #0.75 aes,2.~\ppp^\markup \italic "4 soli, change bow at will" | aes,2.~ | aes,2.~ | aes,2.\< |
...
\set Staff.midiExpression = #1.0 f,2.\pp^\markup \italic "tutti" | \once \stemUp des2. | ...
```
Text and MIDI CC11 only; no pitch, rhythm or dynamic changes. The basses keep their protected tie from 82 and their "4 soli" at 87, so from 83 to 86 the cellos thin first, and at 87 the cellos return tutti as the basses thin. Pages 7–8 have room; the texts do not collide. This touches the ending only as text; the coda's pitches, the plagal close and C5 on top are unchanged, so nothing changes for the scherzo thread.

### 2. Bars 75–77: the counter-melody is in three groups in unison at p–mp, against the theme in two violin sections (recorded; optional fix)

**Problem.** From 75 the cello counter-melody (E♭3–F3, A♭3–D♭4, B♭3–D♭4) is doubled in unison by Bsn 1 and Hn 2, at p rising to mp, while the theme is in Vln I and Vln II in octaves at mp. One string group has two winds in unison with it.

**Evidence.** PDF 76: "If several wind instruments play in unison with one group of strings, the latter will be overpowered." PDF 79: unison of the three groups is used "for preference in loud passages or for a heavy piano effect". Rimsky's soft pairings are two-way: cellos + bassoon (Ex. 91, PDF 268) or cellos + horn (PDF 79; Ex. 92, PDF 269). Dvořák 7/ii at A (bar 11) keeps its counter-voice in the cellos alone (pizz. figures) under the violins' theme, and adds winds only at the cadence.

**Why not applied.** The texture is protected (c117 "Adler's three-choir mix", c247 "A′ texture plan"). My answer to that reason: the three-choir mix is right from 77 to 82, where the crescendo leads to f and ff and PDF 79 places it. At 75–76 it is the heavy-piano case. **Optional fix** (untested): Hn 2 enters at 77 instead of 75, `aTwoHnTwo = { R2.*6 | bes2(\p\< des'4)\mp | r2 c'4(\mf\< | ...`. The counter-line then grows Vc (71) → Vc + Bsn 1 (75) → + Hn 2 (77) → + Hn 1 (78). I leave the decision to the composer.

### 3. Bars 10–13: the bassoon pair under the horn solo moves in open fourths and fifths (recorded)

**Problem.** In the harmonic basis under Hn 1, the two bassoons are a fourth apart at 10 (B♭2/E♭3), a fifth at 11 b3 (D♭3/A♭3) and a fifth at 13 b1–2 (E♭3/B♭3, under the horn's G4).

**Evidence.** PDF 90 rule 4: fourths and fifths count as discords and should go to instruments of different colour unless they are to be emphasised. PDF 97 rem. 4: when the bass is a separate part (here the bass clarinet), the wind parts above should form an independent whole so that "intervals of open fourths and fifths will be obviated".

**Why not applied.** Each alternative I tried breaks an earlier fix. At 13, D♭4 in Bsn 1 brings back the D♭4–C4 octave with the horn that c117 removed from the harp. G3 doubles the horn's leading tone. At 11, F3 brings back c252's octaves with the horn. At 10 the fourth is inside a complete root-position E♭ triad with the horn's G3 on top, so it sounds as a close 6/4 shape. Each case lasts two beats or less, at pp. Recorded only.

### 4. Holistic comparison with Dvořák 7/ii: would a conductor take this for a master's work? (recorded)

What matches. Dvořák opens his Poco adagio with the theme in a woodwind solo (Cl 1, bars 1–8) over a bassoon counter-line and pizzicato strings, then gives the theme to the violins at A (11) with a cello counter-voice. This movement does the same kind of thing: the horn solo over bassoons and harp (7–14), then the violins with the cello counter-melody (23–30). Both put a horn melody in piano at the centre (Dvořák at B, 32; here 7–14 and 67–70). Both end on a held soft note after a thinned texture. The scoring follows Rimsky-Korsakov's examples almost everywhere I checked: the octave doublings at 45–46 and 57–60 are the methods of PDF 78 and Ex. 87, the coda's muted horns with violas are Ex. 92, and the climax tremolo is balanced as in Ex. 100.

Where it differs. Dvořák's movement is turbulent. There is a sudden f in winds and horns at 9–10, soft wind chords answering the strings at 22–28 (Ob, Cl, Fag, Hn 3·4: `r4 f--( f--) r`), and five ff points (Violino_I lines 121, 143, 162, 186, 226). This movement has two ff points (61, 79), and its first wind forte is at 59. That is closer to Dvořák 9/ii, which the brief names as the model, and c233 recorded the two ff peaks as different in kind. I do not recommend adding outbursts. A conductor would accept the orchestration as professional. What would mark it as a master's work is the melodic material, which is outside this lens's reading.
