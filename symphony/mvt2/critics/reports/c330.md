## Findings

The chapter supports the cyclic design at every place where it applies. I found nothing that justifies a score change, so none is applied. The findings record what I checked, ranked by how much each matters to the symphony's arc. Finding 2 asks for one line in coherence/mvt2.md.

### 1. The opening follows the concerto slow-movement model Adler names, and that is what keeps theme A's colour fresh after movement I (record, no change)

**Bars:** 1-14.

**Evidence:**
- Adler (PDF 263) says Dvořák and Tchaikovsky open their concerto slow movements with the woodwind choir before the soloist, so "the solo color is therefore very fresh, even after the first movement has been heard". Ex. XVII-7 and XVII-8 show a wind-only opening of eight to ten bars.
- Theme A's colour is not new to the symphony: movement I gives its S head to Hn 1 with the cellos over harp, in A♭, at I 46-52 (mvt1.md). Movement II's theme A is Hn 1 over harp, in A♭, at 7-14.
- The score separates the two in the way Adler describes. Movement I's last sound is strings and Hn 3·4. At 3-6 the strings drop out and a wind chord (B.cl. A♭2, Cl 2 E♭3-F♭3-E♭3, Hn 4 A♭3, Hn 3 C4-D♭4-C4) plays alone for four bars. Hn 1 enters only at 6 b3, and the cellos that sat under the horn in movement I are silent until 15. Hn 2-4 do not play under the solo (PDF 264, Mozart).

**Recommendation:** none. This answers the chapter's one cross-movement risk. Keep 3-6 string-free and the cellos out of 7-14 (c310 recorded the same result from movement I's side).

### 2. Six motto-tail statements, six scorings, no colour competition from the soloist's own family (record; one line for mvt2.md)

**Bars:** 8, 24, 32-34, 67-69, 72, 85-89.

**Evidence:**
- Haydn changes which voice the flute doubles from one phrase to the next (PDF 288, Ex. XVII-24). Mozart keeps the soloist's own family out of the accompaniment (PDF 264 horns, PDF 267 flutes).
- The MIDI shows each statement scored differently, and none of them has its own family accompanying it:
  - 8: Hn 1 alone on top; bassoons, B.cl. and harp below; Hn 2-4 silent.
  - 24: Vln I and II in unison over the cello counter-melody.
  - 32-34: B.cl. E♭3-D3-D♭3-C3, the pitch and instrument of movement I's first motto (I 1-4). The C.a. plays C4-C♭4-B♭3-A♭3 a sixth above. Both clarinets rest (c027).
  - 67-69: Hn 1 E♭4-D4-D♭4-C4-B♭3; Bsn 1 a third below from 68; Hn 2-4 silent.
  - 72: violins in octaves with Fl 1.
  - 85-89: Cl 1 E♭5-D5-D♭5-C5. Cl 2 rests until 89, where it enters niente on E♭4.
- One cyclic link at 32-34 is not written down anywhere. Movement I's motto is stated first by B.cl. and cellos (I 1-4), then by the cor anglais a fifth higher (I 5-9; mvt1.md "Opening"). At II 32-34 the same two instruments play the tail together. The transition therefore brings back both of movement I's opening motto colours at once, with the B.cl. at its movement I pitch.

**Recommendation:** no score change. Composer: in coherence/mvt2.md, row "Motto tail … 32–34", after "Cor anglais a sixth above (C4 | C4 C♭4 B♭3 | A♭3)", add: "the cor anglais is movement I's second motto instrument (I 5–9), so both of movement I's opening motto colours return together". The finale thread may want this, because mvt1.md (its cycle 330) already asks the finale not to add another B.cl. tail at E♭3.

### 3. Registral distance at the cyclic solos: two close placements, both settled (record, no change)

**Bars:** 32-34, 47-50, 63-64, 67-69, 85-89.

**Evidence:** Adler (PDF 274): with a simple accompanying figure, keep the accompaniment registrally distant from the solo, "especially when dealing with woodwind solo instruments", although rhythmic contrast makes this less important. From the MIDI:

| Bars | Cyclic line | Nearest accompaniment | Distance, rhythm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32-34 | B.cl. E♭3-C3 | C.a. C4-A♭3 above | sixth; same rhythm at 33 |
| 47-50 | C.a. C♯4-D5 | Vln II F♯5-A5 above; violas off-beat below | third to sixth above; Vln II moves at 48 b3, 49 b1, b3, b4 |
| 63-64 | Tbn 1 C4-E3 | Vc A2 below, Vla E4 above | fourth to a tenth; sustained tremolo |
| 67-69 | Hn 1 E♭4-B♭3 | Bsn 1 B♭3-G3 | third; different values |
| 85-89 | Cl 1 E♭5-C5 | violas C4-F4, then Vln II A♭4 from 87 | octave, then fourth; all sustained |

- Two lines break the rule.
  - **32-34:** the accompaniment is above the solo and in its rhythm. This is the Fidelio kind of direct doubling (PDF 283), not an accompanying figure. The B.cl. is marked p < mf > mp against the C.a.'s p, and the C.a. lies in its weakest fifth (c276). c111 and c140 set this deliberately, and finding 2 gives it a cyclic reason.
  - **47-50:** Vln II sits above the quotation at pp. Earlier critics recorded this and kept it: c294 cited Adler PDF 169, 184; c324 recorded it again; c263's do-not-touch list includes "48-50 C.a. quotation without Vln II doubling". The violas' off-beat quavers supply the rhythmic contrast Adler says lowers the risk, and the C.a. is p against pp.
- I have no new reason to reopen either.

**Recommendation:** none. If a later lens wants the quotation clearer, the place to test is Vln II at 49 b3-50 b2 (G5-F5-G♭5-A5), the only line moving above the C.a. while it holds D5 and C♯5; the A5 at 49 b1-2 must stay (c202, do-not-touch).

### 4. The timpani link at 84-85 is the symphony's Beethoven device and runs into the scherzo (record, protect)

**Bars:** 83-85; scherzo 9-12.

**Evidence:**
- In Ex. XVII-13 (PDF 273) "the timpani is very important because it plays the motive that joins the two phrases of the melody". In the Beethoven the timpani's motive is the concerto's own opening motive, so the joining figure is also the cyclic one.
- Here the timpani alone play E♭3-C3-A♭2 (4. 8 4) at 84-85. The figure joins the harp's theme A head (83) to Cl 1's last motto tail (85).
- The scherzo's first timpani passage (intro.ily, v321) begins `b,4 b,8 b,8 g,4 | e,4`, the same figure a major third lower, and the line continues into the finale (scherzo.md line 10; mvt1.md cycle 310: I 179-180 → II 84-85 → III 9 → IV 9, 143).
- So the one solo timpani figure in movement II is the link of a chain across all four movements, as in Beethoven.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep the rhythm 4. 8 4, the pitches E♭-C-A♭ and the timpani alone at 84 b1-b2. The scherzo and movement I threads rely on them.

### 5. The two quotations follow Adler's dialogue and recitative principles in opposite ways (record, no change)

**Bars:** 47-50, 63-64.

**Evidence:**
- **S head, 47-50.** Dialogue (PDF 257) fixes a timbre in the listener's mind. Movement I's last statement of its minor S head is the solo cello and cor anglais duet at I 171-174 (mvt1.md). Movement II quotes it in the cor anglais, solo, p. The quotation keeps the colour that movement I last attached to that form of the theme, so the link is heard as a memory.
- **P head, 63-64.** The quotation changes colour completely, from movement I's strings to Tbn 1 ff "marcato". It also cuts across the B section's ff close the way an orchestral interruption cuts across an accompanied recitative (PDF 276, Ex. XVII-17, 18). Orff's rule (PDF 288) is met: the line sits in Tbn 1's strongest range, in the gap between Vc A2 and the violas' E4, with Tbn 2 silent.
- One quotation is a recollection in the same colour and the other an interruption in a new one, which matches their two roles in the cyclic plan (cyclic-notes §3).

**Recommendation:** none. Both are protected.

### 6. The hand-over to the scherzo: pitch reference and a rested horn (record, no change)

**Bars:** 82-92; scherzo 1-3.

**Evidence:**
- Adler (PDF 275) says to "lead a singer to his first pitch", and compares singers with horn players, who place notes by feel.
- The scherzo opens with Hn 1 alone on G♯4 pp after the pause. The last chord of II holds that pitch as A♭4 in Vln II from 87 to the fermata at 92, directly under Vln I's C5, so the last inner pitch heard is the next movement's first.
- PDF 263 asks that the solo colour be fresh. Hn 1's last note in II is C4 at 82 b1-2 (aTwo.ily). Hn 1 has about 50 seconds of rest plus the pause before its scherzo solo, and the only horn sound in between is muted (Hn 3·4, 87-90). The scherzo's open horn is a new colour after the muted echo, and Hn 3·4's "via sord." at 91 gives them the pause to unmute (scherzo "senza sord." at its 73).
- The scherzo's timpani E2 at its bar 3 answers II's last timpani A♭2 (84-85) a major third lower, on the drum the muta at 92 retunes.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep Vln II's A♭4 to the end, Hn 1 out of 83-92, and the muted Hn 3·4 at 87-90.

### 7. The upper octave is held back for one climax, and the coda returns to the register movement I ended in (record, no change)

**Bars:** 58-63, 79-80, 83-92.

**Evidence:**
- Bizet doubles the voice at the upper octave only at its climax (PDF 279). c303 checked this for the form: B♭6 first sounds at 79.
- For the arc between the movements:
  - Movement I ends on C5 at ppp.
  - Movement II goes no higher than A6 before 79 (63, Fl 1 and Vln I).
  - It reaches B♭6 once (79).
  - Its coda ends on the same C5, on the same string (sul D, c256).
  - The scherzo begins two notes lower (G♯4).
- The movement's register opens and closes on movement I's pitch. As in the Carmen aria, the upper octave comes once, at the climax.

**Recommendation:** none. Keep B♭6 only at 79 (c299) and the C5 ending.
