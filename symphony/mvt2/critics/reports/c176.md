## Findings

### 1. Bassoon 1 plays 75 to 79 b3 without a breath, crescendo p to ff (about 17.7 s). Applied.
**Problem.** In A′, bassoon 1 doubles the cellos' counter-melody: `es2( f4 | aes2 des'4 | bes2 des'4) | ces'2 c'4( | des'4 c'4 bes4) \breathe`. Bars 75–77 are at ♩=52, 78 at 50 and 79 at 48. The first comma is after 79 b3. Horn 2, on the same line, rests at 78 b1–2. Bassoon 1 does not rest there, because c134 gave it the C♭4 with the cellos. So the only wind that carries the line into the ff at 79 has no breath before it.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 24 item 11: a slur is played "usually in one breath". The slur ends at 77, which is where the player can breathe. PDF 30, Ex. 33: the comma is written where the breath is wanted. Adler part 1 PDF 175: the long-breath exception is the oboe's alone.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoBsnOne):** `... | bes2 des'4-\tag #'part \mf) \breathe | ces'2-\tag #'part \f c'4( ...`. The cellos keep the line, and the C♭4 at 78 (c134) is unchanged.

### 2. The contrabassoon has no breath at 80, but the bass clarinet and bassoon 2 on the same line do. Applied.
**Problem.** At 79–82, B.cl., Bsn 2 and Cbsn play the same bass line, D♭–D♭ B♭–E♭–A♭. B.cl. and Bsn 2 have `des2 bes,4 \breathe` at 80. The Cbsn has `des,2 bes,,4 | es,2.` with no comma, which is about 14 s at ff in its lowest octave.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 70: a large bore "demands a considerable amount of air ... allow as many breathing spaces as possible". PDF 74: the contrabassoon is "large and unwieldy". PDF 30, Ex. 33: the section breathes together at one comma. This is the same reasoning c086 Finding 2 used for the low brass at 60.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, aTwoCbsn):** `des,2.\ff | des,2 bes,,4 \breathe | es,2.\> | ...`. The breath comes at the same point as the B.cl. and Bsn 2, so no new gap appears in the bass. The basses hold through it.

### 3. At 61–62, flute 2 and oboe 1 slur their tenuto half notes while every other wind tongues them; oboe 1 also slurs the F5 that clarinet 1 ties. Applied.
**Problem.** In the Largamente tutti all the winds change chord together on each half note, with tenuto lines. Ob 2, Cl 2, B.cl., the bassoons, Cbsn, horns, trombones and tuba tongue each note. Fl 2 (`aes''2--( des'''2-- | f'''2-- fes'''2--)`) and Ob 1 (`f''1--( | f''2-- fes''2--)`) are slurred. So at 61 b3 and 62 b1 and b3 two players connect while the rest of the section re-attacks. At 61–62 b2, Ob 1 and Cl 1 play F5 in unison. The clarinet ties the note into 62. The oboe re-articulates it under a slur. The two halves of the unison therefore start 62 differently.
**Evidence.** Sebesky PDF 23: the articulation marks "apply to all wind instruments", and a tenuto line means full value. In Ex. 47, 50 and 51 (PDF 32–36, images), every part that moves with the lead carries the lead's marks. PDF 80: unisons of oboe and clarinet are strong, but they carry more risk of an intonation conflict than octaves, so the two players need to move together.
**Fix (bSec.ily):** Fl 2: `aes''2--\ff des'''2-- | f'''2-- fes'''2-- |`. Ob 1: `\stemUp f''1--~\ff | f''2 fes''2-- |`, tied like Cl 1. Ob 2: `des''1--~\ff | des''2 aes'2-- |`. Without this tie the shared chord stem shows one tie over a two-note chord, which reads as if both notes were tied. Vln I also holds its D♭6 across the bar. No pitch changes. allpairs shows only removals: the Ob 1 octave entries at 62 b1 are gone, and there are no new pairs.

### 5. Horn spacing, register and mutes follow the reading. No change.
- The horns stay within a sixth of each other wherever voice leading allows (PDF 103). The two wider places, 29 b3 (D♭4/E♭3, a seventh resolving) and 81 b3 (octave, leading tone left), were closed at c147 and c122.
- The muted Hn 3·4 at 87–90 (written C4–B♭3) is the low register where Sebesky prefers the straight mute to stopping (PDF 37). The players have about 18 s to put mutes in.
- No horn goes near the pedal range (PDF 33) or above written G♭5 (PDF 32).
- The trombone quotation avoids the awkward position jumps of Ex. 27 (PDF 28). The bass trombone's D2 at 64 is a trigger note (Ex. 28, PDF 29). The tuba's F♭1 at 62 is the bottom of the BB♭ normal range (Ex. 44, PDF 39).

### 6. Flute 2 and oboe 2 play only at the two ff peaks. No change.
Fl 2 plays only 61–62 and 79–80, and Ob 2 likewise. Each first note is a cold ff entry: Fl 2 on A♭5, Ob 2 on D♭5. In both places the note sits inside the full tutti and is doubled by the violins (Vln II A♭4/A♭5 range, Vln I D♭6/F6). Sebesky's concern about exposed entrances (PDF 46) does not apply. Giving them earlier notes would add colours that c124 and c157 deliberately left out.
