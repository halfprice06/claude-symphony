## Findings

### 1. What movement III must do (no score change; relay to the coordinator)

Movement II carries out the cyclic plan. The link at 1–2 matches movement I v220 part by part. Each quotation is heard once, the S-head rhythm occurs only in the C.a. at 47–50, no new theme opens with a rising sixth, there is no tam-tam, chorale or C-major material, the middle section is in D♭, and the close is plagal with D♭–C on top. The changes from c121 to c128 leave all shared material untouched (pitches change only at 25–28, 43, 74–79 and 81, all inside movement II's own material). The reading adds three things to the list for movement III.

**Withheld sounds must be spent once, at the decisive point (Casanelles PDF 227, 245, 247–248).** *Man of Steel* leaves its drums out of the first climax and brings them in only when the hero commits. *Annihilation* saves its synthetic sound for the end. Movement II keeps back the trumpets (tacet throughout; their last sound in the symphony is movement I's accented B♮ at 170), the string tremolo (used only under the trombone quotation at 63–64, as movement I's sound), the C7 violin peak (I reaches A♭6, II reaches B♭6) and the falling major motto in full brass. III should not use these in passing before its arrival in C major. The trumpets' first entry should be either the soft undoubled melody or the B→C.

**Sounds that movement II has already used must not be presented in III as new:** melody in parallel sixths and thirds (32–34, 41–44), muted horns with cor anglais (87–88), harp harmonics (83), a single forte low-brass quotation of the P head (63–64), and a woodwind choir alone (31–36).

**Still owed:**
- The harp and timpani "melodic moments" are three notes each in II (harp A♭–C–E♭ at 83, timpani E♭–C–A♭ at 84). III should give the harp a real line. The D♭ episode's single recall of theme B is a natural place for it, since the harp already carried theme B's triplets at 53–56.
- Hn 3·4 end II muted. Adler PDF 308 requires "senza sord." or "open" at their first entry in III. II's part already prints "via sord." at 91 (c128), which is the placement Adler asks for: "as soon as the mute is to be removed". Keep it.
- Timpani: II ends tuned A♭2, C3, D♭3, E♭3 (muta at bar 5, no later change). III's first timpani label must give the retuning, done in the pause, for example "Timp. in G, C, E♭, G (muta from movement II: A♭→G, D♭→C…)".
- Threads: the E♭/E♮ thread (II's last viola note is E♭3 sul C; close it in III's violas with D♯→E); the stop chord B–D–F–A♭, heard in II at 33 and 64 and never resolved; resolve it once in III with the trumpets' B→C.

**The joint (c120, unchanged):** a pause, then a loud attack in which II's last A♭ chord becomes a German sixth (A♭, C, E♭, F♯) resolving to V of C. III's title block should state "after a pause", where II's says it follows movement I attacca. That keeps the shared template of PDF 212 and 222.

### 2. bSec.ily header and movement-2-plan.md describe an older score (applied in the scratch copy for bSec.ily)

### 3. Correction to the record: the only rising C♭→C is a mixture inflection, not B→C (no score change)

**Problem.** c120 (finding 11, and the relay) says that movement II has no B♮ rising to C, so the trumpets' B→C stays reserved for III. The MIDI scan shows one rising semitone on these pitch classes: cellos C♭4 (78 b1, over F♭) to C4 (78 b3, over E♭). It is spelled C♭, and it is ♭3̂→3̂: the A♭-minor colour of ♭VI turning into the major cadential 6/4. It is not a leading tone resolving to a tonic.

**Evidence.** Casanelles PDF 239: the meaning of a stock device "might have its meaning modified" by its setting. Here the setting is a mixture chord in A♭, not a dominant of C, and the line belongs to the protected counter-melody 75–82. PDF 200: referential meaning depends on what the listener knows. The listener hears this as the counter-melody brightening, and nothing in it sounds like movement I's unresolved B♮ (Hn 1 at 14, Tpt/Hn at 170).

**Fix.** None in the score. For the relay, change the line to: "Movement II has no leading-tone B→C. Its only rising C♭/B→C is Vc 78 (♭3̂→3̂ over the cadential 6/4)."

### 4. The cor anglais as movement I's voice (checked; no change)

The C.a. plays theme B's second phrase at 41–44 and the movement I quotation at 47–50. Casanelles PDF 219 and 248 suggest keeping a palette for each layer, and the C.a. belongs to movement I's layer (motto at I 5–8 and 171–174). I considered moving 41–44 to clarinet 1, as the plan first had it. I rejected that. The line A♭3–B♭4 lies in the clarinet's throat register, which is why c051 chose the C.a.'s strong low register, and Fl/C.a. 41–44 is protected. The quotation is already set apart by other means (PDF 201: structure carried by more than one parameter): two and a half beats of rest before it, a new key (F♯ minor), "poco rit."/"poco sostenuto", the pickup, movement I's rhythm, and a new accompaniment of off-beat violas sotto voce with Vln II moved up an octave.

### 5. Checks that pass (no change)

- **Envelopes at the joint (PDF 206–208):** Hn 4 relieves Hn 3's C4 at bar 1 b2. No part holds a note longer than a player can sustain. The longest hold is Cl 2's morendo E♭4 at 89–92, about 19 s under the rit., inside the 20-second breathing plan from c126.
- **Measured crescendos (PDF 216–217, Fig. 5.7):** 53–61 and 75–79 write their intermediate levels, and both are steepest in the last third (sextuplets and brass at 59–61; f at 78 into ff at 79).
- **Verisimilitude (PDF 215, 239):** nothing depends on a balance that only the rendering can produce. Tbn 1 at 63 plays alone over strings that drop sf>p. The harp harmonics at 83 and the timpani at 84 sound over a ppp pedal with nothing else attacking.
- **Timbre as structure (PDF 201, 204):** each tail statement has its own colour, apart from the reprise (68 = 8 in horn, 72 = 24 in violins). The last one, in clarinet at 85–89, is the only clarinet statement.
