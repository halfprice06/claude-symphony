## Findings

All points are record-only. I propose no change to the score, so I did not build a scratch copy. Every principle in the range is met. The changes since c408 that affect dynamics or colour (bars 54-55 and 84-92) follow the reading. The cello 9-8 at 73-74 changed pitches only; its hairpins are as c498 recorded.

### 1. Bars 22, 57 and 77: mp and mf entries outside the brass (PDF 136, "wider application"; no change)

The re-entry rule is written for horns, trombones and tuba. Rimsky says it "is capable of wider application", so I scanned every other part for entries at mp or mf after a rest:
- 22 b3, Vln I and II tutti, mp, on the pickup to the plain statement, over Vla/Vc/Cb at p. Most of Vln I have rested since 4. This is a melody marked one level above its accompaniment, not a colour entry. The plain statement 23-30 is protected (c247).
- 57 b3, Cl 1·2 mp<. Cl 2 has rested since bar 6. They enter two beats after Fl 1/Ob 1 start p<, but inside the crescendo that runs from 53 to 61. Vln II, which shares the oboe's octave, is already at mf from 56. So the clarinets enter below the strings they sit beside and at about the level the woodwind has reached by b3. This is PDF 130's gradual addition, not a neutral mp. The same reasoning covers Vla mp< (accepted early in the history) and Cb mf at 57 (back after four bars, level with the cellos).
- 77, Fl 1 and Cl 1 mp<. Protected (c124, c246, c248).

In each case the mp or mf joins a crescendo already under way. Rimsky's objection is to an entry that sets a flat, middle colour, and that does not happen here. No change.

### 2. Bar 63: sf-p by case (b) with one wind note, not a wind chord (no change; c408 record)

PDF 129 case (b) puts the short chord in the woodwind and the held chord in tremolando strings. At 63 the strings do play the tremolo chord sf>p and the B.tbn and Cb pizz. reinforce the attack. The woodwind gives only Fl 1's A6, one short sf note. A short Ob/Cl chord on 63 b1 would be closer to the letter of the book. But the woodwind cut-off at 63 clears the space for the Tbn 1 quotation on 63 b1½, which is protected, and the Fl 1 A6 is c070's resolution of the G♯6, a single-voice event. The book itself calls case (b) "less effective" and it is used here for one bar only. Keep it.

### 3. Bars 63-66: the woodwind leaves before the brass (no change)

PDF 130's order for a diminuendo is brass, then woodwind, then strings. After 61-62 the woodwind stops at 63, the brass (Tbn 1, B.tbn) continues through 64, and the strings release at 65-66. The order is reversed because the brass carries the quotation, which the book does not cover. The 81-83 and 87-92 releases follow the book's order exactly, so the movement shows the rule where no quotation interferes. No change.

### 4. Bars 81-82: one-bar diminuendo in all groups, then elimination (no change; c218 record)

In 81 every group makes a ff>p hairpin in the same bar, and the instruments then drop out over 81-83 in brass, woodwind, string order. PDF 130 asks for natural diminuendo "combined with" elimination for a long diminuendo, and that is what happens here. The hairpins all end at p on 82 b1, and the woodwind and violin hairpins in 82 end at pp or niente before their rests, so no hairpin is left without a target. No change.

### 5. Bars 57-60: the printed crescendo pauses at 58 b4 (no change; c198 record)

Fl 1, Ob 1 and the violins have p< from 57, a short > at 58 b4 into the breath, then f at 59 and < into ff at 61. In print the first crescendo has no stated peak (the mf and mp at 58 b4 are hidden, MIDI only). A player reads it as "grow, taper into the breath, f". PDF 130 describes a long crescendo built by adding instruments; here the addition continues across the taper (woodwind at 57, brass at 60), so the build is still continuous in scoring even though it dips in dynamics. c198 chose this shape for the 4-3 suspension at 58 b4 (A&S PDF 348). It is consistent with the reading. No change.

### 6. Bars 53-55 and 84-92: post-c408 changes checked against the reading (no change)

Bsn 1's pp<p at 54-55 (c478) is a pp re-entry (PDF 136) one level below Hn 1's pp<mp, the harmonic basis kept quieter than the line (PDF 133). The coda mutes (c468: violins and violas con sord., cellos and basses open) give the closing bars the "decrease in resonance" PDF 129 asks of an echo, under the C.a./Hn 3·4 echo of 61-62 at 87-88. Cl 2's morendo at 91 into ppp at 92 is PDF 130's clarinet diminuendo "to a breath". All follow.
