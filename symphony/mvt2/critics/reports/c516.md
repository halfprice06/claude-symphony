## Findings

The string writing follows Adler ch. 2 in every bar I checked. I found nothing that a player would stop to question, so I recommend no change. The findings below are ranked by how close each comes to a real problem, and each says why it holds.

### 1. Non-divided tremolo double-stops at 61–64 (follows; no change)

**Bars.** bSec.ily: bSecVla 61–64 `<des' f'>1:32\ff ... non div.` | `<bes des'>2:32 <aes fes'>2:32` | `<e' c''>1:32` | `<e' c''>2:32\p`; bSecVlnTwo 63–64 `<e'' c'''>1:32 ... non div., trem.`.

**Check.** Adler PDF 29–30: a double-stop must lie on adjacent strings; both notes may be stopped; triple-stops need f or mf. Every pair here is two notes on adjacent strings. Viola: D♭4/F4 on G and D strings, first position (fingers 4 and 2, a third shape); A♭3/F♭4 on G and D, half position; E4/C5 on D and A. Violins II: E5/C6 on A and E, third position (fingers 2 and 3). The one awkward pair is the viola's B♭3/D♭4 at 62 b1–2. D♭4 lies below the open D, so both notes cannot be on the G string, and B♭3 must be taken on the C string in third position (4th finger on C, 2nd finger on G). That is a shift up and back inside a two-beat ff tremolo, which is routine. The marking "non div." is what Adler PDF 30 recommends when every player is to play both notes, and it keeps the climax chord full. At 64 the pairs are double-stops at p, not triple-stops, so the f/mf rule does not apply; Dvořák writes undivided tremolo pairs at pp in the Largo (strings.notesL.ily 186–187).

**Fix.** None. If the composer wants the viola to avoid the C-string shift, the only change needed is at 62 b1–2: `<bes des'>2:32 <aes fes'>2:32` → `<bes des'>2:32^\markup \small \italic "div." <aes fes'>2:32^\markup \small \italic "non div."`. I do not recommend it: the change of label costs more attention than the shift.

### 2. Mute changes are all timed (follows; no change)

**Check.** Adler PDF 60: "Enough time must be allowed to put on or take off the mutes ... otherwise, chaos may result." From the `\mt` steps:
- "mettere sord." at 4 (Vln I except the soloist, Vln II, Vla, Vc): first muted entries at 15 and 22 b3, about 38 s later.
- Solo violinist: last note 22 b2, rejoins muted at 25 ("solo player: mute on, join at bar 25" in the part): seven beats at ♩=52, about 8 s.
- "via sord." at 31: strings rest 31–36, about 22 s, before "senza sord." at 37 (Vln II), 41 (Vc), 52 b4 (Vln I).
- Violas "mettere sord." at 82 b3 (score) / 83 (part), muted entry at 84 b3: one beat at ♩=44, 83 at ♩=52, two beats of 84, about 7 s. This is the shortest change in the movement. It is enough for a clip-on or a wire mute, and the violas have no note in between.
- Vln II mute at 83, entry 87 (about 14 s); Vln I mute at 83, entry 89 (about 21 s).
- "via sord." at the final bar for the scherzo: the change happens in the pause between movements.

**Fix.** None.

**Fix.** None.

### 4. Open strings avoided on held expressive notes (follows; no change)

**Check.** Adler PDF 33: an open string cannot take a fingered vibrato. I listed every held note that falls on an open-string pitch. Each one is assigned to a stopped string by a string name: violas G3 at 64 b3–66 "sul C"; cellos D3 at 52 under "sul G" (51–52); Vln II E4 at 20 (E5 is the open string, so it is stopped on D anyway); Vln I C5 "sul D" at 1–3 and 89–92. The only open strings in sustained use are the basses' D2 and A1 at 64, after the tremolo, at p with a diminuendo, where the open ring helps. Adler PDF 64 also asks for a string name only where the pitch could be played on more than one string. Vln II "sul G" at 64 b3 starts on B3, which can only be on the G string, but the marking is needed for the D4 that follows and the D♭4 at 65.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Divisi, unisono, "half" and tutti words in score and parts (follows; no change)

**Check.** Adler PDF 30: if "div." is missing the player may play the chord as a double-stop; "unis." must appear when single notes return. PDF 32: "half" for part of the section, and "all/tutti" when everyone returns. Every chord in the string staves is under a "div." or "non div." word, and the single notes of a divided passage either carry "unis." or are drawn as a unison with two heads (`\unisHead`, Vla 25–26, 28; c335). "4 soli" and "tutti" are used for part-sections, and each "4 soli" that is followed by more music returns with "tutti" (Cb 71, Vc 87). The v516 parts book prints the same words in the Violin I, Violin II, Viola, Violoncello and Double bass parts: "div." at Vla 23, Vc 18 and 21, Vln II 37, Vln I 62; "non div." at Vla 61 and Vln II 63; "unis." at Vln I 64, Vln II 45, Vla 81. No divided chord has more than two notes, so Ex. II-12 and "div. a3" do not arise.

**Fix.** None.

### 6. Pizzicato and arco (follows; no change)

**Check.** Adler PDF 54 and PDF 57–58. Every switch is timed: Vla 40 b3 is marked up-bow before the pizz. at 41, and its arco at 45 follows two beats' rest. Cb 37 starts pizz. after the six-bar rest of 31–36, and its arco at 45 follows three beats' rest. Cb 63 has no rest, but the half note before it is marked up-bow, which is Adler's remedy, and the return to arco at 64 comes three beats later at ♩=54. The violas' pizzicato triplets (41–44) last four bars at ♩=60, about 12 notes a bar, which is short enough to avoid the fatigue Adler warns of on PDF 57. No chord is plucked, so the roll direction of Ex. II-59 does not arise. c508 recorded the same timings; nothing has changed since.

**Fix.** None.

### 7. No string harmonics (not applicable; do not add)

**Check.** Adler PDF 62–68 sets out secure natural harmonics (partials 2–6, the string named) and artificial harmonics (a fourth above a stopped note, not on the bass). The movement has none in the strings. Its quietest string sounds, the coda's muted violins and violas, are notes held over the cadence, and the C5 in Vln I is the common tone with movement I. Natural harmonics on the D string would remove the vibrato that holds that note, and the harp already supplies the harmonic colour at 83. I recommend adding none.
