In the local corpus I read the strings of Dvořák 9/ii (mutopia/DvorakA_O95/Sym9/Sym9-lys/strings.notesL.ily). In the Largo's closing passage the theme passes from single solo strings ("1 violin", "1 viola", "1 cello") to the tutti sections (line 124 Vln I, line 377 violas, a line in octaves, line 500 cellos), with no woodwind on the tune. This is the restraint of 23-30 and 71-76 here.

On the lens question. Theme A is the tune a listener takes away. It is heard in three registers, an octave apart each time (horn A♭3 at 7, violins A♭4 at 23, violins A♭5/A♭4 at 71), and each time with more doubling. Its tail is the motto. Theme B's second phrase, with the rising fourth A♭-D♭, is its memorable part. It is heard twice in full: soft in the cor anglais at 41, then loud in octaves at 57-60. This is the same order of doubling that Rimsky-Korsakov describes for Snegourotchka (PDF 61). The reading gives no reason to change either theme or any of the string doublings. All my findings are decisions to keep the score as it is. Each one is stated so that later critics do not reopen it on this chapter's grounds.

## Findings

### 1. Bars 79-82: keep c187's cor anglais on the violas' octave (no change)

**Question.** At the second ff peak the theme is in three lines: Vln I + Fl 1 on B♭6, Vln II + Ob 1 + Cl 1 on B♭5, and Vla + C.a. on B♭4. c187 justified the cor anglais from the combined-groups pages (PDF 76-78). Is it right by the string pages?

**Evidence.** PDF 62 (a), with the diagram checked on the page image, gives Vln I / Vln II / Violas as the first double-octave layout "for full cantabile melodies extremely tense in character, and in forte passages for choice". 79-82 is exactly this layout, at ff, on the movement's most intense phrase. PDF 63's note says the unequal numbers do not matter much, because the octaves support each other's partials. So the strings alone would be acceptable. PDF 55 adds that melodies given to the violas "are generally doubled by other strings or by the wood-wind". The cor anglais is the woodwind that joins the violas most naturally (Ex. 15, PDF 57). Ex. 15 and Ex. 17 (PDF 57-58, image PDF 191) also show what it does to the sound: it "makes no essential difference to the compound tone" and is "absorbed in the musical texture". So it makes the weakest octave firmer without adding a new colour at the top of the climax. Ex. 32 (PDF 210-211, Antar Adagio, also in D♭) keeps its winds on repeated harmony and puts no wind on the tune. That is the alternative, and it rests on the cellos joining the violas' octave there (Vla+Vc). Here the cellos are on the protected counter-melody. The cor anglais takes their place as a filler for the viola octave, so it is the better choice for this score. Its B♭4-E♭4 (written F5-B♭4) is in its middle register. It leaves the theme with the violas at 81 b2 and releases with them at 82 b3, so it adds nothing to the counter-melody (c074) or to the harp pickup (c063).

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 2. Bars 23-30: theme A in Vln I+II unison without woodwind (no change)

**Question.** PDF 57 says the violin unison "is usually attended by doubling of the melody in some departments of the wood-wind". 23-30 has none, and the line reaches f at 28.

**Evidence.** Ex. 12 (PDF 186), the example Rimsky-Korsakov gives for this layout, has Vln I+II in unison sul D, then sul A, with no wind at all. The 200 dpi image shows its line in the B4-E5 range over pp violas, divided cellos and basses. 23-26 here has the same layout: sul D, A♭4-E♭5, over p violas, the cello counter-melody and basses. PDF 61 also gives the plan that the score follows in its repeats: the melody first undoubled, then doubled in the woodwind when it returns louder (Snegourotchka [58], [60], [65], [68]). Theme A gains winds only at 77 (Fl 1, Cl 1) and 79 (Ob 1, C.a.), as c067/c079 set up. Adding a wind at 23-30 would remove the step from the second statement to the third. Dvořák 9/ii keeps its string statements of the Largo theme free of woodwind in the same way (strings.notesL.ily lines 122-124, 375-377).

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 3. Bars 53-60: theme B unison, then octaves (no change)

**Evidence.** 53-56: Vln I+II in unison sul A, F5-G♭5 range, p < mf, with the harp triplets and the Cl 1/Hn 1 counter-line below. This is PDF 55 ("both in unison, fuller resonance") and PDF 57 (Ex. 12 moves to the A string as its line rises; so does this one). At 57 the line rises to A♭6, and the sections split into octaves ("ord.") with Fl 1 on the upper octave and Ob 1 on the lower. This is PDF 58-59: Vln I and II in octaves for the very high register, since a high line "become[s] too isolated from the rest of the ensemble unless doubled in octaves". The change from unison to octaves comes at the point where the register calls for it.

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 4. Bars 44-47 and 61-62: the two cello doublings of theme B (no change)

**Evidence.** 44 b4-47: oboe 1 has theme B's B♭-minor head, the cellos double it an octave lower on the D string (tenor clef, "sul D"), and bassoon 1 doubles the cellos (c087). PDF 56 cites Antar [63]: "the same melody in D♭ maj. on the D string (doubled by the bassoons)". This passage uses the same string, the same doubling instrument and the same key area. 61-62: the augmented head A♭-D♭-F-F♭ is in Vln II and Vc in octaves, and Vln I holds D♭6 above. PDF 60-61 (e) calls violins-and-cellos octaves the layout for "very expressive passages" and "more resonant" than violas and cellos. Ex. 30 (PDF 207-208) shows the same separation of Vln I from a Vln II + cello line. Both passages are protected; the reading supports them.

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 5. Bars 27-28: unison up to B♭5 against PDF 62 Note I (no change)

**Question.** Note I says melodies "exceeding the middle of the 5th octave are generally doubled an octave below". The book does not say how the octaves are counted. For the low limit ("below the middle of the 1st octave") to mean the extreme low register, the count must start at C1. The upper limit is then about F♯5, and the unison at 27-28 (F5 tied, B♭5, A♭5, at f) goes past it for about two beats.

**Evidence.** The word is "generally", and the note is about melodies "lying in" the extreme register, not touching it at a peak. The only melodies that stay above G5 are 57-60 and 71-82, and both are in octaves. At 23-30 the unison against the octaves at 71 is the main difference between the second and third statements of theme A (c101, "violin unison/octave plan", protected). Doubling 27-30 an octave lower would remove that difference to correct a two-beat peak.

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.

### 6. Divided strings (no change)

**Evidence.** PDF 62 Note II rules out progressions in octaves within one divided section of violas, cellos or basses. Viola div. 23-30: no two halves an octave apart. Viola div. 84-86: C4-E♭4, C4-F4. Cello div. 18: C4 over a held C3 for one beat, then away, so no progression. Cello div. 22: no octave. Vln I div. 62 b3-63 is a third and a fourth, and violins are exempt anyway. PDF 59 (b) (violin divisi in octaves) does not arise.

**Decision.** Keep. Fix: none.
