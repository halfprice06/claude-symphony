## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 31–37: the bass clarinet's solo is in the wrong register and an octave above movement I's pitch (applied)

**Problem.** Movement I's critics asked for one real bass-clarinet solo (cyclic-notes, critics 54 and 74). Bars 31–36 are that solo, and they are also the tail statement that leads from A into B. The solo sounds E♭3–C4–E♭4–D4–D♭4–C4–B♭3–A♭3, written F4–F5. The whole line lies in the clarino register. Movement I opens with the motto in the bass clarinet and cellos at E♭3–D3–C3–D♭3–C3 (bars 2–3), and its coda gives the bass clarinet the same pitches again (175–179). Movement II states the tail in the same instrument an octave higher, so the symphony's opening colour does not come back.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 188: the orchestrator decides on the tune's register first and then picks the instrument "befitting the emotional as well as the tonal intent". If the register changes, the choice changes too. Adler part 1 PDF 217: the bass clarinet's chalumeau is "the most distinctive, the warmest, and the most effective sound", and "as it goes up, it loses some of this quality". Adler PDF 19: colour clarifies content. One octave lower, the solo lies entirely in the chalumeau (written F3–F4), and the tail E♭3–D3–D♭3–C3 falls on movement I's own pitches in movement I's own instrument.

**Answer to the protection (c123, c129: woodwind choir 31–36).** The change keeps everything those cycles protected. The passage stays woodwinds alone on the A♭ pedal. The common-tone diminished seventh at 33 b2 is complete (C.a. B3, B.cl. D3, Bsn 1 F3, A♭ pedal). The cor anglais stays a sixth above the solo in normal order (c111). The C.a. pickup and F5 at 36–37 (c139) are unchanged. To keep the sixth, the C.a. moves down with the solo, to C4–A♭3, written G4–E♭4, the warm low part of its range (Adler part 1 PDF 205). Bassoon 1's A♭3 at 32 would have sat between the C.a. and the solo, so it now enters at 33 b2 on the diminished seventh's F (a dissonant entry, PDF 187); at 32 C.a., B.cl. and Bsn 2 complete the chord. The solo ends on D♭3 at 37 (before, it doubled Vln II's D♭4).

### 2. What movement III must do (relay; no score change)

Movement II still carries out the plan. Each quotation is heard once. The S-head rhythm occurs only at 48. No theme opens with a rising sixth. There is no tam-tam, no trombone chorale and no C-major material. The middle section is in D♭, and the close is plagal with D♭–C on top. The reading adds the following to the list for movement III.
- **Trumpets with timpani at the C-major arrival.** Adler PDF 21: in the Classical orchestra the timpani played "invariably" with the trumpets. Movement II has separated the two (trumpets tacet, timpani melodic at 84). When III brings back the trumpets for the arrival, coupling them with the timpani on C and G gives the arrival a sound from the symphony's past that movement II never used. The trumpets' earlier soft entry (the owed melody, or B→C) should come without the timpani.
- **The owed soft trumpet melody.** Adler PDF 187 gives a soft, high lyrical line to muted brass, and PDF 188–189 list the muted trumpet as an affinity doubling for the oboe and the C.a. Keep the first statement undoubled (c190). For a second statement, a muted trumpet at pitch with the C.a. would join movement I's motto voice to the instrument the symphony has held back.
- **Heterophonic doubling (PDF 189, Ex. XV-34)** is not used in movement II. It can be a new colour for III's C-major statement of the S head in the violins.
- **The D♭ episode.** Movement II's 61 is already a ff D♭-major tutti, without trumpets, and since c133 its bass goes down to the contrabassoon's D♭1. III's forte D♭ must not sound like a second copy of it. Give it the trumpets, and resolve it D♭ → cadential 6/4 → V7 → I with B→C (cycle 180).
- **The bass clarinet.** After finding 1, movement II has restated the tail at movement I's pitch in the B.cl. III should not do this a third time. A B.cl. line in III should use new material.
- The items owed from c120 and c130 are unchanged.

### 3. The attacca join in the parts: Hn 3 cannot turn the page (relay of the c136 item; comment fix applied)

**Problem.** Movement I's Hn 3 holds C4 ppp across the attacca and continues it in movement II's bar 1. Movement I's Hn 3 part is one page, printed as page 1, a right-hand page. Movement II's Hn 3 part also starts on a right-hand page 1 (c135). In a folder the player must therefore turn the page while holding the note, and with one player on the part nobody else can turn it. The four string parts have the same join (three pages in movement I, ending on a right-hand page), though there the inside player can turn.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 22: every note and timbre is part of the composition. The common-tone link depends on these five notes continuing unbroken (do-not-touch since v01). PDF 191, Ex. XV-37: a held note passed on with overlaps. That works only if the player can keep holding the note.

**Fix (movement I side, for the coordinator):** print movement I's Hn 3 part so that it ends on a left-hand page (for example, a blank or title page 1 and the music on page 2), facing movement II's page 1. Do the same for the four string parts, or print movement II bars 1–3 for those parts as a lead-in at the foot of movement I's last page. Nothing needs to change in movement II's parts book. **Applied:** the intro.ily comment said Hn 3 holds C4 "since movement I bar 180 … about 18 s". Since movement I's cycle 140 it enters at 181, so the hold is about 13 s before bar 1. The comment is updated, and Hn 4's relief on bar 1 b2 is kept.

### 4. Bar 37: the shared F5 from c139 puts the cor anglais at the top of its range (checked; keep)

c139 made the C.a. pickup continue to F5 in unison with flute 1. Written, this is C6, in the range Adler marks "thin, pinched" (part 1 PDF 205, Ex. VII-47). Flute with C.a. is not one of Adler's affinity pairs (PDF 188–189), and he warns that an at-pitch doubling changes the basic colour. The clean dovetail would follow PDF 192, Ex. XV-40: flute 1 takes the C5 pickup over the bass clarinet's C on the same beat, and the C.a. rests until 41. I tested this, and it makes 9 pages (it adds the flute staff to the 31–36 system), so it cannot be used. Between the two remaining versions, c139's shared note is the better one. It is one soft quarter under the flute's first note, and it removes the seam c128 had to accept. Keep it.
