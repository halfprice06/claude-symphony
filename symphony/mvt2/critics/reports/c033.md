## What else I studied

**Dvořák 9/ii in the local corpus.** I tabulated which parts play and their dynamics in each measure from 84 to 106 (`reference-corpus/musicxml-converted/xml/Dvorak_Sym9_mvt2.musicxml`, MCTL measure numbers):
- At 91 the woodwinds enter p and grow to mf by 94.
- The strings, already playing, go p (93), mf (94), f (95), ff (96). The trumpets join f at 95, and all the brass are ff at 96.

This is Rimsky-Korsakov's PDF 127/130 procedure, and it is the model the movement names.

## Findings

### 1. Bar 57: the woodwinds enter the main build at the level the strings have already reached (applied)

**Bars:** 57–59.

**Problem:** at 57, the octave leap of theme B's last statement, Fl 1 and Ob 1 enter mf after 12 and 4 bars of rest, and the bass clarinet enters mp. The violins and cellos are already mf there. The new colours start at the old level and rise together with the strings to f at 59. So the second half of the movement's main crescendo gains bodies but not slope. The build's tension curve flattens at the point where it should steepen.

**Evidence:**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127 ("Amplification and elimination of tone qualities"): "While the first group effects the crescendo gradually, the second group enters piano or pianissimo, and attains its crescendo more rapidly. The whole process is thereby rendered more tense as the timbre changes."
- PDF 136: re-entries at mp/mf produce "a colourless and common-place effect". The remark is "capable of wider application".
- PDF 130: strings, then woodwind, then brass. The order is already right here; only the entry level is wrong.
- Dvořák 9/ii: the woodwinds enter p under a string crescendo (MCTL 91) and catch up by 94.

**Fix (applied):** `bSec.ily`. Fl 1 `\stemDown aes''4(\p\<^\markup \bold "1." ...`, Ob 1 `\stemDown aes'4(\p\<^\markup \bold "1." ...` and B.cl. `f2\p\< ges2 | ...`. The f at 59 stays in all three.

I left Cl 2 (mp) and Bsn 1 (mp) unchanged because their staff partners Cl 1 and Bsn 2 are already playing at mp, and shared staves need equal simultaneous dynamics. The composer may move all four to p at 57 if he accepts a small dip for Cl 1/Bsn 2.

### 2. Bars 75 and 78: the A′ build repeats the same flat entries (applied)

**Bars:** 75–79.

**Problem:**
- At 75 Fl 1 joins Vln I's melody at mp, the same level as the violins.
- At 78 b3 horns 1·2 re-enter f on the pickup to the ff peak, after seven bars' rest for horn 1 and a two-beat rest for horn 2. This is the horns' own return to the theme's counter-melody.
- PDF 136 calls an f re-entry "less successful". PDF 130 says the brass are the group "most facile in producing dynamic shades, glorifying crescendo chords into the most brilliant sforzando climaxes". A one-beat p< from the horns gives the arrival at 79 the brass swell that the A′ peak otherwise lacks, since it has no trumpets or trombones (a recorded choice; this keeps it).

**Evidence:** Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 127, 130 and 136, as in finding 1.

**Fix (applied):** `aTwo.ily`:
- Fl 1 at 75: `aes''4.(\p\< c'''8 f'''4~`.
- Hn 1 and Hn 2 at 78: `r2 c'4(\p\<` (equal dynamics on the shared staff), with ff at 79 unchanged.

Bsn 1 keeps f at 78 b3 because its staff partner Bsn 2 is f at 78.

### 3. Bar 82: every group fades together; the brass should leave first (applied)

**Bars:** 81–83.

**Problem:**
- Horns 3·4 and flute 2 leave at 81–82 (c024). After that, horns 1·2, all woodwinds, timpani and strings make the same p > pp in 82 and stop together at the barline. Only violas, cellos and basses continue into 83.
- The harp harmonics (pickup to the coda, 82 b3) enter under a horn C4 that is still sounding. Their balance is an open point since c029.

**Evidence:**
- Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 130: "Diminuendo effects are accomplished by the elimination of the instruments in the reverse order (brass, wood-wind, strings)."
- PDF 127: the diminuendo is "the transition from a complex to a simple timbre by the suppression of one of the groups".
- Dvořák 9/ii MCTL 98–101: trombones out first, then trumpets to pp, woodwind out at 101, strings last.

**Fix (applied):** `aTwo.ily`, Hn 1 and Hn 2 at 82: `c'2)\p\> r4\!` (Hn 1 keeps `\partCombineAutomatic` after it). The woodwinds hold to the barline, and the violins stop at 83 while the lower strings continue. The order across 81–83 then becomes:
1. Hn 3·4 (81)
2. Hn 1·2 (82 b3)
3. woodwinds and violins (83)
4. violas, cellos and basses (continue)

The chord keeps its third in Cl 1 (C5) and the cellos (C4). This is related to c029's open "harp harmonics balance 82–86" point but does not close it.

### 4. Bar 53 b4: horn 1's return after 38 bars of rest is p (suggestion, not applied)

**Bars:** 53–55.

**Problem:** horn 1 has not played since 14. It re-enters on the counter-line at p together with Cl 1, under violins that are also p. By PDF 136 a horn re-entry after a long rest should be pp or ff. The start of the main build is the place where a pp entry and a faster crescendo (PDF 127) would make the horn's arrival at mp at 55 audible as growth.

**Fix:** `bSec.ily`, Hn 1 and Cl 1 at 53: `r2 r4 aes8(\pp ...`, with the existing `\<` moved onto the first note of 54 and mp at 55 unchanged.

I did not apply this because it weakens an inner line under two sections of violins. The composer should judge it against the c019 balance note.

### 5. Bar 79: timpani re-enter f in an ff tutti (suggestion, not applied)

PDF 136 includes the kettle-drums in its order of economy. Its pp/ff rule for re-entries "is capable of wider application". At 79 the timpani return after eight bars on a D♭ roll marked f, while every other part is ff. Marking a roll one level below the tutti is a common balance practice, so this is minor. If the composer wants the peak to sound as a single arrival, use `des2.:32\ff` at 79 with the existing `\>` at 81.

### 6. Bars 30–32: the transition's harmonic basis (suggestion, not applied)

Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 116: when the phrase is in the woodwind, "the harmony should be entrusted to the horns". PDF 121: woodwind tutti is formed "more frequently with the support of horns". PDF 126: hand a part over on a common note.

Horns 3·4 already hold A♭3/E♭3 pp at 30. If they tie these over into 31–32 (`aes2.~ | aes2.~ | aes2\> r4\!` and `es2.~ | es2.~ | es2\> r4\!`), the bass clarinet's entry gets a basis of a different colour. A to transition is then joined by a common chord, and the double-reed choir still takes over at 32–33 with the diminished seventh at 33 untouched.

This partly qualifies c020's "woodwinds alone" for two bars. It adds no V7 of D♭, which was c020's reason, so I offer it only as an option.
