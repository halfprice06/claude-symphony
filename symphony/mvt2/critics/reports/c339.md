## Findings (ranked)

1. **Holistic verdict: the movement stands up to a comparison with Mahler 4/iii. No change.** Bars 1-92. Mahler's movement rests on four procedures. (a) A long soft opening in one choir before the full orchestra is heard. (b) A plaintive minor second idea in the oboe at a slower tempo. (c) One full-orchestra eruption held back until late. (d) An ending that dissolves on the dominant of the next movement's key, with the timpani taking up the bass motif. This score does each of these in proportion to its 92 bars. (a) Bars 3-14 are string-free winds and horn, and full strings come only at 23. (b) The F♯-minor oboe quotation at 47-50 is marked poco rit. / poco sostenuto and is the B section's minor episode. (c) The tutti is withheld until 57-61; trumpets are tacet throughout and trombones play only at 60-64, a restraint like Mahler's (his Fourth has no trombones). (d) Timpani E♭-C-A♭ at 84-85 under the harp's harmonics, then a morendo into the scherzo's G♯ by common tone (c329, c330). Casanelles PDF 271-273 explains why the soft passages work. Each new colour (harp harmonics 83, Hn 1 over the timpani rim roll 67) enters over a still pedal after the previous layer has stopped, as the trumpets do over the string A in Fig. 6.1. The colour carries the meaning: distance (mute, rim, harmonic), not only a lower dynamic. A conductor who knows Mahler 4/iii would find these procedures handled competently. Fix: none.

2. **Theme A's reprise varies the setting, not the line (71-78), where Mahler varies the line itself. Recorded, not re-raised.** Mahler's returns of the G-major theme are melodic variations: figuration, new metre, faster tempo. Here the melody at 71-77 is 23-29 an octave higher, with the viola eighths, harp, and the cello and horn counter-line as the variation. By Casanelles PDF 276 the octave move is a real change of spectrum, so the reprise does sound different. The melody is protected (theme A, c013; A′ texture 71-82), and c319 declined a turn at 72. Mahler's case is a variation movement by genre, and this movement is ABA′ by plan, so the comparison does not answer c319's reason. Fix: none.

3. **One climax against Mahler's one; this score has two ff peaks (61 and 79). No change.** Mahler saves fff for a single sudden coda chord. Here 61-64 (Largamente, ff) and 79 (ff, B♭6) are both full tuttis. Their colours differ as Casanelles PDF 275 asks. At 61 the brass are graded below the winds (Hn 3·4, Tbn f under woodwind ff), because loud brass add dissonant upper partials. At 79 there are no trombones and the flutes and Vln I take the top. The second peak is therefore brighter and lighter, not just a repeat. The plan was settled at c303 and c333 (deceptive cadences lead to both peaks). Fix: none.

4. **Soft entries over pedals (3, 65-67, 83-85) follow Fig. 6.1. No change.** At 3 the woodwind pp enters while the strings' ppp-pp chord dies out (hairpins to niente at 3). At 65-67 the strings fade to the basses' pp pedal before Hn 1's p entry over the timpani's ppp rim roll. At 83-85 the harp harmonics (p) sound over Vc/Cb ppp, then Cl 1 enters from niente. In every case the pedal is marked below the entering line and the earlier layer has cleared, which is the order in Fig. 6.1 (PDF 272: woodwinds pp cut off, strings hold, trumpets enter). Fix: none.

5. **Printed dynamics state the heard result (Casanelles PDF 271, 280). No change.** Spot checks: C.a. solo p over viola pizz. pp and Fl 1 pp at 41; Ob 1 p over Vc pp at 44-45 (cellos double the oboe as colour, PDF 277-278); harp pp-p under the Hn 1/Cl 1 counter-line at 53-56 (c241); Hn 1 p<mp over Cb pp at 67-68. Mahler's habit of marking each line to its heard weight is followed. The hidden MIDI levels from c338 move score.midi towards the printed result: one level above the printed entry dynamic at 23 (mp to hidden mf) and 71 (p to hidden mp), resolution one level lower. They do not contradict the print. Fix: none.

Neither wind is exposed below the violins in its weak low register (PDF 275). Fix: none.
