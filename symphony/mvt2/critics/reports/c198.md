## Findings

### 1. Bar 58: the 4–3 suspension sits inside a crescendo, so its resolution is the loudest note of the figure (applied)

**Bars and parts.** 58, the theme B melody in Fl 1 (G♭6), Ob 1 (G♭5), Vln I (G♭6) and Vln II (G♭5).

**Problem.** Since c151 the melody holds G♭ from b2½ into b4. There it is the 4th over the D♭ bass, and it resolves to F on b4½. The figure is the same as bar 42's (A&S PDF 357–358, Ex. 21-23a: the 7th of V7 held into I as a 4–3). At 42 the score follows A&S PDF 348: the cor anglais holds mp through the bar and the diminuendo starts on the suspension (c098). At 58 the hairpin that starts at 57 b1 runs on to the f of 59. The suspension is not the peak, and its resolution F arrives louder still. That is the opposite of "dissonant suspensions receive something of an accent and their resolutions are softer" and of a resolution that is "a relaxation, a decrease in intensity" (PDF 348). In v198's score.midi the violins' velocities rise 80 → 83 from the suspension to the resolution, and the winds' 52 → 57.

**Why this is not a repeat.** c098 left 58 alone and gave one reason: "Mozart's 7–6 chain (PDF 355, Ex. 21-17) also lets a crescendo run through suspensions." The image of that example shows otherwise. *Cresc.* is in the bar before bar 139 in all three staves. Bar 139, where the 7–6 chain starts, is marked *p* in the violin and in both piano staves. The *f* comes four bars later, after the chain. c098's own table (row 13) describes it the same way ("cresc. into the chain and p at its start"). So the precedent c098 cited supports shaping the suspension, not running through it. Beethoven's Ex. 21-37 (PDF 365) does the same: *cresc.*, then a hairpin to *p*, then the suspended chord.

**What the fix keeps.** The build's levels stay as they are (c158 protects the dynamics of 53–61): p at 57 in the winds, mf in the violins, f at 59, ff at 61. The breath after 58 (Fl 1, Ob 1, Hn 1, c046/c166) already marks a phrase break, so the short softening falls where the players breathe. The crescendo now ends on the suspension instead of the barline. The accompanying voices (Hn 1 and Vc 9–10, Cl 1·2, B.cl., Bsn, violas, Cb) keep their crescendo to 59 and carry the build across the melody's breath. That is also how 42 works: the melody pair is shaped and the accompaniment keeps its own hairpin.

**Fix (applied, sections/bSec.ily, four lines, each with a `% c198` comment).**
```
% bSecFluteOne, bar 58   (Ob 1 the same an octave lower: ges''8 ... f''8)
aes'''4. ges'''8~ ges'''4~ ges'''8-\tweak stencil ##f \mf \> f'''8)-\tweak stencil ##f \mp \breathe |
% bSecVlnOne, bar 58     (Vln II the same an octave lower)
aes'''4.) ges'''8(~ ges'''4~ ges'''8-\tweak stencil ##f \f \> f'''8)-\tweak stencil ##f \mf |
```
The printed score shows the 57 crescendo hairpin ending at 58 b4 and a short diminuendo hairpin over b4–b4½, then f at 59 as before. The two dynamics in each line are hidden and act only on score.midi. Without them LilyPond's MIDI crescendo, which has no target, overshot: the violins reached velocity 96 at 58 b3, above the f (83) of 59. With them, the MIDI peaks on the suspension and drops on the resolution: winds 48 → 44, then 58 at 59; violins 81 → 73, then 83 at 59. No pitch, rhythm, slur, bowing or printed level changes. The c151 rhythm and the c110/c119 A♭ ceiling are untouched.
