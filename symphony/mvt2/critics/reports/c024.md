## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Bars 79–81: the ff peak of A′ has only the double basses at 16' pitch (applied)
**Problem.** At the movement's highest note (B♭6, 79) everyone plays ff, but the bottom octave is the double basses alone. The cellos are on the counter-melody with horns 1·2 and Bsn 1; Bsn 2 and B.cl. are an octave higher (D♭3); the contrabassoon and tuba are silent.
**Evidence.** In all twelve ff chords of the appendix (PDF 505–507, chords 1–12 and 17–20) the root is doubled in the low octave by two to four instruments: Fag./C-fag., Tbn III/tuba, Vc, Cb. Ex. 204–209 (PDF 367–376) do the same. Bar 61 here follows the rule (Cbsn, tuba, Cb on D♭2); 79 does not, though it is the louder peak.
**Fix (aTwo.ily).** Add a contrabassoon part (it last played at 62, so it comes back ff, as PDF 136 asks):
`aTwoCbsn = { R2.*8 | des,2.\ff | des,2 bes,,4 | es,2.\> | aes,,2\p r4 | }`

### 2. Bars 79–80: a sixth's gap between D♭5 and B♭5 in the upper chord (applied)
**Problem.** Above the clarinets' and oboe 2's D♭5 the next note is B♭5 (Ob 1, Vln II), then F6 (Fl 2), B♭6 (Fl 1, Vln I). D♭5 is also doubled by Cl 1 and Ob 2.
**Evidence.** The appendix spaces the upper woodwinds in close position (thirds and fourths) above the brass (505 chords 1–5; 507 chords 17–20). PDF 125 says the upper and lower parts are seldom left apart without the middle filled.
**Fix (aTwo.ily).** Oboe 2 takes F5 instead of the doubled D♭5, then B♭4 on 80 b3 to reach its old G4:
`aTwoOboeTwo = { R2.*8 | f''2.~\ff | f''4 f''4 bes'4 | g'2.\> | aes'2.\p | }`
(F5 over the B♭ bass then G4 over E♭: no parallel fifths. The checker shows no new pairs.)

### 3. Bar 61: violins I hold a single D♭6 over a thin upper octave (applied)
**Problem.** At the main climax the space from A♭4 to D♭6 holds only Ob 1 (F5) and the two flutes (A♭5). Five instruments sit on A♭4 (Hn 1, Tpt 1, Ob 2, Cl 1, Vln II). Vln I plays one note.
**Evidence.** In the appendix the violins at the top of a ff tutti play two-, three- and four-note chords (505 chords 1–5 and 7; 507 chords 17–20 with div.). The brass layout at 61–62 is protected, so I fill the gap in the strings, which the tables also use.
**Fix (bSec.ily).** `<f'' des'''>1\ff^\markup \italic "non div."` at bar 61 in `bSecVlnOne` (a sixth, F5 on the A string and D♭6 on the E). Bar 62 is unchanged.

### 4. Bars 81–82: horns 3·4 hold into the final p chord of A′ (applied)
**Problem.** The diminuendo 81–82 thins nothing: all four horns, all the woodwinds and the strings reach the p chord at 82 together, and the coda starts ppp at 83.
**Evidence.** PDF 130: a diminuendo is made by removing the instruments in reverse order, brass first, then woodwind, strings last. The coda already does this (horns out at 88, clarinets morendo, strings last).
**Fix (aTwo.ily).** Horns 3·4 end with the hairpin at 81 and rest at 82:
`aTwoHnThree = { R2.*8 | f'2.\ff | f'4 f'4 f'4 | es'2.\> | R2.\! | }`
`aTwoHnFour  = { R2.*8 | aes2.\ff | aes4 aes4 aes4 | g2.\> | R2.\! | }`
The chord at 82 stays complete (Cl C5/A♭4, Ob A♭4, Vla E♭4/A♭4, Hn 1·2 + Vc + Bsn 1 C4, Bsn 2/B.cl. A♭2, Cb A♭1). Horns 1·2 keep the counter-melody's last note.

### 5. Bar 57: horn 2's first note of the movement is mp, in unison with clarinet 1 (applied)
**Problem.** Horn 2 is silent from bar 1 to 56 and enters mp at 57 on F4–D♭4, doubling Cl 1 note for note through 60. The brass therefore join the 53–61 crescendo before its peak, at a middle dynamic.
**Evidence.** PDF 136: after a long rest horns, trombones and tuba should re-enter pp or ff; mp/mf re-entries are "colourless and commonplace". PDF 130: in a long crescendo brass come last. Trumpets, trombones, tuba and horns 3·4 already enter ff at 61; horn 2 is the exception.
**Fix (bSec.ily).** Horn 2 rests 57–60 (Cl 1 keeps the notes) and enters ff at 61:
`bSecHnTwo = { R1*8 | R1*8 | R1*4 | R1*4 | f'1\ff | ges'2 aes'2 | R1*2 | }`
Horn 1's counter-line 53–60 is unchanged. Its entry at 53 is p, which PDF 136 calls "less successful" than pp. I leave it, because cycle 8 chose p/mp horn re-entries and the line is a solo.

### 6. Bar 79: a partial tutti without trumpets (no change)
PDF 121 says a wind-and-horn tutti does not reach the volume of the brass. Bar 79 is such a tutti, while 61 has full brass. I do not ask for trumpets. Economy (PDF 136) is better served by keeping them for 61–64, and with finding 1 the 79 chord has a full bass. I record this so that a later critic does not mistake it for an oversight.
