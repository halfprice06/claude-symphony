## What else I studied

I used Gould PDF 28–29 (ch. 1, double-stemmed writing: two stems mean two parts, so a single part should use normal stem rules) and MOLA *Guidelines* PDF 4–5 (the score must show unambiguously who plays) as support for the part-combining points. To find which notes the part-combiner puts in the forced-direction voices "one" and "two", I parsed the voice tracks in score.midi. Fl 1·2 and Ob 1·2 at 57–60 are in voice "one" although the second player rests. The cause is that the crescendo from 57 runs into the a2/divisi entry at 61, so the combiner does not switch to the solo voice there. `\partCombineSoloI` does not change this (tested), but a local `\stemDown` does. I also checked the LilyPond 2.24 part-combiner source to confirm that its solo voice has neutral stems.

## Findings (ranked)

### 1. Violas 37–44 and 53–60: stem direction changes from group to group in the triplet accompaniment. Applied.
**Problem.** The figure lies across the middle line of the alto clef (A♭3–G♭4). LilyPond gives some groups down-stems (aes–des'–f') and others up-stems (des'–aes–des', bes–des'–ges', ges–bes–es'). The result: in bar 37 the four groups go down, up, down, up. In 59–60 one slur is printed below the notes and the next above, and the "3"s change sides. This happens in the movement's main accompaniment texture for 16 bars.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 38: with unequal numbers of notes either side of the middle line, the majority takes the correct direction. des'–aes–des' has two notes above the line and one only two steps below, so it takes down-stems. PDF 39, "visual considerations": in a passage like this, keep one direction "so that all slurs … remain next to their noteheads". The example on that page is a slurred figure of the same kind.
**Fix (bSec.ily, bSecVla).** Add `\stemDown` before the first `\tuplet` at 37, `\stemNeutral` before `f1\pp` at 45, `\stemDown` before the `\tuplet` at 53 and `\stemNeutral` before `<des' f'>1:32\ff` at 61. Result: all beams below, all slurs above, all tuplet numbers below.

### 2. Flute 1 and oboe 1, 57–60: up-stems on a single part high above the stave, and no "1." on the oboe. Applied.
**Problem.** Fl 2 and Ob 2 rest in 57–60, but the combiner keeps Fl 1 and Ob 1 in voice "one" (see above), so all their stems point up. In the flute, notes on 1–4 ledger lines get stems pointing away from the stave, and the beam at 58 floats about seven spaces above the top line. The oboe solo also has no "1." because the combiner never registers a solo there. Cycle 5 added "1." labels to the flute and other solo entries, but this one was missed.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 38: notes above the middle line take down-stems. PDF 33 and 35: ledger-line groups are beamed back towards the stave, with stems reaching it. PDF 28–29: an up-stem in a shared stave tells the player that a second part is present.
**Fix (bSec.ily).** In bSecFluteOne, `\stemDown aes''4(\mf\<^\markup \bold "1." …` at 57 and `\stemUp aes''2(\ff` at 61. In bSecOboeOne, `\stemDown aes'4(\mf\<^\markup \bold "1." …` at 57 and `\stemUp f''1(\ff` at 61. The `\stemUp` restores the voice-one direction for the two-part bars 61–62.

### 3. Horns 1·2, bar 71: "2." printed twice. Applied.
**Problem.** aTwoHnTwo has `^\markup \bold "2."` and the part-combiner also prints its own soloIIText "2.", so two "2." labels are stacked over the entry.
**Evidence.** MOLA PDF 4–5 asks for unambiguous labels. A duplicated label looks like an error and makes the player check whether something else is meant.
**Fix (aTwo.ily).** `aTwoHnTwo = { es2\p aes8( g8 | …` (remove the markup and keep the combiner's label).

### 4. Horns 3·4, end of bar 82: stray treble clef. Applied.
**Problem.** In cycle 5, the coda lines `\clef treble` were added to cancel the bass clef at 79–82. Cycle 14 moved the horns to the middle register and removed that bass clef, but the coda clef stayed. A redundant treble clef now prints at the end of the 79–82 system, on a stave that is already in treble.
**Evidence.** Gould PDF 21–23 (cycle 5's reading): a clef change is printed only where the clef changes. A cautionary clef without a change makes the player look for a register shift that does not exist.
**Fix (coda.ily).** `codaHnThree = { R2.*4 | aes2.\pp …` and `codaHnFour = { R2.*4 | f2.\pp …` (delete `\clef treble`).

### 5. Harp, bar 83: two "p" marks for one player. Applied.
**Problem.** score.ly prints harp dynamics once, from the LH staff (DynamicText direction UP), for both hands. codaHarpR also carries `\p` on the pickup, so two p marks appear, one between the staves at beat 1 and one under the RH at beat 3.
**Fix (coda.ily).** `r2 es'8( f'8 | …`. The LH `\p` at 83 covers both hands, and the PianoStaff Dynamic_performer keeps the MIDI volume.

### 6. Violin I (and flute 1) on four to five ledger lines: 57–59 and 76–81. Not applied.
B♭6, A♭6 and G6 stack 4–5 ledger lines in Vln I at 58 and 76–81, in long held notes at ff. Gould PDF 40 says ledger lines are drawn thick so the player can count them at a glance, and five lines is about the limit of that. Vln I is not part-combined, so `\ottava #1` from 75 beat 3 to 81 beat 1 in aTwoVlnOne (and from 57 to 59 in bSecVlnOne) is allowed. Octave signs are the subject of the cycle-25 reading (PDF 42–48), so I leave the decision to that cycle. Flutes should stay as written, because flutists read this register routinely and an ottava is not allowed on a shared stave where Fl 2 sounds (79–80).

### 7. Viola triplets 37–60: a "3" under every beat (96 tuplet numbers). Not applied.
After the first bar of each passage the triplet quavers are self-evident. Gould's tuplet chapter is outside the excerpt, but common practice (and MOLA's call for uncluttered scores) supports printing the numbers for the first two beats and omitting them afterwards. Fix: in bSecVla, `\omit TupletNumber` after bar 37 and after bar 53, and `\undo \omit TupletNumber` before 53. I did not apply this because it is house style, not an error.
