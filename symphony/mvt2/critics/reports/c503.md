## Findings

I found nothing that needs a score change. Every principle in the reading that applies to a symphonic slow movement is met. The findings are ranked by how close each comes to a weakness, and each gives the reason the text should stay.

**1. The intro-coda frame and its proportion, bars 1-6 and 83-92 (PDF 263, Ex. XVII-7; Ex. XVII-8).** Adler's two concerto slow movements both begin with the woodwinds. In the Canzonetta that wind material also returns as the coda (96-118), so the frame takes 35 of 118 bars, about 30 percent. Here the frame is 6 + 10 bars, about 17 percent of the duration (60 of 345 s). I checked whether the coda really closes the frame. It does, in pitch and in colour: at 3-6 Cl 2 plays E♭-E♭-F♭-E♭ and Hn 3 plays C-C-D♭-C over the B.cl. pedal (the minor iv as a neighbour); at 87-88 the C.a. and Hn 4 play F-F♭-E♭ under Hn 3's A♭, and Cl 1 has D♭-C on top. The colours match too: horns 3·4 and a reed in both places, with Hn 1 out of both (it only takes its pickup at the end of 6). A frame as long as the Canzonetta's would not suit this movement. Bars 1-6 are a link from movement I's chord, not a separate theme, and the coda already has three solo ideas over its pedal (harp 83, timpani 84-85, Cl 1 85-89). Adding bars to either end would also move the B climax off its 65 percent point and lengthen a movement that critics have already timed against Dvořák 9/ii (c443). Fix: none.

**2. Horn use around the horn solos, bars 1-14, 53-58, 61-62, 67-70, 78-81, 87-90 (PDF 264-266, Ex. XVII-9).** Adler points out that Mozart keeps the orchestral horns for "the introduction and during interludes" so they do not interfere with the solo horn. This score follows that exactly. Hn 3·4 play in the introduction (1-6) and in the coda (87-90). The second horn joins Hn 1 only in the two tuttis (61-62, 78-81), which here play the part of interludes. Hn 1's three solo spans (6-14, 53-58 as counter-line with Cl 1, 67-70) have no other horn under them. At 75-77 Hn 2 takes the counter-melody while Hn 1 rests, so the same principle applies the other way around. Fix: none.

**3. Cut-backs after the tuttis and the arrival on the tonic, bars 61-67, 70-71, 79-83 (PDF 267 item 3; PDF 268).** In Ex. XVII-10 at C the tutti brings in the soloist, the orchestra drops away "drastically", and a sustained tonic in horns, cellos and basses confirms the tonic's return. The two parts of that rule appear here, but at different points. The cut-back happens at 63 (strings sf > p under Tbn 1's ff quotation, B.tbn sf > p, Fl 1 a single sf crotchet) and again at 82-83 (from the ff peak to a ppp A♭ pedal in four soli cellos and basses). The sustained pedal happens at 64-70: Cb E♭ held for six bars with a timpani roll at 67-70 under Hn 1. At the tonic return at 71 the horns are not used. The basses, bassoons and harp mark it instead. Adler's Mozart horn rule (Finding 2) explains why: Hn 1 has just finished its solo on B♭3 at 70, and another horn on the tonic at 71 would carry the solo colour into the violins' entry. The A′ texture at 71-82 is also on the do-not-touch list. Fix: none.

**4. Joining phrases with the timpani, bars 52-53 and 84-85 (PDF 273, Ex. XVII-13).** In Beethoven's Op. 61/i the timpani play the motive that links the two halves of the woodwind melody. The coda does the same: the harp states theme A's head in harmonics at 83, the timpani answer E♭-C-A♭ at 84-85 (pp, soft sticks), and Cl 1 picks up the timpani's E♭ (an octave and more higher, E♭5) at 85 for the motto tail E♭-D-D♭-C (85-89). The timpani also give Cl 1 its first pitch (PDF 275). At 52 the timpani roll A♭ pp < into the D♭ at 53, which is where the build begins. Both uses are placed at a join, not in the middle of a phrase, as the reading recommends. Fix: none.

**5. Colour as a guide to the form; the cor anglais's three entries in 32-52 (PDF 258; PDF 263; PDF 267 item 4).** Adler says dialogue makes the form clearer, and that a solo colour should be fresh when it enters. Row 3 lists the lead colour at each phrase head. No two adjacent phrases share a solo colour. The one place that comes close to Adler's concern about freshness is the cor anglais: it plays at 32-35 (with the B.cl.), on theme B at 41-44, and on the movement I quotation at 47-52, so the quotation's colour has already been heard twice in the previous 70 s. I did not draft a fix. All three placements are protected with stated reasons: 32-34 pairs movement I's first two motto instruments (c263, c330), 41-44 is on the do-not-touch list (c090, c387), and the quotation at 47-53 has been in the cor anglais since c040. Ob 1's head at 45-46 in a different key and register separates 41-44 from 47. The quotation is also set apart by key (F♯ minor), by tempo ("poco rit." 47, "poco sostenuto" 48) and by its new string background (violas sotto voce off-beats, Vln II counter-line). I record this as the nearest point to a violation, not as a change.

**6. Rhythmic independence under the themes, bars 23-30, 37-44, 48-51, 53-56, 71-78 (PDF 259-260, Ex. XVII-3, -4).** The B section and the return use accompaniment figures that neither copy nor imitate the tune's rhythm. Bars 71-78 have the same layout as Tchaikovsky's Ex. XVII-3: a melodic counter-line (cellos) plus simple figuration (viola eighths). The only exception is 23-30, where the strings attack with the violins' melody. That passage is protected as the plain first statement, so that A′ at 71 can differ from it (c007, c477, c489). Its plainness helps the form: it makes the violin return at 71 sound new. Fix: none.

**7. Transparent preparation before solo entries, bars 3-6, 30-31, 47, 65-66 (PDF 268-272, Ex. XVII-11, -12).** Sibelius sets the background for three bars before the soloist, and Bartók for two. This score does the same before each wind or horn solo. At 3-6 there are four bars of soft wind chord before Hn 1. At 30 the Cbn enters pp under the strings' p > pp and holds into the B.cl. solo at 31. At 47 the fp chord comes one bar before the quotation. At 65-66 there are two bars of pp strings over the basses' E♭ before Hn 1 at 67. The two entries without preparation (theme B at 37, the violins at 53) are string or flute entries that start together with their background, as Dvořák 9/ii does at 46 (c233). Fix: none.
