**Score:** average number of other instruments within a tritone of each counter-line note: cellos 23–30, 1.2; cellos 57–60, 1.5; cellos 71–78, 2.2; Hn 1 53–60, 2.7; Hn 1·2 79–82, 2.6; Hn 2 71–78, 3.0. The two peaks of 7 are both in Hn 1's line:

At 58 the horn has the denser spectrum of the two layers (PDF 289). The cellos double it an octave below from 57 b3 (c017), and Bsn 1 joins at 59 (c057). ### 2. The brass dynamics at 60–62 grade density as both books ask (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 59–62, 79–81.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 289: forte brass act like distortion and come forward. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 41 gives the acoustic rule for this: mark horns one degree above the other brass, and use two horns in forte against one trombone.
**Score:**
- At 60, Hn 4 has p< while Tbn 2, the bass trombone and the tuba have pp<.
- At 61, Hn 1·2 are ff and the trombones and tuba f, again one degree apart. Hn 3·4 a2 at f are the two horns that balance one trombone (c064, c086).
- At 79–81 there are no trombones or tuba. Hn 1·2 a2 f double the cellos' and Bsn 1's counter-line, and Hn 3·4 hold the middle of the chord. The ff on top is carried by strings and winds in three octaves.

So the densest brass sound comes at the B climax and the quotation, and the A′ peak stays string-led. c474 Finding 4 already recorded the horns in Vln II's octave at 61–62. Those bars are protected.
**Fix:** none.

### 3. Every solo is marked one level above its accompaniment, the acoustic form of the *Dune* volume control (recorded, no change)
**Bars:** 7, 15, 17, 31, 37, 41, 45, 47, 63, 67, 83, 85.
**What the reading shows:** PDF 290: in a mix a weak instrument can be made louder than instruments that would cover it in a room. An orchestrator can only lower the others.
**Score:** solo against accompaniment:
- Hn 1 p over bassoons, B.cl. and harp pp (7).
- Ob 1 p over strings pp (15).
- Solo violin p over the muted section pp (17).
- B.cl. p over bassoons and Cbn pp (31).
- Fl 1 p over Vln II sul tasto and violas pp (37). The harp, B.cl. and pizz. basses are also p there, but all of them are an octave or more below the flute.
- C.a. p over pizz. violas and cellos pp, with Fl 1 at pp (41).
- C.a. p over strings pp (47 b4).
- Tbn 1 ff over strings sf>p (63).
- Hn 1 p over timpani ppp and basses pp (67).
- Harp harmonics p over ppp (83).

The one level lead is Ob 1 pp at 45, where the whole texture is pp. There the cellos double the oboe at the octave and the bassoons join, so a doubling does the work of the dynamic step. That is the alternative acoustic method the reading's EQ section names (PDF 286–287: choosing who plays instead of how loud). I record it and do not ask for a change.
**Fix:** none.

### 4. Held pedals, timpani rolls and Tbn 1 at 63 are the acoustic forms of compression (recorded, no change)

**What the reading shows:** PDF 290: a sound held at a constant level is heard as more present and thicker, and a compressed timpani stroke lasts longer (Fig. 6.6). PDF 291: one instrument can become thicker than the whole ensemble.

- At 63 the strings drop from sf to p under Tbn 1's ff. One trombone then stands above the ensemble with no help from a dynamic marking on the others, which is the acoustic version of PDF 291.

**Fix:** none.
