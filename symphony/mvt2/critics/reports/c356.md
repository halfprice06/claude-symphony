## Findings

All 25 applicable principles hold. I found no violation of the assigned reading in these four instruments, so every finding below is recorded with no change. They are ranked by how much each could matter in rehearsal.

### 1. Clarinet 2 holds E♭4 for 89–92 on one breath (recorded, protected)

**Bars:** 89–92, `codaClTwo`.

**Problem:** This is the longest wind span in the movement: about 19 s under the rit., plus the fermata. It is the morendo hand-over at the plagal close.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 210 says a good clarinettist commands "the faintest pianissimo … beginning with the lowest" register. Written F4 is the top of the chalumeau in Ex. VII-56, where a ppp costs very little air. Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 36 says the clarinet can reduce its tone "to a mere breath". c316 found that a breath would leave a hole, because nobody else holds E♭4 after the cor anglais releases at 90.

**Fix:** None. If a conductor finds the span too long at a slow fermata, the player can take a silent breath and re-enter niente with the hairpin from 89 (Adler PDF 211). That needs no new marking.

### 2. Bass clarinet low C at 8 (recorded)

**Bars:** 8, `aOneBassCl`.

**Problem:** Concert B♭1 needs a low-C instrument.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 216 says writing to low C is "still risky". The part names the instrument "(low C)" in its title and prints the ossia "(no low C: small note)". On a low-E♭ instrument the ossia sounds B♭2. The harp's low B♭ still sounds on the beat.

**Fix:** None. This is the protected motto bass of 7–14 (c126, c156, c226, c306).

### 3. Clarinet crossings of the break at soft dynamics (recorded, not re-raised)

**Bars:**
- Cl 1 at 53–56: written G4→C5 and C5→A♭4, slurred, pp<p.
- Cl 2 at 57–59: written C5–B♭4–D♭5–C5, slurred, mp<f.
- Cl 2 at 72: written G4→C5, pp.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 210 says the break "presents problems to all but the most expert player". He also says that "in the end, it presents no difficulties whatsoever". Every crossing is slurred, at a moderate tempo, and doubled or covered by strings. The throat A♭4 (written B♭4) at 71–79 is the "rather pale" colour, used for an inner voice.

**Fix:** None. This was kept by c126, c178 and c226. I have no new book reason.

### 4. Contrabassoon pp entry on A♭1 at 30 and the re-attack after the breath at 33 (recorded)

**Bars:** 30–36, `aOneCbsn` and `transCbsn`.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 230 says the contrabassoon is "a little bit more stubborn … speaks a bit more slowly at the bottom". PDF 226, which applies to it by PDF 230, says only the lowest perfect fifth is hard to play pp. On the contrabassoon that fifth is B♭0–F1, a minor third below A♭1. The entry at 30 sounds together with the cadence chord, so a slow start is covered. After the breath at 33, Bsn 2's A♭2 (held to 34 b1, c220) covers the re-attack. Frequent rests (PDF 230): the contrabassoon plays only 18 bars in the movement.

**Fix:** None.

### 5. Bassoon 2's F♭2 at 62 and the contrabassoon's B♭0 at 80 (recorded)

**Bars:** 62 (`bSecBsnTwo`) and 80 (`aTwoCbsn`).

**Evidence:** These are the only bassoon-family notes in the lowest perfect fifth of either instrument. Both are ff, where Adler PDF 226 expects and allows the firmer attack. The B♭0 is a single quarter at the bottom of the range, between D♭1 and E♭1. It suits the slow speech at the bottom (PDF 230) and was checked by c184 and c306.

**Fix:** None.

### 6. Changing to A clarinets for the scherzo (recorded)

**Bars:** 91–92, `codaClOne` and `codaClTwo`.

**Evidence:** Adler PDF 215, item 1, says to give a player "enough time to change instruments, and allow for a moment of adjustment". Clarinet 1 stops at 90 and has 91–92 plus the pause. Clarinet 2 changes in the pause, as its part says. The pause is long enough for the timpani to retune all four drums. The scherzo's clarinets first enter at its bar 7. The B♭/A choice follows PDF 210–211: B♭ for A♭ and D♭ major, A for E major. The clarinets also rest through both sharp-key passages of this movement (47–52, 63–64), so no B♭ part has to read five sharps.

**Fix:** None. The two parts word the change slightly differently: Cl 1 has "(for the Scherzo)", Cl 2 has "(for the Scherzo, in the pause)". Both are correct for the player, because Cl 1 can change before the pause.
