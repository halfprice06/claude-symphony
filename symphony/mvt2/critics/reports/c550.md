### 2. Recorded, no change: the link from movement I is continuous in sound and pitch

### 3. Recorded, no change: the three pedal passages are carried by scoring, not new harmony

Casanelles's analysis of the *Inception* cue (PDF 205) treats a fixed four-chord progression as "wallpaper": in such passages, orchestration, dynamics and rhythm become the main elements. Earlier passes did not apply this point. The movement's three pedal passages each follow it:
- **65–70, V7 of A♭ over the E♭ pedal.** Vln II, violas and cellos hold the chord pp and fade at 65–66. Hn 1 plays the motto tail alone at 67–69, p < mp > pp, over a ppp timpani roll "near the rim". Bsn 1 enters a third below at 68, and the violins' p pickup at 70 b3 leads into the return. The colour changes three times over one chord.
- **31–36, A♭ pedal.** The bass clarinet solo, then the cor anglais above it, then the common-tone diminished seventh at 33 over the contrabassoon and Bsn 2 pedal.
- **83–86, A♭ pedal.** Harp harmonics at 83, the timpani's E♭3–C3–A♭2 at 84–85, divided violas from 84 b3, and Cl 1's motto tail from 85.

In all three, the scoring changes under one held harmony. Nothing to change.

### 4. Recorded, no change: both builds follow the staged crescendo of Figs. 5.7–5.8

Fig. 5.7 writes each step of the crescendo as its own dynamic, and Fig. 5.8 shows a slow rise followed by a steep one (PDF 216–217). The build at 53–61 follows this shape:
- Violins p at 53, mp at 54, mf at 56, f at 59, with Fl 1 and Ob 1 entering p at 57 over the violins' mf, so the doubling adds weight without a jump.
- The steep last part is bar 60: Hn 4 enters p, Tbn 2, bass trombone and tuba enter pp<, and the timpani roll pp< to f, all under "poco allargando", arriving at f/ff at 61.

The second build (75–79) is shorter and steeper. It goes from p in the winds and basses at 75, to mp at 77, to f at 78, with the timpani pp< on 78 b3 into ff at 79. Over its four bars, two are a slow rise and two are a fast one. Neither peak stops dead, which PDF 231–232 says an acoustic orchestra cannot do anyway:
- 62's ff hands over to the sf> tremolo and Tbn 1's ff entry at 63.
- 79–81 thin out to p at 82, while the timpani roll at 81 fades to niente.

Nothing to change.

### 5. Recorded, no change: reserved sounds and II's place between I, the scherzo and the finale

The *Annihilation*, *Gravity* and *Man of Steel* examples (PDF 217, 228, 233, 235, 238) make the point that a sound used in only one place carries the most weight there. II follows this at symphony level:
- Trumpets are tacet. The finale brings back the trumpet-and-timpani pairing at its 199–204.
- Trombones play only at 60–64, with no chorale, and Tbn 1 plays only the quotation.
- String tremolo appears only at 61–64, muted brass only at 87–90, and harp harmonics only at 83.
- There is no percussion besides timpani. In the B section, the pizzicato basses and the viola and harp triplets carry the pulse (PDF 233, Price's pulsations in place of percussion).

Theme A follows PDF 217–218. It is first stated by the horn, the warm and inward voice, and its last statement is the most intimate: three harp harmonics at 83, answered by the timpani. That answer is the source of the scherzo's motive x a major third lower.

The joins with the other movements hold:
- The bar-92 muta (A♭→E, C→G, D♭→B, E♭→E) matches the scherzo's bar-1 drums.
- Vln II's A♭4 under the fermata becomes the scherzo Hn 1's G♯4.
- The last chord stays a plain triad, ready for the finale's German sixth on A♭.
- The closing motto tail at 85–89 is answered a major third lower by the scherzo (B–A♯–A–G♯) and the finale (G–F♯–F–E).

This is PDF 236's "journey": each return of an idea is in a new state. Nothing to change.
