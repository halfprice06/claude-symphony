## Findings

### 1. Bars 61–62: the woodwind harmony is buried inside the brass. APPLIED
**Problem.** At the climax chord of the B section Ob 2 plays F4, in unison with Tpt 2 and Hn 2. Cl 1 plays D♭4, in unison with Tpt 3 and Hn 3. Cl 2 plays F3, between Tbn 2 (F3) and Tbn 1 (A♭3). At ff none of these three can be heard. Between A♭4 and A♭5 the chord has only Fl 2 (F5), so the octave above the trumpets is thin.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov: "If trumpets and trombones take part in a chord, flutes, oboes and clarinets are better used to form the harmonic part above the trumpets" (PDF 110). Every diagram on PDF 111 puts 2 Ob + 2 Fl and 2 Cl above the trumpets, with horns and trombones below. Clarinets in unison with trumpets are a listed juxtaposition ("2 Trumpets + 2 Cl", PDF 106). Dvořák 9/ii bar 96 uses this layout (clarinets A4/E5 with the trumpets, oboes and flutes above).
**Fix (bSec.ily).** Oboe 1 holds F5 above the trumpets at 61 and rejoins the melody at 62. Oboe 2 takes the melody's lower octave at 61. The clarinets go up into unison with the trumpets. The order Fl–Ob–Cl stays intact, as critic 009 asked (PDF 67).
```
bSecOboeOne 61–62:  f''1(\ff | f''2 fes''2) | R1*2 |
bSecOboeTwo 61–62:  aes'2\ff des''2 | des''2 fes''2 | R1*2 |
bSecClOne   60–62:  … | c''1\< | aes'2\ff des''2 | bes'2 ces''2 | R1*2 |
bSecClTwo   61–62:  f'1\ff | ges'2 aes'2 | R1*2 |
```
After the change the chord at 61 is Fl A♭5/F5, Ob F5/A♭4, Cl A♭4/F4 with Tpt A♭4/F4/D♭4. At 62 it is Fl F6/D♭6, Ob F5/D♭5, Cl B♭4/G♭4. Cl 1 moves to C5 at 60 (it was E♭4) so that it reaches A♭4 at 61 without parallel fifths against the bass A♭–D♭.

### 2. Bar 62, beats 1–2: the major seventh G♭–F lies inside the trumpets. APPLIED
**Problem.** Over the G♭ bass Tpt 1 has F5 (the melody) and Tpt 2 has G♭4, so both notes of the major seventh are in one colour, at ff, in the most penetrating group.
**Evidence.** "Discords of the seventh or second are preferably entrusted to instruments of different tone colour" (PDF 102). The diagrams on PDF 102–103 split each seventh between trumpets and horns.
**Fix (bSec.ily).** `bSecTptTwo = { R1*24 | f'1\ff | bes'2 ces''2 | R1*2 | }`. G♭4 stays in Hn 2, so the seventh now lies between a horn (G♭4) and a trumpet (F5). Tpt 2's B♭4→C♭5 is in unison with Hn 1.

### 3. Bars 79–82: four horns ff against two single clarinets; flute 2 and oboe 2 idle. APPLIED
**Problem.** At the A′ peak the horns play ff: Hn 1·2 have the counter-melody in unison, Hn 3·4 have A♭3/D♭3. The only upper harmony is Cl 1 D♭5 and Cl 2 A♭4, plus the viola tremolo. Fl 2 and Ob 2 are silent through the whole of A′. Between Ob 1's B♭5 and Fl 1's B♭6 the octave is empty.
**Evidence.** "Played forte, the horns are more powerful than the wood-wind; balance may be established by doubling the upper harmonic parts" (PDF 108). The diagram on PDF 109 shows 2 Fl, 2 Ob and 2 Cl above four horns f, with the bassoon on the bass.
**Fix (aTwo.ily, new variables).**
```
aTwoFluteTwo = { R2.*8 | f'''2.~\ff | f'''2. | R2.*2 | }
aTwoOboeTwo  = { R2.*8 | des''2.~\ff | des''4 des''2 | g'2.\> | aes'2.\p | }
```
F6 fills the empty octave under Fl 1's B♭6–A♭6 and stops before the E♭7 bar. I tried longer versions: Fl 2 B♭5 or E♭6 at 81 made octaves with the bass or the timpani, and Ob 2 D♭→B♭ or D♭→C made octaves with the counter-melody (a doubled seventh). The version above makes none of these. Dynamics match Fl 1 and Ob 1 on their shared staves. This adds inner harmony only. The octave-doubled melody and counter-melody that critics 011 and 012 protected are unchanged.

### 4. Bars 79–82: horns 3·4 double the bass instead of filling the middle. APPLIED
**Problem.** Hn 4 plays D♭3 (79–80), B♭2 (81) and C3 (82). Through 79–80 it doubles the bass with B.cl. and Bsn 2, and Hn 3 adds A♭3 a fifth above. The horns' weight goes into the bass, which already has Cb, Bsn 2, B.cl., timpani and harp. Above the horns' D♭4 there is a gap up to the clarinets.
**Evidence.** Rimsky-Korsakov's four-horn chords keep the horns in the middle and leave the bass octave to the bassoon (PDF 101: "the duplication being effected by the bassoon"; PDF 108 diagram: "4 Corni p", bassoon doubling the bass below).
**Fix (aTwo.ily).**
```
aTwoHnThree = { R2.*8 | f'2.\ff | f'4 f'4 f'4 | es'2.\> | es'2.\p | }
aTwoHnFour  = { R2.*8 | aes2.\ff | aes4 aes4 aes4 | g2.\> | aes2.\p | }
```
The `\clef bass` that cycle 5 added for the low notes is removed, because the notes now sit in treble clef. The horn chord is D♭4 (Hn 1·2), F4 and A♭3, then E♭4/G3 for the E♭7 and E♭4/A♭3 at the cadence. This also removes three octave pairs that Hn 3·4 made with the counter-melody at 80→81 in v14. The finding is independent of finding 3 and can be taken alone.

## Note on bars 71–78 (not a new finding)
The open point "clarinets inside the theme's octave 71–78" is supported by PDF 112: clarinets normally go with the violas and cellos, not with the violins. Moving the clarinets an octave down, though, would put them on top of horn 2's counter-melody (A♭3–D♭4). I would leave the passage as it is and close the point.
