## Findings

I found no place where the score breaks a principle of Unit 18 in a way that can be fixed outside the protected passages. In a form review the useful result is therefore a set of verified records, ranked by how likely a later critic is to raise them again. None of these findings is applied, and the scratch copy is unchanged.

### 1. Bars 53–56: the 6/3 bridge still works after the later harp changes (record, no change)

**Bars:** 53–56.

**Check.** c053 introduced the bridge D♭–C | B♭–G♭ | E♭–C | A–B♭ (I–V6/5 | IV6–ii6/5 | ii7–vii∅7 | vii°7/vi–vi). c137 and c142 later changed the harp figure at 53–56, so I checked the bridge again against the reading.
- **Articulation.** All strings carry one continuous crescendo from p at 53 to mf at 56. Nothing inside the bridge is accented, and the goal chords (D♭ at 53, B♭ minor at 56) carry the weight. This is the "beginning and end articulated" rule (PDF 291).
- **Outer voices.** C2/E♭5 → B♭1/D♭5 are two 10ths, which PDF 293–294 allows for a short stretch.
- **5ths.** PDF 292 and 294 warn that two 6/3s under outer 10ths produce 5ths in an upper voice. At 53 b4 → 54 b1 the inner voices are Hn 1/Cl 1 A♭3–B♭3 → D♭4 (contrary to the soprano) and harp C4 → G♭3. There are no 5ths and no octaves.
- **Doubling.** At 53 b4 the leading tone C is tripled (C2 basses, C3 cellos, C4 harp). PDF 292 forbids doubling the leading tone only "if the tonic triad follows". IV6 follows here, and the bass C descends to B♭ as in the passing V6 of Ex. 18-11a (PDF 297). Allowed.

**Fix.** None. Keep 53–56 as it is. It is the one passage that uses the reading's central technique, and it does so correctly.

### 2. Bar 36 b3 → 37: doubled leading tone into the tonic is a justified exception (record, no change)

**Bars:** 36–37.

**Problem.** On 36 b3 only two pitches sound. The bass clarinet's C3 is the last note of the motto tail. Flute 1 and the cor anglais play C5, the pickup to theme B. At 37 they move to D♭3 and F5 over the D♭ tonic. This is an implied V6 as incomplete neighbour to I (PDF 296: "V6 as N or IN to I") with its leading tone doubled at two octaves. PDF 292 says: "Remember not to double the leading tone if the tonic triad follows."

**Why it stays.** The rule exists so that two voices do not both resolve C→D♭ in octaves. Here only the bass clarinet resolves. The upper C5 leaps to F5, and that leap is theme B's rising-fourth pickup (c071, c111). The bass clarinet's C3 is the motto tail at movement I's pitch (c140). Both notes are protected. The texture has two voices, and PDF 302 (note) treats 6/3 passages in instrumental music as reduced-voice settings. A fix would mean dropping the pickup's C (contradicting c111) or ending the tail on another note (contradicting c140).

**Fix.** None. Record this as an accepted exception next to c132's list (doubled leading tone at 24 and 72), so a later harmony critic does not raise it.

### 3. The three joins use pedals, not 6/3 bridges; this matches Dvořák (record, no change)

**Bars:** 31–36 (A♭ pedal), 65–70 (E♭ pedal), 83–86 (A♭ pedal).

**Observation.** PDF 290–291 presents a 6/3 series as the natural sound of a transition. None of this movement's three joins uses one. Each holds a pedal, and the B section and the reprise arrive by phrase modulation (37) or straight from V (71).

**Evidence that this is not a defect.** The reading says such a passage "can function appropriately as a transition" (PDF 290). That makes it one means among others, not a rule. Dvořák 9/ii, the main model, uses a pedal at both of its joins: a D♭/A♭ pedal in cellos and basses at 43–45 before C♯ minor at 46, and a C♯ pedal at 94–100 before the reprise at 101. All three joins here are also protected: the woodwind choir 31–36, the retransition 65–71, and the coda background 83–92. A 6/3 bridge at 31–36 would also break the cyclic rule that the B section starts in D♭ with no bridge (c020, c133).

**Fix.** None.

### 4. The b phrase 15–22 uses neighbour and passing 6/3s correctly (record, no change)

**Bars:** 15–22.

**Check.** This phrase has the most 6/3 chords outside the B section and is not on the do-not-touch list for harmony. Bar by bar:
- 15 I6 opens the phrase as an expansion of the tonic (PDF 291, the Mendelssohn I6). It moves to IV at 16 by a half step in the bass, the neighbour 6/3 that works best in major (PDF 296).
- 17–18 IV–I6–ii over D♭–C–B♭ is a passing 6/3 (Ex. 18-19c, PDF 301).
- 19 b3 vii°6 of F minor (E–G–B♭ over G) is a neighbour between two F-minor chords (Ex. 18-9, 18-19a). Its doubling is the bass G (G3 cellos, G4 Vln II), the standard choice that avoids doubling the leading tone E.
- 21 b3 V7/V lasts one beat, so V falls on the downbeat of 22 (Ex. 18-11b, PDF 297). Vln I's E♭5–D5 leads into V's leading tone, the melodic equivalent of the reading's suspension.

**Fix.** None.

### 5. Cadential "6 for 5" and the coda's 5–6 follow the synopsis (record, no change)

**Bars:** 13, 29, 81 (b3); 85–88.

**Check.**
- At 13, 29 and 81 the top voice moves through C over the E♭ bass before I. That is "III6 representing V" (PDF 299–300, Ex. 18-16, 18-17) and Ex. 18-19k ("6 displacing 5", I(6)–V7–I). It lets theme A's tail keep its C without weakening V–I.
- In the coda, the A♭ pedal goes 5–6 (C–E♭ at 85 to C–F at 86). Then the bass falls to F for IV6 at 87 and to D♭ for iv at 88. This is Ex. 18-19l: a 5–6 in which the first chord substitutes for a 5/3, leading to the predominant (PDF 301).

Both passages are protected (theme A line, plagal close 87–92), and both follow the reading.

**Fix.** None.
