## Findings

### 1. Bars 71 and 75 b1: clarinet 2 holds the theme's first note in unison with Vln II (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** In A′, Vln II carries the lower octave of theme A. Each phrase opens on A♭4 (`aes'4.` at 71 and 75). Clarinet 2 enters pp on the same downbeat with a held A♭4 (`aes'2.`), so for a beat and a half the background wind sustains the melody's own note in unison. The viola figure also touches A♭4 on beat 2. The clarinet is meant to support the harmony (Adler PDF 136), but on these two downbeats it doubles the tune instead and blurs the attack of the lower octave.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 135: background figures go in neutral registers and should keep away from the melody line's register. Brahms keeps the background off the foreground so the cellos dominate "in their best voice." PDF 144 rule 3: separate the theme from the other parts by register. This is the same reason c219 gave at 9 and 11 (the horn should not land on a note a bassoon already holds).

**Fix (applied, /tmp/critic-247/work/sections/aTwo.ily, aTwoClTwo).** At 71 and 75, clarinet 2 starts on the chord's fifth E♭4. The rest of each phrase is unchanged:

```
es'2.(\pp | aes'4-- f'8 bes'8~ \once \stemUp bes'4 | aes'2. | des'2) r4 |
es'2.(\p\< | aes'4-- f'2) | g'2\mp\< r4 | ...
```

E♭4 is still a chord tone, and it moves to A♭4 at 72 and 76 against the bass's A♭→F and D♭ (a fifth to a third, no parallels). The 72 b2 B♭ anticipation (c061) and the 74 D♭4 (c073) are kept.

### 2. Bar 75 b2: harp doubles the third of the I6 in the middle (applied in scratch)

**Problem.** At 75 b2 the bass is C (I6 of A♭: Cb C, harp LH C 8vb, Bsn 2 C3). The harp RH chord `<aes c' es'>` adds a fourth C at C4, in the middle of the texture, on the beat where the bass reaches the third. c207 removed exactly this doubling from the violas at 27 b2, the same harmony in the first string statement. A′ had the fault back in the harp.

**Evidence.** Adler PDF 152, Ex. V-27 ("good"/"not good"): in I6, IV6 and V6 the third, which is the bass, is doubled near the bottom only, which keeps the open sound of the inversion.

**Fix (applied, aTwoHarpR bar 75):** `r4 <aes es'>4\arpeggio r4 |` (was `<aes c' es'>`). The harp still arpeggiates the chord on beat 2, and c127's rule (no harp attacks on the counter-melody's notes) still holds.

### 3. Bar 73 b3: Vln II lands on clarinet 2's held A♭4 (not applied)

The same fault as finding 1 remains at 73. Vln II's `c''4. bes'8 aes'4` arrives on A♭4 while clarinet 2 holds `aes'2.`. I tested the alternatives on paper. E♭4 makes compound fifths with the bass (B♭4/E♭2 at 72 b3, then E♭4/A♭1). C5 moves the unison to 73 b1 (Vln II's C5). Moving the clarinet off the beat breaks c126's slur plan. If the composer wants it fixed, the cleanest option is clarinet 2 resting at 73 (`r2.`) and re-entering on D♭4 at 74. This thins the wind support for one bar, which Adler PDF 136 counts as optional. I recommend leaving it unless the composer hears the unison as a problem.

### 4. Bar 87: IV6 with its third doubled in the middle (recorded, protected)

Over the bass F (Vc, Cb), the root D♭ is only in clarinet 1's D♭5. The third F appears five times, including the C.a.'s F4 and the violas'/horn 4's F3. In letter this is Adler's "not good" doubling (PDF 152, Ex. V-27). It comes from the protected F–F♭–E♭ coda line (C.a., lower violas, horn 4) and the plagal close. The line's chromatic motion justifies the doubling, so I propose no change.

### 5. Bars 45–46: held background under the minor head (recorded, no change)

Theme B's minor head (Ob 1 with Vc and Bsn 1 an octave below) lies over two bars of whole notes in Vln II, violas, Bsn 2 and basses. This is Adler's octave statement over a quiet background followed by a change of texture at 47 (PDF 136, Ex. V-12). It follows the reading and needs no change.
