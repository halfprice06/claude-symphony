## Findings

This reading has now been applied five times (c076, c166, c204, c316, c344), and the score meets it almost everywhere. I found one new and very small point, which I applied. The other findings record checks against the reading and need no change.

### 1. Viola "lower half sul C" at 87 names the only string the notes can be played on. Applied.

Bar 87, codaVla. The lower half of the divided violas plays F3-F♭3-E♭3 (87-92) with horn 4. The score and the part print "lower half sul C" over it. All three notes lie below the viola's open G3, so they can only be played on the C string. Rimsky-Korsakov's string marks are colour choices where a note can be taken on two strings: PDF 26 (the fourth-position limit on a lower string) and PDF 27 (each string's colour, open versus stopped). Table A (PDF 29) shows the C string as the only string below G3. Everywhere else the movement uses string names in exactly this way: sul D for C5 at 1 and 89, sul C for the G3 at 64-65 (which would otherwise be the open G), and sul A for the basses' E♭2. At 87 the mark tells the player nothing, and it adds a line of text to a crowded system.

Fix (applied, sections/coda.ily, codaVla, bar 87):
```
<f aes>2.\pp | <fes aes~>2. | <es aes>2.~-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "change bow at will" ...
```
The `-\tag #'score ^\markup \italic "lower half sul C" -\tag #'part _\markup \italic "lower half sul C"` was removed. Pitches, dynamics, the div. of 84 and the bowing text at 89 are unchanged, so score.midi does not change. I added a c366 comment line. This leaves c194's divisi and c020's line alone. Only the redundant word goes.

### 2. Vln I at 71-73: theme A's peak E♭6-D6-D♭6-C6 at pp-p is above the soprano's top C6. Recorded, protected.

Rimsky-Korsakov PDF 27 says that violin notes higher than the soprano's top C6 "lose in expression and warmth of tone". At the return (71-73) Vln I carries the theme an octave above Vln II. The held E♭6 and the motto tail D6-D♭6-C6 lie a third or less above that line, at pp-p espr., sul E. The notes are well below the PDF 26 limit (A6) and are long values in a slow melody, so they are playable. The warmth is supplied by the lower octave (Vln II sul D, the warm string by PDF 27) and the cello counter-melody. The 71-74 octaves (c101) and the Vln I register plan 74-78 (c299, c309, c341) are protected. Rimsky's caution is about tone, not about whether the passage can be played, so I give no new reason to reopen them. Nothing to change.

### 3. Bass clarinet solo, 31-36: its crest lies in Table B's "middle, dull" register. Recorded.

The solo sounds E♭2-E♭3, which is written F3-F4. The opening arpeggio and the close (A♭2-C3, written B♭3-D4) are in the low register, "full, dark". The crest E♭3-D3-D♭3-C3 (written F4-E4-E♭4-D4, mf) lies in the middle register, which Table B (PDF 35, image) calls "dull". These notes are near the bottom of the scope of greatest expression (about D4 to C6 written, the same as the clarinet's, PDF 33). They play easily and speak well at mf, and c140 put the solo here for a cyclic reason: it is the pitch and instrument of movement I's first motto statement. PDF 38 also asks for the bass clarinet's darkness, not brightness. No change.

### 4. Cor anglais at 32-34 in its low register, p, a sixth above the solo. Recorded.

PDF 38: the English horn's low register "is fairly penetrating". At 32-34 the C.a. holds C4-C♭4-B♭3-A♭3 (written G4-E♭4) at p, a sixth above the bass clarinet. c276 raised it from pp because the oboe family cannot play pp this low (Adler part 1 PDF 174-175, 198). The bass clarinet is a degree louder (p< mf), and the cor anglais moves only where the solo holds. The woodwind choir 31-36 is protected. No change.

### 5. Contrabassoon pedal 30-36. c344's optional diminuendo stays open.

PDF 38: the double bassoon's very deep notes are "very powerful in piano passages". The A♭1 pedal is pp, with a breath at 33 b3. Under the bass clarinet's last A♭2 at 35-36 it sits an octave below a fading note. c344 proposed `aes,,2.~\>` at 35 and `aes,,2\ppp r4` at 36. As a playability point, I add that a pp held this low for about 11 s after the breath is easy on the contrabassoon. The diminuendo is about balance, not endurance. I did not apply it, because the woodwind choir is protected.

### 6. Upper strings above the practical limit: only B♭6 at 79. Follows.

Using the PDF 26 limits I read from the image (Vln A6, Vla A5, Vc A4, Cb written G4), the only note above a limit is Vln I's B♭6 at 79. It is a dotted half, ff, reached by the scale C6-F6-G6-A♭6 at 78 b3, and doubled by flute 1. That is two of PDF 26's conditions: a long value, and a note reached by a scale. The A6 at 63 is a tremolo, a third condition. The one skip in the approach, C6-F6, lies below the limit. No change.
