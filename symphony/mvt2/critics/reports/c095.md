## What else I studied

I read HISTORY.md for the earlier engraving cycles (5, 15, 25, 35, 45, 55, 65, 75, 85) so as not to repeat closed points (B♭6 at pitch, beaming and tuplet numbers, cello clefs 44–47, the 8-page layout). I looked at all eight pages of v95 at 130 dpi and the doubtful places at 220–400 dpi. Small images are easy to misread for stem direction, so I added a temporary Scheme hook to a diagnostic copy of score.ly. For every Stem it logged the bar, beat, staff, direction, staff-position of each notehead, beaming and source line. A short script then listed (a) single, unbeamed stems pointing against Gould's rule 14 on staves where only one source line sounds in that bar, and (b) middle-line stems whose neighbours in the same part all point the other way (rule 15). Every case in the findings came from that list and was checked in the PDF. The diagnostic copy is not part of the proposed change.

## Findings

### 1. Middle-line notes against the surrounding stem direction (15 places) — applied
**Bars:** Hn 1 12 b2 (B♭4, the theme's appoggiatura); harp LH 11 b3 and 75 b3 (D♭, under 8 bassa); Vc 21 b1, Cb 21 b1; Vc 42 b1 and b4; Vc 53 b1, Cb 53 b1; Cl 1 and Hn 1 54 b3 (B♭4); BCl 58 b4, Cb 58 b4; Cl 2 72 b3; Vc 88, Cb 88.
**Problem:** LilyPond points every middle-line stem down. Gould (PDF 27) says to continue the direction of the surrounding stems when they all go one way. Her example is a bass line in which the middle-line note between up-stemmed neighbours, across barlines, takes an up-stem; the down-stem version is printed as "not". In each bar listed, the notes before and after point up and only the middle-line note points down. At Cl 2 72 the problem is worse: the B♭4 quaver (beamed, stem up) is tied to a B♭4 crotchet with its stem down, so the tie joins two stems going opposite ways.
**Fix (applied in /tmp/critic-095/work):** `\once \stemUp` before each note, e.g.
- aOne.ily 11: `f'4 \once \stemUp bes'4\mp\>`
- bSec.ily 218 (Vc 42): `\once \stemUp des4(\p\> bes,4 c4 \once \stemUp des4)`
- aTwo.ily 74 (Cl 2 72): `bes'8~ \once \stemUp bes'4`
- coda.ily 38/42 (Vc/Cb 88): `\once \stemUp des2.` / `\once \stemUp des,2.`
and the same at aOne 47, 91, 101; bSec 65, 105, 78, 221, 230, 231; aTwo 105. This changes notation only; pitches, rhythm and MIDI are unchanged. The horn line at 12 is protected for its music, not its stems.

### 2. Bassoon 1 at 68: up-stems above the middle line — applied
**Bars:** 68 (Bn 1 B♭3 half note, A♭3 crotchet, labelled "1.").
**Problem:** Both notes are above the bass-clef middle line (D3), so Gould PDF 27 gives down-stems. The part-combiner keeps Bn 1 in voice one (stems up) because Bn 2 enters at 69. The staff has only one player in this bar, so it reads as a solo line printed upside down. It also sits directly above the horn solo, whose stems point up.
**Fix (applied):** retr.ily 13: `\tweak Stem.direction #DOWN bes2(\pp^\markup \bold "1." \tweak Stem.direction #DOWN aes4 | g2.) | g2 r4 |`. I tried `\stemDown … \stemNeutral` first, but the combiner's voice setting overrode it, so the change needs a per-note tweak. Bar 69, where Bn 2 enters, keeps its two-voice layout.

### 3. Clarinet 2 at 75–76 shown as a lower voice under an empty upper voice — applied
**Bars:** 75–76.
**Problem:** Cl 2 plays alone from 71 to 76; Cl 1 enters at 77. At 71–74 the combiner prints "2." and normal stems. At 75–76 it switches to two-voice layout: a whole-bar rest for Cl 1 sits on top and Cl 2's A♭4, F4 and A♭4 hang on down-stems. The same player looks different in consecutive bars, and the stems break Gould PDF 27. The double-stemmed layout (PDF 28) is for two parts that both sound.
**Fix (applied):** aTwo.ily, aTwoClOne: `\partCombineSoloII R2.*2 | \partCombineAutomatic g''4(\mf\< es''8 …`. The clarinets now read as one line from 71 to 76 and split at 77 as before. The shared dynamic p< at 75 still prints once.

### 4. Same layout for Cl 2 at 91–92 and Bn 2 at 58 — not applied
**Bars:** coda 91–92 (Cl 2 E♭4 with Cl 1's rest and fermata above); 58 (Bn 2 with Bn 1's whole rest above).
**Problem:** These are the same kind of case as finding 3. I tried `\partCombineSoloII` before Cl 1's R2. at 91. It produced an "unterminated tie" warning because Cl 2's E♭4 is tied across 89–92 and the combiner state cannot change inside the tie. Bn 1's rests at 57–58 sit inside a deliberate `\partCombineApart` (c057), and the printed rest tells the player that Bn 1 waits.
**Recommendation:** leave both as they are. Gould's double-stemmed layout (PDF 28) is acceptable when the rest carries information, as the fermata does at 92.

### 5. Cello tenor clef 44–47 — no change (protected)
The placement follows Gould exactly. The tenor clef goes in after the rest at 44 b4 and before the phrase (PDF 22). The return to bass clef goes before the barline of 48 at the next phrase (PDF 22–23). Gould's other rule (PDF 21: up to at least three ledger lines rather than changing clef) would allow bass clef throughout, since the passage peaks at E♭4, two ledger lines. This is not an error, and cycle 65 protected these clefs, so I do not re-raise it.

### 6. Ledger lines: tuba F♭1 at 62 (4 lines) — no change
Gould's "up to at least three ledger lines" (PDF 21) is a minimum, not a limit. Four lines below the bass stave is normal tuba reading, and an 8vb sign for one note would be worse. B♭6 at pitch for flute and violins remains closed (c025).
