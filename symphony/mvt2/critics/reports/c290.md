## Findings

**1. Bars 67–70: the timpani roll does not fade with the horn (applied in the scratch copy).**
- *Problem:* Hn 1 plays the motto tail p< mp> and reaches pp on B♭3 at 69–70. The E♭3 roll under it stays at ppp from 67 to the tied stroke at 70 b3 with no diminuendo. Taken against the horn, the roll gets relatively louder exactly as the horn arrives on its last note. The same roll at 2–3, which opens the movement's first six-bar pedal interlude, ends with a niente hairpin (`aes,2:32\>~ aes,8\! r8`). Bar 70, which closes the matching interlude before the reprise, does not.
- *Evidence:* Rimsky PDF 127: the move from a complex timbre to a simpler one, by taking a group away, "belongs essentially to the diminuendo". c289 (Finding 1) left this passage open, citing PDF 139 and Ex. 298, and proposed cutting the roll after 68. I did not take that version, for three reasons. (a) The tied E♭ at 70 b3 and the basses' held E♭2 are the common note on which the violins' E♭ pickup enters. That is the transfer method of PDF 125–126, and c056/c273 recorded it. (b) A roll held under a whole soft horn phrase has a Brahms precedent (1/iv, Più Andante). (c) The passage is protected, so the lighter change is the safer one. A diminuendo gives the elimination the reading asks for, keeps both joins, and repeats bar 3's shape, so the two interludes (1–6 and 65–70) now end alike.
- *Fix (sections/retr.ily):* `retrTimp = { R2.*2 | es2.:32\ppp~^\markup \small \italic "near the rim" | es2.:32~ | \once \override Staff.Hairpin.circled-tip = ##t es2.:32~\> | es2:32~ es8\! r8 | }`
- *Result:* built in /tmp/critic-290/work: 0 warnings, 8 pages, 0 outer-voice parallels. analysis.txt is identical to v290's. The MIDI note list (all 1453 notes, with bar and beat) is identical to v290's and the length is the same (345.33 s), so no bar was dropped and no note moved. The change is visible only in print. The parts book is 30 pages, with the timpani part still on one page. This adds to c056 and c146 and undoes neither. The horn line, the harmony, the bass pedal and "near the rim" are unchanged.

**2. Hand-overs between instruments (recorded, no change).**
- Five hand-overs follow PDF 125–126 exactly, on a shared note. At 36–37 the C.a. pickup C5 moves to F5 for an eighth under Fl 1's entry (c271). At 52–53 the C.a. holds F4 under the violins' F5. At 82 b3 the harp's pickup enters under the violins' held A♭5. At 89 Cl 1 and Vln I share C5. At 69–70 the violins' E♭ enters over the basses' held E♭2.
- Two hand-overs use an adjacent attack instead. At 6, Cl 2's E♭3 ends on beat 2 and Hn 1's E♭3 pickup comes on beat 3. At 47, Ob 1's C♯5 ends on beat 3 and the C.a.'s C♯4 comes on beat 4.
- Both are deliberate and should stay. Bar 6 repeats movement I's own entry into its S theme (I 45–47: Cl 1 E♭4, then Hn 1 E♭3; recorded in mvt1.md). Bar 47 holds the quotation back so it enters alone after the poco rit. (c048). Overlapping either would blur a cyclic entry. PDF 125–126 describes a single phrase split between two colours. Both of these places start a new phrase.

**3. Echo of the F–F♭–E♭ line at 87–89 (recorded, no change).**
- Hn 3 plays F4–F♭4–E♭4 open, f to dim., at 79–81. At 87–89 it returns in Hn 4 con sord., an octave lower, with the C.a. at pitch and the lower violas.
- This matches PDF 128–129 point by point. The second statement is weaker. It is in muted brass after the open phrase. It is imitation at the octave with less resonance. The C.a. is related in colour to the muted horns (PDF 107, c104). Ex. 252 (PDF 417) uses the same means: an oboe phrase taken up by a muted trumpet off stage and then by the cor anglais.
- No woodwind echoes a string phrase anywhere in the movement. At 15–22 the oboe and solo violin play different phrases in dialogue, which PDF 128 allows ("repeated phrases of different character ... scored in a manner most suitable to each").

**4. Crescendo and diminuendo by adding and removing groups (recorded, no change).**
- The build to 61 is the PDF 127 method. Strings start at 53, woodwind and horns join, and Tbn 2, bass trombone and tuba enter pp< at 60 and reach f at 61, a quicker crescendo than the groups already playing.
- The diminuendos remove groups in the order the book gives. At 80–83 brass and woodwind leave first. At 90 the Cl 1, C.a. and Hn 3·4 stop, leaving strings and Cl 2 morendo.
- Bar 67–70 was the one place where a group held its level through a diminuendo (Finding 1).

**5. Chord changes between groups at 62→63 (recorded, no change).**
- Under PDF 126–127, every part continues regularly when the A minor of the quotation follows the F♭ (E) major brass chord. Hn 3·4's C♭5 (B4) is taken up by the violas' C5 in the same octave. The E/F♭ stays as E in the bass. Nothing makes a false relation.
- The cyclic rule keeps the rise B→C (leading tone to C) for the finale. Here B is the fifth of E rising to the third of A minor, not a leading tone moving to a C tonic, so the rule is kept. The stop chord's C♭ still falls a semitone at 33 and 64→65 (c250).
