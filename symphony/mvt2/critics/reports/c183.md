## Findings

No change to the music is needed this cycle. On this reading every key area in the movement is prepared, confirmed or deliberately left unconfirmed in ways the unit describes. The findings below record the parts of that check that c093 did not cover, so later critics do not reopen them.

### 1. The secondary key areas: preparation, confirmation, departure (no change)

**Bars:** 45–53, 62–65.

**B♭ minor, 45–46.** This is vi at the start of B2, after V at 44. The only applied tone is the A♮ lower neighbour at 46 b2. There is no F-major chord and no cadence. By PDF 213 §4 this is a tonicization ("not just one or two chords"), so it correctly has no key signature.

**F♯ minor, 47–51.** This area follows all three stages of the reading.
- Preparation: the new key's dominant comes first (PDF 216 §6). C♯7, spelled D♭7, is held four beats under the poco rit. at 47. It is reached from G♭ at 46 b3, so G♭ is IV of D♭ before and a VI-related chord of F♯ minor after, the long-range pivot of PDF 218.
- Confirmation: an authentic cadence with a line to the new 1̂ (PDF 219 §9). At 49 b2–50 b1 the harmony is iv–N6–V–i, and the top line in Vln II/cor anglais is F♯5–G5–E♯5–F♯5. The violas' off-beat A supplies the third of i at 50.
- Return: the last chord has a double meaning (PDF 219 §10). G♯7 at 51 is V7 of V in F♯ minor and V7 in D♭. The departing tonic F♯ becomes the chord's 7th, G♭. That is the PDF 220 device (the added 7th neutralises the temporary key) carried over to a return to IV. The tonic D♭ then arrives at 53 "in a place of some prominence", with theme B in both violin sections.

This is the best-made modulation in the movement. Do not change it.

**A minor, 63–64.** Its dominant comes before its tonic: E at 62 b3 is the F♭ chord of the protected F♭/E thread (PDF 216 §6). The area is never confirmed: i at 63, then vii°7 at 64 b3, then E7♭9 at 64 b4, and E slips to E♭ at 65. That is right for an area the form wants to leave at once (PDF 218: the end of one area as "a springboard"). Tchaikovsky 6/iv does the same: its ♭VII climax (73–81) is never cadenced and the main key's V6/5 follows directly (83). c093 Finding 5 covered the A-minor signature; this adds the corpus support for leaving the area unconfirmed.

### 2. Retransition 65–70: the dominant pedal against PDF 219–220 (no change)

**Bars:** 64 b4–71.

**What the reading asks.** After a long sojourn away from the tonic, the reading asks for the returning V to become a seventh chord at once. The 7th neutralises the temporary key and gives V7's drive to I (PDF 220 §10). The score does this on the first beat after A minor. At 65 the strings play E♭2–B♭2–G3–D♭4. That one chord does two things. D♭, the tonic of the B section, becomes the 7th. G♮, the tone that cancels D♭ major's G♭, is in the violas. The 7th returns at 68 b2 (horn) and stays through 69–70 (Bsn 2 D♭3, resolving to C3 at 71). The basses hold E♭ under the violins' E♭–F pickup at 70 b3, as c093 Finding 2 required.

**The weak spot.** Bar 67 and 68 b1 are the only stretch of the pedal without the 7th. At 67 there is only E♭ (Cb E♭2, timpani E♭3, horn E♭4). At 68 b1 bassoon B♭3 and horn D4 sound over the pedal, which is V of V over V. D♮ is ♯4̂, the tone the reading names as the one that turns chords toward V (PDF 214 §5). For about four seconds E♭ therefore sounds closer to a local tonic than to V7, which is the opposite of what §10 asks of a retransition.

**What I tested and decided.** On paper I tested holding Vln II's D♭4 one more bar, into 67, with the niente moved to the end of 67. I decided against it, for three reasons:
1. 65–66 have already made E♭ a seventh chord, and a held bass note keeps that meaning through one bare bar.
2. The D♮ at 68 b1 lasts one beat, is the motto tail's chromatic step (5̂–♯4̂–4̂–3̂), and goes to D♭ on the next beat. Over the pedal, c043's thirds B♭/D – B♭/D♭ – A♭/C – G/B♭ give 8–7–6–5, which is the same V7/V → V7 meaning the tail has at 8, compressed over the pedal.
3. c174 recorded that 65–70 clear the texture on purpose before the horn's soft entry, and the plan gives horn 1 this bar alone.

Leave 65–70 as written.

### 3. The movement never makes V a key area (no change)

**Bars:** 7–30, 71–82.

**Point.** PDF 220 §11 says V as a key area is "of particular importance in creating musical form", and three of the four formal uses on PDF 228 are tonicized V. In this movement E♭ is only ever a harmony: half cadences at 10, 22 and 26, the pedal at 65–70, and V♭9 at 81. The b phrase (15–22) tonicizes vi (C7–F minor at 19–20, then deceptively C7–D♭ at 20–21) and ends on V.

**Why no change.** The departure the form needs is supplied by IV (D♭, 37–61), and §11's point is about contrast of key area, not about V in particular. The model behaves the same way: Dvořák 9/ii's Largo theme (7–21) contains no G♮ and puts its middle phrase on IV, not V. The half cadences are also graded the way PDF 215 describes. The two internal ones (10, 26) use diatonic IV/ii → V, so "the V wants to continue moving" into the consequent. The section-ending one (22) uses an applied chord with D♮ in the top line (5̂–♯4̂–5̂). Tonicizing E♭ at 10 or 26 would mean changing the protected horn solo and theme harmony (7–14, 23–30) and would weaken the I–IV plan by adding a second contrast key.

### 4. The return at 71 compared with Dvořák's (no change)

**Bars:** 64–71.

Dvořák 9/ii leaves the A-major climax for the tonic with no dominant (100→101). He uses the common tone C♯ = D♭, which has been in the bass since 96. The reading allows that only after "a brief sojourn" (PDF 219 §10). After a long one it asks for V7 (PDF 220). This movement spends 28 bars (about 117 s) away from A♭, so the six-bar E♭7 follows the reading more literally than the model does. The common-tone return is already used in the movement, in the link at 1–2 (C held, E→E♭), and using it again at 70–71 would repeat that effect. Keep the dominant pedal.

### 5. Coda 86: ♯4̂ goes down, not up (no change; ending unchanged)

**Bars:** 85–89.

PDF 214 §5 treats ♯4̂ as a leading tone to 5̂. At 86 the clarinet's D5 sounds over the A♭ pedal (A♭–C–F–D, vii∅7 of V over the tonic) and then falls to D♭ at 87 (IV6). This is the motto tail as a chromatic passing line, not an applied chord resolving, and it gives the coda the "varied repetition of the final cadence" that PDF 220 §11 describes (IV6–iv–I in place of 81–82's V♭9–I). The plagal close is protected by the cyclic rules. **No change to 83–92.** The scherzo handover (last chord A♭–C–E♭ with C5 on top; Hn 1's G♯4 = A♭) is untouched.
