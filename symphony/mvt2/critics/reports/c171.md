## Findings

All findings are recorded checks. I found nothing in the melody that the assigned reading says is wrong and that a change would improve. Earlier critics already made the fixes this reading calls for: tenuto and swell on the syncopated D5 at 49 (c048), the hemiola 80–81 (c048/c148), the unaccented trombone pickup at 63 (c148), and the rhyming rhythm of 38/40 (c081).

### 1. Theme A's peak moves from a weak beat (A) to a strong beat (A′): keep (recorded)
- **Bars:** 12, 28, 76 (B♭ on beat 2, a quarter); 79 (B♭6 on beat 1, dotted half tied into 80, ff).
- **What the reading says.** A&S PDF 50: unusually high notes carry a built-in accent. PDF 51: the most natural rhythm puts long notes on strong beats, and a rhythmic accent on a weak beat works against the metre. In the A statements the theme's highest note is a quarter on beat 2, right after the F tied over the barline, so its accent is placed against the metre. At 28 the f on that beat adds a performance accent (PDF 49). At 79 the same pitch becomes a long note on the downbeat of the last four-bar group (PDF 53), with register, length, dynamic and hypermeter all on the same beat. The tune saves its "most natural" form of the peak for the reprise. A listener hears the reprise peak as the place where the theme's high note finally falls on the beat.
- **Comparison.** Dvořák places the Largo tune's high point on a weak beat (bar 17, beat 4, quarter) and does the same in its reprise. This score goes one step further than Dvořák at 79. Elgar 1/iii and Tchaikovsky 5/ii (horn theme) also let the peak arrive late in the phrase on a long note.
- **Fix:** none. Do not move the B♭ at 12/28/76 to a strong beat. That would use up the effect of 79.

### 2. The tied high notes of theme A are syncopations shaped as the reading requires (recorded)
- **Bars:** 7–8 and 11–12 (horn), 23–24 and 27–28 (violins), 71–72 and 75–76.
- **Reading.** PDF 51: "the beginning of a note is heard as stronger than its continuation", so a note held from beat 3 into the next downbeat contradicts the metre. Ex. 3-6 marks the syncopated note itself. Each tied E♭ and F starts on beat 3 under a crescendo, and the diminuendo starts on the tied downbeat. The performance accent therefore sits on the syncopated attack and not on the barline (PDF 49).
- **Effect on memorability.** This tie over the barline, followed by the chromatic E♭–D–D♭–C, is the theme's most distinctive rhythm. Ex. 3-14 (Schubert) uses the same device: long tied notes across the barline inside an eight-bar phrase. The theme can be sung back from its rhythm alone (`4. 8 4~ | 4 4 4`), which is the test this lens asks for.
- **Fix:** none.

### 3. Theme B's head appears in two metric positions; both are normal rhythmic groups (recorded)
- **Bars:** 36 b4 → 37 and 52 b4 → 53 (C–F as a pickup into the downbeat); 41 and 57 (A♭–D♭–F inside the bar, with F on beat 3); 44 b4 → 45 (oboe pickup A♭–D♭).
- **Reading.** PDF 52, Ex. 3-9a/b: rhythmic groups begin either on the strong part of the bar or on an unaccented beat; both are normal. The antecedent (37, 53) starts from the upbeat and lands its long F on the downbeat. The consequent (41) and the tutti statement (57) start on the downbeat and put the dotted F on the secondary strong beat 3, as an appoggiatura over IV (PDF 54–55, Ex. 3-15c). So the rising fourth is heard first as an upbeat and then as a downbeat arpeggio. It is varied, not irregular. At 61 the augmented head (A♭–D♭–F–F♭ in half notes) restores the downbeat placement at the climax.
- **Fix:** none. The rising-fourth pickups are already protected (c161).

### 4. Dissonance placement in the melodies follows the reading (recorded)
I listed every melody note dissonant with the bass in all the theme statements. Each one is one of the following:
- **Weak-beat passing or neighbour tones of a half beat:** 9 b2½, 10 b3½, 13 b2½, 25/73 b2½, 29/77 b2½, 42 b2½, 54 b2. These are the norm (PDF 54).
- **Chordal sevenths on beat 3:** 8/24/72 (D♭ over the E♭ bass) and 12/28/76 (A♭ over B♭). These are consonant within the chord and resolve down by step.
- **Accented dissonances on strong beats:** 37 b3, the G♭ eighth over D♭, an accented neighbour as in Ex. 3-15b. 41/57 b3, the F over G♭, a 1½-beat appoggiatura resolving up to G♭ (Ex. 3-15c). 79 b1, the B♭6 appoggiatura. Each is a strong-beat dissonance of the kind A&S name on PDF 54.
- **The G♭–F 4–3 at 42/58 b4.** It is held from the strong beat 3 and resolves on b4½. PDF 50 says a divided beat mirrors the bar, and PDF 55 says a suspension is accented relative to its resolution. At the beat level the dissonance is on the strong half of beat 4 and the resolution on the weak half, so it follows the rule. The pair at 42 is protected (c151).
- **55 b4: passing D♭5 in the violins against the harp's C4 struck on the same beat.** A minor ninth for one beat at mp, at the weakest point of the bar, between two chord tones (E♭ → C). This is normal for a passing tone (PDF 54). I recommend no change.

No melody dissonance falls on a strong beat without preparation or stepwise resolution.

### 5. Hypermeter and phrase lengths support the tunes (recorded)
- **Bars:** 7–30 (three eight-bar phrases); 37–44 (4+4); 45–47 (three bars) and 48–52 (five bars); 53–60 (4+4, with the climax at 61 on a hypermetric downbeat); 61–64 (2+2: climax, then the quotation on the third bar of the group); 67–70 (horn tail, four bars); 71–82 (4+4+4, with the B♭6 on the downbeat of the third group); coda 83–86, 87–90, 91–92.
- **Reading.** PDF 53–54: strong odd-numbered bars in four-bar groups, phrases ending on tonal goals, three- and five-bar groups allowed. The only irregular groups are in the middle section (45–52), where the quotation is held back. That is the part of the movement where irregularity serves the form. The themes themselves are square, as in every model on this lens's list (Dvořák 9/ii 7–18, Ex. 3-13). For a tune meant to be remembered, that is correct.
- **Fix:** none.

### 6. Memorability verdict against the models (recorded)
Theme A has a single rhythmic signature (`4. 8 4~`) in bars 1, 3 and 5 of its eight bars and one chromatic hook (E♭–D–D♭–C). It peaks on a weak beat and has its strongest form held for the reprise. It is as singable as the Dvořák Largo tune, with fewer repeats of its cell: Dvořák uses `8. 16 4` in 10 of 12 bars, this theme uses `4. 8` in 3 of 8. Theme B is more lyrical and less rhythmic. Its `8 8` neighbour cell recurs at 37, 38, 40 and 42, and 38/40 rhyme (c081). A listener will leave humming theme A, and that is the right balance for a main theme and its contrasting second song. c131 and c141 already recorded theme B's narrow first phrase as design, so I do not raise it again.
