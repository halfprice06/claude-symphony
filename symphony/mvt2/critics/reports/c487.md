**1. Dvořák's Largo gives a muted horn pair an imitation of the theme's head at the end of its A section. This score gives that moment to the bass clarinet. (Recorded, no change.)**

Bars 29-31. Dvořák 9/ii, bars 42-45, just before the C♯-minor middle section at 46:
- Horns 1·2 (in E, con sordini, p<) play `cs8.( e16 e4) cs8( b a4)` twice, then `cs8. e16 e4 cs8. e16 e4` and `cs8. e16 e2.~` (hornImL; hornIImL a third or sixth below).
- At concert pitch this is F4 A♭4 A♭4 F4 E♭4 D♭4: the Largo theme's head (`f4. af8 af2 | f8 ef df2`) in diminution, in thirds.
- Above it the violins close with `ef'4.( df8 ef4 bf) | df2( af) | df( af~ | af4)` (violinImL).

This is the reading's "imitation and freer contrapuntal writing" (PDF 41), done in a slow movement by two soft muted horns, the softest brass weight (PDF 44).

The same point in this score is the end of theme A's first violin statement at 30 and the start of the transition at 31:
- At 29-30, Hn 3·4 only sustain: Hn 3 D♭4 to C4, Hn 4 E♭3.
- At 31, the bass clarinet takes the head (`es,8 f,8 aes,8 c8 | es2.`) and then the motto tail.

So the score already echoes the head at the same seam, but in a woodwind instead of a brass pair. Moving it to horns would break two protected passages: the woodwinds-alone choir at 31-36 (c186, c191) and the plain first statement at 23-30. The bass clarinet already carries the echo, so a horn echo would duplicate it. No change. The comparison is recorded so that the next texture or holistic critic has the corpus reference.

**2. The brass counterpoint that exists is free counterpoint, and every line is independent where it needs to be. (Follows; agrees with c337 Finding 4.)**

**3. At 60 the low brass sustain the harmony rather than adding a line. (Recorded, no change.)**

Tbn 2's E♭3 and G♭3 halves fall on the notes that the cellos and Bsn 1 reach on beats 1 and 3 of the counter-line (E♭3-F3-G♭3). The trombone therefore adds a skeleton of that line, not a new strand. This is the sustaining role of principle 2 (PDF 41), not counterpoint. It also resolves the A♭7 chord's seventh correctly, G♭3 to F3 at 61. The bar is protected: "pp< at 60" (c349, c477 list) and "bar 60 moving brass lines" (c367). No change.

**4. Horn weights follow "two horns = one trombone". (Follows; agrees with c337 Finding 3.)**

At 79-81 the counter-melody pair (Hn 1·2 a2, f) weighs one trombone. Each harmony horn (Hn 3 F4, Hn 4 A♭3, both f) weighs half of that. The counter-melody therefore leads the brass without any dynamic difference, as PDF 44 implies. At 61-62 the f marking on the Hn 3·4 a2 matches Tbn 2's f, and the single Hn 1 and Hn 2 are marked one level up at ff.

**5. Each brass line lies in a register where it is heard at every entry. (Follows; agrees with c337 Finding 6.)**

The 79-81 pair sits in the horn's middle register. It is doubled by Bsn 1 and the cellos, so it carries inside the ff tutti.

**6. There is no imitation, inversion or stretto between groups. (Recorded, no change.)**

Ex. XI-23 and XI-24 build their climaxes by imitative entries and a stretto (PDF 42, 44, 47). This score builds 53-61 by adding groups: strings, then woodwinds, then brass. That order is protected (c124 rule for 37-60; build order 53-61), and c457 records that leaving out wind imitation at 57-60 was a choice. Both of the reading's examples are fast fugatos. Dvořák's slow-movement imitation is the soft horn echo of Finding 1, and this score has its equivalent in the bass clarinet. No change.
