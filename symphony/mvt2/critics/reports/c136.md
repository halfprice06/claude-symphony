## Findings

The brass writing in v136 follows chapters 9–10 almost everywhere. The two findings below are small labelling faults that the reading names directly. Findings 3–5 record checks that need no change, so later critics need not repeat them.

### F1. Bar 91: the full score does not show that horns 3·4 take their mutes off. Applied.

**Problem.** Horns 3·4 play con sord. at 87–90. Their parts say "via sord." at 91 (c128), but in the score this was part-only, so the conductor's score shows the horns ending muted with no instruction to remove the mutes. The score is inconsistent with itself: the strings' "via sord." at 31 is printed in the score.

**Evidence.**
- Adler PDF 295: "when muting devices are called for, the instructions to the performer must be explicitly notated in both the parts and the score."
- Adler PDF 308 and 321: as soon as the mute comes out, "senza sordino" or "open" must appear. The real "senza sord." belongs at Hn 3·4's first entry in movement III (relayed by c088). "via sord." in the rest at 91 is the advance warning, and the score should carry it as the parts do.

**Fix (coda.ily, `codaHnThree` and `codaHnFour`, bar 91).** Remove the part tag on both. This is the same arrangement as "mettere sord." at 82, which is untagged on both horns and prints once on the shared staff:
```
codaHnThree = { ... aes2\> r4\! | R2.^\markup \italic "via sord." | R2.\fermata | }
codaHnFour  = { ... es2\> r4\!  | R2.^\markup \italic "via sord." | R2.\fermata | }
```
I tried tagging only horn 4 first. The part-combiner then drops horn 3's markup on the combined rest, so the text has to be on both. Checked on page 8 of the test build: "via sord." prints once above the Hn 3·4 rest at 91, the page still holds one system, and the Horn 3 and Horn 4 parts still show it.

### F2. Bar 63: the trombone 1 part does not mark the quotation as a solo. Applied (part only).

**Problem.** Trombone 1 first plays at 63, after about four and a half minutes of rest, and its first note is the movement-I quotation, ff marcato and alone over the strings' sf>p tremolo. In the score the shared staff prints "1." and "marcato". The Trombone 1 part prints only "marcato", so the player cannot tell that this is an exposed line, and not a tutti doubling to balance against the orchestra. Every horn solo in this score has "solo" in its part ("solo, dolce espr." at 6; "1. solo" at 67).

**Evidence.** Adler adler2 PDF 6 separates the trombone's roles: soloist, warm harmonic background, contrapuntal partner. A player needs to know which role a passage is. Adler PDF 292 warns that "the full force of a brass section can obliterate the rest of the orchestra", and Ex. X-77 to X-78 (adler2 3, PDF 316) print "Solo" over the trombone solos. At 61–62 this player's colleagues (Tbn 2, B.tbn) play mf as background. At 63 Tbn 1 must lead.

**Fix (bSec.ily, `bSecTbnOne`, bar 63):**
```
r8 e8->\ff^\markup \italic "marcato"-\tag #'part ^\markup \italic "solo" c'4.-> b8 bes8 a8 |
```
Part-only, so the score, the layout and the protected quotation's notes and dynamics are unchanged. Checked in the regenerated parts book (page 19).

### F5. Not a finding: Hn 4's octave from 60 to 61.

Horn 4 goes from written E♭4 p< to written E♭5 f across a breath. Adler PDF 304 warns against wide leaps, but an octave on one fingering is the horn's most natural interval (partials 6 to 12 on the same valve). The a2 at 61–62 is settled (c064/c069/c086). No change.
