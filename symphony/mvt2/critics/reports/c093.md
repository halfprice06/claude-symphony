## Findings

### 1. Bar 61: the D♭ cadence keeps 5̂ on top. Keep it; do not reopen (no change)

**Bars:** 60–61. **Point:** At the movement's main cadence in the new key, Fl 1 leaves the leading tone C6 (60) for A♭6 (61), so the highest note of the ff arrival is 5̂ and not 1̂. Taken literally, A&S PDF 219 §9, PDF 226 §12 and Points for Review 6 (PDF 228) ask for a melodic motion to 1̂.

**Why no change:** The reading also says the melody can do this in the leading line rather than the highest instrument. Vln I carries C6–D♭6 at full strength (c039, c059), and the cadence is a root-position V7–I with that 7̂–1̂. PDF 218 describes a cadence that does not close the tension and serves as "a springboard for a modulation to yet another key area". That is exactly what 61 does: within one bar it moves to F♭ (62) and A minor (63). c073 protected the "IAC at the climax 61, which keeps the climax open", and the reading supports that reason. Fl 1's A♭6 is also the start of the protected A♭6→A6 thread (c070) and the registral peak c051 asked for. A fix that delays A♭6 to beat 3 (`des'''2 aes'''2~`) would give a textbook PAC, but it closes a key area the form wants to leave at once and takes the peak off the downbeat. Record this as settled.

### 2. Open item "bass rest under pickup at 70": close it and keep the bass E♭ (no change)

**Bars:** 70 b3 → 71. **Point:** c059 (Adler, the Strauss example) proposed silencing the double basses under the violins' E♭–F pickup to the reprise. At 70 b3 the Cb E♭ is now the only sustained note under the pickup. Horn, bassoons and timpani stop on beat 3, and Bsn 2's D♭3 resolves to C3 at 71 after one beat (c052).

**Evidence:** A&S PDF 219–220 §10. After a long key area (here 28 bars of D♭ plus A minor), the dominant must be kept as the dominant of the main key until the tonic arrives: "V7 has so strong a drive toward I that the listener is prepared for the return". If the bass is cut at 70 b3, the pickup sounds over nothing and the return is set up by the violins alone. That is the effect A&S describe for a *brief* sojourn (§10, first paragraph), not for this one. c082 analysed the pickup's F as an unprepared 9th over the dominant (A&S Unit 27), which needs the E♭ under it. c059's own test also produced a false parallel in analysis.txt. Keep `retrCb` as it is and remove the item from the open list.

### 3. Open item "ii6 at 45": close it and keep vi (no change)

**Bars:** 44–45. **Point:** c012 suggested ii6 instead of the B♭-minor chord at the start of B2.

**Evidence:** The consequent of B1 ends on V at 44 (C.a. C4 over A♭). The next phrase starts on vi: B♭ in Bsn 2 and Cb, with the theme-B head D♭–E♭–D♭–C in Ob 1, Bsn 1 and Vc. That is the deceptive V→vi across the phrase join, the move PDF 219 §9 uses to extend the key area before the authentic cadence comes (here at 53 and 61). It also makes vi the springboard of PDF 218 for the remote keys of B2 (B♭ minor → G♭ = enharmonic F♯ → C♯7 at 47). ii6 has G♭ in the bass. That would anticipate the G♭ of 46 b3, so the bass would stand still across 45–46, and V–ii6 at a phrase start is a weaker continuation than V–vi. Keep 45 as it is.

### 4. Bars 31–36: the tonic stays stable before the phrase modulation; do not add a 5–6 (no change)

**Bars:** 30–36. **Point:** From the PAC at 30 to the new key at 37 the music stays on A♭ for seven bars (about 25 s), and 35–36 is a bare A♭–E♭ fifth under the bass clarinet's A♭–B♭–C. A&S PDF 217 (§8, Ex. 14-6 to 14-8) gives I5–6 as the standard way to make a tonic that has become a goal "active" before a modulation. I tested a 5–6 on paper: Bsn 1 `es2( f4~ | f2) r4` at 35–36, giving I–vi6–IV into 37.

**Why no change:** The motion is already there. At 33–34 Bsn 1 goes A♭→F→E♭ over the pedal (6–5), so the F has just been heard and resolved. A second E♭→F would repeat the same neighbour two bars later and blur the stable tonic that c020 and c023 chose deliberately for a phrase modulation at 37 (PDF 223: "the section … begins anew, without any transition"). The 36→37 join already does what PDF 216 §6 asks for: the bass clarinet's C4 leads to D♭4 (melody to the new 1̂), and the metre, tempo, theme and scoring all change at a double bar.

### 5. Bars 63–64: a two-bar tonicization carries a key signature (no change)

**Bars:** 63–64. **Point:** By PDF 213 §4 ("not just one or two chords"), A minor is a tonicization. It has i, vii°7 and V7, never returns to i, and slips to E♭ at 65. A key signature usually marks a modulation.

**Why no change:** Writing these bars without a signature would mean either the five-flat D♭ signature with naturals on A, B, D, E and G♯ throughout, or an early three-flat signature that announces A♭ two bars before it arrives. Both are harder to read than two bars of A minor. The trombone's ff quotation reads cleanly in the A minor signature, and the cautionary signatures at 64 are already protected (c045). Keep the notation.
