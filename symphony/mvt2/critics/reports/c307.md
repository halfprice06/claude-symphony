## Findings

All are recorded or "no change". Nothing is applied, because every candidate is either already settled with a reason I cannot answer, or lies inside a protected passage.

### 1. Chordal third (PDF 144–145): 13 beats lack any third or sixth. All are passing beats or by design (no change)

**Evidence.** The scan flagged 6 b3, 10 b3, 22 b2, 34 b3, 35–36 (the woodwind choir's open A♭–E♭, by design), 37 b4, 45 b4, 70 b3 and 83 b3. At 22 b2 the upper cellos' G3 leaves for B♭3 for one beat inside their answer figure, and G sounds again on b3. At 37 b4 the flute's E♭5 is a passing note and the viola's F4 falls on beats 1 and 3. At 45 b4 the melody's passing C replaces D♭ in all three octaves. Moving the violas to D♭3 there would put a minor ninth under the cellos' C4, so I do not propose it. c272's open point (the third only in the violas' off-beats at 50–51) is now closed in practice. c275 put A5 in Vln II at 50 b2, and c274 put C3 in the cellos on 51 b1. I recommend closing that point in HISTORY.md.

### 2. Rule 7 (PDF 140, 145–146): cellos on the bass's pitch class on beat 1 in 27 of 56 shared bars (no change; recorded exception stands)

**Evidence.** Ten of the 27 are the coda 83–92. c167 #3 recorded those as an exception under footnote 2 (PDF 139) and Ex. 204 (PDF 157), and the composer accepted that "so that it is not raised again". The corpus supports it. Dvořák's last chord also puts cellos over the basses (corpus bar 101 on), though he divides them D♭/A♭. Five more are downbeats where the cello counter-melody meets the bass an octave higher: 24, 25 and 72–74 (F, A♭, B♭). These are points of a contrapuntal line, not habitual doubling, and the counter-melodies are protected. The rest (46–47, 49–50, 57, 59, 63–64, 79) lie inside the c124 area or are tutti and quotation bars. No change.

### 3. Winds on string lines (PDF 164, 166): dynamics and horn use (no change)

**Evidence.** Wherever a wind doubles a string lead at p–mf, it is one level softer: Fl 1 p< against Vln I mf< at 57, Hn 1 mp against the cellos at 57, and Hn 2 p<mp under the cellos at 75–77 (c217). Equal levels come only at f and ff (59, 78, 79–81), where Sebesky's reason (purity at soft levels) no longer applies, and c167 recorded them. The horn sits on the cello line in about 13 of 92 bars, always at a build. That meets "use sparingly".

### 4. Rule 6 (PDF 140; Ex. 214 bars 11–12, PDF 168): unison violins at 53–56 and 75–77 (no change)

**Evidence.** In both places the violins play in unison with no lower octave. At 53–56 the theme returns at p–mp. The counter-line is in Hn 1/Cl 1 in sixths with the theme (c201), which is Ex. 214's own device (the lead horn "moves downward in parallel sixths with the lead violins"). The octaves arrive at 57 with the crescendo, as rule 6 asks for a "particularly strong ensemble sound". At 75–77 c299 put Vln I at pitch so that B♭6 is first heard at 79 (Ex. 215: intensity added bar by bar). c217 #6 and c297 #1 already recorded 53–56. No change.

### 5. Climax and quotation, 63–64 and 79–81 (PDF 149, 168, 173): two settled departures (no change)

**Evidence.** At 79–81 the strings alone sound only B♭ and D♭, and the winds and horns complete the chord. Sebesky's "each group complete" (PDF 149) is the rule for a string section heard alone. In a tutti the reading's stronger rule is "voice the strings as simply as possible in the high register" (PDF 168), which the three-octave B♭ follows. At 63–64 Ex. 216 would tacet the cellos under the trombone. Here the cellos' A2 tremolo stays below the trombone's E3–C4, and the violas' E4/C5 sits just above it at sf>p, with a MIDI fade (c300). c217 #4 left this as an option, and c268 protected the string tremolo. No change.

### 6. Reduced forces and antiphony (PDF 139 footnote, 162–163): soli plan matches the reading and Dvořák (no change)

**Evidence.** The solo violin against the muted section (17–22) is Ex. 212's statement and answer. The four solo basses (65–70, 87–92) and four solo cellos (83–86) are never fewer than four players, so footnote 2 (PDF 139) is respected. The step-by-step thinning matches Dvořák 9/ii from bar 101 ("4 cellos", "2 basses", "2 cellos", "1 cello"). Nothing to change.
