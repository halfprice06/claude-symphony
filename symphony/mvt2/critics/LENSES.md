# Critic lenses (cycle N uses lens ((N-1) mod 10) + 1)
1. Melody and memorability: themes A and B, their contour, rhythm, climaxes and continuation; would a listener hum them afterwards? Compare with the great slow-movement tunes of Dvořák, Tchaikovsky, Brahms, Rachmaninoff and Elgar.
2. Harmony and voice leading: progressions, chromatic lines, pedals, inner voices, dissonance treatment, the modulation plan (A♭ – D♭ – F♯ minor – A minor – A♭) and the common-tone link from movement I.
3. Form, pacing and proportion: section lengths, the tension curve, transitions, the middle section's build and climax, the return, the coda. Compare with corpus slow movements.
4. Orchestration: balance, doubling, register, colour, each section's use, the climax, the string-free passages. Sources: Rimsky-Korsakov and Adler.
5. Engraving and notation (Gould's *Behind Bars*, MOLA): dynamics, hairpins, slurs, beaming, triplets, clefs, part-combining labels, rehearsal and tempo marks, metre changes, system and page layout. Inspect many pages of the PDF.
6. Playability, instrument by instrument: ranges, breathing, bowing, harp pedalling, timpani tuning and retuning, brass endurance, divisi, solos.
7. Texture and counterpoint: inner-voice interest, accompaniment figures, counter-melodies, the bass line, imitation and dialogue between groups.
8. Expression: dynamics, articulation, phrasing slurs, tempo changes, rubato indications and expressive text.
9. Holistic comparison with one specific master slow movement, a different one each time this lens comes up. Would a conductor believe a master wrote this? Also compare with the version 10 cycles earlier and flag anything that got worse.
10. Cyclic design and emotional arc: the link from movement I, the motto's tail, the two quotations, the coda, and how this movement sits between movement I and the scherzo (movement III), with the finale (movement IV) after it; read symphony/coherence/*.md.
