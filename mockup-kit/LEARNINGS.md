## What a listener noticed that measurement missed

Nobody in the loop could hear; Dan listened to a few renders. Each of his notes named a problem that the loop's measurements either did not look for or had passed. These are the most useful lessons for the next mockup: ask a person to listen early, and add a measurement for each kind of problem below from the first cycle.

- **Accents with too much attack** (Dan, 2026-10-10, on the earlier sample-player renders of movement I; carried into this loop as rule 4 of the brief). The accent target then was a peak boost of 3 dB or more, and accents reached 6-8 dB with a sharp front edge. Fix: accents are emphasis and weight, about 2-4 dB, with no hard transient; put the weight in the volume envelope, not in velocity or a louder layer (section 7).
- **Timpani rolls too loud and growing** (Dan, 2026-10-09, earlier renders; rule 5 of the brief). Long rolls covered the orchestra. Fix: a roll starts at its printed dynamic and settles under the texture in the volume envelope, and only a printed crescendo makes it grow (section 10). The rule can overshoot: by c25 the ppp rolls sat 14-23 dB under the orchestra and needed +3-4 dB back (c25).
- **Soft passages too quiet for headphones** (Dan, after c07: he had to turn the volume to maximum to hear the soft parts, which made the loud parts painful). The master's loudness range (23.6 LU) was right for a concert recording and had been measured as correct. Fix: a delivery master with a slow gain ride on the quiet passages only, plus soft-level trims in the mix; master LRA about 23.6 LU, delivered file 14.4-16.3 LU from c08 on (16.0 in c26), with the climax kept (section "Delivery master").
- **A "bowing" pulse on the final held chord** (Dan, after c24). Each string section held one sample for 15-25 s, so the 3.95 s sustain loops of the sections ran in step and the tone colour cycled together. A level profile (c17) had already flattened the loudness part of the loop, so the level measurements passed; the colour cycle remained (spectral centroid autocorrelation at the loop period 0.39-0.63 per stem). Fix: split the held notes into staggered same-pitch re-bows at different times per section, with a fitted level shape for each re-bow; master 100-300 Hz loop autocorrelation 0.58 -> 0.24 (c26; sections 6, 16). Not yet heard by Dan.
- **Harsh trombone accents at the climax** (Dan, after c24). The accents passed the 2-4 dB level rule (bodies 1-3 dB over the surrounding marcato notes in c25), but the accent sample at a raised CC11 (the fff layer) put a 2-5 kHz burst 7-13 dB over the note's own body. Fix: plain Sus MAX at lower velocity, CC11 at the printed dynamic, and a short swell in the volume envelope; 2-5 kHz attack minus body went to -1..-9 dB, and the master's 2-5 kHz jump at the worst accent from +10 to +2 dB (c26; section 7). Not yet heard by Dan.
- **Too wet and mushy** (Dan, 2026-11-07, on Movement I c42 and Movement II c37). The shared hall at `fx_volume` 0.19 had passed every check, yet the numbers already pointed at it: 300 ms after a cutoff the tail had dropped only 9-10 dB against 13-17 dB in a real recording, and the late decay ran 2.5 s against 1.5-1.95 s (c05, c17; section 12). Fix for every movement: `fx_volume` 0.19 -> 0.095 (-6 dB on the hall return; Opus mixer volumes are linear amplitude, e.g. Main 0.28 against Close 1.0 measured 11 dB under), sends unchanged, a full rebuild of every track. Check: the master's drop 0.3 s after each tutti cutoff at least 13 dB, and the repeated-note dips (S1) against the previous render. Also from 2026-11-07: a 160 Hz low cut on the hall input (`reverb.low_cut_hz`; the Opus convolution reverb's Bandpass also has `lowFilterFreq` / `lowCutFreq`, factory 30 Hz), so the bass reaches the hall less. If the late decay still runs over 2 s, shorten the IR with Opus's IR envelope (Simple: one Decay slider for length and fade-out; Full: end time and fade-out; Opus manual p.133-134) rather than changing halls. First result (scherzo c01, fx_volume 0.095 + 160 Hz low cut): tutti cutoff drops 13.9-16.2 dB (10.3-11.7 at 0.19), late decay T60 unchanged at 3.5-3.7 s, so the level change does not shorten the hall; only the IR envelope can. Movement I c44 found the envelope: the ConvolutionReverb insert's `irSetup` dict (`reverb.ir_setup` in `mix.json`). `simpleDecay` does nothing in any `envelopeMode`; `endPos` 0.5 with `decayLength` 0.5 in mode 1 or 2 took a test track's late slope from 2.61 to 1.54 s, and with the drier return the master's drop 0.3 s after a tutti cutoff went from 13.0-14.1 to 17.8-20.8 dB (fermata late RT 2.13 -> 1.78 s, -5..-25 dB slope 2.42 -> 1.29 s). The other half of the mush was the samples' own releases: string Sus MAX ships 323-931 ms and horns 316-562 ms. Capping them at 150 ms (strings) and 237 ms (horns) took the stem drop 0.15 s after a phrase end from -4..-9 to -8.5..-13 dB, with no ending falling over 10 dB in its first 0.05 s (so none sounds clipped). Movement II c38 (drier hall alone) measured a stem median drop of 17.5 dB at +0.3 s and a late decay of 1.19 s.

What these have in common: the loop measured level, timing and loudness range well, and missed periodic movement and changes in tone colour (spectrum over time). For the next mockup, measure from the start the 2-5 kHz attack energy of every accent against its body, the spectral centroid and band levels folded at the sample loop period on every note longer than about 6 s, and the delivered file at one listening volume.

## 1. Loading Opus patches by script

- Patch loading is scriptable. Opus's VST3 state is gzip-compressed JSON (a `Performance` with a `zones` list); write it with `TrackFX_SetNamedConfigParm(tr, 0, "vst_chunk", base64)` (c00).
- An `.oib` patch file is a 20-byte header followed by zlib/gzip JSON holding one `Instrument`; the plugin state is a 28-byte header, the gzip JSON, and a tail (`opus_state.py`: `HDR = 28`, `load_oib` reads from byte 20).
- Put the patch into a zone as `{"class": "InstrumentZone", "keyMin": 0, "keyMax": 127, "velMin": 1, "velMax": 127, "octave": 0, "groupIndex": 0, "instrument": <oib JSON>}` (c00).
- The first 4 bytes of the state (little-endian u32) must equal the total blob length minus 16, or Opus truncates the JSON (c00).
- A zone that holds only a locator path, without the full instrument JSON, crashes Opus (c00).
- Everything a patch does is editable in that JSON before loading: articulation rows, mic columns and volumes, per-mic pans, the main channel pan, mixer inserts (reverb, sends, the hall's high cut), script filters (EWSO, RTKiller, ConSordino, FingerPosition), and the starting keyswitch (c01-c13).
- Check `mixer.channels[main].pan` of every patch you load. The Hollywood Strings DIV A KS Masters ship with the main (Master) channel panned (1st Violins DIV A -0.74, Celli DIV A +0.33); every section and solo patch used here had it at 0. The main channel pans after the reverb return, so a seat set on the mic channels does not undo it: a solo track on 1st Violins DIV A measured 21 dB R-L, hall included, and -5 dB after setting pan 0, panLeft -1, panRight 1 (c13).
- Read keyswitch maps and sample ranges from the `.oib` files, not from screenshots or memory (`opus/dump_ks.py`, `ks_maps.json`; patches added later go into `ks_maps_extra.json`). The Harp Maestro range error (section 9) was found by reading every group's keyMin/keyMax from the file (c01).
- Any state change (mics, reverb, rows, pans inside Opus) needs the patch reloaded; a cache that reuses old state files hides the change. The c00 build reused existing `.b64` files, so state edits had no effect until the cache check was removed (c01 critic C).
- Write gzip with `mtime=0` so a generated state is byte-for-byte reproducible. The build can then compare the new state with the last one written and skip the patch reload when they are equal (c07, `build_reaper.py --midi-only`). Before a build, list which states will change (c13 `statecheck.py`): loading one new articulation row means a full rebuild of that track (c11, c12). Reloading a patch is expensive in memory (section 11).

## 2. KS Master patches: articulation rows and keyswitches

- A KS Master loads only its first rows by default: the string KS Masters load six (Sus MAX, Sus Marc LITE, Leg Slur MAX, Staccatissimo, Stac On Bow, Marc Shrt). Keyswitches to unloaded rows are ignored and the current articulation keeps playing (c02: pizz. notes measured as bowed sustains, time to peak 600-720 ms, decay 1-1.7 dB).
- To load a row, set `layerMatrix.rows[i].loaded = true` and `active = true`, and on the matching `articulations.trigger` entry set `useKey = true` and a `triggerName` (c02). After this, pizz. decayed 10-16 dB within 400 ms of the peak and onsets rose in 0-10 ms (c02, c03).
- Match row names case-insensitively: the cor anglais row is "Sus Max", not "Sus MAX" (c02).
- Opus names keys with C0 = MIDI 24 (`triggerName = NOTE[key % 12] + str(key // 12 - 2)`). String KS Master keyswitches start at 24; examples: 1st Violins Leg BC MAX 48, Slur Runs 42, 2nd Violins Leg Port MAX 46; Celli KS Master keyswitches sit at 80-90 (Leg Port MAX 89); Harp KS Master keyswitches are 21-35 (`ks_maps.json`). The DIV A KS Masters use the same map as their section (c10, c13).
- Setting `articulations.currentKeySwitch` in the state does not reliably select the first articulation. Trombone 1's first two Sus Accent notes (selected only by state) measured -8.8 / -8.6 dB attack brightness against +5.6 dB for the same articulation reached by a keyswitch (c03). Send every track's first keyswitch as a MIDI note in a silent pre-roll bar (section 11; c06). The `currentTrigger` and `lastArticulationId` fields were not tested.
- Keyswitch notes of velocity 1 and 0.03 qn length work (c00-c13).
- Put keyswitches and CC15 changes in the preceding rest (rest start + min(0.5 qn, 25% of the rest)), not on the note onset. In c00, six of eight 535-650 ms late entries coincided with a CC15 change on the note (c01 critic A; correlation only, cause not isolated).
- With a rest before the note, a keyswitch lead of 55 ms or more was always enough (c03: median lead 69 ms, minimum 55 ms). A re-tongue keyswitch 0.012 qn before the note worked inside a wind portato passage (c07). Unverified: whether a 3 ms lead fails.
- Do not send a keyswitch while a note on a legato patch is still sounding; end the legato note 0.01 qn before the keyswitch (c05 guard). Every held-note-to-tremolo check passed in the renders made after the guard without memory pressure (c05, c07-c13), but the guard's effect was not isolated from the session restart (section 11). The exception that worked is switching between two legato rows for one target (Leg Port MAX, section 7).

## 3. Controllers in Hollywood Orchestra

- CC11 (Expression) crossfades the dynamic layers and also sets volume (HO manual 2.1.3) in the string, flute, oboe, cor anglais, bassoon, bass clarinet and contrabassoon KS Masters. **Not in the Hollywood Brass KS Masters (1FH, 1TP, 1TB, 1TU) or the Clarinet KS Master: there CC1 crossfades the layers** and CC11 drives only the Global Group volume (mvt1 c09, read from each layer group's `cc 1` rescaler in the `.oib`; section 20). Movement II held CC1 at 53-89 on these patches, so its brass and clarinets played the mp-f samples at every dynamic. Read each patch's layer controller from the `.oib` before the first build.
- CC1 (Mod wheel) is not a vibrato-depth control. In the flute, oboe, cor anglais, bassoon and contrabassoon KS Masters it crossfades a "Sus Non Vib" and a "Sus Vib" layer (`Amount Controller 1->Volume` tables): Vib fades in over CC1 55-64, Non Vib fades out over 64-76, and from 77 up only Vib sounds, so CC1 80-127 all sound the same (c17, read from the `.oib` files). The clarinet and bass clarinet KS Masters have no Vib layer. Read the CC tables in the `.oib` before planning any CC1 change.
- Do not hold CC1 inside that crossfade. At CC1 67 (both layers sounding) a ff flute's 1.5-4 kHz amplitude modulation rose from 10.5 to 16.9 dB p95-p5 and the stem 2.2 dB louder (c17); removing the cap brought it to 8.1 dB with a regular 4.3 Hz vibrato (c18). Inferred: the two layers beat against each other. To reduce vibrato, test CC1 54 or lower (Non Vib only); not tried.
- CC7 is MIDI volume, and Opus applies it as linear amplitude: the level follows 20·log10(CC7/127) (c13: fitted slopes 0.8-1.1 inside timpani rolls; c02: CC7 16 -> 13 measured 0.6-1.5 dB, where 20·log10 predicts 1.8). A CC7 lane built as 127·10^(dB/40) therefore delivers half of each planned dB change. Use 127·10^(dB/20), or put exact dB in a REAPER volume envelope.
- CC15 drives the Con Sordino script in the string KS Masters (c02). It is a filter emulation, not muted samples, and it does not lower the level much. In the Solo French Horn KS Master it does nothing: muted and open notes at the same CC11 matched in spectrum to 0.1 dB (c09); the script is a strings EQ. For horn mutes use a second ReaEQ after Opus (low shelf 300 Hz -6, bell 1.8 kHz +3, high shelf 4.5 kHz -8) and automate its Wet parameter from 0 to 1 at the con sord. mark: the 2-8 kHz to f0..4f0 ratio moved 8 dB (c09: -17.7 -> -25.8 dB), and the EQ lowered the level enough that no extra role cut was needed.
- CC70 is Finger Position in the string KS Masters: 0-38 FP1, 39-76 FP2, 77-114 FP3, 115-127 FP4 (HO manual pp. 92-95). Higher positions move notes to lower strings. Used for sul D/G/C (127) and sul A (20 up to MIDI 74, 127 above). Unverified: no stem difference was measured.
- Other mapped controls in the string KS Masters (`ks_maps.json`): CC5 Legato Time, CC22 True Legato mono/poly (below 64 = polyphonic, HO manual p. 91), CC64 Repetition Sustain, CC65 Portamento, CC68 Legato, CC69 Repetition, CC36 Reset RR. Apart from CC36 and CC69 (next point), none of these were tested; portamento was done with the Leg Port MAX row instead (section 7).
- Send CC36 = 127 (Reset RR, in every HO KS Master's `controllerMap`) on every track and CC69 = 0 (Repetition script off) on the strings in the pre-roll, so each render starts from the same round-robin position (`RR_RESET_QN`, `REP_OFF_FAMILIES` in `build_reaper.py`). Two renders of unchanged string data were then 96-99% the same in 50 ms frames, where before most frames differed; it changed no levels (c24, c25). Winds that got only CC36 still differed in 12-20% of frames (Flute 1, Bassoon 1); CC69 = 0 on the winds was not tried. Reproducible renders make small changes measurable by comparing two renders.
- Velocity: on legato targets it is meant to set transition speed (HO manual 2.1.1), but see section 5 for what was measured. On short articulations, harp and timpani it sets loudness.
- Velocity on the felt timpani and harp is the dynamic. Harp CC11 should be held constant, or soft notes are attenuated twice (c01: Harp Maestro measured 19 dB from pp to mp against about 7 dB for strings).
- Small velocity steps do not give metric weight to plucked notes. +6/+3/-3 by beat on pizz. and harp notes gave the downbeats only 0.5-1.2 dB more peak (2-3 dB planned) and raised the basses' pizz. peak spread from 1.7 to 3.3 dB, because round robins vary more than the steps (c16, reverted).
- Metric weight on pizz. and harp lines works as a level step in the volume envelope: +1.5 dB on the downbeat, +0.5 on beat 3 of 4/4, -1 off the beat, at full value on the attack and held to the next onset, once per onset, skipping accents and lead lines. Downbeat minus the rest of the bar went from -1.6..+1.3 to +0.5..+2.9 dB, and the onset spread did not grow (c20, `METRIC_DB`).

## 4. CC11 response and per-track level

- CC11 is far from linear in dB, and it flattens at the top. Within 115 sustained notes it moved the level 5.7 dB per 10 CC at CC 0-40, 3.5 at 30-60, 2.5 at 50-80 and 1.2 at 88-128; wind patches averaged 1.8 (c06 critic C). Crescendos starting at CC 72 or above grew a median 3.3 dB, those starting under 60 grew 5.9 dB (c06 critic C).
- The c02 finding that strings barely respond below CC 40 (about 1.1 dB per 14 CC inside single notes) was from a few notes and is not borne out by the c06 measurement over 115 notes. Use the volume envelope for exact fades below pp either way.
- The level per CC11 step differs by patch: fitted 0.17-0.36 dB/CC across tracks (c02: Oboe 0.175, Trombone 0.173, Vln I 0.300, Flute 0.364, Celli 0.358). Later fits: violins 0.23-0.25, violas 0.31-0.325, clarinet 0.17, clarinet 2 0.20, bass clarinet 0.26, bassoons 0.30-0.34, oboe/contrabassoon 0.19-0.22, horns 0.27-0.34 (c03, c05, c06). Balance set at one dynamic therefore drifts at another.
- Some patches jump in mid-range: from the CC 52-64 bin to the 64-80 bin, bass clarinet +10.9 dB, Flute +7.6, Clarinet 2 +7.6, celli +7.3 (c02).
- The dynamic layer crossfades can be read from the patch file: each layer group (e.g. "Bs mp", "Bs f") has a 128-entry CC11 rescaler `tableValues` on its Source volume. In the Bassoon, Bass clarinet, Contrabassoon and Clarinet KS Masters the next layer fades in right at the printed pp/p (CC11 30-36; Bassoon "Bs f" 0.12 at CC 34, 0.37 at 36, 0.6 at 38), so a pp swell of +4 CC adds a louder layer: +8-11 dB on one bassoon pitch (c22). Read the tables before writing any CC11 swell near a boundary.
- Keep soft swells off a layer boundary by holding CC11 at the note's starting value (for long notes starting at CC 40 or less) and putting the swell in the volume envelope: the median pp swell went from +3.3 to +1.2 dB over 50 notes (c23, `SWELL_CC_CAP` "*"). A cap at CC 36 recovered only 2 dB, because the boundary starts at 33 (c22). Some samples swell by themselves: Bassoon B♭2 still rose 7 dB at a flat CC 33 and needs a level shape (c23).
- At pp-p the Basses KS Master sits 5-12 dB under the Celli KS Master at the same CC11; at f the gap is 0-3 dB (c06). Soft passages need about +3 to +5 dB on the basses (c06: Cb minus Vc over 18 soft bars, median -8.1 -> -2.6 dB).
- The top layer runs to CC 127 (FL KS Master layer ranges 0-55, 56-93, 94-127). With ff at CC 104, f to ff measured only +2.5 LU; ff at 114 and fff at 124 restored the growth (c02).
- A dynamic table that worked: ppp 22, pp 34, p 46, mp 58, mf 72, f 88, ff 114, fff 124 (c02-c13).
- A hairpin without a printed target that moves 14 CC measured a median +2.6 dB, and 38 of 95 moved less than 1.5 dB or the wrong way; targeted hairpins of more than 16 CC measured a median 7 dB (c02). Use about 22 CC for untargeted hairpins from mp or softer (c02).
- An untargeted swell must not overshoot a later printed goal, or the goal sounds no louder. Look ahead to the next rest of 2 qn or more (at most 16 qn): from mp or louder, cap the swell 8 CC under the next louder mark; from p or softer, cap it at the mark itself, because an 8 CC margin flattened short solo swells (Horn CC 68 -> 52) (c11). CC11 capping plus +2 dB at mf arrivals raised the goal only about 1 dB against the phrase start (c11); per-note level trims on the phrase's notes did the rest (c12: mf goal 3.7 dB over the phrase start).
- An untargeted < followed within 0.3 qn by an untargeted > (messa di voce) must return to its starting level; with +22 / -14 CC steps the line ratcheted up one dynamic level and the next printed mp sounded no louder (c09).
- Soft hairpins in exposed lead lines need a dB ramp as well as CC11. At p, CC11 moves only 0.1-0.2 dB per step and sample unevenness of 2-4 dB then decides the direction. +3 dB on crescendos starting at CC11 66 or lower (in a messa di voce, released across the decrescendo) and -2 dB on lone decrescendos took the median lead-line crescendo from +1.7 to +5.3 dB; the delivered LRA rose only 15.8 -> 16.0 LU (c14).
- That gain also lifts unmarked < > swells early in a phrase, which then outgrow the phrase's printed peak (c14: an mp crest 2.4 dB under an earlier swell that got +3). List such swells and give them no soft gain: the horn swell then sat 2-3 dB under its printed crest (c15).
- Any per-note shaping on the same notes cancels a soft hairpin's dB ramp: phrase-top lifts by pitch, a hot-pitch trim with an early/body shape, or a note trim. Six solo crescendos with a +3 dB ramp measured -1.7..+2.0 dB; removing the pitch-contour lift inside soft lead hairpins and raising the ramp to 4-6 dB gave +0.7..+4.5 dB (c22). A sustain sag in the sample (flute F5) still took 2-4 dB.
- Accompaniment soft hairpins need the ramp too: +2.5 dB on 131 wind, brass and string soft hairpins outside the lead lines raised section crescendos from +0.7 to +2.5 dB (c22). Exclude a decrescendo that runs into the final chord: the ramp's release after it raised a fermata chord by 2.3 dB.
- When per-note trims on a soft swell are larger than the hairpin's own gain, the swell plays backwards. A +2.5 dB messa di voce in the envelope, released to -1 dB across the decrescendo, turned a violin < > the printed way (+1.1 / -2.3 dB) with the trims left in place (c18).
- In lead-line slurs of 4 or more notes, clip(0.3 dB x semitones above the slur's mean pitch, -1.5, +2) dB, stepped at the moved onsets, raised the top note over the rest of its slur from a median +1.3 to +3.7 dB; it became the loudest note in 13 of 23 slurs (9 of 25 before), with no change to the delivered LRA (c18). Turn it off inside soft lead hairpins (c22, above).
- Under "molto sostenuto" (long tutti notes), a +2.5 dB ramp from 40% of each note that falls back to 0 at the next onset made every new chord start under the end of the old one (master -1.7 dB at a bar line). Hold the ramp through the next onset (+0.25 qn) and release it at 40% of the next note: the median step went -1.6 -> -0.8 dB and falls of 4 dB or more 13 -> 8 (c14, c15). The remaining 5.5-6.7 dB falls on Flute 2, Bassoon 2 and Horn 3 were the samples' own (c16).
- Because CC11 flattens at the top, crescendos to f or louder need a dB ramp in the volume envelope as well: +4 dB winds, +3 strings, +2 brass, rising across the hairpin (c06). Crescendos arriving on mf took {wind 2, string 2, brass 1} dB (c11). Wind growth over the bar-60 crescendo went from -1..+0.7 to +1.7..+3.6 dB (c06).
- Hold that ramp until the first onset after the arrival note (cap 4 qn), then release it over 1 qn. Releasing from the arrival note itself made every f/ff arrival fall 3-6 dB in its first beat; holding it cut the falls to 1-3.6 dB and raised the climax bar 2.4 dB (c08). Do not release over 0.25 qn: that sounds like fp (c06).
- Consecutive crescendos to f or louder with no softer mark or decrescendo between them (within 6 qn) should chain: the first holds its dB gain without release and the second starts from it (c09: wind section at the arrival -30.8 -> -27.8 dB, fall into it 4.6 -> 2.1 dB). Let the second one add more on the winds (+0.75 x the gain, c10: wind slopes +1.1..+3.4 dB) after simulating the peak; a string rise there simulated the downbeat 2 dB over the old peak (c10). The same applies to an mf arrival followed by another crescendo: hold the mf gain to the next crescendo's start and release it while the new ramp rises (`HP_MF_BRIDGE_REL`); a 5.5 dB sag between them went to 4.8 dB (c22).
- A crescendo's arrival note must not take a long-note CC11 swell that falls back: a +4 then -6 CC swell put a printed f arrival under the bar before it. Holding the swell on the arrival note, with -3 dB at the start of the three-bar crescendo opening over its last 5.5 qn and +1.5 dB on the arrival bar, took the delivered rise over that crescendo from +1.5 to +5.4 dB, each bar louder than the one before (c20, `ARRIVAL_HOLD_SPANS`).
- When the instruments of a ff tutti chord arrive from different crescendos, some carry the crescendo's dB gain and some enter cold. The single winds' levels spread over 13 dB on one chord; per-track roles evened it to 5 dB and halved the fall into the next bar (2.2 -> 1.2 dB) (c11).
- Check every ff arrival that has no hairpin into it. Cycles of crescendo gain had raised the first ff climax 2.8 dB while the second (no hairpin, so no gain) stayed put, 4.8 LU under the first; +2-2.5 dB on the strings, +2-6 dB on the winds that entered cold and -1 dB on the first climax brought the gap to 1.6 LU and lowered the delivery limiter 3.5 -> 3.0 dB (c12).
- An +8 CC11 boost is about +2.5-3 dB at mid-range slopes (c01 critic B). End such boosts at the next dynamic or rest, and fade them out toward f, or they inflate climaxes (c02: celli 5-10 dB over the tune from a cantabile boost that ran 40 qn).
- Keep CC11 as the layer control and make balance corrections with level only (track volume envelope), so the timbre of each printed dynamic is kept, and the level shaping can be measured on the post-fader stems against the plan (c02-c13).
- Per-track dynamic trims: a trim that is 0 dB at p (CC 46) and reaches a set value at f (CC 88) and ff (CC 114), read from the printed dynamic curve (not from CC11, so accents do not move it), corrected the slope differences (c02-c04). Soft trims need their own anchors (ppp/pp/p) with per-track overrides; a single soft trim for all winds left the bassoons 5-9 dB under the clarinets at pp (c06). A trim that tapers linearly from the p anchor to the f anchor still adds most of the p value at mp (c07 critic B: +3.6 dB at mp from a p +5); check mp passages after setting soft trims.
- Raising the soft levels in the volume envelope narrows the loudness range without changing the CC11 layers. Global soft trims went from {ppp -2, pp 0, p +2} to {+1, +3, +3.5} (c10) and then ppp +3 (c11); per-track overrides moved by the same amounts. Master LRA 25.4 -> 23.9 LU (c06), 23.8 -> 21.6 LU (c10). A commercial recording of a Dvořák symphony movement measured 21.8 LU.
- Adjacent level spans that each ramp in and out on their own are both at full value where they meet, and their dB values add (c09: +9 dB where +4 met +7, -18.5 where -6 met -8). Crossfade a span into the next one over about 0.25 qn instead (c09: values at the boundaries then fell between the two).
- A level span that starts on an sf (for example a divisi or tremolo-chord cut) should start exactly on the sf and crossfade over [sf, sf + 0.25 qn]. Starting it 0.1 qn before left a dip ahead of the accent; starting it after the attack simulated the chord 10-14 dB louder and the master at +2.1 dBFS (c10).
- sf from f or louder: a short spike, then fall 10 CC (not to mf) with the 0.25 qn point at spike - 4. Falling to mf from ff dropped Vln I 8.1 dB in the first beat; the new rule gave -2.8 then -2 to -3 dB per beat (c06).
- A pp chord of many sustained parts can be as loud in short-term loudness as the p solo with thin accompaniment that follows it (c12). Cut the loudest stems of the chord (there the horns, 2-4 dB over the rest) instead of the global ppp trim: lowering ppp by 1.5 dB moved that section only 0.3 dB, because most of the chord was marked pp (c12).
- A printed morendo needs explicit level steps: -1 / -2 / -3 dB on all tracks over its last bars took a 3.6 dB fall to 6.0 dB and the final chord 5.6 dB under the opening chord (c22).
- To make a phrase's printed crest stand out, lower the unmarked swells before it rather than the crest. A -2.5 dB role on a horn solo's p < > swell raised the printed mp crest from +0.9 to +3.4 dB over it, and removing a +2.5 dB swell gain on the violins raised theirs from +0.8 to +2.9 dB; the delivery figures did not move (c24). When a solo crest covers another voice, lower the covering voice or the accompaniment first, and lower a crest only if it then stays 3 dB or more over its own phrase's earlier swells (loop rule from c24).
- An untargeted decrescendo in an inner part falls one dynamic level (14 CC) and can leave a held chord over a tune that falls 5-9 dB through its own printed decrescendo. Give such a hairpin the tutti's arrival dynamic as its target: the chord then fell 1-5 dB with the tune (c26, `HP_TARGET_AT`).
- A level offset (role) that ends on the downbeat of a held note drops that note at once by the role's depth (5.5 dB on a coda clarinet's last note). End lifts at the end of the phrase or at the printed decrescendo, and release them over its length (c25).
- Old negative note trims go stale. Trims set against sample swells in early cycles, with a 0.6 s ramp, later made 4-7 dB holes in the middle of printed crescendos once CC shaping had removed the swells. Measure a note with no trim before adding or keeping a negative trim; on crescendo notes use a step (0.1 s ramp) at a smaller depth, and on a printed decrescendo ramp the trim over the hairpin's length (2.1 s over a 2 qn decrescendo gave an even 4.6 dB fall across the bar) (c25).
- A note trim that ramps in over 0.6 s lets a soft wind entry speak late under strings that re-articulate on the same beat (oboe 4.7 dB under the second violins at the onset); a 0.1 s ramp brought it to -0.9 dB (c24, `NOTE_TRIM_RAMP_S`).

## 5. Legato patches (Leg Slur MAX, Leg BC MAX)

- Legato patches are monophonic: a chord on a legato patch drops notes. Keep chords and simultaneous divisi notes on Sus patches, or split a divisi voice onto its own track (section 8) (c00-c13).
- A legato transition needs overlap; 0.04 qn worked (c00-c13). Apply the overlap only inside one slur, and end the last note of a slur 0.05 qn early when a new slur starts, so the patch re-attacks (c01).
- Never overlap into the same pitch: the old note-off arrives after the new note-on and stops it (c01: dips of -18 to -33 dB; an ff oboe half note fell from -37 to -98 dB).
- The HO legato scripts join only within an octave. A 16-semitone Leg BC MAX step (celli, ppp) sat 18 dB under its body for 0.8 s; the same note on Sus MAX with a 0.12 s entry lead reached body level by +0.2 s (c09). Play steps over 12 semitones as a new entry.
- A disconnected note on Leg Slur MAX plays the sustain layer (HO manual 2.1.3), so one patch can carry a whole line (c02).
- Leg BC MAX (bow-change legato) is in the 1st/2nd Violins, Violas and Celli KS Masters, not in the Basses KS Master. For connected unslurred string notes and slur-to-slur joins at mf and above it cut join dips from -8..-12 dB to -4..-5 dB (c02 -> c03). Joins inside a slur measured about -2 dB (c03).
- At pp-p, whether Leg BC MAX or Leg Slur MAX (overlapped, with a small CC11 bow-change notch) joins better is a property of each join, not of each section. Moving all soft joins to Leg Slur took the violas' median dip 5.6 -> 3.1 dB, but Violins II went 4.8 -> 6.2 and the celli 5.1 -> 5.3, with new 7-11 dB holes on short joins that had been fine on Leg BC (c11). Render both versions once, then keep a per-join list: that took 16 joins 2.5-5 dB shallower and halved the celli's joins of 7 dB or more (22 -> 11) (c12).
- Leg BC MAX targets at pp have a bump in the sample about 0.15-0.5 s after the onset (peak minus body 4-8 dB). Do not dip CC11 over that span: the dip lands in the transition sag, which comes before the bump, and deepened it to 7.5-10 dB holes about 0.5 s long (c07, corrected c11). Moving such joins to Leg Slur MAX was the fix that worked where it worked (above).
- Turn on legato speed: KS Masters ship with `EWSO.legato_speed_active = false` and `RTKiller.active = false`; the dedicated Leg Slur MAX patches have both true (c02). The LegatoSpeed script maps velocity 30-127 to a sample start offset of 0-4800 samples (about 100 ms).
- Measured legato lag (crossover of new-pitch over old-pitch energy, harmonics 1-4, from the MIDI onset): with velocity from the printed dynamic (41-63), strings 200-250 ms median, horns 125 ms, woodwinds 80-140 ms (c01). With legato speed on and velocity 105-120, strings 222-310 ms, horns 185-385 ms (c02, c03).
- Do not rely on legato velocity to move timing. Velocity 120 against 105 gained 25-80 ms in c03 (critic C), but on the Violins I KS Master velocity 108 against 124 gave crossovers identical to the millisecond (c11). Move the note instead: per-onset lead offsets of 0.05-0.07 s moved crossovers by 0.05-0.07 s (c11). The response is not always 1:1: most notes moved 0.05-0.13 s for 0.03-0.10 s of added lead, but one Leg BC join (2nd Violins C5 -> F5) moved 0.29 s for 0.10 s and arrived early (c15). Re-measure every per-note lead after a render.
- Tune legato leads per articulation, not only per section. Hollywood Strings Leg Slur MAX targets crossed 0.05-0.08 s later than Leg BC MAX targets at the same lead (2nd Violins, Violas, Celli); offsets of Leg Slur +0.025..+0.05 s and Leg BC -0.03 s brought both to about +0.01 s (c15).
- Soft legato is slower on the Flute KS Master: at CC11 50 or less the median crossover was +0.093 s against +0.03 s at mf and above, and an extra 0.06 s lead gated on CC11 brought the soft solo to +0.038 s (c15).
- The legato lag is deterministic (interquartile range about ±40 ms; two renders identical note for note, c03 critic C), so compensate by moving legato target notes earlier and keeping CC11/CC1/volume on score time.
- The lag depends on the source pitch and the interval. Viola transitions leaving C4 crossed over 0.22-0.37 s after the onset against a 0.05 s median for every other viola transition (c06 critic A); Solo Horn departures from F4 crossed 0.19-0.31 s late, and an extra 0.15 s lead brought them to +0.05..+0.18 s; Celli A♭3 -> C4 crossed at +0.23 s and an extra 0.15 s for that interval brought it to -0.05 s (c11). Keep per-source-pitch, per-interval and per-onset lead tables alongside the per-family lead.
- Check every per-pitch or per-interval extra in both directions. The Solo Horn F4 extra for falling steps made the F4 -> B♭4 fourth land 0.13-0.16 s early, and a pair offset of -0.12 s brought it to -0.03 s (c19); a viola extra for rising steps made the falling steps from the same pitch 0.07-0.14 s early, and negative pair extras of the same size brought them to -0.04..+0.03 s (c23). Some pairs are late every time: Oboe D♭5 -> C5 and F5 -> E♭5 by 0.13-0.16 s, fixed with +0.10 s (c19).
- After the true-stereo hall (section 12), a crossover measure can read the old note's hall tail as lateness. Change a lead only when the crossover and the new pitch's own rise time agree; 20 notes chosen that way all moved to within 0.08 s (c19).
- Leads that worked: strings 0.17 s with velocity 124 (Violins II and Violas 0.20 s), brass 0.10 s with velocity 124. Result: string crossover medians 250-275 -> 50-85 ms, horns 190-200 -> 85-108 ms (c03); Vln II +0.053 -> +0.023 s, Vla +0.060 -> +0.043 s with the 0.20 s lead (c07). Woodwinds: oboe/bassoon 0.06 s, bass clarinet 0.05 s, cor anglais 0.08 s, flute/clarinet 0.03 s (c03, c04). A DIV A half-section patch used as a solo legatos faster than its full section: 0.12 s matched the section's timing (c11: median crossover -0.03 s at 0.17 s, +0.02 s at 0.12 s).
- Convert leads from seconds to qn on the real tempo map going back from the onset, caesuras included, not at the onset's own tempo: after a caesura or a slow rit. step the onset-tempo conversion made 0.17 s leads 0.25-0.30 s long (c07).
- Cap the lead at 0.45 of the previous note's length and move the previous note's end with it so the overlap survives (c03). On fast slurred notes (0.33-0.5 qn triplets) that cap binds, so lead tables have no effect there (c11). Raising the cap to 0.75 for such a passage, with +0.10 s pair extras, moved slurred viola triplets from a median crossover of +0.053 to -0.017 s (worst +0.22 -> +0.14) with note-on spacing still at least 0.23 s (c19, `LEG_CAP_FRAC`).
- In soft codas, legato targets at velocity 124 can be too fast and present; velocity 90 for soft (CC11 40 or less) string legato targets was used (c08; effect not measured separately).
- Fast slurred notes under 0.25 s smear on legato patches (transitions of 160-320 ms; a viola sextuplet figure ran 230 ms behind, c03 critic C). Sus MAX with 0.03 qn overlap was used instead; a residual 90-120 ms lag remained (c03). The blur is not from the overlap: ending each note 0.01 qn early at v100 did not change how clearly each note sounds (c11: dominance share 0.27 -> 0.26).
- No Hollywood Strings articulation tried separates slurred notes of about 0.16 s. On viola sextuplets of 0.157 s per note, the own-pitch share was: Leg Slur MAX (v127, 0.02 qn overlap, no lead) 0.03, because the legato voice held one pitch per group (c14); Detache 0.32 against 0.32 for Sus MAX, with the pitch taking over later (c15); the "Stac Leg BC + Slur + Port MAX" row 0.39 against 0.30, at 3.3 dB lower level (c16). Keep such figures on Sus MAX. The Slur Runs row was not tried; a dedicated runs patch is the remaining option.
- About one Hollywood Strings legato join in ten (61 of 606) plays a transition spike 5-12 dB over both note bodies for 0.05-0.2 s. The spike is the same with the volume envelope removed, repeats within 0.5 dB between renders, and occurs at velocity 124 as well as 60-80, so velocity does not remove it (c21). A volume notch of (spike - 2) dB over the spike's span with 0.04 qn ramps took 14 of 16 listed joins under 5 dB; spiking joins went 62 -> 47 -> 37 -> 35 over c21-c23 (`JOIN_NOTCH`).
- A spike can move about 0.1 qn (0.1 s) between renders with identical MIDI and envelope: a notch that fit c22 left a +8 dB stem / +4 dB master spike in c23. Make each notch cover the positions seen in the last two renders (c23).
- Measure note lengths for articulation choice on the tempo map without caesuras or other expressive time, or a fermata-like slowing turns a short note into a legato target (c05).

## 6. Soft entries, re-attacks, sags and sample blooms

- Soft sustained string and brass entries reach level slowly: arrival (within 6 dB of the early peak) 300-1010 ms for pp/p strings, 355-495 ms for soft horn/trombone/tuba entries, against 60-150 ms for winds at the same moments (c03 critic C). The times repeat across renders, so they are sample attack, not streaming.
- Moving soft entries earlier (strings 0.12 s at CC11 <= 50 / 0.06 s above; brass 0.10 / 0.05 s; bassoons 0.06 s) with CC11 held at the entry's own level from the moved onset brought string arrival medians to Vln I 310, Vln II 488, Vla 230, Vc 100 ms (c03).
- Larger per-onset leads (extra seconds per entry from a measured lag minus 0.05 s, total capped at 0.30 s) helped loud and mid entries but not every pp string entry: Vln I qn 171 0.38 -> 0.20 s, Vc 0 0.36 -> 0.12, Cl1 0.17 -> -0.04, but Vln II qn 42 0.24 -> 0.45 and Vla 54 0.33 -> 0.54 (c07). Inferred: the soft first-note sample starts quietly and grows over 0.3-0.4 s, so extra lead alone only makes the quiet part earlier. Measure the shape of the first 0.3 s before adding lead to a pp entry.
- Lead plus a fill does work on soft string chord entries (pp, CC11 22-46) that reach their body 0.3-0.65 s after the other voices on the beat: an extra entry lead of 0.06-0.15 s (total under 0.30 s) plus a 3-4 dB volume fill held 0.3-0.4 s after the moved start brought them to +0.1..+0.3 s with no overshoot of the body in the first 1.8 s; the median over 82 entries went 0.07 -> 0.05 s (c23). Measure lateness against the body at +0.8..+1.6 s; a body taken earlier hides the slow rise.
- Legato targets on solo winds, horns and string sections often sag 5-12 dB for the first 0.15-0.4 s at flat CC11 and then swell; the shape repeats from render to render (c08 critic A, 35 notes). Fill the hole with a volume-envelope lift that rises in 0.03 s, holds to the measured end of the hole, and is gone 0.25 s later; dB = dip - 2 or 3, cap 7-8 (c08-c11). A fixed 0.10 s hold left most of the longer holes; holding to the measured end (+0.15..+0.4 s) brought 14 of 15 joins from 7-11 dB to 3-5 dB (c11). A fill may also start before the onset (`pre_s`) when the hole does (c10). Write its breakpoints exactly, not on the envelope grid (section 15).
- Generate the fills from the previous render instead of by hand (`tools/auto_fills.py PREV PARTS`): dB = dip - 3 (cap 6), hold to the sag minimum + 0.05 s, raise existing fills by min(3, dip - 4). One pass cut the wind and horn legato joins dipping 5 dB or more from 73 to 22 and the celli's from 45 to 25 (c16), but 9 of 110 fills then rose 2-5 dB over the note body at +0.3..+0.6 s. Add an overshoot rule: if the 200 ms peak from the fill's own start to onset + 0.4 s exceeds the louder body by more than 2 dB, lower the fill by (over - 1) dB or delete it, and never raise and lower one entry in the same pass (c17: wind joins over +2.5 dB 13 -> 8). Rerun it on every render; lowering fills for overshoot can reopen the hole (c18: three joins went from 4-5 to 6.4-7.2 dB). Exclude hand-set entries and entries the pass removes and re-adds on alternate renders (`--exclude Track:onset`; c20-c23).
- A fill placed on a join that already has a transition spike (section 5) adds its dB to the spike. Check each fill against the 30 ms peak, not only the 200 ms body: lowering 8 such fills by half or more removed the spikes they made, with no new dips of 5 dB or more (c21). Two of eleven new fills in c23 again sat on spikes.
- Two string sections in unison sag together at legato joins, so they do not fill each other's dips (sum dips 5-7 dB at six joins). Fill each section at the shared joins (2.5-5 dB, held to the sag minimum): sum dips fell to 1.6-4.5 dB (c23).
- Measure flute, oboe and cor anglais joins on a 200 ms window (one vibrato cycle). Their high notes carry about 10 dB of 4-5 Hz amplitude modulation, which a 30 ms window reads as holes (c17).
- Hold a fill only until the sample's own rise begins. A Solo French Horn Sus MAX attack at f reached body - 6 dB only at +0.34 s and then swelled 3 dB over its body; a fill held to +0.35 s overshot the body by 4-10 dB, and +8 dB held to +0.10 s gave -5 dB at +0.10 s and a +2 dB peak (c11). Measure the note's shape out to +0.6 s before choosing the hold.
- Do not stack level shaping into a join. A negative note trim that reached full depth at a legato target's moved onset deepened the transition sag from 3-5 to 8-10 dB (c09): let note trims ramp in over about 0.6 s and end them at the next note's moved onset. At soft string bow-change joins, an 8 CC notch, a taper on the slur's last note and a bloom-correction ramp ending on the join added 2.4-5.2 dB to the sample's own 4.4 dB sag (c13): do not taper or ramp down into a note that the next note joins, and keep bow-change notches at 3-4 CC.
- A same-pitch re-attack on Leg Slur MAX at pp takes 300+ ms to reach level, leaving 7-15 dB holes in the stem (c03 critic A). Merge same-pitch repeats in legato string lines into one note with a CC11 notch (10 CC portato, 14 CC bow change, 8 CC at CC11 <= 40) (c03, c04). Printed bow marks inside a held note get the same notch (14 CC above CC 40, 6 at pp and softer); Vln I dipped about 2 dB and recovered within 0.125 qn (c06).
- Long notes on some MAX sustain layers swell 8-17 dB at constant CC11: Oboe F5, cor anglais A4, Flute B♭6 (c02). Soft unslurred Sus MAX string chords bloom 6-9 dB inside each note (c02). A soft re-attack on Leg Slur MAX after a gap rose 14 dB over 2.5 qn (c04 critic C). These are per-sample, so correct them per note or per pitch (section 13). Do not apply such bloom corrections to a crescendo's arrival note (c10).
- Fixes that worked: per-note level trims keyed by onset (0 dB at the onset to the full trim at onset + 0.6 s), found by a residual scan against the track's CC11 fit (c03: notes over +5 dB); a volume ramp of 0 to -4.5 dB over 2 qn on soft re-attacks (c04). Keying trims by pitch and minimum length hit the wrong notes (c03 critic B). The 0.6 s ramp-in is wrong for plucked and struck notes (section 13).
- Wind portato (tenuto inside a slur) on a legato patch re-starts on the slow non-legato layer: Flute 2 bloomed 0.6 s after the beat (c04 critic A). Re-tongued on Sus at ff it left 6-12 dB holes at each note (c06 critic A). What worked best: a tenuto to a new pitch as a Leg Slur target (velocity 100, 10 CC notch); a repeated pitch re-tongued on Sus with the previous note ending 0.01 qn early and a 0.04 s lead; unslurred tenutos crossfaded 0.03 qn. Join dips went 5.7-8.0 -> 4.1-5.2 dB (c07). A flute Leg Slur transition up to F6 still dipped 10 dB inside the transition itself.
- A KS Master joins an overlapping re-tongued Sus note into a following Leg Slur note as a legato transition. With no lead the new note reached body - 6 dB only at +0.33 s; a 0.12 s lead at velocity 100 brought it to +0.10 s (c23, `SOST_JOIN_LEAD_S`).
- A string note that follows a legato note with no rest but switches to Sus MAX (e.g. a div. chord) re-attacks slowly like an entry. Giving it the entry lead, CC11 held at the entry level and velocity 105 or more, and ending the old notes 0.03 qn after the new score onset, cut viola joins of 6 dB or more from 8 to 2 (c06, c07). Treat a "solo"/"tutti" change the same way (c09: a tutti entry with an extra 0.10 s lead went from 0.25 s at -56..-52 dB to -45 dB by +0.14 s).
- Lead soft wind re-attacks that follow a note with no gap only at listed onsets, never as a blanket rule. A blanket 0.10 s lead on soft Sus MAX bassoon notes, with the previous note ended 0.02 qn after the moved start, gave mixed arrivals (two 0.17-0.25 s arrivals improved, three 0-0.03 s arrivals went to 0.21-0.29 s), and the next same-pitch re-attack (0.03 qn gap, MIDI otherwise identical) opened with an 11-22 dB hole about 0.1 s long, against 3 dB before (c15, reverted). The same 0.10 s lead on four listed chord changes, with the old note ending at the moved start and no overlap, moved the pitch crossovers from +0.05..+0.15 to -0.04..+0.01 s, and the holes before them were unchanged on a 50 ms smoothed measure (7.5 -> 7.4, 4.5 -> 5.0 dB) (c19, c20, `ABUT_LEAD` with an `at` list). Check the repeated notes next to any moved note.
- On the Basses KS Master Sus MAX, a new pitch that abuts the old note takes over a median 0.20 s late (celli 0.00 s), because the old pitch's partials take about 0.46 s to fall 10 dB (celli 0.12 s). Starting every abutting arco pitch change 0.16 s early, with the old note ending at the moved start, brought the median to +0.03 s (Cb minus Vc +0.165 -> +0.06 s) (c20). Skip the lead when the next note re-attacks the same pitch (`skip_next_same`). One new 7.7 dB trough of 0.06 s appeared.
- A soft sustained chord pad re-attacked on every chord change troughs because the sample blooms slowly. On 2nd Violins Flautando (sul tasto) at pp, half-note changes troughed 7-15 dB about 0.5 s after each change (bloom about 1.2 s). Hold common tones as one note, start the moving voices early and overlap them with the old chord: 0.15 s lead and 0.35 qn overlap halved the median trough (10 -> 5.4 dB, c15); 0.45 s and 0.7 qn cleared two of the three remaining 9-11 dB troughs (c16).
- Hollywood Strings sustain samples (Sus MAX and Leg Slur MAX; basses, celli, violas, 1st violins) loop with a period of about 3.95 s in real time, whatever the tempo. - Medium-length sustain notes (3-7 s) show single loop-seam dips of 7-11 dB at fixed times after the moved start, repeating within 0.1 dB between renders. A single raised-cosine lift (80% of the dip, cap 6 dB) took most of them to 4-6 dB; the deepest (11 dB) stayed at 6.6 (c20).
- The level profile fixes only the loudness part of the loop. Keep the held note's CC11, CC1 and volume envelope across the pieces, remove the CC11 bow-change notches and the loop profiles that the re-bows replace, and put each new piece on Sus MAX: a same-pitch re-attack on Leg Slur MAX plays the slow first-note layer (section 6, c03).
- A same-pitch re-bow on Hollywood Strings Sus MAX at pp (v41, CC11 about 30, no lead, the old piece ending 0.03 qn early, keyswitch 0.02 qn before) dips 6-15 dB on the stem for about 0.9 s, and the new piece settles 3-9 dB away from the level of the one it replaces, because Sus MAX and Leg Slur MAX sustains differ at the same CC11. Refit the shapes together with the re-bow times, and after any string patch, mic or hall change.

- Accents: keep `Sus Accent MAX` for f and louder; for softer accents add +8 CC11 decaying over 0.3 qn and velocity +10 (c01). A keyswitched accent measured +3 to +4 dB over the previous bar with +4 to +6 dB attack brightness (c03).
- A high accent velocity adds a transient, not weight: Solo Trombone Sus Accent notes lowered from v121 to v108/105 lost 3-8 dB of peak and only 1-1.3 dB of body (c10). Add weight in the volume envelope instead, once per chord: +2 dB on loud accents (30 ms rise, held 0.25 s, gone by 0.5 s; +3 dB measured 5-8 dB over the neighbours, +2 gave 2-4) (c09, c10). For a single exposed accent that must cut through a tutti, +4 dB (10 ms rise, held 0.12 qn, gone by 0.3 qn) turned an attack 1.7 dB under its body into 2 dB over it; the stem peak rose 6 dB but the master peak did not (c11). On brass this was too harsh (next point).
- Brass accents: the HO accent samples are bright. Solo Trombone `Sus Accent MAX` at v105-110 with an 8 CC11 accent bump (CC11 122, the fff layer) and a 10 ms +3..+4.5 dB step put a 2-5 kHz burst 7-13 dB over the note's own body, which Dan heard as harsh, although the body level met the 2-4 dB rule (c25). Plain Sus MAX at v92-96, CC11 at the printed dynamic with no bump, and a volume swell in seconds (0 at the onset, full at +0.07 s, held to +0.25 s, gone by +0.6 s) took 2-5 kHz attack minus body to -1..-9 dB and the master's 2-5 kHz jump at the attack from +10 to +2 dB (c26, `ACCENT_ART_AT`, `ACCENT_BUMP_AT`, `ACCENT_SWELL_S_AT`). The swell then has to supply the emphasis: +2.5-3 dB left the accents only 0-1 dB over the surrounding marcato notes, +4.5-6 dB gave 1.5-4 dB (c26). Sus MAX speaks more slowly: its first 60 ms sit 2-10 dB under the body, and the attack probe still read the note on time (c26). - Marcato: Solo Trombone `Marc Lng RRx4` (1TB KS Master) at v94-106, played at 0.75 of the written length, gives the separation of a marcato line but not attack weight: its body sits 2-3 dB under Sus MAX at the same dynamic, and its attack rises over about 0.1 s with no peak over the body (c14). +2 dB on the body restores the level. An attack bump must sit where the sample peaks (+0.08..+0.11 s): +3 dB from +0.04 to +0.16 s brought some notes to +2.6..+4.9 dB over their body, but not all (c15).
- A note that should be detached from the next must end early enough that its release does not fill the gap: a dotted note ending 0.1 qn early left no audible break, 0.25 qn gave a 13 dB break (c15).
- Untied repeated pp notes: a 0.03 qn gap is inaudible on winds (0-1.4 dB dip; release tail and hall fill it). 0.06 qn gave 2-6 dB on bassoon and bass clarinet but only 1-2 dB on muted or soft horns, whose tails are longer (c23, `REP_RETONGUE_AT`).
- sf: a short CC11 spike above the current level (+8 from f or louder, held 0.25 qn; +12 from softer levels) and velocity +15 (c02-c04); after the spike see section 4. If a decrescendo starts at the sf, start the hairpin after the spike, not before it (c02 critic B).
- Tenuto arrivals at f or louder under "molto sostenuto": hold CC11 at the arrival value to the note end instead of letting the tenuto bump decay (c08; part of the 1-3.6 dB arrival falls in section 4). Holding the full crescendo dB gain through such a span simulated a 4.9 dB delivery limiter; half of it was used (c10).
- Breath marks: end the note before at `min(0.25 qn, 20%)` early (c01); loud wind and brass notes (CC11 72 or more) at min(0.3 qn, half the note) (c13). The hall fills short gaps at f: a 0.2 s horn breath dipped only 4-5 dB, and 0.3 qn deepened it about 1 dB more (hall decay about 2.5 s) (c13). A loud breath needs a longer gap and a fall into it: a 0.5 qn gap plus a 10 CC fall over the note's last 0.2 qn dipped 7.2 dB (c15). A breath that also has a caesura needs only a short gap, because the caesura gives the pause: cutting loud wind breaths there from 0.3 to 0.12 qn took the master dip before a ff downbeat from 3.2 to 2.0 dB (c19, `CAESURA_BREATH_QN`). Keep listed entry leads after the shorter rest, or the re-entries lose them. Caesuras as tempo-map pauses: +0.22 s and +0.15 s before tutti arrivals (c04), 0.08-0.12 s at solo breath marks and phrase seams (c08). Every tempo-map change needs all items recreated, not the patches (section 15).
- Clarinets on Sus Non Vib were kept throughout; their measured vibrato was 1-8 cents, as expected (c04 critic C).
- A "solo" marking on a string section part needs a solo-sized patch. With the section patch, an +8 CC11 solo boost put Vln I 5.6 dB over the oboe solo that answers it (c08). The 1st Violins KS Master DIV A (half section) on its own track, with the section's notes routed to it by score-onset window, fixed the solo/tutti hand-over hole (6-7 -> 1.3 dB) and sat 5 dB over Vln II (c10). Its gain ended about 6 dB under the section's (section 8).

## 8. Divisi, double stops and shared patches

- Every note of a chord on a section patch is a full-section sample: 2-note chords measured +1.8 to +2.0 dB over single notes on Sus MAX/Flautando, so a divisi passage is about twice as thick as written (c03 critic B). A level cut of -10·log10(k) dB under "div." and -1.5 dB for "non div." double stops, plus -3 dB for tremolo chords, fixed the measured cases (c03 -> c04). The cut must start with the chord (section 4).
- When one divisi voice carries a line, split that voice onto a half-section divisi track and leave the other voice on the section track as a single line. Played as two-note chords on one full-section KS Master, a slurred cello answer was re-attacked as Sus MAX chords and opened with a 10-15 dB hole; on its own Leg Slur track the entry dip was 4.6-6.6 dB (c13). Keep the section track's divisi cut (-3 dB) for the voice it still plays. Splitting also lets both voices play legato: the violas' Sus MAX dyad notes fell from 79 to 49 (c18). The summed level of main + div tracks stayed within ±0.8 dB of the chord version (c18).
- The Hollywood Strings divisi folders hold DIV A / DIV B KS Masters for 1st/2nd Violins, Violas and Celli, with the same keyswitch maps and controllers as the full-section KS Masters (c10, c18, read with `dump_ks.py`). Each one is another Opus instance (section 11).
- Set a DIV A track's gain from one render; there is no fixed offset from the section. The 1st Violins DIV A as a solo with an +8 CC solo boost measured 12 dB over its accompaniment at the section gain +1 and needed -7 dB (c10). The Celli DIV A as half of a divisi answer, without a boost, measured 10-12 dB under its target at the section gain -2 and needed +6 dB (c13). DIV B patches matching the other voice of a full section (Close + Main) needed +2.5 dB at pp (2nd Violins, Flautando) and +4 dB at p-mf (violas) over the section gain, but at ppp the same +4 dB put the viola div voice 7.6 dB over the section's other voice and needed a -5 dB role (c18). A div patch's soft layers are relatively louder; set soft-passage cuts after measuring. A post-render gain fix on one stem saves a second render (section 15).
- Two tracks on the same patch file playing the same pitch together render the identical sample: correlation 0.999, a +6.5 dB coherent sum (Horns 1+2), or partial cancellation at correlation -0.86 (two clarinets: the sum was quieter than one) (c04 critic A). Drop the copy and give the kept note +10·log10(n) dB (c04). But if the dropped copy holds longer than the kept note, the chord loses that pitch when the kept note ends (c17: a horn A♭3 missing for a beat); start the longer copy where the kept note ends, overlapping by about 0.1 qn (c17: +14 dB, the pitch back). Group unisons on the notes each track plays after any re-routing to other tracks: grouping on the source staves kept a note that had been moved to another track as the "kept" copy and left a real player silent for two beats (-86 dB) (c19).
- A recorded a2 patch is the other option. The `2FH KS Master` (two horns; Close 0.5 / Main 1.0) on its own track for the a2 passage needed a track gain 4.6 dB higher than the solo-horn track it replaced (same mics, which had carried a +3 dB unison boost) to match it (c18).

## 10. Timpani (Timpani Flt KS, felt sticks)

- The patch has no CC11; CC7 is the only level control, and it is linear amplitude (section 3). Velocity hardly changes roll level (c01 critic B).
- The roll articulation (Rls) is about 14 dB louder than single strokes (Lng) at the same CC7 and velocity (c01 critic B: p strokes -38 dB, p roll -24 dB).
- Strokes have a steep velocity step: velocity 41 measured -62 to -68 dB, velocity 53 -38 dB; an 8-16 dB step lies between 56 and 64 (c01, c02). Keep stroke velocity at 56 or more (pp) and 60-64 for pp/p strokes that must be heard.
- CC7 values from `127·10^(dB/40)` with roll targets ppp -36, pp -31, p -26, mp -23, mf -17, f -16, ff -11, fff -9 dB, and strokes at roll +20 (pp), +16 (p, raised from +12 in c11), +16 (above), gave usable levels after track gain -7 dB (c01-c11). Because CC7 is linear amplitude, these targets were tuned by ear and measurement, not by the formula; their real spacing is half the listed dB. For hairpins inside a roll, put the missing half of the planned change in the volume envelope relative to the roll's start: a p < f roll grew +9.9 dB instead of +5.3, and a decrescendo roll fell 8.4 dB instead of 4.5 (c13).
- Put the roll settle and soft-roll cut in the volume envelope, not CC7: -5 dB over 3 qn measured -3.6 to -3.9 dB (c02, c03), and soft rolls (p or softer) need a further -6 dB to sit under pp strings (c03). Hold the settle until about 0.5 qn after the roll ends or the next onset; restoring the level at the roll's last onset lifted its ring (c05: the roll then sat 8 dB under Vln I).
- Felt rolls and strokes at f/ff sat 9-11 dB under the tutti in 50-250 Hz at their attack. +6 dB on the attack in the volume envelope (10 ms rise, held 0.25 qn, gone by 0.75 qn) brought them to -5..-6 dB without changing the roll body (c10).
- A crescendo roll tied into a note needs a stroke on that note, and MIDI from LilyPond merges the tie into the roll note, so add the stroke by rule: end the roll at the tied note + 0.03 qn and start a single stroke (Timpani Flt Lng) there, v96 at f, v110 at ff, CC7 4 dB over the roll on the 40·log10 scale (2 dB real), with half the usual loud-attack bump because it already carries the roll's hairpin gain. It peaked 6.4 dB over the roll's last half beat and then decayed (c14).
- Timpani level fixes must be at full value on the stroke: a note trim with a 0.6 s ramp-in lifted the ring and not the stroke (c10). Use a level span or a step (section 13).
- The KS patch loads Close, Main and Surround mics by default (c01). Timpani take 85-100% of the master's 100-300 Hz energy when the Surround mic and a 3.3 s percussion IR are on (c01 critic C). Close 0.2 / Main 0.5 with Surround off took 7 dB out of the timpani's 125-180 Hz (c05), which contributed to a thin low-mid (section 14).
- The settle and soft-roll cut can put the softest rolls too low: by c25 the ppp rolls sat 14-23 dB under the orchestra and were masked in every band but the fundamental. +3 to +4 dB on the ppp rolls kept them under every other stem while making them audible as a low layer, with the master changing under 0.5 dB (c25).
- Long timpani rolls loop too. A periodic inverse profile fitted on the roll (section 6 method, correction clamped to ±3 dB) took the level pulse of a 2.9 s and a 3.1 s roll span from 4.2 to 1.0 dB and from 2.6 to 1.1 dB (c26).
- Missing timpani strokes (-94 and -117 dB) occurred in a render made with a cold disk cache after a reboot, and were present in the next render (c04).

## 11. Memory, sample streaming and render timing

- Memory is the binding constraint for a full Opus orchestra. A freshly loaded 27-instance project holds about 49-52 GB in REAPER (c07-c11); with 29 instances (two DIV A tracks, Close mic only) it reached 56 GB by the end of a render (c13), on a 96 GB Mac. 32 instances (three more Close-only or two-mic patches) loaded to 56 GB with 0 GB compressed after a fresh open and rendered with correct timing (c18). Every extra instance and every extra mic adds to it.
- From c19 on, the 32-instance project opened with `-new` plus `Main_openProject` settled at only 20-23 GB (0 GB compressed, memory flat, so the load wait passed) and grew during the 1x render to 62-63 GB, with 15, 22, 51, 44 and 44 GB compressed at the end of c19-c23; in c21 the Mac had 0.5 GB free (`metrics/memory.txt`). Inferred: Opus now loads most samples while the render plays them, not at open; the cause of the change from c18's 56 GB at open was not found. Timing held: the attack gate gave the same results in every one of these renders (section 16). So a render can still be correct with REAPER near the machine's RAM limit and most of it compressed, and the pre-render memory reading no longer shows the render's peak.
- Re-setting an Opus state with `vst_chunk` on a live track does not free the old instrument's memory. Full 27-track rebuilds took one session from about 49 GB to 57 GB and a later session to 72 GB (c06, c07). Deleting a track does free its instance: deleting and recreating all 29 tracks in a running session went from 57 GB (35 compressed) to 46 GB (25 compressed) (c17). So a full rebuild that recreates the tracks is safe; reloading states in place is what leaks.
- Under memory pressure macOS compresses REAPER's idle sample memory, and a 1x offline render then can start entries late on tracks whose data did not change. c06: REAPER 57 GB with 42 GB compressed, 200 MB free, swap in use; 10 of 19 attack checks failed, every failing entry 0.2-0.3 s late, tremolo arrivals leaving 20-30 dB holes. c07 from a fresh REAPER (49 GB, 0 GB compressed), same machine and preload: 19 of 19 passed, median entry arrival 0.060 s (c06 0.102).
- A large compressed figure is a warning, not a verdict. Renders from a freshly reopened session passed the attack gate with 31-37 GB of REAPER's memory compressed by other work on the Mac (c08, c11, c12, c13). The failing c06 session had also grown through patch reloads in place. Streaming about 55 GB of samples from the external exFAT drive fills the page cache, and macOS compressed 40 of REAPER's 57 GB during one load that started at 0 GB compressed; that render still had correct timing (c17). Check `top -l 1 -o mem -stats pid,command,mem,cmprs` after loading has settled, just before the render starts, not only before the wait, and run the attack probe after every render either way.
- The Mac froze and needed a reset during the second heavy render of one cycle (c04). The cause was not measured; memory pressure is the likely cause (inferred from the 57-72 GB sessions measured in c06-c07). Render once per cycle and check the data (and simulate level changes, section 16) before rendering instead of rendering twice. Three renders in one cycle with a restart before each did not freeze (c09). From c14 to c26 every cycle used one render and fixed level-only problems on the stems afterwards (section 15). With renders ending under 1 GB free in c19-c23 (above), a second render in one session was risky; after the c24 row cut renders ended at 44 GB, but the one-render rule was kept.
- Restart REAPER before rendering after any patch reloads: save, quit, reopen, and wait for loading to finish (c07: 0 GB compressed after the restart, render passed). For changes that do not touch the Opus state, replace only each track's MIDI item, volume envelope and ReaEQ and keep the instance (`build_reaper.py --midi-only`): 27 tracks in about 30 s instead of a full reload (c07).
- Close other memory-heavy processes before rendering (c05 critic A found two other Python processes at about 3.2 GB each during the slow c04 render; c06 had two such jobs plus Spotlight reading the internal disk at about 300 MB/s).
- Wait for Opus to finish loading samples after any load or rebuild: a render right after a full rebuild had 83 late entries; the same project 25 minutes later had 26 (c02). Rule in use: three 10 s readings of REAPER's memory in a row changing by under 50 MB. Loading takes about 8-12 minutes with a warm drive cache (c07, c18) and about 25 minutes after a Mac reset, at about 1 GB per minute (30-70 MB/s) from the external drive (c08). A wait loop with a fixed cap must refuse to render if memory is still growing (cap now 2700 s), or it renders half-loaded patches (c08). From c19 the open settled at about 21 GB in 1-8 minutes and memory grew during the render instead (above), with correct timing. Keep the wait anyway: it takes about 30 s when memory is flat and still catches the c02/c08 case.
- Full-speed offline renders start notes up to 0.6 s late: several tracks at the same moment had about 0.5 s of digital silence then a full attack (c01). Render at 1x offline: woodwind late entries 20 -> 3, strings at one entry 545-575 -> 135-350 ms, brass 515 -> 170 ms (c01).
- `RENDER_1X 1` cannot be set with `GetSetProjectInfo`; edit the `.rpp` line and reopen the project (c01). `render_cycle.py` warns when the line is missing.
- Events on the very first block of an offline render can be dropped: one opening voice at -75 dB in one render, two string sections silent in another, with identical MIDI (c04, c05; c06 critic A). Put a silent pre-roll bar before the music (3 qn at 60 bpm = 3.000 s = 144000 samples at 48 kHz), send each track's first keyswitch and first value of every CC lane in it, and cut it off the master and every stem after the render, reading the time back with `TimeMap2_QNToTime` so analysis keeps the score timeline (c06: Horn 3 opening max -75.0 -> -56.2 dB). Leads of opening notes may start inside the pre-roll; set the silence-check threshold for the cut part accordingly (c07: -40 dBFS). Give the pre-roll's tempo marker its own meter (3/4 for 3 qn). Movement I (4/4) used the score's meter there: the pre-roll became an incomplete bar, REAPER moved the score's first marker to the next barline (4.0 s instead of 3.0 s), and every note, CC and envelope point from score qn 1 on sounded 1/6 qn early (0.08 s at 126 bpm, 0.2 s at 50) in mvt1 c00-c02; it first showed as volume shapes landing 0.09-0.25 s early. `build_reaper.py` now gives the pre-roll 3/4 and stops when any marker's `TimeMap2_timeToQN` differs from its planned qn (mvt1 c03: first audio after silence -0.082 s -> 0.000 s median over 302 entries). Movement II started in 3/4 and was not affected.
- Opus `PreloadSize` in `/Library/Application Support/East West/Opus/Preferences.json` (0 = Harddrive, 1 = SSD/SATA, 2 = SSD/PCIe, order from the Opus binary) was kept at 1. Setting 0 was proposed to fix late entries but not applied: the Opus manual (Audio Engine) says the Harddrive setting keeps more of each sample in RAM, and the late entries came from memory pressure, so a larger preload would add to the cause (c07). Renders at 1 from a fresh session passed every attack check (c05, c07-c23). Change it only with REAPER closed.
- An agent run under Claude Code cannot edit that Preferences.json: the permission system blocked the edit even with the user's approval in chat (c06). The user has to change it in Opus's settings.
- Separate render-engine lateness from sample attack by comparing renders: engine delays change between renders and sessions, sample-attack delays repeat exactly (c01, c03 critic C, c05-c07). Do not compensate engine delays by moving notes (c01).

- Most of an Opus project's memory is the preload of articulation rows that are loaded but never played. Unloading every row a track does not use (`layerMatrix.rows[i].loaded/active = false`; `UNLOAD_UNUSED_ROWS` in `build_reaper.py`) cut the 32-instance project from 61 GB settled to 43 GB, and the render peaked at 44 GB with 0 GB compressed, against 63 GB with 44-51 GB compressed in c21-c23. Stems were bit-identical where nothing else changed (c24). Do this from the first build of any mockup.
- Read memory only after streaming has finished: 20-21 GB readings soon after an open were partial loads of a project that settled at 61 GB about 13 minutes later. Wait for several equal readings 30 s apart (c24). This corrects the c19-c23 reading in this section: the open did not stop loading samples, the readings were taken too early.
- With the unused rows unloaded, reopening the 32-track project took about 10 minutes to settle (18 minutes at 61 GB), and c24-c26 all rendered at 44 GB with nothing compressed. Loading two more rows on two string tracks cost no measurable memory (c26).
- Quit REAPER after each render so analysis runs on a free machine (rule since c24). An idle REAPER left open during analysis had 34 of 45 GB compressed by macOS (c26).

## 12. Mics, reverb and panning inside Opus

- Factory mic balances differ widely between KS Masters: woodwinds Close 1.0 with Main 0.01-0.28 (Main 11-40 dB under Close), horns Close 1.35 / Main 0.44, strings Close 0.66 / Main 1.0, Basses Close 0.94 / Main 1.0, Tuba 1.0 / 1.0, Trombones Close 0.486 (c01 critic C, c05 critic C). Woodwinds then sound nearer than the strings. DIV A patches have only Close (section 8).
- Settings used: woodwinds Close 0.5 / Main 0.8, horns 0.5 / 1.0, harp 0.6 / 1.0, strings and trombones at patch default (c01); timpani Close 0.2 / Main 0.5 (Surround off), tuba 0.45 / 1.0 (c05); basses 0.4 / 1.0 (c05), back to 0.6 / 1.0 in c13 (section 14).
- Mic channels to load are `layerMatrix.columns[n].loaded` plus the `mixer.channels` `sub:` channel `loaded` and `volume` (c01). Loading extra mics (e.g. Surround) adds sample memory (c05 critic C).
- Every patch carries its own reverb: a `ConvolutionReverb` on the `fx:0` channel fed by a `SendProcessor` on each mic channel. Across the KS Masters there were six different IRs and effective reverb levels from -10.6 dB (brass) to -24 dB (harp) (c01 critic C). Replace every patch's reverb insert with one copied from a single patch, set the same `fx:0` volume and `wet` everywhere, and set depth with the per-mic send level only (c01).
- Send levels that kept depth order: strings 0.25, harp 0.3, woodwinds 0.35, trombones/tuba/timpani 0.45, horns 0.5, Close-only DIV A tracks 0.5, with `wet` 2.0 (c01-c13).
- "S. Cal. Hall strings LR Rear" (the strings' factory IR) is a long hall (HO manual: 3.4 s): stems decayed with a median late slope of 3.1 s against 1.5-1.95 s in a real recording (c05 critic C). "New Hollywood Hall 2.0" (from `3BS Sus MAX.oib`, 30 ms pre-delay; ships with Opus) at fx volume 0.19 shortened the late slope to 2.5 s, but the drop 300 ms after a cutoff stayed about 10 dB against 13-17 in the recording, because the raised fx volume kept the early tail (c05). A lower fx volume (toward 0.15) was not tried. 2026-11-07: 0.095 for every movement after Dan heard the renders as too wet (see "What a listener noticed"). The true-stereo IR at the same settings gave a final-release drop of 9.2 / 15.2 / 28.7 dB at 0.3 / 0.6 / 1.0 s (c17).
- The hall's own high cut (`filterActive` true, `highFilterFreq` and `highCutFreq` both set; 5.5 kHz used) darkened the stem tails only about 1 dB in 2-8 kHz against the sustain (c09). A darker master top came from an EQ shelf on Vln I instead.
- The patches pan their own Close channels (Cb +0.62, Vc +0.33, Bn +0.41, Vln I -0.31, etc.), and REAPER's default pan on a stereo track is a balance control; combined, the c00 mix had master coherence 0.07 at 0.8-3 kHz against 0.70 in a recording (c01 critic C). Set every seat in one place.
- Seats can be set inside Opus: on each mic channel `panLeft = seat - width`, `panRight = seat + width`, the `fx:0` reverb return centred at full width (-1 / +1), the main channel centred (section 1), and the REAPER track at pan 0, width 1 (c05, c13; read back from the state). Seats held (Vln I -8.0 dB R-L, Vc +4.1, Cb +5.4), and the master measured correlation 0.52 and side/mid -4.9 dB against 0.55 and -5.3 in the reference (c07 render). Widths used: 0.3 for solo winds, horns, low brass and DIV B divisi tracks, 0.35 for Vc/Cb (0.28 from c18), 0.2 for DIV A tracks, 0.3 for the harps from c20 (c09-c23).
- With a plain stereo IR the reverb tails follow the seat. Moving the seat into Opus did not change that (c05: Vln I sustain -8.0 / tail -8.3 dB; Cb +5.4 / +6.5), and neither did pre-fader channel sends (`SendProcessor.post: false`) (c09: Vln I tail -7.6 dB R-L). The cause is that the hall insert has `trueStereo: false`, so it convolves L with L and R with R. Loading HO's true-stereo IR (`//ir/HOP True Stereo/S. Cal Hall Perc TS FR 3.3s`, shipped in the percussion patches) into the shared insert with `trueStereo: true`, keeping the delay, wet, high cut and fx volume, moved the tails to the centre (c17: Vln I -3.9 -> +0.3, Vln II -3.6 -> +0.2, Bn2 +4.8 -> -0.6 dB R-L). Sustain pans stayed within 1 dB and tail-to-sustain levels within 1.5 dB, except the basses (tail -3.7 dB). No REAPER reverb bus is needed. It is an Opus state change, so every track needs a full rebuild, and any per-note profiles fitted on the old hall need refitting (section 6).
- The true-stereo hall decorrelates the bass: master L/R correlation below 250 Hz fell from 0.50 to 0.37 (c17). Narrowing the Vc/Cb widths from 0.35 to 0.28 did not fix it (c19: 0.52 under 120 Hz, 0.39 at 120-250 Hz, against about 0.60 in two Brahms recordings).
- A side-only low shelf in REAPER does fix it: JS Mid/Side Encoder, then a ReaEQ whose input and output pins are mapped to channel 2 only (pin masks read back as 0,2,0,2; channel 1 has no output pin and passes through), then the JS Mid/Side Decoder, after every other FX on the track (`side_shelf` in mix.json). At 200 Hz -6 dB on six bass tracks the master went to 0.88/0.71, more mono than the references, and the 100-250 Hz band share fell 0.6 dB (c20); -2 dB gave 0.71/0.75/0.56 at 25-60/60-120/120-250 Hz (c21). Start at -2 dB.
- A side-only high shelf at 250 Hz widens the mid band the same way: +1.5 dB on the winds and brass and +3 dB on the single-mic divisi patches took the master 250 Hz-2 kHz correlation from 0.62 to 0.56 (reference 0.55); seats moved by at most about 1 dB (c21).
- The REAPER volume envelope comes after the Opus hall, so any level change while a track rests reshapes its reverb tail: 6-8 dB cuts or swells after the note had stopped (c17). Hold the envelope at the last note's level through each rest of 0.5 s or more, and let it move only in the last 0.35 s before the next entry (`build_reaper.py vol_points`, `tail_hold` in mix.json). Hold it after a track's last note to the end of the project too: roles ending later had lifted a final bass tail 3-6 dB and halved a tuba tail's decay time, and the hold removed the rise (c19).
- REAPER stereo pan mode (`I_PANMODE 5`, `D_PAN`, `D_WIDTH`) also placed seats correctly before c05 (c04: Vln I -8.7 dB R-L ... Cb +5.2), but it moves and narrows the in-patch reverb with the dry sound. Widths: 0.3 gave master correlation 0.76; wind 0.5, horn 0.6, others 0.5-0.6 gave 0.64 against 0.60 in the reference (c03 -> c04). Do not go below 0.3 on section patches: the spaced Main pair folds to near mono (c01 critic C, inferred).

## 13. Starting levels and balance rules

- At equal CC11, the raw patches are far apart. At p (CC 46): solo winds -35 to -45 dB, string sections -46 to -53 dB, double basses -34.6 dB, harp -56 dB (c00, K-weighted). Track gains that brought every solo wind and string section to -46..-52 dB at p: Vln I +5, Vln II +6, Vla +2, Vc 0, Cb -9 (-7 with Close 0.4, -8 with Close 0.6), Fl -1, Ob -5, CA -1, Cl -1, BCl +1, Bn -2, Cbn +2, horns -4, trombones 0, tuba +2 with Close 0.45, timpani -7, harp -4 (c01-c13). These depend on the mic settings in section 12.
- Rimsky-Korsakov's resonance ratios worked as targets: in piano one wind equals one string section; in forte a string section is about +3 dB over one woodwind, a horn about two woodwinds, a trombone about two horns (Principles, "Comparison of resonance"). Measured result at both climaxes after c04: Vln I 3.2-3.5 dB over every single wind (c05 critic B); melody margins +4 to +6 dB at the climaxes after c05-c07, held within 0.7 dB through the c09 EQ changes.
- Accompaniment at the same printed dynamic as a melody needs a level cut (players do it); 3-7 dB cuts over spans worked (c02-c13). A cut set for a loud passage can bury a soft countermelody in the same span; check both (c04 critic B). A cut that ends at a climax should open over the last few beats (c06: role cuts opening over 4 qn), or the level arrives as a jump at the barline. A role row that applies to every track (`"_all"`) is a simple way to take a whole climax down 1 dB (c12).
- Level cuts set early go stale as other tracks are raised. Three 3-6 dB cuts from c02-c06 had left a cello line 8-10 dB under its unison partner and a horn counter-line 3-12 dB under the violins by c20 (c21). Re-check old cuts against their original target whenever the reference track has moved by more than 3 dB.
- Sample level varies by pitch at flat CC11 by 5-14 dB, on every articulation of the patch: Solo Horn F4 +5 to +7 (legato and Sus chords), G♭4 and A♭4 -5; Clarinet G5 +10, A♭5 -5, A♭4 (the written B♭4 throat note) -8; English Horn A4 +9, D♭4 -5; Bassoon D♭3 +5, E♭3 -6, G3 -5; Basses A♭1 +6 to +8, Sus MAX E♭2 -5.5, D♭2 +4 (pizz. even); Violas pizz. F4 +10 (c06-c08, c13). Fit each note's body against its neighbours, average the residual per pitch, and apply a pitch trim to the articulations that show it (c13: +4/+6 dB trims brought each pitch within about 1-3 dB of its neighbours). Horn 1 spread over one phrase went from 13 to 6.6 dB (c06).
- A flat pitch trim on a pitch whose note sags at the start deepens the sag (c08 critic A: horn F4 trimmed -5 dB went to 9.8-12.1 dB sag); give such trims an early and a body value (F4 early +1 / body -5 brought it to 6.6-7.7 dB). The c08 rule that ended pitch trims at onset + 3 qn on long notes was removed in c09; keep the trim for the whole note. A pitch trim on a joined note starts at its moved onset and ramps in over 0.25 qn (c09).
- A pitch trim set at soft dynamics can be wrong at ff or inside a chord. A +6 dB trim on a weak throat-register clarinet A♭4 was too much in a ff sostenuto passage, and a bassoon E♭3 +6 trim on top of a climax role put the bassoon over the melody; skipping the trim there lowered it 5.5 dB (c15, c16). Give pitch trims a list of onsets to skip (`_skip_onsets`), with a note trim in their place where needed, and check every trimmed pitch at each dynamic it appears at.
- Level trims on plucked or struck notes (pizz., harp, timpani) must be a step at full value on the attack: 0 at onset - 0.03 s, full at - 0.01 s, held to the next onset. A 0.25 qn ramp sampled on a 0.125 qn envelope grid reached full depth just before the next note: an -8 dB trim sat at -1 to -2.5 dB on its own attack and cut the following note. With the step, a viola pizz. figure's spread went from 8 to 5.7 dB and its largest jump from 14.5 to 6.7 dB (c12). Pizz. round robins still differ by up to about 6.6 dB (c12). Make the step the default shape for every note trim on a plucked note: a -5 dB step brought a loud harp dyad down 5.0 dB, into the range of the harp's other onsets (c21).
- One dynamic layer of one pitch can be hot on its own: a cello A♭3 at f/ff was 4-6 dB over its neighbouring notes at the same CC11, while the same pitch at p/mp was within 1 dB. When a single note is the loudest stem in a tutti, compare it with the same pitch at other dynamics before changing the whole line; note trims of -3/-4 dB on the loud notes fixed it (c25).
- Find hot notes with a residual scan: per-note level against the track's CC11 fit; correct notes more than 5 dB over (c03-c13).
- A pp/ppp timpani roll should be 3-5 dB under the quietest sustained string section (c02 critic C target; reached in c04 except where a printed crescendo lifts it).
- Use A-weighting or K-weighting, not plain RMS, for balance between high and low instruments; RMS counted celli and basses a few dB higher than a listener hears them (c02 critic B, c04 critic C).

At f/ff the 125 Hz band (loud frames, re 1 kHz) was -9.8 against -0.7 in the reference while soft frames matched it; easing the celli's f/ff trims by 1-2 dB moved it from -10.3 to -10.4 dB (c08). In this score the climaxes leave 90-180 Hz to the basses' second harmonic and the timpani (c08 critic C, inferred).

Replacing the basses' loud-EQ 80 Hz -3 dB shelf with 100 Hz -6 dB took the loudest 10% of frames from -13.4 to -15.9 dB, and 100-250 Hz fell only 0.1 dB (c21). A track can take several wet-automated EQs (one ReaEQ per `loud_eq` spec); a second one, 90 Hz -5 dB over a loud pizz. passage, took that passage's 60-100 Hz from -10.6 to -13.8 dB (c21).
- The Basses KS Master's fundamental is uneven by pitch. On C2-G2 it is as loud as harmonics 2-4 (D♭2 -0.1, D2 +8.6, F2 +9.5 dB), while on E1-B♭1 it sits 12-30 dB under them; tuba, bass trombone and contrabassoon sit 11-24 dB under in the same render. ## 15. REAPER and bridge behaviour

- REAPER ignores `D_VOL` once a track has a volume envelope: the first c02 render lost every track gain (Vln I -7 dB, Cb +8 dB). Put the static gain inside the envelope and set `D_VOL` to 1.0 (c02).
- Writing a volume envelope by script: select the track, get "Volume" (create with action 40406 if missing), make sure it is active (`ACT 1` in the chunk), delete the old points, insert points with `ScaleToEnvelopeMode(GetEnvelopeScalingMode(env), amp)`, then `Envelope_SortPoints` (`build_reaper.py`).
- An envelope written on a fixed grid (0.125 qn here) smears shapes faster than about 0.15 s (fills, attack bumps, pizz. steps). Add each fast shape's own breakpoints to the grid (`vol_bp` in parts.json) when timing within 0.1 s matters (c10, c12).
- Three update paths, from cheapest: `--mix-only` (volume envelope, track gain and ReaEQ; pan stays centre once seats are in Opus), `--midi-only` (recreate each track's MIDI item and envelopes, keep the Opus instance; falls back to a full rebuild for a track whose generated state differs from the last one written), and a full rebuild (reloads patches; costs memory, section 11) (c01-c13). All three take a list of track names.
- A tempo-map change needs every item recreated on the new map, but not the patches: write the tempo map first, then replace each track's MIDI item and envelopes (`--midi-only --tempo`, c08: 303 -> 323 tempo points; c12: 385 points with phrase-final lengthening; attack gate passed both times).
- ReaEQ by script: add with `TrackFX_AddByName(tr, "ReaEQ", false, -1)`, set band types with `TrackFX_SetNamedConfigParm(..., "BANDTYPEn", ...)` (0 low shelf, 8 bell, 4 high pass; band 3 used as the high shelf) and `BANDENABLEDn`; parameter scales are not linear, so set frequency/gain/bandwidth by binary search on the normalized value until `TrackFX_GetFormattedParamValue` shows the target (c04, c05, c09). A second ReaEQ whose "Wet" parameter (found by name) is automated with an FX parameter envelope works as a switchable effect, for example a mute (c09, square step) or an EQ that is on only at the climaxes (c16, linear wet points).
- Render master plus every stem in one pass: `RENDER_SETTINGS 1`, `RENDER_BOUNDSFLAG 2` with the time selection set by `GetSet_LoopTimeRange`, pattern `$tracknumber_$track`, all tracks selected, action 42230 (c00-c13). Keep stem numbering stable by appending new tracks at the end (c01, c04, c10, c13).
- Render into an empty folder. A render into a folder that already held stems of the same names left REAPER idle and the bridge unresponsive (c09; inferred: an overwrite prompt that a script cannot answer). Move or delete the old stems first.
- With no master FX, the rendered stems sum to the master to about -122 dBFS peak (c10, c11, c13). A level-only fix found after a render (one track's gain, or a reshaped volume envelope on one or two tracks) can therefore be applied to the stems as the new-minus-old dB curve, and the master rebuilt as the stem sum, which equals a re-render. Update REAPER with the same data (`--mix-only` or `--midi-only` for those tracks) and save, so the project still matches (c10, c11, c13). Compute the stem's gain curve from the old and new envelope points with the same function that writes them (`build_reaper.vol_points`), not from the plan's spans (c18, c20, c23: stem sum matching the master to 7e-7 to 8e-7 before the change). Keep the first render's files when you do this.
- When rebuilding a master from stems, exclude the master file from the stem glob: `00_Master.wav` matches `[0-9][0-9]_*.wav`, and adding it doubled the master (peak 1.2) (c14).
- A section of a stem can be replaced from an earlier render of the same data (30 ms equal-power crossfades, the new-minus-old envelope applied, the tempo offset corrected) to undo a failed test without a second render (c14, c16). This is a valid render of that data but not an exact one: two renders of identical MIDI on the same patch differed by about -10 dB relative, from round robins (c14). Level-only fixes remain exact.
- REAPER can hang at launch with no window title and flat memory while the bridge stays silent. `sample <pid>` showed it waiting in `OnStartup_LoadProjects -> CheckFaultyProject -> MessageBox`: reaper.ini had a `faultyproject=` entry, and REAPER was asking whether to load that project with FX offline (c11). Without the GUI: kill REAPER, start it with `open -a REAPER --args -new` (an empty project, so the startup script and bridge run), then open the project with a bridge job `reaper.Main_openProject("noprompt:<path>")`. It returned in 17 s and the samples streamed in afterwards; REAPER cleared the entry itself (c11). This was used as the normal restart in c13-c18.
- Opening a 29-instance project that way deadlocked in two of five cycles inside the first Opus `VST3_LoadState` (c15, c17): memory flat at about 0.4 GB, 0% CPU, the bridge reporting "not running", and `sample <pid>` showing the main thread in `__psynch_mutexwait` under `Main_openProject -> FxDsp::LoadState -> VST3_LoadState -> Opus`. Kill REAPER and start again with `-new`, wait about 30 s after the bridge is alive, then open the project; the second attempt worked both times. If memory stays flat for 2 minutes during an open, run `sample` and retry. The 32-instance opens in c18-c23 did not deadlock (1-8 minutes each).
- The quit command (40004) sent by a bridge job can stop at a modal dialog (`sample` shows `runModalForWindow` under `Main_OnCommand`), probably a save prompt, even right after a save (c18, c19). In c20-c23 the quit job timed out at 60 s every time but REAPER had exited. Save first, quit with a 60 s timeout, then check the process and kill REAPER if it is still running.
- Timing: a 354 s render at 1x plus measurement takes about 5-6 minutes; the bridge call needs a long timeout (`--timeout 1800` used) and REAPER is blocked meanwhile (`render_cycle.py`).
- Tempo maps with many markers work: 300-385 square markers (0.25 qn steps inside rit./allargando spans, caesuras, phrase-final steps) caused no problems (c03-c13). Writing MIDI with `MIDI_GetPPQPosFromProjQN` keeps notes on quarter-note positions.
- Crashes and freezes seen: Opus crashed on a locator-only zone (c00); the Mac froze during a second render in one session (c04; memory pressure inferred, section 11); REAPER hung at startup on the faulty-project alert (c11); Opus deadlocked while loading a state on project open (c15, c17); the quit command stopped at a modal dialog (c18, c19). After the reset REAPER reopened the saved project and a state read-back matched the build data exactly (c04), so save before every render (`render_cycle.py` does).

## 16. Measurement methods that worked

- Render one stem per track with the master, and measure the post-fader stems, not only the mix (c00-c13).
- Simulate level-only changes before rendering. Apply each track's new-minus-old volume envelope (dB, on the score-time grid) to the previous render's stems, sum them, and run the delivery master (c10-c13). In c10 it predicted the delivered LRA (14.6 LU) and limiter (3.6 dB) that the render measured, and it rejected two changes that would have pushed the master over 0 dBFS or the limiter to 4.9 dB. It cannot model velocity, articulation, lead or patch changes, and with those at the climaxes it under-predicted the limiter by 0.4-0.6 dB (c11: 3.1 vs 3.5; c12: 2.4 vs 3.0). Leave about 0.5 dB of margin. Tempo changes and controller changes at a climax can cost more: 2.2 vs 3.2 dB when one string legato-join peak at a stretched beat ended 1.5 dB over every other master peak (c16), and 3.0 vs 3.4 dB when a CC1 change made a flute 2.2 dB louder (c17). An FX change on the same track also breaks the simulation: a harp role simulated at -3 dB came out -6, because a new side shelf took 3 dB at the same time (c20). After any FX change, measure the track's level in its other passages and correct the gain with a stem fix if needed.
- After every render, list the master's top 4 peaks and the stems that make them. A single legato-join transition peak can set the delivery limiter; a -4 dB notch over 0.17 qn on that one Vln I join took the limiter from 3.4 to 1.7 dB (c18).
- Gate every render with an attack probe before judging anything else (`tools/probe_attacks.py`, run by `render_cycle.py`; c05-c13). The probe used: about ten isolated reference attacks (sound start = first 10 ms frame reaching max(peak - 20 dB, floor + 6 dB); limit 0.06 s for pizz., trombone and timpani, 0.15-0.20 s for soft strings), four held-note-to-tremolo arrivals (dip in the first 0.45 s against the 0.25 s before, limit 6 dB), and the opening voices (loudest 50 ms in the first 1.5 s above -70 dB). It separated good and bad renders cleanly: c04 failed 11 of 14, c05 1 of 14, c06 10 of 19, c07-c11 0 of 19. From c12 to c23 every failure (0-2 per render) was a known probe artefact, confirmed by comparing the stem with the previous render frame by frame.
- The tremolo-hole check takes the minimum of 10 ms frames inside a tremolo and is noisy to about 2 dB: one check went 4.3 -> 6.6 dB and crossed the 6 dB limit while 25 ms envelopes of the two renders matched within 1 dB (c12, identical in c13). Two checks read 6.7 and 7.6 dB in each of c20-c23 with stems matching c19 within 1 dB. Compare envelopes before treating a marginal failure as a new hole; a 7 dB limit would stop these false failures.
- The peak - 20 dB start measures when a note becomes audible, not when it arrives: Vln I passed at +0.04 s while its body (-6 dB) arrived 0.27 s late (c07 critic C). For soft entries add a body - 6 dB arrival check, and record memory (`metrics/memory.txt`) with every render. The opposite error also occurs: a soft entry reported 0.34 s late because later material in the 1.5 s window got louder, while the stem matched the previous render within 1-2 dB from the first sound (c17, c18).
- Measure holes and levels on stereo power ((L²+R²)/2), not on the mono fold (L+R)/2. A horn sustain showed a 12.7 dB "collapse" in mono because L and R were anti-correlated (corr -0.7..-0.86) for 0.5 s; in stereo power it was one 7 dB frame, and a fill sized to the mono reading made a +6..9 dB bump (c18). Check the L/R correlation before filling a hole.
- Loop pulse on long notes: fold the note's level (detrended) at the loop period and report p95-p5 and the autocorrelation at the period (c16-c18). Level folding misses colour cycles that a level profile has already flattened: also fold the master's 100-300 Hz band level and spectral centroid over any held passage and take the autocorrelation at the loop period, against a short chord of the same voicing as control (0.18 there) (c26).
- Accent harshness: energy in 2-5 kHz over the first 80 ms of the accent minus the note's body, and the accent's 2-5 kHz against the surrounding sections in the same window (c26). Level over the neighbours alone passed accents a listener found harsh.
- Loudness motion, to check that balance work does not flatten the music: from ffmpeg's ebur128 metadata at 10 Hz (frames above -60 LUFS short-term), the 90th percentile of the 4 s change in short-term loudness and the std of the 1 s power mean of momentary loudness minus its 20 s power mean (`tools/motion.py`). Two Brahms slow movements measured 8.55/4.56 and 6.29/3.92 dB; this mockup's mp3 5.0-5.2/2.8-2.9, after five cycles of cuts on solo crests had lowered it. Treat a fall of more than 0.2 dB as a regression to justify (c24).
- Reproducibility: the fraction of active 50 ms frames that decorrelate between two renders of the same data (critic A's `det3.py`). Run it after any change meant to make renders repeat (c25: strings 1-4%, Flute 1 12%, Bassoon 1 20%).
- A notch that lowers a note body also lowers the reference level that a spike detector uses for the neighbouring join, so that join can be listed as a new spike with its audio unchanged (c24). A 10 ms threshold onset probe can flip from +0.12 to +0.34 s on identical audio when a slow string entry has two similar rises and the pre-onset floor moves 0.2 dB (c24). Compare with the previous render's audio before acting on either.
- A plain-amplitude simulation (stem times the dB envelope, master = master + new stem - old stem) predicted ReaEQ and volume-envelope changes within 0.3 dB on band shares and per-pitch fundamentals. On a sustained note with a 3 s trim ramp it came out about 2 dB low (c25). Check long-ramp trims against the render.
- Fast-figure clarity: the share of frames in each note's slot where its own pitch dominates, and the time of first dominance, against a control bar on the old articulation (c14-c16). A pass criterion set before the test (here a share of 0.5 and level within 1.5 dB of the control) made three failed articulation tests quick to close.
- Per-note level: K-weighted, from onset + 80 ms to min(note end, onset + 1.5 s), paired with the CC11 at the onset; fit dB against CC11 per track (dB/CC and level at p/f/ff); scan residuals for hot notes and average them per pitch (c01-c13).
- Note shape: the first 0.3 s against the body (0.3-0.85 qn) finds sagging starts and bumps that a body-only level hides (c08). Measure out to +0.6 s before sizing a fill (c11).
- Per-bar and per-beat stem levels against the printed dynamics, the list of loudest stems per bar, and group power sums (melody / harmony / bass) for balance at climaxes (c01-c13). Bar level against the bar's loudest printed dynamic gives one number for macro dynamics: correlation 0.63 (c00), 0.89 (c02), 0.85 (c07), slope about 4 dB per dynamic step. For climaxes and soft sections, compare the maximum short-term loudness per section and the power mean of each section relative to the integrated loudness (c12).
- Level across every hairpin, and the fall over the first 1-1.5 qn of each crescendo arrival note (c06, c08). The level spread of the single winds on a tutti chord (c11).
- Legato lag: time after the onset when energy at the new pitch's harmonics 1-4 exceeds the old pitch's (crossover), plus arrival within 6 dB; split by articulation, velocity, source pitch and interval (c02-c11). An f0 trace every 20 ms against candidate pitches for fast figures and grace runs (c03).
- Portamento: a harmonic-sum f0 tracker with 43 ms windows did not resolve a glide in a section, because the source and target pitches dominate every frame (c11). Mean energy at the intermediate semitones 0.15-0.45 s around the join, against the plain legato version, showed it (c12).
- Pitch-change timing on bass lines: use a long FFT (8192 points at 48 kHz) and harmonics 2-8 with at least ±1 bin. With 4096 points and harmonics 1-4, a ±2% band below 100 Hz holds 0-1 bins and the spread was too wide to act on (c20).

- Same-patch unisons: stem correlation in 0.5 s windows (c04).
- Space: per-stem L/R balance and correlation, side/mid ratio, tail balance after cutoffs, decay slopes (-5..-25 and -20..-40 dB) and the drop at 0.3/0.6/1.0 s after cutoffs (c01, c04, c05, c08, c09, c13).

## 17. Pipeline lessons

- Keep everything that changes the result in scripts and config files (`mix.json`, `roles.json`, `note_trims.json`, `pitch_trims.json`, `early_fills.json`, `entry_leads.json`, `leg_leads.json`); a setting made only inside REAPER is lost on the next rebuild (BRIEF).
- Keep `parts.json` on score time; add the pre-roll, leads and other offsets only when writing to REAPER, and cut the pre-roll off the rendered files (c06).
- Routing notes between tracks belongs in the extraction step: by score-onset window (a solo track for a "solo" passage, c10) or by window plus pitch threshold (a divisi voice, c13). A fixed pitch threshold fails when the divisi voices cross it; a "rank" rule (a note goes to the split track when another source note starts with it at a lower pitch) split the viola divisi correctly (c18). A window can also route two parts to one new track (the a2 horns, c18). The source track's divisi cut and the hand-over lengths have to be recomputed with the split.
- LilyPond extraction details that cost a cycle each: the slur stop event sits at the onset of the slur's last note, so test `start <= q <= stop` (c01); hairpins often have no stop event or a stop of the other kind, so close a hairpin at the next dynamic, the next hairpin or a rest of 2 qn or more (c01: a third of the printed dynamics were wrong, spans up to 268 qn); `arpeggio-event` needs its own listener (c04); `\removeWithTag #'part` can remove text that a second player on a shared staff needs (c05); a dynamic, mute or harp dynamic printed once on a shared staff may reach only one voice or one track (c05, c08); when a tie is merged into one MIDI note, a slur that starts on the tied continuation belongs to that note's outgoing join only, and marking the whole note as slurred turned an unslurred ff entry after a 14-semitone leap into a first Leg Slur MAX note that played 6-9 dB low (c16).
- Compare onsets that were rounded for output with a tolerance. A "cut notes that start before X" rule tested against rounded onsets cut the target note itself to 0.02 qn in one render and not in another (c09). Diff note durations in parts.json against the previous build around every new rule before rendering.
- Round every controller point computed from a moved note-on in the same direction as the note, or pad it earlier. A moved onset that rounded down by 0.00002 qn put the "entry level" CC11 point after the note-on in about half the cases, so pp entries started for 50-110 ms on the previous phrase's ff layer (attacks +14 dB over the body). Starting the controller 0.01 qn before the note-on fixed every case (c22, `PULL_PAD_QN`). After each build, check that CC11 at every moved note-on equals its value at the score onset (19 -> 5 mismatches, the rest legato targets and arrivals by design).
- Split a held note (re-bows, section 6) at the output stage, after the controller code. Split earlier, each piece took its own long-note swell and entry shape and CC11 rose 32 -> 40 at every re-bow (c26). Compare the CC lanes of parts.json before and after any note split.
- Key per-note tables by the score onset, not by the moved onset that leads produce: a trim keyed 260.892 (the moved onset) instead of 261.0 would not have matched (c25). State in each table which onset it uses.
- Hand fixes made for a symptom (a -9 dB note trim, a +8 dB fill, an extra 0.10 s lead) outlive the bug that caused it. When a bug fix removes a cause, search the trim, fill and lead tables for those notes and remove the compensations in the same cycle (c22: three removed).
- Hairpin rules that fixed measured errors (c06, c09, c11): an untargeted hairpin must not overshoot the next printed mark in its own direction; a decrescendo whose printed target is not below the current level falls 6 CC, and the mark at its end does not raise the level; "niente <" starts 12 CC below the target; see section 4 for messa di voce and look-ahead caps.
- Check the extracted dynamic curve against every printed mark and print any miss over 2 CC (c01).
- Change one family of things per cycle and keep before/after snapshots of tools and config; that is how the c04 vibrato change was cleared as a cause of late attacks (c05 critic A). Record the machine state (memory, compressed memory, other processes) with every render, since it changes render timing (c06-c13). A change that needs its own test render (a new hall IR for every track) should get a cycle to itself, so the level changes riding on the same render do not confound it (c13; in c17 the hall change was made and the new divisi tracks waited a cycle).
- Run an uncertain articulation or controller change as a test with a pass criterion written before the render and a control passage in the same render. Leave a failed test in the render and the tools so the render matches parts.json, and revert it next cycle; if it loaded an articulation row, the revert is a full rebuild of that track (c14-c18: six failed tests closed this way).
- Phrase-final lengthening by tempo steps (x1.015 in the first half of a phrase, x0.96 and x0.92 on the last two beats, whole-beat steps only) added 0.03-0.11 s per phrase and 1.06 s over 14 phrases; leads converted on the real tempo map followed without changes (c12). Skip a phrase that ends where a printed rit. starts, or the tempo jumps back up between them.

## 18. Where `guides/reaper-claude-scripting-guide.md` is wrong or incomplete

- §6.2 and §7 say patch loading inside Opus is not reachable from ReaScript. It is: write the patch JSON into the Opus state with `TrackFX_SetNamedConfigParm(tr, fx, "vst_chunk", ...)` (section 1 here).
- §6.3 says CC1 crossfades dynamic layers and CC11 shapes within a layer, and the §6.6 map uses `dynamics = 1, expression = 11, vibrato = 21`. In Hollywood Orchestra CC11 crossfades the dynamic layers and sets volume, CC1 crossfades a non-vibrato and a vibrato layer (not a depth control), and no CC21 vibrato control is mapped (section 3).
- §6.6 placeholder keyswitches (24-29, "range C0-B0") do not match the KS Masters: string keyswitches run from 24 up to 50, celli use 80-90, harp 21-35. Read them from the `.oib` files rather than from a screenshot (section 2).
- §6.3 "leave 50 ms of keyswitch lead": in practice put keyswitches in the preceding rest, and a keyswitch only works if its articulation row is loaded in the patch state (section 2).
- §10 "Keyswitches have no effect" omits the most common cause here: the articulation row is not loaded in the KS Master.
- §5 `R.tracks()` reports `vol_db` from `D_VOL`, which is wrong once a track has a volume envelope (section 15).
- §6.5 / `R.render` do not mention render speed, memory, or the render folder. Opus on an external sample drive needs 1x offline render (`RENDER_1X 1` in the `.rpp`, not settable through `GetSetProjectInfo`), a wait for sample loading, a silent pre-roll, a REAPER restart after patch reloads, an empty render folder, and an attack-timing check (sections 11, 15). `RENDER_SETTINGS 1` with `RENDER_BOUNDSFLAG 2` gave master plus stems in one pass; the guide's `what = 3` was not used.
